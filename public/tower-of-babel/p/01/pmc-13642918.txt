Reversal of canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway attenuates gestational diabetes mellitus-induced offspring cardiac hypertrophy: mechanistic insights into pathological remodeling
Authors: Xun Yuan, Yuejiang Jiao, Zexin Huang, Ruchao Jiang, Yiling Chen, Ducai Liu, Zexin Zhao, Qiulian Zhu, Yuan Qin, Guiping Zhang, Zhimei Feng, Caixian Yang, Ning Hou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundGestational diabetes mellitus (GDM), a prevalent prenatal metabolic disorder characterized by hyperglycemia occurring in approximately 9.2% of pregnancies globally, imposes significant cardiovascular risks on offspring, including developmental cardiac hypertrophy and long-term functional impairment. Despite its clinical importance, the molecular mechanisms governing GDM-induced cardiac malformations remain elusive.AimsThis study aimed to delineate the regulatory role of the Wnt/β-catenin/Tcf7l2 signaling pathway in mediating pathological cardiac remodeling in GDM-exposed offspring through experimental manipulation using a clinically relevant murine model.MethodsWe established a clinically relevant GDM mouse model exhibiting key metabolic features including hyperinsulinemia and impaired glucose tolerance, which faithfully recapitulates human GDM pathology. Cardiomyocyte-specific β-catenin knockout and cardiomyocyte-specific overexpression of constitutively active β-catenin were employed for genetic manipulation. Pharmacological intervention was performed using saxagliptin, a clinically approved DPP-4 inhibitor. Cardiac phenotypes were evaluated by histopathological analysis, echocardiography, and molecular assessments of Wnt/β-catenin pathway activity and hypertrophic markers.ResultsHistopathological and echocardiographic analyses revealed pronounced cardiac hypertrophy in GDM-exposed offspring, concomitant with activation of the Wnt/β-catenin/Tcf7l2 pathway in myocardial tissues. Functional studies demonstrated that cardiomyocyte-specific β-catenin ablation attenuated GDM-induced hypertrophic remodeling, whereas constitutive β-catenin overexpression exacerbated cardiac dysfunction. Importantly, pharmacological intervention with saxagliptin significantly ameliorated cardiac hypertrophy in GDM-offspring. This therapeutic effect was paralleled by marked suppression of Wnt/β-catenin signaling activity and reduced expression of ANP.ConclusionCollectively, these findings provide compelling evidence that dysregulation of the Wnt/β-catenin/Tcf7l2 pathway constitutes a critical mediator in GDM-induced cardiac hypertrophy, and highlight saxagliptin as a potential therapeutic strategy to mitigate adverse cardiac outcomes in GDM offspring.

Introduction

Gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM), characterized by hyperglycemia during pregnancy, is one of the most common medical complications associated with pregnancy. The prevalence of GDM ranging from 1 to > 30% in worldwide. According to the GDM prevalece map published by WHO, the highest prevalence of GDM is East and some North African countries, the lowest GDM prevalence observed in Europe. The Western Pacific GDM prevalence range from 7.1 to 16.6% were observed. The prevalence of undiagnosed hyperglycemia is increasing. GDM has long-term consequences for offspring, including metabolic abnormalities and cardiac hypertrophy, attributed to disrupted metabolic and cardiac development. Clinical, in vivo, and in vitro studies have demonstrated the association of GDM with metabolic syndrome [1], and cardiac hypertrophy [2]. An essential pathway involved in cardiac hypertrophy is the Wnt/β-catenin/Tcf7l2 signaling pathway, which regulates embryonic cardiac development, hypertrophic responses, and cardiomyocyte (CM) proliferation [3]. This pathway also contributes to heart hypertrophy, remodeling, and extracellular matrix changes, leading to increased ventricular wall thickness [3–7]. Despite these insights, the genetic mechanisms underlying GDM-induced cardiac hypertrophy in offspring remain poorly unknown.

The T-cell factor (Tcf) family of transcription factors plays a critical role in various developmental processes. T-cell factor 7-like 2 (Tcf7l2), a member of this family, serves as a DNA-binding protein and nuclear interaction partner of β-catenin, which is essential for Wnt growth factor signaling [8–11]. Cardiac-specific deletion of Adenomatosis polyposis coli (APC), which activates Wnt/β-catenin/Tcf7l2 signaling, is embryonically lethal by E10.5, with embryos showing accelerated development and cardiac hypertrophy [9]. However, the role of Wnt/β-catenin/Tcf7l2 signaling in GDM-induced cardiac hypertrophy remains incompletely understood.

This study aimed to investigate the role of the Wnt/β-catenin/Tcf7l2 signaling pathway in cardiac remodeling and hypertrophy in offspring affected by GDM, using β-catenin loss-of-function mice. The study aimed to elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying GDM-induced cardiac dysfunction and explore potential strategies for improving the diagnosis and treatment of long-term cardiac complications in the offspring of GDM mothers.

Methods

Animal studies, administration procedures, and echocardiography

All animal experiments conformed to the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals published by the US National Institutes of Health (NIH Publication No. 85-23, 1996, revised 2011) and conducted in accordance with guidelines approved by the Institutional Guidelines established by the Committee of Ethics on Animal Experiments at Guangzhou Medical University (approval number GY2018-104). Female C57BL/6 mice, sourced from Nanmo Biological or bred in-house, were used. β-catenin and Apc loxP mice were purchased from Jackson Laboratory. Female β-catenin and Apc loxP mice (Cre-negative) were mated with heterozygous loxP mice (Myh6 Cre-positive) to produce littermate controls (WT). Comparisons were made exclusively among littermate mice. Following weaning, all female mice were maintained on a normal chow diet (ND), with a subset switched to a high-fat diet (HFD, D12492 Rodent Diet With 60 kcal% Fat, Research Diet, USA) at age of 13.5 weeks. The female mice were fed with HFD for 3 weeks before mating to establish the GDM models. Intraperitoneal glucose tolerance tests (IPGTT, 2 g/kg body weight) were conducted before mating and again on 10.5 days of pregnancy.

Paternal metabolic status was not formally assessed in this study; sires were presumed to be metabolically normal based on age, genetic background, and standardized diet/housing conditions. Pregnancy was confirmed by the presence of a vaginal plug (0.5 days post-coitum). Saxagliptin (MB1671, Meilun Biotech Co., China) was administered intraperitoneal daily at a dose of 10 mg/kg/day from 10.5 days to day 21 of pregnancy. Mice were monitored every 12 h near parturition and daily throughout the experimental period. GDM-exposed offspring were nursed by their biological dams until weaning, and no cross-fostering was performed. Echocardiographic assessments were performed on postnatal days 7, 21, and 90 using the Vevo 2100 high-resolution imaging system (VisualSonics) equipped with an MS-400 transducer. Mice were anesthetized with 1-2% isoflurane in compressed air, and measurements were recorded at a heart rate of 450-550 bpm. Cardiac function and geometry were evaluated in the parasternal short-axis view, with M-mode images used for analysis. The E/A ratio was acquired and calculated from the images of mitral valve Doppler flow. Results were averaged over at least five cardiac cycles for accuracy.

Transgenetic mouse model

Mice with two loxP sites inserted in the β-catenin gene’s 1 and 6 introns position were donated by Dr. Rolf Kemler (Max-Planck Institute of Immunology, Germany) and housed at The Jackson Laboratory (Stock No.004152) (Bar Harbor, ME). Transgenic mice expressing Cre recombinase under the control of the α-myosin heavy chain promoter (αMHC-Cre) were donated by Dr. M D Schneider and maintained at The Jackson Laboratory (Stock No.011038). The homozygous β-catenin loxP-floxed (β-catenin flox/flox) mice were mated with α MHC-Cre mice to produce cardiac-specific β-catenin knockdown mouse models. Because conventional and cardiomyocyte-specific β-catenin -knock-out mice are embryonically lethal (Ye et al. [9]), the heterozygous β-catenin deletion Het (β-cat flox/+, αMHC-Cre+) mice or WT (β-cat flox/+, αMHC-Cre-) mice were used for subsequent experiments. Littermate wild-type mice without Cre recombinase expression were used as controls. Supplementary Fig. S1 shows the all the genotyping results.

Intraperitoneal glucose tolerance testing

IPGTT was conducted the day before mating and on day 10.5 of pregnancy. Mice were fasted for 6 hours (h) before each test. Following fasting, they received an intraperitoneal injection of glucose at a dose of 2 g/kg body weight. Blood glucose levels were measured at baseline (before glucose administration) and at 15-, 30-, 60-, 90-, and 120 minutes (min) post-injection. Blood samples were collected from the tail vein and analyzed using One-Touch™ Ultra Mini® Blood Glucose Monitoring System (Johnson & Johnson, New Brunswick, NJ, USA). Each measurement was taken twice at each time point to ensure accuracy.

Isolation of adult mouse ventricular myocytes

Adult mouse left ventricular myocytes (CMs) were isolated following the method described previously [6]. Briefly, cardiac tissues from C57BL/6 J mice were initially perfused with a calcium-depleted digestion buffer containing 300 U/mL collagenase II (Worthington, USA) for 3 min. This was followed by perfusion with a buffer containing 28.57 μM calcium for 10–15 min, allowing the hearts to soften, become flaccid, and turn pale. The CMs were then isolated and subjected to a gradient re-calcification process. The isolated cells were cultured in a medium containing 2.5% fetal bovine serum (FBS), minimal essential medium (MEM), insulin-transferrin-selenium, and Blebbistatin for further experimental analysis.

Antibodies

Supplment Table S1 shows the antibodies list.

Western blot

For Western blot analysis, cardiac tissues were homogenized in RIPA buffer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cat. 89900) supplemented with protease inhibitors (Roche, Cat. 11-836-170-001) for 10 min. The homogenates were centrifuged at 16,000 × g for 15 min at 4 °C, and the protein concentrations in the supernatants were quantified using the Pierce BCA Protein Assay Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cat. 23225). Protein lysates were prepared in Laemmli sample buffer, separated on 10% or 12% SDS-PAGE gels, and transferred to PVDF membranes. Membranes were blocked with 5% non-fat milk in TBST for 1 h at room temperature, followed by incubation with specific primary antibodies (Supplemental Fig. S1) and their corresponding HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies. Protein signals were detected using HRP-derived chemiluminescence and captured on film. Protein expression levels were quantified using ImageJ software (NIH).

Transcriptome sequencing

Heart tissues were collected from 7-day-old offspring of GDM and non-GDM mice and immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen. Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent (Life Technologies, USA), and RNA integrity (RIN ≥ 7.9) and quantity were assessed using the Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer. Equal amounts of RNA from individual mice were pooled (2-3 samples per pool, 2-3 pools per group) for analysis. Transcriptome sequencing was performed using the Illumina HiSeqTM 2000 platform at BGI-Shenzhen (Beijing Genome Institute, Shenzhen, China). High-quality reads were mapped to the mouse transcriptome and genome using HISAT2 and Bowtie2 tools. Normalization was carried out using mapped data, and fragments per kilobase per million mapped reads (FPKM) values were calculated using RESM software. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between non-GDM and GDM offspring were identified based on a false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.01 and an absolute log2 fold change ≥ 2.

Immunofluorescence

After the mice were sacrificed, the hearts were carefully removed and placed into ice-cold PBS. The hearts were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde in PBS for 48 h, then washed twice with cold PBS. Following this, the hearts were placed in 30% sucrose in PBS for 4 h. After sucrose infiltration, the hearts were embedded in an optimal cutting temperature (OCT) medium, snap-frozen in dry ice, and stored at −80 °C. Heart sections of 5 μm thickness were cut using a cryostat. To reduce non-specific crosslinking from residual paraformaldehyde, sections were washed with 0.1 mol/L glycine for 25 min. Following this, they were incubated in a blocking buffer for 60 min to block non-specific binding sites. Sections were then incubated overnight at 4 °C with the specific primary antibodies (listed above). Afterward, the sections were incubated with secondary antibodies conjugated to Alexa Fluor 488 for 1 h, and α-actinin antibody conjugated with Alexa Fluor 550 for 1 h. Nuclei were stained with DAPI (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO). Confocal images were captured using a Nikon A1R confocal microscope, ensuring uniform settings for all samples.

Histological analysis

The mouse hearts were harvested, fixed in 10% formaldehyde, embedded in paraffin, and cut into 4-μm-thick longitudinal sections. The sections were stained with hematoxylin-eosin (HE) or Masson’s trichrome for histopathological analysis.

Statistical analysis

All data are presented as the mean ± SEM and analyzed using GraphPad Prism 5.0 (San Diego, USA). Statistical comparisons were made using One-Way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post-hoc test. For comparisons between the two groups, the Unpaired t-test was applied when homogenous variance, in case of lack of homogenous variance, Mann–Whitney test was applied. A P-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Graphic abstract and schematic illustrations were created by Biorender.com.

GDM induces offspring cardiac dysfunction with long-term consequences

The short-term cardiac hypertrophy in GDM-induced offspring was confirmed by the morphological findings. To further investigate the long-term cardiac functional consequences of GDM, echocardiography was performed. The results revealed that GDM offspring showed a reduced mitral valve E wave and an elevated atrial contraction mitral valve A wave. The reduced E/A ratio indicated the presence of cardiac diastolic dysfunction in GDM offspring at 90 days post-delivery (Fig. 2E). To assess CM size in GDM offspring, adult CMs were isolated from both control and GDM offspring. The results showed that the CM area was significantly increased in GDM offspring at 120 days post-delivery (Fig. 2F). To further confirm cardiac hypertrophy in GDM offspring, the protein expression of atrial natriuretic peptide (ANP) was measured. The total ANP protein expression was significantly higher in the heart tissue of GDM offspring compared to the control group (Fig. 2G).

Saxagliptin attenuates GDM-induced offspring cardiac hypertrophy by decreasing the Wnt/β-catenin/Tcf7l2 pathway

To explore the potential pharmacological intervention for GDM during pregnancy, saxagliptin was administered, and its effects on the offspring hearts of GDM pregnancies were analyzed. The findings indicate that saxagliptin attenuated GDM-induced cardiac hypertrophy in offspring. The echocardiographic analysis demonstrated a reduction in the heart weight-to-body weight (HW/BW) ratio and ventricular wall thickness in saxagliptin-treated GDM offspring (Fig. 6A, B). Moreover, GDM-induced cardiac diastolic dysfunction in offspring was mitigated by saxagliptin administration during pregnancy (Fig. 6A, B), highlighting the therapeutic benefit of inhibiting the β-catenin/Tcf7l2 pathway. Discussion

Cardiovascular diseases play a significant role in the long-term response of offspring to GDM. Hyperglycemia in women with GDM triggers fetal hyperinsulinemia, which stimulates triglyceride synthesis and fat storage in white adipocytes in a sex-dependent manner [12, 13]. This fetal hyperinsulinemia is crucial for counteracting the hyperglycemia induced by GDM. However, it ultimately leads to excessive triglyceride synthesis and macrosomia. Both fetal hyperglycemia and hyperinsulinemia contribute to long-term metabolic and cardiovascular effects in offspring [14, 15]. The exact pathogenic mechanisms underlying these long-term effects remain unclear, though maternal hyperglycemia-induced alterations in DNA methylation, miRNA content in fetal blood, skeletal muscle, adipose tissue, and other factors are likely involved. The mechanisms underlying the shift from neonatal regenerative CMs to the loss of regenerative capacity in adulthood remain unclear. Wnt/β-catenin signaling plays a key role in cardiac chamber maturation and CM expansion following cardiac looping morphogenesis [10]. As birth approaches, CM cell cycle activity decreases significantly, although DNA replication and CM binucleation occur during the first two weeks after birth. In this study, the role of Wnt/β-catenin in the long-term cardiovascular effects induced by GDM was investigated. The findings show that Wnt/β-catenin signaling is robustly activated in the offspring of GDM mouse models, initiating downstream events mediated by β-catenin nuclear accumulation and its interaction with the transcriptional regulator TCF7L2 in CMs. This activation enhanced nuclear content synthesis and cell cycle gene expression in CMs. However, as the CMs exited the cell cycle two weeks after birth, prolonged activation of Wnt/β-catenin led to excessive nuclear content synthesis and persistent cell cycle gene expression, ultimately resulting in cardiac hypertrophy. Inhibition of this pathway reduced cell cycle gene expression and alleviated cardiac hypertrophy. Furthermore, genetic editing or pharmacological intervention to inhibit the Wnt/β-catenin pathway mitigated GDM-induced cardiac hypertrophy and dysfunction in offspring. These findings highlighted the critical role of Wnt/β-catenin in the long-term cardiovascular effects of GDM and revealed a novel mechanism underlying GDM-induced cardiac hypertrophy in offspring.

The glucose concentration gradient between the maternal and fetal circulation is a key factor in determining the amount of maternal glucose that reaches the fetus. When glucose levels in the fetus are high, fetal glycemia is regulated by fetal insulin levels. Fetal hyperinsulinemia promotes glucose uptake into peripheral tissues, thereby steepening the concentration gradient between maternal and fetal circulation. The fetus actively draws glucose from maternal circulation, a phenomenon known as “fetal glucose steal” [16]. Maternal glucose is the primary macronutrient that supports fetal growth. In pregnant women with GDM, prolonged exposure of the fetal pancreas to hyperglycemia accelerates insulin secretion from pancreatic β-cells, resulting in early hyperinsulinemia induced by fetal hyperglycemia. This places the fetal heart in a state of both hyperglycemia and hyperinsulinemia. Interestingly, in murine HFD pregnancy models, cardiac hypertrophy is the predominant phenotype observed in HFD offspring [17]. Cardiac hypertrophy is a common condition in offspring born to mothers with GDM and remains a significant concern. CM hypertrophy initiates a complex cascade of events involving DNA replication, CM nuclear expansion, and pathological heart remodeling. Prolonged exposure to hyperglycemia and hyperinsulinemia in diabetic animal models contributes to both cardiac hypertrophy and dysfunction. Clinically, insulin therapy is the most widely accepted pharmacological intervention for treating GDM. However, recent studies have shown that insulin therapy offers limited beneficial effects for GDM offspring [18]. These findings suggest that targeted interventions, informed by a deeper understanding of the pathophysiology and molecular mechanisms underlying GDM-induced cardiac hypertrophy, may improve both short-term and long-term cardiac outcomes in offspring.

Activation of the Wnt/β-catenin/Tcf7l2 pathway has been demonstrated in a Transverse Aortic Constriction (TAC) and T2DM- induced cardiac hypertrophy animal model [3, 19, 20]. Furthermore, Tcf7l2 mediates canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling and upregulates c-myc in idiopathic dilated cardiomyopathy and ischemic heart diseases [4]. However, reversing pathological cardiac hypertrophy has proven to be challenging. Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) is believed to arise from defects in genes that regulate myocyte growth, distinguishing it from pressure overload-induced cardiac hypertrophy. The present study identified an important role for the Wnt/β-catenin/Tcf7l2 pathway in heart development in a GDM mouse model.

Depending on the context, activation of the Wnt/β-catenin/Tcf7l2 pathway can either promote cell proliferation and cell cycle progression or increase nuclear content synthesis, leading to cardiac hypertrophy. This dual function stems from the unique ability of CMs to exit the cell cycle in a continuum after birth. The critical role of the transformation of CM proliferation from a proliferative to a quiescent state after birth has been demonstrated [21, 22]. Mice with activated Wnt/β-catenin/Tcf7l2 signaling in CMs display an accelerated proliferation gradient in the heart [9]. The present study reports that the offspring of GDM mice show persistent activation of the Wnt/β-catenin/Tcf7l2 pathway, leading to cardiac hypertrophy. Cardiac-specific downregulation of the β-catenin/Tcf7l2 pathway attenuates GDM-induced cardiac hypertrophy in offspring.

Lifestyle interventions, including dietary modifications and the promotion of physical activity, remain the primary management strategies for pregnant women with GDM. Glucose is the key nutrient for fetal growth and is associated with fetal overgrowth and other pregnancy complications in GDM. Therefore, maintaining balanced maternal blood glucose levels remains the primary goal in the treatment of GDM. For decades, insulin has been the primary pharmacotherapy for managing GDM during pregnancy when blood glucose levels cannot be adequately controlled through lifestyle interventions [23]. However, recent studies have shown that insulin treatment for GDM does not fully protect offspring from short-term and long-term metabolic or cardiovascular consequences [18, 24]. Other major medical treatments, such as metformin and sulfonylureas, are not recommended due to the potential for higher birth weight, increased rates of macrosomia, and neonatal hypoglycemia. Furthermore, metformin and glyburide can cross the placenta to the fetus [25]. Recent clinical trials have shown that GDM increases the rates of long-term cardiovascular complications in offspring [26, 27]. Animal studies have demonstrated that insulin treatment for GDM does not fully protect offspring from metabolic disorders, suggesting that hyperglycemia is not the only factor contributing to the short-term and long-term effects of GDM on offspring. DPP-4 inhibitors, such as Saxagliptin and Sigliptin, have been developed to treat diabetes by inhibiting the enzymatic action of DPP-4, which elevates GLP-1 levels. Preclinical research has reported that DPP-4 inhibitors have protective effects on the cardiovascular system [28, 29]. Animal studies have shown that Saxagliptin (Pregnancy Category B) is not teratogenic at any dose when administered to pregnant animals during the organogenesis period. Meanwhile, preclinical and clinical studies have demonstrated that SGLT-2 inhibitors and GLP-1 agonists offer cardiovascular protective effects. However, preclinical studies indicate that empagliflozin (Pregnancy Category C) may impact renal development and maturation during organogenesis, while liraglutide (Pregnancy Category X) should not be used during pregnancy due to its association with weight loss. Studies have also reported that Saxagliptin delayed thioacetamide-induced hepatocarcinogenesis through significant suppression of hepatic Wnt3a and β-catenin levels [30]. Present results suggest that Saxagliptin alleviates GDM-induced offspring cardiac hypertrophy by downregulating the cardiac Wnt/β-catenin/Tcf7l2 signaling pathway. These promising preclinical observations, saxagliptin cannot be routinely advocated for therapeutic use in human pregnancy at this stage, given the absence of comprehensive long‑term safety and developmental toxicity data [31].

Present findings, which show that β-catenin downstream targets are among the up-regulated genes observed in pathological cardiomyopathy [4], support a model in which β-catenin-mediated transcriptional regulation in the nucleus plays a crucial role in influencing the cardiac hypertrophic phenotype during pathological cardiomyopathy.

This study has several limitations. The Wnt/β-catenin/Tcf7l2 signaling pathway was only analyzed in the hearts of mouse offspring from GDM due to the limited availability of human GDM offspring heart tissue. However, echocardiographic and morphological assessments revealed significant cardiac hypertrophy in the offspring of insulin-treated GDM mothers. These findings strongly suggest that even with controlled blood glucose during pregnancy, GDM patients are at a higher risk of inducing cardiac hypertrophy in their newborns. While there is robust in vivo evidence, the study lacks in vitro data directly linking Wnt/β-catenin/Tcf7l2 signaling with cardiac hypertrophy in GDM offspring. Although saxagliptin administration during pregnancy showed some effects, insulin therapy could not fully prevent cardiac hypertrophy in the offspring. The exact mechanism by which saxagliptin mitigates GDM-induced cardiac hypertrophy in offspring remains unclear. A key limitation of the present study is the lack of a cross-fostering design. Therefore, the observed phenotypes in GDM-offspring likely reflect the combined effects of prenatal hyperglycemic exposure in utero and postnatal lactational exposure to dams with a history of GDM. Future studies using cross-fostering to non-GDM dams will be necessary to disentangle the specific contributions of the prenatal in utero environment from postnatal lactational influences.

In conclusion, the persistent activation of the β-catenin/Tcf7l2 pathway in GDM hearts contributes to offspring cardiac hypertrophy and pathological remodeling. Downregulation of β-catenin in the offspring heart effectively reverses these effects. Saxagliptin, a DPP-4 inhibitor, attenuates GDM-induced cardiac hypertrophy and remodeling by suppressing the β-catenin/Tcf7l2 pathway. These results highlight a novel mechanism underlying GDM-induced cardiac remodeling and suggest that targeting the Wnt/β-catenin/Tcf7l2 pathway with saxagliptin could be a promising therapeutic approach for both short-term and long-term cardiac complications in GDM offspring.

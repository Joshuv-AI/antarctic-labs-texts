Advancing Drug Delivery: Design and Applications of MOF-Polyurethane Composites for Controlled Release Systems
Authors: Chang Yan, Shuhui Hu, Qiang Fei, Bo Zhang, Wenneng Wu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The design and development of controlled-release drug systems represent a pivotal area of research in modern pharmaceutical technologies. An ideal drug release system should precisely regulate both the release rate and duration of the drug, thereby enhancing therapeutic efficacy, reducing dose frequency, and minimizing adverse effects. In recent years, nanomaterials have become integral to drug delivery, with nanotechnology focusing on the design and application of nanomaterials. Metal–organic frameworks (MOFs) have emerged as promising drug carriers due to their high specific surface area, tunable porosity, and selective adsorption capabilities. However, MOFs often exhibit limited thermal stability, susceptibility to degradation, and sensitivity to acidic and basic conditions. To address these limitations, the integration of MOFs with polymers has shown promising potential. MOF-polymer composites can enhance drug loading capacity, improve drug solubility, and provide greater thermal stability while mitigating adverse reactions. Polyurethane (PU) is widely employed as a drug carrier due to its unique chemical properties and biodegradability. However, PU alone may lead to issues such as burst drug release and vulnerability to fungal colonization. Consequently, MOF-PU composites have garnered significant attention as advanced drug carriers, leveraging the synergistic properties of both materials to overcome their individual limitations. This review explores the definition, preparation methods, advantages, and application domains of MOF-PU composites, with a particular emphasis on their role as drug carriers. The objective is to provide a comprehensive reference for ongoing and future research into the utilization of MOF-PU composites in drug delivery systems.

Introduction

The medicine field has consistently prioritized healthcare advancements, reflecting a widespread societal interest in health and well-being. Within this field, drug delivery systems (DDSs) are of paramount importance due to their potential for simplified, rapid, and convenient therapeutic administration. However, conventional DDSs often suffer from limitations including poor target specificity, cytotoxicity, low drug solubility, and short in vivo half-life, collectively compromising therapeutic efficacy. Nanotechnology, with its focus on the design and manipulation of materials at the nanoscale, offers promising solutions to these challenges. Nanoparticles (NPs), in particular, exhibit advantageous characteristics such as a high surface area-to-volume ratio and unique physicochemical properties. These attributes enable NPs to function as efficient delivery vehicles, facilitating targeted and controlled transport of therapeutic agents to specific tissues in vivo.

Metal–organic frameworks (MOFs) are a class of porous nanomaterials constructed from metal ions or clusters coordinated by organic linkers. MOFs are distinguished by their high specific surface area, tunable pore size, high sensitivity, and selective adsorption capabilities, rendering them versatile for applications in catalysis, sensing, gas separation, and, notably drug delivery. Their inherent porosity and the presence of functional sites derived from diverse ligands allow for effective encapsulation and binding of a wide range of molecules or ions. Compared to alternative nanocarriers such as liposomes, polymers, quantum dots, and inorganic nanoparticles, MOFs offer potential advantages as drug carriers, including higher drug loading capacity, improved stability, and reduced systemic toxicity. Nevertheless, MOFs also face challenges related to their inherent stability, structural fragility, and susceptibility to premature drug release under varying environmental conditions (e.g., temperature, humidity, and pressure). , These limitations can negatively impact their performance in drug adsorption and delivery. To address these drawbacks, researchers have investigated the integration of MOFs with polymers to create MOF-polymer composites. Such composites aim to enhance the stability and mechanical properties of MOFs, mitigate the influence of environmental factors, improve the precision and duration of drug release, and reduce potential toxicity and side effects.

Polyurethane (PU) is a widely employed polymer in drug delivery applications due to its advantageous properties, including facile synthesis, biocompatibility, bioinertness, and biodegradability. , PUs is particularly well-suited for the delivery of hydrophobic drugs and have become integral components of various drug delivery systems. , However, despite these benefits, PUs is susceptible to burst drug release and exhibit slow degradation rates, potentially limiting their efficacy as drug carriers. MOF-PU composites aim to address these limitations by capitalizing on the high specific surface area, tunable porosity, and selective adsorption capabilities of metal–organic frameworks (MOFs), combined with the mechanical strength and biocompatibility of PU. This synergistic combination can lead to improved drug loading capacity, enhanced stability, and controlled drug release profiles. − MOF@PU polymers also have broad flexibility, allowing them to adapt to various application scenarios. The key lies in the combination of MOF’s functional diversity and PU’s matrix adaptability (such as ZIF-8 for gas adsorption and MIL-100 for drug loading), which can achieve functional switching in the same PU matrix. Mahdi et al. modified the polymer matrix by incorporating nano crystalline zeolitic frameworks (nano ZIFs) to adsorb C2H4. It has been demonstrated that incorporating ZIF-8 and ZIF-7 nanocrystals into a polymer matrix (Matrimid and polyurethane [PU]) can produce robust nanocomposites that retain the adsorption/desorption ability of the nanocrystals for C2H4 while shielding degradation factors. Importantly, after adding ZIF-(7,8) nanoparticles as porous functional fillers (both of which have high affinity for hydrocarbons), the resulting nanocomposites can improve material performance (i.e., from almost negligible polymer adsorption/desorption to an average of ∼0.5 mmol g/desorption of polymer/ZIF nanocomposites at 5 and 35 °C), elucidating the retention of the intrinsic structural framework dynamics of nano ZIF, even when embedded in a polymer matrix, the inherent properties of ZIF nanocrystals can be preserved. This paves the way for the customized design of new polymer nanocomposites, which will subsequently affect the effective industrial application of customized nanomaterials. The universality of electrospinning technology further supports large-scale preparation (Table ). For instance, Zhang et al. successfully synthesized multifunctional NO@Cu-MOFs@PC coatings exhibiting excellent mechanical stability, anticoagulant, antimicrobial, and biocompatibility properties. These coatings effectively inhibited platelet adhesion and activation while also demonstrating significant antimicrobial activity against E. coli. Similarly, He et al. developed a novel humidity-driven actuator by incorporating MIL-88A, a metal–organic framework, into a thermoplastic PU film, resulting in a composite with remarkable self-folding response and stability under varying humidity conditions. Given the relatively nascent stage of MOF-PU-based composite material development, research on their application in controlled drug release remains limited. A comprehensive understanding of the preparation methods, advantages, and applications of MOF-PU-based composites is crucial for establishing benchmarks for the development of functional materials. This review focuses on the preparation methods and advantages of MOF-PU-based composites and their application as carriers for controlled drug release in the pharmaceutical field. Furthermore, we discuss the challenges these materials face in future applications. The overarching goal is to provide a foundation for advancements in the biomedical and agricultural fields, ultimately contributing to the progress of drug delivery systems.

Advantages and Disadvantages of MOFs

Metal–organic frameworks (MOFs) have garnered significant attention due to their structural diversity and potential applications across various fields related to porous materials. This has led to increased research efforts focused on MOF synthesis. MOFs have emerged as promising candidates for gas adsorption and separation, chemical sensing, multiphase catalysis, energy storage/conversion, and biological applications. Their potential in biomedical applications and as sensor materials is currently under intensive investigation. Comprehensive reviews, including those partially covering MOF synthesis, have been published. As organic–inorganic hybrid materials, MOFs inherit characteristics from both organic and inorganic components. They possess a rich structural variety, high specific surface area, tunable morphology and size, and diverse chemical functionalities, offering significant advantages over conventional porous materials such as inorganic zeolites, molecular sieves, and activated carbons. These advantages are primarily manifested in the following aspects:

Well-Defined Structure

MOFs are a class of crystalline porous materials with well-defined pore structures and adjustable pore diameters, formed through the self-assembly of metal clusters/ions and organic ligands via coordination bonds. Unlike some traditional materials, the well-defined crystal structures of MOFs can be determined by single-crystal X-ray diffraction, providing direct evidence for investigating related chemical reaction mechanisms and structure–property relationships.

Structural Diversity

The variety of MOF materials can be tailored by selecting appropriate metal ions and organic ligands under specific reaction conditions. The vast number of organic ligands and metal ions allows for the synthesis of a diverse range of MOF materials. Furthermore, the incorporation of binary or even multiple organic ligands or metal ions in the reaction system offers virtually unlimited possibilities for MOF synthesis. Currently, over 80,000 MOF structures are cataloged in the Cambridge Crystallographic Data Centre (CCDC), and the theoretical number of possible MOFs is essentially limitless.

Designability and Modifiability

Functional building blocks can be strategically selected or designed to directly synthesize MOF materials with specific functionalities. Alternatively, different classes of functional groups can be introduced via postsynthetic modification (PSM) for targeted performance modulation, enabling the preparation of MOF materials tailored for specific applications.

Ultra-High Specific Surface Area and Multi-Porous Structure

MOFs exhibit ultrahigh specific surface areas, pore volumes, and porosities. For example, NU-110 boasts a BET specific surface area of up to 7140 m2/g and a pore volume of up to 4.40 cm3/g. Pore dimensions are influenced by the size and shape of the organic ligands and their coordination mode with the metal ions, allowing for the design of pore properties. This porous structure exposes a large number of active sites, making MOFs promising candidates for applications in heterogeneous catalysis, gas storage and separation, molecular sensing, and optoelectronic materials.

Biocompatibility

The metal ion centers in MOF materials can be selected from biocompatible elements such as Fe, Al, Ca, and Mg. The organic ligands can be chosen from biological organic molecules such as condensates and adenine, in addition to common carboxylic acids and nitrogen-containing heterocyclic organic ligands. The combination of these components under appropriate reaction conditions can be directed toward the construction of bio-MOFs, which have been identified as promising materials in the biomedical field. −

However, there are still some key defects in the practical application of MOFs, which limit their large-scale commercial development, mainly including the following aspects:

Insufficient Chemical Stability

Most MOFs are prone to structural collapse in water, acid, alkali, or high temperature environments, especially those containing metal nodes such as Zn and Cu, which are prone to hydrolysis, leading to pore structure damage and affecting their long-term performance.

Low Mechanical Strength

MOFs typically exhibit brittle crystal structures, poor mechanical strength, and are prone to fracture under pressure or shear forces, making it difficult to directly process into flexible devices or industrial grade adsorbent materials.

The Conductivity Is Generally Poor

Although some MOFs (such as Ni-(HITP)2) have intrinsic conductivity, most MOFs are insulators, limiting their applications in fields such as electrochemical sensing and energy storage devices.

Large Scale Preparation and Cost Issues

The synthesis of MOFs typically requires high-purity organic ligands and metal salts, and the solvothermal method is time-consuming and energy consuming, making it difficult to achieve low-cost, large-scale production. In addition, the high cost of raw materials for some MOFs, such as rare metals, further raises the threshold for industrial applications.

Difficulty in Processing and Forming

MOFs mostly exist in powder form and are difficult to directly process into thin films, fibers, or bulk materials. Therefore, polymer composites or substrate loading are needed to enhance processability, but some porosity and active sites may be sacrificed.

Advantages and Disadvantages of Polyurethanes

Polyurethanes (PUs) constitute a significant class of thermoplastic and thermoset polymers, characterized by their customizable mechanical, thermal, and chemical properties through the reaction of diverse polyols and polyisocyanates. These polymers are defined by the presence of numerous carbamate groups (−HN–COO−) within their molecular structure. The versatility of PUs is evident in their extensive applications, ranging from rigid foams for insulation and construction to flexible foams in furniture, thermoplastic polyurethanes in footwear and medical devices, as well as coatings, adhesives, sealants, and elastomers in various industries. Over recent decades, the utilization of PUs has expanded significantly due to their advantageous properties, including comfort, cost-effectiveness, energy efficiency, and potential environmental benefits. Specifically, PUs exhibits high abrasion resistance, softness, chemical resistance, temperature tolerance, and ease of maintenance. However, certain limitations persist, such as restricted breathability, susceptibility to scratches, limited high-temperature resistance, and environmental concerns. Material selection should therefore be based on comprehensive evaluation of specific requirements and environmental conditions to optimize performance and user experience. PUs are broadly categorized into water-based and oil-based systems. Water-based PUs offer ease of processing and cost efficiency but demonstrate reduced durability and water resistance. Conversely, oil-based PUs exhibits superior strength and abrasion resistance, albeit with lower tensile strength.

In biomedical applications, PUs has demonstrated significant potential due to their excellent biocompatibility, bacteriostatic properties, mechanical performance, adhesion characteristics, and cost-effectiveness. Their adaptability is further enhanced through blending, remolding, and modification with other polymers to meet specific application requirements. For instance, Kim et al. developed a biostabilized PDMS-based polyurethane urea incorporating amphoteric sulfobetaine (PDMS-SB-UU), demonstrating its efficacy as a coating material for polymeric blood-contact surfaces in medical devices. Zhou et al. advanced the field through the development of environmentally friendly waterborne polyurethane nanofiber membranes with enhanced water resistance and impact resistance. The incorporation of hydrophobic small molecules, latex electrophoresis, in situ doping spinning technology, and aziridine cross-linking agents, coupled with heat treatment, resulted in membranes exhibiting superior water and blood intrusion resistance. These advancements offer promising prospects for developing functional textiles in healthcare applications, particularly in harsh environmental conditions.

Preparation of MOF-PU Composites

Metal–organic framework-polyurethane (MOF-PU) composites are synthesized through diverse methodologies, including solvothermal, hydrothermal, sonochemical, and in situ synthesis techniques (Figure ). Each method offers distinct advantages in terms of structural control, material properties, and scalability.

The solvothermal method is a closed-system technique that employs organic or nonaqueous solvents under controlled temperatures and pressures. Although this method is straightforward and offers precise control over reaction conditions, it is associated with several drawbacks, including high temperatures, high pressures, and prolonged reaction times. Su et al. demonstrated the application of this method in the preparation of Ni–Co-MOF-CNTs/PVA/CP (CCP) composites. They first fabricated CNTs/PU (CP) films and then deposited CNTs/PVA onto these films. Next, aminated Ni–Co-MOF nanosheets were synthesized via solvothermal methods, functionalized with streptavidin, and subsequently modified onto the CCP films. This approach highlights the versatility of the solvothermal method in creating advanced composite materials with tailored functionalities.

Hydrothermal synthesis involves chemical reactions in aqueous solutions under high temperatures and pressures. This method is widely recognized for its advantages, including high reactant activity, precise control over solution or interfacial reactions, the formation of metastable and unique condensed phases, reduced air pollution, and low energy consumption. Several studies have demonstrated the versatility of hydrothermal synthesis in material development. For instance, Zhao et al. employed hydrothermal in situ synthesis to prepare Zr-MOFs/PU and PDA/Zr-MOFs/PU foams, which exhibited significantly higher dye adsorption capacities compared to pure PU and PDA/PU. In another study, Vornholt et al. developed CPO-27-Ni-PU films, highlighting the excellent compatibility between MOFs and PUs. They also identified key factors, such as interfacial interactions and structural stability, that enhance the functionality of these materials. Furthermore, Wang et al. synthesized ZnO@MOF@PZS through hydrothermal synthetic polycondensation, resulting in materials with enhanced mechanical properties. These studies collectively underscore the potential of hydrothermal synthesis in designing advanced functional materials for various applications.

The sonochemical method involves applying high-energy ultrasound (20 kHz to 10 MHz) to irradiate materials, which enhances their crystallinity and growth rate, resulting in energy-efficient products. This technique avoids the need for high temperatures, high pressures, and long reaction times. However, the instability of the acoustochemical effect and the immaturity of ultrasound-assisted technology in chemical production pose significant technical and equipment challenges that require further research. − For instance, Borse et al. utilized ultrasonication to enhance environmentally friendly waterborne polyurethanes (WPUs) by incorporating modified nickel-doped metal–organic frameworks (Ni-ZIF-67). They successfully synthesized Ni-ZIF-67@WPU nanocomposites with excellent crystallinity and uniform distribution, which demonstrated superior mechanical properties and thermal stability compared to pristine WPUs. Additionally, they developed ZIF-8@WPU nanocomposites modified with the highly porous metal–organic framework ZIF-8, which exhibited enhanced thermal stability and water vapor permeability compared to pure WPU. In another study, Majidi et al. employed ultrasonication to prepare PU/GO-ZIF-7 nanocomposite coatings. These coatings displayed improved toughness, strain, weathering resistance, UV resistance, and physicomechanical properties, making them suitable for advanced transparent coating applications.

In situ synthesis involves chemical reactions between different elements or compounds under specific conditions, generating one or more ceramic phase particles within a metal matrix. This process enhances the properties of the metal alloy. The method is widely used for synthesizing composites due to its advantages, including low cost, fine and uniform distribution of product particles, no surface contamination of reinforcing phases, and adjustable surface-to-volume ratios of the reinforcing phases. However, it also has drawbacks, such as difficulty in controlling the reaction, low product density, and limited types of reinforcing phases. , Several studies have demonstrated the application of in situ synthesis in material development. For example, Sandra et al. used this method to create UiO-66-phase crystals on polyurethane foam, resulting in MOF@PUF composites. The successful preparation was confirmed through structural and morphological analyses. Li et al. employed in situ hydrothermal synthesis to produce Zr-MOFs-PUF membranes on porous PU foam surfaces. These membranes demonstrated high dye removal efficiency, low cost, and environmental friendliness, making them suitable for wastewater treatment. Additionally, Mu et al. synthesized MoS2@ZIF-8@WPU membranes via in situ synthesis, which exhibited excellent bactericidal and lubrication properties, as well as good air permeability compared to pure WPU. These studies highlight the versatility of in situ synthesis in developing advanced functional materials for diverse applications.

Application of MOF-PU Composites

Metal organic frameworks (MOFs) have shown broad application prospects in gas adsorption, catalysis, sensing, and drug delivery due to their highly adjustable pore structure, large specific surface area, and diverse functional properties. However, the mechanical strength, chemical stability, and functional integration of traditional MOFs still face challenges, limiting their practical applications in complex environments. In recent years, by combining polymer (PU) with MOFs PU@MOFs Hybrid materials provide new ideas for solving these problems. The introduction of PU not only enhances the mechanical toughness and environmental stability of MOFs, but also endows the material with dynamic responsiveness, selective permeation, or multifunctional integration through synergistic effects at the molecular level. For example, the flexible segments of PU can effectively alleviate the brittleness problem of MOFs, while its rich functional groups provide additional active sites or interface interactions for MOFs, further expanding the design dimensions of materials. At present, PU@MOFs composite materials have demonstrated unique advantages in fields such as flexible electronics, intelligent coatings, efficient separation membranes, and biomedical research, becoming a hot topic in interdisciplinary research.

Application of MOF-PU Composites as Drug Carriers in the Pharmaceutical Field

Pharmaceutical drug delivery systems, such as ophthalmic drops, oral administration, intravenous injections, and localized treatments, often fail to precisely target drugs or accurately control dosages. Metal–organic framework (MOF) nanoparticles have emerged as promising candidates for drug transport systems due to their large surface area and highly tunable properties. − However, their rapid metabolism in vivo limits their ability to ensure prolonged blood circulation, enhanced cellular uptake, controlled cargo release, and selective tumor targeting after systemic administration. Polyurethanes (PUs) are widely utilized in biomedical applications owing to their diverse compositions, excellent mechanical properties, and good biocompatibility. − Specifically, PU nanomaterials serve as localized drug release systems with high drug loading capacity, reducing the frequency of anticancer drug administration over the long-term. − Despite these advantages, single PU nanomaterials face challenges, including initial rapid drug release and susceptibility to bacterial and fungal colonization, which limit their effectiveness. To overcome these limitations, integrating MOFs with organic polymers such as PU, chitosan, and PEG has been explored. This combination enhances stability and biocompatibility, significantly improving the overall performance of drug delivery systems. Such advancements highlight the potential of hybrid materials in addressing the challenges of modern pharmaceutical delivery.

Application of UiO-67-PU Composite Material as Drug Carrier in Pharmaceutical Field

Traditional eye medications, typically administered as eye drops, often suffer from low drug utilization and limited effectiveness in treating eye diseases. Nano drug delivery systems offer a promising alternative by enhancing bioavailability and enabling effective drug penetration into deeper tissues, potentially revolutionizing conventional drug delivery methods. UiO-67, a potential nanocarrier for intraocular delivery of brimonidine tartrate in chronic glaucoma treatment, exhibits excellent properties due to its large tetrahedral and octahedral cages within microporous or mesoporous networks. , When combined with biodegradable polymers that enhance residence time and mucosal adhesion, UiO-67 could enable advanced drug delivery systems with improved performance. For instance, Gandara et al. successfully synthesized a functional MOF-based PU film by linking UiO-67 with PU. This film demonstrated superior adsorption and release properties for ocular drugs such as brimonidine tartrate (Figure a). UiO-67@PU exhibited a 2-fold increase in adsorption capacity compared to other MOFs and was more stable in aqueous environments, showing a 60-fold enhancement in drug adsorption compared to pure PU membranes. The significant loading capacity was accompanied by irreversible structural amorphization in aqueous and physiological media, promoting prolonged release kinetics over 12 days (Figure b). Synchrotron XRPD measurements confirmed the presence of brimonidine in UiO-67@PU membranes before and after drug loading (Figure c). These findings highlight the potential of combining MOFs with PU to improve drug stability and loading capacity (Figure d), offering a promising approach for advanced ocular drug delivery.

Application of UiO-66-PU Composites as Drug Carriers in the Field of Medicine

UiO-66 is an ideal candidate for delivering anticancer drugs like doxorubicin (DOX) and paclitaxel due to its high stability in aqueous and blood environments, as well as its excellent biocompatibility. Combining UiO-66 with polymers such as polyurethanes (PUs) enhances drug release control, protection, and targeting capabilities. For instance, Farboudi et al. successfully incorporated DOX and folic acid (FA) into UiO-66 (Figure a) and loaded the composite into carboxymethyl chitosan/poly­(ethylene oxide) (PEO)/PU core–shell nanofibers for controlled release, resulting in the death of MCF-7 cancer cells. The nanofibers exhibited a stable structure between the drug/MOF and the polymer, as confirmed by microscopic analysis (Figure b,c). The system demonstrated high long-term stability, with a drug loading efficiency exceeding 95% and a higher apoptosis rate compared to UiO-66 loaded only with DOX, highlighting its potential for cancer treatment. In another study, Zhang et al. synthesized FA/PU/MOF composites by modifying UiO-66 with FA and using PU as nanocarriers. This composite effectively delivered indocyanine green (ICG) and Sunitinib to cancer cells, achieving high drug loading efficiencies (ICG: 2.87% loading, 90.28% encapsulation; Sunitinib: 2.30% loading, 72.67% encapsulation) and a high singlet oxygen generation rate. Kim et al. developed PU composite membranes containing UiO-66 for removing the organophosphorus nerve agent simulant methyl paraoxon (MPO). By optimizing the polyol type, they created a UiO-66/PU composite film that maintained efficient MPO decomposition through 10 cycles (Figure d), demonstrating superior stability in an N-ethylmorpholine solution compared to a UiO-66 slurry. Additionally, Zhang et al. modified PU foam with polydopamine (PDA) to create a multifunctional carrier and immobilized acid–base bifunctional UiO-66-type MOF membranes on PU@PDA. By adjusting the ratio of organic ligands (2-amino terephthalate and 2-sulfo terephthalate), they developed a catalyst with acid–base bifunctional sites, high activity, and stability, suitable for catalytic drug release. Overall, combining UiO-66 with PU retains the high stability and biocompatibility of UiO-66 while enhancing its drug release control, protection, and targeting capabilities. This synergy underscores the potential of MOF-PU composites in advancing drug delivery systems for various therapeutic applications.

Other MOF-PU Composites are Used as Drug Carriers in the Pharmaceutical Field

Recent studies have explored advanced drug delivery systems using MOF-based nanocomposites for glioblastoma treatment. Bazzazzadeh et al. successfully incorporated the anticancer agents paclitaxel (PTX) and temozolomide (TMZ), along with magnetic MIL-53, into the core and shell layers of PA-g-CS/PU core–shell nanofibers. They investigated the effects of these nanofibers on U-87MG glioblastoma cell death through a combined chemo-thermal therapy approach (Figure a). The results showed that reduced pH enhanced material swelling, attributed to the ionization of amino and carboxyl groups in CS-g-PAA. Conversely, higher temperatures inhibited MIL-53 pore opening, thereby optimizing drug release efficiency (Figure b). These core–shell nanofibers demonstrate significant potential as composite materials for combined thermotherapy and chemotherapy in glioblastoma treatment. In a related study, Souza et al. demonstrated the effectiveness of synchrotron radiation microspectroscopy in tracking the in situ release of the anticancer drug 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) from a drug@MOF/polymer composite (5-FU@HKUST-1/PU) (Figure c). In the system, 5-FU coordinates specifically to HKUST-1 through its coordinatively unsaturated metal centers (CUS) and forms hydrogen bonds with the oxygen atoms in the carboxylate groups. Additionally, HKUST-1 generates hydrophilic channels within the hydrophobic polyurethane matrix, which helps regulate the drug release rate. The synergy between hydrophilic MOFs and hydrophobic polymers enables the design of tunable nanocomposites, mitigating burst effects common in drug delivery systems. However, HKUST-1, a water-sensitive MOF, degrades rapidly in the presence of moisture, , and in situ observation of chemical and physical changes during drug delivery remains challenging due to the accelerated nature of these processes. To address these limitations, Stefaniak et al. , constructed a drug@MOF system for analytical studies of drug release profiles using a stepwise approach. Aliquots were periodically collected from the release medium, and drug concentration was determined by UV–vis spectroscopy and liquid chromatography. An in situ strategy was proposed to monitor 5-FU release from HKUST-1/PU film composites. The interactions and energetics of the drug MOF@PU system were further investigated using synchrotron Fourier transform infrared microspectroscopy (SR-micro FTIR) , at the Diamond Light Source beamline B22, complemented by density-functional theory (DFT) calculations. These techniques revealed the chemical and physical mechanisms, as well as structural transformations, occurring during guest release. Collectively, these studies highlight the potential of MOF-based nanocomposites for controlled drug delivery, though challenges such as material stability and real-time monitoring remain to be addressed.

The Application of MOF-PU Microcapsules or Thermogels as Drug Carriers in the Pharmaceutical Field

The development of advanced drug delivery systems has been driven by the need to improve therapeutic efficacy and address the complexity of biochemical processes. Continuous drug administration has revolutionized disease treatment over the years. However, the complexity of biochemical processes indicates that therapeutic efficacy can be improved by targeting multiple biological pathways or leveraging synergistic effects. To address these challenges, MOF-PU composites have been utilized to form microcapsules for drug delivery systems. These microcapsules protect their contents from external factors, such as oxidation or denaturation, , while enabling rapid release under specific conditions or at targeted sites. This dual approach offers a versatile strategy for controlled drug release, significantly improving the efficacy and applicability of drug delivery systems. These advancements highlight the potential of MOF-PU composites in developing next-generation drug delivery systems with enhanced control and efficacy.

Recent advancements in MOF-based composites have enabled innovative drug delivery systems with enhanced functionality and controlled release capabilities. These advancements highlight the potential of nanopesticides in reducing the environmental and health impacts of traditional pesticide use while maintaining agricultural productivity. Application of MOF-PU Polymeric Material as Carrier in Medical Devices

MOF-based composites have shown significant potential in biomedical applications, particularly in nitric oxide (NO) generation and surface modification for medical devices. Melvin et al. synthesized a water-stabilized copper-azole composite MOF, CuBTTri, H3[(Cu4Cl)3(BTTri)8] (H3BTTri = 1,3,5-tris(1H–1,2,3-triazol-5-yl)­benzene)@PU. They investigated parameters such as MOF particle size, loading, and polymer concentration to optimize the composite’s properties. This composite exhibited promising catalytic activity for biomedical applications, − particularly in generating nitric oxide (NO), a key biomolecule for cardiovascular, immune, and neurological functions, via localized activation of endogenous S-nitrosothiols. , Variations in MOF particle preparation, loading, and PU concentration significantly affected NO generation in the MOF-PU composites (Figure a). Higher MOF loading and increased PU concentrations enhanced NO generation rates (Figure b), due to greater copper exposure on the MOF surface from milling, which reduced particle aggregation. In a related study, Zhang et al. developed a Cu-MOF coating with antithrombotic and anti-infective properties on thermoplastic polyurethane (TPU) surfaces using spin-coating. The polyurethane prepolymer coating enhanced Cu-MOF particle adhesion to the TPU surface and improved MOF stability. The coating can be easily loaded with a large amount of NO gas under high pressure, leveraging the ligand affinity of Cu2+ for NO and the NO-loading capacity of the amino group. This continuous NO release ensures stable delivery from the implanted device over an extended period, with a release cycle of up to 30 days. The coating exhibits excellent stability, anticoagulant, antimicrobial, and biocompatible properties. It holds great potential for medical catheter surfaces, addressing issues like poor anticoagulation and insufficient antimicrobial activity through surface modification. These studies demonstrate the versatility of MOF-based composites in addressing critical biomedical challenges, offering innovative solutions for NO delivery and medical device surface modification.

Summarizing and Looking Forward

The field of biomedicine has increasingly turned to nanotechnology and MOF-based composites to address the limitations of traditional therapeutic drugs, such as low bioavailability, severe adverse effects, and rapid elimination from the body. Nanotechnology utilizes nanoparticles (NPs) as delivery vehicles for therapeutic payloads, enabling targeted and regulated transport within body tissues. Due to their small size and high sensitivity, nanoparticles serve as valuable contrast agents and can be coupled with targeted ligands or functional agents, making them suitable for controlled drug release and bioimaging applications. , Various nanopolymers, including polymer nanoparticles, micelles, magnetic nanoparticles, liposomes, and dendritic polymers, have been successfully fabricated. These nanopolymer carriers have been applied in nanomedicine for gene delivery, magnetic resonance imaging reagents and site-specific drug delivery systems. Among these, MOFs have garnered significant attention due to their large specific surface area, tunable pore structure, porosity, biocompatibility, flexibility, and high drug-loading capacity. However, some MOFs face challenges like poor stability and low conductivity, limiting their practical applications. Combining MOFs with polymers that exhibit excellent biocompatibility , and biodegradability − offers a promising solution to these limitations. These advancements highlight the potential of MOF-polymer composites in overcoming current limitations, though further research is needed to address challenges in commercialization and large-scale production.

The complexity of the organism necessitates a comprehensive understanding of the introduced materials. Currently, there is limited information available regarding the mechanisms of various pathways of MOF-PU composites within the organism. Additionally, the precise pharmacokinetics of these composites remain undetermined. These limitations significantly restrict the application of MOF-PU composites as carriers in drug delivery systems.

MOF materials are typically produced as powders, which poses significant challenges for their application in large-scale production. These challenges include limited mechanical strength, insufficient chemical and abrasion resistance, mass transfer limitations, and poor handling properties. As a result, the systematic synthesis of MOF-PU composites has become a critical area of research to address these limitations.

MOF-PU composites are typically synthesized under high temperatures and pressures, and these complex conditions significantly limit their widespread practical applications. To address this, developing synthesis strategies with mild conditions, as well as green and low-cost approaches, has become a critical research focus. Furthermore, the effect of the reaction environment on the surface properties of the composites requires further investigation to optimize their performance.

The development of MOF-PU matrix composites is still in its early stages. A comprehensive understanding of the relationship between their preparation, structure, and application is essential for establishing criteria to select appropriate methods for creating functionally oriented materials. Despite the challenges in developing MOF-PU matrix composites, the incorporation of polyurethane (PU) has significantly expanded the applications of MOFs, particularly as drug carriers in the pharmaceutical field and beyond. This review focuses on the preparation methods, advantages, and drug carrier applications of MOF-PU composites. These composites are designed to optimize key properties for biopharmaceutical applications, including drug delivery mode, release rate, and utilization rate. The development of MOF-PU-based composites holds immense potential for advancing biomedical technology, especially in drug delivery systems, and could lead to transformative breakthroughs that enhance human life.

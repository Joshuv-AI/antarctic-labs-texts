Cognitive artificial intelligence for automated reservoir analysis and prediction of porosity, permeability, and fluid saturation
Authors: Fossong Guilianno, Kingsley Onyekwere Okengwu, Ugochi Adaku Okengwu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study develops a cognitive computing framework for reservoir characterisation and exploration optimisation in the Gabo Field, Niger Delta, with a focus on enhancing predictive accuracy for porosity, permeability, and fluid saturation. This research work aims to overcome the limitations related to geological complexity, shortage of available data, and complexity-related heterogeneity in the deltaic depositional environments. This work proposes a workflow that combines physics-based petrophysical calculations, Random Forest regression (RF), and Deepseek-R1 cognitive perception to improve predictive abilities, estimate uncertainties, and provide actionable intelligence related to reservoir management. Exploratory data analysis involved Akima spline interpolation and Isolation Forest algorithms. The RF model achieved superior predictive performance, with R² values above 0.98 for all predicted properties, and RMSE values below accepted thresholds. Predicted porosity values ranged between 0.18 and 0.25, clustering at 0.22–0.24, while permeability extended up to ~ 5230 mD, with several zones exceeding 500 mD, highlighting strong flow potential. Water saturation ranged between 0.25 and 0.45, suggesting favourable hydrocarbon saturation. Uncertainty quantification revealed low prediction errors (0.0062 v/v for porosity, 0.0040 log(mD) for permeability, and 0.0106 for saturation), confirming robustness and reliability. Deepseek-R1 cognitive evaluation identified potential bypassed pay zones and provided recommendations for enhanced recovery, including infill drilling and targeted waterflooding in high-permeability intervals. The integration of physics-based calculations, advanced machine learning, and cognitive computing demonstrates significant improvements in reservoir characterisation in geologically complex settings. This study delivers not only high predictive accuracy but also expert-level recommendations for exploration and field development. The proposed workflow contributes to digital intelligence in oil and gas exploration.

Introduction

Hydrocarbon reservoir characterisation remains one of the most critical challenges in petroleum exploration and production, particularly in geologically complex regions such as the Niger Delta1. Estimation and prediction of the most essential petrophysical properties (porosity, permeability, and fluid saturation) are worthwhile for effective reservoir management and hydrocarbon recovery optimisation2.

Traditional reservoir characterisation methodologies mostly rely on empirical correlations and models (permeability-porosity transformation, the Simandoux equation, etc.), which sometimes insufficiently capture the heterogeneity and complexity inherent within deltaic depositional systems3. In the past decades, the integration of machine learning (ML) and artificial intelligence (AI) methodologies has undergone a tremendous rise within the discipline of reservoir characterisation, allowing enhanced accuracy and expedience within the prediction of petrophysical parameters. These advanced computer-based methodologies are capable of consolidating disparate datasets, recognising sophisticated patterns, and providing decision support through probabilistic results that complement risk-informed reservoir management practice4,5. This research project aims to devise and implement a cognitive computing paradigm to characterise the Gabo Field reservoirs in the Niger Delta. Cognitive computing here refers to the use of a reasoning model to interpret quantitative data and provide expert recommendations for field development. The research incorporates Random Forest (RF) algorithms, Deepseek-R1, and log data to automate the prediction and analysis of porosity, permeability, and fluid saturation . By integrating conventional petrophysical measurements with ML methodologies and cognitive computing, this research hopes to enhance the accuracy and reliability of reservoir property predictions while presenting expert-grade intelligence and insights to support effective reservoir management practice as well as production optimisation.

The objectives of this research are to:

i.Design an intelligent system to automatically forecast and analyse porosity, permeability, and fluid saturation using Random Forest (RF), Deepseek-R1, and well log.ii.Assess the performance and precision of the models based on comprehensive statistical analysis and uncertainty quantification.iii.Analyse the geologic determinants affecting the reservoir properties through feature importance analysis as well as lithological classification.iv.Showcase the application of cognitive computing to provide expert-level observations to assist reservoir management and maximise production.

Literature review

Machine learning methods now support many reservoir characterisation tasks. Studies report improved prediction of porosity, permeability, and water saturation from well logs and seismic attributes6–9,4,5,10,11. Review papers map rapid growth of artificial intelligence in petroleum workflows, though many focus on algorithm counts rather than geological controls or data limits12,13. Several studies report high predictive accuracy but provide limited detail on dataset size, depth intervals, and validation strategy. Srivardhan14 achieved R2 values of 0.97 for porosity and 0.96 for water saturation in Browse Basin reservoirs. The study did not state sample count or blind well testing. Mehrabi,10 tested neural network structures in carbonate reservoirs and reported R2 values from 0.62 to 0.98 across lithologies. Model tuning procedures appear, yet cross-field transfer was not evaluated. Similar gaps appear in multi-model comparisons where nine algorithms predict water saturation without detailed error analysis across facies groups15.

Earlier work applied Support Vector Regression to water saturation prediction and reported improvement over empirical equations (Al Anazi & Gates16). The study relied on limited log suites and did not assess performance in strongly heterogeneous intervals. Later neural network models predicted porosity, permeability, and saturation simultaneously from well logs, though training and testing wells often came from the same field, which inflates reported accuracy17–19.

Geological settings strongly control model reliability. Deltaic successions such as the Agbada-Akata system contain rapid facies changes and variable shale content20. Studies in Niger Delta settings show that machine learning performance varies with depositional environment and log quality. Random Forest and related ensembles classified rock facies with clear separation in selected wells, though authors did not test transfer to structurally distant fields21,22. Permeability and pore pressure prediction from logs in the Niger Delta showed a good match to core data in trained intervals, yet validation used nearby depths with similar lithology23. Water saturation prediction in the TM Field relied on tree-based ensembles and achieved strong fits, though uncertainty bounds were not reported24. Several works combine seismic attributes and logs for lithology and porosity prediction. Ejaita and Omonefe,25 integrated multi-attribute seismic data with machine learning regression in the Akaso Field. The study did not report blind well validation, which raises overfitting risk when many correlated attributes enter the model. Khan,26 transformed seismic attributes before probabilistic neural network training in fluvial deltaic reservoirs, yet sensitivity to noise and missing logs was not quantified.

Recent research addresses data scarcity and uncertainty. Deep learning based seismic lithofacies prediction with geology-informed pseudo-well construction and transfer learning improved facies mapping in areas with sparse wells27. This approach injects stratigraphic rules into training data, which reduces unrealistic facies transitions. Porosity prediction with non-deterministic XGBoost produced prediction intervals alongside mean estimates in tight sandstone reservoirs28. Reported uncertainty ranges captured a high percentage of measured porosity values, which supports risk-aware decision-making.

Real-time and multi-source learning expands reservoir monitoring. Models predict rock strength and lithology while drilling using surface and downhole data streams, though performance drops when lithology shifts beyond the training range29–31. Ensemble learning with feature ranking improves permeability prediction from petrophysical logs, yet most studies rely on retrospective datasets rather than real-time deployment32. Hybrid evolutionary and group method of data handling models enhance porosity estimation, though computational cost limits field-scale use33. Boosted algorithms also show improved porosity prediction in sandstone gas reservoirs, with performance tied to careful feature selection34.

Artificial intelligence has recently gained traction in reservoir simulation and decision support. Data-driven proxies accelerate multiphase flow modelling, though many studies use synthetic models rather than full field history matches35. Large language model-driven systems support automated report analysis and operational decision support, yet validation against expert interpretations remains limited36. Broader energy geoscience reviews highlight integration of data analytics with subsurface workflows, though they stress the need for domain constraints and quality control37. Sustainability and field management studies link data-driven modelling with resource efficiency, though quantitative links between prediction accuracy and recovery factor often remain indirect38. Machine learning also supports flow assurance and hydrate prediction in offshore pipelines, which shows the transfer of subsurface data science methods to production systems39.

By and large, published work shows strong predictive skill under controlled data conditions. Many studies rely on single-field datasets, limited blind testing, and minimal uncertainty reporting. Few integrate geological rules, classical petrophysical equations, machine learning ensembles, and uncertainty quantification in one workflow for complex deltaic systems. This study addresses these gaps by combining Random Forest models, deep learning assisted interpretation, cognitive computing tools, and Archie-based petrophysical constraints for reservoir characterisation in a heterogeneous Niger Delta field.

Exploratory data analysis (EDA)

The EDA step in this study aimed at knowing the nature and quality of the well log data from the Gabo Field. The data consisted of relevant petrophysical logs, such as gamma ray (GR), density (RHOB), neutron porosity (NPHI), resistivity (RT), and spontaneous potential (SP), and the borehole depth values. Preprocessing of initial data included cleaning up of column names, transforming numeric data into the right format, and filling in missing data. The negative values were censored and interpolated with an Akima spline technique, which does not overshoot local gradients. This method was selected because it can deal with sharp variations in log information that often occur in heterogeneous reservoir settings. Materials and methods

Materials

Well log data, computational resources (Computer, Random Forest algorithm, Deepseek-R1).

Method

We applied a combination of well log data, physics-based techniques and machine learning to evaluate reservoir properties. A comprehensive workflow was designed for reservoir characterisation in the Gabo field, combining traditional petrophysical calculations (Archie’s law, density–neutron crossover, shale volume estimation) with advanced machine learning (Random Forest regression) and integrative visualisation approaches. Well log data were preprocessed by cleaning, interpolation, and clipping to industry-standard ranges, followed by computation of density-derived and neutron-derived porosities and interpretation of gas-bearing zones via density–neutron crossover analysis. Lithology was classified using gamma ray–based shale volume model, and reservoir zones were delineated based on porosity, volume of shale, and resistivity thresholds. A Random Forest model was trained to predict porosity, permeability, and water saturation, with hyperparameter tuning via grid search and evaluation by R² and RMSE metrics. Finally, custom multi-track well log plots were generated with depth-graduation and horizontal gridlines, and insights were enriched through integration with a local “Deepseek-r1” AI engine via Ollama through an automated process.

The Akima interpolation method

Local slope calculation: The slope at each data point was determined using four neighbouring points (two on each side). This local approach avoids the need for global computations, enhancing efficiency. If insufficient points exist (e.g., near boundaries), slopes are extrapolated using available data.

Weighted average of slopes: Slopes mi ​at point xi​ are computed as a weighted average of adjacent secant slopes (Si−1​, Si​).

Shape preservation: Resists oscillations between points, making it ideal for data with sharp changes.

Petrophysical calculations

Density porosity (φ_density) was computed from bulk density logs, assuming a quartz matrix (2.65 g/cc) and a water-filled porosity matrix (1.0 g/cc). Neutron porosity (φ_neutron) was derived by scaling the raw NPHI measurement by 0.95, correcting for tool inefficiencies.

Combined porosity was calculated by weighted averaging: if Δφ exceeded a gas threshold, porosity = 0.3·φ_neutron + 0.7·φ_density; otherwise, the arithmetic mean of both. Formations were classified into six lithofacies based on Vsh, GR, and RHOB: sandstone, shaly-sand, sandy-shale, shale, limestone, and dolomite. Non-hydrocarbon lithologies had So and Sg forced to zero to avoid spurious saturation values. Reservoir intervals were delineated where porosity ≥ 10%, Vsh < 35%, and resistivity > 20 Ω·m for a minimum thickness of 3 m. These criteria ensure economically viable porosity, low shale content, and sufficient hydrocarbon saturation. Consecutive depth points satisfying the criteria mark individual zones, with average properties (porosity, Sw, So, Sg) tabulated for each zone.

Descriptive statistics, outlier removal and pairplots

Descriptive statistics were displayed for key columns such as gamma ray, density, neutron, resistivity, SP, porosity, permeability, and water saturation. An IsolationForest model was used to detect and remove outliers from the dataset. Analysts manually reviewed outliers to ensure the removal of tool artifacts while preserving geological heterogeneity. IsolationForest is an unsupervised learning algorithm that isolates anomalies by randomly selecting a feature and then randomly selecting a split value between the maximum and minimum values of the selected feature. A pairplot (using Seaborn) was generated to visually inspect the collinearity among the features and targets.

Data splitting and model training

The cleaned dataset was split into features (inputs) and targets (outputs). The features include logs such as GR, NPHI, RHOB, RT, and SP. The targets are the computed petrophysical properties: porosity, permeability, and fluid saturation. The data comprising logs from eighteen (18) wells was then split randomly into training (60%), test (20%), and validation (20%) sets, using a fixed random seed of 42 to ensure consistency. SimpleImputer fills any remaining missing values, and StandardScaler standardises and normalises the features for machine learning analyses. RandomForestRegressor (a non-parametric ensemble method) was used for multi-output regression. A log transform was applied to permeability to reduce heteroscedasticity (a log₁₀ transformation applied to stabilise variance). GridSearchCV was employed to optimise hyperparameters (number of trees, maximum depth, and minimum samples split) using 5-fold cross-validation with R² score as the metric. The best model was selected based on the highest cross-validated R² score. Uncertainty quantification

Predictions were obtained from each tree in the Random Forest ensemble. The mean prediction and standard deviation (uncertainty) were computed over all trees. These uncertainties were then reported and plotted as error bars in the time series plots.

Cognitive analysis via deepseek-R1 (Ollama Integration)

Post-analysis, results were summarised and passed to a local Deepseek-R1 model via the Ollama command line interface (CLI). A cognitive prompt combining statistical summaries, analysis results, and model metrics is used by the model to provide expert-level recommendations and optimal Enhanced Oil Recovery (EOR) strategies for each well. The workflow integrates the Deepseek-r1 version 1.5 model using a temperature setting of 0.6 and a top p of 0.9 (Fig. 1). This integration illustrates the potential for AI‐augmented decision support in reservoir engineering workflows. The insights from the model were printed as part of the output.

Interpretation

Model performance

The Random Forest models demonstrated exceptional predictive capability across all three petrophysical properties. The high R² values (> 0.98) indicate that the models explain over 98% of the variance in porosity, permeability, and water saturation data. The consistently low RMSE values confirm the models’ ability to minimise prediction errors effectively.

The superior performance of Random Forest models over other regression models reflects the robustness of the ensemble mechanism in handling complex, non-linear relationships within the heterogeneous Gabo Field reservoir data. The Random Forest’s non-parametric nature allows it to adapt effectively to diverse datasets without assuming specific functional relationships between inputs and outputs.

Reservoir property

The predicted porosity range of 0.18–0.25 indicates good reservoir quality within the Gabo Field, with sufficient pore space for hydrocarbon storage. The clustering around 0.22–0.24 suggests relatively uniform porosity distribution in the main reservoir units.

Permeability values ranging from 0.14 to 5230 mD, with many zones exceeding 500 mD, indicate excellent reservoir quality and efficient hydrocarbon flow potential. The high maximum permeability values suggest the presence of high-permeability “thief zones” that could significantly impact fluid flow patterns.

Water saturation values between 0.25 and 0.45 indicate moderate to low water content, suggesting higher hydrocarbon saturation levels favourable for production. The lower water saturation values are particularly encouraging for commercial hydrocarbon recovery.

Uncertainty quantification

The low uncertainty values (porosity: 0.0062 v/v, permeability: 0.0040 log(mD), water saturation: 0.0106) indicate high confidence in model predictions. This consistency is crucial for operational decision-making in reservoir engineering applications.

The uncertainty values align with inherent noise levels in well log measurements, particularly in shale-rich intervals where neutron-density crossovers introduce measurement ambiguity.

Feature importance

The dominance of resistivity (RT_COMP.OHMM) in porosity prediction aligns with fundamental petrophysical principles, where resistivity measurements directly relate to formation porosity and fluid content. Similarly, bulk density’s (RHOB_COMP.G/C3) importance for water saturation prediction reflects its direct correlation with formation density and porosity.

The secondary importance of gamma ray (GR_COMP.GAPI) reflects its role in shale volume estimation, which is critical for porosity correction in argillaceous formations typical of Niger Delta reservoirs.

Lithological controls

The predominance of sandstone (42%) over shale (38%) indicates favourable reservoir conditions within the Gabo Field. Sandstone intervals showing porosity ranges of 18–50% and permeability of 50–300 mD represent the primary reservoir units.

Shale-rich intervals (> 35% volume) with suppressed permeability (< 5 mD) and elevated water saturation (> 70%) represent flow barriers and sealing units typical of Niger Delta stratigraphy. These intervals contain capillary-bound water in microporous clay minerals.

Reservoir heterogeneity

The heterogeneous distribution of porosity and permeability shows the occurrence of a complex architecture of reservoirs found in deltaic depositional settings. This heterogeneity implies that the reservoir system is compartmentalised and presents significant implications for the field development strategy. The approach of knowing areas of untapped hydrocarbon potential through the identification of potential bypassed oil zones denoted by the high resistivity and low water saturation signatures is critical in depicting areas that could be explored to further recovery efforts.

Discussion

Quantitative performance and geological consistency

The Random Forest model demonstrates exceptional predictive accuracy for petrophysical properties in the Gabo Field. The achieved R2 values exceed 0.98 for all targets, specifically reaching 0.9831 for porosity and 0.9820 for permeability on validation data. These results outperform previous benchmarks, such as the 0.97 R2 reported by Srivardhan14 and the 0.62–0.98 range in carbonate reservoirs reported by Mehrabi et al.10. The model exhibits a 12% improvement in water saturation predictability over Support Vector Regression techniques, effectively capturing the non-linear interactions inherent in deltaic datasets. Predicted porosity values (0.18–0.25 v/v) and permeability (up to 5,230 mD) align with established geological characteristics of high-quality Niger Delta reservoir units.

Uncertainty and risk-informed decision making

The integration of uncertainty quantification provides a probabilistic foundation for reservoir management. Average validation uncertainties remain low at 0.0062 v/v for porosity and 0.0106 for water saturation, confirming model robustness against measurement noise. These quantitative error margins are critical for reducing subsurface risk during field development. Permutation importance analysis highlights resistivity (RT_COMP.OHMM) and bulk density (RHOB_COMP.G/C3) as the primary drivers for porosity and saturation predictions, respectively. This alignment with petrophysical principles ensures that the model provides scientifically grounded actionable intelligence rather than simple statistical correlations.

Strategic reservoir management and EOR

The identification of high-permeability “thief zones” exceeding 500 mD directly informs well placement and completion strategies. These intervals are optimal candidates for horizontal drilling and hydraulic fracturing to maximize recovery. Furthermore, the workflow delineates bypassed pay zones through integrated resistivity and low water saturation analysis. These zones represent significant opportunities for enhanced oil recovery (EOR) via sidetrack drilling or targeted waterflooding. The identified net pay thickness of 3–23 m confirms the Gabo Field’s commercial potential and supports the feasibility of conventional development within complex deltaic geometries.

Innovation through cognitive computing

The deployment of Deepseek-R1 via the Ollama framework introduces a decision-support layer previously absent in deterministic workflows. By processing statistical summaries, the cognitive engine provides expert-level recommendations that bridge the gap between quantitative modeling and qualitative reservoir engineering expertise41. This approach aligns with industrial shifts toward digital intelligence and data-driven optimization. The successful integration of empirical petrophysics, machine learning, and cognitive AI establishes a scalable framework for rapid reservoir evaluation in similar geologically complex environments worldwide42, 43.

Conclusion

This study establishes a cognitive computing framework for reservoir characterisation in the Gabo Field, Niger Delta, by integrating Random Forest regression with the Deepseek-R1 reasoning engine. The developed system demonstrates exceptional predictive precision, achieving R2 values exceeding 0.98 for porosity, permeability, and water saturation. Quantitative findings reveal favourable reservoir conditions with porosity ranges of 0.18 to 0.25 v/v, water saturation between 0.25 and 0.45, and permeability values extending up to 5,230 mD. Low average uncertainties of 0.0062 v/v for porosity and 0.0106 for saturation confirm high model confidence and reliability for operational decision-making. The integration of Deepseek-R1 provides a novel decision support layer, translating statistical summaries into expert level recommendations for bypassed pay zone identification and enhanced oil recovery. Lithological analysis confirms a predominance of sandstone (42%) and shale (38%), which accurately reflects the typical stratigraphy of the Niger Delta. This innovative workflow offers significant improvements over conventional empirical relationships by effectively managing non-linear interactions and providing probabilistic outputs for risk based management.

The primary limitation of this research is the reliance on well log data alone without the integration of core analysis, which may restrict the spatial resolution of the characterisation. The uncertainty quantification approach is based on model ensemble variance rather than comprehensive uncertainty propagation through the entire analytical workflow. Furthermore, the lithological classification using only well log cutoffs potentially oversimplifies the complex depositional facies inherent in deltaic environments. Accurate facies interpretation would require the integration of biostratigraphic data and detailed sedimentological analysis.

Future studies should prioritize the integration of 3D seismic and core data with well log analysis to enhance spatial reservoir modelling. Developing physics-informed neural networks that incorporate fundamental petrophysical laws could improve prediction accuracy while ensuring geological consistency. Advanced geostatistical techniques for spatial interpolation between wells are necessary to improve field-scale capabilities. Finally, incorporating production data and pressure transient analysis will provide dynamic characterisation to complement these static property predictions.

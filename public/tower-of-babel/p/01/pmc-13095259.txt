Publication trends of dexmedetomidine and perioperative cognitive dysfunction: A bibliometric analysis from 1995 to 2024
Authors: Difei Zhou, Lan Yang, Shuai Miao, Xin Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Introduction:Dexmedetomidine (Dex) has shown potential in mitigating perioperative cognitive dysfunction (POCD). This study aimed to systematically evaluate the research hotspots and development trends in Dex and POCD using bibliometric analysis.Methods:A systematic search was conducted on the Web of Science Core Collection database to identify publications related to Dex and POCD from 1995 to 2024. Bibliometric analysis was performed using VOSviewer (V 1.6.20), CiteSpace (V 6.3.R1), and R V4.3.3.Results:A total of 585 articles on Dex and POCD between 1995 and 2024 were analyzed. These articles involved 1688 authors from 1857 institutions across 140 countries or regions, published in 240 journals, and citing 12,881 sources. The annual growth rate of publications in this field was 16.21%, with a peak of 91 articles in 2021. China led in the number of articles (n = 379), multiple country publications (MCP = 29), and total citations = 4989). The USA ranked second with 74 articles, 18 MCPs, and 2608 citations, followed by South Korea with 29 articles and 533 citations. Harvard University published the most articles (n = 51). Wang Dong-Xin was the most influential author, with an h-index of 7. Anesthesiology led the high-impact journals with an H-index of 11. The keyword analysis identified “neuroinflammation,” “risk,” “injury,” and “efficacy” as the latest research themes.Conclusion:This bibliometric analysis provides a comprehensive overview of the research trends regarding Dex and POCD. Future research should continue to explore the mechanisms, efficacy, and safety of Dex in preventing POCD.

1. Introduction

Perioperative cognitive dysfunction (POCD) is a significant medical issue that refers to the decline in cognitive abilities, such as memory, attention, and processing speed, experienced by patients after surgery.[1] This condition can range from mild, transient impairments to severe, long-lasting deficits that significantly impact patients’ quality of life and functional independence.[2] The exact pathogenesis of POCD remains unclear, but it is believed to be multifactorial, involving factors such as neuroinflammation, oxidative stress, and neurotransmitter imbalances.[3] The incidence of POCD varies widely depending on the patient population and surgical procedure, with estimates ranging from 10% to 50% in various studies.[4,5] This wide variability highlights the potential burden of the disease, which can lead to prolonged hospital stays, increased healthcare costs, and a diminished ability to return to presurgery levels of function.[6]

Among the various therapeutic strategies, dexmedetomidine (Dex), a selective α2-adrenoceptor agonist, has emerged as a promising candidate due to its sedative, analgesic, and anxiolytic properties.[7] Dex has been shown to provide a more natural sleep-like state compared to traditional sedatives, and it may also have neuroprotective effects that could mitigate the cognitive impairments associated with surgery.[8] However, despite its potential benefits, the use of Dex in the context of POCD is not without challenges. Its side effects, such as bradycardia and hypotension, require careful monitoring and management.[9,10] Therefore, a comprehensive understanding of the existing research on Dex and POCD is crucial to inform clinical practice and future research directions.

To gain insight into the current research trends and advancements in the field of Dex and POCD, a bibliometric analysis is essential. Bibliometric analysis is a quantitative research method. By analyzing publication trends, citation patterns, and research collaboration networks, bibliometrics can provide insights into the evolution of a research field, identify key researchers and institutions, and highlight future directions.[11] To date, several bibliometric studies have been conducted on both Dex and POCD individually.[12,13] However, there is a lack of studies that specifically focus on the intersection of these 2 topics. Therefore, there is a need for a dedicated bibliometric analysis that specifically addresses this research area. The present study aims to fill this gap by conducting a comprehensive bibliometric analysis of publications on Dex and POCD from 1995 to 2024. By doing so, we hope to identify key trends, highlight significant contributions, and provide guidance for future research efforts in this important field.

2. Methods

The literature search for Dex and POCD from 1995 to 2024 was conducted on the Web of Science Core Collection. The search formula was (TS = [Dex OR MPV 1440 OR Precedex OR Igalmi OR Sedadex OR Sileo OR Cepedex OR Dexdor OR Dexdomitor]) AND TS = (Postoperative Cognitive Complication OR Postoperative Cognitive Dysfunction OR POCD OR Postoperative Cognitive Decline OR Postoperative Dementia OR Postoperative Delirium OR Postoperative Cognitive Disorder OR Perioperative neurocognitive disorder). To ensure consistency during database updates, the literature search was conducted on September 25, 2024. For this research, only English-language articles were considered. Among the different types of documents, our study focused solely on articles, excluding review articles, proceeding papers, meeting abstracts, book chapters, early access materials, corrections, and retracted publications. The data gathered included publication and citation counts, titles, countries/regions, institutions, journals, author details, and keywords, all in textual format. This bibliometric review was conducted and reported in accordance with the Guideline for Reporting Bibliometric Reviews of the Biomedical Literature.[14]

2.1. Statistical analysis

Relevant data were collected from bibliographic records, and bibliometric indicators were subsequently computed using Microsoft Excel. A thorough academic analysis was facilitated by visualization tools such as VOSviewer (V1.6.20; Centre for Science and Technology Studies, Leiden University, Leiden, The Netherlands), CiteSpace (V6.3.R1; Drexel University, Philadelphia), and R (V4.3.3; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). VOSviewer was utilized to create maps depicting co-occurrences among countries, institutions, authors, and keywords. These maps illuminated key research themes and collaborations, with node sizes representing frequency and colors distinguishing research clusters or average publication years for keywords. Stronger co-occurrence relationships were denoted by thicker lines.[15] CiteSpace was employed to analyze the evolution of research trends on Dex and POCD from 1995 to 2024. It generated time-zone maps that traced historical advancements and emerging patterns, displaying clusters of research topics. The size of these clusters reflected their significance, while the color indicated the duration of research.[16] R V4.3.3 enabled advanced statistical analysis, allowing for the computation of bibliometric indices such as the H-index, G-index, and M-index for prominent authors and institutions. The H-index evaluates both productivity and citation impact, the G-index emphasizes citation range, and the M-index balances paper production and citations.[17] Authors’ H-indices were retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection database. To assess the influence of journals, metrics from Journal Citation Reports were used, including the 2023 impact factor (IF) and quartile ranking (Q1–Q4). IF reflects the average number of citations per article, while quartile ranking categorizes journals into 4 impact groups, with Q1 representing the highest impact group.

3. Results

3.1. Overview of publications

According to the search strategy, 585 English articles on Dex and POCD between 1995 and 2024 fulfilled the inclusion and exclusion criteria (Fig. 1). These publications involved 1688 authors from 1857 institutions across 140 countries or regions. They were published in 240 journals, referencing 12,881 sources. On average, each article had 6.54 coauthors and featured an international collaboration rate of 12.14%. Furthermore, the articles encompassed 1027 keywords, with an average citation count of 18.91 per publication (Fig. 2A). 4. Discussion

This bibliometric analysis unveils a comprehensive overview of the research trends surrounding Dex and POCD from 1995 to 2024. A total of 585 English articles, involving 1688 authors from 1857 institutions across 140 countries or regions, were analyzed. These findings offer valuable insights into evolution, collaboration patterns, and key research themes within this field.

From 2014 to 2021, a notable growth in articles has been observed, which can be attributed to the increasing awareness of POCD, advancements in Dex research, and the subsequent need for comprehensive studies. The dominance of China and the USA can be attributed to their substantial investment in medical research, particularly in anesthesia and perioperative care. Institutionally, Harvard University in the USA tops the list, highlighting its strong research infrastructure and dedication to Dex and POCD studies. Chinese institutions also show impressive output, indicating a growing research capacity in China. High-impact journals like Anesthesiology and PLOS One not only have high citation counts but also reflect the thematic convergence of research through their strong links with other journals. Their focus on anesthesia, critical care, and perioperative management is evident.

Literature with the highest citation count encapsulates the key technologies and perspectives within this field. The top 2 most cited articles both described the sedative effects of Dex in addressing postoperative delirium. Su et al conducted a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial. This trial found that prophylactic low-dose Dex significantly reduces the incidence of delirium in elderly patients aged over 65 years after noncardiac surgery, with no major safety concerns during the first 7 postoperative days.[18] Similarly, an open-label, randomized clinical investigation by Maldonado et al found that Dex sedation significantly reduced the incidence of postoperative delirium and care costs in patients undergoing cardiac-valve procedures compared to propofol and midazolam.[19] The article ranked third in citations, emphasizing risk stratification, risk reduction, and rescue treatment related to postoperative delirium, and proposes that effective perioperative interventions and risk assessment may be the most effective methods for managing this common and serious complication.[20] Collectively, the increasing focus on risk assessment and interventions underscores the evolving landscape of POCD research, guiding future studies and clinical practices in this critical area.

4.1. Research hotspots and trends

The emergence of specific keywords at different time points reveals a shift in research focus over the years. Notably, the years around 2017 have seen a growing interest in exploring the interactions between Dex and specific patient populations, mainly including mechanically ventilated patients. Additionally, studies exploring the combination of Dex and desflurane mainly investigated how these agents interact in terms of anesthesia depth, emergence time, and postoperative cognitive outcomes.[21,22] Understanding these interactions is crucial for optimizing anesthesia protocols to minimize cognitive dysfunction.

Shifting focus to 2018, the attention on sedation practices highlights the crucial role Dex plays in managing perioperative states, particularly in high-risk patient groups. Furthermore, accurate assessment of cognitive function is essential for diagnosing and managing POCD. Research into Dex often includes the use of confusion assessment methods, such as the confusion assessment method for the intensive care unit, to evaluate patients’ cognitive status both before and after surgery.[23]

From approximately 2019 to 2021, this period marks a significant advancement in the field, characterized by more rigorous study designs, such as meta-analyses and double-blind trials,[24,25] aimed at elucidating the comparative efficacy and safety of Dex versus other sedatives like propofol in preventing postoperative delirium.

More recently, around 2022, “reliability” and “reduces delirium” came into focus. This shift reflects the maturing of the research, with studies not only investigating the effectiveness of Dex but also assessing the reliability of various assessment tools and confirming its role in reducing delirium. The emergence of these keywords signals a deepening understanding of Dex’s benefits and the need for consistent and reliable methods to measure its impact on perioperative cognitive outcomes.

The burst keywords provide a deeper dive into the evolution of research hotspots within this field. From 2006 to 2013, the burst terms “reduces agitation,” “mechanically ventilated patients”, and “intensive care unit” indicated an early emphasis on Dex’s sedative and anxiolytic effects in critical care settings. This period saw a surge in interest in its potential to reduce agitation among ventilated patients, highlighting its utility in maintaining patient comfort and facilitating clinical care. The decade from 2011 to 2020 witnessed a burst in keywords related to “critically ill patients,” “elderly patients”, and “activation”, reflecting a broadening of research to include populations more vulnerable to POCD. This shift in focus underscored the need to tailor Dex’s use to specific patient groups, particularly those at higher risk for cognitive decline.

Starting from 2021, a new wave of burst terms emerged, marking a significant shift in research priorities. Neuroinflammation has been increasingly recognized as a pivotal mechanism underlying POCD. Its burst suggests a growing emphasis on understanding the potential neuroinflammatory mechanisms underlying POCD and the role of Dex in mitigating these risks. Neuroinflammation is primarily mediated by cytokines produced by glial cells, including microglia and astrocytes.[26] When these glial cells are excessively activated, they release a large amount of pro-inflammatory cytokines, leading to neuronal damage, synapse loss, and ultimately neuronal death.[27,28] Dex has been shown to reduce inflammation and apoptosis, thereby exerting neuroprotective effects. The neuroprotective mechanism of Dex against neuroinflammation may involve the PI3K/Akt/NF-κB signaling pathway. This pathway plays a central role in regulating cell survival, proliferation, and inflammation. Dex has been shown to upregulate p-Akt expression and downregulate NF-κB activity, thereby inhibiting the inflammatory response and promoting neuronal survival.[29,30] In the context of perioperative care, Dex may offer a promising therapeutic option for reducing the risk of POCD by targeting neuroinflammation. However, the risks associated with the use of Dex should not be overlooked. For example, Dex may interfere with cardiac conduction pathways, especially in patients with sinus bradycardia or atrioventricular block, potentially inducing severe bradycardia, severe atrioventricular block, and ultimately leading to cardiac arrest.[31] Furthermore, the use of Dex in perioperative settings may also interact with other medications, such as opioids and hypnotic-sedative drugs, leading to synergistic effects that further suppress the sympathetic nervous system. This can result in hemodynamic instability and increased risk of perioperative complications.[32] Therefore, further research is needed to fully elucidate the mechanisms underlying the neuroprotective effects of Dex and to optimize its use in clinical practice.

The evolution of these burst terms reflects a clear shift in research hotspots, from initial therapeutic applications in critical care settings to broader patient demographics, and more recently, to the neurobiological mechanisms underlying POCD. However, despite these advancements, several research bottlenecks remain. One significant challenge is the need for more robust clinical trials to definitively establish Dex efficacy in preventing POCD. Additionally, there is a lack of consensus on the optimal dosing strategies and patient populations for Dex administration in perioperative settings. These bottlenecks represent critical areas for future investigation.

4.2. Limitations

Despite the comprehensive scope and depth of this bibliometric analysis, several limitations must be acknowledged. Firstly, the study is confined to English-language publications, potentially excluding relevant research conducted in other languages, thereby limiting the global representativeness of the findings. This linguistic bias could result in an incomplete picture of the research landscape on Dex and POCD. Secondly, the analysis relies on bibliometric indicators such as citation counts and H-indices. These indicators may not fully capture the nuances of research contributions, such as innovative methodologies, practical applications, or the broader societal implications of the findings. Thirdly, citation practices can vary across disciplines and regions, potentially introducing biases in the evaluation of research impact.

5. Conclusion

This study evaluates the research trends, collaboration patterns, and key themes within the field of Dex and POCD. The research hotspots are centered around Dex’s applications in anesthesia and critical care, with a particular focus on perioperative cognitive outcomes. The frontiers involve exploring the underlying mechanisms, risk factors, and potential interventions for POCD and Dex. The important findings of this study can inform clinical practice and future research directions, contributing to improved patient outcomes and quality of care.

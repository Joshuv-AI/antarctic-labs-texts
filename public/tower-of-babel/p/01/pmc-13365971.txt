IFPHOR0086 Intersecting Harms in Brazil: Women’s Health Impacts of Domestic Violence and Institutional Gender-Based Violence in the Justice System
Authors: Vanessa Hacon
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractDomestic violence is a major public health issue in Brazil, which ranks among the countries with the highest femicide rates globally, with severe consequences for women’s health. An additional and insufficiently addressed layer of harm arises from institutional gender-based violence within the justice system. This analysis examines the cumulative health impacts of these intersecting forms of violence.Using a mixed-methods approach, the analysis draws on (1) approximately 300 cases accompanied by Mothers for Justice – Brazil (Mães na Luta), a civil society movement that receives requests for assistance nationwide, and (2) documentary analysis of press reports involving fatal outcomes affecting women and children in judicialized contexts. Cases derive from ongoing support and advocacy activities and are documented through survivor reports, legal records, and case monitoring materials accumulated over time. The analytical approach combines thematic content analysis with an interpretive framework grounded in social determinants of health and gender-based violence.Findings reveal recurring patterns of institutional violence, including dismissal of survivors’ accounts, role inversion between victim and perpetrator, tolerance of litigation abuse by aggressors, and custody reversals in favour of alleged abusers. These practices not only escalate and perpetuate domestic and family violence by legitimizing abusive actors and undermining protective measures, but also subject women and children to additional forms of institutional violence. Reported impacts include chronic stress, depression, anxiety, social isolation, suicidal ideation, suicide, and fatal outcomes involving court-mandated or unsupervised contact with aggressors. Institutional gender-based violence functions as a structural determinant of health, contributing to avoidable morbidity and mortality.

Mechanical power normalized to dynamic lung compliance as a predictor of weaning and prognosis in mechanically ventilated patients: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Yao Yan, Xiaojiao Gu, Huiling Xu, Yanli Wang, Hui Zheng, Wenxia Hu, Jinqiu Ding, Xiaomin Li, Yongpeng Xie
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPredicting weaning outcomes in mechanically ventilated patients remains challenging. While mechanical power (MP) reflects ventilatory load, its accuracy is limited by variability in lung compliance. This study evaluated the predictive value of MP normalized to dynamic lung compliance (Cdyn-MP) for weaning outcomes and prognosis.MethodsA retrospective observational study was conducted in two ICUs in Jiangsu, China. Patients receiving invasive ventilation ≥ 24 h and undergoing a first spontaneous breathing trial (SBT) via T-tube were included. Patients with accidental extubation, neuromuscular disorders, or without available medical records were excluded. Respiratory mechanics data prior to SBT were retrospectively extracted from medical records. Associations and predictive performance were analyzed using restricted cubic spline, Spearman correlation, and time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves.ResultsAmong enrolled patients, 67.7% were successfully weaned, while 32.3% failed. A 100-unit increase in Cdyn-MP was associated with a 32% increased risk of weaning failure (p < 0.001). Cdyn-MP positively correlated with adverse outcomes. Time-dependent ROC curves yielded AUC values of 0.913, 0.771, 0.733, and 0.702 for predicting weaning failure within ≤ 1, ≤3, ≤ 7, and > 7 days after initial SBT.ConclusionCdyn-MP was significantly associated with weaning outcomes and prognosis in mechanically ventilated patients. Introduction

Weaning from mechanical ventilation remains a significant clinical challenge. Prolonged weaning increases medical costs and the risk of ventilator-associated complications. In contrast, premature weaning increases the likelihood of weaning failure, leading to higher rates of reintubation and mortality [1, 2]. Studies have shown that up to 30% of patients who successfully pass spontaneous breathing trials (SBT) still experience weaning failure, necessitating reintubation within 48 h [3]. Thus, identifying novel indicators to complement traditional weaning assessment methods is important for improving weaning success.

Mechanical power (MP) is a comprehensive parameter in mechanical ventilation that integrates tidal volume, respiratory rate, peak airway pressure, and driving pressure, which collectively reflect the total power delivered by the ventilator [4, 5]. MP has been associated with functional lung volume, and its diagnostic value has been shown to improve when normalized to surrogate markers of lung size, such as well-inflated lung tissue assessed via computed tomography (CT), lung compliance, or predicted body weight [6–8]. MP normalized to dynamic lung compliance (Cdyn-MP) may provide a more precise representation of the pathophysiological status of the lungs during mechanical ventilation, reflecting the actual energy transferred from respiratory muscles to lung tissue [9].

This study builds on our previous retrospective analysis using the MIMIC database, which explored the role of Cdyn-MP in guiding weaning [10]. We further investigate these findings through a retrospective cohort study. MP, initially developed for assessing ventilator-induced lung injury, has been associated with weaning outcomes in several studies [9, 10]. However, these studies were largely limited to specific populations or different MP normalization approaches. The present study uniquely evaluates Cdyn-MP measured immediately prior to SBT in a general ICU population, aiming to determine its predictive performance and clinical applicability. The aim of this study is to evaluate the relationship between Cdyn-MP and weaning outcomes and prognosis in mechanically ventilated patients, to support individualized respiratory load assessment and more informed clinical decision-making.

Materials and methods

Study design

This investigation was a retrospective cohort study of consecutive ICU patients meeting eligibility criteria; exposures and covariates were abstracted prior to classification by subsequent weaning outcome. From January 2022 to March 2024, a retrospective observational cohort study was conducted in the intensive care units (ICUs) of the First People’s Hospital and the Second People’s Hospital of Lianyungang, Jiangsu Province, China. Inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) invasive mechanical ventilation for more than 24 h; (2) age between 18 and 75 years; (3) first weaning attempt; and (4) SBT performed using a T-tube. Exclusion criteria included accidental weaning, neuromuscular junction diseases (e.g., myasthenia gravis), incomplete clinical records, and pre-existing limitations on life-sustaining treatment.

The study involved no intervention beyond standard clinical practice. All patient data were de-identified and anonymized prior to analysis to ensure confidentiality. The study was reported following the STROBE guidelines [11].

Data collection

Baseline data for the study cohort, including demographic variables (age, sex, body mass index [BMI], and smoking history), Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) score at ICU admission, admission type, pre-SBT laboratory results, and respiratory mechanics parameters documented before initial SBT, were retrospectively collected from medical records. Respiratory mechanics parameters included tidal volume (VT), respiratory rate (RR), positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEP), plateau pressure (Pplat), peak inspiratory pressure (Ppeak), dynamic driving pressure (ΔPaw), dynamic lung compliance (Cdyn), and fraction of inspired oxygen (FiO2). Weaning outcomes and prognostic indicators, such as duration of invasive mechanical ventilation (IMV), ICU length of stay, and total duration of hospitalization, were also systematically extracted from the records.

Respiratory mechanics and data collection

All clinical and respiratory mechanics data were retrospectively extracted from electronic medical records. Medical records from patients who had received deep sedation with intravenous remifentanil and propofol prior to SBT were reviewed. The target sedation level was documented as a Richmond Agitation–Sedation Scale (RASS) score ranging from − 4 to − 5. All included patients had been ventilated using volume-controlled ventilation according to standard ICU protocols.

Pplat was measured using a 3-second inspiratory hold in the absence of spontaneous breathing effort, with values recorded as the mean of three consecutive measurements. Although plateau pressure measurement enables the calculation of static lung compliance (Cstat), we selected Cdyn as the reference parameter. Cdyn was derived directly from ventilator measurements during volume-controlled ventilation under deep sedation without spontaneous breathing, avoiding the need for inspiratory hold maneuvers that can interrupt ventilation. Cdyn reflects the combined elastic and resistive properties of the respiratory system under actual ventilatory conditions, making it more applicable for assessing the physiological load relevant to weaning. All ventilator parameters, including VT, RR, PEEP, Pplat, and Ppeak, were obtained directly from the ventilator display during a clinically stable phase immediately prior to the first SBT.

MP and Cdyn-MP were calculated using the simplified classical MP formula under volume-controlled ventilation [12]. The calculation formulae are as follows:

MP (J/min) = 0.098 × VT × RR × (Ppeak – ½ΔP).

where VT is tidal volume, RR is respiratory rate, 𝑃peak is peak inspiratory pressure, and ΔP is driving pressure calculated as Pplat – PEEP.

Cdyn-MP (J/min×cmH2O/ml×10⁻³) = MP/Cdyn.

where Cdyn is dynamic lung compliance, calculated as VT/Δ𝑃aw, with ΔPaw being the dynamic driving pressure (Ppeak - PEEP).

To minimize variability and bias, only patients undergoing weaning with the T-tube ventilation strategy were included [13]. Data extraction was performed by trained ICU personnel who had received standardized instruction in ventilator waveform interpretation and chart review. All extracted values were cross-checked by a senior ICU physician against the original ventilator waveforms and electronic health records, and a randomly selected 10% sample was independently reviewed by a second clinician to ensure accuracy and consistency.

Definitions and outcomes

Weaning readiness was defined as the clinical determination that a patient was stable enough to attempt transition from invasive mechanical ventilation to spontaneous breathing [14]. This included adequate respiratory mechanics (tidal volume, respiratory rate), sufficient oxygenation, hemodynamic stability, and absence of major contraindications such as severe metabolic acidosis or uncontrolled infection.

All patients underwent a SBT using a T-tube for 30–120 min, depending on tolerance. SBT failure was defined as the occurrence of any of the following: [14] (1) rapid shallow breathing index (RSBI) > 105; (2) respiratory rate < 8 or > 35 breaths/minute; (3) spontaneous tidal volume < 4 ml/kg; (4) heart rate > 140 beats/minute or a change > 20% accompanied by new-onset arrhythmia; and (5) arterial oxygen saturation < 90%. SBT success was defined as completion of the trial without meeting any of the above failure criteria.

Weaning success was defined as SBT success followed by extubation, with no requirement for reintubation or initiation of non-invasive ventilation (NIV) within 48 h after extubation. Weaning failure was defined as the need for reintubation or initiation of NIV within 48 h after extubation due to signs of respiratory insufficiency, including increased work of breathing, hypercapnia, hypoxemia, or hemodynamic instability [15].

Extubation criteria required SBT success and the ability to maintain adequate oxygenation and ventilation without mechanical support [14]. Reintubation criteria were based on clinical judgment according to the above failure definition. NIV after extubation was used for patients at high risk of post-extubation respiratory failure or for those who failed the initial SBT but were considered likely to recover with short-term support. These patients were reassessed for weaning readiness thereafter [14]. Following SBT failure, patients were returned to conventional ventilator settings, with individualized adjustments (e.g., increased pressure support or PEEP) according to respiratory mechanics, gas exchange, and overall clinical status.

Primary outcomes were weaning success and failure, whereas secondary outcomes included the duration of IMV, length of stay in the ICU, and overall duration of hospitalization. Patients were retrospectively categorized into the weaning success or failure group based on these outcomes as documented in electronic medical records. Time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed to stratify patients based on the interval between initial SBT and extubation. The intervals were defined as ≤ 1 day, ≤ 3 days (1–3 days), ≤ 7 days (3–7 days), and > 7 days, with detailed distribution shown in Supplementary Fig. S1.

Statistical analyses

The sample size was determined by the total number of eligible patients retrospectively enrolled during the study period. A formal sample size calculation was not performed, as the primary objective of this study was exploratory and aimed to reflect real-world clinical practice. Continuous variables were presented as medians with interquartile ranges (IQRs: Q1, Q3) due to non-normal distributions, which were assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Between-group comparisons for continuous variables were conducted using the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables were expressed as counts and percentages, and comparisons were made using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test when appropriate.

Logistic regression analysis was employed to identify risk factors associated with weaning outcomes. Binary associations are expressed as odds ratios (ORs) from multivariable logistic models. Because weaning failure is not a rare outcome, ORs may overestimate the risk ratio and should not be interpreted as RRs; we therefore avoid translating ORs to RRs and interpret effect sizes cautiously [16]. Multicollinearity within the model was assessed using the variance inflation factor (VIF), with a VIF ≥ 5 indicating multicollinearity. Variables with p < 0.05 in univariate analysis and no multicollinearity with Cdyn-MP were included in the multivariate regression model to determine independent predictors of weaning failure. Restricted cubic spline (RCS) regression with three knots placed at the 10th, 50th, and 90th percentiles was applied to explore potential non-linear associations between Cdyn-MP and the risk of weaning failure.

Subgroup analyses were performed to evaluate interactions between Cdyn-MP and weaning outcomes across different strata, including age, sex, BMI, smoking history, admission category, and SOFA score. Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient was used to assess the association between Cdyn-MP and mechanical ventilation-related outcomes, such as duration of invasive mechanical ventilation and ICU stay. ROC curve analysis was performed to assess the discriminative ability of Cdyn-MP for predicting weaning outcomes. Additionally, time-dependent ROC analysis was conducted using the “timeROC” package (version 0.4) [17] in R to evaluate the predictive accuracy of Cdyn-MP over time.

All statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.4.3; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) [18] and Free Statistics software (version 2.2; Beijing, China). A two-sided p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant (Fig.1).

Results

Baseline clinical characteristics

A total of 406 patients undergoing invasive mechanical ventilation were enrolled in this study. Detailed inclusion criteria are presented in Fig. 2. The median patient age was 67.7 years (IQR 55.5–77.9), with males comprising 55.4% (225/406) of the study population. The median Cdyn-MP was 334.4 J/min × cm H2O/ml × 10⁻³ (IQR 141.1–580.1). The frequency distribution across weaning outcomes is presented in Supplementary Fig. S2. Successful weaning was achieved in 67.7% (275/406) of patients, whereas 32.3% (131/406) patients experienced weaning failure (Table 1). Among the weaning failure group, 20.6% (27/131) required reintubation or non-invasive ventilation within 48 h despite initially passing the SBT. Patients in the weaning failure group demonstrated significantly higher SOFA scores at ICU admission, elevated WBC and SCr levels before the initial SBT, and poorer respiratory mechanics (except tidal volume) compared with the patients in the weaning success group. Predictive ability of Cdyn-MP for weaning outcome

Weaning failure was assessed in mechanically ventilated patients by ROC comparative analysis. The areas under the ROC curves (AUC) for Cdyn-MP, MP, Cdyn, and ΔPaw were 0.796 (95% CI 0.753–0.839), 0.774 (95% CI 0.729–0.820), 0.728 (95% CI 0.680–0.776), and 0.723 (95% CI 0.674–0.773), respectively (all p-values < 0.001) (Fig. 2B). These results indicated that Cdyn-MP demonstrated better predictive performance for weaning failure compared with other parameters.

The time-dependent ROC curve analysis showed that the AUCs for Cdyn-MP for predicting the risk of weaning failure among patients extubated within ≤ 1 day, ≤ 3 days, ≤ 7 days, and > 7 days after the initial SBT were 0.913 (95% CI 0.856–0.970), 0.771 (95% CI 0.645–0.896), 0.733 (95% CI 0.639–0.827), and 0.702 (95% CI 0.598–0.807), respectively (all p < 0.001) (Fig. 5 A and B). The clinical characteristics of each group are summarized in Supplementary Tables S1–S4. These findings suggest that Cdyn-MP has higher predictive accuracy for short-term weaning outcomes (i.e., within 1 day after the initial SBT), with predictive performance decreasing over longer intervals. These results imply that daily dynamic monitoring of Cdyn-MP prior to SBT may enhance the prediction of weaning outcomes in clinical practice.

Discussion

This study suggests an association between Cdyn-MP and weaning failure in critically ill patients receiving invasive mechanical ventilation. Cdyn-MP demonstrated superior discriminative ability compared with other commonly used respiratory mechanics parameters, particularly within the first 24 h following the initial SBT. These findings imply that Cdyn-MP may serve as a practical and objective marker to evaluate respiratory load prior to extubation, thereby facilitating individualized weaning strategies. Although Pplat measurement requires a brief end-inspiratory hold during passive ventilation, this is commonly performed in sedated, mechanically ventilated patients during routine assessments prior to the first SBT, without the need for additional sedation. The practicality of Cdyn-MP may vary across ICUs, and its integration into clinical practice will be facilitated by automated, breath-by-breath computation embedded in ventilators or ICU dashboards.

Mechanical ventilation remains a vital life-support intervention in the ICU. However, both delayed weaning and premature extubation are closely linked to poor outcomes [1]. Prior studies have indicated that weaning may account for approximately 40–50% of the total duration of mechanical ventilation [19]. Once the underlying cause of respiratory failure resolves, the accurate timing of weaning becomes a key clinical decision. Although weaning failure has multifactorial pathophysiology [20], an imbalance between respiratory load and respiratory muscle capacity is widely recognized as a leading contributor [21]. Therefore, comprehensive assessment of pulmonary function and ventilatory load prior to extubation is essential for optimizing patient outcomes in the ICU.

MP quantifies the total energy transferred by the ventilator to the respiratory system per unit time. It integrates multiple ventilatory parameters, including VT, inspiratory flow rate, RR, and PEEP, along with parameters such as Ppeak, Pplat, and ΔPaw [6]. MP reflects the mechanical load imposed on the respiratory system and serves as a surrogate for estimating the burden on respiratory muscles during the transition from mechanical ventilation to spontaneous breathing. In recent years, MP has increasingly guided clinical practice in ventilator weaning [5, 9].

Ventilable lung volume is closely related to the spatial distribution of energy within lung tissue, which in turn is influenced by factors such as lung size, heterogeneity, and vascular filling. These factors contribute to regional differences in pulmonary stress and strain [6]. As highlighted by Gattinoni et al. in 2023, normalizing MP to functional lung size improves its clinical relevance [4]. Normalization strategies for MP vary, including predicted body weight (PBW), Cstat, and Cdyn. In this study, we selected Cdyn—measured under controlled, passive conditions immediately prior to SBT—to approximate ventilatory load under dynamic flow conditions relevant to weaning, while acknowledging that Cstat more purely reflects elastic mechanics and PBW approximates lung size. The optimal denominator for weaning risk stratification remains uncertain, and prospective trials directly comparing PBW-MP, Cstat-MP, and Cdyn-MP in standardized weaning protocols are warranted. In this context, Cdyn-MP may provide an individualized, physiologically relevant index of respiratory load for risk assessment in critically ill patients.

Our findings, derived from a large ICU cohort undergoing standardized T-piece SBTs, highlight the potential of Cdyn-MP as a complementary physiological marker for individualized weaning assessment. Univariate logistic regression revealed no significant association between weaning outcomes and baseline variables such as age, sex, BMI, smoking history, admission type, or pre-SBT laboratory values (p > 0.05). In contrast, weaning outcomes were significantly associated with several respiratory mechanics parameters, including pre-SBT PEEP, Pplat, Ppeak, ΔPaw, and Cdyn (all p < 0.05, Table 1). These findings suggest that although patients may demonstrate clinical stabilization prior to SBT, residual impairments in pulmonary mechanics remain a critical determinant of weaning success.

Multivariate logistic regression analysis revealed that SOFA score at ICU admission, FiO2 before initial SBT, Cdyn-MP, and duration of mechanical ventilation were independently associated with weaning failure (see Fig. 3). The SOFA score, which reflects illness severity upon ICU admission, plays a critical role in determining the prognosis of mechanically ventilated patients. This is in line with previous studies [22]. In addition, FiO2 before SBT, which reflects oxygenation status and oxygen delivery capacity, serves as a key indicator for screening weaning, consistent with the findings of Yan Jia et al. [23] Cdyn represents the pressure required for changing lung volume during respiration, and is influenced by lung tissue elasticity and airway resistance. Thus, it is more suitable for assessing lung function during the weaning process [24].

Cdyn, as applied in this study, reflects the integrated elastic and resistive properties of the respiratory system and therefore cannot isolate the independent contributions of airway resistance and lung elastance. While these components were not separately quantified in our analysis, clinicians should recognize PEEP’s dual role in both alveolar recruitment and oxygenation, as well as its contribution to the overall mechanical power burden, when interpreting Cdyn-MP values in the context of weaning. Cdyn-MP reflects the MP exerted on unit ventilated lung volume. An increase in Cdyn-MP indicates that greater respiratory system resistance and energy load must be overcome to achieve target ventilation and oxygenation. An elevated Cdyn-MP indicates the presence of factors that may increase airway resistance or decrease respiratory system compliance, such as mucus retention, pulmonary edema, or chest wall restriction [20]. In this study, Cdyn was selected to normalize mechanical power because it captures functional compliance under dynamic conditions relevant to weaning. Nevertheless, Cdyn-MP should be interpreted alongside other clinical indices, and future studies should consider incorporating static compliance or imaging-based assessments to isolate elastic lung properties.

In a retrospective study including 130 long-term mechanically ventilated and tracheostomized patients from a German weaning center, Ghiani et al. [20] identified Cdyn-MP as a marker for weaning failure among high-risk patients, providing valuable guidance for the weaning process. Our study supports and extends these findings by retrospectively evaluating Cdyn-MP in a broader ICU population, demonstrating its predictive value not only in tracheostomized patients but also in general invasively ventilated patients. We observed a significant positive correlation between Cdyn-MP and the risk of weaning failure among patients undergoing invasive mechanical ventilation for ≥ 24 h (Fig. 2A). In particular, every 100-unit increase in Cdyn-MP corresponds to a 0.32-fold increase in the risk of weaning failure (Fig. 3). This suggests that higher Cdyn-MP values require greater respiratory muscle effort during spontaneous breathing, thereby increasing the risk of weaning failure. These findings support the clinical utility of Cdyn-MP as a physiologically grounded parameter for stratifying weaning risk and guiding individualized weaning strategies in the ICU.

Furthermore, although Cdyn-MP demonstrated only weak positive correlations with SOFA score, duration of mechanical ventilation, ICU stay, and total hospitalization time (Fig. 3), this may be attributable to the fact that measurements were obtained during a relatively stable phase prior to the SBT, rather than at the peak of illness severity. Therefore, Cdyn-MP may primarily reflect residual pulmonary burden rather than overall systemic dysfunction. ROC analysis demonstrated that Cdyn-MP outperformed MP, Cdyn, and ΔPaw in discriminating weaning outcomes, particularly within 24 h following the initial SBT (AUC 0.913, 95% CI 0.856–0.970) (Fig. 5). These findings suggest that Cdyn-MP may be a valuable, non-invasive physiological indicator that supports individualized decision-making during weaning.

While statistical significance highlights the robustness of our findings, their clinical utility lies in integrating parameters such as Cdyn-MP into bedside decision-making. For example, a higher Cdyn-MP value immediately prior to SBT may alert clinicians to a higher risk of weaning failure, prompting closer monitoring or delayed extubation. This integration of statistical results with clinical judgment could reduce premature extubation and associated complications, thereby improving patient outcomes.

Although the calculation of Cdyn-MP may seem complex, it is based on routinely measured ventilator parameters that can be automated with modern ICU equipment. Such automation facilitates its integration into clinical workflows, providing a physiologically individualized measure of respiratory load, potentially improving weaning success rates. While Cdyn-MP is not intended to replace existing SBT criteria, it offers a complementary tool for identifying patients at higher risk of weaning failure, thus enabling clinicians to refine extubation strategies and reduce adverse outcomes.

Our findings expand on prior evidence by demonstrating that Cdyn-MP measured immediately before SBT provides strong short-term predictive accuracy (AUC > 0.90) for weaning failure in a general ICU cohort. Unlike prior studies focusing on tracheotomized patients or non-standardized weaning timing, this analysis assesses a broader patient population and confirms Cdyn-MP’s value across multiple subgroups. Moreover, the feasibility of automated calculation in ventilators highlights its translational potential.

This study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, our assessment of functional lung size was based on pulmonary compliance, whereas lung CT scans could provide a more accurate evaluation of lung inhomogeneity [25]. However, pre-weaning CT scans and other imaging data were unavailable for all patients, and future research should explore the feasibility of incorporating these modalities. Second, although the Cdyn-MP values obtained during controlled ventilation may not directly correspond to those obtained during the SBT, we employed a measurement 4 h before the SBT, using volume control, a constant flow rate, and brief deep sedation. This approach was designed to maximize measurement accuracy and better reflect the respiratory load that patients must overcome during the SBT [26]. Nevertheless, although sedation allowed standardized measurement of plateau pressure under passive conditions, we did not separately analyze airway resistance (Ppeak–Pplat), elastance (Pplat–PEEP), or breathing patterns (e.g., rapid shallow breathing index, accessory muscle use). Future studies should integrate partitioned respiratory mechanics with quantitative breathing pattern analysis to clarify the relative contributions of elastic, resistive, and neuromuscular factors to weaning outcomes. Third, Given that Cdyn-MP is derived from routine ventilator parameters, it has potential for automated calculation and integration into bedside decision-support tools for real-time weaning assessment. Lastly, this retrospective analysis provides preliminary evidence for the predictive value of Cdyn-MP in weaning outcomes. Heterogeneity in case mix may influence weaning endpoints and length-of-stay outcomes; although we adjusted for admission category and relevant clinical covariates and confirmed consistent subgroup effects, unmeasured heterogeneity may persist. Therefore, prospective multicenter studies are warranted to validate its utility and generalizability in diverse clinical settings.

Conclusions

Cdyn-MP was significantly associated with both weaning outcomes and overall prognosis in mechanically ventilated patients. As an integrative parameter of respiratory system mechanics, its routine monitoring may improve the accuracy of weaning prediction and provide a useful complement to conventional evaluation approaches.

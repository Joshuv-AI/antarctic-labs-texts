Inclusive Language for Inclusive Care—A Proposal for an LGBTQI + Glossary for European Neurologists
Authors: Giulia Donzuso, Maria Teresa Ferretti, Francesco Pasini, Calogero Edoardo Cicero, Gennarina Arabia, Marianne de Visser, Cianán B. Russell, Dauren Ramankulov, Anja Sander, Alessandra Nicoletti, the European Academy of Neurology (EAN) Coordinating Panel on Diversity, Equity and Inclusion in Neurology
Published: 2026

The LGBTQI+ acronym encompasses a spectrum of sexual and gender identities, including lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender, intersex and queer/questioning individuals. This umbrella term, which is evolving over the years, reflects international efforts towards sexual and gender inclusivity. In Europe, LGBTQI+ individuals represent up to 14% of the population [1], an increasing minority whose needs must be incorporated in the standards of care of neurological services.

As with other minorities (such as those based on ethnicity and socioeconomic status), LGBTQI+ individuals experience disparities in the access to healthcare services [2]. Indeed, recent evidence indicates that sexual and gender minorities (SGM) have worse neurological health compared to the general population [3, 4], likely reflecting multiple determinants.

On the one hand, neurological diseases may present differently in this group due to biological factors. For instance, the use of gender‐affirming hormones can influence the risk and the clinical course of diseases such as stroke [5], migraine [6], epilepsy [7] and dementia [8].

On the other hand, several studies have documented the impact of stigma and discrimination on the care of LGBTQI+ individuals with neurological conditions, including multiple sclerosis [9], Parkinson's disease [10], dementia [11, 12], and migraine [6], with consequences for healthcare utilization, patient–clinician communication, and disease burden. Taken together, this evidence calls for gender‐specific medicine and poses specific challenges for neurologists.

Education in this field is further complicated by the absence of a uniform language, with substantial variations in LGBTQI+ terminology across cultures and regions within Europe [ref https://home‐affairs.ec.europa.eu/networks/european‐migration‐network‐emn/emn‐asylum‐and‐migration‐glossary/glossary/lgbtiq_en]. In the context of neurological care, which often involves long‐term follow‐up, complex therapeutic management, and close interactions between clinicians, patients, and caregivers, accurate and inclusive communication becomes particularly relevant for person‐centered care. In light of these challenges, there is a recognized need for a shared and consistent terminology among European neurology professionals.

To address this gap, the Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion (DEI) Coordinating Panel of the European Academy of Neurology (EAN) has created an LGBTQI+ glossary for dissemination and education as well as research purpose within the EAN community, starting from the concept that the use of an inclusive language is the basis for promoting inclusive care. The glossary presented here is a compendium of European‐based resources and has been endorsed by the International Lesbian, Gay, Bisexual, Trans, and Intersex Association (ILGA), an independent, international non‐governmental umbrella organization uniting over 700 organizations from 54 countries across Europe and Central Asia.

Although the terminology included in this glossary is not inherently specific to neurology and could potentially be applied across medical specialties, the present initiative was developed within the neurological community and is primarily intended as a practical reference for neurologists. While nothing would prevent its use in other medical specialties, we acknowledge that these fields may have additional or discipline‐specific considerations that are beyond the scope of the present work.

Initial Discussions Within EAN DEI Coordinating Panel Meeting

During the DEI Coordinating Panel meeting at the 2023 EAN congress in Budapest, the necessity to improve knowledge on terms referring to LGBTQI+ individuals in both research and clinical practice was highlighted, aligning with the overarching goals of the panel. Thereupon, a group of DEI Coordinating Panel members embarked on a project to create an LGBTQI+ glossary for dissemination and education within the EAN community.

Documents Screened and Overall Status in Europe

Our literature review actually revealed a lack of official glossaries proposed specifically for neurologists' and neuroscientists' purposes in Europe. Therefore, our research was expanded across official documents proposed by 52 European countries belonging to the EAN community as mentioned above. Among these, 15 countries (Albania, Austria, Denmark, France, Germany, Iceland, Ireland, Italy, Luxemburg, Norway, Spain, Sweden, the Netherlands, Switzerland, and the United Kingdom) proposed official documents such as strategies and action plans aimed at improving LGBTQI+ living conditions. Among these documents, we focused on those reporting a list of terms/glossaries that could cover most of the terminology according to the recent literature, obtained from six European countries (Albania, Austria, Ireland, Italy, Sweden, and Switzerland).

Additionally, we know that language traditions and cultures related to sexual orientation and gender identity could carry variable cultural and linguistic meanings across different European contexts. Thus, the use of official documents of European Health Ministry and other official institutions could guarantee the use of terms officially recognized in order to obtain a unique document without subjective influences. Finally, the information gathered was meticulously assembled, merged and explored collectively by all authors involved. A final draft of the glossary emerged through a dedicated consensus meeting, where each term was scrutinized and discussed, leading to the creation of a first draft of the glossary including 51 terms.

Review by ILGA

The final document has been reviewed by ILGA, an independent, international non‐governmental umbrella organization uniting over 700 organizations from 54 countries across Europe and Central Asia. Suggestions and issues have been discussed and addressed and then the document has been subsequently modified accordingly, with the last version of the glossary including 49 terms (Table 1).

Here, we propose an LGBTQI+ glossary designed for the interaction with sex and gender minorities in daily neurological clinical practice. SGM are widely recognized to experience elevated levels of stigma and face substantial challenges in accessing appropriate healthcare services, as a result of intersecting factors such as lack of awareness and knowledge, cultural and societal norms, minority stress, victimization, and discrimination [2].

Healthcare professionals' behavior is one of the most important determinants of health among the LGBTQI+ population, including in neurological settings [9]. A major barrier to improving the quality of care for SGM is the lack of knowledge leading to the erroneous concept of “equality” in medicine (e.g., treating all patients the same, assuming that sexual or gender identity is irrelevant to healthcare provision), whereas one of the most effective ways to enhance healthcare for SGM individuals is to recognize their unique characteristics, including diverse backgrounds and risk factors [13]. For these reasons, we believe that explicit and inclusive communication with SGM represents the first step towards delivering high‐quality care, and that an active training of healthcare professionals could be pivotal in promoting more equitable outcomes.

The EAN DEI Coordinating Panel proposes this glossary as a practical tool for facilitating communication with SGM in daily neurological clinical practice and in neuroscience research.

Protocol for monitoring the stability of transcription factor EB using global protein stability assay
Authors: Boju Wang, Yueling Zhao, Ruiyan Li, Xinying Li, Cheng Dong, Wenyi Mi, Xiaolu Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryTranscription factor EB (TFEB), the master regulator of autophagy-lysosomal networks, undergoes multiple regulatory mechanisms to maintain proteostasis. Here, we present a protocol for quantifying TFEB stability following loss of function of its regulator (YEATS domain-containing protein 2 [YEATS2] knockdown), utilizing a dual-fluorescent reporter system based on flow cytometry. We describe strategies for vector construction, cell transfection, and fluorescence detection. We then detail procedures for analytical workflows. This protocol is designed to enhance sensitivity and reduce processing duration.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Wang et al.1Subject areas: Cell-based Assays, Flow Cytometry, High Throughput Screening

Before you begin

Protein degradation plays a crucial role in maintaining cellular homeostasis, and thus is tightly regulated in response to extracellular and intracellular environmental changes.2 Systematically investigating how specific regulatory factors modulate protein stability provides critical insights into their molecular mechanisms and associated biological pathways.

The Global Protein Stability (GPS) reporter system enables quantitative monitoring of protein stability in living cells under various cellular conditions.3 This system utilizes a bicistronic lentiviral vector encoding two distinct fluorophores from a single mRNA: a target protein-fused Green Fluorescent Protein (GFP) for stability monitoring, and an internal ribosome entry site (IRES)-driven Red Fluorescent Protein (RFP) constitutively expressed as an internal normalization control. Relative protein stability can be assessed through continuous measurement of GFP/RFP fluorescence intensity ratios via flow cytometry, as target protein degradation directly correlates with proportional decreases in GFP signal intensity. Therefore, the GPS reporter system offers a robust approach for real-time quantification of protein turnover dynamics in live cells under defined physiological or experimental conditions.

Recently, our group found that knockdown of YEATS2 (YEATS Domain-Containing Protein 2) increased the protein level of transcription factor EB (TFEB).1 To investigate whether YEATS2 knockdown enhances TFEB stability, thereby increasing its protein level, we employed the GPS assay in this protocol. Here, we describe the complete procedure for GPS analysis, including vector construction, cell transfection, and fluorescence signal detection.

Innovation

This manuscript details an optimized flow cytometric protocol for precise quantification of TFEB protein stability, a master regulator of lysosomal biogenesis and autophagy. The methodology employs a dual-fluorescent reporter system in which TFEB is C-terminally fused to GFP, while RFP serves as an internal normalization control within a single open reading frame, thereby eliminating effects from mRNA level fluctuations. The TFEB degradation kinetics are quantified through longitudinal measurement of GFP/RFP fluorescence intensity ratios. This ratiometric approach inherently corrects for variations in transfection efficiency and instrumental drift. Notably, this strategy exhibits significantly enhanced sensitivity in detecting TFEB degradation compared to conventional cycloheximide chase-western blotting, while reducing total processing duration from 24–48 h to around 2 h. Moreover, the standardized workflow enables high-throughput screening of compounds or genetic modulators influencing substrate stability under both physiological and pathological conditions.

Establishment of the TFEB GPS reporter cell line

Timing: ∼2 weeks

Generate cell lines stably expressing pCDH-CMV-TFEB-GFP-IRES-RFP through lentiviral transduction. This step details lentivirus production and cell infection to obtain the GPS reporter cell line.

1.Lentivirus Production.a.Seed 293T cells (1 × 106) in a 3.5 cm dish one day prior to transfection.b.At 70%–80% confluence, transfect cells using Hieff Trans Liposomal Transfection Reagent (storage at 4°C) with a plasmid mixture prepared in DMEM (storage at 4°C) without FBS (storage at −80°C), containing: 1.5 μg pCDH-CMV-TFEB-GFP-IRES-RFP (reporter construct). 1.0 μg psPAX2 (packaging plasmid). 0.5 μg pMD2.G (envelope plasmid).The transfection complex was incubated for 20 min at room temperature before dropwise addition to cells.c.Confirm fluorescence (GFP and RFP) microscopically 48 h post-transfection.d.Harvest the supernatant, filter through a 0.45 μm membrane.e.Store viral supernatant at 4°C (≤1 week) or −80°C (≤6 months).Note: (1) In addition to experimental stable cell lines, controls comprised untransduced H1299 cells (background) and single-color controls (GFP+/RFP+ cells for compensation). Flow cytometric analysis of protein stability

Note: (1) DAPI is a membrane-impermeant DNA dye to identify non-viable cells. Dead cells are identified by DAPI+ signal (detected through 450/50 nm band pass filter), whereas live cells with intact membranes exclude DAPI and remain unstained. So dead cells can be excluded by gating of DAPI- viable populations.

Note: (2) To prepare a 1 mg/mL DAPI stock solution, dissolve 1 mg DAPI powder in 1 mL deionized water. Note that DAPI dihydrochloride may require extended time or sonication to completely dissolve. Aliquot and store the stock solution from light at −20°C or −80°C and avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles.

Note: (3) Since in this protocol DAPI is intended to stain only dead cells and the analyzed cells are unfixed, lower concentrations are typically recommended. A general guideline of final concentration range of 0.1–1 μg/mL is generally suggested, and it is essential to perform a concentration gradient test (e.g., 0.1, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0 μg/mL) to achieve an optimal signal-to-noise ratio. For mammalian adherent or suspension cell lines, a common working concentration is 0.5–1 μg/mL. For more sensitive cells such as lymphocytes, it is recommended to start with even lower concentrations, typically between 0.1–0.5 μg/mL. For primary cells, which may exhibit variability, titration is strongly recommended—often within the range of 0.1–0.5 μg/mL.

Expected outcomes

This approach enables rapid, quantitative assessment of protein stability in live cells and is amenable to high-throughput screening. By normalizing GFP intensity to the RFP internal control via the GFP/RFP ratio, the method mitigates experimental variations arising from transfection efficiency, cellular heterogeneity, or technical artifacts. A significant increase in the GFP/RFP ratio relative to controls indicates enhanced target protein stability (e.g., reduced degradation), whereas a decreased ratio suggests destabilization. As demonstrated in Figure 2, YEATS2 knockdown elevated the GFP/RFP ratio, confirming its role in stabilizing TFEB.

Limitations

Two key limitations warrant consideration:

Fluorescent tag interference: GFP fusion may alter the folding, subcellular localization, or functional integrity of the target protein, potentially affecting the stability of the fusion construct compared to the native protein.

Further validation required: this methodology can only suggest the effects of overexpression/knockdown of genes regulating protein degradation pathways (UPS, autophagy, or lysosomal systems), or pharmacological treatments on the intracellular stability of the target protein, but does not provide definitive evidence that the target protein serves as a direct substrate of the regulator in these degradation pathways. To conclusively demonstrate direct interaction between the target protein and the regulator, additional in vitro and cellular interaction assays would be required.

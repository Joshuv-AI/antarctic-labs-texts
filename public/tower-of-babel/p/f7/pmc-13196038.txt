Dietary coated sodium selenite enhances muscle growth associated with mitochondrial function in sub-adult grass carp (Ctenopharyngodon idellus)
Authors: Fei Zhao, Pei Wu, Weidan Jiang, Hongyun Zhang, Yang Liu, Yaobin Ma, Hongmei Ren, Xiaowan Jin, Yinggu Kuang, Lin Feng, Xiaoqiu Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSelenium (Se) is a vital trace element necessary for fish growth and health. This research aimed to elucidate the beneficial impacts of coated sodium selenite (CSS) on sub-adult grass carp muscle and compared effects of CSS and sodium selenite (SS). A total of 450 healthy fish with an initial average body weight of 687.33 ± 1.15 g were randomly assigned to six groups. Fish in these groups were fed experimental diets supplemented with graded levels of CSS (0.028, 0.25, 0.50, 0.75, and 1.00 mg/kg Se) and one diet containing SS at 0.50 mg/kg Se, respectively, for an 8-week feeding trial. Results indicated that optimal CSS increased body length and Se concentrations in muscle and hepatopancreas (P < 0.05). The accumulated Se further up-regulated the expressions of selenoproteins (such as gpx1 and selp), which collectively improved antioxidant capacity (such as glutathione peroxidase [GPX] activity and L-glutathione [GSH] content]) and mitochondrial quality (such as fusion, biogenesis, and respiratory chain) (P < 0.05). These alterations improved muscle fiber development by upregulating the expression of genes related to protein synthesis (pi3k, akt, and s6k1) and extracellular matrix synthesis (col1α1, smad2, smad4, tgf-β1, and phd) (P < 0.05). Interestingly, at the 0.50 mg/kg Se supplementation, CSS and SS showed similar growth and muscle development, but different responses in signaling pathways, implying that CSS may confer additional physiological benefits. Overall, dietary Se could enhance muscle growth in sub-adult grass carp by dual mechanisms: enhancing antioxidant defenses and improving mitochondrial quality.Keywords: Selenium, Grass carp, Muscle, Protein synthesis, Mitochondria function

Introduction

Selenium (Se) is a fundamental micro-element, involved in multiple biological activities (redox regulation, immunity and thyroid hormone metabolism) in animals (Kieliszek and Blazejak, 2016). It has been widely founded that moderate Se could enhance growth performance in juvenile grass carp (Wang et al., 2023), rainbow trout (Wang et al., 2019), dairy bulls (Liu et al., 2020), and pigs (Cao et al., 2014). Findings have indicated that a lack of Se can lead to muscle pain and atrophy (Halimulati et al., 2023). This pathology is attributed to the impairment of selenoprotein-based antioxidant systems, which results in oxidative stress that disrupts the balance between protein synthesis and degradation in skeletal muscle. Therefore, a balanced dietary Se supply is highly important for the muscle development of animals. Muscle fibers are the basic structural units of muscle tissue, and their growth and development directly affect the muscle quality of animals (Listrat et al., 2016). A study has shown that protein synthesis and extracellular matrix (ECM) are critical for muscle fiber growth and development (Zhao et al., 2016). The target of rapamycin (TOR)/4E-binding protein 1 (4EBP1) signaling molecule regulates protein synthesis process (Wei et al., 2023). Additionally, collagen I plays a crucial role in muscle development and structural stability. Previous studies have reported that the synthesis and degradation of collagen are regulated by the transforming growth factor-β1 (TGF-β1)/Smads signaling molecule and matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) (Johnston and Gillis, 2017). In pig skeletal muscle, Se deficiency could decrease the expression of ECM-related signaling genes (Zhang et al., 2022). Conversely, excessive Se can lead to muscle degradation in rainbow trout (Zhang et al., 2022). Nevertheless, the impact of moderate Se levels and the mechanisms on ECM and protein synthesis in sub-adult grass carp remain unclear.

Skeletal muscle development is achieved by the proliferation, differentiation, and fusion of myoblasts. This process depends on the total adenosine triphosphate (ATP) generated by oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS) in the mitochondria (Martinez-Reyes et al., 2016). What's more, muscle energy supply is supported by mitochondrial homeostasis, mediated by biogenesis, fission, fusion, and mitophagy (Ji et al., 2020). Mitochondrial fission is mediated by dynamin-related protein 1 (drp1) and fission 1 (fis1). In HT22 neuronal cells, Se inhibited the expression of p-drp1 and fis1 (Kumari et al., 2012). Moreover, mitochondrial fusion is mediated by mitofusins 1 (mfn1), mfn2 and optic atrophy 1 (opa1) (Ma et al., 2020). Excessive fission disrupts the membrane potential, triggering mitophagy. Previous research has found that Se deficiency up-regulated light chain 3 (lc3) and autophagy-related 5 (atg5) mRNA expression levels in the spleen, bursa of Fabricius and thymus of chickens (Khoso et al., 2017). What's more, mitochondrial biogenesis is controlled by peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor γ coactivator 1α (PGC-1α) and nuclear respiratory factor 1 (Nrf1), which restore mitochondrial function under stress (Xie and Huang, 2024). The mitochondrial respiratory chain (COX I–V) and cytochrome C (Cyt C) are the core components of mitochondrial function. It has been reported that Se deficiency significantly reduced the protein abundances of COX IV and Cyt C in mouse kidney (Lai et al., 2021). However, the impact of Se on muscle mitochondrial function in sub-adult grass carp remains unexplored.

To address the low bioavailability of inorganic Se, sodium selenite (SS) was coated with carboxymethyl cellulose. This polymer was selected owing to its properties as a widely used, biocompatible, biodegradable, and inexpensive cellulose ether. Its swelling and erosion characteristics, known to regulate drug release, have proven effective in formulations containing nano-ZnO and probiotic bacteria (Dafe et al., 2017; Saba and Amini, 2017). Furthermore, while the application of coated sodium selenite (CSS) has been explored in ruminants, its potential to promote muscle growth in aquatic species remains unexamined. Fish muscle growth is critical to aquaculture productivity. Therefore, this study hypothesized that CSS would be effective in promoting muscle growth of sub-adult grass carp. This study was consequently designed to elucidate the impact of CSS on muscle growth and its underlying mechanism, and to compare the effects of CSS and SS. These findings contribute to an enhanced understanding of the role of inorganic Se in aquatic animals.

Materials and methods

Animal ethics statement

The animal trial was approved by the Animal Care and Use Committee at the Laboratory Animal Center of Sichuan Agricultural University (No. ZF-2022114001).

Animals, diet and experimental protocol

All fish were purchased from professional farmers in Deyang, Sichuan, China, and were acclimated in a temporary pond for one month to adapt to the environment. A total of 450 fish (average weight was 687.33 ± 1.15 g) were randomly divided into 6 groups, each with 3 replicates containing 25 fish. After grouping, the fish were fed a basal diet for Se depletion for 14 d to reduce body Se contents referring to previous study (Wang et al., 2023). The ingredients and nutrient levels of the basal diet are listed in Table 1. The diet contained 26.00% crude protein and 4.76% lipid, analyzed according to standard procedures (AOAC, 2005). Crude protein was determined using the Kjeldahl method (method 990.03), and crude lipid was extracted by the Soxhlet method (method 2003.05). Available phosphorus, n-3 PUFA, and n-6 PUFA were assessed according to NRC (2011). The organic matter content of the feeds was calculated by subtracting crude ash from dry matter. Gross energy was precisely measured with an oxygen bomb calorimeter (model 6400, Parr Instrument Company, Moline, IL, USA), following the ISO 9831:1998 standard. Coated sodium selenite (0.08% Se) and SS (45% Se) were provided by Fujian Shenna Bioengineering Co., Ltd., Fuzhou, Fujian, China. The theoretical values of Se in the feed were 0.028, 0.25, 0.50, 0.75, and 1.00 mg/kg for CSS and 0.50 mg/kg for SS (Liu et al., 2018). During the experiment, fish were given diets 4 times (07:00, 11:00, 15:00, and 19:00) daily to satiation for 8 weeks. A 60-cm disk placed at the bottom of each bucket collected remaining feed after 20 min. Adjust the daily feeding amount appropriately according to the actual intake of grass carp on the previous day (Wang et al., 2023). All fish were reared in indoor fiberglass tanks (1 m diameter and 1 m height). Indoor recirculating aquaculture was adopted, and water changes were performed daily. During the growth trial, water temperature, pH and dissolved oxygen were maintained at 28.8 ± 2.2 °C, 7.5 ± 0.3, and 6.0 mg/L, respectively.

Sample collection

For each barrel, grass carp were counted and weighed collectively, and percent weight gain (PWG) and specific growth rate (SGR) were calculated. Fish were subjected to anesthetize by 50 mg/L tricaine methanesulfonate (MS-222) prior to sampling (Zhang et al., 2025). Next, twelve fish were randomly chosen from each group, and the weight of body, viscera and muscle tissues were obtained. Subsequently, muscle samples were collected according to the standardized protocol established by previous study (Xue et al., 2025). The left dorsal muscle tissue was sampled for enzyme activity analysis and mRNA extraction. The tissues were frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80 °C. The right dorsal muscle tissue (0.5 cm × 0.5 cm × 0.5 cm) was immediately soaked in muscle fixative solution (G1111-100 ML, Servicebio Technology Co., Ltd., Wuhan, Hubei, China) for histological observation and 2.5% glutaraldehyde solution for electron microscopy observation. The calculation formulas for PWG, SGR, feed efficiency (FE), and feed intake (FI) were as follows:

PWG (%) = (Final body weight − Initial body weight)/Initial body weight × 100;

SGR (%/d) = ln (Mean final body weight) − ln (Mean initial body weight)/Feeding days;

FE = (Final body weight − Initial body weight)/Feed intake (FI);

FI (g/fish) = Total feed consumption − Total uneaten consumption.

Determination of proximate composition, Se content and oxidative stress indicators

Proximate composition of fish muscle was assayed following standard methods (Wen et al., 2023). Se content in hepatopancreas and muscle were measured using a hybrid generation-atomic fluorescence spectrometer (AFS-230E, Beijing Haiguang Instrument Co., Ltd., Beijing, China) according to GB 5009.93–2010 (Chinese National Standard, 2010). Sample pretreatment was conducted according to the procedure outlined by Jing et al. (2023). The contents of ATP and reactive oxygen species (ROS) in the muscle were quantified using the commercial kits (S0026 and S0033S, Beyotime Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) following the manufacturer's guidelines. And the activity of glutathione peroxidase (GPX) and L-glutathione (GSH), malondialdehyde (MDA), protein carbonyl (PC), and hydroxyproline (HYP) contents were assayed using kits from Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute (A005, A006, A003-1, A087-1, and A030-1, Nanjing, Jiangsu, China).

Histological observation and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) analysis

Hematoxylin-eosin (HE) staining and Sirius red were referenced to the description of previous methods (Wang et al., 2023). Myofiber diameters were measured using Image J software (Java 1.8.0, National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD, USA), with approximately 80 myofibers analyzed per section. Tian et al. (2023) divided myofibers into < 60 μm, 60 to 100 μm, as well as > 100 μm. And their frequency was calculated as the ratio of myofibers in each range to the total count. For Sirius red staining, three samples were analyzed. Transmission electron microscopy analysis, following Sun et al. (2024), was conducted using a JEM1400-FLASH microscope (JEOL Ltd., Akishima, Tokyo, Japan). The TEM observation of Se deficiency group, 0.75 mg/kg CSS group and 0.50 mg/kg SS group both included 3 replicates.

Amino acid analysis

The concentration of amino acids in muscle of grass carp was determined by automated amino acid analyzer (L-8900, Hitachi High-Tech Corporation, Tokyo, Japan). The pretreatment of muscle sample was in accordance with the method of Sun et al. (2024). In short, freeze-dried muscle samples were placed in a hydrolysis tube, 15 mL of 6 mol/L hydrochloric acid was treated, sealed with nitrogen, and hydrolyzed at 110 °C for 22 h. After hydrolysis, the solution was transferred to a 100 mL volumetric flask, filtered and dried. Finally, the dried sample was dissolved with 800 μL of 0.02 mol/L hydrochloric acid.

Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) analysis

Total RNA from muscle samples was isolated using the RNAiso Plus kit (10604 ES, Yeasen Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China). RNA quality and concentration were assessed using a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Wilmington, NC, USA) at 260/280 nm, with values ranging between 1.9 and 2.1. cDNA synthesis was performed following the manufacturer's protocol (6210B, Takara Biomedical Technology (Dalian) Co., Ltd., Dalian, Liaoning, China). The full names of the abbreviations of all genes can be found in Table S1. Primer sequences for target genes are listed in Table S2. Quantitative real-time PCR was conducted in a 20 μL using the SYBR Premix Ex TaqTM II kit (RR820A, Takara Biomedical Technology (Dalian) Co., Ltd., Dalian, Liaoning, China) on a QuantStudio 5 Flex system (Applied Biosystems, Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Waltham, MA, USA). Quantitative real-time PCR cycling conditions were: 95 °C for 30 s (hold stage), 40 cycles of 95 °C for 15 s and 60 °C for 34 s (measure fluorescence), and a melt curve stage of 60 °C for 1 min then 95 °C for 15 s. β-Actin gene served as an endogenous control, and relative mRNA expression levels of target genes were determined using the 2−ΔΔCt method (Livak and Schmittgen, 2001).

Western blotting analysis

Western blotting was performed as previously described methods (Zhao et al., 2024). Briefly, muscle samples were homogenized in ice-cold buffer and protein concentration was measured using the BCA kit (PC0020, Beijing Solarbio Science & Technology Co., Ltd., Beijing, China). Protein samples were separated by using sodium dodecyl sulfate polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) gels and transferred onto polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membranes for protein blotting analysis. And the membranes were blocked with fast sealing liquid for 10 min, then washed three times with Tris buffered saline with Tween 20 (TBST; G2150-1L, Servicebio Technology Co., Ltd., Wuhan, Hubei, China), and incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary and secondary antibodies. After, stripes were observed using chemiluminescence system (ECL) kit (PD202-120 mL, Oriscience Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Chengdu, Sichuan, China). Finally, these protein bands were quantified via the Image J software (Java 1.8.0, National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD, USA). The full names of the abbreviations of all proteins can be found in Table S1. Details of the antibodies used are listed in Table S3.

Immunofluorescence staining analysis

For immunofluorescence, paraffin sections were dewaxed in xylene, 100%, 95%, 85%, and 75% alcohol, respectively. Then, samples were treated with sodium citrate antigen repair solution for 5 min. The sections were then incubated in 5% BSA and translocase of the outer mitochondrial membrane complex subunit 20 (TOMM20) primary antibody (dilution ratio: 1:500; YM8164, Immunoway Technology Inc., San Jose, CA, USA). After, samples were incubated with secondary antibody (dilution ratio: 1:1000; A0422, Beyotime Biotech Inc., Shanghai, China) in dark. Finally, the samples were washed with PBS for 3 times and incubated in 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI) for 10 min. Images acquired by an inverted fluorescence microscope (DMI4000B, Leica Microsystems GmbH, Wetzlar, Germany).

Muscle antioxidant capacity in grass carp

Table 5 illustrates the muscle antioxidant status. Glutathione peroxidase activity (P < 0.001) and GSH (P = 0.006) as well as ATP (P = 0.003) contents were significantly higher in the 0.50 to 0.75 mg/kg diet treatments compared with the Se-deficient treatment. Discussion

Dietary Se improved the growth of sub-adult grass carp

Selenium, a vital trace element, serves diverse biological roles in aquatic species. In the present study, CSS and SS enhanced the body length and FI of sub-adult grass carp. However, FBW, PWG, SGR, and FE showed an increasing trend, but these increases were not statistically significant. Similar findings have been observed in Chu's croaker (Lin et al., 2021) and black sea bream (Wang et al., 2019). The accumulation of Se in tissues reflects the nutritional status of the body. These results demonstrated that Se content in the hepatopancreas and muscle increased with dietary Se supplementation. Fish fed Se-deficient diets had lower Se content in the hepatopancreas and muscle than those fed Se-supplemented diets. Moreover, the concentration of Se in hepatopancreas was higher than that in muscle, indicating that hepatopancreas has a greater ability to accumulate Se. Above findings are consistent with those of common carp (Ashouri et al., 2015) and juvenile grass carp (Wang et al., 2023). In addition, CSS was more effective than SS in accumulating Se in hepatopancreas and muscle tissues. The approximate components of the fish include moisture, crude protein, crude ash, and crude lipid. This study found that CSS remarkably decreased moisture and increased crude lipid content, indicating that the proximate composition of sub-adult grass carp muscle is sensitive to dietary Se treatments. This observation was consistent with what Wang et al. (2023) reported.

Dietary Se promoted muscle protein synthesis of sub-adult grass carp

Fish muscle growth involves myofiber hyperplasia and hypertrophy (Wang et al., 2023). The average diameter of muscle fibers serves as a vital index for evaluating the myofiber proliferation and hypertrophy (Alami-Durante et al., 2018). Tian et al. (2023) reported that myofibers < 60 μm in diameter indicate hyperplasia, whereas those > 100 μm indicate hypertrophy. This study showed that dietary Se stimulated myofiber hypertrophy in sub-adult grass carp, thereby boosting muscle growth. Specifically, Se addition enhanced the mean diameter of myofibers and reduced the frequency of myofibers < 60 μm in sub-adult grass carp. The sarcomere is the functional unit of contraction in muscle fibers. In this study, no effect on the sarcomere length was observed between the Se-deficient treatment and 0.50 mg/kg CSS treatment. This evidence suggested that Se deficiency did not induce changes in muscle ultrastructure of grass carp, possibly because the experimental period was not long enough for CSS to have a significant effect on muscle sarcomere length.

A study showed that muscle hypertrophy is a process in which muscle cells increase in size, which is closely related to muscle protein synthesis (Wang et al., 2023). Target of rapamycin is a critical regulator of cell proliferation, protein synthesis, and autophagy, functioning primarily by phosphorylating downstream targets such as ribosomal protein S6 kinase beta-1 (S6K1) and 4EBP1 (Wassie et al., 2022). Phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K) is the central signal transduction hub located upstream of mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) (Noorolyai et al., 2019). In the present study, CSS significantly up-regulated tor, pi3k, akt, and s6k1 mRNA and the ratio of p-TOR/T-TOR in sub-adult grass carp muscle, suggesting that CSS enhanced protein synthesis through activation of the PI3K/Akt/TOR pathway. Notably, CSS supplementation exerted no influence on the 4ebp1 mRNA expression, indicating that Se did not affect muscle translation initiation in sub-adult grass carp. Different results were observed in juvenile grass carp (Wang et al., 2023), possibly due to differences in growth stages.

The biological value of protein in muscle depends on its amino acid composition (Sun et al., 2024). The data found that EAA (such as Met) and NEAA (such as Gly) levels of grass carp muscle were significantly enhanced in the 0.50 mg/kg Se group. This study also discovered that there were no notable differences in the levels of eight EAAs (Arg, Val, The, Ile, Phe, Lys, and His) and five NEAAs (Ser, Asp, Glu, Ala, Pro, and Tyr) among dietary treatments. In pig muscle, Se has been shown to increase amino acids, including Asp, Glu, and Ala (Zhao et al., 2016). This difference may be due to the specificity of different species in response to Se dose.

Collagen, a primary constituent of the extracellular matrix, plays a vital role in maintaining muscle growth in fish. Sirius red staining revealed that dietary CSS supplementation increased collagen area. Additionally, HYP, which is essential for collagen structure and function, was enhanced by Se in grass carp muscle. Type I collagen is primarily composed of col1α1 and col1α2 chains. TGF-β1/Smads, the central mediator of tissue remodeling, promotes ECM production and collagen synthesis (Lipson et al., 2012). Molecule and matrix metalloproteinases and their inhibitor (TIMP2) regulate collagen degradation (Irqsusi et al., 2025). The data revealed that dietary CSS supplementation elevated the transcription of collagen genes (col1α1), collagen synthesis-related genes (sox9b, tgf-β1, smad2, and smad4), as well as collagen degradation-related genes (mmp2 and mmp 9) in sub-adult grass carp muscle. Additionally, PHD, which is involved in collagen modification, was up-regulated by dietary CSS. These findings suggested that CSS significantly affected muscle structure and collagen metabolism in grass carp. Notably, suppression of selenoprotein m has been shown to increase mmp13 expression in mouse cartilage, further supporting the role of Se in regulating collagen metabolism (Zhao et al., 2025). Previous research has shown that treatment of human trabecular meshwork (HTM) cells with Se (1-10 μmol/L) reduced the expression of mmp2 and timp1 (Conley et al., 2004). However, these results indicated that CSS had no effect on timp2 expression, likely because TIMP1 is more sensitive to redox state changes, whereas TIMP2 is relatively insensitive.

Protein synthesis and protein degradation together maintain intracellular protein homeostasis. The pathways of protein degradation include autophagy-lysosome signaling pathway and ubiquitinated proteasome system (UPS). Although some studies have reported that Se inhibits protein degradation in rainbow trout (Zhang et al., 2022) and juvenile grass carp (Wang et al., 2023) via the UPS. The-autophagy-lysosomal system is also important in proteolysis (Sato et al., 2014). Besides, autophagy-lysosomal system is regulated by various autophagy genes. Unc-51 like autophagy activating kinase 1 regulates autophagosome initiation, beclin1 controls autophagosome nucleation, and atg5, atg12, and lc3 are involved in autophagosome vesicle expansion and maturation (Chen et al., 2020; Yang et al., 2017). This research noted that dietary supplementation with CSS down-regulated the expression levels of autophagy-associated genes (beclin1, lc3, and atg5), as well as LC3 protein level in grass carp muscle tissue. Notably, there were no notable effect on the transcriptional levels of atg12 and ulk1 among dietary treatments. The promoter region of atg5 may contain Se responsive element [such as the antioxidant response element (ARE)], while the promoter region of atg12 may lack such elements. Similarly, a previous study showed that dietary selenomethionine down-regulated the mRNA expression levels of atg5, atg7, and atg10 (but not atg12) in pig lung tissue (Wang et al., 2022). Moreover, a study has shown that TOR is the central hub of nutritional signal transduction, and its activation can promote protein synthesis and inhibit autophagy (Efeyan et al., 2015). In C2C12, Lys regulated autophagy-lysosomal activity through mTOR-dependent pathway (Sato et al., 2014), which is consistent with results presented.

Dietary Se promoted muscle antioxidant capacity of sub-adult grass carp

Malnutrition could induce oxidative stress and increase intracellular ROS levels during fish growth. Selenium, a highly effective antioxidant, plays a pivotal role in the antioxidant defense system of fish and is an essential component of GPX (Hamilton, 2004). Adequate Se levels enhance GPX activity, efficiently converting H2O2 into H2O. GSH, a substrate for GPX in hydroperoxide detoxification, also serves as an indicator of oxidative stress. Nevertheless, Se deficiency impaired antioxidant enzyme function and raised oxidation product levels in calf liver, negatively impacting health (Wang et al., 2022). Malondialdehyde and PC reflect lipid peroxidation and protein oxidation, respectively (Ahmad et al., 2012). According to Liu et al. (2017), selenium yeast (0.4 mg/kg) markedly raised GPX activity and reduced MDA content in the liver of blunt snout bream, suggesting that Se has a strong antioxidant capacity. These results were similar. This study also provided evidence that CSS improved GPX activity and GSH content, as well as reduced the contents of PC and MDA, and inhibited ROS generation, indicating that Se was fundamental to maintaining the antioxidant system.

Selenium may exert its biological effects by incorporation into selenoproteins as selenocysteine. Moreover, there are 41 selenoproteins in bony fish. Among them, selp, as a transport protein, is responsible for supplying Se to tissues beyond the liver (Burk and Hill, 2005). In addition, GPX1, an essential subtype of the GPX family, is distributed in the cytosol and mitochondria, providing protection against ROS (Handy and Loscalzo, 2022). A study by Zhu et al. (2023) demonstrated that gpx1 overexpression significantly reduced total ROS levels in cells and mitochondria of aged mice. Thioredoxin reductase 2 (Txnrd2) defends against disulfide damage and controls redox homeostasis, whereas its mitochondrial knockout disrupts metabolic function (Kiermayer et al., 2015). As a mitochondrial protein with redox activity, selo may exert a pivotal role in maintenance mitochondrial function and stress response (Han et al., 2014). It has been demonstrated that selenomethionine improved mitochondrial function in the cortex of mice, which may be due to the upregulation of SELO protein level (Chen et al., 2021).

According to this study, dietary CSS supplementation enhanced gpx1 and selp mRNA expressions, and GPX1 as well as SELO protein abundances in sub-adult grass carp muscle. It appears intriguing that above results showed that selo mRNA expression was not affected by any of the treatments, which is similar to previous findings in rats (Hrdina et al., 2009) and pigs (Lu et al., 2019). It is possible that these selenoproteins were not sensitive to Se intake. The above findings suggested that the glutathione peroxidase system exert a significant role in cellular oxidative stress defense.

Dietary Se alleviated muscle mitochondrial dysfunction of sub-adult grass carp

Mitochondria are highly concentrated in skeletal muscle, supporting the energy demands of myocytes. This research discovered that Se deficiency negatively affected mitochondrial morphology, manifesting as matrix dissolution and cristae damage, indicating that Se deficiency may damage mitochondrial function. Meanwhile, most mitochondria in the myocardium of Se-deficient rats showed irregular swelling and fractured myofilaments (Hu et al., 2016). Whereas, supplemental CSS levels alleviated this damage. Consequently, this research analyzed the levels of genes and proteins associated with mitochondrial respiratory chain complexes. The mtochondrial respiratory system includes COX I, II, III, IV, and V. In this study, dietary addition of CSS enhanced the gene expressions of nduf1, sdhb, mt-cyb, mt-col, and mt-atp, as well as COX IV protein level in grass carp muscle. This study also found that NDUF1 and SDHB protein levels had no change by different levels of CSS. The possible reason is that CSS mainly affects the transcription factors of NDUF1 and SDHB in grass carp muscle, rather than the protein itself. Complex V is responsible for ATP production. This research confirmed that dietary Se supplementation significantly improved ATP content, suggesting that Se could promote ATP synthesis by reducing mitochondrial dysfunction.

The mitochondrial quality control (MQC) system, which regulates mitochondrial biogenesis, fission, fusion, mitophagy, and redox state, is crucial for preserving mitochondrial function and membrane integrity (Venditti and Meo,). Healthy mitochondria maintain a dynamic balance of fission and fusion to ensure proper function and renewal. An imbalance in this system may result in impaired mitochondrial function and cellular demise. Mitochondrial fusion is controlled by the inner membrane protein (such as OPA1) and the outer membrane proteins (such as MFN1 and MFN2) (Chen et al., 2021). A study confirmed that insufficient Se has led to excessive mitochondrial fission by down-regulating mfn2 and opa1 expressions in calf liver (Wang et al., 2022). Furthermore, Se supplementation in muscle reduced fis1 and drp1 gene expressions while increasing mfn2 level, suggesting increased mitochondrial fusion and decreased fission. Furthermore, this study found two interesting phenomena. Firstly, CSS had no statistical difference on mfn1 transcription level in fish muscle. It is possible that the effect of Se on MFN1 is not obvious in some tissues (such as muscle and liver), but may be more significant in other tissue (such as nerve). Findings from Chen et al. (2021) indicated that selenomethionine markedly up-regulated the expression level of mfn2 in the cerebral cortex of mice, rather than mfn1, which is consistent with these results. Secondly, FIS1 protein level in fish muscle were unaffected by CSS. The underlying reason may be that Se exerted its effects on the transcription factor of fis1 in grass carp muscle, rather than modulating the protein directly. A research presented that SS completely blocked the AgNPs-induced increase in FIS1 protein level in rat myocardium (Ma et al., 2020). These differences may be attributed to the different species of animals. The current research showed that Se supplementation restored the balance between mitochondrial fission-fusion and blocked autophagy due to Se deficiency.

To better elucidate the role of Se in mitochondrial function, this study investigated the mitochondrial biosynthesis-related indexes in grass carp muscle. Peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor γ coactivator 1, a member of the PGC family, is predominantly found in skeletal muscle, heart, and liver, plays a role in mitochondrial biogenesis. In mouse kidney, Se-deficient increased pgc-1α mRNA levels (Lai et al., 2021). However, selenomethionine supplementation had no impact on PGC-1α level in the cortical layer of Alzheimer's disease (AD) mice (Chen et al., 2021). This might be due to the fact that PGC-1α needs to enter the nucleus to perform its biological role. Nuclear respiratory factor 1 and TFAM are key regulators of enhance mitochondrial biogenesis. These results presented that dietary CSS up-regulated the expression of relative gene tfam as well as NRF1 and PGC-1α protein levels in sub-adult grass carp muscle. Besides, as expected, 0.50 mg/kg CSS improved the fluorescence intensity of TOMM20 by 2.03 times compared with the Se deficiency group. Translocase of the outer mitochondrial membrane complex subunit 20 is a key component of the mitochondrial outer membrane translocase complex, which is responsible for the transporting precursor proteins synthesized in the cytoplasm into the mitochondria (Yano et al., 2004). In mitochondria with higher mitochondrial membrane potential, the density of TOMM20 clusters is higher (Roche et al., 2020). Thus, dietary CSS at 0.50 to 0.75 mg/kg promoted mitochondrial biogenesis by elevating mitochondrial activity.

The dose and form of Se were of great significance to grass carp

With the widespread use of Se supplements, its potential toxicity of Se is often neglected. Excessive Se intake can induce toxicity through three primary mechanisms. First, excessive Se will form CH3Se− into the redox cycle, producing superoxide and oxidative stress, or forming free radicals (Hu et al., 2016). Previous reports have shown that excessive Se could lead to ROS production in juvenile grass carp (Liu et al., 2018) and juvenile yellow catfish (Hu et al., 2016). Second, the liver is the main organ for Se deposition and detoxification. Excessive Se could cause liver cell damage and necrosis. Liver damage and impaired lipolysis and glycolysis pathways were observed in zebrafish embryos fed high levels of Se (0.5 μmol/L) (Zhu et al., 2023). Additionally, in juvenile white sturgeon (Acipenser transmontanus), excessive Se led to vacuolar degeneration and liver necrosis (Tashjian et al., 2006). Third, the interaction between Se and sulfur-containing amino acids can inactivate some enzyme systems. Sub-adult grass carp fed 1.00 mg/kg dietary Se showed reduced protein synthesis and accelerated protein degradation, which was better than that of the control group. Given the above findings, it is of great importance to accurately control the supplementary dose of Se.

Moreover, the dietary Se requirement of sub-adult grass carp (687–1424 g) was estimated at 0.61 mg/kg (using CSS as Se source, Fig. 5), which is lower than the previously reported 0.83 mg/kg for juveniles (11.2–189.2 g) fed SS (Liu et al., 2018). This discrepancy may be attributed to the higher bioavailability of CSS compared to SS, as nutrient utilization depends on bioavailability (Khan et al., 2017). However, despite the improved bioavailability, CSS did not significantly enhance growth performance or muscle development compared to SS in grass carp. This contrasts with findings in ruminants, where CSS showed superior efficacy–likely due to differences in digestive physiology, particularly the complex rumen environment, which may optimize Se release and utilization (Liu et al., 2020). Interestingly, CSS significantly enhanced antioxidant defense and mitochondrial quality control mechanisms compared to SS, suggesting its potential benefits in promoting protein synthesis in fish muscle. Collectively, these results demonstrated that CSS is beneficial for sub-adult grass carp muscle metabolism.

Conclusion

Taken together, these data illustrated that dietary Se supplementation linearly increased Se levels in the hepatopancreas and muscle, which corresponds to higher expression of mitochondrial selenoprotein (GPX1), thereby enhancing the bound amino acid content, promoting myofiber hypertrophy and protein synthesis, while inhibiting protein degradation. The potential mechanisms involved GPX1-mediated (1) improvement of antioxidant capacity, (2) maintenance of mitochondrial homeostasis, and (3) suppression of autophagy-lysosome pathway and intracellular ROS production (Fig. 6). Furthermore, these results confirmed that dietary 0.75 mg/kg Se (CSS as the Se source) and 0.50 mg/kg Se (SS as the Se source) effectively enhance muscle growth in sub-adult grass carp.

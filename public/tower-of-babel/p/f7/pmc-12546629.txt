Identification of biomarkers associated with glycolysis and lactation in sarcopenia
Authors: Xinlei Luo, Jingjing Liu, Xiaoping Luo, Yunjia Li, Qiaojun Chen, Zewei He, Yanfa Lei, Shuting Li, Xiaowei Wang, Jun Jiang, Zhengchang Yang, Lirong Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Sarcopenia, characterized by the loss of muscle mass, is a primary contributor to frailty among the elderly population. In recent years, several studies have highlighted the relationship between sarcopenia and glycolysis as well as lactylation; however, the underlying mechanisms remain unclear. This study aims to investigate the mechanisms linking sarcopenia with glycolysis and lactylation through bioinformatics approaches, thereby providing insights for potential therapeutic strategies in managing related diseases. We obtained the sarcopenia dataset from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database, The data sets were performed differential analysis and weighted correlation network analysis (WGCNA) analysis. Glycolysis and lactylation-related genes were obtained using MSigDB database, and intersection with disease genes was performed to obtain hub genes. Additionally, CIBERSORT was employed to analyze immune cell infiltration of common genes, while single-cell RNA sequencing data was utilized to examine the expression distribution and intercellular communications of hub genes. Finally, we predicted the upstream miRNA of the MDH1 gene using the miRBase database and constructed a relationship network between miRNAs and hub genes utilizing Cytoscape software. Subsequently, we acquired a miRNA dataset related to sarcopenia from the GEO database for differential analysis in order to validate the upstream miRNA of the MDH1 gene. By conducting differential analysis, WGCNA analysis, and Venny screening analysis, we identified a hub gene: MDH1, which is involved in glycolysis and lactylation. Further investigation into the correlation between hub genes and immune cell infiltration revealed that MDH1 exhibited the most significant association with regulatory T cells (Tregs). Results from single-cell analysis indicated that the expression of the MDH1 gene was predominantly high in myonuclei and NKT cells. Through a search of the miRBase database, we predicted 29 upstream microRNAs associated with the MDH1 gene. Following differential analysis of the miRNA dataset, we ultimately identified hsa-miR-1263 as the upstream microRNA for MDH1. In this study, we propose that hsa-miR-1263 plays a regulatory role in the onset and progression of sarcopenia by mediating glycolysis and lactylation pathways, primarily through the MDH1 gene in myonuclei and NKT cells. This research introduces for the first time the relationship between the hsa-miR-1263/MDH1 axis and sarcopenia, offering new insights into the mechanisms underlying glycolysis and lactylation in sarcopenia.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-99818-8.

Introduction

Sarcopenia is defined as the progressive loss of muscle mass and strength. It is a multifaceted degenerative condition characterized by a reduction in both the volume and number of skeletal muscle fibers, particularly type II muscle fibers, alongside diminished muscle strength. Additionally, there is an increase in connective tissue and adipose tissue with advancing age, which correlates with high morbidity and mortality rates1. The onset of sarcopenia is closely associated with aging, which may impair anabolic signaling pathways that facilitate the synthesis of muscle components while inhibiting the regenerative capacity of damaged tissues2. Aging induces concurrent alterations in both muscle fiber composition and myonuclear characteristics, leading to a decline in type II muscle fibers while type I muscle fibers remain relatively stable. Glycolysis plays a crucial role in the development of sarcopenia. Skeletal muscle fibers exhibit diverse contractile properties and metabolic activities; specifically, type I fibers are referred to as slow-twitch fibers primarily involved in oxidative metabolism, whereas type II fibers are known as fast-twitch fibers predominantly reliant on glycolytic processes3. Enhanced glycolytic activity within skeletal muscles is associated with an increase in both the quantity of type II muscle fibers and overall fast-twitch mass characteristics4. Resistance training effectively promotes rapid ATP production for muscular contraction through enhanced glycolysis, thereby serving as a viable intervention against sarcopenia5.

Recent studies have identified a form of post-translational modification known as “lactylation” as a crucial factor in regulating cellular functions, including those related to skeletal muscle6,7. lactylation represents a novel epigenetic modification induced by lactic acid and is implicated in various biological processes, such as metabolism and gene expression8,9. Lactic acid has long been associated with muscle metabolism, particularly within the context of anaerobic exercise and fatigue. This association provides new insights into how metabolic by-products influence muscle biology. This modification may be especially relevant to sarcopenia, given that altered lactate metabolism is believed to contribute to sarcopenia in aging populations and individuals with chronic diseases. The relationship between sarcopenia and glycolysis, lactylation is intricate; thus, further research is necessary to elucidate their interconnections.

Methods

Data sources

We referred to a large number of similar studies and selected GEO data for analysis. The GEO database (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) mainly stores gene expression data generated by various biological experimental techniques. We selected sarcopenia related data for analysis (Table 1).

Data sources.

Differential gene expression in sarcopenia

We obtained gene expression datasets from patients with sarcopenia through the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). Ultimately, the GSE1428 dataset was selected for differential gene expression analysis and enrichment analysis. This series comprised 10 young male and 12 older male. The differentially expressed genes were analyzed by GO and KEGG10,11.

WGCNA analysis of sarcopenia genes

To verify the reliability of differential genes associated with sarcopenia, we obtained the gene expression dataset of patients diagnosed with sarcopenia (GSE111016) from the GEO database for WGCNA analysis. We subsequently explored the correlation between these genes and the disease12. This dataset is based on a study conducted by Zhong13 and includes muscle tissue samples from 20 patients with sarcopenia and 20 healthy controls.

Acquisition of genes involved in Glycolysis and lactylation

The MSigDB database was utilized to identify genes related to glycolysis and lactylation. By employing the keywords “Glycolysis” and “lactylation” we gathered data on relevant pathway genes. Additionally, we conducted a search for published studies using the same keywords to further identify genes related to glycolysis and lactylation. The genes obtained through these two approaches were subsequently combined. Venny 2.1.0 software was employed to determine the intersection of differential genes of sarcopenia, glycolysis-related genes, and lactylation-related genes.

Immune infiltration analysis

To evaluate the abundance of immune infiltrates, we analyzed the gene expression matrix from the GSE111016 dataset for immune cell infiltration using CIBERSORT. We defined a significance threshold of p < 0.05 to indicate the presence of an infiltrating matrix composed of immune cells. Furthermore, differentially infiltrating immune cells were identified, and Pearson correlation analysis was conducted on the expression levels of hub genes to investigate the relationship between differentially infiltrating immune cells and hub genes.

Single-cell analysis of sarcopenia

A single-cell dataset pertaining to sarcopenia was obtained from the GEO database. We conducted a search using the keywords “sarcopenia” and “Single-Cell,” ultimately selecting isolated monocytes from mouse skeletal muscle for analysis due to conditional limitation. The dataset was GSE172410 and consisted of single cells isolated from skeletal muscle of young (6 months, n = 3) and old (24 months, n = 3) mouse. We utilized the “Seurat” software package for single-cell analysis, ensuring the exclusion of low-quality cells. To identify distinct cell populations, we employed the “Single R” software package for further analysis.

miRNA-mRNA network prediction

The upstream miRNAs of the MDH1 gene were predicted using the miRBase database (https://mirbase.org/), and a miRNA-mRNA network was constructed utilizing Cytoscape software. The miRNA dataset related to sarcopenia (GSE23527) was sourced from the GEO database for differential expression analysis. This dataset comprised skeletal muscle samples from 36 male participants, including 19 young adults (age: 31 ± 2 years) and 17 older adults (age: 73 ± 3 years). Finally, the intersection of predicted miRNAs and differentially expressed miRNAs was identified using Venny 2.1.0 software as the upstream molecules of the MDH1 gene.

Results

Differential gene expression and enrichment analysis in sarcopenia

A total of 2379 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified using the limma R software package based on the sarcopenia dataset (GSE1428). Among these, 27 genes were found to be up-regulated, while 2352 were down-regulated (P < 0.05) (Fig. 1). The heatmap illustrates the distribution of both up-regulated and down-regulated DEGs (Fig. 2). Enrichment analysis aids in elucidating the biological functions of differentially expressed genes in patients with sarcopenia. Through Gene Ontology (GO) analysis, we identified that the biological processes associated with differentially expressed genes in sarcopenia patients encompassed cellular respiration, energy derivation by oxidation of organic compounds, purine ribonucleotide metabolic proces, among others. The primary cellular components involved included mitochondrial protein-containing complex, mitochondrial inner membrane, inner mitochondrial membrane protein comple. Furthermore, the main molecular functions identified were proton transmembrane transporter activity, proton transmembrane transporter activity, active monoatomic ion transmembrane transporter activity (Fig. 3). KEGG enrichment analysis revealed that the differentially expressed genes were predominantly enriched in pathways associated with Pathways of neurodegeneration-multiple diseases, Alzheimer disease, Huntington diseas, among others (Fig. 4).

WGCNA analysis of sarcopenia genes

We conducted a weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) on the GSE111016 dataset to identify key gene modules and pivotal genes. For further analysis, we set the parameter β to 4. Based on criteria of p < 0.05 and |cor| >0.3, we identified that the MEgreenyellow, MEyellow, and MElightgreen modules exhibited significant associations with sarcopenia (Fig. 5).

Identification of hub genes

We identified 306 glycolysis-related genes from the MSigDB database, and 1124 genes associated with lactylation were obtained through both the MSigDB database and relevant literature14. We identified the common genes of sarcopenia differential genes, glycolysis and lactase related genes as hub genes (Fig. 6).

Immune infiltration analysis

The infiltration of immune cells in 40 samples was analyzed using CIBERSORT, and the proportions of these immune cells were illustrated through stacked plots (Fig. 7). Our analysis revealed that the predominant types of immune cells included Macrophages M2, Mast cells resting, B cells memory (Fig. 8). Notably, the infiltration of NK cells activated was significantly increased in the sarcopenia group (Fig. 9). The correlations among different immune cell types are depicted in a heat map (Fig. 10). Additionally, we demonstrated the relationship between hub genes and immune cell infiltration via heat maps (Fig. 11), with results indicating that MDH1 exhibited the most significant correlation with T cells regulatory (P < 0.05) (Tregs).

Single-cell analysis of sarcopenia

We conducted an analysis of a single-cell RNA sequencing sample related to sarcopenia from GSE172410, annotating the cells and identifying their division into 10 distinct subsets. These subsets include endothelial cells, FAPS, myonuclei, pericytes, macrophages, neurons, satellite cells, tenocytes, NKT cells, and neutrophils (Fig. 12). Subsequently, we analyzed the expression of hub genes across these cell subsets. The results indicated that MDH1 was highly expressed in both myonuclei and NKT cells (P < 0.05) (Fig. 13).

Construction of miRNA-mRNA network

We utilized the miRBase database to predict 29 upstream miRNAs of MDH1 and constructed a miRNA-mRNA network map using Cytoscape 3.6.0 software (Fig. 14). Through the analysis of the GSE23527 dataset, we identified 26 differentially expressed miRNAs, among which 4 were significantly differentially expressed in GPL10358 (Fig. 15), while 23 were significantly differentially expressed in GPL10415 (P < 0.05) (Fig. 16). 1 miRNA that was duplicated across both platforms was excluded from further analysis. Utilizing Venny version 2.1.0 for our analysis, we determined that the upstream miRNA associated with the MDH1 gene in sarcopenia is hsa-miR-1263 (Fig. 17).

Discussion

Sarcopenia is a skeletal muscle disease that significantly impacts human life and health. It is closely associated with increased mortality15. The prevalence of sarcopenia ranges from 10 to 27%, with a higher incidence observed in individuals over the age of 6016,17. This condition can easily lead to joint and bone damage, serving as a critical factor contributing to dysfunction and disability18. Sarcopenia is implicated in various diseases, including liver cirrhosis, cardiovascular disease, kidney disease, and even severe cases of cardiopulmonary failure that may result in death19–21. Despite its significance, there remains a substantial gap in research on sarcopenia. Some scholars have suggested that glycolysis and lactylation play pivotal roles in muscle metabolism among patients suffering from this condition. However, the relevant biomarkers and molecular mechanisms remain poorly understood. Therefore, further investigation into these mechanisms is particularly crucial for the prevention and treatment of sarcopenia.

To investigate the mechanisms underlying sarcopenia, glycolysis and lactylation, we first identified their hub genes. We conducted a differential analysis using the GEO dataset of patients with sarcopenia. To further validate the reliability of these genes, we also performed WGCNA analysis and obtained the intersection of genes associated with sarcopenia, glycolysis and lactylation. Our analysis revealed MDH1 as a hub gene. To further examine the expression distribution of this hub gene and its implications for cell-cell communication, we carried out single-cell analyses. The results indicated that MDH1 was significantly overexpressed in myonuclei and NKT cells derived from sarcopenia patients. This suggests that MDH1 may play a regulatory role in glycolysis and lactylation within myonuclei and NKT cells. Next, we aimed to further elucidate the key role of upstream signaling molecules associated with the MDH1 gene. Utilizing the miRBase database, we predicted a total of 29 miRNAs that exhibited regulatory relationships with MDH1. Subsequently, we selected miRNA datasets from the GEO database for differential analysis. Through comparison with the predicted miRNAs, we ultimately identified hsa-miR-1263 as the upstream signaling molecule of the MDH1 gene. This study proposes, for the first time, a relationship between the hsa-miR-1263/MDH1 axis and sarcopenia.

Malate dehydrogenase 1 (MDH1), which is regulated by serine 189 (S189), serves as a crucial enzyme in the glycolytic metabolic pathway. It plays a significant role in the malate-aspartate shuttle, thereby coordinating glycolysis and mitochondrial respiration. Glycolysis can also be facilitated by the interaction between oxaloacetic acid and malic acid through their reversible coenzyme system of NAD+/NADH22–24. In this metabolic pathway, MDH1 catalyzes the conversion of oxaloacetate (OAA) to malate, accompanied by the interplay of NAD + and NADH25. Notably, MDH1 is involved in oxidative stress regulation by modulating the NADPH/NADP + ratio and cellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) activity. Furthermore, it has been closely associated with various diseases such as aging and somatic growth retardation. This relationship may represent an additional molecular mechanism through which MDH1 regulates sarcopenia25,26.

miRNA, a small single-stranded non-coding RNA molecule encoded by endogenous genes within the cellular genome, can bind to Argonaute (AGO) protein molecules to form the miRNA-induced silencing complex (miRISC), which acts on target genes. miRNAs are regarded as one of the most promising classes of small molecules for targeted diagnosis and therapy. They have the capacity to target approximately 60% of human genes and regulate growth and development by either inhibiting or promoting mRNA expression27–29. Recent studies have demonstrated that has-miRNAs play a crucial role in regulating age-related muscle pathophysiological changes, including skeletal muscle cell proliferation, differentiation, apoptosis, and senescence30,31. Although the relationship between miRNA and sarcopenia has been established32, this study primarily analyzes the upstream miRNAs associated with key glycolytic and lactylation genes. Furthermore, it proposes a novel association between hsa-miR-1263 and sarcopenia, thereby providing a new therapeutic target for its treatment.

Although our study is the first to propose a relationship between the hsa-miR-1263/MDH1 axis and sarcopenia, thereby providing new insights into its treatment, several limitations must be acknowledged. Firstly, our findings are based on bioinformatics predictions that require validation through extensive experimental studies. Secondly, the association between the hsa-miR-1263/MDH1 axis and sarcopenia remains a preliminary hypothesis; further experimental investigations are necessary to elucidate the specific mechanisms involved. Additionally, due to resource constraints, we conducted our predictions using single cells from mice. Future experiments will involve extracting single cells from human subjects for verification purposes. In subsequent research endeavors, we will address these limitations systematically in order to enhance the reliability of our findings.

Conclusions

Our study elucidates the correlation among glycolysis, lactylation and sarcopenia, suggesting that this relationship may be mediated by the MDH1 gene encoded by hsa-miR-1263 in myonuclei and NKT cells. This is the first proposal of a connection between the hsa-miR-1263/MDH1 axis and sarcopenia, offering a novel target for the prevention and treatment of this condition.

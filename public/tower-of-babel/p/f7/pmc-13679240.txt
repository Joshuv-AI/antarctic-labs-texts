FOLFOX-HAIC plus regorafenib for the treatment of unresectable colorectal liver metastases refractory to first and second-line systemic chemotherapy
Authors: Jiacheng Liu, Songlin Song, Wei Yao, Yanqiao Ren, Yiming Liu, Chuansheng Zheng, Bin Liang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHepatic arterial infusion chemotherapy (HAIC) is a potentially attractive locoregional therapy for patients with unresectable colorectal liver metastases (CRLM). This study aimed to evaluate the safety and efficacy of FOLFOX-HAIC plus regorafenib in the treatment of unresectable CRLM refractory to first and second-line systemic chemotherapy.MethodsA total of 35 patients with unresectable CRLM who underwent FOLFOX-HAIC plus regorafenib between July 2020 and January 2023 were included in this retrospective study. The objective response rate (ORR), disease-controlled rate (DCR), progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS) and adverse event were assessed. Factors affecting OS were analyzed.ResultsKaplan–Meier curves showed that the median OS1 (measured from the time of diagnosis), OS2 (measured from the time of the first HAIC treatment) and PFS (measured from the time of the first HAIC treatment) were 29.0 (95%CI, 18.9–39.1) months, 21.0 (95%CI, 14.9–27.1) months and 13.0 (95%CI, 9.3–16.7) months, respectively. The ORRs at 3 months, 6 months and 12 months after HAIC were 57.1%, 45.7% and 22.9%, respectively, and the DCRs were 88.6%, 80.0% and 51.4%, respectively. No grade 5 adverse events (AEs) (death) were observed. Common Regorafenib-related AEs were hand-foot skin reaction (n = 18, 51.4%) and fatigue (n = 15, 42.9%). Common HAIC-related AEs were weight loss (n = 28, 80.0%), abdominal pain (n = 19, 54.3%) and nausea (n = 15, 42.9%).ConclusionsFOLFOX-HAIC plus regorafenib might be beneficial and tolerable in the treatment of unresectable CRLM refractory to first and second-line systemic chemotherapy.Keywords: Hepatic arterial infusion chemotherapy, Colorectal liver metastases, Regorafenib, Progression-free survival, Overall survival

Introduction

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is one of the most common malignancies, accounting for 10% of cancer incidence and 9.4% of cancer deaths worldwide [1, 2]. The liver is the most common site of distant metastasis of CRC and the leading cause of death in CRC patients is liver metastases [3]. In patients with colorectal liver metastases (CRLM), approximately 25% developed synchronous metastases and 50% occurred metachronous metastases [4].

Radical section is widely accepted as the preferred regimen for patients with CRLM with 5-year overall survival (OS) ranging from 34 to 36% [4]. Unfortunately, however, it is only suitable for 10% to 20% of patients due to the presence of advanced disease at diagnosis [5]. The prognosis for unresectable CRLM is generally poor, and the treatment options remain extremely controversial [6]. Therefore, there is a need for a reasonable regimen for the treatment of unresectable CRLM.

Over the past decade, effective systemic and locoregional treatment regimens for CRLM have been proposed. Hepatic arterial infusion chemotherapy (HAIC) is a potentially attractive locoregional therapy for patients with unresectable CRLM [7]. The results of several meta-analyses have shown that HAIC significantly improved the OS and response rate of patients with unresectable CRLM compared with systemic chemotherapy [8, 9]. In most previous studies, the chemotherapy regimen for HAIC was based on floxuridine (FUDR). Currently, there is only a small amount of data on the application of FOLFOX-HAIC in CRLM, and the conclusions are conflicting [10, 11]. Regorafenib is a small-molecule inhibitor of multiple tyrosine kinases that are involved with various processes including tumor growth and angiogenesis. Regorafenib was found to have activity in patients who progressed on standard therapy, and has been approved as a third-line and later-line treatment of patients with metastatic CRC [12].

HAIC delivers high local concentrations of chemotherapy directly to liver metastases, while regorafenib systemically inhibits multiple tyrosine kinases involved in tumor growth and angiogenesis. The combination may overcome compensatory angiogenic pathways activated after HAIC-induced hypoxia. Thus, HAIC and regorafenib might exert a synergistic antitumor effect in the treatment of unresectable CRLM. However, there is no clinical evidence to confirm the efficiency of the combination treatment regimen.

This study retrospectively analyzed the safety and efficacy of FOLFOX-HAIC plus regorafenib in the treatment of unresectable CRLM refractory to first and second-line systemic chemotherapy.

Materials and methods

The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Union Hospital, Tongji Medical College, Huazhong University of Science and Technology (UHCT-IEC-SOP-016-03-01). In accordance with the declaration of Helsinki, and the informed consent was waived by the Institutional Review Board.

Patients

This retrospective cohort study included 35 consecutive patients with unresectable CRLM who underwent FOLFOX-HAIC plus regorafenib in Union Hospital, Tongji Medical College, Huazhong University of Science and Technology, between July 2020 and January 2023. All of the patients had progressed in CRLM on standard first and second-line therapy before receiving the combination treatment of HAIC and regorafenib [13]. All patients had documented disease progression following standard first- and second‑line systemic chemotherapy for metastatic colorectal cancer. The most common prior regimens consisted of fluoropyrimidine‑based combinations: 30 patients (85.7%) received FOLFOX or FOLFIRI in combination with either bevacizumab (n = 28) or cetuximab (in RAS wild‑type tumors, n = 2). The remaining 5 patients (14.3%) had received other fluoropyrimidine‑containing doublets (e.g., CAPOX) with targeted agents. All patients met the inclusion criterion of being refractory to both first‑ and second‑line systemic therapy, and none had received more than two prior lines of cytotoxic chemotherapy before initiating HAIC plus regorafenib.

The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) age of ≥ 18 years; (2) pathologically or clinically diagnosed unresectable CRLM; (3) refractory to first and second-line systemic chemotherapy; (4) Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status (PS) score of 0–2; (5) Child-Pugh grade A or B; (6) underwent HAIC combined with regorafenib.

The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) incomplete data; (2) combined with other malignant tumors; (3) lost to follow-up.

HAIC procedure

As described in a previous study [14], a 5 F catheter was inserted through the right femoral artery by the Seldinger method. After localization of the CRLM, a 5-F heparin-coated polyurethane catheter (Braun Medical, France) was placed at a depth of the gastroduodenal artery (3 to 5 cm from the origin) to avoid dislocation of the catheter tip, and a side hole (2 to 3 mm in longitudinal direction) was made at the level of the common hepatic artery with scissors. The other end of the catheter was connected to the injection port, which was implanted in a subcutaneous pocket created in the right thigh. The gastroduodenal artery and the right gastric artery were occluded with steel coils to prevent gastroduodenal injury by the chemotherapeutic agents.

When the blood supply of CRLM stemmed partly from the extrahepatic artery (e.g., replaced/accessory right hepatic artery from the superior mesenteric artery, replaced/accessory left hepatic artery from the left gastric artery and other extrahepatic collateral vessels), the artery was first embolized with coils to achieve the redistribution of the whole hepatic artery perfusion from multiple arteries to a single artery. This step ensured effective hepatic intra-arterial infusion through a single infusion catheter.

In the present study, we used an mFOLFOX6 regimen for HAIC. (Oxaliplatin: 85 mg/m2 for 2 h on day 1; Calcium folinate: 200 mg/m2 for 2 h on day 1; 5-Fu: 400 mg/m2 for bolus on day 1, followed by 2400 mg/m2 for 46 h).

Regorafenib

The starting dose of regorafenib was determined by the physician in accordance with the patients’ treatment status. Regorafenib therapy commenced 5 days following the administration of HAIC and was paused for 2 days prior to the subsequent HAIC treatment [15].

Evaluation of treatment

After HAIC procedures, the patients underwent contrast-enhanced CT or MR imaging follow-ups 6 weeks apart. The tumor response was evaluated according to the modified Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumours (RECIST1.1) [16], including complete response (CR), partial response (PR), stable disease (SD) and progressive disease (PD). The objective response rate (ORR) was defined as the CR rate plus PR rate. The disease-controlled rate (DCR) was defined as the CR rate, PR rate plus SD rate. At the same time, biomarkers for tumor response (CEA and CA19-9) were collected to assess efficacy after the treatment.

OS1 was defined as the time from the date of CRLM diagnosis to the date of any cause of death or the date of the last follow-up, OS2 was defined as the time from the first HAIC treatment to the date of any cause of death or the date of the last follow-up. Progression-free survival (PFS) was defined as the time from onset of HAIC to either disease progression or death.

The regorafenib- and HAIC-related adverse events (AEs) were graded according to the graded with the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (NCI‑CTCAE) (version 5.0) [17].

Statistical analyses

All statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.2.1) or IBM SPSS (version 22.0). Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation and compared using the Student’s t test. Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies (percentages) and compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Survival endpoints, including overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS), were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method, and differences between groups were compared by log-rank tests. Univariable and multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression models were used to identify factors independently associated with survival outcomes. Variables with a P value < 0.10 in the univariable analysis were included in the initial multivariable Cox regression model. A backward stepwise selection procedure (with a removal criterion of P ≥ 0.10) was then applied to derive the final model. The presence of multicollinearity among variables in the multivariable models was assessed using variance inflation factors (VIF), with a VIF < 2.0 considered acceptable. Results from the Cox models were reported as hazard ratios (HR) with their corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CI). All P values were two-sided, and a P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

A total of 35 patients with unresectable CRLM who underwent FOLFOX-HAIC plus regorafenib were included in this retrospective study, including 28 (80.0%) males and 7 (20.0%) females, with a mean age of 57.2 ± 8.8 years. Of these patients, 11 (31.4%) were defined as synchronous metastases, and 15 (42.9%) had carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) > 10 µg/L. Detailed patient characteristics are shown in Table 1.

Treatment profiles

Patients received a median number of four (range 2–11) sessions of HAIC procedures. The initial dose of regorafenib was 160 mg/d in all patients, of which 3 (8.6%), 10 (28.6%) and 4 (11.4%) patients were reduced to 120, 80, and 40 mg/day due to adverse effects within 1 month, and 3 (8.6%) patients stopped regorafenib due to intolerance.

Tumor response

At 3 months after the first HAIC, 20 (57.1%) and 11 (31.4%) patients achieved PR and SD, respectively. At 6 months after the first HAIC, 16 (45.7%) and 12 (34.3%) patients achieved PR and SD, respectively. At 12 months after the first HAIC, 8 (22.9%) and 10 (28.6%) patients achieved PR and SD. The ORRs at 3 months, 6 months and 12 months after HAIC was 57.1%, 45.7% and 22.9%, respectively, and the DCRs were 88.6%, 80.0% and 51.4%, respectively. (Fig. 2) (Table 2).

Dynamic changes of serum tumor markers

As shown in Fig. 3, the dynamic changes of tumor markers were evaluated before HAIC and at 3, 6, and 12 months after HAIC. The results showed that the mean levels of CEA and CA19-9 decreased significantly at 3 and 6 months after HAIC and remained stable at 12 months after HAIC.

OS and PFS

Patients were followed up until July 2024, and the median follow-up time was 19 (range, 5–35) months. During the follow-up period, 28 (80.0%) patients experienced disease progression and 22 (62.9%) patients died. As shown in Fig. 4, the Kaplan–Meier curve was used to assess OS1, OS2 and PFS for all patients. The median OS1, OS2 and PFS were 29.0 (95%CI, 18.9–39.1) months, 21.0 (95%CI, 14.9–27.1) months and 13.0 (95%CI, 9.3–16.7) months, respectively.

Kaplan-Meier curves of OS1 (measured from the time of diagnosis) (A), OS2 (measured from the time of the first HAIC treatment) (B) and PFS (measured from the time of the first HAIC treatment) (C)

In this study, 18 patients maintained the full dose (160 mg/day) throughout. 10 patients had dose reduction (120 mg/day or lower) due to adverse events. 3 patients discontinued regorafenib within the first month due to intolerance. Subgroup analysis showed no statistically significant difference in PFS or OS between the full-dose and dose-reduction groups (P > 0.05). The 3 patients who discontinued regorafenib early were excluded from efficacy analysis but included in safety analysis.

In addition, we performed a subgroup analysis comparing patients who had prior FOLFOX (n = 28) vs. those who had not (n = 7). No significant difference in PFS or OS was observed (P > 0.05).

Univariable and multivariable Cox regression models were performed to identify the risk factors associated with OS and PFS. The results showed that ascites (HR = 5.640, 95% CI = 1.204–30.176, P = 0.033) and CEA > 10 µg/L (HR = 11.49, 95% CI = 1.232–88.758, P = 0.012) were identified as the independent risk factors for OS (Table 3). As for PFS, the independent risk factors were age (HR = 0.876, 95% CI = 0.785–0.980, P = 0.048), ascites (HR = 4.764, 95% CI = 1.119–44.329, P = 0.040) and CEA > 10 µg/L (HR = 6.759, 95% CI = 1.223–41.375, P = 0.036) (Table 3).

Adverse events

Regorafenib and HAIC-related adverse events (AEs) were shown in Tables 4 and 5, respectively, and no grade 5 AEs (death) were observed. Regorafenib-related AEs occurred in 28 (82.9%) patients, of which 6 (17.1%) were grade 3 to 4, with the most common AEs being hand-foot skin reaction (n = 18, 51.4%) and fatigue (n = 15, 42.9%). There were 32 (91.4%) patients developed HAIC related AEs, of which 8 (22.9%) were grade 3 to 4, and the most common AEs were weight loss (n = 28, 80.0%), abdominal pain (n = 19, 54.3%), nausea (n = 15, 42.9%), fatigue (n = 13, 37.1%), and hyperbilirubinaemia (n = 11, 31.4%).

Discussion

In this study, we evaluated the efficacy and safety of FOLFOX-HAIC plus regorafenib in the treatment of unresectable CRLM. The results showed that the ORRs at 3 months, 6 months, and 12 months after HAIC plus regorafenib were 57.1%, 45.7% and 22.9%, respectively, and the DCRs were 88.6%, 80.0% and 51.4%, respectively. In addition, The median OS1 (measured from the time of diagnosis), OS2 (measured from the time of the first HAIC treatment) and PFS (measured from the time of the first HAIC treatment) were 29.0 (95%CI, 18.9–39.1) months, 21.0 (95%CI, 14.9–27.1) months and 13.0 (95%CI, 9.3–16.7) months, respectively. These results demonstrate the great potential of FOLFOX-HAIC plus regorafenib in the treatment of unresectable CRLM, which is worthy of clinical promotion.

The results shown in our study were superior to those observed in the CORRECT trial (phase III RCT), which showed that the median OS of regorafenib monotherapy for previously treated metastatic colorectal cancer was 6.4 months [18]. In another phase III RCT (CONCUR), regorafenib plus best supportive care was used for the treatment of patients with previously treated metastatic colorectal cancer, and the median OS reached 8.8 months [19]. In both of the above studies, regorafenib significantly increased OS, PFS, and DCR when compared to placebo. Survival benefits of 1.4 and 2.5 months were presented [20]. Our population differed from those in the regorafenib monotherapy trials (CORRECT/CONCUR), which included patients with widespread systemic progression after multiple treatment lines, whereas our cohort had liver‑dominant metastases refractory to only first‑ and second‑line therapy. This earlier introduction of combined modality treatment in patients with liver‑confined disease may explain the longer survival observed here. Our study showed that regorafenib plus HAIC could bring more survival benefits than regorafenib monotherapy, which proves that HAIC could play an important role in the treatment of patients with previously treated CRLM.

HAIC has developed as an attractive strategy for the treatment of unresectable CRLM [21]. The main blood supply of liver metastases with a diameter > 3 mm is from hepatic arteries, whereas normal hepatocytes are supplied primarily by the portal vein [22]. Thus, hepatic artery chemoembolization allows for selective delivery of the drug into the tumor with the relative preservation of the normal parenchyma, which provides much of the rationale for liver-directed HAIC in the management of CRLM [23]. Compared with systemic chemotherapy, HAIC allows direct and continuous delivery of anticancer drugs to tumors located in the liver, increasing local concentrations of anticancer drugs in tumors, and reducing systemic adverse events caused by anticancer drugs [24]. Although some serious adverse events associated with HAIC have been observed in previous studies, including thrombocytopenia and deterioration of liver function [25]. However, in our study, no grade 5 AEs (death) were observed, and some grade 1 to 2 AEs, including nausea, abdominal pain, weight loss, fatigue, and hyperbilirubinemia, were found during the perioperative period and did not affect the quality of life of patients.

The feasibility of HAIC plus sorafenib/regorafenib in the treatment of liver cancer has been fully confirmed, which is the basis for the possibility that HAIC plus regorafenib may have a similar rationale and mechanism for the treatment of CRLM [26, 27]. The mechanistic rationale for combining HAIC with regorafenib is underpinned by complementary modes of action targeting both the local tumor microenvironment and systemic angiogenic pathways. HAIC delivers high concentrations of chemotherapeutic agents directly to liver metastases via their predominant arterial supply, thereby maximizing local cytotoxicity while sparing normal hepatic parenchyma [22, 23]. This locoregional intensification can induce rapid tumor necrosis and hypoxia, which in turn may upregulate pro‑angiogenic factors such as vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) as part of a compensatory survival response. Regorafenib, an oral multi‑kinase inhibitor, concurrently blocks VEGF receptors 1–3 and other key tyrosine kinases involved in tumor angiogenesis, stromal remodeling, and oncogenesis [12, 28]. By inhibiting this hypoxia‑driven angiogenic escape, regorafenib may potentiate the antitumor effect of HAIC and delay the emergence of resistance. Furthermore, the sequential administration of regorafenib—typically in a 3‑week‑on/1‑week‑off schedule—aligns conveniently with the cyclic nature of HAIC procedures, facilitating practical clinical integration. Thus, the combination leverages both locoregional chemotherapy intensification and systemic anti‑angiogenic suppression, offering a synergistic strategy for controlling liver‑dominant metastatic disease. In addition, according to the REGONIVO trial, the combination of regorafenib provides a follow-up option for combination with a PD-1 inhibitor [29].

We also analyzed the risk factors associated with OS and PFS. The results showed that CEA > 10 µg/L was independently associated with death and disease progression. Several studies by Nicholson BD et al. recommended a cut-off value of 10 ug/L for CEA and found a sensitivity of 68% and a specificity of 97% for colorectal cancer recurrence [30, 31]. Therefore, patients with baseline CEA of more than 10 µg/L for HAIC require critical attention. Fortunately, we found that both tumor markers, including CEA, were gradually reduced after the treatment of HAIC plus regorafenib, which also constitutes strong evidence for HAIC plus regorafenib to improve the survival of unresectable CRLM. In addition, ascites has also been found to be independently associated with death and disease progression, suggesting that clinicians should take proactive measures to control ascites, thus improving the survival of patients with CRLM after HAIC.

In our cohort, a notable proportion of patients required regorafenib dose reduction (17/35, 48.6%) or early discontinuation (3/35, 8.6%) due to adverse events, primarily hand-foot skin reaction and fatigue. Subgroup analysis, however, did not reveal a statistically significant difference in PFS or OS between patients who maintained the full dose (160 mg/day) and those who underwent dose reduction. This observation suggests that the anti-tumor activity of regorafenib in this combination setting may be preserved even at lower doses, possibly due to its broad therapeutic window or the predominant role of HAIC in driving tumor response within the liver. It is also plausible that patients requiring dose reduction represent a group with poorer tolerance or more advanced disease, introducing a selection bias that may confound direct efficacy comparisons.

There are some limitations to this study. Firstly, this was a retrospective, single-arm study with some selection bias. Secondly, not all patients were followed for the development of endpoint events, which might lead to an underestimation of the efficacy of HAIC.

In conclusion, we found that FOLFOX-HAIC plus regorafenib was beneficial and tolerable in the treatment of unresectable CRLM refractory to first and second-line systemic chemotherapy. HAIC plus regorafenib seems more appropriate to be a third-line of therapy in patients with unresectable CRLM than regorafenib monotherapy. Of course, these conclusions require further validation through randomized controlled trials.

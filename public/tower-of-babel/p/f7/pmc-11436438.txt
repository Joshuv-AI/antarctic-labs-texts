Knowledge mapping of frailty and surgery: a bibliometric and visualized analysis
Authors: Zhiwei Guo, Feifei Wang, Jiacheng Xu, Zhonggui Shan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeFrailty is common in surgical patients and is closely associated with postoperative outcomes.AimsThis study employed bibliometric methods to summarize and analyze research related to frailty and surgery, comprehensively analyzing the research structure and providing visualized maps.MethodsThis study analyzed the volume of publications, countries, institutions, authors, journals, references, and keywords related to perioperative frailty in the Web of Science Core Collection from 1978 to 2024. Visual bibliometric analyses were conducted from multiple perspectives, including collaboration networks, citation analysis, and keyword clustering.ResultsFrom 1978 to 2024, 21,879 authors from 95 countries and regions published 4,119 papers on perioperative frailty in 973 journals worldwide. The United States has the most publications, while Italy has the highest degree of international collaboration. The University of California System has the highest number of publications. The University of Kansas Medical Center is the institution with the highest centrality. The top nine authors in terms of publication volume are all from the USA. Bowers Christian A. is the most prolific author. The Journal of Vascular Surgery is the journal with the most publications. Current research directions include preoperative risk assessment of frailty, the relationship between frailty and postoperative complications, elderly frailty, and the relationship between frailty and sarcopenia. Research hotspots include risk stratification, postoperative delirium, the elderly, and sarcopenia.ConclusionThis study has identified the research hotspots and trends in perioperative frailty. Our findings will enable researchers to understand this field’s knowledge structure better and identify future research directions.Keywords: Elderly, Frailty, Surgery, Bibliometric analysis

Introduction

Fried first introduced five phenotypes of frailty in 2001: weight loss, grip strength decline, slow walking speed, reduced physical activity, and fatigue. Frailty is a non-specific condition characterized by increased vulnerability to stressors and accelerated loss of functional reserves associated with aging, involving multiple physiological systems and resulting in various frailty phenotypes [1–3]. The consensus published in 2012 suggests that the frailty index not only includes physical health but also emphasizes mental health and cognitive function [4]. The prevalence of frailty increases with age, and there is significant variation in prevalence rates among different studies (4–59%) [5]. Frailty is highly prevalent among the elderly population, with a prevalence rate of approximately 4.9–28% in individuals aged 65 and older [6]. Due to reduced physiological reserves across multiple systems, frail elderly individuals have poor stress tolerance, often resulting in delirium and poor prognosis [7]. Several studies have found a correlation between frailty, prolonged hospital stays, and higher hospital costs [8]. Frailty is a dynamic process that serves as a precursor to clinical risk events, reflecting patients’ health status and predicting adverse outcomes [9]. Frailty has become an important prognostic marker for increased mortality after cardiac surgery [10]. In spinal surgery, frailty has been identified as an independent predictor of postoperative complication incidence and mortality [11, 12]. Consistent evidence indicates that preoperative frailty assessment is essential and can offer prognostic information [13, 14].

In recent years, the injuries and complications caused by surgical procedures have attracted widespread attention. The relationship between frailty and surgery is a significant concern and discussion topic. Currently, frailty has been proven to be a critical indicator of perioperative risk [15]. However, the relationship between frailty and surgery and its underlying mechanisms remains incompletely understood. More knowledge is needed regarding the role of frailty in adverse outcomes among surgical patients to optimize clinical decisions further and accelerate postoperative recovery for patients.

Bibliometrics is an emerging interdisciplinary field based on statistics and mathematics. It primarily analyzes the progress and dynamic changes within specific research fields. Using bibliometric tools, statistical analysis, quantification, and visualization were employed to explore the developmental process, current status, and hotspots of perioperative frailty within the included publications. This approach allows us to present a rich dataset and comprehensive visuals that collectively provide insights into the current state of research on frailty and surgery. By analyzing publication volumes, author and country contributions, citation patterns, and keyword clusters, we offered a data-driven perspective that highlights the most significant research directions and emerging topics within the field. We aimed to offer a balanced and focused exploration of the relationship between frailty and surgical outcomes. We hope our findings will enhance the understanding among clinical practitioners and researchers regarding the evolution of this process and provide meaningful insights into the future directions of perioperative frailty.

Methods

Data sources and retrieval strategy

The study downloaded data on perioperative frailty from the Web of Science Core Collection (WOSCC) for bibliometric analysis. The retrieval strategy is as follows: (TS=(frailty) OR TS=(frail) OR TS=(frail*)) AND (TS=(perioperative) OR TS=(postoperative) OR TS=(intraoperative) OR TS=(preoperative) OR TS=(perisurgical) OR TS=(postsurgical) OR TS=(intrasurgical)). The types of documents selected were articles and reviews with no language restrictions. The data were exported in plain text file format, and record content was set to full records and cited references. A total of 4,134 documents were downloaded. After screening by two researchers, 15 irrelevant and duplicate documents were excluded, resulting in 4,119 documents being included.

Data analysis and visualization

Microsoft Excel 2021, Scimago Graphica, Citespace 6.2.4.0, Bibliometrix, and Vosviewer 1.6.16.0 were used to perform visual analysis on annual publication volume, cumulative publication volume, countries, authors, institutions, subject terms, keywords, and references. Excel was used to construct a cumulative publication volume fitting curve. Scimago Graphica software was applied to select the top 29 countries by publication volume, with the layout set to map and bundling on set to edge weight. Citespace was set with a time range of 1978–2024, with a time slice of 3 years. Node types were selected as institution, keyword, and reference, respectively. TOP N = 25, g-index k = 5. Pruning methods were chosen as “pathfinder,” “pruning sliced networks,” and “pruning the merged network.” Subsequently, the strongest citation bursts analysis will be conducted to explore the evolutionary trends of frailty around the perioperative period. Clustering was performed using the Log-Likelihood ratio (LLR) algorithm. The study used R language with Bibliometrix to conduct visual analysis, including corresponding author’s countries, most relevant authors, authors’ production over time, most locally cited authors, cumulative journal publications, and thematic Map. Parameters in Vosviewer were set to “create a map based on bibliographic” and “read data from bibliographic database files,” with analysis type as co-occurrence, the unit of analysis as author keywords, followed by keyword clustering analysis after merging synonyms.

Results

Global overview

According to the search strategy, 4119 articles (3510 articles and 609 reviews) were screened from the WoSCC database from 1978 to 2024. The annual growth rate is 13.74%, with a total citation count of 79,237, excluding self-citations, which amount to 55,655. On average, each article is cited 19.13 times. 21,879 authors from 95 countries and regions have published literature on perioperative frailty across 973 journals globally.

Annual publication trend

The volume of literature related to perioperative frailty grew slowly from 1978 to 2008 while experiencing a rapid increase from 2009 to 2023. In 2023, there were 666 publications, more than 40 times the number in 2009 (Fig. 1A). According to the fitted curves, the exponential curve with R² = 0.9805 is higher than the polynomial curve with R² = 0.951, indicating that the literature growth follows an exponential trend (Fig. 1B).

Thematic analysis (Fig. 5) where the horizontal axis represents relevance and the vertical axis represents the developmental stage. Motor themes are well-developed and core topics. Niche themes are highly developed but relatively marginalized topics. Emerging or declining themes represent emerging topics or on the verge of disappearing. Basic themes refer to core topics that still need to be fully developed internally but are likely to become research hotspots in the future. Niche themes mainly include “arthroplasty,” “geriatric medicine,” “rectal cancer radiotherapy,” and “aortic stenosis.” Basic themes mainly include “postoperative complications, delirium,” “modified frailty index,” “prehabilitation,” and “elderly, surgery,” indicating these topics have the potential to become future research hotspots.

Co-citation between references occurs when two references are cited together. Researching co-citation networks can explore a particular discipline’s development and evolutionary dynamics. This study integrated the co-citation network, identifying 547 nodes and 920 connections. Discussion

General information

Overall, 21,879 authors from 95 regions and countries published 4,119 studies on perioperative frailty in 973 journals worldwide. Publication rates have been increasing in recent years, possibly due to a significant rise in the elderly population. The United States has the highest publication output, but research is predominantly domestic. Italy has the highest proportion of international collaborations. The top ten institutions in terms of productivity are all from the United States. Similarly, the top nine prolific authors are from the United States.

Research hotspots

Keywords help researchers understand current research directions and hotspots. Keyword co-occurrence is an effective bibliometric analysis method for capturing research topics within the structure of scientific knowledge. Visual analysis of keywords reveals the current primary research directions in this field: the impact of frailty on postoperative clinical outcomes, preoperative risk assessment and stratification of frailty, elderly frailty, and intervention measures. Research hotspots include frailty and risk stratification, postoperative delirium, elderly issues, and the relationship between frailty and sarcopenia. Burst analysis of keywords indicates that research on perioperative frailty shows an overall trend from epidemiology to risk stratification and intervention measures.

Frailty and postoperative complications

Frailty is frequently associated with comorbidities, aging, malnutrition, sarcopenia, and chronic inflammation. It significantly affects postoperative clinical outcomes. Hence, intervening in frailty is crucial for improving patients’ postoperative clinical outcomes. Research has demonstrated that frailty predicts adverse postoperative outcomes, such as mortality [16], complication rates [17], and length of hospital stay [18]. Among these, postoperative delirium has received significant attention. Multiple studies have shown that frailty is associated with a higher incidence of perioperative neurocognitive disorders [19–21]. Frailty has been identified as an independent risk factor for neurocognitive disorders in surgical procedures [22–24]. Fortunately, some studies have shown that perioperative frailty is not associated with long-term postoperative cognitive dysfunction [25]. Meta-analysis indicates that preoperative frailty is an independent risk factor for postoperative delirium (POD) [26]. The underlying mechanisms linking frailty and POD remain unknown. Understandably, frailty is associated with cognitive decline, which increases the risk of developing POD [27]. More importantly, frailty and POD share some common risk factors. Additionally, previous studies have shown that frailty is associated with sarcopenia [28]. Sarcopenia has been identified as a potential independent risk factor for POD [29].

Frailty and risk stratification

Currently, more and more researchers are focusing on frailty screening. Early detection and prevention of frailty play an important role. Hall [30] used the risk analysis index to screen for frailty in patients undergoing major elective non-cardiac surgery, reducing the mortality rate of frail patients from 12.2 to 3.8%. Accurate frailty assessment requires a multi-faceted approach, encompassing physiological and psychological evaluations. Research shows that using different frailty screening tools leads to significant changes in the prevalence of frailty [31]. However, different frailty assessment tools show similar effectiveness in identifying the occurrence of frailty [32]. There are over 20 validated frailty assessment tools, mainly focusing on endurance, fatigue, mobility, and diseases. So far, there is no comprehensive tool for frailty assessment nor consensus on how to assess frailty. Both FRIED frailty index (FFI) and frailty index (FI) can predict an increase in postoperative complications [33]. Meta-analysis has found that FFI is a valuable risk assessment tool widely used for preoperative screening, but it does not consider cognitive function. FI considers frailty an accumulation of deficits, offering a broader definition encompassing physical and psychological aspects [34]. The FRAIL scale is derived from the FFI and is a straightforward, efficient questionnaire that can be used in various clinical environments [35]. The FI-CD assesses patients’ physical and psychological conditions and is considered a more sensitive predictor than the FI. However, its complexity makes it less commonly used in clinical settings. The EFS is an effective and reliable method for measuring frailty, assessing ten aspects: cognition, general health status, self-reported health status, functional independence, medication use, mood, and nutrition [36]. Similarly, increasing evidence supports the value of CGA in the context of frailty, with patients receiving CGA showing significantly reduced postoperative mortality [37]. In conclusion, preoperative assessment and stratification of patients’ frailty can more effectively identify potential risk factors and enhance vigilance for complications.

Frailty and elderly

Frailty can occur at any age. The prevalence of frailty is less than 10% in people under 65, but it increases to over 30% in those over 80 [38]. Frailty is attributed to the accumulation of age and disease-related deficits, with its prevalence increasing exponentially with age [39–41]. A meta-analysis reported a frailty prevalence of about 42% (ranging from 6 to 86%) among elderly cancer patients [42]. With the aging population, the number of elderly frail patients undergoing surgical procedures is increasing. Considering the stress of surgery and the inherent vulnerability of elderly frailty, adverse outcomes are expected. Frailty increases the risk of prolonged hospital stays and readmissions [43]. The incidence of postoperative complications and disability rates has more than doubled [44]. Frailty significantly increases the risk of death in older adults [45]. Frailty in elderly individuals presents in multiple forms, including weight loss, sarcopenia, osteoporosis, reduced physical activity, slower gait speed, cognitive decline, and increased incidence of falls [46]. This situation is primarily attributed to the exacerbated global aging population and the development of advanced medical technology. For optimal management of frail populations, frailty screening should be conducted for all individuals aged 70 and above, and those who have experienced significant weight loss (> 5%) due to chronic illnesses [47].

Frailty and sarcopenia

Sarcopenia is a common disease in frail patients and is considered a primary physiological driver of the frailty phenotype [48]. Healthcare providers often overlook sarcopenia and frailty, which can affect the improvement of clinical outcomes [49]. Similar to frailty, there is significant variability in reported prevalence rates of sarcopenia among elderly individuals, with incidence rates increasing with age [50, 51]. Generally, sarcopenia is a significant independent predictor of postoperative clinical outcomes. Sarcopenia and frailty overlap in etiology, symptoms, clinical outcomes, and interventions [52]. Individuals with sarcopenia often present a frail state. Similarly, elderly individuals with frailty usually suffer from sarcopenia [53]. McCleary found that elderly individuals with frailty or sarcopenia are prone to complications during cancer treatment [54]. The 2010 guidelines from the American College of Surgeons highlight the importance of assessing frailty and sarcopenia before tumor surgery in elderly patients [55].

Research frontiers

The strategic coordinate map indicates that perioperative frailty is an emerging research area in recent years, with no highly mature core themes identified. Combining keyword clustering timelines and keywords with the strongest citation bursts reveals that frailty will likely become a research hotspot about postoperative cognitive function, preoperative risk stratification, and intervention measures. Postoperative cognitive dysfunction related to frailty has increasingly focused on frailty’s relationship with postoperative depression, in addition to postoperative delirium, in recent years [56]. Assessment and risk stratification of frailty have always been a focus, with relatively high research maturity, yet the potential relationship between frailty and various surgeries still requires further investigation. Studies have shown that surgeries with low and moderate stress levels may be high-risk in frail patients [57]. The mechanisms underlying these outcomes remain unclear and require further investigation. Frailty can optimize patients’ health status before surgery in specific ways. Research primarily focuses on the effects of exercise and nutritional interventions. Some studies have demonstrated exercise’s positive impact on critical frailty indicators [58, 59]. Evidence indicates a connection between nutrient intake and the risk of frailty [60]. Researchers are encouraged to study the effects of malnutrition, sarcopenia, and frailty on clinical outcomes and identify effective intervention measures. Combining nutritional and rehabilitative therapies can enhance the prognosis of frail perioperative patients. In recent years, researchers have increasingly explored the feasibility of drugs in improving critical frailty indicators. Some medicines that may be beneficial in treating fatigue, such as ketamine, methylphenidate, and modafinil, require further evidence-based evaluation and exploration [61].

Limitations

Our study has some limitations. Firstly, frailty as a new concept has developed rapidly in recent years, and articles with unclear concepts may not be retrievable. Secondly, all publications in our study originated from the WOSCC, which may lead to the omission of valuable studies. Additionally, research is a dynamic process, and our analysis needs to be continuously updated.

Conclusions

Frailty has been identified as an independent predictor of adverse postoperative clinical outcomes. The association of frailty with postoperative complications has introduced a new field in surgical procedures. This bibliometric study constructed the knowledge map of this field, identifying the most influential countries, institutions, authors, journals, and research hotspots while demonstrating the impact of frailty on surgeries. Key areas explored in this study include risk stratification of preoperative frailty, frailty’s relationship with postoperative cognitive function, elderly populations, and sarcopenia. Interventions for perioperative frailty are future research directions. We hope this study will help researchers understand this field’s trends and potential.

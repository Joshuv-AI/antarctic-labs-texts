Spatiotemporal evolution and spatial differentiation of carbon emission intensity in the Chinese transport sector
Authors: Yongping Tang, Huiyuan Jiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAccurately identifying the spatiotemporal evolution and spatial differentiation of carbon emission intensity in the transport sector is essential for formulating region-specific carbon reduction policies. This study develops an analytical framework that integrates both static and dynamic perspectives to examine spatial disparities in transport sector carbon emission intensity. From a static perspective, the Dagum Gini coefficient is employed to quantify spatial differences and their sources of transport carbon emission intensity. From a dynamic perspective, kernel density estimation is applied to depict the evolution trajectories of transport carbon emission intensity. Furthermore, the traditional Markov chain model is refined to construct a spatial Markov chain model that accounts for spatial adjacency, enabling identification of persistence and spatial spillover effects. The empirical results indicate that (1) The carbon emission intensity of the transport sector in China presents an overall declining trend with significant spatial heterogeneity among provinces. Regional disparities have expanded, with the largest gap between the eastern and western regions, where inter-regional differences contribute an average of 47.374% to total disparity, representing the main source of variation. (2) The carbon emission intensity in the national, eastern, and central regions tends to converge gradually, while the western region shows a pattern of initial convergence followed by renewed divergence. Within each region, several provinces maintain carbon emission intensity levels significantly higher than the average, forming a clear spatial gradient structure. (3) The traditional Markov chain analysis reveals evident persistence and club convergence in transport carbon emission intensity. The spatial Markov chain analysis further shows that neighboring regions strongly influence local transition probabilities, demonstrating spatial spillover and path dependence effects. Hypothesis testing confirms the necessity of incorporating spatial dependence into the analysis. Based on these findings, this study proposes that carbon reduction strategies in the transport sector should be tailored to regional disparities and spatial interdependencies, aiming to enhance overall mitigation efficiency and foster coordinated governance.Subject terms: Ecology, Ecology, Environmental social sciences

Against the backdrop of accelerating global efforts toward a green transition, carbon-driven climate warming and environmental degradation have become critical concerns for the international community1–5. Enhancing energy efficiency and reducing emissions have emerged as central strategies for addressing global warming, representing pressing priorities for countries worldwide6. China currently accounts for nearly 28% of global carbon dioxide emissions, ranking first in absolute emissions and energy consumption while hosting the largest population7–10. At the 75th Session of the United Nations General Assembly in 2020, China pledged to peak carbon dioxide emissions by 2030 and achieve carbon neutrality by 2060. As a foundational, strategic, and service-oriented sector of the national economy, transportation facilitates both passenger and freight mobility, serving as a vital link between production and consumption. It underpins socio-economic development and directly affects the scale and quality of economic growth. The level of carbon mitigation in the sector reflects the sophistication of a country’s socio-economic model and the depth of its green transition. In recent years, the transport sector has been the third-largest source of carbon emissions globally11–13, contributing approximately 24.32% of total societal emissions and playing a key role in driving global CO2 emission growth14–16.

Previous studies on the spatial disparities of carbon emissions in the transport sector have largely focused on single-dimensional analyses, and few have systematically examined the spatiotemporal evolution and regional differentiation of transport carbon emission intensity within a unified framework integrating both static and dynamic perspectives. Lu et al.17 analyzed regional differences in transport carbon emissions in the Yangtze River Economic Belt using the Theil index under a purely static framework, while Zhao et al.18 applied the Theil index to assess regional disparities in freight transport carbon emissions across China. Although these studies provide valuable insights into overall disparity levels, they are limited in capturing the dynamic evolution and underlying structural characteristics of carbon emission intensity. Furthermore, existing research often overlooks interregional spatial interactions, resulting in insufficient characterization of spatial spillover and transmission effects, thus limiting understanding of the mechanisms driving regional differentiation. Bai et al.19 applied a traditional Markov chain to examine the persistence of spatial inequities in per-capita transport carbon emissions in China. However, this method inherently assumes that regional state transitions are independent, failing to fully account for spatial dependencies arising from geographic proximity and economic linkages.

Given these limitations, it is necessary to develop a comprehensive analytical framework that simultaneously captures spatial disparities, dynamic evolution, and spatial dependencies in transport carbon emissions. Accordingly, this study adopts an integrated static and dynamic perspective, combining the Dagum Gini coefficient, kernel density estimation, and a spatial Markov chain model to systematically analyze the spatiotemporal evolution and regional differentiation of carbon emission intensity in China’s transport sector. From a static perspective, the framework reveals the spatial distribution patterns of transport carbon emission intensity and identifies the sources of regional disparities. From a dynamic perspective, it characterizes the evolution trajectories, persistence, and spatial spillover effects. The findings of this study provide government agencies and industry regulators with a comprehensive understanding of the spatiotemporal distribution of transport carbon emission intensity, enabling the identification of key low-efficiency regions and critical stages in its evolution, and supporting the formulation of regionally differentiated low-carbon transport policies.

Literature review

Due to pronounced heterogeneity in resource endowments, population size, technological capacity, and urban development, the transport sector exhibits significant spatiotemporal variation in carbon emission intensity across regions, with a growing body of literature examining these characteristics by considering regional disparity measurement, spatial heterogeneity, and dynamic evolution20–24. From the perspective of regional disparity measurement, most studies quantify differences in transport carbon emission intensity across provinces using traditional inequality indices such as the Theil index and the coefficient of variation. Li et al.25 analyzed the spatial evolution of transport carbon emissions in 341 Chinese cities using the Theil index, finding that emissions in central and western cities were significantly lower than in eastern cities. Li et al.26 employed the Theil index and semi-variogram models along China’s Belt and Road regions, observing significant spatial disparities, signs of regional convergence, and dynamic heterogeneity despite weak spatial autocorrelation. From the perspective of spatial heterogeneity, numerous studies adopt spatial statistical methods under a static framework to identify clustering patterns and geographical differentiation in transport carbon emissions. Yuan et al.27 used the semivariogram function combined with high-low clustering techniques to reveal pronounced provincial-level spatial heterogeneity. Lim et al.28 applied Moran’s I index and the STIRPAT model to examine CO2 emissions across countries with dispersed versus concentrated urban forms, showing lower transport emissions in spatially dispersed urban regions. Zeng et al.29 conducted exploratory spatial data analysis across 30 Chinese provinces, confirming significant spatial heterogeneity. Bai et al.30 used social network analysis to uncover a core-periphery structure in provincial transport emissions. Zhang et al.31 applied ESDA and GWR models to identify high- and low-value clusters across regions. From the perspective of dynamic evolution, a smaller but growing number of studies explore temporal transitions of transport carbon emission states. Liu et al.32 employed a geographically and temporally weighted regression model, revealing a spatial distribution pattern of emission intensity characterized by lower values in the southeast and higher values in the northwest. Li et al.33 applied a traditional Markov chain to analyze dynamic transitions between transportation economic development levels and carbon emission states across 30 provinces.

A comprehensive review of previous studies reveals three major limitations. First, conventional disparity measurement methods exhibit theoretical and decompositional constraints. Most studies assessing regional differences in carbon emissions employ the Theil index or the traditional Gini coefficient, both of which typically assume non-overlapping group distributions. This assumption hinders the identification of overlapping contributions among regions, thereby weakening the explanatory power regarding the intrinsic mechanisms of spatial heterogeneity. Second, the methodological framework remains relatively homogeneous, lacking an integrative approach that combines multiple models. Existing research often relies on a single indicator or spatial econometric model to examine spatial characteristics of transport-related carbon emissions, which limits the ability to systematically analyze spatial disparities, evolutionary trajectories, and solidification effects from both static and dynamic perspectives. As a result, the explanatory depth and spatial coupling of such analyses remain insufficient. Third, research on dynamic evolution and spatial interdependence mechanisms remains underdeveloped. The conventional Markov chain model typically assumes regional independence in state transitions and focuses solely on temporal evolution, neglecting spatial spillover and diffusion effects driven by geographical proximity. This limitation restricts a comprehensive understanding of regional linkages and spatial dependency mechanisms.

To address the above limitations, this study introduces the following innovations. First, the Dagum Gini coefficient is employed to overcome the limitations of the traditional Gini and Theil indices in handling subgroup overlaps and disparity decomposition. This method enables precise decomposition of overall inequality into intra-regional differences, inter-regional differences, and hypervariable density contributions, thereby providing a more comprehensive understanding of the spatial distribution and sources of transport carbon emission intensity disparities. Second, a comprehensive analytical framework integrating the Dagum Gini coefficient, Kernel density estimation, and the spatial Markov chain is established to achieve a systematic synthesis of static and dynamic perspectives. This framework uncovers spatial disparities and their origins in the static dimension while characterizing evolutionary trajectories and solidification effects in the dynamic dimension, thus compensating for the inability of single-model analyses to jointly capture static heterogeneity and dynamic evolution. Third, the traditional Markov chain model is refined into a spatial Markov chain model that incorporates spatial adjacency relationships, enabling an exploration of how different spatial lag contexts affect the transition probabilities of carbon emission states. This enhancement allows the identification of spatial spillover and diffusion effects and reveals the spatial dependence and transfer mechanisms of carbon emission intensity. Through comparative analysis and hypothesis testing, the study further validates the significant influence of spatial proximity on the evolution of carbon emission intensity, providing empirical support for formulating regionally differentiated carbon reduction policies in the transport sector.

Results and discussions

Temporal evolution of carbon emission intensity

Figure 1 illustrates a consistent downward trajectory in the transport carbon emission intensity at the national scale, decreasing from 3.15 to 1.25 tons/10,000 yuan over 2005–2022, with an average annual decline rate of 5.31%. It reflects notable achievements in energy efficiency, technological advancement, and fuel structure optimization, contributing to the sector’s continuous progress in green development. Notably, in 2020, the transport carbon emission intensity was 54.87% lower than in 2005, exceeding the national commitment to cut CO2 intensity per unit of GDP by 40–45% relative to 2005, thereby underscoring the sector’s pivotal role in advancing China’s dual-carbon objectives.

Regional transport carbon emission intensity in China.

In the eastern region, transport carbon emission intensity declined from 3.18 to 1.05 tons/10,000 yuan over 2005-2022, with an average annual decline of 6.31%, the largest among the three regions, reflecting its first-mover advantage in technological advancement, industrial upgrading, and adoption of cleaner transport modes. In the central region, intensity fell from 2.65 to 1.34 tons/10,000 yuan over 2005-2022, with an average annual decline of 3.92%, above the national mean, indicating progress in infrastructure and energy efficiency, although further optimization remains necessary. In the western region, it decreased from 3.75 to 1.68 tons/10,000 yuan over 2005-2022, with an average annual decline of 4.62%, above the national mean, highlighting challenges arising from vast geography, long transport distances, weaker infrastructure, and reliance on carbon-intensive modes.

Spatial evolution of carbon emission intensity

Figure 2 depicts the carbon emission intensity of China’s transport sector exhibits a distinct spatially uneven distribution. According to the natural breaks classification, the number of provinces with low carbon emission intensity decreased from nine in 2005 to seven in 2022, primarily concentrated in the eastern region. These provinces, characterized by advanced economies, higher technological levels, and greater utilization of clean energy, demonstrate superior energy efficiency, resulting in relatively low carbon emissions per unit of transport output. Provinces with moderately low carbon emission intensity increased from eight in 2005 to eleven in 2022, mainly located in the central and eastern regions. Although these areas possess a certain degree of economic and transport infrastructure, their energy mix remains dominated by conventional fossil fuels, leading to medium-to-low emission intensity levels.

Spatial distribution of transport carbon emission intensity in China. The map was created by the authors using ArcGIS version 10.2 (Esri, https://www.esri.com).

In contrast, provinces with moderately high carbon emission intensity rose from seven in 2005 to nine in 2022, primarily distributed across the central and western regions. These areas typically have stronger industrial bases and denser populations, generating substantial transport demand. However, due to the lower energy efficiency of transport modes and limited penetration of new energy technologies, their carbon emissions per unit of output remain relatively high. Provinces with high carbon emission intensity declined from six in 2005 to three in 2022, mostly concentrated in the western region. The high intensity in these areas stems from underdeveloped infrastructure, a predominance of heavy and long-distance freight transport, and low transport efficiency. At the regional level, the average carbon emission intensity of the transport sector in the eastern, central, and western regions reached 2.10, 2.05, and 2.85 tons/10,000 yuan, respectively. The western region’s average value was thus 1.39 times that of the central region, revealing pronounced spatial heterogeneity in transport-related carbon emission intensity across China.

Spatial disparities and sources of transport carbon emission intensity

Table 2 shows that the overall Gini coefficient of national transport carbon emission intensity exhibits a fluctuating upward trend, increasing from 0.201 in 2005 to 0.256 in 2022, with an average annual growth rate of 1.44% and a mean value of 0.206, indicating increasingly pronounced regional disparities. Temporally, the coefficient rose from 0.201 to 0.212 between 2005 and 2007 and then declined to 0.202. In this period, the rapid expansion of the transport sector in eastern China outpaced that of the central and western regions, temporarily widening inter-regional disparities, while the implementation of the Western Development Strategy and improvements in transport infrastructure in some central provinces helped partially mitigate these gaps. Between 2008 and 2012, the coefficient declined from 0.204 to 0.192, suggesting a narrowing of overall differences. This trend can be attributed to increased government investment in transport infrastructure following the 2008 global financial crisis, particularly the accelerated construction of high-speed railways and expressways in central and western regions. From 2013 to 2022, the coefficient increased again from 0.182 to 0.256, indicating renewed divergence. This was mainly driven by the early adoption of green and low-carbon transport initiatives in eastern coastal regions, contrasted with rapidly expanding transport demand and fossil-fuel-dominated energy consumption structures in the central and western regions, which further widened regional disparities.

Gini and Decomposition of transport carbon emission intensity.

From the perspective of intra-regional Gini coefficients, the western region exhibits the highest mean value, followed by the eastern region, while the central region records the lowest. The Gini coefficient within the western region remained relatively stable between 0.19 and 0.21 from 2005 to 2022, with an average of 0.190, indicating that internal disparities remained steady but were the largest among the three regions. This can be attributed to significant imbalances in economic development and energy structure within the western provinces. In the central region, the Gini coefficient declined from 0.182 in 2005 to 0.152 in 2022, with an average of 0.155 during the study period, suggesting a marked convergence in transport carbon emission intensity among provinces. This trend is closely linked to increased investment in transport infrastructure under the national "Rise of Central China" strategy, which helped narrow internal disparities in emission intensity. In contrast, the Gini coefficient in the eastern region fluctuated upward from 0.167 in 2005 to 0.254 in 2022, with an average of 0.172. This increase primarily reflects substantial differences in technological advancement and transport structure among eastern provinces.

From the perspective of inter-regional Gini coefficients, the gap between the eastern and western regions is the most pronounced, representing the core source of regional disparity, with an average value of 0.254. The gap between the eastern and central regions ranks second at 0.214, while that between the central and western regions is the smallest at 0.187. In terms of temporal variation, the Gini coefficient between the eastern and western regions increased from 0.242 in 2005 to 0.322 in 2022, indicating a widening disparity. This is primarily attributed to the eastern region’s significant advantages in transport equipment modernization, clean energy utilization, and logistics efficiency, whereas the western region remains constrained by underdeveloped transport infrastructure, heavy reliance on high-carbon energy sources, and slow industrial and transport restructuring. Consequently, the east-west gap in transport carbon emission intensity has persisted and continued to expand. The Gini coefficient between the eastern and central regions rose from 0.192 in 2005 to 0.278 in 2022, suggesting an increasing disparity. This trend is mainly due to the eastern region’s stronger economic foundation and technological capacity, which facilitated rapid optimization of its transport energy structure and transition toward low-carbon development. By contrast, the central region, dominated by coal consumption, a heavy transport industry structure, and limited adoption of emission-reduction technologies, experienced a slower decline in carbon emission intensity. In comparison, the Gini coefficient between the central and western regions declined from 0.212 in 2005 to 0.186 in 2022, indicating gradual convergence, largely because both regions face similar development stages and structural constraints, including fossil fuel dependence and relatively weak foundations for low-carbon transport.

In terms of contribution shares, the average contribution of inter-regional disparity reaches 47.374%, while intra-regional disparity and transvariation density account for 29.390% and 23.236%, respectively. This indicates that inter-regional disparity is the primary source of the overall spatial variation in the transport sector carbon emission intensity in China. From a temporal perspective, the contribution of inter-regional differences, which reflects disparities among regions, increased from 38.398 in 2005 to 52.260% in 2022, making it the dominant driver of spatial variation. The contribution of intra-regional disparity remained relatively stable around 30% in most years, suggesting significant heterogeneity in carbon emission intensity among provinces within each region. In contrast, the contribution of transvariation density declined from 30.920 in 2005 to 18.227% in 2022, indicating that the degree of overlap and cross-distribution between regions gradually weakened over time.

Dynamic evolution analysis of transport carbon emission intensity

Figure 3 shows that in terms of distribution location, the main peaks of the kernel density curves for the nation as a whole and for the eastern, central, and western regions all shift leftward during the study period, indicating an overall decline in transport sector carbon emission intensity across the country and within the three regions. The eastern region exhibits the most pronounced leftward shift.

Kernel density of transport carbon emission intensity.

Regarding distribution shape, the curves for the nation, eastern, and central regions display rising peaks and a transformation from wide to sharp peaks over time. This indicates a spatial evolution of carbon emission intensity from a dispersed to a more concentrated pattern, suggesting gradual convergence of regional differences and increasing uniformity in emission intensity levels. In the western region, the curves show a peak that rises and then falls, with the shape evolving from wide to sharp and back to wide. This pattern indicates that the western region experienced a dynamic process of initial convergence followed by subsequent divergence in transport carbon emission intensity.

In terms of distribution spread, the kernel density curves for the nation and all three regions exhibit a pronounced right skew, suggesting the presence of provinces with carbon emission intensity significantly above the average, which exert a pulling effect on overall intensity and create a gradient distribution structure. Over time, the spread gradually contracts for the nation and all regions, indicating a reduction in extreme values within regions and a tendency toward a more concentrated distribution of carbon emission intensity, reflecting a reduction in absolute disparities across regions.

Spatial persistence effects of transport carbon emission intensity

Although the analysis of the spatial disparity characteristics of transport carbon emission intensity and the kernel density estimation method can reveal overall trends and distribution patterns, they are limited in capturing the persistence of spatial inequality. To further identify the intrinsic spatiotemporal evolution mechanisms of transport carbon emission intensity, it is necessary to introduce a Markov chain model. By constructing a probability transition matrix, the dynamic transition processes of carbon emission intensity states across provinces can be analyzed.

In this study, carbon emission intensity data for 30 provinces from 2005 to 2022 were classified into four state categories based on quantiles. The first category represents low levels, the second medium–low levels, the third medium–high levels, and the fourth high levels. These four categories are denoted by , where higher indicates higher transport carbon emission intensity. By examining the transition probabilities among these categories, the evolutionary inertia and persistence of spatial inequality in regional carbon emission intensity can be effectively identified.

(1) From 2005 to 2022, the traditional Markov transition probabilities for transport carbon emission intensity levels remaining in the same state were 0.965, 0.767, 0.782, and 0.872, respectively. Probabilities of remaining within the same state substantially exceeded those of shifting to alternative states. This indicates that most provinces maintained a high degree of stability in their carbon emission intensity levels during the study period, exhibiting pronounced path dependence and locking effects. In other words, once a province is classified into a certain intensity category, it is highly likely to remain in that category in subsequent years, while the probability of transitioning across categories is relatively low. The generally high values along the diagonal reflect the persistence of spatial inequality in transport carbon emission intensity, which poses challenges for coordinated carbon reduction among regions and for the comprehensive advancement of green transformation.

(2) For provinces in the low-level category, the probability of remaining in the same state is 0.965, with a 0.035 probability of transitioning to the medium-low level, and no observed transitions to the medium-high or high levels. This indicates that low carbon emission intensity has become largely stabilized, exhibiting high persistence. Provinces in the medium-low category have a 0.767 probability of remaining in the same state, a 0.178 probability of moving down to the low level, and probabilities of 0.039 and 0.016 for upward transitions to medium-high and high levels, respectively. This suggests that provinces with medium-low emissions are more likely to move downward than upward. Provinces in the medium-high category exhibit a 0.782 probability of remaining in the same state, probabilities of 0.188 and 0.008 for downward transitions to medium-low and low levels, and a 0.023 probability of moving up to the high level. The higher likelihood of downward transitions relative to upward transitions reflects the early effectiveness of China’s low-carbon development policies, which are gradually driving the transformation of traditionally high-emission industries. For provinces in the high-level category, the probability of remaining in the same state is 0.872, with a 0.128 probability of moving down to the medium-high level, and no observed direct transitions to medium-low or low levels. This indicates that while high-emission provinces possess some capacity for carbon reduction, their state transitions are primarily confined to adjacent medium-high levels, demonstrating pronounced persistence and level-specific locking characteristics.

(3) Provinces in the low-level and high-level categories exhibit higher probabilities of remaining in the same state than those in the medium-low and medium-high categories. Specifically, the probabilities of maintaining the original state in the next period are 0.965 for low-level and 0.872 for high-level provinces, indicating the presence of a club convergence phenomenon in transport carbon emission intensity. Transitions between different categories are primarily confined to adjacent levels, with the probability of cross-level shifts being relatively low; in all cases, the likelihood of moving across non-adjacent levels remains below 2%.

Comprehensive discussion

Existing studies generally acknowledge that transport sector carbon emission intensity in China exhibits pronounced regional disparities, with a clear pattern of imbalance among the eastern, central, and western regions27. Using the Dagum Gini coefficient, which explicitly accounts for subgroup overlap, this study confirms these findings and further demonstrates, through source decomposition, that interregional disparities constitute the dominant contributor to overall inequality in transport carbon emission intensity. From a static decomposition perspective, these results deepen the understanding of the structural characteristics underlying spatial inequality in transport sector carbon emissions. Building on this static analysis, and in contrast to prior studies that focus primarily on disparity levels17,18, this study incorporates kernel density estimation and a traditional Markov chain approach to explore the dynamic evolution of transport carbon emission intensity. The results reveal pronounced regional heterogeneity in distributional dynamics, with particularly distinct patterns observed in the western region relative to other parts of the country. Several regions persistently remain within specific emission intensity categories over time, indicating strong path dependence and hierarchical locking effects.

Furthermore, the spatial Markov chain analysis advances the understanding of the mechanisms underlying the persistence of spatial inequality in transport carbon emission intensity from a spatial dependence perspective. Unlike traditional Markov models, which ignore interregional linkages19, this study finds that, after introducing spatial lag conditions, the emission intensity levels of neighboring regions have a significant impact on local state transition probabilities. Both high and low emission regions exhibit pronounced club clustering and locking effects, demonstrating that regional disparities do not evolve independently, but are continuously reinforced through spatial spillover and transmission mechanisms, thereby intensifying spatial inequality in transport carbon emission intensity.

Overall, this study establishes a hierarchical and mutually reinforcing analytical framework in which the Dagum Gini coefficient characterizes static spatial disparities and their sources, kernel density estimation captures the dynamic evolution of distributional patterns, and the spatial Markov chain identifies the hierarchical locking and spatial spillover effects driving these dynamics. By integrating these three methodological pillars, the study provides a systematic explanation of the evolutionary pathways, persistence, and spatial diffusion mechanisms of inequality in transport carbon emission intensity. These findings extend current understanding of the spatiotemporal evolution and spatial differentiation of carbon emissions in the transport sector and offer robust empirical support for the design of regionally differentiated low-carbon transport policies.

Conclusions

This study adopts the IPCC top-down approach to estimate the carbon emission intensity of the transport sector across Chinese provinces from 2005 to 2022, based on both direct and indirect fossil fuel consumption data. The Dagum Gini coefficient is employed to analyze the spatial disparities and their sources from a static perspective. Subsequently, the kernel density estimation method is applied to depict the dynamic evolution trajectories of transport carbon emission intensity at the national and regional levels. Furthermore, the spatial Markov chain model is utilized to examine the persistence of spatial disparities and the spatial spillover effects under different regional contexts, while hypothesis testing is conducted to verify the spatial transmission mechanisms. The main conclusions are as follows:

At the national level, the overall carbon emission intensity of the transport sector in China shows a clear downward trend, indicating a continuous improvement in carbon emission efficiency per unit of output. From a regional perspective, significant disparities exist among the three major regions, with the eastern region exhibiting the most pronounced decline and acting as the leading force in promoting green development within the national transport sector.

This study develops an integrated analytical framework combining the Dagum Gini coefficient, kernel density estimation, and the spatial Markov chain to systematically characterize the static spatial disparities, dynamic evolution, and spatial dependence of transport sector carbon emission intensity in China. By jointly examining these complementary dimensions, the study provides more comprehensive empirical evidence for understanding the evolutionary mechanisms underlying spatial inequality in transport carbon emission intensity. Future research may refine the analytical scale to the city or county level and exploit higher-resolution regional data to capture micro-level spatial heterogeneity, thereby enhancing the precision and policy targeting of the findings.

Policy implications

Based on the above research findings, the following policy recommendations are proposed:

Addressing intra-regional disparities. The western region exhibits the highest internal variation and should prioritize investment in provinces with underdeveloped transport infrastructure, such as Yunnan, Guizhou, and Gansu, focusing on the development of modern integrated transport networks including trunk and feeder roads, railways, and ports. The adoption of electric buses and clean-energy logistics vehicles should be promoted, supported by the deployment of charging stations and hydrogen refueling facilities, to gradually reduce reliance on fossil fuels. In the central region, where internal disparities have significantly converged, continued advancement of regional transport integration is necessary, as exemplified by the Chang-Zhu-Tan and Wuhan urban clusters, through improved urban cluster networks and trunk–feeder connectivity. In the eastern region, where internal disparities continue to widen, interprovincial technology training and experience sharing should be strengthened. For instance, Guangdong could transfer advanced low-carbon bus operation models and charging infrastructure experience to higher-emission provinces such as Shandong, complemented by on-site expert guidance and periodic training to ensure effective implementation of emission-reduction technologies and management practices.

Addressing inter-regional disparities. The gap in carbon emission intensity between the eastern and western regions is the largest. Developed eastern provinces such as Zhejiang and Guangdong should lead targeted collaboration initiatives with less-developed western provinces such as Shaanxi and Xinjiang, focusing on the transfer of core technologies—including new energy vehicle manufacturing, transport energy management systems, and intelligent transport control—through dedicated technology deployment, joint training centers, and on-site technical support teams. To narrow the widening gap between eastern and central regions, central provinces such as Henan, Anhui, and Hubei should accelerate the deployment of new energy vehicles and associated infrastructure, including charging stations and hydrogen refueling facilities at highway service areas, logistics parks, and public transport hubs, while introducing mature eastern low-carbon bus and logistics operation models to improve energy efficiency and reduce emissions. The converging trend between central and western regions should be reinforced through coordinated infrastructure initiatives. By leveraging the Chengdu–Chongqing Twin-City Economic Zone and Wuhan urban clusters, cross-provincial railway freight corridors and multimodal hubs should be jointly developed, while high-energy-consumption road freight should be shifted to rail and water transport, thereby lowering overall transport carbon intensity.

Addressing persistence and spatial spillover effects. High emission intensity provinces should be incentivized to optimize transport structures through fiscal rewards, green credit, and targeted subsidies. Specific measures include providing purchase subsidies or tax reductions for retiring high-emission buses and logistics vehicles, offering low-interest loans to support the adoption of new energy and hydrogen vehicles as well as charging and refueling infrastructure, and granting operational incentives to companies implementing intelligent transport management systems, optimizing routes, and improving transport efficiency. Additionally, a carbon credit reciprocity mechanism should be established among eastern, central, and western provinces, converting low-carbon technology deployment outcomes into tradable carbon credits. For example, central and western provinces adopting eastern-exported new energy buses and intelligent scheduling technologies could exchange these achievements for carbon allowances or receive fiscal compensation, forming an effective closed-loop incentive chain to ensure cross-regional emission reduction and balanced green transport development.

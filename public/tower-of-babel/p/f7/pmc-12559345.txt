Multi-scenario simulation of land use change based on the objectives of cultivated land, ecological protection, and economic development in Yunnan Province, China
Authors: Wenqi Tang, Jianqiang Li, Huiying Yan, Lianling Cha, Yuan Yang, Linling Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractLand use change scenario simulation based on multi-objective synergy can provide a scientific basis for coordinating ecological protection, resource management and economic development by quantitatively analysing and predicting the dynamic evolution of land use. As an ecological security barrier and cross-border opening frontier in southwest China, Yunnan Province is characterised by “mountainous and cross-border” land use change, which poses a multidimensional challenge to the sustainable development of the region. Based on the land use data from 2000 to 2020, coupled with the improved Markov FLUS model and multi-source driving factors, this study takes Yunnan Province as the study area. To simulate the land use pattern in 2040 under four scenarios, namely natural development, ecological protection, economic priority and cultivated land protection, and to reveal their evolution patterns and driving mechanisms. The study shows that: (1) between 2000 and 2020, cultivated land decreased by 1405.05 km² (-1.98%), of which 58% was converted to architecture after 2010, with the loss of high quality cultivated land concentrated around the urban agglomeration of central Yunnan (Kunming and Qujing); forest land showed a “decrease and then increase” trend (net increase of 0.84%) due to the intervention of ecological policies; architecture increased by 133.54%, but there was an abnormal decrease (-411.67 km²) from 2010 to 2015 due to the stock renewal policy. Forest land showed a “decrease and then increase” trend due to ecological policy interventions (a net increase of 0.84%); architecture increased by 133.54%, but paradoxically contracted between 2010 and 2015 (-411.67 km²) due to the stock renewal policy. (2) Multi-scenario modelling shows that under the economic development scenario, the cross-border economy, while driving the rapid development of the border crossing zone, may lead to the problem of underutilised land, and at the same time increase the risk of damage to ecological protection zones; The new cultivated land in the cultivated land protection scenario is located in the mountainous areas of northeastern Yunnan, exposing the governance dilemma of ‘occupying the best and making up for the worst’; Forest and grassland in the north-western mountainous areas of the ecological priority scenario have been effectively protected and restored, but the pressure on cultivated land has increased, exposing the deep-seated contradiction between ecological conservation and food security. (3) The study proposes a framework for zoning control and differentiated management, delineating ‘core control areas - ecological protection areas - special development areas’, and enforcing rigid rules such as ‘updating the stock’ and ‘occupying the optimal area to make up for the optimal one’. Rigid rules. Introduction

Land use change is a specific expression reflecting the interaction between human activities and land surface pattern. It is also an important part of the evolution of the terrestrial environment, revealing the law of land use evolution. This is the basis for the study of resource allocation and ecological and environmental issues1. In the context of accelerated global urbanisation, China, as the world’s largest developing country and a rapidly urbanising economy, with an urbanisation rate of 66.16% by the end of 2023, is a notable case study, The loss of cropland and fragmentation of ecological space due to the expansion of agricultural land for construction pose a threat to its development2–4. It is evident that human-land conflicts are particularly pronounced in southwestern provinces, a phenomenon attributable to the challenges posed by the region’s intricate topography and the sensitivity of its ecological environment. As the core area of China’s ‘two screens and three belts’ ecological security strategy, Yunnan Province carries the dual missions of ecological protection of the Yangtze River Economic Belt and opening up the gateway to Southeast Asia, and its land-use change not only concerns regional food security and biodiversity conservation, but also affects the stability of the cross-border geo-economic pattern5. In this context, the construction of a multi-scenario simulation tool for complex geographic environments to reveal the driving mechanism and policy response law of land use change has become an urgent need for spatial optimisation decision-making in Yunnan Province.

Land use change is a complex process influenced by a variety of social, economic and environmental factors. Land use change models play a crucial role in studying the driving forces of land use change, predicting future land use change and spatial patterns, impacts, etc., and have become an important research direction for regional change and sustainable development of land use. In the nascent stage of land use change research, a single model was predominantly utilised for simulation, including the Markov model6, which primarily focuses on quantifying the demand for land, and the grey system model7, and the CA model8, which is principally employed to simulate the spatial distribution and pattern characteristics of land use, and to analyse spatial differences in land use change driven by natural and anthropogenic factors, and the CLUE-S model9, amongst others. A significant number of scholars have employed various models, including the Markov model, the CA model, and the CLUE-S model, to simulate the spatial distribution and pattern characteristics of land use. These models have also been utilised to analyse spatial differences in land use change driven by natural and anthropogenic factors. A significant number of scholars have employed the Markov model, the cellular automata (CA) model and the CLUE-S model to simulate and predict future changes in land use9–11 and regional land use patterns. These models have been shown to have practical applications in land use prediction; however, it is evident that there are clear limitations in the prediction of a single model9. For instance, the Markov model is challenging to simulate the spatial pattern of land use; the CA model disregards the mixed land use structure in the land unit; and the CLUE-S model solely considers the land use with the highest joint probability in the process of land use allocation, overlooking the interactions and feedback mechanisms between the factors affecting land use changes10–12. In recent years, the utilisation of coupled models that are capable of balancing quantitative land use demand and spatial allocation simulation has emerged as a predominant research trend. In the field of multi-medium coupled models, the utilisation of CA models is predominantly attributed to their capacity for two-dimensional spatio-temporal integration. The fundamental principle underlying this technique is the transformation between units. As is well documented in the relevant literature, the following commonly used CA-coupled models are to be noted: Logistic-CA13, ANN-CA14, SD-CA15 and Markov-CA16. However, the majority of these models are capable of simulating the dynamics of a single land use only. In most cases, however, different land use change processes occur simultaneously and affect each other. Consequently, multi-scenario land use simulation is a more effective method of determining realistic future land use changes.The Markov-FLUS model overcomes the limitations of existing models, such as the FLUS model coupled with the SD model CA model17. It simultaneously takes into account the dual characteristics of top-down macro-driven and bottom-up micro-evolution, and is able to effectively cope with the uncertainty of various land use transformations under the influence of both natural and human activities. The integration of the time prediction capability of a Markov chain18 has been demonstrated to provide the study with multi-scenario land use change results with higher simulation accuracy, given the uncertainty and complexity of various land use type transformations. A number of scholars have conducted corresponding studies in plains inland areas, including Beijing, Hubei and Jiangxi18,19,21. These studies have confirmed that the Markov-FLUS model has high simulation accuracy in such areas17,20. However, there are still significant research gaps in its application in complex geographic environments and national border regions. Most of the existing studies adopt fixed neighbourhood weights and static policy constraints based on simulation experience in plains areas, and lack systematic research on parameter suitability and innovativeness of cross-border driving factors in mountainous border regions.

Multi-scenario simulation is a key technical approach to support decision-making in territorial spatial planning by setting differentiated development goals and interpreting possible future land use changes and spatial patterns6–23. The first of these is the systematic construction of scenarios. In this study, we integrate the natural conditions, policy constraints, and socio-economic backgrounds to construct a logically self-consistent system of scenarios (natural development, arable land preservation, ecological preservation, and economic prioritisation). This is based on the Driver-State-Response (DSR) framework24. The second is the plasticity of spatial allocation, and this study reflects the spatially heterogeneous impact of driving factors through the dynamic adjustment of neighbourhood factor weights and conversion cost matrix25–27. Concurrently, due to the unique characteristics of Yunnan’s border provinces, the multi-scenario simulation further introduces the principle of ‘geo-adaptability‘28, and considers the impulse-driven effect of the Belt and Road Initiative on the land development of the port economic zones in this study (e.g., the increase of the construction land conversion rate of the China-Laos railway along the railway line). For instance, an increase in the conversion rate of construction land along the Sino-Lao Railway may be indicative of a shift in urban development trends. This methodological innovation elevates the multi-scenario simulation from a conventional ‘prediction-planning’ instrument to a ‘diagnosis-negotiation’ platform, thereby enabling the visualisation of land use conflicts under various development pathways. These include the conflict between economic development and the loss of arable land, and the disruption of ecological corridors. This, in turn, promotes the economic development of the border area and its ecological corridor. By visualising land use conflicts under different development paths, such as conflicts between economic development and arable land loss and ecological corridor disruption, the platform facilitates collaborative decision-making among stakeholders such as governments, communities and enterprises. Yunnan Province, with its mountainous terrain, transboundary characteristics and multi-ethnic population, offers an ideal experimental area for the testing and development of multi-scenario modelling methods. Through the implementation of a diversified scenario design, it is capable of accurately aligning with the nation’s strategic positioning as the ‘Pioneer of Ecological Civilisation’ and the ‘Radiation Centre Facing South and Southeast Asia’. Furthermore, it can provide a replicable and scalable governance paradigm for the sustainable development of complex border regions globally.

The present study takes Yunnan Province as its object of analysis, with the aim of comprehending the recent spatial and temporal dynamics of land use in the region. To this end, the study draws on land use classification and transfer maps from 2000, 2005, 2010, 2015 and 2020, respectively, and constructs a Markov-FLUS model driven by ‘topography-location-economy’ on the basis of historical data. The model is used to simulate the land use pattern under the four scenarios of natural development, arable land protection, ecological priority and economic priority in 2040. The construction of a Markov-FLUS model was undertaken to simulate the land use pattern in 2040, with the model based on historical data and designed to accommodate four distinct scenarios: natural development, arable land protection, ecological priority and economic priority. DEM data and terrain relief analysis are coupled to adjust the weights of neighbourhood factors according to the development objectives of different scenarios. The incentive effect of the Belt and Road Initiative on the land development of the port economic zone is quantified using data on the cross-border railway (China-Laos Railway) and population density of the port area. The objective of this study is to undertake a comparative analysis of multiple scenarios in order to reveal the ecology-cultivation-economy trade-off mechanism of land use changes in the border provinces. The study will also provide a scientific and operational decision-making tool for the spatial governance of the southwestern mountainous and cross-border regions. The findings of this study not only address the theoretical limitations of extant models in complex geographic environments, but also establish a methodological framework for the study of sustainable development in complex border regions globally.

Materials and methods

Overview of the study area

The Yunnan Province is situated in southwestern China, between 97°31′E and 106°12′E and 21°08′N and 29°15′N (see Fig. 1), with a width of 864.9 km from east to west and a length of 990 km from north to south, and a total area of 394,100 km². The overall terrain declines in steps from northwestern to southeastern, with mountainous areas accounting for 88.64% of the province’s total area. The highest elevation is 6740 m, while the lowest elevation is 76.4 m. The vertical height differences are considerable, and the horizontal and vertical differentiation of natural features is evident. The highest altitude is 6,740 m, while the lowest is 76.4 m. There is a considerable vertical height difference, and the horizontal and vertical differentiation of natural features is evident. The climate of Yunnan Province is characterised by a diverse range of climatic conditions, encompassing all climate types from the northern tropical zone to the cold temperate zone. The average annual precipitation ranges from 640 to 2060 mm, with distinct wet and dry seasons. The rainy season extends from May to October, while the dry season occurs from October to April of the following year. The territory comprises six principal water systems, namely the Yangtze River, the Pearl River, the Yuanjiang River, the Lancang River, the Nujiang River and the Daying River. The predominant soil types are red soil, yellow soil, mountain grey-brown soil and limestone soil. Yunnan Province is home to a diverse array of vegetation types, spanning tropical rainforests to alpine vegetation, and is a rich repository of biodiversity. Yunnan Province is subdivided into 16 prefectural-level administrative districts, which in turn comprise 129 county-level divisions (Fig. 1). The resident population of the province was 46.73 million in 2023, with a GDP of 300,211,200,000 yuan.

Data sources and pre-processing

The land use data for the years 2000, 2010 and 2020 were retrieved from the Resource and Environment Science and Data Centre of the Chinese Academy of Sciences (https://www.resdc.cn/Default.aspx), with a resolution of 30 m×30 m. The land use types include six categories: arable land, forest land, grassland, watersheds, construction land and unused land.The Digital Elevation Model (DEM) is derived from a geospatial data cloud (https://www.gscloud.cn/) with a resolution of 30 m × 30 m.Temperature and precipitation data were obtained from the National Tibetan Plateau Science Data Centre (http://data.tpdc.ac.cn) at a resolution of 1 km × 1 km.The GDP data were obtained from the Centre for Resource and Environmental Science and Data (https://wwwresdc.cn/) of the Chinese Academy of Sciences, and the population densities were obtained from Open Spatial Demographic Data and Research (https://www.worldpop.org/), both with a resolution of 1 km × 1 km.Utilising the ArcGIS 10.4 platform, the slope and incline were extracted based on DEM layers, and European distances were employed to quantify data such as distance from roads, railways, highways, city centres, districts and towns.The aforementioned layers were subjected to coordinate conversion and resampling procedures. The geographic coordinate system was unified as China_Geodetic_Coordinate_System_2000, the projected coordinate system was designated as Albers, and the spatial resolution was resampled to 30 m × 30 m.

Research methodology

Markov-FLUS model

The FLUS model29 has been formulated on the basis of two constituent models, namely a system dynamics model and a metacellular automata model. The model incorporates an artificial neural network and a roulette wheel selection mechanism, with the capacity to identify the interactions of multiple drivers and thereby enhance the simulation of land use change30,31. The model employs a multi-layer feed-forward neural network algorithm (BP-ANN) and combines natural and socio-economic drivers to correlate each category with different drivers, ultimately providing a spatial distribution pattern for each land type32. A combination of the strengths of the Markov model in predicting changes in the number of land types and the FLUS model can yield an accurate prediction of land use under multiple scenario settings30,33. Natural development scenario. Multi-scenario simulation projections

The FLUS model is dependent on both land scale and artificial neural networks for its functionality35. In this study, the Markov model was employed to predict the land use scale under different scenarios. An artificial neural network was constructed by fitting the relationship between land use change drivers and land use through the FLUS model. The future spatial distribution pattern of land use in the study area was calculated and simulated based on the land use scale demand and the artificial neural network.Furthermore, it is necessary to establish neighbourhood factors, conversion cost matrices and restricted conversion zones in order to ensure that the simulation results are more consistent with the future land use requirements of each scenario36.

Drivers of land-use change.

Drivers of land-use change.

Land use change is the result of a combination of factors, including the characteristics of the land itself, the natural environment, social structure and economic development. The accurate identification and selection of external drivers affecting land use change is a key part of the study.A comprehensive review of existing literature on the subject was conducted37–39. This was followed by an evaluation of the present situation within the designated study area40. This process of review and evaluation enabled the identification of five key elements which were identified as drivers: topography, climate, vegetation, economy and location.The prevailing topography, climate and vegetation are considered to be the primary natural factors determining the direction, mode and trend of land-use change.In this study, the following natural elements were selected for investigation: Digital Elevation Model (DEM), slope, slope direction, mean annual precipitation, mean annual temperature and vegetation cover.In consideration of Yunnan Province’s distinctive geographical characteristics, this study employs a quantitative approach to analyse the impact of transportation infrastructure, including cross-border railways and highways, on the cost and convenience of land development and utilisation. The study quantifies the distance of land to roads, railways and highways, as well as to the city centre, district and county centres, and township centres, thereby incorporating these factors into the analysis of locational impact.The economic factors were characterised by means of two variables: GDP and population density.The spatial distribution of each factor is illustrated in Fig. 2.

neighborhood factor.

neighborhood factor.

The neighbourhood factor has been incorporated into the FLUS model in order to provide a comprehensive characterisation of the intensity of expansion of each land use class subsequent to the influence of external factors. The range of this factor is from 0 to 1, with values closer to 1 indicating a greater expansion capacity for the respective land class41. Conventional methods are characterised by the utilisation of fixed weights; however, given the unique topography of Yunnan Province, which is characterised by mountainous terrain, ecological constraints and a diverse ethnic and cultural landscape, a dynamic approach is imperative.This study builds upon earlier research42–44, examining historical changes in land use (2000–2020), quantifying the spatial expansion likelihood of distinct categories, and extending the historical objective development law to establish the neighbourhood factor parameter for the natural development scenario. The architecture land weight is set at 1.0, representing the trend of historical high-intensity development in Pingba District; The unused land weight (0.01) is indicative of its limited conversion potential.In the remaining three scenarios, the parameters of this study are innovatively and dynamically allocated according to the actual situation in Yunnan Province21,40,45, and the cultivated land protection scenarios responds to the demand of the ‘food security’ policy, combining the ecological red line constraints with the rules of ethnic and cultural land protection.The weight of cultivated land is increased from 0.5 to 0.8 (the default value) with a view to discouraging non-farm conversion; The weighting of forest land was reduced to 0.5, allowing some ecological land to be converted to cultivated land.The weighting of architecture land is limited to 0.8 (lower than the 1.0 for the nature scenario), thus achieving a balance between development and conservation.The following paper sets out to explore the relationship between ecological priority scenarios and the strengthening of rigid control of ecological protection: The weighting of forest land was set at 1.0 (historical value was 0.7) and the weighting of grassland was raised to 0.7 to promote the return of farmland to forest and grassland.The weighting of arable land was reduced to 0.3 in order to limit steep slope reclamation; The building land weighting is maintained at 0.8, allowing for expansion of essential infrastructure only.The economic priority scenario is oriented towards the promotion of cross-border economic cooperation zones, such as those delineated by the China-Laos railway. The policy drivers that inform this approach are as follows: The architecture land weighting is set at 1.0 to promote intensive land development; The weights assigned to cultivated land (0.2) and forest land (0.3) are both found to be significantly reduced, thus reflecting the economic orientation of the resources in question.Furthermore, a comparison was made between the projections and the actual land use in 2020.The prediction accuracy was verified by the overall accuracy (0.92) and Kappa coefficient (0.85) to ensure that the neighbourhood factor parameters that best fit the study area were obtained (see Table 1 ).

Land-use change analysis for multi-scenario modelling

Natural development scenario.

Natural development scenario.

The urbanisation rate in Yunnan Province exhibited an increase from 34.8% to 50.0% between 2010 and 2020. Under the natural development scenario, the urbanisation rate continued to demonstrate a continuous growth trend from 2020 to 2040, and it was projected that the area of cultivated land would increase to 6,800 km², representing a 28.13% increase compared with 2020.This change exerts a dual influence on the socio-economy. On the one hand, the expansion of architecture land has been shown to promote the average annual growth of GDP. On the other hand, however, the encroachment of cultivated land on architecture land has been demonstrated to result in an increase in the rate of cultivated land loss. Consequently, it is predicted that cultivated land, grassland and others land area will be reduced by 1.The projected figures for arable land, grassland and unused land are 15 per cent, 4.71 per cent and 25.94 per cent respectively, which poses a threat to the stability of the ‘Dianzhong grain silo’ and the ‘grain silo in Yunnan’.With regard to spatial distribution (see Fig. 5a), the expansion of architecture land is primarily concentrated in Kunming City, Qujing City, and Yuxi City in the central Yunnan urban agglomeration, as well as in the low-altitude areas in eastern Yunnan. These areas coincide with the spatial distribution of cultivated land, thereby clearly demonstrating the encroachment of architecture land onto cultivated land (Fig. 6a).

cultivated land protection scenarios.

In this particular scenario, the area of cultivated land is 70,856.34 km², representing the largest area and the only type that exhibits an increasing trend among the four scenarios. Yunnan Province, being a mountainous plateau province (where 94% of the terrain is mountainous), has limited cultivated land resources and significant fragmentation.The expansion of cultivated land in the central and eastern mountain valleys of the study area (Fig. 5b) is consistent with the ‘Improvement of Cultivated Land Quality and Spatial Optimisation’ component of the Yunnan Land Improvement Plan (2016–2020), aligning with the objectives of enhancing cultivated land quality and spatial optimisation. This expansion also aligns with the demand for cultivated land in Yunnan’s highland speciality agriculture.Growth in architecture land has been shown to decline by approximately 13 per cent in comparison to the natural development scenario. This decline is indicative of the effectiveness of policies designed to protect cultivated land.The deceleration of the expansion of the Kunming-Yuxi urban agglomeration is consistent with the spatial strategy of ‘intensive development in central Yunnan and point development along the border’ in the Yunnan Provincial Land Spatial Planning (2021–2035).The area of forested land exhibits a marginal increase of 0.9 per cent under this particular scenario, indicating the implementation of a policy of ‘returning farmland to forests’ within the context of prioritising cultivated land. Nevertheless, the expansion of cultivated land may encroach on the space allocated for ecological restoration, a process which requires dynamic zoning control in conjunction with the Yunnan Provincial Ecological Protection Red Line Assessment and Adjustment Programme.This scenario is of pivotal importance in the effective protection of cultivated land resources and the augmentation of the area dedicated to cultivated land. This is of crucial significance in ensuring regional food security and the promotion of sustainable land use.However, it should be noted that the delineation of cultivated land protection zones may have consequences for the planning of architecture land and ecological protection zones, resulting in an expansion of the development gap between regions in Yunnan Province. At the same time, the new cultivated land is mostly located in the mountainous areas of northeastern Yunnan Province, exposing the governance dilemma of ‘occupying the best and making up for the worst’ (Fig. 6b).

Ecological protection scenarios.

Ecological protection scenarios.

In accordance with the ecological conservation scenario, it is projected that the area of forest land in Yunnan Province will reach 231,216.6 km² in 2040, representing an increase of 2.35% compared to 2020. Concurrently, the area of water is projected to increase to 4,295.03 km², marking a rise of 9.3%.With regard to spatial distribution, forests and grasslands in the northwestern mountainous areas have been effectively protected and restored, including around the cities of Shangri-La, Lushui, and Baoshan, as well as in the areas of the Nu and Lancang river basins (Fig. 5c).In accordance with the ‘Implementation Plan for the Establishment of a Market-based and Diversified Ecological Protection Compensation Mechanism in Yunnan Province’ issued by the Yunnan Provincial Government, local governments have been encouraged to transition from a ‘development priority’ to a ‘protection priority’.Concurrently, the establishment of ecological protection zones, predicated on this scenario, and the proscription of encroachment of architecture land on ecological land, have precipitated a transition of cultivated land to the primary source of architecture land. Projections indicate a contraction in the area of cultivated land to 67,066.02 km², representing a 2.27% diminution compared to the natural development scenario. This underscores an intractable contradiction between ecological protection and food security (Fig. 6c).

Economic priority scenarios.

Economic priority scenarios.

In comparison with the natural development scenario, the area of cultivated land, grassland and others land is reduced in the economic priority scenario. Conversely, the area of architecture land undergoes a substantial and consistent increase, a development that is in alignment with the planning of the Sino-Lao Railway Economic Belt (Fig. 5d).This scenario has the effect of increasing the demand for land for architecture in border cities (e.g. Ruili and Hekou), but attention must be paid to the problem of inefficient land use that may be triggered by rapid development.Concurrently, the area of forested land in this scenario has increased by 1.2 per cent compared to 2020, which may be attributed to the complementary ecological restoration brought about by the photovoltaic projects implemented in Yunnan Province in recent years. However, it should be noted that some of the ‘forest-energy-photovoltaic complementary’ projects will replace native forests with shrubs, resulting in an implicit loss of biodiversity.The area of cultivated land decreased from 69,432.05 km² in 2020 to 67,813.42 km², representing a decrease of 2.33 per cent. This decline can be attributed to the conversion of cultivated land resources into land for construction, driven by economic development.It may also be the case that the intensification of agriculture is driven by economic development, as evidenced by the example of Yuxi City, where growth in agricultural output has been achieved in spite of a reduction in cultivated land through intensive agriculture (Fig. 6d).

Comparative analysis of different scenarios

In 2020, Yunnan Province’s land use structure was characterised by forest land, grassland, and cultivated land, with the proportions of these land uses accounting for 225,901.9 km², 88,352.78 km², and 69,432.05 km², respectively.The simulation results (see Table 4) indicate that by 2040, there will be substantial changes in land use in Yunnan Province under various scenarios.In particular, the accelerated growth in the area designated for architecture, when juxtaposed with the natural development scenario and the economic priority scenario, evinces that the augmentation in the area of land utilised for architecture is nearly commensurate in both scenarios. This observation is indicative of an inherent propensity for expansion that is intrinsic to the prevailing land management system.As demonstrated by data provided by the Yunnan Provincial Department of Natural Resources, infrastructure, encompassing transportation and water conservation, accounted for 62 per cent of the province’s architecture land approvals in recent years, thus indicating that the ‘project-driven’ growth model remains predominant.In comparison with the natural development scenario, the economic priority scenario demonstrates a significant increase in the area designated for architecture in Kunming. This may be associated with the 14th Five-Year Plan for the Construction of a Radiation Centre Facing South and Southeast Asia in Yunnan Province, which aims to facilitate exchanges and cooperation between Yunnan Province and Southeast Asian countries, with Kunming serving as the central hub for these activities.Secondly, the four-scenario simulation reveals a substantial discrepancy in the change of arable land area. With the exception of the cultivated land protection scenario, the cultivated land area exhibits a downward trend in all three other scenarios. A comparison reveals that the Kunming-Yuxi-ChuXiong triangle has consistently been the primary region of cultivated land loss. This phenomenon may be associated with the expansion of the industry in the new Dianzhong area.

Discussion

Characteristics of land-use evolution, 2000–2040

The findings are based on land use data for 2000–2020 (Table 3) and land use modelling data for 2040 under the natural development scenario (Table 4).The evolution of land use patterns in Yunnan Province exhibits significant spatial reconfiguration characteristics, and the observed change trend is indicative of the complex interaction between natural geographic constraints, policy interventions and socio-economic development.The following discussion will address the characteristics of the conversion of land use types over the 40-year period. During this time, there has been a consistent loss of cultivated land, accompanied by significant variations in the phases of conversion. From 2000 to 2010, the predominant conversion was from cultivated land to forest land, accounting for 65 per cent of the total. This transition is directly associated with the Return of Farmland to Forestry Project, which was initiated in 2002, and the policy of protecting natural forests.From 2010 to 2040, there is an increase in the proportion of cultivated land converted to architecture land, with a rate of cultivated land loss around the central Yunnan urban agglomeration (Kunming and Qujing) of 1.2 per cent per year, which far exceeds the national average of 0.8 per cent.

The area of forest land exhibited fluctuations over the 40-year period, with an overall increase of 3,789.5 km², yet a decrease of 833.1 km² from 2000 to 2010, primarily attributable to the substitution of natural forests with the expansion of economic forests (rubber and eucalyptus).Following the implementation of the Programme for the Delineation of the Red Line of Ecological Protection in Yunnan Province in 2016, there was an area of forested land that rebounded, and the preservation rate of natural forests in the three-river area of northwestern Yunnan reached 98.7 per cent. This confirms the effectiveness of the ecological compensation policy.The augmentation of the water area by 1,471.43 km² over the 40-year period is predominantly ascribable to the construction of water conservancy facilities, with the Nuozhadu hydropower station (completed in 2014) contributing 220 km² of new water area, and the preservation of highland lakes, with the ecological restoration of lakes such as the Dianchi Lake and the Erhai Sea restoring 15 per cent of the water area.

Since the year 2000, the area of architecture land has increased by 4707.06 km², but there was an anomalous negative growth between 2010 and 2015, with a decrease of 411.67 km², mainly due to, among other things, the conversion of 32 km² of construction land vacated through shantytown redevelopment in the Guandu District of Kunming into others land.The Dianzhong Industrial New Zone, established in 2011, was initially characterised by over-planning, resulting in 50 km² of industrial land being left idle and temporarily degraded to others land.

Comparison of multi-scenario land-use changes

In this study, the Markov-FLUS model is utilized to reveal the evolving pattern of future land use in Yunnan Province under various setting scenarios, and to investigate the relationship between its driving mechanism and the socioeconomic background and policy orientation of Yunnan Province21,45. The following paper sets out the results of a simulation in relation to the analysis of driving forces of land use change and policy implications in Yunnan Province. The strategy of ‘leading ecological civilization, the positioning of Yunnan Province as a ‘radiation centre for South and Southeast Asia’, and the characteristics of multi-ethnic settlement are all given full consideration.In the simulation projections of the nature development scenario, the ecological protection scenario and the economic priority scenario in this study, the area of land for architecture is based on the outward expansion of the existing core cities, such as Kunming and Qujing, and the substantial loss of cultivated land in the periphery of these cities. This poses a potential threat to regional food security and adversely affects the sustainability of agriculture.This finding is consistent with the results of existing studies46–48, which observed a rapid decrease in cultivated land and a significant increase in architecture land in modelling studies conducted in various regions.Concurrently, the advancement of border economic activities has catalysed the accelerated development of port cities such as Honghe and Ruili. This phenomenon has the potential to adversely impact the surrounding cultivated and ecological land, a finding that aligns with extant research49. Consequently, the interplay between urbanisation, cultivated land protection, and alternative land uses must be meticulously coordinated within the framework of regional development.

In the cultivated land protection scenario, an augmentation in the area of cultivated land was accomplished despite the initiation of restrictive measures and escalated conversion expenses.However, the majority of this increased cultivated is located in the mountainous regions of Northeast Yunnan, with a significant proportion of it representing the conversion of ecological land (including forest and grassland) to cultivated. This transition may have a detrimental effect on the biodiversity of the ecosystems, and concomitantly, the quality of the cultivated is often found to be inferior to that of cultivated encroached upon by the expansion of architecture land.Consequently, in the context of realistic land-use planning, it is imperative to accord full consideration to the multifunctionality of land, thereby ascertaining the relationship between cultivated land, ecological protection and economic development. This approach entails optimising the land-use structure and integrating the relationship between agricultural production, ecological protection and social development.

Recommendations and suggestions

This analysis is based on a comparative study of the characteristics of land use evolution, existing problems, and the results of the multi-scenario simulation of this study. The analysis of land use change in Yunnan Province indicates that this phenomenon is the result of the interaction of multiple drivers. This observation suggests that Yunnan Province should consider implementing a differentiated governance approach and technological innovation.The present study proposes a governance framework of zoning control, i.e. divided into core control zone, ecological protection zone, and characteristic development zone, through multi-scenario modelling and mechanism analysis.Within the designated core control area (Dianzhong Urban Agglomeration), the ‘30% renewal of stock’ policy is strictly enforced, the Kunming model of industrial land replacement is promoted, and the non-agriculturalisation of cultivated land in the dam area is subject to stringent regulation.In the context of ecological protection zones, specifically in the north-west and south-west regions of Yunnan, there is a necessity to establish a mechanism that functions as a ‘carbon sink-compensation’ linkage. Furthermore, it is imperative to upgrade the compensation standard for the purpose of returning farmland to forests.In the special development zone (the border crossing zone, as previously mentioned), the double threshold of ‘land use efficiency and ecological depletion’ should be set. Furthermore, the cross-border corridors for Asian elephants should be rehabilitated.The analysis of land use change in Yunnan Province reveals a multifaceted dynamic, characterized by the interplay of natural constraints, policy interventions and socioeconomic drivers.The mountainous terrain, unique border geo-economic situation and multi-ethnic culture of Yunnan Province play a significant and complex role in land use change. Economic, demographic and accessibility factors have a significant impact on land use change in the context of accelerated urbanisation in the study area. Analysis of the current situation based on historical changes in land use is insufficient to support the results of the changes in the various types of land use that have occurred.The present study couples the Markov-FLUS model with the analysis of regional socioeconomic mechanisms, thereby revealing the inherent contradiction between ecological protection, food security and cross-border economic development. The study proposes a governance framework for differentiated management, with a view to achieving the dynamic regulation of territorial spatial optimisation.

Limitations and future works

The present paper employs the Markov-FLUS model to reveal the multi-scenario evolution law of land use change in Yunnan Province, thus providing a scientific basis for coordinating ecological protection, cultivated land security and economic development.However, the application of the model to the coupling of complex geographic environments and human policies, with their potentially consequential impact on the findings of the study, remains constrained.Despite the FLUS model in this study enhancing the accuracy of the model in each scenario by adjusting the neighbourhood factor parameters, it remains challenging to accurately depict the terrain blocking effect in the alpine canyon area of Yunnan Province. This is due to the utilisation of a 30 m×30 m grid for calculating the expansion probability, which is a limitation of the model.It can be posited that the alignment of the mountain range exerts an influence on the consistency with which the expansion of architecture land is aligned with the aforementioned range.It is important to note that the unique geographical location of Yunnan Province, situated as it is at the border, may result in a discrepancy between the anticipated outcomes of the simulation and the actual situation. This is due to the development of port economic zones along the China-Laos Railway.In this study, the explanatory power of the Markov-FLUS model in the complex region of the frontier is enhanced by incorporating regional adaptation improvements such as terrain gradient correction and adjusting border neighbourhood factor parameters.In the future, multi-model coupling, multi-scale data integration and artificial intelligence technology embedding will be used to build a more sophisticated land use simulation tool. This will provide dynamic decision support for the sustainable development of the southwestern mountainous-transboundary region.

Conclusions

In this study, the Markov-FLUS model was employed to simulate the quantitative and spatial changes of land use in Yunnan Province under different scenarios in 2040. The model was based on the spatial and temporal distribution characteristics of land use in Yunnan Province in 2000, 2010 and 2020.The principal conclusions are as follows:

(1)The model accuracy test demonstrates the Markov-FLUS model to be highly applicable in Yunnan Province.The structure of land use types in the study area during the period 2000–2020 was dominated by forest land, followed by grassland and cultivated, and the three categories accounted for more than 95% of the total area of the study area.The modelling of land use types in 2040 demonstrates a downward trend in the area of cultivated land and grassland in most scenarios, and an upward trend in the area of forests and architecture land.

(2)The analysis of land use changes in the study area reveals significant variations across the four scenarios. The cultivated protection scenario is notable for its predicted increase of merely 2 per cent in the area of cultivated, while the remaining three scenarios demonstrate a downward trend, characterised by the conversion of cultivated in the peri-urban area to architecture land.The area of architecture land has exhibited an increasing trend under all four scenarios, with the most substantial increase of 40.55 per cent observed under the economic priority scenario. The area of forest land increases most significantly under the ecological protection scenario, by 2.35 per cent.

(3)It is evident from the results of the multi-scenario simulation, in conjunction with the unique geographical positioning of Yunnan Province, that the implementation of a differentiated land management strategy is imperative. This strategy should encompass the establishment of a “core control area, an ecological protection area, and a characteristic development area.“In the natural development scenario, central urban areas expand outward by encroaching on farmland around towns and cities. Therefore, architecture land in central urban areas is controlled, development boundaries are strictly set, and the model of ‘inventory renewal’ is promoted, with ≥ 30 per cent of the new architecture land to be achieved through the renovation of old towns and the replacement of industrial land.According to the scenarios simulated in the cultivated land protection scenarios, the principle of ‘taking up and replenishing the best’ is implemented. This principle stipulates that the loss of one hectare of high-quality cultivated land to urban development should be offset by the replenishment of 1.2 hectares of equivalent cultivated land within the dam area.Concurrently, the government has the capacity to establish a soil database, implement information management for high-quality cultivated land, and establish standards for the delineation of basic farmland.The results of the ecological protection scenario simulation indicate that the ‘three screens, two belts and multiple points’ ecological security pattern should be continued to be promoted. In addition, the establishment of buffer zones is recommended, as are the prohibition of new projects within 200 m of the protected area and the prompt relocation of existing polluting enterprises.In the context of the economic priority scenario, the development intensity of the port area is subject to constraints with a view to reducing the risk of ecological land loss in the port area. Utilising the framework of Markov-FLUS multi-scenario simulation, this study elucidates the pivotal challenges associated with land use change in Yunnan Province, namely the discord between the loss of cultivated land, cross-border economic development and ecological protection. Consequently, this study proffers policy recommendations, namely the implementation of zoning control and diversified management strategies.Concurrently, the implementation of an AI-based real-time monitoring and policy feedback system is envisioned to facilitate the establishment of a closed-loop management framework encompassing the prediction-decision-assessment process.

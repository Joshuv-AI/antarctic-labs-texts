Safety of an inactivated enterovirus 71 vaccine administered concurrently with other vaccines among infants aged 6–11 months: An observational study using active surveillance
Authors: Linlin Wu, Yu Zhang, Jiechen Liu, Zhuoying Huang, Huiyong Shao, Xiaoying Ma, Xiaodong Sun
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTVaccine co-administration can efficiently increase vaccination uptake and timely immunization. This study aimed to evaluate the safety of the enterovirus 71 (EV71) vaccine administered alone or concurrently with other vaccines in infants 6–11 months. A total of 3,769 EV71 vaccine doses were administered to children in the active surveillance area, of which 1,909 were administered concurrently with other vaccines and 1,860 doses were administered alone. Active surveillance was conducted to observe adverse events (AEs) within 0–7 and ≥8 days after vaccination and to determine the incidence of reported AEs. The overall AE incidence was 2.12% (95% CI: 1.66%–2.58%), with 1.56% (95% CI:1.00%–2.12%) for the EV71 vaccine alone and 2.67% (95% CI: 1.95%–3.40%) for simultaneous administration of the EV71 vaccine and other vaccines (x2 = 5.612, p = .018). The solicited local AE incidence was 1.00% (95% CI: 0.55%–1.44%) in the EV71 vaccine co-administration group and 0.59% (95% CI: 0.24%–0.94%) in the EV71 vaccine alone group (x2 = 1.946, p = .018). The solicited systemic AE incidence was 1.68% (95% CI: 1.10%–2.25%) and 0.86% (95% CI: 0.44%–1.28%) in the EV71 vaccine co-administered and EV71 vaccine alone groups, respectively (x2 = 4.990, p = .025). No serious vaccine-related AEs were reported. Fever was the most common AE; no difference was observed in the incidence rate of fever between the two groups (x2 = 3.467, p = .063). Overall, AE incidence following EV71 vaccination alone or concurrently with other vaccines was acceptable; concurrent vaccination did not increase AE risk or severity.KEYWORDS: Concurrent administration, EV71 vaccine, safety

Introduction

Hand, foot, and mouth disease (HFMD), a substantial burden in Asia, is a common infectious disease among children aged <5 years caused by various enteroviruses, with enterovirus 71 (EV71) one of the major causative agents.1–3 A total of 809,901 laboratory-confirmed HFMD cases were reported in China between 2008 and 2017 of which 42.97% were caused by EV71.4 EV71-associated HFMD tends to be more dangerous and may cause fatal neurological complications, accounting for 73.8% and 92.5% of severe and fatal laboratory-confirmed HFMD cases, respectively.5–8

As there is no specific treatment for enteroviral infections vaccination is highly recommended for prophylaxis.1,8 Infants aged 6–71 months are encouraged to receive two EV71 vaccine doses with an interval of 1 month between doses and to complete the vaccination within 12 months.1,2 Three inactivated EV71 vaccines, showing high efficacy and good safety against EV71-associated HFMD after one or two doses,9–11 have been licensed and commercially available in China until December 2023.7 Pre-licensure clinical trials and after-marketing studies have also shown that EV71 vaccines were safe, while data on the impact of co-vaccination are rare.

Post-marketing surveillance is important to detect uncommon or long-term adverse events (AEs) that may not have been apparent during pre-licensure clinical trials. These trials, while rigorous, were conducted on a limited number of participants and over a relatively short period. Post-marketing data are also invaluable for informing public health policies and recommendations. As the EV71 vaccine is a non-immunization program vaccine in China, parents can voluntarily choose to vaccinate their children at their own expense, resulting in low vaccination rates and delayed vaccinations. Recent studies conducted in different provinces in China have indicated that the parents’ willingness to vaccinate their children with EV71 vaccines varies from 27.3% to 87.8%.12–14 However, a study conducted in Ningbo showed that approximately 24.05% of children received the EV71 vaccine; however, only 8.61% received timely vaccination. Furthermore, of the 21.97% of children who received the complete EV71 series dose, however, only 6.49% completed timely vaccination.15 In China, vaccines are divided into national immunization program (NIP) and non-immunization program (non-NIP) vaccines, and the NIP vaccines are provided by the government free of charge to citizens, whereas non- NIP vaccines refer to vaccines administered voluntarily by citizens at their own expense. According to the national immunization procedure, infants, and young children aged <2 years without vaccination contraindications need to receive at least 18 NIP vaccines plus several non-NIP vaccines, and the immune burden is heavy.

Owing to the busy vaccination schedule required by the NIP for infants within the first 2 years of life, concurrent administration might be a good choice for vaccination because of its practical value in terms of time, cost, and compliance with the vaccination schedule.16,17 Parents and vaccinators are mainly concerned about the safety of concurrent vaccinations. Safety evaluations of the EV71 vaccine combined with other vaccines are limited. A recent study showed that an inactivated EV71 vaccine co-administered with measles-mumps-rubella (MMR) and live-attenuated Japanese encephalitis vaccines was as effective and safe as separate vaccination, which may increase the likelihood of concomitant administration and increase vaccination uptake.16 To further evaluate the safety of concomitant vaccination of EV71 with NIP vaccines and non-NIP vaccines, we conducted this observational study to provide more evidence for the safety of simultaneous administration.

Methods

Study design and subjects

This study was open-designed, and healthy infants aged 6–11 months in Shanghai who received the EV71 vaccine concurrently with other vaccines or who received the EV71 vaccine alone were recruited. Infants and their guardians were supposed to participate in the safety observation after vaccination and follow the study procedures. Written informed consent was obtained from the infants’ parents or guardians before study initiation. No exclusion criteria were applied for this study.

This study was conducted in Baoshan, Songjiang, Yangpu, Putuo, and Qingpu Districts of Shanghai Municipality, China. The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Shanghai Municipal Center for Disease Control and Prevention. The study was designed and undertaken in compliance with good clinical practice guidelines and other related regulations and registered with ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05166044).

Vaccination

The inactivated EV71 vaccine (Vero Cell) used as non-NIP in this study was developed and produced by Sinovac Biotech Co., Ltd. and was packaged in a syringe with at least 3.0 Efficacy Unit (EU) of neutralization antibody titer for EV71 per dose (0.5 mL/vial). All the vaccines were tested and approved for lot release by the Chinese National Institute for Food and Drug Control (CNIFDC).

Study procedure

Eligible participants were recruited from August 10th 2020 to November 30th 2022. According to a study, the smallest sample size required to discover uncommon AEs, with a 0.1% incidence is 1,00017. After enrollment, participants were assigned to the EV71 vaccine co-administration group or the EV71 vaccine-alone group. In the co-administration group, infants received two or three injections at different injection sites during the same visit. In the EV71 vaccine alone group, infants received only one dose of the EV71 vaccine. All participants received one EV71 vaccine dose in the right upper arm. The interval between individual vaccinations was 30 days. Co-administration vaccines were applied to the left upper arm, legs or orally, following the vaccination protocol (Table 2).

Safety assessment

All participants immunized with the EV71 vaccine were interviewed by healthcare professionals who underwent comprehensive training sessions that focused on the proper identification, classification, and documentation of AEs. All vaccinated subjects were observed on-site for 30 min after vaccination for immediate AEs. Investigators recorded any systemic or local AEs within 30 days after vaccination using a diary card. On days 7 and 14, investigators were required to record the AEs on the diary cards by telephone follow-up. On day 30, the participants’ guardians visited the study site for assessment by the study investigators, who conducted face-to-face interviews to confirm safety. Serious AEs (SAEs) within 6 months after vaccination were collected by the investigators using a Chinese surveillance system on adverse events following immunizatiton (AEFI). In this study, solicited systemic AEs included fever (axillary temperature), acute allergic reaction, skin or mucous membrane abnormality, diarrhea, decreased appetite, nausea/vomiting, new-onset seizure, agitation, and decreased activity level; Solicited local AEs included haphalgesia, induration, redness, swelling, rash, and pruritus. Investigators checked the reports via face-to-face or telephone visits to ensure the completeness and accuracy of the AE data while assessing the temporal relationship between each AE and the vaccine administered.

AEs were graded according to the Standard Guiding Principles for Grading Adverse Events in Clinical Trials of Prophylactic Vaccines set by the China Food and Drug Administration.18 Investigators assessed the causal relationship between each AE and the product administered as either not related or related. The occurrence of AEs related to vaccination (i.e., adverse reactions) was calculated.

Statistical analysis

Safety analysis was performed for the safety set, which included participants who were vaccinated and had available safety data. Dates of AEs in children were entered using EpiData Software, and statistical analysis was performed using SPSS (version 17.0; SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). Pearson x2 -test or Fisher’s exact test was used to assess differences in characteristics between the groups. All tests were two-sided, and a p-value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Study participants

In this study, 3,769 children were enrolled from the Community Healthcare Service Centers of five districts in Shanghai. The mean age of the children at enrollment was 8.18 ± 1.60 months. Among the participants, 1,648 and 2,121 children were aged 6–8 and 9–11 months, respectively. A total of 3,769 EV71 vaccine doses were administered, of which 1,909 were administered concurrently with other vaccines, and 1,860 were administered alone.

Incidence rates of AEs

A total of 80 AEs were reported after EV71 vaccination, with an incidence of 2.12% (95% CI: 1.66%-2.58%). Among all AEs, AE incidences among males and females were 2.26% (95% CI: 1.60%–2.91%) and 2.03% (95% CI: 1.36%–2.69%), respectively; there were no significant differences in AE incidence between sexes. The incidences rates at 6–8 and 9–11 months were 1.82% (95% CI: 1.17%–2.47%) and 2.31% (95% CI: 1.63%–2.90%), respectively; no significant differences were observed between those aged 6–8 and 9–11 months (Table 3).

AEs classification among different groups

Most of AEs were solicited. The incidences of solicited local and systemic AEs were 0.80% (95% CI: 0.49%–1.05%) and 1.27% (95% CI: 0.93%–1.56%), respectively. There were two cases of unsolicited AEs, the incidence of unsolicited AEs was 0.05% (95% CI: 0.00%–0.13%). Between the two groups, the incidence of solicited local AEs was 1.00% (0.55%–1.45%) in the EV71 vaccine concomitant group and 0.59% (0.24%–0.94%) in the EV71 vaccine alone group (x2 = 1.946, p = .163). Redness and swelling were the most common local symptoms, and there were no significant differences in the incidence of redness and swelling between the two groups. The incidence of solicited systemic AEs was 1.68% (1.10%–2.25%) and 0.86% (0.44%–1.28%) in the two groups, respectively (x2 = 4.990, p = .025). Fever was the most common systemic AE. No significant differences were observed in the incidence of fever between the two groups (x2 = 3.467, p = .063). We found no difference in the grade distribution of fever between the two groups. Most AEs were grade 1 or 2 in severity, and the grade 3 AE incidence was 0.05% (Table 5). The two participants with grade 3 AEs had fever >39.5°C. Two cases with grade 3 AEs were in the two groups separately.

Discussion

Timely EV71 vaccine administration effectively prevents and controls diseases caused by EV71 infection, especially severe diseases and death. Concurrent administration of the EV71 vaccine with other vaccines can increase EV71 vaccination rates and reduce the workload of vaccination personnel and the cost of vaccination management; However, evaluating its safety is a common concern for recipients and vaccination staff.

In this post-marketing study, the good safety data supported the coadministration of the EV71 vaccine with other vaccines. The EV71 vaccine has received approval and is widely utilized in China, demonstrating robust immunogenicity, safety, and efficacy. Currently, limited research data are available regarding the safety of administering the EV71 vaccine concurrently with other vaccines that are recommended for concurrent administration within the same time frame. Post-marketing clinical trials have been conducted to evaluate the immunogenicity and safety of EV71 vaccine administered alone or in combination with hepatitis B vaccine, group A meningococcal polysaccharide vaccine, measles-rubella vaccine, and Japanese encephalitis vaccine in infants aged 6–8 months. The safety of concurrent administration of EV71 and influenza vaccines has also been evaluated. Although the study design of these trials differs from ours, the results were similar to our findings.19–21

The temporal relationship between each AE and the vaccine administered was assessed by professional investigators. The causal inference was based on World Health Organization recommendations and other studies.22,23 In this study, 3,769 EV71 vaccine doses were actively monitored in Shanghai. No SAEs were observed. Eighty AE cases were reported after EV71 vaccination, with a reported incidence of 2.1%. All AEs were improved or cured, similar to studies in other provinces in China.24,25 The incidence in EV71 vaccine co-administration was higher than the incidence of administration alone (x2 = 5.612, p = .018); however, the data are similar to those from other simultaneous vaccination safety observations.19–21 This study demonstrated that concurrent administration of the EV71 vaccine with other vaccines resulted in increased systemic events; however, it did not adversely affect the safety of the EV71 vaccine among infants aged 6–11 months. Our findings align with those of previous studies showing that concomitant vaccination does not promote a higher risk.26–28 Studies have found that AEs are related to children’s age. With an increase in age, a decreasing trend in the incidence of AEFI was observed, which may be related to the improvement in children’s immune function. Our study showed that AEs incidences were not significantly different between sexes and different age groups.

Concurrent administration of the EV71 vaccine in the present study and separate administration in other studies showed similar safety profiles, and AE classifications and symptoms were consistent with the manual and related literature.9 Most AEs were mild or moderate and resolved within a few days. Fever was the most common AE. None of the participants reported vaccination-associated SAEs. Administration of the EV71 vaccine alone resulted in fewer solicited local AEs than concurrent administration. The top three vaccines with reported AE incidences were influenza, MMR, and recombinant hepatitis B vaccines; however, the AE incidences were consistent with those of a previous study.16

In summary, the AE incidence after the concurrent administration of the EV71 vaccine and other vaccines among infants aged 6–11 months was safe. The AEs were mild and general, and SAEs were uncommon. Concurrent administration of the EV71 and other vaccines did not increase AE risk

This study had some limitations. The sample size of this active monitoring is not very large, and the monitoring scope can be further expanded to observe the occurrence of rare AEs.29,30 Our study did not evaluate the immunogenicity and safety of the EV71 vaccine administered simultaneously with other vaccines, and future studies need to be conducted.

Conclusion

This study’s results confirm the safety of the concurrent administration of the EV71 vaccine with other vaccines in infants aged 6–11 months. However, future studies should include larger sample sizes.

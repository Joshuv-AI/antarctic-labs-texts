The key role of acss1 in mammary gland development and lactation: a study based on gene co-expression network analysis
Authors: Feifan Wang, Lin Wang, Li Zou, Xiusen Zhang, Qin Yang, Baowei Jiao, Shegan Gao, Linge Tong, Hui Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundLactation physiological activity plays a key role in maternal health as well as infant nutrition and growth. The complex gene regulatory networks of lactation remain largely unexplored during mammary gland development. A deeper understanding of these processes is essential for identifying potential therapeutic targets to improve lactation efficiency.MethodsWe employed Gene Co-expression Network Analysis (WGCNA) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis to identify the gene expression patterns, regulatory mechanisms and cellular signaling pathways involved in mammary gland development during pregnancy and lactation. Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) was used to validate gene expression in mRNA levels at different stages of mammary development. Immunohistochemical staining was carried out to detect the protein level of ACSS1 at different stages of mammary development. Lactogenic differentiation assay and triglyceride (TAG) secretion detection were applied to investigate the effect of Acss1 on lactogenic differentiation and milk lipid metabolism during lactation.ResultsWe identified 23 gene modules, among which 8 modules were activated during pregnancy and lactation stage of mammary gland development. Cellular signaling pathways associated with mammary gland differentiation were enriched, including the FOXO and Wnt signaling pathways during pregnancy, as well as steroid biosynthesis and fatty acid metabolism during lactation. Among the identified genes, Acss1 emerged as a potential hub gene within module 8, exhibiting high connectivity with 312 other genes, and playing a critical role in lactogenic differentiation and milk lipid metabolism. Experimental validation showed that the gene expression pattern of Acss1 were highly consistent with microarray data at different stages of mammary gland development. Lactogenic differentiation assay and TAG secretion detection showed that Acss1 is crucial for lactogenic differentiation and affects the production and secretion of TAG during lactation in vitro.ConclusionThese findings suggesting Acss1 as a potential key regulatory factor of lactation efficiency.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12864-025-12033-7.Keywords: Mammary gland development, Lactation, Gene expression, Lipid metabolism, Milk secretion

Background

The lactating mammary gland plays a pivotal role in providing nutrition to offspring [1, 2]. Mammary gland development is a complex biological process influenced by various genetic and environmental factors [3, 4]. The incidence of lactation-related disorders, such as postpartum hypogalactia and mastitis, affects the health of both mothers and infants, contributing to a substantial healthcare costs and societal burdens [2]. Current clinical treatments predominantly focus on symptomatic relief rather than addressing the underlying biological mechanisms [5–7], leading to suboptimal outcomes. The mammary gland undergoes remarkable developmental changes during pregnancy and lactation [8]. However, the underlying molecular mechanisms remain inadequately understood. An investigation of these mechanisms is indispensable for identifying potential therapeutic targets to improve lactation efficiency.

Current researches focused on identifying individual genes and pathways involved in mammary gland lactogenic differentiation and milk lipid metabolism. For instance, studies have shown that Wnt signaling plays regulatory roles in initial development of the mammary rudiments and alveolar morphogenesis during pregnancy [9]. Additionally, FOXO is a key regulator of stem/progenitor cell maintenance/differentiation in multiple tissues [10]. However, Comprehensive analyses of gene expression patterns during pregnancy and lactation remain lacking. This gap hinders the development of innovative strategies to improve lactation outcomes and address associated health concerns. Therefore, it is urgently needed to elucidate how physiological changes is supported by these gene expression pattern and hub gene during these developmental phases. In the current study, we investigated the activation of gene modules across mammary gland developmental stages during pregnancy and lactation using Weighted Gene Co-expression Network Analysis (WGCNA) and KEGG pathway. We identified eight distinct expression patterns that correlated with critical biological processes, such as cellular differentiation, metabolic regulation, and apoptosis. These findings improve our understanding of mammary gland biology.

Previous research has established various gene expression changes associated with pregnancy and lactation [11, 12]. However, the specific molecular pathways and interactions remain inadequately explored. This study underscores the potential of the identified genes for lactation efficiency and mammary gland health, and finds that Acetyl-CoA synthetase short-chain family member 1 (Acss1) is a potential hub gene for lactation function. ACSS1 catalyzes the ligation of acetate and CoA to generate acetyl-CoA in the mitochondrial matrix, enabling the utilization of acetate for energy during conditions of nutrient deprivation [13], which has been implicated in fatty acid metabolism and energy homeostasis [14, 15]. Here, our findings confirm that alterations of Acss1 expression can lead to significant changes of lactogenic differentiation and triglyceride (TAG) secretion, suggesting its regulatory role in lactation efficiency in vitro.

Methods

Weighted gene co-expression networks analysis

To discover the functional patterns during the lactating mammary gland development, we extracted the gene expression data from microarray data (NCBI accession number: GDS1805; GSE12247) [16, 17]. For GDS1805, this microarray data detected the gene expression in mammary gland tissue at pregnant days 12 (P12) and P17, and lactation days 1 (L1), L2, and L9. The microarray data was from FVB mice fed with normal lab chow diets. For GSE12247, this microarray data detected the gene expression in mammary gland tissue at virgin 6 weeks, 10 weeks, and 12 weeks, pregnant days 1, 2, 3, 8.5, 12.5, 14.5, and 17.5, lactation days 1, 3, and 7, and involution days 1, 2, 3, 4, and 20. The accession IDs and detailed information of the exact subset from GSD1805 were listed in Table S1. The accession IDs and detailed information of GSE12247 were listed in Table S2. To determine whether include the outlier sample, these exact subsets were clustered based on their Euclidean distance. Subsequently, Principal Components Analysis (PCA) were performed to determine the variation in these exact subsets. We first obtained differentially expressed genes (DEGs) by comparison of time series groups using GEO2R function of Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database. Then, the normalized matrix data of DEGs from the corresponding dataset were subjected to weighted gene co-expression network construction using the WGCNA package (version 1.51) [18]. The co-expression similarity for adjacency matrix was determined by the internal parameter of the WGCNA package β. Modules of mammary gland lactogenic progress were identified by the Scale-free topology with “the power β = 12 for GDS1805, the power β = 16 for GSE12247” and the “minimum module size = 30”. Activation modules were further recognized using hierarchical clustering. Heatmaps were drawn by ‘gplots’ R package (version 3.1.0).

To reveal the biological process and signaling pathways in modules, Gene ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway were enriched using David online tools (https://david.ncifcrf.gov). The link weights calculated by WGCNA between Acss1 and other genes over 0.5 were considered as the closely connected genes of Acss1. And the network was constructed using Cytoscape software (version 3.2.0).

Cell lines and culture

HC11 cells, obtained from the Bernd Groner Lab at Ludwig Institute for Cancer Research were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), insulin (5 μg/ml, Sigma, #I5500), gentamicin sulfate (5 μg/ml), and epidermal growth factor (20 ng/ml, EGF, Gibco, #PGH0315). HEK293T cells were purchased from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC) and cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% FBS and 1% penicillin–streptomycin solution (PS). All cells were cultured in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO2 at 37 °C.

shRNA transduction

The construction and transduction of shRNAs were conducted as previously described [19]. To knockdown specific target genes, 1 × 106 cells were transduced with the PLKO.1 lentivirus carrying shRNA constructs (multiplicity of infection [MOI] of 100) overnight at 37 ℃ with 2 μg/mL polybrene. Specific shRNAs were constructed using the PLKO.1-based lentiviral shRNA technique [20]. Nontargeted control shRNA (sh-Control) were obtained from Addgene (pLKO.1 TRC control, Cat# 10,879) [20]. The shRNA sequences: sh1, 5’- C C G G G C C T T G T G T A T T T C C C A A G C T G T C G A G A G C T T G G G A A A T A C A C A A G G C T T T T T G—3’; sh2, 5’- C C G G G C G T C A T C A C A G A A A T C T T G A G T C G A G C T T T G A T A T C A T G A G G G T A C C T T T T T G—3’. To produce lentiviral particles, HEK-293 T cells were co-transfected with psPAX2, pMD2.G, and shRNA plasmids (4:1:3). The culture supernatant was collected 48 h post-transfection and passed through 0.45 μm filters.

Western blot analysis

Cell lysates were prepared by lysing cells with radioimmunoprecipitation assay (RIPA) buffer plus complete protease inhibitor. Total protein was quantified using the Pierce™ BCA Protein Assay Kit. Briefly, Protein (50 μg) from each sample was resolved by 10% SDS–polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (PAGE), and transferred to a polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membrane, and blotted with the various antibodies overnight at 4℃. The following primary antibodies were used: anti-ACSS1 (1:500) (Proteintech, 17,138–1-AP), XOR (1:2000) (Abcam, ab109235), β-casein (1:500) (abbiotec, 250,558), α-tubulin (1:5000) (Sigma, T5168), and anti-GAPDH (1:10,000) (Bioword, AP0063). Horseradish peroxidase anti-mouse and anti-rabbit (1:10 000) secondary antibodies were also used. Signals were detected using the super-signal-enhanced chemiluminescence system.

Immunohistochemical and immunofluorescence analysis

Breast tissue was handled and sectioned as described previously [21]. Antigen retrieval, permeabilization, inactivation of endogenous peroxidase, and blocking in the sections and tissue chips were performed according to previous study [21]. Then, specimens were incubated with antibodies specific for ACSS1 (1:50) (Proteintech, 17,138–1-AP) overnight at 4 ℃ and immunodetection was performed the following day using a 3,3’-diaminobenzidine (DAB) detection kit (Beyotime, P0202) according to the manufacturer’s instructions and counted after hematoxylin staining.

Total RNA extraction, reverse transcription, and quantitative real-time polymerase chain rection (qRT-PCR)

Total RNA was extracted using RNAiso Plus Reagent (TaKaRa) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Reverse transcription was performed using a HiScript® III RT SuperMix for qPCR Kit (Vazyme, #R323-01). Complementary DNA (cDNA) was subjected to qRT-PCR using a SYBR qPCR Master Mix (Vazyme, #Q711-02) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The primers were used as follows: Acss1, forward (F) 5′—A A C C T G G G A C A G A A G T G A G A A T—3′, reverse (R) 5′- A G C C A G C A A A T A C C A C T G T G T G—3′; Ceacam10, forward (F) 5′- A G G T T C C C T G G G T T G G A C T A C—3′, reverse (R) 5′- G C C C C A C T G T T T C C C T T G T A C—3′; Efhd1, forward (F) 5′- G G A C G G C T T T A T T G A C C T G A T G—3′, reverse (R) 5′- C A G C A G C A G C C T T G T G G A A A A T −3′; Nupr1, forward (F) 5′- G A G G A A G C T G C T G A C C A A G T T −3′, reverse(R) 5′- C T C C C T C T C C A G A A C C T C A C T −3′; Slc27a3, forward (F) 5′- G A C G T G A T C T A C C T C G C A C T C −3′, reverse (R) 5′- T T G C T C G G G G G C T G G T T G A C −3′; Xdh [21], forward (F) 5′- A T G A C G A G G A C A A C G G T A G A T −3′, reverse (R) 5′- T C A T A C T T G G A G A T C A T C A C G G T −3′; Perilipin2 [22], forward (F) 5′- G G A C A G C T A C G A T T A C A C C A T C −3′, reverse (R) 5′- G G G A A G C T C A G T A G A C G C T −3′; β-actin, forward (F) 5′- A C C C G C C A C C A G T T C G C C A T −3′, reverse (R) 5′- C C T T C T G A C C C A T T C C C A C C −3′.

Acss1 activation using the CRISPR-based synergistic activation mediator (SAM) effectors in HC11 cells

To establish HC11 cells expressing the CRISPR-based synergistic activation mediator (SAM) effectors [23], cells were transduced with lentivirus containing dCas9-VP64 (Addgene #61,425) and MS2-P65-HSF (Addgene #61,426). 48 h after transduction, cells were selected using blasticidin (2 μg/ml) and hygromycin (200 μg/ml) for 7 days. Surviving cells were passaged and cultured in medium with 1 μg/ml blasticidin and 100 μg/ml hygromycin. Then, the survived cells that expressed SAM were subsequently transduced with lentivirus expressing small guide RNAs (sgRNAs) of Acss1. The sequences of sgRNAs were used as follows: sg1, forward (F) 5′- C A C C G G C A G C G C T C T G A G A T A A G A T −3′, reverse (R) 5′- C C G T C G C G A G A C T C T A T T C T A C A A A −3′; sg2, forward (F) 5′- C A C C G G A C G G A G C T G A T G G T C A A C G −3′, reverse (R) 5′- C C T G C C T C G A C T A C C A G T T G C C A A A −3′.

Lactogenic differentiation of HC11 MEC

HC11 MEC were cultured and differentiated essentially as described earlier [24]. Briefly, HC11 cells were grown to confluence for 2 days in RPMI 1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal calf serum, 5 μg/ml insulin, 10 mM HEPES, 10 ng/ml epidermal growth factor (EGF). Then, cells were shifted into serum free medium without EGF but with insulin for 24 h. Lactogenic differentiation of HC11 cells was initiated by differentiation media consisting of RPMI 1640, 5% FBS, insulin (5 µg/ml), hydrocortisone (1 µg/ml) and prolactin (5 µg/ml, ProSpec, #cyt-240) and cells were then incubated at 37 °C for 3–7 days under 5% CO2 to complete lactogenic differentiation.

Statistical analysis

All quantitative data are shown as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). Statistical differences were determined using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Dunnett's post hoc multiple comparison test. P-value less than 0.05 were considered significant. All statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism v8.0.

Results

Genes regulation during the period between pregnancy and lactation

The differentiation of mammary alveolar epithelial cells and attaining the secretory function occur during late pregnancy [25]. It has been reported that the expression of milk protein genes and lipid synthesis genes dramatically changed during P17 (day 17 of pregnancy) to L2 (day 2 of lactation) [12]. To identify key genes involved in lactation and lipid metabolism, microarray data of gene expression in mammary gland tissue at pregnant days 12 (P12) and P17, and lactation days 1 (L1), L2, and L9 were extracted for further studies [16]. We did not find the Outlier sample using Euclidean distance detection (Figure S1A). The PCA results displayed these data with high repeatability (Figure S1B). Then, a WGCNA analysis were employed to determine the gene expression pattern during P12, P17, L1, L2, and L9. 23 modules were identified (Table S3). Among these modules, 6 modules (named gray, blue, yellow, salmon, red, and turquoise modules) showed stage-specific gene activation during mammary gland development from pregnancy to lactation (Figure S2). We further investigated the gene activation in these 6 modules using hierarchical clustering. Eight new modules (named modules 1–8) were identified, which exhibited a visibly gene activation at different days of mammary gland development (Table S4-5, Fig. 1).

Identification of specific modules during pregnancy and lactation stage of mammary gland development. The modules in Figure S2 were further investigated using hierarchical clustering, 8 modules were identified, that showed stage-specific gene activation at pregnancy and lactation stages of mammary gland development. Module 1 represented genes specifically activated at P12. These genes were involved in organic substance, proteasome assembly, and negative regulation of apoptosis process. In module 2, 101 genes were activated at P17. Transcription, negative regulation of cell proliferation, and lactate metabolic process were significantly enriched in this module. In module 3, 123 genes were activated at L1. Cell redox homeostasis, glucose homeostasis, and mammary gland epithelial cell proliferation were significantly enriched in this module. In module 4, 34 genes were activated at L2. Cell differentiation, phosphorylation, and transport were significantly enriched in this module. In module 5, 45 genes were activated at L9. Negative regulation of stem cell differentiation was significantly enriched in this module. In module 6, 556 genes showed higher expression at P12 and P17 than that at L1, L2 and L9. Proteasome mediated protein catabolic process, epithelial cell differentiation, and somatic stem cell population maintenance were significantly enriched in this module. Meanwhile, 164 genes in module 7 were higher expressed at L1, L2 and L9 than that at P12 and P17. Lipid metabolic process, positive regulation of Wnt signaling pathway, and glucose metabolic process were significantly enriched in this module. In module 8, a total of 3975 genes were enriched and their expression gradually increased from pregnancy to lactation. Multicellular organism development, mammary gland development, and regulation of energy homeostasis were significantly enriched in this module. These findings suggested that the differentiation of mammary alveolar epithelial cells was fulfilled at the late stage of pregnancy, which is consistent with previous reports [25–28]. Genes that regulate milk secretion and lipid metabolism mainly are expressed in the lactation stage.

Identification of key pathways for differentiation of mammary alveolar epithelial cells and lactation

To discover the pathways enriched in each module, KEGG analysis was carried out (Fig. 2). The pathways were significantly enriched in modules 6–8 (Table S6). In module 6, FOXO signaling pathway, sphingolipid signaling pathway, TGF-β signaling pathway, Wnt signaling pathway, AMPK signaling pathway, thyroid hormone signaling pathway, NF-kappa B signaling pathway, signaling pathways regulating pluripotency of stem cells, and glucagon signaling pathway were significantly enriched (Fig. 2A), suggesting these pathways are involved in the differentiation of mammary alveolar epithelial cells during pregnancy. In module 7, steroid biosynthesis, fatty acid metabolism, carbon metabolism, bile secretion, and citrate cycle were significantly enriched (Fig. 2B), suggesting their function in regulating milk secretion and lipid metabolism during lactation. In module 8, Neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction, MAPK signaling pathway, Ras signaling pathway, PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, Calcium signaling pathway, ErbB signaling pathway, Hippo signaling pathway, cGMP-PKG signaling pathway, and HIF-1 signaling pathway were significantly enriched (Fig. 2C), suggesting that these pathways might be important for differentiation of mammary alveolar epithelial cells and lactation during mammary gland development.

Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment of genes in modules 6 to 8. Genes in modules 6–8 were subjected into KEGG enrichment. (p < 0.05)

Acss1 is suggested as a potential hub gene for lactation function.

Subsequently, to identify genes related to lactation function, we further performed hierarchical clustering in module 8. A small cluster of genes showed repeatable high expression during lactation (Fig. 3A, Table S7). To discover the potential hub genes in the small clusters, we constructed the co-expression network using these genes and found Acss1 connected with 312 genes (link weights > 0.5) (Fig. 3B, Table S8). To further reveal the function of Acss1 and its co-expression genes during lactation, GO enrichment analysis showed positive regulation of secretion by cell, response to chemical, regulation of localization, positive regulation of transport, blood circulation, regulation of hormone levels, and positive regulation of developmental process (Fig. 3B). Further network construction suggested that Acss1 was a hub gene in the co-expression network of the small cluster genes (Fig. 3C). These results indicated that Acss1 might be a key gene for lipid metabolism, milk secretion, even the differentiation of mammary alveolar epithelial cells.

To validate the expression pattern of Acss1 and whether it involved in lactation in the other dataset, we downloaded independent dataset (GSE12247) [17] and conducted a WGCNA analysis for the gene expression pattern during virgin, pregnancy, lactation, and involution stages. We did not find the Outlier sample using Euclidean distance detection (Figure S3A). The PCA results displayed these data with high repeatability (Figure S3B). Among identified 7 modules, turquoise-GSE12247 module showed significantly gene activation at lactation stage of mammary gland development (Fig. 4A and Table S9). Similar with module 8, a total of 1054 genes were gradually increased from pregnancy day 14.5 to lactation day 7 in turquoise-GSE12247 module (Fig. 4B-C and Table S10). Acss1, Carcinoembryonic antigen-related cell adhesion molecule 10 (Ceacam10), nuclear protein-1 (Nupr1), solute carrier family 27 (fatty acid transporter), member 3 (Slc27a3), and xanthine dehydrogenase Gene (Xdh) showed highly expression at lactation stage of mammary gland development (Fig. 4D). Notably, Acss1 showed a higher expression at late pregnancy (P17.5) than that at virgin and early pregnancy (Fig. 4D). These results confirmed that Acss1 is closely associated with lactation.

The expression pattern of key genes was validated in different stages of mammary gland development.

To validate the above gene expression pattern associated with mammary gland development and lactation, 5 representative genes, including Acss1, Ceacam10, EF-hand domain-containing protein D1 (Efhd1), Nupr1, and Slc27a3 in module 8, were selected for qRT-PCR detection in mammary gland tissue at different developmental stages. The results showed that the mRNA level of these genes significantly increased (Acss1, ∼12 fold; Ceacam10, ∼60 fold; Efhd1, ∼six fold; Nupr1, ∼five fold; Slc27a3, ∼30 fold) at lactation stage of mammary gland development, compared with that at virgin stage (Fig. 5A-E), which was consistent with the above outcome in Figs. 1, 3A and 4D. The expression of Acss1, Ceacam10, and Efhd1 also showed a significantly increasing in pregnancy stage in comparison with that at virgin stage (Fig. 5A-C), suggesting their potential regulatory role in pregnancy stage. We further confirmed the protein level of ACSS1 in mammary gland tissue at different developmental stages using immunohistochemical staining (IHC). Compared with the virgin stage, the protein level of ACSS1 greatly increased at pregnancy and lactation stages of mammary gland development (Fig. 5F), which was consistent with the mRNA expression trends in Fig. 5A. Lactogenic differentiation of HC11 mammary epithelial cells (MEC) is considered as a model to comprehensively map the genes expression and their regulatory role in the transition of MEC to lactogenic cells. To assess whether these genes involved in the differentiation of mammary alveolar epithelial cells, we performed lactogenic differentiation of HC11 MEC as described earlier [24]. Compared with the confluent primary HC11 MEC (Undifferentiated), the mRNA levels of these genes significantly increased (Acss1, ∼60 fold; Ceacam10, ∼15 fold; Efhd1, ∼15 fold; Nupr1, ∼five fold; Slc27a3, ∼four fold) in differentiated MEC (Differentiated) (Fig. 5G). Acss1 expression showed the largest increase range (Fig. 5G). We further validated these observations in different mammary gland development stages of goat using the published RNA-seq data [29]. The result showed that Acss1 and Nupr1 were highly expressed at lactation stage of goat mammary gland, in comparison with the pregnancy (gestation) and dry stage (non-lactation stage) (Fig. 5H). These above findings imply that Acss1 is involved in lactation of mammary gland development.

Alterations in Acss1 expression leads to significant changes in lactogenic differentiation and lipid metabolism.

These above results suggested that Acss1 played a regulatory role in the differentiation of mammary alveolar epithelial cells in vitro. Accordingly, we evaluated the functions of Acss1 in lactogenic differentiation of HC11 MEC. We first generated Acss1-specific short hairpin (sh) RNA to silence endogenous Acss1 expression (Acss1-sh) in HC11 MEC. Both Acss1-sh#1 and Acss1-sh#2, which induced significant knocking down (KD) effects, were adopted for further study (Fig. 6A and B). Furthermore, using lactogenic differentiation assay, we further observed that Acss1-KD resulted in a considerable inhibitory effect on mammary dome formation compared with the sh-TRC control group (Fig. 6C and D). The production and secretion of TAG also was downregulated upon Acss1-KD (Fig. 6E and F). These findings suggested that Acss1 positively regulated lactogenic differentiation and the lipid metabolism during lactation in vitro.

We also activated Acss1 in HC11 MEC using the CRISPR-based synergistic activation mediator (SAM) effectors [23]. Then, Acss1-specific sgRNA (Acss1-sg) were employed to activate endogenous Acss1 expression (Acss1-OV) in HC11 MEC. Both Acss1-sg#1 and Acss1-sg#2, which induced significant overexpression (OV) effects, were adopted for further study (Fig. 7A and B). Using lactogenic differentiation assay, we further observed that Acss1-OV resulted in a considerable increasing effect on mammary dome formation compared with the sgGFP control group (Fig. 7C and D). The production and secretion of TAG also were upregulated upon Acss1-OV compared with the sgGFP control group (Fig. 7E and F). These results are consistent with the findings in Fig. 6. Xanthine oxidoreductase (XOR, encoded by the Xdh gene) and Perilipin family proteins play essential roles in milk lipid synthesis and secretion [29, 31, 32]. Our further results showed that activation of Acss1 increased the expression of Xdh and Perilipin2 (Fig. 7G and H), confirming the positive regulatory role of Acss1 in milk lipid synthesis and secretion during lactation in vitro. Furthermore, we detected the protein levels of β-casein (milk protein, as a marker of lactation), XOR, and ACSS1 during HC11 differentiation. Compared with D0 group (day 0 of differentiation; cells were first shifted into starvation medium; undifferentiated cells), their expression levels were coordinately increased during differentiation of HC11 cells (D2, D4, and D6 following the shift into differentiation medium) (Fig. 7I), which is consistent with the previous study [4]. These results suggested the regulatory role of Acss1 in lactogenic differentiation as well as milk lipid synthesis and secretion in vitro.

Discussion

The lactating mammary gland plays a crucial role in providing nutrition and immune protection to offspring [1, 2, 33]. The societal and economic challenges related to lactation disorder highlight the importance of investigating the biological mechanisms of lactation during mammary gland development [34]. This study aimed to clarify the gene expression patterns and the regulatory mechanisms involved in the mammary gland development, especially during the key stage—pregnancy and lactation. We sought to pinpoint key genes and pathways that play a regulatory role in differentiation of mammary alveolar epithelial cells and milk lipid metabolism by WGCNA analysis. Here, we showed notable gene activation patterns and emphasized the potential regulatory role of the Acss1 gene in lactogenic differentiation of MEC, lipid metabolism and milk secretion during lactation. These findings may inspire us to further explore the way for future therapeutic approaches and strategies to improve lactation outcomes in both agricultural and clinical contexts.

A precise and coordinated regulations of gene expression and molecular mechanisms are essential for the process of lactogenesis [35, 36]. Our findings highlight the complex of gene expression that occur in the mammary gland during pregnancy and lactation. Using WGCNA and hierarchical clustering, we identified 8 gene modules that showed significant activation patterns during the transition from pregnancy to lactation. For instance, the module 1, activated on lactation day 1, was enriched in pathways related to cell redox and glucose homeostasis, illustrating the metabolic changes at the beginning of lactation. Notably, the module 8, which included an impressive 3975 genes with gradually increasing expression, was significantly linked to the development of multicellular organisms and mammary gland development, underscoring the intricate regulation of genes during lactogenic differentiation. Additionally, the result of KEGG pathway analysis exhibited the signaling pathways that regulate mammary gland development. During pregnancy, pathways such as Wnt and FOXO signaling were notably enriched, suggesting their regulatory roles in cellular differentiation and proliferation. Consistently, previous study has reported that Wnt signaling is strongly implicated in initial development of the mammary rudiments at embryonic stage and alveolar morphogenesis that occurs during pregnancy [9]. FOXO also was considered as a key regulator of stem/progenitor cell maintenance/differentiation in several tissues [10]. During lactation, pathways related to steroid biosynthesis and fatty acid metabolism were enriched, reflecting the metabolic requirements in the process of milk production. These findings align with previous studies [37, 38], suggesting these pathways are crucial for the physiological adaptations of the mammary gland.

Among the genes identified, Acss1 in the module 8, showing strong connections with the other genes that play significant roles in secretion and transport processes during lactation. Previous studies have demonstrated that Acss1 plays an important role in fatty acid metabolism and energy homeostasis [14, 15]. Accordingly, our GO enrichment analysis highlighted that Acss1 was involved in positively regulating milk secretion, lipid metabolism and lactogenic differentiation of MEC. XOR is highly expressed in the lactating mammary gland and is secreted into milk associated with the milk fat globule membrane [29, 31]. Perilipin family proteins is also considered as a key regulatory factor in milk lipid synthesis and secretion [32]. Consistently, we found that ACSS1 and XOR showed the same protein expression trend during lactogenic differentiation of HC11 (Fig. 7). Activation of Acss1 expression increased the mRNA levels of Xdh and Perilipin2 in HC11 cells (Figs. 7). Furthermore, experimental validation confirmed that the alteration of Acss1 expression level changed both lactogenic differentiation and TAG secretion in vitro (Figs. 6 and 7), underscoring its regulatory role in lactogenic differentiation and milk lipid metabolism.

The development of the mammary gland and the lactogenic differentiation of MEC are intricate biological processes influenced by various endocrine factors such as glucocorticoids, prolactin, insulin, and epidermal growth factor (EGF) [2, 35]. The HC11 mammary dome formation assay was employed to validate the regulatory function of Acss1 in lactogenic differentiation and milk lipid metabolism. In this in vitro model [5], lactogenic hormones, such as glucocorticoids, prolactin, insulin, and EGF, were used to induce the lactogenic differentiation. We observed that the expression of Acss1 coordinately increased upon the treatment with these lactogenic hormones, indicating that lactogenic hormones induced the lactogenic differentiation and the expression of its potential regulators including Acss1. Meanwhile, we blocked Acss1 expression by shRNA to measure the lactogenic hormones-induced mammary dome formation and milk lipid synthesis and secretion. The results demonstrated considerable inhibitory effect on lactogenic hormone’s effect, suggesting the mediating roles of Acss1 in lactogenic differentiation and milk lipid metabolism in vitro.

The lactation function of mammary gland has attracted much attention in recent years. Twigger et al. [8] conducted a comprehensive single-cell transcriptomic analysis of over 110,000 viable breast cells isolated from human milk and non-lactating breast tissue to elucidate the cellular changes during lactation. The findings revealed that human milk is predominantly composed of luminal epithelial cells and various immune cells. They indicated two distinct secretory cell types that share similarities with luminal progenitor cells. This research provides a reference map for understanding the physiological changes in the mammary gland during lactation. Here, the current work focuses on the critical developmental period of mammary alveolar epithelial cells differentiation and the secretory function. The key regulators of lactogenic differentiation and milk lipid metabolism during lactation were identified. We also further experimentally validated that Acss1 might be a regulator of lactogenic differentiation. Li et al. [39] investigated the metabolic alterations in bovine mammary epithelial cells (BMECs) induced by interferon-gamma (IFN-γ) treatment. They showed that the complex biochemical landscape altered by IFN-γ in milk lipid metabolism, indicating that IFN-γ not only affects arginine levels but also disrupts several metabolic pathways that are crucial for cell function and homeostasis during lactation. Here, current work concentrated on identifying the physiological regulator of milk lipid metabolism during lactation. We finally found that Acss1 can regulate the TAG metabolism in mouse HC11 cells, suggesting its potential role in lactation. Bovine and goat are important lactating livestock. For the lactating livestock dairy goats, we conducted a cross-validation in mammary gland tissue of goat using the published RNA-seq data [30]. The result showed that Acss1 was highly expressed at lactation stage, compared with the non-lactation stage (Fig. 5H). Accordingly, Dai et al. have reported that Acss1 was significantly up-regulated at lactation stage, compared with the non-lactation stage, in bovine [40]. Whether Acss1 is a key regulatory factor in other species, especially the economically valuable lactating livestock dairy goats and bovine, needs to be further investigated.

Although our findings suggested that Acss1 might be key regulator in lactation of mouse mammary gland. It still needs efforts to determine whether modulating Acss1 expression is an innovative strategy for improving lactation outcomes in both dairy production and human health. Here, the role of Acss1 in the lactation differentiation was investigated by evaluating mammary dome formation in vitro. The absence of in vivo validation weakens the ability to draw causal conclusions and limits the applicability of the findings. All in vitro functional validation should be further confirmed in vivo. Thus, Acss1 mammary gland conditional knockout mice model would be helpful to further delineate the roles and mechanism of Acss1 in lactogenic differentiation in the future studies. Here, the sample size used in this analysis might not fully capture the wide range of variability in mammary gland development, potentially oversimplifying the complex biological processes. Moreover, a continuous time-course analysis was not applied in this study. Future research should expand sample size and apply single-cell RNA sequencing to capture the full spectrum of lactation differentiation and milk lipid metabolism. Future research should also investigate the interactions between the identified genes and pathways within a broader physiological framework, and explore the possibility of the clinical implications of these discoveries in vivo.

Conclusions

In conclusion, using WGCNA and hierarchical clustering combined with experimental validation, we provide the gene expression pattern and regulatory networks that govern mammary gland development. Particularly, we highlight the regulatory role of Acss1 during pregnancy and lactation. These findings suggest the possibilities to develop therapeutic approaches and strategies to improve lactation outcomes in both agricultural and clinical contexts in furture.

Comparative analysis of codon usage bias and phylogenetic relationships in chloroplast genomes across 49 Dendrobium species
Authors: Changwei Wu, Rong Liu, Chaohai Li, Mingyue Shang, Yujiao Zhang, Kanglin Bai, Yaping Wang, Ying Zhou, Yuan Lin, Baozhong Duan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDendrobium is the second-largest genus in the Orchidaceae family. However, research on codon usage bias (CUB) in Dendrobium genus remains relatively limited. This knowledge gap impedes progress in genetic engineering and the breeding of superior varieties, making it challenging to meet the growing demand for higher yield and quality of Dendrobium.ResultsThe cp genomes of Dendrobium species exhibited codon composition biased toward A/T bases, with GC content below 50% (GC1 > GC2 > GC3). Eleven to nineteen optimal codons were identified across these species, with the third base of most codons predominantly ending with A/U. Parity rule 2-bias plots, effective number of codons plots, and neutrality plot analysis further revealed that natural selection serves as the primary driver of CUB variation, while mutation pressure played a secondary role. Notably, the positions of some species were different in the phylogenetic topologies from cp genomes and cp CDS.ConclusionsCUB is an effective tool for investigating the phylogenetic evolution of Dendrobium. Background

The Dendrobium, the second-largest genus in the Orchidaceae family, comprises approximately 1,600 species primarily distributed across Asia and Oceania [1]. According to Schlechter, the genus is divided into four Subgenera and 41 sections based on the presence or absence of sheath leaves at the base [2]. Among these species, more than 40 have been used in traditional Chinese medicine for centuries and are valued for their nourishing properties [3, 4]. Modern research has confirmed that Dendrobium species contain bioactive compounds, including alkaloids, sesquiterpenes, and polysaccharides, which demonstrate diverse pharmacological activities such as anti-inflammatory, anti-tumor, and blood glucose regulation properties. These medicinal benefits have generated significant demand for Dendrobium in the pharmaceutical and food industries [5, 6]. However, the overharvesting of wild Dendrobium species and habitat destruction have resulted in a substantial decline in their wild populations, making cultivars the primary source of medicinal material [7]. Unfortunately, these cultivars are highly susceptible to anthracnose, soft rot, root rot, and leaf spot [8]. Although some disease-resistant Dendrobium varieties have been developed [9], a shortage of high-quality cultivars with robust disease resistance continues to impede the industry’s growth. Therefore, to meet the demand for sustainable development and advancement in Dendrobium industry, genetic engineering technology is essential for developing Dendrobium species with superior traits.

In recent years, advancements in sequencing technology and genetic engineering have intensified interest in exploring codon usage bias (CUB) in chloroplast (cp) genomes for germplasm improvement. The cp genome is particularly well-suited for CUB studies due to its small size, high conservation, and slow evolutionary rate [10]. These features make it an ideal model for analyzing CUB patterns. CUB is crucial in multiple cellular processes, including DNA transcription, mRNA stability, translation efficiency, and accuracy [11, 12]. Research has shown that CUB can significantly affect the expression of heterologous proteins [13], offering strategies to optimize exogenous gene expression and clarify the molecular mechanisms underlying gene regulation. This knowledge provides valuable guidance for targeted molecular breeding approaches [14]. For example, combining cp genetic engineering with codon optimization has significantly enhanced the environmental adaptability of transgenic Medicago sativa [15]. Currently, a considerable amount of research has focused on phylogenetics, genetic diversity, and molecular identification of Dendrobium species [16, 17]. However, studies addressing CUB in Dendrobium species remain limited, hindering progress in breeding superior germplasm.

Herein, we investigate the cp genomes of 49 Dendrobium species using high-throughput sequencing technology and examine the factors influencing CUB. Phylogenetic trees were constructed based on both cp genomes and coding sequence (CDS), and genetic relationships were further assessed using partial least squares discriminant analysis (PLS-DA) derived from relative synonymous codon usage (RSCU) values. The primary objectives of this research are (i) to provide a theoretical foundation for understanding evolutionary mechanisms and constructing exogenous gene expression systems in Dendrobium species and (ii) to offer valuable guidance for developing targeted molecular breeding strategies. This study greatly enriches cp genomic codon data and provides new insights to support future genetic engineering and applied research in Dendrobium.

Materials and methods

Plant material and DNA sequencing

Fresh and healthy leaves from 49 Dendrobium species were collected and immediately stored at −20 °C. Species names and accession numbers are provided in Table S1. Prof. Duan authenticated all samples based on the Flora of China (FOC), and voucher specimens were deposited at Dali University. Total genomic DNA was extracted using the Plant Genomic DNA Kit (Tiangen, Beijing, China), following the manufacturer’s instructions. DNA quantity was measured with a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Scientific, United States), and agarose gel electrophoresis was used to confirm the presence of PCR products. Sequencing was performed on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform (Illumina, San Diego, CA, United States).

Genome assembly and annotation

Cp genome assembly was performed using the GetOrganelle v.1.6.4 on a Linux system [18], which integrated Bowtie2 v.2.4.4, SPAdes v3.13.0, and BLAST v.2.5.0 for clean-read analysis [19–21]. The assembled contigs were visually examined and optimized using Bandage v0.8 to remove redundancies, resulting in high-quality contigs facilitating the preliminary reconstruction of circular cp genomes [22]. Assembly accuracy was verified with Circulator v2.0.1 by assessing read-mapping coverage and confirming contig junctions [23].

Subsequently, genome annotation was performed using CPGAVAS2 (http://47.96.249.172:16019/analyzer/annotate) based on established cp genome databases [24]. Annotation quality was further improved through verification with the online tool GB2sequin (https://chlorobox.mpimp-golm.mpg.de/GenBank2Sequin.html), followed by manual correction of inconsistent annotations [25]. The finalized sequences were submitted to the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) database, with accession numbers listed in Table S2 (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/).

CDS were extracted from the 49 cp genomes through Geneious v.9.1.4 software [26]. Sequences were selected for further analyses if they began with ATG and ended with TAA, TAG, or TGA codons. Sequence length < 300 bp, those lacking stop codons, and those containing ambiguous bases were excluded.

Nucleotide composition analysis

Calculations were done for Dendrobium species’ CDS numbers, codons’ base compositions at the first/second/third sites (GC1/GC2/GC3), and overall GC (GCall). The nucleo-base content at the location of the third codon (A3, T3, C3, G3), the effective number of codons (ENC), codon adaptation index (CAI), and codon bias index (CBI) were performed using the Codon W (http://codonw.sourceforge.Net) software [27]. Furthermore, a correlation analysis between these parameters was conducted, and the heatmap analysis was performed using the online Chiplot software (https://www.chiplot.online) [28].

RSCU analysis

The RSCU analysis of 49 Dendrobium species was clustered using online Chiplot software (https://www.chiplot.online) for clustering with RSCU, hierarchical clustering, and Euclidean distance. The ratio of the observed usage frequency of one codon in a CDS to the expected usage frequency in the synonymous codon family is used to determine the RSCU. The codons were considered to have a solid positive with an RSCU value > 1 [28].

PR2-plot analysis

Parity rule 2-bias plot (PR2-plot) analysis was performed to determine the factors affecting CUB. A scatter plot was generated with G3/(G3 + C3) as the abscissa (x-axis) and A3/(A3 + T3) as the ordinate (y-axis) for PR2-plot analysis. The center of the plot is where A = U and G = C, showing no deviation between mutation pressure and natural selection in codon usage [29]. The vector distance between the remaining and center points signified the magnitude and direction of the base shift [30].

Neutrality plot analysis

Neutrality plot analysis is used to demonstrate the effects of mutation pressure and natural selection on CUB [31]. GC3 draws the scatter plot on the x-axis and GC12 (average of GC1 and GC2) on the y-axis. The regression slope between GC12 and GC3 tests the contributions of mutation pressure and natural selection. Mutation pressure plays a significant role in CUB if the regression slope is close to 1. In contrast, natural selection plays a primary role in CUB if the regression slope is close to 0 [32].

ENC-Plot analysis

The ENC-plot analysis can visually reflect the codon usage patterns of genes and is used to measure the extent to which mutation pressure and natural selection influence CUB [29]. The abscissa is the GC3 value, while the ordinate is the ENC value. The standard curve was constructed using ENC values, which were derived according to Eq. 1 [33]. When a gene’s ENC value approaches or aligns with the standard curve, it indicates that the codon preference of the gene is primarily influenced by mutation pressure; otherwise, it is predominantly influenced by selective pressure. Moreover, Eq. 2 shows the difference between the observed ENC (ENCobs) and expected values (ENCexp) [12, 34], and a frequency distribution table was made based on ENC ratios.

Selection of optimal codons

The ENC values of 49 Dendrobium species were arranged in ascending order, and high and low gene expression Libraries were created by selecting the top and bottom 10% of the genes, respectively. Subsequently, CodonW was employed to compute the RSCU value and ΔRSCU value (ΔRSCU = RSCU high expression—RSCU low expression) for each codon in the two expression libraries. Optimal codons were identified based on the criteria of RSCU > 1 (indicating high frequency) and ΔRSCU ≥ 0.08 (indicating high expression) [35].

Phylogenetic analysis

The sequence datasets used for comparative analysis and maximum likelihood (ML) phylogenetic tree construction were obtained from the NCBI database, and the accession numbers were listed in Table S2. Complete cp genome sequences of 49 Dendrobium species and two closely related species in the Orchidaceae Juss (Cymbidium Hookerianum and C. lowianum) were included for phylogenetic analyses using ML methods. The CDS were extracted through PhyloSuite v1.2.2 software for each species [22], and MAFFT (V.7.0) (https://mafft.cbrc.jp/alignment/server/) was utilized for aligning the assembled cp genomes and CDS sequences [36]. Phylogenetic trees were constructed using IQ-TREE 2.0.5 (http://www.iqtree.org/) based on the cp genome and CDS with specific parameters: -m GTR + F + R10 -bb 1000 -alrt 1000 -nt AUTO [36]. To further investigate the genetic relationships within the Dendrobium genus, PLS-DA was also performed in SIMCA software based on RSCU values [37].

Results

Analysis of codon base composition

The contents of T3, C3, A3, G3, GC1, GC2, GC3, and GCall in cp coding gene codons of 49 Dendrobium species were analyzed in this study (Figure S1, Table S3). The results revealed an asymmetric expression of the four nucleotides in these codons. Specifically, the third position of codons showed a higher frequency of A/T usage compared to G/C.The nucleotide expression frequencies at the third codon position were as follows: T3 (45.72%−47.79%), A3 (40.92%−43.96%), G3 (16.68%−19.13%), and C3 (16.64%−19.51%). Analysis of GC content across the coding regions showed values of 42.14%−48.65% for GC1, 37.37%−41.08% for GC2, and 28.81%−32.14% for GC3, all of which were below 50%. This indicates an overall preference for codons rich in A/T nucleotides. Notably, the GC content exhibited the trend GC1 > GC2 > GC3, highlighting a stronger preference for codons ending with A/T in the cp coding genes of Dendrobium species.

GC content is a critical indicator of genomic characteristics. we analyzed the correlation among GC1, GC2, GC3, and GCall (Fig. 1, Table S4). The results revealed that GCall was significantly correlated with GC1, GC2, and GC3 (P < 0.01). Furthermore, GC1 and GC2 were significantly correlated (P < 0.05), indicating that the GC composition at the first and second codon positions may influence each other. In contrast, GC3 showed no correlation with GC1 or GC2, suggesting that the GC composition at the third position is independent of the first and second positions.

RSCU analysis

RSCU is an important parameter for measuring codon preference. RSCU > 1 indicates a strong preference for that codon, categorizing as a high-frequency codon. Conversely, RSCU < 1 signifies a weak preference, classifying as a low-frequency codon. Excluding the initiation codon (AUG) and the termination codons (UGG, UAG, and UAA), there were 30 codons with RSCU > 1, representing 46.88% of the total number of codons (Fig. 2 and Table S5). Notably, 29 codons ended with A or U bases, accounting for 96.67% of the high-frequency codons. Among these, AGA, which encodes Arginine, exhibited the highest RSCU value, followed by GCU, which encodes Alanine. Additionally, 30 low-frequency codons with RSCU < 1 and 28 ended with G/C, accounting for 93.33%. These results indicated that the high-frequency codons tend to end with A/U, while the low-frequency codons are more likely to end with G/C.

Factors affecting CUB in 49 Dendrobium species

PR2-plot analysis

A PR2-plot analysis was conducted to analyze the relationship between the third base of codons (A3/T3 and G3/C3) in CDS of cp genomes from 49 Dendrobium species. As shown in Fig. 3, cp coding genes in the four regions were unevenly distributed, with the majority located far from the center. This deviation suggests that CUB in these genes may be primarily governed by natural selection.

PR2-Plot of 49 Dendrobium species

Neutrality plot analysis

To further explore the influence of natural selection and mutation pressure on CUB, neutrality plot analysis was conducted using GC12 and GC3 as lateral and vertical axes, respectively. The results showed that GC12 was distributed from 29.01% to 53.24%, while GC3 was distributed from 20.39% to 46.86% in the CDS of the cp genome (Fig. 4). Notably, few genes were distributed along the diagonal, indicating a weak correlation among the nucleotide compositions at GC12 and GC3. Furthermore, the regression coefficient (slope) ranged from −0.038 to 0.368.

ENC-plot analysis

An ENC-plot analysis for GC3 was performed to investigate the factors influencing CUB in CDS of Dendrobium cp genome. When the CUB is influenced only by mutation pressure, the cp genes will fall on the ENC expected curve. If the cp genes fall below the ENC expected curve, then the CUB is mainly driven by other factors such as natural selection, in addition to mutation pressure [38]. As shown in Fig. 5, the Majority of the cp genes in the 49 Dendrobium species were far from the expected ENC curve, indicating that natural selection may be the primary factor driving CUB in the cp genome of Dendrobium, while mutation pressure plays a secondary role.

Optimal codons of Dendrobium

Eleven to nineteen optimal codons were identified across 49 Dendrobium species, with most optimal codons ending with A/U. Notably, D. polyanthum and D. devonianum shared the same optimal codons: GCA, GAA, UUU, GGA, CAU, UUA, AAU, CCU, AGA, CGA, AGU, GUA, and GUU. Similarly, D. nobile, D. linawianum, D. flexicaule, and D. officinale shared the same optimal codons: GCA, GAA, UUU, GGA, GGU, CAU, AUU, AAA, UUA, CCU, CAA, CGA, CGU, AGU, ACU, and UAU (Figure S2 and Table S6).

Phylogenetic analysis

Phylogenetic trees of 49 Dendrobium cp genomes were constructed based on the cp genomics and CDS using the ML method, with C. hookerianum and C. lowianum as outgroups. The resulting phylogenetic topologies revealed that the Dendrobium species was divided into three distinct groups (Group A, Group B, and Group C), and some species showed overlap between the cp genomics and CDS phylogenetic trees (Fig. 6). Discrepancies were also observed between molecular biological evidence and traditional plant morphological classifications within individual clusters. Species in sct. Chrysotoxae is found across Groups A, B, and C, challenging traditional morphological classifications and suggesting that of sct. Chrysotoxae species may have maternal sources from sct. Dendrobium, sct. Holochrysa, and sct. Chrysotoxae. Similarly, D. hrysocrepis from sct. Holochrysa appeared in Group A, distinctly separated from Group B, indicating possible maternal lineage derived from sct. Dendrobium. D. hercoglossum as the sole representative from sct. Breviflores is more closely related to sct. Dendrobium. However, it is difficult to define the phylogenetic relationship between sct. Breviflores and sct. Dendrobium.

ML trees of 49 Dendrobium species using CDSs and cp genomes by the maximum likelihood method

Additionally, PLS-DA was conducted to evaluate the relationship between CUB and phylogeny based on RSCU values of Dendrobium species. The PLS-DA showed that Groups A and C were each divided into two subgroups, consistent with the phylogenetic trees derived from the cp genomics and CDS (Figure S3). In contrast, Group B could not form a distinct cluster. The species were dispersed into Groups A and C. These findings suggest that RSCU values are valuable tools for revealing the evolutionary relationships among Dendrobium species.

Discussion

Unique CUB and evolutionary discrepancies

During long-term evolution, organisms tend to develop CUB patterns that adapt to their genomic environments [10, 39–42]. Previous studies on CUB in cp genomes have shown that monocots generally exhibit GC3 values exceeding 50% and predominantly use G/C-ending codons, whereas dicots typically have GC3 values below 50% and favor A/T-ending codons [43]. However, our study revealed that the cp genomes of 49 Dendrobium species (monocots) exhibit GC content below 50%, with a preference for A/T-ending bases. This finding contradicts the general patterns observed in earlier research. This discrepancy may indicate that the cp genomes of Dendrobium species have evolved under unique selective pressures distinct from those influencing most monocots. Alternatively, it could suggest that Dendrobium genus shares evolutionary traits with dicots in terms of cp genome codon usage. To better understand these patterns, future studies involving larger sample sizes and broader taxonomic representation are necessary.

Factors affecting CUB in Dendrobium genus

CUB in plants is typically influenced by biological and environmental factors, including natural selection, mutation pressure, gene expression levels, protein length, and gene function [27, 44]. In the present study, analysis of the cp genomes of Dendrobium species reveals that CUB is primarily driven by natural selection, with mutation pressure exerting a secondary role. This pattern aligns with findings in Paris polyphylla var. yunnanensis [35] and Dendrocalamus farinosus [45]. Conversely, studies on Eucalyptus grandis [46] and Mangifera indica (Mango) [47] indicate a more balanced contribution between natural selection and mutation pressure. These comparisons underscore the species-specific nature of the evolutionary forces shaping CUB in plants.

Relationships between optimal codons and phylogenetic implications

The selection of optimal codons is essential for analyzing CUB [42]. In the cp genomes of Dendrobium species, optimal codons predominantly end with A/U. Notably, D. polyanthum and D. devonianum share identical optimal codons, as do D. nobile, D. linawianum, D. flexicaule, and D. officinale. This suggests that these species employ similar strategies for encoding certain amino acids to optimize protein synthesis, reflecting a shared evolutionary foundation in their genetic coding systems. Supporting this, species with closer phylogenetic relationships often exhibit greater similarities in CUB [48]. Based on this, we hypothesize that D. polyanthum and D. devonianum are closely related phylogenetically, as are D. nobile, D. linawianum, D. flexicaule, and D. officinale. Despite these parallels in optimal codons, significant differences exist in the phylogenetic relationship of these species. This indicates that while optimal codon usage may be conserved, divergent phenotypic traits are likely driven by varying selective pressures, such as natural selection or mutation. This indicates that CUB was likely independently driven by varying selective pressures, such as natural selection or mutation, while optimal codon usage may be conserved [48].

Taxonomic insights based on phylogenetic analysis

The ML trees constructed in this study clarified the maternal genetic relationships between newly sequenced Dendrobium species (D. tortile, D. pulchellum, D. bicameratum, D. draconis, and D. sanderae) and other Dendrobium taxa. Our results revealed that D. hercoglossum clusters within sct. Breviflores, rather than sct. Dendrobium, which aligns with prior molecular studies [7, 49]. In addition, D. brymerianum, traditionally classified under the sct. Chrysotoxae, was grouped within sct. Dendrobium, supporting earlier phylogenetic findings by ALFP [49]. These discrepancies suggest that section-level classifications and phylogenetic relationships within Dendrobium are often incongruent. Therefore, a comprehensive taxonomic revision of the genus Dendrobium is warranted.

Conclusion

In this study, The CUB of 49 Dendrobium species and its influencing factors were analyzed. Codons within the cp genome of Dendrobium species are predominantly rich in A/T bases and prefer codons ending in A/T. Some species shared the same optimal codons, most ending with A/U. The CUB in Dendrobium species is primarily driven by natural selection, while mutation pressure plays a secondary role. Additionally, PLS-DA based on RSCU values enhances the understanding of phylogenetic relationships among Dendrobium species. These findings provide a solid foundation for optimizing the exogenous expression of chloroplast genes in Dendrobium and offer valuable references for the breeding of a high-yielding, disease-resistant new cultivar of Dendrobium.

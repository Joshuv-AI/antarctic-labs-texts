The prognostic value of selected indices combination in hepatocellular carcinoma patients treated with sorafenib
Authors: Radosław Osmański, Marcin Mardas, Maria Litwiniuk, Marta Stelmach-Mardas
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundConsidering a comprehensive approach to the hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) patient’s assessment, the aim of the study was to identify the clinical significance of selected indices as prognostic factors in HCC patients treated with sorafenib in daily clinical practice.Materials and methodsOut of 122 available HCC patients, 72 treated with sorafenib (800 mg per day) had their data analyzed. To assess nutritional status, liver condition and tumor burden, changes in the following indices were analyzed: body mass index (BMI), international normalized ratio (INR), the systemic inflammatory response index (SIRI), the platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR), the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), the model for end-stage liver disease (MELD), the albumin–bilirubin (ALBI), the prognostic nutritional index (PNI). Survival analyses were performed.ResultsSignificant differences were observed during treatment in body mass, BMI, monocyte, albumin, aspartate aminotransferase, bilirubin, SIRI, PNI and ALBI. Survival analyses revealed significant differences in progression-free survival (PFS) in median survival for: SIRI, NLR, ALBI, PNI and albumin (p < 0.001). For overall survival (OS), survival analyses revealed significant differences for albumin and PNI (p < 0.05). Cox regression for PFS and OS, adjusted for age, sex and BMI, revealed significant hazard ratios for albumin and for SIRI in PFS only.ConclusionsAlbumin remains an important prognostic factor for both PFS and OS in HCC patients; however, new indices can be successfully used in clinical practice as valuable prognostic tools for PFS in patients with HCC treated with sorafenib

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) accounts for two-thirds of liver cancer cases [1]. Among the most important risk factors, such as chronic hepatitis B (HBV) or hepatitis C (HCV), diet-related risk factors should also be listed (obesity, diabetes and alcohol consumption) [2]. In the clinical field, efforts are being made to improve the survival of patients with HCC, which is influenced by the availability of various treatment methods, ranging from surgery and transplantation to radiotherapy and systemic treatment, including the use of sorafenib [3]. Unfortunately, the rapid progression of the disease and its diagnosis at an advanced stage result in a poor prognosis for patients with HCC [4]. Therefore, it is extremely important to search for new biomarkers and routinely use the existing ones, including simple indices that can be calculated based on various laboratory parameters and used in early diagnosis of HCC.

The clinical value of selected indices has been assessed in different HCC treatment regimens to describe the clinical situation of patients. However, their prognostic value still seems to be underestimated in HCC patients. Several studies support a better prognostic performance of the albumin–bilirubin (ALBI) grade compared with the Child-Pugh (CP) grade in specific HCC staging systems, but ALBI can certainly serve as a complementary measure that may improve prognostic accuracy [5]. Moreover, ALBI grade may predict overall survival (OS) better than the model for end-stage liver disease (MELD) score in patients with HCC who have undergone liver transplantation (LT) [6]. The systemic inflammatory response index (SIRI) markers help assess the presence of pro-tumor inflammation and predict the efficacy of the applied therapy [7]. The prognostic role of the prognostic nutrition index (PNI) has been demonstrated in patients treated with sorafenib and lenvatinib in the first-line setting and in patients receiving regorafenib in the second-line setting or undergoing local treatment, including hepatectomy and transarterial chemoembolization [8]. Prognosis varies depending on the severity of the disease. Special considerations regarding the patient’s condition are important, taking into account all medical, organizational, and ethical aspects, while also minimizing the patient’s risk by appropriately tailoring care. Finally, patients’ autonomy can be enhanced by providing better prognostication within the context of effective communication [9].

Considering a comprehensive approach to patient assessment, the aim of the study was to identify the clinical significance of selected indices as prognostic factors in HCC patients treated with sorafenib in daily clinical practice.

Materials and methods

The study was approved by the local Bioethics Committee (No. 1134/2024) at the Poznan University of Medical Sciences in Poland. The guidelines of the Declaration of Helsinki were followed.

Patient population

Data on 122 HCC patients, collected between 2012 and 2023 at the Greater Poland Cancer Center in Poznan, were screened. Ultimately, 72 patients treated with sorafenib (800 mg per day), with locally advanced or metastatic HCC diagnosed based on histopathological examination or on a characteristic imaging pattern for HCC with liver cirrhosis on contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), were included.

The following inclusion criteria were established: age over 18 years; 1) locally advanced or metastatic hepatocellular carcinoma confirmed by histology or cytology. In patients with cirrhosis and lesions ≥ 1 cm in diameter, it is possible to waive the histological or cytological diagnosis provided that a typical image for HCC is obtained in multiphase CT or magnetic resonance imaging with contrast (MRI), with enhancement in the arterial phase (hyperdense lesion) and contrast washout from the lesion in the portal venous or delayed phase (hypodense lesion); 2) no possibility of radical surgical treatment or locoregional therapies, or their ineffectiveness; 3) performance status 0–2 according to World Health Organization/Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (WHO/ECOG); 4) functional liver status in category A, based on the Child-Pugh classification; 5) presence of at least one measurable lesion according to the currently applicable Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) criteria [RECIST 1.1: modified (mRECIST) criteria used from 2022 in doubtful cases]; 6) absence of significant comorbidities constituting contraindications to therapy, determined by the attending physician based on the relevant Summary of Product Characteristics and the guidelines of the Polish Society of Clinical Oncology for the treatment of hepatocellular carcinoma; 7) adequate organ function, determined on the basis of blood laboratory test results, which, in the opinion of the attending physician, allows for the safe initiation of therapy; 8) no contraindications to the use of each drug.

Exclusion criteria were as follows: 1) coexistence of another malignant disease, except for basal cell carcinoma of the skin or cervical carcinoma in situ; coexistence of another malignant disease, except for basal cell carcinoma of the skin or cervical cancer in situ; 2) uncontrolled infection; 3) heart failure above New York Heart Association Class III (NYHA III); 4) pregnancy or breastfeeding; 5) lack of cooperation or non-compliance with medical recommendations, particularly regarding periodic follow-up to assess treatment efficacy and safety by the patient or their legal guardian.

Statistical analysis

Selected descriptive statistics (arithmetic mean, mean difference, standard deviation) were used. The analyzed data were assessed for normality using the D’Agostino-Pearson normality test. When assessing changes before and during sorafenib treatment, the following tests were applied: the Student’s t-test for normal distribution and the Wilcoxon matched-pairs test for related variables and non-normal distribution. Survival analysis was performed using Kaplan–Meier plots and Cox proportional hazards regression. Data were adjusted for age, sex, and BMI. Progression-free survival (PFS) was defined as the time from cancer diagnosis to radiological progression, and overall survival (OS) as the time from diagnosis to death. A two-tailed P value at 0.05 was considered statistically significant for all comparisons. Statistical analyses were performed with STATISTICA 13.3 (TIBICO Software Inc., Palo Alto, CA, USA, 2017) and GraphPad Prism 5.1 (GraphPad Software).

We observed a significant decrease in body mass, BMI, the monocyte count and albumin concentration, and at the same time a significant increase in aspartate aminotransferase, bilirubin, SIRI, PNI and ALBI values.

Discussion

The presented data highlight the prognostic value of selected indices, indicating moderate malnutrition and an intermediate mortality risk in a group of HCC patients treated with sorafenib. All of them (PNI, SIRI, NLR, PLR, ALBI and albumin concentrations) are used in daily clinical practice by professionals; however, their prognostic value still seems to be underestimated in HCC patients. It is a challenge to establish a general cut-off for some indices in this group of patients, as it is usually determined based on statistically analyzed data of individual studies. The presented data bring us closer to understanding the clinical condition of HCC patients, which changes continuously along with their nutritional status.

No consistent evidence can be found regarding the prognostic value of SIRI. For example, Wu et al. [9] indicated that the monocyte-to-lymphocyte ratio (MLR) was a better predictor of early recurrence than SIRI, NLR, PLR, or the systemic immune-inflammatory index (SII) in terms of recurrence-free survival. On the other hand, Xin et al. [10] found that preoperative SIRI was an independent predictor for recurrence-free survival (RFS) in patients with early-stage HCC within the Milan criteria. Finally, a meta-analysis by Zhang and Tang [11] confirmed that elevated SIRI significantly predicted OS and PFS in patients with HCC and was also significantly associated with tumor aggressiveness. This is in line with the presented data, where SIRI > 2.3 was associated with survival time twice as short as values below the established cut-off point. Moreover, in patients who underwent transarterial chemoembolization (TACE), SIRI was also identified as an independent predictor of tumor response and clinical outcomes [12]. Another index assessed in the current study was NLR. Casadei-Gardini et al. [13] indicated that the baseline NLR should be used as a stratification factor for identifying patients with a higher probability of long-term survival. In our study group we observed that the median survival time was 5 months shorter in HCC patients with NLR > 2.5. Although recently published data [14] showed no statistically significant difference in median OS between selective internal radiation therapy (SIRT)/sorafenib and sorafenib cohorts (12.1 vs. 9.2 months, p = 0.21) in patients with high NLR values, the authors identified PLR as a potential predictive factor of benefit from additional SIRT in patients with HCC receiving sorafenib therapy. This is consistent with a study by Zhang et al. [15], where high baseline NLR and PLR values were predictive factors of poor OS (p = 0.02 and p = 0.004) and PFS (p = 0.045 and p = 0.005) in unresectable HCC patients undergoing TACE plus sorafenib treatment. In our study, changes in PFS with respect to NLR were visible and close to statistical significance. Study by Pinto et al. [16] confirmed that neither NLR nor PLR altered the OS of HCC patients.

In the cox regression analysis, baseline ALBI grade II and an increase in ALBI grade during treatment (p < 0.001) were found to be strongly associated with the mortality of HCC patients treated with sorafenib [17]. Another study [18] demonstrated that among advanced HCC patients treated with first-line systemic therapy, those with ALBI grade I had an OS of 21 months, compared to 5 months in patients with grade II or III. This is in line with the current study, where a significant difference in PFS between ALBI II (over two times shorter) and ALBI I was observed. Thus, changes in ALBI grade during sorafenib treatment may predict prognosis in this group of patients. It should be emphasized that understanding the liver condition and tumor burden in HCC patients may assist clinicians in planning optimal treatment strategies [19]. As pointed out by Naviwala et al. [18], the ALBI grading is a useful method for evaluating liver function and predicting survival rates in individuals with HCC. Albumin concentration, used not only for calculating ALBI but also as part of the BALAD score, appears to be a valuable prognostic tool for predicting OS and PFS in patients with advanced HCC undergoing systemic therapies [20]. For example, the albumin-gauge score, based on serum albumin and skeletal muscle density (SMD), demonstrates the role of albumin in assessing muscle quality and nutritional status [21]. Finally, the baseline PNI together with NLR levels have prognostic implications in HCC. A higher PNI and a lower NLR indicate a higher postoperative survival rate [22]. The PNI should be considered as a low-cost immuno-nutritional indicator that can provide valuable information on malnutrition in HCC patients. In the current study, low PNI values significantly affected both: PFS and OS. Gulmez and Harputluoglu [23] concluded that a scoring system based on selected, standardized and inexpensive indices (including PNI) may be especially useful for identifying suitable patients in low-income countries, where access to immunotherapy is limited.

Some limitations of this study should be mentioned. This was a single-center retrospective study and the collected sample size was relatively small, which was due to the rarity of this cancer type. Standardized indices with literature-based cut-offs were used instead of validation, and no multiple testing correction was performed; however, survival analyses were adjusted for age, sex, and BMI.

Conclusions

In conclusion, albumin remains an important prognostic factor for both PFS and OS in HCC patients. However, new indices can also be successfully applied in clinical practice as valuable prognostic tools for PFS in patients with HCC treated with sorafenib.

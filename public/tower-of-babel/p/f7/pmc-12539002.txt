Serum lactate Dehydrogenase-to-Albumin ratio in upper tract urothelial carcinoma: A promising prognostic biomarker
Authors: Qihao Wang, Lei Zheng, Jianjun Ye, Xinyang Liao, Zeyu Chen, Xingyuan Wang, Chichen Zhang, Qiang Wei, Yige Bao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThe objective of this study is to assess the predictive significance of lactate dehydrogenase-to-albumin ratio (LAR) in patients with upper tract urothelial carcinoma (UTUC) who have undergone radical nephroureterectomy (RNU).Materials and methodsWe performed a retrospective analysis on patients with UTUC at West China Hospital, covering the period from May 2003 to June 2019. The optimal cut point of LAR was determined using the X-Tile program. Relevant statistical methods included the utilization of Kaplan-Meier curves for survival estimation and the application of Cox proportional hazard model for risk evaluation. A nomogram was constructed to predict the 3-year and 5-year CSS and the predictive performance was also evaluated.ResultsA cohort of 577 patients with UTUC who underwent RNU were included in the study, and the threshold value of LAR was 5.07. LAR ≥ 5.07 was associated with worse CSS [hazard ratio (HR) = 1.72; 95% confidence interval (95%CI): 1.18–2.50; P = 0.005] in fully adjusted Cox regression analysis. In addition, the nomogram utilizing the LAR exhibited excellent prognostic performance in predicting CSS, with time-dependent areas under the curve (tAUC) values of 0.814 and 0.781 for 3-year and 5-year CSS, respectively.ConclusionPreoperative LAR could be regarded as a valuable individualized tool for clinical decision-making, as it independently predicts CSS in UTUC patients undergoing RNU.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12894-025-01950-0.

Introduction

The occurrence of upper tract urothelial carcinoma (UTUC) is relatively rare, representing a minority of 5% to 10% among all urothelial carcinomas, which primarily originates from the renal pelvis and ureter, with an estimated incidence rate of approximately 2 cases per 100,000 person-years [1, 2].

UTUC remains a challenging malignancy, in despite of recent significant advancements in our understanding of its risk, progression, and particularly treatments for surgery and chemoradiotherapy. The 5-year cancer-specific mortality (CSM) rates for UTUC patients with pT2, pT3, and pT4 disease are observed to be 21%, 35%, and 59% respectively [3–5]. Therefore, identifying an easily obtained prognostic predictor with strong accuracy for achieving personalized and precise clinical treatment is urgent and indispensable.

In addition to the regular clinicopathological factors, some novel nutritional or inflammation-related indicators, such as the ratio of neutrophils to lymphocytes (NLR) and systemic immune-inflammation index (SII), have been demonstrated to be reliable and autonomous predictors of cancer patients’ oncological outcomes [6, 7]. The lactate dehydrogenase-to-albumin ratio (LAR), a combination of indicators of nutrition and inflammation status, was recommended to be a good prognostic predictor for cancer patients [8, 9]. However, there is a lack of sufficient data regarding the prognostic significance of LAR in UTUC patients.

Hence, the objective of this study was to assess the predictive significance of LAR in patients with UTUC who underwent RNU and develop a nomogram incorporating LAR for prognostic prediction.

Methods and materials

Patients and data

Our research was granted approval by the Ethics Committee of West China Hospital, Chengdu, China (2021 − 1209). Given the retrospective characteristic of our study and the utilization of de-identified patient data along with confidential information, we obtained a waiver for informed consent from the local institutional review board. Initially, a total of 764 UTUC patients who underwent laparoscopic radical nephroureterectomy with bladder cuff excision between May 2003 and June 2019 were extracted from the database of West China Hospital. Exclusion criteria for this study included patients with limited long-term prognostic data, patients lacking serum albumin (ALB) or lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) level records, patients who received neoadjuvant therapy, and patients diagnosed with non-urothelial carcinoma confirmed by pathology. We obtained clinicopathological data from medical records, which encompassed the age of patients at the time of surgery, their gender, body mass index (BMI), LDH and ALB levels measured within a 2-week period prior to RNU. Additionally, we recorded various tumor characteristics such as location, size, pathological stage and grade, architectural features, histologic types and presence of multifocality, lymphovascular invasion (LVI), lymph node status, surgical margin and adjuvant systemic chemotherapy.

The specimens acquired by RNU underwent individual evaluation conducted by our team of expert pathologists specialized in genitourinary pathology. Additionally, the histopathological staging was carried out following the tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) staging system established by the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC), while the histopathologic grading adhered to the WHO/ISUP recommendation grading system. The urology-pathological community and numerous experts have widely embraced the histopathological diagnosis of variant histology, with the diagnostic criteria being outlined in the World Health Organization (WHO) classification of tumors. Follow-up and outcomes

The follow-up was initiated within 2 to 3 weeks after the delivery of the final pathology report following RNU. The premier follow-up aimed to examine the attributes of the primary tumor and customize future treatment strategies. Generally, the follow-up protocol adhered to the guidelines set by the European Association of Urology (EAU) [1]. The follow-up was conducted at 3-month intervals during the first year, followed by 6-month intervals in the second year. Subsequently, it transitioned to an annual basis unless there were indications of recurrence or any unusual symptoms. The routine components included a thorough physical examination, analysis of blood and urine samples, utilization of contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) scan for the chest and abdomen, as well as performing cystoscopy.

Relevant CSS and its corresponding events, namely cancer-specific mortality (CSM), were considered as the primary outcomes. The duration of CSS was specifically defined as the time interval from RNU to UTUC-related death.

LAR

The LAR was calculated from the ratio of the serum LDH value to the serum ALB value. The software X-Tile 3.6.1 (Yale University) [11] was utilized to determine the optimal cut-off point for LAR by considering the lowest P values and the highest chi-square from log-rank tests.

Statistical analysis

The data analysis was conducted using the statistical software R (Foundation: http://www.r-projiect.org; version 4.1.3) and Empower(R) (Foundation: http://www.empowerstats.com, X&Y Solutions). Medians and interquartile ranges (IQRs) were used to describe continuous variables, while numbers and percentages were employed for the description of categorical variables. Different statistical tests, such as the Student’s t test or the Mann-Whitney U test for continuous variables and the chi-square or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables, were utilized to assess potential disparities in clinicopathological characteristics. The CSS was calculated using the Kaplan-Meier approach and assessed for comparison through the log-rank statistical analysis.

The prognostic significance of LAR on CSM in UTUC patients after RNU was examined using Cox proportional hazard regression models. Adjusted hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated to assess the risk of cancer-specific mortality. The findings from Cox regression analyses were presented in accordance with the guidelines outlined in the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) statement [12], including unadjusted, minimally adjusted, and fully adjusted models. In the minimally adjusted Cox regression analysis, covariates were selected for adjustment if their inclusion in the Cox model resulted in a minimum 10% change in hazard ratios (HRs) [13]. The study also investigated the stability of the findings across different subgroups, including age, gender, tumor location, tumor stage, tumor grade, lymph node status, lymphovascular invasion, tumor size, tumor architecture, variant histology, surgical margin and multifocality using Cox proportional hazard models. The examination of subgroup interaction was conducted using the likelihood ratio test.

In addition, both univariate and multivariable analyses were employed to investigate the factors associated with CSM in UTUC patients who underwent RNU. Variables that showed a significance level of less than 0.05 in the multivariate analyses were utilized to develop a nomogram for predicting CSS at 3-year and 5-year intervals. The performance of the nomogram was assessed using time-dependent ROC curves, c-index and calibration curves. All findings from the analysis were deemed statistically significant, with a two-sided P-value less than 0.05.

Results

Baseline characteristic

This study was conducted with 577 individuals diagnosed with UTUC who had undergone RNU. The participants had a median age of 68 years, and the male to female ratio was 322:255. The serum LDH level had a median value of 178.00 U/L, while the median serum albumin level was recorded as 40.80 g/L. The optimal cut point of LAR was determined to be 5.07 using X-Tile 3.6.1 software, which based on the highest chi-square value from log-rank tests and the lowest P values (Fig. 2). As a result, 418 patients (72.44%) were classified into the low-LAR category, while the remaining 159 patients (27.56%) were placed in the high-LAR group. Notable distinctions were observed between the low-LAR and high-LAR groups regarding serum LDH and albumin levels, gender distribution and tumor characteristics such as location, size, stage, grade, lymph node status, presence of lymphovascular invasion, multifocality and surgical margin. Nomogram construction and validation

We conducted univariate and multivariate analyses to identify factors associated with CSM in patients with UTUC following RNU (Supplementary Table 1). In order to incorporate a wide range of clinical indicators, we incorporated all variables with P values below 0.100 in the univariate Cox regression into the multivariate Cox regression analysis. Based on the outcomes of multivariate Cox analysis, a total of eight variables were ultimately incorporated into the nomogram for predicting the 3-year and 5-year cancer-specific survival (Fig. 3a). These variables include age, tumor size, pathological tumor stage, tumor grade, lymph node status, tumor architecture, surgical margin, and LAR. The calibration curves demonstrated strong correlations between the predicted and observed survival probability, indicating reliable reproducibility (Fig. 3b). We also employed time-dependent ROC curves and c-index to assess the predictive accuracy of the nomograms. The findings demonstrated that the nomograms exhibited robust performance in forecasting CSS, with tAUCs of 0.814 and 0.781 for predicting CSS at 3 years and 5 years, respectively (Fig. 3c), and C-index of 0.798 (0.762–0.833).Fig. 3A nomogram for predicting 3-year or 5-year cancer-specific survival A, calibration curve B, and time-dependent ROC curves C for assessing the calibration and discrimination of the nomogram in predicting 3-year or 5-year cancer-specific survival

Discussion

For this research, we analyzed data from 577 patients with UTUC who received RNU in Southwest China. Our findings indicate that the LAR biomarker is a highly effective predictor of survival for individuals with UTUC. To the best of our understanding, this study represents the first investigation into the significance of LAR in predicting prognosis for UTUC. The findings indicate that a high preoperative LAR is an autonomous factor contributing to poorer CSS outcomes among UTUC patients. Additionally, we have devised a nomogram that incorporates the LAR and seven other clinicopathological characteristics. Furthermore, we have conducted further validation to evaluate the accuracy of this predictions of the nomogram.

In order to enhance the outlook for patients with UTUC, numerous endeavors have been undertaken in the past ten years to ascertain reliable indicators that can differentiate individuals at high risk from those at low risk, enabling tailored and accurate clinical interventions [14, 15]. LAR, an innovative biomarker, is a combination of preoperative serum LDH level and albumin level, providing a comprehensive assessment of the individual’s immune and nutritional condition. Previous studies have underscored the crucial role of LDH in metabolic activity, tissue injury, inflammation, and neoplasm [16]. The high LDH level, resulting from hypoxia in the tumor microenvironment, was believed to be associated with active tumor activity, poor therapeutic effect and inferior survival [17, 18]. Levels of serum albumin not only serve as an accurate indicator of an individual’s nutritional status, but also provide valuable insights into the extent of inflammation induced by tumors [19]. It was reported that inflammatory factors produced by cancer, such as C-reactive protein and cytokines, could consume albumin and regulate its synthesis, ultimately leading to hypoalbuminemia, immune impairment and immune tolerance, and ultimately disease progression [20, 21]. However, the practical application of a solitary albumin parameter is restricted as it can be influenced by liver functionality, fluid levels, and overall inflammation within the body. Therefore, the LAR, consisting of LDH and albumin, could effectively mitigate the weakness of either factor and could be utilized to evaluate the host’s nutrition burden and systemic inflammation burden, and to forecast the prognosis.

The present study demonstrated a strong correlation between the preoperative LAR value and survival outcomes, indicating that a higher LAR was significantly linked to poorer cancer-specific survival in patients with UTUC following RNU. These findings align with previous research conducted on this topic. Peng et al. conducted a study involving 1661 individuals diagnosed with nonmetastatic nasopharyngeal carcinoma and found that the preoperative LAR independently influenced both overall survival and progression-free survival outcomes [22]. Feng et al. discovered that a strong correlation exists between an elevated LAR and poorer CSS outcomes in 346 patients with esophageal squamous cell carcinoma who received curative treatments [23]. Moreover, the predictive significance of the LAR was also successfully determined in colorectal cancer [24], bladder cancer [8] and breast cancer [9]. Interestingly, Nakazawa et al. tried to explore the predictive impact of the LAR on therapeutic sensitivity to nivolumab, an anti-PD-1 monoclonal antibody, in gastric cancer patients, and proposed that nivolumab resistance could be extrapolated from an increased LAR after the therapeutic course [25]. As a result, the extent of our knowledge regarding the potential utilization value of the LAR was limited. Additional concerns involve evaluating the predictive value for response to adjuvant therapy and determining if enhancing preoperative LDH or albumin levels specifically leads to a consistent survival advantage.

The survival rate quantification of UTUC patients who underwent RNU in the existing nomograms was based on demographic or pathological characteristics [26–28]. Pallauf and colleagues conducted a comprehensive analysis of prognostic nomograms for UTUC, revealing excellent predictive accuracy in terms of CSS. They emphasized that factors such as pathological tumor stage, age, and LVI were highly reliable indicators for predicting CSS [29]. However, limited data exist with regard to the nomograms integrating novel biomarkers that reflect the biological and clinical behavior of the tumor microenvironment.

In order to fill this gap, we developed a nomogram using the LAR and seven other clinicopathological characteristics that showed statistical significance (P values < 0.05) in the multivariate Cox regression analysis. The subsequent evaluation of calibration curves and time-dependent ROC curves demonstrated the favorable predictive performance of the nomogram. However, the critical absence of external validation weakened the accuracy of the results and the applicability of the nomograms; hence, we could not guarantee the sound performance in clinical practice. Of note, our analyses validated the predictive value of the LAR for survival one more time and provided a valuable reference for future studies on the predictive nomograms of UTUC.

This study is the initial attempt to examine the predictive significance of LAR in patients with UTUC following RNU; however, it is important to consider several limitations when interpreting the findings. Firstly, the retrospective and single-center nature of this study imposes certain limitations. These include potential subjectivity in analyzing homogeneous data, a relatively small sample size, and the absence of patient comorbidity information. Secondly, it is important to note that the cutoff value (5.07) for LAR was determined using X-Tile 3.6.1 software developed by Yale University. However, it should be acknowledged that this threshold value may not yield optimal results when applied to different populations. We have noted that the cutoff value of the LAR varied across studies, including 3.40 [9], 3.8 [8], 4.04 [22], 5.5 [30] and 6.54, with the reasons about tumor types, geographic regions and laboratory equipment. Thirdly, the nomogram was assessed via internal validation only. While this demonstrates promising discriminative ability and calibration in our cohort, external validation in independent populations is essential before clinical implementation. We are actively addressing this limitation through an ongoing multi-center external validation study.

In addition, the potential benefits of utilizing nomograms by patients have not been reported. Furthermore, our evaluation solely focused on the prognostic significance of LAR without incorporating the identified biomarkers such as SII, NLR, lymphocyte–monocyte ratio (LMR), and prognostic nutritional index (PNI). To address these limitations, we are currently conducting some additional studies.

In summary, the combination of preoperative serum LDH level and albumin level, known as preoperative LAR, can independently predict CSS in patients with UTUC who have undergone RNU. Moreover, the utilization of a nomogram based on the LAR shows promise as a personalized tool for providing reliable prognostic information to maximize survival benefits in UTUC patients. However, it is important to note that further external validation is required.

Bridging the semantic gap in medical image segmentation via multi-scale dependency and attention-guided enhancement
Authors: Mingrong Li, Daming Zhang, Qing He, Hengyan Liu, Chenglong Zhou, Mingfang Li, Xiaobo Zhou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The encoder–decoder paradigm has emerged as the prevailing framework in medical image segmentation, and recent studies within this paradigm have demonstrated its remarkable effectiveness for lesion delineation. However, because the encoder compresses high-dimensional inputs and the decoder must reconstruct the target from the encoder’s limited latent representation, a fixed encoder–decoder pipeline inevitably introduces a semantic gap between the two stages. To bridge this gap, we present MAFormer, a novel U-shaped network tailored for medical image segmentation. Specifically, we design a Multi-scale Dependency Feature Construction (MDFC) module that refines the skip-connection pathway to fuse semantic information across hierarchical levels. In addition, we propose an Attention Representation Reinforcement Module (ARRM) that strengthens encoder–decoder semantic alignment via bidimensional similarity computation and a hierarchical masking strategy. Extensive experiments on GlaS, Synapse and ISIC2018 datasets confirm that MAFormer consistently surpasses state-of-the-art encoder–decoder methods on both large and small scale datasets. In particular, it achieves higher Dice scores, underscoring the effectiveness of MAFormer in improving overall segmentation accuracy.

Introduction

The breakthroughs of deep learning in the field of medical image segmentation are not only reflected in the improvement of segmentation accuracy but also provide novel technological means for clinical diagnosis1 and treatment planning2, thereby assisting doctors in precise lesion localization and treatment strategy formulation. Among the representative CNN models in the development of this technology, U-Net3 and V-Net stand out. Their encoder-decoder architecture4, coupled with skip connections5, has become a classic model for image segmentation tasks. Furthermore, medical image segmentation is an end-to-end, pixel-level task where each pixel has a corresponding label. The encoder-decoder structure is highly suitable for such tasks, as it downsamples the image from high resolution to extract image representations and then upsamples the lowest-level abstract semantic features to recover spatial feature information of the image.

Specifically, the encoder section effectively captures a rich set of features in the image, ranging from low-level textures to high-level semantics, through a series of deeply stacked convolutional layers and pooling operations6. The shallow layers of the network are responsible for preserving fine spatial information and local textures, while the deeper layers capture global context and semantic relationships. Furthermore, the decoder gradually restores the original resolution of the image using upsampling strategies designed to compensate for the loss of spatial information caused by pooling operations. During this process, skip connections serve as a bridge for information transfer, directly copying the features extracted by the encoder to the decoder, ensuring that crucial low-level detail information is preserved throughout the successive downsampling and upsampling stages.

However, this straightforward and direct feature transfer method has certain limitations when capturing non-local multi-scale information. The role of skip connections at different levels and locations varies significantly in the final learning outcome, with some connections potentially introducing redundancy or interference in the information fusion process. Additionally, the combination of skip connections exhibits noticeable data dependency7, meaning that due to variations in image texture, scale, noise, and other characteristics across different datasets, the required connection strategy needs to be adjusted accordingly. The root cause of these phenomena may lie in the semantic gap8 between the encoder and decoder. Specifically, the encoder, while processing input data, tends to extract high-level abstract features that help capture global semantic information, whereas the decoder focuses more on utilizing local detail information for fine-grained reconstruction. When there is a disparity in the information hierarchy between the two, the directly copied features may fail to fully meet the decoder’s need for fine-grained details.

For these challenges, Attention U-Net9 introduces an adaptive attention gating mechanism into the skip connection path, effectively extracting features from the encoder that truly contribute to target segmentation while suppressing background or noise information. This approach alleviates, to some extent, the semantic gap between the encoder and decoder; however, its attention mechanism primarily focuses on single-scale feature selection and lacks the ability for cross-scale interaction. UCTransNet10 improves skip connections through the Channel-wise Cross-layer Transformer (CCT) module, enhancing cross-layer feature fusion via channel attention. Nevertheless, it remains limited in spatial context modeling and does not fully address the inherent semantic discrepancies among features of different scales. UDTransNet11 integrates the Transformer into the encoder–decoder architecture, leveraging self-attention to capture long-range dependencies, but it fails to sufficiently preserve local detail features during feature fusion. On the other hand, U-Net 3+12 adopts full-scale skip connections and deep supervision strategies, establishing richer feature interactions across multiple scales to achieve more detailed information integration and capture contextual information at various levels and ranges. However, its dense connections may introduce redundant information and lack an effective feature selection mechanism. Furthermore, many researchers have extended the conventional encoder–decoder architecture by incorporating attention mechanisms, residual connections, and multi-scale fusion modules, such as dilated convolutions13, pyramid pooling14, and Transformer structures15. Although attention mechanisms or full-scale aggregation modules can partially alleviate the inconsistency between encoder and decoder features, the inherent hierarchical differences in the feature extraction process, along with the limitations of existing methods in various aspects, make it difficult for such compensatory strategies to achieve optimal fusion. Moreover, when dealing with data characterized by complex structures or highly diverse features, the aforementioned improvement strategies face performance bottlenecks due to their insufficient exploitation of full-scale information. Consequently, the inherent semantic discrepancies among multi-scale features at different encoding stages and the semantic gap between the encoder and decoder remain significant.

To address these challenges, we designed an end-to-end segmentation network based on a U-shaped architecture–MAFormer, which integrates two attention-based modules. To tackle the semantic gap between multi-scale feature maps and the insufficient cross-scale feature correlation during the decoding process, we proposed the Multi-scale Dependency Feature Construction (MDFC). This module models cross-channel semantic relationships through the Channel Interaction Mechanism and strengthens the response of key features based on weight allocation. Additionally, we designed a parallel pooling structure to efficiently fuse multi-scale features, capturing global salient features. Finally, we introduced a Multi-Head Spatial Attention Mechanism to decouple long-range dependencies between spatial locations, collaboratively optimizing the saliency representations of different regions. Subsequently, we introduced the Attention Representation Refinement Module (ARRM), which synergistically integrates the channel-wise correlation matrix and spatial-domain similarity matrix, coupled with a masking mechanism to enhance feature representations. Furthermore, we proposed a Channel Dimension Expansion and Compression Strategy to achieve efficient cross-channel information selection and fusion. Related work

Skip connections

Skip connections were first introduced in deep residual networks16, effectively alleviating gradient vanishing and information degradation issues in deep network training through cross-layer feature transmission and residual mapping mechanisms. In the field of medical image segmentation, U-Net3 was among the earliest successful models to utilize skip connections. Specifically, U-Net employs a symmetric encoder-decoder architecture, integrating multi-scale features from the encoder with corresponding decoder features via skip connections, thus effectively combining low-level detailed information with high-level semantic information. Building upon U-Net, R34-U-Net3 replaced the original encoder with ResNet-34, significantly improving feature extraction capabilities. UNet++17 further enhanced feature reuse and information flow by introducing nested dense skip connections and deep supervision mechanisms. MultiResUNet18 proposed a multi-resolution skip connection approach, boosting feature extraction performance by aggregating feature representations across varying resolutions, thereby improving adaptability to targets at multiple scales. Non-local U-Net19 incorporated non-local operations within skip connections, enabling the model to capture long-range dependencies and global contextual information within images. However, these skip connection approaches typically rely on simple feature concatenation or element-wise addition, limiting their capacity to deeply explore feature correlations and accurately capture interactions between local and global information.

Attention mechanisms

Inspired by selective human visual attention, attention mechanisms dynamically emphasize critical information to enhance model performance. Academic exploration of attention originated from machine translation tasks and was first introduced by20. Within the field of medical image segmentation, Attention U-Net9 proposed attention-gating mechanisms, enabling models to automatically focus on target structures and suppress irrelevant regions. Inf-Net21 was specifically designed for the automatic segmentation of COVID-19 infection regions in chest CT images, utilizing a parallel partial decoder to aggregate high-level features while integrating implicit reverse attention and explicit edge-attention mechanisms to overcome challenges such as significant variations in infected regions and low contrast between infected areas and normal tissues. Recently, driven by the success of Transformer architectures in natural language processing, Transformer-based segmentation methods have garnered considerable interest. TransUNet22 combines Transformers for global context modeling with U-Net to capture local details. Swin-UNet23 introduces a shifted-window mechanism to enhance global modeling capabilities and significantly reduces computational complexity, achieving outstanding performance in multi-organ and cardiac segmentation tasks. MCTrans24 employs multi-scale feature embeddings and both intra-scale and inter-scale self-attention mechanisms to effectively model cross-scale dependencies among pixels. Nevertheless, despite the excellent global modeling capability of Transformers, they exhibit limitations in effectively capturing local detailed information. Furthermore, although multi-scale feature fusion mechanisms improve adaptability to targets at various scales, the representation capabilities of local and global features still require enhancement. Therefore, further investigation is needed to enable decoders to more accurately recover spatial details and effectively integrate semantic information.

Integration of skip connections and attention mechanisms

In recent years, the integration of skip connections with attention mechanisms has positively contributed to the accurate segmentation of lesions. For example, UCTransNet10 proposed a channel-wise attention-driven feature fusion framework to optimize the multi-scale feature aggregation process. Building upon this concept, UDTransNet11 further introduced a dual attention mechanism to simultaneously model both channel and spatial relationships, enabling more comprehensive feature interactions and semantic alignment. MSD-Net25 combined multi-task learning with attention mechanisms, designing collaborative segmentation and boundary detection decoders that exploit multi-scale spatial contextual information and semantically consistent features, thereby enhancing segmentation accuracy. UNet#26 employed dense skip connections along with full-scale skip connections for deeper multi-scale feature aggregation, while incorporating attention mechanisms to optimize feature transfer pathways, assisting in the precise localization of organ boundaries and subtle targets. MRCTransUNet27 incorporates a lightweight MR-ViT and an inter-attention module, employing skip connections only in the initial layer while integrating MR-ViT and RPA modules in deeper layers to bridge the semantic gap and enhance the capacity for contextual information modeling. Vivim28, a medical video segmentation framework built upon Video Vision Mamba, leverages a Temporal Mamba Block to compress long spatiotemporal sequences and introduces a cross-frame boundary-aware affine constraint, thereby improving the discrimination of ambiguous lesions and achieving efficient and accurate medical video segmentation. Moreover, specific segmentation tasks such as histopathological image segmentation inspired models like HistoSeg29, which integrated skip connections with lightweight attention mechanisms to effectively capture both local and global features across multiple scales, achieving accurate boundary delineation. Additionally, MedT30 proposed a gated axial attention mechanism, augmenting self-attention modules with additional gating controls. Nevertheless, existing studies still exhibit room for improvement regarding interactions between global and local features, recovery of spatial details, and fusion of semantic information.

Methodology

Overview

The proposed MAFormer is built upon an encoder–decoder architecture to mitigate the semantic gap between the encoder and the decoder, as illustrated in Fig. 1. The model is composed of two key modules. To integrate channel importance and spatial dependency information during the skip-connection transmission process, thereby enabling the model to more precisely fuse edge details and semantic features across different levels, we introduce the Multi-scale Dependency Feature Construction (MDFC) module to refine the feature transmission pathway in skip connections. MDFC models features from both channel and spatial dimensions: in the channel dimension, it computes inter-channel correlations through a channel interaction strategy and employs a weighting mechanism to emphasize critical information, followed by parallel pooling to capture global average information and extract the most salient feature responses; in the spatial dimension, it leverages a multi-head attention mechanism to establish dependencies among different positions, thereby enhancing the saliency of feature representations at the spatial level. On this basis, in order to further strengthen the decoder’s semantic alignment with encoder features and to improve spatial reconstruction accuracy of multi-scale features during progressive upsampling, we introduce the Attention-based Representation Reinforcement Module (ARRM). ARRM first obtains query, key, and value representations through feature embedding and linear projection, and then computes dual-dimensional similarities by combining channel similarity matrices with spatial similarity matrices. A masking mechanism is subsequently applied to suppress noise and irrelevant features, thereby enhancing semantic alignment between the encoder and decoder. Finally, channel expansion and compression operations are employed to further optimize feature representation. Compared with existing encoder–decoder architectures, MAFormer enhances skip-connection feature transmission through MDFC and reinforces semantic alignment and spatial reconstruction in the decoding stage through ARRM, and the synergistic effect of the two modules effectively alleviates the semantic gap between the encoder and decoder.

Multi-scale dependency feature construction (MDFC)

In medical image datasets, organs and lesions often exhibit diverse morphological characteristics. Traditional models, due to their relatively simple architectures and limited adaptability to complex features, struggle to effectively capture such intricate structural information. To address this challenge, recent research has primarily focused on integrating attention mechanisms to enhance the feature representation capability of models. Attention U-Net9 introduces spatial attention gating into skip connections to adaptively filter encoder features and suppress background noise; however, its attention mechanism is restricted to single-scale feature interactions and fails to model cross-scale semantic associations, leading to the loss of fine segmentation details for complex structures. UDTransNet11 employs the self-attention mechanism of Transformers to learn global dependencies and leverages deformable convolutions to strengthen local feature extraction; nevertheless, it tends to overlook the synergistic optimization of spatial long-range dependencies when balancing local detail and global context.

To address these challenges, we propose the Multi-Scale Dependency Feature Construction (MDFC) module. As illustrated in Fig. 2, specifically, we first employ a channel interaction strategy to compute correlations across channels, leveraging a weighted mechanism to highlight critical feature representations. Subsequently, parallel pooling operations are introduced to capture global average information and extract the most salient feature responses. Finally, multi-head spatial attention is incorporated to capture long-range dependencies between different spatial locations, enabling the synergistic optimization of local and global feature representations and enhancing the saliency expression across spatial positions.

Attention Representation Reinforcement Module(ARRM)

In medical image segmentation tasks, effective feature representation is crucial for accurately identifying the morphology of lesions. However, traditional approaches that rely solely on stacking convolutional layers and attention heads not only increase computational cost but also introduce redundancy while extracting meaningful features. HistoSeg29 enhances feature representation by introducing lightweight convolutions and a Sigmoid-based fast attention mechanism. However, its attention computation primarily relies on simple channel-level transformations, lacking effective modeling of spatial dimension information interactions. UCTransNet10 employs a multi-head attention mechanism to perform cross-channel information interaction in the channel dimension and incorporates instance normalization to stabilize gradient flow. Nevertheless, its attention mechanism is primarily focused on the channel dimension, with relatively limited capability in capturing spatial contextual information, which may lead to the loss of critical spatial structural details in segmentation tasks.

To address these limitations, as illustrated in Fig. 3, this study proposes a novel attention mechanism, termed the Attention Representation Reinforcement Module (ARRM), which aims to enhance the representational power of feature maps by integrating channel similarity and spatial similarity matrices with a masking operation. Experiments

Datasets

In this study, the performance of the proposed model was evaluated using three publicly available datasets, which encompass different scales. Specifically, the GlaS dataset represents a small-scale dataset, while the Synapse and ISIC2018 datasets belong to the large-scale category. This diversity in dataset scale is deliberately designed to comprehensively validate the model’s applicability and robustness across varying data volumes.

Small-scale datasets

The Gland Segmentation (GlaS) dataset31 originates from the MICCAI 2015 Colon Histology Image Challenge and is specifically designed for gland segmentation tasks. This dataset consists of 165 samples, with 85 allocated for training and 80 for testing. All samples are derived from H&E-stained histological tissue sections of 16 colorectal adenocarcinoma patients, providing a reliable data foundation for gland segmentation research.

Large-scale datasets

The Synapse Multi-Organ Segmentation dataset32 was initially introduced for the MICCAI 2015 Multi-Atlas Abdomen Labeling Challenge. This dataset comprises 3,779 axial CT images from 24 cases, with 12 cases (2,211 images) designated for training and 12 cases (1,568 images) for testing. It covers the segmentation of eight abdominal organs, including the aorta, gallbladder, and spleen. Unlike the approach adopted by22, this study employs a five-fold cross-validation strategy during the training phase and computes the average score of the five models during the testing phase to enhance the stability and reliability of the results.

The ISIC2018 skin lesion segmentation dataset33, as a key component of the MICCAI 2018 Skin Lesion Analysis Challenge, is specifically designed for melanoma detection research. This dataset provides 2,594 dermoscopic images along with their corresponding ground truth annotations as the training set. Since the ground truth annotations for the test set are not publicly available, this study employs a five-fold cross-validation approach to evaluate the dataset, ensuring the reliability and reproducibility of the experimental results.

Implementation details

The model was implemented in PyTorch and trained on an NVIDIA 4060 Laptop GPU.

To effectively mitigate the issue of model overfitting, two data augmentation strategies were employed during training: random horizontal flipping and random rotation. The Adam optimizer was selected to optimize the model parameters.

To ensure the reliability of experimental results, a repeated stratified 5-fold cross-validation scheme was adopted, and the mean and standard deviation (std) of performance metrics were calculated based on multiple experimental results. During model training, a cosine annealing learning rate scheduling strategy, as proposed by34, was utilized. Additionally, a composite loss function was constructed by combining cross-entropy loss and Dice loss.

For model evaluation, the Dice coefficient and the 95% Hausdorff Distance (HD95) were chosen as the core evaluation metrics. All images were resized to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$224 \times 224$$\end{document} pixels before being fed into the network. The optimal hyperparameters were determined through multiple experiments. Specifically, the learning rate was set based on the dataset: 0.001 for GlaS and Synapse, and 0.0001 for ISIC2018. The batch size was set to 4 for GlaS and 24 for both Synapse and ISIC2018.

Ablation study

To validate the effectiveness of each component within MAFormer, we conducted ablation experiments on the GlaS dataset, with the results summarized in Table 3. The evaluation employed Dice coefficient and HD95 as performance metrics, comparing the full model against variants with specific modules removed.

First, within the MDFC module, removing the Channel Interaction Strategy (CIS) led to a Dice drop from 92.13 to 90.97 and an HD95 increase to 6.25, indicating that CIS effectively models inter-channel correlations and facilitates the adequate fusion of multi-scale features in skip connections. Eliminating Parallel Pooling (PP) reduced the Dice to 91.28, suggesting that the joint use of max pooling and average pooling on the same feature level captures both the strongest local responses and global average features, thereby enhancing the comprehensiveness of feature representation and improving the model’s ability to perceive both local salient details and overall contextual information. This finding confirms that CRM, through the channel similarity matrix and masking mechanism, emphasizes key channel features while suppressing redundant information, thereby strengthening semantic alignment between encoder and decoder. When the Spatial Refinement Module (SRM) was removed, Dice decreased to 91.12 and HD95 rose to 6.24, validating that SRM enhances spatial saliency modeling of target regions and improves background suppression via the spatial similarity matrix. Visualization analysis

The model was trained and evaluated on the GlaS, Synapse, and ISIC18 datasets. As shown in Fig. 4, the quantitative segmentation results of the MAFormer model across multiple tasks exhibit a high degree of consistency with the ground truth annotations, further demonstrating the segmentation performance and validating the effectiveness of the proposed method (Fig. 5). The first two columns present visualizations on the Synapse dataset, where it can be intuitively observed that the proposed model excels in modeling multi-scale targets, achieving particularly accurate segmentation of small organs such as the pancreas. In the gland segmentation task on the GlaS dataset, the model not only effectively suppresses false positive regions but also achieves precise delineation of boundary contours. In the ISIC18 dataset, under conditions of either clear or blurred lesion boundaries, existing methods often suffer from under-segmentation or over-segmentation, as illustrated in Fig. 6. In contrast, the MAFormer demonstrates more robust segmentation performance in both scenarios. This quantitative analysis indicates that the model achieves precise sub-region segmentation, thereby verifying its effectiveness in gland, multi-organ abdominal, and skin lesion segmentation tasks, and significantly enhancing segmentation accuracy.

To further validate the role of the attention mask mechanism in the ARRM module for regional focus and feature optimization, we compared the feature visualization results before and after applying the mask. As shown in Fig 5, without the mask, the feature responses are relatively diffuse: although they cover the target region, the boundaries remain blurred and are accompanied by strong background interference, making it difficult to form highly discriminative feature representations. In contrast, after applying the mask, the model effectively concentrates attention on the regions of interest, with activations in non-target areas being significantly suppressed. The target boundaries become clearer and more compact, and the overall morphology aligns more closely with the actual structure. This phenomenon is consistently observed across multiple samples, providing strong evidence that the attention mask enhances the model’s capability for regional focus and improves the quality of feature representation.

Conclusion

In this study, we propose an end-to-end medical image segmentation network, MAFormer, based on a U-shaped architecture to mitigate the semantic gap between the encoder and decoder. The model incorporates a multi-scale dependency feature construction module and an attention-based representation enhancement module. These mechanisms enable the model to effectively bridge the semantic disparity between the encoder and decoder while fully capturing both local and global feature interactions, thereby facilitating spatial detail reconstruction and semantic information fusion. Our method is simple, efficient, and capable of producing high-quality pixel-level segmentation outputs. Experimental results on the GlaS, Synapse, and ISIC-2018 datasets demonstrate that our model outperforms existing approaches, achieving superior classification performance. In future work, we aim to further reduce computational complexity and conduct a more comprehensive analysis of the impact of hyperparameters on model performance to refine our approach and address its current limitations.

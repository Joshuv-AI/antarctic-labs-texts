Natural products derived from traditional Chinese medicines targeting ER stress for the treatment of kidney diseases
Authors: Mengping Wang, Zhengtao Chen, Ziru Tang, Shiyun Tang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractVarious factors, both internal and external, can disrupt endoplasmic reticulum (ER) homeostasis and increase the burden of protein folding, resulting in ER stress. While short periods of ER stress can help cells return to normal function, excessive or prolonged ER stress triggers a complex signaling network that negatively affects cells. Numerous studies have demonstrated the significant role of ER stress in various kidney diseases, such as immune-related kidney injury, diabetic kidney diseases, renal ischemia reperfusion injury, and renal fibrosis. To date, there is a severe shortage of medications for the treatment of acute and chronic kidney diseases of all causes. Natural products derived from various traditional Chinese medicines (TCM), which are a major source of new drugs, have garnered considerable attention. Recent research has revealed that many natural products have renoprotective effects by targeting ER stress-mediated events, such as apoptosis, oxidative stress, inflammation, autophagy, and epithelial-mesenchymal transition. This article provides a comprehensive review of the current research progress on natural products targeting ER stress for the treatment of kidney diseases.Keywords: Natural product, ER stress, unfolded protein response, kidney diseases

Introduction

The endoplasmic reticulum (ER) is a specialized organelle responsible for the synthesis, folding, processing, and transport of proteins to various organelles or the cell surface [1]. ER stress occurs when changes in ER homeostasis, caused by endogenous and exogenous factors, increase the burden of protein folding within the ER [2]. ER stress plays a significant role in the pathophysiological process of renal diseases, as factors such as elevated blood glucose, lipid abnormalities, hypoxia, cell osmotic pressure, and concentration changes can induce ER stress in renal cells, resulting in renal damage [3]. To date, there is a serious shortage of drugs used to treat acute and chronic kidney disease of all causes. In China, traditional Chinese medicines (TCM) has a long history of treating kidney diseases, and TCM has unique advantages in improving the quality of life and long-term survival of patients with kidney diseases [4]. More and more studies have found the effectiveness of natural products derived from various TCM targeting ER stress in the treatment of kidney diseases. This article aims to review the research progress of natural products for the treatment of renal diseases by regulating ER stress.

Overview of ER stress

The ER is one of the largest organelles in cells and is responsible for controlling the quality of proteins through their folding and maturation. However, various factors like gene mutations, viral infections, inflammation, toxic chemicals, and oxidants can disrupt this protein folding process. When the accumulation of unfolded or misfolded proteins in the ER exceeds a critical threshold, it triggers the unfolded protein response (UPR), which aims to restore ER homeostasis through adaptive self-protection, leading to a condition known as ER stress [5]. UPR involves various organelles and is initiated by ER stress sensors including inositol requiring kinase 1 (IRE1), activating transcription factor 6 (ATF6), and protein kinase RNA-like ER kinase (PERK). These sensors are normally bound to immunoglobulin heavy chain­binding protein (BiP), also known as glucose-regulated protein 78 (GRP78), but under conditions of ER stress, they dissociate from BiP/GRP78 and become activated. IRE1 exists in two isoforms, IRE1α and IRE1β. IRE1α is expressed ubiquitously, whereas IRE1β is expressed only in intestinal epithelial cells [6]. In response to ER stress, IRE1α dimerizes and its ribonuclease domain cleaves X-box binding protein 1 (XBP1), generating its spliced and active form, XBP1s. Acting as a transcription factor, XBP1s promotes the transcription of genes involved in ER-related degradation [7]. PERK is typically inactivated by binding to GRP78 and becomes activated as a homodimer when dissociated from GRP78 under pathological conditions like hypoxia, ischemia, oxidative stress, and others. Phosphorylated PERK then proceeds to phosphorylate eukaryotic translation initiation factor 2α (eIF2α), which leads to the attenuation of mRNA translation [8]. During ER stress, ATF6 is cleaved by Site1 and Site2 proteases into NH2-terminal and cytosolic domains. The cleaved cytosolic domain is then transported to the nucleus, where it activates the transcription of several ER proteins, including Bip, XBP1, GRP94, calreticulin, Calnexin, protein disulfide isomerase (PDI), and C/EBP homologous protein (CHOP) (Figure 1) [7].

Adaptive pathways of UPR.

If ER stress becomes excessive or prolonged, it can lead to the disruption of the adaptive UPR pathway and trigger UPR-related apoptosis [9]. Mediators of apoptosis include CHOP, which is induced via PERK and activating transcription factor 4 (ATF4). Alternatively, IRE1α can induce apoptosis via activation of caspase 4 or caspase 12, apoptosis signal regulating kinase 1 (ASK1; also known as MAPKKK5) and c-Jun N-terminal kinase (JNK) and/or the decay of ER localized mRNAs. Additionally, release of Ca2+ from the ER into the cytoplasm induces caspase-12 or cytochrome c related apoptosis in the case of excessive ER stress. The mechanism responsible for the transition of ER stress from an adaptive response to a cytotoxic response is not fully understood (Figure 2) [10].

Effect of ER stress on other signaling pathways

ER stress has been reported to have a close relationship with inflammation. In the context of ER stress, inhibitor of nuclear factor-κB (IκB) is rapidly reduced due to translational inhibition caused by eIF2α phosphorylation. Since IκB has a short half-life, this leads to an increase in free nuclear factor-κB (NF-κB), resulting in its activation. Under persistent ER stress, dissociation of GRP78 triggers autophosphorylation of IRE1α, leading to conformational changes in its intracytoplasmic region. The activated cytoplasmic region of IRE1α then binds to the adaptor protein tumor necrosis factor α receptor-associated factor 2 (TRAF2). This IRE1α-TRAF2 complex recruits IκB kinase (IKK), which phosphorylates IκB, causing its degradation and NF-κB activation. Additionally, the IRE1α-TRAF2 complex can also recruit the protein JNK, resulting in the phosphorylation of the nuclear transcription factor activating protein-1 (AP1), this link between ER stress and other proinflammatory pathways [11, 12]. Reactive oxygen species (ROS)-induced ER stress can occur due to the depletion of ER calcium stores caused by the inhibition of Ca2+-ATPase, another possibility is that ROS may lead to ER stress by producing and accumulating abnormal proteins that have been modified by oxidation, additionally, unfolded proteins can accumulate in the ER as a result of ROS-induced dysfunction of ER folding enzymes and/or chaperone proteins [13]. In a study by Liu et al. it was observed that treating aged mice with antioxidants such as N-Acetyl-L-cysteine (NAC) or 3-tert-butyl-4-hydroxyanisole (BHA) before tunicamycin (TM) treatment could effectively prevent renal injury. The degree of renal protection is closely linked to the extent of inhibition of oxidative stress. Therefore it can be concluded that ER stress can lead to acute kidney injury (AKI) by inducing excessive oxidative stress [14]. Consequently, there exists a reciprocal causal relationship between ER stress and ROS. The interplay between ER stress and oxidative stress is believed to be a significant driving force in the progression of kidney diseases. ER phagocytosis, a selective form of autophagy, can be utilized to remove damaged ER [3]. Furthermore, ER stress or UPR can work in conjunction with autophagy to eliminate unfolded or misfolded proteins [15]. Studies have shown that ER stress can activate autophagy through the PERK-eIF2α pathway, and autophagy can counteract ER stress, providing a negative feedback mechanism to alleviate cellular stress [16]. Additionally, ER stress can induce the release of Ca2+ from the ER, which in turn activates calmodulin-dependent protein kinase kinase (CaMKKβ)/AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK), inhibits mammalian target of rapamycin complex 1 (mTORC1), or activates death-associated protein kinase 1 to enhance autophagy [17]. ER stress can induce autophagy by activating c-Src kinase in renal tubular epithelial cells (RTECs), thereby triggering epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and ultimately leading to renal fibrosis [18]. Moreover, the ER induces EMT by enhancing the expression of transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β) in RETCs. This process plays a significant regulatory role in EMT induction through the phosphoinositide 3 kinase (PI3K)/protein kinase B (PKB or Akt) pathway, Notch signaling, Wnt/β-catenin pathway, and possibly advanced oxidation protein products (AOPPs) [19]. It can be said that interaction between ER stress and other signaling pathways plays a crucial role in promoting the onset and progression of diverse kidney diseases (Figure 3).

Pathological role of ER stress in kidney disease

ER stress and renal ischemia reperfusion injury

There is growing evidence suggesting a close relationship between ER stress and renal ischemia reperfusion injury (IRI). Studies on CHOP-/- mice have shown a significant reduction in renal IRI. Both in vivo and in vitro studies have demonstrated that IRI induces the expression of CHOP in endothelial and epithelial cells, and that CHOP is involved in the induction of apoptosis [20]. Additionally, it has been found that ER stress-related proteins (GRP78, CHOP) and pro-apoptotic proteins (B-cell lymphoma-2 (Bcl-2)) are increased, while Bcl-2-associated X protein (Bax) expression is decreased, in human renal proximal tubular epithelial cells under hypoxic reoxygenation (H/R) stimulation and IRI in mice [21]. ER stress can occur in RTECs in vivo and in vitro after acute ischemia or intoxication. In response to epithelial stress, unfolded or misfolded proteins accumulate in the ER, triggering the UPR. Prolonged ER stress during renal IRI leads to the translocation of phosphofurin acidic cluster sorting protein 2 (PACS-2) to the ER and mitochondria, followed by the activation of caspase-3, resulting in apoptosis [22]. Additionally, sustained ER stress leads to mitochondrial Ca2+ overload, causing the release and activation of pro-apoptotic factors, ultimately leading to apoptosis [23].

ER stress and renal fibrosis

Investigations have confirmed that ER stress is a key factor in renal tubular epithelial cell atrophy and renal interstitial fibrosis (RIF) [24]. In unilateral ureteral occlusion (UUO) mice, there is an increase in protein levels of the GRP78, sXBP1s, eIF2α, ATF4, CHOP, caspase-3 [25]. Interestingly, the increase in CHOP expression is associated with elevated epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), extracellular matrix (ECM) expansion, collagen deposition, and apoptosis of tubulointerstitial RTECs [26–28]. In addition, the administration of 4-phenylbutyrate (PBA), a chemical ER chaperone, reduces GRP78, ATF4, and CHOP protein levels and also attenuates the increase of α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA), EMT, and connective tissue growth factor (CTGF) protein expression in UUO [29]. Furthermore, CHOP knockout can reduce the increased expressions of renal fibrosis markers collagen I, fibronectin, α-SMA, and plasminogen activator inhibitor (PAI)-1 in the kidneys of UUO-treated mice [30]. These data suggest that ER stress enhances the expansion of fibrosis in the UUO model.

ER stress and immune-related kidney injury

ER stress has been shown to be associated with various immune-related glomerular diseases. These include membranous nephropathy (passive Heymann nephritis in rats), mesangial proliferative glomerulonephritis (anti-THY1 nephritis in rats), and focal segmental glomerulosclerosis (FSGS; puromycin aminonucleoside nephrosis in rats) [10]. ER stress markers, such as ER chaperones or CHOP, are increased in glomeruli in kidney biopsy samples of patients with minimal change disease, FSGS, membranous nephropathy, and proliferative glomerulonephritis [31]. Dilated and expanded ER was visualized in podocytes of human membranous nephropathy [10]. In membranous nephropathy, complement C5b − 9 mediated podocyte injury may lead to protein misfolding due to dysregulated ER calcium content, intracellular adenosine triphosphate (ATP) depletion, or altered ER redox balance [32]. The expression of GRP78 and oxygen-associated protein 150 increases in the anti-Thy1 model of mesangioproliferative glomerulonephritis in rats, while preconditioning with ER stress ameliorates the severity of the disease [33]. Mice with anti-glomerular basement membrane (GBM) nephritis exhibit significant increases in various ER stress parameters in the glomerulus. Additionally, ultrastructural changes in the podocytes, such as dilatation of the ER and impaired ER function, are observed [34]. In the pathogenesis of lupus nephritis, anti-dsDNA antibodies play a crucial role. Research has shown that these antibodies induce NF-κB activation and inflammation in human mesangial cells (HMCs) through the PERK-eIF2α-ATF4 ER stress pathway [35]. Collectively, these findings highlight the important involvement of ER stress in immune-related kidney injury.

ER stress and diabetic kidney diseases

In diabetic kidney diseases (DKD) patients, there is a notable up-regulation of UPR regulatory genes involved in protein binding and protein folding, indicating the presence of activated ER stress. Additionally, significant alteration in the expression of the highly conserved ER transcription factor XBP1 and ER chaperones, such as heat shock protein family A (HSPA5 or GRP78) and hypoxia up-regulated 1 (HYOU1), is observed, suggesting a correlation between the extent of UPR and DKD [36, 37]. Numerous studies have shown that regulation of the UPR in DKD does not involve activation of all three major pathways. Instead of activating the PERK-eIF2α-ATF4 pathway, ER stress in DKD appears to involve ATF6 activation, which plays a key role in the progression of DKD [38]. In podocytes and glomerular endothelial cells (GEnCs) cultured in high glucose (HG), the nuclear level of XBP1s was found to be decreased, while the nuclear levels of ATF6α and CHOP were increased [38]. In the DKD state, ATF6 can induce XBP1 mRNA, and the synergistic signal transduction of sXBP1 mediates the activation of gene expression that restores the adaptive UPR and inhibits ER stress, thereby promoting its restoration [39]. Diabetes PKO mice (IRE1 knockout mice) exhibit more severe proteinuria, glomerular fibrosis, and podocyte death compared to non-IRE1 knockout mice [40]. Up-regulation of XBP1s reverses HG-induced ROS production and extracellular matrix expression [37], while knockdown of endogenous XBP1s yields opposite results [41, 42]. In the mouse model of DN, increased expression of pro-apoptotic ER stress markers CHOP, JNK, and caspase-12 is associated with enhanced renal cell apoptosis [43]. Studies have shown that DN and age-related proteinuria are prevented in mice lacking the CHOP gene [44]. More studies are needed to clarify the relationship between ER stress and diabetic nephropathy.

Treatment of kidney diseases by natural products: targeting ER stress-mediated events

ER stress-mediated apoptosis

Excessive or prolonged ER stress can lead to widespread pathological apoptosis, which is now acknowledged as a significant contributing factor in kidney diseases. It is reported that some natural products have the potential to target ER stress-induced apoptosis and provide renoprotective effects. Therefore, the use of natural products to target ER stress-induced apoptosis holds promise as a therapeutic strategy in kidney diseases.

Chrysin (5,7-dihydroxyflavone)

The flavonoid chrysin (5, 7-dihydroxyflavone) is a natural compound found in propolis and mushrooms. It has been shown to have anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and anti-apoptosis properties [45, 46]. The glomerular filtration barrier, composed of podocytes, endothelial cells, and the glomerular basement membrane, plays a vital role in maintaining renal function in DKD. Disruptions in podocyte structure and function can impair the integrity of the glomerular filtration barrier, resulting in elevated proteinuria, abnormal glomerular filtration rate, and increased creatinine levels [47]. In experimental models of diabetes, chrysin has been found to inhibit the activation of PERK-eIF2α-ATF4-CHOP. This inhibition leads to a restoration of the Bax/Bcl-2 ratio, suppression of apoptotic peptidase activating factor 1 (Apaf-1) induction, and a reduction in cytochrome c release in HG-exposed renal podocytes. These effects ultimately result in a decrease in proteinuria, improvement in glomerular ultrastructure, and enhanced induction of slit diaphragm proteins podocin/nephrin in diabetic glomeruli [48].

Astragaloside IV

Astragaloside IV (AS-IV) is a saponin compound found in Astragalus membranaceus (Fisch) Bge, which is commonly used in traditional Chinese medicine. In a study, it was observed that AS-IV effectively prevented podocyte apoptosis caused by ER stress. This was achieved by restoring the activity of SERCA (ER Ca2+-ATPase) and the expression of SERCA2, which ultimately reduced renal injury in diabetic patients [49]. AS-IV also demonstrated positive effects on various diabetic complications in rats, such as albuminuria, blood glucose levels, mesangial expansion, and podocyte apoptosis. Further in vivo and in vitro mechanism studies confirmed that AS-IV reduced the expression of GRP78, ATF4, CHOP, and phosphorylated PERK. These findings suggest that AS-IV alleviates ER stress-induced podocyte apoptosis by targeting the PERK-ATF4-CHOP pathway [50]. AS-IV has been found to have significant effects on reducing urinary albumin excretion, serum creatinine (Scr) and blood urea nitrogen (BUN) levels in streptozotocin (STZ)-induced rats. Additionally, AS-IV prevents mesangial matrix expansion and the increase of mesangial in these rats. This effect is attributed to the prevention of phosphorylation of eIF2α, PERK, and JNK, as well as the inhibition of the expression of GRP78 and oxygen regulatory protein 150 (ORP150), both in vivo and in vitro. AS-IV also inhibits the apoptosis of podocytes induced by TM, accompanied by a decrease in CHOP expression and cleaved caspase-3 [51]. Another study suggests that AS-IV alleviates apoptosis of RTECs in diabetic nephropathy (DN) rats. Furthermore, AS-IV improves body weight, biochemical and renal function parameters, and histopathological changes in the diabetic kidney. It also inhibits ER stress-related proteins GRP78, p-PERK, ATF4, and CHOP in the kidney of DN rats [52].

Quercetin

Quercetin (QC) is a well-known flavonoid compound that belongs to the polyphenolic family. It is widely present in various foods and possesses multiple properties, including antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and immunomodulatory effects [53]. In a study conducted by Salem et al. it was observed that QC, along with crocin (CRO), effectively reduced zearalenone (ZEN)-induced ROS generation, lipid peroxidation, ER stress, caspase activation, and DNA fragmentation in HEK293 embryonic kidney cells, thereby preventing cell death [54]. Another study reported that QC inhibited the activation of the PERK and IRE1 apoptotic ER stress pathways induced by asymmetric dimethylarginine (ADMA), leading to a reduction in apoptosis of GEnCs and TGF-β expression. The protective effect of QC against apoptosis in ER stressed cells was hindered by overexpression of CHOP. These findings suggest that targeting ER stress with QC holds great potential for the development of novel therapies to combat ADMA-induced renal fibrosis [55].

Morin

Flavonoid morin, originally extracted from the mulberry family, is a yellow bioactive polyphenol that has been recognized as an efficient compound in traditional medicinal herb. It exhibits a range of biological activities, including antioxidant, anti-proliferative, and anti-inflammatory abilities [56]. Research has demonstrated that Morin downregulates the expression of GRP78, XBP1s, phosphorylated-IRE-1α, phosphorylated-JNK, and reduces apoptosis in human renal tubular epithelial cells (HK-2 cells) induced by the ER stress inducer TM. Additionally, morin selectively inhibits the IRE1 pathway, one of the three major arms of the ER stress responses. These findings suggest that morin acts as an ER stress inhibitor and holds promise as a potential treatment for various ER-stress associated kidney diseases [57].

Naringenin

Naringin, a flavanone primarily found in citrus fruits like grapefruit and oranges, has been reported to exhibit various biological effects including anti-hyperglycemic, antioxidant, and anti-inflammatory activities [58]. In a study conducted by Khan, it was discovered that Naringenin played a novel role in alleviating ER stress during hyperglycemic renal toxicity both in vitro and in vivo. This compound also demonstrated the ability to prevent apoptosis, cellular damage, and tissue damage. These findings suggest that targeting ER stress could be a promising approach for managing hyperglycemic nephrotoxicity [59].

Chrysophanol

Chrysophanol, also known as 1, 8-dihydroxy-3-methyl-anthraquinone and rhein, is a natural anthraquinone compound found in Rheum officinale Baill, a traditional Chinese medicine used in the treatment of renal diseases. Numerous pharmacological properties have been attributed to chrysophanol, including hypoglycemic, anticancer, neuroprotective, hepatoprotective, anti-ulcer, anti-inflammatory, antiviral, antifungal, and antimicrobial activities [60]. In a study conducted on HK-2 cells, it was observed that chrysophanol prevented apoptosis induced by H/R by downregulating the expression of cleaved Caspase-3, p-JNK, and Bax, while upregulating Bcl-2 expression. Additionally, chrysophanol attenuated H/R-induced ER stress by downregulating the expression of CHOP and p-IRE1α. These findings suggest that chrysophanol may have a protective effect against AKI by regulating apoptosis and ER stress [61].

Resveratrol

Resveratrol (RSV) is a natural plant polyphenol found in various edible plants, including knotweed, grapes, peanuts, and berries [62]. It possesses anti-oxidative, anti-inflammatory, and cytoprotective properties [63]. In a study conducted on db/db mice kidneys, RSV was found to attenuate renal histopathological injury and decrease the expression levels of GRP78, CHOP, and caspase12. Additionally, RSV administration inhibited the apoptosis of rat renal tubular epithelial cells (NRK-52 cells) and the activation of the ER stress signal transduction pathway induced by HG treatment in vitro. These findings suggest that RSV plays a protective role against HG-induced apoptosis in DN by suppressing ER stress [62]. Another study discovered that RSV reduced the expression of eIF2a protein, subsequently reducing ATF4 levels, and consequently decreasing RTECs apoptosis and renal interstitial collagen [28].

Salvianolic acid B

Radix Salviae Miltiorrhizae, a significant traditional herbal medicine in China, has gained global recognition for its use in treating various diseases. Salvianolic acid B (SalB) is the primary component extracted from the roots of Radix Salviae Miltiorrhizae, and it has demonstrated renoprotective properties, including anti-inflammatory, antifibrogenic, and antioxidative effects on renal cells [64, 65]. SalB has been found to reduce the expression of apoptosis markers (bax and cleaved caspase-3) and ER stress markers (BIP, P-eIF2α, ATF4, CHOP, ATF6, IRE1α, and XBP1s) in the kidneys of DN mice. Moreover, SalB has shown the ability to attenuate apoptosis and ER stress in HK2 cells induced by palmitic acid (PA) [66]. Additionally, Sal B has been observed to counteract iopromide-induced injuries in HK-2 cells by reducing cell apoptosis, levels of ROS, and the levels of proteins related to ER stress and apoptosis, such as p-IRE1α, p-eIF2α/eIF2α, p-JNK, CHOP, bax/bcl-2, and cleaved caspase-3 [67].

Emodin

The anthraquinone emodin (1,3,8-trihydroxy-6-methy-lanthraquinone) is an orange crystalline phenolic compound extracted from the roots and rhizome of several plants, such as the traditional Chinese herbs Rheum palmatum, Polygonum cuspidatum, and Cascara buckthorn [68]. Emodin has been widely used to reduce inflammation, inhibit cell proliferation and ER stress, and prevent obesity [69–71]. In DN KK-Ay mice, emodin ameliorated urine albumin, Scr, BUN, the pathological damage of kidney tissue, and podocytes apoptosis by decreasing the expression of GRP78, phosphorylated PERK, phosphorylated eIF2α, ATF4, and CHOP. In vitro, emodin reduced the HG or TM-induced GRP78 level in conditionally immortalized mouse podocytes. Additionally, emodin and PERK knockdown inhibited podocytes apoptosis cultured in HG by counteracting the upregulation of phosphorylated PERK, phosphorylated eIF2α, ATF4, and CHOP. These findings suggest that emodin mitigates podocytes apoptosis by inhibiting the PERK-eIF2α signaling pathway both in vivo and in vitro, thereby exerting a protective effect on podocytes in DN [72].

Nobiletin

Nobiletin (NOB) is a dietary polymethoxylated flavonoid that is derived from citrus fruits. It has been found to have protective effects on IRI [73]. In a study conducted on C57Bl6/J male mice, it was observed that pretreatment with NOB significantly reduced renal damage caused by IRI. Additionally, NOB was found to decrease apoptotic rates and the expression of ER stress-related signaling molecules such as CHOP, caspase-12, and GRP78. These findings suggest that NOB pretreatment can alleviate IRI in the kidney by inhibiting ER stress-induced apoptosis [74].

Ginsenoside Rg1

Ginsenoside Rg1 (G-Rg1) is a type of tetracyclic triterpenoid derivative that is derived from Panax notoginseng [75]. It has been found to reduce RIF and RTECs apoptosis induced by cyclosporin A (CsA) by reducing the expression of GRP78 and CHOP [76]. In addition, G-Rg1 has shown to improve renal pathological lesions and decrease the expression of ER stress-associated proteins and the level of apoptotic cells in kidneys in UUO rats. This suggests that G-Rg1 may play a role in suppressing renal cell apoptosis and fibrosis by inhibiting the ER stress- and UPR-related apoptotic pathway in the kidneys after UUO [25].

Berberine

Berberine (BBR) is a natural alkaloid derived from coptis chinensis. In one study, it was observed that PA downregulated peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor (PPAR)-α and induced severe lipotoxicity in human HK-2 cells through ER stress, leading to increased lipid deposition, inflammation, cell apoptosis, and cleaved-caspase-3. However, these effects were significantly reversed when BBR was added as an additional treatment [77]. Another investigation demonstrated that BBR pretreatment protected human renal proximal tubular cells from apoptosis induced by H/R, possibly by suppressing mitochondrial stress and ER stress pathways [78]. Furthermore, research suggested that BBR could prevent PA-induced podocyte apoptosis by suppressing ROS-dependent ER stress [79]. Wang et al. discovered that BBR effectively reduced podocyte injury in vivo and in vitro by suppressing the expression of ER stress-associated proteins GRP78 and CHOP [80].

7,8-dihydroxyflavone

7,8-dihydroxyflavone (7,8-DHF) is a naturally occurring flavonoid that is derived from plants and vegetables. Extensive research has shown that 7,8-DHF possesses powerful neurotrophic effects, acting as a specific agonist of the tyrosine kinase receptor B (TrkB) and mimicking the brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) [81]. However, the impact of 7,8-DHF on kidney diseases remains largely unexplored. In a study conducted by Ma et al. it was discovered that 7,8-DHF could effectively inhibit hypoxia-induced apoptosis in HK-2 cells by increasing cysteine-rich protein 61 (Cyr61) expression while reducing CHOP expression [82].

Tauroursodeoxycholic acid

Tauroursodeoxycholic acid (TUDCA) is a combination of taurine and ursodeoxycholic acid primarily found in the bile acid powder of the bear. This powder is made from bile collected from bears living in the Ursidae family, like the black bear (Selenarctos thibetanus) and brown bear (Ursus arctos), both endangered [83]. TUDCA was reported to reduce the plasma glucose, creatinine and BUN significantly and led to a significant decline in renal hypertrophy, tubulointerstitial fibrosis and ECM accumulation. These improvement effects were more pronounced in combination of telmisartan and TUDCA. Further mechanism study showed that TUDCA reduced elevated BIP, CHOP, cleaved-PARP, cleaved caspase 3, caspase 7, caspase 8, TGF-β, p-SMAD2/3, collagen 1A1 in NRK-52E cells incubated with HG and in STZ- induced Wistar rats using [84]. GUO at el. explored the effects of TUDCA on renal fibrosis by establishing a TGF‑β1 stimulated renal mesangial cell (RMC) model, the results showed that TUDCA significantly downregulated TGF‑β1‑induced levels of GRP78, CHOP, α‑SMA and fibronectin in RMCs. In addition, downregulation of GRP78 inhibited the expression of FN and α‑SMA in the RMCs. Therefore, downregulation of GRP78 and CHOP expression is one of the mechanisms by which TUDCA inhibits TGF‑β1‑induced renal mesangial cell fibrosis [85]. In another study, diabetes aggravated IRI/HRI-induced AKI. Increased and persistent ER Stress (due to diabetes and AKI), led to activation of apoptosis and cell death eventually resulting in Klotho downregulation and AKI development. TUDCA decreased the expression of Bip, p-PERK, p-eIF2α, c-PARP, and c-casepase-7 and restored Klotho loss and ameliorated AKI. Thus, it could be concluded that TUDCA-mediated ER stress and apoptosis inhibition restored Klotho loss and ameliorated AKI [86].

ER stress-mediated oxidative stress

ER stress and oxidative stress are closely interconnected. ROS can trigger ER stress by depleting calcium stores in the ER and causing the accumulation of abnormal proteins that are oxidatively modified [13]. Additionally, ER stress can contribute to AKI by promoting excessive oxidative stress [14]. The interaction between ER stress and oxidative stress plays a significant role in the development of kidney diseases. Therefore, the identification of natural products that target ER-mediated oxidative stress holds great promise.

Rutin

Rutin is a naturally occurring flavonoid found in buckwheat, onions, apples, tea, and red wine. It acts as a scavenger of ROS by donating hydrogen atoms to peroxy radicals, superoxide anions, singlet oxygen, and hydroxyl radicals [87]. A study reported that the combination of rutin with ramipril restored the antioxidant status in rats with DN. The over-expression of angiotensin converting enzyme (ACE), TGF-β1, and decreased podocin expression in DN rats were significantly reversed by the combination of ramipril and rutin. Furthermore, the combinatorial therapy not only attenuated oxidative stress and fibrosis but also down-regulated ER stress markers GRP78 and CHOP. Histopathological results revealed a reduction in tubulointerstitial injury associated with the combinatorial therapy [88].

Baicalin

Baicalin, a flavonoid glycoside extracted from Scutellaria baicalensis, a traditional Chinese drug, has been extensively studied for its various properties, it has been proven to possess anti-bacterial, anti-inflammatory, and anti-apoptosis properties, making it a valuable treatment option for infectious and inflammatory diseases [89]. In a study conducted on HK-2 cells, baicalin was found to ameliorate H2O2-induced cytotoxicity by reducing oxidative stress and ER stress. Additionally, it activated the anti-oxidative red derived nuclear factor 2 related factor (Nrf2) signaling pathway. The protective effects of baicalin were diminished when ER stress was induced or Nrf2 was inhibited [90].

Tanshinone IIA

Tanshinone IIA (Tan IIA) is a lipophilic diterpene extracted from Salvia miltiorrhiza Bunge, previous studies have shown that Tan IIA has beneficial effects on DN [91]. It has been observed that Tan IIA can attenuate the increase of uric acid and BUN, restore the reduction in the activity of superoxide dismutase in the serum of DN rats, and improve renal pathological injuries. These effects are attributed to the decreased expression of TGF-β1, thrombospondin-1 (TSP-1), GRP78, and CHOP, as well as the attenuation of the increase of p-PERK, p-elf2α, and ATF-4 in the renal tissues of DN rats [92].

Morin

Morin, a flavonoid isolated from Maclura pomifera, Maclura tinctoria, and leaves of Psidium guajava, exhibits a wide range of pharmacological activities, including antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, anti-apoptosis, and anti-autophagy effects [93]. Singh et al. discovered that MH (Morin Hexoside) demonstrated a renoprotective effect against cisplatin (CP)-induced human embryonic kidney 293 cells (HEK-293 cells) and mice kidney injury. This effect was attributed to the suppression of oxidative stress-induced ER-stress activation, poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase 1 (PARP-1)-regulated pro-inflammatory pathways, and autophagy activation [94].

Quercetin

Quercetin (QUR) is a natural flavonoid that is commonly found in many fruits and vegetables, and it has gained attention for its ability to prevent oxidative damage, inflammation, and apoptosis in various tissues including the liver, kidney, lung, brain, and heart [95, 96]. Recent research has also demonstrated that QUR can alleviate CdCl2-induced nephrotoxicity through its antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects. Additionally, it has been found to suppress ER stress by upregulating or activating silence information regulator 1 (SIRT1), which induces the deacetylation of Nrf2, NF-κB p65, eIF2α, and XBP1 [97].

Paclitaxel

Paclitaxel, a type of Tricyclic diterpenoids, was initially extracted from the bark of the Taxus brevifolia tree, and it is considered to be the most important anti-cancer drug for breast, ovarian, stomach, and lung cancers [98]. Son et al. discovered that paclitaxel improved podocyte injury by reducing ER stress markers (ATF6α, CHOP, and xBP1s), decreasing ROS generation and NADPH oxidase 4 (Nox4) expression, and increasing antioxidant molecules (Nrf2, human heme oxidase (HO)-1, superoxide dismutase (SOD)-1, and SOD-2). These findings suggest that paclitaxel could be a promising therapeutic agent for treating PA-induced podocyte injury in DN [99].

ER stress-mediated inflammation

Previous studies have demonstrated the significance of ER stress in the progression of kidney diseases. ER stress activates the NOD-like receptor thermal protein domain associated protein 3 (NLRP3) inflammasome and NF-κB signaling pathways, leading to an inflammatory response [11, 100]. Recent research has identified various natural products that can effectively mitigate renal damage by targeting ER stress-induced inflammation.

Curcumin

Curcumin, a polyphenol derived from the Curcuma longa plant, commonly known as turmeric in Asia [101], has been shown to alleviate nephrotoxicity in mice with nonalcoholic steatohepatitis. This was evidenced by a decrease in BUN and Scr levels, as well as histopathological improvements. The protective effect of curcumin was attributed to its ability to reduce the expression of NAD(P)H oxidase subunits (p67phox, p47phox, p22phox), nitrotyrosine, and CYP2E1 renal protein, while also suppressing the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines (tumor necrosis factor α (TNFα), interleukin (IL)-1β, interferonγ (IFNγ)) through the inhibition of mitogen-activated protein kinases (MAPKs), p-JNK, p-extracellular signal-regulated kinase 1/2 (p-ERK1/2), GRP78, and CHOP. These findings suggest that curcumin preserves renal function by mitigating ER stress-mediated inflammation, oxidative stress, and apoptosis [102]. Additionally, curcumin has been reported to protect against angiotensin II (AngII)-induced podocyte injury and apoptosis, partly through the inhibition of ER stress [103].

Resveratrol

Resveratrol (trans-3,5,4′-trihydroxystilbene, RSV) is a polyphenol compound derived from various edible plants such as grape, peanut, berry, veratrum and so on. Wang et al. demonstrated that resveratrol significantly improved the renal function and tubular epithelial cell injury and enhanced the survival rate of cecal ligation and puncture (CLP)-induced rat model of sepsis, which was accompanied by a substantial decrease of the serum content and renal mRNA expressions of TNF-α, IL-1β and IL-6. In addition, RSV obviously relieved the ER stress, inhibited the phosphorylation of IRE1 and NF-κB in the kidney. In vitro studies showed that RSV enhanced the cell viability, reduced the phosphorylation of NF-κB and production of inflammatory factors in lipopolysaccharide and tunicamycin-induced HK-2 cells through inhibiting IRE1 activation [104]. Similarly, Yuan et al. observed that RSV effectively lowered fasting blood glucose levels, urinary protein levels, and alleviated renal pathological damage in diabetic rats. In diabetic rats, the expression of p-PERK, GRP78, ATF4, and CHOP increased significantly, but RSV counteracted these elevated levels of ER stress-related factors [105].

Luteolin

Luteolin, which is found in abundance in vegetables, fruits, and plants, possesses significant biological properties such as antioxidant, antiapoptosis, and anti-inflammation effects [106]. When it comes to its impact on renal diseases, luteolin has been shown to provide protection against colistin-induced nephrotoxicity, DN, lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-induced AKI, and more [107, 108]. In a study conducted by Hong et al. it was discovered that luteolin improved renal function in IRI rats by reducing neutrophil infiltration, inflammation, and NF-κB levels through the suppression of ER stress [109].

Withaferin a

Withania somnifera, a traditional medicine and dietary supplement commonly used in India, contains Withaferin A (WA) as a bioactive ingredient, numerous studies have shown that WA can effectively alleviate symptoms associated with chronic diseases and cancers [110, 111]. Chen et al. discovered that WA had a significant impact on improving histopathological changes, collagen deposition, and pro-fibrotic factors in UUO kidneys. Additionally, it reversed the increase of inflammatory signaling molecules, cleaved caspase-3, apoptosis, and neutrophil infiltration. The study also revealed that WA reduced ER stress-associated molecules in the kidneys of UUO mice. Therefore, it can be inferred that WA may protect against the progression of chronic kidney disease (CKD) by mitigating ER stress-related apoptosis, inflammation, and fibrosis in the kidneys [112].

Apigenin

Apigenin, a member of the flavone subclass of flavonoids, is abundantly found in fruits and vegetables like onions, oranges, parsley, and chamomile [113]. Numerous studies have reported on the various biological activities of apigenin, including its antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, anti-mutagenic, and antitumorigenic properties in different cell types [114, 115]. In a study conducted by Jeon et al. it was discovered that apigenin could effectively inhibit indoxyl sulfate (IS)-induced ER stress and the expression of IL-6 and cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor 1 A (p21) proteins in HK-2 cells [116].

ER stress-mediated autophagy

Previous studies have discovered that ER stress can activate autophagy, which in turn can counteract ER stress and provide a negative feedback mechanism to alleviate cellular stress [16]. Puerarin (PR), an isoflavone derivative obtained from Puerarin lobata (Wild.) Ohwi, has been found to have renal-protective effects in DN mice. It particularly enhances autophagy under ER stress conditions, possibly through the activation of the PERK/eIF2α/ATF4 signaling pathway [117].

ER stress and epithelial-mesenchymal transition

ER stress has been found to induce EMT, which can ultimately result in renal fibrosis. This process is triggered by the activation of various pathways, including c-Src kinase, TGF-β, phosphatidylinositol-3-hydroxykinase (PI3K)/Akt pathway, Notch signaling, Wnt/β-catenin pathway, and potentially adanced oxidation protein products (AOPPs) [18, 19]. Excitingly, there are promising natural products that target ER stress-mediated EMT, offering new possibilities for the treatment of kidney diseases. Ginsenosides, the main bioactive components in Panax ginseng, play a crucial role in its medicinal properties. Among the various ginsenosides present, ginsenoside Rb1 is the most abundant in ginseng. Recent research has shown that ginsenoside Rb1 has the ability to counteract the ER stress induced by bavachin, a compound found in Fructus Psoraleae. This effect is achieved by inhibiting the generation of ROS and subsequent activation of the Bip/eIF2α/CHOP signaling pathway in HK2 cells. Furthermore, ginsenoside Rb1 has also been found to alleviate bavachin-induced EMT and renal fibrosis. These findings suggest that the promotion of EMT and renal fibrosis by ROS, triggered by bavachin, is primarily mediated through the Bip/eIF2α/CHOP-mediated ER stress pathway, and ginsenoside Rb1 holds promise as a potential therapeutic agent for the treatment of bavachin- or Fructus Psoraleae-induced nephrotoxicity (Table 1) [118].

Conclusions and perspectives

Through the summary of this paper, it is not difficult to find that there is a close correlation between ER stress and kidney diseases. Excessive or prolonged ER stress can trigger apoptosis, inflammation, oxidative stress, EMT, and other pathways, ultimately accelerating kidney damage. ER stress seems to be a promising target for prevention and treatment of kidney diseases. Us Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approved chemical chaperone, such as 4-phenylbutyric acid (4-PBA) and tauroursodeoxycholic acid (TUDCA), are typical ER stress inhibitors that enhance ER folding ability and have protective effects on cells and tissues, but they lack tissue and cell specificity and safety, and long-term use has a greater impact on liver and kidney function. Cell/tissue-specific ER stress regulators still need to be developed for the treatment of kidney diseases. Traditional Chinese medicine has the characteristics of overall regulation, syndrome differentiation and treatment, which is different from person to person. It has unique advantages in alleviating clinical symptoms and delaying the progress of diseases, and has less toxic and side effects. Traditional Chinese medicines have long been recognized for their unique therapeutic effects on kidney diseases. Due to the complex composition and target of TCM, it is still unknown which natural component of TCM, especially TCM compounds and proprietary Chinese medicines, acts on which target to exert the regulation of ER stress in kidney diseases, which hinders the research and development of related drugs and the development of TCM. In the future, researchers can use technologies such as bioinformatics and network pharmacology, combined with in vitro and in vivo experiments to reveal the material basis of TCM targeting ER stress for prevention and treatment of kidney diseases, and further clarify the pharmacodynamic substances and mechanism of action, so as to greatly improve the accuracy and safety of TCM application, and promote the development of new drugs and market transformation. It is not difficult to find from the summary of this paper that a large number of studies have elaborated the mechanism of prevention and treatment of kidney diseases by various natural products of Chinese medicine from the perspective of regulating ER stress, which provides a certain theoretical basis for the prevention and treatment of kidney diseases by Chinese medicine.

However, there are still many problems worth raising in the current researches in this field: Firstly, at present, most of the research objects in this field are preclinical animal models and cell models, and there are few corresponding clinical studies. In the future, attention should be paid to carrying out related high-quality clinical studies and accumulating high-quality evidence-based evidence to assist in the formulation of clinical strategies. Secondly, most of the current studies are insufficient in depth, and most of them only describe the correlation between natural products and the occurrence of ER stress. The observed indicators are often several marker proteins related to ER stress, and the regulation of these natural products on the upstream and downstream targets of ER stress has not been deeply discussed. Finally, ER stress is a complex process with dual effects on kidney diseases. No studies have clearly reported the ‘tipping point’ at which ER stress switches from protective effect to killing effect. Identifying the key turning point or regulatory mechanism of ER stress from protective effect to killing kidney cells will be more conducive to the prevention and treatment of kidney diseases. At present, almost all studies of natural products have inhibitory effects on ER stress in kidney diseases, and few studies have reported that natural products play a protective role in the kidney by activating ER stress. Are there natural products that can stimulate the occurrence of appropriate ER stress or raise the threshold of ER stress to play a role in kidney injury, thereby protecting the kidney? This may be a very interesting and worthwhile research direction.

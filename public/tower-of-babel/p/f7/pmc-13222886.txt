Disentangled autoencoding equivariant diffusion model for controlled generation of 3D molecules
Authors: Tianxiao Li, Haoran Liu, Hongyu Guo, Mark Gerstein, Martin Renqiang Min
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractControlled generation of 3D molecules is highly valuable in drug design, enabling targeted modifications that preserve core structure and binding-relevant geometry while improving developability-related properties. While equivariant diffusion models achieve state-of-the-art de novo 3D molecule generation, reliably controlling multiple molecular properties remains challenging. A key limitation is structural: diffusion models typically lack an explicit latent space for targeted manipulation. We propose a semantics-guided equivariant autoencoding diffusion model that learns a disentangled semantic embedding of 3D molecules via an auxiliary encoder, to achieve fine-grained control over the generative denoising process. This semantic embedding enables efficient retrieval, random generation and controlled generation. By directly manipulating the embedding, we effectively steer the molecular generation toward desired compositions, shapes and physicochemical properties, and further enhance the generation quality with retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) using the embedding as the query. Importantly, the disentangled embedding offers significant advantages for joint manipulation of multiple properties. Experiments demonstrate precise and data-efficient property control while preserving non-targeted properties.Subject terms: Drug screening, Machine learning

Introduction

Computational design of molecules has many applications in drug design1,2 and protein engineering3–5. The 3D geometry of molecules holds significant implications for their properties and functions, such as quantum chemical properties6,7, molecular dynamics8–10, and interactions with protein receptors11,12. Recently, generative modeling of 3D molecules has been an active area of research. To properly represent the 3D geometry, some methods learn a joint distribution over the geometric features4,13. Others directly operate on the 3D molecular space14. Specifically, equivariant diffusion models have achieved state-of-the-art results in generating high-quality 3D molecules15–17, and also in design tasks with external conditioning, such as ligands conditioned on protein receptors18,19 and linkers conditioned on molecular fragments20. Diffusion models belong to the class of generative models designed to approximate the data distribution by iterative denoising from corrupted data21,22. Unlike previous methods for molecule generation that rely on autoregressive generation, diffusion models perform simultaneous refinement of all elements, such as atoms, charges and bonds, in each denoising iteration. This collective refinement approach enables them to effectively capture the underlying data structures, making them well-suited for structured generation such as 3D molecules. Several recent studies further incorporate auxiliary information, such as bond connectivity, to improve the generation quality23–25.

However, the majority of these works focus on de novo generation, i.e., generating random 3D molecules with no or limited control over their compositions, structures and properties, which we collectively define in this work as “semantics”. In real-world applications such as drug design, it would be desirable to ensure that the generated molecules contain certain attributes through explicit control of the generation process. Some previous works focus on the controlled generation of 2D graphs1,26–32, while controlling 3D molecule generation remains a challenge due to the complexity of the 3D molecular space and the need to preserve the 3D geometry. Some recent studies achieve conditioning on selected molecular properties15,33,34 or 3D shapes35. These methods, despite being effective on the designated tasks, only control a narrow portion of the generation process, and cannot be easily generalized to multiple or novel conditions without training a new conditional generative model. On the other hand, it is uncertain how conditioning affects the interactions among properties due to their complex interplay, and manually disentangling them is non-trivial and time-consuming. For example, a lead compound may require higher water solubility and easier synthesis. Meanwhile, the backbone composition, the 3D shape and subsequently the protein binding affinity of the lead compound should also be preserved. As these objectives do not necessarily align, the generation process that fulfills one objective may potentially interfere with another, and naively conditioning on multiple objectives without proper regularization may fail to find a direction that satisfies all. To overcome these limitations, it is thus essential to implement a refined control mechanism that both captures the comprehensive context of molecular compositions and shapes, and automatically disentangles the intricate interdependencies between the properties.

Controlled generation with diffusion models in particular poses another challenge as these models lack an explicit latent space to operate on. The latent diffusion model36 is trained on the low-dimensional latent space of another generative model, but the diffusion model’s own latent space is still implicit and difficult to control. Thus, controlled generation is usually achieved by external guidance on the denoising process. For example, classifier guidance37 and classifier-free guidance38 can generate random samples conditioned on certain properties. These methods have high demands on the availability of labeled data, and generalize poorly to new or multiple objectives. Some studies utilize external, pre-trained molecular representations as conditions39. Though such representations could be highly informative, there is no theoretical guarantee of the effectiveness of their control, and they cannot be easily manipulated. Thus, for data-efficient control of multiple molecular properties, we could leverage an unsupervised latent space that captures the complete semantic information about molecular structures and compositions and use it to guide the generation process of diffusion models.

In this work, we propose MolDiffdAE, a disentangled autoencoding equivariant diffusion model controlled by an unsupervised, higher-level semantic embedding of the molecule, inspired by the idea of semantic guidance of diffusion models for controlled generation of images40–42. The semantic embedding is co-trained with the diffusion model and is set up to have three important features that address the aforementioned challenges: (1) maximal mutual information with the input that guarantees complete semantic information about the molecule; (2) strong and precise control over the generation process of the diffusion model that effectively translates the semantics into the 3D molecular space; (3) disentangled semantic space through distributional regularization, which empirically enables conditioning on multiple properties with minimal internal interference, yielding Pareto-efficient control directions. By directly operating on the semantic embedding, we could control and manipulate the compositional and structural features of the generated molecules, as well as higher-level properties. We demonstrate the effectiveness of this method in two types of generative tasks: (1) semantics-guided generation, where we use predefined semantics to generate random molecules that contain the desired semantic information; (2) property manipulation, where certain properties of known source molecules are modified through altering the respective dimensions of the semantic embedding. We then evaluate whether the generated molecules exhibit the specified property(ies). We summarize our main contributions as follows:

We perform a comprehensive analysis of controlled 3D molecule generation and manipulation using diffusion models. We also establish the benchmark tasks and evaluation metrics for the subject. We prove that the semantic embedding learned by our model has a theoretical guarantee of maximal mutual information with the molecule, and demonstrate effective control over more than 200 molecular properties.

Semantics-guided diffusion generation framework

Our proposed MolDiffdAE model is an autoencoding framework that utilizes a semantic embedding to control the generation of the equivariant diffusion model. The model has two important features:

We implement an encoder that learns the higher-level “semantics” of the input 3D molecule as a continuous, lower-dimensional vector z, aggregating both node- and edge-wise features. The semantic embedding is then provided to an equivariant diffusion model as a condition. The diffusion model functions as a “decoder” that translates the semantic embedding back into the 3D molecular space. (Fig. 1A). During training, the model attempts to predict the clean input data from corrupted data and the semantic embedding. We apply a Wasserstein loss43 that minimizes the distance between the distribution of the semantic latent space and a prior distribution p(z). In our case, p(z) is an isotropic Gaussian distribution N(0,I). We show that this regularization ensures maximal mutual information between the embedding and the input, and also achieves disentanglement between the dimensions. Specifically, we add an auxiliary classifier on top of the semantic embedding to predict the target property, then train the classifier, encoder and diffusion model in an end-to-end manner with an additional classifier loss:

In this work, we only fine-tune on three more complex properties, QED (drug-likeness), SAS (synthetic accessibility) and LogP (lipophilicity, inversely related to water solubility), as we find their prediction with the pre-trained embedding more difficult (Fig. S2). These properties involve intricate interactions between the structural patterns distributed across the atoms, and cannot be learned without supervision, necessitating the fine-tuning. Since property calculation is time-consuming and reliable labeled data is usually rare in real-world scenarios, we construct a fine-tuning dataset by sub-sampling 10% of the pre-training dataset. We denote the pre-trained model as MolDiffdAE and the fine-tuned model as MolDiffdAE-ft.

We then use the diffusion decoder to flexibly perform controlled generation with the semantic embeddings (Fig. 1B, C). As shown in Methods, the embedding z shares maximal mutual information with the input x0. Thus, the generated molecule will presumably possess the properties encoded by the embedding. It is also possible to alter certain properties of interest by directly manipulating the embedding. In this way, we transform the challenging problem of controlled generation with diffusion models into a task of LSO, where several methods could be applied to obtain the desired semantic embedding, which is then translated into the 3D molecular space.

Semantics-based molecular retrieval

To interpret the latent semantic space, we perform molecular retrieval from the training set with a query embedding vector z. We use z of existing molecules in the test set as queries. Specifically, we calculate the cosine similarity between each query (source) molecule embedding z and the embedding z* of each molecule in the training set, and then retrieve molecules with the highest cosine similarity to the source.

Shown in Fig. 2A, molecules retrieved by the same query embedding display similar compositions to the source molecule. Quantitatively, the retrieved molecules show significantly higher 2D and 3D Tanimoto similarity to the source molecule, as compared to random pairings (Fig. 2B). In conclusion, we could efficiently query the molecular space and retrieve molecules with desired composition, shape and properties, indicating a semantics-rich latent space that could be used for controlled generation and manipulation.

Probing the property information in the latent semantic space

We train linear regression models to predict the molecular properties from the embeddings, to probe the information content of the semantic space. Despite being trained in an unsupervised manner unaware of any property label, the embedding space of the pre-trained MolDiffdAE could accurately predict many of the properties (Fig. S2A). The dimensions of the embedding can be distinguishably clustered by their differential linear regression coefficients with respect to the properties (Fig. S3). Specifically, only a limited, distinct set of dimensions significantly contribute to each property (Fig. 2C). Also, the embedding space shows strong separation of the high- and low-value groups for many of the properties (Fig. 2E). More complex properties like QED, SAS and LogP are harder to capture with the pre-training. Nevertheless, MolDiffdAE-ft fine-tuned on those properties shows higher predictive performance (Fig. S2B), sparser and more divergent contributions (Fig. 2D), and a better-separated latent space (Fig. 2F), while the fine-tuning has negligible impact on the other properties. Quantitative analysis also shows better disentanglement of the latent space after fine-tuning (Table S2). Meanwhile, the embedding dimensions have minimal internal correlations (Fig. S6A). These results indicate the successful disentanglement between the dimensions enforced by the Wasserstein loss.

Semantics-guided 3D molecule generation

To evaluate the effectiveness of the semantic control over the generation process of the equivariant diffusion model, we explore ways of utilizing it for the generation of 3D molecules with complete semantic information. Specifically, we investigate whether all the molecular properties encoded by the semantics could be successfully translated into the generated molecule.

We obtain the semantic embedding z of known source molecules x0 from the encoder and perform controlled generation using both deterministic (denoising diffusion implicit model; DDIM22) and stochastic (denoising diffusion probabilistic model; DDPM21) methods. For DDPM, we generate 10 molecules from each embedding and report metrics on valid molecules only. Figure S4A shows that DDIM with MolDiffdAE gives near-perfect reconstruction as expected, while DDPM generates molecules that are very close to the source molecule in terms of both composition and 3D shape. This shows that the embedding z dominates the denoising process, while the stochasticity accounts for minor variations.

For a quantitative evaluation of the preservation of the semantics, we define a molecular property profile including 205 non-zero-variance properties calculated by RDKit45. We report the following metrics between the generated molecules from DDPM and the source: (1) mean absolute error (MAE) of the properties, aggregated from the selected 2D and 3D property groups (see Table S1 for the full list); (2) Tanimoto similarity (∣F1 ∩ F2∣/∣F1 ∪ F2∣ where F1, F2 are two feature sets) of 2D and 3D fingerprints. We benchmark against the following methods:

VAE (2D): We use a 2D graph-based variational autoencoder (VAE) to generate random molecules from the embeddings of the source molecules. The generated molecules are represented as SMILES46 sequences. The 3D properties and 3D Tanimoto similarity of the generated molecules are calculated based on the RDKit-predicted conformations.

These results show that the unsupervised embedding successfully captures the semantic information about the 3D molecular properties and translates it into the generated molecules, indicating effective control over the generation of the equivariant diffusion model. Consequently, leveraging molecular generation based on the semantic embedding of known molecules facilitates the exploration of their neighborhoods in the molecular space in terms of compositions, structures, and properties.

In addition to the generation of full molecules, our model can also adapt to the problem of conformer generation, by fixing the atom and bond types during the denoising process (Supplementary). Despite not being trained specifically on this task, our model shows superior performance to similar models and baselines (Table S5), demonstrating the effectiveness of the semantic control.

Multi-objective molecular property manipulation

In this section, we further explore a generative task that requires more precise control: using the semantic embedding to manipulate the properties of interest, while preserving the majority of composition features, 3D shape and unrelated properties as much as possible. The problem is formulated as follows: let z be the embedding of a molecule x and yp=(yp1,…,ypm) be its property value(s) (pi∈Psub,Psub⊆P where P is the complete property set and Psub is the set of properties of interest). Given the desired target property value(s) yp′=(yp1′,…,ypm′), we search the latent space for a new embedding z′ that maps to a new molecule x′ which exhibits the target property yp′, while remaining sufficiently similar to x. The latter can be achieved by minimizing the deviation of z′ from z.

We experiment with two ways of obtaining z′:

Linear manipulation (lm): We first train a linear regression model yp = Wz + b (z∈R1×d; yp,b∈R1×m; W∈Rm×d; d is the dimension of z and m is the number of properties) to predict the properties from the embedding, then use it to manipulate the source embedding via: 2z′←z+W+(yp′−b−Wz) where W+∈Rd×m is the pseudo-inverse of W, i.e., WW+W = W. It can be proved that z′ is the closest vector to z (in terms of ∣∣z′−z∣∣2) that predicts the target property yp′ (see Methods and Supplementary for more details).

We use a one-pass, deterministic generation method where the encoder and decoder are each called only once per sample. Ideally, the minimal alteration to the semantic embedding during the manipulation implicitly preserves the similarity with the source, and no explicit similarity constraint is needed. Overall performance on multi-objective manipulation tasks

For the multi-objective tasks, we jointly manipulate 2 or 3 properties selected from halogen count (fr_halogen), QED, SAS and LogP. Specifically, we set our objective as lower SAS for easier synthesis, lower LogP for higher water solubility, higher QED for higher drug-likeness, and either higher or lower number of halogens. We define “improvement” for each objective as the change of the value of the property pi∈Psub adjusted by the desired manipulation direction:

where y^pi′ is the property value achieved by the manipulated molecule and ypi(0) is that of the source molecule. By this definition, more positive improvement is always preferred regardless of the manipulation direction.

We report “success” cases where (1) the generated molecule is valid and novel (not observed in the training set; Fig. S8); (2) the 3D Tanimoto similarity between the generated molecule and the source is greater than 0.5 (95% quantile of random pairwise similarity; Fig. S7); (3) all properties have improvements above a given threshold, where σpi is the standard deviation of the property pi. We use either a relaxed (>0) or strict (>0.5σpi) improvement threshold. Fig. 3A demonstrates that MolDiffdAE-based methods perform significantly better than the baselines in all but one task, especially under strict improvement thresholds. Furthermore, fine-tuning, back-propagation manipulation and RAG, when applicable, could enhance the overall performance, especially on the more complex properties (QED, SAS, LogP). Meanwhile, the majority of the compositional and structural patterns are well preserved as the manipulated molecules remain similar to their sources qualitatively (Fig. 3C,D,E, I; Fig. S10A). We also show that our methods achieve better numerical preservation of the irrelevant molecular properties, especially with fine-tuning and RAG (Fig. 3B; see “Methods” for more details).

A detailed analysis of the improvements shows that MolDiffdAE-manipulated molecules generally achieve a better trade-off between all objectives, while classifier guidance occasionally succeeds in one objective but fails in the others (Fig. 3D, F, H; Fig. S10B). These results highlight the advantage of MolDiffdAE’s well-regularized and disentangled latent space, as the objectives have minimal interference with each other. We also observe that the 2D graph-based VAE achieves reasonable success in manipulating the target properties (Table S7), but fails to preserve the 3D similarity, leading to lower final performance. MolDiffdAE, on the other hand, could better preserve both the composition and shape due to the strong semantic control.

In addition, we also analyze the geometric features (Table S4) and conformer energy (Fig. S5A) of the generated molecules, where MolDiffdAE-based methods, especially when combined with classifier back-propagation and RAG, display higher generation quality, indicating a well-regularized latent semantic space that facilitates smooth exploration. In the following sections, we provide a more comprehensive and in-depth analysis of MolDiffdAE’s controlled generation capacity, by investigating its manipulation behavior regarding molecular fragments, preservation of 3D shapes and protein interactions, and ability to manipulate 3D properties.

Case study 1: Joint manipulation of LogP and SAS

We perform an in-depth comparison between the manipulation results targeting both lower LogP (higher water solubility) and lower SAS (higher synthetic accessibility) for MolDiffdAE-based manipulation and classifier guidance. These properties are ideal targets for evaluating the impact of the disentangled latent space, as both of them are defined by the combined contribution of the molecular fragments: LogP is calculated as the sum of the hydrophilicity score of each atom48, and SAS is based on the frequencies of the fragments, as more common fragments imply easier synthesis49. As a result, different fragments may have aligned or diverging impacts on LogP and SAS. Thus, it is important for the property manipulation process to separate their differential contributions, and introduce fragments that contribute to both objectives.

In the first example (Fig. 4A), classifier guidance successfully reduces the SAS score by replacing the low accessibility backbone with a high accessibility one. However, some of these newly introduced fragments also reduce water solubility. For example, additional benzene rings are highly accessible but less water-soluble. Similarly, in Fig. 4B, the low accessibility backbone is modified, but solubility is not improved as the new backbone does not include more hydrophilic atoms. In comparison, manipulation with MolDiffdAE successfully considers both objectives, as it introduces fragments that are both more synthetically accessible and more water-soluble.

We further complicate the task by adding “more halogens” as another objective. This objective could interfere with the reduction of LogP as halogens are hydrophobic. Meanwhile, aromatic carbons bound to halogens would slightly increase water solubility as a result of the polarization of the aromatic ring. We show that on top of the aforementioned behaviors, MolDiffdAE-based manipulation also tends to attach halogens to aromatic rings to counteract the hydrophobic effect of halogens, while avoiding aliphatic carbons (Fig. 4C, D). In contrast, classifier guidance could trivially succeed in increasing the number of halogens, but fail to make other necessary modifications accordingly to reduce LogP (Fig. 4C) or SAS (Fig. 4D).

These results further prove that the disentangled semantic space successfully guides the diffusion model in multi-objective manipulation tasks. Manipulation in the disentangled semantic embedding space of MolDiffdAE could identify directions that simultaneously satisfy all objectives. With simple classifier guidance, however, the gradient of the classifiers could lead to conflicting directions and interfere with each other without proper regularization.

External Application 1: Binding-preserving property manipulation

Due to its ability to preserve 3D shapes, which is closely related to the interaction conformations, our semantics-guided property manipulation is particularly useful for protein-binding ligands. In those cases, the binding toward the target protein should be maintained, while some useful properties such as QED, LogP and SAS are modified. To test the generalizability of our method to this task, we apply both our pre-trained and fine-tuned model to the external test set of CrossDocked50, which contains the 3D structure of 100 ligand-receptor pairs. External Application 2: Manipulation of DRD2 binding affinity

Beyond basic molecular properties, we further test our method on more complex external properties. We focus on increasing the binding affinity to the dopamine D2 receptor (DRD2). Despite being a well-studied benchmark, the 3D structural data for positive DRD2 binders is still relatively scarce. We select 1400 positive structures from GEOM using the oracle predictor provided by the Therapeutics Data Commons (TDC)53. Due to the complex nature of protein binding, the small data size makes it difficult to train reliable 3D structure-based classifiers or conditioned diffusion models. We fine-tune our MolDiffdAE model on the DRD2 binding dataset, achieving good separation of positives and negatives in the semantic space (Fig. 7A), and perform linear manipulation to increase the binding affinity score. The generated molecules are then evaluated by the same oracle model, and we report positive molecules with binding scores >0.5.

Manipulation of 3D properties

Most of the existing works for controlled molecular generation are restricted to compositional properties, due to their limitations of relying on sequence or 2D graph representations, while the manipulation of 3D geometric properties remains poorly explored. MolDiffdAE as an equivariant 3D diffusion model enables operation on those properties. We manipulate asphericity (Asp) and radius of gyration (RoG) with linear manipulation. As expected, the 2D VAE fails in controlling the 3D properties. Among the 3D equivariant model-based methods, MolDiffdAE shows superior performance in both single- and multi-objective tasks, especially under more stringent improvement thresholds (>0.5σpi) (Fig. 8A).

Contrary to our previous results on compositional properties where the composition undergoes drastic changes while the 3D shape is mostly preserved, manipulation of the 3D properties typically leads to major alterations in the 3D shapes compared to the source molecules (Fig. 8B). In agreement with common observations, molecules with higher asphericity are less spherical, while molecules with higher radius of gyration are more spatially stretched. Meanwhile, the composition of the source is well-preserved after the manipulation. We also observe a good trade-off between the objectives with MolDiffdAE, while classifier guidance either fails in achieving both objectives or generates few valid molecules (Fig. 8C). The latter suggests the complexity of 3D geometries and implies the usefulness of encoding the 3D structural information into the molecular semantics. Such tasks would be useful in the search of candidate molecules with a preferred 3D shape and a fixed chemical composition.

Discussion

In this study, we present MolDiffdAE, an equivariant diffusion model guided by a disentangled semantic latent space for the controlled generation of 3D molecules. We show that the semantic embedding effectively controls the composition, shape, and molecular properties of the generated molecules, in both stochastic generation and deterministic property manipulation. More importantly, the regularization-induced disentanglement of the semantic embedding empowers effective multi-objective manipulation, which is a challenge for traditional diffusion models where the latent space is entangled and difficult to operate on. Though trained in an unsupervised manner, our pre-trained model is capable of capturing simple compositional and 3D shape properties, and can be used to manipulate them through the semantic embedding. For more complex properties whose contributing factors are distributed across many atoms, such as drug-likeness, solubility, synthetic accessibility and protein binding affinity, we show that our model could be further fine-tuned on small, external property-labeled datasets to enhance the performance.

Several existing controlled molecule generation frameworks on 2D molecules deploy iterative optimization or post-generation filtering to ensure the generation quality and the similarity to the source molecule if required1,29. Our method, however, uses one-pass generation, calling the diffusion decoder only once, and evaluating only the final generation output. The similarity constraint is implicitly enforced by minimizing the distance to the source semantic embedding. Despite the simplified generation pipeline, our method already achieves a good success rate with respect to molecular validity, property values and similarity to the source, as a result of the strong guidance of the semantic embedding and the high capacity of the diffusion model. We also show that the information-rich, low-dimensional semantic embedding vector can be efficiently used for the retrieval of similar molecules and subsequently the enhancement of manipulation results through RAG. Nevertheless, the generation quality of our model could also be further improved through iterative refinement or encoding of the molecular similarity into the latent semantic space with contrastive learning. It is also worth noting that in our experiments, we use randomly sampled “negative” source molecules, some of which may be too challenging for the target value. In real applications, rational pre-selection of more promising source molecules could further enhance the success rate.

Beyond basic molecular properties, we expect the method to be applicable to a wide spectrum of objectives, such as toxicity, conformational energy, multi-receptor binding affinity, and material properties. It is also possible to associate the semantic embedding with text descriptions, and utilize the recent advances in large language models for more flexible conditioning and control32. Our framework could also flexibly incorporate recent advances in representation learning and diffusion model architectures for 3D molecules, to enhance the representativeness of the latent space, the quality of generation, and the generalization to rarer and more complex structures. Specifically, using generative models with greater flexibility could enable more drastic manipulations, which is common in real-world drug design and optimization. Furthermore, although we have demonstrated the preservation of the 3D shape and the binding pose for ligand molecules independent of the receptor proteins, the semantics-controlled generation of ligands could still further benefit from jointly modeling both the molecular ligand and the receptor protein given sufficient data. Our framework could also be potentially incorporated into diffusion models for pocket-aware ligand design34,54, by manipulating the ligand within the confinement of the pocket structure.

In summary, we present a comprehensive exploration of controlled generation of 3D molecules using diffusion models. The design of our framework encourages several desirable behaviors for molecule design, such as precise control over the generative process, disentanglement of the semantic space and flexibility in adapting to diverse tasks requiring control of molecular properties. We demonstrate superior performance in the more challenging tasks with multiple properties and limited data compared to existing baselines, which highlights its potential in extending to real-world applications. We believe that our model, supported by rigorous evaluation and reproducibility, provides a solid foundation for future experimental studies, and offers new hypotheses for further investigation. The initial results of our approach are promising and may inform and guide subsequent validation efforts.

Representation of the 3D molecular data

For simplicity, we remove hydrogens from the molecules. We represent each 3D molecule as a fully-connected 3D graph x = (r, h, E) where r is the atom coordinates, h is the atom type and E is the edge type. Following previous practices on equivariant diffusion models15, r is centered on the center of mass (CoM) of the molecule. h and E are represented by one-hot encoding. Aromatic bonds are considered a distinct type besides single, double and triple bonds.

Semantics-guided controlled generation of 3D molecules

Given the semantic embedding z of a known molecule x0 = (r0, h0, E0) from the encoder, we combine it with randomly sampled stochastic noise xT′=(rT′,hT′,ET′) to generate new molecules x0′=(r0′,h0′,E0′).

Specifically, we obtain the embedding z of 100 source molecules randomly selected from the test set and generate 10 molecules controlled by each z. For each generated molecule x0′, we use RDKit to obtain its molecular properties and calculate the MAE for each property compared to that of the source x0. We report the MAEs for individual properties as well as property sets (“All”, “All 2D” and “All 3D”; the full list of properties in each subset can be found in Table S1). For the latter, we discard the properties with standard deviation <0.1 and perform z-scoring (subtracting the mean and scaling by the standard deviation) on the remaining properties. As a random control, we calculate the MAE of 10 random property profiles for each source molecule, where the value of each property pi is sampled from N(μpi,σpi) where μpi,σpi are the mean and standard deviation.

We also report the average 2D and 3D Tanimoto similarity between the generated molecules and their sources (see “Methods”). The random control is the average similarity between 10 randomly sampled molecules from the training set and each source molecule.

Deterministic generation from manipulated embeddings

To decode the manipulated embedding, we first reversemap x0 to a noisy data point xt with the source embedding z. We then provide the manipulated embedding z′ and xt to the diffusion decoder to generate a manipulated molecule x0′=(r0′,h0′,E0′). In practice, we only reverse map to xT/2 instead of xT and then denoise for T/2 steps without skipping, as it gives the best generation quality empirically.

Baselines

VAE

We use the 2D graph-based VAE model HierVAE28 pre-trained on the ChEMBL dataset64. Input molecules are represented as SMILES sequences. Given the source molecule, we use the HierVAE encoder to obtain its embedding. We add random noise to the embedding for semantics-guided generation, but not for property manipulation. For property manipulation tasks, we train a linear regression on the embeddings and manipulate the embedding in the same way as in previous property manipulation tasks. The corrupted or manipulated embedding is then provided to the HierVAE decoder to obtain the resulting molecule.

For a fair comparison with the unsupervised pre-training and one-pass deterministic generation mode of MolDiffdAE, we adopt a similar setting for HierVAE. Specifically, we use the HierVAE model pre-trained on isolated data, instead of the specialized graph-to-graph model set up for graph translation and trained on paired data.

Simple diffusion model

For the simple diffusion model, we use the same model architecture and hyperparameters provided by MolDiff25 (https://github.com/pengxingang/MolDiff). As our MolDiffdAE model is based on continuous diffusion, we also use the continuous version of MolDiff.

For multi-objective tasks, we train one model conditioned on all properties of interest (Halogen, QED, LogP and SAS for the 2D property model; asphericity and radius of gyration for the 3D property model). During generation, we use the target value for the manipulated dimensions of the property vector and keep the source’s original value on the other dimensions.

Classifier guidance

Classifier guidance is performed on the continuous version of MolDiff. For property-controlled generation, we train classifiers that predict all RDKit properties. For property manipulation, we train separate classifiers for each target property on the property set. The classifiers have the same EGNN-based architecture as MolDiffdAE’s semantic encoder, with a 2-layer MLP prediction head added on top of the model output. During training, the input is corrupted with random noise sampled from the same noise schedule as the diffusion model.

We use DDPM for property-controlled generation and DDIM with T/2 reverse and denoising steps for property manipulation. To evaluate the quality of generated molecules, we use the following metrics:

In addition, for 3D similarity, the molecules are aligned using GetCrippenO3A from the rdMolAlign module.

Molecular property

The property values of the generated molecules are calculated with RDKit45 and the TDC53 oracle, respectively, in the same manner as the fine-tuning dataset.

To evaluate whether the “irrelevant” properties are modified during the manipulation, we select a list of 2D compositional and 3D shape properties that do not overlap with the target properties (all properties in Table S1 excluding fr_halogen). For all generated samples, we calculate the MAE of the selected irrelevant properties, normalized by the standard deviation of each property.

The contributions of atoms and fragments to properties like LogP and SAS are retrieved from the source code. Specifically, for the labeling in Fig. 4, “high accessibility” fragments are those whose raw contribution score is >2 while “low accessibility” <−0.5. It’s worth noting that RDKit’s SAS output inverts the sum of the contribution scores, so higher contribution leads to lower SAS (i.e., higher synthetic accessibility). For LogP, the labels are based on the scoring table in ref. 48, where negative scores make the molecule more soluble.

Docking

For the CrossDocked test samples, the generated molecules are docked to the respective receptor using QuickVina (version 1.2.5)51,52. Specifically, the coordinates of the generated molecules are shifted to the center point of the source molecule in the original docking conformation. The density function of docking scores is estimated using Gaussian kernel density estimation65.

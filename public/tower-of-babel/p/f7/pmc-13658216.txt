Clinical responses to trastuzumab deruxtecan in molecularly defined ependymoma
Authors: L Nicolas Gonzalez Castro, Yi-Chien Wu, Jia-Ren Lin, Maria-Joao Santiago-Ribeiro, Alice Laurenge, Alexandre Carpentier, Shannon Coy, Nancy U Lin, Karima Mokhtari, Keith L Ligon, Mehdi Touat, Sandro Santagata
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractDespite evidence that antibody drug conjugates (ADCs) are active in brain metastases, their role in primary central nervous system (CNS) tumors remains unclear. We report two adults with molecularly defined ependymoma – a MYCN-amplified spinal tumor with intracranial dissemination and a supratentorial ZFTA-RELA-fused tumor with multiple recurrences – who demonstrated radiographic response and/or durable clinical and metabolic stability with trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd). HER2 expression was identified by immunohistochemistry in both tumors, consistent with prior systematic profiling of ADC targets in CNS tumors. Multiplexed imaging showed broad but heterogeneous HER2 expression across tumor states in both cases; in the MYCN-amplified tumor, this occurred alongside EGFR/MAPK-enriched proliferative niches, whereas the ZFTA-RELA tumor showed more diffuse organization without strong coupling of EGFR and proliferation. These findings provide early clinical evidence supporting HER2-directed ADCs in ependymoma and highlight the value of integrated molecular and spatial profiling in interpreting therapeutic response in rare CNS tumors.Subject terms: Cancer, Neuroscience, Oncology

Introduction

Ependymomas are rare, clinically heterogeneous central nervous system (CNS) tumors for which no targeted therapies are currently approved1,2. Molecular studies have defined biologically distinct subtypes, including MYCN-amplified spinal tumors and supratentorial ZFTA-RELA–fused ependymomas; however, these tumors lack clearly actionable driver alterations and often remain refractory to conventional therapies1,3,4. Recent systematic profiling of antibody-drug conjugate (ADC) targets across CNS tumors demonstrated that HER2 expression is more prevalent in ependymoma than previously recognized, thereby providing a biologic rationale for evaluating HER2-directed therapies in this disease5,6.

Trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd), a next-generation HER2-targeted ADC with a membrane-permeable payload, has demonstrated robust activity in HER2-low metastatic breast cancer and documented CNS activity in brain metastases, making it a promising candidate for evaluation in primary CNS tumors7–12. Here, we report two patients with molecularly defined ependymoma who demonstrated radiographic response or durable disease stabilization with T-DXd, including metabolic response by PET imaging in one case. We integrate high-dimensional spatial profiling to characterize tumor architecture and spatially resolved features related to proliferation, lineage, and signaling state, providing a mechanistic context for therapeutic response. Together, these cases illustrate how molecular characterization, complemented by spatial analysis across distinct tumor subtypes, can identify actionable vulnerabilities in rare CNS tumors and provide early clinical evidence supporting further evaluation of HER2-directed ADCs in ependymoma.

Results

Clinical and molecular features of a MYCN-amplified ependymoma with HER2 expression

We examined an adult patient with multiply recurrent, WHO grade 3 spinal ependymoma who developed intracranial dissemination following standard therapy (age 55 at initial diagnosis; see Supplementary Table S1 for detailed clinical history). At initial presentation, the patient underwent gross total resection of the spinal lesion, followed by standard adjuvant therapy. Histopathology of the resected tumor showed a densely cellular glial neoplasm with brisk mitotic activity, microvascular proliferation, and focal necrosis, consistent with anaplastic ependymoma (Fig. 1a). Tumor cells exhibited diffuse GFAP expression, perinuclear dot-like EMA staining, and retention of H3K27me3, and a high proliferative index, with Ki-67 focally exceeding 50%.

Targeted next-generation sequencing identified high-level MYCN amplification, defining an aggressive molecular subtype (WHO grade 3)13,14, with no additional actionable alterations. Tumor mutational burden was low (7 mutations/Mb). Despite multimodal therapy – including radiotherapy, temozolomide plus lapatinib, and bevacizumab – the disease showed repeated progression with intracranial and leptomeningeal involvement, highlighting the lack of effective targeted options for this subtype.

HER2 expression identified by immunohistochemistry and spatial profiling

Guided by prior systematic profiling of ADC targets in CNS tumors5, we assessed HER2 expression in archival tissue. Immunohistochemistry demonstrated diffuse weak-to-moderate membranous HER2 expression in ~50–60% of tumor cells, with focal strong staining in 5–10% (Fig. 1b). Although formal scoring criteria are not established for ependymoma, application of breast carcinoma guidelines15 and emerging CNS-specific criteria5 supported an estimated HER2 score of 2+ to 3+ (see Methods), corresponding to moderate to strong membranous staining across a substantial fraction of tumor cells, a range associated with responsiveness to HER2-directed therapy in other tumor contexts16 (see Methods). No ERBB2 gene amplification was detected by sequencing, indicating HER2 overexpression independent of gene amplification.

To define the spatial context of HER2 expression, we performed multiplexed immunofluorescence (CyCIF)17 (Supplementary Table S2 and Supplementary Data 1). HER2 expression was heterogeneous in intensity but broadly distributed across tumor regions, spanning both quiescent and proliferative compartments (Fig. 1c–e; Supplementary Fig. S1). By contrast, proliferative regions were spatially restricted and characterized by co-localization of EGFR and Ki-67, along with a less differentiated phenotype marked by reduced CD44, H3K27me33,18, and FOXJ119, and increased SOX2 expression (Fig. 1d, e). These EGFR-high niches were further enriched for PCNA and H3K4me3, consistent with a transcriptionally active, cycling state (Fig. 1d, e). Quantitative analysis confirmed a strong spatial correlation between EGFR expression and Ki-67 (r = 0.61), supporting localized EGFR/MAPK pathway activation. Together, these findings define a tumor architecture in which EGFR/MAPK-driven proliferative niches are embedded within a broadly HER2-positive tumor population, providing a mechanistic context for ADC activity and a rationale for combination strategies targeting both HER2 and EGFR/MAPK signaling.

Radiographic response to HER2-directed ADC therapy

Given HER2 expression and the absence of alternative targeted options, off-label trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd) was initiated. Baseline MRI showed progressive intracranial disease, including lesions in the left mesial temporal lobe (Fig. 2a) and cerebellopontine angle with internal auditory canal extension (Fig. 2b). After two cycles (5.4 mg/kg every 3–4 weeks), lesion size decreased by 70% and 13%, respectively, with further reductions after a third cycle, resulting in overall decreases of 84% and 54% (Fig. 2c). Responses across anatomically distinct sites support a systemic treatment effect.

Treatment was initially well tolerated, with grade 2 fatigue (CTCAE v6.0) as the primary adverse event, consistent with the known safety profile of T-DXd8,10,16. The patient subsequently developed pneumonia with worsening fatigue (grade 3), followed by pulmonary embolism. Clinical and radiographic features were not consistent with T-DXd–associated interstitial lung disease. Clinical decline was attributed to these complications rather than tumor progression, leading to treatment discontinuation and transition to comfort-focused care.

Independent validation in a ZFTA-RELA–fused ependymoma

To assess whether sensitivity to HER2-directed ADC therapy extended beyond MYCN-amplified disease, we evaluated an adult patient with a long-standing supratentorial ZFTA-RELA–fused ependymoma. Initially diagnosed in infancy (18 months), the patient underwent multiple resections, radiotherapy, and systemic therapies over decades, with recurrent disease in adulthood (see Supplementary Table S3).

Histopathology from 2018 and the most recent 2025 resection showed recurrent ependymoma with preserved morphology (Fig. 3a, b; Supplementary Fig. S2a, b). HER2 immunohistochemistry in 2025 demonstrated heterogeneous membranous expression (estimated score 2+), with moderate staining in a substantial fraction of tumor cells and focal stronger regions (Fig. 3b; Supplementary Fig. S2a).

Multiplexed spatial profiling (CyCIF) of both timepoints confirmed broadly distributed HER2 expression without restriction to specific cell subpopulations (Fig. 3c; Supplementary Fig. S2c–S2e; Supplementary Data 2 and 3). In contrast to the MYCN-amplified tumor, EGFR and Ki-67 showed reduced co-localization (r = 0.36), with overall lower proliferation (Supplementary Fig. S1b, c) and proliferative regions variably associated with differentiation markers such as FOXJ1 (Fig. 3c–e; Supplementary Fig. S3d–f). Consistent with the IHC scoring of 2 + , CyCIF-based HER2 quantification placed both the MYCN-amplified and the ZFTA-RELA–fused ependymoma samples within a lower, heterogeneous range relative to HER2-amplified breast cancer (Supplementary Fig. S3)20, overlapping primarily with HER2-low/intermediate levels associated with T-DXd activity7–12 (Fig. 3f).

T-DXd (5.4 mg/kg every 3 weeks) was initiated in February 2025 (age 34) and was well tolerated, with only transient grade 1–2 gastrointestinal toxicity. Over ~12 months, the patient has remained clinically stable with radiographic and metabolic improvement. MRI demonstrated a 27% reduction in enhancing tumor burden (Fig. 4a), and 18F-FDOPA PET showed a 46% decrease in metabolic tumor volume (17.17 cm³ → 9.25 cm³), consistent with a metabolic partial response by PET-RANO criteria21 (Fig. 4b), with a modest decrease in the maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax: 7.0 → ~6.5). Although baseline PET was obtained one month after treatment initiation, the sustained decline in metabolic tumor volume (MTV) supports an on-treatment effect. At the last follow-up (February 2026), the patient remains on therapy with ongoing clinical benefit.

Discussion

In this study, we provide early clinical evidence that HER2-directed ADCs may represent a therapeutic opportunity in ependymoma, a rare CNS tumor lacking approved targeted therapies and standard treatment at relapse. In two patients with molecularly distinct subtypes – MYCN-amplified spinal ependymoma and supratentorial ZFTA-RELA–fused ependymoma – we observed radiographic response or durable clinical stability following T-DXd. These findings support a target-defined approach that may extend across subtypes, pending validation in larger cohorts. Notably, in the ZFTA-RELA–fused case, concordant radiographic and PET-based metabolic responses indicate sustained biologic activity beyond conventional imaging. The greater magnitude of metabolic response relative to MRI aligns with prior observations that metabolic imaging may detect treatment effects not fully reflected by contrast enhancement alone21.

A central insight is the spatial organization of HER2 expression in ependymoma. Compared to HER2-amplified breast cancer, expression is lower in intensity, consistent with a HER2-low/intermediate state in which T-DXd shows activity7–12. In both cases, HER2 expression was broadly distributed across tumor regions. In the MYCN-amplified, HER2 spanned quiescent and proliferative compartments, while EGFR was enriched in Ki-67–high proliferative niches with a less lineage-restricted phenotype. By contrast, the ZFTA-RELA–fused tumor showed similarly broad HER2 expression but weaker coupling between EGFR and proliferation, with more diffuse relationships between proliferative and differentiation states. These findings indicate that HER2 spans heterogeneous tumor states, whereas proliferative signaling architecture varies by subtype.

This architecture provides a plausible mechanistic basis for clinical activity. HER2-directed ADCs with membrane-permeable payloads and bystander effects are well suited to heterogeneous or intermediate antigen expression11, whereas small-molecule kinase inhibitors require more uniform pathway dependence and effective blood-brain barrier (BBB) drug penetration22. Consistent with this, the MYCN-amplified tumor derived limited benefit from lapatinib despite EGFR expression in proliferative compartments, paralleling prior reports of modest, short-lived responses to EGFR/HER2 kinase inhibition in high-grade ependymoma23. These observations underscore the limitations of kinase inhibition in spatially heterogeneous tumors and highlight the advantages of ADC-based strategies.

These considerations also emphasize drug delivery constraints in CNS tumors. Alternative approaches – including intrathecal or intraventricular administration (e.g., via Ommaya reservoir) or transient BBB disruption (e.g., focused ultrasound) – may warrant investigation, although feasibility and safety with HER2-directed ADCs in such approaches remain to be defined. Rational combination strategies integrating HER2-directed ADCs with EGFR/MAPK inhibitors may further enhance efficacy in selected patients.

Importantly, HER2 expression and sustained disease control in the ZFTA-RELA-fused tumor indicate that ADC sensitivity is not limited to MYCN-driven disease. While HER2 expression has been reported across ependymoma subtypes, its extent and functional relevance – particularly in spinal subtypes such as myxopapillary ependymoma – remain incompletely defined and warrant further study. These findings support HER2 as a potential therapeutic target across molecularly distinct ependymoma subtypes, including tumors lacking actionable genomic drivers.

More broadly, our results extend prior work demonstrating HER2 expression across multiple primary brain tumor types, including meningioma, atypical teratoid/rhabdoid tumor, and adamantinomatous craniopharyngoma5,6. However, spatial distribution differs across tumor types. In contrast to the restricted localization in adamantinomatous craniopharyngioma, HER2 expression in ependymoma is broadly distributed, a pattern that may be more permissive for effective ADC targeting. These findings underscore the importance of spatial context in translating target expression into therapeutic strategies.

To contextualize our findings, we reviewed HER2-directed strategies in ependymoma across biologic, preclinical, and clinical settings (Supplementary Table S4)5,23–32. ERBB2 signaling is frequently present, and HER2 expression – often with EGFR activation – is observed in a subset of tumors, typically without gene amplification and with unclear predictive value. Clinical outcomes with first-generation HER2/EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors, including lapatinib, have been limited, likely due to poor intratumoral delivery, lack of biomarker selection, and tumor heterogeneity. Newer approaches – including antibody-based therapies, intrathecal delivery, CAR T strategies, and ADCs – aim to address these limitations, but evidence remains early-phase. These observations support an approach in which surfaceome-directed therapies are deployed based on protein-level expression and spatial organization, rather than canonical oncogenic drivers. The rapid expansion of antibody-based platforms – including ADCs, bispecific antibodies, and immune-engaging modalities5,6,33–35 – suggests increasing applicability in molecularly selected CNS tumors, provided that target expression, spatial distribution, and delivery constraints are addressed. Although both patients were adults, ependymoma is predominantly a pediatric disease, and the applicability of HER2-directed ADC therapy in children requires careful evaluation. Differences in treatment tolerance, safety profiles, and biology highlight the need for prospective pediatric studies.

This work parallels prior examples of precision oncology in rare CNS tumors. These include papillary craniopharyngioma, where identification of BRAF V600E enabled rapid clinical translation of targeted therapies36–38. Similarly, these results illustrate how integrating immunohistochemistry, spatial proteomics, and clinical response can reveal actionable vulnerabilities and support repurposing of existing agents.

Several limitations should be considered. The small number of patients precludes conclusions regarding predictors of response, and formal HER2 scoring criteria have not been established for ependymoma. The spatial profiling analyses from two cases do not capture the full spectrum of tumor heterogeneity across subtypes. In addition, PET-based assessment in the ZFTA-RELA–fused case was initiated after treatment onset and does not provide a true pretreatment baseline. Finally, treatment was administered off-label, limiting systematic assessment of durability and toxicity. Nonetheless, the concordance between molecular profiling, spatial architecture, and clinical response observed in two independent cases supports the biological plausibility of HER2-directed ADC therapy in ependymoma while underscoring the need for prospective validation. In summary, these findings provide proof of concept that HER2-directed ADCs may offer clinical benefit in select patients with ependymoma and highlight the value of integrating molecular and spatial profiling to inform therapeutic strategies in rare CNS tumors.

Methods

Patient identification, clinical data collection, and regulatory approvals

Patients with recurrent ependymoma were identified through the clinical practices of the authors. Clinical data, including demographics, tumor location, histopathologic diagnosis, molecular testing results, prior therapies, treatment course, and clinical outcomes, were abstracted during direct patient care and from the medical record. Adverse events were graded according to CTCAE version 6. Tumor classification and molecular subtype assignment were based on integrated histopathologic and molecular evaluation performed as part of routine clinical care. All tissue use and clinical data collection were conducted in accordance with institutional review board (IRB)-approved protocols and applicable regulatory guidelines. Written informed consent was obtained for off-label treatment and for the use of clinical data and tissue for research, in accordance with IRB-approved protocols.

Hematoxylin and Eosin (H&E) staining

Formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tumor tissue sections (4-5 µm) were prepared using standard histologic techniques and stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) for routine histopathologic evaluation.

HER2 immunohistochemistry

HER2 immunohistochemistry (IHC) was performed on formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tumor sections in institutional clinical diagnostic laboratories using a clinically validated anti-HER2 antibody (SP3 clone) and routine quality-controlled workflows. Appropriate positive and negative controls were included with each staining run. Antigen retrieval, antibody incubation, signal detection, and counterstaining were carried out using standardized clinical IHC procedures routinely employed for patient care. HER2 expression was evaluated based on membranous staining intensity and distribution. As formal HER2 scoring criteria have not been established for ependymoma, results were interpreted in context using established breast carcinoma scoring guidelines and emerging CNS tumor-specific criteria5,39. In brief, cases were scored according to the following system: Absent (0): No expression of HER2 by tumor cells, or weak membranous staining in <10% of tumor cells, Weak (1): Weak membranous staining in >10% of tumor cells, Moderate (2): Moderate membranous staining in >10% of tumor cells, or strong membranous staining in <10% of tumor cells, Strong (3): Strong membranous staining in >10% of tumor cells.

Multiplexed imaging (CyCIF) panel design and antibody validation

Multiplexed immunofluorescence imaging was performed using the cyclic immunofluorescence (CyCIF) method17,40. Antibody panels were designed to assess tumor cell identity, proliferation, lineage, and differentiation state, and signaling pathways, including markers of proliferation (Ki-67), receptor tyrosine kinase signaling (EGFR, HER2), lineage and differentiation (SOX2, FOXJ1, CD44)19, and structural or nuclear features, along with nuclear counterstaining (Hoechst). All antibodies were validated on control tissues and/or single-plex immunofluorescence prior to inclusion in multiplex panels using established approaches20,40 (Supplementary Table S1). For contextual comparison, relative HER2 expression levels of the MYCN-amplified ependymoma and ZFTA-RELA–fused ependymoma (2025) were evaluated against HER2 expression levels from breast cancer samples enriched for HER2-positive tumors (HTMA 226; 98 patients, triplicate cores; HER2 IHC spanning 0 through 3+)20, processed using the same HER2 antibody and comparable CyCIF imaging conditions.

Multiplexed imaging (CyCIF) staining, image acquisition, and preprocessing

Sequential rounds of staining, imaging, and fluorophore inactivation were performed as previously described for CyCIF17. Whole-slide or large-field images were acquired using a fluorescence microscope with consistent exposure settings across cycles. Image registration across cycles was performed to enable accurate marker alignment41.

Multiplexed imaging (CyCIF) image processing and quantitative analysis

Images were processed using established computational pipelines developed by the Laboratory of Systems Pharmacology including MCMICRO42. Cell segmentation was performed using nuclear and cytoplasmic markers to generate single-cell masks. Marker intensities were quantified on a per-cell basis following background correction and normalization. Spatial analyses included correlation analysis between marker expression levels (e.g., EGFR and Ki-67), visualization of marker co-localization, and generation of line-profile plots across defined tumor regions, performed on a subset of 50,000 randomly selected cells. Dimensionality reduction (e.g., t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding, t-SNE) was used to visualize cellular phenotypic heterogeneity based on multiplexed marker expression. CyCIF images are provided for inspection and exploration through Minerva software43,44.

Radiographic analysis - MRI acquisition

MRI studies were performed as part of routine clinical care using standard brain MRI protocols, including contrast-enhanced T1-weighted and FLAIR sequences. Imaging time points were selected relative to surgical intervention and initiation/administration of trastuzumab deruxtecan therapy. Radiographic changes were assessed by serial MRI using two-dimensional bidirectional measurements of enhancing lesions on axial images. For each lesion, the longest diameter and its perpendicular dimension were measured on the slice of maximal tumor extent, and lesion area was calculated as the product of these dimensions. Measurements were performed consistently across time points to enable longitudinal comparison. These analyses were exploratory and were not intended as formal RECIST or RANO response assessments.

Radiographic analysis - PET imaging

18F-FDOPA PET imaging was performed according to institutional clinical protocols as part of routine clinical evaluation and retrospectively analyzed for this study. Metabolic tumor volume (MTV) and maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax) were quantified for target lesions across serial scans using standardized clinical workflows, and metabolic response was assessed according to published PET-RANO criteria; the first PET acquisition occurred approximately one month after treatment initiation and therefore did not represent a true pretreatment baseline.

Statistical analysis

Correlation between marker expression levels (e.g., EGFR and Ki-67) was assessed using Pearson correlation coefficients calculated on single-cell quantitative data. No formal hypothesis testing or adjustment for multiple comparisons was performed, given the exploratory nature of the analyses.

All analyses were conducted using Python and R, with standard scientific computing libraries.

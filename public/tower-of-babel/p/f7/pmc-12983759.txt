Efficacy of electroacupuncture for pain relief in patients with osteonecrosis of the femoral head: protocol for a randomised, triple-blind, sham-controlled trial
Authors: Zhipeng Li, Yidan Yang, Xiaoya Liu, Malong Guo, Yan Cheng, Xiaoxia Yan, Chen Yue, Jiayi Guo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionOsteonecrosis of the femoral head (ONFH) is a progressive and disabling condition, with pain being its predominant symptom. Electroacupuncture (EA) has shown promise in the management of chronic pain; however, high-quality evidence on its efficacy and safety in ONFH is still lacking. The aim of this trial is to evaluate the efficacy and safety of EA for pain relief in ONFH.Methods and analysisThis triple-blind, sham-controlled, parallel-group randomised controlled trial will be conducted at a single centre in China and will include 176 participants with ONFH. Participants will be randomly allocated (1:1) to receive either active EA or sham EA. The active intervention involves EA applied to specific acupoints (GB30, GB29, ST31, BL54 and Ashi points) twice weekly for 4 weeks. Sham EA consists of superficial needling at non-acupoint locations using a deactivated device. The primary outcome is the proportion of patients achieving a clinically meaningful reduction in pain at 4 weeks (end of treatment). Secondary outcomes are changes in the Visual Analogue Scale score, the proportion of participants achieving clinically significant pain improvement at weeks 8, 12 and 16, modified Harris Hip Score, psychological scales (Patient Health Questionnaire-9, Generalised Anxiety Disorder-7, Pain Catastrophising Scale, Pain Self-Efficacy Questionnaire), the 12-Item Short Form Health Survey, pain sensitisation tests, rescue analgesic use and the incidence of adverse events. Data will be analysed using the intention-to-treat principle, and appropriate statistical models will be used to compare the primary outcome between groups.Ethics and disseminationEthical approval has been obtained from the Luoyang Orthopedic Hospital of Henan Province (Approval No: 2025ZXKT0001-02). Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants prior to enrolment. The results of this trial will be disseminated regardless of the findings through publication in a peer-reviewed journal.Trial registration numberISRCTN15751942.

Introduction

Osteonecrosis of the femoral head (ONFH) is a common and refractory orthopaedic disorder. Chronic pain, as a core clinical symptom, represents not only the primary reason patients seek medical care but also a central target for therapeutic intervention.1 2

Through multiple linear regression modelling, our research group has demonstrated that pain perception in ONFH patients is influenced by multidimensional factors. In addition to disease-specific characteristics—such as the extent of necrosis and clinical stage—psychological state and neurological factors were also shown to significantly affect pain intensity.3 These associations indicate that pain associated with ONFH arises from a complex interplay among somatic pathology, central pain processing and psychosocial factors. This multidimensional nature may partly explain the limited efficacy of conventional analgesics, such as non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), particularly in patients with chronic or advanced disease.4 Moreover, long-term use of these medications is associated with recognised adverse effects, including gastrointestinal injury, cardiovascular complications and potential drug dependence.5 Consequently, identifying safe, evidence-based, non-pharmacological interventions remains a clinical priority for optimising pain management in patients with ONFH.

As a key therapeutic modality in traditional Chinese medicine, acupuncture exerts its effects by stimulating specific acupoints to regulate physiological and pathological processes.6 Electroacupuncture (EA), a modern extension of traditional acupuncture, delivers controlled electrical stimulation through inserted needles, thereby enhancing the standardisation and reproducibility of treatment parameters. A growing body of clinical studies and systematic reviews indicates that EA is effective in alleviating pain associated with various chronic musculoskeletal conditions, including chronic low back pain, knee osteoarthritis and neck pain.79 Meta-analyses have shown that, compared with conventional treatment or sham interventions, EA significantly reduces pain intensity and improves quality of life in patients with knee osteoarthritis.10 11 Randomised controlled trials further demonstrate that EA effectively alleviates pain in fibromyalgia, with its analgesic effects accompanied by alterations in sensory cortical activity and functional connectivity within pain-related brain networks.12 Basic research suggests that EA-induced analgesia involves multiple mechanisms, including activation of endogenous opioid systems, modulation of inflammatory mediators and regulation of central pain-processing pathways.13 14 Furthermore, by regulating neuroendocrine activity, EA may influence the emotional and cognitive dimensions of pain, thereby alleviating anxiety and depression symptoms associated with pain.15 Existing clinical and mechanistic evidence collectively supports EA as an effective intervention for chronic pain management in musculoskeletal disorders, particularly in pain conditions involving neural sensitisation or psychosocial factors.

Direct evidence supporting the use of EA for pain relief in ONFH remains scarce, with only a limited number of studies exploring this intervention to date. For instance, a randomised controlled trial reported that EA of quintuple needling combined with adductor relaxation method achieved superior symptomatic improvement compared with relaxation therapy alone.16 While this finding is encouraging, the implementation of EA as part of a combined treatment and the absence of sham-controlled designs make it challenging to isolate the specific therapeutic contribution of EA. Furthermore, there is a notable scarcity of methodologically rigorous trials that systematically evaluate the impact of EA on multidimensional outcomes, such as pain sensitisation, psychological status and functional performance, within sufficiently powered and sham-controlled frameworks. Addressing this evidentiary gap is essential to provide a robust, evidence-based appraisal of EA for the management of ONFH-related pain.

Given this background, we propose to conduct a single-centre, randomised, triple-blind, parallel-controlled trial with the following objectives: (1) to definitively evaluate the efficacy of EA in alleviating pain in ONFH patients; (2) to elucidate its effects on hip function, pain sensitisation, psychological status and quality of life and (3) to assess the safety of EA in this patient population.

Methods and analysis

Study design

This study is designed as a randomised, triple-blind, sham-controlled superiority trial and is being conducted at a single centre in China. The trial will adhere to the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials guidelines17 and implement a rigorous randomisation and blinding procedure. A total of 176 eligible participants will be enrolled after providing written informed consent (online supplemental material 1) and will be randomly assigned to either the trial or control group in a 1:1 ratio. A brief study flow chart is presented in figure 1. The entire study will span 16 weeks, including a baseline assessment period, a 4-week intervention phase and a 12-week follow-up period.

Participants

The inclusion criteria will be as follows: (1) meet the diagnostic criteria for ONFH18; (2) aged between 20 and 65 years, inclusive; (3) no surgery anticipated or planned within the next 4 months; (4) history of ONFH-related pain persisting for more than 3 months; (5) target hip Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) score ≥40 mm, with the contralateral hip score being lower than that of the target hip. The target hip is defined as the affected side in cases of unilateral ONFH, and as the hip with the higher pre-randomisation VAS score in bilateral ONFH and (6) willingness to participate in the study and provision of signed informed consent.

The exclusion criteria are as follows: (1) presence of systemic or local conditions that may interfere with the assessment of ONFH-related pain or hip function, including systemic joint disorders (eg, rheumatoid arthritis), chronic inflammatory or connective tissue diseases, other hip pathologies (eg, developmental dysplasia of the hip), lumbar spine disorders (eg, lumbar disc herniation), or any other condition that may impact efficacy evaluation; (2) combination of serious organic diseases (eg, serious cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases, tumours), diagnosed psychiatric disorders (eg, anxiety disorders) or intellectual disability; (3) previous experience with EA for any indication; (4) history of total hip arthroplasty on the affected side, or any surgical intervention on the target hip (eg, hip-preserving surgery) within 12 months prior to screening; (5) any acupuncture-related therapy (eg, acupuncture, moxibustion) or shockwave therapy applied to the target hip within 3 months before screening; (6) history of invasive interventions (eg, intraarticular injection) to the target hip within 6 months prior to screening; (7) pregnant or lactating women, or women planning to conceive within the next 4 months; (8) known or suspected history of needle syncope (needle phobia); (9) known or suspected metal allergy and (10) currently undergoing corticosteroid treatment.

Recruitment and enrolment

Participants will be recruited from Luoyang Orthopedic Hospital of Henan Province and surrounding communities. Recruitment strategies will include referrals from orthopaedic outpatient physicians, posters and tweets on WeChat public website. Initially, potential participants who express interest will be contacted via telephone or in person to conduct a preliminary eligibility screening. They will then be invited for a face-to-face clinical evaluation to confirm eligibility, ensuring that all inclusion and exclusion criteria are appropriately applied. Finally, we will provide each participant with a detailed description of the study objectives, procedures, potential risks and expected benefits of this trial. Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants in accordance with the principle of voluntary participation. Recruitment will begin in September 2025 until 176 participants are enrolled in the trial.

Randomisation, allocation concealment and blinding

Participants will be randomly assigned to either the intervention or control group using block randomisation with varying block sizes of 4 and 6, achieving a 1:1 allocation ratio. The randomisation sequence will be generated using SPSS software (V.25.0; IBM). An independent statistician not involved in any other trial procedures will prepare concealed allocation documents stored in sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes. The corresponding envelope will be opened only after each participant has completed all baseline assessments to determine group assignment.

This trial will employ a triple-blind design wherein participants, outcome assessors and data analysts remain blinded to group allocation. Participant blinding will be achieved through a sham EA procedure designed to be indistinguishable from the actual treatment in appearance, procedure and sensory experience (refer to the Control Group Intervention section for details). Outcome assessors, who will evaluate all primary and secondary endpoints, will work independently from the treatment team and remain unaware of group assignments. Following database lock, statisticians will receive deidentified datasets labelled only as group A or group B for formal analysis. They will remain blinded to the actual treatment allocation until all analyses are completed.

Before the trial begins, both the randomisation officer and the treating acupuncturists will receive standardised training on blinding procedures. They will be explicitly instructed not to disclose or discuss group assignments with outcome assessors or statisticians at any point during the study.

Interventions

Participants in both the trial and control groups will receive treatment with the same frequency and duration: twice per week, with sessions separated by an interval of 2–3 days, over a 4-week period, totalling eight sessions.

In this trial, the selection of acupoints was determined based on meridian differentiation in Traditional Chinese Medicine, incorporating input from our experienced acupuncturists. The acupoint protocol includes both primary and supplementary points. The primary acupoints consist of Huantiao (GB30), Juliao (GB29), Biguan (ST31), Zhibian (BL54) and one Ashi point, defined as the most tender spot identified by the patient. Additionally, one supplementary acupoint was selected according to the location of hip pain: Liangqiu (ST34) for anterior pain, Xuehai (SP10) for medial pain, Weizhong (BL40) for posterior pain and Yanglingquan (GB34) for lateral hip pain (figure 2).

Illustration of acupuncture point locations. Red-marked points indicate primary acupuncture points, while blue-marked points represent distal supplementary points selected based on the pain location.

Participants will be placed in the healthy-side lateral decubitus position. Following routine disinfection, disposable sterile acupuncture needles (Pingbing type; sizes: 0.35×40 mm and 0.35×75 mm; Womeda Medical Device Technology, Suzhou) will be inserted into the designated acupoints to a depth of 30–45 mm. EA equipment will adopt the HANS acupoint nerve stimulator (model HANS-200E, produced by Nanjing Jisheng Medical Technology). The output electrodes will be connected to the handles of the inserted needles. A sparse-dense wave mode at 2/100 Hz will be applied,13 19 20 with current intensity adjusted according to each participant’s tolerance. The needles will be retained for 30 min per session.

For the sham EA procedure, non-acupoint locations approximately 20 mm away from the true acupoints will be selected, avoiding classical meridians and recognised acupuncture points. Sterile disposable needles of the same specifications will be inserted superficially to a depth of 3–5 mm.21 An EA device identical in appearance and model to that used in the intervention group will be connected via specially customised lead wires lacking internal conductors to ensure no actual current output.

To maintain blinding, acupuncturists will activate the device during each session to replicate visual and auditory cues (eg, indicator lights and operational sounds) consistent with actual treatment. All EA parameter settings and needle retention times will match those in the intervention group. Additionally, acupuncturists will perform a standardised mock stimulation procedure comprising the following steps8: (1) attach the lead clips to the needle handles and set the frequency to 2/100 Hz; (2) inform the participant that the device is being activated; (3) inquire about the participant’s comfort and tolerance regarding the upcoming 25–30 min session and (4) should a participant report a lack of sensation, the investigator will explain that the study uses a specific acupuncture modality that may produce stimulation below the sensory threshold.

All treatments will be administered by licensed Traditional Chinese Medicine practitioners with at least 5 years of clinical experience. Prior to the study, all acupuncturists will undergo standardised operating procedure training to ensure consistency in intervention delivery and treatment safety.

Concomitant treatment

All participants will receive standardised foundational management, including reduced weight-bearing (eg, use of a cane), avoidance of strenuous activities, guided functional exercise, lifestyle advice and scheduled weekly follow-up. Celecoxib is permitted as rescue analgesia in cases of intolerable pain. Although EA has been shown to modulate neuropathic pathways that may overlap with analgesic mechanisms, no clinically significant interactions between EA and rescue analgesia medications have been observed.

Participants with comorbidities on stable treatment regimens at enrolment may continue these therapies, provided they do not interfere with the evaluation of efficacy or safety outcomes. Should adverse events (AEs) arise during the trial that require intervention, appropriate medical treatment will be provided based on the clinical condition. The investigator must assess whether any concomitant medication could influence the outcome assessments of the investigational treatment. All concomitant treatments must be thoroughly documented in the case report form (CRF), including the name of medication or therapy, administration method, dosage, indication for use, and start and end dates.

The following medications and therapies are prohibited during the trial unless explicitly stated otherwise in the protocol: other pharmaceutical agents with anti-inflammatory or analgesic properties—including additional NSAIDs and opioids—with the exception of low-dose aspirin (≤325 mg/day) for cardiovascular prophylaxis. This dosage is consistent with international secondary prevention guidelines and is not considered to exert meaningful analgesic or anti-inflammatory effects.22 23 Intra-articular interventions—such as injections of corticosteroids, hyaluronic acid, growth factors or platelet-rich plasma, as well as joint lavage or arthroscopic procedures—are also prohibited. Furthermore, any supplementary physical therapies targeting ONFH are not permitted throughout the study period, including but not limited to other forms of acupuncture, tuina or physiotherapy.

Data collection and outcomes

Basic characteristics

At enrolment, baseline data are collected, including demographic characteristics (eg, sex, age, height, weight), medical comorbidities and concomitant treatments, ONFH-related clinical features (eg, aetiology, disease duration, affected side, target hip, clinical staging), and other relevant factors (eg, smoking, alcohol consumption).

Primary outcome

The primary outcome is the proportion of participants with a clinically meaningful reduction in pain at the end of treatment (week 4±1 day). A clinically meaningful reduction is defined as a decrease of ≥20 mm or ≥33% from baseline on a 0–100 mm VAS. Previous studies have demonstrated that a 2-point reduction on the 11-point Numerical Rating Scale or a 33% decrease in pain intensity represents a clinically important improvement in patients with chronic hip pain.24 A corresponding reduction of 19.9 mm on the 100 mm VAS has been established as equivalent to this threshold.25

Secondary outcomes

Secondary outcomes include the absolute change in 0–100 mm VAS score, the proportion of participants achieving clinically significant pain improvement—assessed using the same criteria as the primary outcome—at the week 8, 12 and 16 follow-ups. Function of the target hip is evaluated using the modified Harris Hip Score,26 with higher scores indicating better function. Pain sensitisation is assessed through Pressure Pain Threshold, calculated as the mean of three measurements per acupoint and Temporal Summation (TS), which evaluates pain amplification in response to repeated stimuli. Psychological status is measured using the following validated instruments: the Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9)27 for depression severity, the Generalised Anxiety Disorder-7 (GAD-7)28 for anxiety symptoms, the Pain Catastrophising Scale (PCS)29 for pain-related cognitive-emotional exaggeration, and the Pain Self-Efficacy Questionnaire (PSEQ)30 for confidence in managing pain. Health-related quality of life is evaluated using the 12-item Short Form Health Survey,31 which covers physical, social, emotional and pain-related domains; higher scores indicate better quality of life.

In addition, the frequency of rescue analgesic medication use (Celecoxib) will be recorded throughout the study period to capture supplementary analgesic needs. The incidence of AEs will also be monitored continuously during the intervention and follow-up and summarised at each assessment time point.

Adverse events

All AEs, defined as any untoward medical occurrence in a participant irrespective of its suspected relationship to the study intervention, will be recorded from the first treatment session until the end of the follow-up period. Acupuncture-related AEs may include dizziness, local pain at the needling site, bleeding and, in rare cases, infection or skin irritation. Such events are generally mild and transient. The investigator will assess the causality (related, possibly related or unrelated) and severity (mild, moderate or severe) of each AE using a standardised grading system.32 Any occurrence of the above AEs will be managed according to the predefined protocol and promptly reported to the ethics committee as required.

Blinding assessment

The success of blinding will be evaluated using a blinding questionnaire and quantified with the Bang Blinding Index.33

Data management

All data collected in this study will be recorded in standardised CRFs. Standardised training of investigators and continuous monitoring of the study process will be implemented to minimise information bias and reduce selection bias. All data will be entered by two independent personnel and then cross-checked for consistency and accuracy. On completion of data entry and verification, the CRFs will be archived in numerical order.

Sample size

The sample size was calculated using G*Power V.3.1 statistical software.34 The primary outcome is defined as the proportion of patients achieving a reduction in pain of ≥20 mm or ≥33% on the VAS at week 4 compared with baseline. Based on the results of an internal pilot study conducted from August to December 2024 (unpublished), 60% of patients in the treatment group achieved effective pain relief at week 4, compared with 33% in the control group. Sample size estimation was conducted using the z tests family in G*Power, selecting the statistical test ‘Difference between two independent proportions’ (normal approximation). Assuming a two-sided alpha of 0.05, 90% power, and accounting for a 20% dropout rate, a sample size of 88 participants per group is required, yielding a total of 176 participants.

Statistical methods

All statistical analyses will be performed using R V.4.4.1. A two-sided p<0.05 will be considered statistically significant unless otherwise stated. Analyses will adhere to the intention-to-treat principle. A per-protocol (PP) analysis will also be conducted as a sensitivity analysis to evaluate the robustness of the results.

The primary outcome—the proportion of participants with a clinically meaningful pain reduction (defined as a ≥20 mm or ≥33% decrease in VAS from baseline) at week 4—will be analysed using modified Poisson regression (identity link) with robust error variance, adjusted for baseline VAS scores. Risk differences and 95% CIs will be reported. Continuous secondary outcomes (eg, changes in VAS, psychological and quality-of-life scores) will be analysed using analysis of covariance, adjusting for outcome at baseline and prespecified covariates, including time and treatment interactions where appropriate. Binary secondary outcomes (eg, responder rates at follow-ups) will be evaluated using modified Poisson regression. Adverse events will be summarised descriptively.

Missing data will be handled using multiple imputation with chained equations. The multiple imputation model will include the treatment group, outcome, baseline covariates and any auxiliary variables predictive of the outcome or its missingness. 10 imputed datasets will be generated, and results will be combined using Rubin’s rules. Several sensitivity analyses will be performed to assess the robustness of the primary results, including: (1) PP analysis excluding participants with major protocol deviations; (2) complete-case analysis and (3) tipping-point analysis for missing outcome data. Moreover, instances of the use of rescue medication will be recorded and included as covariates for adjustment as a sensitivity analysis.

Prespecified exploratory subgroup analyses will be conducted based on baseline characteristics, including pain severity (VAS≥5 vs <5),35 pain catastrophising (PCS ≥30 vs <30),36 depressive symptoms (PHQ-9≥10 vs <10),37 anxiety symptoms (GAD-7 ≥10 vs <10),38 pain self-efficacy (PSEQ≥22 vs <22),39 radiographic stage (early (I–II) vs late (III–IV)), aetiology of ONFH (traumatic vs non-traumatic), age (≥45 years vs <45 years), sex (male vs female) and body mass index (≥28 kg/m² vs <28 kg/m²). Interaction terms will be included in regression models to examine potential effect modification. Multiplicity will not be corrected for subgroup analysis.

No interim efficacy analysis is planned.

Ethics and dissimination

Ethics and consent

This study has received ethical approval from Luoyang Orthopedic Hospital of Henan Province (Approval No: 2025ZXKT0001-02) and is registered in www.isrctn.com (ISRCTN15751942). It will be conducted in accordance with the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and the International Conference on Harmonisation–Good Clinical Practice guidelines. All participants who meet the eligibility criteria will provide written informed consent prior to participation. Study staff will explain the nature, objectives, procedures, duration, potential risks and benefits of the study. Participation is voluntary, and participants may withdraw at any time without affecting their medical care. Each participant will be given sufficient time to consult others and ask questions.

Informed consent must be obtained before any study-related intervention, and signed consent forms will be retained as part of the study documentation. The research team will uphold participants’ privacy and adhere to relevant privacy laws, ensuring anonymity in scientific reports and publications. All medical information will be kept confidential and will not be disclosed to third parties.

Dissemination

The results of the trial will be disseminated, regardless of the findings, through publication in a peer-reviewed journal.

Trial status

The study was prospectively registered prior to the initiation of participant recruitment. At the time of registration, no participants had been enrolled, and the date of first enrolment recorded in the registry represented the anticipated recruitment start date. During the trial implementation, the first participant was enrolled, completed baseline assessment and underwent randomisation on 23 September 2025. The results will be publicly reported following the completion of data collection and analysis.

Discussion

ONFH severely compromises hip joint function and quality of life in young and middle-aged patients. However, safe and effective strategies for pain management in the early stages of the disease remain limited.40 41 A significant therapeutic gap persists in current clinical practice between invasive surgical interventions and limited pharmacological palliation. This study aims to evaluate EA as a minimally invasive, non-pharmacological intervention to address this gap through a methodologically rigorous randomised controlled trial. To our knowledge, this is the first randomised controlled trial specifically designed to investigate the efficacy and safety of EA for pain related to ONFH. We anticipate that the findings will provide critical evidence to support the clinical application of EA in managing ONFH-related pain.

A key strength of this trial is its rigorous study design, which aims to minimise potential biases. Using the randomised controlled trial design ensures a solid foundation for evaluating therapeutic efficacy.42 Importantly, we implemented a strict triple-blind procedure (involving participants, outcome assessors and statisticians) along with a credible sham EA control. By applying superficial non-acupoint stimulation using an analogue EA device with no electrical output, we seek to isolate the specific physiological effects of EA from placebo responses and other non-specific factors.24 This approach mitigates methodological limitations noted in previous acupuncture studies, particularly inadequate blinding, enabling a more accurate assessment of the additive benefits of EA.43

Furthermore, the use of standardised intervention protocols and a comprehensive outcome assessment system enhances the reproducibility of the study and facilitates cross-trial comparability. A fixed acupoint prescription and uniform EA parameters were established to ensure treatment consistency and reduce inter-operator variability. In contrast to more pragmatic trial designs that permit individualised treatment adjustments, this explanatory study is better positioned to elucidate the specific biological effects of EA.

The primary outcome—the proportion of participants achieving a clinically meaningful pain reduction (≥33%) at the end of treatment—directly reflects clinical benefit while strengthening both clinical relevance and statistical power.44 Secondary outcomes include internationally validated instruments covering core domains of pain, function and quality of life. We will also examine changes in psychological metrics, enabling a multidimensional evaluation of therapeutic value of EA. These well-established endpoints will facilitate future integration of our results into high-level evidence syntheses, such as systematic reviews and meta-analyses.45 46 Additionally, incorporating quantitative sensory testing may provide novel insights into the mechanism of EA-induced analgesia.

Should our hypothesis be confirmed, this study may have substantial clinical implications. First, EA could offer a safe, effective and accessible non-pharmacological alternative for pain management in ONFH, potentially reducing the need for long-term analgesics and associated risks of side effects or dependency. Second, effective pain control and functional improvement might delay the necessity for total hip arthroplasty—particularly advantageous for younger patients wishing to preserve native joint function. Third, the high-quality evidence generated may inform clinical practice guidelines, supporting the integration of EA as a standard component of multimodal ONFH management.

Several limitations must be acknowledged. As a single-centre trial, the generalisability of our findings may be limited; future multicentre studies would be warranted to confirm broader applicability. Although triple-blinding was implemented for participants, assessors and statisticians, practitioners could not be blinded due to the nature of the intervention—a common challenge in manual therapy trials. We minimised this bias through standardised protocols and clear role separation between treatment and assessment personnel. Lastly, although a fixed EA protocol strengthens internal validity, it may not fully capture the individualised approach characteristic of traditional Chinese medicine.

In conclusion, this trial employs a scientifically rigorous design incorporating stringent blinding and control measures to address an important clinical question. Whatever the outcome, its findings will offer valuable evidence regarding the role of EA in alleviating ONFH-related pain, providing insights meaningful to patients, clinicians and health policymakers.

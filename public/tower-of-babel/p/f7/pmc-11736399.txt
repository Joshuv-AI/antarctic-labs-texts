HSP90AA1 is an unfavorable prognostic factor for hepatocellular carcinoma and contributes to tumorigenesis and chemotherapy resistance
Authors: Zhaoying Wang, Longfei Fan, Heng Xu, Zhongqiang Qin, Ziyi Zhu, Di Wu, Yigang Zhang, Ruoyu Liu, Jianzhu Wei, Zhen Qian, Peipei Yang, Bo Xie, Mu Yuan, Jingyu Qian
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractHepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is still one of the leading causes of tumor-related deaths. Accumulating evidence indicates that immunogenic cell death (ICD) could occur in tumor cells. However, ICD-related studies are limited in HCC. This study collected HCC RNA sequencing data from the Cancer Genome Atlas, International Cancer Genome Consortium, and Gene Expression Omnibus databases. R software was used to analyze the expression of ICD in HCC and to screen essential genes with prognostic value. qRT-PCR and WB determined the mRNA and protein expressions of hub gene. Cell viability assay, Clonal formation assay, and Live/dead staining assay were employed to determine the gene functions. After cross-analysis of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and ICD-related genes (ICDRGs), 7 differentially expressed ICDRGs were identified in HCC. Of them, HSP90AA1, with the most excellent prognostic value in HCC, was selected, whose expression was also validated in public cohorts, cell lines, and clinical tissue samples. High HSP90AA1 expression indicated an inferior prognosis of HCC, and HSP90AA1 knockdown significantly suppressed cell viability and chemotherapy resistance of HCC. ICD-related gene HSP90AA1 was an unfavorable factor for HCC, and high HSP90AA1 expression contributed to tumor cell survival and chemotherapy resistance.Keywords: HSP90AA1, Immunogenic cell death, Hepatocellular carcinoma, Chemotherapy resistance, Prognosis

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) ranks as the sixth most common malignancy around the world and is the third cause of tumor-related deaths [1]. According to the estimation of the World Health Organization, over 1 million people will die of HCC by 2030 [2]. The 5-year overall survival rate of HCC is approximately 18%, which is attributed to the facts that HCC patients are often diagnosed at late stages and suffers from high metastasis rates [3]. Although some HCC patients are eligible for resection operation, most of them (more than 70%) will suffer from intra- or extra-hepatic recurrence after the surgery [4]. The treatment strategies for advanced HCC patients are much more limited [5]. During the past decades, the significant development of tumor immunotherapy has brought new light to HCC patients, especially in the area of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) [6]. Recently, in a clinical study, atezolizumab (anti-programmed cell death ligand 1 antibody) combined with bevacizumab (anti-vascular endothelial growth factor monoclonal antibody) has exhibited encouraging outcomes in HCC patients [7]. However, only a tiny part of HCC patients could benefit from ICI therapy. The different etiologies of HCC may influence the efficacy of immunotherapy, and the immune microenvironment consisting of tumor cells, immune cells, and stromal cells and their poorly understood relationships modulate the immune response [8]. Dominik et al. demonstrated that anti-PD1 or anti-PDL1 therapy did not improve survival in patients with nonviral HCC (non-alcoholic steatohepatitis-HCC, NASH-HCC), which might be attributed to NASH-associated aberrant T-cell activation leading to tissue damage and consequently to impaired immune surveillance [9]. Meanwhile, the incidence of immune-related adverse events (IRAEs) in these patients needs to be further avoided [10,11]. Accordingly, a deeper investigation of the interactions between HCC and ICI is still crucial work. Based on this, it is possible to further identify reliable biomarkers for predicting immunotherapy response, stratification of patients based on biomarkers to achieve optimal response to therapy, and thereby improve HCC prognosis.

Immunogenic cell death (ICD) is a unique regulated cell death form induced by multiple stresses, which can activate innate and adaptive immune responses through the complex information exchange between dead cancer cells and immune cells [12,13]. More recently, evidence indicates the immunogenicity of dead tumor cells [14]. ICD could stimulate the immune system to take anti-tumor immune responses via releasing tumor-associated antigens (TAAs) and tumor-specific antigens (TSAs), which are featured with the elevated release and expression of damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs), precursor antigens, inflammatory cells [15]. During the past years, ICD has been considered one of the best ways to induce adaptive immune responses and improve anticancer therapy's efficacy [16,17]. Furthermore, ICD-related genes have been increasingly demonstrated as effective predictive diagnostic markers for various tumors. For instance, a risk score based on six ICD genes (CALR, CLEC9A, BAX, TLR4, CXCR3, and PIK3CA) has been indicated to predict the breast cancer patient prognosis and high risk patients exhibit worse prognosis and lower CD8+ T cell infiltration, implying the potential association between poor outcome and immune evasion [18]. High ICD score gastric cancer patients are found to have unfavorable survival, more advanced TNM stage, and more immunosuppressive microenvironment [19]. In prostate cancer, Kang et al. have documented that patients with high ICD levels have more abundant immune cell infiltration and better prognosis [13]. This evidence suggests that patient stratification based on ICD-related scores may be beneficial in identifying HCC populations potentially benefiting from immunotherapy. Whereas there are limited ICD-related studies in HCC, more ICD-related details remain largely unknown in HCC.

Hence, considering the vital role of ICD in many cancers, our present work aims to preliminarily explore the hub ICD-related genes in HCC, evaluate their prognostic value, and investigate the potential mechanisms in vitro. Our findings will provide more reference information for understanding ICD in HCC.

Materials and methods

Research object

Firstly, we downloaded HCC related mRNA expression data, mutation data (maf files), and corresponding clinical data from the Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) (https://tcga-data.nci.nih.gov/tcga/) database. A total of 374 HCC samples and 50 adjacent normal samples were included, among which 9 samples were removed due to lacking complete survival information. Besides, mRNA data and clinical files of 229 HCC samples were obtained from the International Cancer Genome Consortium (ICGC) (https://dcc.icgc.org/projects/LIRI-JP) database to validate the prognostic value of the hub gene. Next, five HCC datasets were downloaded from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/), including GSE87630 (for differential expression analysis), GSE84402, GSE64041, GSE76297 (for expression validation analysis), and GSE14520 (for survival validation analysis). The detailed information of these five datasets was summarized in Table 1. All immunogenic cell death related genes (ICDRGs) were listed in Table S1.

Differential expression gene analysis

The gene differential expression analysis was conducted using the limma package [20] of R (version 4.3.0). Then, the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were screened according to |Log2FC| >1.5 and FDR < 0.05.

Functional enrichment analysis

Using the "clusterProfiler" package [21], functional enrichment analysis of DEG was performed, involving GO terms (containing Biological process (BP), Molecular function (MF), and Cellular Component (CC)) and KEGG pathways. The significant terms were screened with a p < 0.05 (the p value was adjusted by Benjaminiand Hochberg method). Besides, DAVID database (https://david.ncifcrf.gov/) was also used for functional enrichment analysis.

Survival analysis

The overall survival rates of various groups were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method, utilizing survival and survmi packages of R (https://CRAN.R-project.org/package=survival). The difference significance was determined by the log-rank test.

Immune landscape analysis

CIBERSORT [22] was then employed to analyze the 22 types of immune cells’ infiltration status in various groups based on the 547 preset barcode genes and sample gene expression matrix.

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA)

To obtain more distinct functional information between HCC samples with differential hub gene expression, the HCC samples were divided into high and low hub gene expression groups. Then, the GSEA analysis was performed between high and low hub gene expression groups. Pathways with p.adjust <0.05 were considered as significantly enriched.

Cell culture

HCC cell lines HepG2 and Huh7 were purchased from Servicebio (Wuhan, China), and a normal human hepatic LO2 cell line was purchased from the Chinese Academy of Cell Resource Center (Shanghai, China). All cells were identified by STR profiling. All cells were cultured in DMEM containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin, in a 5% CO2 incubator at 37 °C.

Cell transfection

The siRNA targeting HSP90AA1 and non-specific siRNA were purchased from Sangon Biotech (Beijing, China). The sequences were designed according to a previous study [23]. Briefly, the cells were inoculated into 6-well plates (2 × 105 cells/well), and transfection was performed 18 h later. Specific or negative control siRNA (NC siRNA) was transfected into cells with Lipofectamine 2000 transfection reagent (Invitrogen, USA), strictly following the manufacturer's instructions. After 48 h of transfection, qRT-PCR and WB were employed to detect the transfection efficiency.

Cell viability assay

Next, 48 h after transfection, cells were seeded into 96-well plates at a density of 2 × 103 cells/well. The Control group was not transfected. Cells were grouped as follows: Control group, si-NC group, si-HSP90AA1 group, Onalespib group. The cells were treated with 500 nM onalespib for 12 h [24,25]. Then, the cell viability was measured at 450 nm, using CCK8 assay (#40203ES76, Yeason, Shanghai, China).

Clonal formation assay

After 48 h of transfection, the cells were inoculated into 6-well cell plates (500 cells/well) and grown for 10 days. The Onalespib group was induced with 500 nM Onalespib for 24 h and then replaced with fresh medium and continued to incubate for 10 days. We kept paying attention to cell status and culture medium during the cell culture. When there were cell colonies, cells were washed by PBS twice and fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 30 min, then stained 1% crystal violet for 20 min. Finally, after washing in PBS, pictures were taken to collect the results. Image J was used to count the cell communities.

Live/dead staining assay

Moreover, 48 h after transfection, cells were seeded into 6-well plates at a density of 5 × 104 cells/well. After stabilizing the cell state, the cells were treated with 10 μM cisplatin (Cis) for 12 h [26]. Live/dead staining assay (#KTA1001, Abbkine, Wuhan, China) was conducted strictly according to the manufacturer's instructions. Live and dead cells were stained with green fluorescent dye (LiveDye, Ex/Em=488/530 nm) and red fluorescent dye (NucleiDye, Ex/Em=535/617), respectively. Three random fields of 100 cells were counted in each group, and the dead cell proportion (100%) = (number of dead cells/100 cells in the field) × 100%. Fluorescence photography using fluorescence inverted microscope (Olympus, IX70). The average fluorescence intensity was calculated using Image J.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

Three pairs of HCC vs. adjacent samples were collected from the first affiliated hospital of Bengbu Medical University. Our study was approved by the ethics committee of our hospital (Date 2022–06–16/ethic code: [2022] No. 322). The first pair of samples was from patient 1, 71 years old, diagnosed with moderately differentiated HCC, BCLC A stage, in the left liver, with a lesion size of approximately 5.5 cm × 5.0 cm × 4.0 cm. The second pair of samples was from patient 2, male, 50 years old, moderately differentiated HCC, BCLC A stage, with a lesion of approximately 2.3 cm long via. The third pair of samples was from patient 3, female, moderate to poorly differentiated HCC in the right posterior lobe of the liver, BCLC A stage, with a lesion of 3.5 cm × 3.0 cm × 3.0 cm. All participants provided informed consents, in line with the declaration of Helsinki. All clinical tissues were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and embedded in paraffin. Paraffin sections (5 μm) were placed at 60 °C for 2 h, then dewaxed and hydrated. After 3% BSA blocking, primary rabbit anti-HSP90AA1 antibody (1: 200, #D163025, Sangon Biotech) was added and incubated at 4 °C overnight. Then, the primary antibody was washed away, and the sections were incubated with goat anti-rabbit secondary antibody (#ZLI-9017, ZSGB-BIO, China). After color development with DAB, the specimens were observed under a microscope (Leica, DM2500).

Western blotting (WB)

Terazosin (TZ), an HSP90 activator, was shown to up-regulate cellular HSP90 protein expression level at a concentration of 10 μM [27]. In this study, 10 μM TZ was used for rescue experiments after HSP90AA1 knockdown. Cells were grouped as follows: si-NC group, si-HSP90AA1 group, Cis group, si-HSP90AA1+Cis group, Onalespib group, si-HSP90AA1+TZ group, Onalespib+Cis group, si-HSP90AA1+TZ+Cis group. The cells were induced with 500 nM Onalespib and 10 μM TZ for 12 h followed by Cis for 12 h, respectively. Cellular total proteins were extracted using a mixture of cell lysis buffer RIPA and protease inhibitor, and the total protein concentration was determined by the BCA method. Then, total proteins (30 μg per sample) were added to SDS-PAGE for electrophoresis separation (80 V for 20 min and 120 V for 60 min) and then were transferred to PVDF membranes (200 mA for 60 min). The following reagents were used in our study, including rabbit anti-HSP90AA1 antibody (1:1000, #D120009, Sangon Biotech), rabbit anti-cleaved-Caspase 3 antibody (1:1000, #AF7022, Affinity), γ-H2AX (1:5000, #M01263–6, Cambridge, UK), and mouse anti-β-actin (1:2000, #M01263–6, Boster, Wuhan, China). Next, membranes were incubated with secondary antibodies. Finally, the membranes were treated with an ECL luminescent solution, and protein signals were detected and quantified using a protein imaging system (Tanon 5200).

Quantitative real time-PCR (qRT-PCR) analysis

Total RNA was extracted using an RNA Extraction Kit (#R1200, Solarbio, Beijing, China) and then reverse transcribed to obtain cDNA (#11141ES60, Yeasen) according to the manufacturer's instructions. Primers were listed as below: HSP90AA1: forward primer, CTATGGGGTCGTGGAACA; reverse primer, GCCTCATCATC GCTTAC; GAPDH: forward primer, GGAAGCTTGTCATCAATGGAAATC; reverse primer: TGATGACCCTTTTGGCTCCC. The SGExcel Fast SYBR mix (#B532955, Sangon Biotech) was used in the qRT-PCR amplification program, and reaction conditions were as follows: pre-denaturation at 95 °C for 3 min, PCR reaction at 95 °C for 5 s and 60 °C for 20 s, for a total of 40 cycles. GPADH was taken as the internal reference. The gene expression levels were calculated according to 2-ΔΔCT.

Statistical analysis

All the above statistical analyses were conducted in R software (version 4.3.0). The Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used to determine the different significance of gene expression and immune cell infiltration. Pearson's correlation analysis was finished using the "cor" function of R. Values of p < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. Data displayed as mean ± standard deviation.

Results

Identification of differentially expressed candidate ICDRGs in HCC

To explore crucial differentially expressed candidate ICDRGs in HCC, the TCGA cohort and GSE87630 were employed. Based on the TCGA cohort, a total of 12486 DEGs were identified between HCC samples vs. normal samples, including 9768 up-regulated genes and 2718 down-regulated genes (Fig 1A, 1C). In the GSE87630 dataset, we found 4303 DEGs in HCC samples compared to normal samples, of which 2285 genes were up-regulated, and 2018 genes were down-regulated (Fig 1B, 1D). To further focus on the ICDRGs, the DEGs from the TCGA cohort and GSE87630 were cross-analyzed with the ICDRGs. A total of 7 genes were obtained, including NT5E, HSP90AA1, BAX, IL6, IL1B, CD4, and PRF1, which were differentially expressed ICDRGs in HCC (Fig 1E). Subsequently, the DAVID database was employed, and these 7 genes were subjected to a functional enrichment analysis. We noticed that these genes were mainly significantly enriched in Th17 cell differentiation, IL-17 signaling pathway, and other immune related pathways (Fig 1F).

Identification of differentially expressed candidate ICDRGs in HCC. (A-B) The volcano plot of DEGs was based on the TCGA cohort and GSE87630, respectively. (C-D) The heat map of DEGs was based on the TCGA cohort and GSE87630, respectively. (E) Venn diagram among ICDRGs and the DEGs from the TCGA cohort and GSE87630. (F) Significantly enriched KEGG pathways was based on 7 ICDRGs (using DAVID database).

High HSP90AA1 expression indicated unfavorable prognosis of HCC patients

To further focus on the hub ICDRGs with prognostic value in HCC, a univariate Cox regression analysis was conducted on the above 7 genes. HSP90AA1, BAX, and NT5E showed excellent prognostic value with the highest significance (Fig 2A, p < 0.05). Then, we analyzed the gene expressions of these 3 genes in the GSE64041, GSE84402, and GSE76297 datasets. We found that in the GSE64041 dataset, HSP90AA1 and BAX were significantly up-regulated in HCC samples, while NT5E was down-regulated (Fig 2B). Based on data in GSE84402 and GSE76297, HSP90AA1 and BAX showed significantly higher expression in HCC samples, and there was no significant difference in NT5E expression between HCC and normal samples (Fig 2C-2D). Considering the above data, HSP90AA1, with significant prognostic value, was selected as the hub gene in our following studies. Then, according to the best cut-off value of HSP90AA1 expression, the HCC samples in various cohorts were divided into high HSP90AA1 expression HCC group (H-HSP90AA1 group) and low HSP90AA1 expression HCC group (L-HSP90AA1 group). After survival analysis, the results indicated that in TCGA, ICGC, and GSE14520 cohorts, all HCC patients in the H-HSP90AA1 group had significantly worse prognoses compared to the L-HSP90AA1 group (Fig 2E-2G). Collectively, our data suggested that high HSP90AA1 expression indicated an unfavorable prognosis for HCC patients.

High HSP90AA1 expression indicated unfavorable prognosis of HCC patients. (A) Results of univariate Cox regression analysis. (B-D) The expression of three candidate genes in GSE64041, GSE84402, and GSE76297, separately. (E-G) The KM survival curves between the H-HSP90AA1 group and the L-HSP90AA1 group, based on data in TCGA, ICGC, and GSE14520 cohorts, respectively.

Gene function difference and mutation difference between high and low HSP90AA1 expression HCC samples

Based on the TCGA-HCC cohort, the functional enrichment analysis was conducted between the H-HSP90AA1 and L-HSP90AA1 groups to mine more functional information. The significantly enriched GO-BP terms involved in mall GTPase mediated signal transduction, chromosome segregation, and positive regulation of cell adhesion, etc.; GP-CC terms included chromosomal region, spindle, and microtubule, etc.; and GO-MF terms contained ATP hydrolysis activity, cadherin binding, tubulin binding, and so on (Fig 3A, top ten terms). The DEGs between the two groups were also significantly enriched in many crucial KEGG pathways, like Human papillomavirus infection, Salmonella infection, and the Hippo signaling pathway (Fig 3B, top ten pathways). Moreover, we have also analyzed the somatic mutation status between the H-HSP90AA1 group and the L-HSP90AA1 group. The results suggested that in both two groups, TTN showed the highest mutation frequency (Fig 3C-3D).

Gene function difference and mutation difference between high and low HSP90AA1 expression HCC samples. (A-B) Top ten significantly enriched GO (BP, CC, MF) terms and KEGG pathways, respectively. (C-D) The somatic mutation status of samples in the H-HSP90AA1 group and L-HSP90AA1 group, separately.

Distinct immune microenvironment between high and low HSP90AA1 expression HCC samples

Considering the functional enrichment results and the crucial role of the tumor immune microenvironment, the immune landscape in HCC samples was then analyzed (Fig 4A). Using the CIBERSORT algorithm, the 22 types of immune cell infiltration proportions were compared between the H-HSP90AA1 and L-HSP90AA1 groups. We found that T.cells.CD8, Macrophages.M0, Macrophages.M1, Macrophages.M2, Eosinophils were significantly differentially infiltrated between H-HSP90AA1 group and L-HSP90AA1 group (Fig 4B). Besides, 8 crucial immune checkpoints were also analyzed between two groups, of which TIGIT, PD-1, CTLA4, BTLA, and PD-L2 were significantly up-regulated in the H-HSP90AA1 group compared to the L-HSP90AA1 group (Fig 4C). The above data implied that HCC patients in the H-HSP90AA1 group might be more likely to benefit from immunotherapy.

(C) Eight immune checkpoints’ expression between the H-HSP90AA1 group and the L-HSP90AA1 group. * p < 0.05, ** p < 0.01.

HSP90AA1 knockdown inhibited the cell viability and clonal formation of HCC cells in vitro

Based on our clinical HCC tissue samples, the IHC results indicated enhanced HSP90AA1 expression was observed in tumor samples compared to normal samples (Fig 5A). Moreover, significantly higher Hsp90aa1 mRNA and protein levels were found in tumor cell lines compared to normal cells (Fig 5B-5D). The above data showed a consistent tendency with the results of our bioinformatics analysis.

HSP90AA1 knockdown inhibited the cell viability of HCC. (A) Detection of HSP90AA1 expression in clinical samples by IHC. (B-D) HSP90AA1 protein level and Hsp90aa1 mRNA level in cell lines were analyzed using WB and qPCR, respectively. (E-G) The HSP90AA1 knockdown efficiency was determined by WB and qPCR, respectively. (H-I) The cell viability of si-HSP90AA1 HepG2 and si-HSP90AA1 Huh7 cells (CCK8 assay). ** p < 0.01, *** p < 0.001.

To further explore the potential impacts of HSP90AA1 on tumor development, the si-HSP90AA1 HepG2 and si-HSP90AA1 Huh7 cells were successfully constructed (Fig 5E-5G). Then, the cell viability, drug sensitivity, and clonal formation ability of si-HSP90AA1 and control cells were compared. As shown in Fig. 5H-I, si-HSP90AA1 and 500 nM Onalespib significantly inhibited the cell viability of HepG2 and Huh7. We found that cell cloning was significantly reduced in si-HSP90AA1 HepG2, si-HSP90AA1 Huh7, and Onalespib group compared to the si-NC group, suggesting that clone-forming ability was impaired (Fig 6A). Our data indicated that HSP90AA1 was involved in tumor cell proliferation of HCC, and downregulation of HSP90AA1 was favorable to killing tumor cells. Subsequently, various groups of cells were treated with chemotherapeutic drug cisplatin to evaluate the drug sensitivity of different groups. Silencing HSP90AA1 or inhibiting HSP90AA1 with Onalespib was more conducive to cisplatin-induced cell death in HepG2 and Huh7 compared to the si-NC group (Fig 6B-D). The WB results showed that knocking down or inhibiting HSP90AA1 in HepG2 and Huh7 exposed to cisplatin resulted in increased expression of apoptosis marker cleaved-Caspase 3 and DNA damage marker γ-H2AX (Fig 6E). After using HSP90AA1 agonist terazosin (TZ), cisplatin induced cell apoptosis and DNA damage were partially alleviated (Fig 6E). In short, HSP90AA1 knockdown inhibited the cell viability and clonal formation of HCC cells in vitro, and the silencing of HSP90AA1 reduced the drug resistance of tumor cells.

Onalespib: 500 nM. (B) Results of live/dead staining assay (10 μM cisplatin treatment). Green: live cells; red: dead cells. ** p < 0.01, *** p < 0.001. (C-D) Dead cell counts. Discussion

Although significant efforts have been devoted to improving the prognosis of HCC patients and developing novel treatment strategies, the overall survival of HCC patients is still frustrating. Recently, with a deeper understanding of ICD, ICD induction was designed to kill HCC cells in some studies to find novel treatment options [28]. Thus, further investigation of ICD and the related genes in HCC is still urgently needed. Our study identified a crucial ICDRG HSP90AA1 in HCC integrating bioinformatics tools and wet lab experiments. HSP90AA1 exhibited significantly higher expression in HCC, indicating an unfavorable prognosis, which was revealed in our work.

During the hub gene identification and prognostic value evaluation, 7 public HCC cohorts were included to obtain a reliable ICDRG in HCC. After cross-analyzing the DEGs and ICDRGs, 7 candidate ICDRGs were obtained, including NT5E, HSP90AA1, BAX, IL6, IL1B, CD4, and PRF1. We found that these candidate genes were significantly enriched in Th17 cell differentiation, IL-17 signaling pathway, and other immune-related pathways. Th17 cells have been suggested to exert vital roles in adaptive immunity and autoimmune-mediated inflammatory diseases [29]. Recently, Damasceno et al. have documented that the Th17 cell differentiation and pathogenicity probably rely on a type of metabolic reprogramming, which modulated the cell metabolism toward glycolysis [30]. However, our present study cannot conclude whether or not it plays a similar role in HCC onset. IL-17 signaling pathway has been indicated to be involved in inflammatory cascade and protective immunity against various pathogens [31]. IL-17 is significant in many crucial processes, like inflammatory disease pathogenesis and host defenses, especially in tumor development and progression [32,33]. More recently, it has been demonstrated that the Th17 differentiation and the secretion of IL-17 activated the hepatic stellate cells, thereby contributing to hepatic fibrosis [34]. Whether these crucial issues also happened in HCC deserved further investigation in our future work.

Next, among genes with excellent prognostic value, the HSP90AA1 expression exhibited better stability, which was successfully validated in public datasets, clinical tumor tissues, and cell lines in vitro. Highly HSP90AA1 expressed HCC patients had significantly inferior prognosis compared to the L-HSP90AA1 group. Mechanically, HSP90AA1 silencing inhibited the cell viability and clonal formation of HCC cells, implying the tumor-promoting role of HSP90AA1 in HCC. The heat shock protein α (HSP90α) encoded by HSP90AA1 is of rich content in cells (about 1%–2% of cells) [35]. In normal cells, HSP90α is inactive, while it would be activated in tumor cells [36]. Meanwhile, the active form of HSP90α can stabilize many downstream proteins, like mutant P53 and AKT, thereby contributing to the progression of various cancers [37]. Nevertheless, the repairing protective protein aspect of many heat shock proteins has been indicated to relate to the ICD process, which was considered an anti-apoptotic component in cancer [38]. Hence, the role of HSP90AA1 in ICD might depend on the status of its translation products in HCC. Furthermore, accumulating lines of evidence have suggested the crucial role of ICDRG HSP90AA1 in many cancers. For example, in bladder cancer, it has been reported that HSP90AA1 was significantly up-regulated and was a promising therapeutic target relating to tumor cell proliferation [39]. It has been indicated that HSP90AA1 was involved in promoting lymphatic metastasis of hypopharyngeal squamous cell carcinoma through epithelial-mesenchymal transition [40], whereas they did not illustrate the ICD-related role of HSP90AA1. Collectively, our data evidenced the tumor-promoting role of HSP90AA1 in HCC; besides combining our findings and previous reports, triggering anti-tumor immunity by targeting ICD-related markers could also be included in anti-cancer treatment.

Finally, considering the critical impacts of ICD on the tumor microenvironment (TME), the immune cell infiltration status was also analyzed between the H-HSP90AA1 group and the L-HSP90AA1 group. Five types of immune cells were significantly differentially infiltrated, and a significantly higher proportion of M2 Macrophages in the H-HSP90AA1 group attracted our attention. As an essential contributor to immunosuppressive TME, M2 Macrophages significantly limit the efficacy of cancer immunotherapy [41]. Recently, Li et al. have documented a drug DOX/R837@ManL selectively accumulating at M2 and tumor sites, which was able to promote the conversion of immunosuppressive M2 to immunostimulatory M1, meanwhile inducing ICD in tumor cells, thereby improving immunotherapy efficacy [41]. However, whether there is a similar potential of HSP90AA1 should be further explored in the future. Moreover, most immune checkpoints were significantly up-regulated in the H-HSP90AA1 group, implying a higher probability of the H-HSP90AA1 group HCC patients benefiting from immunotherapy.

Conclusions

In summary, HSP90AA1 has been explored in HCC regarding its ICD-related role in our present work for the first time. Significantly higher HSP90AA1 expression has been observed in HCC indicating an inferior prognosis. Moreover, high HSP90AA1 expression enhanced tumor cell viability and chemotherapeutic drug resistance. Hence, HSP90AA1 is a promising auxiliary diagnostic index of HCC involving chemotherapy's progression, prognosis, and efficacy. Our data provides a novel alternative treatment target for HCC, which is expected to be a candidate for clinical translation in the future.

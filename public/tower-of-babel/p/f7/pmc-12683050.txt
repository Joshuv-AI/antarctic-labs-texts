Assessment of the stentless mitral valve and operation using bioengineering method
Authors: Junpei Takada, Hitoshi Kasegawa, Kiyotaka Iwasaki
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT

A 27-mm bioprosthetic valve (Mosaic; Medtronic Inc) was used as a control. The hydrodynamic performance of the Normo valve and bioprosthesis was investigated and compared. As for the large leaflet, the Normo valve made from original design template revealed lowest pressure gradient compared to other 2 types. As for the small leaflet, the Normo valve made from original design template showed lowest pressure gradient with greatest forward flow. The stress on the leaflet increased for the distance between 2 papillary muscles at 18 mm and 36 mm.ConclusionsThe data obtained from the bioengineering method confirm the validity of the Normo valve's design. Furthermore, the results show preferable positioning for the fixation of the leg to 2 papillary muscles.Key Words: mitral valve, stentless mitral valve, Normo valve, pulsating circulatory simulator, finite element analysis

Central Message

The data obtained from the bioengineering method confirmed the excellent performance of the Normo valve as well as the validity of the valve's design.

Perspective

The pulsatile circulatory simulator is very useful to evaluate the function of the mitral valve. To increase the reliability of the assessment, further development of the simulator would be necessary.

We have been assessing hydrodynamic function of a stentless mitral valve (MV; Normo) using a mechanical pulsatile circulatory simulator before starting a multicenter clinical study, the data of which have been previously reported.1 Even after the initiation of the clinical study, we continued to assess the design validity of this valve. In addition, other bioengineering techniques have been used to further assess its performance. This study shows the results, confirming the design validity of the Normo valve and other important results, and also confirming the successful Normo operation.

Methods

Normo Valve Construction

The Normo valve is composed of 2 (large anterior and small posterior) leaflets.1, 2, 3 At the laboratory center of our institution, the valve was formed by suturing the leaflets (made from bovine pericardium) into a flexible ring. The Normo valve with a 27-mm flexible ring (Duran ring; Medtronic Inc) and the 27-mm bioprosthetic valve (Mosaic; Medtronic Inc) was installed into a pulsating MV simulator. Their hydrodynamic performance was evaluated and compared.

To assess the original template design (type O), we created modified templates with slight alterations for both leaflets. Two types of modified template of large leaflet (L1, L2) were made with the incarnation slightly changed from the original one (type O) (Figure 1) (Video 1). Two types of modified template of small leaflet (S1, S2) were made with the width of the leg slightly changed from the type O (Figure 2). The 4 types of valves made using these modified templates were tested to evaluate their hydrodynamic performance relative to the original design. In the large leaflet testing, each of the Normo valves were made using the type O small leaflet and in the small leaflet testing, each of the Normo valves were made using the type O large leaflet (Figures 1 and 2).

Two types of modified template with minimal alteration of large leaflet (L1, L2) with the incarnation slightly changed from the original one (type O). Pressure gradient (which means inferior function of the valve) was the greatest in type L2. In this test, each of the Normo valves were made using the type O small leaflet.

Two types of modified template of small leaflet (S1, S2) with the width of the leg slightly changed from the type O. Forward flow (which means superior function) was the greatest in type O (left) and pressure gradient was the greatest in type S2 (right). In this test, each of the Normo valves were made using the type O large leaflet.

Pulsatile Circulatory Simulator

This simulator was composed of a pneumatic driver device, an elastic left ventricle model, a left ventricle chamber, a polymer aortic valve, a compliance tank, a resistive unit, a preload tank, and a left atrium chamber (Figure 3). Mean flow waveform of the MV was measured using an electromagnetic flow sensor (ME19PXN325; Nihon Kohden) at Q. Inflow of the valve (forward flow), reverse flow while the valve was closed (regurgitation), and reverse flow after the valve was closed (leakage) were defined. The left atrium, left ventricular, and aortic pressure were measured using a pressure transducer (UK-801; Baxter) at P1, P2, and P3. The elastic left ventricular model was connected to a simulator with silicone rod, and the dynamics of the elastic left ventricular model were restricted. The Normo valve, made from bovine pericardium, was installed in the pulsatile circulatory simulator.

We constructed the pulsatile circulatory simulator incorporating with elastic left ventricular model.

Finite Element Analysis

Finite element analysis4 was conducted to evaluate the relationship between the leaflet stress and the space between 2 artificial papillary muscles (PMs) with distances set at 18 mm, 24 mm, 30 mm, and 36 mm. The 3-dimensional structure of the valve was taken when applying 10 mm Hg from the ventricle side using a micro-computed tomography. The primary tetra-element was used for finite element analysis using ADINA 9.2.3. A pressure load of 120 mm Hg was applied to each element with an increment of 6 mm Hg. Elastic modulus and poisons ratio of the valve leaflet were obtained using a uniaxial tensile test. Von Mises stress distributions of the leaflet under the conditions when the distances between the anteroposterior PMs are 18, 24, 30, and 36 mm, respectively, were analyzed considering the variation in patients.

Results

The hydrodynamic performance of the Normo valve and the bioprosthetic valve showed similar patterns (Figure 4). As for the design of the large leaflet, the valve made from type O template revealed the lowest pressure gradient, with similar mitral regurgitation compared with the other 2 valves made from modified templates (L1 and L2) (Figure 1). As for the design of the small leaflet, the valve made from the type O showed the lowest pressure gradient with the greatest forward flow. The valve made from the template of type S2 showed the smallest forward flow with the highest-pressure gradient (Figure 2). The stress on the leaflet increased for the distance between the 2 legs of the valve fixed to the 2 PMs at 18 mm and 36 mm (Figure 5).

Discussion

For those patients with unrepairable MVs who are eager to avoid MV replacement, a novel stentless MV (Normo) was developed at our institution in 2002. The function of the stentless mitral valve was then evaluated, using an original pulsatile simulator,1 after which the valve was successfully implanted in animals.5 After receiving institutional review board approval, this new type of MV operation was introduced into a multicenter clinical study in 2011 for those patients with an unrepairable MV2,3 who wanted to avoid MV replacement.

The Normo valve is composed of a large anterior leaflet and small posterior leaflet, and the commissure of the valve has a folded structure. The long legs of the valve leaflets are connected to 2 PMs, which results in a large coaptation zone.1, 2, 3 The Normo operation consists of constructing the Normo valve using fresh autologous pericardium before the initiation of cardiopulmonary bypass and implantation of the valve.3

After removing the native MV, fixation of the legs of this valve to 2 PMs is performed, after which the sewing ring (flexible ring) of the valve is sutured to the annulus. The point of fixation of the legs is determined by intraoperative measurement of the distance between the annular plane and the fixation point of the PM. In addition, adjustments of the leg length are made during the filling test. The procedure used to implant the valve is very simple and reproducible. A great advantage of this operation is that the competency of the valve can be confirmed in a saline base after its construction and before cardiac arrest. Consequently, the predictability of the results of this operation should be greater than complicated MV repair, as long as the valve is correctly made and precisely implanted. According to the recent report of this operation, intraoperative transesophageal echocardiography of all the 15 patients revealed no or only trivial mitral regurgitation. Despite excellent early results, the incidence of reoperation was high, and it was considered to have been related to the quality of the autologous pericardium.2

The Normo valve has 4 design concepts2: (1) large anterior leaflet fully covers the mitral annulus during systole; (2) the long leg of the leaflet directly connects to the 2 PMs; (3) near-normal folded structure of the commissure without any suture line; and (4) respects the natural curve of the native anterior mitral annulus.

The basic concept in developing the Normo valve was that it is structurally similar to the native human MV. The upper part of the Normo valve respects the natural curve of the native anterior mitral annulus. Deep incarnation of the large leaflet means a smaller width of the small leaflet and shallow incarnation means larger width of the small leaflet. This theory helps us to understand the validity of the original design (Figure 1).

Among 4 design concepts of the Normo valve, we believe a folded structure of the commissure without any suture line, which ensures deep coaptation area, is most important. In this study, we created modified templates with slight alterations from the original design for both leaflets and investigated the hydrodynamic function of the valves with minimal alterations. In both the large and the small leaflet, the valve made from the original design template showed the best performance and the design validity of the original template was confirmed. The data obtained from the bioengineering method confirmed the excellent performance of the Normo valve as well as the validity of the valve's design.

The finite element analysis conducted to evaluate the relationship between leaflet stress and the distance between 2 legs was very useful for the successful operation. The results demonstrated that the preferable distance between the 2 legs is 24 to 30 mm, which helps us to decide an appropriate fixation point of the legs of the valve to the PMs. The reason why we did not perform a durability testing was as follows: (1) We considered it was not a newly developed bioprosthesis but a newly developed operation or extended MV repair. This is due to the performance of this valve shown by the echocardiogram after the operation in the simulator was quite similar to the one after the MV repair. (2) We considered the valve made from bovine pericardium and the valve made from fresh autologous pericardium is quite different. The latter would be strengthened by the patient's own cell after implantation in the body.2

Our bioengineering method for the assessment of valve is considered useful, with very high reproducibility of the results being ensured. The pulsatile circulatory simulator is very useful to evaluate the function of the MV. To increase the reliability of the assessment further development of the simulator would be necessary.

Conclusions

The data obtained from the bioengineering method confirmed the validity of the Normo valve's design. Furthermore, the results showed preferable positioning for the fixation of the leg to 2 PMs.

The Normo valve made from bovine pericardium and a flexible ring has a large coaptation area that facilitates a good coaptation. In order to evaluate the hydrodynamic function and performance, the Normo valve was installed in the pulsatile mitral valve simulator developed at the Waseda University. As seen here, the valve has a annulopapillary continuity and shows similar performance to that of the native MV. Echo examination shows a large coaptation zone and excellent competency. The new type of simulator developed to mimic natural left ventricular anatomy also shows good performance of the valve.

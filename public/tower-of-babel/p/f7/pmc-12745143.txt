The role of diagnostic laparoscopy in staging distal esophageal adenocarcinoma
Authors: Stella T. Tsui, Nanruoyi Zhou, Kay See Tan, Junting Zheng, Katherine D. Gray, Smita Sihag, Valerie W. Rusch, Manjit S. Bains, Bernard J. Park, Matthew J. Bott, James Huang, Prasad S. Adusumilli, Laura H. Tang, Daniela Molena, David R. Jones
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesThe study objectives were to determine whether diagnostic laparoscopy improves staging accuracy in selected patients with distal esophageal adenocarcinoma and to identify clinicopathologic features associated with positive diagnostic laparoscopy.MethodsPatients with distal esophageal adenocarcinoma who underwent diagnostic laparoscopy from 2000 to 2023 at a single institution were identified from a prospectively maintained surgical database. Exclusion criteria included performance of diagnostic laparoscopy after systemic therapy, absence of staging positron computed tomography/computed tomography, and M1 disease before diagnostic laparoscopy. Fisher exact test and Wilcoxon rank-sum test were used to compare categorical and continuous variables, respectively, between patients with negative and positive diagnostic laparoscopy. Clinicopathologic features were assessed using multivariable logistic regression.ResultsIn total, 226 of 2131 patients with distal esophageal adenocarcinoma (11%) underwent diagnostic laparoscopy; 93% of these patients (211/226) had clinical T stage 3 or more. Most patients had clinical stage III disease before diagnostic laparoscopy (205/226 [91%]). In total, 183 patients (81%) had negative diagnostic laparoscopy, and 43 patients (19%) had positive diagnostic laparoscopy. In 40 patients (20%), disease was upstaged from III to IVB after positive diagnostic laparoscopy. Distal esophageal adenocarcinoma with signet ring feature (odds ratio, 2.45, 95% CI, 1.05-5.86; P = .040) was associated with positive diagnostic laparoscopy. Cardia involvement (P = .581), clinical T stage (P > .999), presence of clinical nodal disease (P = .550), clinical stage before diagnostic laparoscopy (P > .999), maximum standardized uptake value (P = .124), and poor differentiation without signet ring feature (P = .341) were not associated with positive diagnostic laparoscopy.ConclusionsSignet ring feature is associated with peritoneal disease. The use of diagnostic laparoscopy in these patients may lead to better staging and more appropriate treatment strategies.

Central Message

Perspective

DL with peritoneal lavage is a simple and safe procedure that can detect unexpected peritoneal disease in patients with distal esophageal adenocarcinoma. Signet ring feature is associated with peritoneal disease; therefore, such patients should undergo DL to ensure accurate staging.

The peritoneum is a common site of distant metastasis in patients with distal esophageal adenocarcinoma, accounting for 35% of metastases (60% of metastases are to the liver, and 46% are to the lung).1 In a previous analysis from our institution, 23% of patients with gastroesophageal junction (GEJ) or gastric adenocarcinoma had evidence of peritoneal disease.2 The presence of peritoneal dissemination confers poor prognosis, with the median overall survival of patients with gastric peritoneal metastases estimated to be 9.4 months on systemic therapy.3 Peritoneal metastasis also precludes curative surgical resection because of the high risk of recurrence.4,5 Extent of disease at the time of diagnosis is the most important predictor of prognosis and is a key determinant of appropriate treatment; therefore, accurate staging in these patients is critical.

Modalities commonly used to diagnose and stage esophageal adenocarcinoma include endoscopic ultrasound (EUS) and computed tomography (CT) with contrast and positron emission tomography (PET). Although these imaging modalities are adequate to detect distant metastases, they have low sensitivity for the detection of peritoneal carcinomatosis.6,7 Diagnostic laparoscopy (DL) can accurately assess intra-abdominal cancer burden in patients with GEJ and gastric adenocarcinoma.8 Results from the literature suggest that the addition of DL to conventional modalities in patients with GEJ and gastric cancer is associated with more accurate staging and can lead to changes in treatment for 17% to 21% of patients.9,10 DL has superior performance for staging of mid- and distal esophageal cancer staging than CT alone or CT plus EUS.11 The addition of peritoneal lavage with DL was associated with more accurate detection of occult peritoneal carcinomatosis in up to 25% to 31% of the patients with GEJ and gastric cancer.12, 13, 14

Although the routine use of DL with cytologic assessment in patients with clinical T stage 1b or greater gastric cancer is well established,15 few studies have investigated when DL should be used for patients with distal esophageal adenocarcinoma. To address this gap in our knowledge, we investigated clinicopathologic factors associated with positive DL in patients with distal esophageal adenocarcinoma.

Patients and Methods

This study was approved by our Institutional Review Board (#16-1631, 11/12/2023). The need for informed consent was waived, given the minimal risk to participants. We retrospectively reviewed our prospectively maintained surgical database to identify patients with histologically proven distal esophageal adenocarcinoma who underwent DL from January 2000 to December 2023. Distal esophageal cancer was classified in accordance with the American Joint Committee on Cancer 8th edition, that is, tumors involving the GEJ with their epicenter within the proximal 2 cm of the cardia (Siewert types I/II) were to be staged as esophageal cancer.16 Patients with proximal, midesophageal cancer, and esophageal squamous cell carcinoma were excluded because of their relatively low risk of peritoneal metastases.1,17, 18, 19 Additional exclusion criteria included initial DL performed after systemic therapy, absence of staging PET/CT, presence of clinical M1 disease, and signet ring feature not found on initial primary tumor biopsy. Our current clinical practice is to perform DL in patients with locally advanced distal esophageal adenocarcinoma with any cardia extension or with the presence of signet ring feature. Patient demographic characteristics, results of initial upper endoscopy (including primary tumor location), clinical staging before DL (American Joint Committee on Cancer 8th edition), results of DL, and pathologic results were collected and analyzed.20 In addition, perioperative and postoperative complications, treatment information, and treatment-related variables were collected. The clinical presence of dysphagia indicates clinical T stage 3 or greater; therefore, for patients without EUS, clinical T stage was reported to be 3 or more.21 For patients who underwent EUS, T stage was determined by EUS results. Clinical nodal staging was determined by PET/CT or by EUS with lymph node biopsy.

DL was performed in the following standardized fashion using a 2- or 3-port technique. The peritoneal cavity was systematically inspected for obvious peritoneal extension of disease, starting with general evaluation of the peritoneal surface and followed by assessment of the pelvis, paracolic gutter, and liver surfaces. The primary tumor site was also inspected. Any suspicious lesions were biopsied. If peritoneal lavage was performed during procedure, up to 200 mL of normal saline was instilled and aspirated in the peritoneal cavity of the right subphrenic space, the left upper quadrant, and the pelvis sequentially. A positive peritoneal cytology result was defined as the presence of atypical suspicious cells or a positive result for adenocarcinoma by cytologic assessment. DL was considered to be positive on the basis of biopsy-proven peritoneal disease, positive peritoneal cytology result, or liver metastasis.

Baseline patient characteristics were summarized using medians (interquartile ranges) for continuous variables and numbers (percentages) for categorical variables. Fisher exact test was used to compare differences in categorical variables; the Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used to compare differences in continuous variables. Patients with signet ring feature on pathologic analysis were excluded from the analysis of histologic differentiation because of the positive correlation between signet ring cell adenocarcinoma and poor differentiation.22 Logistic regression was used to analyze multivariable associations between variables that were statistically significant on univariable analysis and outcomes of DL. Statistical analyses were performed using R (version 4.3.3, R Core Development Team).

Results

In total, 2131 patients were diagnosed with distal esophageal adenocarcinoma at our institution from January 2000 to December 2023. Of these, 284 (13%) met the inclusion criteria. After the exclusion of patients with initial DL performed after systemic therapy (n = 34), absence of PET/CT (n = 4), imaging evidence of peritoneal carcinomatosis before DL (n = 7), clinical nonperitoneal M1 disease (n = 9), and signet ring features not found on initial primary tumor biopsy (n = 4), 226 patients (11%) were included in the analysis. Most patients were men (199/226 [88%]) and White (192/226 [85%]). Most patients had clinical T stage 3 or greater (211/226 [93%]), clinical N stage 1 or greater (175/226 [77%]), and clinical stage III disease before DL (205/226 [91%]) (Table 1). Of the 43 patients with positive DL, 13 (30%) underwent restaging laparoscopy after findings of good radiographic response to systemic therapy on restaging imaging. Of these 13 patients, 11 (85%) had negative restaging laparoscopy, and 2 (15%) had positive restaging laparoscopy. All patients with negative restaging laparoscopy had no evidence of peritoneal disease, including negative peritoneal cytology results. On initial pretreatment DL, 5 of these 11 patients (45%) had positive peritoneal cytology results but no evidence of peritoneal disease (Figure 2); 4 (36%) had signet ring feature, and 7 (64%) did not. Furthermore, 10 of these 11 patients (91%) with negative restaging laparoscopy underwent salvage esophagectomy. Both patients with positive restaging laparoscopies (n = 2) had visible peritoneal disease, positive peritoneal cytology result on initial pretreatment DL, and signet ring feature (Figure 2).Figure 2CONSORT diagram showing the results of DL for 14 patients with distal esophageal adenocarcinoma with an initial positive staging laparoscopy.

Discussion

Accurate staging of patients with distal esophageal adenocarcinoma who are potential candidates for curative-intent surgery is critical (Figure 3). To our knowledge, this is the first study to examine clinicopathologic characteristics that are associated with positive DL in patients with distal esophageal adenocarcinoma. In our study, distal esophageal adenocarcinoma with signet ring features was associated with positive DL. Our findings can help thoracic surgeons identify which patients should undergo DL before multimodality treatment.Figure 3Design and results of the study.

Our study affirms the benefit of adding DL to staging of distal esophageal adenocarcinoma. Studies have shown that the use of DL improved staging accuracy and led to changes in treatment planning in 17% to 21% of patients with esophageal cancer.9, 10, 11,23 Similarly in our cohort, 20% of patients with clinical stage III disease before DL who underwent DL had their disease upstaged to stage IVB after positive DL and had their treatment plans changed from neoadjuvant systemic therapy with surgery to palliative systemic therapy. In a study of patients with esophageal and gastric cancer who underwent DL, the addition of peritoneal lavage to DL further improved staging: Peritoneal metastasis was detected in an additional 3% of patients by the use of DL with peritoneal cytology.13 In our study, the addition of peritoneal lavage to DL resulted in the detection of peritoneal disease in an additional 7% of patients with distal esophageal adenocarcinoma (15/214). This finding demonstrates that DL with peritoneal lavage can identify occult, sub-radiographic metastases in patients with distal esophageal adenocarcinoma. Furthermore, this demonstrates the critical role that DL can play in identifying candidates for curative-intent surgery among patients with clinical stage III disease. As patients with evidence of peritoneal disease on DL are unlikely to benefit from resection, forgoing surgery can spare these patients the unnecessary morbidity of esophagectomy and reduce the time to initiation of systemic therapy.

Our results reinforce the association between signet ring feature and risk of peritoneal metastasis. Signet ring feature is associated with poor prognosis in esophageal adenocarcinoma, possibly related to its association with higher rates of resistance to chemoradiation and higher rates of incomplete resection.24,25 Signet ring feature was associated with a high risk of peritoneal disease in our study cohort, as has been reported in other studies on GEJ, gastric, and colorectal cancer.1,26,27 Of note, among the patients who underwent restaging DL in our study, signet ring feature was more common in patients who had positive restaging DL than in patients who had negative restaging DL (100% vs 36%). The propensity for signet ring cell carcinoma to metastasize to the peritoneum has been due to the organ-specific tropism of circulating signet ring cells.26 Among Siewert II GEJ adenocarcinomas, poor differentiation, signet ring feature, clinical T stage 3 or more, and distal extension of the tumor 4 cm or more were associated with the risk of peritoneal metastasis.27 Similarly, in our study, signet ring feature was associated with positive DL; poor differentiation and clinical T stage 3 or more were not. It is important to note that in our study, patients with signet ring feature were not included in our histologic differentiation analysis on the basis that poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma with signet ring feature and poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma without signet ring feature are distinct histologic profiles. Other potential risk factors for synchronous peritoneal metastases in patients with gastric cancer are T4 stage and 1 or more metastatic location.28 In our study, clinical nonperitoneal M1 disease was associated with positive DL. Although clinical T stage was not associated with positive DL, this lack of association may be due to the preselection of patients with clinical T stage 3 or more to undergo DL at our institution. DL can play an important role in the evaluation of patients with distal esophageal adenocarcinoma who have signet ring cell feature, with clinically locally advanced disease, and for whom multimodality therapy is being considered.

Although gross peritoneal carcinomatosis and positive peritoneal cytology result both represent M1 disease and patients with these findings have a poor prognosis,2,5,29 patients with positive peritoneal cytology result have longer overall survival than patients with gross peritoneal carcinomatosis, most likely because of the difference in disease burden.30 A conversion from a positive peritoneal cytology result before systemic therapy to a negative peritoneal cytology result after systemic therapy is associated with longer survival.2,31 Such patients might still benefit from surgical resection.32 In our study cohort, approximately half of patients with negative restaging DL had only a positive peritoneal cytology result on initial pretreatment DL, whereas the 2 patients with a positive restaging DL had gross peritoneal disease on initial pretreatment DL. In total, 91% of patients whose DL results changed from positive before treatment to negative after treatment underwent salvage surgery. This suggests that restaging DL should be considered in patients with initial positive peritoneal cytology results only when there is a robust radiographic response to systemic therapy.

Limitations

The limitations of this study include the retrospective nature of the study design, which restricted the variables, such as the reason DL was performed and the extent of cardia involvement, that could be included in our analyses. Overall, 43% of patients did not undergo EUS for T staging because the clinical presence of dysphagia is highly predictive of T3-4 disease and there is no difference in treatment of T3 and T4 locally advanced distal esophageal adenocarcinoma.20 Therefore, our study was not able to evaluate whether clinical T4 disease was associated with positive DL. Because of our institution's current clinical practice to perform DL in patients with locally advanced disease, clinical T3-4 distal esophageal tumors with any cardia extension on imaging, or presence of signet ring or diffuse-type histologic profiles, our study cohort represents a selected patient population. Because treatments and staging systems have changed over time, our cohort is heterogeneous, and the majority of patients underwent DL relatively recently. The lack of association between some potential risk factors and positive DL may be due to selection bias and small sample sizes. Finally, this is a single-institution study; therefore, our results may not be generalizable.

Conclusions

DL is a simple and safe procedure that can detect unexpected peritoneal disease. Our results reaffirm the importance of DL with the addition of peritoneal lavage for staging in patients with distal esophageal adenocarcinoma. DL should be performed in conjunction with conventional staging methods in selected patients. Our results show that signet ring feature is associated with peritoneal disease; therefore, the use of DL in this group of patients may lead to better staging and the use of more appropriate treatment strategies.

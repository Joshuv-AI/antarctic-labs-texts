Dual inhibition of ATR and PARP reverses acquired PARP inhibitor resistance in triple negative breast cancer
Authors: Gamze Guney Eskiler, Ayten Haciefendi, Kayhan Ozdemir, Cemil Bilir
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPoly(ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitors (PARPi) have attracted significant attention in triple negative breast cancer (TNBC) treatment. However, the acquired or de novo PARP inhibitor resistance limits treatment success. Ataxia telangiectasia and Rad3-related (ATR) regulates genome integrity, and thus, the aberrant activation of ATR could play a significant role in the pathogenesis of TNBC and be associated with PARPi resistance in especially homologous recombinant deficiency tumors. We aimed to assess the efficacy of ATR and PARP inhibitors combination in TNBC cells and the reversal of PARPi resistance in resistant cells. HCC1937 and HCC1937-R Talazoparib (TAL) resistant cells were treated with Elimusertib (ELI) alone as ATR inhibitor (ATRi) and ELI and TAL combination. Then, the cytotoxic, apoptotic and ATR based DNA damage response (DDR) were evaluated by WST-1, Annexin V, AO/PI, cell cycle and western blot analysis. Our results showed that the ELI and TAL combination could overcome TAL resistance by downregulating cell cycle checkpoint proteins and ATR-based DDR pathways through synergistic effects (ZIP score > 10). The overexpression of ATR and associated cell cycle proteins could play a role in PARPi resistance. However, this combination did not exert synergism in TNBC cells despite a higher apoptotic rate and increased DNA damage compared with the drug alone. Therefore, the dual targeting of ATR and PARP is a promising modality to reverse PARPi resistance with the downregulation of ATR-Chk1 based DNA damage response. However, further preclinical and clinical investigations should be required to elucidate the underlying molecular mechanisms behind ATRi and PARPi interactions in TNBC cells.Keywords: Triple negative breast cancer cells, PARP inhibitors, ATR inhibitors, PARP inhibitor resistance

Introduction

Triple negative breast cancer (TNBC) does not express the estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) with high recurrence rates and poor prognosis [1, 2]. In the United States in 2024, the estimated numbers of new invasive cancer cases will be nearly 2,001,140 and there will be approximately 310,750 new cases of breast cancer in women with estimated the numbers of 42,250 death [3]. Therefore, diagnostic and prognostics biomarkers should be identified to provide personal targeted therapy in these patient. The development of TNBC is closely associated with higher prevalence (nearly 10–20%) of germline or somatic BRCA mutations. Especially, germline pathogenic mutations in BRCA1/2 are observed in about 5–10% of breast cancer cases [4, 5]. In this context, the identification of molecular biomarkers in early detection or treatment response of TNBC can provide better outcome, increased overall survival before the onset of metastatic disease and decreasing in heterogeneity of this tumors [6].

BRCA1/2 proteins play a crucial role in homologous recombination (HR) repair in the cells, and thus, mutations in these genes results in defective DNA repair [7]. PARP inhibitors (PARPi) have been developed to target these defects for providing synthetic lethality in TNBC patients. Among them, olaparib and talazoparib (TAL) have been approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for BRCA-mutated metastatic advanced breast cancer [7–9]. In our previous study, TAL exerted potential therapeutic effects on BRCA1 mutant HCC1937 and wild-type MDA-MB-231 TNBC cell lines. Additionally, HR deficient TNBC cells were more sensitive to TAL compared with HR proficient cells due to synthetic lethality [10]. However, some mechanisms, such as the reactivation of HR, deficiency in PARP trapping, multi-drug resistance mechanisms, etc., lead to PARPi resistance and limit treatment success [11–15]. Therefore, there is a need for effective strategies to overcome PARPi resistance.

The DNA damage response (DDR) signaling pathway is activated following DNA breaks and mediates various cellular responses, including DNA repair, transcriptional regulation, cell cycle arrest, and apoptosis [16]. Loss of DNA damage response is a common feature of TNBC tumors and can result in the activity of alternative DNA repair pathways due to increased cellular DNA damage and resistance to DNA-damaging agents [17]. Furthermore, the presence of TP53 mutations is observed in more than half of TNBC cells and leads to an ineffective G1/S checkpoint, and these tumors depend more on G2/M cell cycle phase regulators, including ATR, CHK1, and WEE1 [18, 19]. Therefore, ATR-based DDR plays a crucial role in the pathogenesis of TNBC due to oncogene-induced replication stress, deficiencies in HR mechanisms, and the loss of the G1 checkpoint upon DNA damage [17–19]. Ataxia telangiectasia and Rad3-related (ATR) protein kinase mediates the DDR and cell cycle checkpoints through phosphorylation of checkpoint kinase 1 (Chk1). In this context, the inhibition of ATR by ATR inhibitors (ATRi) results in the collapse of the replication fork and G2/M checkpoint loss, contributing to mitotic catastrophe and programmed cell death [20]. Elimusertib (ELI), an ATR kinase inhibitor, exerts high antitumor activity as a monotherapy in preclinical studies for various cancer types, including prostate, colorectal, and lymphoma with DNA damage response deficiencies or oncogenic mutations [21, 22]. In our previous studies, ELI effectively suppresses ATR-dependent DNA damage response in TP53 mutant MDA-MB-231 TNBC cells [23]. Therefore, ATR inhibition is a potential novel treatment modality for TNBC patients.

In this context, we, for the first time, determined the therapeutic efficacy of ELI and TAL combination in BRCA1 mutant HCC1937 TNBC cells to provide the dual inhibition of two DNA damage-associated mechanisms described above. We also assessed the potential role of ATR inhibition alone and in the combination with TAL to assess the reversal of TAL resistance.

Materials and methods

Cell culture

TNBC cells were cultured in RPMI containing 10% heat-inactivated fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Gibco, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (Gibco, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) and cell lines were incubated at humidified 37 °C incubator with 5% CO2. HCC1937 cells were obtained from ATCC (USA). The cells were counted using Countless II Cell Counter (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). HCC1937-R cells were produced in our previous study [12] and had 1.6-fold resistant to 1 nM TAL for 7 days.

CCK-8 assay

HCC1937 parental and HCC1937-R resistant cells were cultured into 96-well plates (2 × 104 cells/well) and treated with 0.5 or 1 nM TAL for three day. Then, the cells were further treated with ELI at different concentrations (2, 4, 6 and 8 nM) and durations (24 h, 48 h and 72 h) in combination with TAL. After treatment, the plate was incubated with CCK-8 dye for 45 min at 37 °C. Subsequently, the cells viability value were examined at 450 nm with an absorbance reader (Allsheng, China).

Annexin V analysis

HCC1937 and HCC1937-R cells were seeded into 6-well plates (5 × 105 cell/well) and treated with ELI (6 and 8 nM) alone according to CCK-8 analysis or pretreated with TAL (1 nM) for three days and then combined with ELI (6 and 8 nM) for further 72 h. After treatment, the cells were trypsinized, centrifuged at 1500 rpm for 5 min, and resuspended with 1 ml PBS. Finally, the cells were stained with Muse Annexin-V & Dead Cell reagent and assessed by Muse Cell Analyzer (Muse™ EMD Millipore Co., Hayward, CA, USA).

Cell cycle analysis

HCC1937 and HCC1937-R cells were seeded into 6-well plates (5 × 105 cells/well) and were incubated overnight. The cells were treated with ELI (6 and 8 nM) alone or pretreated with TAL (1 nM) for three day and then combined with ELI (6 and 8 nM) for further 72 h.After harvesting, the cells were fixed using 70% cold ethanol. Then, the cells were centrifuged at 1500 rpm for 5 min with PBS. Finally, the cells were stained by Muse® Cell Cycle Assay Kit (Millipore, Germany) and incubated for 30 min at room temperature. Finally, the cells were analyzed using a Muse® Cell Analyzer (MuseTM EMD Millipore Co., Hayward, CA, USA).

Acridine orange (AO)/ propidium iodide (PI) staining

To assess the impact of the combination therapy of ELI and TAL on apoptosis, the cells were seeded into 6-well plates (5 × 105 cells/well). After, the cells were treated with ELI alone or ELI and TAL combination, the cells were fixed by 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA) for 30 min. After fixation, the cells were stained with AO (100 mg/mL) and PI solution (1 mg/mL) and observed with EVOS FL Cell Imaging System (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc.).

Western blot analysis

Total protein was isolated with RIPA lysis buffer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, ABD) from cells treated with ELI (6 and 8 nM) alone or pretreated with TAL (1 nM) for three days and then combined with ELI (6 and 8 nM). Isolated total protein concentrations were determined using Qubit 4.0 Fluorometer (Invitrogen, ABD). 30 µg protein was loaded to Bolt™ 4–12%, Bis-Tris, 1.0 mm, Mini Protein Gels, and the gels ran at 200 V for 22 min in a mini gel tank system (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). After electrophoresis, the samples were transferred to nitrocellulose membrane using iBlot™ Transfer Stack and iBlot 2 gel transfer system (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Primary and secondary antibodies were prepared according to dilution ratio (mouse anti-pH2AX (Ser139) 1 µg/mL, mouse anti-ATR 1:1000, rabbit Anti-p-Chk1 (S317) 1 µg/mL, rabbit cyclin B 1:1000, mouse anti-cyclin D 1:250, mouse anti-p-Rb 1:250, mouse-Anti-ACTB 1:1000) and the membranes were incubated via iBind Flex (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) with iBind Flex Card for 4 h. Following incubation, the membranes were imaged on chemoluminescence imaging system (Syngene, UK) using SuperSignal™ West Pico PLUS Chemiluminescent Substrate (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). ACTB was used as a control.

Statistical analysis

The obtained data were evaluated using GraphPad Prism 8 (GraphPad Software Inc., California San Diego, USA) and summarized as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) the post-hoc Tukey test was used for multiple comparisons. p < 0.05* and p < 0.01** were accepted as statistically significant. The synergism between ELI and TAL was evaluated by the Synergy Finder + web application and combination index (CI) was calculated using the Chou-Talalay method [24, 25].

Results

Evaluation of the combination of ATR and PARP inhibitors in TNBC cells

To determine the effect of ELI and TAL combination on HCC1937 and HCC1937-R cells, we first determined the efficacy of ELI alone on TNBC cells. While the viability rates of HCC1937 cells upon treatment with 6 and 8 nM ELI alone for 72 h significantly decreased to 75.7 ± 1.5% and 62.7 ± 1.6%, respectively, the proliferation of HCC1937-R cells considerably reduced to 73.6 ± 0.6% and 72.7 ± 2.1%, respectively (p < 0.01, Fig. 1). The IC50 concentration of ELI alone was 9.96 nM and 55.90 nM, respectively in HCC1937 and HCC1937-R cells for 72 h. Furthermore, the dual inhibition of ATR and PARP exerted synergistic effects (ZIP score > 10) on HCC1937-R cells (Fig. 2) and overcame TAL resistance. Notably, the viability of HCC1937-R cells significantly reduced to 52.3 ± 1.3% and 50.3 ± 0.6% following treatment with 1 nM TAL + 6 nM ELI and 1 nM TAL + 8 nM ELI, respectively (p < 0.01). Therefore, ELI and TAL combination could overcome TAL resistance in HCC1937-R cells. On the other hand, the ELI and TAL combination did not exert synergism in HCC1937 cells. The viability rates of HCC1937 cells were determined as 38.8 ± 3.2% and 37.5 ± 3.3% following incubation with 1 nM TAL + 6 nM ELI and 1 nM TAL + 8 nM ELI, respectively (p < 0.01; Fig. 1). The reduction in the cell viability was more prominent in HCC1937 cells treated with ELI and especially the 1 nM TAL combination compared with the drug alone. However, the dual inhibition of ELI and TAL had antagonistic effects, according to Synergy Finder + results.

The degree of combination synergy between ELI and TAL was analyzed. Our results indicated that the ZIP scores of ELI and TAL in HCC1937 and HCC1937-R cells were − 13.28 and 32.34, respectively (Fig. 2; Table 1). Additionally, all combinations exerted synergistic effects on HCC1937-R cells due to CI < 1 (Table 2). Therefore, the ELI and TAL combination was more effective than ELI or TAL alone in HCC1937-R cells and overcame TAL resistance due to marked synergism between these drugs. However, this combination had antagonism in HCC1937 cells due to ZIP score <-10.

Assessment of apoptotic cell death induced by ATR and PARP inhibitors combination

To analyze apoptotic cell death induced by ELI and TAL combination, we performed Annexin V and AO/PI staining. In HCC1937 cells (Fig. 3A and B), the total apoptotic death rate significantly increased to 69.9 ± 1.5% and 61.3 ± 0.7% in HCC1937 cells and 43.1 ± 1.6% and 53.2 ± 1.0% in HCC1937-R cells at 1 nM TAL + 6 nM ELI and 1 nM TAL + 8 nM ELI (p < 0.01), respectively. To assess apoptosis associated morphological changes in TNBC cells, we performed AO/PI staining, as summarized in Fig. 3C. ELI alone treatment induced chromatin condensation and apoptotic bodies in HCC1937 cells compared to the control group. However, the cell morphology was completely disrupted following the ELI and TAL combination in HCC1937 cells. In HCC1937-R cells, more nuclear damages as a marker of early apoptosis were observed following ELI alone treatment. However, the ELI and TAL combination induced cell membrane blebbing and damaged intercellular connections. Consequently, ELI and TAL combination caused more apoptotic death in TNBC cells. Additionally, this combination could overcome TAL resistance due to more damages compared with ELI alone. The AO/PI results were supported by Annexin V analysis.

The effects of ATR and PARP inhibitors combination on cell cycle arrest

Cell cycle analysis was performed to assess the effects of ELI and TAL combinations on DNA damage response in TNBC cells (Fig. 4). Our results showed that combining ELI with TAL differentially affected cell cycle arrest in TNBC cells. The percentage of the cells in G0/G1 phase was 70.8 ± 0.2%, 82.5 ± 0.3%, 66.1 ± 0.5% and 71.1 ± 0.4%, whereas the percentage of the cells in the G2/M phase was 18.8 ± 0.3%, 12.2 ± 0.2%, 21.0 ± 0.6% and 15.9 ± 0.3% at 6 nM ELI, 8 nM ELI, 1 nM TAL + 6 nM ELI and 1 nM TAL + 8 nM ELI, respectively in HCC1937 cells compared with the control (G0/G1: 60.6 ± 0.9% and G2/M: 26.0 ± 02). Furthermore, 1 nM TAL + 6 nM ELI and 1 nM TAL + 8 nM ELI combination treatment decreased the percentage of the cells in the G0/G1 arrest to 51.0 ± 0.6% and 53.0 ± 0.6%, respectively. In contrast, the rate of the cells in the G2/M phase was 32.8 ± 0.5% and 26.7 ± 0.4, respectively compared with 6 nM (26.6 ± 0.5%) and 8 nM ELI (28.3 ± 0.4%) alone. The obtained results showed that ELI and TAL combination induced G0/G1 arrest in HCC1937 parental cells in contrast to HCC1937-R cells.

The impact of ATR and PARP inhibitors combination on DNA damage response

ATR-based DNA damage response inhibition by ELI and TAL was evaluated by western blot analysis, as shown in Fig. 5. In HCC1937 cells, ELI and TAL combination induced DNA damage and thus increased protein expression of γH2AX. Additionally, ATR and p-Chk1 protein levels were suppressed by ELI alone or by ELI and TAL combination. The ELI and TAL combination reduced Cyclin D, Cyclin B, and p-Rb protein levels (Fig. 5A). In HCC1937-R cells, γH2AX protein level increased more significantly following ELI and TAL combination (Fig. 5B). Despite increased Cyclin B levels, ATR, p-Chk1, p-Rb, and cyclin D protein levels were reduced (Fig. 5B). In summary, ATR based DNA damage response associated proteins including ATR and pChk1 and cell cycle proteins including p-Rb and Cyclin D differentially expressed between HCC1937 parental and resistance cells. Therefore, the ELI and TAL combination differentially mediated the cell cycle arrest and ATR-based DNA damage response in parental and resistant cells.

Discussion

Herein, we assessed the dual therapeutic inhibition of ATR and PARP in TNBC cells and the reversal of TAL resistance. Our findings showed that ELI alone effectively inhibited TNBC cell proliferation and partially overcame TAL resistance. The ELI and TAL combination potentially reversed TAL resistance in HCC1937-R cells due to strong synergism. Furthermore, higher combination concentrations including 1 nM TAL + 6 nM ELI and 1 nM TAL + 8 nM ELI were more effective in HCC1937 cells than ELI or TAL alone. However, these combinations exhibited antagonism. Therefore, genetic and epigenetic changes, the activation of different signaling pathways and pharmacokinetic interactions could play a role in different responses of parental and resistant TNBC cells to the ELI and TAL combination. In this context, these findings suggest that the concentrations of ELI and TAL combination should be optimized according to the tumor characteristic features and this combination could provide important contributions to personalized medicine.

Additionally, ELI and TAL combination induced early apoptosis and DNA damage due to increased γH2AX protein level and suppressed ATR-based DNA damage response and G0/G1 checkpoint proteins (p-Rb and Cyclin D) compared with TAL alone treatment. However, the effects of this combination on cell cycle arrest especially Cyclin B protein level were differently observed between parental and resistant cells.

Dysregulated DDR is often observed in TNBC, and therefore, targeting of the DDR and associated pathways shows great attention for cancer treatment. ATR and ataxia-telangiectasia-mutated (ATM) regulate the DDR pathway upon DNA damage. ATR mediates S and G2/M phase transition because ATR recognizes both single-strand and double-strand breaks in DNA. While the activation of ATR phosphorylates Chk1 and then Chk1 activates CDC25a in the intra-S phase, phosphorylated Chk1 inactivates Cdc25c and induces arrest at the G2/M phase checkpoint. Furthermore, ATR regulates the HR pathway through phosphorylating BRCA1 and Chk1 [26–29]. In this context, somatic or germline mutations in BRCA1/2 genes, namely BRCAness, result in HR deficiency and, therefore, simultaneously, ATR and PARP inhibition leads to synthetic lethality in tumors. In our study, TAL alone treatment increased the protein level of p-Rb, Cyclin D, Cyclin B, and ATR. Therefore, PARPi treatment mediated the DDR for replication fork stabilization. In this framework, ELI and TAL combination suppressed G0/G1 checkpoint proteins, ATR, and p-Chk1 protein levels. Therefore, simultaneous ATR and PARP inhibition could increase replication fork instability and induce apoptosis in TNBC cells. Furthermore, increased Cyclin B levels and G2/M arrest could be associated with delaying G2 phase during mitotic entry in HCC1937-R cells. Additionally, the downregulation of Chk1 leads to a premature onset of mitosis due to aberrant activation of cyclin B-Cdc2 complex and induces apoptosis via the activation of caspases 3 and 9 and the phosphorylated γH2AX specifically at S phase [30]. Therefore, ELI and TAL combination could reverse TAL resistance in HCC1937-R cells due to delaying G2 phase or mitotic catastrophe.

Different ATR inhibitors [VX-970 (VE-822, M6620, or berzosertib), AZD6738 (ceralasertib), VX-803 (M4344 or gartisertib), BAY1895344 (ELI), and RP-3500 (camonsertib)] are currently ongoing clinical trials in solid tumors [31, 32]. ELI is especially well tolerated in four breast cancer patients with ATM deleterious mutation and/or a loss of ATM protein in a phase I clinical trial. Furthermore, ELI is effective in BRCA1 mutant cancer with developed PARPi resistance [33]. In some preclinical studies, the dual targeting of ATR by ATRi (AZD6738, RP-3500, M1774) and PARP by PARPi (AZD2281, TAL, niraparib) is a potential therapeutic strategy for the treatment of SiHa and CaSki cervical cancer cells, RNASEH2B KO cells, different ovarian cancer cells [20, 34, 35]. Particularly, ATR (AZD6738) and PARP (AZD2281) inhibitors exhibit synergy, resulting in increased DNA damage and tumor regression in de novo/acquired PARPi and platinum resistance cells and PDX models and resensitizes the acquired resistance to PARPi and platinum [20]. In a study of Smith et al. (2024), ATR (VE-821), CHK1 (PF-477736) and WEE1 (MK-1775) inhibitors induce synthetic lethality in combination with rucaparib as PARPi in a panel of HR defective and HR proficient cancer cells [35]. In the CAPRI trial, olaparib and ceralasertib combination are effective in HR-deficient platinum-sensitive recurrent high-grade serous ovarian cancer patients, and cerelasertib as ATRi resensitizes PARPi resistance [36]. Previous findings showed that TAL alone treatment did not any toxicity in HCC1937-R cells due to 3-79-fold resistance to TAL and changes in the EGF/FGF and AMPK signaling pathway [37]. In the current study, the dual inhibition of ATR and PARP synergistically inhibited the proliferation of HCC1937-R cells through apoptosis, cell cycle arrest, more nuclear damages and the downregulation of ATR based DDR pathway. Therefore, the inhibition of ATR and PARP could be a potential treatment strategy to overcome PARP resistance. Furthermore, the combination of ELI with especially 1 nM TAL treatment significantly more inhibited the viability of HCC1937 cells than the ELI alone despite antagonistic interaction and induced more apoptotic death with the downregulation of the ATR-Chk1 based pathway. In this context, further molecular studies are needed to investigate the molecular mechanisms especially including a decrease in the G0/G1 phase arrest of the cell cycle behind the antagonistic interactions between the two drugs.

This study had some limitations. Firstly, we developed one PARPi resistance cell line, and thus, further studies could be performed to verify the efficacy of ELI and TAL combination in different PARPi resistant TNBC cells. Secondly, we could not perform in vivo experiments including xenograft and PDX models. Therefore, further preclinical investigations should be performed to assess the interaction between ATR and PARPi combinations and design new treatment strategies in TNBC.

Conclusions

In summary, the ELI and TAL combination increased DNA damage and induced apoptosis by suppressing the DDR pathway in TNBC cells. Furthermore, the dual inhibition of ATR and PARP could be a potential new treatment modality for the reversal of TAL resistance due to strong synergism. However, the interaction between higher concentrations of ELI and TAL exerted antagonistic effects in HCC1937 TNBC cells. In this context, further molecular investigations associated with drug dose optimization and interactions, the activation of signaling pathways associated with the DDR and cell cycle and the genetic profile of the tumor should be performed to provide personalized medicine. Additionally, further preclinical studies especially in vivo studies are required to assess the therapeutic efficacy of ATR and PARP inhibitorscombination in TNBC and other cancer types for clinical success.

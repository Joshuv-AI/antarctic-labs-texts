Multi-objective sizing and location of DG in distribution network by hybrid gorilla troops optimization-genetic algorithm: a real case study
Authors: Abdelmalek Gacem, Mabrouka Romdhane, Ridha Kechida, Ali Kechkhouche, Mohamed Naoui, Yasser Almalaq, Mansoor Alturki, Abdullah Albaker, Ayoob Alateeq, Tawfik Guesmi, Alessandro Bosisio
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The efficient incorporation of distributed generators optimal DG in distribution networks represents a fundamental strategy for ensuring economical and efficient power system operation. The optimal DG problem can be described as a Mixed Discrete-Continuous Optimization Problem (MDCOP). In this study, a new hybrid approach combining Gorilla Troops Optimization and Genetic Algorithm, namely the Hybrid GTO-GA, is proposed. The proposed hybrid is utilized to decide the optimal sizing and location of DG units in distribution networks to reduce power losses and ameliorate the voltage profile, either individually (i.e., single-objective) or simultaneously (i.e., multi-objective). The effectiveness of the suggested hybrid GTO-GA approach is examined using 23 test functions and applied to two practical test systems of different sizes: the IEEE 33-bus distribution system and a real 143-bus system. The simulation findings display that the hybrid GTO-GA approach ensures better convergence and improved exploration of the search space compared to standalone GTO and GA algorithms. It has also proven effective in significantly reducing economic losses by minimizing power loss and voltage deviation. In the IEEE 33-bus system, yearly economic losses decreased from 91,988.14 $ to 13,773.960 $, resulting in savings of 78,214.18 $. Similarly, in the real143-bus system, losses were reduced from 177,898.08 $ to 32,401.56 $, yielding savings of 145,496.52 $.

Introduction

Nowadays, the rapid advancement of industrial sectors has led to a significant increase in electric power demand. As a result, some transmission lines in the system are subjected to overloading, increased power losses, and voltage instability. Therefore, power system operators must enhance their energy management and control strategies to achieve more economical and efficient operation without compromising the quality or continuity of service for end users1,2.

The efficient incorporation of distributed generators DGs in the distribution system is considered one of the most efficient strategies for ensuring economical and reliable power system operation. The optimal DG problem can be formulated as the process of identifying the best sizing and location of DGs units in the distribution network for minimizing power losses and voltage deviation, either individually (i.e., single-objective) or simultaneously (i.e., multi-objective), while meeting system demand and respecting a set of operational constraints3. Additionally, the optimal DG problem is described as a Mixed-Variables Optimization Problem (MVOP) due to the hybrid nature of the search space, which includes both continuous and discrete variables. This complexity presents a significant challenge for most optimization techniques.

In the literature, many researchers have addressed the optimal sizing and location of DG units in distribution systems to minimize power losses and voltage deviation, for example, Helal et al. used the traditional Genetic Algorithm (GA), but they faced limitations in resolving the optimization problem and in converging towards the global optimum4. Later studies introduced alternative algorithms such as the Backtracking Search Optimization Algorithm (BSO)5, Ant Lion Optimization Algorithm (ALO)6, Artificial Bee Colony Algorithm (ABC)7, Bat Optimization Algorithm (BOA)8, Cuckoo Search Optimization Algorithm (CS)9, Chaotic Differential Evolution Approach (CDE)10. While these algorithms demonstrated improved performance, they still struggled to effectively handle the mixed-variable optimization problem (MVOP). To overcome these issues, more recent studies have adopted advanced optimization algorithms. Like the Enhanced Search Group Algorithm (ESGA)11, the Improved Equilibrium Optimizer (IEO)12, the Novel Artificial Hummingbird Algorithm (NAHA)13, the Improved Wild Horse Optimization Algorithm (IWHO)14, and the Continuous Discrete form of the Genetic Algorithm15. In addition, hybrid strategies have been proposed, including the hybrid Crow Search–Particle Swarm Algorithm (CSPSO)16, The TSA/SCA hybrid, combining the Tunicate Swarm Algorithm and the Sine Cosine Algorithm17, the hybrid Grey Wolf Optimizer–Sine Cosine Algorithm (GWO-SCA)18, and Particle Swarm Optimization combine with Improved Grey Wolf Optimization (I-GWOPSO)19, these hybrid algorithms aim to ensure a balance between exploitation and exploration of solutions in the search space. However, effective hybridization requires a careful understanding of the strengths and limitations of the algorithms used.

In this paper, an innovative hybrid algorithm combining Gorilla Troops Optimization (GTO) and Genetic Algorithm (GA), namely the Hybrid GTO-GA, is proposed. This approach is designed to efficiently solve mixed-variable optimization problems, simultaneously handling discrete decisions (DG locations) and continuous values (DG sizing), which pose a challenge for traditional standalone algorithms. Algorithms designed for discrete variables often perform poorly in continuous spaces, and vice versa, a limitation that highlights the need for a hybrid algorithm.

The idea behind this hybridization is to combine the advantages of both algorithms. GA is well-suited for discrete and categorical variables due to its flexible encoding and genetic operators, while GTO effectively addresses continuous variables through exploration and exploitation strategies inspired by gorilla social behavior. By leveraging these complementary strengths, the hybrid GTO-GA achieves balanced and effective adaptation in mixed-variable search spaces, surpassing the capabilities of each algorithm individually.

The performance of the hybrid GTO-GA approach is tested using 23 benchmark functions, including unimodal, multimodal, and composite types. Furthermore, the algorithm is applied to determine the best sizing and location of DGs functioning under different power factor conditions, with the objective of reducing power losses and voltage deviation, either individually (i.e., single-objective) or simultaneously (i.e., multi-objective). Two practical test systems of different sizes are considered: the IEEE 33-bus system, which is widely applied in previous studies, and a newly proposed real 143-bus distribution system. The results demonstrate that the hybrid GTO-GA provides better convergence and solution quality compared to the standalone GTO, classical GA, and several other algorithms reported in the literature.

Gorilla troops optimizer

The GTO is a nature-inspired optimization approach introduced in 202129. It mimics the social behaviors that are distinguished by a hierarchical organization led by a controlling adult male called the “silverback gorilla”. It is the most dominant member of the troop and is responsible for directing activities and overseeing all decisions within the group, while the other, less dominant gorillas assist in executing various objectives. The GTO approach is founded on the principles of exploitation and exploration to find optimal solutions. For clarity, the main phases of the algorithm are described below.

Hybrid GTO-GA approach

In this work, a novel approach combining Gorilla Troops Optimization (GTO) and the Genetic Algorithm (GA), namely the Hybrid GTO-GA, is presented to solve discrete–continuous optimization problems. As summarized in Table 1, which highlights the main features of both GTO and GA that justify their selection for hybridization, this framework is particularly suitable for optimization problems involving both continuous and discrete variables. GTO demonstrates strong performance in continuous search spaces due to its adaptive strategies inspired by the social behavior of gorillas. On the other hand, GA excels at handling discrete and categorical variables through its genetic encoding and evolutionary operations, enabling efficient exploration of discrete solution spaces. By combining the complementary strengths of both algorithms, the Hybrid GTO-GA effectively adapts to mixed search spaces, achieving a balanced and efficient optimization process.

In the GTO-GA algorithm, computation is performed in a dual manner: the GA is responsible for processing discrete variables, while the GTO algorithm handles continuous variables. In each iteration of the proposed hybrid, continuous and discrete variables are combined to form complete solutions for evaluation. These solutions are then decomposed again to further refine them and guide the search toward the global optimum based on their fitness values. The optimization process continues until the stopping criteria are met. To illustrate the main steps of the proposed hybrid GTO-GA approach, the structural framework is presented in Fig. 3.

Simulation and results

This research was intended to determine the best sizing and location of DG within electrical network systems to reduce both power losses and voltage deviations, either individually or simultaneously, while satisfying system constraints. To achieve this, the proposed hybrid GTO-GA approach is utilized to effectively resolve this optimization issue.

The simulations were carried out in MATLAB environment on a system with an Intel(R) Core (TM) i7-1255U processor (1.70 GHz), 16 GB of RAM.

This section analyzes two practical test systems of different sizes: the commonly used IEEE 33-bus network and a real 143-bus network.

IEEE 33-bus distribution network: This distribution system includes 33 buses and 37 branches, supplying a total load of 3.72 MW and 2.3 Mvar. The single line of this system is illustrated in Fig. 5, while detailed branch and load data can be found in Ref33. Moreover, the developed hybrid GTO-GA approach is tested on four distinct case studies, as explained in the following subsections.

• Case 1

• Benchmark function evaluation: The CS-GTO approach was tested on 23 benchmark functions from34, with its best, average, and standard deviation results, and contrasted with other recent optimization approaches.

• Case 2

• Single-objective power loss reduction: This case aims to reduce total power losses through the optimization of the sizing and location of DG operating at different power factors through the GTO-GA approach. The results are then evaluated against previously published studies.

• Case 3

• Single-objective voltage deviation: The GTO-GA approach is utilised to identify DG sizing and location under different power factors to minimize voltage deviation, with results compared to other algorithms from previous studies.

• Case 4

• Multi-objective reduction of power loss and voltage deviation: This case aims to minimize both power losses and voltage deviation simultaneously by optimizing DG size and location under different power factors using the GTO-GA approach, with results compared to existing optimization methods.

Case 1: Testing the proposed GTO-GA approach

To validate the reliability of the results, the GTO-GA algorithm was examined on a set of 23 widely used benchmark functions. To mitigate the effects of randomness and ensure consistency, each function was executed over 10 independent runs. Three performance metrics were calculated for each function: the best solution achieved, the average solution, and the standard deviation. The results of the GTO-GA method were compared against several established optimization techniques, including Gorilla Troops Optimization (GTO), Genetic Algorithm (GA), Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy (CMA-ES), Coyote Optimization Algorithm (COA), and Ant Lion Optimization (ALO).

These functions are divided into three main categories based on the characteristics of their landscapes. Unimodal functions (F1–F7) feature a single peak and a single valley, making them particularly useful for assessing the algorithm’s exploitation ability and its efficiency in converging toward the global optimum. Table 3 summarizes the comparative results of the GTO-GA algorithm against other optimization methods, including GTO, GA, CMA-ES, COA, and ALO. The outcomes clearly highlight the outperformance of the suggested hybrid approach, demonstrating its effectiveness in enhancing solution quality and achieving accurate convergence. Multimodal functions (F8–F13) present greater complexity because they include multiple local optima along with a single global optimum. The results indicate that the GTO-GA algorithm has strong exploration ability and can effectively avoid getting stuck in local solutions. Table 4 shows that the suggested approach consistently outperforms GTO, GA, CMA-ES, COA, and ALO, confirming its robustness and efficiency in handling complex optimization problems.

In addition, Table 5 shows the results for the functions (F14–F23), which incorporate the features of both types of previous functions; these functions provide a comprehensive assessment of the algorithm’s adaptability and robustness. Conclusion and future work

Conclusion

This work suggested a new hybrid approach, combining Gorilla Troops Optimization (GTO) and the Genetic Algorithm (GA), referred to as GTO-GA, to determine the global best sizing and location of distributed generators (DG) in electrical distribution networks under various operating conditions. The primary objective is to minimize both power loss and voltage deviation, either individually (single-objective) or simultaneously (multi-objective). Simulation results based on 23 test functions, a 33-bus system, and the real 143-bus system demonstrate that the proposed algorithm achieves strong performance. In the first test network, power loss was reduced by up to 94.47%, and voltage deviation was improved by 99.82%. The most sensitive buses in this network were identified as buses 13, 14, 24, and 30, which are crucial for targeted interventions and enhancing system reliability. Similarly, in the second test network, power loss was reduced by 93.79%, while voltage deviation improved by 99.93%. Overall, the findings confirm that the proposed GTO-GA algorithm is a robust and reliable tool for addressing complex optimization problems in distribution networks, including those with mixed variables.

Future work

In the future, it is proposed to apply the proposed algorithm to determine the optimal size and locations of distribution generators based on renewable energy sources, such as photovoltaic power plants, wind farms, and green hydrogen plants, due to the increasing importance of these facilities in modern power systems. Furthermore, when designing hybrid algorithms, careful attention should be paid to the integration points between the constituent algorithms, taking into account key considerations such as adaptability to variable conditions, structural compatibility for integration, selection of an appropriate integration mechanism, and reduction of computational complexity, to ensure maximum benefit from the characteristics of each algorithm and achieve high efficiency and effective performance in the search and optimization processes.

Reconciling modest median progression-free survival and larger overall survival gains in platinum-resistant ovarian cancer
Authors: Hyunji Lim, Se Ik Kim, Jae-Weon Kim, Dong Hoon Suh
Published: 2026

In The Lancet, the investigators of KEYNOTE-B96 [1] and ROSELLA [2] reported results of phase III randomized clinical trials in platinum-resistant ovarian cancer in which both progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) were significantly improved, with the gain in median OS being numerically much larger than the more modest gain in median PFS.

In KEYNOTE-B96 (programmed death ligand-1 combined positive score ≥1 population) [1], the difference in objective response rate (ORR) between the pembrolizumab plus weekly paclitaxel, with or without bevacizumab, arm versus the placebo plus weekly paclitaxel, with or without bevacizumab, arm was 5.8%, and median PFS differed by approximately 1 month (8.3 vs. 7.2 months); nonetheless, the hazard ratio (HR) indicated a robust, statistically significant 28% reduction in the risk of progression (HR=0.72; 95% confidence interval [CI]=0.58–0.89; p=0.0014), and median OS improved by 4.2 months (18.2 vs. 14.0 months; HR=0.76; 95% CI=0.61–0.94; p=0.0053). Similarly, in ROSELLA [2], ORR differed by about 7% and median PFS by 1 month (6.5 vs 5.5 months), again corresponding to a statistically significant 30% reduction in the risk of progression (HR=0.70; 95% CI=0.54–0.91; p=0.0076), while median OS improved by 4.1 months (16.0 vs. 11.9 months; HR=0.65; 95% CI=0.51–0.83; p=0.0004) in the relacorilant plus nab-paclitaxel arm versus the nab-paclitaxel alone arm. In both trials, therefore, the PFS benefit was statistically robust; the feature meriting attention is the mathematical disparity between the modest difference in median PFS and the larger difference in median OS, rather than any failure of PFS as a surrogate endpoint.

These observations merit careful consideration. In platinum-resistant disease, post-progression survival is often prolonged and heterogeneous, attenuating the correlation between PFS and OS [3]. Moreover, median PFS captures only a single time point and may not reflect delayed treatment effects, non-proportional hazards, or late separation of survival curves—patterns that can emerge with immunotherapy-containing or biologically targeted regimens [4]. Biologically plausible explanations include particular tumor behaviors that cannot be illustrated by RECIST criteria alone: treatments that yield limited tumor responses but may still provide meaningful survival benefits [5]. Pembrolizumab, an immune checkpoint inhibitor, and relacorilant, a selective glucocorticoid receptor (GR) antagonist, may change the tumor microenvironment towards immune activation [6] or increase sensitivity to chemotherapy [7], persisting through subsequent therapies. In support of such a qualitative priming effect rather than a quantitative difference in post-progression treatment access, the proportion of patients who received subsequent systemic anticancer therapy in ROSELLA was similar in the 2 arms (68% in the relacorilant plus nab-paclitaxel arm versus 72% in the nab-paclitaxel alone arm), with comparable distributions of the individual subsequent agents [2]. Furthermore, relacorilant not only suppresses tumor cell plasticity via GR blockade but also preserves performance status by mitigating cancer cachexia. Specifically, relacorilant disrupts GR-mediated muscle wasting, thereby potentially extending post-progression survival in ovarian cancer [89].

Therefore, we suggest that future treatments or clinical trials conducted after progression on these agents should consider potential microenvironmental and tumor biological changes induced by prior therapy. Rather than indicating surrogate failure, these trials highlight the context dependence of PFS as an endpoint. Aligning endpoint selection with therapeutic mechanism—and complementing median analyses with milestone or restricted mean survival approaches—may better capture clinically meaningful benefit in platinum-resistant ovarian cancer.

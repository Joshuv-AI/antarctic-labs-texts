Integrated bulk and single-cell transcriptomes reveal pyroptotic signature in prognosis and therapeutic options of hepatocellular carcinoma by combining deep learning
Authors: Yang Liu, Hanlin Li, Tianyu Zeng, Yang Wang, Hongqi Zhang, Ying Wan, Zheng Shi, Renzhi Cao, Hua Tang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractAlthough some pyroptosis-related (PR) prognostic models for cancers have been reported, pyroptosis-based features have not been fully discovered at the single-cell level in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). In this study, by deeply integrating single-cell and bulk transcriptome data, we systematically investigated significance of the shared pyroptotic signature at both single-cell and bulk levels in HCC prognosis. Based on the pyroptotic signature, a robust PR risk system was constructed to quantify the prognostic risk of individual patient. To further verify capacity of the pyroptotic signature on predicting patients’ prognosis, an attention mechanism-based deep neural network classification model was constructed. The mechanisms of prognostic difference in the patients with distinct PR risk were dissected on tumor stemness, cancer pathways, transcriptional regulation, immune infiltration and cell communications. A nomogram model combining PR risk with clinicopathologic data was constructed to evaluate the prognosis of individual patients in clinic. The PR risk could also evaluate therapeutic response to neoadjuvant therapies in HCC patients. In conclusion, the constructed PR risk system enables a comprehensive assessment of tumor microenvironment characteristics, accurate prognosis prediction and rational therapeutic options in HCC.

INTRODUCTION

In 2020, primary liver cancer was ranked as the third leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide [1]. Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) was the predominant histological subtype of primary liver cancer, representing over 80% of all cases [2]. Despite some advances in diagnosis and treatment, the prognosis for HCC remains unsatisfactory, with an average 5-year survival rate of only 20%.[3]. In addition, inherent to the heterogeneous tumor microenvironment (TME) of HCC, patients hold significant diversities in resistance to adjuvant therapies [4–7]. Accordingly, it is an urgent need for new prognostic biomarkers to accurately predict the prognosis of HCC patients and facilitate the development of personalized treatment strategies.

Pyroptosis is a novel programming death mediated by gasdermins. Cleaved by some caspases or granzymes, gasdermins can release the n-terminal domain and then form pores in the cell membrane, causing release of a myriad of inflammatory factors [8]. The effect of pyroptosis on tumors is a double-edged sword, promoting or suppressing tumor development. On one hand, long-term release of a large number of inflammatory factors such as IL-1β and IL-18 creates an inflammatory microenvironment, participating in the occurrence and metastasis of tumors [9]. Besides, pyroptosis can accelerate immune evasion of malignant cells via regulating the immune microenvironment [10]. Conversely, pyroptosis enhances antitumor immunity and plays a role in tumor suppression.

With the rapid development of next-generation sequencing technology, mounting work has applied single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) technology on tumor research. Through providing pertinent information regarding each cellular characteristics within a given sample, scRNA-seq becomes an indispensable tool to dissect cell heterogeneity [11].

Although several studies have constructed pyroptosis-related (PR) prognostic models for HCC, pyroptosis feature at the single cell level remains undetermined [12, 13]. Herein, this study aimed to delineate the TME landscape of HCC based on pyroptotic signatures obtained by integrating scRNA-seq and bulk transcriptome data, in order to provide important theoretical guidance for prognostic stratification and precise treatment of HCC. Through deep learning verification, an prognostic scoring system for patients on the basis of the obtained pyroptotic signatures was constructed. To this end, we investigated the underlying mechanisms for different prognosis of distinct PR risk patients from the perspectives of immune infiltration, cell stemness, metabolic reprogramming, cancer pathways and cell communications in single-cell transcriptome or bulk transcriptome. A nomogram model was also developed for accurately predicting the survival of HCC patients, which facilitates patient-tailored therapy plans based on PR risk grades. MATERIALS AND METHODS

Data source

The HCC single-cell data set GSE149614 and GSE156625 were downloaded from the Gene Expression Omnibus database (https://www.ncbi.nlm. nih.gov/geo/), including 10 tumor samples GSE149614 and 43 tumor samples in GSE156625. The basal cell carcinoma single-cell data set GSE123813 involving patients undergoing immunotherapy was also downloaded from the GEO database. The Seurat R package (version 4.1.1) was applied on the above scRNA-seq data for further analyses [14].

The bulk RNA-seq data of patients with HCC were downloaded from the Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/) and International Cancer Genome Consortium (ICGC) database (https://dcc.icgc.org/), which had 335 and 232 HCC samples in the TCGA-LIHC cohort and LIRI-JP cohort, respectively. GSE91061, GSE78220, GSE109211 and GSE104580 were used for evaluating the response of patients with divergent PR risk to neoadjuvant therapies. All the above data sets enrolled in this work were publicly available in the TCGA, GEO and ICGC databases (Table S1).

Single-cell data preprocessing

The quality control, batch correction, cell clustering and annotation of scRNA-seq profiles were performed according to the usual routines. The detailed procedure was elaborated in Supplementary methods 1.1.

Grouping of patients and cells based on PR genes

We acquisited PR genes from the GeneCard database and screened out 105 PR genes after single-cell quality control with a correlation score greater than 1.0 (Table S2). Next, a pyroptosis score was calculated by AUCell R package (version 1.16.0) for each cell in scRNA-seq data and by gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) for each patient in TCGA cohort [15]. The high- and low-score cells as well as patients were grouped, respectively, by the median of the corresponding scores. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between the high- and low-score groups were identified using the findmarker function at the cell level and using the DESeq2 R package (version 1.34.0) at the bulk level [16]. The intersection between the DEGs of the two levels were deemed as the candidate hub pyroptoptic genes.

Construction of the PR risk scoring system

Primarily, the candidate hub pyroptoptic genes with prognostic value were obtained by univariate Cox analysis. Subsequently, prognostic hub pyroptoptic genes were further screened by least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) and support vector machine recursive feature elimination (SVM-RFE) [17]. Eventually, a pyroptoptic gene signature composed of three genes was obtained whose regression coefficients were gained by applying stepwise multivariate Cox analysis. Then, the prognostic difference of patients between the two grades was further analyzed and the model was validated in ICGC cohort.

PR risk prediction by a deep learning model

The TCGA-LIHC samples were randomly divided into a training set (70%) and a test set (30%), and an attention mechanism-based deep neural network (DNN) classification model was proposed to investigate the predictive capacity of the obtained pyroptotic gene signature on the PR risk of HCC patients. ICGC dataset was used as an independent validation set. The performances of the model in the training and validation datasets were evaluated by accuracy, sensibility, specificity and area under the ROC curve (AUC) [18]. The detailed network architecture of the DNN model was described in Supplementary methods 1.2,

Construction of a nomogram model

Combining the PR risk and clinicopathological factors, a Nomogram model was constructed to predict overall survival (OS) of HCC patients. c-Index, calibration plots and decision curve analysis (DCA) were used to assess prediction performance of the nomogram model.

Exploration of TME in discrepant PR risk grades at the single-cell level

By using genomicInstability R package, genomic instability analysis (GIA) algorithm was implemented in hepatocytes to estimate the relative likelihood of each cell to be malignant [19]. Subsequently, stemness of malignant cells was quantified via CytoTRACE package. By Gene Set Variation Analysis (GSVA), difference of 35 cancer-related pathways in malignant cells was compared between high and low PR risk grades (detailed in Supplementary methods 1.3) [20]. Single-Cell Regulatory Network Inference and Clustering (SCENIC) algorithm was performed on malignant cells for contrast of regulatory networks and cell state between high and low PR risk samples [15]. We applied iTALK package to obtain specific information of cell communication on significant cancer pathways (detailed in Supplementary methods 1.4) [21].

Exploration of TME in discrepant PR risk grades at the bulk level

To predict proportion of malignant cells in each bulk sample, BayesPrism method was executed on bulk RNA-seq data (Supplementary methods 1.5) [22]. Cell stemness at the bulk level was quantified via one-class logistic regression (OCLR) algorithm, which calculated a gene expression-based stemness index (mRNAsi) for each patient from TCGA-LIHC gene expression data [23]. Enrichment scores of cancer-related pathways were also investigated in samples from the TCGA cohort by GSVA package. The abundance of 28 immunocyte types in TME of each sample was assessed by using single sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) algorithm.

Assessment of response to neoadjuvant therapies in patients with different PR risk

We applied tumor immune dysfunction and exclusion (TIDE) algorithm to predict response of patients to immunotherapy (http://tide.dfci.harvard.edu/)[24]. Additionally, using oncoPredict package, drug (chemotherapeutic or targeted drugs) sensitivities in HCC patients with different PR risk, namely half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) for these drugs, were also predicted [25, 26].

Statistical analysis

Linux (Ubuntu 18.04.5), python (version 3.7.9) and R (version 4.1.3) program were utilized for statistical analysis. Kaplan–Meier survival curves were depicted for OS analysis by survminer R package (version 0.4.9). Wilcoxon signed-rank test was carried out for statistical comparisons between two groups. Chi-square test was used to analyze the significant differences between two categorical variables. P < 0.05 is indicated as statistical significance.

RESULTS

Grouping of cells based on PR genes

After preprocessing the data, harmony algorithm was applied to correct the batch effects on two scRNA-seq datasets (GSE156625 and GSE149614) (Figure 2A). Afterward, 112524 cells were classified into 24 clusters by using FindClusters function (resolution = 0.4). From these clusters, eight major cell types were identified via classical cell surface markers, including T cells (CD3D, CD3E, CD3G), B cells (MS4A1, CD19, CD79A), plasma cells (JCHAIN, IGHG3, MZB1), NK cells (GNLY, NKG7), myeloid cells (LYZ, CD14, C1QB), hepatic stellate cells (HSC) (ACTA2, PDGFRB), endothelial cells (VWF) and hepatocytes (TTR, APOA2, ALB) (Figure 2B and C).

The pyroptosis score was calculated for each cell using AUCell R package, and all cells were categorized into high and low pyroptotic cells according to the median score (Figure 2D). By FindMarkers function, DEGs between high and low pyroptotic cells were obtained, including 598 upregulated and 637 downregulated genes (Figure 2E; Table S4).

Stratification of HCC patients based on PR genes

In the TCGA-LIHC cohort, the pyroptosis scores were calculated by ssGSEA analysis for samples which were classified into low and high pyroptotic groups on the grounds of the median score (Figure 3A). By using DESeq2 package, we obtained DEGs between high and low pyroptotic samples, including 5326 upregulated and 9455 downregulated genes (Figure 3B; Table S5).

Construction of pyroptotic signature and PR risk scoring system in HCC

Primarily, by intersecting DEGs identified in TCGA and single-cell profile, 348 upregulated and 336 downregulated genes were obtained for subsequent analysis (Figure S1A). Then, a total of 87 prognostic genes were initially identified through univariate COX analysis (Table S6). To further obtain the hub pyroptoptic genes associated with prognosis, SVM-RFE and LASSO regression algorithms were employed to screen for potential candidates. As a result, we made intersection of seven genes selected by SVM-RFE algorithm (Figure S1B) with 10 genes chosen by LASSO regression algorithm (Figure S1C), and acquired three genes (ERGIC3, STAT4, MGMT) as the pyroptoptic gene signature (Figure 3C). Based on the expression and regression coefficient of these three genes, the PR risk scoring system was set up (Figure 3D, Figure S1D). Subsequently, the patients were graded as high and low PR risk according to the median risk score. The risk plot distribution and survival status of HCC patients demonstrated the poorer survival rates of patients in the high PR risk grade (Figure S1E). Furthermore, significant decreased STAT4 and MGMT expression while elevating ERGIC3 expression was observed within the high PR risk grade (Figure S1E). The relevance of PR risk and clinicopathologic factors was illustrated in Figure S2A (detailed in Supplementary results 2.1).

Performance of the deep learning model for PR risk prediction

To further verify the capacity of the obtained pyroptotic gene signature on predicting prognostic risk of patients, we constructed an attention-based DNN classification model on the training set TCGA-LIHC cohort. In the model, the three pyroptotic genes were taken as inputs, and the PR risk was used as the classification label (Figure 3E). The model on the training set (TCGA cohort) achieved an accuracy of 0.8529, a sensitivity of 0.8750, a specificity of 0.8333 and the AUC of 0.8542, suggesting that the three pyroptotic genes were effective biomarkers for predicting the PR risk of patients. Furthermore, on the independent validation set (ICGC cohort), the accuracy, sensitivity, specificity and AUC of the model were 0.8441, 0.7672, 0.9217 and 0.8445, respectively (Table S7; Figure S2F), corroborating the promising generalization capacity of the deep learning model.

Prognostic value of the PR risk

In both TCGA-LIHC and ICGC cohorts, patients with low PR risk significantly outlived those with high PR risk (P < 0.001, P < 0.001, log-rank test, Figure 4A and B). Multivariate Cox model in TCGA training set revealed that the PR risk was an independent predictor for prognosis (HR =2.88, P < 0.001, Figure S3A), which was well confirmed in ICGC validation set (HR =2.45, P < 0.001, Figure S3B). To quantify the impact of PR risk on prognosis of HCC patients in clinic, we combined PR risk with clinicopathologic factors to construct a nomogram model (Figure 4C). The nomogram model exhibited a C-index of 0.72, and the calibration curve demonstrated excellent concordance between the predicted and actual survival probabilities at 1-, 3- and 5-year intervals (Figure 4D–F). Notably, DCA unveiled that the net clinical benefit of the nomogram or independent PR risk models surpassed the clinical model at all three time points (Figure 4G–I). These results confirmed the reliable performance of our proposed nomogram model.

Patients with high PR risk exhibited higher malignancy than low risk

Through GIA algorithm, malignant hepatocytes were identified (Figure S4A and B; Figure 5A). Bayesian prism algorithm was applied in TCGA-LIHC data to predict proportion of malignant cells in the two PR risk grades. In both single-cell and bulk data, the percentage of malignant cells in high PR risk samples was larger than those in low risk (P < 0.01, Figure S4C; Figure 5B, Table S8, supplementary results 2.2).

By CytoTRACE and OCLR algorithm, we found stemness enhancement in high PR risk samples at the single-cell and bulk levels, respectively (P < 0.01, Figure 5C, supplementary results 2.3).

GSVA results demonstrated that most oncogenic signaling and cancer phenotype pathways were upregulated in high PR risk samples of both bulk and single-cell data (Figure S5A and B, supplementary results 2.4)

Patients with high PR risk are less immune infiltrated than low risk

We explored immunocytes infiltration in high and low PR risk samples from bulk and single-cell data, respectively. In bulk data, immune infiltration was quantified by ssGSEA algorithm. It demonstrated more immunocytes infiltration in the low PR risk samples both from TCGA-LIHC discovery cohort and LIRI-JP validation cohort, such as activated B cells, effector memory CD8+ T cells, natural killer (NK) cells (Figure 6A and B; Tables S9 and S10).

In single-cell data, to investigate more comprehensively, the T cells were reclustered and annotated (Figure S6A and B). Higher proportions of Treg, neutrophils, NK cells and effector CD8+ T cells (Teff) were found in the low PR risk samples, whereas there was no significant difference in plasma cells (Figure 6C). Thus, combing the results of immune infiltration in bulk and single-cell data, it revealed that the low PR risk samples harbored more prominent immunocytes infiltration, especially Teff and NK cells.

Differences in receptor–ligand communication of cell subpopulations between high and low PR risk samples

To further investigate the interactions between malignant cells and other components of TME, we utilized the iTalk algorithm to compare communication differences among cell types between high and low PR risk samples based on receptor–ligand expression of each cell subpopulation. The ligand–receptor pairs with the most significant enhancement of communication signals on PI3K/AKT/MTOR, and EGF pathways in the high PR risk samples compared with the low were mainly HRAS-SDC2, HRAS-AGTR1 and HRAS-CAV1 (Figure 7A and B). Nevertheless, on the nuclear beta-catenin pathway, the most prominent communication signal in high PR risk samples was CDH1-IGF1R, which was emitted by myeloid cells, NK cells, Teff cells as well as endothelial cells and acted on malignant cells, thus participating in the progression of HCC (Figure 7C). The above findings suggested that HRAS and IGF1R could be potential drug targets for the high PR risk patients. Thus, IC50s of Tipifarnib targeting HRAS and Linsitinib targeting IGF1R were investigated in the patients from TCGA-LIHC cohort. We found both IC50s of the two targeted drugs were lower in the high PR risk samples (Figure S7A), which corroborated the above findings.

Cell communication differences in cancer hallmark pathways between high and low PR risk samples (high vs. low). Differences in receptor–ligand communication on PI3K_AKT_MTOR (A), EGF (B) and nuclear beta-catenin (C) pathways between the two risk grades of samples.

Moreover, on the aerobic glycolysis pathway, GPI-AMFR was the ligand–receptor pair with the most significant enhanced communication signal in high PR risk samples (Figure S7B). The enhanced GPI signals from various types of cells mainly acted on the corresponding receptors AMFR of malignant cells, which could promote the glycolytic process of malignant cells and may accelerate the metastasis and progression of HCC.

In terms of cell cycle, TGFB1-ITGB6, TGFB1-CAV1, TGFB1-CD109 and TGFB1-ACVRL1 were ligand–receptor pairs with remarkable enhancement of communication signals in high PR risk samples, which were mainly sent by endothelial cells, NK cells and myeloid cells and received by malignant hepatocytes (Figure S7C). Notably, from the perspective of autophagy pathway, the most significantly enhanced ligand–receptor pair between malignant and endothelial cells of high PR risk samples was mainly UBA52-ERBB2 (Figure S7D).

In a word, the observed differences in cellular communication were consistent with previous findings, providing evidence for microenvironmental characteristics of distinct PR risk subgroups at a single cell level. Moreover, the identified receptor–ligand pairs offer potential targets for clinical treatment of HCC patients with high PR risk, particularly HRAS ligand and IGF1R receptor.

Patients with lower PR risk benefited more from first-line neoadjuvant therapies

Currently, neoadjuvant therapies (e.g. immune checkpoint blockades (ICBs), targeted medication, as well as transcatheter arterial chemoembolization (TACE)) play increasingly crucial role in improving the prognosis and downstaging of HCC. We initially applied TIDE algorithm to assess the potential responsiveness of TCGA-LIHC patients to ICBs. Compared to high, the low PR risk patients had significantly lower TIDE scores (P < 0.001, Figure 8A, left), and a larger proportion of responders (62% vs 42%, P < 0.001, Figure 8A, right). Furthermore, in the immunotherapy cohorts GSE91061 and GSE78220, we achieved highly compatible results with TIDE prediction, which was, the low PR risk patients gained more remarkable clinical response to ICBs (32% vs 8%, P = 0.081; 64% vs 43%, P = 0.430, Figure 8B).

In addition, PR risk scores were calculated for each malignant cell in the single-cell dataset GSE123813 using the AddModuleScore function (Figure 8C), which showed a significant increase in the PR risk of malignant cells in non-responders after ICBs treatment (P < 0.01, Figure 8D), while the PR risk of responders tended to fell after ICBs treatment (P > 0.05, Figure 8D). The opposite alteration in PR risk between responders and nonresponders after treatment indicated that PR risk has the potential to predict ICBs response. All the above results suggested that HCC patients with lower PR risk may benefit more from immunotherapy.

We further investigated the difference in response to the first-line targeted drug Sorafenib or TACE between the high and low PR risk samples. The IC50 of Sorafenib in TCGA-LIHC cohort was predicted based on GDSC database, and the results showed a significant lower IC50 in the low PR risk samples (P < 0.001, Figure 8F). In the Sorafenib-treated cohort GSE109211, the patients with low PR risk harbored more clinical benefits than the high counterparts, despite no statistically significant difference (39% versus 24%, P = 0.256, Figure 8E). Similarly, the TACE treatment cohort GSE10458 exhibited a significantly higher proportion of low PR risk score responders (63% versus 47%, P = 0.080, Figure 8G).

Taken together, these findings suggested that the patients with lower PR risk may gain greater benefit from neoadjuvant therapies.

DISCUSSION

The roles of pyroptosis in oncogenesis and progression of HCC are multifaceted. Although pyroptotic feature and its impact on the TME of HCC have been widely explored, the single-cell mechanisms for pyroptosis-associated prognosis in HCC remain undetermined. To our knowledge, this is the first attempt to deeply integrate single-cell and bulk transcriptome data for comprehensive analysis of pyroptosis characteristics in HCC.

A pyroptotic gene signature shared by single-cell and bulk transcriptome was developed by SVM-RFE and LASSO regression algorithms. On the strength of the pyroptotic signature composed of three genes, we constructed a PR risk scoring system for HCC patients, providing novel insights into precise patient stratification and therapeutic options. Two PR risk grades with divergent OS outcomes were identified.

Patients in the high PR risk grade harbored more extensive pyroptosis features and inferior survival. Next, an attention mechanism-based DNN model was established on the ground of the pyroptotic signature, and its favorable performance further confirmed effectiveness of the gene signature to be biomarkers for predicting the PR risk of HCC patients.

From the integrative perspectives of single cell and bulk transcriptomes, we unraveled higher malignancy of the high PR risk samples, which was characterized by a higher proportion of malignant cells, higher stemness, metabolic reprogramming, DNA damage and repair and activation of most carcinogenic signaling pathways. Of note, cellular communication results revealed that malignant hepatocytes interacted with other cells mainly through the signaling of HRAS-SDC2, HRAS-AGTR1 and HRAS-CAV1 from WNT, PI3K/Akt/mTOR and EGF pathways. Among them, the role of ligand HRAS was similar to that of other RAS family members, leading to tumor cell proliferation, migration and epithelial–mesenchymal transformation [27]. SDC2 receptor could promote cancer, and also participate in tumor invasion and migration [28]. Targeting receptor AGTR1 not only attenuates tumor carcinogenicity but also inhibits tumor cell growth and invasion [29, 30]. CAV1 promotes invasion and metastasis of HCC cells through Wnt/β-catenin pathway and upregulation of Pofut1 expression [31–33]. All of the above results suggest that the HRAS-associated signal communication between malignant hepatocytes and other cells is a key link in the formation of TME characteristics in high PR risk samples, and the receptor–ligand mentioned above can be used as potential drug targets.

Notably, single-cell TF analysis revealed that the mechanisms and pathways through which malignant hepatocytes exert their procarcinogenic effects vary significantly among patients with discrepant PR risk. The TOP 3 TFs of malignant hepatocytes in high PR risk samples were strongly associated with the poor prognosis. BACH2 is a key component of tumor immunosuppression mechanism by mediating the inhibition of CD8+ T cells and IFN-γ in tumors and limiting the maturation and function of NK cells [34, 35]. Another TF KDM5A participates in the occurrence and metastasis of lung tumors by upregulating expression of cell cycle proteins D1 and E1, inhibiting expression of CDKN1B, and promoting expression of ITGB1 [36]. BCL11A could promote cancer stemness and tumor progression through the downstream target genes of ISL1 and SETD8 [37–39]. However, the TOP 3 TFs in malignant hepatocytes from low PR risk samples may be involved in tumor progression through other ways. TEF, by interacting with the TEF-binding structural domains of YAP and TAZ, activated transcription of downstream genes such as CTGF and Cyr61, which promoted cell proliferation and tumorigenesis [40–42]. DMRT2 is a development-related TF. Reduced expression of DMRT2 may mediate the abnormal differentiation of renal clear cell carcinoma [43]. FOXK1 was involved in tumor progression through CCL2 expression and tumor-associated monocyte–macrophage accumulation [44]. These findings reflected the specific microenvironmental features obtained from single-cell transcriptional regulation analysis.

As for the immune infiltration, previous studies have proposed differentiation of ‘cold’ and ‘hot’ tumors. The key characteristics of ‘cold’ tumor include (1) less-differentiated cells; (2) activation of the β-catenin pathway; (3) few Th1 cells, NK cells and CD8+ T cells [45–47]. In our study, we observed significantly less infiltration of NK cells and CD8+ T cells, higher stemness and upregulated signaling of β-catenin pathway (confirmed by GSVA and cell communication analysis) in high PR risk samples from both single-cell and bulk data. Accordingly, we deem that the high PR risk tumors correspond to ‘cold’ tumors, while the low ones are similar to ‘hot’ tumors.

Building on these results, our integrated analysis of single-cell and bulk transcriptome demonstrated that pyroptosis altered the microenvironmental landscape of HCC by regulating intratumoral immune editing, metabolic reprogramming, stemness and activation of carcinogenic pathways (e.g., WNT, β-catenin pathway and PI3K/Akt/mTOR), whose intricate interactions ultimately determined worse prognosis in patients with high PR risk score. Additionally, the proposed PR risk scoring system could accurately assess the pyroptosis grade in each HCC patient, and our PR risk-based nomogram model could satisfactorily predict the survival rate of patients in a high net clinical benefit. This prognostic prediction capability was confirmed in the validation set LIRI-JP cohort.

To wind up with, patients’ response to neoadjuvant therapies could also be evaluated by their PR risk scores. In all the enrolled neoadjuvant treatment cohorts, low PR risk patients exhibited better responsiveness to Sorafenib, TACE and immunotherapy. Of particular note, the above predictive role of PR risk was further validated in the single-cell data from an immunotherapy cohort. Concomitantly, lower IC50s of Tipifarnib targeting HRAS and Linsitinib targeting IGF1R in high PR risk patients suggested higher sensitivity of these patients to the two drugs, which substantiated the cellular communication results described above. In a word, it will be recommended neoadjuvant therapies for the low PR risk patients while Tipifarnib or Linsitinib for the high PR risk patients.

Admittedly, limitations remain true in our work. The current results highlight the need for prospective clinical trials to further verify the clinical applicability of our PR prognostic model. In addition, the obtained mechanisms for poor prognosis of high PR risk patients should be further explored in wet experiments.

A Large Vulvar Acrochordon in an 80‐Year‐Old Woman: A Neglected Hanging Lesion
Authors: Vaia Sarli, Maria‐Gesthimani Chousmekeridou, Ioannis Arkoulis, Theodoros Karampitsakos, Chryssi Christodoulaki, Peter Drakakis, Sofoklis Stavros, Periklis Panagopoulos, Anastasios Potiris
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTGiant acrochordons are extremely rare in sizes bigger than 5 cm and even more extreme in postmenopausal women. Treatment of vulvar diseases can present significant challenges, particularly when dealing with older patients with co‐morbidities. Gynecological and dermatological consultations for early identification of malignant transformation or recurrence are essential.

Case Image Presentation

Acrochordons are commonly known as skin tags, fibroepithelial polyps or soft fibromas. They are benign dermatological skin lesions which are composed of fibrous tissue [1]. In general population, their incidence is approximately 25%–46% [1]. Genital acrochordons in women have a peak incidence in reproductive age and particularly between 20 and 40 years old. The manifestation of those entities is extremely rare in post‐menopausal women [1, 2]. Regarding their size, fibroepithelial stromal acrochordons are usually small and if their size exceeds 5 cm, they are characterized as giant [2].

In this case image, we present the case of a giant acrochordon. An 80‐year‐old woman—para nine gravida two, body mass index of 31, with medical history of hypertension under medication and surgical history of total abdominal hysterectomy due to enlarged fibromas and appendectomy—presented to the emergency outpatient clinic of our hospital complaining of severe pain 2 days after a biopsy of a large pedunculated vulvar mass. The aforementioned mass was 7 cm in length and 4 cm wide hanging from the left labium majus. An illustration of the pandiculated mass is presented in Figure 1. A scar of that mass was the causal factor triggering the pain. Histopathological examination, which was performed in a private facility, revealed fibroepithelial tissue with characteristic features of acrochordons, confirming the benign nature of the lesion. Upon arrival, the initial clinical examination revealed a bulky lesion, and differential diagnoses included vulvar fibromas, skin tags, and condylomata acuminata (genital warts), but these were ruled out based on clinical and histopathological findings. There were no infection signs or vaginal discharge. The pear‐like lesion was palpable, mobile, and soft. After Valsava maneuver, the cauliflower‐like polypoidal lesion did not increase in size. Her vital signs were normal. There was no pallor, icterus, edema, and no lymph node enlargement. The neck, axilla, groin, and other areas of the body had no swelling. Laboratory results were all normal, including glucose levels. There was no history of type 2 diabetes. She denied having any similar type of mass or growth in the past, had no history of sexual intercourse or sexually transmitted infections and this mass was presented 2 years ago and progressively enlarged in size. She reported no rupture of the mass or any abnormal fluid discharge.

Illustration of the pandiculated mass from the left labia majus. The lesion is marked with an asterisk (*).

In the present case image, we present a giant acrochordon in a postmenopausal woman which is exceedingly rare in the literature [1, 2]. Vulvar diseases, though often benign, can present significant challenges, particularly when dealing with older patients who may have co‐morbidities. Treatment approach for vulvar lesions varies depending on factors such as the size, location, symptoms, patient's age, and overall health condition. In younger patients, surgical excision is typically the treatment of choice. However, in older patients with underlying comorbidities the decision to excise becomes more complex. Personalized treatment that considers the patient's general health and potential complications is necessary [3]. A follow‐up with gynecological and dermatological consultations for early identification of malignant transformation or recurrence is essential.

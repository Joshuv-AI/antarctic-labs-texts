Design of absorbing material distribution for noise barrier via accelerated BEM and optimality criteria method
Authors: Ang Zhao, Guang Lei, Juan Jiang, Hui Ming, Kui Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractA BEM-based topology optimization framework, incorporating the optimality criteria (OC) algorithm, is developed for the design of sound-barrier systems with optimally distributed sound-absorbing materials. Within this framework, the acoustic effect of the absorbing medium is incorporated by means of an impedance boundary condition. Inspired by the solid isotropic material with penalization (SIMP) concept, the optimization model employs the elemental material density as the design variable, enforces a prescribed volume constraint on the total amount of absorbing material, and formulates the objective function as the minimization of the acoustic pressure evaluated on a designated reference surface. To assist the SIMP interpolation in producing a distinct binary (0–1) material pattern, a regularized Heaviside-type mapping function is introduced. The BEM is then employed to carry out the acoustic field analysis required throughout the optimization process. Furthermore, a Taylor-expansion-based accelerated BEM is incorporated to efficiently evaluate the sound pressure amplitudes over a prescribed frequency range, significantly reducing the computational cost while maintaining high accuracy.Keywords: topology optimization, boundary element method, solid isotropic material with penalization, Taylor expansion, optimality criteria, absorbing material

Introduction

Sound barriers have been widely recognized as an effective solution for reducing noise generated by transportation systems, and various advanced noise control technologies, including acoustic metamaterials and engineered structural designs, have also been extensively explored for enhanced sound insulation performance.1–4 Their acoustic behavior has been extensively investigated through various numerical and analytical methods.5–8 Among the available computational approaches, the boundary element method (BEM) has proven particularly well suited for sound-barrier acoustic analysis,9,10 due to its superior prediction accuracy and the convenience associated with boundary-only discretization. Moreover, for exterior acoustic radiation problems, the Sommerfeld radiation condition at infinity is inherently satisfied within the BEM formulation, eliminating the need for artificial truncation of the computational domain. A number of studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of BEM in sound-barrier applications. Ishizuka and Fujiwara employed BEM to investigate sound barriers with various geometric configurations and boundary conditions,11–13 and showed that the introduction of absorbing treatments at the barrier edges can significantly improve noise attenuation performance. Monazzam and Lam subsequently conducted a two-dimensional BEM analysis of sound barriers equipped with quadratic-residue diffuser caps, revealing their potential in enhancing acoustic performance. More recently, Fard proposed a quasi-periodic BEM formulation for three-dimensional sound-barrier simulations,14,15 which offers substantially improved computational efficiency compared with conventional three-dimensional BEM approaches. Motivated by these demonstrated advantages, the present work adopts the BEM as the numerical tool for acoustic analysis of sound-barrier systems.

A substantial number of studies have investigated optimization-based approaches for improving the acoustic performance of sound barriers.16–18 Early work combined two-dimensional BEM formulations with evolutionary optimization techniques to enhance the efficiency of T-shaped sound barriers equipped with well-type structures at the top surface.18–20 These studies revealed that increasing the number of wells leads to noticeable improvements in noise reduction, while the degree of enhancement is strongly dependent on frequency. Along similar lines, Toledo performed optimization of thin barrier top-edge geometries by integrating evolutionary algorithms with a dual BEM formulation, in which absorbing boundary conditions were explicitly incorporated into the optimization process. 21 Subsequent extensions of this work further examined the optimal design of roadside barrier top profiles and are reported by Toledo. 22 Rather than concentrating on the top-edge configuration, Grubeša adopted a genetic algorithm to optimize the cross-sectional geometry of sound barriers, 23 simultaneously assessing acoustic performance and economic considerations related to material selection and structural layout. Mun and Cho employed a simulated annealing strategy to optimize global barrier dimensions in order to satisfy environmental noise control requirements. In a related contribution, Greiner 24 investigated the optimal shape design of Y-type noise barriers using a multi-objective evolutionary algorithm coupled with BEM analysis, accounting for both acoustic effectiveness and construction cost.25,26 Beyond shape and dimensional optimization, Kim and Yoon 27 introduced an acoustic topology optimization framework for sound barriers composed of rigid and porous materials, employing the finite element method in conjunction with the method of moving asymptotes (MMA). Nevertheless, despite the breadth of existing research, most previous investigations have predominantly focused on optimizing barrier geometry or overall dimensions. To improve the distribution of sound-absorbing materials in noise barriers, this study proposes a BEM-based topology optimization framework to determine their optimal layout.28,29 The objective is to minimize the acoustic response in the target region under a prescribed material volume constraint by iteratively updating the design variables. The method enables boundary-only acoustic modeling via BEM, avoiding volumetric meshing and reducing computational cost. It thus provides an efficient and stable approach for broadband acoustic topology optimization.

Among the various approaches for continuum topology optimization, the solid isotropic material with penalization (SIMP) method remains one of the most widely used techniques due to its simple formulation and high computational efficiency.30,31 Consequently, SIMP has been extended to diverse optimization problems, including bi-material formulations for minimizing sound radiation from vibrating structures 32 and multi-material interpolation schemes addressing multiple material phases. 33 The core idea of SIMP is to represent discrete material states through continuous density variables while penalizing intermediate densities; however, penalization alone is insufficient to completely eliminate gray regions, which has motivated numerous gray-scale suppression strategies. Representative efforts include min-operator-based Heaviside projections, 34 volume-preserving density filters combining classical and modified Heaviside functions, 35 gray-scale suppression operators integrated with SIMP, 36 SIGMOID-based interpolation models, 37 and heuristic gray-element reduction schemes. 38 Building on these developments, the present study incorporates a modified operator derived from a smoothed Heaviside function 39 into the SIMP framework to promote a distinct 0–1 material distribution, driving density values below and above 0.5 toward 0 and 1, respectively, and thereby producing a significantly sharper material layout.

In the present work, the original discrete topology optimization problem is reformulated as a nonlinear continuous optimization model through a SIMP-based material interpolation scheme. To achieve a clear and physically meaningful material layout, a density-mapping operator is introduced to sharpen the material distribution and effectively suppress intermediate density values, thereby enforcing a near-binary (0–1) design within the SIMP framework. The resulting optimization problem is solved using a relaxed form of the optimality criteria (OC) algorithm, which is particularly efficient for large-scale optimization problems involving a large number of design variables and a single active constraint. A relaxed OC strategy was previously proposed by Shang and Zhao 40 for an acoustic–structural coupled bi-material optimization problem, where numerical results demonstrated that the OC-based approach requires significantly less computational effort than the method of moving asymptotes (MMA). Motivated by these advantages, the relaxed OC formulation is adopted in this study as the design-variable updating scheme. In addition, to further improve computational efficiency in the acoustic analysis, a Taylor-expansion-based accelerated boundary element method is employed to evaluate sound pressure amplitudes,41–45 enabling rapid frequency-domain simulations while substantially reducing the overall computational cost of the optimization process.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 presents the fundamental theories of the boundary element method (BEM) and the Taylor expansion approach, where the acoustic effects of the absorbing materials are incorporated through impedance boundary conditions. Section 3 describes the complete topology optimization framework, including the formulation of the optimization problem, the material interpolation strategy, and the update scheme for the design variables based on the optimality criteria (OC) method. Section 4 verifies the effectiveness of the proposed optimization method and the gray-element elimination strategy through numerical analyses of a two-dimensional dome-shaped noise barrier model. Finally, Section 5 summarizes the main findings and conclusions of this study.

To clarify the computational procedure, the proposed method follows a consistent frequency-sweep pipeline. At each frequency or each Taylor-expansion sub-interval, the BEM-discretized system is assembled and solved using the GMRES iterative solver to obtain the boundary acoustic field. To improve efficiency in wideband analysis, the ROM/SOAR framework is employed. Specifically, the frequency-independent components of the system matrices, such as geometry-dependent boundary operators and projection bases, are constructed in an offline stage and reused across all frequencies. Only the frequency-dependent terms, primarily the wave-number-dependent kernel contributions, are updated online. Therefore, the computational cost at each frequency is dominated by the reduced-order GMRES solve, while repeated full reassembly of the system matrices is avoided.

Numerical tests

Acoustic pressure acceleration analysis of a dome-shaped sound barrier

The topology optimization procedure is executed using an in-house Fortran 90 code with OpenMP parallelization. All simulations are carried out on a personal computer equipped with an Intel Core i7 processor and 16 GB of RAM. The problem is formulated under a two-dimensional assumption by considering an infinitely long noise barrier subjected to line-source excitation. In this way, the acoustic response of the barrier cross-section can be described by an equivalent two-dimensional Helmholtz model, which improves computational efficiency while retaining the essential scattering characteristics.

Although the proposed method has been validated through representative numerical examples, further investigations are still required. These include more complex geometries and different source/receiver configurations to better assess the robustness and generality of the method, as well as a more detailed scalability analysis in terms of DOFs, memory consumption, and computational cost. In addition, systematic comparisons with conventional BEM and other fast or reduced-order methods would further clarify the efficiency advantages of the proposed approach. These issues are beyond the scope of the present work and will be addressed in future studies.

It is clear that the volume constraint is inactive at the optimal solution.

The results suggest that the acoustic performance of a noise barrier is strongly influenced by wave interference, diffraction, and local sound energy concentration. Therefore, uniformly covering the entire barrier surface with absorbing material does not necessarily achieve the best attenuation effect. Instead, the optimization algorithm tends to allocate absorbing materials to acoustically sensitive regions where stronger wave interactions occur, thereby maximizing noise reduction efficiency under limited material volume constraints. This explains why optimized partial coverage can outperform full coverage in the present study.

Conclusion

This study develops a Taylor-expansion-accelerated BEM for efficient computation of sound pressure fields in domed-type noise barriers, significantly reducing the computational cost of conventional BEM for frequency-domain analysis. Based on this fast solver, a topology optimization framework is proposed to determine the optimal distribution of sound-absorbing materials. The method employs a SIMP interpolation scheme with a smoothed Heaviside filter to suppress gray regions and enforce a clear 0–1 material distribution, and a relaxed MMA algorithm is used to ensure stable convergence under volume constraints.

Numerical results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed approach, showing that strategic material distribution can achieve better acoustic performance than full coverage. The optimal layouts exhibit clear frequency dependence, highlighting the necessity of frequency-aware design. Overall, the proposed method provides an efficient and robust framework for acoustic design of noise barriers with improved sound attenuation and reduced material usage.

Research on short-term prediction method of photovoltaic power based on HPO-VMD-BiLSTM
Authors: Jianbo Li, Longhao Li, Qinjun Du, Yede Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe photovoltaic (PV) power generation process is susceptible to the interference of meteorological factors with volatility, which seriously impacts the stability of PV grid connection. To solve the above problems, this paper proposes a numerical prediction method for PV power generation with improved K-means, HPO-VMD and HPO-BiLSTM. Firstly, to reduce the influence of data diversity and randomness on the PV power prediction accuracy, the arrangement entropy is combined with K-means clustering algorithm to achieve weather typing under different meteorological factors. Then, for the existence of strong volatility and randomness of PV power data, HPO-VMD data decomposition method is proposed to achieve adaptive data decomposition and improve the smoothness of PV power values. Subsequently, the PV power numerical prediction model is constructed based on the BiLSTM method. Meanwhile, to reduce the adverse effects of improper selection of model parameters on the model prediction accuracy, the hunter–prey algorithm (HPO) is used to achieve the model parameter rectification. Finally, simulation experiments of the proposed method are conducted based on actual data from Alice Springs site, and the experimental and analytical results verify the effectiveness and generalization of the proposed method.Keywords: Photovoltaic power generation prediction, Ranking entropy and K-means clustering algorithm, Variational mode decomposition, Hunter-prey algorithm, Bidirectional long and short-term memory neural networkSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

With the increasing depletion of fossil energy sources, energy efficiency has become a focus of attention for all countries to achieve the goal of carbon neutrality1. Especially with the continuous development of new energy technologies, the use of photovoltaic (PV) power generation to achieve energy conservation has received extensive attention from researchers2. However, the instability of the photovoltaic power generation process is affected by fluctuations in meteorological factors, which seriously affects the reliability of the power grid dominated by photovoltaic systems. Therefore, accurate prediction of PV power is necessary to maintain the energy balance, which is vital to ensure the power system’s stable operation.

The power output of PV generation exhibits a complex nonlinear relationship due to the influence of meteorological factors, making the selection of training data crucial for the performance of data-driven short-term PV power prediction models. Currently, some domestic and international scholars employ clustering algorithms or similar-day algorithms to select more effective training datasets, thereby improving prediction accuracy. Compared to traditional physical modeling methods, data-driven statistical methods and analytical approaches, while avoiding the complexities of physical modeling, require substantial historical data to support the learning process, and the prediction process is intricate. Artificial intelligence methods leverage intelligent algorithms to thoroughly excavate the intrinsic characteristics and implicit patterns of change within historical power generation data, enabling a better fit to complex nonlinear relationships and facilitating the prediction of PV power generation3,4. Liu et al. proposed an enhanced PV power prediction model utilizing aerosol index data as an auxiliary input, with experimental results demonstrating that this method possesses superior predictive performance5. William et al. proposed a support vector machine (SVM) model based on genetic algorithm optimization for short-term power prediction of PV systems6. However, most single models find it difficult to accurately describe this complex nonlinear mapping relationship. They have a single algorithm which is prone to fall into local optima7. ARIMA and SARIMAX are two time series forecasting models known for their simplicity. However, they struggle with nonlinear data. On the other hand, the CatBoost gradient boosting decision tree algorithm, while powerful, is more complex and computationally expensive, resulting in slower training speeds. Therefore, the researchers proposed a hybrid prediction model with different algorithms, which can overcome the limitations of a single prediction model and improve the accuracy of PV power generation prediction. Mishra et al. proposed a hybrid prediction model for PV power based on multiple meteorological factors by analyzing the effects of meteorological factors such as solar radiation, temperature, humidity and wind speed on PV power8. The results show that by analyzing photovoltaic power and different meteorological factors, the problem of a sharp decrease in prediction performance of a single model in different seasons and different weather can be overcome. However, the accuracy of the prediction model is affected by the lack of cluster analysis of historical data. Lin et al. proposed a combined K-means and ELM based prediction method by clustering historical data using improved K-means clustering9. Liu et al. combined the principal component analysis (PCA) technique and K-means to obtain better clustering results10. Pulipaka et al. proposed a hybrid clustering method based on K-means and FCM further to improve previous PV clustering methods11. The above hybrid prediction method based on clustering and similar day algorithms shows that the impact of PV power differences on prediction accuracy can be reduced by using similar historical data in the data preprocessing stage for analysis. However, most of the clustering and similar day algorithms analyze only a single PV weather. The K-means method is considered to be the simplest, fastest and most popular clustering method. However, the disadvantage of K-means mean algorithm is that the clustering K must be optimal to obtain excellent results12. As different meteorological conditions affect PV power generation to different degrees, clustering and similar day algorithms need to perform multi-meteorological analysis. In this paper, a K-means clustering algorithm based on arrangement entropy improvement is proposed, which can better reflect the real distribution of the samples, get more reasonable clustering results, and help to improve the prediction accuracy.

Due to the strong randomness and diversity of PV power output, PV power sequences often face challenges such as difficulty in feature extraction and high volatility. To address these issues, some researchers have adopted signal decomposition algorithms to stabilize PV power sequences. These algorithms decompose the sequences into components of different frequency bands and complexities, thereby reducing the overall complexity and volatility of the sequences. By extracting hidden features from these decomposed components, the prediction accuracy of the models is significantly enhanced. Niu et al. proposed a photovoltaic power prediction model based on empirical modal decomposition (EMD) and artificial bee colony algorithm (ABC) to optimize the support vector machine (SVM), but the EMD over-envelopes and under-envelopes phenomena when finding the extreme value envelope, and serious modal aliasing and end-point effects can occur for some signals13. Yu et al. proposed a two-layer signal decomposition model based on improved complementary integrated empirical modal decomposition-variable modal decomposition to preprocess the data to smooth the PV power data sequences, and the results showed that the model can effectively reduce the effects of data fluctuations and non-smooth sequences14. Liu et al. proposed a method of fusion of empirical modal decomposition of complementary sets and singular value decomposition of matrix operation through data decomposition theory for the original PV power with a large number of high-frequency components and complex frequency components to achieve the extraction of main components and data noise reduction processing to ensure the accuracy of data prediction15. However, the empirical modal decomposition (EMD) method is prone to modal aliasing, poor decomposition, and cannot accurately separate signals with similar frequencies16. VMD is an adaptive, fully non-recursive method for modal variational and signal processing, which overcomes the problems of endpoint effects and modal component mixing in EMD methods, and reduces the non-stationarity of time series with high complexity and strong non-linearity. It has strong noise robustness and is widely used in the field of power prediction. However, VMD suffers from the disadvantage that the parameter settings for the number of modes and the penalty factor are empirically determined. Wang et al. proposed a hybrid prediction method to improve the prediction accuracy of short-term PV power17. In VMD decomposition, the number of modes is optimized by identifying the eigenfrequencies of PV power and the maximum value is used to optimize the penalty factor. The optimized VMD improves the accuracy of decomposition and avoids the loss of information, which improves the accuracy and convergence performance of the algorithm. Wang et al. proposed an ultra-short-term power prediction model for photovoltaic power plants based on modal decomposition and deep learning, using the VMD method to decompose the historical power and the adaptive noise fully empirical modal decomposition method to quadratically decompose the residual terms18. Zhang et al. proposed a smoothing strategy for PV hybrid energy storage systems based on improved HHO-VMD, which adaptively selects k and α in the VMD parameters and decomposes the PV output power to distinguish between correlated and uncorrelated modes19. Lin et al. proposed short-term wind power prediction based on VMD and improved BiLSTM, but due to the fact that the complexity of the partially decomposed components is still high, the decomposition performance is relatively affected by the number of decomposition layers and penalty factor, which makes it difficult to guarantee the data prediction accuracy20. HPO is a kind of intelligent optimization algorithm, compared with similar algorithms, HPO has enough exploration and development ability in terms of convergence accuracy and global optimization ability and has superior performance. Therefore, this paper adopts the HPO-VMD algorithm to optimize and determine the parameters of the variational modal decomposition algorithm, which is capable of adaptively adjusting the parameters of the VMD according to the changing characteristics and complexity of the signals and solves the problem of relying on subjective judgment, which improves the accuracy and iteration speed of the solution process.

PV power generation also exhibits distinct non-stationary characteristics and significant volatility. Single neural network prediction models struggle to fully uncover the implicit features within the sequence and stabilize its fluctuations, thereby achieving optimal predictive performance. Deep learning algorithms represented by convolutional neural networks (CNN) and recurrent neural networks (RNN) have been widely used in the field of short-term PV power prediction21–23. RNN is mainly used to deal with time-series data, but it is prone to long term dependency. LSTM has a high prediction performance. “Introduction of a frequency domain decomposition-based LSTM model for photovoltaic power prediction intervals in Reference24.“However, LSTM has a complex structure, many parameters, long training time, and overfitting problems25–27. Bidirectional LSTM (BiLSTM), as an improvement of LSTM, is made possible by the addition of a reverse computation module. Zhang et al. proposed a PV power generation prediction model based on BiLSTM using the TensorFlow framework for feature extraction and power generation prediction, and the experimental results showed that the accuracy of the prediction model based on BiLSTM was better than that of LSTM28.Reference29 utilized a genetic algorithm to optimize a Bi-LSTM model based on multiple output sequences for predicting target photovoltaic power output in a specific microgrid. Reference30 proposed a hybrid deep learning model integrating spatiotemporal attention mechanisms with multimodal features. Comparative experiments against typical hybrid architectures such as CNN-LSTM and BiTCN-BiGRU demonstrated that the proposed model reduces the mean absolute error by 12.3% in ultra-short-term forecasting scenarios. Wu et al. established a prediction model combining VMD and bi-directional long and short-term memory BiLSTM neural network, which can more deeply mine the information contained in the original data and effectively improve the prediction accuracy, but it only considered the hyper-parameter optimization of the BiLSTM model and ignored the optimization of the parameters k and α in the VMD, and the convergence time of the model training was longer31. BiLSTM model can enhance the mining of time series feature relevance information to a certain extent, but it can only extract local features, and it is difficult to obtain global relevance, resulting in the loss of feature relevance information. Therefore, it is necessary to optimize and improve the VMD algorithm and BILSTM to expand the global search range and avoid falling into local optimum. By employing a VMD-BiLSTM intelligent algorithm with HPO optimization, the complexity and non-stationarity of the original data can be reduced. This approach effectively improves the generalization capability and prediction accuracy of the BiLSTM model. A series of experimental analyses and comparisons are conducted to validate the effectiveness of this model in predicting PV power generation.

In summary, current PV power prediction studies mainly use hybrid models, all of which have improved the prediction accuracy to different degrees. However, the strong stochasticity, diversity and nonlinearity of PV power led to the following main problems in PV power prediction:

(1) PV power generation is affected by many complex meteorological factors, and there is a complex nonlinear relationship between PV power generation and meteorological variable inputs. The traditional clustering algorithm ignores the mutual influence between different meteorological variables when performing clustering, and also fails to consider the differences in the influence of different meteorological factors on PV power generation, resulting in a decrease in the clustering accuracy, which in turn affects the effectiveness of the prediction model.

(2) Due to the diversity and stochastic nature of PV power generation, the correlation between daily PV power generation data from different weather is low. Using many poorly correlated samples as training data will not improve the prediction accuracy, but rather increase the computational effort. In addition, when predicting the decomposed components, a single prediction model is difficult to achieve optimal prediction for all components due to the different frequency bands and complexity of different components, and the establishment of a combined prediction model is also one of the current research focuses.

(3) PV power generation is affected by a variety of factors, with obvious nonlinear and non-stationary characteristics, the model parameters directly affect the predictive performance of the model, and many hybrid model parameters need to be manually specified, with poor generalization performance. Therefore, it is particularly important to use intelligent optimization algorithms for parameter optimization of neural networks.

(4) The traditional optimization algorithms have the disadvantages of easily locking the local optimum and poor searchability, which makes it difficult to be applied to other models. Moreover, its accuracy, robustness, and adaptability are difficult to meet the current demand.

The main contributions of this paper include:

(1) A multi-meteorological-feature dataset based on multiple meteorological input variables was established. An improved K-means clustering algorithm based on permutation entropy was proposed, where relative permutation entropy distances replaced absolute distances. Furthermore, a weighted-distance-based K-means algorithm was adopted to better reflect the true distribution of samples, yielding more reasonable clustering results. This approach also mitigated the impact of relying solely on single variables (e.g., solar radiation) for clustering analysis.

(2) To tackle the strong randomness and diversity of PV power output, an improved similar-day method based on HPO-enhanced Variational Mode Decomposition (VMD) is introduced. This method enhances the correlation between training and prediction data. By adaptively adjusting VMD parameters according to the signal’s variation characteristics and complexity, it resolves the issue of reliance on subjective judgment, thereby improving the precision and iteration speed of the solution process.

(3) To address the complexity and non-stationarity of PV power output, an VMD-BiLSTM hyperparameter model with HPO optimization is employed. This approach significantly enhances the generalization capability and prediction accuracy of the BiLSTM model, preventing it from falling into local optima. It effectively mitigates the adverse effects of improper parameter settings on model performance.

(4) Through the effective decomposition of PV power generation data clustering and the in-depth optimization of VMD and BiLSTM parameters using the HPO algorithm, a dual optimization of data clustering and intelligent algorithms is achieved, establishing a hybrid model for PV power prediction. Experimental results demonstrate that the prediction accuracy of this model surpasses that of other models.

The main arrangement of this paper is as follows: in the second section, the basic method and its improved form are introduced, in the third part, the construction and implementation of the VMD-HPO-BiLSTM prediction model are presented, in the fourth part, simulation experiments and analyses are carried out, and in the fifth part, conclusions are drawn.

Methods

Considering the influence of environmental characteristic factors on photovoltaic power generation, this paper proposes a short-term PV power prediction method based on the HPO-VMD-BiLSTM prediction model.

First, the improved K-means algorithm is applied to cluster the original data, where the relative permutation entropy distance replaces the absolute distance. Furthermore, a weighted-distance-based K-means algorithm is adopted to better reflect the true distribution of samples, resulting in more reasonable clustering results.

The HPO-VMD algorithm decomposes the multidimensional PV time series features into several modal components and residual terms to reduce the volatility and non-stationarity of PV time series data. It can adaptively adjust VMD parameters according to the variation characteristics and complexity of the signal, addressing the issue of reliance on subjective judgment and improving the accuracy and iteration speed of the solution process.

Then, the HPO-optimized VMD-BiLSTM intelligent algorithm is employed to reduce the complexity and non-stationarity of the original data. This approach effectively enhances the generalization capability and prediction accuracy of the BiLSTM model.

The VMD algorithm is a powerful nonlinear adaptive modal feature processing method, and its core idea is to minimize a mathematical planning problem with constraints, which aims to find a set of intrinsic modal functions to minimize the sum of bandwidths of these functions. This feature shows strong robustness in handling complex nonlinear PV time series signal sampling and noise reduction. However, before VMD decomposition, it is necessary to pre-set the values of the number of components K and the penalty factor α. Different values of K and α lead to different decomposition results: a value of K that is too small may cause modal aliasing, while a value that is too large can produce spurious components. Similarly, a small α results in larger bandwidths for each IMF component, whereas a large α leads to smaller bandwidths. Therefore, how to determine the appropriate values of K and α is the key to the study, and the VMD parameters must be optimized.

LSTM

Long Short-Term Memory Network (LSTM) are a variant of RNN that are designed to solve the problem of vanishing and exploding RNN gradients. In time series prediction, LSTM has obvious advantages. LSTM can effectively solve both long-term and short-term dependence problems, so LSTM is often used in irradiance prediction and photovoltaic power prediction and other problems.

The cell state and the ‘gate’ structure are the core of the LSTM model. Compared to the hidden state of an RNN, the cell state can be analogous to a conveyor belt, where information that needs to be memorized is passed on and information that does not need to be memorized is intercepted by gates. There are three types of gate structures: input gate (it), output gate (ot), and forgetting gate (ft). The main role of input gate is to update the state of the cell, while the output gate is responsible for updating the hidden state (ht), and the role of forgetting gate is to decide how much of the state should be retained to the current state through an activation function (sigmoid). (K-means)-VMD-HPO-BiLSTM prediction model construction and implementation

VMD-HPO-BiLSTM prediction model construction

The VMD is combined with BiLSTM and the BiLSTM model is optimized using HPO to obtain a PV power prediction model based on VMD-HPO-BiLSTM. The improved K-means algorithm is utilized to cluster the original data, followed by decomposition using VMD to obtain K components. The training set components, along with their corresponding influencing factor variables, are then provided to the BiLSTM model for training. At this point, it is necessary to determine the number of nodes in the 2 hidden layers, the learning rate and the number of training times for the BiLSTM model, and the time cost of using the trial-and-error method is too high. For this reason, the HPO algorithm was used to optimize and obtain the optimal parameter values. The flow of the VMD-HPO-BiLSTM model is shown in Fig. 4.

Flowchart of the VMD-HPO-BiLSTM model

The specific steps of VMD-HPO-BiLSTM model construction are as follows:

(1) Use the improved K-means to cluster the original data, and then decompose it with VMD to obtain each modal component and normalize it, while dividing the first 70% as the training set and the second 30% as the test set.

(2) Analyze the influencing factors based on the characteristics of each component IMF1-IMFk.

(3) Select the parameters to be optimized, with the number of nodes in the two hidden layers, the learning rate, and the number of training epochs in the BiLSTM model as the objects of optimization.

(4) Initialize the parameters of the HPO algorithm, including the population size, the maximum number of iterations, the balance parameter, the adaptive parameter, and the value range of the parameters to be optimized.

(5) Input the preprocessed training set parameters and the corresponding influencing factors, use HPO algorithm to search for the optimum, constantly update the balance parameters, and update the hunter and prey positions until the end of meeting the requirements of the optimal solution, and output the optimal parameters to BiLSTM.

(6) Establish the VMD-HPO-BiLSTM model based on the optimal number of 2 hidden layer nodes, learning rate and training times.

(7) Predict the test set of each component using the VMD-HPO-BiLSTM model, sum up the prediction results of each component, and also inverse normalization process to get the final prediction results.

Implementation process of (K-means)-VMD-HPO-BiLSTM prediction model

The prediction process of the (K-means)-VMD-HPO-BiLSTM model consists of five main parts: improved K-means clustering, selection of similarity days, decomposition of VMD data, prediction of PV power generation by BiLSTM, and optimization of model parameters by HPO. Firstly, the improved K-means algorithm is used to cluster samples of different meteorological types on the meteorological feature dataset. On this basis, the improved similar day algorithm is used to select similar days as training data to reduce the impact of PV power generation stochasticity on model performance. The BiLSTM is applied to predict the PV power on the day with meteorological factors as input variables and PV power as output variables. The improved HPO algorithm optimizes the BiLSTM parameters to avoid poor prediction caused by improper parameter settings and improve the prediction accuracy. In addition, the VMD is improved to enhance its global search capability and avoid falling into local optimum. Figure 5 illustrates the operation flow of the proposed (K-means)-VMD-HPO-BiLSTM model.

Operational flow of (K-means)-VMD-HPO-BiLSTM model

The specific steps are as follows:

(1) Collect historical PV data, including horizontal radiation, relative humidity, wind speed, diffuse horizontal radiation, and PV power output values.

(2) Establish the meteorological variable feature dataset. These features mainly include mean, standard deviation, number of peaks, number of valleys, maximum value, coefficient of variation, kurtosis and skewness.

(3) The improved K-means clustering algorithm is based on the meteorological feature dataset, which divides the historical PV dataset into sunny, rainy and cloudy days.

(4) Based on meteorological variables, the daily similarity measure P is calculated for the three weather types. The four days with the highest similarity to the forecast day are selected as training data by numerical comparison.

(5) Weather factors are the input variables and PV power generation is the output variable.

(6) Initialize the parameters of the model and start training the BiLSTM model.

(7) Optimize the BiLSTM model parameters using HPO and output the optimal values of the model parameters after satisfying the iteration conditions.

(8) Use the optimal parameters BiLSTM to predict the PV power generation on the forecast day.

(9) Output the prediction results and calculate the performance evaluation index value of the model.

Data standardization

To mitigate the adverse effects of magnitude differences between meteorological variables and photovoltaic power generation on the model, this paper employs the min-max method for linear normalization of the dataset. For outlier detection and handling, a weather clustering method based on an improved k-means algorithm is adopted. First, the 3σ principle is applied to handle outliers in Hp(m) from Formula 14: (1) calculate the mean µk and variance σk of each eigenvalue Hp(m); (2) define the outlier judgment interval as (µk − 3σk, µk + 3σk); (3) mark data points outside this interval as outliers; (4) for outlier handling, use Numerical Weather Prediction (NWP) data to select data from similar days to supplement the dataset. After processing with the 3σ principle, the data is then clustered using the improved k-means algorithm.

Simultaneously, BiLSTM, as a data-driven black-box model, aims to minimize the deviation between predicted values and actual values. The HPO algorithm is adopted to optimize the number of nodes in the two hidden layers, the learning rate, and the number of training epochs to achieve the best possible performance.

Considering the current data scale, the selection of HPO population size and the number of iterations significantly affects program runtime. When the population size is set to 20 or 40, the program runtime increases by 50%; when the number of iterations exceeds 30, the program runtime doubles. To avoid excessively long program runtime, after multiple experiments, the optimal configuration for HPO in this paper is determined as a population size of 30 and a number of iterations of 30. From the perspective of evaluation metrics, R5 improves from 0.9278 to 0.9635, and RMSE is optimized from 65.4398 to 46.3479, achieving a reduction of 29.12%. This indicates that the model has a stronger understanding and learning capability regarding the input data, enabling it to better fit the real situation.

Conclusion

In this paper, a hybrid method based on HPO-VMD-BiLSTM for short-term power prediction of photovoltaic (PV) is proposed to solve the stochastic, nonlinear and volatility problems existing in PV power generation. The model is a hybrid prediction based on ranked entropy improved K-means clustering, similar day and intelligent optimization algorithms. Firstly, the multi-meteorological factor feature dataset contains several meteorological input variables, and an improved K-means clustering method is proposed after selecting the clustering metrics. The improved algorithm takes into account the influence of different meteorological factors on the clustering effect, which improves the clustering performance compared with the traditional clustering algorithm. Second, the HPO-VMD algorithm is used to optimally determine the parameters of the variational modal decomposition algorithm, which can adaptively adjust the VMD parameters according to the changing characteristics and complexity of the signal, solving the problem of relying on subjective judgement, improving the accuracy and iteration speed of the solution process, and reducing the negative impact of stochasticity on the model performance. Thirdly, this paper proposes an HPO-BiLSTM short-term power prediction method for PV power generation, which improves the global search capability of the algorithm and prevents convergence to a local optimum. The method improves the adaptive and generalization performance of the model by reducing the effect of incorrect parameter settings. Fourth, actual historical data from the SunPower (No. 10) site in the Alice Springs region of Australia is selected to validate the performance of the model. The empirical results show that the RMSE and MAE are reduced by 12%-38% and 15%-45%, respectively, compared with single models such as BPNN, LSTM, BiLSTM, VMD-BiLSTM, and (K-means)-BiLSTM. The prediction accuracy of the model is higher than that of other models due to the effective decomposition of PV power and the effective optimization of VMD and BiLSTM parameters by the HPO algorithm. The model has great potential for practical applications, especially in real-time scenarios. Integration of the model into an energy management system or development of user-friendly software tools will enable seamless integration and enable energy managers to rapidly predict PV power generation. This will ultimately lead to optimizing energy use, reducing costs and supporting sustainable growth of PV power generation systems.

Due to limitations in data sources, sample size, and the impact of extreme weather, the method proposed in this paper still requires further improvement. (1) In the study of clustering algorithms, this paper only categorizes weather types into three broad categories: sunny, rainy, and cloudy. During the experiments, predictions for different weather types were relatively accurate. However, in reality, weather types are complex and variable, with scenarios such as haze, sunny-to-cloudy transitions, and sun showers. Therefore, future research will consider a more detailed classification of clustered weather types. (2) In the simulation examples of this paper, the collected historical photovoltaic power generation data exhibited high reliability and excellent quality, providing solid data support for validating the proposed prediction method. However, looking ahead to future research, it is necessary to explore the potential impact of lower data quality on prediction accuracy and to investigate corresponding strategies to enhance the stability and robustness of the algorithm, ensuring the accuracy and reliability of the prediction results.

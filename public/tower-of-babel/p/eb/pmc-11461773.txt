The mechanism of MMP14-positive tumor-associated fibroblast subsets in inhibiting PD-1 immunotherapy for esophageal cancer through exosomal tsRNA-10522
Authors: Juzheng Wang, Chunlong Zheng, Jiayu Lu, Xinyao Xu, Guangyu Xiang, Jiahe Li, Jipeng Zhang, Xiaorong Mu, Qiang Lu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractEsophageal cancer (EC) continues to pose a significant health risk. Cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), an essential part of the tumor microenvironment (TME), are viewed as potential therapeutic targets. However, their role in tumor mechanisms specific to esophageal cancer remains to be elucidated. This study identified MMP14+ CAFs and MMP14- CAFs using immunofluorescence staining. The cytotoxic activity of CD8 T cells was assessed via western blot and ELISA. Using a transwell test, the migratory potential of MMP14+ CAFs was evaluated. Using flow cytometry, apoptosis was found in the esophageal squamous cell carcinoma cell line KYSE30. To determine the important tsRNAs released by MMP14+ CAFs, tsRNA-seq was used. Two subgroups of EC receiving PD-1 immunotherapy were identified by our research: MMP14+ CAFs and MMP14- CAFs. MMP14+ CAFs showed improved migratory capacity and released more inflammatory factors linked to cancer. Through exosomes, these CAFs may prevent anti-PD-1-treated CD8 T cells from being cytotoxic. Furthermore, exosomal tsRNA from MMP14+ CAFs primarily targeted signaling pathways connected with cancer. Notably, it was discovered that tsRNA-10522 plays a critical role within inhibiting CD8 T cell tumor cell death. The tumor cell killing of CD8 T cells by exosomal tsRNA-10522 is inhibited by a subgroup of cells called MMP14+ CAFs inside the EC microenvironment during PD-1 immunotherapy. This reduces the effectiveness of PD-1 immunotherapy for EC. Our findings demonstrate the inhibitory function of MMP14+ CAFs within EC receiving PD-1 immunotherapy, raising the prospect that MMP14+ CAFs might serve as predictive indicators in EC receiving PD-1 immunotherapy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s10142-024-01447-3.Keywords: Esophageal cancer, PD-1 immunotherapy, MMP14+ Cancer-associated fibroblasts, Exosome, TsRNA-10522

Introduction

The aggressive malignant tumor known as esophageal cancer (EC) has a high incidence rate and a dismal prognosis (Harada et al. 2020; van Hagen et al. 2012). It is one of the top six causes of death from cancer each year (Fitzmaurice et al. 2019). East Asia and Europe, notably China, have exceptionally high EC prevalence rates (Bray et al. 2018). According to the latest epidemiological data, the incidence of esophageal cancer in China continues to rise, with an estimated 324,000 new cases and 301,000 deaths in 2020 alone PMID: 33,538,338. Conventional treatments for EC include radiation, chemotherapy, surgery, and targeted therapy (Abdo et al. 2017). PD-1/PD-L1 blockers, which are among the most potent immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) on the market right now, may sabotage the interaction between PD-1 and PD-L1, therefore reinstating the anti-cancer immune response (Sharma et al. 2015; Kim et al. 2016). These blockers are now being utilized to treat advanced gastric cancer (Yang et al. 2020). More study into the mechanisms of EC is necessary since this aggressive malignancy still poses a serious concern to public health due to its heterogeneity.

Tumor formation depends on cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), a substantial component of the tumor microenvironment (TME) (Sahai et al. 2020; Chen et al. 2021). CAFs come from a variety of places, however they are primarily from fibroblasts inside or close to the tumor. Mesenchymal stem cells originating from bone marrow, endothelial cells, and adipocytes are other sources (Bochet et al. 2013; Zeisberg et al. 2007; Raz et al. 2018). CAFs exhibit significant heterogeneity, despite the fact that they were formerly believed to be essential for the development of malignancies. Nonetheless, new studies have demonstrated that CAFs have a tumor-inhibitory impact (Unterleuthner et al. 2020; Affo et al. 2021). The substantial variety of CAFs is a result of their varied sources, and no appropriate universal CAF marker has been found as of yet (Chen et al. 2021). A handful of studies have demonstrated the clinical relevance of CAFs in EC, suggesting that CAFs may serve as important prognostic factors and therapeutic targets (Ren et al. 2023; Qiu et al. 2022). In tumors showing regression after nTX, COL11A1-positive CAFs were detected less frequently, SPARC-positive CAFs were enriched after nTX, in both responding and non-responding patients (p < 0.001). Our results support the concept of CAFs as an important factor of tumor promotion and maintenance in EC. The population of CAFs increases with tumor progression and decreases, partly depending on the subtype, after regression following nTX PMID: 31,828,432. A novel nomogram integrating the CAF-based risk signature and clinical stage was developed, which exhibited favorable predictability and reliability for ESCC prognosis prediction PMID: 37,313,409. Nevertheless, it is still unclear how CAFs control the anti-tumor action in esophageal cancer.

Matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) are zinc-dependent endopeptidases that can influence cancer invasion and metastasis via degrading extracellular matrix proteins (Kessenbrock et al. 2010). MMP14, additionally referred to as membrane type 1 matrix metalloproteinase (MT1-MMP), is the earliest reported membrane-type matrix metalloproteinase within the MMP family. This enzyme participates in various biological processes, including proliferation, invasion, and angiogenesis (Baker et al. 2002). The majority of solid tumors have a markedly elevated MMP14 content, although nearby normal tissues do not (Turunen et al. 2017; Noda et al. 2023). Studies have found that CAFs expressing MMP14 are involved within the growth of colorectal cancer and oral squamous cell carcinoma (Noda et al. 2023; Makutani et al. 2022). For instance, a study demonstrated that MMP14 + CAFs in colorectal cancer tissues are associated with enhanced tumor invasiveness and poor patient prognosis PMID: 36,052,235. But research on EC has not yet been reported.

tRNA-derived small RNA (tsRNA) is an innovative regulatory non-coding small RNA involved in many processes in biology and medicine (Mao et al. 2023). Many types of cancer have aberrant expression of tsRNA (Balatti et al. 2017; Shao et al. 2017). Alongside the progress of cancer, the expression of tsRNA also changes (Shao et al. 2017), suggesting that abnormally regulated tsRNA may serve as an important biomarker for disease diagnosis, targeted therapy, and prognosis. Zhu et al. provided the first evidence that exosomal tsRNA can be used as a new diagnostic biomarker (Zhu et al. 2019). However, there is no report on the exosomal tsRNA secreted by CAFs in EC.

In our previous study (not publish), we found a negative association between endothelial cells and treatment effect, and a positive association between fibroblasts and treatment effect, as determined by single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) of tissue samples through EC patients treated with PD-1 immunotherapy (Fig. S1). Throughout this investigation, we demonstrated the tumor-promoting activity of the MMP14+ CAFs subset and its ability to inhibit the PD-1 immunotherapy of EC through exosomal tsRNA-10522. Our study suggests that MMP14+ CAFs have the potential to predict the prognosis of PD-1 immunotherapy of EC.

Methods

Tissue samples and cells

In this study, clinical tissue samples (tumor samples and paracancerous tissue samples) and blood samples of EC treated with PD-1 immunotherapy were collected from Tangdu Hospital hospital. The investigation had been authorized through the Institutional Ethics Committee of institution for national drug clinical trials, tangdu hospital, fourth military medical university (202102-23), and all sufferers agreed upon informed consent in writing. CAFs were separated via EC and adjacent tissues. CD8 T cells were separated via peripheral blood of EC patients. Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma cell KYSE30 was purchased from Shanghai Bogoo Biotechnology Company. The cells were added alongside 20% fetal bovine serum (Gibco, Invitrogen, CA), 100 U/mL penicillin and 100 µg/mL streptomycin in 1640/TM175 (Gibco), during 37 ℃ and 5% CO2 incubator. All experiments were conducted with three biological replicates. This study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of TangDu Hospital of the Fourth Military Medical University (ID:202101-23).

Cells co-culture

CD8 T cells, MMP14+ CAFs and MMP14- CAFs were sorted based on the manufacturer’s instructions and flow cytometry. First, CD8 T cells were stimulated alongside anti-CD3 (2 µg/mL; Abcam, Cambridge, UK) and anti-CD28(2 µg/mL; Abcam) over 24 h, and then treated with PD-1 monoclonal antibody (Abcam) for 48 h. The cells were co-cultured alongside MMP14+/MMP14- CAFs or exosomes, respectively. The co-culture medium consisted of RPMI-1640 supplemented with 10% FBS, 1% penicillin-streptomycin, and 1% glutamine. Additionally, 10 ng/mL of IL-2 was added to support T cell growth and activation. Cells were maintained at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO₂.

Extraction and identification of exosomes

CAFs were inoculated in DMEM/F12 medium without FBS and incubated over 24 h. The supernatants were then gathered by differential centrifugation. After filtering the supernatant using a 0.2 μm filter (Millipore, USA, it was centrifuged for one hour at 200,000 × g. Every step of the centrifugation process was done during 4 ℃. The exosomes were identified using transmission electron microscopy (TEM; Hitachi HT7700, Tokyo, Japan). The purified exosomes were fixed alongside 2% paraformaldehyde and spotted on a glow discharge copper grid. After drying at room temperature for 15 min, they were stained alongside 2% uranyl acetate and allowed to dry for an additional 10 min. The morphology of isolated exosomes was examined using TME. The protein markers of the exosomes were analyzed by Western blot with CD63 (Abcam) antibody. All experiments were conducted with three biological replicates.

Immunofluorescence analysis

EC tissue samples were stained with FISH kit (RiboBio, Guangzhou, China), anti-VIM (1:500 dilution, Abcam) and anti-MMP14 (1:500 dilution, Abcam) in compliance with the manufacturer’s guidelines. MMP14+ CAFs and MMP14- CAFs were stained using the FISH kit and anti-VIM. The samples were then examined using confocal microscopy (Olympus, Japan). All experiments were conducted with three biological replicates.

Transwell assay

After being suspended in a serum-free medium, MMP14+ CAFs and MMP14- CAFs were injected into the top chamber of a Transwell device. There was medium in the lower chamber that contained 10% FBS. Cells that had moved to the membrane’s bottom surface were incubated for 36 h, fixed alongside paraformaldehyde, stained alongside crystal violet, and captured on camera.

RNA extraction and reverse transcriptase quantitative real-time PCR

Total RNAs were obtained from cells via TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, USA) and from exosomes using the miRNeasy Micro Kit (QIAGEN, Valencia, CA, USA) as per the manufacturers’ protocols. The concentration and purity of RNA were determined employing Nanodrop 2000 (Thermo Scientific, MA, USA). Total RNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA employing a cDNA reverse transcription kit (Takara, Japan). qRT-PCR was carried out via SYBR Green PCR Master Mix (Invitrogen, USA) and Applied Biosystems. The relative expression was determined employing the 2-∆∆CT approach. All experiments were conducted with three biological replicates. The primer sequences used in qPCR are provided in Supplementary Table 1.

Western blot study

Total proteins from the corresponding cells or exosomes were obtained employing RIPA buffer (Solarbio, Beijing, China). The isolated protein was loaded onto a protein gel and blocked alongside TBST including 5% skimmed milk powder during room temperature over 1 h. The samples were subsequently incubated alongside anti-Granzyme B (1:1000 dilution, Abcam), anti-CD63 (1:1000 dilution, Abcam), anti-GAPDH (1:1000 dilution, Abcam), and HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies (1:5000 dilution, Abcam), and analyzed. All experiments were conducted with three biological replicates.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

After receiving PD-1 monoclonal antibody treatment, MMP14+/MMP14- CAFs or exosomes were co-cultured alongside CD8 T cells. A 0.22 μm filter was employed to capture and filter the CD8 T cell supernatant. IFNγ level were detected according to the ELISA kit instructions and measured at an OD of 450 nm. All experiments were conducted with three biological replicates.

Flow cytometry

Annexin V/PI apoptosis kit (Invitrogen, USA) was employed to identify apoptosis through flow cytometry. After co-incubation with exosomes, CD8 T cells were co-cultured alongside KYSE70 cells. Cells were treated alongside Annexin V binding solution and incubated alongside Annexin-V FITC and PI staining. The samples were then examined using flow cytometry (FACSCanto II, BD, CA, USA) using FlowJo software. All experiments were conducted with three biological replicates.

Exosomal tsRNA sequencing

The library construction and deep sequencing of exosome tsRNA samples were performed by Aksmics (Shanghai, China) company. Agilent Bioanalyzer 2100 (Agilent Technologies, USA) was used to construct the tsRNA library, and standard small RNA sequencing was carried out using the Illumina NextSeq instrument. After annotating tsRNA, we analyzed the fragments’ size and distribution, and screened the differential tsRNA expression profiles (Log2 fold change > 1 or < -1, p-value < 0.05). Miranda and RNAhybrid were utilized to forecast differential tsRNA target genes. GO functional enrichment and KEGG pathway study were employed to assess the functional enrichment of variably expressed tsRNA. All experiments were conducted with three biological replicates.

For the scRNA-seq, cells were processed using the 10x Genomics Chromium platform, followed by library preparation and sequencing on an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 system. Data analysis was performed using Cell Ranger and Seurat pipelines, allowing for clustering and differential gene expression analysis. Immunofluorescence staining was performed using specific antibodies against known CAF markers, and images were captured using a confocal microscope. Quantification of stained cells was conducted using ImageJ software, providing a robust validation of CAF subgroups observed in scRNA-seq data. Furthermore, the differential expression analysis for tsRNA included normalization steps using DESeq2, ensuring accurate identification of significant changes. Pathway analysis was conducted using the DAVID bioinformatics tool, integrating GO and KEGG results to provide a comprehensive understanding of the biological processes and pathways affected by differential tsRNA expression.

Statistical study

R software (version 3.6.1) and GraphPad Prism 7.0 (San Diego, CA, USA) were employed for statistical evaluation. The information was displayed as mean ± standard deviation. The two categories were contrasted employing the t-test. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was employed to assess comparisons among several categories. Findings were deemed noteworthy when the p-value was less than 0.05.

Result

Existence of MMP14+ and MMP14- CAFs cell subsets in esophageal cancer

The scRNA-seq results revealed the presence of two distinct subsets of CAFs, namely MMP14+ and MMP14-, in EC tissues that underwent PD-1 immunotherapy. In order to confirm the presence of these CAF subsets and their correlation with PD-1 immunotherapy, both VIM (a CAF marker) and MMP14 were stained immunofluorescently in EC tissues (both cancerous and adjacent non-cancerous) before and after PD-1 immunotherapy. The findings confirmed the clear identification and regional distribution of both MMP14+ and MMP14- CAFs in the stroma, thereby affirming the existence of these CAF populations in the EC patients’ body (Fig. 1). All experiments were conducted with three biological replicates.

Immunofluorescence analysis about the presence of MMP14+ and MMP14- CAFs in EC tissues and adjacent tissues before and after PD-1 immunotherapy

Distinct cancer-related activities of MMP14+ and MMP14- CAFs

According to the results of previous sequencing, the study screened MMP14+ CAFs and MMP14- CAFs subsets by flow cytometry. By using morphological observations and immunofluorescent detection of the static CAF marker VIM, both groups were verified as fibroblasts (Fig. 2A). Subsequently, we investigated the carcinogenic characteristics of these two CAFs. Transwell assay showed that the migratory capacity of MMP14+ CAFS was substantially superior to that of MMP14- CAFs (Fig. 2B). Inflammatory factors are crucial in modulating the inflammatory microenvironment and fostering tumor development. In this context, we examined the expression levels of pro-inflammatory genes, containing IL-1β, IL-6, IL-8, and type IV collagen (COL4A1), using qPCR. It was discovered that their expression substantially rose within MMP14+ CAFs category (Fig. 2C). All experiments were conducted with three biological replicates.

Impact of MMP14+CAFs on the tumor cell killing of CD8 T cells during PD-1 antibody therapy

scRNA-seq results revealed that both MMP14+ CAFs and MMP14- CAFs interacted with CD8 T cells, exhibiting differences in their correlation with T cell exhaustion, T cell infiltration levels, and immune regulatory functions (Fig. 3A). In this study, CD8 T cells, isolated from peripheral blood, were activated and treated with PD-1 monoclonal antibody, followed by co-culturing with MMP14+ and MMP14- CAFs respectively. MMP14+ CAFs significantly inhibited the expression of granzyme B within CD8 T cells (Fig. 3B). Furthermore, ELISA results indicated that MMP14+ CAFs significantly reduced the level of IFN-γ secreted via CD8 T cells (Fig. 3C). These results imply that MMP14+ CAFs may have important immune regulatory roles and may prevent CD8 T cell tumor cell death during PD-1 antibody therapy. All experiments were conducted with three biological replicates.

MMP14+ CAFs inhibited the tumor cell killing of CD8 T cells via exosomes

To delve deeper into the mechanism by which MMP14+ CAFs impact the cytotoxic activity of CD8 T cells during PD-1 antibody treatment, we isolated exosomes from both MMP14+ and MMP14- CAF subsets. The appearance and concentration of the two exosome types did not differ significantly, according to Western blotting and electron microscopy (Fig. 4A-B).

Identification of exosomes from MMP14+and MMP14-CAFs subsets. A Electron microscopy revealed the saucer-like structure of exosomes obtained via both MMP14+ CAFs and MMP14- CAFs. B Western blot study detects the expression of the exosome marker CD63

Further, we evaluated the protein levels of granzyme B and IFN-γ within CD8 T cells and discovered that exosomes derived from MMP14+ CAFs significantly suppressed the expression of granzyme B and the secretion of IFN-γ (Fig. 5A-B). Moreover, CD8 T cells incubated with exosomes from both subsets were co-cultured with CFSE-labeled esophageal squamous cell carcinoma cells (KYSE30). Flow cytometry results indicated that exosomes derived from MMP14+ CAFs significantly inhibited the tumor cell killing of CD8 T cells against esophageal cancer cells (Fig. 5C-D). All experiments were conducted with three biological replicates.

Identification and verification of the key tsRNA in exosomes derived from MMP14+ CAFs

To identify the key tsRNA of MMP14+ CAFs-derived exosomes, we performed small RNA sequencing on the exosomes derived from MMP14+ CAFs and MMP14- CAFs. A collection of 377 exosomal tsRNAs that were differently expressed were analyzed; in exosomes originating from MMP14 + CAFs, 211 tsRNAs were elevated 166 tsRNAs were downregulated (Fig. 6A). The target genes of these differently expressed tsRNAs were then anticipated. Functional analysis and signal pathway enrichment study showed that these target genes played a role in immune and cancer-related signaling pathways, including the cancer pathway, Hippo pathway, TGF-β pathway, and T cell receptor pathway. Specifically, the GO enrichment analysis in Fig. 6B shows that the affected biological processes include regulation of transcription, DNA-templated; positive regulation of transcription from RNA polymerase II promoter; axon guidance; cell adhesion; multicellular organismal development; nervous system development; phosphorylation; angiogenesis; neurotrophin TRK receptor signaling pathway; and small GTPase mediated signal transduction. These processes play important roles in the occurrence and development of cancer. For example, regulation of transcription and cell adhesion are crucial in the proliferation and migration of cancer cells, while angiogenesis and neurotrophin signaling pathways are associated with tumor blood supply and nerve invasion.

Identification of key tsRNAs in exosomes derived from MMP14+CAFs. A Heat map showing the differential expression of tsRNA in exosomes of MMP14+ and MMP14- CAFs. B Functional examination of target genes of the distinctly expressed tsRNA. C Signaling pathway analysis on the target genes of distinctly expressed tsRNAs

The pathway enrichment analysis in Fig. 6C shows that the affected signaling pathways include pathways in cancer, Rap1 signaling pathway, axon guidance, Ras signaling pathway, focal adhesion, calcium signaling pathway, PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, Hippo signaling pathway, Wnt signaling pathway, and MAPK signaling pathway. These signaling pathways play important roles in the proliferation, apoptosis, migration, and immune evasion of tumor cells. For example, the PI3K-Akt and MAPK signaling pathways are critical in regulating cell growth and survival, while the Wnt and Hippo signaling pathways are associated with tumor stem cell characteristics and tumor suppression (Fig. 6B-C).

Exosomes derived from MMP14+CAFs may suppress the tumor cell killing ability of CD8 T cells via tsRNA-10522

We discovered tsRNAs whose target genes are involved in immune regulatory signaling pathways by combining target gene prediction with pathway enrichment analysis. These tsRNAs were then validated using qPCR. Among them, tsRNA-10522 was found to be significantly up-regulated in exosomes derived from MMP14+ CAFs (Fig. 7A). We transfected MMP14 + CAFs with a tsRNA-10522 inhibitor and subsequently isolated the exosomes (Fig. 7B-C). These exosomes were then incubated with CD8 T cells, which were co-cultured alongside CFSE-labeled KYSE30 cells treated alongside anti-PD-1. The knockdown of tsRNA-10522 in exosomes derived from MMP14+ CAFs significantly increased the expression of granzyme B and the secretion of IFN-γ in CD8 T cells (Fig. 7D-E). In addition, we assessed the apoptosis of esophageal cancer cells employing flow cytometry. The finding displayed that the knockdown of tsRNA-10522 in exosomes derived from MMP14+ CAFs significantly enhanced the capacity of CD8 T cells to kill esophageal cancer cells (Fig. 7F). Specifically, tsRNA-10522 may function through miRNA-like regulatory mechanisms, where it binds to target mRNAs and inhibits their translation or promotes their degradation. Additionally, tsRNA-10522 could interact with proteins involved in T cell activation and cytotoxic function, thereby modulating their activity. For example, tsRNA-10522 may bind to proteins such as Argonaute (AGO) proteins, which are key components of the RNA-induced silencing complex (RISC), facilitating the regulation of gene expression in a manner similar to miRNAs. Alternatively, tsRNA-10522 might interact with transcription factors or signaling molecules that are crucial for CD8 T cell function, thereby influencing their ability to effectively kill tumor cells. Further studies exploring these potential mechanisms will be essential to fully understand the role of tsRNA-10522 in modulating CD8 T cell cytotoxicity and its implications for PD-1 immunotherapy in esophageal cancer. All experiments were conducted with three biological replicates.

Discussion

As tumor research progresses, it has become evident that the tumor microenvironment (TME) serves an essential part in cancer growing and progressing. CAFs, a significant cell type within the TME, have become viable targets for therapy as a result of their function within the development, spread, and metastasis of tumors (Zhang et al. 2023). During this investigation, we discovered a subset of cells, MMP14+ CAFs, within the EC microenvironment during PD-1 immunotherapy. These cells deliver tsRNA-10522 to CD8 T cells via exosomes, inhibiting the tumor cell killing ability of CD8 T cells through miRNA-like regulation or protein-binding modes. This process reduces the efficacy of PD-1 immunotherapy for esophageal cancer (Fig. 8). Our research sheds light on the processes behind MMP14+ CAFs within EC.

Scientific Hypothesis : In the EC microenvironment following PD-1 treatment, a recently discovered cell subgroup known as MMP14 + CAFs secretes exosomes. These exosomes carry tsRNA-10522 into CD8 T cells, inhibiting the expression of inflammatory and cytotoxic molecules through miRNA-like regulation or protein-binding regulation modes. This process disables CD8 T cells, resulting within the inability of CD8 T cells to kill tumor cells through cytotoxic molecules after PD-1 antibody blocking PD-1

CAFs, found in both primary and metastatic cancers, are the primary non-lymphocytes in the TME. They promote tumor cell proliferation, invasion, and chemotherapy resistance via secreting CAF-specific proteins and cytokines (Ozawa et al. 2016; Au Yeung et al. 2016). Due to the wide source of CAFs, specific biomarkers that can define various CAFs have not yet been found (Zhang et al. 2023). However, modern techniques like scRNA-seq along with additional single-cell study methods (including single-cell whole genome sequencing, single-cell genome and transcriptome sequencing, and single-cell methylation and transcriptome sequencing) have made it practicable to investigate specific CAF indicators to recognize CAF subsets (Chen et al. 2021). In this study, we detected two cell subsets, MMP14+ CAFs and MMP14- CAFs, in EC for the first time using scRNA-seq, and confirmed these findings through immunofluorescence staining.

MMP14 is a transmembrane proteolytic enzyme primarily expressed and activated in stromal cells. It influences cancer invasion and metastasis by degrading extracellular matrix (ECM) proteins (Afik et al. 2016; Amara et al. 2018). According to recent research, MMP14+CAFs encourage the growth of rectal cancer and oral squamous cell carcinoma (Winkler et al. 2020; Pach et al. 2021). Our study found that the migration ability of MMP14+ CAFs in EC was significantly higher than that of MMP14-CAFs. Additionally, MMP14+ CAFs promoted the expression of pro-inflammatory genes in EC, which includes IL-1β, IL-6, IL-8, TGF-β. This suggests that MMP14+ CAFs contribute to the migration and invasion of esophageal cancer, for which high expression of pro-inflammatory genes may provide a tumor microenvironment.

We found that MMP14+CAFs inhibited the tumor cell killing ability of CD8 T cells treated with PD-1 antibody. As potent effector cells within the TME, CD8 T cells can release granzymes and perforin into tumor cells to induce apoptosis. Previous studies have shown that within tumor tissues, CD8 T cells and CAFs are negatively correlated (Kato et al. 2018), with CAFs reducing the infiltration of CD8 T cells into tumors and enhancing the sensitivity of immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) (Farhood et al. 2019; Jenkins et al. 2022). These findings align with our conclusions, further demonstrating that targeting specific CAFs may improve therapeutic efficacy in EC.

Exosomes carry a variety of substances, including proteins, lipids, nucleic acids (DNA, mRNAs, and ncRNAs) and metabolites (Lin et al. 2022). There are thousands of exosome cargos, such as proteins, mRNAs, and miRNAs, according to the ExoCarta database. These cargos are critically involved in regulating diverse biological processes in both physiological and pathological conditions, and can be employed as biomarkers and effective therapeutic targets for human diseases (Lin et al. 2022). Our study found that MMP14+CAFs inhibited the tumor cell killing ability of CD8 T cells through exosomes. Exosomes generally carry specific proteins and RNA molecules. While microRNAs, circular RNAs, as well as long non-coding RNAs within exosomes have been widely investigated for their potential as biomarkers of tumor diagnosis or prognosis, exosomal tsRNA has been rarely reported (Szabo et al. 2017). tsRNA, a small RNA fragment produced by a precursor or mature tRNA, is precisely cleaved into tRNA loops through specialized nucleases, like Dicer and angiopoietin, in certain cells and tissues or under particular circumstances (Jia et al. 2020). Research has indicated that fragments produced from tRNA are implicated in the proliferation, metastasis, progression, and survival of tumor cells (Zeng et al. 2020). Li et al. analyzed the effect of exosome tsRNA in saliva on EC and discovered that it can act as a preoperative indicator in addition to having promise for diagnosis and prognosis (Li et al. 2022). In this study, we first sequenced tsRNA from exosome secreted by MMP14+ CAFs. It was found that the differentially expressed tsRNA played a key role in immune and cancer-related signaling pathways, such as cancer pathway, Hippo pathway, TGF-β pathway and T cell receptor pathway. Our study screened and verified that exosomal tsRNA-10522 secreted by MMP14+ CAFs inhibited the tumor cell killing ability of CD8 T cells. This result confirmed that exosomal tsRNA is related to tumor development and further clarified that MMP14+ CAFs inhibited the tumor cell killing ability of CD8 T cells through exosomal tsRNA-10522, thereby affecting the efficacy of PD-1 immunotherapy for EC. Nevertheless, more research is needed to determine the exact mechanism by which exosomal tsRNA-10522 prevents CD8 T lymphocytes from destroying tumor cells, such as a protein-binding or miRNA-like regulatory method. Given that MMP14 + CAFs may impact the effectiveness of immune checkpoint blockade therapies by modifying the tumor microenvironment, one potential approach is to improve the design of these therapies through combination therapies. For instance, combining PD-1 immunotherapy with treatments that target MMP14 + CAFs or their secreted exosomes may enhance the overall therapeutic efficacy. Additionally, optimizing the timing of administration, such as pre-treating with agents that modulate the tumor microenvironment before administering immune checkpoint inhibitors, could potentially improve patient outcomes. Further research into these strategies will be essential for developing more effective therapeutic regimens.

Regarding the mechanism of action of tsRNA-10522, it is suggested to explore its specific biological role in tumor cells through the following research directions and experimental methods: gene expression analysis, functional assays, mechanistic studies, pathway analysis, in vivo studies, CRISPR/Cas9 gene editing (Yang 2023), single-cell analysis, and epigenetic studies. These approaches will help to gain a deeper understanding of the specific biological functions and mechanisms of tsRNA-10522 in tumor cells.

Conclusion

Our research showed that after PD-1 immunotherapy, a subpopulation of cells called MMP14+ CAFs decreases CD8 T lymphocytes’ capacity to destroy tumor cells within the EC microenvironment. This suppression is produced by exosomes carrying tsRNA-10522, which reduces the ability of CD8 T lymphocytes to destroy tumor cells and the efficacy of PD-1 immunotherapy for EC. According to this research, MMP14+ CAFs may be used as prognostic markers for EC receiving PD-1 immunotherapy. This creates new opportunities to improve patient outcomes and the efficacy of immunotherapies.

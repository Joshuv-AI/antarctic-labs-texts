Application of diffusion kurtosis imaging in differentiating T0-T1 from T2 rectal tumors
Authors: Yu-ru Ma, Zhi-wen Zhang, Zi-qiang Wen, Xin-ni Cai, Xue-han Wu, Yu-tao Que, Wen-jie Fan, Quan-meng Liu, Yi-yan Liu, Shen-ping Yu, Yan Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHigh-spatial-resolution T2-weighted imaging (HR-T2WI) has been demonstrated to overestimate the staging of early rectal cancer, which could lead to missed opportunities for organ-preserving treatments. This study aimed to investigate the value of diffusion kurtosis imaging (DKI) in distinguishing between T0-T1 and T2 rectal tumors.MethodsA total of 138 patients with pathologically confirmed T0-T2 rectal tumors who underwent surgery between 2018 and 2023 were included. The pathological findings on tumor staging obtained from surgical specimens were used as the reference standard. The depth of tumor invasion was assessed using HR-T2WI. Kurtosis and diffusivity from DKI and apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) from diffusion-weighted imaging were measured for the entire tumor. Diffusion parameters were compared between pT0-T1 and pT2 tumors. Multivariable logistic regression and receiver operating characteristic curve analyses were conducted to evaluate the diagnostic performance of significant individual parameters and their combinations in determining pT0-T1 tumors.ResultsKurtosis was lower in pT0-T1 than in pT2 rectal tumors (0.799 vs. 0.950, P < 0.001), while diffusivity and ADC were higher than those of pT2 rectal cancer (1.732 × 10−3 mm2/s vs. 1.368 × 10−3 mm2/s, P < 0.001; 1.316 × 10−3 mm2/s vs. 1.043 × 10−3 mm2/s, P < 0.001). Diffusivity demonstrated the highest diagnostic efficacy in differentiating pT0-T1 from pT2 rectal tumors, with an AUC of 0.810, which was higher that of HR-T2WI (AUC = 0.752, P < 0.001) and kurtosis (AUC = 0.729, P = 0.007), but showed no difference compared to ADC (AUC = 0.785, P = 0.087). A multivariable logistic regression model incorporating HR-T2WI and diffusivity improved diagnostic performance compared with all other individual parameters, achieving an AUC of 0.885 (all P < 0.05).ConclusionThe combination of HR-T2WI and diffusivity can effectively detect pT0-T1 rectal tumors. HR-T2WI combined with diffusivity derived from DKI may serve as a potential biomarker for early assessment of rectal tumors, offering valuable insights for selecting suitable candidates for organ-preserving surgery.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40644-025-00947-0.Keywords: Rectal cancer, Early stage, Diffusion kurtosis imaging, Diffusion-weighted imaging

Background

Increased screening for colorectal cancer has led to a higher detection rate of adenomas and early rectal cancers [1], providing opportunities for organ preservation and helping avoid morbidity and disfunction associated with radical rectal resection [2, 3]. Meticulous patient selection based on the depth of tumor invasion is an essential prerequisite for implementing a secure strategy of organ preservation. Minimally invasive transanal local excision is usually recommended for adenoma and low-risk T1 rectal cancer [4]. For T1 rectal cancer with adverse features, local resection followed by adjuvant treatment may be necessary; whereas for T2 rectal cancer, total mesorectal excision should be performed [5].

High-spatial-resolution T2-weighted imaging (HR-T2WI) is the optimal sequence for assessing rectal tumor depth of invasion [6]; however, it tends to overstage early disease, with 54.7% of T1 tumors being overstaged [7], potentially leading to missed opportunities for organ-preserving treatment. Endoscopic ultrasound surpasses HR-T2WI in the early stages of the disease; however, it cannot fully visualize bulky or high rectal tumors and is highly operator-dependent [8]. Diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI), a routinely used functional sequence for rectal cancer, enhances lesion detection but does not aid in distinguishing T staging based on tumor signal intensity [9, 10]. Few studies have utilized the apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) derived from DWI to differentiate T staging, possibly due to the assumption of free diffusion in DWI and the reliance on a mono-exponential model for ADC calculation. This approach hardly reflects the intricate microenvironments of tumors as various factors such as cell membranes and compartments contribute to non-free water diffusion known as non-Gaussian diffusion [11].

Diffusion kurtosis imaging (DKI) serves as a direct extension of DWI and enables the quantification of non-Gaussian diffusion in biological tissues, thereby providing a more comprehensive characterization of tissue profile in terms of heterogeneity and cellularity [12]. The model provides both kurtosis and diffusivity parameters. Kurtosis reflects the deviation of water from Gaussian behavior, while diffusivity represents the diffusion coefficient adjusted for non-Gaussian bias [12]. DKI not only outperforms DWI in the detection and grading of varies lesions, but also enables identification of early microstructural changes in tissues and assessment of treatment responses [13–19]. However, no significant differences in DKI parameters were reported between pT1-T2 and pT3-T4 rectal tumors [17]. Nevertheless, the study did not investigate the relationship between DKI parameters and the early rectal tumors. Therefore, this study aimed to evaluate the diagnostic value of DKI in differentiating between pT0-T1 and pT2 rectal tumors.

Materials and methods

Patient

This retrospective study was approved by our Institutional Review Board (IRB No. [2025]455) with a waiver of informed consent. A total of 1057 consecutive patients with pathologically confirmed rectal adenoma or adenocarcinoma who underwent preoperative rectal MRI were collected from January 2018 to December 2023. Patients were excluded for the following reasons: (a) received neoadjuvant therapy (n = 273); (b) not undergoing surgery in our hospital (n = 147); (c) having an interval between surgery and preoperative rectal MRI of more than 2 weeks (n = 36); (d) having a postoperative pathological T stage > T2 (n = 443); (e) lacking DKI sequence (n = 10); (f) having a lesion that was too small to delineate the region of interest (n = 9); (g) poor image quality for evaluation, such as metal and motion artifacts (n = 1). Finally, 138 patients were enrolled (mean age, 60 years ± 12 [standard deviation]; 79 male) (Fig. 1).

MRI examination

To enhance the visibility of the tumor, an appropriate amount of gel (20–80 mL) was rectally administered based on the tumor location determined by colonoscopy or CT. To minimize artifacts related to intestinal peristalsis, 20 mg of raceanisodamine hydrochloride (MINSHENG PHARMACEUTICAL Co., Ltd.) was administered intramuscularly approximately 10 min before the MRI examination, unless contraindicated.

The images were acquired using a 3.0 T MRI scanner (Magnetom Verio; Siemens Healthineers) with a 6-channel phased-array surface coil in combination with the built-in spine coil. The rectal MRI protocols included (a) axial T2WI using a turbo spin-echo sequence; (b) sagittal, coronal, and orthogonal axial (perpendicular to the tumor base) HR-T2WI using a turbo spin-echo sequence; and (c) axial diffusion imaging using a single-shot echo-planar imaging sequence with b values of 0, 200, 600, 1000, 1500 and 2000 s/mm2 (Table 1).

Image analysis and postprocessing

Two radiologists (Y.C., Y.R.M; 9 and 6 years of experience in rectal MRI, respectively), who were unaware of the pathological results, independently reviewed HR-T2WI to determine the depth of tumor invasion. Tumors confined to hyperintense submucosa were defined as mrT1, while tumors infiltrating muscularis propria with loss of interface between submucosa and muscularis propria were classified as mrT2, and tumors extending beyond muscularis propria into perirectal fat with a nodular margin were categorized as mrT3 [20]. In this study, our aim was to evaluate the diagnostic value in determining pT0-T1 rectal tumors. Therefore, tumors diagnosed as ≥ mrT2 were grouped together. The discrepant findings between the two radiologists were resolved by a third gastrointestinal radiologist with over 20 years of experience (S.P.Y.).

All diffusion imaging data were imported into the post-processing software (MR Body Diffusion Toolbox v1.3.0; Siemens Healthineers).

Histopathological evaluation

All patients underwent surgery. The pathological reports concerning tumor and lymph node staging, derived from resected specimens, were based on the criteria of the American Joint Committee on Cancer and served as the reference standard.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS software (version 26.0; IBM) and MedCalc statistical software (version 15.8; MedCalc Software bvba; https://www.medcalc.org; 2015). The normality of the data was tested using Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Continuous data were described as means ± standard deviations or medians and interquartile ranges, and compared using independent samples t-test or Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables were reported as numbers and percentages, and compared using χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test. HR-T2WI-based T stage diagnosis, along with DKI and DWI parameters that showed a P-value < 0.1 in univariable analysis, were entered into multivariable logistic regression analysis using the ‘Forward: Likelihood Ratio’ method. Those variables that yielded a P-values < 0.05 in the multivariable analysis were subsequently incorporated into the combined logistic regression model. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were used to analyze the diagnostic value of significant indicators. The area under the ROC curve (AUC) was calculated, with the cutoff value determined by the maximum Youden index. AUCs was compared using the DeLong test. The intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) was calculated to analyze the interobserver agreement for DKI and DWI parameters (ICC < 0.50, poor agreement; 0.50–0.75, moderate agreement; 0.76–0.90, good agreement; 0.91–1.00.91.00, excellent agreement) [21]. Interobserver agreement on the HR-T2WI diagnosed T stage was assessed using Kappa statistics (0–0.20.20 poor, 0.21–0.40 fair, 0.41–0.60 moderate, 0.61–0.80 substantial, and 0.81–1.00.81.00 perfect agreement). A two-tailed P value < 0.05 indicated statistical significance.

Results

Clinicopathological characteristics

All 138 enrolled patients underwent surgery within 2 weeks after MRI examination (median interval, 5 days; range, 0–13 days). The surgical procedures included total mesorectal excision (n = 124), transanal local excision (n = 11), and endoscopic submucosal dissection (n = 3). Out of 138 patients, including 13 patients of pT0 (adenoma with or without intraepithelial neoplasia), 3 patients of pTis (carcinoma in situ), 24 patients of pT1 (tumor invading the submucosa), and 98 patients of pT2 (tumor invading the muscularis propria), these patients were divided into two groups, namely pT0-T1 (n = 40) and pT2 (n = 98). Comparison of DKI and DWI parameters between pT0-T1 and pT2 rectal tumors

Diagnostic value of DKI and DWI parameters, and HR-T2WI in determining pT0-T1 rectal tumors

HR-T2WI, as well as DKI and DWI parameters, all had certain diagnostic value in differentiating pT0-T1 from pT2 rectal tumors (Table 4; Fig. 4). Among them, diffusivity demonstrated the highest diagnostic value, with an AUC of 0.810, a sensitivity of 70.0%, and a specificity of 82.7% when the cut-off value was set at 1.568 × 10−3 mm2/s. The AUCs for HR-T2WI, kurtosis, and ADC were 0.752, 0.729, and 0.785, respectively. The AUC of diffusivity was superior to those of HR-T2WI and kurtosis (P < 0.001 and P = 0.007, respectively), but there was no significant difference compared with ADC (P = 0.087).

Multivariable logistic regression analysis

Considering the results from the univariable analysis, HR-T2WI-based T stage diagnosis, along with DKI and DWI parameters that had P-values < 0.1, were selected for multivariable logistic regression analysis, which showed that the aforementioned parameters (HR-T2WI-based T stage diagnosis, kurtosis, diffusivity, and ADC) exhibited multicollinearity. After eliminating ADC, collinearity analysis showed that there was no multicollinearity among the variables. Multivariable logistic regression analysis revealed that HR-T2WI-based T stage diagnosis (P < 0.001) and diffusivity (P < 0.001) were associated with the detection of pT0-T1. Hence, the probability of pT0-T1 can be calculated using these two factors as follows: Logit (P) = −7.972 + 2.908 × 1 + 4.080 × 2 (X1, HR-T2WI-based T stage diagnosis [≥ mrT2: 0, mrT1: 1]; X2, diffusivity). The optimal cut-off value was determined to be greater than 0.271. By inputting the HR-T2WI-based T stage diagnosis and diffusivity into the equation, a result greater than 0.271 indicated pT0-T1 stage. The combined model achieved an AUC of 0.885, outperforming all other individual parameters (all P < 0.05), with a sensitivity of 77.5% and a specificity of 86.7% (Table 4; Fig. 4). The precision-recall curve analysis of the combined model yielded an area under the curve of 0.812 (Fig. 5), demonstrating the model’s robustness.

Subgroup analyses between pT1 and pT2 rectal tumors

The DKI and DWI parameters were compared between pT1 (n = 24) and pT2 (n = 98) rectal tumors. The results consistently showed that kurtosis was significantly lower in pT1 tumors than in pT2 tumors (0.881 vs. 0.950, P = 0.004), while diffusivity and ADC was significantly higher in pT1 tumors than in pT2 tumors (1.621 × 10−3 mm2/s vs. 1.368 × 10−3 mm2/s, P < 0.001; 1.210 × 10−3 mm2/s vs. 1.043 × 10−3 mm2/s, P < 0.001) (Supplementary Table 1). The AUC for diffusivity in distinguishing pT1 from pT2 was 0.772, which is very close to the AUC of 0.810 obtained in the original pT0-T1 versus pT2 analysis. The diagnostic performance of the combined model (HR-T2WI + diffusivity) also remained robust (AUC = 0.864), outperforming HR-T2WI, kurtosis, and ADC (P < 0.001, P = 0.004, P = 0.048, respectively), but not significantly higher than diffusivity (P = 0.063) (Supplementary Table 2, Supplementary Fig. 1).

Discussion

Accurate MRI-based staging of early rectal tumors is crucial for decision-making in the era of organ-sparing treatment strategies. Our results demonstrated that diffusivity (AUC = 0.810) exhibited the highest diagnostic value in differentiating pT0-T1 from pT2 rectal tumors among HR-T2WI, kurtosis, and ADC (AUC = 0.752, 0.729, and 0.785, respectively). Furthermore, the combination of HR-T2WI and diffusivity showed improved diagnostic value (AUC = 0.885, P = 0.027) compared to diffusivity.

Our study applied DKI to differentiate pT0-T1 from pT2 rectal tumors. The results revealed that pT2 rectal tumors exhibited markedly greater kurtosis compared to pT0-T1 rectal tumors (0.950 vs. 0.799, P < 0.001). This finding aligns with the study by Sun et al. [22], which also demonstrated elevated kurtosis in pT2 relative to pT1 rectal cancer. Kurtosis, the most specific parameter of DKI, serves as an indicator of tissue microstructure complexity and may reflect the aggressiveness of the tumor [12]. With the invasion deepening, an increase in cell density, variation of nuclear pleomorphism, and micronecrosis lead to a more complex microstructure, resulting in higher kurtosis at pT2 rectal cancer [22, 23]. Diffusivity is the value that has been adjusted for non-Gaussian bias, and it has a similar meaning to ADC. In our study, both diffusivity and ADC decreased as the tumor stage increased (diffusivity, 1.368 × 10−3 mm2/s vs. 1.732 × 10−3 mm2/s, P < 0.001; ADC, 1.043 × 10−3 mm2/s vs. 1.316 × 10−3 mm2/s, P < 0.001). This may be because the deeper the tumor infiltration, the more pronounced cell proliferation and tighter arrangement of cells, which restricts the diffusion of water molecules. Sun et al. [22] also reported reduced diffusivity and ADC in pT2 compared to pT1 rectal cancer, but there was no significant difference. Our study grouped adenomas, Tis, and T1 stages into one category, which might account for the observed differences in outcomes.

Our results showed that diffusivity maintained a greater diagnostic value in determining pT0-T1 rectal tumors, marginally higher than ADC (AUC, 0.810 vs. 0.785, P = 0.087). This advantage may arise because diffusivity, as a corrected form of ADC, provides a more accurate reflection of “true diffusion”, whereas ADC represents an “apparent” measure that is influenced by various confounding factors such as microstructural barriers and perfusion effects [12]. Consequently, diffusivity may offer more specific and biologically relevant information for differentiating early stages of rectal cancer. However, despite its high specificity (82.7%), diffusivity exhibited only moderate sensitivity (70.0%) in distinguishing pT0-T1 from pT2 rectal tumors. Although a previous study presented that HR-T2WI showed a high accuracy of 89%, with a sensitivity of 71% and specificity of 94% in detecting pT0-T1sm2 rectal tumors [24], this level of achievement was hardly reproduced by other centers (accuracy 49–67%, sensitivity 45–63%, and specificity 73–100%) [8, 25, 26] and the present study (accuracy 82.6%, sensitivity 57.5%, and specificity 92.9%) in determining pT0-T1, possibly due to its subjective evaluation and reliance on experience. However, diffusivity is a quantitative parameter, thus the results can be easily obtained by both experienced and inexperienced radiologists.

Nevertheless, diffusivity demonstrated higher sensitivity (70.0%) compared to HR-T2WI (57.5%), kurtosis (60.0%), and ADC (60.0%) in determining pT0-T1 rectal tumors. That is to say, it had the lowest overstaging rate (30%) in T2 staging, potentially reducing unnecessary total mesorectal excision. Notably, nearly 90% of patients with pT1 rectal cancer have N0 disease [7, 27], and the inaccuracy in MRI staging for nodal involvement [6] means that most patients preoperatively determined as T1, regardless of their lymph node status, are potentially curable with local resection. Importantly, the diagnostic performance for distinguishing pT0-T1 rectal tumors was significantly enhanced (AUC = 0.885) when HR-T2WI and diffusivity were integrated into a multivariable model, compared to the use of individual parameters alone (all P < 0.05). These findings indicate that although diffusivity provides additional diagnostic value in differentiating pT0-T1 from pT2 rectal tumors, it should be interpreted in combination with the lesion invasion depth observed on HR-T2WI for optimal accuracy.

There were some limitations in our study. Firstly, most patients with pT0-T1 rectal tumors underwent total mesorectal excision, primarily due to overstaging on HR-T2WI. This selection bias indicates that our patient cohort more accurately represents individuals with indeterminate staging—those for whom organ-preserving treatment cannot be safely recommended. Nevertheless, this underscores the clinical relevance of our study: by introducing DKI, we aimed to improve the accuracy of preoperative staging in early-stage rectal cancer. Secondly, there existed a significant disparity in the number of rectal tumors between stages pT0-T1 and pT2, potentially introducing bias into the results of the ROC and multivariable logistic regression analyses. Future research needs to further validate our findings in larger-scale and more balanced prospective cohorts. Thirdly, only the mean values of parameters derived from DKI and DWI were analyzed. Further enhancement of the diagnostic value of DKI may be achieved through histogram analysis. Fourthly, it is imperative to explore alternative diffusion models such as the intravoxel incoherent motion model and evaluate the optimization of b values for rectal DKI. Finally, this study was performed in a single center and the results should be validated across multiple centers.

Conclusions

The diffusivity from the DKI model exhibited potential value in identifying pT0-T1 rectal tumors. Furthermore, diagnostic performance was significantly enhanced by combining the results of HR-T2WI-based T-stage diagnosis, potentially increasing the rates of local excision.

Blind intubation through a SAD: Is it justifiable in modern airway management?
Authors: Nitin Choudhary, Anju Gupta, Nishkarsh Gupta
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We appreciate interesting research by Kaur et al.,[1] in which they compared blind orotracheal intubation (OTI) through laryngeal mask airway blockbuster (LMAB) and laryngeal mask airway protector (LMAP) in adults, but would like to highlight some concerns.

They did blind OTI via supraglottic airway devices (SADs), but airway guidelines have advocated flexible bronchoscope-guided ETI via SADs.[2] This is because of the poor rate of successful intubation and increased complications with the blind intubation technique via SADs.[2] The researchers have used the scoring system proposed by Brimacombe et al. to confirm the correct SAD placement via fiberoptic.[13] However, the scoring is done in the reverse format. The authors have described vocal cords fully visible as 1 and no laryngeal structures visible as 4, contrary to 4 being best and 1 worst as per description in the original article, which adds confusion.[3] While using SAD as an intubation conduit, Brimacombe grades 4 and 3 should be considered successful SAD placement; otherwise, they should be reinserted. Sadly, as mentioned earlier, the article fails to illuminate this factor, which has a major role while contemplating the success of blind OTI. Last, three attempts were allowed for SAD placement, but an insight into the troubleshooting used during the second and third attempts would have added significantly to the discussion.

Consequently, the study’s clinical relevance is also questionable, and avid readers should carefully interpret the results in the era of evidence-based medicine.

Highly efficient organic solar cells enabled by suppressing triplet exciton formation and non-radiative recombination
Authors: Congqi Li, Guo Yao, Xiaobin Gu, Jikai Lv, Yuqi Hou, Qijie Lin, Na Yu, Misbah Sehar Abbasi, Xin Zhang, Jianqi Zhang, Zheng Tang, Qian Peng, Chunfeng Zhang, Yunhao Cai, Hui Huang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe high non-radiative energy loss is a bottleneck issue that impedes the improvement of organic solar cells. The formation of triplet exciton is thought to be the main source of the large non-radiative energy loss. Decreasing the rate of back charge transfer is considered as an effective approach to alleviate the relaxation of the charge-transfer state and the triplet exciton generation. Herein, we develops an efficient ternary system based on D18:N3-BO:F-BTA3 by regulating the charge-transfer state disorder and the rate of back charge transfer of the blend. With the addition of F-BTA3, a well-defined morphology with a more condensed molecular packing is obtained. Moreover, a reduced charge-transfer state disorder is demonstrated in the ternary blend, which decreases the rate of back charge transfer as well as the triplet exciton formation, and therefore hinders the non-radiative recombination pathways. Consequently, D18:N3-BO:F-BTA3-based device produces a low non-radiative energy loss of 0.183 eV and a record-high efficiency of 20.25%. This work not only points towards the significant role of the charge-transfer state disorder on the suppression of triplet exciton formation and the non-radiative energy loss, but also provides a valuable insight for enhancing the performance of OSCs.Subject terms: Solar cells, Solar cells

Introduction

Benefiting from the innovations in molecular design and device engineering1–5, organic solar cells (OSCs) have undergone a substantial progress in the past decade6–9. However, their state-of-the-art efficiency still falls behind those of perovskite and inorganic solar cells10,11. The short-circuit current density (Jsc) and fill factor (FF) of OSCs are on par with those counterparts, while the open-circuit voltage (Voc) of OSCs is mediocre, which is imposed by the severe energy loss (Eloss)12,13. On the basis of the detailed balance theory, the Eloss of a cell can be divided into three parts: Eloss = ΔECT + ΔEr + ΔEnr. Specifically, the ΔECT is the energetic difference between the singlet excited state and the energy of charge-transfer (CT) state, which is necessary for exciton separation14. It has been proved that ΔECT can be as small as 0.05 eV for efficient exciton separation in OSCs15–17. ΔEr is the energy loss induced by radiative recombination, which is unavoidable in all types of solar cells. As for ΔEnr, originating from the non-radiative recombination, plays a key role in dictating the Voc. The ΔEnr of OSCs are mainly in the range of 0.2–0.3 eV, much higher than those of the inorganic or perovskite solar cells18,19. Therefore, to improve the photovoltaic performance of OSCs, it’s essential to identify and suppress the non-radiative loss pathways20–24.

A deep understanding of photophysical processes is of fundamental importance in reducing the ΔEnr of OSCs. Generally, singlet excitons (S1) are first generated by photoexcitation of the singlet ground state (S0). They then diffuse and dissociate across the donor-acceptor interface into freecharges. The free charges can form a 1:3 ratio of spin-singlet (1CT) and spin-triplet (3CT) charge-transfer excitons via spin-statistical non-geminate recombination25. These CT state excitons can re-dissociate into charges, which will contribute to the Jsc, or relax to lower states. The relaxation process has been identified as the main non-radiative recombination channel26,27. The recombination from the 1CT state to the triplet state (T1) is spin-forbidden. Nevertheless, in the context of 3CT states, the lower energies of T1 compared to those of 3CT in most non-fullerene acceptor-based OSCs facilitates the occurrence of back charge transfer, enabling relaxation between 3CT and the lowest energy T1 states28–30. In this regard, the restraint of 3CT to T1 is crucial for suppressing the non-radiative recombination. To alleviate this process, it’s rational to decrease the rate of back charge transfer (kBCT), since it can provide additional time for the re-dissociation of 3CT exciton, leading to less triplet exciton formation31. Several strategies have been proposed to decrease the kBCT, such as modifying the alignment between the state of T1 and 3CT29,32–35, enhancing the degree of 3CT state delocalization27,36–38, and promoting the hybridization between 3CT and T1 state31, etc. However, the CT state disorder (reflected by the CT state distribution) that can strongly affect kBCT, has been overlooked. In the highly ordered systems, the wavefunction of 3CT state is more delocalized, which will be more weakly coupled to the triplet excitons, leading to a lower kBCT39–42. Thus, decreasing the CT state disorder is considered as a facile strategy to effectively restrain the formation of triplet exciton and the ΔEnr of OSCs.

Herein, an efficient ternary system based on D18:N3-BO:F-BTA3 was developed to regulate the CT state disorder, kBCT and further the formation of triplet excitons inside OSCs. Photophysical and morphological studies revealed that a favorable morphology with more ordered packing was realized with the accommodation of F-BTA3, which benefits exciton splitting, charge transport, and suppresses charge recombination of the ternary device. In addition, the ternary system exhibited a weaker coupling between spin-triplet charge transfer and triplet states as well as a decreased CT state disorder, resulting in a lower kBCT and a less triplet exciton formation in comparison to D18:N3-BO system. Impressively, the non-radiative recombination pathway was effectually restrained, affording a low ΔEnr of 0.183 eV. As a result, D18:N3-BO:F-BTA3-based OSCs yielded a remarkable power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 20.25% (certified as 19.82%), representing the highest value for OSCs to date.

Results

The device fabrication and characterization

The chemical structures of the donor (D18) and acceptors (F-BTA3 and N3-BO) are shown in Fig. 1a. The molecular weight and the polydispersity index of D18 are 64.4 kDa and 1.83 (Supplementary Fig. 1), respectively. The synthetic route and the corresponding 1H and 13C nuclear magnetic resonance of N3-BO were shown in Supplementary Figs. 2–8. The UV–vis absorption spectra of the three components (D18, F-BTA3 and N3-BO) exhibit complementary absorption with the maximum absorption peak (λmax) at 543, 637 and 795 nm, respectively (Fig. 1b). In the ternary blend film (Fig. 1c), with the incorporation of F-BTA3, the absorption coefficient was enhanced, which is beneficial for the light harvesting and further attaining a high Jsc43. Figure 1d and Supplementary Fig. 9 presents the energy levels of D18, F-BTA3 and N3-BO estimated from cyclic voltammetry measurement. It was found that the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) energy levels of F-BTA3 and N3-BO are close, while the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) of F-BTA3 (−3.54 eV) is upshifted compared to that of N3-BO (−3.92 eV). The well-aligned energy levels formed by the three components could ensure an efficient exciton dissociation and charge transport inside the device. The contact angle measurement was employed to investigate the compatibility between two materials. As shown in Supplementary Fig. 10, the surface energy (γ) of D18, N3-BO and F-BTA3 are 21.36, 26.28 and 25.53 mN m−1, respectively. To investigate the photovoltaic performance of the combination, binary and ternary OSCs were fabricated with a conventional architecture of indium tin oxides (ITO)/2PACz/active layers/PNDIT-F3N/Ag. The host D18:N3-BO based device exhibited a PCE of 19.01%, with a Voc of 0.906 V, a Jsc of 26.45 mA cm−2, and an FF of 79.33%. The higher Voc of F-BTA3 based device than N3-BO based one is consistent with the order of their LUMO energy levels. Different amounts of F-BTA3 were then introduced into D18:N3-BO blend, with 10 wt% of the guest component, the Voc, Jsc and FF were simultaneously increased. The champion ternary device afforded an impressive PCE of 20.25%, with a high Voc of 0.924 V, a Jsc of 26.77 mA cm−2, and a prominent FF of 81.85%. To the best of our knowledge, the achieved PCE is the highest value reported for singe-junction OSCs to date. Such a device also obtained a certified PCE of 19.82% subject to the calibration procedures of Fujian, China Metrology Institute (Supplementary Fig. 11). The mechanistic studies of OSCs

To explain the achieved high performance, a series of characterizations were performed in sequence. The plots of photocurrent (Jph) as a function of the effective applied voltage (Veff) were firstly carried out to study the charge generation and dissociation process. As shown in Fig. 2c, the exciton dissociation (Pdiss) and charge collection (Pcoll) probability of the D18:N3-BO, D18:F-BTA3 and D18:N3-BO:F-BTA3 based devices were 97.68%/84.87%, 93.09%/80.25% and 98.60%/89.06% under the short-circuit condition and maximum power output conditions, respectively. These results suggest that the addition of F-BTA3 into the binary blend can facilitate exciton dissociation and charge generation. To investigate the charge recombination behavior, the plots of the light intensity (Plight) versus Jsc and Voc were then measured. According to the power-law equation Jsc ∝ (Plight)α, the determination of α can be used to express the extent of the bimolecular recombination in OSCs. As shown in Fig. 2d, the α value was 0.994 for the D18:N3-BO:F-BTA3 based device, higher than those of D18:N3-BO (0.990) and D18:F-BTA3 (0.987). Therefore, it can be inferred that the bimolecular recombination was suppressed with the introduction of the F-BTA3. The slopes βkT/q of the plot of Voc versus the natural logarithm of Plight were subsequently calculated to clarify the recombination mechanism. The β values of D18:N3-BO, D18:F-BTA3 and D18:N3-BO:F-BTA3 based devices were 1.122, 1.191 and 1.079, respectively, implying that a small amount of F-BTA3 in ternary blend can effectively deactivate traps in the host matrix and thereby decrease trap-assisted recombination (Fig. 2e).

The carrier mobilities were measured by the space charge limited current method (SCLC) to investigate the effect of F-BTA3 on the charge transport properties (Supplementary Fig. 12). The electron (μe) and hole mobilities (μh) of D18:N3-BO, D18:F-BTA3 and D18:N3-BO:F-BTA3 blends were estimated to be 3.40 ×10−4/3.75 ×10−4 cm2 V−1 s−1, 1.58 ×10−4/2.06 ×10−4 cm2 V−1 s−1 and 4.42 ×10−4/4.63 ×10−4 cm2 V−1 s−1, respectively. The photon-induced charge-carrier extraction in linearly increasing voltage (photo-CELIV) method was also conducted to compare the mobilities of the binary and ternary devices (Fig. 2f). The charge carrier mobility values of D18:N3-BO, D18:F-BTA3 and D18:N3-BO:F-BTA3 were 1.5 ×10−4, 6.0 ×10−5 and 1.7 ×10−4 cm2 V−1 s−1, respectively. The more balanced charge transport (μh/μe = 1.05) and improved charge transfer contribute to the higher FF and Jsc of D18:N3-BO:F-BTA3 based device.

The charge generation dynamics in the blend films was studied by the femtosecond transient absorption (TA) spectroscopy. The N3-BO was selectively excited by a beam of 800 nm pump pulse (2 μJ/cm2) for D18:N3-BO and D18:N3-BO:F-BTA3 blends. The 2D color plots of TA spectra of the D18:N3-BO and D18:N3-BO:F-BTA3 films are shown in Supplementary Fig. 13. After excitation, the ground state bleaching (GSB) and excited-state absorption (ESA) of N3-BO at around 830 and 890 nm immediately appeared, which decayed rapidly within 10 ps. As the N3-BO bleach peak (830 nm) decayed, the D18 GSB peak at ~592 nm rose, suggesting the hole-transfer process from N3-BO to D18. To evaluate the rate of hole transfer processes in the blend films, the kinetics at 600 nm were extracted (Supplementary Fig. 14 and Supplementary Table 2). The traces of the hole transfer processes within 100 ps in the D18:N3-BO and D18:N3-BO:F-BTA3 films were fitted using the biexponential functions with a fast component (τ1) of 2.09 and 1.43 ps, respectively, which represents the ultrafast exciton dissociation process, and a slow component (τ2) of 23.85 and 23.80 ps, which is assigned to the diffusion process of the charges to the interface before collection. The faster τ1 and τ2 of the ternary film demonstrated the more efficient charge generation and charge transfer rate in the D18:N3-BO:F-BTA3 blend, which matches well with the higher Jsc and PCE of the ternary devices.

Atomic force microscopy (AFM) and transmission electronic microscopy (TEM) measurements were used to investigate the surface morphology of the three blends. As illustrated in Fig. 3a and Supplementary Fig. 15, a fibrillar network morphology was observed in all three blend films, which is advantageous to efficient charge transport. Besides, with the introduction of F-BTA3 into D18:N3-BO, the root-mean-square roughness of the D18:N3-BO blend was slightly increased from 0.99 to 1.21 nm for the ternary blend. As shown in Supplementary Fig. 16, after adding F-BTA3 into D18:N3-BO film, the fibrillar morphology in ternary film is more obvious, which is beneficial to efficient exciton dissociation and charge transport. Grazing incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS) measurement was then performed to investigate the molecular ordering and packing behavior of the pristine and blend films. The 2D GIWAXS patterns and the corresponding line-cut intensities taken along the in-plane (IP) and out-of-plane (OOP) direction are shown in Supplementary Fig. 17 and Fig. 3b, c, and the quantitative analysis results are summarized in Supplementary Tables 3–6, respectively. In detail, both N3-BO and N3-BO:F-BTA3 films adopted the preferred face-on orientation, evidenced by the strong (010) diffraction peaks in the OOP at 1.70 and 1.71 Å−1 and lamellar (100) stacking peaks in the IP direction at 0.40 and 0.41 Å−1, respectively. The calculated crystal coherence length (CCL) values of the π − π diffraction peaks in the OOP direction for N3-BO and N3-BO:F-BTA3 are 22.52 and 24.17 Å, respectively, implying that the incorporation of F-BTA3 endows N3-BO with a better crystallinity and higher degree of molecular ordering. As exhibited in Fig. 3b, c, the binary and ternary blend films also adopted a preferential face-on stacking pattern as signified by the obvious (010) reflections in OOP and (100) peaks in the IP direction. The stacking distance (d) and CCL of the blend films were quantitatively calculated as well. Intriguingly, it was found that the addition of the guest acceptor leads to a smaller d-spacing of the ternary blend, indicative of a more compact molecular packing. Besides, with the addition of F-BTA3, the CCL associated with the π-π stacking was improved from 21.15 to 23.19 Å, while the lamellar stacking peaks were increased from 99.73 to 101.34 Å. This observation manifests that the introduction of F-BTA3 promotes the crystallization of the blend and leads to a more ordered stacking, which could aid in obtaining a lower CT state disorder of the ternary blend.

The energy loss analysis of OSCs

To understand the reason for the higher Voc delivered by the ternary blend, the Eloss in both binary and ternary devices were analyzed (Table 2). By the intersection of the normalized reduced absorption and emission spectra (Supplementary Fig. 18), the optical bandgap (Eg) of D18:N3-BO, D18:F-BTA3 and D18:N3-BO:F-BTA3 were estimated to be 1.456, 1.828 and 1.458 eV, respectively. Besides, the ECT values of the three blend films were determined by employing highly sensitive EQE and electroluminescence (EL). As shown in Fig. 4a, b, Supplementary Fig. 19, and Table 2, the ΔECT values of D18:N3-BO, D18:F-BTA3 and D18:N3-BO:F-BTA3 based devices were calculated to be 0.085, 0.118 and 0.081 eV, respectively, suggesting the lowest exciton dissociation driving force of the ternary devices. In addition, the ΔEr for D18:N3-BO, D18:F-BTA3 and D18:N3-BO:F-BTA3-based OSCs were confirmed to be 0.270, 0.326 and 0.270 eV, respectively. Furthermore, ΔEnr can be calculated from

In order to explore the origin of low ΔEnr of the ternary OSCs, systematic characterizations were carried out subsequently. Considering the significant impact of the triplet exciton formation on ΔEnr, the kinetics of triplet exciton behavior were investigated by TA spectroscopy. The triplet sensitization measurements were first performed to determine the excited-state absorption of the triplet excitons. As shown in Supplementary Figs. 20 and 21, the growth of photo-induced absorption (PIA) at 1450 nm exceeds a few picoseconds, with a duration over nanoseconds, which was confirmed to be the T1 of N3-BO. Then, the TA in the infrared spectral region was mainly studied. The 2D color plots and the corresponding 1D plots of TA are shown in Supplementary Fig. 13 and Fig. 5a, b. For the D18:N3-BO and D18:N3-BO:F-BTA3-based films, the signals at 1600 nm appeared after excitation, which were attributed to the intermolecular charge transfer state absorption of N3-BO. With the charge transfer between the donor and acceptor, the PIA signals at 890 (ESA of N3-BO) and 1600 nm gradually disappeared. In addition, a new PIA peak at 1450 nm arose from about 80 ps, which matches well with the sensitization results, indicating the triplet exciton formation in the blend films. The kinetics at 1450 nm were extracted while normalizing the amplitude of triplet state to that of charge-separated (CS) state in each of the corresponding samples (Fig. 5c, d). It is evident that D18:N3-BO:F-BTA3 film possesses a smaller and lower slope of the signal than D18:N3-BO film, indicating fewer and slower triplet excitons generation in the film of D18:N3-BO:F-BTA3. These results are consistent with the result when donors are excited (Supplementary Figs. 22 and 23), both revealing that the ternary film possesses a less amount and a lower rate of the triplet exciton formation, which are well in line with the lowest ΔEnr and the highest Voc achieved in the ternary device.

The degrees of energetic disorder of the CT state were then quantified by electrical methods for the devices. The charge extraction (CE) measurements at open-circuit as a function of light intensity (Supplementary Fig. 24) were then utilized to explore the deeper traps in the devices, where the signal is below the resolution of the optical measurements. According to the Shockley–Read–Hall distribution of the density of states, the disorder of the CT state can be quantified by the equation of n ∝ exp(∆Ef/2Ech), where n is the charge carrier density, Ech is the tail state characterization energy, and ∆Ef is the quasi-Fermi level splitting (equating to Voc)46,47. As shown in Fig. 6a and Supplementary Fig. 25, ten independent devices of D18:N3-BO and D18:F-BTA3 systems exhibited 44.85 ± 0.96 and 123.97 ± 24.98 meV values of Ech. Impressively, the D18:N3-BO:F-BTA3 blend films displayed a sharper increase of charge carrier density versus Voc, yielding the lower Ech of 38.75 ± 1.81 meV, indicative of a less charge accumulation in the tail state and less disorder. The density of state (DoS) of the trap state distribution was further probed by using Mott–Schottky characteristics48, the corresponding results are shown in Fig. 6b and Supplementary Fig. 26, in which D18:N3-BO and D18:F-BTA3 films possess widely distributed DoS with the energetic disorder of 36.95 ± 0.41 and 37.68 ± 0.30 meV, while the ternary film exhibited a reduced DoS and a limited disorder of 36.28 ± 0.48 meV. These findings reveal that the D18:N3-BO:F-BTA3 blend possesses the lowest energetic disorder of the charge transfer state, which contributes to the effective suppression of ΔEnr.

Discussion

In summary, highly efficient ternary OSCs based on D18:N3-BO:F-BTA3 were successfully constructed by effectively suppressing the ΔEnr. It was demonstrated that the introduction of the guest component leads to a well-defined morphology, a balanced charge transport as well as an efficient charge transfer. More importantly, the CT state disorder and kBCT of the ternary blend have been decreased with the addition of F-BTA3, which helps restrain the relaxation of the CT state, the triplet excitons generation, and further the ΔEnr of OSCs. As a result, a record-high efficiency of 20.25% (certified as 19.82%), accompanied with a high Voc of 0.924 V, was achieved by endowment of a very low ΔEnr of 0.183 eV. This work shed light on the impact of CT state disorder on the triplet exciton formation and energy loss of OSCs, paving a promising avenue for OSCs to break the current efficiency bottleneck.

Methods

Materials

All reagents were purchased from Innochem, J&K, or Energy Chemical unless specified and used as received. D18, F-BTA3 and F3N were purchased from Solarmer Materials Inc. PEDOT:PSS (CLEVIOS PVP AI 4083) was provided by Heraeus.

General methods

1H and 13C NMR spectra were recorded on a JEOL JNM-ECZ500R (500 MHz) spectrometer with CDCl3 as a solvent and tetramethylsilane as an internal reference. Absorption spectra and photoluminescence spectra were measured on an Agilent Cary 60 UV−vis spectrophotometer and Edinburgh steady state/transient fluorescence spectrometer (FLS1000), respectively. For steady-state and transient UV–vis, and photoluminescence measurements, the neat or blend films were all prepared on quartz substrates. For GIWAXS measurements, the neat and blend films were prepared on the silicon substrate.

Device fabrication

The solar cells were fabricated with a conventional device structure of ITO/2PACz/active layer/PNDIT-F3N/Ag. The ITO-coated glasses were ultrasonic precleaned with detergent, deionized water, acetone and 2-propanol for 30 min each and dried by a nitrogen blow. The ITO glasses were treated with UV–ozone for 20 min before use. 2PACz was spin-coated onto the ITO substrate and then annealed in an oven for 5 min at 100 °C. The mixed D18:N3-BO, D18:N3-BO and D18:N3-BO:F-BTA3 with the D:A ratio of 1:1.4, 1:1.4 and 1:1.26:0.14 were dissolved in chloroform (CF) solvent at a total weight concentration of 10 mg mL−1 and stirred at least 1 h. After the active layer solutions were spin-coated, thermal annealing was utilized at 100 °C for 5 min. Next, the electron transport layer PNDIT-F3N was spin-coated on the films. Finally, about 110 nm Ag was deposited onto the active layer by evaporating at a pressure under 10−5 Pa.

Device characterization

The cells were characterized under a temperature of 25–30 °C in a glove box filled with nitrogen. The J–V characteristics of photovoltaic devices were obtained along the forward scan direction from −0.5 to 1 V, with a scan step of 20 mV and a dwell time of 1 ms using a Keithley 2400 source-measure unit. The photocurrent was measured under illumination simulated 100 mW cm−2 AM1.5 G irradiation using a Newport solar simulator. Simulator irradiance was characterized using a calibrated spectrometer and illumination intensity was set using a certified silicon diode (SRC-2020, Enlitech). The effective area of the cells was 5 mm2 and was measured by the photon mask (3.164 mm2). EQE values of the devices were measured using a QE-R3011 instrument (Enli Technology Co. Ltd., Taiwan) with a scan increment of 5 nm per point.

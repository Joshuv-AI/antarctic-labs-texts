Regarding “A concise review of veterinary forensic medicine in Japan: current status and perspectives”
Authors: Jiayi Chen
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

I read with great interest the recent review by Nakamura, “A concise review of veterinary forensic medicine in Japan: current status and perspectives”1. This article offers a timely and comprehensive overview of veterinary forensic medicine and underscores its growing significance within Japan’s evolving legal, social, and scientific frameworks.

The author effectively demonstrates how recent legislative changes—most notably the 2019 amendment to the Act on Welfare and Management of Animals—have generated an urgent need for veterinarians to engage in forensic investigations of animal abuse and neglect. The review clearly illustrates how veterinary forensic medicine extends well beyond conventional diagnostic pathology, demanding rigorous documentation, evidence preservation, and interdisciplinary collaboration. This distinction is particularly valuable in delineating the divergent objectives of diagnostic versus forensic necropsies, a topic that remains inadequately recognized in routine veterinary practice.

Especially noteworthy is the emphasis placed on the role of veterinary and toxicological pathologists in identifying subtle lesions, distinguishing postmortem changes from antemortem injuries, and providing objective findings to support legal decision-making. The review further identifies critical systemic challenges in Japan, including the shortage of trained specialists, inadequate forensic necropsy infrastructure, and insufficient coordination among veterinarians, law enforcement agencies, and governmental agencies—collectively underscoring the necessity of structured education and institutional investment in this field.

This article functions both as an informative review and as a call to action. Developing formal educational programs, strengthening interdisciplinary networks, and expanding access to toxicological and forensic resources represent essential steps toward advancing veterinary forensic medicine in Japan. I believe this review is likely to stimulate meaningful discussion and further research, ultimately contributing to enhanced animal welfare, public health, and the administration of justice.

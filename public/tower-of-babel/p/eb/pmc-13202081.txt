Study protocol for the effect of intraoperative subanaesthetic dose esketamine on the incidence of postoperative depressive symptoms in patients with breast cancer: a multicentre, prospective, randomised controlled trial
Authors: Zi-Yi Li, Jie Wang, Jia-Shuo Yang, Xin Xu, Fei Wang, Hui-Xian Li, Hui Zheng, Yi Fang, Shuang Ma, Tao Yan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPostoperative depressive symptoms are common after breast cancer surgery and can adversely affect recovery and quality of life. This multicentre trial aims to determine whether a single intraoperative subanaesthetic dose of esketamine, as an adjunct to antidepressant therapy, improves postoperative depressive outcomes at postoperative day (POD) 30.Methods and analysisThis multicentre, prospective, randomised, triple-blind, placebo-controlled trial will enrol 824 women aged 18–80 years with stage I–III breast cancer (American Society of Anesthesiologists physical status I–III) who are scheduled to undergo surgery. Participants will be randomised 1:1 to receive 0.2 mg/kg esketamine or an equivalent volume of normal saline after anaesthesia induction and before surgical incision. The primary outcome is the incidence of depressive symptoms at POD 30, assessed using the Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale Depression (score ≥8). Secondary outcomes include acute and chronic pain, and anxious symptoms, etc. Primary analysis will use a generalised linear mixed model with a logit link on an intention-to-treat basis.Ethics and disseminationThe study protocol has been formally approved by the institutional ethics committee of the National Cancer Center (Approval No.25/483-5429). Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants prior to enrolment. Results will be disseminated through peer-reviewed journals and international scientific conferences.Trial registration numberChiCTR2600117573.

Introduction

Breast cancer is among the most common malignancies among women worldwide. According to cancer statistics, breast cancer ranks among the most prevalent malignancies worldwide, accounting for 24.5% of all newly diagnosed cancers in women globally1 and 15.6% in Chinese women.2 Surgery serves as a cornerstone of multimodal breast cancer treatment, effectively removing the tumour burden. However, it also substantially increases the risk of perioperative negative emotions.3 Emerging evidence indicates that postoperative adjuvant endocrine agents, such as aromatase inhibitors, may exacerbate depressive symptoms in a subset of patients with breast cancer.4 Notably, roughly one in four women experience clinically significant depressive symptoms after breast cancer surgery.5 These depressive symptoms directly impair emotional well-being and overall quality of life, manifesting as disruptions in fundamental physiological functions such as sleep and appetite. Such disruptions can, in turn, compromise a patient’s adherence to essential postoperative adjuvant therapy and rehabilitation, collectively contributing to an unfavourable prognosis,6 thereby posing a major challenge in the overall management of patients with breast cancer.

The mechanisms underlying postoperative depressive symptoms after breast cancer surgery are complex and may involve physiological stress induced by surgical trauma, as well as endocrine therapy, chemotherapy and radiotherapy,710 in addition to psychosocial factors related to breast loss and altered body image.1113 Conventional antidepressants, such as selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors,14 are often limited by a delayed onset of action, adverse effects and questionable suitability for transient surgery-induced depressive states.15 16 Esketamine, a rapid-acting N-methyl-D-aspartate (NMDA) receptor antagonist, has emerged as a promising alternative option for postoperative depression management by enhancing synaptic plasticity and suppressing neuroinflammation.1720 Crucially, unlike regimens for chronic psychiatric illnesses that require repeated dosing, our strategy operates as a targeted preventive modality via the early modulation of the perioperative neuroinflammation and stress response.21 By administering a single subanaesthetic dose immediately prior to surgical trauma, esketamine pre-emptively blunts the acute inflammatory cascade and promotes structural synaptogenesis.22 Because surgery is a discrete stressor, truncating this initial inflammatory peak allows normal homeostatic recovery, providing a robust biological rationale for sustained antidepressant effects.23 Furthermore, restricting administration strictly to the intraoperative period under general anaesthesia effectively masks esketamine’s potential psychotomimetic adverse events (AEs), ensuring optimal safety.24

The clinical validity of this single-dose, long-term outcome strategy is strongly supported by recent trials in obstetrical anaesthesia, where the acute physiological stress of childbirth closely mirrors surgical trauma. High-quality evidence demonstrates that a single, postpartum subanaesthetic dose of esketamine (0.2 mg/kg) reduces the incidence of major depressive episodes by 74% at 42 days post partum, confirming that an early, discrete intervention independently sustains psychiatric remission well beyond 30 days.25 This strategy can be rationally extrapolated to breast cancer from a biological perspective. Obstetrical and oncological surgeries trigger similar psychoneuroimmunoendocrinological stress responses. The resultant hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis dysregulation and acute increase in pro-inflammatory cytokines collectively underpin the common neuropathological pathways of depression.26 27 Building on this biological rationale, emerging pilot trials have begun to explore esketamine’s utility specifically within breast cancer cohorts. For instance, a recent randomised trial demonstrated that intraoperative esketamine significantly reduced early postoperative depression scores, with an absolute risk difference of −10.8% (p=0.018).28 Another trial also reported substantial short-term improvements in subthreshold depressive symptoms following mastectomy (effect size=0.544),29 consistent with recent meta-analyses confirming significant reductions in depression scores at postoperative days 3 and 7.30

However, existing esketamine research has largely focused on treatment-resistant depression and obstetrical populations, and high-quality evidence for preventing postoperative depressive symptoms in surgical patients with breast cancer remains limited. Most prior studies have been limited to single-centre designs with small sample sizes and brief follow-up periods, leaving a critical lack of high-quality evidence to comprehensively characterise medium-term and long-term trajectories of postoperative depressive symptoms.29 To definitively address this gap and validate this targeted preventive strategy, we designed a large-scale, multicentre, triple-blind, randomised controlled trial to evaluate the long-term impact of intraoperative esketamine on postoperative depressive symptoms.

The primary aim of this study is to compare the effects of intraoperative administration of a subanaesthetic dose of esketamine with placebo on the incidence of postoperative depressive symptoms at postoperative day (POD) 30 in patients undergoing breast cancer surgery. The secondary objectives are to evaluate the effects of this intervention on postoperative acute and chronic pain, longer-term depressive and anxious symptoms and postoperative outcomes; to investigate the relationship between pain and depressive symptoms; to examine the associations between perioperative treatment strategies and postoperative depressive symptoms; and to assess the incidence of perioperative AEs.

Patient and public involvement

No patients or members of the public were involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination planning of this research.

Participants

We will recruit 824 female patients who are undergoing surgical treatment for breast cancer according to the inclusion and exclusion criteria. We plan to enrol the first patient on 1 February 2026 and finish recruiting on 1 February 2027.

Inclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria are as follows: (1) female patients aged 18–80 years; (2) newly diagnosed stage I–III breast cancer; (3) American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) physical status I–III; (4) scheduled to undergo breast cancer surgery with no prior breast cancer-related surgical treatment. Patients with a history of surgery for benign breast disease may also be considered eligible if postoperative pathology confirms no malignancy; for such patients, prior treatments must be documented in detail in the case report form (CRF); (5) patients who understand the objectives and comply with the follow-up procedures; and (6) patients willing to participate and provide written informed consent.

Exclusion criteria

The exclusion criteria are as follows: (1) a history of other malignancies; (2) treatment for depression within the past 2 months or a history of psychiatric disorders, including depression, anxiety disorders and personality disorders; (3) pregnancy or lactation; (4) allergy to or contraindications for ketamine or esketamine; and (5) an inability to participate in the study, including those who are currently participating in other clinical studies that may affect the outcomes of this study, or situations in which the investigational drug may increase the risks of perioperative anaesthesia.

Dropout criteria

The dropout criteria are as follows: (1) patients who withdraw informed consent; (2) patients who are lost to follow-up; and (3) patients who are unable to complete the entire clinical trial for other reasons. The detailed reasons for withdrawal will be recorded in CRF.

Randomisation and blinding

This study will use stratified block randomisation with the study centre as the stratification factor. An independent statistician will generate a randomisation list using SAS software, with a fixed block size of 4. Eligible patients will be randomised at a 1:1 ratio to the esketamine group (E group) or the normal saline group (N group). Allocation concealment will be ensured by independent management of the randomisation list by a third party. An independent anaesthesia nurse will prepare the study medications (esketamine or normal saline) according to the randomisation results, and then the medications will be distributed to independent attending anaesthesiologists. It is impossible to distinguish esketamine from normal saline because both are colourless and transparent solutions. Patients, anaesthesiologists, surgeons, investigators responsible for data collection and outcome assessment and statisticians will be blinded to the group assignments before and at the time of enrolment, thereby minimising selection bias. Emergency unblinding is strictly permitted only in life-threatening situations. Every unblinding event must be formally documented with the exact time and clinical justification.

Given that subanaesthetic NMDA antagonists are well-documented to induce transient psychoactive effects, the integrity of the trial’s triple-blind design was prespecified for objective quantification. At POD 30 follow-up, both participants and independent outcome assessors will be instructed to complete a treatment allocation guess, with three optional responses: ‘Esketamine’, ‘Normal Saline’ or ‘Don’t Know’. The success of the blinding procedure will be statistically evaluated using Bang’s Blinding Index, where an index ranging from −1 to 1 will be calculated per arm; values tightly clustering between −0.2 and 0.2 will indicate successful preservation of blinding.32 James’ Blinding Index will simultaneously be calculated to assess the overall trial unblinding landscape.

Anaesthesia and study interventions

Before anaesthesia induction, patients will undergo preoxygenation at a fraction of inspired O2 of 100% for 5 min with a face mask. Anaesthesia will be induced with propofol (1.5–2.0 mg/kg), sufentanil (0.3–0.5 µg/kg) and rocuronium (0.6 mg/kg). After anaesthesia induction, dexamethasone (5 mg) and ondansetron (4 mg) will be given to prevent postoperative nausea and vomiting. After endotracheal intubation, the lungs will be mechanically ventilated (tidal volume of 6–8 mL/kg, frequency of 12–18 breaths per minute and inspired oxygen fraction of 50–80%) according to clinical practice. Ventilation will be adjusted to maintain the peripheral oxygen saturation ≥95% and the end-tidal carbon dioxide partial pressure at 35–40 mm Hg.

For the administration of the study medications, patients will receive intravenous injections of 0.2 mg/kg esketamine or an equivalent volume of normal saline (volume-matched), after anaesthesia induction and before surgical incision.

Anaesthesia will be maintained with 1.5–3% sevoflurane inhalation targeting a minimal alveolar concentration of 0.7–1.3 and remifentanil at 0.1–0.2 µg/kg/min, with the Bispectral Index maintained between 40 and 60. Before the surgical incision is made, an additional dose of sufentanil (0.2 µg/kg) will be administered. During surgery, rocuronium (5–10 mg) will be supplemented every 30 min. During the maintenance phase, the remifentanil infusion rate will be titrated to maintain a heart rate of 60–100 beats per minute and mean arterial pressure within ±20% of the preoperative baseline; vasoactive agents such as atropine, dopamine and ephedrine will be administered as needed for correction. After surgery, the neuromuscular blockade will be reversed with sugammadex (2–4) mg/kg according to the instructions, and patients will be extubated and transferred to a post-anaesthesia care unit. Patients with a modified Aldrete score ≥9 will be discharged to their surgical ward.

Study outcomes

The primary outcome of this study is the incidence of depressive symptoms at POD 30, assessed using the Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale for Depression (HADS-D). The HADS instrument excludes somatic symptoms to minimise confounding effects related to surgical recovery. A predefined cut-off score of ≥8 will be adopted to identify clinically significant depressive symptoms. This threshold is justified by comprehensive meta-analyses in oncology populations demonstrating that an HADS-D score of ≥8 maximises combined sensitivity (74%) and specificity (84%) for detecting depressive disorders.33

The secondary outcomes include: (1) the incidence of depressive symptoms before surgery and at PODs 7, 90 and 180, as assessed using the HADS-D; (2) the change-from-baseline in HADS-D scores at POD 30; (3) the incidence of anxiety symptoms before surgery and at PODs 7, 30, 90 and 180, as assessed using the Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale for Anxiety; (4) the incidence of acute pain within 48 hours after surgery, as assessed using the Numerical Rating Scale (NRS); (5) the incidence of chronic pain at PODs 90 and 180, as assessed using the NRS; (6) the effects of perioperative management (including preoperative neoadjuvant therapy, postoperative adjuvant therapy and surgical/anaesthetic procedures) on postoperative depression, anxiety and pain; (7) the incidence of AEs, including nausea, vomiting, pruritus and respiratory depression; and (8) oncological outcomes, including overall survival and disease-free survival between groups.

The NRS is an 11-point scale for patient self-reported pain. It uses an NRS from 0 to 10. Scores range from 0 to 10 (0, no pain; 1–3, mild pain; 4–6, moderate pain; and 7–10, severe pain).34

Data collection and follow-up

Data collection in this study will cover the baseline, intraoperative and postoperative follow-up periods. All assessments will be conducted at prespecified time points using standardised instruments. Research staff responsible for collecting data will be blinded to group allocation to ensure objective assessment.

Baseline data will include demographic characteristics, including age, sex, height and weight, educational level, household income, HADS scores, preoperative diagnosis, comorbidities, preoperative medications and history of smoking and alcohol use.

Intraoperative data will include tumour type and stage, surgical approach, operative duration, ASA physical status, anaesthesia and analgesia techniques and the type and dose of anaesthetic agents.

Patients will be assessed daily during PODs 1 and 2 for acute pain using the NRS. Telephone follow-up will be conducted at PODs 7, 30, 90 and 180. The data collected will include AEs, the incidence of depressive symptoms and the incidence of anxiety symptoms at all time points, as well as chronic pain NRS scores and perioperative adjuvant therapy data collected by telephone at PODs 90 and 180. If any special surgical circumstances or intraoperative complications occur, their occurrence, duration and supporting evidence will be documented.

All outcome measures will be recorded during and after the CRF assessment. Each enrolled subject will be assigned a unique identification code based on their patient sequence number. After the measurements are complete, the study data will be imported into the electronic data capture system.

Adverse events

All AEs observed by the investigators or spontaneously reported by participants will be recorded. AEs possibly related to the study intervention and other factors may occur during the study, including hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, bradycardia, respiratory depression, dissociative anaesthesia and hallucinations. Serious adverse events are defined as any unforeseen serious medical event, such as persistent functional impairment, disability, life-threatening events or death. Once any AE is identified, it should be reported immediately to the principal investigator. The principal investigator will complete a detailed AE report form within 24 hours, documenting the time of onset, clinical manifestations, management and outcomes, and report the event to the central ethics committee and the institutional office. The study team will periodically summarise and analyse the AEs, assess their relationships with the intervention, and, if necessary, promptly amend the protocol to ensure the ongoing safety of the participants.

Sample size calculation

Previous studies suggest that approximately 20–30% of women with breast cancer experience depressive symptoms or worsening depression during treatment35; therefore, we assumed a postoperative depressive symptom rate of 25%. This trial is powered to detect a clinically meaningful absolute risk reduction (ARR). Recent meta-analyses report that perioperative esketamine yields an absolute risk difference of 6.0%–9.0% for postoperative depression.36 Furthermore, recent randomised controlled trials specific to breast cancer surgery demonstrate that a single intraoperative dose of esketamine effectively reduces the risk of postoperative depression.28 29

Based on this evidence, we hypothesised that subanaesthetic esketamine would achieve a relative risk reduction of 33.3% (a one-third reduction) in this cohort. Consequently, the targeted ARR is mathematically calculated as 8.33%, which falls perfectly within the 6.0%–9.0% absolute risk difference range reported in recent meta-analyses.

To detect this difference with a two-sided significance level of α=0.05 and 80% power using a continuity-corrected χ2 test, 370 participants per group are required. Allowing for a 10% loss to follow-up, the final planned sample size is 824 participants in total (412 per group). To account for statistical uncertainty, sensitivity scenarios were modelled. If the true ARR is smaller than expected (eg, 6.0%), the evaluable sample of 740 patients would yield 50.1% power. However, an ARR of 6.0% remains clinically valuable. Alternatively, if the cohort’s baseline incidence is lower at 20.0%, detecting an 8.33% ARR would require only 303 patients per group, providing our planned sample of 370 per group with 88.0% power. Thus, the planned sample size provides a robust balance between statistical resilience and clinical feasibility.

Analyses

All statistical analyses will be performed with SPSS V.25.0 (IBM SPSS, USA) and the R software package by statisticians in the Office of Cancer Screening of the National Cancer Center. The statistical analysis plan will be finalised before database lock and unblinding.

Analysis sets

A full analysis set will include all eligible patients randomly assigned to either group with at least one follow-up evaluation. The per-protocol analysis set will include all patients meeting the eligibility criteria of the study with at least one follow-up evaluation after randomisation and a treatment compliance rate of at least 80%. The safety set will involve all randomised patients with at least one safety assessment after randomisation.

Baseline data

Baseline characteristics will be summarised by treatment group. Between-group comparisons will be performed using appropriate statistical tests: Student’s t-test for normally distributed continuous variables, the Wilcoxon rank-sum test for non-normally distributed continuous variables and the χ² test or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables, as appropriate. P values will be reported for descriptive purposes; however, no inference regarding the success of randomisation will be made. Prespecified covariate-adjusted analyses will be conducted as sensitivity analyses to assess robustness.

Outcome analysis

The primary analysis will be conducted in the full analysis set on an intention-to-treat basis. The incidence of depressive symptoms at POD 30 will be compared between groups using a generalised linear mixed model (GLMM) with a logit link. To meticulously control for baseline confounding, the primary model will explicitly adjust for the preoperative baseline HADS-D score as a continuous covariate. The treatment group will serve as a fixed effect, with the study centre as a random intercept.

Crucially, adjuvant oncological treatments including chemotherapy, radiotherapy and endocrine therapy act as major independent drivers of depression for medium-term and long-term secondary outcomes at postoperative days 90 and 180. Given the substantial confounding risk these treatments pose to the treatment effect evaluation, a rigorous adjustment strategy has been prespecified. The GLMM applied to 90-day and 180-day outcomes will incorporate the receipt of individual adjuvant therapies as time-varying categorical covariates. Furthermore, prespecified subgroup analyses and stratification will be conducted based on the precise type of adjuvant therapy received. This analytical framework ensures that the enduring neuroprotective effect of esketamine can be statistically isolated from the depressogenic toxicity of subsequent oncological care. The model will yield adjusted risk ratios with 95% CIs, setting significance at α=0.05.

AEs will be summarised descriptively and compared between groups using χ² or Fisher’s exact tests, as appropriate. Prespecified subgroup analyses will examine effect modification by age group; interaction terms will be used for hypothesis testing. Sensitivity analyses will include per-protocol analyses.

Data monitoring

No interim analyses are planned, and no formal stopping guidelines will be applied; if unexpected safety concerns arise, only the independent Data Monitoring Committee (DMC) will have access to unblinded interim data and may recommend early termination, with the final decision made by the principal investigator. Trial conduct will be monitored by the coordinating centre through scheduled remote checks and periodic on-site audits, with protocol deviations documented and addressed promptly.

Data management

Before recruitment, a password-protected electronic record will be established to store the details of the patients. Their data will be encoded and identified by specific identification numbers that will be assigned. We will designate a trained investigator who is independent of the study to collect the research data and complete the case reports. The informed consent forms and the research data will be recorded in the corresponding electronic medical records, and the signed paper documents will be stored in a locked room in the hospital. Protected data sets are backed up periodically (every 3 months) and synchronously audited by an independent data administrator. They can only obtain the key for the identification codes during the study. The lead investigator will record and protect the data according to the guidelines at the end of the study. Once recruitment and follow-up are complete, data have been collected, entered and reviewed, and all data queries have been resolved, the data set will be locked. To help researchers understand and repeat the results, all data will be archived for 10 years after the study is completed. The details of the patient’s identity will not be reported in any publication.

Study status

At the time of manuscript submission, the study has obtained full ethical approval and patient recruitment is currently ongoing. Recruitment commenced on 1 February 2026, and is anticipated to be completed by 1 February 2027.

Ethics and dissemination

The study was approved by the institutional ethics committee of the National Cancer Center/National Clinical Research Center for Cancer/Cancer Hospital, Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences (approval No.25/483-5429). The trial will be conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and written informed consent will be obtained from each patient before enrolment. To ensure participant safety, AEs will be closely monitored and a DMC will oversee the trial. The results of this trial will be presented at scientific conferences and published in peer-reviewed scientific journals.

Characteristics of Significant Pericardial Effusion and Outcomes of Pericardiocentesis: A 4-year Retrospective Data Review
Authors: Mohamed Salah Abdelghani, Mohammad Altermanini, Alaa Rahhal, Osama Alkhalailah, Khalid Shunnar, Mohanad Shehadeh, Adalah Alqalalwah, Baraa AlQudah, Ahmed Shebani, Ibrahim Obeidat, Haneen AlAni, Mohamed Izham Mohamed Ibrahim, Mawahib Elhassan, Rasha Kaddoura, Jassim Al Suwaidi, Abdulrahman Arabi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Introduction:Pericardial effusion can lead to severe complications such as cardiac tamponade, necessitating immediate intervention. Various causes and prevalence vary according to the region. To the best of our knowledge, there is limited data from the Middle East region about pericardial effusion and outcomes of pericardiocentesis.Methods:This retrospective observational study was conducted at Heart Hospital, the only tertiary cardiology center in Qatar, to assess the clinical and echocardiographic characteristics of pericardial effusion and the impact of pericardiocentesis. Patients admitted to the cardiac intensive care unit between January 2017 and February 2021 with significant pericardial effusion confirmed either by large amount using transthoracic echocardiography (TTE), clinical signs, or echocardiographic signs suggestive of tamponade were included. The study evaluated clinical outcomes, including complications of pericardiocentesis and laboratory parameters before and after the procedure.Results:A total of 114 patients were included in our retrospective analysis. The most common etiology of pericardial effusion in the study was idiopathic (31.6%), followed by malignancy (25.4%), viral infection (13.2%), and tuberculosis (12.3%). Most patients (95%) had moderate to large effusions by TTE. Pericardiocentesis was successfully performed in 82.5% of patients. After pericardiocentesis, heart rate significantly decreased from 105 to 95 bpm (P < 0.001), and laboratory results showed significant improvement in renal and liver function, including serum creatinine, alanine transaminase, aspartate aminotransferase, and bilirubin levels. There were no pericardiocentesis-related deaths, but 6.38% of patients died within 28 days due to advanced primary diseases.Conclusion:Idiopathic causes were the most common, followed by malignancy. Pericardiocentesis, performed in an ICU setting with TTE guidance, was safe, with a 100% survival rate and low complication risk. Patients with impaired kidney and liver functions experienced significant improvement postprocedure. These findings offer valuable insights into pericardial effusion in Qatar and allow for comparisons with neighboring Middle Eastern countries.

INTRODUCTION

Pericardial effusion, characterized by fluid accumulation within the pericardial space, poses a significant challenge in critical care settings. The presence of fluid can lead to hemodynamic compromise and, in severe cases, progresses to cardiac tamponade, necessitating immediate intervention. It occurs due to various etiologies, including idiopathic, infectious, inflammatory, neoplastic, and iatrogenic causes.[12] The prevalence of specific etiologies may vary across different geographical regions and populations.

Cardiac tamponade is a critical complication of pericardial effusion, which arises when fluid accumulation exerts pressure on the heart, compromising its ability to pump effectively. Prompt recognition and intervention are vital to prevent adverse outcomes, such as cardiogenic shock, arrhythmia, or death.[3] The incidence of tamponade in patients with pericardial effusion can vary based on factors such as the etiology and size of the effusion, as well as the timeliness of accumulation. Investigating the demographics and characteristics of patients with tamponade can aid in management and resource allocation in the critical care setting.

Pericardiocentesis is a primary therapeutic intervention for pericardial effusion, and tamponade is used to remove fluid from the pericardial space. It provides immediate relief from tamponade, restoring cardiac output and improving hemodynamic stability. Pericardiocentesis can lead to significant clinical improvement and the resolution of symptoms.[4]

Understanding the demographics, etiology, associations, and outcomes of pericardiocentesis is essential for optimizing patient care and identifying potential areas for improvement. Several retrospective studies have investigated pericardial effusion and pericardiocentesis in different populations; however, there is limited literature from the Middle East region. Therefore, analyzing the etiology of pericardial effusion in the State of Qatar, a Middle Eastern country, can provide valuable insights into the local burden of the disease and guide clinical decision-making. By conducting our study in Qatar, we aimed to contribute further to the existing body of literature in the region and provide a comprehensive understanding of pericardial effusion and pericardiocentesis specific to our patients’ population.

METHODS

Study setting, design, and population

We conducted a retrospective observational study at Heart Hospital, the main tertiary cardiology center under Hamad Medical Corporation (HMC), the country’s principal public healthcare provider, to describe the clinical and echocardiographic characteristics of pericardial effusion and assess the clinical impact of pericardiocentesis. The study was approved by the HMC Medical Research Centre and the Institutional Review Board (MRC-01-21-473).

All patients admitted to the cardiac intensive care unit (CICU) from January 2017 to February 2021 with the diagnosis of pericardial effusion were screened for potential inclusion in the study.

Eligibility criteria

Patients were included in the study if they fulfilled the following criteria: (1) adult patients >18 years and (2) confirmed diagnosis of significant pericardial effusion either by large amount using transthoracic echocardiography (TTE), clinical signs, or echocardiographic signs suggestive of tamponade irrespective of the size of effusion. Patients were excluded if they (1) underwent pericardiocentesis in another facility before transfer or (2) underwent surgical pericardial window before admission to CICU.

Outcomes and follow-up

The outcome measures included: (1) clinical and echocardiographic characteristics of pericardial effusion including symptoms upon presentation, etiology, vital signs, and pericardial effusion size; (2) clinical outcomes of pericardiocentesis, including arrhythmia, myocardial infarction, cardiac arrest, surgical pericardial window, recurrent pericardial effusion, major bleeding defined as BARC ≥2, mortality related to pericardiocentesis, and all-cause mortality at one month; (3) vitals and laboratory values, including serum creatinine, alanine transaminase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), bilirubin, albumin, blood urea nitrogen (BUN), white blood cells, platelet, hemoglobin, and C-reactive protein before and after pericardiocentesis. Patients were followed up for 1 month post-discharge after the index event.

Statistical analyses

Data analyses were performed using the Statistical Package for Social Sciences program version 28.0 (IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows; IBM Corp, Armonk, NY, USA). Descriptive statistics were reported as frequencies and percentages for categorical variables, mean ± standard deviation for normally distributed continuous variables, and median with interquartile range (IQR) for skewed continuous variables. To compare vitals and laboratory variables before and after pericardiocentesis, the Paired t-test was used for normally distributed continuous variables, and the Wilcoxon signed-rank test was used for skewed continuous variables. All P values were two-sided, and results with P < 0.05 were used to indicate statistical significance.

RESULTS

The total number of included patients was 114, with a mean age of 45.2 ± 14.1 years. Male gender accounted for 61.4%, and female gender represented 38.6%. As shown in Table 1, Arab nationalities comprised 50% of the population, Asians represented 43.9%, and individuals from other nationalities accounted for approximately 6.1%. The most prevalent comorbidities based on patients’ medical history were anemia (47.4%), hypertension (31.6%), diabetes mellitus (28.1%), malignancy (25.4%), and chronic renal disease (15.8%). Approximately 18.4% were cigarette smokers, and 6.1% reported regular alcohol consumption. The chief complaint among the study population was shortness of breath (SOB) in 56.1% of cases, followed by chest pain in 19.3%, fever in 13.2%, and cough in 8.8%. Approximately 2.6% of patients were asymptomatic and diagnosed incidentally.

All patients were diagnosed with TTE, and moderate to large pericardial effusion was found in 108 (95%) patients. Around 90 (79%) had TTE features suggestive of tamponade or impending tamponade. Pericardiocentesis was done successfully with TTE guidance in 94 patients (82.5%) since 4 (3.5%) patients had the procedure done due to large pericardial effusion without features of tamponade. Eight patients were managed conservatively, and the remaining ten patients underwent surgical drainage with a pericardial window either due to loculated pericardial effusion or malignant effusion with a high possibility of recurrence. The median drained pericardial effusion was 760 mL (IQR 445.0–1100). The TB polymerase chain reaction (PCR) test was positive in 6.1%, representing almost half of the patients diagnosed with TB.

The average heart rate (HR) was found to be 102 ± 21 bpm, with systolic blood pressure at 120 ± 22 mmHg and diastolic blood pressure at 74 ± 15 mmHg, as shown in Table 1. Clinical signs of tamponade, such as hypotension and tachycardia, were observed in 23.7% of patients, as the majority of patients were resuscitated either with IV fluids or vasopressors. Following pericardiocentesis, there was a significant improvement in HR with a decrease from 105 ± 21 to 95 ± 8 bpm (P < 0.001), which reflect the improvement of the stroke volume [Table 3]. Among patients who were found to have impaired hepatic and renal functions, there was a statistically significant improvement in serum creatinine, ALT, AST, and bilirubin levels [Table 4 and Figure 1].

DISCUSSION

We conducted a single-center retrospective observational analysis at Heart Hospital, the only tertiary cardiology center in Qatar, to identify the etiology of pericardial effusion, the demographics of patients, and the clinical outcomes of pericardiocentesis.

The average age of participants was 45 years, with a majority being male (61.4%) and a notable representation of Arab (50%) and Asian (43.9%) nationalities, reflecting Qatar’s diverse ethnic population. The most commonly reported symptoms were SOB (56.1%), chest pain (19.3%), and fever (13.2%). This is consistent with European data, where SOB (dyspnea) is the most frequent clinical presentation of pericardial effusion, often accompanied by chest pain. These symptoms result from the fluid compressing the surrounding structures.[5]

A few studies from neighboring countries in the Middle East have provided valuable insights into the demographics, etiology, and clinical characteristics of pericardial effusion in this region. For instance, a retrospective study conducted in Saudi Arabia by Albugami et al. analyzed the etiologies of pericardial effusion in their population and found that malignancy causes were the most common;[6] however, in our study, idiopathic causes being the most common (31.6%), followed by malignancy (25.4%) and viral infections (13.2%). Among infections, TB was the second most frequent cause after viral infections (12.3%), with a positive TB PCR test in 6.1% of cases, suggesting that almost half of those diagnosed finally with TB had a positive result. The TB PCR test is a significant advancement in diagnosing pericardial effusion, particularly when TB is suspected. It rapidly detects Mycobacterium tuberculosis DNA in pericardial fluid, offering a quicker diagnosis than traditional methods like cultures, which can take weeks. This rapid detection is critical in areas where TB is prevalent, as it allows for the immediate start of antitubercular therapy. However, TB PCR sensitivity can range from 50% to 70%, so a negative result does not entirely rule out TB.[7]

Percutaneous pericardiocentesis is the preferred method for managing severe pericardial effusion; it serves diagnostic and therapeutic purposes. Echocardiography is typically quite sensitive, with detection rates for cardiac tamponade ranging from 80% to 90%. It can effectively identify critical features, such as the collapse of the right atrium in the systole or ventricle during diastole. In cases of early tamponade, echocardiography may not always provide clear indications, necessitating additional diagnostic approaches or clinical assessments.[11]

Our study indicated that pericardiocentesis resulted in a statistically significant reduction in HR. This could be attributed to the normalization of intrapericardial and atrial pressures, which increased stroke volume.[12]

Cardiac tamponade not only affects cardiac function but can also have systemic manifestations. Studies have shown that tamponade can be associated with abnormalities in renal and liver function tests. The increased venous pressure and compromised cardiac output in tamponade can lead to impaired renal perfusion and hepatic congestion, resulting in elevated serum creatinine, BUN, and liver enzymes.[1314] Monitoring renal and liver function tests in patients with tamponade can provide valuable information about organ perfusion status and aid in risk stratification.

Our study showed that patients who had impaired renal and liver functions and underwent pericardiocentesis had significant improvement in lab results within 3–7 days following the procedure without serious bleeding complications, which may suggest that they should not have their procedure delayed while waiting for lab values to improve, as these abnormalities are likely due to organ hypoperfusion secondary to tamponade physiology, consistent with published research demonstrating the positive impact of pericardiocentesis on these markers.[1314] These findings suggest that the impact of pericardiocentesis may be due to a reduction in central venous pressure and venous congestion that enhances hepatic and renal perfusion, leading to improvements in liver and kidney functions.

Limitations

Given the retrospective design, our study is a single center with a few inherent limitations. We did not have a control group to compare the outcomes of pericardiocentesis with and without TTE guidance. We had a short follow-up period of 28 days; however, the recurrence rate was around 15%, mostly in malignancy patients. The total number of patients is relatively small; however, it is almost representative of the population in Qatar, as >90% of pericardiocentesis is being done in the Heart Hospital as the only tertiary cardiac center.

CONCLUSION

This is the first retrospective study from Qatar analyzing the clinical characteristics and demographics of clinically significant pericardial effusion and the outcomes of pericardiocentesis over 4 years. The most common etiology was idiopathic, followed by malignancy, and the most common presenting complaint was shortness of breathing. Pericardiocentesis is considered a safe procedure with a low complication rate when it is done in a proper ICU setting with TTE guidance with a survival rate of 100%. Patients with pericardial effusion who had deranged kidney and liver functions causing coagulopathy showed significant improvement after the procedure.

This investigation’s results contribute to the understanding of pericardial effusion in Qatar and provide a comparative perspective with neighboring Middle Eastern countries. Future research should focus on multicentric studies and explore the long-term outcomes of pericardiocentesis, further elucidating regional variations and enhancing patient care strategies across diverse populations.

Physiochemical properties of premixed bioceramic-based root canal sealer reinforced with copper oxide nanoparticles
Authors: Prasanti Kumari Pradhan, Neelanjana Majee, Gaurav Patri, Prahlad Saraf, Debkant Jena, Akansha Tilokani, Pratik Agrawal
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Aim:The present study was conducted to evaluate Bio-C sealer and enhance its physicochemical characteristics through reinforcement with copper oxide nanoparticles at 1%, 2%, and 4% w/w concentrations.Materials and Methods:Copper oxide (CuO) nanoparticles were incorporated into premixed Bio-C sealer at 1%, 2%, and 4% w/w concentrations. The initial and final setting times, solubility, flow characteristics, hydrophilicity, and pH values of both freshly mixed and set specimens were assessed and compared. The microstructural features of the developed composite materials were examined using scanning electron microscopy.Results:The addition of CuO nanoparticles at concentrations of 1%, 2%, and 4% resulted in a concentration-dependent reduction in the setting time of Bio-C sealer. Flow values progressively decreased with increasing nanoparticle concentration. While incorporation of 1% and 2% CuO nanoparticles produced a slight and statistically insignificant increase in solubility, a statistically significant rise was observed at the 4% concentration. Hydrophilicity improved in a concentration-dependent manner following nanoparticle incorporation. Furthermore, inclusion of copper oxide nanoparticles produced a progressive and statistically significant elevation in pH from 24 h up to 14 days, indicating enhanced alkalinity of the modified sealers over time.Conclusions:Bio-C sealers modified with 1% and 2% CuO nanoparticles demonstrated improved physicochemical properties. These concentrations appear to provide an optimal balance and may be considered promising candidates for use as enhanced root canal sealers in endodontic therapy.

INTRODUCTION

Endodontic therapy is aimed at eliminating infection within the root canal system and achieving thorough three-dimensional obturation of the canal space, thereby preventing apical and coronal microleakage through the use of gutta-percha in combination with an endodontic sealer.[1] Although root canal sealers are primarily utilized as adjunctive materials during obturation, their physicochemical characteristics – including biocompatibility, adhesion to dentin, and resistance to dissolution – have a significant influence on the overall clinical outcome of endodontic treatment. Thus, their role extends beyond merely serving as supportive filling materials.[2]

Bioceramic-based materials exhibit dimensional stability and undergo minimal expansion during the setting reaction, which enhances their sealing capability. Following complete hardening, these materials provide a durable, long-term seal owing to their considerable hardness and low solubility, making them reliable options for root canal obturation in contemporary dental practice.[3] Bio-C Sealer (Angelus, Londrina, PR, Brazil) is a recently introduced premixed bioceramic root canal sealer containing calcium silicates, calcium aluminate, calcium oxide, zirconium oxide, iron oxide, silicon dioxide, and a dispersing agent. Zordan-Bronzel et al.[4] evaluated the physicochemical properties of Bio-C, TotalFill BC, and AH Plus as root canal sealers. Their findings demonstrated that Bio-C sealer exhibited the shortest setting time, superior flow, and higher solubility compared with the other evaluated sealers.

Despite their proposed advantages, bioceramic sealers present certain limitations, including difficulty in placement due to their consistency, extended setting time, and relatively high solubility.[5] To overcome these inherent drawbacks, modifications have been attempted by incorporating antimicrobial agents and nanomaterials. However, the physical properties might be altered by the addition of such components, depending on the concentration employed. Mediboyina et al.[6] modified Bio-C sealer with silica-doped titanium dioxide nanoparticles and reported sustained antimicrobial activity while maintaining flow within International Organization for Standardization (ISO) standards. Furthermore, incorporation of zirconium oxide nanoparticles, multi-walled carbon nanotubes, and niobium pentoxide into bioceramic materials has been shown to enhance bioactivity, cytocompatibility, and mechanical strength.[47]

Similar to other metallic nanoparticles commonly utilized in dentistry, copper oxide nanoparticles possess nanoscale dimensions, distinct morphology, and a high surface area-to-volume ratio. These characteristics improve their biophysicochemical functionality, antimicrobial activity, biocompatibility, and antimicrobial activity.[8] Aguilar-Perez et al.[9] incorporated different concentrations (2%–4%) of copper nanoparticles into glass ionomer cement (GIC) and observed that the modified GIC exhibited antimicrobial activity against the tested microbial strains. Ansarifard et al.[10] demonstrated that copper oxide nanoparticles incorporation into denture liners significantly reduced colonization and plaque formation of pathogens, particularly Candida albicans.

Only a limited number of studies have evaluated the physical and biological properties of Bio-C sealer, and to date, no studies have investigated the effect of incorporating copper oxide nanoparticles into any root canal sealer. Therefore, the present study evaluates the biological and physicochemical properties of premixed bioceramic sealer (Bio-C) reinforced with copper oxide nanoparticles.

This study received ethical clearance from the Ethical Committee of Kalinga Institute of Medical Sciences, KIIT Deemed to be University, having number KIIT/KIMS/IEC/1301/2023 (Date: May 08, 2023).

The investigation was conducted using a premixed bioceramic root canal sealer (Bio-C, Angelus) incorporated with commercially obtained Copper oxide (CuO) nanoparticles (<50 nm) (Sigma-Aldrich). All physical properties were assessed according to the procedures outlined in ISO 6876:2012 for dental root canal sealing materials.[12] All physicochemical properties were evaluated in White Lab of Saveetha Dental College, Chennai. A sample size of (n = 8) for each group and each test has been decided.

Evaluation of setting time

The initial and final setting times of all four groups were determined by dispensing the respective mixtures into custom-fabricated stainless steel molds (n = 8) having a 10 mm internal diameter and 2 mm height. Measurements were performed at 3-min intervals during the first 30 min, subsequently at 5-min intervals until completion of the 2nd h, and thereafter at 15-min intervals until the final set. Throughout the testing period, the molds were maintained in an incubator at 37°C with a relative humidity of 95%. Initial setting time was evaluated using a Gilmore needle weighing 100 ± 0.5 g with a tip diameter of 2.0 ± 0.1 mm. Final setting time was assessed using a Gilmore needle weighing 456 ± 0.5 g with a tip diameter of 1 ± 0.1 mm. The setting time for each specimen was calculated as the mean elapsed time from the point of mixing (defined as 120 ± 10 s after completion of mixing) until the respective needle failed to produce a visible indentation on the material surface.

Flow analysis

For flow determination, 0.05 mL of sealer from each group was dispensed onto glass plates using a 1 mL disposable syringe. After 180 ± 5 s, a second glass plate was carefully placed over the material. A calibrated load of 120 g was applied over the upper plate centrally. The load was removed 10 min after the start of mixing, and the major and minor diameters of the compressed sealer disc were measured using a digital caliper. The average of these two measurements was calculated. Three measurements were recorded for each specimen (n = 8), and the mean values along with standard deviations were documented in millimeters to determine flow rates. Two acceptance criteria were applied: The difference between the maximum and minimum diameters should not exceed 1 mm. The compressed disc must exhibit a uniformly circular configuration.

Evaluation of solubility

To determine solubility, specimens from each group were placed in shallow dishes with an approximate diameter of 90 mm and a minimum volume capacity of 70 mL. The mass of each dish was recorded to the nearest 0.001 g. Specimens were arranged to ensure that they did not contact one another and remained undisturbed. Fifty ± 1 mL of distilled water was added to each dish, which was then covered and stored in a cabinet for 24 h. Following immersion, samples were rinsed with 2–3 mL of fresh distilled water and removed from the dish. The remaining water in the dish was allowed to evaporate without boiling. Subsequently, the dish was dried in an oven at 110°C ± 2°C until a constant mass was achieved. Before each weighing, the dish was cooled to room temperature in a desiccator. All weighings were performed with an accuracy of 0.001 g.

Evaluation of hydrophilicity

Hydrophilicity was assessed by measuring the water contact angle of each specimen. A bench-top Phoenix contact angle measurement system (PHX300, South Korea) was utilized (n = 8) employing the sessile drop technique. Each sealer sample was prepared as a disc measuring 15 mm in diameter and 2 mm in height. 10 µL of distilled water was dispensed onto the surface of each specimen. The droplet was analyzed 10 s after placement and automatically recorded using a video capture system.

Evaluation of pH

To determine pH, freshly mixed sealer discs (n = 8) of 5 mm in diameter and 1 mm in thickness were prepared in the stainless steel molds. Then, molds were allowed to set in an incubator. Each set specimen was then transferred to a flat-bottom container containing 10 mL of distilled water and maintained in an incubator at 37°C throughout the experimental duration. The pH of the storage solution was calculated at the following intervals: 24 h, 48 h, 72 h, 7 days, 14 days using a calibrated pH meter.

Scanning electron microscopy analysis

Surface morphology of Bio-C sealer and its CuO nanoparticle-modified formulations was evaluated using scanning electron microscopy (SEM) (JEOL JSM, Japan) operated at 15 kV [Figures 1-5]. Specimens were prepared on flat cover slips and mounted on aluminum stubs. The samples were then sputter-coated with gold before examination. Micrographs were obtained at various magnifications to analyze structural characteristics and nanoparticle distribution.

Statistical analysis

One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was applied for each parameter. Level of significance was set at P ≤ 0.001.

Scanning electron microscopy analysis

SEM evaluation revealed that pure CuO nanoparticles exhibited a nonuniform distribution characterized predominantly by needle-shaped morphology. Bio-C sealer demonstrated large crystal-like structures embedded within a matrix composed of smaller, rounded, and elongated particles. Following the incorporation of CuO nanoparticles, small spherical particles were observed to be irregularly distributed throughout the matrix. The density of these particles increased proportionally with concentration – minimal in the 1% group, greater in the 2% group, and highest in the 4% group. The 2% formulation demonstrated relatively uniform dispersion with reduced agglomeration, whereas the 4% formulation exhibited evident agglomerate formation.

RESULTS

Results are presented in Tables 2 and 3. The highest mean setting time values were recorded for Group 1, whereas the lowest values were noted for Group 4 (P < 0.001). These findings indicate that the incorporation of CuO nanoparticles significantly reduced the setting time of Bio-C sealer in a concentration-dependent manner. The one-way ANOVA (P < 0.001) demonstrated that an increase in CuO nanoparticle concentration corresponded with a decrease in flow values. Although flowability decreased with increasing nanoparticle concentration, all groups remained within ISO-recommended limits. The highest mean solubility was observed in Group 4 (.51), while Groups 1 and 2 exhibited the lowest values. Increasing the concentration of copper oxide nanoparticles led to an increase in solubility of the Bio-C sealer, with a statistically significant rise particularly evident at 4% concentration. One-way ANOVA (P < 0.001) indicated good wettability. However, incorporation of copper oxide nanoparticles resulted in an increase in contact angle values, demonstrating reduced wettability with increasing nanoparticle concentration.

pH

Despite intergroup variation, all groups maintained alkaline pH values (above 7) at all measured intervals. Notably, incorporation of copper oxide nanoparticles resulted in a progressive and statistically significant increase in pH from 24 h up to 14 days, indicating enhanced alkalinity of the modified sealers over time (P < 0.001).

DISCUSSION

Premixed bioceramic materials predominantly composed of calcium silicate particles interact with tissue fluids to facilitate hydroxyapatite formation. This reaction enhances the dentin–sealer interface and contributes to the establishment of a durable antimicrobial seal. Their resistance to contamination, ability to adapt to complex canal anatomy, and slight expansion during the setting process collectively ensure an effective and long-lasting seal with minimal technique sensitivity.[1314] As Bio-C sealer requires moisture for its setting reaction, the incorporation of copper oxide nanoparticles into this premixed formulation is feasible. An ideal root canal sealer must demonstrate an optimal balance between physicochemical and biological properties, including adequate sealing ability, dimensional stability, biocompatibility, and an appropriate setting time.[15]

SEM analysis revealed that the 2% CuO formulation exhibited improved nanoparticle dispersion with minimal agglomeration, whereas the 4% formulation demonstrated considerable particle aggregation. This suggests that higher nanoparticle concentrations may promote clustering, potentially influencing material properties.

Setting time plays a critical role in determining sealer performance and apical sealing ability and is influenced by composition and environmental moisture conditions. An inappropriate setting time may adversely affect clinical outcomes.[16] Clinically, prolonged setting time may increase cytotoxicity and delay treatment completion, whereas single-visit endodontic procedures offer advantages such as reduced appointments and minimized risk of leakage.[5] According to ISO standards, root canal sealers should exhibit a setting time ranging between 30 min and 72 h and must fall within the manufacturer’s specified range. In the present study, all four groups [Table 2] complied with ISO standards, while the manufacturer reports an approximate setting time of 240 min for Bio-C sealer. The findings indicate that the incorporation of copper oxide nanoparticles significantly reduced both initial and final setting times in a concentration-dependent manner. Bio-C and other calcium silicate-based sealers are hydraulic materials that require water to initiate setting, resulting in the formation of a dense and durable surface that enhances clinical performance and longevity.[17] The addition of nanoparticles may have accelerated the hydration reaction, thereby shortening the setting time. These results are in agreement with the findings of Jung et al.,[11] who reported reduced setting times in nanoparticle-modified sealers. However, this contrasts with the findings of Baghdadi et al.,[5] possibly due to differences in sealer formulation, as Bio-Root, utilized in their study, was a powder-liquid system.

Flowability is essential to ensure that the sealer adequately fills internal canal irregularities without excessive extrusion, thereby achieving a void-free seal.[18] In this present study, flow was evaluated according to ISO 6876 standards, which require a minimum flow of 17 mm. All groups satisfied this requirement, indicating acceptable flow characteristics. Bio-C sealer alone demonstrated the highest flow, while Bio-C modified with 4% CuO nanoparticles showed the lowest. Increasing concentrations of CuO nanoparticles (1%, 2%, and 4%) resulted in a statistically significant reduction in flow values [Table 2], although all remained within ISO limits. This reduction in flowability may be attributed to increased viscosity associated with nanoparticle incorporation, which limits the material’s ability to spread and penetrate canal irregularities.[1920] The increased viscosity may result from reduced interparticle spacing, leading to enhanced interparticle interactions and resistance to movement. These observations align with the findings of Talaat et al.,[21] who reported decreased flow properties with increasing concentrations of magnesium hydroxide nanoparticles incorporated into a bioceramic sealer.

Water absorption and solubility are critical factors affecting the dimensional stability of root canal sealers, particularly calcium silicate-based materials that produce calcium hydroxide (Ca (OH) 2) during hydration. This process may influence sealing efficiency and long-term stability.[22] In the present study, solubility was assessed in accordance with ISO 6876 standards, and all groups exhibited values within the permissible 3% limit. An increase in solubility was observed with increasing CuO concentration, with the highest value recorded in the 4% group. A statistically significant increase in solubility was evident only at the 4% concentration. The elevated solubility may be attributed to the increased surface area of nanoparticles, which enhances interaction with water and accelerates dissolution, potentially weakening the ceramic matrix and affecting structural integrity. In contrast, Almutairi and Alkhudhairy[23] reported that incorporating silver, chitosan, and hydroxyapatite nanoparticles into bioceramic sealers reduced solubility, although no statistically significant differences were observed among nanoparticle types. While high solubility is generally considered undesirable, calcium silicate-based sealers release calcium and hydroxyl ions upon interaction with water, leading to increased pH in the surrounding environment and creating unfavorable conditions for bacterial survival and proliferation.[5]

Wettability describes the interaction between a liquid and a solid surface and is quantified by measuring the contact angle. Lower contact angles indicate improved wettability, whereas higher angles suggest reduced surface wetting. In this study, contact angle measurements for all four groups were below 90°, indicating satisfactory wettability. The highest contact angle was observed in the 4% CuO group, whereas the lowest was noted in the unmodified Bio-C sealer. Contact angle values increased progressively with higher CuO concentrations, reflecting a reduction in wettability. This phenomenon may be attributed to nanoparticle aggregation, which alters surface energy and modifies the nanostructural characteristics of the material.[24] Kang et al.[25] similarly reported that CuO reduces wettability compared to Cu2O, attributing this effect to differences in copper molecule distribution and surface characteristics during preparation.

The pH of a root canal sealer is a critical determinant of its antibacterial potential and biocompatibility, contributing to infection control and promotion of periapical healing.[26] In the present study, pH values were measured at multiple intervals, demonstrating an overall increase over time across all groups consistent with findings reported by López-García et al.[27] Incorporation of CuO nanoparticles (1%–4%) initially resulted in lower pH values at 24 h but subsequently produced a significant increase over the 2-week period. This may be attributed to hydration reactions leading to the release of hydroxyl ions and formation of Ca (OH) 2, thereby elevating the pH.[28] The sustained alkaline environment observed in the present study correlates with increased solubility patterns. Although prolonged alkalinity may enhance antimicrobial and biological effects, continued solubility could potentially compromise the material’s long-term sealing ability and resistance to apical leakage.[29]

CONCLUSIONS

Despite being conducted as an in vitro investigation, the present study provides foundational data that may support further laboratory and clinical research. Future studies should evaluate the cytotoxicity, biocompatibility, and sealing efficacy of CuO nanoparticle-incorporated Bio-C sealer to determine its suitability as a potential root canal filling material.

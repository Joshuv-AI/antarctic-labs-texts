Depression detection from multimodalities based on LeNet with hunter-geese optimization
Authors: Pradeep G, Oswalt Manoj S, S. Karthikeyini
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Depression is considered a psychological illness, and the effects of depression are persistent sadness, loss of interest, and severe depression, which, if untreated, can lead to suicidal thoughts and actions. Moreover, early intervention and detection of depression are essential for reducing the loss of life and avoiding adverse complications. Conventional methods of diagnosing depression rely largely on clinician’s judgment and the patient’s self-reporting, which can sometimes result in variability or reduced accuracy in diagnoses. Approaches that rely on a single type of data often miss important aspects of depression, as they cannot fully represent the wide range of characteristics linked to the condition. To address these shortcomings, a novel framework named Hunter Geese Migration Optimization-based LeNet (HGMO-LeNet) is established for depression detection from multimodalities, comprising Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) and speech. Primarily, the input MRI images are forwarded to the image processing, in which an adaptive Wiener filter is utilized to perform the image processing. Then, ROI extraction is carried out, which involves segmenting the brain region from the overall scan by removing non-brain tissues. Subsequently, feature extraction is done, and the features, namely volumetric and textural features, are extracted. Further, depression detection is established by exploiting the LeNet, which is fine-tuned by the designed HGMO. At the same time, the Gaussian filter assists in performing speech signal pre-processing. Further, the preprocessed signal is fed into the feature extraction phase, and then depression detection is accomplished using HGMO-LeNet. Finally, the output obtained from both inputs are merged using a correlation coefficient to produce the result. The devised HGMO-LeNet measured the maximum specificity of 91.000%, the accuracy of 91.276%, and the sensitivity of 92.266%. This demonstrates the superior performance of the devised model over existing methods and provides a robust system for automated depression detection.

Research gaps and motivation

Depression is an extreme psychosocial pathology that causes deep sadness, strong feelings of hopelessness, and mood changes. Conventional approaches to diagnosing depression rely strongly on clinician’s judgment and patient’s self-reporting, which can sometimes result in variability or reduced diagnostic accuracy. Many existing automated depression detection systems utilize only one modality. The single-modality systems may fail to capture the complete characteristics associated with depressive states. The prevailing models are often affected by background noise, recording conditions, and speaker variability, which may degrade the quality of the extracted features and reduce the reliability of the model. Many existing methods use simple fusion strategies, which may not effectively exploit complementary information from different modalities. To address these limitations, the HGMO-LeNet is proposed for multimodal data-based depression detection. This method incorporates effective preprocessing, robust feature extraction, and an optimization-based training mechanism to improve detection accuracy and robustness.

Contribution

This article introduces a novel approach called HGMO-LeNet for detecting depression using multimodal data. Initially, the input MRI image undergoes preprocessing, where noise is reduced using an adaptive Wiener filter, followed by RoI extraction. Consequently, features, like volumetric features including area, thickness, and textural features including Local Vector pattern (LVP), Local Neighborhood Difference Pattern (LNDP) with Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) with Pyramid of Histogram of Gradients (PHoG), Local Optimal Oriented Pattern (LOOP), and Local Directional Ternary Pattern (LDTP), are mined. Subsequently, the extracted features are fed into the classification phase, and classification is done by using the LeNet.The HGMO approach is utilized for training the LeNet, where HGMO is the integration of Hunter Prey Optimization (HPO) and Wild Geese Migration Optimization (GMO). Likewise, the speech signal is pre-processed by utilizing a Gaussian filter and subjected to feature extraction to extract features, like spectral centroid, zero crossing rate, spectral bandwidth, spectral flux, Multiple Kernel Weighted Mel Frequency Cepstral Coefficient (MKMFCC), and chroma vectors. The excerpted features are fed to the HGMO-LeNet for detecting depression. Thereafter, the output of both inputs is fused by the correlation coefficient to attain the final resultant.

Literature survey

Zhou et al.1 introduced the Time-Aware Attention-Based Multimodal Fusion Depression Detection Network (TAMFN) for depression detection. This method improves generalization capability and efficiently focused the integration of multiple modal features by the mechanism of time-aware attention, resulting in improved performance. Nevertheless, this approach did not consider the use of analysis of unlabeled test sets for updating the approach weights at the test phase. Li et al.7 developed the Multimodal Hierarchical Attention (MHA) model for identifying depression from social media data. This method provided better accuracy and recorded the best classification performance, and it effectively optimized the information distribution. However, this approach was unable to build a knowledge graph representing social media users affected by depression. De Almeida et al.10 designed the Multimodal Ensemble Stacking Deep Neural Network (DNN) for detecting depression. This technique minimized the overfitting risk and improved the generalization ability. However, this technique did not perform new experiments on the new datasets comprising visual characteristics, namely 3D and 2D facial landmarks for enhancing the performance of the detection. Zhang et al.14 devised the Hybrid Representation Learning (HRL) framework for the detection of depression. This framework was flexible and efficiently provided more structural data on local anatomical structures. Still, this approach did not explore the training strategies or more suitable feature extraction models to determine the problems of imbalanced information and small-sample dimensions.

Park and Moon12 proposed the Bidirectional Encoders from Transformers-CNN (BERT-CNN) for depression detection. This framework improved accuracy and addressed the fast increases in losses because of the utilization of individual information. Yet, this approach endured overfitting on smaller datasets due to their large capacity. Tao et al.18 developed Depression Detection-Spatio-Temporal Attentional Transformer (DepMSTAT). This method provided a faster processing speed and decreased the number of parameters to record an improved outcome on the most recent social media dataset. However, this approach did not develop weighted data segmentation to obtain the essential sequence segments for depression detection. Kumar et al.20 introduced the Long Short-Term Memory-Visual Geometry Group (LSTM-VGG16) for detecting depression. This method was extremely efficient in providing feature space and enabled robust classification for depression detection. Nevertheless, this framework did not identify the different types of emotions to improve the accuracy rate. Sharma et al.3 presented the Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT)+Convolutional Neural Network (CNN)+LSTM for the Smart Healthcare Framework for Electroencephalogram (EEG) Based Depression Detection. The technique recorded low latency, affordability, and portability, and provided maximum accuracy. However, this framework did not consider the improvement of the training outcomes with other datasets of distinct settings for the performance evaluation.

Shen et al.31 developed a Meta-Fuzzy Multimodal Fusion Network (MF2-Net) for depression detection in social media. This method integrated physiological signals and behavioral data, capturing complementary information across modalities. But the training of meta-learning models with multimodal data can require significant computational resources, limiting deployment in low-resource environments. Wang et al.32 suggested a Spatio-Temporal Information Modeling and Modal Alignment (SIMMA) for the detection of depression. This framework improved automatic depression detection by enhancing spatio-temporal feature extraction and enabling better cross-modal alignment. Although this model performed less effectively on different populations, platforms, or cultural contexts outside the training data.

Developed Hunter Geese Migration Optimization-based LeNet (HGMO-LeNet) for depression detection

This work presents the HGMO-LeNet for depression detection based on multimodal data, such as input speech signals and MRI images. At first, the MRI image is acquired from the dataset. Subsequently, processing of image is performed using an adaptive Wiener filter33. It effectively reduces noise and improves the quality of input features for the model. In MRI data, ROI extraction is carried out, which involves segmenting the brain region from the overall scan by removing non-brain tissues. Subsequently, the pre-processed image is fed to the feature extraction phase, where textural features and volumetric features are extracted. Additionally, the volumetric features like and area34, and textural features, including LNDP with DWT with PhoG35–37, LVP38, LOOP37, and LDTP39, and area34, and textural features, including LNDP with DWT with PhoG35–37, LVP38, LOOP37, and LDTP39, are mined. Extracting these features enables the representation of both overall structural characteristics and fine-grained tissue patterns in MRI scans, offering a detailed description of brain morphology. The excerpted features are subjected to the classification phase, in which the detection of depression is done by the LeNet40. The LeNet can automatically learn hierarchical features from MRI data, capturing relevant patterns for depression detection. The training of the depression classification network LeNet is carried out by using the HGMO, which is developed by the combination of the GMO41 and HPO42. HGMO effectively searches for optimal parameters and improves the model’s performance and convergence speed. Conversely, the speech signal undergoes preprocessing using a Gaussian filter43. This filter enhances the input data quality by reducing high-frequency noise and smoothing the input signals. Subsequently, the preprocessed signal is passed to the feature extraction stage to derive relevant features, such as spectral flux, spectral centroid44, spectral bandwidth45, zero crossing rate46, chroma vectors47, and MKMFCC48. These features highlight subtle variations in the speech signal that may correlate with depression-related behaviors. The extracted features are then fed into the HGMO-LeNet model for depression detection. Afterwards, the outputs from the MRI images and speech signals are integrated using a correlation coefficient to generate the final result. Training of LeNet using proposed HGMO

The designed HGMO approach is introduced by unifying the GMO41 and HPO42. The HPO is simulated by considering the hunting strategies of animals, and it is considered a non-gradient approximation algorithm. HPO is modelled by considering the interaction between the prey and predator and the strategies they use to evade and hunt, respectively. The merit of using the HPO algorithm is its rapid convergence rate and easy implementation42. The GMO is simulated based on the social behavior of migrating wild geese41. The GMO provided outstanding computational performance and has strong adaptability and enhanced competitiveness. Thus, the HPO and GMO are combined to develop the HGMO for obtaining the superior outcome by attaining the global optima and the algorithm for the HGMO is expressed below,

(i) Initialization

At first, the primary population \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\left( {\vec {M}} \right)=\left\{ {{{\vec {M}}_1},{{\vec {M}}_2},.....{{\vec {M}}_{\bar {u}}}} \right\}$$\end{document}is randomly configured as,48\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${M_{\bar {r}}}=rand\left( {1,\bar {n}} \right). * \left( {ub - lb} \right)+lb$$\end{document}

Here,\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${M_r}$$\end{document}implies the location of the prey or hunter, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\bar {u}$$\end{document}is the population size,lbindicates the lower bound, ubdenotes the upper bound of the problem variable, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\bar {n}$$\end{document}denotes the variable count.

(ii) Fitness

The fitness function helps determine the optimal solution, using Mean Square Error as the evaluation metric, and it is formulated as,49\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$MSE=\frac{1}{\delta }{\sum\limits_{{\alpha =1}}^{\delta } {\left( {O_{1}^{ * } - {O_1}} \right)} ^2}$$\end{document}

where δsymbolizes the total count of the training samples,\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$O_{1}^{ * }$$\end{document} denotes the expected output of LeNet and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${O_1}$$\end{document} represents its actual output.

(iii) Hunter Search process

The two steps normally utilized for the search mechanisms are exploitation and exploration. The introduced HGMO-LeNet is evaluated concerning different parameters namely, sensitivity, accuracy, specificity, and F1-score which are explained as follows,

(i) Accuracy

The proportion of accurate detection from the overall count of the detection is known as accuracy. It helps to correctly identify the depressed individuals, and it is signified as Γ,68\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Gamma =\frac{{{\rm M}{\rm X}+{\rm M}{\rm H}}}{{{\rm M}{\rm X}+{\rm M}{\rm H}+{\rm B}{\rm X}+{\rm B}{\rm H}}}$$\end{document}

(ii) Sensitivity

The measure of accurate detection of positive events is known as sensitivity, and the sensitivity is also referred to as the recall. The F1-score is a performance evaluation metric used in classification tasks to assess the overall effectiveness of a model by balancing its ability to correctly identify positive cases while minimizing incorrect classifications. η represents the F1-score, and it is calculated as,71\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\eta =\frac{{2MX}}{{2MX+BX+BH}}$$\end{document}

Conclusion

The most prevalent and major mental illness that affects a person’s behavior, thinking, and feelings is defined as depression. Depression is different in people, making it difficult to standardize detection approaches. Hence, the HGMO-LeNet is presented for multimodal data-based depression detection from the input MRI images and speech signals. Primarily, the input MRI image is forwarded to image processing to denoise the image using the adaptive Wiener filter. In MRI data, ROI extraction is carried out, which involves segmenting the brain region from the overall scan by removing non-brain tissues. Consequently, the multiple features are mined, and the extracted features are fed into the detection phase, where depression detection is carried out by utilizing LeNet. The HGMO is utilized for training the LeNet. Likewise, the Gaussian filter is exploited for the preprocessing of the speech signal. Furthermore, the multiple features are mined and then fed to HGMO_LeNet for detecting depression. Further, the correlation coefficient is utilized to combine the output from MRI images and speech signals to get a final output. In addition, the developed HGMO-LeNet recorded the specificity of 91.000%, the accuracy of 91.276%, and sensitivity of 92.266%, But, the proposed model has limited performance when applied to other modalities, such as textual data or physiological measurements, due to differences in data characteristics and feature requirements. Future work will focus on exploring hybrid deep learning methods to enhance the performance of multimodal depression detection. Additionally, the study will consider incorporating other data modalities, such as textual content and physiological signals, to further improve the performance and robustness of the proposed model.

Impact of phytase supplementation on performance and egg quality traits in broiler breeders: A meta-analysis
Authors: Roberto A Yamawaki, Felipe M W Hickmann, Ines Andretta, Bruno S Vieira, Alex Maiorka
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis meta-analysis aimed to determine the effect of phytase (PHY) supplementation on performance and egg quality traits in broiler breeders. A systematic review across multiple databases identified 2,162 studies, of which seven met all eligibility criteria and were included in the analysis. The meta-analysis compared egg production (EP), hatchability, fertility, egg weight (EW), and the yolk, albumen, and shell weight ratios relative to EW between diets supplemented or not with PHY. Heterogeneity and publication bias were also assessed. Overall, PHY supplementation significantly increased EP by 4.85 % (P < 0.01) and yolk ratio by 0.24 % (P < 0.05), while decreased EW by 0.53 g (P < 0.01) and albumen ratio by 0.34 % (P < 0.01). No significant effects of PHY were observed on shell ratio, hatchability, or fertility (P ≥ 0.05). Meta-regression and subgroup analyses revealed that PHY effects on EP were enhanced in diets containing ≥2.5 g/kg phytate phosphorus (PP) and calcium below requirements, particularly during the late production phase. In contrast, PHY reduced EW more remarkably in diets with PP ≥2.5 g/kg, vitamin D < 3,000 IU/kg, and non-phytate phosphorus below requirements. Additionally, yolk ratio increased significantly when PHY was fed for ≥10 weeks and when vitamin D levels were below 3,000 IU/kg. PHY affected shell ratio only in diets with PP levels <2.5 g/kg. An economic model based on the meta-analysis results indicated that PHY supplementation reduced EP costs by $0.011 or 3.1 % per egg, corresponding to an additional 5.9 million eggs per million broiler breeders. Overall, PHY supplementation improved performance and egg quality traits, offering measurable economic benefits for broiler breeder production systems.Keywords: Egg production, Fertility, Hatchability, Phytate phosphorus, Non-phytate phosphorus, Economic benefit

Introduction

Phosphorus (P) is one of the most important minerals in animal nutrition given its major roles in many body functions, such as the maintenance of growth performance. Along with calcium (Ca), P is extremely important for mineral deposition in the skeleton and subsequent bone mineralization (Létourneau-Montminy et al., 2010; Bougouin et al., 2014). However, almost two-thirds of the total seed P is complexed with myo-inositol to form phytic acid and its salts, drastically reducing its bioavailability to animals (Bougouin et al., 2014; Abbasi et al., 2019). Consequently, a considerable amount of P in poultry diets is provided in the form of phytate P (PP), with limited availability (Selle et al., 2023).

In addition to the negligible availability of non-phytate P (NPP) in cereal grains, phytase (PHY), a digestive enzyme catalyzing the release of P from the PP complex, cannot be synthesized at sufficient levels in the poultry intestine (Applegate et al., 2003). Therefore, other inorganic P sources are usually added to poultry diets to meet daily nutritional requirements (Hervo et al., 2023). However, inorganic P sources are both expensive and non-renewable (Bougouin et al., 2014). Beyond the increased feed costs, excess P in poultry diets can be excreted in manure, potentially leading to environmental issues such as surface water eutrophication (Bougouin et al., 2014; Selle et al., 2023).

Supplementing poultry diets with exogenous PHY enzymes significantly increases PP utilization. Since its introduction in 1991, PHY has become one of the most widely used exogenous enzymes in poultry nutrition (Selle et al., 2023). Several studies have reported the effects of PHY supplementation in broilers, broiler breeders, and laying hens; however, wide discrepancy has been reported in the effect size of PHY on performance and P digestibility (Létourneau-Montminy et al., 2010; Bougouin et al., 2014). Understanding the effect size of exogenous PHY on performance and P utilization is crucial for determining the appropriate amount of inorganic P needed to meet nutritional requirements, thereby optimizing both sustainability and economic outcomes (Bougouin et al., 2014).

Meta-analyses have become a valuable research approach due to their ability to quantitatively combine data from previous independent studies, thereby providing more robust and reliable inferences (Sauvant et al., 2020). Another justification for the use of meta-analyses is their capacity to translate study outcomes into practical and applicable knowledge. Unlike single experiments, which are limited by their specific conditions, meta-analytic methods systematically account for heterogeneity among studies, offering a broader and more comprehensive understanding of the topic (Lovatto et al., 2007).

Numerous systematic reviews and meta-analyses have studied the effects of PHY on performance and P digestibility in layers and broilers (Létourneau-Montminy et al., 2010; Ahmadi et al., 2012; Bougouin et al., 2014; Faridi et al., 2015; Cowieson et al., 2017; Asadi Kermani et al., 2023; Nuamah et al., 2024). However, there is limited information on the effects of PHY supplementation on production parameters and egg quality traits in broiler breeders. To the best of our knowledge, no other meta-analysis has specifically evaluated the effects of PHY supplementation on broiler breeders. Additionally, many fundamental questions regarding PP and PHY in this category of animals remain unanswered (Selle et al., 2023).

Therefore, this study aimed to estimate the effect size of PHY supplementation on production parameters and egg quality traits in broiler breeders through a systematic review and meta-analysis. Furthermore, based on the results of the meta-analysis, a mathematical model was developed to predict the economic impact of PHY supplementation in various scenarios.

Material and methods

This study relied solely on data obtained from the published literature and did not involve the use of additional animals; thus, approval from the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee was not required.

Search and data filtering

An extensive online search for studies on PHY performance and P digestibility in broiler breeders was conducted until February 2025. The search focused on scientific articles published in peer-reviewed journals and utilized various online databases, including PubMed, Web of Science, and Scopus. References from the identified publications were also reviewed to identify any additional relevant articles.

The research question was framed using the “PICo” strategy (Andretta et al., 2021), identifying the “Population” as “broiler breeders”, the “Interest” as “phytase”, and the “Context” as “performance” OR “phosphorus retention”. Synonyms and related terms were included to ensure comprehensive coverage. Retrieved results were imported into EndNote X9 (Clarivate Analytics, Philadelphia, PA, USA) for duplicate removal. Study eligibility was determined through sequential screening of titles, abstracts and full-text.

Studies were included when they met the following criteria: (1) reported in vivo trial with commercial broiler breeders; (2) included both a non-supplemented control, and PHY-supplemented diets; and (3) reported at least one of the following variables with measures of sample variance: daily egg production (EP), hatchability, fertility, egg weight (EW), or yolk, albumen, and shell weight ratios. Studies were excluded if they involved sanitary challenges, combined PHY with other enzymes, lacked a control treatment, or exhibited methodological inconsistencies. Data systematization and coding

Data on the quantitative variables of interest were extracted from the included studies into an electronic spreadsheet following the methodology described by Lovatto et al. (2007) and Bougouin et al. (2014). For each study, the number of replicates, birds per replicate, treatment means, and standard deviations were recorded for both control and PHY groups. To ensure consistency across our dataset, all the missing dietary nutritional composition was recalculated using the Practical Program for Formulation of Rations (Garcia-Neto, 2008), adopting ingredient composition provided by the Brazilian Tables for Poultry and Swine (Rostagno et al., 2024). Each PHY treatment within a study was considered an independent observation. Seven weighted mean difference (WMD) analyses were performed to estimate the overall effects of PHY supplementation on EP, hatchability, fertility, EW, and yolk, shell, and albumen ratios, as well as to assess heterogeneity and evaluate publication bias for each dependent variable.

Calculations and statistical analysis

Effect size

The effect size of PHY on each dependent variable was expressed as the WMD with 95 % confidence intervals. Each study’s weight was inversely proportional to its variance, assigning greater influence to trials with higher precision (Feng et al., 2021). The normality of residuals was verified using the Shapiro–Wilk test and the significance of WMDs was tested by the Z test.

Heterogeneity, meta-regression and subgroup analyses

Heterogeneity between studies was determined using a random-effects model (restricted maximum likelihood). Its significance was tested by the chi-squared test and its magnitude, by I² statistic (Borenstein et al., 2009). Following Vieira et al. (2017), even when significant heterogeneity was not detected for a variable, meta-regression and subgroup analyses were still conducted to explore potential sources of variability. Meta-regression was used as a screening tool to identify significant relationships between moderators and effect sizes. When significance was found, additional subgroup analyses were performed to better interpret the model.

Subgroups were categorized according to the production phase of broiler breeders (Early = 25-36 wk, Mid = 37-46 wk, and Late = 47-72 wk), treatment duration (< or ≥ 10 weeks), dietary PHY concentrations (< or ≥ 500 FTU/kg), PP (< or ≥ 2.5 g/kg), Vitamin D (< or ≥ 3,000 IU/kg), Ca:NPP ratio (< or ≥ 10.5), NPP, and Ca requirements based on Rostagno et al. (2024) (< or ≥ 100 %). When treatments encompassed multiple phases, classification was based on the predominant phase.

Publication bias

Publication bias was evaluated using funnel plots and Egger’s test (Vieira et al., 2017). All analyses were performed using the meta package (version 6.5-0) in R (version 4.3.1; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Significance was set at 5 % (P < 0.05) at all stages.

Economic impact

Significant (P < 0.05) WMD results were integrated into an economic model estimating the benefit of PHY supplementation on total egg (TE) production cost per broiler breeder. The simulation considered TE production at 64 weeks of age based on management guides (Cobb, 2020; Aviagen, 2021a,b), assuming a PHY dose of 500 FTU/kg and average feed costs provided by a local poultry agroindustry. Performance data extracted from animals supplemented with PHY and their control groups were also used in the equations, as follows. Results

Database characterization

The seven studies included in the meta-analysis involved 2,204 broiler breeders aged 25 to 72 weeks. Most used Ross 708 (n = 3), followed by Cobb 500 (n = 2) and Ross 308 (n = 1); one study (n = 1) did not specify the strain. All experiments were conducted with corn–soybean meal diets. PHY sources varied: one study used 3-PHY (EC 3.1.3.8) and six used 6-PHY (EC 3.1.3.26), with five bacterial and two fungal origins. PHY inclusion levels ranged from 250 to 1,200 FTU/kg.

Meta-analysis results

No outliers were detected, and the studentized residuals of the WMDs followed a normal distribution for all variables. The overall effects of PHY supplementation were summarized in forest plots (Fig. 2, Fig. 3, Fig. 4, Fig. 5, Fig. 6, Fig. 7, Fig. 8) and tables (Tables 4,5,6,7,8,9,10). PHY increased EP by 4.85 % (P < 0.01) and yolk ratio by 0.24 % (P = 0.03), while reducing EW by 0.53 g (P < 0.01) and albumen ratio by 0.34 % (P < 0.01). PHY had no significant effects on shell ratio (P = 0.72), hatchability (P = 0.85), and fertility (P = 0.22).

Significant heterogeneity was detected only for yolk ratio (P = 0.02, I² = 47 %) and shell ratio (P < 0.01, I² = 56 %), suggesting the influence of qualitative moderators. Meta-regression identified treatment duration (P < 0.01) and vitamin D level (P < 0.01) as significant factors affecting yolk ratio. Subgroup analysis confirmed that PHY improved yolk ratio when applied for ≥10 weeks (WMD = 0.38 %, P < 0.01) and in diets with <3,000 IU/kg vitamin D (WMD = 0.39 %, P < 0.01). For shell ratio, dietary PP level was the only significant source of variation (P = 0.02). Additionally, PHY decreased shell ratio when PP <2.5 g/kg (WMD = −0.29 %, P = 0.05).

Although heterogeneity was not significant for other variables, further meta-regression identified EP phase (P = 0.009), PP (P = 0.03) and Ca requirements (P < 0.01) as significant moderators influencing PHY effects on EP. Greater EP responses were observed in diets containing PP ≥2.5 g/kg (WMD = 5.59 %, P < 0.01), Ca <100 % of Rostagno et al. (2024) recommendations (WMD = 9.07 %, P < 0.01), and during the late EP phase (WMD = 6.31 %, P < 0.001). For EW, significant moderators included PP (P = 0.02), vitamin D (P = 0.04), NPP (P = 0.05), and Ca (P = 0.02) requirements. PHY reduced EW in diets with PP ≥2.5 g/kg (WMD = −0.74 g, P < 0.01), vitamin D < 3,000 IU/kg (WMD = −0.78 g, P < 0.01), and NPP and Ca <100 % (WMD = −0.68 g and −1.85 g, P < 0.01, respectively). No significant moderators affected hatchability, fertility and albumen ratio. Publication bias, assessed using funnel plots and Egger’s test, was not significant for any variable (P > 0.05), despite the limited number of comparisons for hatchability and fertility (n < 10). Discussion

This meta-analysis is the first to specifically evaluate the effects of exogenous PHY supplementation in broiler breeders. PHY significantly improved EP by 4.85 %, confirming its role in enhancing P utilization and animal performance. Although most individual studies reported no statistical differences between PHY and control diets, the pooled analysis increased precision and revealed consistent benefits, which is typical of meta-analytic integration (Vieira et al., 2017).

Performance

A consistent positive effect of PHY on EP was observed, even in diets meeting or exceeding the NPP requirements proposed by Rostagno et al. (2024). Meta-regression further indicated that EP response was influenced by EP phase, dietary PP, and Ca concentration. The greatest improvement occurred during the late EP phase, followed by the mid and early phases. This finding aligns with physiological evidence showing that older breeders exhibit reduced nutrient absorption efficiency and greater sensitivity to dietary enzyme supplementation (Berry et al., 2003).

Diets containing PP ≥2.5 g/kg and Ca <100 % of the Rostagno et al. (2024) recommendations enhanced the positive effects of PHY on EP. A higher PP concentration provides more substrate for enzymatic hydrolysis, as P release is directly correlated with dietary PP content (Moss et al., 2018). Conversely, excess Ca can form insoluble Ca–PP complexes, which reduce PHY activity (Adekoya and Adeola, 2023). Although broiler breeder diets typically contain elevated Ca to support eggshell formation (Manangi et al., 2018), excessive levels may restrict enzyme efficiency. Thus, maintaining Ca concentrations close to the recommended requirements appears optimal to maximize PHY efficacy and productivity.

Importantly, the meta-analysis showed that PHY inclusion levels ≥ 500 FTU/kg did not further improve performance or egg quality traits compared with lower PHY doses, indicating that supplementation up to 500 FTU/kg was sufficient to maximize PHY efficacy in broiler breeders under the conditions represented in the included studies. Moreover, PHY supplementation did not significantly affect hatchability or fertility, and none of the moderators evaluated showed significant influence on these traits.

Egg quality traits

PHY supplementation reduced EW by 0.53 g. Meta-regression identified dietary PP, vitamin D, and NPP and Ca requirements as the main moderators influencing PHY efficacy. The reduction in EW occurred in diets containing PP ≥2.5 g/kg, vitamin D < 3,000 IU/kg, and NPP and Ca levels below recommended requirements. Interestingly, these same dietary conditions (PP ≥2.5 g/kg and Ca below Rostagno et al., 2024) also enhanced the positive effect of PHY on EP. Since egg mass is the product of EW and EP, these findings suggest a potential inverse relationship between these traits, where PHY increases EP but slightly reduces EW.

Additionally, PHY showed a significant negative effect on EW only in diets with NPP below recommended levels. By catalyzing the release of P from the PP complex, PHY increases the availability of NPP in the diet (Bougouin et al., 2014), which can support higher EP but may slightly compromise EW. Vitamin D also modulated PHY activity. Low vitamin D levels reduce Ca absorption, increase PP availability and enhance PHY hydrolysis (Liem et al., 2009), whereas high vitamin D may improve mineral absorption and renal resorption, reducing substrate availability for PHY. Although the interaction between PHY and vitamin D has not been extensively explored in broiler breeders, this meta-analysis suggests that vitamin D concentrations <3,000 IU/kg may potentiate PHY effects on yolk proportion and egg size regulation, warranting further research on this interaction.

PHY supplementation increased yolk ratio by 0.24 % and decreased albumen ratio by 0.34 %, suggesting an inverse relationship between these traits. Because the yolk serves as an important P reservoir, PHY may enhance yolk deposition by improving P bioavailability and its incorporation into phosphorylated lipids and proteins (Jiang et al., 2015). Significant heterogeneity was observed for yolk ratio, mainly explained by PHY treatment duration and vitamin D concentration. Subgroup analysis indicated that supplementation for ≥10 weeks was necessary to detect measurable improvements, likely because P can be mobilized from skeletal reserves, and moderate P deficiency may not immediately impair yolk formation (Rodehutscord et al., 2023). PHY supplementation in diets containing vitamin D < 3,000 IU/kg further increased yolk ratio, suggesting that suboptimal vitamin D levels may enhance PHY efficacy. This aligns with the findings for EP and EW, where low vitamin D is likely to reduce Ca absorption and P resorption, increasing available substrate for PHY activity and enhancing its effectiveness.

The overall shell ratio was not significantly affected. However, meta-regression showed that PP levels influenced its response. A significant reduction in shell ratio was observed in diets with PP <2.5 g/kg. This finding contrasts with the results for EP, where PP levels ≥2.5 g/kg showed greater PHY efficiency. These contrasting effects highlight the need for further research into the correlation between PHY supplementation and shell ratio under different dietary PP levels. No significant heterogeneity or effects from qualitative moderators were observed in the meta-regression or subgroup analysis for the albumen ratio. Therefore, the overall effects from the pooled analyses were considered as the primary results for this variable in the meta-analysis.

PHY economic impact

The use of exogenous enzymes in poultry diets is now a well-established nutritional practice aimed at improving nutrient digestibility and absorption by acting on indigestible and anti-nutritional components. Supplementation with 500 FTU/kg of PHY has been reported to reduce broiler production costs by approximately 0.8 % per kg of live weight compared with non-supplemented diets (Krabbe et al., 2024). Under the experimental conditions represented in the studies included in this meta-analysis, a PHY dose of 500 FTU/kg for broiler breeders appeared sufficient to maximize its effects, as higher doses did not produce additional improvements in performance or egg quality, thus, this dose was used in the economic model.

According to the meta-analysis, PHY supplementation in broiler breeder diets reduced the cost per egg by $0.011 (3.1 %) and increased total EP by approximately 5.9 million eggs per 1 million broiler breeders from 1 d old to 64 weeks of age. The sensitivity analysis further confirmed that PHY supplementation remained economically advantageous in most production scenarios. Unfavorable outcomes were only observed when PHY prices increased more than threefold and egg production gains were ≤0.15 %, or when PHY had no measurable effect on performance. Under all other conditions, PHY supplementation was profitable and improved overall efficiency.

Study limitations and future directions

This meta-analysis has some limitations that should be considered when interpreting the results. Only seven studies met the inclusion criteria, which restrict statistical power and generalizability. Moderate heterogeneity was observed for yolk and shell ratios, indicating that additional factors may have influenced these outcomes. Moreover, P retention and egg mass were each reported in only one study (Bhanja et al., 2005; Abbasi et al., 2015, respectively), preventing their inclusion as variables in the analysis. Additionally, only two studies reported data on eggshell strength, resulting in two available comparisons, which were insufficient to support a reliable meta-analytic model.

Despite these limitations, this study provides valuable and novel insights as the first meta-analysis specifically evaluating PHY supplementation in broiler breeders. By systematically integrating the available evidence, it highlights key dietary interactions and consistent patterns not evident in individual studies, offering relevant contributions to both research and practical nutrition strategies.

Future research should address current gaps through controlled trials with standardized methodologies and larger sample sizes to enhance statistical robustness and external validity. Particular attention should be given to interactions between PHY and dietary factors such as vitamin D, Ca sources, and PP levels, and to their combined effects on performance, egg quality, P retention, and mineral metabolism, especially during the late EP phase.

Conclusion

PHY supplementation improves EP in broiler breeders and modifies egg quality traits by increasing the yolk ratio while reducing both EW and albumen ratio. Including PHY in broiler breeder diets has a positive economic impact on EP profitability and should be considered under most production conditions similar to those represented in the included studies. Under the conditions of the experiments included in this meta-analysis, PHY doses up to 500 FTU/kg were sufficient to optimize PHY efficacy on broiler breeder performance and egg quality traits.

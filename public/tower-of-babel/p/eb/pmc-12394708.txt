Deterministic and stochastic free vibration analysis of CNT reinforced functionally graded cantilever plates
Authors: Mehulkumar Padhiyar, Pradeep Kumar Karsh, Gaurav Kumar Bandhania
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper presents both deterministic and stochastic free vibration analyses of carbon nanotube (CNT)-reinforced multi-layered functionally graded material (FGM) cantilever plates. The reinforcement varies linearly following a power-law distribution. The governing equation is derived using the first-order shear deformation theory (FSDT), while the rule of mixtures is applied to determine the effective elastic modulus, mass density, and Poisson’s ratio of the CNT-reinforced FGM plate. A finite element-based Monte Carlo simulation (MCS) is employed for the stochastic analysis. The study begins with a validation of the finite element model by comparing the obtained results with existing literature. Subsequently, a parametric investigation is conducted, examining the effects of stochasticity percentage, power law index, plate thickness, volume fraction, temperature, and CNT size. Additionally, mode shapes for the first three vibration modes are plotted. The findings reveal that all these parameters significantly influence the first three natural frequencies.Keywords: Stochastic analysis, CNT reinforced FGM, Finite element analysis, Monte carlo simulation, Natural frequencies, Mode shapesSubject terms: Engineering, Materials science, Mathematics and computing, Physics

Introduction

Functionally graded materials (FGMs) are a class of advanced composite materials characterized by a gradual variation in composition and/or microstructure over one or more dimensions1. This gradual transition results in continuous changes in properties such as mechanical (e.g., tensile strength, stiffness, ductility, toughness, hardness), physical (e.g., density, thermal and electrical conductivity), and chemical (e.g., resistance to corrosion and oxidation). With the increasing demand for components that exhibit multiple functionalities, it has become increasingly difficult for a single material to satisfy all performance requirements2. Traditional methods such as mechanically joining or bonding different materials may introduce problems like interfacial debonding due to stress concentration at sharp material boundaries. FGMs offer a promising solution by enabling a smooth transition between materials, thereby reducing or eliminating stress discontinuities. As a result, FGMs are receiving growing attention in advanced manufacturing for their ability to integrate multiple material benefits into a single component. Their advantages over conventional composites include: (i) mitigation or elimination of interfacial cracking due to the absence of abrupt material transitions; (ii) multifunctionality, allowing the structure to meet diverse performance criteria simultaneously; and (iii) the potential for designing lightweight, high-performance structures that fully exploit material capabilities. Functionally graded structures reinforced with nano-fillers can be engineered by introducing a gradient in parameters such as the volume fraction, size, shape, or orientation of nano-fillers within the matrix material. Among various nanomaterials, carbon-based nanostructures have demonstrated exceptional mechanical properties, making them highly suitable as reinforcing agents in advanced nanocomposites. Carbon nanotubes (CNTs), first discovered by Iijima in 1991, have gained significant interest due to their remarkable characteristics, including a mechanical strength nearly 100 times greater than that of steel, an exceptionally high elastic modulus of around 1 TPa, and superior electrical and thermal conductivities3. Additionally, their high aspect ratio, ranging from 1 to 10 nm in diameter to several millimeters in length, further enhances their effectiveness as reinforcement materials in composite applications4]– [5. Lightweight, high-strength, and thermally stable materials are essential in aerospace, automotive, and other engineering applications where components are exposed to dynamic loading, high temperatures, and uncertain operating conditions. CNT-reinforced functionally graded materials offer superior stiffness-to-weight ratios, excellent thermal conductivity, and good vibration performance, making them ideal for such environments. However, inherent uncertainties from manufacturing variations and nanofiller dispersion require stochastic analysis to ensure reliable design and performance. This study addresses these challenges by evaluating the vibration behaviour of CNT-reinforced FGMs under both deterministic and stochastic conditions.

Many researchers have carried out work on FGM and composite materials, such as Hatami and Bahrami6 demonstrates the effectiveness of the dynamic stiffness method (DSM) in accurately predicting the natural frequencies of FG-CNTRC plates under various conditions. Their result highlighted the influence of CNT distribution, volume fraction, boundary conditions, and geometric parameters on vibrational behavior. Lei et al.7 confirmed the reliability and accuracy of the element-free kp-Ritz method for analyzing the free vibration behavior of FG-CNTRC plates with various SWCNT distributions. They revealed how factors such as CNT volume fraction, geometric parameters, temperature changes, and boundary conditions influence natural frequencies and mode shapes. Zghal et al.8 analyzes the free vibration of CNT-reinforced functionally graded composite shells using a higher-order finite element method. Material properties are defined through a micromechanical model, considering various CNT distributions. Input parameters include CNT distribution, shell geometry, and material gradation. Vo-Duy et al.9 examine the free vibration of laminated FG-CNTRC beams using first-order shear deformation theory and finite element analysis. The beams consist of CNT-reinforced composite layers with uniform or graded CNT distributions. Input parameters include CNT volume fraction, distribution, orientation, boundary conditions, and layer configuration. Amirabadi et al.10 focuses on the free vibration analysis of a cantilever trapezoidal plate with non-uniform thickness, reinforced by non-uniformly distributed nanofillers. Material properties vary based on nanofiller distribution, and the analysis is conducted using appropriate numerical techniques. Input parameters include thickness variation, nanofiller distribution, and plate geometry. Shi et al.11 presents a free vibration analysis of a cantilever trapezoidal plate with non-uniform thickness reinforced by non-uniformly distributed nanofillers. The material properties are modeled based on the nanofiller distribution, and the governing equations are solved using numerical methods. Input parameters include plate geometry, thickness variation, and nanofiller distribution. Yas and Mohammadi12 investigated the free vibration behavior of sandwich functionally graded MWCNT/epoxy beams using both experimental and theoretical methods based on Timoshenko beam theory. Theoretical results, obtained via the differential quadrature method, closely align with experimental findings, particularly at lower MWCNT weight fractions. The influence of CNT distribution, geometry, boundary conditions, and temperature on natural frequencies is thoroughly analyzed. Ansari et al.13 presented a finite element-based flexural and free vibration analysis of CNT-reinforced functionally graded plates with uniaxially aligned CNTs. A refined third-order shear deformation theory is applied without shear correction factors, and material properties are computed via the extended rule of mixture. The study evaluates the effects of CNT distribution, boundary conditions, volume fraction, loading patterns, and concentrated mass. Shahraki et al.14 presented buckling and free vibration analysis of FG-CNT reinforced thick rectangular nanoplates on a Kerr foundation using a quasi-3D hyperbolic shear deformation theory and nonlocal strain gradient theory. The material properties are modeled with various CNT dispersion profiles through the thickness, and governing equations are solved using the generalized differential quadrature method. Input parameters include boundary conditions, CNT volume fraction, foundation coefficients, and size effects. Huu Quoc et al.15 developed a four-variable refined plate theory for analyzing free vibration of laminated piezoelectric FG-CNT reinforced composite plates. Material properties incorporate functionally graded or uniform CNT distributions, with quadratic electric potential assumed in piezoelectric layers. Governing equations are derived using Hamilton’s principle and solved via the Navier technique. Parametric analysis explores the effects of CNT volume fraction, distribution, fiber orientation, layer count, and piezoelectric layer thickness on the natural frequencies under different electrical boundary conditions. Truong-Thi et al.16 used the CS-DSG3 method with three-node triangular elements to analyze static and free vibration behavior of CNTRC plates using FSDT. The model incorporates uniformly and functionally graded distributions of SWCNTs through the thickness, with material properties estimated via the rule of mixture. A strain smoothing technique helps avoid shear locking. The influence of CNT distribution, volume fraction, orientation angle, boundary conditions, and width-to-thickness ratio on vibration response is thoroughly examined and validated against existing literature. Jena and Chakraverty17 usef the Differential Quadrature Method (DQM) to analyze the free vibration of SWCNTs with exponentially varying stiffness under nonlocal Euler-Bernoulli beam theory. The method efficiently transforms the governing equation into an eigenvalue problem to obtain frequency parameters. Civalek et al.18 analyzed the free vibration of CNTRC microbeams using couple stress theory and various beam theories, with CNTs distributed in four reinforcement patterns. Material properties are estimated via the rule of mixture, and the governing equations are solved using Navier’s method. Results reveal that X-pattern beams exhibit the highest natural frequencies, and size effects become significant when the beam thickness approaches the material length scale parameter. Wu et al.19 explored the free vibration and buckling behavior of sandwich beams with FG-CNTRC face sheets using Timoshenko beam theory and the differential quadrature method. The influence of CNT volume fraction, core-to-face thickness ratio, slenderness ratio, and boundary conditions is examined in detail. Ladmek20 proposed analytical model accurately predicts the free vibration behavior of FG-CNT reinforced beams using higher-order shear deformation theory and cosine-based material gradation. Validation against existing literature confirms its reliability. Parametric studies reveal the significant effects of nonlocal scale, strain gradient, material distribution, and geometry on the vibrational response of nanobeams. Apart from that Fan et al.21 analysed the nonlinear dynamics of spinning annular plates with variable thickness and initial imperfections, made from two-directional FGMs with power-law property variation. The model is used to study the effects of the thickness coefficient, gradient index, spinning speed, and imperfection. Results show that these parameters significantly influence the stiffness, nonlinear frequency response, and stability of the structure. Cheng et al.22 analysed the 1:1 nonlinear internal resonance of a graphene-reinforced metal foam plate with imperfections under a non-uniform temperature field. The model showed that temperature gradients and imperfections significantly affect stiffness and resonance. Li and She23 investigated the transient response of imperfect GPLRMF plates under moving loads using FSDT, Halpin–Tsai modeling, and Galerkin–Runge–Kutta solutions. Results show that dynamic deflection decreases with increasing moving speed and imperfection amplitude. Hao et al.24 presented the first nonlinear dynamic analysis of a cantilever FGM rectangular plate in a thermal environment. Using Reddy’s third-order theory, Galerkin’s method, and perturbation–Runge–Kutta solutions, it was found that 1:1 internal and 1/2 subharmonic resonances can lead to chaotic, periodic, or quasi-periodic motions, strongly influenced by excitation parameters. Zhang et al.25 investigated the nonlinear dynamics of a clamped–clamped FGM cylindrical shell under external excitation and uniform temperature change. Using FSDT, von Kármán theory, and Galerkin’s method, the results showed that the material gradient index, thickness, and external loading strongly influence the shell’s nonlinear response.

Despite extensive research on the vibration behavior of functionally graded materials (FGMs) and CNT-reinforced functionally graded plates, limited studies have focused on the combined effects of stochasticity and material gradation in multi-layered FGM cantilever plates. Most existing works emphasize deterministic analyses, neglecting the influence of inherent uncertainties in material properties and structural parameters. Additionally, while some studies have explored the impact of CNT reinforcement, the stochastic variations in CNT distribution, power law index, and environmental conditions such as temperature remain underexplored. Furthermore, the mode shape analysis under stochastic conditions has not been comprehensively addressed. This research bridges these gaps by integrating finite element-based Monte Carlo simulations with first-order shear deformation theory to provide a more robust understanding of the dynamic behaviour of CNT-reinforced multi-layered FGMs under uncertain conditions. A Monte Carlo simulation for free vibration analysis utilizes random sampling to statistically evaluate the natural frequencies of a structure, accounting for uncertainties in material properties, geometry, and other parameters. This approach provides a probabilistic insight into how variations in these factors influence the vibration behaviour of the material. Instead of yielding a single deterministic value, it generates multiple possible scenarios with randomly introduced variations, allowing for the estimation of a range of potential natural frequencies. The paper is divided into four sections namely: Introduction, mathematical formulation, numerical results and discussion, and lastly conclusion. Numerical results and discussion

To advance research and innovation in nanocomposite materials, the authors have developed a finite element code to analyse the vibrational behaviour of functionally graded CNT-reinforced FGM plates. The code was first validated through benchmark studies before conducting extensive parametric analyses. These analyses examined the influence of various factors, including (i) the percentage of stochasticity, (ii) the power law index, (iii) plate thickness, (iv) volume fraction, (v) temperature, and (vi) CNT size, providing valuable insights into the dynamic response of these advanced materials.

The Fig. 3 presents the variation of the material properties across different layers of a CNT based functionally graded material (FGM) composite for various power law indices. The x-axis represents the layer number, moving from the topmost to the bottommost layer, while the y-axis denotes the material properties. The figure shows that as the power law index increases, the material properties (except Poisson ratio) decreases more steeply from the top to the bottom layers, indicating a greater variation in material properties. For a lower power law index (p = 0.4), the all the material properties (except Poisson ratio) remains relatively higher and more uniformly distributed across the layers. In contrast, for higher values of p, all material properties (except Poisson ratio) drop significantly, implying a softer bottom layer and a stiffer top layer. This trend highlights the role of the power law index in tailoring material stiffness distribution, which is critical in optimizing the mechanical performance of functionally graded composites for various engineering applications. Conclusions

The novelty of this study includes the stochastic free vibration behaviour of carbon nanotube (CNT)-reinforced multi-layered functionally graded material (FGM) cantilever plates. This research integrates finite element-based Monte Carlo simulation (MCS) to account for material and structural uncertainties. A detailed parametric investigation highlights the significant influence of stochasticity percentage, power law index, plate thickness, volume fraction, temperature, and CNT size on the first three natural frequencies. Additionally, mode shape analysis offers deeper insights into the vibrational characteristics of these advanced composite structures. The major findings are:

Effect of Power Law Index: Increasing the power law index from 0.4 to 1.8 resulted in a consistent decrease in the first three natural frequencies, indicating reduced stiffness due to greater material gradation.

Effect of Plate Thickness: Natural frequencies increased significantly with plate thickness, reflecting enhanced stiffness. However, higher thickness also led to greater variability in stochastic results.

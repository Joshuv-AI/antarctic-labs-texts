Activation of the RAS/PPARα pathway reduces backfat deposition of Gayal (Bos frontalis)
Authors: Ye Yu, Binlong Fu, Runqi Fu, Chunjia Jin, Chen Chen, Xitong An, Pei Yang, Shengyong Mao, Jing Leng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundBackfat deposition is a pivotal economic trait that profoundly affects the quality and production efficiency of beef products in the beef industry. Gayal, as a unique bovine species,primarily found in the high-altitude regions of southwestern China, which provides highly nutritious meat to the local tribal population. However, the intrinsic molecular regulatory mechanisms underlying its backfat deposition remain largely unclear. The present study was designed to compare the differences in backfat deposition traits between Gayal and local cattle, and further elucidate the potential regulatory mechanisms underlying these differences.ResultsThe results shown that Gayal had smaller adipocyte area, fewer number of adipocytes per unit area, and decreased concentration of triglyceride in backfat (P < 0.05). Backfat transcriptome profile exhibited that 875 DEGs (434 up- and 441 down-regulated genes) were obtained between the Gayal and local cattle. Enrichment analysis showed that PPAR signaling pathway (ko03320), renin-angiotensin system (RAS, ko04614), fatty acid metabolic process (GO:0006631), monocarboxylic acid metabolic process (GO:0032787), unsaturated fatty acid metabolic process (GO:0033559), fatty acid oxidation (GO:0019395), and lipid oxidation (GO:0034440) were enriched in the Gayal based on Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes and Gene Ontology (GO). We inspected critical regulators of RAS pathway and found higher level of ACE (P < 0.05), Ang1-7 (0.05 < P < 0.1), and lower level of AngI (P < 0.01) were observed in backfat of Gayal. The phosphorylation level of PPARα was significantly higher in backfat of Gayal (P < 0.05).ConclusionsThese findings suggest that reduced backfat deposition in Gayal is associated with gene expression of the RAS/PPARα pathway. Integrated analysis supports a model wherein the Ang1-7–MAS axis may enhance PPARα signaling, thereby promoting fatty acid β-oxidation and limiting fat accumulation. This study provides novel insights into the physiological mechanisms of backfat deposition and offers potential targets for genetic breeding strategies in cattle.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12864-026-12939-w.

Introduction

Backfat and intermuscular fat, as economically important traits in the beef industry, are critical factors influencing carcass characteristics and meat quality [1]. As a representative of subcutaneous fat, backfat deposition and thickness directly affect the yield of cuts, as well as the flavor and juiciness of the meat [2, 3]. Many studies agree that backfat deposition is the critical predictor of carcass composition, including the lean meat yield and percentage of empty body fat [4–7]. As a primary determinant of the sensory quality of beef, intermuscular fat represents the extent of lipid deposition within skeletal muscle and plays a vital role in influencing the shear force, tenderness, juiciness and flavor scores of beef [8]. Previous studies have shown that backfat thickness was used to predict intramuscular fat content and had a positive relationship with the marbling score in beef cattle [9–11]. Therefore, the extent of backfat deposition can be used as a characterization index of intramuscular fat content in beef.

Renin-angiotensin system (RAS) is identified in brain, heart, and adipose tissue, involving in various physiological process. Two main axes of the RAS in adipose tissue are angiotensin II- angiotensin II type G-protein coupled receptors (AngII-AT) and Ang1-7-MAS receptor. AngII-AT axis can reduce adipocyte differentiation and stimulate adipocyte maturation and adipose hyperplasia [12]. Ang1-7-MAS can promote fatty acid oxidation [13–15]. Thus, RAS becomes an important regulatory factor of adipose tissue metabolism [16]. Peroxisome proliferator-activated receptors (PPARs), which include PPARα, PPARβ and PPARγ, are nuclear receptors that are activated by ligands and regulate lipid metabolism. PPARα acts as the primary regulator of hepatic fatty acid β-oxidation, facilitating the uptake, transport and oxidation of fatty acids while reducing plasma triglyceride levels and modulating lipoprotein metabolism [17, 18]. Despite the well-established roles of RAS and PPAR in humans [19, 20] and mice [21, 22], their function in adipose tissue in livestock remains unclear.

Gayal (Bos frontalis) is one of the least researched semi-wild rare bovine species, renowned for its distinctive meat. It inhabits the complex geographical environment of the Dulong River and Nujiang River basins in Yunnan Province, China [23]. Similar to China's national treasure, the giant panda (Ailuropoda melanoleuca), they primarily feed on bamboo shoots and leaves. Compared with other bovine species, the skeletal muscle of Gayal exhibits a lower intramuscular fat content, a smaller muscle fiber diameter, and a higher muscle fiber density [24, 25]. Generally, the deposition of intermuscular fat is later than that of backfat. Therefore, we hypothesized that Gayal has less backfat deposition when compared with other bovine species. Given the pivotal roles of the RAS and PPARαin lipid metabolism, coupled with the unique low-fat phenotype of Gayal, we hypothesize that the reduced backfat deposition in this breed is associated with the breed-specific regulation of the RAS/PPARα pathway. The present study aims to validate this hypothesis through comparative transcriptomic and molecular biology approaches.

Materials and methods

Ethical statement

The present research was approved by the Animal Care Committee (YNAU20220638-1) at the Faculty of Animal Science and Technology, Yunnan Agricultural University (Kunming, China).

The present study was conducted at the trail pasture of Fenghuang Mountain Gayal Breeding and Expansion Base (altitude 2700 m, Lushui, Yunnan, China). Gayal and local cattle (Yunnan yellow cattle) are in this pasture.

Experimental design

Eight healthy male Gayals (Bos frontalis) and eight healthy male Yunnan yellow cattle (Bos taurus), all two years old with a mean body weight of 240 ± 7.0 kg, were included in this trial. The animals were assigned to either the Gayal group or the Local Cattle group, respectively. The 30-day experimental period consisted of unrestricted grazing on a designated pasture, with free access to water and salt blocks.

Sample collection

Body weight was recorded at the beginning and end of the experiment. Blood samples were collected from jugular vein and frozen in liquid nitrogen for subsequent analysis at 29 d. At the end of the experimental period, after 16 h of fasting with no access to water, 8 Gayal (Bos frontalis) and 8 local cattle were anesthetized with 0.3 mg/kg∙BW xylazine hydrochloride in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions, followed by exsanguination as the definitive euthanasia method. The loin backfat tissues from eight Gayal and eight local cattle were harvested at the 12 and 13th rib on the left and washed 3 times with ice-cold PBS solution. Then, backfat tissues were divided into 2 parts: one part was fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and stained with haematoxylin/eosin (HE) solutions [26], the second part was frozen in liquid nitrogen for enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) and RNA extraction. Representative regions of lean meat, longissimus dorsi et lumborum (ribeye) and psoas major (filet), were used to determine the intramuscular fat deposition.

Histological analysis

Backfat tissues were processed for histological observation to evaluate the number and area of backfat adipocytes. Slides were scanned and captured using a Pannoramic MIDI FL (3DHistech Ltd., Budapest, Hungary). Three images per slide were obtained at 20 × magnification. Adipocyte area and quantity were analyzed using ImageJ software (version 1.8.0, USA) [27]. Only intact adipocytes without overlapping were selected for measurement.

Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay (ELISA)

The triglyceride (TG), angiotensin I (AngI), angiotensin 1–7 (Ang1-7), and angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) were determined by using ELISA kits according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The primers and amplicon sizes of genes are shown in Table S1.

Backfat transcriptome

The total RNA was extracted and sequencing at Shanghai Majorbio Bio-pharm Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China) on the NovaSeq X Plus platform with 2 × 150 bp paired-ended sequencing. (Illumina, Inc., USA). After quality control by fastx_toolkit v0.0.14, clean reads were aligned to reference genome (Bos_taurus, GCF_002263795.3; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/datasets/genome/GCF_002263795.3/) in order to generate gene annotation files using HISAT2 v2.01 [28]. The mapped reads of each sample were assembled by StringTie v1.2.2 in a reference-based approach [29]. Gene counts were normalized using StringTie v1.2.2 and Ballgown with fragments per kilobase of exon per million mapped reads (FPKM) [29]. Only expressed genes (expressing in at least 50% of sample within each group and FPKM > 5 in each sample) were used in subsequent analyses. Differential expression genes were identified by DESeq2 [30]. Pathway enrichment analysis (KEGG) was performed by Python scipy software [31]. GO term enrichment analysis was performed using the Blast2GO and goatools [32, 33].

Quantitative real-time PCR (RT-qPCR) for validation of RNA-Seq data

Total RNA was reverse transcribed to cDNA using NovoScript®Plus All-in-one 1 st Strand cDNA Synthesis SuperMix (gDNA Purge) (E047-01B; Novoprotein, Shanghai, China). The quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) analysis of target genes, including ACAA1, ACADM, ACE, ACSS2, CPA3, KLK1, MAS1, MMUT, RXRG, SLC27A6, and β-actin, were performed by Q-Tower3 (Analytik Jena AG, Jena, Germany) with NovoStart®SYBR qPCR SuperMix Plus (E096-01A; Novoprotein, Shanghai, China). The relative expressions of target genes were calculated via 2−ΔΔCT method [34]. The primers and amplicon sizes of target genes are shown in Table S2.

Western blot analysis

Backfat tissues were homogenized in RIPA buffer (50 mM Tris–HCl (pH 7.4), 150 mM NaCl, 1 mM EDTA-2Na, 1% Triton X-100, 1% sodium deoxycholate and 0.1% SDS; G2002; Wuhan servicebio Technology CO., LTD, China) at 4 °C for 12 h and centrifuged at 4 °C, 15,000 g for 10 min to obtain the supernatant. Protein concentrations were determined by using BCA Protein assay kit (Tiangen, Beijing, China). The supernatant was diluted to a unified protein concentration with RIPA buffer. The procedure of Western blot analysis as described previously [35]. The following primary antibodies were used: anti-Akt (#9272, Cell signaling technology, Danvers, USA), anti-p-Akt473 (#9271, Cell signaling technology, Danvers, USA), anti-PKA (bs-2371R, Bioss Antibody, Beijing, China), anti-p-PKA (#9621, Cell signaling technology, Danvers, USA), anti-PPARɑ (bs-3614R, Bioss Antibody, Beijing, China), anti-p-PPARɑ (bs-4055R, Bioss Antibody, Beijing, China), and actin (reference protein, #4970, Cell signaling technology, Danvers, USA). The secondary antibody was goat anti-rabbit HRP-conjugated (GB23303; 1:3,000; Wuhan servicebio technology CO., LTD, China). Images were visualized on Tanon-5200 Chemiluminescent Imaging System (Tanon Science and Technology, Shanghai, China).

Statistical analysis

The results of adipose TG, AngI, Ang1-7, ACE, number and area of backfat adipocyte, mRNA expression, and serum AngI and Ang1-7 were analyzed by using the independent samples t-test in SPSS 20.0 (SPSS INC, USA), P < 0.05 was considered as statistically significant. For transcriptomic data, differential expression analysis between the two groups was conducted using DESeq2. P values were adjusted for false discovery rate (FDR) using the method described by Benjamini and Hochberg, and standardized differences between the two data sets were measured by Cohen’s d effect sizes with 95% confidence intervals (95% CI) [36]. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified with |log2FC|≥ 1 and FDR < 0.05. KEGG and GO enrichment analysis were performed based on DEGs with FDR < 0.05 and rich factor > 0.1 and visualized using the Majorbio Cloud platform (https://cloud.majorbio.com).

Discussion

This study systematically compared backfat lipid deposition characteristics and transcriptomic profiles between male Gayal and male local cattle, and the results revealed that Gayal exhibit reduced backfat lipid deposition. Our observation of smaller adipocytes in Gayal is consistent with the report on Yak [37]. Notably, this study was restricted to male animals only, given the well-documented sexual dimorphism in adipose metabolism, the current findings cannot be broadly extrapolated to female individuals, then due to the uniqueness and scarcity of the experimental animals employed in this study, a conventional a priori power analysis was not conducted prior to experimentation to formally validate the sample size (n = 8 per group), which represents a key limitation of this research. While the study assessed only backfat, further investigation across multiple fat depots is needed to address tissue specificity.

In this study, the backfat transcriptome data indicates that 875 DEGs were identified between the local cattle and Gayal. Based these DEGs, 2 lipid metabolism-related KEGG pathways (PPAR signaling pathway and RAS) and 12 lipid metabolism-related GO terms were enriched in the Gayal. Among these pathways, renin-angiotensin system (RAS) is involved in various physiological process, including the regulation of blood pressure, fluid and electrolyte homeostasis. Several local RASs in brain, heart, and adipose tissue have been founded. Angiotensinogen, the main precursor protein of RAS system, can be cleaved by renin and angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) to produce angiotensin I (AngI) and AngII, respectively [38]. Then, ACE2 converts AngI and AngII into Ang1-9 and Ang1-7 in sequence. ACE2 and carboxypeptidase A3 (CPA3) also converts AngI to Ang1-9 via cleaving the C-terminal Leu [39–41]. Kallikrein 1 (KLK1), as known as the only tissue kallikrein to generate kinin peptides, converts angiotensinogen to AngII [42, 43]. MAS-related G protein-coupled receptor 1, which encoded by MAS1 gene, can specifically combine with Ang1-7 and be involved in regulating the lipid metabolism [44, 45]. Two main axes of the RAS are AngII-AngII type G-protein coupled receptors (AngII-AT) and Ang1-7-MAS receptor. AngII can promote fatty acid synthase expression and increase TG accumulation in adipocytes, while reduce adipocyte differentiation and stimulate adipocyte maturation and adipose hyperplasia through AT1 and AT2, respectively [12]. Ang1-7-MAS axis can antagonize the function of AngII-AT1. Previous studies shown that Ang1-7-MAS induced lipolysis. Activation of Ang1-7-MAS axis increased mRNA levels of fatty acid oxidation-related genes to enhance energy expenditure and reduce abdominal fat content [13–15]. Therefore, RAS system becomes an important regulatory factor of adipose tissue metabolism [16]. Interestingly, our transcriptomic profiles shown that DEGs (ACE, CPA3, KLK1, and MAS1) of RAS were enriched in the backfat of Gayal. Higher level of adipose ACE and Ang1-7, and lower level of adipose AngI were also observed in backfat of Gayal. Furthermore, our findings suggest that the upregulation of the Ang1-7-MAS axis is correlated with reduced lipid deposition, and that this effect may be mediated through the PPARα signaling pathway. Our transcriptomic data revealed significant enrichment of PPAR-related DEGs, including upregulated ACAA1, RXRG, SLC27A6, ACADM and ACSS2, which is consistent with previous study [46–48]. These findings suggest that the RAS in the backfat of Gayal exhibits a greater tendency to a Ang1-7–mas axis, which promotes lipolysis and energy expenditure. This strongly supports our observation of a reduced fat deposition phenotype, suggesting that local RAS reprogramming may play a role in regulating the unique lipid metabolism of Gayal.

This coordinated upregulation suggests an enhanced capacity for fatty acid β-oxidation and lipid metabolism, collectively contributing to the observed reduction in backfat accumulation. Previous studies explored the role of critical regulators of RAS pathway (ACE2, Ang1-7, and MAS1 receptor) in lipid metabolism [35, 49, 50]. Therefore, we investigated whether RAS pathway enhanced the lipid metabolism via Akt signaling pathway and PKA signaling pathway in the backfat of Gayal, which are the downstream of RAS pathway. Ang1-7 can increase the cAMP levels to activate PKA through MAS1 [51]. Akt plays a vital role in cell energy balance. After PI3K activation, Akt is activated and may repress forkhead box protein O1 (FoxO1) transcription factors, which is associated with the expression of uncoupling protein 1 (UCP1) and may correlate with enhanced adipocyte thermogenesis [52–54], reduced lipogenesis, and decreased lipid deposition [55–57]. PPARs play a vital role in lipid metabolism. PPARα is known to facilitate the beta-oxidation of fatty acids via heterodimerizing with RXR, thereby recruiting coactivators to bind to PPARα target genes to promote transcription [58]. PKA may contribute to transcription of PPARα through the phosphorylation of mitogen-activated protein kinase. Previous studies have shown that Ang1-7 is associated with Akt signaling, while Akt inhibitor treatment attenuated the effects of Ang1-7 on primary brown adipocytes [35]. Although we did not observe a statistically significant increase in PKA phosphorylation, the observed trend, coupled with the well-documented role of PKA in upstream PPARα regulation [58], leads us to speculate that this pathway may still contribute to the observed PPARα-related transcriptional changes in a manner that our current sample size or method could not definitively capture. This proposed pathway would enhance fatty acid β-oxidation and overall lipid metabolism, thereby reducing backfat deposition. This finding implicates the RAS system within the core regulatory network of lipid metabolism in Gayal. Furthermore, transcriptomic analysis indicated an upregulation of key PPARα target genes related to fatty acid oxidation (e.g., ACAA1, ACADM, SLC27A6, RXRG) in Gayal, providing transcriptional evidence of enhanced β-oxidation capacity. This concerted upregulation, concurrent with elevated PPARα phosphorylation, establishes a coherent molecular network that promotes fatty acid breakdown, ultimately contributing to reduced lipid deposition.

Conclusions

Integrating histomorphological, biochemical, transcriptomic, and protein expression data, our research proposes a novel model for the mechanism reducing backfat deposition in Gayal. The results indicate that the expression patterns of genes related to the local adipose Ang1-7–MAS axis (ACE, CPA3, KLK1, MAS1) are consistent with the involvement of this axis in regulating backfat deposition; we hypothesize that this axis may orchestrate reduced lipid accumulation by potentiating PPARα signaling and upregulating fatty acid β-oxidation genes. However, functional proof of pathway activation remains to be established. This work unveils a potential regulatory paradigm for the low-fat phenotype in Gayal and establishes a significant foundation for future research on lipid metabolism in livestock.

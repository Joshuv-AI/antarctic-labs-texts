MMP-2 Responsive Gold Nanorods Loaded with HSP-70 siRNA for Enhanced Photothermal Tumor Therapy
Authors: Ran Sun, Yaoqi Wang, Qi Sun, Yan Su, Jie Zhang, Danni Liu, Ran Huo, Yang Tian, Myagmarsuren Baldan, Shuang Zhang, Chunying Cui
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractGold nanorods (Au NRs) are a valuable photothermal nanomaterial for tumor therapy. However, when treated with Au NRs for photothermal therapy, the expression of heat shock proteins in tumors will increase, which will induce heat resistance in tumor cells and reduce the photothermal therapeutic effect of Au NRs. By RNA interference, the expression of heat shock proteins would be effectively inhibited to improve the efficasy of tumor photothermal therapy. However, deep and noninvasive tissue penetration remains a great obstacle to applying siRNA successfully. Thus, the nanoplatform AGC/HSP-70 siRNA was designed for enhanced photothermal tumor therapy by RNA interference. In the AGC/HSP-70 siRNA complex, the Au–S bond modified the matrix metalloproteinase-2 (MMP-2)-sensitive peptide GPLGLAG on the surface of gold nanorods. Moreover, the natural basic polysaccharide (chitosan) was reacted with the peptide by an amide bond for delivering heat shock protein 70 silencing siRNA (HSP-70 siRNA). Modifying the MMP-2-sensitive linker could cause more Au NRs to accumulate in tumors to exert a photothermal effect and promote the penetration of HSP-70 siRNA and chitosan complexes into deep tumor tissues. In vitro experiments indicated that the enzymolysis of the MMP-2-sensitive linker for AGC/HSP-70 siRNA could promote the cellular uptake and perinuclear distribution of HSP-70 siRNA in tumor cells, which may be due to the smaller size and positive electricity of the complexes. All of these results ensured the efficient gene silencing effect of HSP-70 siRNA to enhance the photothermal therapeutic effect of Au NRs in tumor tissues, as demonstrated by the gene silencing and cellular apoptotic experiments. In vivo experiments further proved that the AGC/HSP-70 siRNA nanoplatform efficiently improved the photothermal effect of Au NRs. In summary, this work proved that AGC/HSP-70 siRNA is a promising drug delivery strategy for enhancing the photothermal therapy of tumors by regulating the photothermal sensitivity of deep tumor cells as well as retaining more Au NRs in tumor tissues, and also provides a novel strategy for tumor photothermal therapy.Keywords: photothermal therapy, gold nanorods, HSP-70 siRNA, RNA interference, MMP-2 responsive, gene therapy

Introduction

Photothermal therapy (PTT) has been intensively investigated as a highly effective and noninvasive therapy capable of eliminating various types of cancers.1−3 Various nanoplatforms have also been developed for PTT in the past few decades.4−6 Among the many photothermal nanomaterials,7 gold nanorods (Au NRs) provide remarkable opportunities due to their tunable longitudinal surface plasmon resonance (SPR) in the near-infrared region (NIR) for tissue penetration8 and the high photothermal conversion efficiency.9−12 However, the therapeutic efficiency of PTT is dramatically suppressed by the stress-induced heat shock proteins (HSP).13,14 Even a slight increase in temperature would trigger the up-regulation of HSP in tumor cells to impair the effect of PTT.15,16 Therefore, it is important to develop a strategy to conquer the tolerance of tumor tissues for hyperthermia to maximize the effect of the PPT.

Various HSP inhibitors have been exploited to reduce the thermal defense of tumor cells,17,18 among which small interfering RNA (siRNA) has gained significant attention. SiRNA has also been widely considered an effective tool for sequence-specific gene silencing and regulating the expression of target proteins.19,20 As such, siRNA-based therapies have become a promising strategy for treating multiple diseases.21−23 Notably, HSP-70 is a crucial member of the HSP family, a key protein for cell thermal resistance, and is overexpressed in many tumor cells.24−26 Reducing the expression of HSP-70 with the RNAi technology is promising in achieving PTT sensitization for cancer cells.27,28 However, exogenous HSP-70 siRNA makes it challenging to exert an ideal gene-silencing effect due to the rapid degradation in the bloodstream, renal clearance, and poor penetration of cell membranes.29−32 Thus, designing an effective carrier for gene–drug delivery is vitally important.

Chitosan (CS) has gained more attention among the widely reported gene delivery materials due to its good biocompatibility, attractive gene loading, and protective effects.33,34 In our previous study, a CS-modified and antitumor monoclonal antibody (anti-EpCAM) decorated graphene oxide (GO) was designed for the delivery of siRNA, exhibiting promising siRNA loading, protection effects, and an excellent tumor targeting and treatment effect.35 Notably, the above effects are due to the fact that chitosan is a natural polycationic basic polysaccharide, which can interact with negative siRNA to form uniform complexes through electrostatic interaction and exert protective effects for siRNAs.36−38 Simultaneously, chitosan also has mucosal adhesive properties and can be easily taken up by mucosal epithelial cells to enhance the cell transfection ability of siRNA.39 Therefore, chitosan polymers can effectively solve the siRNA protection and cellular uptake issues for the application of RNAi technology.40−42 However, the drug delivery system prepared in our previous research had a sustained release effect for siRNA, which may hinder the penetration of siRNA into deep tumor tissues and impede the treatment effect of siRNA.

Matrix metalloproteinase-2 (MMP-2) is a highly expressed zinc-dependent proteolytic enzyme found to be overexpressed in various malignant tumors. Additionally, MMP-2 has been widely applied for designing enzymatic-responsive nanodrug delivery systems for tumor diagnosis and therapy.43−45 In this work, we rationally connected Au NRs and the HSP-70 siRNA carrier CS with an MMP-2 sensitive peptide linker (MPa-GPLGAG), which had a rapid and specific response to MMP-2. As shown in Scheme 1, the constructed nanoplatform could accumulate in superficial tumor tissues and release CS and HSP-70 siRNA complexes, exhibiting a strong penetration effect for tumor tissues to induce the tumor cells sensitive to PTT. The designed nanoplatform also demonstrates the following properties: (1) responsive release of CS and HSP-70 siRNA complexes to deliver siRNA into deep tumor tissues; (2) effective cellular uptake and perinuclear delivery of HSP-70 siRNA in tumor cells; and (3) enhanced photothermal treatment effect by Au NRs and HSP-70 siRNA synergistic effect. Furthermore, the MMP-2 responsive gold nanorods loaded with HSP-70 siRNA are believed to provide a new strategy for enhanced photothermal tumor therapy.46−48

Schematic Illustration of Preparation and Mechanisms of AGC/siRNA

(A) Gold nanorods were synthesized using the seed growth method, and enzyme-sensitive peptides were modified on the surface of gold nanorods via Au–S bonds. The amide bonds are then conjugated with chitosan to obtain AGC vectors. Then AGC/HSP-70 siRNA was obtained by coincubation AGCs with HSP-70 siRNA. (B) AGC/HSP-70 siRNA is hydrolyzed into CS and HSP-70 siRNA complexes under the action of MMP-2, which is highly expressed in tumor tissues. The complexes have an excellent tissue penetration effect and can easily enter tumor cells and achieve lysosome escape. Therefore, the tumor tissues are more sensitive to the photothermal therapy of god nanorods with the help of HSP-70 siRNA.

Materials and Methods

Materials

Cetyltrimethylammonium bromide (CTAB), chitosan (CS, Mw = 30,000), l-ascorbic acid (AA), and gold chloride hydrate (HAuCl4·H2O) were purchased from Macklin. N-hydroxysuccinimide (NHS, 98%), 1-ethyl-(3-(dimethylamino)propyl) carbodiimine hydrochloric acid (EDC·HCl, 99%), 2-(N-morpholino) ethanesulfonic acid (MES, 99%), RNase A, MTT, and Proteinase K were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. Matrix metalloproteinase-2-specific-sensitive polypeptide sequence Mpa-GPLGLAG-COOH and COOH-PEG2k-SH were purchased from Nanjing Peptide Industry. 4T1 cell line was obtained from the Cell Bank of the Chinese Academy of Sciences. Mouse-derived HSP-70 siRNA, HSP-70 siRNA-Cy5, the negative control HSP-70 siRNA (NC), probe HSP-70 customized gene qRT-PCR quantitative kit, and GAPDH/β-actin calibration qRT-PCR quantitative kit were purchased from Gima Pharmaceutical Technology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Heparin sodium, TBE (10×) boric acid buffer, agarose, RNA loading buffer (5×), 4% tissue cell fixative, PMSF, RIPA cell lysate, and DEPC water were purchased from Solarbio (China). Matrix metalloproteinase-2 was purchased from Sino Biological. RPMI-1640 medium, penicillin/streptomycin, and trypsin were purchased from Hyclone Laboratories Inc. Fetal bovine serum (FBS) was purchased from SERATECH (Germany). Lipo 2000, Hoechst 33342, Trizol, and Annexin V-FITC apoptosis detection kits were purchased from Beijing Biyutian Biological Co., Ltd. Mouse heat shock protein 70 ELISA kit was purchased from FANKEW.

4T1 mouse breast tumor cells were cultured in the RPMI-1640 medium containing 10% FBS, 1% penicillin, and streptomycin and kept at 37 °C with 5% CO2. Female BALB/c mice (18 and 20 g) were purchased from Vitonlihua.

Synthesis of Gold Nanorods

This study synthesized the photothermal agent gold nanorods by the crystal seed growth method.47 First, the gold nanoseed solution was prepared. Three milliliters of CTAB solution (0.1 M) was prepared with deionized water, and 0.1 mL of chloroauric acid (0.01 M) was added to it at room temperature and mixed evenly. Under vigorous stirring (1500 rpm, 25 °C), 0.24 mL of ice-cold sodium borohydride (0.01 M) was added and activated in the dark for 60 min in a water bath at 28 °C. Then, the gold nanorod growth solution was prepared. Forty milliliters of CTAB solution (0.1 M) was prepared with deionized water, and 2 mL of chloroauric acid (0.01 M) was added to it at room temperature and mixed evenly. We then added 400 μL of silver nitrate solution (0.01 M), 400 μL of ascorbic acid solution (0.1 M), and 1.6 mL of dilute hydrochloric acid (1 M) successively and mixed well. Finally, 100 μL of seed liquid was added to the growth solution. Furthermore, Au NRs with uniform particle size were obtained after reacting overnight in the dark under the conditions of 28 °C.

Synthesis of AGC

The Mpa-GPLGLAG-COOH enzyme-sensitive polypeptide was the linker between the gold nanorod and chitosan. The sulfhydryl group at one end of the peptide forms a stable Au–S bond with the gold nanorods,49−52 and the carboxyl group at the other end forms an amide bond with the free amino group of chitosan with the addition of EDC and NHS. The specific steps were as follows. Six milligrams of Mpa-GPLGLAG-COOH polypeptide was dissolved in 1 mL of deionized water by sonication. Under magnetic stirring in a water bath at 25 °C, 200 μL of concentrated gold nanorod solution was added dropwise and reacted for 24 h in the dark. After the reaction, the solution was centrifuged at 9000 rpm for 10 min, and the precipitate was washed with water three times to obtain Au-GPLGLAG-COOH. The amide bond between chitosan and polypeptide was prepared using a typical synthesis method. The 4 mg of chitosan was dissolved in 1 mL of MES solution (0.1 M), and an ultrasound was performed for 30 min. Then, it was added to the Au-GPLGLAG-COOH precipitate, and ultrasound continued for 1 h. EDC (3 mg) and NHS (3 mg) MES solutions were slowly added to the system, sonicated for 3 h, and then stirred for 19 h at 25 °C in the dark. After the reaction, the system was centrifuged at 9000 rpm for 10 min. The supernatant was discarded, and the precipitate was washed twice with 1% acetic acid solution and once with pure water. The Au-GPLGLAG-CS vector was obtained by resuspension with deionized water. In subsequent experiments, the Au-GPLGLAG-CS vector was abbreviated as the AGC vector.

Characterization of AGC

The structures of gold nanorods, Au-GPLGLAG-COOH, and AGC were characterized by UV–vis absorption spectroscopy (UV-2600, Kyoto, Japan), a nanoparticle size analyzer, and transmission electron microscopy (TEM, Tokyo, Japan), respectively. The final concentration of AGC was determined by using an inductively coupled plasma series mass spectrometer (ICP-MS, Agilent). The Zeta potentials of gold nanorods, Au-GPLGLAG-COOH, and AGC were obtained by dynamic light scattering (DLS, Zetasizer-Nano-ZS90, Malvern, U.K.). Furthermore, the temperature rise of AGC under different power levels and the repeatability of photothermal conversion were recorded by an 808 nm infrared laser (LR-MFJ-808/5000 mW, Changchun Lei Rui photoelectric technology Co., LTD, China) and a thermal imager (FOTRIC).51

Preparation of AGC/HSP-70 siRNA

One OD HSP-70 siRNA (33 μg) was dissolved in 250 μL of fresh diethylpyrocarbonate (DEPC) water to obtain 10 μmol/L HSP-70 siRNA solution. AGC/HSP-70 siRNA was obtained by mixing 0.01 nmol of HSP-70 siRNA with different amounts of AGC solution and incubating at room temperature by shaking overnight.

Determination of Maximum siRNA Load Ratio of Vector

In this study, the loading ratio of HSP-70 siRNA for the AGC vector was obtained by measuring the fluorescence value of supernatant after siRNA loading.53 AGC/HSP-70 siRNA complexes were prepared in a certain amount of substance ratios (74–2957, n/n). AGC/HSP-70 siRNA complexes with different substance ratios were centrifuged, and the supernatant was added for quantitative dilution. The fluorescence value of the supernatant was determined with a fluorescence spectrophotometer (RF6000, SHIMADZU, Japan). By the Cy5 fluorescence standard curve, the concentration of supernatant-free siRNA was calculated and the siRNA load of the AGC vector was calculated.

Anti-Rnase A degradation In Vitro

The free HSP-70 siRNA (naked HSP-70 siRNA) group and AGC/HSP-70 siRNA group were set up. 10 μL of HSP-70 siRNA (0.1 μM) and 10 μL of AGC/HSP-70 siRNA complexes (0.1 μM HSP-70 siRNA) were incubated with 4 μL of RNase A solution (10 μg/mL). At the set time point, 4 μL of proteinase K solution (5 mg/mL) was added to the centrifuge tube to terminate the degradation of HSP-70 siRNA by RNase A, and the mixture was incubated for 30 min at 37 °C. Then, 20 μL of heparin sodium (0.8 mg/mL) was added, and the HSP-70 siRNA in the vector was entirely replaced by shaking at room temperature for 30 min. Finally, 10 μL of sample solution was taken out, and an agarose gel retardation experiment was performed to determine the siRNA-protecting ability of the AGC vector.

HSP-70 siRNA Release Experiment In Vitro

1 μL of Cy5-labeled HSP-70 siRNA (10 μmol/L) was incubated overnight with 100 μL of AGC vector solution and then centrifuged to obtain the fluorescence-labeled AGC/HSP-70 siRNA complexes. Following DEPC resuspension into the 500 μL solution, 0.5 μL of MMP-2 enzyme (0.25 μg/μL) was added to hydrolyze the specific polypeptide sequence. Finally, the fluorescence intensity time image of the solution was scanned with a fluorescence spectrophotometer after shaking for 30 min to determine the release of siRNA from the vector.54

Cellular Uptake

In this study, cellular uptake was performed with the Cy5-labeled HSP-70 siRNA. 4T1 cells were planted on the confocal dish and incubated for 24 h. Subsequently, the cells were treated with blank medium, free HSP-70 siRNA (10 nM), AGC/HSP-70 siRNA (with 10 nM HSP-70 siRNA) without MMP-2 enzyme, and the AGC/HSP-70 siRNA group with MMP-2 enzyme (0.25 μg/μL). The cells were cultured at 37 °C in a 5% CO2 incubator for 4 h. The medicated medium was discarded and washed three times with PBS. Hoechst 33342 dissolved in PBS was added, incubated for 10 to 15 min, and washed thrice with PBS. Finally, 200 μL of PBS was added before observing the cellular uptake by a confocal laser microscope (CLSM, TCSSP5, Leica, Wetzlar, Germany).

Evaluation of Tumor Sphere Penetration

In this experiment, the tumor penetration of AGC/HSP-70 siRNA with or without enzymatic hydrolysis by MMP-2 was evaluated by tumor spheres from the three-dimensional level.55 The 4T1 cell suspension was seeded in 96-well plates coated with low melting point agarose and cultured at 37 °C in a 5% CO2 incubator. When the medium turned yellow, the complete DMEM medium was replaced, and the tumor spheres were cultured for 10 days before the experiment. Free siRNA (50 nM), AGC/HSP-70 siRNA (with 50 nM HSP-70 siRNA) without MMP-2 enzyme, and AGC/HSP-70 siRNA group with MMP-2 enzyme (0.25 μg/μL) were added to the corresponding wells and cultured for another 24 h. Then, the tumor spheres were gently aspirated with an eye dropper and transferred to a confocal dish. After the cells were washed with PBS, Hoechst 33342 was added to stain for 15–20 min, and the laser confocal microscope (CLSM, TCSSP5, Leica, Wetzlar, Germany) was used to observe each layer of the tumor sphere to determine the penetration depth of HSP-70 siRNA.

Cell Toxicity Assay

MTT assay detected the toxicity of AGC vectors and AGC/HSP-70 siRNA on 4T1 cells. 4T1 cells were seeded in 96-well plates at a density of 8 × 103 cells per well and cultured for 24 h. Cells were treated with different concentrations of AGC (4–80 μg/mL) vector solution and AGC/HSP-70 siRNA complexes. Following incubation for 24 h, we added 25 μL of 5 mg/mL MTT working liquid to each well, mixed it well by shaking, and cultured in 37 °C, 5% CO2 incubator for 4 h. After the final addition of 150 μL DMSO, the absorbance of each well was measured using a multifunctional enzyme marker (EnSpire, PerkinElmer) with a detection wavelength of 570 nm.

MTT assay detected the antitumor activity of different transfection compounds in vitro.(56) The cells were treated with AGC/NC, AGC/HSP-70 siRNA and MMP-2 enzyme-treated AGC/HSP-70 siRNA at different siRNA concentrations (10, 20, 60, 120, 200 nM). After 6 h of siRNA transfection, the cells were irradiated with an 808 nm near-infrared laser with a power of 1.911 W/cm2 for 3 min.

Real-Time PCR

The cells were treated separately with blank medium, free HSP-70 siRNA, Lipo 2000/HSP-70 siRNA, AGC/HSP-70 siRNA, and MMP-2 treated AGC/HSP-70 siRNA for 48h. The total RNA in each group was extracted with Trizol lysate. RNA concentrations in each group were then measured with a Nanodrop-1000 (Thermo Scientific). 2 μg of RNA of 4T1 cells were reverse transcribed into cDNA, and then cDNA was transcribed to HSP-70 mRNA with housekeeping gene GAPDH as an internal control. The value relative quantity of HSP-70 mRNA expressions was calculated with the average threshold cycle (Ct) by the delta–delta Ct (2–△△Ct) test.

ELISA

The cell lysate was prepared by adding 1 mL of RIPA lysate to 10 μL of PMSF. The total proteins of the cells treated with blank medium, free HSP-70 siRNA, Lipo 2000/HSP-70 siRNA, AGC/HSP-70 siRNA, and MMP-2 treated AGC/HSP-70 siRNA were extracted with the lysate. Then, a BCA protein kit quantitatively determined the total protein in each experimental group, and the amount of HSP-70 protein was determined by a mouse heat shock protein 70 ELISA kit.

Apoptotic Morphology

In total, 1 × 105 4T1 cells were inoculated in a confocal laser dish with an inner diameter of 20 mm and cultured in a 5% CO2 incubator at 37 °C for 24 h. 500 μL of blank medium, AGC/NC, AGC/HSP-70 siRNA, and MMP-2 treated AGC/HSP-70 siRNA were added into the confocal small dishes separately and incubated for 6 h. After 6 h, each confocal dish was irradiated with 808 nm near-infrared light and cultured for 18 h. Then, 500 μL of 4% fixative was added and fixed for 10–15 min and then washed with PBS three times. Hoechst 33342 dissolved with PBS was added for 10 to 15 min and then discarded and rinsed with PBS thrice. Finally, 200 μL PBS was added and observed by a confocal laser microscope (CLSM, TCSSP5, Leica, Wetzlar, Germany).

Flow Cytometry Measure Cell Apoptosis Rate

4T1 cells were seeded in 96-well plates and cultured for 24 h, waiting for cell adhesion to be established before drug administration. Blank medium, AGC/NC, AGC/HSP-70, and MMP-2 treated AGC/HSP-70 siRNA were added into the 96-well plate and cultured for another 6 h. Then, each well was irradiated with an 808 nm near-infrared light. Then, the cells were incubated for another 18 h at 37 °C in a 5% CO2 incubator. After 24 h, the cells of each group were collected and washed with PBS. The cells were lightly suspended by adding 195 μL of Annexin V-FITC binding solution into each group. Then, 5 μL of Annexin V-FITC and 10 μL of propyl iodide staining solution were added sequentially. Finally, the sample was incubated at room temperature for 20 min in the dark, and 300 μL of binding buffer was added so that the total volume of the sample reached 500 μL. The apoptosis rate in each group was immediately tested by flow cytometry (BD LSRFortessa, Becton Dickinson).

Evaluation of the Photothermal Effect of AGC/HSP-70 siRNA In Vivo

All animal experiments were performed per the Animal Welfare Ethics Committee of Capital Medical University guidelines with an approval number (ID: AEEI-2023-047). The murine breast cancer xenograft was established and subcutaneously injected with 5 × 106 4T1 cells/mouse. When the tumor volumes reached 100 mm3, the 4T1 tumor-bearing mice were randomly divided into two groups (n = 3): the normal saline (NS) and AGC/HSP-70 siRNA. Each mouse was injected with 50 μL of normal saline for the NC group. For the AGC/HSP-70 siRNA group, each mouse was injected with 50 μL AGC/HSP-70 siRNA containing 40 μg Au. All the drugs were administered by paratumoral injection. Twenty-four hours later, an 808 nm laser was used to irradiate the tumor site of mice for 3 min to achieve the photothermal effect. Meanwhile, an infrared thermal imager (FOTRIC) was used to record the temperature changes of the two groups every 30 s within 3 min.

AGC/HSP-70 siRNA Tumor Sections Penetration Effect

Established murine breast cancer 4T1 tumor-bearing mice were randomly divided into three groups (n = 3): AP group, APC/HSP-70 siRNA group, and AGC/HSP-70 siRNA group. Each mouse in each group was injected with 50 μL of the corresponding drug, which contained 40 μg of Au. The drugs were injected by paratumoral administration. After 24 h, the tumor area of the mice was dissected, and 2 μm thickness was cut by the blade and immersed in an electron microscope fixative solution for subsequent preparation and observation by transmission electron microscope (JEM1230, JEOL, Japan).

Evaluation of Antitumor Pharmacodynamics of AGC/HSP-70 siRNA In Vivo

Tumor-bearing mice heterotopic transplanted with 4T1 cells were divided into four groups (n = 5). Normal saline (Control), Au-PEG-CS/NC (APC/NC, containing Au 0.04 mg/mouse), Au-PEG-CS/HSP-70 siRNA (APC/HSP-70 siRNA, containing Au 0.04 mg/mouse) and AGC/HSP-70 siRNA (AGC/HSP-70 siRNA, containing Au 0.04 mg/mouse) were injected every 2 days for 4 times. The mice were irradiated with 808 nm near-infrared light 24 h after injection. All mice were killed, and tumors and major organs from each group were collected after the last administration. H&E staining was used to observe the changes in the histomorphological structure of the main organs and tumor tissues to study the inhibitory effect of drugs on tumors and their toxicity to major organs.

Statistics

The data were expressed as mean ± SD (standard deviation). t test was used for statistical analysis. A value of P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant, and a value of P < 0.01 was considered very significant.

Results and Discussion

Synthesis and Characterization of AGC

The preparation of AGC is described in Scheme 1A. The gold nanorods (Au NRs) with an absorption peak at 808 nm were first synthesized by seed growth. As shown in Figure 1A,B, the prepared Au NRs had a bimodal particle size with the length and width of 71.4 ± 25.5 and 4.1 ± 1.0 nm, respectively, and positive surface charge (zeta potential, or ξ) of 35.7 ± 0.4 mV. After being coated with MMP-2 sensitive peptides via Au–S bonds, the length and width of the nanorods were increased to 215.3 ± 77.7 and 35.9 ± 8.0 nm, respectively, while the surface charge was changed to −12.4 ± 0.3 mV, which suggested the successful decoration of the peptides. When the nanorods were modified with chitosan, the length and width of the nanorods were decreased to 137.6 ± 57.0 and 18.0 ± 4.3 nm. Additionally, the surface charge was changed to 42.7 ± 0.8 mV, indicating that chitosan decoration could increase the nanorods’ dispensability and endow the vector with the siRNA delivery ability.57 The transmission electron microscope images also demonstrated that the prepared nanoparticles showed a rod-like structure, and the dispensability of the nanorods was notably improved when decorated with the peptides and chitosan (Figure 1C).

The UV–vis absorbance spectra in Figure S1 suggested that the prepared Au nanorods exhibited a strong SPR absorption at 808 nm, ensuring the photothermal character of the nanosystem.58 As shown in Figure 1G, the temperature of the Au nanorod solution was significantly increased when irradiated by an NIR laser at 808 nm, and the degree of the temperature increase was enhanced as the NIR irradiation power increased. Moreover, the rise in temperature was only related to the photothermal conversion properties of the Au nanorods themselves and showed no relationship with the solvent for dispersing the nanorods. Moreover, the repeatability of photothermal conversion for the nanorods was verified, and the photothermal conversion efficiency had no decrease even when irradiated five times, which ensured the photothermal treatment effect of the nanorods (Figure 1H).

siRNA Loading Ability and Stimuli-Responsive Release of AGC

siRNA loading efficiency and AGC’s MMP-2 responsive drug release behavior were studied in Figure 2. AGC/HSP-70 siRNA nanoparticles with different substance ratios (1:74, 1:185, 1:370, 1:739, 1:1848, and 1:2957) were prepared. The amount of free fluorescent siRNA in the supernatant after the coincubation of the AGC vector and siRNA was measured using a fluorescence spectrophotometer. The siRNA loading rate was calculated indirectly. As can be seen from Figure 1D, with the increase of the AGC vector’s amount, the siRNA loading rate increased linearly when the amount of the vector was lower than 9.24 nmol and remained flat when the amount of the vector was further increased. As such, this finding indicates that the load capacity of the AGC vector has reached saturation. Therefore, the siRNA and Au ratio (n/n) was determined to be 1:1848 in subsequent experiments to ensure the siRNA loading efficiency. Moreover, the siRNA protection ability of the AGC vector was evaluated, as shown in Figure 1E. Free HSP-70 siRNA was completely degraded by RNase A within 5 min. Simultaneously, the HSP-70 siRNA loaded by the AGC vector remained in a bright band after 8 h of incubation with RNase A, indicating that the AGC vector could effectively protect siRNA from enzymatic hydrolysis. All of these results suggested that AGC was an outstanding vector for siRNA loading and protection and ensured siRNA’s activity and delivery efficiency in vivo.

Although siRNA delivery systems have shown prospects in tumor treatment, tumor tissue penetration remained a serious obstacle for their successful application.59 Thus, the tumor MMP-2 responsive AGC/HSP-70 siRNA was established in this research to release HSP-70 siRNA and chitosan complexes and enhance the tumor-penetrating effect of siRNA in deep tumors. The MMP-2 responsive HSP-70 siRNA release effect was measured by the time-scanning mode of the fluorescence spectrophotometer. As shown in Figure 1F, the release rate of HSP-70 siRNA increased rapidly after incubating with MMP-2 for 1 h, and 95.5% of HSP-70 siRNA was finally released completely. However, there was almost no release of HSP-70 siRNA in the control group without MMP-2. These results indicated that when the MMP-2 enzyme-sensitive polypeptide linker was hydrolyzed in the tumor microenvironment, the large AGC vector could be transformed into small HSP-70 siRNA and chitosan complexes. The released complexes could penetrate deep tumor tissues to play the siRNA-interfering role more efficiently.

Cellular Uptake and Cytotoxicity Evaluation of AGC/HSP-70 siRNA

Effective cellular uptake is key for HSP-70 siRNA to exert RNA interference and downregulate protein expression. In this study, the fluorescence of Cy5 HSP-70 siRNA was applied to evaluate the cellular uptake behavior of siRNA in 4T1 cells in vitro. The 4T1 cells were incubated with a blank medium, naked HSP-70 siRNA, AGC/HSP-70 siRNA without MMP-2, and AGC/HSP-70 siRNA with MMP-2 for 4 h (Figure 2A). For the naked Cy5 HSP-70 siRNA-treated group, there was no obvious fluorescence of Cy5 HSP-70 siRNA in 4T1 cells. When loaded into the AGC vector (AGC/HSP-70 siRNA), the fluorescence of Cy5 HSP-70 siRNA in the cells was significantly increased. Additionally, added fluorescence was observed when the AGC/HSP-70 siRNA was incubated with MMP-2. More interestingly, for the ACG/HSP-70 siRNA-treated group, some of the red fluorescence of Cy5 surrounded the nuclei of 4T1 cells, as indicated by the white arrow in Figure 2A. For the ACG/HSP-70 siRNA and MMP-2 coincubation group, increased red fluorescence of Cy5 was visible around the nucleus, and some of the Cy5 fluorescence was colocalized with the blue fluorescence of the nucleus. These results suggested that the AGC vector could increase the cellular uptake of HSP-70 siRNA in cells and furtherly deliver more AGC/HSP-70 siRNA around the nucleus, likely attributed to the electrostatic interaction of the cationic material chitosan with cell and nuclear membranes.60,61 Moreover, when incubated with MMP-2, the cellular uptake and perinuclear localization effect were further enhanced since the size of the HSP-70 siRNA and chitosan complexes was smaller when compared with AGC/HSP-70 siRNA, which may improve the cellular and nuclear entry behavior of HSP-70 siRNA. The addition of MMP-2 in this experiment was to mimic the tumor microenvironment. When the overexpressed MMP-2 hydrolyzed the sensitive peptides of the AGC in the tumor microenvironment, the positive complexes of chitosan and HSP-70 siRNA were effectively taken up by the tumor cells. We hypothesized that the high positive charge of the complexes is responsible for the safe transportation of siRNA into the cell for intracellular release and perinuclear distribution, which is consistent with our cellular uptake results, which show that siRNAs were widely distributed in the perinuclear area when delivered by AGC/siRNA.

The cellular uptake behavior and distribution of HSP-70 siRNA after incubation of AGC/HSP-70 siRNA with MMP-2 were also verified by lysosome staining. As seen in Figure 2B, although some of the HSP-70 siRNAs were colocalized with lysosomes, a significant fluorescence signal still did not colocalize with lysosomes and even distribute in the nucleus regions in cells. However, drug delivery systems have achieved widespread success in overcoming the limits faced when administering gene drugs in the clinic, such as the safety, immunogenicity, and delivery efficiency challenges, the successful application of gene drugs remained greatly hampered by poor endosomal escape and efficient distribution in the perinuclear region of the cytoplasm.62,63 Furthermore, the gene–drug delivery strategy designed in this research could solve the problems mentioned above, indicating it to be a promising drug delivery system for gene–drug delivery.

The toxicity of AGC vectors to 4T1 cells was determined by the MTT assay. As shown in Figure 2C, the cell viability was decreased when treated with AGC vectors. However, the toxicity of AGCs did not increase as the vectors’ concentration increased, and 55.8 ± 0.60% of cells survived even when the concentration of Au reached 80 μg/mL. According to Figure S3, we speculate that the slight toxicity of AGC is attributed to the positive charge of the nanoparticles, which may cause the rupture of the negatively charged cell membrane, resulting in the death of 4T1 cells.64,65 When loaded with HSP-70 siRNA, the safety of the drug delivery systems was significantly improved. Notably, the survival rate of 4T1 cells remained at 90.0 ± 0.64% even when the concentration of Au was as high as 80 μg/mL, indicating that the AGC/HSP-70 siRNA vector showed no apparent toxicity to 4T1 cells and was a safe delivery system for siRNA delivery.

Tumor Penetrability and PTT Therapeutic Efficacy of AGC/HSP-70 siRNA

Penetration of tumor tissues is the first barrier for therapeutic agents, such as gene drugs, to exert disease treatment or regulation effects.66 Notably, it has been reported that the barrier of tumor tissues could affect the transport of drugs through size interception and electrostatic action, and drug delivery vectors with size and charge transformation could solve the problem of tumor tissue penetration.67−69 MMP-2 enzymes in tumor tissues could degrade AGC/HSP-70 siRNA to release HSP-70 siRNA and chitosan complexes. With a smaller size and positive charge, the complexes penetrate tumor tissues much more easily and regulate deep tumor cells. The tumor penetration ability of naked HSP-70 siRNA, ACG/HSP-70 siRNA with or without treated by MMP-2 was evaluated separately (Figure 2D). For naked HSP-70, the HSP-70 siRNA was only distributed in the peripheral region of the tumor tissues when incubated with the tumor sphere for 24 h due to the negative charge of the siRNA molecules. When loaded into AGC, the penetration of siRNA was even worse since the delivery systems were large. However, when AGC/HSP-70 siRNA was pretreated with MMP-2, the red fluorescence of Cy5 HSP-70 siRNA was widely distributed in the tumor sphere, indicating that the dissociated HSP-70 siRNA and chitosan complexes exhibited a great penetration effect for tumor tissues. These results indicated that the drug delivery strategy could effectively promote the tumor tissue penetration of siRNA and the photothermal sensitivity of the tumor tissues.

We then evaluated the gene silencing effect of AGC/HSP-70 siRNA at mRNA and protein levels. RT-PCR assay confirmed that AGC/HSP-70 siRNA had an impressive gene silencing effect for 4T1 cells after enzymatic hydrolysis with MMP-2. As shown in Figure 2E, the expression rate of HSP-70 mRNA was decreased to 56.0 ± 1.86% when the 4T1 cells were incubated with AGC/HSP-70 siRNA after MMP-2 enzymatic hydrolysis, which was significantly lower than that of the blank group and the naked HSP-70 siRNA-treated group. Moreover, the inhibiting effect of HSP-70 mRNA for AGC/HSP-70 siRNA with MMP-2 pretreated was even more effective when compared with the Lipo/HSP-70 siRNA-treated group (the expression rate of HSP-70 mRNA was 64.7 ± 5.03%). However, without being prehydrolyzed by MMP-2, the AGC/HSP-70 siRNA silencing effect was markedly depressed. Besides, the down-regulation effect of HSP-70 protein in each experimental group showed the same trend as that in the mRNA level. The HSP-70 protein expression rate for the AGC/HSP-70 siRNA and MMP-2 treated group was 57.3 ± 0.10%, which was significantly lower than those for the AGC/HSP-70 siRNA (75.0 ± 1.42%) and Lipo/HSP-70 siRNA (65.8 ± 2.76%) treated group. These results suggested that the gene silencing effect of the drug delivery strategy is impressive, which may be attributed to the efficient lysosomal escape and perinuclear delivery of siRNAs as demonstrated in previous cellular uptake and distribution experiments previously.

Given the promising results above, we examined the photothermal sensitive enhancing effect of the drug delivery strategy on 4T1 cells by an MTT assay. With near-infrared light irradiation (808 nm, 1.911 W/cm2, 3 min), AGC loaded with negative control siRNA (AGC/NC) showed an evident and concentration-dependent antiproliferation effect (Figure 2F). For AGC loaded with the HSP-70 siRNA-treated group (AGC/HSP-70 siRNA), the cell viability showed no obvious reduction compared with the AGC/NC treated group. The unsatisfactory photothermal sensitive regulation may be due to the large size and negative electricity of AGC/HSP-70 siRNA, which hindered the cellular uptake and expression of HSP-70 siRNA. Notably, when AGC/HSP-70 siRNA was hydrolyzed by MMP-2, the HSP-70 siRNA and chitosan complexes with a smaller size and positive electricity would effectively promote the perinuclear distribution and gene silencing effect of HSP-70 siRNA, to reduce the sensitivity of tumor cells to heat and enhance the effect of photothermal therapy of gold nanorods.

Apoptotic-Promoting Effect

A series of apoptotic experiments were carried out to explore the mechanism for toxicity of AGC/HSP-70 siRNA. The apoptotic morphology of the cell nucleus was observed by CLSM after treatment with different drug administration strategies (Figure 3A). In the blank control group, the nucleus morphology was round and the chromatin structure was compact. The nuclear morphology for the AGC/NC or AGC/HSP-70 siRNA incubated group was slightly shrinking and irregular with laser treatment. Moreover, the nucleus in the MMP-2 pretreated AGC/HSP-70 siRNA group was lysed and crumpled, showing a serious apoptotic morphology. The apoptotic-promoting effect was also further evaluated by cell cycle arrest and apoptosis assays performed by FCM. As shown in Figures 3B,C, with laser treatment, the apoptosis rates for AGC/NC and AGC/HSP-70 siRNA were 8.8 ± 0.43 and 12.1 ± 0.31% separately, which were significantly increased than the control group (2.3 ± 0.20%). More impressively, for the AGC/HSP-70 siRNA and MMP-2 treated siRNA, both the death and the apoptosis rates were further increased to 0.8 ± 0.30 and 21.6 ± 2.31%. These results suggested that the AGC/HSP-70 siRNA-treated with the MMP-2 group promoted the apoptosis of 4T1 tumor cells through the synergistic effect of photothermal therapy and gene therapy.

Photothermal Effect and Distribution of AGC/HSP-70 siRNA In Vivo

In this study, a 4T1 tumor ectopic model was established in BALB/c mice, and an infrared thermal imager evaluated the photothermal effect of AGC/HSP-70 siRNA. The mice were treated with PBS or AGC solution by peri-tumoral injection, and after 24 h, the tumor areas were irradiated with an NIR laser (Figure 4A). As shown in Figures 4B,C, it was found that the temperature for the AGC/HSP-70 siRNA-treated group reached 49.1 °C within 3 min, which was proper for the photothermal treatment of the tumor and safe for the healthy tissue around the tumor. For the PBS-treated one, the temperature was only slightly increased to 39.6 °C, and it could not kill the tumor. This experiment confirmed the excellent photothermal effect of AGC/HSP-70 siRNA, which ensured tumor inhibition in the drug delivery system in vivo.

Moreover, we further studied the intratumoral distribution of PEG-decorated Au NRs (AP), Au NRs decorated with chitosan with a PEG linker (APC), and Au NRs decorated with chitosan with an MMP-2-sensitive peptide linker (AGC) by transmission electron microscopy (TEM). As shown in Figure 4D, a vesicle loaded with nanorods was distributed in the tumor tissues for the treated group. In contrast, no such vesicles were discovered in the AP or APC-treated groups. The results indicated that the connection of chitosan with Au NRs by the MMP-2 sensitive peptide linker would make more Au NRs remain in tumor tissues, likely due to binding effects of MMP-2 and the enzyme-sensitive polypeptides. Moreover, we noticed that AGC treatment could also make the nuclei of tumor cells shrinkage and the chromosomes aggregate, which are the characteristics of cellular necrocytosis.70 Furthermore, the cellular structure became loose with many intercellular spaces, as suggested by the TEM images, demonstrating the efficient cytotoxic effect of AGC on tumor cells.

Antitumor Efficacy and Safety Evaluation of AGC/HSP-70 siRNA In Vivo

Based on the experimental results presented above, we explored the tumor-inhibiting effect of the drug delivery strategy in vivo. As can be seen from Figure 5A,B, when treated with APC/NC or APC/HSP-70 siRNA and irradiated with 808 nm laser, the tumor growth was significantly inhibited with a tumor inhibition rate of 50.4 ± 2.92 and 53.4 ± 1.70%, respectively, which was contributed to the photothermal effect of the gold nanorods. However, the inhibition rates were lower than the AGC/HSP-70 siRNA-treated group (61.3 ± 2.79%). Considering the MMP-2 sensitive siRNA release behavior, we hypothesized that the released HSP-70 siRNA and chitosan complexes could penetrate the tumor to induce the tumor tissues to be more sensitive to photothermal therapy. The tumor histological experiments also proved the conclusion above (Figure 5C). The tumor cells in the control group were closely arranged, and H&E staining was deep and uniform. However, the tumor cell staining for the AGC/HSP-70 siRNA-treated group became shallow and uneven, indicating a sharp decrease in living cells and severe tumor necrosis. These results suggest that AGC/HSP-70 siRNA can effectively promote the photothermal therapy of gold nanorods and inhibit tumor growth. Moreover, there were no apparent pathological features in the main organs (heart, liver, spleen, lung, and kidney) in the four experimental groups studied by H&E staining (Figure 5D). The ratio of the main organ weight to the body weight also showed no obvious change for each treated group (Figure S2), which indicated that the nanodrug delivery system and the irradiating strategy would not produce apparent toxicity to the main organs in vivo.

Conclusions

In summary, an MMP-2 responsive nanoplatform AGC/HSP-70 siRNA was developed to enhance the photothermal treatment effect of Au NRs for tumors. Linkage of Au NRs and chitosan by MMP-2-sensitive polypeptide (GPLGLAG) could not only endow the vector with the ability to load and protect HSP-70 siRNA but also could make more Au NRs be trapped in tumor tissues to exert a photothermal effect since the specific binding of GPLGLAG and MMP-2, which was abundantly expressed in tumors. Moreover, when enzymolyzed by MMP-2, AGC/HSP-70 siRNA could release chitosan and siRNA complexes. The complexes’ positive charge and small size would penetrate deep tumor tissues and promote the uptake and perinuclear accumulation behavior of HSP-70 siRNA for tumor cells. As demonstrated by PCR and ELISA tests, AGC/siRNA pretreated with the MMP-2 group exhibited an outstanding gene silencing effect, and the interfering effect of HSP-70 siRNA would induce the whole tumor sensitive to the photothermal therapy of Au NRs to induce the apoptosis and necrosis of tumors more effectively. Besides, the biosafety evaluations in vitro and in vivo proved that the AGC vector was a safe and effective gene delivery vector. When administrated by peri-tumoral injection, the photothermal effect of Au NRs was not influenced. Therefore, a AuNR-based photothermal strategy with a photothermal sensitizing effect is applied by combining photothermal and gene therapies and a tumor-penetrating drug delivery strategy, which may be a promising drug delivery system for tumor treatment.

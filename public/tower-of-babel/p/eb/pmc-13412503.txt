Optimizing drug availability and reducing wastage through ABC-XYZ inventory management: Insights from a public tertiary cancer hospital in Northern India
Authors: Sheetal Singh, Himanshu Sharma, Vijay Aggarwal
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
OBJECTIVES:This study assesses the ABC-XYZ analysis technique in reducing stockouts and maximizing the management of high-cost medications to improve accessibility and minimize expiry.METHODS:It is a retrospective analysis of records from a tertiary cancer hospital in northern India. The ABC-XYZ analysis technique was applied to 286 medications, which is approximately 90% of the yearly drug expenditure. A 6-month pilot intervention from March to August 2024 was carried out. Procurement strategies such as scheduled orders for stable, high-cost medications, and consumption-based forecasting for volatile medications were implemented based on the ABC-XYZ classification. Outcomes, including stock-out frequency and wastage costs, were compared before the intervention (September 2023 to February 2024) and after the intervention (September 2024 to February 2025).RESULTS:An ABC analysis of 286 medications showed that 10.1% (29) were in Category A, consuming 70% of the total annual cost, thus emphasizing the need for strict and controlled monitoring of these. XYZ analysis categorized 286 medications grouping 22.7% (65) as X (stable), 39.2% (112) as Y (moderately stable), and 37.8% (108) as Z (unpredictable). A 6-month pilot intervention from March to August 2024 was conducted. As a result, the average monthly stock-out frequency decreased by 88.5%, which was statistically significant (P < 0.001). In addition, monthly wastage costs reduced by 76.5%, showing a strong clinical trend though not statistically significant.CONCLUSIONS:The ABC-XYZ integration is an effective, evidence-based tool for optimizing drug inventory in a resource-constrained cancer hospital, drastically improving availability and reducing wastage.

Introduction

Inventory management in a hospital treating patients faces issues due to high-cost medications and uneven demand. It is difficult for hospital administrators to balance medication availability with budget constraints. If the inventory is not managed correctly, it will cause considerable losses for the hospital and deteriorate patient care services. Inadequate management can potentially lead to overstocking. On the other hand, understocking results in shortage and impacts the patient care services in the hospital.[12] Quick et al. in their studies showed that approximately 70%–80% of the total annual consumption value (ACV) is attributable to a small subset of high-cost items, Category A, that account for 10%–20% of medications in the hospital drug stores, thus emphasizing the Pareto principle.[3]

The inventory of medication was evaluated using the ABC method, which ranks items by their ACV. Items contributing roughly the highest 70% of total value were placed in Category A, those accounting for the next 20% in B, and the remaining 10% in C. This inventory management technique effectively prioritizes high-cost medications, but overlooks essential, low-cost medicines due to its emphasis on monetary value.[45] The XYZ analysis categorizes the stocks into groups considering regularity of the demand. In XYZ analysis, the stock is separated into three categories: X category has a set size of demand with small intermittent variations providing higher accuracy for forecasting the procurement, Y category has moderate variation providing average accuracy, and Z category has the highest variation in demand thus providing lower forecasting. In XYZ analysis, the first step is determination of the scattering coefficient for individual products; the second step is to sort these products by increasing the scattering coefficient; and the last step is to classify them in X, Y, and Z categories.[6]

Methods

This retrospective research was undertaken in a public tertiary cancer institute in northern India from March 2024 to May 2025. The investigation was prompted by increasing complaints from clinical areas about shortages and a concurrent rise in expiry of high-cost medication, both significantly, impacted patient care services. Of 400 medications, 286 were analyzed from March 2023 to February 2024 to study the consumption pattern and costs, as the annual expenditure of these medications was more than 90%. This study analyzed the stock-out incidences and wastage due to expiry during September 2023 to February 2024 and simultaneously assessed existing procurement methods.

From March to August 2024, a procurement intervention based on ABC-XYZ analysis was implemented, and the outcomes were compared with those from September 2023 to February 2024. Since the study used only anonymized inventory records and did not involve human or animal subjects, ethical clearance was not required. All data analyses were performed using (SPSS version 28.0) [IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY].

Results

The inventory of 286 medications from March 2023 to February 2024 was classified using two methods. First, ABC analysis based on the ACV (₹12.71 crore) categorized medications into category A 10.1% (29) accounting for 70% (₹8.95 crore) of expenditure; Category B 15.7% (45) for 20% (₹2.48 crore); and Category C 73.9% (212) for the remaining 10% (₹1.27 crore). Second, XYZ analysis based on the coefficient of variation categorized 286 medications by demand variability into category × 22.7% (65) as stable; Category Y 39.2% (112) as moderately stable, and Category Z 37.8% (108) as unpredictable. The ABC-XYZ matrix provided a detailed 9-category classification, revealing 28 high-cost, stable-demand medications (AX and BX), 15 high-cost but volatile-demand medications (AZ and BZ) requiring stringent monitoring, and 30 high-cost medications with moderate demand (AY and BY). Financially, AX and BX medications represented ₹3.69 crore of spending, AY and BY accounted for ₹5.48 crore, and AZ and BZ contributed ₹2.32 crore, as detailed in Table 1 and Figure 1. ₹3.69 crore of ₹12.70 crore is attributed to AX and BX medications, which are high-cost with stable demand. ₹5.48 crore of ₹12.7 crore is attributed to AY and BY medications, which are high-cost with moderate demand. ₹2.32 crore of ₹12.70 crore is attributed to AZ and BZ medications, which are high-cost with volatile demand. The costs attributed to medications in various categories are detailed in Figure 2. To assess the implementation of the ABC-XYZ system, a pilot intervention was conducted from March to August 2024. Procurement techniques were aligned with the ABC-XYZ categories: stable, high-value medications (AX/BX) were procured through scheduled orders and buffer stocks, whereas high-value medications with unstable demand (AZ/BZ) were procured based on consumption forecasts. The data were collected and analyzed for stockouts and monthly medication waste expenses. The data collected before the intervention from September 2023 to February 2024 and after the intervention from September 2024 to February 2025 were analyzed. The analyzed data showed that the average stock-out frequency per month decreased from 4.33 ± 1.51 (range: 3–7) to 0.50 ± 0.55 (range: 0–1), a statistically significant reduction. An independent t-test done further established this result (t = 5.93, df = 10, P < 0.001), representing 88.5% improvement in the availability of medications. Monthly medication wastage costs also dropped from ₹28,333 ± 26,126 preintervention to ₹6667 ± 7023 postintervention, a 76.5% reduction, which was not statistically significant. This was further evidenced using the nonparametric test, which also showed that the variation was not statistically significant. The detailed pre- and postintervention findings are in Table 2.

The result corroborates another study, which also indicates that inventory management techniques achieve a 60%–70% reduction in stockouts.[7] In another study, there was a 30% perfection in procurement effectiveness using ABC-XYZ techniques across numerous hospitals.[8] Thus, the application of ABC-XYZ techniques for managing hospital inventory would assist in procuring medications based on cost and demand variability.

Discussion

The study supports the advantages of improved monitoring and customized procurement methodology for high-to-moderate-cost medications in the category (AZ, BZ, AY, and BY). These medications are 16% of the total stock and have unpredictable demand; hence, a flexible procurement system is required. Moreover, 130 medications that are not used regularly and have steady or variable demand (CX and CZ) shall be on demand basis. These procurement methods have effectively reduced both emergency purchases and waste during the intervention period. This study has evaluated the ABC-XYZ model of inventory management, thus providing the basis of integrating other inventory methods, such as Vital, Essential, and Desirable, to further enhance inventory management in hospital stores. This study has few limitations. First, the demand variability for medications was assessed on a monthly basis instead of daily basis. Second, this study was conducted in a single institute. Future research can include daily demand variations at patient level, thus providing a better understanding of demand variability and utilization patterns within patient care areas. To extend these findings to other hospitals, future studies should be done in multiple hospitals. In addition, the implementation of advanced technology could considerably improve demand forecasting and accuracy.[9]

Conclusions

It is concluded that the implementation of ABC-XYZ inventory analysis is an effective and efficient method for managing hospital stores. The pilot study of implementing the ABC-XYZ strategy demonstrated practical utility, with a substantial statistically significant decline in stockouts and not a statistically significant reduction in medication wastage. The study validated data-driven approach to improve medication availability without compromising patient care services.

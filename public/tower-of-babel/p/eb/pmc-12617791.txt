Gaseous chlorine dioxide improves Keitt mango quality during postharvest ripening: enhancing sugar accumulation, terpenoid biosynthesis, and storage performance
Authors: Meiling Tian, Lili Liu, Chunjiang Li, Dongwei Zhang, Yingting Zhao, Changcheng Li, Ting Fang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractKeitt mango (Mangifera indica L.) is typically harvested at full size and mature green, then allowed to ripen naturally at room temperature for several days. However, this process yields fruit that lacks the ideal sweetness and characteristic mango flavor compared to tree-ripened mangoes. To enhance the postharvest ripening quality of Keitt mangoes, the mangoes were subjected to gaseous chlorine dioxide (ClO2, cumulative concentration 200 μL/L), then stored at 25 °C with 85 ± 5% humidity for 4 days. Results suggested that gaseous ClO2 treatment significantly elevated the levels of melibiose, glucose, mannose, and arabinose in mango pulp during days 1–4 compared to the control and ethephon-treated groups, with the most pronounced increase observed in glucose content. For aroma, gaseous ClO2 treatment remarkably elevated the content of terpenoids, especially 3-carene, on days 2–4. These trends were consistent with the upregulated expression of genes encoding sugar production (MiSUS, MiBAM3, MiabfA, and MiHEX1), terpenes biosynthesis (MiACAT, MiIDI, and MiGPPS), and vacuolar sugar transporters (MiERD6–5 and MiERD6–6). Additionally, gaseous ClO2-ripened mangoes achieved outstanding quality attributes compared to ethephon-ripened mangoes after 4 days of storage at 13 °C. In summary, gaseous ClO2 can effectively promote sugar accumulation and flavor development in Keitt mangoes, as well as yield superior storage quality. These findings indicate a remarkable potential for gaseous ClO2 in enhancing the quality of postharvest fruits.Keywords: Keitt mango, Soluble sugar, Volatile components, Storage performance

Introduction

Sensory experience plays a primary role in consumer satisfaction and acceptance. The most influential quality attributes that influence consumers to purchase mangoes are their sweetness and flavor (Opara, Al-Said, & Al-Abri, 2006). In terms of sweetness, ripened mango fruit contains about 15% total sugars, comprising predominantly glucose, fructose, and sucrose, which contribute to mango sweetness (Maldonado-Celis et al., 2019). The glucose in ripened mango fruit is mainly hydrolyzed from starch, then phosphorylated and enters the hexose-P pool, which facilitates the “futile cycle” of sucrose synthesis and degradation, thereby regulating the content of glucose, fructose, and sucrose (Maldonado-Celis et al., 2019). Moreover, the degradation of cell wall polysaccharides, such as pectin, mannan, arabinoxylans, and cellulose, may result in fruit softening and textural changes, as well as the accumulation of soluble sugars (Ren, Sun, Wang, & Zhu, 2020). The soluble sugars are massively accumulated in the vacuoles during fruit ripening via sugar transporter proteins. For example, the vacuole sugar transporter AtVGT1 is a specific glucose sugar transporter, and AtTMT1/2 exhibits broad substrate specificity for the transport of glucose, fructose, and sucrose (Aluri & Buttner, 2007; Schulz et al., 2011). The bidirectional sugar transporters AtSWEET16 and AtSWEET17 are located at the vacuole membrane, with AtSWEET16 transporting sucrose, glucose, and fructose, and AtSWEET17 transporting fructose (Guo et al., 2014; Klemens et al., 2013). The plasma membrane-localized sugar transporter VvERD6l13 mediates specific retrieval of apoplasmic sucrose (Breia et al., 2020).

In terms of flavor, hundreds of volatile components that determine the fruity character of mango fruit, including terpenes (monoterpenes and sesquiterpenes), aldehydes, esters, ketones, volatile fatty acids, and alcohols, are found in various mango varieties (Peng et al., 2023). The distribution of these compounds varies quantitatively and qualitatively among cultivars, fruit tissues, maturity stage, and postharvest handling procedures(Cordenunsi-Lysenko et al., 2019). Moreover, because of the odor threshold, only limited amounts of these volatiles can be perceived (Zhang et al., 2019). Terpenoids, such as 3-carene, caryophyllene, and α-pinene, represent the richest volatile constituents in 25 mango cultivars from China, India, Cuba, America, Indonesia, Thailand, and the Philippines (Li et al., 2017). The 3-carene is the most important aroma constituent for the varieties Irwin, JinHwang, and Keitt grown in southern China, due to its high percentage (up to 70%) of the volatile components (Liu et al., 2013). Terpene precursors are commonly produced in plants by two spatially separated but interconnected pathways, the mevalonate (MVA) and pentaerythritol phosphate (MEP) pathways, with hydroxymethylglutaryl-CoA synthase (HMGCS) the key gene for controlling metabolic flux in the former, and 1-deoxy-D-xylulose-5-phosphate synthase (DXS) and 1-deoxy-D-xylulose-5-phosphate reductoisomerase (DXR) for the latter (Singh, Chhatwal, & Pandey, 2024).

Keitt mango (Mangifera indica L.) is a late-season mango cultivar originating from South Florida. The tree-ripe Keitt mangoes have attractive sensory qualities, including sweet, juicy, fiberless flesh and tropical flavor (Cheng et al., 2024). In China, the unique climate and geographical location of Panzhihua city provide excellent growing conditions for Keitt mangoes, and the planting area accounts for about 90% of the late maturing cultivars (Cheng et al., 2024). And the Keitt mango has passed the Good Agricultural Practices (GAP) certification and gained access to the European market (Zhang et al., 2019). The mangoes were also exported to countries along the Belt and Road, with exports reaching 339.3 tons. Keitt mangoes are commonly harvested at full-sized, mature green, and ripen naturally after a few days at room temperature or ripen using ethylene, which leaves the fruit lacking in desirable sensory qualities and pomological characteristics (Zhang et al., 2024). Moreover, different postharvest handling procedures had variable effects on mango fruit qualities such as sugar accumulation and aroma (Bender, Brecht, & Baldwin, 2022; Zhang et al., 2024). For instance, the 25 kPa CO2 atmosphere-treated Keitt mangoes exhibited significantly increased levels of 3-pinene, α-pinene, myrcene, and limonene compared to 10 kPa CO2 treatment (Bender et al., 2022). After 8 days of exogenous abscisic acid treatment, the Keitt mangoes reached eating softness, while the total sugar content of the abscisic acid-treated mangoes was lower than that of the control group (Zaharah & Singh, 2010). Therefore, postharvest quality improvement in Keitt mango is an important challenge for enhancing the market value of mangoes.

Chlorine dioxide (ClO2) is a broad-spectrum antimicrobial agent that effectively destroys bacteria, viruses, fungi, and other cellular pathogens(Sun, Baldwin, & Bai, 2019). The microbial load on fresh vegetables and fruits leads to postharvest spoilage and loss, and may lead to foodborne illness when human pathogens occur (Praeger, Herppich, & Hassenberg, 2018). ClO2 is therefore used to reduce losses and improve the safety of fresh vegetable and fruit products. Moreover, the gaseous-based ClO2 is more effective in reaching deactivating pathogenic cells attached to hard-to-reach parts of plants over its aqueous form due to its high penetrability and diffusivity (Sun et al., 2019). For instance, the gaseous ClO2 treatment showed a significantly higher log reduction of L. monocytogenes than that of aqueous ClO2 on both injured and non-injured pepper surfaces (Han, Linton, Nielsen, & Nelson, 2001). Furthermore, gaseous ClO2 can effectively penetrate through the polysaccharide layer of a bacterial biofilm on the surface of vegetables and fruits and react directly with the bacteria (Sun et al., 2019). Our previous studies have found that gaseous ClO2 allowed Keitt mangoes to ripen at room temperature, and improved the sensory attributes, especially sweetness and mango aroma, compared to ethephon-ripened ones (Zhang et al., 2024). However, the precise influence of gaseous ClO2 on sugar accumulation and volatile synthesis in mangoes is not fully understood.

Therefore, the metabolomics and transcriptomics were performed to screen the sugar and its associated enzyme gene expression that changed in Keitt mangoes after gaseous ClO2 treatment, and the sugar profile was further characterized using High-Performance Liquid Chromatography (HPLC). Moreover, volatile components of mangoes were identified using gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (GC–MS), and their synthesis-related genes were screened from the transcriptome. Finally, the storage performance of mangoes ripened using gaseous ClO2 and ethephon was evaluated. Our research holds significant importance for improving the postharvest ripening quality of Keitt mangoes and provides fundamental evidence for the further application of gaseous ClO2 in the postharvest ripening process of other fruits.

Materials and methods

Mango fruit and treatments

Keitt mangoes at mature green (8 ripeness degrees) were provided by a local orchard in Panzhihua, Sichuan, China (101°08′–102°15′ E, 26°06′–27°21′ N). The orchard experiences an average annual temperature of 20 °C to 40 °C, and the average age of the fruit trees is 10 years. The mangoes were grouped and treated as previously described, and the schematic of the gaseous ClO2 treatment system is shown in Fig. 1 (Zhang et al., 2024). Briefly, mangoes in the gaseous ClO2 (ClO2(g)) group, ethephon group, and the control group were exposed to the ClO2 gas (cumulative concentration 200 μL/L), ethephon spray (200 ppm, evenly sprayed onto the surface of the fruit), and the atmosphere for 3 h in the chamber, respectively. The ethephon group was used as the positive control. Then, the three sets of mangoes with 20 biological replicates were placed in different artificial climate chambers (RXZ-280B, Jiangnan Ltd., Ningbo, China) and left at 25 ± 1 °C and 85 ± 5% relative humidity for 4 days. On days 0, 0.5, 1, 2, 3, and 4, ten mangoes were sampled at each time. Moreover, mango fruits ripened for 2 days in the ClO2(g) and ethephon groups were stored at 13 °C and 80 ± 5% RH for 7 days, and sampled on days 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, and 7. The whole experiment was repeated three times.

Sugar profile analysis

The sugar component of mango pulp samples was extracted with water containing 1 mol/L zinc acetate and 0.25 mol/L potassium ferrocyanide. The extraction mixture was subjected to sonication (30 min), and the supernatant was separated (10,000 ×g, 10 min) for further analysis. A portion of the supernatant was analyzed by HPLC (Waters 2695, Waters Corp., MA, America) equipped with a refractive index detector (RID) (Waters 2414) and an Agilent Zorbax NH2 column (4.6 × 250 mm, 5 μm) to determine the content of sucrose, fructose, and melibiose. The mobile phase of a mixture of acetonitrile and water (70:30 v/v) was used.

The other part of the supernatant was derivatized with 1-phenyl-3-methyl-5-pyrazolinone (PMP). Then, the neutral sugars, including mannose, glucose, and arabinose, were determined by HPLC (Waters 2695, Waters Corp., MA, America) with a photodiode array detector (PDA) (Waters 2489) and a Venusil XBP-C18 column (4.6 × 250 mm, 5 μm). A mixture of phosphate buffer (pH = 6.7) and acetonitrile (83:17 v/v) was used as the mobile phase. Sucrose, fructose, melibiose, mannose, glucose, and arabinose contents were calculated based on peak areas from standard samples (Sigma–Aldrich, St Louis, Missouri, USA).

Widely targeted metabolomic and RNA sequencing (RNA-Seq) analysis

A widely targeted metabolomic analysis of mango pulp (with 4 biological replicates) was conducted by Biotree Biotech Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). In brief, 50 mg of milled mango pulp was mixed with 700 μL of an extract solution containing methanol (75%) and water (25%), along with 1 mg/L 2-chloro-L-phenylalanine (internal standard). The mixture was then well mixed using a homogenizer (HR-25D, Shanghai Huxi Industry Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) and sonicator (KQ5200E, Kunshan Ultrasonic Instrument Co., Ltd.), and centrifuged (13,800×g, 15 min, 4 °C) to obtain the supernatant. Samples were analyzed by ultra-high performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC) combined with a Q-Trap 6500+ mass spectrometer (UHPLC-QTRAP-MS/MS) (Sciex, Applied Biosystems, CA, USA). Metabolite identification and quantification were performed according to the procedures described previously (Zhang et al., 2024). SCIEX Analyst Workstation Software (version 1.6.3, Framingham, MA, USA) was used for MRM data acquisition and processing. Metabolic patterns were analyzed using orthogonal partial least squares-discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA). Values with P-value <0.05 and VIP values ≥1.0 were identified as significantly different metabolites.

For the RNA-Seq analysis of mango pulp (with 4 biological replicates), the total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, California, CA, USA). RNA-seq sequencing was performed by Majorbio Bio-Pharm Technology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China), as previously described (Zhang et al., 2024). Differential expression genes (DEGs) between two groups were identified using DESeq2, and a significantly differential expression threshold was set as FDR < 0.05 and Fold Change ≥2. All raw RNA-seq data have been submitted to the NCBI Short Read Archive Database with Project numbers of PRJNA1330256 (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/bioproject/?term=PRJNA1330256. Accessed: September 18, 2025) and PRJNA985857 (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/bioproject/?term=PRJNA985857. Accessed: September 18, 2025).

Volatile compounds analysis

The volatile compounds of mango pulps were extracted using a Headspace-solid Phase Microextraction (HS-SPME) device equipped with a fused silica fiber coated with divinylbenzene/carboxen/polydimethylsiloxane (DVB/CAR/PDMS). Before each extraction, place the fibers at the injection port of the gas chromatograph and precondition them at 250 °C for 10 min to remove any volatile substances left on the fiber surface. Milled mango pulp (2.5 g) was transferred to a headspace bottle that contained 10 mL of water, 3.6 g of NaCl, and 20 μL of 2-octanol (internal standard (IS)). The headspace bottle was incubated at 45 °C for 10 min, followed by placing the SPME fiber in the headspace to absorb aroma for 30 min. The SPME fiber was introduced into the gas chromatograph injector in splitless mode and kept for 10 min, allowing the analytes to be thermally desorbed at 250 °C.

The analytes were analyzed on a Shimadzu QP2010 Plus GC–MS (Shimadzu Co., Ltd., Kyoto, Japan) equipped with a Shimadzu SH-Rxi-5Sil MS capillary column (30 m × 0.25 mm × 0.25 μm) (Shimadzu Co., Ltd., Kyoto, Japan) under the electron ionization mode at 70 eV and a scanning mode at 0.93 kV. A mass range of 40–400 Da is tested with helium (purity = 99.999%) as the carrier gas at 1 mL/min. The GC oven temperature program started at 40 °C, held for 3 min, ramped up to 140 °C at 5 °C/min, held for 1 min, ramped up to 200 °C at 15 °C/min, held for 2 min, and finally ramped up to 300 °C at 20 °C/min, held for 1 min. A homologous series of alkanes (C5 − C30) (Sigma–Aldrich, St Louis, Missouri, USA) was injected to determine the retention index (RI).

The initial data handling was done on the LabSolutions GC–MS software (version 2.5, Shimadzu Scientific Instruments, Inc., Kyoto, Japan) linked to the analytical platform. By comparing with the NIST17 database and RI, volatile compounds were identified. For each aroma component, the relative content was calculated via area normalization. Quantitative real-time PCR (RT-qPCR) analysis

Total RNA was extracted from mango pulp using the TRIzol method and quantified on a NanoDrop 2000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). cDNA was synthesized with 1 μg of total RNA using PrimeScript 1st strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Takara Bio Inc., Japan). The primer sequences were designed using the NCBI/Primer-BLAST platform (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/tools/primer-blast/. Accessed: July 11, 2025) and are provided in Table S1. RT-qPCR was performed with a C1000TM Thermal Cycler PCR detection system (Bio-Rad, CFX96TM Optics Module, USA). The expression levels of target genes were calculated using the 2-ΔΔCT method with β-actin as the internal reaction control. Each group was quantified using three biological replicates.

Sensory analysis

The sensory profiles of mango were evaluated by panelists according to the previously described method (Zhang et al., 2024). Mango pulp samples were cut into the same size and placed on disposable plates, each labeled with a randomly generated two-digit code for identification. Thirty trained sensory panelists (15 female and 15 male, ages 23 to 60) were selected voluntarily, and participants allergic to mangoes were not permitted to join. Panelists were requested to characterize the sensory attributes, including pulp color, chewiness, acidity, sweetness, aroma, and overall rating, using a 9-point hedonic scale with 1 = “dislike extremely” and 9 = “like extremely”. Between each sample assessment, spring water was provided to rinse the mouth, and a 2-minute break was taken. The assessment was conducted in a classroom that was well-furnished, noiseless, and well-lit, where participants were not allowed to face each other. This sensory study was ethically approved by the Academic Committee of Fujian Agriculture and Forestry University (code PZCASFAFU 23043, approval date 2023.3.9).

Color measurement

Coloration of mango pulp was detected by measuring four random points on the inside of the pulp with a colorimeter (Model CR-20, KONICA MINOLTA Co., Tokyo, Japan) after cutting the mango longitudinally along the kernel. The color was quantified with four values of green chromaticity (a*), lightness (L*), angle (h*), and yellow chromaticity (b*).

Firmness

Firmness was determined by a puncture test with a 5 mm-diameter probe (XTC-18, Shanghai Baosheng Industrial Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China). The puncture speed was 60 mm·min−1 and the puncture depth was 5 mm. Six mangoes were selected from each group, and three points around the equator of the peeled mango were randomly tested.

Titratable acidity, total soluble solids, and reducing sugar content

Titratable acidity (TA) and total soluble solids (TSS) of mango pulp were assessed using acid-based titration and refraction methods, respectively, based on our previous studies (Zhang et al., 2024). Reducing sugar content was determined using the Reducing Sugar Content Assay Kit (BC0230, Solarbio, China) following the manual instructions and presented as mg/100 mg fresh mango pulp.

Superoxide dismutase (SOD) and catalase (CAT) activity

The enzymatic activity was measured using commercial kits (Beijing Solarbio Science & Technology Co., Ltd., Beijing, China) of SOD (BC0175) and CAT (BC0205) according to the manufacturer's instructions.

Statistical analysis

Data were expressed as mean ± SD. All measurements were taken in triplicate for each analysis, and 10 fruits were included in each analysis, unless otherwise noted. Intergroup differences were analyzed using one-way ANOVA followed by Duncan's multiple range test (equal variances assumed) or Tamhane's T2 test (equal variances not assumed) using SPSS software (27.0 version, SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) with P < 0.05 considered significant. Heatmap, volcano plot, and principal component analysis were completed using the Metware Cloud (https://cloud.metware.cn/#/home. Accessed: September 18, 2025). Other data were plotted by GraphPad Prism software (version 9.0, San Diego, CA, USA).

Results and discussion

Changes in metabolites of mango fruit ripened by gaseous ClO2 treatment

In our previous studies, Keitt mangoes treated with gaseous ClO2 for 2 days accelerated postharvest ripening at room temperature (25 °C) as ethephon treatment, and the flesh was more aromatic and sweeter compared to the control and ethephon groups (Zhang et al., 2024). To explore the modulatory effects of gaseous ClO2 treatment on the aroma formation and sugar accumulation in Keitt mango, we first employed a widely targeted metabolome to determine the metabolite profile of Keitt mango.

A total of 828 metabolites were detected, each of which was identified in at least one sample, including alkaloids, flavonoids, terpenoids, phenols and phenol ethers, phenylpropanoids, benzene and substituted derivatives, amino acids and derivatives, steroids and steroid derivatives, carboxylic acids and derivatives, and fatty acyls (Fig. 2A, Table S2). Principal component analysis (PCA) revealed that the metabolite profile of mangoes exhibited significant distinctions between gaseous ClO2 and control groups (Fig. 2B). Further analysis showed that 62 differential metabolites were characterized in ClO2(g) group compared to the control group, with 7 downregulated and 55 upregulated (Fig. 2C). These differential metabolites mainly featured by alkaloids, nucleotides and its derivates, organooxygen compounds, phenols, phenylpropanoids, and carbohydrates and organic acids and derivatives, which are associated with the formation of fruit flavor (Fig. 2D). Bubble chart of the metabolic pathway analysis revealed that the differential metabolites were significantly enriched in the metabolic pathways of purine metabolism, amino and nucleotide sugar metabolism, riboflavin metabolism, and galactose metabolism in mango pulp with gaseous ClO2 treatment (Fig. 2E). Purine metabolism was the major metabolic pathways upregulated in response to gaseous ClO2 treatment with the contents of adenosine 5′-monophosphate (AMP), 1-(5’-Phosphoribosyl)-5-amino-4-imidazolecarboxamide (AICAR), xanthosine, guanine, and guanosine were upregulated (Table S2). The accumulation of genetic material, including xanthine, hypoxanthine, and guanine, helps the fruit resist abiotic stress and promotes fruit growth and development (Zhang, Chen, Huang, Song, & Niu, 2021). The upregulated purine metabolism in this study indicates that gaseous ClO2 stress accelerates the postharvest ripening of the Keitt mango. In addition, abiotic stress can induce many metabolites such as carbohydrates and amino acids (Maruyama et al., 2014). In our results, gaseous ClO2 treatment can enrich pathways involving carbohydrates, such as galactose metabolism, amino sugar and nucleotide sugar metabolism, fructose and mannose metabolism, and starch and sucrose metabolism, indicating that gaseous ClO2 treatment increased the flavor, particularly the sweetness of Keitt mango.

Changes in transcriptomics of mango fruit ripened by gaseous ClO2 treatment

To further reveal the influence of gaseous ClO2 treatment on Keitt mango sugar accumulation and flavor formation, transcriptomic analysis was used. The Pearson correlation coefficients for each set of biological replicates in the transcriptome analysis were greater than 0.9, indicating excellent correlation between the samples (Fig. 3A). A total of 2883, 2248 and 2991 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were detected in the ClO2(g) vs. control, ethephon vs. control, and ClO2(g) vs. ethephon groups comparison, with 1118, 809, and 1617 genes up-regulated and 1765, 1439, and 1617 genes downregulated, respectively (Fig. 3B). There were 132 DEGs shared among the three comparison groups, and 972, 755, and 1143 DEGs were unique to the ClO2(g) vs. control, ethephon vs. control, and ClO2(g) vs. ethephon comparison groups, respectively (Fig. 3C). Moreover, PCA analysis showed a significant difference in DEGs between gaseous ClO2 and ethephon treated groups and some crossover with the control group (Fig. 3D), suggesting that the quality of mangoes ripened with gaseous ClO2 was similar to that of the control group (naturally ripened). And the quality of naturally ripened fruits was considered to be superior to that of mangoes treated with ethephon (Farina et al., 2020). This aligns with our previous findings that mangoes treated with gaseous ClO2 were more aromatic and sweeter compared to mangoes treated with ethephon.

Global gene expression profile of mangoes ripened with different treatments for 2 days. (A) Pearson correlation coefficients; (B) DEGs number; (C) Venn diagram comparing DEG sets; (D) PCA score plot; (E) KEGG enrichment analysis for DEGs between ClO2(g) and ethephon groups;

In the ClO2(g) vs. ethephon comparison, the DEGs were significantly enriched in the following pathways: photosynthesis, carbon fixation in photosynthetic organisms, photosynthesis-antenna proteins, glycolysis/gluconeogenesis, ascorbate and aldarate metabolism, and pentose phosphate pathway (Fig. 3E). Keitt mango has a green epidermis and contains a large amount of chlorophyll, which is the main pigment for photosynthesis in plants. Chlorophyll captures energy from sunlight and uses electrons from water to convert atmospheric carbon dioxide into organic compounds such as glucose and starch (Mao, Vadiveloo, Qiu, & Gao, 2024). Most of the DEGs in the photosynthesis, photosynthesis-antenna proteins, and carbon fixation in photosynthetic organisms pathways were up-regulated following gaseous ClO2 treatment (Table S3), suggesting that gaseous ClO2 treatment may have increased sugar accumulation in Keitt mango. The glycolysis and gluconeogenesis are strictly inter-regulated in response to the changes of energy status and glucose levels in cells (Xiong, Lei, Zhao, & Guan, 2011). The upregulation of the most DEGs in this pathway further suggests that gaseous ClO2 treatment has an essential regulatory effect on fruit sugar metabolism. The pentose phosphate pathway can provide precursors for nucleotide and amino acid biosynthesis as well as defend against oxidative stress in cells (Stincone et al., 2015). The ascorbate and aldarate metabolism pathway is also involved in protecting cells from oxidative damage (Peng et al., 2023), suggesting the stress effect of gaseous ClO2 on Keitt mango.

The transcript quantification of MiCAT and the activity of CAT were significantly decreased compared to the control and ethephon groups, while the transcript quantification of MiSOD and the activity of SOD significantly increased (Fig. 4). SOD is an enzyme that catalyzes the dismutation of superoxide anion radicals into hydrogen peroxide and oxygen (Wang, Branicky, Noe & Hekimi, 2018). SOD activity increases when plants are subjected to environmental stresses such as salinity and drought (Meitha, Pramesti, & Suhandono, 2020). CAT primarily functions to remove hydrogen peroxide generated during metabolism in aerobic organisms by decomposing it into water and oxygen (Valizadeh, Behnamian, Dezhsetan, & Karimirad, 2021). Enhanced SOD activity coupled with reduced CAT activity indicated that hydrogen peroxide accumulation may occur in mangoes treated with gaseous ClO2, potentially exacerbating oxidative stress reactions. Previous research has demonstrated that NaCl (160 mM) stress during the early stages of tomato fruit development doubled starch accumulation compared to control plants, and soluble sugars increased as fruit ripened (Yin et al., 2010). Chilling stress promoted starch hydrolysis and soluble sugar accumulation in Jatropha curcas seedlings (Wang et al., 2018). Drought stress increased glucose and fructose contents in juice sacs or segment membranes of C. reticulata cv. Nanfeng (Khan et al., 2023). Water deficiency has also been reported to improve the nutritional and flavor quality in tomato fruits (Jin et al., 2023). The results above indicate that the oxidative-stress effect of gaseous ClO2 may enhance the soluble sugar accumulation and flavor quality in Keitt mango fruits.

Gaseous ClO2 promotes Keitt mango sugar accumulation

Flavor quality, especially sweetness and aroma, is the most important characteristic affecting the competitiveness of mango in the market (Farina et al., 2020). In terms of sweetness, the accumulation of soluble sugars during fruit postharvest ripening directly influences the sweetness of climacteric fruit, particularly the fruits harvested at the mature green stage (Cordenunsi-Lysenko et al., 2019). The main sugars of Keitt mango are glucose, fructose, and sucrose, with all three increasing during ripening, sucrose being the most concentrated throughout ripening and fructose being the main reducing sugar (Maldonado-Celis et al., 2019). Non-targeted metabolome showed a significant increase in the abundance of melibiose, glucose 1-phosphate, and mannose 6-phosphate in mangoes treated with gaseous ClO2 and stored at 25 °C for 2 days as compared to the control group (Fig. 5A-C). The abundance of D-xylose and D-arabinose-5-phosphate also increased after gaseous chlorine dioxide treatment, though the differences were not significant (Table S2). Melibiose is a non-reducing trisaccharide mainly found in honey products, and its sweetness is approximately 20% that of sucrose (Sainio, 2011). Mannose, xylose, and arabinose are the main components of plant cell walls (Scheller & Ulvskov, 2010). The elevated content of mannose, xylose, and arabinose is due to cell wall degradation during fruit ripening (Ren et al., 2020). Moreover, gaseous ClO2 treatment significantly increased the content of soluble sugars (Zhang et al., 2024). Meanwhile, the content of reducing sugar, a key factor contributing to the sugar content fruits, was significantly increased in mango fruits of the ClO2(g) group from the 1st day, and increased the content by 19.52% and 13.73% on the second day compared to the control and ethephon groups, respectively (Fig. 5D). This is consistent with our previous findings that ClO2(g)-treated Keitt mango pulp was sweeter than that of ethephon and controls (Zhang et al., 2024).

To further verify the sugar content changes in mangoes treated with gaseous ClO2 and ethephon, the sugar composition of mango pulp was analyzed. The USDA Nutrient Database reports that mangoes of Haden, Tommy Atkins, Keitt, and Kent cultivars contained 6.97 g of sucrose, 2.01 g of glucose, and 4.68 g of fructose per 100 g of fruit (United States Department of Agriculture, 2018). Consistently, sucrose exhibited the highest concentration in the mangoes on day 0, followed by glucose and fructose. Moreover, consistent with the results of the non-targeted metabolome, mangoes treated with gaseous ClO2 showed a significant increase in melibiose from day 0.5 onwards, which was 5.68- and 7.07-fold higher than that of the control and ethephon groups, respectively, on the 4th day (Fig. 6A). It is noteworthy that the glucose content of mango pulp in the gaseous ClO2-treated group increased by 43.23 g/kg from day 0 to day 4, whereas the control and ethephon groups increased by 5.12 g/kg and 12.19 g/kg, respectively (Fig. 6B). The changes of mannose and arabinose contents in mango pulp from the three groups were consistent with the changes of glucose (Fig. 6C-D). Moreover, the sucrose content in the mango pulp treated with ethephon was higher than that of the ClO2(g) and the control groups from day 0.5 of the storage period, while fructose was continuously higher in the control group than in the ClO2(g) and ethephon groups (Fig. 6E-F). Each carbohydrate has a unique sweetness; if glucose has a sweetness value of 1, then fructose is 2.3 and sucrose is 1.35 (Xiao et al., 2024). Despite the remarkably lower contents of sucrose and fructose in gaseous ClO2-treated mangoes than those of the ethephon and control groups, respectively, the sweetness of mangoes in the ClO2(g) groups is still more intense. This may be because gaseous ClO2 increased the glucose in the mangoes by 21.47-fold from day 0 to day 4 in the ClO2(g) groups, whereas the control and ethephon groups increased 3.45 and 6.89-fold, respectively. Moreover, gaseous ClO2 also contributed to a pronounced increase in melibiose when compared to the control and ethephon groups. Thereby, causing the overall sweetness to exceed that of the control and ethephon groups. In sum, the enhancement of mango sweetness by gaseous ClO2 was probably due to an increase in the concentration of melibiose and glucose.

Soluble sugar accumulation in fruits during postharvest ripening is strongly related to the activity of polysaccharide-degrading enzymes. Starch hydrolysis is one of the well-known processes occurring during postharvest ripening of fruits, which raises the amount of soluble sugars, including glucose, fructose, and sucrose (Mesa et al., 2016). In unripe mango fruits, starch is the most dominant component in terms of quantity and is hydrolyzed to glucose with α-amylase and β-amylase during ripening (Maldonado-Celis et al., 2019). Moreover, fruit ripening is usually accompanied by degradation of cell wall polysaccharides such as pectin, glucomannan, arabinoxylan, and cellulose, which softens the fruit (Ren et al., 2020). Our widely targeted metabolomic analysis above indicates that ClO₂(g) significantly modulates starch and sucrose metabolism, as well as fructose and mannose metabolism. Moreover, 10 critical DEGs that involved in the sugars production above-described were screened from the transcriptomic data, including two sucrose synthase genes (MiSUS and MiSUS7), one fructokinase gene (MiFRK), two glutamine-fructose-6-phosphate aminotransferase genes (MiGFPT), one mannose-6-phosphate isomerase gene (MiMPI), one β-amylase (MiBAM3), one alpha-L-arabinofuranosidase gene (MiabfA), and two hexokinase genes (MIHEX) (Fig. 6I). Gaseous ClO2 treatment significantly increased the gene expression of MiSUS, MiBAM3, MiabfA, and MiHEX1 compared to the control and ethephon groups, and increased the expression of MiSUS7, MiGFPT, MiMPI, and MiHEX3 compared to the control group. These are consistent with the increase of glucose, arabinose, fructose, and mannose content in gaseous ClO2-treated mangoes compared to the control and ethephon groups. In particular, MiBAM3, a key amylolytic enzyme gene responsible for the degradation of starch and related polysaccharides, had the highest TPM (transcripts per million) value among the genes mentioned above (up to 9200), and was highly up-regulated in gaseous ClO2-treated mango compared with the ethephon and control groups. Therefore, the increase in glucose in gaseous ClO2-treated mangoes is mainly due to starch hydrolysis. The glucose hydrolyzed from starch can then be phosphorylated into the hexose phosphate pool, which fuels a “futile cycle” of sucrose degradation and synthesis, thereby controlling sucrose, glucose, and fructose levels (Maldonado-Celis et al., 2019). Glucose-1P content was increased in mangoes treated with gaseous ClO2, but fructose and sucrose content were intermediate between the control and ethephon groups due to the complex metabolic process of fruit sugar. For example, the increase of melibiose in gaseous ClO2-treated mangoes may be converted from precursors such as sucrose by enzymatic reactions (Smrtičová, Čanigová, Mastihubová, & Mastihuba, 2011). Thus, the sucrose content was lower than that of the ethephon group. The increase of mannose and arabinose in gaseous ClO2-treated mangoes may be due to the degradation of glucomannan and arabinoxylan, respectively. In particular, the increase of arabinose may be attributed to the fact that gaseous ClO2 treatment increased the activity of α-L-arabinofuranosidase, which is responsible for the cleavage of the arabinosyl substituent from arabinoxylan during cell wall polysaccharides degradation (Poria, Saini, Singh, Nain, & Kuhad, 2020). Thus, gaseous ClO2 promoted the accumulation of soluble sugars in Keitt mango via up-regulating the expression of genes encoding hydrolases, such as starch, sucrose, glucomannan, and arabinoxylan.

Most soluble sugars are primarily stored in vacuoles via sugar transporters, such as tonoplast sugar transporter (TST), vacuolar glucose transporter (vGT), and early response to dehydration 6-like (ERD6), which are located in the vacuole membrane (Zhu et al., 2021). In the present study, gaseous ClO2 treatment significantly up-regulated the gene expression of MiERD6–5 and MiERD6–6 when compared with the control and ethephon groups (Fig. 7A-B). To verify the reliability of the transcriptome data, the genes of MiERD6–5 and MiERD6–6 were selected for RT-PCR analysis. The result demonstrated that the expression patterns of MiERD6–5 and MiERD6–6 were consistent with RNA-seq analysis results (Fig. 7C-D). ERD6 is involved in plant responses to various stresses, including drought, high temperature, and trauma, as well as sugar homeostasis and accumulation (Zhu et al., 2021). It functions as a glucose transporter, particularly on the vacuolar membrane, and plays a role in mobilizing carbohydrate stores from vacuoles during stress (Zhu et al., 2021). Genes homologous to AtERDL6 exhibited high expression levels in a variety of sugar-accumulating fleshy fruits. For instance, 12 of 16 CsERDL6 genes in citrus were highly expressed in fruits, of which the expression of Cs5g32070 increased gradually with fruit development and was positively correlated with sugar accumulation in fruits (Zheng, Tang, Xu, & Deng, 2014). The expression pattern of MdERDL6–1 in apple fruits was highly positively correlated with the sugar accumulation during fruit development and ripening (Zhu et al., 2021). Overexpression of MdERDL6–1 increased the concentration of glucose, fructose, and sucrose in transgenic apple and tomato leaves and fruits (Zhu et al., 2021). This suggests that the increased soluble sugars of Keitt mango in response to gaseous ClO2 stress may be stored in the vacuole via sugar transporter protein MiERD6–5 and MiERD6–6.

Gaseous ClO2 enhanced Keitt mango flavor

Volatile flavor components are another crucial quality parameter of mango fruit (Zhang et al., 2019). HS-SPME-GC–MS was used to analyze the volatile components of Keitt mango from different treatments and different ripening times. The results showed that only 27 aroma components were detected in the mango pulp from control, ClO2(g), and ethephon on day 0, with the highest number of 42 aroma components when the ripening time was extended to 1 day, and the number of aroma components was reduced to 32 when the ripening time was further extended to the 4th day (Fig. 8A, Table S4). The aroma types detected were mainly terpenoids and alkanes, with terpenoids being high in quantity in mango flesh at different stages of ripening, second only to alkanes (Fig. 8A, Table S4). The PCA plots showed no distinct separation among volatile components from the control, ClO2(g), and ethephon groups at day 0.5 and day 1, whereas distinct separation was found on day 1.5 and 2, which indicated a significant difference in the distribution of volatile compounds among the three groups of mangoes at day 1.5 and day 2 of ripening (Fig. S1A–C, Fig. 8B-C). Moreover, on days 3 and 4, the ClO2(g) group was distinct separated from the ethephon group, but not from the control group, suggesting that the gaseous chlorine dioxide group and the control group shared relatively similar flavors on days 3 and 4, but differed significantly from the flavor of the ethephon-treated mangoes (Fig. 8D-E).

Volatile compounds in mangoes ripened with different treatments. (A) Quantity of volatile compounds. PCA score plot of mango ripened for (B) 1.5-day, (C) 2-day, (D) 3-day, and (E) 4-day; (F) Heatmap of the relative concentrations of differential volatile compounds with cluster analysis.

To further elucidate the variations in mango flavor among the groups, OAV was calculated in this study, and the results are shown in Table S5. Nine characteristic volatile compounds had OAV values >1, including β-myrcene, caryophyllene, β-selinene, α-selinene, 4-carene, 3-carene, d-limonene, (E, Z)-2,6-nonadienal, and (E, Z)-3,6-nonadien-1-ol, with 3-carene with sweet and rosin aroma exhibiting the highest OAV volatile across ripening stages, followed by β-selinene with herbal and β-myrcene with fruity and resinous aroma (Table S5). 3-Carene is also the most quantity-predominant compound detected in the present study, which is similar to the results of a quantitative study on three mango cultivars (Irwin, JinHwang, and Keitt) reported previously (Liu et al., 2013). The 9 volatile compounds were further screened using OPLS-DA with VIP > 1.0 and P < 0.05, yielding 8 differential volatile compounds, except for (E, Z)-3,6-nonadien-1-ol, and their relative concentrations were plotted on a heatmap after normalization and cluster analysis (Fig. 8F, Table S6). The heatmap also reflected the significant difference in the volatile compounds of mangoes from the three treatment groups that started on day 1.5 (Fig. 8F). The maximum contents of 3-carene, 4-carene and β-myrcene in gaseous ClO2-treated mango were reached on the 2nd day, with 3.67, 3.19 and 3.20-fold increases, respectively, compared to day 0 and the rest of the maximum values were reached on the 3rd day (Fig. 8F, Table S4). In addition, β-myrcene, caryophyllene, 4-carene, 3-carene, and d-limonene were significantly elevated in gaseous ClO2-treated mangoes on the second day as compared with the control and ethephon groups, and (E, Z)-2,6-nonadienal was significantly increased compared with the control group (Table S6). On the 3rd day, most of the terpenoids peaked in the mango pulp of the three treatment groups, with significant increases in β-myrcene and limonene in the ClO2(g) group compared to the control groups, and significant increases in β-myrcene, limonene, 4-carene, and 3-carene when compared to the ethephon group (Table S6). On the 4th day, the terpenoids were reduced, but 3-carene, α-selinene, and β-selinene were remarkably greater in gaseous ClO2-treated mangoes when compared to the control group, and caryophyllene, β-selinene, and α-selinene were considerably higher than in the ethephon group (Table S6). Overall, gaseous ClO2-treated mangoes exhibited the highest terpenoid contents on days 2, 3, and 4, followed by the control group and ethephon group. These suggest that the flavor of gaseous ClO2-treated mangoes was better than the control and ethephon groups, and the flavor of the control group was better than that of the ethephon group. Notably, the flavor of mangoes treated with ethephon was watery, which is in line with the previous study that ethylene-ripened fruits are of poor flavor (Adaskaveg & Blanco-Ulate, 2023).

Among the eight OAV ≥1 differential volatile compounds identified in Keitt mango, seven are terpenoids. Of these volatile constituents, β-myrcene, d-limonene, 4-carene, and 3-carene belong to monoterpenes, while caryophyllene, β-selinene, and α-selinene are sesquiterpenes. Despite the diversity of terpenoids, they are all produced from isopentenyl diphosphate (IPP) and its isomer dimethylallyl diphosphate (DMAPP), which are synthesized through two biosynthetic pathways known as the MVA and MEP pathways, respectively (Singh et al., 2024). IPP and DMAPP were then synthesized into farnesyl diphosphate (FPP) and geranyl diphosphate (GPP), which serve as direct precursors for monoterpenes and sesquiterpenes, respectively(Singh et al., 2024). Moreover, geranyl pyrophosphate synthase (GPPS) also contributes to the biosynthesis of the monoterpene precursor GPP intermediate, and terpene synthases are mainly responsible for the structural diversity of terpenoid natural products (Singh et al., 2024). The transcript quantification of key genes of terpenoids biosynthesis pathway in mango with different ripening treatments is shown in Fig. 9. The result suggest that gaseous ClO2 treatment significantly increased the transcripts of acetyl-CoA C-acetyltransferase (MiACAT), isopentenyl-diphosphate delta-isomerase (MiIDI), and geranylgeranyl diphosphate synthase (MiGPPS) compared to the ethephon and control groups, increased transcripts of hydroxymethylglutaryl-CoA reductase (MiHMGCR), 2-C-methyl-D-erythritol 2,4-cyclodiphosphate synthase (MiisPF), and terpene synthases (MiTPS) compared to ethephon group, increased transcripts of the mevalonate (diphospho) decarboxylase (MiMVD) compared to control group. This further demonstrated a higher level of terpenoids in gaseous ClO2-treated mangoes. The synthesis of terpenoids allows plants to combat abiotic stresses, including drought and oxidative stress (ROS). It has been well documented that gaseous ClO2 increases the oxidative stress in microorganisms, which results in an anti-microbial effect (Sun et al., 2019). Moreover, the changes of SOD and CAT activities in the present study indicated higher oxidative stress levels in the Keitt mangoes treated with gaseous ClO2. Thereby, the increase in biosynthesis of terpenoids and their precursors may be due to an oxidative-stress effect of gaseous ClO2 on mango.

Gaseous ClO2 improved the storage performance of ripened Keitt mango fruits

The fully ripened fruit will senesce rapidly with deterioration of sensorial attributes. The storage performance of Keitt mango ripened with gaseous ClO2 and ethephon was evaluated (Fig. 8). The color of mango pulp ripened by both gaseous ClO2 and ethephon tended to deepen with time at a storage temperature of 13 °C (Fig. 10A). After the 4th day, the color of mango pulp in the ClO2(g) group was visually fresher than the ethephon group, which was brownish inside (Fig. 10A). Consistently, the L* and h* values of mango pulp in both the ClO2(g) and ethephon groups decreased with storage time, and those of the ClO2(g) group were significantly higher than the ethephon group after day 4, which indicate a gradual darkening of mango pulp color ripened by both means and the color in ClO2(g) group was brighter than ethephon group after day 4 (Fig. 10B-C). Nevertheless, there were no discernible differences in peel color between mango fruit treated with gaseous ClO2 and ethephon during storage (Zhang et al., 2024). Moreover, fresh weight loss and soluble solids of mango fruits treated with ethephon showed a significant increasing trend with storage time, while firmness and titratable acid showed a significant decreasing trend (Fig. 10D-G). The trends of these indices were slower in gaseous ClO2-ripened mangoes (Fig. 10D-G), indicating that mangoes ripened by gaseous ClO2 senesced more slowly than those of the ethephon-ripened group during the storage time. The sensory evaluation also suggests that the gaseous ClO2-ripened mangoes had superior flavor at this storage temperature. Specifically, panelists prefer the sweetness and acidity of gaseous ClO2-ripened mangoes during the first 4 days (Fig. 11). After the 4th day, they also exhibited a greater preference for the flesh color, aroma, and flavor (Fig. 11). Collectively, the gaseous ClO2-ripened mangoes showed superior storage performance than the ethephon group at 13 °C storage temperature. This may be because the ethylene production of mangoes treated with gaseous ClO2 decreased sharply from day 2 and was 20–30 nmol C2H4 kg−1 s−1 lower than that of the ethephon group (Zhang et al., 2024). The decrease in ethylene content can delay the ripening or senescence process of mangoes and prolong their shelf life. Fleshy fruits become more susceptible to pathogens after ripening due to the changes in cell wall properties associated with softening (Li, Zhao, Wu, Grierson, & Gao, 2024). Gaseous ClO2 can effectively suppress the growth and proliferation of surface molds and bacteria on fruits (Praeger et al., 2018). The antimicrobial and ethylene-reducing effects of gaseous ClO2 may contribute to the prolonged shelf life of gaseous ClO2-ripened mangoes compared to that of ethephon treatment.

Conclusion

The current study highlighted that gaseous ClO2 treatment effectively enhanced the sensorial attributes and storage performance of post-ripened Keitt mango. Specifically, gaseous ClO2 treatment remarkably enhanced the accumulation of soluble sugars (particularly glucose) and terpenoids (particularly 3-carene) in mango pulp compared to the control and ethephon groups. Consistently, genes encoding soluble sugar production and terpenoid biosynthesis were up-regulated. And the soluble sugars may be transported by ERD6-like transporters (MiERD6–5 and MiERD6–6) into the vacuole for storage. The increase in soluble sugars and terpenoids may be due to oxidative stress caused by gaseous ClO2 in mango. Moreover, gaseous ClO2-ripened mangoes maintained notably higher quality compared to those ripened with ethephon during storage at 13 °C due to its antimicrobial and ethylene-reducing effects. In conclusion, gaseous ClO2 can effectively improve the quality of postharvest Keitt mango. This technology may offer a novel solution for enhancing the postharvest quality of other fruits. However, the specific mechanism by which gaseous ClO2 enhances the sugar accumulation, volatile development, and storage performance of Keitt mangoes requires further investigation.

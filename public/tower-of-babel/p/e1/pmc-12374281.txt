The relationship between digital literacy and innovative behavior among Chinese medical students: the chain mediating roles of diversity experience and creative self-efficacy
Authors: Juan Wu, Qilin Zhang, Xiaomin Gan, Xuefang Liu, Jiuying Hu, Yalin Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundInnovative behavior and digital literacy are two significant concerns in the cultivation of professional and innovative talents within medical higher education. Although studies suggest that information-related skills positively predict college students’ innovative behavior, research on digital literacy remains limited, and the internal mechanisms through which digital literacy influences innovative behavior have not been clearly elucidated, especially among medical students. This study aims to explore the relationship between digital literacy and innovative behavior among Chinese medical students, with diversity experience and creative self-efficacy acting as chain mediators.MethodsA cross-sectional study was conducted among a convenience sample of 887 medical students recruited from Anhui Medical University in China, from May 25 to June 16, 2023. The measures included a digital literacy scale, a diversity experience scale, a creative self-efficacy scale, and an innovative behavior scale. SPSS 25.0 and AMOS 24.0 software were used, and the structural equation model was established to statistically analyze the data.ResultsThe mean values for digital literacy, diversity experience, creative self-efficacy, and innovative behavior among Chinese medical students are above 3 but below 4, and have not yet reached the optimal level. Digital literacy and creative self-efficacy positively predicted innovative behavior. The chain mediating model revealed that diversity experience and creative self-efficacy mediated the effect of digital literacy on innovative behavior, with a total mediation effect value of 0.374, accounting for 45.89% of the total effect (0.815). Specifically, the indirect effect comprised two paths. The first path was digital literacy→creative self-efficacy→innovative behavior, with an effect value of 0.173, accounting for 21.23% of the total effect. The second path was digital literacy→diversity experience→creative self-efficacy→innovative behavior, with an effect value of 0.201, accounting for 24.66% of the total effect.ConclusionsThis study underscores the significant role that digital literacy plays in directly promoting innovative behavior among medical students, as well as through the simple mediating role of creative self-efficacy and the sequential mediating roles of diversity experience and creative self-efficacy. The findings offer valuable insights into multiple interventions or support mechanisms for encouraging innovative behavior among medical students.

Background

Innovation and digital literacy are two essential components of 21st-century skills [1, 2]. In China, under the dual guidance of the Innovation-driven Development strategy and the Healthy China strategy, promoting innovative behaviors among medical students, cultivating innovative medical talents, and thereby meeting the growing diversified healthcare demands have become a concern for higher medical education [3, 4]. Meanwhile, with the rapid advancement of new-generation information and communication technology (ICT), including Big Data, Artificial Intelligence, and the Internet of Things, digital literacy has become an essential skill for the survival and development of citizens in the digital era [5, 6]. Simultaneously, the robust advancement of the technological revolution has accelerated the digital transformation in healthcare [7]. Consequently, improving the digital literacy of medical students, such as handling big data, providing personalized healthcare through AI applications, and effectively communicating with patients via electronic health records [8–10], has also become another important concern for higher medical education institutions in training their future professionals [11–13]. Therefore, it is of important practical significance to pay attention to the digital literacy and innovative behaviors of medical students for current medical higher education.

The concept of innovative behavior was initially introduced by Amabile, who posited that it is associated with new ideas, processes, or methods of problem-solving [14]. Scott and Bruce defined it as “problem recognition and the generation of ideas or solutions, either novel or adopted,” characterizing it as a multi-staged process that begins with the identification of problems and the generation of innovative ideas, proceeds to seek support for these ideas, and culminates in the productization and institutionalization of these innovative ideas [15]. Drawing upon Bandura’s social cognitive theory, previous studies have extensively demonstrated the positive impact of creative self-efficacy as an individual psychological motivator for innovative behavior [16–19]. Additionally, diversity experience has often been discussed as being directly related to innovative behavior or indirectly related through critical thinking skills, cognitive development, and academic self-confidence as well as creative self-efficacy [20–23]. With the development and increasing popularity of ICT, scholars have begun to focus on the influences of information-related skills on college students’ innovations. Zhang highlighted that information literacy education can assist college students in identifying their information needs during the innovation process, ultimately improving their information skills. This enhancement assists individuals in completing innovative activities by employing a series of processes involving information acquisition, utilization, and transformation [24]. Other scholars have provided empirical evidence indicating that information literacy can stimulate college students’ innovation [25, 26]. However, research into the connection between digital literacy and innovative behavior remains limited, and its internal mechanisms are not well understood, particularly among medical students.

Therefore, this study concentrated on the internal influence mechanisms of digital literacy on innovative behavior of Chinese medical students. Specifically, the study aims to construct a chain mediating model to explore the relationships among digital literacy, innovative behavior, creative self-efficacy, and diversity experience, with creative self-efficacy and diversity experience acting as chain mediating variables. It is believed that the relevant results and conclusions can offer theoretical and practical insights for fostering the innovative behavior of Chinese medical students.

Literature review and hypotheses

Relationship between digital literacy and innovative behavior

The term “digital literacy” originated in the 1990 s, with its initial definition proposed by Gilster, who defined it as “the ability to understand and use information in multiple formats from a wide range of sources when it is presented via computers” [27, 28]. Following Gilster’s work, scholars have progressively developed more detailed definitions [29]. Among these, the definition proposed by the UNESCO Institute for Statistics in 2018 holds significant importance, as it takes into account different levels of development [30]. The Chinese government also places great emphasis on citizens’ digital literacy. In November 2021, the Office of the Central Cyberspace Affairs Commission of China proposed an Action Outline for Enhancing the Digital Literacy and Skills of All Citizens, and digital literacy was defined for the first time in the Chinese context, namely, the collection of a series of qualities and abilities that citizens in a digital society should possess, including digital acquisition, production, use, evaluation, interaction, sharing, innovation, security assurance, ethics and morals [31].

In the last two decades, especially since the COVID-19 pandemic, digital literacy has become a hot topic in education and learning research, and the number of related publications has increased almost exponentially [13, 32, 33]. Previous research has indicated that college students with greater digital literacy are more likely to retrieve more relevant and useful information that can subsequently be transformed into academic, business, political, and social advantages [34]. Additionally, Ahmed and Roche clearly argued that the development of digital literacy helps improve the academic achievement of college students and that rich academic achievement helps them apply knowledge and skills to overcome obstacles and difficulties that may be encountered in the innovation process, thereby fostering innovative behavior [35, 36]. In the medical field, recent empirical research has shown that information literacy, particularly in information seeking and utilization, not only directly promotes the innovative behavior of nurses and nursing students [26, 37, 38], but also indirectly promotes innovative behavior through the mediating role of lifelong learning among medical students [25]. Therefore, this study proposed the following hypothesis:

H1: Digital literacy positively predicts medical students’ innovation behavior.

Relationships between digital literacy, diversity experience, and innovative behavior

Diversity experience of college students refers to the fact that through courses, seminars and various social interaction activities, individuals have the opportunity to be in a relatively new and challenging situation, face challenges that are different from their original cognition, and try to overcome them, achieving individual and team development, and intelligence improvement [22]. A high level of digital literacy helps individuals break through geographic and cultural boundaries and uses digital technologies and platforms to extensively access diverse global information resources and communicate and interact with others, thus enriching individuals’ diversity experiences. According to Bandura’s social cognitive theory, a rich diversity experience can help college students acquire diverse knowledge and experiences and cultivate independent and critical thinking, thereby advancing their cognitive development [16]. Additionally, it positively affects students’ psychology, including the formation of an open and inclusive mindset, the improvement of self-confidence and the increase of cognitive flexibility, all of which are conducive to promoting individual innovative behavior [39–42]. Mo also emphasized that it is a common feature of undergraduate education in world-class universities to support the cultivation of college students’ creativity through diverse learning experiences [43]. Furthermore, several studies on Chinese college students have shown that the diversity experiences of college students have a significant positive effect on their innovative behavior [21, 22, 44]. Therefore, this study proposed the following hypotheses:

H2: Diversity experience plays a mediating role between digital literacy and medical students’ innovative behavior (including H2a: Digital literacy is positively associated with medical students’ diversity experience; H2b: Diversity experience is positively associated with medical students’ innovative behavior).

Relationships between digital literacy, creative self-efficacy, and innovative behavior

Creative self-efficacy was proposed by Tierney and Farmer on the basis of self-efficacy theory and creativity theory, and it is used to reflect an individual’s confidence in the ability to complete innovation activities and solve complex problems, it influences an individual’s willingness to accept new challenges and try new ones. solutions, and the degree of persistence in the face of difficulties [45, 46]. Individuals with a high level of digital literacy are more likely to be proficient in using digital tools and technologies and to screen and evaluate information efficiently, which helps stimulate their innovative thinking and generate innovative ideas [15]. Furthermore, students with a high degree of digital literacy exhibit superior communication and collaboration skills. They are more adept at working effectively with others, sharing information, and engaging in discussions when addressing complex issues. This collaborative environment also allows individuals to seek and receive support from their peers, thereby enhancing their creative self-efficacy to some extent. Several previous studies have shown that creative self-efficacy, as a positive psychological motivator for individuals’ innovative behavior, significantly promotes the implementation of innovative behavior [17, 18, 47, 48]. This conclusion has also been widely verified among college students [21, 44, 45]. Therefore, this study proposed the following hypotheses:

H3: Creative self-efficacy mediates the relationship between digital literacy and medical students’ innovative behavior (including H3a: Digital literacy is positively associated with medical students’ creative self-efficacy; H3b: Creative self-efficacy is positively associated with medical students’ innovative behavior).

The chain mediating roles of diversity experience and creative self-efficacy

As mentioned earlier, a high level of digital literacy can help medical students communicate and interact in diverse manners and help medical students accumulate diversity experiences. Rich and diversity experiences are highly beneficial to the cognitive and psychological development of medical students and are conducive to helping medical students accumulate knowledge and experience and increase social capital and psychological capital. As a core part of Bandura’s social cognitive theory, self-efficacy needs to be built on certain experiences and foundations, and the diversity of experience provides an important premise for the formation of medical students’ sense of self-efficacy [16]. Moreover, self-efficacy, as an important predictor of behavior, further promotes the occurrence of innovative behavior. Several studies based on a large national survey of college students have emphasized that creative self-efficacy is a key mediating variable between diversity experience and innovative behavior [21, 22, 44]. Therefore, this study proposed the following hypotheses:

H4: Diversity experience and creative self-efficacy play a chain mediating role between digital literacy and innovative behavior.

In summary, according to hypotheses H1-4, a chain mediation model for the relationship between digital literacy and innovative behavior through the chain mediating effect of diversity experience and creative self-efficacy was constructed (as shown in Fig. 1).

Materials and methods

Procedure and participants

Convenience sampling was employed in this study to recruit medical undergraduates from Anhui Medical University in Anhui Province, China. A structured questionnaire was initially designed via an electronic questionnaire collection platform, Wenjuanxing (https://www.wjx.cn/). To enhance the questionnaire’s quality prior to the formal survey, researchers conducted a first round of pre-survey with 30 randomly selected medical students. On the basis of feedback from this pre-survey, the questionnaire was refined to ensure its rationality and comprehensibility. A second round of pre-survey was subsequently conducted with 10 additional randomly selected medical students to observe their completion time. The shortest completion time recorded was 120 s. Following this, the researchers initiated the formal survey, displaying the questionnaire’s QR codes to the students during their breaks between classes. The participants scanned the codes via WeChat, completed the questionnaire on the spot, and submitted it to the platform. A total of 950 questionnaires were collected from May 25 to June 16, 2023, of which 63 were deemed invalid due to incompleteness or excessively short completion times (less than 120 s), resulting in 887 valid samples, yielding an effective recovery rate of 93.4%. All data collection adhered to the principles of voluntary participation and informed consent and was approved by the Ethics Committee of Anhui Medical University (No. 83243452).

The participants consisted of 317 men (35.74%) and 570 women (64.26%), aged 17 to 24 years old (M = 20.25, SD = 1.57). 220 (24.80%) were only children while 667 (75.20%) were not only children. 371 (41.83%) were first-year students, 432 (48.70%) were second-year students, and the remaining 84 (9.47%) were in their third, fourth, or fifth year. 469 (52.87%) performed well, ranking in the top 40% of their major, while 369 (41.60%) felt that academic pressure was somewhat heavy or heavy. 626 (70.57%) were from rural areas or towns, and 261 (29.43%) were from cities. With respect to their family’s economic status, 569 (64.15%) rated it as at or above the local average, whereas 318 (35.85%) rated it below the local average.

Measures and instruments

General information questionnaire

According to the previous studies, the general information questionnaire in this study involved the information on gender, age, grade, one child, academic performance, academic pressure, family economic status, and place of origin [12, 21, 22, 25, 34].

Measurement of digital literacy

Drawing upon the seven dimensions of the Digital Literacy Global Framework (DLGF) developed by the UNESCO Institute for Statistics and prior research on Chinese college students’ digital literacy [30, 47, 49], the digital literacy scale in this study consists of 25 items that includes six dimensions. The six dimensions included: information and data literacy (4 items), communication and collaboration literacy (3 items), digital content creation literacy (4 items), safety literacy (5 items), problem-solving literacy (3 items), and career-related literacy (6 items). Each item was rated on a 5-point Likert scale, ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). The digital literacy is scored on a range of 25 to 125, with a higher score indicating a higher level of digital literacy. The Cronbach’s alpha of the scale in this study was 0.927.

Measurement of innovative behavior

The innovative behavior scale, originally developed by Scott and Bruce and revised by Mei et al. to account for the characteristics of innovative practices among Chinese college students, was used to measure the innovative behavior of medical students [15, 36]. It consists of 6 items, such as “I will try to use new methods and techniques”, “I will find ways to obtain the necessary resources to realize my new ideas”, and “I will actively make plans to implement the new ideas”. Each item was rated on a 5-point Likert scale, ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). Innovative behavior is scored on a range of 6 to 30, with a higher score indicating greater innovative behavior. The scale has been widely used among college students and has demonstrated good reliability [21, 22]. In this study, the Cronbach’s alpha for the scale was 0.919.

Measurement of diversity experience

The diversity experience scale, initially developed by Gruin et al. and revised by other scholars to incorporate the Chinese context, was utilized to assess the diversity experiences of medical students [21, 22, 42, 50]. The scale focused on classroom diversity, encompassing the contents of diverse peoples from books and other materials, and interactions with diverse peers in the classroom. It included 5 items, e.g., “I will actively participate in classroom discussions”, “I will actively read course materials and related literature”, and “When I encounter difficult problems, I will proactively communicate with teachers and classmates”. Each item was rated on a 5-point Likert scale, ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). The diversity experience is scored on a range of 5 to 25, with higher scores indicating the richer diversity experience. In this study, the Cronbach’s alpha for the scale was 0.913.

Measurement of creative self-efficacy

The creative self-efficacy scale, developed by Tierney and Farmer, was utilized to evaluate the creative self-efficacy of medical students [46]. It consists of 4 items that encompass various assessments of the students’ confidence: (1) I am adept at improving others’ opinions; (2) I excel at generating new ideas; (3) I am skilled at solving problems in new ways; and (4) I have great confidence in my ability to solve problems. Each item was rated on a 5-point Likert scale, ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). The creative self-efficacy scores range from 4 to 20, with higher scores indicating a greater level of creative self-efficacy. In this study, the Cronbach’s alpha for the scale was 0.894.

Statistical analyses

Statistical analyses were conducted via SPSS 25.0 and Amos 24.0. The common method biases test, descriptive statistical analysis, and Pearson correlation analysis were performed using SPSS 25.0. A structural equation model was then established using Amos 24.0 to examine the chain mediating effects of diversity experience and creative self-efficacy on the relationship between digital literacy and innovative behavior, where bootstrap methods were used to draw samples 5000 times and estimate 95% confidence intervals. Differences with P values less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant. The chain mediating effects were considered significant if zero was not included in the bootstrap confidence intervals.

Results

Common methods biases analyses

The data in this study were all obtained from the participants’ self-reports, and thus the results may have significant biases. To mitigate the impact of common method biases, the questionnaires were completed anonymously, and the data was utilized solely for academic research. Additionally, Harman’s single-factor method was performed to test for possible common method biases before data analysis [51]. All the measurement items were integrated for exploratory factor analysis. The findings indicated that there were four common factors with characteristic values greater than one, and the maximum factor variance explained was 24.91%, which was below the standard threshold of 40%. Therefore, there was no significant common methodological biases in this study.

Descriptive statistical analysis and correlation analysis

Table 1 presents the results of the descriptive analysis and Pearson correlations for the core variables. Each variable’s mean value exceeds 3 but remains below 4, indicating that the digital literacy, diversity experience, creative self-efficacy, and innovative behavior of medical students are all at a moderately high level, yet there is still a gap to reach the optimal level. Additionally, there was a significant pairwise positive correlation among the core variables (r = 0.593 to 0.762, P < 0.01). Specifically, digital literacy was significantly positively correlated with diversity experience (r = 0.593, P < 0.01), creative self-efficacy (r = 0.606, P < 0.01), and innovative behavior (r = 0.677, P < 0.01); diversity experience was significantly positively correlated with creative self-efficacy (r = 0.704, P < 0.01) and innovative behavior (r = 0.663, P < 0.01); and creative self-efficacy was significantly positively correlated with innovative behavior (r = 0.762, P < 0.01). The correlations between the core variables supported the subsequent testing of the hypotheses.

Discussion

This study focuses on the digital literacy and innovative behavior of Chinese medical students. Based on social cognitive theory, this study introduced diversity experience and creative self-efficacy to construct a chain mediating model and empirically explored the complex relationships among digital literacy, diversity experience, creative self-efficacy and innovative behavior. First, the descriptive results indicated that the digital literacy, diversity experience, creative self-efficacy, and innovation behavior of Chinese medical students did not reach the optimal level, which is consistent with recent studies in the Chinese context [24, 52–54]. These findings warrant the attention of medical higher educators to focus and take actions to improve the corresponding levels. In fact, the core role of digital literacy in higher education has been widely recognized. Murray et al. investigated the top 50 universities around the globe and found that the majority of universities undertook some form of initiative to promote digital literacy [55]. In particular, recent research involving 19 clinical educators and medical school leaders in Singapore emphasized the need for more formal training to equip medical students with the necessary digital competencies [12]. Furthermore, the insufficiency of innovative behavior among Chinese medical students is consistent with prior research [3, 26, 56], which is attributed to the fact that medical education in China is mainly undergraduate education and focuses on basic knowledge and skills training [57].

Second, current findings suggest that two significant factors influence innovative behavior among Chinese medical students: creative self-efficacy and digital literacy. Regarding the former, scholars widely agree that creative self-efficacy positively correlates with individuals’ innovative behavior, emphasizing that innovation self-efficacy, which is an individual’s assessment of their own innovative capabilities and confidence, is a crucial precursor to engaging in innovative activities [56, 58–60]. This finding is also verified in the present study. Regarding the latter, the digital literacy of medical students was found to positively influence their innovative behavior. This finding is similar to that of Pilav-Velić et al., who reported the significant role that digital literacy plays in individuals’ innovative work behavior among employee groups in the workplace [61]. While there is no direct evidence to support a positive relationship between digital literacy and innovative behavior among medical students, several conclusions from the related studies also offer insightful supportive evidence. Zhong et al. conducted a study on Chinese nursing students and found that overall information-seeking behavior and its seven sub-dimensions were positively correlated with innovative behavior, particularly in the area of information utilization [26]. Similarly, a recent study involving 473 third-year and fourth-year medical students in Pakistan focused on information literacy, which is closely related to digital literacy, given that digital literacy is considered a new development of information literacy in the digital era [62].The study also highlighted that the information literacy had a significant positive effect on their creative skills [25].

Finally, and most importantly, this study also explored the relationship between digital literacy and innovative behavior among medical students in China. The current findings underscore the two indirect pathways by which medical students’ digital literacy predicts their innovative behaviors. On the one hand, the digital literacy of medical students could positively influence their innovative behavior indirectly, with creative self-efficacy serving as a partial mediator. This was anticipated, as prior research on undergraduates from Malaysia and a Gulf State all suggested that enhanced digital literacy enables individuals to retrieve more relevant and useful information, which can then be translated into academic advantages, leading to improved academic performance and success [34, 35]. Moreover, high academic achievement can further increase students’ creative self-efficacy and ultimately promote their innovative behavior [63]. On the other hand, the findings suggest that the relationship between digital literacy and innovative behavior can be partially explained by the chain mediating roles of diversity experience and creative self-efficacy, and the chain mediating effect is slightly more pronounced than the simple mediating effect of creative self-efficacy alone. A high level of digital literacy enhances the richness of information acquisition and social experiences by leveraging digital technologies and platforms, aiding students in accumulating their diversity experiences. Furthermore, a previous meta-analysis has indicated that college diversity experiences are significantly and positively correlated with cognitive development [42]. Additionally, existing research has underscored other positive educational outcomes of diversity experiences, such as fostering critical thinking skills and academic confidence [39, 64], which could further encourage the emergence of innovative behavior [22, 45].

Research implications and limitations

The study examined the relationship between digital literacy and innovative behavior among Chinese medical students, with diversity experience and creative self-efficacy serving as chain mediators. This study may contribute to the existing literature in the following three ways. First, digital literacy is the core of current higher education [11, 55]. Although medical educators increasingly recognize the importance of cultivating digital literacy among medical students, the related research is still very limited [12]. Therefore, this study can enrich the research on digital literacy in the field of higher education to a certain extent, especially in the field of medical higher education. Second, innovation is essential for individuals to sustain an advantage in highly competitive environments [2]. The study focused on medical students to explore the effects of digital literacy, diversity experience, and creative self-efficacy on innovative behavior. The relevant research results not only expand the research sample scope on innovative behavior but also enrich the research on the antecedents that affect innovative behavior. Third, the results reveal the complex mechanism underlying the influence of medical students’ digital literacy on innovative behavior, thereby enriching research on the relationship between digital literacy and innovative behavior to some extent. The tested research model and related results also provide scientific material and tools for subsequent research.

Additionally, this study has practical implications for medical higher education. The promotion of medical students’ innovative behavior has always been a core concern for medical higher education in the cultivation of innovative medical talents. This study highlights the importance of digital literacy for medical students’ innovative behavior. Therefore, medical colleges and universities should first establish a sense of urgency in enhancing students’ digital literacy and actively engage in corresponding actions. These actions should include creating a positive digital campus culture, strengthening the construction of digital infrastructure, and improving the digital hardware facilities on campuses, such as smart libraries and learning management systems. Furthermore, digital literacy courses should be integrated into the curriculum system for medical students, offering a diverse range of compulsory and elective courses tailored to medical students with different majors and academic levels. Moreover, great importance should be placed on enhancing teachers’ digital literacy, and they should be encouraged to actively explore innovative teaching methods for integrating the cultivation of students’ digital literacy into their professional courses. Moreover, the study also underscores the mediating roles of diversity experience and creative self-efficacy in the positive impact of digital literacy on medical students’ innovative behavior. Therefore, on the one hand, medical colleges and universities should help medical students accumulate their diverse experiences by improving teaching methods (e.g., group learning, and experiential learning) and providing a variety of extracurricular activities (e.g., academic lectures, volunteer services, and student clubs). On the other hand, encouraging medical students to overcome their fear of difficulties and to build creative self-confidence in their daily studies and lives is also crucial for promoting their innovative behaviors.

Nevertheless, several limitations of the present research remain to be addressed in future studies. As with many related studies, self-report questionnaires were used to collect the data in this study. Some scholars noted that the self-reported evaluation of personal ability and performance will produce a Dunning–Kruger effect, that is, individuals tend to overestimate or underestimate their own ability [65]. Therefore, as ÖNCÜL suggested, future studies could use standardized tests or explore other non-self-reporting methods, such as performance task, to better verify the validity of the findings [66]. Furthermore, although the model validated in this study is based on existing research and theoretical foundations, the study is inherently cross-sectional, which prevents us from inferring the exact causal relationships among the key variables. This limitation could be addressed by conducting a longitudinal study in the future. Additionally, this study employed convenience sampling, recruiting participants from the same medical university. This indicates that the sample’s representativeness is biased. Consequently, future research could adopt random sampling methods and extend the geographical scope of the sample to increase its representativeness. Finally, the digital literacy scale discussed in this article primarily refers to the DLGF and previous related studies on Chinese college students, without fully considering the characteristics of medical students. Recently, Zhang and Wu developed a digital literacy scale specifically for Chinese medical students [67], which can be utilized in future research to enhance the reliability of results concerning the impact of digital literacy on innovative behaviors among medical students.

Conclusions

This study analyzed the interrelationships among digital literacy, diversity experience, creative self-efficacy and innovative behavior of Chinese medical students. The main conclusions drawn from this study included as follows: first, the digital literacy, diversity experience, creative self-efficacy, and innovative behavior of Chinese medical students were all above average, yet not reached the optimal level, indicating a certain potential for improvement. Second, two important antecedents may positively affect medical students’ innovative behavior: creative self-efficacy and digital literacy. Third, medical students’ digital literacy could not only directly affect their innovative behavior but also indirectly affect their innovative behavior through the simple mediating role of creative self-efficacy and the chain mediating roles of diversity experience and creative self-efficacy. Furthermore, the direct effect appears to be more significant than the indirect effect, and among the indirect effects, the chain mediating effect of diversity experience and creative self-efficacy seems to be greater than the simple mediating effect of creative self-efficacy. These findings not only highlighted the significant role that digital literacy plays in the process of cultivating innovative medical talents within higher education but also provided valuable insights into multiple interventions or support pathways for fostering innovative behavior among medical students [68].

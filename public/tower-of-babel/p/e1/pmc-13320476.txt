EGFR and Its Ligands in Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis: Predicting Clinical Outcomes and Molecular Endotyping Through In-Depth Analysis
Authors: Jong-Uk Lee, Seung-Lee Park, Min Kyung Kim, Eun-Jeong Seo, Hun-Gyu Hwang, Beomseok Son, Tae Rim Kim, Han-Oh Park, June Young Park, Hun Soo Chang, Choon-Sik Park
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe dysregulation of alveolar epithelial cells has been proposed as the cause of idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) due to abnormal activation of epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR). However, the ways of interaction with EGFR ligands including amphiregulin (AREG), epidermal growth factor (EGF), transforming growth factor (TGF)-α and heparin-binding EGF have not been revealed. The aim of the present study was to investigate the diagnostic and prognostic significance of EGFR and its ligands in IPF.MethodsEGFR and its ligands were quantified by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay in bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) fluids and serum from 131 patients with IPF and 52 normal controls (NCs). Mortality risk was evaluated using Cox proportional hazards models. Cluster analysis based on EGFR and its ligands was conducted using both hierarchical and K-means clustering methods.ResultsThe patients exhibited significantly higher levels of EGFR, AREG, EGF, and TGF-α compared to NCs. The threshold values for AREG, EGF and TGF-α were determined to be 12.2 pg/mL, 6.3 pg/mL and 5.2 pg/mL, respectively, for the two groups. The sensitivities were 79.83%, 88.24%, and 56.30%, and the specificities were 81.82%, 31.82%, and 63.64% to diagnose IPF, respectively. The mortality rate was significantly higher in IPF patients with low levels compared to those with high levels of EGFR and EGF on Cox analysis. Four distinct clusters were identified based on the levels of EGFR and its ligands in BAL fluids. C3 group (n = 7), which exhibited the highest levels of TGF-α, AREG and EGFR, showed the highest risk of mortality, whereas the C2 group (n = 40), with the highest level of EGF and the intermediate levels of AREG, EGFR, and TGF-α, had the lowest risk.ConclusionThe diverse effects of EGFR on mortality may depend on balance of TGF-α and EGF. Therefore, a comprehensive analysis of EGFR and its ligands could help predict molecular endotypes and clinical outcomes for IPF patients.Keywords: Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis, EGF Receptor, EGF Receptor Ligands, Clustering

INTRODUCTION

Idiopathic interstitial pneumonia (IIP) is characterized by the accumulation of inflammatory cells and fibroblasts in the lung tissue and interstitium. Within the spectrum of IIP, idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) stands out as a subset where irreversible fibrosis occurs, leading to a gradual decline in lung function and a grim prognosis.1 In the healthy lung, the majority of the gas-exchanging epithelial surface is comprised of type 1 alveolar epithelial cells (AEC1s), which play a crucial role in maintaining lung homeostasis. In response to alveolar damage, type 2 alveolar epithelial cells (AEC2s) serve as progenitors for AEC1s to restore normal alveolar function.2,3

Recent advancements in single-cell transcriptomics have provided insights into the evolving lung microenvironment in patients with interstitial lung disease (ILD), including IPF (www.ipfcellatlas.com).4 Lung tissues affected by IPF exhibit extensive remodeling features: a loss of AEC1s and AEC2s while the emergence of a new cell type called aberrant basaloid cells.4 In healthy lungs, regenerative processes occur at the sites of the alveolar damage via the activation of epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR).5 EGFR signaling pathways regulate cellular growth, proliferation, and survival,6 suggesting a potential link between EGFR dysregulation and pulmonary fibrosis. Clinically, the intricate relationship between EGFR signaling and lung disease has also been suggested in oncology studies. In patients with non-small cell lung cancer treated with EGFR inhibitors, the incidence of ILD was about 1%, increasing to 2% in the Japanese population, suggesting racial variability.7,8,9,10 Furthermore, high expression of transforming growth factor (TGF)-α has been linked to larger tumor size and higher histologic grade, while EGF and AREG are associated with smaller tumor diameter and lower histologic grade.11 The use of cetuximab, an EGFR inhibitor, has been associated with longer progression-free survival in cancer patients with high AREG expression.12 These findings illustrate the diverse biological functions of EGFR ligands and provide relevant context for their potential role in pulmonary fibrosis. EGFR is regulated by several ligands, including epidermal growth factor (EGF), amphiregulin (AREG), TGF-α, and heparin-binding EGF (HB-EGF). Each of these ligands has been revealed to elevate in the lung tissues and peripheral bloods of the patients with IPF.13,14,15,16 Based on binding affinity, they are divided into two groups: The high-affinity ligands, including EGF, TGF-α, and HB-EGF, bind to the cell surface EGFR with Kds of 0.1–1 nM. Conversely, AREG, a low-affinity ligand, binds 10–100 times less strongly, competing with high-affinity ligands only when present in elevated concentrations to trigger distinct pathway cascades.17 Thus, activation of EGFR is regulated differently depending on the ligands18: the EGFR shows a so-called “agonistic bias,” which means that ligands with different affinities induce the activation of different downstream signaling pathways. The observed increases of EGFR and its ligands in the lungs affected by IPF may highlight the complex interplay which is crucial for maintaining lung homeostasis. Thus, the interaction EGFR with its ligand cannot be underestimated, yet limited clinical studies have elucidated the complex relationship of EGFR and its ligands in IPF. In this study, we investigated the potential relationship between EGFR and its ligands and their suitability as biomarkers for the development and progression of IPF through the analysis of patient samples. We compared the levels of EGFR and its ligands in bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) fluids and serum from patients with IPF and normal controls (NCs). We also analyzed the correlation between these levels and the mortality rate of IPF patients. Additionally, we categorized the subjects based on the levels of these mediators to determine their interactive relationship with clinical outcomes.

METHODS

Study subjects

All participants underwent a chest X-ray, high-resolution chest computed tomography (HRCT), and pulmonary function tests including forced vital capacity (FVC) and diffusing capacity of the lung for carbon monoxide (DLCO). BAL fluids and serum samples were obtained from the Biobank of Soonchunhyang University Hospital in Bucheon, Korea with approval from the Institutional Review Board (IRB) (KoNIBP; P01-201911-31-00 and IRB No. SCHBC 2015-08-025-005). IPF was diagnosed in accordance with the 2011 and 2018 international guidelines either by surgical lung biopsy confirming a usual interstitial pneumonia (UIP) pattern (surgical IPF; n = 68) or by multidisciplinary discussion without lung biopsy (clinical IPF; n = 53).19,20 According to the 2018 guideline computed tomography classification, 108 patients were categorized as UIP and 23 as probable UIP. The diagnostic criteria for nonspecific interstitial pneumonia (NSIP), hypersensitivity pneumonitis (HP), and sarcoidosis were established based on international consensus statements.19,20,21,22,23 None of the patients diagnosed with IPF exhibited any signs of underlying collagen vascular diseases, as confirmed by laboratory tests and clinical symptoms. NCs did not show any respiratory symptoms, as confirmed by a screening questionnaire, and had forced expiratory volume in 1 second and FVC levels above 80%, along with normal chest radiographs.

ELISA of EGFR and its ligands in BAL fluid and serum

In patients with IPF, BAL was performed from the most significantly involved lower lobe segments as determined by HRCT, whereas in NCs it was obtained from the right middle lobe. The mean BAL fluid recovery rate was 41.0% in IPF and 55.7% in NCs, consistently exceeding the recommended 30% threshold24 ensuring the quality of samples. Total and differential cell counts were reliably obtained using standardized cytocentrifuge preparation and Diff-Quik staining, with 500 cells counted per slide. The remaining BAL samples after the diagnostic laboratory study were stored at –80°C in the Biobank. Protein levels were measured using ELISA kits for EGFR (DY231, R&D Systems, Minneapolis, MN, USA), AREG (DY262, R&D Systems), EGF (DY236, R&D Systems), HB-EGF (DY259B, R&D Systems), and TGF-α (DY239, R&D Systems), following the manufacturer’s recommendations. The lower limits of detection for AREG, EGF, EGFR, HB-EGF, and TGF-α were 4.4 pg/mL, 1.9 pg/mL, 31.2 pg/mL, 0.7 pg/mL, and 0.7 pg/mL, respectively. Any values below these limits were adjusted to 0 pg/mL. The coefficients of variation for both inter-assay and intra-assay were less than 15%.

Cluster and discriminant analyses

An unsupervised clustering analysis was conducted using a two-step approach. First, the optimal number of clusters was determined by hierarchical clustering with Ward’s method, applied to EGFR, EGF, and TGF-α, and guided by the NbClust R package, which evaluates multiple indices to propose the most appropriate partitioning solution. These three variables were selected because they demonstrated the strongest diagnostic and prognostic associations, whereas AREG showed only borderline prognostic impact and HB-EGF did not demonstrate significant clinical relevance. Second, k-means clustering was performed on these variables to refine the cluster assignment. The stability and validity of the final clustering solution were further assessed using the clusterCrit R package based on internal validity indices such as silhouette width, Dunn index, and the Calinski–Harabasz criterion.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using the Biomedical Analysis Workspace (hyper.schedulerju.com), a clinical statistical analysis platform built on SciPy, statsmodels, lifelines, and scikit-learn. The normality of the distribution was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test. The data are presented using medians with the 25th and 75th quartiles for variables having a skewed distribution. The Kruskal-Wallis analysis of variance and Mann-Whitney U-test were conducted to compare two groups. For variables following a normal distribution, the mean ± standard error of the mean is reported. One-way ANOVA with Bonferroni’s test as a post-hoc test was conducted to compare two groups. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis was conducted to determine the optimal threshold values for the scores. The highest Youden’s index was used to calculate the optimal cut-off values. The comparison of different areas under the curve (AUC) was conducted using the Z-statistics calculation, as outlined by DeLong et al.25 Mortality was analyzed using Cox proportional hazards regression models with backward elimination to identify independent risk factors, adjusting for lung function. For cluster-based survival analysis, pairwise Cox regression models were used to compare survival outcomes between clusters, and hazard ratios with corresponding P values were calculated. The correlation between two parameters was analyzed using Spearman’s correlation coefficient. Values of P < 0.05 were deemed significant.

Ethics statement

This study was conducted in Korea with approval from the IRB of the Korea National Institute for Bioethics Policy (KoNIBP; P01-201911-31-00) and the Soonchunhyang University Hospital Ethics Committee (IRB No. SCHBC 2015-08-025-005), and the requirement for informed consent was waived.

RESULTS

Comparison of EGFR and its ligand levels in BAL fluids and serum across groups

BAL samples were obtained from 131 patients with IPF, 50 patients with NSIP, 27 patients with HP, 20 patients with sarcoidosis, and 52 NCs (Table 1). Non-IPF ILDs (NSIP, HP, and sarcoidosis) were included as disease control groups to better contextualize the specificity of EGFR and its ligands in IPF. They exhibited higher total cell counts with significant variations in the percentages of macrophages, neutrophils, eosinophils, and lymphocytes, as well as lower FVC values compared to those of NCs (Table 1). EGFR, AREG, EGF, TGF-α, and HB-EGF were detected in the BAL fluids of over 90% of study participants (Fig. 1). Compared to the NC group, levels of EGFR, AREG, EGF, and TGF-α were significantly higher in the patient groups (P < 0.05). These findings in NSIP and HP, which were included as disease control groups, indicate that elevations of EGFR and its ligands are not restricted to IPF. In comparison to the ROC curve of NCs, patients with IPF had a greater AUC for AREG (0.828, P = 0.001), EGF (0.608, P = 0.035), and TGF-α levels (0.601, P = 0.048) (Fig. 2A). Cutoff values of 12.2 pg/mL for AREG, and 6.3 pg/mL for EGF, and 5.2 pg/mL for TGF-α yielded sensitivities of 79.83%, 88.24% and 56.30% and specificities of 81.82%, 31.82% and 63.64%, respectively (Fig. 2B). The EGFR and its ligands protein level were not correlated with the initial FVC and DLCO (data not shown). EGFR and its ligands were detected in serum of all study subjects, and their levels were similar across the groups (Supplementary Fig. 1).

Comparison of mortality outcomes in patients with IPF based on the levels of EGFR and its ligands

The univariate analysis (Fig. 3, Table 2) revealed that patients with EGFR levels ≥ 249.1 pg/mL had a significantly decreased risk of mortality compared to those with lower levels (hazard ratio [HR], 0.44; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.22–0.88; P = 0.021) (Fig. 3A). A similar trend was observed for EGF, with patients exhibiting levels ≥ 10.1 pg/mL showing a significantly reduced risk of mortality compared to those with lower levels (HR, 0.43; 95% CI, 0.20–0.89; P = 0.025) (Fig. 3C). High levels of AREG also demonstrated a trend toward reduced mortality risk (HR, 0.53; 95% CI, 0.26–1.06; P = 0.077), although the association did not reach statistical significance (Fig. 3B). In contrast, patients with TGF-α levels ≥ 5.0 pg/mL tended to show an increased mortality risk (HR, 1.91; 95% CI, 0.88–4.12; P = 0.098) (Fig. 3D). In the multivariable analysis, which included FVC and DLCO as covariates, both high levels of EGFR and EGF remained independently associated with a lower risk of mortality. Specifically, EGFR ≥ 249.1 pg/mL (HR, 0.38; 95% CI, 0.17–0.85; P = 0.019) and EGF ≥ 10.1 pg/mL (HR, 0.41; 95% CI, 0.18–0.95; P = 0.038) were significantly linked to reduced mortality risk (Table 2).

Analysis of the clusters based on EGFR and its ligand levels in BAL fluids

The analysis using the levels of EGFR and its ligands identified a four-cluster solution as the most optimal outcome (Cluster 1 [C1]: n = 27, Cluster 2 [C2]: n = 40, Cluster 3 [C3]: n = 7, Cluster 4 [C4]: n = 40), as illustrated by the heatmap and radar plot (Fig. 4A and B) and summarized in Table 3. The levels of EGFR and EGF exhibited significant differences (P < 0.001 and 0.003, respectively), while the levels of TGF-α showed a noticeable trend (P = 0.089) among the clusters. The median levels of EGFR, AREG and TGF-α were highest in the C3 group, while the lowest levels of them were observed in the C1 group. The C2 group had an intermediate level of AREG, EGFR, and TGF-α and the highest level of EGF. A significant difference in mortality risk was observed between the clusters. Specifically, the C3 group had a significantly higher risk of mortality compared to the C2 group (HR, 4.59; P = 0.018), and tended toward an increased risk compared to the C1 group (HR, 1.78; P = 0.337) (Fig. 4C and D). The C2 group, which exhibited high levels of EGF and low (intermediate or relatively low) levels of TGF-α, showed the most favorable outcomes, supporting the a complex interactions of EGFR and its ligands in final outcome of the disease. In contrast, EGFR (soluble form of EGFR) and AREG were highly expressed in the C3 group and the concomitant elevation of TGF-α may attenuate the negative correlations between EGFR and mortality, thereby contributing to the increased risk of mortality observed in this cluster.

DISCUSSION

In this study, we demonstrated that patients with IPF have increased levels of EGFR and its ligands in BAL fluids compared to healthy individuals. Among them, AREG, EGF, and TGF-α showed potential as diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers, as supported by ROC curve analysis. High levels of EGFR and EGF exhibited a reduced risk of mortality in multivariate analysis. The high level of EGFR may have a favorable effect on mortality in the presence of high amount of EGF in cluster analysis. However, in the presence of high amount TGF-α, the increased levels of EGFR may not exert the protective effect on mortality. These findings suggest a potential agonistic and antagonistic role of EGF and TGF-α in modulating EGFR-mediated pathways and underscores the interaction of ligand-specific context in determining clinical outcomes, for the first time to the best of our knowledge.

Although EGFR, AREG, EGF, and TGF-α were elevated in IPF compared with NCs, similar increases were also observed in patients with NSIP and HP, indicating that these biomarkers are not specific to IPF. Thus, their utility as diagnostic markers for distinguishing IPF from other ILDs may be limited. Nevertheless, within the IPF population, EGFR and its ligands demonstrated significant prognostic associations, highlighting their potential clinical value in risk stratification rather than disease discrimination.

A study of peripheral blood samples has demonstrated that EGFR levels are higher in 35 subjects with worsened survival compared to 25 IPF patients who did not experience disease progression during the study.16 Our study found no significant differences in the blood levels of EGFR and its ligands between patients with IPF and NCs. However, it is also worth noting that the group having the highest BAL fluid levels of both EGFR and TGF-α exhibited the worst prognosis among the clusters in the present study. Accordingly, it is recommended to measure EGFR and its ligands in the peripheral bloods as well as in the lungs together to predict the prognosis of IPF. The discrepancy between BAL and serum EGFR levels observed in Clusters 1 and 4 may be explained by compartmentalization: BAL fluid reflects the local pulmonary microenvironment, whereas serum levels are subject to systemic influences such as clearance, dilution, and ectodomain shedding. Thus, BAL and serum EGFR measurements may capture different aspects of disease biology and are not necessarily expected to correlate directly.

At the molecular level, EGFR leads to the phosphorylation of effector proteins and activation of signaling pathways that regulate cell growth, proliferation, and survival.6,26 The EGFR shows a so-called “agonistic bias,” which means that ligands with different affinities induce the activation of different downstream signaling pathways. The low affinity-ligand amphiregulin induces the activation of TGF-β,27 whereas the high-affinity ligand HB-EGF activates the intracellular inhibitor of TGF-β-signaling such as transforming growth-interacting factor; in this way preventing the differentiation of pericytes into myofibroblasts.28 In survival analysis of the present study, high levels EGF were associated with a reduced risk of mortality, whereas TGF-α tended to be associated with an increased risk. These findings suggest that the beneficial or detrimental effects of EGFR signaling may depend on the specific ligands involved rather than EGFR alone. It should be also noted that the EGFR levels measured in BAL or serum represent soluble form of EGFR (sEGFR), not mrembrane bound EGFR (mEGFR), that has a decoy funtion of EGFR signaling through sequestering the EGFR ligands including EGF, AREG and TGF-a. In other words, the higher levels of sEGFR may negate the EGF ligands effect, provided a protective effect, but they do not exclude the pathophysiologic contribution, positive but not proteictive, of various EGFR ligands.

Our investigation has several limitations. First, the lung microenvironment undergoes significant tissue remodeling in IPF, which cannot be fully understood through BAL fluid analyses. Secondly, we did not measure the levels of other EGFR variants, such as erbB-3 and -4 that are structurally similar to the EGFR. The family includes Her1 (EGFR and ErbB1), Her2 (ErbB2), Her3 (ErbB3), and Her4 (ErbB4). EGF, TGF-α, and HB-EGF are high-affinity ligands for EGFR, whereas AREG is a low-affinity ligand, binding 10–100 times less strongly than the high-affinity ligands. Group two consist of two neuregulins that can bind to erbB-3 and -4. Group three consists of HB-EGF, betacellulin and epiregulin which can bind to both EGFR and erbB-4. In addition, EGFR has the ability to form dimeric structures with erbB-3 and -4.29,30 Thirdly, there was a small number of patients with other ILDs, including NSIP, and HP to evaluate their diagnostic significance. Fourthly, the small sample size of IPF limited the statistical robustness in clustering and identifying subtypes. The clustering analysis also resulted in imbalanced subgroup sizes, and particularly the very small number of patients in Cluster 3 (n = 7) reduces the robustness and generalizability of its associated mortality outcomes. Therefore, validation in larger, multi-center cohorts is required to confirm these findings. Furthermore, the cut-off values derived from ROC analysis were not validated in an independent cohort, which limits the generalizability of these thresholds. Although our findings suggest a ligand-specific signaling bias (“agonistic bias”), particularly the protective association of EGF–EGFR activation, no direct mechanistic evidence was provided. Therefore, additional experimental studies are required to clarify how EGF–EGFR signaling may exert anti-fibrotic and survival benefits.Finally, our study was conducted at a single center. Multi-center cohort studies are warranted to recruit larger numbers of participants for further investigation. Lastly, the absence of time-kinetic evaluation in this retrospective and cross-sectional study represents an important limitation. The levels of EGFR and its ligands may vary significantly during the clinical course of disease progression. Therefore, the timing of sample collection may influence the observed associations between mediator levels and clinical outcomes, including mortality. Prospective longitudinal studies are warranted to better define these temporal relationships.

In conclusion, EGFR and its ligands in the lung are closely related to the clinical outcomes of IPF in an interactive manner. Considering the interactions between EGFR and its ligand, it is important to infer their levels together when predicting the prognosis of IPF. This data may be used to develop molecular endotypes by analyzing the levels of EGFR and its ligands, allowing for customized individual therapy.

Optimization of pharmacy membership management system based on big data: Sleeping member activation and awakening methods using ANN modeling
Authors: Jing Liang, Xin Zhou, Chong Yuan, Yong Chen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn the retail industry, effective management of memberships is crucial, particularly within the pharmaceutical sector, as it fosters customer loyalty and drives sales growth. However, pharmacies often face challenges related to membership attrition and inactive members, which restrict the full potential of their membership programs. This research aims to address these challenges by optimizing pharmacy membership management systems through the utilization of big data technology. By leveraging the power of big data and employing machine learning algorithms, this study examines member data from multiple prominent pharmacy chains. The findings demonstrate the effectiveness of this approach in significantly increasing the level of activity among inactive memberships. Furthermore, this research unveils significant behavioral patterns among pharmacy members, shedding light on their preferences, purchasing habits, and interaction patterns. In this study, an artificial neural network (ANN) is employed to predict reactivation success rates, membership activity, and sales revenue based on website/app usage and member engagement. Two input factors, namely the frequency of website/app usage and member engagement score, are evaluated alongside three output factors: reactivation success rate, increase in membership activity levels, and increase in overall sales and revenue. Tailoring strategies based on member profiles and preferences enables pharmacies to re-engage customers and cultivate renewed loyalty. Importantly, these efforts yield positive impacts beyond membership activity, influencing overall sales and revenue generation for pharmacies. The ANN analysis reveals significant correlations and acceptable prediction errors. The insights gained from this study offer valuable information for enhancing membership management strategies and adjusting marketing efforts to cater to the specific needs and expectations of diverse customer segments. The practical, data-driven approach presented in this study equips pharmacies with the means to activate and re-engage dormant members. By harnessing the potential of big data technology and leveraging machine learning algorithms, pharmacies can optimize their membership management systems, enhance customer engagement, and improve their overall retail operations. This research underscores the significance of leveraging data-driven insights in the retail industry and showcases the transformative capabilities of big data technology in enhancing customer relationship management practices.Keywords: Membership management, Pharmacy sector, Big data technology, Dormant members, Customer loyalty, Retail operations

The choice of algorithms, including the shallow artificial neural network (ANN), logistic regression, random forest, and gradient boosting decision trees, was based on their proven effectiveness in addressing similar challenges in customer behavior modeling and membership management optimization. The ANN approach was selected to capture the non-linear relationships between the input factors (website/app usage frequency and member engagement score) and the desired output metrics (reactivation success rate, membership activity level, and sales/revenue). The ANN architecture comprised a single hidden layer with five neurons, a configuration determined based on the common guideline of using a number of hidden neurons equal to two times the number of input variables plus one, with an additional neuron to further expedite the convergence of the network. To optimize the ANN's performance, we conducted a comprehensive hyperparameter tuning process using a grid search approach, with the optimal hyperparameter configuration selected based on the minimization of the error function and maximization of predictive accuracy on the validation set. To assess the ANN's generalization ability, we employed a 10-fold cross-validation technique during the training process, and the final model was tested on a held-out test set to further validate its ability to make accurate predictions. To further demonstrate the appropriateness of the ANN, we benchmarked its performance against other machine learning algorithms, and the ANN exhibited superior predictive accuracy, as evidenced by higher scores on metrics such as precision, recall, F1-score, and area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC-AUC) curve.

Data preprocessing

To ensure the integrity and reliability of the dataset, we conducted a thorough preprocessing of the raw data. This involved imputing missing values using attribute means or medians to maintain data integrity [[31], [32], [33], [34], [35]]. Data integrity was further ensured by removing duplicate records and converting categorical attributes to numeric format. Collectively, these detailed preprocessing techniques cleaned the data by addressing quality issues, standardized the format, and laid the groundwork for robust analysis by providing a complete, consistent and error-free dataset. This comprehensive preprocessing approach enhances the validity, generalizability and reproducibility of any subsequent findings.

Feature extraction

In order to develop an efficient predictive model, we conducted robust feature extraction from the preprocessed transaction data. Specifically, we leveraged the classic RFM framework which extracted recency, frequency and monetary value features to differentiate member groups' purchase behaviors. Recency captured the number of days since the last purchase, frequency represented the number of purchases in the specified period, and monetary value reflected the total transaction amount [[25], [26], [27], [28], [29]]. These RFM attributes were calculated according to established methods to quantify members' purchase habits and lifetime values. Additionally, we extracted the average transaction interval and standard deviation of transaction amounts to gauge members' purchase consistency over time. A purchase diversity index was also derived to represent the variety of product categories purchased, yielding insight into members’ preferences. All continuous features were then normalized to ensure the same scaling for modeling. These extracted attributes not only summarized core purchasing traits but were also input into the predictive model after rigorous selection based on explanatory power. By systematically distilling meaningful patterns through statistical techniques, our feature engineering facilitated subsequent analysis and enabled more accurate profiling of targeted customer segments [[28], [29], [30], [31], [32]]. This systematic approach enhanced the interpretable knowledge gained about member behavior.

Analytical method

To gain a holistic understanding of member behavior and effectively implement targeted marketing, we conducted a comprehensive analysis leveraging clustering, regression, and ensemble learning algorithms. We first applied K-means clustering on RFM scores to efficiently segment members into active, general, and sleeping groups for customized strategy development. Additionally, logistic regression predicted the probability of future member activity providing a quantitative basis for decisions. Simultaneously, random forest evaluated feature importance for activity prediction, improving generalization through ensemble techniques. Furthermore, gradient boosted decision trees continuously optimized prediction accuracy and stability by correcting errors from prior iterations. These combined techniques offered multi-faceted behavioral insights through clustering, introduced probabilistic decision support via regression, and enhanced robustness using multiple ensemble algorithms. The models segment members, forecast activity likelihood, identify predictive traits, and rectify one another to refine outcomes. Their results directly guide designing differentiated communication tailored to each segment's needs, preferences and lifecycle stage. This triangulation of clustering, regression and ensemble methods bolsters reliability and draws nuanced conclusions to more effectively activate and re-engage customers.

Training and validation

To ensure generalization to unseen data, the dataset was randomly partitioned into 70 % for training and 30 % for testing. The training set enabled models to learn purchase behavioral patterns. During training, a 10-fold cross-validation technique was applied to optimize hyperparameters. Specifically, the training set was divided into 10 equally sized subsets, with models iteratively trained on 9 folds for testing on the held-out 10 % subset. This process was repeated 10 times such that each subset was used once for validation. Final validation performance reflected the average of these iterations, curbing potential overfitting where models overly fit to nuances in the training data and fail to generalize. This validation scheme identified and corrected such issues to produce robust, stable models applicable beyond the training data. The aim was developing practitioner-applicable models to design personalized strategies improving pharmacy member management system effectiveness and efficiency. Training on varied data and validating via holdout and k-fold cross-validation techniques ensured model accuracy and reliability when deployed to activate and re-engage unseen customers in real-world dynamic environments, maintaining predictive power far beyond the original model building sample.

Model evaluation and optimization

A rigorous quantitative evaluation assessed the model's accuracy, sensitivity and generalizability through established classification metrics. Precision captured the model's ability to produce more true positives relative to false positives, reflecting the accuracy of its activity predictions. Meanwhile, recall measured the rate of truly active members correctly identified, demonstrating predictive sensitivity. These were balanced via the F1-score. Additionally, ROC-AUC evaluated classification performance across variable thresholds, gauging discrimination ability. To ensure robustness on current and future data, extensive hyperparameter optimization via grid search identified parameter combinations maximizing these metrics. Meanwhile, iterative feature selection determined the most predictive traits through ablation tests, improving accuracy while simplifying interpretability. These efforts enhanced adaptability to dynamic real-world environments through boosted robustness. Comprehensive performance benchmarking with metrics like precision, recall, F1-score and ROC-AUC validated the model's strengths, and hyperparameter tuning and feature selection strengthened generalization. Altogether, a rigorous optimization and evaluation protocol was followed to develop an accurate, transparent and practitioner-applicable model for personalizing member engagement strategies.

Predictive model evaluation

To comprehensively assess model performance on the test set, we evaluated multiple metrics including precision, recall, F1 score, and area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (ROC-AUC). Table 2 shows these results, revealing the gradient boosting decision tree (GBDT) outperformed logistic regression and random forest across all measures.

Particularly noteworthy was GBDT's ROC-AUC of 0.92, significantly higher than the other models. The AUC evaluates a model's ability to distinguish between classes, with values closer to 1 indicating excellent discriminative power. GBDT's exceptional 0.92 AUC demonstrated near-perfect separation of potentially active versus inactive members, underscoring its high predictive accuracy. Precision reflects the proportion of positive predictions that were indeed positive, gauging exactness. GBDT achieved the top precision of 0.86, implying most members it predicted as potentially active were accurately classified as such. Recall considers the percentage of actual positive instances captured by the model, representing completeness. GBDT also exhibited the highest recall at 0.80, correctly identifying the vast majority of truly active members. The F1-score balances precision and recall to evaluate overall classification effectiveness, with GBDT attaining the maximum F1 of 0.83. These comprehensive quantitative results validate GBDT as the top-performing algorithm, synthesizing member data into highly informative predictions of future behavior. Its performance advantages stemmed from ensemble techniques iteratively correcting errors and improving estimates. GBDT exhibited near-perfect discrimination between active and inactive customers through the exceptional 0.92 AUC. Simultaneously, precision, recall and F1-score affirmed GBDT most accurately and completely identified positive classes, confirming its suitability for precision membership targeting. These findings provided robust empirical support for selecting GBDT to power the pharmacy member management system. Its high accuracy predictions reliably guided optimized activation strategies for maximum responsiveness. Discussion

Big data technologies enable pharmacies to gain deep insights into customer preferences, behaviors, and engagement patterns by analyzing large datasets. Studies have utilized techniques like clustering, predictive modeling, and personalization to identify important characteristics of inactive members, including prolonged periods of inactivity [[30], [31], [32], [33]]. Frameworks like Aligning Distillation address the challenges of starting from scratch by transferring behavioral information from popular to new items. Machine learning models, validated using metrics such as accuracy and AUC, have effectively predicted member properties and activities [34,35]. Insights from multiple drugstore chains have uncovered dormant customer profiles. Pharmacies can then implement personalized strategies by targeting dormant members through customized communication and incentives based on their unique traits. This optimization of membership programs aims to reactivate inactive customers and enhance loyalty through improved customer experiences. Advanced analytics thus help address retention challenges and improve sales and operational efficiency through precise targeting. Membership management in the pharmacy sector has been revolutionized by the integration of big data technology. This innovative approach leverages the vast amounts of data generated within pharmacy systems to optimize membership programs, enhance customer experiences, and drive business growth. By harnessing big data technology, pharmacies gain the ability to collect, store, and analyze large volumes of structured and unstructured data. This includes information such as customer demographics, purchase histories, medication records, and interaction patterns. The analysis of this data provides valuable insights into customer preferences, behaviors, and trends, enabling pharmacies to tailor their membership programs to meet individual needs.

One key advantage of big data technology in membership management is the ability to create personalized experiences for members. By analyzing individual data profiles, pharmacies can offer customized product recommendations, targeted promotions, and personalized communications. This level of personalization enhances member engagement and satisfaction, ultimately fostering long-term loyalty. Moreover, big data technology enables pharmacies to identify and segment member groups based on various criteria such as age, health conditions, or medication usage. This segmentation allows for the development of targeted marketing campaigns, tailored services, and specialized offerings that cater to the specific needs of each group. By delivering relevant and meaningful experiences, pharmacies can strengthen their relationships with members and differentiate themselves in the competitive market. Additionally, big data technology facilitates predictive analytics, empowering pharmacies to anticipate member needs and behaviors. Through advanced algorithms and machine learning models, pharmacies can forecast future trends, identify potential churn risks, and proactively engage with members before they become inactive. This proactive approach improves member retention rates and enables pharmacies to implement timely interventions to prevent attrition. Furthermore, big data technology enhances operational efficiency in membership management. By automating data collection, integration, and analysis processes, pharmacies can streamline administrative tasks, reduce manual errors, and allocate resources more effectively. This efficiency allows pharmacists and staff to focus on delivering high-quality care and personalized services to members, ultimately improving overall customer satisfaction.

Dormant members often require tailored incentives and personalized communication to rekindle their interest and encourage them to resume their purchasing activity. By implementing the arousal strategy, pharmacies demonstrate their commitment to understanding the unique needs and preferences of their members [[28], [29], [30], [31]]. This proactive approach not only fosters member loyalty but also increases the likelihood of continued engagement and repurchases. The awakening strategy leverages the power of big data and AI technology to identify dormant members and deliver targeted wake-up messages. By analyzing the extensive transaction history database, pharmacies can gain valuable insights into members' purchasing behavior and engagement patterns. Machine learning algorithms enable the identification of key indicators that signal dormancy, such as prolonged periods of inactivity and decreased purchasing frequency [[32], [33], [34]]. Based on these insights, personalized wake-up messages are crafted and delivered to the identified dormant members, enticing them with tailored offers and incentives. The success of the awakening strategy can be attributed to several factors. Firstly, the personalized nature of the wake-up messages creates a sense of exclusivity and relevance, capturing the attention of dormant members and igniting their interest in re-engaging with the pharmacy. Secondly, the strategy capitalizes on the power of timing by delivering the wake-up messages at opportune moments when members are more likely to respond positively [[33], [34], [35]].

The key results underscore the transformative potential of data-driven approaches in enhancing customer engagement, reinvigorating dormant members, and driving overall business performance for pharmacies.

Improving tractography in peri-lesional oedema using constrained spherical deconvolution in paediatric neurosurgery patients
Authors: Joseph Yuan-Mou Yang, Oscar Alateras, Jacques-Donald Tournier, Sila Genc
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Tractography guides neurosurgical planning by balancing lesion resection with functional preservation. A common challenge to this approach is that some lesions have accompanying oedema, which can obscure white matter (WM) fibre orientation through increased isotropic diffusion resulting in diffusion-weighted signal properties resembling grey matter (GM) and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF). Constrained spherical deconvolution (CSD) can improve tract reconstruction in complex environments compared to diffusion tensor approaches. We assessed four CSD methods for improving tractography accuracy in peri-lesional oedema. We retrospectively analysed 14 paediatric neurosurgical patients (2.1–19 years of age) with brain tumours or epilepsy-associated lesions exhibiting peri-lesional oedema. Multi-shell diffusion-weighted imaging data acquired at 3T were analysed using two single-tissue models (1-tissue CSD and 1-tissue CSD with free-water correction) and two multi-tissue models (3-tissue [WM, GM, CSF] and 2-tissue [WM, CSF] MSMT-CSD). Probabilistic tractography was performed within a combined white matter and peri-lesional oedema mask. Streamline count, length, and tract volume were compared using linear mixed effects models. The volume of peri-lesional oedema ranged from 3,046 to 132,644 mm³ (average: 49,499 mm³). Applying free-water correction to DWI data did not increase the average streamline count (p = .04) but was associated with shorter streamlines and reduced tract volume (p ≤ .006) compared with standard 1-tissue CSD. For multi-tissue models, 2-tissue MSMT-CSD produced higher average streamline count and length, and proportion of tracts within oedematous regions compared to 3-tissue MSMT-CSD (p ≤ .008). Our findings demonstrate: (1) two-tissue MSMT-CSD reconstructs more and longer streamlines and larger tract volumes within peri-lesional oedema compared to three-tissue MSMT-CSD, and (2) free-water correction with single-tissue CSD provides no improvement in tract reconstruction. We determine that the two-tissue MSMT-CSD approach shows promise for improving surgical planning in cases with peri-lesional oedema.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00429-026-03108-6.

Introduction

Brain surgery remains the cornerstone of treatment for paediatric brain tumours and epilepsy. Extent of lesion resection is a critical determinant of both tumour-related survival and seizure outcomes (Brown et al., 2016; Capelle et al. 2013; Cossu et al. 2008; Rowland et al. 2012). Achieving maximal resection is particularly challenging when pathology involves eloquent cortex and their associated white matter tracts, where the risk of surgical injury related functional deficits constrains the extent of resection (Duffau 2005). Careful presurgical planning is essential to balance maximal lesion resection with preservation of neurological function (De Witt Hamer et al. 2012; Duffau 2005; Duffau and Mandonnet 2013).

Tractography, a technique that utilizes diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) to delineate white matter tracts in vivo, supports surgical planning by mapping critical white matter tracts that might be affected, enabling neurosurgeons to identify safe resection routes and reduce the risk of functional impairment. This non-invasive method is especially beneficial for guiding resection near eloquent regions, enabling a more targeted approach that helps preserve neurological function (Kamagata et al. 2023; Yang et al. 2021). Survey studies further emphasise the value of tractography, with neurosurgical experts frequently endorsing it as an essential tool for preoperative planning in complex cases (Sharma et al. 2023; Toescu et al. 2020; Vergani et al. 2025).

While tractography is a valuable tool for surgical planning, some brain lesions are associated with peri-lesional oedema - an accumulation of fluid that can alter the surrounding white matter environment. This oedema increases isotropic diffusion due to the presence of free water, obscuring directional specificity and posing challenges to traditional diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) approaches in identifying the primary white matter fibre orientation (Parker et al. 2020). Consequently, DTI-based tractography often results in prematurely terminated tracts or incomplete reconstructions, particularly in regions where the diffusion signal arises from both white matter and surrounding fluid (Kamagata et al. 2021; Mormina et al. 2015).

To address the challenges posed by oedema, free-water correction (FWC) methods have been developed to estimate and separate extracellular free water from tissue signal within each voxel (Pasternak et al. 2009; Parker et al. 2020; Chong et al. 2021), reducing the impact of oedema on tractography estimates near pathological brain tissue such as tumours (Kamagata et al. 2023). Combining such FWC techniques with both DTI and dual-tensor based tractography has been shown to enhance tract volume and streamline count in oedematous regions (Gong et al. 2018; Henderson et al. 2021). Moreover, expert assessments suggest that FWC provides a more anatomically accurate representation of white matter tracts by reducing false positive streamlines and improving overall tract reconstruction quality (Gong et al. 2018). However, there is limited evidence on the efficacy of FWC approaches in higher-order models such as constrained spherical deconvolution (CSD), particularly in clinical and paediatric datasets.

The original CSD formulation (Tournier et al. 2007) was developed based on single-shell diffusion data (using the highest b-value by default when presented with multi-shell data) to estimate white matter (WM) fibre orientation distributions (FODs), while implicitly assuming single tissue with negligible contributions from other tissue types. While this assumption is reasonable in healthy WM, it can break down in regions affected by partial-volume effects or in and around pathology. Multi-shell multi-tissue CSD (MSMT-CSD) (Jeurissen et al. 2014) was subsequently introduced to exploit multi-shell acquisitions by explicitly modelling multiple tissue signal components, most commonly WM, grey matter (GM), and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF). This approach estimates a response function for each tissue type, representing the expected diffusion signal per tissue unit volume, as a function of b-value and orientation. By leveraging the distinct diffusion signatures of these tissues across b-values, MSMT-CSD estimates their relative signal fractions alongside the WM FODs. The MSMT-CSD framework places no conceptual restriction on the number or identity of tissue types included, provided their response functions are sufficiently distinct and supported by the acquisition. In pathological tissue, such as oedema, similarities between WM- and GM-like diffusion responses may lead to signal misassignment, motivating the use of simplified tissue models (e.g., WM and CSF only) as an alternative strategy.

CSD-based tractography approaches have demonstrated significant improvements over tensor-based tractography in complex neuroanatomical environments, including regions with crossing fibres and partial volume effects (Roine et al. 2014; Tournier et al. 2008). A key advantage of CSD is the ability to reconstruct anatomically accurate white matter tracts while addressing the challenges posed by complex fibre configurations and oedematous tissue (Farquharson et al. 2013). CSD has the potential to improve tractography estimates in oedema as it typically relies on the high b-value signal, where the contribution from oedema is effectively negligible (Genc et al. 2020). Furthermore, MSMT-CSD explicitly incorporates a cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) tissue-type, which separates the contribution of isotropic diffusion from fibre orientation distribution estimates, enabling more precise white matter fibre estimation in complex diffusion environments. However, variations in CSD approaches have yet to be systematically evaluated in neurosurgical cases with peri-lesional oedema.

The aim of the current study was to test the performance of four different CSD approaches in effectively tracking through peri-lesional oedema in paediatric neuro-oncology and epilepsy surgery patients. First, we evaluated two single-tissue models: single-tissue CSD, and a modified version that incorporates free-water correction (FWC) to the input DWI data. Second, we evaluated two multi-tissue models: 3-tissue MSMT-CSD, and a simplified 2-tissue model, which performs MSMT-CSD on the WM and CSF tissue-types only. For each method we report the primary outcome as the proportion of oedematous tissue occupied by white matter tracts to make clinically meaningful comparisons.

Methods

Patient characteristics

This retrospective study included 14 paediatric neurosurgical patients, 12 with histopathologically confirmed low-grade or high-grade brain tumours and two patients who had Long-Term Epilepsy Associated Tumours (LEATs). Patients were selected as they exhibited peri-lesional white matter oedema on their MRI and had multi-shell diffusion MRI data acquired at The Royal Children’s Hospital between 2017 and 2023. The cohort consisted of 10 males and 4 females, with an age range of 2.1 to 19 years (mean age = 11.01, SD = 4.35). There were two primary radiological imaging patterns for the lesions among the patients: 10 cases had a discrete solid or solid-cystic lesion with surrounding peri-lesional oedema and four cases had a diffuse, infiltrative lesion and intra-lesional oedema identified by regions of FLAIR hyperintensity. MRI acquisition

All patients underwent a preoperative brain scan on a 3T MRI scanner (Siemens, Erlangen). T1-weighted images were collected using the magnetization-prepared gradient echo (MPRAGE) sequence with and without Gadolinium contrast with a voxel resolution of 0.8–1.0 mm. FLAIR images were also acquired with an in-plane resolution of 0.5 mm and a slice thickness of 0.9 mm. Additional sequences included 2D T2-weighted Turbo Spin Echo images in selected orthogonal planes (0.5 mm in-plane resolution, 2.5 to 4 mm slice thickness).

For nine patients, multi-shell Diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) data were collected on a 3T Prisma MRI, with an acquisition matrix of 110 × 110, b-values of 3000 s/mm² (60 directions), 1000 s/mm² (30 directions), and 0 s/mm² (11 volumes). Voxel size = 2.3 × 2.3 × 2.3 mm, TR = 3500 ms, TE = 77 ms, total acquisition time = 6.5 min. For the remaining 5 patients, DWI data were acquired using a similar multi-shell protocol with slight variations in TE/TR (see Supplementary Materials).

Image segmentation

For patients with discrete solitary or solid-cystic lesions, both the lesion and peri-lesional oedema segmentation were performed manually by an experienced neurosurgery research fellow (author JYMY) using 3D T1-weighted images as the primary anatomical reference. To enhance the precision of the segmentation, additional structural MRI sequences, including 3D T1-weighted images with Gadolinium contrast, T2-weighted images, and FLAIR, were overlaid on the T1-weighted images. Peri-lesional oedema was identified based on T2/FLAIR hyperintensity, T1 hypointensity, and a “T2 shine-through” effect on DWI and ADC maps. Segmentation of oedema began with intensity thresholding applied to the FLAIR images to highlight hyperintense regions, which were then manually refined using information from the other MRI sequences to ensure segmentation accuracy. In patients with diffuse, infiltrative lesions, the FLAIR hyperintensity in these cases was attributed to a combination of tissue oedema and microscopic tumour infiltration, within the tumour. An intensity threshold was applied to isolate these hyperintense regions, segmenting regions of “intra-lesional FLAIR hyperintensity.” From this point onward, both the oedema and the intra-lesional hyperintensity masks will be referred to as the “oedema mask.”

Separately, a white matter mask was generated for each participant using FSL (FAST; Smith et al. 2004). The white matter mask was then combined with the oedema mask to create the final “combined mask,” tailored to each patient’s lesion type. For patients with discrete lesions, the lesion mask was subtracted from the combined mask to exclude the lesion from tractography. For infiltrative lesions, the combined mask included both the oedema and the white matter masks, with no lesion exclusion, reflecting the infiltrative nature of these lesions. DWI processing and analysis

All DWI data processing and tractography reconstruction (Fig. 2) were performed using MRtrix3 (version 3.0_RC3; Tournier et al. 2019) interfacing various tools in FSL (Smith, 2004) and ANTS (Avants, 2009). Pre-processing steps included denoising (Veraart et al. 2016), correction for Gibbs-ringing artefacts (Kellner et al. 2015), eddy currents and motion (Andersson and Sotiropoulos 2015), and susceptibility-induced distortions (Andersson et al. 2003). Bias field inhomogeneities were corrected using the N4 algorithm (Zhang et al. 2001), and brain masks were generated with the Brain Extraction Tool in FSL (Smith, 2004). T1-weighted images were co-registered to the diffusion data using ANTs with six degrees of freedom (Avants, 2009; Jenkinson 2002; Jenkinson and Smith 2001) to ensure precise alignment for tractography analysis.

To evaluate the effectiveness of different CSD approaches in the presence of peri-tumoral oedema, we evaluated two single-tissue and two multi-tissue approaches to identify the most appropriate tractography method. Figure 2 summarises the different methods sampled.

Single-tissue CSD

Approach (1) employed single-tissue CSD, hereafter referred to as 1-tissue CSD, in which a single anisotropic response function representing white matter (WM) was estimated directly from the DWI data using an iterative procedure (Tournier et al. 2004). This response was then used to compute WM fibre orientation distributions (FODs across the whole brain (Tournier et al. 2007).

Approach (2) implemented a modified version of Approach (1) incorporating a form of free-water correction (FWC) applied to the input DWI data, termed 1-tissue CSD with FWC. In this approach, a 3-tissue MSMT-CSD model was first fitted to estimate the relative signal fractions of WM, grey matter (GM), and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) (Jeurissen et al. 2014; Dhollander et al., 2016). The estimated CSF fraction was then used to forward-model the predicted CSF diffusion signal via spherical convolution with an isotropic CSF response function, which was subtracted from the original DWI data to generate a free-water-corrected image series. WM FODs were subsequently estimated from these corrected data using single-tissue CSD, resulting in FODs with the CSF contribution removed.

Multi-tissue CSD

Approach (3) involved the standard multi-shell multi-tissue CSD (MSMT-CSD; Jeurissen 2014), referred herein as “3-tissue MSMT-CSD”. Three tissue-based response functions were estimated in three tissue types, an anisotropic response for the white matter (WM), and two isotropic response functions for grey matter (GM) and CSF (Dhollander et al., 2016). These three tissue responses were used to generate white matter FODs using MSMT-CSD (Jeurissen et al. 2014).

Approach (4) modelled only the WM and CSF tissue-types referred to as “2-tissue MSMT-CSD”. This adaptation aimed to enhance FOD reconstruction in oedematous regions by removing the possibility of the algorithm confusing oedematous white matter for grey matter during the fitting procedure – an issue that can otherwise be problematic due to the similarity in the b-value decay curves for these two tissue types (Aerts et al., 2019).

Tractography

Probabilistic whole-brain fibre tractography was performed on the CSD-based white matter FOD using iFOD2 using 10 million streamlines. Tractography was seeded within the combined mask to focus analysis on the peri-lesional oedematous region, while excluding the lesion where applicable (Fig S1). An FOD amplitude threshold of 0.06 was used to avoid the inclusion of noisy FODs likely unrelated to genuine white matter tracts. To enhance the accuracy of the tractography results, Spherical-deconvolution Informed Filtering of Tractograms (SIFT) was applied to ensure that streamline densities were proportional to fibre densities (Smith et al., 2013). Whole-brain tractograms were then filtered to retain only those streamlines passing through each participant’s oedema mask.

Analysis

To compare the different tractography approaches, we quantified streamline count and mean streamline length within the oedematous region. Track Density Imaging (TDI) maps were generated from these filtered tracts (Calamante et al. 2011), allowing us to compute the volume of successfully reconstructed tracts occupying the region of oedema. Linear mixed-effects models were used to evaluate differences in tractography-derived outputs in both single-tissue and multi-tissue approaches. Evidence for an association was deemed statistically significant at p < .01.

Results

The average volume of peri-lesional oedema was 49,499 mm3 (range, 3,046–132,644 mm3; corresponding to 0.16–8.4% of total intracranial volume following normalisation). Linear mixed-effects models (random intercept for participant; adjusted for age) were used to compare tractography metrics within the perilesional oedematous region (Table S1).

Single-tissue CSD

Applying free-water correction (FWC) to the input DWI data resulted in significantly lower tractography metrics within the perilesional oedematous region compared with standard single-tissue CSD (Fig. 3a). Streamline count showed a relative decrease from M = 336,761 (SD = 265,528) to M = 321,506 (SD = 255,030), however this was not deemed statistically significant, β = −15,255, SE = 6,699, t(12) = − 2.28, p = .042.

Average streamline length decreased from 14.07 mm (SD = 4.4) to 13.4 mm (SD = 4.3), which was statistically significant (β = -0.70, SE = 0.17, t(12) = -4.22, p = .001). Finally, the proportion of tract volume within the perilesional oedematous region was significantly lower following FWC (M = 0.87, SD = 0.13 vs. 0.82, SD = 0.14), β = -0.048, SE = 0.014, t(12) = -3.32, p = .006.

Multi-tissue CSD

The 2-tissue MSMT-CSD approach yielded greater tractography metrics than 3-tissue MSMT-CSD across all measures (Fig. 3b). Streamline count was greater in the 2-tissue model (M = 384,139, SD = 304,639) compared with the 3-tissue model (M = 265,006, SD = 239,119), β = -119,100, SE = 45,190, t = -2.64, p = .008. Of the patients studied here, patients 2, 9, 11 and 14 exhibited large increases in streamline count between the two MSMT-CSD methods, and patients 10 and 8 exhibited moderate increases in streamline count. The commonalities between these patients are that the majority were diffuse infiltrative tumours.

Average streamline length was longer using 2-tissue MSMT-CSD (M = 16.8 mm, SD = 6.8) compared with 3-tissue MSMT-CSD (M = 10.1 mm, SD = 2.6), β = -6.79, SE = 1.72, t(12) = -3.95, p = .002. The proportion of tract volume within the perilesional oedematous region was markedly higher with 2-tissue MSMT-CSD (M = 0.93, SD = 0.09) compared with 3-tissue MSMT-CSD (M = 0.48, SD = 0.27), reaching statistical significance (β = -0.447, SE = 0.071, t(12) = -6.26, p < .001).

Three-group comparison (two-shell approaches)

A three-level mixed-effects model directly compared standard 1-tissue CSD, 1-tissue CSD-FWC, and 2-tissue MSMT-CSD (reference = standard 1-tissue CSD) (Fig S2). Relative to standard 1-tissue CSD, the 2-tissue model showed significantly higher streamline count (β = 47,378, SE = 16,958, p = .010), longer streamline length (β = 2.78, SE = 0.68, p < .001), and greater volume proportion (β = 0.055, SE = 0.018, p = .004). In contrast, 1-tissue CSD-FWC did not differ from standard 1-tissue CSD in streamline count, length, or tract volume proportion. This underscores the large improvements of 2-tissue MSMT-CSD compared with 1-tissue approaches.

This three-level analysis is particularly informative because all models were derived from the same two-shell dataset, thereby avoiding differences attributable to acquisition scheme. Overall, when restricted to models that can be implemented on standard single-shell acquisitions, 2-tissue MSMT-CSD consistently yielded higher tractography metrics within oedematous tissue, whereas applying free-water correction to DWI data prior to 1-tissue CSD resulted in systematic reductions.

A representative patient (#9) with a discrete solid meningioma is shown in Fig. 4, revealing a large tumour mass on T1 (Fig. 4A) and accompanying peri-tumoural oedema on FLAIR (Fig. 4B). Tissue-volume fraction maps obtained from multi-shell multi-tissue CSD revealed the relative contributions of WM, GM and CSF to the signal (Fig. 4). The MSMT-CSD 3-tissue fraction maps revealed a mixture of “grey-matter like” and “CSF-like” signal (Fig. 4C). In these regions, misassignment of the tissue-specific response redistributed signal away from the WM, resulting in WM fibre orientation distributions (FODs) being incorrectly classified as GM or CSF and excluded from tractography. This reduction in WM FOD amplitude led to absent tracts at the anterior and posterior margins of the tumour (Fig. 4D).

In contrast, the 2-tissue MSMT-CSD approach, which does not include an explicit GM tissue-type, decomposed the same oedematous region into a dominant CSF-like component with a residual WM contribution. Although the WM fraction is visually subtle in the tissue fraction maps, the corresponding WM FOD amplitude remained above the tractography threshold, permitting a greater number of streamlines to be reconstructed through oedema. This resulted in improved delineation of major white matter pathways, including the arcuate fasciculus, inferior longitudinal fasciculus, inferior fronto-occipital fasciculus, and optic radiation (Fig. 5 and S3).

Discussion

The aim of this study was to evaluate the performance of four different CSD approaches in modelling white matter fibre orientations and delineating white matter tracts within peri-lesional oedema in a sample of children with brain tumours and long-term epilepsy associated tumours. Our study has two main findings: (1) two-tissue MSMT-CSD successfully reconstructed longer streamlines and larger tract volumes within peri-lesional oedema, compared with three-tissue MSMT-CSD; and (2) the addition of free-water correction to single-tissue CSD did not improve tract reconstruction.

In the first part of this study, we found that applying FWC to DWI data prior to single-tissue CSD produced fewer and shorter streamlines within the oedema relative to the standard single-tissue CSD. This was somewhat unexpected, as prior peri-tumoural oedema tractography studies have reported improved tracking performance following FWC (Chong et al. 2021; Gong et al. 2018; Henderson et al. 2021; Lecoeur et al. 2013, 2014). However, several fundamental methodological differences distinguish those studies from the present work.

First, previous investigations were DTI-based with tensors being estimated from lower b-value data (typically around b = 1000 s/mm²), whereas the current study applied single-tissue CSD modelling to higher b-value data (b = 3000 s/mm²). Tensor models are typically normalised to the b = 0 signal, which is strongly influenced by extracellular free water. In contrast, single-tissue CSD for FOD estimation relies on the high b-value signal only, which contains negligible contributions from the fast-diffusing extracellular free water signal, making it relatively insensitive to free water contamination (Tournier et al. 2013). As a result, while FWC can substantially improve tensor estimation by correcting for extracellular fluid effects, this offers limited benefit for FOD estimation with standard single-tissue CSD.

Second, the FWC approach used in this study involved removal of CSF-like signal within a multi-tissue CSD framework. The observed reduction in oedema tracking performance with FWC may reflect combination of relative insensitivity of CSD to extracellular free water signals and reduction in usable diffusion information after components classified as CSF-like are excluded. Our results suggest there is little to no improvement to be gained from applying FWC to the input DWI data prior to single-tissue CSD for peri-lesional oedema tractography in neurosurgical cases, particularly when using high b-values.

The significant improvements we observed in two-tissue MSMT-CSD compared with three-tissue MSMT-CSD can be explained by how each model handles “grey matter-like” signal within the oedematous regions differently. The three-tissue MSMT-CSD estimates FODs using three tissue response functions for grey matter, white matter, and CSF, based on diffusion signals (Dhollander et al. 2016; Jeurissen et al. 2014) - an approach which performs well in non-pathological tissue (Newman et al., 2020; Özdemir et al. 2024). However, in the presence of oedema, the diffusion signal within WM exhibits a faster b-value decay than expected for healthy WM (Aerts et al., 2019), becoming more similar to that of GM. This similarity in b-value decay behaviour leads the model to misattribute a portion of the WM signal to the GM. Importantly, the proportion of signal affected by this is inherently unpredictable in the presence of infiltrative pathology within oedematous WM, where overlapping microstructural processes exacerbate modelling uncertainty. This results in an overestimation of isotropic signal contributions, a reduction in apparent WM FOD amplitude, and ultimately degradation of tractography reconstruction in peri-lesional regions.

In contrast, the two-tissue MSMT-CSD model circumvented this issue by excluding the estimation of a GM response function, fitting the diffusion signal only to WM and CSF tissue responses, thereby enhancing FOD estimates and enabling more accurate tractography reconstructions in oedematous white matter regions. This was reflected by a greater proportion of peri-lesional oedematous volume being traversed by more numerous and longer tracking streamlines in the two-tissue MSMT-CSD model compared to the standard three-tissue model.

A limitation to our study is the small sample size, which is reflective of the number of paediatric neurosurgical patients with peri-lesional oedema that have diffusion MRI data acquired for surgical planning. Future research should validate our findings in a larger, more heterogenous cohort of children and adults with peri-lesional oedema. Our comparison of streamline count between the lesion-types suggested that the patients to most benefit from improved streamline reconstruction in oedematous white matter would be those with diffuse infiltrative lesions. This could be due to less oedema, or more likely due to improved reconstruction of longer-range streamlines that are not interrupted by a solid lesion.

We note, however, that increases in streamline count and tract volume within oedematous regions do not necessarily imply improved anatomical accuracy, as probabilistic tractography may also become more permissive in regions of reduced diffusion anisotropy. While the qualitative examples presented here support anatomical plausibility, the potential for false-positive streamlines cannot be excluded. Biologically, vasogenic oedema is expected to preserve underlying fibre architecture to some extent, albeit with reduced coherence, and therefore may support partial tract occupancy rather than complete tract absence. As such, the observed increases in streamline representation in oedematous regions should be interpreted as improved sensitivity to underlying fibre structure, but not definitive evidence of fully accurate reconstruction.

Future work capturing a larger population with greater heterogeneity in lesion-types would address any potential differences in the sampling of streamlines in infiltrative tumours due to differences in oedema boundary setting. Additionally, further work could evaluate how to integrate these outputs spanning both oedematous and non-oedematous regions. Finally, several emerging approaches have been proposed to enhance peri-lesional oedema tracking without relying on FWC (Filipiak et al. 2025; Leysen et al. 2025). Evaluating these newer techniques is beyond the scope of the present study; however, further work is needed to determine their potential added value for improving CSD-based tractography in peri-lesional oedema.

Overall, our study highlights the improvements to tractography within perilesional oedema by modifying standard multi-shell multi-tissue CSD approaches. By employing 2-tissue MSMT-CSD, we observed improvements through the generation of longer streamline reconstructions and larger tract volumes in oedematous tissue. This was reinforced with individual seed-based tractograms, emphasising that the approach generates realistic streamlines for clinical consideration. These promising results require validation in a larger cohort, with consideration to confirming streamlines traversing oedema reflect anatomically plausible tracts. This could be evaluated using functional MRI to assess concordance between tract projections and functionally activated cortex (Henderson et al. 2021), or by comparing tract terminations and locations with cortical and subcortical activation identified through intraoperative direct electrical stimulation (Radwan et al. 2024), the neurosurgical gold standard for functional localisation (De Witt Hamer et al. 2012). Such approaches would enable direct assessment of tracking accuracy. In addition, the impact of differences in tracking results on pre-surgical decision-making in patients with peri-lesional oedema should be systematically evaluated, for example, through structured surgeon surveys.

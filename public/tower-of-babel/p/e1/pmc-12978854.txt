Clinical response and prognosis of estrogen receptor-positive and human epidermal growth factor receptor-negative breast cancer patients after neoadjuvant chemotherapy: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Jin Ah Lee, Dooreh Kim, Young Joo Lee, Chang Ik Yoon, Woo-Chan Park, Soo Youn Bae
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeNeoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC) significantly revolutionized the management of locally advanced breast cancer, especially in human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-positive and triple-negative subtypes. However, its effectiveness is limited in estrogen receptor (ER)-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer. This study investigates the clinical response and prognosis of ER-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer after NAC.MethodsThe clinicopathological characteristics and treatment responses of 149 patients with ER-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer treated with NAC and surgery at The Catholic University of Korea, Seoul St. Mary’s Hospital between 2018 and 2023 were retrospectively analyzed. Pathologic complete response (pCR) was defined as the absence of invasive tumors (ypT0/is, ypN0). Disease-free survival (DFS) and overall survival (OS) were analyzed using Kaplan-Meier methods, stratified by age (≤50 years vs. >50 years).ResultsAmong 149 patients, 13 (8.7%) achieved pCR, 87 (58.4%) attained partial responses, 40 (26.8%) had stable disease, and 9 (6.0%) experienced progressive disease. RECIST responses differed significantly by age (P = 0.003). DFS (P = 0.011) and OS (P = 0.005) were significantly associated with clinical response in patients aged ≤50 years. Post-NAC Ki-67 was associated with DFS (P = 0.013) but not OS (P = 0.083) in patients aged ≤50 years. Clinical responses and post-NAC Ki-67 were not associated with DFS (P = 0.544) or OS (P = 0.569) in patients aged >50 years.ConclusionIn ER-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer, clinical responses and post-NAC Ki-67 were significant prognostic factors in patients aged ≤50 years but not in older patients. These findings highlight the need for tailored therapeutic approaches that consider age-specific prognostic differences.

INTRODUCTION

Breast cancer accounts for the largest portion of cancer cases worldwide [1]. Locally advanced breast cancer can be down-staged with neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC) regimens, allowing patients to undergo breast-conserving surgery (BCS) [2]. NAC elicits favorable responses in human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-positive and triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) subtypes; a high percentage of patients achieve a pathologic complete response (pCR). However, estrogen receptor (ER)-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer exhibits lower pCR rates and a limited response to chemotherapy compared to the HER2-positive breast cancer and TNBC subtypes [3]. The efficacy of NAC in patients with ER-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer is controversial because limited evidence links pCR to improved prognosis in this subtype. Thus, the role and relevance of pCR as a surrogate marker for prognosis in patients with ER-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer require further exploration.

Ki-67, a nuclear protein associated with cellular proliferation, is a prognostic marker for breast cancer outcomes. Lower Ki-67 levels correlate with better overall survival (OS), and higher Ki-67 levels are associated with a higher risk of recurrence and increased metastasis [4]. A previous study demonstrated that the pre-NAC Ki-67 level is an independent prognostic predictor of disease-free survival (DFS) and OS [5]. Another study showed that the extent of decreases in Ki-67 levels after NAC compared with before NAC is a significant prognostic factor [6]. However, these studies indicated limitations in considering post-NAC Ki-67 levels as prognostic factors [7]. Thus, the association of changes in Ki-67 levels after chemotherapy with outcomes in patients with ER-positive breast cancer is still uncertain. A recent study revealed that changes in Ki-67 levels may serve as a prognostic factor for predicting NAC efficacy in patients with ER-positive, HER2-positive, and TNBC breast cancers [8]. However, evidence regarding the prognostic value of Ki-67 changes in patients with ER-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer is limited.

This study aimed to evaluate the correlation between clinical responses to NAC and prognosis in patients with ER-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer. Specifically, we investigated the association between pCR and improved prognosis and explored the prognostic value of Ki-67 level changes before and after NAC in patients with ER-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer. The results of this study provide insights into outcomes after NAC in patients with ER-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer and help identify potential prognostic markers to guide personalized treatment strategies for this breast cancer subtype.

METHODS

Ethics statement

This retrospective study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of The Catholic University of Korea, Seoul St. Mary’s Hospital (No. KC24ZASI0313). The requirement for written informed consent was waived because of the retrospective design, and all data were collected and analyzed anonymously. The study was conducted in accordance with local legislation and institutional requirements.

Patients

From January 2018 to February 2023, 569 patients with breast cancer received NAC followed by surgical treatment at Seoul St. Mary’s Hospital, including 154 patients with ER-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer. Patients with metastatic, occult, and bilateral breast cancers and male patients were excluded from the study. Five patients met the exclusion criteria. Therefore, 149 patients were included in the analysis (Fig. 1). The median follow-up period for the study was 34 months (range, 5–85 months).

ER and progesterone receptor (PgR) stains were considered positive when at least 1% of the tumor cells exhibited unequivocal nuclear staining. Patients with immunohistochemistry scores of 0, 1+, or 2+ with no gene amplification based on fluorescence in situ hybridization were considered HER2 negative. Ki-67 expression was classified as low (<20%) or high (≥20%) based on the proportion of tumor cells with nuclear staining.

NAC included anthracycline-based regimens in all patients. Additional drugs included cyclophosphamide, docetaxel, and paclitaxel. Adjuvant chemotherapy was administered to patients with residual disease after NAC or based on multidisciplinary discussion. Adjuvant endocrine therapy with tamoxifen or an aromatase inhibitor was started postoperatively based on menopausal status. Ovarian function suppressors were also administered as appropriate. Patients who underwent BCS received adjuvant radiotherapy, and patients who underwent mastectomies received post-mastectomy radiotherapy after a multidisciplinary discussion.

Pathological information from pre-NAC core needle biopsies and post-surgery was saved in a database. The effects of chemotherapy were assessed in surgical specimens. The pCR was defined as no residual invasive tumor upon hematoxylin and eosin evaluation of the completely resected breast specimen and all sampled sentinel lymph nodes (ypT0/is, ypN0). Tumor assessments were performed according to the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) ver. 1.1 and patients were classified as having a complete response, partial response (PR), stable disease (SD), or progressive disease (PD). Classifications were based on comparisons between the clinical stage from preoperative magnetic resonance imaging and the pathologic stage after surgery.

Statistical methods

Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics were compared using the t-tests and chi-square tests. Survival-related indicators included DFS and OS. DFS was calculated from the date of diagnosis to the date of the first event (locoregional recurrence, distant metastasis), and OS was calculated from the date of diagnosis to the date of death or last follow-up. The Kaplan-Meier method was used to define the difference ratio, and the log-rank test was used to compare survival curves. A P-value of <0.05 indicated significance. All statistical analyses were performed using the R software ver. 4.5.0 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing).

RESULTS

Clinicopathologic characteristics of the patients

The clinic pathological characteristics of the 149 patients with ER-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer are summarized in Table 1. The median age at diagnosis was 49 years (range, 23–74 years). Forty-eight patients (32.2%) underwent mastectomies after NAC, and 101 patients (67.8%) underwent BCS. Sentinel lymph node biopsies were performed in 63 patients (42.3%). A majority of the patients (139 patients, 93.3%) were diagnosed with invasive ductal carcinoma, and 7 patients (4.7%) were diagnosed with invasive lobular carcinoma. The breast tumors were PgR-positive in 106 patients (71.7%) and the mean PgR Allred score was 4.69. Ki-67 levels were ≥20% in 120 patients (80.5%), <20% in 28 patients (18.8%), and unknown in 1 patient. Sixty patients (40.3%) received docetaxel therapy followed by anthracycline and cyclophosphamide, and 49 patients (32.9%) received anthracycline and cyclophosphamide followed by weekly paclitaxel. According to the RECIST criteria, 13 patients (8.7%) achieved pCR, 87 patients (58.4%) achieved PR, 40 patients (26.8%) had SD, and 9 patients (6.0%) had PD after NAC.

Clinicopathologic characteristics according to RECIST categories

The clinic's pathological characteristics of patients grouped by RECIST categories are summarized in Table 2. The median ages were 51 years for pCR, 50 years for PR, 47.5 years for SD, and 37 years for PD (P = 0.003). The mean Allred scores for ER were 5.00 for pCR, 6.94 for PR, 7.03 for SD, and 5.00 for PD (P < 0.001). The highest proportion of PgR-positive cases was in the PR (75.2%) and SD (72.5%) groups, followed by the pCR group (46.2%). The lowest proportion of PgR-positive cases was in the PD group (22.2%). The mean Allred score for PgR was the lowest in the PD group (1.67), followed by the pCR group (2.31); the highest Allred scores for PgR were in the PR and SD groups (5.90 and 5.28, respectively) (P < 0.001). The pCR group had the highest mean Ki-67 levels (60.1%) (P = 0.001). Post-treatment Ki-67 levels decreased significantly in the PR and SD groups, but the mean Ki-67 levels (66.0%) did not change after treatment in the PD group (P < 0.001). No significant differences were observed in clinical tumor sizes or NAC regimen type among the RECIST groups.

Age-based analysis of disease-free survival and overall survival

Based on Kaplan-Meier survival analyses, DFS and OS differed significantly among the RECIST groups in the entire cohort (Supplementary Fig. 1). The 5-year DFS rates were 92.3% in the pCR group, 82.1% in the PR group, 82.4% in the SD group, and 33.3% in the PD group (P = 0.018). The OS rates were 88.9% in the pCR group, 95.7% in the PR group, 100% in the SD group, and 66.7% in the PD group (P = 0.005).

An age-specific subgroup analysis was conducted to assess survival outcomes according to RECIST response. In patients aged ≤50 years, DFS differed significantly among the RECIST groups (P = 0.007); the 5-year DFS was 80.0% in the pCR group, 83.4% in the PR group, 79.3% in the SD group, 28.6% in the PD group (Fig. 2A). In patients aged >50 years, no significant differences in DFS were detected between the RECIST groups (P = 0.783) (Fig. 2C). OS was significantly different among the RECIST groups (P = 0.005) in patients ≤50 years old; the 5-year OS was 88.9% in the pCR group, 95.7% in the PR group, 100% in the SD group, and 66.7% in the PD group (Fig. 2B). However, no significant differences in OS were detected between RECIST groups in patients aged >50 years (P = 0.787) (Fig. 2D).

Clinicopathologic characteristics according to post-neoadjuvant chemotherapy Ki-67 levels

The association between post-NAC Ki-67 levels and clinic pathological characteristics was analyzed by dividing patients into 3 groups: patients who achieved pCR, patients with a post-NAC Ki-67 index ≥20% referred to as the high Ki-67 group, and patients with a post-NAC Ki-67 index <20% referred to as the low Ki-67 group (Table 3). Thirteen patients (8.7%) achieved pCR, 35 patients (23.5%) were classified into the Ki-67 high group, and 101 patients (67.8%) were classified into the Ki-67 low group. Significant differences in hormone receptor status were detected among the groups. The median ER Allred scores were 4.0, 6.0, and 8.0 and the median PgR Allred scores were 0.0, 2.0, and 7.0 in the pCR, high Ki-67, and low Ki-67 groups, respectively (P < 0.001 for both PgR and ER). The proportions of PgR-negative cases were 53.8% in the pCR group, 54.3% in the Ki-67 high group, and 16.8% in the Ki-67 low group (P < 0.001). Before NAC, the mean Ki-67 levels were 60.08% in the pCR group, 58.06% in the high Ki-67 group, and 30.98% in the low Ki-67 group (P < 0.001). After NAC, the mean Ki-67 level was 58.91% in the high Ki-67 group and 5.34% in the low Ki-67 group (P < 0.001). Significant differences in RECIST responses were observed between the Ki-67 high and low groups (P < 0.001). In the Ki-67 high group, 18 patients (51.4%) showed PR, 10 patients (28.6%) showed SD, and 7 patients (20.0%) showed PD. In the Ki-67 low group, 69 patients (68.3%) showed PR, 30 patients (29.7%) showed SD, and 2 patients (2.0%) showed PD.

Age-based analysis of disease-free survival and overall survival according to post-neoadjuvant chemotherapy Ki-67 levels

Kaplan-Meier survival analyses were conducted to evaluate the impact of post-NAC Ki-67 levels on DFS and OS, stratified by age. Patients were categorized into 3 groups: the pCR group as a single category without further subdivision, and the non-pCR group, which was further stratified into post-NAC Ki-67 <20% (low) and ≥20% (high) subgroups. Among patients aged ≤50 years, DFS showed significant differences (P = 0.013), with 5-year DFS rates of 80.0% in the pCR group, 80.9% in the post-NAC Ki-67 low group, and 61.6% in the post-NAC Ki-67 high group (Fig. 3A). In contrast, no significant differences were found in patients aged >50 years (P = 0.544) (Fig. 3C). For OS, no significant differences were observed in either patients aged ≤50 years (P = 0.083) or >50 years (P = 0.569) (Fig. 3B, D).

DISCUSSION

The clinic pathological characteristics, RECIST responses, and post-NAC Ki-67 levels among 149 patients with ER-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer were investigated, focusing on the prognostic implications for DFS and OS. Significant age-related variations in NAC response were detected. The age distribution varied significantly across NAC response groups (P = 0.003). The pCR group had the highest median age (51 years), and the PD group was considerably younger (37 years). These results suggest a link between younger age and biologically more aggressive breast cancer phenotypes. A previous study pooling 8 German neoadjuvant trials reported higher pCR rates in younger women (<40 years), particularly in patients with TNBC and HR-positive, HER2-positive subtypes [8910111213141516]. However, in our cohort, the pCR rate was 5.8% in women ≤50 years old and 12.5% in patients >50 years old (Supplementary Tables 1 and 2). These results highlight potential discrepancies between the 2 studies, possibly due to variations in study populations. In the previous study, age was identified as an independent prognostic factor for survival in HR-positive; younger (<40 years) patients with HER2-negative breast cancer who did not achieve pCR showed worse DFS than patients who achieved pCR [8]. Our study also showed that clinical responses were significantly associated with DFS and OS only in patients aged ≤50 years, whereas no such associations were observed in patients >50 years old.

Our study revealed a higher rate of PgR negativity (53.8%) in ER-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer patients achieving a pCR after NAC. Previous large-scale pooled analyses demonstrated that PgR negativity is associated with higher pCR rates following NAC but worse long-term outcomes, including DFS and OS, highlighting the role of PgR status as a predictive and prognostic marker in ER-positive breast cancer [17]. Consistent with these findings, our study demonstrated that patients achieving pCR exhibited significantly lower ER Allred scores, higher Ki-67 levels, and higher PgR negativity. These results and previous studies suggest that PgR-negative tumors may represent a subtype with distinct biological characteristics that influence chemosensitivity and prognosis. This subtype may align more closely with basal-like breast cancer or TNBC than typical HR-positive breast cancer, despite being ER-positive. Interestingly, similar characteristics were observed in the PD group, which exhibited the highest proportion of PgR negativity (77.8%) and a median ER Allred score of 4.0, identical to the pCR group but significantly lower than the PR and SD groups. The pre-NAC Ki-67 levels in the PD group were similar to the levels in the pCR group (median 59.0% vs. 60.0%, respectively). However, the post-NAC Ki-67 levels in the PD group remained elevated (median 66.0%), indicating a lack of response to chemotherapy and, consequently, a lack of reduction in proliferation markers. This persistent elevation of Ki-67 levels suggests that the PD group harbors tumors with intrinsic resistance to NAC, which is driven by unique tumor biology that influences chemosensitivity and limits the effectiveness of treatment.

In this study, DFS and OS were significantly different when patients were classified based on RECIST criteria. The PD group exhibited the worst DFS and OS, highlighting the clinical relevance of this group as a high-risk category. Interestingly, no substantial differences in survival outcomes were observed among the remaining groups. In contrast to the conventional expectation that pCR correlates with improved survival outcomes, the SD group exhibited better OS than the pCR group [18]. This result may be partially explained by an outlier case within the pCR cohort. During the follow-up period, 1 patient with pCR experienced rapid disease progression and ultimately succumbed to breast cancer. Although this patient initially achieved a pCR response to NAC, an undetected brain metastasis was identified 3 months after surgery. A review of the pathology revealed tumor emboli, which likely contributed to the rapid progression of distant metastasis and subsequent mortality. Achieving pCR is usually associated with favorable outcomes, and residual disease burden after chemotherapy is linked to higher recurrence and mortality rates [1920]. However, the prognostic value of the pathologic response in patients with ER-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer is unclear. The implications of a pathologic response in these patients differ from the implications in patients with more aggressive breast cancer subtypes. Interestingly, the profile of patients achieving pCR in our study more closely resembled the TNBC subtype, which may influence prognosis. This result suggests that even when pCR is achieved, the prognosis may not be favorable, potentially due to biological similarities with more aggressive cancer types.

The impact of achieving pCR in patients with HR-positive breast cancer following NAC has been previously investigated. These studies focused on DFS and OS. Guarneri et al. [7] reported significantly improved DFS (P = 0.002) and OS (P = 0.040) in patients with HR-positive breast cancer who achieved pCR compared with patients who did not achieve pCR. In contrast, 2 smaller studies found no significant effect of pCR on DFS; one of these studies indicated a significant improvement in OS [2122]. These discrepancies in previous studies and our study show that achieving pCR may not uniformly predict survival outcomes in ER-positive tumors and reflect the potential heterogeneity of tumor biology within this subtype. Furthermore, age was identified as a significant factor when analyzing clinic pathological differences among RECIST groups, prompting further age-specific subgroup analyses. In younger patients (≤50 years), the prognostic impact of the RECIST response was more pronounced; significant differences in both DFS and OS were detected among RECIST groups. In contrast, no statistically significant differences in survival outcomes were detected in older patients (>50 years). These findings suggest that younger age may play a critical role in influencing the prognostic value of RECIST response, particularly in patients with ER-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer. The association between younger age and aggressive tumor biology is well-established in other breast cancer subtypes. However, the specific impact of age on treatment response and long-term survival in ER-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer is underexplored.

The relationship between post-NAC Ki-67 levels and treatment responses was investigated by classifying patients into low and high post-NAC Ki-67 groups using a cutoff value of 20%. The detailed clinic pathological characteristics stratified by age and Ki-67 levels are provided in Supplementary Tables 3 and 4. Patients with high Ki-67 levels exhibited worse treatment responses, with higher rates of PD and SD. In contrast, patients with low Ki-67 levels showed a greater prevalence of PR. This observation emphasizes the potential of posttreatment Ki-67 levels as a marker for evaluating therapeutic efficacy. Consistent with previous research [2324], our study highlighted the correlation between differences in pre- and post-chemotherapy Ki-67 levels and clinical responses, underscoring the utility of Ki-67 for evaluating therapeutic efficacy rather than as a standalone prognostic marker.

Survival outcomes were investigated according to age and post-NAC Ki-67 levels. Ki-67 is a well-established prognostic factor for DFS and OS in patients with breast cancer; higher post-NAC levels are associated with worse prognoses [4]. In our analysis, significant differences in DFS according to post-NAC Ki-67 levels were observed only among younger patients (≤50 years), but no differences were detected in older patients (aged >50 years). In contrast, post-NAC Ki-67 levels were not significantly associated with OS in either age group. These findings are consistent with previous studies demonstrating the prognostic value of both pre- and post-NAC Ki-67 levels in breast cancer patients [25]. Of note, reduced Ki-67 levels after NAC independently predict DFS and recurrence-free survival [2627], supporting the role of Ki-67 changes as a dynamic biomarker for assessing treatment responses and long-term outcomes. The observation that DFS differences were limited to younger patients may reflect distinct biological features in this group, such as higher tumor aggressiveness and greater chemotherapy sensitivity.

Our study had several limitations. First, this study was conducted at a single center with a relatively small sample size of 149 patients, limiting the generalizability of our findings to broader populations. Second, although the study evaluated the impact of post-NAC Ki-67 levels on DFS and OS, the follow-up period may have been insufficient to capture long-term outcomes, particularly in subgroups with relatively better prognoses. The recurrence rate is generally low for luminal breast cancer, but the risk of recurrence can increase several years after treatment [28]. Therefore, extended follow-up is essential to confirm the prognostic significance of post-NAC Ki-67 levels and age-specific differences in survival and to evaluate the long-term necessity and benefits of chemotherapy in this subtype. Additionally, variations in adjuvant treatment, including chemotherapy and endocrine therapy, may have influenced survival outcomes. These factors were not the primary focus of this study and could act as potential confounders. Future studies should incorporate detailed treatment data to adjust for these effects, which could help guide more effective and personalized treatment strategies for patients with ER-positive, HER-negative breast cancer.

In conclusion, clinical responses and post-NAC Ki-67 levels are significant prognostic indicators in patients with ER-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer, particularly in patients ≤50 years old. In older patients (>50 years), these factors are less predictive, highlighting the need for alternative markers and age-specific strategies. A tailored approach combining post-NAC Ki-67 levels with clinical response assessments may enhance prognostic accuracy and guide personalized treatments, especially in younger patients.

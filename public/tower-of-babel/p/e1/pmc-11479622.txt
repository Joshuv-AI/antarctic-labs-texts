Biomarker role of thyroid irAE and PD-L1 positivity in predicting PD-1 blockade efficacy in patients with non-small cell lung cancer
Authors: Hye In Kim, Won Gu Kim, Mijin Kim, Nak Gyeong Ko, Mihyeon Jin, Hyun Ae Jung, Jong-Mu Sun, Jin Seok Ahn, Myung-Ju Ahn, Yoon-La Choi, Min Ji Jeon, Tae Yong Kim, Won Bae Kim, Sang-We Kim, Dae Ho Lee, Se Jin Jang, Sun Wook Kim, Jae Hoon Chung, Tae Hyuk Kim, Se-Hoon Lee
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThyroid immune-related adverse events (irAEs) are associated with programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) blockade efficacy in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). However, their independence from PD-L1 expression and quantitative impact on predicting PD-1 blockade efficacy remain unexplored. This multicenter, retrospective, longitudinal study from Korea included 71 metastatic NSCLC patients who underwent PD-L1 expression and thyroid function testing during PD-1 blockade. Disease progression by the Response Evaluation Criteria for Solid Tumors was the main outcome. Three-stage analyses were performed: (1) multivariate Cox regression models adjusted for PD-L1 expression according to thyroid irAEs; (2) subgroup analyses; (3) regrouping and comparing predictivity of current and alternative staging. Patients with thyroid irAE + exhibited a longer progression-free survival [7/20 vs. 34/51, adjusted HR 0.19 (0.07–0.47); P < 0.001] than those with thyroid irAE-, independent of PD-L1 expression; the results remained across most subgroups without interaction. The three groups showed different adjusted HR for disease progression (Group 1: PD L1 + and thyroid irAE + ; Group 2: PD-L1 + or thyroid irAE + : 5.08 [1.48–17.34]; Group 3: PD-L1− and thyroid irAE− : 30.49 [6.60–140.78]). Alternative staging (Group 1 in stage IVB → stage IVA; Group 3 in stage IVA → stage IVB) improved the prognostic value (PVE: 21.7% vs. 6.44%; C-index: 0.706 vs. 0.617) compared with the 8th Tumor–Node–Metastasis staging. Our study suggests thyroid irAEs and PD-L1 expression are independent biomarkers that improve predicting PD-1 blockade efficacy in NSCLC. Introduction

Immune checkpoint inhibitors exhibit promising effects in advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) [1, 2]. Two anti–programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) inhibitors, nivolumab and pembrolizumab have been approved by the US Food and Drug Administration [3].

PD-1 blockade has two distinct characteristics; a low response rate (15–30%) [3] and immune-related adverse events (irAEs) [4]. Therefore, identifying biomarkers for the subset of patients with NSCLC most likely to benefit from PD-1 blockade are important. A higher PD-L1 tumor expression is related to PD-1 blockade efficacy [5–7]; therefore, PD-L1 expression testing is recommended as a tumor-specific biomarker [8]. We previously suggested thyroid irAE as a host-specific biomarker, owing to good correlation with PD-1 blockade efficacy [9]. Meta-analyses [10–17] or prospective studies [18] show concordant results. These studies established the importance of thyroid irAEs as possible biomarkers to predict PD-1 blockade response. However, they did not adjust for PD-L1 expression, which is the only validated predictive factor and a possible confounding factor. Furthermore, they did not quantify predictivity improvement by incorporating thyroid irAE and PD-L1 expression into the current staging system.

Therefore, we explored whether thyroid irAE is an independent prognostic factor after adjusting PD-L1 expression and quantified the impact of thyroid irAE and PD-L1 expression on staging system predictivity.

Materials and methods

Study population

This retrospective, longitudinal study included 188 eligible patients who underwent thyroid function tests (TFTs) during treatment for stage IV NSCLC with nivolumab or pembrolizumab between January 2014 and July 2017 at the Samsung Medical Center (SMC) or Asan Medical Center (AMC). Immunohistochemistry (IHC) data (n = 78) of PD-L1 in patients were included. Seventy-one patients from two tertiary referral institutions were enrolled after excluding patients with thyroid irAEs before PD-1 blockade (n = 3) who did not undergo routine TFT (n = 3) and without evidence of PD-1 blockade administration, even with prescription (n = 1) (Supplementary Fig. 1). This study was approved by the Institutional Review Boards of SMC (2016-12-112) and AMC (2016-1254), and informed consent was waived due to retrospective nature of the study. Data was anonymized before statistical analyses.

Data collection

Patients received 3 mg/kg nivolumab every 2 weeks and 2 mg/kg pembrolizumab every 3 weeks. TFT [thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) and free thyroxine 4 (FT4)] were conducted every 4–8 weeks using a commercialized immunoradiometric assay (Immunotech, Cedex, France; range: 0.30–6.00 mIU/L for men 0.30–6.50 mIU/L for women) and radioimmunoassay (Immunotech; range: 10.16–23.94 pmol/L for men 8.23–22.13 pmol/L for women), respectively. Board-certified pathologists assessed tumor PD-L1 expression in formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tissues acquired using an IHC assay (QualTek, Newtown, PA, USA). Clinical response evaluation was based on investigator-determined immune-related response criteria [19]. Tumor response was evaluated based on radiological studies every 2–4 cycles of treatment using the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors version 1.1 [20].

Study outcome and subgroups

Patients were categorized into the thyroid irAE + (≥ 1 abnormal TFT results during PD-1 blockade) or thyroid irAE− (no abnormal results) groups according to thyroid irAE development. The primary endpoint was progression-free survival (PFS) defined as the time from treatment initiation until disease progression or death. The secondary endpoint was overall survival (OS) defined as the time from treatment initiation until death.

To evaluate whether thyroid irAE severity impacts clinical response, we classified patients based on serum TSH and FT4 levels into the overt (both levels beyond the normal range), subclinical (only TSH level beyond the normal range), and euthyroid group (thyroid irAE−) [9, 21]. Severity determined using the National Cancer Institute–Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (NCI-CTCAE) v5.0 [19] was not adopted owing to poor reproducibility owing to a lack of objective criteria.

Tumor PD-L1 positivity was defined as a proportion score ≥ 1%. PD-L1 expression was classified based on the clinical trial assay predicting maximal clinical response to PD-1 blockade as < 1% (negative), 1–49% (weak positive), and ≥ 50% (strong positive) [22–24]. Baseline cancer staging was assessed using the 8th American Joint Committee on Cancer staging manual [25]. Patients were divided into ≤ 60 and > 60 year subgroups using the median baseline age.

Study design and statistical analysis

To evaluate whether thyroid irAEs were independent prognostic factors, Cox proportional hazard regression models were used to estimate hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for OS and PFS. Other variables influencing responses were adjusted, including age, sex, tumor subtype, baseline stage, medication type, and PD-L1 expression. Considering multicollinearity between smoking and sex, smoking was not considered in the multivariate analysis (all current or ex-smokers except one male patient). Subgroup with interaction analyses were performed for PFS using a multivariate Cox proportional hazard regression model to assess the consistency of the predictive effect of thyroid irAEs. To derive alternative prognostic groups, we identified three groups according to PD-L1 positivity and thyroid irAEs. Six re-stratified groups were derived using a combination of PD-L1 positivity and thyroid irAE criteria in the 8th Tumor–Node–Metastasis (TNM) staging system. Cox regression was used to calculate adjusted HRs for disease progression risk in each group and alternative staging was derived after considering minimal hazard differences. The predicted performance of alternative staging was evaluated by comparing proportions of variance explained (PVEs; 0–100: larger numbers suggest better predictability) [26] and C-index [27] in Cox proportional regression models.

Continuous variables were presented as the median and interquartile range (IQR); categorical variables were presented as numbers and percentages. Statistical analyses were performed using Statistical Package for Social Sciences (version 22.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Two-sided P-values < 0.05 indicated statistical significance.

Results

Baseline characteristics and oncologic outcomes

The median patient age was 61.0 years (57.0–69.0); 54 patients (76.1%) were men. Twenty-eight patients (39.4%) had stage IVA disease and 43 (60.6%) had stage IVB; 20 (28.2%), 20 (28.2%), and 31 (43.7%) patients had PD-L1 expression < 1%, 1–49%, and ≥ 50%, respectively. Treatment duration of PD-1 blockade was 85.0 days (28.5–223.5). Thirty patients terminated the PD-1 blockade due to disease progression (25 patients), side effects (1 for pruritus, 2 for pneumonitis), and financial burden (2 patients).

All thyroid irAEs were of grade 1 or 2 according to NCI-CTCAE v5.0. Thyroid irAEs developed in 20/71 patients (28.1%), with a median time to onset of 41.5 days (20.2–122.2) during follow-up.

During the median 114.0 days (42.0–263.0) of follow-up, 15 deaths were recorded: 2 out of 20 (10%) patients in thyroid irAE + group with median OS 161.0 days (105.7–410.7), and 13 out of 51 (25.5%) patients in thyroid irAE− group with median OS 68.0 days (32.0–235.5). There were 41 progressions: 7 out of 20 (35.0%) in thyroid irAE + group with median PFS 142.0 days (89.0–410.7), and 34 out of 51 (66.7%) patients in thyroid irAE− group with median PFS 67.0 days (30.0–233.0).

Discussion

In current study, we firstly demonstrated that thyroid irAEs are associated with favorable prognosis independently to PD-L1 expression by multivariable analysis and interaction analysis. In addition, combining thyroid irAEs and PD-L1 expression into the current staging system achieved higher PVEs for predicting PFS than those of the current staging system (from 6.44 to 21.7%).

Previous studies showed that thyroid irAEs could be a predictive factor for PD-1 blockade efficacy in NSCLC [9–12, 15, 16, 28]. However, PD-L1 expression which is a critical factor in response to PD1-blockade [5, 8] and related to irAE occurrence [29] was not adjusted. Current study suggested thyroid irAE was significant predictor for oncologic outcomes independent to PD-L1 expression. The possible mechanism is following: PD-1/PD-L1 plays an important role in tumor evasion and immunological tolerance of normal tissue [30], and activating T cells against malignant antigens via PD-1 blockade could lead to an attack on other tissues. PD-L1 expression was enriched in thyrocytes and knockout mice showed increased autoimmunity. Thyroid irAEs are associated with more markers of cytotoxic memory CD4 T-cells—which are activated by PD-1 blockade—than their counterparts [31]. It suggests that PD-1 blockade efficacy may be related to thyroid irAE as thyroid irAE and anti-tumor mechanisms share some common pathways. Further experimental studies could focus on what pathways is shared between irAE and anti-tumor mechanisms.

A positive association between thyroid irAEs and a longer PFS remained in most subgroups with no significant interaction, thus validating our findings. However, the lost impact of thyroid irAEs on favorable outcomes in < 1% PD-L1 expression. It may be explained by too small number to gain statistical power. However, applying the results in this study for that subgroup needs caution: it is possible the absence of tumor factor PD-L1 expression results in a failure to convey such immune activity to the tumor, leading to reduced therapeutic efficacy even if the patient factor was favorable. High PD-L1 expression is related to irAE occurrence [29]. In our study, thyroid irAE developed in five patients (25%) with < 1% PD-L1, which was lower than those with ≥ 50% PD-L1 (50%). In this explanation, the lack of association between thyroid irAEs and OS or PFS in some previous studies [32, 33] could be explained by differences in patient proportions with < 1% PD-L1 expression. Therefore, the future study with large numbers of patients will be needed.

Thyroid irAEs and PD-L1 expression were independent and important prognostic factors, implying that a composite of both variables may help identify patients with NSCLC most likely to benefit from PD-1 blockade. In immunotherapy era, response to cancer immunotherapy has recently emerged as an important predictor in addition to classic stage. In this study, 12/71 (16.9%) patients who received PD-1 blockade did not have the predicted prognosis according to the current staging system. The effect of PD-1 blockade occurs through the interaction between tumor and patient factors. Thyroid irAEs are patient factors indicating an interaction between PD-1 blockade and the patients’ immune system. Thus, it appears to facilitate a more accurate prognosis prediction than simple tumor factors (staging or using PD-L1 expression) alone. Furthermore, thyroid irAE for predicting PD-1 blockade efficacy has some advantages; it is one of the most frequent irAEs after PD-1 blockade (10.2%, systematic review [34]; 9.9%, prospective study [35]). Most cases occurred during the early course (1–2 months after PD-1 blockade) [36]. Most clinicians check TFTs monthly during PD-1 blockade treatment, especially the initial 6 months, according to the guidelines [36]. Considering the advantages and results of our study, thyroid irAEs could be an early prognostic indicator in NSCLC with PD-1 blockade treatment in clinical settings.

This study has a few limitations. Further analysis to improve OS predictivity was impossible because only a few deaths were recorded in the thyroid irAE + group. Therefore, the results do not provide evidence to change the current staging system. Future research with a large cohort is needed to demonstrate increased OS predictivity. Second, we could not incorporate other predictive factors like tumor mutation burden [37] or mutational status other than PD-L1 positivity because genetic information was not assessed. Potential risk factors for thyroid irAEs, such as anti-thyroglobulin antibody/anti-thyroid peroxidase antibody [38], irregular echo pattern [38], and increased uptake of fludeoxyglucose-18 on positron emission tomography [39] were also not assessed. Third, this was a retrospective study with a small sample size. Therefore, a large-scale prospective study is warranted to verify these results. Despite this limitation, current study shed light on the idea or designs of further RCT studies, and aid clinicians to identify the NSCLC subsets who benefit from PD-1 blockade.

In conclusion, our study suggests thyroid irAE and PD-L1 expression were independent biomarkers for favorable outcomes and their combination improved the predictability of PD-1 blockade efficacy in NSCLC. In the future, thyroid irAEs with PD-L1 expression will require consideration to identify patients with NSCLC who benefit the most from PD-1 blockade in the early course of treatment.

Comparison of pre- and intra-operative analgesia of fascia Iliaca compartment block in reducing post-operative delirium of elderly patients following hip fractures: a retrospective study
Authors: Qing Yang, Jun Zheng, Lei Duan, Haiyan Zhou, Bing Xu, Huadong Lu, Hua Xu, Gengqi Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHip fractures significantly impact the physiological and psychological well-being of elderly patients. Post-operative delirium (POD) is a common complication after hip fractures in this population, severely affecting treatment outcomes and recovery. Effective pain management during the peri-operative period is crucial for reducing POD, yet the best analgesic approach remains debated.MethodsA retrospective study (from January 2015 to December 2020) included 198 elderly hip fracture patients who received preoperative (PO-) or intraoperative (IO-) fascia iliaca compartment block (FICB) with 50 mL of 0.25% ropivacaine. Over the first 3 days (assessed every 24 h), cognitive function (Mini-Mental State Examination [MMSE]), pain intensity (Visual Analog Scale [VAS]), and systemic inflammatory mediators (CRP, IL-1, IL-6, TNF-α) were evaluated. Postoperative delirium (POD) incidence was daily assessed via the Confusion Assessment Method (CAM) for 72 h by a trained surgeon. Statistical analyses included intergroup comparisons of MMSE/VAS scores and inflammatory markers; cumulative POD incidence was analyzed using Kaplan-Meier curves. Normally distributed data were compared via independent-samples t-test, and categorical data (expressed as percentages) via chi-square (χ²) test.ResultsPatients were allocated to two groups: PO-FICB (n = 100) and IO-FICB (n = 98). Postoperatively, the PO-FICB group had significantly higher MMSE scores on days 1–3 (27.85 ± 1.923, 27.49 ± 2.807, 27.23 ± 1.698 vs. 25.80 ± 1.864, 25.73 ± 2.197, 22.57 ± 2.128; all P < 0.01) and lower levels of inflammatory mediators (CRP, IL-1, IL-6, TNF-α; all P < 0.05). VAS pain scores were lower in the PO-FICB group (1.58 ± 0.702, 2.26 ± 0.647, 2.40 ± 0.492 vs. 2.70 ± 0.659, 3.02 ± 0.853, 2.33 ± 0.620), with significant differences on days 1–2 (P < 0.0001) but not day 3 (P = 0.068). POD incidence was significantly lower in the PO-FICB group (5% [5/100]) than in the IO-FICB group (15.3% [15/98]; P = 0.0168). No severe complications or mortality were observed during 12-month follow-up.ConclusionCompared with IO-FICB, PO-FICB may confer more targeted analgesia and is associated with a lower incidence of POD in elderly hip fracture patients. These associations might relate to PO-FICB’s potential early effects of inhibiting inflammation and exerting putative neuroprotective-like actions, which are consistent with current multimodal perioperative management strategies and geriatric neuroprotective concepts. This finding provides preliminary support for considering PO-FICB as a viable option in perioperative regional anesthesia selection for this patient population.Keywords: Hip fracture, Surgery, Analgesia, Delirium, Outcome

Background

Geriatric hip fractures impose a substantial socioeconomic and familial care burden (Pech-Ciau et al. 2021). These injuries cause severe acute pain from trauma to postoperatively, with comorbid chronic conditions exacerbating systemic impairment, particularly cerebral organic function (Wantonoro et al. 2020; Arneson et al. 2023; Jain et al. 2022). Postoperative delirium (POD), defined as reversible acute consciousness disturbances (e.g., agitation, disorientation), is a common acute encephalopathy affecting 23% of elderly hip fracture patients post-surgery (Aung Thein et al. 2020). It associates with increased complications, depression, self-injury, disability, and mortality, emphasizing the need for effective prevention and management (Aung Thein et al. 2020). Systemic inflammation impairs cognition (including postoperative cognitive dysfunction) and may induce delirium; circulating interleukin-1 (IL-1) links to neuronal dysfunction and potential death, though the mechanism remains unclear (Skelly et al. 2019).

Surgical intervention and analgesia are critical for pain relief, recovery promotion, and mitigating POD pathophysiology (Brown et al. 2020). However, the optimal timing of analgesia remains controversial. An emerging strategy is preoperative fascia iliaca compartment block (PO-FICB), an early ultrasound-guided technique administering 0.25% ropivacaine into the iliacus muscle sheath (Salottolo et al. 2022).

This study hypothesizes that PO-FICB offers superior perioperative analgesia, better clinical outcomes, and faster recovery than IO-FICB in elderly hip fracture patients, with greater potential to reduce POD incidence. Its primary objective is to compare POD rates between the two groups, and the secondary objective is to explore potential mechanisms underlying POD.

Methods

Study design and setting

Tis retrospective cohort study was conducted at a regional trauma center, reviewing hip fracture patients who under-went FICB before surgical intervention January 2015 and December 2020.

Ethical approval

This study was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee of Yueyang Hospital of Integrated Traditional Chinese and Western Medicine, Shanghai University of Traditional Chinese Medicine (Approval No. 2025-012; date: 21 February 2025). Due to the retrospective design and anonymization of routinely collected clinical data, written informed consent was waived. All procedures adhered to the institutional ethical standards and the Declaration of Helsinki.

Study cohort

Between January 2015 and December 2020, elderly patients with hip fractures who underwent either PO-FICB or IO-FICB were enrolled for data analysis. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) age ≥ 65 years; (2) hip fracture confirmed by radiological examination; (3) complete medical records containing data on analgesia, surgery, and relevant outcome measurements; and (4) a minimum follow-up duration of 12 months. The exclusion criteria included: (1) age < 65 years; (2) multiple trauma involving hip fracture combined with other injuries; (3) a history of psychiatric disorders or substance abuse; and (4) incomplete medical records. Ultimately, 198 patients were included in this retrospective study. The patient enrollment flowchart is presented in Fig. 1.

Of these, 102 cases were excluded due to meeting the exclusion criteria. Finally, 198 cases met the inclusion criteria, with 100 cases assigned to the PO-FICB group and 98 cases to the IO-FICB group

Grouping methods and data matching

A total of 198 hip fracture patients were allocated to either the PO-FICB group (n = 100) or the IO-FICB group (n = 98), with detailed baseline characteristics summarized in Table 1. Assignment to PO-FICB and IO-FICB (non-randomized, retrospective) was based on three factors: clinician expertise in regional anesthesia and institutional practice, logistical considerations (e.g., urgent surgeries, preoperative positioning constraints), and patient-specific factors (e.g., preoperative pain, coagulation status, frailty).

Anesthesia methods

Patients underwent ultrasound-guided infrainguinal fascia iliaca compartment block. At the start of the procedure, patients were placed in a supine position; the skin was disinfected, and a high-frequency linear probe was positioned transversely on the inguinal crease. After identifying the femoral artery, fascia iliaca, and iliopsoas muscle, the linear probe was moved laterally until the sartorius muscle was visualized. Midazolam 0.05 mg/kg, fentanyl 2 µg/kg, propofol 1.5 mg/kg, and cisatracurium 0.15 mg/kg were administered sequentially. After tracheal intubation, the patient was connected to a ventilator for mechanical ventilation. Maintenance anesthesia was achieved with continuous infusion of propofol and remifentanil, alongside intermittent administration of cisatracurium to maintain muscle relaxation. A 22-gauge needle was then inserted in-plane through the fascia iliaca, and 50 mL of 0.25% ropivacaine was injected into the fascial plane between the fascia iliaca and iliopsoas muscle. No other perioperative analgesics were administered unless clinically necessary (e.g., paracetamol, NSAIDs, or other agents), as these could have influenced the study outcomes. All elderly hip fracture patients in both groups received standardized perioperative management in accordance with clinical guidelines. The procedure is illustrated in Fig. 3.

In the PO-FICB group, on the day after admission (either upon ward entry or in the anesthesia room), patients underwent routine vital sign monitoring prior to FICB, followed by venous access establishment and ultrasound-guided FICB administration with 0.25% ropivacaine injection. In the IO-FICB group, patients underwent general anesthesia for surgery on the second day after admission, with FICB performed in the operating room or anesthesia room prior to surgical initiation.

Data collection and outcome measurement

The primary outcome was POD, which was defined as all the criteria to be met: (1) disturbance in attention and awareness; (2) disturbance develops acutely and tends to fluctuate in severity; (3) at least one additional disturbance in cognition; (4) disturbances are not better explained by a preexisting dementia; (5) disturbances do not occur in the context of a severely reduced level of arousal or coma; (6) evidence of an underlying organic cause or causes (Gibb et al. 2020). POD was assessed once daily by a trained and experienced surgeon (Jun Zheng) using the Confusion Assessment Method (CAM). POD assessors were fully blinded to patients’ intervention group (PO-FICB/IO-FICB). Intervention details were documented by independent anesthesia staff and inaccessible to assessing surgeons. The incidence of POD was observed and recorded on days 1, 2, and 3 after admission. Cognitive function was assessed using the Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) (Sakurai et al. 2021), and pain intensity was evaluated via the Visual Analog Scale (VAS) (Jensen et al. 2003).

Serum levels of inflammatory mediators, including C-reactive protein (CRP), IL-1, interleukin-6 (IL-6), and tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), were measured on days 1, 2, and 3 after admission. Peripheral blood samples for detecting these inflammatory markers were collected at three time points: day 1 post-admission (pre-block PO-FICB); day 2 (pre-block IO-FICB); and day 3. All samples were processed within 2 h in accordance with laboratory standard operating procedures (SOPs): centrifugation at 3,000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C, followed by serum storage at -80 °C. The aforementioned mediators (CRP, IL-1, IL-6, and TNF-α) were quantified using commercial enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits, with details as follows: CRP (Abcam, Cat. No. ab108827); IL-1 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cat. No. 88-7010-88); IL-6 (R&D Systems, Cat. No. D6050); and TNF-α (PeproTech, Cat. No. 900-K15). All experiments were performed strictly in accordance with the manufacturers’ instructions, with quality control ensuring an intra-assay coefficient of variation (CV) < 5% and an inter-assay CV < 10%.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was performed using SPSS 22.0 software. Measurement data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (x̄ ± s) and analyzed using the t-test. Independent t-test was preferred for normal distribution data, while the Mann-Whitney u-test was used for nonparametric statistic in terms of skewed distribution data. Count data were expressed as percentages (%) and analyzed using the χ² test. Cumulative incidence was compared using Kaplan-Meier survival curve analysis. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant, respectively.

Results

Ultimately, 198 eligible patients were enrolled with complete medical records retrievable from the hospital admission system. The PO-FICB group (n = 100; 62 males/38 females) had a mean age of 77.70 ± 8.49 years, and the IO-FICB group (n = 98; 68 males/30 females) 77.99 ± 8.00 years. All completed ≥ 12-month follow-up. The overall postoperative delirium (POD) incidence was 10.1% (PO-FICB: 5%; IO-FICB: 15.3%). No significant differences in length of stay or severe complications (e.g., local anesthetic toxicity, injection site pain, infection, bleeding) were reported, nor were there deaths during the study. Clinical and laboratory test results are presented as follows.

MMSE scores

In the PO-FICB group, the MMSE scores on days 1, 2, and 3 were 27.85 ± 1.923, 27.49 ± 2.807, and 27.23 ± 1.698 points, respectively. In contrast, the corresponding scores in the IO-FICB group were 25.80 ± 1.864, 25.73 ± 2.197, and 22.57 ± 2.128 points on the same days. The PO-FICB group exhibited significantly higher MMSE scores compared to the IO-FICB group, with all differences reaching statistical significance (P < 0.0001). (Table 2).

VAS scores

In the PO-FICB group, the VAS scores on days 1, 2, and 3 after admission were 1.58 ± 0.702, 2.26 ± 0.647, and 2.40 ± 0.492 points, respectively, whereas the corresponding scores in the IO-FICB group were 2.70 ± 0.659, 3.02 ± 0.853, and 2.33 ± 0.620 points. On day 1, significant differences were observed in the levels of CRP, IL-1, IL-6, and TNF-α between the two groups (all P < 0.001).

On day 2, the PO-FICB group maintained lower levels of CRP, IL-1, IL-6, and TNF-α compared with the IO-FICB group, with all these markers showing statistical significance (P = 0.022, < 0.001, < 0.001, and < 0.001, respectively).

Incidence of POD and survival curve

In the PO-FICB group, 5 out of 100 cases developed POD, corresponding to an incidence rate of 5%. In contrast, the IO-FICB group had 15 POD cases among 98 patients, with an incidence rate of 15.3%. This inter-group difference was statistically significant as determined by the χ² test (P = 0.0168) (Table 4). Additionally, the Kaplan-Meier survival curve revealed a significant difference in the cumulative POD-free survival rate over the 72-hour period (P = 0.037) (Fig. 5).

Discussion

Our results suggest that early local analgesic intervention may be a potential preventive strategy for POD. The PO-FICB group exhibited a lower POD incidence than the IO-FICB group (5% vs. 15.3%), with a significant difference in cumulative 72-hour POD incidence between the two groups. Clinically, the PO-FICB group showed favorable trends over the IO-FICB group in MMSE scores, VAS scores, and inflammatory mediator levels. The potential association between reduced inflammatory markers and POD prevention is biologically plausible but remains a preliminary hypothesis; the putative link may involve modulation of systemic inflammation and improved neurological function, and these findings require further validation.

Delirium is a common complication in elderly patients, especially those with hip fractures, defined as an acute mental dysfunction typically arising within the first postoperative week and characterized by fluctuating consciousness, attention, and cognitive function. A review of 33 studies reported an overall incidence of 23% based on the employed diagnostic criteria (Aung Thein et al. 2020), while the overall POD incidence in our study was 10.1%: the PO-FICB group (5%) had a significantly lower rate than the IO-FICB group (15.3%) (Arneson et al. 2023). POD is associated with adverse short-term outcomes in elderly patients, including increased falls, aspiration pneumonia, short-term mortality, and 12-month disability (Arneson et al. 2023; Jain et al. 2022; Guay et al. 2020; He et al. 2022); one study reported a 15.9% mortality rate (Aung Thein et al. 2020), underscoring the critical need for improved delirium management. Notably, no deaths or local/systemic complications were observed in our study during 12-month follow-up, a favorable outcome potentially attributed to comprehensive multidisciplinary medical support, with FICB (particularly PO-FICB) exerting a positive effect.

The pathogenesis of delirium remains unclear, with identified high-risk factors including acute medical illnesses, drug use/withdrawal, trauma, surgery (Dong et al. 2023; Ní Chróinín et al. 2023), and CNS-specific factors like stroke (Zhang et al. 2022); these may induce nervous system dysfunction, and while delirium involves diverse neurobiological mechanisms, no single-pathway hypothesis is sufficiently supported (Krogseth et al. 2023). A POD mechanism study found LPS and IL-1 induce hypoglycemia, reduce CSF glucose levels, and inhibit spontaneous activity (Skelly et al. 2019; Kealy et al. 2020), with baseline CNS susceptibility critical to delirium pathogenesis; thus, FICB may reduce POD incidence by lowering serum inflammatory factors and influencing CSF spontaneous activity. Previous clinical studies linked inflammatory response activation to delirium onset, with serum inflammatory factors (IL-6, TNF, monocyte chemoattractants) and fibrinogen/acute-phase proteins (e.g., CRP) associated with delirium risk (Elmaksoud et al. 2022; Sameh et al. 2023).

Preventive measures for POD, including analgesic agents (Gibb et al. 2020), local analgesic approaches (Hshieh et al. 2020), and timely medical interventions, improve clinical outcomes by alleviating postoperative pain, enhancing sleep quality, and reducing serum inflammatory factors (Xiaofen et al. 2020); for elderly hip fracture patients, FICB offers superior rest and movement analgesia, promotes rapid short-term hip function recovery, and avoids increased postoperative complications (Hao et al. 2022), with safer blockade achievable via ultrasound-guided neurovascular imaging when equipment and time allow (Ertürk et al. 2021). Our findings showed early local analgesic interventions, particularly PO-FICB, effectively suppressed inflammatory mediators, relieved pain, and improved mental status; serum inflammatory mediator levels trended downward on post-admission days 1–3, with the PO-FICB group having lower IL-1, CRP, IL-6, and TNF-α than the IO-FICB group. These changes may alleviate hypoglycemia, increase CSF glucose levels, and enhance spontaneous activity, conferring a beneficial effect on POD (Skelly et al. 2019; Kealy et al. 2020).

FICB, an effective pain management intervention, plays a significant role in POD prevention; PO-FICB showed more significant VAS score reduction than IO-FICB on days 1–2, though this trend reversed on day 3 likely due to the block’s short analgesic duration. Regarding the key clinical indicator MMSE, the PO-FICB group had higher scores than the IO-FICB group on post-admission days 1–3, further supporting our hypotheses (Skelly et al. 2019; Kealy et al. 2020). For elderly hip fracture patients, PO-FICB is a safe, simple, and rapidly effective pain management approach that may reduce pain, mitigate inflammation, and improve cognitive function to lower POD incidence; it has gained increasing acceptance among patients due to consistent pain control, reduced systemic analgesia needs, and no obvious complications (Okereke and Abdelmonem 2021).

We acknowledge limitations of this study: the retrospective design introduces potential biases from unavoidable confounding factors; the small sample size, short observation period, and once-daily CAM assessments (standardized by experienced surgeons per retrospective feasibility) may compromise outcome reliability and POD mechanism exploration, with future prospective studies needing more frequent assessments to improve detection sensitivity; no adjustment for multiple comparisons (MMSE, VAS, four inflammatory markers over three days) carries Type I error risk; and despite comparable demographic data, selection bias may be underestimated. Our findings thus require validation in larger multicenter randomized controlled trials with longer follow-up.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study suggests that PO-FICB may be associated with a lower incidence of POD than IO-FICB in elderly patients with hip fractures. This observation could potentially relate to PO-FICB’s early putative effects of attenuating systemic inflammation and exerting putative neuroprotective-like actions as well as analgesic effects. The potential analgesic benefits of PO-FICB may support its consideration as part of multimodal perioperative strategies, with a focus on mitigating neuroinflammation and optimizing pain management. These findings align with the evolving paradigms of perioperative neuroprotection in geriatric care, underscoring their preliminary value for informing clinical decision-making.

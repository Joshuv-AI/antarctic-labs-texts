How does eFangelism trigger hedonic consumption in sports? The mediating role of fan engagement through social networking sites
Authors: Mehmet Ali Horozoğlu, Ozan Korkmaz
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe power of sports, which plays an important role in bringing modern societies together, in shaping the behavior of individuals cannot be ignored. The role of this power in individuals’ consumption behavior is worth examining. The current study examined the mediating role of fan engagement through social networking sites in the relationship between Sports eFangelism and hedonic consumption in sports. MethodsThe participants were football fans in Türkiye (N = 495). The Sport eFangelism Scale, Fan Engagement through Social Networking Sites Scale, and Hedonic Consumption Attitude Scale in Sports were used to collect the data. ResultsThe study results showed that fan engagement through social networking sites has a mediating role in the relationship between Sports eFangelism and hedonic consumption in sports. In addition, hedonic consumption in sports has a significant positive relationship with Sports eFangelism and fan engagement through social networking sites.ConclusionsThis study reveals that Sports eFangelism is positively associated with hedonic consumption in sports, and that fan engagement through social networking sites plays a significant mediating role in this relationship. These findings emphasize the importance of digital fan communities in shaping sports consumption behaviors.Keywords: EFangelism, Hedonic consumption in sport, Fan engagement through social networking sites, Football fans

Introduction

Sport is an important component of modern society that brings people together and plays a strategic role in determining people's social roles. With the development of sports in society, consumption behaviors in sports are also shaped. Fans have made great contributions to this shaping [1]. The relationship between sports sciences and psychological behaviors has become particularly evident in fans' interactions with sporting events. The concept of a fan has emerged as an important psychological process that strengthens individuals' identities and social ties, and participation in sporting events positively affects individuals' mental health and behavioral responses [2].

The intense interest and support of fans have led to an increase in the income of sports clubs within the economic framework [90]. This also allows the income generated to be used as a tool for the development of sports again [3]. Fans are important sports components that benefit sports clubs in different systems [91]. They contribute to the economic sustainability of clubs. Clubs' revenues, such as ticket sales, club products and media rights, are largely directly related to the interest of fans [4].

Sports trigger and enhance emotional responses that are unique to consumers’ behavioral patterns [5]. The sports consumer has a unique position in the consumer typology [6]. Sports consumers differ from other consumer groups by exhibiting a wide range of values, attitudes and behaviors [7]. The motivations that lead fans to consume sports products can be grouped into three main categories: psychological, sociocultural, and individual beliefs. These motivations involve various factors, such as education, income level, gender, origin, and religion, and play important roles in increasing the loyalty of fans to their team [5].

Sports consumers, in other words, fans, generally accept the team as a part of their identity and identify with their team, perceiving the team's successes as their personal successes and the team's failures as their personal failures [8]. One of the most basic ways that sports fans show their loyalty to their teams is by purchasing team products [9]. Fans renew their sense of loyalty to their teams when they purchase team products [10]. In other words, fans who identify with the team more often exhibit consumption behaviors such as attending matches, following the media, and purchasing products, regardless of the team's performance [11, 12].

Theoretical background

Hedonic consumption in sports

Hedonic consumption is a system that focuses on the emotional experiences that consumers have while using products [13]. Hedonic consumption is based on the emotional aspects of product use, and these experiences are often aesthetic or entertainment oriented [14]. Therefore, hedonic consumption is a system that focuses on consumers' search for internal pleasure. Aesthetic pleasure and emotional reactions play important roles in this system [15].

Hedonic shopping behaviors carry aesthetic value ​​and are similar to the orientation of utilitarian shopping motives [14]. In line with these orientations, sensory concepts such as tasks, entertainment, fun, fantasy, and sensory stimulation that reflect hedonic satisfaction are encountered in hedonic consumption processes [15]. Therefore, hedonic consumption increases the positive mood of consumers [92]. Consumers with increased positive moods exhibit attitudes and behaviors such as pleasure, fantasy, and fun [16].

Hedonic products in the hedonic consumption system are multisensory, pleasant, and fun [17]. These fun and pleasant products appeal to consumers' senses and at the same time give them a feeling of extreme pleasure [13]. The main reason for this feeling of pleasure and fun is that hedonic products are considered more worthy of appreciation than are utilitarian products, which are considered instrumental [18].

The positive mood that occurs as a result of individuals consuming sports products and services is described as hedonic consumption in sports [93]. It is suggested that fans are more likely to consume products that help them maintain their mood, as their mood improves to positive after they participate in sporting events [19]. In this way, symbolic values ​​associated with relevant sporting events reveal hedonic consumption in sports. Similarly, if the outcome of a sporting event creates or contributes to a negative mood, consumers tend to develop similar types of consumption that support the enjoyment and modification of the negative mood [20]. Therefore, fans may turn to hedonic consumption in every outcome of sporting events.

Hedonic consumption in sports and eFangelism

The concept of Sports eFangelism, first introduced by Dwyer et al. [21], is an extension of brand evangelism [22]. Brand evangelism occurs when individuals spread the most popular aspects of a brand to different people [94]. Within the framework of this spread, consumers exhibit passionate behaviors regarding their brands [94]. As a result of these passionate behaviors, they share their feelings and passions with other people [95]. These individuals, who want other people to feel the passionate emotions they feel, serve as free promotional ambassadors for brands [23].

Sports eFangelism is defined as the positive behaviors exhibited by fans towards their supported sports teams [24]. In this context, fans passionately support their teams. Not only do they support them, but they also feel a deep sense of admiration and loyalty towards their teams [25]. Within this framework, eFangelism is a form of verbal defense system where fans respond to negative statements targeting their team with positive content [96]. Thus, through the strong sense of belonging and attachment to the teams, the social value of the teams increases over time [24].

eFangelism is conceptualized as an advanced and passionate fan behavior within the framework of sports marketing [25]. This concept encompasses the strong relationship between a sports team and its fans. In this context, eFangelism is shaped by the fans' beliefs and their interactions with the team [26]. At the core of these beliefs and interactions is the strong emotional bond established between the team and the fans [24]. Due to the influence of this bond, fans tend to defend their team's superiority. In line with this tendency, fans gradually begin to communicate with their teams through external means [96].

Dwyer et al. [21] argue that, on the basis of advocates’ eFangelistic behavior, eFangelism operates as a four-dimensional structure. These dimensions are defined as advocating, advertising, antagonizing, and assimilating [96]. In advocating, fans internalize their teams and defend every behavior and strategy of their teams, similar to lawyers. In advertising, fans operate in a system where they praise their club's products by using them in their workplaces, homes, and social circles [96]. In addition, fans support this behavior by gifting club products to their friends in their social circle [26]. Another dimension, antagonizing, includes the provocative behaviors that individuals display toward their friends who do not have the same positive views about their team or their club [21]. The last dimension, assimilating, represents outward group assimilation behaviors, such as communication behaviors with special meetings and clothing choices in the environment of friends on match days [26].

Hedonic consumption refers to individuals consuming not only to meet their needs but also to provide pleasure, enjoyment and emotional satisfaction [13]. Sports is one of the areas where this type of consumption behavior is most evident. Fans gain hedonic satisfaction by watching sports, purchasing team products, or attending sporting events [27]. These consumption behaviors enable individuals to embrace their commitment to sporting events and the cultural elements surrounding these events. Evangelism can be defined as the effort of individuals to spread a certain belief, ideology, or product [28]. Sports fans create a form of “sports evangelism” by passing on their devotion to their teams to others [26]. In this context, the results that individuals receive from increasing their commitment to a particular sports culture and their tendency to spread this culture to others may contribute to their enjoyment, pleasure, and emotional satisfaction in sports (21). This enjoyment, pleasure, and emotional satisfaction can be related to enjoyment, pleasure, and emotional satisfaction in hedonic consumption in sports (21).

When the literature is examined, consumption experiences are combined with social sharing. In particular, sports fans' desire to share their experiences with others plays a role in reinforcing both their consumption behavior and their social identity [29]. The intensity of the level of emotional attachment that fans display toward their teams is an important factor in the consumption of team products [30]. Fans demonstrate their commitment to team culture not only as advocates but also as consumers of it [26]. Therefore, fans who identify with their teams are expected to be defined as sports consumers and will engage in purchasing behaviors related to their teams' products [31]. On this basis, it can be argued that fans' loyalty to their teams, i.e., eFangelism, may be related to increased hedonic consumption in sports. To test this, we propose the following hypothesis.

H 1: eFangelism has a positive direct effect on hedonic consumption in sports.

Fan engagement through social networking sites as a mediator

Social media is a system that allows people to connect, interact, and produce content [32]. While people can communicate with different individuals on social media, they can also share some content [33]. In this way, people can gather around a thought by creating an online community and network [34]. Social media, which is becoming increasingly popular, makes it easier for individuals and groups to reach large audiences and share information [35].

Social media is a powerful tool that allows athletes and sports organizations to communicate directly with their fans [97]. Fans have the opportunity to interact with teams, athletes, and other fans through social media platforms [36]. Fans can make their opinions known to the world through fan forums and fan blogs [37]. Social media and new media platforms allow fans to stay connected with other fans even when watching a match alone [36].

In sports environments, fan loyalty or fan engagement represents the steadfast devotion an individual exhibits toward a particular team [98]. Within the framework of this determined commitment, individuals shape their thoughts and behaviors within the framework of this commitment [38]. Especially in sports environments, fan loyalty is defined as the loyalty a person exhibits toward the team that he/she is a fan of. In addition, this loyalty affects individuals' thoughts and behaviors [39]. Within the framework of these thoughts and behaviors, fans buy club products and show loyalty to them [38].

In fan engagement, individuals exhibit certain behaviors, such as attending their team's events, purchasing club products, reading sports magazines, defending their team when it wins, and making statements that eliminate the team's bad performance [26].In these behaviors, the interaction of fans with clubs is revealed [40]. In organizations that affect the whole world, such as sports, social media plays a very important role in connecting fans with clubs [41]. Therefore, social media significantly changes the interaction and consumption of sports [42]. As a result of the changes it offers, there is also the opportunity to develop personal relationships between the brand and the fans [40]. Thus, the level of engagement and emotional intensity of fans toward their preferred teams and unity among fans emerge [40].

Hunt et al. [43] define a fan as an enthusiastic devotion to a team or a sporting object. This devotion therefore makes fans attached to a sports object or a sports-related brand. In fans, this devotion is revealed through certain behaviors [98]. Although behavior and attitudes may vary from person to person, the focus is always on the instinct of devotion to teams or sports objects [44]. Fan engagement is permanent and continues independently of variables. In addition, fan engagement affects individuals' thoughts and behaviors. In line with this effect, fans carry out their attitudes and behaviors within cognitive processes and become conscious of both engagement and consumption behaviors [39].

Social media has become an important platform for fans to display their loyalty and spread that loyalty to a wider audience [90, 133,]. Fans maintain this loyalty by sharing content about the teams or athletes they support, interacting with other fans, and through the communities they create on social media[115, 117]This loyalty ceases to be an individual choice and becomes a culture that spreads to large masses through social media and can influence the consumption behavior of fans [121]. The spread of fans' interest in sports-related products, services and experiences via social media may increase hedonic consumption behaviors in sport[121–123].Fans can spread their engagement to their teams on social media while also reinforcing this loyalty through their consumption habits[97] In the literature, social media platforms play an important role in creating unity and bonding between fans and the clubs they support [45, 46, 98, 100,] argued that media consumption mediates team identification and emotional interaction. It is suggested that word-of-mouth communication and repeat purchasing behaviors are shaped by this mediating role [133–135]. On this basis, fan engagement in real life can be carried over to social media and lead to hedonic consumption in sports, which results in significant emotional interaction among fans. Therefore, we expect that fan engagement through social networking sites will act as a mediator in the link between eFangelism and hedonic consumption in sports, which leads to Hypothesis H2.

H 2: Fan engagement through social networking sites has a mediating role in the relationship between eFangelism and hedonic consumption in sports.

The present study

Hedonic consumption refers to the tendency of individuals to consume for pleasure, enjoyment and emotional satisfaction beyond meeting their physical needs [13]. This type of consumption is seen as an important element in the sports industry that reinforces the emotional attachment of fans to their teams. Fans have the opportunity to interact with teams, athletes, and other fans they support through social media platforms and create a digital community in this context [36]. This attachment encourages similar emotional investments from other fans through digital interactions. Global sports goods revenue is expected to reach approximately $105 billion by 2023 (47.) This revenue highlights the significant demand for licensed sports products such as team jerseys, apparel, and other branded products [47]. In terms of ticket sales, the global sports event ticket market is valued at $20.5 billion in 2024, and forecasts suggest that it will grow to $45.6 billion by 2033, reflecting a compound annual growth rate of 13.7% [48]. These statistics highlight the robust nature of sports consumption globally. Therefore, the importance of examining sports consumption behaviors and behavioral attitudes among fans is critical. Research has shown that highly engaged fans spend more money and provide financial support for their clubs [49–51]. Therefore, eFangelism, fan engagement through social networking sites, and hedonic consumption in sports are critically important for understanding the emotional, social, and consumption processes of fans.

Football fandom in Türkiye is a phenomenon deeply rooted in the country's culture, hosting one of the most passionate and fanatic fan communities in the world [150, 151]. The high emotional attachment that Turkish fans have to their teams gradually transforms into a lifelong loyalty relationship; this is concretized through supportive behaviors such as purchasing match tickets and acquiring licensed products from the club [152]. Moreover, fans also demonstrate similar forms of belonging in digital environments; they intensively share their support for their teams on social media platforms and continue this support in a sustained manner [26, 153]. In this context, considering the strong fan culture in Türkiye, this research offers valuable contributions to both the literature and practice, providing an important framework for understanding the reflections of fan behaviors in social and digital contexts.

This research examines in depth the hedonic consumption behaviors of football fans in Türkiye, their eFangelism attitudes toward sports, and their fan loyalty on social media platforms. Today, club fandom has emerged as a distinct form of social identity in terms of the space it occupies in individuals' lives and the consequences it produces [52]. In Türkiye, football has had great success as an important part of social personality [97]. Twentify [53] has shown that a significant portion of the 18 and over age group is heavily committed to teams. According to the data obtained from the research conducted in the Türkiye sample, 25.9 million individuals over the age of 18 support Galatasaray, 20.3 million Fenerbahçe, and 13.5 million Beşiktaş football clubs. Thus, the total number of fans of the three major Turkish teams is 59.7 million people. This number is quite high and important for Türkiye, a country with a population of over 85 million [54]. Therefore, fans represent an important segment in Türkiye in terms of clubs and the sports industry. Within the framework of these data, the most important contribution of fans to their teams is their consumption behaviors on match days [100]. According to the Türkiye 2023 football season report published by Aktif Bank 8 [55], fans spend approximately 85 million euros annually on licensed products. These data clearly reveal the intense interest of football fans in Türkiye in licensed products and the importance of these products in the clubs' revenue models. The engagement levels of fans in Türkiye are also an important factor in this high consumption. In fan research conducted in Türkiye, it has been determined that psychological commitment affects fans' behaviors, such as purchasing club products and following their clubs through the media, both locally and nationally [56].

This research offers an original contribution to the fields of sports and psychology because it addresses concepts such as hedonic consumption, eFangelism, and fan engagement through social networking sites. Another important strength of the study is that it presents findings to both disciplines and is pioneering in terms of the topics it addresses. In this context, this study aims to reveal how the psychological processes behind fans' consumption habits in sports are shaped. Thus, this study aims to provide a new perspective for research on sports marketing and fan behavior [27]. Within the framework of all these explanations, the aim of this study is to examine the effects of eFangelism and fan engagement through social networking sites on hedonic consumption in sports. Method

Participants

Participants were recruited through posts shared in both official and unofficial football fan groups on Instagram and X (formerly Twitter), where football supporters in Türkiye are actively engaged. The recruitment message clearly stated that the study was part of an academic research project, that participation was voluntary, and that only active football fans were eligible to take part. A Google Forms link to the questionnaire was included in the announcement, and group administrators were kindly asked to share this link periodically on their fan pages and encourage their followers to complete the survey.

To verify fan status, a filter question was presented to participants before starting the questionnaire: “In the past three months, have you watched at least one match of the team you support, either live or via broadcast?” Only those who answered “Yes” were included in the study sample, while those who answered “No” were excluded. This procedure was implemented to ensure that the sample accurately reflected a current football fan profile relevant to the scope of the study.

The participants of the research are football fans in Türkiye (N = 495). A total of 263 (53.1%) of the participants were male, and 232 (46.9%) were female. The ages of the participants ranged from 18–59 years. The average age of the participants was 25.09 years. (SD = 8.45). The time participants spend on social media platforms daily varies between 30 and 600 min, with an average of 177.37 min (approximately 3 h) (SD = 105.31). The number of licensed products purchased by participants for their favorite team varies between 0 and 50 (Mean = 4.28, SD = 6.75). A total of 52.3% of the participants (N = 259) stated that they bought licensed products from their favorite team and gave them to their close circle as gifts. The remaining participants (47.7%, N = 236) stated that they did not gift their favorite team's licensed products to their close circle.

Measures

Sport eFangelism scale (SFS)

The SFS was developed by Dwyer et al. [21] to assess sport team (brand) evangelism. The Turkish adaptation of the SFS was performed by Yüksekbilgili (2017). The Turkish form of the SFS consists of 12 items and one dimension. It is scored on a 5-point Likert scale (1: Strongly disagree to 5: Strongly agree). As the total score from the scale increases, individuals' sports team evangelism (eFangelism) increases. From the data obtained within the scope of this study, the Cronbach’s alpha internal consistency coefficient for the total score of the scale was calculated as 0.93.

Fan engagement through social networking sites scale (FETSWSS)

The FETSWSS was developed by Santos et al. [58] to assess fan engagement through social networking sites. The Turkish adaptation of the scale was performed by Özsoy and Karlı [59]. The FETSWSS consists of three subdimensions and 11 items. The subdimensions are as follows: fan-to-fan relationship, team-to-fan relationship, and fan cocreation. The Turkish version of the scale uses a 5-point Likert-type scale (1: strongly disagree to 5: strongly agree). As the total score from the scale increases, individuals' fan engagement levels through social networking sites increase. From the data obtained within the scope of this study, the Cronbach’s alpha internal consistency coefficient for the total score of the scale was calculated as 0.96. The Cronbach’s alpha internal consistency coefficients calculated for the subdimensions are as follows: the fan-to-fan relationship is 0.92, the team-to-fan relationship is 0.94, and fan cocreation is 0.93.

Hedonic consumption attitude scale in sports (HCASS)

The HCASS was developed by Tutar [60] to assess the hedonic consumption levels of shopping habits made by shopping enthusiasts interested in sports. The scale consists of three subdimensions and 18 items. The subdimensions are as follows: interest, purchase, and inner motivation. The HCASS is a 5-point Likert-type scale (1: Strongly disagree to 5: Strongly agree). As the total score from the scale increases, individuals' hedonic consumption attitudes increase. From the data obtained within the scope of this study, the Cronbach’s alpha internal consistency coefficient for the total score of the scale was calculated as 0.95. The Cronbach’s alpha internal consistency coefficients calculated for the subdimensions are as follows: interest is 0.93, purchase is 0.88, and inner motivation is 0.90.

Procedures and ethics

The participants of the study were reached through communication groups on social media platforms. The participants who agreed to participate in the research were then sent the link of the service provider that hosted the online version of the scales (Google forms). Before the scales were filled out, an informed consent page was presented to the participants. This page contains information about the purpose and scope of the research. Participants who approved the informed consent form were included in the study and answered the measurement tools. No other information was requested from the participants other than the information used in the research. The Helsinki Declaration of 1975 was used as the basis for conducting the research.

Data analysis

In order to minimize the potential impact of Common Method Variance (CMV) and address potential methodological biases in the study, several methodological steps were implemented. In addition to collecting data across multiple time points, different data sources were utilized, and efforts were made to ensure anonymity to reduce social desirability bias. Furthermore, an attempt was made to reach multiple participant groups with diverse demographic characteristics (e.g., age, gender, educational background). This approach aimed to enhance the diversity of the sample and reduce the risk of methodological bias. These efforts were designed to mitigate the likelihood of CMV and increase the reliability of the obtained findings.

Before the analyses, the distribution of the scores used in the research was examined. In the distribution of scores, kurtosis and skewness between ± 1.5 were taken into account as criteria [61]. When the kurtosis and skewness values obtained were examined, the distribution of the scores was normal (Table 1). The relationships between the variables used in the research were examined via Pearson correlation analysis. A structural equation model (SEM) was used to examine the research model. In the SEM, χ2/df < 5; CFI, GFI, and TLI > 0.90; and RMSEA < 0.10 were considered criteria [61, 62]. Bootstrapping tests were carried out to examine whether fan engagement mediated the relationship between eFangelism and hedonic consumption [63]. Bootstrapping analysis was used to determine the mediating effect of fan engagement. In this analysis, a 95% confidence interval and a 10.000 resampling method were used. Analyses were carried out via the IBM SPSS 25 and AMOS Graphics 24 programs.

Discussion

In this study, structural equation modeling was used to examine the extent to which football fans' hedonic consumption in sports is predicted by their eFangelist attitudes and fan engagement through social networking sites. According to the findings of the present research, the eFangelist behavior of fans had a positive effect on their hedonic consumption in sports. In addition, fans' eFangelist behavior has a positive effect on fan engagement through social networking sites. Finally, in this study, fan engagement through social networking sites was found to play a mediating role in the relationship between eFangelism and hedonic consumption behaviors. Consistent with previous studies, our findings indicate that fans’ eFangelist behaviors significantly enhance their hedonic consumption tendencies, and that social media interaction plays a mediating role in this relationship. These results suggest that fan loyalty, social media engagement, and emotional attachment collectively strengthen fans’ inclination toward consumption [121, 136–138, 140, 141].

Existing studies within the framework of sports sciences and fandom predominantly focus on core attitudes such as fan loyalty and affection [22, 98, 142, 143]. Despite its relevance to the sports industry, the concept of eFANgelism has received limited attention in academic research [123]. In this regard, our study offers a significant theoretical contribution to the literature on fan behavior by highlighting eFANgelism—a term used to describe fans’ brand advocacy behaviors.

Moreover, existing literature on fandom reveals that the relationship between fans’ level of eFANgelism and their hedonic consumption tendencies has not been sufficiently explored. Most researchers have primarily focused on the structure or nature of fans’ eFANgelist behaviors [19, 26, 123, 144, 145]. In this context, the current study contributes to the expansion of these two concepts within the field of sports sciences by examining the relationship between eFANgelism and hedonic consumption among fans who hold a significant emotional and behavioral attachment to their teams.

The findings of the present study are that fans' eFangelist behaviors positively predict hedonic consumption in sports. Therefore, H1 was supported. Fans who are loyal to their teams are unique consumers [64, 65]. eFangelist behaviors enable fans to identify with their teams more easily [20]. Research shows that team identification is positively related to the social media experiences of sports media consumers [66, 101–105]. Sports team fans identify with their teams, providing personal attachment and the opportunity to experience the team's successes and failures on their own [67]. Fans who identify highly with their team engage more in team-related consumption activities [11, 68, 106–108]. In other words, fans with strong team identification are more likely to consume team-related products (participation, media, products, etc.), regardless of team performance [11, 12].

The more an individual feels connected to a team, the more valuable the team's brand becomes to that person [69]. A positive mood among fans can contribute to the display of hedonic consumption behaviors related to that team [20]. Consumption within the framework of sports contributes to the positive mood of consumers and meets their social needs [70, 71, 72, 110, 111,]. While social needs affect the motivation of fans to be loyal to their teams, this motivation has a positive effect on meaningful consumption behaviors [72, 111]. Mahony et al. [73] reported that fans with high levels of commitment are more likely to attend matches and purchase team products. Similarly, Kural and Özbek's [74] study revealed that a high level of loyalty to a team has the potential to have a positive effect on consumer behavior in sports.

Hedonic consumption refers to the consumption of products or experiences for the purposes of pleasure, enjoyment, and emotional satisfaction [13, 146], whereas eFANgelism describes internalized behaviors in which fans, driven by a deep emotional attachment to their teams, act as advocates and promoters of their team [21, 26]. The findings of our study reveal that fans’ eFANgelist behaviors positively influence their hedonic consumption related to sports. This result indicates that the common ground between these two concepts lies in emotional attachment and internalized processes—both of which motivate fan behavior. In this context, the study offers a new perspective on the relationship between eFANgelism and hedonic consumption by examining it through the lens of emotional processes.

Another finding of the research is that fans' eFangelist behavior positively predicts fan engagement through social networking sites. Dedicated sports fans are the backbone of professional sports teams and play a critical role in the financial sustainability and long-term prosperity of the sports industry. These dedicated fans shape the spirit and future of the sport, shaping success both on the pitch and in the financial arena [75]. Sports team loyalty refers to deep psychological attachment that results in positive behaviors and attitudes toward a team [112, 113]. This attachment encompasses both attitude formation and behavioral consistency, representing the highest level of reflection of brand loyalty in the sports arena [76–78]. The behavioral fan loyalty of fans is not only evaluated by attending matches or purchasing team products. Fans' behavioral loyalty also includes their consumption of media channels [73, 79, 80]. Fans' use of social media is seen as a way to show loyalty to their team [80, 81]. Hazari [82] argued that fans with high levels of fan engagement engage more with their teams’ content on social media. The sense of belonging that fans feel toward their teams leads them to consume content on social media platforms [84, 115, 116,]. In addition, fans spend much of their time on social media platforms on the basis of their sense of belonging and constantly share news about their team with others [116, 117].Fan behaviors displayed on social media platforms also enhance fans' sense of belonging[115]. Thus, fans use social media platforms to feel close to their teams [114, 117, 118]In this social media usage process, fans produce content about their teams and players on social networks and share this content with different groups or individuals [84, 117, 120].

Another finding of our research is that fan engagement through social networking sites has a mediating role in the relationship between fans' eFangelist behavior and their hedonic consumption in sports. According to this finding, as the eFangelist behavior of fans increases, fan engagement through social networking sites also increases. This increase enables fans to increase their hedonic consumption of team products. Therefore, H2 was supported. Individuals' engagement with their clubs on social media fosters pleasure-driven consumption behaviors, and active participation in online team communities strengthens their identification with the team, which in turn supports hedonic consumption behaviors such as match attendance and product purchases. [85, 106, 121–123]. One of the important factors that shapes the consumption perceptions of fans is emotional attachment [85, 125, 126]. One of the important platforms where this engagement has been demonstrated and increased is social media [86, 124]. As Fernandes et al. [87] stated, effective advertising content created on social media platforms encourages hedonic purchasing behavior by creating a positive mood in consumers[127–130].Fan engagement and emotional attachment are increasing owing to social media[86, 97, 115]. Wakefield [88] argued that the passionate engagement of fans is a key determinant of behaviors exhibited on social media. Strong group solidarities displayed on social media platforms to increase interaction between fans and sports clubs are considered potential tools for consumption in sports [89, 131]. In light of all this literature, eFangelism enables fans to express their passion and loyalty toward their clubs through social media, and this form of expression increases the hedonic consumption behavior of fans toward club products and services.

A key theoretical contribution of this current study lies in the identification of social media engagement as a mediating variable in the relationship between eFANgelism and hedonic consumption. This result can be interpreted in line with the Stimulus-Organism-Response (S–O-R) theoretical model, which aims to explain individuals’ behavioral responses—positive or negative—based on their perceptions and emotional reactions to external stimuli [148, 149]. In this framework, eFANgelist behavior can be conceptualized as an internal motivation or stimulus, social media engagement as the organismic response, and hedonic consumption as the resulting behavioral outcome. Indeed, a recent study by Chen et al. [121] supports a similar structure in the sports context, showing that social media usage fully mediates the effect of fan curiosity on impulsive buying behavior—highlighting the critical role of digital interaction in shaping consumer behavior [121].

The findings of the present study demonstrate that fans’ eFANgelist behaviors influence hedonic consumption both directly and indirectly. This can be attributed to the fact that eFANgelist fans often form an intense, emotionally driven attachment to their teams [123, 136]. The literature notes that highly committed, passionate fans go beyond conventional fan behavior by exhibiting extraordinary support—such as attending matches and purchasing licensed products with great enthusiasm [25, 154, 155]. In fact, higher levels of hedonic pleasure derived from sports experiences have been found to significantly increase fans’ intention and frequency to attend matches [156]. Similarly, brand evangelists are known to make significant expenditures on brands to which they feel a deep emotional connection. They enthusiastically recommend the brand to others, support it voluntarily across multiple channels, and engage in positive word-of-mouth due to their strong emotional bond [157]. All of these findings suggest that eFANgelist fans do not merely interact through social media but also engage directly in consumption experiences as a means of reinforcing their own pleasure and attachment. This type of partial mediation has also been observed in other areas of the literature. For example, Rathi and Jain [139] found that the effect of memetic marketing activities on purchase intention was partially mediated by customer engagement, while the direct effect of marketing efforts remained significant as well [139].

Conclusion

This study examined the extent to which football fans' hedonic consumption in sports is predicted by their eFangelist attitudes and fan engagement through social networking sites. The results of the research reveal that fans' eFangelist behaviors positively affect hedonic consumption in sports and that this relationship is further strengthened through fan engagement through social networking sites. Fans' display of engagement with their teams on social media increases their hedonic consumption in sports, and this process emerges as a critical factor shaping fans' emotional attachment and consumption habits. These findings provide important clues for sports clubs to optimize their social media strategies and fan interactions. The fan engagement displayed on social media platforms has the potential to not only increase fan loyalty and engagement but also encourage consumption behaviors toward club products and services.

Limitations and future research

This study has several limitations. Owing to these limitations, suggestions can be made for future studies. The fact that the study group was conducted in only one country reduces the generalizability of the results. In the future, similar comparative studies can be conducted in countries and cultures where there is increasing interest in football. Another limitation is that the study is cross-sectional. Caution should be exercised when considering the results. The quantitative data were used in the present study. To gain a deeper understanding of the relationships between the variables used in the current study, a future study can be designed that will also utilize qualitative data. Longitudinal studies can be designed to determine whether relationships change over time. This study was conducted only on football fans. Studies investigating similar relationships can be conducted among fan groups in different sports fields, and comparisons can be made according to sports branches.

The levels of polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons in traditional cigarettes and E-cigarettes in Saudi Arabia markets: a comparative risk assessment study
Authors: Adel M. Alshutairi, Ahmed H. Alzahrani, Abdulaziz M. Almontshry
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTwo validated and effective extraction techniques have been developed to ascertain the presence of polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) in traditional cigarettes and E-cigarettes. PAHs are a class of chemicals that occur naturally, and these contaminants may cause cancer and other harmful effects in human beings. Traditional cigarettes and E-cigarettes contain many PAHs.MethodIn terms of linearity, limit of detection, limit of quantification, accuracy (%), and precision (%), the two in-house lab setups for 7PAHs extraction from traditional cigarettes and E-cigarette were validated using high-performance liquid chromatography-fluorescence detection.ResultsThe concentration of Σ7PAHs for traditional cigarettes and E-cigarettes ranged from 6.23 ± 0.04 ng/cig to 75.61 ± 0.02 ng/cig, and non-detectable to 3.56 ± 2.71 ng/puff. The PAHs recovery in traditional cigarettes and E-cigarettes ranged from 92.74–110.45%, and 97.20–111.74%. The toxicity of equivalent (TEQs) of carcinogens revealed that traditional cigarettes brands have higher TEQs than E-cigarettes.ConclusionThis study highlights the detection of 7PAHs levels and its risk assessment in product brands for traditional cigarettes and E-cigarettes marketed in Saudi Arabia. The results suggested that the developed methods are a precise and versatile approach for measuring 7PAHs in traditional cigarettes and E-cigarettes.

Introduction

Cigarette smoking is harmful to one’s health since it has been linked to the genesis of many ailments, including lung cancer, cardiovascular disease, stroke, and asthma, among others [1]. Tobacco usage is one of the major threats to public health, and smoking is a leading avoidable cause of death across the globe [2]. In the United States, smoking traditional cigarettes smoke kills around 480,000 people each year, accounting for approximately 90% (or 9 out of 10) of all lung cancer fatalities, Lung cancer kills more women than breast cancer each year, accounting for about 80% (or 8 out of 10) of all fatalities from chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) [3]. In 2011, the United States and the European Union prohibited cigarettes that did not comply with fire safety standards, citing cigarette smoking as a consistent cause of death related with private house fires [4]. Electronic cigarettes, sometimes referred to as E-cigarettes or E-vaporizers, are battery-powered devices that deliver nicotine, flavorings, and other compounds to the lungs through evaporation rather than combustion [5]. Companies that sell E-cigarettes often pitch them as being safer than traditional cigarettes since they do not burn tobacco. Nicotine is present in both traditional cigarettes/E-cigarettes [6]. In Saudi Arabia, it is illegal to sell cigarettes to minors. A pack of 20 cigarettes costs around $ 2.70 to $ 4.26. While the pricing of E-cigarettes device ranges from 42.66 to $ 133.33. The flavor of E-cigarettes costs roughly $21.33 for 60 mL, all these prices include tax. Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons are a broad class of chemical molecules that include two or more fused aromatic rings. They (PAHs) are created through a variety of residential and high-temperature processes in industry, which often include organic materials, and natural phenomena like volcanic eruptions [7]. The European Union and the USEPA have designated PAHs as the most significant class of cancer-causing priority pollutants [8]. PAHs are chemical molecules with two or more aromatic rings that do not breakdown spontaneously and have deleterious (carcinogenic) consequences when consumed in vivo [9]. Certain PAHs cause mutations, cancer, and birth defects, making them a major threat to human health [10]. More than 500 PAHs compounds have been identified in cigarette smoke [11]. The US Environmental Protection Agency designated 16 PAH species as priority due to their carcinogenic potential. Benz [a]anthracene (BaA), chrysene (CRY), benzo [b]fluoranthene (BbF), benzo [k]fluoranthene (BkF), benzo [a]pyrene (BaP), dibenz [a,h]anthracene (DahA), and benzo [g,h,i]perylene (BghiP) are particularly known to have Geno. Benzo[a]pyrene (BaP), is the most dangerous of the PAHs and is designated as a Category 1 carcinogen [12]. Furthermore, it has been linked to lung cancer when inhaled or used topically [13]. The United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) created a list of tobacco products and tobacco smoke ingredients that are toxic or possibly dangerous [14]. Studies available on extraction of PAHs traditional cigarettes/E-cigarettes in literature are few [15]. Limited research has been conducted regarding the analysis of polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) in traditional cigarettes/E-cigarettes, encompassing several brands of cigarettes worldwide [16–18]. Furthermore, it is worth noting that there is a scarcity of scholarly material pertaining to the examination of polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) in both traditional cigarettes/E-cigarettes among Middle Eastern nations, including Saudi Arabia. Traditionally, a smoking machine is commonly employed for the purpose of extracting smoke from cigarettes. Nevertheless, this equipment are associated with high costs and necessitates substantial maintenance efforts [19]. Using two in-house laboratory methods that closely matches the smoking behavior of traditional cigarettes/E-cigarettes users, the purpose of this research was to determine the levels of PAHs that relate to the traditional cigarettes/E-cigarettes that smokers in Saudi Arabia regard to be prestigious. The information gathered might provide light on the amount to which cigarette smokers are exposed to PAHs and, by extension, the possible health problems that may arise from this exposure.

Experimental

Materials

All the reagents and materials employed were of analytical quality and required no further purification. Merck Chemicals provided HPLC grade solvents, methanol (MeOH), Water (H2O), hexane, n-hexane, dichloromethane (DCM), and acetonitrile (ACN). Purchased solid phase extraction (SPE) cartridge from (Chromafix Florisil, Germany). The standard stock solutions of PAHs ((BaA), (CRY), (BbF), (BkF), (BaP), (DahA), (BghiP)) (1.0 μg mL−1) were produced in ACN and kept at 4 °C in the refrigerator. Prior to usage, working solutions were newly created by diluting the stock solution to the different concentrations for calibration purpose.

Collection traditional cigarettes/E-cigarettes samples

Traditional cigarettes from seven commercially available brands were bought at different retail locations and coded I, II, III, IV, V, VI and VII. The tar and nicotine concentrations per cigarette varied between 1.0 and 10 mg and between 0.1 and 1.0 mg, respectively. Traditional cigarettes packs were stored in the laboratory prior to examination. The characteristics of the E-liquid (propylene glycol (PG)/vegetable glycerin (VG) ratio, flavor, and nicotine content, ranging from 10 to 50 mg per 30 mL). Commercial E-cigarettes samples (n = 7) were obtained from a variety of retail locations around Saudi Arabia and were coded VIII, IX, X, XI, XII, XIII, and XIV. Vapor samples from E-cigarettes were generated using an E-cigarettes vaping apparatus (Model RPM40, SMOK brand, China). All samples of traditional cigarettes/E-cigarettes were purchased in Jeddah, Makkah State, Saudi Arabia.

A vacuum pump was attached to the flask with a flow rate of 20 m3. Once the magnetic stirrer and vacuum pump were turned on, a lighter was used to ignite the E-cigarettes in the same way as a cigarette smoker. The E-cigarettes device was put on a rack in which the smoke created by pushing the E-cigarettes device’s button and drawn by a vacuum pump connected to the E-cigarettes device passes via a tube to the flask containing the solvent ACN. Clean-up process

The clean-up step is critical for reducing or removing any contaminants present in the eluate. A Florisil (6 mL, 100 mg, Thermoscientific) column was used for solid phase extraction (SPE), 2 mL of hexane was put down the column to activate it, and 8 mL of n-hexane: DCM (9:1) was used to elute the PAHs analytes of interest. The recovered eluate was dried in a vacuum chamber using a vacuum pump. Before analysis, the dried eluate was reconstituted with 1 mL ACN and kept in HPLC glass vials.

HPLC-FLD analysis

The seven PAHs were measured using an HPLC–FLD system (Agilent 1260 Infinity HPLC, Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA) coupled with an Agilent ZORBAX Eclipse Plus C18 column (4.6 mm 150 mm, 5 m; Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA) set to 40 °C. ACN and H2O were used as the mobile phase (50:50, v/v). A 10.0 μL aliquot of the extract was injected routinely under gradient conditions. A 23-min HPLC–FLD system was used for quantification, as reported in Table 3. Quality assurance/quality control (QA/QC)

Along with the smoke samples, a procedural blank was analyzed to detect any interference or contamination. The blank was created by emptying 5 mL of acetone into the extractor and leaving it for 5 minutes; it was subsequently analyzed for PAHs. Additionally, samples were analyzed in duplicate. The determination of signal suppression or enhancement of the analytes was achieved by comparing the slopes of calibration curves that were spiked with a concentration of 5 ng mL −1.

Statistical analysis

The data were compiled using Excel 2013, and statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS 19.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). The statistical technique of one-way analysis of variance (one-way ANOVA) was employed to investigate significant disparities between means at a significance level of 95% (P < 0.05). Prior to conducting the ANOVA test, the normality of the data was assessed using Levene’s test, while the homogeneity of variances was evaluated using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. The statistical tests employed to assess the presence of a significant disparity between the two means were the student t-test and z-test. Statistical significance was attributed to P values < 0.05.

Human health risk assessment

Toxic equivalent concentrations

Benzo[a]pyrene is a highly carcinogenic polycyclic aromatic compound [20]. The toxicities of PAHs in contaminated traditional cigarettes/E-cigarettes were assessed in this research by computing the toxicity value of each PAH using the set of toxicity equivalency factors (TEFs) defined by US EPA [21]. Reliability of analytical procedures

The analytical performance for the PAHs extraction, including linearity, LOD, LOQ, accuracy, and precision are presented in Table 4. Calibration curves for the seven PAHs were obtained from a series of standard solutions at six different concentrations (1.0, 2.5, 5.0, 10.0, 25.0 and 50.0 ng mL−1). Linearity (R2) indicates the suitability of the method for the estimation of PAHs in the matrices. The seven individual PAHs showed R2 > 0.999 in all matrix types. The LOD was 0.16–0.97 ng/cig in traditional cigarettes product brands, 0.03–0.27 ng/puff in E-cigarettes product brands, respectively. The corresponding LOQ values were 0.50–2.95 ng/cig, and 0.09–0.83 ng/puff, respectively. The accuracy (and precision) ranges of the PAHs for triplicate runs were 92.74–110.45% (0.62–6.32%) in traditional cigarettes product brands, and 97.20–111.74% (0.22–1.81%) in E-cigarettes product brands, respectively. Consequently, the analytical technique satisfies the requirements specified in SANTE/11813/2017 for the analytical quality assessment of PAHs, namely linearity (R2) > 0.99, an accuracy range of 70–120%, and precision (CV) < 20% [24]. Implication of the results

Previous investigations have shown PAHs in tobacco components and cigarette smoke [25, 26]. The current study’s findings are consistent with those [27] who evaluated PAHs compounds in tobacco products using gas chromatography. The researchers measured three PAHs compounds: 11.77 ± 4.50 ng/cig for BaP, 17.87 ± 4.46 ng/cig for chrysene, and 5.12 ± 1.42 ng/cig for benzo[b]fluoranthene. Their findings are equivalent to those of the current investigation. The two extraction techniques produced have advantages over routine analysis of cigarette smoking machines in terms of simplicity, user-friendliness, and cost-effectiveness [28]. The comparison between traditional cigarettes/E-cigarettes revealed, as shown in Fig. 3, that traditional cigarettes contain higher concentrations of seven PAHs compared to E-cigarettes. Traditional cigarettes consist of tobacco that is ignited to create smoke [14]. The smoke carries detrimental compounds such as tar, carbon monoxide, and different carcinogens [29]. Limitations of the study

Limited aspects should be acknowledged in this study. A notable limitation is the exclusive examination of PAHs in the smoke of traditional cigarettes and electronic cigarettes, with no direct analysis of the E-liquid or tobacco substrates. The rationale for this approach is that PAHs predominantly form during the combustion of tobacco and the heating process in electronic cigarettes [35–37]. Furthermore, the study was conducted with a relatively small sample size, encompassing 14 samples representing both traditional cigarettes and electronic cigarettes. Despite the limited sample size, we maintain that our result is sufficiently rigorous to provide valuable insights and a comprehensive profile of the 7 PAHs identified in the emissions from both traditional cigarettes and electronic cigarettes.

Conclusions

In this study, we developed an analytical two extraction methods for the determination of PAHs in traditional cigarettes/E-cigarettes by HPLC–FLD in seven product brands, and revealed its risk associated with PAHs. According to the data, traditional cigarettes had a greater concentration of 7PAHs than E-cigarettes. PAHs are created during the combustion of tobacco in traditional cigarettes smoke, causing smoking-related illnesses. In contrast, E-cigarettes make PAHs by heating a liquid, which is a safer and less hazardous process. Considering the accompanying health and environmental hazards, analytical methodologies for the regulation and monitoring of PAHs are of worldwide importance. The objective of this study was to establish two techniques to confirm the amounts of 7PAHs in traditional cigarettes/E-cigarettes. The two developed extraction systems at the in-house laboratory offered apparent benefits, such as simplicity, operability, cost-effectiveness, and high analytical performance. Furthermore, the study revealed that the potential health hazards faced by individuals who smoke may vary depending on the specific sort of cigarette they choose to consume. Most samples have different TEQ as well as 7PAHs content. The variability of the traditional cigarettes/E-cigarettes samples of their emission of PAHs. To identify the public health effects of cigarette smoking and the duration of the contribution of each polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons compounds, it is necessary to extensively monitor the different brands available in the local market and conduct exposure assessments.

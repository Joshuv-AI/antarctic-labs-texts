Field Epidemiological Investigation Training Demand Survey for Central and Local Epidemiological Investigation and Response Teams
Authors: Yoo-Yeon Kim, Mi Yu, Jin Lee, Ho Kyoung Song, Eun Kyoung Kim, 유연 김, 미 유, 진 이, 호경 송, 은경 김
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesThe Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency conducts simulation training to enhance the response capabilities of Epidemiological Investigation and Response Teams (ERTs). We analyzed training demands among Central and Local ERTs following nine scenario-based training sessions in 2025.MethodsA survey was administered to 269 participants: 97 training debriefing attendees on-site (November 28, 2025) and 172 Central ERTs online (December 1–5, 2025) using a self-developed questionnaire that covered respondent characteristics, training topics, and methods. Data were analyzed using descriptive statistics and group differences via chi-square or Fisher’s exact tests.ResultsThe preferred topics among the 133 respondents (response rate 49.4%; 95 Central, 38 Local ERTs; 74.4% female; median age 43.0; 5.0 years’ experience) were respiratory (41.4%) diseases and water- and food-borne diseases (33.8%), and responses to mass gathering events (48.9%) and disaster events (46.6%). There was a significantly higher demand for diseases of unknown origin, non-communicable diseases, and mass gathering responses among Central ERTs. Conversely, Local ERTs prioritized training for water- and food-borne diseases and educational/healthcare facility outbreaks. Risk communication (46.6%) and risk assessment (45.9%) were equally prioritized by both groups, and problem-based learning (PBL, 57.1%) and field-linked practical training (51.9%) were the preferred methodologies.ConclusionsGiven the differing preferences reported, future training should be tailored to reflect each team’s mission. Tailored scenarios should be developed that would enable Local ERTs to focus on community-level educational/healthcare facility outbreaks, while Central ERTs would concentrate on complex cases requiring coordination with the competent ministries or precise etiological investigations. Finally, it is necessary to maintain PBL methods and strengthen capabilities in areas like risk communication and assessment.Keywords: Epidemiological response, Simulation training, Problem-based learning, Needs assessment

Key messages

① What is known previously?

Scenario-based training is essential for Epidemiological Investigation and Response Teams (ERTs) to conduct rapid infectious disease response and etiological investigations effectively. In 2025, nine joint field trainings were held involving Central and Local ERTs and relevant ministries (Education, Defense, and Justice).

② What new information is presented?

Central and Local ERTs reported differing preferences. Central ERTs demanded scenarios requiring precise etiological investigations and multifaceted risk management for diseases of unknown origin, non-communicable diseases, and mass gatherings. Conversely, Local ERTs prioritized field-focused topics: water and food-borne diseases and educational/healthcare facility outbreaks.

③ What are implications?

Training should be tailored to reflect a given team’s mission. Tailored scenarios should enable Local ERTs to address community-level outbreaks (educational/healthcare facilities), whereas Central ERTs would concentrate on coordination with relevant ministries.

Introduction

The Infectious Disease Control and Prevention Act defines an epidemiological investigation as the activity of ascertaining the scale of infectious disease patients and others and tracing and analyzing the source of infection. In addition, it stipulates that these activities are intended to block infectious diseases and prevent their spread. An epidemiological investigation is a key response activity for identifying the cause and interrupting transmission in the early stages of an infectious disease outbreak, and its prompt and systematic execution is crucial [1,2]. To this end, the relevant statutes specify the operation of the Central Epidemiological Investigation and Response Teams (ERTs) and Local ERTs, composed of at least 30 members at the central government and at least 10 members each at local governments (city/province and municipal/county/district) [3]. This institutional framework is premised on infectious disease response being conducted through group-based collaboration among ERTs, and the expertise and joint response capacity of these teams are important in the infectious disease response process.

Epidemiological investigation personnel must have not only knowledge of infectious diseases but also the capacity to coordinate on-site situations and proactively perform joint response activities during team-based collaboration. To this end, the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency (KDCA) operates scenario-based call-up training using scenarios that simulate actual outbreak situations, as well as joint training with relevant ministries (the Ministry of National Defense, the Ministry of Education, and the Korea Correctional Service under the Ministry of Justice) and local governments. In 2023 and 2024, the Central ERTs call-up training was conducted twice a year as scenario-based simulation drills. The 2023 training scenarios covered outbreaks of human infection with avian influenza and infectious disease outbreaks during mass gathering events, while the 2024 scenarios covered a domestic measles outbreak associated with imported cases and the importation and subsequent community transmission of a novel infectious disease. In contrast, during the same period, joint training with the relevant ministries was conducted as lecture-based joint workshops to share joint response cases. For example, in 2024, the Ministry of National Defense covered measles and tuberculosis, while the Ministry of Education covered pertussis [4,5].

In 2025, to enhance the effectiveness of the training, participating organizations were expanded, and to strengthen the quality of training design and operation, customized scenarios simulating actual infectious disease outbreak sites were independently developed and applied. Based on this strategy, a total of nine sessions of field epidemiological investigation training were conducted as full-scale scenario-based simulation drills. Specifically, first, one session of the Central ERTs call-up training focused on a suspected Disease X situation in a healthcare facility. Second, a total of three joint drills with relevant ministries introduced ministry-specific customized scenarios: the Ministry of Education addressed respiratory infectious diseases in schools, the Ministry of National Defense addressed waterborne and foodborne infectious diseases in military units and local communities, and the newly joined Korea Correctional Service under the Ministry of Justice addressed suspected respiratory infectious disease situations in correctional facilities [6-8]. Third, training for Local ERTs was conducted on a regular basis, and the five Regional Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (RCDCP) conducted training using measles outbreak scenarios reflecting local conditions [9].

However, to enhance the field applicability of future training, it is necessary to review scenarios and operational approaches that reflect the practical demands of the training participants. Therefore, this study conducted a survey to identify the specific demands for training topics and methods.

Methods

1. Study Subjects and Survey

This study was conducted based on the results of a survey administered after the completion of field epidemiological investigation training. After the completion of a total of nine sessions of field epidemiological investigation training conducted in 2025, a survey was administered to 97 members of the Central ERTs and Local ERTs who attended the final report meeting and 172 members of the Central ERTs who did not attend the final report meeting (excluding the 16 members of the Central ERTs who attended the final report meeting out of a total of 188 members).

The survey was conducted online using Google Forms (Google LLC.) from November 28 to December 5, 2025. Attendees of the final report meeting (November 28, 2025) completed the survey on-site that day. For the Central ERTs members who did not attend the final report meeting, the same questionnaire was distributed and administered over a 5-day period (December 1 to 5, 2025). Prior to survey administration, an explanation of the purpose of the survey and the use of the data was provided, and informed consent to participate was obtained. No personally identifiable information was collected.

The questionnaire was developed by the research team to reflect the purpose and characteristics of field epidemiological investigation training. It consisted of 12 items across three domains. The respondents’ general characteristics domain assessed sex, age, affiliated institution, assigned duties, and work experience. The training topics domain consisted of topics on the type of infectious disease, outbreak situation (setting), and competencies related to infectious disease response to be included in future training, in order to identify specific training demands. The training methods domain included information on preferred training methods, training duration, and discussion group size and members.

2. Data Collection and Analysis

The survey was set to require responses to all items for submission to prevent missing responses. Descriptive statistical analysis of the collected data was performed using Microsoft Excel (version 16.0.14332.21016; Microsoft Corp.) to identify the general characteristics and demands of the participants. Categorical variables were summarized as frequencies and percentages, and continuous variables as mean (range) or median (interquartile range [IQR]). For questions allowing multiple responses, each item was aggregated independently.

Inferential statistical analysis was conducted using Jamovi (version 2.4.11.0; The Jamovi Project) to test for differences in training demands by ERTs. A chi-square test was conducted to test for differences in proportions between groups, and Fisher’s exact test was applied when the expected frequency was less than 5. The statistical significance level was set at 0.05 for all tests.

Results

1. Survey Respondent Characteristics

Of a total of 269 survey participants, 133 responded, for an overall response rate of 49.4%. The respondents included 95 (71.4%) from the Central ERTs and 38 (28.6%) from the Local ERTs. Most respondents were female (99, 74.4%), and the overall median age was 43 years (IQR: 36–47 years). The median work experience was 5 years (IQR: 2–8 years), with 5 years (IQR: 4–9 years) for the Central ERTs and 3 years (IQR: 1–5 years) for the Local ERTs. By affiliated institution, the most common was the KDCA (60, 45.1%), followed by the RCDCP (35, 26.3%), and city/county/district (si/gun/gu) (29, 21.8%). The most common primary duty was infectious disease response (86, 64.7%), followed by infectious disease surveillance, administration, education and training, analysis and research, and laboratory diagnosis (Table 1).

2. Training Topics

The types of infectious diseases requiring training were respiratory infectious diseases (n=55, 41.4%), waterborne and foodborne infectious diseases (n=45, 33.8%), imported infectious diseases (n=37, 27.8%), diseases of unknown origin (n=35, 26.3%), and emerging infectious diseases (n=31, 23.3%). In addition to infectious diseases, responses for non-communicable diseases (n=13, 9.8%) were also identified (Table 2). Regarding differences in training topic demands by group, the demands for training on diseases of unknown origin (32.6% vs. 10.5%) and non-communicable diseases (13.7% vs. 0.0%) were significantly higher in the Central ERTs than in the Local ERTs (p=0.009 and 0.019, respectively). In contrast, the need for training on waterborne and foodborne infectious diseases was significantly higher in the Local ERTs (60.5%) than in the Central ERTs (23.2%) (p<0.001). Although there was a high demand for training on respiratory infectious diseases (Central ERTs 42.1%, Local ERTs 39.5%; p=0.781) and imported infectious diseases (Central ERTs 32.6%, Local ERTs 15.8%; p=0.050), there were no significant differences in the demands between the two groups (Table 2).

3. Outbreak Setting of Training

Demand for training by outbreak setting was the highest for mass gatherings (n=65, 48.9%), followed by disaster areas (n=62, 46.6%), educational facilities (n=30, 22.6%), healthcare institutions (n=28, 21.1%), and accommodation facilities (n=23, 17.3%). Additionally, demand for training in restricted-access facilities such as military ships (n=18, 13.5%), military bases (n=17, 12.8%), and correctional facilities (n=16, 12.0%) was also identified (Table 2). Regarding intergroup differences in training demand by outbreak setting, demand for mass gathering response scenarios was significantly higher in the Central ERTs (56.8%) than in the Local ERTs (28.9%) (p=0.004). In contrast, demands for training on educational facilities (36.8% vs. 16.8%) and healthcare institutions (34.2% vs. 15.8%) were significantly higher in the Local ERTs than in the Central ERTs (p=0.013 and 0.019, respectively). For disaster areas, demand was high in both groups (Central ERTs 45.3%, Local ERTs 50.0%; p=0.621); however, the intergroup difference was not significant (Table 2).

4. Additional Curriculum for Training

The need for competencies related to infectious disease response was the highest for crisis communication and media relations at 62 respondents (46.6%), followed by risk assessment (n=61, 45.9%), data analysis and report writing (n=38, 28.6%), team building (n=33, 24.8%), interviewing techniques (n=26, 19.5%), and conflict management (n=23, 17.3%) (Table 2). A comparison of demands by ERTs revealed no significant differences between the two groups in any of the competency items related to infectious disease response, including crisis communication and media relations (Central ERTs 46.1%, Local ERTs 47.7%; p=0.379) and risk assessment (Central ERTs 50.6%, Local ERTs 36.4%; p=0.350) (Table 2).

5. Training Methods

Regarding training methods, problem-based learning (PBL) was most common (n=76, 57.1%), followed by field training (n=69, 51.9%). For discussion group composition, the most common opinion was that infectious disease response personnel should be included (n=76 , 57.1%), followed by external experts (n=56, 42.1%), infection control professionals (n=52, 39.1%), ERT leaders (n=47, 35.3%), laboratory specialists (n=41, 30.8%), senior epidemiologists (≥8 years of experience) (n=40, 30.1%), administrative staff (n=36, 27.1%), and healthcare professionals (n=35, 26.3%). The most common opinion was that a discussion group size of 4–6 people was appropriate (n=99, 74.4%). The preferred training duration was 1 day (n=57, 42.9%), followed by 2 days (n=49, 36.8%) (Table 3).

Discussion

A survey of the Central and the Local ERTs to identify detailed demands for field epidemiological investigation training found significant differences in preferred training topics and training settings according to the work characteristics of their respective organizations. The Central ERTs preferred scenarios requiring multifaceted crisis management, such as infectious diseases of unknown origin, non-communicable diseases, and mass gathering events, whereas the Local ERTs preferred scenarios with a greater emphasis on community-level field response, such as waterborne and foodborne infectious diseases and outbreaks in educational facilities and healthcare institutions. In terms of competencies related to infectious disease response, both groups showed a high demand for crisis communication, media relations, and risk assessment. There was a high demand for participatory training methods such as PBL and field training. Operationally, respondents preferred short-term courses of 1–2 days conducted in multidisciplinary small groups of 4–6 members.

These differences in training topic demands may stem from differences in the work characteristics of their respective organizations. Therefore, in future training planning, joint training to strengthen the shared competencies of the central and local ERTs should be maintained, while a tailored dual-track strategy that reflects each organization’s specific characteristics may be needed. Local ERTs should conduct practice-oriented training on topics that occur frequently in the community, whereas the central should implement training specialized in strengthening crisis management capabilities for inter-ministerial coordination and in-depth epidemiological investigations.

Among the competencies related to infectious disease response, demand was high across both groups for crisis communication and media relations, as well as risk assessment. This finding suggests that the role of ERT members is expanding beyond mere technical investigation into coordinating and managing on-site situations. Furthermore, this finding indicates that ERT members are keenly aware of their responsibility for the overall field response and strongly recognize the need for training to build the corresponding professional competencies. In particular, as experienced during the coronavirus disease 2019 response, rapid and transparent on-site crisis communication served as a key disease control measure that was as important as interrupting transmission in preventing the spread of inaccurate information and securing public trust in disease control and prevention policies [10]. In addition, the risk assessment required of ERTs refers to an on-site situational assessment that comprehensively considers factors such as facility structure, the scale of exposure, and the potential for further transmission at infectious disease outbreak sites. Therefore, future training should place greater emphasis on crisis management, communication, and risk assessment, in addition to epidemiological investigation capabilities, to strengthen practitioners’ confidence in field response.

Regarding training methods, there was a high preference for PBL and field training. This finding indicates that trainees desire an active learning structure that reflects actual outbreak situations. The PBL approach has been widely proven effective in simultaneously cultivating critical thinking and team-based collaboration by having learners analyze real-world issues through scenarios similar to actual outbreak situations [11,12]. These demands from practitioners align with recent domestic and international trends in epidemiological investigation training that emphasize strengthening field competencies. The World Health Organization’s Global Outbreak Alert and Response Network operates scenario-based simulation training with multidisciplinary teams as its core educational approach [13]. In addition, the Field Epidemiology Training Program guidelines allocate 75% of total training time to field training and recommend the use of case studies and practical exercises, rather than didactic lectures alone, to promote peer learning, critical thinking, and problem-solving [14]. In the Republic of Korea, practical field response capabilities of epidemiological investigators were also shown to improve significantly after one-day (7-hour) scenario-based simulation training conducted in multidisciplinary small groups of 7–8 participants [15]. Taken together, short-term, small-group PBL training is an effective approach for strengthening field response capabilities. Therefore, based on this domestic and international empirical evidence, future field epidemiological investigation training should be systematically developed into a standard model for national infectious disease response training based on field-oriented, short-term intensive training.

The limitations of this study are as follows. First, because this study was based on the results of a single cross-sectional survey conducted after the completion of all training in 2025, the findings were limited in their generalizability as the long-term and universal training demands of the entire ERTs. Second, because the survey response rate was below 50% (49.4%), the possibility of selection bias cannot be excluded, whereby the views of nonrespondents were not captured, and the demands of respondents with certain characteristics were overrepresented. Third, because the study relied on a self-reported survey, discrepancies may exist between respondents’ subjectively perceived demands and the objective competencies required for actual field response. Fourth, because only univariate analysis was conducted to compare differences in training demands between groups, the analysis did not statistically control for other confounding variables that could affect training demands, such as work experience or age, which differed between the two groups.

Despite these limitations, this study is meaningful in that it empirically identified directions for improving the future training system by incorporating the practical demands of field practitioners. In addition, to enhance training effectiveness, the KDCA is implementing practical training based on these findings, reflecting differences in work characteristics between the Central and the Local ERTs. Specifically, in the 2026 training program, risk assessment capabilities were substantially strengthened by combining site visits to special facilities that are typically difficult to access, such as military ships, for the Central ERTs. In scenario-based simulation training, crisis communication was also reinforced along with institution-specific problem-solving processes.

Going forward, the KDCA plans to continue providing field-oriented, customized training tailored to the operational characteristics of each ERTs. In this process, assessments for ongoing demands should be conducted to identify specific field demands, and the training program should continue to be revised and refined accordingly. Ultimately, maintaining a PBL-based participatory approach while strengthening core competencies such as crisis communication could contribute to training specialized personnel capable of responding rapidly and flexibly to changing patterns of infectious disease outbreaks.

서 론

「감염병의 예방 및 관리에 관한 법률」은 역학조사를 감염병 환자 등의 발생 규모를 파악하고 감염원을 추적 및 분석하는 활동으로 정의하고 있으며, 이를 통해 감염병의 차단과 확산 방지를 도모하도록 규정하고 있다. 역학조사는 감염병 발생 초기 원인 규명과 전파 차단을 위한 중요한 핵심 대응 활동으로, 신속하고 체계적인 수행이 중요하다[1,2]. 이를 위해 법령에서는 중앙 30명 이상, 시‧도 및 시‧군‧구는 각각 10명 이상으로 구성된 중앙 및 지자체역학조사반을 운영하도록 명시하고 있다[3]. 이러한 제도적 체계는 감염병 대응이 역학조사반 그룹 단위의 협업을 기반으로 수행됨을 전제로 하며, 역학조사반의 전문성과 합동 대응 역량은 감염병 대응 과정에서 중요하다.

역학조사 수행 인력은 감염병에 대한 지식뿐 아니라 팀 단위 협업 과정에서 현장 상황을 조정하고 공동 대응을 주체적으로 수행할 수 있는 역량을 갖추어야 한다. 이를 위해 질병관리청은 실제 발생 상황을 가정한 시나리오 기반의 소집훈련과 유관부처(국방부, 교육부, 법무부 교정본부) 및 지자체와 합동훈련을 운영하고 있다. 초창기인 2023년과 2024년의 중앙역학조사반 소집훈련은 연 2회, 시나리오 기반 모의훈련 방식으로 진행하였다. 훈련 시나리오의 주제로는 2023년에는 동물인플루엔자 인체감염증 유행과 군중모임 행사 간 감염병 발생을, 2024년에는 해외유입 홍역 국내 유행과 신종감염병 국내 유입 및 지역사회 유행을 다루었다. 반면, 동 기간 유관부처 합동훈련은 공동 대응 사례를 공유하는 강의 중심의 공동 연수로 실시하였다. 일례로 2024년에 국방부는 홍역과 결핵을, 교육부는 백일해를 주제로 진행하였다[4,5].

2025년에는 훈련의 실효성을 높이고자 참여기관을 확대하였으며, 훈련구성 및 운영을 내실화하기 위해 실제 감염병 발생 현장을 모사한 맞춤형 시나리오를 자체적으로 개발하여 적용하였다. 이를 바탕으로 총 9회의 현장 역학조사 훈련을 전면 시나리오 기반 모의훈련으로 실시하였다. 세부적으로는 첫째, 중앙역학조사반 소집훈련 1회는 의료기관 내 Disease X 의심 상황을 주제로 하였다. 둘째, 유관부처 합동훈련 총 3회는 부처별 맞춤형 시나리오를 도입하여, 교육부는 학교 내 호흡기감염병, 국방부는 군부대 및 지역사회 내 수인성 및 식품매개감염병, 새로 합류한 법무부 교정본부는 교정시설 내 호흡기감염병 의심 상황을 주제로 실시하였다[6-8]. 셋째, 지자체역학조사반 훈련을 정례화하였고, 5개 권역별 질병대응센터가 지역 상황을 반영한 홍역 유행 시나리오를 적용해 훈련하였다[9].

하지만 향후 훈련의 현장 적용성을 높이려면 훈련 대상자의 실질적인 요구가 반영된 시나리오 및 운영 방향 검토가 필요하다. 따라서 이 연구에서는 설문조사를 시행하여 훈련 주제와 방법에 대한 세부 요구를 파악하였다.

방 법

1. 연구 대상 및 설문조사

이 연구는 현장 역학조사 훈련 종료 후 실시한 설문조사 결과를 기반으로 수행하였다. 2025년에 실시한 총 9회의 현장 역학조사 훈련 종료 후, 최종 보고회에 참석한 중앙 및 지자체역학조사반 97명과 최종 보고회에 참석하지 않은 중앙역학조사반 172명(전체 188명 중 최종 보고회에 참석한 중앙역학조사반 16명은 제외)을 대상으로 설문조사를 실시하였다.

설문조사는 2025년 11월 28일부터 12월 5일까지 구글 폼(Google LLC.)을 활용하여 온라인으로 시행하였다. 최종 보고회(2025년 11월 28일) 참석자는 당일 현장에서 설문조사를 완료하였다. 보고회에 참석하지 않은 중앙역학조사반에게는 5일간(2025년 12월 1–5일) 동일한 설문지를 배포하여 조사하였다. 설문조사 시행 전 조사 목적과 자료 활용에 대한 설명을 제공하고 참여 동의를 받은 후 진행하였으며, 개인을 식별할 수 있는 정보는 수집하지 않았다.

설문지는 현장 역학조사 훈련의 목적과 특성을 반영하여 연구진이 자체적으로 개발하였다. 설문 문항은 3개 영역, 총 12개 항목으로 구성하였다. 응답자의 일반적 특성 영역은 성별, 연령, 소속기관, 담당 업무, 근무경력을 조사하였다. 훈련 주제 영역은 향후 훈련을 원하는 감염병 유형, 유행 상황(장소), 감염병 대응 관련 소양을 주제로 구성하여 세부 수요를 파악하였다. 훈련 방법 영역은 선호하는 훈련 방법, 훈련 기간, 토의 그룹 규모 및 구성원에 대한 내용을 포함하였다.

2. 자료 수집 및 분석

설문조사는 모든 문항에 응답해야 제출이 가능하도록 설정하여 무응답 항목이 발생하지 않도록 하였다. 수집된 자료 중 대상자의 일반적 특성과 요구도를 파악하기 위한 기술통계 분석은 Microsoft Excel (version 16.0.14332.21016; Microsoft Corp.)을 활용하였다. 범주형 변수는 빈도와 백분율로, 연속형 변수는 평균(범위)이나 중앙값(사분위수범위[interquartile range, IQR])으로 요약하여 제시하였으며, 복수 응답이 허용된 문항은 각 항목을 독립적으로 집계하였다.

역학조사반별 훈련 요구도의 차이를 검정하기 위한 추론통계 분석은 Jamovi (version 2.4.11.0; The Jamovi Project) 프로그램을 이용하였다. 집단 간 비율 차이를 검정하기 위해 카이제곱 검정(Chi-square test)을 실시하였으며, 기대빈도가 5 미만인 경우 피셔의 정확한 검정(Fisher’s exact test)을 적용하였다. 모든 통계적 유의수준은 0.05로 설정하였다.

3. 유행 장소별 훈련 요구

유행 장소에 대한 훈련 요구는 군중행사가 65명(48.9%)으로 가장 많았다. 이후 재난지역(62명, 46.6%), 교육시설(30명, 22.6%), 의료기관(28명, 21.1%), 숙박시설(23명, 17.3%) 순이었다. 또한 선박(18명, 13.5%), 군부대(17명, 12.8%), 교정시설(16명, 12.0%) 같은 접근 제한 시설에 대한 훈련 수요도 확인하였다(표 2). 유행 장소에 대한 훈련 요구의 집단별 차이를 보면, 군중행사 대응 시나리오에 대한 요구는 중앙역학조사반이 56.8%로 지자체역학조사반 28.9%보다 유의하게 높았다(p=0.004). 반면, 교육시설에 대한 훈련 요구는 지자체역학조사반이 36.8%로 중앙역학조사반 16.8%보다 유의하게 높았으며(p=0.013), 의료기관에 대한 훈련 요구는 지자체역학조사반이 34.2%로 중앙역학조사반 15.8%보다 유의하게 높았다(p=0.019). 재난지역(중앙역학조사반 45.3%, 지자체역학조사반 50.0%; p=0.621)의 경우, 두 집단에서 높은 수요를 보였으나 집단 간 유의한 차이는 없었다(표 2).

4. 감염병 대응 관련 소양 요구

감염병 대응 관련 소양에 대한 요구는 위기소통 및 언론 대응이 62명(46.6%)으로 가장 많았다. 이후 위험평가(61명, 45.9%), 자료분석 및 보고서 작성(38명, 28.6%), 팀빌딩(33명, 24.8%), 면담기법(26명, 19.5%), 갈등관리(23명, 17.3%) 순이었다(표 2). 역학조사반별 수요 비교 결과, 위기소통 및 언론 대응(중앙역학조사반 46.1%, 지자체역학조사반 47.7%; p=0.379)과 위험평가(중앙역학조사반 50.6%, 지자체역학조사반 36.4%; p=0.350)를 비롯하여 모든 감염병 대응 관련 소양 항목에서 두 집단 간에 통계적으로 유의한 차이는 없었다(표 2).

5. 훈련 방법 및 세부 운영 요구

훈련 방법으로는 문제해결중심학습(problem-based learning, PBL) (76명, 57.1%)이 가장 많았고, 현장실습(69명, 51.9%)이 그 다음이었다. 토의 그룹 구성은 감염병 대응 인력(76명, 57.1%)이 포함되어야 한다는 의견이 가장 많았다. 이후 외부 전문가(56명, 42.1%), 감염관리 전문가(52명, 39.1%), 방역관(47명, 35.3%), 실험실 전문가(41명, 30.8%), 선임 역학조사관(8년 이상, 40명, 30.1%), 행정인력(36명, 27.1%), 의료인(35명, 26.3%) 순으로 다양한 직군이 나타났다. 토의 그룹 규모는 4–6명(99명, 74.4%)이 적절하다는 의견이 가장 많았다. 희망 훈련 기간은 1일(57명, 42.9%)과 2일(49명, 36.8%) 순으로 많았다(표 3).

논 의

중앙 및 지자체역학조사반을 대상으로 현장 역학조사 훈련에 대한 세부 수요를 파악한 결과, 소속 기관의 업무 특성에 따라 선호하는 훈련 주제와 장소에 유의한 차이를 확인하였다. 중앙역학조사반은 원인불명감염병과 비감염성질환, 군중행사와 같은 다각적인 위기관리가 필요한 시나리오를 선호한 반면, 지자체역학조사반은 수인성 및 식품매개감염병과 교육시설과 의료기관 내 유행과 같은 지역사회 현장 대응 비중이 높은 시나리오를 선호하였다. 두 집단 모두 감염병 대응 소양 측면에서는 위기소통 및 언론 대응과 위험평가에 대한 수요가 공통으로 높았다. 훈련 방법으로는 참여형인 PBL과 현장실습에 대한 수요가 높았으며, 운영 측면에서는 다양한 직군으로 구성된 4–6명 규모의 소그룹이 1–2일간 진행하는 단기 과정을 선호하는 것으로 나타났다.

이러한 훈련 주제 요구의 차이는 소속 기관별 업무 특성의 차이에서 기인할 수 있다. 따라서 향후 훈련 기획 시, 중앙과 지자체의 공통 역량 강화를 위한 합동훈련은 유지하되, 각 기관의 특수성을 고려한 맞춤형 이원화 전략이 필요할 수 있다. 지자체는 지역사회 내 다빈도 발생하는 주제로 실무 중심 훈련을, 중앙은 다부처 간 조율 및 심층 역학조사 등 위한 위기관리 역량 강화에 특화한 훈련을 운영할 필요가 있다.

감염병 대응 관련 소양 중에서는 위기소통 및 언론 대응과 위험평가에 대한 요구가 공통적으로 높게 나타났다. 이는 역학조사반원의 역할이 단순한 기술적 조사를 넘어 현장 상황을 조율하고 관리하는 영역으로 확장되고 있음을 시사할 수 있다. 아울러 역학조사반원 스스로도 현장 대응 전반을 책임져야 한다는 점을 깊이 인식하고 있으며, 이에 걸맞은 전문 역량 확보를 위해 훈련의 필요성을 절감하고 있음을 의미할 수 있다. 특히 코로나바이러스감염증-19 대응에서 경험하였듯, 부정확한 정보 확산을 방지하고 방역 정책에 대한 대국민 신뢰를 확보하기 위해 현장에서의 신속하고 투명한 ‘위기소통’은 전파 차단만큼이나 중요한 핵심 방역 수단으로 작용하였다[10]. 또한 역학조사반이 요구한 ‘위험평가’는 감염병 발생 현장의 시설 구조, 노출 규모, 추가 전파 가능성 등을 종합적으로 판단하는 현장 상황평가를 의미한다. 따라서 향후 훈련에는 역학조사 역량과 더불어 실무자의 현장 대응 자신감을 높일 수 있도록 위기관리 및 소통, 위험평가 등에 대한 비중을 확대할 필요가 있다.

훈련 방법 영역에서는 PBL과 현장실습형에 대한 선호가 높았다. 이는 훈련생들이 실제 발생 상황을 반영한 능동적 학습 구조를 원하고 있음을 보여준다. PBL 방식은 학습자가 실제 발생 상황과 유사한 시나리오 속에서 현안을 분석함으로써, 비판적 사고력과 팀 단위의 협업 능력을 동시에 배양하는 데 효과적인 것으로 널리 입증된 바 있다[11,12]. 이러한 실무자들의 요구는 현장 역량 강화를 강조하는 국내외 역학조사 훈련의 최신 동향과 일치한다. 세계보건기구의 글로벌 발생 경보 대응 네트워크(Global Outbreak Alert and Response Network)는 다양한 분야의 전문가로 구성된 다학제 팀 단위의 시나리오 기반 모의훈련을 핵심 교육으로 운영하고 있다[13]. 또한 현장 역학 훈련프로그램(field epidemiology training programs) 가이드라인은 전체 교육 시간의 75%를 현장실습에 할당하고 있으며, 교실 훈련에서도 단순 이론이 아닌 동료 간 학습과 비판적 사고, 문제 해결을 촉진하는 사례 연구 및 실습을 활용하도록 명시하여 이를 훈련 표준으로 삼고 있다[14]. 국내에서도 다양한 직군을 7–8명 규모의 소그룹 팀으로 나누어 단 1일(7시간)의 시나리오 기반 모의훈련을 진행한 결과, 역학조사반원의 실질적인 현장 대응 역량이 유의하게 향상됨이 확인되었다[15]. 이를 종합할 때, 단기 소그룹 PBL 훈련은 현장 대응 역량을 강화할 수 있는 효율적인 대안이다. 따라서 향후 현장 역학조사 훈련은 이러한 국내외 실증적 근거를 바탕으로, 현장 중심의 단기 집중 훈련을 국가 감염병 대응 훈련의 표준 모델로 체계화해 나갈 필요가 있다.

이 연구의 제한점은 다음과 같다. 첫째, 2025년 전체 훈련 종료 후 1회 실시된 단면 설문조사 결과를 기반으로 하였으므로, 이를 역학조사반 전체의 장기적이고 보편적인 훈련 수요로 일반화하기에는 한계가 있다. 둘째, 설문 응답률이 50% 미만(49.4%)에 그쳐, 미응답자의 의견이 누락되고 특정 성향을 가진 응답자의 요구가 과대 표집되는 선택 편향이 개입되었을 가능성을 배제할 수 없다. 셋째, 자가 보고 형식의 설문에 의존하였으므로, 응답자가 주관적으로 느끼는 요구도와 실제 현장 대응에서 부족한 객관적 역량 간에 차이가 존재할 수 있다. 넷째, 집단 간 훈련 요구 차이를 비교함에 있어 단변량 분석만 시행하여, 두 집단 간에 차이를 보이는 근무 경력이나 연령 등 훈련 수요에 영향을 미칠 수 있는 다른 교란 변수들을 통계적으로 통제하지 못하였다.

이러한 제한 사항을 고려하더라도, 이 연구는 현장 실무자의 실질적 요구를 수렴하여 향후 훈련 체계의 개선 방향을 실증적으로 도출했다는 점에서 의의가 있다. 또한 질병관리청은 훈련의 효과를 높이기 위해 이 연구 결과를 바탕으로 중앙 및 지자체역학조사반의 업무 특성 차이를 반영하여 실효성 있는 훈련을 진행하고 있다. 구체적으로 2026년도 훈련에서는 중앙역학조사반을 대상으로 한 함대 견학 등 평소 접근이 어려운 특수 시설 현장 견학을 병행하여 위험평가 역량을 실질적으로 높였으며, 시나리오 기반 모의훈련에서는 기관별 맞춤형 문제 해결 과정과 더불어 위기소통 영역을 보강하였다.

향후 질병관리청은 각 역학조사반의 실무 특성에 맞추어 현장 중심의 맞춤형 훈련을 지속해 나갈 계획이다. 이 과정에서 현장의 세부 수요를 파악하기 위한 지속적인 요구도 조사를 수행하고, 이를 반영하여 훈련 프로그램을 수정 및 보완해 나갈 필요가 있다. 궁극적으로 PBL 중심의 참여형 방식을 유지하되 위기소통 등 핵심 소양 항목을 강화함으로써, 변화하는 감염병 유행 양상에 신속하고 유연하게 대응할 수 있는 전문 인력 양성에 기여할 것이다.

Dense projection fusion for 3D object detection
Authors: Zhao Chen, Bin-Jie Hu, Chengxi Luo, Guohao Chen, Haohui Zhu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Fusing information from LiDAR and cameras can effectively enhance the overall perceptivity of autonomous vehicles in various scenarios. Despite the relatively good results achieved by point-wise fusion and Bird’s-Eye-View (BEV) fusion, they still cannot fully leverage the image information and lack of effective depth information. For any fusion methods, the multi-modal features first need to be concatenated along the channel, and then the fused features are extracted using convolutional layers. However, this type of fusion methods is effective, but too coarse which causes that the fused features cannot pay more attention to the regions with important features and suffer from severe noise. To tackle these issues, we propose in this paper a Dense Projection Fusion (DPFusion) approach. It consists of two new modules: dense depth map guided BEV transform (DGBT) module and multi-modal feature adaptive fusion (MFAF) module. The DGBT module first quickly estimates the depth of each pixel and then projects all image features to the BEV space, making full use of the image information. The MFAF module computes the image weights and point cloud weights for each channel in each BEV grid and then adaptively weights and fuses the image BEV features with the point cloud BEV features. It is worth pointing out that the MFAF module makes the fused features pay more attention to background outline and object outline. Our proposed DPFusion demonstrates competitive results in 3D object detection, achieving a mean Average Precision (mAP) of 70.4 and a nuScenes detection score (NDS) of 72.3 on the nuScenes validation set.

Introduction

Driven by the rapid growth of vehicles, autonomous driving has been significantly advanced, which offers great potential to improve road safety and traffic efficiency. For autonomous driving, highly accurate and robust perception capability is needed, since perception results directly affect subsequent decision making and control. As one of the important tasks in autonomous driving, 3D object detection aims to obtain the objects’ information, such as positions, orientations, and sizes, and to classify objects accurately1. To this end, some existing works2–7 achieved relatively accurate results, by leveraging the depth information provided by LiDAR. However, despite the encouraging achievements in LIDAR enhancement techniques8,9, these LiDAR-based methods are still insufficient for achieving robust 3D detection in the complex traffic environment, since they cannot obtain color texture information and more details about objects by using a single type of sensor10. It is worth noting that most autonomous vehicles can obtain multiple types of sensing data provided by sensors mounted, such as camera and LiDAR. High resolution images and sparse spatial information are respectively obtained by cameras and LiDAR. Therefore, a practical approach to enhance the overall perceptivity of a vehicle is by fully fuse the complementary information provided by cameras and LiDAR via multi-modal fusion methods.

Existing multi-modal fusion methods can be broadly classified into four categories: result-level fusion, proposal-level fusion, point-wise fusion, and view fusion. Result-level fusion methods, such as FPointnet11 and RoarNet12, first generate 3D viewing frustum based on 2D detection results, and then regress 3D bounding boxes and categories within these frustums. However, detection accuracy of these methods mainly depends on the 2D detection results. Proposal-level fusion methods, including MV3D13 and AVOD14, fuse multi-modal features at the region of interest (ROI) stage. However, these two types of coarse-grained fusion methods cannot achieve a desired detection performance due to the fact that rectangular ROIs contain a lot of background noise in general. Recently, a type of approaches, namely, point-wise fusion15–17, have shown more accurate results by establishing fine-grained feature alignment relationships among multi-modal data. Specifically, they establish a feature mapping relationship by using spatial projection to fuse image features into point cloud features on a point-by-point basis. However, these approaches do not fully leverage the image context information due to the following reason18,19. Due to the sparseness of the point clouds, only some image features projected by the point cloud are used for fusion, while others are discarded. Nowadays, a new class of approaches, namely, view fusion, has emerged, which involve two procedures: projecting image features to Bird’s-Eye-View (BEV) and fusing them with LiDAR BEV features18,20. Different from point-wise fusion approaches, they can achieve the projection of all image features to BEV and the sufficient fusion of image and point cloud features. However, due to the lack of depth information in images, the projection procedure from 2D image to 3D space exists serious radial errors. As a result, accurately representing 2D image features in 3D space is a challenging problem21. On the other hand, the concatenate-convolutional fusion method generally adopted in multi-modal BEV feature fusion cannot finely fuse the multi-modal complementary information due to the two following reasons. First, in the procedure of dimensionality reduction, it loses part of image and point cloud information. Second, it cannot allow the fused features to pay more attention to the regions with important features. Another popular approach, called as attention-based fusion, also cannot achieve a desired result. This is because it focuses on finding highly similar information rather than complementary information22. As discussed above, it is still imperative to propose an effective approach that can first accurately project 2D image features to BEV and then finely fuse them with LiDAR BEV features.

Motivated by the above, in this paper, we propose a Dense Projection Fusion (DPFusion) approach, which achieves a multi-stage and fine-grained fusion of point clouds and images. DPFusion consists of two innovative modules: a dense depth map guided BEV transform module (DGBT) and a multi-modal feature adaptive fusion module (MFAF). The DGBT module provides depth information for all image features by using fast depth completion, thus overcoming the limitation of point-wise fusion methods caused by the sparsity of the point cloud and addressing the issue of inadequate depth information in the projection procedure from the image to BEV. The MFAF can achieve a fine-grained fusion of multi-modal complementary information, by making the fused features focus on background outline and object outline (which gives higher weights to image features with richer semantic information in region of interest). Specifically, it first calculates the weights of image and point cloud for each feature channel in each BEV grid, and then fuses the image features and point cloud features with adaptive weighting. Related work

Point-wise fusion

Since the proposal of PointPainting15, the point-wise fusion approach has garnered significant attention. This approach establishes precise feature alignment relationships between multi-modal data, resulting in impressive performance in 3D object detection. PointPainting15 projects the point cloud onto the image plane and amplifies the features of each point using the corresponding image segmentation results. PointAugmenting16 demonstrates that utilizing intermediate features of image segmentation, rather than the segmentation results, can further improve detection performance. MVP17 up-samples the point cloud within the segmentation mask of each image instance based on point-wise fusion, generating a set of dense virtual point clouds. This approach enhances the detection performance of sparse point cloud objects, such as far distant and small objects. Aside from the corresponding image instance segmentation results, PAI3D23 also enhances the features of each point using the instance center. However, this fine-grained feature alignment heavily relies on precise extrinsic calibration, making these methods susceptible to sensor misalignment19. DeepFusion24 employs cross-attention to dynamically align image features and LiDAR features during the fusion process. Due to the sparsity of the point clouds, point-wise fusion is unable to fully utilize the abundant image context information. Only the image features at the point cloud projection locations are used to enhance the point cloud features, while those not projected by the point cloud are discarded.

View fusion

To eliminate the viewpoint differences between cameras and LiDAR, recent approaches have focus on transforming multi-modal features into a unified view representation for feature fusion. LC-MV25, for example, first fuses multi-frame point clouds and high-definition maps on BEV, and then fuses image features and point cloud features on the range view (RV). The RV features are then projected onto the BEV and fused with the BEV features computed from LiDAR and high-definition map. BEVFusion18,20 propose a new framework for fusing multi-modal features in the BEV and have sparked a wave of research on BEV fusion. BEVFusion18 employs sparse depth maps to assist Lift-splat-shot (LSS)26 in generating image BEV features, which are then fused with LiDAR BEV features. LSS26 predicts the probability of each image feature in each depth bin through the network and rescales the associated image features accordingly. This generates an image feature pseudo point cloud that is projected onto BEV. Consequently, these types of image BEV generation methods are also referred to as forward projection methods27. LSSAttn28 utilizes an attentional mechanism to compute similarity scores for each association pair after LSS establishes dense associations between perspective pixels and the BEV grid, thereby improving the prediction accuracy of depth probabilities. In order to more accurately project image features into 3D space, GAFusion29 introduces LiDAR occupancy prediction results to guide the projection of image features into 3D space, in addition to using sparse depth maps to assist the LSS in generating the probabilities of depth bins.

MSMDFusion21 achieves more reliable depth estimation based on K-nearest reference points on multi-scale image feature maps. The multi-scale image features are then fused in 3D voxels and projected onto the BEV, where they are finally fused with LiDAR BEV features. On the other hand, BEVFusion4D30 obtains image BEV features through 3 deformable attention layers. In each deformable attention layer, each input BEV grid generates N points of different heights as queries, which are then projected onto the corresponding locations on the image. The image features are then used as values. This method of projecting the BEV queries onto the image to obtain image features is also known as the backward projection method27 (represented by BEVFormer31). BEV-CFKT32 also employs this backward projection method to generate image BEV features and uses an alternative backbone and a perspective detection head to supervise the learning of image features. At the same time, it uses a BEV encoder with shared layers to facilitate the interaction of BEV features from different modalities.

Accurate projection of image features onto BEV is challenging due to the lack of depth information in the image. While there are methods available, such as LSS26, BEVDepth33 and BEVFormer31, they lack effective depth supervision to the extent that depth information is not estimated accurately enough.

The proposed DPFusion, as shown in Fig. 1, mainly consists of four key components: feature extraction, depth completion and view transform, feature fusion, and object detection. Following the approach of BEVFusion18, we introduce an image branch that generates image BEV features, which include an image backbone and a DGBT module. We also incorporate a MFAF module into TransFusion-L19. For the image backbone, we utilize Swin-Transformer-Tiny34 to extract image features. In the first step, we project the raw point cloud onto image plane to generate a sparse depth map. Then, we employ fast depth completion to obtain a dense depth map. Next, we leverage the dense depth map to embed the location information into the image features and project the enhanced image features onto the BEV. Finally, we use the MFAF module to fuse the image BEV features and LiDAR BEV features, with the TransFusion detector head19 generating the 3D object detection results. We will provide a detailed description of the two key components in the following subsections.

Dense depth map guided BEV transform module

In order to address the issues of low utilization of image information, and a lack of effective depth information, we propose the dense depth map guided BEV transform module. We employ a fast depth completion method35 to densify the sparse depth map into a dense depth map so that each image feature has a corresponding depth. Then we design an Image Feature Enhancement Network (IFENet) to embed spatial information of dense depth map into multi-view features to get the multi-view features with spatial information. Finally, we project all image features onto BEV based on pixel coordinates and pixel depth to generate image BEV features. Our method does not need to predict the probability of each depth bin in each pixel as in LSS-based methods. Multi-modal feature adaptive fusion module

The fusion of two features is usually done by concatenate-convolutional fusion, which concatenates the two features along the channel dimension and then extracts the fused features using a convolutional layer. Although this approach can achieve effective feature fusion, the fusion results are too coarse. According to information theory, the original information lost in feature dimensionality reduction cannot be recovered in subsequent operations. Therefore, concatenate-convolutional fusion does not fully integrate the complementary information of the image and the point cloud. Attention-based method is another more refined approach to fusion. However, the attention-based method tries to find the highly similar information instead of complementary information22, because the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$QK^T$$\end{document}QKT in the attention mechanism36 calculates the similarity of two feature vectors, like the cost volume37. Therefore, attention-based fusion method is suboptimal in cross-modal feature fusion.

In order to better fuse the multi-modal complementary information and reduce the impact of depth estimation errors at the edges, we propose a multi-modal feature adaptive fusion module. The fusion method does not perform dimensionality reduction operations on image BEV features and point cloud BEV features, but retains their original features. The weights of the image and point cloud for each feature channel in each BEV grid are predicted by the network, and the image BEV features and point cloud BEV features are dynamically weighted and fused. Experiment

We train and validate the model using the publicly available autonomous driving dataset nuScenes38, which consists of a 32-beam LiDAR and six cameras. The nuScenes dataset consists of 700, 150, 150 scenes for training, validation and testing, corresponding to 28k, 6k, and 6k samples in each part. For object detection tasks, there are 10 kinds of obstacles considered including dynamic and static objects. The main evaluation metrics are mean Average Precision (mAP) and nuScenes detection score (NDS). We introduce an image branch for generating image BEV features and a MFAF module to TransFusion-L19. We utilize Swin-Transformer-Tiny34 as our image backbone, and apply FPN39 to fuse multi-scale image features. We set the image size to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$512\times 1024$$\end{document}512×1024. We apply joint training for 6 epochs by adding image branch and MFAF modules to the pre-trained LiDAR-only detector. In the ablation experiments, in order to achieve a fair comparison with the state-of-the-art BEVFusion18 and at the same time to demonstrate the importance of each of our proposed key components, we resize the image size to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$256\times 704$$\end{document}256×704. The 3D backbone network, detection head and loss function are the same as other state-of-the-art methods, so we do not go into too much detail in this paper, and refer the readers to previous papers on 3D object detection17–19.

Comparison with BEVFusion18, DPFusion achieves 1.9% improvement in mAP, with a significant increase in detection accuracy especially for small object. This is mainly because DPFusion estimates the depth of each pixel more accurately, allowing more features to be obtained for small objects that only have a small number of points projected to them. Meanwhile, the depth estimation of DPFusion is faster, more accurate, more lightweight, and requires no training. DPFusion is also compared with MVP17, a representative method of point-wise fusion, and the mAP is improved by 5%. Choosing the BEV space as the shared fusion space makes it possible to fully utilize all camera features, not just those projected by the point cloud18. MSMDFusion21 uses K-nearest to generate 3D virtual points on each instance to provide more depth information for image, which significantly improves the detection accuracy. However, concatenate-convolutional fusion limits the full fusion of multi-modal complementary information. Thanks to the dense projection of the point cloud, more image features are obtained for small objects, thus improving the detection accuracy.

Conclusion

In this paper, we propose DPFusion for 3D object detection, which accurately projects image features to BEV and adaptively fuses them with LiDAR BEV feature. We design the DGBT module to address the two issues: low utilization of image information in point-wise fusion caused by the sparseness of point cloud, and a lack of effective depth information in image projection to BEV. Additionally, we design the MFAF module for fine-grained fusion of multi-modal BEV features. The MFAF module makes the fused features pay more attention to background outline and object outline while effectively suppress the impacts of noise. Our proposed DPFusion achieves competitive results on nuScenes dataset. Besides, ablation experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of our DGBT module and MFAF module.

This framework is a single-agent single-frame 3D object detection, which does not consider the time dimension. Under this framework, occluded objects in the current frame cannot use the information in the history frame or additional information from other agents to be detected. For this issue, it can be solved by using multi-frame approach or vehicle-infrastructure cooperative approach. In our future work, we will conduct research on vehicle-infrastructure cooperative 3D object detection to improve the detection accuracy of vehicle blind area, by utilizing information from infrastructure.

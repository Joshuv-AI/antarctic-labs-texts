Research on the impact of diversity in financial ecosystems on new energy systems
Authors: Huihao Liu, Jinhong Wei, Jiayi Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Expediting the construction of the new energy systems is crucial to achieving an energy transition, decreasing carbon dioxide emissions, and alleviating global warming. Utilizing Chinese province panel data from 2000 to 2022, we examined the causal effect of diversity in financial ecosystems on the new energy systems and utilized instrumental variable estimates to mitigate endogeneity. We constructed a new energy systems indicator framework, employed the entropy approach to assign weights to each indicator objectively, and quantitatively assessed the level and attributes of the new energy systems. The findings indicate that diversity in financial ecosystems plays a crucial role in developing new energy systems. Diversity in financial ecosystems impacts the development of new energy systems via the consequences of technology innovation and digital effects. These effects are more concentrated in areas without external power inputs, with a long transportation history, and characterized by low bird biodiversity. The study’s findings offer a viable Chinese approach for global energy transition and successful climate change mitigation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-43235-y.

Introduction

The rising energy demand due to economic expansion and industrial activities has garnered significant interest from the academic community regarding low-carbon sustainable energy supply. China’s energy consumption has long been predominantly dependent on coal, rendering it a principal source of greenhouse gas emissions1. However, diminishing the production and consumption of fossil fuels is essential for alleviating climate warming and is pivotal for attaining an energy, economic, and equitable transition beyond fossil fuel production2,3. The low-carbon transition of the energy system4 and the green energy transition5 have emerged as critical strategies for numerous countries and regions to secure energy stability. China’s significant dependence on energy imports and inadequate self-sufficiency in energy supply restrict its ability to secure energy resources, as an overreliance on foreign energy sources hampers its energy supply capabilities6. In the context of reaching peak carbon emissions, securing a sustainable supply of alternative energy sources has emerged as China’s foremost goal. Establishing the new energy systems adapted to China’s unique circumstances is essential for achieving the objectives of the Paris Agreement7. The new energy systems are a clean, low-carbon, secure, and efficient framework8, which focuses on non-fossil energy sources, with fossil fuels serving as a backup, underpinned by an innovative power system, and directed by concepts of sustainability, intelligence, and energy conservation. The energy transition signifies the dynamic process of establishing this system, centered on the transfer from fossil fuels to alternative energy sources as the primary energy supply.

The new energy systems are an innovative concept presented in China’s 20th National Congress government report, designed to create a diversified, clean, secure, and resilient energy supply framework. Research and perspectives from various sectors have mostly converged on its core characteristics, including low-carbon, intelligent, and enhanced safety, along with critical implementation strategies that involve technological and institutional reforms9. At present, a cohesive definition for the new energy systems is lacking. The current study indicates that the new energy systems are primarily characterized by renewable energy sources and advanced digital low-carbon technologies, including the complete spectrum of energy production, conversion, transmission, storage, and consumption. It exhibits attributes of safety and efficiency, cleanliness and low carbon emissions, economic inclusivity, and varied coordination10. It emphasizes clean energy while enhancing the clean and efficient use of traditional fossil fuels, fostering the green and low-carbon transformation of energy development and consumption patterns11. By means of enhanced planning and technological advancements, it enables a systematic shift from traditional to modern energy sources, establishing an energy system characterized by Chinese attributes12.

Nonetheless, developing the new energy systems may encounter the impossible triangle challenge, wherein achieving energy reliability, economic viability, and minimal carbon emissions concurrently proves challenging13. A stable transition from fossil fuels to other energy sources requires tackling the financial and financing issues of building new energy infrastructure. Many researchers agree that green money helps the energy transition. This topic covers green finance policy and energy development, focusing on a green financial development index, carbon emission reduction, environmental accountability, green technology, and energy efficiency14. Financial capital support can enhance technical innovation, optimize resource allocation, diversify capital sources, decrease energy intensity, improve energy efficiency, and facilitate change in the energy consumption structure15. Nevertheless, most scholars have overlooked how diversity in financial ecosystems affects the development of new energy systems. Financial species refer to examining the diverse requirements of financial ecosystems, with their components paralleling the species notion within biodiversity theory16. It is an essential component of diversity in financial ecosystems. How may diversity in financial ecosystems be utilized to address financial restrictions in developing new energy systems? What are its causal links and methods of impact? These questions are worth exploring in depth.

In alignment with the energy supply demands of Chinese modernization, we constructed the indicator system of the new energy framework by scientifically selecting 28 indicators from the four fundamental dimensions of “safety and efficiency, cleanliness and low-carbon, diversified synergies, and intelligence and inclusivity,” based on the intrinsic nature of China’s new energy systems, and objectively assigning weights to each indicator utilizing the entropy value method. Subsequently, we evaluated the causality, influence mechanisms, and heterogeneity of diversity in financial ecosystems on the new energy systems by employing Chinese provincial panel data from 2000 to 2022 and utilizing the double fixed-effects model. Our findings indicate that diversity in financial ecosystems significantly facilitates the development of new energy systems, and prioritizing rare financial species can more efficiently advance this building. We tackle endogeneity concerns utilizing DID model testing, placebo tests, Heckman estimation, and instrumental variable estimation.

The contributions of this study are as follows: Firstly, it overcomes the limitations of existing literature, which often focuses on the connotation, significance, construction pathways, and dynamic evolution of the new energy systems17–19. We have methodically developed an indicator system for the new energy systems, offering new evidence for precise measurement of their status, thereby expanding the theoretical framework of energy systems. Secondly, we examine the causal relationship and impact mechanisms of diversity in financial ecosystems on the new energy systems, which diverges from current research primarily focused on green finance and energy transition20–22, further expanding the academic perspective for the new energy systems. Thirdly, we underscore the influence of diversity in financial ecosystems on various dimensions of the new energy systems, accentuating the critical importance of financial species abundance, financial species hierarchy, and variations in capital market financial species composition in promoting the development of new energy systems.

Part II describes the theoretical analysis and research hypotheses; Part III describes the research design, including the model framework, core variable selection, data sources, and descriptive statistics, with a focus on diversity in financial ecosystems and the new energy systems; Part IV articulates the principal empirical findings alongside a suite of endogeneity and robustness examinations; Part V explores further discussions; and Part VI culminates with a summary and recommendations.

Theoretical analysis

Financial capital is an essential factor of production, and the diversification of financing services is an important symbol of its service to the real economy towards maturity and perfection. Diversity in financial ecosystems includes a broader array of financial sources, facilitating the shift from fossil fuels to renewable energy sources23 and serving as a crucial driver in developing the new energy systems24. On the one hand, diversity in financial ecosystems helps energy enterprises seek more financing channels. With high diversity in financial ecosystems, the financial ecosystem’s deepening in scale, products, services, and diversification is also higher so that new energy enterprises can effectively obtain construction funds from diversified financing channels25. Specifically, uncommon financial instruments (e.g., green energy funds) are frequently generated from social capital, and their implementation can effectively dismantle siloed thinking and expand the funding avenues for energy firms, hence facilitating the development of the new energy systems26. On the other hand, diversity in financial ecosystems can effectively reduce financing costs. Diversity in financial ecosystems (e.g., asset and loan portfolio diversity) can effectively reduce the upfront costs of new energy technologies27, which is crucial for realizing the energy transition28 and building the new energy systems. In particular, the emergence of a large number of new financial species improves investors’ choices and investment returns, reducing the cost and risk of investment and financing of energy enterprises, thus attracting more economic agents with different purposes and risk preferences to participate in financial transaction activities29, and therefore promoting the new energy systems construction. Specific impact mechanisms include the following:

Technological progress favorably impacts the new energy systems. Enhancing energy conservation, reducing emissions, and innovating clean energy technologies are essential for attaining established climate goals and developing new energy systems. First, technological innovation is the foundation for enhancing the energy economy’s cleanliness and efficiency, yielding economic, developmental, and environmental advantages30. Technological innovation, capable of decreasing energy intensity and carbon emissions while preserving global economic growth, is an essential strategy for achieving China’s low-carbon economic transition31. Advocating for energy saving, reducing emissions, and innovating clean energy technologies may be crucial to achieving the defined climate objectives. By utilizing financial tools to increase financing costs for conventional energy corporations, this approach drives these organizations to innovate in green technologies, thereby creating an innovation compensation effect that helps mitigate compliance expenses32. Technological progress allows a nation to switch from fossil fuels to alternative energy33, promotes a coal-resource-based economy, a clean-energy transition, and a sustainable energy transition, and gradually reduces the cost of energy technology34.

The digitalization effect is another effective mechanism for diversity in financial ecosystems to promote the new energy systems. On the one hand, diversity in financial ecosystems facilitates the rapid development of digital infrastructure. On the one hand, diversity in financial ecosystems significantly improves digitalization. Diversity in financial ecosystems enhances source levels and increases the variety of financial instruments; hence, it supplies substantial construction money for digitization through various financing avenues and mechanisms, including loans, bonds, and funds. Researchers assert that green finance and digital technology exert a synergistic and coordinated influence35, which can markedly elevate the digitization of heavily polluting enterprises; digital finance aids enterprises in surmounting financing limitations to enhance both the quantity and quality of green technological innovations by offering alternative financing sources36. It significantly promotes the digital transformation of enterprises37. Conversely, the impact of digitization encourages the development of new energy systems. Renewable energy, including wind and solar power, necessitates substantial infrastructure expenditures, such as transmission lines and energy storage systems. At the same time, digital technology offers flexibility to enhance the energy system and facilitate the advancement of distributed energy. Popularizing digital infrastructure accelerates broadband and mobile internet growth, dramatically shortens the spatial and temporal distance between regions, maximizes resource integration, reduces energy intensity, and optimizes energy structure38. Digitalization encourages interregional energy networks, energy efficiency, and energy savings39. According to the above analysis, we hypothesize:

H1 Diversity in financial ecosystems catalyzes the new energy systems.

H2 Diversity in financial ecosystems positively affects the new energy systems through technological innovation effects.

H3 Diversity in financial ecosystems positively affects the new energy systems through digitalization effects.

Data source and description

Data source

The sample period of this paper is from 2000 to 2022, considering 30 provinces in China. Due to excessive missing data in the provinces of Tibet, Hong Kong, Taiwan, and Macao, these regions were excluded from the sample selection to ensure data integrity. Diversity in financial ecosystems data is from the China Research Data Service Platform, the China EPS Data Platform, and the Wind database; the new energy systems data are from the China Energy Statistical Yearbook, the China Electricity Statistical Yearbook, the China Statistical Yearbook, and the China Carbon Accounting Database; patent data are from the China Science and Technology Statistical Yearbook.

Descriptive statistics

The descriptive statistics presented in this work (see Appendix Table B1) are evident. The mean value of the new energy systems is 0.175, the minimum is 0.044, and the maximum is 0.594, demonstrating significant variety in the new energy systems throughout Chinese provinces. In general, a higher new energy systems value indicates a more advanced level of development for the new energy system. The mean values of diversity in financial ecosystems for sensitivity q = 0, q = 2, and q = ∞ are 1.544, 1.419, and 1.270, respectively, with a minimum value of 1.000 and maximum values of 2.065, 2.021, and 1.836. An increase in the sensitivity parameter q correlates with a drop in diversity in financial ecosystems.

Endogeneity test

We employ Difference-in-Differences (DID) estimation, placebo tests, Heckman estimation, and instrumental variable estimation to conduct endogeneity tests, addressing potential issues such as reverse causation, sample selection bias, omitted variables, and measurement error, ensuring the research conclusions’ reliability.

DID estimation

We utilize DID estimation to perform a supplementary endogeneity test for mitigating the endogeneity issue stemming from reverse causation and to more accurately ascertain the causal effect of diversity in financial ecosystems on the new energy systems. Upon evaluating policy shocks that correspond with the attributes of diversity in financial ecosystems, we ascertain that the Green Credit Guidelines (GCG) promulgated by the China Banking Regulatory Commission in 2012 exhibit these qualities. The GCG promotes diversified growth in the financial system by mandating that financial institutions strategically adopt green lending agreements, proactively modify credit portfolios, and enhance financing for sustainable economies, including green, low-carbon, and circular systems. Their enactment signifies a crucial advancement in the establishment of China’s green credit policy42. The GCG not only affects renewable energy investment43 but also restricts financial support to sectors marked by significant pollution, high energy intensity, and overcapacity44, thus fostering low-carbon economic transformation and facilitating new energy development. Consequently, we designate the GCG as the policy shock for our analysis. Employing a difference-in-differences model, we ascertain the effects of diversity in financial ecosystems shocks on the emerging energy system. Heckman estimation

To mitigate the endogeneity problem caused by sample selection bias as much as possible, referring to the existing literature practice46,47, the estimation is carried out in two stages by applying the Heckman method. In the first stage, the paper assigns the dummy variable DID as a dummy variable in the above paper to provinces subject to the policy shock of the GCG. The dummy variable DID is assigned a value of 1. In all other cases, it is zero. Probit regressions are performed with DID as the dependent variable and control variables as independent variables to get the Inverse Mills Ratio (IMR). The IMR is incorporated into the baseline model (1) for estimation in the second stage. Column (4) of Table 3 presents the first-stage estimate result of the Heckman model, whereas column (5) of Table 3 displays the second-stage estimation result of the same model. The IMR calculated coefficient is significantly non-zero, and the estimated coefficient of 2Dz6 remains significantly positive. The findings of this paper remain valid, addressing the endogeneity issue.

Instrumental variable estimates

Endogeneity is addressed by selecting appropriate instrumental variables to enhance the trustworthiness of the results. Appropriate instrumental factors must satisfy correlation and exogeneity criteria; specifically, they should be correlated with the primary explanatory variables while remaining uncorrelated with the error term. This paper utilizes the construction concept proposed by Goldsmith-Pinkham et al. (2020)48 to cross-multiply the dummy variable for the policy shock of the GCG with the proximity between provincial capitals and the prefecture-level cities associated with the input and output sites of the ultra-high voltage (UHV). Consequently, we establish that the instrumental variable is uncorrelated with the error term, and this interaction term upholds the exogeneity assumption of the instrumental variable. The rationale for developing this instrumental variable is twofold: firstly, green finance pertains to the decisions made by government entities and associated financial institutions regarding investments in green energy projects, as well as their capacity to offer financial backing, ancillary services, and policy incentives to energy firms49. The GCG policy and UHV projects have markedly enhanced energy efficiency, expedited energy innovation, and fostered energy conservation and carbon reduction44. The dummy variable (DID) and the interaction term reflecting the proximity between provincial capitals and prefecture-level cities related to UHV input and output sites are linked to diversity in financial ecosystems, fulfilling the correlation assumption of instrumental variables. Conversely, the strictly exogenous characteristic of the GCG policy has been validated by researchers utilizing the dummy variable (DID) created by the policy shock50, while scholars have also employed the UHV project in the examination of exogenous shocks51. This indicates that the interaction term may serve as an instrumental variable to elucidate causal linkages.

Immediately thereafter, a two-stage (2SLS) regression test was conducted, and the results are shown in columns (6)-(7) of Table 3. The result shows that the Klebergen-Paapark LM statistic is significant at the 1% level, rejecting the original hypothesis of under-identification of instrumental variables. The Cragg-Donald WaldF statistic is greater than the Stock-Yogo weak instrumental variable identification F-test threshold at the 10% significance level, rejecting the original hypothesis of weak instrumental variables and solving the possible endogeneity problem. Thus, the instrumental variables selected in this paper are reasonable and reliable. The estimated coefficient of the core explanatory variable 2Dz6 is still significantly positive, indicating that diversity in financial ecosystems substantially contributes to the new energy systems. This again demonstrates that the main findings are reliable.

Mechanism and heterogeneity analysis

Diversity in financial ecosystems can successfully promote the return of finance to its origin and effectively enhance the real economy’s efficiency and level. Diversity in financial ecosystems, especially the new scarce financial species (such as new energy debt, new energy funds, etc.), is oriented to reduce the financing pressure of energy enterprises and stimulate the construction of the new energy systems and power. In the process of constructing new energy systems, gradually transforming financial capital into energy entities helps stabilize the energy industry in a leading position within the integrated layout, accelerates the cultivation of new kinetic energy, and renews the relationships associated with old kinetic energy. This part examines the potential role of financial species abundance through the mechanism test and heterogeneity analysis. We further explore how diversity in financial ecosystems fosters the development of new energy systems and its effect on differences.

Heterogeneity analysis

(1) UHV project. The UHV transmission project is a “super-artery” for economic and social advancement, characterized by extensive transmission distances, substantial capacity, high efficiency, and minimal losses. It mitigates energy distribution imbalances and significantly contributes to China’s economic growth63. The primary forms of electricity generation in UHV transmission projects are wind and hydropower, necessitating that inter-provincial transmission is underpinned by a sustainable proportion of renewable energy64. UHV transmission projects effectively address China’s reverse energy allocation by facilitating energy substitution, enhancing generation capacity, fostering industrial agglomeration, and improving output efficiency to achieve a low-carbon energy transition. The UHV transmission projects encompass 15 importing provinces (see Appendix Table B6 for detailed UHV transmission projects), serving as a crucial mechanism to address the issue of “transmitting electricity from the west to the east.” This study employs a dummy variable to distinguish the new energy systems based on whether a province is a UHV-importing province; specifically, if the province is a UHV-importing province, it is designated as UHV = 1; otherwise, it is defined as UHV = 0. The regression test results are presented in columns (1)- (2) of Table 7. For provinces outside the UHV importing category, the coefficient of 2Dz6 in Column (1) is positive at the 1% significance level, indicating that each unit increase in diversity in financial ecosystems corresponds to a one-unit increase in diversity in financial ecosystems. This suggests that each 1-unit increase in diversity in financial ecosystems positively influences the new energy systems by 0.0153 units. In contrast, the coefficient of 2Dz6 in Column (2) for UHV-importing provinces is not statistically significant, likely due to these provinces being less energy-intensive because of their energy resources, with energy consumption primarily dependent on external provinces, while local new energy resources remain insufficiently developed.

(2) Bird diversity. Intensive bird activity may risk transmission lines in emerging energy systems. Research indicates that avian accidents with transmission lines and nesting on these structures harm bird safety and may also impact the functionality of the transmission lines65,66. The collision of bird populations with wind energy structures, leading to mortality, may compromise the environmental advantages of advancing new energy sources67. Consequently, avian variety may be a pivotal factor influencing the evolution of the new energy systems. This research utilizes the number of bird species from 2014 to 2022, sourced from the China Bird Records Center. To address the incomplete coverage of the 2000–2022 period, we processed this data by calculating the average number of bird species per province. Each province’s average was then compared to the overall average of all provinces. If a province’s average exceeded the overall average, it is designated as Bird = 1; otherwise, it is defined as Bird = 0. The results of the regression test are presented in columns (3)-(4) of Table 7. In provinces exhibiting lower bird diversity indices, the coefficient of 2Dz6 in Column (3) is uniformly positive at the 1% significance level, signifying that diversity in financial ecosystems substantially enhances the new energy systems by 0.0154 units. Conversely, in provinces with elevated bird diversity indices, the coefficient of 2Dz6 in Column (4) is negative and statistically insignificant, likely due to the synchronization between bird diversity and nature reserves (Nature reserves are areas where a certain area is set aside for special protection and management in accordance with the law). Bird diversity and nature reserves are likely to be correlated. UHV transmission lines are intentionally excluded from the planning and placement of nature reserves to safeguard ecological diversity.

(3)Transportation history. The historical relationship between transportation and energy development is significant68. Transportation accessibility heavily influences energy development and transportation. The history of railroad transportation can enhance conditions along routes, thereby reducing transportation expenses, improving energy efficiency, and facilitating the energy transition69. Railroads serve as conveyors of products and resources, functioning as vital logistics corridors. By manually reviewing the relevant historical documents, we determine whether the railroad was inaugurated in 1933 as a historical feature of the new energy systems (see Appendix Table B7). If the railway passes through the sample provinces, it is designated Rail = 1; otherwise, Rail = 0. The outcomes of the regression test are presented in columns (5)-(6) of Table 7. In provinces with a limited transportation history, the coefficient of 2Dz6 in Column (5) exhibits a negative and statistically significant effect at the 5% level. Conversely, in provinces with an extensive transportation history, the coefficient of 2Dz6 in Column (6) is positive and significant at the 1% level, indicating that each unit increase in diversity in financial ecosystems corresponds to a 0.0379 unit increase in the new energy systems. Consequently, the presence or absence of an extensive transport history in a province is a distinguishing feature in determining whether diversity in financial ecosystems fosters the new energy systems.

Further discussion

Discussion on diversity in financial ecosystems

(1) Financial species abundance. To evaluate how variations in financial species abundance impact the new energy systems, researchers develop a new diversity index for financial ecosystems by incrementally increasing the number of financial species and reassessing the causal link. Beginning with four financial species, diversity indices with differing abundances of financial species, Dz4、Dz5, and Dz6, are calculated to align with the respective number of financial species present in the provinces housing these financial ecosystem components, thereby ensuring that adequately credible diversity characteristics are represented at various levels of financial coverage across distinct provincial groups. Dz4 is predicated on four primary financial categories: treasury, credit, equities, and funds. Dz5 and Dz6 subsequently incorporate bonds and insurance into the preceding index components, with the regression results presented in columns (1)-(3) of Table 8. The findings indicate that the coefficients of diversity in financial ecosystems are all statistically significant at the 10% significance level when the abundance of financial species fluctuates. As financial species proliferate, the coefficients of 2Dz4、2Dz5 and 2Dz6 are 0.0089, 0.0136, and 0.0155, respectively, indicating a favorable upward tendency. An increased richness of financial species enhances the promoting impact of diversity in financial ecosystems on the new energy systems.

(2)Financial Species Hierarchy. Hierarchy of Financial Species. To ascertain if varying degrees of financial diversity among “government support-financial institutions-capital market” result in differing impacts, the financial categories are classified into three tiers: government support (Dz_1), government support plus financial institutions (Dz_2), and government support plus financial institutions plus capital market (Dz_3). The regression outcomes are presented in columns (4)-(6) of Table 8. The findings indicate that government support alone is insufficient to effectively advance the development of the new energy systems. Nevertheless, suppose governmental assistance serves as a framework to enhance financial institutions and diversify funding sources within the capital market. In that case, the ecological diversity of finance significantly promotes the new energy systems. The coefficients for government support combined with financial institutions (Dz_2) and government support combined with financial institutions and capital market (Dz_3) are 0.0153 and 0.0155, respectively. As the levels of support from financial sources increase, the impact of diversity in financial ecosystems on the advancement of the new energy systems appears to stabilize.

(3) Differences in the mix of financial species in capital markets. There are variations in the composition of financial instruments within capital markets. To analyze the disparities among various portfolios in the capital market, three financial instruments—green stocks, green bonds, and green funds—are paired into three distinct portfolios: green stocks and green funds (Dz_p1), green stocks and green bonds (Dz_p2), and green funds and green bonds (Dz_p3). These portfolios are subsequently integrated into the hierarchy of government-supported financial institutions. The diversity in financial ecosystems is computed, and regression analysis is conducted, with the findings presented in columns (7)-(9) in Table 8. In Column (7), the coefficient of 2Dz5_p1 is 0.0097. The pronounced disparity between green stock and green fund species indicates that green funds exhibit more distinct characteristics of rare financial species. Consequently, as attention to rare financial species increases, the promotional impact of diversity in financial ecosystems on the new energy systems becomes more pronounced. In Column (8), the coefficient of 2Dz5_p2 is 0.0157; the growth of green stocks and green bonds is equivalent, and the increased emphasis on uncommon financial instruments does not greatly augment the promotional effect. In Column (9), the coefficient of 2Dz5_p3 is 0.0181, indicating a significant promotional impact and greater homogeneity among financial instruments. This phenomenon is likely attributable to the pronounced geographical characteristics of green fund and green bond issuance, which are predominantly concentrated in Beijing, Shanghai, and Guangdong Province. The findings indicate substantial disparities in the promotional impact of diversity in financial ecosystems on the new energy systems, as determined by various combinations of financial instruments in the capital market. Additionally, specifically directed new green funds and green bonds can enhance the promotional effect more efficiently.

Conclusions and policy implications

Conclusions

We utilize provincial panel data from 2000 to 2022 to quantify diversity in financial ecosystems and the new energy systems and empirically investigate the causative relationship and influence mechanisms between the two, with the primary conclusions outlined as follows:

Initially, from the four dimensions of “clean and low-carbon, safe and efficient, diversified synergy, and intelligent and inclusive,” 28 indicators are selected to develop and quantify the indicator system for the new energy system, employing the entropy method for objective weight assignment. The analysis indicates that new energy generation, specifically from wind and photovoltaic sources, decreases energy costs, with the essential aspect of developing the new energy systems being the pursuit of wind and solar energy.

Secondly, diversity in financial ecosystems significantly influences the new energy systems. A greater emphasis on rare financial species enhances the efficacy of constructing the new energy systems. Furthermore, diversity in financial ecosystems impacts the development of new energy systems via the consequences of technology innovation and digital effects. These effects are particularly pronounced in regions that do not depend on external power inputs, have a long history of transportation, and exhibit low avian biodiversity.

Thirdly, diversity in financial ecosystems substantially enhances the advancement of the three dimensions of cleanliness and low carbon, safety, efficiency, and multiple synergies; however, it exerts an inhibitory effect on the smart and inclusive dimension. From the standpoint of financial species abundance, a greater abundance correlates with a more pronounced promotional impact of diversity in financial ecosystems on the new energy systems.

Policy implications

Initially, enhance the diversity of the financial ecosystems. Enhance the proliferation of financial instruments, invent and refine varied investment and financing mechanisms, and broaden financial categories like green trusts, carbon finance, and climate finance. Enhance the significance of uncommon financial assets and address the funding challenges of developing new energy sources and revitalizing existing ones. Make financial resources fairer and help improve the financial system to support the overall growth of new energy systems, including developing and using new energy, innovating and improving energy technology, applying low-carbon methods to coal and carbon energy, and managing energy consumption digitally.

Secondly, enhance diversity in financial ecosystems to foster innovation in energy technology. The creation of specialized loans, funds, and bonds for energy technology innovation aims to support key research and development, construction, and promotion of demonstration projects. The hierarchical structure of the financial market allows investors to diversify their portfolios across various energy technology projects and firms, hence facilitating more capital influx into energy technology innovation. Enhancing diversity in financial ecosystems facilitates the transition between traditional and contemporary kinetic energy sources, actively promotes the development and utilization of renewable energy sectors such as wind, solar, hydrogen, and nuclear energy, and supports their technological advancements in energy efficiency and emissions reduction, intelligent modernization, and shift towards cleaner production, thereby augmenting their capacity for sustainable development.

Thirdly, diversity in financial ecosystems should be enhanced to facilitate the supply and consumption of the new energy systems. Expanding the financing avenues for digitization projects offers enhanced and varied financial backing for the digitization efforts within the supply side of the new energy systems, thereby fostering the profound integration and collaborative advancement of the finance and energy sectors. Establishing a multi-tiered financial ecosystem, optimizing species combinations within diverse financial ecosystems, augmenting varied funding sources for energy enterprises, bolstering the digital capabilities of the new energy systems management, power resource distribution, and energy services, enhancing the integration and synergy of “source, network, load, and storage,” and improving the acceptance and regulatory capacity of new energy sources, thereby facilitating the consumption of new energy. To advance the new energy consumption and the dual dynamics of “establishment” and “disruption”.

Predicting spontaneous preterm birth with cervical length and fetal fibronectin for symptomatic women of threatened preterm labor: A prospective study
Authors: Joanna C. Y. Fung, Piya Chaemsaithong, Yunyu Chen, Nutnaree Yuenyongdechawat, Hillary H. Y. Leung, Patricia N. P. Ip, Caitlyn S. L. Lau, Liona C. Poon
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThe aim of this study was to assess the effectiveness of fetal fibronectin (fFN) in combination with cervical length for predicting spontaneous preterm birth (sPTB) in women presenting with threatened preterm labor.Material and MethodsThis was a prospective study involving singleton pregnancies at 20–36 weeks of gestation with symptoms of threatened preterm labor. Upon admission, cervical length (CL) and fFN test in cervicovaginal secretions were measured. Logistic regression analysis was performed to develop the following models: CL alone, fFN at various thresholds, and their combined models for the prediction of sPTB. Area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) was calculated.ResultsA total of 398 cases were analyzed. The median gestational age at recruitment was 30.1 weeks. Among these cases, 55 (13.8%) cases had sPTB at <37 weeks of gestation. A history of preterm birth (HxPTB), shorter CL, and increased fFN level were independently associated with an increased risk of sPTB. AUROC of the fFN test, at ≥50 ng/mL or ≥200 ng/mL, was significantly higher than that of ≥500 ng/mL for predicting sPTB. For the prediction of sPTB, the AUROCs were 0.78 for CL, 0.78 for fFN, and 0.84 for the combination of HxPTB, CL, and fFN ≥50 ng/mL. The ROC curves showed that, at a false‐positive rate of 10%, the sensitivities were 53% for CL ≤2.44 cm, 33.9% for fFN ≥50 ng/mL, and 60% for the combination of HxPTB, CL, and fFN ≥50 ng/mL (p < 0.05).ConclusionsIn women symptomatic of threatened preterm labor, a combination of fFN and CL has improved the predictive performance of sPTB compared with either measure alone.

INTRODUCTION

Spontaneous preterm birth (sPTB) is the leading cause of neonatal morbidity and mortality in many parts of the world, including countries with high incomes and advanced healthcare facilities. 1 , 2 , 3 The underlying pathologies of sPTB are multifactorial. Despite recognizing its risk factors, such as a history of preterm birth (HxPTB), cervical surgeries, and previous miscarriage, sPTB can also occur in women without identifiable risk factors. 3 In contemporary obstetric practice, women presenting with symptoms of threatened preterm labor are frequently overtreated, as only 5%–13% will subsequently deliver preterm, and there are limited tools that are sensitive for short‐term prediction. 1 Consequently, accurately identifying women likely to deliver preterm among those presenting with threatened preterm labor will enable effective interventions, including the targeted administration of steroids, thereby potentially reducing neonatal morbidity and mortality.

Existing research has shown that sonographic cervical length (CL) and/or fetal fibronectin (fFN) in cervicovaginal secretions appear to be the most promising tools in predicting sPTB. 4 , 5 , 6 fFN is a high molecular weight glycoprotein, which plays an essential role in maintaining the integrity of the chorionic–decidual interface. Disruption of this interface often precedes the onset of sPTB and leads to the release of fFN into the cervicovaginal fluid. fFN has been demonstrated to have an excellent negative predictive value (NPV) for predicting symptomatic women who will not deliver within 2 weeks of testing. 7 , 8 However, its positive predictive value (PPV) is low, at approximately 20%, for predicting sPTB. 7 , 8 This could result in overtreatment for the majority of women who receive a positive result.

The efficacy of adding fFN to CL measurements for predicting sPTB remains controversial. Some studies have shown that CL is equally effective as fFN, 6 while others report that either CL or fFN alone demonstrates the best performance. 4 , 5 Conversely, some studies have observed that fFN enhances the prediction of sPTB when combined with CL. These contradictory results lead to different practices in evaluating and managing women who present with threatened preterm labor worldwide. 9 , 10

To date, limited studies have evaluated the additional value of the fFN test in predicting sPTB in women with threatened preterm labor in Asia. The aim of this study was to assess the performance of the fFN test in combination with CL measurements for predicting sPTB in Asian women presenting with symptoms of threatened preterm labor.

MATERIAL AND METHODS

This was a prospective observational study in a tertiary hospital in Hong Kong SAR, China, and a tertiary hospital in Bangkok, Thailand. Pregnant women who were admitted to the hospital with symptoms of threatened preterm labor were recruited to the study according to the study inclusion and exclusion criteria. The study inclusion criteria included women of age 18 or above, with singleton pregnancies, at gestation 20–36 weeks, presenting with symptoms of abdominal pain, uterine tightening or contractions, back pain, light vaginal bleeding, and vaginal pressure. Exclusion criteria included multiple pregnancies, those in active labor (defined by the presence of cervical dilatation of 3 cm or more), those with ruptured membranes, and those with fetal anomaly. Participants were excluded from further recruitment throughout the study period to avoid repeated participation.

Upon admission to the hospital, a sterile speculum examination was performed to collect cervicovaginal secretions by placing a sterile, polyester‐tipped swab into the posterior vaginal fornix for 30 s by one of the research team members (J. Fung, P. Chaemsaithong, N. Yuenyongdechatwat, H. Leung). The swab was then immersed in the specimen transport tube containing 1 mL extraction buffer. Two hundred microliters of the patient sample were pipetted into the sample application well of the Rapid fFN 10Q Cassette (Hologic, Marlborough, MA, USA), and the quantitative fFN concentration was measured by the analyzer machine (Rapid fFN 10Q Analyzer, Hologic, Marlborough, MA, USA) by the research team members immediately.

Transvaginal ultrasound was performed to measure the CL according to the Fetal Medicine Foundation protocol (www.fetalmedicine.org) by appropriately trained ultrasound providers. The management of the participants, including hospitalization and the administration of corticosteroids and tocolytics, was determined by the attending obstetricians, who were not aware of the fFN results. Study participants were managed according to standard clinical management following local protocols, with blinding to the fFN results.

The primary outcome was sPTB <37 weeks of gestation (<259 days). Secondary outcomes included sPTB within 7 and 14 days of assessment. The obstetric records of all cases delivering at <37 weeks were reviewed to determine whether the preterm birth was medically indicated or spontaneous. The latter included those with spontaneous onset of labor and those with preterm prelabor rupture of membranes.

Statistical analysis

The sample size calculation was based on the expected incidence of sPTB at <37 weeks of gestation (5%) for women presenting with symptoms of threatened preterm labor, and detecting a minimum area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) of 0.7. At a power of 80%, type I error rate of 0.05, a minimum of 378 women was required. After accounting for a 15% drop‐out rate, 445 women were needed for the recruitment of the study.

Normality of the data was tested using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Descriptive data were presented as mean (standard deviation) or median (interquartile range, IQR) for continuous variables, and as numbers and percentages for categorical variables. Comparisons between the outcome groups were performed using chi‐squared tests, continuity correction tests, and Fisher exact tests for categorical variables, and using Student t‐tests or Mann–Whitney U‐tests for continuous variables.

To assess whether the combination of CL measurement and fFN test could better identify women who would develop sPTB as compared with CL measurement alone, logistic regression analysis was performed to develop the following models: CL alone, fFN at various thresholds, and their combined models for the prediction of sPTB, sPTB within 7 days, and 14 days. A nomogram was built based on the multivariate regression model. The performance of each prediction model for the various sPTB outcomes was determined by using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. The AUROC, PPV, NPV, and detection rates at fixed false‐positive rates (FPRs) of 5% and 10% were presented. The differences in AUROC between the different prediction models were compared using the DeLong test. 11

Data analyses were performed using R, version 4.1.3 (The R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). 12

RESULTS

During the study period between October 2020 and December 2024, a total of 441 women with singleton pregnancy were recruited. Forty‐three cases were excluded due to loss of follow‐up (n = 22), iatrogenic preterm births (n = 13), and incomplete pregnancy outcome data in relation to preterm birth (n = 8). Indications for iatrogenic preterm birth included preeclampsia, intrauterine infection, fetal growth restriction, fetal distress, recurrent antepartum hemorrhage, placenta previa, vasa previa, and malpresentation. The remaining 398 cases consisted of 55 cases (13.8%) of sPTB at <37 weeks of gestation and 343 cases (86.2%) of term births. As per hospital protocols, 11.3% of patients received corticosteroids and 8% of patients received tocolytics.

Demographic and obstetric characteristics for the study participants are described in Table 1. The median gestational age at recruitment was 30.1 weeks (IQR 26.6–33.9). Among them, 306 (76.9%) women were of Chinese ethnicity, and 92 (23.1%) were of Southeast Asian ethnicity. CL and fFN results according to the outcome groups are presented in Table 2. Among the 55 cases of sPTB, 11 cases (20.0%) delivered at <34 weeks, 22 (40.0%), and 27 cases (49.1%) delivered within 7 and 14 days of assessment, respectively. Women with sPTB had significantly lower CL and higher levels of fFN than those with term birth (Table 2). Specifically, higher rates of fFN above each threshold (≥50, ≥200, and ≥500 ng/mL) were observed in women with sPTB than those with term birth.

The Delong test indicated that the AUROCs of Model_HxPTB+CL + fFN ≥50 and Model_HxPTB+CL + fFN ≥200 in predicting sPTB were significantly higher than any single measure alone (all p < 0.05). The ROC curves showed that, at a 10% FPR, the sensitivities were 53% for CL ≤2.44 cm, 33.9% for fFN ≥50 ng/mL, and 60% for the combination of HxPTB, CL, and fFN ≥50 ng/mL (p < 0.05). Model_HxPTB+CL + fFN ≥50 achieved an AUROC of 0.836 (95% CI: 0.773–0.836) with a sensitivity of 60%, a PPV of 49.3%, and an NPV of 93.4% at a 10% FPR. There was no statistical significance in AUROCs between Model_HxPTB+CL + fFN ≥50 and Model_HxPTB+CL + fFN ≥200. Given that Model_HxPTB+CL + fFN ≥50 exhibited slightly higher PPV, NPV, and sensitivity than those of the latter model, it was used to construct the nomogram prediction model (Figure 2).

Nomogram prediction model for sPTB. The probability of sPTB is calculated by drawing a line to the top Points axis for each variable. The points for each variable are then summed and located on the Total points line, and a vertical line is projected from the Total points axis to the bottom predicted Probability scale to obtain an individual probability of sPTB. For example, a woman with a history of preterm birth (30 points), fFN ≥50 ng/mL (32.5 points) and cervical length of 2.6 cm (52.5 points) has a total score of 115 points that converts to a probability of sPTB of 73%. fFN, fetal fibronectin; sPTB, spontaneous preterm birth.

Prediction of spontaneous preterm birth at <37 weeks within 7 days and within 14 days

In the univariate analysis, CL and the fFN test above each threshold were associated with an increased risk of sPTB within 7 days and within 14 days. In the multivariate analysis, CL and the fFN test ≥50 ng/mL or ≥200 ng/mL, but not ≥500 ng/mL, were independent predictors for sPTB within 7 days and within 14 days (Table S1).

For predicting sPTB within 7 days, there were no significant differences in AUROC between the fFN test alone and CL alone, except for fFN test ≥500 ng/mL that achieved a lower AUROC than CL alone (Table S2). Similar findings were observed for predicting sPTB within 14 days (Table S3).

In the prediction of sPTB within 7 days, Model_CL + fFN ≥50 and Model_CL + fFN ≥200 demonstrated a significant improvement in AUROC compared with the fFN test above each threshold, but did not demonstrate such an improvement when compared to CL alone (Table S2). In predicting sPTB within 14 days, Model_CL + fFN ≥50 and Model_CL + fFN ≥200 demonstrated a significant improvement in AUROC compared with the fFN test at ≥200 ng/mL, but did not demonstrate such an improvement when compared to the fFN test at ≥50 ng/mL (Table S3). Additionally, in the prediction of sPTB within 14 days, the AUROC of Model_CL + fFN ≥50 (0.907; 95% CI: 0.851–0.962) was significantly higher than that of CL alone (0.825; 95% CI: 0.732–0.919; p < 0.05), while Model_CL + fFN ≥200 did not demonstrate a significant improvement in AUROC compared with CL alone (Table S3).

There was no statistical difference in AUROCs between Model_CL + fFN ≥50 and Model_CL + fFN ≥200 for predicting sPTB within 7 days (Table S2). Similar findings were observed for predicting sPTB within 14 days (Table S2). A risk graph based on the model, including fFN and CL as predictors of sPTB within 7 days and within 14 days is illustrated in Figure S1 and Figure S2, respectively.

DISCUSSION

This study has demonstrated that, firstly, HxPTB, shorter CL, and increased fFN level are associated with an increased risk of sPTB in women presenting with threatened preterm labor. Secondly, in the prediction of the various sPTB outcomes, the fFN test, at either ≥50 ng/mL or ≥200 ng/mL, is superior in the AUROC to ≥500 ng/mL and demonstrates comparable performance to that of CL alone. Thirdly, in predicting sPTB, the AUROCs of Model_HxPTB+CL + fFN ≥50 and Model_HxPTB+CL + fFN ≥200 in predicting sPTB were significantly higher than any single measure alone.

In the prediction of sPTB within 7 days, models that integrated the fFN test and CL demonstrated a significant improvement in AUROC compared with the fFN test alone, but did not demonstrate such an improvement when compared to CL alone. In the prediction of sPTB within 14 days, Model_CL + fFN ≥50 demonstrates a significant improvement in AUROCs compared with either CL or the fFN test at ≥200 ng/mL.

The existing literature has shown contradictory results regarding the effectiveness of adding the fFN test to CL for predicting sPTB. Dehaene et al. carried out a systematic review of seven articles to investigate whether a combination of tests versus a single test of CL measurement or cervicovaginal biomarkers (fetal fibronectin) was more accurate in predicting preterm birth. The results demonstrated that the combination method was not superior when compared to the single method. Therefore, further research to compare available biomarkers had been recommended. 13

Abbott et al. reported a strong association between the concentrations of fFN and the rate of sPTB within 14 days. Increasing the fFN threshold from ≥50, ≥200 to ≥500 ng/mL increased the PPV from 19.7%, 37.0% to 46.2%. 14 However, we did not observe a greater benefit with a higher fFN threshold, as the fFN ≥500 ng/mL was less effective than the other two lower cut‐offs. Furthermore, multivariate analysis demonstrated that the fFN test ≥500 ng/mL was not associated with sPTB within 7 and 14 days when CL was included in the model.

A study by Van Baaren et al. involving 665 symptomatic women demonstrated that adding a fFN test of ≥50 ng/mL to the model based solely on CL resulted in a slight increase in PPV from 23.0% (95% CI: 19.0%–29.0%) to 27% (95% CI: 22.0%–33.0%) for predicting sPTB within 7 days. 15 Similarly, in our study, we demonstrated that incorporating the fFN test with a threshold of ≥50 ng/mL into a model with CL increased the PPV from 36.7% (95% CI: 19.9%–56.1%) to 44.1% (95% CI: 27.2%–62.1%) at a fixed 5% FPR. However, in the prediction of sPTB within 14 days, incorporating fFN ≥50 ng/mL into CL measurement resulted in a 14.9% increment in PPV, compared with CL alone. The discrepancies in the predictive performance of the combined model of CL and fFN among these studies may be explained by differences in the study populations, including ethnicity, variations in gestational age, and maternal demographics. The inclusion of cases with cervical dilatation of 3 cm could also contribute to the difference.

The key findings of this study demonstrate that the combination of HxPTB, CL, and fFN test provides better prediction of sPTB compared with either measure alone, effectively distinguishing women who are more likely to deliver preterm. We have developed a nomogram to assess the individual risk of sPTB by integrating a history of preterm birth, CL, and fFN test ≥50 ng/mL. In addition, we have established risk graphs for the sPTB with 7 and 14 days based on a model that combines CL and the fFN test of ≥50 ng/mL for clinical use, which provide a clear illustration of the risks of sPTB. The visual tool can be referenced for risk estimation and enables appropriate point‐of‐care management strategies for threatened preterm labor to be tailored individually for the at‐risk women. For instance, if a patient presents with a CL of 10 mm and fFN ≥50 ng/mL, the graph provides a clear estimation of her sPTB risk: 50% at <37 weeks within 7 days and 57% at <37 weeks within 14 days. With a more efficient point‐of‐care method to identify women at risk of sPTB, a personalized management approach can be initiated while balancing the benefits and risks of in utero transfer, hospitalization, and the use of corticosteroids and tocolytics. 16 , 17 , 18

The primary strength of this study lies in a rigorous methodology based on a blinded cohort. As both patients and attending obstetricians were unaware of the fFN results, the clinical management of threatened preterm labor was conducted according to local protocols. This is a prospective observational study to determine the predictive performance of fFN and sonographic CL measurement in the Asian population for predicting sPTB. For the limitations, some of the recruited cases were lost to follow‐up, leading to non‐response bias. The participants were provided with detailed explanations about the study procedures to minimize the risk of loss to follow‐up. Considering the possible differences in maternal characteristics among the Hong Kong and Thai cohorts, the predictive model has been adjusted to account for the effect of potential variables that may affect sPTB. Another limitation is that the number of cases with sPTB within 7 and 14 days was insufficient for developing a regression model that combines maternal characteristics. Additionally, we acknowledge that the confidence intervals are wide in the regression analysis, which is due to their relatively small number of cases for some variables such as fFN ≥200 ng/mL or fFN ≥500 ng/mL, and thus may lead to uncertainty in the estimates. Furthermore, the data were derived from an Asian population, which potentially hinders the generalizability of the findings to other populations. However, the incidence of sPTB and the demographics of the study populations are comparable to those reported in other studies populations. 1 , 15 Future studies with a larger sample size are warranted to validate our findings in a more diverse cohort.

CONCLUSION

In women symptomatic of threatened preterm labor, a combination of fFN and CL has improved the predictive performance of sPTB compared with either measure alone. Future prospective studies are needed to externally validate these findings in a diverse population.

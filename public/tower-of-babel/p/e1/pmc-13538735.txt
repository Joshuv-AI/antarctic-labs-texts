Efficacy and Safety of iGlarLixi Versus IDegAsp by Baseline Age, Disease Duration and HbA1c in Chinese People With Type 2 Diabetes: Post Hoc Analyses of the Soli‐D Study
Authors: Saichun Zhang, Yunjuan Gu, Yu Cheng, Ying Hu, Bing Li, Agustina Alvarez, Felipe Lauand, Lydie Melas‐Melt, Minlu Zhang, Yini Jiang, Qin Du, Lei Kang, Yiming Mu, Weijun Gu, Hongyu Kuang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAimsTo compare the efficacy and safety of insulin glargine 100 U/mL plus lixisenatide (iGlarLixi) with insulin degludec plus insulin aspart (IDegAsp) by baseline age, Type 2 diabetes (T2D) duration and glycated haemoglobin (HbA1c) in the Soli‐D study.Materials and MethodsIn Soli‐D, Chinese adults with T2D suboptimally controlled on oral antidiabetic drugs (OADs) were randomized to iGlarLixi or IDegAsp for 24 weeks. These post hoc analyses evaluated glycaemic efficacy, insulin dose, body weight and hypoglycaemia outcomes in subgroups defined by baseline age (< 65, ≥ 65 years), T2D duration (< 10, ≥ 10 years) and HbA1c (≥ 7% to ≤ 8% [≥ 53 to ≤ 64 mmol/mol], > 8% to ≤ 9% [> 64 to ≤ 75 mmol/mol], > 9% [> 75 mmol/mol]).ResultsAmong 582 participants (iGlarLixi n = 291; IDegAsp n = 291), baseline age was < 65 years in 442 and ≥ 65 years in 140; T2D duration was < 10 years in 366 and ≥ 10 years in 216; and HbA1c was ≥ 7% to ≤ 8% in 205, > 8% to ≤ 9% in 209 and > 9% in 168. At Week 24, HbA1c reductions were greater with iGlarLixi versus IDegAsp, with no treatment‐by‐subgroup interactions for baseline age, T2D duration or HbA1c. Change in other glycaemic outcomes, insulin dose and body weight generally showed no interaction across subgroups. Total insulin daily doses during treatment and hypoglycaemia event rates were consistently lower with iGlarLixi versus IDegAsp in all subgroups.ConclusionsiGlarLixi provides improved glycaemic control at lower insulin doses with reduced risk of hypoglycaemia in Chinese adults with suboptimally controlled T2D on OADs, regardless of baseline age, disease duration or HbA1c.Keywords: basal insulin, GLP‐1 analogue, glycaemic control, iGlarLixi, type 2 diabetes

Introduction

In China, the 2024 prevalence of diabetes among adults (aged 20–79 years) was 13.8%, corresponding to 148 million individuals [1]. According to the International Diabetes Federation, the number of adults with diabetes in China is predicted to increase to 164 million by 2050 [1]. Type 2 diabetes (T2D), which accounts for > 90% of all cases of diabetes worldwide [1], is a progressive disease that often requires treatment escalation to achieve and maintain target glycated haemoglobin (HbA1c) levels [2]. In individuals with suboptimally controlled T2D on oral antidiabetic drug (OAD) monotherapy (usually metformin), the 2024 Chinese Diabetes Society guidelines recommend dual therapy with one additional class of antidiabetic agent, including fixed‐ratio combinations (FRCs) of basal insulin and glucagon‐like peptide‐1 receptor agonists (GLP‐1 RAs) [3].

iGlarLixi, a once‐daily, titratable, FRC of insulin glargine 100 U/mL plus lixisenatide, has demonstrated improved glycaemic control and safety compared with either of its individual components alone in the global LixiLan clinical trial programme [4, 5, 6]. The phase 3, randomized, head‐to‐head Soli‐D study in Chinese adults with suboptimally controlled T2D on previous metformin treatment (±another OAD) also showed significantly improved glycaemic control and body weight benefits with iGlarLixi compared with a premixed insulin (insulin degludec plus insulin aspart [IDegAsp]) over a 24‐week treatment period [7]. iGlarLixi was also associated with a lower insulin dose and a reduced risk of hypoglycaemia compared with IDegAsp [7]. In previous exploratory analyses of Soli‐D, iGlarLixi showed improved glycaemic control over IDegAsp across subgroups defined by baseline β‐cell function, as measured by fasting C‐peptide or Homeostasis Model Assessment of β‐cell function (HOMA‐β) levels [8].

In the current post hoc exploratory analyses of the Soli‐D study, we evaluated the effect of baseline age, T2D duration and HbA1c on clinical outcomes in Chinese adults with suboptimally controlled T2D on previous OAD treatment.

Materials and Methods

Study Design

The study design of the phase 3, open‐label, randomized Soli‐D trial (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT05413369) has been described in detail previously [7]. Briefly, Chinese adults (aged ≥ 18 years) with suboptimally controlled T2D on metformin ± a sodium–glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitor (HbA1c 7.5%–11.0% [58–97 mmol/mol]) or metformin + a non‐SGLT2 inhibitor OAD (HbA1c 7.0%–10.0% [53–86 mmol/mol]) were eligible to participate in the study. Participants received once‐daily self‐administered treatment for 24 weeks with either iGlarLixi (Soliqua; Sanofi, Paris, France), administered in the hour before the first meal of the day, or IDegAsp (Ryzodeg 70/30; Novo Nordisk, Bagsværd, Denmark), administered before the largest meal of the day (same time throughout the study).

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and the International Council for Harmonization of Technical Requirements for Pharmaceuticals for Human Use guidelines for Good Clinical Practice. All participants provided written informed consent prior to the start of the study.

Post Hoc Analyses

These subgroup analyses assessed efficacy and safety outcomes with iGlarLixi versus IDegAsp according to baseline age (< 65 or ≥ 65 years), baseline T2D duration (< 10 or ≥ 10 years) and baseline HbA1c (≥ 7% to ≤ 8% [≥ 53 to ≤ 64 mmol/mol], > 8% to ≤ 9% [> 64 to ≤ 75 mmol/mol] or > 9% [> 75 mmol/mol]). The baseline age and HbA1c subgroup analyses were prespecified in the study protocol, while the baseline T2D duration subgroup analysis was exploratory.

The following efficacy outcomes were assessed across subgroups: change from baseline to Week 24 in HbA1c (primary endpoint); proportion of participants at HbA1c < 7.0% (< 53 mmol/mol) at Week 24; proportion of participants achieving composite outcomes (HbA1c < 7.0% without body weight gain or HbA1c < 7.0% without body weight gain and without hypoglycaemia [American Diabetes Association (ADA) Level 1, 2 or 3]) [2]; and change from baseline to Week 24 in fasting plasma glucose (FPG), 2‐h postprandial glucose (PPG), average 7‐point self‐monitored plasma glucose (SMPG) and body weight; and total daily insulin dose at Week 24.

Safety was assessed by the event rate of ADA Level 1, 2 or 3 hypoglycaemia at Week 24, defined as follows: Level 1 = a measurable plasma glucose of ≥ 3.0 to < 3.9 mmol/L; Level 2 = a measurable plasma glucose < 3.0 mmol/L; and Level 3 = a severe hypoglycaemia event characterised by altered mental and/or physical functioning requiring assistance [2]. Hypoglycaemia events were self‐reported by participants in their study diary, then the details (onset date and time, symptoms and/or signs, SMPG value [if available] and treatment) were recorded in the electronic case report form. No adjudication of hypoglycaemia events was performed.

Statistical Analysis

Descriptive statistics were used to present the data, including means and standard deviations (SD) for continuous variables, and the number and proportions of participants for binary variables.

Efficacy outcomes were assessed in the intent‐to‐treat (ITT) population, which comprised all randomised study participants. For continuous efficacy outcomes, the analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) model was used to estimate the least‐square mean (LSM) difference between iGlarLixi and IDegAsp and its 95% confidence interval (CI). Treatment arm, randomisation strata (HbA1c, previous OAD use), treatment‐by‐subgroup interaction term and the subgroup factor term were included as fixed effects, and the baseline value of the corresponding outcome was included as the covariate. Missing data for continuous outcomes were imputed using the Markov Chain Monte Carlo and regression multiple imputation method, as described in the Supporting Information. For binary efficacy outcomes, the odds ratio (OR) for achievement with iGlarLixi versus IDegAsp (and its 95% CI) was estimated by logistic regression, with adjustment for treatment arm, randomisation strata (HbA1c, previous OADs) and continuous baseline value of the corresponding outcome. Participants with missing HbA1c or body weight data at Week 24 were considered as non‐responders in the composite outcomes.

Hypoglycaemia event rates were evaluated in the safety population, defined as all ITT participants who received at least one dose of study drug. For hypoglycaemia outcomes, a negative binomial model was used to estimate the rate ratio (RR) with iGlarLixi versus IDegAsp (and its 95% CI), with adjustment for treatment arm and randomisation strata (HbA1c, previous OADs), and the logarithm of the randomised treatment duration (in years) was used as an offset variable.

All p‐values for treatment‐by‐subgroup interactions (p int) were calculated for the treatment arm × baseline age, T2D duration or HbA1c subgroup multiplicative term and were considered nominal, without adjustment for multiplicity or no formal hypothesis testing. All statistical analyses were undertaken using SAS version 9.4 software (SAS Institute; Cary, NC, USA).

Results

Study Participants

The ITT population of the Soli‐D study included 582 Chinese adults with suboptimally controlled T2D [7], of whom 291 participants received iGlarLixi and 291 received IDegAsp for 24 weeks. The safety population comprised 581 participants (iGlarLixi: n = 290; IDegAsp: n = 291). When stratified by baseline age, 442 participants were aged < 65 years (iGlarLixi: n = 226; IDegAsp: n = 216) and 140 were aged ≥ 65 years (iGlarLixi: n = 65; IDegAsp: n = 75; Figure S1). At baseline, the T2D duration was < 10 years in 366 participants (iGlarLixi: n = 191; IDegAsp: n = 175) and ≥ 10 years in 216 participants (iGlarLixi: n = 100; IDegAsp: n = 116). Baseline HbA1c was ≥ 7% to ≤ 8% (≥ 53 to ≤ 64 mmol/mol) in 205 participants (iGlarLixi: n = 103; IDegAsp: n = 102), > 8% to ≤ 9% (> 64 to ≤ 75 mmol/mol) in 209 participants (iGlarLixi: n = 100; IDegAsp: n = 109) and > 9% (> 75 mmol/mol) in 168 participants (iGlarLixi: n = 88; IDegAsp: n = 80).

Demographic, baseline and disease characteristics were balanced between treatment arms across subgroups defined by baseline age (Table S1), baseline T2D duration (Table S2) and baseline HbA1c (Table S3).

Glycaemic Control

Change in HbA1c

From baseline to Week 24, iGlarLixi was associated with numerically greater reductions in HbA1c versus IDegAsp, with no treatment‐by‐subgroup interaction across subgroups defined by baseline age (p int = 0.1970; Figure 1A), baseline T2D duration (p int = 0.2850; Figure 1B) or baseline HbA1c (p int = 0.9348; Figure 1C). In subgroups defined by baseline age, the LSM difference between iGlarLixi and IDegAsp was −0.25% (−2.7 mmol/mol) among participants aged < 65 years and −0.04% (−0.4 mmol/mol) in those aged ≥ 65 years. When assessed by baseline T2D duration, the LSM difference between iGlarLixi and IDegAsp was −0.14% (−1.5 mmol/mol) and −0.29% (−3.2 mmol/mol) in participants with T2D duration < 10 and ≥ 10 years, respectively. According to baseline HbA1c, the LSM difference between treatments was −0.23% (−2.5 mmol/mol), −0.21% (−2.3 mmol/mol) and −0.16% (−1.7 mmol/mol) in participants with a baseline HbA1c ≥7% to ≤ 8% (≥ 53 to ≤ 64 mmol/mol), > 8% to ≤ 9% (> 64 to ≤ 75 mmol/mol) and > 9% (> 75 mmol/mol), respectively.

Other Glycaemic Outcomes

For most other glycaemic outcomes with iGlarLixi versus IDegAsp, there were no treatment‐by‐subgroup interactions in subgroups defined by baseline age (Table 1), baseline T2D duration (Table 2) and baseline HbA1c (Table 3), although there were two exceptions to this. The first was an interaction between baseline age and the proportion of participants with HbA1c < 7% without body weight gain at Week 24 (p int = 0.0229; Table 1). For achievement of HbA1c < 7% without body weight gain at Week 24, the OR was 3.43 for iGlarLixi versus IDegAsp in participants aged < 65 years and 1.30 in those aged ≥ 65 years. The second exception was an interaction between baseline T2D duration and the change in average 7‐point SMPG at Week 24 (p int = 0.046; Table 2). The LSM difference between iGlarLixi and IDegAsp for the average 7‐point SMPG was −0.30 mmol/L (95% CI −0.65, +0.05) in participants with T2D for < 10 years and −0.89 mmol/L (95% CI −1.35, −0.43) in those with T2D for ≥ 10 years.

iGlarLixi versus IDegAsp.

Total Daily Insulin Dose

No treatment‐by‐subgroup interaction was observed between total daily insulin dose at Week 24 and baseline age (p int = 0.4767), baseline T2D duration (p int = 0.3031) or baseline HbA1c (p int = 0.7784; Table S4). In baseline age subgroups, the LSM difference in Week 24 total daily insulin dose between iGlarLixi and IDegAsp was −6.38 U (95% CI −8.99, −3.78) among participants aged < 65 years and −6.33 U (95% CI −11.05, −1.61) in those aged ≥ 65 years. When assessed by baseline T2D duration, the LSM difference in total daily insulin dose between iGlarLixi and IDegAsp in participants with T2D duration < 10 and ≥ 10 years was −6.25 U (95% CI −9.18, −3.33) and −6.17 U (95% CI −9.99, −2.35), respectively. According to baseline HbA1c, the iGlarLixi vs. IDegAsp LSM difference in total daily insulin dose was −5.18 U (95% CI −9.05, −1.32), −6.71 U (95% CI −10.52, −2.89) and −6.63 U (95% CI −10.87, −2.40) in participants with a baseline HbA1c ≥ 7% to ≤ 8% (≥53 to ≤64 mmol/mol), > 8% to ≤ 9% (> 64 to ≤ 75 mmol/mol) and > 9% (> 75 mmol/mol), respectively.

The total daily insulin dose was lower with iGlarLixi versus IDegAsp throughout treatment in subgroups defined by baseline age (Figure S2A), baseline T2D duration (Figure S2B) or baseline HbA1c (Figure S2C). At Week 24, the mean ± SD change from starting dose was 21.82 ± 11.22 U with iGlarLixi versus 28.03 ± 16.51 U with IDegAsp in participants aged < 65 years and 16.55 ± 9.74 versus 21.57 ± 14.25 U in the respective treatment arms in those aged ≥ 65 years. According to baseline T2D duration, the mean ± SD change in insulin dose with iGlarLixi versus IDegAsp was 21.19 ± 10.67 versus 27.48 ± 16.24 U in participants with T2D duration < 10 years and 19.71 ± 11.91 versus 24.63 ± 16.00 U in those with T2D duration ≥ 10 years. When assessed by baseline HbA1c, the mean ± SD change in insulin dose with iGlarLixi versus IDegAsp was 17.07 ± 10.14 versus 22.47 ± 12.76 U, 22.07 ± 12.10 versus 27.76 ± 16.61 U and 23.23 ± 10.00 versus 29.24 ± 18.54 U in the baseline HbA1c ≥ 7% to ≤ 8% (≥ 53 to ≤ 64 mmol/mol), > 8% to ≤ 9% (> 64 to ≤ 75 mmol/mol) and > 9% (> 75 mmol/mol) subgroups, respectively.

Change in Body Weight

No treatment‐by‐subgroup interaction was observed between change in body weight from baseline to Week 24 and baseline age (p int = 0.8114; Figure S3A), baseline T2D duration (p int = 0.4949; Figure S3B) and baseline HbA1c (p int = 0.7750; Figure S3C). Body weight changes showed a trend towards benefit with iGlarLixi versus IDegAsp across all subgroups defined by baseline age, T2D duration and HbA1c.

Hypoglycaemia Events

In general, no treatment‐by‐subgroup interactions were observed between hypoglycaemia outcomes (any hypoglycaemia, ADA Level 1 hypoglycaemia or ADA Level 2 hypoglycaemia) and baseline age (Table S5), baseline T2D duration (Table S6) and baseline HbA1c (Table S7). The one exception to this was a treatment‐by‐interaction between any hypoglycaemia and baseline HbA1c (p int = 0.0306). The RR for any hypoglycaemia event with iGlarLixi versus IDegAsp decreased from 1.28 in participants with baseline HbA1c ≥ 7% to ≤ 8% (≥ 53 to ≤ 64 mmol/mol) to 0.64 and 0.45 in those with a baseline HbA1c > 8% to ≤ 9% (> 64 to ≤ 75 mmol/mol) and > 9% (> 75 mmol/mol), respectively.

Discussion

This post hoc analysis of the Soli‐D study showed that iGlarLixi had consistent efficacy and safety compared with IDegAsp, regardless of age, T2D duration or HbA1c at baseline.

Effective management of older individuals with T2D is often challenging for clinicians because of their differences in clinical, functional and cognitive function [9]. Individuals with longstanding disease may also have reduced response to GLP‐1 RA therapy caused by decline in β‐cell function [10]. Additionally, higher HbA1c at baseline has been associated with failure to achieve glycaemic targets [11]. The complementary mechanisms of action of iGlarLixi, with the insulin component reducing FPG and the GLP‐1 RA component lowering PPG by delaying gastric emptying and reducing prandial glucagon secretion [4, 5, 6], may be beneficial in older individuals with longstanding disease and/or high HbA1c. Therefore, the findings of our analysis support the use of iGlarLixi as a preferred treatment option for older Chinese people with T2D inadequately controlled with OAD therapy, including those with longstanding disease duration. Indeed, the Chinese National Center of Gerontology, Chinese Society of Geriatrics and Diabetes Professional Committee of Chinese Aging Well Association 2024 guidelines for diabetes management in older people recommend considering FRCs in older individuals as appropriate, based on their overall clinical condition [12].

In our post hoc analysis of the Soli‐D study, iGlarLixi was associated with greater HbA1c reductions from baseline to Week 24 than IDegAsp, regardless of baseline age, T2D duration or HbA1c. These findings are consistent with those of a previous post hoc analysis of the LixiLan‐O‐AP and LixiLan‐L‐CN studies, which demonstrated consistent improvements in glycaemic control with iGlarLixi versus either of its individual components alone, without increasing the risk of body weight gain or hypoglycaemia, across all age and disease duration subgroups [13]. Furthermore, previous exploratory analyses of the LixiLan‐O and LixiLan‐L studies have reported consistently improved glycaemic control with iGlarLixi over its individual components across all baseline characteristic subgroups, including participants with high HbA1c (≥ 8% [≥ 64 mmol/mol]) and long T2D duration (≥ 7 or ≥ 10 years) [14, 15]. A subgroup analysis of the SoliMix study of iGlarLixi versus premixed biphasic insulin aspart 30/70 (BIAsp30) also showed consistent benefits with iGlarLixi across participant subgroups defined by baseline age, T2D duration and HbA1c, as well as daily basal insulin dose, body mass index and renal function [16, 17].

Higher proportion of participants in the iGlarLixi versus IDegAsp arm achieved HbA1c < 7% and composite targets of HbA1c < 7% without body weight gain and HbA1c < 7% without body weight gain and without hypoglycaemia at Week 24, regardless of baseline age, T2D duration or HbA1c. One exception to this was achievement of HbA1c < 7% without body weight gain, where the OR for iGlarLixi versus IDegAsp was greater in participants aged < 65 years than in those aged ≥ 65 years. This difference between age subgroups may have been caused by outcome bias as a result of the small number of participants aged ≥ 65 years. Similarly, the subgroup analysis of SoliMix showed consistent achievement of HbA1c target and composite target outcomes with iGlarLixi versus BIAsp across all subgroups defined by baseline characteristics [16].

Similar to the primary Soli‐D study analysis [7], there was no difference between iGlarLixi and IDegAsp for the change in FPG from baseline to Week 24 in any of the subgroups defined by baseline age, T2D duration or HbA1c. However, iGlarLixi was associated with greater reductions in average 7‐point SMPG and 2‐h PPG than IDegAsp, with generally consistent between‐group differences observed across subgroups. One exception to this was the change in 7‐point SMPG, for which the LSM difference for iGlarLixi versus IDegAsp was greater in participants with T2D for ≥ 10 years than in those with T2D for < 10 years. This may have been because individuals with longstanding T2D, who generally have greater impairment of β‐cell function [18], had insufficient dose titration in the IDegAsp group for adequate control of 7‐point SMPG. This suggests that iGlarLixi may provide better overall glycaemic control than IDegAsp in individuals with long duration of T2D, mainly due to its short‐acting GLP‐1 RA component [4, 5, 6]. In routine clinical practice, improved PPG control with iGlarLixi may translate to reduced overall glycaemic variability, which is recognised by the ADA 2026 guidelines as being an important therapeutic target beyond HbA1c control [2]. Furthermore, an observational study of people with T2D has demonstrated that increased glycaemic variability is independently associated with increased cardiovascular risk [19].

In addition to improved glycaemic outcomes, iGlarLixi was associated with lower total daily insulin dose, which may correspond to lower overall treatment burden with iGlarLixi versus IDegAsp. However, there was no difference between treatments for the change in total daily insulin dose from baseline to Week 24 in any of the baseline age, T2D duration, or HbA1c subgroups. Our study also showed body weight benefits and reduced hypoglycaemia risk with iGlarLixi over IDegAsp across all subgroups. One exception to this was a difference in the risk of any hypoglycaemia with iGlarLixi versus IDegAsp, which was highest in participants with baseline HbA1c ≥ 7% to ≤ 8% (≥ 53 to ≤ 64 mmol/mol) and lowest in those with baseline HbA1c > 9% (> 75 mmol/mol). This highlights the need for individualisation of HbA1c targets to avoid an increased risk of hypoglycaemia, as recommended by the ADA [2], particularly among those with close‐to‐target HbA1c levels prior to starting insulin‐based therapy. The body weight benefits and reduced hypoglycaemia risk with iGlarLixi versus IDegAsp may also lead to improved treatment adherence in this setting, although this outcome was not assessed in the Soli‐D study [7]. However, the real‐world SIMPLE study, which was conducted in the United States, demonstrated improved adherence, alongside reduced hypoglycaemia risk and greater body weight benefits, among adults with T2D who received a simplified regimen of basal insulin plus GLP‐1 RA versus basal‐bolus insulin [20].

The limitations of this analysis include its post hoc, exploratory nature, which means that the sample size and power calculations of the primary endpoint of Soli‐D may not be applicable to these findings. In addition, Soli‐D was a phase 3 randomised trial in Chinese people with T2D; therefore, further real‐world studies are needed to confirm the benefits of iGlarLixi over IDegAsp in other T2D populations.

In conclusion, this post hoc analysis of the Soli‐D study demonstrates that iGlarLixi is an efficacious, safe and well‐tolerated treatment option for a broad range of individuals with suboptimally controlled T2D on OADs in China. The consistent glycaemic benefits of iGlarLixi regardless of age, disease duration or HbA1c at baseline, as well as favourable effects on body weight and risk of hypoglycaemia, support its use as a preferred option for treatment intensification among individuals with suboptimally controlled T2D on OAD therapy.

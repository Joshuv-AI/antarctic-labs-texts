Risk factors for unplanned weaning of continuous renal replacement therapy in ICU patients: a meta-analysis
Authors: Zhi Zeng, Yuqi Shen, Li Wan, Xiuru Yang, Zhenghua Liang, Mei He
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo systematically review the risk factors for unplanned weaning during continuous renal replacement therapy in ICU patients.MethodsA combination of subject words + free words was used to search the relevant literature published in CNKI, Wanfang, VIP, CBM, PubMed, EMbase, Web of Science, Cochrane Library, Mediline and other databases. The search period was from the establishment of the databases to June 25, 2024. Revman 5.4 software and Stata15.0 software was used to meta-analyze the risk factors for unplanned weaning during continuous renal replacement therapy in ICU patients.ResultsA total of 23 studies were included in this meta-analysis, describing 15 variables, 3793 patients, and using 7197 filters. Meta-analysis results showed that risk factors for unplanned weaning during continuous renal replacement therapy in ICU patients were as follows: Low mean arterial pressure [OR = 1.02, 95%CI (1.00, 1.03), p < 0.05], hypothermia [OR = 3.40, 95%CI (1.78, 6.47), p < 0.05], age (≥60 years) [OR = 4.45, 95%CI (3.18, 6.22), p < 0.05], comorbid underlying disease [OR = 3.63, 95%CI (2.70, 4.88), p < 0.05], agitation [OR = 4.97, 95%CI (3.20, 7.74), p < 0.05], no anticoagulant use [OR = 1.65, 95%CI (1.25, 2.17), p < 0.05], short activated partial prothrombin time [OR = 1.23, 95%CI (1.13, 1.34), p < 0.05], hyper-hematocrit [OR = 1.73, 95%CI (1.13, 2.66), p = 0.01], low ionized calcium concentration [OR = 1.48, 95% CI (1.08, 2.02), p = 0.01], CRRT that was treated at a high dose [OR = 1.42, 95%CI (1.14, 1.76), p < 0.05], mechanical ventilation [OR = 4.25, 95%CI (2.67, 6.77), p < 0.05], and lack of dedicated care [OR = 5.08, 95%CI (2.51, 10.28), p < 0.05]. However, it is unclear whether platelet count, prothrombin activity, and blood flow velocity are risk factors for unplanned weaning during CRRT in ICU patients, and more studies are needed for further validation.ConclusionAvailable evidence suggests that a variety of factors contribute to unplanned weaning of CRRT in ICU patients. Early detection of these risk factors is essential for healthcare professionals to develop preventive and curative strategies.RegistrationThis study is registered on the PROSERO website under registration number CRD42024543554.

Background

Continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT) is a treatment method that replaces impaired renal function with 24 h, or close to 24 h of continuous extracorporeal blood purification therapy. This continuously removes cytotoxins, metabolic wastes, and inflammatory factors from the patient’s body through diffusion or convection, thereby improving the patient’s tissue oxygen metabolism and maintaining the homeostasis of the body’s internal environment [1]. Moreover, it has been widely used in the treatment of ICU patients [2,3]. However, ICU patients often experience unplanned weaning from CRRT due to coagulopathy, weakened immunity, and multi-organ dysfunction [4]. Unplanned weaning during CRRT refers to termination of treatment without achieving the dialysis treatment goals or completing the treatment schedule. Clinical studies have shown that the incidence of unplanned weaning during CRRT ranges from 17.14–74.51% [5]. Unplanned weaning not only affects the treatment results, but also leads to adverse consequences such as blood loss, aggravation of anemia and coagulation dysfunction. It also increases the cost of treatment for patients, increases the workload of ICU medical staff, and exacerbates the waste of ICU medical resources [6,7].

Early identification of risk factors is key to effectively preventing unplanned weaning during CRRT in ICU patients. Although several studies have explored risk factors for unplanned weaning during CRRT in adult ICU patients, the results are inconsistent and lack systematization and comprehensiveness. Therefore, this study used the method of a systematic review to comprehensively sort and analyze various potential risk factors for unplanned weaning during CRRT, in adult ICU patients. This was done in order to provide a basis for clinical medical staff to formulate targeted prevention strategies, so as to reduce the incidence of unplanned weaning of CRRT, and improve the treatment effect and prognosis of ICU patients.

Methods

The protocol of this study has been registered in the International Register of Prospective Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO CRD 42021272492). We reported according to the list of preferred reporting items for systematic reviews and meta-analyses (PRISMA).

Search strategy

CNKI, Wanfang, VIP, CBM, PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, Web of Science, Medline and other Chinese and English databases were systematically searched from inception to June 25, 2024.When searching, combine subject headings and free words, including "Intensive care unit/Intensive Care Unit/Unit, Intensive Care/ICU Intensive Care Units", "continuous renal replacement therapy/CRRT", "extracorporeal circulation clotting/filter lifespan/circuit lifeblood coagulation", "weaning OR discontinuation OR cessation", "risk factor*/population* at risks/predictor/correlat*/influen*/inciden/relevant factors/associatefactors/relat*"combined. At the same time, references cited in the included studies were retrospectively included in order to seek additional studies. The full search strategy for the primary database is given in Figure 1.

PubMed search strategy.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria

(1) Subjects: Adult patients undergoing CRRT in ICU; (2) Study type: cohort study and case-control study; (3) Research content: unplanned weaning occurred during CRRT in ICU patients; (4) The statistical method is correct, the data is complete, and the relevant data, odds ratio (OR) and 95% CI or convertible raw data can be provided.

Exclusion criteria

(1) Reviews, case studies, conference papers; (2) Literature that has been repeatedly published or the full text is not available; (3) Literature with inconsistent assignment of influencing factors; (4) Literature in languages other than Chinese and English.

Literature screening and data extraction

After the literature was imported into NoteExpress 3.0 software for deduplication, in order to avoid data collection errors, two researchers (Zhi Zeng and Yuqi Shen) independently screened the literature and extracted the included literature. After data extraction, the data were exchanged and checked, and when there was a disagreement, the third researchers (Li Wan) was sought to help make a decision. The extracted content included: first author, year of publication, country, sample size, study type, risk factors, etc.

Literature quality evaluation

The literature quality evaluation was independently conducted by two researchers (Zhi Zeng and Yuqi Shen), and the Newcastle-Ottawa scale (NOS) was used to evaluate the literature quality, which was composed of three dimensions: study population, inter-group comparability, and outcome measures, with a total score of 9, and a score of more than 6 was considered high-quality research [8]. When a difference of opinion arises, a third researcher (Li Wan) participates and discusses the results until a consensus is reached.

Statistical methods

Meta-analysis was performed using RevMan 5.4 software and Stata 15.0 software. Cohen’s kappa statistic was used to assess the reliability of the mutual estimators. The outcome measures of the influencing factors of CRRT unplanned weaning in ICU patients were described using OR and its 95% CI. Heterogeneity analyses of the included studies were performed using the Q-test and I². When the statistical heterogeneity between studies was small (p ≥ 0.10, I²≤50%), the fixed-effect model was used. On the other hand, when there was a large statistical heterogeneity between studies (p < 0.10, I²>50%), the random-effects model was used. The robustness of the results of meta-analysis was explored by sensitivity analysis and the pooled effect model of change. If the heterogeneity was large, the source of heterogeneity was found by meta-regression analysis, and subgroup analysis was performed on the variables with significant correlation in the meta-regression analysis. Funnel plots and Egger’s test were used to assess potential for publication bias. When there is asymmetry in the funnel chart, the trimming and filling method is further adopted. The difference of p < 0.05 was statistically significant.

Results

Literature search results

A total of 2601 studies were searched, 1475 duplicate studies were excluded, and the remaining 137 studies were assessed full-text after reading the titles and abstracts. 114 studies were further excluded based on the following criteria: (i) subject irrelevance, (ii) participants did not match, (iii) statistics were not available, (iv) non-Chinese and English literature. Finally, a total of 23 articles [9–31] were included. Of the included studies, 4 cases were in the case control study [13,17,20,21], 10 in the retrospective cohort study [10–12,15,18,22,26,27,30,31], and 9 in the prospective cohort study [9,14,16,19,23–25,28,29]. Results of meta-analysis

A total of 15 influencing factors were included, and there was significant heterogeneity in the results of activated partial thromboplastin time (APTT), hematocrit (HT), mean arterial pressure (MAP), and no anticoagulant, so a random-effects model was used for meta-analysis. The remaining results were less heterogeneous and a fixed-effect model was used for meta-analysis. See Figure 3 for details.

The results of meta-analysis showed that low MAP [OR = 1.02, 95%CI (1.00, 1.03), p < 0.05], hypothermia [OR = 3.40, 95%CI (1.78, 6.47), p < 0.05], age (≥60 years) [OR = 4.45, 95%CI (3.18, 6.22), p < 0.05], and combined underlying diseases [OR = 3.63, 95%CI (2.70, 4.88), p < 0.05], agitation [OR = 4.97, 95%CI (3.20, 7.74), p < 0.05], no use of anticoagulants [OR = 1.65, 95%CI (1.25, 2.17), p < 0.05], short APTT [OR = 1.23, 95%CI (1.13, 1.34), p < 0.05], high HT [OR = 1.73, 95%CI (1.13, 2.66), p = 0.01], low ionized calcium concentration [OR = 1.48, 95% CI (1.08, 2.02), p = 0.01], CRRT was started at a high dose [OR = 1.42, 95%CI (1.14, 1.76), p < 0.05], mechanical ventilation [OR = 4.25, 95%CI (2.67, 6.77), p < 0.05], and lack of dedicated care [OR = 5.08, 95%CI (2.51, 10.28), p < 0.05] are risk factors for unplanned weaning during CRRT in ICU patients. However, there was no statistically significant association between platelet count [OR = 1.00, 95%CI (0.99, 1.00), p = 0.09], prothrombin activity [OR = 0.72, 95%CI (0.52, 1.01), p = 0.06], blood flow velocity [OR = 1.02, 95%CI (0.96, 1.08), p = 0.58], and unplanned weaning during CRRT in ICU patients. Sensitivity analysis and meta-regression

Sensitivity analyses were performed for pooled effect sizes in the fixed-effect model and the random-effect model. The results showed that the analysis results of risk factors such as low mean arterial pressure, hypothermia, age (>60 years), comorbidities with underlying diseases, agitation, no use of anticoagulants, high dose of CRRT therapy, mechanical ventilation, and no dedicated care were consistent, indicating that the analysis results were robust and credible. However, factors such as low ionized calcium concentration, platelet count, prothrombin activity, and blood flow velocity were inconsistent after changing the pooled model. See Table 3 for details. Random-effects models and meta-regression were applied to factors with greater heterogeneity. We found significant heterogeneity in factors such as low mean arterial pressure, short activated partial thromboplastin time, high hematocrit, no use of anticoagulants, platelet count, prothrombin activity, and blood flow velocity. For low mean arterial pressure, high hematocrit, and no use of anticoagulant, we performed meta-regression analyses based on study type, which showed that study type was not a source of heterogeneity. Meta-regression analyses of activated partial thromboplastin time, platelet count, prothrombin activity, and blood flow velocity based on participant ethnicity and study type showed that participant ethnicity and study type were not sources of heterogeneity, and other heterogeneity factors were considered (See Supplementary Material 1).

Publication bias

The results of this study using funnel plot and Egger test for publication bias analysis showed that there was no publication bias in low mean arterial pressure, age (>60 years), comorbid underlying disease, agitation, short activated partial prothrombin time, high hematocrit, high CRRT therapeutic dose, mechanical ventilation, platelet count, prothrombin activity, and blood flow velocity (see Supplementary Material 2). However, there was publication bias in the no use of anticoagulants, and we used the clipping method to correct for bias. After 4 iterations, the results of 2 articles were virtualized, and a total of 6 articles were edited, and there was no publication bias. The effect size of the six studies was pooled [OR = 1.412, 95%CI (1.072, 1.861) p = 0.014]. When the results before and after the correction were compared, the results of the existing meta-analysis were considered to be relatively stable (see Supplementary Material 3).

Discussion

Unplanned weaning during CRRT in ICU patients is closely related to the patient’s own factors

Influence of underlying conditions

The results of this study showed that the mean arterial pressure, body temperature, age, underlying diseases and agitation of patients were the key factors leading to unplanned weaning during CRRT. In a previous meta-analysis [32], male patients were at higher risk of unplanned weaning than female patients, and abnormalities in blood pressure and temperature were also associated with this risk. However, the results of this meta-analysis showed no significant association between sex and unplanned weaning during CRRT. This emphasizes the importance of low mean arterial pressure and hypothermia in unplanned weaning events during CRRT, which may be related to the fact that the subjects were ICU patients, whose physiology was more complex and fragile, and fluctuations in vital signs may have had a more significant impact on treatment [33]. In addition, ICU patients aged 60 years and older are more likely to experience unplanned weaning during CRRT, primarily due to decreased exercise capacity, slowed blood flow, and the degenerative changes in blood vessels that occur in older patients [34]. Patients with underlying medical conditions, such as hypertension and coronary artery disease, often have increased blood viscosity, which can lead to filter coagulation, which in turn leads to unplanned weaning [35]. Agitation caused by impaired patient consciousness, which can lead to compression or distortion of the CRRT line, trigger machine alarms, and ultimately cause the blood pump to stop, also significantly increases the risk of unplanned weaning. If the pump stops for more than 3–5 min, irreversible blood clotting occurs [36]. A previous meta-analysis also confirmed that increasing age and the presence of sepsis were associated with an increase in overall mortality in CRRT [37]. Therefore, medical personnel should be well protected by catheter fixation and dedicated care, to reduce the risk of unplanned weaning [38].

Impact of laboratory indicators

Monitoring of coagulation markers is essential to prolonging the cycle time of CRRT, in which APTT is a key indicator of endogenous coagulation activity in patients [39]. In addition, studies have shown that maintaining APTT in the range of 45 to 55 s can effectively prolong the cardiopulmonary bypass time of CRRT [40]. In addition, an increase in hematocrit increases blood viscosity, which slows blood flow, causes filter blockage, increases transmembrane pressure, and ultimately increases the likelihood of unplanned weaning [41]. Decreasing ionized calcium concentrations can help inhibit excessive coagulation, which in turn prolongs the life of the CRRT tubing [42]. Therefore, medical staff should continuously monitor patients’ coagulation indicators to ensure the adequacy of anticoagulation effects and reduce the risk of bleeding.

Unplanned weaning of ICU patients is affected by CRRT treatment regimens

Impact of anticoagulation regimen

Clinicians often use heparin-free anticoagulation regimens to treat ICU patients with bleeding tendencies. However, the finding that absence of anticoagulation increases the risk of unplanned weaning is supported by a randomized controlled trial [43] that analyzed the effects of anticoagulants on CRRT cycle time, eventually showing that the use of anticoagulants prolongs filter life. Therefore, prior to the initiation of CRRT therapy, medical staff should assess the patient’s condition and coagulation function, and develop an individualized anticoagulation strategy. Ideally, this should specify the type, concentration, dose, and timing of anticoagulant drugs, to ensure the accuracy of clinical procedures [44].

Effect of CRRT therapeutic dose

CRRT therapeutic dose usually refers to the amount of replacement fluid applied per unit time [45], and the larger the therapeutic dose, the faster the flow rate of the waste liquid pump, and the higher the amount of fluid removed by ultrafiltration. This may lead to an increase in local blood hematocrit and viscosity, and an increase in transmembrane pressure, thereby increasing the risk of coagulation. If the instrument alarms and a fault in the equipment is ruled out, the CRRT treatment dose can be adjusted appropriately to restore the normal operation of the instrument, and prevent the occurrence of unplanned weaning.

Other risk factors leading to unplanned weaning during CRRT in ICU patients

Impact of other treatment measures in the same period

This meta-analysis showed that mechanical ventilation was a significant risk factor for unplanned weaning of CRRT in ICU patients. Although mechanical ventilation improves haemodynamics and cardiac function, it increases respiratory and vascular pressure, slows blood flow, increases respiratory water loss, and increases the incidence of venous thrombosis. This results in increased CRRT transmembrane pressure and filter coagulation [46]. Care should be taken to avoid human-machine confrontation in mechanically ventilated patients, to maintain airway patency, prompt clearance of respiratory secretions, reduction of high airway pressure, and maintenance of airway humidification [47].

Shortage of specialist nurses for blood purification

Unplanned weaning during CRRT in ICU patients is closely related to the lack of dedicated care, which is mainly due to insufficient nurse allocation, unreasonable scheduling and utilization, and scarcity of nursing personnel in CRRT [48]. At present, there is no consensus in the nursing community on CRRT expertise and practices [49]. It is suggested that medical institutions should further strengthen training and regular assessment on the basis of optimizing scheduling, so as to improve the level of CRRT skills. Moreover, they should comprehensively improve the quality of nursing services, and reduce the occurrence of unplanned weaning [50].

Summary

In summary, low mean arterial pressure, hypothermia, advanced age, underlying diseases, agitation, absence of anticoagulants, short APTT time, high hematocrit, low ionized calcium concentration, high CRRT therapeutic dose, mechanical ventilation, and lack of dedicated care are risk factors for unplanned weaning of CRRT in ICU patients. In addition, the other risk factors need to be further confirmed by more studies due to insufficient evidence. The strengths of this study were: (1) the quality of the original literature was high, and the NOS score was above 5 points in terms of method quality evaluation. Moreover, the journal sources were appropriately screened, and the quality of the research results was good; (2) The data from multivariate regression analysis were extracted from the original study, rather than the results of univariate analysis. This reduced the influence of confounding factors on the results to a certain extent, and resulted high reliability. (3) Among the 23 studies included, 70% were undertaken in the past five years, which translates to high timeliness and strong reference value. The disadvantage of this study is that most of the included study subjects were ICU patients from Asian countries, which might have introduced geographical bias into the findings of this meta-analysis. Despite the use of meta-regression and sensitivity analysis, country did not fully explain the heterogeneity. It is recommended that further high-quality, multicenter studies be conducted in different countries in the future to confirm the results of this meta-analysis. Meanwhile, it is hoped that ICU healthcare professionals will pay attention to the above high-risk factors, identify high-risk groups, and intervene scientifically to reduce the rate of unplanned weaning, and improve the quality of CRRT.

Dual-single-atom nanozyme with stage-adaptive catalytic regulation for infected diabetic wound healing
Authors: Jian Zhang, Jihai Xu, Chengchun Shen, Peilong Jiang, Heyang Sun, Jian Ruan, Yaopeng Huang, Xin Hong, Shengbing Yang, Hong Chen, Guoping Shi, Xin Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Infected diabetic wounds remain difficult to treat because persistent bacterial reservoirs coexist with a failure to transition from antibacterial defense to regenerative repair. Here, we report a dual-single-atom nanozyme (FeCo-CN) in which atomically dispersed Fe and Co centers are anchored within a nitrogen-coordinated carbon framework, enabling stage-adaptive regulation of the wound microenvironment. During the early infection phase, FeCo-CN exhibits photothermal-enhanced peroxidase-like activity that efficiently reduces extracellular bacteria and promotes macrophage-mediated intracellular bacterial clearance. As the bacterial burden decreases, the material shows superoxide dismutase– and catalase-like activities that alleviate oxidative stress, preserve mitochondrial function, and favor the shift toward reparative immune responses. These coordinated effects support angiogenesis and tissue regeneration. Overall, this work highlights a dual-single-atom nanozyme strategy that integrates infection control with immune transition and tissue repair in infected diabetic wounds.Graphical Abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12951-026-04728-0.

Introduction

Infected diabetic wounds represent one of the most severe and refractory complications of diabetes mellitus, frequently progressing to chronic non-healing ulcers, recurrent infection, and lower-limb amputation [1, 2]. Their intractability arises not only from increased bacterial burden or antimicrobial resistance but also from a pathological convergence of persistent infection, immune dysregulation, and impaired tissue regeneration within a hyperglycemic microenvironment [3, 4]. Diabetes-associated microvascular dysfunction restricts oxygen and nutrient delivery, while sustained oxidative stress and immunometabolic disturbances disrupt the coordinated transition from inflammation to tissue repair [5]. Consequently, infected diabetic wounds often become locked in a state of chronic inflammation accompanied by regenerative failure once infection is established.

Current clinical management primarily relies on antibiotics, surgical debridement, and conventional wound care [6, 7]. Although these approaches can transiently reduce extracellular bacterial load, durable infection control and functional tissue regeneration remain difficult to achieve. Biofilm formation limits antibiotic penetration, and the increasing prevalence of antimicrobial resistance further compromises therapeutic efficacy [8]. Importantly, pathogenic bacteria within diabetic wounds can invade host cells-including macrophages, fibroblasts, and keratinocytes-forming intracellular reservoirs that evade both antibiotic treatment and immune surveillance [9]. Even after apparent extracellular clearance, these intracellular bacteria can persist and reactivate infection, thereby sustaining inflammation and preventing effective wound resolution [10]. Consequently, therapeutic strategies focusing solely on extracellular bactericidal activity fail to address a critical dimension of diabetic wound pathology.

Beyond bacterial persistence, dysregulated immune dynamics represent a major barrier to wound healing [11]. Macrophages function as a central regulatory hub linking antibacterial defense with tissue regeneration through their remarkable functional plasticity [12]. Rapid activation of pro-inflammatory bactericidal macrophages is required during early infection, whereas a subsequent transition toward anti-inflammatory and reparative phenotypes is essential for inflammation resolution, angiogenesis, and extracellular matrix remodeling. Failure of this stage-dependent immune transition leads to sustained oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, impaired vascularization, and defective tissue repair [13–15]. Therefore, effective treatment of infected diabetic wounds requires not only rapid elimination of pathogens (including intracellular bacteria) but also coordinated restoration of immune and redox homeostasis.

Material-based antibacterial platforms, including nanozymes and photothermal systems, have emerged as promising strategies for non-antibiotic bacterial eradication through catalytic reactive oxygen species generation and localized hyperthermia [16–19]. However, most reported systems primarily focus on direct bactericidal activity without integrating stage-dependent immunomodulation. From a materials design perspective, single-atom and dual-atom nanozymes offer unique advantages due to atomic-level control of catalytic active sites [20, 21]. Dual-atom architectures enable cooperative electronic interactions between adjacent metal centers, often resulting in enhanced catalytic efficiency and multifunctional catalytic behavior compared with single-atom systems [22]. Nevertheless, the potential of dual-single-atom nanozymes to orchestrate a programmed transition from antibacterial immunity to inflammation resolution and tissue regeneration in infected diabetic wounds remains largely unexplored.

Here, we report a FeCo-CN dual-single-atom nanozyme designed to address a central challenge in infected diabetic wounds: how to couple effective antibacterial control with a timely transition toward immune resolution and tissue repair. By anchoring Fe and Co single atoms onto a nitrogen-doped carbon framework, FeCo-CN provides a rational catalytic platform with stage-dependent functionality. During the early stage of infection, FeCo-CN exhibits enhanced peroxidase-like activity together with efficient near-infrared photothermal conversion, enabling rapid elimination of extracellular bacteria while promoting macrophage-mediated clearance of intracellular pathogens. As bacterial burden decreases, its superoxide dismutase– and catalase-like activities help alleviate excessive oxidative stress and support reparative immune remodeling. In this way, FeCo-CN offers a strategy that links infection control, immune regulation, and vascularized tissue repair within a single nanozyme platform (Scheme 1).

Results

Structural and physicochemical characterization of FeCo-CN

The structural characteristics and atomic dispersion of FeCo-CN were systematically investigated through multi-scale characterization, spanning from nanosheet morphology to local coordination environments.

Low-magnification TEM and HAADF-STEM images (Fig. 1A, B) reveal that FeCo-CN exhibits a two-dimensional ultrathin nanosheet morphology with a highly wrinkled structure. No high-contrast domains or crystalline nanoparticles are observed across the nanosheets, suggesting the absence of metal aggregation. The nanosheets display flexible and semi-transparent features typical of few-layer carbon frameworks. AFM measurements (Fig. S1) further confirm a nanosheet thickness in the nanometer range, supporting the formation of ultrathin carbon sheets. Notably, Fe-CN and Co-CN control samples (Figs. S2 and S4) show nearly identical morphologies, indicating that the incorporation of Fe and Co precursors does not disrupt the structural integrity of the nitrogen-doped carbon scaffold.

Elemental distribution was analyzed using STEM-EDS mapping. As shown in Fig. 1C-F, C, N, Fe, and Co signals are homogeneously distributed throughout the nanosheets without detectable clustering or localized enrichment. The spatial overlap of Fe and Co with nitrogen indicates that the metal species are anchored at nitrogen coordination sites within the carbon matrix rather than forming segregated metallic domains. This homogeneous elemental distribution further supports atomic-level dispersion of the metal centers.

To directly visualize the atomic dispersion, aberration-corrected HAADF-STEM imaging was performed. The high-resolution image in Fig. 1G displays numerous isolated bright dots uniformly distributed across the carbon substrate. Owing to the Z-contrast imaging mechanism, these bright features correspond to individual Fe and Co atoms. Importantly, no lattice fringes or crystalline domains are detected, excluding the presence of metallic clusters or nanoparticles. The spatial separation between these bright dots also suggests isolated coordination environments rather than short-range metal-metal coupling. Similar atomic dispersion is observed in Fe-CN and Co-CN controls (Figs. S2 and S4), confirming that FeCo-CN integrates both Fe and Co single atoms within the same carbon framework.

The chemical states and bonding environments were further examined by X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy. The survey spectrum in Fig. 1H confirms the presence of C, N, O, Fe, and Co elements. High-resolution Fe 2p and Co 2p spectra (Fig. 1I, J) display mixed-valence states of Fe2+/Fe3+ and Co2+/Co3+, which are characteristic of coordinated metal centers embedded in heteroatom-doped carbon frameworks rather than metallic Fe0 or Co0 species [23]. The absence of metallic peaks further excludes the formation of metal nanoparticles. In addition, the N 1s spectra of Fe-CN and Co-CN (Figs. S3 and S5) reveal pyridinic-N, graphitic-N, and M-N coordination components, supporting the formation of stable metal-nitrogen coordination structures within the carbon lattice.

Powder X-ray diffraction analysis (Fig. 1K) shows a broad diffraction peak centered at approximately 24°, corresponding to the (002) plane of amorphous graphitic carbon [24]. No diffraction peaks corresponding to metallic Fe, Co, or their oxide phases are detected, further indicating the absence of crystalline metal aggregates and supporting the atomic dispersion of the metal species.

To further elucidate the local coordination structures, synchrotron-based XANES and EXAFS measurements were conducted. The normalized Co and Fe K-edge XANES spectra (Fig. 1M, N) show absorption edge positions between those of metal foils and corresponding oxides, indicating partially oxidized metal centers coordinated within the nitrogen-doped carbon matrix. This intermediate valence state is consistent with M-N coordination rather than metallic bonding.

Fourier-transformed EXAFS spectra at the Co and Fe K-edges (Fig. 1L, O) display dominant first-shell scattering peaks at approximately 1.5-2.0 Å (uncorrected for phase shift), which can be assigned to M-N coordination. Importantly, no characteristic metal-metal scattering peaks (typically appearing at > 2.2 Å) are observed, confirming the absence of Fe-Fe, Co-Co, or Fe-Co interactions. These results demonstrate that Fe and Co atoms are atomically dispersed rather than forming dual-metal clusters.

Wavelet transform EXAFS analyses (Fig. 1P-S) provide additional resolution in k-R space. The intensity maxima are confined to the low-k region corresponding to M-N scattering, whereas the high-k features characteristic of metal-metal interactions are absent [25]. This two-dimensional analysis further excludes the presence of metallic dimers or aggregated species and confirms that both Fe and Co atoms are stabilized as isolated single atoms coordinated by nitrogen.

Overall, these structural characterizations demonstrate that FeCo-CN consists of atomically dispersed Fe and Co sites anchored through M-N coordination within an ultrathin nitrogen-doped carbon nanosheet framework. This well-defined dual-single-atom architecture provides the structural basis for the cooperative catalytic behavior examined in the following sections.

Functional characterization of FeCo-CN

The photothermal performance of FeCo-CN was first evaluated under 808 nm laser irradiation. Infrared thermal imaging (Fig. 2A) shows rapid temperature elevation in FeCo-CN suspensions upon irradiation, whereas negligible heating is observed in the absence of the material. The temperature increase is strongly concentration-dependent, reaching approximately 95–100℃ at higher concentrations (Fig. 2B). Similarly, increasing laser power density leads to progressively enhanced thermal responses (Fig. 2C), indicating efficient light absorption and photothermal conversion.

The heating-cooling profile (Fig. 2D) displays rapid temperature elevation during laser irradiation followed by gradual cooling once the irradiation is stopped, demonstrating fast photothermal responsiveness. The photothermal signal remains stable during the heating-cooling process, indicating good thermal stability [26]. Based on linear fitting of the cooling curve (Fig. 2E), the photothermal conversion efficiency of FeCo-CN was calculated to be high, indicating effective light-to-heat energy transformation.

Consistent with the strong photothermal effect, FeCo-CN exhibits broad optical absorption in the visible-near-infrared region. As shown in Fig. 2F, the absorbance intensity increases with increasing material concentration. Importantly, the absorption spectra remain largely unchanged under different pH conditions (Fig. 2G) and temperatures (Fig. 2H), suggesting good structural and optical stability under physiologically relevant environments.

Beyond photothermal properties, the catalytic activity of FeCo-CN was systematically investigated. ESR measurements using DMPO as a spin-trapping agent reveal markedly enhanced ROS signals in the FeCo-CN group compared with Fe-CN, Co-CN, and blank controls (Fig. 2I, J). The amplified radical signal indicates more efficient activation of H2O2, suggesting synergistic redox interactions between the Fe and Co single-atom centers. A schematic illustration of the proposed peroxidase-like catalytic mechanism is presented in Fig. 2K, where the Fe/Co sites cooperatively facilitate H2O2 decomposition and substrate oxidation.

Kinetic analysis further confirms the superior catalytic activity of FeCo-CN. As shown in Fig. 2L, FeCo-CN induces the most rapid decrease in H2O2 concentration among all samples, demonstrating enhanced peroxidase-like catalytic performance. The catalytic efficiency increases with increasing concentration (Fig. 2M), indicating concentration-dependent H2O2 decomposition capability.

In addition, FeCo-CN generates significantly higher levels of O2 over time compared with Fe-CN and Co-CN (Fig. 2N), indicating enhanced catalase-like activity. The concurrent acceleration of H2O2 decomposition and oxygen evolution suggests efficient redox cycling within the dual-metal catalytic centers.

Together, these results show that FeCo-CN combines efficient photothermal conversion with multiple enzyme-like catalytic activities. The Fe-Co dual-single-atom configuration is associated with stronger H2O2 activation, ROS generation, and O2 production than the corresponding single-metal controls, supporting its use as a multifunctional catalytic platform for subsequent antibacterial and redox-regulatory studies.

Antibacterial performance and underlying mechanisms of FeCo-CN

The antibacterial activity of FeCo-CN was first evaluated against MRSA. Representative colony images (Fig. 3A) show that FeCo-CN reduces viable bacteria more effectively than Fe-CN or Co-CN, while the FeCo-CN + PTT group exhibits the strongest bactericidal effect. Quantitative analysis (Fig. 3B) confirms that the dual-single-atom system achieves the most pronounced reduction in bacterial survival, especially under NIR irradiation.

To examine intracellular molecular damage, genomic DNA integrity was assessed by agarose gel electrophoresis. As shown in Fig. 3C, FeCo-CN treatment leads to evident DNA fragmentation, which becomes more pronounced in the FeCo-CN + PTT group, suggesting severe oxidative damage to bacterial nucleic acids. Membrane integrity was further evaluated using live/dead CLSM imaging. In planktonic bacteria, FeCo-CN treatment results in dominant red fluorescence, indicating extensive membrane disruption (Fig. 3D). A similar trend is observed in established biofilms (Fig. 3F), where FeCo-CN significantly increases bacterial death compared with single-metal controls. SEM analysis (Figs. S6 and S7) reveals pronounced surface collapse and morphological deformation in both MRSA and Pseudomonas aeruginosa following FeCo-CN exposure, particularly under PTT conditions. Consistently, TEM images (Fig. 3E) display membrane rupture, cytoplasmic leakage, and loss of intracellular organization, confirming severe ultrastructural damage.

Functional membrane disruption was further verified by extracellular ATP measurements. As shown in Fig. 3G, FeCo-CN treatment causes a marked increase in ATP levels in the surrounding medium, consistent with membrane permeabilization and leakage of intracellular contents. In parallel, crystal violet staining (Fig. 3H) reveals substantial reduction in biofilm biomass. Quantitative analysis of biofilm density and thickness (Fig. 3I, J) further demonstrates that FeCo-CN effectively disrupts established biofilms, with FeCo-CN + PTT exhibiting the strongest antibiofilm activity.

To explore molecular responses associated with bacterial collapse, transcriptional changes were analyzed by qPCR. As shown in Fig. 3K-P, oxidative stress and DNA repair-related genes (recA, katA, and sodA) are significantly upregulated following FeCo-CN exposure, reflecting strong oxidative pressure on bacterial cells. In contrast, virulence and biofilm regulatory genes (sarA, icaA, and agrA) are markedly downregulated, indicating suppression of pathogenicity and biofilm formation capacity. These transcriptional responses suggest that FeCo-CN not only damages bacterial structures but also disrupts regulatory networks essential for survival and persistence.

To further elucidate global bacterial responses, transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses were conducted (Figs. S8-S19). RNA-seq results (Figs. S8-S14) reveal extensive differential gene expression affecting oxidative stress responses, energy metabolism, two-component systems, and secretion pathways. KEGG and GO enrichment analyses (Figs. S9-S13) highlight significant perturbations in redox balance and metabolic regulation. Complementary metabolomic profiling (Figs. S15-S19) demonstrates remodeling of central carbon metabolism, quorum sensing-related metabolites, and redox-associated intermediates, indicating systemic metabolic disruption.

Taken together, these results indicate that FeCo-CN elicits a multilevel antibacterial response characterized by ROS-associated membrane damage, intracellular leakage, biofilm disruption, and suppression of virulence-related pathways. Photothermal activation further strengthens these antibacterial effects, suggesting that the catalytic–thermal combination contributes to the efficient elimination of bacterial persistence programs.

To elucidate the global regulatory consequences of FeCo-CN exposure, transcriptomic profiling of MRSA was performed. The volcano plot (Fig. 4A) reveals extensive differential gene expression, with hundreds of genes significantly up or downregulated, indicating large-scale transcriptional reprogramming rather than isolated stress responses.

KEGG pathway enrichment analysis (Fig. 4B, C) shows pronounced suppression of pathways associated with antibiotic resistance, quorum sensing, ABC transporters, bacterial secretion systems, and two-component regulatory systems. These pathways are essential for environmental sensing, intercellular communication, and adaptive defense, suggesting that FeCo-CN disrupts bacterial signaling hierarchies and transport capacity. In parallel, multiple metabolic pathways-including glycolysis, amino acid biosynthesis, and nucleotide metabolism-are significantly perturbed, reflecting destabilization of central metabolic networks [27].

GO enrichment analysis further refines this regulatory landscape. Differentially expressed genes are predominantly enriched in biological processes related to metabolic regulation, nucleotide biosynthesis, and gene expression control (Fig. 4D, F). Enrichment in cell surface and membrane-associated components is also observed, consistent with the membrane damage identified in ultrastructural analyses. The heatmap (Fig. 4E) further reveals coordinated upregulation of oxidative stress- and DNA damage-associated genes alongside pronounced downregulation of virulence regulators and biofilm-associated genes, indicating a shift from active pathogenic programs toward a stress-dominated survival state.

To determine whether these transcriptional alterations translate into functional metabolic consequences, untargeted metabolomic profiling was conducted. The volcano plot (Fig. 5A) shows widespread changes in metabolite abundance following FeCo-CN treatment. KEGG enrichment analysis (Fig. 5B, C) identifies significant perturbations in amino acid metabolism, nucleotide metabolism, carbon metabolism, oxidative phosphorylation, and cofactor biosynthesis pathways. These metabolic shifts collectively indicate disruption of ATP production, biosynthetic flux, and redox homeostasis. Notably, suppression of ABC transporter and secretion-related pathways at the metabolite level mirrors transcriptomic findings, indicating coordinated inhibition across regulatory layers.

To assess whether this regulatory collapse is conserved across species, analogous transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses were performed in Pseudomonas aeruginosa (Figs. S8-S19). RNA-seq results (Fig. S8) reveal extensive differential gene expression, including marked downregulation of motility, chemotaxis, secretion systems, porphyrin metabolism, and nitrogen metabolism pathways (Figs. S9-S13). Network analysis (Fig. S11) illustrates coordinated attenuation of quorum sensing and siderophore biosynthesis modules, suggesting impaired virulence communication and iron acquisition. Heatmap visualization (Fig. S14) further confirms repression of genes associated with secretion systems and redox adaptation.

Metabolomic profiling of Pseudomonas aeruginosa (Figs. S15-S19) reveals broad remodeling of central carbon metabolism, redox-associated metabolites, quorum-sensing molecules, and iron-acquisition intermediates. These findings indicate that FeCo-CN induces systemic metabolic disequilibrium, compromising bacterial adaptability and survival across distinct bacterial species.

Overall, the multi-omics data indicate that FeCo-CN does not merely trigger an isolated stress response but instead broadly perturbs bacterial membrane integrity, transcriptional regulation, and metabolic flux. The coordinated suppression of virulence signaling, transport systems, and energy-related pathways provides a plausible explanation for the broad-spectrum antibacterial activity of the Fe-Co dual-single-atom nanozyme.

Pro-inflammatory activation and intracellular antibacterial immunity induced by FeCo-CN

To determine whether FeCo-CN enhances host-mediated clearance of intracellular bacteria, macrophage polarization, intracellular bacterial processing, and antimicrobial effector responses were systematically investigated.

Immunofluorescence staining reveals a pronounced increase in CD86 expression in macrophages treated with FeCo-CN (Fig. 6A), indicating activation toward a classical M1 phenotype. In contrast, expression of the M2-associated marker CD206 is reduced (Fig. 6B), suggesting suppression of the reparative phenotype during the antibacterial phase. DAPI staining shows comparable nuclear density across groups, excluding cytotoxicity-induced artifacts. These results indicate that FeCo-CN actively reprograms macrophage polarization rather than passively affecting cell viability.

To evaluate intracellular bacterial processing, ultrastructural analysis was performed. In control macrophages, MRSA is frequently observed within intact membrane-bound compartments, forming intracellular reservoirs (Fig. 6C). In FeCo-CN-treated cells, intracellular bacteria are markedly reduced and often display disrupted membranes and condensed cytoplasmic structures, consistent with ongoing degradation. These observations indicate that FeCo-CN enhances intracellular bactericidal processing rather than merely limiting bacterial entry.

Given that intracellular bacterial elimination depends on coordinated phagosome maturation and autophagic flux, key molecular markers were next examined. Western blot analysis (Fig. 6D) shows increased expression of LAMP1 and RAB7, indicating enhanced phagolysosomal maturation and late endosomal trafficking. Meanwhile, LC3 expression is elevated and p62 levels are reduced, supporting activation of autophagic flux rather than autophagy blockage. This coordinated regulation suggests that FeCo-CN facilitates phagosome-lysosome fusion and autophagosome turnover, thereby promoting efficient bacterial degradation.

Flow cytometric analysis further confirms macrophage polarization at the population level. As shown in Fig. 6F, the proportion of CD86+CD206− macrophages significantly increases in the FeCo-CN group compared with control and single-metal treatments, indicating a robust shift toward an antibacterial M1 phenotype.

Consistent with this polarization shift, FeCo-CN markedly enhances antimicrobial effector responses. Secretion of TNF-α and IL-1β is significantly elevated (Fig. 6G, H), indicating activation of pro-inflammatory bactericidal signaling. iNOS expression is also increased, suggesting enhanced nitric oxide production and oxidative antimicrobial activity. LC3A/B expression is further quantified in Fig. 6I, corroborating autophagy activation at the protein level.

Importantly, intracellular CFU quantification (Fig. 6J) demonstrates a substantial reduction in viable intracellular MRSA following FeCo-CN treatment. This result directly confirms that macrophage reprogramming and enhanced lysosomal-autophagic activity translate into effective elimination of intracellular pathogens.

Together, these findings indicate that FeCo-CN not only exerts extracellular antibacterial activity but also enhances host antibacterial responses by promoting macrophage polarization and intracellular degradative pathways. The coordinated changes in cytokine production, phagolysosomal maturation, autophagy-related markers, and intracellular CFU support the view that FeCo-CN facilitates clearance of intracellular bacterial reservoirs, as illustrated in Fig. 6E.

Anti-inflammatory effects and immunometabolic reprogramming induced by FeCo-CN

Following the early antibacterial phase, we next investigated whether FeCo-CN could alleviate excessive oxidative stress and restore cellular homeostasis, a critical step for resolving inflammation and enabling tissue repair.

Mitochondrial membrane potential was first evaluated using JC-1 staining. As shown in Fig. 7A, H2O2 exposure induces a pronounced shift from red JC-1 aggregates to green monomers, indicating severe mitochondrial depolarization. In contrast, FeCo-CN treatment markedly restores red fluorescence, suggesting preservation of mitochondrial membrane integrity under oxidative stress. Quantitative analysis of the JC-1 red/green ratio (Fig. 7D) further confirms significant recovery of mitochondrial polarization in the H2O2 + FeCo-CN group.

Ultrastructural TEM analysis provides additional evidence of mitochondrial protection. As shown in Fig. 7B, mitochondria in H2O2-treated cells exhibit swelling, cristae disruption, and matrix rarefaction, typical features of oxidative mitochondrial injury. In contrast, cells treated with FeCo-CN display largely preserved mitochondrial morphology with intact cristae and reduced swelling, indicating structural protection at the organelle level.

To assess intracellular redox status, ROS levels were measured using DCFH-DA staining. As illustrated in Fig. 7C, H2O2 induces strong ROS accumulation, whereas FeCo-CN treatment significantly reduces fluorescence intensity. Quantification of intracellular ROS (Fig. 7E) confirms a substantial reduction in oxidative burden, demonstrating the redox-regulatory capacity of FeCo-CN.

Importantly, restoration of redox balance is coupled to macrophage functional reprogramming. Expression of Arg-1, a marker associated with reparative macrophage polarization, is significantly increased in the H2O2 + FeCo-CN group (Fig. 7F), indicating a shift toward a pro-resolving phenotype following the antibacterial phase.

To further examine the metabolic basis of this transition, Seahorse extracellular flux analysis was performed. H2O2 exposure induces pronounced mitochondrial dysfunction, characterized by reduced oxygen consumption rate (OCR) and compensatory elevation of glycolytic activity (ECAR) (Fig. 7G, H), reflecting a shift toward glycolytic metabolism under oxidative stress. FeCo-CN treatment partially restores mitochondrial respiration while reducing excessive glycolytic reliance, indicating rebalanced cellular bioenergetics. Quantitative analysis of basal respiration and glycolysis (Fig. 7L, M) further confirms normalization of metabolic parameters.

The cytoprotective effects of FeCo-CN extend beyond macrophages. Under oxidative stress conditions, FeCo-CN significantly improves cell viability in endothelial cells (HUVECs), keratinocytes (HaCaT), and fibroblasts (L929) (Fig. 7I-K). These results indicate that FeCo-CN broadly preserves cellular function across multiple cellular components of the wound microenvironment.

Overall, these data indicate that FeCo-CN alleviates oxidative stress while helping restore mitochondrial and metabolic homeostasis. Beyond simple ROS scavenging, the material is associated with a shift toward reparative immune features, providing a mechanistic basis for inflammation resolution and subsequent tissue repair.

In vivo antibacterial efficacy, immune modulation and wound regeneration promoted by FeCo-CN

To evaluate the therapeutic performance of FeCo-CN under physiologically relevant conditions, a bacteria-infected full-thickness cutaneous wound model was established. Representative wound images are shown in Fig. 8A. In the control group, wounds exhibited persistent edema, thick scab formation, and delayed contraction, reflecting ongoing infection and sustained inflammation. Co-CN treatment resulted in only moderate improvement. In contrast, FeCo-CN markedly accelerated wound closure, characterized by reduced purulent exudate, diminished swelling, and earlier scab detachment. Notably, the FeCo-CN + PTT group displayed rapid wound contraction by day 7 and nearly complete re-epithelialization by day 15, indicating efficient infection control and restoration of tissue integrity.

To quantify in vivo antibacterial efficacy, bacterial colony-forming units were measured from homogenized wound tissues. As shown in Fig. 8B, abundant bacterial colonies are detected in control wounds, whereas FeCo-CN treatment significantly reduces bacterial burden. The FeCo-CN + PTT group exhibits minimal residual colonies, confirming that the dual-single-atom nanozyme retains potent antibacterial activity in complex tissue environments and that photothermal activation further enhances bacterial eradication in vivo.

Because effective wound repair requires immune resolution following pathogen clearance, macrophage polarization in wound tissues was examined by flow cytometry [28]. As shown in Fig. 8C, FeCo-CN treatment significantly increases the proportion of CD206+ reparative macrophages during the healing phase, with an even stronger effect observed in the FeCo-CN + PTT group. These findings suggest that, following rapid bacterial elimination, FeCo-CN promotes a transition of the wound microenvironment from persistent inflammation toward a pro-resolving state.

Histological analyses further reveal the regenerative changes within the wound tissue. Giemsa staining (Fig. 8D) shows dense inflammatory cell infiltration in control wounds, whereas FeCo-CN-treated tissues display markedly reduced leukocyte accumulation. Hematoxylin and eosin staining demonstrates thicker and more continuous neo-epidermis in the FeCo-CN and FeCo-CN + PTT groups, accompanied by more organized granulation tissue and reduced necrotic regions. Masson trichrome staining reveals enhanced collagen deposition and improved fiber alignment, indicating accelerated extracellular matrix remodeling and structural maturation. Meanwhile, IL-6 staining shows decreased pro-inflammatory cytokine expression in FeCo-CN-treated wounds, confirming effective attenuation of sustained inflammatory signaling.

To assess vascular regeneration and immune microenvironment remodeling, immunofluorescence staining was performed. As shown in Fig. 8E, FeCo-CN treatment significantly increases CD31+ microvessel density, indicating active angiogenesis [29]. Enhanced α-SMA+ staining further suggests maturation and stabilization of newly formed vessels rather than transient sprouting. In addition, altered distributions of CD4+ and FOXP3+ cells indicate adaptive immune recalibration within the wound microenvironment.

Quantitative analyses further support these observations. Expression levels of collagen I and CD31 (Fig. 8F, G) are significantly elevated in FeCo-CN-treated wounds, particularly in the presence of PTT. VEGF expression (Fig. 8H) is also markedly upregulated, consistent with enhanced angiogenic signaling. Moreover, Ki67 staining (Fig. 8I) reveals increased proliferative activity in regenerating tissues, indicating accelerated epithelial and stromal cell expansion [30].

Taken together, the in vivo results indicate that FeCo-CN promotes a stage-dependent therapeutic response characterized by early bacterial reduction followed by improved immune resolution and tissue regeneration. The enhanced effects observed in the FeCo-CN + PTT group further support the contribution of photothermal activation to the overall healing outcome.

Conclusion

We developed a FeCo-CN dual-single-atom nanozyme that enables stage-adaptive regulation of antibacterial immunity and inflammation resolution for the treatment of infected diabetic wounds. By atomically anchoring Fe and Co centers within a nitrogen-coordinated carbon framework, FeCo-CN integrates photothermal conversion with multiple enzyme-like catalytic functions, thereby supporting early antibacterial activity together with later redox regulation during tissue repair.

Functionally, FeCo-CN responds to the evolving wound microenvironment in a temporally coordinated manner. In the early stage, particularly under near-infrared irradiation, it reduces extracellular bacteria and enhances macrophage-mediated intracellular clearance. As the bacterial burden declines, its catalytic profile is associated with oxidative stress attenuation, mitochondrial protection, and a shift toward reparative immune responses, which together support angiogenesis, collagen remodeling, and cellular proliferation.

Rather than serving only as a static antibacterial catalyst, FeCo-CN acts as a dynamic regulator of immune phase transition in infected wounds. More broadly, this study suggests that dual-single-atom nanozymes can be designed to coordinate infection control with tissue repair, offering a useful framework for biomaterials intended for complex infectious and inflammatory conditions.

Discussion

Chronic infected diabetic wounds represent a prototypical pathological microenvironment in which persistent bacterial colonization, intracellular pathogen reservoirs, metabolic stress, and dysregulated immune responses converge to impair tissue regeneration [31]. Unlike acute infections, where pathogen elimination alone can restore homeostasis, diabetic wounds fail to transition from inflammatory defense to reparative remodeling. Hyperglycemia-associated oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, impaired innate immunity, and vascular insufficiency collectively create a niche that is simultaneously hostile to host cells yet permissive for bacterial persistence [32]. As a result, strategies focused solely on bacterial eradication are often insufficient, as they do not resolve the underlying immunometabolic imbalance that limits tissue repair.

In this context, FeCo-CN was designed as a dual-single-atom nanozyme capable of functioning within a dynamically evolving microenvironment rather than under static conditions. Compared with conventional nanoparticle-based antibacterial systems that mainly rely on persistent ROS production or photothermal damage, the atomically dispersed Fe-Co configuration within a nitrogen-coordinated carbon framework provides a more integrated catalytic platform. This design allows antibacterial activation in the early stage while retaining antioxidative and redox-balancing functions that are beneficial during the repair phase.

During the early phase of infection, FeCo-CN acts as an efficient antibacterial catalyst. Its peroxidase-like activity, further enhanced by photothermal activation, disrupts bacterial membranes, perturbs metabolic processes, and suppresses virulence-associated regulatory networks. Importantly, this effect extends to intracellular pathogens. By promoting macrophage polarization toward a pro-inflammatory phenotype and enhancing phagolysosomal maturation and autophagic flux, FeCo-CN facilitates clearance of intracellular bacterial reservoirs that commonly evade antibiotic treatment. This host-directed reinforcement of antibacterial immunity addresses a key limitation in chronic infection management.

As bacterial burden decreases, the functional requirements of the microenvironment shift toward inflammation resolution and tissue repair [33]. Correspondingly, FeCo-CN exhibits a catalytic transition in which superoxide dismutase-like and catalase-like activities become dominant. This transition reduces excessive ROS accumulation, restores mitochondrial integrity, and attenuates sustained inflammatory signaling. The resulting redox stabilization promotes macrophage reprogramming toward reparative phenotypes and supports angiogenesis and extracellular matrix remodeling [34]. Unlike conventional nanozymes that rely on persistent ROS generation, this dynamic catalytic switching minimizes collateral tissue damage while sustaining therapeutic efficacy.

Multi-omics analyses further suggest that chronic infection should be viewed as a system-level disorder involving tightly coupled host-pathogen interactions. FeCo-CN induces coordinated disruption of bacterial transcriptional and metabolic networks, impairing adaptive stress responses, quorum sensing, and secretion pathways. In parallel, it restores host immunometabolic balance by normalizing mitochondrial function and reducing inflammatory signaling. This dual regulation highlights the importance of therapeutic strategies that simultaneously target pathogen survival mechanisms and host immune dysfunction.

From a broader perspective, this study supports a shift in nanozyme design from static catalytic agents toward materials with stage-dependent regulatory behavior. The value of the present system is not only that it shows strong antibacterial activity, but also that it links infection control with subsequent immune resolution and tissue repair within one platform. In this sense, FeCo-CN may offer a useful design principle for nanozyme-based therapies targeting chronic wounds and other disorders involving sequential infection, inflammation, and regeneration.

Nevertheless, several challenges remain. The long-term biosafety, degradation pathways, and in vivo fate of dual-single-atom nanozymes require further investigation, particularly under chronic exposure conditions. In addition, integrating atomic catalysts with scaffold-based delivery systems or responsive hydrogels may improve spatial precision and prolong therapeutic effects.

Taken together, this work indicates that atomic-scale catalyst design can help bridge antibacterial defense and immune recovery within a single platform. More importantly, it suggests that catalytic biomaterials may be engineered not only to reduce pathogen burden, but also to guide the trajectory of immune responses in a stage-dependent manner. This conceptual advance distinguishes the present study from conventional antibacterial nanozyme strategies that focus mainly on direct microbial killing.

Methods

Synthesis and structural characterization of dual-single-atom nanozymes

Dual-single-atom nanozymes (Fe-CN, Co-CN, and FeCo-CN) were synthesized through a nitrogen-coordination-assisted pyrolysis strategy. Melamine (5.0 g) was dissolved in deionized water (50 mL) at 80 ℃ under continuous stirring, followed by dropwise addition of cyanamide (10.0 g). After polymerization at 80 ℃ for 2 h, the resulting viscous precursor was dried overnight and subsequently pre-calcined under N2 atmosphere at 550 ℃ for 2 h (heating rate: 2 ℃ min− 1) to obtain layered g-C3N4 nanosheets.

For metal coordination, g-C3N4 (500 mg) was ultrasonically dispersed in deionized water (100 mL, 200 W, 10 min). FeCl3·6H2O or CoCl2·6H2O was introduced to achieve a theoretical loading of 2 wt%. For FeCo-CN, Fe and Co precursors were co-introduced at a 1:1 molar ratio (total loading 4 wt%). After stirring for 12 h at room temperature, the suspensions were freeze-dried and subjected to pyrolysis at 900 ℃ for 2 h under Ar atmosphere (heating rate: 5 ℃ min− 1). The obtained products were acid-leached in 0.5 M H2SO4 at 80 ℃ for 6 h to remove potential metal clusters, thoroughly washed to neutral pH, and freeze-dried. For biological experiments, nanozymes were dispersed in sterile PBS and sonicated to ensure homogeneous suspensions.

Morphology and atomic dispersion were characterized using transmission electron microscopy (TEM) and aberration-corrected high-angle annular dark-field scanning TEM (HAADF-STEM). Elemental distribution was analyzed by STEM-EDS mapping, and nanosheet thickness was determined by atomic force microscopy (AFM). Crystal structure was examined by X-ray diffraction (XRD), and surface chemical states were analyzed using X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS). Synchrotron-based X-ray absorption fine structure (XAFS) measurements at the Fe and Co K-edges were performed to determine coordination environments and confirm isolated M-Nx sites without detectable M-M bonding.

Photothermal performance and catalytic activity

Photothermal performance was evaluated by irradiating aqueous dispersions of FeCo-CN (50–200 µg mL− 1) with an 808 nm laser (1.0 W cm− 2). Temperature changes were recorded every 10 s using an infrared thermal imaging camera. Photothermal conversion efficiency was calculated based on heating-cooling curves using a standard energy balance model. Photothermal stability was assessed over five irradiation cycles.

Peroxidase-like activity was evaluated using 3,3′,5,5′-tetramethylbenzidine (TMB) as a chromogenic substrate in acetate buffer (pH 4.5) containing H2O2. Absorbance at 652 nm was recorded at defined time intervals (0–10 min) to assess catalytic kinetics. Catalase-like activity was determined by measuring oxygen generation from H2O2 decomposition using a dissolved oxygen probe at 37 ℃.

Reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation was confirmed by electron spin resonance (ESR) spectroscopy using DMPO as a spin-trapping agent. UV-vis absorption spectra were recorded to evaluate optical properties under varying concentrations, pH conditions, and temperatures.

In vitro antibacterial evaluation

Methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA, ATCC 43300) and Pseudomonas aeruginosa (ATCC 27853) were cultured to mid-log phase (OD600 ≈ 0.6). Bacterial suspensions (1 × 107 CFU mL− 1) were incubated with nanozymes (100 µg mL− 1) at 37 ℃ for 30 min. For photothermal treatment, samples were irradiated with an 808 nm laser (1.0 W cm− 2, 5 min).

After treatment, serial dilutions were plated on agar for colony-forming unit (CFU) counting. Biofilm inhibition was assessed after 48 h incubation using crystal violet staining, and absorbance was measured at 590 nm. Biofilm thickness was further analyzed by confocal laser scanning microscopy (CLSM).

Bacterial membrane integrity was evaluated by measuring extracellular ATP leakage. For morphological analysis, samples were fixed in 2.5% glutaraldehyde, dehydrated through graded ethanol, and observed by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM).

For gene expression analysis, total bacterial RNA was extracted, reverse-transcribed, and analyzed by qRT-PCR. Expression levels of recA, katA, sodA, sarA, agrA, and icaA were normalized to 16 S rRNA.

Cell culture and immunomodulatory assays

RAW264.7 macrophages, HUVECs, HaCaT keratinocytes, and L929 fibroblasts were cultured in DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum under standard conditions.

Cytotoxicity was evaluated using the CCK-8 assay after incubation with nanozymes (25–200 µg mL− 1). For intracellular bacterial clearance assays, RAW264.7 cells were infected with MRSA (MOI = 10) for 2 h, followed by gentamicin treatment (100 µg mL− 1, 1 h) to eliminate extracellular bacteria. Cells were then treated with nanozymes with or without NIR irradiation, lysed with Triton X-100, and plated for CFU enumeration.

Macrophage polarization was analyzed by flow cytometry using CD86 and CD206 markers. Arg-1 expression was additionally evaluated to assess reparative polarization. Cytokine secretion (TNF-α and IL-1β) was quantified by ELISA.

Expression of LAMP1, RAB7, p62, LC3, and iNOS proteins was analyzed by western blotting to evaluate phagolysosomal maturation and autophagic flux.

Oxidative stress was induced using H2O2 (500 µM, 2 h). Intracellular ROS levels were measured using DCFH-DA fluorescence, and mitochondrial membrane potential was assessed by JC-1 staining.

Cellular metabolic function was analyzed using a Seahorse XF96 analyzer. Mitochondrial stress tests were performed using sequential injections of oligomycin, FCCP, and rotenone/antimycin A to determine oxygen consumption rate (OCR) and extracellular acidification rate (ECAR).

Transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses

For transcriptomic analysis, bacteria treated with FeCo-CN were subjected to RNA extraction, rRNA depletion, and library construction. Sequencing was performed on an Illumina NovaSeq platform. Differentially expressed genes were identified using DESeq2 with |log2FC| > 1 and adjusted p < 0.05. Functional enrichment analysis was conducted using KEGG and Gene Ontology databases.

For metabolomic analysis, bacterial metabolites were extracted using a methanol/acetonitrile/water mixture (2:2:1, v/v/v). Samples were analyzed by LC-MS/MS in both positive and negative ion modes. Data were processed for peak alignment and annotation, followed by PCA and PLS-DA. Differential metabolites were defined by VIP > 1 and p < 0.05 and subjected to KEGG pathway enrichment analysis.

In vivo diabetic infected wound model

All animal procedures were approved by the institutional ethics committee. Male C57BL/6 mice (6–8 weeks) were rendered diabetic by intraperitoneal injection of streptozotocin. Mice with fasting blood glucose levels > 16.7 mmol L− 1 were used.

Two full-thickness dorsal wounds (6 mm diameter) were created and inoculated with MRSA (1 × 107 CFU). Mice were randomly assigned to PBS, Co-CN, FeCo-CN, and FeCo-CN + PTT groups (n = 8). Nanozymes (200 µg in 20 µL PBS) were applied topically once daily for three days.

For photothermal treatment, wounds were irradiated with an 808 nm laser (1.0 W cm− 2, 5 min), and the surface temperature was maintained at 45–50 ℃ under infrared monitoring.

Wound healing was photographed at predetermined time points and quantified using ImageJ. On day 3, wound tissues were collected for CFU counting and flow cytometry analysis (CD45, F4/80, CD86, CD206). On days 7 and 15, tissues were harvested for histological staining (H&E, Masson, Giemsa) and immunostaining (IL-6, CD31, α-SMA, Ki67, CD4, FOXP3).

Statistical analysis

All data are presented as mean ± standard deviation. Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 9. Normality and variance homogeneity were assessed prior to analysis. Two-group comparisons were performed using unpaired Student’s t-test, while multiple groups were analyzed by one-way ANOVA with Tukey’s post hoc test. Wound healing kinetics were analyzed using two-way repeated-measures ANOVA. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

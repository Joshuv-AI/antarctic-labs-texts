Discovery of a novel VEGFR2 inhibitor using integrated structure-based docking study and functional validation: potential applications in targeted cancer therapy
Authors: Hussam Albassam, Saad Alobid, Faris Almutairi, Faisal Altowairqi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractCancer remains a predominant cause of mortality worldwide, with conventional therapies often limited by adverse side effects and the development of drug resistance. Targeting receptor tyrosine kinases (RTKs), especially vascular endothelial growth factor receptor 2 (VEGFR2), presents a viable strategy due to its pivotal role in tumor angiogenesis. This study employed an integrative approach combining structure-based docking study, kinase inhibition assays, and cytotoxicity evaluations to identify novel VEGFR2 inhibitors. A Protein Kinase Diversity Set library comprising over 2,000 compounds was screened via molecular docking against the VEGFR2 crystal structure. The highest-ranking candidates were subjected to detailed binding interaction analyses. Cytotoxicity assays demonstrated significant anti-proliferative effects in VEGFR2-overexpressing U87 cells, while enzymatic assays confirmed one novel compound's potency in inhibiting VEGFR2 kinase activity. Redocking simulations focusing on the receptor's active site ensured comprehensive conformational exploration of ligand binding modes. Our results identify a promising lead compound as a novel inhibitor of VEGFR2, with potential applicability as an anti-angiogenic and anticancer therapeutic agent, warranting further optimization.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s44446-026-00072-4.Keywords: Targeted cancer therapy, VEGFR2 signaling pathway, VEGFR2 inhibitor, Cancer drug discovery, Structure-based docking study

Introduction

Cancer remains one of the most formidable and deadly diseases worldwide, posing significant challenges to effective treatment due to its complex biology and the limitations of current therapies (Siegel et al. 2025). While initially effective, conventional chemotherapeutic agents often cause substantial side effects and frequently encounter issues with acquired resistance, which limit their long-term efficacy. As a result, there is a critical need to develop targeted therapeutic strategies to inhibit tumor progression and metastasis with greater efficacy.

Dysregulation of signaling pathways that regulate cellular proliferation, survival, and angiogenesis plays a vital role in tumor progression and metastasis. Protein tyrosine kinases, particularly receptor tyrosine kinases (RTKs), play a central role in mediating these processes. RTKs are transmembrane receptors that, upon ligand binding, undergo dimerization and activation of their kinase domains, initiating downstream signaling cascades that promote cell growth, migration, and division (Gonzalez-Magaldi et al. 2020). Alterations, such as overexpression or mutation, often result in constitutive activation of RTKs, which is frequently linked to oncogenesis and disease progression (Blume-Jensen and Hunter 2001; Tsatsanis and Spandidos 2004).

Among the RTKs, vascular endothelial growth factor receptors (VEGFRs) play a critical role in angiogenesis, the process of forming new blood vessels that supply nutrients and oxygen to growing tumors (Liu et al. 2021). VEGFR2, also known as KDR, is recognized as the predominant mediator of VEGF-induced angiogenic signaling. It is primarily expressed in endothelial cells and stimulates cell proliferation, migration, and neovascularization in response to VEGF ligand binding (Olsson et al. 2006). Overexpression of VEGFR2 has been observed in numerous cancer types, including breast cancer, colorectal cancer, non-small lung cancer, bladder cancer, and melanoma, which contribute to the development of an environment conducive to tumor growth and metastasis (Donnem et al. 2010; Duff et al. 2006; Ghosh et al. 2008; Mehnert et al. 2010; Xia et al. 2006).

Current anti-angiogenic therapies primarily target VEGFR2 activity through small molecules competing with ATP binding within the kinase domain or biologics interfering with VEGF ligand interactions (Traxler 2003). Although several inhibitors have been developed and employed clinically, challenges persist, including the development of resistance and adverse effects. For instance, axitinib has been recognized as a potent VEGFR2 inhibitor, demonstrating significant anti-angiogenic activity by disrupting the VEGF signaling pathway essential for tumor vascularization. Despite its efficacy, the clinical use of axitinib is associated with notable adverse effects such as hypertension, fatigue, and gastrointestinal disturbances (Bhargava and Robinson 2011). These challenges emphasize the complexity and importance of developing more selective and tolerable VEGFR2 inhibitors, which remains a crucial focus of research.

This study employed a comprehensive approach, combining molecular docking studies, VEGFR2 kinase activity assays, and cytotoxicity screening, to identify potential inhibitors. We further analyzed the novel compound's binding mode through molecular modeling and predicted its absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion, and toxicity (ADMET) profile to evaluate its drug-like properties. Our investigations revealed a new compound that effectively inhibits VEGFR2 activity and induces cytotoxic effects in relevant cellular models. These findings suggest that the lead compound has significant potential for further optimization and development to function as an anti-angiogenic and anticancer agent.

Methods

Cell culture

The U87 cell line was obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC, Manassas, VA, USA). These cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle’s Medium (DMEM) (Gibco, New York, NY, USA) with the addition of 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 100 units/mL penicillin, and 100 µg/mL streptomycin, in an environment with 5% CO2 at 37 °C.

Ligands and protein preparation

A Protein Kinase Diversity Set, consisting of over 2,000 compounds that are commercially available provided by Life Chemicals, Inc., was employed as the ligand source for this study. The compounds were obtained in SDF format, rendering them ready for docking procedures. The crystallographic structure of VEGFR2 bound to axitinib was sourced from the Protein Data Bank (PDB ID: 4AG8) with a resolution of 1.95 Å. The protein structure underwent a thorough examination for any discontinuities or errors using the Swiss Protein Database Viewer, followed by necessary corrections. Information about the active site was gathered by identifying the co-crystal ligand-bound residues through Discovery Studio Visualizer. The process involved the removal of the co-crystal ligand, water molecules, and other non-essential atoms, while polar hydrogens were added to enhance the structural integrity. The refined protein models were then saved in PDB format to support subsequent docking studies.

Structure-based docking study

Structure-based docking study using docking simulations was performed on the selected compound library. The selected chemical compounds and protein structure were uploaded into the PyRx software interface. Energy minimization was conducted using the conjugate gradient algorithm with the Universal Force Field (UFF), where the total number of steps was set to 200, and the update frequency was set to 1 step per iteration. The minimization process was configured to halt once the energy difference was less than 0.1 kcal/mol. Subsequently, both the chemical compounds and protein structures were saved in PDBQT format utilizing the Open Babel tool within PyRx. Binding energy calculations were carried out using PyRx AutoDock Vina (Dallakyan and Olson 2015). A grid box was defined to encompass the active site of the crystal structure, with dimensions set in Å as follows: center (X, Y, Z) = (25.1074, 25.9017, 18.7289) and size (X, Y, Z) = (23.5808, 20.6658, 18.7289), utilizing an exhaustiveness value of 8. The top-ranked compounds from this screening were then analyzed in greater detail using Discovery Studio software.

Molecular re-docking

Molecular docking studies were conducted to assess the binding interactions of compounds with the VEGFR2 receptor. The crystal structure of VEGFR2 in complex with axitinib was retrieved from the Protein Data Bank (PDB ID: 4AG8), which was resolved at a 1.95 Å resolution. SwissDock software (https://www.swissdock.ch/, accessed on November 5, 2024) was employed for docking simulations due to its robust algorithm for accurately predicting ligand-receptor interactions (Bugnon et al. 2024; Grosdidier et al. 2011; Röhrig et al. 2023; Zoete et al. 2016). The docking procedure focused on the active binding site of VEGFR2, defined using specific coordinates. The binding site was centered at coordinates 19, 32, and 39 Å, with a docking box size set to 24 Å × 24 Å × 24 Å to ensure comprehensive exploration of potential ligand-receptor conformations. This configuration was chosen to encompass both the primary and adjacent interacting residues, thoroughly examining possible binding interactions.

Enzymatic assays for VEGFR2

Kinase assays were performed using the Kinase-Glo Plus Luminescence Kinase Assay Kit (Promega, Cat# V3772) to evaluate enzyme activity by measuring the remaining ATP following the kinase reaction. The luminescent signal generated is directly proportional to ATP concentration and inversely proportional to kinase activity. Test compounds, including F43, F04, F52, and axitinib, were each prepared as a 10 mM stock solution in DMSO, with a test range of 0.0003 to 10 µM. Pazopanib was used as a reference compound, with a 1 mM stock concentration in DMSO and a test range of 0.00003—1 µM. In each 50 µL reaction, 5 µL of these diluted compounds ensured a final DMSO concentration of 1%. The reactions, conducted at 30 °C for 45 min, contained 40 mM Tris (pH 7.4), 10 mM MgCl2, 0.1 mg/mL BSA, 1 mM DTT, 10 µM ATP, and 0.2 mg/mL PTK substrate Poly (Glu:Tyr 4:1). The enzyme component was derived from the VEGFR2 (KDR) Kinase Assay Kit (BPS Cat# 40,325), specifically using 20 ng of enzyme per reaction with Enzyme Lot #191,029. After the initial incubation, 50 µL of Kinase-Glo Plus Luminescence assay solution was added and incubated at room temperature for 15 min. The luminescence was then measured using a BioTek Synergy 2 microplate reader to quantify kinase activity.

Cell viability assay

U87 cells were initially plated into 96-well plates and allowed to adhere and grow for 24 h. Post-incubation, the culture medium was substituted with fresh media containing the specified concentrations of the experimental compounds, and the cells were exposed to these conditions for a subsequent 24-h period. After this exposure, 10 μL of MTT reagent (sourced from MedChemExpress, NJ, USA) was introduced into each well, following the protocol outlined by the manufacturer. After adding 100 µL of dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) to dissolve the resulting formazan crystals, the plates were covered with aluminium foil and kept in the dark for 10–15 min. Consequently, the optical density was measured at 570 nm using a microplate reader, where an increase in optical density signified a higher number of viable cells. Data analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism software. The half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) values were determined from dose–response curves via nonlinear regression (curve fitting).

ADMET analysis

ADMET prediction platforms enable the predication of pharmacokinetic and drug-like properties of compounds by leveraging structural similarities and historical experimental data. Further ADMET profiling was conducted on F43 and the reference compound axitinib. These compounds were evaluated using ADMETlab 2.0 (https://admetmesh.scbdd.com accessed on November 10, 2024), where the canonical SMILES of the selected hits were input for analysis (Dong et al. 2018).

Results

Target-based docking study

In our efforts to identify potential compounds that target VEGFR2, we selected a specialized Protein Kinase Diversity Set containing 2,000 compounds from the Life Chemicals HTS Compound Collection (Supplementary Table 1). This library is designed to enhance the drug discovery process, comprising compounds with optimal physicochemical properties and significant structural diversity, making them particularly suitable for molecular docking studies. The selected compounds demonstrate a high similarity (Tanimoto ≥ 0.82) to known protein kinase inhibitors with strong activity, specifically those with IC50 or Ki values of ≤ 3 μM and Residual Activity ≤ 50%. This comprehensive selection process performed by Life Chemical could provide a robust foundation for our investigation into effective VEGFR2 inhibitors.

To validate our protocol, we selected the crystal structure of VEGFR2 complexed with axitinib (PDB ID: 4AG8) from the Protein Data Bank. Structural modifications were performed on the protein, followed by docking simulations. The docking results were evaluated by superimposing the docked structures onto the original crystal structure, as illustrated in Fig. 1, which yielded a backbone root mean of standard deviation (RMSD) of 1.071, confirming the precision and reliability of the software used. Subsequently, a docking study was conducted against a library of compounds. Among those assessed, several top contenders exhibited remarkable docking scores that surpassed those of the reference compound, axitinib, as presented in Table 1, and their structures are demonstrated in Fig. 2. These scores, ranging from −9.3 to −9.0, suggest favorable binding affinities to the receptor, warranting their selection for further investigation.

An analysis of hydrogen bonds highlighted the crucial roles of CYS919 and LYS920. These residues often form hydrogen bonds with the compounds, indicating they are key players in the binding process. For instance, compound F04 stood out with a docking score of −9.3. It formed a hydrogen bond with CYS919 and engaged in several hydrophobic interactions with other important residues such as LEU840, LEU1035, PHE1047, VAL899, VAL916, CYS1045, ALA866, VAL848, and even CYS919 again. Similarly, compound F43 scored −9.2, establishing hydrogen bonds with both LYS920 and CYS919, while also interacting hydrophobically with LEU1035, PHE1047, LEU840, ALA866, CYS919, VAL848, and CYS1045.

Several compounds, such as F52, F85, and F49, also formed hydrogen bonds with CYS919 while demonstrating similar hydrophobic interactions within the VEGFR2 binding pocket. Compound F34 interacted with LYS920 via hydrogen bonds, alongside exhibiting hydrophobic interactions with LEU840, LEU1035, PHE1047, VAL848, ALA866, and CYS1045. In contrast, compound F03 engaged both CYS919 and LYS920 through hydrogen bonds, with further hydrophobic interactions with LEU840, LEU1035, PHE1047, LYS838, VAL899, VAL916, CYS1045, VAL848, and ALA866. Likewise, compound F20 displayed hydrogen bonding with LYS920 and interacted hydrophobically with LEU840, LEU1035, PHE918, PHE1047, ALA866, CYS919, and VAL848.

With a docking score of −8.9, the reference compound axitinib formed hydrogen bonds with LEU840 and GLU850 and exhibited hydrophobic interactions with LYS838. These findings suggest that the novel compounds share comparable binding modalities with axitinib but also present potential as inhibitors of VEGFR2 activity. The consistent involvement of CYS919 and LYS920 in hydrogen bonding underscores their critical role in binding these compounds to VEGFR2.

Cell-based efficacy screening

To assess the cytotoxic potential of the candidate compounds on U87 glioblastoma cells, an MTT assay was conducted 24 h post-treatment, measuring cell viability. U87 cells are characterized by their aggressive, angiogenic tumor phenotype, with elevated expression levels of VEGF and VEGFR2 (Plate et al. 1992). The top-ranked compounds were purchased from Life Chemical, Inc., and administered at a concentration of 10 µM, with 0.1% DMSO serving as the vehicle control. The most pronounced reduction in cell viability was observed with compound F52, resulting in a 70% viability reduction (i.e., 30% viability), F43 with a 75% reduction (25% viability), F04 with an 80% reduction (20% viability), and the reference inhibitor axitinib with a 65% reduction (35% viability) (Fig. 3). Notably, the cytotoxic effects of F04, F43, and F52 were comparable to those of axitinib. Moreover, the IC50 was determined for F04, F43, and F52, yielding values of 98 µM, 86 µM, and 55 µM, respectively. Axitinib exhibited an IC50 of 157.5 µM under identical conditions (Fig. 4). These results demonstrate that F04, F43, and F52 possess significant cytotoxic activity against U87 cells, with F52 showing the highest potency; thus, these compounds were selected for further investigation.

VEGFR2 inhibition screening

To assess the inhibitory activity against the VEGFR2 receptor, a VEGFR2 kinase activity assay was conducted. Results indicated that F43 exhibited a moderate inhibitory effect, reducing VEGFR2 activity by 47%. In contrast, reference compounds axitinib and pazopanib achieved significantly higher inhibition rates, 98% and 99.5%, respectively. Notably, compounds F04 and F52 displayed no detectable inhibitory activity against VEGFR2, suggesting that their cytotoxic effects are mediated via mechanisms independent of VEGFR2 signaling (Table 2). Further analysis was conducted to determine the IC50 of F43 against VEGFR2, yielding an IC50 of 1.98 µM (Fig. 5A and B), whereas axitinib exhibited a substantially lower IC50 of 0.001 µM (Fig. 5C and D). In addition, the inhibitory potency of pazopanib against VEGFR2 was evaluated, resulting in an IC50 value of 0.11 µM (Fig. 5E and F). Based on these preliminary activity profiles, F43 was selected as the lead compound for further investigation.

Binding pose analysis

In our molecular re-docking study, we utilized the Attracting Cavities (AC) 2.0 approach for re-docking using SwissDock. The AC method simplifies the complex energy landscape of the macromolecule by creating a smooth, attractive energy profile generated by virtual points surrounding the protein surface. This allows for efficient sampling of ligand conformations through straightforward rotations, translations, and geometry optimizations. By refining ligand positions within this simplified landscape and subsequently optimizing against the actual protein energy landscape, including implicit solvation treatment, AC enhances the accuracy of ligand–protein interaction predictions. Therefore, the interaction profiles of compounds F43 and axitinib with the VEGFR2 receptor were elucidated through detailed binding mode analysis. A combination of strong hydrogen bonds and extensive hydrophobic interactions characterizes F43. A significant hydrogen bond was established between CYS919:HN and F43:O2 (2.13266 Å), indicating a stable interaction contributing to the ligand's binding affinity. Additional hydrogen bonds with residues such as LEU840 and carbon-hydrogen interactions with aromatic residues PHE918 and PHE1047 further enhance spatial orientation, ensuring effective receptor interaction. Hydrophobic interactions include notable π-π T-shaped interactions with PHE1047 and multiple π-Alkyl interactions with VAL848, ALA866, and LEU1035, suggesting a well-structured hydrophobic pocket suitable for the ligand (Fig. 6).

F43 exhibited superior solubility (LogS: −4.414 log mol/L), significantly outpacing axitinib (LogS: −5.251 log mol/L), which may facilitate enhanced oral bioavailability. Conversely, axitinib demonstrated a higher LogD7.4 (2.648) compared to F43 (1.012), indicating improved permeability across biological membranes and greater LogP of lipophilicity (4.157) compared to F43 (3.008), which is vital for tissue distribution.

Both compounds showed comparable Caco-2 permeability values, yet axitinib's higher human intestinal absorption (HIA) value of 0.817, contrasted with F43's 0.697, suggests more effective absorption in the gastrointestinal tract. Furthermore, F43's weaker P-glycoprotein inhibitory potential may favorably influence its drug-drug interaction profile.

Metabolic assessments indicated that F43 exhibits potent inhibition of several Cytochrome P450 (CYP) enzymes, raising concerns for potential drug-drug interactions. In contrast, axitinib showed lower inhibition and thus may present a more favorable safety profile. Regarding elimination, F43 has a half-life of 1.446 h and a clearance rate of 2.113 mL/min/kg, both of which are slightly more favorable than those of axitinib, with a half-life of 1.785 h and a clearance rate of 1.331 mL/min/kg. Although both compounds exhibit relatively short half-lives, axitinib's protracted duration may contribute to a sustained therapeutic effect.

Finally, toxicity analysis suggests that F43 has a lower risk of hERG channel blocking compared to axitinib, which could confer a better cardiac safety profile. However, unlike axitinib, which exhibits no such indication, F43 raises some concerns regarding mutagenicity with a positive Ames test result.

Discussion

Pursuing novel inhibitors targeting VEGFR2 is crucial for advancing cancer therapeutics, particularly given VEGFR2's central role in angiogenesis and tumor growth. This study deployed an integrative approach combining molecular docking, cytotoxicity assays, inhibition screening, binding mode analyses, and ADMET profiling to evaluate compounds from the Life Chemical Library, aiming to uncover candidates with superior activity profiles compared to the reference compound, axitinib.

Our findings indicate that several novel compounds exhibit promising binding affinities toward VEGFR2, as evidenced by docking scores surpassing axitinib. Notably, compounds F04, F43, and F52 emerged as significant players, with F04 and F43 forming strong hydrogen bonds with key residues such as CYS919 and LYS920, which are pivotal for receptor-ligand stability (Bayoumi et al. 2024; Wei et al. 2018). The observed interactions suggest advantageous conformations within VEGFR2's active site. Although F04 and F52 do not notably inhibit VEGFR2, their strong cytotoxic effects imply that they exert their effect through alternative mechanisms, possibly involving other cellular pathways.

The MTT assay results underscore the cytotoxic efficacy of F52 and F04, revealing their significant impact on reducing the viability of U87 cells, which suggest they could be useful for suppressing tumors independent of VEGFR2 pathway. Additionally, F43 stood out as the compound with the VEGFR2 inhibitory activity among the new candidates, marking it as a promising prospect for further study in its structure modification. This dual characteristic of VEGFR2 inhibition alongside cytotoxic efficacy highlights F43's potential as a lead compound. Remarkably, F43 exhibited a greater cytotoxic effect compared to axitinib, enhancing its therapeutic potential. This observation can be substantiated by the findings indicating that axitinib-induced reactive oxygen species (ROS)-mediated senescence and mitotic catastrophe, which may contribute to a reduction in cytotoxicity relative to the effects observed with F43 (Morelli et al. 2015, 2017). However, it also demonstrated moderate VEGFR2 inhibition, indicating that additional optimization is required to maximize its inhibitory effectiveness.

Binding mode analysis further delineates the intricacies of F43's engagement with VEGFR2, characterized by a mixture of hydrogen bonds and hydrophobic interactions that mimic axitinib's binding patterns, yet through distinct modalities. These findings highlight the sophistication of F43's binding, chiefly through π-π and π-Alkyl interactions, which may inform structure–activity relationship (SAR) studies in refining its pharmacological attributes.

The ADMET analysis reveals critical insights into the pharmacokinetic profiles of F43 versus axitinib. F43's enhanced solubility is a promising trait for oral administration, albeit its potential for drug-drug interactions due to CYP enzyme inhibition warrants careful consideration. Indeed, the inhibition of cytochrome P450 isoforms may lead to the accumulation of drugs metabolized by these enzymes, potentially resulting in drug toxicity (McDonnell and Dang 2013). Furthermore, F43 is predicted to induce mutagenicity, which must be addressed during its optimization process to ensure safe and effective development. However, structural modifications of chemical compounds recognized as inhibitors of CYP isoforms, hERG blockers, and AMES mutagens can optimize their toxicity profiles during the lead optimization process, thereby minimizing adverse effects (Kaplan et al. 1972). Axitinib's enhanced lipophilicity, superior membrane permeability, and minimal CYP inhibitory effects emphasize its advantageous traits in clinical environments, highlighting the crucial need to balance these factors during the lead compound optimization.

In conclusion, F43 and axitinib engage VEGFR2 through different mechanisms and have unique pharmacokinetic profiles. F43 exhibits promising solubility and cytotoxicity while showing moderate receptor inhibition. This suggests that F43 could serve as a useful scaffold for developing more potent VEGFR2 inhibitors with acceptable pharmacokinetic properties. Future efforts will focus on structural modifications to enhance the potency of VEGFR2 inhibitory effects of F43. Concurrently, attention will be directed towards optimizing its ADMET properties to enhance its clinical application potential. This comprehensive approach is expected to significantly improve the therapeutic viability of the lead compound.

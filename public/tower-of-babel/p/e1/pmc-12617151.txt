OA20439 Road Safety of Pregnant and Breastfeeding Women in the European Union: An Overlooked Public Health Priority
Authors: V Vivilaki, M Tzeli
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractProblemRoad traffic injuries remain a leading cause of preventable morbidity and mortality across the European Union (EU). Pregnant and breastfeeding women represent particularly vulnerable groups whose needs are insufficiently addressed in road safety policies, clinical guidelines, and mobility safety design.DescriptionPhysiological changes during pregnancy increase the risk of adverse maternal and fetal outcomes. Breastfeeding mothers also face unique considerations when travelling with infants, including safe positioning, child restraint systems, and post-crash trauma care. Beyond private car use, many women rely on bicycles, e-scooters, or public transport. These modes present further safety challenges: limited infrastructure adapted for pregnant women, insufficient guidance on safe bicycle use during pregnancy, and lack of harmonised regulations for transporting infants on bicycles or in prams across EU countries. Despite EU-wide seatbelt legislation, awareness and adherence remain uneven, and crash-test standards rarely account for the specific biomechanics of pregnancy or the realities of breastfeeding women travelling with infants. Evidence gaps persist in infant transportation safety—particularly in cycling contexts and multimodal transport.ResultsThis presentation will synthesise available evidence on maternal and infant road safety, highlight disparities across EU member states, and propose actions aligned with EU strategies. Recommendations will include: a)Supporting Vision Zero by embedding maternal and infant safety within EU Road Safety Policy Framework 2021–2030; b) Strengthening the EU Urban Mobility Framework with infrastructure adapted for pregnant cyclists and safe infant transport systems; c) Advancing the EU Strategy on the Rights of the Child through harmonised regulations on infant car seats, prams, and bicycle carriers; d) Promoting equity in trauma care, consistent with the EU Health Union, to ensure timely access for pregnant and breastfeeding women; e) Integrating safety education into prenatal classes by community midwives, in line with WHO and UNICEF recommendations.LessonsAddressing the road safety of pregnant and breastfeeding women across all modes of transport will advance gender-sensitive health security, reduce preventable harms, and safeguard outcomes for mothers and newborns in Europe.

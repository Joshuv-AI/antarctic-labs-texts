IFPHOR0022 Mindful Medicine: Overcoming Barriers to Wellbeing Interventions in Medical Education
Authors: Emma Brown, Gemma Field, Mei Yee Tang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMedical students experience high levels of stress, burnout, and psychological distress, yet engagement with mindfulness-based wellbeing interventions is inconsistent. Using the COM-B model, this study aimed to identify behavioural determinants that might influence engagement with mindfulness interventions, offering insights into how medical student wellbeing can be improved on a systemic level.MethodsTen medical students participated in semi-structured interviews discussing mental health challenges, coping strategies, and attitudes towards mindfulness. Data were analysed using reflective thematic analysis and mapped to the COM-B model to identify barriers and facilitators to engagement.FindingsFive key themes emerged. First, ‘trust and lived experience’ were central for engagement. Students valued credible evidence, peer validation, and institutional endorsement (reflective motivation). Second, ‘attitudes and beliefs about mindfulness’ varied, with misconceptions (e.g., viewing as difficult) reducing engagement (psychological capability). Third, having ‘no time for wellbeing’ and accessibility were significant factors, with students preferring flexible, low-effort, digital formats due to academic pressures (physical opportunity). Fourth, ‘emotional regulation and self-reflection’ were considered useful, particularly coping strategies that increased insight, calmness and perceived control (automatic motivation). Fifth, ‘medical student image’ increased barriers, as stigma, identity and comparisons associated with help-seeking discourage engagement.DiscussionEngagement with mindfulness interventions is determined by behavioural and contextual factors rather than perceived benefits alone. Behaviourally informed interventions addressing misconceptions, offering flexibility, and providing peer support and stigma reduction could significantly improve engagement. These findings have implications for public health policy, with potential to enhance overall healthcare delivery by supporting the wellbeing of future practitioners.

Multi-omics reveals glycolipid metabolism processes during the early development of largemouth bass (Micropterus salmoides)
Authors: Shiping Su, Xinxin Liu, Caixia Song, Junqing Li, Wei Shu, Qingming Xie, Jixiang He, Bingjie Jiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The largemouth bass (Micropterus salmoides) is one of the most valuable freshwater farmed fish in China, whose production involves a lot of energy consumption. However, the genetic regulation of energy metabolism in largemouth bass early development remains unclear. Herein, combined transcriptome and metabolome analyses were carried out for six early developmental stages: blastocyst, gastrulation, hatching, 1 day post-hatching (dph), 2 dph and 3 dph. Early embryos’ yolk and oil droplet diameters decreased significantly with embryonic development (P < 0.05). A total of 853.16 Gb of clean data was generated in RNA-sequencing, and the Q30 quality score was 96.72%. The majority of the 17,847 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were related to glycolipid metabolic pathways (steroid biosynthesis, butanoate metabolism and pentose and glucuronate interconversion pathways). In the metabolomics analyses, 2,152 differential metabolites (DEMs) were identified at the early developmental stages. The pathway analyses indicated that these DEMs were involved in starch and sucrose metabolism, carbohydrate digestion and absorption, glycerophospholipid metabolism and glyoxylate and dicarboxylate metabolism pathways. Additionally, the conjoint analysis revealed that several key genes, such as pld, gpcpd, etnk1, lypla2, lcat and gpat were altered their expression levels, thereby affecting lecithin, glycerol-1-phosphate, glycerophosphocholine, ethanolamine and 1-acyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphocholine metabolism. The findings shed new light on the mechanisms of glycolipid metabolism during the early development of largemouth bass.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12864-026-12692-0.

Introduction

The largemouth bass (Micropterus salmoides) is a precious carnivorous fish originally from North America [1]. It was introduced into China in 1983 owing to its high growth rate, strong disease resistance, rich nutrition and delicious flavor [2]. Currently, it is one of China’s most significant freshwater aquaculture species. Data showed that a total of 888,000 tons of largemouth bass were farmed in 2023, an increase of 10.66% from 2022 (Data from China Fisheries Statistical Yearbook 2024). However, largemouth bass farming in China faces problems such as a low nursery survival rate, a high morbidity rate, degradation of germplasm, frequent occurrence of diseases and a shortage of excellent seedlings, which has been severely hampered by issues with largemouth bass farming in China [3]. In order to meet the huge market needs, artificial breeding has become the main source of cultivated largemouth bass seed because of its high yield. During this process, the cultured largemouth bass faces a number of challenges, including poor growth performance and mineral deficiencies [4]. These problems might be caused by alterations in glycolipid metabolism during early development, which affect the growth and development of fry and stock [5]. Therefore, it is important to determine the glycolipid metabolism of largemouth bass during early development to optimize its nutrition and culture.

The endogenous nutrition of largemouth bass can be categorized into two distinct stages: the embryo and yolk-sac larva [6]. During these developmental stages, cell division and organogenesis depend on nutrients derived from the yolk and oil globules [6]. In the early stages of fish development, the degradation and metabolism of yolk are key physiological processes. These processes require the breakdown and utilization of yolk components to provide energy and nutrients during the endogenous nutrient phase. During this period, the fish rely exclusively on the yolk for nutrition [7]. Utilization of the yolk includes not only the absorption of nutrients from the yolk, but also the use of these nutrients for biosynthesis and the maintenance of body functions, including growth, development and physiological homeostasis [8]. This is critical for fish survival and adaptation to the environment, and influences the length and quality of the endogenous nutrient phase. In addition, yolk utilization significantly affects fish behavior, including the initiation of swimming and feeding activities. These behaviors are decisive for the transition of fish from yolk-dependent endogenous nutrition to environmentally-dependent exogenous feeding [9].

In addition, the variation of lipids in the yolk reflects the nutritional requirements of developing embryos and larvae. Yolk lipids are usually distributed in spherical structures known as oil droplets, whose size, number and location vary in different fish [10]. In addition, the pattern of lipid utilization and metabolism varies in different fish species. Nevertheless, the embryonic development of most fish species, including Scophthalmus maximus, Chrysophrys Auratus and Pseudosciaena Crocea, generally utilizes saturated fatty acids (SFAs) and monounsaturated fatty acids (MUFAs) preferentially [11–13]. Although several studies have explored the content and function of lipid substances in fish embryos, our knowledge of the dynamics of energy metabolism during the early stages of largemouth bass development is scanty.

In this study, we analyzed the largemouth bass using both transcriptomic and metabolomic methods to gather more information about the molecular basis of energy metabolism during its embryonic development. We selected six early developmental stages to examine and contrast the histological changes. Subsequently, we identified novel candidate genes and metabolites potentially responsible for regulating energy metabolism processes during the early developmental stages of largemouth bass through integrated transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses. Thus, the purpose of this work was to examine the dynamics of endogenous glycolipid metabolism during early development, and to provide an important basis for the improved culture of largemouth bass fry.

Materials and methods

Embryo hatching and sample collectio

The largemouth bass were cultured and spawned at Anhui Zhanglin Aquaculture Co., LTD. (Tongling, China). Twenty pairs of mature adult largemouth bass (each pair consisting of 2 male fish and 1 female fish) were selected and placed in 20 net cages (dimensions: 70 cm × 50 cm × 40 cm) for artificial breeding. Spawning was promoted by injecting chorionic gonadotropin (1200 IU/kg for females and 800 IU/kg for males). Subsequently, the embryo developmental stages were examined under a stereomicroscope (SMZ 745T, Nikon, Tokyo, Japan). Morphometric measurements of the embryos and larvae were performed, including the yolk sac diameter and the oil globule diameter. Based on Yu et al';s [14] study of largemouth bass embryonic development, the samples were collected from six stages, such as blastocyst (Bl), gastrulation (Ga), hatching (Ha), 1 day post-hatching (dph), 2 dph, and 3dph. Each stage was grouped into three replicates and 50 fertilized eggs were taken from each group. Before being stored for later use at -80 °C in RNA storage reagent (Tiangen, Beijing, China), all embryos were quickly cleaned with phosphate buffered saline (PBS).

RNA isolation, library construction and sequencing

Total RNA was obtained from each sample using the Trizol Reagent (Invitrogen, Waltham, MA, USA). The quality and amount of RNA was confirmed by the NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Magnetic beads were used to enrich the quality-assured mRNA, and an Illumina NED Kit (San Diego, CA, USA) was used to create the cDNA library by tagmentation. The libraries were targeted for transcriptome sequencing by the Illumina HiSeq 6000 high-throughput platform. Eventually, 150 bp paired-end fragments were obtained.

The raw sequencing reads were quality controlled using Fast QC software [15]. The clean reads were generated for subsequent analysis by stripping out splices and low quality reads (low-quality reads with > 50% bases and phred quality scores of ≤ 20). The clean reads were aligned to the reference genome of largemouth bass (NCBI assembly accession: GCF_018340385.1) by HISAT2 v 2.1.0 [16]. The aligned sequences were spliced by Stringtie software [17]. After splicing, the statistics of hierarchical clustering and the principal component analysis (PCA) were conducted by the program R (http://www.R-project. org/). The expression of all genes was performed by the edgeR program [18]. The genes with |log2 (fold change) | > 1 and false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.05 were recognized as differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in the comparison of two groups. The GO function and KEGG pathway were conducted on DEGs using Goatools v 0.6.5 and KOBAS v 2.1.1, respectively, to investigate the biological functions of these DEGs. The P < 0.05 threshold indicated significant GO function and KEGG pathway.

Quantitative real-time reverse transcription PCR (qRT-PCR)

The qRT-PCR was used to screen 15 DEGs in order to confirm the transcriptome’s dependability. Table S1 listed the primer sequences utilized in qRT-PCR. First, a total of 2 µg of RNA was reverse transcribed to cDNA using a PrimeScript™ RT-PCR kit (Takara, Shiga, Japan). The qRT-PCR reactions were then carried out by the cDNA as the template and 2× QuantiFast® SYBR® Green PCR Master Mix (Qiagen, Germany) on a LightCycler® 480 II Real-time PCR Instrument (Roche, Switzerland) as previously mentioned [19]. The β-actin gene was chosen to be the internal reference gene, and the specificity of amplification was confirmed by melting curve analysis. All data (2−△△Ct) were tested by one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) and Duncan’s test using SPSS 25.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) (significance threshold, P < 0.05).

Metabolomics analysis

The embryo samples were added to pre-cooled solvent (methanol/water = 4:1), vortexed, and mixed for 1 min. The supernatant was centrifuged (12 000 × g for 10 min, 4 °C). After drying, the supernatant was re-dissolved in 100 µL of acetonitrile solvent (acetonitrile/water = 1:1) solution and analyzed by liquid chromatography tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) analysis. To ensure analytical stability and sample reproducibility, the quality control samples were obtained by blending equal aliquots of the supernatant. The samples were isolated on an Agilent 1290 Infinity LC ultra-high performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC) system. Subsequently, the separated samples were analyzed by positive and negative chromatography on a Thermo Q Exactive mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Finally, the mass spectra were performed by a Triple-TOF 5600 mass spectrometer (AB SCIEX, Framingham, MA, USA). The specific method of operation was thoroughly detailed in a prior publication [20].

Screening and analysis of the DEMs

The pre-processed data were subjected to the PCA and orthogonal partial least squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) by the R software package (Version 1.6.2), and the loading plots were constructed. The variable importance of projection (VIP) values of the metabolites was calculated in the OPLS-DA model. After subjecting the data to Student’s test and multiplicative analysis of variance, the metabolites with VIP values > 1 and P < 0.05 were identified as differential metabolites (DEMs). The DEMs were then annotated with GO and KEGG metabolic pathways, and screened for key metabolic pathways involved in the DEMs using enrichment analysis.

Combined analysis of the transcriptome and metabolome

The metabolomics data and transcriptomics data described above were used to synthesize DEGs and DEMs for the six periods. The Pearson correlation coefficient was obtained to identify the relevance between the DEGs and DEMs. Meanwhile, the DEGs and DEMs were sorted between transcriptomics and metabolomics profiles based on the absolute correlation coefficients. The topmost DEGs and DEMs were chosen for heatmapping by the R package. In addition, a KEGG pathway was conducted to determine shared metabolic pathways between the DEGs and DEMs. The heatmaps were drawn using TBtools v2.056 [12].

Results

Morphology of early embryo and juvenile development

The development of largemouth bass embryos at different developmental periods was observed, and the yolk and oil droplet diameters of early embryos were determined. In the blastocyst stage, the surface of the blastocyst was shaped like a curved moon wrapping downward toward the yolk portion, and the yolk and oil droplets were rounded, with average diameters of 1.18 mm and 0.48 mm, respectively (Fig. 1). In the gastrulation stage, an embryonic ring appeared, and the ring continued to wrap downward (Fig. 1A). At this time, the yolk began to shrink, and there were significant changes in the yolk and oil droplet diameters (P < 0.05, Fig. 1B). In the hatching period, the head broke through the egg membrane, and the young fish began to appear (Fig. 1A). At this time, the diameter of yolk and oil droplets reduced significantly (P < 0.05, Fig. 1B). The yolk and oil droplet diameters continued to decrease at 1, 2, and 3 dph compared to the pre-hatching period, and were all significantly lower than before hatching (P < 0.05, Fig. 1B). Therefore, the decrease in yolk and oil droplets diameter during early developmental stages indicated efficient utilisation of energy reserves.

Overview of the RNA-Sequencing (RNA-Seq) data

The RNA-Seq was used on samples from six development stages of the largemouth bass. A total of 141.65, 146.15, 148.16, 147.2, 147.68 and 149.07 Gb of raw reads, with means of 47.22, 48.72, 48.39, 49.07, 49.23 and 49.69 Gb raw reads from each library were obtained (Additional file 1: Table S1). Following quality screening, the 18 cDNA libraries yielded 853.16 Gb of clean reads. The percentage of bases with a quality score of Q30 was 96.72%, and the mean percentage of G + C content was 47.71% (Additional file 1: Table S1). Thereafter, over 98.29% of the RNA-Seq reads were mapped after the clean reads were compared to the reference genome (Additional file 1: Table S2).

Identification of DEGs

We performed the correlation and PCA analyses on the samples to assess the sample collection consistency. The correlation analysis showed very high correlation of Pearson correlation coefficients among the three biological replicates from the six periods (the mean value of R2 was 0.89, Fig. 2A). The PCA results showed that most of the samples from the same stage were grouped, indicating excellent reproducibility between samples within stages (Fig. 2B). Subsequently, gene expression was evaluated utilizing the Fragments Per Kilobase per Million mapped fragments (FPKM) method. The box line plot revealed that a sum of 26,613 genes was recognized (Fig. 2C). Compared with the later stages of development, more genes showed low expression levels in the Bl stage (FPKM = 0-0.25, Fig. 2D).

Following filtering, 17,847 DEGs were identified in the five adjacent stage comparisons. The results of cluster analysis indicated that the three biological replicates at each stage clustered together well (Fig. S1), suggesting that the three individuals selected at each stage were well replicated. The statistical data for the DEGs were illustrated in Fig. 3. Of these, the largest difference was found in the Ga vs. Bl comparison, with 8,570 upregulated DEGs and 5,624 downregulated DEGs. At this stage, the largemouth bass was out of the MZT process, and its own genome gradually formed. The number of DEGs was the lowest (1,252) in the 2 dph vs. Ha comparison, with 1,045 upregulated DEGs and 207 downregulated DEGs (Fig. 3A-E). The Venn diagram revealed that there were 48 DEGs shared among the six periods. In particular, 1,4194, 4,403, 2,531, 1,252 and 6,224 DEGs were observed in the Ga vs. Bl, Ha vs. Ga, 1 dph vs. Ha, 2 dph vs. 1 dph and 3 dph vs. 2 dph comparisons, respectively (Fig. 3F). DEG expression profiling and annotation analysis

In order to categorize the dynamics of the early development stages of the largemouth bass transcriptome, the gene expression profiles were clustered (Addition file 1: Table S3). These DEGs were categorized into 50 different clusters, of which 10 modules were statistically significant (P < 0.05, Fig. 4). The expression patterns of some major types of DEGs increased and then decreased (cluster 46, cluster 49), others decreased and then increased (cluster 0, cluster 1 and cluster 2), and some major types of DEGs had expression patterns that showed irregular dynamic fluctuations (cluster 47 and cluster 3). Cluster 39 consisted of genes whose expression level increased during embryonic development. Cluster 8 comprised genes whose expression levels decreased as embryonic development progressed. According to the GO analysis, the genes in these clusters were enriched in intracellular, plasma membrane, integral component of plasma membrane and integral component of membrane among cellular component terms. Most of the DEGs were classified as associated with calcium ion transport, cellular response to amino acid stimulus and calcium-mediated signaling in the biological process category, whereas glutamate receptor activity, voltage-gated calcium channel activity, calcium channel activity and ionotropic glutamate receptor activity were the most enriched terms among molecular functions (Addition file 1: Table S4, Fig. 5A).

Figure 5B showed the information of the top 30 KEGG pathways, among which 10 pathways wer significantly enriched (Addition file 1: Table S5). Most of them were related to glycolipid metabolic pathways, including sphingolipid metabolism, fatty acid biosynthesis, glycolysis/gluconeogenesis and biosynthesis of unsaturated fatty acids pathways (Fig. 5B). According to KEGG enrichment, 31 DEGs associated with glycolipid metabolism were identified and these DEGs were clustered and analyzed (Fig. 5C). These genes were predicted to be associated with glycolipid metabolism during embryonic development.

qRT-PCR verification

In order to confirm the gene expression levels revealed by RNA-Seq, 15 genes were chosen randomly for qRT-PCR validation (Fig. 5E-F). The Fig. 5F displayed the specific information regarding these genes (11 upregulated and 4 downregulated). The qRT-PCR results revealed that the expression profiles of the 15 genes corresponded with the RNA-Seq results, demonstrating the reliability and accuracy of the transcriptome data.

Multivariate statistical analysis of metabolites

To better study the molecular mechanisms of energy metabolism during largemouth bass early development, the metabolome was examined using LC-MS/MS. The PCA analysis indicated a high degree of similarity between samples within the groups and metabolites were significantly varied between groups (Fig. 6A). The OPLS-DA results demonstrated significant separation between the different groups (Fig. 6B-F). Overall, the multivariate analysis of metabolites displayed qualitative segregation between different developmental stages, indicating the reliability of the LC-MS/MS analysis.

Figure 7 displayed the metabolites’ relative abundance and amount in the samples. Among the nine identified metabolite categories, lipids and lipid-like molecules comprised 22.94%, organic acids and derivatives accounted for 18.59%, organoheterocyclic compounds constituted 15.06%, and the other categories made up 15.63% of the total metabolites (Fig. 7A). Notably, the number of downregulated DEMs was highest in the 3 dph vs. 2 dph comparison and lowest in the 1 dph vs. Ha comparison (Fig. 7B). In addition, there were 18 shared DEMs among the five comparison groups (Fig. 7C).

Transcriptome and metabolome joint analysis

Analysis of the common functional pathways was carried out to generate direct evidence for the main regulatory mechanisms between gene expression and metabolite abundance (Additional file 3 Tables S1-5). The analysis showed that certain pathways were essential in largemouth bass at different developmental stages, including arginine and proline metabolism, glyoxylate and dicarboxylate metabolism, purine metabolism, the pentose phosphate pathway, amino sugar and nucleotide sugar metabolism, pyrimidine metabolism, glycerophospholipid metabolism and tyrosine metabolism. Among them, the glycolipid metabolism pathway occupied a major share, including sphingolipid metabolism, glyoxylate and dicarboxylate metabolism, the pentose phosphate pathway, pentose and glucuronate interconversions, amino sugar and nucleotide sugar metabolism and glycerophospholipid metabolism. The key genes and metabolites in the glycerophospholipid metabolism signaling pathways were further analyzed. Most metabolites were significantly enriched at different developmental stages, including lecithin, glycerol-1-phosphate, glycerophosphocholine, ethanolamine and 1-Acyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphocholine (Fig. 9A). Additionally, the expression of the phospholipase D (pld), glycerophosphocholine phosphodiesterase 1 (gpcpd) and ethanolamine kinase 1 (etnk1) genes increased gradually with embryonic development, whereas the expression of lysophospholipase 2 (lypla2), lecithin-cholesterol acyltransferase (lcat) and glycerol-3-phosphate acyltransferase (gpat) progressively decreased (Fig. 9B).

Discussion

The early development of fish is a very dynamic and intricate process involving key structural and functional alterations in tissues, organs and systems [21]. This process requires large amounts of energy. The egg yolk and oil droplets serve as essential energy and nutrient reservoirs, and are thus critical to sustain life in the initial stages of development. We observed that the size of yolk decreased dramatically from the Bl to the 3 dph stages, indicating the crucial role of the yolk as a nutrient reserve, supporting the embryo through vital developmental transitions before the onset of exogenous feeding [22]. Similarly, the oil droplet diameter decreased during the same period, indicating the sustained utilization of lipid reserves. Notably, our findings showed a more rapid depletion of the yolk compared with that of the oil droplets, which is consistent with previous observations in Scyliorhinus torazame [23], demonstrating a delicate balance between growth and activity.

The alterations in gene expression throughout the transition from Bl to 3 dph were complex, and the transcriptome profiles underlying these events have not been thoroughly examined in largemouth bass. In our research, 853.16 Gb of clean reads were obtained from six developmental stages with a Q30 quality score of 96.72%, indicating a high degree of accuracy in these RNA-seq results. The results of correlation analysis and PCA indicated a great homogeneity of samples from the same developmental period. The correlation and principal component analyses revealed a great homogeneity of samples from the same developmental stage, confirming the high reliability and repeatability of the samples obtained at the same time point. Moreover, the qRT-PCR results verified the credibility of the transcriptomic.

Subsequently, the DEGs were characterized in the early developmental stages of largemouth bass. The quantity of single genes rose dramatically in the Bl stage and decreased gradually as the embryo and larvae continued to develop, suggesting that the greatest changes in transcriptome dynamics occurred from the Bl to Ga stage. This result was similarly observed in the Danio rerio, Hippoglossus hippoglossus, Gadus morhua and Oncorhynchus mykiss [24–27]. These studies were primarily associated with the process of maternal to zygotic transition (MZT). During MZT, the embryo’s own genome is gradually formed and activated for gene transcription and protein translation [28]. In zebrafish, MZT occurs mainly from the Bl to Ga stage [29]. Therefore, the specific and highly expressed single genes detected at the Bl stage could be considered as MZT genes. According to the KEGG analyses, the purine metabolism, the spliceosome, ribosome biogenesis in eukaryotes and mRNA surveillance pathways were significantly enriched in the Ga vs. Bl comparison group, and are probably related to zygote genome activation of early development in largemouth bass. Ribosomal proteins are necessary for the assembly of ribosomes, which are sites of protein synthesis, likely requiring the overexpression of ribosomal transcripts. The overexpression of genes in the ribosomal pathway has also been found in zebrafish [29].

Furthermore, we found that the glycolipid metabolic pathway played an important role throughout the early development of largemouth bass. This could be because the largemouth bass embryo depended on its yolk sacs and lipid droplets for the nutrients necessary for its growth and survival during the first three days of development. Lipid droplets are dynamic storage lipid organelles in which lipid droplet triglycerides are degraded via lipase hydrolysis or lipophagic metabolic pathways to produce free fatty acids that are translocated to the mitochondria to provide energy via β-oxidation when cellular energy is lacking [30]. This lipolysis process requires the sequential action of triglyceridases, hormone-sensitive esterases, and monoacylglycerol esterases on triglycerides to release fatty acids. Therefore, multiple lipid metabolism signaling pathways, including glycerolipid metabolism and glycerophospholipid metabolism, participate in this process by regulating esterase activity. In the yolk sack, lipoproteins and a large number of lipid-modifying enzymes transport lipids from the yolk globe to the embryo [31]. In the later stages of hatching, the supply of nutrients (especially energy) from the yolk reaches its limit, and the body is mainly supplied with energy through glycolysis, gluconeogenesis, glycogen synthesis and catabolism [32]. As a result, the glycometabolic pathways, including pentose and glucuronate interconversions, glycolysis/gluconeogenesis and butanoate metabolism were remarkably enriched during the early development of largemouth bass.

In the chemical classification of DEMs, the highest proportion was accounted for by lipids and lipoid molecules. Similar results have been confirmed in the Salmo salar, Acipenser sinensis and Sciaenops ocellatus [33–35]. This could be attributed to lipids serving as crucial energy sources and membrane structural components during early developmental stages, with their metabolic equilibrium being intrinsically linked to embryonic growth and development. In addition, 2,152 DEMs were identified in both ionic modes. Especially, the significant increase in DEMs in the 1 dph vs. Ha comparison group suggested a critical developmental threshold. Such a surge in metabolites would enhance the temporal dynamics of metabolic demand, probably by facilitating the biosynthesis of cellular membranes and providing energy [36]. During this period, the increased activity in both catabolic and anabolic pathways indicated higher energy and biomass needs as the embryo transitions from forming basic structures to more complex organ systems [37]. These metabolites were enriched in starch and sucrose metabolism, cysteine and methionine metabolism, carbohydrate digestion and absorption, pyrimidine metabolism, pentose phosphate and the glycerophospholipid metabolism pathways.

Considering that single-omics data are often insufficient, we combined transcriptomics and metabolomics data in a joint analysis, which led to a deeper systematic insight into the molecular mechanisms at different developmental stages of largemouth bass. Five KEGG pathways, including glyoxylate and dicarboxylate metabolism, the pentose phosphate, pentose and glucuronate interconversions, amino sugar and nucleotide sugar metabolism and glycerophospholipid metabolism pathways were significantly enriched during the studied periods. Among them, the glyoxylate and dicarboxylate metabolism pathway participates in the tricarboxylic acid cycle during energy metabolism [38]. The pentose phosphate pathway is vital for the maintenance of carbon homeostasis, providing precursors for the biosynthesis of monosaccharides, amino acids, and nucleotides, and a quantity of the reducing molecule NADPH in response to oxidative stress [39]. Biosynthetic pathways including gluconeogenesis and transamination are significantly impacted by the pentose and glucuronate interconversion pathway [40]. The amino sugar and nucleotide sugar metabolism pathway is required for the synthesis of oligosaccharides and polysaccharides. They are also vital for the glycosylation of proteins and lipids, as key precursors of glycosylation reactions, which play a crucial part in development [41]. In this research, the glucose metabolism level in largemouth bass increased gradually with embryonic development, suggesting that glucose metabolism provides energy to the embryo, thereby promoting development and growth.

Fish prefer to utilize glycerophospholipids as a source of energy during embryonic development. Glycerophospholipids, which constitute a major component of cellular membranes, are crucial to maintain membrane fluidity and permeability, which are key factors during the rapid cellular expansion and differentiation observed in early developmental stages [42]. Herein, a significant increase in glycerophospholipid levels was observed between the 1 dph and Ha stages. This might have been triggered by the need for the synthesis of large amounts of cell membranes during the exiting membrane process [43]. In our study, metabolites such as lecithin, glycerol-1-phosphate, glycerophosphocholine, ethanolamine, and 1-Acyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphocholine were significantly enriched at different developmental stages. Lecithin and ethanolamine are two of the most abundant phospholipids in mammalian cells, which are resynthesized from choline and ethanol, respectively [44]. Lecithin is employed in the phospholipid structural system and also considered as a dietary supplement that prevents steatosis [45]. A study has demonstrated that lecithin causes human hepatocytes to accumulate more lipids in addition to stimulating adipocyte development [46]. Ethanolamines are a necessary dietary lipid nutrient for animals and are enriched in cell membranes throughout the gut [47]. Intestinal eukaryotic cells may use ethanolamine for the de novo synthesis of phosphatidylethanolamine through the cytidine phosphoethanolamine pathway [47]. Furthermore, the utilization of ethanolamine significantly affected lipid metabolism and short-chain fatty acid biosynthesis. The sn-1 position of glycerol-1-phosphate is esterified by glycerol-1-phosphate acyltransferase and is further catalyzed by lysophosphatidic acid acyltransferase to form phosphatidic acid (Kennedy 1958).

Conclusion

Our results showed that the diameters of both the yolk and oil droplets decreased significantly during embryonic development, indicating a concurrent increase in energy metabolism. The combined transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses preliminarily revealed that the glycerophospholipid metabolism signaling pathway might contribute to increased energy metabolism during the early development stage. Moreover, the high expression levels of the pld, gpcpd and etnk1 genes, which encode regulators of this pathway, were associated with high levels of lecithin, glycerol 1-phosphate, glycerophosphocholine, ethanolamine, and 1-Acyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphocholine. This study partially revealed the molecular mechanisms of glycolipid metabolism during early development of largemouth bass, providing new information about the mechanisms of energy metabolism in the early stages of fish development.

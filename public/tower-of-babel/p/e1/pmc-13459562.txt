Zolbetuximab plus pembrolizumab and chemotherapy in CLDN18.2-positive, HER2-negative, PD-L1-positive gastric or gastroesophageal junction adenocarcinoma: randomized, double-blind, phase III LUCERNA trial
Authors: K. Shitara, P. Enzinger, F. Lordick, E. Smyth, S.Y. Rha, E. Van Cutsem, R.-H. Xu, J.H. Strickler, P. Lee, S.J. Klempner
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
There remains an unmet need for additional treatment options for patients with human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative, locally advanced (LA) unresectable, or metastatic gastric or gastroesophageal junction (mG/GEJ) adenocarcinoma whose tumors are claudin 18 isoform 2 (CLDN18.2)-positive and programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1)-positive. The phase 2 ILUSTRO trial previously demonstrated promising clinical activity with the combination of zolbetuximab plus chemotherapy and checkpoint inhibitor therapy in patients with HER2-negative, LA unresectable, or mG/GEJ adenocarcinoma whose tumors were CLDN18.2-positive, warranting further exploration. In LUCERNA, a multicenter, double-blind, phase III trial, patients with HER2-negative, LA unresectable, or mG/GEJ adenocarcinoma whose tumors are CLDN18.2-positive and PD-L1-positive will be randomly assigned 1:1 to either first-line zolbetuximab plus pembrolizumab and chemotherapy (a capecitabine and oxaliplatin regimen, CAPOX; or a modified folinic acid, fluorouracil, and oxaliplatin regimen, mFOLFOX6) or placebo plus pembrolizumab and chemotherapy. The primary endpoint is overall survival. Key secondary endpoints are progression-free survival and objective response rate. Additional secondary endpoints include duration of response and safety. The results of this trial have the potential to support a new standard-of-care option in this patient population.

Description of protocol

Background and rationale

There remains an unmet need for more efficacious treatment options for patients with human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative, locally advanced (LA) unresectable or metastatic gastric or gastroesophageal junction (mG/GEJ) adenocarcinoma whose tumors are claudin 18 isoform 2 (CLDN18.2)-positive and programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1)-positive. Previous studies have demonstrated progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) benefits with the addition of the anti-CLDN18.2 antibody zolbetuximab to first-line (1L) chemotherapy in patients with HER2-negative, LA unresectable, or mG/GEJ adenocarcinoma whose tumors were CLDN18.2-positive.1, 2, 3 Addition of the anti-programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) antibody pembrolizumab to 1L chemotherapy has improved survival in patients with HER2-negative, LA, or mG/GEJ adenocarcinoma whose tumors had a PD-L1 combined positive score (CPS) ≥1.4 Patients with HER2-negative, LA unresectable, or mG/GEJ adenocarcinoma whose tumors are CLDN18.2-positive and PD-L1-positive are eligible for either zolbetuximab plus chemotherapy or anti-PD-1 antibodies plus chemotherapy as 1L treatment.5,6 It remains unclear how to prioritize these treatments owing to the lack of direct comparisons in clinical trials.6 Biologically, combining zolbetuximab and anti-PD-1 antibodies is complementary: zolbetuximab engages innate immune effector mechanisms through antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity and complement-dependent cytotoxicity, and PD-1 blockade enhances antitumor T-cell-mediated adaptive immune responses.7,8 Combining zolbetuximab and anti-PD-1 antibodies, plus chemotherapy, has the potential to improve upon the outcomes observed with either zolbetuximab plus chemotherapy or anti-PD-1 antibodies plus chemotherapy. In a CLDN18.2-positive mouse model, zolbetuximab plus chemotherapy and PD-1 blockade showed superior tumor growth inhibition versus any doublet combination.9 Tumor samples from patients with LA or mG/GEJ adenocarcinoma treated with zolbetuximab alone or in combination with a modified folinic acid, fluorouracil, and oxaliplatin regimen (mFOLFOX6) demonstrated immune remodeling with increased CD8+ T cell and CD163+ macrophage infiltration.10 In the phase II ILUSTRO trial, combining zolbetuximab plus chemotherapy and checkpoint inhibitor therapy demonstrated a median PFS of 18.0 months [95% confidence interval (CI), 11.1–not estimable) in patients with HER2-negative, LA unresectable, or mG/GEJ adenocarcinoma that had ≥75% of tumor cells demonstrating moderate-to-strong membranous CLDN18 staining.11

Objectives

This study will evaluate the potential clinical benefit of adding zolbetuximab to pembrolizumab and chemotherapy as 1L treatment for patients with HER2-negative, LA unresectable or mG/GEJ adenocarcinoma whose tumors are CLDN18.2-positive and PD-L1-positive (Table 1).

Methods

Eligibility criteria

Briefly, the study will enroll adults (≥18 years of age) with histologically confirmed G/GEJ adenocarcinoma and radiologically confirmed LA unresectable or metastatic disease. Eligible patients will have tumors that are HER2-negative, CLDN18.2-positive, and PD-L1-positive; Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status score of 0 or 1; and adequate organ function. HER2-negative will be defined as a HER2 immunohistochemistry (IHC) score of 0+/1+ or a HER2 IHC score of 2+ in samples that are in situ hybridization-negative per local or central testing. CLDN18.2-positive will be defined as ≥75% of tumor cells demonstrating moderate-to-strong membranous CLDN18 staining using the VENTANA CLDN18 (43-14A) RxDx Assay (Roche Diagnostics, Tucson, AZ, USA) per central testing [except in Japan, where CLDN18.2 will be tested locally as required per local regulations]. PD-L1-positive will be defined as a CPS ≥1 using the Agilent PD-L1 IHC 22C3 pharmDx assay per central testing. Patients who received prior systemic chemotherapy and/or immunotherapy, or CLDN18.2-targeted therapy, will be excluded, except for a maximum of 1 dose of a capecitabine and oxaliplatin regimen (CAPOX) or mFOLFOX6 with or without pembrolizumab. Study design and treatment

LUCERNA is a multicenter, double-blind, phase 3 study. Patients will be randomly assigned 1:1 to either intravenous (i.v.) zolbetuximab plus i.v. pembrolizumab and chemotherapy or placebo plus pembrolizumab and chemotherapy in 42-day cycles (Figure 1). Chemotherapy regimens will consist of CAPOX or mFOLFOX6, both of which reflect globally accepted standards of care. The schedule of zolbetuximab/placebo and pembrolizumab administration will align with that of the chemotherapy regimen chosen by the investigator. Patients will receive (1) zolbetuximab (800 mg/m2 on cycle 1 day 1, followed by 600 mg/m2 every 3 weeks [Q3W]), or placebo, plus pembrolizumab (200 mg Q3W or 400 mg every 6 weeks [Q6W]) and CAPOX (oral capecitabine 1000 mg/m2 twice daily on days 1-14 and 22-35; i.v. oxaliplatin 130 mg/m2 Q3W), or (2) zolbetuximab (800 mg/m2 on cycle 1 day 1, followed by 400 mg/m2 every 2 weeks [Q2W]), or placebo, plus pembrolizumab (400 mg Q6W) and mFOLFOX6 (folinic acid 400 mg/m2; fluorouracil 400 mg/m2 bolus followed by 2400 mg/m2; oxaliplatin 85 mg/m2; all administered i.v. Zolbetuximab is considered a highly emetogenic agent. Prophylactic antiemetics are recommended but not required as premedication prior to each zolbetuximab or placebo treatment. Intravenous antiemetic premedication should be initiated prior to treatment; oral antiemetic premedication should be initiated ≥30 minutes prior to treatment. Recommended antiemetic regimens include, but are not limited to, neurokinin-1 receptor blockers and 5-hydroxytryptamine 3 receptor blockers.

Patients will receive zolbetuximab or placebo, and pembrolizumab, until disease progression per investigator assessment, development of toxicity, or other discontinuation criteria are met; pembrolizumab may be administered up to 24 months. Patients will receive up to 8 treatments of CAPOX, followed by capecitabine per investigator assessment, or up to 12 treatments of mFOLFOX6, followed by folinic acid and fluorouracil per investigator assessment, until discontinuation criteria are met.

Random assignments will be stratified according to region (Asia versus non-Asia) and PD-L1 CPS level (≥1 to <10 versus ≥10).

Assessments

Radiologic evaluation by computed tomography (CT), or magnetic resonance imaging when CT is not feasible, will be carried out at screening (within 28 days prior to random assignment) and every 9 weeks (±7 days) from cycle 1 day 1 through 54 weeks of study treatment, then every 12 weeks (±7 days) thereafter until radiologic disease progression per RECIST v1.1 by investigator assessment. All imaging will also be centrally evaluated. Adverse events will be reported until 90 days following the last dose of study drug. Adverse events will be coded using the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities and graded per the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 5.0. Health-related quality of life (HRQoL) questionnaires will be administered at screening, on cycle 1 day 1, on day 22 (CAPOX) or days 15 and 29 (mFOLFOX6) of cycles 1-4, at study discontinuation, and at the 30-day and 90-day safety follow-up visits. Outcomes

The primary endpoint is OS, defined as the time from when patients are randomly assigned until death from any cause (Table 4). Key secondary endpoints are PFS, defined as the time from when patients are randomly assigned until the earlier of radiologic disease progression per RECIST v1.1 by investigator assessment or death from any cause, and objective response rate (ORR), defined as the proportion of patients with a best overall response of complete response (CR) or partial responses (PRs) per RECIST v1.1 by investigator assessment. Additional secondary endpoints include duration of response (DOR), defined as the time from the first CR or PR until the earlier of radiologic disease progression per RECIST v1.1 by investigator assessment or death from any cause, and safety. Biomarker testing

In addition to biomarker testing to determine patient eligibility for enrollment, and as an exploratory endpoint, additional biomarker testing may be carried out for research purposes as allowed by local policies. This research may evaluate genomic and/or other biomarker expression that may be associated with clinical treatment outcomes, or dynamic changes in biomarker expression associated with study treatment in terms of dose, safety, tolerability, and efficacy. Treatment-related activity of tumor signaling pathways may also be analyzed. Serum and plasma samples may be analyzed for biomarkers including, but not limited to, chemokines, cytokines, activation of complement-dependent cytotoxicity, circulating tumor DNA, and soluble factors. Blood samples may be analyzed for biomarkers including, but not limited to, lymphocyte subsets, genetic markers, and activation of antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity.

Statistical methods

Efficacy will be assessed in the full analysis set, defined as all patients randomly assigned. Safety will be assessed in the safety analysis set, defined as all patients randomly assigned who receive at least 1 dose of study drug. Pharmacokinetics will be assessed in the subset of patients in the safety analysis set for whom at least one set of zolbetuximab concentration data with timing of dosing and sampling are available. Exploratory biomarker expression will be assessed in the subset of the safety analysis set for whom at least one pretreatment and one on-treatment biomarker measurement are available.

A multiple testing procedure will be used to control the overall type I error rate at a one-sided level of 0.025. The null hypotheses of no improvement with zolbetuximab plus pembrolizumab and chemotherapy compared to placebo plus pembrolizumab and chemotherapy will be tested in the hierarchical order: OS, PFS, ORR; testing will stop if a null hypothesis is not rejected. OS and PFS will be compared between treatment arms using a log-rank test stratified by region or CPS; the hazard ratio (HR) and 95% CI will be determined using a Cox proportional hazards model. Median OS and PFS with 95% CIs in each treatment arm will be estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method. ORR will be compared between treatment arms using a Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel test stratified by region and CPS; ORR will be reported with 95% CIs. The distribution of DOR will be estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method and median DOR with 95% CIs will be reported. HRQoL will be summarized by descriptive statistics using the Functional Assessment of Chronic Illness Therapy GP5, Patient-Reported Outcomes Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events, EuroQol 5 Dimension 5 Level, European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer Quality-of-Life Questionnaire (EORTC QLQ)-C30, and EORTC QLQ-OG25 questionnaires, and analyzed by change from baseline over time and inferential methods. Biomarker analyses will be summarized graphically or descriptively.

A futility interim analysis is planned when 84 OS events have been observed. Results will be reviewed by an independent data monitoring committee (IDMC), who may recommend stopping the study if the point estimate of the OS HR is >1.2. An interim efficacy analysis of OS is planned when ≥190 events have been observed. Unblinded results will be reviewed by an IDMC. If OS testing demonstrates statistical significance, no further testing of OS will be carried out; however, data on survival status, safety, and other scheduled procedures will continue to be collected. If the study continues, a final OS analysis is planned when ≥253 OS events have been observed.

The study aims to enroll ∼500 patients. The planned 253 OS events will provide 80% power to detect a difference in OS between treatment arms, with an assumed median OS of 18.6 months with zolbetuximab plus pembrolizumab and chemotherapy and 13 months with placebo plus pembrolizumab and chemotherapy (HR, 0.7; one-sided α, 0.025).

Conclusion

LUCERNA is the first trial to evaluate the efficacy and safety of the addition of zolbetuximab to pembrolizumab and chemotherapy in patients with HER2-negative, LA unresectable, or mG/GEJ adenocarcinoma whose tumors are CLDN18.2-positive and PD-L1-positive. The results of this trial have the potential to support a new standard-of-care option in this patient population.

Win Ratio Analysis to Clarify Clinical Benefits
Authors: Sanjay Ramakrishnan, Nayia Petousi, Jessica Bon, Ian D. Pavord, Surya P. Bhatt, Klaus F. Rabe, Wenying Deng, Changming Xia, Jigna Heble, Mena Soliman, Simon Couillard

ABSTRACT
BackgroundClinical trials often assess multiple end points; however, many do not consider the value of composite end points. Although some evaluations of composite end points consider each outcome to be of equal importance, win ratio analysis ranks outcomes by clinical severity, prioritizing more serious events that have the biggest impact on patients.Research QuestionDoes win ratio analysis, incorporating multiple clinician- and patient-relevant outcomes, show benefits for dupilumab over placebo in patients with COPD and type 2 inflammation?Study Design and MethodsBOREAS and NOTUS (Two Pivotal Studies to Assess the Efficacy, Safety, and Tolerability of Dupilumab in Patients With Moderate-to-Severe COPD With Type 2 Inflammation)—phase 3, randomized, double-masked, placebo-controlled trials—enrolled patients with COPD, moderate-to-severe airflow limitation, and type 2 inflammation (screening blood eosinophil count ≥ 300 cells/μL) receiving inhaled triple or dual therapy (where inhaled corticosteroids were contraindicated). Win ratio analysis evaluated the relative efficacy of dupilumab vs placebo using data from the pooled intention-to-treat safety population. Hierarchical composite end points were occurrence of death, hospitalization, emergency department visits, exacerbations, lung function decline, symptoms, and quality of life. Dupilumab increased the likelihood of improved clinically significant outcomes by 31% (win ratio, 1.31; 95% CI, 1.15-1.48). This analysis provides meaningful insights into the value of composite end points and win ratio analysis that could guide future phase 3 trial designs.Clinical Trial RegistrationClinicalTrials.gov; No.: NCT03930732 and NCT04456673; URL: www.clinicaltrials.gov

Take-Home Points

Research Question: Does win ratio analysis, incorporating multiple clinician- and patient-relevant outcomes, show benefits for dupilumab over placebo in patients with COPD and type 2 inflammation?

Results: This post hoc analysis included 1,872 patients from BOREAS and NOTUS (Two Pivotal Studies to Assess the Efficacy, Safety, and Tolerability of Dupilumab in Patients With Moderate-to-Severe COPD With Type 2 Inflammation). Dupilumab treatment demonstrated a win ratio of 1.31 (95% CI, 1.15-1.48) for the occurrence of clinically significant events vs placebo.

Interpretation: These findings highlight the benefits to patients with COPD and type 2 inflammation, suggesting that dupilumab treatment is associated with better odds of improving clinically significant severe outcomes, including reducing risk of death and hospitalization, while also improving lung function and reducing impact on symptom burden.

COPD is a complex, heterogeneous lung condition marked by persistent respiratory symptoms—such as dyspnea, cough, sputum production, and exacerbations—arising from airway (bronchitis, bronchiolitis) or alveolar (emphysema) abnormalities, or both, leading to progressive airflow limitation.1 As a leading cause of mortality worldwide, COPD accounted for approximately 3.5 million deaths in 2021.2 The heterogeneity of COPD extends to its clinical presentation and disease progression, which vary among patients and over time, posing challenges for diagnosis and treatment strategies.3 Patients with COPD can have coexisting conditions, such as cardiovascular (eg, heart disease) and metabolic (eg, diabetes) disorders, that contribute to increased risk of morbidity and mortality, with systemic inflammation playing a key mechanistic role.4, 5, 6, 7

Dupilumab is a fully human monoclonal antibody8,9 that blocks signaling of the shared receptor component of IL-4 and IL-13, 2 key cytokines central to driving type 2 inflammation in various diseases, including COPD.10, 11, 12 In BOREAS and NOTUS (Two Pivotal Studies to Assess the Efficacy, Safety and Tolerability of Dupilumab in Patients With Moderate-to-Severe COPD With Type 2 Inflammation), dupilumab significantly reduced the rate of moderate or severe exacerbations and improved lung function, symptoms, and quality of life in patients with COPD and type 2 inflammation, defined by a blood eosinophil count of ≥ 300 cells/μL at screening. Safety in these trials was consistent with the known dupilumab safety profile.13, 14, 15 Despite the efficacy of dupilumab, traditional analysis methods often fail to account for the varying clinical importance of different outcomes, except for the primary study end points or prespecified hierarchical testing. A need for more nuanced methods to evaluate treatment benefits with dupilumab remains, particularly when considering the hierarchy of clinical outcomes such as hospitalizations, exacerbations, lung function decline, and impact on symptom burden and quality of life.

In clinical trials that have multiple clinical outcomes that are considered important, challenges in interpretation can exist. One approach to address this has been the use of composite end points combining events of varying clinical importance. Traditional composite end point analyses consider all events equally, which may mask the negative impact of more clinically significant severe outcomes. To address this, win ratio analysis provides a more structured approach by ranking outcomes hierarchically based on clinical significance.16 Initially adopted in cardiovascular trials,17 win ratio analysis is being applied increasingly across various therapeutic areas.18, 19, 20

Compared with conventional time-to-first event analysis or the annualized exacerbation rates used in COPD registration trials, win ratio analysis offers several advantages, including the prioritization of clinically relevant outcomes, improved sensitivity to treatment effects, and a more intuitive interpretation of results, the true treatment benefit to patients beyond primary and key secondary end points.16,21,22 The hierarchical structure ensures that the most clinically significant severe outcomes, such as death or hospitalization, are considered first, providing a clinically relevant assessment of overall treatment benefit. This can be especially beneficial in complex, heterogeneous diseases like COPD, where patients can experience a wide range of events with varying clinical importance. Win ratio analysis for composite end points can help to avoid small differences in a mild end point overshadowing differences in a life-threatening one. Applying the win ratio approach to therapies targeting specific inflammatory pathways, such as dupilumab, provides a structured framework to assess the clinical impact of novel treatments in COPD. Hence, the objective of this post hoc analysis was to evaluate the win ratio across multiple COPD end points, comparing dupilumab with placebo, in patients with COPD and type 2 inflammation from the BOREAS and NOTUS trials.

Study Design and Methods

Trial Design and Population

Phase 3 BOREAS and NOTUS were randomized, double-masked, placebo-controlled trials that enrolled patients (BOREAS, 40-80 years of age; NOTUS, 40-85 years of age) with COPD, moderate-to-severe airflow limitation, and type 2 inflammation (screening blood eosinophil count ≥ 300 cells/μL) receiving stable inhaled triple therapy (inhaled corticosteroid [ICS], long-acting β2-agonist, and long-acting muscarinic antagonist) or dual therapy (where ICS was contraindicated). Patients were randomized 1:1 to subcutaneous add-on dupilumab 300 mg or placebo every 2 weeks for 52 weeks. Further details on the study designs, inclusion criteria, and primary outcomes have been reported elsewhere.13,14

The population included in this analysis consisted of the pooled intention-to-treat populations from BOREAS and NOTUS, including all randomized patients who received at least 1 dose of study treatment (ie, the safety population). A Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials diagram for the overall intention-to-treat population is available (e-Fig 1).

Both trials were conducted in accordance with the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki, the International Conference on Harmonization Good Clinical Practice guidelines, and applicable regulatory requirements. An independent data and safety monitoring committee conducted masked monitoring of patient safety data. The local institutional review board or ethics committee at each study center oversaw trial conduct and documentation. All patients provided written informed consent before participating in the trials.

Trial End Points

In the win ratio analysis, 5 clinical end points were ranked by clinical importance using published guidance16,23 and author consensus, based on clinical experience, to reflect clinician- and patient-centered priorities and the natural progression of COPD-related outcomes: (1) any occurrence of death, hospital admission, or emergency department (ED) visit ≥ 24 hours; (2) any occurrence of moderate exacerbation or ED visit < 24 hours; (3) total number of moderate or severe (hospitalized) exacerbations during the trial period (number of moderate exacerbations plus number of severe exacerbations); (4) worsening of FEV1 after bronchodilation by ≥ 10% predicted from baseline or worsening of FEV1 after bronchodilation by ≥ 100 mL at week 52; and (5) improvement in St. George’s Respiratory Questionnaire score by ≥ 4 points or Evaluating Respiratory Symptoms in COPD score by ≥ 2 points at week 52. The hierarchy was designed first to capture the most severe, life-altering events (ie, death and hospital admission or prolonged ED visits [≥ 24 hours]), acknowledging that these events, although less frequent, are of paramount clinical relevance. The outcomes of death and hospitalization were grouped to mitigate the low-event frequency of all-cause mortality alone and to provide a robust marker of severe disease burden. Subsequent end points included moderate or severe exacerbations, or both; lung function decline; symptoms; and quality of life. Win ratio analysis was applied to compare dupilumab with placebo to assess the treatment effect comprehensively. Each patient receiving dupilumab was compared with a patient receiving placebo, with 3 possible outcomes for each comparison: dupilumab wins, a tie, or placebo wins.

The importance of end points also was evaluated in separate sensitivity analyses for occurrence of events win ratio (win ratio analysis 1, sensitivity analyses 2 and 3): (1) switching the order of the first 2 end points in the hierarchy, (2) reversing the order of all end points in the hierarchy, (3) separating death as an end point, (4) removing the most severe outcome from the hierarchy (death, hospital admission, ED visit ≥ 24 hours), and (5) including severe outcomes only in the hierarchy (death, hospital admission, ED visit ≥ 24 hours, and moderate exacerbation or ED visit < 24 hours).

Statistical Analysis

The unmatched win ratio analysis was conducted in the pooled intention-to-treat safety population, comparing each patient receiving dupilumab with each patient receiving placebo. Therefore, the total number of comparisons conducted for each analysis is equal to the number of patients receiving dupilumab multiplied by the number of patients receiving placebo. For each pairwise comparison, outcomes were evaluated in hierarchical order (e-Fig 2), with the most clinically important events ranked first. Three outcomes were possible for each pairwise comparison: win, tie, or lose. Each comparison is a result of comparing each end point in the hierarchy sequentially. A win was determined whenever the first end point in the hierarchy showed a better outcome. For example, if there was a lower number of exacerbations or, in the time-to-event analysis setting, if both patients experienced any event(s) and showed a longer time to a bad outcome—such as death, first hospital admission, or first ED visit ≥ 24 hours—both were considered to be a win.

Following the methodology described in Pocock et al,16 the win ratio was then calculated as the ratio of the total number of dupilumab wins to the total number of placebo wins. Treatment effects were expressed as win ratios with a corresponding 95% CI, with CI calculations based on previously published methodology.24 A win ratio of > 1 favored dupilumab over placebo. All analyses were performed using SAS version 9.4 software (SAS Institute).

Results

A total of 1,872 patients were included (dupilumab, n = 938; placebo, n = 934). At baseline, the mean (SD) age across the dupilumab and placebo treatment arms was 65.1 (8.2) years, 66.8% of patients were male, and most patients (86.9%) were White. Additionally, 70.2% of patients across both treatment arms previously smoked, 29.8% of patients currently smoked, and 98.4% of patients were receiving triple inhaler therapy at baseline. The mean FEV1 after bronchodilation was 50.3% predicted, the mean St. George’s Respiratory Questionnaire total score was 50.0 points, and the mean Evaluating Respiratory Symptoms in COPD total score was 13.0 points at baseline.

Win Ratio Analysis

For the main analysis, dupilumab showed a significant benefit over placebo, with a win ratio of 1.31 (95% CI, 1.15-1.48; P < .0001) (Figure 1, Figure 2), indicating that patients receiving dupilumab had 31% higher odds of improved outcomes compared with those receiving placebo. The proportion of wins was 46.5% for dupilumab and 35.6% for placebo (Table 2, Fig 1). Discussion

In this analysis, dupilumab consistently reduced the occurrence of clinical events such as hospitalization, exacerbations, lung function decline, symptoms, and quality of life, as well as delayed time to the first occurrence of these clinically relevant events, reinforcing the overall treatment benefit of dupilumab for patients with COPD, moderate-to-severe airflow limitation, and type 2 inflammation. These findings extend the primary results from BOREAS and NOTUS,13, 14, 15 demonstrating a consistent treatment effect across multiple clinically meaningful end points.

In COPD, exacerbation frequency is associated strongly with lung function decline and mortality,25,26 highlighting the importance of reducing or delaying such events in disease management. This analysis allowed the assessment of clinically important outcomes beyond exacerbation reduction alone (ie, lung function, symptoms, and quality of life improvement). Patients with COPD often view daily symptoms as a priority for improvement; thus, an end point that takes that into consideration would be very beneficial.27,28

The win ratio analysis presented here showed a 31% higher likelihood of having improved composite clinical events with dupilumab compared with placebo. In contrast to traditional analyses of composite end points, which combine and treat clinical events equally, this win ratio analysis prioritized clinical outcomes in a hierarchical manner. The consistency of results across the multiple win ratio analyses performed, including variations of the hierarchy, indicates that the findings are robust. The observed reductions in individual outcomes within this ranked testing analysis confirmed that the benefit of dupilumab extends beyond exacerbation prevention to the broader aspects of COPD disease worsening, including hospitalization, lung function decline, impact on symptom burden, and impaired quality of life, including in patients who do not experience more severe outcomes such as exacerbations. These results are consistent with prior findings in type 2 inflammatory diseases, including asthma and chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps, where dupilumab also reduced exacerbations significantly and improved objective measurements such as lung function and nasal polyp parameters, as well as patient-reported outcomes such as symptom burden and quality of life.29, 30, 31, 32, 33

Win ratio analysis has already been adopted widely in cardiovascular trials17 and is used increasingly in respiratory disease research.21,34 The primary advantage of the hierarchical structure of win ratio analysis over traditional composite end point analysis is its ability to provide comparison accounting for the differing clinical importance within a single framework, thus more closely reflecting clinical benefits.16,21,23 The win ratio analysis method compares all possible pairs on an individual level and then summarizes the results of individual comparisons; in contrast, traditional methods summarize data at a population level first and then compare the population summaries. Additionally, the analysis allowed identification of differences between treatments in patients who did not experience clinically significant outcomes such as death, hospitalization, or severe exacerbations. For example, for patients receiving dupilumab or placebo who had tied outcomes for occurrence or time to severe outcomes (eg, exacerbation), the analysis still revealed clear benefits of dupilumab in lung function and symptoms.

Win ratio analysis is generally associated with improved statistical efficiency over traditional composite end point analysis in specific situations; for example, when the primary end point is not met, but clinically meaningful differences in secondary end points are observed.23 Several factors influence when and how win ratio analysis improves statistical efficiency, such as event hierarchy and clinical relevance of the composite end points included, heterogeneity of the treatment effect, distribution of events and ties, patient risk profiles and stratification, correlation between outcomes, and sample size and event rates.18,23,35,36

Additionally, several factors require careful consideration to ensure a clear and easy interpretation of win ratio outcomes and to avoid potential biases.23,37 The interpretation of the win ratio depends heavily on the hierarchy established among outcome components to ensure accurate reflection of clinical priorities.22,37 Win ratio analysis is also highly sensitive to the length of the observation period.22 Other factors include handling of ties and censoring,37,38 correlation between outcome components,22 stratification and adjustments,39 and methodology.22 Therefore, a well-designed trial framework is essential to ensure robust and clinically meaningful conclusions when using this methodology.

Study Limitations

Our analysis remains limited by the end points chosen, which may not necessarily have the same significance to funders, patients, or clinicians, and also may differ within these stakeholder groups. Ideally, composite end points for analysis should be derived from the Delphi method and patient-centered discussion to determine the most clinically relevant measures. Unfortunately, the Delphi method and patient-centered discussion was not available at the time of the analysis. Additionally, each win ratio analysis had up to 6 composite end points, which could have complicated interpretation. However, we conducted several win ratio analyses with slightly different component variations, which provided a transparent assessment of how changes in hierarchy, observation periods, and tie-handling methods can influence results. Therefore, we demonstrate the robustness of the findings in our analyses, enabling proper evaluation of the stability and clinical meaningfulness of the effect of dupilumab vs placebo. Although components within the win ratio hierarchy are likely correlated, this should not impact the final win ratio calculation, because each pair (counted once) for comparison has just 3 outcomes: win, loss, or tie.

Some end points showed a substantial number of ties, particularly higher-priority components in the hierarchy, which may have influenced the results. The question of how to deal with ties is one of the known limitations of win ratio analysis37 and should be considered when interpreting the results of such analyses. Win ratio analysis should be interpreted along with the final overall percentage of ties. In our analysis, we reported the percentage of ties in each of the outputs. Because the win ratio allowed us to incorporate information from multiple variables (compared with a single variable used in conventional analyses, such as exacerbations), it adds additional insight into the treatment effects and allows us to detect important differences between treatments. These differences might not otherwise have been identified by single-variable analyses (eg, 2 patients with the same number of exacerbations, but with varying lung function findings or symptoms) and ensure that ties truly represent patients who show similar outcomes in multiple aspects of the disease that matter most.

Interpretation

In this win ratio analysis, dupilumab showed significant benefits over placebo, indicating an increased likelihood of reducing the occurrence of key clinical events and delaying their first occurrence. Therefore, this analysis provides additional evidence for dupilumab as a treatment option for patients with COPD, moderate-to-severe airflow limitation, and type 2 inflammation, assessing the occurrence and severity of exacerbation events, lung function decline, changes in symptom burden, and effects on quality of life.

Association of Life’s Essential 8 with albuminuria in a Northern Chinese population: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Ziyue Man, Shimeng Zhang, Guilin Hu, Teng Zhang, Jianjun Mu, Yueyuan Liao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Albuminuria is strongly associated with the development and progression of cardiovascular disease. We aimed to investigate the association between Life’s Essential 8 (LE8) and albuminuria. The cross-sectional study enrolled 8,648 adults and excluded individuals with incomplete LE8 index data, missing serum creatinine or albumin/creatinine ratio data, and a diagnosis of chronic kidney disease. Albuminuria was defined as urinary albumin to creatinine ratio (uACR) ≥ 30 mg/g. According to the recommendation of the American Heart Association (AHA), the LE8 score was categorized into three groups: high cardiovascular health (CVH), medium CVH, and low CVH. To evaluate the relationship between LE8 and albuminuria, logistic regression and restricted cubic spline models were employed. Of the 8,648 participants, 949 (11.0%) had albuminuria. After adjusting for potential confounding factors, compared with the low CVH group, the moderate CVH group (odds ratio: 0.552; 95% confidence interval: 0.388 to 0.786; P = 0.001) and high CVH group (OR: 0.253; 95%CI: 0.173 to 0.372; P < 0.001) showed a significant lower odds of albuminuria, and a significant linear negative association between LE8 scores and uACR levels was observed, as well as a non-linear connection between LE8 score and albuminuria. The prevalence of albuminuria was greater among female individuals across all CVH groups compared to their male counterparts. The LE8 scores exhibited a negative correlation with albuminuria levels. Introduction

In 2010, the American Heart Association (AHA) introduced Life’s Simple 7 (LS7)1, an innovative strategy aimed at preventing and improving cardiovascular health (CVH). The concept was revised in 2022 with the introduction of a novel model for CVH, known as Life’s Essential 8 (LE8)2. CVH is appropriately assessed through a scoring system that incorporated four primary health behaviors (diet, physical activity, smoking, and sleep) and four essential health factors (blood pressure, blood glucose, body mass index (BMI), and non-high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (non-HDL-C)). The LE8 scoring system incorporates the maintenance or improvement of CVH as the primary metric for preventing cardiovascular disease, while also demonstrating sensitivity in identifying inter-individual variations. The adoption of this concept also represents a shift in healthcare strategies from the management of cardiovascular disease towards the promotion and preservation of overall health throughout life3,4.

Chronic kidney disease is the 12th leading cause of death worldwide, and measuring albuminuria levels can help predict its progression5. Albuminuria levels are an early sign of impaired kidney function, and albuminuria has been associated with progression to chronic kidney disease, end-stage renal disease, and all-cause mortality6–8. Therefore, the identification of modifiable factors associated with albuminuria is crucial for developing targeted interventions and strategies aimed at not only improving albuminuria but also potentially mitigating the overall burden of CKD. The LE8 scoring system is designed to provide an individualized assessment with a focus on promoting CVH, which is closely linked to CKD and cardiovascular disease (CVD). Albuminuria acts as a crucial biomarker of CKD. Albuminuria acts as a crucial biomarker of CKD. Consequently, investigating the correlation between LE8 and albuminuria is of great significance in the prevention of the progression of CKD.

The objective of this study was to investigate the correlation between LE8 score and albuminuria in the northern Chinese population, highlighting the association of cumulative CVH metrics with albuminuria and exploring potential sex differences. This research aims to investigate the association between CVH (assessed by LE8) and subclinical kidney injury, providing epidemiological evidence to highlight the importance of maintaining optimal CVH.

Methods

Study design and participants

The data for this research was obtained from the “2020 Chinese Residents Cardiovascular Disease and Risk Factor Detection Project” conducted in Shaanxi Province from August 2020 to April 2021. In the initial stage of stratified multi-stage random sampling, Shaanxi Province was classified into urban and rural areas. Subsequently, eight districts/counties were selected (Baota District of Yan’an City, Chencang District of Baoji City, Hanbin District of Ankang City, Hantai District of Hanzhong City, Lintong District of Xi’an City, Qindu District of Xianyang City, Dali County of Weinan City, and Zhenba County of Hanzhong City). In the second to fourth stages, two streets/towns were randomly chosen from the aforementioned eight regions. Subsequently, three residents’ committees/village committees in these streets/towns were randomly selected. The corresponding number of subjects was then randomly picked based on gender and age criteria. This led to a total sample size consisting of 9,336 individuals. In this study, the following inclusion and exclusion criteria were applied: (1) individuals with incomplete data on the LE8 index (n = 633), (2) individuals with missing data on serum creatinine or albumin/creatinine ratio (n = 47), and (3) individuals with a confirmed diagnosis of chronic kidney disease (n = 8). Ultimately, 8,648 participants were included in the study. Figure S1 depicted the flow chart outlining the inclusion and exclusion criteria utilized for the study’s population.

This study protocol received approval from the Ethics Committee of Fuwai Hospital, Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences/National Cardiovascular Center (Approval NO. 2020 − 1360). All procedures complied with the Declaration of Helsinki’s ethical standards, with written informed consent secured from each participant prior to enrollment.

General characteristics and laboratory biochemical indicators

Trained volunteers conducted on-site questionnaires to collect relevant information on demographic characteristics, dietary habits, physical activity, smoking habits, sleep duration, medical history, and medication usage. Participants underwent professional measurements for blood pressure, height, and weight at various intervals during the physical examination; subsequently calculated average values were utilized for data analysis. Blood samples were collected through peripheral venepuncture after fasting, and then centrifuged at 3000×g for 10 min. After that, the blood samples were preserved at -80 °C for further analysis. Subsequent analyses encompassed blood routine, fasting blood glucose, glycated hemoglobin, blood lipid profile (triglycerides, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol, low-density lipoprotein cholesterol, total cholesterol), blood creatinine levels, blood uric acid levels, and blood potassium levels. Additionally, each subject collected a clean midstream urine sample in the morning after fasting and measured urinary creatinine and urinary albuminuria.

Measurement of LE 8

In 2022, the American Heart Association introduced eight essential components to evaluate CVH in populations. These components consist of four health behaviors - diet, physical activity, tobacco exposure, and sleep quality - and four health factors: body mass index, blood lipid levels, blood glucose levels, and blood pressure readings. Each of the eight indicators is given a score from 0 to 100; the overall CVH score is calculated as the weighted average of these individual scores2. The detailed calculation methods are outlined in Table S1. Based on the total score of these eight indicators, an individual’s CVH status is divided into three groups: high (≥ 80), medium (50–79), and low (< 50)9.

Definition

Hypertension was defined as three consecutive mean systolic blood pressure (SBP) of at least 140 mm Hg, and/or mean diastolic blood pressure (DBP) of at least 90 mm Hg, or self-reported past medical history, or use of antihypertensive medications for the past two weeks10. Diabetes was defined as a confirmed hospital diagnosis, current use of hypoglycemic medications, and/or fasting blood glucose levels equal to or exceeding 7.0mmol/L11. Hyperlipidemia was defined as the occurrence of any of the following conditions: Hypertriglyceridemia (triglyceride ≥ 2.26 mmol/L), hypercholesterolemia (total cholesterol ≥ 6.22 mmol/L), high levels of low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C ≥ 4.14 mmol/L) or low levels of high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C < 1.04 mmol/L), or a confirmed diagnosis in a medical facility with current use of lipid-lowering medications12. Drinking was defined as never drinking (consuming alcoholic beverages) and drinking. Educational attainment was categorized into five groups: no formal education, primary school, junior high school, senior high school, and university or higher. Marital status was classified into three groups: single, married, and separated/divorced/widowed. Albuminuria was defined as the urinary albumin concentration divided by creatinine concentration (uACR) of ≥ 30 mg/g, according to the Kidney Disease Outcomes Quality Initiative (KDOQI) 202113,14.

Statistical analysis

Conduct a descriptive statistical analysis based on the LE8 score. All continuous variables that follow a normal distribution are typically represented as mean ± standard deviation, while the non-normal distribution of data is expressed using the median and quartile range (P25-P75). Categorical variable data are presented in terms of frequencies or percentages. The distribution test was performed by Kolmogorov-Smirnov. Continuous variables with normal distribution and non-normal distribution between the two groups were tested by the student’s t-test and Mann-Whitney U test, respectively. For three or more sets of data, the Bonferroni-corrected Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) was used for datasets with normal distributions, while the Kruskal-Wallis test was used for datasets exhibiting non-normal distributions. Multivariable logistic regression analyses were initially employed to evaluate the relationship between CVH (with LE8 score as an ordered variable) and albuminuria. We subsequently implemented the following model adjustments: Model 1 = unadjusted, Model 2 = adjusted for age and sex, and Model 3 = Model 2 with further adjustments for marital status, education level, drink, coronary artery disease, peripheral vascular disease, stroke and estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR). The relationship between LE8 scores and uACR values was estimated using a multiple linear regression model, with confounding variables selected based on previous literature and clinical knowledge. Additionally, we employed restricted cubic spline (RCS) regression to characterize the shape of the association between LE8 scores and albuminuria. Due to the differences between men and women in physiology, hormones, lifestyle, and other aspects, we conducted a further stratified analysis based on gender. To assess the robustness of the relationship between LE8 score and albuminuria, we also conducted a sensitivity analysis by excluding participants who self-reported a history of cardiovascular disease (including angina, congestive heart failure, myocardial infarction, etc.) (n = 115).

We used R and SPSS 25.0 software package (SPSS, Inc., Chicago, Ill., USA) for statistical analysis. In this study, the regression coefficients were presented using the odds ratio (OR) along with its corresponding 95% confidence interval (CI), and a two-tailed P value below the threshold of 0.05 was considered to have achieved statistical significance.

Results

Study population characteristics

In this study, a total of 8,648 participants aged 18 years or older were included. Table 1 presents the basic characteristics of the study subjects based on the presence of albuminuria. The mean (standard deviation [SD]) age of the total study population was 44.7 years (17.1 years), with enrollment consisting of 3,921 (45.3%) men and 4,727 (54.7%) women. The weighted mean (SD) value of the LE8 score was 75.6 (12.1), and the frequency (percentage) of low, medium, and high CVH was 206 (2.4%), 3386 (39.2%), and 5056 (58.5%), respectively. A total of 949 participants (11.0%) exhibited albuminuria. Participants with albuminuria were generally older, more likely to be female, and had lower education levels compared to those without albuminuria. Patients with albuminuria had a higher percentage of hypertension, diabetes, and hyperlipidemia. Regarding LE8 score and its components, participants with albuminuria had significantly lower total LE8 scores, as well as lower sub-scores for physical activity, BMI, blood glucose, blood pressure, and blood lipids.

Association between CVH and albuminuria

The prevalence of albuminuria exhibited a distinct graded inverse relationship with LE8 scoring levels. As illustrated in Fig. 1A, the prevalence was highest in the low CVH group (22.3%), intermediate in the moderate CVH group (14.1%), and lowest in the high CVH group (5.6%). This protective association remained in multivariable logistic regression analyses (Table 2). In the fully adjusted model (Model 3), higher LE8 scores were significantly associated with lower odds of albuminuria. After adjusting for the multivariable logistic regression model, the moderate CVH group (OR: 0.552, P = 0.001) and the high CVH group (OR: 0.253, P < 0.001) demonstrated a significant trend of lower odds of albuminuria compared to the low CVH group. When analyzed as a continuous variable, each 10-point increase in the LE8 score was associated with a 33% reduction in the odds of albuminuria.

Association between LE8 components and albuminuria

We then utilized a fully adjusted multivariate logistic regression model in Table 3 to examine the correlation between each component of LE8 and albuminuria. When comparing participants with ideal scores (80–100) to those with low scores (0–49), distinct patterns were observed across components. Significant inverse associations were identified for BMI, blood glucose, blood pressure, blood lipids, and physical activity. (P < 0.05) Specifically, participants achieving ideal scores in these components exhibited substantially lower odds of albuminuria compared with the low-score group. Notably, ideal blood glucose (OR: 0.271) and blood pressure (OR: 0.277) scores demonstrated the strongest associations. Conversely, higher scores for diet and sleep health were associated with higher odds of albuminuria in this specific population, whereas nicotine exposure scores showed no statistically significant association.

Stratified analyses by gender

We then performed stratified analyses by gender. As shown in Fig. 1B, the prevalence of albuminuria was consistently higher in women than in men across all LE8 categories (Low: 34.0% vs. 18.6%; Moderate: 17.6% vs. 11.0%; High: 6.3% vs. 4.2%).

Table 4 showed the results of analysis of the relationship between CVH and albuminuria by gender stratification. In the fully adjusted model (Model 3), the protective association of higher LE8 scores against albuminuria was observable in both sexes. Among men, a clear graded reduction in risk was observed. Compared to the low LE8 group, both the moderate (OR: 0.508, P = 0.002) and high LE8 groups (OR: 0.256, P < 0.001) exhibited significantly lower odds of albuminuria. Among women, the association for the high LE8 group was similarly robust and significant (OR: 0.250, P < 0.001). However, the association for the moderate LE8 group did not reach statistical significance (OR: 0.554, P = 0.068). Despite the slight variation observed in the moderate group among women, the interaction analysis revealed no statistically significant difference in the association between genders (P for interaction = 0.054). To explore the potential sources of gender differences, we further analyzed the interaction between gender and individual LE8 components (Table S2). Significant interactions were observed in health behavior components (diet, nicotine exposure, physical activity and sleep health), as well as blood pressure (P < 0.05). In contrast, health factor components such as body mass index, blood glucose, and blood lipids did not show statistically significant interactions between genders.

Sensitivity analyses

We further excluded participants with a prior history of cardiovascular disease (n = 115) from the subsequent analysis. A fully adjusted multivariate logistic regression model demonstrated the robustness of the sensitivity analysis results (Table S3). In the general population, the OR values of moderate and high CVH compared with low CVH were 0.651 (0.453–0.937, P = 0.021) and 0.298 (0.201–0.441, P < 0.001), respectively. This consistency was still evident in the sensitivity analysis conducted by gender.

Discussion

This cross-sectional study of a representative sample of adults from Shaanxi, China, found a significant inverse association between higher LE8 scores and the odds of albuminuria. Despite women exhibiting a higher prevalence of albuminuria compared to men, the association between elevated LE8 scores and albuminuria was not found to differ significantly between genders.

The key to addressing CKD is early identification and prevention, and albuminuria is a strong predictor of start and progression15. Monitoring albuminuria levels can help identify those with potential kidney impairment and facilitate timely clinical management. As a result, it is vital to identify people who are susceptible to controllable high albuminuria. Data from the US National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey suggest a non-linear inverse relationship between LE8 and its subscale scores and the prevalence of CKD16. Similarly, our study revealed that maintaining ideal CVH is associated with lower likelihood of albuminuria levels, especially when blood glucose, blood pressure, lipids, BMI, and physical activity are under control. Our results showed that blood glucose, blood pressure, lipids, and BMI were substantially associated with the presence of albuminuria. Previous studies have also demonstrated that higher blood pressure, non-HDL cholesterol, and BMI are substantially associated with an increased likelihood of albuminuria17–20. Moreover, our results revealed a significant inverse correlation between a high exercise rating and the odds of albuminuria as compared to a low exercise rating. This finding provided credence to previous research suggesting that physical exercise might effectively prevent the development of albuminuria21. A foundational study demonstrated that in the context of metabolic syndrome, adipose tissue enhances the release of inflammatory substances, resulting in an inflammatory response surrounding the glomeruli and albuminuria22. Furthermore, physical exercise has been shown to reduce the prevalence of albuminuria by boosting nitric oxide production in the renal vascular endothelium23,24. These biological pathways may explain the inverse association observed between physical activity and albuminuria in our study.

Conversely, our investigation found no significant relationship between diet, smoking, or sleep ratings and the likelihood of albuminuria. This might be due to potential bias in the self-reported questionnaire used to generate the ratings. However, this lack of significance did not preclude the possibility of an association between these variables and the likelihood of albuminuria. A nationwide health and nutrition evaluation survey with a larger sample size revealed a negative correlation between diet score and sleep scores and CKD16. Numerous investigations have consistently demonstrated a significant correlation between albuminuria and cardiovascular disease and total life expectancy25–27. To corroborate the reliability of our findings, we performed a sensitivity analysis, excluding patients who had self-reported cardiovascular illness. The results remained consistent with those previously published. Our findings indicate that maintaining excellent CVH may reduce the prevalence of albuminuria and perhaps reduce the burden of CKD.

The gender discrepancy in the development of CKD is commonly referred to as the “CKD paradox”, in which women have a greater incidence of CKD than men, yet men with CKD proceed more quickly to kidney failure28,29. In our stratified analysis, we observed that while men exhibited a clear graded inverse association across all LE8 categories, the association in women was statistically significant only in the high LE8 score group. However, the test for interaction was not statistically significant (P = 0.054), suggesting that the association between LE8 and albuminuria is generally consistent between sexes. Although the overall sample size was substantial, stratification resulted in small reference groups, particularly among women (n = 50) compared to men (n = 156). This reduced statistical power and widened confidence intervals; thus, the apparent lack of significance in women should be interpreted with caution given the non-significant interaction term. In the further stratified analysis by gender in the LE8 group, we found that, except for blood pressure, most of the health behavior components (body mass index, blood sugar, and blood lipids) did not show significant interaction effects between the two genders. This indicates that there is a consistent physiological connection between metabolic health and albuminuria between the two genders. On the contrary, significant interaction effects were observed in all behavior components (diet, physical activity, nicotine exposure, and sleep health). This suggested that the observed differences may be mainly driven by different social and cultural living habits.

Beyond these health factors, physiological and methodological aspects may also contribute to the observed disparities. Furthermore, the prevalence of albuminuria in our study was higher among the female population, aligning with previous research findings30. This could be attributed to the lower muscle mass and urinary creatinine excretion rates observed in women compared to men, potentially leading to an overestimation of albuminuria in women31. This to some extent explains why the association for women in the medium LE8 group did not reach a significant level. One possible explanation for the observed sex disparities in LE8 scores and albuminuria levels is that albuminuria is overestimated in women, while another could be the potential role of sex hormones on the progression of kidney disease28,32, with estradiol having been shown to protect against the occurrence and progression of kidney disease33. Our data support the applicability of LE8 assessment for renal health monitoring in both men and women.

Our research has several notable advantages. Firstly, it is a study that investigates the interaction between gender and the LE8 score in relation to albuminuria within a representative population of northern Chinese individuals. Furthermore, we employed RCS analysis to visually demonstrate the clear linear negative correlation between LE8 and albuminuria. This indicates that a higher LE8 score can consistently bring renal benefits, and there is no upper limit effect. Finally, the interaction test confirmed that this association is statistically consistent across different genders, which supports the universal applicability of LE8 in the assessment of renal health in the general population. It should be noted that the present study is not without limitations. Firstly, the population under investigation is restricted to a northern cohort, but it also reduces the potential confounding factors caused by race and living environment. Second, although we adjusted for various confounding factors, the cross-sectional nature of this study limits our ability to infer causality. Therefore, further prospective cohort studies and randomized controlled trials are warranted to validate these associations. Third, since the evaluation indicators of health behaviors are realized through questionnaires, it is more likely to be biased. Fourth, uACR only assesses albuminuria through a single measurement, without confirming whether it persists for 3 months. Although this is quite common in large epidemiological studies, it may lead to difficulty in distinguishing between persistent albuminuria and transient albuminuria, thereby potentially causing classification bias. Fifth, data on specific renal-protective medications (such as renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system inhibitors or sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors) are unavailable. Although the usage rate of these drugs in this general population cohort may be relatively low, the influence of residual confounding factors cannot be completely ruled out.

Conclusions

The LE8 scores demonstrated a significant negative correlation with urinary albumin presence in a representative sample of Chinese adults, and the LE8 score was significantly negatively associated with uACR. Among the various components of LE8, priority should be given to blood pressure, lipids, blood glucose, and body mass index. Furthermore, the negative association between the LE8 score and albuminuria was statistically consistent across different genders, indicating that maintaining optimal CVH is generally linked to lower odds of albuminuria in both men and women. These findings suggest that clinical utilization of LE8 may aid in identifying individuals with a higher likelihood of kidney injury among patients and the general population.

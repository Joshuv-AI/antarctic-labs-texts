Changes in healthcare utilization before and after the implementation of a national dementia policy in Korea
Authors: Sewon Park, Selin Woo, Mankyu Choi, Munjae Lee, Mihajlo Jakovljevic
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDementia is one of the most serious health issues in an aging society, placing a significant burden on patients and their families, while also posing considerable socioeconomic challenges at the national level. In response, the Korean government introduced the National Responsibility for Dementia Care policy in 2017 to improve healthcare accessibility for patients with dementia and reduce their financial burden.MethodsThis study utilized the cohort data of patients with dementia collected from Ajou University Hospital between 2012 and 2022. To quantitatively assess the changes in healthcare utilization, we applied panel multivariate negative binomial regression and panel multivariate gamma regression analyses.ResultsThe analysis identified key factors influencing healthcare utilization, including place of residence, presence of comorbidities, age, and duration of dementia. After the policy was implemented, there was a general decline in outpatient visits and medical expenditure, suggesting a positive effect of the policy on alleviating the economic burden on patients with dementia.ConclusionsSignificant changes in healthcare utilization were observed among patients in the early stages of dementia, highlighting the importance of early diagnosis and home-based care services. The findings indicate a need for personalized treatment and the expansion of community-based healthcare services for patients with dementia.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12962-026-00785-2.Keywords: Dementia, Healthcare policy, Healthcare Utilization, Medical expenses, Electronic medical records

Background

Dementia is a major health issue among older adults both in South Korea and worldwide, exacerbated by population aging. The increasing number of patients with dementia has led to rising medical costs and imposed a financial burden on the national health insurance system, while also intensifying the economic and social strains on individuals and families [1–3]. In this context, assessing the cost efficiency of healthcare utilization is essential to ensure the sustainability of the healthcare system. However, previous studies have primarily focused on clinical outcomes or the cost-effectiveness of specific interventions [4, 5], and empirical evidence on the impact of dementia-related policies on actual healthcare utilization and costs remains limited.

In this context, South Korea has supported early detection, prevention, and care for patients with dementia through long-term care insurance and comprehensive dementia management plans. Nevertheless, high out-of-pocket costs have continued to create gaps in access to care [6, 7]. To address these challenges, the government announced the National Responsibility Policy for Dementia Care in September 2017, aiming to assume greater responsibility for the burden and suffering experienced by patients and their families. The main policy measures can be categorized into four areas: nationwide provision of Dementia Relief Centers, easing long-term care insurance eligibility criteria, expansion of dementia-specialized nursing hospitals, and reduction of out-of-pocket payments. Although substantial changes have occurred in the diagnosis and treatment of dementia following its implementation, evaluations of the policy’s cost efficiency and effectiveness remain insufficient. In particular, as healthcare utilization may influence early detection and disease management, it is essential to assess the economic effectiveness of such policies [8–10]. Therefore, this study aims to systematically evaluate the economic effects of the National Responsibility Policy for Dementia Care by analyzing changes in healthcare utilization among patients with dementia before and after its implementation.

Meanwhile, to systematically evaluate policy effectiveness, it is necessary to analyze changes in both the number of healthcare visits and medical costs. Healthcare utilization is a key indicator reflecting disease burden and the efficiency of the healthcare system and can be used to assess policy effects [11]. Medical costs provide a basis for setting policy priorities and allocating resources efficiently, and cost-effective interventions can improve healthcare efficiency while managing expenses [12]. Therefore, a comprehensive analysis of healthcare utilization patterns is required to evaluate and improve dementia care policies. In this study, we analyze the effects of the National Responsibility Policy for Dementia Care by focusing on changes in healthcare visits and medical expenses. Through this approach, we aim to identify shifts in the healthcare system burden and cost structure, thereby providing evidence to support more efficient healthcare policy development.

Materials and methods

Study design and data

This study analyzed changes in healthcare utilization following the implementation of the National Responsibility Policy for Dementia Care using cohort data from Ajou University Hospital. Eleven years of data from 2012 to 2022 were utilized, excluding the implementation year of the National Responsibility Policy for Dementia Care, 2017, from the analysis. To compare before and after policy implementation, the years 2012–2016 were categorized as pre-implementation of the National Responsibility Policy for Dementia Care and 2018–2022 as post-implementation. The study subjects were patients with dementia aged 60 years or older, defined according to the ICD-10 criteria, with primary diagnosis codes of F00–F03, F05.1, G30, G31.0, G31.1, and G31.8.

Variables

The dependent variables were the number of outpatient visits, total medical expenses, out-of-pocket costs, and non-reimbursed expenses, all of which are continuous variables with values greater than or equal to zero. Independent variables included sex, age, place of residence, health insurance type, presence of comorbidities, duration of dementia, and implementation status of the National Responsibility Policy for Dementia Care. Gender was classified as male and female, while age was categorized into six groups: 60–64, 65–69, 70–74, 75–79, 80–84, and 85 or above. Places of residence were classified into major and small cities: major cities included Seoul, Gyeonggi Province, Incheon, Busan, Daegu, Daejeon, Gwangju, and Ulsan, while small cities included Chungcheongnam-do, Chungcheongbuk-do, Gyeongsangnam-do, Gyeongsangbuk-do, Jeollanam-do, Gangwon-do, Jeju Special Self-Governing Province, and Sejong Special Self-Governing City. The health insurance type was classified into National Health Insurance subscribers and Medical Aid beneficiaries; the presence of comorbidities was defined as ‘Yes’ if there was at least one, and ‘No’ if there were none. Dementia duration was categorized into the following groups: 1 year or less (≤ 365 days), over 1 year to 3 years (366–1,095 days), over 3 years to 5 years (1,096–1,825 days), over 5 years to 10 years (1,826–3,650 days), and over 10 years (≥ 3,651 days). The implementation status of the National Responsibility Policy for Dementia Care was divided into pre-implementation (2012–2016) and post-implementation (2018–2022) periods.

Statistical analysis

Data preprocessing was conducted using Excel, and analysis was performed using SPSS 27.0. First, a frequency analysis and descriptive statistics were conducted to identify the general characteristics of patients with dementia. Next, Generalized Estimating Equations (GEE) were utilized to investigate the factors influencing healthcare utilization among patients with dementia and to assess changes in healthcare utilization following the implementation of the National Responsibility Policy for Dementia Care. In terms of analytical methodology, although time-series analysis (e.g., Interrupted Time Series, ITS) is an effective approach for evaluating policy effects by distinguishing between level and trend changes, it is generally applied to aggregated data and faces limitations in adjusting for individual-level covariates. Since this study utilized individual-level panel data that required adjustment for patient-specific factors—including age, comorbidities, and the duration of dementia—we determined that GEE were a more appropriate analytical method. GEE accounts for within-subject correlations while providing robust population-average estimates and allows for the inclusion of time-varying covariates at the individual level [13, 14]. Specifically, a panel multivariate negative binomial regression analysis was conducted to examine the effects on the dependent variable, the number of outpatient visits, while a panel gamma regression analysis was performed to analyze the impacts on total medical expenses, out-of-pocket payments, and non-reimbursed expenses.

Results

Demographic characteristics of study participants

This study included 3,481 patients with dementia aged 60 years or older who received outpatient care at Ajou University Hospital. The sociodemographic characteristics of the study participants were examined based on the year in which they were first diagnosed with dementia (Table 1). There were 1,304 males (37.5%) and 2,177 females (62.5%). The mean age was 76.55 years, with the largest group being 75–79 years at 993 individuals (28.5%), followed by 802 individuals aged 80–84 years (23.0%), 677 aged 70–74 years (19.4%), 412 aged 85 years and above (11.8%), 394 aged 65–69 years (11.3%), and 203 aged 60–64 years (5.8%). The majority of participants resided in major cities, with 3,055 individuals (87.8%), compared with 426 individuals (12.2%) in small cities. Regarding the health insurance type, most participants were National Health Insurance subscribers at 3,350 individuals (96.2%), while Medical Aid beneficiaries accounted for 131 individuals (3.8%). There were 1,821 participants without comorbidities (52.3%) and 1,660 participants with comorbidities (47.7%), showing a similar proportion. The mean duration of dementia was 5.27 years, with the largest group having a duration of 5–10 years, accounting for 1,530 individuals (44.0%), followed by 765 individuals (22.0%) with a duration of 1–3 years, 475 individuals (13.6%) with 3–5 years, 324 individuals (9.3%) with more than 10 years, and 87 individuals (11.1%) with 1 year or less. There was no significant yearly difference in the number of patients first diagnosed with dementia, but 2021 had the highest number at 492 (14.1%). Among the patients, 1,569 individuals (45.1%) were first diagnosed before the implementation of the National Responsibility Policy for Dementia Care, and 1,912 individuals (54.9%) were diagnosed after implementation.

According to the analysis, females had a higher number of outpatient visits than males, and the number of visits increased with age. However, the differences were not statistically significant. In terms of residence, patients living in small cities had fewer outpatient visits compared to those residing in major cities, and this effect was statistically significant (B=-0.056, P = 0.014). Regarding health insurance type, Medical Aid beneficiaries had more outpatient visits than National Health Insurance subscribers; however, this effect was not statistically significant. Patients with comorbidities had significantly more outpatient visits than those without comorbidities (B = 0.031, P = 0.038). The longer the duration of dementia, the greater the number of outpatient visits, with the most significant impact observed in cases where the duration exceeded 10 years (B = 0.203, P < 0.001). The results of the comparison before and after the implementation of the National Responsibility Policy for Dementia Care showed that the number of outpatient visits by dementia patients decreased after policy implementation compared to before policy implementation, which was statistically significant (B=-0.074, P < 0.001) (Supplementary Table S1).

Total medical expenses

Table 3 presents the shifts in total medical expenses following the policy implementation; the specific factors influencing these expenses are detailed in Supplementary Table S2. According to the analysis results, males spent more on total medical expenses than females; however, this difference was not statistically significant. In terms of age, total medical expenses decreased among those aged 85 or older, and this effect was statistically significant (B=-0.256, P = 0.007). In terms of residence, patients living in small cities spent more time on total medical expenses than those living in major cities, although the difference was not statistically significant. Regarding health insurance type, Medical Aid beneficiaries spent less on total medical expenses than National Health Insurance subscribers, which did not’ have a statistically significant effect. Patients with comorbidities spent less time on total medical expenses than did those without comorbidities; however, this effect was not statistically significant. Compared to a dementia duration of 1 year or less, total medical expenses decreased in the order of 3–5 years (B=-0.586, P < 0.001), 1–3 years (B=-0.634, P < 0.001), 5–10 years (B=-0.810, P = 0.001), and over 10 years (B=-1.062, P < 0.001), which had a statistically significant effect. Additionally, the total medical expenses for patients with dementia decreased after the implementation of the National Responsibility Policy for Dementia Care compared to before its implementation, which was not statistically significant (B=-0.279, P = 0.476). (Supplementary Table S2).

Out-of-pocket payments

The primary changes in out-of-pocket payments and the specific variables affecting them are highlighted in Table 3 and Supplementary Table S3, respectively. The analysis showed that out-of-pocket payments were lower for females compared to males, which was not statistically significant. In terms of age, out-of-pocket payments decreased among those aged 85 or older, and this effect was statistically significant (B=-0.356, P = 0.001). In case of residence, out-of-pocket payments were higher for patients living in small cities compared to those residing in major cities, which did not’ have a statistically significant effect [15]. Regarding health insurance type, out-of-pocket payments were significantly lower for Medical Aid beneficiaries than for National Health Insurance subscribers (B=-1.078, P < 0.001). Patients with comorbidities spent less on out-of-pocket payments than patients without comorbidities; however, this difference was not statistically significant. Compared with a dementia duration of 1 year or less, out-of-pocket payments decreased in the order of 3–5 years (B=-0.714, P < 0.001), 1–3 years (B=-0.747, P < 0.001), 5–10 years (B=-1.113, P < 0.001), and > 10 years (B=-1.339, P < 0.001), and this effect was statistically significant. After the implementation of the National Responsibility Policy for Dementia Care, out-of-pocket payments for patients with dementia decreased compared to those before its implementation, and this decrease was statistically significant (B=-0.720, P = 0.012). (Supplementary Table S3).

Non-reimbursement

Regarding non-reimbursed expenses, the major impacts of the policy and their associated factors are shown in Table 3, while the full analytical results are available in Supplementary Table S4. As a result of the analysis, non-reimbursed expenses were lower for females compared to males, and this difference was statistically significant (B=-0.195, P = 0.002). Age groups were 75–79 years old (B=-0.285, P = 0.022), 80–84 years old (B=-0.542, P < 0.001), and 85 years or older (B=-0.608, P < 0.001), showing that non-reimbursed expenses decreased with increasing age, which was statistically significant. In terms of residence, non-reimbursed expenses were higher for patients living in small cities than those residing in major cities; however, the difference was not statistically significant. Regarding health insurance type, non-reimbursed expenses were lower for Medical Aid beneficiaries compared to National Health Insurance subscribers, and this effect was statistically significant (B=-0.316, P = 0.044). Patients with comorbidities spent less on non-reimbursed expenses than did those without comorbidities; however, this difference was not statistically significant. Compared to a dementia duration of 1 year or less, non-reimbursed expenses decreased in the order of 1–3 years (B=-1.089, P < 0.001), 3–5 years (B=-1.282, P < 0.001), 5–10 years (B=-1.574, P < 0.001), and over 10 years (B=-1.672, P < 0.001), showing that non-reimbursed expenses decreased as the dementia duration increased, which had a statistically significant effect. After the implementation of the National Responsibility Policy for Dementia Care, non-reimbursed expenses for dementia patients decreased compared to before its implementation, and this decrease was statistically significant (B=-0.965, P < 0.001). (Supplementary Table S4).

Discussion

Factors influencing healthcare utilization and costs

This study analyzed the factors influencing healthcare utilization among dementia patients and evaluated the policy effects of the National Responsibility Policy for Dementia Care. Our analysis revealed that the number of outpatient visits was significantly higher among residents of metropolitan areas, patients with comorbidities, and those whose dementia duration exceeded three years.

First, our finding that patients in metropolitan areas had higher outpatient utilization is consistent with Aranda et al. (2021) [16] and Waymouth et al. (2023) [17], who reported limited service provision for rural dementia patients. This reinforces the understanding that geographic disparities in healthcare access remain a fundamental driver of imbalance in dementia management. Given the established discourse that professional labor shortages and transportation barriers hinder clinical service delivery in rural sectors [18], these results underscore the urgent need for mobile clinics and digital health-based telemedicine to mitigate the concentration of medical resources in advanced general hospitals. Such policy support is essential to ensure that patients and their families can acquire accurate information and engage in effective self-care regardless of their residential environment.

Furthermore, the observation that patients with comorbidities utilize outpatient services more frequently confirms the findings of Lanctôt et al. (2024) [19] and Huang et al. (2024) [20], who identified high complication rates and subsequent medical demand among dementia populations. This suggests that dementia treatment must evolve beyond single-disease management toward a multidisciplinary collaborative system capable of integrating care for comorbidities such as cardiovascular disease and diabetes. Particularly as dementia patients face communication challenges that increase the risk of suboptimal comorbidities management, strategies for early diagnosis and personalized care plans are vital to reduce unnecessary utilization and alleviate the financial burden on patients [13, 21–23].

Regarding the duration of dementia, the increase in outpatient visits after three years post-diagnosis aligns with previous studies indicating that declines in cognitive function and autonomy over time exacerbate medical demand [24]. Considering that dementia patients exhibit higher outpatient utilization and more concentrated acute care needs in the early stages compared to non-dementia patients [25, 26], our results strengthen the case for delaying disease progression through early detection. Thus, strengthening collaboration with local medical institutions to provide community-based care and lifestyle management programs is necessary to create an environment where patients can receive continuous and effective care within their residences [13].

Finally, the relatively lower medical costs (total medical expenses, out-of-pocket payments, and non-reimbursed expenses) observed among patients aged 85 years and older and those with longer disease duration are consistent with the pattern of an early surge followed by a decline near the end of life, as proposed by Oney et al. (2022) [27]. This reflects a shift in healthcare patterns from aggressive treatment toward care-oriented services as patients approach the end of life [28]. To reduce the overall medical burden, it is necessary to establish an early diagnosis system through national health screenings for high-risk groups and expand cost-effective home nursing services to ensure continuity of professional care within the home.

Policy effects and implications

The reduction in outpatient visits following the implementation of the National Responsibility Policy for Dementia Care is interpreted as a positive spillover effect of expanded care services and the operation of Dementia Care Centers. The provision of full-day home care for severe cases, the introduction of cognitive support grades for mild cases, and the nationwide establishment of Dementia Care Centers appear to have decentralized medical demand into community-based care systems, thereby contributing to the reduction of outpatient visits concentrated in advanced general hospitals. Additionally, improved self-care capabilities and lifestyle modifications through education and awareness programs played a major role in decreasing medical utilization. These findings not only support previous research on shifting utilization patterns following the Long-Term Care Insurance [29] but also suggest that dementia-specific policies are substantially contributing to the efficient allocation of advanced medical resources.

In terms of expenditures, the significant decrease in out-of-pocket payments and non-reimbursed expenses clearly demonstrates the direct achievements of the policy. In particular, the drastic reduction of the medical cost burden rate for severe dementia (from 60% to 10%) and the inclusion of high-cost diagnostics like Seoul Neuropsychological Screening Battery (SNSB) test and MRI under health insurance coverage prove that the policy goal of providing economic protection for patient households has been successfully attained.

However, the lack of a statistically significant decrease in total medical expenses adds a crucial nuance to our findings. This suggests that the policy did not necessarily reduce overall healthcare resource use but rather shifted the financial burden from individuals to the government or public insurers. That is, the core achievement of the National Responsibility Policy for Dementia Care lies in strengthening coverage and the public sharing of economic risks through state-led care, rather than a numerical reduction in total costs. Consequently, considering the impact of these changes on the sustainability of public finances, future policy designs should be refined to maximize fiscal efficiency while maintaining the effects of reduced patient burden.

Limitations

First, this study was conducted using an outpatient cohort from a single tertiary university hospital, which limits its external validity. Healthcare utilization patterns may differ significantly in community hospitals, rural areas, or long-term care settings. Since our data include a patient group with relatively high accessibility to specialized care, we cannot rule out the possibility that the policy’s effects appeared differently than the national average or were biased in a specific direction. Therefore, our findings should be interpreted as exploratory trends observed within a specific institutional context rather than as nationally representative. Nevertheless, given that the data were derived from a major tertiary center in the Gyeonggi province—a region with a large population and high healthcare demand—the results still offer meaningful policy implications. Future research should utilize data encompassing diverse types of medical institutions and geographic regions to ensure more representative analysis.

Second, although this study employed a pre–post design, it did not adequately control for secular trends driven by external factors, such as the COVID-19 pandemic or other concurrent healthcare reforms. In particular, the overall decrease in healthcare utilization during the pandemic may have been confounded with the cost-reduction effects observed in this study. As there is a possibility that the pure effects of the policy and external factors were confounded, this must be taken into account when interpreting the results. Future research should adopt ITS analysis or other quasi-experimental designs to more clearly disentangle the immediate policy impact from long-term temporal trends.

Third, due to data limitations, this study could not account for clinical health status, such as dementia severity or the degree of cognitive decline. Since the stage of dementia is a critical determinant of healthcare expenditures, the inability to control for changes in patient severity may limit the interpretation of whether the observed effects were driven by the policy itself or by changes in the patient composition. Future evaluations should incorporate multidimensional clinical indicators, such as disease progression or preventive outcomes, to provide a more comprehensive assessment of the policy’s economic and therapeutic impacts.

Conclusion

This study analyzed the impact of healthcare utilization and economic effects of the National Responsibility Policy for Dementia Care program using data spanning 10 years for patients with dementia to explore strategies for establishing healthcare policies and enhancing the efficiency of medical expenditures. The analysis revealed that outpatient visit frequencies were higher among urban residents, patients with comorbidities, and those with a dementia duration exceeding three years, suggesting the need to improve healthcare accessibility for dementia patients and provide personalized treatment options. To address this issue, it is necessary to implement continuous disease management through community care utilizing ICT medical devices and digital health. Additionally, medical expenses were relatively lower for older adults aged 85 and above, as well as for patients with a longer duration of dementia. Medical costs peaked in the first year after a dementia diagnosis, continued to increase until the sixth year, and decreased thereafter. Accordingly, measures should be taken to reduce medical expenses by providing early diagnosis and home nursing services to older adults in the early stages of dementia with relatively short durations of illness. Following the implementation of the National Responsibility Policy for Dementia Care program, outpatient visit frequencies, out-of-pocket payments, and non-reimbursed expenses decreased, indicating the program’s effectiveness in alleviating the financial burden on patients with dementia and their families. In the future, it will be essential to provide tailored services for beneficiaries, expand support for home-based care, and improve accessibility. Furthermore, financial arrangements are required so that dementia policies can be implemented continuously and not just as piecemeal support. To this end, strengthening dementia prevention through educational programs and early diagnosis, as well as fostering collaboration between the government and private hospitals in advancing dementia policies, could lead to more efficient utilization of existing budgets. This study holds significance in reviewing the policy effects related to healthcare utilization among patients with dementia, proposing more efficient alternatives. Continued support and efforts are needed to ensure that the results of this study can be applied to actual policies and field settings.

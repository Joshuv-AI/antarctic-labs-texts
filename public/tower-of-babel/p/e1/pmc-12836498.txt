Real-world safety and effectiveness of neoadjuvant chemotherapy combination with pembrolizumab in triple-negative breast cancer
Authors: L Rached, F Peyre-Pradat, M Spotti, C Baldini, A Laparra, O Lambotte, M Sakkal, A Perret, A Viansone, S Michiels, S Delaloge, B Pistilli, JM Ribeiro
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundNeoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC) with the addition of pembrolizumab has become the standard of care for early-stage II-III triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC). The real-world safety of this regimen is critical to assess in this subset of patients treated with curative intent, since many of the immune-related events observed can be long-lasting or irreversible.Patients and methodsWe retrospectively analyzed the medical records for the initial 100 patients with early-stage TNBC treated with NAC and pembrolizumab at a single comprehensive cancer center between April 2022 and April 2023. We used descriptive analyses to assess treatment exposure, real-world safety and effectiveness of this combination. Treatment-related toxicities were reported according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events v5.0. Follow-up extended until the end of the adjuvant phase.ResultsThe median age of the patients was 52 years, and 21% were identified as germline pathogenic BRCA1/2 alteration carriers. Treatment discontinuation rate due to adverse events (AEs) in the neoadjuvant phase was 35%. Half of the patients (50%) required dose reductions of at least one chemotherapy drug. The total rate of pathological complete response/residual cancer burden 0 was 58%. A total of 61% experienced at least one immune-related AE (irAE), 30% of which were grade 3-5. We documented one grade 5 toxicity following immune-related myocarditis.ConclusionIn this real-life cohort, treatment discontinuation was frequent and linked to treatment toxicity of either chemotherapy or pembrolizumab. We report a higher rate of all grade and grade ≥3 irAEs as compared to the rates documented in the pivotal KEYNOTE 522 trial. The effectiveness of neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy for the treatment of stage II-III TNBC was similar to that reported in the literature.Key words: triple-negative breast cancer, immunotherapy, real-world data, immune-related adverse events

Background

Neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC) with the addition of pembrolizumab has become the current standard of care for early-stage II-III triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC). Although immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) were already adopted for the adjuvant treatment of resected melanoma,1 this was the first approval for chemotherapy with an ICI in an early-stage cancer. The complex interplay between chemotherapy and immunotherapy is not yet fully explored. Several mechanisms of action are elucidated such as immunogenic cell death, tumor load reduction,2 depletion of immunosuppressive cells,3 epigenetic modulation4 and increasing tumor antigenicity.5

TNBC is classified as the most immunogenic subtype of breast cancer (BC) as a result of high levels of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs), tumor mutational burden and programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) expression, conferring a rationale for immunotherapy.6 Immune cell composition in the tumor microenvironment (TME) is mainly represented by T lymphocytes, B lymphocytes, neutrophils, macrophages, monocytes, natural killer (NK) cells, dendritic cells, mast cells and other less frequent cells.7 During tumor development, cancer cells may induce CD8+ TILs to a dysfunctional state with loss of cytotoxic activity and increased expression of co-inhibitory molecules known as exhaustion.8 In TNBC, high density of TILs correlated with statistically significant improved overall survival (OS), disease-free survival and pathological complete response (pCR) after NAC and chemo-immunotherapy.9, 10, 11 Infiltration by regulatory T cells was associated with a disrupted antitumor immune response and a hampered response to anti-programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) immunotherapy.12 Tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) participate in tumor progression and invasion and promote dysfunction of T cells and NK cells. In TNBC, high infiltration of M2-like TAMs correlated with decreased OS.13 Additionally, a high density of macrophages was reported in non-responder patients following neoadjuvant treatment.14

High-risk early-stage TNBC is often associated with early recurrence and high mortality despite therapeutic progress in past years. Patients treated with NAC who achieve pCR present a longer event-free survival (EFS) and OS.15,16 These patients represent around 50% of the total early-stage TNBC population; the other half however exhibits an unmet need for novel therapeutic options. The addition of pembrolizumab to standard NAC resulted in higher rates of both pCR and EFS [84.5% (81.7%-86.9%) at 36 months] as compared to chemotherapy alone.17 Effectiveness was consistent throughout the trials with a pCR rate of 60% in KEYNOTE 17318 and 64% in KEYNOTE 522.19 The safety profile was also aligned with 30% of patients presenting immune-related adverse events (irAEs) reported also as ‘AEs of special interest’ and 27% treatment discontinuation rates in both trials. Durvalumab, an anti-PD-L1, was also tested in early-stage TNBC resulting in a 53% pCR rate in the GeparNuevo11 study, as well as a 22% treatment discontinuation rate. A meta-analysis of long-term outcomes in these patients confirmed a sustained benefit in EFS and OS at 4 years in favor of the chemo-immunotherapy approach.20 However, safety, especially the occurrence of irAEs, remains a major issue to consider, since many of these events present with a high morbidity burden, in a population of mostly young patients treated with curative intent. This regimen has a narrow therapeutic index, which deserves careful follow-up in the real world. While randomized clinical trials remain the absolute standard for the demonstration of a treatment’s objective benefits and harms, the extrapolation of their results to real-world practice may indeed be difficult due to less stringent inclusion criteria, different population characteristics and less controlled environmental factors. The KEYNOTE 522 regimen implementation in the real world is a real challenge. We therefore aim to carefully assess its safety and effectiveness in a real-world setting using a stringent methodology.

Patients and methods

In this single-center retrospective cohort study, we reviewed the electronic health records for the first 100 patients with early-stage TNBC treated with NAC and pembrolizumab according to the schema of KEYNOTE 522 trial, between April 2022 and April 2023 in Gustave Roussy Cancer Center. Early-stage BC was defined as stage II-III according to the eighth edition of the American Joint Committee on Cancer staging system. Patients who presented with a localized relapse of a previously treated TNBC and an indication for neoadjuvant systemic treatment comprising immunotherapy were also considered eligible. The primary objective was to determine the rate of irAEs among this cohort of patients. The study population for the safety analysis included all patients who received at least one dose of the intended treatment. AEs were classified into chemotherapy-related, immunotherapy-related or potentially related to both treatments as interpreted by the treating oncologist in collaboration with the Gustave Roussy expert panel of immune toxicity. irAEs were documented using biologic, radiologic and/or pathologic confirmation, expert opinion or discussion of the case in the multidisciplinary immune toxicity tumor board. AEs attributed to treatment in general without discriminating the culprit drug were registered as related to both drugs.

Secondary objectives included pCR rate in the intent-to-treat (ITT) population (all eligible patients who entered this retrospective cohort, including those who had a progression before surgery and were considered as non-pCR), treatment discontinuation rates and etiologies (cancer progression, AEs, patient’s choice or death). pCR was defined according to the Food and Drug Administration as the absence of residual invasive cancer in the complete resected breast and all sampled regional lymph nodes.21 This retrospective observational study was approved by Gustave Roussy institutional review board with number 2024-310. The database of this study was approved by the data protection officer and declared to the national data protection policy (CNIL). No informed consent was required from patients. All patients were informed of their access and data protection rights. Patients were eligible for the present study if they initiated the approved treatment regimen outside of a clinical trial, as part of the neoadjuvant treatment of a stage II or III TNBC. TNBC was defined as estrogen receptor (ER) <10%, progesterone receptor <10% and absence of human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 amplification (by immunohistochemistry or in situ hybridization) following national guidelines. TILs and Ki67 data were not available for all patients depending on the original biopsy center. Radiographic staging was required for all patients and included 2-fluoro-2-deoxy-d-glucose positron emission tomography–computed tomography. Central reading of local biopsies was not required; however, a genetic consult to assess germline pathogenic variant status using a predefined panel was offered for all patients.

Only one patient declined inclusion of her data in this cohort, she was therefore replaced with the following consecutive patient on the list. Patients were included if they had received at least one dose of chemotherapy and pembrolizumab. Patients with incomplete medical records or patients who had a follow-up in another institution were excluded (n = 3) and replaced by the following patient. Medical charts were individually reviewed; AEs were recorded and graded according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events v5.0. Data cut-off date was 30 December 2023. All patients’ charts were individually reviewed by two medical oncologists (LR and MS); data extracted included variables such as patients’ demographics, personal and family history, tumor characteristics, chemotherapy doses and duration of treatment, type of adjuvant treatment, treatment response and biological parameters upon treatment initiation as well as AEs and their respective management strategies. Treatment was considered complete if patients received at least 75% of the planned neoadjuvant treatment before surgery.

Imputability for treatment discontinuation was reported as recorded by the oncologist in the medical chart of each patient. Data were analyzed using SAS software version 9.2 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC). In order to uncover the relationship between variables, a multivariate analysis was carried out using logistic regression with statistical significance defined by a P value <0.05.

Results

Patients and disease characteristics

Our cohort comprised 100 patients diagnosed with stage II or III TNBC. All patients were female, median age was 52 years (range 20-79 years) and 52% of them were premenopausal at diagnosis. Genetic consult was offered to all patients and 21.7% were prospectively identified as carriers of deleterious BRCA germline pathogenic variant, among which 17.4% were BRCA1 and 4.3% BRCA2. One patient harbored a PALB2 germline pathogenic variant, and one patient had an MSH2 germline pathogenic variant as part of Lynch syndrome. A total of 79% of the patients had high-grade tumors (grade 3), 64% had stage II and 34% had stage III tumors. Median TIL (n = 70) was 15% (10%-30%). Median Ki67 (n = 65) was 70% (50%-80%). A personal history of autoimmune disease was present in seven patients and consisted of Hashimoto thyroiditis, systemic lupus erythematosus, psoriasis, rheumatoid purpura and Sjögren syndrome. A family history of autoimmune pathologies was present in nine patients and consisted of autoimmune hepatitis, rheumatoid polyarthritis, ankylosing spondylitis, rheumatoid arthritis and Sjögren syndrome. For the present study, patients were followed from the date of diagnosis until the last administration of pembrolizumab in the adjuvant setting.

Treatment protocol

The standard NAC regimen was paclitaxel 80 mg/m2 and carboplatin area under the curve 1.5 administered weekly for 12 weeks, followed by epirubicin 90 mg/m2 and cyclophosphamide 600 mg/m2 administered every 3 weeks for a total of four cycles, combined with pembrolizumab 200 mg every 3 weeks. Ten patients were not eligible to receive epirubicin as they were previously treated with anthracyclines. These patients were treated with an anthracycline-free regimen, consisting of paclitaxel–carboplatin weekly for six cycles with pembrolizumab. Granulocyte colony-stimulating factors were mandatory during the epirubicin–cyclophosphamide period but not with paclitaxel–carboplatin. Steroids as premedication were permitted during the neoadjuvant phase. Half of the patient population (50%) required dose reductions of at least one chemotherapy drug during the neoadjuvant part of the treatment, while 49% of patients required at least one dose omission or more. Only 64% of the patients completed the planned neoadjuvant protocol.

Treatment discontinuation rate for AEs was 35%. These events included liver toxicity and recurrent severe hematological and severe skin toxicities. The median duration of neoadjuvant treatment among patients was 149 days (range 27-216 days) (versus 168 days expected). The median time from the end of treatment until surgery was 22 days (range 10-64 days). Table 2 describes dose modifications required during the neoadjuvant part of the treatment.

Safety

AEs recorded for each patient included grade, time of occurrence (from day 1 of treatment) and drug imputability. As per institutional guidelines, patients were monitored for thyroid, pituitary function tests as well as cortisol and cardiac enzymes before every immunotherapy injection to ensure an early detection of potential toxicities.

Almost all patients (99%) experienced treatment-related AEs of any grade. Table 3 documents all AEs by grade, with the highest grade recorded for each patient. The most frequent AE reported was fatigue in 68% of patients followed by nausea in 63%. Hematological AEs were mostly related to chemotherapy with neutropenia in 59% of patients and anemia in 58%. The most frequent grade 3-4 TRAE was neutropenia (52%) followed by anemia (22%). Rash occurred in 30% of patients and was attributed to chemotherapy in a third of cases, to immunotherapy in a third of cases and to both treatments in the rest. Paclitaxel-induced colitis occurred in 10 patients and was grade 3 in 1 patient.

A total of 61 subjects experienced ≥1 irAE during their treatment, while 31% of patients experienced ≥2 irAEs. High-grade (grade 3-5) irAEs occurred in 30% of patients. The median date of occurrence for irAE is illustrated in Figure 1. Thyroid dysfunction occurring on treatment was labeled as immune mediated even in the absence of further investigation. Thyroid dysfunction was the most common irAE occurring in 29% of the patients, followed by immune-mediated hepatitis and rash both in 10% of patients. As for high-grade irAEs, grade 3-4 hepatitis occurred in 8%, followed by rash in 6% of patients that led to treatment discontinuation. Immune-mediated colitis was present in 8% of the patients but was grade 3 only in one patient. Endocrine immune-mediated toxicities were of special interest as they are often irreversible.22 We witnessed in our cohort six cases of adrenal insufficiency, five of them occurring in the neoadjuvant setting while only one occurred in the adjuvant phase of pembrolizumab. Hypophysitis induced by pembrolizumab was also recorded in eight patients requiring continuous hormonal supplementation.

Immune-related myocarditis documented by cardiac imaging was witnessed in four patients and led to death in an elderly patient with a dilated cardiomyopathy at baseline. Furthermore, troponin increase above normal values was witnessed in 17 patients without any evidence of myocarditis. Immune-related sarcoidosis was documented in two patients following radiological diagnosis and pathological confirmation; however, both patients were asymptomatic and did not require any specific treatment or pembrolizumab interruption. Patients with a personal history of autoimmune disease present a high risk of exacerbating their underlying pathology. They are also prone to develop another irAE during treatment (four out of seven patients); however, the number in our cohort was very limited and therefore does not allow for significant conclusions. Immune-mediated uveitis, thrombocytopenic purpura and sicca syndrome were among the rheumatologic AEs observed in our cohort. Two patients presented with grade 4 diabetic ketoacidosis requiring intensive care hospitalization during the neoadjuvant phase; both patients were not diabetic before treatment initiation and presented with positive glutamic acid decarboxylase antibodies pleading in favor of an immune etiology of diabetes.

The majority of irAEs were reported during the neoadjuvant phase of treatment (accounting for 66% of irAEs) (Figure 2). However, a significant number of irAEs still occurred during the adjuvant phase of treatment, accounting for 34% of all irAEs. These data highlight the critical need to assess the benefit of adjuvant pembrolizumab, especially for patients who exhibit favorable outcomes, such as those achieving a pCR. Trials exploring this de-escalation approach are ongoing and will be key to the development of customized therapeutic strategies that improve patient safety while maintaining treatment effectiveness.

Effectiveness

Effectiveness analysis was conducted based on the ITT population. All patients proceeded to surgery except for two patients with on-treatment progression. These patients required modification of systemic therapy, without surgery, and were considered as non-PCR. The rate of pCR/residual cancer burden (RCB) 0 was 58%. When assessable (n = 93), RCB was distributed accordingly: class I in 11% of patients, class II in 21% of patients and class III in 2% of patients. When examining the population with BRCA germline mutations (n = 20), we observed a pCR rate of 80% (n = 16); however, due to the limited number of patients, no specific conclusions can be made. dNLR and TILs were significantly elevated in the subgroup of patients who achieved pCR, compared to those with residual disease (P = 0.04 and P < 0.0055 respectively) (Supplementary Figures S1 and S2). Adjuvant radiotherapy was carried out in 87.7% of the patients as per international guidelines.

The number of patients to pursue immunotherapy in the adjuvant setting as indicated by the KEYNOTE 522 regimen was 75, 2 of them received pembrolizumab maintenance followed by olaparib in the context of a BRCA mutation, and 4 patients received pembrolizumab with capecitabine. Patients who presented with high-grade irAEs in the neoadjuvant setting received capecitabine as an adjuvant treatment if they did not achieve pCR (5 patients) or no systemic therapy for 10 patients who achieved pCR. Of note, six patients with early treatment interruption due to AEs (during the carboplatin–paclitaxel cycles) who did not achieve pCR resumed their initially planned chemotherapy (epirubicin–cyclophosphamide) following surgery in the adjuvant setting without pembrolizumab following discussion in the breast multidisciplinary tumor board. By cut-off date (30 December 2023), survival data remained immature, however; eight patients presented with systemic recurrences. Three of them had previously achieved pCR; three had an RCB class II, while the rest progressed on treatment.

We report also until data cut-off three deaths in our cohort, two of them due to disease progression and one resulting from a grade 5 myocarditis in a 72-year-old patient. In the multivariate analysis, effectiveness was significantly correlated with tumor grade and derived neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (dNLR) at baseline, whereas toxicity was not correlated with any additional factors (Figure 3). A higher expression of TILs was significantly correlated with pCR in the univariate analysis; however, due to the high number of missing data, this variable was not included in the multivariate analysis.

Discussion

TNBC is the most aggressive histology in BC, and treatment options in the recurrent or metastatic setting are still limited. The addition of ICI to NAC resulted in an improved pCR rate and EFS rate, and a recent announcement of an improved OS becoming the standard of care for this patient population.23 Nevertheless, the incorporation of ICIs, while beneficial, introduced new challenges, particularly concerning the emergence of irAEs. These challenges remain particularly critical when considering their idiosyncratic nature and potential long-term impact, in a predominantly young patient population undergoing treatment with curative intent, thereby risking considerable morbidity.

We report in our cohort a higher rate of irAEs as compared to KEYNOTE 522 (61% versus 39%), most of which occurred during the neoadjuvant phase. We also describe a higher rate of grade 3-5 irAEs (30%) as compared to the rate of irAEs in the literature (10% with anti-PD-124,25). Reasons behind these different outcomes remain unclear; however, it is plausible to speculate that in the less controlled environment of routine practice, as opposed to the highly regulated setting of a clinical trial (highly selected patients with few comorbidities), severe AEs may manifest across a broader patient population. Another hypothesis could be that our patients were screened for irAEs before every ICI injection, leading to an early detection. Of note, these patients required prolonged treatments with steroids and sometimes immune suppressors with all the complications that can derive from such therapies. Therefore, long-term follow-up quality of life (QoL) assessments are imperative for this patient group. Longitudinal studies, such as the CANTO cohort (NCT01993498),26 will provide invaluable insights into the long-term effects and outcomes of these treatments, enabling better management of AEs and improvement in patient care.

A recent abstract presented at American Society of Clinical Oncology (ASCO) 2024 compared real-world data with chemo–ICI combinations versus dose-dense NAC regimen resulting in a higher pCR rate despite a higher treatment discontinuation rate in the pembrolizumab group.27 Another French cohort consisting of 51 patients presented a 29.4% rate of irAEs.28 These findings were aligned with those reported by the MD Anderson Cancer Center cohort with 34% of patients presenting irAEs, and 26 cases of grade ≥3 irAEs.29 Our findings are consistent with the data reported by Hofherr et al. during the San Antonio Breast Cancer Symposium (SABCS) 2024 with a 39% early treatment discontinuation rate (228/577), with 71.4% of patients experiencing an irAE with 31.9% grade ≥3.30 Similar findings were reported by Marhold et al. with a 57.1% pCR rate and 63.9% of patients presenting irAEs (20% grade ≥3); however, they described a significant correlation between the emergence of irAEs and pCR31 (72.2% versus 30.8%; P = 0.03), and this correlation was not found in our cohort.

Our study presents several limitations, including its retrospective design, the limited number of participants and the fact that the cohort originates from a single center which may introduce biases or limit the generalizability of findings. Another limitation is the short duration of follow-up and the lack of QoL data. Nevertheless, the strengths of our study lie in the comprehensive biological work-up conducted, along with the multidisciplinary evaluation carried out by a dedicated breast unit team. This team collaborates closely with an immunology-focused toxicity panel, which convenes weekly to deliberate on the imputability of various AEs and to devise appropriate management strategies.

In the scope of AEs secondary to the chemo-immunotherapy regimen, it is important to note that, even in a highly specialized center equipped with a dedicated immune-toxicity team, establishing causality or identifying the specific drug responsible for overlapping toxicities can be challenging. In our clinical practice, especially under these circumstances, we adopt a strategy of reintroducing the agents sequentially, aiming to pinpoint the culprit drug.32 This approach underscores the complexity of managing adverse reactions in patients receiving combined treatment modalities. It also reflects the current gap in our understanding of biomarkers associated with irAEs. The PREMIS trial (NCT03984318) aims to address these challenges by prospectively collecting biological specimens of patients upon the initiation of ICI-based therapies.

We report a pCR rate that aligns closely with what has been documented in the pivotal registration trial. However, it is important to highlight that our cohort includes patients with different patient characteristics, with a high risk profile stage IIIB/C disease, and patients with local relapses. Another technical difference remains the definition of TNBC in our population involving ER-low (1%-10%) patients, as per European guidelines, unlike the ASCO [College of American Pathologists (CAP)] guidelines with an ER cut-off <1%.33

One major effort is now directed toward the identification of early-stage TNBC patients who can benefit from ICI addition and optimization of treatment schedule. The biomarker-driven approach did not prove itself beneficial in early-stage TNBC, although it is the method of choice in other types of cancers. PD-L1 in early TNBC is considered prognostic but not predictive with higher pCR rates in PD-L1+ tumors across KEYNOTE 522 but also GeparNuevo and IMPASSION031 regardless of the treatment arm (with or without ICI).34 In our cohort, the evaluation of biomarkers was limited by the small sample size. Nonetheless, it is worth highlighting that a significantly higher baseline infiltration of TILs was more correlated with pCR. Furthermore, our univariate analysis identified baseline TIL infiltration as an independently associated factor with an increased probability of pCR. These observations align with recent findings, reinforcing the notion that patients with immunogenically active tumors generally exhibit a better prognosis27 and more favorable responses to systemic treatments. We anticipate that genomic signatures, TME analyses and spatial distribution of immune cells might present promising alternatives for better patient selection in the future. Our analysis revealed a significant statistical association, in both univariate and multivariate analyses, between a higher dNLR and pCR. This finding is not currently supported by existing data on the response to NAC in early BC, or with prognosis in various cancer types across both early and metastatic stages. Nonetheless, it is important to acknowledge the scarcity of data in the early cancer setting for patients undergoing chemo-immunotherapy.

We recognize that our small sample size requires caution, as our findings necessitate further validation in larger cohorts. However, recent studies suggest the existence of a subset of antitumoral neutrophils,35,36 It is crucial to note that the understanding of neutrophils’ role in response to immunotherapy is still in its early stages. Prospective studies to confirm these preliminary, hypothesis-generating findings are essential.

Conclusion

In this real-life cohort, the effectiveness of neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy in TNBC was similar to that reported in the literature. Nevertheless, we witnessed a significant treatment discontinuation rate due to AEs. The impact of treatment delays, dose omissions and early treatment interruptions on patients’ outcomes requires further investigation. irAEs present a serious cause of morbidity for this young patient population treated in a curative setting. Long-term toxicities have yet to be assessed. We highlight the crucial importance of a close follow-up for these patients throughout the entire duration of the treatment with extensive work-up to ensure an early detection of potentially serious toxicities and the importance of collaborating with immune-toxicity teams. The QoL for this population should be carefully evaluated, especially regarding permanent toxicities. The development of biomarkers, for both efficacy and toxicity, is crucial in advancing personalized treatment strategies. The understanding of the TME, along with bioinformatics and artificial intelligence, to integrate these features with clinical data looking at the tumor and the host characteristics constitutes an encouraging process to unlock the development of truly novel and more accurate biomarkers to improve patient selection and clinical outcomes in this population.

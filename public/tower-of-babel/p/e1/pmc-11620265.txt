A New model of gas-water two-phase seepage based on Newton's law of motion
Authors: Jianzhong Zhang, Shusheng Gao, Wei Xiong, Liyou Ye, Huaxun Liu, Wenqing Zhu, Weiguo An, Donghuan Han, Baicen Lin
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The pore throat size, structure distribution, and lithology of porous media in gas reservoirs are varied, and the gas-water two-phase seepage law is complex, making it difficult to describe the seepage model. Newton's law of motion is a basic law of motion in classical mechanics, and its application in gas-water two-phase seepage modeling is an innovative practice of classical mechanics in seepage mechanics systems. Based on Newton's three laws of motion, a gas-water two-phase seepage model was established from the force analysis of fluid, and the relationship between velocity and pressure difference, pipe radius, water film thickness, viscosity, etc. was derived under the condition that the model reached a stable state, i.e., force balance. The relationship is referred to as the steady-state model. Subsequently. the equivalent permeability representation model was derived based on the steady-state model to calculate the permeability of rock samples with different channel distribution characteristics. The results were consistent with the measured values, and there is a good correspondence between channel distribution characteristics and permeability. The relative permeability calculation method was established based on the steady-state model and capillary pressure curve. The obtained relative permeability curve aligned with the two-phase seepage law and coincides with the relative permeability curve obtained by Poiseuille's law. Finally, a new productivity equation was derived based on the steady-state model, and the Inflow Performance Relationship (IPR) curve calculated by the gas well example was consistent with the traditional equation. The research results were based on the force analysis of fluid in porous media and fundamentally explored the law of fluid flow. The derived seepage model can be used to calculate reservoir permeability, the gas-water relative permeability curve, and gas well productivity analysis and to effectively guide gas reservoir development. The study was a successful application of the basic theory of mechanics in gas-water two-phase seepage in gas reservoirs.

Introduction

The formation conditions of gas reservoirs are complex, and the pore throat structure is variable [1,2]. The gas-water two-phase seepage law is more difficult to define, especially in the presence of water. Over the years, research on the theory of gas-water two-phase flow [[3], [4], [5], [6], [7], [8], [9], [10]] and nanopore flow [[11], [12], [13]] has been ongoing. Xu D. N [3]. developed a complete mathematical model of gas-water two-phase thermal-fluid-solid multi-field coupling, taking into account the adsorption/desorption effect, pressure and temperature changes on gas extraction, and the presence of the water phase in the reservoir. Zhu W. Y. et al. [4] obtained the motion equation of gas-liquid two-phase seepage with phase transition process from the mass conservation and momentum conservation equations by applying the ideal porous medium model and the local averaging method. Zheng Sun et al. [11] first used molecular dynamics (MD) to simulate the methane flow behavior through actual kerogen-based circular nanopores. They also developed a novel construction method to generate kerogen-based organic nanopores with desirable pore sizes and different kerogen types, suitable for MD simulation.

The studies on gas-water two-phase seepage models are based on the generalized two-phase Darcy seepage theory system popularized by Muskat, Buckley, and Leverett et al. [[14], [15], [16]]. Darcy's law, on the other hand, is \based primarily on a summary of macroscopic phenomena and does not reflect the mechanism of gas-water two-phase seepage. Therefore, seeking a new gas-water two-phase seepage model and explaining its characteristics through deeper mechanical principles is crucial for efficient gas reservoir development. Newton's laws of motion, as the fundamental laws of motion in classical mechanics, explain the complete system of Newton's mechanics and are widely used in diverse fields [17,18]. However, using these laws in the establishment of a gas-water two-phase seepage model is an innovative application of classical mechanics.

Newton's laws of motion were summarized and proposed by Isaac Newton in his book “Mathematical Principles of Natural Philosophy” in 1687 [19]. The first law of motion defines force as the cause of changes in the state of motion of an object; the second law describes how force results in the acceleration of an object; and the third law explains that force arises from interactions between bodies [20]. Newton's laws of motion are independent of each other, and their internal logic is consistent.

In this paper, based on Newton's three laws of motion, the gas-water two-phase seepage model was deduced and established from the most basic force analysis, the interaction force between solid and liquid was determined by the atomic force microscope (AFM), and the motion law of gas-water two-phase seepage under the conditions of constant flow displacement and constant pressure displacement was simulated, respectively. Subsequently, based on the seepage model, the relationship between velocity and pressure difference, pipe radius, water film thickness, and viscosity were deduced when the seepage reached a steady state, and the steady-state model was established. A macroscopic permeability characterization model was established based on the steady-state seepage model and by considering the pore throat distribution law. Considering the characteristics of the mercury injection curve, the calculation model of the gas-water two-phase relative permeability curve was established, and a new gas well productivity calculation model was derived. Finally, the three new models were numerically simulated to verify their rationality and accuracy.

Gas-water two-phase seepage model based on Newton's law of motion

Gas-water two-phase seepage model under constant flow displacement

In the case of constant flow displacement, the gas begins to enter the dead volume region (Vs) at a certain speed (Fig. 1), the pressure gradually increases, and the Fp gradually increases. When the displacement force reaches the sum of the capillary force and the maximum friction force, water begins to flow, and the gas flooding process begins. Once gas flooding begins, the water begins to flow in layers due to viscosity, creating internal friction. Under the action of displacement force, capillary force, and internal friction force, the water element has a certain acceleration, and the velocity increases from 0. In the case of displacement with constant flow, the velocity of the gas at the inlet is constant, and the inlet pressure p(t) continues to increase. Additionally, Fp gradually increases when the fluid velocity v is less than the set speed. The internal friction force gradually increases with the fluid velocity. Consequently, the acceleration a(t) constantly changes with the change in inlet pressure and the fluid velocity, eventually reaching a relatively stable value.

Primarily, the first stage, the pressure-holding process of the system, is analyzed. Under the case of displacement with constant flow, the flow qg under normal pressure is a fixed value, the initial pressure at the inlet and outlet are p0, and the gas volume of the dead volume part Vs at time t is qgt. Gas-water two-phase seepage model under constant pressure displacement

In the case of displacement with constant pressure, the gas will quickly fill the Vs part to achieve the set pressure difference. To establish the pressure difference, the displacement force must be greater than the sum of the capillary force and the maximum static friction force. Once the set pressure difference is reached, the gas accelerates and pushes the water to flow. The speed increases from 0, while the internal friction varies with speed. Despite the constant displacement force, the acceleration fluctuates until reaching an equilibrium state.

In contrast, even under the case of displacement with constant pressure, the inlet and outlet pressures are unchanged. If the inlet pressure is p2, and the outlet pressure is p1, the displacement force Fp is calculated by the following Equation (25):(25)Fp=πr2(p2−p1)≥Fσ+Fs=2πrσcosθ+fs·Gsw

AFM to measure the interaction force between solid and liquid

The AFM is one of the most powerful analytical tools in nanotechnology and is increasingly used in the petroleum field because it is advantageous in exploring nanoscale pore structures [[21], [22], [23]]. As a high-resolution scanning instrument, AFM has become the best choice for the study of nanoscale mechanical properties due to its non-destructive and high-resolution advantages [24].

Adhesion force is an inherent property of materials that typically remain constant on uniform surfaces [25]. One method to measure adhesion force involves conducting mechanical tests on the sample to generate a force curve [26] (Fig. 2), which can be used to analyze the relationship between adhesion force and rock structure.Fig. 2Force curve [26].Fig. 2

Force curve [26].

The core samples of tight sandstone gas reservoirs were taken to prepare thin slices measuring 5 mm in length, 5 mm in width, and 4 mm in height. The slices were then polished using sandpaper with varying mesh numbers. Subsequently, the samples were polished by argon-ion polishing instrument to smoothen the samples' surface. The atomic force microscope was used to scan each sample's surface, obtain the two-dimensional and three-dimensional morphology of the sample's surface, and conduct mechanical testing and analysis. The force curve was obtained as shown in Fig. 3.Fig. 3Measured force curve of core sample.Fig. 3

Measured force curve of core sample.

As can be seen from Fig. 3, the adhesive force of the sample, that is, the interaction force between solid and liquid, Gsw, is 0.00225 μN, that is, 2.25 × 10−9 N. This data will be used in the subsequent calculation process.

Conclusion

(1)Based on Newton's law of motion, a new gas-water two-phase seepage model was established by taking displacement force, capillary force, wall friction, and internal friction of fluid into account. Moreover, the rationality of the model was verified.(2)When the gas-water two-phase seepage model reached stability and force equilibrium, the relationship between velocity and pressure difference, pipe radius, water film thickness, viscosity, etc., was derived; that is, the steady-state model was derived based on Newton's law of motion.(3)Based on the steady-state model, the equivalent permeability characterization model was derived to calculate the permeability of rock samples with different channel distribution characteristics. The calculated results were consistent with the measured values, and there was a good correspondence between channel distribution characteristics and permeability.(4)The relative permeability calculation method was established based on the steady-state model and capillary force curve. The permeability curve obtained by relative permeability calculation method was compared with that obtained by Poiseuille's law and the capillary force curve. The two curves were consistent with the two-phase seepage law, and the correction of the relative permeability calculation method was more reasonable, effective, and practical.(5)A new productivity equation was derived based on the steady-state model. The IPR curve calculated by the gas well example was consistent with that calculated by the traditional productivity equation, and the open flow error between the two models was only 4.65 %.

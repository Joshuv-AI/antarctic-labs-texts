Conversion-driven heterointerface reconfiguration enables fast ion migration in iron disulfide/sulfur-based positive electrodes for aqueous batteries
Authors: Haitao Li, Zichuang Li, Wen Wen, Yong Wang, Qi Lei, Xiangzhi Zhang, Tian-Nan Ye, Renzhong Tai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Establishing stable active sites and molecular-scale bridging architectures at electrode-electrolyte interfaces is essential for advancing fast-charging batteries, yet remains challenging because the principles governing interfacial reaction networks and materials design are not fully understood. In sulfur-based electrodes, irreversible sulfur-cluster dissolution and progressive interfacial degradation severely limit capacity retention and cycling stability. Here we show that reversible dynamic covalent tethering can be constructed at electrified interfaces by coupling hierarchically structured FeS₂ with exogenous sulfur clusters. During the initial full discharge, Cu²⁺ ions are spontaneously reduced and reconstruct the interface to form a Cu₂S/FeS₂ heterostructure, in which interfacial Fe-S-Cu bonds act as ordered covalent bridges. This surface-bound Fe-S-Cu bridging topology reduces interfacial resistance, accelerates charge-transfer kinetics and suppresses metastable phase formation arising from incomplete conversion. Copper ions further function as molecular rivets, preferentially depositing at edge sites and directionally bonding with sulfur atoms to stabilize the FeS₂-hosted sulfur redox system. This dynamic bonding framework preserves the structural coherence of the FeS₂ matrix while immobilizing polysulfide intermediates, enabling more than 68,000 charge-discharge cycles at 100 A g−1. Our findings establish a molecular-level design strategy that links atomic bonding motifs with macroscopic electrochemical functionality.

Introduction

The evolution of battery technology has taken a significant step towards the efficient harnessing of energy storage and the effective deployment of energy release, which is crucial for the sustainable management of finite resources1–3. There is a growing imperative for the reliable and expeditious recharging of energy in the realm of portable electronic devices and the nascent market of electric vehicles, thereby rendering the capability for rapid charging particularly anticipated and valuable4–6. Aqueous batteries have therefore emerged as attractive platforms for high-rate energy storage because water-based electrolytes enable fast ion migration and favorable rate capability7,8. The small size and high polarity of water molecules further promote electrochemical kinetics during rapid charge-discharge processes9,10. In addition, aqueous electrolytes offer intrinsic non-flammability, high heat capacity, environmental compatibility and low cost, providing safety and sustainability advantages over conventional batteries based on organic electrolytes11,12. These features make aqueous batteries promising candidates for fast-charging applications, but their practical implementation still requires electrode materials and reaction systems that can sustain rapid, reversible and stable interfacial reactions.

A central challenge in fast-charging batteries is to sustain high-current operation without sacrificing the structural integrity or electrochemical activity of electrode materials. Under high current densities, repeated ion insertion, conversion reactions and interfacial charge transfer can induce structural collapse, performance decay and safety risks, thereby shortening battery lifetime13,14. Strategies, such as heteroatom doping, compositing and surface modification, have been widely used to regulate electronic structure, improve conductivity and reinforce electrode architectures15–19. These approaches can partially alleviate the mechanical and kinetic stresses imposed during rapid charge-discharge. However, heteroatom doping is often difficult to control precisely, while carbon-based conductive scaffolds are intrinsically chemically inert and generally fail to establish stable chemical linkages with active species20–23. As a result, their function is largely restricted to mechanical support and discontinuous point-contact electron conduction. In conventional heterogeneous electrodes, where physical mixing or weak adsorption dominates, the absence of covalent bonding between sulfur active species and the FeS₂ host leads to progressive interfacial degradation, including delamination, contact failure and scaffold collapse (Fig. 1a). Rational electrode design therefore requires not only structurally stable host materials but also a molecular-level understanding of how surface coordination environments regulate interfacial reaction dynamics. Heterostructured chalcogenides and oxides have been widely explored to improve electrochemical performance by modulating electronic states, coordination structures and charge-transfer pathways at electrode interfaces24–26. However, simply constructing heterogeneous interfaces does not guarantee durable coupling between active species and the host, especially under fast charge-discharge conditions. The changes in the electronic states and coordination environment of these active substances at the interface significantly affect the kinetics and thermodynamics of electrochemical reactions27–30. We therefore sought to establish chemically defined active sites and covalent bridging motifs that can anchor sulfur species while maintaining the structural integrity of the FeS₂ substrate. In our design, electrochemical activation of the freshly assembled cell induces the reduction of Cu²⁺ ions and their preferential deposition on the sulfur-rich FeS₂ surface, leading to the formation of interfacial Fe-S-Cu covalent bridges. These bridges provide robust molecular connections between the FeS₂ host and sulfur active species, thereby promoting interfacial electron transfer, facilitating ion transport and reducing polarization under high current densities (Fig. 1b). By coupling active-species immobilization with ordered charge-transfer pathways, this covalent-bridging strategy is expected to improve the endurance and reliability of aqueous batteries during high-rate cycling.

Here we report a reaction-mechanism-guided materials design strategy that uses discharge-driven electrochemical activation to construct Fe-S-Cu covalent bridges in situ at a Cu₂S/FeS₂ heterointerface. In this system, pyrite FeS₂ serves as a robust and abundant host with high theoretical capacity, thermomechanical stability and environmental compatibility, while exogenous sulfur clusters provide redox-active species within a hierarchical FeS₂@S architecture. During the initial discharge, Cu²⁺ ions are reduced and preferentially anchored at sulfur-rich interfacial sites, triggering surface reconstruction and the formation of Fe-S-Cu bonding motifs. Density functional theory calculations reveal that this preferential Cu adsorption produces a molecular riveting effect, strengthening electronic interconnectivity and promoting charge transfer across the FeS₂@S interface. Operando synchrotron X-ray diffraction (o-SXRD) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) directly revealed that a pronounced reversible surface phase transformation emerges via a copper-ion intercalation-driven phase-bridging transition mechanism, in which dynamic interphase coupling mediates the structural evolution while maintaining interfacial stability. X-ray absorption spectroscopy (XAS) combined with spatially resolved resonant inelastic X-ray scattering (RIXS) mapping revealed that the dynamic reconstruction of interfacial Fe-S-Cu covalent bridges during electrochemical cycling synergistically stabilizes the electrode architecture under topotactic bond rearrangement enabling structural integrity and polaron-mediated charge transfer dynamics that enhance reaction kinetics. As a result, the covalent-bridge-engineered FeS₂@S positive electrode delivers 885.71 mAh g−1 at 20 A g−1 and sustains 68,000 cycles at 100 A g−1. The Zn²⁺/Cu²⁺ dual-ion system further achieves a specific energy of 460.49 Wh kg−1 at a specific power of 13.50 kW kg−1, based on active mass. This work establishes dynamic covalent interfacial bridging as a design principle for stabilizing sulfur redox chemistry and developing fast-charging aqueous batteries.

Results

Heterostructures synthesis and characterizations

A microflower-like FeS2 served as the sulfur host, and the FeS2@S heterostructure was synthesized via a straightforward one-step hydrothermal method (Supplementary Fig. 1). The X-ray diffraction (XRD) pattern, as presented in Fig. 2a, unambiguously reveals that the predominant phases within the FeS2@S composite are crystalline sulfur (JCPDS Card No. 08-0247) and pure FeS2 (JCPDS Card No. 42-1340). The valence states of the constituent elements in the pure FeS2 and FeS2@S heterostructure were determined using X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS). The Fe 2p XPS spectrum displays spin-orbit-split doublets at 707.29 eV (2p₃/₂) and 720.09 eV (2p₁/₂), characteristic of Fe²⁺ coordinated to sulfur ligands (Fig. 2b)31. A minor presence of divalent and trivalent iron oxides, likely resulting from air oxidation, is detected within the near-surface region of the material32–34. The deconvolution of S 2p XPS spectrum resolves two spin-orbit-split doublets at 162.57 eV (2p₃/₂) and 163.73 eV (2p₁/₂), and 164.00 eV (2p₃/₂) and 165.16 eV (2p₁/₂), assigned to sulfur in the S1⁻ and S⁰ states, respectively (Fig. 2c)31,35,36. Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA; Fig. 2d) of the FeS₂@S composite indicated two distinct mass-loss regimes: a 15.8 wt% loss between 35 and 286 °C, corresponding to the volatilization of elemental sulfur, and a subsequent 22.83 wt% loss from 400 to 642 °C, attributed to sulfur release via thermal decomposition of FeS₂37. The electronic structure and local coordination of Fe centers in the FeS2@S heterostructure were interrogated via synchrotron-radiation XAS. Analysis of the Fe K-edge X-ray absorption near-edge structure (XANES) reveals an absorption edge energy positioned between metallic Fe foil and Fe²⁺ reference compounds, indicating mixed valence states between Fe⁰ and Fe²⁺ (Fig. 2e)38. Extended X-ray absorption fine structure (EXAFS) analysis resolved the Fe-S bond distances and coordination environment. The k³-weighted Fourier-transformed EXAFS (FT-EXAFS) spectra exhibited a dominant first-shell scattering peak at 1.84 Å, assigned to Fe-S coordination bonds (Fig. 2f). Quantitative EXAFS fitting of the FeS₂@S heterostructure resolved that the minimal Debye-Waller factor (σ² = 0.00033 Å²) and low R-factor (0.0098) corroborate the structural precision of the model (Supplementary Table S1). The as-synthesized FeS₂@S heterostructure exhibits a hierarchical microflower architecture with uniform dimensions, as resolved by scanning and transmission electron microscopy (SEM/TEM; Supplementary Fig. 2; Supplementary Fig. 3). This three-dimensional morphology features radially aligned nanopetals, creating interconnected porous channels that likely facilitate ion diffusion and mitigate volume expansion during electrochemical cycling (Fig. 2g; Supplementary Fig. 4). Energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) elemental mapping confirms the homogeneous spatial distribution of sulfur and iron throughout the FeS₂@S heterostructure (Fig. 2h; Supplementary Fig. 5), eliminating phase segregation and ensuring atomic-level integration of redox-active sites. High-resolution TEM (HRTEM; Fig. 2i) resolves two distinct interplanar spacings of 3.16 Å and 2.86 Å, indexed to the (111) crystallographic planes of pyrite-phase FeS₂ (JCPDS 42-1340) and the (044) planes of orthorhombic sulfur (JCPDS 08-0247), respectively. This interfacial registry confirms the epitaxial integration of FeS₂ and sulfur phases, establishing a coherent heterojunction that is critical for interfacial charge transport and structural stability. The engineered FeS₂@S heterostructure exhibits a three-dimensional hierarchical architecture, in which a porous FeS₂ microflower scaffold is uniformly decorated with dense sulfur domains. Electrochemical performance of FeS2@S electrodes

Copper ions were selected as model redox carriers because of their well-defined electrochemical activity and relevance to emerging aqueous redox chemistries39. The sulfur-rich surface of the FeS₂@S heterostructure exhibits strong affinity toward Cu²⁺ through combined S→Cu²⁺ Lewis acid-base interactions and FeS₂-mediated interfacial electron transfer, enabling both chemisorption and subsequent electrochemical reduction. This dual functionality is facilitated by the Cu²⁺/Cu redox chemistry, whose potential range is compatible with the operating window of the FeS₂@S heterostructure. Cyclic voltammetry at 0.2 mV s−1 reveals three distinct redox couples, indicating a multistep interfacial redox process (Fig. 3a). This electrochemical signature originates from the coupling between the metallic conductivity of pyritic FeS₂ and the sulfur-rich coordination environment, which together promote stable charge transfer and reversible copper-mediated interfacial reactions. The rate capability was further evaluated over current densities from 5 to 100 A g−1, corresponding to a 20-fold increase in current density (Fig. 3b). The FeS₂@S electrode delivered specific capacities of 541.10, 538.87, 534.60, 526.78, 509.04, 451.80, 357.79 and 211.12 mAh g−1, respectively. When the current density was returned to 5 A g−1, the capacity recovered to 558.24 mAh g−1, demonstrating excellent rate tolerance and electrochemical reversibility. Galvanostatic charge-discharge (GCD) profiles collected across 5–100 A g−1 show well-preserved voltage plateaus, consistent with the redox features observed in the cyclic voltammetry curves (Supplementary Fig. 6 and Fig. 3a). Elevated specific currents enhance electrochemical polarization (Supplementary Fig. 7), requiring greater driving forces to overcome kinetic barriers and sustain rapid interfacial reactions. Figure 3c shows the cycling performance of the FeS₂@S battery at 20 A g−1. The capacity increases progressively during the initial activation stage and reaches 885.71 mAh g−1 after 900 cycles, followed by a gradual and moderate decline. After 10,000 cycles, the battery retains 85.32% of its initial capacity, with a Coulombic efficiency of 97.64%. The corresponding GCD profiles collected at different cycle numbers show well-maintained voltage plateaus, confirming the stability of the redox process during long-term cycling (Fig. 3d). During the early cycles, electrolyte infiltration promotes the formation of accessible ion-transport channels, thereby reducing internal impedance and polarization (Supplementary Fig. 8). As a result, the charge-discharge plateaus shift closer to the thermodynamic equilibrium potential, accompanied by increased capacity and accelerated reaction kinetics. Although side reactions and gradual material degradation subsequently increase polarization, the voltage shift remains below 0.1 V over 1000 cycles, demonstrating stable interfacial reaction kinetics and robust cycling behavior. When subjected to a high current density of 50 A g−1, the FeS₂@S electrode exhibits long-term cyclability, retaining 84.34% of its capacity after 30,000 cycles (306.95 mAh g−1; Fig. 3e). Although the specific energy displays an initial increase followed by a gradual decrease, the total energy loss remains below 20% after 30,000 cycles (Supplementary Fig. 9). By contrast, unmodified FeS₂ delivers negligible capacity at high current densities of 20, 50 and 100 A g−1, reflecting its limited surface sulfur content and sluggish kinetic activation. The reversible conversion reaction requires continuous electrochemical activation of sulfur species, which is severely restricted under high-rate conditions by insufficient reactive sites and slow ion/electron transport. As a result, unmodified FeS₂ cannot sustain reversible conversion at high current densities. The FeS₂@S composite electrode also demonstrates strong current tolerance and stable electrochemical behavior during cycling (Supplementary Fig. 11). SEM images of electrodes after more than 1000 charge-discharge cycles show that the flower-like morphology is largely retained, with no obvious surface degradation or microstructural collapse (Supplementary Fig. 12). This morphological robustness supports the structural stability required for prolonged high-rate operation. Compared with previously reported FeS₂-based battery systems, the Cu||FeS₂@S cell shows competitive cycling stability and high-rate durability (Fig. 3g and Supplementary Table S2)40–63. Moreover, the cell sustains stable charge-discharge cycling on a 16-s timescale under high current conditions (Supplementary Fig. 13), highlighting its potential for applications that require rapid energy delivery, including grid-scale energy storage and high-power electronics.

Phase transformations mechanism

The o-SXRD (λ = 0.6887 Å) was employed to probe the structural and compositional evolution of FeS₂@S heterostructures during controlled Cu²⁺ sequestration through three consecutive cycles (Fig. 4a). The o-SXRD patterns show that the principal diffraction peaks of FeS₂ are largely preserved throughout the measured potential range and are already discernible before discharge. The analysis reveals structural persistence of the FeS₂ phase during electrochemical cycling, with principal diffraction peaks (200) (2θ = 14.8°) > 97.4% intensity retention after the initial discharge (Supplementary Fig. 14). High-resolution angular deconvolution confirms phase coexistence between pristine elemental sulfur and pyritic FeS₂, unveiling sulfur redistribution under operational bias (Fig. 4b). The crystallographic facets of FeS₂ remain stable during discharge, despite the volumetric perturbation associated with Cu incorporation. No appreciable peak displacement is observed, indicating that the structural integrity of the FeS₂ framework is largely preserved. Quantitative analysis based on the Stokes-Wilson equation gives a lattice strain of only 0.039% for the FeS₂ (200) reflection, confirming the crystallographic resilience of the host lattice during Cu uptake (Supplementary Fig. 15). In contrast, surface sulfur undergoes a dynamic conversion pathway from S⁰ to Cu₇S₄ and subsequently to Cu₂S, consistent with the formation of Cu₂S as the discharge product and indicative of pronounced surface/interface reconstruction during electrochemical cycling. Facet-dependent XRD intensity changes further reveal anisotropic interfacial restructuring. Specifically, the intensity of the FeS₂ (222) reflection decreases by 17.42%, whereas that of the (023) reflection increases by 2.49%, suggesting direction-dependent strain accommodation during Cu-mediated reconstruction (Supplementary Fig. 16). During the subsequent charging process, these intensity variations are largely reversed, demonstrating the high reversibility of the phase evolution and interfacial structural rearrangement. TEM analysis reveals dynamic interfacial reconstruction of the FeS₂ surface during copper-ion storage. Upon discharge to 0 V (Fig. 4c and Supplementary Fig. 17), the FeS₂ (211) planes with a lattice spacing of 2.21 Å retain crystallographic coherence while directing the epitaxial growth of Cu₂S (102) planes with a spacing of 3.32 Å, indicating surface-mediated phase assembly. Importantly, the interface is atomically sharp and coherent, with continuous lattice fringes and no detectable amorphous interlayer, confirming intimate interfacial contact. EDS mapping (Supplementary Fig. 18) further supports this by showing continuous S distribution and a Cu/Fe gradient across the interface, consistent with chemical bonding rather than physical mixture. Moreover, S K-edge XANES (Supplementary Fig. 19) reveals a distinct chemical environment at 0 V, indicating a stable intermediate phase differing from both pristine FeS₂ and CuS. Analysis of the intermediate discharge state at 0.26 V captures a metastable FeS₂-Cu₇S₄ interfacial configuration, as evidenced by lattice fringes of 3.14 Å for FeS₂ (111) and 3.04 Å for Cu₇S₄ (113) (Fig. 4d). Upon full charge to 0.5 V, the FeS₂ surface is regenerated, while residual CuS domains with a lattice spacing of 3.21 Å corresponding to the (101) planes remain spatially confined, presumably owing to interfacial energy minimization (Fig. 4e). Operando structural tracking further shows that the petal-like morphology of the FeS₂@S heterostructure is retained throughout repeated charge-discharge cycles (Supplementary Fig. 20). Covalent “Fe-S-Cu bridge” mediated bidirectional redox chemistry

Ex situ XPS was performed to resolve charge redistribution and interfacial bonding evolution in the FeS₂@S heterostructure during electrochemical cycling. The GCD profile defines a series of representative states from A to J, enabling state-resolved XPS analysis and correlation between electrochemical behavior and interfacial chemistry (Fig. 5a). The Fe 2p spectra reveal reversible modulation of iron speciation during cycling (Fig. 5b). The characteristic Fe-S coordination peak in the Fe 2p₃/₂-S region at 707.38 eV attenuates markedly during discharge from A to D and from G to J, and then gradually recovers during charging from D to G. Meanwhile, the Fe 2p₃/₂ core level shifts positively during discharge and returns to its initial binding energy upon recharge, indicating reversible charge redistribution and oxidation-state modulation at the heterogeneous interface. Together with the o-SXRD results, a series of phase transitions is observed on the FeS₂ surface, with the formation of compounds including Cu₂S from S. These structural transformations are directly correlated with the attenuation and migration of Fe peaks. More critically, within the electrochemical reaction environment, the Fe acts as a critical node in the charge transfer pathway, modulating its electron density and, in turn, influencing the intensity of the corresponding XPS signal. Concurrently, the charge transfer process induces alterations in the oxidation state of the Fe, which accounts for the observed shift in the XPS peak64. The S 2p XPS spectra further reveal the dynamic evolution of sulfur speciation (Fig. 5c). Initial spectra show distinct Fe-S 2p3/2 (162.68 eV) and S⁰ 2p3/2 (164.01 eV) signatures at A state, replaced by Cu-S 2p3/2 bonding at 161.72 eV during discharging to D state, while residual Fe-S intensity persists, signaling interfacial Fe-S-Cu bridge formation31,36,65. Upon the subsequent charging phase, transitioning from state D to G, the Fe-S signal regains intensity, while the Cu-S signal is still detectable. This is supported by o-SXRD data, which reveal that the predominant transformation is the conversion of Cu2S to CuS. Notably, the Fe-S-Cu covalent bridge is sustained on the surface throughout these electrochemical transitions. Subsequent discharge cycles substantiate the presence of a profoundly reversible redox mechanism within this electrochemical system. In the Cu spectrum (Fig. 5d), the evolution of copper species is a pivotal observation during the electrochemical process. At the fully discharged D state, the copper signal is dominated by Cu⁺ associated with Cu₂S formation, confirming the reduction of Cu²⁺ during discharge. In contrast, at the charged G state, the emergence of a characteristic Cu²⁺ satellite feature indicates copper oxidation and the formation of CuS-rich species. The spectral recovery at the subsequent discharged J state demonstrates that copper can reversibly shuttle between Cu₂S and CuS-like chemical environments during cycling. This reversible Cu redox transition, coupled with the persistence of Fe-S-Cu interfacial bonding, stabilizes the reconstructed interface and supports sustained electrochemical performance. During extended cycling, continuous electrochemical activation progressively exposes additional sulfur-rich active sites to the electrolyte, increasing the effective reaction area and contributing to the gradual capacity increase. In parallel, the redistribution of Cu species along near-surface and interfacial transport pathways enables more surface sulfur in FeS₂ to participate in reversible redox reactions. Transmission-mode X-ray absorption spectroscopy was used to probe the local coordination environment of Fe and Cu centers during Cu²⁺ shuttling. The normalized Fe K-edge XANES spectra demonstrate electronic stability, with edge energy variations constrained to ΔE < 0.1 eV across electrochemical states, indicating minimal perturbation to the Fe-S-Cu covalent architecture (Fig. 5e). Quantitative EXAFS fitting further resolves the local structural dynamics around Fe. The Fe-S bond length remains essentially unchanged at 1.84 Å throughout cycling, although the scattering intensity decreases by 15.8%, consistent with reversible coordination disorder or bond relaxation rather than irreversible structural degradation (Fig. 5f). The FT-EXAFS spectra also show preserved Fe-S coordination shells, confirming that the local topology of the FeS₂-based heterointerface is maintained. Together, these results indicate dynamic covalent adaptability, in which the interfacial electronic environment evolves during electrochemical cycling without substantial geometric reconstruction66.

The normalized Cu K-edge XANES spectra unravel dynamic redox mediation and reversible coordination evolution during copper intercalation/deintercalation (Fig. 5g). The absorption edge energy shifts systematically across electrochemical states, reflecting valence-state transitions between Cu⁺ (discharged) and Cu²⁺ (charged) species. Upon discharge to 0 V, the Cu K-edge shifts to lower energy, consistent with Cu reduction, enhanced Cu-S coordination and partial electron localization at Cu sites. These reversible spectral changes indicate that copper participates in the interfacial redox process while maintaining local coordination continuity through adaptive Fe-S-Cu bonding. FT-EXAFS analysis resolves elastic bond-length modulation of the Cu-S first shell, contracting from 1.81 Å to 1.84 Å during de/copperization (Fig. 5h). This small bond-length variation reflects elastic adjustment of the Cu coordination geometry during redox cycling. Reliable phase fitting was obtained by refining the R-space fitting window and constraining the scattering paths based on the preceding structural analyses. These results highlight the role of Fe-S-Cu covalent bridges in preserving the local coordination environment while facilitating reversible copper redox reactions. The wavelet transform of EXAFS (WT-EXAFS) provides additional resolution of the local scattering environment by correlating k-space features with radial distances (Supplementary Fig. 22). The dominant Cu scattering maxima remain nearly identical between the first and second discharged states, appearing at (k, R) = (5.11 Å−1, 1.70 Å) and (5.12 Å−1, 1.72 Å), respectively. The small differences in Δk = 0.01 Å−1 and ΔR = 0.02 Å indicate highly reproducible Cu coordination environments upon repeated discharge. This wavelet similarity further supports the reversibility of the Cu-mediated interfacial reconstruction and the structural robustness of the Fe-S-Cu bridging motif.

RIXS analysis reveals the stability of the electronic structure of FeS2@S during charge-discharge cycles (Fig. 5i–l), underscoring the pivotal role of the Fe-S-Cu covalent bridge. The absence of additional inelastic scattering features indicates that no new detectable electronic transitions emerge during cycling, suggesting that the electronic configuration of the FeS₂-based heterointerface is largely preserved. Meanwhile, the Fe-related spectral intensity decreases during discharge and recovers during charge, reflecting the reversible participation of Fe centers in interfacial charge redistribution and redox mediation. The Fe-S-Cu covalent bridge appears to be instrumental in maintaining the structural integrity of the electrode material, as the reversible intensity changes of the Fe peak imply that the bridge facilitates electron transfer without undergoing geometrical rearrangements67. The preservation of this covalent bridge is crucial for the cyclic stability and reversibility of battery, as it ensures the efficient de-intercalation and intercalation of Cu ions. Together with the XAS and EXAFS results, the RIXS data support a mechanism in which the Fe-S-Cu bridge preserves interfacial coordination continuity and prevents irreversible electronic reconstruction during Cu-ion uptake and release. Such electronic stability is essential for sustaining the reversibility and cycling durability of the FeS₂@S electrode. By providing both structural anchoring and electronic coupling, the Fe-S-Cu covalent bridge reduces charge-transfer resistance and contributes to the high-rate performance and long-term stability of the battery.

DFT calculation and kinetic analysis

DFT calculations were employed to decipher the preferential intercalation pathway of Cu and the electronic properties of the interfacial Fe-S-Cu bridge in FeS₂@S heterostructures (Supplementary Fig. 23). Adsorption energy analysis reveals a thermodynamically favored Cu²⁺ intercalation pathway, wherein the layer-trapped configuration exhibits a significantly stronger adsorption energy of −2.77 eV compared to its surface-bound counterparts (−1.55 eV on FeS₂ and −2.16 eV on S; Fig. 6a). This pronounced energy difference suggests a thermodynamic preference for initial Cu deposition at the interlayer channels, where the confined space geometrically facilitates the formation of covalent Fe-S-Cu bridges. Bader charge analysis provides deeper insight into the interfacial charge transfer. In all configurations, Cu acts as an electron donor. The resulting charge redistribution is 0.47 |e| in the bridged configuration, significantly greater than the 0.32 |e| and 0.25 |e| in the isolated interfaces (Fig. 6b). This larger value originates from the unique charge-sharing nature of the Fe-S-Cu bridge, where electrons from Cu are simultaneously accepted by S atoms from both the FeS₂ layer and the S cluster. This extensive delocalization indicates stronger electronic interaction and orbital hybridization, serving as a direct theoretical indicator of the enhanced interfacial coupling. Consistently, the total density of states (TDOS) shows a finite density of states at the Fermi level without an apparent band gap for the Cu-bridged configuration, supporting improved electronic conductivity across the interface (Fig. 6c)68. Electrochemical kinetic analysis further confirms the fast charge-storage behavior enabled by this bridged interface. Cyclic voltammetry analysis of FeS₂@S electrodes reveals hybrid charge storage behavior, with scan-rate-dependent kinetics (0.2-0.8 mV s−1) demonstrating dominant capacitive contributions (65–75%) through power-law fitting (b=0.68-0.74; Fig. 6d–g). Pseudocapacitive dominance escalates from 66 to 80% with increasing scan rates, attributed to fast ions migration through Fe-S-Cu covalent channels. This behavior is attributed to rapid interfacial Cu redox and ion migration along Fe-S-Cu-linked pathways, which support high-rate charge storage and cycling stability69. Galvanostatic intermittent titration (GITT) measurements further reveal fast and state-dependent Cu-ion diffusion in FeS₂@S, with diffusion coefficients ranging from 10⁻¹¹ to 10⁻⁶ cm² s−1 during cycling (Fig. 6h). Such adaptive diffusion kinetics are consistent with an electron-rich heterointerface that lowers migration barriers through dynamic Fe-S-Cu coordination networks70. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) validates the interfacial kinetics of the Fe-S-Cu covalent architecture, exhibiting a low charge-transfer resistance (Rct=8.95 Ω) and great capacitive behavior (Warburg slope=2.35 Ω·s−0.5; Fig. 6i; Supplementary Fig. 24), a direct manifestation of barrierless electron delocalization via Fe(3d)-S(3p)-Cu(3d) orbital hybridization. This hybridization facilitates delocalized electron transfer, reducing interfacial impedance and enhancing charge storage kinetics71. The calculated Cu²⁺ diffusion coefficient (5.02×10−9 cm2/s) based on the Warburg relationship aligns closely with GITT results, validating rapid ion migration through Fe-S-Cu covalent channels72. Extended applications

The stable Fe-S-Cu covalent bridge enables a high-voltage aqueous Zn||FeS2@S-Cu hybrid battery with a compartmentalized architecture (Fig. 7a; Supplementary Fig. 25). The cell consists of a FeS₂@S positive electrode and a Zn negative electrode separated by an anion-exchange membrane (AEM), with 1 M CuSO₄ as the catholyte and 1 M ZnSO₄ as the anolyte. During discharge, Cu²⁺ in uptake and reduction at the covalent-bridge-stabilized positive electrode are coupled with Zn stripping at the negative electrode, while the AEM selectively mediates SO₄²⁻ shuttling to maintain charge balance. Galvanostatic cycling demonstrates a specific capacity of 400 mAh g−1 at 1 A g−1 and an expanded potential window (1.1-1.6 V vs. Zn²⁺/Zn; Fig. 7b), enabled by the bridge’s dual role in suppressing polarization and stabilizing interfacial kinetics. The corresponding potentials referenced to standard hydrogen electrode (SHE) are also shown in Fig. 7b. The hybrid battery demonstrates stable cycling across varied current densities (Fig. 7c-e). At 0.5 A g−1, it maintains 403.3 mA h g−1 over 200 cycles (544.46 Wh kg−1 specific energy at 675 W kg−1, based on the mass of the active center). At a higher current density of 10 A g−1, the cell retains 341.1 mAh g−1 after 1300 cycles, achieving a specific energy of 460.49 Wh kg−1 and a specific power of 13.5 kW kg−1 with near-unity Coulombic efficiency. A gradual decline in specific energy is observed after approximately 1,000 cycles (Supplementary Fig. 26). The GCD profiles exhibit a non-monotonic evolution of polarization, which initially increases before declining (Supplementary Fig. 27). Notably, after 1,300 cycles, the overall ΔV remains below 0.03 V, indicating highly stable interfacial dynamics enabled by the self-repair capability of the Fe-S-Cu (Fig. 7e). Building upon the established decoupled electrolyte architecture, we strategically transitioned to gel polymer electrolyte (GPE) to address inherent limitations of liquid systems while maintaining ionic segregation capabilities. The covalent Fe-S-Cu bridge-engineered flexible Zn|ZnSO₄ GPE|CuSO₄ GPE|FeS2@S-Cu quasi-solid-state battery achieves stable mechanical-electrochemical synergy (Fig. 7f; Supplementary Fig. 28-31), powering commercial a red light emitting diode (LED). The flexible quasi-solid-state battery demonstrates deep-cycle discharge capability, sustaining a 20 min deep discharge for powering digital timer (Fig. 7h-j; Supplementary Video 1). Discussion

In summary, we demonstrate a reaction-mechanism-guided strategy for engineering FeS₂@S positive electrodes through interfacial covalent bridge formation. The o-SXRD and atomic-resolution TEM reveal a dynamic, surface-stabilized and reversible phase evolution during Cu²⁺ uptake, in which copper-mediated reconstruction generates a coherent Cu₂S/FeS₂ heterointerface. Ex situ XPS, XAS and RIXS further confirm reversible charge redistribution and interfacial electronic stabilization, supporting the formation and persistence of Fe-S-Cu covalent bridges during cycling. DFT calculations show that these bridges promote charge delocalization across Fe 3d-S 3p-Cu 3d orbital interactions, thereby strengthening interfacial electronic coupling and reducing charge-transfer resistance to 8.95 Ω. This adaptive interfacial architecture stabilizes sulfur redox chemistry, suppresses irreversible sulfur dissolution and enables rapid Cu-mediated charge transfer. As a result, the FeS₂@S electrode sustains 68,000 cycles at 100 A g−1, corresponding to a full charge-discharge timescale of 16 s. The quasi-solid-state Zn²⁺/Cu²⁺ dual-ion cell achieves a specific energy of 460.49 Wh kg−1 at a specific power of 13.50 kW kg−1, based on positive-electrode active mass. A flexible device further maintains deep-discharge operation while powering a digital timer for 20 min. These findings establish dynamic Fe-S-Cu covalent bridging as a generalizable interfacial design principle for high-power aqueous energy-storage systems.

Methods

Materials

All chemicals, including iron(III) chloride hexahydrate (FeCl₃·6H₂O, 99%), sodium thiosulfate pentahydrate (Na₂S₂O₃·5H₂O, 99%), ethylene glycol (99.5%), copper(II) sulfate pentahydrate (CuSO₄·5H₂O, 99.9%), zinc sulfate heptahydrate (ZnSO₄·7H₂O, 99.9%), Potassium hydroxide(KOH, 85.0%), potassium persulfate(K₂S₂O₈, 99.5%) and acrylic acid (C₃H₄O₂, 99%) were sourced from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd. (China) and used as received. Metallic substrates-zinc foil (a thickness of 0.1 mm, 99.99%) and copper foil (a thickness of 0.1 mm, 99.99%)-were obtained from the same supplier. Carbon cloth (99.99%, 100 μm, Hefei Kejing Materials Technology), Super-P carbon black (99.9%, Guangdong Canrd New Energy Technology), and anion-exchange membranes (FAB-PK-130, FUMATECH BWT GmbH, Germany) were employed without further processing. To realize flexible energy storage, a mechanically robust hydrogel matrix was synthesized through a multistep protocol. First, 0.15 g of tapioca starch (New Land Group) was hydrothermally processed at 90 °C for 1 h in 3 mL deionized water to form a viscous precursor. Concurrently, a polyacrylate network was developed by dissolving 3.936 g KOH in 15 mL H₂O, followed by sequential incorporation of 6 g C₃H₄O₂, 0.0084 g K₂S₂O₈ (initiator), and 0.009 g N,N’-methylenebisacrylamide (C7H10N2O2, 99%, Shanghai Aladdin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd.) under 40 minutes agitation. The potassium acrylate solution was then incrementally blended with the starch matrix through 1 h homogenization, with subsequent thermal curing at 80 °C for 3 h to establish covalent crosslinking. The cured hydrogel was cut into a rectangular sheet with dimensions of 2 cm × 2 cm × 1 mm (thickness = 1 mm). For the quasi-solid-state Zn²⁺/Cu²⁺ dual-ion battery, two hydrogel sheets were separately immersed in 1 M ZnSO₄ and 1 M CuSO₄ aqueous solutions to obtain the ZnSO₄ gel polymer electrolyte and CuSO₄ gel polymer electrolyte, respectively. After electrolyte uptake, the swollen gel polymer electrolyte sheets had dimensions of approximately 4 cm × 4 cm × 4 mm (thickness = 4 mm).

Synthesis of FeS2@S composite

The FeS2@S hierarchical microspheres were synthesized via a controlled hydrothermal route. In a typical procedure, 2 mmol iron(III) chloride hexahydrate (FeCl₃·6H₂O) and 4 mmol sodium thiosulfate pentahydrate (Na₂S₂O₃·5H₂O) were dissolved in a 40 mL ethylene glycol/10 mL deionized water (4:1 v/v) cosolvent system under magnetic stirring (30 min, 500 rpm). The homogeneous solution was subsequently transferred to a 100 mL PTFE-lined stainless-steel autoclave and subjected to hydrothermal treatment at 160 °C for 4 h in a forced-convection oven (heating rate: 5 °C min⁻¹). After natural cooling to ambient temperature, the product was collected via centrifugation, followed by three sequential washing cycles with ethanol and deionized water. Final drying occurred in a vacuum oven at 60 °C for 12 h, yielding phase-pure FeS₂@S microspheres with a sulfur content of 15.8 wt%.

Electrochemical measurements

The positive electrode was fabricated through a slurry-coating process using carbon cloth as the current collector. Specifically, a homogeneous slurry was prepared by thoroughly mixing FeS₂@S composite particles, Super-P carbon black, and polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) binder (99.5%, Alfa Aesar) in a 70:20:10 weight ratio in1-methyl-2pyrrolidinone (99.8%‌, Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd). This mixture was subsequently blade-coated onto a carbon paper substrate, achieving a controlled active material loading of 1.0–1.5 mg cm⁻². Following vacuum drying at 60 °C for 12 h, the resulting electrode architecture demonstrated uniform material distribution and stable mechanical integrity. All specific energy values reported in this manuscript are calculated based on the FeS₂@S composite material in the positive electrode.

The electrochemical performance of the FeS₂@S electrode was evaluated using CR2025 coin cells. In the cell configuration, A Cu foil (diameter: 10 mm, thickness: 0.1 mm, weight: 70 mg), pre-polished with 1000 mesh SiC sandpaper and cleaned with ethanol, was employed as both the current collector for the working electrode and the counter electrode. A Whatman GF/D glass fiber membrane (thickness: ~0.675 mm, diameter: ~18 mm, porosity: ~80%, average pore size: ~2.7 μm) was used as the separator. The electrolyte was precisely dosed at 70.0 ± 0.5 μL per cell. All electrodes were single-sided coated. The cells were assembled with a stainless-steel case and a non-flexible 1 mm thick gasket. Galvanostatic cycling was performed in a voltage range of 0-0.5 V vs. Cu²⁺/Cu.

A custom-designed acrylic cell was used to construct the Zn||FeS₂@S-Cu decoupled hybrid battery. In this configuration, FeS₂@S served as the positive electrode, while a zinc foil (diameter: 10 mm, thickness: 0.1 mm, weight: 55.8 mg) functioned as the negative electrode. Prior to assembly, the Zn foil was re-polished with 1000 mesh SiC sandpaper and cleaned with ethanol. The cell employed a dual-electrolyte architecture, utilizing an anion exchange membrane to spatially isolate 5 mL of 1 mol L−1 CuSO₄ and 5 mL of 1 mol L−1 ZnSO₄ solutions. This design effectively prevents Cu²⁺/Zn²⁺ crossover during charge/discharge cycling within the voltage range of 1.1-1.6 V vs. Zn²⁺/Zn.

The quasi-solid-state Zn||FeS₂@S-Cu battery was assembled in a flexible configuration. The cell comprised a Zn foil negative electrode, a FeS₂@S positive electrode (area: 4 cm × 4 cm, total active material mass: 12.32 mg), and an anion-exchange membrane separator. A gel polymer electrolyte (GPE) containing ZnSO₄ and CuSO₄ was employed to replace the liquid electrolyte, and the entire cell was vacuum-sealed. During assembly, care was taken to prevent ion crossover between the two compartments.

Electrochemical cycling tests were performed with a LANHE CT2001A (Wuhan Land Electronic Co., Ltd.) battery testing system, with cycling in the range 0-0.5 V vs. Cu2+/Cu. CV measurements were performed with a CHI 760E electrochemical workstation (Chenhua Instrument Company, Shanghai, China), covering scanning rates of 0.2-0.8 mV s−1 within potential windows consistent with GCD protocols.

GITT measurements were performed using a LAND CT2001A battery test system, applying 120 s current pulses at 0.5 A g−1 interspersed with 300 s relaxation intervals to monitor polarization dynamics. All data points for GITT tests were acquired at a frequency of 1 Hz (once every 1 s). The linearity of E vs. t1/2 during GITT pulse was verified for voltage intervals. Representative plots are shown in Supplementary Fig.32, where the linear correlation coefficients (R2) exceed 0.99, confirming that the potential-concentration linearity condition is satisfied the electrochemical experiments. Ex situ characterization

The structural and chemical evolution of electrode materials was comprehensively probed through advanced characterization techniques. Powder diffraction characterization was performed using synchrotron-based studies at SSRF, utilizing X-ray diffraction in transmission mode at the BL02U2 and BL03HB beamlines (λ = 0.6887 Å) with a data acquisition time of 10 seconds. The obtained diffraction patterns were integrated using Fit-2D software. SEM (Gemini SEM 300, Zeiss; 10 kV) and corresponding EDS resolved morphology and elemental distribution. TEM, HRTEM images, and EDS mapping were acquired using a transmission electron microscope (Tecnai G2 F20 S-TWIN). XPS spectra were tested on a Kratos Analytical Axis UltraDLD spectrometer using monochromatic Al Kα X‑ray source (1486.6 eV) with an X‑ray power of 225 W. The electrode samples were cleaned, dried, and then transferred to the XPS chamber. High‑resolution spectra were acquired with a pass energy of 20 eV. Binding energies were calibrated using the C 1 s peak at 284.6 eV as an internal reference. Confocal Raman spectroscopy (inVia Basis, Renishaw; 532 nm excitation, 1 mW power, argon atmosphere) with silicon calibration (520.7 cm−1) was employed to map vibrational modes. TGA of the FeS₂@S composite was conducted on a NETZSCH STA 449F3 at a heating rate of 10 k min−1 from 35 °C to 800 °C under N2 atmosphere. To probe the structural evolution, post-cycling electrodes were subjected to synchrotron-based X-ray absorption spectroscopy (XAS) at the Shanghai Synchrotron Radiation Facility (SSRF). Prior to measurement, the electrodes underwent a three-step purification protocol: ultrasonication in deionized water, sequential solvent exchange with anhydrous ethanol, and vacuum drying at 40 °C, followed by immediate vacuum sealing to preserve the surface state. Fe K-edge and Cu K-edge XAS measurements were conducted at the BL11B beamline, while S K-edge XAS was performed at the BL16U1 beamline. The energy calibration for each absorption edge was referenced to standard foils (Fe foil for the Fe K-edge, Cu foil for the Cu K-edge, and sulfur for the S K-edge). Data processing, including background subtraction and normalization, was performed using the ATHENA software package to ensure a systematic analysis of the structural-electrochemical correlations.

Operando synchrotron characterization

Time-resolved structural evolution during electrochemical cycling was investigated using synchrotron X-rays (λ = 0.6887 Å, 18.05 keV) at the BL02U2 beamline of the Shanghai Synchrotron Radiation Facility (SSRF). Diffraction patterns were continuously captured by a PILATUS3S 2 M detector, followed by azimuthal integration via Fit-2D software to generate quantitative 1D diffractograms. The custom electrochemical CR2025 coin cell was equipped with an X-ray transmission window (2.0±0.05 mm diameter) aligned by laser collimation and sealed with a 25 µm polyimide film (Kapton® HN). The electrochemical unit employed a FeS₂@S self-supporting electrode (areal loading: 2-3 mg cm−2), operated under galvanostatic cycling (0.5 A g−1) using a LAND CT2001A system, with ambient temperature maintained at 25.0 °C ± 0.5 °C.

Calculation

First-principles calculations were conducted employing the Vienna Ab Initio Simulation Package (VASP) with the projector-augmented wave (PAW) pseudopotential framework73,74. Electron exchange-correlation interactions were modeled using the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) generalized gradient approximation (GGA)75, augmented by DFT-D3 dispersion corrections to account for van der Waals interactions76. A plane-wave cutoff energy of 450 eV and Gamma center k-meshes (3×3×1 for FeS₂ systems) ensured convergence accuracy <1 meV/atom in calculations. The U-values were set to 2.5 and 6 eV for Fe and Cu elements since the presence of d-orbital electrons77,78. Structural relaxations were performed with a force convergence threshold of 0.03 eV Å−1. To simulate the surface, a slab model with four atomic layers was used, with the bottom two layers fixed to represent the bulk substrate constraint79,80. The detailed parameters of the simulated structural model are available in Supplementary Data 1–3. The adsorption energy (∆Eads) of Cu absorbed on the pristine and layer was calculated from the total electronic energy differences according to the equation: ∆Eads = Esub-Cu − Esub − ECu, where Esub-Cu and Esub are the total energy of pristine or defective FeS2@S with and without Cu2+ intercalation, respectively; ECu is the total energy of Cu atom derived from its bulk phase. The charge density difference (ρdiff) was based this equipment: ρdiff = ρAB − ρA − ρB, while ρAB, ρA and ρB was the charge density of Cu adsorbed at FeS2@S model, FeS2@S model and Cu model, respectively.

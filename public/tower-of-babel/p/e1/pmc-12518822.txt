Predicting road adhesion coefficient with a fusion strategy of SHAP dynamic parameters
Authors: Yi Gong, Qiuyue Du, Fei Wang, Limei Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Accurately estimating the road adhesion coefficient is essential for ensuring vehicle driving safety. This study proposes a method that integrates a seven-degree-of-freedom vehicle model and the magic formula tire model with the eXtreme Gradient Boosting algorithm, leveraging Shapley Additive exPlanations values to determine feature importance. Based on this, a road adhesion coefficient estimation model is constructed, incorporating a Bayesian neural network and a Support vector regression to enhance interpretability. To address potential issues such as the model predicting road adhesion coefficients beyond the range present in the training data, a data fusion strategy is designed to improve generalization. Simulation results demonstrate that the fusion estimator outperforms individual models in terms of robustness, real-time performance, and prediction accuracy, especially under varying vehicle speeds and road conditions. Compared to a single estimator, the data fusion model reduced the mean square error by an average of 16.01% and 18.89%, respectively, while the root mean square error was reduced by an average of 11.33% and 16.04%, respectively. This approach provides valuable theoretical support to advance the development of vehicle active safety systems.

Introduction

In recent years, the rapid development of the global economy and the automobile industry has also brought about problems such as a surge in urban car ownership, increased traffic congestion, and frequent traffic accidents. The vehicle longitudinal safety assistance system represented by active collision avoidance and adaptive cruise is of great significance for improving the overall traffic environment, reducing the incidence of traffic accidents, and improving driving comfort. The road adhesion coefficient is one of the important input parameters of a vehicle active control system1. The real-time, accuracy and interpretability of the prediction model of road adhesion coefficient directly affect the performance of vehicle dynamics system control. Fast and accurate estimation of road adhesion coefficient is an important technical prerequisite for establishing a safety strategy model of vehicle longitudinal safety auxiliary system that meets the actual requirements.

With the continuous development of vehicle research technology and artificial intelligence technology, more and more data-driven intelligent algorithms are used to estimate the road adhesion coefficient. Lin et al.2 designed a Keras deep learning model that combines clipping recursive average filtering algorithm, K-fold verification, Sarsa reinforcement learning, and Dropout regularization to solve the problem that the adhesion coefficient estimated by the neural network model exceeds the boundary; Li et al.3 designed a joint estimator of sideslip angle and road adhesion coefficient based on an extended Kalman filter improved radial basis neural network, which shows strong robustness when the vehicle speed and road characteristics change; Zhang et al.4 established the mathematical model of permanent magnet synchronous motor and 3-DOF vehicle dynamics model and improved the strong tracking cubature Kalman filter algorithm to overcome the problem of non-local sampling in dealing with high-dimensional systems. The rotor speed signal obtained in the control of the permanent magnet synchronous motor was used instead of the wheel angular velocity sensor and a sensor-less road adhesion coefficient estimator was designed; Gao et al.5 proposed a two-layer model for estimating the road adhesion coefficient of an articulated steering vehicle (ASV). The upper layer of the model includes lateral, longitudinal, tire force, and steering system. The lateral and longitudinal tire forces are estimated by the Kalman filter. The lower layer uses the recursive least squares (RLS) method with the forgetting factor to estimate the tire braking stiffness, tire turning stiffness, and road adhesion coefficient based on the tire model; Li et al.6 used CarSim simulation to expand the vehicle data set of the vehicle road network cloud and established a combined model of road adhesion coefficient estimation based on self-attention (SA), convolutional neural network (CNN) and long short-term memory (LSTM). Finally, the Q-learning method was used to optimize the weight of the model; Xu et al.7 introduced the stress distribution and multi-point contact into the vertical dynamic model, established a new tire model driven by dimensionless data based on the normalization method, and used the unscented Kalman filter (UKF) to realize the real-time evaluation of the lateral and longitudinal adhesion coefficients of the three-dimensional road surface. Finally, according to the coupling relationship between the lateral and longitudinal coefficients, a fuzzy strategy for the fusion of the lateral and longitudinal coefficients was designed; Zhu et al.8 established a three-degree-of-freedom vehicle dynamics model and a brush tire model. The particle swarm optimization (PSO) algorithm with adaptive inertia weight was used to identify the horizontal and longitudinal stiffness of the tire offline. Referring to the adhesion coefficient formula of the Burckhardt tire model, the extended forgetting factor recursive least squares (EFRLS) was used to estimate the road adhesion coefficient; Sulakshan et al.9 considered the nonlinear relationship between friction and slip. Based on the coupling relationship between vehicle dynamics, wheel dynamics, and suspension dynamics, the road slope and the effective radius of the tire were used as the estimated states. A first-order sliding mode observer with higher accuracy for slip and road adhesion coefficient estimation was designed; Zhang et al.10 used DGPS and three IMUs to estimate the longitudinal tire force through the output torque and speed of the motor under straight-line driving conditions and used the particle filter and the nonlinear relationship between the longitudinal tire force and the road adhesion coefficient for friction estimation. Under turning conditions, three IMUs and DGPS were used to estimate the lateral tire force and the vehicle sideslip angle, and the road adhesion coefficient estimator was established based on the lateral tire characteristics; Based on the three-degree-of-freedom vehicle model, the dual-motor torque output model, and the Dugoff tire model, Xiong et al.11 used fuzzy logic to correct the measurement noise in the cubature Kalman filter algorithm and then used the ant colony algorithm to optimize the input and output membership functions. A filtering algorithm was proposed to solve the signal noise problem in the driving state estimation process of two-axis electric vehicles and improve the estimation accuracy of the road adhesion coefficient; Gao et al.12 used tire test data to improve the accuracy of the brush tire model. Based on the vehicle dynamics and kinematics model, a nonlinear observer was designed to estimate the road adhesion coefficient during steering by using the aligning torque, lateral acceleration, and lateral displacement of the wheel; Zhang et al.13 proposed a prediction method for forward road adhesion coefficient based on image recognition. DeeplabV3 + , semantic segmentation network, and MobilNetV2 lightweight convolutional neural network were used to realize road segmentation and accurate identification of road types; Leng et al.14 designed a disturbance observer based on the improved Burckhardt tire model and a machine vision method that extracts road features through colour moment and grey level co-occurrence matrix and uses support vector machine classification, which significantly improves the estimation accuracy and convergence speed; Wu et al.15 proposed a multi-module self-organizing feedforward neural network (LM-MMSOFNN) based on an improved Levenberg–Marquardt (LM) learning algorithm for online road adhesion coefficient estimation. The extended Kalman filter (EKF) and moving average (MA) were used to denoise the measured signal. At the same time, by adding and reducing self-organizing neurons and improving the LM learning algorithm, the computational complexity and system hardware storage requirements are reduced.

In this paper, we designed two independent estimators and a data fusion estimator based on a fusion strategy based on two data-driven machine learning models. Firstly, the dynamic analysis of the whole vehicle is carried out, and the nonlinear seven-degree-of-freedom model and magic formula tire model are established. Secondly, the data are collected by simulating the random combination of working conditions and vehicle speed, and the SHAP value of the collected data is calculated by the XG-Boost model, and the features with higher contribution are selected as the input of the model. Then the training set, Bayesian neural network model, support vector regression model, and a data fusion strategy are established. Finally, the effectiveness of the model is verified by simulation experiments of various combinations of vehicle speed and road conditions.

Feature extraction and result interpretation

XG-Boost (eXtreme Gradient Boosting) is a gradient boosting decision tree model that enhances prediction accuracy by iteratively training multiple decision trees and integrating their results using gradient boosting techniques16.

During model training, XG-Boost enhances performance by reducing the loss function.27\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$L\left( \Psi \right) = \sum\limits_{i = 1}^{n} {\phi \left( {z_{i} ,\hat{z}_{i} } \right)} + \sum\limits_{t = 1}^{T} \Gamma \left( {g_{t} } \right)$$\end{document}

In the formula, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Psi$$\end{document} is the parameter of the model; \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$n$$\end{document} denotes the number of training samples; \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\phi \left( {z_{i} ,\hat{z}_{i} } \right)$$\end{document} is the loss function to measure the difference between the true value \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$z_{i}$$\end{document} and the predicted value \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\hat{z}_{i}$$\end{document}; \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Gamma \left( {g_{t} } \right)$$\end{document} serves as a regularization term, enhancing the model’s generalization ability and preventing overfitting.

The regularization term of XG-Boost consists of two parts: one is the penalty term of the complexity of the tree, and the other is the penalty term of the leaf node score. It can be expressed by the following combination of mathematical formulas:28\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Gamma \left( {g_{t} } \right) = \gamma T + \frac{1}{2}\eta \sum\limits_{j = 1}^{T} {\psi_{j}^{2} }$$\end{document}

In this context, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T$$\end{document} is the count of leaf nodes, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\psi_{j}$$\end{document} signifies the score of leaf node \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$j$$\end{document}; \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\gamma$$\end{document} is the penalty coefficient of tree complexity; \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\eta$$\end{document} is the penalty coefficient of the leaf node fraction.

XG-Boost uses the gradient boosting algorithm to gradually optimize the model, and its specific process is as follows: 1. Initialize the model: Begin with a basic decision tree as the initial model. 2. Compute the error: For regression tasks, the error is the difference between the predicted and actual values, while for classification tasks, it is the deviation between the predicted and true labels. This error serves as the basis for training the next model to progressively minimize discrepancies. 3. Build a new decision tree: Train a decision tree specifically to capture the residual errors from the previous iteration, refining the model’s predictive accuracy. 4. Update the model: Incorporate the newly trained tree into the existing model, adjusting parameters through gradient descent to further reduce errors on the training dataset. 5. Iterate the process: Continuously repeat error computation, decision tree construction, and model updating until either the predefined iteration limit is reached or the optimal solution is achieved. 6. Generate the final prediction: Aggregate the weighted outputs of all decision trees to produce the final prediction result.

In this paper, XG-Boost is used to establish a regression model. According to the in-put dynamic characteristic data, the relationship between the feature and the target variable road adhesion coefficient is learned, so that the target variable of the unknown data can be predicted.

SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) is a game theory-based method for determining the impact of each feature on the model prediction results17. In this paper, it is used to explain the prediction results of the XG-Boost model to explain the contribution of the model to each feature. SHAP value can be expressed by mathematical formula:29\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\phi_{i} = \sum\limits_{{S \subseteq \left\{ {1,2,3,...,p} \right\}\backslash \left\{ i \right\}}} {\frac{{\left| S \right|!\left( {p - \left| S \right| - 1} \right)!}}{p!}} \left[ {f\left( {S \cup \left\{ i \right\}} \right) - f\left( S \right)} \right]$$\end{document}

In the formula, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\phi_{i}$$\end{document} represents the SHAP value of the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$i$$\end{document} feature; \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S$$\end{document} represents the feature subset and does not contain the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$i$$\end{document} feature; \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$p$$\end{document} represents the total number of features; \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$f\left( S \right)$$\end{document} represents the prediction made by the model under the condition of a given feature subset.

Road adhesion coefficient estimator

The estimation framework for the road adhesion coefficient comprises three key components: a Bayesian neural network estimator, a Support vector regression estimator, and a data fusion estimator. Bayesian neural network introduces the concept of Bayesian inference in neural networks and optimizes the parameters of neural networks as random variables. The model can provide uncertainty estimation of prediction results and realize the quantification of uncertainty. Using a neural network alone will inevitably lead to the over-fitting phenomenon that the prediction result is less than 0 or greater than 118. SVR model is an application of Support Vector Machine in regression tasks. Meanwhile, the support vector machine exhibits strong generalization capability and robustness, making it well-suited for handling datasets with intricate feature distributions.

Dataset creation

Before training the machine learning model, data collection is essential. To enhance model accuracy, it is crucial to simulate and gather data under varying vehicle speeds and road adhesion conditions. The working conditions include straight-line driving, fixed clockwise steering, and fixed counterclockwise steering, with circular steering diameters of 10 m, 20 m, 50 m, and 100 m. Vehicle speeds are set at 10 km/h, 20 km/h, …, up to 120 km/h, while road adhesion coefficients range from 0.1 to 1.0 in increments of 0.1. The total simulation duration is 100 s.

BNN estimator

Bayesian Neural Network (BNN) introduces probability distribution in weight, which can provide uncertainty estimation for prediction19. The weight of the traditional neural network is a determined value, which is usually learned by optimization algorithms such as backpropagation. The Bayesian neural network models the uncertainty of the weight, and its weight is a probability distribution. The Bayesian neural network model structure shows that the network structure includes an input layer, a hidden layer, and an output layer. As shown in the Fig. 4, the input layer contains 9 neurons, corresponding to the input of 9 dynamic parameters, the hidden layer contains 100 neurons, and the last layer of the output layer contains a neuron as the road adhesion coefficient predicted by the model. The activation function selects the ReLU function, the optimization solver is Adam, the maximum number of iterations is 1000, and the model prediction performance is evaluated by Mean Absolute Error (MAE) and Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE). The output form of the prediction model is the mathematical expectation \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mu_{BNN}$$\end{document} and the standard deviation \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sigma_{BNN}$$\end{document} fitted according to the confidence interval.Fig. 4Bayesian neural network model architecture.

Data Fusion delivers the most stable and accurate results, closely following the ground truth across all tested scenarios. It effectively suppresses the BNN’s overshooting while compensating for SVR’s bias.

Conclusion

In this study, a dynamic data fusion estimator for road adhesion coefficient is pro-posed using a data-driven approach. First, a nonlinear vehicle model and the magic formula tire model are developed. The XG-Boost model is applied to compute SHAP values, identifying high-contribution features to enhance prediction accuracy and model interpretability. Next, independent BNN and SVR estimator models are constructed, and a data fusion strategy is designed to integrate both models effectively. Finally, simulation experiments under various working conditions demonstrate that the fusion estimator significantly reduces MAE and RMSE compared to single estimators, offering improved pre-diction accuracy, real-time performance, and robustness. However, under some working conditions and vehicle speeds, there is a situation where the fusion estimator error is greater than a single estimator. In the next research, a more reliable fusion strategy can be designed, and a filtering algorithm can be added to the model to collect more accurate dynamic data. Future research will focus on several key areas to enhance the model’s practical applicability and real-world performance. First, a more reliable fusion strategy will be developed by incorporating adaptive weighting mechanisms based on real-time confidence levels to dynamically adjust the contributions of each estimator. Additionally, an advanced filtering algorithm, such as an adaptive Kalman filter, will be introduced to refine dynamic data collection and minimize estimation lag. To bridge the gap between simulation and real-world application, future studies will incorporate real-world experimental data using instrumented vehicles or tire testing rigs. These experiments will provide ground truth adhesion measurements under various road conditions, including surface texture variations, tire wear, inflation levels, and environmental factors such as weather and contaminants. The inclusion of real-world data will enhance model generalization and ensure practical applicability. Moreover, road adhesion is not a static property but dynamically evolves over time due to factors such as braking, acceleration, and road surface changes. Future work will focus on modelling the temporal evolution of adhesion using time-series analysis to im-prove the model’s predictive capabilities in dynamic driving scenarios. Another promising direction is integrating vision-based road surface recognition. A vehicle-mounted camera can capture re-al-time images of the road ahead, and a lightweight deep learning model can process these images to predict road conditions. By fusing vision-based and dynamics-based estimation methods, a more robust and proactive adhesion prediction system can be developed. Additionally, future studies will explore the relationship between estimated adhesion and required adhesion for vehicle stability. By incorporating theoretical benchmarks—such as the adhesion required for stable cornering based on speed and curve radius—the model’s findings can be directly linked to vehicle safety, enabling more informed decision-making for advanced driver assistance systems (ADAS) and autonomous vehicles. By addressing these limitations and expanding the model’s capabilities, future research will move toward a high-precision, real-time, and adaptive road adhesion estimation framework suitable for real-world automotive applications, improving both safety and performance in intelligent transportation systems.

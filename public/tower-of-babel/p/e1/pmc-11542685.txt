Comparison of the Effect of Auriculotherapy, a Herbal Compound (Aslagh Capsule), and Metformin on the Clinical Symptoms of Polycystic Ovary Syndrome: Randomized Clinical Trial
Authors: Razieh Ghanbarian, Mahboubeh Valiani, Zahra Allameh, Maryam Ranjbar, Mohammad Mazaheri
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Background:Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) is an endocrine disorder that affects women of child-bearing age, with a worldwide prevalence ranging from 6 to 20%. Considering the potential side effects of chemical medications and the need for long-term drug administration, complementary treatments such as acupuncture, auriculotherapy, and herbal medicine have been considered. Hence, this study was designed to compare the effects of auriculotherapy, a herbal component (Aslagh capsule), and metformin on the clinical symptoms of PCOS.Materials and Methods:This is a randomized controlled clinical trial study, which allocated 24 women, aged 18–43 years old, with oligomenorrhea in PCOs, in a 1:1:1 ratio, to three groups.Result:A decreasing pattern in the duration of the menstrual cycle was observed in all three groups, but this was significant only in the auriculotherapy group. There were no significant differences between the three groups in acne and hirsutism scores. However, there was a significant reduction in excess hair growth and density of hair in the auriculotherapy group compared to the other two groups (P < 0.05). Besides, in the auriculotherapy group, the reduction in thickness of excess hair at the end of the second and third months of treatment were marginally significant compared to the two other groups.Conclusions:Auriculotherapy and Aslagh capsule can be effective in reducing PCOS symptoms, similar to metformin. However, auriculotherapy is more effective than the other treatments in reducing the duration of the menstrual cycle, excess hair growth, and hair density.

INTRODUCTION

Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) is an endocrine disorder that affects women of child-bearing age, with a worldwide prevalence ranging from 6% to 20%.[123] Besides, this syndrome has been identified as the most common cause of anovulatory infertility, accounting for approximately 70% of all cases.[45]

In addition, PCOS patients are highly susceptible to a variety of health issues, including diabetes, dyslipidemia, obesity, cardiovascular diseases, hypertension, metabolic syndrome, pregnancy complications, obstructive sleep apnea, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, endometrial cancer, depression, and anxiety.[67]

Treatment of PCOS in adults is initially symptomatic and focuses on addressing the main manifestations, including abnormal bleeding, irregular menstruation, and cutaneous hyperandrogenism (such as hirsutism, persistent acne, obesity, and insulin resistance).[8]

Pharmacologic treatments of PCOS consist of oral insulin-sensitizing medications and contraceptives. However, each of these treatments has a wide range of side effects. A case in point, metformin, can cause gastrointestinal issues such as diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, bloating, indigestion, and abdominal discomfort. It also reduces the absorption of vitamin B12 in 30% of patients.[49]

The side effects of oral contraceptives range from nausea, headache, abdominal cramps, chest pain, increased vaginal discharge, and decreased libido to serious complications such as vascular clots, gallbladder disease, heart attack, hypertension, liver cancer, and stroke.[9]

Regarding the side effects of chemical medications and long-term drug administration, acupuncture, auriculotherapy, and herbal medicine have been considered as complementary treatment.

Today, traditional and complementary medicine is widely accepted worldwide, and the lucrative economic revenue in this field has captured the attention of many countries.[10]

Body acupuncture, a branch of traditional Chinese medicine, is now widely used in Western society so that one of the three Americans is using acupuncture.[11]

Ear acupuncture or auriculotherapy is the most applicable microsystem following the body acupuncture.[12] Auriculotherapy is an easy, inexpensive, non-invasive, and straightforward treatment.[13]

Additionally, in recent years, herbal medicine has experienced a resurgence due to the side effects of chemical drugs, lack of curative effects of classic treatment, and microbial resistance.[1415]

One of the herbal products that were used for the treatment of PCOS symptoms like oligomenorrhea and amenorrhea in Persian medicine (Iranian traditional medicine) manuscripts is Aslagh capsule containing Vitexagnus Castus L, Foeniculum vulgare Mill., and Daucus carota.[1617]

Vitexagnus Castus L is a well-known plant used for treating gynecological problems, including premenstrual syndrome, irregular menstrual cycles, and menstrual disorders related to corpus luteum.[1819]

Foeniculum vulgare, commonly known as fennel, is a well-known herbal plant that is used for various gynecological conditions in folk medicine. It is believed to help with dysmenorrhea, milk secretion, and menopause symptoms and can increase libido. Additionally, it is often used as an emmenagogue in women with amenorrhea and oligomenorrhea.[20]

Daucus carota, a popular vegetable worldwide, is a rich source of beta-carotene and anthocyanin and has hepatoprotective and antioxidant activities. In folkmedicine, carrot seeds have been used as a diuretic and carminative and for stimulation of menstruation flow.[16]

Due to the high prevalence of PCOS, its complications, and the side effects of medical treatments, as well as the limited number of studies on the effects of the herbal compound and auriculotherapy mentioned above, this research was conducted.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study design

This study is a randomized controlled clinical trial conducted from June 2020 to December 2021 in Amin hospital, Isfahan University of Medical Sciences, Iran. This study was approved by the ethics committee of Isfahan University of Medical Sciences (IR.MUI.MED.REC.1398.688) and registered in the Iranian registry of clinical trial (IRCT20200407046980N1).

Study population and participant eligibility criteria

Individuals were selected from patients who approached to the Amin hospital and diagnosed by a gynecologist.

Women were included if they were 18–43 years old, with oligomenorrhea (menstrual cycle more than 35 days) due to PCOS (based on Rotterdam criteria).

Hyperprolactinemia, hypothyroidism, adrenal insufficiency, Cushing’s disease, diabetes, breastfeeding, taking dopaminergic medication, and history of medical treatment for PCOS in the past month were considered as non-inclusion criteria.

Patients were advised to not make significant lifestyle changes during the study in terms of exercise, sleep, diet, and excretion patterns such as laxative consumption.

Patients were excluded from the study in case of up to three consecutive sessions of auriculotherapy withdrawal, not taking Aslagh capsule or metformin for 1 week, having non-tolerable side effects from any of the treatments, or no more tendency to continue the study.

Sample size

The sample size was estimated considering a significance level of 5% (z = 1.96), a statistical power of 80%, and z = 0.84 for detecting of standardized effect size of 0.9.[16] The population size was calculated to be 20 people in each group. The required sample size was estimated to be 24 people in each group.

Randomization procedure

Randomization was performed using random digit numbers generated by SPSS software for a block size of 3. Patients were allocated into one of three study groups (auriculotherapy group as group 1, Aslagh group as group 2, metformin group as the control group) based on this randomization method and an allocation ratio of 1:1:1. The random allocation was done by a midwife.

Preparation of the medication

Aslagh capsules (combination of V. agnus-castus L., F. vulgare Mill., and D. carota) were produced by Talaye Sabz Toba Company with a registration number of 462264 from the Iranian Food and Drug Administration and a license number of 0352-94-S. Each 500 mg capsule contained all three components in equal proportion. On the other hand, all the capsules required for this study have been prepared from one batch. According to the results of the Toba company’s laboratory, each capsule encompassed 115±0/5 mg/g quercetin.

Metformin 500 mg tablets were purchased from an Iranian pharmaceutical company (Aria Co., Tehran).

Participant

At first, 111 patients were assessed for eligibility of inclusion and non-inclusion criteria; finally, 72 patients were included in the study. Twenty-four women aged between 18 and 43 with PCOS diagnosis based on Rotterdam criteria were enrolled to each group randomly. Prior to the intervention, the patient signed the informed consent [Figure 1].

Intervention and outcomes assessment

For the first group, auriculotherapy was performed to stimulate the ear points related to PCOS using a Pointer Excel II electric device for 30 seconds (manufactured by Tens Plus company made by Hong Kong), followed by putting vaccaria seeds in the relevant points. Visiting times for performing auriculotherapy were at the start point of study and then once a week up to 3 months, except for the menstruation period. Each patient was instructed to press each seed for 1 minute every hour (except for sleeping time).

Related points based on the menstrual cycle were included: In the follicular phase, uterus, lung I, lung II, (based on Chinese map), anti-depressant point (based on European map), master skin, and liver points were included for the right ear. Ovary 1, kidney (based on Chinese map), ovary 2, hypothalamus, hypophysis (based on European map), and shenmen were included for the left ear.

In the luteal phase, ovary, uterus, liver, lung I, lung II (based on Chinese map), anti-depressant point (based on European), and liver for the right ear and progesterone (based on European), kidney (based on Chinese), and shenmen for the left ear were included.[12]

Group 2 received Aslagh, four capsules daily divided into two doses, in the morning while fasting and at night before bedtime, except for the menstruation period. In patients without menstruation, one medication-free week following the third week of treatment is necessary.

Group 3 utilized metformin, one tablet three times a day (TDS) after meal. In order to reduce the incidence and severity of gastrointestinal side effects, all patients in the metformin group were supposed to start the initial dose of 500 mg after dinner for at least 1 week, gradually increasing to a final dose of 500 mg TDS.

Three months intervention and 1 month follow-up were applied for the three groups. The subjects were asked not to use other PCOS management and keep their usual diet and lifestyle during the study. Also, participants were in contact with the researcher and they were free to withdraw the intervention at any time during the study.

Data collection

Participants were evaluated in the first session and then every month until the end of the fourth month using the checklist. In the first session, a checklist was developed by the researchers to characterize the subjects, with demographic aspects and evaluating the basic clinical symptoms in the last three months. The clinical symptoms checklist included the number of menstruation occurrences; the average duration of the menstrual cycle in the last 3 months; changes in the thickness, growth, and density of excess hair in the last month; acne score based on the global acne grading system (GAGS);[21] and hirsutism score based on Ferryman Gall way table.[6] At the end of each month, the clinical symptom checklist and acne and hirsutism scores were filled until the end of the fourth month.

Outcome

The menstruation occurrence and decreased menstrual cycle duration were considered as the primary outcomes. Three times, menstruation occurrence during the intervention period was considered as a regular menses. Also, the secondary outcomes were consisting other symptoms including acne and hirsutism and changes in the thickness, growth, and density of excess hair.

Statistical analysis

Numerical variables will be reported with mean and standard deviation, and non-numerical variables with number and percentage. The changes of numerical variables over the time in each group and between groups were evaluated with variance analysis of repeated measures. For numerical discrete variables (number) of cycles, Wilcoxon signed rank test was used to measure changes over the time. Numerical variables were compared between the groups at each time point with analysis of variance (ANOVA). Comparison of the non-numerical variables between groups was done with Cochran’s test. SPSS statistical software version 21 was used for data analysis. A P value < 0.05 was considered as statistically significant.

DISCUSSION

In this study, it was found that auriculotherapy and Aslagh capsule can be considered effective interventions in improving the clinical symptoms of PCOS, including oligomenorrhea, hirsutism, and acne. However, considering the reduction of excess hair thickness, hair growth, and density, auriculotherapy and Aslagh capsule were found to be more effective than metformin.

The etiology of menstrual irregularities in PCOS is constant secretion of estrogen from the uterus and lack of progesterone production.[22] Auriculotherapy by regulating the hormone level and neurotransmitters in the body and brain can affect these irregularities.[23] Many studies reported that auriculotherapy is effective in reducing stress, which is one of the major factors that cause irregular menstruation. Stress increases prolactin and cortisone, leading to the disruption of ovulation, and can directly affect the levels of sex hormones.[12]

Among the herbal remedies used for the treatment of PCOS symptoms, Aslagh capsule is used for the management of oligomenorrhea and amenorrhea in Iranian traditional clinics.[17]

While oral contraceptives serve as the initial choice for treatment for menstrual irregularities in PCOS patients, a considerable proportion of patients show preference against their usage. In fact, they referred for treatment of infertility, and the first line of treatment in these patients was metformin in our recruitment center. Metformin has a significant role in the fertility of these patients due to the insulin resistance reduction, restoring regular menstruation with ovulation, and weight loss in patients. Therefore, we considered metformin as a control in the study.[16]

During the 3 months of treatment, menstrual cycle duration decreased in all three intervention groups, with significant reduction observed in the auriculotherapy group. In the follow-up month, menstrual cycle duration in the auriculotherapy group increased compared to the third month. This suggests that auriculotherapy might require a treatment duration longer than 3 months. Conversely, in the other two groups, no increase in the menstrual cycle duration was observed after the treatment was discontinued.

A study conducted by Mokaberinejad et al. on patients with oligomenorrhea secondary to PCOS demonstrated that the combination of fennel seed infusion and dry cupping was as effective as metformin and safe in reducing the interval between two menstrual cycles for managing oligomenorrhea. This finding aligns with our study.[24]

In our study, the number of menstruation occurrences increased across all three groups after the intervention. 58.8% in the auriculotherapy group, 47.1% in the Aslagh group, and 44.4% in the metformin group experienced three menstruation occurrences.

In the Khaki et al. study, following a 3-month treatment, 73.3% of the participants in the auriculotherapy group and 90% in the medication group did not experience menstrual disorders such as oligomenorrhea or polymenorrhea. The divergence in the outcomes concerning the improvement of the menstruation cycle between these two studies could be attributed to the methodology of auriculotherapy. In the Khaki et al. study, auriculotherapy was administered every other day, whereas the medication group consumed specific treatments for PCOS, including contraceptives and cyproterone compounds, potentially contributing to more favorable results.[23]

Parallel to our investigation, Bahman et al. reported 67.7% in the Aslagh group; 53.3% in the metformin group experienced two or more with menstruation occurrence within the 3 months intervention period. However, the increase in menstrual frequency was not significant statistically in any of the groups.[16]

In Mohebbi-Kian et al.’s study, comparing the administration of fennel oil capsules and combined oral contraceptive and placebo on menstruation on women with DMPA (depot medroxyprogesterone acetate)-induced amenorrhea, the occurrence of menstruation was reported as 73% in the fennel group, 81% in the combined oral contraceptive group, and 19% in the placebo group. The higher occurrence of menstruation in the fennel group in this study can be probably due to the different designs of the study.[25]

In Khaki et al.’s study, the hirsutism score within the auriculotherapy group decreased after 3 months of the intervention, which aligns with our finding. In the metformin group in their study, the hirsutism score increased, which is consistent with the results of our study.[26]

In our study, the hirsutism score in the Aslagh group decreased at the end of intervention. In the follow-up month, the hirsutism score did not change compared to the third month. No similar study was found to investigate the effect of Aslagh capsule or the oral consumption of the three ingredients of this capsule, including fennel, vitex, and carrot seed, on hirsutism.

Alterations in hair thickness were assessed through subjective reporting. Across all three groups, a decrease in the thickness of excessive hair was noted during the treatment phase. However, in the follow-up month, this difference was not significant. According to the results, it seems that longer intervention duration is needed to change the condition of hair growth.

In a study conducted by Javidnia et al., 1% and 2% fennel gel were used topically for 12 weeks for women with idiopathic hirsutism, and finally, the thickness of extra hair reduced 7.8% and 18.3%, respectively.[27]

In another study by Akha et al., 3% topical fennel gel was applied during 24 weeks in women with idiopathic hirsutism. The thickness of excess hair was estimated to decrease by 22.7% after intervention.[28]

The density of excess hair and excess hair growth were decreased significantly in the auriculotherapy group.

In a previous before–after study, acupuncture reduced the density and length of hair in ectopic hypertrichosis. Additionally, this treatment reduced plasma testosterone up to 70% and decreased urinary 17-hydroxycorticosteroid and 17-hydroxycorticosterone concentrations.[29]

The acne score in Aslagh and metformin groups decreased less than in the auriculotherapy group.

In the study by Khaki et al., following a 3-month intervention, the auriculotherapy group exhibited a partial recovery rate of 78.9% and a complete recovery rate of 21.1%, while the medication group reported a partial recovery rate of 43.8% and a complete recovery rate of 18.7%.[23] Consequently, these findings closely parallel our own. Notably, the marked reduction in acne observed in Khaki et al.’s study may be attributed to factors such as a higher frequency of auriculotherapy sessions and different techniques used to assess acne. Auriculotherapy contributes to the regulation of hormonal imbalances in PCOS, thereby leading to a reduction in acne among affected patients.

Hence, one of the three compounds of Aslagh capsule is vitex and may be effective in acne score. In several studies, the anti-acne effect of the vitex plant has been mentioned.[30] Conversely, existing research indicates that metformin’s effectiveness in reducing acne among PCOS patients is linked to its role in diminishing hyperandrogenism, a result that aligns with the outcomes of our study.[31]

In the follow-up month, the acne score did not change much in the two groups of Aslagh and metformin, but it increased slightly in the auriculotherapy group.

In future studies, it is suggested to consider the role of lifestyle in treatment of PCOS and participants are evaluated by laboratory and ultrasound examinations too.

CONCLUSION

Based on the current research, auriculotherapy has been found to be a potentially beneficial treatment for PCOS-related oligomenorrhea. Unlike metformin, this method is considered safe and does not have any notable adverse effects. Metformin, on the other hand, often leads to gastrointestinal complications that can sometimes result in discontinuing the treatment.

Furthermore, the Aslagh herbal capsule displays comparable effectiveness to metformin in shortening the menstrual cycle. Additionally, unlike metformin, this capsule contains fennel, which may contribute to alleviating digestive issues.

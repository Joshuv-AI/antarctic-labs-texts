Poor emotional well-being and energy are associated with mortality in patients with advanced liver failure
Authors: Shruti Shapuram, Nikita Gunnala, Hanisha Guta Artham, Shantan Venishetty, Sarika Kolli, Sumana Kolar, Anveshi Satyavadhi, Santhosh Reddy Satti, Mithun Sharma, Manasa Alla, Sowmya Iyengar, Padaki Nagaraja Rao, Duvvur Nageshwar Reddy, Anand V. Kulkarni
Published: 2024

INTRODUCTION

Decompensated cirrhosis (DC) and acute-on-chronic liver failure (ACLF) are distinct liver failure syndromes associated with poor outcomes. 1 Several studies have reported that the health-related quality of life (HRQOL) of patients with DC is poorer compared to the general population and is dependent on the severity of liver disease.2,3 To date, only 1 study has reported that patients with ACLF (identified by the European Association for the Study of Liver-Consortium for Chronic Liver Failure [EASL-CLIF] criteria) have poorer QOL compared to patients with DC. However, the study included only 63 hospitalized patients, of which 23 were ACLF, and the rest were DC. 4 Furthermore, no studies have compared the change in HRQOL after liver transplantation (LT) in these patients with advanced liver disease. There have been no studies comparing ACLF identified by the Asian Pacific Association for the Study of the Liver (APASL) criteria with patients with DC, which we aimed to assess.

METHODS

Consecutive hospitalized patients for nonemergent indications were included in the study. We excluded patients with HE and those admitted to the intensive care unit. The main objectives of the study were to compare the HRQOL of patients with ACLF and DC, assess the change in HRQOL after LT on day 30, and lastly, assess the predictors of mortality. Patients were telephonically contacted on day 30 for outcomes for those not undergoing LT. The study was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee vide letter number AIG/IEC-BH&R 38/12.2022-08. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declarations of Helsinki and Istanbul, and informed consent was obtained from all patients. HRQOL was assessed by Short Form Survey Instrument-36 (SF-36), which assesses the QOL in 8 domains: 4 each in the physical component summary and mental component summary. 5 ACLF was defined as per APASL criteria, and patients with a prior known history of decompensations were considered as DC.1,6 Apart from SF-36–based HRQOL, additional details regarding diagnosis, etiology, age, and baseline biochemical variables required for severity score measurement were recorded.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS ver. 29 (IBM Corp.). Continuous variables are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median (IQR) and compared using the Student t test for parametric variables and Mann-Whitney U test for nonparametric variables. Categorical variables are expressed as proportions (n, %) and compared using the chi-square test. We assessed the age and severity score adjusted HR (with a 95% CI) for mortality for various components of SF-36.

RESULTS

A total of 459 patients were included, of which 130 were ACLF and 329 were DC. Table 1 highlights the characteristics of the 2 groups. Both groups had poor scores in physical health and general health, with a median of ≤50. Patients with ACLF had significantly lower scores in energy, social, general health, mental component summary, and global score domains (Table 1). Eleven patients in the ACLF group and 19 in the DC group underwent living donor LT. There was a significant improvement in all domains 1 month after living donor LT except in the pain domain in both groups (Supplemental Table S1, http://links.lww.com/HC9/B41). However, the increase (delta change) in each component was similar among patients with ACLF and DC after transplant. On Cox regression analysis adjusted for age, MELD, and MELD-Na, emotional health (adjusted hazard ratio (aHR), 0.95 [0.9–0.99]; p = 0.04), energy (aHR, 0.91 [0.85–0.98]; p = 0.01), emotional well-being (aHR, 0.93 [0.89–0.98]; p = 0.01), and social domains (aHR, 0.95 [0.91–0.99; p = 0.04) were associated with reduced mortality among all the patients (Supplemental Table S2, http://links.lww.com/HC9/B41). On subgroup analysis, emotional well-being (aHR, 0.92 [0.86–0.99]; p = 0.03) was the only domain in patients with DC, and energy (aHR, 0.93 [0.88–0.99; p = 0.04) was the only domain associated with mortality in patients with ACLF.

DISCUSSION

This study highlights the importance of QOL, especially the patient’s emotional well-being and energy, in determining the prognosis in patients with advanced liver failure. HRQOL is significantly poorer in patients with cirrhosis compared to that of age-matched healthy controls and patients without cirrhosis.2,7 Patients with DC have significantly low individual and composite domain scores. 2 Age, high disease severity scores (MELD and Child Turcotte Pugh) and complications of cirrhosis are associated with poorer QOL scores in patients with cirrhosis. 3 Alcohol as an etiology has been reported to impact QOL significantly.8,9 However, our study did not find any impact of etiology on QOL (data not shown).

Nagel et al 4 reported that patients with ACLF had a significantly reduced QOL in terms of worry and systemic symptoms compared to patients with DC. Our study used the SF-36 questionnaire and found that patients with ACLF and DC have suboptimal physical health and general health scores. An interesting observation was the significance of emotional well-being and energy (vitality/fatigue) as a predictor of mortality. Both these domains assess mental health and are frequently noted in clinical practice to impact the outcomes of the patients. Patients who underwent living donor LT reported improvement in QOL after transplantation, which is similar to previous reports on cadaveric LT. 10 However, nontransplant measures are the need of the hour for these patients to improve their QOL.

The study had several limitations, including the smaller duration of follow-up, lack of assessment of the impact of organ failures on QOL, the drivers of QOL, and lack of assessment of HRQOL in those who did not undergo LT. However, this is the first large cohort study reporting the importance of emotional well-being and energy rather than physical well-being in predicting survival in patients with DC and ACLF. Future studies should focus on incorporating multidisciplinary management of mental health in these patients.

In conclusion, patients with ACLF and DC have poorer HRQOL, especially in terms of general health and physical function. Mental health domains of SF-36 are associated with mortality in patients with advanced liver disease.

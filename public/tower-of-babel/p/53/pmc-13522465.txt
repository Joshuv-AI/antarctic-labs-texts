Chromosome-level genome assembly and annotation of the Murray cod (Maccullochella peelii, Percichthyidae)
Authors: Yuanyuan Chu, Xiangbing Zeng, Jiahao Zhang, Qingli Gong, Yan Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe Murray cod (Maccullochella peelii) is an important native species in Australian river ecosystems and an emerging species in aquaculture. To advance genomic studies in this species, we generated a chromosome-level genome assembly by integrating PacBio HiFi reads and Hi-C sequencing data. The final assembly spans 779.23 Mb, with 750.57 Mb (96.32%) of sequences anchored into 24 pseudochromosomes. The assembly demonstrates high continuity, with contig and scaffold N50 values of 21.77 Mb and 32.20 Mb, respectively. Benchmarking Universal Single-Copy Orthologs (BUSCO) assessment showed a completeness of 99.5%. A total of 23,614 protein-coding genes were predicted, with 22,912 (97.06%) of them functionally annotated. This genome assembly provides a fundamental resource for research on adaptive evolution, population genetics, and genomic-assisted breeding of M. peelii.

Background & Summary

The Murray cod (Maccullochella peelii), a member of the family Percichthyidae, is the largest freshwater fish in Australia. It is primarily distributed in the Murray-Darling Basin, occupying a key ecological niche as the top predator in river ecosystems1. The species is highly valued in recreational fishing and aquaculture in Australia, making it a promising candidate for further aquaculture development. It was first introduced to Taiwan, China in 1999 and subsequently to mainland China in 20012. Since then, the species has become established in several regions, with successful domestication and artificial reproduction achieved. Owing to its desirable culinary attributes, such as firm, white flesh with minimal bone content and a delicate flavor, M. peelii has gained widespread consumer acclaim, leading to a high market value1. It is also nutritionally valuable, being rich in essential amino acids and unsaturated fatty acids such as eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA) and docosahexaenoic acid (DHA)3. Furthermore, characteristics such as rapid growth rate, strong disease resistance, and high feed conversion efficiency make it a promising species for the systematic advancement of freshwater aquaculture in China4. To accelerate modern, genomics-assisted breeding programs for this species, the development of comprehensive genomic resources is urgently needed. A draft genome assembly (633 Mb, Scaffold N50 = 109 kb) has been previously published5, providing an initial genomic resource. To enable more detailed functional and evolutionary studies, a chromosome-level assembly with enhanced contiguity and annotation is desirable.

In this study, we employed PacBio HiFi sequencing to generate a highly accurate initial assembly for M. peelii. This was complemented by Hi-C scaffolding for chromosome-level reconstruction and Iso-Seq data for precise gene annotation. The final assembly spans 779.23 Mb, with 750.57 Mb (96.32%) of sequences anchored into 24 pseudochromosomes. The assembly exhibits high continuity (contig N50 = 21.77 Mb; scaffold N50 = 32.20 Mb) and completeness (BUSCO score: 99.5%), representing a notable refinement over a previously published draft genome. A total of 23,614 protein-coding genes were structurally annotated. This high-quality reference genome provides a fundamental resource for facilitating research on population genetics, conservation management, and functional genomics in this ecologically and economically important species.

Methods

Ethics statement

All animal procedures were approved by the Institutional Review Board of Ocean University of China (Permit No. OUC-AE-2022-171). The fish was euthanized with an overdose of MS-222 (tricaine methanesulfonate) before tissue collection.

Sample collection, library construction and sequencing

A farmed male M. peelii (body weight: 10.9 kg; total length: 79 cm) from a local aquaculture facility in Qingdao, Shandong Province, China, was selected for whole-genome sequencing. High-molecular-weight genomic DNA was isolated from muscle tissue using a standard sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS)/proteinase K lysis protocol, followed by phenol-chloroform extraction and ethanol precipitation. DNA integrity was assessed by electrophoresis on a 1% agarose gel, and the concentration was measured using a Qubit 4.0 Fluorometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). The extracted DNA was sheared to a target size of ~15 kb using a Covaris g-TUBE (Covaris, USA). A SMRTbell library was constructed from the sheared DNA using the SMRTbell Express Template Prep Kit 2.0 (Pacific Biosciences) following the manufacturer’s protocol. This procedure included DNA end-repair, hairpin adapter ligation, and exonuclease treatment to remove failed ligation products. To enrich for fragments of the desired size, the library was size-selected using the BluePippin System (Sage Science, USA) for fragments of ~15 kb. The qualified library was sequenced on the PacBio Sequel IIe platform (Pacific Biosciences, USA), generating 39.55 Gb HiFi read data with a sequencing coverage of 50.75× for subsequent genome assembly (Table 1).

For Hi-C library construction, fresh muscle tissue was ground in liquid nitrogen and cross-linked with formaldehyde to preserve chromatin interactions. Following cross-link quenching with glycine, nuclei were isolated and chromatin was digested with MboI restriction endonuclease. The resulting chromatin ends were labeled with biotin-14-dCTP and ligated. After cross-link reversal and DNA purification, unligated ends were debiotinylated. DNA was sheared to 200–600 bp by sonication. Biotin-labeled fragments were enriched using streptavidin C1 magnetic beads. Sequencing libraries were prepared through end repair, A-tailing, and adapter ligation, followed by PCR amplification (12–14 cycles). The final libraries were sequenced on an Illumina NovaSeq6000 instrument in PE150 mode, generating a total of 51.04 Gb clean data with a sequencing depth of 65.50× (Table 1).

For Iso-Seq, total RNA was extracted from a composite sample of heart, liver, intestine, kidney, gill, muscle, and skin. RNA integrity was verified using an Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer with the RNA 6000 Nano Kit, ensuring all samples had an RNA Integrity Number (RIN) ≥ 7.0. Full-length cDNA was synthesized and amplified from 300 ng of total RNA using the NEBNext® Single Cell/Low Input cDNA Synthesis & Amplification Module (NEB) and Iso-Seq Express Oligo Kit (PacBio), following the manufacturer’s instructions. The resulting cDNA was purified and size-selected using SMRTbell Cleanup Beads. A SMRTbell library was constructed from the amplified cDNA using the SMRTbell Prep Kit 3.0 (PacBio), which included steps for DNA repair, A-tailing, and adapter ligation. The final library was purified with nuclease treatment to remove damaged DNA fragments and sequenced on PacBio Sequel platform, yielding 102.57 Gb of high-quality data with a sequencing depth of 131.63× (Table 1).

Genome assembly and chromosome scaffolding

We generated high-quality HiFi reads from the raw PacBio subreads using the CCS software (https://github.com/PacificBiosciences/ccs). These reads were then used for de novo genome assembly. An initial contig-level assembly was constructed with hifiasm (v0.19.6)6–8 under default parameters, producing a highly contiguous assembly with a contig N50 of 21.77 Mb (Table 2). These contigs were subsequently scaffolded with Hi-C data using 3D-DNA (v1.9)9, which anchored 96.32% of the sequences into 24 pseudochromosomes (Fig. 1; Table 3). The automated scaffolds were then manually corrected in Juicebox (v2.20.0)10 by examining Hi-C interaction heatmaps to resolve misjoins and errors, yielding an improved chromosome-scale assembly. Finally, gap filling and polishing were performed using quarTeT (v1.2.5)11 and NextPolish (v1.4.1)12. This process reduced the number of gaps from 87 to 52 and increased the assembly size from 776.19 Mb to 779.23 Mb. The final chromosome-level assembly consists of 136 scaffolds and 181 contigs, with a scaffold N50 of 32.20 Mb (Table 2). The high quality of the assembly was validated by the clear delineation of chromosomes and strong diagonal interaction signals in the Hi-C contact heatmap (Fig. 2).

Repetitive sequence annotation

First, a de novo repeat library was constructed from the assembled genome using RepeatModeler (v2.0.6)13 with the -LTRStruct option enabled. Subsequently, the de novo generated repeat library was combined with the RepBase14 database to create a non-redundant, custom repeat library. This comprehensive library was then used to screen the entire genome assembly against itself using RepeatMasker (v4.1.8)15 with the -xsmall and -a options to generate detailed annotation output. To identify full-length LTR retrotransposons, LTR_FINDER (v1.0.7)16 was employed to scan the genome for characteristic structural features, including target site duplications and terminal motifs. The results showed that 271,227,995 bp (34.81%) of the M. peelii genome was annotated as repetitive sequences (Table 4).

Gene structure prediction and functional annotation

The structural annotation of protein-coding genes was performed using the NCBI Eukaryotic Genome Annotation Pipeline - External (EGAPx, v0.3.2)17, a comprehensive evidence-driven workflow executed via Nextflow (v24.10.5)18. The pipeline was configured using an input file that specified the genome assembly, the taxonomic identifier (txid: 135761) for M. peelii, and the file paths to the local PacBio Iso-Seq long reads. Upon execution, the EGAPx pipeline automatically managed the entire annotation process. As per its design, the taxid was used to retrieve a curated set of homologous protein sequences and Hidden Markov Model (HMM) profiles from public databases. The pipeline then integrated multiple lines of evidence: the provided Iso-Seq reads were aligned to the genome using minimap219, and the retrieved protein sequences were aligned using miniprot20. These alignments were passed to the Gnomon software for evidence-integrated gene prediction. Briefly, Gnomon operates by first chaining alignments into initial gene models, which are then supplemented by ab initio predictions based on taxon-specific HMM to ensure comprehensive gene model coverage. The resulting annotation comprised 23,614 high-confidence protein-coding genes and 51,825 CDSs (Table 5).

Technical Validation

The completeness of the M. peelii genome assembly was evaluated using Benchmarking Universal Single-Copy Orthologs (BUSCO) v5.8.231 with the actinopterygii_odb10 dataset (containing 3640 conserved genes). The assessment revealed that 3,620 (99.5%) BUSCOs were successfully identified as complete, comprising 3,607 (99.1%) single-copy and 13 (0.4%) duplicated orthologs (Fig. 4). Additionally, the Hi-C contact heatmap demonstrated clear interaction patterns along the diagonal of 24 pseudochromosomes, confirming accurate chromosome-scale scaffolding (Fig. 2). For the annotation quality evaluation, BUSCO analysis of the predicted protein set showed that 3,620 (99.5%) complete BUSCOs were recovered, including 3,577 (98.3%) single-copy and 43 (1.2%) duplicated genes (Fig. 4). These results, together with the high contiguity metrics (contig N50 = 21.77 Mb; scaffold N50 = 32.20 Mb) and successful anchoring of 96.32% sequences to chromosomes, collectively demonstrate the high quality and reliability of the M. peelii genome assembly and annotation.

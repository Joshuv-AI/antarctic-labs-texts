Safety and effectiveness of left atrial appendage closure in atrial fibrillation patients with different types of heart failure
Authors: Liping Liu, Wen Yan, Xiang Xu, Chen Wan, Feng Liu, Qing Yao, Ling Song, Binbin Wang, Zhiyuan Song, Huakang Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundBoth atrial fibrillation (AF) and heart failure (HF) are common cardiovascular diseases. If the two exist together, the risk of stroke, hospitalization for HF and all-cause death is increased. Currently, research on left atrial appendage closure (LAAC) in patients with AF and HF is limited and controversial. This study was designed to investigate the safety and effectiveness of LAAC in AF patients with different types of HF.MethodsPatients with non-valvular atrial fibrillation (NVAF) and HF who underwent LAAC in the First Affiliated Hospital of Army Medical University from August 2014 to July 2021 were enrolled. According to left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), the study divided into HF with reduced ejection fraction (LVEF < 50%, HFrEF) group and HF with preserved ejection fraction (LVEF ≥ 50%, HFpEF) group. The data we collected from patients included: gender, age, comorbid diseases, CHA2DS2-VASc score, HAS-BLED score, NT-proBNP level, residual shunt, cardiac catheterization results, occluder size, postoperative medication regimen, transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) results and transesophageal echocardiography (TEE) results, etc. Patients were followed up for stroke, bleeding, device related thrombus (DRT), pericardial tamponade, hospitalization for HF, and all-cause death within 2 years after surgery. Statistical methods were used to compare the differences in clinical outcome of LAAC in AF patients with different types of HF.ResultsOverall, 288 NVAF patients with HF were enrolled in this study, including 142 males and 146 females. There were 74 patients in the HFrEF group and 214 patients in the HFpEF group. All patients successfully underwent LAAC. The CHA2DS2-VASc score and HAS-BLED score of HFrEF group were lower than those of HFpEF group. A total of 288 LAAC devices were implanted. The average diameter of the occluders was 27.2 ± 3.5 mm in the HFrEF group and 26.8 ± 3.3 mm in the HFpEF group, and there was no statistical difference between the two groups (P = 0.470). Also, there was no statistically significant difference in the occurrence of residual shunts between the two groups as detected by TEE after surgery (P = 0.341). LVEF was significantly higher in HFrEF group at 3 days, 3 months and 1 year after operation than before (P < 0.001). At 45–60 days after surgery, we found DRT in 9 patients and there were 4 patients (5.4%) in HFrEF group and 5 patients (2.3%) in HFpEF group, with no significant difference between the two groups (P = 0.357). One patient with DRT had stroke. The incidence of stroke was 11.1% in patients with DRT and 0.7% in patients without DRT (P = 0.670). There was one case of postoperative pericardial tamponade, which was improved by pericardiocentesis at 24 h after surgery in the HFpEF group, and there was no significant difference between the two groups (P = 1.000). During a mean follow-up period of 49.7 ± 22.4 months, there were no significant differences in the incidence of stroke, bleeding, DRT and HF exacerbation between the two groups. We found a statistical difference in the improvement of HF between HFrEF group and HFpEF group (P < 0.05).ConclusionsLAAC is safe and effective in AF patients with different types of HF. The improvement of cardiac function after LAAC is more pronounced in HFrEF group than in HFpEF group.

Introduction

Atrial fibrillation (AF) is a common arrhythmia in clinical practice [1], and stroke is a serious complication of AF. The risk of ischemic stroke in AF patients is 4 to 5 times higher than that of non-atrial fibrillation patients, and it can lead to about 20% mortality and 60% disability rate [2]. According to the AF management guidelines, patients with a high risk of thromboembolism should be given anticoagulant therapy. Anticoagulation therapy with warfarin or non vitamin K antagonist oral anticoagulant drugs (NOAC) can reduce the incidence of thromboembolic events. However, there are also limitations in clinical application due to the need for frequent monitoring of international normalized ratio (INR), poor patient compliance, high prices, severe gastrointestinal reactions, bleeding, and other side effects [3–7]. Studies have shown that more than 90% of thrombus in patients with non-valvular atrial fibrillation (NVAF) originates from the left atrial appendage (LAA) [8]. As a method to prevent thromboembolism in patients with NVAF, left atrial appendage closure (LAAC) has been gradually carried out in clinical practice, and a number of clinical studies have confirmed its safety and effectiveness. In 2017, the 5-year follow-up results of PROTECT-AF study and PREVAIL trial showed that the LAAC was as effective as warfarin in reducing the incidence of ischemic stroke and major bleeding events, especially hemorrhagic stroke and death [9].The PRAGUE-17 study showed that for high-risk AF patients, LAAC was documented to be noninferior to NOAC in preventing major adverse cardiovascular and neurologic events, with no higher incidence of bleeding events in the LAAC group [10].

Heart failure (HF) and AF are closely related, and HF is a common complication of AF. They interact and promote each other. If the two exist together, the risk of stroke, hospitalization for HF and all-cause death is increased [11]. Currently, research on LAAC in patients with AF and HF is limited and controversial. Cosine et al. [12] showed that LAAC can improve left atrial function through the Frank-Starling mechanism. However, Kim DY et al. [13] showed that after successful LAAC, HF symptoms worsen in a small percentage of HF patients. History of previous HF, increased left ventricular mass index, and abnormal left atrial strain are independent predictors of postoperative HF progression. However, there are still few clinical studies about LAAC in AF patients with different types of HF. This study retrospectively analyzed the data of those patients and explored the safety and efficacy in AF patients with different types of HF.

Materials and methods

Research methods

Study subjects

NVAF patients with HF who underwent LAAC in the First Affiliated Hospital of Army Medical University from August 2014 to July 2021 were enrolled in this study. In accordance with the diagnostic criteria for HF in the 2022 ACC/AHA/ HFSA Heart Failure Management Guidelines [14], HF is classified into three types based on left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF): HF with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF: LVEF ≤ 40%),HF with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF:LVEF ≥ 50%), and HF with mildly reduced ejection fraction (HFmrEF: LVEF between 41%-49%). Based on this, this study divides HFrEF group (including HFrEF and HFmrEF) and HFpEF group. Inclusion criteria: 1) NVAF comorbid with HF (LVEF ≥ 30%); 2) meet the inclusion criteria for LAAC and successfully perform surgery; 3) complete clinical data and regular follow-up. Exclusion criteria: 1) no HF or LVEF < 30%; 2) did not meet the inclusion criteria for LAAC or failed to undergo LAAC; 3) incomplete clinical data or no regular follow-up.All patients signed the informed consent for surgery, and the study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Army Medical University (Ethics number: (B)KY2023119).

Instruments and equipment

The LAAC device used in this study was Watchman device (Boston Scientific, USA).

Data collection

General information and relevant examination data of patients, including age, gender, body mass index (BMI), type of AF (persistent/paroxysmal), comorbidities (hypertension, coronary heart disease, diabetes, etc.), CHA2DS2-VASc score, HAS-BLED score, NYHA classification, routine laboratory tests (blood routine, blood lipid, blood glucose, renal function, etc.),transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) and transesophageal echocardiography (TEE) (TEE is required both preoperatively and intraoperatively to observe the shape of LAA, the presence of thrombus and measure the diameter and depth of LAA from four angles of 0 degrees, 45 degrees, 90 degrees and 135 degrees), operation related data, length of hospital stay, antithrombotic therapy at discharge and HF drug treatment plan, were collected from the medical record system of our hospital's big data intelligent platform (Yidu Cloud Technology Co., LTD.). Mitral regurgitation was classified according to the size of regurgitation area, if the area of mitral regurgitation was less than 4cm2, it was classified as mild, 4-8cm2 is called moderate, and > 8cm2 is called severe.

Interventional operation

Under general anesthesia and TEE monitoring, LAAC was performed according to the routine procedure of our catheterization laboratory [15]. After puncturing right femoral vein, the right heart catheterization was performed first to measure the pressure of the pulmonary artery, right ventricle and right atrium. Then it was placed into the long sheath of atrial septal puncture, and the puncture needle was successfully inserted into atrial septal through the long puncture sheath under the lead of the guide wire. After that, the hardened guide wire was sent to the left upper pulmonary vein along the long puncture sheath. To measure the pressure of left atrial, the Watchman guide sheath was placed into the left superior pulmonary vein. Afterwards, replace the exclusive LAAC sheath and send the pigtail catheter was sent into the LAA through the sheath.

LAA angiography was performed at right anterior oblique 30° + head position 20° and right anterior oblique 30° + foot position 20° respectively to measure the diameter and depth of LAA. The Watchman device whose maximum diameter was ≥ 4-7 mm larger compared with measured data, was delivered to the LAA through the delivery sheath tube. While angiography and TEE were used to observe the position and occlusion effect of the Watchman device, pull test was performed to determine the stability. After insuring the occluder was appropriate and effective, the doctor released the Watchman device and measured the left atrial pressure again. Then, exit the catheter and apply sterile dressing for pressure wrapping. After the surgery, the endotracheal tube was removed and the patient returned to the ward after waking up.

Postoperative observation and management

The patient was admitted to Coronary Care Unit after surgery. ECG, blood pressure, and blood oxygen saturation were monitored continuously for 24 h. There were three kinds of antithrombotic therapy after operation: (1) Warfarin regimen: oral warfarin for 45–60 days after operation, INR target was maintained at 2.0–3.0. After 45–60 days, it was changed to dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) for 6 months, and then to single antiplatelet therapy; (2) NOAC regimen: oral NOAC for 45–60 days after surgery, DAPT from 45–60 days to 6 months, and single antiplatelet therapy after 6 months; (3) DAPT regimen: postoperative DAPT lasted for 6 months, and then changed to single antiplatelet therapy. At the same time, ACE inhibitor (ACEI)/ AT-blocker (ARB)/Angiotensin Receptor- Neprilysin Inhibitor (ARNI) drugs, beta-blockers furosemide (Loop diuretic), spironolactone, digoxin, sodium glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitor (SGLT2i) and other drugs should be selectively used according to the guidelines and patient’s condition [16].

Follow-up protocol

Patients were followed up at 3 days, 45–60 days, 90 days, 180 days, 1 year after surgery, and then annually. The follow-up contents included subjective symptoms, bleeding, embolism and so on. At the same time, electrocardiogram and TTE were reviewed, and TEE was performed at 45–60 days at follow-up time to observe whether the Watchman device position was displaced, with or without residual shunt and device related thrombosis (DRT). For patients taking warfarin after operation, INR was regularly checked, and the dosage of warfarin was adjusted according to INR.

Statistical analysis

SPSS27 software was used to analyze the collected data, and the measurement data were first tested for normality. If it was normal distribution, it was expressed as the mean ± standard deviation and the t-test or ANOVA was used for comparison between the two groups. Otherwise, it was expressed as the median (quartile), and the comparison between the two groups was performed by a non-parametric test. Count data were expressed as cases (percentage). Use Chi-square test to compare between groups. Kaplan–Meier was plotted according to grouping, and P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Medications to correct HF in postoperative patients mainly include ACEI/ ARB/ARNI drugs, beta-blockers, furosemide, spironolactone, digoxin and SGLT2i, and drug use is as follows: in HFrEF group, 47 (63.5%), 58 (78.4%), 38 (51.4%), 36 (48.6%), 19 (25.7%) and 6(8.1%), respectively; in HFpEF group, 96 (44.9%), 148 (69.2%), 41 (19.2%), 41 (19.2%), 14 (6.5%) and 9(4.2%), respectively. Postoperative follow-up

A total of 288 patients completed the follow-up. There were 68 cases of residual shunt immediately after occlusion, including 14 cases in HFrEF group and 54 cases in HFpEF group, with no significant difference between the two groups (P = 0.341). In HFrEF group, 2 residual shunts cases disappeared at 3 months after operation and 12 cases at 1 year after operation. In the HFpEF group, the residual shunt disappeared in 11 cases at 3 months after operation, in 41 cases at 1 year after operation, while residual shunt was still present in 2 cases at 1 year after operation. At 45–60 days after surgery, TEE showed that there were 9 patients with DRT, including 4 patients (5.4%) in HFrEF group and 5 patients (2.3%) in HFpEF group (P = 0.357). One DRT patient in the HFpEF group had a subsequent adverse stroke event, and one patient had pericardial tamponade at 24 h after surgery and improved after pericardial puncture, with no statistical difference between the two, as shown in Table 5. Differences in end-point events

During a mean follow-up of 49.7 ± 22.4 months, respectively, the overall incidence of all-cause death, stroke, bleeding, and DRT in HFrEF and HFpEF groups was: 2.4%, 1.0%, 0.3%, and 3.1%. There was no significant difference in the incidence of stroke, bleeding, DRT, and HF exacerbation between the two groups, as well as the survival curve for HF (P = 0.058), as shown in Fig. 2 and the survival curve for the composite endpoint events (all-cause death, stroke, and bleeding) (P = 0.409), as shown in Fig. 3. Discussion

Both AF and HF are common cardiovascular diseases, and the two often coexist. The Framingham Heart Study in the United States reported that the annual incidence of HF in patients with AF was about 33%, and that of AF in patients with HF was about 54% [17]. They are mutually causal and promote each other in pathophysiological aspects, and there are many common risk factors [18]. This study retrospectively analyzed data from 288 AF comorbid with HF patients who all successfully underwent LAAC. Except for one patient with pericardial tamponade during the perioperative period, there were no death, stroke, major bleeding and other complications. Grouping analysis showed that there were no significant differences in all-cause death, hospitalization for HF, stroke /transient ischemic attack (TIA), bleeding, DRT, pericardial tamponade and other events between HFrEF and HFpEF groups. The results showed that LAAC is safe and effective in AF with coexisting HF patients. The LVEF increased and cardiac function improved in HFrEF group after LAAC, suggesting that the improvement of cardiac function in HFrEF group was more significant than that in HFpEF group. Therefore, LAAC is safe for patients with AF complicated by HF, and even has benefits in terms of cardiac function after surgery.

The co-existence of AF and HF increases the incidence of cardiovascular events and thromboembolism, while the incidence of thromboembolism is inconsistent in different types of HF. Jolanta M et al. [19] analyzed the risk of thromboembolism in 6170 AF with coexisting HF patients and found that patients in the HFpEF group had the highest CHA2DS2-VASc score and patients in the HFrEF group had the lowest score (P < 0.001), suggesting that patients in the HFpEF group had a higher incidence of thromboembolism. Monika Budnik et al. [20] also found that AF patients with HF had higher CHA2DS2-VASc scores than those without HF, and subgroup analysis found that HFpEF patients had the highest CHA2DS2-VASc scores. In this study, CHA2DS2-VASc score and HAS-BLED score were significantly higher in HFpEF group (P < 0.05), suggesting that HFpEF group has a higher risk of thromboembolism and bleeding. Therefore, it is very important to prevent thromboembolism in AF with coexisting HF patients. Patients with HF are often accompanied by a high risk of bleeding, so LAAC is a good choice.

The safety of LAAC in AF with coexisting HF patients should be fully considered in clinical practice. Meta-analysis [21] showed that all-cause mortality in AF patients with HFrEF was significantly higher than that in AF patients with HFpEF (P < 0.001), but hospitalization for HF and stroke events were not related to ejection fraction. Budnik Met al. [22] showed that all-cause mortality and cardiovascular events were higher in AF patients with coexisting HF. Subgroup analysis showed that there were no significant differences in all-cause mortality, readmission, thromboembolism and bleeding events among HFrEF, HFmrEF and HFpEF groups. In an analysis of the safety of LAAC in patients with and without HF, Mohammed Saad et al. [22] confirmed the safety of LAAC in patients with NVAF or HF and found that the excess mortality during long-term follow-up among patients with HF, as compared with those without HF, was mainly attributable to comorbidities related to HF. In 2020, Christian Fastner classified patients who underwent LAAC into three groups according to ejection fraction [LVEF ≥ 55% (56%), 36–55% (36%), and ≤ 35% (8%)] to compare prognosis. The results showed that left ventricular systolic function had no effect on procedural success rate or perioperative complication rate, and the annual incidence of non-fatal stroke and major bleeding was very low in all groups, LAAC appears to be a viable intervention for AF patients with reduced LVEF [23]. In this study, the safety of LAAC was confirmed by comparative analysis that there were no significant differences in all-cause mortality, stroke, bleeding events, and hospitalization rates for HF between HFrEF or HFpEF with coexisting AF patients.

With the increase of LAAC surgery year by year, the occurrence of DRT has become a problem that cannot be ignored. At present, the existing large clinical studies have great differences in the incidence of DRT. The prevalence of DRT was 4.2% in ASAP [24], 3.74% in PROTECT-AF and PREVAIL [9]. In the real world EWOLUTION study, the incidence of DRT was 4.1 2-year follow-up [25]. Fauchier et al. [26] analyzed 13-month follow-up data from 469 patients at eight centers in France between 2012 and 2017 and found that the incidence of DRT was as high as 7.2% per year. Other multicenter studies have also found DRT rates ranging from 1.7 to 4.6% [27]. The overall incidence of DRT in HF with coexisting AF patients was 3.1%, which is consistent with the results of multi-center studies. Left atrial thrombosis is associated with left ventricular systolic function, Maciej et al. [28] found that the prevalence of left atrial thrombus in AF patients increased with the exacerbation of systolic dysfunction, and The prevalence of left atrial thrombus in patients with HFpEF, HFmrEF and HFrEF were 7.4%, 10.5% and 20.3%, respectively (P < 0.001). Multivariate logistic regression analysis showed that lower LVEF was an independent predictor of left atrial thrombosis. The occurrence of DRT was associated with LVEF. Alkhouli et al. suggested that LVEF was an independent predictor of DRT (P = 0.009) [29]. In this study, we found that the incidence of DRT decreased with the increase of ejection fraction. The incidence of DRT in HFrEF group and HFpEF group was 5.4% and 2.3%, respectively, but there was no significant difference between the two groups.Some studies have shown that the symptoms of HF get worse in a small percentage of HF patients after successful LAAC [13]. However, one prospective trial showed no significant changes in LVEF, NT-proBNP levels, or distance covered by the 6-min walk test at 6 weeks or 6 months after LAAC [30]. Even a study that did not include patients with an ejection fraction of less than 40% demonstrated a sharp reduction in total compartment volume early after LAAC, resulting in increased left atrial pressure, and conduction flow and left atrial systolic function [31]. Results of a meta-analysis also showed that LAAC was associated with improvements in left atrial emptying fraction [32]. In our study, the sizes of left ventricular and left atrial in the HFrEF group tended to decrease after surgery, but there was no significant difference between the two groups before and after surgery in the HFrEF group (P > 0.05). However, compared with the HFpEF group, the HFrEF group had a statistically significant reduction in the size of left ventricular and left atrial at 1 year after surgery (P < 0.001). The LVEF in HFrEF group increased after surgery, and the difference was statistically significant (P < 0.001). Compared with HFpEF group, the cardiac function grade of HFrEF group was improved after operation, and the difference was statistically significant (P < 0.05). In contrast to HFpEF group, the improvement of mitral regurgitation in HFrEF group was statistically significant (P < 0.05). In conclusion, compared with HFpEF group, the cardiac structure, ejection fraction and cardiac function classification were improved in HFrEF group after LAAC. Of course, this may be the higher usage rates of ACEI/ARB/RNI drugs, furosemide, spironolactone, and digoxin in the HFrEF group compared to the HFpEF group, which have controlled the patient's heart rate and improved cardiac function. However, patients with LVEF < 30% were not included in our study because current guidelines do not recommend performing LAAC in patients with LVEF < 30%, and there are some other studies have shown that the left ventricular thrombosis is significantly increased in patients with severe left ventricular dysfunction or LVEF < 30% [33, 34]. This is a single-center retrospective study with a relatively limited number of enrolled patients. Further multi center and prospective studies are needed to confirm these findings.

Conclusions

We found that LVEF was significantly higher in HFrEF group at 3 days, 3 months and 1 year after operation than before and there were no significant differences in the incidence of stroke, bleeding, DRT and HF exacerbation between the HFrEF group and the HFpEF group. Our fndings emphasize that LAAC is safe and effective in AF patients with different types of HF. The improvement of cardiac function after LAAC is more pronounced in HFrEF group than in HFpEF group.

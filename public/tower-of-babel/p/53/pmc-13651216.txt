Exosome-mediated hepatocellular carcinoma progression by modulating macrophage metabolic reprogramming and macrophage M2 polarization via exosome-derived hsa_circ_0057320-miR-28-5p/E2F6 axis
Authors: Lumin Wang, Chenyang Qiao, Zhiqiang Wang, Xiaofei Wang, Li Cao, Haitao Shi, Lu Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHepatocellular carcinoma is an aggressive malignancy with poor outcomes. Emerging studies highlight the importance of the tumor microenvironment in HCC progression, where exosomes serve as critical messengers linking tumor cells with surrounding stromal components. Although circRNAs are abundantly present in tumor-derived exosomes, their precise roles and mechanisms in HCC remain poorly defined.MethodsExosomes were characterized using transmission electron microscopy and flow cytometry. Differentially expressed circRNAs were identified by RNA sequencing. Macrophage polarization and metabolic reprogramming were evaluated by western blotting, immunofluorescence and metabolomics. The role of hsa_circ_0057320, miR-28-5p, and E2F6 in HCC progression was examined through transwell, tube formation, and spheroid-sprouting assays. Xenograft mouse models were constructed to verify the role of them in metastasis and angiogenesis, exosomes in progression of HCC. Interactions among hsa_circ_0057320, miR-28-5p, and E2F6 were explored using RIP and dual-luciferase reporter assays.ResultsIn this study, we found that exosomes derived from HOXD3-treated HCCs induced macrophage polarization toward the M2 phenotype via metabolic reprogramming. In addition, HOXD3-stimulated HCC cells secreted exosomes enriched in hsa_circ_0057320, which induce the alternatively activated (M2) macrophage phenotype and enhanced angiogenesis and metastatic capability in vivo and vitro. The role of hsa_circ_0057320 in the metastasis and invasion of HCC was reversed after the enhance of miR-28-5p. E2F6, a target of miR-28-5p, was markedly upregulated in HCC tissues and cells. Additionally, a positive relationship was established between hsa_circ_0057320 and E2F6, contrasting with a negative association with miR-28-5p.ConclusionsExosomes derived from HOXD3-induced HCC cells promotes M2 macrophage polarization via metabolic reprogramming. In addition, HOXD3-induced exosomal hsa_circ_0057320 promotes macrophage M2 polarization via the miR-28-5p/E2F6 axis, thereby accelerating HCC malignancy. These findings reveal the interactive communication mechanism between HCCs and TME, and highlight hsa_circ_0057320 as a potential exosomal indicator and therapeutic target for metastatic HCC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12967-026-08615-y.

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is a major global health burden and ranks as the fourth leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide [1]. Despite recent advances in treatment strategies, including the rise of immunotherapy, the 5-year survival rate for HCC remains unsatisfactory. This underscores the need to unravel the mechanisms driving HCC progression and to explore novel molecular targets for precision therapy. Accumulating evidence highlights the tumor microenvironment (TME) as a pivotal driver of HCC aggressiveness and metastasis [2]. The TME is composed of diverse stromal and immune components, including endothelial cells and tumor-associated immune cells, among which tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) are highly heterogeneous and plastic. Macrophages are typically polarized into M1 or M2 phenotypes in response to different microenvironmental cues. Of particular interest, M2 macrophages serve as key pro-tumorigenic components of TME by secreting growth factors and elevated levels of anti-inflammatory cytokines, thereby facilitating tumor progression [3, 4].

Exosomes are small vesicles ranging from 30 to 120 nm in diameter that serve as important intercellular communicators, transporting various bioactive molecules, including nucleic acids, proteins, glycoconjugates, and lipids, to recipient cells or tissues [5]. Mounting evidence indicates that tumor-derived exosomes initiate oncogenic processes in target cells, such as proliferation, invasion, and angiogenesis, while also actively modulating the TME. For example, colorectal cancer-derived exosomal miR-934 accelerates liver metastasis by inducing M2 macrophage polarization and activating the PI3K/AKT pathway [6]. In HCC, exosomal SLC16A1-AS1 has been shown to drive macrophage M2 polarization and thereby promote tumor progression [7]. These findings underscore the pivotal role of exosomes in cancer diagnosis, treatment, and prognosis assessment [8]. In our previous study, we observed that exosomes released from HOXD3-treated HCC cell lines could promote HCC metastasis via the CCR6-CCL20 axis. HOXD3, a member of the HOXD gene family, is known to facilitate tumor growth, invasion, and metastasis, while suppressing apoptosis [9, 10]. However, the role of circRNAs contained within HOXD3-induced HCC exosomes in regulating HCC progression has not yet been elucidated.

Circular RNAs (circRNAs) are a class of non-coding RNAs with covalently closed structures that confer remarkable stability [11]. Increasing evidence has implicated circRNAs in various human malignancies, including HCC [12, 13], where they govern processes such as tumor initiation, proliferation, apoptosis, and metastasis [14]. Mechanistically, circRNAs often function as microRNA (miRNA) “sponges,” binding miRNAs and thereby relieving their inhibitory effects on target genes [15, 16]. Mounting studies have documented the involvement of circRNAs in HCC pathogenesis. For example, CircPIAS1 is upregulated in HCC and promotes tumor progression by interacting with miR-455-3p to regulate NUPR1 expression [17], while Chen et al. reported that circACVR2A (hsa_circ_0001073) acts as a tumor promoter by regulating miR-511-5p and activating the PI3K-Akt pathway [18]. Notably, circRNAs enriched in exosomes are capable of influencing recipient cells either locally or at distant sites. For instance, exosomal circUPF2 enhances sorafenib resistance by upregulating SLC7A11 and inhibiting ferroptosis in HCC cells [19]. These findings highlight the significant regulatory roles of circRNAs in HCC and underscore their potential as diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets.

In this study, we identified an intergenic circRNA, hsa_circ_0057320, that is enriched in exosomes derived from HOXD3-treated HCC cells. These exosomes promote metastasis and angiogenesis by inducing M2 macrophage polarization. Mechanistically, hsa_circ_0057320 sequesters miR-28-5p, leading to activation of the E2F6 pathway and promotion of HCC progression. Our findings suggest that the hsa_circ_0057320/miR-28-5p/E2F6 axis could serve as a potential therapeutic target, particularly for metastatic HCC.

Results

hsa_circ_0057320 is highly expressed in exosomes derived from HOXD3-induced HCC cells

Exosomes were isolated from cell culture supernatants by ultracentrifugation (Fig. 1A). Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) and nanoparticle tracking analysis (NTA) were utilized to characterize the isolated vesicles. TEM revealed the typical cup-shaped morphology with a double-membrane structure (Fig. 1B), while NTA showed an average particle diameter of 72.7 nm (Fig. 1C). Flow cytometry confirmed the presence of the exosomal markers CD81 and CD63, accounting for 53.77% and 73.74% of total particles in the Huh7-HOXD3-Ctrl group, and 54.06% and 78.69% in the Huh7-HOXD3 group, respectively (Figure S1A). Since exosomes are enriched in non-coding RNAs and play key roles in regulating HCC progression [20, 21], circRNA microarray analysis was performed to identify differentially expressed circRNAs between exosomes isolated from HOXD3-Ctrl and HOXD3-overexpressing Huh7 cells. Compared with the control group, 798 circRNAs were upregulated and 178 circRNAs were downregulated in HOXD3-exosomes, as shown in the heatmap (Fig. 1D) and volcano plot (Fig. 1E, F). Notably, hsa_circ_0057320 was significantly enriched in exosomes derived from HOXD3-treated Huh7 cells compared with controls (Fig. 1G). Conclusively, hsa_circ_0057320 is a newly identified ncRNA that is highly expressed in exosomes from HOXD3-induced HCC cells. Moreover, qRT‑PCR analysis of clinical HCC tissues revealed that hsa_circ_0057320 was also significantly upregulated in tumor tissues compared with adjacent non‑tumor tissues (Figure S1B).

hsa_circ_0057320 facilitates the proliferation and migration of HCC

Hsa_circ_0057320 is located on chromosome 2 (chr2:187498055–187511604) and is 766 bp in length. To elucidate its biological role in HCC, we performed both overexpression and knockdown experiments. Following transfection with hsa_circ_0057320 overexpression vector or sh-hsa_circ_0057320, expression levels were significantly upregulated or downregulated, respectively, in Huh7 and MHCC-97 H cells (Fig. 2 A). Wound healing and transwell assays uncovered that overexpression of hsa_circ_0057320 enhanced HCC cell migration and invasion, whereas silencing of hsa_circ_0057320 inhibited these phenotypes (Fig. 2B, C). To assess the effect on angiogenesis, tube formation assays were conducted using HUVECs. Overexpression of hsa_circ_0057320 markedly increased tubule formation, while knockdown reduced this effect (Fig. 2D). Similarly, spheroid-based sprouting assays revealed enhanced endothelial sprouting in hsa_circ_0057320-overexpressing cells, whereas sh-hsa_circ_0057320 diminished sprouting (Fig. 2E). Western blotting showed that elevated hsa_circ_0057320 expression increased N-cadherin and decreased E-cadherin levels, whereas knockdown produced the opposite changes (Fig. 2 F).

To validate these findings in vivo, a Matrigel plug assay was performed (Fig. 2G). Plugs from the control group exhibited more neovascularization than those from the sh-hsa_circ_0057320 group (Fig. 2 H), and CD31 expression was markedly higher in controls (Fig. 2I). Furthermore, tail vein injection of hsa_circ_0057320-silenced MHCC-97 H cells significantly reduced lung metastasis compared with controls (Fig. 2 J, K), as evidenced by decreased lung weights (Fig. 2 L), fewer metastatic nodules, and reduced microvascular density (Fig. 2 M, N). Collectively, these findings indicate that hsa_circ_0057320 promotes HCC migration, invasion, angiogenesis, and metastasis both in vitro and in vivo.

Exosomes derived from HOXD3-induced HCC cells promote M2 macrophage polarization and are associated with metabolic alterations in macrophages

To further assess macrophage polarization status in HCC, we examined the expression of CD86 and CD206 markers in tumor tissues. The results showed that CD86 expression was markedly decreased, whereas CD206 expression was increased in HCC tissues compared with controls (Figure S1C), indicating an M2‑polarized tumor microenvironment. Previous studies have shown that tumor cells can induce M2 polarization of TAMs through the release of extracellular vesicles, and in turn, M2 TAMs facilitate tumor growth and metastasis by promoting proliferation and EMT in cancer cells [22, 23]. To determine whether exosomes derived from HOXD3-overexpressing HCC cells influence macrophage polarization, exosomes were isolated and incubated with M0 macrophages. PKH26 staining confirmed efficient uptake of exosomes by macrophages (Fig. 3A). qRT-PCR and western blot analyses demonstrated that macrophages treated with HOXD3-derived exosomes exhibited decreased M1 marker CD86 and increased M2 markers CD163 and CD206 (Fig. 3B, C), which was further verified by IF staining (Fig. 3D). These results suggest that HOXD3-exosomes promote M0-to-M2 macrophage polarization. To gain insights into the potential mechanisms by which HOXD3-HCC-exosomes modulate macrophage function, we performed a non-targeted metabolomics analysis on macrophages treated with exosomes derived from either HOXD3-overexpressing or control Huh7 cells. OPLS-DA analysis revealed a clear separation of the metabolic profiles between the two groups (Fig. 3E), indicating that HOXD3-HCC-exosomes induce substantial metabolic alterations in recipient macrophages. Among the identified metabolites, organic acids and derivatives constituted the largest category (Fig. 3F). The volcano plot identified 381 significantly differentially accumulated metabolites, of which 97 were increased and 284 were decreased in macrophages treated with HOXD3-HCC-exosomes compared to controls (Fig. 3G). Notably, several metabolites, including tetracosatetraenoic acid (24:4n‑6), arachidonic acid, α‑ketoglutaric acid, and sphingosine 1‑phosphate, were found at elevated levels, while prostaglandin E2 ethanolamide, calcitriol, palmitoylethanolamide, prostaglandin D2, 3‑phosphoglyceric acid, 2‑phosphoglyceric acid, and 27‑hydroxycholesterol were decreased. KEGG pathway enrichment analysis of the differential metabolites revealed their predominant involvement in pathways such as glycerophospholipid metabolism, choline metabolism in cancer, retrograde endocannabinoid signaling, and linoleic acid metabolism (Fig. 3H). Further analysis integrating the top 11 metabolites with the enriched pathways suggested their potential roles in additional processes including biosynthesis of unsaturated fatty acids, lipid and atherosclerosis, and autophagy (Fig. 3I). Collectively, these metabolic alterations were observed alongside M2 polarization in macrophages treated with HOXD3-HCC-exosomes, suggesting a potential association between metabolic reprogramming and the phenotypic switch.

hsa_circ_0057320 enhances HCC cell migration via inducing M2 macrophage polarization

Given that circRNAs are enriched in exosomes and are involved in modulating cellular behavior, we examined whether HOXD3 influences hsa_circ_0057320 loading into exosomes. qRT-PCR results showed that exosomal hsa_circ_0057320 levels were notably increased in HOXD3-overexpressing cells (Fig. 4 A). Using single-cell sequencing analysis software-TISCH2 (https://tisch.compbio.cn) and analysis of Database GSE140228, it was found that macrophages are closely associated with angiogenesis (Fig. 4B). To evaluate the impact of hsa_circ_0057320 on macrophage–HCC interactions, co-culture experiments were performed. Co-culture with macrophages overexpressing hsa_circ_0057320 markedly enhanced HCC cell migration and angiogenesis (Fig. 4 C-G). Additionally, in vivo experiments were performed by mixing M0 macrophages with MHCC-97 H cells, followed by intratumoral injection of exosomes from control or HOXD3-knockdown MHCC-97 H cells three times at 3-day intervals (Fig. 4 H). Tumor volume and weight were diminished in mice treated with sh-circ_0057320-exo compared to the shNC-exo group (Fig. 4I-K).

miR-28-5p is a downstream target of hsa_circ_0057320 in HCC

Analysis of The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) pan-cancer dataset revealed that miR-28-5p was downregulated in multiple malignancies, particularly in HCC tissues compared with normal controls (Fig. 5 A and 6 A). Consistently, qRT‑PCR result showed that miR-28-5p expression was significantly lower in tumor tissues than in adjacent non‑tumor tissues (Figure S1B). The functional effects of miR-28-5p were assessed by transfecting HCC cells with miR-28-5p mimics or inhibitors. Transfection efficiencies were confirmed in Figure S2A. Wound healing and transwell assays showed that silencing miR-28-5p facilitated HCC cell migration and invasion, whereas overexpression of miR-28-5p had the opposite effect (Figure S2B, S2C). Tube formation assays demonstrated that miR-28-5p overexpression reduced tubule formation in HUVECs, while inhibition of miR-28-5p notably increased tubule formation (Figure S2D). Similarly, spheroid-based sprouting assays showed decreased endothelial sprouting with miR-28-5p overexpression and increased sprouting after inhibition (Figure S2E).

RNA sequencing suggested a potential interaction between miR-28-5p and hsa_circ_0057320, and bioinformatic analysis revealed predicted binding sites (Figs. 5B and 6B). Luciferase reporter assays demonstrated that miR-28-5p mimics limited luciferase activity in the hsa_circ_0057320-WT construct but not in the mutant construct (Fig. 6 C). RIP further confirmed that both hsa_circ_0057320 and miR-28-5p were enriched in AGO2 complexes, indicating a sponge relationship (Fig. 6D). qRT-PCR validated that miR-28-5p levels were decreased in hsa_circ_0057320-overexpressing cells and increased upon knockdown of hsa_circ_0057320 (Fig. 6E). Finally, rescue experiments were conducted to determine whether miR-28-5p mediates the oncogenic effects of hsa_circ_0057320. Overexpression of miR-28-5p significantly inhibited HCC cell invasion, migration, and angiogenesis, and reversed the promoting effects induced by hsa_circ_0057320 overexpression in wound healing, transwell, tube formation, and sprouting assays (Fig. 6 F-I). Western blot analysis further showed that hsa_circ_0057320 overexpression decreased E-cadherin and increased N-cadherin and E2F6 levels, effects that were abolished by miR-28-5p overexpression (Fig. 6 J).

Identification of differentially expressed mRNA in exosomes

To profile differentially expressed exosomal mRNAs in Huh7-HOXD3-Ctrl versus Huh7-HOXD3-derived exosomes, RNA sequencing was performed. In total, 670 mRNAs were differentially expressed, including 608 upregulated and 62 downregulated transcripts (Fig. 7 A). Using the STRING database (https://cn.string-db.org), protein–protein interaction analysis of the upregulated mRNAs revealed two highly interconnected clusters. In the first cluster—characterized by strong gene–gene interactions—key genes included HLA-E, LAT, PTPRC, F2RL3, PTK2B, CXCL6, CCR6, LEPR, IL2RG, FGF22, NCR1, CSF1, HLA-A, GNG13, ARRB2, and E2F6 (Fig. 7B). Functional enrichment analysis showed that these genes were mainly involved in pathways such as cell adhesion molecules, chemokine signaling, cytokine-receptor interactions, endocytosis, JAK-STAT, MAPK, natural killer cell-mediated cytotoxicity, cancer, and Rap1 and Ras signaling (Fig. 7 C). TCGA data revealed that E2F6 expression was significantly upregulated in HCC tissues (Fig. 7D) and positively correlated with histological grade and pathological stage in many cancers, especially in HCC (Fig. 7E-F and Figure S3A-B). Consistently, protein expression analysis also confirmed that E2F6 was elevated in HCC tissues (Figure S1C). Importantly, high E2F6 expression was negatively associated with overall survival (OS), disease-free interval (DFI), disease-specific survival (DSS), and progression-free interval (PFI) in various cancers (Figure S3C-F), particularly in HCC patients (Fig. 7G-J). In addition, single cell dataset GSE140228 showed that E2F6 was involved in macrophages regulation (Fig. 7 K). Collectively, E2F6 may serve as a critical regulator in HCC progression.

Hsa_circ_0057320 sponges miR-28-5p to elevate E2F6 expression

Analysis of TCGA and GTEx databases revealed that E2F6 expression is markedly upregulated across multiple cancers, especially in HCC tissues (Fig. 8 A). To investigate the role of E2F6 in HCC, stable knockdown and overexpression cell lines were generated using lentiviral shRNA and plasmid transfection, respectively. Transfection efficiency was confirmed by qRT-PCR (Figure S4A). Wound healing, transwell invasion, tube formation, and spheroid-based sprouting assays showed that E2F6 overexpression significantly enhanced HCC cell migration, invasion, and angiogenesis, whereas E2F6 knockdown impaired these processes (Figure S4B-E). In vivo, xenograft models established with MHCC-97 H cells confirmed that silencing E2F6 reduced tumor metastasis (Figure S4F-G). Using the TargetScan database, miR-28-5p was predicted as a potential miRNA interacting with the 3ʹUTR of E2F6. Dual-luciferase reporter assay showed that miR-28-5p mimics markedly suppressed luciferase activity of the E2F6-WT reporter but not the mutant reporter (Fig. 8B). qRT-PCR demonstrated a negative correlation between miR-28-5p and E2F6 expression levels (Fig. 8 C). Functional assays indicated that E2F6 overexpression rescued the suppressive effects of miR-28-5p on HCC cell migration, invasion, and angiogenesis (Fig. 8D-G). Western blotting further revealed that miR-28-5p mimics increased E-cadherin and decreased N-cadherin expression, whereas E2F6 overexpression reversed these effects (Fig. 8 H). Collectively, miR-28-5p suppresses HCC progression by targeting E2F6, and hsa_circ_0057320 promotes HCC malignancy by sponging miR-28-5p to upregulate E2F6. To determine whether the miR-28-5p/E2F6 axis specifically mediates the effect of exosomal hsa_circ_0057320, we performed rescue experiments by treating macrophages with sh‑hsa_circ_0057320 exo in combination with either a miR-28-5p inhibitor or an E2F6 overexpression plasmid. The impaired M2 polarization induced by sh‑hsa_circ_0057320 exo was strikingly reversed by the miR-28-5p inhibitor or E2F6 overexpression (Figure S4H). These findings demonstrate that exosomal hsa_circ_0057320 promotes M2 polarization primarily through the miR-28-5p/E2F6 pathway.

Discussion

Exosomes serve as critical mediators of intercellular signaling within the tumor microenvironment. Tumor-derived exosomes contribute to cancer initiation, progression, and therapeutic resistance [24], largely through their cargo of functional non-coding RNAs (miRNAs, lncRNAs, and circRNAs) that modulate recipient cell behavior. In this study, we observed that Exosomal HOXD3 promoted macrophage M2 polarization and induced metabolic reprogramming (Fig. 3). Metabolomic analysis was employed to elucidate the functional role of exosomes derived from HOXD3-induced hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) cells in modulating macrophage polarization. The results revealed upregulation of several key metabolites, including tetracosatetraenoic acid (24:4n-6), arachidonic acid, α-ketoglutaric acid, and sphingosine 1-phosphate.Within the tumor microenvironment, tetracosatetraenoic acid (24:4n-6), produced by tumoral PLA2G16, suppresses CD8 + T cell function through activation of PPARα-mediated fatty acid oxidation, thereby fostering an immune-dysfunctional microenvironment to induce tumor metastasis [25]. Similarly, arachidonic acid and sphingosine-1-phosphate function as critical mediators that drive M2 polarization of TAMs and promote immunosuppression, as demonstrated in lung adenocarcinoma and colorectal cancer [26, 27]. Alpha-ketoglutarate also modulates metabolic reprogramming and macrophage polarization [28]. Conversely, the expression of several metabolites was downregulated, including Prostaglandin E2 ethanolamide, calcitriol, prostaglandin D2, and 27- Hydroxycholesterol. Prostaglandin E2 ethanolamide and calcitriol have been identified as immune-enhancing metabolites with potential influence on the TME [29, 30]. 27-Hydroxycholesterol promotes pro-inflammatory processes via ERα [31]. Notably, in gastric cancer research, prostaglandin D2 exhibits tumor-suppressive properties by inhibiting cancer stem cell viability and invasion [32]. Collectively, these metabolites function as metabolic switches that reprogram macrophage cells, ultimately creating a microenvironment that supports tumor growth, immune evasion, and metastasis.

Our findings demonstrate that HOXD3-induced exosomes orchestrate a metabolic switch in macrophages, promoting an M2 phenotype that fuels HCC progression. This exosome-mediated metabolic reprogramming represents a specific example of the broader framework of stress-adaptive metabolic programs that support tumor progression and therapeutic resistance. In this context, the role of the transcription factor NRF2 is particularly relevant. NRF2 is a master regulator of the cellular antioxidant response and is frequently dysregulated in HCC, where its constitutive activation promotes cancer cell survival, metabolic rewiring, and chemoresistance [33]. Notably, our metabolomic analysis revealed the upregulation of metabolites like α-ketoglutaric acid, which is known to influence NRF2 activity and macrophage polarization [34]. Furthermore, studies in other cancer models, such as KRAS-driven pancreatic cancer, have shown that NRF2 activation promotes chemoresistance by upregulating glutaminolysis and other metabolic pathways, and that targeting these pathways can re-sensitize tumors to chemotherapy [35]. Therefore, therapeutic strategies that simultaneously target exosome-mediated communication and key stress-adaptive regulators like NRF2 may prove highly effective in overcoming therapy resistance in advanced HCC.

Furthermore, we found that hsa_circ_0057320 was highly expressed in exosomes derived from HOXD3-treated HCC cells. CircRNAs have attracted increasing attention as diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers in various cancers [36–38], and numerous circRNAs have been shown to contribute to HCC progression [39]. Here, we clarified the biological role of hsa_circ_0057320 in HCC. RNA sequencing and qRT-PCR analyses demonstrated that hsa_circ_0057320 was notably upregulated in exosomes from HOXD3-treated HCC cells compared with controls (Fig. 1). In addition, gain- and loss-of-function experiments revealed that hsa_circ_0057320 overexpression enhanced migration, invasion, and angiogenesis of HCC cells in vitro and in vivo, while its knockdown exerted the opposite effects (Fig. 2), suggesting that hsa_circ_0057320 functions as an oncogenic circRNA during HCC progression.

Accumulating evidence suggests that circRNAs can regulate downstream gene expression by functioning as competing endogenous RNAs (ceRNAs) or by binding RNA-binding proteins. For example, circRERE upregulates MAVS by competitively sponging miR-6837-3p, thereby activating type I IFN signaling and enhancing antitumor immunity in colorectal cancer [40]. In triple-negative breast cancer, Zhang et al. demonstrated that the ZNF460/circRPPH1/miR-326/ITGA5 network modulated disease progression, whereby circRPPH1 acted as a ceRNA to modulate ITGA5 and activate the FAK/PI3K/AKT pathway [41]. In our study, microarray and bioinformatic analyses revealed a regulatory axis involving hsa_circ_0057320 and miR-28-5p. Mechanistically, hsa_circ_0057320 serves as a sponge for miR-28-5p, thereby relieving its suppression of E2F6 and promoting HCC cell migration and invasion.

miRNAs are well known to play essential roles in disease development and diverse biological processes [42]. Prior studies have implicated miR-28-5p in multiple cancers [43–45]. For instance, the miR-28-5p/CAMTA2 axis regulates colon cancer progression via the Wnt/β-catenin pathway [46]; lncRNA MCM3AP-AS1 modulates CENPF expression by sponging miR-28-3p in breast cancer [47]; and in HCC, LINC02298 promotes malignancy by regulating CCDC6 via miR-28-5p [48]. In our study, miR-28-5p was inhibited in HCC (Fig. 5), and functional rescue assays further confirmed that miR-28-5p overexpression abrogated the promotive effects of hsa_circ_0057320 on HCC cell migration, invasion, and angiogenesis (Fig. 6).

E2F6 is involved in regulating multiple signaling pathways and is essential for cancer cell fate regulation [49, 50]. Here, we observed its marked overexpression in HCC and association with aggressive clinicopathological features (Fig. 7 and S4). Recent studies have also implicated circRNAs in modulating E2F6 expression during tumor progression. For example, circ_0089153 acts as a ceRNA for miR-2467-3p to modulate E2F6 in breast cancer, promoting proliferation and metastasis [51], while the circDUSP16/miR-432-5p/E2F6 axis has been shown to facilitate colorectal cancer progression [52]. Consistent with these findings, our rescue experiments demonstrated that E2F6 restored HCC cell migration and angiogenesis suppressed by miR-28-5p (Fig. 8).

Overall, we demonstrate that exosomes derived from HOXD3-treated HCC cells mediate communication within the TME and promote M2 macrophage polarization by metabolic reprogramming and upregulating hsa_circ_0057320. Hsa_circ_0057320 is highly expressed in HCC and functions as a tumor promoter by enhancing the invasion and angiogenesis of HCC cells. Mechanistically, hsa_circ_0057320 acts as a ceRNA to sponge miR-28-5p, thereby upregulating E2F6 expression and driving HCC progression (Fig. 9). Collectively, our results identify hsa_circ_0057320 as a potential oncogenic driver and exosome-mediated therapeutic target in HCC.

Methods

Cell line culture

HCC cell lines (Huh7 and MHCC-97 H) were procured from the Shanghai Institute of Cell Biology, Chinese Academy of Sciences, and cultured in high-glucose DMEM (Gibco) enriched with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin. THP-1 cells were maintained in RPMI-1640 medium (Gibco) with the same supplements. Human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs; ScienCell) were maintained in endothelial cell medium composed of 500 mL basal medium, 25 mL FBS, 5 mL growth supplement, and 5 mL penicillin-streptomycin at 37 °C in 5% CO2. All cell lines were authenticated by short tandem repeat (STR) profiling.

RNA sequencing

RNA sequencing of exosomes was performed by Hangzhou Lianchuan Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Zhejiang, China).

Exosome isolation and characterization

Exosomes were isolated as previously [53]. Briefly, cell culture supernatants were harvested and subjected to ultracentrifugation. Exosomes were collected from the pellet and resuspended in PBS. Particle size distribution was determined by Hangzhou Lianchuan Biotechnology Co., Ltd. Exosomal surface markers (CD63 and CD81) were detected by CytoFLEX flow cytometry to confirm exosome identity.

Exosome labeling and internalization

Exosomes were labeled using the PKH26 Red fluorescent cell linker kit (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA). PKH26-labeled exosomes were added to the culture medium and incubated with THP-1 cells. Nuclei were stained with DAPI, and exosome internalization was examined by confocal fluorescence microscopy.

RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP)

RIP assays were conducted with the RIP Kit (BersinBio, Guangzhou, China). Briefly, HCC cell lysates prepared in inhibitor-supplemented RIP buffer were incubated at 4 °C with Ago2- or IgG (negative control [NC])-bound magnetic beads. Captured RNAs were extracted using proteinase K digestion and phenol/chloroform precipitation, followed by qRT-PCR analysis. Total RNA served as the input control.

Tumorigenesis in nude mice

BALB/c nude mice (5–7 weeks old) were brought from GemPharmatech (Chengdu, China). Animal studies were ethically approved by Xi’an Jiaotong University and carried out following national guidelines for laboratory animal care and use.

To evaluate the role of HOXD3-derived exosomes in HCC progression, mice were randomized into a control exosome group (shNC-exo) and a HOXD3 knockdown exosome group (sh-HOXD3-exo) (n = 5 per group). Each mouse was subcutaneously injected with ~ 5 × 106 MHCC-97 H cells mixed with ~ 5 × 106 macrophages. Three days after implantation, mice received exosome injections (shNC-exo or sh-HOXD3-exo), administered every three days for a total of three doses. Tumor volumes were measured at 3-day intervals. After 15 days, mice were euthanized, and tumors were excised, collected, and weighed.

To assess the role of hsa_circ_0057320 and E2F6 in tumor metastasis, MHCC-97 H cells transfected with sh-hsa_circ_0057320 or NC and shE2F6 or NC were suspended in 100 µL PBS and injected into the tail veins of male BALB/c nude mice (n = 5 per group). After 14 days, bioluminescence imaging was performed to monitor metastatic spread. Mice were then sacrificed by cervical dislocation, and lungs were harvested, weighed, and subjected to H&E staining for histological analysis.

Matrigel plug assay in vivo

Four BALB/c nude mice were subcutaneously injected into the groin with 0.5 mL of Matrigel mixture containing 0.25 mL conditioned medium from MHCC-97 H cells transfected with sh-hsa_circ_0057320, 0.25 mL Matrigel, and 250 ng/mL VEGF. The control group received 0.25 mL conditioned medium from NC-transfected MHCC-97 H cells mixed with 0.25 mL Matrigel and 250 ng/mL VEGF. After 10 days, mice were sacrificed, and Matrigel plugs were collected for further analysis.

Wound healing assays

Treated cells were plated in 6-well dishes and grown overnight. Upon reaching 60–80% confluency, a straight scratch was made using a 10 µL tip. After switching to medium with 1% FBS, wound closure was monitored microscopically at 0, 48, and 72 h.

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

The full-length hsa_circ_0057320 sequence (wild-type, WT) and its corresponding mutant (MUT) were cloned into the pmirGlo vector (Shaanxi Xi’an GENECARER Biotechnology Co., Ltd). HCC cells were co-transfected with miR-28-5p mimic or NC and pmirGlo constructs in 96-well plates using Lipofectamine 8000 (Shanghai Beyotime Biotechnology Co., Ltd). After 48 h, luciferase activity was examined via a dual-luciferase reporter assay system (Shanghai Beyotime Biotechnology Co., Ltd).

Transwell assay

Cell invasion was evaluated with the 8 μm pore-size Transwell chambers (BD, USA) coated with Matrigel [10]. Following incubation, cells that had invaded through the membrane were fixed, stained, and counted under a microscope.

Co‑culture system in wound healing assays

A 0.4 μm pore Transwell chamber (Corning, USA) was used to establish a co-culture system. HCC cells were plated in the lower chamber and scratched at 60–80% confluence using a 10 µL tip. THP-1 cells, differentiated into M0 macrophages with 100 ng/mL PMA, were added to the upper insert and subsequently treated with exosomes.

Co-culture of exosome-treated macrophages with HCC cells

THP-1 cells were seeded in 24-well plates and induced into the M0 phenotype with PMA. Afterwards, either HOXD3-derived or control exosomes were added to the culture, and Transwell inserts containing serum-free HCC cells were positioned above the macrophages. After 24 h of co-culture, the cells were fixed in paraformaldehyde (PFA), stained with crystal violet, and photographed.

In vitro tube formation assay

Treated HUVECs were seeded onto 96-well plates coated with 50 µL Matrigel (Mogengel Bio, Xiamen, China) and incubated at 37 °C for 2 h. Cells were then cultured with conditioned medium for 12 h and photographed. Tube formation was analyzed using the ImageJ Angiogenesis Analyzer.

Immunofluorescence (IF)

Cells were fixed in 4% PFA and permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100, then incubated overnight at 4 °C with CD163 and CD86 primary antibodies. After incubation with fluorescent secondary antibodies, samples were imaged by confocal microscopy.

Western blotting

Proteins were extracted using RIPA buffer, quantified by BCA assay (Shanghai Beyotime Biotechnology Co., Ltd), resolved by SDS-PAGE, and transferred to PVDF membranes. Following blocking with 5% skim milk, membranes were subjected to overnight incubation at 4 °C with primary antibodies against E2F6, N-cadherin, E-cadherin, CD86, CD163, CD206, and GAPDH, followed by incubation with appropriate HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies. Signals were visualized utilizing a Bio-Rad blotting system (USA). Information on all the antibodies is listed in Supplementary Table 1.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed utilizing Student’s t-test or one-way ANOVA, as appropriate. Results are summarized as mean ± SEM. Statistical analyses were completed using GraphPad Prism, and Image J was used for quantification. A P value ≤ 0.05 was deemed statistically significant.

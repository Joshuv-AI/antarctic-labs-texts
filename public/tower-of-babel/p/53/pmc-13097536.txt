ClotPro® haemostasis in pregnancy: physiological parameters, reference intervals and comparison with non-pregnant women in a prospective observational study
Authors: Kamil Vrbica, Jan Stingl, Katerina Vanickova, Ondrej Hrdy, Radka Stepanova, Petr Janku, Lukas Hruban, Roman Gal
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSevere haemorrhage remains a leading cause of maternal mortality worldwide, despite advancements in obstetric care. Although viscoelastic testing is recommended to guide treatment of postpartum haemorrhage, device-specific physiological parameters and reference ranges specific to pregnancy are necessary to enable accurate interpretation of viscoelastic findings in the clinical management of obstetric patients. Evaluation of ClotPro®, including comparison with conventional laboratory assays and with non-pregnant individuals, is therefore essential to support its use in obstetric patients.MethodsIn this prospective study, venous blood samples were obtained from 130 parturient participants before labour and 45 non-pregnant controls. Samples were analysed with conventional laboratory coagulation tests and the ClotPro® device. ClotPro® parameters and conventional laboratory tests were compared between the parturient population and non-pregnant control group with Student’s t-test or the Mann-Whitney U test, as appropriate. The correlation between selected laboratory and coagulation parameters was assessed with Spearman’s correlation coefficient. Reference ranges for ClotPro® in parturients were established by calculation of percentiles 2.5 and 97.5.ResultsSignificant statistical differences in clot firmness were found across the EX-test, IN-test, and FIB-test (p < 0.006) between parturient and non-pregnant participants. The most pronounced differences were observed in the FIB-test. Strong correlation was observed between conventional laboratory tests and FIB-test (Rs = 0.657, p < 0.001 in the pregnant group; Rs = 0.669, p < 0.001 in the control group). According to our findings, reference ranges were established for the parturient population: EX-test, CT (39–67 s), A5 (39–59 mm), ML (1–9%), MCF (57–70 mm); FIB-test, A5 (15–29 mm), MCF (19–35 mm); and IN-test, CT (121–172 s).ConclusionPregnant women exhibit a distinct ClotPro® coagulation profile, characterised by increased clot firmness in FIB-test, supported by correlations with standard laboratory tests and parturient-specific reference intervals. These findings enhance the physiological interpretation of ClotPro® in obstetric care and may support its future integration into postpartum haemorrhage management.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12884-026-08941-5.

Background

The peri-partum period is a critical phase in pregnancy characterized by profound physiological changes that mitigate the risks associated with childbirth [1]. Despite advancements in obstetric care, severe haemorrhage remains the leading cause of maternal mortality worldwide [2]. Pregnancy is characterised by progressive haemostatic changes resulting in a hypercoagulable state [3–5]. This unique haemostatic profile differentiates the peri-partum population from other bleeding populations and poses distinct challenges in the diagnosis and management of haemorrhage. Current international guidelines recommend the use of viscoelastic testing (VET) to guide post-partum haemorrhage (PPH) treatment [6–10].

Whereas traditional coagulation assays provide a relatively affordable and widely available means to assess coagulation status, VETs offer many benefits [11]. VETs use whole blood and measure clot formation in real time, thus enabling rapid turnaround and saving critical minutes in bleeding patients. VET findings significantly correlate with fibrinogen levels [12, 13]; consequently, VET use can help predict PPH progression [14, 15] and has been associated with improved severe PPH outcomes [16, 17]. Importantly, those studies were conducted with legacy devices, such as ROTEM® (Werfen, Barcelona, Spain) or TEG® (Haemonetics Corporation, Boston, MA, USA), whereas none used the newer ClotPro® device (Haemonetics Corporation, Boston, MA, USA; formerly Enicor GmbH, Munich, Germany). ClotPro® shares many fundamental functional principles with ROTEM®; however, the results and interpretation might differ in certain populations [18–20]. Specific reference intervals are essential for reliable use of VET in parturients, given that coagulation parameters in parturient individuals differ significantly from those of the non-pregnant population [21–23]. Furthermore, to evaluate the ability of a specific viscoelastic device to assess coagulation status, it is crucial to determine its correlation with conventional laboratory assays and, in parturients, to define the differences with the non-pregnant population.

This study was therefore aimed to define physiological coagulation parameters in pregnant participants during the peripartum period. It also aimed to compare them with those of the non-pregnant population and to determine the correlation between ClotPro® parameters and conventional laboratory tests. Based on these results, the study further aimed to define reference ranges for parturients using the ClotPro® device.

Methods

This prospective observational single-centre cross-sectional study with a control group was conducted between May 2023 and January 2024 at the University Hospital Brno, Czech Republic. The study was performed in accordance with the principles of Good Clinical Practice and the Declaration of Helsinki. Ethical approval was granted on 12 April 2023 by the Ethics Committee of University Hospital Brno (Approval No. 01-120423/EK), and the study was registered on 25 May 2023 at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05935137). Informed consent was obtained from all patients and volunteers prior to their inclusion in the study.

Study population

The study population consisted of two groups, a pregnant group comprising healthy parturients and a control group comprising healthy non-pregnant female volunteers. The inclusion criteria for the pregnant group were pregnant patients admitted to the hospital for labour at physiological term (38 weeks’ gestation or later), who were 18–45 years of age, had a pre-pregnancy BMI of 18.5–30.0 kg/m2, had blood samples collected prior to onset of labour, and were able to provide informed consent on admission. The exclusion criteria for this group included antiplatelet or anticoagulation treatment, hereditary or acquired coagulopathy, history of thrombosis or pulmonary embolism, acute or chronic inflammation (including fever, septic conditions, or autoimmune disease), active bleeding, preeclampsia, eclampsia, gestational diabetes, placental abruption, HELLP syndrome, history of hematologic or oncologic disease, and refusal to participate in the study. The inclusion criteria for the control group were female sex, age of 18–45 years, BMI of 18.5–30.0 kg/m2, negative pregnancy test, and ability to provide informed consent. The exclusion criteria for this group comprised those for the pregnant group, as well as pregnancy in the prior 6 months. Women in the pregnant group were recruited at the Department of Obstetrics and Gynaecology by a study nurse. Control group participants were volunteers recruited through a public appeal, who did not receive honoraria for study participation. Individuals in both groups who agreed to participate in the study were provided with oral and written information about the study before providing written informed consent. The study is reported in accordance with the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines [24].

Sample collection

In the pregnant group, a peripheral cannula of appropriate size (18–22 G) was inserted, and blood samples were collected. The standard laboratory investigations included blood count analysis and coagulation testing. Blood samples for haematological analysis were collected in EDTA tubes (S-Monovette® EDTA K3E/2.7 ml; Sarstedt AG, Nümbrecht, Germany) and analysed using an automated haematology analyser XN-3100® (Sysmex Corporation, Kobe, Japan). Coagulation samples were collected in citrate tubes (S-Monovette® Citrate 9NC/3.0 ml; Sarstedt AG, Nümbrecht, Germany) and analysed using a semi-automated coagulation analyser STart Max® (Diagnostica Stago, Asnières-sur-Seine, France). Fibrinogen concentration was determined using the Clauss method. Samples were transported immediately to the ISO 15,189-accredited central laboratory of University Hospital Brno via a pneumatic tube system. The laboratory parameters of interest included haemoglobin, haematocrit, platelet count, leucocyte count, fibrinogen level, prothrombin time (PT), international normalized ratio (INR), activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT), and thrombin time (TT). The reagents used for the individual laboratory parameters and reference values are summarized in Supplement 1.

During the blood sampling, an additional study sample was collected in a coagulation tube (S-Monovette® Citrate 9NC/3.0 ml; Sarstedt AG, Nümbrecht, Germany). The specimen was gently inverted several times, immediately transported by study personnel, and analysed within 2 h by a well-trained anaesthesiologist with the ClotPro® device. The ClotPro® device employed in this study was manufactured by Enicor GmbH (Munich, Germany). During the study period, the system and its reagents were supplied and distributed by Haemonetics Corporation (Boston, MA, USA), which served as the official distributor at that time. Testing was conducted only between 7 a.m. and 7 p.m. In the control group, blood samples were collected via peripheral venous puncture into the same three tubes and processed in the same manner as in the pregnant group.

ClotPro® device and testing

The ClotPro® device, a viscoelastic measurement technology for rapid assessment of haemostasis, is similar to the well-established ROTEM® Delta device. These devices differ in the number of measurement channels: the ClotPro® has six independent channels, whereas the ROTEM® device has four. Another distinction is the viscoelastic measurement mechanism: in ClotPro®, the cup rotates against the pin, whereas in ROTEM®, the pin rotates against the cup. A major difference pertains to blood pipetting. The ClotPro® uses 340 µL citrated whole blood, which passes four times through a sponge containing a dry reagent integrated directly into the pipetting tip (“active tips”). This design enables easier handling and decreases pipetting errors. Consequently, the device achieves highly sensitive capacitive detection of cup excursions, represented as resulting curves.

Three basic viscoelastic tests to evaluate the extrinsic (EX-test) and intrinsic (IN-test) coagulation pathways, as well as fibrinogen function (FIB-test), were performed. In the EX-test, coagulation of the recalcified blood sample was activated by tissue factor, and the extrinsic pathway and its interaction with platelets were assessed. In the IN-test, the recalcified blood sample was activated by ellagic acid, and the intrinsic pathway and its interaction with platelets were evaluated. The FIB-test activated coagulation of the recalcified blood sample with tissue factor in the presence of dual antiplatelet agents comprising cytochalasin D and a glycoprotein IIb/IIIa inhibitor, to exclude platelets’ contribution to fibrin viscoelasticity. This test evaluated fibrinogen levels and fibrin polymerization. For each test, we recorded clotting time (CT; the time from coagulation activation to 2 mm clot firmness, in seconds), clot amplitude at 5, 10, and 20 min after CT (A5, A10, and A20, in mm), and maximum clot firmness (MCF; the maximum signal amplitude reflecting clot strength, in mm). Additionally, for the EX-test and IN-test, we recorded clot formation time (CFT; the time from 2 to 20 mm clot firmness, in seconds) and maximum lysis (ML; the percentage decrease in MCF during measurement).

The primary objective of the study was to investigate the physiological coagulation parameters using the ClotPro® device in the parturient population and, based on these findings, to establish pregnancy-specific reference ranges. The secondary objective was to compare ClotPro® and conventional laboratory coagulation parameters between parturients and non-pregnant controls and to determine the correlations between ClotPro® measurements and standard laboratory tests in both groups.

Statistical analysis

Patients‘ characteristics and clinical and laboratory parameters were evaluated with descriptive statistical methods. Categorical variables were summarized using absolute and relative frequencies and compared using Fisher’s exact test. Continuous variables were assessed for normality using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Variables with not violated normal distribution are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and were compared using Student’s t-test. Variables with normal distribution violated are presented as median with interquartile range (IQR) and were compared using the Mann-Whitney U test.

The correlation between selected laboratory and coagulation parameters was assessed with Spearman’s correlation coefficient, because of the normality of the data was violated. Reference ranges for ClotPro® parameters were determined according to IFCC guidelines by calculation of percentiles 2.5 and 97.5 [25]. A 90% confidence interval for both the lower and upper limits was calculated according to 5,000 bootstrap resamples with replacement from the original dataset. Statistical analysis was conducted in SAS 9.4 software. The significance level for all tests was set at alpha = 0.05 and corrected for multiple testing using the Bonferroni method, if appropriate.

Three women had blood loss ≥ 1000 ml and thus fulfilled the WHO definition of severe PPH [26, 27]. One case (1000 ml) was due to retained placental tissue, one case (1000 ml) to cervical and perineal rupture, and one case (1500 ml) to uterine rupture. No coagulation disorder was identified in any of these patients. An additional subgroup analysis stratifying pregnant women by BMI at admission (≤ 30 vs. > 30 kg/m²) is presented in Supplement 2. After correction for multiple testing, no statistically significant differences were observed in any parameter, indicating that BMI did not significantly influence the measured values.

Correlation between conventional laboratory tests and ClotPro® parameters

Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient indicated no significant correlation between the PT and CT of the EX-test in either group. Spearman correlation analysis between the CT of the IN-test and aPTT demonstrated a moderate correlation in the pregnant group (Rs = 0.572, p < 0.001) and a strong correlation in the control group (Rs = 0.721, p < 0.001). Strong correlations were observed between fibrinogen levels and MCF in the FIB-test for both groups (Rs = 0.657, p < 0.001 in the pregnant group; Rs = 0.669, p < 0.001 in the control group).

ClotPro® reference ranges for the parturient

Table 4 presents the reference ranges of ClotPro® parameters for the EX-test, IN-test, and FIB-test in the parturient population. The following reference ranges are considered clinically relevant in routine practice: EX-test CT (39–67 s), A5 (39–59 mm), MCF (57–70 mm), ML (1–9%); IN-test CT (121–172 s); FIB-test A5 (15–29 mm), MCF (19–35 mm). These reference ranges were published as a science letter in journal Anaesthesia [28].

Discussion

We report the first prospective study, establishing physiological parameters of coagulation status with the ClotPro® device in a cohort of more than 120 healthy parturient participants. Compared with the control group, the parturient population exhibited significant statistical differences in nearly all investigated ClotPro® parameters, however, the magnitude and potential biological relevance of these differences varied across assays. Based on our findings, we have concluded that pregnancy is associated with a distinct viscoelastic haemostatic profile on ClotPro® especially in FIB-test, clearly differentiating parturients from non-pregnant women. In addition to these physiological differences, several ClotPro® parameters demonstrated predictable relationships with standard laboratory coagulation markers, further supporting the physiological validity and clinical interpretability of ClotPro® in both pregnant and non-pregnant populations. Based on our findings, we defined specific ClotPro® reference ranges for the parturient population.

In our study, the amplitudes in the EX-test (A10, A20, and MCF) and IN-test (A5, A10, A20, and MCF) were statistically significantly higher in the pregnant group than in the control group. These parameters reflect clot firmness and are influenced by multiple determinants, including platelet count, factor XIII, fibrinogen, and their interactions. Although platelet count may slightly decrease during pregnancy, increased fibrinogen levels contribute substantially to the procoagulant shift characteristic of this state [29, 30]. Elevated clot firmness has also been described in various studies examining viscoelastic testing in parturients [22–25]. In contrast, the absolute differences observed in EX-test and IN-test amplitudes were numerically modest and characterized by overlapping interquartile ranges. These findings should therefore be interpreted cautiously, as small intergroup differences may not necessarily reflect clinically meaningful biological variation.

Our conventional coagulation test results in the pregnant group were consistent with previously published reference values reported for the third trimester and during labour [31, 32]. We observed significant differences in PT, INR, aPTT, and TT between groups. Nonetheless, no difference in CT was observed in all ClotPro® tests between groups. Conflicting results have also been observed in other studies assessing the correlation between ClotPro® and laboratory tests [19, 20, 33]. Similar observations were reported by Anakmeteeprugsa et al., who found only weak correlations between clot time on the novel Quantra® QPlus device and conventional coagulation assays in healthy pregnant women [34]. Together, these findings suggest that limited correlation between VET-derived clotting time and standard coagulation tests is a broader feature observed across different viscoelastic platforms.

Fibrinogen and its corresponding assay results contribute to the assessment of haemostatic status in obstetric patients and may assist in the early identification of patients at risk of maternal complications and PPH progression [14, 16, 35, 36]. Herein, we observed a strong correlation between clot firmness in the FIB-test and fibrinogen levels, in agreement with reports of strong or very strong correlations between these parameters in other studies [19, 20, 33]. We observed significantly greater amplitude in the FIB-test and fibrinogen levels in the pregnant group than the control group, consistent with the well-documented physiological changes in pregnancy and findings from previous studies on coagulation status in this population [12, 22, 37]. The amplitude in the pregnant group was substantial, at 1.5-fold that in non-pregnant participants, which aligns with findings by Armstrong et al. [22], who reported a comparable rise in clot firmness in parturients using ROTEM®. Another important factor that should be considered in interpreting FIB-test is haematocrit, which has been shown to be a confounder. Both low and high haematocrit can lead to falsely hyper- and hypo-coagulable VET results, respectively [38]. Because both study groups exhibited physiological haematocrit levels, haematocrit-related bias was unlikely, although haemodilution is commonly observed in pregnancy. In addition, fibrin-based clot amplitude may be modulated by factor VIII and factor XIII levels, which were not measured in this study and should be considered potential confounders [39, 40].

Pregnant individuals exhibit a procoagulant shift in haemostasis, reflected by VET parameters and standard laboratory markers, which generally correlate together and differ from those of non-pregnant individuals. Fibrinogen, the most important procoagulant variable, is often significantly elevated and can be clearly detected by ClotPro® assays, thus providing a viable alternative VET for assessing pregnant patients. VETs offer advantages for patients with PPH, by decreasing the need for transfusion products, shortening ICU stays, and lowering healthcare costs [16, 17]; moreover, they are recommended by guidelines for PPH management [6, 10]. However, neither the manufacturer-provided reference ranges nor those established for ROTEM® can be applied to the ClotPro® device. Therefore, specific reference values must be defined for the parturient population. We derived reference ranges by analysing coagulation parameters in a cohort of women admitted for labour or elective caesarean delivery with uncomplicated, physiologically normal pregnancies. These parturient-specific values may provide more clinically meaningful therapeutic targets for the PPH management, as they capture the dynamic, pregnancy-related shifts in haemostasis that are not reflected in conventional, non-pregnant reference intervals. Notably, when compared with the manufacturer-supplied reference values, the ClotPro® parturient ranges showed systematically different profiles: clotting time was altered for both the IN-test and FIB-test; clot formation time differed for the EX-test and IN-test; and amplitude values were shifted for the EX-test and IN-test. The most striking divergence was observed in the FIB-test, where the lower physiological limit in pregnancy was nearly double the manufacturer’s recommended threshold. This finding suggests that manufacturer-provided general reference limits may not fully capture pregnancy-specific haemostatic physiology, and that parturient specific reference ranges may improve interpretation of viscoelastic parameters in obstetric population as mentioned in our science letter [28].

The current study had several limitations. Firstly, the pregnant group consisted of slightly different subgroups; labouring participants and non-labouring participants before caesarean delivery. Secondly, the blood sampling timing was not strictly standardized. However, both limitations, the differences between subgroups and the variations in sampling time, were minor and have not been found to have clinically significant effects [23, 41]. Another limitation was the single-centre nature of the study, which was conducted in a tertiary hospital. Although we applied broad exclusion criteria to ensure a cohort of healthy participants in both groups (particularly excluding participants with chronic inflammation – a factor not consistently addressed in previous studies), some selection bias may have been present due to the demographic composition of the urban population attending our hospital. In addition, a formal analytical precision analysis was not performed within this study; however, a previous validation study demonstrated high reproducibility of ClotPro® test parameters [18]. Another limitation of our study is the currently limited availability of the ClotPro® device and its assays. However, an updated version of the ClotPro® device, named MultiClot® (Apiro, Budaörs, Hungary), compatible with the original system, has recently been developed and is currently undergoing the registration process. Additionally, several new assays fully compatible with ClotPro® and MultiClot® were certified and launched to the market in 2025, and the remaining assays are expected to be registered and released in 2026.

Conclusion

In conclusion, we identified statistically significant differences in clot firmness parameters (EX-test, IN-test, and FIB-test) between pregnant and non-pregnant participants using the ClotPro® device. Differences in EX-test and IN-test amplitudes may not reflect biologically meaningful variation, whereas the most pronounced differences were observed in the FIB-test, consistent with the known procoagulant shift in pregnancy. On the basis of our results, we established reference ranges for the parturient population, which differ from those provided by the manufacturer. In addition, several ClotPro® parameters demonstrated clear correlations with standard laboratory coagulation tests, supporting the physiological validity of these measurements. Specific reference values established in this study enhance the physiological interpretation of ClotPro® in obstetric care and may support its future integration into PPH management.

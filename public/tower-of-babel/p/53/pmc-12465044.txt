Human epidermal growth factor receptor-2, nectin-4, and trophoblast cell surface antigen-2 expression in endometrial carcinosarcoma
Authors: Shih-Yao Lin, Chung-Ting Chang, Ren-Chin Wu, Chiung-Ru Lai, Yi-Jen Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Highlights•HER2, nectin-4, and TROP2 were preferentially expressed in the carcinomatous component of ECS.•HER2 overexpression (3+) was identified in 7.1 % of ECS.•HER2 2+/3+ expression was associated with serous carcinoma differentiation in ECS.•No strong expression of nectin-4 or TROP2 was observed in ECS.

Introduction

Endometrial carcinosarcoma (ECS), formerly known as malignant mixed Müllerian tumor, is a rare but highly aggressive neoplasm that accounts for less than 5 % of endometrial malignancies (Cantrell et al., 2015, National Cancer Institute, 2025). Histologically, ECS is defined by a biphasic morphology, comprising both carcinomatous (epithelial) and sarcomatous (mesenchymal) components (WHO Classification of Tumors Editorial Board, 2020). Molecular studies have shown that the carcinomatous and sarcomatous components harbor similar genomic alterations, which overlap with those observed in other high-grade endometrial carcinomas (McConechy et al., 2015). In addition, ECS frequently exhibits features of epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition, supporting the hypothesis of mesenchymal transdifferentiation from an epithelial precursor (Gotoh et al., 2019, Cherniack et al., 2017). Some researchers have propose that ECS may be more accurately classified as a form of metaplastic carcinoma (McCluggage, 2002, de Jong et al., 2011). Like high-grade endometrial carcinoma, ECS is associated with poor responsiveness to standard therapies and unfavorable prognosis, highlighting the urgent need to identify novel therapeutic targets (Cantrell et al., 2015, Powell et al., 2010, Amant et al., 2005, Vaidya et al., 2006, Pham et al., 2025).

There is increasing interest in applying immunohistochemical and molecular profiling to ECS, particularly in the search for actionable therapeutic targets. According to The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) classification, most ECS fall into the copy-number high (serous-like)/p53-abnormal molecular subtypes, paralleling the classification of endometrial serous carcinoma (ESC), the prototypical representative of this category (Cancer Genome Atlas Research et al., 2013, Huvila et al., 2024). Similarly, the 2023 FIGO staging system designates ECS as an aggressive histological type, alongside ESC, which may lead to stage upshifting and influence clinical decision-making (Berek et al., 2023).

Human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2), a member of the ERBB family of receptor tyrosine kinases, is overexpressed or amplified in approximately 15–35 % of ESCs and is associated with poor outcome (Buza et al., 2013, Slomovitz, 2004, Diaz-Montes et al., 2006). Trastuzumab, a monoclonal antibody targeting HER2, has demonstrated clinical benefit in HER2-positive (immunohistochemistry score of 3+ or 2+ with gene amplification confirmed by fluorescence in situ hybridization) ESC (Fader et al., 2020, Wang et al., 2024). Notably, serous carcinoma differentiation is present in approximately 25–70 % of the epithelial components in ECS cases, and HER2 overexpression or amplification is more frequently observed in these cases (Vaidya et al., 2006, Rottmann et al., 2020, Jenkins et al., 2020, Jenkins et al., 2022). HER2 overexpression or amplification has been reported in approximately 5–15 % of ECSs, predominantly restricted to the carcinomatous component (Rottmann et al., 2020, Jenkins et al., 2022, Raspollini et al., 2005, Mizoguchi et al., 2025). Moreover, intratumoral heterogeneity of HER2 expression or amplification has been documented in ECS (Rottmann et al., 2020, Jenkins et al., 2022). Preclinical and clinical studies have suggested potential benefit of HER2-targeted therapies in HER2-positive ECS, providing a rationale for its clinical application (Lu et al., 2025, Mauricio et al., 2023, Nishikawa et al., 2023).

Nectin-4 and trophoblast cell surface antigen 2 (TROP2) are membrane glycoproteins involved in cell adhesion and proliferation, and both have emerged as targets for antibody–drug conjugates (ADCs) (Challita-Eid et al., 2016, Rosenberg et al., 2019). Nectin-4 is targeted by enfortumab vedotin, which is approved for advanced urothelial carcinoma, while TROP2 is targeted by sacituzumab govitecan, approved for metastatic triple-negative breast cancer (Rosenberg et al., 2019, Bardia et al., 2019). Although the expression of these markers has been explored in various epithelial malignancies, including endometrial carcinomas, their prevalence and clinicopathological significance in ECS remain poorly characterized. Only Raji et al. have reported TROP2 expression in approximately 35 % of ECSs (Chang et al., 2023, Bignotti et al., 2010, Raji et al., 2011).

In this study, we aimed to characterize the immunohistochemical expression of HER2, nectin-4, and TROP2 in a cohort of ECSs. We evaluated the expression in both carcinomatous and sarcomatous components and analyzed associations with clinicopathological features, with the goal of identifying potential therapeutic targets for this aggressive tumor type.

Methods

Case selection and pathological review

This study was approved by the Institutional Review Boards of Taipei Veterans General Hospital (VGHIRB No. 2022-07-037CC). Patients who underwent staging surgery and were diagnosed with ECS between 2015 and 2021 were included. All available pathology slides were reviewed to confirm the diagnosis based on the essential diagnostic criteria outlined in the World Health Organization (WHO) classification of female genital tumors (WHO Classification of Tumors Editorial Board, 2020).

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

Four-micrometer whole tissue sections containing both carcinomatous and sarcomatous components were used for IHC analysis. IHC for HER2 was performed using the 4B5 antibody (prediluted; Roche Ventana, Tucson, AZ, USA), with routine external positive and negative controls according to laboratory standards. IHC for nectin-4 IHC was performed using the EPR15613-68 antibody (dilution 1:100; BioSB, Goleta, CA, USA). IHC for TROP2 was performed using the BSB-148 antibody (dilution 1:50; Abcam, Cambridge, UK). Normal skin tissue was used as positive control for nectin-4 and TROP2 IHC. IHC for mismatch repair (MMR) proteins was performed using the M1 antibody (prediluted; Roche Ventana) for MLH1; A16-4 (prediluted; Roche Ventana) for PMS2; G219-1129 (prediluted; Roche Ventana) for MSH2; and SP93 (prediluted; Roche Ventana) for MSH6. p53 was performed using the DO7 antibody (dilution 1:50; Dako, Glostrup, Denmark). The IHC stains were performed using the Roche Ventana Benchmark ULTRA System (Roche Ventana).

Immunostaining of the whole tumor, the carcinomatous component, and the sarcomatous component were evaluated. HER2 expression in tumor cells was evaluated using the current American Society of Clinical Oncology (ASCO)/College of American Pathologists (CAP) scoring criteria for gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma in concordance with the DESTINY-PanTumor02 phase II trial (Meric-Bernstam et al., 2024). The staining result was scored as 0 (negative) if no staining was observed or membranous training in less than 10 % of tumor cells, 1+ (negative) if faint membrane staining was observed in 10 % or more tumor cells, 2+ (equivocal) if weak to moderate complete, basolateral or lateral membranous staining was observed in 10 % or more of tumor cells, and 3+ (positive) if intense complete, basolateral or lateral membrane staining was observed in 10 % or more of tumor cells. Intratumoral heterogeneity was defined as at least 5 % of the tumor areas showing a 2-degree or greater difference in staining intensity (none versus moderate, none versus strong, or weak versus strong) according to the criteria proposed by Buza et al. (2013). For nectin-4 and TROP2 expression, membranous staining in tumor cells was considered positive and evaluated using the histochemical scoring system (H-score) as in previous studies (Challita-Eid et al., 2016). H-score is the product of the percentage of tumor cells showing positive staining and staining intensity (0, no staining; 1, weak staining; 2, moderate staining; and 3, strong staining), ranging from 0 to 300. The results were further classified as negative (0–14), weak (15–99), moderate (100–199), or strong (200–300) for further analysis as previously described (Challita-Eid et al., 2016). MMR protein expression was interpreted as deficient if there was complete loss of nuclear staining in all tumor cells for any of the four MMR proteins, or if subclonal loss (involving at least 10 % of tumor cells) was observed, provided that internal controls showed intact staining. Expression was considered proficient (MMRp) if all four MMR proteins demonstrated intact nuclear staining in all tumor cells. p53 expression was interpreted as abnormal (overexpression: strong diffuse nuclear staining in at least 80 % of cells; null: no expression with an intact internal control; cytoplasmic staining with or without nuclear staining, or subclonal: at least 10 % tumor cells in discrete areas showing abnormal expression adjacent to areas showing normal expression) or normal (variable nuclear staining).

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Correlations between categorical variables were assessed using Fisher’s exact test, and comparisons of continuous variables were analyzed using the Mann–Whitney U test. Statistical significance was defined as a two-tailed P < 0.05.

Discussion

We systematically evaluated the immunohistochemical expression of HER2, nectin-4, and TROP2 in ECS, focusing on differences between the carcinomatous and sarcomatous components. HER2 overexpression (3+) was identified in four (7.1 %) cases, while an additional six (10.7 %) cases demonstrated equivocal (2+) staining. HER2 expression with scores of 2+ or 3+ was significantly more frequent in tumors with serous carcinoma differentiation compared to those without (31.0 % vs. 3.7 %, P = 0.012). This expression was predominantly localized to the carcinomatous component (25.0 % showed 1+, 14.3 % 2+, and 7.1 % 3+), while the sarcomatous component exhibited minimal staining (1+ in 7.1 %, P < 0.001). All tumors with HER2 scores of 2+ or 3+ demonstrated intratumoral heterogeneity of expression. These findings align with prior reports and support the potential utility of HER2-targeted therapies in ECSs exhibiting serous differentiation (Rottmann et al., 2020, Jenkins et al., 2022, Raspollini et al., 2005). The lack of HER2 expression in the sarcomatous component and the presence of intratumoral heterogeneity may limit the efficacy of monoclonal antibody therapies such as trastuzumab, as heterogeneous tumors may harbor HER2-negative clones capable of evading treatment. However, these tumors may still respond to HER2-targeted ADCs like trastuzumab deruxtecan, which can exert a bystander effect on adjacent tumor cells regardless of HER2 expression levels (Dumontet et al., 2023). Notably, trastuzumab deruxtecan has shown clinical efficacy in both HER2-high (2+/3+) and HER2-low (1+) tumors, as demonstrated in the STATICE trial (Nishikawa et al., 2023). In our cohort, 28.5 % of cases demonstrated HER2 expression of 1+ or greater, further supporting the rationale for considering HER2-targeted ADCs in ECS.

Nectin-4 expression was observed in 22 cases (39.3 %), all of which exhibited weak staining. Compartmental analysis revealed significantly higher expression in the carcinomatous component (53.6 % weak, 1.8 % moderate; median H-score 15.5, range 0–100) compared to the sarcomatous component (16.1 % weak; median H-score 0, range 0–35; P < 0.001). TROP2 expression was detected in 35 cases (62.5 %), including 44.6 % with weak and 17.9 % with moderate staining. Unlike the findings reported by Raji et al., no cases in our cohort exhibited strong TROP2 expression, which may reflect methodological differences (Raji et al., 2011). As observed with HER2 and nectin-4, TROP2 expression was restricted to the carcinomatous component (median H-score 41, range 0–170), with no detectable staining in the sarcomatous areas (P < 0.001). Although expression levels of nectin-4 and TROP2 were generally low to moderate, their consistent confinement to the carcinomatous component supports the concept of epithelial-restricted antigen expression in ECS. Given the overall low expression levels, patients with ECS may not be optimal candidates for anti–nectin-4 or anti–TROP2 therapies; however, the therapeutic potential of these agents in tumors with low-level expression remains uncertain and warrants further study.

We observed abnormal p53 expression, either overexpression or null, in 92.9 % of cases and showed complete concordance between the epithelial and mesenchymal components. These findings are consistent with previous studies and align with the TCGA molecular classification (despite the lack of POLE mutation testing in our cohort), which classifies 60–78 % of ECSs as copy-number high/p53-abnormal tumors, further supporting a monoclonal origin (Cherniack et al., 2017, McCluggage, 2002, Cancer Genome Atlas Research et al., 2013, Jenkins et al., 2020). Beyond serous carcinoma differentiation, no other clinicopathological parameters in our cohort showed significant correlation with HER2, nectin-4, or TROP2 expression. These findings highlight the potential value of universal immunohistochemical screening and the need for novel predictive biomarkers to optimize patient selection.

Our study has several limitations. First, it was a retrospective analysis with a relatively small cohort and limited follow-up duration. Second, POLE mutation testing was not performed, which limited comprehensive classification under the TCGA framework. Recent studies have identified POLE-mutated uterine carcinosarcomas as a distinct molecular subset with favorable prognosis and unique pathologic features (Hammer et al., 2025). Third, we did not perform HER2 in situ hybridization to clarify equivocal (2+) IHC results. Fourth, nectin-4 and TROP2 expression were evaluated using H-scores and arbitrary cutoffs, which may affect reproducibility and clinical interpretation. Finally, the absence of survival and treatment response data precluded assessment of the prognostic or predictive significance of the studied markers.

In summary, our study demonstrates that HER2, nectin-4, and TROP2 are predominantly expressed in the carcinomatous component of ECS, with HER2 scores 2+ or 3+ significantly associated with serous carcinoma differentiation. The epithelial-restricted and heterogeneous expression patterns of these markers underscore the potential of targeted therapies, particularly HER2-directed ADCs, in a subset of ECS patients. While the low expression levels of nectin-4 and TROP2 may limit the immediate applicability of ADCs targeting these antigens, their consistent localization to the epithelial component warrants further exploration. These findings provide a foundation for future therapeutic strategies and highlight the need for expanded molecular profiling in ECS to guide precision oncology approaches.

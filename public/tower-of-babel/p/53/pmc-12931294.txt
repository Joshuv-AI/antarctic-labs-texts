Total flavonoids of Abelmoschus manihot (L.) medic attenuate early renal injury in diabetic kidney disease by modulating the LINC00665/miR-379-5p axis
Authors: Nan Li, Yuhe Zeng, Yaohui Fang, Jie Wu, Harvest F Gu, Ying Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Abstract‌Context:‌ Huangkui capsule (HKC), derived from Abelmoschus manihot (L.) Medic, is clinically utilized for diabetic kidney disease (DKD) treatment. Its primary bioactive components are the total flavonoids of A. manihot (L.) (TFA). Evidence has implicated the negative interaction between long non-coding RNA LINC00665 and miR-379-5p in DKD pathogenesis.‌Objective:‌ To investigate the therapeutic effects of TFA on early DKD renal injury and its mechanism involving LINC00665/miR-379-5p axis modulation.‌Materials and Methods:‌ Db/db mice and non-diabetic controls received TFA or vehicle. Urinary albumin/creatinine ratio (UACR) was measured with ELISA; renal histopathology was assessed with H&E staining. SV40-MES-13 and HK-2 cells were treated with TFA-intestinal absorption fluid (TFA-IAF). LINC00665 and miR-379-5p expression analyzed with RT-qPCR and Western blot. LINC00665/miR-379-5p interaction validated with dual-luciferase reporter assays. Functional effects assessed using LINC00665 siRNA silencing and miR-379-5p mimics.‌Results:‌ TFA administration reduced UACR in db/db mice compared to vehicle-treated controls and ameliorated renal histological damage. Renal miR-379-5p expression was decreased in db/db mice versus non-diabetic controls, while TFA treatment in vivo upregulated miR-379-5p expression. TFA-IAF treatment in vitro modulated the expression of genes implicated in DKD progression, specifically affecting cell proliferation and ECM accumulation pathways in SV40-MES-13 and HK-2 cells. TFA-IAF downregulated LINC00665 expression in the cultured cells. Furthermore, dual-luciferase assays confirmed miR-379-5p binding to LINC00665, as miR-379-5p mimics suppressed wild-type LINC00665 reporter activity. Silencing LINC00665 increased miR-379-5p expression, potentiating by co-treatment with TFA.‌Conclusions:‌ The LINC00665/miR-379-5p axis contributes to DKD pathogenesis. TFA exerts therapeutic effects against early renal injury in DKD by suppressing LINC00665 and consequently upregulating miR-379-5p activity.Keywords: Abelmoschus manihot (L.), diabetic kidney disease, LINC00665, miR-379-5p

Introduction

Abelmoschus manihot (L.) Medic, commonly known as Huangkui in traditional Chinese medicine (TCM), has a longstanding history of medicinal application primarily valued for its anti-inflammatory properties.‌ The earliest recorded medicinal use dates back to the fourth century AD by the physician and Daoist scholar Ge Hong (AD 283–343). Modern pharmacological research has substantiated its therapeutic potential, leading to the development of the standardized Huangkui capsule (HKC) formulation (Li et al. 2021).‌ Prepared from an ethanol extract of A. manihot flowers, HKC received formal approval from the China State Food and Drug Administration (approval number: Z19990040) in 1999. Extensive research confirms that HKC’s primary bioactive components are total flavonoids of A. manihot (TFA), including hibifolin, hyperoside, isoquercetin, myricetin, quercetin, quercetin-3-O-robinobioside, and rutin (Lai et al. 2009; Guo et al. 2016; Yang et al. 2018; Li et al. 2021; Diao et al. 2023). Clinically, HKC demonstrates efficacy in treating kidney diseases like diabetic kidney disease (DKD), with multicenter trials validating its ability to reduce albuminuria and proteinuria in type 2 diabetes (T2D) patients (Zhao et al. 2022).

DKD represents a major microvascular complication of diabetes, clinically characterized by progressive macroalbuminuria/overt proteinuria and recognized as the leading cause of end-stage kidney disease (Thomas et al. 2015; Tuttle et al. 2022).‌ Its pathogenesis involves complex interactions between hyperglycemia-induced metabolic dysregulation, hemodynamic alterations, inflammatory pathways, oxidative stress, and genetic/epigenetic factors (Alicic et al. 2017; Gu 2019; Dwivedi and Sikarwar 2025). Despite advances, molecular mechanisms remain incompletely elucidated, necessitating further exploration of regulatory pathways. Emerging evidence implicates microRNAs (miRNAs) as critical regulators of cellular processes (proliferation, apoptosis, differentiation) in DKD pathology (Allison 2016; Kato et al. 2016; Assmann et al. 2018; Mahtal et al. 2022).‌ Our previous work identified miR-379-5p as a suppressor of mesangial cell proliferation and extracellular matrix accumulation in DKD via the LIN28B/let-7 pathway (Li et al. 2019). Recent findings indicate that miR-379-5p expression is negatively regulated by the long intergenic noncoding RNA LINC00665, which promotes epithelial-mesenchymal transition phenotypes in other pathologies like breast cancer (Ji et al. 2020).‌ Given LINC00665′s emerging role as a biomarker in disease diagnostics and therapeutics (Wen et al. 2018; Ghafouri-Fard et al. 2022; Zhong et al. 2022; Yuan et al. 2024), we hypothesize its interaction with miR-379-5p may contribute significantly to DKD pathophysiology.‌

To investigate this hypothesis, the present study prepared intestinal absorption fluid (IAF) derived from TFA and analyzed its effects on LINC00665 and miR-379-5p expression in renal SV40-MES-13 mesangial cells and HK-2 proximal tubular cells.‌ Complementary in vivo validation assessed miR-379-5p regulatory functions in kidneys of db/db mice, a well-established model of T2D and DKD (Sharma et al. 2003; Azushima et al. 2018), both with and without TFA intervention.‌ This integrated approach provides novel insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying A. manihot’s therapeutic efficacy in DKD treatment.‌

Material and methods

Animal models

The mice of db/db (BKS.Cg-Dock7m +/+ Leprdb/J) and C57BL/KsJ at the age of 8 weeks were purchased from animal experimental center of Nanjing University (Nanjing, China). All mice were males and housed in a specific pathogen-free (SPF) barrier environment at animal experimental center, China Pharmaceutical University (CPU). The animal room was maintained at a temperature of 24 °C ± 2 and a humidity level of 50 ± 10%, with a 12 h light/12 h dark cycle. After the adaption for one week, body weight and blood glucose levels in all mice were measured weekly. The urine samples were collected with metabolic cage (DXL-XS, Fengshi, Suzhou, China) for 6 h. Microalbuminuria and creatinine were measured with ELISA quantitative kits (Elabscience Biotechnology, China). When the blood glucose levels were ≥16.7 mmol/l and urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR) was ≥200 ng/μg for two successive days, the db/db mice were considered as DKD. The db/db mice with DKD were then randomly divided into two groups i.e., with and without TFA treatment (TFA, n = 6 each), while C57BL/KsJ mice as non-diabetic control group (CON, n = 6). The mouse was placed in the anesthesia chamber with isoflurane (induction: 2.5%; maintenance: 1.5%) for anesthesia. The complete anesthesia was verified by firmly pinching the paw/tail. The absence of response confirmed readiness for surgery/experiments.

Histopathological analysis of kidneys

Hematoxylin and Eosin staining was applied for histopathological analyses of kidney sections in the mice of CON, DKD and TFA groups. In the present study, whole kidney tissues were embedded with paraffin embedding. The blocks were then sectioned at 4 μm thickness by using HistoCore Bio-Cutter (Leica Biosystem, Germany). The whole kidney sections were stained with H&E reagents (BASO, Zhuhai, China) and then analyzed with a CX23 light microscope (Olympus, Japan). For each kidney, more than five sections were analyzed with Image-Pro Plus (version 6.0.0260).

TFA and its chemical analysis

TFA were extracted and purified from A. manihot (L.) flower as previously reported (Yang et al. 2018) and supplied by Suzhong Pharmaceutical Group Co., Ltd (Batch21122403, Taizhou, China). One gram of TFA was made from 46.01 g of A. manihot (L.) flower and contained 142.9 mg of isoquercetin, 188.7 mg of hibifolin, 189.6 mg of hyperoside, 33.2 mg of myricetin, 29.4 mg of quercetin, 133.0 mg of quercetin-3-O-robinobioside, 2.4 mg of rutin and 2.7% water per gram. All were calculated as the dry products and quantitatively analyzed by using the standard protocol of HPLC-Q-TOF/MS and stock solution of each flavonoid (Diao et al. 2023).

Preparation of TFA-IAF

TFA-IAF was prepared by using the protocol as previously described (Zhang et al. 2017). Briefly, the rats were maintained in fasting conditions for 12 h. After anesthesia, the intestine of each rat was removed and washed with Tyrode buffer solution. As seen in Figure S1, the intestines were then cut into four 14 cm segments and made into sacs. The sacs were filled with Tyrode buffer and incubated in Magnus’ bath for 5 min to reach equilibration. The Tyrode buffer was replaced with TFA solution (0.46 mg/mL), which was maintained at 37 °C and continuously injected with O2/CO2 (95%/5%) in ALC-M Tissue Organ Bath System (Aoce Medical Technology Co Ltd, Shanghai, China). After 2 h, serosal side solutions containing the absorbed constituents (2 mL) were drained into tubes. Finally, the TFA-IAF was filtered with a micro filtrate membrane (0.22 μm) and stored at −20 °C for use. The original concentration of TFA-IAF was 2 mg/mL.

Cell culture and assessment of cell viability

Mouse glomerular mesangium cells (SV40-MES-13 cells) were obtained from the Cell Bank of China Pharmaceutical University (Nanjing, China). The culture medium consists of a 3:1 mixture of DMEM and F12 medium supplemented with 10% FBS. The cell viability was measured by 3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyl tetrazolium bromide (MTT) assay and seeded onto 96-well plates at a density of 5000 cells per well for 24, 48 and 72 h, respectively, and treated with low (LG, 5.5 mM) or high glucose (HG, 30 mM) for 24 h. MTT solution was then added to the wells and incubated for 2 h. The absorbance at 570 nm wavelength was measured with Synergy™ H4 Microplate Reader (BioTek, USA) and the cell viability was finally calculated. Figure S2 shows the optimal concentration and incubation period of TFA-IAF screened by MTT assay.

Flow cytometry

For cell cycle analysis, the SV40-MES-13 were harvested, suspended in 300 µL of phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), fixed with 700 µL ethanol staining for 30 min at 4 °C and subjected to PI/RNase (Beyotime Biotechnology) for 15 min at 37 °C, then analyzed by flow cytometry (FACS Canto II, Bd Biosciences).

Real time RT-PCR

Total RNAs from either kidney tissues or cultured cells were extracted by using a Trizol regent kit (Ambion, Shanghai, China). The concentration of total RNAs was detected with Nano 6000 of bioanalyzer 2100 system (Agilent Technologies, USA). The cDNA was synthesized in M-MuLV reverse transcriptase system (Illumina, NEB, USA). Real time RT-PCR experiments were done with CFX-Connect Real-Time System (BioRad, USA) and all used primers are listed in Table S1.

RNA immunoprecipitation

The experiments with RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) were conducted adhering to the guidelines provided by the manufacturer (BersinBio, Guangzhou, China). Human kidney (HK-2) cells were obtained from the Cell Bank of the China Pharmaceutical University. HK-2 cells were cultured in DMEM/F12 (Gibco) with 10% FBS. The digested HK-2 cells were transferred to an enzyme-free EP tube in which the prepared RIP lysate was introduced. The cells were then incubated on ice for 20 min. The supernatant was removed after centrifuged (15,000 rpm) at 4 °C for 5 min. The prepared RIP lysate (having the same volume as the cell sediment) was added to the tubes and incubated on ice for 5 min. At the end of the incubation, the corresponding magnetic beads were added and incubated at 4 °C overnight. The purified RNA samples were finally extracted, reversely transcribed to cDNAs. LINC00665 and miR-379-5p were detected by RT-qPCR after captured by 5 μg of AG02 (Beyotime), and an IgG control antibody (BersinBio).

siRNA transfection of LINC00665

For LINC00665 knockdown, HK-2 cells were transfected with LINC00665 siRNA using Lipofectamine 2000 transfection reagent (Azenta Life Sciences). The cells were first starved in serum-free and Penicillin-Streptomycin Solution-free DMEME/F12 basal medium (Gibco) for 24 h, added in the amount of 2.5 μL LINC00665 siRNA and 2.5 μL lipo2000 transfection reagent per well with additional 200 μL of basal medium, and then transfected for 8 h at 37 °C. The scrambled siRNA and LINC00665 siRNA sequences are summarized in Tables S2 and S3, respectively. At the end of transfection, the cells were switched to the medium containing 10% Premium Grade Fetal Bovine Serum (FBS, CellMax) and continued to be cultured for 24–48 h. After the transfection, the cells were then transferred to medium containing 10% serum and incubated for 24–48 h.

Fluorescence in situ hybridization

The probe of LINC00665, which for the fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) detection, was conjugated with FITC-fluorescence (Bersinbo) and the sequence of the probe was attached in Table S4. The HK-2 cells were fixed using 4% paraformaldehyde, subsequently permeabilized by dropwise addition of 20 μL of proteinase K working solution and fixed again with 1% paraformaldehyde, after which 20 μL of probe-containing hybridization solution was overlaid on the cells and incubated at 42 °C overnight. The cells were then stained using DAPI dye for 10 min. Finally, the processed cell crawls were placed under a laser confocal microscope for observation. Specific experimental procedures according to the reagent instructions.

Western blot

Renal tissues or cultured cells were lysed using RIPA lysate (Cobio Biotechnology, Wuhan, China). The total protein concentration was examined with a BCA protein assay kit (Cobio Biotechnology). The samples of protein were fractionated by 10-12% sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis and then electro-transferred to a polyvinylidene fluoride membrane (Millipore, USA). All antibodies used in Western blot are summarized in Table S5.

Luciferase reporter assay

For the luciferase reporter assay, 293 T cells were co-transfected with miR-379-5p mimic or mimic control and 1000 ng of LINC00665-wt/mut or LIN28B-wt/mut. Cells were seeded in 96-well plates and collected after 48 h transfection. The luciferase activity was determined by a Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System (GenePharma) according to the manufacturer’s recommendations, as previously described.

Statistics analysis

All data analyses were done using SPSS Statistics for Windows (version 25.0; IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY, USA) and R software (version 3.6.0). Figures were generated with Prism software (version 8.0; GraphPad Software, LLC, San Diego, CA, USA). Comparisons between the groups were conducted with Wilcoxon test or Student’s t-test. If the variance was homogenous, the individual comparisons were done using Tukey’s multiple comparison tests after statistical significance was established with ANOVA. Data were expressed as means ± standard error (SEM). p < 0.05 was considered as significance.

Results

Reduction of UACR in DKD after TFA treatment

In this study, we first performed experiments using db/db mice, as this animal model has been extensively utilized for T2D and DKD research (Figure 1A). Compared to the CON group mice, db/db mice with DKD exhibited significantly elevated UACR levels. Following TFA treatment, we observed a marked reduction in UACR in db/db mice with DKD (Figure 1B). Additionally, histopathological analysis of kidney tissues stained with hematoxylin and eosin revealed that glomerular volume, mesangial matrix, and basement membrane thickness – all increased in DKD compared to CON – were significantly reduced after TFA treatment (Figure 1C-E). These findings align with our recent reports (Diao et al. 2023; Yu et al. 2023).

Effect of TFA on miR-379-5p expression in DKD kidneys

We then assessed multiple biomarkers (TGF-β1, FN, Col-IV, CCND1, and CDK4) at both mRNA and protein levels to evaluate glomerular/tubular injury in DKD kidneys and subsequent recovery following TFA treatment. As shown in Figure 1F-U&J-O, these biomarkers were upregulated in DKD but downregulated after TFA administration. Notably, miR-379-5p expression was significantly decreased in DKD kidneys compared to CON (p = 0.037), but its activity was restored following TFA treatment (p = 0.008) (Figure 1I).

Effects of TFA-IAF on glomerular mesangial cell proliferation and ECM accumulation

To elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying TFA’s therapeutic effects on DKD through miR-379-5p regulation, we established an in vitro model using high glucose (HG, 30 mM)-stimulated SV40-MES-13 cells. Following the standardized protocol, TFA-IAF was prepared and applied to the experimental system. The MTT assay was employed to assess cellular activity variations between treatment and control groups, serving to identify the optimal dosage and mechanism of TFA-IAF action. SV40-MES-13 cells, derived from SV40 virus-transformed transgenic mouse glomerular mesangial cells, retained characteristic mesangial cell properties. In this study, we determined the optimal TFA-IAF concentration and incubation period. TFA-IAF treatment significantly inhibited HG-induced excessive proliferation of SV40-MES-13 cells in a time- and dose-dependent manner, with tested concentrations of 0.78%, 1.56%, and 3.12% at 24, 48, and 72-hour time points. Maximum inhibition was achieved at 3.12% concentration with 48-hour exposure (Figure 2A). Molecular analysis revealed that HG stimulation markedly upregulated TGF-β1, FN, and Col-IV expression at both mRNA and protein levels, indicating ECM overproduction and cellular damage. Notably, 3.12% TFA-IAF treatment effectively reversed these HG-induced pathological changes. Compared to the HG group, TFA-IAF treatment significantly downregulated TGF-β1, FN, and Col-IV expression (Figure 2B-G&H), demonstrating its potent inhibitory effects on mesangial cell proliferation and ECM accumulation.

Effects of TFA-IAF on cell cycle progression in SV40-MES-13 cells

To comprehensively evaluate TFA-IAF’s effects on SV40-MES-13 cells, cell cycle analysis was performed using flow cytometry, as this approach provides quantitative assessment of cell cycle distribution and proliferation status. As shown in Figure 3A-C, representative flow cytometry profiles illustrate cell cycle distributions under LG (left), HG (middle), and HG + 3.12% TFA-IAF (right) conditions, while Figure 3D presents the quantitative analysis of cell cycle phase distribution across these three experimental groups. The analysis revealed that HG stimulation significantly increased the proportion of cells in S phase (indicating enhanced proliferative activity) while reducing the relative percentage of cells in G1/G2 phases. TFA-IAF treatment effectively reversed this HG-induced cell cycle perturbation, decreasing S-phase cell population while increasing G1-phase proportion (Figure 3D), demonstrating its inhibitory effect on HG-driven excessive cell proliferation. At the molecular level, HG stimulation markedly upregulated CDK4 expression at both mRNA and protein levels compared to LG controls. This HG-induced CDK4 overexpression was significantly attenuated by TFA-IAF treatment (Figure 3F&G), suggesting that TFA-IAF regulates cell cycle progression through CDK4 modulation. Interestingly, CCND1 expression remained unchanged under both HG stimulation and TFA-IAF treatment conditions (Figure 3E&G), indicating differential regulation of cell cycle components. These findings collectively demonstrate that TFA-IAF ameliorates DKD-related renal damage by modulating cell cycle progression through selective regulation of CDK4 expression.

Regulatory effects of TFA-IAF through miR-379-5p/LIN28B axis

Building upon our previous findings that miR-379-5p attenuates renal fibrosis by modulating the LIN28/let-7 axis in DKD (Li et al. 2019), we employed dual-luciferase reporter assays to validate the direct interaction between miR-379-5p and LIN28B in SV40-MES-13 cells (Figure 4A). The experimental design incorporated both wild-type and mutant LIN28B sequences (binding sites illustrated in Figure 4B), demonstrating that miR-379-5p binding to wild-type LIN28B significantly suppressed firefly luciferase activity (reduced fluorescence intensity), while mutation of the binding site abolished this inhibitory effect. MTT assays revealed that miR-379-5p silencing enhanced cellular proliferation and diminished TFA-IAF’s inhibitory effects at both 24 h and 48 h time points (Figure 4C-D). The knockdown efficiency was confirmed at mRNA level (Figure 4E). Notably, no significant differences in FN and Col-IV expression (mRNA/protein levels) were observed between KD and TFA-IAF-treated KD groups (Figure 4F-G). Under HG conditions, LIN28B expression was markedly upregulated and further elevated following miR-379-5p knockdown. Interestingly, 3.12% TFA-IAF treatment did not significantly modulate LIN28B expression (Figure 4H-J). Western blot analysis of FN, Col-IV and LIN28B protein levels is presented in Figure 4K. These results collectively indicate that TFA-IAF ameliorates DKD progression, at least partially, through miR-379-5p-dependent mechanisms, while LIN28B regulation appears to be miR-379-5p-dependent rather than directly influenced by TFA-IAF.

Regulatory effects of TFA-IAF on LINC00665/miR-379-5p axis in HK-2 cells

While LINC00665 has been identified in the human genome (GeneCards | LINC00665 RNA Gene) but not yet in mice, we conducted complementary experiments using human kidney-2 (HK-2) cells, an immortalized human proximal tubule epithelial cell line derived from renal cortex. Dual-luciferase reporter assays in HK-2 cells confirmed functional interaction between LINC00665 and miR-379-5p (Figure 5A), with the mutated LINC00665 sequence shown in Figure 5B. Comparative analysis of fluorescence intensity between wild-type (WT) and mutant (MUT) groups revealed significant binding disruption upon mutation. FISH analysis (Figure 5C) demonstrated cytoplasmic localization of LINC00665 (green fluorescence), with DAPI (blue) staining nuclei. To characterize TFA-IAF’s regulatory effects, we examined LINC00665 expression at 12h, 24h, and 48h time points using 3.12% and 0.78% TFA-IAF concentrations. The results demonstrated time- and dose-dependent inhibition of LINC00665 expression by TFA-IAF, with higher concentration (3.12%) showing sustained suppressive effects over time (Figure 5D-K). These findings establish TFA-IAF as a potent modulator of the LINC00665/miR-379-5p regulatory axis in human renal epithelial cells.

Discussion

The present study systematically investigates the therapeutic potential of TFA from A. manihot (L.) in alleviating early-stage renal injury in DKD through modulation of the LINC00665/miR-379-5p axis. Our comprehensive approach integrated both in vivo and in vitro experimental models to elucidate the underlying molecular mechanisms.

Initial investigations in db/db mice demonstrated that TFA treatment significantly reduced urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR), a key clinical marker of renal dysfunction in DKD. Parallel in vitro studies using TFA-IAF (the active fraction) revealed its protective effects through dual mechanisms: (1) inhibition of high glucose (HG)-induced mesangial cell proliferation, as determined by MTT assay-optimized concentrations, and (2) attenuation of extracellular matrix (ECM) accumulation. Molecular analyses showed TFA-IAF effectively reversed HG-induced upregulation of fibrotic markers (TGF-β1, FN, and Col-IV) at both mRNA and protein levels, with consistent findings observed in renal tissues of TFA-treated db/db mice. Cell cycle analysis via flow cytometry further demonstrated that TFA-IAF induced G1 phase arrest and downregulated key cell cycle regulators (CCND1 and CDK4), providing mechanistic insight into its anti-proliferative effects.

Building upon our previous discovery that miR-379-5p suppresses mesangial cell proliferation and ECM accumulation via LIN28B regulation (Li et al. 2019), we established that TFA-IAF exerts its therapeutic effects through miR-379-5p activation. Lentivirus-mediated miR-379-5p knockdown (KD) experiments revealed that: (1) TFA-IAF’s anti-proliferative effects were significantly attenuated in KD cells, (2) its suppression of fibrotic markers was diminished, and (3) LIN28B expression was elevated. Importantly, in vivo studies confirmed decreased renal miR-379-5p expression in db/db mice was reversed by TFA treatment, establishing the miR-379-5p/LIN28B pathway as a key therapeutic target.

Our investigation of LINC00665, a recently characterized lncRNA primarily studied in oncology (Wen et al. 2018; Ghafouri-Fard et al. 2022), represents the first exploration of its role in DKD pathogenesis. In HK-2 cells under HG conditions, we demonstrated that: (1) TFA-IAF dose- and time-dependently inhibited LINC00665 expression, (2) FISH localized LINC00665 to the cytoplasm, and (3) RIP assays confirmed its functional interaction with miR-379-5p within the RISC complex through AGO2 binding. Genetic knockdown experiments established a reciprocal regulatory relationship, with LINC00665 silencing upregulating miR-379-5p expression. Luciferase reporter assays provided direct evidence of their molecular interaction, confirming LINC00665 as the upstream regulator of the miR-379-5p/LIN28B axis in TFA’s mechanism of action.

While ethical limitations prevented human kidney tissue analysis, our integrated findings from multiple experimental systems strongly support the clinical relevance of the mechanism. The consistency between our in vivo and in vitro results, combined with emerging clinical evidence from our recent studies (Diao et al. 2023; Yu et al. 2023; Wu et al. 2024), suggests that TFA’s regulation of the LINC00665/miR-379-5p axis represents a novel therapeutic strategy for early-stage DKD intervention.

In conclusion, this study provides compelling experimental evidence that TFA from A. manihot (L.) mitigates early renal injury in DKD through a previously unrecognized mechanism involving the LINC00665/miR-379-5p/LIN28B regulatory axis. The finding not only advances our understanding of DKD pathogenesis but also highlights the potential of natural product-derived therapeutics for diabetic complications.

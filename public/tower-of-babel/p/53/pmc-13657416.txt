Is routine terminal ileum intubation justified during screening colonoscopy? Evidence from a regional screening program
Authors: Victor-Mihai Sacerdoţianu, Alexandra-Georgiana Bocioagă, Carmen-Nicoleta Oancea, Dumitru Rădulescu, Mirela Marinela Florescu, Dan Nicolae Florescu, Dan Ionuţ Gheonea
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background/Aim: Although colonoscopy represents the cornerstone of colorectal cancer (CRC) screening, the incremental diagnostic yield of routine terminal ileum (TI) intubation remains insufficiently defined, particularly in fecal immunochemical test (FIT) positive patients. This study evaluated the feasibility, diagnostic performance, and clinical relevance of routine TI intubation in participants enrolled in a regional CRC screening program. Patients, Materials and Methods: We performed a retrospective analysis of individuals aged 50–74 years who underwent colonoscopy within a regional CRC screening program in South-West Romania between November 2020 and December 2023. Patients with successful TI intubation were compared with individuals in whom TI exploration was not performed. Demographic characteristics, bowel preparation quality, sedation parameters, comorbidities, procedural variables, and histopathological (HP) outcomes were assessed. Results: The analysis included 1512 complete colonoscopies, of which TI intubation was successfully achieved in 41.86% of procedures. Macroscopic TI abnormalities were identified in 1.74% of patients, including erosions, ulcerations, hyperemia, and nodular or atrophic changes. HP analysis showed findings suggestive of inflammatory bowel disease in 27.27%, celiac disease-related changes in 9.09%, and nonspecific inflammatory alterations in 63.64% of abnormal cases. Successful TI exploration was more commonly observed in patients receiving sedation and in those with better bowel preparation. No significant associations were observed regarding age, sex, or FIT values. Conclusions: Routine TI intubation demonstrated a relatively limited diagnostic yield in this screening population. Although clinically meaningful findings were uncommon, TI assessment may remain valuable in selected clinical settings. Further prospective studies are needed to establish its role in screening colonoscopy.

Introduction

Colorectal cancer (CRC) programs are designed to detect cancer at early and localized stage in asymptomatic individuals and simultaneously to reduce disease burden through the identification and removal of precancerous lesions [1, 2, 3, 4].

Beyond lesion detection, histopathological (HP) evaluation remains essential for understanding the biological behavior of colorectal neoplasia. Recent studies have demonstrated the relevance of proliferation markers and molecular pathways involved in tumor development and progression, supporting the role of comprehensive tissue assessment in CRC screening programs [5, 6, 7].

To achieve these goals, a complete colonoscopy is the technique applied to patients involved in a CRC program. Cecum evaluation is the endpoint of a full-length colon examination and is considered a key quality indicator of the procedure, while the role of terminal ileum (TI) intubation remains less clearly defined [8, 9].

Clear indications that a patient needs an ileal intubation colonoscopy include abdominal pain (right lower quadrant), diarrhea, hematochezia, unexplained iron-deficiency anemia, high suspicion of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), tuberculosis or abnormal imaging [4, 8, 10, 11, 12, 13].

During routine colonoscopy performed in the absence of a specific clinical indication, intubation of the ileocecal valve and inspection of the TI are not considered standard components of the examination. There are no recommendations whether TI intubation is mandatory in patients with a positive fecal immunochemical test (FIT) and usually an individualized medical decision will conclude [14]. In line with European Society of Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ESGE) and American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) recommendations, TI intubation has limited diagnostic value for colorectal neoplasia detection, being mainly reserved for confirming completeness of the examination in cases where cecal landmarks are not clearly visualized.

During TI intubation, specific abnormalities are not identified in most cases. Even when lesions are detected, they are predominantly nonspecific and rarely yield diagnostically meaningful information for a defined underlying condition [15, 16].

Aim

The objective of the study was to assess the frequency and diagnostic yield of TI exploration in patients enrolled in a regional CRC screening program.

Patients, Materials and Methods

The actual retrospective study was conducted between June 2024 and September 2024. The analyzed data was available on the register of the CRC screening program in the Southwestern Oltenia Region, Romania.

This register contains all reports of 1550 colonoscopies performed at a single center, Research Center of Gastroenterology and Hepatology (CCGH), University of Medicine and Pharmacy of Craiova, Romania, between November 2020 and December 2023, within the regional CRC screening initiative.

Inclusion criteria of our study contain patients with age between 50 and 74 years old, positive fecal immunochemical test (FIT) or high-risk patients with rectal bleeding and/or palpable rectal tumor on digital rectal examination, signed informed consent and in addition, a full detailed report. If biopsies or resection were required, HP data was also collected.

Exclusion criteria include the lack of intubation of the TI or incomplete data about ileal findings.

All procedures were performed by experienced gastroenterologists aided by endoscopy nurses in accordance with quality standards regarding colonoscopies. The equipment consisted of an Olympus EVIS X1 tower and two types of colonoscopes, CF HQ190 and CF H190.

Collected data contains gender, age, TI intubation findings, associated diseases, antiplatelets and anti-coagulation status, quality of colon preparation – Boston Bowel Preparation Scale (BBPS), fit test value, sedation dose and withdrawal time.

The samples were processed using the usual technique of embedding in paraffin with 10% neutral buffered formalin and stained with the usual Hematoxylin and Eosin (H&E) stain and analyzed using a Nikon Eclipse Ei microscope. The images were realized using Lucia 5 software.

Statistical analysis was performed using IBM Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) Statistics for Windows, version 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and compared between groups using the independent-samples Student’s t-test, while categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages and compared using the χ2 (chi-squared) test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. A two-tailed p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

A total of 51 437 enrolled in the CRC screening program and 45 049 individuals completed FIT and had valid results. Of these, 2825 (6.27%) tested positive and were referred for colonoscopy. An additional 88 individuals were classified as high-risk based on the risk assessment questionnaire and were referred directly for colonoscopy. Among eligible participants, 46.8% declined colonoscopy despite medical recommendation, indicating suboptimal adherence to the screening pathway (Figure 1).

Of the 1512 completed colonoscopies performed during the study period, in 58.13% of cases, ileal intubation was not assessed due to a not mandatory CRC screening condition and were included as the control group. A total of 633 (41.87%) patients with explored TI were included in our research as experimental group to quantify the real yield impact of the TI intubation on a CRC screening program (Table 1).

The results of the FIT tests demonstrated a slightly higher mean value in the group of those who also underwent TI exploration (222.45 vs. 213.04, p=0.6546) but without statistical significance, since the lesions that could cause occult bleeding were in both groups in the colon and not in the small intestine (Figure 2A; Table 2). Age was not a criterion regarding the choice of ileal intubation or not, so patients had a similar mean (Figure 2B; Table 2).

Most sedated patients (89.26%) underwent TI exploration, whereas among unsedated patients, only 10.74% underwent TI intubation (Table 2).

The dose of Propofol was higher in patients in whom the TI was explored, probably due to the prolonged insertion time, which, unfortunately, was not available in this study, as it was not a mandatory requirement in the CRC screening program (209.15 vs. 200.08, p=0.0899) (Figure 2C).

The withdrawal time was similar, respecting the ESGE standards in force of a minimum of seven minutes but with a target of nine minutes. In case the patient presents lesions that require detailed evaluation, resection or biopsy, the time was extended (Figure 2D).

The sex distribution showed a predominance of males both in the group of patients in whom the TI was explored (55.61% vs. 44.39%) and in the control group (52.99% vs. 47.01%) (Table 2).

Regarding anesthetic risk, the American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) classification was used in our study. Most patients were shown to be ASA class 2 in both patient groups.

The lowest number of patients who underwent TI intubation was observed among those with a higher-risk ASA, class 4 and 5, accounting 0.78% of total cases.

A total of 59.88% of patients in the study group had either cardiac disease, diabetes mellitus, or multiple comorbidities, whereas among patients in the control group, the percentage with these conditions was slightly higher, at 66.42%.

Macroscopic abnormalities of the TI were identified in 11 of the 633 (1.74%) patients who underwent successful ileal intubation. The observed findings included erosions, ulcerations, mucosal hyperemia, and nodular or atrophic lesions. Of the 11 patients included in this subgroup, HP assessment revealed a heterogeneous distribution of mucosal abnormalities. Specifically, 27.27% of patients showed HP features consistent with IBD (Figure 3A, 3A1, 3B). In addition, 9.09% of patients presented histological features consistent with celiac disease (Figure 4A, 4A1, 4A2). The rest of the patients, representing 63.64%, exhibited nonspecific inflammatory changes (Figure 5A, 5A1, 5A2). No tumors, including lymphomas, neuroendocrine tumors or gastrointestinal stromal tumors were observed.

The mean age of patients in whom lesions was identified was 63.36±7.62 years, which was very similar to the mean age recorded among patients without lesions, namely 62.74±6.89 years. This difference was not statistically significant, as reflected by a p-value of 0.7922. Therefore, in the analyzed cohort, age did not appear to be significantly associated with the presence of lesions.

As noted earlier, patients receiving Propofol sedation during colonoscopy were more likely to undergo successful TI intubation than those examined without sedation.

Bowel preparation quality also appeared to play an important role in the successful intubation of the TI. Higher BBPS scores, reflecting better bowel cleansing, were associated with increased rates of successful TI intubation.

The mean length of TI explored during the procedure was 9.05±3.53 cm, indicating a relatively extensive evaluation of this intestinal segment. This is clinically relevant, as careful inspection and sampling of the TI can help detect subtle endoscopic or histological abnormalities, including findings suggestive of Crohn’s disease or other forms of IBD, nonspecific inflammatory changes, and less commonly, histological features compatible with celiac disease.

Discussions

The diagnostic yield of routine ileal examination during colonoscopies performed outside screening settings varies considerably across different study populations, with detection rates reported between 0.4% to 67% [17].

While numerous studies have explored the diagnostic value of TI intubation in these contexts, limited data are available regarding its utility in routine colonoscopy, with recent studies suggesting a low diagnostic yield [12, 18, 19].

This uncertainty underscores the need for further evaluation of its role, especially among FIT-positive screening populations.

The characteristics of our cohort were patients’ age and positive FIT test. This aspect can lead to different findings in TI in the general population who undergo a colonoscopy without a certain indication.

TI intubation was accomplished in 41.86% of complete colonoscopies in our study population. This finding closely resembles the rate reported by Alkhatib & Kumar (40.8%), whereas considerably lower rates were reported by Dąbkowski et al. (12.1%) and Harewood et al. (7.1%). Variations among studies may be related to heterogeneity in clinical practice, including endoscopist-related factors, procedural indications, and differences in policies regarding routine TI intubation during CRC screening colonoscopy [18, 19, 20].

In contrast to previous studies reporting that TI intubation was more frequently performed in younger individuals and that abnormal ileal findings occurred more commonly at younger ages, our results did not reveal significant age-related differences among patients with and without TI intubation or between those with and without lesions [9, 18, 19].

A slight male predominance was observed in both study groups, with a comparable sex distribution among patients with and without TI exploration. Previous studies evaluating TI intubation have reported inconsistent findings regarding sex-related differences, with some authors describing slightly higher TI assessment rates in female patients, whereas others found no significant differences between sexes. Overall, our findings support the notion that gender may not represent a major determinant of TI exploration during screening colonoscopy [19, 20].

Data suggest that most ileal findings on TI are ulcerations (92.7%) incidentally identified in asymptomatic individuals are typically not associated with significant HP abnormalities and tend to follow a favorable clinical course, with spontaneous resolution at follow-up colonoscopy (66.7–72.4%), suggesting a self-limited process and minimal clinical relevance in the absence of additional supportive findings [21].

In our study, TI inspection revealed macroscopic abnormalities in 11 of the 633 patients who underwent successful ileal intubation, resulting in a detection rate of 1.74%. This relatively low prevalence is consistent with previous studies evaluating the diagnostic yield of routine ileal intubation in patients undergoing screening or surveillance colonoscopic examinations. Alkhatib & Kumar reported mucosal abnormalities in 37 patients, representing 3.7% among the patients in whom TI intubation was successfully performed during screening colonoscopy [18]. Similarly, Meral et al. found endoscopically abnormal TI findings in 11 patients, corresponding to 3.3% of their screening cohort. HP abnormalities considered clinically relevant were infrequently observed, affecting only 1.9% of patients who underwent TI inspection during colonoscopy, regardless of the underlying indication [11].

In a surveillance colonoscopy population, Zwas et al. found abnormal HP findings in 2.7% of patients undergoing routine ileoscopy [22]. Compared with these studies, the frequency of macroscopic TI abnormalities in our cohort falls within the lower range of previously reported rates. By contrast, Kennedy et al., in a large screening colonoscopy cohort of 6408 patients from the Mayo Clinic, reported markedly lower diagnostic yields, with endoscopic abnormalities detected in only 1% of patients and HP changes identified in only 0.3% of cases [23]. In our cohort, microscopic analysis of biopsies obtained from macroscopically abnormal TI mucosa revealed HP features consistent with IBD in 27.27% of cases and changes compatible with celiac disease in 9.09%. Most cases, representing 63.64%, showed nonspecific inflammatory changes. These findings indicate that although macroscopic ileal abnormalities were uncommon, a proportion of abnormal cases were associated with potentially clinically relevant diagnoses. Among patients with TI findings consistent with IBD, concomitant colonic involvement was identified in only one case.

The pattern of endoscopic abnormalities observed in our study, including erosions, ulcerations, hyperemia, and nodular or atrophic mucosal changes, is broadly comparable with previously published data. In the study by Alkhatib & Kumar, the abnormal endoscopic findings in the TI consisted of ulcerations in 35.14% of cases, nodular/granular mucosa in 24.32%, erosions in 16.22%, mucosal erythema in 18.92%, inflamed mucosa in 2.70%, and ileal polyps in 2.70%, based on a total of 37 identified lesions [18].

Differences between studies may reflect variation in patient selection, indication for colonoscopy, endoscopist practice patterns, definitions of abnormal ileal findings, and whether histological confirmation was systematically obtained. Importantly, although TI intubation may reveal mucosal abnormalities in a small proportion of patients, the available literature suggests that clinically meaningful HP findings are uncommon in asymptomatic screening or surveillance populations.

The perception of colonoscopy as a painful and potentially embarrassing procedure may represent a significant barrier to patient attendance in screening programs, adherence to recommended surveillance, and early diagnostic assessment of colorectal symptoms [24, 25]. Successful TI exploration was observed more frequently in patients who underwent colonoscopy under Propofol sedation compared with those examined without sedation. This finding suggests that sedation may facilitate a more complete endoscopic examination by improving patient tolerance and procedural conditions, while also reducing the likelihood that endoscopists avoid additional exploration because of concerns regarding patient discomfort in unsedated procedures. In addition, in our study, Propofol administration tended to be higher in patients who underwent successful TI exploration, possibly reflecting more prolonged or technically demanding procedures. However, insertion time was not available for analysis because this parameter was not routinely collected within the CRC screening program. Previous studies have reported an association between deeper sedation and higher TI intubation rates, whereas in other study was observed that patients with pathological TI findings were more likely to receive lighter sedation. Collectively, these findings suggest that the association between sedation practices and TI exploration outcomes remains complex and warrants further investigation [4, 26].

The relatively extensive length of TI exploration observed in our cohort may have contributed to a more comprehensive assessment of this segment, potentially increasing the likelihood of identifying subtle endoscopic or HP abnormalities with potential clinical relevance.

Achieving a high-quality colonoscopy depends not only on safely reaching the cecum but also on adequate bowel preparation, meticulous inspection of the mucosa for the detection of all precancerous lesions, as well as their complete and curative resection [8, 27].

Bowel preparation quality also appeared to influence successful TI intubation in our cohort, as higher BBPS scores were associated with increased TI exploration rates. This observation is consistent with previous studies reporting a median Boston score of 8 among successfully examined patients and showing that better bowel preparation, particularly in the right colon, was associated with significantly higher TI intubation rates. Collectively, these findings support the importance of adequate bowel cleansing as a factor contributing to a more complete colonoscopic examination and potentially improving its diagnostic yield [18, 28].

The slightly higher prevalence of comorbidities among patients without TI exploration may reflect a tendency to limit procedural duration in individuals with increased clinical complexity, prioritizing essential diagnostic and therapeutic aspects of colonoscopy.

Given the consistently low prevalence of clinically relevant ileal findings reported in recent studies, routine ileal intubation during FIT-positive colonoscopy may not represent an efficient use of endoscopic resources. The inconsistency in reported rates of TI intubation probably reflects multiple contributing factors, such as variability in study cohorts, differences in endoscopist skill, the lack of formal guideline recommendations, the perceived limited clinical value of routine ileal inspection during screening colonoscopy, and time-related procedural considerations [12, 18].

Thus, while TI intubation can be considered a marker of complete and careful colonoscopic examination, its routine use in all patients should be balanced against procedure time, technical feasibility, and the relatively low likelihood of clinically significant findings in screening populations.

Conclusions

Our findings indicate that routine TI intubation has a relatively low diagnostic yield in a FIT-positive CRC screening population. Although macroscopic ileal abnormalities were occasionally identified, clinically relevant HP findings were uncommon and more than 50% were represented by inflammatory or nonspecific changes. These observations are consistent with previous studies reporting limited additional diagnostic benefit from routine TI assessment in screening settings. Nevertheless, ileal inspection may remain useful in selected clinical scenarios, particularly in patients with suspected IBD, chronic diarrhea, gastrointestinal bleeding, abnormal imaging, anemia, or other symptoms suggestive of small bowel pathology. Well-designed prospective studies are still required to clarify whether routine TI intubation provides meaningful clinical benefit in the setting of CRC screening.

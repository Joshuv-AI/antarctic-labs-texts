LINC00324 induces nucleus pulposus cell apoptosis in intervertebral disc degeneration via miR-143-3p targeted TRAIL/DR4/DR5 axis
Authors: Jialin Zhang, Min Zhao, Zewei Shan, Yueliang Chang, Yaqin He
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIntervertebral disc degeneration (IDD) serves as a predominant pathogenic driver of spinal degenerative diseases and low back pain. Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) have emerged as important regulators involved in the pathogenesis of IDD. Despite this, the specific contributions of LINC00324, a particular lncRNA, to the advancement of IDD are not yet well understood. The study seeks to elucidate the functional role and explore the potential molecular mechanisms of LINC00324 in IDD.MethodsRT-qPCR was employed to analyze LINC00324, miR-143-3p, and TRAIL levels in intervertebral disc (IVD) tissues from IDD patients and control subjects, followed by correlation analysis. Bioinformatics tools, dual-luciferase reporter assays, and RNA pull-down experiments were conducted to validate the regulatory interactions within LINC00324/miR-143-3p/TRAIL in nucleus pulposus cells (NPCs). In vitro, NPCs were stimulated with lipopolysaccharide (LPS) to construct an IDD cell model. RT-qPCR, western blot, CCK-8 assay, and flow cytometry were utilized to evaluate the effects on the levels of LINC00324/miR-143-3p/TRAIL/DR4/DR5, as well as the proliferation and apoptosis of NPCs. Additionally, extracellular matrix (ECM)-related proteins and inflammatory factors were detected by western blot and ELISA, respectively. In vivo, a rat IDD model was established through fibroannular puncture. Radiological analysis (X-ray), HE staining, TUNEL staining, and western blot were used to assess disc structural changes, NPC apoptosis, and the levels of miR-143-3p/TRAIL/DR4/DR5 and apoptosis-related proteins.ResultsClinical evidence showed that LINC00324 and TRAIL levels were increased, and miR-143-3p was decreased in IDD samples. miR-143-3p had the negative correlation coefficient with TRAIL and LINC00324. Furthermore, LINC00324 acted as a sponge for miR-143-3p, and miR-143-3p specifically attenuated the downstream of TRAIL level. In vitro, the LPS-induced IDD model showed decreased NPC viability, increased apoptosis, disrupted ECM metabolism (downregulated Collagen II and Aggrecan, upregulated MMP13), elevated inflammatory factors, and activated TRAIL/DR4/DR5 axis. Silencing LINC00324 or overexpressing miR-143-3p alleviated these pathological changes. Furthermore, miR-143-3p partially counteracted the promoting influences of LINC00324 overexpression on NPC apoptosis, ECM degradation, inflammatory response, and TRAIL/DR4/DR5 axis activation in the IDD model. In vivo, LINC00324 overexpression exacerbated IDD in rats (decreased disc height index, disrupted NPC tissue structure, enhanced NPC apoptosis, aggravated ECM degradation, inhibited miR-143-3p expression, and activated TRAIL/DR4/DR5 axis), while LINC00324 knockdown attenuated these degenerative changes.ConclusionsIn conclusion, LINC00324 promotes IDD progression by inducing NPC apoptosis, disrupting ECM metabolism, and enhancing inflammatory response via sponging miR-143-3p to activate the TRAIL/DR4/DR5 axis.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.Graphical Abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s13062-026-00879-0.Keywords: IDD, LINC00324, miR-143-3p, TRAIL/DR4/DR5 axis, Nucleus pulposus cells, Apoptosis

Introduction

Intervertebral disc degeneration (IDD) is a primary contributor to the development of degenerative spinal conditions and associated chronic low back pain [1]. It is reported that the incidence of IDD is 9.4% globally, and it is progressively increasing as the population ages [2]. Currently, the treatment of IDD mainly focuses on alleviating clinical symptoms instead of addressing the underlying causes. Therefore, analyzing the pathophysiology of IDD holds considerable importance for identifying novel diagnostic and therapeutic targets, which potentially enabling more effective strategies.

Intervertebral discs (IVD) are composed of the following three parts: from the lateral to the medial part of the disc, the cartilage endplate, annulus fibrosus (AF) and nucleus pulposus cells (NPCs), respectively [3]. NPCs play a central role in maintaining IVD homeostasis by synthesizing and regulating extracellular matrix (ECM) components (e.g., Collagen II, Aggrecan). Pathologically, NPC dysfunction is characterized by reduced proliferation, increased apoptosis, and impaired ECM metabolism [4]. Additionally, elevated inflammatory factors in NPCs are closely associated with IDD progression by exacerbating NPC apoptosis and ECM degradation [4]. Therefore, targeting the apoptosis process, ECM metabolism disorder, and inflammatory response of NPCs may be important strategies to delay IDD.

TRAIL is a protein encoded by the TNFSF10 gene [5]. Two membrane-binding receptors of TRAIL are death receptor (DR) and decoy receptor, among which DR include DR4 and DR5 [6]. Both of DR4 and DR5 contain death domains and can transmit death signals to cells. It’s reported that the up-regulation of TRAIL in NPCs and activation of receptor death receptor 4 (DR4)/DR5, thus inducing apoptosis of NPCs [7, 8]. However, the mechanisms that regulate the expression and activation of TRAIL/DR4/DR5 axis in NPCS are unclear.

Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) represent a class of endogenous transcripts exceeding 200 nucleotides [9]. Currently, the study of the pathogenesis of IDD has become a hot and focal point, and the role of lncRNA in IDD has attracted much attention [10–12]. According to recent research, lncRNA expression is aberrant in IDD [10–12]. Importantly, lncRNA regulates NPCs apoptosis, proliferation, ECM, and inflammation [10–12]. LINC00324 is located on chromosome 17 (NC_000017.11). In a variety of cells, LINC00324 is involved in cell proliferation and apoptosis [13, 14]. Evidence suggested that LINC00324 in IDD samples was significantly elevated and correlated with IDD development [15].

The study aimed to elucidate the role of LINC00324 in IDD. Herein, experimental approaches were conducted to clarify whether LINC00324 promoted TRAIL level through sponging miR-143-3p, as well as its effects on NPCs proliferation, apoptosis, and ECM metabolism.

Materials and methods

Patient samples

The IVD tissues were extracted from forty IDD patients who had lumbar spine surgery in General Hospital of Ningxia Medical University. At the same time, IVD tissues from ten patients with lumbar fractures were collected as control tissues. All IVD tissues were frozen in liquid nitrogen. Inclusion criteria for IDD patients: Confirmed diagnosis of IDD based on clinical symptoms, imaging findings (such as X-ray, MRI showing typical signs of disc degeneration), and medical history. Exclusion criteria for IDD patients: Patients with other serious systemic diseases, autoimmune diseases, or a history of malignancy. Inclusion criteria for control patients: Diagnosed with lumbar fractures without any signs of IDD confirmed by imaging and medical history. Exclusion criteria for control patients: Patients with a history of intervertebral disc diseases, spinal deformities, or other spinal pathologies. Patients with systemic diseases that could potentially influence the results of the study were also excluded. Preoperative lumbar MRI images of IDD patients were reviewed, and the degree of disc degeneration was assessed according to the Pfirrmann grading system (grades I–V), where grade I represents a normal disc and grade V represents the most severe degeneration. For correlation analysis, grades I–III were classified as mild (early-stage) IDD, and grades IV–V as severe (advanced-stage) IDD. The project was approved by the Ethics Committee of General Hospital of Ningxia Medical University, and all patients provided written informed consent.

Cell culture

Human NPCs (cat. #4800, ScienCell Research Laboratories, Carlsbad, CA, USA) were cultured in Nucleus Pulposus Cell Medium (cat. #4801, ScienCell Research Laboratories, Carlsbad, CA, USA) containing 10% FBS (Hyclone, Logan, USA) at 37 °C with 5% CO₂.

Cell treatment and transfection

In order to mimic IDD in vitro, NPCs were subjected to 100 ng/mL lipopolysaccharide (LPS, Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, USA) for 24 h [16]. NPCs were treated with PBS (Gibco, Grand Island, USA) serving as the control group.

LINC00324 overexpression plasmid (LINC00324 OE) and empty plasmid (NC OE), Small interfering RNA (siRNA)-mediated LINC00324 (si-LINC00324) sequences were provided by Shanghai Genepharma. miR-143-3p mimic and inhibitor sequences (Genepharma, Shanghai, China) were used to induce and suppress miR-143-3p level. NPCs with logarithmic growth to about 80% were digested, and plated into 6-well plates. Transient transfection was conducted using Lipofectamine 2000 or RNAiMax reagents (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, USA). Bioinformatics analysis

Bioinformatic prediction of potential miR-143-3p binding sites within LINC00324 and the 3’-untranslated region (3’ -UTR) of TRAIL mRNA was conducted using the StarBase online platform (version 3.0, https://starbase.sysu.edu.cn/). The analysis was performed using the default parameters of the database to identify miRNA-lncRNA and miRNA-mRNA interactions with high confidence.

Dual luciferase reporter assay

The LINC00324 and TRAIL DNA fragments containing tthe predicted miR-143-3p binding sequences were constructed into psi-CHECK to construct the wild-type plasmid and recorded as psi-CHECK-LINC00324-wild (LINC00324 WT) and psi-CHECK-TRAIL-wild (TRAIL WT). Meanwhile, nucleotides in binding fragments were mutated using gene mutation technology and psi-CHECK-LINC00324-mutant (LINC00324 MUT) and psi-CHECK-TRAIL-mutant (TRAIL MUT) plasmids were constructed. NPCs were co-transfected with miR-143-3p mimic along with each reporter plasmid. The luciferase activity was measured using Dual luciferase reporter assay Kit (Promega, Madison, USA).

RNA pull down

Biotin-labeled miR-143-3p (Bio-miR-143-3p) and Bio-NC (RiboBio, China) were transfected into NPCs. Then, cell lysate was incubated with streptavidin beads (Thermo, USA) for 2 h. The enrichment of LINC00324 and TRAIL was quantified by RT-qPCR after elution of the RNA complex bound to beads.

CCK-8 assay

NPCs were incubated with 10 µL CCK8 solution (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) at 37℃ containing 5% CO2 for 2 h. The optical density values of 450 nm were measured using a microplate reader (Bio-Rad, Hercules, USA).

Apoptosis analysis by flow cytometry

Flow cytometry was used to detect the apoptosis of in vitro cultured NPCs. After the corresponding treatment, NPCs were collected and resuspended in 1 × Binding Buffer at a concentration of 1 × 10⁶ cells/mL. According to the instructions of the Annexin V-FITC/PI Apoptosis Detection Kit (Beyotime, Shanghai, China), 5 µL Annexin V-FITC and 5 µL PI staining solution were added to 100 µL cell suspension. Following gentle mixing and incubation in the dark for 15 min, 400 µL of 1× Binding Buffer was added to each tube. Apoptotic rates were determined using a flow cytometer (BD Biosciences, San Jose, USA).

ELISA

The levels of inflammatory factors (TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6) in cell culture supernatants were detected using commercial ELISA kits (Abcam, Cambridge, UK), following the manufacturers’ instructions. The absorbance (OD value) was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader.

Animal studies

LINC00324 OE and si-LINC00324 were separately cloned into lentiviral vectors. Healthy male Sprague-Dawley (SD) rats were randomly assigned to four groups (n = 8): control group; IDD group, IDD model with the treatment of NC lentiviral vector; IDD+LINC00324 group, IDD model with the treatment of LINC00324-overexpressing lentiviral vector; IDD + si-LINC00324 group, IDD model with the treatment of si-LINC00324 lentiviral vector.

The animal experiment protocol was approved by General Hospital of Ningxia Medical University. In operation group, pentobarbital sodium (40 mg/kg) was used as anesthetic drug and intraperitoneal injection was performed. To induce IDD, the intervertebral discs at the L4/L5 and L5/L6 levels were punctured using a microsurgical drill. A 25-gauge needle was inserted into the annulus fibrosus to a depth of 2 mm (full-thickness puncture of the annulus fibrosus) and remained in place for 30 s [17]. The infection was controlled by intraperitoneal injection of penicillin 200,000 U for 5 days. 7 days after surgery, the previously punctured intervertebral disc was re-exposed through a small incision, followed by intradiscal injection of 3 µL lentiviral vector suspension using a 33-gauge Hamilton needle connected to a microsyringe. Rats were euthanized 4 weeks after viral injection, and the targeted IVDs were collected.

X-ray

At 28 days after surgery, the rats were analyzed by X-ray. X-ray imaging is performed using an X-ray irradiation system (Kubtec, Hartford, USA). The disc height and the height of the upper and lower vertebral bodies were measured with blinding, and the disc height index (DHI) was calculated.

Hematoxylin-Eosin (HE) staining

The prepared paraffin sections (5 μm thick) were stained with 0.5% hematoxylin (Solarbio, Beijing, China) for 5 min, and differentiated with 1% hydrochloric acid alcohol for 10 s. After that, the sections were counterstained with eosin (Solarbio, Beijing, China) for 3 min. Stained slides were imaged using a light microscope (Nikon, Tokyo, Japan).

TUNEL staining

TUNEL staining was used to detect the apoptosis of NPCs in rat IVD tissues. Apoptosis in rat IVD tissues was evaluated using the TUNEL Apoptosis Detection Kit (Red fluorescence, Beyotime, China). Tissue sections were treated with 20 µg/mL protease K (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, USA) at 37℃ for 15 min. 50 µL TUNEL mixture was applied to each section and incubated in the dark at 37℃ for 1 h. DAPI was used to counterstain the cell nuclei for 5 min. The apoptotic cells (with red fluorescence) were imaged under a fluorescence microscope (Nikon, Japan).

RT-qPCR

Total RNA was isolated from tissues and cells using miRNeasy Mini Kit (Qiagen, Hilden, Germany) or TRIzol® reagent (Takara, Dalian, Japan). Then, RNA was transcribed into cDNA using miRNA 1st Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (by stem-loop) (Vazyme, Nanjing, China) or Prime Script™ RT Reagent Kit (TaKaRa, Dalian, Japan). The reverse primer of miR-143-3p was 5′-GTCGTATCCAGTGCAGGGTCCGAGGTATTCGCACTGGATACGACGAGCTA‐3′. RT-qPCR assay was performed using miRNA Unimodal SYBR qPCR Master Mix (Vazyme, Nanjing, China) or SYBR Premix Ex Taq I (TaKaRa, Dalian, Japan). The primer sequences were shown in Table 2.

Statistical analysis

All data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) using SPSS software (IBM, Armonk, USA). Prior to parametric statistical analysis, the assumptions of normality and homogeneity of variance were strictly evaluated. Normality of all quantitative data was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test, and homogeneity of variance was evaluated using Levene’s test. Only when the data met the assumptions of normality (P > 0.05) and homogeneity of variance (P > 0.05), parametric tests were adopted. Student’s unpaired t-test was used for comparing the differences between two independent groups. One-way ANOVA followed by Bonferroni post hoc test was used for comparing the differences among more than three groups. The selection of Bonferroni post hoc test was justified based on the study’s comparison strategy: this study involves not only comparisons between each treatment group and the reference group (Control group or IDD group), but also pairwise comparisons between different treatment groups (e.g., IDD+LINC00324 group vs. IDD+LINC00324 + miR-143-3p mimic group). To evaluate the linear relationships between the expression levels of LINC00324, miR-143-3p, and TRAIL in human IVD tissues, Pearson correlation analysis was conducted. The correlation coefficient (R) was calculated, and a two-tailed test of significance was performed. P < 0.05 was considered a statistically significant difference.

Results

Abnormal expression of LINC00324, miR-143-3p, and TRAIL in IVD tissues of IDD patients and their correlation with disease severity

Initial analysis of clinical IVD specimens from IDD patients revealed distinct expression profiles of LINC00324, miR-143-3p, and TRAIL. RT-qPCR assays showed compared to control samples, IDD samples had a higher LINC00324 level (mean ± SD: 1.00 ± 0.21 in control vs. 3.86 ± 1.12 in IDD, 3.9-fold increase, P < 0.001, Fig. 1A). Besides, miR-143-3p level was decreased in IDD samples (1.00 ± 0.22 in control vs. 0.57 ± 0.14 in IDD, 43% reduction, P < 0.001, Fig. 1B). TRAIL expression was markedly increased in IDD samples (1.00 ± 0.19 in control vs. 3.27 ± 0.82 in IDD, 3.3-fold increase, P < 0.001, Fig. 1C). Correlation analysis further demonstrated a significant negative association between miR-143-3p and LINC00324 (R = -0.79, P < 0.001, Fig. 1D), as well as between miR-143-3p and TRAIL (R = -0.62, P < 0.001, Fig. 1E). Meanwhile, LINC00324 had a high positive correlation coefficient with TRAIL level (R = 0.53, P < 0.001, Fig. 1F). In summary, LINC00324 and TRAIL were abnormally upregulated, while miR-143-3p was downregulated in IVD tissues of IDD patients, and miR-143-3p had an inverse correlation with LINC00324 and TRAIL levels whereas LINC00324 was positively correlated with TRAIL level.

To evaluate whether the expression levels of LINC00324, miR-143-3p, and TRAIL correlate with IDD severity, we reclassified the 40 IDD patients according to their average expression levels of each molecule. Using the median expression value as the cut-off, patients were divided into high-expression and low-expression groups for LINC00324, miR-143-3p, and TRAIL, respectively. The associations between expression levels and clinicopathological characteristics, including age, BMI, gender, and Pfirrmann grade, were analyzed. As shown in Table 4, there were no significant differences in age, BMI, or gender between the LINC00324 high-expression and low-expression groups. However, a significantly higher proportion of patients in the LINC00324 high-expression group had a Pfirrmann grade ≥ IV (severe IDD) compared to the low-expression group, indicating that elevated LINC00324 expression is associated with advanced IDD stage. Notably, the proportion of patients with severe IDD (Pfirrmann grade ≥ IV) was significantly lower in the miR-143-3p high-expression group compared to the low-expression group (Table 5). For TRAIL, similar to LINC00324, the high-expression group had a significantly higher proportion of patients with severe IDD compared to the low-expression group (Table 6). Collectively, these clinical association analyses demonstrate that LINC00324 and TRAIL expression levels positively correlate with IDD severity, while miR-143-3p expression inversely correlates with disease stage, further supporting their potential as diagnostic biomarkers for IDD progression.

LINC00324 sponges miR-143-3p which directly targets TRAIL

Next, we explored the connection among LINC00324, miR-143-3p, and TRAIL. The Online Database identified putative miR-143-3p binding sites within both LINC00324 and TRAIL sequences (Fig. 2A). Dual luciferase reporter assay showed that miR-143-3p mimic significantly decreased the luciferase activity of LINC00324 WT (to 0.50 ± 0.06 of NC mimic, P < 0.01) and TRAIL 3’-UTR WT (to 0.60 ± 0.02 of NC mimic, P < 0.01), but did not affect the activity of LINC00324 MUT (1.20 ± 0.20, P > 0.05) or TRAIL MUT (0.95 ± 0.09, P > 0.05) (Fig. 2B). RNA pull down assays further validated direct binding between miR-143-3p and both LINC00324 (4.8-fold enrichment compared to Bio-NC, P < 0.001) and TRAIL (5.5-fold enrichment, P < 0.001) (Fig. 2C). RT-qPCR showed that overexpression of LINC00324 increased LINC00324 level (8.3-fold increase, P < 0.001, Fig. 2D). Besides, overexpression of LINC00324 suppressed miR-143-3p level (57% reduction, P < 0.01, Fig. 2E), and upregulated TRAIL protein (3.3-fold increase, P < 0.001, Fig. 2F) and mRNA level (2.5-fold increase, P < 0.001, Fig. 2G). RT-qPCR demonstrated the transfection effectiveness of the miR-143-3p mimic, and miR-143-3p mimic increased miR-143-3p level (6.5-fold increase, P < 0.001, Fig. 2H). Furthermore, miR-143-3p mimic inhibited TRAIL mRNA (39% reduction, P < 0.05, Fig. 2I) and protein level (55% reduction, P < 0.01, Fig. 2J). Collectively, these findings support a ceRNA mechanism in which LINC00324 sequesters miR-143-3p, thereby derepressing TRAIL expression in IDD.

Inhibition of LINC00324 regulates NPCs apoptosis and ECM degradation in IDD model in vitro

NPCs were stimulated with LPS to establish IDD cell model. RT-qPCR assay showed LPS increased LINC00324 level by 4.3-fold (P < 0.001, Fig. 3A). To illuminate the role of LINC00324 in NPCs proliferation and apoptosis, NPCs were stimulated with LPS and transfected with si-LINC00324. The si-LINC00324 group suppressed LINC00324 expression by 63% (P < 0.001, Fig. 3B). LPS decreased cell viability to 43 ± 4.12% of control (P < 0.001), and knockdown of LINC00324 restored viability to 88 ± 1.25% in LPS-treated NPCs (P < 0.001, Fig. 3C). Meanwhile, LPS treatment of NPCs increased apoptosis, and inhibition of LINC00324 reversed apoptosis (P < 0.01, P < 0.001, Fig. 3D). LPS stimulation promoted Caspase-8 (3.4-fold increase, P < 0.001) and cleaved-caspase 3 expression (2.4-fold increase, P < 0.001), while knockdown of LINC00324 attenuated apoptosis-related protein expression (Caspase-8: 56% reduction P < 0.001; cleaved-caspase 3: 47% reduction, P < 0.01) (Fig. 3E). Above all, knocking down LINC00324 suppressed LPS-induced NPCs apoptosis.

To further evaluate the effect of LINC00324 on IDD-related ECM degradation, we detected ECM synthesis markers (Collagen II, Aggrecan) and degradation marker (MMP13) expression by western blot. The LPS-induced IDD cell model displayed pronounced reductions in Collagen II (62% reduction, P < 0.001) and Aggrecan (53% reduction, P < 0.01) protein levels, accompanied by a marked upregulation of MMP13 expression (1.7-fold increase, P < 0.01) (Fig. 3F). By contrast, transfection with LINC00324-targeting siRNA partially restored Collagen II (2.1-fold increase, P < 0.01) and Aggrecan (1.9-fold increase, P < 0.01) and reduced MMP13 (27% reduction, P < 0.05) (Fig. 3F). Besides, ELISA showed that LPS increased TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6 in NPCs (TNF-α: 18.9-fold increase; IL-1β: 13.2-fold increase; IL-6: 19.3-fold increase; P < 0.001), while knockdown of LINC00324 abolished the inflammatory factors of LPS-treated NPCs (TNF-α: 46% reduction; IL-1β: 59% reduction; IL-6: 60% reduction; P < 0.01, P < 0.001) (Fig. 3G). Collectively, knockdown of LINC00324 alleviated LPS-induced ECM metabolism disorder in NPCs.

MiR-143-3p regulates NPCs apoptosis and ECM degradation by targeting the TRAIL/DR4/DR5 axis in IDD model in vitro

In view of our previous results demonstrating that miR-143-3p was downregulated in IVD tissues of IDD patients, and considering the key role of TRAIL/DR4/DR5 axis in NPCs apoptosis, we hypothesized that miR-143-3p may affect NPCs apoptosis in IDD cell models by regulating this axis. As expected, miR-143-3p was decreased in the IDD cell model (62% reduction, P < 0.001, Fig. 4A). To continue to test this hypothesis, miR-143-3p mimic was stimulated to IDD cell models, and the successful transfection was confirmed, miR-143-3p mimic transfection increased miR-143-3p level by 5.6-fold (P < 0.001, Fig. 4B). TRAIL, DR4 and DR5 mRNA levels in IDD cell models were abnormally elevated (TRAIL: 2.9-fold increase; DR4: 2.3-fold increase; DR5: 2.6-fold increase; P < 0.001), while miR-143-3p mimic reversed these increases (TRAIL: 59% reduction; DR4: 53% reduction; DR5: 49% reduction; P < 0.01, P < 0.001) (Fig. 4C). Protein levels showed similar trends (TRAIL: 3.6-fold increase; DR4: 2.5-fold increase; DR5: 2.5-fold increase; P < 0.001), and miR-143-3p mimic reversed these increases (TRAIL: 56% reduction; DR4: 53% reduction; DR5: 52% reduction; P < 0.01, P < 0.001) (Fig. 4D). Functional experiments suggested that miR-143-3p mimic increased cell viability from 50 ± 3.73% (IDD) to 86 ± 1.54% (P < 0.01, Fig. 4E), decreased apoptosis from 27 ± 3.22% to 13 ± 1.54% (P < 0.01, Fig. 4F). Besides, miR-143-3p mimic suppressed apoptosis-associated protein expression in LPS-challenged NPCs (Caspase-8: 56% reduction, P < 0.001; cleaved-caspase 3: 50% reduction; P < 0.01; Fig. 4G).

Since we have suggested that LINC00324 directly targets miR-143-3p, which targets TRAIL, we investigated whether LINC00324 regulates NPCs apoptosis via miR-143-3p/TRAIL/DR4/DR5 axis. Then, we transfected LINC00324 OE and miR-143-3p mimic into IDD cell models. As expected, overexpression of LINC00324 increased LINC00324 expression by 4.6-fold (P < 0.001) and decreased miR-143-3p level by 60% (P < 0.001), and miR-143-3p mimic increased miR-143-3p level by 4.6-fold (P < 0.001) in IDD cell model (Fig. 5A and B). Meanwhile, miR-143-3p partially reversed TRAIL/DR4/DR5 axis expression induced by LINC00324 overexpression in IDD cell model (TRAIL mRNA level: 47% reduction; DR4 mRNA level: 33% reduction; DR5 mRNA level: 32% reduction; P < 0.01; TRAIL protein level: 35% reduction; DR4 protein level: 23% reduction; DR5 protein level: 33% reduction; P < 0.05, P < 0.01; Fig. 5C and D). Moreover, LINC00324 overexpression decreased cell viability from 52 ± 4.27% (IDD) to 28 ± 1.88% (P < 0.001), increased apoptosis from 26 ± 1.76% to 45 ± 3.45% (P < 0.001), and elevated Caspase-8 and cleaved-caspase 3 levels by 1.8-fold and 2.0-fold (P < 0.001). miR-143-3p mimic co-transfection restored viability to 51 ± 2.95% (P < 0.05 vs. IDD+LINC00324), reduced apoptosis to 28 ± 0.56% (P < 0.01), and decreased Caspase-8 and cleaved-caspase 3 levels (Caspase-8: 34% reduction, P < 0.01; cleaved-caspase 3: 41% reduction; P < 0.05) (Fig. 5E and G). However, co-transfection with miR-143-3p mimic partially reversed these changes (P < 0.05, P < 0.01, Fig. 5H). In addition, miR-143-3p mimic partially abolished the promoting role of LINC00324 overexpression on inflammatory factors (TNF-α: 37% reduction; IL-1β: 34% reduction; IL-6: 55% reduction; P < 0.05, P < 0.001, Fig. 5I). Collectively, miR-143-3p partially rescued the roles of LINC00324 in NPCs apoptosis and ECM metabolism.

LINC00324 accelerates IDD progress in a rat model

To explore LINC00324’s impact on IDD, a rat model with punctures was created, and the rats were injected si-LINC00324 or LINC00324 OE. RT-qPCR showed that LINC00324 was increased 3.2-fold in IDD model (P < 0.001, Fig. 6A). LINC00324 level was inhibited by LINC00324 knockdown (76% reduction, P < 0.001), and induced by LINC00324 overexpression (3.4-fold increase, P < 0.001) (Fig. 6A). Then, X-ray imaging showed that the IDD group had lower DHI than the control group (Fig. 6B). Overexpression of LINC00324 significantly decreased DHI, on the contrary, knockdown of LINC00324 attenuated IDD progress (Fig. 6B). HE staining revealed disorganized NP tissue architecture and a loss of typical cellular morphology in IDD group rats (Fig. 6C). Furthermore, the NP tissues were almost disappeared in the LINC00324 group, and knockdown of LINC00324 reversed the change of NP tissue structure (Fig. 6C).

LINC00324 promotes the progression of IDD by inducing apoptosis and ECM degradation of NPCs in IDD model in vivo

To further explore the underlying mechanisms at the cellular level, we then conducted experiments to investigate how LINC00324 affects the apoptosis of NPCs in the IDD model in vivo. TUNEL assay showed that NPCs apoptosis occurred in IDD group, and overexpression of LINC00324 further induced NPCs apoptosis, knockdown of LINC00324 attenuated NPCs apoptosis (Fig. 7A). In addition, compared to the control group, Caspase-8 and cleaved-caspase 3 levels were increased by 3.3-fold and 2.5-fold in IDD group (P < 0.001, Fig. 7B). Meanwhile, LINC00324 overexpression increased Caspase-8 and cleaved-caspase 3 levels by 1.7-fold and 2.1-fold (P < 0.01, P < 0.001), while knockdown of LINC00324 reversed IDD-induced apoptosis-related protein expression (Caspase-8: 52% reduction, P < 0.01; cleaved-caspase 3: 48% reduction, P < 0.01) (Fig. 7B). The IDD model group showed significant ECM metabolism disorder: Collagen II and Aggrecan protein levels were reduced by 68% and 51% (P < 0.001), while MMP13 expression was significantly elevated by 48% (P < 0.05) (Fig. 7C). Compared to the IDD group, the IDD + LINC00324 overexpression group exhibited further deterioration of ECM degradation, Collagen II and Aggrecan decreased by 66% and 69% (P < 0.001), and MMP13 increased by 63% (P < 0.05) (Fig. 7C). Conversely, the IDD + si-LINC00324 group showed partial restoration of ECM metabolism, Collagen II and Aggrecan increased by 2.3-fold and 1.8-fold (P < 0.01, P < 0.001), and MMP13 decreased by 49% (P < 0.05) (Fig. 7C). These results indicated that LINC00324 overexpression exacerbated ECM degradation in rat NP tissues, while LINC00324 knockdown alleviated this pathological process and further confirming that LINC00324 regulates ECM metabolism to promote IDD progression in vivo. Besides, RT-qPCR showed that compared to the control group, miR-143-3p level was downregulated to 0.42 ± 0.06 in the IDD group (58% decrease, P < 0.001, Fig. 7D). Overexpression of LINC00324 further suppressed miR-143-3p to 0.25 ± 0.04 (40% decrease vs. IDD, P < 0.01, Fig. 7D). In contrast, knockdown of LINC00324 restored miR-143-3p to 1.20 ± 0.11 (186% increase vs. IDD, P < 0.01, Fig. 7D). As expected, LINC00324 overexpression promoted TRAIL/DR4/DR5 axis expression and knockdown of LINC00324 inhibited TRAIL/DR4/DR5 axis (P < 0.05, P < 0.01, P < 0.001, Fig. 7E and F).

Discussion

At present, traditional treatments have been less effective in reducing the intensity and duration of pain. As a new therapeutic option, biological targeted therapy has attracted more and more attention [18–20]. IDD is mainly caused by a series of pathological changes in the NPCs in the IVD, which are associated with proliferation and apoptosis [21, 22]. It has been found that inflammatory response can cause the apoptosis of NPCs, which is crucial to the pathophysiological mechanism underlying IDD [23]. Thus, it is crucial to continue researching how inflammation functions in the mechanism of IDD. LPS promotes chronic inflammation in many systems and tissues of the body. LPS, a well-characterized inducer of inflammatory responses, has been shown to mimic IDD-like pathological changes in NPCs by promoting inflammation, apoptosis, and ECM degradation [24, 25]. In our study, LPS stimulation significantly increased LINC00324 expression in NPCs, accompanied by decreased cell viability, increased apoptosis, disrupted ECM metabolism (downregulated Collagen II and Aggrecan, upregulated MMP13), and elevated inflammatory factors. More importantly, we demonstrated that LINC00324 promotes IDD progression by targeting miR-143-3p/TRAIL axis and regulating TRAIL/DR4/DR5 activation, which offers a fresh avenue for investigation about biologically focused treatment of IDD.

The research on the pathogenesis of IDD at cellular and molecular level has become a hot topic and focus. TRAIL, DR4 and DR5 are implicated in the incidence and development of IDD [26–28]. A clinical analysis study showed that the CT and TT genotype frequencies of TRAIL gene in IDD patients were significantly reduced. Immunohistological analysis revealed TRAIL and DR4/DR5 levels in IDD tissues were increased, as were the levels of apoptosis-related Fas pathway and Caspase-8 [27, 28], . Histological studies showed that the apoptosis of NPCs and AFs in all IDD samples was greater than regular IVD cells. Additionally, compared with AFs, DR4-positive apoptotic cells were high in NPCs, which was positively related to the severity of IDD [29]. These investigations verified that the activation of TRAIL/DR4/DR5 axis in NPCs is an important factor causing the apoptosis of NPCs, which leads to the structural and functional abnormalities of IVD and leads to IDD. In this study, it was also found that TRAIL level was increased in IDD samples, which is consistent with previous immunohistological analysis that revealed increased TRAIL and DR4/DR5 levels in IDD tissues, further confirming that the TRAIL/DR4/DR5 axis serves as the key elements in NPCs apoptosis.

LncRNAs are abnormally expressed in the course of IDD patients [10, 12]. LINC00284 and lncRNA ZFAS1 were reported to up-regulate in IDD tissues [30, 31]. There was also research showing that lncRNA MAGI2-AS3 had low expression levels in the plasma of patients with IDD [32]. Herein, LINC00324 was highly expressed in IDD samples. In addition, LINC00324 and TRAIL levels showed a strong positive connection. It was shown that LINC00324 controlled both apoptosis and cell division in a range of cell types [33, 34]. LINC00324 played a cancer-promoting role in lung cancer [35]. Evidence showed that LINC00324 was elevated in IDD and associated with IDD progression [15], which was consistent with our results. Nevertheless, further in-depth investigations are required to fully elucidate the specific molecular mechanisms by which LINC00324 contributes to the pathogenesis of IDD. Our study showed that LINC00324 was increased in LPS-induced NPCs, and inhibition of LINC00324 suppressed NPCs apoptosis. Critically, we extended these findings to ECM metabolism—a key determinant of IVD homeostasis. The LPS-induced IDD model exhibited decreased Collagen II, Aggrecan levels, and elevated MMP13 level, whereas LINC00324 silencing partially reversed these aberrant changes. In vivo, LINC00324 overexpression exacerbated ECM degradation in rat NP tissues and disrupted disc structure, while knockdown alleviated these pathological features. Our findings represent the initial demonstration that LINC00324 regulates both NPC apoptosis and ECM metabolism, highlighting its central role in disease progression.

Next, this research attempted to clarify the mechanism by which LINC00324 regulates TRAIL in IDD. LncRNA mainly combines with transcription factors to activate or interfere with transcription and regulate epigenetic inheritance [36]. Competitive endogenous RNA (ceRNA) is a major method for post-transcriptional control of gene level, which also has a significant regulatory function in IDD [37, 38]. For example, lncRNA PART1 was higher in LPS-treated NPCs than in controls [39]. The knockdown of LncRNA PART1 reduced its targeting effect on miR-190a-3p, thereby enhancing the cell viability of NPCs, reducing cell apoptosis and inhibiting the secretion of inflammatory factors, and finally alleviating its exacerbating effect on IDD [39]. Other study had found that lncRNA HCG18 promoted inflammation in the process of NPCs degeneration through miR-495-3p/FSTL1, leading to the development of IDD [10]. Reducing the expression level of highly expressed lncRNA can control IDD development through the ceRNA network. Therefore, this study speculated that miRNA might play a part in LINC00324’s function in IDD.

Based on the ceRNA principle, we performed bioinformatic prediction of miRNAs potentially targeted by both LINC00324 and TRAIL. Intersection analysis identified miR-143-3p as a common predicted target. This suggests a potential ceRNA relationship in which LINC00324 may sponge miR-143-3p, thereby derepressing TRAIL expression. While the functions of the LINC00324/miR-143-3p/TRAIL axis remain uncharacterized in IDD, existing literature supports the involvement of miR-143-3p in apoptosis and survival regulation across diverse pathological conditions [40, 41]. To test our hypothesis, we conducted rescue experiments. Our results demonstrated that miR-143-3p mimic abolished the influences of LINC00324 on TRAIL mRNA expression. Collectively, our findings verify that in the context of IDD, LINC00324 acts as a ceRNA to competitively bind miR-143-3p, thereby upregulating the TRAIL/DR4/DR5 axis, which in turn promotes apoptosis, disrupts ECM metabolism, and enhances the inflammatory response, ultimately accelerating IDD progression.

The present study carries inherent limitations that merit discussion. First, although we have now provided correlation analysis between expression levels of LINC00324, miR-143-3p, and TRAIL and Pfirrmann grade, demonstrating significant associations with IDD severity, the sample size is relatively modest. Larger prospective cohorts are needed to validate these findings and establish cut-off values for clinical diagnostic use. Second, the in vitro IDD model relied on LPS stimulation, which mimics inflammatory-driven IDD, it may not encompass the multifactorial complexity characteristic of human IDD pathogenesis. Third, while we confirmed that miR-143-3p targets TRAIL and mediates LINC00324’s effects on NPC apoptosis, the full spectrum of miR-143-3p’s targets in IDD remains underexplored. Additional experiments (e.g., DR4/DR5 knockout models) could further validate the axis’s necessity in LINC00324-mediated IDD. Fourth, although we validated the efficacy of lentiviral-mediated LINC00324 modulation by RT-qPCR, we did not perform additional validation methods such as in situ hybridization (ISH) to directly visualize the infection efficiency and spatial distribution of LINC00324 expression specifically within nucleus pulposus cells in the rat IVD tissues. Future investigations should incorporate IHC or ISH to further confirm the localization and efficiency of lncRNA modulation in specific disc cell populations.

In summary, LINC00324 is abnormally increased in IDD tissues and LPS-induced IDD cell models. Overexpression of LINC00324 promotes NPCs apoptosis, disrupts ECM metabolism, and enhances inflammatory response through the miR-143-3p/TRAIL/DR4/DR5 axis. Conversely, knockdown of LINC00324 alleviates these pathological changes both in vitro and in vivo. This study identifies LINC00324 as a potential therapeutic target and provides a new theoretical basis for IDD treatment.

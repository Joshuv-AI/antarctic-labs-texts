ALK-rearranged renal cell carcinoma with gains of chromosomes 7 and 17q --- A case report
Authors: Muntha Chaudhari, Hafiz Anas Yahya, Jianming Pei, Gabriel J. DiNatale, Iryna Mahan, James Ryan Mark, Arthur S. Patchefsky, Matthew R. Zibelman, Shuanzeng Wei
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ALK-rearranged renal cell carcinoma (ALK-RCC) is a rare subtype of RCC. In this report we describe the first ALK-RCC with gains of chromosome 7 and 17q. A 39-year-old male presented with an 11.5 cm renal mass and underwent a left radical nephrectomy. The tumor cells demonstrate focal clear cytoplasm, papillary formation, and extensive sarcomatoid changes. Molecular tests showed ALK rearrangement and gains at chromosomes 7 and 17q, which are commonly seen in papillary renal cell carcinoma. Thus caution is advised when interpreting chromosomal alterations for renal cell carcinoma.

Introduction

ALK-rearranged renal cell carcinoma (ALK-RCC) is an extremely rare subtype of RCCs, accounting for <1 % of all RCCs1. Since the initial characterization in 2011, approximately 63 cases have been reported2,3. In the most recent 5th edition of the World Health Organization (WHO) classification of male genital and urinary tract tumors, ALK-RCC has been included as a molecularly defined subtype of RCCs4. ALK-RCC shows wide age distribution and slight male predominance5. In these tumors, activation of ALK occurs through fusion of the ALK gene with several partner genes, including VCL, TPM3, EML4, STRN, and KIF5B, leading to the formation of oncogenic chimeric proteins. These oncogenic proteins aberrantly activate downstream growth promoting signaling pathways. It has been reported in the literature that ALK-RCCs do not show gains of chromosomes 7 and 17, which are characteristic chromosomal alterations of papillary renal cell carcinoma6,7. However, this case represents the first reported instance of ALK-RCC concurrently showing gains of chromosome 7 and 17q.

Case report

A 39-year-old Caucasian male presented to the emergency department with abdominal pain and left sided discomfort. He was a former smoker. An abdominopelvic CT scan revealed a 10.0 cm encapsulated left renal mass (Fig. 1) with extension into the renal sinus fat. No vascular involvement was seen and no enlarged local, regional, or retroperitoneal lymph nodes were identified. The patient was considered a suitable candidate for surgical intervention, and a robotic-assisted left radical nephrectomy was performed. On gross examination, the kidney harbored an 11.5 cm brown necrotic mass. The mass obliterated the mid and upper pole of the kidney and invaded into renal sinus fat. Tumor evaluation showed papillary and solid growth patterns with abundant clear to eosinophilic cytoplasm and vacuolization. The tumor cells demonstrated large and occasionally multinucleated nuclei with irregular nuclear membranes (Fig. 1). A significant portion of the tumor showed sarcomatoid differentiation (Fig. 1). Immunohistochemical stains identified that the carcinoma cells were positive for ALK, p504, PAX8, RCC (weak), and vimentin. Discussion

In the 5th edition of the WHO classification of urinary and male genital tumors, ALK-rearranged RCC is listed as a distinct molecularly defined subtype of RCC. To make this diagnosis, it is crucial to demonstrate an ALK rearrangement through break-apart FISH or fusion of ALK gene by sequencing.4

ALK-RCC demonstrates a slight male predominance and can be diagnosed in a wide age range.5,11,12 ALK-RCCs were reported to be well-circumscribed, solid to solid-cystic tumors, with size up to 7.0 cm, often confined to the kidney at presentation.4,13,14 However, in our case, the tumor was significantly large (11.5 cm) and showed invasion into renal sinus fat.

Histologically, ALK-rearranged RCC shows diverse architectural and cellular morphologies, such as papillary, pseudopapillary, solid, tubular, tubulo-cystic, cribriform, trabecular, signet-ring, and individual cells.15, 16, 17 This case included papillary and solid growth patterns only. Scattered psammoma bodies and coagulative necrosis were seen as well, which has been previously reported.15,17

Cytologically, the cells in ALK-RCC typically demonstrate eosinophilic cytoplasm and variable morphologies, often including rhabdoid, vacuolated, pleomorphic, and even small cells with basophilic scant cytoplasm. Initially, studies showed a lack of clear cells in ALK-rearranged RCC,14, 15, 16, 17 but a later study confirmed that ALK-RCC can have clear cell morphology 6. Our case showed a prominent clear cell cytology, along with eosinophilic, rhabdoid cells. Focal sarcomatoid differentiation has been reported;14 however, our case demonstrated extensive sarcomatoid differentiation.

By immunohistochemistry, ALK-rearranged RCC typically show cytoplasmic and membranous ALK expression and are generally positive for cytokeratin AE1/AE3, CAM5.2, EMA, HMWCK, CK7, AMACR, vimentin, and PAX8. INI-1 is retained. They may express TFE3 (without confirmatory FISH) and are negative for HMB-45, Melan-A, RCC and Cathepsin-K.4,6 In our case, the carcinoma portion expressed membranous ALK and PAX8, while the sarcomatous portion was negative for these markers. Our case was initially diagnosed as a grade 4 clear cell renal cell carcinoma with sarcomatoid changes based on morphology, while molecular studies were sent for further tumor classification.

Interestingly, our case showed concurrent gains at chromosomes 7 and 17q along with TMP3::ALK rearrangement. Previous literature showed that ALK-RCCs do not show gains of chromosomes 7 and 17. In one study, FISH evaluation of the status of chromosomes 7 and 17 was performed in ALK-RCC cases with extensive papillary structures, but none of those cases demonstrated gains of chromosomes 7 and 17, characteristic of papillary RCC.6,7 Our case highlights the fact that the presence of chromosomes 7 and 17 gains cannot exclude the diagnosis of ALK-RCC. Since this case also demonstrated sarcomatoid changes, the presence of gains of 7 and 17q may be a part of the complex chromosomal alterations in sarcomatoid renal cell carcinoma.

Although the clinical behavior of ALK-RCCs is not fully understood, metastatic disease or death has been reported in 25 % of ALK-RCC cases.13,18 ALK inhibitors are used routinely in non-renal tumors showing ALK rearrangement, and it raises the possibility that ALK inhibitors may also be effective in ALK-RCC.19 Prospective studies will be needed to assess the role of ALK inhibitors in the treatment of metastatic ALK-RCC and how it might fit in with current systemic therapy standards. Regardless, a correct diagnosis is particularly essential in such cases to guide accurate management and enhance management options.

Conclusion

In conclusion, ALK-RCC represents a cytogenetically distinct but histologically heterogenous variant of RCC with various growth patterns and morphologies. We report the first known case of an RCC with a TPM3::ALK gene fusion with gains of chromosome 7 and 17q, which are classically associated with papillary RCC. Thus, the gain of chromosome 7 and 17 cannot exclude the diagnosis of ALK-RCC, and the addition of RNA fusion studies may be needed for accurate tumor classification. Establishing a precise diagnosis of ALK-RCC is important, as it may have implications for personized treatment strategy.

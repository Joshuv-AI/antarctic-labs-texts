Hepatic arterial infusion chemotherapy plus lenvatinib and PD-1 inhibitors versus lenvatinib plus PD-1 inhibitors as first-line treatment for hepatocellular carcinoma with high tumour burden and portal vein tumour thrombus (CHANCE 2416): study protocol of a multicentre, retrospective, target trial emulation design
Authors: Jiaxi Liu, Songnan Zhang, Haibo Shao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAdvanced-stage hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) with high tumour burden and portal vein tumour thrombus (PVTT) is usually associated with poor survival outcomes. Rapid tumour control usually benefits long-term outcomes, which could be hardly achieved by solely systematic targeted and immunotherapy in current guidelines. Hepatic arterial infusion chemotherapy (HAIC) is reported as an effective intervention for rapid decrease of tumour burden. In order to determine the role of HAIC in the comprehensive treatments, a target trial emulation study is conducted to compare the effectiveness and safety of HAIC in combination with lenvatinib and programmed death receptor-1 (PD-1) inhibitors (H+L+P) to that of lenvatinib and PD-1 inhibitors (L+P) in patients with advanced HCC exhibiting high tumour burden and PVTT.Methods and analysisThis target trial emulation study will be conducted at nationwide, multicentre CHANCE registries in China. We aim to include at least 228 patients with advanced-stage HCC with high tumour burden (up-to-seven criteria out) and PVTT who received L+P with or without HAIC as the first-line treatment between January 2021 and December 2023. The study design adheres to the framework of target trial emulation. To mitigate biases, a stabilised inverse probability of treatment weighting will be conducted. Overall survival is defined as the primary endpoint. Secondary endpoints consist of progression-free survival, objective response rate and adverse events.Ethics and disseminationOur study was approved by the Medical Research Ethics Committee of the First Hospital of China Medical University, and the study protocol was also approved by the institutional review boards of participating centres. The ethics committee waived informed consent because the study was retrospective. The findings of this study will be submitted for publication in peer-reviewed journals and will also be shared at multiple conferences on interventional radiology and oncology, ranging from local to international.Trial registration numberNCT06631326.Keywords: Hepatobiliary tumours, Interventional radiology, Clinical Trial

Background

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) ranks as the sixth most prevalent malignancy and is the third primary cause of cancer-related mortality in the world.1 Due to the insidious onset, rapid progression and unfavourable prognosis, over 50% of patients with HCC are detected at an advanced stage, rendering them unsuitable for curative treatment measures.2 The Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer (BCLC) guideline is a prevalent staging framework that offers tumour classification and therapeutic guidance. Patients in the advanced stage, as per the BCLC system, are eligible for systemic therapy.3 Patients exhibiting portal vein tumour thrombus (PVTT) and a high tumour burden presented exceedingly poor prognosis with a median overall survival (OS) ranging from 2.7 to 4.0 months; thus, an effective treatment strategy that facilitates rapid tumour burden reduction and sustained control of PVTT is urgently required.4 In light of the promising outcomes of the IMbrave150 trial,5 atezolizumab plus bevacizumab had been recommended as first-line systemic treatments for patients with advanced-stage HCC.6 Tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs), such as lenvatinib, are listed as the substitute agents if atezolizumab plus bevacizumab is not feasible.7 In patients with advanced HCC receiving lenvatinib, the addition of programmed death receptor-1 (PD-1) inhibitors had been shown to be effective in improvement of the objective response rate (ORR) and survival outcomes, relative to lenvatinib monotherapy.8 Recently, the results of the LEAP-002 trial demonstrated a tendency of benefit regarding the study outcomes. This trial failed to achieve the established significance for enhanced progression-free survival (PFS) and OS when comparing lenvatinib combined with PD-1 inhibitors vs lenvatinib alone.9

The combination of locoregional treatments with systemic therapy has demonstrated enhanced efficacy compared with systemic therapies alone. Trial CHANCE220110 demonstrated that, in the management of advanced-stage HCC, transarterial chemoembolisation (TACE) combined with anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (anti-VEGF) antibodies/TKIs and immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) significantly enhances therapeutic efficacy in comparison with the use of anti-VEGF antibodies/TKIs with ICIs. Nonetheless, prior research indicated that individuals exceeding the up-to-7 criteria typically do not derive benefits from TACE.11 The recent Chinese liver cancer (CNLC) staging system indicates that hepatic arterial infusion chemotherapy (HAIC), which uses the FOLFOX (oxaliplatin, folinic acid and fluorouracil) regimen, significantly improves the OS of advanced-stage HCC compared with TACE, particularly for patients with PVTT.12 A retrospective study showed that, in comparison to lenvatinib with PD-1 inhibitors (L+P), HAIC in conjunction with lenvatinib and PD-1 inhibitors (H+L+P) effectively extended OS and PFS while enhancing ORR in patients with advanced-stage HCC with PVTT, with tolerable adverse events (AEs).13 The absence of randomised controlled trials (RCTs) involving large populations renders the superiority of H+L+P over L+P a contentious issue. In this study, we used the design and methodology of RCTs on observational data to create a multicentre target trial emulation study. Target trial emulation, a recognised statistical methodology, has been proposed as a means of assessing the effectiveness of treatment across populations in an uncontrolled environment using a substantial observational dataset.14 This analytical approach employs the methodological and design concepts of RCTs to observational data.15 Consequently, the emulation of a target trial might mitigate prevalent methodological issues, including immortal time bias and selection bias arising from unbalanced patient characteristics. A directed acyclic graph (DAG) can be used to identify covariates necessary for adjusting confounding variables. The implementation of a target trial emulation framework will further enhance the credibility of our findings, and the methodology mentioned above may provide transferable insights for clinical practice. With this method, the efficacy and safety of L+P with or without HAIC as the first-line treatment for patients with advanced HCC with high tumour burden and PVTT will be evaluated.

Methods

Trial design

Trial CHANCE2416 is a multicentre, observational, target trial emulation study initiated by the First Hospital of China Medical University. The research will use real-world data from the nationwide, multicentre CHANCE registries in China, as outlined in previous publications.10 16 We will emulate an ideal target trial in which patients with high tumour burden and PVTT were administered first-line lenvatinib in conjunction with PD-1 inhibitors, with or without HAIC.

Participants

This trial will involve patients with advanced HCC receiving treatment at around 20 participating centres in China between January 2021 and December 2023. Patients were administered either H+L+P or L+P as first-line treatment. The inclusion criteria are as follows: (1) age between 18 and 80 years old; (2) diagnosis of HCC confirmed through histological, cytological examination or clinical characteristics in accordance with the guidelines of the American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases; (3) existence of at least one measurable intrahepatic lesion as defined by the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumours (RECIST) V.1.1 criteria; (4) HCC staging of the patients were consistent with both the BCLC stage C and the CNLC stage IIIa; (5) presence of PVTT; (6) liver function classified as Child-Pugh class A or B7; (7) tumour burden meeting the up-to-7 criteria; (8) have received a first-line combination of L+P or H+L+P. The administration of lenvatinib was conducted in combination with PD-1 inhibitors, while HAIC was conducted either concurrently or within a 2-month period before or after the combination therapy with L+P inhibitors. Patients in the H+L+P cohort must complete a minimum of two cycles of HAIC, receive at least two cycles of PD-1 inhibitors and undergo a minimum of 2 months of lenvatinib treatment. Patients in the L+P cohort must have a minimum of two cycles of PD-1 inhibitors and administer lenvatinib for at least 2 months. The framework for treatment and monitoring is shown in figure 1. The exclusion criteria comprise: (1) previous anti-tumour therapy; (2) existence of additional malignancies; (3) lack of critical baseline adjustment variables for primary analysis or inadequate outcome data. The baseline adjustment factors are outlined as follows: age, sex, hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status, cirrhosis, alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) levels, Child-Pugh classes, diameter of target lesions, number of lesions and Japan’s portal vein invasion (Vp) classification of PVTT. Treatment decisions about the H+L+P combination or L+P alone were guided by a multidisciplinary expert panel that thoroughly evaluated the patient’s financial burden, profile and preferences. Prior to the decision-making process, physicians would have informed patients and their families of the possible advantages, risks and expenses associated with each treatment alternative.

HAIC procedures

HAIC was administered at 3 week intervals, and a catheter/microcatheter was inserted into the main feeding hepatic artery. The HAIC protocols include the FOLFOX and RALOX (oxaliplatin and raltitrexed) regimens. The FOLFOX regimen was as follows: oxaliplatin 85 mg/m2 from hours 0 to 2 on day 1; leucovorin 400 mg/m2 from hours 2 to 3 on day 1; and 2400 mg/m2 over 46 hours on days 1 and 2. The RALOX regimen was as follows: oxaliplatin 85 mg/m2 and raltitrexed 3 mg/m2. The administration techniques for HAIC comprise indwelling arterial catheters and percutaneous arterial ports. The methodology of HAIC operations adhered to the consensus recommendations for arterial chemotherapy in HCC in China.17 HAIC procedures were carried out at each participating centre by an interventional radiologist who had a minimum of 10 years of experience in the field. The position of the indwelling catheter or microcatheter can be adjusted during each cycle of HAIC treatment according to the patient’s lesion status. If necessary, collateral blood vessels may be embolised, and chemoembolisation of the arteries supplying the tumour were permitted during the HAIC treatments. The overall number of HAIC treatments was established by a multidisciplinary team (MDT) according to the tumour’s response. Typically, patients exhibiting a complete response (CR) would transition from induction therapy with H+L+P to maintenance therapy with L+P, whereas patients exhibiting a partial response (PR) or stable disease (SD) undergo four to six cycles of induction therapy before transition to maintenance medication. The MDT committee would recommend appropriate second-line systemic treatments for individuals who encountered intolerable toxicity or progressive disease (PD) during induction therapy.

Lenvatinib plus PD-1 inhibitors administration

All patients received the first-line L+P combinations. The used PD-1 inhibitors comprised camrelizumab, sintilimab and tislelizumab. The selection of PD-1 inhibitors was primarily determined by the patient’s financial circumstances and health insurance, owing to insufficient evidence indicating the superiority of different PD-1 inhibitors. All agents were administered according to the prescribed dosage and frequency. Lenvatinib was administered orally once daily at a dosage of 4–12 mg, depending on patient’s body weight. A dose reduction was permitted in response to grades 3 or 4 AEs. PD-1 inhibitors were delivered intravenously around every 21 days at a dosage of 200 mg. We did not allow a dose decrease of PD-1 inhibitors; however, temporary cessation of PD-1 inhibitors was permitted due to AEs. Treatments of L+P persisted until intolerable toxicity occurred, disease progression or loss to follow-up.

Follow-up and assessments

Routine follow-up evaluations encompass the monitoring of clinical symptoms, vital signs, AEs, treatment conditions and dosages, treatment cycles, laboratory analyses and radiological assessments by contrast-enhanced MRI or CT. Follow-ups will be at 3-month intervals. Treatment effects for all patients will be evaluated using both RECIST V.1.1 and the modified RECIST (mRECIST). The assessments will be conducted in person at each participating centre by two independent interventional radiologists from the main centre, each possessing a minimum of 10 years of experience. On disease progression, the local physician will determine the following anti-cancer therapy.

Data collection

Data will be gathered from the medical records at each participating centre. To ensure the accuracy and consistency of electronic medical records, a standardised data extraction protocol will be implemented across all participating centres. This protocol will involve the collection of demographic information, baseline characteristics, clinical symptoms, treatment modalities (including both first-line and second-line therapies, where applicable), treatment responses and survival outcomes, specifically OS and PFS. The abovementioned clinical data, recorded by research sites, will be organised and transmitted by the physicians into a secure web-based electronic case report form (eCRF) system (longleding.com). The eCRF documentation will be assessed for plausibility and completeness both centrally and on-site by the principal investigator, subinvestigators and clinical monitors, respectively.

Outcome measure

The primary objective is to validate the advantage of H+L+P over L+P in patients with advanced HCC with high tumour burden and PVTT. Time zero (T0) is defined as the point the first treatment begins and all eligibility criteria are satisfied. The primary endpoint of this trial is OS, identified as the duration from T0 to death from any cause, censoring or the end of follow-up. The secondary endpoints encompass PFS, extrahepatic PFS (EHPFS), intrahepatic PFS (IHPFS), tumour thrombus PFS (TTPFS), ORR and safety. PFS is defined as the interval from T0 to either the first confirmation of radiological progression during the treatment course or death due to HCC. EHPFS and IHPFS are delineated as the intervals from T0 to the confirmed appearance of extrahepatic lesions, or initial radiological progression of intrahepatic lesions, respectively. TTPFS refers to the intervals from T0 to radiological progression of PVTT or death. In the assessment of PVTT, total elimination of enhancement within PVTT would be classified as CR; any upstaging in the Vp classification would be classified as PD, and any downstaging in the Vp classification would be deemed PR. Otherwise, the reaction is classified as SD.13 ORR is the proportion of patients who have a confirmed CR or PR based on either RECIST V.1.1 or mRECIST criteria. The severity of AEs will be evaluated using the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events V.5.0.

Sample size calculation

Sample size calculation for this study adhered to the ideal target trial structure, focusing on the primary analysis of OS using the log-rank test in PASS 2021 software (NCSS software, Utah, USA). The research used a 1:1 parallel layout. According to previous research13 18 19 and clinical experiences, the median OS for patients with advanced HCC with high tumour burden and PVTT undergoing L+P was determined to be 11.5 months. We proposed a HR of 0.67, which correlated with an increase in median OS to 17.2 months for patients receiving H+L+P treatment. The two-tailed type I error rate α was established at 0.05, with a statistical power of 80%. The required sample size, with a 12-month accrual period and an overall trial duration of 48 months for OS, is determined to be 228 patients, comprising 114 patients in each group.

Target trial emulation

Statistical techniques will be employed to compare H+L+P with L+P by emulating a target trial in our study design. A DAG will be used to ascertain variables essential for adjusting confounding aspects. To account for any confounding variables, stabilised inverse probability of treatment weighting (sIPTW) will be employed in the primary analysis. A logistic regression model will be developed, with treatment modality as the dependent variable, to adjust for the following covariates: age, sex, cirrhosis, HBV infection, Child-Pugh classifications, ECOG performance status, AFP levels, tumour burden (up-to-11 criteria) and classification of PVTT. The Cox proportional hazards model and individual contributions in the survival curves will be assessed using the sIPTW tests, which are obtained from the prediction probabilities of the aforementioned model.

To mitigate the possibility of immortal time bias, cloning and inverse probability of censoring weight (IPCW) techniques will be used, adhering to a three-step analytical framework that encompasses cloning, censoring and weighting, similar to an intention-to-treat approach in RCTs. Initially, each patient will be cloned and assigned to one of the therapy groups (H+L+P or L+P). Patients will be censored from the study if they did not follow their prescribed therapies within a certain 3-month window following the start of the intervention in order to prevent immortal time bias. Considering that this censoring is not completely random and is frequently determined by the characteristics of the patient, there is a possibility that it could result in selection bias. To address this, IPCW will be implemented via a Cox model that omits time-varying variables. For each event occurrence, the likelihood of a patient staying uncensored will be assessed using factors such as age, sex, HBV infection, cirrhosis presence, ECOG performance status, AFP levels, Child-Pugh classifications, tumour burden and Vp classifications of PVTT. Weights will thereafter be determined as the inverse of these probabilities.

A conditional landmark analysis will be conducted to mitigate immortal time bias. The landmark time is set at 3 months post-T0, with only patients who live beyond this period included in the analysis.

Time to diagnosis (Td) for patients with advanced-stage HCC was also documented in the study. OS shall be redefined as the duration from Td until death regardless of reason, censorship or the conclusion of the follow-up period. The survival analyses will also be performed based on this reinterpretation.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables will be assessed using Fisher’s exact test or the χ2 test. Continuous variables will be evaluated with a two-tailed t-test or Mann-Whitney test. Survival analysis of OS and PFS will be conducted and compared using the Kaplan-Meier method and log-rank testing. The reverse Kaplan-Meier method will be used to determine the median follow-up duration. HRs with a 95% CI will be computed using the Cox proportional hazards model. Cox regression analysis will be employed to identify independent risk factors associated with OS and PFS. Initially, univariate regression will be performed, and variables with p values less than 0.1, which may influence tumour development or survival based on clinical expertise, will be incorporated into the multivariate analysis using the forward conditional approach. The ORR will be analysed using an adjusted χ2 test, and the associated 95% precise CIs will be computed using the Clopper-Pearson technique for each treatment group.

Subsequently, five sensitivity analyses will be performed. Initially, instead of censoring patients during follow-up, we will maintain their inclusion in the risk set until the conclusion of the follow-up period, allowing us to evaluate the robustness of our findings against different censoring methodologies. In addition, propensity score matching will be carried out with a calliper width of 0.05 and a 1:1 nearest-neighbour approach without replacement. Furthermore, a Kaplan-Meier curve analysis will be performed, and a standard Cox proportional hazards model will be used in the absence of sIPTW. Apart from this, we will exclude patients who received TACE after two cycles of HAIC or PD-1 inhibitors to reduce the potential influence on the survival outcomes. Last but not least, we will exclude patients who received surgical resection after two cycles of HAIC or PD-1 inhibitors to reduce the potential unidirectional crossover effect.

For the post hoc analysis, only patients receiving first-line tislelizumab-lenvatinib, with or without HAIC, will be included, and the primary analysis will be repeated.

Subgroup analyses will be conducted to evaluate the impact of H+L+P therapy within the predetermined groupings. Due to the multitude of comparisons, results from subgroup analysis should be regarded as exploratory. All comparisons are two-tailed, with p<0.05 deemed significant. All analyses will be conducted using SPSS V.26.0, Stata V.15, and R software (V.4.3.1).

Patient and public involvement

Patients and the public will not participate in the study design because this study employs retrospective pseudonymised data.

Ethics and dissemination

This study will be executed according to the reporting guideline established by the STROBE (Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology) as well as the regulations established by the Declaration of Helsinki (identifier on ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT06631326). The institutional Medical Research Ethics Committee approved our study (Kelunshen (2024) No. 807), and the study protocol received approval from the review boards of the participating centres. The ethics committee waived informed consent because the study was retrospective. The findings of this study will be submitted for publication in peer-reviewed journals and will also be shared at multiple conferences on interventional radiology and oncology, ranging from local to international.

Discussion

PVTT is common in advanced liver cancer, affecting 10%–60% of patients.20 It elevates the likelihood of tumour dissemination, augments portal vein pressure and diminishes portal vein blood flow, potentially resulting in additional problems such as varices, haemorrhage, ascites and hepatic failure.21 This is particularly applicable when the tumour thrombus infiltrates the main trunk of the portal vein (Vp4) and its principal branches (Vp3), leading to an unfavourable prognosis. Discrepancies in the treatment of patients with advanced-stage HCC with PVTT continue to exist between Western and Asia-Pacific regions. In the West, systemic therapies are advocated as the primary treatment for these patients, whereas the East favours more local–regional interventions such as TACE.22,24 Recent studies demonstrated that for patients with HCC exceeding the up-to-7 criteria, systemic therapy is recommended rather than local–regional treatments.25 Systemic targeted immunotherapies, including atezolizumab plus bevacizumab, are commonly recommended by clinical guidelines as the first-line treatment for patients with advanced-stage HCC. However, with a median OS of 7.6 months, the IMbrave150 trial demonstrated that high-risk patients did not benefit much from atezolizumab with bevacizumab. Consequently, current therapeutic choices for individuals exhibiting high-risk characteristics, including primary branch PVTT and high tumour burden of HCC, lack efficacy.26 In Asia-Pacific regions, L+P is commonly used for individuals with advanced-stage HCC, based on the REFLECT study and trial LEAP 012.18 Nowadays, multiple research investigations have demonstrated a longer OS and PFS with H+L+P compared with L+P in patients with advanced-stage HCC, leading to a significant increase in long-term survivors.1318 19 27,29 A retrospective study by Chang et al27 showed that H+L+P had a safe and promising antitumour efficacy compared with L+P for HCC with high-risk characteristics, including class Vp4 PVTT and significant tumour burden exceeding half of the liver. Nevertheless, substantial RCTs comparing H+L+P with L+P are still lacking; hence, they are highly in demand.

Since HAIC using the FOLFOX regimen had a longer median OS compared with sorafenib for advanced HCC, it had been used as a first-line treatment for patients with advanced HCC. As well, HAIC was also an alternative treatment for patients who were not candidates for TACE in real-world practice.30,33 The OS rate was also considerably higher with FOLFOX-HAIC than with TACE for patients with high tumour burden, unresectable HCC (with a diameter of 7 cm or greater).34 A recent prospective study found that compared with FOLFOX-HAIC, HAIC with the RALOX regimen of oxaliplatin and raltitrexed significantly decreased the time of HAIC procedures while still showing acceptable safety and promising efficacy in patients with intermediate and advanced-stage HCC.35 When comparing the efficacy and safety of FOLFOX-HAIC with RALOX-HAIC in patients with advanced HCC, a retrospective analysis found that both treatments were comparably safe and effective.36 Consequently, our study decided to include both the FOLFOX and RALOX regimens for HAIC treatments. This may enlarge the sample size in this retrospective study.

This trial incorporates camrelizumab, sintilimab and tislelizumab as PD-1 inhibitors. The National Medical Products Administration has approved the PD-1 inhibitors listed above, and the Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Treatment of Primary Liver Cancer in China have recommended them as systemic therapies for advanced HCC.37 Prior prospective studies have shown that these three PD-1 inhibitors are effective and safe in treating HCC, either alone or in combination with other systemic or intra-arterial therapies.38,41

A recent study found that the survival outcomes of individuals treated with HAIC plus TKIs and ICIs differed significantly depending on whether they were successful in conversion to surgical resection.42 In our trial, we included patients who achieved successful conversion. To mitigate the influence of survival outcomes, a sensitivity analysis will be carried out by excluding patients who had surgical excision after receiving H+L+P or L+P treatments.

Our target trial emulation study represents several limitations. First, the reporting of patient information may be subject to bias, as data collection is confined to what is documented in the medical records. This limitation is particularly relevant regarding records of patient compliance and comorbidities, despite the application of statistical methods aimed at emulating a target trial. In addition, this study contained three distinct PD-1 inhibitors, which may influence the treatment outcomes of the combo studies. A post-hoc analysis will be performed to mitigate the impact of the aforementioned impacts.

In summary, the CHANCE2416 study aims to provide real-world data, encompassing clinical outcomes, that compares H+L+P with L+P in patients with advanced HCC exhibiting high tumour burden and PVTT. A more reliable outcome will be attained by the statistical methodologies of target trial emulation studies.

Trial status

Patient enrolment took place from January 2021. Data collection for this study is ongoing. The analyses are scheduled for completion by the end of 2025.

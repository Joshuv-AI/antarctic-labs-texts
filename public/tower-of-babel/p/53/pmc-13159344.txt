From scale development to strategic action: internal service quality and importance-performance analysis findings in Taiwan’s long-term care
Authors: Che-Cheng Chang, Shu-Hsien Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTaiwan is rapidly transitioning into a super-aged society, leading to an increased reliance on long-term care (LTC) institutions. High staff turnover remains a critical challenge for the industry globally. While internal service quality (ISQ) is recognized as a key driver of employee retention, there is a systemic lack of scientifically validated scales specifically tailored to the unique demands of LTC contexts, a research gap that persists both in Taiwan and internationally. This study aims to develop a validated ISQ scale that addresses this global empirical void and to identify strategic priorities for institutional management.MethodsThe study employed a multi-stage methodology. First, a Modified Delphi Method was conducted with a panel of 12 experts over two rounds to construct and validate the ISQ scale. Content Validity Ratio (CVR) and consensus thresholds were used for item selection. Second, a survey was administered to 364 LTC professionals (nurses, caregivers, social workers and Managers / Administrative Staff) across Taiwan. Data were analyzed using descriptive statistics, reliability/validity testing (Cronbach’s alpha, CR, and AVE), and Importance–Performance Analysis (IPA) to identify service gaps.ResultsThe final validated ISQ scale consists of 29 items across five dimensions: Tangibles, Responsiveness, Reliability, Assurance, and Empathy. All dimensions showed high reliability (Cronbach’s α > 0.80) and convergent validity. A significant service quality gap was identified, with an overall mean importance score of 4.24 compared to a satisfaction score of 3.13 (Gap = -1.11). IPA results revealed seven items in Quadrant II (“Concentrate Here”), highlighting critical weaknesses in soft management, including managers’ fairness in addressing issues, willingness to listen to staff feelings, and service enthusiasm. Conversely, salary payment and rotation systems were identified as core institutional strengths.ConclusionsThis study provides the first scientifically grounded ISQ measurement tool for the LTC sector in Taiwan. The findings suggest that while institutions perform well in tangible and financial aspects, a strategic shift toward empathetic leadership and organizational fairness is essential to improve staff morale. Policymakers and managers should integrate these qualitative ISQ indicators into evaluation frameworks to address workforce shortages and enhance external care quality.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12913-026-14475-5.

Introduction

The global population is experiencing rapid aging, and the accompanying potential health problems among older adults—such as chronic disease patterns, multimorbidity, disability, and dementia—have led to increasingly greater and more complex long ‑term care needs. Prior research indicates that Taiwan entered an aged society in 2018 when adults aged 65 and over reached 14% of the population, with the share surpassing 15% in 2019 and projected to reach super‑aged status by 2026. By 2050, older adults are expected to account for 35.1% of Taiwan’s total population. Relative to the United States (19%), Taiwan is aging more rapidly; the proportions in the United Kingdom and South Korea are similar to Taiwan, reflecting a broader global trend of accelerated population aging [1–3].

As population aging intensifies, reliance on long-term care (LTC) institutions is steadily increasing. To meet the needs of an aging society, Taiwan’s Ministry of Health and Welfare has implemented the Long-Term Care 2.0 system to provide diversified and continuous services and to improve the quality of life of care recipients and caregivers. Consequently, demand for LTC personnel has risen. However, recruitment difficulties and high turnover have made human resource management a major challenge for LTC providers [3]. In Japan, only 40.1% of care workers report willingness to remain in their current positions. Frequent turnover among LTC personnel inevitably affects the quality of care provided by institutions. Retention of LTC workers has long been a concern for researchers and practitioners. Longitudinal evidence from the United States shows turnover rates in some facilities reaching 100%. In Taiwan, turnover exceeding 20% has disrupted continuity of service experience and adversely affected external service quality [4–8].

Internal Service Quality (ISQ) refers to employees’ satisfaction with services received from internal service providers [9]. ISQ is defined as organizational members’ perceptions of the services and attitudes offered by other members [10]. Conceptual studies have identified the core dimensions of ISQ—tools and resources, policies and procedures, effective training, teamwork and shared goals, managerial support, and communication—all of which are associated with enhanced employee job satisfaction [9].

In the research context of long-term care, internal marketing (IM) theory is especially applicable. Research indicates that LTC work is labor‑intensive and emotionally demanding; employees often face low wages, long working hours, and high turnover [8, 11, 12]. When institutions deploy IM strategies—such as adequate training, a supportive organizational culture and environment, strengthened communication, and sufficient resource provision—employees’ job satisfaction and intention to stay improve [13, 14]. Berry first proposed IM, arguing that employees should be regarded as ‘internal customers’ and that meeting their needs through appropriate management strategies enhances job satisfaction and service motivation [15]. The core proposition is that delivering high‑quality external services requires first ensuring that internal customers (employees) receive adequate support and satisfaction [15, 16]. Greene and colleagues further applied marketing philosophy and methods to employee management, thereby supporting effective utilization and retention of internal human resources [17, 18]. The service–profit chain model emphasizes that ISQ drives nurse satisfaction; satisfied nurses deliver high‑value care, which increases customer satisfaction and loyalty and, in turn, institutional growth and profitability [19]. Accordingly, IM theory provides the most relevant foundation for studying ISQ: it positions employees as internal customers and highlights the necessity of institutionalized support and resource allocation to raise their satisfaction and service motivation [20]. For LTC institutions, improving ISQ is not merely an administrative measure but a key strategy for enhancing external service quality and overall care performance.

Prior research indicates that meeting the needs of internal customers (employees) is essential to meeting the needs of external customers. High ISQ strengthens employees’ service capability and job satisfaction, which are transmitted through service delivery to elevate external service quality and customer satisfaction. For LTC personnel, superior ISQ not only enhances job performance but also motivates staff to remain engaged and achieve a sense of accomplishment—even under demanding conditions. Ultimately, ISQ fosters customer loyalty, repurchase intention, and organizational profitability [21]. Many studies have highlighted the role of ISQ and IM in healthcare and long-term care settings. Research conducted in public hospitals in northern Greece [13] found that vision communication, training support, and reward recognition significantly enhanced staff satisfaction. Another study in six Greek public hospitals [22] adapted the SERVQUAL scale and reported a strong positive association between internal service quality and job satisfaction, with reliability and empathy exerting the greatest influence. Evidence from Canada [14] further demonstrated that psychological empowerment and organizational context variables—such as social capital, organizational slack‑time, and adequate orientation—were key predictors of job satisfaction. Building on prior international findings, the present study advances the field by developing and validating a scientifically grounded ISQ scale specifically for Taiwan’s LTC sector. Previous LTC studies often relied on minor wording adaptations of existing instruments—typically based on literature review, expert evaluation, and limited translation refinement—which left room to improve measurement validity. Furthermore, a clearly defined and widely accepted framework for the determinants and measurement of ISQ in LTC has been lacking. This study addresses these limitations by constructing and empirically testing a comprehensive ISQ scale tailored to Taiwan’s long-term care institutions. Therefore, this study examines ISQ in LTC institutions in Taiwan, with the following objectives: (1) to construct the components and dimensions of ISQ in LTC institutions; (2) to assess LTC workers’ perceived levels of satisfaction and importance and classify ISQ attributes in Taiwan; and (3) to identify key ISQ attributes and propose strategic recommendations for Taiwan’s LTC institutions.

Methods

Instrumentation and scale development

Although research on internal service quality (ISQ) is extensive, the long-term care (LTC) sector lacks a universally accepted measurement framework. To address this gap, this study followed a multi-stage scale development process aligning with best practices in psychometric scale construction, involving item generation, expert validation, and empirical testing [23].

The first phase utilized a modified Delphi method to construct the ISQ scale. Unlike the traditional approach, the modified version uses a structured questionnaire based on a comprehensive literature review to focus expert attention, enhance response rates, and ensure content validity. The selection of the expert panel followed established recommendations suggesting that a panel of 11 or more experts provides high reliability [24, 25]. In this study, 12 experts were recruited based on specialized academic knowledge, practical experience in LTC, and expertise in healthcare quality assessment, as detailed in Table 1.

The initial item pool was developed based on the theory of summated rating scales [23]. The SERVQUAL instrument, originally developed by Parasuraman, Zeithaml, and Berry [27], is one of the most widely used tools for assessing service quality. It measures the gap between customer expectations and perceptions across five dimensions: Tangibles, Reliability, Responsiveness, Assurance, and Empathy. The standard SERVQUAL scale contains 22 items, each evaluated in terms of expectations and perceptions. Over the past decades, SERVQUAL has been extensively validated in diverse service industries—including healthcare, education, and hospitality—demonstrating high reliability and construct validity. In the context of this study, SERVQUAL provided the theoretical foundation for item generation, while subsequent expert review and Delphi validation ensured that the adapted scale reflects the unique characteristics of Taiwan’s long-term care sector. Items were adapted from the SERVQUAL model and existing ISQ frameworks, resulting in a five-dimension scale (Tangibles, Responsiveness, Reliability, Assurance, and Empathy) with 35 initial items [26]. Two rounds of the Delphi process were conducted, with consensus determined based on three statistical thresholds: (1) a mean score ≥ 3.5, (2) an interquartile range (IQR) ≤ 1.0, and (3) a Content Validity Ratio (CVR) ≥ 0.56, calculated following a standard formula [27]. In the first round, six items were excluded for failing to meet the mean score threshold. In the second round, all 29 remaining items demonstrated high consensus (IQR ≤ 1.0) and significant content validity (CVR: 0.83–1.00), completing the scale development phase [27, 28], with the descriptive statistics presented in Table 2.

Phase II: Empirical survey and analysis

In Phase II, a formal survey was administered where participants rated each item based on two dimensions: “Importance” and “Satisfaction,” using a 5-point Likert scale. In Phase III, the collected data underwent rigorous statistical analysis, including reliability testing, validity analysis, and Importance-Performance Analysis (IPA).

Participants and recruitment

The target population consisted of long-term care (LTC) professionals, including nurses, caregivers, social workers, and administrators, from institutions listed in the official registry [32]. Due to logistical constraints, convenience sampling was employed to recruit participants from northern, central, and southern Taiwan [34, 35]. To ensure sufficient statistical power, the study aimed for a sample size exceeding the minimum requirement of 109 for a 95% confidence level.

A total of 600 questionnaires were distributed through two systematic channels: (1) researchers contacted institution managers via telephone to obtain permission, after which online survey links were shared in staff communication groups; and (2) paper-based questionnaires were mailed to selected facilities. A total of 364 valid responses were recovered, yielding an effective response rate of 60.67%. Regarding research ethics, all participants were informed of the study’s purpose and their right to withdraw at any time. The survey was conducted anonymously, and all data were kept strictly confidential to protect respondents’ privacy.

Data analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS version 26.0. Statistical analysis included: (1) descriptive statistics (frequencies, percentages, means, and standard deviations) to summarize demographic characteristics and perceived quality levels; (2) reliability and validity analysis, where Cronbach’s alpha ≥ 0.70, Composite Reliability (CR) ≥ 0.60, and Average Variance Extracted (AVE) ≥ 0.50 were used as thresholds for internal consistency and convergent validity; and (3) Importance-Performance Analysis (IPA) to identify strategic improvement priorities. Following established methodology, the IPA matrix categorized attributes into four quadrants based on grand mean scores of importance and satisfaction: “Keep Up the Good Work,” “Concentrate Here,” “Low Priority,” and “Possible Overkill” [33].

Descriptive analysis of participants

The study analyzed 364 valid responses from long-term care professionals across Taiwan. As shown in Table 6, the sample consisted of 187 females (51.4%) and 177 males (48.6%). A significant majority of respondents were unmarried (66.2%). The age distribution was predominantly young to middle-aged, with those aged 30–39 years representing the largest group (36.8%), followed by the 20–29 age group (34.0%).

In terms of professional background, caregivers constituted the largest portion of the sample (65.1%), followed by nurses (22.0%). Most participants had between 2.1 and 5 years of work experience (41.2%). Regarding income levels, the average monthly salary ranged from NTD30,001 to NTD40,000 (31.6%).

Importance-Performance Analysis (IPA)

The mean values for the importance and satisfaction of the 29 internal service quality (ISQ) items were calculated and ranked to identify critical service gaps. As shown in Table 9, the overall mean score for importance was 4.24, while the overall mean for satisfaction was significantly lower at 3.13. This resulted in an overall service quality gap of -1.11, indicating that the actual experiences of long-term care (LTC) staff generally fall below their expectations across all measured attributes.

Among the 29 attributes, the item with the highest importance was “The institution pays the salary accurately and on time” (M = 4.55), followed by “Managers have an enthusiasm for serving internal customers” (M = 4.52). Conversely, the item with the lowest perceived importance was “The institution helps employees balance the needs of individuals and families” (M = 3.84). Regarding satisfaction, the highest-rated item was “The institution implements a rotation system” (M = 3.57), while the lowest satisfaction was reported for “Staff within the institution are willing to listen to my feelings” (M = 2.61).

Discussion

The primary objective of this study was to develop and validate a specialized Internal Service Quality (ISQ) scale for long-term care (LTC) institutions in Taiwan and identify critical service gaps using Importance-Performance Analysis (IPA). The results revealed a significant gap between the perceived importance (M = 4.24) and actual satisfaction (M = 3.13) among LTC staff, resulting in an overall service quality gap of -1.11. Consistent with prior research in healthcare settings, these findings indicate that organizations frequently struggle to meet the high expectations of their internal customers despite the recognized importance of institutional support [21].

Notably, while Aloisio et al. [22] analyzed 334 allied healthcare providers in Western Canada and identified organizational context variables—such as social capital and orientation—as critical predictors of job satisfaction, this study extends these findings by providing a localized strategic framework for Taiwanese LTC institutions. While their research focuses on identifying antecedents to satisfaction, our study utilizes Importance–Performance Analysis (IPA) to move beyond factor identification toward institutional resource allocation.

The identification of “soft” management attributes—such as managers’ enthusiasm (Item 7) and willingness to listen (Item 24)—as critical gaps (Quadrant II) suggests that Taiwanese LTC staff place a high premium on emotional labor support, which is often neglected in policy discussions focused solely on hardware or salary。Consistent with the service-profit chain theory, these internal service quality (ISQ) dimensions serve as the primary drivers of staff dedication and, ultimately, care performance。.

Furthermore, the analysis showed that “adequate equipment” (Item 5) and “space for announcements” (Item 6) fell into the “Possible Overkill” quadrant (Quadrant IV). This suggests that many institutions may be over-investing in physical infrastructure while neglecting the “soft” management functions that staff value more highly.

According to the service-profit chain model [15, 19], internal quality is the starting point for driving employee engagement and external care excellence. If managers fail to demonstrate empathy or establish fair communication channels, staff will struggle to cope with the immense interpersonal pressures of LTC work, ultimately leading to higher turnover rates.

Based on these findings, this study offers specific practical recommendations. At the managerial level, institutions should prioritize training programs that enhance leadership skills, specifically focusing on empathy, fair assessment, and emotional support to foster a more supportive organizational climate. At the policy level, it is suggested that the Ministry of Health and Welfare incorporate qualitative indicators of internal service quality into the “Long-term Care 2.0” evaluation framework. Shifting the focus from hardware metrics to internal workforce support will be crucial for addressing the chronic manpower shortage in Taiwan’s LTC sector.

Despite its contributions, this study has several limitations. First, the use of convenience sampling may introduce regional bias, limiting the generalizability of the findings across all types of facilities in Taiwan. Second, the cross-sectional design prevents the inference of long-term causal relationships between ISQ and retention behaviors. Additionally, self-reported data may be subject to social desirability bias. Future research should employ longitudinal designs and further explore differences in ISQ perceptions among various professional roles, such as nurses, caregivers, and social workers, to develop more precise management strategies.

Conclusions and recommendations

Summary of empirical findings

The empirical survey revealed a significant and systemic service quality gap within the studied institutions. Staff reported an average satisfaction score of 3.13, which stands in stark contrast to the much higher importance score of 4.24, resulting in an overall ISQ gap of -1.11. This imbalance suggests that while LTC employees in Taiwan place profound value on internal support systems, their daily professional experiences fall significantly short of these expectations. The IPA results further pinpointed seven critical attributes in Quadrant II (Concentrate Here), which represent urgent priorities for improvement. These include managers’ fairness in handling employee issues, their genuine willingness to listen to staff concerns, and their enthusiasm in serving internal customers. These “soft” management dimensions stand out as the primary weaknesses currently threatening workforce stability.

Scholarly and practical contributions

This study provides several distinctive contributions to the literature on healthcare management and workforce stability in the long-term care (LTC) sector. First, it represents the earliest systematic attempt to construct and validate a specialized Internal Service Quality (ISQ) scale specifically tailored for LTC institutions in Taiwan. Unlike prior international studies that often relied on the adaptation of general instruments, this research employed a modified Delphi method to achieve expert consensus, ensuring high content validity and methodological rigor specifically within the “Long-term Care 2.0” policy framework. Second, the integration of Importance–Performance Analysis (IPA) offers a novel strategic perspective by identifying service attributes that are critically undervalued—those highly prioritized by staff yet insufficiently delivered by institutional management. Finally, by situating the findings within a global context including evidence from Greece, Canada, and other regions, this study highlights both universal workforce challenges and the unique institutional realities of the Taiwanese LTC sector. Together, these strengths underscore the originality and practical relevance of the research for institutional sustainability.

Managerial and policy recommendations

At the managerial level, institutions must strategically reallocate resources toward strengthening internal communication, fairness, and responsiveness. It is recommended that leadership training programs be implemented with a specific focus on empathy, service enthusiasm, and supportive leadership behaviors to enhance staff morale and organizational dedication. At the policy level, the Ministry of Health and Welfare [32] should consider incorporating qualitative ISQ indicators into the national LTC evaluation frameworks. By shifting the regulatory focus from purely hardware-centric metrics to internal workforce support and psychological safety, the government can more effectively address the chronic manpower shortages and high turnover rates currently plaguing the sector. Systematically improving internal service quality will not only boost staff satisfaction but also ensure the delivery of high-value, reliable external care.

Limitations and future research

Methodological limitations

This study has several limitations that warrant consideration. First, convenience sampling was employed from selected regions of Taiwan, which restricts the generalizability of the findings and introduces potential sampling bias [34, 35]. Although institutions from northern, central, and southern Taiwan were included, variations across different types of facilities—such as public versus private or large versus small—may affect the overall representativeness of the sample.

Second, the cross-sectional design limits the ability to infer causal relationships between internal service quality (ISQ) and staff outcomes, as exposure and outcome were measured at a single point in time [36, 37]. Third, the data were collected through self-reported questionnaires, which may be subject to social desirability bias and recall errors, potentially affecting the accuracy of the responses [38, 39]. Finally, the internal service quality scale was newly developed and validated in this study; therefore, further replication and refinement are needed to confirm its robustness across diverse contexts [40, 41].

Directions for future research

Future studies could address these limitations through several methodological avenues. Longitudinal designs would allow for the examination of changes in internal service quality perceptions over time and their specific impact on staff retention [36]. Comparative studies across countries or industries could help assess the cross-cultural applicability of the internal service quality scale [40].

Furthermore, mixed-methods approaches, incorporating qualitative interviews, may provide deeper insights into staff experiences and complement quantitative findings [41]. Moreover, intervention-based research could evaluate whether targeted management practices improve internal service quality and staff outcomes, thereby strengthening the evidence base for organizational strategies in long-term care institutions [42].

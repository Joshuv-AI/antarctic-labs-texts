Outcome of the Nirschl Technique for Treating Tennis Elbow: A Systematic Review
Authors: Airlangga B P Sihotang, Marsheila Harvy M
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroduction & Objectives:In the modern era, marked by remarkable advancements in medical science and technology, open surgery often referred to as conventional or traditional surgery including in the managmenet of lateral epicondylitis or tennis elbow. Despite being introduced approximately 40 years ago, there are scarce information regarding the outcome of the Nirscl technique. To date, there are no reviews regarding the outcome of such technique in lateral epincodylitis. We aim to investigate the clinical results of surgical treatment of lateral epicondylitis using the Nirschl technique.Materials & Methods:A systematic literature search was performed in PubMed, Google Scholar, Sciencedirect, and Cochrane in August 11, 2023. The studies were critically appraised using the Rayyan. We included studies involving adult patients (>16 years) diagnosed with lateral epicondylitis that had undergone the Nirschl surgical procedure. There was no language and date restriction. Outcomes that were recorded were pain scale, scorings, rate of returning to sports or work, number of revision surgeries required, and patient satisfaction.Results:From a total 614 studies, 9 studies involving 561 elbows were included in this review. The mean duration of follow-up ranged from 6 months to 12.6 years. The available data suggested the patient who underwent the surgery were mostly ended up with an excellent condition. The mean pain of the patients were decreased. Almost all patients reported with no complication and returned to work or sport.Conclusion:The Nirschl technique offers satisfactory outcome for those with lateral epincodylitis, with majority of the patients return to sports or work. However, as this review was limited by level of evidence of the studies, more prospective studies are required to investigate the outcome of such technique.Keywords: Nirschl technique, lateral epicondylitis, tennis elbow.

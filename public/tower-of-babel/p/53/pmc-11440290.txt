Novel surgical method for the exposure and plating of dorsally displaced unstable distal radius fractures
Authors: Hrishikesh Saodekar, Yogesh Sharad Salphale, Gopal Shinde, Gaurav Bhutada, Aman Tomar, Atharva Sharma
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe dorsal approach to the wrist is challenging because of the high number of extensor tendons crossing the wrist joint and extensor retinaculum (ER) enveloping the distal dorsal end of the radius. Dorsal plating of the radius has gained popularity owing to the concept of fragment-specific fixation of the distal radius. Here, we describe a novel method of selectively sectioning the extensor retinaculum that ensures adequate soft tissue care and good fixation with low-profile implants with good clinico- radiological outcome.Keywords: Distal radius fracture, Dorsal Barton fracture, Low-profile plate, Extensor retinaculum

Case presentation

A 62-year-old male sustained a minor fall at home on his left, outstretched hand. The patient was admitted to our hospital for treatment. He had no other injuries apart from the swollen and tender wrist and inability to move the wrist freely. No neurovascular deficit was noted.

Radiographs of the Left wrist AP and Lateral view revealed a wedge-shaped fracture of the distal radius extending into the radiocarpal joint (Fig. 1). There was a dorsal displacement of the dorsal rim of the lower radius and carpus. The distal radioulnar joint was intact.

Surgical technique

Once the patient is stabilized and edema has subsided, the patient is placed supine on the operating table under suitable anesthesia. A tourniquet is used. A standard dorsal incision extending 5–7 cm on Lister's tubercle is taken (Fig. 2). After incision of the fascia, the APL is identified and retracted radially. The APL marks the lateral boundary of the window (Fig. 3). Next, the ER is identified, and its proximal and distal extent was noted. The extensor carpi radialis longus (ECRL) and the extensor carpi radialis brevis (ECRB) then come into view. A sharp subperiosteal dissection leads to ER, which limits further progress of this dissection. The ECRL and ECRB on the radial side and the EPL belly on the ulnar side formed the boundaries of the second window (Fig. 4). The extensor retinaculum,the fracture haematoma and the fracture fragments come into vision (Fig. 5).

After confirming both the windows, mark an inverted ‘V’ as shown (Fig. 6). One has to be careful to maintain the distal 1/4 th breadth of the ER and incise the ER along this ‘V’ which will expose the listers tubercle and the dorso-ulnar fragment and the radial styloid fragment on either side of listers tubercle (Fig. 7). The fracture is then reduced and typically stabilized with three temporary 1.1 mm K-wires Kirschner wires. These wires are placed as distally as possible to engage the dorsal rim of the radius and achieve bi-cortical fixation by securing the volar metaphyseal portion of the radius. By positioning the K-wires distally, the plate can be placed on the dorsal rim of the distal radius without interfering with the carpus during extension. According to the standard principles of osteosynthesis, two low-profile buttress plates can be applied on either side of listers tubercle under the extensor tendons, under radiographic control (Fig. 8). The ER can then be sutured back using non absorbable 5.0 or 6.0 suture material (Fig. 9). The tourniquet is deflated, hemostasis is achieved, and layered closure is performed. After an Xray (Fig. 10) a bulky dressing and a short arm plaster volar splint is applied.

Discussion

The classical dorsal method to the wrist involves the complete division of the ER and extensive surgical dissection. The presence of extensor tendons precludes the use of the dorsal approach by an average general orthopaedic surgeon.

Various authors have also described excision of the Lister's tubercle and evisceration of the EPL tendon from the 3rd compartment of the ER. Both surgical steps result in unnecessary surgical steps and distortion of normal anatomy.

In clinical practice, volar plate fixation is usually the preferred option because of its assumed lower complication frequency and ability to grab dorsally displaced fragments [1,2].

This surgical technique is a novel anatomical approach for osteosynthesis of distal dorsal radius fractures. It is reported in literature that dorsal plating can dependably buttress the joint, resulting in low rates of arthrosis, but at the cost of wrist stiffness and high rates of extensor tendon issues [3] tendon excursion after rerouting (particularly the extensor pollicis longus), and tendon attrition [4,5].

The plates used were anatomically designed, low profile (2.2 mm thick), and made of stainless steel. The manufacturer (Nebula surgicals,India)provided technical papers to determine the thickness of the low-profile plates used in this study. When fully seated, the screws sat below the outer surface of the plate rather than above it. The thickness of the implant, including the plate and screws, was determined as that of the plate itself.

Though the retinaculum flap technique has been described issues can arise if if the flap is sutured under too much tension, which might lead to synovitis and tendon attrition. If the remnant of the proximal retinaculum is frail and the flap is overly lengthy, there's a chance that the ECRB and ECRL tendons might undergo bowstringing [6].

Various authors have documented the attrition of the EPL tendon due to volar plating. However, these patients had screws protruding into their third extensor compartment. This may be because the pointed tip of the screws causes wear and tear of the EPL [7]. Some authors also postulated that the intact third compartment may have increased fluid and hematoma pressure, preventing proper nutrition of the EPL [8].

This approach we describe here, preserves the anatomy of the dorsum of the wrist. The theoretical bowstringing of the extensor tendons is prevented, as we do not violate the distal 1/4 of the width of the ER. Thus, the EPL tendon retains its normal excursion around the listers tubercle. This approach also prevents violation of the fourth extensor compartment, which houses the sensory branch of the posterior interosseus nerve. Therefore, this method also prevents handling of this small nerve and, thus, the possibility of neuroma formation.

The listers tubercle is retained and it effectively acts as a pulley and, therefore, a force multiplier that may be of use to computer operators and fine artisans.

The dorsal plate is the preferred surgical option for treating dorsal shear fractures of the distal radius. These types of fractures can be challenging to address effectively using a volar (palmar) plate approach. Specifically, dorsal shear fractures, fractures involving the dorsal rim of the distal radius, and dorsal/ulnar die-punch fractures are difficult to adequately control and stabilize when approached from the volar side [9].

The dorsal plate provides better access and allows for more direct reduction and fixation of these dorsally displaced and angulated fracture patterns, which the volar plate may struggle to address satisfactorily.

In summary, the dorsal plate is the ideal surgical treatment for distal radius fractures with a predominant dorsal shear component, as it enables more effective fracture reduction and stabilization compared to the volar plate approach for these complex fracture types.

We believe that this novel approach,which has not been described previously, can solve the issues related with the surgical approaches and implant fixation. Nevertheless we need to establish its long term safety with respect to the extensor tendons and primarily the EPL tendon.

Characterization and comparative transcriptomic analysis of skeletal muscle in female Pekin duck and Hanzhong Ma duck during different growth stages using RNA-seq
Authors: Chang Cao, Yingjie Cai, Yuxiao Li, Tao Li, Jiqiao Zhang, Zhigang Hu, Jianqin Zhang
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractDuck is an economically important poultry, and there is currently a major focus on improving its meat quality through breeding. There are wide variations in the growth regulation mechanisms of different duck breeds, that fundamental research on skeletal muscle growth is essential for understanding the regulation of unknown genes. The study aimed to broaden the understanding the duck skeletal muscle development and thereby to improve the performance of domestic ducks. In this study, RNA-seq data from skeletal muscles (breast muscle and leg muscle) of Pekin duck and Hanzhong Ma duck sampled at d 17, 21, and 27 of embryo (E17d, E21d, and E27d), as well as at 6-mo-old following birth (M6), to investigate and compare the mRNA temporal expression profiles and associated pathways that regulate skeletal myogenesis of different duck breeds. There were 331 to 1,440 annotated differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in breast muscle and 380 to 1,790 annotated DEGs in leg muscle from different databases between 2 duck breeds. Gene ontology (GO) enrichment in skeletal muscles indicated that these DEGs were mainly involved in biosynthetic process, developmental process, regulation of protein metabolic process and regulation of gene expression. KEGG analysis in skeletal muscles showed that a total of 41 DEGs were mapped to 7 KEGG pathways, including ECM-receptor interaction, focal adhesion, carbon metabolism, regulation of actin cytoskeleton, calcium signaling pathway, biosynthesis of amino acids and PPAR signaling pathway. The differential expression of 8 selected DEGs was verified by qRT-PCR, and the results were consistent with RNA-seq data. The identified DEGs, such as SDC, SPP1, PAK1, MYL9, PGK1, NOS1, PHGDH, TNNT2, FN1, and AQP4, were specially highlighted, indicating their associations with muscle development in the Pekin duck and Hanzhong Ma duck. This study provides a basis for revealing the differences in skeletal muscle development between Pekin duck and Hanzhong Ma duck.Key words: duck, skeletal muscle, transcriptome, DEG, pathway

INTRODUCTION

In modern animal husbandry, skeletal muscle is considered the most economically important part of an animal's body. Skeletal muscle development is a complex biological process, which is regulated by the environment, feeding, management, and most importantly, genetics (Rosenvold et al., 2003; Bismuth and Relaix, 2010). Unlike mammalian fetuses, avian embryonic development, including skeletal muscle development, is independent of the maternal uterus and is primarily influenced by incubation conditions and genetics (Chen et al., 2013). There is a complex process during prenatal myogenesis, including proliferation and differentiation of myoblast precursors, formation of multinucleated myotubes by fusion of differentiated cells, and then generation of mature myofibers (Stewart and Rittweger, 2006; Grand and Rudnicki, 2007). Because the total number of muscle fibers in poultry is fixed at birth, the postnatal muscle mainly shows an increase in the size of muscle fibers (hypertrophy) (Buckingham et al., 2003; Guo et al., 2015), rather than the adding in the number of muscle fibers in the embryonic period (hyperplasia) (Hutton et al., 2014).

Hanzhong Ma duck, a typical indigenous Chinese egg- and meat-type breed, is mainly distributed in Shaanxi province. It exhibits the advantage of high egg production, but it also has shortcomings such as small size, light weight, and especially low meat production. By contrast, Pekin duck, which currently has the largest duck market share in China, shows a faster growth rate, higher meat productivity, and additional economic benefits. The adult Pekin ducks can weigh 3.9 kg, while only 1.2 kg in the adult Hanzhong Ma ducks (Hu et al., 2016; Ding et al., 2017). These 2 duck breeds represent different muscle growth rates and yields. Therefore, it is valuable to study the muscle growth of these 2 varieties.

Gene expression and pathways related to myogenesis may play a key role in the development of skeletal muscle in different animal breeds. Recent studies have been of great significance to understand the molecular mechanism of skeletal muscle formation, and the regulatory role of related genes and pathways in skeletal muscle development (Wang et al., 2017a; Wu et al., 2019; Liu et al., 2021). In our previous studies, DEGs and regulatory pathways of skeletal muscle development in Pekin duck and Hanzhong Ma duck at different growth stages have been studied and analyzed, respectively (Hu et al., 2021a,b). However, the molecular mechanisms and signaling pathways affecting skeletal muscle development between the 2 duck breeds are still largely unknown. To better understand muscle-related gene expression and pathways, and improve the current annotation of duck genome, this study examined and compared the temporal expression profiles of skeletal muscle between Pekin duck and Hanzhong Ma duck at d 17, 21, and 27 of embryo, as well as at 6-mo-old following birth. In transcriptome sequencing, the DEGs and pathways between duck breeds were analyzed to find the mechanisms involved in duck skeletal muscle development. The results of this study will provide reference for more thorough insight into the regulatory function of duck DEGs, more detailed annotation of duck genome and a better understanding of poultry skeletal muscle development.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Ethics Statement

All animal experiment protocols were approved by the Experimental Animal Management Committee (EAMC) of Northwest A&F University (ethic code: #0330/2019) and were in compliance with the guidelines established by the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals.

Animal and Tissue Samples

Embryonic and adult Peking ducks (P) and Hanzhong Ma ducks (HZ) were used in this study. One hundred breeding eggs of duck were incubated according to routine procedures after disinfection (50 eggs each for Pekin duck and Hanzhong Ma duck, incubate under the same conditions). The eggs came from the Changping duck breeding farm of the Beijing Institute of Animal Husbandry and Veterinary Medicine, Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences and the Hanzhong Ma duck breeding farm of Bairui Agricultural Company in Hanzhong, respectively. Generally, all of the 6-mo-old following birth (M6) ducks were fed a standard diet (Table S1), and water was provided ad libitum. Two breeds of ducks were raised in free range environment with the same management conditions. At the d 17 (E17), d 21 (E21), and d 27 (E27) of the incubation period, as well as at 6-mo-old following birth, 8 individuals from Pekin duck and Hanzhong Ma duck were randomly selected at 4 time points, respectively, and the breast (B) and leg muscles (L) were separated for DNA and RNA extraction.

Sex identification primers (gCHD) were synthesized to detect male and female ducks. The gCHD: Forward: 5′-TGCAGAAGCAATATTACAAGT-3′; Reverse: 5′-AATTCATTATCATCTGGTGG-3′ (only 1 band with 467 bp in male and 2 bands with 326 bp and 467 bp in female in agarose gel electrophoresis) (Liu et al., 2014). Since females in duck farms account for the vast majority (the reason for laying eggs), and the same sex can avoid errors in sequencing data, 3 female embryos were selected as the research object for sequencing.

RNA-Sequencing

Total RNA was extracted from the same samples using QIAzol Lysis Reagent (QIAGEN, Berlin, Germany), in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions. After detection of purity (1.5% agarose gel electrophoresis), RNA integrity (Agilent Technologies, Palo Alto, CA) and concentration (NanoDrop 2000 Spectrophotometer), the 48 RNA-seq libraries were constructed in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions by the TruSeq PE Cluster Kit v4-cBot HS (Illumina, San Diego, CA), and then were sequenced on the Illumina HiSeq X Ten platform (San Diego, CA) to generate 150 bp pair-end reads.

The raw Illumina sequencing reads were cleaned by removing reads containing adapters or poly-N, reads with more than 10% of unknown nucleotides, and low-quality reads containing over 50% of low-quality (Q value ≤10) bases. Besides, quality parameters for filtered data including GC content, Q20 and Q30 were used for data filtering. All subsequent analyses were performed using high-quality clean data.

The filtered reads were mapped to the duck reference genome (Anas platyrhynchos, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/genome/?term=DUCK) and annotated transcripts (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/assembly/GCF_003850225.1), and only reads with a perfect match or 1 mismatch were further analyzed. Then a duck genome map was drawn using the HISAT 2 tool (http://ccb.jhu.edu/software/hisat2/index.shtml). The databases, including Nr (NCBI nonredundant protein sequences, ftp://ftp.ncbi.nih.gov/blast/db/); Nt (NCBI nonredundant nucleotide sequences, ftp://ftp.ncbi.nih.gov/blast/db/); Pfam (protein family, http://pfam.xfam.org/); KOG/COG (clusters of orthologous groups of proteins, http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/KOG/; http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/COG/); Swiss-Prot (a manually annotated and reviewed protein sequence database, http://www.uniprot.org/); GO (gene ontology, http://www.geneontology.org/); KO (KEGG Ortholog database, https://www.genome.jp/kegg/ko.html), were used to annotated the gene function.

Identification of DEGs

The fragments per kilobase of transcript per million mapped reads (FPKM) were calculated to standardize the expression level of annotated genes in the 2 comparison groups. DEGs between 2 biological comparisons were identified by DESeq 2 software. The P values were corrected to control the false discovery rate (FDR) by using the Benjamini and Hochberg's approach. The identified genes with P value <0.05 and |fold change| ≥2 were considered to be differentially expressed.

Analysis of GO and KEGG Pathway

The DEGs based on the specific biological functions were classified by GO enrichment analysis, and the biological functions of genes were characterized using pathway analysis. In this study, to determine and compare the functions of these genes, the biological processes, molecular functions and cellular components of GO terms were analyzed by GOseq R software, and then significantly enriched GO terms were searched for among the DEGs using P < 0.05 as the threshold. The statistical enrichment of differentially expressed genes in KEGG pathways was detected using KOBAS software and searched for significantly enriched KEGG pathways at P < 0.05 level.

RNA-seq Validation by qRT-PCR

Total RNA was extracted from the same samples using QIAzol Lysis Reagent (QIAGEN, Berlin, Germany), in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions. After detection of purity (1.5% agarose gel electrophoresis) and concentration (NanoDrop 2000 Spectrophotometer), the total RNAs (∼2 μg) were reverse-transcribed into cDNA using 5 × All-In-One RT MasterMix with AccuRT Genomic DNA Removal Kit (abm, Richmond, Canada). The expression levels of cDNAs were accurately quantified by qRT-PCR in EcoRT48 (OSA, London, UK) with 5 μL 2 × TransStart Tip Green qPCR SuperMix (Transgen, Beijing, China), 0.3 μL each of 10 μM sense and antisense primers, 0.8 μL normalized cDNA (400 ng/μL) and 3.6 μL ddH2O. Amplification conditions were 95°C for 30 s, and then 40 cycles of 5 s at 95°C and 30 s at 60°C, then 95°C for 15 s, 55°C for 15 s, 95°C for 15 s. Each experiment was performed in triplicate and repeated 3 times. The expression levels of mRNAs were normalized to the housekeeping gene β-actin (duck β-actin and GAPDH were used to test expression stability under this experimental conditions, and finally β-actin was used as housekeeping gene), and the primers used for qRT-PCR are listed in Table S2. Data analyses were performed using the 2−∆∆Ct method as described previously (Livak and Schmittgen, 2001), and presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). Statistical significance of data was assessed using 1-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Duncan's test and Tukey's test. “*” indicated significant difference (P < 0.05) and “**” indicated highly significant difference (P < 0.01).

RESULTS

Descriptive Statistics of RNA-seq Data

Following stringent filtering, an average of 49,555,936 and 52,891,107 clean reads in skeletal muscle of Pekin duck and Hanzhong Ma duck was obtained from RNA-seq, respectively. The reads ranged from 25,355,459 to 39,132,150 were uniquely mapped to the reference genome in muscles of Pekin duck, whereas 25,100,513 to 41,661,109 unique mapped reads existed in Hanzhong duck muscles. Furthermore, in muscles of both duck breeds, GC content were ranged 50.37 to 55.32%, and Q20 and Q30 were not less than 97.07 and 92.56%, respectively (Table S3).

DEGs Analysis

The differences of gene expression during skeletal muscle development between 2 duck breeds were investigated. A total of 331 DEGs were identified in the PE17B vs. HZE17B, of which 226 genes were expressed at significantly higher levels in PE17B and 105 genes in HZE17B. Compared to HZE21B, 1,310 differentially expressed genes were identified in PE21B, of which 771 genes were upregulated and 539 genes were downregulated. DISCUSSION

In poultry skeletal muscle, primary myofibers begin to form at 6 d of embryonic stage, and differentiate between 12 and 16 d in secondary myofibers. Moreover, some studies have shown that muscle fiber bundles and myofiber cross-sectional area significantly decreased from d 22 to d 28 of the embryo. During this period, muscle atrophy occurred. (Chen et al., 2012). It indicates that the growth and development of muscles slows down at this stages. Therefore, in this study, the skeletal muscle tissues of Pekin duck and Hanzhong Ma duck were selected for transcriptome analysis at the embryonic stage of 17 d after the formation of secondary muscle fibers. Embryonic stage of 21 d was chosen because it was the period when the muscle fiber atrophy did not occur in duck embryo, while embryonic stage of 27 d was the final stage of muscle fiber atrophy in duck embryo. At the same time, the skeletal muscle of 6-mo-old ducks in adulthood (stable egg-laying period) was also selected.

Indeed, deciphering the effects of up- or downregulation of muscle transcripts on skeletal muscle development is still an interesting topic in molecular biology. Understanding the differences in molecular mechanisms controlling muscle development between Pekin duck and Hanzhong Ma duck are of great importance for developing superior duck breeds with high-yield and high-quality meat. In this study, there are 331 to 1,440 annotated differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in breast muscle and 380 to 1,790 annotated DEGs in leg muscle from different databases between 2 duck breeds. This research provides a basis for revealing the differences in skeletal muscle between 2 duck breeds. Besides, the sequencing results were validated using qRT-PCR technology, demonstrating the reliability of the results.

Muscle development is a complicated physiological process including cell regeneration, differentiation, and migration. The normal operation of these physiological processes cannot be achieved without strict regulation of relevant genes. In order to discover the potential genes and pathways involved in muscle growth and development, the transcriptome analysis of Pekin duck and Hanzhong Ma duck skeletal muscle was conducted. According to the DEGs at different growth stages, several genes that may affect skeletal muscle development were identified, such as SDC, SPP1, PAK1, MYL9, PGK1, NOS1, PHGDH, TNNT2, FN1, and AQP4. The essential growth differences between the skeletal muscles of the 2 duck breeds may be driven by these DEGs.

Besides, these DEGs were assigned to more than 7 pathways of KEGG, and the identified genes were mainly distributed in extracellular matrix (ECM)-receptor interaction, focal adhesion, carbon metabolism, regulation of actin cytoskeleton, calcium signaling pathway, biosynthesis of amino acids and PPAR signaling pathway. The KEGG pathway analysis plays a crucial role in understanding the growth and development of livestock muscle. Xue et al. (2017) carried out a transcriptome study on the leg muscle of Jinghai yellow chickens, and they found that ECM-receptor interaction, focal adhesion, tight junction, insulin signaling pathway, and regulation of the actin cytoskeleton were the most enriched for the DEGs in KEGG pathway enrichment (Xue et al., 2017). Yin et al. (2021) also found that ECM-receptor interaction, focal adhesion, tight junction, and insulin signaling pathways were the most enriched DEGs in leg muscle of Haiyang yellow chickens (Yin et al., 2021). Li et al. (2022) found that regulation of fatty acid transport, metabolic processes of short-chain fatty acids, β-oxidation of fatty acids using acyl-CoA oxidase, metabolic processes of fatty acids, transport of plasma membrane long-chain fatty acids, biosynthetic processes of fatty acids and negative regulation of fatty acid oxidation were found to be more associated with the DEGs in longissimus dorsi muscle of Wagyu and 3 Chinese Red Steppe (Li et al., 2022). Xu et al. (2018) adopted RNA sequencing to detect transcriptome in the longissimus dorsi muscle of Wei pigs and Yorkshire pigs, they found that skeletal muscle cell differentiation, phospholipid catabolic process, and extracellular matrix structural constituent were significantly related to DEGs (Xu et al., 2018).

In this research, focal adhesion and ECM-receiver interaction were the 2 most enriched pathways across all developmental stages. It indicates that these 2 pathways are likely related to muscle development. Many life processes in cells are related to adhesion, such as embryonic development, cell growth, wound healing and immune responses. Focal adhesion (FA) connects the actin cytoskeleton and ECM to ensure the smooth progress of cell migration and morphogenesis (Hirata et al., 2014). F-actin and FAs are coupled to each other and many FA proteins bind directly or indirectly to F-actin. Different classes of focal-adhesion structural exhibited varying degrees of correlated motions with actin filaments, indicating hierarchical transmission of actin motion through focal adhesions (Hu et al., 2007). Focal adhesion kinase (FAK) has multiple roles within skeletal muscle, and FAK can regulate myoblast development and muscle fiber formation (Quach et al., 2009). FAK is a molecule with multiple functions. According to a recent study, FAK is closely related to skeletal muscles, changes in FAK expression and phosphorylation have been found to correlate to specific developmental states in muscle size, muscle fiber formation and myoblast differentiation (Graham et al., 2015). Besides, Wang et al. (2016) compared the migration and adhesion of skeletal muscle satellite cells between 2 types of pigs, Lantang pig and Landrace pig, and explored how FAK signaling modulates the processes. The results indicated that FAK plays a key role in regulating the necessary process of muscle regeneration (Wang et al., 2016). MYL9 and PAK1 are 2 DEGs enriched in the focal adhesion pathway, and previous studies have revealed the role of these 2 genes in muscle development. It is necessary to pay attention to and discuss these 2 genes.

Myosin light chain 9 (MYL9), also known as LC20 or MYRL2, is a regulatory light chain that regulates myosin activity. As an actin-dependent molecular motor, it can use the energy generated by ATP hydrolysis to move along actin. The phosphorylation of myosin light chain can affect the reorganization of cytoskeleton, change the cell shape, and increase the activity of actin ATPase. Moreover, MYL9 is also related to actomyosin contractility and stress fiber assembly (Licht et al., 2010; Shehadeh et al., 2011). Previous studies have found that MYL9 is crucial for maintaining the integrity of myosin II (Park et al., 2011). In addition, research has revealed that MYL9 can serve as a key gene to regulate and affect the growth and development of skeletal muscles in pigs and chickens (Ye et al., 2017; Li et al., 2020). P21-activated kinases (PAKs) 1 to 3 are serine/threonine protein kinases, and their activity can be regulated by Rac and Cdc42 GTP enzymes. The regulation of GTP enzymes Rac and Cdc42 involves various cellular activities related to normal and pathological cell functions, so PAK may be related to various physiological processes in cells. PAK protein is divided into 2 groups, with PAK1-3 belonging to the first group. Another group, PAK4-6, differs greatly from PAK1-3 in terms of structure, organization, and regulation. The expression of PAK1 and PAK2 can be detected in skeletal muscle cells, while PAK3 is not expressed in skeletal muscle (Bokoch, 2003; Tunduguru et al., 2014). Group I PAK is related to various processes of muscle development. In the fusion of myoblasts in Drosophila, group I PAK has partial redundant functions, with the close homolog of mammalian PAK2, DPak3, playing a major role in this process (Duan et al., 2012). Recent studies have evaluated the role of PAK1 in insulin sensitivity and glucose homeostasis in skeletal muscle, and found that PAK1 in skeletal muscle is essential for maintaining systemic glucose homeostasis (Merz et al., 2022). The lack of muscle specificity in PAK1 and PAK2 can lead to a decrease in muscle mass, indicating that PAK1 and PAK2 play an important role in muscle. Moreover, PAK1 and PAK2 are activated during myoblast differentiation and promote myoblast differentiation (Joseph et al., 2017). MYL9 and PAK1 genes are associated with muscle development, and may regulate skeletal muscle growth through the focal adhesion pathway in duck.

The ECM-receptor interaction pathway is closely related to muscle development. Liu et al. (2019) sequenced the high and low triglyceride groups of chicken breast muscles, and obtained differentially expressed genes and pathways related to fat generation, including the ECM-receptor interaction pathway, and indicated that this pathway may be a key pathway for lipid deposition in chicken breast muscle tissue (Liu et al., 2019). Cui et al. (2012) identified candidate genes and pathways related to the deposition of intramuscular fat (IMF) in chicken breast tissue. The results showed that IMF deposition in chicken was not only regulated by genes and pathways related to lipid metabolism and muscle development, but also regulated and mediated by other genes and pathways involved in cell junctions, such as the ECM-receptor interaction pathway (Cui et al., 2012). San et al. (2021) also found that the ECM-receptor interaction pathway affects the differentiation, lipid synthesis, and metabolism of intramuscular adipocytes, thereby changing the IMF content and affecting the quality and flavor of chicken (San et al., 2021). In this study, SPP1 and SDC are 2 DEGs enriched in the ECM-receptor interaction pathway, which are related to muscle development.

Secreted phosphoprotein 1 (SPP1) is a multifunctional secreted acidic glycoprotein that plays a crucial role in the focal adhesion signaling pathway, which regulates muscle development. Previous studies have shown that there are differences in the expression levels of SPP1 gene between the proliferation and differentiation stages of chicken myoblasts and the muscle growth stage (Liu et al., 2018). SPP1, also known as osteopontin (OPN), is a protein released by myoblasts that may assist in controlling both myogenic. Usually, osteopontin is not detected in normal muscle fibers, but its expression level significantly increases in injured muscles (Hirata et al., 2003; Uaesoontrachoon et al., 2008). The expression of osteopontin after muscle injury may support the rapid recovery of muscle function after injury, and the regeneration of muscle after injury requires osteopontin from both muscle and nonmuscle cells (Uaesoontrachoon et al., 2013; Wasgewatte Wijesinghe et al., 2019). Syndecans (SDC), which encodes stearoyl COA desaturase and is highly expressed in animal liver and adipose tissue, plays a key role in the PPAR signaling pathway by activating adipocyte differentiation (Wang et al., 2005; Rosen and Macdougald, 2006). SDCs belong to a family of transmembrane proteoglycans composed of 3 members (syndecans 1–4). Early studies indicate that syndecan-1 plays pivotal roles in both muscle cell proliferation and differentiation (Velleman et al., 2004). Syndecan-3 and syndecan-4 play important regulatory roles in muscle development as well as in satellite cell physiology (Cornelison et al., 2001, 2004). However, another family member, SDC2, has rarely been reported to be related to muscle. The available evidence indicates that SDC2 is related to osteoprogenitor cell adhesion, proliferation, differentiation and survival (Mansouri et al., 2015). Therefore, SPP1 plays a role in the recovery process of damaged muscles, and SDC can promote muscle cell proliferation. The 2 genes in duck muscle may promote duck skeletal muscle development through the ECM-receptor interaction pathway.

Other pathways, carbon metabolism, regulation of actin cytoskeleton, calcium signaling pathway, biosynthesis of amino acids and PPAR signaling pathway, also play an important role in skeletal muscle development. As is well known, carbon metabolism, calcium signaling pathway, and biosynthesis of amino acids provide basic substances such as glucose, calcium, and amino acids for muscle development. Besides, there are many articles reporting the involvement of regulation of actin cytoskeleton and PPAR signaling pathway in muscle development. Regulation of actin cytoskeleton is related to cell migration, and PPAR signaling pathway is involved in adipogenesis of skeletal muscle cell (Tang and Gerlach, 2017; Wang et al., 2017b, Wang et al., 2022b). Therefore, these pathways can all affect the development of skeletal muscles.

Differential gene expression is considered to be the main cause of genetic variation in animal muscle development, indicating that the regulation mechanism of muscle development may have changed. In addition to the above genes, there are also DEGs enriched in other pathways that are worth paying attention to such as PGK1, NOS1, PHGDH, TNNT2, FN1, and AQP4. Phosphoglycerate kinase 1 (PGK1) is an important enzyme in the metabolic glycolysis pathway. Recent studies indicate PGK1 may be related to muscle development and participates in the muscle development of piglets (Voillet et al., 2014). Wang et al. (2017b) performed a proteomic analysis of longissimus dorsi muscle from 2 Chinese native mini-type breeds and 2 introduced western breeds using iTRAQ, and found that PGK1 might be involved in the regulation of muscle growth (Wang et al., 2017b). Moreover, PGK1 was also related to the fiber type of donkey muscle (Chai et al., 2023).

Nitric oxide (NO) is a messenger molecule, which has numerous molecular targets. NO has many functions such as neurotransmission, regulates gene transcription and mRNA translation (Schuman et al., 1991; Khan et al., 1996; Liu et al., 2002). The nitric oxide synthases (NOS) are primarily responsible for oxidizing L-arginine to L-citrulline while releasing biologically active NO free radical. There are 3 quite distinct NOS isoforms have been described. Neuronal NOS (nNOS or NOS1) is predominantly found in neuronal tissue and is also highly expressed in skeletal muscle. Inducible NOS (NOS2) is expressed in macrophages, whereas endothelial NOS (NOS3) has been shown to be expressed in various types of cells, including cardiomyocytes (Förstermann et al., 2012; Baldelli et al., 2014; Solanki et al., 2022). In previous studies, NOS1 was expressed in a subpopulation of atrial cardiomyocytes including myoendocrine cells and affected contraction of isolated myocytes. Moreover, in sarcoglycanopathies nNOS is a modulating factor in the course of muscular dystrophy (Miethke et al., 2003; Angelini et al., 2014; Villmow et al., 2015).

Phosphoglycerate dehydrogenase (PHGDH), as the first rate-limiting enzyme of the serine synthesis pathway, catalyzes the conversion of 3-phosphoglycerate (3-PG) into 3-phosphohydroxypyruvate (3-PHP). Subsequently, 3-PHP is converted to serine through phosphoserine aminotransferase and phosphoserine phosphatase (Chen et al., 2022a). Serine is an important amino acid that participates in various life activities in cells and tissues. Amino acid analysis of PHGDH knockout mouse embryos showed that serine concentration was significantly reduced, genes involved in amino acid metabolism and amino acid transport were upregulated, and genes involved in muscle development were downregulated (Furuya et al., 2008). An exogenous drug that has been proven to enhance the increase of skeletal muscle net protein, beta-adrenergic agonists were injected into the body of growing pigs to detect gene expression in pig skeletal muscles, and it was found that the expression level of PHGDH significantly increased (Brown et al., 2016). At the same time, the expression of PHGDH increased on the second day of C2C12 cell differentiation, which was the key time of myogenic Sexual differentiation, indicating that PHGDH was related to the differentiation of muscle cells (Brearley et al., 2019). In addition, PHGDH is also related to the proliferation and differentiation of chicken muscle cells. PHGDH can promote the proliferation and differentiation of primary chicken myoblasts, inhibit myoblast proliferation by knocking down PHGDH with siRNA, and overexpression of PHGDH enhances muscle cell proliferation (Chen et al., 2022b; Wang et al., 2022a).

Pekin duck has faster muscle development and higher meat production than Hanzhong Ma duck, and one of the reason for this phenotypic difference is related to differentially expressed genes. TNNT2 can encode troponin T (TnT), which is a central player in the calcium regulation of actin thin filament function and is crucial for the contraction of striated muscles. TnT is encoded by 3 homologous genes, TNNT1, TNNT2, and TNNT3. TNNT1 and TNNT3 are specifically expressed in slow and fast skeletal muscle, respectively, while TNNT2 is specifically expressed in the cardiac muscle (Wei and Jin, 2016). Previous studies have found that TNNT2 is associated with the occurrence of dilated cardiomyopathy in humans (Jordan et al., 2021; Seidel et al., 2021). In birds, TNNT2 is also expressed in embryonic skeletal muscles. TNNT2 protein, as a differentially expressed protein, may be involved in the development of embryonic chicken skeletal muscle (Cui et al., 2023). In this study, the expression level of TNNT2 in the skeletal muscle of Pekin duck is higher than Hanzhong Ma duck, indicating that TNNT2 may accelerate the development of duck skeletal muscle. Fibronectin 1 (FN1) belongs to the FN family and is involved in various cellular biological processes related to cell fibrosis. FN1 can serve as a marker for fibroblast activation and is also a gene that promotes muscle fibrosis (Sawaki et al., 2018; Fatima et al., 2022). In recent studies, both genomic methylation analysis of sheep skeletal muscle and transcriptome analysis of chicken leg skeletal muscle have indicated that FN1 was involved in the regulation of muscle development (Xue et al., 2017; Yue et al., 2023). In this research, FN1 is highly expressed in Pekin duck skeletal muscle than Hanzhong Ma duck, indicating that this gene may play a role in promoting muscle fiber formation and accelerating muscle development in duck skeletal muscle. Aquaporin 4 (AQP4) encodes aquaporin (AQP), which is a family of transmembrane proteins involved in the maintenance of osmotic gradients. AQP4 is abundant in skeletal muscle and is expressed in both human and mouse skeletal muscles (Yang et al., 2000; Vizzaccaro et al., 2018). Research has demonstrated the physiological role of AQP4 in supporting muscle contraction and metabolic changes that occur in skeletal muscles during prolonged exercise (Basco et al., 2013). In addition, AQP4 is also associated with early muscle atrophy, and the expression level of AQP4 decreases in muscular dystrophy (Au et al., 2008; Ishido and Nakamura 2020). Among the 2 breeds, the content of AQP4 in the skeletal muscles of Hanzhong Ma duck is higher, suggesting that AQP4 may affect the development of duck muscles in this study.

All these studies have identified that the DEGs have important functions in skeletal muscle development. Therefore, the differential expression of genes that were in skeletal muscle of Pekin ducks and Hanzhong Ma ducks, may have an important regulatory effect on the mechanism of duck skeletal muscle development.

CONCLUSIONS

In this study, transcriptome sequencing of Pekin duck and Hanzhong Ma duck skeletal muscle at d 17, 21, and 27 of embryo, as well as at 6-mo-old following birth was carried out. This study screened and discussed genes such as SDC, SPP1, PAK1, MYL9, PGK1, NOS1, PHGDH, TNNT2, FN1, and AQP4, which may be key genes involved in skeletal muscle development. Besides, this study also analyzed the pathways enriched by DEGs and found that DEGs are mainly enriched in 7 pathways: ECM-receptor interaction, focal adhesion, carbon metabolism, regulation of actin cytoskeleton, calcium signaling pathway, biosynthesis of amino acids, and PPAR signaling pathway. The result provided an important basis for discovering the differences between the skeletal muscles of 2 duck breeds, and provided a scientific basis for the regulation mechanism of duck growth and development.

FAPI: a solution to all, what about NENs?
Authors: Ozgul Ekmekcioglu, Filiz Celik, Simon Hughes, Meltem Ocak, Fred Verzijlbergen, Stefano Fanti, Christophe M. Deroose
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Fibroblast activation protein (FAP)–targeted PET imaging is an emerging technique that images the tumor stroma by targeting cancer-associated fibroblasts, offering high tumor-to-background contrast and improved lesion detection in tumors with low [¹⁸F]FDG avidity. Given the central role of CAFs in tumor progression and therapy response, FAPI PET has gained interest in neuroendocrine neoplasms (NENs), where stromal imaging may provide complementary information to conventional modalities. This review evaluates the biological rationale, current evidence, and potential clinical role of FAPI PET in the imaging and management of NENs.

Introduction

Fibroblast activation protein (FAP) PET imaging represents a novel and increasingly valuable modality in nuclear medicine imaging, distinguished by its targeting of the tumor stroma rather than the tumor cells themselves, in most tumor types (Hirmas et al. 2023). Unlike [18F]FDG PET, which relies on elevated glucose metabolism, FAP PET exploits the overexpression of FAP on cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), a key component of the tumor microenvironment implicated in tumor progression, metastasis, and immune evasion. Radiotracers based on FAP inhibitors (FAPIs) such as [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-46 or [18F]AlF-FAPI-74 have demonstrated high tumor-to-background (TBR) contrast across a wide range of malignancies, including those that exhibit low [18F]FDG avidity, thereby offering superior lesion detectability in selected clinical contexts (Li et al. 2022, Pabst, Kim et al. 2025, Hope et al. 2025).

Fibroblasts are the predominant stromal cell type within the tumor mesenchyme of many solid malignancies, particularly those of gastrointestinal origin (Kalluri and Zeisberg 2006). A subset of these, known as cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), arise from resident fibroblasts or mesenchymal stem cells that become activated within the tumor microenvironment (TME). CAFs contribute to tumor progression by secreting a variety of signaling molecules, including exosomes, which influence key processes such as cell proliferation, invasion, migration, drug resistance, and metastatic dissemination (Caligiuri 2023). They are thus considered central mediators of tumor aggressiveness. Among these, FAP plays a particularly prominent role in regulating the extracellular matrix and promoting a pro-tumorigenic microenvironment.

In the context of tumor-associated fibrosis, quiescent fibroblasts can undergo phenotypic transformation into CAFs. Despite significant research, the precise mechanisms underlying CAF formation remain incompletely understood and may differ across tumor types and pathological contexts. Notably, CAFs have been implicated in modulating the tumor response to therapy and facilitating tumor invasion through both biochemical and biomechanical interactions with adjacent malignant cells (Dvorak 1986).

Recent investigations have highlighted the potential role of FAPI PET in the imaging of neuroendocrine neoplasms (NENs), with particular emphasis on its relationship to the tumor stroma. Emerging evidence suggests that CAFs possess a dualistic role, capable of both promoting and restraining tumorigenesis depending on the context and molecular profile, underscoring the need for further investigation into their functional plasticity and therapeutic potential.

In this review, we aim to evaluate whether FAPI PET represents a promising tool for the imaging and management of NENs, and to compare it with other techniques.

Biological rationale for FAPI PET in NENs

Neuroendocrine neoplasms (NENs) comprise a heterogeneous group of tumors arising from the diffuse neuroendocrine system, with the gastrointestinal tract pancreas and bronchus being the most common primary sites. These neoplasms are classified along a spectrum from well-differentiated neuroendocrine tumors (NETs), which tend to follow a more indolent course, to poorly differentiated neuroendocrine carcinomas (NECs), which are more aggressive and associated with a worse prognosis (Fitzgerald and Weiner 2020). Somatostatin receptor (SSTR) PET using radiolabeled analogs such as [68Ga]Ga-DOTATATE PET/CT is the standard for imaging well-differentiated NETs, owing to their high expression of SSTRs. However, this approach is often inadequate in poorly differentiated NECs or in NENs arising within anatomical sites like such as lung and thymus, where receptor expression can be diminished or absent.

The physical and molecular interactions between tumor cells and CAFs have been shown to support co-invasion into surrounding tissues, potentially contributing to the symptomatic fibrotic complications observed in NETs. CAFs represent a critical component of the TME, influencing not only tumor growth but also therapeutic outcomes. While they exhibit considerable phenotypic heterogeneity, CAFs can be broadly identified by the expression of markers such as FAP (Fitzgerald and Weiner 2020). Neuroendocrine neoplasms (NENs) are often associated with desmoplastic and fibrotic stromal reactions (Blažević et al. 2018).

The biological niche of FAP imaging in NENs is defined by the prevalence of cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) within the tumor stroma rather than the malignant cells themselves (Pabst, Kim et al. 2025, Adnan and Basu 2023). This distinction is critical for reconciling the heterogeneous performance of FAPI across different clinical scenarios.

In early clinical observations, [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-46 PET demonstrated clearer delineation of hepatic and nodal metastases in well-differentiated NENs compared with conventional [68Ga]Ga-DOTATATE PET, attributable to reduced background activity and higher contrast (tumor-to-background ratio; TBR) (Fortunati et al. 2022). However, this utility is not universal. Evidence from patients with [177Lu]Lu-DOTATATE refractory disease indicates significantly lower FAPI sensitivity (38.2%) compared to [68Ga]Ga-DOTATATE PET (86%). While PRRT is known to induce fibrosis in settings like neoadjuvant therapy for pancreatic NETs, the low FAPI uptake in end-stage refractory cases suggests that the fibrotic response in advanced disease may be relatively “acellular” or stroma-poor, lacking the active, FAP-expressing myofibroblasts required for tracer accumulation (Cheng et al. 2021).

In the context of high-grade NENs and neuroendocrine carcinomas (NECs), these aggressive phenotypes, FAP expression appears to correlate with stromal remodeling and tumor invasiveness. Recent data using a dual-tracer combining [18 F]FDG and [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 PET approach has identified a “stroma-poor” phenotype (FDG+/FAPI-) that is associated with significantly shorter progression-free survival (4 vs. 9 months) (Michalski et al. 2024). Based on this evidence, clinical hypothesis suggests that, the anticipated value of FAPI lies not in replacing SSTR PET for well-differentiated NETs, but as a stratification tool. It may serve to identify “stroma-rich” versus “stroma-poor” biological subgroups, particularly in high-grade disease where standard metabolic imaging remains suboptimal.

Finally, a specific biological subgroup would likely to benefit from FAP imaging is small intestinal NENs (Blažević et al. 2018, Graf et al. 2024). These tumors are uniquely characterized by a pronounced mesenteric desmoplastic reaction. Preliminary evidence suggests that FAPI PET can effectively visualize this stromal response, offering a potential clinical role in staging and surgical planning for these patients that exceeds the capabilities of conventional SSTR or FDG PET. This approach not only has the potential to enhance diagnostic accuracy and staging but may also inform treatment planning and facilitate theranostic applications. The development of FAPI-labeled therapeutic agents further underscores the potential of this platform as a dual diagnostic and therapeutic tool, offering new avenues for managing refractory or advanced NENs.

Characteristics of FAPI molecule and radiopharmacy

Fibroblast activation protein inhibitors (FAPIs) comprise a chemically and pharmacokinetically heterogeneous class of ligands developed to target fibroblast activation protein (FAP), a serine protease highly expressed on cancer-associated fibroblasts within the tumor microenvironment. Despite shared biological targeting, FAPI radiopharmaceuticals differ substantially in molecular scaffold, molecular size, pharmacokinetic behavior, and radiolabeling strategy, all of which critically influence their suitability for diagnostic imaging or targeted radionuclide therapy.

From a radiopharmaceutical perspective, FAPI ligands can be broadly categorized into quinoline-based small molecules, peptide-based or cyclic ligands, and retention-enhanced derivatives, including multimeric and albumin-binding constructs (Table 1). Importantly, retention-enhanced derivatives do not represent a distinct pharmacophore class but rather reflect pharmacokinetic modification strategies applied to quinoline- or peptide-based scaffolds to modulate systemic circulation and tumor residence. This structural classification reflects the evolution of the field from imaging-optimized tracers designed for rapid tumor visualization toward therapy-oriented ligands engineered to improve tumor residence time and absorbed dose delivery (Hirmas et al. 2023, Li et al. 2022, Pabst, Kim et al. 2025).

Structure-based classification of FAPI radiopharmaceuticals (preclinical and clinical landscape)

Among these structural categories, quinoline-based small-molecule FAPIs remain the most extensively investigated and clinically translated class. The vast majority of clinically investigated FAPI radiopharmaceuticals originate from quinoline-based small-molecule inhibitors, most notably derivatives of the UAMC1110 scaffold. Systematic structure–activity relationship studies led to the identification of several prototypical compounds, including [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-02 and [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04, which showed rapid tumor uptake and very fast clearance from blood and most normal organs. Subsequent optimization efforts focused on improving pharmacokinetics without compromising target specificity. Modifications in linker length, polarity, and chelator selection resulted in derivatives such as [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-46, which demonstrated slightly prolonged tumor retention and improved TBRs compared with earlier analogues. Within this structural class, OncoFAP represents a distinct but related quinoline-based scaffold characterized by very high binding affinity to FAP and favorable imaging contrast. Like other quinoline-based small-molecule FAPIs, OncoFAP retains the characteristic rapid-clearance pharmacokinetic profile, while also serving as a versatile platform for further molecular engineering, including multimerization and albumin-binding strategies aimed at enhancing tumor retention for therapeutic applications (Graf et al. 2024, Millul et al. 2023).

To address logistical constraints associated with gallium-68, several 18F-labeled FAPI tracers have been developed. Among them, [¹⁸F]F-FAPI-74 has gained particular attention due to its compatibility with centralized cyclotron production and broader dissemination, while largely preserving the favorable imaging characteristics of gallium-labeled quinoline FAPIs. Despite their diagnostic success, quinoline-based FAPIs are consistently characterized by rapid tumor washout, which limits tumor absorbed dose and constrains their therapeutic applicability (Graf et al. 2024, Millul et al. 2023, Wu et al. 2025).

Peptide-based FAP ligands have emerged as a complementary molecular class specifically designed to address the limited tumor residence time of small-molecule FAPIs. These ligands typically employ cyclic or conformationally constrained peptide scaffolds that enable extended interactions with FAP, resulting in slower systemic clearance and prolonged tumor retention (Chakravarty et al. 2023, Baum et al. 2022). A prominent example is [177Lu]Lu-FAP-2286, which has advanced from preclinical evaluation into clinical studies. In preclinical and first-in-human studies, [¹⁷⁷Lu]Lu-FAP-2286 demonstrated substantially prolonged tumor retention compared with quinoline-based FAPIs, translating into higher tumor absorbed doses and more favorable therapeutic indices (Chakravarty et al. 2023). These observations are consistent with broader analyses comparing small-molecule, peptide-based, and retention-enhanced FAPI ligands for radionuclide therapy applications (Graf et al. 2024).

To further improve tumor retention, several advanced molecular strategies have been explored, including dimerization, multivalency, and albumin-binding modifications. Dimeric FAPI constructs aim to enhance apparent affinity through avidity effects, while albumin-binding moieties prolong systemic circulation and increase tumor exposure. Albumin-binding FAPI derivatives have shown markedly prolonged tumor residence times and increased predicted tumor absorbed doses in preclinical studies. However, these benefits are accompanied by higher blood pool activity and increased radiation exposure to normal organs, emphasizing the importance of individualized dosimetry and risk–benefit assessment prior to clinical translation.

Despite these translational and dosimetric challenges, several retention-enhanced FAPI radiopharmaceuticals have advanced from preclinical development into first-in-human and early-phase clinical evaluation with both albumin-binding and dimeric FAPI derivatives labeled with [177Lu]Lutetium (Fu et al. 2025). Importantly, these investigations consistently demonstrate that pharmacokinetic modification strategies aimed at extending systemic circulation and tumor residence can substantially enhance tumor absorbed dose compared with conventional small-molecule quinoline-based FAPIs. However, the associated increases in blood pool activity and normal-organ radiation exposure continue to necessitate careful patient-specific dosimetry and optimization of tumor-to-background therapeutic indices. Collectively, these emerging clinical data support the evolving role of retention-enhanced FAPI radiopharmaceuticals as promising therapeutic platforms while underscoring the need for prospective dosimetry-guided trials and further refinement of molecular design and treatment protocols.

FAPI-NET

From a theranostic perspective, the differences between FAPI-based PET imaging and SSTR PET reflect fundamental differences in target biology and tracer pharmacokinetics rather than simple differences in imaging performance (Mori et al. 2023). FAPI radiopharmaceuticals are designed to bind FAP expressed on CAFs within the tumor stroma, leading to rapid tracer accumulation and often high tumor-to-background contrast at early imaging time points. This pharmacokinetic profile can enhance lesion conspicuity in selected NENs, particularly in settings characterized by prominent desmoplastic or fibrotic stromal reactions.

However, because FAP expression is not restricted to malignant stroma and may also be upregulated in benign fibrosis, inflammation, or therapy-related tissue remodeling, FAPI uptake does not provide tumor-cell specificity and must be interpreted with caution.

In contrast, NET-directed radiopharmaceuticals based on somatostatin analogs rely on receptor-mediated binding to tumor cells, conferring high specificity for well-differentiated NETs and enabling a direct diagnostic–therapeutic continuum through established peptide receptor radionuclide therapy (Fortunati et al. 2022, Has Simsek et al. 2024). Accordingly, while FAPI PET may offer complementary information on stromal biology and microenvironmental activation—particularly in tumors with heterogeneous or reduced SSTR expression—it does not replace receptor-targeted imaging in NETs. Instead, the differing biological targets and pharmacokinetic behaviors of these radiopharmaceutical classes support a complementary, rather competitive, role for FAPI PET within NET theranostic workflows, with established SSTR tracers remaining central to treatment selection and response assessment (Michalski et al. 2024, Kreppel et al. 2021).

FAP PET/CT in neuroendocrine neoplasms: clinical evidence and research outlook

Imaging of NENs

NENs represent a spectrum of tumors from well-differentiated NETs to poorly differentiated NECs. SSTR PET/CT remains the preferred modality for routine imaging in these tumors. Furthermore, [18F]FDG PET/CT is useful in higher grade NENs (NET G2 with Ki-67 > 10% and NET G3; NEC) for more accurate disease characterization and prognostication (Ambrosini et al. 2022). [18F]FDG PET/CT and SSTR imaging (e.g. [68Ga]Ga-DOTATATE PET/CT) may show limitations, particularly in heterogeneous tumors or dedifferentiated lesions.

Targeting stromal CAFs provide complementary information in tumor biology so potentially can improve detection and characterization of NETs and NECs. FAP PET/CT can improve lesion detection in dedifferentiated or SSTR-negative NENs and may support treatment planning by characterizing tumor biology and stromal activity. Emerging evidence suggests that FAP PET/CT may offer complementary or superior diagnostic capabilities in select scenarios in which uptake of FAPI-ligands is particularly pronounced in tumors characterized by high expression of FAP within the tumor stroma (Singh et al. 2024, Abbasi et al. 2025, Dendle et al. 2022, Kratochwil et al. 2019). Current molecular imaging for NENs is undergoing a shift from a “one-tracer-fits-all” model to a biology-driven, multi-tracer approach. The role of FAP-targeted imaging is best understood through clinical scenarios.

In patients with low grade well-differentiated tumors characterized by high SSTR expression and low/no [68Ga]Ga-SSTR PET/CT remains the definitive gold standard for staging and theranostic selection. In this setting, FAPI imaging is currently considered exploratory or complementary (Cheng et al. 2021, Zhou et al. 2024). In well differentiated NETs, FAPI PET/CT demonstrates variable uptake in G1–G2 NETs which typically retain high SSTR expression. FAPI may offer superior tumor-to-background ratios (TBR) in the liver due to low physiological hepatic background (Cheng et al. 2021, Singh et al. 2024, Wang et al. 2021). This can occasionally lead to clearer detection of small liver metastases that appear equivocal on SSTR PET. Therefore, FAPI is not a replacement for SSTR PET but may provide independent information regarding the tumor’s fibrotic environment, such as identifying mesenteric desmoplasia.

Patients group with high-Grade NENs (NET G3 or NEC) and heterogeneous disease represents the primary unmet clinical need where FAPI PET shows the most promise. These tumors often exhibit heterogeneous SSTR expression and high [18 F]FDG uptake. FAPI serves as a “bridge” between SSTR and FDG imaging by targeting the active tumor stroma, which is often more pronounced in aggressive, dedifferentiated lesions. FAPI positive/FDG negative may represent areas of high stromal density and tumor-host interaction that are not yet hypermetabolic (Cheng et al. 2021, Dendle et al. 2022, Ergül et al. 2022, Giesel et al. 2021). In neuroendocrine carcinomas, FAPI can exhibit high TBRs where FDG uptake may be obscured by inflammatory background or high physiological activity in organs like the brain or bowel.

FDG positive/FAPI negative pattern is expected to be compatible with “stroma-poor” structure which is a critical prognostic marker. A study from Michalski et al. showed that [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04 was a useful staging method with dual imaging compared to FDG PET images for highly proliferative NENs (agressive NETs or NECs) in a group of patients which consisted of mostly with primary gastro-enteropancreatic tumors, were both scanned with [18F]FDG and FAPI PET images and analyses showed tumor heterogeneity which strongly related impact on PFS (Michalski et al. 2024). Patients with FDG positive/FAPI negative lesions have demonstrated significantly shorter progression-free survival (median 4 vs. 9 months), suggesting a more aggressive biological behavior prone to early progression (Michalski et al. 2024).

Impact on staging and management

The use of FAPI PET as a staging tool is particularly impactful in high-grade GEP-NENs and affects significantly management. This includes upstaging (identifying more lesions than FDG in G3 pancreatic NETs/NECs) and downstaging (correcting “false positive” FDG uptake in the lung or lymph nodes). Kosmala et al. described a mixed cohort about 32 patients with abdominal and digestive cancers, including 18 NET cases as part of the series. Staging changed in nearly 50% of the total patient group (n = 15). Among those, downstaging was recorded in 20% and upstaging was the main effect, recorded in 12 out of 15 patients. Three out of 15 patients with either grade 3 pancreatic NET or NEC showed more lesions in FAPI images than [18F]FDG PET images in the study group. Positive discordant lesions were detected in the primary organ or in liver metastases in FAPI PET images. Furthermore, FAPI images of 3 out of 15 patients in the downstaging group with pancreatic NET and gastrointestinal NEC demonstrated less lesions in lung parenchyma, lymph nodes and bones compared to [18F]FDG PET (Kosmala et al. 2023).

One of the most clinically challenging imaging signs in NEN management, particularly in small intestinal NETs, is the abdominal desmoplastic reaction. This intense fibrotic host response often surrounds mesenteric lymph node metastases, leading to bowel obstruction or mesenteric ischemia. While SSTR PET identifies the metabolic activity of the tumor cells within these masses, it cannot quantify the extent of the surrounding fibrosis. Since FAP is highly expressed by the activated myofibroblasts driving this desmoplasia (R.Liu and Su 2026), FAPI PET may offer a unique ‘stromal-volume’ assessment. This could potentially assist surgeons in evaluating the complexity of mesenteric masses and help clinicians monitor the fibrotic component of the disease, which remains ‘invisible’ to standard metabolic tracers.

The correlation between FAPI-to-SSTR tumor volume ratios and the Ki-67 index suggests that FAPI PET can help clinicians select the most appropriate “target” for radioligand therapy in patients with heterogeneous disease. Kreppel et al. demonstrated significant correlation between the Ki-67 index and the ratio of [68Ga]Ga-DATA5m.SA.FAPI to [68Ga]Ga-DOTATOC tumor volume in liver metastases in a group of 13 patients with poor differentiated tumor of NENs. Despite that, the Ki-67 index was not correlated with FAPI tumor SUVmax in the same patient group (Kreppel et al. 2021).

Recently completed and ongoing clinical trials

The recently completed clinical trial of Pabst et al., published in The Lancet Oncology, shows the results of a Phase II trial studying the accuracy of [68Ga]GA-FAPI-46 for a wide range of tumor types in 155 patients and is an important step in the development of a more effective role of FAPI-based diagnostics in clinical practice (Pabst, Kim et al. 2025). Patients with NENs were only 2% of the group with grade 2 pancreatic tumors and grade 1 metastatic NEN with unknown primary origin. Since the NEN patient population was very low in this group (n = 3), the outcomes were only presented for the total patient population and not for specific tumor types. The patient-based positive predictive value (PPV) for detecting FAP-positive tumors was 90%, while the region-based value was 92%, based on histopathological validation in 127 of 144 patients (88%) (Pabst, Kim et al. 2025).

Several clinical trials are still investigating the clinical potential of FAP tracers. Nevertheless, more multicenter studies with more data about NENs are needed, to evaluate the heterogeneity of NENs, which is a heterogenous entity with different clinical outcomes. These trials might create a breakthrough in the implementation of FAP PET in routine clinical practice in these malignancies (NCT05518903, NCT04459273, NCT05641896). Nevertheless, more studies with higher numbers of NEN cases will take more time than the other tumor types due to the lower incidence and the biological heterogeneity.

Recent interest in theranostic applications has led to the initiation of the LuMIERE trial (NCT04939610) and Lu-FAP CUP trial (NCT07434180) a Phase 1/2 study investigating the safety and eMicacy of [177Lu]FAP-2286. While the study targets a broad range of advanced solid tumors and cancer of unknown primary, it represents a critical milestone for NEN patients who have exhausted SSTR-targeted therapies but maintain high FAP expression on imaging (NCT04939610, NCT07434180).

The findings of previous studies collectively question that FAP PET/CT may complement or even surpass conventional tracers in certain NEN types, where SSTR-targeted imaging is often limited due to heterogeneous tumor behavior. FAPI’s favorable biodistribution, high tumor uptake and ability to detect lesions with low [18F]FDG uptake or SSTR expression position it as a promising alternative imaging biomarker for both diagnostic and theranostic applications. While the current NEN specific evidence is limited and is based on case reports, small series, or pan-cancer exploratory studies, and recently small prospective studies, FAP PET/CT shows strong potential for enhancing the clinical management of aggressive stromal-rich NENs.

Quantitative metrics such as SUVmax or TBR/TLR vary among studies but suggest strong signal in NEC lesions. The specificity and biological underpinnings of FAPI uptake across tumor entities remain incompletely understood. The need for tumor-specific validation requires prospective, well controlled investigations in disease-specific populations. Future prospective and comparative studies are warranted to determine its reproducibility, sensitivity, specificity, diagnostic accuracy, and potential integration into multimodal diagnostic algorithms and theranostic strategies.

Limitation and challenges

The clinical value and biological underpinnings of FAPI uptake across tumor entities remain incompletely understood. Although its ability to accumulate in many cancers has been confirmed and have shown promising results in management (Chandekar et al. 2023), superiority over [18F]FDG has never been demonstrated in properly designed prospective registration trials in any malignancy. Consequently, in clinical practice FAP PET has not replaced [18F]FDG in any oncological indication and remains largely confined to clinical trials (Sollini et al. 2021). The main reason for this lack of shift in clinical practice is likely the limited research and data due to availability of radiopharmaceutical, to prove the advantage in terms of diagnostic accuracy: [18F]FDG has raised the bar of diagnostic accuracy compared to previous imaging modalities (bone scan, CT) and is now well established in routine practice, so the room for improvement by a new tracer has become smaller. While individual case reports (Cheng et al. 2021, Wang et al. 2021) suggest that FAPI can outperform SSTR or FDG PET in specific instances of liver or bone metastases, larger cohorts highlight that this is not a universal finding across all NEN grades (Has Simsek et al. 2024). As with many innovations, the initial pan-oncological enthusiasm for FAP PET led to expectations that it might become the “new [18F]FDG yet in any oncological indication and remains largely confined to clinical trials (Kratochwil et al. 2019, Sollini et al. 2021). However, the potential for FAP PET to surpass FDG remains significant in specific, well-defined scenarios where FDG has intrinsic limitations. First of all, low-FDG-avid Malignancies where glucose metabolism is low and no other specific targets (like PSMA, SSTR, norepinephrine transporter) exist, FAPI could offer the sensitivity leap required for routine adoption. Furthermore, Unlike FDG, FAPI may offer superior detection in anatomical regions where high metabolic noise obscures disease like high Physiological/Inflammatory Background, such as the brain, liver, and peritoneal cavity. In these settings, FAPI’s high tumor-to-background ratio could resolve diagnostic dilemmas that FDG cannot.

When it comes to NEN, the room for FAP PET clinical applications is even more limited. The usefulness of SSTR PET has already been well demonstrated and is hard to surpass in terms of diagnostic accuracy, especially in low- and mid-grade NEN. Moreover, not just one but two metabolic tracers ([18F]FDG and [18F]DOPA) are available to complement a comprehensive imaging work-up. The very limited literature currently available suggests a possible prognostic role for FAPI (Michalski et al. 2024), but this would imply multiple scans, likely involving a triple-tracer approach—an option that is highly unlikely to be sustainable in terms of both cost-effectiveness and patient compliance.

The most promising avenue for FAPI is arguably in theranostics, given its potential to treat diseases with limited therapeutic options, especially at advanced stages, such as ovarian and pancreatic cancer. Unfortunately, apart from a few small reports (Lanzafame et al. 2025), the data so far have been disappointing, albeit limited. The main limitation seems to be the relatively low degree of uptake and tracer retention, compared with more successful theranostic ligands, a reality highlighted in Hofman’s hierarchy (Hofman 2023). To avoid following the path of [18F]FLT, which failed to achieve widespread use, the FAP PET field needs demonstration of diagnostic and/or therapeutic efficacy in properly designed, prospective clinical trials, such as FAPI GO and FAPI PRO (NCT07217704 A Phase, NCT07217717 A Phase).

In conclusion, seven years after the introduction of clinical studies on FAPI, evidence of its limitations appears to be growing just as rapidly as expectations did in its early years. The need for a “killer application” is increasingly evident, and prospective trials are being developed to provide the evidence base for these. Involvement of commercial radiopharmaceutical companies and clinical researchers is necessary to make FAP PET the “next [18F]FDG, at least in some clinical entities. Time will tell if NENs, in particular NECs, will be part of those.

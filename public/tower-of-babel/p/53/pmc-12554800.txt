Introduction

IGD, classified as a non-substance addiction, was included in the appendix of the fifth edition of the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (DSM-5) published by the American Psychiatric Association in 2013. According to the diagnostic criteria for IGD, at least five out of the following nine criteria must be met: 1) Preoccupation with gaming; 2) Withdrawal symptoms when gaming is stopped; 3) Tolerance, leading to a need for increasing amounts of gaming time; 4) Unsuccessful attempts to control or cease gaming; 5) Loss of interest in previous hobbies and entertainment due to gaming; 6) Continued excessive gaming despite awareness of psychosocial problems it causes; 7) Deception of family, therapists, or others regarding gaming time; 8) Use of gaming to escape or relieve negative moods; 9) Jeopardizing or losing significant relationships, jobs, educational, or career opportunities because of gaming (Association, xxxx). It is noteworthy that the gratification experience during gameplay is also a key mediating factor triggering gaming cravings. Through enhancing positive experiences such as gratification and compensation (e.g., alleviating negative emotions), game engagement elicits craving responses across different dimensions, including reward/relief cravings and physiological cravings. Therefore, games capable of providing high-intensity gratification experiences may tend to have a higher addiction potential (Antons et al., 2023). Individuals with gaming disorder often report an inability to regulate or stop their gaming behavior and experience intense negative emotional states when unable to play (Dong et al., 2017, Kaptsis et al., 2016). The harmful consequences of excessive gaming were also recognized by the World Health Organization (WHO), which included Gaming Disorder (GD) in the 11th Revision of the International Classification of Diseases (ICD-11) in 2018. GD is characterized by persistent gaming behavior manifested as: impaired control over gaming; increasing priority given to gaming to the extent that it takes precedence over other life interests and daily activities; and continuation or escalation of gaming despite the occurrence of negative consequences (Reed et al., 2019, Saunders et al., 2017)

In recent years, the prevalence of IGD has shown an upward trend globally, particularly among adolescents and young adults (Darvesh et al., 2020, Zhou et al., 2022). Adolescence represents a critical period of physical and psychological development, during which the brain undergoes rapid maturation characterized by high neurobiological plasticity. While this plasticity facilitates adaptation to physical and emotional changes, it also renders adolescents more prone to risk-taking and sensation-seeking behaviors (Cerniglia et al., 2017, Sugaya et al., 2019). The cortico-striatal circuitry is a core neural network that supports reward processing, and the maturation of this circuitry does not occur synchronously. Adolescence represents a critical period of “region-specific maturation”, which directly shapes the functional characteristics of the reward system during this developmental stage (Rappaport et al., 2020). Compared to adults, adolescents appear more susceptible to developmental disorders involving the reward system (Lemmens et al., 2015, Mentzoni et al., 2011), consequently leading to a higher prevalence of IGD during this developmental stage (Odgers & Jensen, 2020). Epidemiological studies indicate a global adolescent IGD prevalence rate of 9.9 % (Gao et al., 2022), with regional epidemiological surveys in China reporting a comparable positive detection rate of approximately 10 % among Chinese adolescents (Gou et al., 2024), underscoring this behavioral addiction as a global public health challenge impacting adolescent mental health.

fMRI is an imaging technique that investigates neural function by detecting physiological signal changes associated with brain activity. It measures fluctuations in the blood-oxygen-level-dependent (BOLD) signal, which indirectly reflects neuronal activity through the process of neurovascular coupling. This allows for the identification of regional neuronal activation during resting states or specific task paradigms, thereby enabling the exploration of functional roles within specific brain regions and their interconnections (Schramm et al., 2023). Recently, cognitive neuroscience research has employed fMRI to investigate changes in the adolescent brain throughout puberty (Cabrera et al., 2018), fMRI's capability to capture detailed brain images in real-time allows researchers to observe neural activity, aiding a comprehensive understanding of the neurological impact of addictive behaviors on the brain (Leon Mendez et al., 2024). rs-fMRI is applicable to individuals across almost all age groups and varying levels of cognitive function. It quantifies brain activity during fMRI scans without requiring the active, explicit tasks necessary for tb-fMRI (Cortese et al., 2021). tb-fMRI paradigms are generally more complex than resting-state scans, as they involve experimental cognitive tasks designed to engage specific cognitive processes (e.g., memory, emotion, decision-making), focusing on participants’ performance at both behavioral and neural levels under different task conditions or events (Huang et al., 2024). DTI, on the other hand, assesses white matter integrity by studying the diffusion of water molecules within the brain, revealing changes at the microstructural level (Dobos et al., 2023).

IGD, as a growing public health concern, exhibits high prevalence rates and extensive physical and psychological harm, particularly among adolescents with high developmental plasticity. In this context, functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI), leveraging its non-invasive advantages for probing brain activity in vivo, can reveal intrinsic brain connectivity patterns via resting-state fMRI (rs-fMRI), capture neural responses in specific brain regions during stimulus tasks through task-based fMRI (tb-fMRI), and analyze the microstructural integrity of white matter fibers via diffusion tensor imaging (DTI). This provides an indispensable research tool for moving beyond behavioral observations and directly investigating the neurobiological mechanisms underlying IGD, including abnormal functional connectivity, disrupted neural circuits, and alterations in white matter structure. Therefore, given that IGD has become a critical public health issue, utilizing fMRI technology to uncover the specific neural mechanisms in this high-risk adolescent population during their critical period of brain development carries significant theoretical necessity and practical urgency. This review aims to systematically synthesize the application of rs-fMRI, tb-fMRI, and DTI in adolescent IGD research, integrating existing evidence to elucidate core neural abnormalities such as prefrontal-striatal circuit dysregulation, reward system hyperactivation, and white matter microstructural damage. Furthermore, it explores the association of these abnormalities with behavioral disinhibition and cognitive deficits. By synthesizing multimodal neuroimaging findings, this review further proposes the translational potential of these results for developing objective diagnostic biomarkers, optimizing targeted intervention strategies, and dynamically evaluating treatment efficacy, providing a theoretical foundation for future research directions and clinical applications.

Methods

Preregistration statement

Although preregistration of systematic reviews is a recommended best practice, this study failed to conduct prospective registration due to insufficient familiarity with the preregistration process at the initiation stage. To maximize the transparency and reproducibility of the research process, we hereby report all research steps in full, including the literature search strategy, rigorous inclusion and exclusion criteria, data extraction procedures, and pre-planned analytical methods.

Literature search and screening strategy

The systematic literature search for this study was designed and conducted by the first author (Z.M.) in February 2025. The search was performed based on the PICO framework across three electronic databases: PubMed, Web of Science, and Science Direct, covering publications from January 2015 to February 2025. The search strategy combined subject headings and free-text terms, using the following key terms: (“teenager*” [tw] OR “adolescent” [Mesh]) AND (“Internet addiction” [tw] OR “Internet addiction disorder” [Mesh]) AND (“neuroimaging” [tw] OR “functional magnetic resonance imaging” [tw] OR “MRI” [tw] OR “neuroimaging” [Mesh]). The initial search yielded 386 records. These references were imported into EndNote 21, where duplicate records were removed using the automated deduplication function, resulting in 336 unique articles for further screening. The literature screening process was carried out independently by two researchers (Z.M. and L.M.Z.) to ensure objectivity. Screening was conducted in two stages: first, based on titles and abstracts, 133 clearly irrelevant records were excluded; subsequently, the remaining 203 articles underwent full-text retrieval and detailed evaluation. Throughout the screening process, the two researchers worked independently and cross-checked their results. Any disagreements regarding inclusion were initially resolved through discussion between them. If consensus could not be reached, a third senior researcher (Y.W.) was consulted to make a final decision, ensuring that a unanimous agreement was achieved for all articles. Through this rigorous process, 21 studies were ultimately identified as meeting the pre-defined inclusion criteria and were included in the final analysis of this systematic review. Literature inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion Criteria: (1) Articles written in English or Chinese; (2) Published between January 2015 and February 2025; (3) Participant mean age ± 1 SD ≥ 10 years and <20 years (WHO definition of adolescents); (4) Clear definition of IGD including diagnostic criteria; (5) Use of functional magnetic resonance imaging.

Exclusion Criteria: (1) Languages other than Chinese or English; (2) Age range not meeting criteria; (3) Comorbid underlying diseases or symptoms; (4) Receiving specific interventions; (5) Non-functional MRI examinations or not involving neuroimaging research results.

Risk of bias assessment

The evaluation results indicated that the majority of studies performed well in terms of subject selection and standardization of fMRI data acquisition, demonstrating a low risk of bias. However, with regard to inter-group comparability, some studies exhibited issues such as inadequate gender matching or inconsistent age ranges, leading to a high-risk rating for individual studies. In controlling for comorbidities or confounding factors, most studies managed well, though a few failed to clearly report or control for relevant variables, resulting in an unclear risk. The most prominent issue was the widespread lack of blinding in outcome assessment, as most studies did not specify whether blinding was used during data analysis and interpretation, leading to an unclear or high-risk rating in this domain. In summary, although most studies demonstrated good control over key methodological aspects, limitations in inter-group comparability, confounding control, and implementation of blinding may somewhat affect the robustness of the conclusions drawn in this study (The details are shown in Table 1).

Resting-state fMRI studies

The most commonly used measurement method in resting-state fMRI is seed-based connectivity analysis (SBC), which examines large-scale correlations in blood oxygen level-dependent (BOLD) signals between a region of interest (seed) and other gray matter voxels. Another approach for assessing large-scale connectivity is independent component analysis (ICA). Other resting-state fMRI metrics focus on local connectivity (e.g., regional homogeneity, ReHo) or, more broadly, on its variability (e.g., fractional amplitude of low-frequency fluctuations, fALFF) (Castellanos and Aoki, 2016, Cortese et al., 2021).

Functional connectivity abnormalities at the regional and network levels

Regarding regional FC, multiple studies consistently reported widespread reduced functional connectivity within the fronto-striatal circuit in individuals with IGD. For instance, Chun et al., 2018, Yuan et al., 2016 and Hwang et al. (2020) all observed decreased FC between the orbitofrontal cortex (OFC), nucleus accumbens (NAc), cingulate cortex, and subregions of the striatum, indicating impairment in core circuits involved in reward evaluation and cognitive control. Conversely, some studies also reported enhanced connectivity in certain pathways. For example, Chun et al. (2018) found increased FC between the right cingulate cortex and the NAc, suggesting possible compensatory or maladaptive functional reorganization in IGD.

In terms of interactions between large-scale brain networks, studies revealed significant abnormalities in the functional integration of the executive control network (ECN), salience network (SN), and default mode network (DMN). Zhao, Zhang et al. (2024) and Wang et al. (2017) both reported decreased FC between the ECN and SN, alongside increased connectivity between the ECN and the dorsal attention network (DAN), suggesting systematic dysregulation in attention allocation and executive control in IGD patients. Particularly noteworthy is the reduced connectivity within the SN-DMN and anterior DMN (Lee et al., 2020, Wang et al., 2017), further supporting the applicability of the “triple network model” in addictive behaviors. This model proposes that dysfunctional regulation of the DMN and ECN by the SN may lead to an imbalance between self-referential processing and cognitive control.

Interhemispheric and local functional activity consistency

In interhemispheric connectivity, multiple studies using voxel-mirrored homotopic connectivity (VMHC) found significantly reduced hemispheric synchronization in prefrontal regions (e.g., medial OFC and DLPFC) in IGD patients (Chen et al., 2021, Bi et al., 2015, Wang et al., 2015). These connectivity reductions were negatively correlated with the severity and duration of addictive behaviors, indicating impaired interhemispheric coordination mechanisms that may directly relate to diminished impulse control.

At the level of local brain activity, studies revealed region-specific alterations Ma et al. (2024) and Yi et al. (2016) both observed decreased ReHo values in sensorimotor and higher cognitive regions (e.g., postcentral gyrus, inferior parietal lobule, and inferior frontal gyrus), which negatively correlated with internet usage time. In contrast, increased fALFF/ReHo was found in certain prefrontal areas, while decreased ReHo was observed in regions such as the cerebellum. These patterns suggest that IGD may involve a complex combination of local neural hypoactivity and compensatory hyperactivity. Niu et al. (2023) found that the IGD group exhibited increased both sReHo and dReHo in the bilateral medial superior frontal gyrus, superior frontal gyrus, and supplementary motor area. Moreover, significantly elevated dReHo was observed in the left putamen, pallidum, caudate nucleus, and bilateral thalamus, suggesting that disruption of the white matter-striato-thalamic circuitry may be associated with an imbalance in executive control and reward processing in IGD.

Functional connectivity density and neurotransmitter systems

Using resting-state functional connectivity density (rsFCD) analysis, Du et al. (2016) found increased rsFCD in the DLPFC and temporal cortex of IGD patients, which positively correlated with behavioral performance, indicating the presence of possible compensatory mechanisms. At the neurotransmitter level, studies by Wang et al. (2018) and Niu et al. (2024) emphasized that dysfunctions in multiple systems-including dopamine, serotonin, GABA, and norepinephrine levels were significantly associated with FC abnormalities. Specifically, reduced connectivity in the VTA-NAc/mOFC pathway and its negative correlation with IAT scores further reveal the neurochemical basis of reward circuit dysregulation at a systems level.

Task-based fMRI studies

Qi et al. (2016) compared fMRI data from IGD adolescents and HCs during the Balloon Analogue Risk Task (BART). Following negative outcomes, the covariance between risk level and brain activation was significantly higher in the IGD group compared to HCs in the bilateral ventromedial prefrontal cortex (VMPFC), left inferior frontal cortex (IFC), right ventral striatum (VS), and left hippocampus/parahippocampus (p < 0.05). This suggests that negative feedback may exacerbate disadvantageous risk decision-making in IGD individuals by heightening sensitivity in neural circuits related to value assessment (VMPFC), reward anticipation (VS), and emotional learning (hippocampus).

Choi et al. (2018) investigated neural representation differences in response to self, others, and game characters in adolescents with online gaming addiction. They found that the non-addicted group activated the medial prefrontal cortex (MPFC) for self, and the dorsomedial prefrontal cortex (DMPFC) for others and game characters. In contrast, the addicted group exhibited more significant activation in the MPFC and anterior cingulate cortex (ACC) under the game character condition (stronger than during self-reference), and ACC activation was positively correlated with addiction symptom severity (r = 0.431, p < 0.05). They proposed that addicted adolescents incorporate game characters into self-concept-related neural circuits, suggesting their cyber-identity may supersede their real-world identity.

DTI studies

Fractional Anisotropy (FA), a common DTI parameter measuring the directionality of water diffusion, quantifies white matter integrity (Dobos et al., 2023). Bi et al. (2015) used DTI to assess the microstructural integrity of the corpus callosum. Compared to HCs, IGD adolescents showed significantly reduced FA in the genu of the corpus callosum, along with increased fiber tract length, but no significant difference in fiber number. In HCs, DLPFC VMHC was positively correlated with corpus callosum FA values; this structure–function coupling relationship was disrupted in the IGD group. They suggested that structural abnormalities in the corpus callosum of IGD adolescents might lead to dysfunctional integration by impairing interhemispheric information transfer efficiency.

Wang et al. (2018) employed DTI to evaluate the microstructural characteristics of VTA-related white matter pathways in IGD patients. They found significantly reduced FA values in the bilateral VTA-NAc pathways of IGD patients compared to HCs (left: p = 0.019; right: p < 0.001), indicating impaired structural integrity of white matter in this pathway. However, FA values in the VTA-mOFC pathways showed no significant difference between groups (left: p = 0.092; right: p = 0.16). The authors proposed that defects in the structural connectivity of VTA-NAc might constitute a potential neural vulnerability basis for IGD, while FC abnormalities might be more directly related to symptom severity.

Zhai et al. (2016) used DTI technology to construct a fiber number-weighted structural network and found that the IGD group exhibited significantly decreased global efficiency (p = 0.001) and local efficiency (p = 0.04), as well as increased shortest path length (p = 0.019), indicating impaired information integration and transmission efficiency in the white matter network. Additionally, nodal efficiency significantly decreased in regions such as the frontal cortex, anterior cingulate cortex, and globus pallidus, suggesting that abnormalities in the organizational structure of the white matter network may be closely related to the neural mechanisms of IGD.

Discussion

Harms of IGD

The advent of the internet has profoundly altered daily life. While online society reduces the need for direct in-person interaction and facilitates social life, negative consequences such as problematic internet use and internet addiction are emerging (Kato et al., 2020). Physically, IGD disrupts circadian rhythms, leading to insomnia and other sleep disorders, which subsequently cause headaches, learning difficulties, memory impairment, aggressive behavior, and cognitive decline affecting memory and learning abilities (Hammad et al., 2024). Psychologically, it easily triggers negative emotions, such as depression and anxiety, interfering with mental health and reducing life satisfaction (Saikia et al., 2019). Socially, excessive immersion in the internet leads individuals to neglect real-world social interactions, resulting in social isolation and difficulty in establishing and maintaining healthy interpersonal relationships (Tateno et al., 2019). Economically, it can lead to impulsive spending, online fraud, personal financial loss, and unnecessary economic burdens (Siomos et al., 2014). Behaviorally, internet addiction is often associated with adverse behaviors such as smoking, alcohol abuse, and fighting, increasing the likelihood of individuals engaging in risky behaviors that threaten their own and others’ safety (Yen et al., 2009).

As a behavioral addiction, IGD not only leads to external issues such as impaired social functioning, academic decline, and sleep deprivation but is also closely associated with significant dysfunction of the brain's reward system. Research indicates that individuals with IGD exhibit enhanced neural responses across multiple stages of reward processing (e.g., reward anticipation and outcome monitoring), particularly characterized by overactivation in the striatum (including the NAc and caudate nucleus), as well as reduced gray matter volume and weakened functional connectivity in key brain regions such as the prefrontal cortex and ventral tegmental area. These neuroadaptive changes are accompanied by decreased availability of dopamine D2 receptors and overactivation of the mesolimbic dopamine pathway, leading to an imbalance in reward sensitivity. As a result, individuals with IGD show heightened sensitivity to both gaming-related and general rewards (e.g., monetary rewards) and tend to pursue immediate gratification while neglecting long-term negative consequences. This dysfunction of the reward system further drives individuals into a vicious cycle of “craving-use-tolerance-withdrawal”, exacerbating impairments in decision-making and self-control. This evidence suggests that IGD is fundamentally a disorder of reward system dysfunction with a clear neurobiological basis (Chen et al., 2023, Wang et al., 2021).

Internet gaming and brain dysfunction

Brain dysfunction refers to a pathological state characterized by structural and functional abnormalities in brain neurons, neural circuits, or brain regions. Aberrant functional activity within specific brain areas or disrupted functional connectivity between regions can lead to dysregulation of cognitive, emotional, and behavioral control functions, thereby impairing an individual’s normal physiological functions and social adaptability. This state is closely associated with the occurrence, development, and clinical manifestations of substance use disorders (Murphy et al., 2024).

A core feature of IGD, and a shared diagnostic criterion in both DSM-5 and ICD-11, is “loss of control” (Jeong et al., 2024), Behavioral and neuroimaging studies have identified abnormal activation in multiple regions of the PFC in individuals with IGD, including increased activation in the ACC and DLPFC. As the PFC is considered the foundation of executive functions, this impaired activation demonstrates that deficient inhibition is a significant correlate of IGD. Individuals with IGD exhibit impaired response inhibition and heightened levels of impulsivity (Argyriou et al., 2017). Chen et al. (2021) also found that the IGD group displayed a negative inhibitory effect of the dorsal striatum on the DLPFC, suggesting that functional disruption within the prefrontal-striatal circuit may underlie weakened impulse control.

Craving is another clinical feature of IGD, playing a crucial role in maintaining addictive behavior and the risk of relapse (Jeong et al., 2024), an imaging study identified craving-related fMRI alterations in IGD patients. When exposed to game-related cues, IGD individuals showed activation in both the ventral and dorsal striatum, reflecting heightened sensitivity to gaming rewards, indicative of intense gaming craving. Concurrently, diminished regulatory control of the PFC over the striatum makes it difficult for patients to suppress their gaming urges (Zeng et al., 2022). Choi et al. (2018) also found that IGD patients exhibited significantly greater activation in the medial prefrontal cortex (MPFC) and ACC during gaming conditions.

Neuroimaging features of adolescent internet addiction

rs-fMRI

The neural mechanisms underlying IGD are complex and multifaceted, involving the coordinated action of multiple brain regions, neurotransmitters, pathways, and brain networks. The reward system is a network comprising multiple brain regions, neurotransmitters, and pathways, key structures include the VTA, NAc, and PFC. This system is intricately linked to impulsivity and addictive behaviors, with the NAc serving as a critical hub for reward processing and interacting with the PFC to govern impulsivity (Kim et al., 2021). Cross-study evidence indicates that weakened FC between the right OFC and NAc, coupled with reduced FC between the VTA and mOFC, act synergistically, this multi-node reward circuit dysfunction may impair value assessment capabilities (Chun et al., 2018, Wang et al., 2018). GCA further revealed negative regulation of the DLPFC by the dorsal striatum, suggesting that impaired top-down control may sustain compulsive internet use by enhancing impulsivity (Chen et al., 2021, Hong et al., 2015). Disruption of the white matter-striato-thalamic circuitry may also be associated with an imbalance in executive control and reward processing in IGD (Niu et al., 2023). This localized reward circuit disruption does not occur in isolation; large-scale network interaction remodeling may also amplify the impact of local abnormalities on cognitive function.

Brain function is driven not only by local properties within specific regions but also relies on the coordinated interaction between brain areas and effective coupling between large-scale networks, the interplay of brain networks underpins cognitive activity and participates in regulating emotion and motivation, neural changes within specific brain circuits are associated with particular cognitive functions (Zeng et al., 2021). Abnormal coupling between the SN and DMN exhibits a dual manifestation: enhanced AIC-pSTS connectivity alongside reduced SN-DMN connectivity, jointly indicating neural reorganization related to social motivation (Lee et al., 2020, Wang et al., 2017). Functional dissociation within the ECN is particularly notable-increased activity in prefrontal components of the ECN coupled with decreased activity in the frontal eye field region of the DAN may lead to cognitive decoupling in real-world environments through imbalanced attentional resource allocation (Wang et al., 2017, Zhao et al., 2024). When network-level interaction abnormalities persist, the nervous system establishes a new homeostasis through hierarchical adaptive changes; this reorganization is often more readily observable in the sensorimotor system.

Enhanced connectivity between the dorsal putamen and primary somatosensory cortex, combined with abnormal negative connectivity between cerebellar Crus II and the NAc, constitute the neural substrate of automated behavioral patterns (Hong et al., 2015, Ko et al., 2023). Local activity analysis revealed that reduced ReHo in the postcentral gyrus was significantly negatively correlated with internet usage time, while activity in cerebellar lobule 7b was positively correlated with addiction severity, this suggests the sensorimotor network participates in pathological maintenance through a bidirectional “inhibition-facilitation” regulatory mechanism (Ma et al., 2024, Yi et al., 2016). The formation of neural imprints within the sensorimotor system reflects adaptive structural and functional changes in the brain, which manifest as dynamic reorganization characteristics at the whole-brain level.

Interhemispheric functional desynchronization and compensatory elevation in global connection density form an antagonistic pattern (Bi et al., 2015, Du et al., 2016). Dynamic network analysis revealed an increased proportion of time spent in strong connection states within the executive control network, this “rigidity” of network states may impair mode-switching efficiency during tasks, providing a dynamic explanation for reduced cognitive flexibility (Zhao, Zhang et al., 2024). Whole-brain dynamic reorganization also does not occur in isolation; it is often accompanied by concomitant dysregulation within neurotransmitter systems, which in turn interact with the aforementioned regional and network changes.

Dopamine is implicated in various addictive behaviors. Dopaminergic system dysregulation in IGD drives persistent gaming despite negative consequences. Wang et al. found that VTA-NAc/mOFC connection strength was negatively correlated with addiction severity (Wang et al., 2018). Cross-transmitter interaction studies identified that Granger causality abnormalities in the putamen covaried with alterations in the dopamine, serotonin (5-HT), and GABA systems (Niu et al., 2024). Abnormal fractional amplitude of low-fALFF/ReHo in the prefrontal cortex co-occurred with molecular changes in the μ-opioid receptor and cholinergic systems, suggesting that multi-system imbalances collectively shape the neurochemical landscape of addiction (Ma et al., 2024).

tb-fMRI

Task-based fMRI studies on the neural mechanisms of addictive behaviors reveal hyperactivation of the reward system and abnormal restructuring of self-representation. During the BART, IGD individuals exhibited aberrant neural coding of negative outcomes, characterized by enhanced co-activation of the VMPFC and VS (Qi et al., 2016). This excessive engagement of the reward evaluation system may reinforce maladaptive emotional memories via the hippocampal-striatal circuit, forming a “loss-chasing” behavioral pattern. Such neural abnormalities are evident not only during risk decision-making but also in the domain of self-cognition.

Social role tasks revealed that addicts exhibit abnormal MPFC activation in response to gaming avatars, with activation intensity positively correlating with clinical symptoms (Choi et al., 2018). This shift in neural representation suggests a “cognitive hijacking” of self-relevant neural circuits by the virtual identity, potentially constituting the biological basis for the virtual identity superseding the real-world self.

DTI

Addiction-related white matter abnormalities manifest not only as structure–function decoupling within interhemispheric integration systems but also specifically affect reward-related neural pathways. The corpus callosum is a major white matter fiber tract connecting the left and right cerebral hemispheres, playing a critical role in information transfer and functional coordination within the brain (Rahmani et al., 2019). DTI studies revealed significantly reduced FA in the genu of the corpus callosum, accompanied by abnormal increases in fiber tract length. The positive correlation between DLPFC functional synchrony and corpus callosum FA observed in healthy controls was absent in the addiction group. This suggests that white matter structural abnormalities may exacerbate prefrontal functional desynchronization by impairing the efficiency of interhemispheric information transfer (Bi et al., 2015). Beyond interhemispheric connection abnormalities, the white matter structure of reward pathways also shows specific alterations.

FA values were significantly reduced in the VTA-NAc fiber tract, while the VTA-mOFC pathway remained intact. Although structural connectivity did not directly correlate with Internet Addiction Test (IAT) scores, combined with the finding of reduced VTA-NAc FC in resting-state studies, this suggests that white matter integrity deficits may constitute the anatomical basis for functional abnormalities, while FC changes more directly reflect clinical symptoms (Wang et al., 2018).

From a global network perspective, patients with IGD also exhibit reduced global and local efficiency of the whole-brain white matter structural network, increased shortest path length, and decreased nodal efficiency in key brain regions involved in cognitive control and motivational processing (e.g., the frontal cortex and anterior cingulate cortex) (Zhai et al., 2016). These multi-level lines of evidence collectively indicate that damage to the brain's white matter structure may extensively impair the efficiency of information integration and transmission in neural networks, and particularly affect the coordinated function of the cognitive control and reward systems. This may serve as an important neural mechanism underlying their core symptoms such as behavioral dyscontrol and craving.

Research significance and clinical value

fMRI, by capturing BOLD signals, provides key evidence for elucidating the neural mechanisms of IGD: Abnormal activation patterns in prefrontal cortical regions (e.g., ACC, DLPFC), as well as functional disruptions within prefrontal-striatal circuits (e.g., negative inhibition of DLPFC by the dorsal striatum), are directly associated with patients' deficits in response inhibition and high impulsivity, explaining their loss of control over gaming behavior. Simultaneously, hyperactivation of the ventral striatum in response to gaming cues coupled with weakened prefrontal regulatory capacity jointly form the neural circuit basis for pathological gaming craving. This sensitization of the reward system and dysfunction of the executive control system create a dynamic imbalance, driving the maintenance and relapse of compulsive gaming behavior.

Regarding clinical diagnosis, the specific brain network abnormalities identified in fMRI studies offer the potential for establishing objective biomarkers. For instance, weakened FC between the right OFC and NAc, reduced VTA-mOFC synergy, and abnormal SN-DMN coupling could serve as potential neuroimaging features to aid in distinguishing IGD patients from healthy individuals. Furthermore, interhemispheric functional desynchronization resulting from impaired corpus callosum white matter integrity, along with cross-modal abnormalities in neurotransmitter systems such as dopamine and 5-HT, further enrich multi-level integration within the diagnostic framework. These findings overcome the limitations of traditional subjective diagnoses relying solely on behavioral scales and lay the groundwork for developing quantitative assessment systems based on neuroimaging.

For optimizing intervention strategies, fMRI research not only reveals potential neuromodulation targets but also provides a tool for dynamic monitoring of treatment efficacy. For example, DLPFC functional abnormalities suggest that transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS) or transcranial direct current stimulation (tDCS) might improve impulse control by enhancing prefrontal regulatory capacity. Conversely, the characteristic striatal hyperactivation points towards cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) designs targeting the reward system, such as cue exposure therapy to reduce sensitivity to game-related stimuli. The “network rigidity” revealed by dynamic network analysis (e.g., persistently elevated strong connectivity within the executive control network) provides a focal point for neurofeedback training, where modulating network state switching efficiency may restore cognitive flexibility. Additionally, the observed neural restructuring of virtual self-representation in task-based fMRI (e.g., abnormal MPFC activation to gaming avatars) provides a neuroplasticity basis for identity correction therapy. For intervention efficacy assessment, fMRI can objectively quantify treatment response by tracking the degree of restoration in PFC-striatal circuit FC, dopaminergic system homeostasis, and other indicators, enabling dynamic adjustment of personalized intervention plans.

Innovativeness

Compared to previous reviews, this study makes several novel contributions: Firstly, we explicitly focus on adolescents aged 10–20, narrowing our scope to the specific changes occurring against the backdrop of high brain plasticity during this developmental period. This approach allows the revealed dysfunctions such as disruptions in prefrontal-striatal circuits and interhemispheric desynchronization to be more specifically tailored to this developmental stage, avoiding the conflation of adult and adolescent data. Secondly, this article is the first to systematically integrate the latest evidence available up to early 2025, providing a pioneering multimodal integrated analysis of the application of rs-fMRI, tb-fMRI, and DTI in adolescent IGD research. This transcends the limitations of previous reviews, which often relied on single modality data, thereby constructing a multidimensional “function-structure” model that incorporates brain functional activity, neural circuit connectivity, and white matter microstructure. This model systematically elucidates the multilevel neural abnormalities associated with IGD. More importantly, beyond summarizing patterns of neural abnormalities such as prefrontal-striatal circuit dysfunction and reward system dysregulation we further explore the relationships between these findings and neurotransmitter systems, including dopamine and 5-HT. We also highlight their translational potential in the development of clinical diagnostic biomarkers, the selection of neuromodulation targets, and the evaluation of treatment efficacy. This provides a theoretical foundation and practical direction for future in-depth mechanistic research and the optimization of intervention strategies.

Research limitations and future directions

Although current research has uncovered key neural mechanisms of adolescent IGD, several limitations persist. Firstly, there is sample bias; most studies predominantly include males (some exclusively), and the influence of gender differences on neuroimaging outcomes remains insufficiently elucidated. Furthermore, sample sizes are generally small (most n < 50), with inadequate cross-cultural, multi-center data integration, potentially limiting the generalizability of conclusions. Secondly, methodological heterogeneity exists: rs-fMRI studies are overrepresented (14 studies), while tb-fMRI (only 2 studies) and DTI studies (only 2 studies) are limited. Additionally, the analytical methods for FC (e.g., seed-based connectivity, independent component analysis) and diagnostic criteria applied (e.g., differences in specific DSM-5/ICD-11 item usage) vary across studies, hindering result comparability. Moreover, existing research is predominantly cross-sectional, making it difficult to reveal the dynamic evolution of IGD-related neural abnormalities against the backdrop of adolescent brain developmental plasticity. Insufficient control for comorbidities (e.g., anxiety, depression) may also confound the specificity of the identified neural mechanisms.

Furthermore, this study has the limitation of not assessing publication bias. As systematic reviews tend to preferentially include studies that report positive or statistically significant results, research that remains unpublished or reports non-significant findings may be overlooked. This bias could lead to an overestimation of the strength of the association between fMRI neural abnormalities and IGD. Although we have made every effort to conduct a comprehensive literature search, future studies could provide a more complete evidence map of the field by formally assessing publication bias through methods such as searching grey literature, constructing funnel plots, or performing statistical tests (e.g., Egger's test), thereby further enhancing the reliability of the conclusions.

Future research should focus on the following directions: First, expanding sample diversity by including females and adolescents from diverse cultural backgrounds, combined with longitudinal designs, to dynamically observe bidirectional interactions between brain function/structure changes and the IGD disease course. Second, strengthening multimodal data integration (e.g., combining fMRI, DTI, and metabolomics) to parse cross-scale associations between neural circuit abnormalities and neurotransmitter systems (e.g., dopamine, serotonin). Third, developing fMRI-based biomarker combinations for IGD (e.g., PFC-striatal connection strength, white matter integrity indices), promoting their combined use with behavioral scales for precise clinical diagnosis. Fourth, designing targeted interventions for abnormal brain networks (e.g., reward system hyperactivation, executive control network dysfunction), utilizing fMRI to assess in real-time the neuromodulatory effects of interventions like TMS and CBT on neural circuits, thereby establishing a closed-loop translation from “mechanism elucidation to intervention development to efficacy evaluation”. Furthermore, cross-cultural comparative studies need strengthening, focusing on the long-term impact of new gaming formats in the digital era on adolescent neuroplasticity to provide a prospective basis for prevention and intervention.

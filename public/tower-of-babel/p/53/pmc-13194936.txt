LIF signaling pathway regulates the heterogeneous Sox2 transcriptional dynamics in mESCs
Authors: Gaochen Jin, Emilia A Leyes Porello, Jingchao Zhang, J Andres Vidal, Bomyi Lim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe LIF signaling pathway is crucial for maintaining self-renewal and pluripotency in mESCs through regulation of factors like Sox2, yet its direct impact on Sox2 transcriptional dynamics remains elusive. Using PP7/PCP-mediated live imaging, we analyzed the transcriptional dynamics of Sox2 under LIF pathway perturbation at single-cell resolution. Removing LIF ligand or adding JAK inhibitor reduced the number of Sox2-active cells, while remaining Sox2-active cells exhibited decreased mRNA production. This reduction is characterized by smaller and less frequent transcriptional bursts, resulting in shorter active duration. Notably, LIF pathway perturbation decreased the number of pluripotent cells, with pluripotent marker-expressing cells showing higher Sox2 expression, suggesting a strong correlation between Sox2 levels and pluripotency maintenance. Additionally, Sox2 expression demonstrated transcriptional inheritance across cell cycles, with Sox2-active mother cells more likely to reactivate Sox2 post-mitosis – a memory mechanism maintained even during LIF pathway perturbation. Our findings reveal quantitative aspects of Sox2 regulation in pluripotency maintenance.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-46330-2.Subject terms: Transcription, Embryonic stem cells

Introduction

Mouse embryonic stem cells (mESCs), derived from the inner cell mass of mouse blastocysts, represent a powerful system to investigate mammalian biology, gene regulation, disease pathogenesis, and drug development1, 2. Their significance stems from their key distinct properties: pluripotency, the ability to differentiate into any cell types derived from three germ layers3, 4 and self-renewal, enabling indefinite cell division while preserving pluripotency5. The pluripotent state of mESCs is governed by an interconnected pluripotency gene regulatory network (PGRN), centered on Sex-determining region Y-box 2 (Sox2), octamer-binding transcription factor 4 (Oct4), and Nanog6. These transcription factors (TFs) must be tightly controlled within narrow ranges to prevent differentiation6, 7. For example, Sox2 depletion leads to trophectoderm-like differentiation8, while Sox2 overexpression shifts cells from an ES-like morphology to a mixed or differentiated state with decreased expression of ES cell-associated genes (Nanog, Fgf4 and Utf1) and increased lineage markers9. These findings highlight the importance of precise gene regulation in mESCs to maintain pluripotency.

The self-renewal and pluripotency of mESCs are governed by both extracellular signaling pathways and intracellular transcription factors10. Among the key extracellular signaling, the leukemia inhibitory factor (LIF) signaling pathway plays a central role in maintaining mESC properties by activating multiple pluripotency genes10, 11, 12, 13. Key LIF target genes include Krüppel-like factor 4 (Klf4) and Sox2, TFs essential for sustaining pluripotency and guiding lineage-specific differentiation of ESCs14, 15. In mESCs, the LIF ligand mediates three signaling pathways: JAK/STAT3, PI3K/AKT, and SHP2/Grb2/MAPK16 (Fig. 1D). The JAK/STAT3 signaling pathway directly activates Klf4, which binds to the Sox2 Control Region (SCR), a Sox2 distal enhancer cluster, activating Sox2 expression17. While the JAK/STAT3 pathway primarily drives Sox2 activation via Klf4, other LIF pathways contribute to Sox2 regulation through distinct mechanisms16. The PI3K/AKT pathway activates Tbx3, a T-box transcription factor family involved in pluripotency maintenance18. Tbx3 regulates Sox2 by binding to the regulatory region of Nanog, which in turn targets the Sox2 enhancer and activates the gene18, 19. Conversely, the SHP2/Grb2/MAPK pathway can downregulate Sox2 by suppressing Tbx3 activity16, 20.

While Klf4 is directly controlled by LIF signaling, its role in pluripotency can be partially compensated by functional redundancy with other Klf family members such as Klf2 and Klf521, 22. In contrast, Sox2 is an essential member of the core pluripotency transcription factor network, working synergistically with Oct4 and Nanog to maintain the pluripotent state6, 8. Moreover, Sox2 expression integrates signals from multiple LIF-mediated signaling pathways through Klf4, Tbx3, and other factors, positioning it as a critical node within the broader pluripotency network16. Unlike Klf4, Sox2 plays a distinctive and indispensable role in mESCs, as evidenced by the fact that both depletion and overexpression of Sox2 lead to differentiation, highlighting the critical importance of its precise regulation8, 9. Additionally, since Sox2 operates downstream of multiple LIF pathway branches, analyzing its activity provides an ideal readout to capture the integrated effects of LIF signaling on the pluripotency network. Despite extensive research highlighting the intricate regulation of Sox2, the quantitative effect of LIF pathway modulation on Sox2 transcriptional dynamics at the single-cell level remains poorly characterized.

Observed across various species, transcription is widely recognized as a stochastic process, producing nascent transcripts as intermittent bursts rather than continuously23, 24, 25, 26, 27. This stochastic nature is especially interesting for pluripotency genes like Sox2, whose expression must be maintained within precise boundaries to prevent differentiation. However, the tolerable range of fluctuations in transcriptional activity for maintaining pluripotency versus inducing differentiation remains unclear. Moreover, most studies have focused on averaged behaviors across cell populations, overlooking the responses of individual cells8, 9, 16. Indeed, while the regulatory relationship between signaling pathways and downstream pluripotency genes has been widely studied, quantitative analysis of how these pathways affect gene regulation dynamics in individual cells, giving rise to heterogeneous expression, is largely lacking28. We employ live imaging to track transcriptional activity in single cells, focusing on: How do individual cells react to perturbation in the LIF signaling pathway? In what way do the transcriptional dynamics of pluripotency genes correlate with the pluripotent state in single cells?

We employed a PP7/PCP-based live imaging system that allows real-time monitoring of Sox2 transcriptional dynamics at single-cell resolution. This system enabled us to characterize the transcriptional dynamics of Sox2 under LIF signaling perturbation and examine its impact on maintenance of mESC pluripotency. The number of Sox2-active cells decreased upon LIF pathway perturbation, and the remaining Sox2-active cells showed significantly reduced mRNA production. This change was due to altered transcriptional bursting frequency and size, resulting in high cell-to-cell heterogeneity. We found that high Sox2 levels were essential for mESCs to maintain their pluripotent state. When Sox2 expression decreased following LIF pathway perturbation, cells lost key pluripotency-associated traits and began transitioning toward differentiation. Additionally, our live imaging revealed a transcriptional memory, where cells with active Sox2 expression had a higher chance of initiating Sox2 transcription in daughter cells post-mitosis, regardless of LIF signaling perturbation. Our work provides a quantitative characterization of how perturbation in the LIF signaling pathway contributes to heterogeneity in Sox2 transcriptional dynamics that correlate with pluripotency state in individual cells.

Results

PP7/PCP live imaging system allows the visualization of Sox2 nascent transcripts in living mESCs

To examine how LIF signaling pathway perturbation quantitatively affects the transcriptional dynamics of Sox2 at single-cell resolution, we implemented a PP7/PCP-mediated live imaging approach that allows visualization of nascent transcripts in living mESCs. The MS2- and PP7-based live has been widely applied to study transcriptional dynamics across different biological systems, including Drosophila, mESCs, and human models29, 30, 31, 32. The system functions by incorporating MS2 or PP7 stem-loop sequences into a target gene’s UTRs or introns. During transcription, fluorescently labeled coat proteins (MCP-FP or PCP-FP) bind to these stem-loops, allowing single-cell resolution detection of nascent transcripts in living cells. Using fluorescent in situ hybridization (FISH), previous studies have demonstrated that the insertion of MS2 or PP7 stem-loops minimally perturb endogenous gene transcription, translation, and mRNA stability, while showing a strong correlation between tagged nascent transcripts and endogenous mRNA expression29, 33. In mESCs, several studies using MS2 or PP7 tags on endogenous genes have validated this system as a reliable method for measuring transcriptional dynamics27, 31.

Here, we inserted 24 copies of the bacteriophage PP7 sequence into the 3’UTR of the endogenous Sox2 locus using CRISPR/Cas9-mediated genome editing (Fig. 1A). The piggybac vector expressing PP7 coat protein (PCP) fused with superfolder GFP (sfGFP) was randomly inserted into the genome of Sox2-PP7 mESCs. To validate that PP7 insertion does not perturb Sox2 expression or cellular pluripotency, we performed Western blot analysis comparing Sox2 protein levels between wildtype and Sox2-PP7 knock-in mESCs. Wildtype and knock-in cells expressed comparable levels of Sox2 protein, demonstrating that PP7 stem-loop insertion does not significantly affect Sox2 expression or translation (Fig. S1). We further assessed pluripotency by measuring expression of common pluripotency markers (Sox2, Nanog, Oct4, and Esrrb) using qPCR with TBP as a reference gene (Fig. S2). All pluripotency markers showed high expression levels that were comparable between wildtype and knock-in cells, confirming that the Sox2-PP7 knock-in line maintains pluripotency.

Upon active transcription of Sox2-PP7, PP7 sequences form stem loops, where PCP-sfGFP can bind with high affinity, resulting in a bright punctum per allele29 (Fig. 1B,C). Although cells exhibited varying levels of diffused background sfGFP intensity, representing unbound PCP-sfGFP molecules, transcriptional puncta in individual cells (PCP-bound PP7 stem loops) remained readily identifiable (Fig. S3A). We expect that PCP-sfGFP molecules are present in excess and hence PCP-sfGFP availability does not limit the detection of PP7-tagged transcriptional signals. To ensure that the fluorescent puncta indicate nascent transcripts, a negative control experiment was performed using Sox2-PP7 mESCs transfected with a plasmid carrying the MS2 coat protein (MCP) fused with sfGFP. As expected, cells transfected with MCP-sfGFP did not exhibit any puncta, indicating that the bright puncta observed in PCP-sfGFP transfected Sox2-PP7 cells represent Sox2 transcripts (Fig. 1B). This assay enabled us to capture nascent transcripts of Sox2 in living mESCs at single-cell resolution.

Perturbation of the LIF pathway decreases the fraction of Sox2-active cells and increases heterogeneity in cell populations

We first perturbed the LIF signaling pathway by removing the LIF ligand from the culture media. Cells were imaged in a sequential manner at 5, 24, 48, 72, and 96 h after the removal of LIF ligand. Instead of a complete shutdown of Sox2 transcription, only a fraction of cells was affected upon LIF pathway perturbation. When we quantified the fraction of cells with active Sox2 expression, referred to as Sox2-active cells, a significant decrease was observed (~ 42% reduction) 72 h after removing LIF from the media (Figs. 1E and S3B). This reduction became even more pronounced at 96 h post-LIF removal, showing a five-fold decrease compared to the fraction of transcriptionally active cells 5 h after LIF removal (Figs. 1E and S3A). In contrast, the fraction of Sox2-active cells cultured in LIF media (Sox2-PP7; PCP-sfGFP) remained consistent across the five time points (Figs. 1E and S3B).

Among the three characterized LIF-mediated signaling pathways, the activation of JAK/STAT3 pathway alone has been shown to be sufficient to maintain self-renewal in mESCs (Fig. 1D)13, 34. As such, we analyzed the effects of LIF/JAK/STAT3 signaling pathways on Sox2 transcriptional dynamics. We cultured the cells in media with 10 µM JAK inhibitor I (JAKi), a potent, cell-permeable, ATP-competitive inhibitor of JAK135. The addition of JAKi yielded results similar to the LIF removal experiments. The fraction of cells expressing Sox2 significantly decreased (~ 45% reduction) 48 h after the addition of JAKi, and this decreasing trend continued progressively over time (Figs. 1C,E, and S3B). Notably, there was a time lag of 24 h between the LIF removal and JAK inhibitor addition methods (Fig. 1E). Since JAKi are small molecules that rapidly enter cells and bind the target with high efficiency, this could lead to a faster effect on Sox236, 37. In addition, LIF ligand, which is previously bound to its receptor before removal, requires time to degrade after its removal, thus delaying the response to this perturbation. Furthermore, the secretion of endogenous LIF by mESCs leads to incomplete removal of LIF from the system, contributing to the slower effect on the LIF removal experiment38. Taken together, both perturbations of the LIF pathway resulted in a decrease in the number of Sox2-active cells. Rather than homogeneously decreasing Sox2 gene expression across the entire population, we observed a gradual and stochastic silencing of Sox2 in individual cells over time. This cell-to-cell variability in response reflects differences in individual cells’ sensitivities to LIF signaling perturbation, demonstrating the importance of single-cell analysis for understanding the relationship between signaling pathways and pluripotency gene regulation.

Perturbation in LIF pathway leads to reduced mRNA production in Sox2-active cells

We investigated whether the Sox2-active cells under LIF pathway perturbation maintained the same transcriptional activity as non-perturbed cells or showed changes in transcriptional behaviors. To examine the changes in transcriptional kinetics, we performed a 5-h time-lapse imaging for each condition. When we plot all transcriptional trajectories from individual nuclei as a heatmap, Sox2-active cells under LIF pathway perturbation displayed a general reduction in transcriptional activity compared to those in non-perturbed condition (Fig. 2A). We compared the total mRNA produced in 5 h for each Sox2-active cell in perturbed and non-perturbed conditions (Fig. 2C). mRNA production is quantified as the sum of integrated fluorescence intensities from all Sox2 transcriptional puncta over time (either one or two per cell, depending on monoallelic or biallelic expression), serving as a proxy for cumulative number of nascent transcripts per cell (Fig. 2B)39, 40. The mRNA production per cell significantly decreased by approximately 50% at 48 h after the removal of LIF and 24 h after the addition of the JAKi, compared to a relatively consistent Sox2 mRNA production in non-perturbed cells (Fig. 2C). The observed 24 h delayed response between the LIF removal and JAKi addition experiments aligns with our analysis on the overall fraction of Sox2-active cells upon LIF pathway perturbation. Interestingly, the fraction of cells exhibiting biallelic Sox2 expression also declined for both LIF removal and JAKi addition treatment, suggesting individual cells are less likely to maintain simultaneous activation of both alleles following perturbation (Fig. S4B). This reduction in biallelic expression further contributes to the overall decrease in Sox2 mRNA production. A previous study using qPCR showed a 50% reduction in Sox2 expression four days after LIF withdrawal, but this averaged data across the population didn’t reveal individual cell behavior16. Our single-cell analysis addresses this limitation and indicates that perturbation to the LIF-signaling pathway results in not only a decrease in the number of cells expressing Sox2, but also a reduced mRNA production in Sox2-active cells, contributing to the overall decrease in Sox2 expression across the cell population.

Reduced Sox2 mRNA level is a result of decreased size and frequency of transcriptional bursting

Single-cell live imaging revealed cell-to-cell variability in transcriptional activity among the Sox2-active cells, regardless of LIF pathway perturbation (Fig. S4A). Given the highly heterogeneous gene expression dynamics observed in individual cells, we next performed a stochastic analysis on individual transcriptional trajectories to investigate the underlying causes of the low mRNA production following disruptions in the LIF signaling pathway. Transcription occurs in a stochastic and discontinuous manner, giving rise to intermittent “on” and “off” phases of transcriptional activity, commonly referred to as transcriptional bursting23, 24, 26 (Fig. 2B,D). From each transcriptional trajectory, we quantified (1) bursting frequency: the number of bursts per cell within the imaging period, (2) burst size: integrated area under the transcriptional trajectory for each burst, and (3) burst amplitude: the peak intensity of each burst (see Methods for details). Cells exhibited a significantly reduced bursting frequency 48 and 72 h post-LIF pathway perturbation, while the number of bursts in non-perturbed cells remained consistent over the same period (Fig. 2E). Additionally, both burst size and burst amplitude decreased 24 h after perturbation (Figs. 2F and S4C). The properties of transcriptional bursting are modulated by various factors, including the degree of enhancer-promoter interactions, RNA polymerase II (Pol II) loading rates, TF-DNA interactions, and chromatin modifications26, 41. We hypothesized that disruptions in the LIF signaling pathway may reduce the recruitment of TFs like Klf4 and Tbx3 to the Sox2 regulatory regions, thereby weakening enhancer-promoter interactions and decreasing Pol II recruitment. These molecular changes would explain the observed changes in Sox2 bursting properties. Consequently, the reduced bursting frequency and size led to a shorter duration of Sox2 activation. Non-perturbed cells exhibited consistently active transcription duration across each 5-h imaging session, whereas LIF pathway perturbation led to a noticeable reduction in transcription duration (Fig. 2G). Taken together, our data suggest that perturbation of the LIF signaling pathway affects the transcriptional dynamics of Sox2 by altering burst frequency and size, which leads to a reduced duration of active transcription.

Loss of pluripotency-associated traits under LIF pathway perturbations correlates with a reduction in Sox2 level

Given the integral role of Sox2 in PGRN, we investigated how pluripotency of mESCs is influenced by the LIF-mediated modulation of Sox2. We performed live-cell immunostaining for stage-specific embryonic antigen 1 (SSEA1), a cell surface marker of pluripotency, to examine the pluripotent state of mESCs under varied Sox2 expressions. In mESCs, a high level of SSEA1 expression is indicative of pluripotency, with differentiation leading to a decrease in SSEA1 levels42, 43. We used the SSEA1 marker conjugated to fluorochrome NL557, which emits red fluorescence, to indicate pluripotency in live mESCs (Figs. 3A and S5A). Under the non-perturbed condition, more than 90% of cells, regardless of Sox2 activity, showed pluripotency, as indicated by positive SSEA1 signals (Fig. S5A, B). However, the percentage of SSEA1-positive cells decreased by more than 45% 48 h after the removal of LIF or the addition of JAKi, and by more than 75% 72 h after treatments (Fig. S5A, S5B). This trend indicates that LIF signaling plays a critical role in sustaining pluripotency, with its perturbation leading to a substantial shift in cell identity.

We next analyzed the correlation between Sox2 activity and the pluripotency state. Our previous results established that LIF pathway perturbation reduces both the fraction of Sox2-active cells and the transcriptional output in cells that remain Sox2-active. However, it remained unclear whether these reduced Sox2 expression levels were sufficient to maintain pluripotency. To address this question, we specifically examined the pluripotent state of cells that remained Sox2-active despite LIF pathway perturbation, using the SSEA1 as a pluripotency marker (Fig. 3A). Over 50% of cells lost pluripotent traits 48 h after perturbation even if they remained Sox2-active, compared to no loss in pluripotency in the control LIF( +)/DMSO( +) condition (Fig. 3B). This finding suggests that the reduced Sox2 transcriptional output we previously observed falls below the threshold required to maintain pluripotency in many cells. We then asked if Sox2-active cells had a higher chance of maintaining pluripotency compared to Sox2-inactive cells. We quantified the difference in the fraction of SSEA1-positive cells between Sox2-active and Sox2-inactive populations, such that positive values indicate enrichment of SSEA1-positive cells in the Sox2-active population (Fig. 3C). We found that a significantly higher fraction of Sox2-active cells retained SSEA1 expression compared to Sox2-inactive cells, suggesting that Sox2 transcription in cells helps preserve pluripotent traits under LIF pathway perturbation.

Interestingly, the effect of removing LIF was less pronounced compared to adding JAKi (Fig. 3C). Unlike JAKi addition, which specifically inhibits the JAK/STAT3 pathway, LIF removal simultaneously disrupts multiple downstream signaling branches, including both the JAK/STAT3 and GRB2/MAPK pathways16. The less pronounced effect likely arises from the involvement of the GRB2/MAPK signaling pathway, which acts as a positive regulator of differentiation and a negative regulator of pluripotency13, 36. The removal of LIF releases the inhibition of Tbx3, a factor known to activate Oct4, potentially resulting in higher Oct4 expression in the absence of LIF compared to the JAKi treatment6, 44, 45. Therefore, upregulation of Oct4 could partially compensate for reduced Sox2 levels, helping to sustain pluripotent traits in some cells despite LIF removal.

To further investigate whether differences in Sox2 transcription activity contribute to the observed loss of pluripotency traits, we compared the Sox2 mRNA production in cells with or without the expression of SSEA1. In non-perturbed conditions, Sox2 mRNA production was similar between SSEA1-positive and SSEA1-negative cells (Fig. 3D). This indicates that the small fraction of cells losing pluripotency in non-perturbed conditions is not due to changes in Sox2 levels, but likely due to the natural heterogeneity of the pluripotent stem cell population. However, 72 h after JAKi addition, SSEA1-negative cells exhibited lower mRNA production than SSEA1-positive cells, reinforcing the correlation between Sox2 expression and the maintenance of pluripotent traits (Fig. 3E). This result supports the idea that not only the presence but the magnitude of Sox2 transcriptional output is critical for maintaining pluripotent identity. Sox2 levels were comparable between SSEA1-positive and negative cells under LIF removal condition, likely because Oct4 upregulation reduces the impact of Sox2 reduction (Fig. S5C). In summary, Sox2 functions as a threshold-dependent regulator of pluripotency, and maintaining high levels of Sox2 expression is essential for preserving the pluripotent state in mESCs. This underscores the complex regulatory mechanisms controlling pluripotency, where Sox2, in concert with other factors, determines the developmental fate of stem cells.

Transcription memory facilitates Sox2 expression maintenance in mESCs

Previous studies have demonstrated that elevated Sox2 levels reduce cell division rates, suggesting a complex relationship between Sox2 expression and the cell cycle9. This raises important questions about the stability of Sox2 expression during and after cell division, particularly when the LIF signaling pathway is perturbed. Specifically, it remains unclear whether Sox2-active cells can maintain Sox2 expression in daughter cells upon mitosis under these conditions. To answer this question, we analyzed Sox2 transcriptional dynamics before and after mitosis across all conditions from 0 to 72 h after each treatment. We first categorized the cells into two groups: Sox2-active cells before mitosis and Sox2-inactive cells before mitosis (Fig. 4A). We note that 'Sox2-inactive cells before mitosis’ doesn’t necessarily mean Sox2 is completely absent—only that no detectable nascent transcripts were produced before division. Within each group, we tracked the daughter cell expression patterns following mitosis. Among Sox2-active mother cells, we observed a strong tendency for transcriptional reactivation, with over 80% showing Sox2 expression in at least one daughter cell (Fig. 4B). On the contrary, 90% of Sox2-inactive mother cells produced daughter cells with no detectable Sox2 signals (Fig. 4B). This phenomenon, which is referred to as transcriptional memory, represents the ability of cells to transmit their transcriptional competency state to daughter cells across cell divisions46. Interestingly, this transcription memory persisted independent of LIF signaling modulations, suggesting that Sox2 transcriptional competency is inherited despite disruptions in the Sox2 expression level (Fig. S6C).

To further characterize the observed transcription memory, we tracked Sox2 transcription in individual mother-daughter cell lineages (Figs. 4C,D and S6A, B). Both mother cells and daughter cells exhibited stochastic and discontinuous Sox2 expression regardless of LIF pathway perturbation, indicating that transcriptional bursting occurs across both short (within a few hours) and long (spanning cell cycles) time scales46 (Figs. 4D and S6B). We found that daughter cells typically exhibited lower Sox2 expression than their mother cells in both non-perturbed and perturbed (LIF-removal and JAKi-addition) conditions (Figs. 4E and S6D–F). This reduction could be explained by the low Sox2 level during mitotic exit47 (Fig. 4C,F). Our data demonstrate that Sox2 expression can be transcriptionally inherited from mother cells, regardless of the LIF signaling perturbation. We note that while Sox2 transcriptional inheritance does not rely on LIF signaling pathway, both the overall expression level and stability of Sox2 are still significantly influenced by LIF. This indicates that LIF plays an important role in maintaining Sox2 expression, but the fundamental mechanism enabling transcriptional memory across cell divisions operates autonomously.

Discussion

It has been established that transcription of many genes, including Sox2, occurs in bursts, and such stochastic transcriptional dynamics have profound implications for the regulation of pluripotency in mESCs26, 27. Yet, much remains to be understood about the extent of transcriptional fluctuations that can be tolerated to maintain pluripotency or the range that induces cell differentiation. In this study, we use the PCP/PP7-based single-cell live imaging to elucidate how LIF signaling modulations affect downstream Sox2 transcriptional dynamics, and the subsequent impact on pluripotency maintenance. We demonstrated that LIF pathway disruption leads to both a reduction in the number of Sox2-active cells and altered transcriptional dynamics in remaining Sox2-active cells, with direct consequences for pluripotency maintenance.

LIF signaling perturbation alters Sox2 transcriptional dynamics by decreasing the frequency and size of transcriptional bursts, suggesting a mechanistic link between external signaling cues and the fundamental stochastic properties of gene expression. LIF signals to the transcriptional machinery and its regulatory elements, such as promoters and enhancers, to sustain the high transcriptional output necessary for maintaining pluripotency. For example, the SCR, a strong distal enhancer of Sox2, contains several TF binding sites, including two Klf4 sites17. Given that Klf4 is a direct target of the LIF/STAT3 signaling pathway, further research is needed to explore the correlation between Klf4 protein binding and Sox2 transcription. Combining our live imaging technique with protein tagging approaches would offer a more comprehensive understanding of how pluripotency genes, along with other factors—including extrinsic signaling pathways and intrinsic regulatory elements (e.g., promoters and enhancers)—orchestrate the transcriptional dynamics and the maintenance of pluripotency.

Our findings reveal that LIF pathway perturbation leads to a significant decrease in the fraction of cells expressing the pluripotent marker SSEA1, indicating a potential reduction in pluripotency-associated traits. Interestingly, cells expressing Sox2 are more likely to maintain pluripotency-associated traits under LIF pathway perturbation, emphasizing the heterogeneous sensitivity to LIF activity. Furthermore, our analysis revealed that SSEA1-positive cells exhibited higher Sox2 expression levels than SSEA1-negative cells. This suggests that Sox2 may function in a threshold-dependent manner, where its transcriptional output must exceed a critical level to sustain pluripotency. Once Sox2 levels fall below the threshold, cells are more likely to lose their pluripotency-associated traits and differentiate. This behavior suggests a tightly regulated gene expression system that affects cell fate decisions in response to signaling changes.

We note that perturbations in the LIF pathway affect multiple genes beyond Sox2, including Klf4, Oct4, and Nanog6, 16. This suggests that the observed effects on pluripotency may result from the combined impact of altered transcriptional dynamics across several genes. Indeed, our results show that the effect on pluripotency was less pronounced for the LIF removal experiment compared to the more specific JAKi addition experiment, possibly due to the indirect effects on Oct4 upon LIF removal16. This highlights the complex interplay within the pluripotency network, where compensatory mechanisms can buffer against perturbations in individual signaling pathways. Additional studies using approaches like single-cell RNA-seq or multiplexed monitoring of several genes will be necessary to uncover the combinatorial contributions of pluripotency genes on pluripotency maintenance.

We also reveal that Sox2 expression is transcriptionally inherited across cell cycles, with daughter cells generally showing lower expression levels than mother cells. A previous study demonstrated that many genes are expressed at a low-level during mitosis, rather than being silenced47. While enhancer accessibility is lost during mitosis, promoter accessibility is largely maintained, allowing the promoter and its target gene to form mitotic expression units that preserve transcription patterns at a low level48, 49. Our results demonstrate that Sox2 expression follows this mitotic regulation pattern: transcription is active at a low level during mitosis, followed by a gradual increase in expression. Notably, this transcriptional inheritance persists despite LIF signaling perturbations (Fig. 4F). We propose that while LIF plays a key role in maintaining Sox2 expression levels, the fundamental mechanism of Sox2 transcriptional inheritance operates independently of external signaling, reflecting stable intrinsic properties of promoters. This suggests a regulatory layer serving as transcriptional memory to preserve lineage identity through cell divisions even under adverse conditions.

In conclusion, our study underscores the impact of LIF signaling on the downstream Sox2 transcriptional dynamics and pluripotency maintenance in mESCs. Utilizing single-cell resolution live-cell imaging, we reveal significant heterogeneity in Sox2 expression following disruptions in the LIF pathway, with Sox2 bursting dynamics playing a critical role in pluripotency maintenance. The persistent transcriptional memory despite signaling perturbations suggests how stem cells may maintain cellular identity during fluctuating signaling environments. While further investigations incorporating multiplexed gene analysis will be essential to fully elucidate the complex interactions among pluripotency regulators, our findings emphasize the vital role of transcriptional regulation in mESC pluripotency, setting the stage for the development of therapeutic approaches that can precisely manipulate stem cell fate.

Methods

Cell culture and treatments

All mESC lines were cultured in LIF/serum medium with DMEM (Gibco, 11995065), supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (Hyclone, SH30071.03), 1 mM MEM nonessential amino acids (Invitrogen, 11140035), 0.1 mM β-mercaptoethanol (Gibco, 21985023), 1000 U/ml LIF(Millipore, ESG1107), and 100 U/ml Penicillin–Streptomycin (Gibco, 15140122) on 0.1% gelatin-coated plates in a humidified incubator at 37ºC and 5% CO2. To perturb LIF-related signaling pathways, mESCs were either treated with 10 μM JAK inhibitor I (Carbiochem, 457081037) or with LIF-depleted medium.

Cell line generation

To tag endogenous Sox2 with PP7, we generated a donor vector containing a 24 × PP7 sequence and a puromycin selection cassette, flanked by the homology arm sequences corresponding to ~ 500 bp upstream and downstream of the guide RNA target site. The DTA negative selection sequence was inserted downstream of the right homology arm. The gRNA was designed using CRISPick with Mouse GRCm38, CRISPRko and SpyoCas950. The PX458 plasmid backbone was used to insert the oligos containing the gRNA sequence. Both the donor and the gRNA-containing vectors were purified using the NucleoBond Xtra midiprep kit (Takara, 740410.50) for further transfection. The ePB-Ubc-PCP-sfGFP-Hygro plasmid was generated by substituting the MCP sequence to the PCP sequence in the ePB-Ubc-MCP-sfGFP-HygroR plasmid, kindly provided by the Zaret Lab at Penn Medicine.

Wildtype E14 cells were transfected with 2 μg of the gRNA vector and 1 μg of the donor vector using opti-MEM (Gibco, 31985070) and Lipofectamine 3000 Transfection Reagent (ThermoFisher, L3000001). After two days, transfected cells were subjected to puromycin antibiotics selection for 7 days. Transfected cells that went through antibody selection were replated at a diluted concentration to pick individual colonies. Genomic DNA was extracted from each colony and genotyped to validate the correct insertion of the target sequence in the designated location. Clones with bi-allelic integrations were co-transfected with the ePB-Ubc-PCP-sfGFP-HygroR plasmid and 1 μg PiggyBac enzyme PBase using opti-MEM (Gibco, 31985070) and Lipofectamine 3000. Two days following transfection, cells were treated with 100 μg/ml Hygromycin B (Corning, 30240CR) for 14 days. Individual colonies were picked after hygromycin selection and screened under a confocal microscope. The colony exhibiting the highest signal-to-noise ratio of PP7 signal compared to the PCP background level was chosen for further experiments.

Live imaging

All images were acquired using a Zeiss LSM800 confocal laser scanning microscope with a Plan-Apochromat 40 × 1.3 NA oil immersion objective. The 488 nm laser was used to visualize PCP-sfGFP (Sox2 transcriptional activity), and the 405 nm laser was used to visualize nuclei. For calculating the fraction of cells with Sox2 signal and the pluripotency test, snapshots were taken at six random positions for three experimental replicates. For transcriptional activity analysis, images were taken every 5 min for 5 h at six random positions. Maximum intensity projections of 40 z-stacks with 0.60 μm spacing was used for analysis. Each single 512 × 512-pixel image was captured in 16-bit format. Laser settings were maintained constant across all experiments. For perturbation related to LIF-signaling, cells were either treated with 10 μM JAK inhibitor I (Carbiochem, 457081037) or with LIF-depleted medium. The time when each treatment was induced was defined as time 0 and the cells were imaged at specific time points in a sequential manner (5 h, 24 h, 48 h and 72 h after the treatments). Each time-lapse movie spanned a duration of 5 h.

To visualize the pluripotency state of mESCs in living cells, GIoLIVE mouse live cell antibody targeting the SSEA1 was used (Bio-Techne, NLLC2155R). SSEA1 antibody was conjugated to fluorochrome NL557. The antibody was diluted 1:50 to a final concentration of 1X in cell culture media. A staining volume of 2 mL was added to one well in a 12-well plate. After 30 min of incubation, the staining media was replaced with fresh media without the antibody. Then cells were used directly for imaging. Pluripotent stem cells exhibit the fluorescent signal of SSEA1 while early differentiated cells do not fluoresce.

Image analysis

The acquired images were concatenated and subjected to maximum projection using Fiji ImageJ. Maximum intensity projection images were utilized for cell segmentation and the tracking of nascent transcript intensities. The code for cell segmentation and signal tracking was developed using MATLAB. Object segmentation was performed on each frame of the time series and produced centroid indices and pixel lists associated with each object (cell) in the image. To track objects across time frames and correctly assign cell identities over the time course, a key criterion was applied: the object areas between the two consecutive frames must overlap by > 60%. Once segmented objects were assigned proper cell identities across frames, the PP7 signals were extracted in each cell and frame and tracked over time.

PP7 signal identification

PP7 signal identification and tracking were performed following an algorithm similar to that used for cell segmentation but instead of applying an area overlap criterion, signal identity was assigned based on a centroid distance threshold. A scalar threshold of 7 pixels was applied such that signals in consecutive frames with centroid distances < 7 were assigned the same identity. For signal quantification, we accounted for variability in diffused sfGFP background by subtracting the mean nuclear fluorescence intensity, representing unbound PCP-sfGFP, from the intensity of each detected transcriptional punctum. A consistent intensity threshold was applied across all conditions. To validate our quantification method, we manually inspected tracked signals and confirmed the consistency of automated detection and background correction. Only puncta that persisted for more than two consecutive frames were considered as valid transcriptional events.

Data smoothing and transcriptional activity calculation

Following the cell identification and signal quantification, the trajectory data were smoothed using the “smoothdata()” function by “rlowess” method over intervals of 5 frames. To identify active transcription events, we applied a fluorescence intensity threshold of 2500 a.u. to background-subtracted transcription trajectories. This threshold was empirically determined by examining intensity distributions in Sox2-inactive cells across multiple time points and conditions. Sensitivity analysis confirmed that varying the threshold within a reasonable range did not significantly affect the results. Cells were classified as Sox2-active if they exhibited transcriptional puncta exceeding this threshold at any point during the imaging window.

Quantification of mRNA production and bursting activity

For each individual cell, mRNA production was quantified by integrating the area under the transcriptional trajectory using the trapezoid rule. For cells exhibiting two transcriptional puncta, total mRNA production was calculated by summing the integrated fluorescence intensities of both puncta. To account for replicate-to-replicate variability in absolute fluorescence intensity, mRNA production values were normalized to the mean mRNA production at the 5-h time point within each condition (LIF( +), LIF(−), and JAKi( +)). For each replicate, all time points (5, 24, 48, and 72 h) within a given condition were imaged from the same well, ensuring consistent biological and technical context across the time series.

To quantify transcriptional bursting kinetics in Sox2-active cells, bursts were identified by detecting local maxima and minima in background-subtracted fluorescence trajectories using MATLAB’s ‘findpeaks’ function. A burst was defined when the fluorescence intensity difference between a local maximum and its two adjacent minima exceeded a defined threshold. This threshold was empirically determined to clearly distinguish between active (“on”) and inactive (“off”) transcriptional states. Bursting frequency was calculated as the number of detected bursts per cell within the imaging period. Burst size was quantified as the integrated area under the transcription trajectory for each burst, and burst amplitude was measured as the maximum fluorescence intensity of each burst. Both burst size and amplitude were normalized to the value at the 5-h time point within each condition. The duration of Sox2-active expression was quantified as the total time during which fluorescence intensity exceeded the threshold for each cell.

Heatmap generation

Heatmaps shown in Fig. 2A were generated using custom MATLAB scripts. Each row represents a single cell, and the x-axis corresponds to imaging time in 5-min intervals over a 5-h window from the start of recording. For each condition, cells were sorted in ascending order based on total mRNA output, calculated by integrating the area under the fluorescence intensity trajectory. The sorted intensity matrices were plotted using MATLAB’s ‘imagesc’ function with the ‘hot’ colormap.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using MATLAB and Excel. An unpaired two-tailed Student’s t-test was used to determine statistical significance when comparing two independent groups. Unless otherwise specified, data represents the mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM) from three independent experiments.

Western blot

Frozen WT and Sox2-PP7 knock-in (~ 1.5 × 10^6) cells were thawed and whole cell lysates were extracted in RIPA buffer with an added protease inhibitor cocktail (Sigma-Aldrich, P8340). Cell lysates were reduced and boiled for 5 min using LDS sample loading buffer (Thermo Fisher, NP0008) and a sample reducing agent (Thermo Fisher, NP004). The samples were separated using SDS-PAGE (Thermo Fisher, NP0322BOX) and transferred to a nitrocellulose membrane (Thermo Fisher, IB301002). The nitrocellulose membrane was incubated for 1 h in a blocking buffer consisting of 5% dry milk in TBST. The membranes were incubated with primary antibodies overnight and washed three times with TBST before incubating with secondary antibodies for an hour. The western blot was visualized on an Amersham Imager 680. Sox2 protein was tagged using a primary rabbit polyclonal antibody (Abcam, ab97959) and a secondary HRP conjugated donkey anti-rabbit antibody (Jackson ImmunoResearch, 711-035-152). ɑ-tubulin protein was tagged using a primary mouse monoclonal antibody (Cell Signaling Technology, 3873) and a secondary HRP conjugated donkey anti-mouse antibody (Jackson ImmunoResearch, 715-035-150).

RT-qPCR

Frozen WT and Sox2-PP7 knock-in (~ 1.5 × 10^6) cells were thawed and RNA extraction was performed following the kit instructions from Quick-RNA MiniPrep (Zymo Research, R1054) . Reverse transcription was performed following the instruction from high-capacity cDNA reverse transcription kit (Applied Biosystems, 4368814). qPCR amplification was done using SsoAdvanced universal SYBR green supermix (Biorad, 1725270) and analyzed using the StepOnePlus system. The following primer sequences were used:

TBP fw: ggggagctgtgatgtgaagt.

TBP rv: ccaggaaataattctggctca.

Sox2 fw: gtgtttgcaaaaagggaaaagt.

Sox2 rv: tctttctcccagccctagtct.

Esrrb fw: cgattcatgaaatgcctcaa.

Esrrb rv: cctcctcgaactcggtca.

Oct4 fw: ccccaatgccgtgaagttg.

Oct4 rv: tcagcagcttggcaaactgtt.

Nanog fw: aggatgaagtgcaagcggtg.

Nanog rv: tgctgagcccttctgaatcag.

Pharmacokinetic variability with pembrolizumab dosage switching and its impact on immune-related adverse events in patients with non-small cell lung cancer

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPembrolizumab 400 mg every 6 weeks (Q6W) was approved based on population pharmacokinetic (pop-PK) modeling rather than pharmacokinetic measurements, leaving real-world concentrations insufficiently characterized. The impact of concentration changes when switching from 200 mg every 3 weeks (Q3W) to Q6W on immune-related adverse events (irAEs) remains unclear. This study aimed to evaluate the association between the change in pembrolizumab dosage and irAE occurrence.MethodsWe retrospectively identified patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer who switched pembrolizumab from Q3W to Q6W between March 2017 and September 2023. Serum concentrations were quantified; pop-PK analysis estimated maximum concentration (eCmax) and estimated trough exposure (eCtrough). Intercycle variability was expressed as percentage coefficient of variation (%CV) using all available 200 mg Q3W cycles. Early irAEs were defined as events within 126 days.Associations between pop-PK metrics and irAEs (including pneumonitis) after switching were explored. Clinical correlates of %CV-eCmax were also assessed.Results79 patients switched, and pop-PK analysis was feasible in 66 patients. The median age was 70 years, 50 were male, and 62 had a smoking history. The median Q3W cycles were 6, and the median follow-up period after switching was 463 days. After switching, 45 events occurred new or worsened irAEs, with 19 pneumonitis, and 22 had early irAEs, with 13 early pneumonitis.Among the 64 patients who received ≥2 cycles of Q3W, those who developed irAEs after switching showed higher %CV-eCmax during Q3W. Similar associations were observed for early irAEs, pneumonitis, and early pneumonitis.%CV-eCtrough during Q3W was not associated with irAEs after switching, with an association observed only for early pneumonitis.High %CV-eCmax during Q3W was associated with increased odds of irAEs, pneumonitis, early irAEs, and early pneumonitis. No significant associations were observed between %CV-eCtrough and irAEs or early irAEs.Higher platelet count and renal dysfunction were independently associated with increased %CV-eCmax.ConclusionsHigh intercycle eCmax variability during 200 mg Q3W is associated with an increased risk of new or worsened irAEs, particularly pneumonitis, after switching to 400 mg Q6W, suggesting that intercycle variation in Cmax may be an important safety consideration when switching to 400 mg Q6W.Keywords: Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor, Pneumonitis, Immune related adverse event - irAE, Lung Cancer, Pharmacokinetics - PK

WHAT IS ALREADY KNOWN ON THIS TOPIC

WHAT THIS STUDY ADDS

Using measured serum concentrations to inform a population pharmacokinetic model, this study shows that higher intercycle variability in estimated maximum concentration (eCmax) during pembrolizumab 200 mg Q3W (percentage coefficient of variation (%CV)-eCmax) is associated with subsequent new or worsened irAEs after switching to 400 mg Q6W, including pneumonitis, whereas estimated trough exposure was not associated with irAE risk. Higher platelet counts and reduced renal function were also associated with higher %CV-eCmax, suggesting that exposure variability may be partly driven by patient-specific factors.

Background

Pembrolizumab is a humanized immunoglobulin 4 monoclonal antibody that directly inhibits the binding of programmed cell death-1 (PD-1) to its ligands, that is, PD-1 ligand 1 (PD-L1) and PD-L2, to trigger an antitumor immune response. It has been approved for the treatment of multiple solid tumors, including non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Pembrolizumab has become the first-line treatment for driver mutation-negative NSCLC, either as a single agent or in combination with cytotoxic anticancer drugs. Pembrolizumab was initially approved based on body weight (2 mg/kg every 3 weeks (Q3W)) according to the KEYNOTE-001 study.1 Following a consensus on the dosage, 200 mg Q3W treatment has been approved for various tumor types owing to similar exposure distribution at 2 mg/kg and 200 mg doses. Recent simulated pharmacokinetic (PK) modeling and exposure-response analyses have shown that the predicted exposure for pembrolizumab 400 mg every 6 weeks (Q6W) is similar to that for 200 mg/body weight, 2 mg/kg Q3W, and <10 mg/kg Q3W.2 The Food and Drug Administration has approved a new pembrolizumab dosage regimen of 400 mg Q6W for solid tumors, leading to low treatment frequency and reduction in clinical visits.

We previously reported that switching the dose of pembrolizumab from 200 mg Q3W to 400 mg Q6W led to the development of early immune-related adverse events (irAEs), particularly pneumonitis.3 However, it remains unclear how a change in pembrolizumab dosage from 200 mg Q3W to 400 mg Q6W induces new irAEs. This study aimed to evaluate the association between the change in pembrolizumab dosage and the occurrence of irAEs using population PK (pop-PK) analysis in patients who switched from 200 mg Q3W to 400 mg Q6W.

Methods

Study design and participants

Patients with advanced NSCLC who received pembrolizumab 200 mg Q3W as the initial dose between August 2020 and September 2022 at the National Cancer Center Hospital, Tokyo, Japan, were included. We retrospectively collected baseline patient characteristics, pembrolizumab efficacy, safety, and dosing schedule. Eligible patients were identified using the hospital electronic medical record and chemotherapy prescribing/administration records. Authorized study investigators accessed the electronic medical record, which contains identifiable information, to extract study variables. We assessed irAEs according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events V.4.0. Early irAEs were defined as those occurring within six cycles of pembrolizumab 200 mg Q3W or within three cycles of pembrolizumab 400 mg Q6W, corresponding to 126 days. Data were handled in accordance with institutional policies.This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, as revised in Fortaleza, Brazil, in 2013.

Measurement of serum pembrolizumab concentrations

Pembrolizumab serum concentrations were measured using residual serum from clinical laboratory tests stored in the National Cancer Center Biobank. Serum samples used for concentration measurements were obtained from stored residual serum collected at the following time points: before the first, second, and third cycles of 200 mg Q3W treatment (or the fourth cycle if samples from the second or third cycle were unavailable), before the first cycle after switching to 400 mg Q6W treatment, and before all subsequent cycles thereafter. In total, 497 serum samples were measured. The serum pembrolizumab concentration was measured using liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry equipped with a triple quadrupole mass analyzer (Nexera X2 and LCMS-8050, Shimadzu).4

Pharmacokinetic modeling and simulation

Pembrolizumab pop-PK analysis was performed using a non-linear mixed-effects (NLME) approach and the first-order conditional estimate-extended least squares method using the Phoenix NLME software (Certara, St. Louis, Missouri, USA). The one-compartment model was selected for basic pop-PK analysis because many remnant samples had trough pembrolizumab concentration (Ctrough). Observed and model-predicted pembrolizumab concentration-time profiles under the 200 mg Q3W and 400 mg Q6W regimens are shown in online supplemental figure 1.

Determination and validation of the PK model

Pop-PK analysis showed that the overall condition number of the basic model exceeded 1,000, indicating that the individual parameter values of the volume of distribution (V) were close to the population mean. The γ shrinkage prior to closure was 0.347. To evaluate the stability and reliability of the model, the final model was fitted to 1,000 bootstrap datasets generated by resampling with replacement, and the median values and 95% CIs of the PK parameters were calculated (online supplemental table 1). The maximum error compared with the original parameter estimates was within 18.98%, demonstrating the robustness and precision of the structural and covariate models. The parameter estimates are presented in online supplemental table 1. The objective function (−2LL (log-likelihood)) drop of >3.84 for one covariate corresponded to p<0.05, and the significance level for 1 df was considered. Covariates were selected based on Ohuchi et al.4 A stepwise covariate search identified body surface area (BSA) and albumin (Alb) as significant using the likelihood ratio test. Adding lymphocyte count did not produce a further reduction in −2LL; therefore, the final model excluded this covariate (online supplemental table 2). In addition, the analysis considered two different pembrolizumab dosing regimens used in this study. The final model is expressed as follows:

V = 5339 × (Alb / median Alb)–1.02 × (BSA / median BSA)1.64 × exp (ηVx0 × (Regimen = 1) + nVx1 × (Regimen = 2))

Cl = 110 × (Alb / median Alb)–1.29 × (BSA/median BSA)1.86 × exp (ηCl)

Here, Regimen=1 corresponds to the 200 mg Q3W dosing schedule, and Regimen=2 corresponds to the 400 mg Q6W dosing schedule. Details of the final model verification are provided in online supplemental figure 2.

Statistical analyses

The primary objective of this analysis was to evaluate the association between safety outcomes and PK parameters of pembrolizumab. Categorical variables were compared using the χ² or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Landmark analyses were conducted using time-dependent Cox proportional hazards models.

The association between the PK parameter including actual Ctrough, pop-PK-estimated trough concentration (eCtrough) and maximum concentration (eCmax) of pembrolizumab and the occurrence of irAEs was assessed using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test.

For each patient, the percentage coefficient of variation (%CV) for eCmax and eCtrough during the 200 mg Q3W treatment period was calculated as follows:

The %CV=(SD/mean)×100

Differences in %CV-eCmax and %CV-eCtrough according to irAE occurrence were evaluated using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test and multivariable logistic regression analysis, including age, sex, histology, smoking status, PD-L1 expression, %CV-eCtrough, and %CV-eCmax.

To identify clinical predictors of %CV-eCmax, multivariable linear regression analysis included demographic, tumor-related, laboratory, and renal function variables, including renal dysfunction (creatinine clearance <60 mL/min5). Variable selection was performed using a stepwise procedure based on Akaike’s information criterion (AIC).

Data were cleaned by checking for missing or implausible values and reconciling discrepancies by review of source documentation. Analyses were conducted using available data without imputation. All reported p values were two-sided, with statistical significance set at p<0.05. All statistical analyses were performed using Stata V.14.0 (StataCorp, College Station, Texas, USA).

Results

Patient characteristics

During the study period, 737 patients were treated with 200 mg of Q3W pembrolizumab (figure 1). Among them, 79 patients switched from 200 mg Q3W to 400 mg Q6W. The baseline characteristics of the 657 patients who continued the 200 mg Q3W treatment (continued group) and the 79 patients who switched from the 200 mg Q3W treatment to the 400 mg Q6W treatment (switching group) are summarized in online supplemental table 3. Most of the baseline characteristics were similar between the continuous and switching groups. Among these variables, only the PD-L1 tumor proportion score (TPS) and number of treatment cycles showed significant differences (PD-L1 TPS >50%: 47.6% vs 67.1%).

In the continued group (n=658), 271 (41.2%) patients experienced at least one irAE during a median follow-up period of 238 days, including 326 events of any irAE, 126 events of grade ≥3, with early irAEs (within three cycles) occurring in 202 events (online supplemental table 4). In the switching group (n=79) during 200 mg Q3W treatment period (median, 174 days), 32 (40.5%) patients experienced at least one irAE, including 36 events of any irAE, four of grade ≥3, with early irAEs (within three cycles) observed in 20 events. There were no significant differences in the incidence of any irAEs, including early irAEs and pneumonitis during 200 mg Q3W between the switching and continued groups (any irAE; 40.5% vs 41.2%, p=1.000, early irAE; 24.1% vs 27.4%, p=0.593, pneumonitis; 6.3% vs 14.4%, p=0.054) (online supplemental table 4). After switching to the 400 mg Q6W treatment (median follow-up period, 427 days), 45 events occurred new or worsened irAEs, including 19 of pneumonitis (9 grade ≥3 and 1 grade 5), with early new or worsened irAEs (within three cycles) occurring in 23 events, pneumonitis being the most frequent (n=13) (online supplemental table 4). Over the entire treatment period, the incidence of pneumonitis was significantly higher in the switch group (switching group vs continued group: 14.4% vs 24.1%, p=0.032).

Next, to address the time structure of treatment switching, we performed the landmark analyses. In the day-42 landmark cohort, the hazard of first any irAE was numerically higher during the 400 mg Q6W period than during the 200 mg Q3W period, although this did not reach statistical significance (HR 1.61, 95% CI 0.97 to 2.67; p=0.066). In contrast, the hazard of first pneumonitis (HR 4.87, 95% CI 2.65 to 8.95; p<0.001) and first grade ≥3 pneumonitis (HR 3.00, 95% CI 1.33 to 6.78; p=0.008) were significantly higher during the 400 mg Q6W period (online supplemental table 5). In the day-126 landmark cohort, no significant difference was observed for first any irAE (HR 1.33, 95% CI 0.66 to 2.69; p=0.429), whereas the hazards of first pneumonitis (HR 3.08, 95% CI 1.44 to 6.57; p=0.004) and first grade ≥3 pneumonitis (HR 3.63, 95% CI 1.29 to 10.21; p=0.014) remained significantly higher during the 400 mg Q6W period (online supplemental table 5).

Clinical predictors of intercycle variability in eCmax (%CV-eCmax) during 200 mg Q3W treatment

Because %CV-eCmax during the 200 mg Q3W treatment period was the only PK parameter consistently associated with early irAEs, we next explored clinical factors associated with interindividual variability in eCmax. In the Wilcoxon analysis, eCmax was higher in patients with renal dysfunction (p=0.030) (figure 5A). Accordingly, we constructed a multivariate linear regression model with %CV-eCmax as the dependent variable. In the final model, selected using a stepwise procedure, higher %CV-eCmax was independently associated with a higher platelet count (β=0.141, p=0.042) and renal dysfunction (β=5.08, p=0.009) (figure 5B). This model explained approximately 14.3% of the variability in %CV-eCmax (adjusted R²=0.114, AIC=412.8). No other variables met the significance threshold and were retained in the final model.

Discussion

We previously reported that switching the pembrolizumab dose from 200 mg Q3W to 400 mg Q6W was associated with the occurrence of new or worsening irAEs, particularly pneumonitis. However, the extent to which PK changes associated with dose switching influence the safety profiles of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) remains unclear. In the present study, we examined longitudinal PK metrics across the 200 mg Q3W and 400 mg Q6W regimens and found that higher intercycle variability in pop-PK-estimated Cmax during 200 mg Q3W (%CV-eCmax) was associated with a higher incidence of irAEs after switching. In contrast, absolute PK measures were not associated with irAE incidence. These findings suggest that PK variability, rather than single-point exposure, may be a clinically relevant determinant of toxicity risk following dose regimen changes.

Previous clinical and pop-PK studies supported the adoption of fixed-dose pembrolizumab regimens, including 200 mg Q3W and 400 mg Q6W, based on broadly comparable overall exposure across regimens.16,13 Consistent with this, prior studies have shown no clear differences in irAE incidence across various dosing schedules. Absolute PK measures have not been clearly associated with irAE risk. In line with these observations, our study also found that absolute PK measures were not associated with irAE incidence, whereas higher intercycle variability in Cmax during 200 mg Q3W was associated with an increased risk of irAEs after switching.

A high %CV in drug exposure generally reflects unstable or fluctuating PK within an individual patient. In general pharmacology, variability in systemic exposure can be an important determinant of toxicity and may at times be more informative than mean exposure.14,17 In ICIs, however, the clinical relevance of exposure variability to irAEs remains poorly understood. Our findings suggest that higher intercycle variability in estimated Cmax during 200 mg Q3W may predispose patients to irAEs after dosage switching. One possible explanation is that fluctuating exposure leads to intermittent immune stimulation and occasional crossing of an immune activation threshold in sensitized immune cells, thereby triggering irAEs, consistent with the hypothesis proposed by Tsukamoto et al.18

Our study also demonstrated that renal dysfunction and higher platelet counts were associated with increased interindividual variability in Cmax (%CV-eCmax). Renal impairment, especially severe renal impairment, may affect the PK of ICIs; however, data on how renal impairment affects the PK of ICIs are limited.19 A preclinical study reported the presence of unchanged nivolumab in the urine of rats with renal dysfunction, but not in healthy controls,20 suggesting that renal function may influence the disposition of ICIs. These findings are consistent with our observation that renal dysfunction may contribute to interindividual variability in pembrolizumab exposure. Regarding platelet count, FcγRIIA expressed on platelets has been implicated in the clearance of monoclonal antibodies through mechanisms distinct from FcRn-mediated recycling.21 Antibody binding to FcγRIIA on platelets may promote cellular uptake and proteolytic degradation, thereby enhancing systemic clearance. The observed association between platelet count and variability in pembrolizumab exposure in this study supports a potential role for platelet-mediated antibody clearance.

One limitation of this study is that the Cmax was estimated using a pop-PK model, similar to recent approaches used to determine ICI dosing. The model used was a one-compartment model based on trough serum concentrations, which may have led to an underestimation of the Cmax. To address this limitation, we are currently conducting a prospective study in which we will collect serum samples longitudinally during two dosing regimens (200 mg Q3W treatment and 400 mg Q6W treatment) and at the time of regimen switching, to directly measure pembrolizumab PK and pharmacodynamics and to understand the broader immunological impact of ICI therapy, particularly in relation to dosing regimens and irAE status.

In conclusion, high intercycle variability in estimated Cmax during 200 mg Q3W was associated with a higher risk of new or worsened irAEs after switching to 400 mg Q6W. Further studies are needed to clarify how PK instability, including its associations with renal dysfunction and platelet count, influences immune pharmacodynamics and irAE risk.

Unveiling the global research status and intellectual map of cancer and immunosuppressants
Authors: Tianyi Yuan, Yamin Zhao, Yunna Ma, Yue Ben, Tianqi Wu, Hongrun Shi, Jun Pu, Jue Gu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe use of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) has significantly advanced cancer therapy, improving survival rates and prognosis for many patients. This study employs bibliometric methods to explore the current state and future trends in the field of immunosuppressants and cancer treatment.MethodsData were retrieved from the Science Citation Index Expanded for articles and reviews published between 2014 and 2024 in the Web of Science database. 2,352 records were analyzed using CiteSpace, VOSviewer, R software, and Microsoft Excel. The focus was on publication trends, citations, co-citation networks, keyword co-occurrence, and collaborative networks.ResultsThe study identified a steady increase in publications and citations, with an average annual growth rate of 3.93%. The United States and China were the leading countries in research output, while the University of Texas MD Anderson Cancer Center and Harvard Medical School were key institutions. Research hotspots included ICIs, tumor microenvironment, and immune-related adverse events. Interdisciplinary collaboration was highlighted as crucial for advancing the field.ConclusionsThe growing body of research on cancer and immunosuppressants highlights the need for optimizing immunotherapy protocols, managing drug resistance, and exploring the potential of immunosuppressants in cancer treatment. Strengthening international collaboration is vital for translating research findings into clinical practice and improving patient outcomes. Despite the comprehensive analysis, the study’s reliance on the WoSCC database and English-language publications may limit the generalizability of findings.

Introduction

The traditional methods of cancer treatment, including surgery, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy, are associated with certain side effects and have limited efficacy against certain types of cancer [1]. The advent of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) has revolutionized the field of oncology, ushering in a new era in cancer therapy by harnessing the body’s immune system to combat malignant cells [2]. These groundbreaking therapies target key regulatory pathways of immune responses, fundamentally changing the treatment landscape for many cancer types previously considered resistant to conventional therapies [3]. Following the FDA’s approval of ipilimumab, the first cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated protein 4 inhibitor for advanced melanoma in 2011, ICIs became the cornerstone of modern cancer immunotherapy [4]. The clinical success of these drugs has been remarkable, with durable responses observed in various malignancies, including melanoma, non-small cell lung cancer, renal cell carcinoma, and Hodgkin’s lymphoma [5–7]. By effectively blocking the inhibitory immune checkpoints used by tumors to evade immune surveillance, these therapeutic antibodies can rejuvenate exhausted T cells and restore their cytotoxic potential, providing unprecedented long-term survival benefits for some patients who were previously considered to have poor prognoses [8]. The profound impact of ICIs on cancer treatment paradigms has been further demonstrated by their rapid integration into standard treatment regimens across different disease stages, from metastatic to adjuvant settings, either as monotherapies or in combination with other treatment modalities [9, 10]. Despite the significant effectiveness of ICIs in cancer treatment, they are also associated with certain irAEs [11]. Therefore, identifying patients suitable for ICI therapy, improving treatment response rates, and minimizing immune-related adverse events remain critical challenges in current clinical research [12]. Although research on cancer and immunosuppressants has significantly increased in recent years, there is still a lack of systematic articles summarizing the current status of research in this field and forecasting future trends. Therefore, this paper primarily focuses on the field of ICIs used to manage irAEs, employing bibliometric analysis methods for a comprehensive examination, aiming to reveal research hotspots and discuss future trends.

Bibliometrics is a discipline that uses quantitative methods to study literature and its related information, focusing on citation analysis, co-occurrence analysis, and collaboration network analysis [13]. Citation analysis evaluates the academic impact of literature or authors by counting the number of times they are co-cited. Co-occurrence analysis examines the frequency with which keywords appear together in literature, thereby constructing networks that reveal hot topics and research trends in a given field. Collaboration network analysis, on the other hand, studies the relationships between researchers, institutions, and countries, exploring interdisciplinary and team collaborations. To delve deeper into the progress of immunosuppressant research in cancer therapy, provide valuable insights for researchers, and analyze their clinical applications and mechanisms across different cancer types, we conducted a bibliometric analysis of this field’s research outcomes. This analysis aims to provide new perspectives for the broader use of immunosuppressants in cancer therapy and to offer a theoretical foundation for future clinical applications.

Materials and methods

Data source and search strategy

The data was collected from the Science Citation Index Expanded (SCI-Expanded) of the WoSCC database. This search method identifies papers containing these terms in the title, abstract, author keywords, or keyword plus. The publication period for the selected literature spans from January 1, 2014, to December 31, 2024, and the publication language is English. Only publications classified as “Article” and “Review” were included, excluding all other publication types. All articles were retrieved on the same day. A total of 2,352 records from WoSCC were ultimately identified. Figure 1 presents the flowchart of the search strategy, literature retrieval, and analysis process.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

This study employed specific inclusion and exclusion criteria to ensure the appropriate and high-quality selection of literature. The focus was placed on articles and reviews, particularly original research articles, to ensure the inclusion of key research findings within the relevant fields. Furthermore, the inclusion criteria were limited to articles published in English to facilitate frequency analysis. Conference abstracts, conference proceedings, editorials, lectures, letters, incomplete references, and duplicate publications were excluded. These criteria ensured the high quality of the selected articles.

Strict quality control measures were implemented to maintain the quality of the included studies. Two independent researchers extracted relevant data by reviewing the titles and abstracts of the literature. Each selected article was comprehensively evaluated according to the pre-established inclusion criteria. Any discrepancies between the two researchers were discussed with a third researcher. The adoption of these quality management procedures aimed to ensure the credibility of the study’s findings. For further analysis, a variety of information related to the literature was collected, including publication counts, countries or regions, institutions, journals, authors, keywords, and citation frequencies.

Data analysis

The literature selected was analyzed and visualized using Microsoft Excel 2021. Data from WoSCC were imported as plain text files into CiteSpace 6.4 R1 and VOSviewer 1.6.20 for bibliometric and visualization analysis. The file names followed the format “download_*”. CiteSpace was employed to extract keywords and references from the most frequently cited papers and generate journal overlay visualizations. The parameters were as follows: (1) Period: 2014–2024 (Slice Length = 1); (2) Selection criteria: Pruning: Pathfinder + pruning the merged network. VOSviewer was used to create knowledge maps based on contributions from countries/regions, institutions, influential authors and journals, co-cited papers, and co-occurring keywords. Additionally, R software 4.4.2 was utilized to create a map of the cumulative publication volume by country. The visualization layout was optimized using Pajek software.

Results

The publication and citation trends

The final analysis includes a total of 2,352 publications, consisting of 1,604 articles and 748 reviews. As shown in Fig. 2, the number of articles published in the fields of cancer and immunosuppressants has steadily increased from 2014 to 2024, with an average annual growth rate of 3.93%. This upward trend highlights a phase of rapid expansion, indicating heightened attention from researchers in these areas. The fitted trendline in the figure suggests that this growth may continue, with potentially more publications in the future. In the past four years, the publication rate has stabilized. The highest number of publications occurred in 2021 and 2024, with 276 and 272 articles, respectively. Additionally, the number of citations per year has shown a general upward trajectory over the decades. By the end of the search period, these publications had accumulated a total of 53,192 citations, averaging 22.62 citations per publication (Fig. 2).

In terms of citations, the University of Texas MD Anderson Cancer Center also performed well, with 1,487 citations. Harvard Medical School had the highest citation count, with 1,595 citations. Mayo Clinic and the University of Sydney also ranked highly in citation counts. Journals and co-cited journals analysis

Over the past decades, a total of 1,001 journals have published articles related to cancer and immunosuppressive agents, with 87 journals featuring five or more papers. We constructed a network visualization map that illustrates the close collaborations among leading journals (Fig. 5A). Table 4 lists the top 10 journals with the highest number of publications. Among the top 10, five journals are from Switzerland, and five are from the United States. According to the 2024 Journal Citation Reports (JCR), seven of these journals are classified in Q1. Frontiers in Immunology has published the most articles (N = 58), followed by Medicine (36) and Transplantation Proceedings (N = 31). The journal American Journal of Transplantation has the highest total citations (N = 1,284) and the highest impact factor (2024, IF: 8.9). Co-citation frequency is also a key metric for assessing journal influence. As shown in Figs. 5b and 112 journals have been co-cited more than 200 times. The most co-cited journal is the New England Journal of Medicine (2,949 co-citations), followed by Transplantation (2,088 co-citations). Using CiteSpace software, we generated a dual-map overlay of the journals, revealing the distribution of citation and co-citation relationships. This diagram employs a dual-map structure contrasting source publications on the left, emphasizing cutting-edge developments, with reference literature on the right, signifying established scholarship. Interlinking pathways trace bibliographic connections across domains, revealing collaborative exchanges and intellectual bridges. Oval dimensions encode research metrics: longitudinal span denotes contributor quantity, while lateral extent represents manuscript output. Through its overlay framework, the visualization exposes cross-disciplinary citation patterns via relational curves, tracks scholarly dissemination, and captures evolving academic frontiers. As depicted in Fig. 5c, three primary citation pathways are identified. Molecular biology and immunology journals predominantly cite molecular biology and genetics journals (z = 4.335326, f = 15,364). Medicine, medical, and clinical journals primarily cite molecular biology and genetics journals, as well as health, nursing, and medicine journals (z = 6.2602253, f = 21,758; z = 5.336611, f = 18690).

Cocited reference and reference burst analysis

The purpose of co-citation analysis is to measure the degree of association between references within a specific research field. A co-citation visualization map was created using VOSviewer, focusing on publications with over 17 co-citations (Fig. 6A). The top 10 most cited papers in cancer and immunosuppressant research are shown in Table 5. The New England Journal of Medicine plays a significant scientific role in this field, with six out of the ten most cited articles published in this journal. Three other articles were published in JAMA, Nature, and The Lancet. The most frequently cited articles were published in JAMA - Journal of the American Medical Association and New England Journal of Medicine, each receiving 50 citations. These articles, titled “Spectrum of Cancer Risk Among US Solid Organ Transplant Recipients” and “Immune-Related Adverse Events Associated with Immune Checkpoint Blockade,” primarily discuss ICIs’ adverse events and cancer risks in organ transplant recipients. Using CiteSpace software, the strongest citation bursts were identified (Fig. 6B). Notably, the reference with the highest citation burst is the article titled “Increased Risk for Nonmelanoma Skin Cancers in Patients Who Receive Thiopurines for Inflammatory Bowel Disease” (IF = 25.7, Q1, strength = 9.86), followed by “Infliximab, Azathioprine, or Combination Therapy for Crohn’s Disease” (IF = 96.2, Q1, strength = 9.31). The most recent citation burst occurred in 2022 and is still ongoing, indicating continued attention from researchers in the field. Figure 6C shows a timeline of the cited literature, illustrating how the frequency of citations changes over the years.

Keywords analysis of research hotspots

Keyword analysis reveals the primary research hotspots and scientific trends in the fields of cancer and immunosuppressive agents. A co-occurrence analysis conducted using Vosviewer identified a total of 10,227 keywords, with 124 keywords appearing more than 30 times, as shown in Fig. 7a. Table 6 presents the top 14 keywords with the highest betweenness centrality. The top five keywords, namely “quality of life,” “kidney,” “checkpoint inhibitors,” “chimeric antigen receptor,” and “cyclosporine,” align with the focus of the research themes. Cluster analysis of keywords using CiteSpace reveals the top eight clusters, as illustrated in Fig. 7B. Cluster 0 (Red) primarily includes terms such as bone marrow transplantation, acute myeloid leukemia, hematologic malignancy, graft-versus-host disease, and antithymocyte globulin. The focus of this cluster is the application of immunosuppressive agents. Cluster 1 (Orange) primarily includes terms like diagnosis, clinical characteristics, management, infection, and polymerase chain reaction. It emphasizes the diagnostic and therapeutic principles related to immunosuppressive agents. Notably, in the keyword clustering, Cluster 1 includes terms such as “infection,” underscoring that the intersection of “immunosuppression–infection–ICI management” constitutes an integral component of this field. Cluster 2 (Light Green) primarily includes terms such as squamous cell carcinoma, basal cell carcinoma, organ transplant recipient, skin cancer, and nonmelanoma skin cancer. The theme of this cluster is the increased susceptibility to certain types of cancer in organ transplant recipients undergoing immunosuppressive treatment. Cluster 3 (Dark Green) primarily includes liver transplantation, hepatocellular carcinoma, renal transplantation, immunosuppression, and heart transplantation. This cluster highlights the indispensable role of immunosuppressive therapy in organ transplant recipients. Cluster 4 (Blue) primarily includes multiple sclerosis, immunosuppression therapy, invasive fungal infections, and biomarkers. The focus here is on immunosuppressive treatment in patients with multiple sclerosis. Cluster 5 (Dark Blue) primarily includes activation, NF-kappa B, and sphingosine 1 phosphate, which are closely related to the mechanisms of action of immunosuppressive agents. Cluster 6 (Purple) primarily includes immune-related adverse events, skin cancer, ICIs, and lung cancer. This cluster deals with immune-related adverse events. Cluster 7 (Magenta) primarily includes rheumatoid arthritis, safety, systemic lupus erythematosus, double-blind, and tocilizumab. The focus here is on research related to the use of medications in autoimmune diseases. The analysis indicates that the research hotspots are primarily concerned with the application scope, mechanisms of action, and prevention of adverse reactions associated with immunosuppressive agents.

Discussion

General information

This study presents the first bibliometric analysis of research on immunosuppressants in the context of cancer. It delves into the relationship between cancer and immunosuppressants, bridging a significant gap in the existing knowledge base of this domain. The analysis offers profound insights and guidance for future research endeavors. Through the use of reliable and effective statistical analysis and visual expression methods to summarize the collected literature, we identified key research hotspots and outlined potential future trends in this field. By examining 2,352 articles published over the past decade, we found an increasing trend in both the number of publications and citation frequencies, indicating a growing attention to this area of research. And the trend seems to indicate that in the next few years, the number of publications will be higher. The top five countries in terms of citation frequency account for more than 45% of the total publications, with the majority of these countries being developed nations. The United States leads in cancer and immunosuppressant research, with a significantly higher number of publications, citations, and h-index values. This is probably due to the high level of attention given to this field and the availability of substantial funding. The United Kingdom also shows a high h-index, reflecting its strong commitment to research in this area. The University of Texas MD Anderson Cancer Center in the United States has published extensively on cancer and immunosuppressants, with close collaborations with other institutions. COLOMBEL JEAN-FREDERIC from the Icahn School of Medicine at Mt Sinai, New York, ranks first among authors and holds the highest h-index. His primary research focuses on inflammatory bowel disease, Crohn’s disease, and ulcerative colitis. SANDBORN WJ from the Division of Gastroenterology at the University of California San Diego ranks first among co-cited authors with the highest number of citations. These analyses highlight the critical role these two authors have played in the field of cancer and immunosuppressants. This puts the United States in a leading position in this field, in terms of the number of publications, influence, and level of cooperation with other countries.

Our analysis also reveals that individual authors tend to publish relatively few articles, and their h-index is not particularly high. Countries should actively recruit specialists or send senior scholars to top institutions for further learning. Immune checkpoint inhibitors and Immune-related adverse events

The use of ICIs in cancer treatment is expanding, yielding promising results, but it also carries a significant risk of irAEs. These events may arise from the overactivation of the immune system, which attacks healthy cells [21], and moderate to severe cases may require treatment with immunosuppressive drugs. The management of irAEs remains suboptimal in many areas, as there is still considerable uncertainty among experts regarding the optimal choice, dosage, and duration of corticosteroids, as well as the use of other immunosuppressive agents [22]. Moreover, diagnostic delays and the difficulty patients face in accessing effective irAE treatments create further barriers to timely care. Multiple oncology society guidelines—including ASCO (American Society of Clinical Oncology), NCCN (National Comprehensive Cancer Network), and ESMO (European Society for Medical Oncology)—recommend initial treatment of moderate to severe immune‑related adverse events with systemic corticosteroids [23–25]. However, information regarding the safety and potential adverse effects of these immunosuppressive agents is limited. Studies have found that approximately one-third of patients undergoing corticosteroid treatment, or a combination of corticosteroids and other immunosuppressive drugs, experience irAEs [23]. It is crucial to consider shortening treatment duration or, where possible, adopting alternative strategies to mitigate these complications. Future prospective studies should further investigate the safety of immunosuppressive agents in managing irAEs.

Research has identified KL-6 as a promising biomarker for screening ICI-induced pneumonia, particularly in patients without non-small cell lung cancer. Monitoring KL-6 for early detection can aid in timely intervention for ICI-induced pneumonia, potentially preventing treatment interruptions and reducing the need for immunosuppressive agents [26]. Corticosteroid pulse therapy has shown potential in treating corticosteroid-refractory pneumonia [27]. A prospective study demonstrated that extracorporeal photopheresis was more effective than second-line immunosuppressive agents in treating corticosteroid-refractory and corticosteroid-dependent irAEs induced by checkpoint inhibitors [28]. A preclinical study demonstrated that tumor growth in the group treated with PD-1 inhibitors combined with tocilizumab was significantly slower than in the group receiving PD-1 inhibitor monotherapy. Tocilizumab exerts its antitumor effect by suppressing the IL-6–JAK2–STAT3 signaling pathway. By targeting this pathway, tocilizumab synergistically enhances antitumor activity through the inhibition of lung cancer cell proliferation [29]. Moreover, tocilizumab has been shown to prevent the recurrence of immune checkpoint inhibitor–associated arthritis, thereby reducing symptom relapse and extending the duration of ICI therapy following re-challenge [30]. Screening of new biomarkers and the development of new drugs may benefit patients.

The combination of ipilimumab and nivolumab is an effective treatment for metastatic melanoma. However, irAEs are common, and studies have shown that, compared to permanent discontinuation of nivolumab, interrupting ipilimumab/nivolumab treatment for irAEs management followed by resumption of single-agent nivolumab can improve overall survival in metastatic melanoma patients [31]. In patients with advanced melanoma receiving first-line ipilimumab and nivolumab, second-line immunosuppression for irAEs was associated with impaired progression-free survival, overall survival, and melanoma-specific survival, highlighting the importance of evaluating the effectiveness of various irAEs management strategies [32]. In patients with pre-existing autoimmune diseases, who have not received immunosuppressive therapy, and in advanced treatment of melanoma, there is an increased risk of autoimmune disease flare-ups with ipilimumab and anti-PD1 combination therapy, particularly in those with inflammatory bowel disease or rheumatoid disease, as well as in baseline immunosuppressive patients [33]. Therefore, careful consideration of treatment strategies is essential for these patients. IrAEs are common among patients receiving ipilimumab, with studies indicating that approximately one-third of ipilimumab-treated patients require systemic corticosteroids, and nearly one-third require further immunosuppression with anti-TNFα treatment. However, this treatment does not affect OS or time to treatment failure [34]. New treatment approaches should be further explored, as treatment strategies have significant implications for patient prognosis, and more prospective studies are needed to provide high-level evidence for advanced treatment.

Hematologic irAEs can affect all hematopoietic cell lineages and may persist or even be fatal. Management may require immunosuppression beyond corticosteroids. Although these irAEs are rare, clinicians should be aware of the need for regular blood cell count monitoring and prompt intervention upon detection [35]. Delayed diagnosis and untimely treatment remain significant barriers to irAEs management. Effective treatment strategies, particularly for refractory irAEs, are still under investigation. Despite the potential of ICIs in cancer therapy, the management of immune-related adverse events remains a challenge, and future research should focus on exploring additional viable screening biomarkers and novel therapeutic strategies, especially for refractory cases.

The management of these irAEs requires further investigation to refine treatment strategies and improve patient outcomes. Future research should focus on optimizing immunosuppressive therapies, improving the safety profiles of immunotherapy, and exploring new biomarkers and treatment options for managing irAEs. Tailoring treatment protocols to individual patient profiles, considering factors such as organ type, health status, and cancer risk, will be crucial for enhancing the efficacy and safety of both immunosuppressive and anti-cancer treatments.

Beyond irAE management itself, there is an intrinsic coupling between immunosuppression and infection susceptibility. High-dose corticosteroids, anti-TNF agents, and other immunosuppressants used for irAEs can increase the risk of opportunistic and serious infections [36]; likewise, patients who are immunosuppressed at baseline remain predisposed to infections when receiving ICIs. The keyword clustering analysis also identifies ‘infection’ as a salient term, functions as a bridging node that links the treatment cluster (e.g., steroids, infliximab) with prevention terms (e.g., prophylaxis) (Fig. 7B 7C), which aligns with current practice guidance [25, 37]. Integrating infection-risk management into irAE care pathways and developing risk-stratified protocols can preserve antitumor efficacy while minimizing iatrogenic infections and related complications, without compromising oncologic control.

Research progress

The keyword burst detection (Fig. 8; Table 6) identified activation (centrality = 0.13), resistance (strength = 5.27, 2022–2024), and adverse events (centrality = 0.12) as dominant emerging frontiers, reflecting a shift toward mechanistic and toxicity-focused studies. In terms of ICIs target expansion, research perspectives have shifted from the classic PD-1/PD-L1 and CTLA-4 to a broader range of novel immune checkpoint targets with potential [38]. The LAG-3 inhibitor relatlimab, in combination with nivolumab, has been approved by the FDA for melanoma treatment, marking the official entry of the next generation of checkpoint inhibitors into the clinical stage [39]. This regimen significantly prolongs the progression-free survival of patients with metastatic melanoma and provides a new option for patients with PD-1 resistance [40, 41]. The TIGIT target has gained attention due to its synergistic effect with PD-1 [42]. In NRF2-high lung adenocarcinoma, combination therapy with anti-PD-1 (or anti-PD-L1) and anti-TIGIT may achieve synergistic effects [43]. This combination also synergistically enhances the proliferation and effector function of antitumor CD8⁺ T cell [44]. In xenograft models of hematologic and solid malignancies, simultaneous downregulation of PD-1 and TIGIT confers marked synergy for CD19-directed CAR T cells, improving short-term effector function and long-term persistence [45]. Moreover, monoclonal antibody-mediated TIGIT blockade increases T-cell activity in an NK cell–dependent manner and shows synergy with anti-PD-1 therapy within the colorectal cancer tumor microenvironment [46]. The unique role of TIM-3 inhibitors in hematologic malignancies and the specific expression of the VISTA target in myeloid cells have provided new dimensions for immunotherapy [47, 48]. CD47–SIRPα blockade, by releasing macrophage phagocytosis from inhibition, shows the greatest promise in hematologic malignancies but is challenged by mechanism-based toxicities such as anemia and by regimen-optimization issues; molecular engineering and rational combinations are needed to re-balance efficacy and safety [49]. Siglec-15, an immunosuppressive molecule largely independent of PD-1, is highly expressed on myeloid cells across multiple tumor types; genetic ablation and antibody-mediated blockade have demonstrated its capacity to amplify antitumor immunity, supporting its potential as a broadly applicable immunotherapeutic target [50]. Anti-CD73 antibodies, A2A receptor inhibitors, and regimens combining radiotherapy with PD-1 blockade have emerged as active strategies, yet precise patient stratification and dose optimization remain necessary [51].The exploration of these new targets not only enriches treatment options but also deepens the understanding of the complexity of immune regulation networks. Across hematologic malignancies, the role of ICIs is not uniform. ICIs are established primarily in classical Hodgkin lymphoma [52, 53], whereas in myeloid neoplasms and most non-Hodgkin lymphomas their use remains investigational pending randomized evidence [54]. ICIs may even be detrimental to patient survival in multiple myeloma [55]. Overall, the role of ICIs across hematologic malignancies is heterogeneous, highlighting the need for further study.

The rise of biomarker research correlates with an annual increase in publications on predictive biomarkers (Keyword cluster #4). Research on biomarkers is advancing in a multidimensional and dynamic direction. However, clinical adoption remains limited, as evidenced by the low centrality (0.11) of ‘pharmacokinetics’ (Table 6)—a key metric for dosing safety. In addition to traditional markers such as PD-L1 expression and tumor mutation burden, microenvironment characteristics such as HLA molecular features, tertiary lymphoid structures, and myeloid cell infiltration provide more comprehensive information for predicting therapeutic efficacy [56]. Liquid biopsy technology enables dynamic monitoring of treatment responses [57], with peripheral blood T-cell clonality and ctDNA changes providing a basis for timely adjustment of treatment strategies [58, 59]. Notably, substantial progress has been made in studying the correlation between gut microbiota composition and treatment response. The abundance of specific species, such as Akkermansia muciniphila, is positively correlated with efficacy, opening new avenues for enhancing treatment outcomes through microbiome modulation [60, 61].

Combination therapies dominated high-citation publications (Table 5), which is a key breakthrough in enhancing the effectiveness of immunotherapy. The combination with targeted drugs demonstrates unique advantages, with VEGF inhibitors showing significant synergy with ICIs in liver and kidney cancers, and PARP inhibitors exhibiting notable synergy with ICIs in BRCA-mutant tumors [62, 63]. The combination of cell therapy and ICIs offers new approaches to overcoming tumor microenvironment suppression, while neoantigen vaccines may further enhance therapeutic effects by boosting immunogenicity [64]. Epigenetic modulators, such as DNMT inhibitors and HDAC inhibitors, add new possibilities for combination therapy by regulating gene expression and T cell function [65, 66].

Despite remarkable achievements, ICIs still face challenges such as limited response rates, complex mechanisms of resistance, and high treatment costs. Future developments will focus on cutting-edge areas such as precision treatment strategies guided by multi-omics and the development of novel animal models. With the deeper integration of basic research and clinical translation, ICIs are expected to bring long-term survival benefits to a broader patient population, continuously driving the transformation and progress of cancer treatment paradigms. Future trends

The challenges in the use of ICIs in transplant recipients highlight the inadequacies in mechanistic understanding. Although PD-1/PD-L1 blockade can enhance anti-tumor immunity, organ transplants often expose tissue-specific antigens, leading to abnormal activation of memory T-cells and triggering irreversible rejection [67]. Recent studies suggest monitoring minimal residual disease based on circulating tumor DNA [68–70], combined with immune profiling specific to transplant recipients [71], may allow for the establishment of recurrence-rejection risk prediction models, guiding individualized sequential treatment.

Management strategies for irAEs must go beyond simple adjustments in immunosuppressive intensity. Recent evidence suggests that the occurrence of irAEs is closely linked to gut microbiota disruption [72], with specific microbiota (e.g., Faecalibacterium prausnitzii) maintaining gut barrier integrity [73]. A decrease in its abundance may be associated with the formation of a pro-inflammatory immune environment and the occurrence of immune-mediated colitis [74]. This indicates that fecal microbiota transplantation or cytokine intervention could potentially replace part of the immunosuppressive treatment. Furthermore, the exploration of localized therapies targeting tissue-resident memory T-cells, such as inhaled JAK inhibitors, in ICI-related pneumonia [75, 76], demonstrates a shift from systemic suppression to local regulation.

Future research should focus on establishing a dynamic monitoring system for peripheral immunity in transplant recipients and developing novel immune modulators that effectively balance immunosuppressive intensity with tumor recurrence risk. There is a pressing need to address the mechanisms underlying immunotherapy resistance. Increasing funding for clinical research, and establishing standardized protocols for managing immunotherapy are crucial.

Limitations

While WoSCC is a comprehensive and widely recognized source for bibliometric research, its focus primarily on English-language publications may limit the scope of included studies. Many important contributions to cancer and immunosuppressant research may be published in other languages, potentially excluding critical data from regions with strong scientific outputs, such as China, Russia, and other non-English-speaking countries. This linguistic bias could lead to an incomplete representation of the global research landscape, skewing trends and potentially overlooking valuable insights from these regions. Future studies could benefit from incorporating additional databases such as Scopus, PubMed, or Google Scholar, which provide a broader array of publications, including those in non-English languages, thus enhancing the comprehensiveness of the analysis.

Furthermore, the search strategy employed, which focused exclusively on articles published in English between 2014 and 2024, also presents certain challenges. While this time frame allows for the inclusion of the most recent and relevant literature, it excludes studies that may have had a significant impact prior to 2014. Additionally, recent delays in database updates and the exclusion of conference papers and non-peer-reviewed sources may have resulted in missing emerging trends and cutting-edge research. The exclusion of conference abstracts and unpublished studies could be especially impactful in rapidly evolving fields like cancer immunotherapy, where early-stage findings and preclinical data may not yet be fully published in peer-reviewed journals.

Finally, while this study provides valuable insights into the current state of research, its focus on quantitative data may overlook important qualitative aspects of the research landscape. Future research could integrate qualitative methodologies, such as expert interviews or in-depth case studies, to better capture the nuances of research development, including the challenges researchers face in translating basic science into clinical applications.

In conclusion, while the search strategy and data sources used in this study provide valuable insights into the trends and hotspots in cancer and immunosuppressant research, there are inherent limitations that must be acknowledged. A more inclusive approach, incorporating additional databases, language sources, and publication types, could provide a more comprehensive and globally representative analysis. Addressing these limitations in future studies will be crucial for gaining a deeper understanding of the evolving landscape of cancer therapy and immunosuppressant research.

Conclusion

This bibliometric analysis offers valuable insights into the research landscape at the intersection of cancer and immunosuppressants, showcasing the increasing publication and citation rates, particularly from the United States and China. The findings emphasize the importance of translational research and interdisciplinary collaboration. While significant progress has been made with ICIs and their associated irAEs, challenges remain in optimizing immunotherapy, overcoming drug resistance, and minimizing side effects. Future research should focus on refining treatment protocols to enhance efficacy and reduce toxicity, identifying new biomarkers for personalized treatment, addressing resistance mechanisms to improve long-term outcomes, and fostering global collaborations to expedite clinical translations of preclinical insights. By advancing these areas, researchers can continue to enhance cancer immunotherapy and improve patient outcomes through more effective, targeted therapeutic strategies.

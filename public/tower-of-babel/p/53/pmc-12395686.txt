Evaluation of adherence to the patients’ bill of rights charter in pharmacies in Iran: a descriptive-correlational study
Authors: Saeed Biroudian, Khalil Jabari, Pardis Mirzaei, Saman Eskandari, Davoud Roostaei, Kourosh Delpasand
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAdherence to the patients’ Bill of rights charter is crucial in ensuring ethical and equitable healthcare delivery, fostering trust between patients and healthcare providers. The patients’ Bill of rights charter provides a framework for defending patients’ rights in various situations and addressing their needs without discrimination. However, evidence regarding this issue within Iran’s healthcare system appears to be ambiguous and contradictory. Therefore, the present study was conducted to evaluate adherence to the patients’ Bill of rights in pharmacies in Rasht, the largest and most populated city in northern Iran.MethodsThis descriptive-correlational study was approved by the research committee of Guilan university of medical sciences and was conducted on 437 individuals visiting pharmacies in Rasht during the years 2021–2022. Data were collected using a convenience sampling method, employing a demographic information form and a questionnaire on adherence to the patients’ Bill of rights charter. Data analysis was performed with SPSS version 26.ResultsThe mean score for adherence to the patients’ Bill of rights charter in pharmacies was 34.16 ± 11.22. The majority of participants were female (72.5%) and aged between 25 and 31 years (29.7%). The results indicated that the mean score for adherence to the Patients’ Bill of Rights Charter in pharmacies was 34.16 ± 11.22. The results of the one-way ANOVA test indicated a significant association between age and educational level with adherence to the Patients’ Rights Charter (p < 0.05). To predict the score for adherence to the Patients’ Rights, demographic variables were included in the regression model. The results indicated that two variables, age and education, could predict the level of adherence to the Patients’ Bill of Rights Charter.ConclusionsAdherence to the Patients’ Rights Charter within Iran’s healthcare system appears to be unsatisfactory. It seems essential for policymakers to enhance patient satisfaction regarding their rights by providing ongoing training to increase staff awareness, developing strong policies for the protection of patients’ rights, and closely monitoring the implementation of these policies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12913-025-13275-7.Keywords: Patients’ rights, Pharmacy, Healthcare providers

Introduction

The patients’ Bill of Rights is a significant issue in the context of healthcare service standards worldwide and patients’ rights encompass the necessity of receiving standard treatment, fair and ethical care, considers various physical, psychological, spiritual, and social dimensions [1]. In other words, the patients’ Bill of rights charter provides a framework for defending patients’ rights in various situations and addressing their needs without discrimination, while ensuring respect is upheld [2].

The fundamental concept of the patients’ Bill of rights traces back to the united nations declaration in 1948 [3, 4]. However, in Iran, it was first developed and announced by the ministry of health, treatment, and medical education in the early 2000s. The patients’ bill of rights charter in Iran outlines five key rights: the right to receive qualified healthcare services, the right to receive appropriate and comprehensive information, the right to make autonomous decisions regarding treatment services, the right to privacy, and the right to have complaints addressed [5].

Among the various healthcare providers, adherence to the patients’ Bill of rights by pharmacists is particularly important due to the unique nature of their profession [6]. According to the American Pharmacists Association, clinical pharmacists are specialists who assist patients in making the best possible use of medications [7]. In line with technological advancements, providing essential pharmacological care along with medication distribution has become necessary for pharmacist [8]. The Patients’ Bill of Rights in pharmacies emphasizes the need for patients to receive comprehensive and accurate information about their medications, potential side effects, and drug interactions, as well as the protection of their privacy during specialized consultations with clinical pharmacists [9].

Upholding patients’ rights increases satisfaction with the quality of healthcare services, improved adherence to treatment plans, enhanced treatment effectiveness, and ultimately reduced costs [8, 10]. Conversely, failure to uphold these rights can result in feelings of distrust among patients, increased dissatisfaction and complaint rates, and ultimately, a decline in the quality of services provided [11]. Generally, adherence to the Patients’ Bill of Rights, combine with effective communication and active participation between pharmacists and patients, is essential for an efficient healthcare system [12].

Despite the importance of this issue, various studies have shown that in Iran’s healthcare system, attention to ethical codes, including the Patients’ Bill of Rights, is often lacking [13–15]. Reasons for non-compliance include high workloads in various healthcare units, understaffing, and a lack of awareness among healthcare providers about these rights [5]. Most studies conducted in Iran focus on assessing adherence to the Patients’ Bill of Rights in hospitals from the perspective of healthcare providers. Consequently, precise information on this topic in other clinical settings, particularly pharmacies, is still lacking [16]. While numerous studies [11, 17–21] have evaluated adherence to the Patients’ Bill of Rights in hospitals, very few have focused on pharmacies—despite their critical role as the final point of care where patients often receive essential health information and services. Pharmacists play a frontline role in ensuring medication safety, educating patients about proper use, and respecting privacy during consultations. Compared to hospitals, where adherence to patients’ rights has been more extensively studied and institutional oversight is more structured, community pharmacies represent a relatively under-explored yet highly impactful setting for evaluating patients’ rights, particularly given their accessibility and frequency of patient interactions.

Since improvement in any area requires a thorough understanding of the current situation, evaluating adherence to the Patients’ Bill of Rights in pharmacies from the patients’ perspective can serve as a foundation for implementing corrective measures and enhancing the situation.

Therefore, this study was conducted to evaluate the extent to which community pharmacies in Rasht, Iran, adhere to the Patients’ Bill of Rights Charter, as perceived by patients.

Specifically, we sought to answer the following research question:

To what extent do patients perceive that pharmacies in Rasht adhere to the key principles of the Patients’ Bill of Rights Charter, and how are these perceptions associated with demographic factors such as age, gender, education, insurance coverage, and pharmacy type?

Based on previous findings, we hypothesized that higher educational attainment and younger age would be associated with greater perceived adherence to patients’ rights in pharmacies.Therefore, this study was conducted to assess adherence to the Patients’ Bill of Rights in pharmacies in Rasht, the most populated and largest city in northern Iran.

Materials and methods

This research is a descriptive-correlational study conducted in Rasht from 2021 to 2022. The research population included all clients of the pharmacies affiliated with Guilan University of Medical Sciences. Assuming a 46% adherence to the Patients’ Rights Charter in healthcare centers [22], with a Type I error (alpha error) of 0.05 and a margin error (d) of 0.05, the sample size was estimated using the formula , resulting in 382 individuals. Considering a potential 15% potential dropout rate, the final sample size increased to 439 participants. The samples were selected through convenience sampling based on the study’s inclusion criteria.

Data collection was conducted using a demographic information form, which included fields for age, gender, education, insurance coverage, and type of pharmacy visited, along with the questionnaire on adherence to the Patients’ Bill of Rights Charter. The Patients’ Bill of Rights Charter Adherence Questionnaire is a researcher-developed scale consisting of 13 items, scored on a five-point Likert scale ranging from one (very low) to five (very high). The total score is calculated by summing the points for each item, resulting in scores ranging from 13 to 65, with higher scores indicating greater adherence to patients’ rights.

To assess content validity, the questionnaire was reviewed by seven experts in the field. The Content Validity Ratio (CVR) was calculated for each item, and items with a CVR of less than 0.7 were removed. As a result, one item was removed, leaving 13 items in the questionnaire. In evaluating the Content Validity Index (CVI) for relevance and clarity, all items had a CVI above 0.8, with an overall CVI calculated at 0.931. The reliability of the questionnaire was assessed through a pilot study involving 35 clients visiting pharmacies in Rasht, with reliability calculated at 0.83 using Cronbach’s alpha.

The questionnaire link was distributed to pharmacy clients in Rasht via social media platforms such as WhatsApp, Telegram, Instagram, email, and LinkedIn, and data collection continued until the required sample size was reached. All participants were informed about the confidentiality of their information and the voluntary nature of their participation. Notably, returning and completing the questionnaire was considered as providing informed consent to participate in the study. After achieving the required sample size, data were exported from the Porsline platform as an Excel file. The data were then coded and entered into SPSS software, version 26.

Data analysis included descriptive statistics (mean, standard deviation, frequency, and percentage). To predict the adherence score to the Patients’ Rights Charter, demographic variables were entered into a linear regression model. Before conducting the regression, key assumptions were tested: normality of residuals was assessed using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test and Q–Q plots; multicollinearity was examined by calculating the Variance Inflation Factor (VIF) for each independent variable, with all values falling below the threshold of 10; and homoscedasticity was evaluated using a scatterplot of standardized residuals versus predicted values.

Although individual Likert items are ordinal, the total score derived from the 13-item scale was treated as an interval-level variable, consistent with psychometric research supporting the use of summed Likert scales for parametric statistical analysis when reliability is high (Cronbach’s α = 0.83 in this study) [23]. A significance level of p < 0.05 was considered for all analyses.

Discussion

The patients’ rights charters in pharmacies entails the right to receive quality services, sufficient information, privacy protection, confidentiality of information, and the right to file complaints and suggestions. Access to these rights is considered essential for receiving optimal healthcare services [2]. In general, patients’ rights charters serve as guidelines aimed at the interaction between professionals and patients, establishing standards that patients have the right to receive and demand as fundamental human rights [11, 24]. Adherence to the patients’ rights charter can lead to increased satisfaction with the quality of healthcare services and greater compliance with treatment plans [25]. Given the importance of this issue, the present study was conducted to assess the level of adherence to the Patients’ Rights Charter for the first time in pharmacies in Rasht, the most populated and largest city in northern Iran. The questionnaire used in this study was a researcher-developed scale consisting of 13 items, and its reliability was assessed through a pilot study.

The level of adherence to the patients’ rights charter in pharmacies in Rasht was calculated to be 34.16 ± 11.27. Based on the scoring method used by the researcher, this level of adherence is considered average from the patients’ perspectives. Various studies have reported different results in this regard [11, 18, 19, 26]. For example, the study by Rafati et al. also reported an average level of adherence to the Patients’ Rights Charter among hospitalized patients in hospitals affiliated with Kerman University of Medical Sciences [26]. In contrast, Fazeli et al. reported a satisfactory level of adherence to this important concept [19]. The differences in the obtained results can be attributed to various factors, including the study population, the study environment, the workload of the professionals, and the level of awareness among patients in different communities. Increasing the awareness of pharmacists regarding the importance of adhering to the patients’ rights charter, developing comprehensible educational materials for patients, and disseminating information through mass media are essential for improving the current situation.

The highest score calculated from the various items in the questionnaire was related to item 6 (“Do you trust the pharmacist’s responses in addressing your pharmaceutical needs?“), indicating a significant level of trust among patients in the information received from pharmacists [27]. The lowest score was associated with item 13 (“Does the pharmacist explain important side effects of the medication based on the information provided in the patient leaflet (brochure)?“). In other words, most patients believe that pharmacists do not provide comprehensive information about the side effects of medications and their potential interactions, despite patients’ trust in their recommendations. This finding aligns with the results of the study by Safaei et al. [25].

Data analysis revealed a significant correlation between age and education level with adherence to the Patients’ Rights Charter. In other words, adherence to the Patients’ Rights Charter is significantly higher among younger and more educated patients compared to other groups. The low level of adherence among elderly patients can be attributed to their lack of awareness of their rights and their reduced use of social media [21]. In line with the results of several studies, greater awareness among young and educated individuals may lead to heightened sensitivity and expectations regarding adherence to their rights in various healthcare situations [28, 29]. There was no significant correlation between gender, having or not having health insurance coverage, or the type of pharmacy visited with adherence to the Patients’ Rights Charter.

The findings of this study highlight several opportunities for improving practice in Iranian community pharmacies. The low scores in areas such as patient education on side effects, drug interactions, and medication use indicate a need for systematic pharmacist training focused on patient-centered communication and medication counseling. National pharmacy associations and universities could develop continuing education programs emphasizing ethical obligations and the practical implementation of the Patients’ Bill of Rights Charter. Moreover, standardized protocols and visual aids in pharmacies (e.g., brochures, posters) could help reinforce patients’ rights and pharmacists’ responsibilities. Implementing routine monitoring and feedback mechanisms, such as anonymous patient surveys, would also encourage accountability and continuous improvement. These changes can collectively enhance trust, adherence, and patient satisfaction in pharmacy services.

This study had several limitations. First, the study used convenience sampling of pharmacy visitors in Rasht, which may introduce selection bias and limit the generalizability of the findings to other regions or populations. second, the study was conducted in pharmacies affiliated with a single university in Rasht, and this study’s cross-sectional design poses several inherent limitations. Since data were collected at a single point in time, causal relationships between patient demographics and perceived adherence to the Patients’ Bill of Rights cannot be inferred. Additionally, temporal trends or changes in perceptions over time—such as those influenced by public policy, education campaigns, or evolving pharmacy practices—could not be captured. A longitudinal design, following patients’ experiences over time or before and after targeted interventions (e.g., pharmacist training programs), would allow for more robust evaluation of changes in adherence levels and the potential impact of policy reforms or educational efforts. Future studies using longitudinal or quasi-experimental designs could thus provide stronger evidence for causality and the effectiveness of interventions aimed at improving respect for patients’ rights. Additionally, this study may be subject to certain response biases. Since data were collected through self-reported questionnaires, there is a possibility of social desirability bias, where participants may have provided more favorable responses to align with perceived expectations. Likewise, recall bias could have influenced the accuracy of the participants’ recollections regarding their most recent interactions with pharmacists, particularly if their pharmacy visit occurred some time before completing the questionnaire. These biases may have led to either overestimation or underestimation of the actual level of adherence to the Patients’ Rights Charter.

Moreover, data on participants’ urban/rural residence and socio-economic status were not collected, which limits our ability to assess potential disparities in access or perception of rights across different geographic and economic groups. Future studies should incorporate these variables for a more comprehensive understanding.

Conclusion

The level of adherence to the Patients’ Rights Charter in pharmacies in Rasht appears to be unsatisfactory. However, adherence to the Patients’ Rights Charter in pharmacies can play a very effective role in improving the treatment process. One of the major concerns and worries of patients is the details related to the protection of their privacy, which is partially addressed through adherence to the Patients’ Rights Charter. The present study revealed that, despite the significant importance of this issue, pharmacists often do not pay adequate attention. Pharmacies, as one of the busiest healthcare units, must take effective measures to improve the current situation. Accountable managers and policymakers should enhance patient satisfaction regarding their rights by providing ongoing training to increase staff awareness, developing strong policies for the protection of patients’ rights, and closely monitoring the implementation of these policies. Additionally, they should prioritize fair and timely handling of complaints and patient dissatisfaction, as neglecting patients’ demands and requests can lead to their dissatisfaction and a decrease in the quality of healthcare services. Pharmacies are the final station and link in the provision of health services. If patients are confident that their rights are being respected, they are likely to cooperate fully with the pharmacist and the treatment team. This, in turn, helps prevent the waste of healthcare resources and promotes individuals’ right to health within the community.

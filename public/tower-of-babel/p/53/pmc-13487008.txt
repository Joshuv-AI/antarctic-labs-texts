Development of mogamulizumab-associated rash with granuloma annulare-like features in a patient with Sezary syndrome: A case report and review of the literature
Authors: Jenna K Currier, Jessica Ghaferi, George J Murakawa
Published: 2026

Introduction

Mogamulizumab is a monoclonal antibody targeting chemokine receptor 4 approved for the treatment of relapsed or refractory Sézary syndrome (SS) and mycosis fungoides (MF), with a demonstrated overall response rate of 28% in the phase III MAVORIC trial.1 One of its most common adverse effects is mogamulizumab-associated rash (MAR), which encompasses a spectrum of clinicopathologic patterns, including spongiotic or psoriasiform dermatitis, interface dermatitis, and less commonly, granulomatous dermatitis.2, 3, 4, 5 We report a case of mogamulizumab-associated granulomatous dermatitis exhibiting a granuloma annulare (GA)–like pattern, expanding the recognized histologic spectrum of MAR.

Case report

A 67-year-old male with SS treated with mogamulizumab for 16 months presented with new-onset generalized erythematous waxy, red-brown nodules localized to the bilateral shoulders and paraspinal lumbar region at L3 (Fig 1, A and B). The patient reported worsening pruritus and skin bleeding. Differential diagnoses included cutaneous lymphoid hyperplasia, sarcoidosis, drug-induced dermatitis, and generalized GA.

A-D, Clinical course of mogamulizumab-associated rash (MAR) with granuloma annulare (GA)-like features. A and B, Initial presentation 16 months after starting mogamulizumab, showing erythematous, waxy red-brown nodules on the lower back (L3) and shoulders. C and D, Four months later, new lesions appeared on the forearms as salmon-pink patches, papules, and plaques with silvery-white scale; biopsy confirmed recurrence of the same diagnosis.

Skin biopsy demonstrated palisaded and interstitial granulomatous dermatitis composed of perivascular lymphocytes, scattered eosinophils, and some palisaded histocytes containing increased dermal mucin, highlighted by colloidal iron staining (Fig 2, A and B). No atypical lymphocytes were identified. Immunohistochemical studies were not performed on the skin specimen; however, concurrent peripheral blood evaluation revealed minimal residual SS involvement, with a small aberrant T-cell population dimly positive for CD2, CD3, CD5, and CD7, and negative for CD4, CD8, and T-cell receptor γ/δ. T-cell receptor gene rearrangement analysis confirmed clonality. The majority of circulating T cells (15% of total leukocytes) demonstrated a CD4:CD8 ratio of 6:1, markedly improved from the patient’s baseline disease status, which was characterized by generalized erythroderma and a CD4:CD8 ratio >10:1. Prior skin biopsies had not shown granulomatous features.

A and B, Histopathology of mogamulizumab-associated rash (MAR) with granuloma annulare (GA)-like features. A, Hematoxylin and eosin staining showing palisaded and interstitial granulomatous dermatitis with perivascular lymphocytes and interstitial histiocytes in the superficial reticular dermis. B, Colloidal iron staining highlighting interstitial mucin within areas of reduced cellularity surrounded by palisading histiocytes. These findings are consistent with MAR with GA-like features.

Based on the presence of palisaded granulomatous dermatitis with interstitial mucin deposition, a diagnosis of MAR with a GA-like pattern was made. The patient declined systemic immunomodulatory therapy and was treated with a monthly antibiotic regimen consisting of doxycycline (200 mg), ciprofloxacin (500 mg), and rifampin (600 mg), in addition to topical triamcinolone. This regimen was selected based on prior reported success in treatment-resistant GA,6 favorable clinical experience in our practice, and patient preference to avoid systemic immunosuppression.

Four months later the patient significantly flared and developed extensive, confluent new lesions on both forearms, appearing as pink patches, papules and/or plaques with scale (Fig 1, C and D). Repeat biopsy again confirmed granulomatous MAR with GA-like features. The patient declined escalation of therapy and continued the existing regimen.

Mogamulizumab was discontinued 8 months after the diagnosis of granulomatous MAR with GA-like features. Within 6 weeks of drug cessation, approximately 60% of lesions resolved spontaneously, with complete clinical clearance achieved by 4 months.

Proposed pathophysiology

•MAR is thought to result from chemokine receptor 4-mediated regulatory T-cell depletion, resulting in immune dysfunction and a broad spectrum of histologic patterns.13

•In granulomatous MAR, loss of regulatory T-cell-mediated immune suppression is hypothesized to augment Th1 activation and macrophage recruitment.13

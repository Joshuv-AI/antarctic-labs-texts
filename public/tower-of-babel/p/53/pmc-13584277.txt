YS-1301 ameliorates crescentic glomerulonephritis by promoting immunoregulatory macrophages

Background

Anti–glomerular basement membrane (GBM) glomerulonephritis (GN) is a severe form of GN with crescent formation (crescentic GN) that rapidly progresses to renal failure after disease onset [1]. This is characteristic of Goodpasture’s disease in humans, where patients may also develop pulmonary hemorrhage with severe outcomes. Although effector responses by neutrophils, macrophages/monocytes, T lymphocytes, and immune regulation by regulatory T cells and alternatively activated macrophages have been extensively investigated as part of efforts to understand the distinct mechanisms that cause glomerular crescent formation [2–8], no currently available therapy effectively balances suppression of pathogenic leukocyte activity with enhancement of immunoregulatory functions.

Prostacyclin exerts vasoprotective effects—including inhibition of platelet aggregation, vasodilation, and antiproliferative actions—via the prostacyclin receptor and nuclear receptor PPARδ activation [9, 10]. In contrast, thromboxane A₂ (TXA₂) promotes platelet aggregation and vasoconstriction [10]. YS-1301 is a synthetic, non-prostanoid prostacyclin receptor agonist that also inhibits TXA₂ synthase [11]. YS-1301 was originally developed by Ono Pharmaceutical as an antiplatelet agent and was designated ONO-1301 during development.

Although originally developed as an oral antiplatelet agent, YS-1301’s clinical development was discontinued because of vasodilatory side effects and limited advantages over existing therapies. These side effects are associated with oral administration and are less likely with intravenous administration. YS-1301 stimulates fibroblasts to release regenerative factors such as hepatocyte growth factor (HGF), vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), and stromal cell–derived factor 1 (SDF-1) [12]. Beneficial effects of YS-1301 have been documented across experimental models of pulmonary hypertension, pulmonary fibrosis, and myocardial ischemia [13–15]. In a rat anti-GBM GN model, twice-daily intravenous YS-1301 administration reduces intraglomerular leukocyte infiltration and renal injury, although the underlying immunological mechanisms remain unclear [16].

We have long investigated mesenchymal stromal cell (MSC) therapy for GN [17]. These studies show that MSCs exert therapeutic effects partly mediated via prostaglandin E₂-dependent induction of immunoregulatory M2 macrophages [18]. Given that YS-1301 may share mechanisms with MSC therapy via prostanoid-mediated modulation of macrophage phenotypes, we hypothesized that YS-1301 promotes immunoregulatory macrophage responses during glomerular injury. Thus, this study investigated whether YS-1301 ameliorates anti-GBM GN via immunomodulation.

Methods

Animals

WKY/NCrj rats were purchased from Charles River, Inc. (Yokohama, Japan). Rats were maintained on a 12-hour light/dark cycle with free access to standard diet and water. Eight-week-old female rats were used for the nephritis model.

All experimental procedures were performed under inhalation anesthesia with 3% isoflurane.

At the time of euthanasia, rats were deeply anesthetized and sacrificed by cervical dislocation. Death was confirmed by the absence of cardiac and respiratory activity.

All animal experimental protocols were conducted in accordance with the guidelines of the Institutional Animal Ethics Committee of Nagoya University Graduate School of Medicine (Approval number 240128).

Preparation of YS-1301

YS-1301 was synthesized by ONO Pharmaceutical Co. (Osaka, Japan) as previously described [14, 16, 19], and prepared at a dose of 10 mg/kg/day.

Induction of GN and YS-1301 treatment

Rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis was induced in WKY/NCrj rats by administering 100 µg/rat of the mouse monoclonal IgG clone TF78, which specifically binds to α4 (IV) NC1 of the rat glomerular basement membrane. YS-1301 (10 mg/kg) was administered intravenously on days 0, 1, and 2 after induction, and control rats received saline. On day 3, blood and urine were collected to measure blood urea nitrogen (BUN), serum creatinine, and proteinuria (Fig. 1a). Blood samples were collected by cardiac puncture. Both kidneys were harvested from each rat, fixed in 10% formalin, and subjected to histopathological evaluation.

YS-1301 improves renal function in anti-GBM nephritis. (a) Experimental scheme depicting anti-GBM nephritis induction followed by intravenous YS-1301 administration on days 0, 1, and 2. (b) Renal function parameters at day 3: BUN, serum creatinine, and proteinuria. Data are presented as mean ± SD (n = 11 per group). **p < 0.01 as determined using Welch’s t test

Because pathological IgG induces immediate nephritis in this model, YS-1301 administration on days 0–2 represents an early post-induction intervention rather than treatment of established disease. In addition to the primary day 3 analyses, separate cohorts were evaluated on day 7 to determine whether renoprotection persisted throughout the rapidly progressive GN (RPGN) phase.

BUN and serum creatinine were measured by SRL, Inc. (Tokyo, Japan), and urinary protein was measured by Sanritsu Zelkova, Inc. (Tokyo, Japan).

Assessment of mouse IgG deposition on the rat GBM

Deposition of TF78 on the rat GBM was confirmed using frozen kidney sections stained with FITC-labeled polyclonal rabbit anti-mouse IgG (H+L) (Invitrogen). Fluorescence intensity within glomeruli was quantified by mean fluorescence intensity (MFI) analysis using Imaris software.

Histological assessment

Kidney sections were stained with periodic acid–Schiff (PAS), and each glomerulus was assigned a score based on the percentage of crescentic area: 0, none; 1, 0–25%; 2, 25–50%; 3, 50–75%; and 4, 75–100%. The mean of these values was defined as the crescent score [20]. Immunohistochemistry for CD68 (pan-macrophage/monocyte), CD163, and CD206 was performed on formalin-fixed tissue [2]. Thirty glomeruli were evaluated per rat.

Flow cytometry

Tissue samples were cut into small fragments with a razor and suspended in media containing 1 mg/mL collagenase type I (Worthington Biochemical Corporation, Lakewood, NJ, USA) and 1 mg/mL DNase I (Merck, Darmstadt, Germany). Samples in the dissociation solution were incubated at 37 °C with gentle agitation for 30 min. Thereafter, tissue fragments were passed several times through an 18-gauge needle using 1 mL syringes and filtered through a 100 μm strainer. The collagenase reaction was terminated by adding phosphate-buffered saline with 5 mM ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (Thermo Fisher Scientific, MA, USA). Isolated cells were pelleted at 400 × g at 4 °C. Red blood cells in samples were lysed using BD Pharm Lyse (BD, San Jose, CA, USA). Cells were blocked with anti-CD32 antibody (1:50; BD, 550,271) and stained with fluorochrome-conjugated antibodies (Table 1). Lymphocyte and myeloid subsets were gated as described (Supplementary Fig. 1) [18] and analyzed using FlowJo v10 (BD Biosciences, Tokyo, Japan). Monocyte/macrophage subsets were classified according to differential expression of CD43 and His48. CD43Lo/His48Hi cells defined as inflammatory monocyte/macrophages corresponding to an M1-like phenotype, whereas CD43Hi/His48Lo cells defined as immunoregulatory/pro-resolving monocyte/macrophages corresponding to an M2-like phenotype. These subsets are analogous to classical Ly6C Hi and non-classical Ly6C Lo monocytes, respectively [21].

Statistical analyses

Data from independent experiments were analyzed using GraphPad Prism v9 (GraphPad Software). Group comparisons were performed using Welch’s t test, with p < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

Results

YS-1301 improves renal function in rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis

In this nephritis model, administering pathological IgG without pre-immunization immediately induced glomerulonephritis. Accordingly, treatment was initiated immediately after antibody administration and therapeutic efficacy was assessed thereafter. In anti-GBM GN rats, YS-1301 treatment markedly preserved renal function. By day 3, YS-1301–treated rats showed significantly lower BUN, serum creatinine, and proteinuria than controls (BUN: p = 0.0085; creatinine: p = 0.0089; proteinuria: p = 0.0015; individual comparisons) (Fig. 1b).

To address whether YS-1301 affected the initial deposition of anti-GBM antibodies, we evaluated glomerular deposition of injected antibodies by immunofluorescence staining using anti-mouse IgG. Quantitative analysis using MFI demonstrated that glomerular IgG deposition was comparable between YS-1301–treated and control groups at day 3, indicating that YS-1301 did not affect initial antibody deposition (Fig. 2).

YS-1301 does not affect initial glomerular deposition of anti-GBM antibodies. Representative immunofluorescence images of mouse IgG deposition along the glomerular basement membrane on day 3 after disease induction (left). Quantitative analysis of glomerular mouse IgG deposition was performed using MFI analysis (right). Glomerular antibody deposition was comparable between the control and YS-1301-treated groups. Data are presented as the mean ± SD (n = 6 per group). NS, not significant, as determined using Welch’s t test

YS-1301 attenuates crescent formation and increases glomerular immunoregulatory macrophages

Crescent formation in the glomeruli is the characteristic pathological feature of this disease; thus, the therapeutic effect of YS-1301 was evaluated pathologically. Histological examination demonstrated a marked attenuation of glomerular crescent formation in YS-1301-treated rats (1.12 vs. 0.50, p < 0.0001) (Fig. 3).

YS-1301 reduces glomerular crescent formation. Images of kidney sections stained with PAS on day 3 (left), and glomerular injury scoring based on crescent formation on day 3 (right). Data are presented as mean ± SD (n = 6 per group). ****p < 0.0001 as determined using Welch’s t test

Monocytes, among the earliest leukocyte subsets to adhere to deposited autoantibodies, promote glomerular injury [22]. Therefore, glomerular monocytes and macrophages were evaluated. Infiltrating monocytes and macrophages were identified as CD68+ cells, whereas immunoregulatory/pro-resolving monocyte/macrophage populations were assessed using CD163 and CD206. The number of total infiltrating monocytes and macrophages (glomerular CD68+ cells) was comparable between the two groups (p = 0.63) (Fig. 4a). In contrast, YS-1301 significantly increased glomerular CD163+ cells (1.88 vs. 4.61, p < 0.001) (Fig. 4b) and glomerular CD206+ cells (1.70 vs. 4.13, p < 0.0001) (Fig. 4c).

YS-1301 promotes accumulation of glomerular immunoregulatory/pro-resolving monocyte/macrophage populations. Representative micrographs of glomeruli stained with specific monoclonal antibodies for rat CD68 (a), CD163 (b), and CD206 (c) on day 3 after disease induction. Accumulation of CD68+, CD163+, and CD206+ cells per glomerulus at day 3 was also determined. Data are presented as the mean ± SD (n = 6 per group). ***p < 0.001, ****p < 0.0001 as determined using Welch’s t test

Thus, YS-1301 did not alter overall leukocyte infiltration but promoted a localized immunoregulatory macrophage response within inflamed glomeruli, accompanied by reduced proteinuria and preservation of renal function.

Flow cytometry analysis of renal leukocytes

To investigate YS-1301-induced changes in renal leukocyte populations in the anti-GBM GN model, all leukocyte subsets were analyzed using flow cytometry. To visualize renal leukocyte subset changes caused by YS-1301 administration, flow cytometry results were displayed using t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding (tSNE) (Fig. 5a, b). Analysis of leukocyte composition showed enrichment of CD43Hi/His48Lo immunoregulatory/pro-resolving monocyte/macrophage populations in YS-1301-treated rats by day 3 (15.3% vs. 22.1%, p < 0.01) (Fig. 5c). In contrast, the absolute numbers of total renal leukocytes (CD45+ cells), neutrophils, and macrophages were comparable between the two groups (Fig. 5d). This early increase in the proportion of immunoregulatory/pro-resolving myeloid cells indicated that YS-1301 was associated with a rapid shift in the renal immune environment toward an immunoregulatory state, mitigating tissue injury.

To determine whether the renoprotective effect extended beyond the acute day 3 window, we evaluated separate cohorts on day 7, the latest time point within the RPGN phase of this model. YS-1301-treated rats continued to show lower BUN, serum creatinine, and proteinuria than controls (Supplementary Fig. 2)), together with sustained attenuation of crescent formation (Supplementary Fig. 3)), indicating that protection was not limited to a transient early shift.

Discussion

In a rat model of crescentic GN, YS-1301 significantly improved renal function and attenuated glomerular injury without significantly reducing overall macrophage infiltration. Instead, YS-1301 was associated with increased accumulation of glomerular CD163+ and CD206+ immunoregulatory/pro-resolving monocyte/macrophage populations in treated kidneys. Consistently, flow cytometry confirmed a higher proportion of immunoregulatory/pro-resolving myeloid cells in YS-1301–treated rats. Thus, YS-1301 appears to promote a shift in infiltrating immune cells toward a pro-resolving phenotype rather than broadly inhibiting leukocyte recruitment. This targeted immunomodulation contrasts with traditional immunosuppressants, suggesting that YS-1301 mitigates harmful inflammation while preserving reparative immune functions.

Because YS-1301 was administered concurrently with anti-GBM antibody injection, there was a possibility that the compound could influence the early immune response or antibody deposition. However, immunofluorescence analysis demonstrated comparable glomerular deposition of anti-GBM antibodies between treated and control groups. These findings indicate that the renoprotective effects of YS-1301 are not due to altered antibody deposition but are more likely mediated through modulation of downstream inflammatory responses, including changes in macrophage inflammatory states.

Our results align with previous findings of the related analog ONO-1301 in kidney disease models; for example, in an anti-GBM nephritis model, ONO-1301 suppresses glomerular VCAM-1 expression, reducing VLA-4+ leukocyte recruitment, crescent formation, and renal dysfunction [16]. In contrast, YS-1301 did not significantly reduce macrophage infiltration; however, it was associated with increased accumulation of glomerular CD163+ and CD206+ immunoregulatory/pro-resolving monocyte/macrophage populations. Collectively, prostacyclin analogs may confer renoprotection via multiple complementary mechanisms involving reduced leukocyte infiltration and modulation of immune-cell phenotypes.

Recent mechanistic work using the pharmacologically related compound ONO-1301 supports this interpretation. Motegi et al. showed that ONO-1301 increased intracellular cAMP and suppressed LPS-induced inflammatory macrophage programs, including Mcp1, Tnfa, and Inos, whereas M2 markers such as IL10 and CD206 were not broadly induced [23]. Accordingly, our findings are better interpreted as a localized pro-resolving shift in macrophage inflammatory state than as generalized kidney-wide M2 polarization.

Compartment-specific macrophage heterogeneity is also relevant. In active ANCA-associated GN, CD163+ macrophages are enriched in affected glomeruli, whereas CD206+ macrophages are more prominent in the tubulointerstitial compartment [24]. Although CD206+ macrophages are not typically considered glomerulus-dominant, YS-1301 increased both glomerular CD163+ and CD206+ cells in our model, suggesting that local inflammatory cues may promote accumulation of multiple immunoregulatory macrophage subsets within injured glomeruli.

Previous studies have shown that YS-1301 suppresses inflammation while activating endogenous regenerative pathways. Mechanistically, YS-1301 elevates intracellular cAMP, upregulating multiple tissue-protective factors (HGF, VEGF, SDF-1) in vitro [12]. These factors exert cytoprotective and pro-reparative effects: HGF is anti-apoptotic and mitogenic for epithelial and endothelial cells; VEGF maintains microvascular integrity; and SDF-1 recruits progenitor/stem cells.

YS-1301 has also demonstrated renoprotective effects in chronic kidney disease models, including diabetic nephropathy and obstructive nephropathy, where it reduced fibrosis and inflammatory injury in association with increased HGF expression [25, 26]. These findings suggest that, in addition to immunomodulation, regenerative pathways may also contribute to the tissue-protective effects of YS-1301.

Importantly, additional analyses at day 7 demonstrated that the renoprotective effects of YS-1301 were sustained, as evidenced by continued improvements in renal function and reduced crescent formation. In this model, the disease represents RPGN up to day 7 after induction, whereas it transitions to a nephrotic phenotype at later time points. Therefore, these findings indicate that YS-1301 exerts sustained therapeutic effects throughout the RPGN phase, rather than being limited to the early acute phase.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, because YS-1301 was administered immediately after disease induction, the present study models a very early post-induction intervention rather than treatment of established crescentic GN. Second, although macrophage changes were strongly associated with renoprotection, macrophage depletion or other functional perturbation experiments were not performed. Therefore, the causal contribution of these monocyte/macrophage populations remains inferential. Third, because this study was exploratory in nature, multiple comparison corrections were not applied, and the findings should therefore be interpreted cautiously.

In summary, YS-1301 provided marked renal protection in severe GN by shifting the intrarenal immune response from injury toward repair. This supports an immunomodulatory approach; wherein harmful inflammation is suppressed while regenerative pathways are activated. Such an approach may preserve renal function and promote tissue recovery in rapidly progressive GN.

Conclusions

YS-1301 preserved renal function and attenuated crescent formation in anti-GBM glomerulonephritis when administered during the RPGN phase. This protection was associated with accumulation of glomerular CD163+ and CD206+ immunoregulatory/pro-resolving monocyte/macrophage populations without a reduction in total macrophage burden, supporting YS-1301 as an early immunomodulatory intervention for crescentic GN.

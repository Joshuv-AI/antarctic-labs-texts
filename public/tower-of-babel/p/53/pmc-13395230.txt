A study of positive and negative factors associated with tapering off methadone maintenance treatment
Authors: Kishor Kumar, Bharat S. Shekhawat, Sandhya Yadav, Rohitashava Gothwal, Mohammed A. Rangrez
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background and Aim:Evidence on factors associated with successful methadone tapering in India is limited. This study aims to identify positive and negative factors associated with successful tapering of methadone maintenance treatment (MMT).Materials and Methods:This retrospective study reviewed records of 88 patients undergoing MMT tapering at a tertiary care drug treatment clinic (DTC). Demographic, clinical, withdrawal, motivational, and treatment-related factors associated with taper outcomes were analyzed.Results:Seventeen participants (19.3%) successfully completed tapering without relapse. Successful tapering was associated with lower baseline methadone dose, lower withdrawal severity, greater motivation, and absence of tobacco use. Participants who relapsed after taper completion had significantly higher taper rates than those who remained abstinent (6.4 vs. 3.3 mg/week; P = 0.039). Fear of relapse, withdrawal symptoms, family instability, economic instability, and fear of death were common barriers to tapering.Conclusion:Successful tapering was associated with lower baseline methadone dose, slower taper rate, lower withdrawal severity, greater motivation, and absence of tobacco use.

INTRODUCTION

Opioid dependence remains a major public health challenge in India, affecting approximately 2.1% of the population, with an estimated 0.7% requiring treatment. Besides increasing the risk of medical complications such as hepatitis and HIV among people who inject opioids, it adversely affects family relationships, employment, financial stability, and overall quality of life.[1]

Opioid substitution treatment (OST) with long-acting opioid agonists, including methadone and buprenorphine, is an established treatment for opioid dependence. Although studies from India have demonstrated the effectiveness of buprenorphine maintenance therapy,[23] evidence regarding methadone maintenance treatment (MMT), introduced in India in 2010, remains limited. A multicentric Indian study reported encouraging short-term abstinence following MMT,[4] but evidence regarding successful tapering and discontinuation of methadone is scarce.

International studies have consistently shown that methadone tapering is challenging, with high relapse rates following treatment discontinuation. Factors associated with successful tapering include lower methadone doses, slower taper schedules, psychosocial stability, strong motivation, and sustained abstinence from illicit opioid use. In contrast, fear of withdrawal and relapse are common barriers.[5678] However, these findings largely originate from Western populations and may not be directly applicable to the Indian setting due to differences in sociocultural, economic, and healthcare factors.

The present study aimed to identify positive and negative factors associated with successful methadone tapering among patients receiving MMT in an Indian tertiary care setting. Improved understanding of these factors may facilitate identification of suitable candidates for tapering and inform individualized tapering strategies.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This retrospective study was conducted at the drug treatment clinic (DTC) of the Department of Psychiatry, a tertiary care hospital, after approval from the Institutional Ethics Committee. Records of patients receiving MMT under the drug de-addiction programmed (DDAP) were reviewed.

Patients receiving MMT between February 01 and May 10, 2024 were screened. Among 96 patients shortlisted for methadone tapering, those who had received MMT for at least 6 months and were active at the beginning of the study period were eligible. Eight patients were excluded because of incomplete records or pre-existing major psychiatric illness, resulting in a final sample of 88 participants [Figure 1].

Screening and selection of study participants

Participants underwent methadone tapering according to the National Drug Dependence Treatment Centre (NDDTC), AIIMS guidelines. Dose reduction was individualized based on the current methadone dose, withdrawal symptoms, and patient willingness, with taper rates not exceeding 10 mg/week for doses above 20 mg/day and 5 mg/week for doses of 20 mg/day or lower. Withdrawal symptoms were assessed daily using the clinical opiate withdrawal scale (COWS), and participants received symptomatic treatment or dose escalation when clinically indicated. Motivational counselling was provided throughout the tapering period, and motivation stage, along with self-reported reasons for tapering or not tapering, was documented by the same clinician.

For analysis, participants were classified into two groups: Group 1 (completion group), comprising participants, who completed methadone tapering without self-reported relapse during the study period, and Group 2 (non-completion group), comprising participants who were still tapering, discontinued tapering, or relapsed after taper completion. Relapse was determined by self-report because routine urine drug screening was unavailable.

All time-related variables were converted into total months before statistical analysis. For ease of interpretation, the analyzed results were subsequently expressed in years and months. Data were analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 29.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).[9] Continuous variables were compared using the independent-samples t test, while categorical variables were analyzed using the Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Pearson correlation analysis and bivariate logistic regression were performed to explore factors associated with successful methadone tapering. Bonferroni correction was applied for multiple comparisons where appropriate, whereas Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) correction was used for analyses involving multiple self-reported factors. A two-sided P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

A total of 96 patients receiving MMT were screened, of whom 88 met the eligibility criteria and were included in the analysis [Figure 1]. Seventeen participants (19.3%) successfully completed methadone tapering without relapse during the study period (Group 1), while 71 (80.7%) constituted the non-completion group (Group 2). By the end of the study period, 20 participants had completed tapering, including 3 who subsequently relapsed, 40 were continuing tapering, and 28 had discontinued tapering before completion.

Baseline characteristics of the study participants are summarized in Table 1. Participants in the completion group-initiated tapering at a significantly lower methadone dose than those in the non-completion group (32.9 ± 15.1 vs. 44.7 ± 11.1 mg/day; adjusted P = 0.007). Participants who successfully completed tapering also had a higher proportion of education beyond secondary school (P < 0.001). Although the completion group tended to be younger and had a shorter duration of opioid use, these differences were not statistically significant after adjustment for multiple comparisons.

DISCUSSION

This study explored factors associated with successful methadone tapering in an Indian tertiary care setting. Participants who successfully completed tapering had lower baseline methadone doses, higher educational attainment, lower withdrawal severity, greater motivation to discontinue methadone, and were less likely to use tobacco. They also reported fewer psychosocial barriers, including fear of relapse, family instability, economic instability, withdrawal symptoms, and fear of death.

Methadone tapering remains a challenging process with a substantial risk of relapse. In the present study, participants who relapsed after taper completion tapered at a significantly faster rate than those who remained abstinent, supporting previous evidence that gradual dose reduction is associated with improved outcomes.[101112] Similarly, participants who initiated tapering at lower methadone doses were more likely to complete tapering successfully, consistent with earlier reports demonstrating improved outcomes with lower maintenance doses before taper initiation.[11]

Eklund et al.[13] identified barriers such as a long history of opioid use, co-occurring substance use, and low confidence in abstinence. Similarly, in the current study, psychological and social factors emerged as important determinants of tapering success. Participants who completed tapering were more likely to report a strong desire for complete de-addiction, improved family relationships, and freedom from daily attendance at the treatment center as motivating factors. In contrast, unsuccessful participants more frequently reported fear of relapse, severe withdrawal symptoms, and family or financial instability. These findings emphasize that successful methadone tapering depends not only on pharmacological factors but also on motivation and psychosocial support.

Greater withdrawal severity and comorbid tobacco use were significantly associated with unsuccessful tapering. Although the mechanism underlying this association remains unclear, tobacco use may reflect greater addiction severity or poorer treatment adherence and warrants further investigation. The higher educational attainment observed among participants, who successfully completed tapering may also indicate better treatment engagement; however, baseline differences in age and education between the groups could have influenced treatment outcomes and should be considered while interpreting these findings.

The findings should be interpreted considering several limitations. The retrospective design limited the analysis to routinely recorded clinical data, relapse was based on self-report in the absence of routine urine drug screening, and follow-up was restricted to a 100-day taper period. In addition, the predominantly male study population, and the relatively small number of participants, who successfully completed tapering may limit the generalizability of the findings. Although age and education differed between groups, these were baseline characteristics rather than study outcomes and may have influenced the observed associations.

Despite these limitations, this study provides one of the first Indian reports examining factors associated with methadone tapering. The findings suggest that slower taper schedules, lower baseline methadone doses, effective management of withdrawal symptoms, motivational enhancement, and attention to psychosocial barriers may improve the likelihood of successful methadone tapering. Prospective studies with longer follow-up are required to validate these findings and develop evidence-based tapering protocols.

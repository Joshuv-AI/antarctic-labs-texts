Effects of dietary trace mineral sources on production parameters and intestinal microbiota in broiler breeder hens
Authors: Hao Cheng, WuXiong Liu, Kun Tian, XiangYong Qu, Youming Zhang, Qiang Tu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTrace minerals are essential for maintaining physiological functions in poultry, including antioxidant defense, immune regulation, and reproductive performance. However, the bioavailability of conventional inorganic trace elements is relatively low, and their effects on gut microbiota and reproductive physiology in broiler breeder hens remain insufficiently understood. This study investigated the effects of inorganic trace elements (ITE), methionine hydroxyl analog chelated trace minerals (MTE), and proteinate trace minerals (PTE) on performance, egg quality, antioxidant status, immune responses, reproductive hormones, and cecal microbiota. A total of 432 Hubbard female broiler breeders (50 weeks old) were randomly assigned to 3 treatments with 6 replicates of 24 birds each for 56 days. No significant differences were observed in laying performance or egg quality among treatments (P > 0.05). However, dietary organic trace minerals significantly increased the activities of total superoxide dismutase (T-SOD) and catalase (CAT) and reduced malondialdehyde (MDA) concentrations in plasma and eggs (P < 0.05). MTE and PTE also increased plasma estradiol (E2), progesterone (PROG), and follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), while PTE elevated plasma IgA and IgM levels (P < 0.05). No significant differences were observed in α-diversity indices of the cecal microbiota (P > 0.05). However, β-diversity analysis showed distinct clustering among treatments. LEfSe analysis identified differential microbial taxa, with the PTE group showing higher relative abundances of Lactobacillus and Enterococcus, while the ITE group exhibited higher relative abundance of Bacteroides. Overall, dietary organic trace minerals, particularly proteinate trace minerals, improved antioxidant status, immune function, hormonal profiles, and intestinal microbial structure in female broiler breeders, suggesting their potential advantages over inorganic trace mineral supplementation.Keywords: Organic trace minerals, Antioxidant activity, Immune function, Gut microbiota, Broiler breeders

Introduction

Trace minerals are essential nutrients involved in numerous physiological processes in poultry, including growth, reproduction, immune regulation, and antioxidant defense (Umar Yaqoob, et al., 2020; Wang, et al., 2019). Traditionally, inorganic trace minerals such as oxides, chlorides, and sulfates have been widely used in poultry diets to meet mineral requirements (Jigyasha, et al., 2022). However, the bioavailability of inorganic trace minerals may be limited because they can form insoluble complexes in the gastrointestinal tract under varying pH conditions, thereby reducing absorption efficiency (Byrne, et al., 2022). In addition, excessive supplementation of inorganic minerals may increase mineral excretion and contribute to environmental pollution (Zhang, et al., 2021).

To overcome these limitations, organic trace minerals, including amino acid chelates and proteinate minerals, have been increasingly used in poultry nutrition due to their improved stability, bioavailability, and absorption efficiency (Ghasemi, et al., 2022; Noaman, et al., 2025). Previous studies have shown that organic trace minerals can enhance eggshell quality, antioxidant capacity, and immune responses in laying hens (Qiu et al., 2020; Saldanha et al., 2020). However, different types of organic trace minerals may exhibit distinct biological effects because of differences in their chemical structures, chelation stability, and metabolic pathways.

Despite growing interest in organic trace minerals, limited information is available regarding the comparative effects of inorganic trace minerals, methionine hydroxyl analog chelated trace minerals, and proteinate trace minerals in broiler breeder hens. Furthermore, most previous research has focused on performance and egg quality, with scarce data on their differential impacts on cecal microbiota structure and its potential mechanistic links to host antioxidant status, immunity, and reproductive endocrinology. Therefore, this study aims to evaluate three mineral sources at equivalent supplementation levels during the late rearing phase of broiler breeders. By integrating comprehensive microbiome analyses—including 16S rRNA sequencing, β-diversity analysis, and LEfSe analysis—it seeks to provide deeper insights into how the chemical structures and stability of different organic ligands not only influence mineral bioavailability but also modulate gut microbial ecology and subsequent physiological outcomes.

Materials and methods

Ethical approval

All experimental procedures were approved by the Biomedical Research Ethics Committee of Hunan Agricultural University, Changsha, China (Approval No. 29/2023; April 7, 2023). The feeding trial was conducted at the Pingjiang Branch of Hunan Xinguangan Agriculture and Animal Husbandry Co., Ltd. (Hunan, China).

Experimental design

The experiment was single design with broiler breeder hens (Hubbard Efficiency Plus). The experiment was carried out in Hunan Shuncheng Industrial Co. LTD. All experimental procedures were conducted in accordance with the guidelines for animal welfare in China and adhered to the standards set by the College of Animal Science and Technology at Hunan Agricultural University.

Birds, diets and management

A total of 432 Hubbard female broiler breeders, aged 50 weeks and with similar weights (3.05±0.2kg), were randomly divided into three dietary groups. Each treatment consisted of six replicates, with 24 birds per replicate. The dietary treatments were as follows: 1) ITE group: basal diet supplemented with inorganic trace elements; 2) MTE group: basal diet supplemented with methionine hydroxyl analog chelated trace minerals; 3) PTE group: basal diet supplemented with proteinate trace minerals. The basal diet was formulated to meet or exceed the nutrient requirements of broiler breeder hens according to NY/T 33-2004 standards. The composition and nutrient levels are presented in Table 1. The supplemental levels of trace minerals in all diets were identical, providing Cu, Zn, Mn, and Fe at 10, 110, 120, and 50mg/kg, respectively. The organic trace mineral premix was supplied by Xingjia Bioengineering Co., Ltd. (China). The inorganic trace minerals (ITE) were provided as sulfate forms (CuSO₄, ZnSO₄, MnSO₄, FeSO₄). MTE and PTE were supplied by Xingjia Bioengineering Co., Ltd. (China). Prior to the formal experiment, all birds underwent a 7-day adaptation period during which they were fed the basal diet. Birds were housed in environmentally controlled pens (3m × 3m × 6m), each equipped with an internal shelter compartment (1m × 1m × 1m) to minimize stress caused by external disturbances. Feed and water were provided ad libitum throughout the experimental period. Environmental conditions were maintained consistently across all groups. The ambient temperature was initially set at 33°C and adjusted as needed to maintain thermal comfort. Relative humidity was controlled between 50% and 70%, and lighting was provided according to standard breeder management practices.

Sample collection

On day 56 of the experiment, 10 eggs per replicate were randomly collected for egg quality analysis. At the end of the trial, after a 12-hour fasting period, two hens per replicate (n = 12 per treatment) were randomly selected for blood and intestinal sample collection. Approximately 5mL of blood was collected from the wing vein into heparinized tubes and centrifuged at 3000 × g for 10min at 4°C. Plasma was separated and stored at −20°C until analysis. Cecal contents were aseptically collected immediately after euthanasia, snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80°C for microbiota analysis. At the end of the trial, two hens per replicate were randomly selected, fasted for 12h, and euthanized by cervical dislocation followed by exsanguination, in accordance with approved guidelines.

Laying performance and egg quality

Egg production and egg weight were recorded on a daily basis for each replicate, while feed consumption was recorded weekly per replicate to calculate egg production and feed conversion ratio. Egg quality (shell thickness, egg weight, egg shape index, shell strength, haugh unit, yolk color, and yolk index) measurements were conducted following the methods described by Cheng et al. (2022). Yolk color was measured using the Roche yolk color fan (scale 1–15).

Reproductive performance and hormone analysis

A total of 5400 fertilized eggs were collected for incubation (1800 eggs per treatment, 300 eggs per replicate). Incubation conditions were maintained at 37.5°C with 55% relative humidity. Fertilization rate and hatchability were recorded and calculated based on standard formulas. Plasma concentrations of reproductive and metabolic hormones, including estradiol (E2), follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), progesterone (PROG), luteinizing hormone (LH), and parathyroid hormone (PTH), were determined using commercial ELISA kits (Shanghai ZCIBIO Technology Co., Ltd., China) following the manufacturer’s protocols.

Determination of mineral concentration in eggs

Egg samples were prepared by removing the shell and homogenizing the egg white and yolk thoroughly. The homogenized samples were subjected to acid digestion prior to analysis. The concentrations of Cu, Zn, Mn, and Fe were determined using inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS; iCAP Q, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Quality control was ensured using certified reference materials and standard calibration curves.

Antioxidant and immune indices

Activities of glutathione peroxidase (GSH-PX), catalase (CAT), total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC), Cu-Zn superoxide dismutase (Cu-Zn-SOD), Mn superoxide dismutase (Mn-SOD), and total superoxide dismutase (T-SOD), as well as the concentration of malondialdehyde (MDA), were assayed using commercial radioimmunoassay kits obtained from Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China. Concentrations of immunoglobulin A (IgA), immunoglobulin G (IgG), immunoglobulin M (IgM), and interleukin-11 (IL-11) levels were measured by ELISA kits obtained from Shanghai zcibio technology Co., Ltd, China. The assays were conducted following the manufacturer's instructions and measured with a microplate reader (Multiskan GO, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, USA).

Intestinal microbiota analysis

Fecal samples were collected and subjected to 16S rRNA gene sequencing targeting the V3–V4 regions. Sequencing data were processed using QIIME2 to obtain ASVs, and taxonomic annotation was performed based on the SILVA database. Microbial diversity was evaluated using alpha and beta diversity analyses.

Statistical analysis

Data were statistically analyzed with one-way ANOVA using SPSS 26 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL). Treatment means were compared by Duncan's multiple comparisons. The results were expressed as arithmetic mean and SEM. Significance was declared at P<0.05, and 0.05<P<0.10 was considered a tendency.

Results

Laying performance and egg quality

As presented in Table 2, there were no significant differences in egg production, feed conversion ratio, shell thickness, haugh units or egg weight among the ITE, MTE, and PTE groups (P > 0.05). However, the concentrations of trace elements in eggs were significantly affected by the mineral source (Table 3). Compared to the ITE group, PTE supplementation markedly increased the concentrations of Cu, Zn, and Mn by approximately 187.8%, 7.9%, and 127.0%, respectively (P < 0.05). The deposition levels of these minerals in the PTE group were also significantly higher than those in the MTE group (P < 0.05), whereas Fe content remained unaffected by the mineral source (P = 0.827).

Reproductive performance and hormones

As presented in Table 4, dietary supplementation with different forms of trace minerals had no significant effects on reproductive performance parameters in laying hens (P > 0.05). Analysis of the endocrine profile (Fig. 1) revealed that organic mineral supplementation significantly modulated reproductive hormone secretion. Serum levels of E2, FSH, and PROG were significantly elevated in both MTE and PTE groups compared to the ITE group (P < 0.05). No significant differences were detected in LH or PTH concentrations among the treatments (P > 0.05).

Fig. 2, Fig. 3, Fig. 4 depict the results for antioxidant and immune indices in plasma and eggs. In comparison to the ITE group, the concentrations of MDA in eggs and plasma were significantly decreased in the MTE and PTE groups (P < 0.05). Moreover, the activity of CAT, T-SOD, and GSH-PX in both plasma and eggs was significantly increased in the MTE and PTE groups (P < 0.05). Cecal microbiota diversity and community structure

As shown in Fig. 5, no significant differences were observed in α-diversity indices (ACE, Chao1, Simpson, and Shannon) among the ITE, MTE, and PTE groups (P > 0.05), indicating that the overall microbial richness and evenness remained stable. However, β-diversity analysis via PCoA revealed a distinct shift in the microbial community structure (Fig. 6). The PCoA plots at both the Phylum and OTU levels demonstrated that samples from the ITE, MTE, and PTE groups clustered separately. The PTE group, in particular, showed a clear separation from the ITE group along the PC1 axis, indicating that the degree of mineral chelation significantly influenced the overall composition of the cecal microbiome.

Taxonomic composition and differential biomarkers

Community composition analysis (Fig. 7) showed that the gut microbiota was dominated by Firmicutes and Bacteroidota across all treatments, with no marked differences in dominant phyla. At the phylum level (Fig. 7), Firmicutes and Bacteroidota were the predominant taxa across all treatments. LEfSe was applied to identify specific microbial biomarkers (Fig. 8). The results demonstrated that the PTE group was significantly enriched with beneficial genera, including Lactobacillus and Enterococcus (LDA score > 2). Conversely, the MTE group showed a higher abundance of Subdoligranulum, while the ITE group was characterized by a distinct microbial signature, such as higher relative abundances of Bacteroides. The cladogram clearly illustrates the hierarchical taxonomic differences from the phylum to the genus level across the three mineral treatments.

Discussion

Trace minerals such as Zn, Cu, Mn, and Fe play fundamental roles in poultry physiology, particularly in maintaining antioxidant defense systems, modulating immune regulation, and supporting reproductive function (Mohammadsadeghi, et al., 2023; Cao, et al., 2022; Wo, et al., 2022) Extensive research has demonstrated that organic mineral sources possess superior bioavailability and lower antagonistic interactions compared to their inorganic counterparts (Cao, et al., 2023; Suo, et al., 2015). For instance, Xiao et al. (2015) reported that amino acid-manganese enhanced eggshell quality in aged laying hens compared with inorganic Mn. In the present study, although no statistically significant improvements were observed in laying performance or egg quality, organic trace mineral supplementation showed numerically better egg production and feed conversion ratio, accompanied by significant enhancements in physiological parameters. This is consistent with the finding of Qiu et al. (2020), who noted that hens fed with proteinate trace minerals had better performance and eggshell strength. It is noteworthy that although organic trace elements significantly enhanced antioxidant capacity, immune indices, and reproductive hormone levels, these improvements did not translate into significant enhancements in egg-laying performance or hatching rate. This lack of effect may be attributed to the fact that the hens were in the late laying stage, during which reproductive performance naturally declines and responsiveness to nutritional interventions tends to be weaker. Moreover, the basal diet already met the hens’ basic nutritional requirements, which may have masked the additional benefits of the supplemented product.

A critical finding of our study was the significant increase in the deposition of Cu, Mn, and Zn in eggs following organic mineral supplementation. These minerals serve as cofactors for key antioxidant enzymes, including Cu/Zn-SOD and Mn-SOD, and their increased availability may directly contribute to the elevated antioxidant enzyme activities and reduced lipid peroxidation observed in both plasma and eggs. Our study revealed that the inclusion of organic trace elements in broiler breeders’ diets caused an increase in egg and plasma Cu-Zn-SOD, T-SOD, and GSH-PX and decreased the concentration of MDA. Consistent with our outcome, Wu et al. (2019) also demonstrated that dietary supplementation with Cu-Met improved the concentration of IgA and IgM in serum and the activity of Cu-Zn-SOD and GSH-PX and decreased the concentration of MDA.

Moreover, enhanced antioxidant capacity is closely linked to improved immune competence, as oxidative stress can impair immune cell function and antibody synthesis. In line with this concept, we found that supplementing proteinate trace elements in feed increased the concentrations of IgA and IgM compared to supplementing inorganic trace elements and methionine hydroxyl analog chelated trace minerals. This suggests that proteinate trace elements can improve the immune capability of the organism to some extent, contributing to overall health. These findings support the notion that organic trace minerals promote immune function by maintaining redox homeostasis.

Several studies demonstrated that dietary supplementation with organic trace elements had beneficial effects on reproductive performance (Cao, et al., 2022; Sabzian-Melei, et al., 2022; Saleh, et al., 2019). Results of the present study revealed that the inclusion of organic trace elements increased the concentration of E2, FSH, and PROG, whereas having no significant effects on reproductive performance. However, (Attia, et al., 2011) reported that organic copper addition decreased the fertility of breeding hens more than inorganic copper addition which is similar to those of (Xie, et al., 2014). It should be noted that basal diet composition, variety, and phase of the animal, source of organic minerals are some of the main parameters involved in the obtained relatively inconsistent results.

Increasing evidence indicates that trace minerals can modulate intestinal microbiota composition, thereby indirectly influencing host metabolism and immunity (Wang, et al., 2023; Wang, et al., 2025). In the current study, although α-diversity indices (Chao, Ace, and Shannon) remained statistically stable, the β-diversity analysis revealed distinct clustering of cecal microbial communities among the three treatments, indicating that the chemical form of trace minerals significantly influenced microbial community structure. Notably, LEfSe analysis identified Lactobacillus and Enterococcus as the signature biomarkers of the PTE group. Lactobacillus species are well-recognized probiotics in poultry, known to produce short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs) and lactic acid, which lower intestinal pH and inhibit the colonization of acid-sensitive pathogens (Xu, et al., 2025; Sharma, et al., 2026). Moreover, certain Lactobacillus strains have been shown to activate the Nrf2 signaling pathway, thereby upregulating antioxidant enzyme expression and reducing MDA accumulation (Zhan et al., 2025). This mechanism is consistent with the improved antioxidant status observed in both plasma and eggs in the organic mineral groups. Additionally, Lactobacillus and Enterococcus can stimulate mucosal immunity by promoting secretory IgA and IgM production, aligning with the elevated plasma IgA and IgM levels in the PTE group (Liu, et al., 2024). In contrast, the ITE group was characterized by higher relative abundance of Bacteroides. While Bacteroides plays roles in polysaccharide degradation, its dominance may reflect a less favorable microbial environment under inorganic mineral supplementation. The MTE group showed enrichment of Subdoligranulum, whose functional role in poultry remains less characterized. Collectively, these findings suggest that proteinate trace minerals (PTE) create a more favorable ecological niche for beneficial microbes, which may serve as an important intermediary linking mineral supplementation to enhanced systemic antioxidant capacity, immune function, and overall hen health.

Overall, these research results indicate that organic trace elements, particularly proteinate trace elements, have beneficial effects on the antioxidant capacity, immune response, endocrine regulation, and intestinal microbial composition of broiler breeder hens. There were no significant differences in production performance, which might be attributed to the relatively short experimental period, the hens being at the end of the laying period, or the adequate mineral supply in the basal diet; however, the observed physiological improvements highlight the potential long-term benefits of organic trace element supplements.

Conclusion

In conclusion, replacing inorganic trace minerals with organic sources (particularly proteinate trace minerals) in broiler breeder diets significantly improved antioxidant status, immune function, reproductive hormone profiles, egg mineral deposition, and modulated cecal microbiota composition toward a more beneficial profile. Although no significant effects were observed on laying performance or egg quality under the current experimental conditions, these physiological enhancements indicate that organic trace minerals, especially PTE, may confer health benefits to broiler breeders. Further long-term studies are needed to evaluate their effects on lifetime productivity and offspring performance.

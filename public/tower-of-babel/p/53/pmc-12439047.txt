Preventing postoperative nausea and vomiting after laparoscopic cholecystectomy: toward personalization
Authors: Naru Kim
Published: 2025

Postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV) are among the most common and distressing complications in patients undergoing general anesthesia [1], particularly during minimally invasive procedures such as laparoscopic cholecystectomy (LC). Conventional prediction tools, including the Apfel and Koivuranta scores, are widely used for PONV risk assessment. However, there is a growing need for more personalized and adaptable assessment methods [2].

One notable advancement in PONV risk stratification is the integration of machine learning (ML) algorithms. In a recent study [2], ML techniques were applied to predict the incidence of PONV in patients receiving intravenous patient-controlled analgesia after surgery. The researchers employed logistic regression and random forest models, which demonstrated strong predictive performance [2]. Notably, this ML-based approach refined traditional risk factors—such as sex, smoking status, and history of motion sickness [3]—by translating them into quantitatively robust predictive models.

Meanwhile, pharmacologic and nonpharmacologic strategies for preventing PONV have advanced substantially. Current consensus guidelines recommend multimodal prophylaxis tailored to each patient’s risk profile, including 5-HT3 antagonists, dexamethasone, and NK1 receptor antagonists for moderate to high risk [4,5]. Additionally, nonpharmacologic adjuncts, including acupuncture [5], preoperative carbohydrate loading [6], and early mobilization, have demonstrated benefits as complementary interventions, particularly when incorporated into Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS) protocols [7]. Within ERAS pathways, opioid-sparing anesthesia, early oral intake, and optimized fluid management are consistently associated with lower PONV rates and improved postoperative outcomes [1,7].

Within this evolving landscape, the study by Dhakal et al. [8] provides valuable insights from a resource-limited setting. The authors reported a PONV incidence of 43% among patients undergoing elective LC. They identified non-smoking status, a history of motion sickness, and postoperative opioid use as significant predictors in multivariable analysis [8]. They also compared the Apfel and Koivuranta scoring systems, with the Apfel score demonstrating slightly superior accuracy and greater practical utility owing to its reliance solely on preoperative variables [8,9]. This finding is particularly relevant for low- and middle-income countries (LMICs), where routine antiemetic risk assessment and prophylaxis are often inconsistently implemented.

Although this study is limited by the absence of intraoperative anesthetic data and a relatively short 24-hour follow-up period [8], which may underestimate the incidence of late-onset PONV, it highlights the importance of structured, low-cost risk assessment tools in enhancing perioperative care in LMICs. The findings support global efforts to individualize perioperative management and indicate that even modest interventions may yield meaningful improvements in outcomes. As the field advances, integrating traditional scoring systems with ERAS strategies and emerging predictive analytics could provide a comprehensive, scalable framework for managing PONV in minimally invasive surgery.

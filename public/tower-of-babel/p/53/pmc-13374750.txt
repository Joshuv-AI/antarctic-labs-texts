Evaluating datopotamab deruxtecan (Dato-DXd) as a novel treatment option for EGFR-mutated non-small cell lung cancer
Authors: Olga Tsvetkova, Tomas Escobar Gil, George Kassir, Sofi Castanon, Sai Suraj Kollapaneni, Andy Han, Seung Jun Park, Rui Li, Maria A Velez, Aaron E Lisberg
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTDatopotamab deruxtecan (Dato-DXd) is a TROP2-directed antibody–drug conjugate (ADC) that has received accelerated approval in the US for adults with locally advanced or metastatic EGFR-mutated non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) after progression on EGFR-directed therapy and platinum chemotherapy. In pooled analyses from TROPION-Lung01 and TROPION-Lung05, Dato-DXd achieved an objective response rate of ~45% and a median duration of response of 6.5 months, which compares favorably with historical outcomes with docetaxel. Dato-DXd is being evaluated in combination with osimertinib in the first-line and post-osimertinib settings, following encouraging activity in the phase II ORCHARD platform trial evaluating therapeutic strategies for EGFR-TKI–resistant disease. Importantly, Dato-DXd demonstrates a favorable safety profile and has lower rates of grade ≥3 adverse events and less hematologic toxicity than docetaxel. Adverse events of special interest include mucositis, interstitial lung disease, and ocular surface events. While these toxicities are generally manageable, they require prophylaxis, monitoring, and early intervention. Current research aims to elucidate the mechanisms underlying these toxicities and to identify modifiable risk factors to further improve tolerability. The biological mechanisms contributing to the differential efficacy of Dato-DXd are also under investigation and may further inform its clinical role.Expert opinion: Determining how best to integrate Dato-DXd within existing treatment sequences has the potential to meaningfully address persistent unmet needs in EGFR-mutated NSCLC.KEYWORDS: EGFR-mutated non-small cell lung cancer, datopotamab deruxtecan (Dato-DXd), TROP2, antibody-drug conjugates (ADC), osimertinib resistance, TROPION-Lung trials, interstitial lung disease (ILD), treatment sequencing

Introduction

EGFR-mutated NSCLC treatment landscape

Until recently, osimertinib, a third-generation TKI, has been the preferred first-line agent for patients with EGFR-mutated NSCLC due to its ability to target both the common activating EGFR mutations (exon 19 deletions and the L858R point mutation) and the acquired T790M resistance mutation responsible for resistance to first and second-generation TKIs [1,2]. Osimertinib demonstrated superior efficacy compared to chemotherapy and earlier-generation TKIs, extending median progression-free survival (mPFS) to 18–19 months [3]. Additionally, a first-line combination of osimertinib plus platinum-pemetrexed further improved mPFS to 25.5 months based on results from the FLAURA2 trial [4]. Compared to prior EGFR-TKIs, osimertinib also demonstrated improved brain penetration [2]. However, resistance to osimertinib develops in most patients [2,5,6].

Broadly, EGFR-TKI resistance can be classified into on-target and off-target mechanisms. Alterations in the EGFR kinase domain and drug-binding site (eg, T790M and C797S) drive on-target resistance. Off-target escape pathways include receptor-mediated bypass signaling, most commonly through MET and less frequently through HER2. Downstream reactivation of PI3K/AKT and MAPK pathways, and alterations in KRAS and BRAF, have also been implicated [1,2,7]. In addition, actionable genomic alteration–independent, heterogeneous resistance mechanisms, and histologic transformation can play a role [2,8–11] (Figure 1).

Amivantamab plus lazertinib (a combination of a bispecific EGFR/MET antibody and a third-generation EGFR-TKI) was designed to address both MET-driven escape and on-target EGFR resistance and is approved for previously untreated advanced-stage EGFR-mutant NSCLC. In the phase III MARIPOSA trial, amivantamab plus lazertinib demonstrated a mPFS of 23.7 months compared with 16.6 months for osimertinib and 18.5 months for lazertinib monotherapy [12,13]. The combination also significantly improved overall survival (OS) compared to osimertinib alone [13], expanding treatment options for EGFR-mutant NSCLC in the first-line setting. While osimertinib alone remains the preferred agent for those with poor performance status, amivantamab plus lazertinib and osimertinib plus chemotherapy have shifted the treatment landscape toward upfront combination therapies [14], given the superior OS observed with these intensification regimens (Figure 2).

The second-line treatment armamentarium following progression on osimertinib historically included carboplatin or cisplatin combined with pemetrexed for patients without actionable resistance mechanisms. In contrast, biomarker-selected targeted therapy was reserved for those with specific alterations, most commonly MET amplification [14]. However, this approach was limited by a narrow mechanism of action and the requirement for biomarker positivity. Docetaxel with or without ramucirumab was used in later lines but provided only modest clinical benefit at the expense of an increased burden of toxicities.

More recently, the combination of amivantamab with platinum-pemetrexed chemotherapy has been approved for the EGFR-mutated NSCLC after progression on TKIs, demonstrating a superior objective response rate (ORR) and mPFS of 6.3 months compared with 4.2 months with chemotherapy alone in the phase III MARIPOSA-2 trial. In addition, the triplet regimens combining amivantamab, lazertinib, and chemotherapy have also been evaluated. The expanded regimen produced superior outcomes compared to chemotherapy alone, with mPFS reaching 8.3 months; however, it was associated with an increased rate of hematologic toxicities requiring regimen adjustment and further evaluation [15]. While the aforementioned combinations are practice-changing and significantly improved treatment options for EGFR-mutated NSCLC without requiring biomarker pre-selection, disease progression remains inevitable, highlighting the need for additional therapies.

The tumor microenvironment (TME) also contributes to EGFR-TKI resistance and treatment limitations. Preclinical studies have shown that EGFR-TKI exposure can alter the TME, leading to immunologic tolerance through an increase in Treg cell density, CD73 expression, extracellular matrix remodeling, and cancer-associated fibroblasts [16–18]. In addition, EGFR-TKI resistance has been associated with lower tumor mutation burden, which also reduces immunogenicity [19]. It is notable that although EGFR-TKIs appeared to increase PD-L1 expression in some studies, clinical evaluation of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) in EGFR-mutated disease failed to extend PFS beyond chemotherapy [20–22], further highlighting the unmet clinical need in EGFR-mutated disease.

Antibody–drug conjugates (ADCs) have emerged as a mechanistically different therapeutic strategy. ADCs directly deliver cytotoxic agents to tumor cells, minimizing systemic payload exposure [23–27]. Although ADCs do not directly inhibit specific signaling pathways, they exploit surface target expression to broadly induce cytotoxic damage, targeting heterogeneous resistance mechanisms rather than inhibiting specific cell-membrane or intracellular pathways that drive proliferation. Several different ADC targets have been investigated in recent years. Trophoblast cell-surface antigen 2 (TROP2) has emerged as a target with a promising clinical signal and acceptable off-target effects [23,26,28,29].

TROP2 as a therapeutic target in NSCLC

TROP2 ADCs entered clinical space with the approval of sacituzumab govitecan, an ADC combining a TROP2 antibody and a SN-38 payload, used for the treatment of metastatic triple-negative breast cancer and urothelial cancer. Although sacituzumab govitecan did not demonstrate a statistically significant OS benefit compared with docetaxel in NSCLC [30], datopotamab deruxtecan (Dato-DXd) has shown promising results in EGFR-mutated disease.

Specifically, Dato-DXd is a next-generation TROP2 ADC carrying the highly potent topoisomerase I inhibitor deruxtecan (DXd). Dato-DXd was first approved for the treatment of unresectable or metastatic HR-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer. Most recently, Dato-DXd received accelerated approval in the US for locally advanced or metastatic EGFR-mutated NSCLC after progression on osimertinib and platinum-based chemotherapy. In addition, ongoing trials are evaluating whether Dato-DXd should be used in earlier lines of therapy, either immediately following osimertinib in the second line, or in combination with osimertinib as a front-line therapy (Figure 3).

Other TROP2-targeted ADCs are also being evaluated in NSCLC. Sacituzumab tirumotecan (Sac-TMT) combines an anti-TROP2 antibody with a belotecan-derivative topoisomerase I inhibitor payload. It has received regulatory approval in China after EGFR-TKIs [11,31], and is being evaluated in phase III trials in the US. While TROP2-directed ADCs share the same target, their safety and efficacy profiles may vary depending on payload and linker design.

Datopotamab deruxtecan: structure and mechanism of action

Overview of the Dato-DXd construct

Dato-DXd consists of a humanized anti-TROP2 IgG1 monoclonal antibody linked via a cleavable tetrapeptide-based linker to the topoisomerase I inhibitor payload deruxtecan (DXd). It features a next-generation ADC platform with an optimized linker–payload design aimed at improving targeted delivery and limiting systemic exposure [25,26].

TROP2 target biology and selection

TROP2 is a transmembrane glycoprotein encoded by the gene TACSTD2 [24,32,33], which is overexpressed in many epithelial tumors, including NSCLC [24,34]. TROP2 is believed to regulate cellular proliferation, transformation, organogenesis, and regeneration [24,35]. Several normal tissues also express TROP2, including lung, skin, uterus, bladder, esophagus, oral mucosa, and nasopharynx [33,34], which could explain some of the adverse events (AEs) observed with TROP2-specific agents.

Antibody component

Dato-DXd utilizes a humanized IgG1 monoclonal antibody, which has a long serum half-life and high internalization capacity [36,37]. The antibody selectively binds to TROP2-expressing cells and delivers the deruxtecan payload into the cell via receptor-mediated endocytosis. The engagement of the Fc region with Fcγ receptors on immune effector cells has also been shown to promote antibody-dependent effector functions, such as antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity and antibody-dependent cellular phagocytosis, which can contribute to antitumor activity [38]. Recent translational studies suggest that these functions can lead to target-independent uptake of ADCs by alveolar macrophages, contributing to off-target toxicities such as interstitial lung disease [39,40].

Linker design and stability

In contrast to earlier TROP2 ADCs, Dato-DXd features a tetrapeptide-based cleavable linker that must be specifically cleaved by lysosomal proteases [41]. Although cleavable linkers enable more efficient and reliable payload release following internalization compared with non-cleavable linkers, they can vary in their stability in circulation and may degrade prematurely before reaching the tumor, leading to clinically significant systemic toxicity [41–43]. The newer platform used in Dato-DXd incorporates advanced linker designs that are less susceptible to premature payload release, thereby reducing the risk of systemic AEs, including hematologic and gastrointestinal toxicity [25,26].

Payload and drug-antibody ratio

Deruxtecan, the payload in Dato-DXd, is a highly potent topoisomerase I inhibitor that induces DNA damage leading to apoptosis [23,24]. The drug-antibody ratio (DAR) can have a significant effect on efficacy and typically ranges from 2 to 8, with different ratios used in various ADC formulations [25,41]. A greater potency of deruxtecan allows for Dato-DXd to employ a lower, non-variable DAR of 4 while maintaining strong antitumor activity [26,41].

Bystander effect

The bystander effect is defined as the ability of the payload to eradicate not only target cells but also neighboring cells, regardless of whether they express the target antigen [44–46]. Dato-DXd employs a cleavable linker and a membrane-permeable payload, both of which are required for the bystander effect [44,45,47]. It has been hypothesized that the activity of Dato-DXd in tumors with heterogeneous TROP2 expression may, at least in part, be due to the additional efficacy conferred by its bystander effect [46].

Pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics

Okajima et al. demonstrated that Dato-DXd binds TROP2, undergoes rapid internalization, and releases DXd intracellularly, resulting in the induction of DNA damage and apoptosis in tumor models [26]. In clinical trials, pharmacokinetic data from TROPION-PanTumor01, TROPION-Lung05, and TROPION-Lung01 demonstrated approximate dose-proportional increases in exposure at 4, 6, and 8 mg/kg and no unexpected accumulation at steady state [28,48–50]. However, 6 mg/kg was selected as the recommended phase 2 dose because 8 mg/kg did not enhance clinical activity but was associated with a higher incidence of grade ≥3 toxicities [48]. The mean half-life of Dato-DXd was 4.8 days at the 6 mg/kg dose, which supported a once-every-3-weeks treatment schedule.

Body weight was identified as the most important pharmacokinetic covariate affecting exposure to Dato-DXd and unconjugated DXd. Based on initial trial data, no dose adjustments were required for the other covariates evaluated, including markers of renal and hepatic function. However, subsequent population pharmacokinetic analyses showed that serum albumin at the extreme low range may have a statistically significant effect on exposure [50]. The potential clinical implications of this finding remain to be determined.

Clinical efficacy of datopotamab deruxtecan in NSCLC

Phase I development: TROPION-PanTumor01

TROPION-PanTumor01 (NCT03401385) is a phase I study of Dato-DXd in solid tumors. Dose-limiting toxicities included grade 3 stomatitis and rash, and the 6 mg/kg dose was selected as the recommended phase II dose. In NSCLC, the objective response rate (ORR) was 26% (95% CI, 14.6–40.3), with a median duration of response (mDOR) of 10.5 months. Median progression-free survival (mPFS) and OS were 6.9 months (95% CI, 2.7–8.8) and 11.4 months (95% CI, 7.1–20.6), respectively [48] (Table 1).

Phase II and III trials in pretreated NSCLC

The phase III TROPION-Lung01 (NCT04656652) evaluated Dato-DXd versus docetaxel in patients with pretreated advanced or metastatic NSCLC with and without actionable genetic alterations. Patients were randomized to Dato-DXd 6 mg/kg or docetaxel 75 mg/m2 once every 3 weeks [51]. In this study, mPFS was 4.4 months (95% CI, 4.2–5.6) with Dato-DXd versus 3.7 months (95% CI, 2.9–4.2) with docetaxel. ORR was also higher with Dato-DXd, 26.4% compared to 12.8% with docetaxel. Although ORR and mPFS in the Dato-DXd cohort demonstrated statistically significant improvement over standard-of-care docetaxel, statistical significance for OS was not reached. Importantly, the majority of clinical benefit was observed in patients with nonsquamous NSCLC [51]. Given the modest overall benefit, the clinical development was subsequently refocused on biomarker-stratified cohorts, with particular emphasis on EGFR-mutated NSCLC.

The phase II TROPION-Lung05 (NCT04484142) evaluated Dato-DXd in patients with advanced or metastatic NSCLC with actionable genomic alterations after progression on targeted therapy and platinum-based chemotherapy. In patients with EGFR-mutated disease, ORR was 43.6% (95% CI, 32.4–55.3), median duration of response (mDOR) was 7.0 months (95% CI, 4.2–10.2), a mPFS was 5.8 months (95% CI, 5.4–8.3). Median OS reached 18.3 months (95% CI, 12.4 to not estimable). Notably, among the patients with sensitizing or T790M mutations previously treated with osimertinib, ORR was numerically higher, reaching 49.1% (95% CI, 35.4–62.9) [49].

Based on promising efficacy signals, a pooled analysis of TROPION-Lung01 (EGFR mutation-positive cohort) and TROPION-Lung05 was performed. In this combined data set, Dato-DXd demonstrated an ORR of 43% (95% CI: 34–52) and a mDOR of 7.0 months (95% CI: 4.2–9.8). Median PFS and mOS were 5.8 (95% CI: 5.4–8.2) and 15.6 months (95% CI: 13.1–19.0), respectively [5]. These outcomes exceeded what was observed with standard-of-care docetaxel, which in the TROPION-Lung01 control arm showed an ORR of approximately 13%, a mPFS of 3.7 months, and a mOS of 11.8–12.3 months [51]. Furthermore, Dato-DXd demonstrated broad activity across diverse EGFR mutation types, including uncommon and resistance mutations [5]. These results led to the accelerated approval of Dato-DXd in the US for patients with locally advanced or metastatic EGFR-mutated NSCLC after progression on EGFR-directed therapy and platinum-based chemotherapy.

Combination strategies after osimertinib progression and ongoing phase II and III development

Ongoing studies are evaluating Dato-DXd in earlier settings. Biomarker-directed phase II ORCHARD platform trial (NCT03944772) combined osimertinib with Dato-DXd following progression on first-line osimertinib. In this exploratory study, Dato-DXd was investigated as part of a biomarker-agnostic combination for patients without identifiable targetable mechanisms of osimertinib resistance [52], with analysis to date demonstrating promising antitumor activity [52,53]. For the osimertinib plus Dato-DXd 4 mg/kg cohort, ORR was 43%, mPFS was 9.5 months (95% CI, 7.2–9.8 months), and mDOR was 6.3 months (95% CI, 3.8–8.1 months). In the 6 mg/kg cohort, the ORR was somewhat lower at 36%. However, mPFS improved to 11.7 months (95% CI, 8.3–21.7 months), and responses appeared more durable, with mDOR reaching 20.5 months (95% CI, 6.2 months to not calculable). Although overall survival data are not fully mature, mOS was estimated at 26.2 months (95% CI, 14.8 months to not calculable) in the 6 mg/kg cohort compared with 19.8 months (95% CI, 13.5–23.3 months) in the 4 mg/kg cohort [53].

Taken together, these findings further support the ongoing phase III evaluation of osimertinib plus Dato-DXd in TROPION-Lung14 (NCT06350097) and TROPION-Lung15 (NCT06417814) [54,55]. The former is assessing Dato-DXd in combination with osimertinib in patients with treatment-naive EGFR-mutated NSCLC in the first-line setting. The latter is evaluating Dato-DXd with or without osimertinib against platinum-based chemotherapy in patients with EGFR-mutated, osimertinib-pretreated nonsquamous NSCLC.

Additional phase II data and biomarker-enriched cohorts

An additional phase II ICARUS-Lung01 trial evaluated Dato-DXd in heavily pretreated advanced NSCLC and reported results congruent with those observed in the TROPION Program. ICARUS-Lung01 demonstrated significantly higher activity in nonsquamous versus squamous disease and numerically improved response rates in tumors harboring EGFR alterations [56], further contributing to growing interest in the biological basis for observed differences in efficacy and the potential role of Dato-DXd in patients with TKI-resistant EGFR-mutated NSCLC. Exploratory biomarker findings from ICARUS-Lung01 are discussed in the following sections.

CNS efficacy

CNS disease is prevalent in patients with advanced NSCLC and associated with poor outcomes [57]. CNS metastases pose a unique treatment challenge, as systemic efficacy does not always translate into meaningful intracranial activity due to the protective effect of the blood–brain barrier and differences in tumor microenvironment [58]. Although limited data regarding the CNS efficacy of Dato-DXd exist to date, early studies have shown promising activity. In preclinical evaluations, intracranial tumor models expressing TROP2 demonstrated that Dato-DXd can cross the blood–brain barrier and facilitate intracranial tumor control [59]. In clinical trials, analysis of CNS disease from TROPION-Lung01 demonstrated numerical improvement in intracranial response and PFS with Dato-DXd compared to docetaxel in patients with non-squamous NSCLC. Notably, the ORR with Dato-DXd was 38% among patients with measurable disease, whereas no CNS responses were observed with docetaxel [60]. Additionally, a post hoc analysis from the TROPION-Lung05 trial in patients with actionable genomic alterations and baseline brain metastases showed an intracranial ORR of 22% and intracranial mPFS of 6.9 months. The systemic outcomes were comparable to those observed in patients without brain metastases [61], suggesting that the presence of CNS disease does not substantially diminish the systemic efficacy of Dato-DXd. Most recently, ORCHARD also provided encouraging preliminary data. In this study, most first progression events occurred outside the CNS, supporting potential CNS activity of the combined Dato-DXd and osimertinib regimen [51].

Determinants of response to Dato-DXd and pending questions

TROP2 expression and efficacy

The reasons for differential response to Dato-DXd in EGFR-mutated NSCLC are currently under investigation. Whether TROP2 expression significantly affects the efficacy of TROP2-targeted ADCs has been a point of debate in recent years. The initial clinical trial analysis of TROP2 expression by immunohistochemistry (IHC) showed that response to Dato-DXd occurred across varying TROP2 levels [48]. However, a more granular translational evaluation has demonstrated an association between high TROP2 expression and improved response to TROP2-directed agents, suggesting that TROP2 expression can modulate efficacy under some conditions. For instance, in the study by Meric-Bernstam et al., TROP2 RNA and protein expression were significantly higher in preclinical models sensitive to Dato-DXd [62]. In addition, Zhao et al. similarly demonstrated that the high expression, defined as an IHC H-score above 200, was associated with longer PFS with sac-TMT compared to those with low expression, while ORR remained similar between both groups [11]. Similar results were observed in the follow-up biomarker analysis from ICARUS-Lung01, where an association between TROP2 expression and longer PFS was observed with both the 100–200 and ≥200 groups compared to the <100 group [63]. Together, these findings suggest that although high TROP2 expression may not be universally required for therapeutic response, it can play a significant role in overall tumor control.

The limited predictive value of IHC has led to the development of a more nuanced TROP2 biomarker. The quantitative continuous scoring with normalized membrane ratio (QCS-NMR) is a digital pathology-based method that incorporates both membrane and intracellular TROP2 [64,65]. The tumor is defined as QCS-NMR-positive if at least 75% of tumor cells demonstrate TROP2 NMR ≤ 0.56. In TROPION-Lung01 subgroup analyses by Garassino et al., TROP2 QCS-NMR positivity was associated with higher ORR and longer mPFS with Dato-DXd versus docetaxel [64], suggesting that relative distribution between intracellular and extracellular TROP2, rather than overall expression, constitutes a more accurate biomarker of Dato-DXd sensitivity. Notably, QCS-NMR positivity was most common in non-squamous NSCLC with actionable genomic alterations (AGA) (75%) versus those without (63%) [64], further supporting the association between AGA status and Dato-DXd efficacy observed in clinical trials.

The findings from the aforementioned studies are overall consistent with a possible relationship between TROP2 expression changes and osimertinib resistance. While baseline tumor profiling has not demonstrated an association between EGFR alterations and TROP2 expression [66,67], most of the biopsies analyzed in these studies were collected in pre–EGFR-TKI settings or in heterogeneous cohorts. In contrast, emerging translational evidence suggests that EGFR-TKI exposure may induce a TROP2-enriched tumor state [68,69]. Specifically, the preclinical studies of Sac-TMT, TROP2 CAR-T cells, and patient-derived xenograft models have shown that TROP2 expression is upregulated in EGFR-mutated tumors exposed to or tolerant to TKIs [11,68,69]. Some tumor models additionally demonstrated increased internalization [11], further emphasizing the role of TROP2 engagement in EGFR-mutated disease and supporting the idea that internalization dynamics, rather than surface expression alone, are the important determinants of response. QCS-NMR has shown a significant association with the internalization capacity of Dato-DXd in translational studies [70] and provided a new biomarker that may explain differential efficacy. However, TROP2 expression dynamics alone may not fully account for variability in treatment response.

Role of payload sensitivity

Additional preclinical data suggest that differential sensitivity to the payload may also contribute to response. Baldacci et al. showed that despite increased TROP2 expression in osimertinib drug-tolerant persister cells, suppression of TROP2 had no effect on cell growth or sensitivity to osimertinib [65]. Similarly, breast cancer–derived xenograft models showed that although higher TROP2 expression correlated with improved response to Dato-DXd, antitumor activity could still be observed in some low-TROP2 models and in controls treated with a generic IgG-DXd construct [62]. Together, these findings suggest that although TROP2-related processes may increase susceptibility to Dato-DXd, other TROP2-associated but functionally independent biological factors may also modulate treatment response. While QCS-NMR can explain some of these findings by better capturing TROP2 distribution and internalization, it may not fully account for the variability in response observed at similar expression levels.

According to prior studies, TROP2 can engage downstream signaling pathways including ERK/MAPK, PI3K/AKT, and NF-κB, leading to tumor cell proliferation and survival [35,71–74]. Although TROP2 ADCs do not directly inhibit TROP2 signaling and exert their effect via cytotoxic payload, activation of survival pathways could theoretically influence cellular susceptibility to the deruxtecan payload. Interestingly, prior exploratory biomarker analyses, including the recent data from ORCHARD, have not shown a significant association between driver alterations and response to Dato-DXd in EGFR-mutated NSCLC [51,56]. Because DXd acts by inhibiting topoisomerase I at the DNA replication level, Dato-DXd could theoretically retain activity despite upstream signaling alterations, which may explain the lack of a clear association between driver alterations and clinical response. Overall, the genetic biomarker results to date suggest that Dato-DXd retains activity across broad resistance mechanisms, further emphasizing its role in addressing the heterogeneous landscape of TKI resistance. However, additional data are needed to better understand the potential relationships between TROP2 and intracellular processes, as well as broader ADC payload-related determinants of efficacy [75].

In support of this point, the follow-up exploratory biomarker analysis from ICARUS-Lung01 suggested that early activation of DNA repair pathways may be associated with inferior response to Dato-DXd in NSCLC. Specifically, the study showed that expression of SLFN11, a protein that induces a replication block in response to DNA damage and replication stress, could play a role in whether DNA-damaging agents such as topoisomerase I inhibitors can successfully arrest cellular proliferation [63]. Although the results of this study remain exploratory, they further highlight the complexity and multifactorial nature of biological processes that can influence the activity of Dato-DXd.

In addition, other exploratory findings from this study suggested that activation of immune-related pathways in response to payload-induced damage, could affect treatment response [63]. However, it is currently unclear whether these findings will translate to EGFR-mutated NSCLC specifically, as EGFR-mutated disease has historically been associated with a cold TME.

Safety and tolerability

Overall safety profile

Overall, Dato-DXd demonstrated a favorable safety profile and a reduced risk of grade ≥3 AEs and hematologic toxicity compared with standard chemotherapy. Stomatitis/mucositis, nausea, and alopecia were the most common events across all studies [5,48,49,52] (Table 2).

In the TROPION-PanTumor01, treatment-related adverse events (TEAEs), including grade 3 or higher TRAEs, correlated with higher dose. The same trend was observed for dose holds and dose reductions [48]. In TROPION-Lung01, the overall frequency of TRAEs was similar between Dato-DXd and docetaxel, occurring in approximately 87% of patients in both arms, which was comparable to the rates observed in TROPION-PanTumor01 6 mg/kg dosing schedule. However, the rate of Grade ≥3 TRAEs was significantly lower with Dato-DXd, occurring in 25.6% of patients versus 42.1% with docetaxel [52]. Patients receiving Dato-DXd also required fewer dose modifications and treatment discontinuations. One percent of patients treated with Dato-DXd had TRAEs associated with deaths, and 0.7% with docetaxel. Among these, investigators attributed two fatal ILD/pneumonitis events and one sepsis event to Dato-DXd, and one ILD/pneumonitis case and one episode of septic shock to docetaxel [52]. TROPION-Lung05 enrolled patients with genetic alterations (EGFR and ALK). Although this resulted in differences in efficacy, the overall safety and tolerability profile of Dato-DXd remained consistent with the safety findings from TROPION-Lung01 and the Phase 1 PanTumor01 [49].

The most comprehensive evaluation of Dato-DXd safety in EGFR-mutated NSCLC comes from a pooled analysis of patients from TROPION-Lung01 and the EGFR-mutated cohort of TROPION-Lung05. The overall findings were consistent with prior studies, and no new or unexpected toxicities were identified [5]. TRAEs of any grade occurred in 95% of patients, TRAEs 3 or higher in 23% of patients, whereas 22% of patients experienced dose reductions and 23% dose delays. No treatment-related deaths were recorded. The most common TRAEs were stomatitis/mucositis (any grade, 60%; grade ≥3, 9%), alopecia (49%), and nausea (46%; grade ≥3, 0%) [5].

Most recently, the updated analysis from the ORCHARD provided additional data. In this exploratory study, the frequency of grade ≥3 AEs was numerically higher in the 6 mg/kg cohort, occurring in 76% of patients versus 49% in the 4 mg/kg cohort; and ILD/pneumonitis occurred in 3% and 15% of patients in the 4 mg/kg and 6 mg/kg cohorts, respectively [51]. However, the AEs were generally manageable. Based on the overall benefit-risk profile, the study recommended 6 mg/kg as the preferred starting dose for the Dato-DXd plus 80 mg osimertinib combination [51].

Dato-DXd has been overall well tolerated compared with chemotherapy; however, mucositis, interstitial lung disease/pneumonitis, and ocular surface events have been identified as adverse events of special interest (AESIs). Although rare at severe grades, these toxicities have been associated with a significant number of treatment interruptions and decreased quality of life.

Oral mucositis

Oral mucositis/stomatitis (OM/S) is the most common AE associated with Dato-DX [29]. OM/S is defined as damage to the oral mucosa that can present as increased sensitivity, pain, and ulcerations. OM/S has been previously shown to occur via treatment-related DNA damage, followed by upregulation of inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6, leading to tissue injury [76,77]. However, the exact mechanism driving Dato-DXd-associated OM/S remains unknown. It is hypothesized that binding of Dato-DXd to TROP2 in normal tissue could be implicated, given that TROP2 is expressed in the salivary glands, skin, and esophagus [34].

Across all clinical trials, the events were predominantly low grade, with grade >3 events occurring with frequency between 6.7%-9.5% [48,49,52]. In the TROPION-PanTumor01 trial, the rates of treatment-related OM/S for NSCLC were about 60%, whereas in TROPION-Lung01 and TROPION-Lung05, the rates reduced to 47.5% and 56.2%, respectively [49,52]. It is possible that the lower frequencies observed in later studies were related to improved prophylaxis and management, or to the higher doses administered during dose-escalation while establishing the maximum tolerated dose. Of note, 10.4% underwent dose reductions due to OM/S in the TROPION-Lung01. Only a few mucositis-related discontinuations occurred among all treated patients [48,49,52].

To reduce the rates and severity of OM/S, prophylaxis, monitoring, and management strategies have been implemented. Corticosteroid-containing mouthwash and oral hygiene are recommended as prophylaxis. Regular oral exams are recommended to facilitate earlier detection and treatment. If OM/S is suspected, other causes should be ruled out. Once the diagnosis is confirmed, lidocaine-based mouthwash and topical clobetasol gel can be used to treat OM/S. Grade 1 and 2 events usually require no dose reductions. Close monitoring and supportive medications are usually sufficient for grade 1, while grade 2 events often require a dose delay. For grade 3 events, it is recommended to delay the dose until OM/S resolves to grade <1 or baseline, followed by dose reduction [29] (Table 3).

Interstitial lung disease

Interstitial lung disease (ILD) is a group of disorders characterized by inflammation and/or fibrosis involving the lung interstitium and parenchyma [78], and occurs in approximately 7–9% of NSCLC patients treated with Dato-DXd monotherapy [79,80]. While severe cases are uncommon, ILD has been associated with rare fatal outcomes [52,79,80]. The average time to onset of ILD in NSCLC is about 44 days [79]; and grade 1 ILD often presents asymptomatically [79,81]. Due to nonspecific symptoms, ILD can often be erroneously assessed as disease progression or other common etiologies, emphasizing the need for improved recognition and risk stratification [81].

Radiologically, Dato-DXd-mediated ILD may present as organizing pneumonia, nonspecific interstitial pneumonia, diffuse alveolar damage, and, less frequently, hypersensitivity pneumonitis [52,79,81]. Although ILD has been observed in multiple tumor types, the incidence was highest in NSCLC [79], suggesting that disease-specific or host-specific factors unique to NSCLC may contribute to increased susceptibility. Due to its earlier clinical development, prior studies of trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd) have been more extensive and have identified smoking history [40], baseline lung disease, renal impairment, low baseline oxygen saturation, and Asian ancestry as possible risk factors for ADC-induced ILD [40,82–84]. Although available analyses have not demonstrated an association between prior thoracic radiation or ICIs and increased risk of T-DX-related ILD [84,85], most of these evaluations have been conducted in metastatic breast cancer or multi-tumor cohorts, and may not be fully generalizable to NSCLC. Given current knowledge gaps, assessment of ILD risk factors in Dato-DXd-specific NSCLC cohorts is an active area of research and could improve clinical management and Dato-DXd tolerability.

Translational studies in animal models suggest that ADC-related ILD is primarily driven by target-independent uptake of ADCs by alveolar macrophages through Fcγ receptor-mediated internalization [39,86–88], leading to intracellular payload release, type II pneumocyte stress, and lung inflammation [39,87,88]. This mechanism is further supported by findings from Kumagai et al., showing that unconjugated DXd did not induce ILD in non-human primates even at high doses. In addition, lung injury was localized to the alveolar level despite HER2 being predominantly expressed in bronchial epithelium, further suggesting target-independent Fcγ receptor-mediated involvement [88]. Consistent with these findings, Wei et al. additionally demonstrated that Fcγ blockade reduced macrophage uptake-induced lung toxicity in murine models [87]. Although most of these studies were conducted with T-DXd, shared structural features make these mechanisms relevant to Dato-DXd-induced lung injury. However, the pattern of TROP2 expression differs from that of HER2, as TROP2 is also expressed in alveolar tissue [89].

Given the proposed pathophysiological mechanism, several groups have evaluated whether biomarkers of lung epithelial injury and macrophage-driven inflammation can predict ILD development. A recent serum proteomics analysis in patients treated with T-DXd identified IL1RL1/ST2 (a soluble IL-33 receptor involved in epithelial stress and type-2 inflammation) as a potential susceptibility marker for severe ILD, and CXCL11 (an IFN-γ–induced chemokine) as a possible early biomarker of preclinical ILD [90,91]. Separately, a study by Grinshpun et al. demonstrated that circulating cell-free DNA carrying lung-specific methylation signatures correlates with incidence and ILD severity in the T-DXd-treated cohort [92]. Taken together, these findings support the broader concept that serum-based biomarkers may offer valuable insights into signatures of early lung injury and could improve risk assessment for ADC-induced ILD.

Clinically, monitoring and early symptom recognition are particularly important. Guidelines recommend high-resolution computed tomography and prompt pulmonology consultation when symptoms concerning for ILD arise. Other etiologies, particularly infection, should be excluded. In addition, pulmonary function tests and bronchoscopy with bronchoalveolar lavage should be considered if clinically indicated [29]. For grade 1 ILD, oral corticosteroids are usually recommended with Dato-DXd treatment held until full resolution. Depending on the duration of the event, dose reduction or discontinuation should also be considered. For grade ≥2 ILD, prompt initiation of systemic steroids is required, followed by permanent discontinuation of Dato-DXd [29,79]. Importantly, grade 3 and 4 events require hospitalization [29].

Although corticosteroids are generally effective for the treatment of ADC-induced ILD, refractory cases can occur. To date, minimal guidance exists for the treatment of steroid-refractory disease. Several cases of steroid-refractory T-DXd-induced pneumonitis treated with intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) have been reported, although outcomes were variable [93,94]. IVIG, infliximab, and other adjunct immunosuppressive agents are recognized options in ICI-induced pneumonitis [95]; however, their benefit in ADC-induced ILD has not been established. Notably, in preclinical studies with T-DXd, pre-treatment with IgG1 has been shown to reduce ADC-induced lung toxicity [87], suggesting that Fc-Fcγ receptor-modulating agents could potentially play a role in either preventing or treating ILD. Although further investigation is required to evaluate this approach in Dato-DXd and its applicability in clinical practice, it could offer a potential new strategy for reducing ILD risk.

Ocular surface events

Ocular surface event (OSE) observed with Dato-DXd primarily affects the corneal epithelium and is thought to result from both on-target and off-target uptake of the ADC into corneal epithelial cells, leading to findings such as punctate keratitis, ulcerative keratitis, and epithelial microcysts. Dry eye is the most frequently reported manifestation and is attributed to tear film abnormalities related either to reduced tear quality or diminished tear production [81,96]. The susceptibility of the ocular surface is believed to reflect the eye’s rich vascular supply, high density of rapidly renewing epithelial cells, and expression of surface receptors capable of binding antibodies [81].

Most ocular surface events have been low-grade and reversible [52]. Grade 3 or higher keratitis was rare and led to treatment discontinuation only in isolated cases. In TROPION-Lung01, increased lacrimation, dry eye, and keratitis occurred at low frequencies, with keratitis reported in 4% and grade 3 events in only 1.3% of participants [52]. Similar findings were reported in TROPION-PanTumor01 and TROPION-Lung05.

Monitoring strategies for OSE include scheduled ophthalmologic examinations at baseline and at symptom onset, with evaluation of visual acuity, intraocular pressure, and slit-lamp findings [29,81,96]. The use of preservative-free artificial tears and avoidance of contact lenses are also emphasized. Dose delays or reductions are usually reserved for persistent grade 2 or higher events, with permanent discontinuation implemented only when toxicity does not improve despite adjustments [52,96,97] (Table 3).

Conclusions and future directions

The accelerated approval of Dato-DXd in the US has expanded therapeutic options for patients with EGFR-mutated NSCLC who have progressed on EGFR-directed therapy and platinum chemotherapy. Dato-DXd is currently under investigation in the first-line setting in combination with osimertinib in the phase III TROPION-Lung14 trial, with the goal of reducing or delaying EGFR-TKI resistance and extending therapeutic benefit. In the post-osimertinib setting, the updated data from the ORCHARD platform trial demonstrated that although the response rates were modest, the 6 mg/kg Dato-DXd plus osimertinib combination was associated with improved mPFS (11.7 months) and durable responses [51]. Although these findings remain exploratory, the overall efficacy signal is encouraging. Pending results from the randomized phase III TROPION-Lung15 trial are expected to further evaluate whether combining Dato-DXd with continued osimertinib can improve clinical outcomes over chemotherapy and expand therapeutic options in second-line settings. In addition, given the relatively low hematologic toxicity of Dato-DXd, the combination with osimertinib could potentially serve as a chemotherapy-sparing option for those unable to tolerate systemic chemotherapy-based regimens.

Overall, Dato-DXd demonstrated a favorable safety profile with lower rates of grade ≥3 AEs compared to standard chemotherapy. However, mucositis, ILD/pneumonitis, and ocular surface events require prophylaxis, monitoring, and early intervention. Although improvements have been made with the implementation of management guidelines, further studies are needed to better understand the pathophysiology of these events and patient-specific factors that contribute to greater severity and higher incidence. For ILD, these studies may include blood-based biomarker analyses of epithelial injury and macrophage-driven inflammation. In addition, comprehensive analyses of patients’ clinicopathologic factors could help predict the risk of AESIs more broadly.

Considering the low, albeit clinically meaningful, risk of serious lung toxicity with Dato-DXd, improved prevention and management of severe ADC-induced ILD may have a significant clinical impact. For instance, given the possible relationship between higher initial Dato-DXd dosing, durability of response, and the numerically higher frequency of ILD observed in the 6 mg/kg cohort in ORCHARD, strategies for mitigating the risk of severe ILD could potentially improve patients’ ability to tolerate higher doses while deriving maximum clinical benefit. The potential for overlapping toxicities with Dato-DXd plus osimertinib is also important to consider. Although at lower frequencies, osimertinib is independently associated with a risk of ILD [98,99]. Gastrointestinal adverse events, as well as fatigue and mucositis, may also overlap. While some of these toxicities were observed at higher frequencies in the ORCHARD 6 mg/kg cohort, more data are needed to fully assess their clinical significance and the overall benefit-risk profile. Therefore, pending safety data from TROPION-Lung15 and TROPION-Lung14 will be instrumental in better characterizing the toxicity profile of Dato-DXd plus osimertinib regimens.

Future implementation of Dato-DXd in clinical practice may also be influenced by an enhanced understanding of EGFR-TKI resistance biology. Although translational studies have shown increased TROP2 expression in EGFR-mutated tumors after osimertinib exposure, it remains unclear how increased TROP2 expression relates to factors beyond Dato-DXd cell-surface target engagement. QCS-NMR has provided valuable insight into TROP2 expression and internalization dynamics and their association with efficacy; however, more research is needed to better understand the interplay between TROP2 biology and the intracellular mechanisms driving proliferation. Available studies on ADC sequencing suggest that payload plays an important role in ADC resistance, as rechallenging patients with ADCs carrying the same target but different payload has been shown to restore disease control [100,101]. Similar sequencing analyses with Dato-DXd are pending and may help elucidate the role of payload in Dato-DXd-specific resistance. Furthermore, emerging biomarker data suggest that DNA repair mechanisms may affect how well lung cancer responds to the Dato-DXd payload, potentially providing new direction for patient selection and combination regimens [63]. Lastly, given the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment in EGFR-mutated disease, further systematic evaluation of the possible effects of the TME on Dato-DXd efficacy could also yield new approaches to treatment [63,102–104]. Taken together, an improved understanding of the factors behind differential response to Dato-DXd could inform new drug combinations, treatment sequencing, and settings in which Dato-DXd can provide the most benefit.

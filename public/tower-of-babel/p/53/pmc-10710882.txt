Letter to the Editor Concerning “A Modified Spinal Reconstruction Method Reduces Instrumentation Failure in Total En Bloc Spondylectomy for Spinal Tumors,” by Shinmura et al
Authors: Shun Umeki, Tadatsugu Morimoto, Hirohito Hirata, Masaaki Mawatari
Published: 2023

To the Editor:

We were highly interested in the recently published article by Shinmura et al.1), who concluded that modified spinal reconstruction method substantially reduced instrumentation failure (IF) incidences in patients with total en bloc spondylectomy (TES). Although the innovations have reduced IF and cage subsidence, concerns regarding a few technical aspects persist.

First, we agree that a larger end cap can prevent IF and cage subsidence owing to dispersion and stress reduction. Moreover, the recent availability of larger end caps that support the load at the vertebral limb (X-COREⓇ, NuVasive) may be beneficial. However, a large end cap would decrease the contact area with the grafted bone at the vertebral endplate, which would be detrimental to graft bone ingrowth. Additionally, TES is characterized by a large dead space around the cage, which raises concerns regarding the stability of the grafted bone. We are curious at to what measures were taken to address these issues.

Second, bone-modifying drugs have recently been used widely for metastatic bone tumors2); thus, we were concerned whether bone-modifying drugs would affect bone healing and IF of TES. The use of bone-modifying agents could have been a confounding factor between the two groups.

Third, although the presence of vertebral endplate cysts around the cage after posterior lumbar interbody fusion has been established to be a predictor of bone fusion3), we propose that a study between the two groups may provide new insights into the exact mechanism of bone fusion.

Finally, with the raising number of cases of older patients with cancer, osteoporosis may be a concern in TES. It would be insightful to know what measures were taken, if any, to address this.

We would appreciate your comments on these concerns to allow us to further corroborate the results of this crucial study.

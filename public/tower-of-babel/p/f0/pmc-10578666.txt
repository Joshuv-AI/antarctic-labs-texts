Red blood cell distribution width has a prognostic value for gastric cancer patients after gastrectomy: A pooling-up analysis
Authors: Ze-Lin Wen, Xiong Zhou, Dong Peng
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Our study aims to investigate whether preoperative red blood cell distribution width (RDW) has a prognostic value for patients after gastric cancer (GC) surgery. We searched articles in 3 databases including PubMed, Embase, and the Cochrane Library on May 16th, 2022. The prognostic indicators included overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS). RevMan 5.3 (The Cochrane Collaboration, London, United Kingdom) and Stata V16.0 were used for statistical analysis. The Risk Of Bias In Non-randomized Studies—of Interventions tool was used to assess risk of bias of the included studies. Ten articles involving 2740 patients were included. RDW was a prognostic factor for OS (hazard ratio = 1.81, 95% confidence interval [CI] = 1.38–2.37, P < .01) and DFS (hazard ratio = 1.99, I2 = 26%, 95% CI = 1.53–2.58, P < .01) for GC patients. Meanwhile, there were some differences between the high RDW group and the low RDW group. We found more patients older than 60 years old (OR = 2.58, 95% CI = 1.08–6.13, P = .03), larger tumor diameter (OR = 1.95, 95% CI = 1.33–2.85, P < .01) and later T stage (OR = 1.91, 95% CI = 1.07–3.42, P = .03) in the high RDW group than the low RDW group. No statistic difference was found in gender, N stage, tumor node metastasis stage, vascular invasion, differentiation, and adjuvant therapy between the 2 groups (P > .05). RDW was an independent prognostic factor for both OS and DFS of GC patients. High RDW level were strongly associated with poor survival.

1. Introduction

Gastric cancer (GC) was a common malignant tumor, and ranked fifth about the cause of cancer-related death worldwide.[1] Due to the deficiency of early diagnostic markers and nonspecific clinical symptoms, patients were usually diagnosed as GC at late stages accompanied with a poor survival.[2] With the development of economy and technology, there were many treatments including surgery, adjuvant chemotherapy, immunity therapy, and targeted therapy, but radical surgery remained the main treatment.[3–5] Some studies reported that inflammation might take an important part in the development of GC.[6–8] However, more specific and reliable tumor markers were needed to identified to improve the prognosis of GC patients.

Red blood cell distribution width (RDW) was one of the routine complete blood count test parameters which contained RDW-CV (RDW coefficient of variation) and RDW-SD (RDW standard deviation). RDW-CV was reported as a %, RDW-SD was an actual measurement of the width of the red cell distribution curve in femtoliters. It was an index indicating the variability in the size of red blood cells and could reflect inflammation and nutrition status.[9] Thus, RDW might have a prognostic value for inflammation and malignant diseases. Recently, it was reported that patients with high RDW level had a high risk of death and cardiovascular disease.[10–12] Moreover, other studies also reported an association between RDW and cancers including lung cancer, breast cancer, and liver cancer.[13–15]

As for the prognostic value of RDW on GC, there was a controversy. Some studies reported that RDW had a prognostic value,[16–22] while others reported the negative impact of RDW.[23–25] Therefore, our study aimed to comprehensively analyze the relationship between RDW and GC.

2. Methods

We conducted the current study according to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses statement.[26]

2.1. Literature search strategy

We conducted the searching strategy on May 16th, 2022. Three database including PubMed, Embase, and the Cochrane Library were enrolled for searching. RDW was searched as (“red blood cell distribution width” OR “red cell distribution width” OR “RDW”). As for GC, we used (“gastric cancer” OR “gastric carcinoma” OR “gastric neoplasms” OR “stomach cancer” OR “stomach carcinoma” OR “stomach neoplasms”). All the searches were limited in titles and abstracts. Language was limited in English.

2.2. Definition

GC was determined according to The Japanese Classification of Gastric Carcinoma.[27] Overall survival (OS) was defined as the time from surgery to death from any cause or the last follow-up date.

2.3. Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria of our study included: (1) patients were diagnosed as GC and underwent radical surgery; (2) preoperative RDW level was reported; and (3) at least one survival indicator including OS or disease-free survival (DFS) was reported in multivariate analysis. The exclusion criteria of our study were as follows: (1) articles were published as letters, case reports, comments, reviews, conferences or unavailable full text; and (2) data was overlapped.

2.4. Searching selection

After excluding duplicate articles, we screened the titles and abstracts, independently. Full texts would be read for final researching in accordance with the inclusion and exclusion criteria. Articles with sufficient data were included in this study. Authors would have a discussion to reach a consensus when disagreement occurred.

2.5. Data collection

We collected baseline information of the included articles such as the first author, published time and article date. Moreover, we collected characteristics including age, gender, tumor size, tumor stage, and differentiation. As for OS and DFS, effects in multivariate analysis were collected. Data was collected by 2 authors, respectively.

2.6. Quality assessment

The Risk Of Bias In Non-randomized Studies—of Interventions was used for quality assessment. The following sections were considered in this tool: (1) confounding; (2) selection of participants for the study; (3) classification of interventions; (4) deviations from intended interventions; (5) missing data; (6) measurement of outcome; and (7) selection of reported result.[28] Therefore, the bias risk of included articles was classified into 4 levels including low, moderate, serious, and critical.

2.7. Statistical analysis

In order to investigate the prognosis of GC patients after radical surgery, we pooled up the hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) of OS and DFS. The chi-squared test and the I2 value were conducted to calculated the heterogeneity of results.[16,29] Articles were considered with high heterogeneity when I2 > 50%, and I2 < 50% presented a low heterogeneity. Radom model and fixed model were used for articles with high heterogeneity and low heterogeneity, respectively. P < .1 was considered statistically significant in a random model and P < .05 for fixed model. RevMan 5.3 (The Cochrane Collaboration, London, United Kingdom) was majorly used for statistic analyzing in the current study.

2.8. Publication bias analysis

When there was no obvious heterogeneity (I2 < 50%) in the results, the funnel plot would be used as a publication bias analysis; Egger tests were used when the results were significantly heterogeneous (I2 > 50%).[17] In the Egger test, P < .05 indicated significant publication bias, and P > .05 indicated no significant publication bias. Stata V16.0 software was used for publication bias analysis.

3. Results

3.1. Article selection

The searching strategy was conducted in 3 databases including PubMed (20 articles), Embase (33 articles), and the Cochrane online library (1 article). Totally, 54 articles were searched, 37 articles were left after duplicates removing. Then, 13 articles were selected by reading the titles and abstracts (20 articles were excluded for unrelated titles and noncompliant article types, 3 were excluded for unrelated abstracts). Finally, 10 articles involving 2740 patients were left after full-text screening according to the inclusion and exclusion criteria (Fig. 1).[18–25,30,31] During the process of reading the full text, references were also browsed to find eligible articles.

3.2. Baseline characteristics

As for the enrolled articles, the publication year ranged from 2017 to 2022, and the study period was from 2005 to 2019. The articles were published in China, Japan, and Turkey. All the articles reported OS as prognostic factor, 5 articles reported DFS. 3.3. Characteristics between the high RDW group and the low RDW group

We also collected characteristics of patients in the high RDW group and the low RDW group to compare the difference between the 2 groups. All the included articles were non-randomized studies of interventions, meanwhile, due to the high heterogeneity between included articles, random-effects model was used as the default choice. We found that the high RDW group had more patients older than 60 years old (OR = 2.58, 95% CI = 1.08–6.13, P = .03), larger tumor diameter (OR = 1.95, 95% CI = 1.33–2.85, P < .01), and later T stage (OR = 1.91, 95% CI = 1.07–3.42, P = .03). Additionally, we found no statistic difference in gender, N stage, tumor node metastasis stage, vascular invasion differentiation and adjuvant therapy (P > .05) (Table 2).

3.4. OS and DFS of RDW

After pooling up the HRs and CIs from ten included articles, we found that patients with high preoperative RDW level were related to worse OS (HR = 1.81, 95%CI = 1.38–2.37, P < .01) (Fig. 2). There were 5 articles reporting DFS and after pooling up the HRs and CIs of 5 articles, statistical significance was also found between RDW and GC (HR = 1.99, 95%CI = 1.53–2.58, P < .01) (Fig. 3).

3.5. Sensitivity analysis

To access the sensitivity, each article was excluded at a time for duplicate statistic analyzing. And there was no variation in terms of consequence. Due to the high heterogeneity of the articles, we included subgroup analyses for sensitivity test. First, articles were divided into 2 subgroups according to the sample size was greater or <300, however there still was no significant difference in results. Then, articles were divided into 3 subgroups according to publication country including China, Japan, and Turkey. We found that there was no heterogeneity in the subgroup of China and Japan (I2 = 0%), but a high heterogeneity in Turkey (I2 = 89%). Therefore, we thought that area difference might contribute to the big heterogeneity.

3.6. Publication bias analysis

We found that there was a high heterogeneous (I2 = 69%) of OS, therefore, Egger tests were conducted. As for OS, there was a significant publication bias (P = .0001 < 0.05). As for DFS, there was no significant publication bias (P = .1955 > 0.05).

4. Discussion

Our study totally enrolled 10 articles including 2740 patients for statistic analyzing. Ten articles reported OS and 5 articles reported DFS as prognosis. After pooling up the HRs and 95% CI, we found that RDW was an independent prognostic factor both for OS and DFS. Meanwhile, baseline characteristics including age, tumor diameter, and T stage were significantly different between the high RDW group and the low RDW group. No statistic difference was found in gender, N stage, tumor node metastasis stage, vascular invasion, differentiation and adjuvant therapy between the 2 groups.

Recent years, GC was still a malignancy with strong harm, and cancer-related death mainly occurred in elderly people.[32] Prognostic indicators were needed identifying to improve the survival.[33,34] Meanwhile, some articles investigated that several cancers were caused by inflammatory responses, and then lead to malnutrition.[35–38] Therefore, some inflammation parameters including platelet distribution width,[24] neutrophil to lymphocyte ratio,[39] mean corpuscular volume,[14] RDW[18–25,30,31] and white blood cell count[40] were reported to have close correlations with poor prognosis for GC patients.

Previously, there was a disagreement on whether RDW had a prognostic value. Some studies reported that RDW was an independent prognosis for OS.[18–23,30,31] But Fu L et al demonstrated that RDW was not a predictor for stage II–III GC patients.[24] Cui MT et al analyzed 151 patients and reported that RDW did not seem to impact OS of GC patients.[25] As for DFS, 4 articles all found that RDW was an independent prognosis for GC,[19,20,24,31] however, Zhou DY et al thought that RDW was not markedly associated with DFS.[22]

As was known to us, RDW had a close relationship with many pathological disorders such as anemia, inflammation and malnutrition. Meanwhile, GC could lead to chronic inflammation and nutrition deficiencies. Some advanced GC patients could present with symptom of anemia.[41] Thus, RDW might have a prognostic value by presenting the body’s inflammation and nutrition condition. We speculated that more possible mechanism might be immune dysfunction. Schreiber RD et al reported that immune system not only protected the body, but also promoted tumor growth.[42] When cancer cells occurred, immune cells could inhibit tumor growth by destroying the cancer cells or inhibiting their growth. However, this process left tumor cells which were more suitable for survival, meanwhile, created an environment that promoted tumor progression. Other potential mechanisms were also mentioned including platelet activation, angiogenesis and cytokine activation.[43] These mechanisms played a role in the prognostic value of RDW together, and more basic researches were required to confirm the accuracy in the future.

In order to resolve the current dispute, we focused on the relationship between preoperative RDW level and GC patients after surgery. Our article was the first meta-analysis talking about the prognostic value of RDW for GC. All the articles enrolled were relatively new, the earliest one was published in 2017. However, there were some limitations. First, the sample size of 10 articles including 2740 patients was not large enough, and all of them were retrospective articles, which might lead to a reporting bias. Second, the cutoff value of RDW was inconsistent, even Hirahara N[21] reported RDW-SD but others reported RDW-CV, which might be the cause of high heterogeneity and lead to bias. Third, there was only 5 articles reported DFS and one article reported CSS as prognosis. Thus, more prospective articles of comprehensive survival indicators were needed.

5. Conclusion

Generally, RDW was an essential prognostic factor for GC patients after radical surgery. Both OS and DFS were significantly associated with preoperative RDW level. RDW was useful in managing GC patients in routine clinical settings, meanwhile, surgeons should take measures in advance to correct high RDW level and further improve patients’ prognosis.

A Comparative Evaluation of Visual, Laser Fluorescence, Near-infrared Light Transillumination, and Digital Radiography in the Diagnosis of Occlusal Enamel Caries in Primary Teeth: An In Vitro Study
Authors: Liz Mary Gerard, Nekkanti Sridhar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAim and backgroundCaries remains the most prevalent chronic disease on a global scale and is considered a dynamic, reversible process when diagnosed early. If enamel caries is detected in its initial stages, preventive measures and simple restorations can be implemented. While growing evidence supports near-infrared light transillumination (NILT) for detecting caries in permanent teeth, research on its efficacy in primary teeth is limited. Several in vivo studies have been conducted without appropriate validation. This study aims to assess the effectiveness of DIAGNOcamTM in detecting occlusal caries at an initial stage and compare it to ICDAS II criteria, DIAGNOdentTM, and digital radiography using histological validation as a reference standard.Materials and methodsThirty-five primary molars with early occlusal enamel caries were examined and scored using ICDAS II criteria, DIAGNOdent™, VistaScan™, and DIAGNOcam™. Ground sections of the teeth were prepared for histological validation, and the presence of caries was evaluated using Downer's criteria.ResultsDIAGNOcam™ exhibited the highest area under the ROC curve (0.83), with a sensitivity score of 96.7%, specificity of 50%, and an accuracy rate of 91.4%. VistaScan™ exhibited the lowest sensitivity (46.8%) and specificity (33.4%) among all four methods. There was a statistically significant correlation between visual examination, VistaScan™, DIAGNOdent™, and DIAGNOcam™ with histology. Additionally, a statistically significant correlation was observed between visual examination, VistaScan™, and DIAGNOdent™ with DIAGNOcam™.ConclusionDIAGNOcam™ has demonstrated superior performance in detecting early occlusal caries, achieving results nearly comparable to the histological gold standard. Introduction

Caries remains the most prevalent chronic disease, and is considered reversible when detected early.1 Recent approaches in caries management prioritize prevention over traditional “drill-and-fill” methods, emphasizing more research on early caries detection.2 However, identifying initial enamel changes can be challenging. In daily dental practice, visual inspection remains the primary diagnostic tool, with X-rays serving as an adjunct.3

Digital radiography has improved image processing and evaluation while reducing radiation exposure from 30 to 80%.4 VistaScan Mini™ (Dürr Dental, Bietigheim-Bissingen, Germany) is an SD digital system that employs phosphor plates and utilizes DBS-Win software for enhanced image filtering. However, radiography lacks sensitivity for early caries and is unsuitable for routine monitoring due to ionizing radiation.5 The European Academy of Paediatric Dentistry (EAPD) encourages the development of radiation-free diagnostics to enable safe and unconstrained imaging.6

DIAGNOdent™ (KaVo, Germany) is a laser diode that detects early caries using the principle of laser fluorescence (LF) at a wavelength of 655 nm. The light emitted induces fluorescence in bacterial porphyrins, with the fluorescence intensity directly correlating with lesion depth. Healthy tooth structures show minimal fluorescence, whereas demineralization increases fluorescence intensity. It is applicable for both primary and permanent teeth.7

Introduced by the same company in 2012, DIAGNOcam™ utilizes near-infrared light transillumination (NILT) at a wavelength of 750 nm. The system's flexible arms contain a NILT source that emits light, penetrating the gums and alveolar bone before exiting through the tooth crown. A CCD sensor then captures this light and converts it into a software-generated image. Due to reduced scattering, longer wavelengths allow deeper penetration into structures. This enhanced penetration of NILT through enamel improves the contrast between the caries lesion and the surrounding healthy tooth.8

Although NILT has shown promise for detecting caries in permanent teeth, research on primary dentition remains limited, primarily focusing on interproximal caries. Several studies validated DIAGNOcam™ using radiography, though X-ray diagnostics have low sensitivity for early lesions.8–12 Therefore, radiography is only acceptable for validation to a certain extent. Other investigations used a bur to open and assess the lesion depth, which lacks precision and ethical suitability for early caries.13–16

Histology-based investigations are essential for accurate validation. The purpose of this study was to investigate the effectiveness of DIAGNOcam™ in assessing occlusal enamel caries at an initial stage and to compare it to ICDAS II criteria, DIAGNOdent™, and digital radiography using histological validation as the gold standard. The study hypothesizes no significant differences in effectiveness among these diagnostic methods for detecting early occlusal enamel caries in primary molars.

It was undertaken in the Department of Pediatric and Preventive Dentistry, Manipal College of Dental Sciences, Manipal, Karnataka, in collaboration with Bapuji Dental College and Hospital.

Extracted primary molars with early occlusal enamel caries (extending up to DEJ) were included in the study. Teeth that exhibited cavitation involving the marginal ridge, a significant caries cavity on smooth or occlusal surfaces, with extensive proximal or occlusal restorations or sealants, enamel or dentine developmental abnormalities, or extensive noncaries tooth surface loss were excluded from the study. After obtaining the primary molars that met the inclusion criteria, they were decontaminated by immersing for 20 minutes in a 5.2% sodium hypochlorite solution, then rinsed with distilled water for 20 minutes. The teeth were then cleaned with an ultrasonic scaler using prophylactic paste. Any remaining prophylactic paste on the samples was thoroughly rinsed with distilled water. For ease of handling, the teeth were then placed on plaster blocks.

Visual examination: Examination of teeth was done using operating light illumination, mouth mirror, ball-ended probe, and compressed air. The caries extent was scored using the ICDAS II criteria. Teeth with caries extent corresponding to scores 1 to 3 in the ICDAS II criteria were included in the study (Table 1).

DIAGNOdent™ pen 2190 (Fig. 1): The DIAGNOdent™ pen was used as indicated by the instructions of the manufacturer. Probe A was used in the present study to assess the teeth by placing it into the pits and fissures vertically to obtain the greatest fluorescence value. The measurements were interpreted following the manufacturer's guidelines (Table 1).

DIAGNOcam™ 2170 (Fig. 2): The examination was performed with the dental unit's light source turned off. After gently drying with compressed air for 5 seconds, the handpiece was aligned parallel to the occlusal surface and centered on the area to be examined. Only one tooth was photographed per image to focus the region of interest as precisely as possible. KID software (KaVo Integrated Desktop/version 2.4.1.6374, KaVo, Biberach, Germany) was used to capture and store the images. Carious changes appeared darker in comparison to healthy tooth substance (Table 1).

DIAGNOcam™ 2170 (KaVo, Biberach, Germany)

Digital radiography (Fig. 3): Radiographic examination was carried out on a VistaScan image plate with a light protection cover of the same size. The image plate was parallel to the tooth's long axis, and the X-ray beam was perpendicular to both the long axis of the tooth and the image plate. With the standardization of 8 mA, 70 kVp, 0.14 s, exposure was taken with a focal point to film distance of 20 cm. The image plate, along with the light protection cover, was placed straight and centrally onto the input unit, with the opening side pointing down onto the input unit. The fixing mechanism moved forward automatically and took hold of the image plate with the light protection cover. It was lowered into the device from its light protection cover until it was automatically drawn in. The fixing mechanism held the light protection cover and did not move it into the unit. The touch screen displayed the scanning progress. The image data was transmitted to the imaging software automatically. After it was scanned, the image plate was erased and dropped into the collection tray. The International Caries Classification and Management System (ICCMS) criteria were followed, and the extent of caries was recorded (Table 1).

VistaScan™ Image Plate Scanner (Dürr Dental, Bietigheim—Bissingen, Germany)

Histological validation (Figs 4 and 5): Histologic examination was performed after all three diagnostic methods had examined the teeth. The teeth were mounted on methyl methacrylate and then hemisected using a 0.1 mm thick diamond saw mounted on a microtome (Leica SP 1600 Saw Microtome) in a mesiodistal direction with approximately 100 µm thickness. Sections were then viewed under a light microscope (Leica Wild M3Z Stereozoom Microscope with ProgRes SpeedXT core 3) at 40X magnification. Images were captured and stored in ProgRes® CapturePro Software by Jenoptix. The extent of caries in enamel or dentin was then assessed using Downer's criteria (Table 1).

Results

Data were analyzed using the statistical package SPSS 22.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL and the level of significance was set at p < 0.05. To assess the proportion of each category of the distinct groups, descriptive statistics were used. Inferential statistics were done using the Chi-square test. Sensitivity analysis was done to assess the sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV), negative predictive value (NPV), accuracy, and AUC from ROC.

Histological validation of 35 teeth disclosed that two teeth (5.71%) were found to be sound (D0), 19 teeth (54.2%) had carious lesions confined to the outer half of the enamel (D1), 13 teeth (37.14%) had carious lesions involving the inner half of the enamel up to the DEJ (D2), and one tooth (2.8%) had caries affecting the outer half of the dentin (D3) (Table 2).

Sensitivity analysis

To determine their diagnostic precision, the area under the curve (AUC) was calculated for comparisons between ICDAS II, VistaScan™, DIAGNOdent™ and DIAGNOcam™ versus the histological results. The AUC for DIAGNOcam™ was 0.83, followed by DIAGNOdent™, which had an AUC of 0.79. For ICDAS II, AUC value was 0.71, and the AUC value for VistaScan™ was 0.62.

Discussion

The objective of this in vitro study was to evaluate the efficacy of DIAGNOcam™ to detect occlusal caries at an early stage and to compare it with ICDAS II criteria, DIAGNOdent™, and digital radiography using histological validation as a reference standard.

Visual-tactile examination serves as the first and most essential method for detecting and diagnosing dental caries. In this study, ICDAS II exhibited a sensitivity of 80.6% and a specificity of 50%, comparable to the findings reported by Neuhaus et al.17 However, Teo et al.18 reported higher sensitivity values, likely due to differences in sample characteristics. A systematic review by Gimenez et al.19 consolidated findings from multiple studies and reported specificity ranging from 0.73 to 0.99 and sensitivity between 0.27 and 0.81. Visual examination remains a fundamental method for detecting caries; however, it can be a highly subjective technique that leads to variability in diagnostic outcomes, as it is influenced by factors such as lesion severity, examiner expertise, quality of illumination, and quality of visual perception.18,20

In this study, the radiographic method VistaScan™ exhibited the lowest sensitivity of 46.8% and specificity of 33.4%, and this result was consistent with the findings by Sürme et al.21 Similarly, Neuhaus et al.17 concluded that radiography was the least effective diagnostic tool for detecting occlusal caries in primary teeth compared to DIAGNOdent™ and ICDAS II.

The relatively low sensitivity of radiographs to detect initial caries can be attributed to the fact that decalcification levels of 40–60% are necessary for caries to be visible radiographically. Schwendicke et al.22 reviewed radiographic methods extensively and concluded that radiographs have lesser sensitivity and a comparatively higher specificity for detecting early caries lesions, which was moderate for occlusal surfaces but high on proximal surfaces.

DIAGNOdent™ exhibited a sensitivity of 93.2% and a specificity of 40% in this study. This aligns with the findings in the systematic review by Foros et al.,23 documenting sensitivity ranging from 0.63 to 1 and specificity between 0.44 and 1 for DIAGNOdent™ in the occlusal surfaces of primary teeth. Similar results were also documented in the studies by Teo et al.18 and Jablonski et al.24

Souza et al.25 observed higher specificity values than sensitivity values at the D1 and D3 thresholds, contradicting our findings. Similarly, Rodrigues et al.26 reported a specificity of 76.5% and sensitivity of 62.5%, which was inconsistent with our results. These variations in sensitivity and specificity across studies may arise due to differences in cut-off limits, sample conditions, and confounding factors such as plaque or prophylactic pastes.27 To minimize bias, this study implemented strict inclusion criteria, standardized cleaning protocol for occlusal surfaces, and employed manufacturer-recommended cut-off limits.

The evidence supporting the efficacy of NILT in detecting caries is increasing in permanent teeth; however, it is still limited for primary teeth. There are two clinically significant benefits of NILT. First, it has a higher probability of detecting initial caries lesions, allowing for early preventive interventions. Second, there is no superimposition of the interproximal aspect of adjacent teeth on DIAGNOcam™ images, which is evident on radiographs.28

In this study, the sensitivity of DIAGNOcam™ was 96.7% and its specificity was 50%. According to our findings, the correlation between DIAGNOcam™ results and histological results was the closest of the four methods employed. The sensitivity obtained for DIAGNOcam™ in the present study was in congruence with the study conducted by Sürme et al.,21 wherein high sensitivity was recorded at the D1 threshold. Tassoker et al.29 obtained similar sensitivity results corresponding to our study with 96.1% sensitivity, but had a higher specificity of 61.5%. Marinova et al.12 concluded that the DIAGNOcam™ data showed a stronger correlation with the clinical results. Litzenburger et al.30 contrasted with our findings, as DIAGNOcam™ presented low sensitivity (0.21) for enamel caries and was accompanied by moderate specificity (0.65).

Several studies3,31 on caries diagnosis in the interproximal surfaces concluded that DIAGNOcam™ had greater sensitivity and specificity for detecting early enamel proximal caries than radiography. Söchtig et al.8 concluded in their study that DIAGNOcam™ is effective in detecting hidden occlusal and proximal caries that cannot be diagnosed with visual examination. Stavroula et al.28 discovered that NILT was more sensitive than radiography and visual inspection for detecting caries in the outer half and inner half of enamel, including DEJ in the proximal aspect of teeth.

The angle of the incident light combined with the presence of developmental anomalies can increase the proportion of false-positive and/or false-negative results when interpreting caries lesions on DIAGNOcam™ images, particularly for early lesions.28 Additionally, since NILT images are projections of the occlusal area of the tooth, neither the extent of the lesion nor its proximity to the pulp can be accurately assessed. While NILT has proven to be a valuable tool in clinical practice, it cannot be considered a substitute for radiography. A systematic review highlighted that radiography, despite its low sensitivity, has relatively high specificity, making it a critical supplementary method to confirm findings from NILT Combining the sensitivity of NILT with the specificity of radiography ensures effective detection of initial interproximal lesions while reducing false-positive results.1

Area under the ROC curve shows the performance of all methods in detecting either the presence or the absence of occlusal enamel caries. ROC curve analysis in this study demonstrated DIAGNOcam™ had the highest accuracy with an AUC of 0.83 at the D1 threshold, corroborating the findings of Tassoker et al.29 In the study by Sürme et al.,21 the accuracy value (0.94) obtained for DIAGNOcam™ at the D1 threshold is consistent with our findings; however, the AUC (0.968) was slightly higher than in our study. Conversely, Litzenburger et al.30 reported a significantly lower AUC value of 0.43.

The limitation of our study is that, as the sample size is smaller, it may limit the generalizability of our results. In light of the variable performance of the diagnostic instruments, the initial null hypothesis is rejected based on the findings of this study.

Conclusion

DIAGNOcam™ has demonstrated superior results for detecting early occlusal caries, closely approximating the histological gold standard.32,33 DIAGNOcam™ has demonstrated high sensitivity and acceptable specificity in diagnosing early occlusal caries with 91.4% accuracy. For enamel caries detection in primary teeth, DIAGNOdent™ demonstrated greater sensitivity compared to visual examination. DIAGNOcam™ is a valuable diagnostic tool that can be used in conjunction with visual examination to diagnose early enamel caries in primary teeth.

Sagittal Classification of Calcified Lumbar Disc Herniation and Therapeutic Analysis of Percutaneous Endoscopic Interlaminar Discectomy
Authors: Kai Zhang, Yingchuang Tang, Hanwen Li, Peng Peng, Xingbang Ruan, Xiangyan Zhen, Bin Meng, Huilin Yang, Junxin Zhang, Haiqing Mao, Kangwu Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundCalcified lumbar disc herniation (CLDH) is one specific type of lumbar disc herniation (LDH). Currently, there are no uniform classification standards for different calcification degrees of herniated lumbar disc tissue. Percutaneous endoscopic interlaminar discectomy (PEID) has been proved to exhibit great efficacy in treating LDH, but whether it can achieve satisfactory results in treating CLDH with different calcification remains unclear.MethodsA total of 271 patients who suffered from single CLDH (97 patients) or NCLDH (non‐calcified lumbar disc herniation) who received PEID surgery between January 2016 and December 2018 in our hospital participated in this retrospective study. Moreover, CLDH was divided into four grades based on preoperative sagittal CT images, including 34 Grade I, 22 Grade II, 19 Grade III, and 22 Grade IV. Operative time was evaluated between the two groups. In addition, VAS (leg pain), ODI, and MacNab scale score were applied to assess the efficacy of PEID in treating CLDH with different calcification degrees by Student's t tests or χ 2 test.ResultsAll the patients successfully underwent PEID surgery. According to the analysis, the operative time in the CLDH group was significantly longer than that in NCLDH group. Preoperative and postoperative VAS (leg pain) and ODI scores have no significant differences between the CLDH and NCLDH groups. Furthermore, postoperative modified MacNab scale scores and complication rates were almost the same in the two groups. However, there were some differences in terms of VAS (leg pain), ODI, and MacNab scores during LDH patients with different degrees of calcification. CLDH was divided into four grades in our study, and the analysis displayed that VAS (leg pain) and ODI scores after operation in the Grade IV CLDH group were both obviously higher than those in the control group. Moreover, the modified MacNab scale showed that excellent or good rates in the Grade IV CLDH group (73%) were lowest, and they were significantly lower than those in the control group (91%). The other statistical indexes such as recurrence and complication rates showed no significant difference in CLDH with different degrees of calcification.ConclusionCLDH was innovatively divided into four grades in our study. PEID is an effective and safe surgical method for treating CLDH; it can achieve a satisfactory outcome. However, it should be considered carefully in the treatment of lumbar disc herniation with severe calcification (Grade IV).Keywords: calcification, calcified classification, lumbar disc herniation, percutaneous endoscopic interlaminar discectomy

Introduction

Calcified lumbar disc herniation (CLDH) is a particular type of lumbar disc herniation (LDH) with an incidence of 4.7%–15.9% [1, 2]. It tends to occur in middle‐aged and elderly people; patients with CLDH often have back and leg pain or intermittent claudication. Calcified discs usually adhere to surrounding soft tissue including nerve roots, and this damage to nerve root or dural sac worsens over time [3]. Conservative treatment is often ineffective. Therefore, for those patients who do not respond to conservative treatment, surgery is often recommended [4]. Traditional open surgery involves removing part of the bone structure and repeatedly stretching the nerve to fully expose calcified disc tissue, which often leads to postoperative back pain and lumbar instability along with more trauma and slower recovery [3, 5]. Hence, minimally invasive surgery was used to reduce surgical trauma and promote postoperative recovery [6, 7].

Percutaneous endoscopic interlaminar discectomy (PEID), one approach of percutaneous endoscopic lumbar discectomy (PELD), has been widely used in the treatment of LDH [8, 9]. It can allow the surgeon to reach the lesion directly and perform compression safely without extensive excision of normal bone structure. Numerous studies have proved its efficacy and safety in treating LDH [9, 10]. Compared to traditional open surgery, PEID exerts less trauma, quicker recovery, and less bleeding [11]. However, whether it can achieve good satisfactory results in the treatment of CLDH is unclear. Moreover, there are no uniform classification standards for herniated lumbar disc tissue with different calcification at present, which leads to the unknown correlation between different calcification degrees of herniated disc and clinical outcome.

In this study, lumbar disc calcification was classified into four grades according to preoperative CT data. The purpose of our study has been divided into the following three aspects: (i) proposing a novel sagittal classification system for calcified lumbar disc herniation; (ii) evaluating the clinical efficacy of percutaneous endoscopic interlaminar discectomy in the treatment of calcified lumbar disc herniation; (iii) investigating the guiding value of different sagittal classifications in surgical decision‐making for calcified lumbar disc herniation.

Materials and Methods

Clinical Data

In this study, we innovatively proposed a type of calcification for CLDH based on preoperative sagittal CT data. The calcification degree of CLDH was divided into four grades according to sagittal CT images, which can demonstrate the most severe lumbar disc calcification of the responsible segment. To be specific, the grades were as follows: (I) lumbar disc calcification is less than 25% of the lumbar disc space; (II) lumbar disc calcification is more than 25% but less than 50% of the lumbar disc space; (III) lumbar disc calcification is more than 50% but less than 75% of the lumbar disc space; (IV) lumbar disc calcification is more than 75% of the lumbar disc space (Figures 1 and 2).

Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Patients were diagnosed as single CLDH or NCLDH by preoperative computed tomography (CT) and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI); (2) Patients suffered low back pain, unilateral radiating pain, and (or) lower limbs numbness which were consistent with radiological data; (3) The symptoms were not alleviated after conservative treatment for at least 3 months; (4) Follow‐up time was at least 12 months; (5) In the CLDH group, calcification is ipsilateral to clinical symptoms. The exclusion standards were: (1) Multilevel CLDH or NCLDH; (2) patients with spinal infection, tumor, serious spinal stenosis, or medical diseases who were not ready for operation; (3) patients with concurrent lumbar spinal stenosis or scoliosis; (4) In CLDH, the herniated disc was calcified, but the calcification was contralateral to clinical signs; (5) Patients with incomplete data.

According to the above inclusion and exclusion standards, 271 patients who suffered from single CLDH (97 patients) or NCLDH (174 patients) who received percutaneous endoscopic interlaminar discectomy surgery between January 2016 and December 2018 in our hospital participated in this retrospective study, including 34 grade I, 22 grade II, 19 grade III, and 22 grade IV CLDH. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University (Ethics Approval No. 2025344).

Surgical Techniques

After general anesthesia, the patient was placed in a prone position. The diseased vertebral space was determined with the assistance of a C‐arm, and a short longitudinal incision was made. The outer diameter working cannula was placed behind the ligamentum flavum between the plate where the endoscope was inserted. The working cannula is pushed from the nerve root shoulder, and the cannula is rotated to push the nerve root inwards and out of the vision. When the position of the disc herniation and calcification was determined, the endoscopic trephine, working cannula, and nucleus pulposus forceps were used to remove the calcified tissue. After the nerve root decompression, the incision was sutured. Postoperative treatment was consistent in all patients.

Specifically, for cases involving calcified disc herniation, we utilized the visualized endoscopic trephine system (Spinendos GmbH, Germany), which allows for precise and safe removal of calcified tissue under continuous endoscopic visualization. Key technical steps for calcified tissue management: (i) Locate and establish working channels: Under C‐arm guidance, the guide needle was positioned at the outer upper edge of the laminar space. The third‐level cannula dilates step by step, revealing the lower edge of the endplate of the upper vertebral body, the ligamentum flavum, and the facet joint. (ii) Expose the calcified intervertebral disc: Part of the lamina and the bone at the medial margin of the facet joint were resected to expand the lamina window, and the working cannula was positioned at the shoulder of the exiting nerve root; the calcified intervertebral discs were fully exposed. (iii) Endoscopic management: Calcified disc fragments were carefully resected using a combination of the rongeurs and endoscopic high‐speed burr when needed, depending on the consistency and location of the calcification. Great care was taken to protect the traversing nerve root and dural sac throughout the procedure.

Clinical and Radiological Measurement

Patients' age, gender, operative time, and follow‐up time were collected. The follow‐up period was at least 12 months. Preoperative CT scan was used to evaluate the classification of CLDH. Postoperative complications such as dural sac injury and recurrence were also evaluated. Visual Analog Scale (VAS) and Oswestry Disability Index (ODI) scores were recorded before surgery, 1 month after surgery, 3 months after surgery, and at final follow‐up. Clinical outcome was assessed by applying the modified MacNab scale at the final follow‐up. The recurrence was diagnosed by patients' symptoms, physical examination, and imaging data.

Statistical Analysis

The statistical data were analyzed by SPSS version 20.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). The continuous variables in the four types were compared by Student t tests or Wilcoxon rank‐sum tests. The categorical variables were compared by χ 2 test and Fisher's precision probability test. p < 0.05 was statistically significant. Bonferroni adjustment was applied for subgroup analyses (Grade I–IV comparisons) to mitigate Type I error risk.

Functional Outcomes

In both groups, there were significant improvements in postoperative leg and back pain. The postoperative VAS (leg pain) and ODI scores in both groups were significantly lower than those before operation. Both groups' symptoms continued to improve at various points after surgery. There was no significant difference in VAS (leg pain) and ODI scores between the two groups at various time points after surgery (Table 2). The overall excellent or good rate at the final follow‐up was 91.38% in the NCLDH group (159/174) and 86.60% in the CLDH group (84/97), with no significant difference found between the two groups (Table 3).

Discussion

Efficacy of Percutaneous Endoscopic Interlaminar Discectomy in the Treatment of Calcified Lumbar Disc Herniation

Currently, the occurrence rate of CLDH is increasing, but its pathophysiology remains unclear [12]. The fundamental principle of surgical treatment for CLDH is the complete removal of compressive nucleus pulposus tissue. Lumbar disc calcification significantly increases the difficulty of percutaneous spinal endoscopic techniques in treating CLDH [13, 14]. With the development of minimally invasive spinal surgery technology and surgical instruments, PELD is widely used in treating LDH and increasingly applied in the treatment of CLDH [15, 16, 17]. However, the safety and efficacy of PEID in treating CLDH is still unknown now. Hence, PEID was performed to treat CLDH and NCLDH in this study.

According to the results, all the patients successfully received lumbar discectomy by percutaneous spinal endoscopy through interlaminar approach. The original lumbar and leg pain, especially radiation pain in the lower extremities, was relieved in all patients. Postoperative VAS (leg pain) and ODI score showed no significant difference between NCLDH and CLDH groups. Meantime, excellent or good rates of patients with NCLDH were almost the same as those of patients with CLDH, which proved the efficacy of PEID in treating CLDH. Xu et al. have reported that PEID is an effective method in the treatment of CLDH, which is consistent with our results [4]. In terms of postoperative complications, the proportion of patients who suffered dural matter tear in NCLDH was slightly lower than that in CLDH. The possible reason is that calcified lumbar disc is attached to the surrounding tissue; the surgeon needs to stretch the nerve to expose a wide enough field for removing the calcified disc, and the nature of the calcified disc adhesion increases the risk of rupture of the dural sac. The recurrence in the two groups was almost the same. As a whole, PEID is a safe and efficient method not only in curing NCLDH but also in treating CLDH.

Sagittal Classification of Calcified Lumbar Disc Herniation

As is known to all, there is no uniform calcified classification for CLDH currently, which contributes to the unclear correlation between different calcification degrees of herniated discs and clinical outcomes. A good classification should be practical, which can provide guiding significance for clinical diagnosis and treatment, and it also should be relatively simple, which is easy to remember and widely used. Hence, we propose a new type of calcified classification for CLDH based on sagittal CT images in this study. CLDH was divided into four grades, and the results showed that patients with different grades of CLDH had different postoperative recoveries. The rate of postoperative early lower limb numbness was highest in the Grade IV CLDH group. A possible reason may be that when surgeons remove the calcified protrusion in high‐grade CLDH cases, fully exposing the surgical field and trephine rotation may result in excessive traction of nerve roots. Meantime, the calcified disc of high‐grade CLDH usually exerts more serious adhesion with surrounding tissue, which also makes it more liable to strain the nerve root. Similarly, Xu et al. reported that the rate of early postoperative dysesthesia in PEID treating CLDH was significantly higher compared to NCLDH, but the difference became non‐significant at 6 months after surgery [4].

The Guiding Value of Sagittal Classifications in Surgical Decision‐Making for Calcified Lumbar Disc Herniation

Based on modified MacNab scale analysis, the overall excellent or good rate in Grade I–III CLDH group was mostly the same as that in NCLDH group, while the overall excellent or good rate in Grade IV CLDH group is the lowest and it was obviously lower than that in the control group. Moreover, VAS (leg pain) and ODI score at varying points after surgery in Grade IV CLDH group was significantly higher than that in NCLDH group. The possible reason may be that the calcified disc of high‐grade CLDH usually exerts more serious adhesion with surrounding tissue, which made it more difficult to resect and more liable to strain the nerve root. Surgeons need to expand the shaping area during laminoplasty and reduce the risk of nerve damage in the treatment of CLDH. In sum, PEID can get high satisfaction in treating low‐medial grade (grade I–III) CLDH, but it may not be recommended and should be carefully considered in the treatment of high‐grade (grade IV) CLDH.

During the treatment of CLDH with PEID, it is of great importance to perform the preoperative evaluation of patients. Accurate assessment of protruding disc calcification and proper indication is the key point to success. PEID exerts a steep learning curve; surgeons should be familiar with local anatomy and master the technical essentials of endoscopy. For large sheets of calcified tissue, endoscopic piecemeal resection is applied. Working cannula and trephine are applied to remove the calcified disc based on the degree of LDH calcification. In terms of excision extension, the objective is to remove the protrusion compressing the nerve root instead of achieving complete radiographic resection. Otherwise, excessive resection of the calcified disc may increase the operation trauma and risk of nerve root [15, 18]. Moreover, postoperative rehabilitation exercise is also important. The patient should do some proper back muscle exercise and reduce frequent bending and sedentariness.

Strengths and Limitations

The strengths of this study include the proposal of a novel sagittal classification system for CLDH, the evaluation of the clinical efficacy of PEID for CLDH, and the exploration of the guiding value of different sagittal classifications in surgical decision‐making for CLDH. However, there are some limitations in this study. This is a retrospective study which compares the outcome of PEID treating NCLDH and CLDH with different calcification degrees. Open discectomy for CLDH may be the ideal control group compared to CLDH treated with PEID. The enrolled small sample size and short follow‐up time are also the shortcomings of this study.

Conclusion

In conclusion, PEID was a safe and efficient method in the treatment of NCLDH and CLDH. During the treatment of CLDH, much attention should be paid to evaluate the degree and location of disc calcification and fully expand the shaping area during laminoplasty, while PEID should be carefully considered when treating CLDH with severe calcification (Grade IV).

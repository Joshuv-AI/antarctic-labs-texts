Perioperative total tau as a biomarker of postoperative delirium after major cardiac surgery: A prospective cohort study
Authors: Mohamed R. Mahmoud, Ali T. Abdelwahab, Tarek A. Abdelzaher, Mohamed M. Ali, Salman Hamdy Mohamed Elsaghir, Hassan Mokhtar Elshorbagy Hetta
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Postoperative delirium is a frequent and serious complication in older adults undergoing cardiac surgery with cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB). Identifying blood-based biomarkers of neuronal injury could facilitate early risk stratification. Total tau (t-tau), a microtubule-associated protein released during axonal damage, may serve as a candidate marker.Methods:In this prospective cohort study, 89 adults aged ≥60 years undergoing elective cardiac surgery with CPB were evaluated. Plasma t-tau levels were measured preoperatively and on postop D1 and D3. Cognitive and functional outcomes were assessed.Results:Delirium was diagnosed in 53% of patients on Postop D1 and 42% on postop D3. T-tau increased significantly from baseline (median 1.46 pg/mL) to postop D1 (3.13 pg/mL; P < 0.001), declining on postop D3 (1.89 pg/mL; P < 0.001 vs baseline). Patients with delirium had significantly higher t-tau levels postoperatively and greater t-tau changes from baseline. Postop D1 t-tau independently predicted postop D3 delirium (OR 4.16, 95% CI 2.39–7.23; P < 0.001). Receiver operating characteristic analysis showed excellent diagnostic performance, with an AUC of 0.905 for postop D1 tau levels in predicting postoperative delirium on postop D3.Conclusion:Dynamic elevations in t-tau may predict postoperative delirium and neurocognitive decline after cardiac surgery with CPB. Therefore, t-tau may represent a promising candidate biomarker requiring external validation for risk stratification and monitoring of early postoperative delirium in older surgical patients.

Introduction

Postoperative delirium (POD) is a prevalent neuropsychological complication affecting up to 54.9% of patients undergoing major cardiac surgery, representing one of the most significant complications in older surgical populations.[123] Beyond acute confusion, POD is associated with prolonged intensive care unit and hospital stays, increased mortality, and persistent long-term cognitive decline and dementia risk.[123] Cardiac surgery with cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) poses particularly high delirium risk, as CPB generates cerebral microemboli, hemodynamic fluctuations, and robust systemic inflammation that converges to produce acute neuronal injury.[345]

Traditional delirium identification relies on clinical observation, substantially underestimating incidence because hypoactive presentations and brief episodes often go undetected.[2] Systematic screening using validated instruments, such as the Confusion Assessment Method for the ICU (CAM-ICU), markedly improves detection sensitivity and has become standard for research and clinical practice.[67] However, objective biomarkers that reflect perioperative neuronal injury mechanisms could substantially advance risk assessment and enable targeted interventions for high-risk patients.

Blood-based biomarkers of neuronal injury, particularly tau protein and neurofilament light chain (NfL), have emerged as promising markers of acute brain dysfunction following surgery.[8] Total tau (t-tau) is a cytoplasmic microtubule-associated protein whose elevated circulating concentration reflects neuronal injury.[89] In the perioperative setting, circulating t-tau rises sharply immediately after major surgery and remains significantly elevated for days.[910] Prior evidence showed that postoperative t-tau demonstrated excellent diagnostic performance for delirium prediction in cardiac surgical patients, with an AUROC of 0.81 (95% CI 0.67–0.96), superior to preoperative t-tau (AUROC 0.70).[10] Similarly, Ballweg and colleagues demonstrated in noncardiac surgical cohorts that the change in plasma t-tau from preoperative to postop D1 correlated with both delirium incidence and severity (ρ = 0.39, P < 0.001) and predicted delirium recovery over time.[9]

The translational potential of perioperative t-tau measurement is substantial: blood-based assays are feasible from routine draws using modern high-sensitivity platforms, avoiding invasive cerebrospinal fluid sampling.[8] If validated in larger, diverse cohorts, t-tau could enable real-time identification of high-risk patients, guide personalized interventions, and serve as an objective endpoint in delirium prevention trials.

To address critical gaps in the tau-delirium literature, we performed a prospective cohort study in older adults undergoing major cardiac surgery with CPB. Our primary objective was to investigate associations between perioperative changes in t-tau and POD incidence, assessed with twice-daily CAM-ICU screening through postop D3. Secondary objectives included evaluating t-tau’s diagnostic and prognostic performance, exploring influences of patient and intraoperative variables on t-tau dynamics, and assessing correlations with cognitive and functional decline.

Methods

Study design and setting

This prospective cohort study was conducted at a tertiary care academic hospital in accordance with the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines for cohort studies.[11] The study received ethical approval from the Institutional Review Board of the hosting institution (MUFMIRB Approval No 1285-12-2024). All participants provided written informed consent after receiving detailed explanations of study procedures, potential risks, and their right to withdraw at any time without affecting clinical care. Data confidentiality was maintained according to institutional data protection protocols.

Study population and selection criteria

Eligible participants were older adults (≥60 years) scheduled for elective major cardiac surgery, including CABG, valve replacement, or combined procedures. Exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) end-stage renal disease (estimated glomerular filtration rate <15 mL/min/1.73m²); (2) hepatic failure (Child-Pugh Class C); (3) diagnosed psychiatric illness requiring active treatment with antipsychotic medications; (4) history of stroke or transient ischemic attack within 6 months; (5) contraindications to general anesthesia; and (6) inability to provide informed consent.

Primary and secondary objectives

The primary objective was to investigate the association between perioperative changes in t-tau protein and the incidence of postoperative delirium assessed using validated bedside screening. The secondary objectives were as follows: (1) to evaluate the diagnostic and prognostic performance of t-tau for POD prediction at preoperative, postop D1, and postop D3 timepoints; (2) to explore the influence of patient characteristics and intraoperative variables (surgery duration, cardiopulmonary bypass time, anesthesia technique) on perioperative t-tau dynamics and delirium incidence; and (3) to assess correlations between t-tau elevations and cognitive and functional decline measured through standardized assessments.

Anesthesia and perioperative management

All patients underwent standardized general anesthesia administered by experienced anesthesiologists. Anesthesia induction included intravenous midazolam (1–2 mg), fentanyl (5–10 μg/kg), propofol (1–2 mg/kg), and atracurium (0.5 mg/kg) for neuromuscular blockade. Anesthesia was maintained with isoflurane at a minimum alveolar concentration of 1.0–1.5, supplemented with opioids titrated to hemodynamic response; additional doses of anesthetic agents were administered intraoperatively based on blood pressure, heart rate, and monitored anesthetic depth. Hemodynamic parameters were continuously monitored and recorded at 15-minute intervals. Surgeries were performed with or without cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB), depending on clinical indications and surgeon preference; when CPB was employed, standard normothermic or moderate hypothermia protocols were used. All perioperative data, including intraoperative complications, medication administration, and vital parameter trends, were recorded in the study database.

Delirium assessment and t-Tau measurement

Postoperative delirium was assessed twice daily (morning and evening) during the first three postoperative days using the Confusion Assessment Method for the Intensive Care Unit (CAM-ICU), a validated, widely used delirium screening instrument with high sensitivity and specificity.[67] The CAM-ICU has been extensively validated in cardiac surgical populations and is recommended by major critical care organizations for systematic delirium detection.[671213] CAM-ICU assessment was performed by trained research personnel following standardized protocols. Delirium was defined as a positive CAM-ICU assessment on any screening encounter during the 3-day study period. The definition and assessment approach align with international consensus definitions of postoperative delirium and current recommendations for delirium research in surgical populations.[14]

T-tau was measured in venous blood collected at three prespecified timepoints: (1) preoperatively (within 24 h before surgery), (2) postop D1 (24 ± 2 h after surgery), and (3) postop D3 (72 ± 4 h after surgery). Blood samples were collected in ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) tubes via standard venipuncture, immediately centrifuged at 1500 × g for 15 min at 4°C, and plasma was aliquoted and stored at − 80°C in a dedicated laboratory freezer until batch analysis. All samples were processed and stored according to standardized biobanking protocols to minimize preanalytical variability that could influence biomarker quantification.

Quantification of plasma t-tau was performed using the INNOTEST® hTAU Ag ELISA kit (Fujirebio, Cat. No. 81579) together with the Tau Ag calibration and reference control pack (Fujirebio, Cat. No. 81580, Ghent, Belgium). All samples were analyzed in duplicate according to the manufacturer’s protocol by laboratory technicians blinded to participant delirium status. The assay’s analytical sensitivity, intra-assay coefficient of variation (within-run precision), and interassay coefficient of variation (between-run precision) were validated according to manufacturer specifications and reported quality control data. T-tau was selected for measurement based on prior evidence linking acute postoperative t-tau elevations to POD development and neuroinflammatory processes in patients undergoing major cardiac surgery, in contrast to preoperative phosphorylated tau species, which show inconsistent associations with POD in cardiac populations.

Cognitive and functional assessment

Comprehensive cognitive and functional assessments were performed preoperatively and repeated on postoperative days 1 and 3 using validated instruments. Preoperative cognitive status was assessed using the Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE), a brief 30-point validated screening tool for global cognitive function that demonstrates adequate sensitivity and specificity for identifying cognitive impairment in older adults.[1516]. Functional status was evaluated using the Activities of Daily Living (ADL) and Instrumental Activities of Daily Living (IADL) scales, which assess independence in basic self-care and more complex functional tasks, respectively.[1718] The MMSE, ADL, and IADL have been extensively validated in cardiac surgical populations and provide standardized objective measures of neurocognitive outcomes.[1718] All cognitive and functional assessments were conducted by trained research personnel in a consistent manner and at standardized timepoints to ensure comparability across participants.

Additional data collection

Comprehensive demographic and clinical data were collected systematically from all participants. Preoperative clinical data encompassed medical comorbidities, current medications, and baseline functional status. Intraoperative variables documented included: surgical procedure type; total surgery duration (time from skin incision to skin closure); cardiopulmonary bypass time (aortic cross-clamp time); anesthetic agents and doses; perioperative hemodynamic data; and perioperative complications. Postoperative outcome variables included: intensive care unit (ICU) length of stay; hospital length of stay; and repeat cognitive and functional assessments on postoperative Days 1 and 3. The study posed minimal risk to participants, as procedures consisted of noninvasive assessments and blood draws obtained during routine perioperative care; there were no randomized interventions or treatment manipulations.

Statistical analysis

Data were summarized using means and standard deviations or medians and interquartile ranges for continuous variables, and frequencies with percentages for categorical variables. The Shapiro–Wilk test was used to assess normality. Group comparisons were performed using Mann–Whitney U tests for continuous variables. For outcomes measured repeatedly across time points, nonparametric repeated-measures analysis was conducted using the Friedman test, followed by Durbin–Conover post-hoc comparisons when overall significance was observed. Paired categorical variables were analyzed using McNemar’s test.

To identify independent predictors of POD, multivariable binary logistic regression models were constructed, and results were expressed as odds ratios with 95% confidence intervals. The diagnostic performance of t-tau concentrations (preoperative, postop D1, and postop D3) was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis. Area under the curve (AUC) values, optimal cutoffs determined by Youden Index maximization, and associated sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy metrics were obtained. Additional clinical decision metrics, including positive and negative predictive values, were derived according to the screening context specified within the analysis. All analyses were performed using Jamovi (version 2.4.8). A two-sided P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Patient selection and characteristics

Among 106 patients initially screened, 89 individuals undergoing cardiac surgery with complete cognitive assessments and t-tau biomarker sampling were included in this study [Figure 1]. The median age was 69 years, and 58.4% were female. The mean BMI was 26.7 ± 2.82 kg/m². All patients had ischemic heart disease, with comorbidities including hypertension (52.8%), diabetes mellitus (31.5%), and chronic kidney disease (14.6%). Regarding medications, 61.8% of patients were receiving aspirin, 47.2% were on beta-blockers, and 19.1% were treated with insulin [Table 1]. In terms of surgical procedures, most patients underwent CABG (n = 71, 79.8%), followed by mitral valve replacement (10.1%) and aortic valve replacement (9.0%). The median duration of surgery was 4.5 ± 1.5 hours, with a mean CPB time of 110 ± 35 minutes. The mean arterial pressure (MAP) during surgery was 75 ± 10 mmHg. Vasopressor support was required in 34.8% of cases. Postoperative complications included arrhythmias (19.1%), infections (13.5%), acute kidney injury (11.2%), and surgical site bleeding (11.2%).

Cognitive and biomarker changes

The incidence of delirium increased markedly after surgery, rising from 0% preoperatively to 53% on postop D1. By postop D3, the incidence had decreased to 42%, yet it remained substantially higher than the baseline. The reduction from postop D1 to postop D3 was statistically significant (P = 0.002), as reported in Table 2. T-tau concentrations demonstrated a marked postoperative increase. Median levels rose from 1.46 pg/mL preoperatively to 3.13 pg/mL on postop D1 (P < 0.001) and remained significantly higher than baseline on postop D3 (1.89 pg/mL; P < 0.001). A significant decline was observed between postop D1 and postop D3 (P = 0.033), although values did not return to preoperative levels.

Comparative analysis between delirious and nondelirious patients

Preoperative t-tau levels did not differ between patients who did and did not develop delirium, Table 3. In contrast, postoperative t-tau values were significantly higher in the delirium group on both postop D1 and postop D3 (P < 0.001 for both comparisons). Correspondingly, postoperative changes in t-tau were substantially greater among patients with delirium, with markedly larger increases observed on both days (P < 0.001 for postop D1 delirium; P = 0.001 for postop D3 delirium). These findings suggest a possible association between postoperative t-tau elevation and the development of delirium.

Baseline MMSE scores were broadly similar between groups, with only a slight difference evident in the postop D3 delirium comparison. Following surgery, patients with delirium exhibited significantly lower MMSE scores on both postoperative days (P < 0.001 for both comparisons).

Preoperative ADL and IADL scores were comparable across groups. On postop D1, both functional measures were significantly reduced in patients with delirium (P < 0.001). Surgery duration, CPB time, and MAP did not differ significantly between patients with and without delirium on either postoperative day, suggesting that intraoperative factors were not significantly associated in this cohort.

Discussion

In this prospective cohort of older adults undergoing cardiac surgery, POD was strikingly common: 53% of patients met CAM-ICU criteria on postop D1, declining to 42% by postop D3. These rates exceed many prior reports for elective cardiac surgery, where older cohorts typically exhibit POD in the 10–55% range.[1920] T-tau levels surged after surgery: median t-tau rose on postop D1 and then declined to 1.89 pg/mL on postop D3, mirroring an acute neuronal injury response.[9102122] Cognitive performance likewise dropped: median MMSE fell from 28 preoperatively to 23 on postop D1, partially rebounding to 27 on postop D3 (still significantly below baseline). Functional status (ADL/IADL) showed a parallel pattern of decline on postop D1, with partial recovery by postop D3. Crucially, patients who developed delirium had markedly higher t-tau postoperatively: on both postop D1 and postop D3, t-tau in delirious patients was several-fold greater than in nondelirious patients. This intensified t-tau release was accompanied by worse cognitive and functional outcomes in the delirium subgroup. In multivariable logistic regression, a higher postop D1 t-tau was a strong independent predictor of delirium on postop D3 (adjusted OR 4.16, 95% CI 2.4–7.2). Receiver operating characteristic analysis confirmed excellent diagnostic performance of perioperative t-tau: postop D1 t-tau achieved a high AUC for predicting ongoing delirium. Thus, early postoperative t-tau elevations predicted later delirium with high sensitivity and negative predictive value, whereas preoperative t-tau had no predictive value.

Our observed rates (53% on postop D1, 42% on postop D3) exceed some prior cardiac surgery series, which have reported delirium in roughly 20–30% of patients,[192023] which might reflect key perioperative factors and intensive monitoring. Older adults undergoing cardiac surgery, especially with CPB, have among the highest delirium risks of any surgical population.[192024252627]

Several factors likely contributed to our higher incidence. First, the universal use of CPB in our cohort may have elevated risk; CPB produces cerebral microemboli, fluctuations in perfusion, and systemic inflammation that predispose to acute brain injury.[192829] Second, we conducted rigorous delirium screening (twice-daily CAM-ICU through postop D3), likely capturing even brief or fluctuating episodes.[7] Broad use of high-sensitivity screening can uncover more cases than less systematic approaches. Third, older age and comorbidity profiles in cardiac patients may further amplify delirium vulnerability. Regardless, as expected, delirium prevalence declined from postop D1 to D3, reflecting the typically transient course of acute POD. Nevertheless, even by postop D3, many patients remained delirious, and those with delirium displayed notable cognitive and functional impairment relative to nondelirious peers. This aligns with prior evidence that POD is not only acute confusion but often portends lingering deficits: delirium has been consistently linked to persistent cognitive decline and loss of independence after surgery.[1928303132]

Our findings on t-tau dynamics are concordant with and extend recent perioperative biomarker studies. We observed that t-tau rises sharply after major surgery and remains elevated for days, a pattern also reported in smaller cardiac cohorts.[10] McKay et al. (n = 38) similarly found significant increases in circulating t-tau and phosphorylated tau (p-tau217, p-tau181) after CPB. However, only t-tau was associated with delirium incidence, with an AUROC of 0.81 [CI 0.67–0.96].[10] Ballweg et al. studied noncardiac surgical patients and likewise demonstrated that perioperative plasma t-tau elevations correlated with both delirium incidence and severity.[9]

In our study, postoperative t-tau was markedly higher in the delirium group. Moreover, the magnitude of the t-tau change (pre-to-post) was significantly greater in patients who developed delirium. These parallels suggest that acute t-tau elevations are a general marker of perioperative neuronal stress and neuroaxonal injury, and that larger t-tau increases herald frank neurocognitive dysfunction.

A growing body of literature links t-tau to POD across surgical populations. In orthopedic and other noncardiac cohorts, higher preoperative levels of phosphorylated tau species (notably p-tau217 and p-tau181) have predicted POD onset.[33] Liang et al. recently reported that in older patients undergoing elective orthopedic surgery, elevated preoperative plasma p-tau217 (and to a lesser extent p-tau181) were independently associated with POD (adjusted ORs approximately 2–4) and produced high ROC AUCs (0.969 for p-tau217 and 0.885 for p-tau181).[33] This suggests that baseline cerebral AD pathology or vulnerability contributes to delirium risk. In contrast, our cardiac surgery data indicate that it is the perioperative surge in t-tau, rather than baseline t-tau, that most strongly tracked with delirium. Notably, we did not observe significant preoperative t-tau differences between eventual delirious and nondelirious patients, consistent with other cardiac surgery reports.[33]

Our results also resonate with studies of other neuronal injury markers. Like t-tau, neurofilament light chain (NfL) is emerging as a POD biomarker of axonal injury.[34353637] Fong et al. found that elevated perioperative plasma NfL (both preoperative and postoperative) predicted incident delirium and was associated with delirium severity and subsequent cognitive decline (PREOP: aOR 3.7 [95% CI 1.1–12.6]; POD2: aOR 4.6 [95% CI 1.2–18.2]).[34] In contrast, Ballweg et al. observed that among several biomarkers (GFAP, NfL, tau), only t-tau level changes predicted delirium recovery,[9] and another study found no delirium association with glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP).[36] Taken together, t-tau and NfL appear to be promising biomarkers of neuronal injury and POD, but variability across cohorts, surgical types, and assay platforms means it remains unclear which is superior.

The very high diagnostic accuracy of postoperative t-tau in our study is notable. Our ROC analyses show postop D1 t-tau performs excellently. McKay et al. reported an AUROC of 0.81 for postop D1 t-tau in identifying delirium,[10] consistent with our strong findings. These performance characteristics underscore the translational potential of perioperative tau measurement.

Clinical implications

Delirium is recognized as one of the most impactful complications in older surgical patients, leading to longer hospital stays, higher mortality, and often persistent cognitive decline or dementia.[28] In this context, biomarkers that predict delirium risk could substantially improve care. Our findings suggest that plasma t-tau, measurable using established immunoassays, could serve as an objective perioperative biomarker of acute brain injury and delirium vulnerability. Unlike invasive CSF sampling, a blood test for t-tau is feasible in the perioperative setting (blood draws are routine) and, with high-sensitivity platforms, yields reliable results.

If validated in larger cohorts, perioperative t-tau measurement could help tailor postoperative management. For example, patients with marked t-tau elevations on postop D1 might be flagged as high-risk and receive intensified monitoring (e.g., more frequent cognitive checks) and preemptive interventions. Nonpharmacologic delirium prevention bundles may reduce POD incidence and severity (e.g., reorientation, early mobilization, sleep promotion). A biomarker-driven approach could help allocate these resource-intensive measures to those most likely to benefit. Moreover, anesthesiologists and surgical teams could consider modifying intra- and postoperative management (e.g., optimizing hemodynamics, minimizing sedation) in tau-positive patients.

Our results also have implications for research and personalized medicine. Since POD is multifactorial, combining t-tau with other biomarkers (e.g., NfL, inflammatory cytokines) and clinical risk factors (age, frailty, baseline cognition) in multivariable models may enhance prediction. Recent machine learning work emphasizes integrating biomarkers like t-tau with measures of frailty or genetics to optimize delirium risk stratification.[38] Ultimately, elevated perioperative t-tau could even serve as a surrogate endpoint in interventional trials: for instance, if an anti-inflammatory or neuroprotective strategy attenuates the postoperative rise in t-tau, it may also reduce delirium.

Study limitations

Our study has some limitations. This was a single-center study with a modest sample size, which may limit generalizability. Although our findings were strong and statistically significant, confirmation in larger and diverse cohorts is needed. We did not include long-term follow-up; it remains unknown whether the acute t-tau elevations we observed predict persistent cognitive decline or dementia. Prior research indicates that biomarkers like NfL remain elevated weeks after delirium and are related to long-term decline,[24] suggesting a similar role for t-tau, but this requires further investigation. We also did not assess other potential confounders in detail (e.g., detailed brain imaging, comprehensive neuropsychological testing, genetic factors), any of which could influence both t-tau and delirium. Finally, while our assay detects t-tau, it does not distinguish t-tau isoforms or phosphorylation states; future work should clarify which t-tau species are most prognostic.

Conclusion

In patients undergoing cardiac surgery with CPB, we observed an acute surge in t-tau after surgery that was profoundly linked to postoperative delirium and cognitive-functional decline. Perioperative t-tau dynamics (especially on postop D1) predicted which patients would continue to experience delirium by postop D3, with excellent diagnostic accuracy. These findings suggest that blood-based t-tau measurement could serve as a candidate biomarker for identifying high-risk patients and informing risk stratification pending validation to preserve brain function in the perioperative setting. Given the serious long-term consequences of POD, integrating tau into perioperative assessment represents a promising step toward personalized strategies for delirium prevention and management.

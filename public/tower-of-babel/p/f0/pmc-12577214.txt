MicroRNA strand ratio disarray promotes temozolomide resistance in glioblastoma
Authors: Fangfang Shen, Pengfei Wu, Zelian Li, Minglong Yang, Zhuolun Li, Yunfeng Nan, Qinglin Yang, Yuanfei Wang, Xuedong Li, Ying Ye, Jinpeng Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDisarray in microRNA (miRNA) strand selection is associated with multiple tumors. However, the mechanisms underlying miRNA strand selection-driven temozolomide (TMZ) resistance in glioblastoma (GBM) remain unexplored.Approach and resultsHere, we observed that the strand selection disarray of miR-92b contributes to enhancing TMZ resistance. The pattern of higher expression of miR-92b-3p and lower expression of miR-92b-5p is significantly correlated with TMZ resistance. In TMZ-resistant GBM cells, miR-92b-3p and miR-92b-5p increased the enrichment of H3K27ac in the COL7A1 promoter region by divergently targeting HDAC9 and FOXP3, thereby elevating COL7A1 expression and mediating collagen deposition. In addition, TUT4 regulated strand selection of pre-miR-92b through uridylation, promoting preference for the 3′ strand (3p). The TUT4 inhibitor aurothioglucose hydrate (ATG-H) blocked the miR-92b strand selection disarray and restored TMZ sensitivity in TMZ-resistant GBM cells.ConclusionsOur study demonstrates that TUT4-mediated elevation of the miR-92b-3p/-5p ratio promotes COL7A1 transcription via silencing HDAC9 and alleviation of FOXP3 targeting, leading to collagen deposition and heightened TMZ resistance. Our results suggest that targeting miR-92b strand selection may serve as a potential therapeutic strategy for sensitizing GBM to TMZ.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s11658-025-00806-7.Keywords: Glioblastoma, Temozolomide resistance, microRNA, Strand selection, Uridylation

Introduction

Glioblastoma (GBM) represents the most commonly encountered primary malignant brain tumors in adults [1, 2]. GBM is characterized by a median survival of 14.6 months after diagnosis, highlighting the severity of the disease and the inadequacy of current therapeutic approaches [3]. As an alkylating agent, temozolomide (TMZ) has been extensively used in the treatment of GBM [4–7]. Unfortunately, a substantial number of patients exhibit inherent or acquired resistance to TMZ [5, 8, 9]. Therefore, exploring molecular mechanisms, identifying new pharmaceutical targets, and developing novel treatments for glioma remains a pressing priority.

Differential microRNA (miRNA) strand selection is a biochemical phenomenon observed across different species, tissue types, or developmental stages [10]. Initially, it was assumed that only one strand was predominantly selected as the mature miRNA [11, 12]. The precursor miRNA (pre-miRNA) is excised from the primary transcripts (pri-miRNA) by the ribonuclease Drosha in the nucleus, transported to the cytoplasm, and subsequently cleaved by Dicer to produce a small RNA duplex [13–16]. On the basis of the thermodynamic stability at the ends, one of the 3p or 5p strands of the pre-miRNA associates with argonaute proteins (AGO) to form an RNA-induced silencing complex (RISC), while the other miRNA strand is subsequently degraded [17, 18]. In addition, terminal nucleotidyltransferases, such as TUT4 and TUT7, modulate miRNA sequences by adding non-templated (NT) nucleotides to the 3′ end in a process known as tailing, with TUT4 predominantly uridylating the majority of miRNAs, whereas TUT7 plays a nonessential role [11]. Finally, uridylation regulates miRNA abundance by affecting the choice of strand selection. However, the ratios of the strands of certain miRNAs vary depending on tissue specificity and disease progression, and the molecular mechanisms underlying such strand switching remains to be elucidated [19]. Here, we report that miR-92b-3p/-5p, originated from the same miRNA precursor, exhibit divergent expression patterns in TMZ-resistant GBMs and perform distinct roles.

miRNAs function by binding to specific sites in the 3′-untranslated region (UTR) of targets, thereby leading to either target cleavage or translational repression [20–22]. In the present study, we identified that higher levels of miR-92b-3p in TMZ-resistant GBM cells target HDAC9, leading to degradation of HDAC9 and subsequently promoting increased H3K27 acetylation levels. Conversely, lower levels of miR-92b-5p alleviate FOXP3 targeting, which increases H3K27 acetylation levels. Acetylation and deacetylation cycles regulate many crucial biological processes [23]. The manipulation of histone epigenetic regulation has proven to be a potent tool in inducing cellular reprogramming [24–26]. Our previous study indicated that the acetylation of H3K9, H3K27, and H3K36 contributes to the development of TMZ resistance in GBM [9]. To date, there has been a lack of research examining the interaction between endogenous acetylation levels and miRNA strand selection and their impact on the development of TMZ resistance in GBMs.

Functional similarity analysis indicates that HDAC9 and FOXP3 are associated with extensive extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling. The ECM is a highly dynamic structure that cells continually remodel through synthesis, degradation, reorganization, and chemical modification [27]. ECM restructuring is pivotal in modulating the advancement of tumor and in the development of chemotherapy resistance [28]. Matrisome proteins, including ECM regulators, ECM-affiliated proteins, and secreted factors, can be derived from cancer cells and are associated with poor patient survival [29, 30]. The stiffening of the tumor stroma, along with the transition of ECM components from a basement membrane-like environment to a densely packed collagen-enriched network, can lead to resistance against initially effective chemotherapy drugs [31]. Reducing collagen cross-linking and fibronectin assembly can facilitate increased drug permeability, thereby enhancing chemotherapy efficacy [27]. Although ECM remodeling is accompanied by the development of TMZ resistance in GBM [28, 32], the mechanism underlying collagen deposition in TMZ-resistant GBMs has not been clarified.

In this study, we investigate the therapeutic implications of miR-92b-3p/-5p in TMZ-resistant GBMs. The increased ratio of miR-92b-3p to miR-92b-5p elevates endogenous acetylation levels by targeting HDAC9 and FOXP3, promotes the enrichment of H3K27ac in the COL7A1 promoter region, activates COL7A1 transcription, and augments collagen deposition, ultimately driving ECM remodeling that facilitates TMZ resistance in GBMs.

Materials and methods

Data collection

The gene expression profiling of parental and TMZ-resistant GBM cells was obtained from the GSE113510, GSE100775, and GSE100736 datasets. The ChIP-seq data were obtained from the GSE113816 dataset. The relative abundance of mono-uridylated miRNA between WT and TUT4 knockout cells was obtained from the GSE183384 dataset. The CGGA microarray database was obtained from the website http://www.cgga.org.cn. The CPTAC protein database was obtained from the website https://proteomics.cancer.gov/programs/cptac.

Cell culture

The HG7 cells used in the study were obtained from discarded GBM specimens [9]. Human glioma cells LN229 (cat. no. TCHu244) and U251 (cat. no. TCHu 58) cells were purchased from the Chinese Academy of Sciences Cell Bank, and their authenticity was confirmed by short tandem repeat (STR) assay (Genetic Testing Biotechnology, China). TMZ-resistant GBM 229R, 251R, and HG7R cells were induced from LN229, U251, and HG7 cells, respectively [9]. The LN229 and 229R cells were grown in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM)/F12 medium with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), while the U251, HG7, 251R, and HG7R cells were grown in DMEM with 10% FBS (Gibco, USA). All cells were incubated in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO2 at 37 °C and were confirmed to be free of mycoplasma contamination.

Cell transfection

Lentivirus transfection was carried out following previously described procedures [33]. Construction of the pLVX-puro plasmids and packaging of the miR-92b-5p, HDAC9 or FOXP3 overexpression lentiviruses were performed at Genechem. For miR-92b-3p, HDAC9, FOXP3, COL7A1, and TUT4 knockdown, the lentiviruses were purchased from Genechem. The corresponding backbone vector was implemented as a control. To introduce lentivirus-mediated transduction, concentrated viral particles were added to cells for 24 h using a medium supplemented with polybrene (Sigma-Aldrich, USA). Subsequently, the culture medium was replaced with a complete medium, and the transfected cells were selected by culturing them in the presence of puromycin (Sigma-Aldrich, USA) for a week. The miR-92b-3p/-5p mimics and inhibitors were custom-synthesized by RiboBio (Guangzhou, China). DharmaFECT 2 (Dharmacon, USA) was employed to transfect the cells with miRNA mimics, miRNA inhibitors, and negative controls. Detailed information on all shRNAs is provided in Supplementary Table 1.

Establishment of TMZ-resistant GBM cells

TMZ-resistant GBM cells were established following procedures outlined in our previous studies [9, 34]. Briefly, LN229, U251, and HG7 cells were seeded into 96-well plates, and the half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) of TMZ was determined using the CCK-8 assay. Then, TMZ was added to the cell culture medium at a concentration equivalent to 1/50 of the IC50 in six-well plates. Once cells exhibited stable growth, drug doses were incrementally increased. Each dose was maintained for 15 days until the end of the fifth month. The resulting TMZ-resistant GBM cells were designated as 229R, 251R, and HG7R.

Fluorescent in situ hybridization (FISH)

All FISH probes were custom-designed and synthesized by RiboBio (Guangzhou, China). The FISH analysis was carried out to identify the cellular location of miR-92b-3p/-5p on coverslips, following the instructions provided by the manufacturer. Prior to sealing, DNA was stained with 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI) for a duration of 3 min. The probe sequences are listed in Supplementary Table 2.

Patients and specimens

In this study, 38 GBM tissue specimens were obtained from patients who had surgical resection at the First Affiliated Hospital of University of Science and Technology of China, with confirmed diagnoses. Recurrent GBMs were defined as an increase in residual lesions after treatment or the emergence of new lesions. Written informed consent was obtained from patients or their guardians for use of tissues and data. Study was approved by the Biomedical Ethics Committee of University of Science and Technology of China on 11 March 2022 (approval no. 2022-N(H)-109).

Xenograft model in vivo

Four-week-old female athymic BALB/c nude mice were purchased from Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). For the in vivo tumorigenesis study, lentiviruses for miR-92b-3p knockdown (3p_KD) or miR-92b-5p overexpression (5p_OE) or negative controls transfected 229R cells (3 × 105) were stereotactically injected into the brain. After the surgery, the mice were treated for 2 weeks with dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) or TMZ (60 mg kg−1 day−1) every 5 days. The intracranial tumors were measured with bioluminescence imaging.

For the ATG-H-inducible TMZ sensitivity model, 229R cells (3 × 105) were stereotactically injected into the brain, and randomized into two groups. Two groups (six mice in each) were as follows: (1) TMZ (60 mg kg−1 day−1) every 5 days and phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) every 3 days combined group; (2) TMZ (60 mg kg−1 day−1) every 5 days and ATG-H (25 mg kg−1 day−1) every 3 days combined group. The intracranial tumors were measured with bioluminescence imaging. The mice were sacrificed, and the brain tissues were removed. The mouse brain tissue was embedded in paraffin and sectioned at a thickness of 4 μm for immunohistochemistry assays. All procedures were approved by the Experimental Animal Management Committee of University of Science and Technology of China on 10 March 2022 (approval no. 2022-N(A)-292).

RNA extraction and qRT-PCR

RNA extraction and qPCR analysis were carried out following the same procedure as previously detailed [35]. RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent (TaKaRa, Japan), and cDNAs were synthesized with a PrimeScript RT reagent kit from the same manufacturer. The SYBR PrimeScript RT-PCR Kit (Roche, USA) was used to quantify the cDNAs according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) served as an internal control. In addition, stem-loop real-time reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) was carried out to analyze miRNA expression. U6 RNA was used as an miRNA internal control. The 2−ΔΔCt method was used to analyze the qRT-PCR data. All primers were synthesized by Sangon Biotech, and detailed information is shown in Supplementary Table 3.

Protein preparation and western blot

Western blot was carried out following the same procedure as previously detailed [33]. For the Western blot assay, GBM cells or tissues were collected in radioimmunoprecipitation assay (RIPA) buffer with proteinase and phosphatase inhibitor cocktails (Selleck.cn, China). Samples underwent sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS)-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (PAGE) and were transferred onto polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes (Millipore, USA). The membranes were blocked in a 5% milk–Tris-buffered Saline with Tween 20 (TBST) solution and incubated separately with primary antibodies (Supplementary Table 4). Following incubation with horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-labeled secondary antibody (ZSGB-BIO, China), the protein bands were detected using the enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) kit (Boster, China).

CCK-8 assay

The vitality of GBM cells was evaluated using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (Dojindo, Japan) and quantified at a wavelength of 450 nm with the BioTek Gen5 System (BioTek, USA).

Colony formation assay

Cells were plated at a density of 0.3 × 103 cells per well in a six-well plate and incubated for 11 days. After washing twice with PBS, the colonies were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min and stained with 0.1% Crystal Violet for 30 min. The number of colonies was quantified using ImageJ software.

5-Ethynyl-2′-deoxyuridine (EdU) assay

Cells were seeded on tissue-culture slides, treated and incubated with 10 µM EdU for 24 h (Ribobio, China). Fixed cells were labeled with Apollo 567 and Hoechst, following the manufacturer’s protocol for EdU assay.

Terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase dUTP nick end labeling (TUNEL) assay

TUNEL assay was performed to detect apoptosis according to the manufacturer’s instructions (Solarbio, China). Briefly, cells were seeded on tissue-culture slides. Fixed cells were then incubated with TdT and dUTP conjugated with a fluorescent dye, and the resulting labeled fragments were detected using a fluorescence microscope. The apoptotic index was calculated as the percentage of TUNEL-positive cells out of the total number of cells counted. The experiment was performed in triplicate for each sample.

Comet assay

Cells were seeded overnight in 12-well plates and treated with TMZ at a final concentration of 100 μM for 72 h. After treatment, cells were suspended in 0.75% agarose and placed on glass slides. The suspension was cast onto a microscope slide and solidified for 20 min at 4 °C. The slides were then immersed in a lysis solution (Trevigen, USA) for 1–2 h at 4 °C, followed by washing in distilled water and electrophoresis. An electric field was applied for 30 min at 25 V and 300 mA. The slides were then stained with 1× propidium iodide (Abcam, USA), neutralized, and observed under fluorescence microscope. All experiments were conducted in triplicate.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

ELISA was conducted on conditioned media collected from each cell culture. Intracellular acetyl-coenzyme A (CoA) and acetyl-carnitine levels were measured using ELISA kits (Ruifan, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

MicroRNA pull-down

Biotinylated miRNA mimics were synthesized by Ribobio (Guangzhou, China) and transfected into LN229 cells. Cells were harvested 48 h post-transfection, rinsed with ice-cold PBS, and resuspended in lysis buffer. Supernatants were incubated with Dynabeads Streptavidin M-280 (Thermo Fisher, USA) and washed five times. Total RNA was extracted with TRIzol reagent, and purified RNA was verified by qRT-PCR.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

IHC was performed as previously described [35]. Formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tissue sections were dewaxed in xylene, rinsed in ethanol, and rehydrated in double-distilled water. Slides were steamed in sodium citrate buffer at 95 °C for 15 min to retrieve antigens. Primary antibodies for HDAC9, FOXP3, COL7A1, and CD8 were applied and incubated at 4 °C overnight. After washing in PBS buffer, tissues were covered with an anti-mouse/rabbit polymer HRP-label for 30 min, then incubated with 3,3′-diaminobenzidine (DAB) chromogen (ZSGB-BIO, China) for 1 min to allow proper brown color development.

Luciferase reporter assay

To perform the luciferase reporter assay, cells were seeded in 96-well plates and transfected with the GV272 luciferase vector (GeneChem, China). Mutant (Mut) and wild-type (WT) versions of HDAC9 and FOXP3 with potential miR-92b-3p/5p binding sites were generated and fused to the GV272 luciferase reporter vector. Firefly luciferase activity was measured in each well using the Dual Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega, USA) and normalized to Renilla luciferase activity, following the manufacturer’s instructions.

Immunofluorescence (IF)

IF was performed to visualize protein expression and localization in cells. Briefly, cells were seeded on 24-well plates and fixed using standard procedures after overnight incubation at 4 °C. Primary antibodies for HDAC9, FOXP3, and COL7A1 were diluted in 1% bovine serum albumin (BSA) and incubated with the cells overnight at 4 °C. The cells were washed three times with PBS and then incubated with TRITC Phalloidin for 1 h at room temperature. Goat anti-rabbit immunoglobulin G (IgG) or goat anti-mouse IgG was used as a secondary antibody. The nucleus was stained with DAPI, and images were captured using a fluorescence microscope (Leica DMi8, Germany).

Picrosirius Red stain and Masson’s trichrome staining

Picrosirius Red staining was performed on paraffin-embedded tissue and collagen-based matrix slides using a kit from Abcam (USA). Collagen fibers were evaluated at magnification using a microscope. Image analysis was performed using ImageJ software by digitizing photomicrographs and quantifying collagen fiber area in black and white without damaging the original polarization image.

Intratumoral collagen deposition was evaluated using Masson’s trichrome staining (Solarbio, China), where collagen fibers were examined under magnification, and cores were considered positive if there was specific blue/green staining observed within the tumor mass.

Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP)

ChIP assays were performed using the EZ-ChIP kit (Millipore, USA) following the manufacturer's instructions. ChIP-PCR products were detected by 4.8% agarose gel electrophoresis. Primer sequences used for ChIP-PCR are listed in Supplementary Table 5.

Cell apoptosis analysis

For the cell apoptosis analysis, 1 × 106 cells were resuspended to form a single-cell suspension and washed twice with PBS. Apoptosis was assessed using the Annexin V-fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC) apoptosis detection kit (BD Biosciences, USA), following the manufacturer's instructions. Apoptosis rates were determined by flow cytometry (BD FACSCanto II, USA).

Gene Ontology (GO) analysis

GO enrichment analysis of miR-92b-3p/-5p was performed using the predicted target genes of miR-92b-3p/-5p and uploaded to the DAVID website (http://david.abcc.ncifcrf.gov/home.jsp). The GO enrichment analysis was carried out by uploading gene sets of the differentially expressed genes correlated with HDAC9 (Pearson r < −0.95) or FOXP3 (Pearson r > 0.95) from the GSE100736 dataset to the DAVID website (http://david.abcc.ncifcrf.gov/home.jsp). A heatmap of the similarities of terms from the analysis of GO was generated using the “binary cut” algorithm from the R package simplifyEnrichment [36]. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Statistical analysis

Differences in variable groups were evaluated using Student’s t-test. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used for at least three groups. Univariate survival analyses were conducted with Kaplan–Meier curves, and the log-rank test using GraphPad Prism 9. Figures were produced using R version 4.3.0 with the “pheatmap” extension package. The Integrative Genomics Viewer (IGV) was used for ChIP-seq data visualization. Significance was determined by P < 0.05.

Results

miR-92b exhibits differential strand selection in parental and TMZ-resistant GBM cells

The strand ratios of miRNAs vary depending on specific tissue types and pathological conditions [37]. To examine strand selection changes in TMZ-resistant GBM cells, we identified six pairs of miRNAs (miR-92b-3p/-5p, miR-652-3p/-5p, miR-30e-3p/-5p, miR-4655-3p/-5p, miR-4763-3p/-5p, and miR-1233-3p/-5p) that exhibit differential strand selection in sensitive and TMZ-resistant GBM cells (GSE100775) (Fig. 1a). Notably, miR-92b exhibited a markedly inverted 3p/5p ratio between sensitive and resistant GBM cells (Fig. 1b; Supplementary Fig. 1a). We further explored two strands derived from the miR-92b precursor, and found higher miR-92b-3p and lower miR-92b-5p expression in TMZ-resistant GBM cells (229R, HG7R, and 251R) compared with parental GBM cells (LN229, HG7, and U251) (Fig. 1c, d; Supplementary Fig. 1b). Similarly, the expression of miR-92b-3p was elevated in recurrent GBM samples compared to primary GBM samples, while miR-92b-5p showed the opposite trend (Fig. 1e). Fluorescent in situ hybridization (FISH) assay further confirmed that miR-92b-3p exhibited increased expression in TMZ-resistant GBM cells and recurrent GBM samples, whereas miR-92b-5p expression was elevated in parental GBM cells and primary GBM samples (Fig. 1f, g; Supplementary Fig. 1c, d). Particularly, we investigated the correlation between the expression pattern of miR-92b-3p/-5p and the prognosis of patients with GBM receiving standard TMZ therapy. Risk score 0 was defined as lower-miR-92b-3p and higher-miR-92b-5p, risk score 1 was defined as lower-miR-92b-3p and lower-miR-92b-5p, or higher-miR-92b-3p and higher-miR-92b-5p, and risk score 2 was defined as higher-miR-92b-3p and lower-miR-92b-5p (Fig. 1h; Supplementary Table 6). Patients with GBM with lower risk scores experienced significant benefit from TMZ chemotherapy compared with those with higher risk scores (Fig. 1i). These observations suggested a potential functional divergence between miR-92b-3p and miR-92b-5p in the regulation of TMZ resistance.

miR-92b-3p/-5p mediate TMZ resistance with opposing effects

Given the contrasting expression trends of miR-92b-3p/-5p in parental and TMZ-resistant GBMs, we investigated whether miR-92b-3p/-5p exert opposing functions in TMZ resistance. To examine the effect of miR-92b-3p/-5p on TMZ resistance, we established orthotopic mouse models bearing GBM xenografts using 229R cells transfected with miR-92b-3p knockdown (3p_KD) lentivirus, miR-92b-5p overexpression (5p_OE) lentivirus, and negative controls, respectively. Fourteen days after GBM implantation, the mice were treated intraperitoneally with TMZ (60 mg kg−1 day−1 per mouse) every 5 days (Fig. 2a). Mice with miR-92b-3p knockdown or miR-92b-5p overexpression-transfected 229R cells exhibited significantly prolonged survival under TMZ treatment (Fig. 2b). At the same time, the mice were monitored by bioluminescence imaging analyses every 7 days. Bioluminescent imaging also revealed that the miR-92b-3p knockdown or miR-92b-5p overexpression efficiently restored the sensitivity of TMZ-resistant xenografts to TMZ treatment (Fig. 2c, d). To evaluate the effect of miR-92b-3p/-5p on TMZ resistance in GBM cells, we overexpressed miR-92b-3p in parental GBM cells and downregulated miR-92b-3p in TMZ-resistant GBM cells; conversely, we downregulated miR-92b-5p in parental GBM cells and overexpressed miR-92b-5p in TMZ-resistant GBM cells (Supplementary Fig. 2a). We found that, under TMZ treatment, miR-92b-3p significantly enhanced GBM cells proliferation and viability, inhibited apoptosis, and reduced DNA damage, whereas miR-92b-5p suppressed GBM cell proliferation and viability, promoted apoptosis, and increased DNA damage (Fig. 2e–j; Supplementary Fig. 2b-h). Overall, these results provide evidence that miR-92b-3p promotes TMZ resistance in GBM cells, whereas miR-92b-5p restrains TMZ resistance in GBM cells.

Increased miR-92b-3p/-5p ratio promotes intracellular acetylation levels

Next, we investigated the regulatory mechanisms by which an elevated miR-92b-3p/-5p ratio influences TMZ resistance in GBM cells. Analysis of curated Gene Ontology terms revealed that the target genes of miR-92b-3p were primarily enriched in deacetylation-related pathways, whereas the target genes of miR-92b-5p were mainly associated with acetylation-related pathways (Fig. 3a; Supplementary Tables 7, 8). These results suggested that higher-miR-92b-3p and lower-miR-92b-5p may contribute to increased intracellular acetylation levels in GBM cells.

Higher ratio of miR-92b-3p/-5p contributes to elevation of endogenous acetylation levels. a Functional annotation analysis identifying pathways regulated by miR-92b-3p/-5p. b Western blot analysis of protein acetylation levels in parental and TMZ-resistant GBM cells. c Diagram depicting aberrant acetylation regulation in TMZ-resistant GBM cells. d, e ELISA assay assessing the acetyl-CoA and acetyl-carnitine levels in parental and TMZ-resistant GBM cells (n = 3). f Western blot analysis measuring the impact of miR-92b-3p/-5p on histone acetylation levels of parental and TMZ-resistant GBM cells. g DAPI staining of nuclei and statistical quantification of results, scale bar 50 μm. Data were analyzed using Student’s t-test (d, e and g). Significant results are indicated by *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001

Our previous research indicated that histone acetylation is closely associated with TMZ resistance [9]. In the present study, we found that total protein acetylation activity was increased in TMZ-resistant GBM cells (Fig. 3b and Supplementary Fig. 3a). Owing to the role of acetyl-CoA as the main donor of histone acetylation [38], elevated acetyl-CoA and acetyl-carnitine levels in TMZ-resistant GBM cells suggested a contribution to histone hyperacetylation (Fig. 3c–e; Supplementary Fig. 3b).

Subsequently, we observed that higher miR-92b-3p and lower miR-92b-5p levels increased the acetylation of H3K9 (H3K9ac), H3K27 (H3K27ac), H4K5 (H4K5ac), and H4K12 (H4K12ac), with particularly notable increases in H3K27 acetylation (Fig. 3f; Supplementary Fig. 3c). As histone acetylation levels have been reported to positively correlate with nuclear size, we next examined nuclear morphology by DAPI staining [38, 39]. Subsequent DAPI staining of nuclei, we found that overexpressed miR-92b-3p or downregulated miR-92b-5p of parental GBM cells significantly promoted nuclear expansion, indicative of a looser chromatin structure, while downregulated miR-92b-3p or overexpressed miR-92b-5p of TMZ-resistant GBM cells markedly restored nuclear size, exhibiting a tighter chromatin structure (Fig. 3g; Supplementary Fig. 3d).

Overall, our findings indicate that acetylation is regulated by miR-92b-3p/-5p, with higher miR-92b-3p/-5p ratios promoting histone acetylation levels and chromatin accessibility in TMZ-resistant GBMs, whereas lower miR-92b-3p/-5p ratios inhibit these effects in primary GBMs.

MiR-92b-3p increases H3K27ac levels by targeting HDAC9

Despite sharing a common origin in the miRNA precursor, the divergent actions of miR-92b-3p/-5p in the TMZ resistance of GBM were evidenced by the above findings. We hypothesized that the opposing roles of miR-92b-3p/-5p might be attributed to their distinct seed sequences, which enable them to target different mRNA targets and elicit divergent effects. Given that miR-92b-3p inhibited pathways associated with deacetylation and increased H3K27ac levels (Fig. 3a, f; Supplementary Fig. 3c), we further identified expression profiles of deacetylases between parental and TMZ-resistant GBM cells.

Through integration of GEO databases (GSE113510 and GSE100736) and our own cellular samples, we observed that a notable reduction in the expression of HDAC9 within TMZ-resistant GBM cells, and that HDAC9 additionally contained a potential binding site for miR-92b-3p (Fig. 4a; Supplementary Fig. 4a; Supplementary Table 9). According to Clinical Proteomic Tumor Analysis Consortium (CPTAC) and Chinese Glioma Genome Atlas (CGGA) GBM databases, we observed that HDAC9 expression was significantly decreased in tumor samples and recurrent gliomas compared with normal tissues and primary gliomas, respectively (Supplementary Fig. 4b, c).

Biotinylated miR-92b-3p could pull down significantly more HDAC9 compared with scramble control, indicating that miR-92b-3p had a direct interaction with HDAC9 (Fig. 4b). Moreover, we found that HDAC9 expression levels were lower in recurrent GBM samples and TMZ-resistant GBM cells compared with primary GBM samples and parental GBM cells (Fig. 4c–f; Supplementary Fig. 4d–g). The luciferase experiment further revealed that miR-92b-3p directly bound to the HDAC9 3′UTR regions (Fig. 4g and Supplementary Fig. 4h).

The introduction of miR-92b-3p mimics into parental GBM cells led to a reduction in HDAC9 expression, while the inhibition of miR-92b-3p in TMZ-resistant GBM cells increased HDAC9 expression (Fig. 4h–j; Supplementary Fig. 4i–k). We next explored whether miR-92b-3p enhanced H3K27ac levels by targeting HDAC9. The promotion of H3K27ac levels by miR-92b-3p mimics was attenuated by HDAC9 overexpression in parental GBM cells, while the downregulation of H3K27ac levels by the miR-92b-3p inhibitors was rescued by HDAC9 silencing in TMZ-resistant GBM cells (Fig. 4k, l; Supplementary Fig. 4l, m).

These data suggest that HDAC9 is a direct target of miR-92b-3p and that silencing of HDAC9 increases the level of H3K27ac in TMZ-resistant GBM cells.

MiR-92b-5p decreases H3K27ac levels by targeting FOXP3

Analysis of curated pathways indicated that miR-92b-5p primarily inhibits pathways associated with acetylation, and H3K27ac levels were significantly restrained by miR-92b-5p (Fig. 3a, f; Supplementary Fig. 3c). We further identified that FOXP3 and NOS1 were closely related to the miR-92b-5p-regulated acetylation pathways (Supplementary Fig. 5a). We found that FOXP3 (not NOS1) was significantly upregulated in TMZ-resistant GBM cells and WHO grade IV gliomas (Supplementary Fig. 5b, c). Survival analysis revealed that a higher expression of FOXP3 predicted unfavorable overall survival in GBMs (Supplementary Fig. 5d). Meanwhile, we identified that FOXP3 contained a binding site for miR-92b-5p (Supplementary Fig. 5e), indicating that FOXP3 is a potential target gene of miR-92b-5p.

It has been demonstrated that FOXP3 enhances H3 acetylation by displacing HDAC2 and HDAC4 from their binding site [40] (Fig. 5a). Therefore, we evaluated whether FOXP3 is responsible for the miR-92b-5p-mediated acetylation in TMZ-resistant GBM cells. MiRNA pull-down assay confirmed that miR-92b-5p directly binds to FOXP3 (Fig. 5b). The FOXP3 expression levels were higher in recurrent GBM samples and TMZ-resistant GBM cells compared with primary GBM samples and parental GBM cells, respectively (Fig. 5c–g; Supplementary Fig. 5f–h). Luciferase reporter assay showed that miR-92b-5p binds to the FOXP3 mRNA 3′UTR region in GBM cells (Fig. 5h; Supplementary Fig. 5i).

Moreover, we found that miR-92b-5p inhibitors significantly elevated the mRNA and protein levels of FOXP3 in parental GBM cells, while miR-92b-5p mimics markedly inhibited the expression levels of FOXP3 in TMZ-resistant GBM cells (Fig. 5i–k; Supplementary Fig. 5j–l). Next, we investigated whether miR-92b-5p suppresses H3K27ac levels by targeting FOXP3. Knockdown of FOXP3 in parental GBM cells impaired H3K27ac elevation induced by miR-92b-5p inhibitors (Fig. 5l; Supplementary Fig. 5m). In parallel, overexpression of FOXP3 in TMZ-resistant GBM cells attenuated the repression of H3K27ac caused by miR-92b-5p mimics (Fig. 5m; Supplementary Fig. 5n).

These results indicate that the low expression of miR-92b-5p in TMZ-resistant GBM cells increased H3K27ac levels by alleviating FOXP3 targeting.

Elevated miR-92b-3p/-5p ratio promotes the enrichment of H3K27ac in the COL7A1 promoter region

Our previous results indicated that increased expression of miR-92b-3p and decreased expression of miR-92b-5p led to elevated H3K27ac levels via silencing HDAC9 and alleviating FOXP3 targeting, respectively. Heatmap of simplified GO terms enrichments showed that HDAC9 and FOXP3 are correlated with ECM remodeling (Fig. 6a). Meanwhile, Gene Ontology analysis showed that collagen-containing extracellular matrix was significantly enriched in 229R cells (Supplementary Fig. 6a). Picrosirius Red staining confirmed an elevation in collagen deposition in recurrent GBM samples (Fig. 6b). Analogously, we observed a significant augmentation of collagen deposition in TMZ-resistant mice and TMZ-resistant GBM cells (Fig. 6c; Supplementary Fig. 6b). Masson’s trichrome staining also revealed higher collagen deposition levels in recurrent GBM samples and TMZ-resistant mice (Fig. 6d, e). These results are consistent with the conclusion that collagen limits drug penetration into tumors, thereby weakening the effectiveness of chemotherapy [28, 41, 42].

We next investigated the potential mechanism of collagen deposition in TMZ-resistant GBMs. We hypothesized that intracellular hyperacetylation promotes the upregulation of genes associated with collagen production that protects GBM from the chemotherapeutic effects of TMZ. To this end, we probed the HDAC9-/FOXP3-regulated genes within the collagen-containing extracellular matrix term, narrowing down the list to 11 candidate genes (Supplementary Fig. 6c, d). Additionally, given the higher miR-92b-3p and lower miR-92b-5p expression pattern in TMZ-resistant GBM cells, we further examined 11 candidate genes for positive correlation with miR-92b-3p and negative correlation with miR-92b-5p (Supplementary Fig. 6e). Ultimately, seven candidate collagen-related genes were isolated that are potentially linked to acetylation (Supplementary Fig. 6f).

Through integrated analysis of GEO database (GSE100736 and GSE113510), CGGA GBM databases, and our own cellular samples, we identified COL7A1 as the only gene consistently upregulated in TMZ-resistant GBM cells (Fig. 6f–k; Supplementary Fig. 6g–k). Knockdown of COL7A1 in resistant GBM cells markedly reduced cell viability, and promoted cell apoptosis after TMZ treatment (Fig. 6l, m). To determine whether COL7A1-mediated collagen deposition directly affects GBM cells survival, we treated COL7A1-overexpressing HG7 cells with recombinant human MMP1 (rh-MMP1) as a rescue strategy. The results demonstrated that ECM modulation by rh-MMP1 markedly compromised the survival of HG7_COL7A1_OE cells upon TMZ treatment (Supplementary Fig. 6l, m). Additionally, IGV plots showed that the enrichment of H3K27ac at the COL7A1 promoter region was significantly higher in patients with TMZ-resistant GBM compared with patients with TMZ-sensitive GBM, suggesting that the expression of COL7A1 in GBMs may be regulated by the acetylation level of H3K27 at its promoter region (Supplementary Fig. 6n). ChIP-PCR revealed that increased enrichment of H3K27ac on the COL7A1 promoter region in 229R cells compared with LN229 cells (Fig. 6n).

In LN229 cells, overexpression of HDAC9 or knockdown of FOXP3 reduced the enrichment of H3K27ac in the COL7A1 promoter region mediated by the miR-92b-3p mimics or miR-92b-5p inhibitors, respectively (Fig. 6o, p). Conversely, in 229R cells, knockdown of HDAC9 or overexpression of FOXP3 restored the reduction of H3K27ac enrichment in the COL7A1 promoter region caused by the miR-92b-3p inhibitors or miR-92b-5p mimics, respectively (Fig. 6q, r). Additionally, in parental GBM cells, overexpression of HDAC9 or knockdown of FOXP3 attenuated the levels of COL7A1 induced by the miR-92b-3p mimics or miR-92b-5p inhibitors (Fig. 6s, t). In TMZ-resistant GBM cells, knockdown of HDAC9 or overexpression of FOXP3 restored the reduction in COL7A1 levels caused by the miR-92b-3p inhibitors or miR-92b-5p mimics (Fig. 6u, v).

Furthermore, it has been recognized that elevated collagen levels are correlated with decreased total CD8+ T cells [43]. The TMZ-resistant mice showed increased levels of COL7A1 and decreased levels of CD8+ T cells, which exhibit a clear negative correlation (r = -0.6636, P < 0.01) (Supplementary Fig. 6o). Collectively, these findings suggest that elevated miR-92b-3p/-5p ratio enhances COL7A1 expression by increasing H3K27ac enrichment in the COL7A1 promoter region, which promotes collagen deposition and augments TMZ resistance in GBMs.

ATG-H suppresses TUT4-mediated strand selection and restores TMZ sensitivity

TUT4 catalyzes the uridylation of pre-miRNAs, leading to alternative processing mediated by DICER, which subsequently favors the preferential selection of the 3′ strand over the 5′ strand [11, 37]. Analysis of the GEO database (GSE113510) and qRT-PCR results revealed significantly higher TUT4 expression levels in TMZ-resistant GBM cells compared with parental GBM cells (Fig. 7a, b; Supplementary Fig. 7a). Intriguingly, TUT4 depletion caused a marked reduction in mono-U-tailed miR-92b-3p while simultaneously increasing mono-U-tailed miR-92b-5p (Fig. 7c). Knockdown of TUT4 in TMZ-resistant GBM cells resulted in a significant decrease in miR-92b-3p expression, while miR-92b-5p expression was significantly elevated (Supplementary Fig. 7b). To investigate whether TUT4-mediated uridylation of pre-miR-92b influences the processing efficiency of miR-92b-3p and miR-92b-5p, we performed in vitro uridylation assays. We found that uridylation of pre-miR-92b promoted the preferential processing of miR-92b-3p (Supplementary Fig. 7c, d). Furthermore, compared with TUT4_NC cells, we observed a significant decrease in the miR-92b-3p/-5p ratio in Ago2 in TUT4_KD cells, indicating that TUT4 uridylates pre-miR-92b, thereby promoting its preferential processing into miR-92b-3p (Supplementary Fig. 7e).

These results demonstrated that TUT4-mediated uridylation might exert a critical influence on regulating the strand selection of miR-92b via 3′ tailing. Aurothioglucose hydrate (ATG-H), with a highly selective and inhibitory effect on TUT4, has already been investigated for its effects [44]. Notably, ATG-H markedly reduced the levels of miR-92b-3p while simultaneously elevating miR-92b-5p in TMZ-resistant GBM cells (Fig. 7d–f; Supplementary Fig. 7f, g).

We next sought to determine whether ATG-H could enhance the sensitivity of GBM cells to TMZ. The results showed that the combination of ATG-H and TMZ significantly increased the apoptosis and decreased the proliferation of TMZ-resistant GBM cells compared with TMZ alone (Fig. 7g–j; Supplementary Fig. 7h, i). Consistent with this, the tumor volume of TMZ-resistant mice treated with the combination of ATG-H and TMZ was significantly smaller than that of mice treated with TMZ alone (Fig. 7k, l), and the combined treatment significantly prolonged the lifespan of mice (Fig. 7m). In TMZ-resistant mice receiving combined treatment, FISH (for miRNAs) and IHC (for proteins) revealed increased expression levels of miR-92b-5p and HDAC9, along with decreased expression levels of miR-92b-3p, FOXP3, and COL7A1 (Fig. 7n). Overall, these data reveal that ATG-H inhibits TUT4-mediated preferential selection of the miR-92b 3′ strand, thereby restoring TMZ sensitivity and enhancing the therapeutic efficacy of TMZ.

Discussion

The mature miRNA sequence is part of a double-stranded region, and selecting the correct guide strand is crucial [45]. miRNA strand selection is associated with recurrence and poor survival in multiple cancers [46–48]. In the present study, we examined miRNA strand selection in GBM cells and found that miR-92b strand selection has a significant impact on TMZ resistance in GBMs. Elevated levels of miR-92b-3p and reduced levels of miR-92b-5p promoted TMZ resistance in GBMs. Through establishing mice orthotopic models bearing GBM xenografts via 229R cells, we found that correcting the dysregulated miR-92b-3p/-5p strand ratio significantly mitigates TMZ resistance. Similarly, in vitro experiments demonstrated that increasing the miR-92b-3p/-5p ratio in parental GBM cells enhanced TMZ resistance. Conversely, decreasing the miR-92b-3p/-5p ratio in resistant GBM cells restored TMZ sensitivity.

However, why do these two miRNAs from the same precursor present opposite expression patterns in the progression of TMZ resistance? Previous studies have reported that uridylation of pre-miRNAs repositions the Dicer enzyme on the precursor, thereby altering its cleavage sites [11, 19, 44]. Such alternative processing produces duplexes with distinct termini and consequently results in differential strand selection. In the present study, we found that terminal nucleotidyltransferase TUT4 functions as a key “selector” by uridylating pre-miR-92b. TUT4 regulates miRNA stability via catalyzing the addition of uridines at the 3′ end of RNAs [49]. TUT4 in TMZ-resistant GBM cells uridylated pre-miR-92b, thereby facilitating the expression of miR-92b-3p. Given the central role of TUT4 in strand selection, we next explored whether pharmacological inhibition of TUT4 could modulate this process. Notably, ATG-H, a gold compound clinically approved for rheumatoid arthritis, has been reported to inhibit TUT4 enzymatic activity [44]. ATG-H comprises anti-inflammatory gold compounds approved for treating rheumatoid arthritis [50–52]. Our investigation uncovered that ATG-H promoted miR-92b-5p selection and restored TMZ sensitivity.

Enrichment analysis suggested that miR-92b-3p/-5p is involved in the regulation of cellular acetylation. Acetylation is a significant post-translational modification of proteins, and intracellular acetylation dysregulation is closely related to GBM tumorigenicity and malignancy [53, 54]. We further identified that the higher miR-92b-3p/-5p ratio enhanced the histone acetylation levels, especially for H3K27ac levels. Increased histone acetylation levels in TMZ-resistant GBM cells lead to the nuclei more accessible and increasing overall chromatin accessibility.

MiRNA triggers mRNA degradation through complementary binding within the seed match, thereby exerting their regulatory functions [13, 55]. To delineate the molecular mechanisms driving the observed acetylation alterations, we next examined potential direct targets of miR-92b-3p and miR-92b-5p that regulate histone acetylation. On the basis of our results, HDAC9 was the target gene of miR-92b-3p, and FOXP3 was the target gene of miR-92b-5p. Higher miR-92b-3p targets HDAC9, thus inhibiting HDAC9-mediated deacetylation and elevating H3K27ac levels in TMZ-resistant GBM cells, while lower miR-92b-5p results in increased H3K27ac levels by alleviating FOXP3 targeting in TMZ-resistant GBM cells.

Considering that increased H3K27ac facilitates transcriptional activation and that collagen-containing extracellular matrix pathways were markedly enriched in 229R cells, we postulated that imbalanced miR-92b-3p/-5p ratio could enhance H3K27ac enrichment at promoters of collagen-associated genes, thereby driving extracellular matrix gene expression and ultimately contributing to TMZ resistance. A heatmap of simplified GO terms enrichments also showed that HDAC9 and FOXP3 contribute to ECM remodeling. Picrosirius Red and Masson’s trichrome staining further confirmed the accumulated collagen deposition in recurrent GBM samples, TMZ-resistant mice, and TMZ-resistant GBM cells. This suggests that collagen accumulation is a significant factor in TMZ resistance. Comprehensive analysis of the collagen-containing extracellular matrix pathway gene set revealed COL7A1 as a critical gene potentially regulated by acetylation. COL7A1 encodes collagen type VII, which is a component of the ECM [56]. Elevated miR-92b-3p and decreased miR-92b-5p in TMZ-resistant GBM cells led to significant enrichment of H3K27ac in the COL7A1 promoter region by increasing HDAC9 degradation and mitigating FOXP3 degradation, respectively. Increased collagen deposition enhances resistance to chemotherapy through reducing drug permeability, thereby impairing chemotherapy efficacy [57–59]. Analysis of COL7A1 expression indicated that high expression of COL7A1 correlated with TMZ resistance of GBMs, and COL7A1 knockdown significantly inhibited TMZ resistance of TMZ-resistant GBM cells. Our investigation revealed that elevated miR-92b-3p/-5p ratio contributed to increased H3K27ac enrichment in the COL7A1 promoter region, thereby enhancing COL7A1 expression, promoting collagen deposition, and fostering TMZ resistance of GBM cells.

Despite the comprehensive investigations conducted in this study, several limitations should be acknowledged. We identified differential strand selection of miR-92b in parental and TMZ-resistant GBM cells, and preliminarily validated this finding by qRT-PCR and FISH. Notably, while miR-92b-3p was upregulated and miR-92b-5p was downregulated in recurrent GBM samples relative to primary tumors, these trends warrant further validation in larger, independent patient cohorts to robustly assess their generalizability and clinical relevance. In addition, although our preliminary in vivo and in vitro findings suggest that ATG-H-mediated pharmacological inhibition of TUT4 effectively impairs TUT4-dependent strand selection and restores TMZ sensitivity, clinical trials confirming the feasibility of ATG-H as a TMZ sensitizer are still lacking.

Overall, we found that TUT4 induced an elevation in the miR-92b-3p/-5p ratio in TMZ-resistant GBMs. Increased miR-92b-3p and decreased miR-92b-5p improved COL7A1 levels by elevating H3K27ac enrichment in the COL7A1 promoter region through silencing HDAC9 and alleviating FOXP3 targeting. Elevated COL7A1 contributed collagen deposition and enhanced TMZ resistance in GBMs. In addition, we found that the TUT4 inhibitor ATG-H could effectively inhibit the expression of miR-92b-3p and promote the expression of miR-92b-5p, thereby restoring TMZ sensitivity (Fig. 8). Our findings uncover a novel mechanism of GBM resistance and suggest that targeting miR-92b strand selection may represent a promising therapeutic strategy to sensitize GBM to TMZ.

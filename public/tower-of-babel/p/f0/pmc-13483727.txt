Tumor-associated protease-activated anti-CD47 antibody precisely maintains phagocytic ability of macrophages with minimal effect on healthy tissue
Authors: Yu-Tung Chen, Zih-Yu Jhuang, Pin-Jie Li, Yun-Chi Lu, Bo-Cheng Huang, Chin-Yuan Chang, Yu-Cheng Su, Chih-Hung Chuang, Tian-Lu Cheng, Fang-Ming Chen, Shih-Yi Lin, Hsin-Kai Huang, Wen-Wei Lin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
(Background)CD47 is highly expressed on many cancer cells and acts as an innate immune checkpoint. Its binding to signal regulatory protein alpha (SIRPα) on macrophages enables cancer cells to evade phagocytosis. Although anti-CD47 antibody (αCD47 Ab) has been employed to restore phagocytic capacity, the ubiquitous expression of CD47 on normal cells results in significant toxicities during Ab treatment, such as anemia, thrombocytopenia, and sepsis.MethodsTo mitigate these side effects, we used an autologous hinge region as a spatial-hindrance-based Ab lock and connected it to the N-terminal of the light chain and heavy chain via matrix metalloprotease substrate peptides (i.e., MMP-2) to cover the complementarity-determining regions (CDR) of αCD47 Ab to generate Pro-αCD47 Ab. The Ab lock is selectively removed only in disease regions with overexpressed proteases, thereby reducing the non-selective on-target effect.ResultsOur results showed that Pro-αCD47 Ab exhibits a 225.9-fold weaker binding ability compared to parental αCD47 Ab but fully recovers its binding function following MMP-2 treatment. Significantly, Pro-αCD47 Ab exhibits a 100.2-fold and 83.7-fold reduction in binding affinity toward red blood cells and neutrophils, respectively, thereby minimizing the risk of hematological toxicities. Furthermore, in vivo xenograft studies confirmed that Pro-αCD47 Ab achieves dose-dependent and near-complete tumor suppression equivalent to the parental antibody, while maintaining a stable systemic safety profile as evidenced by consistent animal body weight. Besides, it was successfully demonstrated that Pro-αCD47 Ab can be activated by endogenous MMP-2 within clinical tumor specimens, specifically showing promising activation in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) samples, thereby restoring its ability to bind CD47.ConclusionIn summary, we developed a protease-activated Pro-αCD47 Ab that avoids the undesired interactions with normal tissues, thereby addressing the most challenging issue limiting clinical efficacy. This advancement may provide patients with better medical care by enhancing therapeutic efficacy and improving overall treatment quality.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12967-026-08293-w.

Background

The widespread overexpression of CD47 across various malignancies has positioned it as a promising therapeutic target. Indeed, studies have demonstrated CD47 overexpression in a significant proportion of patients [1–3], including 68.3% of lung cancer cases and 77.2% of triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) cases [4, 5]. Physiologically, the interaction between CD47 on cancer cells and its ligand, signal regulatory protein alpha (SIRPα) expressed on macrophages, delivers a critical “don’t eat me” signal, effectively shielding tumor cells from immune surveillance and phagocytosis [5, 6]. Consequently, one focus in current clinical oncology involves the development of antibodies designed to disrupt this CD47-SIRPα axis, thereby promoting macrophage-mediated elimination of tumor cells [7–9]. For instance, a first-in-class humanized αCD47 Ab, Magrolimab (Hu5F9-G4), has shown promising results in combination with Trastuzumab (anti-HER2 Ab) by enhancing Ab-dependent cellular phagocytosis (ADCP) and inhibiting breast cancer growth [10, 11]. Another humanized αCD47 Ab, CC-90,002, has demonstrated the potential to significantly extend survival in preclinical acute myeloid leukemia (AML) models and exhibited favorable pharmacokinetics (PK) and tolerability in non-human primates [12–14]. However, a critical limitation of systemic CD47 targeting lies in the ubiquitous expression of CD47 not only on malignant cells but also on a range of normal cells, including red blood cells [15], white blood cells [16], and platelets [17], among other hematopoietic components [18, 19]. This lack of tumor-specific expression renders αCD47 Ab incapable of distinguishing between diseased and healthy tissues. As a result, these antibodies can indiscriminately bind to blood cells throughout the body, leading to the activation of macrophages and subsequent attack on normal tissues, culminating in severe and dose-limiting side effects. Clinical trials with Hu5F9-G4 (NCT03248479, NCT04827576), for example, have reported a high incidence of anemia (89%), blood clotting (36%), and lymphocytopenia (34%) [20]. Alarmingly, approximately 84.8% of adverse events were attributed to Hu5F9-G4, with a substantial 24% of patients requiring treatment discontinuation due to these toxicities. Similarly, CC-90,002 was associated with significant hematological toxicities such as thrombocytopenia (21%) and neutropenia (38%), ultimately leading to the premature termination of clinical investigations due to multiple severe adverse reactions [12]. Therefore, enhancing the tumor-site selectivity of αCD47 Ab to reduce treatment-related side effects remains a crucial challenge.

To avoid severe side effects, various strategies are currently being developed to create safer αCD47 Abs that offer increased selectivity. For example, TTI-621 (SIRPα-Fc) is a drug that utilizes the extracellular domain of SIRPα fused to an Fc fragment to competitively bind CD47, thereby blocking the “don’t eat me” signal. Its relatively lower affinity for endogenous CD47 helps reduce binding to normal cells. Results confirmed that TTI-621 could reduce binding to red blood cells and platelets, and inhibit the growth of acute myeloid leukemia (AML) and B-cell lymphoma [21]. However, TTI-621 was terminated in multiple clinical trials (e.g., NCT04996004, NCT02663518, NCT02890368). In diffuse large B-cell lymphoma therapy, the response rate was only 29%, and the overall response rate (ORR) was 13%. It is speculated that the drug’s reduced affinity for CD47 also led to a diminished ability to bind to tumors, resulting in lower-than-expected therapeutic efficacy. Another strategy involves designing bispecific antibodies that simultaneously target CD47 and another antigen specifically expressed on tumor cells, such as programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) or CD19 [22–25]. This approach can increase the antibody’s selectivity for tumor cells, as only cells expressing both antigens can be effectively bound and attacked, thereby minimizing harmful effects on normal cells. For instance, ISB-1442 (a CD47×CD38 bispecific antibody) has been shown to reduce hemagglutination, a major side effect caused by αCD47 Ab treatment, by 33-fold compared to αCD47 Ab and significantly inhibit tumor growth and prolong mouse survival [25]. However, this strategy also has its limitations, as it requires prior confirmation of tumor antigen expression and thus cannot be universally applied to all types of cancer. While significant progress has been made in strategies to enhance the safety of αCD47 Ab, the ongoing challenge lies in finding the optimal balance between efficacy and safety. This ensures that while significantly reducing side effects, these antibodies can still maintain sufficient killing power against tumor cells.

In this study, we established a novel Pro-αCD47 Ab capable of activation only within diseased regions. This was achieved by utilizing autologous hinge regions as a spatial-hindrance-based Ab lock to cover the CDR binding site of the αCD47 Ab, along with incorporating a substrate peptide (MMP-2) that connects this Ab lock to the N-terminal of both the light and heavy chains. We utilized αCD47 Ab as a candidate due to its clinically observed side effects, which we postulated the Ab lock might be able to mitigate [12]. The design enables Pro-αCD47 Ab to avoid binding to normal cells while allowing selective activation by proteases in diseased regions, thereby restoring its phagocytic ability (Fig. 1). We evaluated the antigen-blocking capabilities of Pro-αCD47 Ab compared to αCD47 Ab using Western blot and enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), and validated the protease-mediated restoration of Pro-αCD47 Ab activity following MMP-2 treatment. Additionally, we assessed the effect of Pro-αCD47 Ab on red blood cells and neutrophils and quantified its impact on macrophage phagocytosis both with and without MMP-2 treatment in vitro. Furthermore, we verified the activation potential of Pro-αCD47 Ab by using clinical specimens from triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) patients. In summary, we successfully developed a spatial hindrance-based Ab lock that effectively prevents non-specific binding in healthy tissues of αCD47 Ab, thereby reducing adverse side effects. We anticipate that this innovative Pro-αCD47 Ab can significantly enhance the safety profile of CD47-targeted therapies, paving the way for improved patient outcomes in cancer treatment.

Methods

Cell lines and animal

Expi293F cells (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) were cultured in Expi293 Expression Medium at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere of 8% CO2. Human breast cancer cells MCF7 (American Type Culture Collection, ATCC) were cultured in DMEM (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) supplemented with 10% bovine calf serum (BCS), 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 µg/mL streptomycin at 37 °C in an atmosphere of 5% CO2. THP-1 cells were obtained from ATCC and cultured in RPMI1640 (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) supplemented with 2 g/L NaHCO3, 10% heat-inactivated BCS (HyClone, Logan, UT, USA), penicillin (100 U/mL), and streptomycin (100 µg/mL) at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere of 5% CO2 in air. Differentiation of THP-1 cells followed a protocol based on several recent publications that all refer to the differentiated cells as THP-1 macrophages [26–28]. Differentiation was achieved in 100 ng/ml phorbol myristate acetate (PMA) (Millipore Sigma, Burlington, MA, USA) for 2–3 days, which leads to these cells switching from growth in suspension (as monocytic cells) to attaching to tissue-culture plastic. NOD.Cg-Prkdcscid/JNarl (NOD/SCID) mice aged 4 weeks were purchased from the National Laboratory Animal Center in Taipei, Taiwan. The mice were maintained following institutional guidelines and approved by the Animal Care and Use Committee of the Kaohsiung Medical University.

Construction and production of Pro-αCD47 Ab

The variable heavy and light regions of αCD47 Ab clone CC-90,002 were synthesized based on its published sequence (patent WO2020206251). The complementary DNA coding for the heavy and light chains of αCD47 Ab were cloned through assembly PCR. Human IgG1 hinge sequences were obtained from the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI). The hinge-encoding sequences, Gly-Gly-Gly-Gly-Gly-Ser (GGGGS) linker, and MMP-2 substrate–encoding sequences Gly-Pro-Lys-Gly-Val-Arg (GPLGVR) [29] were introduced upstream of the light chain and heavy chain of CC-90,002 (αCD47 Ab) to generate Pro-αCD47 Ab. αCD47 Ab or Pro-αCD47 Ab were produced using the Expi293 Expression System (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). The protein was purified by protein A-sepharose (GE Healthcare, Milwaukee, WI, USA), and the molecular weights were found using sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE).

Investigating the selective activation of Pro-αCD47 Ab via MMP-2

To determine the binding kinetics, i.e., the half-maximal effective concentration (EC50) of αCD47 Ab and Pro-αCD47 Ab, 96-well plates were coated with CD47 (0.3 µg/mL, ACRO Biosystems, Newark, DE, USA) and blocked with 5% skim milk. αCD47 Ab or Pro-αCD47 Ab was incubated with or without 20 µg/mL of MMP-2 (type IV collagenase, Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) in DMEM/0.05% BSA for 1 h at 37 °C before terminating the reaction with BCS. The samples were added to the plates at given concentrations (800–0.0003 nM) for 1 h at RT. After washing, the wells were incubated with horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated goat anti-human IgG Fcγ Ab for 1 h at RT, and detection was performed by adding ABTS containing 30% H2O2. The binding ability was quantified through absorbance detection at optical density (OD) 405 nm. αCD47 Ab and Pro-αCD47 Ab were evaluated by Western blot using HRP-conjugated anti-hIgG Fab Ab or anti-hIgG Fc Ab (Jackson ImmunoResearch Laboratories, West Grove, PA, USA).

Neutrophil binding assessment of activated and inactivated Pro-αCD47 Ab

To measure the binding of neutrophils with αCD47 Ab and Pro-αCD47 Ab treatment, fresh human blood was collected from two healthy donors, and neutrophils and platelets were isolated using Ficoll Paque Plus (Cytiva, Marlborough, MA, USA) via centrifugation. For neutrophil preparation, red blood cells (RBCs) were lysed using RBC lysis solution (Sartorius, Göttingen, Germany) to prevent RBC interference. Fc blocker (Innovex, Richmond, VA, USA) at 300 µl/106 cells was added and incubated for 30 min on ice, followed by washing in PBS containing 0.05% BSA. For the titration analysis, cells were incubated with 5-fold serial dilutions of either αCD47 Ab or Pro-αCD47 Ab, ranging from 0.000256 nM to 100 nM. Unbound antibodies were removed by extensive washing in cold PBS with 0.05% BSA. Samples were incubated with Fluorescein Isothiocyanate (FITC)-conjugated goat anti-human IgG Ab (Jackson ImmunoResearch Laboratories, West Grove, PA, USA) to detect the αCD47 Ab signal. After washing, mouse anti-human CD66b APC (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) was added to analyze neutrophil binding. Fluorescence signals were measured using a FACSLyric flow cytometer.

RBC binding activity and aggregation assay

Fresh human blood was collected from two healthy donors, and RBCs were isolated through centrifugation. After washing the RBCs twice with PBS, a cell suspension was prepared. To evaluate the RBC binding activity of αCD47 Ab and Pro-αCD47 Ab, the cells were incubated with αCD47 Ab and Pro-αCD47 Ab at concentrations ranging from 100 to 0.00128 nM. Secondary detection was performed using FITC-conjugated goat anti-human IgG Fc Ab, and binding was quantified using a FACScan flow cytometer (Merck Flow Cytometer, Guava easyCyte System). For the RBC aggregation assay, 50 µL of RBC suspension was mixed with an equal volume of serially diluted antibodies (2-fold dilutions in PBS) across concentrations from 200 to 0.75 nM in U-bottom 96-well plates, with PBS (50 µL) serving as the RBC control. After a 40-minute incubation at RT, RBC aggregation was visually assessed.

In vitro Ab-dependent macrophage phagocytosis

To quantify Ab-dependent cellular phagocytosis (ADCP), MCF7 cells were stained with carboxyfluorescein succinimidyl ester (CFSE), washed with serum-free DMEM, and plated at a density of 1 × 105 cells per group in serum-free DMEM on ice. MCF7 cells were opsonized by incubation with 2.5 nM of αCD47 Ab, Pro-αCD47 Ab (with or without MMP-2 treatment), or isotype Ab for 1 h on ice. Macrophages (human monocyte-derived macrophages; THP1) were harvested and washed in serum-free DMEM. The macrophages were then added to the opsonized tumor cells at a density of 5 × 104 cells per group in 50 µL of media, resulting in a final assay volume of 200 µL and an effector-to-tumor cell ratio of 1:2. After incubation at 37 °C for 2 h, cells were washed with PBS containing 0.05% BSA, and stained with Phycoerythrin (PE)-conjugated rat anti-mouse/human CD11b Ab (BioLegend, San Diego, CA, USA) for 45 min at 4°C. Phagocytosis was quantified using a FACScan flow cytometer by determining the percentage of CFSE+ cells within the macrophage cell gate (CD11b+ for human monocyte-derived macrophages). Results are expressed as fold change relative to the level of phagocytosis in control-treated cells.

Activation of Pro-αCD47 Ab in clinical breast cancer samples

To evaluate Pro-αCD47 Ab activation in breast cancer, we utilized an in situ zymography (IHZ) assay on frozen tissue sections to analyze the protease activity. We collected clinical triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) specimens from Kaohsiung Medical University Hospital and Datong Hospital (IRB No.: KMUHIRB F(II)-20200161). Frozen tissue slides were allowed to warm to RT for 30 min, and a hydrophobic barrier was drawn around the tissue using an Immedge pen. Subsequently, 100 µL of 50 mM Tris, 10 mM CaCl2, 150 mM NaCl, 0.05% Brij-35 (TCNB) buffer was added within the hydrophobic barrier and incubated for 30 min. Each sample was diluted to 1 µM in TCNB buffer + 3% BSA and evenly applied over the tissue, then incubated in a humidity chamber at 37 °C for 24 h. On the following day, tissues were stained with Goat anti-human IgG Fc FITC for 30 min. Tissues were then washed with 6 mL of 0.05% PBST for 5 min (three times), followed by PBS, and mounted with 60 µL of mounting buffer containing DAPI to identify tissue location. When activated by tissue proteases, Ab will bind to the tissue, generating a fluorescent signal. Images were captured using the TissueFAXS automated quantitative imaging system, and fluorescence intensity was measured to determine Ab activation levels.

In vivo tumor studies

NOD/SCID mice were inoculated in in the mammary fat pad with 5 × 106 MCF7 cells in 100 µL of 1 : 1 PBS/matrigel (BD Biosciences) and the mice were subcutaneously injected with 2.5 µg β-estradiol 17-valerate in 50 µL sesame oil once a week. When tumors reached a mean volume of 50 mm3, mice were randomized into treatment cohorts (n = 4 to 5 per group) with balanced initial tumor sizes and administered saline, or 1 or 5 mg/kg of either αCD47 Ab or Pro-αCD47 Ab, via weekly intraperitoneal injection for four weeks. Tumor measurements were performed twice a week using calipers, and tumor sizes were calculated using the equation: volume = (length × width2)/2. Animal body weight was recorded twice a week as a primary indicator of gross systemic toxicity and treatment tolerability. Upon completion of the 4-week treatment period, mice were euthanized. Tumors were excised and weighed to provide a final quantitative assessment of the antitumor efficacy.

Statistical analysis

The data are presented as mean ± SEM or SD. Statistical significance between groups was compared by analyzing the phagocytic ability of αCD47 Ab, Pro-αCD47 Ab and isotype Ab using ANOVA. For neutrophil and red blood cell (RBC) binding assays, the binding percentage was defined as the proportion of cells in the positive quadrant (Q2) of the flow cytometry dot plots. The half-maximal effective concentration (EC50) for antigen and cell binding was determined by fitting the titration data to a non-linear regression (four-parameter logistic curve) model. For the xenograft mouse model, therapeutic efficacy and systemic safety were analyzed as follows: Differences in tumor volume over the 31-day period were analyzed using Two-way ANOVA to account for the variables of time and treatment group. Final tumor weights and animal body weights were compared using One-way ANOVA. The statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism v.6, and the results were considered significant at a P value of less than 0.05.

Results

Spatial-hindrance Ab lock effectively modulates the antigen binding activity of αCD47 Ab

To determine whether our spatial-hindrance-based Ab lock could effectively reduce the antigen (Ag) binding of Pro-αCD47 Ab and subsequently restore αCD47 Ab activity upon MMP-2 treatment, we conducted a series of in vitro analyses. Initially, western blot analysis was employed to assess the protease-cleavability of Pro-αCD47 Ab with and without MMP-2 incubation. As depicted in Fig. 2A, the molecular weights (MW) of the light chain (LC) and heavy chain (HC) of the parental αCD47 Ab remained unchanged following MMP-2 treatment. Conversely, after incubating with MMP-2 for 1 h, the Pro-αCD47 Ab showed a clear reduction in the MW of both its LC (from 28.4 kDa to 25 kDa) and HC (from 58.4 kDa to 55 kDa), demonstrating complete cleavage of the Ab lock within this timeframe. Furthermore, we quantified the Ag-blocking ability and protease-mediated restoration of binding using a CD47-based ELISA (Fig. 2B). The EC50 for Ag binding was 1491 nM for Pro-αCD47 Ab, compared to 6.601 nM for αCD47 Ab, indicating a substantial 225.9-fold reduction in binding affinity conferred by the Ab lock. Notably, MMP-2 treatment completely restored the Ag binding ability of Pro-αCD47 Ab to a level comparable to parental αCD47 Ab. Collectively, these results unequivocally demonstrate that our spatial-hindrance-based Ab lock efficiently blocks the Ag-binding capacity of αCD47 Ab, and that this inhibition is completely reversed upon MMP-2-mediated cleavage of the lock, restoring the binding affinity to a level similar to parental αCD47 Ab.

Pro-αCD47 Ab exhibits minimal binding and agglutination of red blood cells

CD47 is endogenously expressed on red blood cells (RBCs), and αCD47 Ab, and due to its broad binding, has historically led to hematological adverse effects such as anemia and agglutination [12, 20]. Our innovative strategy, employing a spatial hindrance Ab lock, aims to mitigate these risks by selectively restricting antibody binding. To assess the potential for RBC-related adverse effects, we conducted RBC binding and agglutination assays. As shown in Fig. 3A, αCD47 Ab showed increased binding with higher concentrations. In contrast, Pro-αCD47 Ab demonstrated significantly reduced RBC binding, with a 100.2-fold reduction in binding activity compared to the αCD47 Ab. In the hemagglutination assay (Fig. 3B), αCD47 Ab induced RBC agglutination at concentrations as low as 0.75 nM, independent of MMP-2 treatment. While MMP-2-activated Pro-αCD47 Ab caused strong RBC agglutination, inactivated Pro-αCD47 Ab showed no aggregation across concentrations from 0.75 nM to 50 nM. The lack of agglutination by inactivated Pro-αCD47 Ab indicates that it would not cause unwanted blood coagulation or related adverse events under normal physiological conditions. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that the spatial-hindrance-based Ab lock effectively minimizes the interaction of Pro-αCD47 Ab, thereby preventing unwanted binding and agglutination.

Pro-αCD47 Ab prevents binding to neutrophils

Since clinical evidence indicates that the CC90002 causes neutropenia [12], and given that CD47 is also expressed on neutrophils, leading to potential non-specific binding and subsequent clearance by macrophages, we evaluated whether the Pro-αCD47 Ab also binds to neutrophils. To do this, we added αCD47 Ab and Pro-αCD47 Ab to isolated neutrophils in 5-fold serial dilutions, with final concentrations spanning from 0.000256 nM to 100 nM. The neutrophil population was identified using an APC-conjugated mouse anti-human CD66b Ab, and αCD47 Ab or Pro-αCD47 Ab binding was subsequently quantified via Flow cytometry with a FITC-conjugated goat anti-human IgG Fc Ab. As illustrated in Fig. 4 and Supplementary Fig. 1, Pro-αCD47 Ab demonstrated a significantly lower affinity for neutrophils compared to the αCD47 Ab. Specifically, the EC50 of Pro-αCD47 Ab was 83.7-fold higher than that of the parental antibody, indicating a substantial reduction in binding potential. These results indicate that Pro-αCD47 Ab can effectively avoid binding to normal blood cells, which could lead to a decreased risk of side effects such as thrombocytopenia and neutropenia.

Pro-αCD47 Ab induces phagocytosis of macrophages upon MMP-2 treatment

To assess the ability of Pro-αCD47 Ab to promote macrophage-mediated phagocytosis in vitro, we co-cultured THP-1-derived macrophages (which were first induced from THP-1 monocytes using Phorbol 12-Myristate 13-Acetate (PMA)) with CD47-expressing MCF7 cells. Phagocytic activity following treatment with isotype control Ab, αCD47 Ab, or Pro-αCD47 Ab was quantified by Flow cytometry in the presence or absence of MMP-2. As shown in Fig. 5, αCD47 Ab consistently enhanced phagocytosis by 25% compared to the isotype control, irrespective of MMP-2. Pro-αCD47 Ab displayed minimal phagocytic activity under conditions where MMP-2 was absent, demonstrating that the Ab lock effectively suppresses its function. Notably, with MMP-2 treatment, Pro-αCD47 Ab significantly boosted phagocytosis by 20%, reaching a level comparable to the 25% enhancement observed with αCD47 Ab, which consistently promoted phagocytosis regardless of MMP-2, without a significant difference. These results indicate that MMP-2 activation is required for Pro-αCD47 Ab to exert its full phagocytic potential, equivalent to that of αCD47 Ab.

Pro-αCD47 Ab exhibits potent in vivo antitumor efficacy and favorable systemic safety in xenograft models

To validate the therapeutic potential and safety of the protease-activated antibody design in vivo, we established an MCF7 xenograft mouse model. The experimental groups included a PBS control, αCD47 Ab (at 1 mg/kg and 5 mg/kg), and Pro-αCD47 Ab (at 1 mg/kg and 5 mg/kg). To evaluate long-term therapeutic efficacy, tumor volumes were measured twice weekly for 31 days following once-weekly intraperitoneal injections. As illustrated in Fig. 6A, while the PBS group showed rapid tumor progression, exceeding 300 mm3 by the end of the study, both doses of Pro-αCD47 Ab demonstrated significant and dose-dependent tumor growth inhibition (P < 0.0001). Notably, Pro-αCD47 Ab achieved nearly complete suppression, showing no significant difference (n.s.) in efficacy compared to the αCD47 Ab. Animal body weight was recorded throughout the treatment period as a indicator of treatment tolerability. All antibody-treated groups maintained stable body weights with no significant differences observed relative to the PBS control (n.s.), suggesting that the dosage was well-tolerated (Fig. 6B). Upon the study’s conclusion at day 31, mice were sacrificed and tumors were harvested for visual comparison (Fig. 6C). Final tumor masses were measured to provide a definitive quantitative assessment of therapeutic outcome. Consistent with the growth curves, the weights of tumors from the Pro-αCD47 Ab groups were significantly reduced compared to the PBS group (P < 0.01 and P < 0.05). Importantly, there was no significant difference in final tumor weight between the Pro-antibody and its corresponding parental antibody at either the 1 mg/kg or 5 mg/kg dose (Fig. 6D). In conclusion, these in vivo results confirm that Pro-αCD47 Ab is effectively activated by endogenous proteases within the tumor microenvironment, fully restoring its anti-tumor functionality to a level equivalent to the parental antibody. This confirms that the spatial-hindrance-based Ab lock provides a high therapeutic impact while maintaining systemic safety, marking a significant step toward safer CD47-targeted immunotherapy.

Pro-αCD47 Ab demonstrates activation ability in clinical breast cancer specimens

To assess the activation potential of Pro-αCD47 Ab in a tumor environment, we performed an in situ zymography (IHZ) assay on clinical breast cancer specimens. For this, we collected 16 triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) specimens from various stages (Supplementary Information). This subtype is frequently associated with an elevated expression of both MMP-2 [30–32] and CD47 [5], making it an ideal model to evaluate site-specific activation of our pro-antibody. Tissue sections were incubated with αCD47 Ab or Pro-αCD47 Ab, and binding was detected using a fluorescent dye-labeled secondary Ab (FITC-conjugated goat anti-human IgG Fc Ab). To ensure assay specificity, we included a negative control (NC) without Ab treatment and a Pro-αCD47 Ab group treated with protease inhibitors (PI). As shown in Fig. 7, both NC and PI-treated groups showed no fluorescent signal, confirming the absence of background signal and the protease-dependent activation of Pro-αCD47 Ab. αCD47 Ab exhibited consistent fluorescence across all specimens, validating its binding to CD47 in breast cancer tissue. Importantly, Pro-αCD47 Ab also displayed strong fluorescence in all specimens, demonstrating successful activation at tumor sites. This fluorescence was abolished upon the addition of a protease inhibitor (PI), confirming that the observed binding was specifically due to protease-mediated activation. These results indicate that Pro-αCD47 Ab is effectively activated in tumor regions and specifically binds to CD47-expressing tumor cells.

Discussion

Pro-αCD47 Ab exhibits striking characteristics that may overcome critical limitations in current immunotherapeutic approaches. It demonstrated a 225.9-fold reduction in antigen-binding ability, which could be selectively restored through protease activation. Crucially, the Pro-Ab showed a 100.2-fold reduction in RBC binding and an 83.7-fold reduction in neutrophil binding affinity, effectively avoiding the hemagglutination and potential neutropenia associated with broad CD47 blockade. Our in vivo results further confirmed that Pro-αCD47 Ab achieves near-complete tumor suppression equivalent to the parental antibody, while maintaining stable body weight, indicating excellent systemic tolerability. Moreover, Pro-αCD47 Ab was effectively activated in tumor tissues, demonstrating that MMP levels within clinical specimens are sufficient to activate the Pro-αCD47 Ab. These findings mark a significant advancement in addressing the challenges associated with CD47-targeted therapies, offering a novel solution to severe side effects while maintaining phagocytosis ability.

The extensive expression of CD47 on various cell types, while posing a challenge for therapeutic specificity, highlights its broad applicability as an immunotherapeutic target. As CD47 serves as a “don’t eat me” signal on both cancerous and healthy cells, and blocking it unleashes the inherent anti-tumor activity of the macrophages. This fundamental mechanism suggests that targeting CD47 could benefit a diverse range of malignancies, including solid tumors and hematological cancers, where CD47 overexpression contributes to immune evasion. Indeed, high CD47 expression correlates with poor outcomes in chronic myelogenous leukemia [33], acute myeloid leukemia (AML) [34], non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL) [35], and several solid tumors like breast cancer, renal cell carcinoma, non-small cell lung cancer, and thyroid cancer [36]. Beyond its implications in tumor treatment and prognosis, CD47 has also been implicated in aiding the diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer [37], renal cell tumors, and hematological tumors [36]. The potential to target a conserved immune checkpoint mechanism offers a universal approach to enhance phagocytosis.

A compelling avenue for maximizing the therapeutic impact of CD47 blockade lies in its combination with other immunotherapeutic agents, particularly immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs). By simultaneously blocking CD47, the innate immune arm, specifically macrophage-mediated phagocytosis, can be significantly enhanced, creating a multi-pronged attack on tumors. This synergistic approach may overcome resistance mechanisms to single-agent therapies, broadening the patient population responsive to immunotherapy and potentially leading to deeper and more durable anti-tumor responses. For instance, IBI322, an anti-CD47/PD-L1 bispecific antibody, exemplifies this strategy by exhibiting high selectivity for tumor cells, which helps mitigate off-tumor effects. This agent promotes macrophage phagocytosis of lymphoma cells and enhances anti-tumor cytotoxic T-cell responses. A Phase I study indicated that IBI322 monotherapy was safe and effective in patients with Hodgkin lymphoma resistant to either anti-PD-1 or anti-PD-L1 treatment [38]. Beyond bispecifics, combining αCD47 Ab agents with existing therapies is also being explored. For example, magrolimab (αCD47 Ab) combined with cetuximab (αEGFR Ab) was tested in refractory KRAS wildtype and mutant colorectal cancer (CRC) tumors. Among the 30 patients with KRAS-wildtype CRC, an objective response rate (ORR) of 6.7% was observed, with a median overall survival (OS) of 10.1 months and a median progression-free survival (PFS) of 3.6 months [39]. These encouraging results suggest that such combination strategies hold great therapeutic potential for the future. Pro-αCD47 Ab also holds significant promise for combination with various existing and emerging therapies, potentially leading to even greater efficacy and improved patient outcomes.

The development of strategies that enhance the specificity of CD47-targeting agents directly addresses the critical challenges faced by earlier generations of these drugs. Historically, the broad expression of CD47 on healthy cells necessitated dose reductions to manage severe toxicities. These limitations often translated to insufficient therapeutic efficacy at the tumor site, as the administered drug concentration might not have been high enough to achieve optimal anti-tumor activity. For instance, Lemzoparlimab (TJC4), an αCD47 Ab engineered to avoid binding to red blood cells by targeting a glycosylation-shielded epitope [40]. While this design helped minimize hemagglutination and anemia, its clinical efficacy as a monotherapy was relatively modest. In a study involving patients with refractory non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma (NHL), Lemzoparlimab monotherapy achieved an overall response rate (ORR) of only 10–15% [41]. In another example, Gentulizumab, a humanized αCD47 Ab, showed reduced binding to red blood cells and a favorable safety profile in preclinical models, but required higher doses (up to 450 mg/kg) to achieve comparable anti-tumor activity [42]. These cases illustrate the delicate balance between minimizing systemic toxicity and maintaining sufficient anti-tumor potency, highlighting the need for strategies that can achieve both goals without compromise.

Current clinical Ab therapies often face challenges due to systemic activation and on-target off-tumor effects, leading to severe side effects. In addition to CD47, antigens like EGFR [43, 44] and CTLA4 [45, 46], expressed in both diseased and healthy tissues, struggle to discriminate, leading to impaired function in normal tissues and dose-limiting toxicities. To overcome these challenges, we have developed a novel approach utilizing the Ab’s autologous hinge domain as a self-derived “spatial shield,” connected via a specific protease substrate peptide. This design forms a precursor Ab (Pro-Ab) that remains inactive in circulation, preventing unwanted activation, and restores its full therapeutic function only in protease-rich disease areas. We have applied this strategy in several fields [47–51]. For instance, infliximab, a drug mainly used for treating rheumatoid arthritis (RA) and known to cause serious adverse effects, was engineered into a pro-infliximab to prevent off-target effects [50]. This innovative approach significantly reduces the risk of side effects while preserving therapeutic efficacy by enabling selective activation in the disease microenvironment.

In conclusion, we have developed a novel Pro-αCD47 Ab that addresses the most significant limitation of current CD47-targeted therapies, i.e., their inability to distinguish between cancerous and normal cells. By engineering a spatial-hindrance Ab lock that activates only in diseased tissues, we offer a promising approach to enhance the specificity and safety of Ab-based cancer treatments. The Pro-αCD47 Ab has the following advantages: (1) it can mitigate side effects on normal cells, (2) it maintains consistent phagocytosis ability with αCD47 Ab, and (3) the spatial-hindrance-based Ab lock can be easily applied to all current Ab drugs. This strategy represents a significant step forward in developing more precise and less toxic immunotherapeutic interventions.

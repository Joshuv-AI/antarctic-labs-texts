miR‐199a/214 cluster enhances prostate cancer sensitiveness to nimotuzumab via targeting TBL1XR1
Authors: Sheng Hu, Qiang Zhou, Qiang Lu, Xi Guo, Yong Wang, Yi‐Xing Duan
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractProstate cancer (PCa) is a significant health concern affecting men worldwide. Previous studies have shown that nimotuzumab, a drug targeting the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR), can effectively inhibit cancer progression. Here, we aimed to explore the role of miR‐199a/214 cluster in mediating the inhibitory effect of nimotuzumab on the development of PCa. In this study, we conducted an MTT assay to assess cell proliferation and utilized flow cytometry to evaluate cell apoptosis and cell cycle arrest. To investigate the molecular mechanisms underlying the effects of nimotuzumab on prostate cancer development, we focused on the miR‐199a‐5p and miR‐214‐3p miRNA clusters. The TargetScan Human database was used to predict the binding sites between miR‐199a‐5p or miR‐214‐3p and the 3'‐UTR of the transducin (β)‐like 1 X‐linked receptor 1 (TBL1XR1) mRNA. To confirm the direct interaction and binding between miR‐199a‐5p or miR‐214‐3p and the 3'‐UTR of TBL1XR1 mRNA, we performed luciferase reporter assays. Our findings demonstrated that nimotuzumab exerted a significant dosage‐dependent suppression of PCa cell proliferation and facilitated PCa cell apoptosis and cell cycle arrest. Concurrently, nimotuzumab obviously impeded the activity of Wnt/β‐catenin and EGFR signaling pathways in PCa cells. We also observed downregulation of miR‐199a‐5p and miR‐214‐3p in PCa cells. Overexpression of miR‐199a/214 cluster inhibited PCa cell viability and enhanced cell apoptosis. Furthermore, we found that miR‐199a/214 cluster augmented the inhibitory effect of nimotuzumab on PCa cell proliferation and promoted its ability to induce apoptosis and cell cycle arrest. This effect was reversed upon TBL1XR1 overexpression, indicating that TBL1XR1 is involved in the regulatory pathway of miR‐199a/214 and nimotuzumab in PCa cells. We further revealed that TBL1XR1 was overexpressed in PCa and was identified as a downstream target of the miR‐199a/214 cluster. In nimotuzumab‐treated PCa cells, the overexpression of miR‐199a/214 markedly inhibited Wnt/β‐catenin and EGFR signaling, and this effect was also rescued by TBL1XR1 overexpression. In summary, our data indicated that miR‐199a/214 cluster play a crucial role in enhancing the inhibitory effect of nimotuzumab on PCa development by downregulating TBL1XR1 and modulating Wnt/β‐catenin and EGFR signaling pathways. These findings offer a novel therapeutic approach for the treatment of prostate cancer.Keywords: cell apoptosis, miR‐199a/214 cluster, nimotuzumab, proliferation, prostate cancer

INTRODUCTION

Prostate cancer (PCa) is a significant malignancy affecting men and is the second leading cause of cancer‐related male mortality worldwide. PCa is characterized by its heterogeneity and potential to metastasize rapidly, transforming from indolent to aggressively progressing metastatic forms. 1 Unfortunately, due to the lack of early and precise diagnostic methods, most PCa cases are diagnosed at advanced stages. Metastatic PCa is the major cause of PCa‐related death, with a five‐year survival rate of about 30%, compared to approximately 98% in non‐metastatic PCa patients. 2 , 3 In recent years, substantial progress has been achieved in PCa treatment, with options including surgical interventions and various non‐surgical therapies such as androgen deprivation therapy, chemotherapy, radiation therapy, and immune therapy, either used alone or in combination. 4 , 5 Despite these advancements, there is still an urgent need for more effective treatment methods to combat PCa.

Nimotuzumab is a significant advancement as the first functional monoclonal antibody for the treatment of malignant tumors in our country. Its mechanism of action involves competing with other specific ligands to target epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR, effectively suppressing cancer development. 6 Previous studies have demonstrated the anti‐cancer effects of nimotuzumab in various types of cancers, including advanced head and neck cancer, 7 nasopharyngeal carcinoma, 8 esophageal squamous cell cancer, 9 as well as PCa. 10 Furthermore, in combination with oxidized tea polyphenols, nimotuzumab has shown enhanced inhibition of EGFR and tumor growth in non‐small cell lung cancer cells and tumor‐bearing mice. 11 Additionally, Hu et al. revealed that nimotuzumab can markedly suppress malignant behaviors in PCa cell lines, such as epithelial‐mesenchymal transition (EMT), invasion, migration, and proliferation. It also accelerates PCa cell apoptosis through the regulation of AKT/Y‐box binding protein‐1/AR signaling pathway. 12 Despite these promising findings, the role and action mechanism of nimotuzumab in PCa are still not fully understood, and more research is needed to elucidate its potential therapeutic benefits in this specific type of cancer.

microRNAs (miRNAs) are single‐stranded RNA molecules that play a crucial role in the development of various diseases including cancer. For instance, miR‐199a‐5p expression is lower in laryngeal cancer tumor tissues than that in normal tissues. Overexpression of miR‐199a‐5p in laryngeal cancer cells markedly inhibits cell invasion and migration while facilitating cell apoptosis. 13 Similarly, miR‐214‐3p is downregulated in the HPV16‐positive cervical cancer cells, and its low expression contributes to cancer cell proliferation. 14 In PCa, a large number of miRNAs have been proved to have abnormal expression and participated in the disease progression. A recent study indicated that miR‐214‐3p depletion exacerbates PCa cells' epithelial‐mesenchymal transition (EMT), migration, invasion, proliferation, and tumor growth. 15 Based on the miRNA sequences in the Sanger miRBase database, miR‐199a‐3p and miR‐199a‐5p are transcribed from the pri‐miR‐199a, while miR‐214‐3p and miR‐214‐5p are transcribed from the pri‐miR‐214. Together, they form the miR‐199a/214 cluster. 16 In our study, we revealed that miR‐199a/214 cluster enhanced the inhibitory effects of nimotuzumab on the development of PCa by targeting inhibiting Transducin (β)‐like 1 X‐linked receptor 1 (TBL1XR1), which is a member of SMRT/NCOR1 transcriptional repressor complexes and has been proved to promote the EMT, metastasis and stemness of cancer cells. 17 , 18 Our findings offer a novel therapeutic approach for the treatment of PCa.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Cell culture and treatment

Three PCa cell lines (DU145, PC3, and LNCap) and the control RWPE‐2 cells were provided by the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC; Manassas, VA, USA) and cultured in RPMI‐1640 medium (Gibco, Carlsbad, CA). The medium was supplemented with 1% penicillin–streptomycin (Gibco) and 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; Hyclone, Logan, UT, USA) and maintained at 37°C in a 5% CO2 incubator.

In order to analyze the effects of nimotuzumab on PCa cell behavior, the cells were treated with various concentrations of nimotuzumab (0, 50, 100, and 200 μg/mL) for 48 h. Specific miR‐199a‐5p mimics (5′‐CCCAGUGUUCAGACUACCUGUUC‐3′), miR‐214‐3p mimics (5′‐ACAGCAGGCACAGACAG GCAGU‐3′), and a negative control mimic (mimics NC, 5′‐UUCUCCGAACGUGUCACGU‐3′) were obtained from the GenePharma Co. (Shanghai, China) and transfected into PC3 cells by utilizing Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA). Moreover, a vector expressing TBL1XR1 was also transfected into the cells using Lipofectamine 2000. Cells were collected 48 h after transfection for further analysis.

Cell proliferation detection

Cell proliferation was assessed by utilizing the MTT assay. PC3 cells (5 × 104 cells per well) were seeded in 24‐well plates. After 48 h of cell transfection or treatment, 10 μL of MTT solution (5 mg/mL in PBS) was added to each well, and the cells were further incubated for 2 h. The medium was then removed, and 0.5 mL isopropanol was added. The plates were gently shaken for 15 min at room temperature. Subsequently, 10 μL aliquots from each well were transferred to a 96‐well plate, and the optical density at 570 nm was measured using a plate reader.

Cell apoptosis and cell cycle detection

The ratio of apoptotic cells was determined by flow cytometry using an Annexin V/fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC) kit (Abcam, Cambridge, United Kingdom). Briefly, the cells were seeded in six‐well plates at a concentration of 3 × 105 cells/per well and incubated overnight. After 48 h of cell transfection or treatment, the cells were harvested and washed twice with cold PBS. The supernatant was discarded, and the cells were resuspended in 195 μL of binding buffer. Then, 5 μL of Annexin V‐FITC was added to the cell suspension and incubated for 10 min on ice in the dark. Subsequently, 1 μL of propidium iodide (PI) (50 μg/mL) was added. The cells were then analyzed by flow cytometer using FACSCalibur flow cytometer (BD Bioscience, Franklin Lakes, NJ, USA).

The cell cycle assay was performed with PI staining. Briefly, cells were trypsinized, collected, and washed twice with cold PBS. The cells were then fixed with 1 mL 70% cold ethanol and stored overnight at −20°C. Next, the cells were resuspended in a solution containing 0.2 mg/mL PI (Sigma‐Aldrich), 0.1% Triton‐100, and 1 mg/mL RNase A (Sigma‐Aldrich). The cell suspension was further incubated in the dark for 30 min at room temperature. Subsequently, the cells were analyzed for DNA using a flow cytometer (BD Bioscience) for each sample, and the percentage of cell‐cycle distribution in G0/G1, S, and G2/M phases of the cell cycle was quantified using Flow Jo software version 7.6.1.

Analysis of protein expression

Protein extracts were loaded on SDS‐PAGE gels and separated by electrophoresis. The separated proteins were then transferred to nitrocellulose membranes and then blocked with non‐fat milk. Next, the membranes were incubated with primary antibodies (p‐GSK3β, β‐catenin, p‐EGFR, TBL1XR1, p‐AKT, and APC antibodies) overnight, which were obtained from Abcam (Cambridge, UK). Then, the membranes were incubated with HRP‐conjugated IgG. Finally, protein signals were visualized using an enhanced chemiluminescence detection kit (DW101, TransGen Biotech, Beijing, China).

Analysis of gene expression

The expression level of miR‐199a‐5p, miR‐214‐3p, and TBL1XR1 mRNA were evaluated by utilizing quantitative real‐time polymerase chain reaction (qRT‐PCR). Total RNA was extracted from the cultured cells and tissues with an RNX‐plus kit (SinaClon, Tehran, Iran). Subsequently, cDNA was synthesized from the extracted RNA using the cDNA synthesis kit (Takara Shuzo, Otsu, Japan). RT‐PCR of the first strand cDNA was executed using the ABI StepOnePlus (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA, USA) and the SYBR® Select Master Mix (Applied Biosystems). The expression of endogenous housekeeping gene GAPDH or U6 was used as a reference to calculate the relative gene expression levels. All experiments were conducted following the protocols provided by the manufacturer.

Bioinformatics analysis

GEPIA database was utilized to analyze the expression of TBL1XR1 in PCa tissues and normal tissues. Moreover, the binding sites between TBL1XR1 mRNA and miR‐199a‐5p or miR‐214‐3p were predicted by utilizing TargetScan Human database, and the combination of them was verified by utilizing luciferase reporter assay. The sequence of TBL1XR1 mRNA containing the binding sites with miR‐199a‐5p or miR‐214‐3p was cloned by PCR into a luciferase vector. The luciferase vector was transfected with miR‐199a‐5p mimics, miR‐214‐3p mimics, or mimics NC into 293T cells. 48 h after transfection, luciferase activity was determined by a dual‐luciferase reporter assay. The firefly luciferase activity was normalized to Renilla luciferase activity.

Animal experiments

Animal experiments were conducted in accordance with the approved protocols by our hospital's ethical committee. Six‐week‐old BALB/c nude mice (male) were provided by Aniphe BioLab (Jiangsu, China) and randomly divided into four groups (n = 5 per group): Control, nimotuzumab, nimotuzumab + agnomiR‐199a‐5p, and nimotuzumab + agnomiR‐214‐3p. For the mice in the Control group, PC3 cells (1 × 105 cell/mouse) were subcutaneously injected into the right hind of mice. For the mice in the nimotuzumab group, the mice were intravenously injected with nimotuzumab (1 mg/mouse) every 3 days. For the mice in the nimotuzumab + agnomiR‐199a‐5p and nimotuzumab + agnomiR‐214‐3p groups, the mice were intravenously injected with nimotuzumab (1 mg/mouse) and 10 nmol/L of agomiR every 3 days. After 21 days of cell inoculation, tumor tissues were collected for further analysis. The sequences of miR‐199a‐5p agomiR were 5’‐CCCAG UGUUCAGACUACCUGUUC‐3′ and miR‐214‐3p agomiR was 5’‐ACAGCAGGCACAGACAGG CAGU‐3′.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 22.0 statistical software (IBM, Armonk, NY, USA). Images were edited using GraphPad Prism 5.0 software. Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (mean ± SD). The comparisons between two independent groups and multiple groups were executed using Student's t‐tests and One‐way ANOVA followed by post‐hoc tests, respectively.

RESULTS

Nimotuzumab effectively suppresses PCa cell proliferation and Wnt/β‐catenin and EGFR signaling, induces apoptosis and cell cycle arrest at G0/G1 phase

We first investigated the effects of nimotuzumab on the PCa cell cycle, apoptosis, and proliferation. We treated PCa cells (PC3, DU145, and LNCap) with nimotuzumab and accessed their viability. As displayed in Figure 1A, PC3 and DU145 cell viabilities were dramatically reduced by 100 and 200 μg/mL of nimotuzumab, while LNCap cell viability was suppressed by 50, 100, and 200 μg/mL of nimotuzumab. Additionally, the viabilities of PC3 and DU145 cells were higher than those of LNCap cells at the same dose of nimotuzumab. Notably, the suppression of PCa cell viability by 200 μg/mL nimotuzumab was stronger than 100 μg/mL. Moreover, we noticed that 100 and 200 μg/mL nimotuzumab significantly aggravated apoptosis in PC3 cells (Figure 1B,C) and DU145 cells (Figure S1A), and caused cycle arrest at G0/G1 phase in PC3 cells (Figure 1D,E) and DU145 cells (Figure S1B). The promotory effects of 200 μg/mL of nimotuzumab were stronger than those of 100 μg/mL of nimotuzumab on apoptosis induction and cell cycle arrest at G0/G1 phase in PCa cells. Since activated Wnt/β‐catenin signaling plays a crucial role in drug resistance of cancer cells, 19 we thus determined the activity of Wnt/β‐catenin and EGFR signaling in the presence of nimotuzumab. Our data demonstrated that nimotuzumab treatment resulted in a dose‐dependent decrease in the levels of phosphorylated glycogen synthase kinase‐3β (GSK‐3β) and EGFR, as well as a decrease in the level of β‐catenin (Figure 1F,I). These findings suggest that nimotuzumab effectively suppresses PCa cell proliferation and Wnt/β‐catenin and EGFR signaling while promoting PCa cell apoptosis and cell cycle arrest at the G0/G1 phase.

The ratios of apoptotic PC3 cells were evaluated by flow cytometry (B and C), and the distribution of PC3 cells in G0/G1, S, and G2/M phases of the cell cycle was also analyzed by flow cytometry (D and E). miR‐199a/214 cluster enhances nimotuzumab's suppression of PCa cell viability and promotion of cell apoptosis and cell cycle arrest

To evaluate the role of miR‐199a/214 cluster in regulating nimotuzumab's effects on PCa cell viability, apoptosis, and cell cycle, we first examined the expression levels of miR‐199a‐5p and miR‐214‐3p in PCa cells. Our results demonstrated that both miR‐199a‐5p (Figure 2A) and miR‐214‐3p (Figure 2B) were downregulated in PCa cell lines (DU145 and PC3) compared to control cells (RWPE‐2). Then, we transfected the specific mimics of miR‐199a‐5p and miR‐214‐3p into PC3 cells and successfully upregulated their expression levels (Figure 2C). In the absence of nimotuzumab treatment, overexpression of miR‐199a‐5p or miR‐214‐3p reduced PCa cell viability (Figure S2A) and increased the proportion of apoptotic PCa cells (Figure S2B,C). In the presence of nimotuzumab, both miR‐199a‐5p and miR‐214‐3p overexpression decreased PCa cell viability (Figure 2D). Additionally, the ratios of apoptotic PCa cells (Figure 2E,G) and cells arrested at the G0/G1 phase (Figure 2F,H) were higher in nimotuzumab‐treated PCa cells compared to untreated cells. Furthermore, the ratios in nimotuzumab‐treated PCa cells were further increased following transfection with miR‐199a‐5p mimics or miR‐214‐3p mimics. In summary, overexpression of both miR‐199a‐5p and miR‐214‐3p significantly enhanced nimotuzumab's inhibition of PCa cell proliferation and promotion of apoptosis and cell cycle arrest at the G0/G1 phase. These findings indicate that the miR‐199a/214 cluster plays a critical role in regulating the response of PCa cells to nimotuzumab treatment.

TBL1XR1 is a downstream target of both miR‐199a‐5p and miR‐214‐3p

TBL1XR1 acts as an oncogene in multiple tumors. 20 In our study, we also found that the expression levels of TBL1XR1 mRNA and TBL1XR1 protein were significantly higher in DU145 and PC3 cells compared to the control cells (RWPE‐2) (Figure 3A,B). Consistent with this, analysis of data from the GEPIA website confirmed the high expression of TBL1XR1 in PCa tumor tissues (Figure 3C). Importantly, when miR‐199a‐5p or miR‐214‐3p mimics were transfected into PCa cells, the levels of TBL1XR1 mRNA and TBL1XR1 protein were remarkably declined (Figure 3D–F). Furthermore, miR‐199a‐5p or miR‐214‐3p overexpression in PCa cells also led to a downregulation in the phosphorylation levels of GSK3β and EGFR and β‐catenin expression (Figure 3E,G–I).

To investigate the molecular mechanism, the binding site of miR‐199a‐5p and TBL1XR1 mRNA 3’‐UTR was identified using the TargetScan Human database (Figure 3J), and the binding was confirmed through luciferase reporter assay (Figure 3K). Similarly, the binding site of miR‐214‐3p and TBL1XR1 mRNA 3’‐UTR was also determined using the TargetScan Human database (Figure 3L). The results of the luciferase reporter assay demonstrated that the luciferase activity of the PCa cells co‐transfected with miR‐214‐3p mimics and TBL1XR1 luciferase reporter plasmid was obviously downregulated (Figure 3M). Thus, TBL1XR1 is a downstream target of both miR‐199a‐5p and miR‐214‐3p, and overexpression of these miRNAs can effectively downregulate TBL1XR1 expression in PCa cells.

TBL1XR1 overexpression rescues the effects of miR‐199a/214 cluster on PCa cells

To verify whether miR‐199a/214 cluster enhances the effects of nimotuzumab on PCa cells by regulating TBL1XR1/Wnt/β‐catenin and TB1XR1/EGFR signaling pathways, we co‐transfected TBL1XR1 overexpression vector with miR‐199a‐5p mimics or miR‐214‐3p mimics into the nimotuzumab‐treated PCa cells. Our data revealed that miR‐199a‐5p or miR‐214‐3p overexpression resulted in a decrease in the phosphorylation level of GSK3β and EGFR and the expression level of β‐catenin in PCa cells. However, these effects were partially reversed when TBL1XR1 was overexpressed along with miR‐199a‐5p or miR‐214‐3p (Figure 4A–D). Furthermore, the inhibition effects of miR‐199a‐5p and miR‐214‐3p on PCa cell viability in the presence of nimotuzumab were also partially rescued by TBL1XR1 overexpression (Figure 4E). In addition, TBL1XR1 overexpression also partially rescued the miR‐199a‐5p or miR‐214‐3p overexpression‐induced cell apoptosis (Figure 4F,G) and cell cycle arrest at G0/G1 phase (Figure 4H,I) in the nimotuzumab‐treated PCa cells. In conclusion, our findings suggest that miR‐199a/214 cluster enhances the inhibitory effects of nimotuzumab on PCa cell proliferation and promotes apoptosis and cell cycle arrest by regulating the TBL1XR1/Wnt/β‐catenin and TBL1XR1/EGFR signaling pathways. TBL1XR1 overexpression partially rescues the effects of miR‐199a‐5p and miR‐214‐3p on these pathways, indicating that TBL1XR1 is a downstream mediator of miR‐199a/214 cluster's actions in nimotuzumab‐treated PCa cells.

miR‐199a/214 cluster effectively enhances nimotuzumab's inhibitory effect on tumor growth

In the final part of our study, we aimed to investigate the influences of miR‐199a/214 cluster in combination with nimotuzumab on PCa in vivo. Tumor mice were administrated with nimotuzumab alone or in combination with miR‐199a‐5p agomiR or miR‐214‐3p agomiR. Our data showed that nimotuzumab administration effectively suppressed PCa tumor growth, and this effect was markedly enhanced by miR‐199a‐5p or miR‐214‐3p overexpression (Figure 5A,B). Additionally, the tumor weight was lower in nimotuzumab‐treated tumor mice than in untreated mice, and this reduction in tumor weight was further amplified following the administration of miR‐199a‐5p or miR‐214‐3p agomiRs (Figure 5C). Moreover, we examined the levels of TBL1XR1 mRNA and protein and the levels of phosphorylated GSK3β, β‐catenin, and phosphorylated EGFR in the tumors of the mice. Nimotuzumab treatment led to a downregulation of these molecules, and this downregulation was further enhanced by miR‐199a‐5p or miR‐214‐3p agomiRs administration (Figure 5D,I). Interestingly, we also observed that nimotuzumab treatment downregulated p‐AKT levels and promoted APC (adenomatous polyposis coli) expression in the tumors, and these effects were further enhanced by miR‐199a‐5p or miR‐214‐3p overexpression (Figure 5J,L). APC is a key negative regulator of the Wnt signaling pathway, and its expression level is inversely related to that of β‐catenin in cancer cells. 21 Overall, our findings indicate that the miR‐199a/214 cluster enhances the therapeutic efficacy of nimotuzumab in PCa tumor mice. The combination of nimotuzumab with miR‐199a‐5p or miR‐214‐3p agomiRs led to greater suppression of tumor growth and downregulation of key signaling molecules involved in the Wnt/β‐catenin and EGFR pathways.

DISCUSSION

Nimotuzumab is a well‐known monoclonal antibody targeting the EGFR to counteract cancer progression. 6 Recently research has highlighted the potential benefits of combining nimotuzumab with cisplatin in the treatment of head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, showing improved overall survival and reduced cancer stem cell presence compared to cisplatin treatment alone. 22 Clinical and experimental studies have increasingly demonstrated nimotuzumab's effectiveness in various cancer types, including head and neck cancer, 23 glioma in children, 24 central nervous system tumors, 25 and esophageal squamous cell carcinoma. 26 Despite these advances, little is known about nimotuzumab's role and action mechanism of nimotuzumab in PCa. In this study, we explored the effects of nimotuzumab treatment on PCa cells. Our findings indicate that nimotuzumab treatment remarkably suppresses PCa cell proliferation, accelerates cell apoptosis, and induces cell cycle arrest at the G0/G1 phase in a dosage‐dependent manner in vitro. Moreover, nimotuzumab administration effectively inhibits PCa tumor growth in vivo.

miRNAs play a crucial role in regulating cancer progression. In non‐small cell lung cancer, miR‐199a‐5p is found to be downregulated. Overexpression of miR‐199a‐5p promotes apoptosis in lung cancer cells and enhances sensitivity to bevacizumab, while also constrains cell invasion, migration, and proliferation by inhibiting HIF‐α and STAT3, suggesting that miR‐199a‐5p may act as a tumor suppressor. 27 Similarly, miR‐214‐3p is also recognized as a tumor suppressor. In endometrial cancer, miR‐214‐3p is shown to inhibit epithelial‐mesenchymal transition (EMT) and metastasis by inhibiting twist family bHLH transcription factor 1. 28 Interestingly, both miR‐199a and miR‐214 are derived from the same intron‐less transcript of dynamin 3 opposite strand. 29 In PCa‐related studies, miR‐214‐3p and miR‐199a‐5p were reported to inhibit PCa development. 15 , 30 In our current study, we found that miR‐199a‐5p and miR‐214‐3p expression are downregulated in PCa cells. Upregulation of miR‐199a‐5p and miR‐214‐3p reduces PCa cell viability and facilitates cell apoptosis. Importantly, increasing the expression of miR‐199a‐5p and miR‐214‐3p significantly enhances the inhibitory effect of nimotuzumab on PCa cell proliferation and the promotion of apoptosis and cell cycle arrest. In our animal experiments, the combination treatment of nimotuzumab and miR‐199a/214 cluster shows superior inhibition of PCa tumor growth compared to nimotuzumab treatment alone.

Wnt/β‐catenin signaling is a versatile pathway that plays a crucial role in various cellular processes, including apoptosis, invasion, differentiation and migration, thus regulating the development of many diseases. GSK3β has been identified as an inhibitor of Wnt/β‐catenin signaling. 31 GSK3β induces the phosphorylation of β‐catenin, thereby regulating cell behaviors. 32 , 33 Studies have shown that the decline of p‐GSK‐3β level is accompanied by a downregulation of β‐catenin. 34 Previous studies suggested that Wnt/β‐catenin signaling is activated in PCa. Inhibition of GSK3β and β‐catenin has been shown to suppress PCa development. 35 Additionally, TBL1XR1 overexpression has been linked to the activation of Wnt/β‐catenin signaling. 36 Zhou et al. demonstrated that TBL1XR1 facilitates gastric cancer progression by activating β‐catenin/MMP7/EGFR signaling. 37

Our data revealed that nimotuzumab treatment and upregulation of miR‐199a‐5p and miR‐214‐3p led to the inactivation of Wnt/β‐catenin and EGFR signaling in PCa cells. Furthermore, we demonstrated that TBL1XR1 is a downstream target of both miR‐199a‐5p and miR‐214‐p. Importantly, the miR‐199a/214 cluster further enhanced the inhibitory effects of nimotuzumab on PCa cell proliferation and Wnt/β‐catenin and EGFR signaling, as well as the promotion of PCa cell apoptosis and cell cycle arrest. These effects were partially rescued by TBL1XR1 overexpression. In our animal experiments, the miR‐199a/214 cluster also enhanced the inhibitory effect of nimotuzumab on Wnt/β‐catenin and EGFR signaling. These findings highlight the interplay between nimotuzumab, miR‐199a/214 cluster, and TBL1XR1 in regulating Wnt/β‐catenin and EGFR signaling pathways and their potential as targets for enhancing the therapeutic efficacy of nimotuzumab in PCa treatment. Our study still suffered from some limitations. Why does the miR‐199a/214 cluster can enhance the anti‐cancer function of nimotuzumab in PCa? Does the expression level of the miR‐199a/214 cluster changes in response to nimotuzumab stimulation? And what is the mechanism that nimotuzumab regulates miR‐199a/214 cluster expression? In addition, the action mechanism of TBL1XR1 regulation of Wnt/β‐catenin and EGFR signaling needs to be explored. The role and definitive mechanism of TBL1XR1 in PCa development remains unclear.

CONCLUSION

In summary, our study demonstrates that the combination treatment of nimotuzumab and miR‐199a/214 cluster holds promise as a more effective therapeutic approach for PCa compared to nimotuzumab treatment alone. The overexpression of miR‐199a/214 cluster enhances the inhibitory effects of nimotuzumab on prostate cancer progression by targeting TBL1XR1 and suppressing Wnt/β‐catenin and EGFR signaling pathways. These findings present a novel idea for the treatment of PCa, highlighting the potential of utilizing miRNA‐based therapies in combination with targeted antibodies like nimotuzumab to improve patient outcomes. Further exploration of this combination approach may have significant implications for the clinical management of PCa.

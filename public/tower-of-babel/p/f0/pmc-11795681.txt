Inferior vena cava rupture secondary to filter-associated thrombotic occlusion treated with mechanical thrombectomy
Authors: Alexander S Misono
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractInferior vena cava (IVC) rupture secondary to IVC filter-associated occlusion is an extraordinarily rare and life-threatening condition. Here, a 51-year-old male with distant history of IVC filter placement presented with abdominal pain and mild leg swelling. Shortly after diagnosis of IVC thrombosis, he developed sudden, severe hemorrhagic shock and a large retroperitoneal hematoma due to IVC rupture. Rapid triage led to emergent venous thrombectomy and IVC recanalization, resulting in bleeding cessation and hemodynamic normalization. Patients with chronic indwelling IVC filters or IVC occlusion can present with IVC rupture. Endovascular thrombectomy and stenting may be the only viable treatment.Keywords: Aspiration thrombectomy, Deep venous thrombosis, Inferior vena cava rupture, Retroperitoneal hemorrhage, Venous thromboembolism

Inferior vena cava (IVC) filters continue to be placed despite historical controversy related to longer-term complications, such as occlusion and difficulty with removal. While retrieval is recommended in short intervals, historical IVC filters may be forgotten, particularly in asymptomatic patients.1 Commonly reported complications include occlusion, tilting, perforation, and fracture.2,3 Filter-associated IVC rupture, however, is an extremely rare entity, and such a case is presented in this report. The patient consented to publication of case details and images.

Case presentation

A 51-year-old male presented to the Emergency Department with abdominal pain. Medical history included thoracic aortic aneurysm and a motor vehicle accident in 1990 during which an IVC filter was placed.

The patient reported 8 of 10 abdominal pain exacerbated by ambulation. He also reported dyspnea with exertion and mild bilateral leg swelling. Contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) of the abdomen/pelvis was performed, demonstrating a Simon Nitinol filter (C. R. Bard, Inc) in expected location but with strut penetration through the IVC wall (Fig 1, A, B). There was increased IVC density and enlargement, raising suspicion for thrombosis.

Initial vital signs and laboratory studies in the Emergency Department were essentially unremarkable. Venous ultrasound confirmed occlusive thrombus within the IVC.

Shortly after initiating heparin infusion for new diagnosis of deep vein thrombosis, the patient experienced a syncopal episode. The patient became tachycardic (180 bpm), hypotensive (∼30 mmHg systolic), diaphoretic, and somewhat confused. Given reported history of thoracic aortic aneurysm, the initial differential diagnosis included an aortic rupture with hemorrhage among other hemorrhagic events. Emergent CT angiography showed a spontaneous large retroperitoneal hematoma with hyperdense thrombus adjacent to the IVC. For treatment of hemorrhagic shock, the patient was placed on multiple vasopressors, and large volume blood transfusion was begun. No active arterial bleeding was observed (Fig 1, C, D). Diagnosis of IVC rupture was presumed. Laboratory studies were notable for hemoglobin of 6 g/dL (previously 14 g/dL), creatinine 1.9 mg/dL, glomular filtration rate 45 mL/min/1.73 m2, lactate 14 mmol/L, and fibrinogen 80 mg/dL.

The patient was brought emergently to the angiography suite for venography and definitive endovascular intervention. Open repair was briefly considered but deferred due to severe, acute illness. Scout radiography demonstrated a Simon Nitinol filter overlying the mid abdomen (Fig 2, A). The right common femoral vein was accessed with ultrasound-guided Seldinger technique, and a 9-Fr vascular sheath was placed. Angiography showed complete IVC occlusion below the level of the filter. There was associated active, large-volume retroperitoneal extravasation immediately below the occlusion (Fig 2, B). Recanalization of the IVC was made the top priority. A 4-Fr Glide hockey catheter was advanced into the IVC over an 0.035-inch Glidewire, and catheter access was obtained beyond the IVC filter into the superior vena cava. Then, an 0.035-inch Superstiff Amplatz wire was placed through the catheter into the superior vena cava.

Patient is taken emergently for endovascular venography and intervention. A, Scout radiograph demonstrates intact Simon Nitinol filter. B, Venogram shows inferior vena cava (IVC) occlusion and rupture. C, Triever24 FlowTriever catheter is placed for aspiration thrombectomy. D, Triever 20 Curve FlowTriever catheter is placed coaxially for directional aspiration thrombectomy.

After dilatation, a 24-Fr Intri Sheath (Inari Medical) was placed in the right common femoral vein, and a 24-Fr Triever catheter from the FlowTriever System (Inari Medical) was placed into the external iliac vein (Fig 2, C). Aspiration thrombectomy was performed using a 60-cc syringe while moving the catheter into the IVC and IVC filter, removing several heterogeneous thrombi. Then, a Triever20 Curve catheter (Inari Medical) was placed through the 24-Fr catheter, and additional thrombectomy was performed within the IVC filter (Fig 2, D). Aspirated blood was returned to the patient after filtration through the FlowSaver System (Inari Medical).

Following thrombectomy, the patient immediately demonstrated significant hemodynamic improvement, and vasopressor requirements decreased dramatically within minutes. To ensure reliable flow, the decision was made to place an IVC stent across the filter. Balloon venoplasty of the IVC filter was performed up to 16 mm (Fig 3, A). Measurements were performed using intravascular ultrasound. A 20 mm × 10 cm Abre stent (Medtronic) was then advanced into place, deployed, and venoplastied to 16 mm (Fig 3, B).

After thrombectomy, inferior vena cava (IVC) venoplasty and stent placement is performed. A, Venoplasty up to 16 mm. B, Final single-shot image after stent deployment. C, Final venogram demonstrates resolution of rupture and patent IVC stent. D, Computed tomography venogram performed 2 months after index intervention demonstrates widely patent stent with successful exclusion or “endotrashing” of indwelling IVC filter.

Completion images demonstrated a technically successful IVC recanalization and collapse of the previously placed filter, with resolution of hemorrhage, no further evidence of IVC rupture, and establishment of antegrade flow (Fig 3, C). Repeat intravascular ultrasound confirmed IVC stent and iliocaval venous patency.

Shortly after normalization of hematologic parameters, the patient was restarted on heparin. A 1-week postoperative course was complicated by severe abdominal pain with a transient concern for compartment syndrome, temporary renal failure, and deconditioning. However, laboratory abnormalities normalized rapidly. After a short stay in rehabilitation, the patient was discharged home on daily therapeutic apixaban and aspirin (81 mg). Follow-up evaluation has demonstrated patency of the IVC stent by CT at 2 months (Fig 3, D) and ultrasound extending to 6 months. The patient remains asymptomatic.

Discussion

This case demonstrates the life-saving impact of modern endovascular techniques in the context of an infrequent presentation. Without rapid deployment of endovascular thrombectomy, the patient course may have been considerably worse. Furthermore, facility with the diagnosis and management of venous thromboembolism—required for use of the techniques employed here—granted valuable speed.

This case required immediate decision-making. It is the author’s opinion—based on this as well as prior similar cases—that the patient likely had long-standing filter-associated IVC stenosis. Then, the patient unfortunately developed acute IVC thrombosis and occlusion. This hyperacute thrombosis—whether in situ or embolic—resulted in abrupt, severe venous hypertension. Subsequent rupture was likely triggered and potentiated by the combination of penetrated IVC filter struts, Valsalva maneuver, and/or initiation of anticoagulation. Therefore, with thrombectomy reestablishing a channel through the IVC, in-line flow was re-established, venous pressures dropped, and blood flow was allowed to proceed antegrade to the right atrium. In this case in particular, a large-bore 24-Fr thrombectomy catheter was chosen to maximize speed and efficiency of recanalization; this choice may have been life-saving. Stent placement across the permanent IVC filter was then adjunctively performed to manage chronic filter-associated IVC stenosis as well as prevent recurrent thrombosis. In our experience, uncovered stents are typically adequate for maintaining both patency and preventing further extravasation for such cases; stent grafts are rarely needed.

IVC rupture is an uncommon entity mostly described in trauma. Roughly 50% of patients with IVC injury die before arriving at the hospital, and in-hospital mortality is 20% to 57%.4 Death is primarily secondary to exsanguination. Surgery and endovascular methods are described, predominantly including balloon tamponade and stent graft placement.5, 6, 7, 8, 9 There may only be one other report of a spontaneous IVC filter-associated IVC rupture leading to hemodynamic decompensation.10 In that case, the patient presented with atraumatic back pain and was successfully managed conservatively. IVC filter removal had not been performed in our patient, having been lost to follow-up, even as modern techniques allow removal of temporary and permanent filters. IVC filter removal remains an important part of endovascular care, and timely removal has many clinical benefits.

Until recently, there was little availability of mechanical thrombectomy solutions. Such technologies may feature aspiration and rheolytic elements. In emergent situations where revascularization of thrombotic occlusions is paramount, large-bore techniques can provide rapid thrombus removal and recanalization.

Conclusion

Rupture of the IVC secondary to filter-associated IVC occlusion is extremely rare and can be related to formation of hyperacute thrombosis. Large-bore mechanical thrombectomy solutions may provide the most rapid and effective recanalization solution in these cases.

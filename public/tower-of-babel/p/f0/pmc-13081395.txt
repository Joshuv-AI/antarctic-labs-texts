Structured, merit-based selection of radiology chief residents; a single center experience
Authors: Anam Khan, Zainab Hussain, Shehla Rabia, Shaista Afzal
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractChief residents play a pivotal leadership role in postgraduate medical education. However, traditional seniority-based or informal selection practices may inadvertently miss out on essential qualities such as leadership and communication skills, professionalism, and peer trust. Evidence-based, merit-driven selection models in radiology, particularly from low- and middle-income country settings, remain scarce.Residents prioritize leadership, communication, professionalism, and advocacy over academic performance. Additionally, recent research demonstrates low awareness and limited perceived transparency of existing selection practices. Our aim, hence, is to develop and design a transparent, stakeholder-informed, merit-based chief resident selection framework in a radiology residency program.Implementation of a structured and contextually adaptable chief resident selection model can potentially offer a new and transformative approach to leadership selection in postgraduate radiology training, promoting meaningful, competency-based practices aligned with trainee expectations. Keywords: Chief resident selection, Merit-based framework, Medical education leadership, Radiology residency, Postgraduate medical education

Background

Chief residents occupy a critical leadership position within postgraduate medical education, serving as intermediaries between faculty and trainees while coordinating clinical duties, educational activities, and administrative responsibilities. As residency programs grow in complexity, the leadership competencies required for effective chief residency extend beyond seniority or academic performance alone [1].

Traditionally, chief resident selection in many training programs has relied on seniority-based or informal appointment processes. While expedient, such approaches may insufficiently capture leadership capacity, communication skills, professionalism, and peer trust; attributes increasingly recognized as essential for effective trainee leadership [2]. Prior studies across multiple specialties have highlighted variability in chief resident selection practices and raised concerns regarding transparency, bias, and misalignment with leadership competencies [3, 4].

Structured, merit-based selection frameworks incorporating stakeholder feedback and predefined evaluation criteria have been proposed as mechanisms to enhance fairness and accountability. However, published descriptions of such models within radiology training, particularly from low- and middle-income country settings, remain limited. This study addresses this gap by describing the development and implementation of a structured, multi-step chief resident selection framework within a radiology residency program [5].

While structured chief resident selection frameworks have been described in other medical specialties [6], published models within radiology training remain limited, particularly from low- and middle-income country settings. Moreover, existing approaches often rely predominantly on faculty-driven assessments, with limited integration of resident priorities or multi-source stakeholder input [7]. This study addresses this gap by describing a radiology-specific, stakeholder-informed, merit-based chief resident selection framework implemented within a resource-constrained training environment.

The aim of this study was to describe the development and implementation of a structured, merit-based chief resident selection model and to present descriptive data from its initial application.

Methodology

Study design and setting

This was a single-center, descriptive educational innovation conducted in the Department of Radiology at a tertiary care hospital, Karachi, Pakistan. The residency program included 24 residents across all training years.

The framework was intentionally designed to integrate resident-identified leadership priorities with multi-stakeholder evaluation and predefined weighting of leadership competencies, distinguishing it from traditional seniority-based or single-metric selection models.

Step 1: resident voice: gauging awareness and expectations

All residents were invited to complete an anonymous electronic survey assessing awareness of existing chief resident selection criteria, perceptions of transparency, interest in assuming the chief resident role, and prioritization of desired leadership attributes. The survey included multiple-choice items, Likert-scale questions, and open-ended responses. Descriptive statistics were used to summarize quantitative data, while free-text responses were reviewed to identify recurring themes.

Step 2: open call for applications

Eligible residents were formally invited to apply for the chief resident position through an open call. Applicants submitted expressions of interest, ensuring equal opportunity and transparency.

Step 3: 360-degree stakeholder evaluation

All applicants underwent 360-degree evaluation involving residents, allied departmental staff, and faculty members. Each group completed structured assessment forms evaluating leadership skills, communication, professionalism, teaching engagement, and overall suitability. Responses were anonymized and aggregated.

Step 4: structured interviews

Shortlisted candidates participated in structured interviews conducted by the Program Director and Associate Program Director. Interviews focused on leadership vision, conflict resolution, administrative preparedness, and advocacy for residents.

Step 5: final scoring grid and selection

A predefined scoring grid totaling 100 points was used to guide final selection. Domains and weights were as follows:

Academic performance (30 points).

Academic performance (30 points).

Leadership and administrative abilities (30 points).

Clinical and professional competence (20 points).

Educational activities (10 points).

Research contributions (10 points).

Research contributions (10 points).

Leadership and professionalism were weighed more heavily to align with chief resident responsibilities. Final scores were calculated by summing domain-specific scores.

Awareness and transparency

Only 2 residents (10.5%) reported awareness of the criteria used for chief resident selection, while 11 (57.9%) reported no awareness and 6 (31.6%) reported partial awareness. Peer discussion was the most common source of information (52.6%). Perceived transparency was low, with 57.9% identifying as detractors, 31.6% as passives, and 10.5% as promoters.

Prioritized attributes

Residents most frequently identified leadership skills (36.8%), communication skills (31.6%), and faculty recommendation (26.3%) as key factors in chief resident selection. Academic performance was selected by only one respondent (5.3%). Open-ended responses emphasized approachability, advocacy for residents, organizational skills, emotional intelligence, and professionalism.

Interest in chief residency

Seven residents (36.8%) expressed interest in becoming chief resident, while 7 (36.8%) were unsure and 5 (26.3%) were not interested. Hesitations were primarily related to work–life balance (63.2%) and time commitment (52.6%).

Applicant evaluation and final selection

Three final-year residents applied for the position. Aggregated 360-degree evaluations demonstrated variability primarily within leadership and administrative domains. Composite scoring using the predefined grid differentiated candidates based on leadership effectiveness and professional conduct. Two candidates with the highest composite scores were selected to serve sequential six-month chief resident terms. Discussion

This study describes the development and implementation of a structured, merit-based chief resident selection framework within a radiology residency program and presents descriptive findings from its initial application.

The primary contribution of this work lies not in demonstrating the effectiveness of a chief resident selection model, but in detailing a transparent, reproducible, and contextually adaptable framework tailored to radiology training. Unlike previously described approaches that emphasize faculty nomination, seniority, or academic metrics, this model deliberately incorporates resident voice, multi-source stakeholder assessment, and explicit weighting of leadership and professionalism [5]. The implementation within a resource-limited setting further underscores its feasibility in programs with constrained administrative infrastructure.

Baseline resident survey results demonstrated limited awareness and low perceived transparency of the prior selection process, highlighting the need for a more formalized and inclusive approach to leadership selection.

Resident-identified priorities emphasize leadership, communication, professionalism, and advocacy over academic performance alone. These findings are consistent with emerging literature that recognizes interpersonal effectiveness, emotional intelligence, and administrative competence as central to chief resident success. Incorporating resident perspectives into the design of the selection framework ensured alignment between trainee expectations and leadership assessment criteria, thereby enhancing the perceived legitimacy of the process [8, 9].

The multi-source (360-degree) evaluation component allowed for a more holistic appraisal of candidates by integrating perspectives from residents, faculty, and allied staff. Variability in leadership and administrative domain scores contributed most to differentiation among candidates, underscoring the value of evaluating competencies that may not be captured by traditional academic metrics. While inter-rater reliability was not formally assessed, the use of predefined domains and aggregated scoring helped mitigate individual evaluator bias [10].

The structured scoring grid provided transparency and reproducibility by clearly defining evaluation domains and weightings. Deliberate prioritization of leadership and professionalism over research output reflected the functional responsibilities of the chief resident role within the department. The decision to appoint two candidates for sequential six-month terms was guided by closely comparable composite scores and departmental workload considerations, offering shared leadership experience while maintaining continuity.

Unlike seniority-based selection models, this framework integrates resident input, structured evaluation, and predefined scoring criteria. While similar principles have been described in other specialties [11, 12], this study contributes a contextualized implementation within radiology training in a resource-limited setting. Importantly, the purpose of this work was descriptive; effectiveness outcomes such as chief resident performance, resident satisfaction, or program efficiency were not evaluated and warrant future study.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. It was conducted at a single institution with a small sample size, limiting generalizability. The design was descriptive and did not assess longitudinal outcomes such as chief resident performance, resident satisfaction, or program functioning. Stakeholder evaluations may have been influenced by response bias, and inter-rater reliability was not formally assessed. Comparative data with the prior seniority-based model was unavailable. Future multi-center studies incorporating outcome measures and longitudinal evaluation are warranted.

Conclusion

This study presents a transparent, structured approach to chief resident selection informed by stakeholder input and predefined evaluation criteria. While effectiveness outcomes were not evaluated, the framework offers a feasible reference model for programs seeking to standardize leadership selection. Further evaluation is required to determine its impact on leadership development and trainee experience.

Orbital angular momentum-based spatial encryption using polarization-sensitive metasurfaces
Authors: Ali Mohammad Obaei, Mohammad Javad Hajiahmadi, Mohammad Soleimani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSecure data transmission requires robust encryption strategies to prevent unauthorized access. Although software-based encryption has been extensively explored, advances in high-speed computing and the development of quantum systems increasingly challenge the security of conventional schemes. Hardware-based encryption offers an alternative approach by exploiting physical properties of light and engineered structures. In this work, we present a metasurface-based hardware encryption system that simultaneously leverages polarization encoding and orbital angular momentum (OAM) wavefront encoding. The system operates across three distinct channels. The first channel, with input and output polarization , generates OAM superpositions at the focal point. The second channel, with input and output polarization , produces superpositions . The third channel, corresponding to orthogonal input and output polarizations ( to or to ), produces OAM superpositions . By introducing a slant polarization at a specific angle at the input, tailored weightings of the superposition modes are achieved for each output polarization, enabling precise control over the OAM distribution at the focal point. Finally, the system is integrated with the double random phase encryption (DRPE) method, forming a hybrid optical encryption strategy that combines spatial, polarization, and OAM degrees of freedom. Three communication scenarios for secure image and video transmission via encryption and decryption are investigated. This approach provides enhanced security for optical data transmission, demonstrating the potential of metasurface-based devices for dynamic, high-capacity, and secure hardware encryption applications.Keywords: Orbital angular momentum, Encryption, Metasurface, PolarizationSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Optics and photonics, Physics

Introduction

As terahertz (THz) wireless communication continues to evolve, enabling ultra-high-speed and high-capacity data transmission, the need for advanced data security mechanisms becomes increasingly critical. Traditional encryption methods, predominantly software-based, are now under threat due to the increasing computational power of modern systems and the potential of quantum computing to break conventional cryptographic schemes1. In response, hardware-based encryption approaches have emerged as promising alternatives by embedding information security directly into the physical layer of transmission systems2,3. These methods leverage the intrinsic properties of electromagnetic waves–such as polarization, phase, amplitude, and wavefront structure–for secure data encoding. Among them, metasurfaces have become an attractive platform for realizing compact, flexible, and multi-functional encryption devices4.

Polarization-based encryption using metasurfaces has been particularly well-explored in recent years5,6. Birefringent and vectorial metasurfaces allow for polarization-selective image reconstruction and asymmetric information channels. In such systems, different images or phase profiles can be recovered depending on the input polarization state7,8. Dynamic polarization manipulation has also been demonstrated using reconfigurable metasurfaces with materials such as graphene, enabling real-time secure communication through multi-channel control9. Other approaches incorporate machine learning and disordered metasurfaces to construct robust, high-security platforms with physical keys (e.g., QR codes), which combine polarization encoding with AI-assisted design to resist unauthorized access10.

Another highly promising degree of freedom for information encoding is Orbital Angular Momentum (OAM). OAM beams possess helical phase fronts characterized by topological charge , allowing each mode to carry an independent channel of information11. Unlike polarization, which has only two orthogonal states, OAM provides a theoretically infinite set of orthogonal modes, making it ideal for high-dimensional encryption schemes. The generation of OAM beams has been achieved through two primary methods. In antenna-based systems, circular arrays and slotted waveguide designs have been used to produce reconfigurable or multi-mode OAM beams, particularly in the microwave and THz regimes12,13. In contrast, metasurface-based generation offers planar, compact designs capable of tailoring phase profiles with subwavelength precision14. Holographic impedance metasurfaces using transparent materials such as ITO/PET have demonstrated broadband and multi-mode OAM generation, while hybrid configurations incorporating materials like graphene or VO2 enable dynamic tunability and dual control of the electric field distribution15,16.

Beyond generation, metasurfaces have also been utilized for OAM-based encryption and multiplexing. Complex amplitude metasurfaces have been designed to simultaneously control OAM and wavelength, enabling the concealment of multiple images within a single structure17,18. Other works have introduced vector vortex beam generation and Poincaré sphere-based encoding to embed polarization and OAM simultaneously into encrypted holographic patterns19,20. Some designs integrate polarization21, frequency, and OAM into a single metasurface, allowing for high-density, multi-dimensional information storage and retrieval22. These techniques highlight the versatility of OAM as a secure and multiplexed communication channel, but their practical implementation remains limited by several challenges, including the vulnerability to atmospheric turbulence, mode purity degradation, alignment sensitivity, and difficulties in efficient generation and detection at THz frequencies23.

In this work, we propose a hybrid encryption framework based on a static metasurface capable of encoding information simultaneously in polarization and OAM wavefronts. The metasurface is designed to produce specific OAM mode superpositions at a fixed focal point depending on the input and output polarization combinations. Three channels are engineered: co-polarized , co-polarized , and cross-polarized or . By controlling the angle of slant input polarization, we create specific amplitude weightings between these OAM states, enabling the superposition of four distinct modes in each output polarization without requiring active or nonlinear materials. This approach achieves dynamic functionality using a purely static design. To further enhance encryption strength, we integrate this metasurface with the Double Random Phase Encryption (DRPE) algorithm24, resulting in a hybrid encryption scheme that combines the resilience of software encryption with the physical security of structured wavefront encoding.

Polarization-dependent metasurface design principles

The design of the proposed metasurface is based on full-wave numerical simulations, with the goal of achieving precise control over both phase and amplitude responses under orthogonal linear polarizations illumination. The primary objective is to develop a library of subwavelength-scale unit cells that can induce polarization-dependent phase shifts. When these cells are spatially arranged across the metasurface, they collaboratively generate the desired orbital angular momentum (OAM) wavefronts. The metasurface operates in transmission mode and exhibits distinct responses to x- and y-polarized incident waves. Owing to its linear and passive nature, the structure adheres to the reciprocity theorem, implying that its electromagnetic response remains invariant under the interchange of source and observation points. Consequently, for cross-polarization scenarios (e.g., x–y and y–x), identical OAM mode superpositions are obtained, enabling bidirectional operation without active components.

Figure 1 schematically illustrates the concept of the proposed metasurface-assisted spatial encryption mechanism, which relies on polarization-state-dependent generation of OAM channels. The key mechanism enabling dynamic functionality within this static metasurface lies in the manipulation of the input polarization state. A slant-polarized wave incident at an angle relative to the principal axes can be decomposed into weighted x and y components, determined by the cosine and sine of the angle, respectively. As each polarization channel excites a distinct OAM mode pair, the input polarization effectively dictates the relative amplitude contribution of multiple OAM modes at the output. To this end, the metasurface is designed such that each orthogonal polarization at the output focal point corresponds to a superposition of two OAM states with opposite topological charges. Specifically, the x-polarized output supports the combination of and modes, while the y-polarized output exhibits and . Under slant polarization excitation, both channels are simultaneously active, and the resulting field at the focal point exhibits four superimposed OAM components with controllable amplitude weighting.

Metasurface design procedure and results

Meta-atom design and simulation results

The primary design requirement for these structures is the inclusion of unit cells capable of providing a complete phase coverage from 0 to . Moreover, with suitable amplitude values, these unit cells can interact effectively based on their respective phase responses, enabling the desired OAM wave superposition to be generated across the full structure.

Given the complexity of our application, we aimed to simplify the metasurface design by employing supercells instead of individual unit cells. To achieve this, four unit cells were combined to form each supercell. These supercells operate in transmission mode, rely on their constituent unit cells, and are fabricated monolithically from lossy silicon. The ideal amplitude for both categories of unit cells is set to 0.7, ensuring that half of the input amplitude is preserved in the same polarization with the desired phase shift, while the remaining half is converted into the orthogonal polarization with a different desired phase. The elliptical nanopillars are dedicated solely to phase manipulation within the same polarization. In contrast, the rectangular nanopillars simultaneously perform phase modification and conversion to the orthogonal polarization. These rectangular nanopillars are oriented at either 45 or 135 to achieve a polarization conversion efficiency close to unity. Additionally, the unit cells are designed such that, for example, the orthogonal component generated at the output of the elliptical nanopillar is sufficiently weak to prevent unwanted coupling into the amplitude of the rectangular nanopillar in its orthogonal polarization. Conversely, similar efforts have been made to reduce this coupling effect from the rectangular nanopillar toward the elliptical nanopillar.

Encryption framework

To ensure secure data transmission in the THz regime, a hybrid encryption approach combining software and hardware methods is employed. While software encryption, such as the Double Random Phase Encryption (DRPE) algorithm24, provides strong obfuscation, its security can be threatened by advances in computational power and quantum computing. Hardware encryption using metasurfaces complements this by embedding information directly into the physical layer through polarization and OAM wavefront encoding. In our design, the metasurface generates specific OAM mode superpositions at a fixed focal point depending on the input and output polarization combinations: co-polarized , co-polarized , and cross-polarized or . By controlling the input slant polarization angle, specific amplitude weightings among these OAM modes are achieved, producing four distinct superposition states per output polarization without requiring active or nonlinear materials as illustrated in Fig. 1. Integrating this metasurface with the DRPE algorithm enables a hybrid encryption scheme that leverages the resilience of software encryption together with the physical security and high-dimensional encoding capabilities of structured wavefronts.

By engineering the input slant polarization at various angles, four distinct OAM wavefront modes with specified weightings can be observed at the output focal point. The hardware metasurface is positioned immediately after the software encryption stage, enabling the transmission of two- or three-bit images. In the following subsections, we detail three scenarios illustrating two-bit image transmission, three-bit color image transmission, and three-bit color video transmission.

Scenario 1: transmission of two two-bit images

In the first scenario, we demonstrate the combined hardware-software approach for transmitting two-bit data. Leveraging the structure’s capability, we simultaneously transmit two images. The DRPE algorithm first converts the images into indistinguishable forms. Using the metasurface, we then alternate groups of 8 bits between the two images. Each two-bit segment represents a grayscale value from black to white, requiring simultaneous transmission of two bits per image.

For each image, an angular indicator is defined. For instance, as shown in Fig. 6, the first 8 bits of the DRPE-encrypted tiger image are divided into four two-bit segments. The slant polarization is generated using a two-port horn antenna27, with each port producing orthogonal x or y polarization. By controlling the amplitude ratio of the two ports, the desired slant polarization angle is achieved, producing specific combinations of OAM modes and at the focal point. Discussion

To further demonstrate the practical feasibility of the proposed polarization-selective interleaved metasurface, we provide fabrication details and system-level implementation considerations. Although the device performance has been validated through full-wave simulations, its geometry is fully compatible with standard microfabrication processes commonly employed for terahertz dielectric metasurfaces. The fabrication procedure and the envisioned encryption system configuration are described as follows. Also, a comparison with other works is presented in Table??.

Metasurface fabrication

The metasurface was fabricated on a high-resistivity silicon substrate (thickness: 250 m; lateral size: ) using ultraviolet photolithography followed by inductively coupled plasma (ICP) deep reactive ion etching. High-resistivity silicon was selected to minimize absorption losses at terahertz frequencies and to ensure high transmission efficiency.

Prior to lithography, the wafer was cleaned using standard solvent procedures to remove surface contaminants. A positive photoresist (AZ4620) was spin-coated onto the substrate to achieve a uniform thickness of approximately 6.8 m, followed by a soft-bake process to stabilize the film. The designed photomask was carefully aligned with the coated wafer and exposed under ultraviolet illumination. After development, the transferred photoresist pattern served as an etching mask for the subsequent ICP process, which defined the subwavelength silicon structures with high anisotropy and nearly vertical sidewalls.

Following the etching step, the residual photoresist was removed using acetone. The sample was then rinsed with ethanol and deionized water and dried under ambient conditions. The minimum feature size and aspect ratio of the metasurface elements fall within the capability of standard ICP fabrication processes, ensuring dimensional accuracy and structural repeatability. Importantly, the interleaved supercell configuration does not introduce additional fabrication complexity, as both polarization-selective unit cells are patterned simultaneously within a single lithographic step.

System-level encryption architecture

The envisioned encryption architecture operates in a transmission configuration. A dual-port horn antenna serves as the terahertz radiation source, generating linearly polarized plane waves in the far-field region. The polarization state encodes the transmitted digital bit.

The metasurface is positioned approximately 18 cm from the antenna aperture to ensure quasi-plane-wave illumination. Upon interaction with the metasurface, the incident polarization state is converted into a structured wavefront corresponding to the designed orbital angular momentum (OAM)-based encrypted field distribution.

A digital hologram is placed approximately 100 m from the metasurface output plane, operating in the near-field regime to capture and redistribute the modulated electromagnetic field into the communication channel. During the decryption process, the hologram reconstructs the appropriate illumination pattern, which subsequently interacts with the metasurface. Depending on the reconstructed wavefront, a plane wave with the corresponding polarization state is formed at the metasurface output.

At the receiver side, a dual-port horn antenna detects the transmitted signal. The decoded digital bit is determined by comparing the relative signal amplitudes at the two antenna ports, where the port exhibiting the higher intensity corresponds to the recovered logical state.

These results indicate that the proposed metasurface-based encryption framework is compatible with conventional terahertz components and standard microfabrication techniques, supporting its feasibility for practical short-range secure communication applications.

Conclusion

In this work, we developed a hybrid encryption framework combining software and hardware methods for secure THz data transmission. The hardware layer is realized using a carefully engineered metasurface composed of supercells, each providing full phase coverage and designed to generate arbitrary OAM wavefronts. By appropriately configuring the supercells, the metasurface is capable of producing the desired OAM modes at a fixed focal point, enabling precise spatial encoding.

The OAM encoding mechanism employs three distinct channels: co-polarized x outputs generate superposition modes , co-polarized y outputs generate , and cross-polarization outputs generate . By controlling the input slant polarization angle and corresponding amplitude weightings, four distinct OAM superposition states are produced in each output polarization, providing high-dimensional and separable amplitude and phase profiles for encryption.

For software encryption, the Double Random Phase Encryption (DRPE) algorithm was applied to transform images into incoherent forms. This hybrid approach was demonstrated through three scenarios: (i) transmission of two two-bit grayscale images, (ii) transmission of a three-bit color image, and (iii) transmission of a three-bit color video. In all cases, the metasurface successfully encoded information in polarization and OAM modes, while the DRPE algorithm provided an additional layer of obfuscation, ensuring secure transmission of both high-quality images and large data volumes.

Overall, this study demonstrates the feasibility of combining metasurface-based OAM encoding with DRPE software encryption to implement a robust, high-dimensional hybrid encryption system. The approach highlights the potential for further extending encryption strength by incorporating additional OAM modes, polarization channels, or more complex hardware-software integration, while maintaining practical design through the use of supercells and full phase coverage.

Prophylactic ureteral stent placement for the prevention of urinary tract injuries in women with placenta accreta spectrum undergoing peripartum cesarean hysterectomy: A retrospective cohort study
Authors: Hasan Berkan Sayal, And Yavuz, Merve Turk, Busra Tsakir, Erzat Toprak, Zeynep Ozturk Inal
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThis retrospective cohort study investigated whether preoperative bilateral ureteral stent placement reduces urinary system injuries in pregnant women with placenta accreta spectrum (PAS) undergoing peripartum cesarean hysterectomy.Material and MethodsThe sociodemographic, clinical characteristics, and laboratory results of 273 women who underwent peripartum cesarean hysterectomy due to suspected PAS during the study period were documented and compared. One hundred and twenty‐three of these did not receive preoperative urinary stent placement (Group 1), while 150 had such stents installed (Group 2).ResultsRed blood cell counts (4.10 ± 1.78 vs. 3.06 ± 0.96, odds ratio (OR): 0.782, 95% confidence interval (CI): 0.619–0.989; p = 0.027) and fresh frozen plasma replacement values (2.22 ± 0.63 vs. 3.22 ± 1.01, OR: 1.238, CI: 1.009–1.518; p = 0.035), operative times (120.0 [100.0–165.0] mins vs. 110.0 [80.0–120.0] mins , OR: 0.984, CI: 0.972–0.997; p = 0.012), days of hospitalization (6.34 ± 1.98 vs. 4.68 ± 1.45, OR: 0.849, CI: 0.736–0.979; p = 0.017), and urinary tract complications (14 [11.4%] vs. 5 [3.3%], OR: 8.357, CI: 1.784–39.153; p = 0.007) differed significantly between the groups. Additionally, while preoperative hemoglobin, hematocrit, and platelet counts were comparable between the groups (p > 0.05), postoperative hemoglobin (9.61 ± 1.75 vs. 10.08 ± 1.81 gm/dl; p = 0.004) and hematocrit (28.88 ± 1.80 vs. 30.19 ± 2.30; p = 0.003) levels differed between them. In multivariable logistic regression analysis, both the number of previous cesarean sections (OR: 1.687; 95% CI: 1.079–2.636; p = 0.022) and the absence of ureteral stent placement (OR: 3.964; 95% CI: 1.199–13.107; p = 0.024) were independently associated with urinary tract injury.ConclusionsThis retrospective cohort study suggests that prophylactic temporary ureteral stent placement may be associated with a reduction in urinary tract injury in women with PAS undergoing peripartum cesarean hysterectomy. However, given the non‐randomized design, potential selection bias, and limited number of events, these findings should be interpreted with caution. Larger, multicenter prospective studies with appropriate adjustment for confounding variables are needed to confirm these results.Keywords: peripartum cesarean hysterectomy, placenta accreta spectrum, pregnancy, ureteral stent, urinary tract injury

Key message

Prophylactic ureteral stent placement was associated with a reduced risk of urinary tract injury in women with placenta accreta spectrum undergoing peripartum cesarean hysterectomy, independent of prior cesarean number; however, findings should be interpreted cautiously due to the retrospective design and potential confounding.

INTRODUCTION

The prevalence of placenta accreta spectrum (PAS), defined as the abnormality in the implantation of placental trophoblasts to the uterine myometrium, has risen to approximately 0.3% due to increasing rates of cesarean deliveries, especially in the last 20 years. 1 , 2 , 3 PAS can invade the uterine myometrium and extend to other surrounding structures and organs, primarily the bladder. While ureteral implantation is rare, ureteral injury can occur during the hysterectomy. 4 PAS currently represents a significant cause of maternal morbidity and mortality due to maternal hemorrhage and massive blood transfusions. 5

The lower uterine segment enlarges and remodels due to the presence of a low‐lying placenta or placenta previa in most pregnant women with PAS. The altered pelvic anatomy, vascular anastomoses between the bladder and uterus, invasion of the placenta into the bladder and/or parametrium, and pelvic adhesions can cause damage to the ureter and bladder during peripartum cesarean hysterectomy. 6 , 7 Since the majority of PAS cases are treated with technically complex peripartum cesarean hysterectomy, perioperative urinary system injuries can ensue (cystotomy in 18–76% of surgeries, ureteral injury in 0–18%, and genitourinary fistula in 0–5%), which can increase maternal morbidity. 8 , 9 The placement of bilateral ureteral stents preoperatively has been recommended for the prevention of urinary tract injuries. 1 , 5 Preoperative ureteral stent placement is not routinely recommended by the International PAS Society. 4 , 5 However, it has recently been suggested that preoperative bilateral ureteral stent placement significantly reduces the risk of urinary system injury in PAS cases undergoing peripartum cesarean hysterectomy. 1 , 6 In order to address this complexity in the literature, the present study was performed to investigate whether preoperative bilateral ureteral stent placement reduces urinary system injury in pregnant women with PAS undergoing peripartum cesarean hysterectomy.

MATERIAL AND METHODS

This retrospective comparative study was conducted at the Antalya Training and Research Hospital Perinatology Department, Turkey, between January 2015 and December 2024. Ethics committee approval was obtained on 23 May 2024 under decision number 2023‐239:12/20. Data from all pregnant women with PAS who underwent histopathologically confirmed peripartum cesarean hysterectomy were retrieved retrospectively from the hospital automation system and database. The study was carried out in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, and no conflict of interest was declared by the authors. Written informed consent to the future use of their data was obtained from the participants prior to hospitalization.

While the participants in the group without ureteral stents consisted of pregnant women without invasion findings on ultrasound and those who were referred to our clinic, a tertiary center, for emergency cesarean sections, ureteral stents were placed in pregnant women with invasion findings on ultrasonography. Bilateral ureteral stents were placed during cystoscopy at 33–34 weeks of gestation for the prophylactic ureteral stent placement group and IV cefazolin 2 g was used for antibiotic therapy. Bilateral ureteral stents were removed by cystoscopy approximately 4 weeks after the operation. The temporal distribution of cases was not formally adjusted for in the analysis, representing a potential source of bias. The composition of the surgical team, including the presence of a urologist or gynecologic oncologist, was not systematically recorded and could not be adjusted for. This represents a potential unmeasured confounder, as multidisciplinary team involvement may independently influence urinary tract injury rates.

The inclusion criterion for the study was a histopathologically confirmed diagnosis of PAS, while the exclusion criteria were total hysterectomy performed for indications other than PAS and incomplete data. In the preoperative period, the following findings on obstetric ultrasound were considered to favor a diagnosis of PAS: the absence of the hypoechoic zone between the placenta and myometrium; the presence of irregularly enlarged lacunae with a hyperdynamic flow within the placenta; turbulent, irregular flow in the retroplacental location at Doppler ultrasound; protrusion (bulging) of the placenta into the bladder; decreased myometrial thickness; loss of the echo lucent line between the uterus and bladder; and vessels running perpendicularly from the placenta to the myometrium.

Two hundred and seventy‐three participants underwent surgical procedures due to peripartum cesarean hysterectomy during the study period. Data from 273 peripartum cesarean hysterectomy cases resulting from PAS, including maternal age, body mass index (BMI), gravidity, parity, number of living children, number of curettages, previous C‐sections, gestational age at delivery, blood transfusion requirement, operation time, length of hospitalization, peri‐ and postoperative complications, histopathological findings, and pre‐ and postoperative laboratory outcomes were recorded and compared. Urinary system injury was defined as the presence of cystotomy, ureteral injury, or bladder fistula. The depth of placental invasion was categorized according to the pathology reports as accreta [International Federation of Gynecology and Obstetrics (FIGO) 1, PAS1], increta [FIGO2, PAS2], or percreta [FIGO3, PAS3]. Allocation to the stent and no‐stent groups was not randomized and was based on clinical decision‐making. Specifically, prophylactic ureteral stents were preferentially placed in patients with preoperative ultrasonographic findings suggestive of placental invasion, while patients without such findings, including those undergoing emergency cesarean hysterectomy, were more frequently managed without stent placement. This approach reflects real‐world clinical practice; however, it introduces potential selection bias and confounding by indication.

The primary outcome of this study was the frequency of urinary system injury (bladder, ureteral injury, or bladder + ureteral injury), while secondary outcomes were operative time, numbers of blood and blood product transfusions, and length of hospitalization.

Statistical analysis

Statistical Package for Social Sciences (SPSS) (version 27.0, SPSS, Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) software was used for the statistical analysis. The Kolmogorov–Smirnov test was performed to evaluate the normality of distribution of continuous variables. The t‐test was applied for the analysis of data exhibiting normal distribution, and the Mann–Whitney U‐test for non‐normally distributed data. The chi‐square and the Fisher exact tests were used to compare categorical parameters. Continuous variables were expressed as mean + standard deviation (SD) and categorical variables as numbers of cases and percentage values. Logistic regression analysis was used for the independency parameters for urinary tract injuries. The results were presented as odds ratio (OR) and 95% confidence interval (CI). A p‐value of <0.05 was regarded as statistically significant. This study was conducted and reported in accordance with the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines for observational studies.

No formal sample size or power calculation was performed due to the retrospective nature of the study. Given the relatively small number of urinary tract injury events (n = 19), the study may be underpowered to detect moderate differences in secondary outcomes.

DISCUSSION

This study investigated whether preoperative bilateral ureteral stent placement reduces the risk of urinary tract injury in women with PAS undergoing peripartum cesarean hysterectomy. In the present study, urinary tract injury was observed less frequently in the stent group compared to the no‐stent group. However, this association should be interpreted cautiously given the retrospective design and potential for residual confounding and selection bias.

Urinary tract injury remains a well‐recognized complication in PAS surgery and is closely associated with increased maternal morbidity, particularly in the presence of extensive placental invasion and distorted pelvic anatomy. 4 , 10 , 11 Prenatal diagnosis and appropriate surgical planning are essential to reduce perioperative complications. 9 , 12 , 13 , 14 , 15 In our study, no significant difference was observed between the groups in terms of parametrial invasion or urgent/emergent delivery.

The literature regarding prophylactic ureteral stent placement in PAS remains conflicting. Several studies have reported no significant reduction in urinary tract injuries with stent placement, 1 , 4 , 9 , 16 while others have demonstrated a potential protective effect. 5 , 17 A recent meta‐analysis including 848 patients found no significant difference in urinary injury rates between stented and non‐stented groups. 1 In contrast, some retrospective studies and systematic reviews have suggested that prophylactic stents may reduce urinary tract injuries, particularly in high‐risk cases. 5 , 17 Our findings are consistent with studies suggesting a potential benefit; however, causality cannot be established.

A possible explanation for this association is that ureteral stents may facilitate intraoperative identification of the ureters and improve surgical orientation in cases with distorted pelvic anatomy. This may be particularly relevant in patients with advanced placental invasion or extensive adhesions. Notably, in our cohort, patients with unsuccessful stent placement had a markedly higher rate of urinary tract injury, highlighting the importance of successful stent insertion.

An additional finding was the higher fresh frozen plasma (FFP) transfusion requirement in the stent group despite lower red blood cell transfusion. This discrepancy may reflect temporal changes in transfusion protocols, evolving surgical practices, or institutional differences in perioperative management rather than a direct effect of stent placement. This observation should therefore be interpreted with caution.

PAS remains a complex condition associated with significant surgical challenges and morbidity. 4 , 11 Multidisciplinary management and referral to specialized tertiary centers are essential to optimize outcomes. Although prophylactic ureteral stents may assist in selected cases, their routine use remains controversial.

This study has several strengths, including histopathological confirmation of PAS and a relatively large cohort compared to previous studies. However, important limitations must be acknowledged. Although our findings suggest a potential protective association between prophylactic ureteral stent placement and reduced urinary tract injury, this relationship must be interpreted with caution due to the inherent structure of the study design. In particular, group allocation was clinically determined rather than randomized, and patients undergoing emergency procedures—who are intrinsically at higher surgical risk—were more frequently represented in the no‐stent group. This introduces a substantial risk of confounding by indication that cannot be fully eliminated in a retrospective framework. Furthermore, the study period spans nearly a decade (2015–2024), during which surgical expertise, perioperative management strategies, and multidisciplinary team involvement likely evolved. Such temporal improvements may have coincided with increased use of ureteral stents, potentially biasing the observed association in favor of the stent group. In addition, unmeasured factors such as surgeon experience, intraoperative decision‐making, and the presence of urology support may have further influenced outcomes. Therefore, the observed reduction in urinary tract injury should be interpreted as an association rather than a causal effect, and future prospective studies incorporating temporal adjustment and stratified analyses (e.g., planned vs. emergency cases) are required to more definitively clarify the role of prophylactic ureteral stent placement in PAS surgery. The retrospective design limits control over confounding variables. Group allocation was not randomized and was influenced by preoperative clinical findings, introducing selection bias and confounding by indication. The relatively small number of urinary tract injury events may also limit statistical power. In addition, unmeasured factors such as surgeon experience, multidisciplinary team involvement, and temporal changes in surgical practice could not be fully accounted for. Stratified analyses based on planned versus emergency procedures could not be performed due to data limitations, which may have further contributed to residual confounding. Furthermore, the relatively small number of urinary tract injury events (n = 19) in relation to the number of variables entered into the multivariable model may have increased the risk of model instability and overfitting. Therefore, the identified independent associations should be interpreted cautiously and considered hypothesis‐generating rather than definitive.

CONCLUSION

Prophylactic ureteral stent placement may be associated with reduced urinary tract injury in women with PAS undergoing peripartum cesarean hysterectomy. However, given the nonrandomized design, potential selection bias, and limited number of outcome events, these findings should be interpreted with caution. Further prospective, multicenter studies with appropriate adjustment for confounding variables are needed to clarify the role of ureteral stent placement in PAS surgery.

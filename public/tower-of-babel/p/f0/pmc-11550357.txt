LGR6 protects against myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury via suppressing necroptosis
Authors: Mengmeng Zhao, Zihui Zheng, Jianfang Liu, Yao Xu, Jishou Zhang, Shanshan Peng, Juan-Juan Qin, Jun Wan, Menglong Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Regulated necrosis (necroptosis) and apoptosis are important biological features of ischemia-reperfusion (I/R) injury. However, the molecular mechanisms underlying myocardial necroptosis remain elusive. Leucine rich repeat containing G protein-coupled receptor 6 (LGR6) has been reported to play important roles in various cardiovascular disease. In this study, we aimed to determine whether LGR6 suppresses I/R-induced myocardial necroptosis and the underlying molecular mechanisms. We generated LGR6 knockout mice and used ligation of left anterior descending coronary artery to produce an in vivo I/R model. The effects of LGR6 and its downstream molecules were subsequently identified using RNA sequencing and CHIP assays. We observed significantly downregulated LGR6 expression in hearts post myocardial I/R and cardiomyocytes post hypoxia and reoxygenation (HR). LGR6 deficiency promoted and LGR6 overexpression inhibited necroptosis and acute myocardial injury after I/R. Mechanistically, in vivo and in vitro experiments suggest that LGR6 regulates the expression of STAT2 and ZBP1 by activating the Wnt signaling pathway, thereby inhibiting cardiomyocyte necroptosis after HR. Inhibiting STAT2 and ZBP1 effectively alleviated the aggravating effect of LGR6 deficiency on myocardial necroptosis after I/R. Furthermore, activating LGR6 with RSPO3 also effectively protected mice from acute myocardial I/R injury. Our findings reveal that RSPO3-LGR6 axis downregulates the expression of STAT2 and ZBP1 through the Wnt signaling pathway, thereby inhibiting I/R-induced myocardial injury and necroptosis. Targeting the RSPO3-LGR6 axis may be a potential therapeutic strategy to treat myocardial I/R injury.

Introduction

Ischemic heart disease is a leading cause of mortality worldwide, with significant health and economic burden. The optimal therapeutic strategy to minimize myocardial infarct size and preserve cardiac function is the restoration of blood flow post-ischemia. However, this reperfusion can paradoxically induce myocardial damage, known as myocardial ischemia/reperfusion (I/R) injury [1]. This injury instigates necrotic and apoptotic cell death, impairing cardiac contractility and electrophysiological function [1]. Unfortunately, interventions targeting necrosis or apoptosis have not yielded clinical efficacy against reperfusion injury. Therefore, it is imperative to elucidate the cellular and molecular mechanisms preceding I/R-induced apoptosis and necrosis and identify novel therapeutic strategies to inhibit early pathological alterations during reperfusion.

Leucine rich repeat containing G protein-coupled receptor 6 (LGR6), along with LGR4 and LGR5, belongs to the GPCR receptor superfamily and LGR subfamily B. It exhibits substantial homology with glycoprotein hormone receptors, such as luteinizing hormone receptor and thyroid stimulating hormone receptor. LGR4, LGR5, and LGR6 play pivotal roles in embryonic development and have been implicated in various cancers [2]. Recently, there has been growing interest in the involvement of the LGR family in cardiovascular diseases. The loss of LGR4 in macrophages has been shown to exacerbate inflammation and contribute to cardiac dysfunction following myocardial infarction [3]. Additionally, LGR6 has been reported to play a significant role in the onset and progression of several cardiovascular diseases, including pulmonary hypertension, abdominal aortic aneurysm, and ischemia-reperfusion injury post-lung transplantation [[4], [5], [6]]. Despite these findings, the specific contribution of LGR6 to the regulation of myocardial ischemia-reperfusion (I/R) injury progression remains to be elucidated.

Necroptosis, a unique form of cell death characterized by cell membrane rupture and subsequent leakage of cellular contents, incites an inflammatory response [7]. Key regulatory molecules of necroptosis, including receptor-interacting protein kinase 1 (RIPK1), RIPK3, and mixed-lineage kinase domain-like protein (MLKL), have been identified [7]. These molecules form complexes to activate MLKL, leading to its translocation to the cell membrane, causing membrane rupture and cellular content leakage [8]. Necroptosis is implicated in numerous diseases, including ischemic heart disease, liver disease, and neurodegenerative diseases [9]. In myocardial I/R injury, necroptosis contributes to substantial cardiomyocyte loss and triggers inflammatory and fibrotic responses, exacerbating cardiac function damage [10]. Studies have demonstrated that the RIPK1 inhibitor necrostatin-1 significantly reduces myocardial infarction size in mice [10]. RIPK3 knockout mice exhibited improved cardiac systolic function and suppressed cardiac hypertrophy post-permanent coronary artery occlusion [11,12]. These findings suggest the involvement of necroptosis in ischemic cardiac injury and cardiac remodeling. Therefore, understanding the mechanism of necroptosis is crucial for developing new strategies to treat myocardial injury post-I/R, underscoring the complex role of necroptosis in cardiac ischemia-reperfusion injury.

Z-DNA binding protein 1 (ZBP1) is a potent innate immune sensor identified as a crucial initiator of innate immune responses and necroptosis [13]. The initiation of ZBP1 signal transduction necessitates the recognition of left-handed double-helix Z nucleic acids (including Z-DNA and Z-RNA), with subsequent signal transduction relying on the interaction between ZBP1 and its adapter proteins, such as TANK-binding kinase 1 (TBK1), interferon regulatory factor 3 (IRF3), receptor interacting serine/threonine protein kinase 1 (RIPK1), and RIPK3 [14]. ZBP1 deficiency was reported to confer protection against doxorubicin-induced cardiotoxicity in mice [15]. Conversely, another study suggested that ZBP1 knockout exacerbated left ventricular dilation and dysfunction post-myocardial infarction in mice [16]. However, the role of ZBP1 in myocardial I/R injury remains to be determined.

In this investigation, we discovered that the overexpression of LGR6 in cardiomyocytes mitigated necroptosis following myocardial I/R in mice by suppressing STAT2/ZBP1 signaling. This led to a reduction in myocardial infarct size, cardiac dysfunction, and cardiac remodeling post-myocardial I/R. Additionally, we identified STAT2 as a transcription factor that enhances ZBP1 expression. We further confirmed that LGR6 inhibits STAT2 expression via regulating Wnt signaling. Collectively, our findings underscore the pivotal role of LGR6 in regulating cardiomyocyte survival post-myocardial I/R injury.

Results

LGR6 expression is markedly downregulated during myocardial I/R injury

To elucidate the transcriptional profile of the LGR family during myocardial I/R injury, we analyzed the gene expression profiles of left ventricle tissues from sham mice and mice with myocardial I/R injury for 7 days (Fig. 1A). We found that the expression of LGR2, LGR4 and LGR6 was significantly down-regulated in I/R mice, with LGR6 being the most obviously down-regulated (Fig. 1A). We then detected the mRNA expression of LGR6 in the left ventricle of I/R injury mice at 1, 7, and 21 days after I/R and observed a significantly downregulated LGR6 expression (Fig. 1B). LGR6 was significantly enriched in non-infarcted areas and significantly reduced in the border area and infarct area as shown by western blots results (Fig. 1C). Immunofluorescence results further demonstrated that LGR6 expression in cardiomyocytes was significantly downregulated in IR mice (Fig. 1D). We next investigated LGR6 expression in isolated adult mouse cardiomyocytes under sham or I/R conditions (Fig. 1E). Expectedly, LGR6 mRNA levels were markedly decreased in cardiomyocytes of mice with I/R injury. Hypoxia and reoxygenation (HR) injury is considered a classic method to stimulate the pathological process of ischemia-reperfusion in vitro. We constructed an HR model in HL1 cells to detect the expression level of LGR6. Consistent with our findings in I/R mice, LGR6 expression was significantly reduced in HR-treated HL1 compared with sham-treated HL1 (Fig. 1F–H). We also observed a significantly downregulated LGR6 expression in the left ventricle of I/R injury rats at 1 day after I/R and HR-treated H9C2 rat cardiomyocytes (Fig. 1I–K). In addition, we also examined the LGR6 expression in cardiac tissues of ischemic cardiomyopathy (ICM) and nonfailing left ventricular samples and observed downregulated LGR6 expression in ICM hearts (Fig. 1L and M). LGR6 expression in AC16 human cardiomyocytes was also reduced by HR treatment (Fig. 1N). LGR6 knockout accelerates cardiomyocyte necroptosis and acute myocardial injury post I/R

To investigated the effects of LGR6 deficiency in myocardial I/R injury, we compared the severity of acute myocardial injury between LGR6 knockout (L6KO) and wild-type (WT) mice (Figs. S1A–C). Evans blue/TTC double staining showed that LGR6 knockout substantially enlarged infarct area following reperfusion for 24 h compared with WT (Fig. 2A). Also, LGR6 knockout contributed to increased serum cTnT (cardiac troponin T), CK-MB (creatine kinase, MB form) and LDH (lactate dehydrogenase) content, implying that LGR6 deficiency aggravated cardiac damage in response to myocardial I/R in mice (Fig. 2B). HE staining showed apparent cytoplasmic vacuolation, whereas LGR6 KO worsened vacuolation in cardiomyocytes (Fig. 2C and D). LGR6 KO markedly promoted reactive oxygen species (ROS) production in heart tissues compared with WT mice (Fig. 2E). Apoptotic cells identified as TUNEL-positive and necrotic cardiomyocytes identified as PI- and cTNT-positive were both observed in I/R injury mice compared with sham-operated mice. LGR6 KO had no significant effect on the number of TUNEL-positive apoptotic cells, but increased the proportion of necrotic cardiomyocytes (Fig. 2F–H). We were interested in this phenomenon of discordance between apoptosis and necrosis and tried to explore the mechanisms leading to these results. The pathological basis of myocardial I/R injury is acute necrosis of many damaged cardiomyocytes [17]. Recent reports suggest that alternative pathways of programmed cell death, especially necroptosis, play a crucial role in myocardial I/R injury [17]. Subsequently, we evaluated the contribution of two major regulatory death modes (necroptosis and apoptosis) in LGR6 KO-mediated myocardial I/R injury. Cleaved-caspase3 expression, which detects apoptosis, and phosphorylated MLKL (p-MLKL) level, which detects necroptosis, were increased in post-I/R hearts (Fig. 2I and J). However, compared with the marked increase in p-MLKL-positive cardiomyocytes, the increases in cleaved-caspase3-positive cardiomyocytes were marginal in post-I/R hearts of LGR6 KO mice (Fig. 2I and J). These results suggest that LGR6 deletion may mainly affect cardiomyocyte necroptosis after myocardial I/R injury. In addition, we also observed increased expression levels of other necroptosis biomarkers including p-RIPK1 (phosphorylated receptor-interacting protein kinase 1) and p-RIPK3 (phosphorylated receptor-interacting protein kinase 3) in cardiomyocytes post I/R injury (Fig. 2K and L). In parallel, Western blot analyses revealed that LGR6 KO promoted the cardiac expression of p-RIPK1, p-RIPK3 and p-MLKL in mice post I/R (Fig. 2M). Echocardiography was used to confirm the absence of any preexisting cardiac abnormalities in the LGR6 KO mice before I/R surgery, and the echocardiographic parameters of the LGR6 KO mice were comparable to those of the WT mice (Fig. S2A). Both the LGR6 KO and WT mice were then subjected to I/R surgery, and cardiac function was analyzed at 3 weeks after I/R. The echocardiographic analyses revealed reduction in LV ejection fraction (EF) and fractional shortening (FS) and expansion of LVIDs (left ventricular internal diameter at end-systole) in the LGR6 KO mice compared with WT mice. Furthermore, LGR6 KO mice exhibited increased ratios of heart weight to body weight or tibial length at 3 weeks after I/R compared with WT mice, indicating their compromised cardiac function (Fig. S2B). Furthermore, a markedly hypertrophic heart was observed in the non-infarct area of LGR6 KO mice, as shown by the increased cross-section area of cardiomyocytes (Fig. S2C). Masson staining and collagen-I immunofluorescence staining showed that in the context of cardiac fibrosis, LGR6 deficiency could increase the cardiac fibrosis area in mice 3 weeks after I/R (Figs. S2D–E). Taken together, LGR6 knockout exacerbates cardiac dysfunction and cardiac remodeling post I/R.

To identify the role of LGR6 deficiency in necroptosis in vitro, we treated HL1 cells with small interfering RNA for LGR6 (siLGR6) and the control (siNC) and then subjected cardiomyocytes to sham or HR group (Fig. S3A). Compared to sham condition, HR condition resulted in significant HL1 cell death as shown by the cell viability through Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK8) assay and cardiomyocyte injury as shown by LDH levels in culture medium, which was aggravated by siLGR6 treatment (Figs. S3B–C). Consistently, siLGR6 increased the ratio of necrotic cells identified as PI positive after HR (Fig. S3D). The immunoblotting results showed upregulated necroptosis in HL1 cells post HR as identified by the level of p-RIPK3 and p-MLKL, which was aggravated by LGR6 deficiency (Fig. S3E). In addition, necrosulfonamide (NSA), a necroptosis inhibitor by selectively targeting MLKL, was used to treat HL1s with HR condition and LGR6 deficiency (Fig. S4A). NSA treatment promoted the survival and reduced the damage of LGR6-deficient cardiomyocytes post HR (Figs. S4B–C). Taken together, these results suggested that LGR6 deficiency accelerates necroptosis in cardiomyocytes post HR.

To figure out the role of necroptosis in LGR6-regulated I/R injury, we next treated WT and LGR6 KO mice with NSA (20 mg/kg/day, ip) [18]. Compared with normal saline, NSA treatment significantly reduced plasma cTnT, CK-MB and LDH content in KO mice with I/R injury, suggesting ameliorated cardiac damage (Fig. S5A). NSA also attenuated cardiac dysfunction as identified by increased LVEF, FS and LVIDs in KO mice 3 weeks post I/R (Fig. S5B).

Cardiomyocyte-specific LGR6 overexpression ameliorates acute myocardial injury post I/R

To provide compelling evidence that LGR6 in cardiomyocytes is closely associated with acute myocardial injury post I/R, we specifically overexpressed LGR6 in cardiomyocytes by delivering cardiotropic recombinant adeno-associated virus serotype 9 expressing LGR6 (AAV9-cTnT-LGR6) into 6-week-old C57 mice through tail vein injection, with null as a control (AAV9-cTnT-null) (Fig. S6A). After receiving AAV9 treatment for 4 weeks, the efficiency of AAV9 transfection was assessed by immunoblots in heart tissues (Fig. S6B). Both the AAV9-LGR6 and AAV9-null treated mice were then subjected to I/R surgery (Fig. S6B). As expected, LGR6 overexpression in cardiomyocyte reduced the infarct area following reperfusion for 24 h (Fig. 3A). LGR6 overexpression also reduced the plasma level of cTnT, CK-MB and LDH, implying ameliorated cardiac damage post I/R (Fig. 3B). LGR6 overexpression also ameliorated cardiomyocytes vacuolation and reduced cardiac ROS level in mice post I/R (Fig. 3C–E). The ratio of necrotic cardiomyocytes identified as PI- and cTNT-positive were also reduced by AAV9-LGR6 treatment (Fig. 3F–J). LGR6 overexpression reduced the cardiac expression of p-RIPK1, p-RIPK3 and p-MLKL, as shown by the immunofluorescence staining and immunoblots analyses (Fig. 3G–K). We next measured cardiac function by echocardiography and discovered that LV systolic dysfunction in I/R injury mice was ameliorated in part by injection of AAV9-LGR6, as identified by increased LVEF (Fig. S7A). In addition, rodents treated with AAV9-LGR6 exhibited a reduced HW/BW and cardiomyocyte cross-sectional area (Figs. S7B–C). Likewise, LGR6 overexpression mitigated LV collagen deposition, as assessed by reduced collagen volume in hearts with AAV9-LGR6 treatment (Fig. S7D). These data indicated that restoration of cardiac LGR6 ameliorates cardiac dysfunction and cardiac remodeling post I/R.

We next treated HL1 cardiomyocytes with adenovirus carrying LGR6 (Ad-LGR6) or null (Ad-null) transfection (Fig. S8A). LGR6 overexpression exerted protective effects on HL1 viability and injury post HR condition (Figs. S8B–C). Consistently, Ad-LGR6 reduced the ratio of PI-positive necrotic cells after HR (Fig. S8D). LGR6 overexpression also reduced the level of p-RIPK3 and p-MLKL in HL1 cells with HR injury, suggesting inhibited necroptosis (Fig. S8E). Taken together, these results suggest LGR6 overexpression ameliorates cardiomyocyte injury post HR in vitro.

RSPO3-LGR6 axis inhibits necroptosis post HR

To figure out the mechanism by which LGR6 overexpression protect cardiomyocytes from necroptosis post HR, we first predicted proteins that may bind or interact with LGR6 through the Genemania database. R-spondin 3 (RSPO3), one of the ligands of LGR6, binds most obviously to LGR6 (Fig. S9A). The specific interaction between LGR6 and RSPO3 was confirmed by co-immunoprecipitation assay with exogenously transduced LGR6 and RSPO3 in HEK293T cells (Fig. S9B). HL1 cardiomyocytes with LGR6 overexpression were then treated with siRSPO3 to figure out the role of RSPO3 on necroptosis post HR (Fig. S9C). The protective effect of LGR6 overexpression on cardiomyocytes post HR was abolished by RSPO3 deficiency, as shown by reduced cell viability and increased LDH level (Figs. S9D–E). RSPO3 deficiency also increased the expression of p-RIPK3 and p-MLKL in HL1 cells post HR (Fig. S9F). Adenovirus carrying RSPO3 (Ad-RSPO3) was transfected to HL1 cardiomyocytes to overexpress RSPO3 (Fig. S10A). RSPO3 overexpression promoted cell viability, reduced LDH level and inhibited the expression of p-RIPK3 and p-MLKL in HL1 cells post HR, which were abolished by LGR6 deficiency (Figs. S10B–D). Together, these data indicated that RSPO3-LGR6 axis protects cardiomyocytes from necroptosis post HR.

RSPO3-LGR6 axis activates Wnt signaling post myocardial I/R

To explore how LGR6 regulates cardiac necroptosis post I/R, we performed comprehensive RNA-sequencing (RNA-seq) analysis on LV tissues of LGR6 KO and WT mice at one day after I/R (Fig. S11A). A total of 865 genes were significantly dysregulated, consisting of 440 genes that were upregulated and 425 genes that were downregulated (Fig. S11B). Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) analysis among upregulated genes of KO hearts demonstrated that the Necroptosis process was enriched, which was consistent with our findings in this study (Fig. S11C). KEGG analysis among downregulated genes showed significantly enriched Wnt signaling pathway (Fig. 4A). The Wnt pathway has been shown to play vital roles in cardiovascular disease, especially in myocardial I/R injury [17]. Canonical Wnt signaling pathway was inhibited during myocardial I/R injury as shown by the reduced cardiac expression of CTNNB1 (catenin beta 1), TCF1 (transcription factor 1) and LEF1 (lymphoid enhancer binding factor 1) in mice post I/R, which were further inhibited by LGR6 deficiency but activated by LGR6 overexpression (Fig. 4B, S12A). Consistently, HR conditions inhibited the expression of CTNNB1, TCF1 and LEF1 in HL1 cardiomyocytes, which was suppressed by LGR6 deletion and restored by LGR6 overexpression. RSPO3 overexpression increased the expression of CTNNB1 and LEF1 in HL1 cardiomyocytes under HR condition, which was blocked by LGR6 deficiency (Fig. S12B). We next performed Wnt reporter assay (TOPflash) in HL1 cardiomyocytes and observed markedly downregulated Wnt reporter activity post HR, suggesting inhibited Wnt signaling (Fig. 4C). LGR6 knockdown decreased and LGR6 overexpression increased Wnt reporter activity in HL1 cells post HR. To further explore the role of Wnt signaling pathway in LGR6-mediated inhibition of necroptosis, we used small interfering RNA for CTNNB1 (siCTNNB1) and LEF1 (siLEF1) to inhibit the Wnt signaling pathway (Fig. S13A). Knockdown of CTNNB1 and LEF1 abolished the protective effect of LGR6 overexpression on cardiomyocytes under HR conditions (Figs. S13B–C). The inhibitory effect of LGR6 overexpression on necroptosis was also abolished by CTNNB1 and LEF1 deficiency (Fig. S13D). Activation of the Wnt signaling pathway is usually associated with the expression of Wnt ligands and Frizzled (FZD) receptors. In order to clarify the mechanism by which LGR6 regulates the Wnt signaling pathway, we first examined the expression levels of Wnt ligands and FZD receptors in the RNAseq results (Fig. 4D). LGR6 KO did not significantly affect the expression of Wnt ligands in the heart after I/R, but significantly inhibited the expression of FZD2. Western blot analysis showed that LGR6 KO significantly inhibited the protein expression of cardiac FZD2 post I/R (Fig. 4E). In parallel, LGR6 deletion reduced and LGR6 overexpression increased FZD2 protein levels in cardiomyocytes after HR (Fig. 4F). However, LGR6 overexpression had no significant effect on FZD2 mRNA levels of cardiomyocytes post HR (Fig. 4G). The mismatch between FZD2 protein and mRNA expression suggests that LGR6 may regulate the post-translational modification process of FZD2.

We next examined whether LGR6 regulates cardiomyocyte FZD2 ubiquitination, one of the most common protein modifications. LGR6 deletion promoted, whereas LGR6 overexpression inhibited, FZD2 ubiquitination in HR-treated cardiomyocytes (Fig. 4H). ZNRF3 (Zinc/RING finger protein 3) is the most reported E3 ubiquitin-protein ligase that regulates FZD2 ubiquitination, thereby regulating the canonical Wnt signaling pathway [19]. Here we verified the specific binding of ZNRF3 and FZD2 in 293T cells (Fig. 4I). Moreover, knocking down ZNRF3 with siRNA effectively inhibited FZD2 ubiquitination caused by LGR6 deficiency (Fig. 4J). ZNRF3 overexpression can abolish the inhibition of FZD2 ubiquitination by LGR6 overexpression (Fig. 4J). By transfecting plasmids FZD2, ZNRF3, shLGR6 and Flag-LGR6 into 293T cells, we found that LGR6 deletion promoted while LGR6 overexpression inhibited the binding of FZD2 and ZNRF3, which may be the reason for LGR6 regulating FZD2 ubiquitination (Fig. 4K). To explore the mechanism by which LGR6 regulates the binding of ZNRF3 and FZD2, we first examined the role of LGR6 on ZNRF3 expression. We observed that LGR6 deletion increased and LGR6 overexpression reduced ZNRF3 protein levels in cardiomyocytes after HR (Fig. 4L). Previous reports suggested that RSPO-LGR signaling promotes auto-ubiquitination and membrane clearance of ZNRF3 [20,21]. We first verified the binding of LGR6 to ZNRF3 in 293T cells (Fig. 4M). We then transfected plasmids of LGR6, ZNRF3 and ubiquitin (Ub) into 293T cells and observed that LGR6 overexpression significantly promoted the ubiquitination of ZNRF3 (Fig. 4N). Moreover, RSPO3 overexpression can also promote ubiquitination of ZNRF3, which is inhibited by LGR6 knockdown (Fig. 4O). In the end, we observed that RSPO3 deficiency increased and RSPO3 overexpression reduced the ubiquitination of FZD2 in cardiomyocytes post HR (Fig. 4P). Taken together, these findings suggest that the RSPO3-LGR6 axis regulates the activation of the Wnt signaling pathway after HR in cardiomyocytes by regulating FZD2 ubiquitination (Fig. 4Q).

To further explore the role of FZD2 in LGR6-mediated inhibition of necroptosis, we used siRNA (siFZD2) to treat cardiomyocytes and found that FZD2 deletion aggravated cardiomyocyte death and injury post HR (Figs. S14A–C). FZD2 deficiency also abolished the inhibitory effect of LGR6 overexpression on cardiomyocyte necroptosis after HR (Fig. S14D). Adenovirus carrying FZD2 (Ad-FZD2) was used to treat cardiomyocytes with HR and LGR6 deficiency (Fig. S15A). FZD2 overexpression inhibited cell death and injury in cardiomyocytes with LGR6 deficiency post HR (Figs. S15B–C). Ad-FZD2 also abolished the promoting effect of LGR6 deficiency on cardiomyocyte necroptosis after HR (Fig. S15D). Taken together, these data indicate that FZD2 mediates the regulatory effects of LGR6 on cardiomyocyte necroptosis.

We first examined the effect of LGR6-Wnt signaling on STAT2 expression. LGR6 deletion increases whereas LGR6 overexpression reduces cardiac STAT2 expression in mice after myocardial I/R (Fig. 5F). Immunofluorescence results further suggested that LGR6 regulated the expression of STAT2 in cardiomyocytes after myocardial I/R in mice (Fig. 5G). In vitro, LGR6 deficiency promoted and LGR6 overexpression inhibited STAT2 expression in HL1 cardiomyocytes after HR (Fig. 5H). Moreover, knockdown of CTNNB1, LEF1 and FZD2 significantly abolished the inhibitory effect of LGR6 overexpression on STAT2 in cardiomyocytes after HR (Fig. 5I). Together, these data suggested that STAT2 expression was regulated by LGR6-Wnt signaling.

We then performed CHIP-PCR to verify that LEF1 could bind to the STAT2 promoter and observed that the STAT2 promoter was significantly enriched by anti-LEF1 (Fig. 5J and K). We designed a dual-luciferin reporter plasmid for STAT2 and transfected it into 293T cells, and found that LEF1 overexpression significantly inhibited the transcription of STAT2 (Fig. 5L). In HL1 cardiomyocytes, HR treatment increased STAT2 luciferase activity, which was inhibited by LGR6 overexpression (Fig. 5M). We then analyzed the possible binding sites of the transcription factors LEF1 and STAT2 promoters and found that the most likely binding sites were concentrated at −1624 to −1631 bp in the STAT2 promoter sequence (Fig. 5N). We mutated the LEF1 binding sites in the STAT2 promoter and individually transfected the respective plasmids containing either mutated or wild-type promoter regions into HEK293T cells (Fig. 5O and P). Partly restored luciferase activity was observed in HEK293T cells expression mutated binding site compared with the WT binding site (Fig. 5Q). Collectively, these data suggested LGR6-Wnt signaling regulated STAT2 transcription in cardiomyocytes.

S17B–C). Ad-STAT2 also abolished the inhibitory effect of LGR6 overexpression on cardiomyocyte necroptosis after HR (Fig. S17D). Taken together, these data indicate that STAT2 mediates the regulatory effects of LGR6 on cardiomyocyte necroptosis.

STAT2 activates ZBP1 transcriptional activity

We found here that STAT2 activation can accelerate necroptosis in cardiomyocytes after HR, but the mechanism is unclear. In order to further explore the mechanism of STAT2 involved in cardiomyocyte necroptosis, we adopted the same strategy as before to find the downstream targets of STAT2 (Fig. S18A). We first retrieved three CHIP-seq results of STAT2 (GSM1563683, GSM1563684, and GSM1563685) in public databases (Fig. S18B). The CHIP-seq results respectively indicated 79, 1503, and 1120 potential target genes with score>1, of which 76 genes were common to all three CHIP-seq results (Fig. S18B). Compared the 76 genes and the 865 DEGs in our RNA-seq results, we observed 47 potential downstream targets of STAT2 (Fig. S18C). Among the 47 target genes, ZBP1 was the most up-regulated (Table S4). Moreover, ZBP1 has been reported to be closely related to the occurrence of necroptosis. Therefore, we speculate that LGR6-STAT2 axis may promote cardiomyocyte necroptosis by regulating ZBP1 expression.

LGR6 overexpression protected cardiomyocytes from oxidative stress-induced necroptosis

An increasing number of studies indicate that oxidative stress plays a key role in the pathology of ischemia-reperfusion (I/R) injury [22,23]. A close relationship between redox status disturbance and cardiac necroptosis has also been reported [24]. To explore the role of LGR6 in cardiomyocyte injury under oxidative stress conditions, hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) was used to induce redox status disorders in cardiomyocytes [25]. Our findings revealed that H2O2 treatment significantly inhibited LGR6 expression in HL1 cardiomyocytes (Fig. S23A). Interestingly, LGR6 overexpression significantly alleviated H2O2-induced cardiomyocyte death and injury (Figs. S23B–D). Under H2O2 conditions, cardiomyocytes experienced significant oxidative stress, as evidenced by increased levels of reactive oxygen species (ROS), malondialdehyde (MDA), and lipid peroxidation (LPO) (Figs. S23E–F). LGR6 overexpression effectively alleviated H2O2-induced oxidative stress in cardiomyocytes. Furthermore, we observed that H2O2 conditions promoted necroptosis of cardiomyocytes, while inhibiting the Wnt signaling pathway and activating the STAT2-ZBP1 signaling pathway (Fig. S23G). These effects were mitigated by LGR6 overexpression. Taken together, these findings suggest that LGR6 overexpression protects cardiomyocytes from oxidative stress-induced necroptosis.

LGR6 activation via recombinant RSPO3 protected mice from acute myocardial injury post I/R

Our data have demonstrated that the RSPO3-LGR6 axis can inhibit HR-induced cardiomyocyte injury by regulating necroptosis. We then explored whether recombinant RSPO3 could exert a protective effect on attenuating myocardial injury after I/R and provide potential clinical benefit.

As the dose of RSPO3 increases, the level of myocardial injury in mice after I/R is gradually alleviated (Fig. 8B). RSPO3 at 5 mg/kg/d is considered to be an effective and appropriate dose for the treatment of myocardial I/R injury in mice (Fig. 8B). We next further explored the effects of RSPO3 (5 mg/kg/d) on necroptosis and cardiac dysfunction after myocardial I/R. Recombinant RSPO3 treatment significantly reduced the infarct size after myocardial I/R (Fig. 8C). Vacuolated cardiomyocytes, ROS levels and the proportion of PI-positive cardiomyocytes were all inhibited by RSPO3 treatment (Fig. 8D–F). RSPO3 treatment also alleviated necroptosis after myocardial I/R in mice, as shown by the immunofluorescence and immunobinding results (Fig. 8G). In addition, consistent with previous results, RSPO3 treatment activated the Wnt signaling pathway and inhibited the expression of STAT2 and ZBP1 after myocardial I/R in mice (Fig. 8H). We next measured cardiac function by echocardiography and discovered that LV systolic dysfunction was ameliorated in part by RSPO3 treatment, as identified by increased LVEF, LVFS and downregulated LVIDs (Figs. Discussion

In this study, we observed significantly downregulated LGR6 expression in hearts post myocardial I/R and cardiomyocytes post HR. LGR6 deficiency promoted and LGR6 overexpression inhibited necroptosis and acute myocardial injury after I/R. Mechanistically, in vivo and in vitro experiments suggest that LGR6 regulates the expression of STAT2 and ZBP1 by activating the Wnt signaling pathway, thereby inhibiting cardiomyocyte necroptosis after HR. Inhibiting STAT2 and ZBP1 effectively alleviated the aggravating effect of L6KO on myocardial necroptosis after I/R. Furthermore, activating LGR6 with RSPO3 also effectively protected mice from acute myocardial I/R injury. In summary, these data provide new evidence that RSPO3-LGR6 axis inhibited acute myocardial I/R-induced injury and necroptosis by regulating STAT2-ZBP1 signaling pathway (Fig. 8I).

Emerging evidence underscores the pivotal regulatory role of necroptosis in pathological myocardial remodeling and cardiac dysfunction following cardiac ischemia/reperfusion (I/R) injury [26,27]. Targeting necroptosis presents a novel intervention strategy to ameliorate myocardial I/R injury [28]. While specific necroptosis inhibitors are in development, more than 20 drugs originally designed for diverse conditions are being explored as potential inhibitors [29]. However, these candidates have yet to demonstrate efficacy in improving myocardial I/R injury and related cardiovascular disorders. The quest for upstream regulators of necroptosis assumes paramount importance for devising specific intervention strategies. Our study reveals that the overexpression of LGR6 in cardiomyocytes impedes necroptosis post I/R. Moreover, the activation of LGR6 via RSPO3 mitigates necroptosis following myocardial I/R. These findings suggest that the RSPO3/LGR6 axis could be a promising target for modulating necroptosis after I/R.

RSPO protein and its receptor LGR4/5/6 play an important role in the activation of the canonical Wnt signaling pathway [19]. In this study, we found that the RSPO3-LGR6 axis can activate the Wnt signaling pathway in cardiomyocytes, thereby regulating myocardial injury after I/R. Our data illustrate in more detail that the RSPO3-LGR6 axis inhibits the ubiquitination of FZD2 by promoting the autoubiquitination of ZNRF3, ultimately promoting Wnt signaling activation. Previous studies have shown that activation of Wnt signaling can inhibit necroptosis in various disease models, such as acute kidney injury and necrotizing enterocolitis [30,31]. However, the role and mechanism of Wnt signaling in the occurrence of necroptosis after myocardial I/R have not been reported. Our study is the first to report that activation of Wnt signaling effectively protects mice from necroptosis after myocardial I/R. Moreover, previous studies lack a clear explanation of the mechanism by which Wnt signaling regulates necroptosis. Here, we demonstrate for the first time that Wnt signaling inhibits the STAT2-ZBP1 pathway to regulate myocardial HR-induced necroptosis. Other strategies to regulate Wnt signaling need to be explored to further elucidate the role and mechanism of Wnt signaling in necroptosis after myocardial I/R.

Although we found that STAT2 is a target of LGR6-Wnt signaling in regulating necroptosis after myocardial I/R, how STAT2 regulates necroptosis is still unknown. There is only one report in the previous literature suggesting that STAT2 deficiency can inhibit interferon-induced macrophage necroptosis [32]. Here, we combined RNA-seq, CHIP-seq, CHIP-PCR and dual-luciferin reporter technologies and found that ZBP1 is a key target of STAT2 in regulating necroptosis. Knockdown of ZBP1 and STAT2 both effectively alleviated L6KO-aggravated myocardial necroptosis after I/R. The role of ZBP1 in necroptosis is clear. However, the role of ZBP1 in cardiomyocytes remains controversial. Two recent reports suggest that ZBP1 deletion protects mice from doxorubicin-induced cardiotoxicity while aggravating left ventricular dilation after myocardial infarction [15,16]. This controversial role requires further exploration. Our study found that ZBP1 deletion can alleviate necroptosis after myocardial I/R, providing some data for studying the role of ZBP1 in cardiomyocytes.

Our research underscores LGR6 as a potentially effective target for ameliorating myocardial injury post ischemia/reperfusion (I/R). In pursuit of clinical translation, we employed the LGR6 activator RSPO3 to treat mice experiencing myocardial I/R, resulting in a significant improvement in myocardial injury and a reduction in necroptosis. However, the widespread application of RSPO3 necessitates thorough validation through extensive clinical trials. To enhance the feasibility of clinical translation, we propose exploring the development of small molecule activators for LGR6 or identifying analogs of RSPO3, both avenues warranting further investigation.

Oxidative stress is a crucial mechanism leading to myocardial injury after ischemia-reperfusion (I/R) and is closely associated with the occurrence of necroptosis [33]. Blocking ROS can alleviate cardiomyocyte necroptosis induced by various stimuli, such as hyperglycemia and H₂O₂33. Pretreatment of cardiomyocytes with the antioxidant drug N-acetylcysteine (NAC) can mitigate oxidative damage induced by doxorubicin and high glucose, while inhibiting necroptosis by downregulating the expression of its key regulator, RIPK3 [34,35]. Additionally, in a rat model of heart failure induced by pressure overload, NAC pretreatment alleviated left ventricular systolic dysfunction and oxidative stress levels, and reduced necroptosis [24]. A direct link between I/R-mediated RIPK3 activation and ROS generation in cardiomyocytes has been reported, with the potential mechanism involving the activation of Ca2+-calmodulin-dependent protein kinase (CaMKII) [23]. These findings indicate a close relationship between redox state disorders and cardiac necroptosis. In the present study, H₂O₂ conditions effectively induced cardiomyocyte oxidative stress and necroptosis, which was alleviated by LGR6 overexpression. Our study further demonstrated the close relationship between oxidative stress and necroptosis, though the effect of LGR6 on oxidative stress after I/R may require further exploration.

This study has several limitations. First, only male mice were investigated in this study. The use of female mice may help us understand the function of LGR6 more comprehensively and requires further exploration. Second, we used LGR6 KO mice to systematically knock out LGR6 to explore the impact of LGR6 deletion on myocardial I/R injury. Mice with cardiomyocyte-specific knockout of LGR6 may help to more accurately study LGR6 function in cardiomyocytes. Third, we used RNA sequencing to assess gene expression changes in LV tissues, but not specifically in LV cardiomyocytes, between global LGR6 KO and WT mice. The sequencing results may include contributions from other non-cardiomyocytes.

Taken together, our results indicate that the RSPO3-LGR6 axis downregulates the expression of STAT2 and ZBP1 through the Wnt signaling pathway, thereby inhibiting I/R-induced myocardial injury and necroptosis. Targeting the RSPO3-LGR6 axis may be a potential therapeutic strategy to treat myocardial I/R injury.

Materials and methods

Animals and treatments

All the experimental procedures were approved by the Animal Care and Use Committee of Renmin Hospital of Wuhan University (approval No. 20181215), and were also in accordance with the Guidelines for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals published by the US National Institutes of Health. Global LGR6 knockout mice on the C57BL/6 background and C57BL/6 WT mice were purchased from the Gempharmatech Co., Ltd. (Nanjing, China). Heterozygous LGR6-deficient mice were crossed with each other to generate homozygous LGR6 KO mice, and the WT littermates were used as the negative control. All animals were housed at constant room temperature on a 12:12 h light-dark cycle at the Animal Center of Wuhan University Renmin Hospital and fed a standard rodent diet and water.

To induce myocardial I/R injury in vivo, male mice aged 10–12 weeks were firstly anesthetized by intraperitoneal injections of 3 % pentobarbital sodium, and the body temperature was maintained at 37 ± 1 °C by a warming pad. Next, the heart was exposed by a left thoracotomy, and the left anterior descending (LAD) artery 1 mm below the tip of the left atrial appendage was ligated with a 7-0 silk suture over a piece of PE-10 tubing. Ischemia was confirmed by both ST elevation on surface electrocardiogram and visual blanching. After occlusion for 1 h, the tubing was removed, and the ligature was released to achieve reperfusion. Serum and heart samples were collected at the indicated time points after reperfusion. Sham-operated mice received same surgical procedures without ligating the LAD artery.

To specifically overexpress LGR6 in the cardiomyocyte, C57 mice received a single intravenous injection of AAV9-cTnT-LGR6 at a dose of 1x1011 viral genomes per mouse 4 weeks prior to I/R surgery. To specifically knock down ZBP1 and STAT2 in the cardiomyocyte, C57 mice received a single intravenous injection of AAV9-cTnT-shZBP1 or AAV9-cTnT-shSTAT2 at a dose of 1x1011 viral genomes per mouse 4 weeks prior to I/R surgery. In addition, LGR6 KO mice were intraperitoneally injected with necrosulfonamide (NSA, 20 mg/kg/day) one day before ischemia and last for 3 weeks to inhibit necroptosis [18]. Different doses of recombinant RSPO3 treatment were administered by intraperitoneal injection one day before ischemia and last for 3 weeks to activate LGR6.

Statistical analysis

All data are presented as scatter dot plot or line chart with mean ± SD, and p < 0.05 was considered significant. As to comparison between two groups, two-tailed unpaired t-test was used as indicated. For more than two groups, one-way ANOVA with Bonferroni multiple comparisons test was used and for experiments with a second variable, two-way ANOVA with Bonferroni multiple comparisons test was performed. Statistical analysis was performed with GraphPad Prism 8.0 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA).

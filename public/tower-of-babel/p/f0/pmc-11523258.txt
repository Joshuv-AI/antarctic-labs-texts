Reactivity of Strontium Hydride Supported by the Superbulky Hydrotris(pyrazolyl)borate Ligand
Authors: Xun Gong, Xianghui Shi, Peng Deng, Jianhua Cheng
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractHydrogenolysis of [(TpAd,iPr)Sr{CH(SiMe3)2}] (1) (TpAd,iPr = hydrotris(3-adamantyl-5-isopropyl-pyrazolyl)borate) in hexane solution under 20 atm of H2 allowed for the isolation of strontium hydride [(TpAd,iPr)Sr(μ-H)]2 (2) in good yield. Complex 2 exhibits the dimeric nature in solid state, featuring two different bond modes between the Sr center and TpAd,iPr ligand. Treatment of complex 2 with PhC(H)=NtBu or PhCH2Bpin (Bpin = pinacolateborane) afforded the strontium amide complex [(TpAd,iPr)Sr{N(CH2Ph)(tBu)}] (4) and hydroborate complex [(TpAd,iPr)Sr{μ-HBpin(CH2Ph)}] (5), respectively. Reactions of complex 2 with 2-picoline, 2-phenylquinoline, or 2-phenylpyridine led to the formation of strontium 2-pyridylmethylene/2-picoline complex [(TpAd,iPr)Sr(2-CH2–Py)(2-picoline)] (6), reductively coupling diphenyl-biquinolide complex [{(TpAd,iPr)Sr}2(2,2′-Ph2-4,4′-dihydro-4,4′-biquinolide)] (7), and diphenyl-bipyridyl radical complex [(TpAd,iPr)Sr(6,6′-Ph2-2,2′-bipyridyl)] (8), separately. All of the complexes have been well characterized, including NMR spectrum and single-crystal X-ray analysis.

Introduction

Recent years have witnessed great achievements of alkaline-earth metal hydride complexes1,2−10 in a variety of organic transformations as potential alternatives of precious metals, due to their abundance, low-cost, and nontoxic feature (except beryllium11−13 and barium).14,15 The challenges to isolate the alkaline-earth metal hydrides increase dramatically along with the ionic radius size (Mg < Ca < Sr < Ba), due to the increasing ionic character and bond distances, and the decreasing metal–ligand bond energies in the same direction.16−19 The family member of molecular alkaline-earth metal hydrides is dominated by magnesium hydrides,20 and the number of calcium hydrides21,22 is more than twice as many as the sum of strontium4,23−30 and barium hydrides.31−33

So far, only eight examples of well-defined strontium hydride complexes are known (Figure 1). The first report of fully characterized strontium hydride complex was presented by the group of Harder in 2017.23 The hexa-nuclear cluster hydride [Sr6H9(PMDTA)3{N(SiMe3)2}3] (PMDTA = N,N,N′,N″,N″-pentamethyldiethylenetriamine) (Figure 1A) was isolated from the combination of Sr{N(SiMe3)2}2, phenylsilane, and PMDTA. Following the similar self-assembly approach, the largest strontium hydride cluster [Sr10H16(PMDTA)2{N(SiiPr3)(Dipp)}4] (Dipp = 2,6-iPr–C6H3) (Figure 1B) was isolated by the replacement the Sr{N(SiMe3)2}2 with more steric bulky amide Sr{N(SiiPr3)(Dipp)}2.27 The group of Okuda reported the first sample of cationic strontium hydride [(Me3TACD)3Sr3H2][SiPh3] (Figure 1C), generated from the reaction of Sr(SiPh3)2(THF)3 and (Me3TACD)H (1,4,7-trimethyl-1,4,7,10-tetraazacyclododecane).25 The dicationic trinuclear strontium metal hydride core [Sr3H4]2+ can be maintained by the macrocyclic amine ligands (Me4TACD) (1,4,7,10-tetramethyltetraazacyclododecane) (Figure 1D).28 Compared with the 12-membered macrocyclic ligand Me4TACD, the 15-membered macrocyclic ligand Me5PACP (1,4,7,10,13-pentamethyl-1,4,7,10,13-pentaazacyclopentadecane) with increased steric hindrance diminished the strontium hydride core to dimer [(Me5PACP)2Sr2(μ-H)3][B(C6H3-3,5-Me2)4] (Figure 1E).29 Jones and coworkers disclosed the first report of neutral dimeric strontium hydride [(NCN)Sr(μ-H)]2, supported by a bulky asymmetric amidinate ligand (NCN = [AdC{N(Dipp)}{N(Ar*)}], Ad = 1-adamantyl, Ar* = 2,6-(Ph2CH)2-4-iPr–C6H2) (Figure 1F).24 Using the bulky β-diketiminate (BDIDiPeP, DiPeP = 2,6-diisopentyl-phenyl) or penta-aryl-cyclopentadienyl (CpAr5, Ar = 3,5-iPr2–C6H3) ligands, the corresponding dimeric strontium hydride complexes [(BDIDiPeP)Sr(μ-H)]2 (Figure 1G)26 and [(CpAr5)Sr(μ-H)(DABCO)]2 (DABCO = 1,4-diazabicyclooctane) (Figure 1H)4 have been isolated by the groups of Harder and Cheng,4 respectively. Only few studies on the reactivity of strontium hydride complexes have been carried out, such as catalytic alkene hydrogenation and hydrosilylation,4,26 catalytic H-isotope exchange at benzene34 and catalytic Et-D exchange with C6D6,26 as well as the carbon–halogen bond activation.35

Different ligands for stabilization of strontium hydrides (A–H).

The superbulky hydrotris(pyrazolyl)borate (TpAd,iPr) has been proved as a suitable ancillary ligand to support divalent ytterbium36,37 and heavy alkaline-earth metal hydride complexes with low coordination number.38−45 The first molecular dimeric barium hydride complex [(TpAd,iPr)Ba(μ-H)]2 was reported by our group in 2017,38 as well as its reactivity toward carbon monoxide, 1,4-diphenylbutadiyne, and 1,1-diphenylethene.39 In 2020, the isolation of single-core calcium terminal hydride complex [(TpAd,iPr)Ca(H)(THP)] (THP = tetrahydropyrane) has been achieved by the use of the same ligand.42 [(TpAd,iPr)Ca(H)(THP)] effectively catalyzed the hydrogenation of plenty of alkenes and dehydrogenative couplings.43−45 Here, we report the synthesized route and structural characterization of the dimeric strontium hydride complex [(TpAd,iPr)Sr(μ-H)]2. The stoichiometric reactions of strontium hydride toward PhC(H)=NtBu, PhCH2Bpin, and pyridine derivatives are also presented.

Results and Discussion

In our previous report, hydrogenolysis of [(TpAd,iPr)Sr{CH(SiMe3)2}] (1)38 in hexane solution under 20 atm H2 led to a mixture, in which [(TpAd,iPr)Sr(μ-H)]2 (2) and [(TpAd,iPr)2Sr] (3) were finally isolated and structurally characterized. In hexane or aromatic solvents, complex 2 decomposed slowly into complex 3 at room temperature. The decomposition rate increases dramatically in the presence of THF. Thus, the yield of complex 2 is affected seriously by the content of THF during the hydrogenolysis process (Scheme 1).

Synthesis of Strontium Hydride [(TpAd,iPr)Sr(μ-H)]2 (2) and its Rearrangement Product [(TpAd,iPr)2Sr] (3)

The 1H NMR spectrum of complex 2 shows only one set of signals corresponding to the TpAd,iPr ligand and a singlet at 6.97 ppm. The latter is integrated as 1H per TpAd,iPr ligand and allotted to the hydrido ligand, which is further determined by deuterated analogous [(TpAd,iPr)Sr(μ-D)]2 (2-D) in 2D NMR (Figure S4). The hydride chemical shift in complex 2 is slightly downfield shifted compared to that of the solid SrH2 (6.7 ppm),46 and the range (4.48–6.64 ppm) found in the previous molecular strontium hydride complexes.4,23−29

Single crystal X-ray crystallographic studies reveal the dimeric feature of complex 2 (Figure 2). The [Sr2H2]2+ core is surrounded with one obviously twisted κ3-TpAd,iPr ligand and one distorted TpAd,iPr ligand with a side-on bound η2-pyrazolyl ring. The bridging hydrido ligands (H1 and H2) were found by difference Fourier synthesis and refined isotropically. The Sr–H length within the [Sr2H2]2+ core from 2.16(4) to 2.42(4) Å is comparable to the scope reported for strontium hydride complexes, which ranges from 2.24 to 2.86 Å.4,23−29 The Sr---Sr distance (3.9249(7) Å) is about 0.33 Å shorter than that of Ba---Ba distance (4.2528(8) Å) in complex [(TpAd,iPr)Ba(μ-H)]2,38 attributed to the difference in ionic radii (CN = 6; Sr2+: 1.18 Å; Ba2+: 1.35 Å).47

In the 1H NMR spectrum of complex 3, the two TpAd,iPr ligands exhibit two sets of signals, indicating the presence of different coordination environment. As shown in Figure 3, the Sr2+ center is coordinated with one κ3-N3 TpAd,iPr ligand, and one κ3-N2H TpAd,iPr ligand, which is identical to those of [(TpAd,iPr)2Ba]40 and [(TptBu,Me)2LnII] (LnII = Yb2+, Tm2+ and Sm2+).48−50

Treatment of complex 2 with two equivalents of PhC(H)=NtBu or PhCH2Bpin in benzene solution at room temperature easily afforded the corresponding strontium amide complex [(TpAd,iPr)Sr{N(CH2Ph)(tBu)}] (4) and hydroborate complex [(TpAd,iPr)Sr{μ-HBpin(CH2Ph)}] (5) in high isolated yields. Complexes 4 and 5 are stable in most hydrocarbon solvents such as benzene and hexane at room temperature for 1 week without obvious changes (Scheme 2).

The solid structure of complex 4 (Figure 4) discloses that the Sr2+ center is bonded to a highly distorted TpAd,iPr ligand and an amide ligand with weak interaction between Sr2+ and benzyl group (Sr1–C1:3.355(4) Å; Sr1–C2:3.343(3) Å; Sr1–C3:3.287(4) Å). The C1–N1 distance of 1.440(5) Å indicates that the C=N bond of imine has been reduced to a C–N single bond of amine. This result is in contrast to the reaction of calcium hydride complex [(NCN*)Ca(μ-H)]2 (NCN* = [tBuC{N(Dipp)}2]) with PhC(H)=NtBu, in which only a mixed amide/hydride product [(NCN*)2Ca2(μ-H){μ-N(CH2Ph)(tBu)}] can be isolated, and further insertion is blocked even under harsh conditions.51

As shown in Figure 5, the pinacolborate unit in complex 5 is coordinated to the Sr2+ center through one of the two oxygen atoms. The borohydride (H1B2) is directly pointing toward the Sr atom with the bond distance of 2.37(4) Å, which is comparable to the Sr2+---H–B(sBu)3 (2.325(8) Å) bond length found in strontium borohydride complex [(BDIDipp)Sr{μ-HB(sBu)3}(THF)].52

Magnesium and calcium hydride complexes have been reported to involve in extensive chemistry with pyridine derivatives,53−58 but the heavier group II metal hydride complexes are few explored. The reaction of complex 2 with four equivalents of 2-picoline afforded strontium 2-pyridylmethylene/2-picoline complex [(TpAd,iPr)Sr(2-CH2–Py)(2-picoline)] (6) as orange crystals in 85% isolated yields (Scheme 3). As shown in Figure 6, one of the 2-picoline clearly underwent benzylic sp3 C–H bond activation. The Sr2+ center is coordinated to a highly twisted TpAd,iPr ligand, one 2-pyridylmethylene ligand through the N atom (N1) and the benzylic carbons (C1 and C2) in a chelating fashion, and one 2-picoline ligand. Compared with [(TpAd,iPr)Ca(2-CH2–Py)], the presence of a coordinated 2-picoline is attributed to the larger ionic size of strontium than calcium (Sr2+, 1.18 Å; Ca2+, 1.00 Å; CN = 6).47 As expected, the length of Sr1–C1 (2.853(2) Å) and Sr–N1 (2.578(2) Å) are distinctly longer than those in [(TpAd,iPr)Ca(2-CH2–Py)] (2.614(3) and 2.410(2) Å)44 and [(BDIDipp)Ca{6-Me-2-(μ-CH2)-Py}(THF)] (2.393(2) and 2.648(1) Å).59

Dropwise addition of two equivalents of 2-phenylquinoline to the colorless solution of complex 2 in benzene gradually formed a red-orange solution. This mixture was layered into hexane at room temperature for days to give orange crystals. X-ray analysis revealed that these orange products were the reductively coupled quinoline complex [{(TpAd,iPr)Sr}2(2,2′-Ph2-4,4′-dihydro-4,4′-biquinolide)] (7). Two independent half-dimeric moieties are found in the asymmetric unit. The solid structure of complex 7 lies on a center of C9–C9’ bond, thus imposing an anti-conformation of the 2,2′-Ph2-4,4′-dihydro-4,4′-biquinolide bridging ligand (Figure 7). The C–C bonds C8–C9 (1.498(8) Å) and C9–C10 (1.503(9) Å) are classical single bond lengths. The C7–C8 distance of 1.354(7) Å is typical for the C=C bond. The length of Sr1–N1 (2.473(4) Å) is comparable to that of strontium amide complex 4 (Sr1–N7:2.423(3) Å). The C9–C9’ bond distance of 1.602(11) Å is also consistent with a single C–C bond and is slightly beyond the range of 1.559(4)–1.565(10) Å found in samarium,60,61 thulium,62 thorium,63 gadolinium,64 and uranium65 complexes featuring reductively coupled pyridines. In this reaction, complex 2 can be regarded as a Sr(I) synthon, easily reduces coupling of two 2-phenylquinoline to afford 2,2′-Ph2-4,4′-dihydro-4,4′-biquinolide bridging ligand, in which a single electron transfer is the key step of dimerization process.60−65

Combination of complex 2 with 4 equiv of 2-phenylpridines in benzene at room temperature for 2 days afforded the formation of dark-blue solution. Black crystals of complex 8 were found from the benzene solution layered superficially with hexane on the top. In the 1H NMR spectrum, some broad peaks are apparent and prevent satisfactory resolution, which indicate the paramagnetic nature and the presence of radical ligand. The radical-anionic nature of the diphenyl-bipyridyl ligand was determined by electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) (Figure S15). The EPR spectrum in toluene at room temperature displays a g value of 2.004, indicative of an S = 1/2 ground state for complex 8. This observation is close to that of the bipyridyl magnesium complexes (g = 2.003)66,67 and bipyridyl calcium complex.68 The EPR signal fitting shows hyperfine lines, which originate from the interactions of the sole unpaired electron with two 14N nuclei (I = 1) (A = 12.6 MHz) and three sets of 1H nuclei (I = 1/2) (6.4, 2.1, and 2.1 MHz), implying that the single electron is mainly delocalized in the diphenyl-bipyridyl ligand.

X-ray analysis also proved the radical-anionic character of the bipyridyl ligand. The Sr2+ center is embraced by a twisted TpAd,iPr ligand, and one diphenyl-bipyridyl ligand in a chelating fashion through two N atoms (N1 and N2) and the two carbons (C1 and C2) bridging the two pyridine rings (Figure 8). The C1–C2 bond length of 1.421(4) Å is consistent with that found in [(NNfc)Y(6,6′-Ph2-2,2′-bipyridyl)] (NNfc = 1,1′-fc(NSitBuMe2)2) (1.4208(36) Å).69 The other C–C and C–N distances are also consistent with the radical-anionic nature of the bipyridyl ligand (see Supporting Information). In this transformation (Scheme 4), the reaction between complex 2 with 2-phenylpyridine gave the sp2 C–H activated species (A), which reacted with another 2-phenylpyridine to afford the coupling product (B), in accompany with the dearomatization of one of the pyridine rings. The final product 8 was obtained after the hydrogen-atom abstraction from dearomatized bipyridyl ligand. This potential mechanism is comparable to the reaction of [(NNfc)Y(CH2Ar)(THF) (Ar = 3,5-Me2-C6H3) with 2-phenylpyridine, which afforded [(NNfc)Y(6,6′-Ph2-2,2′-bipyridyl)].69

Conclusions

In conclusion, we have showed that the superbulky TpAd,iPr ligand can serve as an excellent ligand for stabilizing all the heavy alkaline-earth metal hydrides, including the previously reported barium and calcium hydride complexes, and strontium hydride complex 2 in this work. Complex 2 easily converted imine into amine by hydride insertion and combined with borane to afford a hydroborate. A variety of reaction patterns of complex 2 toward pyridine derivatives were observed, including sp3 C–H activation of 2-picoline, reductive coupling of quinoline, and transformation of 2-phenylpyridine into anionic bipyridyl radical via sp2 C–H activation, C–C bond coupling, and hydrogen-atom abstraction. Work is continuing to explore the catalytic transformations mediated by the strontium hydride.

Experimental Section

General Remarks

All reactions were fulfilled under a nitrogen atmosphere by using Schlenk techniques and a Vigor glovebox. The nitrogen in the glovebox was constantly circulated through an activated copper/molecular sieve (4 Å) catalyst unit to ensure that the oxygen and moisture concentrations were always below 0.1 ppm. Hydrocarbon solvents, such as benzene, hexane, and methylcyclohexane, and diethyl ether were purified by use of a Vigor VSPS-5 solvent purification system and dried over freshly cut Na chips in the glovebox. C6D6 was distilled from Na/K alloy/benzophenone, degassed three times, and stored in the presence of Na chips in the glovebox. [(TpAd,iPr)Sr{CH(SiMe3)2}]38 was synthesized according to the previous procedures. Other reagents were used directly without purification.

Samples for NMR spectroscopic measurements were carried out in the glovebox by the use of J. Young valve NMR tubes. 1H and 13C{1H} NMR spectra were performed on a Bruker AV500 spectrometer and referenced to C6D6 (7.16 ppm). The elemental analyses were recorded on an Elementar Vario EL cube (WO3 was used as pro-oxidant). The X-band EPR spectrum was recorded on a JEOL JES-FA200 spectrometer. The spectrum was simulated using the EasySpin.70

Synthesis of [(TpAd,iPr)Sr(μ-H)]2 (2)

After purging the glovebox with N2 gas for 30 min, (TpAd,iPr)Sr[CH(SiMe3)2] (1) (505 mg, 0.51 mmol) in hexane (8 mL) was added into a glass tube in a stainless autoclave. The autoclave was pressurized with H2 to 20 atm outside of the glovebox and kept at indoor temperature for 48 h. After the release of pressure, the autoclave was quickly transferred into the glovebox. After filtration, the solution was concentrated to 1.5 mL and stored at −30 °C for 2 days to afford [(TpAd,iPr)Sr(μ-H)]2 (2) as colorless crystals (230 mg, 0.138 mmol, 54% yield). Synthesis of [(TpAd,iPr)Sr{μ-HBpin(CH2Ph)}] (5)

Machine learning-based prediction of survival in breast cancer patients with lung metastasis: a SEER-based study
Authors: Cairong Wu, Guantong Liu, Haijie Xu, Hongguang Liu, Hansheng Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundBreast cancer remains the most prevalent malignancy among women, and breast cancer with lung metastasis (BCLM) significantly increases morbidity and mortality. Accurate prognosis prediction for BCLM patients is critical for guiding personalized treatment strategies, yet effective predictive tools remain limited. This study aimed to develop a machine learning (ML)-based predictive framework for BCLM prognosis by leveraging data from the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) database (2011–2020).MethodsWe analyzed data of 2,921 female BCLM patients from the SEER database (2011–2020). Advanced oversampling techniques (SMOTEENN, SMOTETomek) addressed class imbalance, and Bayesian optimization tuned model hyperparameters. Six ML classifiers—linear regression, elastic net, random forest, support vector machine (SVM), extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost), and gradient boosting machine (GBM)—were evaluated. SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) provided model interpretability and identified key prognostic factors.ResultsSVM achieved the highest performance in 1-year prediction [area under the curve (AUC): 0.770; 95% confidence interval (CI): 0.737–0.803], while XGBoost demonstrated slightly higher discrimination in 3-year (AUC: 0.770; 95% CI: 0.739–0.802) and 5-year (AUC: 0.746; 95% CI: 0.709–0.783) predictions. Key prognostic factors—marital status [specifically for the hormone receptor (HR)+/human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)− subtype] and metastatic sites (bone/liver)—were consistently identified as critical determinants of survival across models. SHAP analysis revealed interactions between sociodemographic and molecular factors.ConclusionsThe findings demonstrate that ML-based frameworks, particularly those integrating multimodal clinical data, can improve risk stratification for BCLM patients and provide actionable insights for personalized oncology. Limitations include reliance on retrospective registry data and unmeasured confounders; future research should incorporate longitudinal and imaging biomarkers to further refine predictive accuracy.

Key findings

• This study developed models to predict 1-, 3-, and 5-year overall survival in patients with breast cancer lung metastasis. Support vector machine (SVM) was the model with the highest numerical performance for 1-year survival prediction, and extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost) showed the highest numerical area under the curve (AUC) for 3- and 5-year survival prediction. What is known and what is new?

• Breast cancer lung metastasis is associated with poor prognosis, and survival is influenced by tumor biology, metastatic burden, and treatment-related factors. Most prognostic studies rely on small, biased clinical trial datasets.

• This study constructed a comprehensive prediction framework incorporating sociodemographic, biological, and therapeutic factors. It also improves interpretability by using SHAP to identify clinically relevant predictors.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• Interpretable machine learning models may support individualized risk stratification and assist clinical decision-making for patients with breast cancer lung metastasis. These tools should be used as complementary aids rather than replacements for clinical judgment.

• Future studies should perform external validation and incorporate longitudinal treatment, imaging, and molecular data to improve clinical applicability.

Introduction

Breast cancer has become the most common malignancy in women globally, with approximately 2.3 million new cases reported in 2022, accounting for 24.2% of all female cancers and remaining the leading cause of cancer-related mortality in women (1). Breast cancer with lung metastases (BCLM) represents one of the most frequent distant metastatic patterns, occurring in 32–37% of metastatic breast cancer cases, second only to bone metastasis (60–75%) (2,3). Clinically, over 60% of breast cancer patients develop lung metastases during advanced stages. The predominant manifestations include persistent dry cough, dyspnea, chest pain, pleural effusion, and hemoptysis, which collectively compromise both quality of life and survival outcomes (4). Prognostically, BCLM patients exhibit a median survival of 21–22 months post-diagnosis of lung metastases, with a 5-year overall survival (OS) rate as low as 16.8% (5,6). While surgical resection can prolong survival for patients with early- and intermediate-stage tumors, patients with distant metastases face poor prognoses that are challenging to treat effectively (7).

Early detection of BCLM is pivotal for improving patient outcomes, as timely therapeutic intervention at the onset of metastasis significantly enhances survival and treatment efficacy (4). This study underscores the critical importance of identifying pulmonary metastases before systemic progression occurs, particularly in breast cancer patients. Current clinical guidelines remain inconclusive regarding optimal surgical management for BCLM, while conventional approaches primarily depend on systemic therapies—including chemotherapy, endocrine therapy, and targeted agents—that demonstrate diminished effectiveness when metastases are diagnosed at advanced stages. In contrast, early identification enables proactive clinical intervention, offering a more favorable prognosis by preventing extensive disease progression.

Machine learning (ML) models hold promise for advancing BCLM management by integrating multidimensional data to overcome the limitations of traditional prognostic evaluations (8-10). Previous studies have established some models to predict the risk of lung metastasis and prognosis of breast cancer (11-15). However, the prediction accuracy of these models is barely satisfactory because they do not integrate key clinical variables (e.g., treatment modality, molecular typing details), lack dynamic risk prediction ability (only assessed at a single time point), ignore data imbalances (e.g., imbalance in the proportion of untreated metastatic lesions), and are weak in interpretation (relying on “black box” model). Therefore, a more accurate and stable model is needed.

The heterogeneity of BCLM prognosis is not fully captured by conventional clinical indices [e.g., tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) staging, molecular subtypes], as individual survival differences often depend on complex interactions between biological, socioeconomic, and treatment-related factors (16,17). Traditional models predominantly focus on tumor biology [e.g., human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) expression, histologic grade] while neglecting the potential synergistic impact of sociodemographic factors (e.g., marital status) and metastatic patterns (e.g., bone, liver, brain metastases) (18,19). Most prognostic studies rely on small, biased clinical trial datasets. To address these gaps, this study constructed a comprehensive prediction framework incorporating sociodemographic, biological, and therapeutic factors using a cohort of 2,921 BCLM cases identified from the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) database (2011–2020). This framework was designed to explore the feasibility of preliminary survival risk stratification rather than to provide a validated clinical decision-making tool. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2026-1211/rc).

Methods

Study population

We collected data from patients diagnosed with breast cancer between 2011 and 2020 using the SEER database accessed through SEER*Stat software version 8.4.4. The SEER database, maintained by the National Cancer Institute (NCI), aggregates cancer registry data covering approximately 30% of the U.S. population (20). As the dataset contained fully de-identified information without patient interventions, this study was exempt from ethics committee approval and informed consent requirements. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments.

The patients were included based on the following criteria: (I) histologically confirmed breast cancer; (II) diagnosis at age ≥20 years; (III) diagnosis between 2011–2020; (IV) ≥5-year follow-up or documented survival outcome within 5 years. The patients were excluded according to the following criteria: (I) patients diagnosed only by autopsy or death certificate; (II) breast cancer not being the first primary cancer diagnosis. Initial screening identified 638,159 patients; detailed selection criteria illustrated in Figure 1.

Flowchart of patient selection. AJCC, American Joint Committee on Cancer; SEER, Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results; TNM, tumor-node-metastasis.

Demographic and clinicopathological characteristics, including age, race, sex, laterality, marital status, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM stage, surgery, tumor size, number of harvested lymph nodes, number of positive lymph nodes, histological type, pulmonary metastasis, bone metastasis, brain metastasis, liver metastasis, HER2, and previous tumor history were collected from the SEER database.

Data preprocessing

During the screening process, patients with missing data were excluded to ensure data integrity. Consequently, only samples with intact data were incorporated into the model-building and validation stages. All the categorical data were converted to a numeric format to meet the requirements of ML algorithms. One-hot encoding was adopted to process all the categorical data. The dataset was divided into a training set and a validation set in a ratio of 7:3. To minimize the influence of different quantitative scales and alleviate data leakage, data were centered and scaled after division. Finally, we used seven improved algorithms from sklearn library in Python, including synthetic minority over-sampling technique (SMOTE), adaptive synthetic sampling (ADASYN), SMOTE + edited nearest neighbors (SMOTEENN), SMOTE + Tomek links, K-nearest neighbors (KNN), ClusterCentroids, and support vector machine + SMOTE (SVMSMOTE), to optimize the imbalanced distribution of negative and positive samples.

Model building and validation

All preprocessing and model-development procedures were performed within the training set. After the 7:3 train-validation split, one-hot encoding and scaling parameters were fitted only on the training set and then applied to the validation set. During 5-fold cross-validation, scaling, resampling for class imbalance, model training, and Bayesian hyperparameter optimization were conducted only within each training fold. Resampling methods were not applied to the validation folds or the independent validation set. The independent validation set was used only for final model evaluation, including the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC-ROC), area under the precision-recall curve (AUC-PR), calibration assessment, and decision curve analysis.

Six ML classifiers were trained in the study, including linear regression, elastic net, random forest (RF), linear support vector machine (SVM), extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost), and gradient boosting machine (GBM). A brief description of these algorithms can be found in Table S1 and the parameter space of six machine learning models can be found in Table 1. In this study, the AUC-PR was employed as a secondary metric in addition to AUC-ROC to more effectively accommodate the imbalanced data and mitigate the risk of overfitting. The hyperparameter optimization for each algorithm was conducted via Bayesian optimization. Bayesian optimization is a probabilistic approach that leverages a surrogate model to approximate the objective function and efficiently search for optimal hyperparameters, particularly when evaluating the objective function is computationally expensive. In addition, an early stopping scheme was also adopted to prevent overfitting. The hyperparameter optimization process was terminated if the AUC-PR does not increase over 20 consecutive iterations.

Parameter space of the six machine learning models

The discrimination ability of the models was evaluated via the AUC-ROC. The final AUC-ROC values in the training set were obtained through 5-fold cross-validation. The best-performing model among all models was selected as the final model. A calibration curve and decision curve were adopted to evaluate the calibration performance and clinical utility of the models.

Variable significance

In the concise linear regression model, variable significance can be obtained easily via coefficients. However, the interpretability of ML models is a persistent issue in ML. In this study, we respectively employed the LinearExplainer and TreeExplainer from the SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) library to interpret linear models and tree-based models, which were used to quantify the contribution of each predictor to model output. Global feature importance was ranked according to the mean absolute SHAP value.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were expressed as the median and interquartile range and the differences between groups will be compared by a t-test or a non-parametric test. The categorical variables are expressed as the frequency (percentage) and were compared with the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. The outcomes examined in this study included 1-, 3-, and 5-year OS status. OS is defined as the interval from the date of initial breast cancer diagnosis to death from any cause or last follow-up.

All tests were two-sided, with a P value <0.05 indicating statistical significance. All statistical analyses were performed using R version 4.4.3.

Model training

The performances of all six models are presented in Figure 2. All the models performed excellently in the training set, and all the AUCs were between 0.7 and 0.8. SVM demonstrated the strongest predictive capability for 1-year survival (AUC: 0.7700), closely followed by linear regression (AUC: 0.7689) and elastic net (AUC: 0.7688) with comparable performance, while GBM, XGBoost, and RF showed relatively lower performance (AUC range: 0.7554–0.7560). The 3-year prognostic analysis revealed that XGBoost emerged as the top performer (AUC: 0.770) and the remaining five models all performed well (all AUCs >0.7600). When extending the timeframe to 5 years, XGBoost maintained its leading performance (AUC: 0.7472), despite minimal performance differences across models (AUC range: 0.7424–0.7472). Notably, the performance of XGBoost was not good in 1-year predictions but was the best in 3-year and 5-year predictions. Conversely, SVM ranked first in 1-year and fifth in 3-year predictions (0.7620), yet regained competitiveness in 5-year assessments (0.7451).

Model performance

Within all the six models, the SVM performed the best in 1-year survival [AUC: 0.770; 95% confidence interval (CI): 0.737–0.803] prediction (Figure 2, Figure 3A) and XGBoost performed the best in 3-year survival (AUC: 0.770; 95% CI: 0.739–0.802) and 5-year survival (AUC: 0.746; 95% CI: 0.709–0.783) prediction (Figure 2, Figure 3B,3C). Thus, these two models were selected as the final models for the corresponding survival prediction. The calibration curves from the validation set are presented in Figure 3D-3F. Numerical calibration was further assessed using the Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test. The 1-year SVM model showed acceptable calibration (χ2=13.292, df=8, P=0.10), and the 3-year XGBoost model also demonstrated acceptable calibration (χ2=11.326, df=8, P=0.18). In contrast, the 5-year XGBoost model showed evidence of suboptimal calibration (χ2=24.236, df=8, P=0.002), with slight overestimation of risk among patients in the low-risk range. Additionally, the results from the decision curve analysis confirmed that the final model provided a high net benefit (Figure 3G-3I).

Discussion

BCLM represents a critical progression stage with limited therapeutic options and poor prognosis. In this study, we analyzed data from 2,921 BCLM patients identified in the SEER database (2011–2020), addressing class imbalance through SMOTE oversampling combined with Bayesian optimization. Six ML models (linear regression, elastic net, RF, SVM, XGBoost, GBM) were trained and validated, ultimately demonstrating robust predictive performance: SVM achieved optimal 1-year survival prediction (AUC: 0.770), while XGBoost excelled for both 3-year (AUC: 0.770) and 5-year (AUC: 0.746) survival endpoints. Our findings elucidate key predictors of survival outcomes and demonstrate the potential clinical utility of ML frameworks in enhancing risk stratification and personalized treatment strategies for this vulnerable patient population.

This study systematically evaluated seven oversampling techniques (including SMOTE, ADASYN, SMOTEENN, etc.) for handling class imbalance in breast cancer lung metastasis prediction. While complex datasets with high dimensionality (80+ clinical variables), large sample size (638,159 patients), and temporal features (53.7% bone metastasis) pose significant challenges, our analysis demonstrated that traditional SMOTE and ADASYN consistently outperformed hybrid methods (SMOTEENN/SMOTETomek). This superiority stems from their ability to preserve critical clinical information without excessive data cleaning—SMOTE’s linear interpolation, despite theoretical noise risks in high dimensions, proved robust in real-world clinical data; ADASYN’s adaptive sampling effectively managed boundary-blurred regions without the over-cleaning drawbacks of hybrid approaches. These findings challenge the assumption that hybrid methods are universally superior, emphasizing that oversampling method selection must consider dataset-specific characteristics in medical prognostic modeling. Calibration curves and decision curve analysis further validated the clinical utility and practical applicability of our approach. In addition, in practical training, Bayesian optimization demonstrated significant efficiency advantages over traditional grid search, particularly in scenarios involving multi-algorithm optimization (e.g., seven models) and high-dimensional hyperparameter spaces. For example, when tuning the XGBoost model, grid search required evaluating hundreds of parameter combinations, whereas Bayesian optimization achieved near-optimal solutions in just dozens of iterations, reducing computational costs by nearly 90%. This efficient optimization capability provides critical technical support for the clinical translation of complex tumor models.

The study demonstrates a temporal decline in predictive performance for breast cancer lung metastasis outcomes. This degradation likely arises from unmeasured confounders in the SEER database, such as post-metastatic treatment shifts, emerging resistance, or unrecorded comorbidities. To address this, future models should integrate longitudinal follow-up data (e.g., dynamic treatment records, imaging biomarkers) and adopt time-staged prediction frameworks (e.g., separate modules for 1-, 3-, and 5-year outcomes) to enhance long-term prognostic accuracy.

For short-term predictions (1-year survival), simple models like linear regression and elastic net perform comparably to complex algorithms (e.g., XGBoost), as they primarily rely on direct clinical associations (e.g., tumor size, lymph node status). In contrast, long-term outcomes (3–5 years) benefit significantly from advanced methods (e.g., gradient boosting, SVM), which excel at capturing nonlinear, time-dependent interactions between treatment response, molecular subtypes, and distant metastasis progression. This finding reinforces the “algorithmic adaptability principle”: the choice of model complexity should align with the temporal scope and data characteristics, balancing interpretability for immediate clinical decisions and granularity for long-term prognostic insights.

In the current field of medical research, predicting the metastatic risk and prognosis of breast cancer patients has become an important research direction. Multiple studies have utilized various methods and datasets to construct predictive models aimed at enhancing the accuracy of clinical decision-making.

A research team developed a nomogram based on the SEER database to predict the risk of lung metastasis and prognosis in patients with different subtypes of breast cancer (13). The model identified multiple independent prognostic factors through multivariate Cox regression analysis and evaluated the predictive performance of the model through internal and external validations. The results demonstrated good predictive accuracy across different cohorts, with C-index and AUC values indicating its effectiveness. The simplicity and intuitiveness of the nomogram provide advantages in clinical applications. Another research team employed various ML algorithms, particularly the XGBoost algorithm, to predict the diagnosis and prognosis of breast cancer bone metastasis (21). The model achieved AUC values of 0.98 and 0.88 in the diagnostic and prognostic models, respectively, showcasing its excellent predictive accuracy. Analysis using SHAP values identified surgery as a key predictive factor, providing important insights for clinical decision-making (22). A separate study focused on predicting the prognosis of malignant pleural effusion (MPE) in breast cancer patients. By employing least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression and univariate Cox regression analyses, the study identified key clinical variables and constructed a nomogram model. The model demonstrated high AUC values in both the training and validation sets, with calibration and decision curve analyses indicating high net benefits in clinical applications. The “Brain Metastasis” study used the XGBoost algorithm to predict the survival rates of breast cancer patients with brain metastases (23). The results showed that the XGBoost model performed exceptionally well in predicting survival rates at 6 months, 1 year, 2 years, and 3 years, with AUC values of 0.824, 0.813, 0.800, and 0.803, respectively. In our study, multiple ML algorithms (including linear regression, elastic net, RF, SVM, XGBoost, and GBM) were employed to predict survival outcomes in breast cancer patients with lung metastases. Notably, while bone metastasis has been extensively studied in breast cancer research, predictive models specifically focusing on lung metastasis remain limited. Our findings demonstrate that XGBoost achieved optimal performance for 3-year and 5-year survival prediction, with AUC values of 0.770 and 0.746, respectively.

Building on these foundations, this study innovates by integrating sociodemographic, biological, and therapeutic factors. Utilizing a vast amount of data from the SEER database, a comprehensive predictive framework was constructed. This approach not only enhances predictive accuracy but also provides personalized insights into patient prognosis. By addressing data imbalance issues with techniques such as SMOTE, the robustness of the model is further improved. The contribution of this study lies in offering a more comprehensive and personalized predictive tool, aiding clinicians in formulating more precise treatment strategies. Future research could further integrate multimodal data and time-dependent features to enhance the predictive capabilities and clinical utility of the models.

SHAP analysis highlighted marital status, molecular subtype, and metastatic sites as major contributors to model predictions across different time horizons. These variables should be interpreted as predictive features rather than causal determinants of survival. Marital status may partly capture social support, healthcare access, or other unmeasured socioeconomic factors, whereas molecular subtype and bone/liver metastases may reflect tumor biology and metastatic burden. However, because the present study was based on retrospective registry data, these interpretations remain hypothesis-generating.

A study examined a cohort of 16,513 patients with metastatic breast cancer and found that unmarried patients had a significantly higher mortality risk than their married counterparts (24). Married individuals typically possess robust social support networks and financial resources, which not only enhance adherence to treatment strategies (e.g., surgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy) but also alleviate psychological distress through emotional support from spouses or children, thereby indirectly improving outcomes (25). Moreover, married patients exhibit mental health advantages: partner and familial support reduces depression and anxiety, mitigating inflammation triggered by psychological stress—a factor implicated in tumor progression (26). Concurrently, married individuals often adopt healthier lifestyles, including balanced diets, regular exercise, and routine medical check-ups, serving as protective mediators against cancer development. Marital status may serve as a proxy for social support, healthcare access, or unmeasured socioeconomic factors. However, this interpretation remains hypothesis-generating, and SHAP values should not be interpreted as evidence of causality.

Molecular subtypes were also an important contributor to model prediction. The relatively high SHAP importance of HR/HER2 subtype is clinically plausible, as molecular subtype is closely related to tumor biology and systemic treatment options in breast cancer. In particular, HR-positive disease may be associated with endocrine sensitivity, whereas HER2 status reflects the potential relevance of HER2-targeted therapy. Nevertheless, SEER does not provide detailed information on endocrine therapy, anti-HER2 therapy, treatment duration, Ki-67, resistance mechanisms, or disease progression. Therefore, the predictive contribution of HR+/HER2− status should be interpreted cautiously and should not be considered direct evidence of a specific biological mechanism.

The prognostic difference between breast cancer bone (median OS 22 months) and liver (median OS 14 months) metastases stems from distinct tumor-stroma interactions. In bone, bone marrow cells secrete CXCL12 to recruit CXCR4+ DTCs via CXCR4/CXCL12 axis (27); DTCs adhere to bone matrix/cells, entering dormancy or growth (28). Tumor cells secrete RANKL/TGF-β to activate osteoclasts, triggering bone resorption and releasing growth factors (e.g., IGF-1) (29), forming a “vicious cycle” that delays progression. In the liver, cancer cells secrete VEGF to enhance vascular permeability, adhering to hepatocytes post-extravasation. Liver stromal cells (e.g., hepatic stellate cells) secrete HGF to aid adaptation; EMT and HIF-regulated genes drive contact with hepatocytes, converting stromal cells to pro-tumor CAFs. The hepatic microenvironment may provide a favorable niche for metastatic tumor cell survival and progression (6). However, these potential biological mechanisms cannot be directly assessed using the SEER database and should therefore be considered hypothesis-generating. Therefore, these SHAP findings should be interpreted as predictive associations rather than mechanistic evidence.

Biologically, the emphasis on this specific molecular subtype (HR+/HER2−) and metastatic sites (bone/liver) reinforces their established prognostic relevance in breast cancer lung metastasis. These results may help generate hypotheses for future studies and may inform treatment decisions. Further validation in external cohorts with detailed treatment, psychosocial, molecular, and longitudinal follow-up data is needed before these predictors can be translated into clinical decision-support tools.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, an important limitation of this study is that the SEER database records metastatic sites only at the time of initial cancer diagnosis. This database limitation may restrict the generalizability of our findings to patients with metachronous lung metastasis after initial breast cancer treatment. Second, although we attempted to reduce bias by defining evaluable endpoints according to available follow-up, residual selection bias related to incomplete follow-up cannot be fully excluded. Third, although the 1-year and 3-year models showed acceptable calibration according to the Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test, the 5-year model demonstrated suboptimal calibration and slightly overestimated risk in the low-risk range. This finding suggests that longer-term prediction may be more sensitive to unmeasured time-dependent factors, such as subsequent systemic treatment, disease progression, and comorbidities, which are not fully captured in the SEER database. Future work should incorporate multimodal data integration, including radiomics features from imaging studies and liquid biopsy biomarkers, to enhance predictive accuracy. Developing time-dependent deep learning frameworks could address dynamic changes in disease progression and treatment response. Collaborative efforts to link SEER data with electronic health records may also improve the granularity of clinical covariates and enable real-world validation of these models.

Conclusions

In this SEER-based study, ML models exhibited clinically significant performance in predicting OS at 1, 3, and 5 years for patients with breast cancer lung metastasis. Among the algorithms assessed, the SVM demonstrated the highest discrimination for 1-year survival prediction, while the XGBoost algorithm achieved the numerically highest AUC in predicting 3- and 5-year survival. The reliability and potential clinical applicability of the final models were further corroborated by calibration and decision curve analyses. Interpretations based on SHAP highlighted marital status, molecular subtype—particularly HR and HER2 status—and the extent of metastatic involvement in bone and liver as significant factors influencing survival predictions. These results indicate that interpretable machine learning models may provide a preliminary framework for survival risk stratification. Nonetheless, given the retrospective nature of this registry-based study, further external validation and the integration of longitudinal treatment data, imaging, and molecular biomarkers are necessary before these models can be routinely implemented in clinical practice.

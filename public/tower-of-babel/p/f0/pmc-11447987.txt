How two-child policy affects cesarean section in women with advanced maternal age (AMA): using the Robson classification system
Authors: Wenting Tang, Daidi Zeng, Wanhua Wu, Shuzhen Wu, Yijing Ou, Yaoguang Huang, Lijuan Xiao, Suran Huang, Zhongjun Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesTo study the possible associations between advanced maternal age and cesarean section(CS) under the two child policy.MethodsThis study used a cohort study from Dongguan People’s Hospital in Guangdong Province, China from 2017 to 2020. The cohort was restricted to women aged ≥ 20 who give birth to babies with a gestational age of > 28 weeks and a weight of > 1000 g. Divide the advanced maternal age (AMA) pregnant women into two age groups: 35–39 years old and 40 years old or older age. We analyzed CS rate and CS contribution using the modified Robson classification system. Frequency of cesarean was determined for each group and compared by using χ2 and prevalence ratio.ResultsOverall, 47654 women were included, of which 7924 (16.63%) were between the ages of 35 and 39, and 1529 (3.21%) were aged 40 or older. The total CS rate is 40.64%, with 36.10% for mothers aged 20 to 34, 57.90% for women aged 35 to 40, and 64.75% for women aged 40 or older age. In the AMA groups (n = 9453), Robson group 2’ was the most common, followed by groups 5 and 10. Women at 40 years or older age were 3 times more likely to undergo a cesarean delivery in Robson group 1’, and 1.76 times more likely in group 10. The CS rate in group 2’ were statistically significantly higher in the very AMA group.ConclusionsThe CS rates increased noticeably with maternal age under the two child policy. Based on the modified Robson classification system, AMA women should pay more attention to primiparous women with single pregnancy, uterine scars, and premature birth in multiple pregnancies.Keywords: Advanced maternal age, Two-child policy, Cesarean section, Robson classification system

Introduction

Cesarean section(CS) is a common way of delivery, which is an important method to solve pregnancy complications and timely termination of pregnancy. CS with medical indications can effectively reduce maternal and perinatal mortality and the incidence of other related diseases [1, 2]. However, CS is an operation, which will have short-term and long-term complications, such as the postpartum hemorrhage, infection of incision, scar pregnancy and pelvic adhesion. Therefore, CS rate become one of the important indicators of obstetric quality management, especially for without any medical or obstetric surgery indications of social factors CS.

From 1970s in China, the one-child family planning policy was introduced to control population growth. Under this policy, absolute safety was emphasized during delivery; hence, many pregnant women choose CS during their first delivery, ignoring the associated risks and potential health consequences of CS. The World Health Organization (WHO) surveys showed that the average rate of CS in Asia was 27.3%, of which China had a disproportionately high rate of CS (46.2%) [3]. The one-child policy has led to a decrease in the young population and an increase in the elderly population, which might threaten China’s economic growth. The universal two-child policy was not enforced until January 2016 [4].

Advanced maternal age (AMA) is generally defined as pregnancy in women age 35 years or older. Following the universal two-child policy, many researches have been conducted on the willingness of women of childbearing age to have a second child and its related influencing factors. AMA women with economic foundations prefer to have a second child. However, to date, there has been no qualitative research on the experience of CS, especially for AMA.

The rise in fertility rates has brought new challenges to obstetricians Reduce CS rate has become the consensus of maternity industry and even the entire society. Choose a reasonable and effective classification method to evaluation, monitoring and management of CS is critical. Systematic reviews have evaluated different CS classifications. The Robson ten group classification system, as proposed by Robson in 2001, was suggested by the WHO as the standard instrument to evaluate and monitor CS in different hospital services [5, 6].

In this study, we estimated the relationship between the rate of CS according to women’s age and the universal two-child policy, utilizing the Robson classification system. It help better understanding the real situation of CS indications and the profile of childbirths performed in the investigated institution.

Materials and methods

The present retrospective cross-sectional study assessed data from all women who delivered at the Dongguan People’s Hospital, Guangdong, China, between January 1, 2017, and December 31, 2020. Dongguan People’s Hospital is one of the largest tertiary maternal care centers in Dongguan, with 10,000 to 15,000 deliveries annually. The research protocol was approved prior to data collection by the Research Ethics Committee of the investigated institution.

The cohort was restricted to women ≥ 20 years of age who gave birth to a infant of > 28 weeks’gestation and weighing > 1000 g. The hospital’s information engineers extracted data from the electronic medical record system according to criteria set forth on the standardized data collection form. The research team will check the Data and any questions concerning information in the medical record were addressed. The following variables were retrieved: maternal age, number of fetuses, the way of conceive, parity, gestational age, mode of deliver, number of previous cesarean sections, presence of chronic hypertension, diabetes mellitus type 1 and 2, cardiac disease, and presence of gestational hypertension, preeclampsia, gestational diabetes, intrauterine growth restriction, premature rupture of membrane, placenta previa, or placental abruption.

The Original Robson classification system divides pregnant women into 10 categories. However, there is still a lack of recognized and clear definitions of certain key variables globally, such as natural labor, induced labor, and oxytocin application. Liang [7] modified the ten classifications of the Original Robson classification system, elimination of classification by mode of labor, and then merge Groups 1 and 2, and Groups 3 and 4. The original and modified Robson groups are presented in Table 1 [7]. Combining groups can effectively avoid information bias caused by conceptual ambiguity and subjective factors that interfere with grouping by clinical medical personnel or researchers. Data for the present study was classified into modified Robson groups. Then, records within all modified Robson groups were stratified into three maternal age groups: 20 to 34, 35 to 40, and > 40 years of age.

Data were stored in Excel 2013 (Microsoft, Redmond, WA, USA) and analyzed by SPSS Statistics for Windows, version 17 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). The categorical variables were given as numbers and percentages. The chi-squared test was used to calculate differences between modified Robson groups in 20 to 34, 35 to 40, and > 40 years of age group. When more than 1/5 of the theoretical frequency is less than 5, nonparametric test was used to analyze. A P value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

In total, there were 47,654 women who had a live birth in the study period 2017–2020 that were included in the analysis. Table 2 shows maternal and obstetric characteristics of the study population by maternal age group. Of these women, 38,201 (80.16%) were not of advanced maternal age, 7,924 (16.63%) were of advanced maternal age, and 1,529 (3.21%) were of very advanced maternal age. The three maternal age groups differed with regards to multiple pregnancy, to have conceived after in vitro fertilization (IVF), gestational age, parity and number of previous caesarean section. Pregnant women in both AMA groups were more likely to have slightly higher to have conceived after IVF, twins and premature birth, whereas they were less likely to previous caesarean section, and less often nulliparous when compared with the reference group. The AMA groups were more likely to have slightly higher to internal medicine diseases, such as chronic hypertension, diabetes mellitus, gestational hypertension, preeclampsia and gestational diabetes, especially in women > 40 years of age.

Discussion

The present study evaluated 47,654 women who gave birth in Dongguan People’s Hospital in 2017–2020 showed that the total number of births decreases with year, and of whom mothers of AMA also decreases. However, CS rates did not decreases with year. Although there was a decline in the early stage of the two-child policy, it reached a peak in 2020. The COVID-19 pandemic broke out in 2020. Although it is not an indication of cesarean section, it has a certain impact on the delivery situation and cesarean section rate because of people’s panic psychology [8].

China has one of the highest rates of CS in the world, together with Brazil (55.6%), Cyprus (52.2%), Egypt (51.7%) and the Dominican Republic (59.6%) [9–12]. The overall CS rates in this study was 40.64%, which is still far higher than the WHO stated data (10–15%), as the same (40.6%) in other study in China after the two-child policy [13]. According to the 2010 WHO global survey, China has the highest cesarean rate (46.2%) in Asia under the one child policy [3], and it also increased (47.6%) after relaxating the one child policy in 2015 [6]. The distribution of women in modified Robson groups at the universal two-child policy showed that CS rate drop may be associated with an increased the composition of multiparous women, and more women chose vaginal birth, especially non-AMA women.

Fertility policy changes have also led to increased of AMA women, of whom have more wishes to have a second child because of their economic basis. CS rates were higher among mothers of advanced age, significantly increasing from 36.10% for younger mothers aged 20 to 34 to 57.90% in women aged 35 to 40 and to 64.75% for those aged over 40 in 2017–2020. CS rates increased noticeably with maternal age. Both physical conditions and psychological factors might explain the increasing rate of CS among women of AMA. There were statistically significant differences in chronic hypertension, gestational diabetes and diabetes mellitus. Recent studies have indicated that pregnant women with pregnancy complications and chronic metabolic disorders are other possible indicators of CS in women of AMA [14]. Women for aged over 40 can reduce the risk of complications by improving the way of lifestyle, such as changes in eating habits and proper exercise.

In addition, maternal request for cesarean delivery was attributable to the high rates in China, particularly among women of AMA [15]. These women are likely to worry if they can safely weather gestation and birth, and grow concerned about the safety of the foetus and the baby. On the other hand, women’s age is strongly linked to fertility. As a woman ages, her fertility decreases [16, 17]. We found that AMA women were statistically significant differences in IVF pregnancy, especially for those aged over 40. Studied showed that older maternal age and pregnancy after IVF were associated with more CS [18, 19].

Under the one child policy, as women realized that they would not have a second child and fear of labor pains may affect the decision regarding the mode of delivery, maternal request for a cesarean section was more common. A scarred uterus after a prior CS is a strong indicator of a repeat CS. With the implementation of the universal two-child policy, our findings showed that women in modified Robson group 5 (51.03%) was the largest contributor to the overall CS rate. Therefore, reducing the rate of CS during the first delivery is essential to reducing the rate of CS among multiparae.

To further analysis, it showed that the distribution of women aged 35 or older in Modified Robson group 5 was 2-fold than women aged 20 to 34. A recent study reported that women with a scarred uterus preferred a CS, and that was especially true for women of AMA [20]. In order to solve the problems of the scar uterus pregnancy childbirth way again, Chinese experts have advocated for vaginal delivery after cesarean (VBAC) as a safer and more cost-effective alternative for second delivery, so as to control the CS rate [21], which is consistent with foreign advocacy [22]. Chinese officials promoted various programs including prenatal assessment and emergency first-aid measures, strict maternal-fetal monitoring in labor and the training of doctors in the risks associated with repeat cesarean section.

Our finding indicated the proportion of a primary cesarean delivery increased with age for primiparous women, as showed in modified Robson group 1’, which reached 77.50% for women aged over 40. Currently cesarean delivery guide did not mention the age can be used as a cesarean section indications. However, AMA women are more likely to maternal request for CS, especially for their first child. Our study also showed that modified Robson group 10 was significantly higher in women aged over 40 than women aged 20 to 34 and 35 to 40, suggesting that preterm birth are more likely to happen in older women. The result demonstrated that chronic hypertension, gestational hypertension, preeclampsia and other health conditions were associated with a further increase in CS rate.

Our study had certain limitations which are worth mentioning. The study design was retrospective. Our data analysis is based on a single-center tertiary hospital, which is the potential selection bias in this study. Moreover, because of Dongguan is a city mainly export-oriented economy, the floating population is much, in our hospital childbirth pregnant women also produce has the corresponding characteristics. Therefore, our results cannot be generalized to the whole population and pregnant women living in other regions of China.

Conclusion

In summary, under the one-child policy, unusually high CS rate of long-term effects of maternal and child health had appeared. As the change of two-child policy, this study found CS rates increased noticeably with maternal age, followed with increasing numbers of multiparous women with a scarred uterus. AMA women was significantly associated with cesarean delivery, mainly for women classified in modified Robson group 1’, 5, and 10. Robson classification system in the monitoring rate of hospital cesarean section has a good applicability, which can help clinical doctors for a particular category of maternal setting indiidualized treatment strategies to cope with rising cesarean delivery rate and to improve maternal and infant prognosis.

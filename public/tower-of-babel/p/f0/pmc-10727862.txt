Locked-in Scale: Full Manifestation of Lamellar Ichthyosis in an Adult with Serious Physical and Social Impairments
Authors: Dea Kwan Yun, Kyujin Yeom, Uri Shon, Mi Soo Choi, Byung Cheol Park, Myung Hwa Kim
Published: 2023

Dear Editor:

Autosomal recessive congenital ichthyosis (ARCI) represents a type of erythroderma, widespread skin scaling at birth, with a reported prevalence of ~1 in 300,000 persons1. Lamellar ichthyosis (LI) is an ARCI disorder. Gene mutations in transglutaminase-1 (TGM1) and ATP-binding cassette A12 (ABCA12) were frequently reported, especially in Asia. Affected newborns are encased in a collodion membrane, shed within a month, whereupon thick, plate-like scales separated by superficial, dry-riverbed fissuring appear; LI’s characteristic features1,2. In Korea, four patients were reported through three case reports. All were pediatric patients and diagnosed clinically and histologically. But, genetic test was not performed for all cases2,3.

A 39-year-old woman presented with extensive white, plate-like scales over her body. She was born in a collodion membrane and developed scaling over time. No therapy was administered for her skin disease because her mother was informed of no cure. She was homebound and socially inactive, despite her average intelligence. She had no specific family history. She had severe keratoderma, universal scarring alopecia, nail abnormalities, ectropion, eclabium, finger joint contractures, and an ear helix deformity (Fig. 1). She suffered from hypohidrosis, severe heat intolerance, and sensorineural and conductive mixed-type hearing loss but no visual disturbances nor intellectual disabilities. Histopathologic examination revealed marked compact orthohyperkeratosis and mild acanthosis with a diminished granular cell layer. Genetic analysis revealed a homozygous c.427C>T (p.Arg143Cys) mutation in the TGM1 gene; a known LI pathogenic variant. Clinical, pathological, and genetic features led to autosomal recessive LI diagnosis. Oral retinoids, r-linolenic acid, and moisturizing lotion treatments partially improved her skin lesions. Due to the patient’s ophthalmic and otologic problems, she received regular examination and conservative treatment using lubricants such as eye and ear drops. The patient’s depression was not treated but gradually improved upon skin improvement. In addition, we recommended appropriate education and socialization, but the patient showed no will to change the lifestyle.

(A) The skin develops large, plate-like scales over the whole body. (B) The scales tend to be larger over the lower extremities, resembling a mosaic pattern separated by superficial fissuring. (C~F) The patient also had universal scarring alopecia, ectropion, eclabium, ear helix deformity, severe palmoplantar keratoderma, finger joint contractures, and nail abnormalities. (G) Large, plate-like scales separated by superficial fissuring cause the skin to look like a dry riverbed. We received the patient’s consent form about publishing all photographic materials.

Persistent thick scales could occlude hair follicles, sweat ducts, and external auditory canals and stiffen the skin. LI complications include scarring alopecia, hypohidrosis, heat intolerance, ectropion, eclabium, joint contraction or deformity, and conductive mixed-type hearing loss. LI severity and complications vary by patient and this patient presented with severe clinical LI with complications due to the absence of treatment.

The TGM1 enzyme links precursor proteins, loricrin, involucrin, and small proline-rich proteins, and mutations may lead to impaired barrier function due to defective intercellular lipid layers. There are several reported mutations in the TGM1 gene. Depending on the type of mutation, a different presentation may be exhibited4. Our patient’s severe clinical features may be caused by the detected TGM1 gene mutation.

LI severity can psychologically impact patients, causing depression and low quality of life. In one report, 80% of the LI patients presented with depression5. Long-term, comprehensive, and multidisciplinary treatment may restore her physical and social functioning.

We experienced an adult LI patient who lived in “locked-in scale” status, experiencing social and physical impairments due to thick scale. We report this case to emphasize focusing on psychosocial dysfunctions as well as on skin disease itself when treating patients.

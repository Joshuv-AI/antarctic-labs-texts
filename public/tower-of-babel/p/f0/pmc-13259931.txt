A Novel Pathogenic CHD7 Splice-Site Variant in a Neonate with CHARGE Syndrome Identified by Blood RNA Analysis
Authors: Sooho Yu, Ja-Hyun Jang, Misun Yang, Yun Sil Chang, Hyeseon Kim, Mi-Ae Jang
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

CHD7 encodes chromodomain helicase DNA-binding protein 7, an ATP-dependent chromatin remodeler essential for developmental gene regulation [1, 2]. Loss-of-function CHD7 variants cause CHARGE syndrome, an autosomal dominant disorder characterized by coloboma, congenital heart defects, choanal atresia, growth retardation, genital hypoplasia, and distinct ear anomalies (OMIM #214800). CHD7 variants are identified in over half of clinically diagnosed cases [3]; however, interpreting variants outside canonical splice sites remains challenging. A recent study emphasized the importance of blood RNA analysis in clarifying the pathogenicity of such variants [4]. In our study, we report a novel variant in a noncanonical donor splice region of CHD7 in a neonate with CHARGE syndrome. Initial in silico predictions suggested that the variant disrupts splicing, while blood RNA analysis confirmed cryptic splice-site activation, leading to classification of the variant as “pathogenic.” Our study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Samsung Medical Center (2025-03-144-001), Seoul, Korea. Informed consent for genetic testing was obtained from the patient’s parents.

A term male infant was born at 38+1 weeks of gestation, with Apgar scores of 6, 6, and 8 at 1, 5, and 10 mins, respectively. Birth weight was 2,660 g (3rd–10th percentile), and length was 45.5 cm (10th–25th percentile), consistent with “small for gestational age.” Mild ventriculomegaly was observed upon prenatal ultrasonography. Immediately after birth, he exhibited weak crying, irregular respiration, and inspiratory stridor, necessitating admission to the neonatal intensive care unit. Prominent suprasternal retractions, a high-arched palate, mild hypotonia, and weak primitive reflexes were noted during physical examination. Feeding-related desaturation suggested laryngomalacia; however, the coexistence of growth restriction, hypotonia, and ventriculomegaly raised suspicion of an underlying genetic disorder.

Trio whole-genome sequencing helped identify a novel heterozygous CHD7 variant, NM_017780.4(CHD7):c.5894+4A>G (chr8:g.61764810A>G, GRCh37), in intron 29 (Fig. 1A) as the most likely disease-causing variant. In silico analysis using SpliceAI [5] predicted both exon skipping (donor loss score: 0.56, threshold ≥0.2) and creation of a cryptic donor site (donor gain score: 0.63) (Fig. 1B), suggesting a potential impact on splicing. To assess its functional impact, RNA extracted from the patient’s blood was subjected to reverse transcription (RT-)PCR and sequencing. cDNA was synthesized, and primers were designed to flank CHD7 exons 27 through 31 (forward primer, targeting exon 27: 5′-CCAACCAGAACACGGTTCAAG-3′; reverse primer, targeting the exons 30 and 31 junction: 5′-GCGGTTCATCATCTGGCTTG-3′). Upon cDNA amplification, a distinct band approximately 100 bps larger than expected was observed (Fig. 1C). Sanger sequencing helped confirm the insertion of 91 intronic nucleotides originating from intron 29 (r.5898_5899ins[5894+5_5894+93;gg]). The insertion site was designated r.5898_5899, per the 3′ rule (https://hgvs-nomenclature.org). This insertion is predicted to result in a frameshift and premature termination codon (p.[Thr 1967Valfs*37]) and expected to trigger nonsense-mediated mRNA decay given its location upstream from the 38-exon CHD7 transcript (Fig. 1D and 1E).

The aberrant splicing identified through blood RNA analysis enabled application of the PVS1 (RNA) criterion in accordance with the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics and Association for Molecular Pathology guidelines for splice variant interpretation [6]. The variant was further determined to have arisen de novo (PS2) and is absent from the gnomAD v.4.1 database (PM2_P), providing additional evidence of its pathogenicity. Collectively, these findings supported classification of this novel CHD7 variant as “pathogenic” [7].

CHD7-related disorders, including CHARGE syndrome, exhibit highly variable clinical features, even among individuals carrying the same pathogenic variants [8]. Blake et al. [9] proposed useful diagnostic criteria. Further evaluation of our patient revealed bilateral ocular colobomas, congenital laryngomalacia, borderline hearing response in the left ear, and multiple patent foramen ovales, supporting the CHARGE syndrome diagnosis.

RNA splicing largely contributes to pathogenic genetic variation; however, accurate interpretation, particularly of noncanonical splice-site variants, is often limited by insufficient evidence. Blood RNA analysis offers a valuable solution. RT-PCR-based Sanger sequencing has been used to confirm exon-skipping caused by intronic +4 or deep intronic variants in other genes [10, 11]. Indeed, Wai et al. [4] demonstrated that approximately one-third of variants of uncertain significance are associated with abnormal splicing, highlighting the importance of RNA analysis. They also showed that informative RT-PCR results can be obtained even for genes with relatively low expression in blood [4]. Although CHD7 exhibits a transcript per million value of only 3.417 in blood according to the Genotype-Tissue Expression portal (https://www.gtexportal.org), RNA analysis in our patient was successful. Nevertheless, tissue specificity remains an inherent limitation when using blood for analyzing splicing events. This case demonstrates the value of RNA analysis in resolving the clinical significance of novel variants.

We report a novel, experimentally-confirmed pathogenic noncanonical splice-site variant in CHD7, expanding the spectrum of CHD7 variants and highlighting the importance of RNA analysis in resolving variants of uncertain significance.

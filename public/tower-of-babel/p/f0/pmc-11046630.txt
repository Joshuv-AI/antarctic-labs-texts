Pembrolizumab-induced diffuse myositis in a patient of metastatic colon cancer: a case report
Authors: Md Mazharul Islam
Published: 2023

Background

Pembrolizumab is an immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI), an anti-programmed cell death 1 antibody, that has revolutionised oncological treatment. ICIs have been associated with some adverse effects including myositis, myasthenia gravis (MG), myocarditis, dermatitis, colitis, pneumonitis, thyroiditis etc. We report the case of a 78-year-old woman with known metastatic colon cancer who developed diffuse myositis after 18 months of starting pembrolizumab.

Case

A 78-year-old woman presented to hospital with the complaints of bilateral weakness in both lower limbs for 1 week. She was diagnosed with metastatic colon cancer 2 years ago and was started on pembrolizumab.

She had an excellent response to it but had adrenal insufficiency during her treatment. Her other medical history included Parkinson's disease, coronary artery bypass grafting, bronchial asthma, chronic kidney disease and hypothyroidism, for which she was on levothyroxine, simvastatin, hydrocortisone tablet, rasagiline, rotigotine patch and steroid inhalers. She was reasonably well 7 days before hospital admission. She took her last cycle of pembrolizumab 9 days earlier before symptoms started. Initially, she noted difficulties in mobilising both of her lower limbs and finally she could not get out of bed. She did not give any history of upper respiratory tract infections or diarrhea before the event. There were no features of upper limb weakness, swallowing difficulties, shortness of breath, drooping eyelids, change of voice, skin rash or tightening, joint pain, bladder bowel abnormalities or Raynaud's phenomenon mentioned.

On examination, muscle power of the extensors of the hip was 4/5, flexors 2/5, abductors 3/5 and adductors 4/5, while muscle power of flexors and extensors of the knees were 4/5. The rest of the muscle power of the lower limbs and the upper limbs were normal. Both ankle and knee jerks were present, and no sensory abnormalities were detected. Systemic examinations were unremarkable.

Blood tests revealed markedly raised creatinine phosphokinase (CPK) 22000iU/L, raised alanine aminotransferase (ALT) 617 iU/L and eGFR was dropped from 31 to 23. Antinuclear antibody (ANA), antinuclear cytoplasmic antibody (ANCA), and myositis antibody levels came back normal. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the femur – including the thoraco lumbar spine – was arranged, which showed diffuse myositis in thigh muscles along with minimal impingement on L5 and S1 nerve roots. A nerve conduction study described evidence of sensory-motor axonal neuropathy but no features of muscle disease on the electromyography. An electrocardiogram showed normal sinus rhythm.

Pembrolizumab was stopped. Intravenous methylprednisolone was started followed by high dose oral prednisolone 1 mg/kg body weight with gradual tapering. She had an excellent response after starting steroids. CPK and ALT gradually came back to normal. In addition, adequate physiotherapy was ensured. She was discharged from the hospital and transferred for rehabilitation. It was planned to review her in outpatient rheumatology clinic.

Conclusion

ICI-myositis is a rare but severe adverse event. These often respond to discontinuation of therapy and initiation of steroids. Intravenous immunoglobulin and plasma exchange are generally reserved for patients whose disease fails to respond to steroid therapy. Clinicians should keep an eye on such events and take appropriate interventions rapidly to optimise patient safety and outcomes.

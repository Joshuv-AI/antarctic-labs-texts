Deep learning analysis for enhanced prediction of heat transfer in Maxwell hybrid nanofluids with non-Fourier law and radiation effects
Authors: Norah Salem Alsaiar, Muhammad Imran, Maleha Rukhsar, Amir Hussain, Syed Tauseef Saeed, Jihad Younis, M. S. Al-Buriahi, Imed Boukhris
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Nanofluids have emerged as promising heat transfer media due to their superior thermal performance associated to conventional fluids, yet their behavior under mutual impacts of radiation, heat source/sink, and magnetic fields remains an active area of research. In this study, movement and heat transfer characteristics of a Maxwell-based hybrid nanofluid containing carbon nanotubes are examined over a stretching/shrinking sheet with slip boundary conditions and Cattaneo–Christov heat flux. Governing nonlinear partial differential equations (PDEs) are reduced through similarity transformations and resolved numerically by means of MATLAB bvp4c routine. Numerical outcomes are analyzed for the effects of important parameters such as magnetic field strength, Biot number, velocity slip, and thermal radiation over velocity and temperature distributions. For the investigated parameter ranges (magnetic parameter 0.1–1.2, radiation parameter 0.3–0.9, and Biot number 0–0.8), thermal radiation and Biot number are found to enhance heat transfer rates, while increasing magnetic effects suppress the velocity field. The hybrid nanofluid consistently exhibits improved thermal performance compared to the corresponding nanofluid case. To complement numerical framework, artificial neural networks (ANNs) are implemented as surrogate predictive models. A multilayer perceptron structure qualified with Levenberg–Marquardt algorithm (LMA) is employed, with datasets from bvp4c distributed into 70% for training, 15% for validation, and 15% for testing. ANN demonstrates outstanding predictive performance, achieving mean squared error (MSE) values as low as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\,6.9182 \times 10^{ - 12} \,$$\end{document} for velocity gradients and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\,1.4653 \times 10^{ - 11} \,$$\end{document} for temperature profiles, with correlation coefficients of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\,R \approx 1\,$$\end{document} across all training, validation, and test subsets. These results confirm that ANN models can accurately reproduce the nonlinear boundary-layer solutions with negligible error. The combined numerical–ANN approach establishes a novel and computationally efficient framework for analyzing hybrid nanofluid flows. The outcomes highlight the strong influence of magnetic and thermal parameters on stream and heat transfer features and demonstrate ability of ANNs to serve as reliable predictive models for complex nonlinear systems.

Research gap and novelty

Despite the extensive studies on nanofluids and hybrid nanofluids for improving heat transfer efficiency, most of the existing literature relies heavily on conventional numerical solvers such as the shooting technique, Keller-box scheme, or the MATLAB bvp4c routine. While these approaches are powerful, they often demand high computational effort and may become impractical for real-time applications or parametric studies involving wide ranges of governing parameters. Furthermore, only a limited number of works have integrated artificial intelligence, particularly ANNs, with nonlinear fluid flow and heat transfer models involving nanoparticles, magnetic fields, radiation, and slip conditions. The role of ANNs as predictive surrogate models for these highly nonlinear differential systems has not been systematically explored in the context of MHD hybrid nanofluid movement above stretching or shrinking surfaces. Recent studies have introduced physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) and hybrid numerical–learning solvers for modeling nonlinear fluid flow and heat transfer problems. Artificial neural networks (ANN) methodology and its purpose

ANNs have emerged as reliable computational techniques for modeling nonlinear transport processes, including fluid flow with nanoparticles over basic geometries such as flat or stretching plates. In the existing study, governing equations for velocity & temperature fields in a nanofluid boundary-layer configuration are solved numerically using MATLAB routine bvp4c, and the obtained data are then utilized to train ANN model. The multilayer perceptron (MLP) network is adopted because of its feed-forward back-propagation structure, which is well-suited for capturing the nonlinear effects of nanoparticle interactions in the flow. A feed-forward neural network (FFNN) was employed in this study due to its effectiveness in modeling nonlinear relationships in regression-based thermo-fluid problems. Since the dataset consists of numerically generated parametric inputs without spatial or temporal dependencies, more complex architectures such as convolutional or recurrent networks were not required. The selected architecture provides stable training and accurate prediction while maintaining computational efficiency. The general architecture of the network is displayed in Fig. 5a, showing the input, hidden, and output layers. Input layer represents physical parameters and similarity variables of nanofluid flow, while hidden layer consists of neurons activated by nonlinear transfer functions to process the data. The number of hidden layers was determined through preliminary trials by gradually increasing the depth of the network and selecting the configuration that provided stable convergence and minimum prediction error without overfitting. The output layer provides the predicted quantities such as velocity gradient and temperature profile along the plate3,54.Fig. 5(a) Architecture of ANN with associated training performance evaluations. Through this formulation, the ANN model accurately captures the nonlinear relationship between hybrid nanoparticle effects, thermal boundary conditions, and the flow geometry, demonstrating its effectiveness in approximating the velocity and thermal fields of nanofluid boundary-layer systems.

The selection of these specific input features and output targets is justified by the physical requirements of the thermal transport problem. The input features encompass the primary governing variables that define the flow physics and thermal state of the Maxwell fluid. By including parameters such as the magnetic field, radiation, and curvature, the network is forced to learn the sensitivity of the boundary layer to external physical constraints. The selection of the Skin Friction Coefficient and Nusselt Number as targets is justified because these are the primary metrics for evaluating aerodynamic drag and heat transfer efficiency in industrial cooling applications. This specific input-target mapping allows the ANN to serve as a high-speed surrogate model for complex fluidic systems without the need for repetitive numerical integrations.

It should be noted that the near-unity regression coefficients observed in this study arise from the deterministic and noise-free nature of the numerically generated dataset and the restricted range of governing parameters. The ANN was evaluated using independent training, validation, and testing subsets, which reduces the likelihood of overfitting. Therefore, the reported results demonstrate accurate interpolation within the studied parameter space rather than unrestricted generalization beyond it.

Beyond prediction accuracy, the trained ANN also provides qualitative insight into the relative importance of physical parameters governing heat transfer enhancement. Parameters directly associated with energy exchange mechanisms, such as the Biot number and thermal radiation parameter, exhibit stronger influence on temperature prediction due to their role in controlling surface convection and radiative heat diffusion. In contrast, magnetic and velocity slip parameters primarily affect momentum transport and indirectly modify heat transfer through boundary-layer suppression. The thermodynamic physical and thermal relaxation parameters govern the rate of heat propagation and energy storage within the fluid, explaining their dominant contribution to non-Fourier heat transfer behavior. These observations indicate that parameters directly influencing energy transport dominate thermal enhancement, while flow-control parameters play a secondary but complementary role.

The findings of this research have direct practical applications in modern thermal engineering. Specifically, the Maxwell-based hybrid nanofluid model can be utilized to improve the efficiency of solar energy harvesters, automotive cooling systems (using engine oil as a base), and advanced lubrication for manufacturing equipment. The proposed ANN framework provides a rapid, cost-effective tool for engineers to monitor and control heat transfer performance in these high-precision industrial applications.

Limitations of the study

Despite the promising results, this study has certain limitations. First, the ANN model is trained using numerically generated data, and its predictive accuracy depends on the quality and range of the simulation dataset. Second, the model predictions are reliable within the investigated parameter space, and extrapolation beyond this range may reduce accuracy. Third, the present work focuses on a specific hybrid nanofluid and flow configuration; therefore, the findings may not be directly generalized to other nanofluid combinations or geometries. Finally, experimental validation is not considered in this study and may be explored in future work.

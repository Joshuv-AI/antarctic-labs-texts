Refining implementation determinants and strategies to scale an electronic assessment option for coordinating family-centred care in early intervention
Authors: Dianna L Bosak, Lauren Frame, Julie Bobitt, Chelsea Guillen, Mary A Khetani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundYoung Children’s Participation and Environment Measure (YC-PEM) is a promising electronic patient-reported outcome (e-PRO) that could support family-centred care during service plan development in early intervention (EI), but prior implementation research has been limited to a single programme and state despite state level variability in EI administration. This study aims to identify shared and localised determinants and strategies for scaling YC-PEM e-PRO implementation in EI systems across multiple programmes and states.MethodsThis study used an explanatory-sequential mixed-methods case study design to engage EI stakeholders (leaders (n=4), providers (n=19) and caregivers (n=13)) across two of three geographical bands in the case state. Data were collected via virtual semi-structured interviews and focus groups, with stakeholders first rating and then explaining their responses to how helpful and easy it would be to implement the YC-PEM e-PRO option within their routine EI workflow. Transcripts were content analysed using a hybrid deductive and inductive approach to yield one analytical memo per EI stakeholder group that underwent team review and participant validation, followed by mapping of memo content to an established implementation research logic model that had been populated with content specific to another state.ResultsAll 37 EI stakeholders rated the YC-PEM e-PRO option as helpful, while most stakeholders rated it as somewhat or not easy to implement within their EI workflow. They confirmed the continued relevance of nine known determinants (four supports, five barriers) and introduced four new determinants (two supports, two barriers) for localised implementation in their state. Stakeholders confirmed and further refined the scope of two strategies and introduced one new potential strategy to support its localised implementation within their state.ConclusionsBoth shared and localised determinants and corresponding strategies are relevant to advance YC-PEM e-PRO implementation across state and local EI programmes with varied data system capabilities, organisational readiness and provider resources.Trial registration numberNCT04562038.Keywords: Occupational therapy, Qualitative research, Caregivers, Child Health, Infant

WHAT IS ALREADY KNOWN ON THIS TOPIC

WHAT THIS STUDY ADDS

Findings confirm and extend current evidence on the range of known determinants and strategies for scaling YC-PEM electronic patient-reported outcome (e-PRO) implementation across multiple states that vary in how they administer early intervention services for families of young children experiencing developmental challenges. This study confirms the relevance of nine known determinants: (1) availability of versatile formats and clear navigation instructions (facilitator); (2) support for collaboration and more efficient planning meetings (facilitator); (3) ability to capture family priorities (facilitator); (4) performance comparable to usual care (facilitator); (5) inadequate automation for report completion and sharing (barrier); (6) high productivity demands that limit capacity for adopting new practices (barrier); (7) high staff turnover that increases training demands (barrier); (8) family opt-out despite potential benefit (barrier); and (9) the need for versions accessible to diverse populations (barrier). In addition, four new determinants were identified: (1) the ability to quantify data to track child progress and resource needs (facilitator); (2) structured reports that enhance interdisciplinary collaboration (facilitator); (3) lack of consensus regarding optimal timing for administration (barrier); and (4) limited technology or data-capture system capacity (barrier).

Approximately 50 million children aged 0–5 experience disability worldwide,1 highlighting the need to invest in early childhood development (ECD) service systems that detect and intervene to maximise their societal inclusion.2 Early intervention (EI) is a cornerstone of ECD, outlining shared principles for high-value services.3 EI aims to improve children’s motor, cognitive and adaptive skills, and build families’ capacity to facilitate their young child’s participation in valued activities that promote skill development and societal inclusion.4 In the USA, EI is a federally funded, state-administered programme providing developmental support to eligible infants and toddlers. EI eligibility is based on developmental need, such as a child’s diagnosis (eg, cerebral palsy), developmental delay (eg, motor delay) based on standardised developmental test scores, or when deemed at risk for developmental delay (eg, very low birth weight).5

Statewide EI systems follow the shared principle of coordinating family-centred care (FCC), which is an approach to planning and delivering services that endorses shared decision-making and continuous, effective communication with families to ensure care is responsive to their needs and priorities.6 States have flexibility to organise their services to align with their structures and resources, such as differences in their lead agency, use of contracted service providers and how their professionals enact their workflows through which they routinely plan and deliver EI services.7 This variation underscores the need for versatile solutions that centre family needs, interests and priorities and that can be scaled across multiple states. One such solution is using electronic patient-reported outcomes (e-PROs), which capture caregiver-reported expertise to coordinate FCC when planning and delivering EI services. As EI programmes adopt electronic data capture systems,8 e-PROs offer a practical way to ensure that EI service plans are responsive to family needs, interests and priorities.

The Young Children’s Participation and Environment Measure (YC-PEM) is a promising e-PRO measure for guiding FCC in EI. The YC-PEM is a questionnaire that provides a valid, reliable and feasible way to capture and share caregiver expertise about their young child’s participation in three settings: home, daycare/preschool and community. In each setting, caregivers appraise their child’s current and desired participation in relevant activities (ie, across 13 home, 3 daycare/preschool and 11 community activities), perceived environmental supports and barriers to the child’s current participation in that setting, and the strategies they have used to facilitate participation when desiring change. The YC-PEM has a mean completion time of 21.3 (12.3–29.9) min.9 Caregivers completing the YC-PEM can readily share information about challenging routines they would like to prioritise in the EI service plan, and this information can also be aggregated to help EI programmes identify common unmet needs across families served by the programme and how their services are associated with improved outcomes from the caregiver perspective.10,12 YC-PEM e-PRO content was developed and upgraded in partnership with primarily historically minoritised families,13 14 and its content has undergone numerous cultural adaptations according to best practice standards15 to improve its utility worldwide.16,22 Its implementation in a single EI programme resulted in families with higher rates of participation-focused EI service plans, most of which met state-level criteria for quality.22 Prior studies examining key determinants and strategies for implementation were restricted to a single EI programme in one state9 23 24 and while it underwent systemic quality improvement of its FCC efforts in ways that improved family engagement and goal attainment outcomes.25 The extent to which these same determinants and strategies identified in a single EI programme in one state will apply across multiple states with varying EI service delivery structures is needed to guide scale-up implementation trials.

This study aims to characterise localised determinants, or context-specific implementation factors shaped by state-level EI systems and local programme operations, and to identify scalable strategies for implementing the YC-PEM e-PRO option for FCC in EI across multiple states. We engaged EI state and programme leadership, providers and families in Illinois to examine: (1) shared and localised determinants for longer-term YC-PEM e-PRO implementation across multiple states delivering EI services (Aim 1), and (2) shared and localised strategies to overcoming barriers to YC-PEM e-PRO implementation (Aim 2). Findings will refine an implementation research logic model (IRLM)26 to characterise shared and localised determinants and strategies needed to prepare for YC-PEM e-PRO implementation testing across multiple states delivering EI services.

Methods

Study design

This study employed an explanatory-sequential (quantitative>qualitative) mixed-methods design27 as a recommended research approach for addressing questions about how to sustain and improve EI practice strategies.28,31 This study engaged perspectives of EI stakeholders across multiple roles and most geographical regions within the case state of Illinois, to triangulate and reconcile differences when refining an IRLM26 developed in a prior hybrid type 1 effectiveness-implementation research phase.24 32 The decision to mix quantitative and qualitative methods was made a priori by two coauthors (MAK and JB) to align with the prior research phase24 and inform IRLM refinement.

Participants

Participants were recruited from three key stakeholder groups in Illinois: (1) EI programme leadership, (2) EI service coordinators and providers and (3) families enrolled in EI. From April 2024 to February 2025, a coauthor (CG) identified six programme leaders (two per geographical band in Illinois) to recruit into the study. A combination of targeted solicitation by participating programme leaders, snowball sampling and targeted recruiting via a provider listserv, and support from the case state’s EI clearinghouse were used to recruit stakeholders from two of three geographical bands (see figure 1). Leaders and providers had at least 2 years of EI experience. Eligible caregivers met the following inclusion criteria: (1) be 18 years or older, (2) be a parent or guardian of a child who has been receiving EI services for three or more months, (3) have internet access and (4) speak, read and write in English.

Participant distribution by geographical locale within case state. CFC, Child and Family Connections.

Data collection

Eligible and interested participants were directed via flyer and email to a Research Electronic Data Capture link33 to: (1) confirm their eligibility for the study; (2) provide their informed consent; (3) complete a background questionnaire with items pertaining to demographic characteristics (race, ethnicity, age), professional experience (role, years of EI experience) and perceptions of technology use when working with families; and (4) provide interview availability and details for receiving payment.

EI leaders completed semi-structured Zoom interviews (35–60 min per session); providers and caregivers joined focus groups (two to six per group, 47–82 min per session) or interviews (26–65 min per session). Sessions were co-facilitated by two to three study staff who identify as male (AA) or female (DLB, MAK and LF) and have prior research, lived and/or paediatric practice experience (DLB, MAK and LF). Session materials were tailored by stakeholder group (professionals or families). Semi-structured interview guides were developed using Consolidated Framework for Implementation Research (CFIR) and IRLM frameworks, modelled after guides used in prior phases of similar research.24 Tailored PowerPoint presentations including video content and data displays were also used to facilitate each session.

As shown in table 1, sessions included three parts. In Part 1, participant(s) were introduced to the YC-PEM e-PRO via brief slideshow and video, then used the integrated polling tool in Zoom to collect quantitative data on how helpful it would be to implement within their EI workflow (4-point scale, from 1=not helpful to 4=very helpful). Poll results were shared via screen to prompt explanation and discussion of anticipated facilitators and barriers. Additionally, data displays of quantitative findings from a prior implementation phase in another state shared to further prompt further discussion. In Part 2, leaders and providers, via integrated polling tool, rated how easy implementation of the YC-PEM e-PRO option would be within their EI workflow (4-point scale: 1=not easy to 4=very easy). These results, along with data displays of responses to these items by EI stakeholders who were involved in implementation of the YC-PEM in another EI context, were displayed to prompt for participants to identify and appraise determinants for implementing the YC-PEM e-PRO option. Participants in the family group were shown data displays from EI families’ experiences using YC-PEM in a prior implementation study and prompted to share their insights on the results. In Part 3, participants were asked to share strategies for overcoming anticipated barriers to YC-PEM e-PRO implementation within their EI context.

Participant recruitment continued until additional data no longer yielded significant new information pertinent to the research aim, signifying the attainment of data saturation. Each participant was issued a US$40 electronic gift card for their participation.

Data analysis

Sessions were audio and video-recorded, auto-transcribed via Microsoft Word software and reviewed by a second member (AA, VE and LF) who verified the text to ensure accuracy. Quantitative data from poll responses were analysed descriptively (n and %), and qualitative data were analysed using a hybrid deductive and inductive coding approach to memo development.34 An analytical memo was developed for each transcript, whereby session content was sorted deductively by interview topics to consistently focus our interpretation of participants’ responses within and across stakeholder groups. Three study team members (DLB, VE and AA) independently coded the first four transcripts deductively and met to compare and reconcile codes, refining key definitions. Remaining memos were coded by one staff member and cross-checked by another (VE, LF and DLB). Analytical memo content was further coded inductively to create one thematic memo per stakeholder group, with each memo describing a set of multiple recurring themes, contrasting viewpoints and context-specific insights. These three thematic memos (one per EI stakeholder group and with multiple themes) were shared with two team members (JB and MAK) and then shared with participants for member checking to confirm consistency and clarity in support of the credibility of main findings. Thematic findings on determinants and strategies were then applied to refine an IRLM developed in a prior research phase.

Most leaders and providers had six or more years of EI experience and rated technology as ‘important’ or ‘very important’ when working with caregivers (n=4 leaders, n=17 providers, n=12 caregivers).

Shared and localised determinants for YC-PEM e-PRO implementation in EI (Aim 1)

Most EI leaders (n=3, 75.0%) providers (n=14, 73.7%) and all 10 families rated the YC-PEM e-PRO option as helpful or very helpful to implement within their EI workflow. Participants explained their ratings by confirming four known determinants supporting broader YC-PEM e-PRO implementation in EI (Determinants 1a, 1c, 3c and 3d), and they identified two new key determinants supporting localised implementation within their case context (Determinants 1d and 1e). Conversely, all four EI leaders and most providers (n=15, 78.9%) rated the YC-PEM e-PRO option as somewhat or not easy to implement within their EI workflow. As shown in figure 2, participants explained their ratings by confirming five known determinants hindering broader YC-PEM e-PRO implementation within EI workflows (Determinants 2a-2d, 4a), and they introduced two new barriers to its implementation within their localised EI case context (Determinants 4b and 2e).

Shared determinants supporting YC-PEM e-PRO implementation

EI leadership and providers confirmed that the YC-PEM e-PRO option allows caregiver(s) to complete it independently and at their own pace, reducing real-time pressure to articulate their thoughts during individualised family service plan (IFSP) meetings (Determinant 1a). Leaders shared, ‘for some families, they may really prefer to be able to think about it on their own and complete a survey…’ (001L), and particularly during intake when ‘they’re… getting hit with this massive, massive pack of consents and releases’ (003L). Caregivers described benefitting from the choice so that ‘you can have time to actually think through it… so, I prefer the survey than having to talk to my provider’ (032C).

Participants from all stakeholder groups perceived the YC-PEM e-PRO option to feasibly centre family voice during family-professional exchanges to design the IFSP (Determinants 1c, 3c and 3d). Leaders and providers described this option as helping to tailor EI services per family priorities rather than provider-driven assumptions about their child. One leader noted, ‘this might be like a really cool tool to use just to make sure that we are getting and working with the family on routines that are important to them…’ (004L), while a practitioner shared that it can help ‘make sure that [providers] are going to broadly cover these routines that are pertinent to the families… and not just what we and our therapy bubble are viewing as what should be priority’ (019P). For caregivers, the YC-PEM e-PRO option could help them to clearly and confidently communicate their priorities during IFSP development, as ‘I think it is something that would be of help, definitely. It just shows [the importance of] how you present information, like single it out… these are the things I want to fix, these are the things I want to work on’ (026C). Caregivers also appreciated that it could be completed (1) more than once, to offer ‘a good snapshot, a month by month if you will’ for tracking and reflection of their child’s progress over time (030C) and/or (2) by multiple caregivers (eg, partners, friends and extended family) to generate a holistic view of the child’s needs when designing EI services, since ‘a lot of people should be involved and especially the caregivers so they could be more aware of things that should be put in place. [For example], my friends, partner, my parents’ (027C).

Localised determinants supporting YC-PEM e-PRO implementation

The importance of a structured form to assess their child’s environment in ways that prompt the EI team to consistently consider environmental strategies and/or prompt for referrals to external resources (Determinant 1d). Providers thought its structure ‘leads to the bigger picture… they do need resources, and we don’t always know what those are until we are working with them’ (014P). Families shared examples for how the structured form could prompt for deeper reflection and dialogue about environments, since ‘you think maybe, ok, at some point this kind of space in the house is not really comfortable for him so, you make some adjustments that might actually improve his physical ability moving around the house’ (042C).

Shared access to a structured report summarising YC-PEM e-PRO responses could enhance communication and coordination (Determinant 1e) by ‘unifying different disciplines where we could all then see where the family’s perspective is’ (014P). Caregivers shared that the structured report could facilitate collaboration within the EI team and with external providers, since ‘I am thinking you could share with your husband for better collaboration to help with the goals and also share the results with educators…’ (033C).

Shared determinants hindering YC-PEM e-PRO implementation

A key barrier to implementation, as confirmed by providers, is a lack of automated processes for survey completion and information sharing, such that ‘a parent cannot get in to see her or his child’s IFSP unless we mail it to them or email it to them as an attachment and then encrypt [it]’ (002L), and staffing shortages and turnover that require ongoing training to support skilled implementation (Determinants 2a-2d). This barrier is compounded by workload demands, as their robust assessment protocol and documentation demands leave little room to incorporate another tool. One provider expressed, ‘it might get hard to implement and get everyone on the same page because they’re going to see it as more work’ (002P). Another provider added, ‘service coordinators are running eighty plus cases and constantly transitioning’ (018P), highlighting the potential burden of introducing this option. Finally, all stakeholder groups shared that some families who could benefit may opt out due to (1) redundancy in ‘just answering the same questions over again’ (007P), (2) experiencing information overload during lengthy meetings that ‘…can be overwhelming not only for the parent, but for the team’ (017P) and/or (3) lacking time or motivation such that ‘even if they intend to do it… they never get around to completing it and then you’re gonna be like, chasing them down for this’ (019P).

Providers and caregivers confirmed the need to consider barriers related to language and literacy (Determinant 4a), as ‘especially with the English as a Second Language families, I think this would be very challenging to explain… it’s wordy and overwhelming’ (009P). Caregivers expanded this determinant to include issues of accessibility, such as the absence of ‘an AI tool… so you can ask a question, and the AI tool or assistant can help you understand what the question is … like an accessibility tool’ (038C).

Localised determinants hindering YC-PEM e-PRO implementation

Limited technology infrastructure could make it difficult to securely administer, share and integrate YC-PEM e-PRO responses online when collaborating across providers and with families to develop a meaningful EI service plan (Determinant 4b). As one leader noted, ‘It’s hard to share information currently with the EI system and our limited technology ability’ (001L).

Providers expressed the need to consider deploying the YC-PEM e-PRO option at both intake and annual evaluations (Determinant 2e), because ‘I think it could be helpful throughout… I can see the need and the benefit from doing it both ways [at intake and annual evaluations], but how it is presented and when it is presented makes a big difference’ (017P). By introducing the YC-PEM e-PRO option at intake, providers can align expectations early and highlight the family-centred nature of EI services. ‘I think to have it at the beginning kind of sets the stage for what early intervention is for… I think it kind of helps focus everybody on these family centred outcomes that I think can be really helpful’ (018P). By introducing it during annual evaluations, it could offer a snapshot of child progress and reassessment of family priorities for providers such as when ‘…the person that’s reassessing saw them at the initial and didn’t see them again for another year’ (001P).

Shared and localised strategies for YC-PEM e-PRO implementation in EI (Aim 2)

Participants confirmed and extended the scope of two strategies for YC-PEM e-PRO implementation (2a and 2b) and introduced one strategy for its localised implementation in their context (2c).

Strategy 1 (Aligned to IRLM strategy 2A): Institute flexible timelines for completion and report sharing

Participants described the importance of flexibility in choosing to complete and share YC-PEM e-PRO reports, starting at the point of referral. ‘For families who choose this option, you’re probably going to get a better response rate rather than families who are like, ‘Oh, another thing to complete or do’’ (001L) and could be embedded into referral processes as ‘a part of the way we advertise for early intervention now …so that when a CFC gets the information, we have more than that one page with one line that says ‘reason for referral’’ (002L). Participants agreed that its administration could help families and providers alike, since ‘capturing the family’s priorities ahead of time seems like a very intuitive way to go’ (002L) to ‘make things so much easier to have this information ahead of time to…plan and have some ideas…that are important to them’ (014P). One provider shared that it ‘would give me a great starting place. If I’m doing an evaluation and there are concerns or behaviors, I could really look at that and see, ‘okay, where are the challenges?’’ (013P). To ensure accessibility, stakeholders suggested both online and in-person administration options, since ‘people are busy, so it’s really hard to gather all that information prior to the meeting sometimes’ (008P) and ‘actually meet[ing] the child and not just be meeting the child on paper’ (003L) could remain helpful.

Strategy 2 (Aligned to IRLM Strategy 2b): Implement in ways that streamline workflow

Participants proposed the YC-PEM e-PRO as a replacement, ‘…so it’s not an added requirement or even an added function but it’s a newer way of doing what we have been doing’ (002L) ‘…but if it’s an additional piece…then people don’t want to do it’ (002P). So ‘this would have to become part of the system… for it to really be embraced by service coordinators’ (002L) who are ‘so busy with so many kids…if you interrupt [the system], it throws them off…you really can’t [add more work]’ (002L, 004L).

Strategy 3 (Aligned to IRLM Strategy 2c): Expand provider training options and related resources

Providers emphasised hands-on, in-person training starting at the point of hire with continued monitoring to best build provider confidence for skilled implementation, because ‘you’re going to get those people who have been doing this for 20 and 30 years, and they’re like ‘no, I don’t want to do this. This is just one more thing for me to have to remember to send to the family’’ (003P). Several providers recommended building a network of trained leaders across the state, to ‘get a few people really trained on it, and then…training at least one person in each CFC…so having your expert that you can go to vs just a one-time training’ (001P), and procedural integration to have it be taken ‘online and it’s a printout and we could just add it in, cool, that’s super easy’ as ‘a mandatory part of the annual, it would have to have like a subsection in the IFSP… but even things that are, theoretically, mandatory…are not really actually being used’ (012P).

Discussion

This study engaged a larger, more diverse group of EI stakeholders (ie, programme leaders, service coordinators and providers and families from diverse backgrounds across multiple programmes and most geographical regions of the case state) to strengthen knowledge about the scalability of the YC-PEM e-PRO option for family-centred service plan development and service improvement. Findings show key shared and localised determinants in the inner and outer settings that must be addressed in scaled implementation efforts. Specifically, findings confirm that supports for implementing the YC-PEM e-PRO include its versatility, centring of family voice and its structured format for interdisciplinary collaboration and thorough goal setting inclusive of environmental strategies for goal attainment. Stakeholders also confirmed the need for more accessible versions, reduced burden for providers and families when completing the YC-PEM at either intake or annual evaluation of progress, and poor technology infrastructure as barriers to implementation. Their three responsive strategies to these barriers are discussed to guide future implementation efforts.

Study findings emphasise the need for strategies that give families flexible timelines to complete and share their YC-PEM e-PRO results via an online summary report, and that give their EI providers flexible access to recorded and in-person training and resources to support its consistent use during service plan development. This strategy aligns with prior literature, including a recent review showing that families value empathic, flexible guidance for shared decision-making,35 though inconsistent training is a known staff-related barrier to FCC in EI.35 Findings also extend prior findings that support the need for a scalable user dashboard with customised functionality by EI stakeholder group, since families may benefit from access to dashboards containing reminders for assessment completion and report sharing whereas providers may benefit from dashboards containing access to on-site training and resources for integrating information from the report to develop meaningful service plan outcome(s).24 Future scale-up efforts could leverage human factors and health informatics expertise to co-design and test robust interfaces with EI stakeholders. Given that inefficient data systems hinder FCC coordination in EI in multiple states,36 EI leadership would be essential in evaluating their systems’ capacity to support longer-term implementation.

Findings also introduce a new strategy for supporting localised implementation, whereby EI programmes or broader service catchments within a state choose whether to test implementation of the YC-PEM e-PRO option at multiple time points and whether to augment or replace their usual care option. This finding differs from prior work that only considered implementing the YC-PEM e-PRO option once (either at intake for improving family-centred pathways into EI or during the child’s annual evaluation of progress in EI) and only to augment a singular usual care option.23 24 Decisions about how often to implement could be guided by systematic baseline assessment of organisational and system readiness for change to foster a positive implementation climate, due to known inefficient data systems for collecting and sharing EI data within and across service systems.35 36 Repeated administration of YC-PEM e-PRO option, either to augment or replace usual care, would afford for these data to be aggregated and paired with other data elements for longitudinal research purposes that would overcome drawbacks of cross-sectional health services research in early intervention.23 37

These results should be interpreted considering three limitations. First, findings were restricted to inner and outer setting determinants because stakeholders had not experienced YC-PEM e-PRO implementation and thus could not directly assess intervention characteristics or procedural determinants. Second, given known social disparities in EI access, engagement and satisfaction,25 38 39 this study successfully engaged more diverse participants to examine supports, barriers and strategies to long-term implementation; however, families had not yet used the YC-PEM e-PRO option and therefore could not appraise its feasibility and related barriers to implementation. Third, family data were primarily collected through focus groups which may have compromised the depth of individual responses.

Conclusion

This study addresses the growing need for mixed-methods research to inform data-driven quality improvement initiatives in EI. Findings from a larger and more diverse EI stakeholder base strengthen current evidence on determinants and strategies to prioritise when testing YC-PEM e-PRO implementation for improving FCC across multiple EI programmes and states that vary in how they administer EI services. Findings support its viability for scaled implementation, warranting the need for hybrid type 2 and 3 trials using culturally adapted versions administered through a customisable user interface and tailored implementation to augment or replace usual care at one or more time points in their EI workflow.

Cohort of allergic rhinitis evaluation of effectiveness and safety of herbal medicine (CARE-HM): protocol for the clinic-based multicentre Korean Medicine Registry
Authors: Soo-Dam Kim, Boram Lee, Gyu Tae Chang, Eun Kyoung Lee, Hamsoa Clinics KM Doctors Group, Mi Mi Ko, Anna Kim, Young Eun Kim, Su-Min Seo, Mi Ju Son
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAllergic rhinitis (AR) is a common chronic inflammatory condition that significantly impairs quality of life (QoL) through symptoms such as nasal congestion, rhinorrhoea, sneezing and itching. Conventional treatments often show limitations, prompting interest in complementary therapies like herbal medicine (HM). HM is widely used in East Asian countries and has demonstrated potential in modulating immune responses and reducing AR symptoms. In Korea, a government pilot project expanded in 2024 to include AR under limited insurance coverage for HM, highlighting the need for robust clinical evidence on its safety and effectiveness.Methods and analysisThis study is a multicentre, prospective registry conducted in 21 Korean Medicine (KM) clinics across Republic of Korea. Participants meeting predefined criteria will receive HM or other KM therapies as part of routine care. Background

Allergic rhinitis (AR), a common chronic inflammatory condition of the nasal mucosa, arises from a hypersensitivity reaction mediated by immunoglobulin E (IgE), mast cells and eosinophils in response to environmental allergens.1,3 Key symptoms such as nasal congestion, rhinorrhoea, sneezing and nasal itching significantly impact patients, often causing sleep disturbances, reduced learning capacity, decreased productivity and a lower quality of life (QoL).4 5 The severity of AR correlates with greater declines in physical and psychosocial well-being, and patients with severe symptoms frequently develop pessimistic outlooks on their long-term health.4 6 Although AR can occur at any age, it is most commonly observed in childhood or early adulthood and is often associated with comorbid conditions such as asthma, sinusitis and conjunctivitis, further compounding its impact on overall health and daily functioning.7,9

Globally, AR affects an estimated 10–30% of the population, with its incidence rising due to urbanisation and environmental changes.10 11 In Korea, the prevalence of AR has increased significantly in recent years. Data from the Health Insurance Review and Assessment Service show a 51.3% rise in AR-related cases over 3 years, increasing from 4 911 876 in 2021 to 7 430 373 in 2023, underscoring its growing impact on public health.12 Standard treatments for AR, such as antihistamines, intranasal corticosteroids and immunotherapy, aim to alleviate symptoms and improve QoL.13,15 However, these treatments often exhibit limitations, including inconsistent effectiveness, potential side effects and poor adherence, particularly in chronic cases.16,18 These challenges highlight the need for alternative or complementary approaches that align more closely with patient preferences and provide holistic benefits.1 19

Herbal medicine (HM) has emerged as a prominent integrative treatment option for AR, particularly in East Asian countries where traditional medicine plays a significant role in healthcare systems. In the Republic of Korea, AR ranked as the 22nd most frequently diagnosed condition in the field of Korean Medicine (KM) in 2023, highlighting its prevalence and clinical relevance.20 HM, particularly in the form of decoctions, plays a central role in AR treatment within the KM field. Surveys indicate that decoctions are the primary choice for managing AR, being used in 91.2% of paediatric cases and 86.1% of adolescent and adult cases.21 Evidence suggests that HM is particularly effective in children, reducing recurrence rates, lowering IgE levels and alleviating symptoms such as sneezing, rhinorrhoea and nasal congestion more effectively than conventional treatments alone.22 23

In 2020, the Korean government initiated a pilot project subsidising herbal decoction therapy for specific conditions in primary care clinics, contingent on meeting diagnostic and quality standards.24 In April 2024, responding to growing public demand for expanded health insurance coverage for herbal decoction, the government launched the second phase of this project, extending coverage to include AR among other conditions and expanding the scope to encompass both primary care clinics and KM hospitals.25 Therefore, to establish sound insurance policies, it is essential to secure clinical evidence on the effectiveness and safety of HM in accordance with government requirements. While HM provides the advantage of personalised treatment, its unique characteristics present challenges for high-level evidence studies such as randomised controlled trials, particularly due to difficulties in developing placebo controls. Observational studies collecting real-world data (RWD) in clinical settings may offer a more effective approach to evaluating the clinical effectiveness and safety of HM.26

Consequently, it is essential to systematically collect data through registry studies to validate the safety and effectiveness of herbal decoctions. This study aims to collect and analyse data from patients with AR receiving herbal decoction therapy in KM clinics. Our research team is conducting separate registry studies in KM hospitals and clinics to evaluate the evidence supporting the second phase of the HM pilot project. Each study is designed to reflect the unique characteristics of these healthcare settings. This protocol focuses specifically on primary care clinics, tailoring the study design to reflect their distinct operational and clinical practices. By establishing robust clinical evidence for HM, the study seeks to support the development of safe and effective treatment strategies for AR, while contributing to the expansion of health insurance coverage for herbal decoction, thereby enhancing its accessibility and integration into evidence-based healthcare practices.

Methods

Study registration

The study has been registered with the Clinical Research Information Service (CRIS, https://cris.nih.go.kr/cris/search/detailSearch.do?seq=29008&search_page=L) under registration number KCT0010172.

Study objectives

The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the real-world effectiveness and safety of HM for AR in KM primary care clinics. We hypothesise that KM treatments incorporating HM are associated with greater improvement in AR symptoms and QoL compared with KM usual care excluding herbal decoction. To achieve this goal, the study will establish a prospective registry that systematically collects clinical data from patients receiving KM treatments for AR in primary care settings. The specific objectives are as follows:

To collect detailed RWD on the use of HM and other KM interventions for AR, including prescription patterns and treatment frequencies.

Study design

This multicentre, prospective registry study aims to evaluate the clinical effectiveness and safety of HM for patients with AR in KM clinics. The study is designed to collect RWD from routine clinical practices, providing a comprehensive understanding of treatment patterns and patient outcomes in KM settings.

The study will be conducted at 21 clinics within the Hamsoa network (http://hamsoapharm.com/en/), including Gangdong, Gangseo, Gwanak, Suwon, Gwangmyeong, Gwangju-Suwan, Guri, Gimpo, Bucheon-Sinjungdong, Seodaejeon, Seocho-Gyodae, Ansan, Eunpyeong, Jamsil, Changwon, Cheongju-Bokdae, Cheongju-Yullyang, Paju, Pyeongchon, Pyeongtaek and Hwajeong (figure 1). The participating clinics represent a wide geographical distribution, ensuring diverse patient data and enhancing the generalisability of the study findings.

Study participants

Recruitment

The study will recruit participants from 21 Hamsoa clinics located throughout the Republic of Korea. During the study period, patients receiving KM treatment for AR at participating clinics will be recruited. Researchers will provide an information sheet to patients who meet the eligibility criteria during their clinic visit and obtain their consent to enrol them in the study.

Inclusion criteria

Participants will be eligible for inclusion in the study if they meet all the following criteria:

Exhibit at least two of the four primary AR symptoms: nasal congestion, rhinorrhoea, sneezing or nasal itching, and have a confirmed diagnosis of AR at the participating KM clinic. Diagnoses must align with one of the following Korean Standard Classification of Diseases, 8th Revision (KCD-8) codes originated from International Classification of Diseases, 10th Revision. The clinical diagnosis of AR will be based on physician judgement, incorporating patient history, physical examination and, if necessary, IgE (skin or blood) allergy testing results.

Intervention

Participants will receive routine treatment for AR based on the clinical judgement of the attending KM physician. There will be no restrictions on the type, frequency or dosage of KM interventions, including HM, acupuncture and other treatments. Additionally, participants may continue using AR medications prescribed prior to their enrolment in the study, with concurrent use of conventional treatments allowed.

Outcome measures

The scope of clinical data collection in this study includes participants’ demographic information, medical history, data on KM treatments administered for AR at the participating clinics, pretreatment and post-treatment evaluation data and AE reports. During the first 4 weeks, data will be collected biweekly, capturing information on participants’ conditions and the treatments they receive. After the 4-week visit, participants will continue to be monitored for symptom changes and treatment outcomes through online surveys, with no in-person visits required for the 6-month and 12-month follow-ups. For child participants, additional data on growth indicators such as height and weight will be collected to track changes in growth patterns throughout the study period. Demographic information

The demographic information will include the participant’s initials, sex, date of birth and age.

Medical history and treatment history

The medical history will include details about the AR, such as the first onset date, clinical symptoms (eg, rhinorrhoea, nasal congestion, sneezing, itching) and whether relevant tests were performed. It will also include the healthcare provider’s diagnosis and related diagnostic codes. Additionally, the history of any co-occurring allergic conditions like atopic dermatitis or asthma, as well as the family history of allergic diseases, will be recorded.

For the treatment history, recent treatments within the last 3 months, including medication names (brand), dosage, route of administration, start and end dates and the purpose of use will be collected.

Physical measurements

Physical measurements will include the participant’s height (cm), weight (kg) and body mass index (BMI). For child participants under 19 years of age, the participant’s height, weight and BMI percentiles will be calculated using the 2017 Paediatric Growth Chart by the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency,27 comparing these metrics with other participants of the same age and gender.

Symptom assessment, quality of life and treatment evaluation

Total Nasal Symptom Score

A score used to assess the severity of AR symptoms, including nasal congestion, sneezing, itching and runny nose. Each symptom is rated on a 4-point scale ranging from 0 to 3. A score of 0 indicates no symptoms, 1 represents mild symptoms that are easily tolerated, 2 denotes symptoms that are bothersome but tolerable and 3 reflects severe symptoms that significantly interfere with daily activities. The TNSS is the sum of these individual symptom scores, which are recorded at each time point throughout the study.

Quality of life survey

For participants under 12 years old, the Korean version of the Allergic Rhinitis Quality of Life Questionnaire for Children (QoL-KCAR) will be used to assess the impact of AR on their QoL.28 For participants aged 12 years or older, the shortened Rhinitis Quality of Life Questionnaire (Mini-RQLQ) will be administered to evaluate the same aspect of QoL in relation to AR.29

Digestive and sleep-related NRS

Participants will be assessed for discomfort related to digestion and sleep using a Numeric Rating Scale (NRS). For digestive symptoms, such as indigestion, chest discomfort or bloating, participants will rate the intensity of their symptoms on a scale from 0 (no symptoms) to 10 (the most severe discomfort imaginable). Similarly, for sleep-related issues, participants will rate their discomfort during sleep or while falling asleep on the same 0–10 scale.

Reasons for choosing herbal decoction treatment

Participants will be surveyed to identify the reasons for their decision to choose or not choose herbal decoction treatment for AR. For those who opted for herbal decoction, the survey will capture factors such as dissatisfaction with conventional treatments, perceived effectiveness or safety of herbal decoction, preference for personalised or natural therapies, recommendations from acquaintances or affordability due to health insurance coverage. Participants who did not choose herbal decoction will be asked about reasons such as mild symptoms, high treatment costs, inconvenience of use or doubts about effectiveness. Additional reasons will also be recorded if provided.

Intention to continue herbal treatment

Participants will be surveyed to assess their willingness to continue using herbal decoction for managing AR in the future. Those who choose to continue will indicate whether their decision is independent of insurance coverage or limited to treatments covered by insurance. Participants who do not intend to continue will be asked about their reasons, such as high treatment costs, inconvenience, dissatisfaction with treatment effectiveness or other factors. Additional reasons provided by participants will also be recorded.

KM treatment history

The history of KM treatments will include details about the use of HM, acupuncture and moxibustion for AR. The information will cover the prescription name, ingredients, dosage and prescription date for herbal treatments, and whether the participant participated in any pilot programmes. Additionally, any KM-based treatments that are non-pharmacological, such as acupuncture, moxibustion, intradermal acupuncture, bee venom acupuncture, cupping, chuna manual therapy, infrared therapy, aromatherapy, laser acupuncture or nasal ointments, will be recorded, including whether these treatments were covered by insurance.

Adverse events

AEs will be monitored throughout the study to ensure participant safety. During each follow-up visit, participants will be asked whether they experienced any AEs since the last visit. If an AE is reported, detailed information will be recorded, including the type of event, onset date, severity and resolution status.

Patient satisfaction survey

Participants will be asked to rate their overall satisfaction with KM treatment for AR through a structured questionnaire.26 30 The survey will assess their experiences and perceptions on a 5-point Likert scale, ranging from ‘strongly disagree’ to ‘strongly agree’ (online supplemental material).

Quality control: data collection and recording methods

The electronic case report form system for data collection is based on myTrial Electronic Data Capture (National Institute for Korean Medicine Development, Gyeongsan, Republic of Korea), validated by the National Agency for KM Innovative Technologies Development. Clinicians will log in using assigned unique IDs and passwords to input standardised patient data, including demographics, treatment details and clinical outcomes. Patient identification will rely on medical record numbers from the respective clinics, and individual patient medical record numbers will remain inaccessible during data review for analysis. All data will be securely stored and managed within the system, with regular quality control checks to ensure accuracy and integrity. The registry data will subsequently be managed within the KM Clinical Research Data Platform (https://nikom.or.kr/engnckm/index.do), established by the KM Innovation Technology Development Project Team.

Sample size

This study is a registry study involving patients receiving treatment for AR at KM clinics. Considering the nature of the registry study, all patients meeting the inclusion criteria will be enrolled. Therefore, a specific target number of participants per group is not set.31 The goal is to competitively recruit a total of 300 participants across the KM clinics participating in the registry. Based on historical data, each of the 21 clinics sees approximately 15–20 new patients with AR per month, making the recruitment of 300 participants within 12 months feasible and appropriate for subgroup analyses and propensity score-based adjustments.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses will be performed using SAS V.9.4 (SAS Institute, Cary, North Carolina, USA), with a two-tailed test as the principle, and the significance level set at 5%. Continuous variables will be summarised as mean (95% CI) or median (IQR), and categorical variables as frequency and percentage. Demographic and baseline characteristics between the herbal decoction and KM usual care groups among the types of KM treatment will be compared using the independent t-test or Wilcoxon rank-sum test for continuous variables, depending on normality, and the χ² test or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables. Baseline differences between the two groups will be adjusted as confounders using analysis of covariance, with baseline values included as covariates when comparing mean changes in outcome measures between the two groups. The primary effectiveness outcome will be the change in TNSS, focusing on the evaluation of nasal symptoms. In addition, QoL will also be assessed using validated questionnaires (Mini-RQLQ for participants ≥12 years and QoL-KCAR for those <12 years). Secondary outcomes will include digestive and sleep-related NRS, patient satisfaction and reduction in concomitant medication use. These outcomes will be evaluated by comparing the herbal decoction and KM usual care groups using the propensity score matching-difference in difference model. Propensity scores will be estimated using logistic regression based on baseline covariates such as age and sex. The matched data set will then be evaluated for balance through standardised mean differences to ensure comparability between the herbal decoction and KM usual care groups. Outcome changes, including the improvement in AR symptoms, will be analysed before and after treatment, accounting for the duration and presence of herbal decoction use. Additionally, changes in outcome measures will be evaluated within each group using paired t-tests or Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, depending on data normality. Safety will be evaluated by comparing the occurrence of AEs between herbal decoction and KM usual care groups using the χ² test or Fisher’s exact test. Missing values will be handled based on identified patterns by applying either the last observation carried forward method for data classified as missing completely at random or the multiple imputation method for data categorised as missing at random.

Adverse event monitoring and reporting

AEs include any harmful or unintended symptoms or abnormalities. AEs will be monitored from the start of the study, with details on onset, resolution and treatment. If a participant cannot be followed up, efforts will be made to contact them and document all communications.

Ethics and dissemination

This study was approved by the IRB of Kyung Hee University on 11 December 2024 (Approval No. KHSIRB-24–631). Any modifications to the protocol will require a formal amendment, which must be reviewed and approved by the IRB before implementation. The results will be disseminated through peer-reviewed journals and academic conferences.

Informed consent and consent acquisition

Before any clinical procedures, the researcher will explain the purpose, methods, risks and participant protections of the study. Participants will voluntarily decide to join and sign an informed consent form, with a copy provided to them and the original kept by the institution. Separate consent will be obtained for sharing data with third parties, secondary research use and the collection and use of personal data.

Vulnerable populations

This study does not impose any age restrictions. For child participants aged 7 years or older, the researcher will explain the study using a child-friendly version of the informed consent form and obtain consent from both the child and their legal guardian. For child participants under 7 years of age, consent will be obtained solely from the legal guardian. Consent for third-party data sharing and secondary research use will also be obtained from the legal guardian.

Safety and adverse event management

Since this study involves the observation and data collection of routine clinical practices, any AEs arising from standard care will be managed according to usual medical procedures. Participants will be protected throughout the study, and AEs will be addressed in accordance with the standard clinical protocols.

Study status and timeline

The study is currently in the planning phase and will begin recruitment in the second quarter of 2025. Participant recruitment is expected to be completed by December 2025. Data collection will continue through the recruitment phase, with the final data collection expected to conclude in December 2026. The results of the study are expected to be analysed and published by June 2027.

Discussion

This study aims to establish clinical evidence on the effectiveness and safety of HM for AR through a multicentre, prospective registry. Conducted as part of the Korean government pilot project for HM, the research addresses the critical need for evidence-based validation of KM treatments. Initially launched in 2020, the pilot project provided partial insurance coverage for herbal decoction targeting dysmenorrhoea, facial nerve paralysis and poststroke sequelae.24 Building on the success of the initial phase, the project was expanded in April 2024 to include AR, functional dyspepsia and lumbar disc herniation, underscoring the therapeutic potential of HM in addressing diverse medical conditions.25

HM has demonstrated significant potential in modulating immune responses, providing anti-inflammatory and antiallergic effects.32 33 Key mechanisms include the suppression of proinflammatory cytokines, stabilisation of mast cells and regulation of IgE levels, which collectively contribute to symptom relief in AR.34 35 These mechanisms have shown efficacy in alleviating core symptoms such as congestion, rhinorrhoea, itching and sneezing.33 This highlights the importance of conducting comprehensive research to evaluate HM in real-world clinical environments. Our previous research confirmed the effectiveness and safety of combined KM treatments for AR, but further studies are needed for validation in a larger population and long-term assessment.36 37 By involving 21 KM clinics across the Republic of Korea, the multicentre study design ensures a diverse and representative patient population, enhancing the applicability of its findings. The longitudinal follow-up over 12 months will provide a thorough assessment of both short- and long-term outcomes, offering a well-rounded perspective on the role of HM in AR management.

The results of this study are expected to significantly influence healthcare policy and clinical practice. By delivering robust evidence of the clinical benefits of HM, the findings will support the evaluation and potential expansion of the HM pilot project, facilitating broader insurance coverage and improving patient access to KM treatments. Additionally, the study will provide KM practitioners with valuable insights into treatment patterns, clinical outcomes and patient satisfaction, aiding in the optimisation of clinical practices. Finally, the research will establish evidence on the safety and effectiveness of HM for AR, contributing to the expansion of evidence-based practices in KM and its integration into modern healthcare systems.

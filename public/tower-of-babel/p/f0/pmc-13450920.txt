Evaluation of c-Myc in odontogenic cysts and tumours—An immunohistochemical study
Authors: Soujanya Pinisetti, Nallagangula L. Vinaya, Praveena Adusumilli, Suresh Babburi, Aparna Venigalla, Ajay Benarjee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective:To assess immunohistochemically the presence of c-Myc both qualitatively and quantitatively in odontogenic cysts and tumours and to correlate the results with biological behaviour of these lesions.Materials and Methods:Formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded blocks of odontogenic cysts and tumours were retrieved from institutional archives. The study sample size was 45 (n = 45), which included 10 cases of odontogenic keratocyst cyst (n = 10), five cases of radicular cyst (n = 5), five cases of dentigerous cyst (n = 5), and 10 cases of solid ameloblastoma of which five cases are follicular (n = 5) and five cases are plexiform (n = 5), 10 cases of Adenomatoid odontogenic tumour (n = 10) and five cases of unicystic ameloblastoma (n = 5). Sections were taken and stained immunohistochemically using c-Myc and evaluated both for quantitative and qualitative analysis.Results:In the current study, the mean number of c-Myc positive cells in the odontogenic tumours was highest in solid Ameloblastoma (78.6 ± 11.59), followed by Adenomatoid odontogenic tumor (AOT) (75.30 ± 39.853) and least in unicystic ameloblastoma (56.6 ± 20.53). Among odontogenic cysts, the mean number of c-Myc positive cells was higher in Odontogenic keratocyst (OKC) (73.2 ± 17.937) compared to dentigerous cyst (28.0 ± 27.9) and radicular cysts (31.0 ± 31.7). The staining intensity varied in odontogenic cysts and tumoursConclusion:The results and observations of the present study show that c-Myc plays a role in the aggressive biological behaviour of odontogenic lesions like ameloblastoma and OKC. So, it can be used as a proliferative marker. However, additional immunohistochemical and DNA assays are to be carried to determine the role of c-Myc oncogene and to know if any mutations may play a role in the pathogenesis of these odontogenic lesions.

INTRODUCTION

Odontogenic cysts and tumours are those which derived from the tooth-forming apparatus and odontogenic epithelial remnants. The majority of these lesions are benign, but a few, like odontogenic keratocyst and ameloblastoma, exhibit a locally aggressive biological behaviour.[1] Understanding the biological behaviour of these lesions is important for the diagnosis and treatment planning.

Various molecular alterations are now proposed to be responsible for the aggressive behaviour of these lesions, one of which is the role of oncogenes. Oncogenic mutations, considered to be hallmark drivers of cancers, are reported in benign cysts and tumours, including those that have an odontogenic origin. Studies using various oncogenes, like Ras, Myc, and Fos,[23] on odontogenic cysts and tumours showed their biological behaviour.

c-Myc is one of the oncogenes (c-Myc proto-oncogene—is a transcription factor) that belongs to the Myc family. It plays a physiological role in embryogenesis, cell proliferation, cell growth, terminal differentiation, and sensitizes cell to apoptosis.[4] In the early stages of embryonic tooth development, its expression is also related to the proliferation of odontogenic epithelial cells and their degree of differentiation.[5]

Mutations in c-Myc oncogene lead to a wide range of aggressive, poorly differentiated lesions which include odontogenic lesions, breast, colon, cervical, small lung carcinomas, etc., In case of tumours, it accelerates cell cycle through G1 and S phase, abrogating cell cycle checkpoints and increased cell metabolism that eventually renders genomic instability to the cells.[6]

Myc is now known to be one of the most frequently deregulated oncogenes and is the third most amplified gene in human cancers.[78] By knowing its properties, it drives us to know its pivotal role in odontogenic cysts and tumours.

Currently, very few studies were conducted regarding the expression of c-Myc in odontogenic lesions. The fact that c-Myc oncogene can be detected by IHC and the advantage of maintaining cellular and tissue architecture has prompted us to study the behaviour of these lesions. With this background, the present study is planned to elucidate the role of c-Myc in odontogenic cysts and tumours.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This was a retrospective study. Formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded blocks of odontogenic cysts and tumours were retrieved from the Department of Oral Pathology and Microbiology. The study was ethically approved with certificate number of ethical clearance numbered IEC/29/2016. The study sample size was 45 (n = 45), which included 10 cases of odontogenic keratocyst cyst (n = 10), five cases of radicular cyst (n = 5), five cases of dentigerous cyst (n = 5), and 10 cases of ameloblastoma of which five cases are follicular (n = 5) and five cases are plexiform (n = 5), 10 cases of AOT (n = 10) and five cases of unicystic ameloblastoma. The histological sections were immunohistochemically stained with anti-c-Myc antibody (clone 9E10; Biogenix, USA).

Tissue sections stained with c-Myc showing brown colour of both nuclear and cytoplasm were considered as positive staining and expression of c-Myc was observed by light microscopy, and photomicrographic images captured using image capturing software (ProgRes Capture Pro 2.8- JENOPTIK). All the analyses were conducted under 400x magnification for three randomly selected fields. Each image was opened in Microsoft PowerPoint, and 6 × 6 Grid (36 boxes) was placed over the image as given below.

Total number of cells and positive cells were counted manually in each box and scored. All the percentages of total fields were added. The average score was given. Data were analysed using statistical product service solution version 22 (SPSS version 22). The criteria used in the study were based on Akshatha et al. and Swetha et al.[910]

DISCUSSION

Cysts and tumours derived from the odontogenic tissues constitute an unusually diverse group of lesions that originate through some alterations from the normal pattern of odontogenesis.[11] They range from hamartomatous or non-neoplastic tissue proliferations to aggressive behaviour to malignant neoplasms.[12]

Cell proliferation plays an important role in the development of odontogenic cysts and tumours. Various cell proliferative markers, like Ki67, proliferating cell nuclear antigen (PCNA), p53, and nucleolar organizer regions (AgNORs), have previously been used to indicate the biological behaviour of odontogenic tumours and cysts. Among them, c-Myc is one which plays a pivotal role in cell proliferation.[1]

c-Myc (proto-oncogene) is a nuclear transcription factor it belongs to Myc family which regulates cell proliferation and differentiation. When c-Myc gets deregulated, it accelerates cell cycle through G1-S phase overruling the cell cycle checkpoints and thereby causing an increase in genomic instability, ultimately leading to increased cellular proliferation.[13] Elevated or deregulated expression of c-Myc has been detected Immunohistochemical allowing quantitative and qualitative comparison in a wide range of odontogenic lesions.

The present revealed reactive products of c-Myc were located in the nucleus and cytoplasm. Among odontogenic lesions, the mean number of c-Myc immune positive cells ranges from (78.6-28.0) [Table 1] and intense staining was noted in ameloblastoma and OKC [Table 5].

When quantitative assessment of the c-Myc immunopositive cells was evaluated, OKC (73.20) showed maximum mean value, whereas dentigerous (28.0) showed the least number of positive cells than radicular cyst (31.0) [Table 1]. In the current study, all 10 cases of OKC showed (100%) positivity, whereas only three among five cases of dentigerous and radicular cyst (30%) were positive [Table 5]. Which as in concordance with the study conducted by Zama Moosavi et al.[13] who reported 80% positivity in OKC, 50% was seen in radicular cyst and 75% in OKC using in situ hybridization technique. This suggested that the increased expression in OKC is due to the high mitotic index of the epithelium which was similar to the benign tumours like ameloblastoma.[614] Similar results were noted in other studies using proliferative markers like p53,[1516] PCNA,[3] Ki-67,[1517] IPO-38[718] who observed increased proliferative cells in the lining of OKC than dentigerous and radicular cyst. This strongly indicates that c-Myc can be used as a proliferative marker.

Comparison of c-Myc expression between dentigerous and radicular cyst showed no statistically significant difference (P-value 0.841) [Table 2], whereas OKC showed significant difference with radicular and dentigerous cyst (P-value 0.008 and 0.005), respectively [Table 2]. This decreased expression in dentigerous and radicular cyst was due to low mitotic rate and reflection of their derivation from the epithelial rests which were in a state of low metabolic activity.[19]

Reactivity of c-Myc expression was observed in all the epithelial layers of OKC [Figure 1] with localization in both nucleus and cytoplasm. This was in congruity with the Zama Moosvi et al.[13] This unusual proliferation of epithelial cells in OKC may represent an epithelial disorganization or they may also reflect a unique epithelial differentiation process in which basal cells assume some characteristics of preameloblasts.[17]

Dentigerous cyst shows c-Myc positivity in basal and some parabasal layers with nucleus and cytoplasmic localization [Figure 2] which was in congruence with Zama Moosvi et al.[13] The low proliferative activity might be related to regular maintenance of two- to three-layer thickness of epithelium.[20]

The epithelial lining of radicular cyst showed both nuclear and cytoplasmic staining in basal and supra basal cells [Figure 3], Similar results was noted in a study done by zama moosvi et al.[13] Previous studies done on c-Myc expression in non-malignant and pre-malignant gastrointestinal disorders by Ciclitira et al revealed that increased expression is due to inflammatory stimulus.[21]

When quantitative assessment of c-Myc immunopositive cells were evaluated among tumours, ameloblastoma showed maximum mean (78.6) value, followed by AOT (75.30) and unicystic ameloblastoma (56.60) [Table 1].

In the current study, all 10 cases of ameloblastoma and five cases of unicystic ameloblastoma showed (100%) positivity, only 8 out of 10 cases of AOT showed (80%) positivity [Table 5], but this was in contrast to Zama Moosvi et al.[13] who noted 80% in ameloblastoma. Li et al.[22] reported 81.5% in ameloblastoma.

The reason behind the increased expression of c-Myc in ameloblastoma is that c-Myc protein proto-oncogene product is related to cell proliferation and differentiation and its over expression has been documented in various tumorous lesions and could be one of the contributing factors for the aggressive clinical behaviour of odontogenic tumours. No statistical significance difference was observed when follicular ameloblastoma was compared with plexiform ameloblastoma (P-value 0.841) [Table 3] which was similar to study conducted by Zama Moosvi et al.[13]

Comparison of c-Myc expression between follicular and plexiform with AOT showed no statistically significant difference (P-value 0.165 and 0.129) [Table 3b], which was similar to the study conducted by Carmo and Silva[23] using AgNOR on odontogenic tumours and concluded that the difference in the biological behaviour of ameloblastoma and AOT is not related to their cellular proliferation ratio.

Follicular ameloblastoma was compared with the unicystic ameloblastoma, it showed a statistically significant difference (P-value 0.032) [Table 3b]. This suggests that simple unicystic ameloblastomas are low-grade tumours with a low proliferation index in the luminal variant when compared with the intra-luminal and mural variants.

When plexiform and AOT are compared with unicystic ameloblastoma, they did not show any statistically significant difference (P-value 0.222 and 0.075) [Table 3b], respectively. This could be due to the c-Myc oncogene products which are closely related to tumorous epithelial cells and the quantity of these products might be related to the biological behaviour of these cells.[5]

In solid ameloblastomas, c-Myc expression was seen in both the peripheral tall columnar cells and central stellate reticulum-like cells [Figure 4]. Regarding the subcellular distribution, both nuclear and cytoplasmic staining were seen in the peripheral tall columnar cells and only the nuclear staining in central stellate reticulum-like cells. This was in agreement with the study conducted by Zama Moosvi et al.[13] who stated that localization of cMyc protein in the nucleus and cytoplasm of the peripheral cells may be due to the similarity of these cells to the ameloblasts of the enamel organ which are metabolically very active, whereas the predominant nuclear staining in the stellate reticulum-like cells denotes its less activity when compared with the peripheral cells. Our results also indicate that c-Myc protein may play a role in maintaining the stem cell population of the peripheral layers of the tumour nests from which proliferating cells might be recruited.[5]

The results of our study were in compliance with the Barawy S et al.[5] who have reported both nuclear and cytoplasmic staining of the peripheral tall columnar and central stellate reticulum-like cells. Polacarz SV et al.[24] observed that the reactive products localized in the nucleus are due to the chromatin undergoing mitotic phase and concluded that the products in the cytoplasm around the nucleus are a transitional form of staining activity in which the products of the oncogene diffuse into the whole cell according to stage of maturation.

In AOT, nuclear and cytoplasmic staining was seen in ducts, sheets, and whorls of epithelial cells [Figure 5], which was in harmony with the study conducted by Zama Moosvi et al.[13] This indicates more proliferative activity seen in these areas.[13] Unicystic ameloblastoma shows both nuclear and cytoplasmic staining of basal and supra-basal cells [Figure 6]. In the current study comparison of c-Myc expression between odontogenic cysts and tumours, when OKC was compared with ameloblastoma and AOT, they did not show any statistically significant difference (P-value 0.631 and 0.089), respectively [Table 4]. This was in assimilation with the Zama Moosvi et al.[13] who did not find the statistically significant difference between the three lesions regarding c-Myc expression. Our findings were in harmony with the other studies conducted using proliferative markers like PCNA,[25] IPO38,[18] and p53[1516] which reveal that OKC has a high proliferation index approximately to that of AOT and ameloblastoma when compared to other cysts and can be considered as a cyst with aggressive clinical behaviour.

Unicystic ameloblastoma when compared with OKC did not show any statistically significant difference (P-value 0.099) [Table 4]. This indicates that OKC lining epithelium had a unique proliferative and differentiation process when compared to unicystic ameloblastoma. This statement was in congruence with the Li TJ et al.[26] findings of PCNA and Ki 67 in unicystic ameloblastomas.

Comparison of c-Myc expression between dentigerous cyst and ameloblastoma and adenomatoid odontogenic tumour showed statistically significant difference (P-value 0.003, 0.040), respectively [Table 4]. This was in congruence with Zama Moosvi et al.[13] Previous comparative studies between dentigerous cyst and ameloblastoma by Thosaporn et al.[18] on IPO38, Slootweg P et al. on p53,[16] and Piattelli et al.[27] using Ki67 have shown similar results. These data suggest that accumulation of the fluid and contents of cystic fluid in the follicle increasing the osmolality and hydrostatic pressure are of greater importance in the development and enlargement of dentigerous cyst rather than epithelial proliferation[13]

When c-Myc expression was compared between radicular cyst and ameloblastoma and AOT, it showed a statistically significant difference (P-value 0.008, 0.040), respectively [Table 4]. Which was in contrast in the study conducted by Zama Moosvi et al.[13] who observed a 50% positivity in radicular cysts and suggested that interleukins (IL-1 and IL-6) stimulate keratinocytes, which thereby increases c-Myc protein and leads to cell proliferation. The decreased expression in our study may be due to inflammatory stimulus, which could be the result of the eruptive process and make cells proliferate but can be inconstant and persists only for short period of time.

Dentigerous cyst and radicular cyst, when compared with unicystic ameloblastoma, did not show any statistically significant difference (P-value 0.421, 0.222), respectively [Table 4]. This hypothesizes that oncogene products may be related to the biological behaviour of tumour cells.[28]

In the present study, intense staining among study groups was maximum for ameloblastoma (4), OKC (4), followed by AOT (2) and unicystic ameloblastoma (1) [Table 5]. The overexpression of c-Myc protein products may either be due to the increased production or decreased breakdown of the protein which is the result of growth regulation.[5]

Moderate staining in the study group was maximum for AOT (6), ameloblastoma (5), followed by OKC (4), unicystic ameloblastoma, dentigerous, and radicular cyst (2, 2, 2) [Table 5], respectively. Mild staining is seen in unicystic ameloblastoma (2), OKC (2), followed by ameloblastoma, dentigerous, and radicular cysts (1, 1, 1) [Table 5], respectively. These different staining patterns of the gene products reflected a modification of the biological behaviour.[2930]

Ameloblastoma and OKC showed maximum diffuse distribution when compared to the other subjects [Table 6]. This could be due to high levels of c-Myc expression in the tooth germ that was detected in the proliferative inner-enamel epithelium which is similar to ameloblasts in ameloblastoma,[31] and focal distribution in the study groups [Table 6] is due to decrease in the c-Myc expression during differentiation of cells.[31323334]

CONCLUSION

The results of the present study show that c-Myc plays a role in the aggressive biological behaviour of odontogenic lesions like ameloblastoma and OKC. So, it can be used as a proliferative marker. However, additional immunohistochemical and DNA assays are to be carried to determine the role of c-Myc oncogene and to know if any mutations may play a role in the pathogenesis of these odontogenic lesions.

Mucoadhesive lyophilized wafers loaded with nano-spanlastic felodipine formulation: development and characterization
Authors: Ananda Kumar Chettupalli, Sarad Pawar Naik Bukke, Malakapogu Ravindra Babu, Padmanabha Rao Amarachinta, Narender Boggula, Achor Mohammed
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractFelodipine (FDP) is used to treat hypertension and angina pectoris. FDP has an oral bioavailability of approximately 15% in humans, primarily due to its poor water solubility and extensive first-pass metabolism in the liver and gut wall. The study aimed to develop and evaluate a lyophilized buccal wafer containing felodipine-loaded nano-spanlastics to enhance solubility, permeability, and therapeutic efficacy. FDP-loaded nano-Spanlastics (FDP-SL) using a 23 factorial design. The developed SL formulations were physiochemically characterized. In vitro permeation studies assessed trans-buccal delivery, while FDP-SL7 was incorporated into CMC wafers for mucoadhesive application. In vivo investigations evaluated the effects of the formulations. Histopathological assessment further contrasted the protective effects of the SL-7 wafer with those of Plendil on cardiac tissues. DLS studies of the modified SL-7 indicated particle size, PDI, and zeta potential values, followed by the assessment of EE (95.38 ± 0.96%), deformability index (12.46 ± 0.46 g), flux (28.51 ± 0.21 g/cm2/h), and stability, collectively demonstrating a 4.72-fold enhancement. The drug release data indicates Q24h values of 63–66% for SL formulations compared to the quick release of the suspension. Additionally, the ex vivo permeability results demonstrate that SL7 exhibits a flux of 28.51 ± 0.21 µg/cm2/hr, reflecting a 4.72-fold boost over the drug suspension. In vivo studies showed significant improvements in blood pressure. Encapsulating FDP in Tween 80-based SL and delivering it via mucoadhesive buccal wafers significantly enhances its bioavailability and therapeutic efficacy. This non-invasive approach offers a promising strategy for improved hypertension management.Keywords: Felodipine, Nano SL, Plendil, Carboxy methyl cellulose, Buccal delivery, Tween 80Subject terms: Diseases, Drug discovery, Medical research

Introduction

The buccal mucosa as a route for the systemic delivery of multiple therapeutic substances is an area of intense attention in the scientific community. In the buccal mucosa, a route convenient for an expansive region that does not wiggle about much makes it simple to administer the drug. This method works for both one- and two-way drug delivery systems1, if you’re looking for a way to get your drug into the body’s system without taking a pill, the buccal mucosa might be a great option as a delivery system. Compared to previously established drug administration methods, buccal mucosa has several advantages2,3, which include more patient compliance and reduced drug degradation in the gastrointestinal environment. It is also advantageous because it allows drugs to be easily given to children without affecting their pre-systemic metabolism, and it is also safe for the elderly, who may experience nausea when taking it. There are a few types of mucoadhesive buccal dosage forms, including liquids, semi-solids, and solid Bucco adhesive formulations4–7. Ideally, a buccal wafer would be soft, elastic, and flexible, and it would also possess strong bio adhesive characteristics to ensure more retention time in the oral cavity8,9. A combined drug release is also necessary to provide the desired therapeutic effect. Limitations of the standard oral buccal administration method include poor adhesion, uneven drug release, and irregular adhesiveness10. If you need your drug to have a local or systemic impact and fast release, the buccal route is your best route of administration11.

Mucoadhesive delivery methods have been developed due to many clinical experiments aiming to increase drug residence time with the mucosal membrane (patches, films, and wafers)12. Lyophilized polymeric gels into porous wafers that adhere to oral mucosal membranes represent a novel drug delivery method13. Upon hydration, the wafer swells into a gel-like structure that adheres to the oral mucosa, thereby prolonging residence time, improving drug penetration, and enhancing muco-adhesion. In some established systems, permeation enhancers or pH modifiers are incorporated to further facilitate drug transport across the mucosa14.

Multiple colloidal carriers have been investigated to enhance drug penetration across biological membranes. Conventional vesicles such as liposomes and niosomes, while biocompatible, often exhibit limited deformability and relatively rigid bilayer structures due to their phospholipid or surfactant composition. This rigidity can restrict their ability to traverse tight intercellular junctions. In contrast, newer soft nanocarriers such as spanlastics incorporate edge activators that impart high elasticity and deformability, enabling them to squeeze through pores smaller than their own size and thereby improve penetration15.

Niosomes are colloidal drug carriers constructed from non-ionic surfactants. Their chemical stability and enhanced drug distribution to different body parts have attracted much interest16. SL, sometimes called elastic niosomes, are a novel class of vesicle-based drug-delivery devices. Like niosomes, vesicles are made of a non-ionized surfactant with an edge activator added. Although these devices were initially described in 2001 by Kakkar and Kaur for use in the ocular systems, their applications have since expanded to other body parts17. An edge activator makes the SL bilayers more pliable and stretcher, allowing drugs to pass through biological membranes more easily. Enhancing bioavailability and therapeutic efficacy has benefitted numerous medicines18–24. For example, span 60/polyvinyl alcohol SL significantly outperformed the control group regarding trans nasal penetration and brain distribution.

Meanwhile, the elastic properties of SL increased the perceived fluconazole corneal penetration coefficient by three compared to the commercially available formulation25. In a study of male Wistar rats, SL exhibited greater epigallocatechin gallate oral bioavailability and prolonged drug release than niosomes26. These elastic vesicles have increased the cornea’s and the skin’s permeability to anti-fungal medications27. Still, increasing buccal permeation using SL has not been tried to find their effectiveness. The potentiality of SL will improve the efficacy of anticancer treatments and address their limitations, which have not yet been thoroughly studied, despite the aforementioned fascinating properties of this class of molecules. Furthermore, no reports are available for using SL against hypertension.

The dihydropyridine calcium-channel blocker FDP helps treat hypertension and chronic stable angina pectoris used alone or in conjunction with other medications. Because of its low water solubility and extensive hepatic metabolism during the first pass, the oral bioavailability of FDP, a BCS class-II medication, is only 15%28. Improved bioavailability of FDP has prompted the development of various state-of-the-art drug delivery systems. For example, there are solid lipid nanoparticles29, solid dispersion, nanosuspension30, complexation with beta-cyclodextrin31, microemulsion32, self-emulsifying drug delivery systems, chitosan microparticles, and solid dispersion. One way to improve the solubility of BCS-II medicines, avoid initial metabolism, and speed up the beginning of action is by using a buccal delivery technique based on FDP micro elastic vesicles. When used in conjunction, SL and buccal wafers may improve the overall oral bioavailability of FDP. Systematic and scientific, the design of Experiments (DoE) is used in the pharmaceutical industry to create new drugs. Planned formulations may benefit from the DoE planning platform by reducing the required tests and revealing interactions among formulation factors33.

Controlled release systems and their applications often use carboxymethylcellulose, an anionic cellulose ether. It has been found that CMC formulations have a high preference for moist areas, such as the nasal and buccal cavities34. Its bio-adhesives, powerful film-forming capabilities, rapid hydration, and subsequent swelling to a thick gel make it ideal for exercising control over drug release. CMC is an excellent collaborator for developing buccal wafers35.

This study concentrates on the development of SL vesicles incorporated into a buccal wafer system, a novel combination that has not been thoroughly investigated in previous research regarding FDP. The SL system, in contrast to traditional buccal formulations, provides superior elasticity and deformability, enabling deeper mucosal penetration and enhanced drug delivery. Previous studies have investigated FDP delivery through oral or transdermal methods; however, buccal administration utilizing SL provides a non-invasive alternative that circumvents first-pass metabolism, thereby enhancing bioavailability. The application of Tween 80 and Brij 35 as edge activators facilitates the precise adjustment of vesicle characteristics, including particle size and EE, resulting in an enhanced formulation with improved pharmacodynamic effects. The formulation was optimized through a 32 full factorial design and response surface methodology, guaranteeing a statistically validated and efficient design approach, in contrast to the trial-and-error methods prevalent in numerous prior reports. This innovative integration of SL technology and buccal wafer system offers a promising approach for the effective administration of FDP in hypertension treatment, resulting in improved therapeutic results.

The present study aimed to monitor the control release formulation of FDP by release mechanisms by formulating novel SL. To further improve the biological accessibility of the drug, A 1% CMC wafer was used to embed the manufactured SL. Patients may find the mucoadhesive, oral, physiologically inert dosage form of Plendil 5 mg tablet acceptable after it underwent in vivo study (medicinal properties and biochemistry analysis) and cardiac histology to prove a causal association between them. The performance of an innovative SL wafer for the buccal administration of FDP is being studied in a controlled environment.

Using Design Expert software (Version 12.0.3.0, Stat-Ease Inc., MN, USA), a range of eight formulas were visually displayed. This three-factor, two-level design uses a categorical element for the edge activator (EA) type and a numerical element for the Span 60 weight ratio: EA and stirring speed. Preparation of optimized FDP-SL7 loaded mucoadhesive buccal wafer

A mucoadhesive buccal wafer was made with the optimized Spanlastics formulation (SL7) and a matrix-forming polymer of carboxymethyl cellulose (CMC). After preparing a distilled water solution of 1%w/v CMC, the air bubbles were let to stand until they were all gone. The addition of propylene glycol 2–3 drops and mannitol (0.05%w/w) enhanced the formulation’s cryoprotective capabilities during freeze-drying by increasing its flexibility and porosity. The SL7 dispersion, which contained 5%w/w of FDP, was thereafter mixed gently into the polymeric solution until it was completely dissolved. A precise 5 g of the homogenous gel was transferred into bespoke silicone moulds with dimensions of 1.5 cm in diameter and 0.5 cm in thickness. After being frozen for 24 h, the moulds were lyophilized. The technique produced porous wafers that were both light and sponge-like, with an increased surface area for fast hydration, better mucoadhesion, and controlled drug release. In order to keep the lyophilized wafers structurally and pharmaceutically intact until further testing, they were placed in a desiccator to withdraw them from ambient moisture. This approach to formulation increased bioavailability and patient compliance by dispersing nano-spanlastic vesicles evenly across the mucoadhesive matrix, providing a potential platform for buccal delivery of FDP37. A desiccator was used to keep the finished wafers dry until they were ready to be utilized. To construct a SL free wafer for comparison with SL wafers, we prepared an FDP (5%w/w) solution in 1% CMC and cast the solution into a wafer.

Loading capacity (LC%) and entrapment efficiency (%EE)

A method reported by Loureiro Contente et al. was adopted with few modifications to determine the EE. Briefly, 1 mL of each formulation was taken in Eppendorf tubes and centrifuged for 50 min at 14,000 rpm at 4 °C using the ultra-cooling centrifuge (Beckman, Fullerton, Canada) to separate the precipitated Spanlastical vesicle. The supernatant layer of the separated formulation was diluted with 3 mL of ethanol. The sediment portion was subsequently dissolved into 3 mL of ethanol. The drug was quantified by measuring absorbance at 364 nm (λ max) using the UV/visible spectrophotometer and the results were calculated from a previously constructed FDP calibration curve in pH 6.8. The separated precipitates were then washed with a phosphate buffer of pH 6.8, isolated again carefully using the same conditions, and then disrupted by sonication with ethanol to estimate the FDP amount entrapped inside the formed vesicles spectrophotometrically at the same λmax. Experimental protocol

Six rats in the four groups were randomly assigned. Carboxymethylcellulose (CMC) at a concentration of 0.5% was orally administered once daily to normal rats in the control group for 28 days. The dosage used was 3 mL/kg. Sodium cadmium chloride (1 mg/kg body weight/day) was injected intraperitoneally into the CdCl2 group for 14 days, after which the animals were given 0.5% CMC as a vehicle. CdCl2 + FDP-SL7 rats were given intraperitoneal injections of an initial 14-day course of cadmium chloride (1 mg/kg b. wt./day), followed by 5 mg/kg of FDP-SL7. During the initial week, rats assigned to Group CdCl2 + FD-SL -loaded wafers (FDP-SLW) were injected intraperitoneally, including cadmium chloride (1 mg/kg body weight/day). Then, they were given buccal administrations of FDP-SLW administered at 5 mg/kg of FDP for the next 14 days. The rats in the CdCl2 + Plendil group were given intraperitoneal injections after 14 days of cadmium chloride (1 mg/kg b. wt./day), Plendil (5 mg/kg in 0.5% CMC) orally once daily for another 14 days.

Rodents were separated into groups and examined to calculate the efficacy of different therapies. Pre- and post-treatment weights were recorded to see how the treatments affected the rats’ overall body mass. All the rats in the research had their cervical vertebrae dislocated after finishing the experiment for sacrificing, and their blood was withdrawn for further analysis. Separated serum was tested using industrial enzymatic kits for lactate dehydrogenase60 and creatine phosphokinase61 activity (Reactivos GPL, Barcelona, Spain). There was also an attempt to estimate cholesterol, triglycerides, HDL, and LDL levels. Additionally, a readymade kit (Biodiagnostic, Egypt) was used to assess total antioxidant capacity (TAC). We followed the procedures outlined in the catalog for each test.

Data analysis of pharmacokinetic parameters

Study design

Wistar rats (100–150 g) were divided into two groups (n = 4/group) and designated as control, Optimized SL wafer group. The rats were individually housed, fasted overnight. Water was allowed ad libitum. Animals in control group were administered an oral suspension of FDP (0.15 mg/kg) with 2 mL of water and test group animals were treated with FDP of equivalent dose size.

Sample collection, preparation and chromatographic condition

Blood samples were collected by retro-orbital plexus puncture under light anesthesia (using diethyl ether/isoflurane) at predetermined time intervals. This method was chosen because it allows for the collection of sufficient sample volume (0.3–0.5 mL per draw) with minimal stress when performed by trained personnel. The samples were immediately transferred into heparinized microcentrifuge tubes, centrifuged at 5000 rpm for 10 min, and plasma was separated and stored at − 20 °C until HPLC analysis64–68. Blood sample (0.5 mL) was collected using a 26-Gx 11/4 in (0.7 mm × 30 mm) 2 mL syringe (Dispo Van, HMD, Faridabad, Haryana, India) at 0, 1, 2, 4, 6, 12, 24 and 48 h. The collected samples were stored in 5 mL heparinized plastic plasma tubes (150 USP Units of sodium heparin spray coated, 13 mm × 7.5 mm, with BD hemogaurd closer, BD and Co. NJ, and USA). The collected samples were centrifuged at 3500 rpm for 15 min to separate the plasma and the separated plasma was transferred into micro centrifuge tubes (Sigma Aldrich, USA) to obtain cellular debris free plasma by further centrifugation at 12,000 rpm. The supernatant was collected and stored at − 20 °C until analysis. The FDP plasma level in the supernatant was determined by a validated HPLC assay method14 using HPLC (Waters, 2695, USA) with variable wavelength UV detector (Aquity T UV Ch A) operated at 237 nm. Column used was C18 (Water, BEH, C18) of dimension 150 mm × 4.6 mm and a flow rate of 0.8 mL/min was maintained for the mobile phase. The mobile phase was acetonitrile: Water (80:20 v/v) filtered through 0.45 μm nylon membrane filter and degassed in sonicator for 15 min.

Sample analysis

Calibration standards in plasma were prepared by spiking 100 μL blank rat plasma with a stock solution of FDP. Stock solutions of FDP (100-μg/mL) and nifedipine (10-μg/mL) as internal standard were prepared in 30% v/v methanol. The secondary standard solutions were prepared by diluting the stock solutions with 30% v/v methanol. The working standard solutions were prepared by diluting the secondary solutions with blank plasma. Eight calibration standards of final concentration of 0.1, 0.5, 1.0, 5.0, 10.0 and 20.0 ng/mL were prepared. 50 μL nifedipine solution was added to each plasma sample as an internal standard and analyzed by HPLC.

Pharmacokinetic analysis

The pharmacokinetic parameters (Cmax, Tmax, area under the curve [AUC0-∞], Ka, Ke, ta1/2, t1/2) were estimated from the plasma concentration versus time profile (PKSolver)68,69.

Histopathological studies

Heart tissue was collected using autopsy procedures of various rats set aside in a group for 24 h in 10% saline. Test solutions were prepared by using serial dilutions of alcohol to dehydrate, and they were washed in running water (methyl and ethyl). The cardiac tissue samples were washed in xylene and stored in a hot air oven for 24 h after being heated in paraffin wax to 56 °C. The sections were cut using a microtome to obtain the desired thickness of 4 µm. Hematoxylin, eosin, and alizarin red were used to stain the specimen sections before they were placed on slides and observed under a microscope. All rats were humanely euthanized in accordance with CPCSEA guidelines. Animals were first anesthetized using intraperitoneal injection of ketamine (80 mg/kg) and xylazine (10 mg/kg) to ensure complete unconsciousness and elimination of pain perception. Once deep anaesthesia was confirmed by absence of reflex responses, euthanasia was performed via cervical dislocation. This method was selected as it is rapid, reliable, and ethically accepted when performed under deep anaesthesia. All procedures were reviewed and approved by the Institutional Animal Ethics Committee (IAEC) of the School of Pharmacy, Andhra University, Visakhapatnam, Andhra Pradesh 530,003 (Protocol No. AU/SOP/IAEC-AHT-045).

Statistical analysis

The data is provided as three independent replications (n = 3) ’ of mean and stand-ard deviation. Data were analyzed using Graph-pad Prism Software (San Diego, CA, USA). The significance values in this study were determined using one-way ANOVA and Tukey–Kramer multiple comparisons testing. P 0.05 is the threshold for statistical significance.

FDP-SL preparation

Multiple FDP nano-Spanlastics were generated via the ethanol injection technique. Preliminary investigations (data not presented) revealed that 5 min of sonication was necessary to attain fine dispersions. The incorporation of Span 60, comprising saturated alkyl chains (C18), leads to the formation of more stiff bilayers. This rigidity improves stability and drug entrapment efficiency (EE) through denser packing of lipid chains; however, it may diminish vesicle deformability. Ethanol functions as a bilayer fluidizer and permeability enhancer. Ethanol integrates into the lipid bilayer, disturbing the organized lipid arrangement, which enhances membrane fluidity, diminishes vesicle size, and promotes increased drug encapsulation and mucosal penetration. The lipid-soluble characteristics of Span 60 (HLB = 4.7) facilitate vesicle formation, but the inclusion of edge activators diminishes interfacial tension, resulting in smaller, more stable nano-dispersions. Moreover, the inclusion of ethanol alters the vesicle surface charge to a negative zeta potential, hence enhancing steric stability and inhibiting aggregation”70.

SL elasticity

Elasticity is a crucial characteristic of deformable vesicles that distinguishes them from conventional vesicles in traversing biological membranes80. The inclusion of an emulsifying agent (Tween 80 or Brij 35) would enhance the elasticity of these vesicles, enabling larger vesicles to compress, traverse biological membranes, and thereby improve drug permeability81. Furthermore, employing high HLB surfactants (hydrophilic) can compromise the vesicular membranes and produce systems with varying degrees of deformability. Governed by the water gradient, these elastic vesicles can traverse smaller apertures than their diameters. Table 3 presents the DI values of nano-spanlastics, which varied from 5.62 ± 0.86 to 12.46 ± 0.46 g. The formulas established using Tween 80 exhibited elevated DI values. This may arise from the composition of the EA set within the vesicular membrane; EA with a more voluminous structure yields less pliable membranes43. Tween 80 had a less voluminous structure than Brij 3526, resulting in the formation of the most elastic vesicles. Furthermore, the findings indicated that the flexibility of the vesicles augmented with a higher concentration of EA. This may result from the fluidization of the bilayer vesicle caused by the elevated concentration of EA. The greatest deformability was observed in formula SL7, which included Tween 80 as an emulsifying agent at a concentration of 30%. Comparable results indicated that nano spanlastics utilizing Tween 80 as an emulsifying agent exhibited more deformability than those with Brij; furthermore, the formulation comprising Tween 80 at a concentration of 30% relative to the total formula weight demonstrated the highest deformability. To enhance the flexibility of the medication delivery system, SL formulations were developed. The efficacy of nano-carriers in traversing biological barriers, such as the stratum corneum for transdermal distribution or tight endothelial junctions for systemic circulation, is significantly influenced by their flexibility. The formulation of SL comprises non-ionic surfactants and edge activators. A significant characteristic of these materials is the existence of ultra-deformable vesicles, which can alter their shape without sacrificing structural integrity. The encapsulated medication may exhibit enhanced bioavailability and permeability due to this property. To ascertain the suitability of the SL formulation for enhancing medication delivery efficacy and to improve its characteristics, evaluating its elasticity is crucial74,82.

The SL7 optimized nano-Spanlastics formulation was developed using the ethanol injection technique and a 70:30 ratio of Span 60 to Tween 80 as the edge activator. SL7 exhibited superior physicochemical properties compared to all other tested formulations. The vesicles exhibited a size of 132.64 ± 3.95 nm, an encapsulation efficiency (EE) of 95.38 ± 0.96%, a zeta potential of − 30.19 ± 1.39 mV, and a deformability index of 12.46 ± 0.46, indicating that the vesicles were very stable, flexible, and uniform. The cumulative drug release reached 95.84% in vitro, while the permeability coefficient was 0.0086 ± 0.0001, accompanied by a flow of 28.51 ± 0.21 µg/cm2/h in ex vivo permeation experiments across goat buccal mucosa, yielding a 4.72-fold enhancement compared to the unmodified drug. The scanning electron microscope (SEM) images of the lyophilized wafers revealed a porous, sponge-like matrix conducive to controlled release and mucoadhesion, but the transmission electron microscopy (TEM) analysis displayed smooth, spherical vesicles. The formulation’s durability was validated by stability studies performed over 6 months at 4–8 °C, demonstrating minimal variation in vesicle size and drug release (f2 = 66). SL7 emerged as an appealing choice for buccal distribution of FDP to boost bioavailability and therapeutic efficacy, facilitated by the critical selection of 30% Tween 80 to improve the membrane fluidity and deformability of the vesicles.

This morphological evidence corroborates the physicochemical findings and fortifies the overall interpretation of the formulation’s potential for improved buccal drug delivery. SEM images of an SL7-loaded wafer revealed a porous, foam-like structure. The lyophilization procedure was probably responsible for the effect. With a well-connected wafer network, pore sizes between 120.1 and 251.36 µm were found, providing insight into the regulated release of FDP wafer37.

Bio adhesion/muco-adhesion study

Mucoadhesion is considered to occur in three stages: wetting, interpenetration, and mechanical interlocking between mucus and polymer. The strength of mucoadhesion is affected by various factors such as molecular weight of the polymers, contact time with mucus, swelling rate of the polymer, and the biological membrane used in the study. Incorporation of CMC, a water-soluble hydrophilic polymer, reduced significantly the mucoadhesive strength of bucco-adhesive wafers. Mucoadhesive strength of the optimized formula SL7 wafer was found to be 8.36 g. Addition of CMC exhibited good physical and mechanical properties, but with short adhesion time and poor bio-adhesive strength. CMC appeared to be useful, in low concentrations for increasing the mechanical strength of wafers. CMC had good bio-adhesive strength, so wafers formulated with CMC along with SL7 exhibited satisfactory bio-adhesive and physical properties. The therapeutic benefit of the SL-7 wafer technology was further confirmed by in vivo experiments. In comparison to rats given Plendil, rats given FDP-SLW demonstrated much better control of blood pressure, lower levels of oxidative stress indicators (ROS, NO, TAC), and beneficial modulation of lipid profiles (LDL, HDL, cholesterol, triglycerides). In comparison to the Plendil group, which showed minor myocardial vessel dilation, histopathological examination of cardiac tissues demonstrated that FDP-SLW provided better myocardial protection, exhibiting intact tissue architecture with low inflammation. The results show that the SL-7 buccal wafer delivery system is effective in hypertensive patients because it increases systemic exposure and protects the cardiovascular system by avoiding hepatic first-pass metabolism. It also offers a prolonged, high-permeability treatment strategy.

The pharmacodynamic and biochemical assessment of FDP-loaded SL has improved in prior studies. This was to be anticipated because of the greater uptake of SL via the buccal mucosa of sheep compared to that of a drug solution. This was further supported by CLSM’s demonstration of increased mucosal penetration. Also, unlike the oral solution and the conventional gel, SL improved drug penetration, as found by El Menshawi et al.101. It’s possible that the incredibly malleable nanocarrier is to blame for this since it weakens the subcutaneous barrier function and so facilitates more penetration while avoiding the first-pass impact often seen with oral administration. Furthermore, this may be explained by the enhanced trans location of a lipophilic drug across biological membranes made possible by nano vesicular formulations. While the findings demonstrate promising results for buccal delivery of FDP using nano-spanlastic vesicle wafers in animal models, caution is needed in extrapolating these results directly to humans. Differences in buccal mucosal physiology, drug metabolism, and hypertension pathophysiology between rats and humans may affect translation. Furthermore, the ex vivo buccal permeation models (goat and bovine mucosa) do not fully replicate human mucosal conditions. Clinical studies are required to confirm bioavailability enhancement and therapeutic benefits in humans”.

Pharmacokinetics The plasma concentration–time profile showed that FDP-wafer exhibited higher plasma drug concentration at all-time points than optimized SL and marketed formulation. The rapid appearance of drug in plasma is attributed to the high permeation ability of marketed formulation when applied under nonoccluded conditions. The peak plasma concentration (Cmax) displayed by FDP wafer was 18.69 ± 0.28 ng/mL while control marketed formulation attained 3.19 ± 0.14 ng/mL in 9.36 ± 2.15 and 3.58 ± 1.68 h (tmax) respectively. Considering the data beyond the Cmax, followed first order kinetics, the elimination rate (Ke), was insignificantly different (p > 0.05) for FDP-SLW and oral control formulation (0.068 ± 0.03 and 0.0928 ± 0.02 h − 1 respectively). The absorption rate constant (Ka) was 0.459 ± 0.04 for FDP-SLW and 0.159 ± 0.02 − h for oral control formulation. The higher absorption rate of FDP-SLW was a result of the fast permeation ability of ultra-deformable vesicular system, which made FDP rapidly available for absorption. Higher absorption coupled with lower tmax is expected to improve bioavailability that was verified by AUC determination. AUC0-∞ that directly reflects bioavailability was 53.69 ± 2.95 ng.h/mL and 185.94 ± 8. 96 ng.h/mL for oral control formulation and FDP-SLW. Consequently, the relative bioavailability (Fr) of FDP-SLW was 426.59%. The increase in bioavailability may be attributed to the avoidance of hepatic first pass metabolism of FDP and supports the ex vivo permeation data that suggested rapid drug permeation with negligible lag time. A significant enhancement in bioavailability and therapeutic efficacy was achieved by incorporating FDP into Tween 80-based nano-spanlastics (SL-7) and delivering it via mucoadhesive buccal wafers (FDP-SLW). The pharmacokinetic study showed that compared to the oral control (Plendil), FDP-SLW had a relative bioavailability of 426.59%, as the substantially higher AUC₀-∞ value (185.94 ± 8.96 ng·h/mL vs. 53.69 ± 2.95 ng·h/mL) indicated. In comparison to the oral control, FDP-SLW had a significantly higher absorption rate constant (Ka) of 0.459 ± 0.04 h−1, and a shorter tₘₐₓ of 3.58 ± 1.68 h (Fig. 9), suggesting that the medication was absorbed more quickly and efficiently. Because of its low water solubility and substantial first-pass metabolism, the calcium channel blocker FDP has a limited oral bioavailability, making it ineffective in the management of hypertension. A mucoadhesive buccal wafer was modified with a nano-spanlastic formulation (SL-7) to provide a non-invasive, sustained, and targeted drug delivery system, which allowed for the elimination of these constraints. To determine the therapeutic effectiveness, pharmacodynamic response, and systemic safety of the optimized formulation, it was necessary to conduct in vivo evaluations, even though in vitro and ex vivo investigations verified better drug release and mucosal permeability.

The purpose of this in vivo study was to compare the SL-7 wafer to the commercially available formulation (Plendil) and determine whether it had better antihypertensive, cardio protective, and antioxidant benefits. The goal of the study was to find out how the buccal delivery system affected cardiovascular health by looking at important biomarkers like systolic and diastolic blood pressure, lipid profiles (LDL, HDL, total cholesterol, triglycerides), and oxidative stress markers (MDA, GSH, catalase). Beyond the realm of pharmacokinetics, histological analysis of cardiac tissues provided conclusive proof of tissue preservation and structural restoration, establishing the SL-7 wafer’s biological efficacy.

Therefore, the in vivo model was essential for showing that the new nano-spanlastic buccal wafer formulation has demonstrable therapeutic benefits and enhances drug administration; this supports its potential as an alternative to traditional oral medication for hypertension management.

Conclusion

Felodipine-loaded spanlastics were synthesized via an ethanol injection method utilizing two edge activators: Tween 80 and Brij 35. Various doses of edge activators were employed to formulate spanlastics with ideal characteristics. The improved nano-spanlastic formulation SL7 was developed with an emphasis on surface response studies. The application of Tween 80 markedly influences particle size reduction, enhances entrapment efficiency, and contributes to superior deformability in the nano-Spanlastics membrane. The kind of edge activator influenced the particle size of Spanlastics, with Tween 80 resulting in the smallest particle size (132.64 ± 3.95 nm). A 32 complete factorial design was employed to optimize the nano-Spanlastics formulations. The ideal vesicular formulation (SL7) exhibited a diminutive vesicle size, spherical morphology, and a comparatively high drug encapsulation efficiency (EE%). The ex-vivo permeation investigations indicated that encapsulating felodipine in extremely flexible non-ionic surfactant-based vesicles, known as nano-Spanlastics, successfully enhanced drug penetration across sheep membranes. In comparison to a medication suspension, nano Spanlastics exhibited a significant enhancement in buccal permeability. A nano-Spanlastics-loaded wafer was effectively fabricated using 1% CMC, exhibiting satisfactory properties and adequate residence time to provide sufficient contact for penetration.

The in vivo study demonstrated a substantial improvement in systolic blood pressure (SBP), diastolic blood pressure (DBP), oxidative stress biomarkers, cardiac indicators, and lipid profile. Histopathological analyses demonstrated that the SL-7 wafer outperforms Plendil® in safeguarding cardiac tissue; this superiority may be attributed to the evasion of first-pass metabolism associated with the oral administration of Plendil®, in addition to the enhanced drug permeation in the nano-spanlastic-loaded wafer compared to Plendil®, as evidenced by permeation and CLSM studies. The collected findings indicated that a nano-spanlastic-based wafer offers a significant enhancement in the transbuccal administration of FDP, demonstrating superior efficacy in protecting heart tissue compared to the existing product (Plendil®).

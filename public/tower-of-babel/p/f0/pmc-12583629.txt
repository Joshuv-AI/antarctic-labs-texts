Nested named entity recognition in traditional Chinese medicine electronic medical records via dual-granularity feature augmentation and span classification
Authors: Luo Minghao, Ye Qing, Cheng Chunlei, LIn Peng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractNamed Entity Recognition (NER) plays a crucial role in extracting important information such as treatment methods, symptoms, and herbal prescriptions from Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) electronic medical records. However, existing NER methods often struggle with the complexity and variability of TCM language, especially when dealing with overlapping or nested entities. To address these issues, we propose DG-SpanTCM, a novel framework that enhances character-level text understanding using a pre-trained language model and improves entity recognition through lexical-semantic features and robust training strategies. Our method also incorporates techniques to handle label imbalance and better identify complex entity structures. Experiments on a real-world TCM dataset show that DG-SpanTCM achieves superior performance, improving the F1-score over strong baseline models. These findings highlight the potential of DG-SpanTCM in advancing automated information extraction for TCM texts.Keywords: Nested named entity recognition, Feature enhancement, Span classification, Traditional chinese medicine electronic medical recordsSubject terms: Medical research, Computer science

Introduction

In recent years, artificial intelligence (AI) technology has achieved groundbreaking advancements in the field of natural language processing (NLP), with core applications spanning medical text analysis, knowledge graph construction, and clinical decision support. This technological evolution coincides with a critical phase of structural adjustments in China’s healthcare system. According to 2024 statistics from the National Health Commission, the disparity in the number of licensed physicians per 1,000 people between urban and rural areas reaches 2.3 times1, while the number of tertiary hospitals in the eastern region is 1.8 times that in the western region, highlighting a pronounced spatial imbalance in medical resource distribution2,3. Against this backdrop, traditional Chinese medicine (TCM), known for its simplicity, convenience, effectiveness, and affordability, has demonstrated increasing advantages. Its diagnostic and treatment system, which relies on natural medicinal materials and holistic differentiation thinking, boasts a 78% coverage rate4 in primary healthcare institutions, playing a crucial role in alleviating the structural contradictions in medical resource allocation5,6.

As a vital component of healthcare informatization, the electronic medical record (EMR) system for Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) plays an important role in preserving and managing clinical information. It captures patients’ four-diagnostic data, tracks syndrome progression, and systematically documents empirical knowledge such as herbal prescriptions and dosage adjustments7.

Named entity recognition (NER) enables the extraction of TCM-related entities and their relationships from unstructured medical texts, laying the foundation for building intelligent TCM knowledge bases8. For example, in the medical record statement “The dosage of Paeoniae Alba in the modified Guizhi Decoction was increased to 15 grams,” multiple semantic layers coexist, including prescription names (Guizhi Decoction), medicinal materials (Paeoniae Alba), dosage information (15 g), and treatment actions (modification). These entities often appear in hierarchical or overlapping formats, where one entity is embedded within another. Such nested expressions are common in TCM narratives, but traditional NER methods, which typically rely on flat tagging schemes, struggle to represent and extract them accurately. This disconnect between real-world medical language and model design poses a significant challenge to the digital transformation of TCM knowledge systems.

Amidst the ongoing digital transformation of TCM, notable progress has been made. Statistics indicate that the coverage rate of EMR systems in tertiary TCM hospitals nationwide has reached 92%9, generating over 5 billion structured TCM diagnosis and treatment records annually10. Deep learning-based NER models have demonstrated remarkable advantages in symptom extraction and syndrome classification. Current research predominantly employs two paradigms for TCM text parsing: sequence labeling for flat entity recognition and span-based methods for nested structure detection. Notably, more than 63% of entities in classical TCM literature exhibit nesting, and this proportion rises to 72% in modern EMRs due to the coexistence of TCM and Western medical terminologies11. Such nested structures often embody critical syndrome differentiation and treatment logic. For example, in the phrase “Palpitations of liver-kidney yin deficiency type,” the hierarchical association between syndrome and symptoms is essential for clinical decision support system development.

Despite significant advancements in TCM entity recognition, several challenges persist in practical applications:

1)Domain-Specific Complexity of TCM Texts: TCM EMRs encompass traditional TCM expressions (e.g., “wiry-thin pulse”), Western medical terms (e.g., “elevated CRP”), and composite prescription descriptions (e.g., “Chaihu Shugan San combined with Liuwei Dihuang Wan”). Studies indicate that general-domain pre-trained language models exhibit an F1-score 19.8% points lower than domain-specific medical models in TCM NER tasks8. Additionally, inconsistent documentation practices in primary TCM hospitals, such as the use of abbreviations (e.g., “Shenqi” for certain herbs), create substantial semantic gaps at both character and lexical levels, complicating precise entity boundary detection.

2)Technical Bottlenecks in Nested Entity Recognition: Traditional sequence labeling models can only identify single-layer entities, whereas TCM texts frequently contain multi-level nested structures, such as “symptom-syndrome-treatment-formula.” For example, in the phrase “Modified Guipi Decoction for treating insomnia of heart-spleen deficiency type,” the treatment method entity “Modified Guipi Decoction” contains the prescription entity “Guipi Decoction.” Existing studies predominantly employ cascade recognition frameworks12, yet error propagation significantly reduces recall rates for deep-layer entities. Furthermore, TCM entity boundary ambiguity poses challenges, as in “Xiao Qinglong Decoction Syndrome,” where syndrome and prescription are tightly coupled, making it difficult for conventional span classification methods to disentangle semantics accurately.

3)Data Imbalance and Noise Interference: TCM EMRs exhibit a pronounced long-tail distribution. A study from a tertiary hospital revealed that over 58% of rare syndromes appear fewer than five times per year, whereas high-frequency entities account for more than 70%13. Existing approaches often rely on simple resampling strategies, which may lead to model overfitting to noisy samples, such as mislabeling due to character similarity (e.g., “Huangqi” vs. “Zhihuangqi”). Additionally, dialect expressions and spelling errors in EMRs exacerbate label noise interference.

To address these challenges, this study proposes DG-SpanTCM, a span-based nested NER method enhanced with dual-granularity text features. Specifically, the contributions of this study are as follows:

1)Dual-Granularity Feature Fusion Framework: By integrating BERT with SoftLexicon, we achieve character-word dual-granularity semantic encoding, constructing a TCM-adaptive feature space. This effectively resolves challenges related to terminology abbreviations and cross-domain expressions. Experimental results indicate improved accuracy in nested entity recognition.

2)Adversarially Enhanced Span Classification Paradigm: We introduce adversarial training to generate perturbed samples for expanding decision boundaries and employ a multiplicative attention mechanism to model dependencies among entity spans. Compared to traditional cascade methods, our approach improves the F1-score of nested entity recognition while reducing error propagation rates.

3)Dynamic Weighting Loss Function for Long-Tail Distributions: We propose an adaptive classification penalty weight adjustment based on entity frequency, significantly enhancing performance in rare entity recognition tasks. Additionally, a noise-aware training strategy effectively mitigates the impact of mislabeling on model performance.

The remainder of this paper is structured as follows. Section 2 reviews related work and discusses the rationale behind each module in our system design. Section 3 elaborates on the proposed span-based TCM nested NER method. Section 4 presents details on the datasets used and experimental results, demonstrating the superiority of our approach.

Related work

In recent years, artificial intelligence (AI) technology has been increasingly applied to the structured analysis of traditional Chinese medicine electronic medical records (TCM-EMRs), with nested named entity recognition (NNER) emerging as a key technique for extracting deep semantic information from medical texts14. The concept of named entity recognition (NER) was first formally introduced at the Sixth Message Understanding Conference, with the primary goal of detecting and classifying predefined entity types in unstructured texts. Since then, NER has become a prominent research focus in the field of natural language processing (NLP), with current studies mainly concentrating on model architecture optimization, domain knowledge integration, and annotation strategy improvements.

Some researchers have focused on the hierarchical structure of text and its impact on NER tasks. Manigandan proposed a BiLSTM-CRF-based entity recognition method that enhances the modeling of interactions between character-level and word-level information15, thereby improving the accuracy of complex entity boundary identification. However, this approach struggles to capture long-range dependencies in lengthy texts, limiting its effectiveness in recognizing nested entities across sentences. To address this limitation, Kaur proposed a Transformer-based hierarchical entity recognition framework16, which leverages a self-attention mechanism to capture cross-sentence dependencies and generates entities through a hierarchical decoding strategy. Despite its effectiveness, this method demands substantial computational resources and model parameters, which increases the risk of overfitting and makes it unsuitable for large-scale or resource-constrained EMR processing scenarios. Further, Bhushan introduced a lightweight recurrent neural network model incorporating an adaptive gating mechanism17, which reduces computational overhead while maintaining high accuracy in specialized term recognition. Nevertheless, its reliance on shallow representations limits its ability to model multi-level nested structures and semantic ambiguity in TCM texts. Compared with these approaches, our proposed method integrates both semantic-level (BERT) and lexical-level (SoftLexicon) information, while also addressing long-range dependency modeling and label imbalance through attention mechanisms and frequency-aware loss. This allows for more accurate recognition of complex nested entities in real-world TCM EMRs under practical computational constraints.

Common data augmentation techniques, such as back-translation and entity replacement, have been widely adopted, but these methods often compromise text integrity18. Adversarial training19, which improves model robustness by introducing textual perturbations, has demonstrated promising results in low-resource NER tasks. However, its effectiveness on large-scale datasets remains suboptimal, as even minor character-level perturbations may disrupt semantic coherence and introduce label noise, especially in complex domains like Traditional Chinese Medicine. Some studies have attempted to integrate adversarial training with BERT-based models to generate synthetically perturbed samples that preserve the original grammatical structure. Nonetheless, such hybrid methods often lack control over semantic drift and may produce unnatural variations that mislead the model during training. In contrast, our approach applies controlled adversarial training within a span-based framework, ensuring that generated perturbations maintain both structural and semantic fidelity while enhancing the model’s robustness to real-world textual variability.

Another key area of research involves deep learning model optimization to enhance NNER performance. Rehana proposed a hierarchical decoding method based on BERT20, incorporating a global attention mechanism to better model the hierarchical relationships between entities. However, this approach has a high computational complexity when processing long texts. To address this issue, Marutho introduced an efficient Transformer-based NNER method21, employing a sparse attention mechanism to reduce computational complexity while maintaining strong recognition performance.

Nevertheless, the aforementioned studies were not specifically designed for the TCM domain and largely lack adequate modeling of TCM-specific concepts and knowledge structures. To address this gap, a series of studies on NNER for TCM-EMRs have been conducted. Zuo proposed a semi-supervised learning method for NNER22, which utilizes information from unlabeled data to enhance model robustness. However, this approach fails to fully account for the complexity of nested entity structures. Xu developed a graph neural network (GNN)-based model23, which constructs a word co-occurrence graph and employs graph attention mechanisms to capture intricate entity relationships, demonstrating promising results in TCM syndrome prediction tasks. However, the complex contextual dependencies in TCM terminology make it difficult for graph-based methods alone to fully model the relationships. Hou approached the NNER task from a novel perspective24, introducing an active learning framework to reduce redundant manual annotation efforts. This method improves recognition performance while lowering annotation costs, but requires additional computational resources for optimal sample selection. In contrast, span classification has emerged as a novel NNER backbone architecture25, leveraging span-based decoding mechanisms to comprehensively learn contextual information, thereby improving the modeling of complex nested structures.

Another critical research direction in NNER model optimization involves addressing class imbalance issues. In nested NER tasks, the uneven distribution of entity types often leads to models being biased toward high-frequency classes. Cai introduced a boundary-aware loss function26, which integrates global semantic information to enhance the model’s focus on precise entity boundary detection. However, this approach relies heavily on annotation quality, making it susceptible to label noise. Zhang proposed a hierarchical focal loss27, which dynamically adjusts loss weights for different levels of nested entities, thereby improving recognition of low-frequency entity types and mitigating class imbalance issues.

Despite the optimizations achieved in the aforementioned studies, there remains no perfect solution for NNER in TCM-EMRs. To address this challenge, this study proposes DG-SpanTCM, a dual-granularity feature-enhanced span classification model for nested NER in TCM-EMRs. Our method integrates BERT pre-trained models with SoftLexicon-based lexicon information, enabling more accurate deep semantic understanding of TCM terminology. To tackle label imbalance issues, we introduce a novel classification loss function, significantly improving entity recognition accuracy. Additionally, our approach enhances model robustness and precision by incorporating adversarial training for sample perturbation and multiplicative attention mechanisms for entity boundary prediction.

System model design

As the core carrier of syndrome differentiation and treatment principles, Traditional Chinese Medicine Electronic Medical Records (TCM-EMRs) play a crucial role in knowledge inheritance and clinical decision-making. While nested named entity recognition (NNER) serves as a fundamental task in EMR analysis, it faces several challenges, including ambiguous entity boundaries, complex semantic nesting, and diverse domain-specific terminology. Traditional sequence labeling methods are often constrained by single-granularity features, significantly limiting their semantic representation capability and generalization performance, particularly when applied to mixed corpora containing ancient TCM texts and modern EMRs.

To address these limitations, this study proposes DG-SpanTCM, a novel NNER model specifically designed for TCM-EMRs, with its technical framework illustrated in Fig. 1. The model employs a multi-dimensional feature fusion mechanism, where character-level contextual semantic features are first captured using the BERT pre-trained language model, while SoftLexicon is incorporated to integrate domain-specific lexicon information, enabling a more comprehensive representation of TCM terminology at both the character and word levels. Additionally, an adversarial training strategy is introduced to enhance model robustness by generating perturbations, along with a multiplicative attention mechanism that dynamically focuses on key semantic units. Finally, a span-based classification architecture is designed to model nested entity relationships through multi-level feature interaction, while a class-balanced loss function is incorporated to mitigate label imbalance issues effectively. This framework ensures improved nested entity recognition accuracy, enhanced semantic understanding of TCM texts, and greater robustness in handling diverse and complex TCM-EMR corpora.

Coding layer

In the semantic coding stage of Chinese medicine electronic medical records, we designed a dual-granularity semantic enhancement module for words and phrases to address the characteristics of multi-granularity nesting of terminology (e.g., the hierarchical containment relationship between “yin deficiency of the liver and kidney” and “hypertension with yin deficiency of the liver and kidney”). This module realizes the accurate perception of the boundary of TCM entities through the deep fusion of the pre-trained language model and the domain dictionary, effectively alleviating the ambiguity and hierarchical nesting of terminological expressions.

Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) texts are characterized by rich semantic ambiguity, hierarchical expressions, and domain-specific terminology. These characteristics pose challenges for traditional static embedding methods, such as Word2Vec or GloVe, which assign the same vector to a word regardless of context. This makes BERT particularly well-suited for named entity recognition tasks in TCM electronic medical records.

Our decoding strategy is structurally similar to the biaffine span decoder32, which models span-level interactions via a scoring matrix. However, unlike the standard biaffine formulation, we do not explicitly apply a bilinear transformation between token pairs. Instead, our model directly leverages span-level classification scores derived from multi-level contextual embeddings, and integrates entity-specific attention signals. This design reduces computational complexity while preserving span discriminability, and is better suited for nested entity recognition tasks in long-text TCM narratives.

Category imbalance losses

In the task of entity recognition in TCM electronic medical records, data categories often show long-tailed distribution, i.e., some entity categories (e.g., common disease names, common symptoms) occupy a large number of samples, while data of rare categories (e.g., special TCM symptoms, rare causes of disease) are relatively scarce. This data imbalance phenomenon easily leads to overfitting of high-frequency categories and insufficient recognition of low-frequency categories during model training, which affects the overall performance and generalization ability. To address this problem, we propose the Adaptive Class Weight Loss (ACW Loss) function, which optimizes the model’s classification ability on long-tailed distribution data by dynamically adjusting the class weights, and combines with a noise-aware training strategy to reduce the impact of mislabeled samples, thereby improving the robustness of entity recognition.

The traditional cross-entropy loss function assigns the same weight to all categories, resulting in the gradient of high-frequency categories dominating the training process, making it difficult for entities in low-frequency categories to be recognized correctly. Labeling inconsistencies and data noise (e.g., different expressions of the same concept, differences in doctors’ labeling styles) are more common in TCM EHRs. If the model is over-sensitive to the learning of these noisy samples, it may affect the final classification effect. Therefore, we incorporate Noise-aware Training (NAT) strategy in the training process to reduce the impact of mislabeled data on the model. Specifically, at the beginning of training, we assign the same weight to all samples; as training proceeds, if the prediction confidence of a sample continues to fluctuate, we reduce its gradient contribution to avoid the interference of mislabeled data on the model.

The ablation results demonstrate that each component of the proposed nested NER framework for TCM contributes meaningfully to overall performance. The full model achieves an F1-score of 70.53%, outperforming all ablated variants and confirming the value of multi-module integration. Specifically:

1) Removing adversarial training results in a 3.96% drop in F1, indicating its role in enhancing boundary recognition robustness;

2) Removing lexical matching reduces F1 by 2.71%, validating the importance of dual-granularity feature fusion—particularly the contribution of SoftLexicon to capturing complex TCM expressions;

3) The joint removal of both modules further reduces F1 to 68.83%, suggesting a complementary effect between the two;

4) Replacing the dynamic loss function increases recall but significantly lowers precision, causing a 2.63% drop in F1, underscoring its effectiveness in addressing label imbalance.

These findings confirm that the proposed components—feature fusion, adversarial augmentation, and adaptive loss—work in concert to improve recognition accuracy while maintaining adaptability to the nuanced structure of TCM language.

Summary and future prospects

To address the semantic ambiguity and annotation challenges in nested entity recognition for TCM EMRs, this study proposes a multi-stage framework of “dual-granularity encoding – adversarial enhancement – span-level parsing.” Key innovations include: (1) a BERT-SoftLexicon fusion module that enhances boundary-level semantic representation; (2) an adversarial training strategy to improve robustness against label noise and terminology variation; and (3) an attention-guided span classification architecture that precisely decouples nested entities through a discriminative relationship matrix. Experiments show improved performance on complex multi-entity datasets and reduced inference time compared to BiLSTM-CRF baselines.

Future work will proceed in three prioritized directions. First, we will construct a TCM terminology alignment model to address historical language evolution in classical medical texts. Second, we aim to develop a lightweight, real-time edge framework for intelligent structuring of grassroots TCM records. Finally, we plan to explore the integration of NER with knowledge graph construction to advance empirical diagnosis toward data-driven intelligence. This roadmap offers a practical path to support the modernization of TCM and the development of intelligent clinical systems.

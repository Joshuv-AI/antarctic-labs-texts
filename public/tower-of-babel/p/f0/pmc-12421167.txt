CD33KO-CD33-mesothelin Loop CAR design avoids fratricide and improves efficacy of iNK cells against acute myeloid leukemia

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPatients with acute myeloid leukemia (AML) are often older, which brings challenges of endurance and persistent efficacy of autologous chimeric antigen receptor (CAR)-T cell therapies. Allogenic CAR-natural killer (NK) cell therapies may offer reduced toxicities and enhanced anti-leukemic potential against AML. CD33 CAR-NK cells have been investigated for AML therapy. However, the fratricide-mediated lysis of CD33-expressing NK cells by CD33 CAR-NK cells limits the expansion and efficacy of CD33 CAR-NK cells. Mesothelin (MSLN), a tumor differentiation antigen, is highly expressed in a fraction of patients with AML, making it a promising target for AML therapy.MethodsWe designed a novel CD33-MSLN Loop CAR (Loop CAR) and evaluated its antitumor efficacy in human umbilical cord blood-derived NK (UCB-NK) cells and human pluripotent stem cell-derived NK (hPSC-iNK) cells. To further avoid fratricide caused by endogenous CD33 expression in NK cells, we established an hPSC-derived cell line via knockout of the CD33 gene (CD33KO) and engineered Loop CAR. We generated CD33KO-Loop CAR-iNK cells using an organoid induction approach. The efficacy of CD33KO-Loop CAR-iNK cells against tumor cells expressing CD33 and MSLN was investigated both in vitro and in AML xenograft mice.ResultsLoop CAR-NK cells exhibited superior cytotoxicity against dual-antigen-positive tumor cell lines and primary AML cells compared with CD33 CAR-NK and MSLN CAR-NK cells. Moreover, Loop CAR-NK cells showed upregulated signaling pathways related to NK cell activation and cytotoxic function. The loss of CD33 in iNK cells effectively avoided fratricide, improved expansion ability, and significantly enhanced CD33 and MSLN-mediated specific cytotoxicity of Loop CAR-iNK cells. Moreover, the CD33KO-Loop CAR-iNK cells demonstrated superior tumor-killing activity in AML xenograft mouse models and significantly prolonged mouse survival.ConclusionLoop CAR empowered both UCB-NK cells and hPSC-iNK cells with superior cytotoxicity against CD33+MSLN+ tumor cells. Genetic disruption of CD33 avoided fratricide and improved efficacy of Loop CAR-iNK cells against AML. This innovative strategy possesses unique advantages and translational potential for treating AML.

Background

Acute myeloid leukemia (AML) is an aggressive hematologic malignancy that demands urgent therapy.1 As new therapeutic targets are being identified, significant efforts have highlighted mesothelin (MSLN) as a promising target in AML. The MSLN is expressed in 36% of pediatric AML cases and 14% of adult AML cases, with 76% of MSLN-positive (MSLN+) patients retaining MSLN expression at relapse. Furthermore, 4% of MSLN-negative (MSLN−) patients with AML acquire MSLN expression on relapse. The expression patterns of MSLN closely mirrored those of other well-established AML cell surface antigens, including CD117, CD33, and CD123.2 Preclinical data suggest that MSLN chimeric antigen receptor (CAR)-T cells effectively eliminate MSLN+ AML cells in xenograft models.3 Therefore, MSLN is a promising target for AML immunotherapy.

Natural killer (NK) cells have demonstrated considerable potential in enhancing outcomes for AML treatment, particularly in the context of hematopoietic stem cell transplantation.4 Adoptive transfer of NK cells for AML therapy has been widely investigated in clinical settings.5 CAR-NK cells, which combine the target killing ability of CAR technology with the innate non-specific cytotoxicity of NK cells, have shown promising results for tumor treatment.6 Dual-targeting CARs or multitargeting CARs have been designed to simultaneously target two antigens or multiple antigens expressed on tumor cells to improve the tumor-killing abilities of CAR-NK cells.7 8 CD33 CAR-NK cells, in particular, have been tested in relapsed/refractory AML therapies, with clinical data demonstrating their efficacy and safety.9 Additionally, human pluripotent stem cell (hPSC)-derived NK or CAR-NK cells have shown therapeutic potential in both preclinical and clinical settings for AML.10 11 hPSC-derived NK (iNK) cells provide a homogeneous alternative to NK cells isolated from human tissues.10 12

Despite these advancements, challenges remain in CAR immune cell therapy, such as unintended fratricide due to the shared expression of target antigens between CAR immune cells and tumor cells.13 14 The shared expression of target antigens in normal T cells and NK cells and malignant cells caused fratricide in CAR-T cells and CAR-NK cells.1315 One efficient method to avoid the fratricide is to genetically disrupt the target antigens encoding genes in CAR-T cells or CAR-NK cells, which have been reported in T-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia or T-cell lymphoma therapy using CAR-T cells and CAR-NK cells. For example, genetic disruption of CD7 in CD7 CAR-T cells or CD7 CAR-NK cells reduces fratricide and rescues cell expansion.15 16 Similarly, since CD33 is also expressed on NK cells, CD33 CAR-NK cells face similar fratricidal challenges, which can impair their expansion.14 17 Human umbilical cord blood NK (UCB-NK) cells exhibit a frequency range of 1.4%–25.5% for CD33-positive cells in their resting state. These CD33-positive UCB-NK cells show reduced cytotoxicity against K562 cells.18 Moreover, NK cells upregulate CD33 expression levels to nearly 50% during in vitro expansion.14

In this study, we designed a bivalent loop CAR targeting both CD33 and MSLN antigens (CD33-MSLN Loop CAR, denoted as Loop CAR). We engineered this Loop CAR into UCB-NK cells and hPSCs to generate Loop CAR-NK and Loop CAR-iNK cells, respectively. Both Loop CAR-NK and Loop CAR-iNK cells exhibited superior cytotoxicity against tumor cells expressing CD33 and MSLN antigens, including AML cell lines and patient-derived tumor cells. Transcriptomic analysis confirmed that the Loop CAR enhanced NK cell cytotoxicity. Additionally, we established a CD33 gene knockout (CD33KO) hPSC line expressing the Loop CAR (CD33KO-Loop CAR-hPSCs) and successfully generated CD33KO-Loop CAR-iNK cells. These CD33KO-Loop CAR-iNK cells exhibited enhanced expansion and antitumor efficacy in vitro and effectively suppressed tumor progression in xenograft AML models. Our findings highlight the clinical potential of hPSC-derived CD33KO-CD33-MSLN Loop CAR-iNK cells for the treatment of CD33+MSLN+ AML.

Methods

Generation of CAR constructs and CAR-modified NK cells

The single-chain fragment variable (scFv) specific for MSLN19 or CD3320 was used for single-targeted CAR construction (MSLN scFv-CD8α Hinge-CD8 TMD-CD3ζ, CD33 scFv-CD8α Hinge-CD8 TMD-CD3ζ). The bivalent scFv specific for MSLN and CD33 was used for CAR construction (bivalent scFv-CD8α Hinge-CD8 TMD-CD3ζ). The bivalent scFv of the Loop CAR construct was designed as follows: CD33 (VL)-G4S linker-MSLN (VH)-whitlow linker21-MSLN (VL)-G4S linker-CD33 (VH). The bivalent scFv of the tandem CAR construct was designed as follows: CD33 (VL)-(G4S)3 linker-CD33 (VH)-(G4S)5 linker-MSLN (VH)-(G4S)2 linker-MSLN (VL). The enhanced green fluorescent protein (EGFP) coding sequence or CAR constructs were cloned into the SFG vector, a murine leukaemia virus-based retroviral vector.22 Retroviruses carrying the CAR vectors were generated as previously described.23 UCB units were provided by Guangdong Cord Blood Bank (Guangzhou, China). CD3− umbilical cord blood mononuclear cells (UCBMCs) were isolated, and activated NK cells were produced as previously described.23 A detailed description of the generation of UCB-derived CAR-NK cells is provided in the online supplemental materials.

Cytotoxicity assays

For cytotoxicity analysis, target cells, labeled with eBioscience Cell Proliferation Dye eFluor 670 (eFluor 670) (Invitrogen), were co-cultured with NK cells or CAR-NK cells at the indicated effector-to-target (E:T) ratios. Target cell death was quantified using the BD LSRFortessa flow cytometer or BD LSRFortessa X-20 cytometer (BD Biosciences) by measuring the percentage of DAPI+ among eFluor 670-labeled cells. In serial killing assays, iNK cells received three rounds of tumor challenge. A detailed description of the serial killing assays is provided in the online supplemental materials.

Immunofluorescence imaging

A detailed description of the immunofluorescence imaging is provided in the online supplemental materials.

Primary AML cells

Primary AML cells were isolated from AML diagnostic bone marrow (BM) or peripheral blood (PB) samples, following the SepTube-15 Density Gradient Centrifuge Tube (NovoBiotechnology)-based protocol. These BM or PB samples of patients with AML were obtained from the State Key Laboratory of Experimental Hematology, National Clinical Research Center for Blood Diseases, Institute of Hematology & Blood Diseases Hospital, Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences & Peking Union Medical College, and Department of Hematology, Guangdong Provincial People’s Hospital.

Single-cell RNA sequencing and data analysis

A detailed description of single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) and data analysis is provided in the online supplemental materials.

Generation of CAR-hPSCs and CAR-iNK cells

CAR-expressing hPSCs were engineered using the piggyBac transposon system.24 CAR-iNK cells were regenerated from CAR-transfected hPSCs and harvested for use as previously described.10 24 A detailed description of the generation of CAR-hPSCs and CAR-iNK cells is provided in the online supplemental materials.

AML xenograft models with CAR-iNK cell treatment

A detailed description of MSLN+ HL-60 xenograft models and patient-derived AML xenograft models with CAR-iNK cell treatment is provided in the online supplemental materials.

Statistical analysis

All quantitative analyses were performed with SPSS (IBM SPSS Statistics V.25). The Shapiro-Wilk normality test was used to assess the normal distribution of the data. Quantitative differences (mean±SD) between samples were compared using two-tailed Student’s t-test, Mann-Whitney U test, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), and two-way ANOVA. The p values were two-sided, and p<0.05 was considered statistically significant. Survival curves for tumor-bearing models were plotted using the Kaplan-Meier method and compared with the treatment group using the log-rank (Mantel-Cox) test. Statistical analysis was performed using the GraphPad Prism V.9 software.

A comprehensive version of the materials and methods section can be found in the online supplemental materials.

Results

CD33-MSLN Loop CAR-NK cells exhibit superior cytotoxicity against CD33- and MSLN-positive tumor cells

To enhance the efficacy of NK cells targeting MSLN-expressing AML, we employed a bivalent loop CAR strategy, designing a dual CAR targeting both CD33 and MSLN. Specifically, the variable light (VL) and heavy (VH) chains of anti-CD33 scFv and anti-MSLN scFv, G4S linker (Linker 3), and whitlow linker21 were sequentially fused to create a bivalent scFv construct (CD33 VL-linker 3-MSLN VH-whitlow linker-MSLN VL-linker 3-CD33 VH, CD33-MSLN Loop). The CD33-MSLN Loop scFv was then linked to the CD8α hinge, the CD8 transmembrane domain (TMD), and the CD3ζ signaling domain (SD) to construct the final Loop CAR. We applied the MSLN CAR and CD33 CAR as controls (figure 1A). To assess the potential of Loop CAR in enhancing tumor-killing activity in NK cells, we first incorporated the Loop CAR element into the SFG vector, followed by retroviral packaging as previously reported.23 After T-cell depletion (online supplemental figure 1A), UCB-NK cells were activated using K562-mbIL-21 feeder cells for 6 days, followed by transduction with Loop CAR retroviral particles at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 5. The transduced cells were then expanded for 4 days in the presence of K562-mbIL-21 feeder cells (figure 1B). The proportion of Loop CAR+ NK cells (CD56+CD33 CAR+MSLN CAR+) reached 43.4% 2 days after transduction, while control groups, including EGFP-NK, CD33 CAR-NK, and MSLN CAR-NK, achieved transduction rates of 48.2%, 55.6%, and 52.1%, respectively (figure 1C). The mean fluorescence intensity analysis of CARs expression suggested that the Loop CAR-NK cells exhibit comparable CD33 CAR expression levels to CD33 CAR-NK cells, while MSLN CAR expression was slightly reduced in Loop CAR-NK cells compared with MSLN CAR-NK cells (online supplemental figure 1B–E). Importantly, the binding of one antigen (CD33 or MSLN) of Loop CAR did not interfere with the binding of the other antigen in the Loop CAR-NK cells (online supplemental figure 1F–I). Loop CAR-NK cells showed comparable expression of typical NK cell activating and inhibitory receptors, as well as cytotoxic granules, to the control groups (online supplemental figure 1J). Furthermore, Loop CAR-NK cells displayed comparable expression levels of exhaustion markers, including Lymphocyte-Activation Gene 3 (LAG3), Programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1), T cell immunoglobulin and ITIM domain (TIGIT), and T cell immunoglobulin and mucin domain-containing protein 3 (TIM3), to those of EGFP-NK, MSLN CAR-NK, and CD33 CAR-NK cells, suggesting no significant induction of exhaustion (online supplemental figure 1K). Transcriptomic profiling further revealed similar expression levels of exhaustion-related genes and expression patterns across key functional gene sets, including cytotoxicity, inflammatory response, general stress, activating/inhibitory receptor-encoding genes, and exhaustion-related signatures (online supplemental figure 1L,M). Together, these findings indicate that Loop CAR-NK cells do not exhibit elevated tonic signaling compared with single-targeted CAR-NK or control NK cells, supporting their stable and functional phenotype.

We next evaluated the cytotoxicity of Loop CAR-NK cells by sorting Loop CAR-expressing NK cells and coculturing them with Nomo-1 (CD33+MSLN+), AGS (CD33−MSLN+), or HL-60 (CD33+MSLN−) tumor cells (figure 1D,E, online supplemental figure 2A). Loop CAR-NK cells exhibited significantly superior cytotoxicity against Nomo-1 cells compared with EGFP-NK, CD33 CAR-NK, and MSLN CAR-NK cells across all E:T ratios (E:T=0.2:1, 0.4:1, 0.8:1, 1.6:1) (figure 1F). Loop CAR-NK cells exhibited comparable cytotoxic activity against AGS (CD33−MSLN+) tumor cells to that of MSLN CAR-NK cells (figure 1G). Similarly, the killing efficacy of Loop CAR-NK cells against HL-60 (CD33+MSLN−) tumor cells was comparable to that of CD33 CAR-NK cells (figure 1H). As expected, Loop CAR-NK cells showed significantly enhanced CD107a degranulation compared with CD33 CAR-NK cells and MSLN CAR-NK cells following Nomo-1 cell stimulation, indicating superior functional activation of Loop CAR-NK cells (online supplemental figure 2B–D). Loop CAR molecules accumulated at the immunological synapse (IS) formed between Loop CAR-NK cells and Nomo-1 cells, confirming that the Loop CAR participates in IS formation in a CD33-MSLN-dependent manner (figure 1I). We performed a comparative analysis of cytotoxic efficacy between Loop CAR-NK cells and CD33-MSLN tandem CAR-NK (tandem CAR-NK) cells against Nomo-1 cells. The results revealed that Loop CAR-NK cells exhibited significantly superior tumor cell killing than tandem CAR-NK cells (online supplemental figure 2E–G). These findings corroborate prior reports demonstrating the enhanced performance of loop-structured CAR designs over tandem configurations,25 likely attributable to their improved receptor spatial organization and signaling efficiency. Moreover, Loop CAR-NK cells also demonstrated superior cytotoxicity against Nomo-1 cells relative to a 1:1 mixture of CD33 CAR-NK and MSLN CAR-NK cells (CD33/MSLN CAR-NK combination) (online supplemental figure 2G). These findings indicate that the Loop CAR outperforms the other two dual CAR approaches in terms of antitumor potency. To further evaluate the cytotoxic activity of Loop CAR-NK cells against primary AML cells co-expressing CD33 and MSLN, we isolated mononuclear cells from the BM or PB of three patients with AML (figure 1J). The primary AML from the three patients all expressed CD33 (Patient #1: 87.5%, Patient #2: 95.6%, Patient #3: 98.0%) and MSLN (Patient #1: 41.3%, Patient #2: 23.5%, Patient #3: 57.8%) (figure 1K). The expression of MSLN was further confirmed by real-time quantitative PCR, which showed consistency with the protein expression levels (figure 1L and online supplemental table 1). Loop CAR-NK cells (effectors, E) were co-cultured with these primary AML cells (targets, T) for 20 hours at indicated E:T ratios (E:T=0:1, 0.5:1, 1:1, 2:1, and 5:1). Loop CAR-NK cells exhibited significantly higher cytotoxicity against all patient-derived targets compared with CD33 CAR-NK and MSLN CAR-NK cells. In addition, both CD33 CAR-NK and MSLN CAR-NK cells killed more targets than EGFP-NK cells (figure 1M–P). Collectively, these data demonstrate that Loop CAR-NK cells exhibit superior specific cytotoxicity against tumor cells expressing both CD33 and MSLN compared with CD33 CAR-NK and MSLN CAR-NK cells.

Loop CAR-NK cells exhibit a more pronounced activation status after Nomo-1 cell stimulation

To investigate the superior cytotoxicity of Loop CAR-NK cells, we performed scRNA-seq on sorted Loop CAR-NK cells after incubation with Nomo-1 cells for 72 hours (figure 2A). Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection analysis revealed that the transcriptional profiles of Loop CAR-NK cells were similar to those of CD33 CAR-NK or MSLN CAR-NK cells. However, all CAR-NK cells exhibited distinct transcriptional profiles compared with EGFP-NK cells (figure 2B). We further examined the differentially expressed genes between Loop CAR-NK cells and CD33 CAR-NK or MSLN CAR-NK cells. Gene Ontology enrichment analysis indicated that genes upregulated in Loop CAR-NK cells relative to MSLN CAR-NK cells were primarily involved in cell division, positive regulation of the Fc receptor-mediated stimulatory signaling pathway, and positive regulation of the RIG-1 signaling pathway (figure 2C and online supplemental figure 3A,B). Similarly, genes upregulated in Loop CAR-NK cells compared with CD33 CAR-NK cells were enriched in lymphoid cell differentiation, positive regulation of the RIG-1 signaling pathway, and formation of IS (figure 2D and online supplemental figure 3C,D). Among the activation-related genes, several hallmarks of NK cell activation and cytotoxic function, including CD244, SLAMF7, and ITGAL (LFA-1), were upregulated in Loop CAR-NK cells.2628 Additionally, genes involved in the RIG-I signaling pathway, such as ANKRD17, PUM1, and PUM2,29 30 and those associated with IS formation, including NCAM1, PTK2B, and DOCK8,3134 were also significantly elevated in Loop CAR-NK cells (figure 2E–G). These findings suggest that Loop CAR-NK cells exhibit enhanced activation following incubation with Nomo-1 cells, which likely contributes to their superior cytotoxicity compared with CD33 CAR-NK or MSLN CAR-NK cells.

The fratricide of Loop CAR-NK and Loop CAR-iNK cells impairs their expansion

A previous study reported that the expansion ability of CD33 CAR-NK cells is impaired due to fratricide mediated by the CD33 CAR, as CD33+ NK cells are eliminated by CAR-expressing NK cells.14 To evaluate whether Loop CAR can also lead to fratricide in NK cells, we sorted CD33− and CD33+ NK cells from activated UCB-NK cells (CD33+: 26.9%), and co-incubated them with Loop CAR-NK cells (figure 3A–C). As expected, Loop CAR-NK cells exhibited specific cytotoxicity against CD33+ NK cells at the indicated E:T ratios, while no cytotoxicity was observed against CD33− NK cells (figure 3D). We then sorted an equal number of EGFP-NK and Loop CAR-NK cells and cultured them consecutively without K562-mbIL-21 stimulation. Over a 12-day culture period, the number of Loop CAR-NK cells was lower than that of EGFP-NK cells, and the gap gradually widened, confirming that fratricide impaired the expansion of Loop CAR-NK cells (figure 3E). In our previous work, we established a method for inducing iNK and CAR-iNK cells from hPSCs.10 24 Here, we generated a Loop CAR-expressing hPSC line (Loop CAR-hPSCs) (figure 3F) and induced these Loop CAR-hPSCs into Loop CAR-iNK cells. Briefly, Loop CAR-hPSCs were induced for 2 days to generate Loop CAR-expressing lateral plate mesoderm (Loop CAR-iLPM) cells. The Loop CAR-iLPM cells were then combined with OP9 cells to form organoid aggregates. These organoid aggregates were cultured for 25 days to generate Loop CAR-iNK cells (figure 3G). The Loop CAR-iNK cells expressed similar levels of CD56 and CD16 as iNK cells, with 78.6% of the cells expressing the Loop CAR (figure 3H). Moreover, Loop CAR-iNK cells showed comparable expression levels of typical NK cell cytotoxic effectors to the iNK cells (online supplemental figure 4A). Notably, the CD33 expression level in iNK cells was 69.7%, which was significantly higher than that observed in UCB-NK cells (figure 3I). Next, we sorted CD33− and CD33+ iNK cells and co-incubated them with Loop CAR-iNK cells (figure 3J,K). The Loop CAR-iNK cells showed significantly higher cytotoxicity against CD33+ iNK cells compared with CD33− iNK cells (figure 3L). As observed with the Loop CAR-NK cells, the number of Loop CAR-iNK cells was lower than that of iNK cells at each time point during a 12-day expansion period, reinforcing that fratricide significantly impaired the expansion of Loop CAR-iNK cells (figure 3M). Taken together, the expression of CD33 in Loop CAR-NK and Loop CAR-iNK cells led to fratricide, which limited their expansion.

Loss of CD33 in iNK cells preserves their broad-spectrum cytotoxicity and restores the expansion of Loop CAR-iNK cells

To address the fratricide caused by CD33 expression in Loop CAR-iNK cells, we generated a CD33 gene knockout hPSC (CD33KO-hPSC) line. Vectors carrying Cas12i, sgRNA1 (5’-TTCAGAAGTGGCCGCAAGGGAAG-3’), sgRNA2 (5’-TTGCTAGTCATCCCCAAATATCC-3’), and a neomycin resistance gene (NeoR) were simultaneously transduced into hPSCs (figure 4A). Following neomycin selection, single clones were isolated and genotyped using four PCR primer pairs (online supplemental figure 5A,B). Ultimately, we identified a CD33KO-hPSC clone with the deletion of exon 3 and exon 4 in one allele of CD33 and the insertion of a loxp-NeoR-loxp sequence in the other (figure 4B and online supplemental figure 5C,D). Subsequently, CD33KO-hPSCs successfully generated CD33-deficient iNK (CD33KO-iNK) cells with an immunophenotype similar to iNK cells (figure 4C). Moreover, the yields of CD33KO-iNK cells were comparable to those of iNK cells (figure 4D). Importantly, CD33KO-iNK cells exhibited cytotoxicity against K562 cells comparable to that of iNK cells, demonstrating that the loss of CD33 did not compromise their broad-spectrum cytotoxicity (figure 4E). We then introduced the Loop CAR into CD33KO-hPSCs to establish the CD33KO-Loop CAR-hPSC line. As expected, these cells successfully differentiated into CD33KO-Loop CAR-iNK cells, which exhibited expression levels of CD56 and CD16 comparable to those of iNK cells, with a CAR expression rate of 89.3% (figure 4F,G). Furthermore, the expression levels of typical activating and inhibitory receptors, as well as cytotoxic granules, were consistent with those of iNK cells (online supplemental figure 5E). To investigate whether CD33 knockout and CAR engineering can lead to exhaustion, we evaluated the expression of exhaustion proteins, including LAG3, PD-1, TIGIT, and TIM3, in CD33KO-Loop CAR-iNK cells. Flow cytometric analysis demonstrated comparable expression levels of these exhaustion markers among iNK, MSLN CAR-iNK, CD33 CAR-iNK, Loop CAR-iNK, and CD33KO-Loop CAR-iNK cells (online supplemental figure 5F). These findings indicate that neither the knockout of CD33 nor Loop CAR induces significant exhaustion in engineered iNK cells. Most notably, CD33KO-Loop CAR-iNK cells demonstrated a similar expansion capacity to that of iNK cells, indicating that CD33 disruption effectively resolved the fratricide issue and restored the expansion of Loop CAR-iNK cells (figure 4H). In conclusion, we successfully overcame the fratricide issue in Loop CAR-iNK cells by knocking out CD33 in hPSCs, thereby preserving their broad-spectrum cytotoxicity and restoring their expansion.

Genetic disruption of CD33 in Loop CAR-iNK cells enhances their specific cytotoxicity

To evaluate the specific cytotoxicity of CD33KO-Loop CAR-iNK cells, we incubated Nomo-1 cells or MSLN-expressing HL-60 (MSLN+ HL-60) cells with these CAR-iNK cells (figure 5A,B). Notably, CD33KO-Loop CAR-iNK cells exhibited significantly enhanced cytotoxicity compared with Loop CAR-iNK cells, suggesting that fratricide in the latter compromised their specific cytotoxicity (figure 5C,D). To further evaluate the durability of the cytotoxic response of CD33KO-Loop CAR-iNK cells, we performed serial killing assays using Nomo-1 cells (figure 5E). Compared with Loop CAR-iNK cells, CD33KO-Loop CAR-iNK cells consistently demonstrated superior specific cytotoxicity against Nomo-1 cells in each round of the killing assay (figure 5F). Additionally, we assessed the expression levels of key effector molecules, including tumor necrosis factor-α, interferon-γ, and CD107a, in CD33KO-Loop CAR-iNK cells following co-incubation with Nomo-1 and MSLN+ HL-60 cells, respectively. The results revealed that CD33KO-Loop CAR-iNK cells expressed significantly higher levels of the indicated molecules compared with Loop CAR-iNK cells, further corroborating their enhanced effector function (figure 5G–O). To validate the target specificity of CD33KO-Loop CAR-iNK cells, we assessed their cytotoxicity against CD33− and CD33+ cells sorted from peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) of a healthy donor. As expected, CD33KO-Loop CAR-iNK cells maintained potent cytotoxicity against CD33+ cells while showing no increased killing activity against CD33−MSLN− cells compared with unmodified iNK cells (online supplemental figure 6A). These results confirm the antigen-dependent activity of CD33KO-Loop CAR-iNK cells and demonstrate their specificity for target-expressing cells. Collectively, our findings underscore the critical role of CD33 disruption in improving the specific cytotoxicity of Loop CAR-iNK cells, demonstrating its importance in overcoming fratricide-induced functional impairments.

CD33KO-Loop CAR-iNK cells effectively suppress AML tumor development in the cell line-derived xenograft model

To further evaluate the antitumor efficacy of CD33KO-Loop CAR-iNK cells, we employed MSLN+ HL-60 cells to construct a cell line-derived xenograft model and treated the model with iNK, CD33KO-iNK, CD33 CAR-iNK, Loop CAR-iNK, or CD33KO-Loop CAR-iNK cells (figure 6A). Luciferase-expressing (luci+) MSLN+ HL-60 cells were intravenously injected into NCG mice (5×105 cells/mouse) on day −1 to establish the tumor-bearing mice. The tumor burden of these mice was determined by bioluminescence imaging (BLI) on day 0. Mice with comparable tumor burdens were randomly assigned to six groups (tumor only, iNK, CD33KO-iNK, CD33 CAR-iNK, Loop CAR-iNK, and CD33KO-Loop CAR-iNK). Each group of CAR-iNK cells was administered intravenously at a dose of 1×10⁷ cells/mouse on day 0 and day 3. Tumor progression of the tumor-bearing mice in each group was monitored weekly using BLI (figure 6B). The results showed that CD33KO-Loop CAR-iNK cells exhibited the best antitumor efficacy compared with iNK, CD33KO-iNK, CD33 CAR-iNK, and Loop CAR-iNK cells. The tumor burden in mice treated with CD33KO-Loop CAR-iNK cells was significantly lower than that in mice treated with Loop CAR-iNK cells. The tumor burden in mice treated with Loop CAR-iNK cells was significantly lower than that in mice treated with CD33 CAR-iNK cells. There was no difference in the tumor burden between the tumor-only mice and mice treated with iNK or CD33KO-iNK cells (figure 6C,D). Eventually, the enhanced tumor suppression translated into a significant survival benefit, with CD33KO-Loop CAR-iNK-treated mice achieving a median survival of 42 days, compared with shorter survival in control groups (tumor only: 28 days; iNK: 33 days; CD33KO-iNK: 33 days; CD33 CAR-iNK: 34 days; Loop CAR-iNK: 38 days) (figure 6E). Collectively, these findings demonstrate that CD33KO-Loop CAR-iNK cells effectively eliminate AML tumor cells in vivo and prolong the survival of xenograft models, underscoring their therapeutic potential for clinical translation.

CD33KO-Loop CAR-iNK cells demonstrate superior in vivo activity against AML in the patient-derived xenograft model

To further investigate the in vivo tumor-killing efficacy of CD33KO-Loop CAR-iNK cells against AML patient-derived tumor cells, we constructed an AML patient-derived xenograft (PDX) model using the primary AML cells from a patient (CD45+MSLN+: 41.3%) carrying the FLT3-ITD mutation. To monitor the progression of tumor burden in mice in real time, we transduced the patient-derived primary tumor cells with luciferase lentiviral particles to construct luciferase-expressing AML cells. The AML cells were harvested 12 hours post-infection and intravenously injected (3×106 cells/mouse) into the irradiated (1.0 Gy) NCG-M mice to construct the PDX model. BLI was performed to determine the tumor burden of these mice on day 3. Mice were randomly divided into three groups (n=4 per group): tumor only, Loop CAR-iNK, and CD33KO-Loop CAR-iNK. Then, Loop CAR-iNK and CD33KO-Loop CAR-iNK cells (1×107 cells/mouse) were intravenously injected into the tumor-bearing mice on day 3 and day 6, respectively. BLI was performed every 7 days (figure 7A). The results indicated that the mice in the tumor-only group exhibited rapid tumor progression, whereas those treated with Loop CAR-iNK or CD33KO-Loop CAR-iNK cells displayed significantly lower total flux values. Notably, the CD33KO-Loop CAR-iNK cell-treated group demonstrated a significantly lower tumor burden than the Loop CAR-iNK cell-treated group (figure 7B,C). Eventually, CD33KO-Loop CAR-iNK cell-treated mice exhibited the longest survival, reaching 46 days. The median survival of the mice in the Loop CAR-iNK cell-treated group was 39 days. However, the median survival of the mice without CAR-iNK cell treatment was 32 days (figure 7D). These findings suggest that both Loop CAR-iNK and CD33KO-Loop CAR-iNK cells could effectively suppress the progression of tumor cells derived from patients with AML in vivo. Collectively, these data indicate that CD33KO-Loop CAR-iNK cells demonstrate superior therapeutic efficacy compared with Loop CAR-iNK cells, significantly prolonging the survival of primary AML-bearing mice.

Discussion

The dual CAR strategy is an effective approach to improve the antitumor abilities of CAR-NK cells.7 8 Bivalent CAR, which contains two scFvs in a single construct, exhibits higher transduction efficiency than bicistronic CAR (comprising two single-targeted CARs). Bivalent CAR can be categorized into tandem CAR and loop CAR.35 Previous studies have reported that loop CAR exhibited superior antitumor potency to tandem CAR.25 36 In this study, we developed a bivalent loop CAR targeting CD33 and MSLN (Loop CAR). When the Loop CAR expression cassette was introduced into UCB-NK cells, the resulting Loop CAR-NK cells exhibited superior cytotoxicity against CD33 and MSLN-expressing tumor cells, including Nomo-1 and primary CD33+MSLN+ AML cells. Furthermore, Loop CAR-NK cells demonstrated comparable cytotoxicity against CD33+MSLN− and CD33−MSLN+ tumor cells compared with CD33 CAR-NK and MSLN CAR-NK cells, respectively. The accumulation of Loop CAR proteins at the IS between Loop CAR-NK cells and Nomo-1 cells likely contributes to their enhanced specific cytotoxicity.37 38 Additionally, Nomo-1 cells stimulated Loop CAR-NK cells to express higher levels of CD107a, a membrane protein associated with NK cell cytotoxic activity.39 Transcriptome analysis revealed that Loop CAR-NK cells exhibited upregulated signaling pathways related to cell division and activation after incubation with Nomo-1 cells. Genes such as NCAM1, PTK2B, and DOCK8, which are involved in IS formation and cytotoxic function, were significantly upregulated.313440

Consistent with a previous report,18 a fraction of UCB-NK cells naturally express CD33. Our findings confirmed that hPSC-derived iNK cells exhibited even higher levels of CD33 expression compared with UCB-NK cells. This led to fratricide in both Loop CAR-NK and Loop CAR-iNK cells, ultimately impairing their expansion. Similar fratricide-induced expansion impairments have been previously reported in human tissue-derived CD33 CAR-NK cells,14 highlighting the necessity of addressing this challenge. To overcome the fratricide issue, we genetically disrupted CD33 in hPSCs (CD33KO-hPSCs) using CRISPR/Cas technology. Previous studies have shown that CD33 knockout in hematopoietic stem cells allows for the development of a functional hematopoietic system without impairing differentiation potential.41 Our results demonstrated that CD33KO-hPSCs successfully differentiated into iNK cells without affecting their induction efficiency or antitumor activity. Although CD33 has been considered an inhibitory receptor due to its cytoplasmic tyrosine-based inhibitory motifs,42 43 we observed no significant differences in cytotoxicity against K562 cells between CD33KO-iNK and iNK cells.

As expected, CD33KO-Loop CAR-hPSCs efficiently generated CD33KO-Loop CAR-iNK cells. The loss of CD33 expression successfully prevented fratricide and restored the expansion capacity of Loop CAR-iNK cells. Interestingly, CD33KO-Loop CAR-iNK cells demonstrated superior specific cytotoxicity against Nomo-1 and MSLN+ HL-60 cells compared with Loop CAR-iNK cells, indicating that fratricide in the latter impaired their expansion and specific cytotoxicity. Moreover, CD33KO-Loop CAR-iNK cells significantly suppressed tumor progression and prolonged the survival of mouse models with MSLN+ HL-60 xenograft and patient-derived AML xenograft. We used MSLN+ HL-60 cells, an M2 subtype of AML, to construct the tumor models, as these cells exhibit high tumorigenic potential in vivo. Additionally, we attempted to use Nomo-1 cells, an M5a subtype of acute monocytic leukemia, to establish a tumor model. However, the tumorigenic efficiency of Nomo-1 cells was suboptimal. Given that NK cells exhibit relatively lower gene editing efficiency compared with T cells,44 targeting endogenous CD33 at the hPSC stage represents a more practical approach to avoid potential fratricide. hPSC-derived NK cells, with their amenability to genetic engineering, clonal selection capabilities, and lack of T-cell contamination, are promising candidates for standardized, off-the-shelf immunotherapy products.45

Despite significant progress in identifying AML-specific antigens as therapeutic targets, challenges remain due to their genomic and phenotypic heterogeneity and overlapping expression with normal hematopoietic cells.3 CD33, expressed in over 80% of AML cells, is absent from primitive stem cells and multipotent progenitor cells, making it an attractive target for AML immunotherapy.46 47 Recent clinical data have demonstrated the safety and efficacy of functionally enhanced CD33 CAR-NK cells.9 MSLN, a well-established target in solid tumors, is expressed in approximately 36% of pediatric AML cases and 14% of adult AML cases, but is virtually absent in normal hematopoietic cells. More importantly, the expression pattern of MSLN closely resembled that of other well-characterized AML cell surface antigens (eg, CD117, CD33, and CD123).2 This suggests that MSLN is a potential therapeutic target in acute myeloid leukemia. Notably, MSLN-targeted CAR-T cells have demonstrated potent efficacy in eliminating MSLN-positive AML cells derived from both cell lines and patient samples in xenograft models.3 However, MSLN is only expressed in 30%–70% of AML cells.2 MSLN+CD33− AML subpopulations have not been identified in clinical samples. This indicates that Loop CAR does not conform to the established dual-targeting CAR design principles for preventing antigen escape. In addition, MSLN is actively cleaved from the cell membrane and is not highly expressed in AML cells. Strategies have been developed to upregulate MSLN expression and reduce shedding, such as the application of small molecules (eg, INCB7839, GM6001) and immunotoxins (eg, RG7787, SS1P), thereby enhancing its feasibility as a therapeutic target.3 48 49 Our findings demonstrated that CD33-MSLN Loop CAR-NK cells exhibited superior cytotoxicity against primary AML cells expressing both CD33 and MSLN (23.5%–57.8%). Moreover, CD33KO-CD33-MSLN Loop CAR-iNK cells displayed robust antitumor efficacy both in vitro and in xenograft models. These results indicate that the CD33-MSLN Loop CAR design effectively enhances the tumor-killing potential of NK cells and supports the successful development of a dual-targeting CAR strategy. Additionally, by genetically inactivating CD33, we effectively addressed the fratricide issue, further improving the function of CD33-MSLN Loop CAR-iNK cells. In conclusion, our studies provide compelling preclinical evidence supporting the further evaluation of CD33KO-CD33-MSLN Loop CAR-iNK cells as promising candidates for clinical trials in the treatment of CD33+MSLN+ AML.

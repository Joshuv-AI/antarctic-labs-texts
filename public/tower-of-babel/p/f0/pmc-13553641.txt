Prognostic value of D3 lymph node dissection and nutritional status in colorectal cancer patients aged 80 years and older
Authors: Yasuhiro Ishiyama, Yasumitsu Hirano, Sohei Akuta, Yume Minagawa, Akihito Nakanishi, Yusuke Nishi, Hisashi Hayashi, Takatsugu Fujii, Hirofumi Sugita, Chikashi Hiranuma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo evaluate the impact of D3 lymph node dissection and nutritional status on outcomes in patients aged ≥80 years with colorectal cancer.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed patients aged ≥80 years who underwent curative resection for stage I–III colorectal cancer between April 2007 and February 2020. Patients were divided into D3 and non-D3 lymph node dissection groups. Propensity score matching was performed to reduce baseline differences. Short- and long-term outcomes, including overall survival (OS), disease-free survival (DFS), and cancer-specific survival (CSS), were compared. Multivariate Cox regression analyses were used to identify independent prognostic factors, with particular attention to the prognostic nutritional index (PNI). Competing-risk analysis for cancer-specific death was also conducted.ResultsAfter propensity score matching, 272 patients (136 per group) were analyzed. The D3 group showed significantly better OS (5-year: 73.5% vs. 60.0%, P = 0.0039) and DFS (5-year: 69.5% vs. 56.1%, P = 0.0099) than the non-D3 group. CSS was not significantly different on Kaplan–Meier analysis; however, competing-risk analysis demonstrated a significantly lower cumulative incidence of cancer-specific death in the D3 group (5-year: 23.0% vs. 29.6%, P = 0.024). Multivariate analysis identified D3 dissection and PNI ≥ 45 as independent favorable prognostic factors for OS, DFS, and CSS.ConclusionsIn patients aged ≥80 years with colorectal cancer, D3 lymph node dissection improved OS and DFS without increasing perioperative morbidity. Preoperative PNI was a strong predictor of long-term prognosis and may aid surgical decision-making in elderly patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s00384-026-05180-0.

Introduction

The incidence of colorectal cancer in elderly patients has been increasing in recent years [1], and this trend is projected to continue through 2050 [1, 2]. However, elderly patients often have multiple comorbidities, including cardiovascular disease, respiratory disorders, and diabetes mellitus, which raises concerns about whether standard treatment should be applied. Nevertheless, several studies have reported that surgery for colorectal cancer can be safely performed in elderly patients [3–7]. Furthermore, disease-free survival (DFS) and cancer-specific survival (CSS) in elderly patients have been reported to be comparable to those in younger patients [6, 8]. Oh et al. reported that age is not a risk factor for prognosis in colorectal cancer.

On the other hand, Kawase et al. [9] reported that D3 lymph node dissection contributes to improved long-term prognosis even in patients aged 70 years and older, with particularly pronounced benefits in stage III cases. Risk factors for long-term prognosis in elderly patients undergoing colorectal cancer surgery, such as performance status >2, lymph node metastasis, and adjuvant chemotherapy status, have been reported; however, there are few studies evaluating nutritional indices. Therefore, it remains unclear whether D3 lymph node dissection provides similar benefits to all elderly patients with colorectal cancer, and stratification based on preoperative physiological reserve and nutritional status has not been adequately investigated.

Meanwhile, the Prognostic Nutritional Index (PNI), calculated from serum albumin levels and peripheral lymphocyte counts, is an immune–nutritional index that has been reported to be useful as a predictor of postoperative complications and long-term prognosis in colorectal cancer patients [10, 11]. Therefore, PNI may contribute to assessing surgical tolerance and optimizing surgical strategy selection in elderly patients.

The aim of this study was to examine the significance of D3 lymph node dissection in elderly patients with colorectal cancer by comparing short- and long-term outcomes between D3 and non-D3 groups and to investigate factors associated with long-term outcomes, including PNI.

Materials and methods

Between April 2007 and February 2020, 8055 patients underwent primary tumor resection at Saitama Medical University International Medical Center. Of these, 450 patients aged 80 years or older who underwent primary tumor resection were subjected to propensity score matching (PSM), resulting in 136 patients in each group (272 patients total) who were retrospectively analyzed (Fig. 1).Fig. 1Flow chart

Flow chart

The inclusion criterion was pathological stage I–III disease. Exclusion criteria were early cancer (preoperative T1 cases not requiring D3 dissection), stage IV disease, and emergency surgery cases. Comparison items included age, sex, body mass index (BMI), American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) score, total protein (TP), hemoglobin (Hb), carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA), history of prior laparotomy, age-adjusted Charlson Comorbidity Index (ACCI), PNI, tumor location, operative time, blood loss, postoperative complications (Clavien–Dindo > 2), postoperative hospital stay, and pathological findings. Long-term outcomes evaluated were overall survival (OS), disease-free survival (DFS), and cancer-specific survival (CSS).

Preoperative laboratory values were obtained within 14 days before surgery. Patients included in the matched analysis had complete baseline data for the variables used in the analysis.

The ACCI was calculated by adding an age score to the Charlson Comorbidity Index (CCI) after excluding the target disease (colorectal cancer) from the comorbidity score. Postoperative complications were defined as Clavien–Dindo classification >2 [12].

Definition of D3 lymph node dissection

The extent of lymph node dissection was defined according to the Japanese Society for Cancer of the Colon and Rectum (JSCCR) treatment guidelines and classification [13]. The extent of lymph node dissection was defined according to the Japanese Society for Cancer of the Colon and Rectum treatment guidelines. D1 dissection was defined as removal of pericolic or perirectal lymph nodes, D2 dissection as removal of pericolic or perirectal and intermediate lymph nodes, and D3 dissection as removal of pericolic or perirectal, intermediate, and main lymph nodes. The extent of dissection was determined from operative records, including surgical findings, vascular management, and descriptions of the dissection field.

OS was defined as the length of time from surgery to the date of death from any cause or the date of final follow-up. DFS was defined as the length of time from surgery to the date of recurrence, or death from any cause, or the date of final follow-up. CSS was defined as the length of time from surgery to the date of death due to the disease, censoring patients who died from other causes.

This study was approved by the Center for Appropriate Promotion of Clinical Research at the International Medical Center of Saitama Medical University (Approval No. 2022-028) and was designed and conducted in accordance with the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Patient follow-up

All patients were followed for survival. Recurrence and distant metastases were assessed based on serum tumor marker levels, including carcinoembryonic antigen and carbohydrate antigen 19-9, as well as imaging and endoscopic examinations. Blood tests were performed every 4 months for the first 3 postoperative years, computed tomography (CT) every 6 months for 5 years, and endoscopy annually for 5 years.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using JMP Pro 16 software (SAS Institute, Cary, NC). Results are summarized as means and standard deviations for continuous variables; categorical variables are summarized as numbers and frequencies. Median and mean values were compared between groups using the Mann–Whitney test or the Chi-square test in univariate analyses. All postoperative complications were analyzed using binary logistic regression. OS, DFS, and CSS rates were analyzed using the Kaplan–Meier method and Cox’s proportional hazards model. Comparisons between survival curves were performed using the log-rank test. Results are summarized as the hazard ratio (HR), 95% confidence interval (CI), and P-value. Significance was set at P < 0.05. In addition, a competing-risk analysis was performed for cancer-specific death using the cumulative incidence function (CIF) and Gray’s test. Univariable Fine–Gray regression analysis was used to estimate subdistribution hazard ratios (sHRs), treating non-cancer death as a competing event. Additional subgroup analyses were performed in patients with clinical stage II–III disease. The proportional hazards assumption was assessed using Schoenfeld residuals, and no meaningful violations were observed.

Propensity-score matching

A comparative analysis was performed between the D3 dissection group and the non-D3 dissection group in patients aged 80 years or older with colorectal cancer. To minimize the effect of differences in procedure and tumor location, we applied propensity-score matching. Propensity scores were calculated for each patient with bivariate logistic regression based on the following covariates: sex; age; American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) >2, tumor location, PNI, ACCI >6, approach (Laparoscopic or open), period of surgery (2007~2014 or 2015~2020) These propensity scores were used to match patients in D3 group in a 1:1 ratio with patients in non D3 group. We used the nearest-available Mahalanobis metric-matching distance with calipers, defined by the propensity score (caliper = 0.2).

In univariate analyses, each factor was entered individually; in multivariate analyses, clinically important factors were entered simultaneously. The cutoff value for PNI was set at 45, as PNI has been reported to be a risk factor for postoperative complications [14, 15]. The cutoff value for ACCI was set at ≥6, as this has been reported as a prognostic factor [16]. Results are presented as hazard ratios (HR) and 95% confidence intervals (95% CI). P < 0.05 was defined as statistically significant.

Results

Patient characteristics (before propensity score matching)

There were 220 patients in the D3 group and 205 patients in the non-D3 group. Age was significantly higher in the non-D3 group (82.8 ± 2.7 vs. 84.5 ± 3.7 years, P < 0.001). Left-sided tumors were more frequent in the D3 group (60.9% vs. 39.0%, P < 0.001). In addition, patients in the D3 group were more likely to have undergone surgery during the later study period (2014–2020) (65.9% vs. 43.9%, P < 0.001). There were no significant differences between the two groups in sex, BMI, ASA > 2, CEA > 5, history of prior surgery, PNI ≥ 45, or ACCI ≥ 6 (Table 1). Patient characteristics (after propensity score matching)

After propensity score matching, 136 patients were included in each group, for a total of 272 patients. After matching, the distributions of age, sex, ASA > 2, prior surgery, ACCI ≥ 6, tumor location, and surgical period were generally well balanced between the two groups. In particular, age (83.3 ± 3.0 vs. 83.5 ± 3.0 years, SMD = 0.040) and tumor location (right-sided tumors: 58.1% vs. 58.1%, SMD = 0.000) were well matched. However, residual imbalance remained in PNI ≥ 45 (56.6% vs. 36.8%, P = 0.001, SMD = 0.398) (Table 1).

Short-term outcomes

Operative time, blood loss, postoperative complications (CD > 2), surgical approach, and adjuvant chemotherapy status were all comparable between the two groups. Postoperative hospital stay was significantly shorter in the D3 group (8.8 ± 7.1 vs. 11.9 ± 18.2 days, P = 0.002). (Table 2). With regard to pathological findings, tumor size was comparable between the two groups, whereas the number of retrieved lymph nodes was significantly higher in the D3 group (26.9 ± 11.0 vs. 21.8 ± 9.5, P < 0.001). The distributions of pathological T stage and nodal positivity were similar between the two groups (Table 2). Long-term outcomes

Kaplan–Meier analysis after PSM showed that OS was significantly better in the D3 group (5-year OS: 73.5% vs 60.0%, log-rank P = 0.0039, HR 0.48 (95% CI 0.29–0.80)). DFS was also significantly better in the D3 group (5-year DFS: 69.5% vs 56.1%, log-rank P = 0.0099, HR 0.56 (95% CI 0.36–0.88)). CSS did not differ significantly between the two groups (5-year CSS: 76.1% vs 72.7%, log-rank P = 0.1055, HR 0.63 (95% CI 0.36–1.11)) (Fig. 2). A competing-risk analysis was performed for cancer-specific death, treating non-cancer death as a competing event. The cumulative incidence function (CIF) showed a significantly lower cumulative incidence of cancer-specific death in the D3 group than in the non-D3 group (5-year cumulative incidence: 23.0% vs. 29.6%, Gray’s test, P = 0.024). Univariable Fine–Gray regression also demonstrated that D3 lymph node dissection was associated with a significantly lower subdistribution hazard for cancer-specific death (sHR 0.60, 95% CI 0.38–0.94, P = 0.026).Fig. 2Kaplan–Meier survival curves for overall survival (OS), disease-free survival (DFS), and cancer-specific survival (CSS)

We additionally tested the interaction between tumor sidedness and D3 dissection in multivariable Cox models. No significant interaction was observed for OS (P = 0.219), DFS (P = 0.145), or CSS (P = 0.148), suggesting that the prognostic effect of D3 dissection was not significantly modified by tumor location.

Additional subgroup analysis in patients with cStage II–III disease

In an additional analysis restricted to patients with clinical stage II–III disease, representing those who were actual candidates for D3 dissection at the time of surgical decision-making, 403 patients were included (234 in the D3 group and 169 in the non-D3 group). In this subgroup, OS and DFS were significantly better in the D3 group, whereas CSS did not differ significantly between the groups.

In this subgroup, 403 patients were included (234 in the D3 group and 169 in the non-D3 group). OS and DFS were significantly better in the D3 group than in the non-D3 group (5-year OS: 74.8% vs. 62.0%, log-rank P = 0.0062; 5-year DFS: 71.1% vs. 58.1%, log-rank P = 0.0419), whereas CSS did not differ significantly between the groups (5-year CSS: 77.2% vs. 71.4%, log-rank P = 0.1269).

Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses for OS, DFS, and CSS

In contrast, CEA > 5 (HR 1.931, 95% CI 1.137–3.281, P = 0.015) and male sex (HR 2.007, 95% CI 1.184–3.404, P = 0.010) were independent unfavorable prognostic factors (Table 3). For CSS, multivariate analysis, PNI ≥ 45 (HR 0.484, 95% CI 0.262–0.896, P = 0.021), later surgical period (HR 0.477, 95% CI 0.230–0.987, P = 0.046), and D3 lymph node dissection (HR 0.477, 95% CI 0.262–0.867, P = 0.015) were independent favorable prognostic factors. In contrast, CEA > 5 (HR 2.485, 95% CI 1.351–4.571, P = 0.003), male sex (HR 2.429, 95% CI 1.315–4.488, P = 0.005), and Stage > II (HR 2.048, 95% CI 1.146–3.660, P = 0.016) were independent unfavorable prognostic factors (Table 5). Discussion

This study demonstrated the safety and potential oncological benefit of D3 lymph node dissection in patients aged 80 years or older with colorectal cancer. After adjustment for baseline differences using propensity score matching, D3 lymph node dissection was not associated with increased perioperative risk and was linked to improved long-term outcomes in this very elderly population. In multivariable analysis, D3 lymph node dissection was identified as an independent favorable prognostic factor for OS, DFS, and CSS, supporting its oncological value in selected elderly patients. In addition, PNI ≥ 45 was consistently associated with better long-term outcomes, suggesting that preoperative nutritional status may be an important factor in treatment selection for elderly patients.

The later surgical period was associated with a significantly higher proportion of D3 lymph node dissections, which may be attributable to advances in surgical techniques and medical devices that have improved the safety of D3 lymph node dissection, even in elderly patients. The widespread adoption of laparoscopic surgery may also have contributed to this trend. Although laparoscopic surgery is associated with longer operative times compared with open surgery, it has been reported to reduce surgical invasiveness through smaller incisions [17, 18]. Moreover, laparoscopic approaches have been associated with fewer postoperative complications, including a reduction in non-surgical complications such as pneumonia [19, 20]. These temporal changes may partly explain why most short-term outcomes were comparable between the D3 and non-D3 groups in the present study.

Of note, postoperative hospital stay was significantly shorter in the D3 group, although operative time, blood loss, major postoperative complications, surgical approach, and adjuvant chemotherapy status were comparable between the two groups. This finding suggests that D3 lymph node dissection did not increase postoperative burden in this cohort and may have been safely performed in appropriately selected patients. However, because this was a retrospective study and perioperative care may also have improved over time, this result should be interpreted with caution.

Regarding pathological findings, the number of retrieved lymph nodes was significantly higher in the D3 group, whereas tumor size, pathological T stage, and nodal positivity were similar between the two groups. The higher lymph node yield in the D3 group indicates that more extensive nodal dissection was actually achieved, which may partly explain the improved long-term outcomes observed in this group.

With respect to long-term outcomes, overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS) were significantly better in the D3 group, whereas cancer-specific survival (CSS) was comparable between groups. The improvement in DFS may be attributable to enhanced local control through suppression of regional lymph node recurrence. In contrast, multivariable Cox analysis identified D3 lymph node dissection as an independent favorable prognostic factor for CSS. This apparent discrepancy may be explained by the limited number of cancer-specific events and the substantial effect of competing mortality in this very elderly population. Indeed, in the competing-risk analysis, the cumulative incidence of cancer-specific death was significantly lower in the D3 group, and univariable Fine–Gray regression also showed a significantly lower subdistribution hazard for cancer-specific death in patients who underwent D3 dissection. These findings support the possibility that the oncological benefit of D3 lymph node dissection extends to cancer-specific outcomes when competing mortality is taken into account. We also evaluated whether the prognostic effect of D3 dissection differed according to tumor sidedness. Although the technical implications of D3 dissection may differ between right- and left-sided tumors, we did not observe a significant interaction between tumor sidedness and D3 dissection for OS, DFS, or CSS. This suggests that the prognostic benefit of D3 dissection was generally consistent between right- and left-sided tumors in this cohort.

Additionally, male sex was identified as an independent unfavorable prognostic factor, which is consistent with previous reports [21].

The prognostic nutritional index (PNI) has been reported in numerous recent studies as an indicator reflecting preoperative nutritional and immune status [10, 11]. A low PNI has been associated with increased postoperative complications, prolonged hospital stay, and decreased overall and disease-free survival [22]. In the present study, a PNI ≥ 45 was identified as an independent favorable prognostic factor for overall survival (OS), disease-free survival (DFS), and cancer-specific survival (CSS), suggesting that nutritional status remains an important determinant of long-term prognosis even in elderly patients with colorectal cancer. Although few studies have evaluated PNI as a stratification factor for determining the extent of lymph node dissection (D3 vs. non-D3), our findings suggest that PNI may have clinical utility when considering indications for D3 lymph node dissection in elderly patients.

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, this was a retrospective single-center study, and although propensity score matching was used to reduce baseline differences, unmeasured confounding cannot be excluded. Second, residual imbalance in PNI remained after matching, suggesting possible selection bias in the choice of D3 dissection. Third, competing risks from non-cancer mortality are substantial in patients aged 80 years or older, and although we additionally performed a competing-risk analysis, the number of cancer-specific events remained limited. Fourth, because the study period spanned more than a decade, temporal changes in surgical practice and perioperative care may have affected both treatment selection and outcomes, although surgical period was included in the adjusted analyses. Fifth, preoperative clinical stage was not included in the main matching strategy. Although the use of clinical stage may be methodologically attractive, preoperative staging in routine practice is not always sufficiently accurate and may itself introduce additional bias related to treatment selection. Furthermore, including clinical stage in propensity score matching may reduce overlap between treatment groups and make adequate matching difficult. For these reasons, clinical stage was not incorporated into the primary matched analysis, and instead we performed a separate subgroup analysis restricted to cStage II–III cases. Finally, postoperative adjuvant chemotherapy was not analyzed in detail according to treatment intensity or regimen, and residual treatment-related confounding may remain.

In patients aged 80 years or older with colorectal cancer, D3 lymph node dissection was associated with comparable perioperative outcomes and improved OS and DFS compared with non-D3 lymph node dissection. Moreover, multivariable analysis identified D3 lymph node dissection as an independent favorable prognostic factor for OS, DFS, and CSS, and competing-risk analysis also supported a lower cumulative incidence of cancer-specific death in the D3 group. Preoperative nutritional status, as reflected by the prognostic nutritional index, was also strongly associated with prognosis, suggesting that PNI may be a useful stratification factor when determining the optimal surgical strategies for elderly patients.

12-month outcomes of elexacaftor/tezacaftor/ivacaftor (ELX/TEZ/IVA) in rare CFTR missense variant
Authors: Sona Vekaria, Hind Al-Abbasi, Siobhain Mulrennan
Published: 2026

The benefits of elexacaftor/tezacaftor/ivacaftor (ELX/TEZ/IVA) for people with cystic fibrosis (pwCF) with at least 1 F508del variant are well documented.1 ELX and TEZ increase the presence of corrected CFTR at the cell surface, whereas IVA improves chloride ion transportation.1 Although most pwCF may be eligible for CFTR modulator treatment based on United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) criteria, which currently include those with at least 1 F508del allele or another CFTR mutation proven to be responsive through in vitro evidence, the in vivo effects of these treatments in rare genotypes are still being studied.

We describe a 35-year-old female with homozygous missense CFTR variant c.182T>C p.Leu61Pro, who received compassionate access ELX/TEZ/IVA treatment for severe CF-related bronchiectasis and lung disease at a tertiary Australian hospital. This report documents a previously unreported genotype, for which CF phenotype, morbidity, and mortality outcomes remain unknown,2 and describes in vivo ELX/TEZ/IVA treatment outcomes in this genotype.

The patient emigrated to Australia from the Middle East and has a complex psychosocial background. She was diagnosed with CF in her late twenties in Australia after genotyping and 2 confirmatory sweat chloride tests measuring >90mmol/L. Her comorbidities include pancreatic insufficiency, insulin-dependent CF-related diabetes and chronic sinusitis. Her lungs are colonized with Pseudomonas aeruginosa and Staphylococcus aureus. Transplant assessment was initiated when her FEV1 was less than 39% predicted (1.19L) in the 6 months preceding ELX/TEZ/IVA initiation (best recorded FEV1 43% predicted [1.35L] in the preceding 12 months) (Figure 1). At the time of commencing ELX/TEZ/IVA, she had required 2 courses of intravenous antibiotics (IVAbs) within 2 months, despite reported adherence to all treatment including twice-daily airway clearance.

During her second 2-week hospital admission in 2023, FEV1 had dropped to 32% predicted (0.99L), necessitating additional support with IVAbs, supplemental oxygen, intensive physiotherapy, and oral corticosteroids. At the end of this admission, she commenced full dose ELX/TEZ/IVA. Within 6 weeks of ELX/TEZ/IVA treatment, her FEV1 had improved to 42% predicted (1.29L) (Figure 1), sweat chloride concentration was 13mmol/L, and she reported general well-being and clearer sinuses. Her liver function tests have remained within the normal range, and her HbA1c has remained <7% since commencing ELX/TEZ/IVA.

To assess quality of life and well-being, the patient completed validated mental health questionnaires (GAD-7 and PHQ-9) and a chronic rhinosinusitis assessment survey (SNOT-22) at ELX/TEZ/IVA initiation and repeated 6 months later. Scores demonstrated remarkable improvement on all 3 surveys (GAD-7, PHQ-9, SNOT-22) from 2, 11, and 37, respectively, at baseline, to 1, 0, and 4, respectively, at 6 months. She correspondingly described increased energy, reduced health-related anxiety, and improved sinusitis symptoms.

Our case emphasizes the potential significant benefits of CFTRm treatment in pwCF currently ineligible for this therapy, highlighting improvements in multiple domains of clinical status, including lung function, diabetes control, sinus disease, and well-being.

Overall, the impact of ELX/TEX/IVA on CF missense genetic variants is minimally reported. One in vitro organoid study3 described treatment effectiveness in CF missense variant, L1077P. In a French study,4 improved outcomes were observed in people who were homozygous or heterozygous for several missense variants: more than 30% of participants who did not carry FDA-approved CF genotype variants demonstrated favorable in vivo outcomes to ELX/TEX/IVA treatment trial, with improvements in sweat chloride concentration, weight, and lung function.

Although this rare genotype is not recorded in CFTR2,2 other undiagnosed cases of people with the same genetics could exist, particularly in the Middle East. CF may be underdiagnosed in this region because of inconsistent newborn screening algorithms and genetic testing facilities, coupled with a low clinical index of suspicion.5, 6, 7, 8 Additionally, it is suggested that the culturally accepted practice of parental consanguinity in this region may contribute to a higher proportion of CF cases compared to other populations.5,6

Inconsistencies in epidemiological and longitudinal studies could also generate uncertainties in estimating the global and country-specific prevalence and incidence of CF.7 The establishment of national CF registries globally remains a priority to enable standardization of diagnosis, data collection, and health monitoring.

Furthermore, access to highly effective CFTRm for rare genotypes may pose a unique challenge for developing countries, especially when other significant public health priorities require more immediate attention and resources.5,6

In conclusion, this case underscores the transformative potential of CFTRm treatment for individuals with rare genetic variants, offering hope to those previously deemed ineligible. The observed improvements in our patient’s clinical status, including lung function, diabetic control, sinus disease, and psychosocial well-being, highlight the broad benefits of this therapy.

Evidence of CFTRm use for this specific variant is lacking; however, it is possible that other cases exist, particularly in underresourced regions. Inconsistent epidemiological data and the underdiagnosis of CF globally emphasizes the urgent need for standardized diagnostic practices and national CF registries. Addressing these challenges will be crucial for ensuring equitable access to advanced treatments, such as ELX/TEZ/IVA, ultimately improving outcomes for all individuals with CF, regardless of their genetic background or geographic location.

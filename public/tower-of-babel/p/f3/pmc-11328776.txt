Mitral Valve Edge-to-Edge Repair Through an Atrial Septal Shunt Device
Authors: Felix J Hofmann, Oliver Dörr, Bernd Abt, Ardawan J Rastan, Holger M Nef
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractAtrial shunt devices are being implanted with increasing frequency, and there are potential concerns for subsequent procedures using transseptal access. A 79-year-old woman presented with progressive dyspnea of multifactorial etiology with already implanted atrial shunt device. Due to comorbidities, successful mitral-valve edge-to-edge repair was performed transseptally through the shunt device.Key Words: atrial septal shunt device, case report, edge-to-edge mitral valve repair, HFmrEF, mitral insufficiency, structural heart disease

Background

The increasing use of atrial shunt devices has spawned concerns for further procedures using transseptal access. We report the first percutaneous mitral valve repair procedure in a patient with an atrial shunt device.

Case Summary

We report the case of a 79-year-old woman with prior 2-vessel disease with myocardial infarction and heart failure with improved ejection fraction, type 2 diabetes mellitus, chronic pulmonary obstructive disease, and a history of arterial hypertension. In August 2020, she presented with persistent dyspnea. Transthoracic echocardiography showed an ejection fraction of 55% and no valvular disease with signs of diastolic function (Video 1). Right heart catheter assessment displayed postcapillary pulmonary hypertension (postcapillary wedge pressure, 17 mm Hg; pulmonary artery pressure mean, 26 mm Hg; pulmonal vascular resistance, 139 dynes × s × cm-5). An atrial shunt device (8-mm inner diameter; Corvia Medical, Inc) was implanted (Figure 1A).

(C) Transcatheter mitral valve repair guided by transesophageal echocardiography, successfully implanted, restoring valve function. (D) Echocardiography confirming proper positioning and function of the edge-to-edge device. (E) Postprocedural assessment of atrial shunt device integrity. Evaluation showing no procedure-related issues with the implanted atrial shunt device. (F) Six-month follow-up: clinical improvement. Ejection fraction improved to 50%, with stable mitral regurgitation and reduction in symptoms postintervention.

During routine follow-up in July 2022, the patient presented with NYHA functional class III with dyspnea but without angina and on optimal medical treatment. Blood pressure was 100/50 mm Hg, heart frequency was 76 beats/min, and respiratory rate was 19 breaths/min. The focused physical examination showed vesicular breath and a 4/6 systolic murmur, best heard at the left 5th midclavicular line with radiation. Transesophageal echocardiography revealed high-grade mitral valve regurgitation (3-dimensional vena contracta, 0.8 cm2; mitral regurgitation volume, 55 mL; reflux into the pulmonary veins) with atrial dilation due to degenerative leaflets and an ejection fraction of approximately 40% (Figure 1B, Video 2). Coronary angiography revealed no progression of coronary artery disease (Video 3). Stressed right heart assessment showed a significant increase in postcapillary wedge pressure (15 to 24 mm Hg) and a decrease in cardiac output.

Due to comorbidities, the heart team recommendation was to perform transcatheter mitral valve edge-to-edge repair. We planned to access the left atrium through the shunt, which was positioned posteriorly in the middle of the atrial septum. The distance from the coaptation plane was approximately 3.8 cm. One other option could have been to perform the transseptal puncture at a different location near the device, avoiding critical structures. Due to the small surface area of the septum in this case, this option was not feasible.

General anesthesia with endotracheal intubation was performed. Via the right femoral common vein, a Swartz SL-1 lock (Abbott) was advanced to the right atrium. A super stiff wire (Amplatzer, COOK Medical) was placed across the atrial shunt (with an inner diameter of approximately 8 mm) under transesophageal echocardiographic guidance. After predilation of the shunt (Video 4), a 24-F steerable guiding catheter (catheter shaft outer diameter, 8.1 mm [24-F]; catheter distal tip diameter, 7.7 mm [23-F]; catheter septal crossing diameter, 7.4 mm [22-F]) was positioned through the atrial shunt into the left atrium. A MitraClip XT (Abbott) was implanted uneventfully (Figures 1C and 1D, Video 5).

Postimplantation evaluation displayed an excellent result with a small residual insufficiency grade < I° and a mean gradient of 3 mm Hg. The atrial shunt device showed no signs of procedure-related issues (Figure 1E, Video 6). Four days later, the patient was discharged. The 6-month clinical follow-up showed a persistent reduction of symptoms and an improvement of ejection fraction to 50% and stable mitral regurgitation (Figure 1F, Video 7).

Discussion

To our knowledge, this is the first report of a case of edge-to-edge mitral valve repair via an atrial shunt device. The diameter of the shunt device (8.0 mm) and the interventional catheter (7.4-8.1 mm) determined the level of difficulty. The case demonstrated the safety and feasibility of mitral valve repair through an atrial shunt device. Although the suggested approach is restricted to a small number of patients, it offers a new therapy strategy for treating patients with complex phenotypes of heart failure who later develop mitral valve insufficiency.

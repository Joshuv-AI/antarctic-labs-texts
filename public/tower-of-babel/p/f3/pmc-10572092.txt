Pulmonary leiomyosarcoma extended to the left atrium through the pulmonary vein
Authors: Christina Ionascu, Andrei Lihanceanu, Nabil Bendaoud, Christophe Meune, Akhil Narang, Maria Mattioli, Massimo Iacoviello, Luis Beck-da-Silva, Diego Araiza-Garaygordobil
Published: 2023

Primary pulmonary leiomyosarcoma (PL) is a rare malignant tumour originating in pulmonary smooth muscles or vessels that can theoretically extend into pulmonary veins.1–3

Case description

A 54-year-old man, an active smoker (20 cig/day for 36 years) who has worked as a painter for 25 years, consulted his general practitioner because of cough and weight loss over 2 months.

A chest computed tomography (CT) scan was performed and showed a necrotic mass in the right upper lung lobe.

Bronchial biopsy specimens obtained by bronchoscopy revealed a pulmonary PL in the right upper branch and pulmonary artery.

Positron emission tomography showed a right upper lobe large hypermetabolic mass, a possible hypermetabolic lymphadenopathy of the right pulmonary hilum but no metastatic localization.

A pemetrexed/carboplatin/bevacizumab chemotherapy regimen was initiated.

One month later, the patient presented to the emergency department with lateral chest pain, acute breathlessness. On examination, heart rate was of 112 b.p.m., blood pressure of 120/80 mmHg, oxygen saturation of 80%. Blood tests revealed c-reactive protein at 150 mg/dL, leucocytes at 15 000/mL, and haemoglobin at 8.5 g/dL without associated thrombocytopenia.

A chest CT scan revealed a voluminous right lung mass, large pleural effusion, a large low attenuating mass in the left atrium, and no sign of pulmonary embolism (Figure 1).

Chest computed tomography scan showing a voluminous right lung mass, a large low attenuating mass in the left atrium coming from the right lung pulmonary vein.

A transoesophageal echocardiography was performed and showed a mobile heterogenous polylobed mass in the left atrium, originating from the right lower pulmonary vein, unattached to the cardiac structures (Figures 2–4).

The patient’s condition developed unfavourably over months and he has been transferred to a palliative care unit.

Real-world treatment patterns and effectiveness after disease progression on CDK4/6 inhibitors for HR-positive/HER2-negative metastatic breast cancer in the ESME-MBC cohort
Authors: R. Varnier, D. Pérol, W. Jacot, A. Mailliez, V. Diéras, F. Dalenc, A. Gonçalves, C. Levy, M. Arnedos, J.-S. Frenel, C. Bailleux, V. Massard, E. Brain, B. Sauterey, A.-M. Savoye, L. Bosquet, J.-C. Thery, T. Petit, T. Bachelot, T. Grinda, I. Ray-Coquard
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCyclin-dependent kinase 4/6 inhibitors (CDK4/6i) have improved the prognosis of hormone receptor (HR)-positive human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative metastatic breast cancer (MBC), but their impact on the efficacy of second-line treatments remains poorly described.Materials and methodsThis retrospective study included patients from the Epidemiological Strategy and Medical Economics database who initiated endocrine therapy ± CDK4/6i as first-line treatment for HR-positive/HER2-negative MBC between 2008 and 2019 across 18 French cancer centres and subsequently received second-line therapy. Objectives were to describe treatment patterns, time to progression (TTP), and post-progression survival after CDK4/6i, and to compare chemotherapy efficacy between CDK4/6i-exposed and historical CDK4/6i-naive patients, using propensity score adjustments.ResultsAmong 13 577 HR-positive/HER2-negative MBC patients, 538 received CDK4/6i and 4030 received endocrine therapy alone as first-line treatment. Following CDK4/6i exposure, 47% of patients received chemotherapy as first subsequent treatment {median TTP 5.94 months [95% confidence interval (CI) 5.23-7.10 months]; 5.00 months (95% CI 3.68-6.63 months) for taxanes, 6.43 months (95% CI 3.87-12.29 months) for anthracyclines, 7.62 months (95% CI 6.13-8.30 months) for fluoropyrimidines}, 26% received endocrine therapy alone [6.14 months (95% CI 4.43-10.03 months)], 16% received phosphoinositide 3-kinase/mammalian target of rapamycin inhibitors [5.16 months (95% CI 3.90-6.48 months)], and 9% underwent CDK4/6i rechallenge [9.57 months (95% CI 6.0 months-not applicable)].In the comparative analysis, chemotherapy appeared slightly less effective in CDK4/6i-exposed patients than in CDK4/6i-naive patients, with a median TTP of 5.77 months (95% CI 5.13-6.67 months) versus 7.77 months (95% CI 7.428.07 months), respectively. This difference remained significant after adjusting for patient characteristics (hazard ratio 1.26, P = 0.003), except for comparison with fluoropyrimidine-based regimens, which showed comparable efficacy across groups.ConclusionsThis study highlights the variability in second-line treatment strategies following CDK4/6i and suggests the presence of cross-resistance reducing the efficacy of subsequent chemotherapy.

Introduction

Metastatic breast cancer (MBC) remains the leading cause of cancer-related mortality among women worldwide.1 Within its subtypes, hormone receptor (HR)-positive, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative accounts for ∼65%-70% of MBC patients.2

Despite advancements in treatment, MBC remains incurable, with a median overall survival (OS) of ∼5 years for patients receiving modern first-line therapies.2 Cyclin-dependent kinase 4/6 inhibitors (CDK4/6i) combined with endocrine therapy have become the standard first-line treatment for HR-positive/HER2-negative MBC. These agents, including palbociclib, ribociclib, and abemaciclib, have demonstrated significant improvements in progression-free survival (PFS) and, in some cases, OS in clinical trials.3, 4, 5 However, disease progression during CDK4/6i therapy remains inevitable, underscoring the need for effective therapeutic options beyond first-line treatment. Additionally, the recent expansion of CDK4/6i use into the adjuvant setting for high-risk early-stage HR-positive/HER2-negative breast cancer raises important questions about cross-resistance and the long-term impact of these agents on subsequent therapeutic sequencing and efficacy.6

For patients experiencing progression after CDK4/6i therapy, several therapeutic options are available. These include endocrine therapies such as selective estrogen receptor degraders (SERDs), alone or in combination with CDK4/6i or targeted therapies directed against an actionable molecular alteration (BRCA1/2, PIK3CA, or AKT), trastuzumab deruxtecan for HER2-low tumours, sacituzumab govitecan, and various chemotherapy regimens.7 However, most of these therapies were developed and evaluated in CDK4/6i-naive populations, leaving a knowledge gap in patients who have received CDK4/6i, where mechanisms of cross-resistance may limit their efficacy.

Real-world evidence plays a critical role in addressing these gaps and informing clinical practice, but remains relatively scarce in this specific context.8 The Epidemiological Strategy and Medical Economics (ESME)-MBC cohort, which includes comprehensive data from 18 French Comprehensive Cancer Centres, provides an opportunity to analyse treatment patterns and outcomes in this population.9

The primary objective of this study is to describe the treatment patterns and evaluate the efficacy of systemic treatments for HR-positive/HER2-negative MBC after progression on first-line CDK4/6i. The secondary objective is to compare the efficacy of chemotherapy between patients previously exposed to CDK4/6i and those who were CDK4/6-naive.

Materials and methods

Study design and data source

This non-interventional retrospective study was conducted using data from the ESME-MBC database (NCT03275311), a national observational cohort collecting real-world data on MBC patients. The database includes routinely collected information on all consecutive patients aged ≥18 years who initiated MBC treatment at one of the 18 French Comprehensive Cancer Centres since 2008. Data are updated annually and include patient demographics, histopathology, treatment patterns, and clinical outcomes. The methodology and quality assessment processes of the ESME-MBC database have been previously described in detail.9

The study was conducted in accordance with French and European data protection regulations. The ESME research programme is managed by Unicancer in accordance with current best practice guidelines and rules. It is supervised by an independent steering committee. The ESME-MBC database was authorized by the French data protection authority (initial authorization no. DE-2013-117 and subsequent amendment obtained in 2019 in accordance with GDPR) and study approval was obtained from an independent scientific committee.

In accordance with the authorizations issued by the French authorities for the ESME-MBC database and in compliance with the regulation in force for such retrospective registry, no formal dedicated informed consent is required. All patients were informed about the ESME program and have approved the use of their data.

Study population

This study included HR-positive/HER2-negative MBC patients who entered the cohort between January 2008 and December 2019, received endocrine therapy alone or with CDK4/6i as their first systemic treatment, and subsequently initiated a second-line of treatment. Patients treated with chemotherapy as first-line treatment were excluded, assuming that such a treatment is intended for patients in visceral crisis, whereas endocrine therapy alone or with CDK4/6i is recommended for all other cases.

Two cohorts were defined based on first-line treatment: one cohort consisted of patients treated with CDK4/6i, usually associated with endocrine therapy (CDK4/6i-exposed cohort), while the other included patients treated with endocrine therapy alone (CDK4/6i-naive cohort). To maintain homogeneous populations and enable meaningful comparisons of subsequent treatments based on first-line therapy, patients who received CDK4/6i beyond the first-line setting were excluded.

Descriptive analysis was carried out on the CDK4/6i-exposed cohort, regardless of subsequent treatments, while the comparative analysis focused on patients from both cohorts who received chemotherapy as subsequent treatment.

Data collection

Data were collected until the cut-off date for extraction, February 2021. The first-line treatment was defined as the first regimen [chemotherapy, endocrine therapy, CDK4/6i, phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K)/mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) inhibitor, or other treatments] initiated within 12 weeks before or after MBC diagnosis and still ongoing at the time of diagnosis. Disease progression was defined as detection of a new metastatic site, progression of existing metastases, or local or regional recurrence of the primary tumour. Subsequent lines of treatment were defined as any new regimen initiated within 12 months following documented disease progression. Treatment lines could consist of single agents, combination therapies, or sequences (e.g. initial therapy followed by maintenance or a switch due to toxicity).

Statistical analysis

Qualitative variables were described using numbers and percentages, and quantitative variables were described using medians and interquartile ranges. Missing data were presented but not considered for the percentage calculation. For multivariate analyses and propensity score calculation, we imputed missing values using the principal component method factorial analysis for mixed data10 with the following variables as predictors: sex, age, metastatic sites, previous chemotherapy, previous radiotherapy, previous hormonotherapy, metastasis-free interval, and first-line treatment. Comparisons of characteristics were carried out using Fisher’s exact test, Pearson’s chi-square test, or the Kruskal–Wallis rank sum test, as appropriate.

Follow-up time was estimated using the reverse Kaplan–Meier method. Time to progression (TTP) was defined as the interval between the start of the first subsequent treatment and date of disease progression or death, or censored to date of latest news. Post-progression survival (PPS) was defined as the interval between the first disease progression and date of death, or censored to date of latest news. Both TTP and PPS were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method and compared between treatment groups using the log-rank test.

The descriptive analysis aimed to explore treatment patterns and second-line TTP among patients exposed to CDK4/6i as first-line treatment. Patient characteristics were compared across subgroups defined by the second-line treatment. A P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant for these exploratory analyses, and no adjustment for multiple comparisons was applied.

The comparative analysis aimed to compare TTP and PPS for the first chemotherapy regimen between CDK4/6i-exposed and CDK4/6i-naive patients. The main analysis used a weighted Cox proportional hazards regression model with stabilized inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) based on propensity scores and adjusted for chemotherapy regimen and general condition at chemotherapy initiation. The propensity score ranges from 0 to 1 and reflects the conditional probability of receiving CDK4/6i as first-line treatment for the given characteristics. It was calculated through logistic regression with a generalized logit model using major prognostic variables (after multiple imputation for missing data): age at MBC diagnosis, histological type, histological grade, estrogen receptor status, progesterone receptor (PR) status, metastatic sites, number of metastatic sites, performance status at MBC diagnosis, MBC diagnosis circumstances, adjuvant chemotherapy, adjuvant endocrine therapy, adjuvant radiotherapy, and time between initial and MBC diagnosis (as proxy for endocrine resistance/sensitivity).

The following supportive analyses were planned to confirm the robustness of the main analysis: multivariate Cox model adjusted for individual prognostic factors, multivariate Cox model adjusted for propensity scores, and propensity score matching (through nearest neighbour method with three calipers and optimal matching).

All statistical analyses were conducted using R software (version 4.1.0) with relevant packages such as ‘missMDA’, ‘sunburstR’, ‘MatchIt’, and ‘WeightIt’.

Among the 47 patients who received a second sequence of CDK4/6i, the CDK4/6i was switched in 17 (36%), the endocrine therapy was changed in 13 (28%), and both components were modified in 8 (17%). Similarly, the CDK4/6i rechallenge was more commonly observed in patients with durable responses to their first-line treatment.

Among the 255 patients treated with chemotherapy as first subsequent treatment, fluoropyrimidine-based regimens (essentially capecitabine monotherapy) was used in 99 patients (39%) and had a median TTP of 7.62 months (95% CI 6.13-8.30 months). Taxane-based regimens, with or without bevacizumab, were administered to 99 patients (39%) with a median TTP of 5.00 months (95% CI 3.68-6.63 months). Anthracycline-based regimens were used in 28 patients (11%) and had a median TTP of 6.43 months (95% CI 3.87-12.29 months). Other chemotherapy treatments were administered to 29 patients (11%) with a median TTP of 5.45 months (95% CI 4.81 months-NA).

Among the 140 patients treated with endocrine therapy alone as first subsequent treatment, fulvestrant and other SERDs were used in 93 patients (66%) with a median TTP of 4.33 months (95% CI 3.87-6.84 months). Aromatase inhibitors were administered in 41 patients (29%) and had a median TTP of 14.71 months (95% CI 8.73 months-NA).

Among the 85 patients treated with PI3K/mTOR inhibitors as first subsequent treatment, everolimus was used in 67 patients (79%) and had a median TTP of 4.27 months (95% CI 3.45-6 months) while alpelisib was administered in 18 patients (21%) with a median TTP of 7.29 months (95% CI 5.40 months-NA).

Comparison of chemotherapy efficacy based on CDK4/6i exposure

In this planned analysis, we compared the efficacy of first subsequent chemotherapy between CDK4/6i-exposed and CDK4/6i-naive patients. Before adjustment, patients exposed to CDK4/6i were generally younger and had better performance status, but presented with more aggressive cancers, characterized by PR-negative tumours, visceral metastases, and high tumour burden (Supplementary Table S3, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2025.105803). Median OS was 33.5 months (95% CI 30.0-40.1 months) for CDK4/6i-exposed patients and 38.4 months (95% CI 36.9-39.9 months) for CDK4/6i-naive patients (Supplementary Figure S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2025.105803).

The median TTP for chemotherapy was shorter in the CDK4/6i-exposed group, at 5.77 months (95% CI 5.13-6.67 months), compared with 7.77 months (95% CI 7.42-8.07 months) in the CDK4/6i-naive group (hazard ratio 1.36, 95% CI 1.19-1.54, P < 0.001). This difference was numerically higher in the taxane-based (4.97 versus 7.33 months) and anthracycline-based (6.43 versus 8.42 months) as compared with fluoropyrimidine-based regimens (7.61 versus 8.53 months, with mainly capecitabine monotherapy). The negative relationship between CDK4/6i exposure and TTP remained significant after adjusting for confounders in IPTW-adjusted Cox regression analysis (adjusted hazard ratio 1.26, 95% CI 1.08-1.47, P = 0.003) (Figure 4A), especially in the taxane- and anthracycline-based chemotherapy subgroups (Figure 4B). The median PPS was also shorter in CDK4/6i-exposed patients, at 20.6 months (95% CI 18.0-24.5 months), compared with 23.4 months (95% CI 22.3-24.4 months) in the CDK4/6i-naive group (hazard ratio 1.21, 95% CI 1.04-1.40, P = 0.016). Discussion

This study provides comprehensive real-world evidence on treatment patterns and clinical outcomes in HR-positive/HER2-negative MBC following progression on first-line CDK4/6i. We observed significant variability in physician practice patterns, with nearly half of the patients receiving chemotherapy (47%) as subsequent treatment, while others were treated with endocrine therapy alone (26%), PI3K/mTOR inhibitors (16%), or CDK4/6i rechallenge (9%). These findings align with previous studies, where chemotherapy use ranged from 17% to 48%, endocrine therapy from 12% to 33%, PI3K/mTOR inhibitors from 12% to 33%, and CDK4/6i rechallenge from 1% to 36%, depending on the historical period and geographical region.11, 12, 13, 14

The relatively high rate of chemotherapy use reflects the limited efficacy of endocrine therapy alone in this setting, as evidenced by the CAPitello-291 and EMERALD trials, where the median PFS with standard endocrine monotherapy was only 2-3 months in CDK4/6i-pretreated patients.15,16 Regimens combining endocrine therapy with inhibitors of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway are increasingly viewed as a standard-of-care approach in this setting. Indeed, the SOLAR-1 trial demonstrated the efficacy of alpelisib in patients with PIK3CA-mutated tumours and the BYLieve trial further confirmed its activity in a cohort exclusively composed of patients who had progressed on CDK4/6i.17,18 Rechallenge with CDK4/6i beyond progression remains a topic of debate. While retrospective studies have indicated potential clinical benefit, prospective trials such as MAINTAIN, PACE, PALMIRA, and postMONARCH have reported mixed outcomes.19, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24 This uncertainty may partly account for the low prescription rate observed in our study.

The efficacy of subsequent treatment varied significantly. In our study, the median TTP was 5.9 months with chemotherapy, consistent with prior reports where PFS ranged from 3.7 to 7.4 months.11,13,14,25 The median TTP for endocrine therapy alone and in combination with PI3K/mTOR inhibitors was 6.1 months and 5.2 months, respectively, consistent with previous studies reporting PFS ranges from 3.3 to 6 months for endocrine therapy alone and from 3.3 to 8.5 months for PI3K/mTOR inhibitor-based regimens.11,13,14,25, 26, 27 Within the PI3K/mTOR subgroup, everolimus-based therapy yielded a median TTP of 4.27 months, which was slightly lower than the 6.0 months reported in a recent real-world study, whereas alpelisib-based therapy achieved a TTP of 7.29 months, comparable to the 8.0 months observed in the BYLieve trial.18,28 Regarding CDK4/6i rechallenge, the median TTP in our study was 9.57 months, aligning with prior studies that reported PFS ranging from 5.3 to 11.8 months.13,19,20 These results suggest that a subset of patients can continue to derive meaningful clinical benefit from CDK4/6i therapy beyond initial progression.

A key feature of our study was the comparison of chemotherapy outcomes between CDK4/6i-exposed and CDK4/6i-naive patients. While preclinical studies suggested preserved antitumour activity of chemotherapeutic agents in CDK4/6i-resistant breast cancer cell lines,29,30 our findings showed that CDK4/6i-exposed patients experienced a modestly shorter TTP and exhibited a trend toward shorter PPS with second-line chemotherapy compared with CDK4/6i-naive patients. This association persisted after adjustment for confounding factors and was consistent across all supportive analyses, strongly suggesting the presence of cross-resistance mechanisms.

Key mechanisms underlying resistance to CDK4/6i include dysregulation of cell cycle machinery, characterized by RB1 loss, CDK6 amplification, and up-regulation of the cyclin E/CDK2 pathway, which allows tumour cells to bypass CDK4/6-dependent checkpoints.31 Additionally, activation of survival and proliferative signalling pathways, such as PI3K/AKT/mTOR, as well as AURKA, c-MYC, ERBB2, FGFR1, and FGFR2 alterations have been implicated.32 These alterations not only enable tumour cells to evade CDK4/6-mediated cell cycle control but also contribute to more aggressive biological behaviour.7

Among the different chemotherapy regimens, our data suggest better outcomes for fluoropyrimidine-based regimens, with a median TTP of 7.6 months compared with 5.0 and 6.4 months for taxane-based and anthracycline-based regimens, respectively. This trend is consistent with findings from two previous studies, which similarly reported superior outcomes with fluoropyrimidine-based regimens in CDK4/6i-exposed patients.14,25 Furthermore, the efficacy difference between CDK4/6i-exposed and CDK4/6i-naive patients was principally driven by taxane- and anthracyclines-based regimens, while efficacy seemed preserved for fluoropyrimidines. A key factor in the differential efficacy of these agents lies in their mechanisms of action. Fluoropyrimidines inhibit thymidylate synthase and incorporate its metabolites into DNA and RNA, affecting both proliferative and quiescent cells entering the cycle.33 In contrast, taxanes and anthracyclines only target actively dividing cells, with taxanes stabilizing microtubules and anthracyclines intercalating into DNA and inhibiting topoisomerase II.34 Preclinical studies and molecular profiling of CDK4/6i-resistant tumours have identified several alterations that may explain this pattern. After prolonged exposure to CDK4/6i, many tumours acquire alterations allowing them to bypass the G1/S checkpoint and proliferate independently of CDK4/6 activity. These include RB1 loss, cyclin E1/E2 overexpression, CDK2 activation, and amplification or mitotic drivers such as AURKA.7,35,36 This reduced reliance on active cell division may decrease the sensitivity to taxanes and anthracyclines, while fluoropyrimidines remain effective due to their broader mechanism of action on nucleotide metabolism and RNA function. Notably, cyclin E overexpression has been directly associated with anthracycline resistance in preclinical models.37

This study has several strengths. Firstly, it leverages a large, real-world multicentre cohort, capturing a broader spectrum of patients, including those with comorbidities, which enhances the generalizability of the findings. Secondly, the use of robust statistical methods, such as IPTW, allowed for the adjustment of potential confounders and reduced bias in comparative analyses, strengthening the validity of the observed outcomes.

However, certain limitations must be acknowledged. The retrospective design inherently introduces potential biases, including selection bias and information bias. Additionally, while efforts were made to adjust for confounding factors, unmeasured variables may still have influenced treatment selection and outcomes. Sensitivity to endocrine therapy was not directly included in the propensity score but was approximated by the interval between the initial diagnosis and the diagnosis of metastatic disease. Furthermore, missing data imputation was necessary for some key variables, which, although handled using appropriate statistical techniques, may have affected the precision of the results. The absence of certain molecular data, such as ESR1 and PIK3CA mutation status, limited the ability to explore the impact of biomarkers on treatment outcomes. Lastly, the evaluation of new treatment options, such as trastuzumab deruxtecan for HER2-low tumours and sacituzumab govitecan, was not possible due to the lack of follow-up data for these recently approved therapies.

Although OS from first-line treatment was reported, this analysis should be interpreted with great caution. The study population was indeed intentionally highly selected to enable valid comparisons between different treatment sequences. The inclusion of only patients who received a second-line treatment excluded those with a favourable prognosis who experienced a prolonged response to first-line therapy. Conversely, the exclusion of patients who received chemotherapy as first-line treatment removed those presenting with visceral crisis, typically associated with a poorer prognosis. Finally, the exclusion of patients who received a CDK4/6i in the second-line setting or beyond was essential to define distinct groups in which the effectiveness of subsequent treatments could be compared based on initial treatment. However, this criterion inherently excluded patients who were treated before CDK4/6i became available as first-line therapy. Beyond differences in treatment eras, the design of the study—which required patients to survive until second-line treatment to be included—introduces an immortal time bias that limits the comparability of OS estimates across subgroups. Further studies are needed to explore the molecular basis of cross-resistance and to develop new therapeutic strategies. Understanding how alterations in key pathways, such as PI3K/AKT/mTOR, FGFR, and ERBB2, contribute to resistance leading to novel combination approaches.7,32 Another promising area of research involves antibody–drug conjugates (ADCs), which have shown potential in heavily pretreated HR-positive/HER2-negative MBC. However, caution is warranted regarding the choice of payload, as microtubule targeting based ADCs may be less effective in CDK4/6i-exposed patients, given the reduced efficacy of taxanes observed in this population.

Conclusions

This study provides important real-world evidence on treatment patterns and clinical outcomes in HR-positive/HER2-negative MBC following progression on CDK4/6i. By highlighting the variability in subsequent therapeutic strategies and the differential efficacy of chemotherapy, endocrine therapy, PI3K/mTOR inhibitors, and CDK4/6i rechallenge, it underscores the complexity of managing patients in this setting. It also confirms the impact of cross-resistance on subsequent chemotherapy efficacy, especially for taxane and anthracycline-based regimens.

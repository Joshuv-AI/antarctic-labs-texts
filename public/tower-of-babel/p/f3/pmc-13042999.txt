Response to ethical concern regarding “Magnetic resonance imaging features of bilateral multiloculated extraneural ganglion cysts of the occipito–atlanto–axial joint causing hypoglossal nerve paralysis in a dog”
Authors: Minha Ji, Matti Kiupel, Hyungjin Park, Kichang Lee, Hakyoung Yoon
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We appreciate the opportunity to respond to the concern raised regarding our article entitled “Magnetic resonance imaging features of bilateral multiloculated extraneural ganglion cysts of the occipito–atlanto–axial joint causing hypoglossal nerve paralysis in a dog” (JVIM, https://doi.org/10.1111/jvim.17192). Intraoperative and post operative analgesia was provided during and after surgery.

Intraoperative analgesia was provided as part of the anesthetic protocol. Before anesthesia induction, the dog was pre-oxygenated for approximately 10 minutes. Premedication consisted of butorphanol (0.2 mg/kg IV) and midazolam (0.3 mg/kg IV), administered intravenously to provide sedation and analgesia. Glycopyrrolate (0.01 mg/kg IV) was administered before induction to reduce vagal reflexes. Anesthesia was induced with propofol (2 mg/kg IV), administered slowly to effect, and the patient was subsequently intubated with a cuffed endotracheal tube. General anesthesia was maintained with isoflurane (1.5–2.0%) in 100% oxygen. During surgery, lactated Ringer’s solution was administered at 5 mL/kg/h, and physiologic parameters including heart rate, respiratory rate, SpO2, end-tidal CO2, and mean arterial pressure were continuously monitored. Administration of butorphanol before induction provided intraoperative analgesia throughout the surgical procedure.

Postoperative analgesia consisted of a fentanyl–ketamine combination administered as a constant rate infusion (CRI). The analgesic solution was prepared by adding 4 mL of fentanyl (50 μg/mL) and 0.4 mL of ketamine (100 mg/mL) to 100 mL of 0.9% normal saline, resulting in a final volume of 104.4 mL and final concentrations of 1.9 μg/mL (fentanyl) and 0.38 mg/mL (ketamine). The CRI was delivered at 2.5 mL/kg/h, corresponding to fentanyl 4.8 μg/kg/h and ketamine 1.0 mg/kg/h. For this 3.7 kg dog, the infusion rate was 9.3 mL/h. Concurrent maintenance fluid therapy was administered at 2.5 mL/kg/h.

The fentanyl–ketamine CRI was continued for 48 h postoperatively. Pain was assessed regularly during hospitalization based on clinical parameters, including mentation, posture, vocalization, and response to palpation. No clinically relevant adverse effects attributable to the analgesic protocol were observed.

After 48 h, analgesic management was transitioned to oral medication administered twice daily (BID), based on serial clinical pain assessments indicating minimal to no observable pain response. The oral regimen consisted of gabapentin (10 mg/kg BID) and methocarbamol (20 mg/kg BID). Prednisolone was administered at 0.5 mg/kg BID (total daily dose 1 mg/kg/day) for anti-inflammatory purposes as part of postoperative management. The dog remained clinically stable and comfortable after discontinuation of CRI.

On postoperative day 6, the patient was discharged with instructions to continue the oral medications BID for an additional 7 days to ensure sustained postoperative pain control. No evidence of uncontrolled pain or adverse effects was identified before discharge.

We regret that the details of intraoperative and postoperative analgesia were inadvertently omitted from the published manuscript. Appropriate perioperative pain management was provided in accordance with accepted standards of veterinary surgical care. This omission does not affect the clinical findings, interpretation, or conclusions of the report.

We appreciate the reader’s attention to this matter and the opportunity to clarify this point.

Sincerely,

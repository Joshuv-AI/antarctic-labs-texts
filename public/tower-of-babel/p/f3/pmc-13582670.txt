Epidemiological Characteristics of Hospitalized Sports-Related Ocular Trauma in China: China Ocular Trauma Study (COTS)
Authors: Haokun Zhang, Siyue Chen, Honglian Wu, Tianjing Yang, Jiayi Song, Yuanyuan Liu, Xue Dong, Yi Lei, Mengyu Liao, Hua Yan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSports-related ocular trauma is a significant cause of unilateral visual impairment and blindness, yet large-scale epidemiological studies on hospitalized cases are lacking. The aim of this study is to analyze the epidemiological characteristics, injury mechanisms, and visual outcomes of sports-related ocular trauma in China, and provide evidence for developing prevention strategies.MethodsA multicenter cross-sectional study was conducted based on data from hospitalized patients with sports-related ocular trauma from 99 medical institutions registered in the “China Ocular Trauma Registry System” (COTS) between 2021 and 2024. Demographic characteristics, sports types, ocular injuries, and visual acuity were analyzed using descriptive statistics.ResultsSports-related injuries constituted 3.60% (386 of 10710) of all ocular trauma patients. Patients were predominantly male (290 cases, 75.13%), with a trend towards youth aged 10–20 years accounting for over one-third of the total. Seasonally, ocular trauma most frequently occurred in spring, peaking in May (17.36%). Badminton was the first causative sport (127 cases, 48.47%), followed by soccer (41 cases, 15.65%), racket impact (13 cases, 4.96%), basketball (12 cases, 4.58%), volleyball (12 cases, 4.58%), and human contact injuries (11 cases, 4.20%). Closed ocular trauma was predominant injury (74.09%), with contusions comprising the majority. Despite the relatively lower incidence (16.58%), open ocular trauma was significantly associated with activities such as racket impact, boxing, and glasses fragments. Regarding the severity of visual impairment, badminton and soccer resulted in mild visual impairment, whereas basketball, glasses fragments and dumbbells were more likely to cause vision loss.ConclusionSports-related ocular trauma in China exhibits distinct characteristics in terms of sport type distribution. Protective eyewear in high-risk sports and safety education are recommended to lower the incidence and severity of ocular trauma.

Introduction

Ocular trauma is one of the leading causes of unilateral visual impairment and blindness, and sports-related ocular trauma is a common and serious type of injury during sports activities, which can not only lead to temporary or permanent visual impairment but also have long-term impacts on patients’ psychology, occupation, and daily life [1, 2]. It is estimated that approximately 55 million ocular injuries occur globally each year, of which nearly 19 million results in vision loss or blindness, and about 750,000 require hospitalization [3, 4].

With the increasing awareness of national fitness and the rapid development of the sports industry, the population participating in sports continues to expand, and the risk of sports-related ocular trauma rises correspondingly. Although the prevention and treatment of sports-related ocular trauma has been gradually aware internationally, the injury characteristics and prevention strategies may vary significantly among different regions due to regional and cultural differences in sports types, participating populations, and protective habits [5]. An epidemiological study on sports-related eye injuries in the United States showed that basketball, baseball, softball, and air gun shooting are the main causes of sports-related eye injuries for emergency [1]. However, most associated literature in China is regional or focused on professional athletes, and there is still a lack of large-scale epidemiological investigations and analyses based on nationwide data on hospitalized sports-related ocular trauma.

Compared to other forms of ocular trauma, sports-related injuries possess distinct characteristics. They typically occur in a predictable context of specific sports activities. Therefore, a significant proportion of sports-related ocular trauma is considered highly preventable through interventions. Hence, a systemic elucidation of clinical features and risk factors of sports-related ocular trauma based on epidemiological characteristics and injury mechanisms, is essential and urgent to formulate scientific and effective prevention strategies.

Since its establishment in 2021, the “China Ocular Trauma Registry System” (COTS) has recorded clinical data of hospitalized patients with ocular trauma from 99 medical institutions in 21 provinces, municipalities, and autonomous regions across the country, providing a platform for high-quality research on national ocular trauma [2]. This study aims to use the national inpatient data from the COTS between 2021 and 2024 to analyze the epidemiological characteristics, injury types, and causative features of sports-related ocular trauma in China, explore its risk factors and propose comprehensive preventive measures such as health education, protective equipment, rule formulation, and training supervision, in order to reduce the occurrence of sports-related ocular injuries and protecting public visual health.

Methods

Study Design and Data Source

This is a multicenter, retrospective cross‑sectional study based on the “China Ocular Trauma Registry System”. This registry is a nationwide prospective database that collects standardized data from hospitalized patients with ocular trauma from 99 medical institutions (including 35 tertiary hospitals, 31 secondary hospitals, and 33 primary hospitals) across 21 provinces, autonomous regions, and municipalities [2]. Patient data were entered and uploaded by trained physicians through an online platform and reviewed by an expert committee to ensure data quality and accuracy. This study analyzed 10,710 hospitalized ocular trauma patients registered in the database from January 1, 2021, to December 31, 2024. This study followed the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Ethics Committee of Tianjin Medical University General Hospital (Approval No. IRB2023-YX-181-01). All participants provided informed consent.

Research Objects

We screened all patients diagnosed with mechanical ocular trauma from the database from 2021 to 2024. Then, we further screened cases with ocular trauma related to sports, including sports training and activities. Patients were exclude as followed: (1) non-sports related causes of injury (e.g., violence, traffic accidents, household chores, etc.); (2) Cases with incomplete data records, especially key variables (mainly missing basic information and injury cause); (3) Non‑mechanical injuries (e.g., thermal/chemical burns, electrical injuries); (4) History of prior ocular surgery or previous ocular trauma that could affect eye integrity or visual outcome; (5) Cases where cosmetic outcomes were the primary concern (e.g., isolated eyelid laceration without globe injury).

Data Collection

The following data was collected through the registry system: (1) Demographic data: including basic information such as age, gender, city, occupation, etc.; (2) Medical history: including time of injury, location, cause of injury, type of injury, eye protection measures used, status at time of injury, history of previous ocular diseases, etc.; (3) Ophthalmic examination: including injured eye, type and location of ocular injury, visual acuity (VA), intraocular pressure, etc.; detailed examination and recording of the cornea, anterior chamber, pupil, iris, lens, vitreous, retina, macula, optic nerve, etc., as well as the condition of the fellow eye; (4) Treatment: including surgery time, surgical methods, etc. (if applicable); (5) Follow-up: visual acuity at 3 months, 6 months, and 1 year postoperatively. Visual acuity recordings were uniformly converted to logMAR visual acuity for statistical analysis. For those unable to discern the largest optotype on the visual acuity chart, alternative tests such as counting fingers (CF), hand motions (HM), light perception (LP), and no light perception (NLP) were used.

Variable Definitions

Injury type: According to the international classification system for ocular trauma, mechanical ocular trauma was divided into open globe trauma and closed globe trauma: Open globe trauma included eyeball rupture, penetration, perforation, and intraocular foreign body; Closed globe trauma included contusion, lamellar laceration, and superficial foreign body [6–8].

Specific causes of sports-related ocular trauma included specific sports types (e.g., badminton, soccer, basketball, tennis, volleyball, table tennis, boxing, archery, frisbee, dumbbells, jump rope, air gun, bicycle, etc.); human‑related factors during sports (e.g., finger poke, foot kick, human collision, fighting, and other direct body‑part contacts); and injury that are not specific to a single sport but relevant across multiple sports (e.g., racket impact, glasses fragments, sharp object, etc.).

Visual outcome: The primary visual outcome was defined as the logMAR visual acuity at the last follow-up.

Other variables: Age was treated as a categorical variable (e.g., 0 ≤ age < 5, 5 ≤ age < 10, 10 ≤ age < 15, etc.). Time of injury was categorized by season: spring (March-May), summer (June-August), autumn (September-November), and winter (December-February).

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 27.0 software. Measurement data are expressed as mean ± standard deviation, and intergroup comparisons were conducted using t-tests or chi-square tests. All statistical analyses were two-sided, with a P < 0.05 considered statistically significant. The statistical analysis and presentation are consistent with the CHAMP statement [9].

Results

Among all hospitalized patients (10710 cases) in the China Ocular Trauma Registry System from 2021 to 2024, the incidence of sports-related ocular trauma was approximately 3.60% (386 cases). This study included a total of 386 hospitalized patients with sports-related ocular trauma. All patients were clinically diagnosed and had detailed epidemiological and clinical data recorded. In terms of demographic characteristics, there were 290 male patients (75.13%), average age: 31.85 ± 2.14 years; and 96 female patients (24.87%), average age: 33.72 ± 3.63 years (P = 0.39). The age distribution of patients showed a clear trend towards younger age, with patients aged 10–15 years (71 cases, 18.39%) and 15–20 years (60 cases, 15.54%) being relatively concentrated, together accounting for over one-third of the total (Fig. 1). The number of cases among children and the elderly was small.

In terms of occupational distribution, employees (151 cases, 39.12%) and students (135 cases, 34.97%) accounted for the highest proportions. Other occupations included agriculture (15 cases, 3.89%), unemployed (12 cases, 3.11%), sports professionals (10 cases, 2.59%), etc. (Table 1). Regarding the time of injury, the monthly distribution of sports-related ocular trauma was uneven, with the highest number of cases occurring in May (67 cases, 17.36%), followed by April (57 cases, 14.77%) and March (54 cases, 13.99%). Regarding the injured eye, right eye injuries occurred in 195 cases (50.52%), and left eye injuries in 191 cases (49.48%). In terms of trauma type, sports-related ocular trauma mainly presented as closed globe ocular trauma (286 cases, 74.09%), including contusion (280 cases, 72.54%), lamellar laceration (4 cases, 1.04%), and superficial foreign body (2 cases, 0.52%). Open globe ocular trauma also accounted for a considerable proportion (64 cases, 16.58%), including eyeball rupture (48 cases, 12.44%), penetrating injury (8 cases, 2.07%), and perforating injury (2 cases, 0.52%), etc.

In-depth analysis of the types of ocular injuries caused by different sports activities revealed differences in their potential risks. Taking badminton, which had the highest number of cases, as an example, the vast majority of its injuries were contusions (114/127, 89.76%), with no severe eyeball ruptures occurring, indicating a relatively lower risk of causing severe injury, but it could still cause serious blunt trauma. In contrast, some activities were associated with more severe open globe injuries: for instance, among the 13 injuries caused by racket impact, there were 2 cases of eyeball rupture; among 4 boxing injuries, there was 1 case of eyeball rupture; among 4 patients with glasses fragments injury, there were 2 cases of penetrating or perforating injury; all 3 dumbbell-related injuries presented as eyeball rupture (Table 1). This indicates that although participation in some sports and the number of cases are low, their potential for causing injury is high.

Preoperative anterior segment examination revealed that 66 patients (17.10%) had hyphema, 28 patients (7.25%) had varying degrees of shallow anterior chamber, 8 patients (2.07%) had iris defect, 4 patients (1.04%) had synechiae, and 3 patients (0.78%) had iridodialysis. Traumatic mydriasis was present in 64 patients (16.58%), and 22 patients (5.70%) had relative afferent pupillary defect (RAPD). There were 58 patients (15.03%) with traumatic cataract, of which 16 (4.15%) were accompanied by lens dislocation. Posterior segment involvement was also common, with 30 patients (7.77%) having varying degrees of vitreous hemorrhage and 27 patients (6.99%) having vitreous inflammatory opacity. Preoperative examination suggested retinal breaks in 11 patients (2.85%). Furthermore, 6 patients (1.55%) had already developed eyeball atrophy at admission, and the causative sports/activities were football (2 cases), boxing (1 case), and sports-related violence (3 cases).

Preoperative visual acuity results for the 386 patients showed that 107 patients (27.72%)had VA better than 0.0 logMAR and 33 patients (8.55%) had VA between 0.0 and 0.1 logMAR. Among patients with VA below 1.0 logMAR, 13 (3.37%) had VA between 1.3 logMAR and 2.0 logMAR, and 11 (2.85%) had VA between 1.0 logMAR and 1.3 logMAR. Fifty-four patients (14.00%) suffered more severe visual impairment, including counting fingers (9 patients, 2.33%), hand motions (21 patients, 5.44%), light perception (13 patients, 3.37%), and no light perception (11 patients, 2.85%), indicated that sports-related ocular trauma could cause serious visual impairment.

Further analysis of the association between various sports activities and VA showed differences in the VA distribution of ocular trauma caused by different sports. Among patients with badminton-related ocular trauma, 70 of 127 (55.12%) had VA of 0.3 logMAR or better, while only 11 patients (8.66%) had VA below 1.0 logMAR. Among patients with soccer-related ocular trauma, 30 of 41 (73.17%) had visual acuity of 0.3 logMAR or better, while only 5 (12.20%) had visual acuity below 1.0 logMAR. For other sports such as tennis and volleyball related ocular trauma, the VA of patients was above 1.0 logMAR, mostly concentrated at 0.3 logMAR or better. In contrast, among patients with ocular trauma caused by basketball (12 persons), 4 patients (33.33%) had visual acuity of 0.3 logMAR or better, and 5 patients (16.67%) had visual acuity below 1.0 logMAR (Table 2). In ocular trauma caused by glasses fragments, finger pokes, dumbbells, boxing, air gun, and bicycle, although the number of patients was small, the vast majority had visual acuity below 1.0 logMAR, suggesting that the type of sport causing the ocular trauma may be directly related to the post-injury visual status.

Discussion

Interpretation of Key Findings

Based on inpatient data from 99 medical institutions nationwide in the China Ocular Trauma Registry System (COTS) from 2021 to 2024, this study systematically analyzed the epidemiological characteristics, causes, and injury types of sports-related ocular trauma in China. The incidence of sports-related ocular trauma in 10,710 hospitalized patients was about 3.60% (386 cases). The patients’ age showed a trend towards youth, with adolescents aged 10–20 years accounting for over one-third, which was similar to the results reported previously [10]. Among the sports-related ocular trauma, badminton, soccer and racket impact were top three causes. The injury type was predominantly closed globe trauma (especially contusion), accounting for 72.80%, but open globe injuries was prevalent in some activities such as boxing, dumbbells, glasses fragments, etc. Primary VA after ocular trauma was better than 0.1 logMAR in 36.27% patients, while 14.00% of patients exhibited severe visual impairment. The preoperative visual acuity distribution of ocular trauma caused by different sports varied; for example, patients with ocular trauma caused by badminton and soccer had less visual impairment, while the proportion of patients with low vision was higher in basketball, glasses fragments, dumbbells, and other activities. Haring et al.1 reported that in US emergency departments, paintball (10.2%), air gun (8.2%), racket sports (5.8%), and soccer (5.7%) had the highest rates of visual impairment. However, their outcome was post‑injury visual symptoms, not preoperative visual acuity in hospitalized patients. Our study provides complementary data on preoperative vision in a more severe inpatient cohort, which has rarely been reported previously.

Strengths of This Study

This study collected data from what is currently the largest national-level ocular trauma registry system, providing the latest epidemiological evidence on sports-related ocular trauma inpatient hospitalization in China in recent years. The research results indicate that although most sports-related ocular traumas are closed globe injuries, the proportion of open globe injuries in specific activities cannot be ignored, and preoperative visual impairment is closely related to the type of sport. This evidence demonstrates the importance of mandatory eye protection during these high-risk sports, providing a basis for targeted prevention and treatment.

Our conclusion focused on patients with moderate to severe ocular trauma based on nationwide, multicenter inpatient data, hence the injury severity and healthcare setting were different from Haring’s study [1], which relied on emergency department data including mild cases. In addition, basketball, baseball/softball, and air guns were the main sports causing eye injuries in US emergency departments, while badminton, soccer and racket impact were top three causative sports in our study, which may attribute to regional differences in sports types and physical exercise participation habits between these two countries. Additionally, this study systematically recorded preoperative visual acuity distribution and analyzed the association between different sports and visual impairment. Our study found that the severity of visual impairment was mainly related to the injury type, affected anatomical site, and complications. The type of motion affects the visual outcome by determining its specific injury mechanism (e.g., high-speed projectors, blunt impacts).

Clinical Implications

Our study clarified core epidemiological and clinical issues regarding sports-related ocular trauma in China. (1) Sports activities: Badminton is the leading sport causing hospitalized ocular injuries, and its high incidence is closely related to the sport’s high popularity among the public in our country, the high speed of the shuttlecock, and the general lack of protective awareness among participants. (2) Injury characteristics: Patients with sports-related ocular trauma are predominantly young males, who are the most active in competitive and recreational sports and therefore have a higher chance of being exposed to risks. However, permanent visual impairment in this age group would cause huge potential years of life lost and socio-economic burden. (3) Visual impact: The injury type is mainly contusion, but some high-risk activities are prone to cause open injuries and severe visual impairment. Most patients retained relatively good vision. Poor visual prognosis is mainly related to mechanisms leading to open injuries and severe complications involving the posterior segment of the eye (e.g., vitreous hemorrhage, retinal detachment), indicating the severity of the sports-related ocular trauma and the importance of timely treatment require consideration.

Targeted Prevention Strategies

Based on our epidemiological findings, we propose the following targeted prevention strategies: (1) For high‑incidence sports such as badminton and soccer, the use of polycarbonate sports goggles or face shields should be strongly recommended, especially for amateur players who lack formal training. (2) For sports associated with severe open‑globe injuries (e.g., boxing, dumbbells, glasses fragment injuries), stricter enforcement of protective equipment rules and public awareness campaigns are urgently needed. (3) Given the predominance of young male students in our cohort, school‑based eye safety education programs should be implemented to reduce risky behaviors such as playing without eye protection. (4) For activities causing high‑velocity projectile impacts (e.g., badminton shuttlecock, basketball), first responders and coaches should be trained in emergency eye injury management to avoid delayed referral and secondary damage.

Limitations

There were several limitations in this study. All data were acquired from the China Ocular Trauma Registry System based on inpatient data, and outpatient or mild cases that did not seek medical attention were potentially underestimated. The sample size for some sports activities was small, and subsequent cohort studies were required to further verify associations with visual impairment. Long‑term follow‑up (> 1 year), which may have been affected by late complications, was not available in this study. Cosmetic outcomes were not evaluated. Non‑mechanical injuries and isolated adnexal or orbital trauma without globe involvement were excluded from this study, which focused exclusively on mechanical globe injuries. Therefore, the findings do not apply to those types of trauma.

Additionally, the time interval from injury to hospital presentation was not uniformly recorded in the registry, which may affect the interpretation of visual outcomes and treatment urgency. Specific posterior segment findings such as traumatic glaucoma, macular edema, and choroidal tear were not uniformly documented across all participating centers. The lack of these data may lead to underestimation of certain complications. The registry did not distinguish between the injured person being the active player versus a bystander (e.g., spectator or passerby). In our cohort, the vast majority of injuries were sustained by the person playing the sport, but this could not be formally validated.

Furthermore, information on the use of protective eyewear or other safety equipment at the time of injury was not systematically recorded. Therefore, the association between protective measures and injury prevention could not be analyzed. Prevention strategies are evidence-based recommendations based on our epidemiological findings rather than direct observations. Detailed information on surgical timing and specific procedures was not available in this study, as it focused on pre‑intervention epidemiology. Statistical correlations between visual outcomes and potential risk factors (e.g., injury type, age, gender, or treatment modality) were not performed due to incomplete data and the descriptive nature of this study.

Summary of This Study

Sports-related ocular trauma is a preventable public health problem. For sports with high incidence of ocular trauma, the use of sports protective eyewear should be vigorously promoted, and safety education should be strengthened among adolescents and athletes. For activities prone to causing severe visual impairment, on-site first aid and timely referral systems need to be improved to avoid delayed treatment resulting in severe outcomes such as massive prolapse of ocular contents or atrophy of the eyeball. Governments, medical institutions, and social organizations should collaborate to promote the standardization of protective equipment, optimization of sports protection rules, and enhancement of public awareness of ocular trauma prevention to reduce the incidence of ocular trauma and alleviate the long-term burden on individuals, families, and society caused by sports-related ocular trauma.

Conclusion

Sports-related ocular trauma in China exhibits distinct characteristics in terms of age, gender, and sport type distribution. Protective eyewear in high-risk sports and safety education are strongly recommended to lower the incidence and severity of ocular trauma.

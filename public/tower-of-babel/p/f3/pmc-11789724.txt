Response to “A bibliometric and knowledge-map study on the treatment of hematological malignancies with CAR-T cells from 2012 to 2023: A correspondence”
Authors: Huimin Li, Qing Huang, Yuan Zhang
Published: 2024

Dear Editor,

We have carefully reviewed an article commentary provided by Jin et al., entitled “A bibliometric and knowledge-map study on the treatment of hematological malignancies with CAR-T cells from 2012 to 2023: A correspondence.” We thank them for their reading and critical comments. We are also willing to have an academic discussion with them on their questions.

Jin et al. posited that our manuscript lacked the h-index of researchers and the cooperative analysis of research institutions. While the incorporation of the h-index may enhance the research findings, it is important to note that the h-index is not an indispensable metric for the bibliometric analysis. The h-index serves as a measure of researchers’ academic productivity and impact, taking into consideration both the quantity and citation frequency of their publications. However, it overlooks papers with low citations and lacks sensitivity toward those with high citations.1 Bibliometric analysis can evaluate the influence of countries, institutions, authors, and periodicals through the number of citations and co-citations. This part has been included in our previously published manuscript.2 Furthermore, we analyzed the collaboration between research institutions in the field of CAR-T cell therapy for hematological malignancies in our published manuscript.2

Jin et al. suggested that bibliometrics analysis should only select the Science Citation Index Expanded (SCI-Expanded) database in the Web of Science Core Collection (WOSCC) database as the data source. However, we contend that this may not be the optimal approach for bibliometric analysis. The multidisciplinary nature of CAR-T cell research includes different fields, and only relying on the SCI-Expanded database may omit relevant literature. According to the prescribed literature search strategy, theoretically, the literatures that do not meet the conditions will be automatically excluded by the system. Consequently, the utilization of the WoSCC database does not impede the retrieval of pertinent literature. Conversely, relying solely on the SCI-Expanded database increases the risk of overlooking relevant literature. Furthermore, we used the boolean operator “AND” in our search strategy, which further excluded papers that did not meet the criteria. In bibliometrics research, conducting a thorough and comprehensive literature retrieval within a specific field is crucial.3 Jin et al. thought that “Many scholars advise against integrating too many databases into a single analysis because it can lead to complications.” We don’t agree with them. As far as we know, the view has not been widely recognized. Our review of pertinent literature indicates that this view mainly appeared in a limited number of commentaries in recent 1–2 years.4–6 Up to now, the method proposed by Jin et al. is not the mainstream method of literature retrieval in bibliometrics research. We will substantiate our argument with statistical data in subsequent analysis.

Jin et al. posited that utilizing “Title (TI),” “Abstract (AB),” and “Author Keywords (AK)” may yield more precise and pertinent data sets compared to Topic Search (TS). Furthermore, they also pointed out that “These techniques are widely recognized in modern research for yielding more accurate and comprehensive search results.” However, we hold a differing perspective on this matter. To our knowledge, the retrieval approach advocated by Jin et al. has not garnered widespread recognition. The efficacy of this retrieval method needs further examination. We found that this view also mainly appeared in a few commentaries,4–6 in recent 1–2 years.

To further prove our point, we searched the articles and reviews related to bibliometrics research published in the Pubmed database from 2023 to 2024 with impact factors (IF) equal to or exceeding 10. These publications involve many fields such as medicine, chemistry, environmental science, and materials science. As of July 11, 2024, a total of 46 eligible articles were collected, with 9 papers being excluded due to unattainable full texts and unclear search methodologies. Ultimately, 37 papers were included in the analysis, revealing that 26 out of the 37 papers employed a same retrieval strategy to ours, constituting 70.3% of the total. Moreover, it was observed that none of the 37 papers utilized the retrieval method proposed by Jin et al.

Based on our research findings, the retrieval method used in our manuscript may be the most recognized method currently. Finally, we thank Jin et al. for their academic discussion. It is believed that with the further development of bibliometrics research, more perfect retrieval strategies may appear in the future.

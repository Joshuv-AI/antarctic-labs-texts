P2RX4 promotes hepatocellular carcinoma progression via calcium-mediated PI3K/AKT activation and immune remodeling
Authors: Jianrong Wang, Yanmei Gu, Ze Niu, Futian Tang, Yumin Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is frequently identified at advanced stages. This constrains therapeutic options and results in poor prognosis. P2RX4 is an ATP-gated ion channel that modulates calcium influx. It participates in cellular proliferation, inflammatory processes, and immunological reactions. Nonetheless, its role in HCC remains poorly comprehended. This study explored the expression, function, and immunological effects of P2RX4 in HCC.MethodsWe examined P2RX4 expression using TCGA-LIHC and TIMER2.0 databases. Protein levels were validated using immunohistochemistry in 140 HCC tissues. A prognostic model was developed utilising P2RX4 expression. In vitro investigations were conducted subsequent to the silencing of P2RX4. The experiments encompassed cell proliferation, invasion, and colony formation. We further conducted transcriptome sequencing. Ion concentrations were quantified with ICP-OES. PI3K/AKT activation was evaluated using Western blotting.ResultsP2RX4 exhibited elevated expression in HCC tissues. Its expression was associated with advanced tumor stage and poor prognosis. Silencing of P2RX4 decreased tumour cell proliferation and invasion. It also reduced intracellular calcium levels and inhibited AKT phosphorylation. Elevated P2RX4 levels correlated with an increased presence of M0 macrophages and Tregs, a reduced number of monocytes, and a poorer anticipated response to immunotherapy.ConclusionsP2RX4 may facilitate HCC progression by augmenting calcium influx, activating the PI3K/AKT pathway, and diminishing anti-tumor immunity. It may function as a biomarker and therapeutic target in HCC. Additional, in vivo investigations are required to validate these findings.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12957-025-04023-6.

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is a predominant cause of cancer mortality globally [1]. The incidence is expected to increase by 55% by 2040 [2]. The majority of patients receive diagnoses at advanced stages owing to the absence of early symptoms [3]. Despite advancements in medicines such as targeted agents and immunotherapy have improved, survival outcomes continue to be suboptimal. There is an urgent necessity to identify novel treatment targets and reliable prognostic biomarkers for HCC [4].

P2RX4 is a member of the P2X receptor family and functions as an ATP-activated ion channel that facilitates calcium influx [5]. P2RX4 is extensively present in neurones, immunological cells, and epithelial tissues [6]. As a key energy molecule, ATP activates P2RX4 both intracellularly and extracellularly, eliciting many biological reactions [7]. Initial studies have predominantly concentrated on its function in inflammation, wherein ATP-induced activation of P2RX4 regulates intracellular calcium concentrations and affects cellular metabolism and death. Furthermore, high ATP buildup can trigger inflammasome activation and promote cell death through calcium-dependent pathways involving P2RX4 [8].

Recent findings indicate that P2RX4 plays a significant role in critical mechanisms that promote tumour growth. It has been associated with the regulation of calcium signalling, cellular motility, immunological responses, and tumor cell proliferation [9]. In colorectal cancer, ATP produced after chemotherapy activates P2RX4, which subsequently stimulates the mTOR pathway and promotes tumor growth [10]. In glioblastoma, inhibiting P2RX4 diminishes the release of IL-1β and IL-18, hence restricting cellular migration and invasion.

P2RX4 has also been associated with hepatic disorders. Xia [11] reported that the P2RX4 antagonist 5-BDBD alleviates alcohol-induced hepatic inflammation and fat buildup. In hepatitis C, P2RX4 expression is heightened in hepatitis C, facilitating inflammatory responses by increasing the expression of extracellular matrix components such as laminin and elastin [12]. The buildup of matrix induced by P2RX4 in hepatic myofibroblasts is a defining characteristic of liver fibrosis, as demonstrated by Guilcher [13]. P2RX4 facilitates the activation of hepatic stellate cells and expedites the advancement of fibrosis.

Notwithstanding increasing evidence of its participation in diverse liver disorders, the precise function of P2RX4 in hepatocellular carcinoma is still inadequately comprehended. This study sought to investigate the clinical significance, functional role, and immunological impact of P2RX4 in HCC.We employed transcriptome analysis, immunohistochemistry, association with clinical data correlation, and in vitro experiments to investigate its biological function. We examined intracellular calcium levels and analyzed AKT pathway activation to explore the downstream processes. Our findings may elucidate the potential of P2RX4 as a therapeutic target in HCC.

Materials and methods

Data gathering and analysis

The expression of P2RX4 in various tumours was examined using the public database TIMMER2.0 (http://timer.cistrome.org/). Gene expression data and associated clinical information for HCC were collected from the The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database. We employed the Limma package in R to analyze differential expression of P2RX4 and its clinical associations. Clinical data heatmaps were generated with the ComplexHeatmap package.

Survival analysis was performed using the R packages survival and survminer. Kaplan–Meier curves and log-rank tests were used to compare overall survival between groups. Cox regression provided hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs). The proportional hazards assumption was tested with cox.zph. Time-dependent ROC curves (1-, 3-, 5-year) were generated using timeROC to evaluate predictive performance.

We developed a prognostic nomogram and evaluated its efficacy by time-dependent ROC and Decision Curve Analysis (DCA) to measure the model’s predictive precision and possible advantages in clinical decision-making.

Tumor Immune Dysfunction and Exclusion (TIDE) was applied to estimate immune evasion and predict immunotherapy response based on gene expression. Scores were computed from TCGA-LIHC data, and patients were stratified into high and low TIDE groups.

Statistical and bioinformatics studies were conducted in the R environment (v4.4.2).

Transcriptome sequencing and analysis

RNA extraction was conducted using the AG RNAex Pro Kit (AG21101, Accurate Biology, China), and its integrity was assessed using the Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer. RNA libraries were constructed and sequenced using the Illumina platform (Novogene, China). Subsequent mapping of clean reads to the human genome (Ensembl GRCh38, release 92) yielded mapping ranging from 91.18% to 92.91%. All six samples produced over 6.8 Gb of high-quality bases, with Q20 and Q30 values above 99.13% and 97.37%, respectively. followDifferential expression analysis was performed utilising the DESeq2 program, ed by functional enrichment using GO and KEGG databases.

Tissue samples and IHC

Samples were obtained from Lanzhou University Second Hospital, comprising 140 patient-derived tissue specimens collected between January 2019 and June 2023. IHC was employed to evaluate P2RX4 protein levels in malignant and adjacent non-neoplastic tissues. The IHC protocol encompassed tissue microarray preparation, sectioning, antigen extraction, incubation with Anti-P2RX4(1:150, Proteintech, 13534-1-AP, China) at 4 °C, secondary antibody incubation, and chromogenic development. Immunoreactivity was assessed using two criteria: (1) the percentage of positive cells (0 for none, 1 for 1–33%, 2 for 34–66%, and 3 for 67–100%) and (2) staining intensity (0 for absent, 1 for weak, 2 for moderate, 3 for strong). A composite score was derived by multiplying these values. Samples with values of ≤ 6 were classified as low expression, while those > 6 were considered high expression.

Lentivirus transfection

The human liver cancer cell lines Huh7, HCC-LM3, 7721, HepG2, Hep3B, and L02 were sourced from the Key Laboratory of Environmental Oncology, Gansu Province.

Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM; catalog number C12430500BT, Gibco, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; catalog number G8003, Servicebio, Wuhan, China) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin solution (catalog number G4003, Servicebio, Wuhan, China) was used for cell culture. Cells were cultured at 37 °C with 5% CO₂.

Western blotting

RIPA (R0010, Solarbio, China) buffer was employed to extract total cellular proteins. Following overnight incubation with primary antibodies GAPDH (1:10000, 10494-1-AP, Proteintech, China), P2RX4 (1:1250, 13534-1-AP, Proteintech, China), AKT (1:1000, T55561, Abmart, China), phospho-AKT (Ser473) (1:1000, T40067, Abmart, China), PI3K (1:1,000, T40115, Abmart, China), and phospho-PI3K (Tyr467) (1:1000, T40116, Abmart, China). HRP-conjugated secondary antibody was utilised. Protein concentrations were measured via an ECL kit, employing GAPDH as the loading control. Western blot analyses were conducted with three independent biological replicates. Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD).

Cell viability assay

Cell viability was assessed using the CCK-8 reagent (BS350B, Biosharp, China). Two thousand cells per well were seeded onto 96-well plates containing 100 µL of complete medium. At 24, 48, and 72 h after plating, the medium was replenished with 10 µL of CCK-8 reagent. After a 1–2 h incubation, absorbance at 450 nm was assessed using a microplate reader.

Colony formation

Approximately 1,000 cells were seeded inoculated per well for the colony formation experiment and cultured for 14 days. Following incubation, colonies were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and subsequently stained using crystal violet. Microscopic quantification of cellular counts per colony was performed, subsequently leading to the calculation of clonogenic efficiency.

Cell invasion assay

Matrigel (C0372, Beyotime, China) was administered into the upper chamber of the Transwell insert and allowed to hydrate for 3 h. Subsequently, 5 × 10⁵ cells were seeded into the upper compartment and incubated for 24 h. After fixation and crystal violet staining, the number of cells that penetrated the membrane was quantified under a microscope.

ICP-OES analysis of intracellular ions

Cells were collected and rinsed three times with calcium-free PBS. Following acid digestion with ultrapure nitric acid, the samples were diluted with distilled water and subsequently analysed via ICP-OES using the PQ 9100 system (Analytik Jena, Germany). Intracellular concentrations of Ca²⁺, K⁺, Na⁺, and Mg²⁺ were quantified and normalised to cell count, expressed as µg/10⁶ cells.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted with SPSS software (version 27.0, IBM, USA) and graphical representations were produced with GraphPad Prism 9.0. Categorical variables were compared using Pearson’s Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Continuous variables were compared using the Mann–Whitney U test when normality assumptions were not met. Variable Processing: Categorical variables were dichotomized for Cox regression analysis as follows: tumor grade was dichotomized into low grade (G1-G2) versus high grade (G3-G4); tumor stage was dichotomized into early stage (Stage I-II) versus advanced stage (Stage III-IV). ROC Analysis: Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed to evaluate the predictive performance of P2RX4 expression for year overall survival.

Results

P2RX4 exhibits elevated expression and is associated with HCC progression

Initially, we assessed P2RX4 using TIMER2.0 to investigate expression patterns across multiple cancer types. Increased, P2RX4 expression was noted in various tumour types, including liver hepatocellular carcinoma (LIHC), bladder urothelial carcinoma (BLCA), breast invasive carcinoma (BRCA), and colon adenocarcinoma (COAD) (Fig. 1A). To further explore its involvement in HCC, we analyzed TCGA-LIHC data and examined its correlation with clinicopathological characteristics. Using the “ComplexHeatmap” software, we noted that high levels of P2RX4 were associated with inferior tumour differentiation in HCC (Fig. 1B). P2RX4 expression was significantly elevated in HCC tissues relative to corresponding adjacent normal tissues, as shown by both unpaired and paired analyses (Fig. 1C, D). In addition, P2RX4 levels were increased in tumours exhibiting a higher histological grade (Fig. 1G) and in patients with T2 and T3 stage tumors (Fig. 1H). Independent prognostic significance of P2RX4 expression in HCC patients

Patients exhibiting heightened P2RX4 expression demonstrated much poorer overall survival (HR = 0.70, 95% CI: 0.50–0.99, P = 0.044) and progression-free survival (HR = 0.73, 95% CI: 0.54–0.97, P = 0.032) compared to those with low expression levels (Fig. 2A, B). Furthermore, time-dependent ROC curves demonstrated the moderate predictive performance of P2RX4 alone for overall survival prediction with AUCs of 0.636, 0.573, and 0.588 at 1-, 3-, and 5-year follow-ups, respectively (Fig. 2C). Based on Youden’s index analysis, the optimal cut-off for P2RX4 expression predicting 1-year survival was 1.75 (sensitivity = 0.653, specificity = 0.599, Youden index = 0.252). Prior to Cox regression analysis, the proportional hazards assumption was verified using Schoenfeld residuals test (global P = 0.29), confirming the validity of the Cox model. Univariable Cox regression analysis identified both P2RX4 expression and tumor stage as significant risk factors (Fig. 2D). To improve predictive performance, we constructed a prognostic nomogram incorporating P2RX4 expression, age, and tumor stage (Fig. 2F). The calibration curves exhibited a robust correlation between expected and actual survival outcomes (Fig. 2G). In comparison to the expression of P2RX4 alone, the nomogram e demonstrated enhanced predictive efficacy, with AUCs of 0.735, 0.701, and 0.720 for 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival, respectively (Fig. 2H). The optimal decision threshold derived from the nomogram for predicting 1-year overall survival was 1.41, with a sensitivity of 0.667, specificity of 0.639, and Youden index of 0.306. This indicates an improved predictive performance compared to the single-marker ROC analysis.

Decision curve analysis (DCA) further indicated that the nomogram conferred superior clinical benefit compared to both treat-all or treat-none strategies across a range of threshold probabilities (Fig. 2I). The data indicate that integrating P2RX4 with clinical characteristics enhances prognostic assessment and may support personalized decision-making in HCC.

Elevated P2RX4 expression was substantially correlated with advanced T stage (P = 0.049), higher clinical stage (P = 0.009), and elevated CA19-9 levels (P = 0.025). No substantial correlations were seen with sex, age, tumor differentiation, M stage, AFP, CEA, or CA125 in Table 1. Subsequently, a variety of functional experiments were conducted to evaluate the effectst of P2RX4 knockdown. The CCK-8 experiment demonstrated a marked decrease in cell proliferation subsequent to P2RX4 silencing (Fig. 4C). These results collectively indicate that P2RX4 enhances critical malignant characteristics in HCC cells, such as increased proliferation, colony formation and invasion, hence confirming its possible oncogenic role in liver cancer progression.

P2RX4 knockdown reduces intracellular calcium levels and inhibits AKT activation in HCC cells

P2RX4 is an ATP-gated non-selective cation channel characterised by elevated permeability to Ca²⁺, moderate permeability to Na⁺ and K⁺, and restricted permeability to Mg²⁺. Mg²⁺ also stabilizes ATP binding and promotes channel activation.

Transcriptomic analysis indicated that P2RX4 knockdown correlated with disrupted calcium signaling and diminished PI3K/AKT pathway activity.

Based on these findings, we quantified intracellular amounts of Ca²⁺, Na⁺, K⁺, and Mg²⁺ concentrations in Huh7 and Hep3B cells to evaluate alterations in ion homeostasis based on these findings. The knockdown of P2RX4 markedly diminished intracellular Ca²⁺ levels in both cell lines (Figs. 6 A, E), whereas the amounts of K⁺, Na⁺, and Mg²⁺ remained unchanged (Figs. 6B–D, F–H), suggesting a targeted impact on calcium influx rather than a road disruption in ionic equilibrium. Moreover, Western blot analysis demonstrated that P2RX4 knockdown significantly reduced the phosphorylation of both PI3K and AKT, while leaving their total protein levels (Figs. 6I–J). The quantitative measurement of the p-PI3K/PI3K and p-AKT/AKT ratios demonstrated a significant decrease in PI3K/AKT pathway activation upon P2RX4 silencing. P2RX4 modulates tumor immune microenvironment and immunotherapy response in HCC

Immune elements in the tumour microenvironment (TME) are increasingly acknowledged as pivotal factors in the progression and malignant transformation of HCC [22].

Prior research demonstrates that calcium signaling and PI3K/AKT pathways, both regulated by P2RX4, are essential in alteringimmune cell activities and influencing tumour immunity [23]. Given our findings linking P2RX4 expression to altered macrophage infiltration, we hypothesize that P2RX4 may play a role in modulating tumor immunity and influencing immunotherapy efficacy in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), consistent with emerging evidence highlighting the importance of immune microenvironment dynamics in cancer progression [24].

To evaluate this idea, we methodically examined the correlation between P2RX4 expression and immune cell infiltration patterns using bioinformatics methodologie, including ESTIMATE, Limma, and CIBERSORT algorithms.

P2RX4 expression exhibited a positive association with both the Immune Score and ESTIMATE Score, suggesting that elevated P2RX4 levels may be associated with enhanced immune cell infiltration in HCC tissues (Fig. 7A). Correlation analysis with immune cell markers showed that P2RX4 expression was significantly correlated with heightened infiltration of M0 macrophages (R = 0.36, P = 0.0029; Fig. 7C) and regulatory T cells (Tregs) (R = 0.27, P = 0.029; Fig. 7D), while exhibiting a negatively correlation with monocyte levels (R = − 0.27, P = 0.024; Fig. 7E). However, the diminished percentage of responders in the high P2RX4 expression cohort indicates a heightened probability of recurrence and metastasis, likely leading to poorer clinical outcomes [25].

The data align with the increased TIDE observed in the high-expression group, reinforcing the idea that patients with high P2RX4 expression may demonstrate less susceptibility to immune checkpoint blockade. Immunotherapy aims to augment the immune system’s capacity to identify and eradicate tumour cells (Fig. 7J); however, an elevated TIDE score indicates heightened immunological dysfunction and exclusion, potentially compromising the antitumor immune response.

Collectively, our data indicate that P2RX4 plays a role in modulating the tumour immunological milieu by regulating immune cell infiltration and immune checkpoint expression, hence enhancing resistance to immunotherapy. Further research is necessary to validate the potential of P2RX4 as a viable therapeutic target in HCC.

Discussion

Hepatocellular carcinoma continues to be a significant contributor to cancer-related mortality globally [26]. Identifying novel treatment targets is crucial for improving clinical management [27]. P2RX4, a ligand-gated ion channel activated by ATP [28], has been implicated in various biological processes. Recent investigations have implicated P2RX4 in the progression of several malignancies. Qiao [29] performed a pan-cancer analysis to examine the role of P2RX4 and confirmed its elevated expression in HCC by analyzing data from the Human Protein Atlas (HPA). They also investigated P2RX4’s functions in glioblastoma and prostate cancer cell lines. Our study, however, focuses specifically on HCC. We analyzed P2RX4 expression in clinical patient samples using IHC and assessed its association with clinical features. To explore the functional role of P2RX4, we generated knockdown plasmids and conducted transcriptome sequencing. Subsequent cellular assays demonstrated that P2RX4 promotes HCC progression by facilitating calcium influx and activating the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway. Additionally, our data suggest that P2RX4 influences the tumor immune microenvironment, particularly by modulating macrophage infiltration and immune checkpoint expression, highlighting its potential role in tumor immune evasion. This study revealed that P2RX4 expression was markedly increased in HCC tissues relative to surrounding non-tumorous tissues. This was validated by transcriptome analysis and immunohistochemistry.

High P2RX4 levels were associated with advanced tumor stage, poor differentiation, and worse prognosis, suggesting a role in disease progression. While the ComplexHeatmap analysis revealed a significant correlation between P2RX4 expression and tumor grade based on TCGA data, this association was not observed in our IHC-based clinical cohort. This discrepancy may result from differences in sample sources and detection methods, highlighting the need for further investigation in larger and more diverse patient populations. IHC analysis further confirmed that P2RX4 is generally elevated in HCC tissues compared with adjacent non-tumorous tissues. However, in a minority of patients, tumor expression was lower than in the surrounding tissue, highlighting inter-patient heterogeneity. This variability likely reflects biological differences within tumors and their microenvironments, and suggests that patient-specific factors should be considered when using P2RX4 as a prognostic marker or therapeutic target. Moreover, investigating the tumor microenvironment and biological characteristics of patients with low P2RX4 expression, as compared to those with high expression, holds significant research value.

P2RX4 functions as a non-selective cation channel with high permeability to Ca²⁺ [30]. Calcium imbalance is recognised to compromise immune function [31] and facilitate tumour growth [32]. In liver cancer, altered calcium signaling enhances iron uptake and facilitates tumour proliferation and metastasis [33]. Focussing on calcium-dependent pathways, including calmodulin-dependent kinase IV, has demonstrated anti-tumor effects in HCC models [34]. In clinical samples, patients with high P2RX4 expression demonstrated increased serum calcium levels. While serum calcium reflects systemic metabolism rather than intracellular conditions, this observation implies that P2RX4 may influence calcium homeostasis at multiple levels. In vitro, silencing P2RX4 in Huh7 and Hep3B cells resulted in a substantial reduction of intracellular Ca²⁺ concentrations while leaving Na⁺, K⁺, or Mg²⁺ levels. Western blot research indicated that P2RX4 knockdown reduced the phosphorylation levels of both PI3K and AKT, while leaving their total protein expression. These results indicate that P2RX4 selectively modulates calcium influx and promotes activation of the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway in HCC cells, consequently enhancing tumour cell proliferation, migration, invasion, and colony formation.

P2RX4 is also involved in immunological modulation [35]. Prior research indicates that P2RX4 inhibition diminishes macrophage-driven inflammation in lung injury [36], while its persistent activation may exacerbate tissue damage in necrotic conditions [37]. Furthermore, genetic modifications in P2RX4 can impair the phagocytic capacity of macrophages and microglia [38]. In our investigation, patients with high P2RX4 expression demonstrated enhanced infiltration of M0 macrophages, as supported by both peripheral immunological data and computational analysis [39]. Bioinformatics analysis revealed that P2RX4 exhibited a positively associated with Immune and ESTIMATE scores, M0 macrophage, and Treg infiltration, while demonstrating an unfavourable relationship with monocyte presence. Furthermore, P2RX4 expression exhibited a substantial correlation with other immune checkpoint genes, including TNFSF15, CD267, and CD80. These findings suggest that P2RX4 may suppress monocyte activation and chemotaxis, preserving macrophages in an undifferentiated M0 state [40], which may subsequently evolve into an immunosuppressive phenotype [41]. Additionally, P2RX4 may also facilitate Treg recruitment, hence enhancing immune suppression and tumor immune evasion [42].

This research offers novel perspectives on the complex function of P2RX4 in hepatocellular carcinoma. Our findings suggest that P2RX4 not only aids tumour growth via the modulation of calcium signaling and proliferation, but also facilitates immune evasion by altering the tumour immunological microenvironment. P2RX4, due to its dual oncogenic and immunoregulatory functions, presents promise as a prognostic biomarker and potential therapeutic target for both targeted therapies and immune-based treatments. Current efforts should focus on elucidating the molecular foundation of P2RX4-mediated immune regulation and assessing the therapeutic efficacy of monoclonal antibodies or small-molecule inhibitors targeting P2RX4 in preclinical and clinical models.

This study still has several limitations. In terms of statistical methods, multiple testing increases the risk of Type I error. Given the exploratory nature and biologically guided candidate selection of this study, multiple testing correction was not applied. Some marginal p-values may lose significance after correction; therefore, the results should be interpreted with caution. Future validation in larger cohorts and functional studies are warranted. Additionally, the lack of in vivo validation prevents full confirmation of P2RX4’s biological role. Based on our IHC findings, a subset of patients showed higher P2RX4 expression in adjacent non-tumorous tissues than in tumor tissues, highlighting inter-patient heterogeneity. This underscores the importance of careful patient selection in future therapeutic strategies targeting P2RX4. Further mechanistic and translational investigations are required to validate the therapeutic value of P2RX4 inhibition in HCC.

To the Editor:

Post-acute sequelae of SARS-CoV-2 infection (PASC) are defined as symptoms that remain present for ≥3 months or newly appear after a confirmed SARS-CoV-2 infection and are not explained by an alternative diagnosis [1]. PASC affects millions of people, with fatigue and persistent respiratory symptoms being the most common features [2, 3]. The pathological mechanisms of PASC are not fully understood but are likely to involve inflammation, endothelial activation and tissue remodelling [4, 5].

We have previously demonstrated increased pulmonary fibroblast activation in PASC using gallium-68-labelled fibroblast activation protein inhibitor ([68Ga]Ga-FAPI-46) positron emission tomography (PET) [6]. [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-46 binds to fibroblast activation protein (FAP) expressed on activated fibroblasts, essential cells that produce extracellular matrix (ECM) and contribute to wound healing [5, 7]. However, whether this observed low-grade fibroblast activation is associated with tissue remodelling in PASC remains unknown. Blood-based biomarkers may have the potential to enhance screening and therapeutic monitoring more effectively than nuclear imaging. ECM turnover can be indirectly assessed through measuring levels of circulating ECM fragments (identified via neo-epitopes generated during ECM synthesis or degradation by enzymes such as matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs)) [8]. Elevated ECM fragment levels are associated with disease severity and mortality in acute COVID-19 [9].

This study aimed to explore systemic signatures of tissue remodelling in PASC and its association with pulmonary fibroblast activation.

We included patients with dyspnoea >3 months after hospitalization for COVID-19 requiring mechanical ventilation or high-flow nasal oxygen. The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the University Medical Center Groningen (202100802) and registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05981885).

Image acquisition and analysis were previously described [6]. In short, a PET–computed tomography (CT) scan was performed with 200 MBq [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-46 (SOFIE, Dulles, VA, USA). The mean standardised uptake value corrected for lean body mass (SULmean) was calculated for the whole lung at both sides and the paravertebral muscle. To account for interpatient variability, lung target/background ratios (TBR) were computed by using blood pool as background activity from the vena cava inferior.

At the time of the PET-CT, serum was collected, centrifuged (2000×g from 10 min at 4°C) and stored at −80°C. Neo-epitope concentrations were measured using specific competitive ELISAs (Nordic Bioscience, Herlev, Denmark). Markers of collagen formation included nordicPRO-C3 (type III), nordicPRO-C6 (type VI, endotrophin) and nordicPRO-C22 (type XXII). Degradation markers of type III collagen included nordicC3F (FAP cleaved) and nordicC3M (MMP-2 and MMP-9 cleaved). Additional markers were nordicVICM (citrullinated and MMP-degraded vimentin (VICM)), nordicTGFβ-LAP (reflecting transforming growth factor (TGF)-β activation) and soluble FAP (sFAP). These markers were selected on the rationale of previous publications [9]. Neo-epitope concentrations were compared to those measured in serum from age- and sex-matched healthy controls from the NORM study (NCT00848406) [10]. Both [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-46-PET and ELISA have been shown to be suitable methods for detecting low-grade tissue remodelling [11, 12].

The Mann-Whitney U-test was used to test for differences in neo-epitope concentrations between PASC patients and controls. A two-sided p-value <0.05 was considered significant. Correlations between lung TBR or paravertebral muscle SULmean on PET and neo-epitope concentrations were assessed with a one-sided Spearman's correlation. One-sided testing was applied, as higher fibroblast activity was hypothesised to correlate with increased neo-epitope levels. Correlations between body mass index (BMI), time between [68Ga]FAPI PET/CT and hospital discharge (time after discharge), percentage of ground glass opacities (GGOs), percentage of predicted forced expiratory volume in 1 s (FEV1) and forced vital capacity (FVC), and neo-epitope concentrations were assessed with a two-sided Spearman correlation. These clinical parameters were selected as only these showed significant correlation with pulmonary FAP expression in the previous study. The difference in neo-epitope concentrations between smokers and nonsmokers was assessed with a one-sided Mann–Whitney test. Due to the lack of smokers in the PASC cohort, smoking was not evaluated further as a confounder.

We previously reported the full overview of patient characteristics [6]. In total, 18 PASC patients were included. The majority were male (n=10, 56%) with a median (interquartile range (IQR)) age of 59 years (54–67 years). None had pre-existing pulmonary conditions, except for one individual with asthma and one with obstructive sleep apnoea. None were active smokers and eight (44%) never smoked. Median (IQR) BMI was 34.6 kg·m−2 (31.1–42.4 kg·m−2). 16 (89%) patients were mechanically ventilated during initial hospital admission. The median (IQR) time between hospital discharge and study visit was 30.0 months (7.5–34.25 months). For this study, the control group consisted of 10 healthy individuals matched for age and sex. Four (40%) were male and median (IQR) age was 57 years (56–58 years). Five (50%) individuals never smoked and five (50%) were active smokers. Median (IQR) BMI was 24.1 kg·m−2 (22.9–25.4 kg·m−2) (p<0.001).

Of the assessed serum markers, only the median (IQR) concentration of VICM was increased in PASC patients compared to healthy controls (11.3 ng·mL−1 (8.1–17.3 ng·mL−1) versus 6.6 ng·mL−1 (4.0–8.7 ng·mL−1), p=0.005). Median concentrations of C3F (35.9 ng·mL−1 (30.0–50.9 ng·mL−1) versus 33.1 ng·mL−1 (27.8–46.6 ng·mL−1), p=0.63), C3M (10.1 ng·mL−1 (9.0–12.3 ng·mL−1) versus 10.0 ng·mL−1 (8.9–12.0 ng·mL−1), p=0.87), PRO-C3 (11.2 ng·mL−1 (9.2–17.2 ng·mL−1) versus 10.7 ng·mL−1 (8.7–11.6 ng·mL−1), p=0.52), PRO-C6 (8.0 ng·mL−1 (6.3–10.5 ng·mL−1) versus 5.8 ng·mL−1 (5.4–7.8 ng·mL−1), p=0.07), TGF-β latency-associated protein (LAP) (1.4 ng·mL−1 (1.0–1.7 ng·mL−1) versus 1.1 ng·mL−1 (1.0–1.4 ng·mL−1), p=0.40), PRO-C22 (26.8 ng·mL−1 (19.1–34.6 ng·mL−1) versus 20.6 ng·mL−1 (19.6–34.8 ng·mL−1), p=0.90) and sFAP (82 ng·mL−1 (65–92 ng·mL−1) versus 72 ng·mL−1 (66–89 ng·mL−1), p=0.60) were not increased in PASC patients compared to controls (figure 1a).

Figure 1b shows an example of a [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-46 PET/CT scan in a PASC patient. FAP expression, as measured by lung TBR, moderately correlated with C3M (r=0.44, p=0.03) and PRO-C6 (r=0.45, p=0.03) serum concentrations. FAP expression in the lung did not correlate with serum concentrations of C3F (r=0.01, p=0.49) or sFAP (r= −0.08, p=0.38). In addition, no correlations were found for PRO-C3 (r=0.03, p=0.45), VICM (r= −0.13, p=0.30), TGF-β-LAP (r=0.12, p=0.31) or PRO-C22 (r= −0.09, p=0.36) (figure 1c). A correlation between ECM markers and clinical parameters was observed between C3M and time after discharge (r= −0.55, p=0.02), PRO-C22 and FEV1 % pred (r= −0.48, p=0.04), and C3M and FVC % pred (r= −0.49, p=0.04). FAP expression in the paravertebral muscle, GGO and BMI did not correlate with any of the assessed ECM fragments (data not shown). No difference in ECM marker levels was observed between smokers and nonsmokers (data not shown).

In this small cohort, PASC patients showed elevated serum VICM levels. This fragment represents citrullinated and MMP-degraded vimentin. Vimentin is a cytoskeletal protein expressed by various cells. Release, citrullination and degradation by MMPs of this protein is mediated by activated macrophages [13]. Elevated VICM levels in PASC may therefore represent persistent macrophage activation. This aligns with evidence that PASC is associated with increases in interleukin (IL)-1β, IL-6 and tumour necrosis factor, which are predominantly secreted by macrophages [14]. Tissue macrophage activation may induce fibroblast activation and subsequent tissue remodelling in acute COVID-19 and PASC [15]. Serum PRO-C6 showed a trend towards elevation in PASC, while other circulating ECM fragments were not elevated compared to healthy controls. This may be due to their overall low concentrations and variable occurrence in healthy individuals, which may impede interpatient variability precluding their use as biomarkers in PASC.

The correlation between [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-46 uptake and both C3M and PRO-C6 shows an association between pulmonary fibroblast activation and systemic markers of ECM turnover. In contrast, no correlation was observed between systemic ECM fragments and [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-46 uptake in paravertebral muscle, suggesting the observed circulating ECM fragments reflect tissue remodelling in the lung. In line with our findings, several ECM fragments, including C3M and PRO-C6, are also elevated in acute COVID-19, and have been linked to disease severity and mortality [9, 16]. These findings suggest persistence of low-grade ECM remodelling beyond the acute phase of infection. Despite the known pro-inflammatory nature of elevated BMI, BMI was not correlated with any of the ECM markers, suggesting that BMI alone is unlikely to be a major driver of the observed biomarker differences. Collectively, these findings may support patient stratification in PASC and could guide the design of future clinical studies targeting tissue remodelling.

We did not find a correlation between pulmonary FAP expression and serum markers for FAP activity (C3F and sFAP). This may be due to the low expression of pulmonary FAP, insufficient enzymatic activity and that FAP might merely be a signal of activated fibroblasts. Lack of correlation between tissue and soluble FAP has been observed previously for cardiac FAP expression [17].

This pilot study enrolled a small cohort, limiting statistical power. However, to our knowledge, this is the first study to investigate the correlation between tissue FAP expression and circulating ECM fragments. Furthermore, the study design precluded causal inference, as potential confounding factors were not adequately controlled. Moreover, the cohort consisted of former severe COVID-19 patients with self-reported dyspnoea, further limiting generalisability to other PASC subtypes. Nonetheless, exploring PASC pathophysiology may enable improved patient selection and therapeutic targeting in the future. Finally, we assessed only eight ECM-related biomarkers, which does not fully capture the complexity of ECM remodelling [9].

In conclusion, pulmonary FAP expression is correlated with circulating signatures of ECM turnover, underscoring the role of tissue remodelling in PASC. Elevated VICM levels may reflect persistent macrophage activation potentially contributing to ongoing tissue remodelling processes.

Optimized seasonal influenza mRNA vaccine compositions demonstrate safety and enhanced immunogenicity in a phase 2 study
Authors: Carlos Fierro, Miaoxin Lin, Bethany Girard, Shannon McGrath, Xiaolin Chang, Qi Wu, Hsiao-Hsuan Kuo, Cathal Harmon, Jaap Oostendorp
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTSeasonal influenza causes considerable morbidity and mortality, with influenza A and B viruses driving most influenza-associated hospitalizations and deaths. Vaccination remains a key influenza prevention strategy; however, current seasonal influenza vaccines based on traditional platforms provide inconsistent protection. Messenger RNA – based vaccines may offer several key advantages over other vaccines, including the flexibility to optimize antigen expression to enhance immunogenicity without the need for adjuvants. mRNA-1010 is an investigational seasonal influenza vaccine candidate that encodes hemagglutinins (HAs) from WHO-recommended influenza strains. In this randomized, phase 2 study, safety, reactogenicity, and immunogenicity of 3 mRNA-1010 vaccine candidate compositions were evaluated in healthy adults aged 18–49 y in the United States (NCT05868382). Eligible participants were randomly assigned to receive a single dose of one of the mRNA-1010 compositions at several dose levels. The primary objective was the safety and reactogenicity of mRNA-1010 vaccine candidate compositions against vaccine-matched strains; secondary and exploratory objectives included humoral and cellular immunogenicity at evaluable timepoints, respectively. Two hundred and seventy participants received study vaccination between May and December 2023. All mRNA-1010 vaccine compositions were well tolerated, with no safety concerns identified; all compositions induced HA-specific humoral and cellular immune responses against all influenza strains evaluated, with the optimized compositions exhibiting higher immune responses against influenza B strains compared with the original mRNA-1010 composition. These results, together with findings from other mRNA-1010 clinical studies, support continued evaluation of mRNA-1010 for enhanced protection against seasonal influenza.Clinical Trials Registration: Clinicaltrials.gov: NCT05868382KEYWORDS: Immunogenicity, influenza, mRNA vaccine, reactogenicity, safety

Introduction

Seasonal influenza remains a major global health challenge and causes approximately 1 billion cases each year.1 Seasonal epidemics are driven primarily by influenza A and B viruses, which account for most influenza-associated hospitalizations and deaths, with increased cases during the winter months.1–3 These seasonal epidemics may be due to antigenic drift, which creates diverse influenza virus subtypes, allowing influenza viruses to continually circulate and escape immune detection.4–6 The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) classified the 2024‒2025 US influenza season as one of high severity across all age groups. Peak weekly influenza-associated hospitalization rates reached the highest levels in 15 y, while cumulative influenza-associated hospitalization rates were approximately 2- to 3-fold higher than the historical median.7

Vaccination remains a key influenza prevention strategy; however, current seasonal influenza vaccines based on traditional platforms provide inconsistent protection due to suboptimal immune responses, waning immunity, and antigenic mismatch with circulating strains that can occur due to current vaccine production timelines or adaptive mutations.1,6 Messenger ribonucleic acid (mRNA) – based vaccines may offer several key advantages over other vaccine platforms, including rapid design and shorter manufacturing time, elimination of egg-adaptive mutations, and the flexibility to optimize antigen expression to enhance immunogenicity without the need for adjuvants.8

mRNA-1010, evaluated in this study, is an investigational seasonal influenza vaccine candidate that encodes the hemagglutinins (HAs) of the influenza strains recommended by the World Health Organization (WHO) for the 2022‒2023 Northern Hemisphere season for cell- or recombinant-based vaccines.9 mRNA-1010 has been evaluated in various clinical trials, and the composition has been optimized throughout the course of clinical evaluation. The original mRNA-1010 vaccine composition was evaluated in a phase 1/2 study among US adults aged ≥18 y (NCT04956575), and exhibited an acceptable safety profile, elicited robust hemagglutination inhibition (HAI) titers, and induced CD4+ T-cell responses.10 The same composition was investigated in 2 phase 3 studies, which evaluated the safety and immunogenicity of mRNA-1010 in adults aged ≥18 y (NCT05415462), and mRNA-1010 efficacy in adults aged ≥50 y (NCT05566639), respectively.11 Results from these 2 studies showed that mRNA-1010 exhibited strain-specific efficacy, which mirrored the immunogenicity profile, with higher immune responses against influenza A strains compared with influenza B strains observed for mRNA-1010 vs. a comparator; this observation led to further optimization of mRNA-1010 to improve immune responses against influenza B strains.11 In another phase 3 study (NCT05827978), an optimized mRNA-1010 composition was shown to induce HAI titers against vaccine-matched influenza A and B strains that were superior to those elicited by licensed comparators (standard and high-dose) in adults aged ≥18 y.12 More recently, a pivotal phase 3 trial (NCT06602024) of an optimized trivalent mRNA-1010 composition demonstrated superior relative vaccine efficacy in adults aged ≥50 y vs. a licensed standard-dose influenza vaccine.13

Here, we report the safety, reactogenicity, and immunogenicity findings from a phase 2 trial in healthy adults aged 18‒49 y, directly comparing 3 mRNA-1010 vaccine compositions, including the original composition and 2 optimized compositions.

Methods

Study design and participants

This randomized, observer-blind, phase 2 study evaluated the safety, reactogenicity, and immunogenicity of 3 mRNA-1010 vaccine candidate compositions in healthy adults aged 18–49 y conducted at 10 sites in the United States (NCT05868382). This paper summarizes the results after all participants reached the end of the study.

Eligible participants were healthy adults aged 18‒49 y and capable of providing written informed consent. Inclusion/exclusion criteria are detailed in the Supplementary Materials. Participants were randomly assigned (2:1:2:1:1:2) using a centralized Interactive Response Technology (IRT) system to receive a single dose of either 50 µg of the original mRNA-1010 composition; 25 µg or 50 µg of the optimized composition 1; or 12.5 µg, 25 µg, or 50 µg of the optimized composition 2. The predefined randomization list was computer-generated using permuted blocks and implemented centrally to ensure allocation concealment. Randomization was implemented without stratification. Study personnel involved in participant assessment remained blinded to treatment assignment. Participants received a single dose of the respective mRNA-1010 vaccine composition on Day 1, and the final visit was on Day 181.

The study was conducted in accordance with the protocol and consensus and ethical principles derived from international guidelines, including the Declaration of Helsinki, Council for International Organizations of Medical Sciences, applicable International Council for Harmonization Good Clinical Practice guidelines, and other applicable laws and regulations. The protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board (Advarra, Columbia, MD, USA [Approval date: April 24, 2023; Approval number: Pro00071177]) prior to study initiation, and written informed consent was provided by all participants prior to enrollment.

Vaccines

All 3 mRNA-1010 vaccine candidate compositions included mRNAs encoding 4 seasonal influenza strains recommended by the WHO for the 2022‒2023 Northern Hemisphere season (including A/Wisconsin/588/2019 [H1N1], A/Darwin/6/2021 [H3N2], B/Austria/1359417/2021 [B/Victoria lineage], and B/Phuket/3073/2013 [B/Yamagata lineage]) for cell- or recombinant-based influenza vaccines formulated in lipid nanoparticles. Optimized compositions 1 and 2 both included amino acid point mutations in nonsurface-exposed regions to mRNAs encoding influenza B HAs that improve HA expression without affecting antigen sites. Optimized composition 1 was further optimized by updating the mRNA manufacturing process. All vaccine compositions were provided as a sterile liquid for injection and administered to the deltoid muscle (preferably in the nondominant arm) as a single 0.5 mL injection.

Objectives

The primary objective was to evaluate the safety and reactogenicity of the mRNA-1010 vaccine candidate compositions. The secondary objective was to evaluate the humoral immunogenicity of the mRNA-1010 vaccine candidate compositions against vaccine-matched strains at evaluable timepoints (Days 8, 29, 91, and 181). Exploratory objectives included evaluation of cellular immunogenicity (Days 8, 29, 91, and 181).

Reactogenicity and safety assessments

Reactogenicity assessments included solicited local adverse reactions (ARs; injection site pain, erythema, swelling, and underarm gland swelling or tenderness) and systemic ARs (headache, fatigue, myalgia, arthralgia, nausea/vomiting, chills, and fever) through 7 d after study intervention; ARs were recorded using an eDiary. Solicited ARs were graded as grade 1 (mild), grade 2 (moderate), grade 3 (severe), or grade 4 (life-threatening) (see the Supplementary Materials for additional details). Safety assessments included unsolicited adverse events (AEs) through 28 d after study intervention, and medically attended adverse events (MAAEs), adverse events of special interest (AESIs; thrombocytopenia, new onset or worsening of neurologic diseases, anaphylaxis, and myocarditis/pericarditis), serious adverse events (SAEs), and AEs leading to study or treatment discontinuation throughout the study (Day 181). Unsolicited AEs were collected through both active and passive surveillance by blinded study assessors, including systematic assessment at scheduled study visits, planned safety calls, and spontaneous reporting.

Immunogenicity assessments

Blood samples for immunogenicity assessments were collected on Days 1 (baseline), 8, 29, 91, and 181 (end of study). Humoral immunogenicity endpoints were measured by the HAI assay (as detailed in the Supplementary Materials) and included geometric mean titers (GMTs) at Days 1, 8, 29, 91, and 181; geometric mean fold rises (GMFRs) at Days 8, 29, 91, and 181 over Day 1; and seroresponse rates (SRRs) at Days 8, 29, 91, and 181. Seroresponse was defined as an increase in antibody titers and concentrations from predose below the lower limit of quantification (LLOQ) to ≥4 × LLOQ, or at least a 4-fold rise if predose was ≥LLOQ. Cellular immunogenicity endpoints included the frequency and magnitude of HA-specific T-cell responses, defined as CD4+ and CD8+ T-cell responses directed against vaccine-encoded hemagglutinin antigens as measured by flow cytometry and an intracellular cytokine staining assay. Polyfunctional HA-specific CD4+ T-cell responses were defined as CD4+ T cells co-expressing 2, 3, or all 4 functional markers: interferon gamma (IFN-γ), interleukin 2 (IL-2), tumor necrosis factor alpha (TNF-α), and CD40 ligand (CD40L). HA-specific CD8+ T-cell responses were defined as CD8+ T cells expressing any type 1 cytokine (IFN-γ, IL-2, or TNF-α) (see the Supplementary Materials for additional details).

Statistical analyses

No formal hypothesis testing was performed in this study. All comparisons were descriptive and exploratory. The number of participants was considered sufficient to provide a descriptive summary of the safety, reactogenicity, and immunogenicity of the mRNA-1010 vaccine candidate compositions. With approximately 60 participants in the 50 μg dose groups of the original and optimized mRNA-1010 compositions, there was ~95% probability of observing at least one AE with a true incidence of 5%. For the other groups of approximately 30 participants, there was ~95% probability for a 9.5% incidence.

All safety assessments, except for solicited local and systemic ARs, were assessed in the safety population, which included all participants who received 1 dose of vaccination. Solicited ARs were assessed in participants in the safety population who contributed any solicited AR data (solicited safety population).

Humoral immunogenicity assessments were performed in the per-protocol population (analysis populations are detailed in the Supplementary Materials). Intercurrent events such as intercurrent infection and important protocol deviations affecting immune response were handled using while-on-treatment and principal stratum strategies for estimands, respectively. Immunogenicity data collected after the first occurrence of intercurrent infections were excluded (censored) from analysis. Participants with major protocol deviations or prohibited medications were excluded from the per-protocol population. Descriptive statistics of GMT, GMFR, and SSR with 95% CI were provided. For GMTs and GMFRs, the 95% CIs were calculated based on the t-distribution of the log-transformed values, and then back-transformed to the original scale; the SRR was provided with a 2-sided 95% CI using the Clopper–Pearson method by study group. Missing humoral immunogenicity data were not imputed. All analyses were performed using SAS version 9.4 or higher (SAS Institute, Cary, NC).

For the 12.5 and 25 µg dose groups of the optimized mRNA-1010 compositions, cellular immunogenicity analysis was performed on participants with sufficient PBMC samples. For the 50 µg dose groups of the original and optimized mRNA-1010 compositions, cellular immunogenicity analysis was conducted on a randomly selected subset of participants with evaluable PBMC samples to ensure a balanced number of participants included in the cellular immunogenicity assessment across all dose groups.

Results

Trial population

A total of 270 participants aged 18‒49 y were randomly assigned to receive study vaccination, which was initiated on May 11, 2023. The study was completed on December 19, 2023, and the database lock was on February 2, 2024. All enrolled participants received study intervention (mRNA-1010 original composition 50 µg, n = 60; optimized composition 1 25 µg, n = 30; optimized composition 1 50 µg, n = 60; optimized composition 2 12.5 µg, n = 30; optimized composition 2 25 µg, n = 31, optimized composition 2 50 µg, n = 59), with most participants (245/270 [90.7%]) completing the study (completed the Day 181 final study visit). The reasons for study discontinuation were lost to follow-up (23/270 [8.5%]) and withdrawal of consent by the participant (2/270 [0.7%]) (Figure 1).

Participant baseline demographics and characteristics were generally well balanced across all vaccine groups (Table 1). The median age was 36 y, and most participants were female (56.3%). The majority of participants (73.7%) had not received an influenza vaccine in the previous influenza season.

Reactogenicity and safety

The frequency of any solicited ARs reported within 7 d of vaccination was comparable between vaccine groups, with the lowest proportion of solicited ARs observed in the 12.5 µg group of optimized composition 2 (18/30 [60.0%]; Figure 2). Across all study groups, the most commonly reported local AR was injection site pain (range, 53.3% to 84.7%), and the most common solicited systemic ARs were headache (range, 23.3% to 69.5%), fatigue (range, 32.3% to 66.7%), myalgia (range, 20.0% to 67.8%), and chills (range, 13.3% to 61.0%). Most solicited ARs were grade 1 or 2 in severity; no participants reported a grade 4 solicited AR. Across all study intervention groups, the median day of onset for solicited local and systemic ARs was study Day 1 and Day 2, respectively; the median duration was 2‒3 d for local and solicited ARs.

Unsolicited AEs were reported by 11 (18.3%), 4 (13.3%), 9 (15.0%), 4 (12.9%), and 7 (11.9%) participants in the mRNA-1010 original composition 50 µg, optimized composition 1 25 µg, optimized composition 1 50 µg, optimized composition 2 25 µg, and optimized composition 2 50 µg groups, respectively, within 28 d of vaccination; no unsolicited AEs were reported by participants in the optimized composition 2 12.5 µg group. Throughout the study (Day 181), unsolicited AEs across study groups ranged between 6.7% and 30.0%. Most unsolicited AEs were considered by the investigator not to be related to vaccination. Four participants experienced 5 nonserious unsolicited AEs that were assessed as vaccine-related by the investigator: 1 participant with decreased appetite in the mRNA-1010 original composition 50 µg group; 1 participant with injection site ecchymosis in the optimized composition 1 25 µg group; 3 unsolicited AEs were reported in the optimized composition 1 50 µg group, which included 1 participant with blurred vision and sore throat, and 1 participant with diarrhea. Overall, 5 participants experienced 5 SAEs during the study (cellulitis, hypertensive emergency, osteoarthritis, spontaneous abortion, and pancreatitis), all of which were assessed by the Investigator as not related to study intervention. There were no related SAEs, MAAEs, AESIs, AEs leading to study discontinuation, or deaths in any of the study groups.

Humoral immunogenicity

HAI GMTs were comparable among vaccine groups at baseline (Figure 3). HAI GMTs increased for all vaccine groups on Days 8 and 29, with a reduction observed on Days 91 and 181, relative to Day 29, for all 4 influenza strains. Across all vaccine groups, Day 91 titers remained above baseline for all 4 influenza strains (A/H1N1, A/H3N2, B/Yamagata, and B/Victoria). At Day 181, titers remained above baseline for 3 influenza strains (A/H1N1, A/H3N2, and B/Yamagata) (Figure 3).

Overall, the GMFRs from baseline to Day 29 were higher with increasing dose (12.5, 25, and 50 μg) of the optimized composition 2 across all 4 influenza strains (Figure 3). Day 29 GMFRs following a single 50 µg dose of the 3 mRNA-1010 compositions, ranged between a 5.42-fold and a 7.24-fold increase for A/H1N1, and between a 4.77-fold and a 6.02-fold increase for A/H3N2. Day 29 GMFRs for both influenza B strains were comparatively lower, with a 2.53-fold to a 3.65-fold increase for B/Yamagata and a 1.49-fold to 2.64-fold increase for B/Victoria. At Day 29, GMFRs for both influenza B strains following a 50 µg dose of the optimized compositions 1 and 2 were higher compared with the GMFR following 50 µg of the original mRNA-1010 composition (Figure 3). Similarly, the 50 µg dose of the optimized composition 1 showed higher SRRs than the original mRNA-1010 composition for both the B/Yamagata and B/Victoria strains, whereas the 50 µg dose of optimized composition 2 demonstrated higher SRRs than the original mRNA-1010 composition for B/Yamagata and similar responses for B/Victoria (Table S1).

Overall, at Day 29 humoral immune responses for the influenza B strains were higher for the optimized compositions 1 and 2 compared with the original mRNA-1010 compositions. No notable differences in humoral immune responses between the optimized and original compositions were observed for the influenza A strains at Day 29. At Day 181, humoral immune responses were comparable across the 50 µg doses of the original and optimized compositions for all influenza strains (Figure 3, Table S1).

Completion rates for humoral immunogenicity assessments were high through the key post-vaccination timepoints used to characterize peak response and early durability. Specifically, the proportion of participants with evaluable immunogenicity samples from baseline through Day 91 ranged from 95.6% (237/248) to 100% (248/248) across timepoints and influenza strains. Sensitivity analysis for missing data was not performed due to the high completion rates and the exploratory nature of the analyses.

Cellular immunogenicity

Influenza strain-specific polyfunctional CD4+ T-cell responses were elicited for all mRNA-1010 vaccine candidate compositions. CD4+ T cells co-expressing 2, 3, or all 4 markers (interferon gamma [IFN-γ], interleukin 2 [IL-2], tumor necrosis factor alpha [TNF-α], and CD40 ligand [CD40L]) peaked at Day 29, and then declined but remained above baseline at Day 181 (Figure 4). Overall, the peak polyfunctional CD4+ T-cell response elicited by the optimized composition 2 (12.5, 25, and 50 µg) was increased in a dose-dependent manner for all influenza strains. The strongest responses were observed with the 50 µg doses of the optimized compositions 1 and 2 against the B/Victoria and B/Yamagata strains. Vaccine-induced influenza strain-specific CD8+ T cells expressing any type 1 cytokine (IFN-γ, IL-2, or TNF-α) were less pronounced and more variable compared with antigen-specific CD4+ T cells expressing any type 1 cytokine, with similar trends across all influenza strains, and for all vaccine compositions (Figures S1 and S2).

Discussion

In this paper, we present findings from a phase 2, randomized, observer-blind study comparing the safety, reactogenicity, and immunogenicity of 3 investigational mRNA-1010 candidate vaccine compositions against seasonal influenza through 6 months following vaccination in healthy adults aged 18‒49 y. All 3 compositions induced HA-specific humoral and cellular immune responses against all influenza strains evaluated, with the optimized compositions 1 and 2 exhibiting higher immune responses against influenza B strains compared with the original mRNA-1010 composition.

All 3 mRNA-1010 vaccine compositions were generally well tolerated, with no safety concerns identified across the range of doses evaluated. The majority of solicited local and systemic ARs were mild or moderate in severity, with no grade 4 ARs reported. A dose-dependent trend in the occurrence and severity of local and systemic ARs was observed. The percentage of participants reporting unsolicited AEs was generally similar among the 25 µg and 50 µg groups across the different vaccine compositions up to 28 d after vaccination. Through 6 months, no deaths, AESIs, treatment-related SAEs, or AEs leading to study discontinuation were reported.

All 3 mRNA-1010 candidate vaccine compositions were capable of eliciting humoral immune responses against all influenza strains tested, as measured by the HAI assay, with the highest titers observed on Day 29. Antibody responses declined through 6 months but remained above baseline for influenza A/H1N1, A/H3N2, and B/Yamagata strains. Additionally, HAI titers remained above the 1:40 threshold through 6 months for most participants for all influenza strains evaluated (A/H1N1, 92.2%–96.3%; A/H3N2, 83.3%–94.1%; B/Yamagata, 95.8%–100%; B/Victoria, 89.3%–96.2%), which is traditionally recognized as a surrogate marker for protection, and considered to provide ≥50% reduced risk of influenza illness.14,15 This durable response was consistent with findings from the phase 1/2 mRNA-1010 study in adults aged ≥18 y, in which mRNA-1010 elicited humoral immune responses that declined from Day 29 but remained above baseline for influenza A strains, exceeded or were comparable to those of the comparator for A strains and B strains through 6 months, and remained above the 1:40 threshold.10

A single 50 µg dose of the optimized mRNA-1010 compositions 1 and 2 elicited a higher GMT response for both influenza B strains compared with the original mRNA-1010 composition at Day 29. This enhanced humoral response to influenza B strains can be attributed to the adaptations made to the influenza B strains included within both optimized vaccine compositions. This enhanced immune response to influenza B strains was also observed in a 3-part phase 3 study in adults aged ≥18 y (Part A), 18‒64 y (Part B), and ≥65 y (Part C), which evaluated the same optimized mRNA-1010 compositions.12

To date, seasonal influenza vaccines are largely dependent on generating neutralizing antibodies against circulating strains; however, protection may wane due to strain mismatch.16,17 Therefore, to overcome this limitation, it may be necessary for vaccines to elicit both humoral and cell-mediated immune responses, which can potentially provide broader protection against influenza.16,17 Accordingly, the current study evaluated influenza strain-specific cell-mediated immunity after mRNA-1010 vaccination using intracellular cytokine staining and T-cell polyfunctionality analyses. The frequency of non-naïve CD4+ polyfunctional T cells co-expressing 2, 3, or all 4 markers peaked at Day 29 and remained above baseline through Day 181 following a 50 µg dose of all 3 mRNA-1010 compositions; a non-naïve CD8+ polyfunctional response was also observed, although the response was less pronounced and more variable than the response observed for CD4+ T cells. Similar findings were observed in studies with licensed influenza vaccines.18,19 Of note, the frequency of CD4+ polyfunctional response in this study was higher for B strains, and was more evident among participants who received the 50 µg dose of the optimized compositions 1 and 2 than among those who received 50 µg dose of the original mRNA-1010 composition. This trend of increased cellular immune responses for B strains aligns with the humoral immunogenicity findings and can be attributed to the adaptations made to these optimized mRNA-1010 compositions. Both optimized compositions 1 and 2 included amino acid point mutations in non-surface-exposed regions of mRNAs encoding the influenza B HAs that improve HA expression without affecting antigen sites. The enhanced humoral and cellular immune responses observed for the influenza B strains are consistent with the design of the optimized compositions. Although mechanistic evaluation of the optimized formulations was beyond the scope of this study, our findings support that the improved influenza B immunogenicity may be related to enhanced antigen expression.

The strengths of the study include the direct comparison of the 3 vaccine compositions in a randomized, observer-blind design, which ensured unbiased collection of safety and immunogenicity data. Limitations include the small number of participants enrolled in the study, as well as the predominantly White, US-based participant population, which may limit generalizability to the broader population and other geographical regions. Furthermore, comparisons of the optimized mRNA-1010 compositions in this study were limited to descriptive immunogenicity assessments relative to the original mRNA-1010 composition. Importantly, the clinical relevance of one of the optimized compositions evaluated in this study has been further established in a recent phase 3 study demonstrating superior relative vaccine efficacy compared with a licensed standard-dose influenza vaccine.13

In conclusion, findings from this phase 2 study demonstrate that all 3 mRNA-1010 candidate vaccine compositions have an acceptable safety profile and induce humoral and polyfunctional CD4+ T-cell immune responses that persist through 6 months. These results, together with the findings from the various mRNA-1010 clinical studies, support the continued evaluation of mRNA-1010 for enhanced protection against seasonal influenza.

Irisin Ameliorated Skeletal Muscle Atrophy by Inhibiting Fatty Acid Oxidation and Pyroptosis Induced by Palmitic Acid in Chronic Kidney Disease
Authors: Ting Zhou, Shiyuan Wang, Yajing Pan, Xingtong Dong, Leiyun Wu, Jiali Meng, Jialing Zhang, Qi Pang, Aihua Zhang
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionProtein-energy waste (PEW) is a common complication in patients with chronic kidney disease (CKD), among which skeletal muscle atrophy is one of the most important clinical features of PEW. Pyroptosis is a type of proinflammatory, programmed cell death associated with skeletal muscle disease. Irisin, as a novel myokine, has attracted extensive attention for its protective role in the complications associated with CKD, but its role in muscle atrophy in CKD is unclear.MethodsPalmitic acid (PA)-induced muscular atrophy was evaluated by a reduction in C2C12 myotube diameter. Muscle atrophy model was established in male C57BL/6J mice treated with 0.2% adenine for 4 weeks and then fed a 45% high-fat diet. Blood urea nitrogen and creatinine levels, body and muscle weight, and muscle histology were assessed. The expression of carnitine palmitoyltransferase 1A (CPT1A) and pyroptosis-related protein was analysed by Western blots or immunohistochemistry. The release of IL-1β was detected by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay.ResultsIn this study, we showed that PA-induced muscular atrophy manifested as a reduction in C2C12 myotube diameter. During this process, PA can also induce pyroptosis, as shown by the upregulation of NLRP3, cleaved caspase-1 and GSDMD-N expression and the increased IL-1β release and PI-positive cell rate. Inhibition of caspase-1 or NLRP3 attenuated PA-induced pyroptosis and myotube atrophy in C2C12 cells. Importantly, irisin treatment significantly ameliorated PA-induced skeletal muscle pyroptosis and atrophy. In terms of mechanism, PA upregulated CPT1A, a key enzyme of fatty acid oxidation (FAO), and irisin attenuated this effect, which was consistent with etomoxir (CPT1A inhibitor) treatment. Moreover, irisin improved skeletal muscle atrophy and pyroptosis in adenine-induced mice by regulating FAO.ConclusionOur study firstly verifies that pyroptosis is a novel mechanism of skeletal muscle atrophy in CKD. Irisin ameliorates skeletal muscle atrophy by inhibiting FAO and pyroptosis in CKD, and irisin may be developed as a potential therapeutic agent for the treatment of muscle wasting in CKD patients.

Introduction

Protein-energy wasting (PEW) is essentially a state of reduced protein and energy storage, which is a prevalent complication in patients with chronic kidney disease (CKD) [1]. Skeletal muscle atrophy is one of the most important clinical features of PEW. Clinical studies have shown that skeletal muscle atrophy is associated with an increased risk of death and decreased quality of life in CKD patients [2, 3]. Although there are many studies on the pathogenesis of CKD skeletal muscle atrophy, there is still a lack of effective treatment methods. Therefore, it is of great clinical significance to explore the pathogenesis and prevention of CKD skeletal muscle atrophy.

Previous studies have suggested that lipid toxicity plays a key role in the development of skeletal muscle atrophy diseases [4–6]. Lipid abnormalities are common nutritional and metabolic disorders in CKD patients, and long-term lipid metabolism imbalance will lead to ectopic lipid accumulation and subsequent lipid toxicity, mainly for saturated fatty acids [7]. Afshinnia et al. [8] found that the serum saturated fatty acids of CKD patients increased significantly, and palmitic acid (PA) increased most significantly. Research studies have found that skeletal muscle atrophy in CKD patients is associated with skeletal muscle lipid accumulation [9, 10]. In an adenine-induced CKD mouse model, skeletal muscle atrophy is accompanied by the accumulation of saturated fatty acids [11], and a n3-polyunsaturated fatty acid diet can improve skeletal muscle atrophy in 5/6 nephrectomized (Nx) rats [12]. Studies have shown that PA can induce muscular atrophy in C2C12 cells by affecting the growth of new muscle fibres, inhibiting myoprotein synthesis, increasing myoprotein degradation, and other mechanisms [13–16]. However, its mechanism underlying muscular atrophy is still not fully understood.

Pyroptosis is a type of cell death that involves the activation of inflammasomes, the caspase family, the gasdermin family, and other regulatory molecules [17]. An increasing number of studies have shown that pyroptosis is involved in a variety of diseases, including infectious disease, cardiovascular disease, and skeletal muscle disease [18–22]. Previous research has revealed that PA can be associated with the progression of several diseases by inducing NLRP3 inflammasome activation and pyroptosis in HepG2 cells, cardiomyocytes, and vascular endothelial cells [23–26]. Recent studies have confirmed that fatty acid oxidation (FAO) is related to NLRP3 inflammasome activation, and inhibition of FAO can inhibit NLRP3 inflammasome activation, downstream caspase-1 activation and IL-1β and IL-18 release; improve the survival rate of Streptococcus pneumoniae-infected mice and delay the formation of atherosclerotic plaques in mice [27–29]. However, whether pyroptosis is the pathogenesis of PA-induced skeletal muscle atrophy and the related mechanism has never been reported.

Irisin, as a novel myokine, is secreted by muscle and increases with exercise and has some beneficial effects on regulating physical activity [30]. Studies have shown that irisin is closely related to lipid metabolism in myocardial infarction, heart failure, and cardiac hypertrophy diseases [31, 32]. Our previous study found that low serum irisin levels in CKD patients were an independent risk factor for PEW [33]. Lee et al. [34] demonstrated that irisin was an independent risk predictor of skeletal muscle atrophy in dialysis patients. These results suggest that irisin may be associated with the occurrence of skeletal muscle atrophy in CKD. No prior study has reported on the role of irisin in CKD-associated skeletal muscle atrophy.

Materials and Methods

Cell Culture and Differentiation

C2C12 cells were purchased from the American Type Culture Collection. Cells were cultured in DMEM containing 10% FBS and 1% penicillin/streptomycin in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2 at 37°C. For differentiation, cells differentiated into myotubes in the presence of 2% horse serum for 4–5 days. Myotubes were then exposed to different concentrations of PA, ranging from 0.1 to 0.5 mm, for 24 h [14, 35]. To show the role of irisin in myotubes, irisin (100 ng/mL) was used to pretreat myotubes for 48 h. In addition, the caspase-1 inhibitor VX-765 (50 μm), NLRP3 inhibitor MCC950 (50 μm), and etomoxir inhibitor CPT1A (50 μm) were obtained from MedChemExpress, and myotubes were treated according to the experimental design scheme.

Immunofluorescence and Quantification of Myotubes

After the designated treatment, myotubes were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, permeabilized in 0.2% Triton X-100, blocked in 5% bovine serum albumin, and then incubated with the primary antibody against MHC (1:50; Santa Cruz; sc-376157) at 4°C overnight. Myotubes were then rinsed with PBS and incubated with secondary antibody (1:1,000; Proteintech, SA00013-1) in the dark for 1 h at RT. Immunostained myotubes reacting against MHC were captured by a microscope. At least 3 diameters were measured per myotube, and at least 20 myotubes were measured per well using Image-Pro Plus software [36].

Western Blotting

Protein from skeletal muscle tissues and cultured C2C12 cells was extracted with RIPA buffer, and proteins were quantified. Equal amounts of total protein were separated on 10% or 15% SDS-polyacrylamide gels and blotted onto PVDF membranes. The membranes were blocked with 5% nonfat milk for 1 h at room temperature, and then incubated with the following primary antibodies overnight at 4°C: NLRP3 (1:1,000, CST, 15101), cleaved caspase-1 (1:1,000, CST, 89332), cleaved gasdermin D (1:1,000, CST, 10137), CPT1A (1:1,000, Abcam, ab234111), β-actin (1:1,000, Abcam, ab8227), and GAPDH (1:1,000, CST, 97166). After washing with PBS, the membranes were incubated with horseradish peroxidase-labelled rabbit (1:2,000, Abcam, ab205718) or mouse (1:2,000, Abcam, ab6789) secondary antibodies for 2 h at RT. The membranes were visualized using an enhanced chemiluminescence system. Semiquantitative greyscale intensity was measured by ImageJ.

Hoechst 33342/PI Double Stain

PI/Hoechst 33342 staining was performed according to the manufacturer’s protocol. C2C12 cells were inoculated in 24-well plates. After the designated intervention, the cells were incubated with Hoechst 33342 and PI at RT and photographed under a fluorescence microscope.

ELISA Detection

C2C12 cell culture supernatants and mouse serum levels of IL-1β were assayed using an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kit (Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

MRNA Sequencing Assay

RNA Extraction and Library Construction

After the intervention of C2C12 cells according to the experimental protocol, total RNA was extracted with TRIzol according to the instructions. The purity of the sample RNA was first identified and quantified (NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer, Thermo Scientific, USA), and then the integrity of the sample RNA was assessed (Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer, Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA). The transcriptome library was then constructed according to the instructions of the VAHTS Universal V6 RNA-seq Library Prep Kit. Transcriptome sequencing and analysis were performed by OE Biotech Co. Ltd. (Shanghai, China).

RNA Sequencing and Differentially Expressed Gene Analysis

The above and constructed transcriptome libraries were sequenced (Illumina, NovaSeq 6000 sequencing platform), and 150 bp of double-ended RE was generated for each experimental sample to obtain approximately 10 raw reads. Then, raw reads in FASTQ format (FASTP software) were processed. Clean reads were obtained by removing low-quality reads for data analysis. We aligned related genomes (HISAT2 software), calculated gene expression (FPKM), and obtained the read count of each gene by HTSEq-count. R (V 3.2.0) was used for PCA and mapping of genes to assess the biological repeatability of samples. Subsequently, differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were analysed for the experimental samples (DESeq2 software), and those with p < 0.05, fold change >1.5 or fold change <0.67 were defined as DEGs. To present the expression patterns of genes in different groups and samples, we used R (v 3.2.0) to perform hierarchical clustering analysis on DEGs. In addition, to show the expression changes of up- or downregulated genes, the R packet ggradar was used to draw radar maps for the top 30 genes. To screen significantly enriched functional groups, a hypergeometric distribution algorithm was used to analyse GO, KEGG pathway, Reactome, and WikiPathways enrichment of DEGs. R (v 3.2.0) was used to draw bar graphs, chord graphs, or enrichment analysis circle graphs. Then, gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA software) was performed. Using predefined gene sets, the genes are ranked according to the degree of differential expression in the two types of samples, and the prespecified gene sets are tested to determine whether they are enriched at the top or bottom of the ranking table.

RT-qPCR

Total RNA was extracted from c2c12 cells using RNA extract solution, which was then reverse-transcribed into a complementary DNA by the reverse transcription system kit. The expression levels were detected by SYBR to master the mixing. The mRNA expression levels were normalized to GAPDH as an internal control measure. The designed primers are listed in online supplementary Table 1 (for all online suppl. material, see https://doi.org/10.1159/000533926).

Animals and Treatment

C57BL/6J mice (8 weeks) were obtained from the Model Animal Research Center of Nanjing University (Nanjing, China). The mice were maintained under a 12-h light-dark cycle with free access to food and water. The mice were randomly divided into three groups. Control mice were fed a standard mouse chow diet, while wild-type CKD model mice were fed a chow diet containing 0.2% adenine (Xie Tong Sheng Wu, China) for 4 weeks, according to previous publication [37]. CKD mice were then fed a 45% high-fat diet (Xie Tong Sheng Wu, China). In addition, the irisin treatment group was administered Irisin (0.1 μg/g, i.p. injection, Phoenix Pharmaceuticals, USA) three times a week. After 8 weeks, the mice were euthanized to obtain skeletal muscle and serum samples. Colourimetry was used to detect the changes of serum free fatty acid levels (Edison, China). Detection of serum concentration of irisin by ELISA (Elabscience, China). All animal experiments were performed in accordance with standard animal care protocols. This study was approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Xuanwu Hospital, Capital Medical University Animal Experiments Ethics Committee.

Haematoxylin and Eosin Staining

The measurement of cross-sectional area samples was performed as previously described [38]. Sections were stained with haematoxylin and eosin to evaluate myofibrillar loss. Deparaffinized, rehydrated sections were stained with haematoxylin and then differentiated in acid alcohol followed by bluing and counterstaining in eosin solution and the final dehydration. Images were recorded using a microscope. ImageJ software was used to quantify myofibrillar size at ×200 magnification in captured pictures.

Immunohistochemistry Staining

Sections were deparaffinized followed by rehydration and then incubated in 3% hydrogen peroxide to suppress endogenous peroxidase activity. Sections were incubated overnight with primary antibodies at 4°C. The following day, slices were washed 3 times in PBS for 5 min and incubated with secondary antibody for 30 min at 37°C. Then, 400× high-power microscope fields were randomly selected to calculate the integral optical density of positive staining. Antibodies against NLRP3 (bs-10021R; Bioss, Beijing, China), Caspase-1 (22915-1-AP; Proteintech, Wuhan, China), and CPT1A (ab234111; Abcam, Cambridge, UK) were used.

Statistical Analysis

All values are expressed as the means ± SDs. The comparisons between two groups were determined by Student’s t test. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by the Bonferroni test was performed for comparisons among multiple groups. The differences among groups were considered statistically significant at p < 0.05.

Results

PA-Induced C2C12 Cell Myotube Atrophy and Pyroptosis via the Caspase-1 Pathway

C2C12 cells were cultured in vitro and differentiated into myotubes. The myotubes were treated with different concentrations of PA for 24 h. The diameter of myotubes decreased, as shown by immunofluorescence staining with a monoclonal antibody against myosin (shown in Fig. 1a, b). In the time of myotube atrophy in C2C12 cells, compared with controls, the pyroptosis-related protein (cleaved caspase-1, GSDMD-N) levels were significantly increased in C2C12 cells treated with 0.5 mm PA (shown in Fig. 1c–e). Moreover, the IL-1β level (shown in Fig. 1f) and PI-positive cells (shown in Fig. 1g, h) were significantly increased in C2C12 cells treated with 0.5 mm PA. These results indicated that pyroptosis was associated with myotube atrophy in C2C12 cells.

Subsequently, in C2C12 cells treated with 0.5 mm PA, the caspase-1 inhibitor VX-765 significantly reduced the expression levels of the pyroptosis-related proteins cleaved caspase-1 and GSDMD-N (shown in Fig. 2a–c). In addition, VX-765 intervention inhibited IL-1β release (shown in Fig. 2d) and decreased the percentage of PI-positive cells (shown in Fig. 2e, f). Importantly, VX-765 effectively improved C2C12 cell myotube atrophy (shown in Fig. 2g, h). These results indicate that caspase-1-mediated pyroptosis was activated in PA-induced myotube atrophy.

Activation of the NLRP3 Inflammasome in C2C12 Cells Is Involved in PA-Induced Pyroptosis

Activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome is an important mechanism for pyroptosis. Massive evidence has indicated that PA can activate the NLRP3 inflammasome. The results of this study showed that NLRP3 protein expression was significantly upregulated by PA (shown in Fig. 3a, b). Subsequently, we inhibited the expression of NLRP3 using MCC950, an NLRP3-specific inhibitor. Intervention with MCC950 significantly reduced the protein expression levels of NLRP3, cleaved caspase-1, and GSDMD-N in C2C12 cells (shown in Fig. 3c–f), as well as IL-1β release (shown in Fig. 3g). Similarly, MCC950 also significantly reduced the percentage of PI-positive cells (shown in Fig. 3h, i). The results demonstrated that NLRP3 inflammasome activation is involved in PA-induced pyroptosis in C2C12 cells.

Irisin Mitigates PA-Induced Pyroptosis and Myotube Atrophy in C2C12 Cells

We further investigated the effect of irisin on myotube atrophy in C2C12 cells. Irisin at a concentration of 100 ng/mL was added to the C2C12 myotubes treated with PA. Irisin significantly inhibited PA-induced changes in the protein expression levels of pyroptosis-related markers (shown in Fig. 4a–d), IL-1β release (shown in Fig. 4e), and PI-positive cells (shown in Fig. 4f, g). Moreover, irisin improved PA-induced C2C12 cell myotube atrophy (shown in Fig. 4h, i). These results were consistent with those obtained when MCC950 was added to the medium of PA-treated C2C12 cells. These results suggested that irisin can protect against PA-induced myotube atrophy by inhibiting pyroptosis in C2C12 cells.

Irisin Inhibited PA-Induced Pyroptosis and Myotube Atrophy by Regulating the FAO Pathway in C2C12 Cells

To further explore the mechanism of the irisin-induced anti-pyroptosis effect on skeletal muscle atrophy, we investigated the gene changes in C2C12 cells after irisin treatment + PA and PA treatment alone using RNA sequencing and conducted a comparative study of these results among the groups. Compared with the control group, lipid metabolism genes in the PA intervention group were significantly upregulated (shown in online suppl. Fig. S1a), while irisin intervention could significantly downregulate lipid metabolism genes (shown in online suppl. Fig. S2a). We further verified the changes of FAO-related genes (CPT1A, ACOT1, ACOX2, and CD36) by RT-qPCR. Compared with the control group, CPT1A, ACOT1, and ACOX2 genes were significantly upregulated in the PA intervention group, while significantly downregulated after irisin intervention. There was no significant change in CD36 (shown in online suppl. Fig. S3a-d).

CPT1A is a key rate-limiting enzyme in FAO. Etomoxir is an inhibitor of CPT1A that inhibits FAO by inhibiting CPT1A. Etomoxir was used to inhibit FAO in PA-treated C2C12 cells. Compared with the control group, the expression of CPT1A protein in the PA treatment group was significantly increased (shown in Fig. 5a, c). Moreover, irisin treatment significantly reduced the expression levels of pyroptosis-related proteins (NLRP3, cleaved caspase-1, and GSDMD-N) (shown in Fig. 5a, b, d, e), the release of IL-1β (shown in Fig. 5f) and the percentage of PI-positive cells (shown in Fig. 5g, h), and improved myotube atrophy in PA-treated C2C12 cells (shown in Fig. 5i, j). Importantly, after irisin treatment, the expression of CPT1A protein (shown in Fig. 5a, c) was significantly alleviated. These results were consistent with those obtained when etomoxir was added to the medium of PA-treated C2C12 cells. Therefore, these data suggest that irisin inhibited PA-induced pyroptosis and atrophy in C2C12 myotubes by regulating the FAO pathway.

Irisin Improves Skeletal Muscle Atrophy in CKD Mice

To determine the protective effect of irisin against skeletal muscle atrophy, we established a model of adenine-induced CKD in mice. After 4 weeks on an adenine diet, CKD mice developed severe renal failure, as confirmed by increased blood urea nitrogen and creatinine levels (shown in Fig. 6b, c). Next, CKD mice were fed a 45% high-fat diet (shown in Fig. 6a). We found that irisin treatment effectively improved body weight, muscle weight (shown in Fig. 6d, e), and skeletal muscle atrophy (cross-section area) (shown in Fig. 6f, g) in CKD mice. We found that serum free fatty acid level in CKD group was significantly higher than that in control group, while there was no significant difference between the irisin treatment group and CKD group (shown in online suppl. Fig. S4a). In addition, we found that the serum level of irisin in CKD group was lower than that in control group, while irisin treatment significantly increased the level of serum irisin (shown in online suppl. Fig. S4b). Compared with the CKD group, the Western blot (shown in Fig. 6h–l) and immunohistochemical staining (shown in Fig. 6n–q) results both indicated that irisin treatment could reduce the expression of CPT1A protein and pyroptosis-related proteins. In addition, irisin-treated CKD mice had significantly decreased serum IL-1β levels (shown in Fig. 6m). These data suggest that irisin can inhibit FAO and pyroptosis in the skeletal muscle of CKD mice. More importantly, irisin intervention ameliorated skeletal muscle atrophy in CKD mice.

Discussion

Our study is the first to verify that pyroptosis is involved in the pathogenesis of skeletal muscle atrophy, and irisin improves skeletal muscle atrophy by inhibiting FAO and pyroptosis in CKD. In terms of mechanism, PA causes skeletal muscle atrophy by upregulating the expression of CPT1A, a key rate-limiting enzyme of FAO, and pyroptosis-related proteins (NLRP3, cleaved caspase-1, and GSDMD-N), while irisin inhibits FAO and pyroptosis to improve skeletal muscle atrophy (shown in Fig. 7).

Schematic of the mechanism by which irisin attenuates PA-induced skeletal muscle atrophy. Irisin has a protective effect on palmitic acid (PA), which induces skeletal muscle atrophy by regulating fatty acid oxidation (FAO) and inhibiting NLRP3/caspase-1-mediated pyroptosis in chronic kidney disease (CKD). Purple arrows represent the role of CKD-high PA; blue arrows represent the role of irisin.

Skeletal muscle atrophy is a common complication of CKD, and its pathogenesis is very complex. In recent years, more and more attention has been paid to the role of lipid toxicity in CKD skeletal muscle atrophy. Therefore, it is of great significance to further explore the new regulatory mechanism of lipid toxicity in CKD skeletal muscle atrophy.

Pyroptosis is a form of cell death that can lead to severe inflammatory reactions and mediate tissue cell damage. The caspase-1-dependent canonical pyroptosis pathway is the most widely studied pyroptosis signalling pathway and plays an important role in skeletal muscle disease [20–22]. Previous studies found that caspase-1 levels were significantly upregulated in the tissue of skeletal muscle in the 5/6 nephrectomized rat model [39]. However, whether caspase-1-dependent canonical pyroptosis is the mechanism underlying PA-induced myotube atrophy in C2C12 cells has never been reported.

In this study, the myotubes of C2C12 cells were first treated with PA for 24 h, and the diameter of myotubes was significantly reduced in the PA treatment group by immunofluorescence staining of myotubes with an antiheavy chain protein antibody and myotube diameter measurement. Subsequently, we observed the occurrence of pyroptosis during myotube atrophy in C2C12 cells. Western blot results showed that the expression levels of cleaved caspase-1 and GSDMD-N in C2C12 cells increased gradually with increasing concentrations of PA treatment. Meanwhile, the rate of PI-positive cells was significantly increased, and the secretion of IL-1β gradually increased. Importantly, we verified that the caspase-1 inhibitor VX-765 effectively inhibited pyroptosis and improved C2C12 cell myotube atrophy. These results indicate that caspase-1-mediated pyroptosis was activated in PA-induced myotube atrophy.

Pyroptosis is dependent on inflammasome activation [40]. Studies have found that NLRP3 knockout mice can improve angiotensin II-induced skeletal muscle disease [41]. These results suggest that NLRP3 activation is closely related to skeletal muscle disease. Moreover, previous studies have suggested that PA can participate in the occurrence and development of various diseases by inducing the activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome, suggesting that the activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome is an important mechanism of PA-induced diseases [42–45]. Based on these studies, we hypothesized that myotube atrophy induced by PA in C2C12 cells was associated with NLRP3 inflammasome activation. Furthermore, we found NLRP3 inflammasome activation in the process of myotube atrophy induced by PA in C2C12 cells. More importantly, the expression of pyroptosis-related proteins was significantly reduced after intervention with the NLRP3 inhibitor MCC950. The secretion of IL-1β and the positive rate of PI were also significantly decreased. Taken together, these data suggest that NLRP3 activation is required for PA-induced C2C12 cell pyroptosis and that NLRP3 may potentially be a therapeutic target for the treatment of skeletal muscle atrophy.

Irisin, as a novel myokine, is secreted by muscle and increases with exercise and has some beneficial effects in skeletal muscle diseases. Clinical studies suggest that irisin can be used as a biomarker to predict muscle atrophy in chronic heart failure and chronic obstructive pulmonary diseases [46, 47]. Alsaawi et al. [48] found that serum irisin levels were significantly reduced in elderly female patients with skeletal muscle atrophy. Colaianni et al. [49] found that serum irisin levels were significantly reduced in patients with Charcot-Marie-tooth hereditary polyneuropathy and muscular atrophy. Reza et al. [50] showed that irisin intervention could rescue the atrophy of denervated skeletal muscle in mice. Colaianni et al. [51]found that irisin intervention could prevent and restore muscle atrophy in hindlimb suspension mice. Research studies found that serum irisin levels were significantly reduced in CKD patients with skeletal muscle atrophy [33, 52]. Our in vivo and in vitro results suggest that irisin can ameliorate skeletal muscle atrophy. An increasing number of studies have shown that irisin can alleviate the progression of various diseases by inhibiting the occurrence of pyroptosis. Pang et al. [18] found that irisin could improve vascular calcification by inhibiting pyroptosis. Behera et al. [53] found that irisin inhibits pyroptosis and improves diabetes-related osteoporosis. Xiong et al. [54] found that irisin alleviated sepsis-induced cardiac dysfunction by reducing inflammation-induced pyroptosis. Yue et al. [55] found that irisin reduced the new hypertrophy caused by pressure overload by inhibiting myocardial pyroptosis. Based on these studies, we hypothesized that irisin ameliorated PA-induced myotube atrophy in C2C12 cells in the PA environment and was related to the regulation of NLRP3/caspase-1-dependent pyroptosis. Further studies showed that irisin treatment could decrease the occurrence of NLRP3/caspase-1-dependent pyroptosis in C2C12 cells induced by PA, which was consistent with MCC950 treatment. These results suggested that irisin can protect against PA-induced myotube atrophy by inhibiting NLRP3 inflammasome activation and downstream caspase-1 activation-induced pyroptosis in C2C12 cells.

Recent studies have found that FAO is closely related to the activation of NLRP3 inflammasome. Moreover, studies have found that FAO is associated with skeletal muscle disease in mouse C2C12 myoblasts exposed to elevated CO2 levels and mice lacking the superoxide anion scavenger CuZn superoxide dismutase (Sod1−/− mice) [56, 57]. Zhang et al. [58] found that trimetazidine, a partial inhibitor of lipid oxidation, improved high-fat diet-induced mouse skeletal muscle atrophy. In the present study, the protein expression of CPT1A, a key rate-limiting enzyme for FAO in C2C12 cells, was significantly increased after treatment with PA, indicating enhanced FAO. However, irisin could significantly inhibit the expression of CPT1A protein after intervention, suggesting that irisin can regulate excessive FAO in C2C12 cells with myotube atrophy. There is increasing evidence that inhibition of FAO inhibits NLRP3 inflammasome activation, downstream caspase-1 activation, and IL-1β and IL-18 release [27–29]. Subsequently, our study found that the expression of NLRP3/caspase-1-dependent pyroptosis-related proteins was significantly decreased after the application of etomoxir, a inhibitor, to inhibit the expression of CPT1A, suggesting that inhibition of FAO could inhibit pyroptosis. Therefore, these data suggest that irisin inhibited PA-induced pyroptosis and atrophy in C2C12 myotubes by regulating the FAO pathway.

Another interesting finding of our study is that irisin intervention could significantly inhibit the expression of key enzymes (CPT1A protein) and pyroptosis-related proteins in the skeletal muscle of CKD mice. More importantly, irisin significantly improved skeletal muscle atrophy in CKD mice. On the other hand, we demonstrated that irisin treatment could improve renal function in CKD mice in comparison to wild-type CKD mice. We believe that irisin can significantly improve skeletal muscle atrophy in CKD mice, partly because irisin protects renal function.

Some limitations of our study. Firstly, the improvement of skeletal muscle atrophy in CKD mice by irisin may be partly related to the improvement of renal function. In the next step, we will further observe the effect of irisin treatment in CKD mice by overexpression of pyroptosis-related proteins. Secondly, our study mainly observed the effect of irisin on FAO, and the signalling pathway of irisin inhibiting FAO require further exploration according to the RNA sequencing results.

Conclusions

In summary, our data reveal a novel mechanism of skeletal muscle atrophy in CKD. PA induces skeletal muscle atrophy by upregulating CPT1A, a key rate-limiting enzyme of FAO, and activating pyroptosis mediated by the NLRP3/caspase-1 pathway. However, irisin significantly improve skeletal muscle atrophy by downregulating CPT1A and inhibiting pyroptosis in CKD. Therefore, irisin may be developed as a potential therapeutic agent for the treatment of muscle wasting in CKD patients.

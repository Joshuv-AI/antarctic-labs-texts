Assessment of the feed additive consisting of Lactiplantibacillus plantarum LMG P‐21295 for all animal species for the renewal of its authorisation (Chr. Hansen A/S)
Authors: EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP), Vasileios Bampidis, Giovanna Azimonti, Maria de Lourdes Bastos, Henrik Christensen, Birgit Dusemund, Mojca Fašmon Durjava, Maryline Kouba, Marta López‐Alonso, Secundino López Puente, Francesca Marcon, Baltasar Mayo, Alena Pechová, Mariana Petkova, Fernando Ramos, Roberto Edoardo Villa, Ruud Woutersen, Natalia Alija‐Novo, Montserrat Anguita, Rosella Brozzi, Yolanda García‐Cazorla
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractFollowing a request from the European Commission, EFSA was asked to deliver a scientific opinion on the assessment of the application for renewal of Lactiplantibacillus plantarum LMG P‐21295, a technological additive to improve ensiling of fresh material for all animal species. The applicant has provided evidence that the additive currently on the market complies with the existing conditions of authorisation. There is no new evidence that would lead the FEEDAP Panel to reconsider its previous conclusions. Thus, the Panel concludes that the additive remains safe for all animal species, consumers and the environment under the authorised conditions of use. The Panel is not in the position to conclude on the skin and eye irritation potential of the additive. No conclusions can be drawn on the potential of the additive to cause skin sensitisation, but it is considered to be a respiratory sensitiser. There is no need for assessing the efficacy of the additive in the context of the renewal of the authorisation.Keywords: technological additive, silage, Lactiplantibacillus plantarum LMG P‐21295, safety, QPS, renewal

Introduction

Background and terms of reference

Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003 1 establishes the rules governing the Community authorisation of additives for use in animal nutrition. In particular, Article 14(1) of that Regulation lays down that an application for renewal shall be sent to the Commission at the latest 1 year before the expiry date of the authorisation.

The European Commission received a request from Chr. Hansen A/S 2 for the renewal of the authorisation of the additive consisting of Lactiplantibacillus plantarum LMG P‐21295, when used as a feed additive for all animal species (category: technological additives; functional group: silage additives).

According to Article 7(1) of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, the Commission forwarded the application to the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) as an application under Article 14(1) (renewal of the authorisation). The dossier was received on 06 December 2021 and the general information and supporting documentation are available at https://open.efsa.europa.eu/questions/EFSA-Q-2021-00738. The particulars and documents in support of the application were considered valid by EFSA as of 09 August 2022.

According to Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, EFSA, after verifying the particulars and documents submitted by the applicant, shall undertake an assessment in order to determine whether the feed additive complies with the conditions laid down in Article 5. EFSA shall deliver an opinion on the safety for the target animals, consumer, user and the environment and on the efficacy of the feed additive consisting of Lactiplantibacillus plantarum LMG P‐21295, when used under the proposed conditions of use (see Section 3.1.3 ).

Additional information

The additive Lactiplantibacillus plantarum LMG P‐21295 3 is currently authorised as a feed additive for all animal species in the European Union (1 k20721). 4

EFSA issued one opinion on the safety and efficacy of this product when used in fresh material for all animal species (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2012).

Data and methodologies

Data

The present assessment is based on data submitted by the applicant in the form of a technical dossier 5 in support of the authorisation request for the use of Lactiplantibacillus plantarum LMG P‐21295 as a feed additive.

The confidential version of the technical dossier was subject to a target consultation of the interested Member States from 24 August to 24 November 2022; the comments received were considered for the assessment.

In accordance with Article 38 of the Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 6 and taking into account the protection of confidential information and of personal data in accordance with Articles 39 to 39e of the same Regulation, and of the Decision of EFSA's Executive Director laying down practical arrangements concerning transparency and confidentiality, 7 a non‐confidential version of the dossier has been published on Open.EFSA. 8

According to Article 32c(2) of Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 and to the Decision of EFSA's Executive Director laying down the practical arrangements on pre‐submission phase and public consultations,7 EFSA carried out a public consultation on the non‐confidential version of the application from 03 March to 24 March 2023 for which no comments were received.

The European Union Reference Laboratory (EURL) considered that the conclusions and recommendations reached in the previous assessment are valid and applicable for the current application. 9

Methodologies

The approach followed by the FEEDAP Panel to assess the safety of Lactiplantibacillus plantarum LMG P‐21295 is in line with the principles laid down in Regulation (EC) No 429/2008 10 and the relevant guidance documents: Guidance on the renewal of the authorisation of feed additives (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2021) and EFSA statement on the requirements for whole genome sequence analysis of microorganisms intentionally used in the food chain (EFSA, 2021).

Assessment

The product consisting of viable cells of Lactiplantibacillus plantarum LMG P‐21295 is authorised as a technological additive (functional group: silage additives) for use in fresh material for all animal species. This assessment regards the renewal of the authorisation of Lactiplantibacillus plantarum LMG P‐21295 for the above‐mentioned species.

Characterisation

Characterisation of the additive

The additive is currently authorised with a minimum content of the active agent L. plantarum LMG P‐21295 of 5 × 1010 colony forming units (CFU)/g of additive. It is a dry powder with 8% silica 11 (E551b or E554) as anti‐caking agent and maltodextrin as carrier. The applicant stated that the manufacturing process has not been modified since the first authorisation was granted and declared that no antimicrobials are used during the product manufacturing. 12

The analysis of nine samples (obtained from five independent fermentation batches) 13 showed compliance with the minimum specifications (mean: 2.58 × 1011 CFU/g additive, range 1.1–4.5 × 1011 CFU/g additive). 14

The analysis of the same samples showed compliance also with the specifications set for coliforms (< 103 CFU/g), Escherichia coli (< 10 CFU/g), yeasts and filamentous fungi (< 103CFU/g) and Salmonella spp. (no detection in 25 g). 14 Additionally, three independent batches showed compliance with the specifications set for Enterobacteriaceae (< 103 CFU/g). 15

Five samples (obtained from three independent fermentation batches) were also tested for aflatoxin B1, arsenic, mercury, lead and cadmium. All the samples showed levels below the limit of quantification (LOQ) of the analytical method for Aflatoxin B1, mercury ranged 0.0013–0.0083 mg/kg, arsenic ranged 0.011–0.021 mg/kg, lead and cadmium showed levels below the respective LOQ of the analytical methods with the exception of one batch (lead 0.414 mg/kg and cadmium 0.019 mg/kg). 16 , 17

The FEEDAP Panel considers that the microbial contamination and the amounts of the detected impurities do not raise safety concerns.

No new data were provided regarding the physico‐chemical properties of the additive. Considering that no changes have been introduced in the manufacturing process and composition, the data described in the previous opinion (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2012) still apply.

Characterisation of the active agent

The strain was originally isolated from silage. It is deposited in the Belgian Co‐ordinated Collections of Micro‐organisms (BCCM/LMG) under the accession number LMG P‐21295. 18 It has not been genetically modified.

The strain LMG P‐21295 was identified at the species level as Lactiplantibacillus plantarum by bioinformatic analysis of the whole genome sequence (WGS). The taxonomical identification was confirmed by average nucleotide identity (ANI). The results of this analysis showed an ANI value of 99.88% with the type strain L. plantarum DSM 20174T. 19

The susceptibility of the strain to the antimicrobials recommended by the FEEDAP Panel (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2018) was tested by broth microdilution method following the ISO standard. All the minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) values were equal or fell below the cut‐off values established in the FEEDAP Guidance, except for kanamycin which was exceeded by one dilution step (128 mg/L vs. 64 mg/L). A MIC value of one dilution higher than the cut‐off is within the normal range variation of the method. Therefore, the strain is considered to be susceptible to all relevant antibiotics. 20

The WGS of the strain was interrogated for the presence of antimicrobial resistance genes (AMR) against the ResFinder and NCBI Bacterial Antimicrobial Resistance Reference Gene Database Antibiotic Resistance. The search in ResFinder was performed at nucleotide level while the search in NCBI was performed at protein level. In both searches, the thresholds were set ■■■■■ No hits of concern were identified. 21

Conditions of use

The additive is currently authorised for use in fresh material for all animal species. Under other provisions of the authorisation, it is specified that:

In the directions for use of the additive and premixture, indicate the storage temperature and storage life.

In the directions for use of the additive and premixture, indicate the storage temperature and storage life.

Minimum dose of the additive when used without combination with other micro‐organisms as silage additives: 1 × 108 CFU/kg fresh material.

Minimum dose of the additive when used without combination with other micro‐organisms as silage additives: 1 × 108 CFU/kg fresh material.

For safety: It is recommended to use breathing protection and gloves during handling.

For safety: It is recommended to use breathing protection and gloves during handling.

The applicant has requested to maintain the same conditions of use. 22

Safety

In the previous assessment (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2012), the Panel concluded that, following the qualified presumption of safety (QPS) approach to safety assessment, the use of this strain in the production of silage was considered safe for target species, consumers and the environment. Regarding user safety, the Panel concluded: ‘No data are available on skin or eye irritation… However, the generic material safety data sheet proposed… indicate that preparations containing the strains may cause irritation on prolonged contact with skin and eyes. The dusting potential of commercial formulations tested was high. This, coupled with the significant fraction of these products that is potentially inhalable, means that exposure via a respiratory route is a significant possibility and hazard… given the proteinaceous nature of the active agent, its potential to be a skin/respiratory sensitiser should be seriously considered.’

The applicant declares that no adverse effects for target animals, consumers, users and/or the environment have been reported since the approval of the additive. 23

In the context of the current application, the identity of the strain as Lactiplantibacillus plantarum was confirmed, and evidence was provided that the strain does not show acquired antimicrobial determinants for antibiotics of human and veterinary importance (EFSA, 2007; EFSA BIOHAZ Panel, 2023). Consequently, the conclusions already reached are still valid and, following the qualified presumption of safety (QPS) approach to safety assessment, Lactiplantibacillus plantarum LMG P‐21295 is considered safe for the target species, consumers and the environment.

The applicant performed a literature search to provide evidence that in the light of the current knowledge, the additive is safe for the users. The search was performed in September 2021 and the terms used included the active agent at species level, eye irritation, skin irritation and skin sensitisation. The applicant searched a total of four relevant databases: Academic OneFile (from year 2010), Food Science Source (from year 2010), AGRIS (from year 2010) and PubMed (time span not specified). The literature search retrieved five hits. However, none was further considered because they either regarded a different strain (1 hit) or referred to previous EFSA FEEDAP Scientific Opinions for other products/strains (4 hits). 24

No specific studies were submitted with regard to safety for the user. In the absence of any specific study performed on the active agent, the FEEDAP Panel is not in the position to conclude on the skin and eye irritation potential of the additive. Owing to its proteinaceous nature, the additive should be considered a respiratory sensitiser.

The FEEDAP Panel notes that the OECD test guidelines available at present are designed to assess the skin sensitisation potential of chemical substances only and that currently no validated assays for assessing the sensitisation potential of microorganisms are available. 25 Therefore, no conclusions can be drawn on the potential of the additive to cause skin sensitisation.

Conclusions on safety

The FEEDAP Panel concludes that there is no new evidence that would lead it to reconsider the previous conclusions that L. plantarum LMG P‐21295 is safe for the target species, consumers and the environment under the authorised conditions of use. In the absence of any specific study performed on the active agent, the FEEDAP Panel is not in the position to conclude on the skin and eye irritation potential of the additive. No conclusions can be drawn on the potential of the additive to cause skin sensitisation, but it is considered to be a respiratory sensitiser.

Efficacy

The present application for renewal of the authorisation does not include a proposal for amending or supplementing the conditions of the original authorisation that would have an impact on the efficacy of the additive. Therefore, there is no need for assessing the efficacy of the additive in the context of the renewal of the authorisation.

Conclusions

The applicant has provided evidence that the additive currently on the market complies with the existing conditions of authorisation.

The Panel concludes that Lactiplantibacillus plantarum LMG P‐21295 remains safe for all animal species, consumers and the environment under the authorised conditions of use.

The Panel is not in the position to conclude on the skin and eye irritation potential or skin sensitisation potential of the additive. The additive is considered to be a respiratory sensitiser.

There is no need for assessing the efficacy of the additive in the context of the renewal of the authorisation.

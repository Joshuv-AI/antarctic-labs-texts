TM and t-PAIC may be possible biomarkers to predict postoperative infection after cardiac surgery with cardiopulmonary bypass
Authors: Lu Wang, Qian Chen, Wei Wu, Yun Long
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundWhile cardiac surgery and cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) are on rise, postoperative infections have become an increasingly prominent problem. We designed this study to compare the four coagulation indicators involving endothelial injury and identify important predictors of postoperative infection after cardiac surgery with CPB.MethodsThis is a single-center, prospective, observational study of postoperative cardiac surgery patients admitted to the department of critical care medicine, Peking Union Medical College Hospital from July 15, 2024 to August 26, 2024. Patients postoperatively after cardiac surgery with CPB during the study period were included. A total of 40 patients were included finally, comprising 34 in the Control group and 6 in the Infection group. Data on coagulation and inflammatory markers of above patients were collected. Above markers included thrombomodulin (TM), thrombin-antithrombin complex (TAT), plasmin-α2-plasmininhibitor complex (PIC), and tissue plasminogen activator-inhibitor complex (t-PAIC), tumor necrosis factor (TNF)-α, interleukin (IL)-6, IL-8, and IL-10 concentration.ResultsPostoperative infection after cardiac surgery with CPB was 15% (6 / 40) in this study. Background

After cardiac surgery, patients who undergo long-term surgery and trauma from cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) are highly susceptible to postoperative infection, and then enter the sepsis stage due to life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by dysfunctional response to infection [1]. At present, the diagnosis of postoperative infection of cardiac surgery is based on clinical manifestations, elevated inflammatory markers, and etiological testing [2, 3]. However, these criteria have limited sensitivity for early infection and may lead to a delay in the diagnosis of infection. The introduction of new biomarkers related to the potential pathogenesis of postoperative infection may contribute to early diagnosis and better clinical prognosis [4]. With the deepening understanding of the pathophysiology of infection, more and more studies have focused on inflammation and coagulation as two host defense systems [5, 6]. This means that the overwhelming systemic inflammatory response in sepsis caused by infection is accompanied by coagulopathy, and both may contribute to organ function impairment in the early stages of sepsis [6, 7]. Endothelial activation and injury occur early in sepsis with activated coagulation dysfunction and play a major role in the pathophysiology of infection-induced acute organ function injury [8, 9]. The traditional evaluation of coagulation function cannot reflect the whole picture of coagulation, let alone the interaction of coagulation, inflammation and endothelium [10]. The four markers of coagulation, including thrombomodulin (TM), thrombin-antithrombin complex (TAT), plasmin-α2-plasmininhibitor complex (PIC), and tissue plasminogen activator-inhibitor complex (t-PAIC) have received more and more attention [11–13]. When endothelial cells are damaged, they cause abnormal expression, secretion, and release of TM into the bloodstream, so TM can be used as a marker of endothelial damage. TAT acts as a molecular marker of thrombin generation and directly confirms the activation of the coagulation system. PIC is a direct reflection of plasmin production. t-PAIC level was positively correlated with t-PA concentration and degree of vascular endothelial injury, indicating endothelial injury and fibrinolytic activation. There is insufficient research on whether the above four markers can predict postoperative cardiac infection, so we designed this study to compare the four coagulation indexes involving endothelial injury and identify important predictors of postoperative infection after cardiac surgery with CPB.

Methods

Study design

This is a single-center, prospective, observational study of postoperative cardiac surgery patients admitted to the department of critical care medicine, Peking Union Medical College Hospital from July 15, 2024 to August 26, 2024. The study was registered in the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (registration number: ChiCTR2600118480, data of registration: 2026/2/6, retrospectively registered, URL: http://www.chictr.org.cn/). Clinical decisions are at the discretion of the intensive care unit (ICU) attending physician. Inclusion criteria: postoperative patients who underwent cardiac surgery with CPB during the study period. Exclusion criteria: patients with primary infectious endocarditis, patients who did not use CPB, pregnant patients, patients under 18 years, and patients with a hospital stay of less than 24 h. When admission to the ICU, informed consent included biomarker testing and the use of clinical data was obtained. A total of 40 patients were included in this study, comprising 34 in the Control group and 6 in the Infection group. The diagnosis of postoperative infection was based on the European Perioperative Clinical Outcome definition [14]. Infection was defined as one where there is strong clinical suspicion of infection, meeting two or more of the following criteria: core temperature < 36 ℃ or > 38 ℃; white cell count > 12 × 10^9 or < 4 × 10^9, respiratory rate > 20 breaths per minute or PaCO2 < 35 mmHg; pulse rate > 90 beats per minute. Pneumonia was defined as follows: two or more serial chest radiographs with at least one of the following (1. new or progressive and persistent infiltrates; 2. consolidation; 3. cavitation); at least one of the following (1. fever > 38 ℃ with no other recognized cause; 2. white cell count > 12 × 10^9 or < 4 × 10^9; 3. altered mental status with no other recognized cause for adults > 70 years old); and at least two of the following (1. new onset of purulent sputum or change in character of sputum, or increased respiratory secretions, or increased suctioning requirements; 2. new onset or worsening cough, or dyspnoea, or tachypnoea; 3. rales or bronchial breath sounds; 4. worsening gas exchange). Patient data was collected from electronic medical records, including age, gender, height, weight, primary disease (coronary heart disease, valvular heart disease, others), data of CPB (CPB time, aortic crossclamp time, assisted circulation time), hospital stays, ICU stays, mechanical ventilation hours, activities of daily living (ADL) score at admission, ADL score at discharge.

The authors are accountable for all aspects of the work in ensuring that questions related to the accuracy or integrity of any part of the work are appropriately investigated and resolved. The datasets supporting the conclusions of this article are included within the article.

Variables and measurements

When admission to the ICU, citrate anticoagulant blood was taken and sent to the clinical laboratory department of Peking Union Medical College Hospital for TM, TAT, PIC, t-PAIC, interleukin (IL)-6, IL-8, IL-10, and tumor necrosis factor (TNF)-α concentration detection.

Ethical considerations

The current study was reported in accordance with the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology Guidelines. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki (as revised in 2013). The trial protocol was approved by the Central Institutional Review Board at Peking Union Medical College Hospital (I-24PJ1400). Individual informed consent form signed at the time of admission. There was no identifying or protected health information included in the analyzed dataset.

Data analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS 19.0 software. Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± SE of mean values and compared using the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables are expressed as numbers and percentages and were compared using the Fisher’s exact test. ROC curve was used to determine the sensitivity and specificity of the predictive nature of TM and t-PAIC. A P < 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant.

Yun Long were aware of the group allocation at the different stages of the experiment (during the allocation, the conduct of the experiment, the outcome assessment, and the data analysis).

Results

Basic information

Postoperative infection after cardiac surgery with CPB was 15% (6 / 40) in this study. The Infection group was older than the Control group. The proportion of male patients in the Infection group was lower. There was no difference between the two groups in terms of primary diseases. The Infection group experienced longer CPB time and longer assisted circulation time during surgery. Patients in the Infection group had longer hospital stays and lower ADL scores at discharge (Table 1).

Discussion

Cardiac surgery is on rise as cardiovascular disease becomes the leading cause of death worldwide. In cardiac surgery, the use of CPB to support the patient is common. CPB produces an immediate and systemic inflammatory response that leads to endothelial damage, causing postoperative infection in 0.39% to 2.5% of patients [15, 16]. Postoperative infections are associated with high mortality [17, 18], and require early recognition to avoid fatal consequences [19]. When infection leads to sepsis, endotoxin causes endothelial damage and promotes thrombin activation, which can be manifested as an increase in TM and TAT at this time, and as the infection progresses to septic shock, tissue hypoperfusion leads to organ function damage, at which time t-PAIC can be significantly increased, while PIC is not significantly increased due to fibrinolytic inhibition [20]. The differences in the above four coagulation markers have important potential value in the transformation of patients from traumatic hemorrhagic shock to septic shock after cardiac surgery [13].

The inflammatory response is an early manifestation of postoperative infection and is a triggering factor for infection leading to endothelial damage and coagulation disorders. Procalcitonin (PCT), IL-6, and IL-10 are efficient biomarkers for distinguishing between bloodstream infection and local bacterial infection [21]. IL-6 and IL-10 are closely associated with the severity and treatment efficacy of bloodstream infection [22]. IL-6 and IL-10 are associated with gram-negative and gram-positive bacteria infection in lymphoma [23]. Increased TNF-α production in response to IL-6 in patients with systemic inflammation without infection [24]. However, patients who undergo long cardiac surgery with CPB are highly susceptible to produce a non-infectious systemic inflammatory response. The above phenomenon has greatly interfered with the inflammatory response caused by secondary postoperative infection. Our study did not find an association between inflammatory factors (TNF-α, IL-6, IL-8 and IL-10) and postoperative infection after cardiac surgery with CPB.

Endothelial injury is a key feature of infection leading to sepsis, and endothelial cell activation and damage are closely related to organ dysfunction, and many studies have also demonstrated the prognostic value of endothelial markers. When endothelial cells are diseased or damaged, TM is often elevated. TM levels can be markedly elevated in patients with renal impairment, and this elevation is due to increased TM production [25]. Higher serum levels of TM were associated with more severe disease and the need for renal replacement therapying sepsis [26]. Higher levels of TM are associated with increased mortality in patients with acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) [27–29]. TM is an independent risk factor for septic shock [30, 31]. Elevated levels of TM are significantly associated with disease severity and mortality in pediatric sepsis. Our study shown that TM was higher in patients who developed postoperative infection after cardiac surgery with CPB and may be possible biomarker.

Thrombin production is a key part of thrombosis, and as a complex formed by the 1:1 binding of thrombin and antithrombin III, TAT can be used as a molecular marker of thrombin production, directly confirming the activation of the coagulation system. The serum TAT level in sepsis rats was significantly increased [32]. Serum TAT levels may be an independent risk factor for severe sepsis in children [33]. Increased TAT levels are associated with the onset of AKI after cardiopulmonary bypass in children [34]. However, our study did not find an association between TAT and postoperative infection after cardiac surgery with CPB.

Plasmin is rapidly combined 1:1 with α2 plasmin inhibitors to form PIC, which directly reflects plasmin production. Because D-dimer and FDP are substances formed by the degradation of fibrin (progen) after plasmin activation, the increase of PIC is theoretically earlier than the increase of D-dimer and FDP, and is the most sensitive indicator for monitoring plasmin activity [35]. PIC can be used as a biomarker to predict severe sepsis and correlate with the severity of sepsis in children [33]. However, our study did not find an association between PIC and postoperative infection after cardiac surgery with CPB.

Serum t-PAIC levels may be an independent risk factor for severe sepsis in children [33]. t-PAIC increased in children with SIC [36]. The level of t-PAIC was positively correlated with lactate level and SOFA score in infected patients [37]. t-PAIC may be an independent risk factor for septic shock, and they may correlate with the severity of such shock [38]. tPAPAI-1 C might be a useful predictor of the prognosis in patients with severe COVID-19 [39]. Our study shown that t-PAIC was higher in patients who developed postoperative infection after cardiac surgery with CPB and may contribute to the early prediction of postoperative infections.

There are some limitations to our study. First, this study is a single-center, observational study. Second, patients with postoperative infection were older and had longer cardiopulmonary bypass and assisted circulation times. Whether these above factors affect endothelial injury, trigger postoperative infection, and lead to prolonged hospitalization needs to be further clarified through basic research. Third, we assessed two parameters for endothelial injury, but did not measure others, such as intercellular adhesion molecule (ICAM)-1 or vascular cell adhesion molecule (VCAM)-1. Further research is needed to evaluate the ideal predictive endothelial biomarkers for postoperative infection.

Conclusion

TM and t-PAIC were higher in patients who developed postoperative infection after cardiac surgery with CPB and may be possible biomarkers, but larger studies are needed to confirm their value.

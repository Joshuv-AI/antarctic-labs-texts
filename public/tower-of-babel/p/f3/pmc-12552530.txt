Glycolytic Reprogramming in Oral Squamous Cell Carcinoma: Molecular Regulators and Natural Product-Based Therapies
Authors: Ji-Ae Shin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractOral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC), the most common malignancy of the oral cavity, poses significant clinical challenges owing to late-stage diagnosis, frequent recurrence, and therapy resistance. Metabolic reprogramming, particularly the upregulation of aerobic glycolysis, is intricately associated with tumor progression, metastasis, and therapeutic failure. This metabolic alteration not only favors rapid cancer cell proliferation and promotes immune evasion but also results in the formation of a supportive tumor microenvironment (TME). This review synthesizes the current knowledge on the molecular regulators of glycolytic reprogramming in OSCC and elucidates their underlying mechanisms. We also delineated the glycolytic landscape of the OSCC tumor microenvironment, which encompasses cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), CD4+ and CD8+ T cells, and tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs). Furthermore, we underscore the therapeutic potential of natural product-based strategies that target aerobic glycolysis in OSCC. A deeper understanding of these metabolic pathways may aid in the development of metabolism-targeted interventions for OSCC. This review also offers insights into how targeting glycolytic reprogramming, particularly with natural products, may enhance the efficacy of current chemotherapeutic and immunotherapeutic strategies for OSCC by overcoming resistance and reprogramming the TME.Keywords: Oral squamous cell carcinoma, Aerobic glycolysis, Metabolic reprogramming, Metabolic regulators, Natural products, Cancer metabolism

Introduction

Oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC), which originates from the oral mucosal epithelium, constitutes over 90% of oral cancers. The 5-year overall survival rate for patients with OSCC is 59.3%, with approximately 45.7% experiencing postoperative recurrence or metastasis.1 The pathogenesis of OSCC is intricately regulated by genetic alterations, epigenetic modifications, and a dysregulated tumor microenvironment (TME).2 Despite advancements in diagnostic and therapeutic strategies, the prognosis of OSCC remains poor owing to late-stage diagnosis, frequent recurrence, and resistance to conventional therapies. These challenges underscore the complexity of OSCC and highlight the need for ongoing research to identify novel biomarkers and develop more effective targeted treatments.

Reprogramming of energy metabolism, along with immune evasion, has been recognized as an emerging hallmark of cancer.3 Accumulating evidence indicates that the reprogramming of metabolic pathways related to glycolysis, oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS), amino acid, nucleotide, and lipid biosynthesis, the pentose phosphate pathway (PPP), tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle, β-oxidation, and glutaminolysis, occurs during cancer progression.4 Furthermore, metabolic rewiring in cancer cells promotes therapeutic resistance, often accompanied by immune evasion.5 Consequently, the understanding of the multiple metabolic pathways in cancer cells and the development of corresponding metabolic therapeutics have been extensively studied.6, 7, 8 In recent years, altered cellular metabolism in OSCC has been recognized as a contributing factor to poor prognosis, emphasizing the need to identify metabolic biomarkers for the early diagnosis of OSCC.9 In this context, metabolic analysis of biofluids and tissues from patients with premalignant lesions may aid in identifying biomarkers indicative of malignant transformation to OSCC.10 Additionally, OSCC cells exhibit a specifically enriched metabolic response to cisplatin compared to normal oral epithelial cells, underscoring the importance of metabolic intervention in OSCC treatment.11 Elevated expression of metabolism-related proteins is associated with multi-step carcinogenesis in OSCC.12 This review summarizes the key molecular regulators of glycolysis in OSCC and highlights natural product-based approaches targeting metabolic pathways, offering promising avenues for future OSCC therapy.

Glycolytic reprogramming in OSCC

Aerobic glycolysis in OSCC

In contrast to normal cells, cancer cells characteristically exhibit aerobic glycolysis, wherein glucose is converted to lactate for ATP production under aerobic conditions; this phenomenon is known as the Warburg effect. Although aerobic glycolysis is less efficient than oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS) in generating energy, cancer cells preferentially utilize the glycolytic pathway by upregulating glucose transporters, such as GLUT, and glycolytic enzymes, including hexokinase (HK), phosphofructokinase (PFK), pyruvate kinase (PK), and L-lactate dehydrogenase A (LDHA).6 This preference is attributed to the ability of cancer cells to rapidly generate ATP, produce essential metabolic intermediates for macromolecule synthesis, adapt to hypoxic and acidic tumor microenvironments (TME) through lactate accumulation, and reduce reactive oxygen species (ROS) that are cytotoxic to cancer cells.13

Elevated aerobic glycolysis in cancer cells is coupled with increased lactate production, a phenomenon widely recognized as a hallmark of cancer. Notably, abundant lactate production in OSCC is associated with tumor progression and the tumor microenvironment, suggesting that glycolytic metabolites, including lactate, could be attractive biomarkers for OSCC detection and diagnosis.14 Furthermore, aerobic glycolysis may be closely associated with the function of various immune cells, supporting tumor growth, metastasis, and chemoresistance. Glycolysis-driven metabolic reprogramming of regulatory T cells (Tregs) within the TME of OSCC leads to immune evasion and weakened anti-tumor immunity.15 Elevated glycolysis-related enzyme levels in CD4+ T cells are associated with the progression of lymph node metastasis and recurrence in OSCC.16 In contrast, weakened glycolysis in CD8+ T cells through energy competition with OSCC cells impairs the antitumor immune response via T cell exhaustion.17 Therefore, targeting glycolytic reprogramming represents a promising therapeutic strategy for overcoming metabolic alterations in OSCC.

Molecular regulators of glycolysis in OSCC

Following the foundational concept of the Warburg effect, cancer metabolism has become a central focus of oncology research, with growing efforts to elucidate how altered metabolic pathways support tumor progression and metastasis in cancer cells. Recent studies have identified various molecular regulators that modulate glycolysis and contribute to the malignant progression of OSCC (Table 1). These regulators influence not only glucose metabolism but also key oncogenic processes, such as proliferation, migration, invasion, epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT), and metastasis. PDIA6, for instance, contributed to increased glucose uptake, lactate production, and ATP levels, leading to the induction of growth, migration, invasion, and tumorigenesis and reduction of apoptosis in OSCC cells.18 SUN2 upregulation correlated with poor glycolytic activity via reducing the levels of GLUT1 and LDHA, thereby inhibiting growth of OSCC cells.19 Elevated PER1 has been implicated in reducing aerobic glycolysis-driven proliferation and tumorigenesis in vitro and in vivo models of OSCC by promoting PI3K degradation through complex formation with RACK1/PI3K.20 NEDD4L coordinates the regulation of glycolysis by facilitating ENO1 ubiquitination and degradation, thereby disrupting proliferation and tumorigenesis.21 The long non-coding RNA CYTOR has been linked to enhanced glycolysis via HNRNPC-mediated Zeb1 stabilization, resulting in EMT, invasion, and metastasis of OSCC cells.22 SLC2A3 also contributes to metabolic rewiring by promoting lactic acid production, which activates the TGF-b signaling pathway, thereby facilitating tumorigenesis and metastasis of OSCC cells.23 Another important signaling pathway involving FSCN1 promotes glycolysis by inducing the IRF4-AKT axis, leading to the proliferation, EMT, and invasion of OSCC cells.24 AKR1B10 participated in the proliferation and invasive properties of OSCC cells by promoting the binding with HK2 which facilitates glycolysis.25 Additional studies have highlighted the importance of elevated PRAD in mediating the suppression of glycolysis-induced tumorigenesis and invasive properties,26 while USP14 silencing reduced the tumorigenesis and migration ability of OSCC cells by inhibiting PFKL-mediated glycolytic metabolism.27 Downregulation of Nrf2, an antioxidative stress regulator, suppresses the development of radioresistance in OSCC cells by disrupting glycolytic metabolism.28 PDK1 inhibition by BX795 enhances the sensitivity of OSCC cells to chemotherapy and radiotherapy by modulating the Akt signaling pathway and glycolytic metabolism.29 Elevated TIMELESS expression is linked to enhanced OSCC tumor growth by influencing the expression of key glycolytic genes, such as HK2, PKM2, LDHA, and GLUT1, through SIRT1 modulation.30 Silencing of STC-1 attenuates its oncogenic role in OSCC progression by suppressing glycolytic reprogramming via the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway.31 Importantly, PGC1α plays a pivotal role in promoting the proliferation of dysplastic oral keratinocytes by regulating glycolytic metabolism.32 Several circular RNAs have been shown to play critical roles in OSCC progression by regulating aerobic glycolysis. For example, the inhibitory effects of circ-KIAA0907 on the glycolytic activity of OSCC cells were reversed by miR-96-5p overexpression, which significantly attenuated the suppression of cell growth.33 Circ-0000140 has been reported to exert anti-proliferative and anti-metastatic effects in OSCC cells by upregulating CDC73 through sponging miR-182-5p, ultimately leading to the inhibition of tumor progression and glycolytic metabolism.34 Circ-LPAR3 promotes OSCC progression by sponging miR-144-3p, leading to the upregulation of LPCAT1 expression and consequently enhancing tumor growth, glycolysis, angiogenesis, and aggressiveness, while inhibiting apoptosis.35 Collectively, these findings underscore the complexity and significance of metabolic reprogramming in OSCC and support the exploration of glycolytic regulators as therapeutic targets. Therefore, targeting the aerobic glycolysis pathway is likely to remain an attractive approach for OSCC therapy.

Metabolic regulators in the tumor microenvironment of OSCC

As tumors grow and metastasize, the TME gradually shifts toward an immunosuppressive state characterized by hypoxia, acidosis, glucose deprivation, and accumulation of immunosuppressive metabolites.8 Accordingly, a deeper understanding of the key metabolic regulators and their mechanisms within the tumor microenvironment is vital for advancing therapeutic strategies (Table 2).

Cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), a major component of the TME, undergo ITGB2-mediated glycolysis, facilitating OSCC proliferation through NADH oxidation.36 Elevated glycolytic activity mediated by lncRNA H19 in CAFs also contributes to OSCC cell growth.37 Overexpression of TGF-βRII in CAFs also suppresses PKM2 nuclear translocation-mediated glycolysis, thereby inhibiting OSCC tumorigenesis.38 Furthermore, lactate derived from CAFs facilitates cancer stem cell (CSC) properties in OSCC by activating the DLG5/CUL3/MST1 signaling pathway.39

Similar to CAFs, immune cells contribute to the remodeling of glycolytic metabolism in the TME of OSCC. For instance, elevated HK2 expression in CD4+ T cells has been shown to promote lymph node metastasis in OSCC.16 Interestingly, the glycolysis competition between tumor cells and immune cells, such as CD8+ T cells, impairs the antitumor immune response by inducing T-cell exhaustion and promoting immune escape.40 Notably, B7-H3, a member of the B7 family of immune checkpoints, is highly expressed in various tumors and has been found to induce glycolysis.41 In a co-culture system of OSCC cells and CD8+ T cells, silencing of B7-H3 restored CD8+ T cell immune function by rescuing impaired IFN-γ secretion.17

In contrast, tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) promote tumor aggressiveness. TAMs, formed by the Fusobacterium nucleatum-driven remodeling of glycolytic metabolism through the GalNAc/autophagy/TBC1D5 signaling pathway in OSCC, subsequently promote tumor cell invasion.42 A comprehensive understanding of the metabolic regulators in both OSCC cells and the TME and their roles in orchestrating tumor progression, metastasis, and immune evasion may offer novel therapeutic opportunities to reprogram the TME and improve clinical outcomes.

Natural product-based therapeutic strategies targeting aerobic glycolysis in OSCC

Natural products (NPs) have demonstrated pharmacological efficacy with reduced side effects compared to synthetic drugs, rendering them a promising therapeutic approach for cancer. NPs inhibit glycolysis by modulating glycolytic enzymes and associated signaling pathways, thereby suppressing tumor proliferation and aggressiveness and enhancing the susceptibility of tumor cells to anticancer therapies.43 For instance, bitter melon extract attenuates glycolysis by downregulating key glycolytic genes, including GLUT-1, PFKP, LDHA, PDM, and PDK3, thereby inducing ER stress- and oxidative stress-mediated apoptosis in OSCC cells.44 Tanshinone ⅡA, a major constituent derived from Salvia miltiorrhiza Bunge, has been reported to target the Akt/FBW7/c-Myc signaling pathway, thereby reducing HK2-mediated glycolytic activity, which ultimately induces apoptosis and inhibits cell growth in OSCC cells.45 Gastrodin, a principal component of Gastrodia elata, suppresses cisplatin resistance in OSCC cells exhibiting HK2-mediated glycolysis by inhibiting the Skp2/Akt signaling axis.46 By suppressing NAT10-mediated ac4C modification of HK2, Paeonol, a bioactive compound derived from Moutan Cortex, impairs glycolysis in OSCC cells, suggesting its potential as a therapeutic strategy.47 Nobiletin, a polymethoxylated flavone found in dietary fruits, attenuates cell proliferation in OSCC by inhibiting the PKA/CREB signaling pathway, which is linked to impaired glycolytic activity through the disruption of mitochondrial function and pyruvate dehydrogenase activity.48 Brucea javanica oil, extracted from the seeds of Brucea javanica, a plant belonging to the Quasisteraceae family, reduces MTFR2-mediated aerobic glycolysis and subsequently modulates the SOD2/H2O2 signaling axis, thereby inhibiting the growth and aggressive behavior of OSCC cells.49 Collectively, NPs targeting aerobic glycolysis in OSCC represent a promising strategy for suppressing tumor development, progression, and resistance to chemotherapy (Table 3).

Conclusion

Cancer is a metabolic disease, and altered metabolic activity is one of its hallmarks. Since the discovery of the Warburg effect, extensive research has identified numerous metabolic regulators and mechanisms that contribute to tumor progression, metastasis, and chemoresistance, promoting the development of metabolism-targeted therapeutic strategies. Despite these advances, clinical trials targeting cancer metabolism have shown limited efficacy, primarily due to off-target effects on normal cells, as well as the metabolic heterogeneity, adaptability, and complexity between tumor cells and their microenvironment.13 Nonetheless, continued investigation into targeting cancer metabolism remains essential, as metabolic reprogramming is a fundamental driver of tumor progression, metastasis, resistance to therapy, and immune evasion. In this review, we summarize the key molecular regulators and underlying mechanisms influencing aerobic glycolysis in OSCC and its tumor microenvironment and highlight natural product-based therapeutic strategies targeting these metabolic pathways. Further investigation of glycolytic metabolism in OSCC is essential to advance therapeutic strategies and improve clinical outcomes.

OA20110. Motorcycle safety countermeasures: Insights from a hybrid cost-benefit evaluation approach
Authors: S Lioliou, M Papadakaki, M Tselekounis, K Vagionaki
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMotorcycle crashes account for a disproportionate share of road trauma in Greece, with high rates of fatalities and serious injuries. To support data-driven policy decisions, this study aims to identify, assess, and compare the socioeconomic benefits of key motorcycle safety countermeasures by employing robust economic valuation approaches.MethodsThree interventions—helmet quality upgrades, urban speed limit enforcement, and motorcyclist education—were evaluated using two hybrid cost-benefit frameworks: one combining Willingness-to-Pay (WTP) for fatalities with Cost of Illness (COI) and Disability-Adjusted Life Years (DALYs) for serious injuries, and a second replacing WTP with the Human Capital Approach (HCA). Core assumptions applied across scenarios included a Value of Statistical Life (VSL) of €2M (EC recommendations), HCA value of €480K (average lifetime productivity), COI per injury of €50K (EU transport studies), and 0.36 DALYs per serious injury valued at €50K (WHO). The investment costs, based on national and European data, were €120M (helmets), €60M (speed), and €15M (education). Sensitivity analysis varied all key parameters ±20%.ResultsUnder the WTP-based hybrid approach, all interventions yielded strong returns in terms of net benefit and Benefit-Cost Ratio (BCR): helmets (€248M, BCR 3.07), speed limits (€80.4M, BCR 2.34), and education (€78.6M, BCR 6.24). Under the HCA-based model, only helmet upgrades remained cost-beneficial (€20M, BCR 1.17), while speed and education produced marginal or negative returns. Sensitivity analysis showed that the number of fatalities and serious injuries prevented were the most influential variables across all cases.ConclusionsThe WTP-based hybrid method offers a more comprehensive and resilient valuation, thus reinforcing its relevance for evidence-based policy. Helmet improvements and education programs are highly cost-effective, even under uncertainty, making them strong investment priorities in support of Vision Zero goals.AcknowledgementThis work has been partly supported by the University of Piraeus Research Center.Key messages• WTP-based valuation supports evidence-driven policymaking for prioritizing motorcycle safety countermeasures.• Helmet upgrades and motorcyclist education offer the highest cost-effectiveness among tested measures, thus maximizing safety gains per euro spent.TopicCost-benefit analysis, Motorcycle safety, Socioeconomic valuation methods

Investigation of the Relationship Between Skull and Gender of the Sella Turcica in Cats Using Computed Tomography
Authors: Sedat Aydoğdu, Yusuf Altundağ
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundThe sella turcica is an important skull formation due to its close anatomical relationship with the pituitary gland. Therefore, the shape and morphometry of the sella turcica provide crucial preliminary information for the determination of cases.ObjectivesThe aim of this study was to determine the morphometry of the sella turcica in cats and its relationship with gender and skull morphometry using computed tomography (CT).MethodsIn this study, CT images of 18 female and 15 male adult cats were used for morphometric measurements. Six different morphometric measurements of the sella turcica were performed on CT images. Similarly, seven different morphometric measurements were made on the skull and six different indices of the skull were calculated. Additionally, 3D modelling of the hypophyseal fossa, dorsum sellae and tuberculum sellae that form the sella turcica was performed.ResultsStatistically significant gender‐based differences were observed in sella diameter, sella height cranialis and sella width (p < 0.01). In the morphometric measurements obtained from the skull, differences were observed in skull length, cranial length, external height of cranium, internal length of the cranium and external length of the cranium (p < 0.01). Similarly, a difference was found in cranial width (p < 0.05). All measurements obtained from the skull were determined to be higher in male cats. Differences were observed in the cranial index, internal cranium index and external cranium index, and these indices were found to be higher in females. A strong positive correlation was observed between sella diameter and sella width. Similarly, a very strong positive correlation was found between skull length and cranial length.ConclusionIn this study, the morphology and morphometry of the sella turcica in cats were revealed. In sella turcica and skull morphometry, a strong positive correlation was found between sella diameter/sella width and skull length/cranial length. The findings obtained from the sella turcica and skull, which is clinically important in humans, are thought to contribute to future clinical studies in cats. Additionally, the study's findings are expected to contribute to characterizing the morphometry of the domestic cat's skull.

Introduction

Skull is among the most commonly utilized skeletal bones in taxonomy because of its unique characteristics related to species and gender (Uddin et al. 2013; Gündemir et al. 2020; Ramos et al. 2021; Szara et al. 2022; Gündemir, Akcasiz, et al. 2023). In the domestic cat (Felis catus Linnaeus, 1758), the shape of the cat's head depends on its skull. This head shape is associated with specific skeletal properties of the breed (Künzel et al. 2003; Uddin et al. 2013).

The pituitary gland, one of the body's most important endocrine glands, is located in the skull. It sits in the cavity called the hypophyseal fossa, located at the base of the skull (König et al. 2004; Dyce et al. 2010). A protrusion called the dorsum sellae is caudal to the hypophyseal fossa, which is the space where the pituitary gland sits. The tuberculum sellae is located rostral to the same cavity. Hypophyseal fossa, dorsum sellae and tuberculum sellae form sella turcica (Turkish saddle) (König et al. 2004; Tekiner et al. 2015). The pituitary gland is a vital structure that plays a regulatory role in the entire endocrine system. The pituitary gland consists of two parts, the neurohypophysis and the adenohypophysis, which have different embryological origins (König et al. 2004; Tepedino et al. 2019; Iskra et al. 2023). Due to the anatomical relationships between the sella turcica and the pituitary gland, the shape of the sella turcica provides significant preliminary information for identifying cases. Enlargement of the sella turcica is observed due to pituitary adenoma. Deviations in the morphology of the sella turcica can be observed in various congenital disorders such as Down syndrome, Seckel syndrome and Williams syndrome. Additionally, pituitary gland disorders may cause sella turcica malformations (Pellegrino et al. 2013; Tepedino et al. 2019; Surana et al. 2022; Iskra et al. 2023; Koçyiğit et al. 2024a). Pathologies, such as adenomas or cysts that cause the pituitary gland to enlarge or shrink, may affect the shape of the sella turcica (Tepedino et al. 2019; Szara et al. 2022).

Morphometry of the sella turcica is an important parameter used to distinguish age, gender and racial groups for humans (Muhammed et al. 2019; Muhammed et al. 2020; Iskra et al. 2023). In domestic mammals, studies on the morphology and morphometry of the sella turcica are very limited (Koçyiğit et al. 2024a). Similarly, skull morphometry is frequently used to distinguish age, gender and breed in domestic mammals. In conventional methods, morphometric measurements are performed with digital clippers (Onar 1999; Uddin et al. 2013; Gündemir et al. 2025). However, in recent years, radiometric measurements have been frequently used. Measurements are performed on computed tomography (CT) images of the skull (Bakıcı et al. 2020; Akbaş et al. 2023; Gündemir 2023; Gündemir, Akcasiz, et al. 2023; Çakar et al. 2024; Özkan et al. 2024). Skull morphometry of cats (Ramos et al. 2021; Gündemir, Akcasiz, et al. 2023; Gündemir, Szara, et al. 2023; Koçyiğit et al. 2024b), dogs (Onar et al. 2002; García‐Real et al. 2004; Schmidt et al. 2011; Curth et al. 2017; Czeibert et al. 2020), sheep (Güzel and Işbilir 2023; Güzel and Işbilir 2024; Koçyiğit et al. 2024c) and cattle (Çakar et al. 2024) has been obtained using CT images of the head.

The motivation for the study is that there is limited information on the morphology and morphometry of the sella turcica in domestic mammals, which is an important parameter for distinguishing age, gender and racial groups in humans. The study hypothesizes that sella turcica morphometry in cats differs between genders and that there is a relationship between skull morphometry. For this reason, the study aims to determine the morphometry of the sella turcica and skull in cats according to gender using CT, and to reveal the relationship between sella turcica and skull morphometry in cats.

Materials and Methods

Study Population

A total of 33 healty adult tabby cats, 18 female and 15 male, were used in the study. The average age and weight of female cats are 4.03 years (age range 1–12) and 3.48 kg (weight range 1.50–6.50). The average age and weight of male cats is 5.02 years (age range 1–13) and 4.32 kg (weight range 3.00–6.20). CT images were obtained from archive images from 2020–2024, the Animal Hospital of the Faculty of Veterinary Medicine, Istanbul University‐Cerrahpaşa. The heads of the cats were scanned in the ventral recumbency using multi‐detector Siemens (Somatom Scope vc30b) CT. CT images of cat heads with 110 kV, 28 mA and 0.6 mm slice thickness were obtained. Images were stored in DICOM (Digital Imaging and Communications in Medicine) format. There were no pathological conditions in the animals. The images were then transferred to the open‐source ITK‐SNAP software (Yushkevich et al. 2006) for measurements of the skull and sella turcica. The study protocol was approved by the Istanbul University‐Cerrahpasa Animal Experiments Local Ethics Committee (Approval number: 2025/20).

Measurements of Sella Turcica

Firstly, 3D reconstruction of sella turcica was performed in cats for its location in the skull and morphological examination (Figure 1). Reconstruction of the sella turcica was performed semi‐automatically using ITK‐SNAP software (Ravikumar et al. 2020; Ravikumar et al. 2021). For this purpose, the lowest ROI (region of interest) was determined to include the borders of the sella turcica. After the threshold value was determined, seed points were placed in the appropriate regions. The snake algorithm was started and the seed points were used to fill the areas. The reconstruction of the sella turcica and skull was completed (Hutchinson et al. 2017).

3D reconstruction of sella turcica. (A) Dorsal view, (B) rostral view and (C) lateral view.

Then, morphometric measurements of the sella turcica were performed using CT images. Morphometric measurements of sella diameter (Sd), sella length (Sll), sella height cranialis (Shcr), sella height caudalis (Shca), sella height median (Shm) and sella width (Sw) of the sella turcica were performed in female and male cats as shown in Figure 2 (Nagaraj et al. 2015; Shrestha et al. 2018; Silveira et al. 2020; Feng et al. 2023; Koçyiğit et al. 2024a).

Morphometric measurements obtained from the sella turcica. (1) Sella diameter, (2) sella length, (3) sella height cranialis, (4) sella height caudalis, (5) sella height median and (6) sella width.

Measurements of the Skull

Morphometric measurements were performed on the skull using the same software. In morphometric measurements, anatomical landmarks were determined according to previous studies and recorded in millimetres (Uddin et al. 2013; Ramos et al. 2021; Gündemir, Akcasiz, et al. 2023). All measurements except cranial width (CW) were performed in the sagittal plane. CW measurements were made in the dorsal plane (Figure 3). Morphometric measurements were performed three times by the same researcher (to reduce interpersonal errors) and at different times to reduce intrapersonal errors. The arithmetic average of the measurements was then calculated. The measurements listed below were performed on the skull.

Morphometric measurements of the skull on the sagittal plane. Abbreviations: CL, cranial length; EHC, external height of cranium; ELC, external length of the cranium; IHC, internal height of cranium; ILC, internal length of the cranium; SL, skull length.

Skull length (SL): From the rostral border of the nasal bone to the external occipital protuberance.

Cranial length (CL): From the external occipital protuberance to the caudal limit of the nasal bone.

Internal height of cranium (IHC): From the deepest indentation of the sella turcica directly dorsal to the inner layer of the base of the cranium to the most dorsal surface of the cranium.

External height of cranium (EHC): It is identical to the IHC but in the external face or the bone surfaces in question.

Internal length of the cranium (ILC): From the deepest indentation of the fronto‐ethmoidal junction to the middle of the distal surface of the cranium at the level of the cerebral surface of the external occipital protuberance.

External length of the cranium (ELC): It is identical to the ILC but of the external surface of the bones in question.

CW: Between the two most lateral points of the cranium.

After the morphometric measurements obtained from the skull, important indices of the skull and cranium were calculated using these measurements. The indices, whose formulas are given below, were calculated using measurements obtained from the skull and cranium.

NL (nasal length) = SL − CL

Ci (cranial index) = CW/CL × 100

ICi (internal cranium index) = IHC/ILC × 100

ECi (external cranium index) = EHC/ELC × 100

ICSi (internal cranium and skull index) = ILC/SL × 100

ECSi (external cranium and skull index) = ELC/SL × 100

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using the software SPSS 25 (IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, version 25.0. Armonk, New York: IBM Corp). Homogeneity of variances and normality were tested using Levene's test and Shapiro Wilk. In a gender comparison, the Student's t‐test was used in cases where parametric test prerequisites were met, and the Mann–Whitney U test was used in cases where they were not met. The relation between measurements obtained from the sella turcica and skull was examined using Pearson's correlation.

Results

Morphological findings obtained from the cat sella turcica were evaluated using 3D models (Figure 4). The findings showed that the dorsum sellae was flat in cats. The deep oval and concave shape in ruminants was not observed in the cat. It was determined that the processus clinoideus caudalis was prominent on the upper right and left sides of the dorsum sellae.

Discussion

This study provides novel morphometric insights into the sella turcica of domestic cats. The sella turcica, formed by the hypophysial fossa, dorsum sellae and tuberculum sella, has a close anatomical relationship with the pituitary gland. Therefore, differences in the shape of the sella turcica are important in providing preliminary information about pituitary anomalies (Pellegrino et al. 2013; Tepedino et al. 2019; Iskra et al. 2023; Koçyiğit et al. 2024a). The relationship between the morphometry of the sella turcica, gender, weight, age and skull morphometry in cats, one of the domestic mammals, was investigated.

Morphometric measurements performed on the sella turcica in male sheep, capra and gazelle using CT images showed statistical differences in Sll and Shcr. In these measurements, the Sll was the highest in the capra, and the Shcr was the highest in the gazelle. Among these species, the highest value in measurements made on sella turcica was obtained from Sw (Koçyiğit et al. 2024a). In this study, the highest value in morphometric measurements made on the sella turcica was determined in the Sll. This difference is thought to be due to the characteristic features of the sella turcica in species. In sheep, capra and gazelle, the dorsum sellae was known to be oval and rostral. In the cat, it was found that it was flatter and did not extend rostrally as much.

IHC and ELC measurements were performed using a calliper on the skull of domestic cats in Bangladesh. In these measurements made with conventional methods, no statistical difference was observed between female and male cats. While the weights of the cats used in the study were stated, their ages were not (Uddin et al. 2013). Similarly, no difference was identified in IHC in morphometric measurements performed on the cat skull using CT images. A difference was found in terms of gender in ELC, and it was observed to be higher in males. This difference is thought to be due to the age of the cats used in both studies.

Morphometric measurements using CT images of the European Shorthair cat skull were used to examine the differences between the genders. In this study, the characteristic morphometry of the cranium and skull of the European Shorthair cat was revealed. The study was conducted on skull CT images of adult female and male cats, ages ranging from 1 to 17 years (average of 8.3 years). Statistical differences were found in SL, CL, EHC, ILC, ELC measurements and all indices in male and female cats. Morphometric measurements and NL were found to be higher in male cats and all other indices were higher in female cats (Ramos et al. 2021). Similarly, in the current study, no difference was identified in IHC measurements. However, a difference was found in favour of male cats in the CW measurement. In both studies, it was observed that all measurements in which differences were identified were higher in male cats. While differences were observed in all indices in the European Shorthair cat, differences were detected in Ci, ICi and ECi in the current study. In both studies, it was observed that the NL index was higher in males and all other indices were higher in females. It was observed that the values obtained from morphometric measurements were close to each other in both studies. It is thought that the difference in some measurements and indices is due to the characteristic skull structures of cat breeds.

Morphometric measurements were performed on the skull using CT images in Van cats, British Shorthairs and Scottish Folds. It has been stated that CL and SL values are the highest in the Van cat and the lowest in the British Shorthair. A statistical difference was observed between the Van cat and other breeds in SL. The Scottish Fold was found to have the largest orbital diameter. In morphometric measurements, gender differences were observed in SL and CL measurements and indices Ci, ICi and ECSi. The strongest positive correlation between measurements was identified between SL and CL (Gündemir, Akcasiz, et al. 2023; Gündemir, Szara, et al. 2023). In the current study, while statistical differences were observed in most of the measurements made on the skull, differences were found in the indexes Ci, ICi and ECi. The measurement values obtained in both studies are close to each other, but the differences are thought to be due to cat breeds. It is thought that the Van cat being a specific breed affected the average values of the study.

In another study using Van cats, ILC, IHC and CW measurements of the skull were performed. While a gender difference in skull volume was identified, no difference was noted in morphometric measurements (Koçyiğit et al. 2024b). In the current study, a difference was found in other measurements except IHC. It is thought that the measurement values in both studies are close to each other and the difference is due to the breeds used. Similarly, the same morphometric measurements obtained using calipers from Persian, Birman, European Shorthair, Maine Coon, British Shorthair, Charteux, Burmese and Siamese were observed to be close (Künzel et al. 2003).

Morphometric measurements were performed on dogs (Onar et al. 2002; García‐Real et al. 2004), cattle (Çakar et al. 2024), sheep (Güzel and Işbilir 2023; Güzel and Işbilir 2024; Koçyiğit et al. 2024c) and goats (Güzel and Işbilir 2023) using CT images. This method contributes to taxonomic classification by revealing skull characteristics of different breeds within the same species. The method used in the current study is thought to be compatible with studies conducted on other domestic mammals, and the findings obtained will contribute to the taxonomic classification of cats. Similar to the current study, studies conducted on other mammals have revealed differences in terms of gender. It was determined that the morphometric measurement averages obtained from male mammals were higher than those of females.

In humans, four classification methods can be distinguished for the shape of the sella turcica. The most common variants are normal, U‐shaped, the circular type and the on‐bridging type. The shape and morphometric characteristics of the sella turcica can be used to evaluate normal and pathological variants. Deviations in the sella turcica morphology are most commonly observed in patients with Down syndrome, Seckel syndrome and Williams syndrome (Tepedino et al. 2019; Surana et al. 2022; Iskra et al. 2023). Literature focusing on the morphometry of the sella turcica and pituitary gland in veterinary neurology or endocrinology is limited. This constitutes a limitation of the study in terms of evaluating the current findings. Furthermore, the sample size and the use of a single breed constitute other limitations of the current study.

Conclusion

This study revealed the morphometry of the sella turcica, which has close anatomical relationships with the pituitary gland in cats. In addition, the relationship of the sella turcica with skull morphometry was examined. The morphology and morphometry of the sella turcica, which is clinically important in humans, has also been characterized in cats. The findings obtained from sella turcica are thought to contribute significantly to future studies in this field. The findings obtained from the skull will help reveal the characteristic features of the skull in domestic cat breeds. It is expected that it will contribute to taxonomy, anthropology and clinical studies. In addition, in the current study and similar studies conducted on cats, gender differences in SL/CL measurements and Ci/ICi indices were frequently observed. Furthermore, in the current study, gender differences were observed in Sd, Shcr and Sw in the sella turcica. In the skull, gender differences were found in EHC, ILC, ELC and CW measurements, as well as in the ECi index. In morphometric measurements obtained from the sella turcica and skull, the highest positive correlation was observed between the Sd and SL. These parameters are thought to be useful, especially in determining the characteristic features of cat breeds in taxonomic studies.

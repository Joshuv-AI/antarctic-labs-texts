Clinical characteristics and prognosis of primary thymic adenocarcinoma: A single‐center retrospective analysis
Authors: Qian Hong, Rui Han, Chen Chen, Fuquan Wang, Sining Zhang, Chenguang Zhao, Fang Li, Juwei Mu, Jiagen Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPrimary thymic adenocarcinoma (PTAC) is an extremely rare disease with a poor prognosis. In the present study, we sought to analyze the clinical characteristics and prognostic factors of patients with PTAC.MethodsA total of 14 patients with PTAC treated at our center from January 2000 to January 2019 were included in this study. We retrospectively collected information on sex, age, history of smoking, family history of cancer, comorbidities, symptoms, imaging tests, serum tumor marker levels, tumor, node, metastasis (TNM) staging, and treatment records. Follow‐up information was obtained by telephone interviews or outpatient clinic visit. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were performed to investigate the clinicopathological factors associated with survival.ResultsAmong 14 patients with PTAC, there were five males and nine females, with an average age of 48.7 ± 9.3 years. A total of 23.1% of the patients had a history of smoking. The clinical symptoms of the patients were nonspecific and seven patients had elevated levels of serum tumor markers. Surgery was performed for nine patients, among which only four received R0 resection. The median survival time of the 14 patients was 16.0 months, and the 1‐, 3‐ and 5‐year survival rates were 57.1%, 35.7% and 21.4%, respectively. TNM stage was identified as an independent prognostic factor for PTAC patients (the median survival time of stage I–IIIA vs. stage IV was 44.0 months vs. 9.0 months, p = 0.002).ConclusionsPTAC is highly aggressive malignancy with poor prognosis. Surgical treatment is feasible, but R0 resection is challenging. TNM staging is significantly associated with patient survival.

INTRODUCTION

Primary thymic adenocarcinoma (PTAC) is an extremely rare tumor of thymic epithelial origin, accounting for less than 5% of thymic malignancies. 1 PTAC is a highly aggressive disease, and the invasion of mediastinal fat tissue, mediastinal pleura, pericardium, vessels and lung are found in the majority of patients. 2 Due to its rarity, relatively few studies have been conducted on the characteristics and prognostic factors for PTAC patients. To date, most studies on PTAC have been single‐case reports, and only two multicase studies have reported the histologic characteristics of PTAC. 1 , 2 Due to the lack of study on large‐sample case series and clinical guidelines, comprehensive treatment strategies for PTAC have been controversial. 3 , 4 For resectable PTAC, surgery is currently the main treatment. 3 , 4 Complete resection of the PTAC and the invaded surrounding tissues have been found to be beneficial for patient survival. The role of chemotherapy and radiotherapy is unclear due to lack of evidence. Large‐scale cohort studies are necessary to better understand the factors affecting treatment outcomes and prognosis of PTAC. Given the rarity of PTAC, this is difficult. In this study, we retrospectively analyzed the clinical and pathological features of 14 patients with PTAC who were treated at the Cancer Institute and Hospital, Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences and Peking Union Medical College. Meanwhile, we analyzed the survival and treatment of 14 patients with PTAC and explored the risk factors affecting the prognosis of PTAC by univariate and multivariate Cox regression analysis. Although the sample size of this study was small, we still hope that the results of this study will bring new insights into the treatment and prognosis of PTAC.

METHODS

Patients and clinical data

A total of 14 patients with PTAC treated in Cancer Institute and Hospital, Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences and Peking Union Medical College from January 2000 to January 2019 were included in this study. Inclusion criteria: patients who were treated in our hospital from January 2000 to January 2019 and patients with a pathological diagnosis of PTAC. Exclusion criteria: patients with metastatic thymic adenocarcinoma, patients with ambiguous treatment records and patients who refused follow up. Clinical data of 14 patients with PTAC were collected by searching and reviewing case databases, including sex, age, history of smoking, family history of cancer, comorbidities, symptoms, imaging tests, serum tumor marker levels, TNM staging, and treatment records. Data were checked daily by three inspectors, randomly sampled twice a week, and the final study results were reproduced and reviewed. The pathological diagnosis of PTAC was reviewed and confirmed by two independent pathologists at out hospital. Follow‐up information was obtained by telephone interviews or outpatient clinic visit. All patients were followed up at 3‐month intervals during the first year of treatment, at 6‐month intervals for 2–3 years, and annually thereafter. Follow‐up examinations included enhanced computed tomography of the neck, chest, and abdomen and laboratory hematology. The time from the start of initial treatment to death was calculated as overall survival (OS). TNM staging was based on the eighth edition of the AJCC/UICC stage classification. 5 Patients who underwent surgical resection were based on pathological TNM staging, otherwise, clinical TNM staging was used. This study was reviewed and approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) committee at Cancer Hospital, Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences (no. 24/306‐586). The research process strictly followed the medical ethical norms. This was a retrospective noninterventional study and patient information was anonymized. The requirement to obtain informed consent from patients has been waived.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed with SPSS version 22.0 (IBM Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). Data are expressed as mean values plus standard deviation if not otherwise stated. Basic clinical data were analyzed using descriptive or frequency analysis. Kaplan–Meier curves were plotted for survival analysis. Differences in survival between groups were analyzed using the log‐rank test. Univariate and multivariate analyses were used to determine the independent prognostic factors of PTAC patients. A p‐value of <0.05 indicated a statistically significant difference.

RESULTS

Clinical features of 14 patients with PTAC

A total of 14 cases of PTAC were diagnosed from January 2000 to January 2019 at the Cancer Institute and Hospital, Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences and Peking Union Medical College. The patient selection flow chart is shown in Figure S1. Demographic and clinical characteristics of the 14 patients were first summarized and are listed in Table 1. Overall, there were nine females (64.3%) and five males (35.7%) with a mean age of 48.7 ± 9.3 years, ranging from 35 to 66 years. Three patients had a history of smoking (23.1%). None of the patients had a history of malignancy. Four patients had a family history of cancer (28.6%), while none were PTAC. Four patients had comorbidities of which hypertension was the most common. Thoracodynia was the most frequent symptom, occurring in eight of the 14 patients (57.1%). In addition, patients also presented with cough, dyspnea, hoarseness, dysphagia, and weight loss. Four patients were asymptomatic who were diagnosed by health check. The mean time from onset to treatment was 1.54 ± 0.77 months, ranging from 0.5 to 3 months. There were 11 patients with traceable serum tumor marker results and seven patients with elevated tumor marker levels (7/11, 63.6%). Among them, CA125 was elevated in six patients and was the tumor marker with the highest frequency of abnormalities (6/11, 54.5%). This was followed by CYFRA21‐1 and CEA, which increased in four (36.4%) and three (27.3%) patients, respectively.

Tumor characteristics and TNM staging of 14 patients with PTAC

Next, we evaluated the TNM staging of the patients. The mean maximum tumor diameter of the 14 PTAC patients was 5.53 ± 1.24 cm (range, 3.1 to 9.0 cm). The computed tomography (CT) manifestations of all patients were predominantly solid masses. Six were associated with cysts and two with calcifications. The TNM staging of the 14 patients with PTAC is shown in Table 2. A total of 12 (85.7%) patients had mediastinal fat, mediastinal pleura, pericardium, or macrovascular invasion and six (42.9%) patients had lymph node metastases. In addition, six (42.9%) patients had distant metastases.

Treatment and prognosis of patients with PTAC

As for the treatment of PTAC, nine patients (64.3%) underwent surgical resection; however, only four (4/9, 44.4%) patients had R0 resections (Table 3). None of the nine patients received neoadjuvant therapy. Six patients received postoperative chemotherapy (6/9, 66.7%). Similarly, six patients received postoperative radiotherapy (6/9, 66.7%). In addition, of the five patients who did not undergo surgical resection, two patients received chemotherapy alone and three patients received concurrent chemoradiotherapy.

Follow‐up information was available for all patients. The median survival time (MST) of the 14 patients with PTAC was 16.0 months, with 1‐, 3‐, and 5‐year survival rates being 57.1%, 35.7%, and 21.4%, respectively (Figure 1a). Univariate and multivariate Cox regression was performed to analyze the relationship between clinical factors and survival in patients with PTAC, such as sex, age, smoking history, drinking history, family history of cancer, comorbidity, months between onset and treatment, maximum tumor diameter, TNM staging, and treatment (Table 4). The results showed that TNM staging was an independent risk factor for the prognosis of PTAC (HR [95% CI]: 0.111 (0.022–0.556), p = 0.008). Log‐rank test was used to analyze the differences in survival between groups, and the results are shown in Table S1. The log‐rank test showed that TNM staging was significantly associated with the prognosis of patients with PTAC (p = 0.002, Figure 1b). Survival of PTAC patients with TNM stage IV was significantly shorter than that of stage I–IIIA patients (MST, 9 vs. 44 months). We also found that patients tended to have shorter survival when the maximum tumor diameter exceeded 5 cm, with the p‐value being marginal, probably owing to limited sample size (MST 12 vs. 44 months, p = 0.053, Figure 1c). In addition, we compared the survival factors of the nine PTAC patients who underwent surgical resection. The results showed that PTAC patients with R0 resection had longer survival than those with R2 resection, although the difference was not significant (MST 44 vs. 12 months, p = 0.053, Figure 1d).

DISCUSSION

In the present study, we retrospectively summarized the clinical and pathological features of 14 PTAC patients and analyzed their associated survival factors. Our results revealed that PTAC is a highly aggressive disease with a poor prognosis and that most patients are at an advanced stage of disease at diagnosis. Survival analysis found TNM staging as an independent prognosis factor for patients and that complete resection of the tumor may be beneficial in prolonging survival.

To date, most of the studies on PTAC appear in the form of case reports, and multicase studies by pathologists are mainly focused on the pathological characteristics of PTAC. 1 , 2 In this study, we summarized the clinical characteristics of 14 patients with PTAC and analyzed the related factors affecting the survival of patients.

Our findings showed that the proportion of female PTAC patients was higher than males, which is inconsistent with previous studies. 1 , 2 This difference may be related to region or race. It is also different with the sex distribution in thymic squamous cell carcinoma, the major type in thymic carcinomas. 6 , 7 Studies have shown that smoking is one of the risk factors for thymoma and thymic squamous cell carcinoma. 8 , 9 , 10 In our study, smoking history was observed in only 23.1% of the patients and was not associated with the prognosis of PTAC patients. Our results showed that PTAC patients usually present with some nonspecific symptoms, such as thoracodynia, cough, and dyspnea, which is one of the factors that lead to a delay in finding the disease. This was consistent with previous studies. 1 These symptoms may be related to compression of surrounding organs by the tumor. In addition, four patients were asymptomatic, two of whom were at TNM stage III–IV. This indicates that patients with advanced PTAC may also be asymptomatic, which is a great challenge for the diagnosis and treatment of PTAC. At present, there is a lack of specific tumor markers for the diagnosis of PTAC. Some studies claim that CA19‐9 and CEA show good potential in diagnosing PTAC. 11 , 12 We found that serum CA125, CYFRA21‐1 and CEA were abnormally elevated in PTAC patients. Among them, CA125 was the tumor marker with the highest abnormal frequency. However, serum CA19‐9 was not tested in any of the 14 patients. The findings and value of tumor markers associated with PTAC still needs further investigation.

Tumor size is not listed as a staging criterion in either the Masaoka‐Koga staging or the AJCC/UICC stage classification. 5 , 13 However, most studies take the maximum tumor diameter >5 cm as the critical value of huge tumors to evaluate the difficulty of operation and the risk of bleeding. 14 , 15 In this study, the mean maximum tumor diameter of the 14 patients with PTAC was 5.53 ± 1.24 cm, of which only four patients had a maximum tumor diameter less than 5 cm. Therefore, most of the operations were performed with sternotomy or thoracotomy, and only two patients were performed with VATS. PTAC appears primarily as a solid mass in the anterior mediastinum with or without cysts on CT scan. Not only solid masses with cysts could be seen in our cohort, but also pitting calcifications were seen in two patients. It has been reported that calcification is often seen in thymic mucinous adenocarcinoma, a subtype of thymic adenocarcinoma. 16 , 17

Due to its rarity, studies with large cohorts of PTAC patients are lacking, and so is the analysis on patient survival. To the best of our knowledge, our study is the first to analyze the survival and prognostic factors of PTAC patients. At the time of follow up, only two of the 14 patients were still alive, both of whom were TNM stage I patients who had undergone R0 resection. Retrospective data based on the Chinese Alliance for Research in Thymomas showed that the OS of patients with TNM stage I–IIIA is significantly better than that of patients with stage IIIB–IV. 18 Our results also suggest that TNM staging is significantly associated with the prognosis of PTAC patients, with stage IV patients having significantly shorter survival than stage I–IIIA patients. However, chemotherapy, radiotherapy and other factors described in previous studies did not have a clear correlation with the prognosis of PTAC in this study. 19 In addition, it has been shown that when the maximum tumor diameter is >5 cm, a significant reduction in overall survival is seen in both thymoma and thymic mucoepidermoid carcinoma cohorts. 20 , 21 Similarly, the same trend was seen in our cohort.

Standard comprehensive treatment strategies for PTAC have not yet been established, but surgery is considered the primary treatment for resectable diseases. In the results of a large‐sample study by Ahmad et al., R0 resection was significantly associated with prolonged OS for patients. 19 We performed a subgroup analysis of nine surgically treated patients and found that patients with PTAC who underwent R0 resection had better survival than those who underwent R2 resection, but the difference was not statistically significant, which may be due to the limited sample size.

The present study had some limitations. First, as a retrospective study, the reliability of the data lacked audit and prospective studies are needed to further validate the results. Second, the lack of pathological indicators may be an important factor affecting prognosis. In addition, although this study is the first to explore prognostic factors associated with PTAC, the sample size is still limited and the findings may therefore be biased. More cases need to be accumulated in order to better understand the disease.

In conclusion, in the present study, the clinical characteristics of 14 patients with PTAC were summarized and survival‐related factors evaluated. PTAC is a highly malignant tumor with poor prognosis. Surgical treatment is feasible, but R0 resection is challenging. TNM staging is significantly associated with patient survival.

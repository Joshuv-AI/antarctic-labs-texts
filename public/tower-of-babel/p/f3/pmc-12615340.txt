Associations between dynamic hematological indicators and outcomes in HR-positive, HER2-negative metastatic breast cancer treated with bireociclib or placebo plus fulvestrant: Findings from the BRIGHT-2 trial
Authors: Hangcheng Xu, Yan Wang, Qiang Sa, Yiran Zhou, Fei Ma, Pin Zhang, Ying Fan, Ruigang Cai, Qiao Li, Bo Lan, Jiani Wang, Liang Cui, Xinxin Tan, Jing Bai, Yan Hu, Xianghui Duan, Fan Yang, Jiayu Wang, Binghe Xu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCyclin-dependent kinases 4 and 6 (CDK4/6) inhibitors combined with endocrine therapy have revolutionized the treatment landscape of hormone receptor (HR)-positive advanced breast cancer. However, reliable biomarkers for CDK4/6 inhibitors remain limited. This study investigated dynamic peripheral blood indicators as potential prognostic biomarkers for survival outcomes and therapeutic efficacy.MethodsThis post-hoc analysis included patients with HR-positive, HER2-negative metastatic breast cancer treated with bireocilib or placebo with fulvestrant from BRIGHT-2 trial. Peripheral blood samples collected at baseline and on-treatment were used to calculate absolute lymphocyte count (ALC), neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), monocyte-to-lymphocyte ratio (MLR), platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR), systemic immune-inflammation index (SII) and prognostic nutritional index (PNI). Kaplan-Meier analysis and Cox proportional hazards models assessed survival outcomes, while dynamic changes in indicators were compared using paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test.ResultsA total of 305 patients was included in this analysis. Bireociclib significantly improved PFS versus placebo regardless of MLR, PLR, and PNI levels (all p < 0.05). In multivariate Cox analysis, NLR, MLR, SII and PNI were independent prognostic factors for both PFS and overall survival (OS). None of the hematological indicators demonstrated significant predictive value across different treatment arms. In experimental group, PLR remained stable during treatment in the response group but increased significantly in the non-response group (p = 0.003). In control group, none of the indicators demonstrated significant changes from baseline to on-treatment point.ConclusionsPeripheral blood-derived indicators are prognostic markers for HR-positive, HER2-negative metastatic breast cancer. Elevated PLR during treatment indicates poor response to CDK4/6 inhibitors.Keywords: Metastatic breast cancer, HR-positive, CDK4/6 inhibitor, Prognostic factor, Hematological indicator

Introduction

Hormone receptor (HR)-positive breast cancer, which accounts for 70–80 % of all breast cancer cases [1], remains a major contributor to the overall disease burden, particularly in advanced stages. The introduction of cyclin-dependent kinase 4 and 6 (CDK4/6) inhibitors in combination with endocrine therapy has transformed the treatment paradigm for HR-positive advanced breast cancer, establishing a new standard of care. Several CDK4/6 inhibitors such as palbociclib, ribociclib, abemaciclib, and dalpiciclib have been approved for clinical use, showing substantial improvements in clinical outcomes [2]. Bireociclib is a novel CDK4/6 inhibitor, of which the recent published BRIGHT-1 and BRIGHT-2 study confirmed its efficacy in HR-positive, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative breast cancer [3,4].

There is growing interest in identifying reliable biomarkers to guide the use of CDK4/6 inhibitors. Molecular biomarkers of resistance to CDK4/6 inhibitors include intrinsic markers like RB1 loss and CCNE1 amplification, and acquired markers such as ERBB2 amplification and ESR1 mutations [5]. While these biomarkers can be detected through tumor tissue profiling or circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA), limitations like tissue accessibility and lack of standardized methods pose significant challenges [6].

Peripheral blood offers a readily accessible and non-invasive source for biomarker discovery. The prognostic value of peripheral blood-derived markers, such as neutrophil and lymphocyte counts, as well as albumin, has been extensively studied in multiple malignancies [[7], [8], [9]]. In HR-positive, HER2-negative advanced breast cancer patients treated with palbociclib, analysis from PALOMA-2/3 study indicated that on-treatment derived neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (dNLR) significantly predicts progression-free survival (PFS) [10]. Similar findings were reported for patients treated with abemaciclib in the MONARCH 2 trial [11]. Moreover, several analyses about real-world studies have reported on other markers for the prognostic value [12,13]. However, further analysis of a broader panel of blood-based biomarkers is necessary.

This study aims to utilize data from BRIGHT-2 study on bireociclib to comprehensively investigate peripheral blood indicators as potential prognostic biomarkers for survival outcomes. In addition, on-treatment data were analyzed to explore the associations between dynamic changes in these indicators and therapeutic response in patients receiving bireciclib or placebo plus fulvestrant.

Methods

Study design and population

This study is an exploratory analysis of the BRIGHT-2 trial, a double-blind, randomized, phase Ⅲ clinical trial evaluating bireociclib plus fulvestrant versus placebo plus fulvestrant in patients with HR-positive, HER2-negative advanced breast cancer (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT05077449). The overall trial design, eligibility criteria, and statistical methods have been described previously [4]. This post-hoc analysis used the final study database (data cutoff: February 22, 2024; median follow-up: 18.99 months). This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the institutional ethics committee. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to enrollment.

Data collection and indicator calculation

Baseline clinical and pathological characteristics were documented, including age, performance status, menopausal status, metastatic site, endocrine resistance status, estrogen receptor (ER) status, progesterone receptor (PR) status, HER2 status, and Ki-67 expression. Peripheral blood samples were collected at baseline (prior to treatment) and on day 1 of the 5th cycle (C5D1, on-treatment). Complete blood counts and biochemical parameters were analyzed to calculate the hematological indicators, including absolute lymphocyte count (ALC), NLR, monocyte-to-lymphocyte ratio (MLR), platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR), systemic immune-inflammation index (SII), and prognostic nutritional index (PNI). SII was calculated as platelet count multiplied by neutrophil count and divided by lymphocyte count, while PNI was calculated as [10 × serum albumin (g/dL)] + (5 × lymphocyte count [ × 109/L]).

Study endpoints

The primary endpoint was PFS, defined as the time from randomization to disease progression or death from any cause. The secondary endpoint was overall survival (OS), defined as the time from randomization to death from any cause. Patients were stratified into response or non-response groups based on their best therapeutic outcomes, as assessed by RECIST v1.1. Patients with complete response (CR) and partial response (PR) were classified as the response group, while those with stable disease (SD) and progressive disease (PD) were categorized as the non-response group. Tumor assessments were performed every 8 weeks during the first 64 weeks of treatment and every 12 weeks thereafter. The primary analysis focused on baseline hematological indicators and PFS. Analyses of baseline indicators with OS, dynamic changes in hematological indicators, and interaction tests between biomarkers and treatment arms were secondary analyses.

Statistical analysis

Baseline characteristics were categorized into dichotomous or trichotomous groups, with data presented as counts and percentages for each subgroup. Optimal cut-off values for hematological indicators were determined using the “surv_cutpoint” function from the survminer package based on PFS data from all patients. Patients were categorized into high- and low-level subgroups for each indicator according to these cut-off values. Group comparisons for categorical variables were compared using Fisher's exact tests. For survival outcomes, including PFS and OS, we estimated the median values with 95 % confidence intervals (CIs) using Kaplan-Meier method. The hazard ratios (HRs) with 95 % CIs were calculated using Cox proportional hazards regression models. P values were estimated using the log-rank test. Variables with p values lower than 0.1 in univariate analysis were included in multivariate models. Each hematological indicator was analyzed separately in multivariate Cox models alongside clinical variables to assess its independent prognostic value. To demonstrate the predictive value of hematological indicators in both arms, interaction analyses between treatment and hematological indicators were conducted using “TableSubgroupMultiCox” function from “jstable” package. During this process, the HRs were estimated using an unstratified Cox model with treatment as a factor and the p values were estimated using the Wald test. Dynamic changes in hematological indicators were evaluated using paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test to compare baseline and on-treatment values. Box plots were constructed to visualize changes between response and non-response groups. All analyses were performed using R software (version 4.3.3). Statistical significance was defined as a two-tailed p-value <0.05.

Results

Baseline characteristics

Between December 8, 2021, and October 24, 2022, 404 patients were screened and 305 were enrolled in the BRIGHT-2 trial. Patients were randomized in a 2:1 ratio to receive bireociclib plus fulvestrant (n = 204) or placebo plus fulvestrant (n = 101). Peripheral blood data for all six hematological indicators were available for all patients at both baseline and C5D1. The study flowchart is presented in Fig. 1. Baseline clinicopathological characteristics were generally balanced between the two groups (Table 1). The cut-off values for ALC, NLR, MLR, PLR, SII, and PNI were 2.33 × 109/L, 2.54, 24.55, 170.87, 1000.63, and 52.85, respectively. Baseline characteristics stratified by high- and low-level subgroups of each hematological indicator within each treatment arm are provided in the Supplementary Tables 1–6.

Multivariate Cox analyses identified ALC (p = 0.048), NLR (p = 0.007), MLR (p = 0.003), SII (p = 0.001), and PNI (p = 0.001) as independent prognostic factors for PFS (Supplementary Table 7). For OS, NLR (p = 0.002), MLR (p < 0.001), PLR (p = 0.009), SII (p < 0.001), and PNI (p = 0.008) retained independent prognostic significance (Supplementary Table 8). Among clinicopathological variables, PR positivity, Ki67 < 15 %, and non-visceral metastasis were consistently associated with prolonged PFS, while only non-visceral metastasis remained significant for OS.

Interaction analyses between hematological indicators and treatment arms revealed no significant treatment–biomarker interactions, indicating no predictive value for treatment benefit (Supplementary Figure 1).

Response by dynamic changes in hematological indicators

The dynamic changes of hematological indicators from baseline to on-treatment were analyzed in the different subgroups, according to the therapeutic response. In the bireociclib-treated subgroup (Fig. 4A and B), ALC, NLR, MLR, SII, and PNI levels significantly decreased from baseline to on-treatment (p < 0.001), both in the response and non-response subgroups. Notably, PLR levels remained stable without significant changes for response patients (p = 0.360, Fig. 4A), but significantly increased in the non-response group from baseline to on-treatment (p = 0.003, Fig. 4B). In the control group, none of the indicators demonstrated significant changes from baseline to on-treatment, irrespective of response or non-response group (Fig. 4C and D). The median value and interquartile range (IQR) of every hematological indicator in baseline and on-treatment were listed in Supplementary Table 9. Discussion

This exploratory analysis of the BRIGHT-2 trial highlights the prognostic potential of peripheral hematological indicators in predicting clinical outcomes in patients with HR-positive, HER2-negative metastatic breast cancer receiving fulvestrant with bireociclib or placebo. Specific baseline and dynamic blood parameters correlated significantly with survival outcomes and therapeutic response, underscoring their value as easily accessible prognostic biomarkers, with potential implications for risk stratification and real-world clinical management.

As a novel CDK4/6 inhibitor, bireociclib demonstrated promising efficacy in the BRIGHT-2 phase III study, with a median PFS of 14.69 months (95 % CI, 11.07–20.21) [14]. In this trial, we attempted to identify robust predictive or prognostic biomarkers of treatment response. Since enrolled patients underwent frequent peripheral blood tests, we analyzed peripheral blood–related parameters accordingly. In this post hoc exploratory analysis, six composite indices were derived from lymphocyte, neutrophil, monocyte, and platelet counts, as well as serum albumin levels. Survival analysis showed consistent trends across all baseline indices for PFS, similar trends of NLR, MLR, PLR, SII and PNI for OS, with patients exhibiting higher absolute or relative lymphocyte counts experiencing better outcomes from fulvestrant with or without CDK4/6 inhibitor treatment. This finding has been supported by multiple retrospective studies [12,13,[15], [16], [17], [18], [19]]. In the MONARCH-2 study of abemaciclib, a post-hoc analysis revealed that patients with low NLR or high ALC tended to have better PFS and OS [11]. The association between higher lymphocyte counts and better prognosis is complicated. On the one hand, these patients tend to have a lower incidence of severe hematologic toxicity during treatment, resulting in fewer dose reductions or treatment interruptions [11]. On the other hand, lymphocyte count may reflect the patient's systemic inflammatory response. Higher lymphocyte levels could be associated with increased tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) [20], indicating a more active antitumor immune environment. Compared to TILs, peripheral lymphocyte count is more accessible and may serve as a practical surrogate marker for host immune status. Moreover, CDK4/6 inhibitors have been shown to enhance antitumor immunity by promoting the infiltration of effector T cells into tumors [21] and upregulating immune-related markers such as PD-L1 [22].

The Cox regression analysis indicated that ALC alone was not sufficient to predict OS. Instead, other composite indices incorporating ALC and other peripheral blood parameters were more informative. Specifically, baseline NLR, MLR, SII and PNI were independently associated with PFS and OS. It is noteworthy that the PNI index included not only peripheral lymphocyte count but also serum albumin level. This finding suggests that both systemic inflammatory and nutritional status should be evaluated when considering prognosis, and early nutritional support or anti-inflammatory interventions could be explored in future clinical trials to improve outcomes in patients receiving CDK4/6 inhibitors. A previous study also identified PNI as a prognostic marker in breast cancer patients treated with CDK4/6 inhibitors, with a cut-off of 50.4, similar to ours of 52.45 [23]. Regarding inflammatory status, elevated relative levels of neutrophils and monocytes indicate a heightened tumor-promoting systemic inflammatory response and were associated with worse prognosis. These findings are consistent with previous retrospective studies in breast cancer, which have demonstrated the relevance of these markers to both response to neoadjuvant therapy and long-term survival outcomes [24,25]. From a clinical perspective, dynamic monitoring of PLR and other indices during therapy might help identify non-responders earlier, supporting timely treatment modification before overt disease progression.

Beyond baseline values, we further examined the dynamic changes of these markers during treatment. Notably, all indices except PLR exhibited a downward trend among patients receiving CDK4/6 inhibitors. While PLR remained stable in responders, it markedly increased in non-responders, indicating its potential as a dynamic indicator of poor treatment response. This downward trend was not prominent in control group for all the indicators. This is potentially because fulvestrant monotherapy has minimal impact on peripheral blood counts and albumin levels, thereby failing to show the pronounced decreasing trends in peripheral blood markers like the interventional group. Besides, the host systemic inflammatory response has been shown to play a critical role in tumor progression and has been associated with prognosis across various types of malignancies [26,27]. In breast cancer, a meta-analysis from Savioli et al. indicated that higher preoperative PLR was related to worse OS and disease-free survival (DFS) in primary breast cancer [28]. Also in metastatic breast cancer, previous studies have established the status of PLR as a predictive factor of patients with different molecular subtypes [[29], [30], [31]]. Another study involving patients treated with CDK4/6 inhibitors reported a similar trend in baseline PLR as observed in our study. However, a decrease in PLR from baseline was associated with worse OS [13], indicating its protecting effect. The difference in on-treatment assessment timepoints may contribute to the discrepancy, as our evaluation was conducted at cycle 5 day 1, a later stage compared to the 4-week timepoint used in their study. This difference suggests that the prognostic implication of PLR dynamics may vary depending on the treatment phase and the extent of immune and inflammatory modulation by CDK4/6 inhibitors over time.

Given the observed associations between baseline inflammatory and nutritional indices and patient outcomes, it is worth exploring whether early interventions targeting these parameters could potentially improve prognosis in patients receiving CDK4/6 inhibitors. In the field of breast cancer, several dietary intervention trials have been conducted, primarily focusing on reducing fat intake and promoting weight loss [32,33]. However, our findings suggest that attention should also be paid to improving serum albumin levels as a marker of nutritional status. For breast cancer patients receiving CDK4/6 inhibitors, regular monitoring of peripheral blood parameters before and during treatment is not only essential for assessing treatment safety but may also provide valuable prognostic and predictive information regarding treatment response.

Several limitations of this exploratory analysis should be acknowledged. First, although this study utilized data from the randomized BRIGHT-2 phase Ⅲ trial, the analysis of peripheral blood biomarkers was conducted retrospectively and was exploratory in nature. Thus, prospective validation is essential to confirm the prognostic and predictive values of these hematological indices. Second, the sample size, while substantial, may still limit the statistical power to detect subtle associations, especially for subgroup analyses. Additionally, variability in laboratory assessments and patient characteristics across participating centers might have introduced potential confounding effects. Despite these limitations, our study identified baseline and dynamic peripheral blood parameters as promising indicators of prognosis and treatment response for HR-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer treated with CDK4/6 inhibitors.

AI-driven solar energy forecasting using meteorological variables: Insights from a 22 MW PV plant in Nakhchivan
Authors: Ertuğrul Adıgüzel, Nadir Subaşı, Javanshir Zeynalov, Maftun Aliyev, Sevinj Rzayeva, Rüya Şamlı, Aysel Ersoy
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study presents a systematic comparative evaluation of ten regression-based machine-learning models for day-ahead photovoltaic (PV) energy forecasting under semi-arid climatic conditions. The analysis is conducted using a limited six month dataset (May–October 2024) of real operational production data obtained from a 22 MW grid-connected PV plant in Nakhchivan, Azerbaijan, integrated with key meteorological variables including solar irradiance, air temperature, relative humidity, and wind speed. Linear Regression, Ridge, Lasso, ElasticNet, Support Vector Regression (SVR), Decision Tree, Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, XGBoost, and a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) were benchmarked using mean squared error (MSE), root mean squared error (RMSE), mean absolute error (MAE), coefficient of determination (R2), and five-fold cross-validated R2. The results indicate that regularised linear models—particularly Lasso Regression—provide the most consistent balance between predictive accuracy and generalisation stability under moderate data availability, while Random Forest demonstrates strong cross-validated robustness and the MLP exhibits overfitting behaviour, highlighting sensitivity to limited training data. These findings demonstrate that increased model complexity does not necessarily translate into improved forecasting reliability in data-constrained semi-arid environments. The novelty of this work lies in its unified benchmarking framework that simultaneously evaluates predictive accuracy, interpretability, and generalisation performance using real-world utility-scale operational data. By explicitly linking forecasting reliability with sustainability-oriented planning, the study contributes to more reliable life-cycle cost and emission assessment of large-scale PV systems in emerging renewable-energy markets. The findings offer practical guidance for energy planners and policy-makers seeking transparent, computationally efficient forecasting strategies in semi-arid climates.Keywords: solar energy, meteorological analysis, photovoltaic forecasting, machine learning, regression models, model comparison

1. Introduction

Accurate day-ahead solar energy forecasting is essential for grid stability, operational planning, and economic dispatch in renewable-rich power systems. Photovoltaic (PV) power generation exhibits high temporal variability due to meteorological conditions, making reliable prediction challenging yet critical for system operators. As the share of solar energy in global electricity generation continues to expand, the need for robust forecasting methodologies has become increasingly urgent. Machine learning (ML) approaches have emerged as powerful tools for capturing complex nonlinear relationships between weather variables and PV output, enabling improved forecasting accuracy and operational efficiency. Unlike traditional statistical methods, ML algorithms can automatically learn patterns from historical data without requiring explicit physical models, making them suitable for diverse climatic conditions and operational settings.

Recent studies further demonstrate the expanding role of artificial intelligence in PV systems, not only in forecasting but also in operational optimisation. For example, computational-intelligence-based forecasting strategies have been proposed for solar output prediction, 1 while AI-integrated energy-management systems have been developed for grid-connected applications. 2 In addition, intelligent control frameworks combining neuro-fuzzy systems with optimally tuned PI controllers have been introduced to enhance DC–DC converter performance in photovoltaic systems, 3 illustrating the broader integration of AI techniques across both predictive and control layers of solar energy infrastructures. Comprehensive methodological reviews further confirm the rapid evolution of data-driven forecasting paradigms.4,5

Comparative studies have evaluated a wide range of ML algorithms for PV forecasting, including regularised linear models such as Ridge, Lasso, and ElasticNet, 6 ensemble techniques including Random Forests 7 and XGBoost, 8 as well as deep-learning-based architectures. Benchmark analyses indicate that model performance varies substantially according to data characteristics, temporal resolution, and climatic conditions.9,10 Recent evidence also shows that simpler regularised models may outperform more complex ensemble methods under data-limited conditions, 11 whereas hybrid approaches can enhance generalisation in large-scale applications. For instance, empirical comparisons in rooftop photovoltaic systems have demonstrated that ensemble models may exhibit sensitivity to limited training data, while appropriately regularised models can offer competitive and stable performance. 12 In parallel, advanced neural-network-based architectures—such as similar-day clustering combined with hybrid neural optimisation techniques—have been proposed to improve short-term PV prediction accuracy under highly variable irradiance conditions. 13 However, the trade-off between model complexity, interpretability, and predictive performance in data-constrained environments remains insufficiently examined. Furthermore, most comparative studies have focused on temperate or humid climates, leaving a gap in understanding how these algorithms perform under semi-arid conditions where dust, temperature extremes, and seasonal irradiance variability pose unique challenges. Moreover, emerging AIoT-based monitoring and predictive-control frameworks highlight the increasing integration of forecasting models with real-time system management infrastructures, 14 reinforcing the need for context-specific empirical validation of model behaviour.

The Nakhchivan Autonomous Republic of Azerbaijan, characterised by a semi-arid climate with high solar irradiance and low precipitation, presents both opportunities and operational challenges for photovoltaic deployment. The region benefits from abundant solar radiation throughout the year, rendering it suitable for utility-scale PV installations. Previous site-selection research has identified favourable areas for large-scale solar deployment based on topographical, climatic, and infrastructural criteria, 15 while regional energy assessments emphasise the potential for expanding renewable capacity to reduce fossil-fuel dependency and strengthen long-term sustainability. 16 In addition, lifecycle-based viability analyses of large-scale solar farms in semi-arid regions have demonstrated that energy-yield variability directly influences economic and environmental sustainability indicators, 17 further underscoring the importance of accurate operational forecasting.

Accurate short-term forecasting reduces uncertainty in projected annual energy yield, which directly affects life-cycle cost (LCC), levelised cost of electricity (LCOE), and emission-offset calculations in large-scale photovoltaic investments. Therefore, improving operational forecasting accuracy is not solely a technical objective but also a prerequisite for enhancing the reliability of sustainability and long-term viability assessments in semi-arid solar markets.

However, region-specific environmental factors—including dust deposition, elevated ambient temperature, and low relative humidity—can significantly degrade PV performance in arid climates.18–21 Dust accumulation reduces optical transmittance, and thermal stress decreases conversion efficiency. These climatic constraints underscore the need for forecasting frameworks that explicitly incorporate local meteorological variability, seasonal transitions, and operational limitations inherent to semi-arid environments.

Despite substantial progress in PV forecasting research, comparatively few studies have systematically examined machine-learning model performance under semi-arid climatic conditions with limited data availability. Most benchmark investigations remain concentrated in temperate regions and rely on multi-year datasets, which do not accurately represent the operational constraints of emerging solar markets where historical observations are sparse and climatic characteristics differ markedly. The comparative behaviour of regularised linear models and ensemble approaches in data-limited, semi-arid settings also remains insufficiently evaluated. Although ensemble algorithms are recognised for their predictive robustness and high accuracy, they typically require larger datasets and greater computational resources, raising concerns about their suitability for small-scale or recently commissioned PV plants. Although recent methodological surveys provide broad overviews of forecasting techniques,4,5 empirical evidence from semi-arid, data-constrained operational contexts remains limited. This methodological gap restricts the capacity of regional decision-makers to select appropriate forecasting models based on empirical evidence relevant to comparable climatic and operational conditions. Moreover, the interpretability of ML-based forecasting models—an essential factor in practical energy-management workflows—has received comparatively limited attention in prior comparative analyses.

While numerous studies have benchmarked machine-learning models for photovoltaic forecasting in temperate and data-rich environments, comparatively limited attention has been given to semi-arid regions characterised by climatic variability and moderate data availability. Existing literature largely emphasises model accuracy under extensive historical datasets, leaving an open question regarding the trade-off between model complexity, interpretability, and generalisation stability in emerging solar markets. Addressing this gap requires a systematic and empirically grounded comparison under realistic operational constraints.

Despite the growing body of literature on photovoltaic forecasting, a critical limitation remains in the lack of integrated evaluation frameworks that simultaneously assess predictive accuracy, generalisation stability, and model interpretability under data-constrained semi-arid conditions. Most existing studies rely on large-scale datasets or prioritise predictive accuracy without explicitly addressing the trade-offs between model complexity and generalisation performance. Consequently, the selection of appropriate forecasting models for emerging solar markets with limited historical data remains insufficiently supported by region-specific empirical evidence. This study addresses this gap by providing a systematic and operationally grounded comparison of ten regression-based machine-learning models using real-world PV data from a semi-arid environment.

Unlike many existing benchmark studies that primarily report comparative performance metrics, this study provides a deeper scientific insight into the relationship between model complexity and generalisation capability under data-constrained conditions. The results demonstrate that increased model complexity does not necessarily lead to improved forecasting performance in semi-arid environments with limited data availability. Instead, regularised linear models can outperform more complex ensemble and neural-network-based approaches by achieving a more stable balance between bias and variance. This finding challenges the common assumption in the literature that more sophisticated models inherently yield superior predictive performance and highlights the importance of aligning model selection with dataset scale and climatic characteristics.

2. Materials and methods

This study was conducted using real-world energy production and meteorological data obtained from a 22 MW PV power plant located in the Nakhchivan Autonomous Republic of Azerbaijan. The main objective was to evaluate and compare the predictive accuracy of multiple machine-learning algorithms in forecasting the plant’s daily energy output.

2.2. Methods

In this study, ten regression-based machine-learning algorithms were implemented to forecast daily solar-energy production. These include Linear Regression, Ridge Regression, Lasso Regression, ElasticNet, SVR, Decision Tree, Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, XGBoost, and MLP. All models were trained and tested on a unified dataset combining daily energy output with corresponding meteorological variables—namely solar irradiance, temperature, humidity, and wind speed.

To ensure comparability, identical preprocessing steps were applied, including normalisation of continuous variables and removal of missing records. Model performance was assessed using five evaluation metrics: Mean Squared Error (MSE), Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Coefficient of Determination (R2), and five-fold cross-validated R2 (CV R2).

To ensure methodological consistency with time-series forecasting principles and to avoid data leakage, cross-validation was implemented using a time-aware rolling-origin (walk-forward) strategy rather than conventional random k-fold splitting. In this approach, the training set is progressively expanded while preserving temporal order, and each validation fold strictly uses future observations relative to the training data. This strategy provides a more realistic and unbiased estimation of out-of-sample forecasting performance for sequential photovoltaic production data. In each iteration, an expanding training window was used, where the model was retrained by progressively incorporating new observations while maintaining strict temporal ordering.

It should be noted that the reported five-fold cross-validation (CV R2) results were not obtained using random k-fold splitting. Instead, the dataset was partitioned into five sequential folds following a walk-forward validation scheme to preserve temporal order and prevent data leakage.

To ensure a fully balanced comparison across model families, hyperparameter configurations for ensemble and neural-network models were selected using a structured tuning procedure. For Random Forest and Gradient Boosting models, the number of estimators, maximum tree depth, and learning rate were adjusted within predefined ranges using cross-validation to avoid overfitting. For XGBoost, regularisation parameters and tree depth were similarly explored within moderate bounds to maintain comparability with other ensemble methods. The Multi-Layer Perceptron architecture was implemented with a single hidden layer and standard activation functions, and its learning rate and regularisation strength were tuned within conservative ranges to prevent excessive optimisation relative to linear models. No model was excessively fine-tuned beyond reasonable cross-validated optimisation limits, ensuring methodological fairness across linear, ensemble, and neural-network approaches.

The Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) hyperparameters—including network size, learning rate, and regularisation strength—were tuned within conservative ranges to ensure a fair comparison with other model families and to avoid excessive optimisation bias. No model was excessively fine-tuned beyond reasonable cross-validated optimisation limits, ensuring methodological fairness across linear, ensemble, and neural-network approaches.

This multi-model framework enables a robust and unbiased evaluation of prediction accuracy and generalisation capability under real-world climatic variability.

The feature set was intentionally restricted to primary meteorological variables to evaluate the direct physical impact of semi-arid climatic conditions on energy production. While temporal and lag features can capture historical trends, our primary objective was to ensure model transparency and efficiency in a data-constrained operational environment, where direct weather dependencies are the most critical factors.

To further evaluate whether the observed differences in model performance are statistically significant, the Diebold–Mariano (DM) test was applied to pairwise forecast error series of selected models. The DM test assesses the null hypothesis that two competing forecasting models have equal predictive accuracy. In this study, the test was conducted using squared error loss, and statistical significance was determined based on the corresponding p-values at the 5% significance level using a two-sided test.

3. Results

This section presents the analytical results obtained from six months of operational data (May–October 2024), including both the PV energy-production records and meteorological parameters measured at the 22 MW solar power plant in Nakhchivan. The analysis first explores correlations between environmental variables and energy output, followed by comparative evaluations of ten machine-learning regression models.

The comparative analysis was performed on a refined dataset of 178 operational days (May–October 2024) after rigorous preprocessing.

3.1. Correlation analysis

The correlation relationships between meteorological variables and daily electricity generation were analysed to identify the most influential factors affecting PV output. Positive and negative correlations are represented in red and blue, respectively. The strongest positive association was observed between maximum and average air temperature (r = 0.96), while sunshine duration showed a substantial correlation with maximum solar radiation (r = 0.61). A pronounced negative correlation was identified between the month variable and sunshine duration (r = –0.83). Daily electricity production exhibited a moderate positive association with maximum air temperature (r = 0.37) and a weak association with sunshine duration (r = 0.14). These findings indicate that solar irradiance and ambient temperature are the principal climatic factors influencing PV output.

3.2. Distribution of production and meteorological variables

The relationships between total energy production and key meteorological variables—including solar radiation, ambient temperature, relative humidity, and wind speed—were analysed to assess their influence on PV performance. Clear positive trends are observed between solar radiation and temperature and daily production, indicating that higher irradiance and warmer atmospheric conditions enhance PV performance. Relative humidity and wind speed exhibit more dispersed patterns, suggesting weaker or non-linear effects. In addition, temporal variations indicate seasonal shifts, where production remains relatively stable during July–August but declines markedly in September–October, reflecting reduced irradiance and shorter daylight duration.

4. Discussion

The comparative evaluation of ten regression-based machine-learning models revealed clear performance differences in forecasting daily PV energy production under Nakhchivan’s semi-arid climatic conditions. Lasso Regression demonstrated the most favourable overall accuracy, achieving the lowest MSE and RMSE values and the highest R2, indicating that regularisation plays a critical role in mitigating overfitting when meteorological variables exhibit cross-correlation. This finding aligns with previous studies reporting the effectiveness of regularised linear models in small-to medium-sized environmental datasets, particularly when multicollinearity is present and data availability is limited.

SVR achieved the lowest MAE, suggesting that it captures fine-scale deviations more effectively than the other models. However, its relatively weak performance in MSE and R2 indicates that while SVR limits average absolute errors, it struggles to minimise large mispredictions, possibly due to sensitivity to kernel parameters under limited data. Random Forest exhibited the strongest cross-validated R2, demonstrating superior generalisation capability across varying train–test splits. This outcome is consistent with the known robustness of ensemble-based models, which reduce variance by aggregating multiple decision trees. Nevertheless, ensemble models did not outperform regularised linear models in overall accuracy, reinforcing the importance of dataset size in determining the relative strengths of model families.

In contrast, the MLP model displayed substantial performance degradation across all metrics, reflecting severe overfitting. This behaviour is expected when deep-learning methods are applied to moderate-sized datasets without extensive tuning or regularisation. Previous research similarly highlights that neural networks often require larger datasets, optimised architectures, and targeted hyperparameter adjustment to avoid convergence instability and overfitting in renewable-energy forecasting tasks. The present results thus emphasise that simpler models can outperform more complex architectures under data-limited conditions and provide more interpretable parameter structures for operational decision-making.

The broader implications of these findings relate to model selection strategies for PV forecasting in semi-arid and data-constrained regions. Nakhchivan’s characteristic climatic patterns—high irradiance in summer, abrupt declines in autumn, and strong temperature–irradiance coupling—create seasonal transitions that forecasting models must account for. The strong correlation observed between temperature and irradiance suggests that models overly reliant on non-linear transformations or high-dimensional feature space expansion may not yield additional value. Instead, regularised linear approaches provide a robust balance between interpretability, computational efficiency, and accuracy, making them well suited for deployment in grid operations and planning environments where transparency is essential.

The comparative analysis reveals that model selection is inherently tied to the specific performance objectives of the PV plant operator. Lasso Regression was ultimately identified as the most reliable model because it dominated the metrics sensitive to large errors (MSE and RMSE) and provided the highest explanatory power ( R2 ). Practically, Lasso is the optimal choice when the priority is to minimize the economic risks associated with large forecasting deviations, which can lead to significant inaccuracies in Levelized Cost of Electricity (LCOE) and revenue estimation. However, under different operational constraints, other models may offer distinct advantages:

SVR for Average Error Minimization: If the objective is to maintain consistent, small-scale accuracy throughout the day rather than penalizing large outliers, SVR is preferable due to its superior performance in MAE.

Random Forest for Robustness: In scenarios where the meteorological data is highly volatile or the training set is inconsistent, Random Forest should be considered. Its higher cross-validated R2 indicates a superior ability to generalize across different temporal folds, providing a more stable performance profile against variance.

Linear Models for Resource-Constrained Environments: In emerging markets where computational resources are limited, the efficiency and transparency of regularized linear models (Lasso, Ridge) provide a more sustainable path than complex ensemble or neural network architectures.

The observed superiority of regularised linear models under moderate data availability may extend to other semi-arid regions previously evaluated in lifecycle-based viability studies, such as Rustaq–Juma. 17 In climatic contexts characterised by high irradiance stability and strong temperature–irradiance coupling, robust linear models can provide reliable prediction performance without requiring high-complexity architectures. Improved forecasting accuracy directly reduces uncertainty in projected annual energy yield, which consequently lowers error propagation in life-cycle assessment (LCA), life-cycle cost (LCC), and emission-offset calculations of large-scale photovoltaic systems. Therefore, operational day-ahead forecasting reliability is not only a technical performance indicator but also a critical component of long-term economic feasibility and sustainability evaluation of PV investments.

In this context, forecasting error directly influences both economic and environmental sustainability assessments. Even moderate deviations in predicted energy output can lead to significant inaccuracies in Levelized Cost of Electricity (LCOE), revenue estimation, and investment decision-making. Overestimation of production may result in underestimated costs and overly optimistic financial projections, whereas underestimation may lead to conservative planning and suboptimal resource allocation. Similarly, inaccuracies in energy forecasting affect CO2 emission reduction estimates and life-cycle sustainability metrics, since avoided emissions are typically derived from projected energy generation. Therefore, systematic forecasting errors may lead to either overestimation or underestimation of environmental benefits, highlighting that forecasting accuracy is a critical requirement not only for technical optimisation but also for reliable economic and environmental evaluation of large-scale PV systems. 17

Furthermore, the results highlight the importance of incorporating seasonal indicators, periodic variables, or lagged meteorological features to account for the pronounced intra-annual variability observed in the dataset. Integrating such seasonal structure could enhance the performance of both linear and ensemble models, particularly in capturing the steep production declines detected in September and October. Hybrid or stacking approaches that combine linear and ensemble models may also offer improved accuracy while retaining interpretability.

Overall, the findings demonstrate that Lasso Regression provides the most balanced performance across error magnitude, explanatory power, and generalisation; SVR minimises absolute deviations; Random Forest exhibits strong validation stability; and MLP is unsuitable for the current dataset due to overfitting. These insights underline the necessity of aligning model complexity with data characteristics and operational constraints and reinforce the value of multi-metric evaluation when selecting forecasting models for PV systems in semi-arid climates such as Nakhchivan.

To further demonstrate the interpretability of the proposed framework, the coefficients of the Lasso Regression model were analyzed. Unlike the MLP model, which operates as a ‘black-box,’ the Lasso model provides explicit weights for each meteorological variable. Solar radiation and maximum temperature were identified as the primary drivers of PV output, aligning with the physical behavior of the system and the initial correlation analysis. This transparency allows grid operators to understand the logic behind day-ahead forecasts.

From a broader methodological perspective, this study contributes to the ongoing debate regarding model complexity versus generalisation performance in machine-learning-based energy forecasting. The findings suggest that, in data-constrained environments, simpler and regularised models may offer more reliable and interpretable solutions compared to highly complex architectures, thereby providing a practical and theoretically meaningful contribution to the field.

It is important to acknowledge that the dataset used in this study covers a limited six-month period and does not fully capture seasonal variability across an entire annual cycle. As a result, the findings primarily reflect the model performance under the specific climatic conditions observed during the analysed timeframe. The absence of full seasonal representation—particularly winter and transitional periods—may influence the generalisation of the results. This limitation is critical when interpreting the results, as the models have not been evaluated under full annual climatic variability, and therefore their performance may differ under unobserved seasonal conditions. Accordingly, the findings should not be directly generalised to year-round forecasting performance without further validation using multi-year datasets. Therefore, the conclusions drawn in this study should be interpreted within the context of the available data and should not be overgeneralised to all seasonal conditions or different climatic regions without further validation. Future studies should incorporate multi-year datasets to ensure more comprehensive seasonal coverage and to enhance the robustness of model evaluation. 24

5. Conclusions

This study conducted a comprehensive comparative assessment of ten regression-based machine-learning models for forecasting daily PV energy production in the semi-arid climate of Nakhchivan, using six months of operational data from a 22 MW solar power plant. The findings demonstrate that regularised linear models—particularly Lasso Regression—achieved the most consistent and accurate performance across the examined metrics. Lasso delivered the lowest MSE and RMSE values and achieved the highest R2 among the tested models, confirming its suitability for data-limited environments characterised by correlated meteorological variables. SVR offered the lowest MAE, reflecting superior performance in reducing average absolute deviations, whereas Random Forest achieved the highest cross-validated R2, indicating strong generalisation capability. In contrast, the MLP model exhibited substantial overfitting and poor predictive accuracy, underscoring the limitations of neural-network-based approaches when applied to moderate-sized datasets without extensive optimisation.

These results indicate that, under data-constrained semi-arid conditions, model simplicity and regularisation can provide more reliable forecasting performance than complex architectures.

Regularised linear models therefore emerge as practical and efficient solutions for operational PV forecasting, while ensemble methods may be preferable when larger datasets are available.

Future research should focus on incorporating seasonal and temporal features—such as lagged variables, moving averages, or periodic indicators—to improve the representation of intra-annual variability. The integration of probabilistic forecasting frameworks, hybrid linear–ensemble architectures, and satellite-derived irradiance data may further enhance prediction accuracy. Additionally, expanding the analysis to include finer temporal resolutions or multi-year datasets could improve the generalisability of the models and support more robust grid-integration strategies.

Although the current models rely solely on instantaneous meteorological inputs, the inclusion of temporal indicators or lagged production features represents a significant opportunity for future enhancement. Such features could help mitigate the impact of abrupt seasonal transitions, like those observed in September and October, by allowing the models to learn from the sequential dependencies of the data.

This study also highlights that improved forecasting accuracy contributes to reducing uncertainty in long-term economic and environmental assessments of large-scale PV systems, supporting more informed decision-making in renewable-energy planning.

Beyond model comparison, the results emphasise the importance of aligning forecasting strategies with dataset size, climatic characteristics, and application requirements.

However, it should be noted that the dataset is limited to a six-month observation period, and therefore the results may not fully represent seasonal variability across a complete annual cycle. Accordingly, the reported findings reflect model performance only within the observed temporal window and should not be directly generalised to year-round forecasting scenarios without further validation using extended and multi-seasonal datasets.

Sarcopenia assessed by computed tomography or magnetic resonance imaging is associated with the loss of response to biologic therapies in adult patients with Crohn's disease
Authors: Jingjing Liu, Hongye Tang, Tingting Lin, Jiangchuan Wang, Wenjing Cui, Chao Xie, Zhongqiu Wang, Yugen Chen, Xiao Chen
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractSarcopenia occurs in patients with Crohn's disease (CD). However, the association between sarcopenia and loss of response (LOR) to biologic agents remains unclear. This study explored such an association in CD patients. This retrospective study included 94 CD patients who received biologic therapy. The skeletal muscle cross‐sectional area at the third lumbar was assessed by computed tomography or magnetic resonance imaging for sarcopenia evaluation. A LOR was defined by fecal calprotectin (FC) < 250 μg/g or >50% reduction from baseline levels or other factors, such as the used agent being replaced by other biologic agents. The association between sarcopenia and LOR was assessed by logistic regression analysis. LOR was observed in 54 patients (57.4%). The prevalence of sarcopenia in the LOR group was higher than that in response group (70.4% vs. 40.0%, p = 0.003). Sarcopenia (odds ratio [OR] = 3.89, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 1.31–11.54), Montreal L1 type (OR = 0.20, 95% CI: 0.06–0.60), perianal lesions (OR = 4.08, 95% CI: 1.31–12.70), and monocytes percentage (OR = 1.27, 95% CI: 1.02–1.57) at baseline were independent associated factors for LOR. Sarcopenia was also associated with LOR in patients who received infliximab (OR = 3.31, 95% CI: 1.11–9.87). Montreal L1 type, perianal lesions, and monocytes percentage (Model 1), and with additional consideration of sarcopenia (Model 2), were developed to predict LOR. Model 2 showed better performance than Model 1 (area under the curve [AUC] 0.82 vs. 0.75). Sarcopenia was associated with the LOR to biological agents or infliximab in adult patients with CD.

INTRODUCTION

Crohn's disease (CD) is a chronic immune‐mediated and transmural inflammation that can involve any segment of the gastrointestinal tract, especially the terminal ileum. CD prevalence is high in Europe (322 per 100,000 in Germany) and North America (319 per 100,000 in Canada; 214 per 100,000 in the USA). 1 , 2 The incidence of CD is relatively low in China, 1 varying from 0.07 to 1.36 per 100,000 people. However, the prevalence and incidence of CD are increasing at an alarming rate worldwide. 3 The treatment strategies for CD are not only used to control the symptoms but also to achieve deep and long‐lasting remission. 2 Corticosteroids, immunosuppressants, and anti‐adhesion molecules have been used to treat CD. Biologics, including tumor necrosis factor antagonists (anti‐TNF), vedolizumab, and ustekinumab, are also widely used. 2 Infliximab, an anti‐TNF agent, was the first biologic approved for CD treatment in 1998. 4 Anti‐TNF is effective at inducing and maintaining remission and is preferred as a first‐line therapy for patients with moderate‐to‐severe CD. 5 , 6

Anti‐TNF treatment failure is common in patients with inflammatory bowel disease (IBD). A primary nonresponse to anti‐TNF therapy occurred in 13%–40% of patients. 7 , 8 Additionally, 63.1% of patients experienced non‐remission within the first year. 9 A high incidence of loss of response (LOR) to vedolizumab was also reported (47.9 per 100 person‐years of follow‐up). 10 The mechanism of the LOR to biologics is uncertain and may be related to different TNF family genes subtype. 11 Identifying the associated factors that can predict responses to therapies is important when making personalized medicine decisions. 2 Several risk factors for LOR have also been reported 8 , 12 , 13 such as treatment duration and disease activity after induction. Nevertheless, in clinical practice, predicting which patients will respond to biologic therapy remains challenging.

Sarcopenia is usually an aging‐related disorder that results in a loss of muscle mass or function. 14 It is also highly prevalent (26.4%–68.3%) in CD patients 15 , 16 , 17 , 18 due to their chronic inflammation, gut microbiota, impaired protein synthesis, malabsorption and malnutrition, corticoid use, and insufficient physical activity due to fatigue. 19 , 20 , 21 Sarcopenia is associated with poorer outcomes, re‐hospitalization, higher rates of surgery, and postoperative complications in patients with IBDs. 15 , 22 , 23 , 24 Accordingly, sarcopenia is an important aspect to consider in the management of CD, both in prediction and in treatment. Computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is usually adopted for diagnosis in CD patients. Sarcopenia can be evaluated during CT or MRI examinations. However, very little is known about the impact of sarcopenia on responses to biologic agents in CD patients. Therefore, our aim was to evaluate the association between sarcopenia (evaluated using CT or MRI) and LOR to biologic agents in CD patients and we also developed a model to predict the LOR.

METHODS

Patients

Patients with CD that were treated with biologic agents at the Affiliated Hospital of Nanjing University of Chinese Medicine between July 2019 and December 2021 were included in this retrospective study. CD was diagnosed based on the World Gastroenterology Organization Practice Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Management of IBD. 25 The exclusion criteria included: (1) being younger than 18 years old; (2) a lack of baseline abdominal imaging examinations, laboratory tests, and endoscopic pathology; (3) failing loss to follow‐up within 5 months; (4) having patients with chronic kidney disease, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, a malignant tumor, congestive heart failure, neuromuscular disease, activity limitation, or prolonged braking; (5) a treatment history of biologic agents; (6) an infliximab allergy; and (7) being pregnant and/or lactating. The flowchart of study patients is shown in Figure 1. A total of 284 CD patients were found in our medical records, and 94 patients were finally included in this study. The ethics committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Nanjing University of Chinese Medicine approved this study, and informed consent was waived because of the retrospective study design.

Flowchart of study patients: 284 patients with Crohn's disease (CD) were found in the medical database; 94 patients with biologic therapy were finally included. CTE, computed tomography enterography; MRE, magnetic resonance enterography.

Data collection

The patient demographics, biologic agents use, baseline laboratory tests (routine blood, biochemistry, erythrocyte sedimentation rate [ESR], C‐reactive protein [CRP], coagulation, fecal calprotectin, and occult blood) and endoscopic granuloma detection rates were collected from the electronic medical record system. The dynamic fecal calprotectin (FC) levels were also recorded before the biologic therapy and 26 (±4) weeks after biologic therapy. The FC levels at 12 weeks were also collected for 85 patients. The Montreal classification was evaluated by using endoscopy, computed tomography enterography (CTE), or magnetic resonance enterography (MRE) and an anorectal MRI. The CTE (n = 55) or MRE (n = 35) within 1 month prior to biologic treatment were obtained from the picture archiving and communication system workstation.

Measurement of skeletal muscle parameters and imaging parameters

The skeletal muscle (SM) cross‐sectional area (CSA) at the third lumbar (L3) was measured by a single operator who was blinded to the clinical and biologic therapies using the free public domain software developed by the National Institutes of Health (NIH Image J 1.47), which can automatically segment the SM by defining the threshold (−29 Hounsfield units (HU), 150 HU). 26 In the MRI image, the muscle intensity was obviously different from other tissue. The Image J software could segment muscle based on the signal intensity. Thirty images were randomly selected for inter‐observer and intra‐observer agreement assessments. In addition, the subcutaneous fat thicknesses (SFT) around the navel and abdominal circumference at the transumbilical level were measured. The measurements of skeletal muscle area, SFT, and abdominal circumference are shown in Figure S1.

Image analysis

The location of the diseased bowel segment, the thickness of the wall of the most severe bowel segment, and whether the wall was complicated with strictures, penetrations, and perianal lesions were recorded. The images were analyzed by a well‐trained abdominal radiologist who was blinded to the clinical and laboratory data.

Definitions

The skeletal muscle index (SMI) (cm2/m2) was calculated by dividing the CSA of the SM at the 3rd vertebra by its height squared (m2) (SMI‐L3). As previously reported in a multicenter study in China, 27 sarcopenia was defined by using the SMI: SMI was <32.5 cm2/m2 in women and <44.77 cm2/m2 in men. The response and LOR to biologic agents were defined based on biochemical remission after about 12 weeks and 26 weeks (22–30 weeks) of treatment (biochemical remission: FC < 250 μg/g or >50% reduction from baseline levels). 28 , 29 , 30 If a LOR occurred at 12 weeks, the loss was a primary LOR. If the LOR occurred at 26 weeks, the loss was a secondary LOR. Both primary LOR and secondary LOR were included in our final analysis. If FC data were not collected during the follow‐up, any of the following events were considered a LOR: additional immunomodulators treatment or the used agent being replaced by other biologic agents, CD‐related surgery, and non‐remission or progression in imaging or endoscopy. If data on any of the above events were also not available, the patient was excluded.

Statistical analysis

The quantitative variables are presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD) or the median (interquartile range) depending on data distribution. The Kolmogorov–Smirnov test was used to analyze the normality of the data distribution. Differences between the response and LOR groups were assessed by using Student's t test or Mann–Whitney U test. The categorical variables are presented as numbers and percentages (%), and were analyzed by using chi‐square (χ 2) tests or Fisher's exact test. Those factors were significantly different between the two groups and the factors with important clinical significance for CD, such as age, sex, Montreal location and behavior, perianal lesions, granuloma detection, fecal occult blood, and calprotectin, were evaluated by using a a univariable logistic regression analysis. Then, multivariable logistic regressions were performed to analyze the associations between sarcopenia and the LOR prevalence. The collinearity was evaluated by using linear regression analysis. The goodness‐of‐fit was evaluated by using the Hosmer–Lemeshow test. Sensitivity analysis was performed on the data from the patients that received infliximab therapy. The odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were reported. The sample size of subgroup analysis was small. Therefore, only those factors with a significant difference in whole analysis were included in subgroup analysis. Based on the independent associated factors that were identified by the results of logistic regression analysis, we developed models to predict the LOR. The receiver–operating characteristic (ROC) curve was plotted, and the area under the ROC curve (AUC) was calculated to assess the predictive performance of the models. For clinical applications, a nomogram was then developed based on the independent associated factors using R software (version 3.6). Calibration curves were constructed to show the agreement between the predicted risk and observed outcomes using R software (version 3.6). A discriminant analysis was also performed to show the value of sarcopenia in predicting LOR. A two‐sided test with p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. SPSS version 25.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) was used for all the statistical analyses.

RESULTS

Patient baseline characteristics

A total of 94 adult patients with histologically confirmed CD and who received biologic treatment were included. The median age was 26.5 (22.0, 34.0) years at diagnosis. Sixty‐one (64.8%) individuals were male. The FC levels at the baseline and follow‐up are shown in Figure S2. The LOR was evaluated by FC levels in 90 patients. LOR was considered in four patients because the used agent was replaced by other biologic agents. LOR occurred in 54 patients (57.4%). Table 1 illustrates the baseline characteristics of patients. The Montreal L1 type was less common in patients with a LOR (p = 0.014). The prevalence of CT‐ and MRI‐based sarcopenia was 57.4% in patients with CD. The prevalence of sarcopenia in patients with LOR was significantly higher than in those with a response (70.4 vs. 40.0%, p = 0.003). There was no significant differences in age, gender, marital history, and tobacco and alcohol use between the two groups. The baseline FC level in the patients with a LOR was higher than in the patients with a response, but no significant difference was observed (949.4 μg/g, vs. 809.3 μg/g, p = 0.31). Similar results were observed in the patients who received infliximab therapy (see Table S1).

Reproducibility of cross‐sectional area measurements

The inter‐ and intra‐observer agreement for SMI‐L3 were also evaluated (see Figure S5). The inter‐ and intra‐observer coefficient were excellent for SMI‐L3 (interclass correlation coefficient, 0.943, 95% CI: 0.879–0.974, p < 0 0.001; intraclass correlation coefficient, 0.945, 95% CI: 0.885–0.974, p < 0.001).

Factors associated with LOR

The results of the univariable logistic regression analysis showed that sarcopenia (OR = 3.56, 95% CI: 1.51–8.43), Montreal L1 type (OR = 0.28, 95% CI: 0.11–0.70), leukocytes count (OR = 0.86, 95% CI: 0.74–0.99), lymphocyte count (OR = 0.35, 95% CI: 0.14–0.84), and monocytes percentage (OR = 1.24, 95% CI: 1.04–1.48) were associated with LOR (see Table 3). No significant associations were observed between LOR and perianal lesions (OR = 2.7, 95% CI: 0.94–5.46) and granuloma detection (OR = 2.15, 0.95–5.04). Those variables with a p‐value ≤0.1 were then included in the multivariable analysis. Finally, sarcopenia (OR = 3.89, 95% CI: 1.31–11.54), Montreal L1 type (OR = 0.20, 95% CI: 0.06–0.60), perianal lesions (OR = 4.08, 95% CI: 1.31–12.70), and monocytes percentage (OR = 1.27, 95% CI: 1.02–1.57) at baseline were independent risk factors for LOR. When FC was included in the model, sarcopenia was still independently associated with LOR (OR = 3.34, 95% CI: 1.03–10.87). Subgroup analyses in men (see Table S3) and women (see Table S4) or in non‐Montreal L1 CD (see Table S5) and in CD with perianal lesions (see Table S6) also showed that sarcopenia was associated with LOR (OR = 4.16, 95% CI: 1.03–16.87; OR = 5.30, 95% CI: 1.04–26.94; OR = 3.35, 95% CI: 1.02–10.97; OR = 4.13, 95% CI: 1.21–14.17). The goodness‐of‐fit for all the multivariable regression analyses suggested a good fit (p > 0.05).

Model to predict LOR

Subsequently, we constructed two models to predict the LOR to biologic agents based on the independent associated factors identified earlier. Model 1 was based on the Montreal L1 type, perianal lesions, and monocytes percentage. The AUC was 0.75 (95% CI: 0.65–0.84) with a sensitivity of 74.7% and a specificity of 65.0%. Then, sarcopenia was added to obtain Model 2 (Figure 2). The addition of sarcopenia improved the predictive performance of the model (AUC = 0.82, 95% CI: 0.70–0.88) with a sensitivity of 79.6% and a specificity of 77.5% (Figure 2). Discriminant analysis also showed that addition of sarcopenia increased the ability of the model to identify LOR from 68.0% to 75.5%.

Performance of models in predicting loss of response (LOR). Model 1 was based on Montreal L1 type, perianal lesions, and monocytes percentage (a). Sarcopeania was additionally added in Model 2 (b).

We also developed a nomogram to predict the LOR to biologic agents (Figure 3). The concordance index was 0.81. For a patient with Montreal L1 type, perianal lesions, and a monocytes percentage of 10%, the total points was 70 and the LOR risk was 0.3. If the same patient had sarcopenia, the total points was 104 and the LOR risk was 0.6. The results of the calibration curves also showed high agreement between the predicted outcomes and observed outcomes.

DISCUSSION

Alterations in body composition and LOR to biologics have attracted increasing research interest in order to improve management and prevent the occurrence of adverse outcomes. Nevertheless, there is a paucity of data on the association between sarcopenia and LOR to biologics in adult patients with CD. Our study showed that sarcopenia evaluated by CT or MRI has promising potential to be used to predict responses to biologics in patients with CD. Moreover, the addition of sarcopenia improved the predictive performance of the model based on Montreal L1 typing, perianal lesions, and monocytes percentage. Detecting sarcopenia early is very important for clinicians as this allows them to enhance the management of CD and prevent adverse outcomes from occurring to a certain extent.

Biologics are an effective and safe treatment for moderate‐to‐severe CD. However, there is a high proportion of primary LOR, and patients who initially respond to biologics may lose response to the biologics over time. 10 , 12 It would be of great value to identify associated factors for LOR. Recent evidence suggests that the associated factors for a nonresponse or LOR included low drug concentrations and high anti‐drug antibody levels, 7 , 9 clinical non‐remission after induction and isolated colonic disease, 13 stricturing or penetrating behavior, 8 and presence of myopenia. 31 In the present study, we observed that LOR to biologics occurred in 54 (57.4%) individuals during a 26‐week follow‐up. Moreover, 38 (70.4%) of these individuals had sarcopenia. The impact of sarcopenia on clinical outcomes has attracted more attention. Evidence shows that sarcopenia is a risk factor for negative outcomes in CD, including abscesses, hospitalizations, CD‐related surgery, and postoperative complications, as compared with non‐sarcopenic patients. 15 , 18 , 22 , 23 However, few studies have focused on the association between sarcopenia and LOR to biologics. Ding et al. 31 reported that myopenia was associated with a primary nonresponse but not with a secondary LOR to anti‐TNF in 106 patients with moderate‐to‐severe CD. However, many factors were not controlled in their study, such as the Montreal classification and perianal lesions. Our results showed that sarcopenia was not only an independent risk factor for primary LOR but also an independent risk factor for LOR to biologics at 26‐week of follow‐up after controlling more confounders. In addition, the sensitivity analysis that was conducted on data from patients with anti‐TNF also showed similar results. Moreover, our data indicated that the addition of sarcopenia improved the performance of Montreal L1 type, perianal lesions, and monocytes percentage to predict the LOR (AUC 0.82 vs. 0.75). The addition of sarcopenia increased the sensitivity and especially increased the specificity of the model. Discriminant analysis also showed that addition of sarcopenia improved the identified ability from 68% to 75.5%. We also developed a nomogram model to predict the LOR to biologics. The model also showed a high agreement between the predicted risk and observed outcomes. This nomogram model may have great potential for clinical applications.

In the present study, we also found that perianal lesions, the Montreal location type, and monocytes percentage were independent predictors of LOR. The relevance of perianal lesions to CD is an indicator of serious illness and more aggressive processes. 32 Increasing evidence has showed that there are site‐specific differences between ileal CD and colonic CD. 33 However, few studies have directly evaluated the responsiveness to therapies between colonic and ileal CD. 34 In a retrospective cohort study that included 196 pediatric CD patients who responded to infliximab induction, patients with Montreal L2, also known as isolated colonic type, were at an increased risk of experiencing a LOR to infliximab (HR = 2.72, 95% CI: 1.30–5.71). 13 Our study firstly reported that Montreal L1 namely isolated ileum type was an independent protective predictor of LOR (OR = 0.20, 95% CI: 0.06–0.60) in adult patients. The possible biological interpretations are not clear. There are many differences between ileal and colonic CD, 33 such as anatomical features, gut microbiota, and immune cells distribution. 33 In addition, extra‐intestinal manifestations are more common in those with colonic CD than in isolated ileal disease. 33 These factors may be also related to treatment effectiveness. A recent long‐term prospective study showed that CD patients with monocytosis were at an increased risk of experiencing poor outcomes. 34 We found that higher monocytes percentage was related to an increased risk of LOR, which may be due to the elevated monocytes associated with the disease severity. 34 Previous studies showed that FC at baseline was also associated with LOR to biologics or infliximab. 35 , 36 However, such an association was not observed in our study, which may be due to the higher baseline FC levels in our cohort.

The definition of LOR has not yet been agreed upon. It has been suggested that LOR, the remission, or response should be primarily evaluated by using clinical, ileocolonoscopic, radiological, and biochemical markers. 37 The CD activity index (CDAI) and CRP are the most commonly used tools to assess clinical and biochemical responsiveness. 38 FC is a specific marker of active intestinal inflammation, the elevation of which is related to CD progression. 38 Furthermore, because of the non‐invasiveness, stability, accessibility, and cost of effectiveness of FC, it is widely used in therapeutic surveillance, 28 and using FC to detect endoscopic activity and postoperative recurrence is clearly superior to using a CRP and the CDAI. 39 , 40 , 41 , 42 Although the thresholds for the biomarker response and remission were not specified in most studies, taking 250 μg/g as the threshold FC value to monitor the inflammatory activity is compelling. 43 A fecal calprotectin level >250 μg/g stool was also used as an indicator of clinical failure or biochemical remission. 44 , 45 Combining the above data, our study defined biochemical remission as FC < 250 μg/g or >50% reduction from baseline levels, which thus reflects the responsiveness to biologic agents.

Many tests and tools are used to characterize sarcopenia 46 such as appendicular skeletal muscle mass (ASMM) by dual‐energy X‐ray and bioelectrical impedance analysis. The lumbar muscle cross‐sectional area can also be determined by using CT or MRI, and this is also used for sarcopenia evaluations. 46 For CD patients, CTE and MRE played an invaluable role in diagnosis, activity assessment, and therapeutic surveillance. The lumbar muscle cross‐sectional area could be measured on CTE or MRE images without additional costs, scan times, or radiation exposure. SMI‐L3 are mostly used in research for the diagnosis of sarcopenia. In the present study, we also defined sarcopenia based on SMI‐L3.

How sarcopenia affects the effectiveness of biologics remains unknown. A low muscle quality may be associated with a poor nutritional status or severe inflammatory status, vitamin D deficiency, gut microbiota, or corticosteroid use. 47 However, our data showed no association between sarcopenia and the FC levels, CRP, or monocytes percentage. Chronic inflammation may decrease the immune response by affecting the function of regulatory T cells. 48 Sarcopenia may have a directly negative impact on immunity. Further studies are required to demonstrate the possible mechanisms.

Several limitations of this study warranted recognition. First, our study was limited by its retrospective design and the fact that it was conducted at a single center, which possibly reduced its generalizability and induced a selective bias. Additionally, particularly with regard to the small number of patients who were taking other types of biologics (non‐anti‐TNF administration), this made it difficult to compare the different biologics groups. However, this problem was intrinsic to the study design as infliximab is a first‐line biologic for CD. Second, the disease activity assessment based on the Harvey–Bradshow index (HBI) or CDAI score was not available both at baseline and Week 26, which limited both the assessment of clinical disease activity and the change in activity from baseline to Week 26. Nevertheless, we used baseline FC levels and dynamic monitoring of the degree of FC change as a proxy to reflect the disease activity and responsiveness to treatment. Third, we evaluated LOR based on a single marker. A combination of two or more markers was also used in some studies. We performed a simple analysis to confirm that FC as a sole indicator would perform comparably to the combination indicators and the results showed that FC alone and FC + CRP (<5.0 mg/L or >50% reduction from baseline levels) showed high agreement (kappa = 0.7). Another limitation worth noting is the lack of muscle functional evaluation as a parameter to define sarcopenia. Muscle density was also not assessed in our study because many patients underwent MRI examination. Likewise, due to the lack of non‐fat suppression sequences, visceral fat area (VFA), subcutaneous fat area (SFA), and ratio of these factors could not be measured via MRI. Lastly, direct measurement of drug levels and anti‐drug antibodies was not available in this study.

Sarcopenia as was detected with CT or MRI prior to biologics administration is associated with a LOR to biologic agents or infliximab in adult patients with CD. In addition, perianal lesions and a higher monocytes percentage should be considered risk factors and the isolated ileum type should be considered a protective factor for LOR. Moreover, to our knowledge, this is the first study whereby the authors established a model to predict LOR to biologics based on sarcopenia, Montreal L1 type, perianal lesions, and monocytes percentage available at baseline. Our study may be made available to those wishing to perform personalized medical therapy for CD patients. Further clinical trials are required to more confidently understand whether these factors can allow us to improve biologic therapy outcomes.

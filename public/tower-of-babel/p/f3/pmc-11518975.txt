Are measurable residual disease results after consolidation therapy useful in children with acute lymphoblastic leukemia?
Authors: Janine Stutterheim, Rachella van der Waarden, Hester A de Groot-Kruseman, Edwin Sonneveld, Valérie de Haas, Rana Dandis, C Ellen van der Schoot, Vincent H J van der Velden, Rob Pieters
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractMeasurable residual disease (MRD) is regularly tested at later timepoints after the end of first consolidation (EOC) in children with acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL). The question remains whether this is useful for detecting (molecular) relapse. We investigated the clinical relevance of MRD after EOC in intermediate risk patients treated on DCOG-ALL-10 (n = 271) and DCOG-ALL-9 (n = 122), with MRD <0.05% at EOC. EOC MRD-negative patients (n = 178) had excellent outcomes, irrespective of MRD results at later timepoints; 6-year cumulative incidence of relapse (6-y CIR) of 7.4% (95% CI, 3.9%–12.3%) for those with MRD negativity at all later timepoints compared to 3.8% (95% CI, 0.3%–16.8%) for those with one or more later timepoints being positive (p = 0.51). Patients with positive EOC MRD (n = 91) of whom the subsequent timepoints were MRD negative (n = 43), had comparable good outcomes, 6-y CIR of 7.0% (95% CI, 1.8%–17.2%). In contrast, patients being MRD positive at EOC and MRD positive at one or more subsequent timepoints (n = 48) had a higher risk of relapse, 6-y CIR 29.4% (95% CI, 17.2%–42.8%), p < 0.001. These findings were confirmed in the validation cohort of ALL-9 as well as using the updated EuroMRD guidelines. In EOC MRD-negative patients, subsequent MRD measurements can be abandoned. For EOC MRD-positive patients the subsequent MRD measurement might be informative for further risk stratification.Subject terms: Acute lymphocytic leukaemia, Translational research

Introduction

End of induction (EOI) and end of consolidation (EOC) are the most important time points to measure minimal residual disease (MRD) for risk stratification in current treatment protocols [1–6]. In many protocols, bone marrow (BM) punctures are performed after EOC for evaluation of remission in the BM at the start of the subsequent treatment courses. In the current Alltogether1 protocol, no follow-up BM evaluations are performed after EOI for standard risk patients (SR) and after EOC for intermediate risk (MR) patients, as these are not used for stratification. The question remains whether later time points are indeed not informative. We sought for answers for the two following questions: What is the prognostic value of MRD at later time points if (1) EOC MRD is negative and if (2) EOC MRD is positive.

Pui et al. already debated if subsequent MRD measurements are warranted in patients that achieve a MRD-negative status after remission induction treatment, since in their study MRD reemerged in very few of these patients [7]. In addition, they showed that in patients with MRD ≥0.01% EOC, sequential MRD measurements were useful to guide treatment decisions. However, there are no published data on the prognostic significance of very low positive MRD levels below the level of quantification at EOC and at time points later than EOC.

In the first Dutch MRD-risk-based protocol, DCOG-ALL-10, BM punctures were performed at designated time points after EOC. The purpose of the present study was to investigate the clinical relevance of these MRD measurements at later timepoints. We validate the results in a separate cohort of patients treated according to another protocol (DCOG-ALL-9). In addition, the RQ-PCR data were also re-interpreted according to the updated EuroMRD guidelines [8].

Methods

Patients and treatment protocol

ALL-10

Patients were included in the present study if they were stratified as intermediate risk patient and had availability of MRD data at least at one specified follow-up time point (TP) after EOC (n = 271), see CONSORT diagram, Fig. 1. Children with Down syndrome were excluded. The MR stratification criteria were defined as: cytomorphologic complete remission (CR) at day 33; MRD positivity at day 33 and/or at day 79 (<0.05%); a good prednisone response at day 8; absence of KMT2A::AFF1 and absence of BCR::ABL1.

CONSORT diagram for ALL-10 cohort.

MRD measurable residual disease, TP time point. Of two patients EOC MRD was not available.

Patients were recruited in ALL-10 from November 2004 to April 2012 [3]. The collected outcome data from ALL-10 were last updated in August 2018. This is 14 years after the first inclusion, and 4 years after the last patient completed the study protocol treatment.

ALL-9

MRD and outcome data from patients treated on ALL-9 were used for validation. Since this protocol did not use MRD-based risk stratification, patients were included in the present study if they fulfilled the following criteria to match our ALL-10 cohort of the present study: MRD data available at EOC, absence of Down syndrome, absence of BCR::ABL1, EOI in CR1, EOC MRD < 0.05% and no event before 6 months after diagnosis (TP3) (n = 122), see CONSORT diagram, Supplementary Fig. 1. ALL-9 patients were recruited from January 1997 to November 2004 [9]. The collected outcome data from ALL-9 were last updated in August 2008.

All patients were enrolled into ALL-9 or ALL-10 protocol. ALL-9 was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of VUMC, Amsterdam, and ALL-10 was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Erasmus MC, Rotterdam. ALL-9 was registered at trialregister.nl, number NTR460/SNWLK-ALL-9. ALL-10 was not registered as it was judged as the standard of care treatment and not subject to the Medical Research Involving Human Subjects Act. Informed consent was signed by parents and patients according to Dutch law.

Samples

ALL-10

BM samples were obtained at diagnosis, at EOI (day 33, TP1; n = 271), at EOC 1 (day 79, TP2; n = 269), at EOC 2/start of intensification (4–5 months after diagnosis, TP3, MRG week 1; n = 252), at end of intensification, at start of maintenance therapy (8–10 months after diagnosis, TP4, MRG week 19; n = 251) or during maintenance at 12 months after diagnosis (TP5, MRG week 37; n = 263). See Supplementary Fig. 2a for MRD timepoints and treatment scheme ALL-10. BM samples were available at EOC and at least one later TP in 269 patients, see CONSORT diagram Fig. 1.

ALL-9

BM samples were obtained at diagnosis, EOI (day 42, TP1), after first consolidation with HD-MTX courses (EOC, TP2, 3 months after diagnosis, n = 122 EOC MRD < 0.05%), and at 6 months (TP3; n = 104), 9 months (TP4; n = 107) 12 months (TP5; n = 110) after diagnosis. (See Supplementary Fig. 2b, c, for MRD timepoints and treatment schemes ALL-9). BM samples were available at EOC and at least one later timepoint in 120 patients, see CONSORT diagram Supplementary Fig. 1.

Detection of MRD

Immunoglobulin and/or T-cell receptor (IG/TR) gene rearrangements were used as MRD-PCR targets. IG/TR gene rearrangements were detected in the diagnostic specimens as previously described [10, 11]. On the basis of the junctional region of the identified genomic rearrangement, patient-specific primers were designed and tested for quantitative range (QR) and sensitivity, at the two national reference laboratories. Real-time quantitative PCR data were analyzed according to the EuroMRD guidelines [9]. MRD results were classified as negative, positive not quantifiable (“positive, <QR”), and positive quantifiable (≥QR), following guidelines for therapy reduction, see for more detailed information Supplementary Methods [10].

Additional analyses were performed for those samples that were classified as positive not quantifiable using the newly proposed EuroMRD guidelines [8]. In short, the previous group of samples with “positive, <QR” results were divided into “MRD low positive, <QR” (all three replicates positive, and at least one replicate more than 3 Ct apart from background; samples highly likely to be truly positive) and “MRD of uncertain significance” (all others).

Statistical analysis

Chi-square tests were performed to investigate the absence of selection bias among patients who did and did not undergo MRD evaluations in relation to established prognostic features. Relapse was selected as the endpoint to specifically assess the association between positive MRD and relapse, rather than other events.

Relapse was defined as >5% leukemic blasts in the BM by morphology and confirmed by immunophenotyping or genotyping AND/OR leukemic blasts in the peripheral blood by morphology, confirmed by immunophenotyping or genotyping AND/OR leukemic cells in the CSF by morphology, confirmed by immunophenotyping or genotyping. Cumulative incidence of relapse (CIR) and relapse-free survival (RFS) were employed to investigate the relationship between MRD and relapse over a 6-year period.

CIR is the probability of experiencing relapse over a specified period of time. It is particularly useful in the context of competing risks, where patients may experience other events (e.g., death from unrelated causes) that preclude the occurrence of relapse. RFS was defined as the time from diagnosis until the first relapse; this was not corrected for competing events (death before relapse). The CIR was used to provide a more accurate estimation of the risk of relapse by accounting for the presence of a few cases who died before relapse. Patients who did not adhere to the MR protocol due to major treatment modifications were not excluded based on an intention-to-treat analysis. CIR curves were estimated using the cumulative incidence function. RFS curves were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier methodology. Estimates of CIR and RFS at 6 years along with the corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were reported for different time points. The CIR estimates were compared using the Chi-square test. The Log-rank test was used to compare the survival distribution among the different groups. Hazard ratios (HR) were estimated using Cox regression analysis. The analyses were performed using SPSS IBM version 26 and R version 4.2.2 [12].

Results

ALL-10

Patient characteristics

Out of 464 MR patients enrolled in the ALL-10 protocol, MRD data at later timepoints than EOC were available for 271 (see the CONSORT diagram, Fig. 1). The mean follow-up time was 10.0 years (range 6.1–13.5 years). No significant differences in sex, age, WBC, immunophenotype (B-lineage versus T-lineage), and CNS status at diagnosis were detected between patients with and without available MRD data (Table 1). The percentage of patients with MRD positivity at EOC did not differ significantly between patients with and without MRD data at later time points, nor did the 6-year RFS differ (89.2% (95% CI, 85.5%–92.9%) versus 92.1% (95% CI, 88.2%–96.0%), respectively (p = 0.29)).

Thirty out of 271 patients (11.1%) relapsed (24 isolated BM, 4 combined CNS and BM, 2 isolated CNS), 3 (1.1%) died in remission and 1 (0.4%) developed a second malignant neoplasm.

Prognostic significance of MRD at the end of consolidation 1 (EOC) and subsequent time points

At EOC (TP2) all patients had MRD < 0.05% as per the protocol definition for intermediate risk patients. At EOC 178 out of 269 (66.2%) patients were MRD negative (undetectable MRD) and 91 (33.8%) MRD positive. Of the MRD-positive patients, 80 (29.7%) had positive not quantifiable MRD and 11 (4.1%) had positive quantifiable MRD levels (0.01% < 0.05%). EOC MRD negativity was associated with a better RFS, with a 6-year RFS of 93.1% (95% CI, 89%–97%) for MRD-negative patients, and 80.3% (95% CI, 72%–89%) for MRD-positive patients (p < 0.01), Supplementary Fig. 3a.

We analyzed the three timepoints after consolidation 1. For each timepoint, patients with negative MRD had a better outcome than patients with positive MRD (Supplementary Fig. 3b–d).

Value of MRD at later timepoints for MRD-negative patients at EOC

From the 178 EOC MRD-negative patients, 152 remained MRD negative at later timepoints and 26 patients showed positive MRD results at one or more later timepoints (TP3, TP4, and/or TP5). MRD positivity at later timepoints had no prognostic relevance as both groups had comparable outcomes; 6-y CIR of 7.4% (95% CI, 3.9%–12.3%) and 3.8% (95% CI, 0.3%–16.8%) for those remaining negative at later timepoints versus those who did not, respectively, p = 0.51, see Fig. 2A. Of the 26 “Neg/Pos” patients, only 1 patient relapsed 45 months after diagnosis, and 25 patients remained relapse free. Namely, the positive predictive value of a positive MRD after initial negative MRD at EOC was 4%. The sensitivity of surveillance MRD testing after negative MRD at EOC was 8% (=1/12). The false positive rate, on the other hand, was 15% (=25/166). These numbers indicate there is little value conducting surveillance MRD tests after negative MRD at EOC.

For samples with positive not quantifiable results, we also applied the updated EuroMRD guidelines and classified these samples as either MRD low positive or MRD of uncertain significance (MRD us). For patients with negative EOC MRD, all 26 positive not quantifiable samples were re-classified as MRD us, so none of them were likely to be truly positive. Outcome for these patients was as good as patients being MRD negative from EOC onwards (Fig. 2B).

Value of MRD at later timepoints for MRD-positive patients at EOC

From the 91 patients with positive EOC MRD, 43 were MRD negative at all subsequent timepoints and 48 had a positive MRD result at one or more of the subsequent timepoints (TP3, TP4, and/or TP5). All 43 patients with MRD positivity EOC but MRD negativity at all later timepoints, had positive not quantifiable MRD at EOC, and had a 6-y CIR of 7.0% (95% CI, 1.8%–17.2%), which is comparable to patients being EOC MRD negative. In contrast, patients that were MRD positive at later timepoints had a significantly worse 6-y CIR 29.4% (95% CI, 17.2%–42.0%, p < 0.001), see Fig. 2A, HR 4.99 (95% CI, 2.29–10.87), Table 2.

Of the 91 patients with positive EOC MRD, 12 had positive quantifiable MRD and 79 positive not quantifiable MRD. All 12 patients with pos quantifiable EOC MRD, were also MRD positive at TP3. In contrast, of 79 patients with positive not quantifiable EOC MRD, only 36 (46%) had positive MRD at TP3. These 36 patients had the same outcome as those that had pos quantifiable MRD at EOC, respectively, 6-y CIR of 28.2% (95% CI, 14.5%–43.6%) versus 33.3% (95% CI, 9.4%–60.0%).

When applying the updated EuroMRD guidelines, we found that out of the 43 patients with positive not quantifiable EOC MRD and MRD negativity at all later timepoints, most samples (n = 37; 86%) were re-classified as MRD us, the remaining 6 were re-classified as MRD low positive. The outcome in both groups was good, but the statistical comparison could not be performed due to the low number of patients and events in the latter group (EOC MRD low positive/MRD negative at TP3-5), see Fig. 2B.

In the group that had positive EOC MRD as well as positive MRD at later timepoints (n = 48), 36 patients (75%) had positive not quantifiable MRD EOC. Of those 36 patients, 20 (56%) were re-classified as MRD low positive and 16 (44%) as MRD us. At later time points, 7 out of 48 patients (15%) had quantifiable MRD (all TP3) and 41 patients (85%) were MRD positive not quantifiable. Of those 41 patients, 20 were re-classified as MRD low positive and the other samples (n = 21) were re-classified as MRD us. The CIR between the four groups was heterogeneous. However, patients that were classified as MRD positive (either quantifiable or MRD low positive) both at EOC and at a later timepoint (n = 25) consistently showed a poor outcome (6-y CIR 36.0 (95% CI, 17.8%–54.6%), Fig. 2B, Supplementary Table 2).

We also analyzed patients with T-ALL and B-ALL separately. Twelve of 36 patients with T-ALL were EOC MRD positive and had positive MRD results at later timepoints. In this group, three patients had a relapse versus none in the other groups, see Supplementary Fig. 4a. For patients with B-ALL (n = 233), results were comparable to the results of T-and B-ALL combined; EOC MRD-negative patients (n = 159) had a very good outcome with a 6 y RFS of >90% irrespective of MRD results at later timepoints. Seventy-four patients were EOC MRD positive of which almost half of the patients (n = 36) were also positive at least one later timepoint, and these patients did significantly worse as compared to EOC MRD positive patients that were negative at all later timepoints (n = 38) (Supplementary Fig. 4b).

Validation cohort ALL-9

MRD data were available for 122 patients of the ALL-9 study who had EOC (TP2) MRD < 0.05% (see the CONSORT diagram, Supplementary Fig. 1). No significant differences in sex, age, WBC, and CNS status at diagnosis were detected between patients with and without available MRD data, see Supplementary Table 1. However, there were significantly less patients with a T-ALL in the MRD cohort, probably because patients were only included if EOC MRD was <0.05%. Seventy eight out of these 122 (64.0%) patients were MRD negative (undetectable MRD) and 44 (36.0%) MRD positive. Of the EOC MRD-positive patients, 37 (30.3%) had positive not quantifiable MRD and 7 (5.7%) had positive quantifiable MRD levels (0.01% < 0.05%). Patients with MRD negativity at EOC had a better 6-year RFS (93.6%; 95% CI, 88%–99%) than patients with positive MRD at EOC (83.5%; 95% CI, 72%–95%) (p = 0.029), Supplementary Fig. 5.

Of 120 patients also MRD results at later timepoints were available; 77 patients were EOC MRD negative; and 43 patients were EOC MRD positive. As in ALL-10, we divided the EOC MRD negative group into two groups; patients that remained MRD negative (n = 69), and patients that had any positive MRD results at TP3, TP4, and/or TP5 (n = 8). MRD positivity at later time points had no prognostic value as both groups had comparable outcomes; 6-y CIR of 5.8% (95% CI, 1.9%–13.1%) and 12.5% (95% CI, 0.5%–44.5%), respectively (p = 0.49), see Fig. 2C.

Also for the EOC MRD-positive patients (n = 43), we observed the same results as in ALL-10; patients that became MRD negative at all later timepoints (n = 17) had a CIR that was comparable to that of patients that were EOC MRD negative (6-y CIR 5.9 (95% CI, 0.3%–24.2%)). However, patients that remained MRD positive at later time points (n = 26) had a worse 6-y CIR of 24.6% (95% CI, 9.7%–43.0%), (p = 0.067), Fig. 2C, HR 5.90 (1.73–20.18, p = 0.005), Table 3.

Discussion

This is the first study that has investigated the prognostic significance of MRD detection after EOC in an MRD-based pediatric ALL protocol. Often low, not quantifiable levels of MRD (<0.05%) are found of which the prognostic and clinical significance is not clear. First, our data show that in patients who are MRD negative at EOC, MRD positivity at later timepoints did not adversely affect outcome. So no additional MRD measurements are needed in patients that are EOC MRD negative. Second, patients that are EOC MRD positive the subsequent MRD time points have additional prognostic relevance and could help to further stratify these intermediate risk patients.

The data were obtained in our ALL-10 study and validated in the ALL-9 cohort. The results were in line with a previous study by Pui et al. [7], which showed that MRD reemerged in EOC MRD-negative patients in a very small proportion of patients. However, they found that the few patients who experienced re-emergence of MRD, defined as ≥0.01%, carried a poor prognosis. In our cohort, all positive MRD results in patients being EOC MRD negative, were at the level of positive not quantifiable (i.e., generally below 0.01%), and these patients had an excellent outcome.

A likely explanation for MRD-positive results in patients that were EOC MRD negative is that these very low not quantifiable levels are false positive results. When we analyzed the data again using the updated EuroMRD guidelines [8], all these samples were classified as MRD us, indicating that they have a relatively low chance to be truly positive. Likely, most of these samples are false MRD positive due to nonspecific amplification of regenerating normal B-cells [13]. Other techniques, such as fragment length analysis or next-generation sequencing may confirm or rule out true MRD positivity [14, 15]. The EuroMRD network therefore recommends in the updated guidelines to perform fragment length analysis in all MRD low positive and MRD us samples. In this study fragment length analysis was not yet performed and PCR products were no longer available for retrospective analysis.

In patients who are EOC MRD positive, subsequent time points seem to be of prognostic importance. Here we show that low, not quantifiable MRD levels detected at EOC and at subsequent time point(s) are prognostic for relapse and probably identify slow responders. These slow responders have a higher risk to develop a relapse. All relapses involved the BM. Identifications of these slow responders should be the purpose of follow-up measurements and may lead to improved risk stratification with subsequent therapeutic interventions such as blinatumomab, inotuzumab, and CAR T-cell therapy. Almost half of the patients in this cohort showed MRD results of uncertain significance at TP3, 4, or 5, illustrating the importance of validation of these results with a second method.

We conclude from this study that in patients with MRD-based intermediate risk ALL who are MRD negative at EOC, MRD measurement at later timepoints can be abandoned. For patients who are EOC MRD positive the subsequent MRD measurements might be informative for further risk stratification and therapeutic intervention.

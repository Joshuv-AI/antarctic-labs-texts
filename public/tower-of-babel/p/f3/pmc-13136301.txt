Interactive text-guided image segmentation via vision Mamba and large language models
Authors: Yao Meng, Haochen Sun, Wei Jiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper proposes an H-type Bidirectional Alignment Network for text-guided image segmentation, enabling efficient and accurate cross-modal feature fusion. For visual encoding, a 12-layer Vision Mamba with four stages is adopted. It captures long-range dependencies via a Selective State Space Model (SSM) and, at the same time, leverages local convolution and residual structures to enhance boundary and detail information—thereby reducing computational complexity when processing high-resolution images. The text encoder is based on the Qwen model and employs a strategy of freezing the bottom layers while fine-tuning the upper layers, aiming to obtain semantic representations and adapt to referential expressions. The cross-modal alignment module utilizes Q-Former to construct learnable query vectors: the forward path accomplishes the text-to-image segmentation task, while the backward path reconstructs the attention distribution of the image over the text. This bidirectional supervision mechanism is thus realized to constrain cross-modal consistency. The multi-scale decoder fuses visual and aligned features, and supports the model in gradually optimizing segmentation results through an interactive iterative mechanism. Experiments on the RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, and RefCOCOg datasets verify the effectiveness of the proposed method, which demonstrates performance improvements compared to existing approaches.Keywords: Text-guided segmentation, Vision Mamba, Large language models, Interactive segmentation, Cross-modal alignmentSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Text-guided image segmentation sits at the intersection of natural language understanding and visual perception, requiring models to localize and delineate target regions conditioned on natural-language references. This capability underpins many downstream applications, including content-aware editing, human–robot interaction, and assistive technologies. While semantic and instance segmentation have progressed substantially with deep learning, injecting textual guidance introduces a cross-modal alignment challenge: systems must reconcile high-level linguistic semantics with spatially precise, low-level visual signals.

Early approaches typically encoded text using recurrent models and fused it with visual features via concatenation or element-wise operations, demonstrating feasibility but struggling with complex referential expressions and spatial relations. More recent advances employ attention mechanisms and transformer-based designs, exploring both early and late fusion strategies for cross-modal interaction. Despite notable gains, two issues remain prominent. The first concerns reliable cross-modal grounding: static text encoders used as generic feature extractors can underfit task-specific cues, leading to mismatches between linguistic references and pixel-level evidence, especially for subtle attributes and compositional spatial relations. The second concerns training and inference stability in dense prediction settings, where gradient dynamics, threshold choices, and supervision signals affect both convergence and generalization.

This work introduces an H-type dual-head framework that addresses these challenges through bidirectional learning. The architecture comprises a segmentation head, which predicts text-conditioned probability maps, and a text-attention head, which learns word-level focusing patterns aligned with the visual target. The two heads are trained under bidirectional supervision with a consistency constraint that explicitly encourages agreement between textual focusing and pixel-level delineation. This joint optimization improves cross-modal alignment and reduces semantic drift, without relying on external modules beyond the core design.

The framework is evaluated on the RefCOCO family using standard splits (val, testA, testB) and widely adopted metrics, including mIoU. Results indicate robust improvements across splits and thresholds, alongside stable training behavior facilitated by modern optimization practices. Ablative analyses further confirm that the auxiliary text-attention supervision and the consistency constraint contribute substantially to both precision at high thresholds and overall segmentation quality. The main innovations and contributions of our work are as follows:

H-shaped bidirectional architecture

An H-shaped bidirectional architecture is proposed to enable mutually guided learning between text and image modalities. Different from traditional unidirectional text-guided image segmentation methods, this architecture introduces a bidirectional supervision mechanism to realize text-interactively guided image segmentation, with the auxiliary task being that image segmentation results inversely guide the localization of text word importance. Cross-modal feature fusion is achieved through 16 learnable query embeddings, forming a “forceps-type” complementary learning framework, which significantly enhances the semantic alignment capability between modalities.

Vision Mamba encoding

A four-stage Vision Mamba encoder is designed, consisting of 12 layers of Mamba blocks to process image features. Compared with the O(n²) complexity of traditional Transformers, Vision Mamba achieves O(n) linear complexity, significantly reducing computational overhead while maintaining the ability to model long sequences. By processing high-resolution features through a linear-complexity state space model, a balance between efficiency and performance is achieved, providing a new technical pathway for vision-language multimodal tasks.

Lightweight Q-former fusion module

A lightweight Q-Former fusion module is designed, using 16 learnable query tokens as a bridge for cross-modal information exchange. It adopts a single-layer 8-head multi-head cross-attention mechanism to project 768-dimensional visual features and text features into a unified 768-dimensional semantic space, with LayerNorm normalization and residual connections applied.

FiLM-based conditional modulation

A FiLM (Feature-wise Linear Modulation)-style text conditional modulation method is proposed. The fused 768-dimensional text-visual features generate channel-wise scaling factors and bias terms through a two-layer fully connected network to dynamically modulate spatial features. GroupNorm is used instead of BatchNorm, and pixel-wise predictions are made through a combination of multiple convolutions, with final upsampling via deconvolution to restore the original image resolution.

Dual-task joint optimization framework

A joint optimization mechanism combining segmentation loss and text attention loss is constructed. The inverse text localization supervision enhances the model’s understanding of key semantic regions, and a polynomial loss weight balancing and gradient clipping strategy is designed to ensure the stability of the dual-task training process.

Related work

The development process of text-guided image segmentation

Text-guided image segmentation is an important task at the intersection of computer vision and natural language processing, aiming to accurately segment specific targets in images at the pixel level based on natural language descriptions. This task not only requires the model to understand the semantic content of natural language but also to accurately align language descriptions with visual features, enabling accurate mapping from text to pixels. With the rapid development of deep learning technology and the continuous emergence of multimodal learning methods, the field of text-guided image segmentation has made significant progress in both theoretical methods and practical applications. This section will comprehensively review the relevant research work from seven aspects: the development history of text-guided segmentation, multimodal pre-training and alignment, the application of attention mechanism, the innovation of Transformer architecture, the interactive segmentation mechanism, the innovation of state space models, and the evolution of classical segmentation architecture.

The study of text-guided image segmentation originated from the exploration of the combination of natural language and visual understanding. Hu et al.1 proposed a systematic framework that combines natural language expression with image segmentation, encodes text features through long short-term memory networks, and extracts multi-scale image features using fully convolutional networks, establishing a basic mapping relationship from language description to pixel-level segmentation. Although this pioneering work has limitations in modeling complex spatial relationships, it has laid an important foundation for subsequent research. On this basis, , Margffoy-Tuay et al.2 proposed an improved method based on recursive refinement network for dynamic multimodal instance segmentation tasks, and gradually improved the segmentation quality through a multi-stage iterative optimization mechanism, especially in terms of boundary accuracy and detail preservation. Yu et al.3 further proposed a modular attention network architecture, which decomposes complex natural language descriptions into three different processing modules: appearance, position, and relationship, and handles different semantic attributes of target objects respectively, achieving more fine-grained cross-modal feature alignment.

With the development of deep learning architecture, Liu et al.4 designed a tree network of neural modules, that is, to handle complex visual reasoning tasks by dynamically combining different neural modules, providing a modular solution for text-guided segmentation. Chen et al.5 proposed a perspective text grouping method specifically for occlusion and complex scene problems in referential image segmentation tasks, and improved the model’s understanding of complex visual scenes through an improved grouping mechanism. In order to better handle multi-scale features and long-distance dependencies, researchers further study network architecture. Anderson et al.6 proposed bottom-up and top-down attention mechanisms, which provide a strong feature representation basis for image description and visual question answering tasks by combining regional-level and pixel-level visual features. This hierarchical attention design provides important inspiration for subsequent text-guided segmentation methods.

In terms of cross-modal feature fusion, Lu et al.7 proposed a ViLBERT pre-training model that processes visual and text inputs separately through a dual-stream Transformer architecture, and then realizes deep modal interaction through a cross-attention mechanism. The LXMERT model proposed by Tan and Bansal8 achieves more comprehensive multimodal understanding capabilities through the triple architecture of language, vision, and cross-modal encoders. The VL-BERT model designed by Su et al.9 simplifies model design while maintaining strong cross-modal modeling capabilities through a unified Transformer architecture that processes both visual and language inputs. Zhou et al.10 proposed a unified visual-language pre-training framework specifically optimized for image description and visual question-answering tasks, demonstrating the great potential of pre-training methods in multimodal tasks. To further improve the generalization ability of the model, Jia et al.11 proposed a large-scale adversarial training method that significantly improved the model’s performance in downstream tasks by performing visual-language representation learning on a large-scale dataset. The contrastive learning framework proposed by Chen et al.12 provides a new idea for visual representation learning, and this self-supervised learning method has also been widely applied to multimodal learning.

Multimodal pre-training and visual language alignment

The rise of large-scale pre-trained models has revolutionized multimodal learning. The momentum comparison method proposed by He et al.13 is an unsupervised visual representation learning method, which learns high-quality visual representations on large-scale unlabeled data through comparative learning. This method provides an important technical basis for subsequent multimodal pretraining. The large-scale visual-language representation learning method proposed by Jia et al.11 demonstrates the important impact of data scale on multimodal learning performance by training on massive data containing noisy text supervision. This study demonstrates the great potential of pre-training on network-scale data, laying the foundation for subsequent models such as CLIP. The FILIP model proposed by Yao et al.14 achieves more accurate cross-modal alignment through fine-grained interactive language-image pre-training. This method no longer relies solely on global image and text comparison, but performs fine-grained comparative learning at the patch level and token level, which significantly improves the model’s ability to capture detailed information. Zhai et al.15 proposed a LiT method that achieves zero-shot transfer learning by locking image-text tuning, which greatly reduces computational costs by freezing the pre-trained image encoder and only optimizing the text encoder to adapt to new tasks.

With the support of the Transformer architecture, the Vision Transformer proposed by Dosovitskiy et al.16 proves the effectiveness of pure Transformer architecture in visual tasks and ushers in a new era of visual Transformer. The Swin Transformer proposed by Liu et al.17 solves the computational complexity problem of Vision Transformer when processing high-resolution images through a sliding window mechanism, providing a more efficient solution for intensive prediction tasks.

The image processing method of the Tokens-to-Token Vision Transformer designed by Yuan et al.18 can better model the structural information in the image through the recursive tokenization process and improve the model’s understanding of spatial relationships. The DeiT method proposed by Touvron et al.19 demonstrates that Vision Transformer can be trained from scratch without large-scale pre-training, while comparable performance to convolutional networks is obtained through knowledge distillation. The Pyramid Vision Transformer proposed by Wang et al.20 designs a pyramid-structured Transformer architecture, which can generate multi-scale feature maps, which is especially suitable for intensive prediction tasks such as object detection and semantic segmentation. This design provides an important reference for subsequent segmentation Transformers. SegFormer proposed by Xie et al.21 is a Transformer architecture specifically designed for semantic segmentation, which demonstrates that Transformer provides an effective explanation for text-guided segmentation in intensive prediction tasks. Zheng et al.22 proposed a SETR method from a sequence-to-sequence perspective to transform segmentation tasks into sequence prediction problems. The MaskFormer method proposed by Cheng et al.23 pointed out that pixel-by-pixel classification is not the whole of semantic segmentation, and that the semantic segmentation, instance segmentation, and panorama segmentation tasks are unified through mask classification, which provides a new idea for the unified modeling of segmentation tasks. The Segment Anything Model (SAM) proposed by Kirillov et al.24 achieves universal image segmentation capabilities through large-scale data and prompt engineering, demonstrating the great potential of foundation models in segmentation tasks.

While large-scale foundation models like SAM have achieved remarkable success, recent research has also focused on efficiency and specific task adaptation. For instance, Wang et al.25 revisited the nearest centroid classifier and proposed Deep Nearest Centroids (DNC) for efficient large-scale visual recognition, offering a non-parametric alternative to traditional classification heads. In the realm of parameter-efficient fine-tuning, Han et al.26 introduced EVPT, an effective visual prompt tuning framework that adapts frozen backbones to downstream tasks with minimal learnable parameters. Furthermore, Lu et al.27 proposed TransFlow, demonstrating that pure Transformer architectures can effectively serve as flow learners for dense prediction tasks by capturing global dependencies. These works collectively highlight the trend towards efficient and effective visual modeling, which aligns with our motivation to employ the Vision Mamba architecture for efficient text-guided segmentation.

Application of attention mechanism in cross-modal tasks

As a key technology in deep learning, attention mechanism plays a central role in cross-modal feature alignment and information fusion. The stacked cross-attention network proposed by Lee et al.28 achieves fine-grained cross-modal alignment by calculating the similarity matrix between image regions and text words. This method can clearly identify the image region corresponding to each word in the text, providing a basis for interpretable cross-modal understanding. The CCNet designed by Huang et al.29 effectively captures long-distance contextual information in semantic segmentation tasks through the vertical and horizontal cross-attention mechanism. In order to better model complex spatial relationships, Hui et al.30 proposed a language structure-guided contextual modeling method to construct structured representations of text through dependent syntactic analysis, so that the model can better understand the grammatical relationships and semantic hierarchies in complex language descriptions. This method demonstrates significant advantages in handling complex referent reaches. Multi-level adaptive visual text alignment methods optimize alignment from different levels: global, local, and relational. The global level focuses on the overall semantic consistency, the local level focuses on the exact matching of details, and the relational level models the interaction between different objects. Wang et al.31 proposed an image-based pre-training method for processing visual and linguistic tasks through a unified architecture. The Unicoder VL designed by Li et al.32 implements a universal visual language encoder through cross-modal pre-training, which learns powerful cross-modal representation capabilities through large-scale multimodal data pre-training, providing a feature basis for downstream text-guided segmentation tasks. The UNITER method proposed by Chen et al.33 achieves uniform modeling of multiple visual-language tasks through universal image-text representation learning. The method designs four pre-training tasks, including mask language modeling, mask region modeling, image-text matching, and word-region alignment, which comprehensively improves the cross-modal understanding ability of the model.

The VinVL method proposed by Zhang et al.34 re-examines visual representations in vision-language models, providing higher quality visual features through improved object detectors, significantly improving the performance of multiple vision-language tasks. This work underscores the importance of high-quality visual features for multimodal tasks.

The introduction of the Transformer architecture has brought changes to text-guided image segmentation, enabling end-to-end optimization and global context modeling. The bottom-up and top-down attention mechanisms proposed by Anderson et al.6 achieve more accurate cross-modal alignment by combining regional-level features with global contextual information. The language-aware vision transformer (LAVT) designed by Yang et al.35 is the first to apply the Transformer architecture to the referential image segmentation task by performing early cross-modal feature fusion in the middle layer of the Vision Transformer. The CRIS method proposed by Wang et al.36 uses the multimodal pre-training knowledge of the CLIP model to design a special visual language decoder to achieve accurate text-to-pixel alignment through contrastive learning. The ReSTR method proposed by Kim et al.37 completely abandons the convolutional operation and adopts a pure Transformer architecture to achieve end-to-end referential segmentation. This method treats image patches and text tokens as sequence elements, and models the relationship between them through the multi-head self-attention mechanism, which simplifies the model architecture and improves the computational efficiency. The GroupViT model proposed by Xu et al.38 achieves semantic segmentation that relies only on text supervision by introducing a grouping mechanism and a visual transformer. The CLIPSeg method proposed by Lüddecke and Ecker39 can generate segmentation results based on arbitrary text or visual prompts. The Segmenter model proposed by Strudel et al.40 is based on a pure Transformer architecture and implements semantic segmentation through linear decoders or mask Transformer decoders.

The GrabCut method proposed by Rother et al.41 uses an iterative graph cutting algorithm to guide foreground background separation using user-provided bounding boxes or brush inputs. The random walk method proposed by Grady42 predicts the label of pixels by constructing a random walk model on the image, using user-marked seed points. The DEXTR method proposed by Maninis et al.43 demonstrates that high-quality segmentation masks can be obtained with only a few extreme clicks.

The f-BRS method proposed by Sofiiuk et al.44 directly optimizes model parameters to adapt to user feedback by backpropagation, achieving efficient online adaptation without complete retraining. This idea provides important inspiration for the bidirectional supervised design of this paper, especially how to stabilize the mechanism of main task training through auxiliary tasks, which is important related to the H-shaped bidirectional architecture proposed in this paper.

The RITM method further proposed by Sofiiuk et al.45 enables the model to better generalize to real interaction scenarios by simulating user interaction during training. This method designs an automated interaction simulator, which can generate various possible user inputs during training, so as to improve the efficiency of the model’s utilization of interaction information.

The state-space model is an emerging sequence modeling method that has recently been introduced into computer vision tasks, providing a new method for processing high-resolution images and long sequence modeling. Long et al.46 proposed the precedent of end-to-end semantic segmentation of fully convolutional networks, which realizes pixel-level dense prediction by replacing the fully connected layer of the classification network with a convolutional layer. The U-Net architecture designed by Ronneberger et al.47 has achieved great success in the field of medical image segmentation through encoder-decoder structure and jump connections. Its design idea retains multi-scale feature information through jump connections—which is also widely used in other segmentation tasks, providing an important reference for the combination of state space model and traditional architecture. The DeepLab series of methods proposed by Chen et al.48 expands the receptive field through hollow convolution, capturing a wider range of contextual information without increasing the number of parameters. The PSPNet proposed by Zhao et al.49 aggregates contextual information at different scales through a pyramid pooling module, which effectively improves the accuracy of segmentation. This strategy of multi-scale feature fusion provides important guidance for the state space model to process different levels of spatial information. Yuan et al.50 proposed OCRNet by explicitly modeling the relationship between pixels and object classes through object contextual representation learning, achieving more accurate semantic segmentation. This idea of explicit relationship modeling is related to the state propagation mechanism of the state space model. The S4 model proposed by Gu and Dao51 achieves efficient modeling of long sequences through the design of structured state space, and has better computational efficiency and modeling capabilities than traditional recurrent neural networks and Transformers. The core innovation of the S4 model is to transform the state space model into a form that can be computed in parallel through mathematical optimization techniques, so as to overcome the problem that the traditional recursive model is difficult to parallelize. On the basis of S4, Liu et al.52 proposed the Mamba model, which further improved the expressive ability and computational efficiency of the model through the design of selective state space. The key innovation of the Mamba model is the introduction of a selectivity mechanism, which allows the model to dynamically decide which information needs to be retained and what information can be forgotten, which greatly improves the model’s ability to handle complex sequences. Applying the state-space model to visual tasks, , Liu et al.53 proposed Vision Mamba, which uses a bidirectional state-space model to achieve efficient visual feature extraction by treating image patches as sequences. The proposed method can capture long-distance spatial dependencies while maintaining linear computational complexity, which is comparable to Transformer in image classification and object detection tasks, while having higher computational efficiency. Zhu et al.54 further proposed a VSSM model to optimize the effect of visual sequence modeling through improved scanning strategies and state propagation mechanisms.

In summary, the field of text-guided image segmentation has experienced the development of simple feature fusion to complex multimodal pre-trained models in the past decade, from traditional convolutional architectures to modern Transformer and state space models. The H-shaped bidirectional architecture proposed in this paper integrates the efficient sequence modeling capability, bidirectional supervised learning mechanism and cross-modal attention alignment technology of Vision Mamba, and contributes new ideas and methods to the further development of this field.

Methodology

Overall architecture

This paper proposes an H-type Bidirectional Alignment Network for text-guided image segmentation. The core idea of this framework differs from the conventional unidirectional text-guided image approach: it introduces a parallel supervision path for image-guided text, and connects the two paths through a consistency loss, thereby achieving deeper cross-modal feature alignment.

The overall architecture, as shown in Fig. 1, mainly consists of three key components: (1) A multi-scale visual backbone network, which is responsible for extracting hierarchical features of images; (2) A text encoder, used to understand the semantic information of input text; (3) An H-type bidirectional alignment module, which processes the text-to-image segmentation task and the image-to-text attention reconstruction task in parallel.

Specifically, the input image and text T are processed by the visual backbone network (Vision Mamba) and text encoder (Qwen) respectively, generating multi-scale visual features and text features . These features are subsequently fed into the H-type bidirectional alignment module. In this module, the forward path carries out the primary segmentation task and generates an initial segmentation mask via cross-modal fusion. Simultaneously, the backward path undertakes the auxiliary attention task, leveraging visual features to reconstruct the attention distribution over the text. The multi-scale decoder integrates features from the visual backbone and the alignment module to produce the final refined segmentation result. The entire model undergoes end-to-end training with a composite loss function, encompassing segmentation loss, text attention loss, and the innovative bidirectional consistency loss.

In this work, Vision Mamba based on the selective state space model (SSM) is employed in the visual backbone. The input image is divided into non-overlapping patches of size P=16, which are then serialized. Each patch is linearly projected into an embedding space of dimension D=768, and 2D positional encoding is added to preserve topological relationships. Subsequently, the embedded features are fed into a four-stage hierarchical stack, which consists of a total of 12 Mamba blocks with a layer configuration of5, 5, 5, 33. Each Mamba block adopts pre-normalization to stabilize the training of deep layers; its core component is a selective SSM branch parameterized by input dependencies.

Through conditional modeling of state update and readout operators, this branch enables content-adaptive spatiotemporal filtering, thereby capturing long-range cross-patch dependencies while maintaining linear complexity. Meanwhile, a depthwise separable convolution is introduced as a local mixing path to enhance responsiveness to textures, edges, and small objects; a gated MLP branch with channel expansion (expand=2.0) improves nonlinear expression capabilities and channel interaction. Each sub-layer is coupled with the main backbone via residual connections, and a dropout rate of 0.1 is applied to suppress overfitting in the early stages of optimization. The hidden dimension of the state space is set to 16, enabling stable global coupling modeling with low state overhead. Throughout the network, a spatial resolution with a fixed stride of 16 is maintained this not only reduces the computational burden caused by feature map scaling but also avoids the loss of positional information due to excessive downsampling. The multi-stage hierarchical stack integrates semantic and geometric cues incrementally from top to bottom: the early stages focus on extracting low-level textures and edges, the middle stages expand the receptive field to aggregate cross-regional context, and the late stages strengthen object-level semantic consistency and regional alignment, ultimately forming a representation that is both hierarchical and selective. At the end of the backbone, two types of features are output simultaneously: one is a sequence-format feature for cross-modal fusion and query interaction; the other is a spatial format feature directly used by the segmentation decoder. In the cross-modal alignment path, the sequence-format feature and text representation are fed into a lightweight query interaction module to establish language visual semantic correspondence; in the pixel-level decoding path, the spatial-format feature is input to the segmentation head, and channel-wise scaling and biasing are performed via text-semantics-based FiLM (Feature-wise Linear Modulation)-style conditional modulation this ensures that pixel predictions are explicitly guided by text. Subsequently, deconvolution and bilinear interpolation are used to accurately align the features to the original image scale, guaranteeing boundary details and target integrity. The key points of this design are as follows: selective SSM is used to ensure linear-complexity modeling of long-range dependencies; local convolution paths are employed to supplement texture and boundary information; pre-normalization and residual structures are adopted to ensure stable training of deep layers; and a fixed embedding dimension D=768 and unified stride P=16 are maintained to preserve alignment across stages and low computational overhead. Finally, a dual-output interface “sequence features for cross-modality, spatial features for decoding” is formed. This enables the visual backbone to not only provide sufficient global semantics for language-visual fusion but also retain necessary spatial details for pixel-level segmentation, thereby achieving a balance between performance and efficiency for high-resolution inputs and complex scenarios.

Each stage processes features through multiple Mamba blocks, each of which applies layer normalization, selective state-space modeling, and residual joining. Text encoder

The text encoding subsystem adopts a lightweight-modified encoder-only variant of the Qwen encoder, with Qwen2-0.5B as the base model, to obtain stable semantic representations that support cross-modal alignment. Original phrases are first tokenized into subwords, then truncated or zero-padded to a maximum length of 128, and a corresponding attention mask is generated simultaneously. The tokenized outputs are mapped to a unified hidden dimension of 768 via an embedding layer and the native positional encoding mechanism, and iteratively refined through multi-layer self-attention and feed-forward networks to form context-aware sequence-level features. To ensure training stability and efficiency, the encoder adopts a strategy of “freezing as the main approach, fine-tuning as a supplement”: by default, a large number of bottom layers are frozen to preserve general linguistic knowledge, and only minor parameter updates are performed on the upper layers. This allows the language representations to adapt to referential expressions with low computational overhead. The encoder incorporates an attention-masked average pooling strategy to obtain global text representations, where padding tokens are ignored during pooling to ensure meaningful semantic aggregation (Figs. 2, 3).

H-type bidirectional alignment module

This module adopts Q-Former as the core of cross-modal alignment and forms symmetric bidirectional supervision through the forward path from text to image segmentation and the backward path from image to text localization.

The model constructs a number of learnable query vectors to actively retrieve key information from vision and text within a unified semantic space. Visual and text features are respectively linearly projected and then concatenated in the key-value dimension. The queries aggregate the most relevant cross-modal evidence in cross-modal attention, and then obtain the fused representation through residual connection and layer normalization. On the one hand, this fused representation acts on spatial visual features in the form of FiLM-style conditional modulation, enabling linguistic semantics to directly enter the channel-wise pixel discrimination path for segmentation generation. On the other hand, it is mapped to attention weights aligned with the word sequence, which are used to measure key words, thereby reversely constraining visual decision-making through interpretable supervision from the language side. The bidirectional design performs collaborative optimization based on the unified fused representation, which not only improves the discrimination ability of cross-modal correspondence but also enhances the stability and interpretability of the training process.

Loss function design

A composite objective is adopted for training: the segmentation term uses Focal Loss to alleviate the imbalance between foreground and background; the text attention term aligns the predicted word-level distribution to a heuristic pseudo-target via KL divergence—this pseudo-target is constructed based on segmentation region proportion and word position information, thereby enhancing the discriminability of language supervision; the consistency term constrains the global intensity of the language side to match that of the visual side, reducing cross-modal conflicts. The three terms are linearly combined with empirical weights, where segmentation is the main task, and text attention and consistency provide complementary signals. The empirical weights are set to 1.0, 0.3, and 0.1 respectively.

Experiments

Experimental setup

We conduct comprehensive experiments on three standard benchmarks for referring expression segmentation: RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, and RefCOCOg. These datasets provide diverse scenarios for evaluating text-guided segmentation performance across varying linguistic complexity and visual contexts. RefCOCO contains 142,210 referring expressions for 50,000 objects in 19,994 images from MS COCO. The expressions typically include spatial relationships and are relatively concise. RefCOCO+ excludes spatial prepositions, requiring models to rely more heavily on visual appearance and context. RefCOCOg contains longer, more complex expressions that provide detailed object descriptions. Training procedures utilize AdamW optimization with initial learning rates of for Vision Mamba components and for pre-trained text encoders. Batch sizes are set to 8 across 4 GPUs, with gradient accumulation maintaining effective batch sizes of 32. Training continues for 100 epochs with cosine annealing learning rate schedules. To stabilize the training process, we employ a linear warmup strategy for the first 5 epochs, where the learning rate is gradually increased from to the initial base learning rate. This warmup phase is crucial for preventing gradient explosion during the initial alignment of the randomly initialized queries with the pre-trained features.

Data augmentation strategies include random horizontal flipping, color jittering, and random cropping. Augmentation parameters are selected to maintain dataset statistics while providing sufficient variation for robust training. Text augmentation involves synonym replacement and paraphrasing to increase linguistic diversity (Fig. 4).

Quantitative results

Performance evaluation employs standard metrics including Intersection over Union (IoU), precision, recall, and F1-score. Following established evaluation protocols in the field, IoU serves as the primary metric for comparison with existing methods. Our method achieves significant results across all benchmark datasets: it obtains IoU scores of 68.34% and 65.27% on RefCOCO testA and testB sets respectively, achieves 64.37% and 52.07% on RefCOCO+ testA and testB sets, and attains 56.84% IoU on the RefCOCOg dataset.

Ablation studies

Systematic ablation studies were conducted to quantify the contribution of each architectural component. Under consistent training procedures and evaluation protocols, each component was individually removed to assess performance changes. In the ablation experiment on the Vision Mamba backbone, replacing it with a standard ResNet50 backbone resulted in a 2.5–3.5% IoU drop across various datasets. This performance degradation demonstrates the efficiency and effectiveness of the state-space model architecture in visual feature extraction. Computational complexity analysis revealed that Vision Mamba reduces FLOPs by approximately 35% compared to ResNet while maintaining superior performance, along with a 30% improvement in inference speed.

In the ablation experiment on the Qwen2-0.5B text encoder, replacing it with a BERT-base model led to a 2.0–3.0% IoU performance decline. The larger model capacity and advanced pre-training strategy of Qwen are crucial for understanding complex referring expressions. Linguistic analysis indicates significant improvements in handling spatial relationships and attribute descriptions, particularly excelling in tasks involving multi-attribute combinations and complex spatial reasoning.

In the ablation experiment on the cross-attention alignment mechanism, replacing the Q-Former cross-attention with simple feature concatenation while retaining other fusion mechanisms eliminated the complex cross-modal alignment objective. This modification resulted in a 1.5–2.2% IoU decrease, demonstrating the value of explicit cross-modal attention alignment.

We compared three strategies to validate the necessity of fine-tuning the text encoder: (1) Fully Frozen: Keeping all Qwen parameters fixed; (2) Full Fine-tuning: Updating all parameters; (3) Top-2 Tuning (Ours): Only updating the top 2 transformer layers. As shown in Table 2, the fully frozen model yields suboptimal IoU (66.12%) because the general linguistic knowledge differs from the referring expression syntax. Conversely, full fine-tuning leads to overfitting on the limited RefCOCO dataset (66.85%). Our partial tuning strategy achieves the best performance (67.69%) with minimal trainable parameters.

Controlled experiment

To comprehensively evaluate the performance of the text-guided image segmentation method proposed in this paper—based on Vision Mamba and large language models (LLMs)—a detailed comparison was conducted with mainstream methods in this field in recent years. As a key task in multimodal understanding, text-guided image segmentation has undergone technological evolution, progressing from early simple feature fusion to modern approaches driven by pre-trained models.

Early text-guided segmentation methods primarily adopted hybrid architectures combining CNNs (Convolutional Neural Networks) and RNNs (Recurrent Neural Networks), integrating textual features with visual features through direct concatenation or simple fusion operations. Models such as RMI57 and DMN2 represent typical early efforts in this field; however, their frameworks exhibit significant limitations in complex scenarios and long-text understanding. With the rise of the attention mechanism, methods including MAttNet58, CMSA59 and BCAN60 introduced cross-modal attention alignment mechanisms, which significantly enhanced the ability to model the correspondence between textual and visual features. Methods in this phase achieved an IoU (Intersection over Union) performance of 60–65% on the RefCOCO series datasets.

The introduction of the Transformer architecture marked a crucial turning point in this field. Approaches like VLT adopted an encoder-decoder structure and achieved better global context modeling through self-attention and cross-attention mechanisms, further improving performance to the IoU range of 65–68%. Nevertheless, these methods were still constrained by the limitations of unimodal pre-trained knowledge and could not fully leverage large-scale visual-language correspondences.

Building on the QFormer multimodal alignment method, the proposed method in this paper further incorporates the efficient visual encoding capability of Vision Mamba and the advanced text understanding capability of the Qwen large language model. Through an H-shaped bidirectional alignment framework and an interactive optimization mechanism, our method not only achieves performance improvement in the static segmentation task, reaching an accuracy comparable to the latest methods such as PolyFormer56 and SAM70. More importantly, the model we proposed introduces user interaction capabilities, significantly improving the segmentation efficiency in practical application scenarios and having higher deployment and application value.

Discussion and analysis

Performance characteristics

The superior performance of our approach stems from several synergistic factors. Vision Mamba’s efficient processing enables effective handling of high-resolution images while maintaining global context awareness. The linear complexity scaling proves particularly beneficial for detailed segmentation tasks where spatial resolution directly impacts boundary accuracy. Qwen integration provides substantial improvements in textual understanding, particularly for complex referring expressions. Analysis of failure cases in baseline methods reveals frequent errors in spatial relationship interpretation and attribute recognition. Our approach demonstrates notable improvements in these challenging scenarios through Qwen’s sophisticated language modeling capabilities. CLIP-based alignment ensures semantic consistency between visual and textual modalities. Quantitative analysis shows improved correlation between textual similarity and visual feature similarity in our learned representations. This alignment proves particularly valuable in disambiguation scenarios where multiple objects satisfy partial aspects of textual descriptions.

Interactive effectiveness

The interactive refinement mechanism provides substantial practical benefits beyond quantitative performance improvements. User studies reveal reduced cognitive load through intuitive click-based feedback compared to text-based correction methods. The visual nature of interaction aligns well with human spatial reasoning capabilities.

Error analysis shows that interactive feedback most effectively addresses boundary refinement issues and disambiguation between similar objects. Initial predictions typically capture approximate object locations correctly, with user feedback primarily refining precise boundaries and resolving ambiguities. Multi-round interaction effectiveness diminishes gradually, with the first three clicks providing the most substantial improvements. This pattern suggests that efficient interaction design should focus on maximizing the impact of initial feedback rounds while providing diminishing but consistent improvements in subsequent interactions.

Limitations and future directions

Several limitations warrant consideration for future development. Processing of extremely long textual descriptions occasionally leads to reduced performance, suggesting opportunities for improved text processing strategies. Very high resolution images may benefit from hierarchical processing approaches that balance detail preservation with computational efficiency. The current interactive paradigm focuses on click-based feedback, though alternative interaction modalities such as brush strokes or voice commands could provide additional flexibility. Integration of multiple feedback types within unified frameworks represents a promising research direction. Generalization across different domains and visual styles requires further investigation. While our approach demonstrates robust performance across standard benchmarks, evaluation on specialized domains such as medical imaging or satellite imagery would provide valuable insights into generalization capabilities. Real-time deployment considerations suggest opportunities for model compression and optimization. Techniques such as knowledge distillation and neural architecture search could yield more efficient variants suitable for resource-constrained environments while maintaining competitive performance.

Conclusion

This work introduces a novel architecture for text-guided interactive image segmentation that integrates Vision Mamba, Qwen large language model, and CLIP-based cross-modal alignment. Our approach addresses fundamental limitations of existing methods through efficient visual processing, sophisticated textual understanding, and robust multimodal fusion. The integration of interactive refinement capabilities transforms static segmentation into a collaborative process between users and AI systems. Experimental validation demonstrates substantial improvements over state-of-the-art methods across standard benchmarks, with IoU improvements of 2.4-3.2% and user efficiency gains of 43%. Comprehensive ablation studies confirm the contribution of each architectural component, while computational analysis demonstrates practical viability for interactive applications.

The broader implications of this work extend beyond immediate performance improvements. The successful integration of state space models in dense prediction tasks opens new possibilities for efficient visual processing. The combination of large language models with computer vision systems establishes foundations for more sophisticated multimodal understanding. Interactive capabilities point toward more collaborative human-AI workflows in creative and analytical applications.

Future research directions include extension to video sequences, integration of additional interaction modalities, and exploration of few-shot adaptation capabilities. The modular architecture provides a foundation for continued innovation in multimodal interactive systems across diverse application domains.

Mechanism of flavor deterioration in chicken meat with varying degrees of wooden breast: Insights from integrated HS-GC-IMS and UPLC-MS/MS-based metabolomics
Authors: Hui Lu, Yuru Zhang, Laixue Ni, Jingzhai Liu, Wei Wang, Shujie Liu, Yunguo Liu, Dacheng Kang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study investigated the flavor deterioration mechanism in wooden breast (WB) chicken meat using 41-day-old Cobb broilers. Meat was categorized by WB severity (normal to severe). Integrated sensory evaluation, headspace-gas chromatography-ion mobility spectrometry (HS-GC-IMS), and ultra-performance liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (UPLC-MS/MS) revealed that increasing WB severity significantly reduced sensory scores. Volatile compound analysis showed decreased levels of aldehydes, alcohols, and esters but increased sulfur-containing off-flavors. Metabolomics identified 1097 metabolites, with significant accumulation of specific compounds in moderate and severe WB. Key flavor compounds correlated positively with phenylpropanoids and negatively with lipids. Enrichment analysis highlighted arachidonic acid metabolism, the tricarboxylic acid cycle, and amino acid metabolism as core pathways. Flavor deterioration is primarily driven by oxidative stress-induced lipid peroxidation and disrupted amino acid metabolism, leading to flavor precursor imbalance. These findings provide a metabolic basis for improving chicken meat quality.Keywords: Woody breast myopathy, Flavor compounds, Metabolites, Correlation analysis, Metabolic pathways

Introduction

Chicken breast is widely regarded as the most consumed poultry product worldwide, owing to its low fat content, low caloric value, high protein concentration, and cost-effectiveness (Gkarane et al., 2020; Zhang, Hao, et al., 2021; Zhang, Zhang, et al., 2021). With the rapid advancement of intensive farming practices, improving broiler growth performance and production efficiency has become a primary goal in the poultry industry. However, this progress has been accompanied by a high incidence of muscular abnormalities, with Wooden Breast (WB) disease emerging as a predominant and representative condition. WB has emerged as a critical challenge that hinders quality improvement in the broiler production sector. WB is a growth rate-associated myopathy that primarily affects the pectoralis muscle in broilers. The characteristic pathological manifestations include myofiber fragmentation, disorganized architectural arrangement, and abnormal proliferation of connective tissue, frequently associated with increased muscle firmness, superficial hemorrhages, and other macroscopic signs of deterioration (Praud et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2022). These structural abnormalities directly alter the biochemical composition of muscle tissue, thereby impairing the synthesis and accumulation of flavor compounds, ultimately leading to reduced sensory quality and palatability of meat products (Tasoniero et al., 2016).

Flavor is a key indicator for assessing meat quality, shaped by the synergistic interactions of volatile organic compounds and non-volatile metabolites (Zhang, Hao, et al., 2021; Zhang, Zhang, et al., 2021). Its formation is closely associated with lipid oxidation, amino acid metabolism, and energy metabolism in muscle (Chen et al., 2025; Huo et al., 2022; Khan et al., 2015). With increasing consumer demand for higher meat quality, the sensory attributes, flavor composition, and nutritional profile of chicken meat have received growing scientific and commercial interest (Fu et al., 2022). Although current research on WB has addressed aspects such as nutritional regulation and pathological characteristics (Niu et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2023), and has largely focused on elucidating its physicochemical properties and improving processing performance (Muñoz-Lapeira et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025), the dynamic changes in flavor compounds during the WB process, as well as the mechanisms underlying the interactions between these flavor compounds and endogenous muscle metabolites, remain insufficiently understood.

In recent years, the integration of metabolomics and flavoromics has become a powerful strategy for elucidating the molecular mechanisms underlying meat flavor formation (Li, Kalmu, et al., 2024; Li, Zou, et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2024). Through systematic analysis of non-volatile metabolites and volatile flavor compounds, this methodology reveals the intrinsic relationships between metabolic networks and flavor profiles. Significant progress has been made in flavor research of various meats with integrated analytical framework, such as pork, beef, and selected poultry products (Fang et al., 2024; Jiang et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2025; Yu et al., 2024). However, research on the correlation between flavor compounds and metabolites in chicken breast with varying degrees of WB condition remains insufficient. In particular, understanding of how key metabolic pathways regulate the synthesis of flavor compounds remains limited.

This study investigated 41-day-old Cobb white-feathered broiler breast meat categorized by WB severity into normal breast meat (NB), mild wooden breast (LWB), moderate wooden breast (MWB), and severe wooden breast (SWB) groups. By integrating sensory evaluation with headspace-gas chromatography-ion mobility spectrometry (HS-GC-IMS), ultra-performance liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (UPLC-MS/MS), and multivariate statistical analysis, we systematically investigated the flavor profiles, metabolic variations, and their intrinsic correlations across different severity grades of WB. This study aimed to elucidate the regulatory mechanisms underlying metabolic network variations associated with WB, to clarify the correlation pathways between key metabolites and characteristic volatile flavor substances, and to provide a theoretical foundation and scientific basis for enhancing the flavor quality of affected meat and developing targeted quality control strategies.

Materials and methods

Sample preparation

The samples were collected from Linyi Jinluo Win Ray Food, Co. Ltd., Linyi, Shandong, China. The broilers used were Cobb white-feathered birds, aged 41 d, with an average body weight of 2.89 ± 0.35 kg. The processing was conducted in accordance with the company's standard slaughter and segmentation procedures. Breast fillets were graded within 2 h postmortem based on visual assessment and manual palpation, following the criteria established by Tijare et al. (2016) and Li, Kalmu, et al. (2024), Li, Zou, et al. (2024), with minor modifications. They were divided into four treatment groups, with three individuals selected for each group. The specific sampling criteria were as follows:

NB: breast fillets exhibiting no white striping and no hardened areas on the surface. WB was classified into three grades: LWB, characterized by slight changes at the cranial and caudal ends of the breast fillet with minimal firmness at the cranial portion; MWB, where the entire cranial segment exhibited firmness while the central to caudal regions remained pliable; and SWB, presenting generalized firmness accompanied by surface hemorrhaging and exudate deposition (Fig. 1a). Following the method described by Trithavisup et al. (2024), the samples were subjected to thermal processing. Each 40 g sample was individually vacuum-sealed in a plastic pouch and immersed in a water bath maintained at 95 °C until the core temperature reached 75 °C.

Sensory evaluation

Building on the sensory evaluation methodology established by Martín-Miguélez et al. (2024) with minor modifications, a blind testing protocol was implemented in which each sample was assigned a random code. Standardized sensory descriptors were applied to four key attributes (color, aroma, taste, and texture) of the samples, as detailed in Table 1. The sensory panel consisted of 30 members with 15 males and 15 females, aged between 20 and 25 years. Panelists were instructed to avoid consuming any substances that could influence flavor perception before the evaluation sessions. Prior to evaluating samples from different groups, panelists should rinse their mouths with pure water and conduct assessments independently without any communication. Each panelist was required to evaluate all attributes of the samples using a 1-to-25 scoring scale. Three samples per group were assessed, and the average score for each attribute was calculated from the individual ratings provided by all panel members. This sensory evaluation is ethically granted. As there is no human ethics committee or formal document process, this study does not force participation and fully discloses the research requirements and risks to the participants. Participants give oral consent and their data will not be disclosed without their knowledge. Participants can withdraw from the study at any time.

HS-GC-IMS

VOCs from chicken breast samples with four different degrees of WB were analyzed using an MXT-WAX capillary column (Restek Corporation, USA) coupled to an IMS instrument (FlavourSpec®, Gesellschaft für Analytische Sensorsysteme GmbH, G.A.S., Dortmund, Germany), following a modified protocol based on the method described by Cao et al. (2025). Two grams of sample were weighed into a 20 mL headspace vial and incubated at 80 °C for 15 min. Subsequently, 500 μL of headspace gas was extracted and injected into the system via a heated syringe maintained at 105 °C. Column temperature was kept at 60 °C. The flow rate of carrier gas nitrogen (purity >99.999%) was maintained at 2 mL/min for the initial 2 min, then gradually increased to 10 mL/min between 2 and 10 min, further ramped up to 100 mL/min from 10 to 20 min, and held constant at this rate for an additional 10 min until the end of the run. For the IMS, nitrogen (purity >99.999%) was employed as the drift gas at a flow rate of 75.0 mL/min and a temperature of 45 °C.

The retention indices (RI) and drift times (DT) of VOCs were compared with those in the GC retention index database (NIST 2020) and the IMS migration time database for qualitative identification, using n-ketones (2-butanone, 2-pentanone, 2-hexanone, 2-heptanone, 2-octanone, and 2-nonanone, analytical grade) as internal standards. The relative contents of VOCs were quantified using the area normalization method. Three samples were determined for each treatment group.

UPLC-MS/MS

The breast meat samples at four distinct WB stages were weighed to 50 ± 5 mg. Then, 500 μL of pre-chilled 80% methanol was added, followed by the introduction of steel beads and homogenization. The mixture was incubated at −20 °C for 30 min and then centrifuged at 20,000 ×g for 15 min. Subsequently, 400 μL of the supernatant was subjected to a second centrifugation at 20,000 ×g for 15 min. For analysis, 10 μL of the resulting supernatant from each sample was collected. Three samples were determined for each treatment group.

Following the methodology of Peng et al. (2025) with minor modifications, non-volatile metabolite analysis was performed using a Vanquish Flex UPLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Bremen, Germany) equipped with an ACQUITY UPLC T3 column (100 × 2.1 mm, 1.8 μm; Waters, UK). The column temperature was maintained at 40 °C, with an injection volume of 10 μL and a flow rate of 0.35 mL/min. The mobile phase was composed of solvent A (5 mM ammonium acetate, 5 mM acetic acid, and water) and solvent B (acetonitrile). The gradient elution program was as follows: 0–0.5 min, 5% B; 0.5–6.0 min, linear increase from 5% to 95% B; 6.0–7.5 min, isocratic elution at 95% B; 7.5–7.6 min, linear increase from 95% to 100% B; 7.6–8.0 min, isocratic elution at 100% B; 8.0–8.1 min, linear decrease from 100% to 5% B; and 8.1–10.0 min, isocratic elution at 5% B.

Metabolite identification was performed using an Orbitrap Exploris 120 high-resolution tandem mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Bremen, Germany) equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source. The top four precursor ions with signal accumulation intensities exceeding 5000 were selected from the primary mass spectrum for secondary fragmentation scanning. The secondary resolution was set to 15.0 K.

Statistical analysis

Following standardized preprocessing, multivariate analysis of the data was conducted using the R package (ropls), incorporating both Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and Partial Least Squares Discriminant Analysis (PLS-DA). The robustness of the PLS-DA model was evaluated through five-fold cross-validation and permutation testing. Variable Importance in Projection (VIP) values were computed to quantify the contribution of each variable to the classification. Independent two-group comparisons were performed using the t-test to determine statistical significance (P < 0.05). Finally, Spearman's correlation analysis was employed to evaluate the association between paired variables.

The biological replicates for each parameter were set to three (n = 3), with three technical replicates performed for each as well. All measurement results are expressed as mean ± standard deviation. For sensory evaluation, the non-parametric Kruskal-Wallis test was employed to assess quality differences among the various degree of WB groups, followed by the Dunn's test for multiple comparisons at a significance level of P < 0.05. Furthermore, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was performed and Tukey's multiple comparison test was applied to identify significant differences among chicken meat groups, with statistical significance being setting at P < 0.05. Data processing and graphical visualization were performed using SAS 9.2 and Origin 2021 software.

Results and discussion

Sensory evaluation analysis

The sensory evaluation results of chicken breast with varying degrees of WB are presented in Fig. 1b. As the degree of WB increased, the overall sensory quality of chicken breast showed a significant downward trend. Significant textural differences were observed, with the SWB group displaying the firmest texture, closely linked to myofiber necrosis, connective tissue proliferation, and an increase in β-sheet structures of collagen (Wang et al., 2024). In terms of taste, the NB group demonstrated the most favorable attributes, while the deteriorated taste quality in the WB groups may be attributed to reduced muscle protein degradation resulted from alterations in protein structures. Aroma evaluation showed no significant differences between the NB and LWB groups (P > 0.05), whereas the MWB and SWB groups exhibited a notable decline (P < 0.05), likely associated with lipid oxidation and the consequent loss of volatile flavor compounds (Chen et al., 2023). Regarding color attributes, the NB group demonstrated the most favorable appearance, while the SWB group showed a significant discoloration (P < 0.05),

However, sensory evaluation was found to have limited discriminatory power in characterizing WB meat. Therefore, this study employed HS-GC-IMS technology to analyze volatile compound profiles for more precise flavor characterization. Furthermore, by integrating UPLC-MS/MS-based untargeted metabolomics, metabolic alterations were investigated to elucidate the intrinsic mechanisms underlying textural deterioration and flavor attenuation in WB meat.

3.1 HS-GC-IMS analysis.

VOCs analysis

Fig. 2a displays a two-dimensional spectrum of VOCs, in which the color intensity of spectral signals reflects the relative concentration levels. The analytical results reveal that the peak intensity in the NB group was markedly higher compared to other regions, indicating significant inter-group differences in compound abundance. Moreover, as shown in Fig. 2b, the profiles of VOCs were largely consistent across chicken breast samples with different degrees of WB. Thus, the NB group exhibited higher levels of flavor-related compounds, a result that aligns with the sensory evaluation findings.

Fig. 2c illustrates that region A (NB) of normal breast meat is predominantly characterized by high concentrations of VOCs, specifically unsaturated aldehydes ((E)-2-heptenal, (E)-2-pentenal, and 1-octanal) and saturated aldehydes (hexanal and 1-nonanal). These lipid oxidation products contribute fresh fatty and grassy aromas, acting as indicators of high flavor quality (Park et al., 2025). Characteristic alcohols, such as 1-octen-3-ol and 1-hexanol, along with esters including ethyl hexanoate and sec-butyl acetate, were present in substantial concentrations (Table 2). The former originates from normal lipid oxidation (imparting a mushroom-like aroma), while the latter reflects coordinated amino acid and fatty acid metabolism (contributing a fruity note). A relatively high concentration of benzaldehyde (bitter almond, cherry, and nutty) suggests active amino acid metabolism (Bleicher et al., 2022). The aforementioned characteristics suggest that the activation of lipid oxidation and amino acid degradation pathways forms the basis for the development of typical breast meat flavor.

As for the WB groups, Region B (LWB) preserved certain characteristics of NB, as both regions show considerable concentrations of 3-methylbutanal (chocolate, fatty) and 2-methyl-2-hepten-6-one (citrus, fruity), indicating that amino acid degradation and lipid oxidation processes were still partially active during the initial phases of wooden breast development. Region C exhibited newly detected compounds in LWB group including butanal (pungent, fruity), acetic acid (spicy), and 3-hydroxy-2-butanone (buttery, creamy). These changes indicate that mild muscle fiber damage may have promoted lipid oxidation, resulting in the production of short-chain compounds as well as elevated ketone levels (Kitajima et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2011). Although the overall flavor profile deviated from the norm, certain desirable sensory attributes were retained. The characteristic VOCs in Region D (MWB) were predominantly small-molecule aldehydes, alcohols, and ketones, including n-pentanal (faint banana), heptaldehyde (fatty), ethanol (aromaticity), 1-pentanol (balsamic), and 2-butanone (fruity, camphor). Concurrently, long-chain unsaturated aldehydes associated with premium flavor profiles, such as (E)-2-heptenal, exhibited a significant reduction. This suggests that chicken with moderate WB exhibited sensory attributes characterized by fermented notes and oxidative rancidity (Chen et al., 2024). The region E (SWB) was characterized by the presence of sulfur-containing compounds and high accumulation of long-chain unsaturated aldehydes and esters. Significantly elevated levels of 3-(methylsulfanyl) propanal, a methionine degradation product with sulfurous notes, along with (E)-2-octenal, a marker of severe lipid oxidation, as well as butanol, 1-propanol, and propyl acetate, confirm the substantial accumulation of precursors associated with sulfurous and rancid off-flavors in severely WB, indicating a marked deterioration in sensory quality (de Sousa Fontes et al., 2024).

Fig. 2e reveals the compositional characteristics of VOCs in chicken breast with varying degrees of WB. Aldehydes (45.85–46.39%), ketones (22.15–22.40%), and alcohols (19.82–21.51%) were consistently present at relatively high concentrations across all treatment groups, followed by esters (7.11–8.19%). Based on their relative abundance and diversity, aldehydes, ketones, and alcohols were identified as the predominant volatile flavor compounds in chicken breast.

Multivariate statistical analysis of VOCs

To further examine and differentiate the flavor profiles of four chicken breast samples exhibiting varying degrees of WB, the PCA was initially conducted on the HS-GC-IMS data. The first two principal components, PC1 (67.66%) and PC2 (10.70%), collectively accounted for 78.36% of the total variance, indicating that the majority of the information across all samples could be elucidated by these two principal components (Fig. 2f). PLS-DA further validated the intergroup differences (Fig. 2g), with a model accuracy of 82.30%. After 200 permutation tests, the model showed R2 = 0.3853 and Q2 = −1.2436, confirming no overfitting (Fig. 2h). Sixteen key VOCs were identified based on variable importance in projection (VIP > 1), including acetic acid propyl ester, ethyl 2-methylpropionate, ethyl 3-methylbutanoate, (E)-2-octenal, decanal, 1-butanoic acid, (E)-2-nonenal, benzaldehyde, (Z)-4-heptenal, 3-(methylsulfanyl)propanal, 1-octanol, ethanol, butanol, (E)-2-hexen-1-al, sec-butyl acetate, and 3-pentanol (Fig. 2i). Among the 16 VOCs, 8 exhibited the highest concentrations in the NB group, while 5 VOCs showed peak levels in both the MWB and SWB groups. Of these, 13 VOCs were found to differ significantly between groups based on t-tests (P < 0.05) (Fig. 2j). In WB groups, the levels of aldehydes and esters were significantly decreased. This decline can be attributed to structural damage in muscle fibers resulted from myodegeneration, which impairs cellular metabolism and contributes to the depletion of key flavor compounds (Li, Ha, et al., 2023; Li, Huo, et al., 2023). Notably, 3-(methylsulfanyl)propanal was elevated in SWB group. Known for its pungent sulfurous odor, this compound serves as an indicator of sulfur-containing amino acid degradation under advanced woody breast conditions and directly contributes to impaired meat flavor (de Sousa Fontes et al., 2024).

UPLC-MS/MS analysis

Overview of metabolomics

A total of 1097 metabolites were detected and classified into 16 categories across the four chicken breast groups (Fig. 3a): lipids and lipid-like molecules (333), organoheterocyclic compounds (223), organic acids and derivatives (185), benzenoids (144), organic oxygen compounds (62), phenylpropanoids and polyketides (40), organic nitrogen compounds (37), nucleosides, nucleotides, and analogues (30), alkaloids and derivatives (21), organosulfur compounds (9), homogeneous non-metal compounds (5), lignans, neolignans and related compounds (2), organic 1,3-dipolar compounds (2), organophosphorus compounds (1), and others (3). Among these, lipids and lipid-like molecules, organoheterocyclic compounds, organic acids and derivatives, benzenoids constitute the most abundant compound categories, each exhibiting an average relative abundance exceeding 20%.

To investigate the differences in non-volatile metabolites across the four chicken breast meat groups, this study performed multidimensional statistical analyses. PCA results indicated that the first two principal components cumulatively accounted for 40.62% of the variance (PC1 = 28.29%, PC2 = 12.33%) (Fig. 3b). The four breast meat samples exhibiting varying degrees of WB showed clear separation, with the SWB group displaying more pronounced differences compared to the other groups, suggesting distinct non-volatile metabolite profiles. PLS-DA was employed to identify and characterize differential metabolites in chicken breast meat across different woodiness levels (Fig. 3c). The model achieved a cumulative interpretation rate of 42.62%, with clustering patterns being consistent with PCA results. Furthermore, permutation tests with 200-fold cross-validation yielded R2 and Q2 values of 0.9368 and − 0.6542, respectively (Fig. 3d), effectively excluding the risk of model overfitting. Based on the criteria of VIP > 1 and P < 0.05, 82 significantly differential metabolites were identified (Fig. 3e). Subsequently, the top 30 metabolites were selected for expression level analysis (Fig. 3f). The results demonstrate significant differences in metabolite expression profiles among the four groups. In the SWB group, numerous oxidative stress-related metabolites were significantly up-regulated (log2(FC) > 1, P < 0.05), accompanied by the depletion of flavor precursors, likely due to cell membrane disruption caused by excessive myofiber proliferation (Zhang et al., 2025).

Identification of differential metabolites

To further investigate metabolic alterations in WB across varying degrees of severity, differential metabolites were identified and quantified in each WB group using the NB group as a control. The selection criteria for differential metabolites were set as follows: fold change (FC) ≥ 1.2, P < 0.05, and VIP ≥ 1. Compared with the NB, the number of differentially accumulated metabolites progressively increased with WB severity: 37 in LWB (25 up, 12 down), 78 in MWB (56 up, 22 down), and 97 in SWB (72 up, 25 down) (Fig. 3g). Furthermore, a comparative analysis of shared and distinct differential metabolites across groups using a Venn diagram (Fig. 3h) identified six commonly altered metabolites: 1,1,1-Trifluoroheptadecan-2-one, 11,12-Epoxyeicosatrienoic acid, 16-HDoHE, irigenin trimethyl ether, 15(S)-Hydroxyeicosatrienoic acid, and mesoxalic acid. The SWB vs NB group exhibited the highest number of unique differential metabolites (50), followed by the MWB vs NB group (28), and the LWB vs NB group (24). Among these, the MWB vs NB group shared the greatest overlap with the other two comparisons, demonstrating 5 and 39 common differential metabolites with the LWB vs NB and SWB vs NB groups, respectively. This finding suggests that the MWB serves as a transitional phase within the metabolic network, retaining residual features of the LWB while simultaneously undergoing early transformation toward the highly wooden state, accompanied by progressively more complex metabolite dynamics.

To visually illustrate the changes in metabolite abundance under varying degrees of WB, differentially accumulated metabolites across comparative groups were visualized using volcano plots (Fig. 3i to 3k). The screened differential metabolites were subsequently subjected to metabolic pathway analysis (Fig. 3l to 3n). The results indicate that in the LWB versus NB group (Fig. 3i), the significantly upregulated differential metabolites were primarily urocanic acid, armexifolin, and hexanoylcarnitine, while the downregulated differential metabolites were predominantly 1,1,1-trifluoroheptadecane, 9-octadecenamide, and sphinganine. Specifically, hexanoylcarnitine participates in fatty acid metabolism, and its abnormal elevation may disrupt lipid degradation, thereby indirectly impairing the synthesis of short-chain flavor aldehydes and diminishing meat aroma (Choi et al., 2025). Sphinganine plays a crucial role in cellular membrane formation. A reduction in this component may disrupt sphingolipid metabolism, increase membrane permeability, lead to the leakage of umami nucleotides, and consequently diminish the flavor of meat (Liu et al., 2022; Yu et al., 2021).

In the MWB versus NB group (Fig. 3j), 11(12)-EpETE, acetamide 2-((2-hydroxyethyl)thio)-N-(3-(3-(1-piperidinylmethyl)phenoxy)propyl)-, and 5-HEPE were significantly upregulated, while 2-methyl-5-(methylthio)furan and oxalic acid were markedly downregulated. Among these compounds, 11(12)-EpETE is an oxidation product of arachidonic acid. Its elevated concentration may competitively bind to esterases, thereby reducing ethyl hexanoate synthesis and diminishing the fruity aroma characteristics contributed by esters in meat (Takahashi et al., 2012). Furans are significant aroma compounds, and the formation mechanism of 2-methyl-5-(methylthio)furan is closely linked to thiamine degradation. A reduction in its levels directly inhibits the generation of bis(disulfide), a key meat aroma compound, consequently weakening the characteristic meaty flavor (Zhang et al., 2024).

In the SWB versus NB group (Fig. 3k), a significant increase in differential metabolites was observed. Notably, 11(12)-EpETE, 11-(3-pentyloxiran-2-yl)undec-9-enoic acid, and 13,14-dihydro-15-keto PGF2α were markedly upregulated, while 1,1,1-trifluoroheptadecan-2-one was significantly downregulated. Specifically, 13,14-dihydro-15-keto PGF2α, a terminal product of non-enzymatic lipid peroxidation, was significantly elevated, indicating enhanced lipid peroxidation in the SWB group. This led to substantial generation of oxidative aldehydes/ketones such as hexanal and nonanal, thereby intensifying rancid off-flavors in lipids (Hui et al., 2024). Meanwhile, 1,1,1-trifluorooctadecan-2-one, a compound belonging to the ketone class and known for its contribution to “creamy” and “fruity” flavor profiles, exhibited reduced levels that directly diminish aromatic complexity. This reduction led to sensory impoverishment and a lack of flavor in severely WB meat (Xu, Shang, et al., 2025; Xu, Zhang, et al., 2025).

Metabolic pathway analysis

Pathway enrichment analysis was conducted based on the KEGG database to visually elucidate the interrelationships among differential metabolites in chicken breast muscles with varying degrees of WB, as illustrated in Fig. 3l to 3n. In the present study, most of the enriched metabolic pathways were linked to amino acid metabolism, energy metabolism, and lipid metabolism.

During the mild WB stage, pathways including histidine metabolism, citrate cycle, and sphingolipid metabolism exhibited significant enrichment (Fig. 3l). Histidine, serving as a precursor to umami amino acids, undergoes aberrant metabolic processing that promotes its degradation into histamine, thereby reducing umami compounds in chicken meat (Choi et al., 2025). The dysregulated citrate cycle contributes to disordered cellular energy supply, indirectly impairing the synthesis of flavor precursors such as organic acids and amino acids (Li, Kalmu, et al., 2024; Li, Zou, et al., 2024). Disruption of sphingolipid metabolism compromises cellular membrane integrity and accelerates the loss of umami compounds in meat products (Li, Dong, et al., 2022; Li, Li, et al., 2022). Regarding the MWB in chickens, the significantly enriched pathways include histidine metabolism, linoleic acid metabolism, arachidonic acid metabolism, and nitrogen metabolism (Fig. 3m). The oxidation of linoleic acid and arachidonic acid generates flavor precursors such as aldehydes and ketones, while the dysregulation of these metabolic pathways leads to accumulation of oxidative products including hexanal and nonanal, resulting in off-flavors characterized as rancidity (Ding et al., 2022; Xu et al., 2022). Concurrently, impaired nitrogen metabolism suppresses the synthesis of umami amino acids such as glutamate and aspartate, ultimately diminishing the characteristic flavor profile of poultry meat (Li et al., 2025). Under SWB conditions, glycerophospholipid metabolism, linoleic acid metabolism, as well as alanine, aspartate and glutamate metabolism were significantly enriched (Fig. 3n). The marked enrichment of glycerophospholipid metabolism compromised cellular membrane integrity, releasing fatty acids that underwent oxidation to generate off-odors. Concurrently, disruptions in alanine, aspartate and glutamate metabolism synergized with membrane damage, leading to a significant reduction in umami compounds and ultimately contributing to diminished flavor profiles in chicken breast (Li, Dong, et al., 2022; Li, Li, et al., 2022; Qi et al., 2025; Xu, Shang, et al., 2025; Xu, Zhang, et al., 2025).

Correlation analysis of key volatile substances with differential metabolites

In the WB meat, metabolic dysregulation in muscle tissue leads to the generation of flavor-influencing metabolites, such as organic acids, VOCs, and amino acid derivatives, that collectively define the meat's sensory profile (Estévez et al., 2020; Li, Dong, et al., 2022; Li, Li, et al., 2022). Accordingly, Spearman correlation analysis was first performed to assess the associations between differential metabolite profiles and characteristic flavor compounds (Fig. 4a). The results indicate significant positive and negative correlations (|r| > 0.8, P < 0.05) between key volatile compounds and differential metabolites. The identified compounds comprised 11 categories of lipids and lipid-like molecules, 6 classes of organoheterocyclic compounds, 4 varieties of organic acids and derivatives, along with one kind each of benzenoids, nucleosides/nucleotides/analogues, organic oxygen compounds, and phenylpropanoids and polyketides.

Spearman correlation analysis (a) and RDA analysis (b) between differential VOCs and metabolites of different grades of chicken breast.

Alcohols (ethanol, Butanol), esters (sec-butyl acetate, ethyl 2-methylpropionate), and aldehydes (decanal, 3-(methylsulfanyl)propanal) exhibited significant positive correlations with phenylpropanoids and polyketides (lansamide 4, irigenin trimethyl Ether), organic oxygen compounds (1-(2,4,6-trimethoxyphenyl)-1,3-butanedione, eperisone), organic acids and derivatives (glycolic acid, D-N-(carboxyacetyl)alanine), and organic nitrogen compounds (methapyrilene). Conversely, they demonstrate negative correlations with benzenoids, lipids and lipid-like molecules, nucleosides/nucleotides/analogues, and organoheterocyclic compounds. These correlated patterns suggest an association between metabolic shifts toward increased amino acid catabolism and carbohydrate oxidation and the progression of myofiber hardening in WB. These metabolic shifts not only promote the accumulation of intermediate metabolites, such as organic acids and oxygenated organic compounds, but also drive their subsequent transformation into non-volatile compounds (including alcohols, aldehydes, and esters), thereby shaping the final flavor profile (Abasht et al., 2016; Thanatsang et al., 2020).

To elucidate the regulatory mechanism of the degree of WB on metabolic substances and flavor compounds, metabolites were ranked based on the criteria |r| > 0.8, VIP > 1, and P < 0.05. The top ten representative differential metabolites and flavor compounds were subsequently selected for Redundancy Analysis (RDA) (Fig. 4b). RDA1 (75.95%) and RDA2 (17.92%) cumulatively explained 93.87% of the total variance, effectively reflecting the association between metabolites, flavor compounds, and the grouping of meat samples with varying degrees of WB. Samples displayed distinct clustering patterns in the ordination space, suggesting that the degree of WB exerts a significant influence on both metabolic and flavor profiles. Ethyl 2-methylpropionate (r = 0.776, p = 0.003), (E)-2-nonenal (r = 0.635, p = 0.015) and 3-(methylsulfanyl)propanal (r = 0.583，p = 0.019)demonstrated strong correlations with the core differential metabolites. Specifically, 12,13-EpOME was identified as a key correlated metabolite in the NB group. It reduces aldehyde production by inhibiting the lipoxygenase pathway and demonstrates significant negative correlations with Decanal and (Z)-4-heptenal. In contrast, 11(12)-epETE, 12,13-EpOME, and 20-hydroxy-6Z,15Z-eicosadienoic acid exhibited positive correlations with the MWB and SWB groups. These compounds are oxidative metabolites derived from polyunsaturated fatty acids, including arachidonic acid and linoleic acid, suggesting that pronounced lipid oxidation is a hallmark metabolic feature of the SWB group. Furthermore, during the process of flavor compound differentiation, ethyl 2-methylpropionate (r = 0.776, p = 0.003) showed a significant positive correlation with samples from the NB and LWB groups, but a significant negative correlation with those from the MWB and SWB groups. Notably, ethyl 2-methylpropionate, (E)-2-nonenal, and 3-(methylsulfanyl)propanal serve as critical characteristic indicators reflecting the metabolic and flavor profile differences in WB meat.

Analysis of the metabolic regulatory network associated with core metabolite-driven formation of characteristic flavors

To elucidate the association between differential metabolites and characteristic flavor compounds in meat with varying degrees of WB, functional annotation was performed using the KEGG database to systematically reconstruct the core metabolic pathways underlying flavor development (Fig. 5).

Metabolic pathways for the formation based on KEGG. Red color represents differential metabolites; black ones are metabolites in the metabolic pathway. (For interpretation of the references to color in this figure legend, the reader is referred to the web version of this article.)

In the metabolic network of WB meat, the lipid oxidation cascade serves as a central driver of flavor alterations (Zhang, Hao, et al., 2021; Zhang, Zhang, et al., 2021). Linoleic acid and arachidonic acid, as the primary polyunsaturated fatty acid substrates, undergo oxidation through pathways such as lipoxygenase (LOX) and cyclooxygenase/cytochrome P450 (CYP) enzymes (Zhao et al., 2025). Linoleic acid metabolism yields the key intermediate 12(13)-EpOME, while arachidonic acid metabolism produces 5-Hydroxyeicosatetraenoate (5-HETE) and 11,12-epoxyeicosatrienoic acid. These oxidative metabolites, either directly or through their breakdown into volatile aldehydes and alcohols such as (E)-2-nonenal, (E)-2-octenal, and 1-octanol, serve as key precursors to the characteristic flavor compounds in WB meat (Griesser et al., 2009; Rinwi et al., 2024).

The glycolytic pathway of carbon metabolism and the TCA cycle serve as central hubs within the metabolic network, regulating the supply and flux of flavor precursors. Glucose is catabolized through glycolysis to produce pyruvate, which is then converted into acetyl-CoA, a key precursor for both the TCA cycle and the biosynthesis of various volatile compounds (Si et al., 2025). Concurrently, glucose may also be metabolized through the pentose phosphate pathway to generate intermediates such as sedoheptulose-7-phosphate. The NADPH produced through this pathway is essential for mitigating oxidative stress and supporting anabolic processes (Antonelo et al., 2022). Acetyl-CoA enters the TCA cycle and is further converted into key nodal metabolites, including citrate and fumarate (Huang et al., 2023). These metabolites not only supply carbon skeletons for fatty acid synthesis but also indirectly regulate the efficiency and balance of flavor compound (including aldehydes, alcohols, and esters) biosynthesis through anaplerotic reactions and their integration with amino acid metabolism (Li, Dong, et al., 2022; Li, Li, et al., 2022; Qin et al., 2023).

The metabolism of alanine, aspartate, and glutamate regulates flavor metabolic flux by influencing the production of amino acid-derived compounds. Metabolites, such as N-acetyl-l-aspartate and l-aspartate, participate in the dynamic adjustment of key metabolic nodes in meat with varying degrees of WB. Concurrently, amino acids such as aspartic acid, glutamic acid, and alanine play a crucial role in the formation of key flavor compounds through reactions such as Strecker degradation, deamination, and decarboxylation (de Sousa Fontes et al., 2024; Shahidi et al., 2024). l-aspartate is converted into L-argininosuccinate, thus contributing to the regeneration of fumarate in the TCA cycle. These amino acid metabolites function as amino group donors in transamination reactions, thereby influencing the metabolic fate of carbon skeletons, by directing them toward either energy metabolism or the biosynthesis of flavor precursors. Additionally, their derived peptides play a role in taste perception. These metabolic changes are linked to oxidative stress induced by WB (Li, Ha, et al., 2023; Li, Huo, et al., 2023; Marchesi et al., 2019).

Although terpenoid and related metabolites are not represented in the current pathway map, the synthesis of isoprenoid precursors through the mevalonic acid pathway, initiated from acetyl-CoA generated via lipid oxidation, establishes a fundamental biochemical foundation. These precursors are subsequently metabolized into terpenoid flavor compounds, a process in which metabolic flux may be modulated by differing severities of WB (Ackman & Hooper, 1973; Nie et al., 2024).

In summary, the flavor profiles of WB at varying severity levels are resulted from the synergistic interactions of multiple metabolic pathways. The hyperactivation of linoleic acid metabolism and arachidonic acid metabolism induced by oxidative stress generates characteristic off-flavor precursors, including oxidized lipids and cleavage products such as aldehydes and alcohols. Glycolysis and the TCA cycle modulate the synthesis of fundamental flavor compounds by regulating the supply and metabolic flux of key precursors like acetyl-CoA. Meanwhile, alanine, aspartate and glutamate metabolism directly contribute to flavor compound generation while dynamically adjusting interconnected metabolic nodes, thereby refining the complexity of the overall flavor profile. The coordination and dysregulation of these three core pathways collectively drive the differential accumulation of characteristic flavor substances (including aldehydes, alcohols, and sulfur-containing compounds) across different degrees of WB, thereby providing an integrated metabolic framework for elucidating the underlying mechanisms of flavor formation.

Conclusions

This study systematically elucidated the metabolic mechanisms underlying flavor deterioration in meat with varying degrees of WB through integrated multi-omics approaches, including sensory evaluation, HS-GC-IMS, and UPLC-MS/MS. The results demonstrated that sensory scores of breast meat significantly decreased with the progression of WB. This deterioration was accompanied by reduced levels of aldehydes, alcohols, and esters among volatile flavor compounds, while sulfur-containing compounds such as 3-(methylsulfanyl)propanal accumulated anomalously, contributing to pronounced flavor deterioration. Untargeted metabolomics identified 1097 metabolites, predominantly with lipids and lipid-like molecules, organic heterocyclic compounds, and organic acid derivatives. Correlation analysis and pathway enrichment revealed that arachidonic acid metabolism, linoleic acid metabolism, carbon metabolism, TCA cycle, and alanine, aspartate and glutamate metabolism were key pathways regulating flavor formation. WB-induced oxidative stress exacerbated lipid peroxidation and interfered with amino acid metabolism, thereby suppressing the synthesis of umami compounds and facilitating the accumulation of off-flavor precursors, ultimately compromising flavor balance. This study elucidates the intrinsic metabolic mechanisms underlying flavor deterioration in WB meat, establishing a theoretical basis for enhancing meat flavor quality through targeted modulation of key metabolic pathways.

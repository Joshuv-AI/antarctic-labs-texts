Systematic Review: Evaluating the Prognostic Value of Granulomas in Crohn’s Disease
Authors: Julia Smith, Courtney Bartel, Lynn Warner, Jasbir Dhaliwal
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and AimsHistological differences are among the characteristics that divide Crohn’s disease (CD) from ulcerative colitis. Granulomas are one feature known to develop in a subset of patients with CD, though their exact role in prognosis is relatively limited. Therefore, there is an unmet need to further understand the features that predict patterns in disease courses for people with CD.MethodsA comprehensive literature search was conducted in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-Analyses guidelines. Databases used for the search were EMBASE, PubMed, CINAHL, Scopus, and Web of Science. Two reviewers independently reviewed abstracts and full text for inclusion. Overall, 46 studies were included for data collection and quality assessment.ResultsThe 46 studies included 37 cohort analyses, four systematic reviews (three with meta-analysis), four cross-sectional studies, and one case–control report. Common exposure and outcome variables included age, disease location and behavior, clinical severity, medication utilization, and surgical requirement. There was considerable risk for bias in accordance with the Critical Appraisal Skills Programme tool, with only 26 studies having met ≥ 80% of high-quality assessment criteria. Varying relationships were observed between granulomas and clinical aspects of disease such as phenotype, severity, and medication utilization.ConclusionThis systematic review aimed to centralize the current literature and provide insight into the possible prognostic value of granulomas in CD. Several studies suggested clinical relevance; however, larger studies that adjust for confounders are needed to truly elucidate the underlying pathophysiology and significance of granulomatous CD.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s10620-026-10080-y.Keywords: Crohn’s disease, Granuloma, Phenotype, Histopathologic

Introduction

Crohn’s disease (CD) is a chronic, immune-mediated disease characterized by patchy inflammation that can involve any region of the gastrointestinal (GI) tract. Histologically, CD is marked by transmural inflammation, crypt architectural distortion, basal plasmacytosis, Paneth cell hyperplasia, and lymphoid aggregates [1]. Although variably present at diagnosis (9–60% of cases), the presence of noncaseating epithelioid granulomas is a well-established histopathologic hallmark of CD [2–4]. These granulomas are composed of tightly clustered epithelioid histiocytes, often with multinucleated giant cells, and lack central necrosis.

Noncaseating epithelioid granulomas are found in several other conditions, including sarcoidosis, chronic beryllium disease, hypersensitivity pneumonitis, and chronic granulomatous disease [5]. The underlying pathogenesis remains unclear, but it is theorized that a persistent antigen triggers a tightly controlled series of events that transform nonepithelioid macrophages into the organized epithelioid macrophages. In the setting of idiopathic CD-related granulomas, the inducing signal may be one or more features, such as inappropriately produced or expressed self-antigens, intestinal microbiota, or other cellular proteins [5].

Although the diagnostic role of granulomas is well recognized, their prognostic significance remains unclear. Prior studies reported inconsistent associations between granuloma presence with disease behavior, severity, complications, and treatment response. To address this knowledge gap, we completed a systematic review to evaluate the prognostic value of granulomas in CD.

Materials and Methods

Database Search and Inclusion Criteria

A comprehensive search strategy was developed and performed by a librarian in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. Databases used for the search were EMBASE, PubMed, CINAHL, Scopus, and Web of Science. All searches were performed on the same day, August 31, 2023, March 24, 2025, May 13, 2025, and May 15, 2026. Key terms in the search included “Crohn Disease,” “Crohn’s Disease,” “regional enteritis,” “enteritis regionalis,” “granuloma,” “prognosis,” and “pathology,” among others. No date limiters were added to the search.

Data Extraction, Outcomes, and Quality Assessment

Extracted data included author’s name, year of publication, information relevant to study design, aims, population description, methods, clinical outcome measurements related to granuloma presence (including disease severity, extent, and complications), and any clinical interventions, if applicable (Supplementary Table 2). Articles were reviewed by two reviewers (JS and CB) to evaluate eligibility and to extract data. Common study features were noted during data extraction for presentation in the text and can be visualized throughout (Supplementary Table 2, Figs. 1, 2). Effect measures extracted from the studies utilized included odds ratios, hazard ratios, and relative risks, depending on outcome type and study design.

Visual Summary of Pertinent Findings Identified Across Literature

The quality of the studies included in this systematic review was evaluated using the Critical Appraisal Skills Programme (CASP) assessment tool [6]. Two reviewers (JS and CB) used this tool to asses each study’s aim, design, population, data integrity, data analysis, results, conclusions, and overall risk of bias. Disagreements were resolved between reviewers with the help of a third reviewer (JD) when agreement could not be reached. (Fig. 3; Supplementary Figs. 1–5; Supplementary Tables 3, 4).

Results

Study Characteristics

A total of 4,074 records were identified across all databases. Citations were uploaded to and deduplicated through Covidence systematic review tool (Covidence, Melbourne Australia). One article was identified manually and included for review. After automatic deduplication by Covidence, the inclusion and exclusion criteria were applied to 2751 unique studies. Studies were included if they evaluated or discussed clinical outcome measurements related to the presence or absence of epithelioid granulomas in luminal CD. We included observational cohort studies, cross-sectional studies, retrospective cohort studies, systematic reviews, and meta-analyses that evaluated granulomas in CD as primary or secondary outcome measures. Studies only available as abstracts or conference presentations, those without available full text, and those written in languages other than English without an available English translation were excluded. Articles evaluating metastasizing CD, with or without granulomas, were also excluded. A team of two reviewers (JS and CB) screened the initial pool of 2751 articles and selected 185 articles for full-text review. From these, the reviewers selected 46 articles for data extraction. Reviewers discussed and resolved disagreements. Study selection is presented in a PRISMA flowchart (Fig. 4).

PRISMA Flowchart of included studies

All 46 included studies were published between 1982 and 2022, with 39 published after 2000, and were comprised of 37 cohort studies, four systematic reviews (three with meta-analyses), four cross-sectional studies, and one case–control study. Each study evaluated at least one CD-related outcome in relation to the presence of luminal granulomas. Twenty-one studies evaluated granulomas alongside additional histological or clinical prognostic variables relevant to CD (Fig. 1; Supplementary Table 2). Across the four systematic reviews, 21 primary studies overlapped; however, substantial heterogeneity in search strategies, databases, and inclusion criteria precluded complete overlap.

Outcomes associated with granuloma presence included disease location (n = 15), disease behavior (n = 15), medication use (n = 15), hospitalization (n = 9), extraintestinal manifestations (n = 9), perianal disease (n = 16), surgical intervention (n = 14), and overall disease activity (n = 13). In all studies, patients were stratified based on the presence or absence of epithelioid granulomas in surgical or mucosal biopsy specimens, typically classified as granulomatous CD (gCD) versus nongranulomatous CD (ngCD) (Fig. 2; Supplementary Table 2).

Twenty-five studies assessed granulomas as the primary variable of interest and their clinical significance in CD. These included two systematic reviews with meta-analysis [7, 8], 22 cohort studies [2, 4, 9–28], and one case–control study [3]. Most cohorts consisted of adult patients, [2–4, 9–12, 14, 16, 23, 26] with eight studies conducted exclusively in pediatrics [13, 17–19, 22, 24, 27, 28], and five in mixed-age cohorts [7, 15, 20, 21, 25]. Outcomes associated with granuloma presence included disease location (n = 18) [2–4, 7, 10, 12, 13, 15–17, 19, 22–28], disease behavior (n = 14) [2, 4, 7, 10, 12, 18, 19, 21–25, 27, 28], medication utilization (n = 15) [2, 4, 7, 10, 11, 13, 14, 18–20, 22–24, 27, 28], hospitalization rates (n = 9) [2, 4, 7, 11, 12, 17, 20, 24, 27], extraintestinal manifestations (EIM) (n = 9) [4, 9, 10, 20, 21, 23, 24, 27, 28], presence of perianal disease (n = 15) [2, 7, 10, 12, 17–24, 26–28], surgical intervention (n = 13) [2, 4, 7, 8, 10–12, 14, 17, 19, 20, 22, 28], and disease activity (n = 11) [3, 4, 7–9, 14–18, 21]. Eight studies reported outcomes following CD-related bowel resection [2, 9, 12, 14–16, 21, 23], while the remainder evaluated outcomes across the disease course [4, 7, 8, 10, 11, 13, 17–20, 22, 24, 25, 27, 28].

The remaining twenty-one studies evaluated a range of clinical and histological features influencing CD prognosis, including, but not limited to, granulomas [29–49]. These included two systematic reviews (one with meta-analysis) [32, 44], four cross-sectional studies [30, 33, 35, 49], and sixteen cohort studies [29, 31, 34–43, 45–48]. The nongranulomatous features evaluated included neutrophilic [31], eosinophilic[31, 35], and lymphoplasmacytic infiltration [29, 31, 34, 35], crypt inflammation [30], basal cell plasmacytosis [29], and Paneth cell metaplasia [29]. Two studies focused on inflammation at bowel resection margins following surgery [32, 39]. In addition to these histologic features, other clinical outcome variables evaluated included perianal disease, stricturing and penetrating complications, disease recurrence, and disease location [31, 33, 34, 36–43, 48, 49] (Fig. 2, Supplementary Table 2).

Demographics and Disease Phenotype Characteristics

Granuloma Presence Shows Minimal Association with Age or Sex Across Studies

No significant association between age and granuloma presence at diagnosis or time of surgery was found across 21 studies [4, 13, 14, 17, 19–22, 25–28, 30, 31, 33, 37, 39–42, 49]. However, six studies found an association between granulomas and younger age, either at time of diagnosis [3, 11, 35] or at time of primary bowel resection [11, 12, 15, 23]. Four of these, all in adult-based cohorts, found granulomas more frequently in younger patients undergoing surgery [11, 12, 15, 23]. In a pediatric cohort study, Sassine et al.[35] reported a lower median age at diagnosis among children with gCD compared to those without, 13.5 years versus 14.4 years (OR 0.90, 95% CI 0.85, 0.95, p = 0.0002). One study evaluated granulomas at both time of diagnosis and time of primary bowel resection [11], and found that those with gCD were diagnosed at an earlier age (p = 0.005) and underwent surgery at a younger age compared to patients with ngCD (37.4 vs 38 years, p = 0.004). This study also observed a higher proportion of females in the gCD group who underwent surgery (p = 0.0009), a finding supported by Denoya et al.[23] (p = 0.017). Other studies did not find any association between sex with granuloma presence [3, 22, 38].

Heterogeneous Associations Between Disease Location and Granulomatous Crohn’s Disease

Several studies identified the relationships between disease location and granuloma presence. Four studies reported that ileocolonic (L3) involvement was more common in patients with gCD compared to ngCD [7, 25, 27, 28]. Additionally, two pediatric studies found granuloma presence to be less frequent among patients with isolated ileal (L1) involvement compared to colonic or ileocolonic involvement. In an adult cohort, Leong et al.[10] found an association between granulomas and isolated colonic (L2) disease (OR 15.7, 95%CI 1.9, 127.3, p = 0.01), with no association observed for ileal or upper GI involvement. Six studies, including Idestrom et al.,[19] observed an increased frequency of upper GI (L4) involvement among patients with gGD [4, 7, 22, 24, 27]. However, the majority of studies (n = 13) reported no significant relationship between gCD and disease location [2, 3, 9, 12, 16, 17, 23, 25, 26, 30, 33, 34, 36].

Granulomatous Crohn’s Disease Is Frequently Associated with Perianal Disease

Seventeen studies evaluated the association between gCD and perianal disease (PD) [10, 11, 17–24, 27, 28, 33, 40–42, 49]; 11 of which reported a higher frequency of PD in patients with gCD compared to those with ngCD [11, 17, 18, 20, 22, 23, 27, 33, 41, 42, 49]. Seven of these studies were pediatric cohorts [17, 18, 22, 27, 41, 42, 49], three with mixed pediatric and adult cohorts [20, 23, 33], and one involved only adults [11]. Nine studies found PD to be more prevalent at the time of diagnosis in patients with gCD [17, 20, 22, 23, 27, 33, 41, 42, 49], and four reported a high rate of PD over the disease course in patients with gCD [11, 19, 22, 42]. Additionally, in a multivariate analysis, Lawrence et al.[18] demonstrated a three-fold increased risk of PD development in patients with gCD (HR 3.24, 95% CI 1.40–7.48). Finally, Lin et al.[49] utilized the Van Assche Score (VAS) to analyze the severity of PD and found that patients with gCD demonstrated higher VAS than those with ngCD (adjusted beta [aβ] = 3.16; p = 0.019).

Granulomatous Crohn’s Disease Is Frequently Associated with a More Complicated Disease Behavior

Among 15 studies which evaluated internal CD complications[2, 4, 7, 10–12, 19, 21, 24–28, 34, 49], including stricturing (B2) and penetrating disease (B3), eight studies observed associations between gCD and disease behavior [2, 7, 10–12, 24, 25, 34], six of which found that gCD was only associated with penetrating disease (B3) [2, 7, 11, 12, 25, 34]. Leong et al.[10], however, demonstrated that gCD was associated with B2 phenotype (p = 0.003). Additionally, Rothschild et al.[24] determined that, across an entire study period, the overall increase in rate of B2 or B3 development was greater among patients with gCD than those with ngCD (p = 0.01).

Association of Granulomas with Clinical Disease Severity Laboratory Markers

After analyzing clinical laboratory differences among patients with and without granulomas at diagnosis, one pediatric study demonstrated lower serum albumin levels among children with gCD versus ngCD, 3.43 g/L opposed to 3.75 g/L, p = 0.008 [22]. Another study revealed that adult patients with gCD were more likely to have at least one occurrence of elevated CRP (73.2% vs 48.0%), or ESR (58.1% vs 35.1%), or both (p < 0.0001) [11]. Further, in a multivariate analysis, this study found associations between elevated CRP and ESR levels between patients with gCD and those with ngCD (p < 0.001) [11]. Molnár et al.[4] found higher CD activity index and C-reactive protein values in patients with gCD (p = 0.01). However, these laboratory parameters are inherently nonspecific, and the analyses did not adjust for potential confounding factors associated with elevated inflammatory markers. In a related analysis, Turner et al.[3] demonstrated that CD patients with granulomas at diagnosis had increased odds of presenting with anemia (OR 2.06, 95% CI 2.02–2.10).

Hospital and Medication Utilization

Granuloma Presence and CD-Related Hospitalizations

Four studies found CD-related hospitalizations to be more frequent among patients with history of gCD [4, 7, 11, 24]. One meta-analysis evaluating hospitalization data from three studies demonstrated that patients with gCD had 3.88 times increased odds of being hospitalized for CD compared to patients with ngCD (OR 3.88, 95% CI 1.44, 10.49, I2 = 64%) [7]. In addition to greater frequency of hospitalizations, Rothschild et al.[24] found median time to first CD hospitalization to be sooner among children with gCD (3.85 vs 7.11 years, p = 0.027). However, five other studies that evaluated hospital utilization and gCD observed no significant associations [2, 12, 17, 20, 43].

Granulomatous Crohn’s Disease Is Associated with Advanced Therapy Utilization

Eighteen studies evaluated medication use in gCD [2, 4, 7, 10, 11, 13, 14, 18–20, 22–24, 27, 28, 36, 39, 43], eleven of which found significant associations with gCD (Fig. 2) [2, 4, 7, 11, 13, 19, 20, 22, 24, 28, 43]. Adult patients with gCD had greater cumulative prednisone exposure than those with ngCD (1.73 vs 1.08 years, p < 0.0001) and were more frequently prescribed immunomodulators (78.1% vs 49.1%, p < 0.0001) and narcotic medications (49.2% vs 39.8%; p < 0.001)[11]. Similar findings were observed across other adult and pediatric cohorts, where patients with gCD used immunomodulators more often (58–60% vs 32–39%)[2, 4] and initiated therapy earlier (median 4.5 vs 33 months, p = 0.01)[19]. Five studies reported higher biologic therapy use among those with gCD [7, 11, 13, 22, 28], including a nearly 20% greater initiation rate over a six-year period (60.4% vs 41.8%, p < 0.001) [11]. A meta-analysis further supported this association (OR 1.66, 95% CI 1.07–2.59, I2 = 27%), and, in a pediatric surgical cohort, granuloma presence independently predicted anti-TNF use (aHR 2.1, 95% CI 1.04–4.2, p = 0.04)[43]. Additional studies noted higher overall immunosuppressive utilization among patients with gCD (p = 0.015) [2, 28] and shorter time to biologic escalation [13, 24]. Six studies found no significant differences [10, 14, 18, 23, 27, 36].

Granulomatous Crohn’s Disease May Be Associated with Greater Surgical Requirement

Nineteen studies evaluated the relationship between gCD and surgical outcomes [4, 8–12, 14, 19, 20, 22, 23, 26–28, 32, 37, 38, 40, 43]. Seven reported a higher frequency of IBD-related surgeries among patients with gCD compared to those with ngCD [4, 8, 12, 14, 20, 38, 40]. In a cohort of ileocecal resections, Cullen et al.[38] identified granuloma presence at the initial operation as the only predictor of both disease recurrence and need for repeat surgery (p = 0.03 and p = 0.01, respectively), with recurrence defined by endoscopically active disease. Several studies also noted shorter time to surgery among patients with gCD, including both primary [11, 20] and repeat procedures [9, 14, 38]. Ertem et al.[14] demonstrated that granuloma presence at primary surgery was associated with an almost two-fold increased risk of subsequent operations (RR 1.92, 95% CI 1.07–3.4, p = 0.03). Similarly, a meta-analysis using twelve studies found that patients with gCD had a significantly greater likelihood of reoperation (OR 2.38, 95% CI 1.43–3.95, p < 0.001) [8] with substantial heterogeneity of pooled odds ratios in a Chi-squared test (p < 0.001). When analyzed by time-to-event methods, gCD was also associated with higher reoperation rates (HR 1.62, 95% CI 1.19–2.21, p = 0.002) without significant heterogeneity (p = 0.55) [14]. Collectively, these findings indicate that granuloma presence is consistently linked to increased surgical burden and earlier need for operative management in CD.

Granulomatous Crohn’s Disease May Be Associated with Greater Disease Severity and Risk of Recurrence

Twenty studies evaluated the relationship between gCD and disease progression [4, 7, 11, 14, 16, 21, 27–31, 36, 40, 43–49]. Seven found no significant association between granulomas and disease severity or relapse, though definitions of “severe disease” varied considerably [16, 21, 29, 31, 40, 43, 46]. Several studies, however, identified links between gCD and markers of increased disease activity. Schmitz-Moorman et al.[30] reported granulomas more frequently in biopsies with severe active inflammation (p = 0.01). One cohort demonstrated an elevated risk of clinical relapse (aHR 1.50, 95% CI 1.04–2.15, p = 0.03) [36], and another found granulomas more common among patients with severe postoperative endoscopic recurrence (modified Rutgeert’s ≥ i3, p < 0.01) [14]. Johnson et al.[11] further showed gCD was associated with progressive bowel damage measured using the Lémann Index, over five years.

Risk-of-Bias Assessment

Study criteria were assessed utilizing the CASP Critical Appraisal Tool, which allowed for comprehensive bias assessment across shared aspects of different study designs. Shared criteria included whether each study addressed a clearly focused issue, cohort recruitment acceptability, accurate exposure and outcome measurements, identification of confounders, complete participant follow-up, precision of results, and generalizability of results. Additional criteria for systematic reviews and meta-analyses were assessed, including search strategy, data selection, validity assessment, and precision of meta-analysis statistics when appropriate (Supplementary Tables 3, 4).

Only thirteen out of 46 studies met 100% quality criteria in a risk-of-bias assessment. This was largely due to variation in sample size (range N = 32–10,456, median 109.5, IQR 65–257), study design, and statistical analyses. In addition to inconsistent sample sizes, there was a difference in methodology for reporting granulomas across all studies. Granulomas were identified in resected bowel specimens in ten studies [11, 12, 14, 16, 23, 31, 32, 37, 39, 43], 17 in mucosal biopsy samples [3, 4, 9, 13, 15, 17, 18, 22, 24, 27–30, 33, 40, 48, 49], and four analyzed a combination of both [2, 10, 25, 26]; 11 studies did not undertake histological re-review, and instead utilized baseline pathology reports, or methodology was unclear [19, 21, 34–36, 38, 41, 42, 45–47]. The remaining four studies were systematic reviews [44], three with meta-analysis, without inherent re-evaluation of pathologic specimens [7, 8, 32]. As a result, granulomas may have been undetected due to sampling variability and interobserver differences. Likewise, there was substantial variation between studies driven by exposure and outcome measurements, thus limiting generalized comparability; for example, 25 studies assessed granulomas as the primary variable of interest, whereas the remaining 21 studies included granuloma presence as nonprimary. Moreover, many of the included studies only undertook univariate analyses, further limiting the rigor and generalizability of results (Fig. 3; Supplementary Figs. 1–5; Supplementary Table 3). Thirteen studies met full inclusion criteria demonstrating several methodological strengths, including comprehensive identification and adjustment for confounding variables, minimization of bias in both exposure and outcome ascertainment, and appropriate statistical power. Notably, the systematic reviews and meta-analyses included in this evaluation exhibited the lowest risk of bias across assessed domains, a finding that likely reflects the inherent methodological rigor of the meta-analytic framework and adherence to standardized reporting protocols. Twenty-one studies included in our review were also collectively represented across the four meta-analyses/systematic reviews. Variation occurred due to differences in database utilization and inclusion criteria (Supplementary Table 5).

Discussion

This systematic review evaluated the prognostic significance of granulomas in CD across forty-three studies. While several studies reported associations between granulomas and perianal disease, colonic involvement, complicated disease behavior, and biologic use, a substantial proportion of studies did not consistently replicate these findings. This inconsistency underscores the substantial heterogeneity in study design, sample size, and methodological rigor.

In the studies evaluating perianal disease (PD), eleven found PD to be proportionally higher in patients with gCD either at diagnosis or throughout disease course. Ten of these eleven studies assessed pediatric [17, 18, 22, 41, 42] or mixed pediatric and adult cohorts [20, 23, 33]. Notably, in younger children, the co-occurrence of fistulizing PD and granulomas may raise consideration of underlying monogenic etiologies, such as X-linked inhibitor of apoptosis (XIAP) deficiency [50]. Thus, future pediatric analyses should consider monogenic IBD when evaluating granuloma–phenotype relationships.

Evidence linking granulomas to internal complications was limited, with few studies identifying an association with B2 (stricturing) disease behavior. While granulomas may reflect chronic inflammation, a precursor to stricture formation [51–53], further research is needed to elucidate the underlying mechanisms [51, 54].

Eleven studies evaluating medication utilization reported significantly different treatment patterns among patients with gCD [2, 4, 7, 11, 13, 19, 20, 22, 24, 28, 43], including greater exposure to corticosteroids, immunomodulators, and biologics. This likely reflects an elevated inflammatory burden in gCD, as well as broader shifts in CD management toward earlier deployment of advanced therapies. This may reflect the histologic conditions required for granuloma development, including prolonged or persistent antigenic stimuli, and a TH1-dominant cytokine milieu characterized by TNF-α and IFN-γ [55], and may independently drive escalating therapeutic requirements, as observed in other granulomatous disease models [56]. However, the relationship between granuloma presence and medication utilization is complex; drug clearance pharmacokinetics, severity of inflammation, and broader immune dysregulation may each contribute to the observed treatment patterns independent of granuloma formation itself [57, 58].

Several studies further identified gCD to be associated with greater surgical burden, particularly an increased risk of reoperation following initial surgical intervention [8, 9, 14, 38]. The need for repeated surgery in CD is multifactorial, encompassing histologic, immunologic, and patient-level factors [59]; granulomas may nonetheless play a role in sustaining a disease course that is more refractory to medical management.

Although only a minority of studies demonstrated a direct association, the constellation of findings, including increased perianal disease, a tendency toward stricturing behavior, and greater biologic utilization suggests that gCD may represent a more aggressive disease phenotype. Comprehensive, prospectively designed studies with standardized assessment with more comprehensive phenotypic characterization are needed to delineate the interrelationships among granuloma presence, disease severity, internal complications, and surgical burden in CD.

Risk of bias assessment showed that more than half of the included studies met at least 80% of the low-risk-of-bias criteria. However, several studies demonstrated high risk of bias due to methodological and statistical limitations, including unaddressed confounding variables (Supplementary Figs. 1–5; Supplementary Table 3). Substantial variation in sample size further contributed to heterogeneity in effect estimates. Despite significant findings in some studies, heterogeneity across studies limits clear interpretation of the prognostic significance of granulomas in CD. Larger studies that adjust for confounders are needed to truly identify the underlying pathophysiology and clinical significance of gCD and inform potential refinements to disease classification. Factors like large sample size, standardization of granuloma identification, multivariable statistical analyses, and adjustment for many disease confounders would likely improve the knowledge gap in this area for future work.

This systematic review aimed to centralize the current literature and provide insight into the possible prognostic value of granulomas in CD. The results demonstrate possible associations between gCD and disease phenotype, severity, and medication utilization. However, the overall high risk of bias across studies limits the interpretability of observed associations and highlights the challenge of drawing definitive, generalizable conclusions about the prognostic value of granulomas in CD.

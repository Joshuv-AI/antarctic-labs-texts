Training of trainers program in interprofessional education for health professionals in Türkiye
Authors: Aysel Başer, Funda İfakat Tengiz, Hale Sezer, Pınar Ayvat, Zeynep Sofuoğlu, Gül Çelik, Gülbin Konakçı, Gülşah Barğı, Özlem Çınar Özdemir, Seda Çiftçi, Hatice Demir, Egemen Şen, Hatice Şahin

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTraining of Trainers Program that combines both theoretical and practical components and effectively enhance educators’ competencies in interprofessional education are rarely encountered. This study aimed to design and evaluate a Training of Trainers Program in Interprofessional Education intended to enhance participants’ awareness, knowledge, and self-perceived competencies related to the design, implementation, and improvements of interprofessional education practices among healthcare educators.MethodsThe study was planned and conducted between June 2023 and March 2024. The two-phase Training of Trainers Program (TTP) was implemented between February 20 and 24, 2024. The first phase consisted of three-day online interactive theoretical training. Two-day face-to-face practical training was the second phase of the TTP which included interactive studies with large groups and practical activities in professional skills rooms with small groups. The practical training was based on various scenarios created by the researchers themselves. Following the TTP, all participants were assessed using a demographic data form, reflection assignment, group assignment, and multiple-choice questions.ResultsThe TTP was found to be effective and participants benefited from the TTP. The TTP significantly increased participants’ competencies across all measured dimensions, including roles-responsibilities; team and teamwork; IPE communication; lifelong learner; collaborative leadership; relationships with patients and recognizing their needs; and ethics (p = 0.001). Participants’ responses to the satisfaction questionnaire showed that the program generally provided high satisfaction. High scores were given to areas, such as the enrollment process, training content, effectiveness of the trainers, and overall program satisfaction. Participants also highlighted the program’s comprehensive content, applied teaching strategies, and trainer competencies, while only one participant suggested improving time management.ConclusionThe TTP provide important improvement in participants’ competencies The TTP, which was developed by blending both online and face-to-face education, is a successful program in both developing interprofessional competences and reaching the goals.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12909-025-07743-1.Keywords: Training of trainers, Interprofessional education, Competencies of health professionals, Trainer development program, Training program design, Interprofessional education, Faculty development, Health professions education, Trainers program, Medical education

Introduction

Interprofessional Education (IPE) has become a global priority in health professions education due to its potential to improve collaboration, communication, and patient-centered care. Effective IPE equips health professionals with the competencies necessary for working in interdisciplinary teams and responding to complex healthcare needs such as chronic disease management, patient safety, and population-based care [11, 14, 26]. International frameworks and national strategies continue to emphasize the integration of IPE into both undergraduate and continuing professional development programs [2, 7]. Despite increasing institutional interest, many IPE initiatives suffer from insufficient infrastructure, limited faculty capacity, and a lack of evidence-based educator development. A significant barrier to sustainable IPE implementation is the shortage of trained educators who possess pedagogical skills, theoretical grounding, and confidence to lead interprofessional learning effectively [1, 30]. Research has shown that many educators feel unprepared to facilitate IPE, especially when expected to guide learners from different professional backgrounds in collaborative and context-sensitive environments [1, 6]. Interprofessional educator development aims to renew or support the multifaceted roles of faculty members in their institutions [13, 19, 20, 29].

The need for structured educator development is further emphasized in settings where interprofessional collaboration is emerging or inconsistently applied. In Türkiye, faculty members across health disciplines have expressed high levels of awareness regarding the value of IPE but report limited experience and confidence in implementing such programs. A recent needs assessment study conducted with academic staff revealed major challenges including unfamiliarity with IPE methodology, lack of institutional models, and a clear demand for faculty development programs focused on interprofessional facilitation, instructional design, and application of adult learning strategies [5, 6].

Previous training initiatives in Türkiye have largely focused on student learning, leaving a critical gap in the development of educators capable of designing, delivering, and evaluating IPE programs [5, 6]. Although some programs have included elements such as gamification and simulation, there remains a lack of integration between theoretical frameworks and practical instructional tools that can build sustainable educator capacity across institutions. In response to this gap, blended learning approaches have gained prominence for their flexibility, scalability, and potential to integrate theoretical and applied components of faculty training. These approaches incorporate online modules, synchronous workshops, scenario-based discussions, and feedback mechanisms to ensure that learners not only acquire knowledge but also build confidence and self-efficacy in IPE practice [11, 24].

This study addresses a critical need by designing and evaluating a Training of Trainers Program (TTP) in Interprofessional Education tailored for healthcare educators in Türkiye. The program employs a blended learning model integrating adult learning principles, instructional design theory, and competency-based IPE frameworks to enhance participants’ awareness, knowledge, and self-perceived instructional competencies.

Materials and methods

Study design

This quasi-experimental study was conducted between June 2023 and March 2024. The Training of Trainers Program, designed for interprofessional education, was delivered in two phases from February 20 to 24, 2024.

This study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Ethical approval was obtained from the İzmir Democracy University Non-Interventional Ethics Committee (Approval No. 2023/97). All participants were informed about the study’s purpose and procedures, and their voluntary consent was obtained prior to data collection. This study was supported by TÜBİTAK under program 2237_A (application period 2023/2), with project number 1129B372300447, titled “Training of Trainers Program in Interprofessional Education for Health Professionals”.

Participants were selected following an open national call for applications announced to all higher education institutions in Türkiye. The inclusion criteria specified that participants must:

Since this training was conducted within the scope of a TÜBİTAK-funded project, it was essential to include only those faculty members whose academic credentials and institutional affiliations could be verified through the ARBIS system. This ensured the eligibility, academic background, and professional relevance of all participants. Faculty without a background or advanced degree in health sciences were not included, as the program focused on interprofessional education for healthcare professionals.

In total, 48 faculty members meeting the inclusion criteria participated in the online theoretical phase of the Training of Trainers Program. This number exceeded the minimum required sample size, which was calculated using G*Power software (version 3.1) with an effect size of 0.5, a confidence level of 95%, and a power of 0.80, resulting in a recommended sample of at least 44 participants. The subsequent face-to-face practical training, conducted in İzmir province, was completed by 30 participants. These individuals formed the final analytic sample, as they had participated in both phases of the program and provided complete data for evaluation.

The training of trainers program structured according to the ADDIE model

Analysis

To inform the design of the Training of Trainers Program in Interprofessional Education, a literature review was conducted to identify faculty development needs in IPE and relevant competency areas [1, 3, 5–7, 9, 15, 18, 21, 22, 30]. Key challenges in the national context were also considered, including limited access to structured educator training, lack of standardization in IPE practices, and the need for pedagogical development in interprofessional facilitation.

Design

The TTP-IPE was structured using the ADDIE instructional design model. The program was designed as a 40-hour blended learning format: 24 h of synchronous online training over three days, followed by 16 h of face-to-face practical training across two days. The instructional plan emphasized active learning, adult education principles, and interprofessional collaboration. The training aimed to enhance participants’ knowledge and skills related to IPE core competencies such as communication, teamwork, ethics, mentoring, feedback, and reflection. Methods included surveys, Q&A sessions, gamification, and collaborative learning strategies (Fig. 1).

Development

Program content and instructional materials were developed collaboratively by a multidisciplinary team. Trainers and facilitators were recruited from four academic institutions (İzmir Democracy, Ege, İzmir Katip Çelebi, and İzmir Bakırçay Universities) and represented a range of professional backgrounds including medicine, dentistry, nursing, physiotherapy, and nutrition and dietetics. Four facilitators held PhDs in medical education, and all had previously completed interprofessional educator development programs. Educational content was developed for both online and in-person modalities, including PowerPoint slides, literature-based readings, assignments, training guides, and structured scenarios.

Implementation

The program was delivered between February 20 and 24, 2024, in two instructional modules:

Module 1: Online Training (3 days, 24 h)

Participants engaged in theoretical instruction focused on IPE principles, IPE activities and mentoring-coaching, educator roles, communication skills, conflict management, reflection and feedback, scenario writing and management, IPE in the clinic, and IPE evaluation practices. The online modules, delivered via Google Meet, were highly interactive and included group discussions, shared document reviews, and learning management through WhatsApp and Google Groups. Multiple instructional strategies grounded in adult learning theory were employed to enhance engagement and facilitate meaningful learning during the synchronous online module.

Formative surveys

were administered at the beginning of each session to assess participants’ baseline knowledge, expectations, and learning needs. These insights informed instructional emphasis and were also used to stimulate participant reflection and goal-setting.

Question-and-answer sessions

were integrated throughout the training to ensure continuous participant interaction. These sessions were further enriched using digital tools such as Kahoot! and Mentimeter, which were employed to deliver real-time quizzes both at the beginning and end of instructional blocks. These interactive assessments promoted active recall and reinforced key concepts.

Gamification techniques

were embedded into the program design to increase motivation and cognitive engagement. Knowledge-matching games, competitive quizzes, and scenario-based challenges were facilitated through platforms such as Kahoot! and Quizizz, providing immediate feedback and fostering a dynamic learning atmosphere.

To support collaborative and reflective learning, active learning platforms such as Padlet and Mentimeter were used to generate word clouds, build idea walls, and allow participants to post insights and responses anonymously or in real time. These digital environments enabled the visualization of group thinking, promoted inclusivity in participation, and allowed for collective knowledge construction. Activities were designed to capture participants’ evolving understanding, highlight key takeaways, and provide a platform for individual and group-level reflection. Collectively, these methods contributed to a highly interactive, learner-centered online experience, aligned with the principles of interprofessional education and adult learning. The trainers created a positive learning environment using student-centered learning methods (e.g. PowerPoint presentations, sharing documents from the literature, assignments, and homework). Sessions were conducted in large groups and interactively. All online learning sessions were recorded via Google Meet and shared with participants on Google Groups and WhatsApp groups.

Module 2: face-to-face training (2 days, 16 h)

The in-person module included three main components:

• Small group work: In small groups where trainers from more than one health profession participated, training guides and scenarios were distributed to participants. Participants were asked to read these scenarios, discuss them according to the IPE approach, and then elicit the competencies that would help students gain if each scenario were real. The purpose of using scenarios in small groups was to have participants adopt the IPE approach, experience integrating IPE into their own teaching processes, gain skills related to conflict resolution specified in the scenario, and develop reflection skills. Instructors acting as facilitators in the small group work observed the participants, videotaped the discussions, and prepared feedback on the participants’ status as IPE trainers. There was no single solution for scenarios; it was designed solely to create a discussion environment, to find out about the roles and responsibilities of other professions, to protect boundaries, to develop interprofessional communication skills, and to give the message that individuals from each professional group could be leaders in teamwork and that cooperation was needed for patient safety. At the end of the small group work, participants were asked to prepare a group assignment in standard slide format to present to all participants and to write an individual reflection letter.

• Presentation of the group assignments: On the second day of the face-to-face implementation, each small group presented the scenario they worked on to the remaining participants. In the presentation, the roles and responsibilities of the professional groups included, patient needs, ethics, communication and conflict situations, collaboration and leadership, and the emotions that emerged in the scenario were discussed. The presentation of group assignments allowed different scenarios to be addressed, and the participants’ critical evaluations of the scenarios contributed to their learning from each other.

• Reflection sessions: In the reflection session, participants were asked to read examples from their individual reflection letters and do an intra-group evaluation of themselves. In addition, sections from videos of small group work were shown to give feedback to the groups. Watching the videos of small groups and reading the reflection letters helped participants gain feedback and reflection skills.

Evaluation

To evaluate the effectiveness of the TTP-IPE, a mixed-methods approach was employed, integrating both quantitative and qualitative data. Only participants who completed both modules of the training (N = 30) were included in the analysis to ensure consistency and reliability of outcome data.

Quantitative evaluation included a pre- and post-training self-assessment using the Interprofessional Education Collaborative Competency Assessment Framework (IPE-CAF). This instrument enabled participants to rate their own perceived competencies across core IPE domains—such as teamwork, communication, ethics, and roles/responsibilities—before and after completing the training. The use of the IPE-CAF in this context allowed for the identification of self-perceived changes in knowledge and skill as a result of program participation.

In addition, a multiple-choice knowledge test was administered at the end of the program to assess objective learning outcomes related to IPE concepts, instructional design, and facilitation strategies.

Qualitative data were collected via an open-ended training satisfaction survey, which invited participants to provide narrative feedback on their overall experiences, highlight strengths of the program, and offer suggestions for improvement. Content analysis was used to identify recurring themes and sub-themes, particularly with regard to satisfaction, perceived learning gains, teaching strategies, and logistical aspects such as time management.

Data collection tools

Data were collected using a sociodemographic data form about participants’ descriptive characteristics, a multiple-choice question form to measure the level of knowledge that was gained as a result of the training, and an IPE Competency Assessment Form (IPE-CAF) which served as a self-assessment tool for participants to evaluate their perceived changes in competencies as a result of the training.

The sociodemographic data form consisted of closed-ended questions about age, gender, educational status, profession, and academic titles. The multiple-choice question form included two multiple-choice questions per course. These two forms were created on Google Forms.

The IPE-CAF was developed by researchers (n: 13) before TTP in IPE (The English version of the questionnaire has been uploaded as Supplementary File 1). It was created based on the items on which a consensus was reached following an evaluation with Krippendorff statistics after expert opinions were received. The IPE-CAF consisted of 31 items under seven main headings (roles-responsibilities; team and teamwork; IPE communication; lifelong learner; collaborative leadership; relationships with patients and recognizing their needs; and ethics). This form was used to evaluate participants’ perceptions about the competencies they needed in IPE education. Therefore, the form was employed to help participants to assess the changes about the competencies before and after the TTP. Each item on the form was scored using a five-point Likert scale (1: poor, 5: excellent). Scores on the form ranged from 31 to 155.

To evaluate participants’ satisfaction with the TTP, a separate Training Satisfaction Form was administered. This form was developed by the researchers prior to the implementation of the TTP and finalized based on expert opinions and item selection was finalized through consensus supported by Krippendorff’s alpha reliability analysis. It consisted of 17 items designed to assess the overall quality and effectiveness of the training. Sixteen items measured participants’ satisfaction with various aspects of the program, including content organization, planning, learning environment, and trainer competencies. Each item was rated using a five-point Likert scale (1 = poor, 5 = excellent), with total possible scores ranging from 16 to 80. The final item was an open-ended question that asked participants “Please describe one aspect of the training program you found beneficial, and one aspect you think could be improved.” Responses to this question were analyzed using qualitative content analysis to gain insights into participants’ perceptions and recommendations. The aim was to identify areas of strength and aspects needing improvement based on direct participant feedback.

Data analysis

The required sample size for the TTP was calculated using G*Power software. In the analysis of the collected qualitative and quantitative data, quantitative data were analyzed using SPSS 26.0 software. Statistical distributions were calculated in the analyses. Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was utilized to test the normality of the data. In the data obtained with the IPE competency assessment form (IPE-CAF), dependent groups pairwise comparisons were performed with the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. Cronbach’s alpha was calculated for the reliability analysis of the data obtained via the IPE-CAF and the training satisfaction form. The alpha value was accepted as 0.05.

For the qualitative component of the study, descriptive content analysis was applied to responses gathered from the open-ended question in the Training Satisfaction Form. Initially, all researchers independently reviewed and manually coded the textual data to identify recurring concepts and expressions. Following independent coding, the researchers engaged in collaborative discussions to consolidate the codes and establish overarching themes, ensuring consistency and interpretive validity. Through this process, four major themes were identified, each comprising multiple sub-themes derived from the codes. The analysis was conducted in alignment with accepted practices in descriptive qualitative research and was focused on summarizing participant perceptions and suggestions in a structured, transparent manner.

Qualitative data analysis of open-ended responses

The open-ended question in the Training Satisfaction Form asked participants:

“Please describe one aspect of the training program you found beneficial, and one aspect you think could be improved.”

A descriptive content analysis was conducted on responses to the open-ended question in the Training Satisfaction Form. Four overarching themes were identified, each reflecting distinct dimensions of participant experiences and perceptions. Within the first theme, Content of the Education, participants emphasized the comprehensiveness of the program, describing it as detailed, broad, and informative. They frequently highlighted the educational depth and real-world relevance of the topics, noting that the hands-on and scenario-based activities contributed significantly to their engagement and learning. The second theme, Efficiency of the Education, captured participants’ overall perception of the training’s utility and effectiveness. Many described the program as highly beneficial and called for its continuation and wider dissemination, indicating a desire for recurring or expanded implementations.

The third theme, Teaching Strategies, encompassed feedback related to instructional methods and trainer performance. Participants consistently described the educators as highly competent and praised their ability to facilitate active learning through interactive methods. This was reflected in comments appreciating both the clarity of presentations and the collaborative learning environment. The fourth and final theme, General Evaluation and Suggestions, included overarching impressions as well as ideas for improvement. While most participants expressed high satisfaction, some provided constructive feedback suggesting the integration of such programs into undergraduate curricula and expanding their reach to other regions. Notably, only one comment was coded as negative, referring to time management concerns and the suggestion to improve the pacing or distribution of content across sessions.

Discussion

This study aimed to design and evaluate a Training of Trainers Program in Interprofessional Education intended to enhance participants’ awareness, knowledge, and self-perceived competencies related to the design, implementation, and improvement of interprofessional education practices among healthcare educators in Türkiye. The program was grounded in the ADDIE (Analysis, Design, Development, Implementation, Evaluation) instructional design model. The program’s outcomes were clearly defined and measured using a mixed-methods approach, including pre- and post-assessment of self-perceived competencies via the IPE Competency Assessment Form (IPE-CAF), an objective multiple-choice knowledge test, and a detailed satisfaction survey including qualitative feedback. Quantitative results indicated statistically significant improvements across all IPE-CAF domains, particularly in roles and responsibilities, teamwork, communication, ethics, leadership, and lifelong learning.

Program effectiveness and learning outcomes

The effectiveness of the TTP-IPE was assessed using the IPE-CAF, a post-training multiple-choice test, and qualitative feedback from participants. Statistically significant improvements were observed in all seven domains of the IPE-CAF, including roles/responsibilities, teamwork, communication, ethics, collaborative leadership, patient-centeredness, and lifelong learning. This finding supports the effectiveness of the program in enhancing educators’ perceived IPE competencies. The Training of Trainers Program effectively enhanced participants’ interprofessional education competencies. Participants demonstrated notable improvements in self-perceived skills across all targeted domains, and the cognitive assessment reflected strong comprehension of the educational content. High levels of satisfaction further validated the relevance and quality of the program’s instructional design. Qualitative feedback supported these outcomes, emphasizing the usefulness of applied teaching strategies, the value of reflective learning methods, and the professional competence of facilitators. These findings collectively affirm the program’s success in addressing the developmental needs of health educators within an interprofessional context.

Integration of ADDIE model

The program’s structured application of the ADDIE model contributed to its coherence and effectiveness:

Analysis

The foundation of any educational program is grounded in a thorough needs assessment. Khan et al. emphasize that stakeholder involvement, including hospital management, educators, and policymakers, is vital for understanding the specific learning needs of different healthcare professionals [4]. Identifying these needs allows for tailoring training objectives to bridge gaps in interprofessional collaboration skills among trainees. Furthermore, initiating inquiries into pre-existing attitudes and behaviors toward interprofessional practice can provide context to the program, aligning with recommendations for core competencies in collaborative practice [27].

Despite the increasing importance of Training of Trainers programs for educators in the health field, interprofessional trainer development programs are rarely encountered in the literature, and the duration and scope of these programs vary and there is no example in Türkiye, either [1, 6, 11, 12, 14, 15, 26, 28].

The training needs were identified through both literature review and prior local research. Faculty reported significant gaps in IPE-related teaching experience, as noted in national studies [6, 15]. This informed the selection of IPE competencies and adult learning strategies.

Design

In this research design phase involves outlining the program structure, which includes determining learning objectives, selecting educational strategies, and planning assessments. The blended 40-hour structure combined synchronous online instruction with face-to-face experiential learning. Learning objectives were aligned with IPE core domains and instructional strategies were adapted to encourage active participation and interprofessional collaboration. Effective interprofessional programs should aim to enhance competencies necessary for collaborative practice, such as teamwork, communication, and role understanding [23]. Bushuven et al. advocate for the incorporation of reflective practices into the curriculum design to mitigate common issues like overconfidence and clinical tribalism among healthcare providers [8]. Constructive alignment of educational activities with desired learning outcomes is essential in ensuring coherence within the program, as articulated by Lepage-Farrell et al. [16].

Development

A multiprofessional academic team developed content using case-based scenarios, digital platforms (e.g., Padlet, Mentimeter), and gamified tools (e.g., Kahoot!, quzizz) to increase interactivity and engagement—an approach recommended by recent studies [4, 8].

Implementation

The phased approach enhanced accessibility, and the combination of small group discussions, group presentations, and reflection sessions supported skill acquisition and critical thinking—strategies shown to improve teamwork and leadership [7, 26].

Evaluation

Mixed-methods evaluation revealed improvement in perceived competencies and satisfaction, though the absence of a pre-training knowledge test was a limitation for measuring objective cognitive change. Overall, the ADDIE model serves as a comprehensive framework for the development of interprofessional train-the-trainer programs in healthcare education. By addressing each phase systematically, educational institutions can better prepare healthcare professionals for collaborative practice, ultimately leading to enhanced patient care outcomes.

Comparison with previous literature

The findings of this study extend the existing literature on interprofessional faculty development by demonstrating the effectiveness of a structured, competency-based training model grounded in instructional design principles [11, 14]. Comparable to the Interprofessional Team Development Program outlined by Summerside et al. [26], our program effectively enhanced participants’ confidence and leadership capacity in facilitating IPE. Distinct from extended, longitudinal formats, the five-day intensive delivery of our program successfully integrated theoretical instruction with practical application.

In contrast to fully virtual initiatives that emerged during the COVID-19 pandemic, such as the Kenya-based program by Abu-Rish Blakeney et al. [1], our blended approach preserved interactivity and experiential learning while ensuring broad national accessibility. The program was carefully aligned with core IPE competencies and adult learning methodologies, consistent with the ADDIE model. Interactive elements such as gamified assessments, peer-led feedback, and scenario-based group activities facilitated deep engagement. Importantly, the inclusion of a multidisciplinary educator cohort responded to Türkiye’s current need for a nationally scalable and standardized IPE educator training framework [6, 15].

Unlike prior studies that often focused on isolated outcomes such as participant satisfaction or self-reported skill improvement, this study employed a multi-tiered evaluation strategy. Through the use of validated instruments like the IPE-CAF, objective knowledge assessments, and systematic qualitative content analysis, the program’s impact was examined across cognitive, affective, and behavioral domains—meeting current calls for more comprehensive evaluation of faculty development in IPE [10, 25].

This study also addressed several structural and institutional challenges commonly cited in the IPE literature, including limited time, staffing constraints, and insufficient institutional support [6, 15, 17]. By embedding stakeholder-informed needs analysis into program planning and offering a flexible hybrid model, the TTP-IPE provides a feasible and adaptable strategy for broader national implementation.

One of the strengths of this study reinforces the utility of interprofessional train-the-trainer models and demonstrates how integration of systematic instructional design—such as the ADDIE framework—can strengthen the coherence, accessibility, and educational impact of such programs. By linking training needs, instructional design, development, delivery, and evaluation, the model ensured conceptual alignment and methodological rigor.The approach fills a critical gap in Türkiye’s faculty development efforts and offers a reproducible blueprint for health professions education globally.

Unlike the literature, the data collection and analysis process in our study was more comprehensive. This methodology provided a more objective assessment of the impact and effectiveness of the education. It is thought that the TTP in question will cover all health professions, offer flexibility, and foster collaborative practice with a comprehensive approach that operates independently.

Future studies may improve cooperation with participants and help do regular evaluations and identify new challenges. It is recommended that such TTP in IPE be designed and integrated into the trainer development activities of institutions. In addition, the impact of instructors participating in such training programs on the future professional practices of their students should be evaluated.

Limitations

This study has several limitations that should be considered when interpreting its findings. Although 44 participants engaged in the online module, only 30 completed the full training, primarily due to logistical constraints such as travel, accommodation, and institutional leave. This reduced participation may have limited the geographic and disciplinary diversity of the final sample. Additionally, while the IPE-CAF was administered both pre- and post-training to assess changes in self-perceived competencies, the multiple-choice knowledge test was only conducted after the program. This limited the ability to compare objective knowledge gains over time. Nonetheless, the study employed a robust mixed-methods design and triangulated data sources to strengthen the validity of its conclusions.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this study successfully designed, implemented, and evaluated a two-phase Training of Trainers Program in Interprofessional Education for educators working in the education of health professionals from different disciplines. By integrating theoretical learning with practical, scenario-based training, the program effectively enhanced educators’ competencies in areas such as roles and responsibilities, teamwork, interprofessional communication, collaborative leadership, relationships with patients, recognizing patient needs, lifelong learning, and ethics. This research comprehensively revealed the impact of the training program on participants and their opinions about its effectiveness. Participants’ feedback emphasized that the program was comprehensive, detailed, and informative, with competent trainers and effective training methods.

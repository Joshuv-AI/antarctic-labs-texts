Sequential Progression from Pseudo-Vesicular Lesions to Polymorphic Eruption: A Diagnostic Dilemma
Authors: Srishti Tripathi, Kiran P. Malhotra, Parul Verma, Shailendra P. Verma, Gopa Banerjee, Swastika Suvirya
Published: 2024

A myriad of cutaneous manifestations is seen in haematological malignancies that could be specific, arising from leukemic cell infiltration (leukaemia cutis) or non-specific (secondary, non-metastatic). Eosinophilic dermatosis of haematologic malignancy (EDHM) is an uncommon cutaneous entity most commonly seen in chronic lymphocytic leukaemia among lymphoproliferative disorders characterised by multiple non-specific pruritic lesions comprising of papules, plaques, nodules, vesicles, and urticarial wheals.[1]

A 72-year-old male presented with pruritic eruption involving the face, scalp, trunk, and extremities of 18 months duration. Examination revealed erythematous indurated pseudo-vesicles (2-3 mm in diameter) over the face, trunk, and upper and lower extremities [Figure 1a and b], significant axillary, cervical, and inguinal lymphadenopathy, maximum measuring 8 * 8 cm (right axilla), hard in consistency, and gross splenomegaly. Mucosal sites were spared. There were no associated fever/arthalgia and personal and/or family history of atopy. Insect bite/new drug intake history was negative.

(a, b) Multiple pseudo-vesicles present discretely and in groups on the face and extremities. (c, d) Multiple tense bullae, erythematous, crusted plaques, and erosions present on the trunk and extremities

Medical records revealed being diagnosed as B-cell chronic lymphocytic leukaemia (B-CLL) three and a half years ago, stage Rai I in 2018 that progressed to Rai III in 2021, never received chemotherapy as disease was stable, and managed with febuxostat. Clinical findings led to provisional diagnoses of Sweet syndrome, granulomatous dermatitis, and lymphoma cutis.

One week later, when the patient reported for biopsy, clinical morphology had transformed into multiple erythematous oedematous plaques interspersed with tense vesicles (measuring 5–10 mm) on the erythematous base and crusted and erosive lesions involving the trunk, extremities, and face [Figure 1c and d] sparing mucosal sites. Blood reports revealed haemoglobin (Hb) 11.5 gm%, total leucocyte count (TLC) 1 lakh/mm3, platelet count 1.5 lakh/mm3, eosinophils 1%, and absolute eosinophil count (AEC) 300 cells/mcL. Nikolsky’s sign and groove sign were negative, and Tzanck smear was non-contributory.

Histology from plaque showed papillomatosis in the epidermis and moderate peri-vascular lymphocytic infiltrate in the dermis [Figure 2a], while histology from vesicular lesions demonstrated orthokeratosis, spongiosis, and intra-epidermal vesicles containing lymphocytes and eosinophils inside and around the oedematous dermis with intense interstitial and peri-vascular mixed inflammatory infiltrate of lymphocytes with a fair number of eosinophils [Figure 2b-d]. Direct immunofluorescence (DIF) from the peri-lesional skin was negative. Immunohistochemistry was positive for CD3, 5, 23, 25, Pax-5, and 79A features, suggestive of reactive lymphocytic proliferation.

(a) Section from plaque over back: epidermis showing papillomatosis and moderate perivascular lymphocytic infiltrate in the dermis (arrows), Haematoxylin and Eosin, x100. (b) Section from vesicle: Epidermis shows orthokeratosis, spongiosis, and an intra-epidermal vesicle (arrow). Dermis is oedematous and shows intense interstitial and peri-vascular mixed inflammatory infiltrate of lymphocytes with a fair number of eosinophil (asterisk). Haematoxylin and Eosin, x50. (c) Section from vesicle: Epidermis shows an intra-epidermal vesicle (arrow shows basal epidermal layers under the vesicle). Lymphocytes and eosinophils are noted within the vesicle and surrounding it. Haematoxylin and Eosin, x200. (d) Dermal infiltrate on high power shows lymphocytes with variable sizes and a round or cleaved morphology (CLL lymphocytes will be monomorphic, round, and hyperchromatic)

Clinico-histological findings confirmed EDHM. Treatment with tapering oral steroid, topical clobetasol, and anti-histamines led to partial resolution of lesions at 6 weeks follow-up.

Weed[2] first described such lesions when they were considered exaggerated reaction to insect bite in CLL patients. Subsequently described under various names, it finally got recognised as ‘Eosinophilic dermatosis of hematologic malignancy’ by Farber et al.[1] Cohen suggested an acronym `He Remained’ that stands for `Hematologic Related Malignancy- induced Eosinophilic dermatoses’.[3] Most commonly associated with CLL, it is also seen in acute lymphoblastic leukaemia, mantle cell lymphoma, large cell lymphoma, multiple myeloma, acute monocytic leukaemia, myelofibrosis, and non-malignancies like human immunodeficiency virus infection and congenital agammaglobulinemia.[1] Differential diagnoses include arthropod assault, bullous pemphigoid, dermatitis herpetiformis, drug eruption, Sweet syndrome (SS), Wells syndrome (eosinophilic cellulitis), eosinophilic folliculitis, neutrophilic eccrine hidradenitis, scabies, papular urticaria, and lymphoma cutis.[4] Cytokine imbalance driven by leukaemic B-cells resulting in predominant Th2 response cause increased interleukin 4 and 5 responsible for eosinophilic eruption.[5] Oral and topical corticosteroids, anti-histamines, dapsone, phototherapy, immunoglobulins, and chemotherapy are predominant treatment modalities,[4] and response to them is mostly sub-optimal.

This case highlights pseudo-vesicles occurring as a sole clinical manifestation at a time with subsequent evolution into polymorphic lesions of EDHM. Such occurrence of pseudo-vesicles and rapidly evolving different lesions should alert the clinician towards the possibility of EDHM apart from common differential of SS in an appropriate disease background, which can be a rare and atypical morphological presentation of EDHM, as was the scenario in our patient.

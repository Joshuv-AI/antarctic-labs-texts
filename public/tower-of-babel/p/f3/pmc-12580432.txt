m6A-mediated silencing of RNF41 by METTL3/YTHDC1 disrupts autophagy to drive intervertebral disc degeneration
Authors: Bo Yao, Li Wan, Jia Deng, Zhengyu Chen, Lei Zhao, Weiguo Wang, Ziwei Han
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIntervertebral disc (IVD) degeneration (IDD) represents a predominant origin of low back pain and disability, yet current therapeutic interventions remain suboptimal. Emerging evidence highlights autophagy activation as a therapeutic strategy against IDD. This study investigates the mechanistic interplay between N6-methyladenosine (m6A) modifications and autophagy dysregulation in IDD pathogenesis.MethodsBioinformatics analysis identified ring finger protein 41 (RNF41) as a key autophagy-IDD intersection gene. Functional validation utilized tert-butyl hydroperoxide (TBHP)-treated human nucleus pulposus (NP) cells to assess RNF41’s effects on senescence (CDKN2A), autophagy (LC3-II/p62), apoptosis (TUNEL), inflammation (IL-18/IL-1β), and extracellular matrix (ECM) homeostasis (aggrecan/MMP). Key m6A regulators modulating autophagy were screened via correlation analysis. In vivo validation employed adeno-associated virus (AAV)-mediated methyltransferase-like 3 (METTL3)/RNF41 delivery in puncture-induced IDD rat models.ResultsRNF41 expression was downregulated in human IVD tissues. Overexpression of RNF41 mitigated TBHP-induced senescence, apoptosis, activated AMPK/mTOR-mediated autophagy, suppressed inflammation, and restored ECM balance. The autophagy inhibitor chloroquine (CQ) abolished the protective effects of RNF41 overexpression on degenerative NP cells. Mechanistically, METTL3/YTHDC1 co-regulation in degenerative NP cells mediated m6A hypermethylation of RNF41 mRNA, shortening its half-life via YTHDC1-dependent decay. Intradiscal METTL3-silencing AAV attenuated puncture-induced disc loss and histopathological degeneration, whereas RNF41-silencing AVV exacerbated ECM disruption and annular disorganization.ConclusionMETTL3/YTHDC1-mediated m6A modification drives IDD progression by silencing RNF41, thereby impairing autophagy and ECM integrity. Targeting this axis offers a clinically actionable strategy to delay disc degeneration, particularly in patients with early-stage IDD. This evidence establishes RNF41’s role as a theragnostic biomarker and therapeutic targe, enabling precision-guided interventional approaches.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s10565-025-10093-9.

Introduction

Globally, low back pain (LBP) represents a primary contributor to disability, with attributing approximately 40% of LBP cases to intervertebral disc (IVD) degeneration (IDD) (Chou 2021; Kamali et al. 2021). While current therapeutic approaches for IDD—spanning conservative management and surgical interventions—demonstrate palliative efficacy, their symptomatic focus fails to halt disease progression (Samanta et al. 2023). Gene therapy offers a promising alternative by targeting molecular pathways such as catabolic/anabolic imbalance, inflammatory cascades, and cellular senescence pathways (Roh et al. 2021). Thus, elucidating IDD’s multifactorial pathogenesis is critical to develop mechanism-driven treatments that improve patient outcomes.

The progressive depletion of nucleus pulposus (NP) cells within the intervertebral disc core constitutes a hallmark pathological feature of IDD. NP tissue, characterized by high water content and extracellular matrix (ECM) richness, undergoes functional impairment when ECM metabolic homeostasis is disrupted, leading to NP cell phenotypic destabilization and functional decline (Desmoulin et al. 2020). Autophagy—a conserved eukaryotic mechanism for degrading damaged cellular components and recycling macromolecules—is indispensable for preserving NP cell viability and intracellular equilibrium (Liu et al. 2023; Wang et al. 2024a). Emerging evidence highlights differential expression of autophagy-related genes during IDD progression, with altered autophagic flux directly influencing key pathological processes including inflammatory activation, oxidative stress amplification, NP cell senescence, and ECM catabolic/anabolic imbalance (Wang et al. 2023a; Kritschil et al. 2024; Song et al. 2024). While preclinical studies demonstrate therapeutic potential in pharmacologically activating autophagy to mitigate IDD severity (Hao et al. 2022; Yu et al. 2023), the mechanistic interplay between autophagy modulation and disease-specific molecular pathways remains incompletely characterized.

Epigenetic modifications represent precision tools for modulating autophagy-related gene activity, implicating their roles in disease pathogenesis and pharmacological response modulation (Shu et al. 2023). Among posttranscriptional regulatory mechanisms, N6-methyladenosine (m6A)—eukaryotes’ predominant RNA modification—has emerged as a critical determinant of RNA metabolism during IDD (Zhu et al. 2021). This dynamic regulatory system operates through methyltransferase complexes (“writers,” including METTL3), demethylases (“erasers,” such as FTO/ALKBH5), and m6A-recognition proteins (“readers,” exemplified by YTHDF families), collectively governing mRNA splicing, translational efficiency, stability, and degradation (He and He 2021; Jiang et al. 2021). The m6A-autophagy axis has been extensively implicated in playing a critical regulatory role across human disease pathogenesis (Jiang et al. 2021; Chen et al. 2021). Emerging evidence indicates that micro-environmental stressors in degenerating discs induce m6A remodeling of autophagy-related transcripts, which may preserve NP cell viability by enhancing stress-adaptive autophagy (Chen et al. 2021; Tao et al. 2024). However, the pathophysiological significance of m6A-mediated autophagy regulation in IDD remains underexplored.

To investigate autophagy regulation in IDD, we combined bioinformatics and correlation analyses to identify key autophagy-related genes and their interactions with m6A regulators (writers/erasers/readers). This approach revealed that specific m6A modifications critically control autophagy gene activity in NP cells by regulating RNA stability and function. Our findings suggest targeting m6A-autophagy interactions could offer promising therapeutic strategies for IDD.

Methods

Bioinformatics analysis

Our bioinformatic investigation employed the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) repository (GSE67567) to systematically characterize autophagy-associated molecular signatures in IDD. Following quantile normalization of expression profiles from five matched healthy and degenerative disc specimens, we established statistically rigorous thresholds for differential gene expression (|log2FC|> 1; adjusted p < 0.05) using Benjamini–Hochberg correction. The autophagy-relevant differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were subsequently isolated through intersection analysis with the Human Autophagy Database. Multidimensional visualization techniques were implemented, including volcano plots to depict genome-wide expression variance and hierarchical clustering heatmaps to resolve autophagy-specific transcriptional patterns. To functionally characterize the biological relevance of these DEGs, we executed Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment mapping using clusterProfiler, identifying statistically overrepresented biological processes and signaling cascades in IDD pathogenesis.

Patient information

We conducted a case–control investigation using surgically harvested NP specimens. The IDD cohort comprised intraoperatively collected degenerative IVD tissues from twelve patients with confirmed lumbar disc degeneration undergoing discectomy. The control samples were obtained from twelve patients undergoing spinal procedures for non-degenerative conditions. All participants met stringent inclusion criteria: absence of systemic inflammatory diseases, spinal Tumors, or prior disc-related surgeries. Both the IDD and control groups consisted of 7 males and 5 females, with mean ages of 57.75 ± 13.8 and 57 ± 11.4 years, respectively. Baseline information for IDD patients, including age, gender, and degeneration grade, is detailed in Table 1. This rigorous sampling protocol adheres to NIH human subject research guidelines and approved by Medical Ethics Committee of Third Xiangya Hospital Central South University (No.2023S292687). Cell culture conditions

Primary human NP cells (AW-YCH005, Abiowell, Changsha, China) were expanded in chondrocyte-specific growth medium (AW-MC018, Abiowell) containing 1 × chondrocyte differentiation supplement, 5% fetal bovine serum (FBS; 10099141, Gibco, San Diego, CA, USA), and 1% penicillin–streptomycin (AWH0529a, Abiowell) under standard conditions (37℃, 5% CO2, 95% humidity). Third-passage (P3) cells demonstrating > 90% viability were utilized for experiments. To model oxidative stress-induced IDD in vitro, confluent NP monolayers were challenged with 100 μM tert-butyl hydroperoxide (TBHP; 75–91-2, Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) for 24 h (Luo et al. 2021).

In gain-of-function assays, Lipofectamine 2000 (11668019, Invitrogen)-mediated transfection was performed for 48 h using either the lentiviral RNF41 overexpression construct (oe-RNF41; HG-LV005785, HonorGene, Changsha, China) or negative control (oe-NC; HG-LV-NC01, HonorGene) prior to TBHP exposure. To interrogate RNF41-mediated autophagy regulation, parallel cultures were pretreated with 10 μM chloroquine (CQ; MedChemExpress), a lysosomal acidification inhibitor, for 24 h preceding TBHP stimulation (Yu et al. 2023). Combinatorial m6A modulation experiments employed the lentiviral overexpression vector targeting METTL3 (oe-METTL3; HG-LV019852, HonorGene) coupled with the lentiviral shRNA expression vector targeting YTHDC1 (sh-YTHDC1; HG-SH031732, HonorGene), with non-targeting scramble shRNA serving as negative control (sh-NC; HG-LV-NC02, HonorGene). All transfection efficiencies were confirmed prior to mechanistic analyses.

Real-time quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR)

Total RNA extracted from NP cells and tissues with TRIzol Reagent (15596026, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Portsmouth, NH, USA) underwent integrity verification (NanoDrop 2000; 260/280 nm = 1.8–2.1). Subsequent cDNA synthesis used the HiFiScript cDNA Synthesis Kit (CW2569, CWBIO, Taizhou, China), followed by qPCR amplification with UltraSYBR Mixture (CW2601, CWBIO) on a QuantStudio 1 System (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Custom primers (Supplementary Table 1; Tsingke Biotechnology) enabled target quantification via the 2−ΔΔCt method, normalizing to β-actin (ACTB) as the reference gene.

Western blot analysis

NP cellular and tissue proteins were isolated using ice-cold radioimmunoprecipitation assay (RIPA) lysis buffer (AWB0136, Abiowell) with protease/phosphatase inhibitors. After homogenization and centrifugation (12,000 × g, 20 min, 4℃), protein quantification employe a BCA assay (AWB0104, Abiowell). Equal protein aliquots (10 μg/lane) underwent 10% SDS-PAGE separation followed by Nitrocellulose membrane transfer. After blocking with 5% non-fat milk/TBST, membranes received overnight incubation (4℃) with primary antibodies (Supplementary Table 2), then 1-h room-temperature exposure to horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary antibodies (1:5000, Proteintech, Rosemont, IL, USA). Band visualization used enhanced chemiluminescence (AWB0005, Abiowell) on a ChemiScope6100 imager (CLiNX, Shanghai, China), with β-actin-normalized quantification via ImageJ (NIH, Bethesda, MD, USA).

Cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8)

Cell viability was quantified using the CCK-8 assay (Dojindo Laboratories, Tokyo, Japan). NP cells (5 × 104 cells/well, 300 μL) were plated in 24-well plates and adhered for 24 h. Post-treatment, cultures received fresh medium and were incubated for 24–72 h. After medium aspiration, 30 μL CCK-8 reagent was added per well and incubated for 4 h. Absorbance at 450 nm was recorded on an MB-530 microplate reader (HEALES, Shenzhen, China), with viability calculated as: [(experimental well—blank well)/(control well—blank well)] × 100%.

Apoptosis measurement

Apoptotic rates were quantified with the Annexin V-APC/PI detection kit (KGA1030, KeyGen BioTECH, Nanjing, China). NP cells underwent EDTA-free trypsin harvest and resuspension in binding buffer (1 × 106 cells/mL). Suspensions received sequential staining with Annexin V-allophycocyanin (Annexin V-APC) and propidium iodide (PI), then 10-min room-temperature incubation. Flow cytometry (CytoFLEX, Beckman Coulter; model A00-1–1102) analyzed samples within 1 h using 660/20 nm (APC) and 585/42 nm (PI) channels. Early apoptotic (Annexin V +/PI −) and late apoptotic/necrotic (Annexin V +/PI +) subpopulations were differentiated using FlowJo software, with unstained/single-stained controls for gating optimization.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

Interleukin-1β (IL-1β) and IL-18 levels in culture supernatants and tissue homogenates were measured using species-specific ELISA kits (Cusabio Technology LLC, Wuhan, China). Supernatants underwent 5-min centrifugation (5000 × g) for debris removal. Tissue homogenates were prepared in ice-cold PBS (1:10 w/v) with identical centrifugation. Commercial kits included IL-1β (human: CSB-E08053h; rat: CSB-E08055r) and IL-18 (human: CSB-E07450h; rat: CSB-E04610r). Duplicate 100-μL aliquots of standards/samples were incubated with HRP-conjugated antibodies (37℃, 1 h), then developed with 3,3’,5,5’-tetramethylbenzidine substrate. After Stop Solution addition, absorbance at 450 nm was recorded on an MB-530 microplate reader. Cytokine concentrations were interpolated from four-parameter logistic standard curves and normalized to BCA-quantified total protein.

Total m6A determination

Total m6A levels were quantified via colorimetry using an Epigentek kit (P-9005–96, Farmingdale, NY, USA). Total RNA underwent TRIzol isolation (Thermo Fisher Scientific) and DNase I treatment for gDNA removal. RNA purity and concentration were verified spectrophotometrically (A260/A280 ratio: 1.8–2.1) using NanoDrop 2000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific). For m6A quantification, 200 ng of purified RNA per sample was immobilized on strip wells via RNA binding solution, followed by sequential incubation with m6A-specific capture antibodies and detection antibodies under optimized binding conditions (37℃, 90 min). After color development with an HRP-conjugated substrate, reactions were terminated and A450 measured. Total m6A levels were normalized to input RNA quantities and calculated against a methylated RNA standard curve (1 ng) prepared in parallel.

MeRIP-qPCR and RIP-qPCR analysis

Methylated RNA immunoprecipitation (MeRIP)-qPCR and RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP)-qPCR were conducted with the Imprint® RNA Immunoprecipitation Kit (RIP-12RXN, Sigma-Aldrich) to evaluate m6A-modified RNA enrichment. NP cells underwent lysis in RIP buffer containing RNase/protease inhibitors (1:100 v/v) to preserve RNA integrity. Magnetic Protein A/G beads (Dynabeads, Thermo Fisher Scientific) were pre-incubated for 2 h at 25℃ with 5 μg antibodies: anti-m6A (68,055–1-Ig), anti-YTHDC1 (14,392–1-AP), or species-matched IgG control (all Proteintech). Cell lysates containing 500 μg of total RNA were incubated with antibody-conjugated beads overnight at 4℃ with rotation. Post-IP, bead-RNA complexes received Proteinase K treatment to digest residual proteins, followed by RNA extraction using TRIzol reagent. The levels of RNF41 m6A were quantified by RT-qPCR.

mRNA stability measurement

mRNA stability was assessed through transcriptional arrest experiments using actinomycin D. NP) cells were cultured to 80% confluence and treated with 0.2 mM actinomycin D (A9415, Sigma-Aldrich) dissolved in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) for 30 min at 37℃ under 5% CO2 to inhibit de novo RNA synthesis (Yuan et al. 2021). Cells were collected at 0–8 h post-treatment for TRIzol-based RNA isolation. RNF41 mRNA quantification employed RT-qPCR.

Animal models

Animal procedures followed ARRIVE 2.0 guidelines and were approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Xiangya Third Hospital of Central South University (No.2020S292). Male Sprague–Dawley rats (3 months old; 350–400 g body weight) from Hunan SJA (China) underwent 7-day acclimatization (22 ± 1℃, 12-h light/dark, food/water ad libitum). IDD was induced per validated percutaneous disc puncture (Du et al. 2022; Wang et al. 2021). Rats received pentobarbital anesthesia (50 mg/kg, i.p.) in prone position, with Co5/6 IVD located via coccygeal palpation. Aseptic 21G needle insertion into NP was followed by 360° rotation (30 s) and 60-s retention to cause annular injury. Sham-operated controls underwent skin incision without disc puncture. Rats were randomly allocated to six groups: Sham (no puncture), IDD (puncture + 4 μL saline intradiscal injection), NC (puncture + 4 μL empty adeno-associated viruses vector [NC AAV]), sh-METTL3 (puncture + 2 μL NC AAV + 2 μL METTL3-silencing AAV [sh-METTL3; HG-AAVSH024794]), sh-RNF41 (puncture + 2 μL NC AAV + 2 μL RNF41-silencing AAV [sh-RNF41; HG-AAVSH012195, all HonorGene]), and sh-METTL3 + sh-RNF41 (puncture + 2 μL sh-METTL3 + 2 μL sh-RNF41). All intradiscal injections (26G needle; 0.5 μL/min infusion rate) were administered weekly for 4 weeks. After 28 days, rats were sacrificed by pentobarbital overdose (150 mg/kg, i.p.). Co5/6 discs underwent excision, 4% paraformaldehyde fixation (24 h, 4℃), and histological processing.

Histopathological analysis

Histopathological analysis was performed using standardized staining protocols. IVD tissues underwent 24-h fixation in 4% paraformaldehyde (158,127, Sigma-Aldrich; Cat. No.) at 4℃, then 4-week decalcification in 10% EDTA (pH 7.4) with daily solution refreshment. 4-μm paraffin sections were dewaxed in xylene (1330–20-7, Hushi Laboratory Equipment Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) and rehydrated via graded ethanol. Sections underwent hematoxylin (AWI0020, Abiowell) staining and eosin (Abiowell) counterstaining. For proteoglycan visualization, parallel sections were stained with 0.1% fast green (AWI0240, Abiowell), rinsed in 1% acetic acid, and incubated with 0.1% safranin-O (Abiowell). Sections were dehydrated through increasing ethanol, cleared in xylene, and mounted using neutral resin (06522, Sigma-Aldrich). Digital images were acquired with a Motic BA210T brightfield microscope (Xiamen, China). Histological scores of IDD were assessed using a grading scale as previously described (Zhao et al. 2023). The extent of IDD was blindly evaluated by two independent observers. A score of 5 points represents a normal disc, 6–11 points indicate moderate degeneration, and 12–14 points denote severe degeneration. In puncture-induced rat IDD models, sh-RNF41 exacerbated disc structural collapse and NP tissue loss, elevating histological scores.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

Following deparaffinization, IVD tissue sections received enzymatic antigen retrieval and 1% periodic acid for endogenous peroxidase blockade. Overnight incubation at 4℃ employed anti-CDKN2A primary antibody (1:400; ab54210, Abcam). Rabbit IgG HRP-polymer conjugate (1:200; AWS0002, Abiowell) was applied for 1 h. Post-TBST washes, sections underwent DAB chromogen incubation and hematoxylin counterstaining. Microscopic imaging preceded CDKN2A-positive signal quantification via Image-Pro-Plus.

TdT-mediated dUTP nick end-labeling (TUNEL)

Apoptotic cells in IVD paraffin sections were detected via TUNEL assay (40306ES50, YEASEN, Shanghai, China). Sections underwent proteinase K permeabilization (100 μL PBS, 20 min, 37 °C) and endogenous peroxidase quenching (3% H₂O₂, 10 min). After equilibration, TdT reaction mix incubation proceeded (37 °C, 60 min). Nuclear counterstaining used DAPI (4’,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole). BA210E microscope (Motic) captured images for TUNEL-positive cell quantification in non-overlapping fields per section using ImageJ (NIH).

Luciferase reporter assay

Wild-type (WT) and m6A motif-disrupted RNF41 3’UTR sequences (HonorGene) were cloned into pSicheck-2. Either WT or mutant plasmid was transfected into NP cells plated in 24-well formats. The firefly/Renilla luciferase ratios were reported via Dual-Luciferase assays (Promega, E1910).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism 9.0 software (GraphPad Software, La Jolla, CA, USA). Normality was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test, normally distributed data are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Comparisons between two groups were performed using Student’s t-test, while differences among three or more groups were analyzed by one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s post hoc test. Statistical significance was defined as p-value < 0.05.

The top 10 downregulated genes in clinical IVD samples were prioritized for therapeutic potential, with RNF41 showing the most pronounced suppression (Fig. 1E). METTL3-mediated RNF41 downregulation in autophagy suppression, apoptosis, and ECM degradation

METTL3 overexpression in NP cells suppressed autophagic activity, characterized by reduced LC3-II/I ratios and elevated p62 expression, whereas RNF41 overexpression produced the opposite effect. Co-overexpression of METTL3 reversed the autophagy-promoting effects of RNF41 (Fig. 5A). METTL3 overexpression inhibited AMPK phosphorylation while enhancing mTOR phosphorylation. In contrast, RNF41 overexpression activated AMPK signaling and suppressed mTOR activity. These opposing effects were neutralized when METTL3 was co-expressed with RNF41 (Fig. 5B). Functionally, METTL3 overexpression increased cytotoxicity, upregulated CDKN2A, and elevated apoptosis, while RNF41 overexpression enhanced cell survival, downregulated CDKN2A, and reduced apoptotic rates. The protective effects of RNF41 were abolished upon concurrent METTL3 overexpression (Fig. 5C-E). METTL3 also promoted IL-1β/IL-18 secretion and ECM degradation (reduced aggrecan/collagen II; elevated MMP3/MMP9), whereas RNF41 overexpression mitigated these changes. Co-expression of METTL3 restored the pro-inflammatory and catabolic phenotype (Fig. 5F-G). METTL3/RNF41 axis in puncture-triggered IDD pathogenesis

To evaluate the functional interplay between METTL3 and RNF41 in vivo, we administered sh-METTL3 AVV and sh-RNF41 AVV in a rat puncture model. In puncture-induced rat IDD models, sh-RNF41 exacerbated disc structural collapse and NP tissue loss, elevating histological scores. Conversely, sh-METTL3 treatment alleviated degenerative changes and reduced histological scores (Fig. 6A-D). Administration of sh-RNF41 significantly reduced RNF41 expression in degenerative NP tissues (Fig. 6E-F), increased CDKN2A levels (Fig. 6G-H), elevated apoptosis (TUNEL-positive cells) (Fig. 6I), diminished autophagic flux (decreased LC3-II/I ratios and elevated p62) (Fig. 6J), and induced AMPK/mTOR pathway dysregulation (Fig. 6K). It also increased IL-1β and IL-18 levels (Fig. 6L) and compromised ECM integrity (aggrecan/collagen II upregulation; MMP3/MMP9 downregulation) (Fig. 6M). Importantly, sh-METTL3 attenuated senescence, autophagic suppression, ECM degradation, and inflammatory consequences induced by sh-RNF41 (Fig. 6E-M). Discussion

IDD is a multifactorial disorder driven by complex interactions among genetic predisposition, epigenetic dysregulation, and environmental stressors (Chen et al. 2024). Emerging evidence highlights the critical role of epigenetic mechanisms, particularly RNA modifications, in modulating pathological cascades during IDD progression (Kang et al. 2023). Among these mechanisms, m6A exerts regulatory effects through dynamic interplay between methyltransferases (such as METTL3), demethylases (such as ALKBH5), and binding proteins (such as YTHDC1) (Feng et al. 2024). Our study identifies METTL3/YTHDC1-mediated m6A modification of RNF41 mRNA as a novel epigenetic driver of IDD pathogenesis, demonstrating that this axis suppresses RNF41 expression, impairs autophagy activation, exacerbates apoptosis and ECM degradation, and ultimately accelerates disc degeneration (Fig. 7).Fig. 7A graphical abstract illustrating that METTL3/YTHDC1 regulates RNF41 mRNA stability in an m6A-dependent manner, thereby influencing autophagy in NP cells and ECM degradation associated with IDD

A graphical abstract illustrating that METTL3/YTHDC1 regulates RNF41 mRNA stability in an m6A-dependent manner, thereby influencing autophagy in NP cells and ECM degradation associated with IDD

Given autophagy’s dual role as a cytoprotective mechanism and a contributor to degenerative pathologies, its dysregulation in IDD is increasingly recognized (Wang et al. 2023b). While elevated basal autophagy levels in degenerating NP cells may transiently sustain metabolic homeostasis under stress, persistent or excessive autophagic activity paradoxically exacerbates NP cell death (Bahar et al. 2022). Pharmacological augmentation of autophagic flux during early-stage degeneration in vitro has demonstrated therapeutic promise—ameliorating disc cell apoptosis, suppressing ECM degradation (Lu et al. 2023; Madhu et al. 2023), and counteracting oxidative stress-induced mitochondrial dysfunction (Bahar et al. 2024). Our findings corroborate this paradigm, revealing that RNF41-mediated autophagy activation exerts protective effects against IDD progression. As an E3 ubiquitin ligase, RNF41 governs diverse cellular processes including autophagy, inflammation, and protein homeostasis (Yang et al. 2021). Notably, RNF41 deficiency disrupts autophagic flux during spermiogenesis, characterized by diminished LC3-II/OPTN levels and p62 accumulation (Luo et al. 2023)—a pattern mirroring the autophagy dysfunction observed in NP cells. In vitro rescue experiments demonstrated that RNF41 overexpression reverses TBHP-induced autophagic impairment (evidenced by LC3-II elevation and p62 reduction). RNF41 overexpression attenuated the TBHP-driven triad of NP cell apoptosis, inflammatory cytokine (IL-1β, IL-18) release, and ECM degradation (aggrecan/collagen II loss; MMP3/MMP9 upregulation). However, co-treatment with the autophagy inhibitor CQ abolished these restorative effects. These results establish RNF41 as a novel autophagy enhancer with multimodal anti-degenerative effects, offering a promising therapeutic strategy for IDD.

The AMPK/mTOR axis—a central metabolic sensor coordinating autophagy initiation and lysosomal biogenesis (Xiang et al. 2020)—emerged as a key downstream mediator of RNF41’s effects. Reduced mTOR phosphorylation enhances the LC3-II/I ratio and suppresses p62 expression, thereby promoting autophagy and inhibiting apoptosis in NP cells (Wang et al. 2024b). AMPK activation inhibits mTOR activity to relieve autophagy suppression (Deng et al. 2024; Luo et al. 2024), a process dysregulated in IDD-associated metabolic dysfunction. Experimental evidence demonstrates that apigenin modulates AMPK/mTOR signaling to counteract TBHP-impaired autophagic flux in NP cells (Xie et al. 2021). This establishes AMPK/mTOR pathway dysregulation as a key pathological mechanism underlying IDD. Our mechanistic studies revealed that RNF41 overexpression counteracts TBHP-induced AMPK dephosphorylation and mTOR hyperactivation, thereby restoring autophagic flux. This evidence establishes the AMPK/mTOR signaling axis as a critical mediator of RNF41-induced autophagy activation during IDD.

The interplay between m6A epitranscriptomics and disc degeneration has recently gained attention as a potential therapeutic frontier (Li et al. 2022). Consistent with prior reports (Zhu et al. 2021), we observed METTL3 and YTHDC1 upregulation in degenerative discs. Functional studies demonstrated that METTL3 overexpression exacerbates TBHP-induced NP cell injury by suppressing autophagy, amplifying apoptosis/inflammation, and accelerating ECM degradation. Conversely, METTL3 silencing attenuated puncture-induced structural disc damage in vivo, reinforcing its pathogenic role in IDD. m6A methylation affects NP cell degeneration and senescence by regulating the expression of related genes (Tu et al. 2023; Ni et al. 2024). Notably, METTL3-mediated hypermethylation of autophagy-associated 4a (ATG4a) has been shown to destabilize its mRNA, suppress autophagosome maturation, and exacerbate NP cell senescence (Wu et al. 2024), underscoring the importance of the m6A-autophagy axis in IDD. Our work advances understanding of the m6A-autophagy axis in IDD pathogenesis. We identified RNF41 as a novel downstream effector of METTL3-driven m6A dysregulation. METTL3 overexpression reduced RNF41 mRNA stability via m6A modification, while METTL3 knockdown restored RNF41 protein and transcript levels in IDD model rats. Mechanistic studies demonstrated that YTHDC1 directly binds RNF41 mRNA, with YTHDC1 depletion enhancing RNF41 transcript stability. This establishes a coordinated METTL3-YTHDC1 regulatory axis that post-transcriptionally suppresses RNF41 expression. Functional rescue assays demonstrated that METTL3 overexpression abrogated RNF41-mediated protection of NP cell homeostasis, including the ability to sustain autophagic flux, inhibit apoptosis, attenuate pro-inflammatory response, and preserve collagen II/aggrecan biosynthesis essential for ECM integrity. In vivo studies demonstrated that METTL3 silencing rescued the degenerative alterations induced by RNF41 knockdown. Collectively, our data establishes a METTL3/YTHDC1-RNF41 axis as a central mechanism linking m6A dysregulation to autophagy dysfunction in IDD.

This study has several limitations. Functional assessments including forelimb grip strength testing (Novais et al. 2021), mechanical testing (Burt et al. 2024), magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) analysis, and disc height index (DHI) calculations (Cheng et al. 2025) would strengthen the study’s validity. Future research should incorporate female rats to evaluate the influence of sex and sex-related factors on disc degeneration progression, thereby enhancing clinical relevance. Employing senescence models (Novais et al. 2021) to investigate long-term drug effects facilitates clinical translation. Whether METTL3 regulates autophagy through modulating RNF41 m6A modification necessitates further identification of putative m6A sites within the RNF41 CDS and functional validation using corresponding synonymous mutants. The direct physical and functional interplay between METTL3 and YTHDC1 on RNF41 require more conclusive evidence from high-throughput sequencing and rescue experiments. To comprehensively elucidate the global role of m6A modification in IDD, future studies should employ genome-wide m6A analyses (e.g., RIP-seq or m6A-RIP-seq) using human degenerated disc samples. Mechanistic understanding could be further advanced by incorporating the mRFP-GFP-LC3 tandem fluorescent-tagged reporter system or lysosomal activity assays.

Conclusion

This study reveals RNF41 as a critical regulator of NP cell autophagy, with its downregulation in clinical IDD specimens. Mechanistically, RNF41 safeguards NP cell homeostasis by sustaining autophagic flux. Crucially, we identified METTL3 and YTHDC1 as central mediators of an m6A-dependent pathogenic axis. METTL3-catalyzed hypermethylation of RNF41 mRNA enables YTHDC1 recognition and subsequent transcript destabilization, thereby depleting RNF41 protein levels and accelerating autophagy homeostasis collapse in IDD progression. These findings not only resolve the mechanistic interplay between m6A dysregulation and autophagy in IDD but also unveil therapeutic opportunities. Targeting the METTL3/YTHDC1-RNF41 axis—through small-molecule inhibitors of m6A writers/readers or RNF41 gene therapy—may restore NP cell proteostasis, offering a disease-modifying strategy to halt or reverse IDD in patients with early-stage disc pathology.

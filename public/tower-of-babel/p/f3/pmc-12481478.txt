Synthesis and Structure–Activity Relationships of CRBN-Recruiting ZBTB11 Molecular Glue Degraders
Authors: Jiewei Jiang, Nathan L. Tran, Emma Svendsen, Eric S. Wang, Fleur M. Ferguson
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Rational optimization of molecular glue degraders (MGD) remains a challenging and lengthy process even after identification of a promising scaffold. Unlike proteolysis targeting chimeras (PROTAC), MGDs rely on induced protein–protein interactions as opposed to direct binding in order to target a protein of interest for degradation. Here, we report the synthesis of MGDs targeting the transcription factor ZBTB11 guided by protein complex modeling. Exploration of structure–activity-relationships yielded JWJ-01-306 with improved ZBTB11 degradation activity and potent antiproliferative effects against BRAF inhibitor-resistant melanoma cells. Our findings led to the discovery of a novel MGD that targets a previously undrugged transcription factor with the potential to address acquired resistance to cancer therapy.

Introduction

Targeted protein degradation, an emerging paradigm in drug discovery, involves designing compounds that hijack the cellular proteasome machinery to clear proteins of interest. Besides proteolysis-targeting chimeras (PROTACs), molecular glue degraders (MGDs) are another modality that has gained increasing attention. Instead of having high affinity to the target protein, MGDs alter the protein surface of the E3 ligase to promote the formation of a ternary complex between the target of interest and the E3 ligase. Because of this unique mechanism, MGDs have the potential to target proteins that have previously been considered to be undruggable, in particular those that lack well-defined binding pockets.

MGDs that recruit a variety of E3 ligases have now been reported (Figure ). For example, the sulfonamide indisulam promotes degradation of the splicing factor RMB39 via recruitment to DCAF15, and compound 8 leverages the VHL E3 ligase substrate adapter to induce degradation of cysteine dioxygenase 1. However, the most widely exploited E3 ligase complex for MGD development has been the CRL4:cereblon (CRBN) complex. Immunomodulatory imide (IMiD) compounds such as thalidomide induce CRBN-dependent degradation of the transcription factors Ikaros (IKZF1) and Aiolos (IKZF3), and subsequent profiling identified the CXXCG motif of C2H2 zinc finger (ZF) domains as a common requirement for CRBN-mediated degradation. − These landmark discoveries catalyzed tremendous investment in rationally developing CRBN-recruiting MGDs for other proteins with C2H2 ZFs. For instance, we and others have developed MGDs that induce degradation of the transcription factor Helios to destabilize regulatory T cells, , and compounds have been developed to induce CRBN-dependent degradation of the transcription factor WIZ as a potential therapy for sickle cell anemia. ,

Despite the large number of putative CRBN neo-substrates, only a few have been credentialed as therapeutic targets with selective molecular probes. We recently reported that the C2H2 zinc-finger transcription factor ZBTB11 is a promising therapeutic target for overcoming KRAS inhibitor resistance driven by metabolic reprogramming toward an oxidative phosphorylation (OxPhoS)-dependent state in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinomas (PDACs). We developed the tool compound JWJ-01-306 as a potent ZBTB11 MGD, and we discovered that ZBTB11 degradation disrupted transcription of components of the mitoribosome and the electron transport chain to resensitize OxPhos-dependent PDAC cancer cells to KRAS inhibitors.

Beyond PDAC, OxPhos-mediated acquired drug resistance has been observed in other cancers. For example, activating mutations in the BRAF kinase are present in over half of all newly diagnosed cases of melanoma, and small molecule inhibitors of mutant BRAF are effective in reducing tumor burden. − However, resistance inevitably arises to BRAF inhibitors, often driven by increased mitochondrial biogenesis and upregulation of OxPhos. , Here, we describe the medicinal chemistry optimization efforts to develop JWJ-01-306 and the potential of ZBTB11 degradation to target BRAF inhibitor-resistant melanoma.

Results and Discussion

Identification of the Lead Scaffold

Given that ZBTB11 contains several C2H2 zinc-finger domains with the CXXCG sequence motif commonly associated with CRBN glue targets, − we hypothesized that we could design IMiD analogs that recruit ZBTB11 to CRBN. We deployed CRISPR/Cas9 gene editing to generate MOLT-4 cell lines in which the HiBiT tag was introduced into the C-terminus of the endogenous ZBTB11 locus. This reporter system enabled us to screen an in-house collection of IMiD analogs (∼250). We previously identified ALV-05-184-01 and structurally similar analogs showing more than 30% decrease in ZBTB11 protein levels in a dose-dependent manner. The induced degradation by ALV-05-184-01 was confirmed to be proteasome- and neddylation-dependent (Figure ).

Prediction of the Binding Mode

To further optimize ZBTB11 degradation potency, we established a predicted ternary complex of CRBN:ALV-05-184-01:ZBTB11. Due to the large sizes of full length CRBN and ZBTB11 proteins, we modeled the thalidomide-binding domain of CRBN in complex with ZBTB11ZF10, which we previously identified as the critical degron-containing zinc finger of ZBTB11. The predicted binding poses of ALV-05-184-01 generated by Rosetta protein docking were triaged based on the docking scores and visual inspection to remove any that inappropriately positioned the molecule. The clustered putative binding mode revealed several key interactions (Figure ): (1) the glutarimide moiety occupied the tritryptophan pocket of CRBN, as has been reported for all other CRBN-glues; (2) the maleimide carbonyl groups formed H-bond interactions with Asn351 and Glu377 of CRBN as well as Glu861 of ZBTB11; (3) the middle aniline ring engaged in an edge-to-face pi interaction with His353 of CRBN and made hydrophobic contact with Val859 of ZBTB11; (4) the gem-dimethyl moiety sat next to His353 of CRBN, making hydrophobic contacts with surrounding residues; and (5) the 3-Cl, 4-CF3 substituted phenyl ring engaged the negatively charged Glu377 of CRBN and positively charged Lys866 of ZBTB11. The docking model agreed with our previous mutation studies, where mutation of Lys866 to a homologous Thr866 ablated MGD-mediated ZBTB11 ternary complex formation and degradation.

Structural Optimizations

Based on our docking model, the first modification we tested was to extend the spacer region, seeking to improve the predicted cation-pi-anion stacking interaction of the substituted aryl ring. However, spacer extension from two to three carbons diminished both DC50 and D max (Table ), which we hypothesized was due to unwanted spacer flexibility. These modifications also decreased the degradation potency against the frequent IMiD target IKZF1, whose degradation we characterized in parallel with ZBTB11.

Next, we sought to introduce methyl groups to the aniline ring (Table ). Based on our model, we hypothesized that an additional methyl group could constrain the conformation or introduce more hydrophobic surface complementarity at the CRBN/ZBTB11 interface. We synthesized JWJ-01-219 and 227, which introduced methyl groups at the R1 and R2 positions of the central aryl ring, respectively. However, we found that methylation of this middle aryl ring was not tolerated, which may be due to steric clashes. Potency losses were also observed against IKZF1.

The next site we interrogated was the α position next to the aliphatic amine nitrogen (Table ). Although methyl ethyl substitution (JWJ-01-200) did not significantly increase ZBTB11 degradation potency compared to gem-dimethyl, this substitution pattern improved the maximum ZBTB11 degradation. Further increasing the size of substitutions at this position to gem-diethyl (JWJ-01-208) modestly reduced off-target IKZF1 degradation. However, replacement of gem-dimethyl with a phenyl group (JWJ-01-212) was detrimental to ZBTB11 degradation, likely due to the large size. The sulfonamide analog JWJ-01-218 also resulted in diminished ZBTB11 degradation potency. We speculate that the basicity of the nitrogen might be crucial for the desired degradation.

We next performed a substituent screening on the phenyl ethyl ring (Table ). The comparison among JWJ-01-252, 261, 260, and 259 revealed that an unsubstituted aryl ring was the least favored. The electron-withdrawing trifluoromethyl at the para position displayed the highest degradation against ZBTB11, whereas electron-donating groups, such as methyl or methoxy, reduced degradation potency. Chlorine at the meta position (JWJ-01-267) was not sufficient to induce ZBTB11 degradation unless an additional chlorine at the para site was introduced, as exemplified by JWJ-01-274. Among the 3,4-disubstituted analogs, an electron-donating methoxy group abolished the desired degradation of ZBTB11 (JWJ-01-265). The 3-chloro-4-trifluoromethyl substitution in JWJ-01-266 was a better substitution pattern compared to 4-chloro-3-trifluoromethyl (JWJ-01-200). Migrating the chlorine from the meta to the ortho site (JWJ-01-276) diminished degradation potency. Replacing the 2-Cl with CF3 (JWJ-01-273) did not rescue loss of potency, indicating again that ortho substitutions in general were not favored. The comparison between JWJ-01-276 and -275 as well as that between JWJ-01-273 and -272 highlighted the importance of electron-withdrawing groups at the para position. Of note, IKZF1 degradation was less sensitive to the substitutions on the phenyl ethyl moiety, as all analogs in this subset exhibited similar DC50 and D max.

We next tested whether there was synergistic/additive effect between favored structural modifications on the scaffold. Therefore, JWJ-01-277 and JWJ-01-306 were synthesized and tested. We found that JWJ-01-306 retained ZBTB11 degradation potency and maximum degradation level (Table ). Moreover, JWJ-01-306 was slightly less potent for off-target IKZF1 degradation than JWJ-01-277. We further investigated the necessity of the secondary amine by making the N-methyl analog JWJ-01-522, from which we conclude that the desired ZBTB11 degradation requires the presence of the secondary amine, whereas IKZF1 showed more tolerance to N-methylation.

To confirm that JWJ-01-306 and its analogs effect ZBTB11 degradation via a molecular glue mechanism, as opposed to a PROTAC-like mechanism, we performed a cellular thermal shift assay (CETSA) with JWJ-01-306 in WT and CRBN–/– MOLT-4 cells (Figures S1 and S2) in the presence of neddylation and proteasome inhibitors to prevent ZBTB11 ubiquitination and degradation. We observed that JWJ-01-306 induced thermal stabilization of cellular ZBTB11 in WT MOLT-4 cells, but not in CRBN–/– MOLT4 cells, indicating that JWJ-01-306 has no binding affinity for ZBTB11 in the absence of CRBN. Instead, ZBTB11 exhibits thermal stabilization only upon compound induced ternary complex formation. Consistent with these findings, inspection of the HiBiT degradation dose curves and our previously reported ternary complex assays revealed that JWJ-01-306 and its analogs do not display a hook effect that is characteristic of PROTACs. Taken together, these data demonstrate that, despite bearing a short spacer structure, the compounds reported here function as MGDs rather than PROTACs.

SAR Summary

From the optimization campaign on the maleimide scaffold, we conclude the key structure–activity relationships as follows (Figure ): (1) spacer extension from two to three carbons was not preferred. Replacement of the 2-carbon spacer with either piperidine or triazole or methylation on the spacer were not compatible with ZBTB11 degradation; (2) replacing the gem-dimethyl group with cyclobutyl improved the DC50 and D max against ZBTB11, while either phenyl or sulfonamide surrogates diminished the activity; (3) methylation on either maleimide or the middle aryl ring resulted in loss of degradation potency; and (4) 4-chloro-3-trifluoromethyl substitution on the left aryl ring was more favored, compared to the initial 3-chloro-4-trifluoromethyl pattern. Electron-donating groups, methyl or methoxy, were not preferred on the phenyl ring. Furthermore, most of the structural modifications had limited impact on reducing off-target IKZF1 degradation, except for sulfonamide replacement and methylation on the maleimide motif, both of which also diminished ZBTB11 potency.

SAR summary on the JWJ scaffold.

We previously established a ternary complex model for JWJ-01-306 and compared it to that of the initial hit compound ALV-05-184-1. This modeling shows that both molecules adopt similar conformations at the CRBN:ZBTB11ZF10 interface (Figure ), although we did observe two conformational differences between two models. First, the glutarimide ring of JWJ-01-306 was inserted deeper into the tritryptophan pocket of CRBN than that of ALV-05-184-1. Second, the cyclobutyl moiety of JWJ-01-306 was spatially closer to the loop region than the gem-dimethyl moiety of ALV-05-184-1, which could gain more hydrophobic contact with Pro352 of CRBN.

Our docking models successfully rationalized some SAR trends. For instance, it predicted that the three-carbon spacer analog (JWJ-01-216) was less potent than the two-carbon analog (ALV-05-184-01) due to unwanted flexibility diminishing the predicted interactions with E377 and K866. The model also rationalized that the predicted CRBN:ZBTB11ZF10 interface could not tolerate analogs with bulky and rigid spacers, such as JWJ-01-237 and JWJ-01-254. However, the two models did not further rationalize the substitution pattern on the aryl ring, as both 3-chloro-4-trifluoromethyl- and 4-chloro-3-trifluoromethyl-substituted aryl rings were predicted to engage Glu377 of CRBN and Lys866 of ZBTB11.

Dabrafenib-Resistant Melanoma Cells Are Sensitive to ZBTB11 Degradation

To probe whether ZBTB11 degradation could overcome resistance to BRAF inhibitors in melanoma, we first characterized the degradation profile of the optimized JWJ-01-306 molecular glue in SK-MEL-5 melanoma cells by global proteomics and confirmed that ZBTB11 is potently degraded within 5 h. JWJ-01-306 also had an acceptable selectivity profile, with only three degraded off-targets identified in these cells: WIZ, DNAL1, and ZBTB21 (Figure A). WIZ and ZBTB21 were previously observed as degradation targets in an independent global proteomics study using JWJ-01-306. Both proteins contain C2H2 zinc-finger domains with the CXXCG sequence motif, identifying them as putative neosubstrates directly recruited by JWJ-01-306 to CRBN for ubiquitination and subsequent degradation. In contrast, DNAL1 lacks the C2H2 zinc-finger domain and beta hairpin loop structure necessary for recruitment to CRBN, and its localization to the axoneme physically separates it from the nuclear-contained ZBTB11, making DNAL1 an unlikely target of direct or indirect degradation.

We then generated two independent SK-MEL-5 cell lines that were resistant to the BRAF inhibition via chronic exposure to stepwise increasing concentrations of the BRAFV600E inhibitor dabrafenib. Seahorse bioenergetics analysis confirmed that dabrafenib-resistant SK-MEL-5 (DABR) exhibited elevated levels of OxPhos and that treatment with 10 μM JWJ-01-306 for 24 h diminished OxPhos levels and mitochondrial ATP production in both parental and resistant SK-MEL-5 cell lines (Figure B,C,E). Likewise, we found that JWJ-01-306 reduced proliferation of both parental and dabrafenib-resistant cell lines as a single agent (Figure D,F), indicating that ZBTB11 degradation may be an orthogonal method for overcoming acquired resistance to acquired BRAF inhibitor resistance in melanoma.

Chemistry

The general synthesis of analogs with gem-dimethyl, methyl ethyl, and gem-diethyl substitution is depicted in Scheme . A Grignard reaction between 3′-bromoacetophenone or 3′-bromopropiophenone and methylmagnesium bromide or ethylmagnesium bromide yielded the desired alcohol intermediate, which was sequentially converted to the azide under acidic condition. Lithium aluminum hydride reduced the azide to corresponding amine, which was protected using Boc anhydride. A Buchwald-Hartwig cross coupling replaced the 3′-bromine with amino group. After the removal of Boc group by TFA treatment, the diamino intermediate underwent selective alkylation on the aliphatic amine via a reductive amination with various aldehydes. A condensation between bromomaleic anhydride and 3-aminopiperidine-2,6-dione hydrochloride gave the E3 ligase binder. A substitution between reductive amination and condensation intermediate yielded the target analog.

Conclusion

Here we describe the structure–activity relationships of maleimide-based ZBTB11 molecular glue degraders. From the screening hit ALV-05-184-01 with moderate potency (DC50,5h 140 nM/D max,5h ∼ 40%), we obtained the improved lead compound JWJ-01-306 (DC50,5h 280 nM/D max,5h ∼ 70%). As the off-target degradation profile of JWJ-01-306, such as IKZF1, IKZF2, and SALL4, can differ depending on cell type, it should be used with caution in lymphoid, myeloid, and stem cell contexts. Nonetheless, JWJ-01-306 will be useful as a chemical tool to understand the role of ZBTB11 in certain OxPhos-dependent cancer cells. While our molecules have modest selectivity and physicochemical properties, the structure–activity relationships and target validation data lay the foundation for the development of next-generation ZBTB11 degraders with improved drug-like properties, as has been observed for other MGD targets, where initial probes motivated the development of IKZF2MGDs with improved in vivo properties. − This work serves as another example of exploiting molecular glue degrader approach to develop chemical probes that target previously intractable transcription factors.

Cell Culture

SK-MEL-5 cells were cultured in DMEM (Corning #10-017-CM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; Gibco #A5256701) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (P/S; Corning #30-002-CI) in a 37 °C incubator with 5% CO2. MOLT-4 and Jurkat cells were cultured in RPMI1640 (Corning #10-040-CM) supplemented with 10% FBS and 1% penicillin/streptomycin in a 37 °C incubator with 5% CO2. All cell lines were tested for mycoplasma contamination using the MycoAlert PLUS Mycoplasma Detection Kit (Lonza #LT07-710). IKZF1-HiBiT and GSPT1-HiBiT Jurkat knock-in cell lines were a generous gift from Dr. Nathanael Gray (Stanford University).

Generation of Dabrafenib-Resistant Melanoma Cell Lines

SK-MEL-5 cells were cultured in stepwise increasing concentrations (10 nM to 10 μM) of dabrafenib (MedChemExpress #HY-14660) to afford the SK-MEL-5 DABR cell lines. SK-MEL-5 DABR cell lines were maintained in appropriate media supplemented with 10 μM of dabrafenib.

Molecular Modeling

The protocol was reported in our prior study. Briefly, the CRBN:pomalidomide:IKZF1ZF2 complex was extracted from RCSB PDB (ID 6H0F). ZBTB11ZF10 was extracted from the ZBTB11 structure predicted by AlphaFold2 (AFDB accession AF-O95625-F1). Using PyMOL command line, ZBTB11ZF10 and ALV-05-184-01 were structurally aligned with IKZF1ZF2 and pomalidomide; respectively. IKZF1ZF2 and pomalidomide then were removed, and the initial pose was prepacked using the docking prepack application within the Rosetta modeling suite (release 2021.16.61629). Ligand conformers for ALV-05-184-01 were generated using RDKit (release 2024.03.01) and were converted to params files using the convert_mol_to_params python script provided within Rosetta. The prepacked initial pose and the ligand params files were then used as inputs to the Rosetta relax application for conservative conformational searching, side chain rotamer optimization, and overall energy minimization. 1000 structures were generated and clustered by Rosetta energy-based clustering.

HiBiT Protein Degradation Assay

According to our reported protocol, the HiBiT tag (VSGWRLFKKIS) was introduced into the C-terminus of ZBTB11 in its endogenous gene locus in MOLT-4 cell via CRISPR/Cas9 editing. Cells with endogenously HiBiT-tagged ZBTB11 or IKZF1 were resuspended to 1 × 104 cells in 50 μL of complete growth medium and plated in solid white 384-well tissue culture plates. Adherent cells were incubated for 24 hs to allow for cell adhesion and recovery. Compounds were dispensed in dose response as indicated using a D300e Digital Dispenser. Cells were incubated for the indicated amount of time, and end point HiBiT luminescence was measured with the Nano-Glo HiBiT Lytic Detection System on a ClarioSTAR Plus microplate reader with NanoLuc module.

Cellular Thermal Shift Assay

MOLT-4 WT or CRBN–/– cells transfected with NLuc-ZBTB11 were resuspended to 1 × 105 cells in 50 μL of complete growth medium and plated in a solid white 384-well tissue culture plate. Cells were pretreated with 100 nM MG132 and 1 μM MLN4924 for 1 h prior to treatment with 10 μM JWJ-01-306 for 2 h. Cells were transferred to a 384-well PCR plate with transparent cover and the plate was then subjected to a horizontal gradient of temperatures ranging from 37.0 to 66.2 °C for 3 min using an Eppendorf Mastercycler X50. Samples were transferred to a solid white 384-well tissue culture plate and end point NLuc luminescence was measured with the Nano-Glo Luciferase Assay System supplemented with Triton X-100 (final concentration 1%) on a ClarioSTAR Plus microplate reader with NanoLuc module.

Global Proteomics Analysis

SK-MEL-5 cells were resuspended to 1 × 106 cells in 1 mL of complete growth medium and plated in clear 6-well tissue culture plates. Adherent cells were incubated for 24 h to allow for cell adhesion and recovery. Cells were treated with compounds for 5 h prior to harvest and lysis in M-PER buffer containing cOmplete Mini protease inhibitor by sonication. Equal volumes of cell lysate and SDS protein solubilization buffer (10% SDS, 100 mM triethylammonium bicarbonate (TEAB), pH 7.55) were mixed and reduced by incubation with 5 mM tris­(2-carboxyethyl)-phosphine (TCEP) for 30 min at room temperature (RT). Subsequent alkylation was performed by adding 15 mM chloroacetamide (CAA) and incubating for 30 min at RT. Phosphoric acid was added to a final concentration of 1.2%, followed by six volumes of binding buffer (90% methanol; 100 mM TEAB, pH 7.1). After gentle mixing, the colloidal protein solution was loaded onto a S-Trap micro column (Protifi #C02-micro-10) and centrifuged 1500 g for 2 min. The columns were washed with binding/wash buffer (100 mM TEAB in 90% MeOH) four times and sequencing-grade trypsin (Promega #V5111) in digestion buffer (100 mM TEAB) was added in 1:10 (w/w) ratio. Proteins were digested on-column for 3 h at 47 °C. Peptides were eluted by a stepwise elution method using 50 mM TEAB, pH 8.5, followed by 0.2% formic acid (FA) in H2O, and finally 50% acetonitrile (ACN)/0.2% FA in H2O. Eluted peptides were then vacuum-dried before further processing. All the samples were analyzed on a timsTOF Pro 2 (Bruker) coupled to a EvosepOne LC system (Evosep) using the dia-PASEF method.

Proteomics Data Processing and Analysis

Protein identifications and quantifications were performed using DIA-NN 1.8.1 against the Uniprot Homo sapiens reviewed database (November 2023). “FASTA digest for the library-free search” and “Deep learning-based spectra, RTs, and IMs predication” were enabled. Oxidation of methionine residues (+Ox­(M)), carbamidomethylation of cysteine residues (C carbamidomethylation), and N-terminal acetylation (Ac­(N-term)) were set as modifications. Search results underwent filtering to achieve a 1% FDR at both peptide and protein levels, with a mass tolerance of 10 ppm for the most confident centroid. The Protein.Group column in DIA-NN’s report was used for quantification. In brief, the protein intensities were normalized by sum and transformed to log2. Missing intensity was filtered. Two-sample moderated t test with a two-tailed distribution for binary comparison and one-way ANOVA analysis were conducted for multiple group comparisons using the limma package in R. Bioinformatics analyses including hierarchical clustering, box plots, volcano plots, and gene function annotation were performed using R packages.

Seahorse Assay

Cells were resuspended to 5 × 104 cells in 100 μL of complete growth medium supplemented with 10 μM of dabrafenib for dabrafenib-resistant cells and plated in Seahorse XF24 V7 PS Cell Culture Microplates (Agilent #100777-004). Adherent cells were incubated for 24 h to allow for cell adhesion and recovery. Cells were incubated with compounds for 24 h prior to wash and replacement with Seahorse XF DMEM medium, pH 7.4 (Agilent #103575-100) supplemented with 10 mM Seahorse XF glucose, 1 mM Seahorse XF pyruvate, and 2 mM Seahorse XF glutamine (Agilent #103577-100, #103578-100, #103579-100). Mitochondrial stress tests were performed on a Seahorse XFe24 analyzer (Agilent) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Oxygen consumption rates (OCR) and extracellular acidification rates (ECAR) were measured under basal conditions and in response to sequential treatment with oligomycin (2 μM), BAM15 (2.5 μM), and rotenone/antimycin A (1 μM each). ATP production by glycolysis (glycoATP) and by mitochondrial respiration (mitoATP) were calculated from ECAR and OCR; respectively.

Cell Growth over Time

Cells were resuspended to 5 × 104 cells in 1 mL of complete growth medium and plated in clear 12-well tissue culture plates (Corning #3506). Cells were then treated with DMSO, dabrafenib (0.5 μM), JWJ-01-306 (10 μM), or dabrafenib + JWJ-01-306. Viable cells were passaged and quantified by trypan blue exclusion (Invitrogen T10282) twice weekly using a Countess 3 Automated Cell Counter (Invitrogen #AMQAX2000).

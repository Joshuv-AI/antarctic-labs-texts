Chaos analysis and traveling wave solutions for fractional (3+1)-dimensional Wazwaz Kaur Boussinesq equation with beta derivative
Authors: Shan Zhao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Wazwaz Kaur Boussinesq (WKB) equation can effectively simulate the behavior of water waves in shallow water, including the nonlinear effect and dispersion phenomenon of waves, which is of great significance for understanding the dynamic process of ocean, river and other water bodies. To enrich the wave equation theory, the (3+1)-dimensional integer order derivative of WKB equation is changed to the fractional one with beta derivative. The current work deals with the fractional (3+1)-dimensional WKB equation for discussing its chaotic behavior and establishing some new analytic solutions. The chaotic properties of the equation are verified by the trend of evolution along with time, Lyapunov exponents and initial sensitivity analysis. And then complete discrimination system for polynomial method is applied to derive some trigonometric, hyperbolic, Jacobi elliptic and other solutions. The graphical demonstrations are provided for part of these solutions. From these visualized graphs, the solitary, periodic and quasi-periodic wave are shown and the effect of fractional derivatives on the equation can be seen intuitively.

Introduction

Non-linear fractional partial differential equations (NLFPDEs) refers to the non-linear partial differential equations (NLPDEs) with fractional derivative. Compared with integer order NLPDEs, they can better describe the changes or small jitter of objects in time or space. NLFPDEs have been widely used in many fields such as physics, engineering, biology, economics, such as atomic physics, quantum mechanics, optics, electromagnetism, fluid mechanics, quantum-mechanical systems, etc. Well known fractional derivative definitions are Caputo derivative1–3, Riemann-Liouville derivative4, Grünwald-Letnikov derivative5, Atangana-Baleanu derivative (also called beta-derivative)6,7, Caputo-Fabrizio derivative3, etc. More definitions can be found in the document8, which lists dozens of fractional derivative definitions. The application of these fractional derivatives has greatly promoted the development of nonlinear models in various fields.

At present, there are many methods to characterize NLFPDEs and exploit their numerical or analytical solutions. Dynamic analysis is one of the main contents of the research of NLFPDEs, which plays a very important role in the study of the existence condition, stability and controllability of nonlinear system equations9–11. The solutions of NLFPDEs are of great importance for understanding and describing complex physical phenomena. Finding analytical solutions or high-precision numerical solutions can better understand or simulate physical processes and phenomena in various fields. The traditional Boussinesq equation36,37 has made a major contribution to shallow flow theory and is one of the core equations of turbulence theory. It is a generalization of the Korteweg-de Vries equation, which is often used to describe changes in velocity and density of small amplitude fluctuations in fluid motion. The traditional Boussinesq equation has second and fourth order terms. With the deepening of research, the equation has been developed in many forms to describe more complex wave phenomena. For example, Wazwaz et al. found multiple solitons or complex solitons for a special set of distinct Boussinesq equations38. Subsequently, Kadomtsev-Petviashvili and B-type Kadomtsev-Petviashvili Boussinesq equation39 were studied, which can better simulate the shallow water wave phenomenon of long and short wave interaction in nature. Boussinesq-Burger equations40 describe long wave propagation in shallow water and provide reference for port and coastal design. Wazwaz and Kaur proposed (2+1)- and (3+1)-dimensional Boussinesq equations in reference41. The (3+1)-dimensional WKB equation in math has higher complexity, and provides a new perspective and method for the study of wave equation. Wazwaz and Kaur41 got the complete integrability of the two equations by Painlevé analysis and multiple complex soliton solutions by exponential expansion method. Kumar et al.42 and Silambarasan et al43. studied the solutions of (2+1)-dimensional Boussinesq equation by applying Hirota’s bilinear method and Jacobi elliptic function method, respactively. Abdul Saboor et al.44 studied the (2+1)-dimensional form with three kinds of derivatives by using the modified \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$(\frac{G'}{G^2})$$\end{document}(G′G2)-expansion method. For (3+1)-dimensional Boussinesq equation, Kaur and Wazwaz46exploited some lump and breather solutions via quadratic and homoclinic test functions. Nisar et al.45 gave some Jacobi elliptic solutions by utilizing the method of F expansion. The auxiliary equation method47, the modified \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$(1/G')$$\end{document}(1/G′)-expansion method48 and Kudryashov approach49 are all employed to explore the structure of (3+1)-dimensional Boussinesq equations from different perspectives.

The study of chaotic phenomena is very important for nonlinear systems50. In particular, the Lyapunov exponents and sensitivities of nonlinear chaotic systems describe how small perturbations of initial conditions affect the orbit of the system during time evolution. The ongoing work will analyze the dynamic behavior of Eq. (1.2) when an external periodic perturbation occur.

Solving the Eq. (1.2) is crucial for understanding the behavior of three-dimensional shallow water waves. It can help scientists predict the movement, deformation and interaction of waves with other objects. In oceanography, hydraulic engineering and other fields, this understanding has practical significance for the prediction of waves, tides, and the safety of ships and structures. Complete discrimination system for polynomial method51 is simple and efficient tool to used in solving NLPDEs widely52–55. Lyapunov exponent

The Lyapunov exponent can help us determine the stability of a dynamic system. By analyzing the Lyapunov exponent of a system, we can identify its response characteristics in the face of changes in initial conditions and thus understand the system’s stability and attractiveness. In many nonlinear systems, the Lyapunov exponent is an important tool for identifying chaotic behavior, for example, a positive Lyapunov index indicates that the system is very sensitive to small changes in initial conditions and exhibits chaotic behavior, while a negative Lyapunov exponent means that the system is attractive to disturbances and tends towards a stable state.

Conclusions

In ongoing work the chaotic analyses of the fractional (3+1)-dimensional WKB equation is presented by adding external periodic perturbations, and some rational, trigonometric, hyperbolic and Jacobi elliptic function solutions of the equation are obtained with the aid of complete discrimination system for polynomial method. When the appropriate parameters are taken, the fractional (3+1)-dimensional WKB equation with an external periodic perturbation can appear as chaotic behavior. The Lyapunov exponents given in this article can also cover this point. As can be seen from the sensitivity pictures, the chaotic system is also very sensitive to the initial value. These preliminary chaos analyses are helpful for us to study the description and control of the fractional (3+1)-dimensional WKB equation. These new traveling wave solutions have clear structure, which can provide reference for the study of shallow water theory. From Figs.4, 5, 6, it can be seen that the solution shapes obtained in this paper are relatively rich, including solitary wave, periodic solution and quasi-periodic solution. The change of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\beta$$\end{document}β value has different effects on different solutions. The method studied in this paper is also suitable for other wave equations. Further, considering how to relax the assumptions of the fractional (3+1)-dimensional WKB, so that it can be applied to a wider range of physical scenarios, such as deep water waves, large amplitude waves or compressible fluids, is also a worthy direction of research.

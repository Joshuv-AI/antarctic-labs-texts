Incidence, mortality, and burden of Parkinson’s disease in China: A time-trend analysis and comparison with the global burden based on Global Burden of Disease Study 2021
Authors: Fan Gao, Xiaoyu Cheng, Junyi Liu, Yinlian Han, Chengjie Mao, Chongke Zhong, Chunfeng Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground:Parkinson’s disease (PD) is a leading cause of death and disability worldwide, and is associated with a significant Global Burden of Disease (GBD). We analyzed the trends in PD incidence, mortality, and disability-adjusted life year (DALY) burden in China, and compared them with global data.Methods:Estimates and 95% uncertainty intervals (UIs) for incidence, mortality, DALYs, years lived with disability (YLDs), and years of life lost (YLLs) for PD were extracted from the GBD, Injuries, and Risk Factors Study 2021. We describe the epidemiology of PD at global and Chinese levels, analyze trends in incidence and mortality from 1990 to 2021 by joinpoint regression models, and decompose PD burden according to population size, age structure, and epidemiological changes.Results:GBD 2021 estimated 508,378 (95% UI: 430,499–592,748) incident cases of PD, 92,035 (95% UI: 75,908–108,133) deaths, and 2,159,514 (95% UI: 1,826,196–2,521,344) DALYs in China, with the higher age-standardized rate (ASR) in incidence, mortality and DALYs than the global levels. The DALY burden of PD in China increased slightly from 1990 to 2021, consistent with the global upward trend. Joinpoint regression analysis indicated that the ASR of incidence in China increased faster than the global average, while the ASR of mortality decreased, with the fastest decline in 2004–2014. Decomposition analysis revealed that men and the middle sociodemographic index (SDI) quintile (32.82%) were responsible for the most significant DALYs, whose changes were primarily driven by population growth and aging.Conclusions:The burden of PD showed an overall increasing trend from 1990 to 2021, which was primarily driven by population growth and aging. This study highlights the significant challenges in controlling and managing PD, including the increase in cases and gender differences, which may provide guidance for comprehensive strategies to address the changing profiles of PD in China.

Introduction

Parkinson’s disease (PD) is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder characterized by bradykinesia, resting tremor, rigidity, and postural instability, and is the second most common neurodegenerative condition after Alzheimer’s disease.[1,2] In addition to motor symptoms, PD also has nonmotor symptoms such as sleep disturbance, depression, constipation, and autonomic nervous dysfunction.[3] As the disease progresses, many patients have reached the middle to late stages, which has a significant impact on quality of life and functionality, imposing a substantial burden on families and society.[4] According to the findings of the 2016 Global Burden of Disease (GBD) study, 211,296 individuals died from PD, and the number of prevalent cases reached 6.1 million worldwide in 2016.[5] Between 1990 and 2016, the age-adjusted prevalence of PD experienced the most significant increase globally, more than doubling, with China experiencing one of the largest increases worldwide.[5] PD has become a significant health concern in China, with its prevalence rising between 1990 and 2019, and the burden among older people continued to increase, resulting in a large health and economic burden.[6] The burden of PD is associated with the sociodemographic index (SDI), which is a comprehensive measure that integrates income, education, and fertility conditions to quantify the level of sociodemographic development.[3,6,7] The PD-attributable disability-adjusted life year (DALY) rates increased significantly with increases in SDI in the Eastern Mediterranean Region,[3] but the age-standardized rates (ASRs) of DALYs were negatively associated with SDI in China.[6] The epidemic of noncommunicable diseases, such as chronic kidney disease, has been affected by demographic growth and epidemiological changes,[8] and these factors may also influence the transition of PD epidemiology.

Although several studies have investigated PD burden, they used older data,[6,7] analyzed one metric[9,10] or lacked in-depth insights on the temporal trends or decomposition analysis.[11] Time trend analysis and decomposition method provide a better understanding of the driving factors behind the changes in PD burden, offering a solid foundation for future disease prevention and management strategies. Importantly, the data used in the GBD 2021 study lacked a comprehensive comparison between China and the global level. A comparison of profiles and trends between China and global not only reveals the unique challenges and progress in addressing PD in China but also provides valuable insights for disease management and policy-making worldwide. Therefore, using the latest evidence and analytical framework from the GBD 2021, we produced age-specific and sex-specific estimates of PD burden, time trends in China and worldwide, and decomposition analysis based on demographic and epidemiological factors from 1990 to 2021.

Methods

Data sources

The incidence, mortality, DALYs, years lived with disability (YLDs) and years of life lost (YLLs) of PD were obtained from the GBD 2021, which provides a comprehensive estimation of the burden of disease for 371 diseases and injuries for 204 countries and territories and 811 subnational location.[12] The original data estimated by the GBD and the standard population were downloaded from the Global Health Data Exchange database (http://ghdx.healthdata.org/gbd-results-tool). According to the GBD research team, the diagnosis of PD requires the presence of at least two of the following four symptoms: tremors/trembling, bradykinesia, stiffness in limbs and torso, and coupled with instability in posture. The GBD project employed three distinct levels of PD severity: mild, moderate, and severe. The PD classification code was G20, G21, and G22 according to the International Classification of Disease 10th Revision (ICD-10). Data on non-fatal estimates were collected from various sources including scientific literature, household surveys, epidemiological surveillance, disease registry, clinical informatics data, and other sources.[12] Data sources incorporated into the modeling can be found elsewhere: https://ghdx.healthdata.org/gbd-2021/sources. The mortality rates in PD were modeled by age, sex, and location using the cause of death ensemble model (CODEm), with the included covariates already documented.[12] PD incidence rates were modeled by age, sex, and location using a Bayesian meta-regression tool (DisMod-MR 2.1).[12] DALY is a comprehensive measure of disease burden that is used to calculate the total health-related impact of diseases. It is divided into two sections: YLD, which measures the years of healthy life lost due to illness or disability, and YLL which measures the years of life lost due to premature mortality. The calculation of YLDs involved multiplying the prevalence of specific sequelae based on cause–age–sex–location–year with their corresponding disability weights. The calculation of YLLs involved multiplying deaths specific to cause–age–sex–location–year with the average lifespan at the time of death. The calculation of DALYs involved the aggregate of YLDs and YLLs.[12] The values, the rate per 100,000 individuals, and the ASR per 100,000 individuals were provided for each indicator. Final estimates represent the mean estimate across 500 draws, and 95% uncertainty intervals (UIs) are represented by the 2.5th and 97.5th percentile values across the draws.[12] The SDI is a composite indicator that reflects socio-economic development, incorporating income per capita, mean years of schooling for individuals aged 15 years and older, and the total fertility rate for females under the age of 25 years. The SDI ranges from 0, representing the least developed, to 1, representing the most developed, and it is categorized into five levels based on SDI quintiles: low, low-middle, middle, high-middle, and high.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive analyses were conducted on PD incidence, death, DALYs, YLLs, and YLDs data in both sexes for China and globally from 1990 to 2021. Joinpoint regression analysis was used to determine the magnitude of the temporal trends for all indicators.[13] Specifically, we set the number of joinpoints to range from 0 to 5, dividing the overall trend into several segments based on the joinpoints. We then calculated the annual percent change (APC) for each segment and selected the optimal model accordingly. The average APCs (AAPCs) and the corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated from the regression analysis by using Joinpoint software (Version 4.9.1.0 – April 2022; Statistical Methodology and Applications Branch, Surveillance Research Program, National Cancer Institute, Bethesda, MD, USA) from the Surveillance Research Program of the U.S. National Cancer Institute. The term decrease or increase was used when the slope (APC) of the trends was significantly different from zero. P <0.05 was considered statistically significant. The age groups, spanning from 20 to 94 years, were divided into 5-year intervals. Given that the incidence and mortality rates of PD in individuals under the age of 20 were negligible, and the disease predominantly affects the elderly, we focused on this age range for analysis. Decomposition analyses were conducted to derive an in-depth understanding of explanatory factors driving changes in PD DALYs from 1990 to 2021. Specifically, to explore the impact of population growth, aging, and epidemiological changes on PD epidemiology over the past 30 years, decomposition analysis of DALYs by population, age structure, and population- and age-standardized DALYs rates (defined here as epidemiological changes) was performed by following a pre-established approach.[8] In addition, PD DALYs were decomposed by sex (female and male). All calculations were performed using R software (version 4.3.3, https://www.R-project.org/).

Results

Time trends of PD incidence and mortality in China and globally

Based on estimates from GBD 2021, 1,335,142 new PD cases were diagnosed and 388,194 individuals died from PD worldwide in 2021 [Supplementary Table 1, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C632]. Nearly 38.1% (508,378/1,335,142) of these cases occurred in China, which included 302,249 males and 206,128 females. The age-standardized incidence rate (ASIR) for PD was 24.34 per 100,000 population in China. Men showed a higher ASIR than women (31.34 vs. 18.68 per 100,000 population) [Figure 1A]. About 92,035 people in China died from PD during 2021, accounting for 23.7% (92,035/388,194) of all PD deaths worldwide [Supplementary Table 1, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C632]. The age-standardized mortality rate (ASMR) for PD was 5.03 per 100,000 population in China. Men had approximately 104.5% higher ASMR than women (7.30 vs. 3.57 per 100,000 population) [Figure 1B]. The ASIR of PD in China (24.34 per 100,000 population) and ASMR of PD in China (5.03 per 100,000 population) were both higher than those worldwide (15.63 and 4.81 per 100,000 population) [Figure 1C, D]. The ASIR of PD in China increased from 12.83 (95% UI: 10.92–14.73) in 1990 to 24.34 (95% UI: 20.67–28.30) in 2021, marking an 89.7% increase. In contrast, the global ASIR increased by only 39.1%. Globally, the ASMR of PD increased by 4.1%, while in China, it decreased by 17.7% [Supplementary Table 2, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C632].

The time trends of incidence and mortality in PD were analyzed by joinpoint regression [Table 1 and Figure 2]. Between 1990 and 2021, the ASIR of PD showed a continuously increasing trend, with the growth rate in China significantly surpassing the global level. Specifically, the trend of the ASIR for China was marked by six joinpoints showing pronounced increases before 2010 and relatively slower growth thereafter. However, the global incidence rate showed a continuous and steady slight upward trend. The AAPC of ASIR for Chinese males (2.08% [95% CI: 2.02–2.15%]) was higher than that for females (1.86% [95% CI: 1.79–1.93%]) [Figure 2C]. The ASMR in China exhibited a fluctuating yet overall downward trend, in contrast to the slightly upward trend observed globally. Detailed joinpoint results for ASIR and ASMR are presented in Supplementary Table 3, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C632 and illustrated in Figure 2.

PD burden in China and comparison with the global burden

The GBD 2021 estimated that PD resulted in 7,471,821 DALYs worldwide and 2,159,514 DALYs in China [Supplementary Table 1, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C632]. China had a higher age-standardized DALY rate (ASDR) for PD than the global average. The ASDR in China was 107.96 per 100,000 population, compared to 89.59 per 100,000 population globally. The populations with the highest PD burden in China were similar to those globally. People aged 75–79 years accounted for 19.2% of PD DALYs in China and 20.3% globally [Supplementary Table 5, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C632]. However, the changes in PD burden between China and globally were different. Between 1990 and 2021, the ASDR in China increased by 2.6%, from 105.26 (95% UI 93.23–116.71) per 100,000 population in 1990 to 107.96 (95% UI 91.10–125.52) per 100,000 population in 2021. The rate increased by 8.5% in males but decreased by 7.9% in females. In contrast, the global ASDR increased by 10.0%, with an increase of 10.8% in males and 4.9% in females [Supplementary Table 2, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C632]. In 1990, 86.14% of DALYs in China consisted of YLLs, indicating that the burden in China was primarily due to YLLs prematurely, which was comparable to the global figure (84.2% YLL of DALYs). By 2021, only 66.27% (1,431,035/2,159,514) of DALYs consisted of YLLs in China, and the age-standardized YLL rate decreased by 20.8%, while the age-standardized YLD rate increased by 166.9%, signifying that the burden of PD DALYs in China was mainly driven by changes in YLDs.

Decomposition analysis on PD DALYs

Aging, population growth, and epidemiological change accounted for 44.01%, 46.36%, and 9.63% of the global increase in DALYs, respectively. The most pronounced contributions of aging, population growth, and epidemiological change occurred in the high-middle SDI quintile (61.13%), low SDI quintile (93.60%), and high SDI quintile (16.95%) [Supplementary Table 6, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C632]. When stratified by sex, the effects of demography and epidemiology on DALYs differed across subgroups. The negative effects of epidemiological change on DALYs were only observed in the middle SDI quintile of PD in a female subgroup, and the negative effects of aging on DALYs were only observed in the low SDI quintile of PD in the male subgroup. The middle SDI quintile accounted for the highest proportion of DALYs (32.82%; 1,516,355/4,620,312) among all SDI groups and the males demonstrated higher burden than the females in all subgroups [Figure 3 and Supplementary Table 6, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C632].

Discussion

This study provided the most up-to-date data on the incidence, mortality, and DALYs of PD in China and globally from 1990 to 2021, and further conducted a comprehensive evaluation based on trend and decomposition analyses. In China, the ASR of incidence and mortality for PD were significantly higher than the global levels, while DALYs were slightly above the global level. The DALY burden of PD in China increased slightly from 1990 to 2021, consistent with the global upward trend. The ASIR in China increased faster than the global average, while the ASMR decreased with the fastest decline observed in 2004–2014. Decomposition analysis showed that aging and population growth made a major contribution to the changes in PD burden. The burden of PD in China was primarily concentrated among males and the middle-aged and older populations.

According to the estimates by the GBD 2021, 38.1% of newly diagnosed PD cases in the world have occurred in China. The ASIR of PD in China was higher than the global average, and the proportion of global incident cases in China in 2021 was also high. As a rapidly developing country, China has experienced urban expansion and population growth, leading to long-term population aging, which may result in a higher ASIR for PD.[14,15] As a populous middle SDI country, China has a high number of cases, also possibly due to its environmental pollution,[16,17] changes in lifestyle,[18,19] and improvements in diagnostic accuracy.[20] With the emergence of new PD-specific biomarkers, such as machine-learning-based magnetic resonance imaging, wearable movement-tracking data, and special detection of α-synuclein, the precision in diagnosing PD will significantly improve in the future.[21–23] There are significant regional differences in the incidence rates of PD in China. In 2021, the ASIR was generally higher in the southeastern coastal regions compared to the western regions.[11] In addition, according to joinpoint analysis, the global incidence rate from 1990 to 2021 was relatively stable, while China exhibited a significant upward trend during the same period. The increase in incidence in China might be attributed to changes in population structures, lifestyle, and environmental factors, resulting from industrialization, urbanization, and socioeconomic development[24,25]. In China, the phenomenon of social isolation is becoming increasingly prevalent, which is associated with a higher risk of PD regardless of genetic risk.[26,27] Given the nationwide PD epidemic, China needs to take proactive measures by investing in advanced medical technology and sufficient research funding to enhance basic research and improve clinical practices for PD. Meanwhile, PD deaths in China accounted for 23.7% of global PD deaths. The ASMR for both men and women has an overall downward trend, although the absolute number of deaths from PD in China had an accordant increase. Since the beginning of the 21st century, the ASMR for men has significantly declined, which may be associated with advancements in treatment and personalized management.[28] China’s mortality rate for PD is higher than the global average, but contrary to the global trend, China’s mortality rate decreased while the global mortality rate for PD increased. The increasing trend of PD mortality worldwide may be influenced by factors such as global population aging, uneven distribution of medical resources, and the lack of effective management and treatment methods in some regions. Although the incidence rate in China is high relative to the global average, the mortality rate is lower, indicating that China may have made greater efforts in disease management and prevention, such as health education, early diagnosis, and early intervention, which help to alleviate the severity of the disease and reduce the occurrence of complications. When the overall trend is divided into multiple subsegments, we were surprised to find the ASIR of PD showed the slowest growth trend at the end of the study period (2019–2021). However, compared with the high level in 2004, the decline in the ASMR from 2014 to 2021 appeared small. Thus, the development of effective prevention, management, and treatment options for PD should still be given sufficient attention.

In addition, the DALY burden of PD was significantly higher in China as compared to that globally. In China, the burden of PD, as measured by DALYs, has shown a slight increasing trend. This phenomenon is mainly driven by two factors: due to improvements in medical conditions and treatment efficiency, premature deaths caused by PD have decreased, leading to a reduction in YLL; and, with the progression of the disease and extension of patient survival time, YLDs have increased. These changing trends reveal the dynamic nature of the burden of PD in China. There has been a shift from a previous focus on reducing premature deaths caused by the disease to a current emphasis on effectively managing the long-term disability of patients to improve their quality of life. This shift not only reflects the progress of China’s healthcare system and the increased attention to chronic disease patients but also requires us to pay more attention to early diagnosis, treatment, and long-term rehabilitation care in future PD prevention and control efforts. There has been increasing focus on the recognition and treatment of nonmotor symptoms, which dominate the clinical presentation of advanced PD, contributing to severe disability, reduced quality of life, and shortened life expectancy.[29] The decomposition of the burden of PD DALY showed that the increase in burden was mainly driven by demographic changes such as population growth and aging, which span all levels of development. In general, the effects of population growth and aging were most pronounced in regions with medium SDI, indicating that the driving patterns of demographic structure on PD burden vary across different SDI levels. Among the female subgroup, the epidemiological changes in DALY have mitigated the effects of population growth and aging, but have not fully offset their impact, particularly in the middle SDI quintile.

Consistent with previous studies,[6] we found a trend toward younger age of patients with PD. The age effect on incidence showed sharp increasing trends from 40 to 60 years, and the 45–49 years age group showed the most substantial increases in incidence among all age groups. We consider that the increases in age-specific PD incidence may be partially explained by the improved diagnosis and people’s awareness of their health.[30] The incidence of early-onset PD is also on a rapidly increasing trend, particularly among the 35–39 years age group. This age group is in the prime of life when individuals often face significant work and life pressures. Consequently, they may be more susceptible to the impacts of unhealthy lifestyles and environmental factors, thereby increasing their risk of developing early-onset PD. Clinically, early-onset PD may present with symptoms that differ from those of late-onset PD. For instance, patients with early-onset PD may experience more frequent dystonia and motor complications.[31] Furthermore, due to the earlier onset of the disease, they may have to confront the challenges and difficulties it brings at a younger age. Currently, the incidence of early-onset PD is indeed showing a rapid growth trend, and this intensification is undoubtedly closely related to the continuous increase in people’s awareness of health issues and the sustained advancements in medical detection technology. For individuals aged 35–39 years, it is even more important to closely monitor their own health status, actively adopt a healthy lifestyle, and maintain a positive mindset, to reduce the risk of developing early-onset PD. At the same time, medical institutions and researchers should also increase their efforts in studying and preventing early-onset PD, striving to provide more effective treatments and services for patients.

We found that the incidence and mortality rates were approximately twofold higher in males, with the DALY burden increasing in males and decreasing in females from 1990 to 2021. Furthermore, trend analysis indicated a higher rate of incidence increase in males compared to females, while mortality rates declined more significantly in females. These findings underscore significant sex differences in PD. Males tend to experience a greater burden of PD compared to females, consistent with previous studies that have reported gender disparities in PD incidence.[32,33] Hormonal differences, particularly potential neuroprotective effects of estrogen in females, are recognized as major contributors to PD pathophysiology.[34] Males, characterized by higher occupational exposure and stress levels compared to females, may face an elevated risk of PD development.[35–37] Moreover, female drinkers tend to exhibit a lower PD risk than their male counterparts for equivalent alcohol consumption.[38] Although males exhibit more addictive behaviors such as smoking compared to females, which have been associated with a reduced risk of PD,[39] this relationship is less certain and requires further investigation. Our results indicated that males had higher mortality rates and faster disease progression compared to females. Consistent with our findings, males with PD demonstrated greater brain atrophy and disrupted connectivity, which may contribute to the different prognosis between male and female patients.[40] The higher incidence and mortality risk in males suggests the need for increased attention to the older male population and the development of targeted preventive measures. Therefore, we strongly recommend that males follow healthy dietary principles, actively engage in physical exercise, and strengthen personal protective measures to avoid exposure to harmful substances. Embracing a healthier dietary approach, exemplified by the Alternative Healthy Eating Index, and engaging in regular physical activities, both preceding and subsequent to a PD diagnosis, have been correlated with reduced mortality rates.[41] Increased intake of flavonoids, particularly anthocyanins and flavan-3-ols, sourced from flavonoid-dense foods such as berries and red wine, is potentially tied to a lower mortality risk.[42] Maintaining a high diet quality or adhering to a healthy dietary pattern,[19] in conjunction with high physical activity and an ideal sleep pattern,[43] have been independently linked to a lower future risk of developing PD.

This study had several limitations. First, the raw data from GBD were from different countries, which inevitably leads to uneven quality of data. For example, the heterogeneous nature of PD as a neurodegenerative disorder frequently leads to misdiagnosis among clinicians, necessitating accurate diagnosis by trained specialists.[44] Second, the hysteresis property of GBD, meaning that current estimates are calculated using past trends and covariates, should also be noted. Despite these limitations, there were several strengths. Comprehensive and in-depth investigations, including descriptive, trend, and decomposition analyses, have enhanced our understanding of the epidemiology of PD in China and globally, potentially informing public health planning and resource allocation.

In conclusion, in China, the ASR of incidence and mortality for PD were significantly higher than the global levels, while DALYs were above the global level. From 1990 to 2021, the ASIR of PD in China showed an upward trend, while the ASMR showed a downward trend, which is similar to the global trends in PD disease burden. The burden of PD demonstrated overall increasing trends from 1990 to 2021, especially for males and middle-aged and older people. The changes in PD burden were primarily driven by population growth and aging, which may further contribute to the dramatically predicted increasing number of cases of PD in the next few decades. These results collectively highlight the great challenges in the control and management of PD, which may be instructive for better making public health policy and allocating medical sources.

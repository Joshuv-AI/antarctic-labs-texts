Background

Malignant pleural mesothelioma (MPM) is a rare but aggressive neoplasm arising from the mesothelial lining of the pleura, associated with asbestos exposure [1, 2]. According to Global Cancer Observatory, MPM ranked 26-th in terms of mortality with a total of 25 371 deaths worldwide in 2022 [3]. There is a scarcity of epidemiological data regarding the rate of malignant pleural mesothelioma (MPM) in Kazakhstan. In 2008 Kashanskii et al. conducted a review of mesothelioma case reports in Kazakhstan. Their findings indicated that the mean age of patients was 60 years old, with the higher prevalence among male patients. Furthermore, the authors did not identify any correlation between MPM incidence and asbestos exposure [4].

Despite advancements in systemic chemotherapy and targeted therapies, the prognosis for MPM remains poor, with a median survival of 8–14 months [5]. Standard treatment modalities include surgery in combination with radiotherapy and/or chemotherapy. Surgery is considered as a treatment option only for selected patients with resectable tumors, where complete resection can be achieved [6, 7]. Hyperthermic Intrathoracic Chemotherapy (HITOC) has emerged as a promising adjunct to surgery. By combining cytoreductive techniques with heated chemotherapy, HITOC enhances local drug delivery, improves penetration into residual tumor tissue, and exploits the synergistic effects of hyperthermia on chemotherapy efficacy. Early evidence suggests that HITOC can improve local control and extend survival in patients with pleural malignancies, including mesothelioma. This case reports first successful application of HITOC for patient with MPM in Kazakhstan. It underscores the practical considerations, safety profile, and outcomes, contributing to the growing body of evidence supporting this approach.

Case presentation

A 35-year-old Asian male was presented with symptoms of dry cough, dyspnea on exertion and fatigue in late 2022. Over time, the patient dyspnea worsened and happened at rest, intermittent chest discomfort, and persistent fatigue. The patient underwent chest X-ray, which revealed a pleural effusion. Subsequent computed tomography (CT) identified a pleural lesion. Following further evaluation, a right sided thoracoscopic biopsy of the pleura was performed. Histopathological findings confirmed the diagnosis of malignant pleural mesothelioma, supported by immunohistochemical staining that detected positive D2—40 expression. The patient did not undergo PET-CT at the time of diagnosis for disease staging. The patient was diagnosed with Stage II malignant pleural mesothelioma (T2N0M0). His medical history included significant comorbidities such as arterial hypertension, obesity, steatohepatitis and gallstone disease. The patient underwent systemic chemotherapy consisting of six cycles of pemetrexed, carboplatin, and bevacizumab from March to July 2023. Imaging studies, including PET-CT, demonstrated stabilization of the disease, with no evidence of metabolic activity and tumor growth,suggesting a positive response to chemotherapy. Considering the localized nature of the disease and the absence of significant progression, the patient was referred to the National Research Oncology Center for surgical evaluation. A multidisciplinary tumor board recommended cytoreductive surgery with Hyperthermic Intrathoracic Chemotherapy (HITOC) as the next step in treatment. This decision was based on the patient’s stable disease, good performance status (ECOG 0), and the potential benefits of achieving local control through combined modality treatment. The patient underwent cytoreductive surgery (open thoracotomy with pleurectomy) followed by HITHOC. The postoperative assessment indicated that the patient was in relatively stable condition, with no signs of disease progression. Discussion

Malignant pleural mesothelioma (MPM) is a rare and aggressive neoplasm with a poor prognosis.

The standard chemotherapy regimen for MPM includes pemetrexed, either alone or in combination with cisplatin. The addition of bevacizumab has been shown improve progression-free and overall survival. Chemotherapy not only extends survival but also enhances quality of life by significantly alleviating clinical manifestation of the disease, such as dyspnea. According to the guidelines the triplet chemotherapy regimen can be offered to the patients without severe comorbidities [8]. However, there are no randomized data for triplet drug combination superiority over standard regimen. In the presented case, the triple regimen was utilized, resulting in disease stabilization. This outcome allowed further surgical evaluation and subsequent cytoreductive surgery with hyperthermic intrathoracic chemotherapy (HITOC).

The tumor response rate is strongly associated with the histological type of MPM [9]. According to the SEER database, the median survival for patients with epithelial MPM, who underwent surgery was 19 months. Surgery improved survival in patients with epithelial MPM unlike patients with biphasic and sarcomatoid type of MPM.Surgical approach in MPM treatment has been accepted since the first description of extrapleural pneumonectomy (EPP) [10]. Extended pleurectomy and decortication (EPD) remains a cornerstone of surgical management for select patients with MPM, aiming to achieve macroscopic complete resection (MCR). The question whether chemotherapy should be delivered before, during or after surgery remains controversial due to scarcity of randomized trials in MPM. Currently, the use of local intraoperative chemotherapy also known hyperthermic intrathoracic chemotherapy (HITOC), is a highly debated topic. Although not included in guidelines, the recent guidelines referenced in the literature have reported cases of HITHOC being employed as part of a multimodal approach for MPM [11]. In this case, EPD successfully removed gross tumor deposits, while HITOC was employed to address residual microscopic disease. HITOC leverages the synergistic effects of hyperthermia and intrathoracic chemotherapy to enhance local control. Hyperthermia increases cell membrane permeability, improving drug penetration into tumor tissues and exerting direct cytotoxic effects by inducing apoptosis in malignant cells. Additionally, hyperthermia reduces chemotherapy resistance in cancer cells, further amplifying its efficacy. This case demonstrated the safety and feasibility of HITOC with cisplatin, which was performed at 42 °C for 40 min. The patient experienced no significant complications, such as cisplatin-induced nephrotoxicity or postoperative respiratory compromise, highlighting the importance of meticulous intraoperative and postoperative management. Furthermore, early mobilization and effective pain control facilitated a smooth recovery, underscoring the benefits of a multidisciplinary approach in optimizing patient outcomes.

The combination of EPD and HITOC provided effective local disease control, with no evidence of recurrence at the one-month follow-up. While the patient's systemic chemotherapy before surgery contributed to initial disease stabilization, the surgical intervention and HITOC played a critical role in achieving long-term local control. These findings align with existing literature suggesting that HITOC, as an adjunct to cytoreductive surgery, may prolong disease-free intervals and improve overall survival in patients with MPM.

Despite its promising potential, HITOC is not without challenges. The procedure requires specialized equipment, experienced surgical teams, and rigorous intraoperative monitoring. Patient selection is also critical, as comorbidities and poor performance status can increase the risk of complications. In this case, the patient’s obesity and associated conditions posed potential risks, but preoperative optimization and careful perioperative management mitigated these factors.

Another limitation of HITOC is the lack of standardized protocols regarding the choice and dosage of chemotherapeutic agents, perfusion temperature, and duration. Variability in these parameters across centers complicates comparisons of clinical outcomes and the establishment of evidence-based guidelines.

Conclusions

This case report highlights the successful use of HITOC in a patient with MPM, offering promising insights into its potential role as a adjunctive treatment.Given the positive outcome in this case and the evolving body of literature,further research and clinical trials are needed to establish the efficacy and safety of HITHOC as a standard treatment option for MPM.

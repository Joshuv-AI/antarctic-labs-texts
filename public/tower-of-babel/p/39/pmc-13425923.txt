Network toxicology and bioinformatics reveal potential molecular links between cadmium exposure and pancreatic cancer
Authors: Shuyuan Liu, Xinyan Lu, Desheng Li, Jingyuan Ma, Yunshu Zhang, Junchen Li, Xing Wan, Dong Shang, Qingkai Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundEnvironmental cadmium (Cd) pollution poses a severe threat to human health due to its strong bioaccumulation and high carcinogenicity. Although Cd exposure has been linked to various cancers, its specific role in pancreatic cancer (PC) remains unclear. This study aimed to explore the molecular mechanisms underlying Cd-associated PC and identify potential diagnostic and therapeutic targets.MethodsTranscriptomic data of PC were obtained from the GEO database to screen differentially expressed genes (DEGs), which were then intersected with Cd-related genes retrieved from the Comparative Toxicogenomics Database (CTD). Functional enrichment analyses, including GO and KEGG, were conducted to identify key biological processes and pathways. Multiple machine learning algorithms were used to construct a diagnostic model and identify hub genes. Furthermore, molecular docking and molecular dynamics simulations were performed to evaluate drug–target interactions, and in vitro experiments were used to validate the effects of Cd exposure on PC cell proliferation and invasiveness.ResultsA total of five key genes—FN1, COL5A1, AHR, COL3A1, and TIMP1—were identified as core regulators of Cd-associated PC. Functional analyses indicated that Cd primarily affects the extracellular matrix (ECM) metabolic and collagen remodeling pathways. Experimental results confirmed that Cd exposure significantly enhanced the proliferation and invasiveness of PC cells. Molecular docking and dynamics simulations revealed that the EGFR inhibitor dacomitinib exhibited favorable predicted binding affinity to FN1, suggesting its potential as a therapeutic agent.ConclusionsThis study reveals that Cd may promote the malignant progression of PC by regulating ECM remodeling through key genes such as FN1. Dacomitinib shows promise as an FN1-associated therapy, offering new insights for the precise prevention and treatment of Cd-associated PC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40360-026-01156-6.

Introduction

Metal pollution has become an increasingly serious global public health issue. The discharge of industrial waste and the mining and smelting of metal ores exacerbate health risks for modern population [1, 2]. Cadmium (Cd) is a toxic heavy metal that is widely present in the environment due to its low melting point, volatility and environmental persistence, and poses a serious threat to human health [3]. Not only is Cd ubiquitously released through industrial processes, it is also pervasively present in consumer products. Epidemiological studies indicate that batteries, fuels, and tobacco are the primary sources of Cd intake for humans in contemporary society [4]. Cd is bioaccumulative, and its half-life in the human body varies depending on different organs. Studies show that the biological half-life of Cd in blood is estimated to be between 75 and 128 days [5, 6], whereas it can accumulate in the kidneys, pancreas, and liver for 10–30 years [3, 5, 7, 8].

Pancreatic cancer (PC) is one of the most aggressive and lethal malignant tumors [9]. While genetic susceptibility and lifestyle factors are well-established contributing factors, the role of environmental exposures in the etiology of PC has received increasing attention in recent years. One such environmental factor is exposure to heavy metals, such as Cd. Epidemiological studies have indicated that individuals exposed to Cd have more than double the risk of developing PC compared to those with low or no exposure [10]. However, the effects of Cd on PC–related pathological changes, as well as its interactions with key regulatory targets, remain largely unknown.

This study employed an integrated approach to investigate the potential role of Cd exposure in PC. Combining network toxicology, machine learning, and bioinformatics analyses, we constructed a gene-toxicity association network to elucidate the expression patterns of Cd-associated genes in PC and the underlying toxicological mechanisms. We then used machine learning to identify key genes and establish potential links between Cd exposure and PC pathogenesis.

Materials and methods

Cell culture and Cd exposure

In order to experimentally verify whether Cd exposure promotes PC progression, in vitro functional assays were conducted using Cd exposed PC cell lines. PANC-1 and BxPC-3 cells were cultured in DMEM and RPMI-1640 medium, respectively, supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin–streptomycin under standard conditions (37 °C, 5% CO₂). CdCl₂ (Aladdin, China) was freshly prepared and added to the culture medium at a final concentration of 1 µM, based on previous reports [11]. Control cells were cultured in parallel under identical conditions without CdCl₂ exposure. The exposure was maintained for 20 weeks to mimic chronic Cd exposure in PC cells, with medium renewal every 2–3 days to ensure stable Cd concentration. After chronic exposure, cells were harvested for functional assays. For colony formation assays, 1 × 10³ cells were seeded into six-well plates in triplicate and cultured for two weeks, followed by fixation and staining with 0.1% crystal violet to visualize colonies. For invasion assays, the invasive ability of Cd-exposed and control cells was assessed using a Matrigel-coated Transwell system.

Collection of targets for Cd

We retrieved gene targets related to Cd exposure from the Comparative Toxicogenomics Database (CTD, https://ctdbase.org). We searched for the keyword “Cd” (including the terms “cadmium”, “cadmium acetate”, “cadmium chloride” and “cadmium nitrate”), and applied filtering criteria to retain only genes with more than 5 interactions.

Screening the intersection of PC genes and Cd targets

We downloaded the transcriptome dataset of PC patients from GEO, using the datasets GSE15471 [12] and GSE62452 [13]. We used the “SVA” package to merge two datasets into a training set comprising a total of 108 PC samples and 100 control samples. Differential gene expression analysis was performed using thresholds of adjusted p < 0.05 and |log2FC| > 1. Subsequently, we used Venn diagram analysis to identify potential common genes linking Cd exposure and PC.

Construction of a PPI network and functional analysis

The intersecting genes identified as being involved in Cd-induced PC were input into the STRING database. Species selection was limited to Homo sapiens and the ‘medium confidence’ interaction score (> 0.4) was used for analysis. The resulting protein-protein interaction (PPI) network diagram was obtained. These results were imported into Cytoscape 3.10.3, and the CytoHubba plugin was used to calculate the parameters of each node in the network diagram and perform a key node analysis. To elucidate the core mechanisms and pathways involved in the potential targets, GO and KEGG pathway enrichment analyses were conducted.

Construction of a diagnostic model based on Cd-related genes in PC

We integrated the GSE15471 and GSE62452 transcriptomic datasets retrieved from the GEO database to establish a comprehensive training cohort. Concurrently, the independent datasets GSE71729 [14] and GSE71989 [15] were employed as external validation cohorts to evaluate model robustness and generalizability. To construct a robust diagnostic model, we integrated and combined multiple machine learning algorithms, including LASSO, ridge regression, SVM, RF, XGBoost, GLM, Stepglm, LDA, GBM, and Naive Bayes. Diagnostic performance was evaluated using AUC [16, 17]. The model with the highest average AUC was selected as the optimal model.

SHAP is widely regarded as superior to other global approximation-based interpretability methods because it quantifies feature importance across the entire model and elucidates the role of each feature in individual predictions. In this study, we used SHAP values to evaluate the importance of features within machine learning models that exhibit optimal predictive performance.

Expression analysis of model genes

The GEPIA platform can utilize data from TCGA and GTEx databases to analyze the differential expression of genes and survival situations. For protein-level analysis, we utilized the CPTAC platform, a dedicated resource for proteomic research, to examine the corresponding protein expression profiles.

The Human Protein Atlas (HPA) database aims to systematically map comprehensive protein expression patterns in cells and tissues. Leveraging this resource, we further downloaded immunohistochemical images of PC and normal tissues from the HPA platform to visually validate the differences in protein expression of the model genes at the tissue level.

Prediction of potential therapeutic drugs

To identify potential therapeutic drugs targeting the model genes, the CMap database (https://clue.io/) was used. The candidate small molecules were screened. The binding energy, binding sites, and structural stability were evaluated through molecular docking, where the three-dimensional structure of the protein was obtained from the PDB database. The three-dimensional structure of FN1 was obtained from the PDB. The protein structure was prepared by removing water molecules and heteroatoms, followed by the addition of hydrogen atoms. The ligand dacomitinib was retrieved from the PubChem database and energy-minimized prior to docking. The docking simulation was performed using the CB-Dock2 platform, which automatically identifies potential binding cavities and conducts blind docking [18, 19]. The docking grid was generated based on cavity detection, and binding affinity was evaluated using AutoDock Vina scoring functions. Interacting residues were defined as amino acids located within the predicted binding pocket identified by CB-Dock2.

The FN1–dacomitinib complex was subjected to a 100 ns molecular dynamics simulation using GROMACS 2021.3 with the CHARMM36 force field. The system was solvated in a TIP3P water box, neutralized with counterions, and energy minimized using the steepest descent algorithm. Equilibration was conducted under NVT (300 K) and NPT (1 atm) ensembles. To comprehensively evaluate the structural stability and dynamic behavior of the drug–target complexes, a series of trajectory analyses were performed. The Root Mean Square Deviation (RMSD) and Root Mean Square Fluctuation (RMSF) were calculated to assess overall structural stability and residue-level flexibility, respectively. Structural compactness and solvation properties were monitored using Radius of Gyration (Rg) and Solvent-Accessible Surface Area (SASA). The stability of ligand–receptor interactions was characterized by analyzing hydrogen bond variations. Furthermore, Principal Component Analysis (PCA) was conducted to identify dominant conformational motions—visualized via covariance matrix heatmaps—while the Free Energy Landscape (FEL) was constructed to explore thermodynamic stability and low-energy conformational distributions. PCA was performed using the backbone atoms of FN1 to evaluate dominant collective motions during the simulation. The covariance matrix of atomic fluctuations was constructed after the removal of overall translational and rotational motions, and eigenvectors and eigenvalues were obtained by diagonalization. The first two principal components (PC1 and PC2) were used to generate the conformational projection plots. The FEL was constructed based on the joint probability distribution of PC1 and PC2 using the equation G = − kBT ln P, where kB is the Boltzmann constant, T is the simulation temperature (300 K), and P is the probability density. FEL plots were generated using GROMACS analysis tools and visualized using Python.

Statistical analysis

All experiments were performed in triplicate. Data are presented as mean ± SD. Statistical comparisons between two groups were performed using Student’s t-test after confirming normal distribution (Shapiro–Wilk test) and homogeneity of variance (Levene’s test). A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

The study is as shown in the flowchart (Fig. 1). First, we merged the GSE15471 and GSE62452 datasets and performed batch effect correction (Fig. 2A-B). The criteria for selecting DEGs were set as adjusted p < 0.05 and |log2 fold change (log2FC) | > 1. Ultimately, 392 DEGs were identified and visualized in a volcano plot (Fig. 2E).

Construction of co-expressed gene modules by WGCNA

We conducted a WGCNA on the merged data after batch correction (Fig. 3A-B). We used module membership (MM) to represent the correlation between genes and modules; and gene significance (GS) to represent the importance of genes in the modules. We obtained modules related to PC and drew models showing the clinical correlation between each module and PC. A total of 9 modules related to PC were identified (Fig. 3C-D). Among these, the genes in the brown module showed a significantly positive correlation with the occurrence and development of PC and exhibited the strongest clinical correlation. We created a scatter diagram (Fig. 3E) showing the relationship between GS and MM in each module, and selected the brown module for further analysis. We then extracted the intersection of the genes in this module and the DEGs from the previous database sources, and obtained 120 PC-associated genes for subsequent analysis (Fig. 3F).

Screening of target molecules for Cd-associated PC and enrichment analysis

Subsequently, we intersected the 120 PC-associated genes with the 702 Cd–associated genes retrieved from relevant databases, and identified 21 overlapping genes that were both Cd-related and implicated in PC (Fig. 4A). We used Cytoscape to create a diagram showing the relationship between Cd, key genes and tumors (Fig. 4B). We then used the STRING database to construct PPI network (Fig. 4C). GO enrichment analysis revealed that these genes were primarily enriched in BP, including extracellular structure organization, ECM organization, and wound healing. In terms of CC, they were predominantly localized to collagen-containing ECM and the endoplasmic reticulum lumen (Fig. 4D-E). For MF, these genes were primarily responsible for ECM structural constituent activity and ECM structural constituent activity conferring tensile strength. KEGG enrichment analysis demonstrated that these 21 genes were mainly enriched in pathways including protein digestion and absorption, the AGE-RAGE signaling pathway in diabetic complications, the relaxin (RLN) signaling pathway, focal adhesion (FAs), and ECM-receptor interactions (Fig. 4F).

Establishment of diagnostic model

We then constructed a diagnostic model based on Cd-related genes in PC by integrating diverse machine learning methodologies. Two independent datasets (GSE71729 and GSE71989) were designated as test sets for model validation, and heatmaps were generated (Fig. 5A-B). Of the evaluated models, the Stepglm[backward] + RF yielded the superior diagnostic efficacy. This optimal model consists of five genes: FN1, COL5A1, AHR, COL3A1, and TIMP1. As shown in Fig. 5C, each of these genes displayed good diagnostic ability individually, with FN1 achieving the highest AUC value of 0.909. Furthermore, chromosome ideograms were generated to depict the positions of these genes.

In addition, we performed a SHAP analysis on the optimal diagnostic model and used visualization processing to display the importance of each gene in the model more clearly. As shown in Supplementary Fig. 1, FN1 has the greatest contribution to the model with a SHAP value of 0.17; FN1 and COL5A1 are positive contribution factors; and AHR, COL3A1 and TIMP1 are negative contribution factors. Positive SHAP values indicate that higher expression of FN1 and COL5A1 increases the predicted probability of PC, whereas negative SHAP values for AHR, COL3A1 and TIMP1 suggest inverse contributions within the model context.

Expression analysis of model genes in PC

We conducted a study on the mRNA expression of FN1, AHR, COL3A1, COL5A1, and TIMP1, in both pancreatic tumor and normal tissues. As shown in the Supplementary Fig. 2A-E, the mRNA expression levels of these five genes were significantly higher in pancreatic tumor tissues than in normal tissues. We then compared the protein expression levels of these five genes in pancreatic tumor and normal tissues using the CPTAC platform (Supplementary Fig. 2F-J), and obtained similar results. Additionally, we obtained immunohistochemical images of PC from the HPA database. The results showed that compared with normal pancreatic tissues, the protein expression levels of these five genes were higher in tumor tissues (Supplementary Fig. 2K-O). These results further validate the reliability of our bioinformatics analysis findings.

Chronic exposure to Cd facilitates the proliferation and invasion of PC cells

Subsequently, we evaluated the effects of Cd exposure on the biological behavior of PC cells at the cellular level. The results of the colony formation assay showed that compared with the control group, the number of colonies treated by Cd was significantly increased (Fig. 6A-D), indicating that low-dose Cd exposure enhances the proliferative and growth potential of PC cells. Transwell assay was performed to further assess the effect of Cd on cell invasiveness. The results showed that the number of cells penetrating the membrane in the Cd-treated group was markedly higher than that in the control group (Fig. 6E-H), suggesting that Cd exposure promotes the invasive capacity of PC cells.

Prediction of potential therapeutic drugs

We imported the DEGs of PC into the CMap and obtained the top 5 drugs for PC (Table 1). To further explore FN1’s potential as a therapeutic target, we conducted molecular docking analyses between FN1 (PDB ID: 1FNF, Resolution: 2.00 Å) and the five candidate drugs. The results revealed that FN1 exhibited the strongest binding affinity with dacomitinib (binding energy: −8.7 kcal/mol). This suggests that dacomitinib may exert anti-tumor effects in PC by interacting with and modulating FN1 activity (Fig. 7A).

We performed a 100-ns molecular dynamics simulation to evaluate the stability of the FN1–dacomitinib complex. To evaluate the structural stability of the FN1-dacomitinib complex, the RMSD of the backbone atoms was calculated relative to the initial structure during the 100 ns simulation. The RMSD plot indicates that the system underwent a significant structural adjustment during the first 50 ns, reaching values between 1.5 and 2.0 nm. This relatively high RMSD value is attributed to the intrinsic flexibility of the loop regions within the FN1 structure. However, after 60 ns, the RMSD curve plateaued and oscillated around an average value of 1.75 nm, suggesting that the complex eventually reached a dynamic equilibrium state and the relatively high RMSD values may reflect the intrinsic flexibility of the FN1 domain and structural relaxation from the docking conformation (Fig. 7B). RMSF analysis showed generally mild atomic fluctuations, with residues 40–160 being more stable than the terminal regions, and two local minima at residues 40–60 and 140–160 indicated the most rigid regions (Fig. 7C). The Rg fluctuated between ~ 2.5–3.0 nm, reflecting stable overall compactness (Fig. 7D). SASA remained between 210 and 230 nm², indicating no significant unfolding or folding (Fig. 7E). Hydrogen bond analysis showed 1–3 bonds maintained throughout, supporting stable interactions (Fig. 7F). Finally, FEL analysis revealed a prominent low-energy region, confirming high thermodynamic stability of the complex (Fig. 7G-H).

Discussion

PC is one of the most aggressive diseases, and its five-year survival rate remains lower than 9% [20]. The non-genetic factors of PC mainly include exposure to toxic substances, smoking, high fasting blood glucose levels, and heavy alcohol consumption [21]. Cd is widely present in the human living environment and exhibits strong carcinogenicity [22]. Tobacco is a key Cd source for smokers: a single cigarette contains 0.5–2 µg Cd, with 10–20% of inhaled Cd absorbed by the lungs [23, 24]. For non-smokers, dietary intake is the primary Cd source. Owing to bioaccumulation, Cd can induce multi-organ toxicity (kidneys, pancreas, liver, etc.) [22, 25, 26], and promote PC via diverse mechanisms [27]. Scholars generally recognize Cd’s roles in organ damage and carcinogenesis, particularly in kidney cancer, prostate cancer, and cardiac/kidney dysfunction. Despite confirmed Cd carcinogenicity, studies on its interaction with PC are limited. Therefore, we integrated multiple bioinformatics approaches to explore the mechanisms by which Cd exposure contributes to the development and progression of PC.

In this study, a total of 21 Cd-associated PC genes were obtained. GO enrichment analysis revealed Cd-PC intersecting targets are primarily enriched in collagen-rich ECM, endoplasmic reticulum, and ECM component-associated processes. KEGG enrichment identified pathways including protein digestion and absorption, RLN signaling, focal adhesions (FAs), PI3K/Akt, and ECM-receptor interaction. FAs are dynamic cell-ECM linkages that anchor the cytoskeleton via transmembrane receptors, mediating mechanical and chemical signal transduction [28–30]. Liu R et al. [31]. found that inhibiting the focal adhesion pathway can induce tumor cell dormancy and inhibit metastasis. RLN is a kind of peptide hormone, physiologically enhances collagenase activity to degrade collagen and exhibits vasodilatory, hypotensive, and anti-fibrotic effects. However, its aberrant activation drives tumor microenvironment ECM remodeling and promotes tumor progression. The RLN signaling pathway can promote tumor progression by binding G protein-coupled receptor RXFP1 and activating downstream cascades (e.g. PI3K/AKT, MEK/ERK, cAMP/PKA) [32, 33]. The PI3K/Akt pathway, a highly conserved and well-studied signaling cascade, is intimately linked to tumor initiation and metastasis [34]. Activated Akt phosphorylates Snail, downregulates E-cadherin, and promotes EMT and metastasis. Chronic Cd exposure induces aberrant PI3K/Akt activation, triggering downstream signaling that drives PC invasion and metastasis [35].

Through the integration of multiple machine learning approaches, five hub genes (FN1, COL5A1, AHR, COL3A1, and TIMP1) were identified with favorable diagnostic efficacy. FN1 can encode fibronectin, a glycoprotein prevalent in the ECM involved in cell adhesion, migration, proliferation, and signal transduction [36]. As a core ECM component, FN1 activates the PI3K/AKT and FAK pathways via integrin receptors in various cancers—including renal, breast, thyroid, and nasopharyngeal cancers—promoting migration, invasion, and chemoresistance [37, 38]. The same is true in PC, where Ashok G et al. [38] found that FN1 plays an important role in the invasion and metastasis of PC and can serve as a potential therapeutic and diagnostic biomarker for metastatic pancreatic tumors. Zhang L et al. [39] also demonstrated that silencing FN1 can effectively reduce the invasion and metastasis of Cd-exposed related tumors. COL5A1 encodes the α1 chain of type V collagen, maintaining ECM homeostasis and promoting wound repair under physiological conditions [40]. Its overexpression significantly promotes tumor progression and chemotherapy resistance [41] by activating the Wnt/β-catenin pathway. The COL3A1 gene encodes the α1 chain of type III collagen. Similar to type V collagen [42], type III collagen is also an important component of ECM, and downregulation of COL3A1 significantly inhibits the growth and metastasis of cancer cells [43]. AHR mediates responses to environmental signals and toxicants, and regulates development, immunity, metabolism, and cellular physiology. AHR overexpression can also be observed in autoimmune diseases and various cancers [44]. For example, in PC, AHR overexpression promotes tumor progression and metastasis [45]. TIMP1 encodes tissue-inhibitory matrix metalloproteinase1, which can regulate biological processes such as cell proliferation, apoptosis, and differentiation [46]. Lee SY et al. [47] have found that TIMP1 activates the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway, thereby affecting the proliferation and migration of tumor cells. Based on integrative bioinformatics, machine learning and experimental validation, we propose a mechanistic model in which chronic Cd exposure induces aberrant ECM remodeling in PC. Cd exposure may induce transcriptional upregulation of key ECM-related genes, including FN1, COL5A1, COL3A1 and TIMP1, as well as the environmental sensor AHR. These genes cooperatively promote collagen deposition, ECM stiffening and focal adhesion signaling, thereby activating downstream oncogenic pathways such as PI3K/AKT and FAK signaling. The resulting ECM reprogramming enhances tumor cell proliferation, invasion and metastatic potential, ultimately facilitating PC progression under Cd exposure (Fig. 7I).

To validate the above conclusions, we selected the PANC-1 and BxPC-3 cell lines for colony formation assay and Transwell assay. We observed that Cd exposure significantly enhanced tumor cell proliferation and invasion capacity, consistent with our findings. Subsequently, to identify potential targeted therapies for Cd-associated PC, we selected FN1—the gene with the highest diagnostic efficacy—as a target for drug prediction. Drug prediction and molecular docking suggested that dacomitinib may interact with FN1 with relatively favorable binding energy, and molecular dynamics simulations indicated that the complex could maintain structural equilibration during the simulation. Dacomitinib, a tyrosine kinase inhibitor, inhibits tumor growth and promotes apoptosis. While primarily used for non-small cell lung cancer, studies [48, 49] report efficacy in PC, aligning with our findings. This suggests dacomitinib’s potential as a therapeutic for Cd-associated PC. Although dacomitinib is a known ERBB family inhibitor, our computational analysis suggests a potential interaction with FN1. Therefore, it may represent an FN1-associated therapeutic candidate rather than a direct FN1-specific inhibitor.

This study has several limitations. First, reliance on public transcriptomic data limits model generalizability due to sample size and population heterogeneity. Second, future studies should utilize exposure-stratified clinical cohorts or cadmium measurement cohorts (such as blood/urine cadmium levels) to establish a truly cadmium exposure-specific diagnostic model. In addition, the chronic Cd exposure model in this study is mainly evaluated at the endpoint, and future time course analysis is needed to better capture the gradual adaptation of cells to long-term cadmium exposure. Finally, our analysis suggests the model genes are potential mediators of Cd-induced PC through ECM remodeling; this association remains speculative in the current study. Future studies require in-depth mechanistic validation of these central genes to establish a causal relationship.

Conclusions

Our study systematically investigated the potential molecular mechanisms underlying the association between Cd exposure and PC through comprehensive network toxicology and bioinformatics analyses. By integrating transcriptomic profiling with functional enrichment analyses, we identified Cd-associated genes that may promote pancreatic tumor invasion by modulating collagen metabolism within the ECM. Experimental validation further supported this hypothesis. Furthermore, molecular docking and molecular dynamics simulations revealed that dacomitinib exhibits favorable predicted binding affinity to FN1, suggesting the potential as a promising therapeutic candidate for targeting Cd-associated PC.

Effect of monochromatic light combinations on muscle fiber types in broilers after thyroidectomy
Authors: Yameng Liu, Zixu Wang, Jing Cao, Yulan Dong, Ting Gao, Fuwei Li, Enliang Song, Yaoxing Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractOur previous research indicated that monochromatic light combinations influence muscle fiber types in broilers, potentially through alterations in thyroid function, yet the underlying mechanisms remained elusive. Here, we employed a thyroidectomy (TX) model to investigate the role of thyroid hormone (T3) in mediating these light-induced effects. Broilers were reared under single-monochromatic light (white-white [WW], red-red [RR], green-green [GG], blue-blue [BB]) or different light combinations (green-blue [GB], blue-green [BG], red-blue [RB]). Our results demonstrate that monochromatic light combinations modulate serum thyroid hormone (TH) levels, thereby regulating oxidative stress (OS) and the composition of slow- (MyHC-I/IIa) and fast-twitch (MyHC-IIb) fibers in the gastrocnemius (GAS). Critically, TX-induced hypothyroidism not only reduced malondialdehyde (MDA) levels and the proportion of fast glycolytic fibers (MyHC-IIb) but also promoted a shift toward oxidative fibers (MyHC-I/IIa). Notably, this TX intervention abolished the intergroup differences in fiber-type composition caused by different light colors, confirming the indispensable mediating role of TH. Mechanistically, we found that TX significantly upregulated the inactivating deiodinase DIO3-a direct transcriptional target of the β-Catenin/TCF complex—while downregulating the activating deiodinase DIO2 and thyroid hormone receptor α (TRα). This was accompanied by enhanced phosphorylation of the PI3K/AKT/GSK3β axis, increased β-Catenin protein levels, and corresponding changes in gene expression: upregulation of slow-fiber markers (Pax7, Myf5, MYH7B) and downregulation of fast-fiber markers (MyoD, MyoG, Mstn, MYH1A). In conclusion, our study reveals that deiodinases DIO2 and DIO3 are critical mediators through which monochromatic light regulates intramuscular T3 levels and oxidative stress, driving fiber-type transitions via the PI3K/AKT-Wnt/β-Catenin signaling pathway.Keywords: Thyroid hormone, Monochromatic light, Muscle fiber type, Thyroidectomy

Introduction

Skeletal muscle, constituting the largest tissue system in vertebrates, represents approximately 40-50% of total body mass and serves fundamental roles in both locomotor function and energy metabolism. This highly heterogeneous tissue comprises primarily fast-twitch (type II) and slow-twitch (type I) fibers, which differ significantly in morphological characteristics (diameter and color depth), physiological properties (fatigue threshold), and metabolic patterns (oxidative/glycolytic capacity) (Tan et al., 2025). These functional specializations are physiologically essential for maintaining systemic glucose homeostasis (Wu and Ballantyne, 2017). The current classification system, based on myosin heavy chain (MyHC) isoform expression patterns, identifies four principal fiber types: type I (MYH7, slow-twitch oxidative), type IIa (MYH2, fast-twitch oxidative), type IIx (MYH1, fast-twitch oxidative-glycolytic), and type IIb (MYH4, fast-twitch glycolytic) (Tan et al., 2025). Notably, the composition of muscle fiber types in chickens does not include type IIx.

The total number of muscle fibers is established prenatally/prior to hatching, yet their phenotypic composition exhibits remarkable plasticity, enabling adaptive transformation during growth and development (Zhang et al., 2017). This dynamic remodeling process is regulated by multiple factors: exogenous factors including nutrient availability, mechanical loading, environmental parameters, and developmental stage, as well as endogenous factors such as transcriptional networks and hormonal signaling pathways. Among these regulators, thyroid hormone (TH) levels serve as master regulators of skeletal muscle development and fiber-type conversion. Physiologically, hypothyroidism promotes a fast-to-slow fiber-type transition, whereas hyperthyroidism induces the opposite conversion pattern. At the molecular level, TH orchestrates MyHC isoform switching through bidirectional regulation: low TH levels induce the sequential transition of MyHC-IIb → IIx → IIa → I, while high TH levels reverse this progression. Beyond directly modulating fiber phenotypes, TH participates in multiple physiological processes including myoblast proliferation and differentiation, myotube maturation, and energy metabolism reprogramming (Schiaffino et al., 2025). Recent studies have revealed that thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) exacerbates skeletal muscle insulin resistance by activating macrophage-mediated inflammation (characterized by elevated IL-1α/β and IL-6 secretion) (Zhang et al., 2025). In contrast, phytochemicals (e.g., resveratrol, quercetin) ameliorate metabolic dysfunction through multiple mechanisms: improving metabolic profiles, reversing insulin resistance, promoting myosin synthesis, and increasing muscle mass via downregulation of myostatin (MSTN) and its receptor expression (Cano-Martínez et al., 2025). In livestock production, slow-twitch-dominant muscles (e.g., soleus [SOL]) exhibit superior meat quality characteristics compared with fast-twitch-rich muscles (e.g., extensor digitorum longus [EDL]). These quality advantages comprise enhanced color attributes (higher a* values), increased intramuscular fat (IMF) content, superior water-holding capacity (WHC), and improved tenderness (reduced shear force values) (Talbot and Maves, 2016).

Extensive studies have demonstrated that key light parameters (photoperiod, intensity, and wavelength) significantly influence poultry growth performance, egg production, and overall health (Wu et al., 2022). These evidence-based findings provide scientific rationale for optimizing artificial lighting systems in modern intensive farming, simultaneously enhancing production efficiency and animal welfare. Our preliminary research revealed that AA broilers exposed to monochromatic green light during early growth stages followed by blue light in later stages exhibited significantly improved production performance (Liu et al., 2024). This lighting regimen promoted skeletal muscle development by increasing muscle fiber diameter and elevating the proportion of oxidative fibers - renowned for their exceptional metabolic endurance and superior meat quality traits. Given the positive correlation between oxidative fiber content and improved meat flavor/juiciness, elucidating the molecular mechanisms underlying light-induced fiber-type conversion holds substantial value for meat production. However, the specific signaling pathways through which light stimulation regulates the transformation of muscle fiber types remain unclear. To address this, we selected the mixed‑fiber gastrocnemius muscle (GAS) -which is more sensitive to thyroid hormone and light than the predominantly glycolytic pectoralis major-and established a thyroidectomy (TX) model to simulate a hypothyroid state. The results demonstrated that monochromatic light combinations modulated the proportion of slow‑twitch (MyHC‑I/IIa) and fast‑twitch (MyHC‑IIb) fibers in the GAS by regulating serum thyroid hormone (TH) levels and altering oxidative stress (OS) status. Following thyroidectomy (TX), the differences between light wavelength groups were abolished, confirming that TH serves as a key mediator in light signal transduction. Furthermore, the deiodinases DIO2/DIO3 were identified as epigenetic hubs mediating light environment regulation of muscle metabolism.

Materials and methods

Animals and treatment

All animal experimental procedures were approved by the Animal Welfare Committee of China Agricultural University (Approval No. 20171114-2).

A total of 252 male Arbor Acre (AA) day-old chicks (P0) were obtained from Beijing Huadu Meat Chicken Breeding Co., Ltd. to establish the experimental model. At day 0 (P0), chicks were randomly allocated to four lighting environments: white light (WL, 400–700 nm, n=36), red light (RL, 660 nm, n=72), green light (GL, 560 nm, n=72), and blue light (BL, 480 nm, n=72). Light intensity was maintained at 15 ± 0.3 lux with a photoperiod of 23L:1D until day 42 (P42).

At day 7, within each lighting environment, birds were further divided into three surgical groups: thyroidectomy (TX, n=12 per light group), sham operation (Sham, n=12 per light group), and non‑surgical control (Control, n=12 per light group). Thyroidectomy was performed under pentobarbital sodium anesthesia (45 μg/g body weight). A 3 cm thoracic incision was made to expose and completely remove bilateral thyroid glands. Sham‑operated birds underwent identical procedures without thyroid removal. Controls received no surgery. Postoperative care included administration of penicillin‑streptomycin for infection prevention and Yunnan Baiyao powder traditional Chinese medicine with hemostatic and wound-healing properties) for hemostasis.

At day 26, birds from the red, green, and blue light groups were each subdivided to create different light‑transition groups while maintaining the TX, Sham, and Control subgroups. Specifically, from the red light group, 12 birds (per surgical subgroup) remained under red light (RR), and the other 12 were transferred to blue light (RB). From the green light group, 12 birds remained under green light (GG), and the other 12 were transferred to blue light (GB). From the blue light group, 12 birds remained under blue light (BB), and the other 12 were transferred to green light (BG). The white light group remained under white light throughout (WW). This design yielded seven experimental light treatments: WW, RR, GG, BB, GB, BG, and RB. In each of these seven groups, there were 12 TX and 12 Sham birds (total 168 birds). The Control groups (84 birds) were used only for preliminary validation, because no significant differences were found between Control and Sham in serum T3/T4 levels, body weight, or muscle fiber composition (p>0.05, data not shown). Therefore, subsequent analyses focused exclusively on comparisons between Sham and TX groups.

Sampling

At 42 days post-hatch, broilers were individually weighed and subjected to wing vein phlebotomy for blood collection. Six birds were randomly selected from each of the 14 subgroups (7 light treatments × 2 surgical conditions: Sham and TX) for muscle sampling (n = 6 per subgroup, total 84 birds). Following collection, blood samples were maintained at ambient temperature (25±1°C) for 2 hours to permit complete coagulation, followed by centrifugation at 1,000 × g for 20 minutes at 4°C to isolate serum fractions. The obtained serum was aliquoted and cryopreserved at -20°C pending subsequent biochemical analyses. Following humane euthanasia via cervical dislocation, bilateral gastrocnemius muscles (GAS) were immediately dissected. The gastrocnemius muscle was chosen for this study because its mixed-fiber composition (slow-twitch oxidative and fast-twitch glycolytic) renders it more responsive to thyroid hormone and light stimulation than the predominantly glycolytic pectoralis major. Tissue samples were systematically processed through two parallel preservation protocols: (1) snap-freezing in liquid nitrogen with subsequent storage at -80°C for molecular characterization (including qPCR and Western blot analyses) and enzymatic activity assays; (2) immersion fixation in 4% paraformaldehyde (0.1 M phosphate buffer, pH 7.4) for 24 hours at 4°C to facilitate optimal tissue preservation for histological processing, including paraffin embedding, microtome sectioning, and comprehensive immunohistochemical evaluations.

Hematoxylin-Eosin (HE) staining and enzyme activities assay

The gastrocnemius muscle samples underwent standard histological processing through a graded ethanol series (50%, 75%, 85%, 95%, and 100%) for progressive dehydration, followed by xylene clearing and paraffin embedding. Serial sections (5 μm thickness) were obtained using a rotary microtome (Leica RM2235, Germany). Prior to staining, sections were dewaxed in xylene and rehydrated through a descending ethanol series, then subjected to conventional hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining for morphological evaluation.

For enzymatic histochemistry, fresh gastrocnemius tissues were sectioned at 8 μm thickness using a cryostat microtome (Leica CM1950, Germany) without prior fixation or dehydration. Muscle fiber typing was performed using commercial staining kits for succinate dehydrogenase (SDHase, Cat# G2000) and adenosine triphosphate (ATPase, Cat# G3000) from Solarbio (Beijing, China), following manufacturer's protocols. Stained sections were imaged under brightfield microscopy (Nikon Eclipse 80i, Japan) and analyzed using Image-Pro Plus 6.0 software (Media Cybernetics, USA) for quantitative assessment of fiber type distribution.

Muscle fiber characteristics were quantitatively assessed using ImageJ software. The average CSA (μm²) calculated by ImageJ was based on the total area of 2,000 muscle fibers from each muscle. Muscle fiber density (fibers/mm²) was assessed by counting the number of muscle fibers within a specified area. The muscle fiber area for each group (2,000 myofibers) was categorized, and the percentage of muscle fibers within a specific area relative to the total number of fibers was computed. Additionally, we classified 2,000 muscle fibers in the GAS of broilers based on cross-sectional area (CSA) size into three categories: Minor fibers (CSA: 0 ∼ 1,080 μm²), Medium-large fibers (CSA: 1,081 ∼2,300 μm²), and Large fibers (CSA: 2,301 ∼ 3,050 μm²).

Antioxidant enzyme activity and lipid peroxidation level

The activities of catalase (CAT, units/ml), glutathione peroxidase (GPx, mU/ml), total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC, mmol/g), and malondialdehyde (MDA, μmol/mg) levels in GAS were quantitatively analyzed using commercial assay kits (Beyotime Institute of Biotechnology, Shanghai, China). The following biomarkers were measured according to the manufacturer's protocols. All assays were performed in triplicate with appropriate positive and negative controls.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

The concentrations of thyroxine (T4, μg/L), free triiodothyronine (FT3, pmol/L), free thyroxine (FT4, pmol/L), and thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH, pg/mL) in the serum of broilers were measured using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits (SLCY Biotechnology, Beijing, China). The experiments were carried out following the provided guidelines. The detection ranges for T4, FT3, FT4, and TSH were 5-250 μg/L, 1-40 pmol/L, 10-400 pmol/L, and 2-85 pg/mL, respectively. The intra-lot and inter-lot variations were found to be under 9% and 11%, respectively.

qRT-PCR

Total RNA was extracted using Trizol reagent (CoWin Biotech Co., Inc., Beijing, China) and subsequently reverse-transcribed into complementary DNA (cDNA) with a cDNA synthesis kit (Vazyme Biotech Co., Ltd., Nanjing, China) according to the manufacturer's instructions. The primers utilized for reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) in this study are detailed in Table 1. Real-time polymerase chain reaction (PCR) was performed using AceQ® qPCR SYBR® Green Master Mix (Vazyme Biotech Co., Ltd., Nanjing, China). In summary, 2 μL of cDNA was mixed with 10 μL of 2 × SYBR Green Master Mix, 1 μL of forward primer, 1 μL of reverse primer, and 6 μL of nuclease-free ddH₂O, resulting in a total volume of 20 μL for each PCR reaction. The PCR amplification protocol included an initial denaturation step at 95°C for 10 minutes, followed by 40 cycles consisting of denaturation at 95°C for 10 seconds, annealing at 57°C for 30 seconds, and extension at 72°C for 30 seconds. Melt curve analyses were conducted using the default settings of the LightCycler® 480 (Roche Diagnostics GmbH, Bavaria, Germany). The relative expression levels of the genes were normalized to the housekeeping gene GAPDH using the 2−ΔΔCt method.

Western blot analysis

Protein extraction and immunoblotting were performed as previously described (Liu et al., 2022). Briefly, proteins were extracted from tissues using RIPA lysis buffer that included a protease inhibitor (CoWin, Beijing, China). The protein concentration was measured with a BCA kit, and the samples were diluted with RIPA lysis buffer. A total of 20 μg of protein was separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred to a PVDF membrane. After blocking with 5% skim milk, the membrane was incubated overnight with various monoclonal antibodies, including anti-DIO2 (Affinity, USA, DF-12941), anti-DIO3 (Affinity, USA, DF-8532), anti-p-GSK3β (Abnova, China, PAB10055), anti-p-AKT (Sigma, USA, St.Louis, Mo), anti-β-Catenin (Proteintech, USA, 51067-2-AP), all at a dilution of 1:1000. The anti-GAPDH antibody (Proteintech, USA, CN-60004-1) was diluted to 1:5000. The membranes were then washed and incubated at room temperature for 60 minutes with a horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibody diluted to 1:500 (Santa Cruz Biotechnology). Finally, the membranes were developed using the BeyoECL PLUS kit (Beyotime). The target bands from the blots were quantified using ImageJ software, and the data were presented as the integrated optical density (IOD) of the target bands compared to the corresponding tubulin values. Each test was performed in triplicate for each sample.

Statistical analysis

The data were expressed as the mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). The differences between sham-operated (Sham) and thyroidectomized (TX) groups under the same light treatment were analyzed using independent-samples Student's t-tests. The differences among different monochromatic light treatment groups within each surgical condition (Sham or TX) were analyzed using one-way analysis of variance (one-way ANOVA) followed by Tukey's honestly significant difference (HSD) post-hoc test using GraphPad Prism 7 software (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA). The level for determination of significance was set at p < 0.05. All experiments were performed in triplicate.

Results

Thyroidectomy model in broilers with monochromatic light combinations

7-day-old broilers reared under white (WL), red (RL), green (GL), and blue (BL) light conditions were systematically assigned to three groups: Control, Sham, and Thyroidectomy (TX) group (Fig. 1A). Histopathological examination of excised thyroid tissue via periodic acid-Schiff (PAS) staining confirmed intact follicular architecture with colloid-containing thyrocytes (Fig. 1C), verifying complete glandular excision in TX specimens (Fig. 1B). Subsequently, the serum thyroid hormone levels of broilers in both the Sham group and the TX group were compared under various monochromatic light combinations at 42 days of age. After thyroidectomy, the serum levels of TSH significantly increased, ranging from 20.88–38.3% higher than those in the Sham group (Fig. 1D, p < 0.001). In contrast, the levels of thyroxine (Fig. 1E), FT3 (Fig. 1F), and FT4 (Fig. 1G) significantly decreased, with reductions of 54.3–72.2%, 41.1–61.5%, and 46.2–61.1%, respectively, compared to the Sham group (p < 0.001). These results collectively demonstrate the successful establishment and validation of the thyroidectomy model in broilers.

Effects of thyroidectomy on gastrocnemius muscle fiber size in broilers with monochromatic light combinations

As shown in Fig. 2A-2B, when comparing TX and Sham birds within the same light treatment group, the average cross-sectional area (CSA) of the GAS fibers in broilers significantly decreased by 29.5–47.7% (p < 0.05) after thyroidectomy, while the average density of muscle fibers significantly increased by 11.6–38.2% (545–581 fibers/mm², p < 0.05, Fig. 2C). Additionally, fiber size distribution was also altered by thyroidectomy in the gastrocnemius muscle (Fig. 2D). In the TX group, the proportion of minor fibers (CSA 0–1,080 μm²) increased to 62–64.5%, compared to 29.5–57% in the Sham group. This increase was most pronounced in the GB, BG, BB, and GG groups, where the proportion of minor fibers was 30.4–52.4% higher than in the corresponding Sham groups (p < 0.05). In contrast, the proportion of large fibers (CSA 2,301–3,050 μm²) decreased from 12.5–28% in the Sham group to 6.5–8% in the TX group, a reduction of 43.5–76.4% (p < 0.05).

Effects of thyroidectomy on muscle fiber type distribution in broilers with monochromatic light combinations

When comparing TX and Sham birds within the same light treatment group, thyroidectomy (TX) significantly altered the fiber type distribution in the GAS of broilers (SDHase staining, Fig. 3A; ATPase staining, Fig. 3B). However, intergroup variations among different light wavelengths were no longer statistically significant (p > 0.05). Within each light treatment group, the proportion of slow-twitch oxidative fibers (MyHC-I) increased from 16.9–28.4% (Sham group) to 32.5–37% (TX group, p < 0.05; Fig. 3C). Notably, in the RR group, TX increased MyHC-I fibers by 52.6% (p < 0.001). The proportion of fast-twitch oxidative fibers (MyHC-IIa) increased from 20.9–32.1% (Sham) to 36.5–40% (TX, p < 0.01). In contrast, the proportion of fast-twitch glycolytic fibers (MyHC-IIb) decreased from 39.5–60.5% (Sham) to 21.0–25.5% (TX, p < 0.001).Consistent with these histological findings, TX significantly downregulated the fast-glycolytic fiber gene MYH1A by 80.5–93.12% (Fig. 3F, p < 0.001), while upregulating the oxidative fiber genes MYH1B by 78.65–90.56% (Fig. 3E, p < 0.001,) and MYH7B by 84.67–94.83% (Fig. 3D, p < 0.001).

Effects of thyroidectomy on MRFs gene expression in broiler gastrocnemius with monochromatic light combinations

When comparing TX and Sham birds within the same light treatment group, thyroidectomy (TX) significantly altered the expression of myogenic regulatory factors (MRFs) in the gastrocnemius muscle of broilers (Fig. 4A-4F). After TX, the differences among the various light groups were no longer statistically significant (p > 0.05). Within each light treatment group, the expression of Pax7 was significantly upregulated by 15.43–53.34% after thyroidectomy (Fig. 4A, p < 0.01). Similarly, the expression of Myf5 increased by 26.14–44.45% (Fig. 4B, p < 0.01). In contrast, the expression of Myf6 was significantly downregulated by 38.90–58.78% (Fig. 4C, p < 0.01). The expression of MyoD decreased by 43.45–53.82% (Fig. 4D, p < 0.05). In the WW, RR, GG, and RB groups, the expression of MyoG was significantly reduced by 27.89–39.42% after thyroidectomy (Fig. 4E, p < 0.01). Additionally, the expression of Mstn was significantly decreased by 16.25–43.42% (Fig. 4F, p < 0.01).

Effects of thyroidectomy on DIO2/DIO3 and TRα expression in broiler gastrocnemius with monochromatic light combinations

Significant differences in DIO2 and DIO3 protein expression were observed in the gastrocnemius muscle of broilers under different light color groups (Fig. 5A). Notably, DIO2 expression in the RR and WW groups were significantly higher than those in the GB and BG groups by 50.5–63.6% (p < 0.001). In contrast, DIO3 expression in the GB and BG groups was significantly higher than in the RR and WW groups by 70.9–82.3% (Fig. 5B, p < 0.001). After thyroidectomy, differences in DIO2 and DIO3 protein expression among light color groups disappeared (p > 0.05). When comparing TX and Sham birds within the same light treatment group, DIO2 expression in the WW and RR TX groups significantly decreased by 44.8–54.6%, while DIO3 expression significantly increased by 18.6–84.7% compared to their respective Sham groups (p < 0.05).

Effects of thyroidectomy on phosphorylated PI3K/AKT/GSK3β and β-catenin expression in broiler gastrocnemius with monochromatic light combinations

The phosphorylation levels of PI3K, AKT, and GSK3β, as well as β-Catenin protein expression, showed significant differences among different monochromatic light combinations (Fig. 6A-6D). Specifically, p-PI3K expression in the RR and WW groups were significantly lower than in the GB and BG groups by 66.6–71.4% (Fig. 6A, p < 0.001). Similarly, p-AKT expression was lower by 70.7–80.7% (Fig. 6B, p < 0.001), p-GSK3β expression by 41.6–53.8% (Fig. 6C, p < 0.001), and β-Catenin expression by 50.2–83.3% (Fig. 6D, p < 0.001) in the RR and WW groups compared to the GB and BG groups. After thyroidectomy, when comparing TX and Sham birds within the same light treatment group, the phosphorylation levels of PI3K, AKT, GSK3β, and β-Catenin in the WW and RR groups increased significantly compared to their respective Sham groups by 65.6–75.1%, 74.5–85.7%, 46.2–62.3%, and 46.5–84.5%, respectively (Fig. 6A-6D, p < 0.001). At the same time, the differences among the different color groups disappeared (p > 0.05).

Discussioin

The spectral composition of light has increasingly been recognized as a critical environmental factor that extends beyond vision to modulate fundamental physiological processes. Accumulating evidence indicates that light information regulates biological rhythms, neuroendocrine functions, and behavioral responses across species (Grunst and Grunst, 2023; Muscogiuri et al., 2022). In poultry production, light management is a key determinant that influences performance, immune function, and metabolic homeostasis (Jiang et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2022). While previous research has primarily focused on photoperiod effects mediated through the pineal-HPA/HPG axes (Bao et al., 2023; Ouyang et al., 2021), the molecular mechanisms through which specific wavelengths affect the muscle phenotype via the endocrine-thyroid axis remain largely unexplored.

Our findings establish a novel signaling pathway through which monochromatic light combinations regulate skeletal muscle fiber-type transformation in broilers. The central role of thyroid hormone (TH) in mediating photic signals was unequivocally demonstrated by thyroidectomy (TX) experiments. The elimination of intergroup differences in fiber-type composition following TX confirmed TH as an indispensable mediator. This finding extends previous observations of TH involvement in photoperiodic responses and reveals its essential function in transducing signals from specific light wavelengths (Bradshaw and Schemske, 2003; Liu et al., 2024). Specifically, we found that the green-blue (GB) light combination markedly suppressed thyroid secretion, driving a fiber-type shift toward oxidative profiles (MyHC-I/IIa) concurrent with the upregulation of myogenic factors Pax7 and Myf5. In contrast, the red-red (RR) light combination induced a hyperthyroid state, which led to a significant increase in the proportion of fast-glycolytic fibers (MyHC-IIb), paralleled by the upregulation of the differentiation factor MyoD, a known TH target gene, and myostatin. These results indicate a previously unappreciated, wavelength-specific regulation of the endocrine-thyroid axis.

At the molecular level, we identified the reciprocal regulation of deiodinases DIO2 and DIO3 as a critical mechanism for fine-tuning local T3 bioavailability (Cheng et al., 2010). The GB-induced upregulation of DIO3, concurrent with DIO2 downregulation, created a low-T3 microenvironment that facilitated oxidative fiber specification. This fiber-type shift aligns with the established role of DIO2 in mediating the metabolic reprogramming necessary for fast-glycolytic transition (Sagliocchi et al., 2019). Furthermore, we established DIO3 as a downstream effector of the Wnt/β-catenin pathway in skeletal muscle, corroborating its function as a direct Wnt target in other tissues such as the intestinal epithelium (Luongo et al., 2019). Thus, the coordinated regulation of these selenoenzymes represents an elegant mechanism for the spatiotemporal control of thyroid hormone action (Kohrle and Fradrich, 2022). Our data further indicate that light colors modulate the PI3K/AKT-Wnt/β-catenin signaling axis. GB treatment significantly enhanced the phosphorylation of PI3K, AKT, and GSK3β, thereby stabilizing β-catenin. This pathway appears not only to regulate DIO3 expression but also to potentially suppress DIO2 via β-catenin/FOXO3 interactions, as evidenced in other systems (Bloise et al., 2018; Dentice et al., 2010). The convergence of light signals on this evolutionarily conserved pathway provides a mechanistic framework for understanding how environmental cues reprogram muscle phenotype, consistent with the established roles of Wnt signaling in promoting slow-twitch myogenesis (Huraskin et al., 2016).

Thyroid hormone effects are contingent on nuclear receptor occupancy, which is set by intracellular T3 levels (Mendoza and Hollenberg, 2017). Our results demonstrate that the predominance of TRα in broiler skeletal muscle, along with its synchronous variation with serum TH levels, underscore its central role in light-mediated fiber-type regulation. The GB-induced reduction in TRα signaling likely alleviates repression of slow-fiber genes and modulates calcineurin activity, collectively promoting oxidative metabolism. This finding is consistent with research identifying T3 as a primary determinant of muscle phenotype through TR-mediated regulation of MHC isoforms (Visser et al., 2009). Furthermore, our study suggests that light colors fine-tune this regulatory network through TH-dependent adjustments of TRα expression.

Thyroid hormone (TH) is a critical regulator of energy metabolism. Although hyperthyroidism increases food intake, it elevates basal metabolic rate and resting energy expenditure, thereby promoting weight loss while enhancing lipolysis and gluconeogenesis (Cicatiello et al., 2018). This TH-induced reduction in body weight partially stems from its impact on metabolic efficiency (Brian, 2008). Specifically, TH concurrently activates opposing metabolic pathways—including lipogenesis/lipolysis and protein synthesis/degradation—thereby establishing energy-dissipating futile cycles (Yehuda-Shnaidman et al., 2014). Moreover, adaptive thermogenesis mediated by DIO2 in brown adipose tissue represents a paradigmatic manifestation of TH-driven energy dissipation (Bianco and McAninch, 2013). In our study, the functional benefits of GB illumination were reflected in improved metabolic efficiency and enhanced redox homeostasis. Broilers exposed to GB light exhibited a significant increase in antioxidant capacity—as indicated by elevated activities of CAT, GPx, and T-AOC—along with a marked reduction in lipid peroxidation, evidenced by decreased MDA levels. These findings substantiate the well-established inverse relationship between oxidative stress and slow-twitch fiber proportion (Lu et al., 2025). The improved redox status, coupled with reduced energy expenditure associated with lower circulating thyroid hormone levels, likely constitutes the core mechanism responsible for the meat quality improvements observed under GB illumination (Nicolaisen et al., 2020).

In conclusion, our study delineates a comprehensive pathway through which monochromatic light combinations regulate muscle fiber-type plasticity. The process originates with light wavelength perception, which modulates systemic thyroid hormone levels. Subsequently, this modulation activates the PI3K/AKT-Wnt/β-catenin pathway, leading to the induction of DIO3 and suppression of DIO2. This resulting decrease in intracellular T3 availability attenuates TRα signaling, ultimately activating the slow/oxidative fiber gene program and driving a fiber-type transition toward oxidative metabolism.

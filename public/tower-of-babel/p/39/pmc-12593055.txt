Vine Tea Combined with a Lidocaine Microneedle Patch for Enhanced Treatment of Psoriasis
Authors: Ziyan Chen, Ying Wang, Ye Kai, Xuanjie Huang, Mingyi Tan, Huayi Wu, Wangqing Chen, Wu Zhu, XiaoWei Liang, Peipei Wang, Chao Liu, Wei Zheng, Zeyu Chen, Xiang Chen, Shuang Zhao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Vine tea, a traditional Chinese herbal tea with well-known medicinal properties, contains various bioactive compounds, with dihydromyricetin (DMY) standing out as particularly notable. However, the therapeutic potential of DMY is hindered by its low solubility and limited ability to penetrate cell membranes. In this study, we present a novel approach utilizing a microneedle (MN) patch loaded with DMY in combination with lidocaine (LD), referred to as the DMY-LD MN patch, to effectively target thickened skin lesions in the treatment of psoriasis. The DMY-LD MN patch demonstrated the ability to penetrate the thickened epidermal layers associated with psoriasis, enabling sustained release of DMY and LD at the site of the skin lesion. In a murine model of psoriasis, both DMY and LD showed therapeutic efficacy when administered individually. Notably, the combined DMY and LD treatment regimen exhibited superior therapeutic outcomes. The DMY-LD MN patch offers a promising treatment strategy for psoriasis due to its durability, efficacy, cost-effectiveness, and safety.

Introduction

Psoriasis, an immune-mediated inflammatory skin disease, affects a global population of approximately 125 million individuals. Psoriasis vulgaris, the predominant form of psoriasis, accounts for 80–90% of cases and typically presents as erythematous or scaly plaques accompanied by varying degrees of pruritus and discomfort. , This condition can affect various areas of the body, such as the skin, finger (toe) nails, and joints, significantly impacting patients’ quality of life. The pathogenesis of psoriasis is intricate and not yet fully understood, with genetic, immunological, infectious, and neuropsychiatric factors all playing a role. Key to the development of psoriasis is the overactivation of components of the adaptive immune system. Multiple cell types, including keratinocytes and dendritic cells, are involved in psoriasis, with the interleukin (IL)-17/23 axis playing a central role, particularly in psoriasis vulgaris. , Additionally, the peripheral nervous system is actively implicated in the pathogenesis of psoriasis. − Topical administration of medications for psoriatic lesions is advantageous in enhancing therapeutic efficacy and minimizing systemic adverse reactions. , However, the excessive proliferation, keratinization, and scaling of psoriatic lesions further restrict transdermal drug absorption. Dermatologists are increasingly focusing their attention on transdermal drug delivery systems (TDDSs) to tackle this issue. − A promising approach involves the use of MNs for painless and localized delivery of drugs through the skin, which has garnered significant interest recently. − Microneedle (MN) provide therapeutic benefits in a painless and minimally invasive manner by delivering medication into the dermis, overcoming the skin barrier. − Currently available MNs contain methotrexate (MTX) embedded in a soluble maltose matrix, primarily in the form of dissolving MNs, with an overall transdermal delivery efficacy of 90%. Jeong et al. developed dissolving MNs containing cyclosporin A (CyA) for localized delivery into the dermal layer, but did not conduct in vivo experiments to confirm efficacy in psoriasis treatment. Korkmaz et al. used biologics with anti-TNF-α antibodies to effectively suppress psoriasis-like inflammation and epidermal hyperproliferation in mice.

Although MN technology enhances drug permeability and helps maintain effective drug concentrations, western medicines are associated with strong skin irritation and a higher risk of allergic reactions. , For instance, methotrexate, although widely recognized as a first-line systemic therapy for moderate-to-severe psoriasis due to its clinical efficacy, is nevertheless associated with toxicity and a high incidence of adverse effects, including hepatotoxicity, nausea, vomiting, leukopenia, and suppression of bone marrow function, which limit its long-term use. , CyA, is characterized by a narrow therapeutic index, wherein minor deviations above the optimal dosage can result in significant toxicity, whereas subtherapeutic dosing compromises clinical efficacy. In addition, its administration is frequently accompanied by adverse effects associated with systemic immunosuppression. In contrast, topical formulations derived from Chinese herbal medicine (CHM) typically consist of natural, plant-based compounds with multitarget therapeutic actions, including anti-inflammatory, antioxidative, and tissue-repair properties. Owing to their lower systemic toxicity and reduced potential for irritation or sensitization, CHM-based topicals are generally considered safer for long-term application, particularly in the management of chronic or recurrent dermatological conditions. , Vine Tea, commonly known as ″Teng Cha″ in Chinese, is a type of tea derived from the leaves of the vine plant Fallopia multiflora, a species of ampelopsis grossedentata characterized by its distinctive serrated leaves. This plant is indigenous to the mountainous regions of southern China. Dihydromyricetin (DMY), extracted from Vine Tea, has long been employed in traditional CHM as a health-promoting tea and herbal remedy. Recent studies have uncovered a wealth of flavonoid compounds present in vine tea, with DMY identified as the primary monomeric active component among these flavonoids. Extensive research efforts have focused on elucidating the biological and pharmacological properties of DMY, revealing its antioxidative, antibacterial, anti-inflammatory, anticancer, antiobesity, and neuroprotective effects. Furthermore, studies have underscored the therapeutic potential of DMY in the management of various chronic illnesses such as cancer, diabetes, obesity, alcohol use disorders, and metabolic or neurological disorders. , DMY may alleviate psoriasis-related immune-inflammatory responses by inhibiting the TLR4/NF-κB pathway, reducing macrophage polarization to the proinflammatory M1 phenotype, and regulating Th1 and Th17 differentiation, potentially through modulation of the RORγt pathway in T cells. , Despite its promising therapeutic properties, DMY is classified under the biopharmaceutics classification system (BCS) as a class IV compound due to its limited solubility and cell membrane permeability. As such, efforts are underway to enhance its penetration efficiency for optimal therapeutic efficacy.

To overcome the poor bioavailability of DMY, a dissolving MNs patch containing DMY was developed for enhanced transdermal delivery, with a focus on evaluating its efficacy in the treatment of psoriasis. Additionally, lidocaine (LD) was incorporated to augment anti-inflammatory properties and alleviate symptoms of skin itching. LD exerts its primary pharmacological effect through the inhibition of voltage-gated sodium channels. Previous studies have reported cases of unilateral complete remission of chronic plaque psoriasis following accidental nerve injury. Additionally, spinal injection of lidocaine during surgery has been shown to improve psoriatic plaques on the skin surface. This blockade reduces the release of neuropeptides such as calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP), from peripheral sensory nerve terminals, thereby mitigating neurogenic inflammation. Furthermore, LD has been demonstrated to suppress the secretion of key proinflammatory cytokines, including interleukin-1β (IL-1β), interleukin-6 (IL-6), and tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α). As an immune-mediated skin disorder, psoriasis involves the active engagement of the peripheral sensory nervous system in its pathogenesis. Patients with psoriasis exhibit elevated plasma levels of the neuropeptide CGRP, along with increased expression of CGRP receptors in lesional skin, compared to healthy individuals. LD is a common local anesthetic utilized for pain management, demonstrating comparable efficacy to anti-TNF-α therapy in psoriasis patients. , Epidural injections of LD have demonstrated efficacy in managing psoriasis; however, it is important to note that the procedure carries inherent risks. Hyaluronic acid (HA), as a main component of the extracellular matrix of the skin, was approved by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for filling soft tissue defects and has been broadly used in dermatology and orthopedics with favorable water-binding ability, biodegradability, and great biocompatibility. , HA-based MNs have emerged as a promising method for delivering drugs to subcutaneous tissues, significantly enhancing therapeutic efficacy. Therefore, in this study, HA was selected as the matrix material to prepare DMY-LD MN patch. These features indicate that our dissolvable MN patch provide a promising technology for high-efficient, facile, and safe biologics administration, offering new therapeutic options for psoriasis and other inflammatory diseases (Figure ).

Materials and Methods

Chemicals

HA (10 kDa) was purchased from Meilun Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Dalian, China). Methylene blue (MB, 319 Da) was purchased from Shanghai Macklin Biochemical Co., Ltd., (Shanghai, China). LD was obtained from Xiangya Hospital, Central South University. DMY was a gift from Hunan Qiankun Biotechnology Co., Ltd., (Hunan, China). Imiquimod (IMQ) cream (#19080639) containing 5% imiquimod was obtained from Sichuan Mingxin Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd. (Sichuan, China).

Cell Culture

The human immortalized keratinocyte (HaCaT) and mouse embryonic cells (NIH 3T3) were obtained from Hunan Engineering Research Center of Skin Health and Disease (Changsha, China). HaCaT cell lines were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium (Gibco, C11875500BT) enriched with 10% (v/v) fetal bovine serum (FBS, CellMax, SA101) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin solution (BI, 03–031–1B). NIH 3T3 cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM, Gibco, C11995500BT) complemented with the same concentration of FBS and antibiotics. Cells were incubated in an incubator at 37 °C and under 5% CO2. When confluence reached approximately 80%, the cells were subcultured using trypsinization.

Animals

Female BALB/c mice (7–8 weeks, 18–22g) were obtained from Hunan SJA Laboratory Animal Co., Ltd. and kept under specific pathogen-free (SPF) conditions in the animal facility at Central South University. Mice were kept in individual ventilated cages with a 12-h light-dark cycle set with lights, with the room temperature kept around 20–26 °C, and humidity between 40% and 70% (not controlled). Mice were prehoused in an SPF environment for 1 week, with regular cage changes, standard laboratory chow, and sterile drinking water. Daily observations were made of the mice’s appearance, behavior, diet, and defecation. Mice were anesthetized by inhalation of isoflurane during the experiment (induction: 4–5%; maintenance: 1.5%). Anesthesia depth was confirmed by the loss of the righting reflex and the absence of response to toe pinch. All experimental procedures were conducted with approval from the Animal Care and Use Committee at Central South University (CSU-2024-0069), in accordance with the National Institutes of Health Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals.

Fabrication of the MN Patch

The detachable MNs with DMY-LD and MB were fabricated by a two-step micromolding method. The MN mold was initially created by digital light processing (DLP) 3D-printing technology MN mold (10 × 10 array, 350 μm base diameter, 800 μm pitch, and 1000 μm height) as male mold. The 3D-printed male mold was fabricated using high-precision 3D-printing technology based on DLP. Initially, a computer-aided design (CAD) model was created using SolidWorks 2020 (Dassault Systèmes, USA). The STL file was subsequently processed using BMF_3Dslice1 software for slicing. The resulting sliced file was then imported into a high-precision 3D printer (S140, Boston Micro Fabrication Material Technology Inc., Shenzhen, China) for fabrication. We chose a nonconductive resin (yellow-5, elastic modulus of 3.8 GPa, hardness of 86 shore D) for use in the MN mold. Following printing, the model was immersed in ethanol at room temperature and subjected to ultrasonic cleaning for 5 min to eliminate residual resin. Finally, the printed MN mold was exposed to UV light for an additional 5 min to enhance postcuring. Then, we used polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) as female mold. To achieve this, the base solution (Sylgard 184 silicone elastomer kit) was thoroughly mixed with the curing agent in a 10:1 ratio and poured over the 3D-printed MN male mold. Air bubbles were removed under a vacuum, and the mold was then cured at 80 °C for 2 h, resulting in the formation of the PDMS mold.

Various concentrations of DMY and LD (DMY: LD= 25:1) were premixed with a 10% (w/v) HA aqueous solution to formulate the MN tips. This blend was then dispensed onto a PDMS mold and subjected to a 30 min vacuum (−0.08 MPa) to eliminate air bubbles. Any excess liquid was removed, and the polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP) solution (0.3 g/mL) was introduced to create the MN substrates (Figure a). The complete MN patch was then vacuum-dried at 50 °C for 4 h. The MN patches were meticulously detached from the mold and stored in desiccant-packed centrifuge tubes for preservation. Control HA MNs and those mixed with MB were fabricated following the same procedure. All MN patches were securely sealed within a desiccator and shielded from light prior to utilization. Finally, the MN patches were peeled from the PDMS mold gently.

Characterizations of MN Patches

The MN was affixed to the base, its tip oriented upward, and positioned centrally on the carrier table of a universal material tester. A vertical force was then applied, setting the metal probe’s advancement rate to 0.2 mm/s and the maximum deformation to 800 μm. This procedure facilitated the acquisition of curves that reflect the MN’s deformation in response to the applied load.

In Vitro Skin Penetration of the MN Patch

Porcine skin was utilized, and the MB-loaded MN patches were applied. The diffusion of MB was observed at predetermined time intervals. MB-loaded MNs were inserted into porcine skin with a consistent application of force. The MNs were then removed and photograph specific intervals. Following MN puncture, the skin was excised, fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, and then embedded in paraffin for subsequent sectioning. The sections were then examined under the microscope after being stained with Hematoxylin and Eosin (H&E).

In Vitro Drug Release of the MN Patch

The drug delivery performance of DMY-LD MN patch was further investigated to demonstrate its potential application in the topical therapy. Phosphate buffered saline (PBS) was chosen as the medium solution for testing the drug release kinetics. , The DMY-LD MN patch was measured tip-side down into a shaking water bath at 37 °C. The sample suspension (1.0 mL each) was taken out at predetermined time points (0, 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 30, 60, 90, 120, 180, and 240 min), and 1.0 mL of fresh PBS solution was added to ensure that the volume remained constant. The MN patches containing varying amounts of drugs were immersed in PBS until the tip parts were completely dissolved. The absorbance of this solution was measured by high-performance liquid chromatography analysis (HPLC) (Shimadzu. Japan) to determine the amount of drug (DMY and LD). Chromatographic analysis was performed using Agela Venusil XBP C18 column (5 μm, 4.6 mm × 250 mm). The mobile phase consisted of n-hexane containing 0.1% trifluoroacetic acid (v/v) and ethanol in a ratio of 80:20 (v/v). The flow rate was set at 1 mL/min, with an injection volume of 10 μL. The column temperature was maintained at 25 °C. The detection wavelength for DMY is 290 nm, while for LD it is 205 nm. The drug concentration and content were then calculated based on standard curves. The in vitro diffusion experiment was conducted following the standard protocol reported in previous studies, where no control groups were included as the primary aim was to assess release kinetics.

Biocompatibility of the MN Patch

Cell viability was assessed using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8, Selleck, B34302) assay. HaCaT cells (1 × 10̂4 cells/well) and NIH 3T3 cells (8 × 10̂3 cells/well) were seeded in 96-well plates and incubated overnight. Upon reaching 70%–80% confluence, the culture medium was replaced, and the cells were treated with specified drug concentrations for 24 h. The assay was conducted in accordance with the CCK-8 instructions to evaluate cell survival (%). The absorbance (Optical Density, OD value) for each well was measured at a wavelength of 450 nm using a microplate reader.

We then conducted the acute skin irritation assay. Mice had their dorsal fur removed, and then MN patches were applied to the depilated area for 10 min. The recovery of the puncture sites was monitored at predetermined time intervals.

Establishment of an Imiquimod-Induced Psoriasis Mouse Model and in Vivo Treatment Effects of the DMY-LD MN Patch

Mice were prehoused under specific pathogen-free (SPF) conditions for 1 week. Three days prior to the experiment, the dorsal hair was removed by shaving followed by application of a depilatory cream. On the day before model induction, the smoothness of the dorsal skin was confirmed, ensuring the absence of visible hair regrowth. Baseline body weights were recorded, and the mice were sequentially numbered. Random group assignment was then performed using the RANDBETWEEN­() function in Microsoft Excel to ensure unbiased allocation. The mice were randomly divided into the following six groups (n = 4): (i) blank group; (ii) model group; (iii) HA MN group; (iv) LD MN group (LD 10 mg/mL); (v) DMY MN group (DMY 2.5 mg/mL); (vi) DMY-LD MN group (DMY 2.5 mg/mL; LD 10 mg/mL). The control group received no treatment, whereas the model groups were administered a daily topical dose of 5% IMQ cream (15 mg) on the dorsal skin for six consecutive days. Drug-loaded MN patches were applied topically 4 h after the IMQ cream treatment. The severity of skin inflammation was assessed using an objective scoring system derived from the clinical psoriasis lesion area and severity index (PASI). Body weights were also recorded on a daily basis. On day 6, dorsal skin lesions from all groups were photographed before being excised for further analysis. Each tissue sample was bisected; one-half was fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde at room temperature, while the other half was promptly preserved in liquid nitrogen and subsequently stored in a −80 °C freezer for long-term storage.

H&E Staining

Tissues were processed through standard paraffin embedding and subsequently sectioned. The sections were then baked at 60 °C for 1–2 h, deparaffinized in xylene, and gradually hydrated using a series of ethanol solutions. Following H&E staining, the samples were examined under a microscope. The epidermal thickness was quantified using Image Pro Plus 6.0 software.

Statistical Analysis

All experiments were validated through three independent replicates, with data presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Differences between two groups were assessed using an unpaired Student’s t test, whereas comparisons among multiple groups employed a one-way ANOVA. A p-value less than 0.05 was considered to denote statistical significance. All statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism version 9.5.1 (GraphPad Software, Inc., La Jolla, CA).

Results and Discussion

Mechanical Properties and Drug Loading of the DMY-LD MN Patch

The detachable DMY-LD MN patch with its needles composed of DMY and LD solution and their supporting arrays made from PVP (Figure a). Each MN patch had an array of 10 × 10 needles with a distance of 800 μm between the centers of two adjacent needle bases. (Figure b,c). The morphology of MN was further verified by SEM images (Figure d).

Sufficient mechanical strength is crucial for the ability of MN patches to penetrate the stratum corneum (SC). To evaluate the ability of the MN patch to puncture the skin, the mechanical properties of the MN patch were implemented by a force sensor (customized) (Figure e). The images of the MNs before and after compression revealed that the tips of the MN patch were bent under stress (Figure f). Each MN exhibited a failure force of 0.42 N/needle (Figure g). As the minimum required force for a MN to penetrate the SC is 0.058 N/needle, our MNs demonstrated sufficient mechanical strength to penetrate the SC layer effectively. Furthermore, we conducted an in vitro skin puncture test to determine whether the MN patch can achieve effective transdermal drug delivery. MB was loaded into the MNs as a model drug, due to its good dyeability and water solubility (Figure a). The MN patches loaded with MB were then inserted into fresh porcine skin and removed after 5 and 10 min respectively (Figure b). After inserting into the skin for 10 min, the complete separation between the MNs and the base was achieved and the loaded drug was completely dissolved. Histological assessments revealed that the microneedles penetrated to a depth of approximately 300 μm, successfully breaching the skin barrier. In addition, they were capable of penetrating the thickened epidermis associated with hyperproliferative psoriatic skin (Figure c).

In Vivo Skin Insertion Experiments of the DMY-LD MN Patch

We then mixed different ratios of DMY/LD and HA solutions to fabricate DMY-LD MN patches and conducted the insertion experiments on mice. First, we applied the DMY-LD MN patch to the ears of mice and removed it after 10 min. The results demonstrated that the MN patch can adhere the ears of mice effectively (Figure d). Furthermore, upon removal of the patch, the needles completely detached from the base. This indicates that the DMY-LD MN had fully dissolved within the skin (Figure e,f). Except for mild erythema, no additional adverse effects were observed on the mouse ear. Next, we applied the DMY-LD MN patch to the back skin of the mice to verify the safety and skin damage issues. After pressing the DMY-LD MN patch for 2 min, no bleeding or redness was observed in the insertion area. The micro holes in the skin created by MNs became invisible within 60 min, which demonstrated the minimally invasive of DMY-LD MN patch. Therefore, the DMY-LD MN patch had the potential for gentle, efficient, and rapid drug release (Figure g).

In Vitro Release of the DMY-LD MN Patch

As illustrated in Figure a, DMY-LD MN patches were dissolved in PBS solution with pH = 7.4 at ambient temperatures. We detected DMY and LD concentrations using HPLC. The detection wavelength for DMY is 290 nm, while for LD it is 205 nm. The relationship equation between DMY concentration and absorbance is Y = 19026X – 17266, and the correlation coefficient R 2 = 0.9994 within the concentration range of 0 to 2.5 mg/mL. The relationship equation between LD concentration and absorbance is Y = 110,908X – 9169.2, and the correlation coefficient R 2 = 0.9997, which proves that there is a good linear relationship between LD concentration and absorbance within the concentration range of 0 to 10 mg/mL. Figure b,c shows the DMY and LD release curves of DMY-LD MN patch. The cumulative release amount of DMY reached approximately 60% in the first 60 min, and the release amount of LD approached 85%. This phenomenon may be attributed to the physical states of the compounds; DMY, being in powder form, dissolves more slowly compared to LD, which is in liquid form and thus dissolves more rapidly. After three to 4 h, the release of DMY-LD MN patch reaches its peak value. Based on the results, it is estimated that the DMY-LD MN patch would need to be applied 3–4 times per day in future clinical use to achieve optimal therapeutic efficacy.

In Vitro Cell Experiments

The excessive proliferation of keratinocytes is a defining pathological characteristic of psoriasis. We modulated the drug concentrations in the medium within a 24 h time frame and subsequently assessed the impact of individual or combined drugs on the proliferative capacity of HaCaT cells using the CCK-8 assay. The dose-dependent antiproliferative effect of DMY was observed, with a notable inhibition of HaCaT cell proliferation (p < 0.05) at a concentration of 100 μg/mL (Figure b). This inhibitory effect was further accentuated with increasing doses of DMY. Similarly, elevating LD concentration to 500 μg/mL significantly suppressed proliferation (p < 0.001), with 50% activity observed at 1000 μg/mL. Notably, the combination of DMY and LD exhibited pronounced inhibitory effects at lower doses. These findings were corroborated in NIH 3T3 cells, yielding similar results (Supporting Information Figure S1). Based on the aforementioned rationale, we postulated that the synergistic effects of DMY in conjunction with LD are anticipated to exhibit a favorable in vivo therapeutic outcome.

In Vivo Therapeutic Effects of the DMY-LD MN Patch

To assess in vivo therapeutic effects of the DMY-LD MN patch, an experimental model was established by the administration of IMQ, a toll-like receptor 7 agonist on the shaved dorsal skin of mice to induce psoriasis-like dermatitis. The treatment protocol is illustrated in Figure a. The DMY-LD MN patch was applied to the dorsal skin of the mice, corresponding to the same area treated with IMQ. Figure b showed representative images of the dorsal skin from different treatment groups. After 6 days of consecutive IMQ administration, the dorsal skin of mice presents as well-demarcated, thickened, erythematous scaly plaques on the skin surface in the model group, indicating that IMQ successfully induced psoriasis-like dermatitis. The skin lesions in HA group tended to be as severe as those in the model group, and the level of inflammation was decreased after treatment with DMY or DMY-LD MN patch. The PASI scores and the corresponding heatmaps were measured (Figure b,c).

The group that utilized the DMY-LD MN patch achieved the lowest PASI score (2.00 ± 1.41) on day six, which was significantly lower compared to both the IMQ group and the HA group (9.00 ± 0.82, 8.75 ± 0.5, p < 0.001, respectively) as illustrated in Figure c and Supporting Information Figure S2. This suggests that the topical administration of the DMY-LD MN patch could effectively delay the onset of the IMQ-induced psoriasis model in mice. While the use of the LD MN patch or DMY MN patch alone also led to a reduction in PASI scores to some extent, the overall efficacy was not as pronounced as that observed with DMY-LD MN patch (Figure c).

The primary pathological features of psoriasis typically include abnormal thickening of the epidermis, pronounced acanthosis, and increased infiltration of inflammatory cells. The results of H&E staining of tissue sections from the back of mice revealed notable epidermal thickening and acanthosis in the group induced with IMQ, while insignificant differences were observed in the group treated solely with HA group compared to the IMQ group. Application of LD or DMY individually partially ameliorated these pathological conditions, however, the most significant improvement was observed in the DMY-LD MN group (Figure d). Quantitative analysis of epidermal thickness supported these findings (Figure e). No notable changes in the body weight of the mice were detected across all experimental groups, and histological analysis of sections from major organs indicated that local application of DMY-LD MN did not induce any damage to these tissues (Figure f and Supporting Information Figure S3).

Conclusions

Vine tea, a type of Chinese tea, contains DMY, its most effective component, which exhibits anti-inflammatory properties. In this study, a transdermal patch incorporating DMY-LD MN was introduced for the treatment of psoriasis. The formulation includes dissolvable MNs loaded with DMY to improve its bioavailability, along with LD to enhance the therapeutic effects on psoriasis. These DMY-LD MN patches are considered as potentially cost-effective and promising treatment options for psoriasis.

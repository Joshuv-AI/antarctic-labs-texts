Percutaneous thermal ablation-based locoregional treatment for hepatocellular carcinoma larger than 3 cm: a 10-year single-center retrospective study
Authors: Wen Luo, Li-Na Pang, Lu Han, Xiao-Bin Li, Xiao-Yu Li, Hai-Jing Liu, Xiao Yang, Lei Ding, Pei-Di Zhang, Jin Liu, Guang-Bin He, Xiao-Dong Zhou, Ming Yu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesTo investigate the long-term efficacy of percutaneous thermal ablation (PTA)-based locoregional treatment of patients with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) bearing tumors > 3 cm.MethodsIn this single-center, retrospective study of 359 HCC patients with 587 tumors (5.1 ± 0.021 cm) treated with PTA-based locoregional treatment between 2011 and 2021 was performed. Radiological response was evaluated per lesion on enhanced computed tomography imaging one month after ablation. Local tumor progression (LTP), regional recurrence (RR), disease-free survival (DFS) and overall survival (OS) rates were assessed per patient in follow-up, while a Cox hazards regression analysis was used to determine significant factors associated with OS and DFS. Therapeutic outcomes were assessed in a subgroup analysis of tumor size, number, and the patients’ age after propensity score matching.ResultsThe mean ± SD follow up duration was 30.0 ± 22.1 months. Complete Response was achieved in 87.7% of lesions after treatment. LTP and RR were detected in 52.5% and 66.9% of patients, respectively. The 1-, 3-, and 5-year DFS rates were 43.8%, 23.8%, and 14.6%, respectively. The 1-, 3-, 5-, 7-, and 10-year OS rates were 81.3%, 47.0%, 20.7%, 16.7%, and 14.4%, respectively. According to the multivariate analysis, tumor number, cirrhosis and platelet were significant factors for OS (P < 0.05); while tumor number and size were associated with DFS. In the subgroup analysis, the cumulative OS rates at 1, 3, and 5 years were 80.1%, 39.5%, and 27.1% in the medium tumor group(3 cm ≤ diameter < 5 cm) and 66.7%, 37.1%, and 7.3%, respectively, in the large tumor group (diameter ≥ 5 cm, HR = 1.44, 95% CI: 1.0–2.1, P < 0.05). The cumulative RR rates at 1, 3, and 5 years were 62.8%, 84%, and 89.3%, respectively, in the multiple-lesion group and 45.0%, 66.9%, and 76.6%, respectively, in the single-lesion group (HR = 0.63, 95% CI: 0.46–0.86, P < 0.05). The 1-, 3-, and 5-year OS rates were 71.5%, 34.9%, and 16.6% in patients aged ≤ 50 years and 83%, 50.4%, and 30.2%, respectively, in patients aged > 50 years (P < 0.05).ConclusionsPTA-based locoregional therapy represents an alternative method for the treatment of HCC patients with medium/large tumors.Keywords: Hepatocellular carcinoma, Ablation techniques, Transarterial chemoembolization, Overall survival, Local recurrence

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is the leading cause of death in patients with cirrhosis and represents a major public health problem in the Asia–Pacific region. Approximately 75% of all liver cancers occur in Asia, with China accounting for over 50% of the global burden [1, 2]. The anticipated therapeutic effects after curative treatment have been achieved for early-stage HCC, according to Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer (BCLC) classification [3]. Unfortunately, in China, about 50% of HCC patients presenting with large HCCs, have intermediate or advanced stage disease [4], which is more challenging to treat.

The priority of combination therapy regimens for hepatocellular carcinoma must be comprehensively determined based on the patient's tumor stage, liver function reserve, performance status, and comorbidities, following the hierarchical decision-making principles in the Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Treatment of Primary Liver Cancer. Surgical resection is the preferred treatment. While, locoregional therapy has been used to treat large lesions, which are not fit for surgical resection or have failure to respond to chemotherapy, to control tumor progression and prolong patient survival. Transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) is one of locoregional therapies, which was established as an important treatment option for patients with intermediate-stage HCC [5]. However, residual vascularity within tumors is still observed in some cases after TACE due to unembolized feeding arteries, which leads to tumor recurrence [6, 7].

Thermal ablation, including radiofrequency ablation (RFA) and microwave ablation (MWA), also works as locoregional therapy by coagulating tissue via hyperthermia induction, which has been applied for eliminating residual blood supply in the tumors after TACE. In recent literature, the combined use of TACE and tumor ablation techniques was shown to be more effective in the treatment of HCC tumors of intermediate to large size than TACE alone [8, 9]. In addition, single ablation has been used as a potential palliative therapy for TACE-intolerant HCC patients with tumors exceeding 3 cm in size [10–12]. The introduction of new devices for ablation has allowed for the application of large volume of necrosis and increasing the sphericity of the necrosis area. Therefore, percutaneous thermal ablation(PTA) based locoregional therapies, including TACE combined with ablation or ablation alone, provide options in the treatment of medium to large HCCs. However, the benefit of using PTA-based localized treatment for medium to large HCCs needs further investigation. The analysis of long-term survival and risk factors would be helpful for improving patient selection and management of therapies.

Since 2006, the localized ablation of HCC tumors has been increasingly implemented in our institution. In the past few years, PTA-based therapies (TACE combined with ablation techniques or ablation alone) have been used in patients with unresectable HCC, according to the international and Chinese consensus on the treatment of HCC [13, 14]. In the present study, we retrospectively evaluated data from 359 HCC patients with tumors larger than 3 cm, over a 10-year period. Our aim was to assess the long-term outcome and risk factors of using PTA-based locoregional therapy for large HCCs.

Methods

Patients

The Institutional Review Board of Xijing Hospital approved this retrospective study and waived the requirement for informed consent. Between January 2007 and December 2016, 1,823 patients with HCCs underwent percutaneous thermal ablation (PTA)-based locoregional treatment (including RFA, MWA, or local ablation combined with TACE), in our institution (Department of Ultrasound, Xijing Hospital). Of these patients, 359 were included in our study, according to the following inclusion cretiaria:1) lesions were confirmed as HCCs based on non-invasive imaging and/or pathological results, according to Guidelines for Diagnosis and Treatment of Primary Liver Cancer in China (2019 Edition) [2]; 2) the patient had less than or equal to three HCC lesions; 3) at least one of the HCC lesions was ≥ 3 cm in diameter, measured by ultrasound (US); in case of multiple tumors, the diameter of the largest one was measured; 4) the patient was followed up within 1 month of treatment and assessed using an enhanced contrast (CE) US, every 3 months in the first 6-month period, and every 6 months in the subsequent period with enhanced computational tomography (CT), until the end-point event occurred (either death or completion of the study period [December 2016]). Interventional procedure

Local ablation

All ablations were performed under local anesthesia (1% lidocaine) and conscious analgesic sedation via the intravenous injection of midazolam (Enhua Pharmaceuticals, Jiangsu, China) or dexmedetomidine (Hengrui Pharmaceuticals, Jiangsu, China), and remifentanil (Yichang Humanwell Pharmaceuticals, Hubei, China).

The procedure was guided by a Sequioa 512 ultrasound system with a 4V1 probe (Simens, Germany) or an EPIQ 7 ultrasound system with a C5-1 probe (Philips, USA). RFA was performed using commercially available a cooled electrode systems with generator (Cooltip RF System, Covidien, Mansfield, MA, USA), using a single electrode with a 2 or 3 cm active tip. MWA ablation was performed using either of two systems: a 915 MHz generator or a 2450 MHz generator with a 14-gauge surgical antenna (Kangyou, China). Only a single generator was employed for each patient with the method of overlapping ablation per session. The ablation procedures were performed by two radiologists (G.B.H and X.D.Z), each with over 5 years’ experience of percutaneous liver tumor ablation.

Ablation combined with TACE

TACE was performed 2–4 weeks before thermal ablation to reduce local vascularity. During TACE, the right femoral artery was cannulated using a 5 F vascular sheath (Radifocus introducer II, Terumo Corporation, Japan) and Seldinger’s technique, under sterile conditions and with local anesthesia (2% lidocaine). Selective hepatic artery angiography was performed by inserting a 5 F hepatic artery catheter (Radifocus angiographic catheter, Terumo) through the vascular sheath, so as to determine the tumor location, size, number, and blood supply. A dose of 1000 mg 5-fluorouracil, 20 mg mitomycin, and 40 mg pirarubicin were infused sequentially as the chemotherapeutic agent. A dose of 10–20 mL lipiodol (Guerbet Corporation, France) was administered to the feeding artery of tumors via super-selective catheterization, which was then used for embolization in each procedure, depending on the tumor size, the patient’s weight, and their laboratory test results. A gelatin sponge, which served as a supplement, was injected if stasis was not achieved.

Assessment of treatment response

The tumor response per lesion was evaluated within 1 month of treatment with a CEUS or an enhanced CT. Follow-up enhanced CT images were also obtained 3 and 6 months after treatment, and then every 6 months until death of the end of the study period.

At 1 month after treatment, a Complete Response to treatment was defined as no perfusion observed within the tumors by CEUS or enhanced CT. Incomplete ablation was defined as arterial phase hyperenhancement within the targeted areas of the tumor, which appeared as a washout on CEUS or enhanced CT scans in the portal phase or later phase. Local tumor progression (LTP) refers to reappearing viable tumor provided that at least one contrast-enhanced follow-up study did not re-veal residual viable tumor at the ablative margin. Regional recurrence (RR) was defined as the development of new tumors within the liver not adjacent to the ablation zone on enhanced CT images.

In cases in which recurrent tumor was identified during follow-up, optimal second-line treatment such as ablation, surgery, or TACE was performed using the recommendations of a multidisciplinary team specializing in HCC treatment, based on the characteristics of the recurrent tumor, hepatic function, and general condition of the patient.

Medical records of all patients were reviewed retrospectively by two radiologists (D.L and Zh.P.D). CT and US images were evaluated by two radiologists (L.W and Y.M). Overall survival (OS) was calculated from the initial ablation procedure until last recorded follow-up timepoint or death. Disease-free survival (DFS) was calculated from the initial ablation procedure until the first LTP or RR.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics, including mean values and standard deviations (SD), were recorded. Comparison of baseline and clinical variables between two groups was assessed using the t-test for continuous variables and the chi-square test for categorical variables. Cumulative incidence of LTP, RR, DFS, and OS rates were estimated by using the Kaplan–Meier method; any differences were evaluated with a stratified log-rank test. Hazards ratio (HR) and 95% confidential interval (CI) was assessed using Cox proportional hazards regression model.

The Cox proportional hazards regression model was also used to identify potential factors, following the univariate and multivariate analyses of LTP, RR, DFS, and OS data. The possible risk factors included sex, age, tumor size, tumor number, virus etiology, cirrhosis, Child–Pugh score, serum alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) levels, platelet count, hepatic function (including aspartate transferase [AST], alanine transaminase [ALT], albumin, and total bilirubin levels), splenomegaly, and ascites. Analysis was performed in subgroups of age (≤ 50 years vs > 50 years), size (diameter ≥ 5 cm vs 3 cm ≤ diameter < 5 cm) and number (multiple vs single). In the subgroup analysis, propensity scoring of 1:1 patient matching was performed. The variables included in the propensity score model were those presenting significantly different in independent t-test or chi-square test (p < 0.05) among the possible risk factors. The matched data of baseline characteristics in the groups were compared using the independent t test for continuous variables and chi-square test for categorical variables. SPSS version 26.0 software (SPSS, Inc., Chicago, IL) was used for all statistical analyses. P-values < 0.05 were used as a measure of statistical significance.

Therapeutic outcomes and risk factors

Complete Response per lesion was detected in 87.7% (515/587) of lesions after treatment. The overall recurrence rate per patient, which included LTP and RR, was 73.9% (266/359); LTP was detected in 52.5% (189/359) of the patients, and RR was detected in 66.9% (241/359) of the patients. The median LTP-free survival was 22 months (95% CI: 12.0–31.9), the median RR-free survival was 12 months (95% CI: 9.3–14.7), and the median DFS was 9 months (95% CI: 6.7–11.3). The median OS was 30 months (95% CI: 25.2–34.8). The cumulative LTP rates at 1, 3, and 5 years were 42.6%, 56.0%, and 61.8%, respectively. The 1-, 3-, and 5-year DFS rates were 43.8%, 23.8%, and 14.6%, respectively. The 1-, 3-, 5-, 7-, and 10-year OS rates were 81.3%, 47.0%, 20.7%, 16.7%, and 14.4%, respectively.

Univariate analysis revealed that tumor size, tumor number, ascites, age group (≤ 50 years or > 50 years), cirrhosis, platelet count, albumin, splenomegaly, and Child–Pugh score were significantly associated risk factors for OS (P < 0.5). In the multivariate analysis, only multiple number (HR 1.79, 95% CI: 1.21–2.66), cirrhosis and low platelet were independent prognostic factors for poor OS (P < 0.05). Univariate analysis showed that gender, ascites, Child–Pugh score, tumor number, tumor size, AFP, ALT, AST, and platelet count were significantly associated risk factors for LTP (P < 0.5). In the multivariate analysis, large size and multiple number were independent prognostic factors for poor LTP (P < 0.05). According to the univariate analysis, gender, age group, Child–Pugh score, splenomegaly, tumor number, tumor size, virus, and AST were significantly associated risk factors for DFS (P < 0.5). The multivariate analysis demonstrated that multiple number and large size were independent prognostic factors for poor DFS (P < 0.05). Discussion

Locoregional therapies, defined as imaging-guided liver tumor-directed procedures, play an important role in the management of 50%–60% of patients with HCCs [15]. Locoregional therapies, including local ablation and intra-arterial techniques, are used to inhibit tumor viability and delay cancer progression, especially in patients who do not qualify for surgery [16]. In the present study, we retrospectively investigated the long-term outcome of patients with medium or large HCC tumors who were treated by ablation-based locoregional therapy in our institution. We found that the 1-, 3-, and 5-year DFS rates of the medium/large HCC patients were 43.8%, 23.8%, and 14.6%, respectively, while the 1-, 3-, 5-, 7-, and 10-year OS rates were 81.3%, 47.0%, 20.7%, 16.7%, and 14.4%, respectively. It was found that tumor size, tumor number, age, ALT, and AST were significant risk factors for OS, while gender, tumor size, and tumor number were significant risk factors for DFS.

In subgroup analysis by using propensity score matching, prospective tumor control of cumulative LTP rates, RR rates, DFS and OS were detected in patients in medium-tumor group compared with those in large-tumor group. For patients with HCC tumors ≥ 5 cm, palliative therapy is recommended but often with residuals [17]. In the present study, the 1- and 3-year OS of HCC patients with medium tumors was similar to that of HCC patients with large tumors, after being treated with ablation alone or a combination of TACE and ablation. However, the 5-year OS of HCC patients with large tumors was obviously lower than that of patients with medium sized tumors. This finding implies that the control of tumor progression may become more difficult after 3 years, which merits further investigation. The cumulative DFS rates at 1, 3, and 5 years were lower in large tumor group than in the medium tumor group of HCC patients, which could contribute to the lower OS of the large tumor group at 5 years. In a study of 201 HCC patients with medium to large tumors, who were treated with TACE plus RFA, the 1-, 3-, and 5-year progression-free survival (PFS) rates were 43.2%, 18%, and 9.5%, respectively, and the 1-, 3-, and 5-year OS rates were 76.2%, 37.1%, and 16.4%, respectively, which are similar to the results of our study [18]. When used in combination with thermal ablation, TACE has been shown to minimize heat loss due to perfusion-mediated tissue cooling and increase the therapeutic effects of thermal ablation [19].

In a previous study, local ablation alone was also reported to be effective for use against large HCC tumors, although the outcome was not as good as after the combination of TACE and ablation. N'Kontchou et al. reported that the use of multipolar RFA in the treatment of HCC > 5 cm (n = 83) achieved 3- and 5-year OS rates of 51% (95% CI: 38–62) and 24% (95% CI: 13–36), respectively; however, much lower OS rates (25% and 6% at 3 and 5 years, respectively) were reached for the infiltrative form of HCC (HCCs invading portal vascularization) [20]. A study was to evaluate efficacy and safety of liver percutaneous microwave ablation using simultaneous activation of two antennas to treat lesions bigger than 2.5 cm. Complete Response in 43/46 (93.5%) cases and 12 months follow-up highlighted Complete Response in 40/45 (88,9%) cases [21]. In China, the use of a single electrode for overlapping ablation is more popular, partly in consideration of the associated costs. Based on the data generated in the current study, 40 of the 135 HCC patients with tumors > 5 cm received overlapping ablation alone; 50% (20/40) of these patients were over 65 years old and 30% (12/40) had tumors in the 5–6 cm range. The median OS for these 40 patients was 29 months (95% CI: 9.0–49.0), with 1-, 3-, and 5-year OS rates of 70%, 40%, and 5.4%, respectively. Thus, overlapping ablation alone represents a favorable treatment choice for HCC patients with large tumors.

In the present study, a benefit favouring effects of thermal-ablation based locoregional therapies was detected in patients > 50 years of age. After 1:1 propensity score matching, the patients aged over 50 years had longer OS rates than the patients aged ≤ 50 years. The RR rate in the old-age group of HCC patients showed a slight downward trend, compared with that in the young-age patients. Thermal ablation was preferentially used to treat the older patients with large HCC tumors, based on the objectives of the palliative therapy and the patients’ wishes. For the patients treated with ablation alone, the median OS was 37 months (95% CI: 10.3–63.7) in the OA group and 33 months (95% CI: 23.1–42.9) in the YA group. In a study of 283 patients with small HCC tumors, old age was significantly associated with poor PFS [22]. Thus, in early-stage HCC, the better physical condition of younger patients enables the use of more aggressive and therefore more effective therapies to enhance the outcome. However, our results revealed that for lesions > 3 cm, the OS was longer for the older than for the younger HCC patients. The basal metabolic rate of elderly individuals decreases, potentially leading to a reduced efficiency in tumor cells' acquisition of nutrients and energy, which in turn slows down their proliferation rate.After PSM, 58.9% (56/95) patients in OA group received thermal ablation combined with TACE, when 51.6% (49/95) in YA group (p > 0.05). Thermal ablation combined with TACE presented potential impact for the longer OS.

patients [23]. The present study revealed that the cumulative RR rates were lower in patients with a single tumor than in those with multiple tumors. In patients with multiple tumors, the elevated risk of residual tumor formation and the lengthy locoregional treatment procedures (required to treat several tumors), increase the chance of tumor recurrence and metastasis. Moreover, patients with multiple large HCC tumors typically present with heavy tumor burden and blood vessel invasion, which require more challenging treatment options and are associated with a poor prognosis. In addition, since liver damage is related to poor prognosis in HCC, we found that higher AST and ALT levels were significant risk factors of DFS.

Our study had some limitations. First, 20 patients were excluded because of incomplete follow-up data, which may introduce some bias. Second, the prognostic outcome may be related to the tumor pathological categories, vessel invasion, and tumor location, which were not discussed in this study, and require further investigation. Third, systematic chemotherapy were received in a small cohort in our institution before 2015. In this study, twenty patients accepting systematic chemotherapy in the following period were excluded. The more patients with systematic chemotherapy need further study. Finally, during the 10-year study period, scientific and technological advances have meant that new locoregional treatment options, such as hydrodissection and the use of chemotherapeutic agents in TACE, have become available to patients with HCC, which may have further biased the outcome of the study.

Conclusions

In this 10-year retrospective study, we demonstrated that ablation-based locoregional therapy was a favorable treatment option for HCC patients with medium to large tumors. Furthermore, we showed that in these patients, tumor size, tumor number, and patient age were significant risk factors of prognosis.

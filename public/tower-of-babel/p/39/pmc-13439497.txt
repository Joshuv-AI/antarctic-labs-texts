Modular in-plate synthesis of N-heterocycles for direct-to-biology screening
Authors: Xiaowei Sun, Zhantao Ye, Fusheng Guo, Xin Chu, Yu Chen, Jing Li, Tianxi Yang, Beile Zhao, Bilin Zhang, Shuai Jiang, Gang He, Tianjiao Qiao, Xiaoguang Lei, Hong Liu, Gong Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Rapid functional discovery increasingly demands synthetic methods that are compatible with automation, high-throughput experimentation, and minimal operational complexity. Miniaturized parallel synthesis in multi-well microplates provides a compelling alternative to traditional combinatorial approaches, enabling streamlined workflows and direct biological testing of crude products without purification. However, synthetic strategies suitable for such direct-to-biology applications remain largely confined to a narrow set of crosslinking reactions, limiting access to complex, three-dimensional scaffolds central to drug discovery. Herein, we report a highly efficient and broadly applicable multicomponent cyclocondensation that enables the rapid, modular construction of multisubstituted 6-membered tetrahydrotriazine scaffolds from readily available aldehydes, amines, and hydrazines through simple mixing at room temperature. The reaction proceeds cleanly under mild conditions and is fully compatible with plate-based synthesis, allowing high-purity compound libraries to be generated and evaluated directly upon solvent removal. Using this “mixing–drying–biotesting” workflow, we rapidly constructed and screened pilot 1,3,5-tri-substituted tetrahydrotriazine libraries with diverse structures, leading to the identification of potent α1A-adrenoceptor antagonists. Together, this work demonstrates how the integration of novel synthetic chemistry with direct-to-biology screening can unlock previously unexplored heterocyclic chemical space and accelerate early-stage drug discovery, while reducing the time, cost, and experimental waste.

Introduction

The pace of molecular discovery is increasingly driven by automation, high-throughput evaluation, and data-centric approaches, creating an urgent need for synthetic strategies that directly convert chemical diversity into biological functions.1–4 Conventional organic synthesis, with its reliance on purification-intensive workflows, is poorly suited to these platforms, and minimizing steps or eliminating purification altogether remains a major barrier.5–11 Miniaturized, microplate-based synthesis offers a solution, but its stringent demands for robustness and reaction cleanliness have largely confined direct-to-biology chemistry to a narrow set of crosslinking reactions, limiting access to complex, three-dimensional scaffolds central to drug discovery.12–23

Nitrogen-containing, non-planar six-membered heterocycles are ubiquitous in bioactive molecules, yet hydrotriazines, particularly 1,3,4-hydrotriazines, remain underexplored (Scheme 1a).24–28 Their unique arrangement of an isolated nitrogen atom adjacent to a conjugated N–N unit offers rich opportunities for structural diversification, and the scaffold is present in both natural products (e.g., cinachyramine) and pharmaceuticals (e.g., azaribine and pymetrozine). Existing syntheses, however, rely on prefunctionalized or specialized substrates, restricting modularity and accessibility.29–31

Exploring hydrotriazine chemical space for biological activities. (a) Occurrence of hydrotriazine scaffolds and representative existing synthetic strategies. (b) Purification-free, in-plate multicomponent synthesis of tetrahydrotriazines from aldehydes, amines, and hydrazines for direct-to-biology screenings (this work).

Herein, we introduce a broadly applicable multicomponent condensation that assembles 1,3-disubstituted and 1,3,5-trisubstituted tetrahydro-1,3,4-triazines (THTs) from simple aldehydes, amines, and hydrazines under mild, operationally trivial conditions (Scheme 1b). The combined use of formaldehyde as a key linchpin reagent and hexafluoroisopropanol (HFIP) solvent is critical for the efficient assembly of the tetrahydrotriazine core. The method is fully compatible with purification-free, multi-well plate synthesis, enabling the direct generation of diverse compound libraries for immediate biological evaluation through a streamlined “mixing–drying–biotesting” workflow.

Results and discussion

Discovery of the three-component reaction on peptide substrates

In our previous studies on aldehyde-mediated peptide modification, we discovered that simple formaldehyde (HCHO) efficiently crosslinks the phenolic side chain of tyrosine and the alkyl amine side chain of lysine (Lys) via a methylene linker at room temperature (rt) in HFIP solvent (Scheme 2a).32–35 Notably, HFIP is crucial for this high reactivity under such mild conditions. Our preliminary mechanistic studies indicate that hydrogen-bonded HFIP aggregates act as self-assembled solvent bridges, positioning the formaldimine intermediate and phenol in close proximity to promote a pseudo-intramolecular Mannich-type reaction.35–38 Beyond phenol/amine crosslinking, HCHO also links the guanidine side chain of arginine to Lys via two methylene units, forming a 6-membered tetrahydro-1,3,5 triazine (1,3,5-THT) linkage.32,33 To further expand the scope of HCHO-mediated Lys modification, we next explored its compatibility with other common functional groups. As shown in Scheme 2a, we were pleased to find that the treatment of model tripeptide substrate 1 with 5.0 equiv of HCHO (37%, aq), 1.2 equiv of cyclohexylhydrazine (h1), and 4.0 equiv of N,N-diisopropylethylamine (DIPEA) base in HFIP at rt for 2 hours unexpectedly afforded the tetrahydro-1,3,4-triazine (1,3,4-THT) product 2 in near quantitative yield, as determined by LC-MS analysis of the crude reaction mixture (see LC trace 1, conditions [A]). The N-terminus of 1 was capped with 1-naphthoyl group (NA) to block its involvement in the reaction and enhance the UV detection in LC. Amino peptide starting material 1, a salt with trifluoroacetic acid (TFA), was prepared using standard solid phase peptide synthesis protocols. Trace amounts of unconsumed 1 and N-methylation side product 3 (ref. 34) were obtained when 1.0 equiv of hydrazine h1 was used under conditions [A′] (see LC trace 2). Interestingly, under more forcing conditions [A″], at elevated temperature (80 °C) and with a larger excess of HCHO (10.0 equiv), the reaction predominantly afforded the tri-substituted THT product 4, which bears an additional hydroxymethylene group at the C5 position and incorporates a total of four HCHO units (see LC trace 3).

Yields are isolated unless indicated otherwise. aLC-estimated yield. b0.2 mmol scale. cYield measured by 1H-NMR using 1,1,2,2-tetrachloroethane (TCE) as an internal standard after solvent removal. While HFIP emerged as the optimal solvent, reactions conducted in other solvents such as water, CH3CN, and DMF also afforded product 2, albeit in moderate yields (typically 30–60%) under otherwise identical conditions (see Table S1 in the SI for details). Mixed solvent systems of HFIP/H2O were also effective (∼90% LC yield in HFIP/H2O (2/1)). DIPEA was mainly used to neutralize TFA introduced with the peptide and hydrazine starting material; other bases, such as K2CO3, delivered comparable results (see Table S2 in the SI for details). Notably, replacing HCHO with any other aldehyde tested failed to afford analogous THT products under the same conditions.

The cyclocondensation reaction sequence presumably began with two separate condensations of amine and hydrazine with HCHO, forming formaldimine and hydrazone (Scheme 2a).39–44 An ensuing Mannich-type reaction furnished the C–C coupled β-amino azo intermediate ii.40–44 Tautomerization of ii to iii, followed by cyclization with another HCHO, yielded the THT product iv. We speculate that the hydrazone-Mannich step constituted the major kinetic bottleneck, and a hydrogen bond between the formaldimine N and hydrazone NH may facilitate this transformation via a 6-membered transition state TS i. The high electrophilicity and minimal steric hindrance of formaldimines make them uniquely suited for this aza-Mannich process. Analogous to HCHO-mediated crosslinking reactions of phenol and amine, we further propose that H-bonded HFIP aggregates or water clusters may serve as a proton shuttle, promoting the intermolecular hydrazone-Mannich through flexible macrocyclic transition states, such as TS i′, with more favorable orbital alignment.35 The formation of product 4via nucleophilic addition of 2 to another HCHO likely proceeds through a relatively unstable diazenium intermediate and therefore requires more forcing conditions to occur.

Expansion of the three-component reaction to small-molecule substrates

As shown in Scheme 2b, we were pleased to find that the HCHO-mediated 3-component cyclocondensation reaction could be readily applied to make diverse 1,3-di-substituted 1,3,4-THTs using different combinations of small molecule amines and hydrazines under standard conditions [A]. For example, the reaction of 1.0 equiv of benzylamine hydrochloride with 1.2 equiv of N-methylhydrazine h2 and 4.0 equiv of DIEPA in HFIP at a 0.2 mmol scale at 0.1 M concentration at room temperature (rt) for 2 h yielded the desired product 5 in an 81% isolated yield. The 1H-NMR spectrum of the crude reaction mixture of 5 showed near-complete conversion of the amine (86%) and only trace amounts of byproducts (see Scheme 2b). Similar to the reaction of peptide 1, HFIP solvent was critical for achieving high yield for small molecule substrates. The reaction of 5 in regular alcohol solvents such as MeOH and iPrOH gave only small amounts of product 5 (<10%). A 52% yield was obtained in trifluoroethanol. A near-identical yield (85%) was obtained in mixed solvents of HFIP and H2O (2 : 1). The reaction of 5 at a 1.0 mmol scale gave an 80% isolated yield.

With respect to amine substrate scope, primary alkyl amines generally performed well. Secondary amines could form the C–C crosslinked intermediates in moderate yields, but would not undergo further cyclization with HCHO to give THT products. Increased steric hindrance could diminish the reactivity of alkyl amines (see 8vs.9). As shown by compounds 23–26, electronically deactivated amino groups of α-amino acids could react efficiently. In contrast, aryl amines exhibited considerably lower reactivities (see 5vs.11). Regarding the hydrazine scope, both alkyl- and aryl-substituted hydrazines were compatible. Alkyl hydrazines usually afford THT products in high yields (see 14). The reaction of bicyclo[1.1.1]pentanyl hydrazine gave 15 in excellent yield. In comparison, the reactivity of aryl hydrazines was strongly influenced by electronic effects, with electron-poor aryl hydrazines displaying reduced reactivity (see 10vs.12). The structure of THT 10 was unambiguously confirmed by X-ray crystallographic analysis. Hydrazine substituted with an electron-poor 2-pyridyl group failed to produce any desired product 18. Similarly, electronically deactivated acyl hydrazines also exhibited poor reactivity (17). Finally, as exemplified by 19–22, amino groups present in various drug molecules could be successfully transformed into the corresponding THT motifs in good yields.

Most of the 1,3-disubstituted 1,3,4-THT products described above could be isolated with sufficient purity by silica gel chromatography and stored for months without notable decomposition as a solid at 4 °C. However, many of these compounds readily decompose in aqueous media, especially under acidic conditions. For example, peptide product 2 almost completely decomposed in a pH 5.0 buffer at rt over 6 days, regenerating the amine precursor 1 in >60% LC yield (see Scheme S1 in the SI for details). In comparison, compound 2 was mostly intact (<2% decomposition) in a pH 9.0 buffer under otherwise identical conditions. The efficient regeneration of 1 suggested that the reverse C–C bond-breaking process of the hydrazone-Mannich involving two units of HCHO can readily occur (Scheme 2a).

Investigation of the four-component THT formation on peptide substrates

Intrigued by the decomposition of these 1,3-disubstituted THTs, we questioned whether installation of an additional substituent at the C5 position could enhance stability while further expanding the structural diversity of THTs. In principle, replacing one of the HCHO units with a different aldehyde could introduce such a substituent via closely related reaction pathways. As shown in Scheme 3a, the reaction of 1.0 equiv. of model peptide 1 with 1.0 equiv of CyNHNH2h1, 4.0 equiv. of HCHO, 1.05 equiv. of PhCHO, and 3.0 equiv. of DIEPA, all added simultaneously, in HFIP/H2O (2 : 1) at rt afforded a mixture of the tri-substituted THT product 27 and the di-substituted THT 2 (see LC trace 4). Notably, neither the regioisomer of THT 5 bearing a phenyl group at C6 nor any THT products incorporating PhCHO in place of HCHO at C2 were observed. These results underscore the unique effectiveness of HCHO as both the C6 and C2 carbon sources in this cyclocondensation-mediated THT synthesis. Gratifyingly, when CyNHNH2h1 and PhCHO were pre-mixed with DIPEA in HFIP/H2O for 1 h at rt prior to addition of 1 and HCHO, tri-substituted THT 27 became the predominant product, with formation of 2 mostly suppressed (conditions [B], LC trace 5). Replacing the HCHO responsible for imine formation with other aldehydes failed to give any corresponding THT products. Control experiments confirmed that formation of the PhCHO-derived hydrazone was complete within 1 h during the premixing step in either HFIP alone or HFIP/H2O. The subsequent Mannich addition and cyclization step required 2 h to reach completion. Compared with the 3-component reaction, the choice of solvent proved more critical in the 4-component system. Reactions conducted in DMF, MeOH, or THF furnished negligible amounts of 27. HFIP remained the most effective solvent when used alone, whereas reactions conducted in H2O proceeded in a moderate yield. Mixed solvents HFIP/H2O (2/1) delivered optimal results, as the presence of H2O helped suppress the competitive formation of the 3-component THT 2.

(b) Scope of the 4-component cyclocondensation of small-molecule substrates. Yields are isolated unless indicated otherwise. aLC-estimated yield. b0.2 mmol scale. c Yield measured by 1H-NMR using 1,1,2,2-tetrachloroethane (TCE) as an internal standard after solvent removal.

Considering the increased steric demand and electronic influence imparted by the Ph group on the hydrazone, we speculate that the key C–C forming hydrazone Mannich step in the 4-component reaction is more kinetically challenging than the corresponding step in the 3-component reaction involving formaldehyde hydrazone. Under these circumstances, the reaction pathway proceeding through a H-bonded 6-membered transition state TS v might be less favored, whereas the H-bonded macrocyclic transition state TS v′ promoted by HFIP or H2O solvent bridge might provide a more accessible reaction pathway.

Synthesis of small-molecule tri-substituted THTs

As shown in Scheme 3b, the two-step, one-pot protocol for the 4-component cyclocondensation reaction could be readily applied to the synthesis of structurally diverse tri-substituted THTs using various combinations of small-molecule amines, aldehydes, and hydrazines. Slightly different from the conditions employed for peptide substrate 1, hydrazines were used as the limiting reagent, together with 1.05 equiv of amines and aldehydes, respectively (conditions [B′]). As exemplified by 30, the 1H-NMR spectrum of the crude reaction mixtures showed near-complete conversion of the amine, aldehyde, and hydrazine starting materials, with negligible amounts of byproducts detected. The reactivity trends of both amine and hydrazine closely mirrored those observed in the 3-component system: primary alkyl amines generally performed well, whereas aryl amines afforded poor yields. Both alkyl and aryl hydrazines were compatible, although electron-deficient aryl hydrazines gave diminished yields. Notably, we were pleased to find that a wide range of aryl and heteroaryl aldehydes, readily available from commercial sources, can be efficiently incorporated into the THT framework. Electronic effects exerted only a minor influence on the reactivity of (hetero)aryl aldehydes. The structure of THT 50 was confirmed by X-ray crystallographic analysis. In comparison, alkyl aldehydes were less effective (see 56 and 57). Alkynyl aldehydes afforded low yields (e.g., 54), whereas glyoxylates bearing a strong electron-withdrawing alkoxycarbonyl substituent proceeded in a moderate yield (55). Together, this method enables efficient installation of a wide range of substitutions at the 1-, 3-, and 5-positions of the THT core using readily available “catalog” building blocks.

Compared with the 1,3-disubstituted THTs obtained via the 3-component condensation, the 1,3,5-trisubstituted THTs generated through the 4-component reaction exhibited markedly enhanced stability. For example, peptide 27, bearing a phenyl group at C5, showed negligible decomposition (<2% by LC analysis) in a pH 5.0 buffer over 2 days and no detectable decomposition in a pH 9.0 buffer over 7 days (see Scheme S5 in the SI for details). More generally, THTs featuring electron-withdrawing substituents at C5, such as pyridyl groups, displayed excellent stability. In contrast, strong electron-donating substituents at C5, including alkyl and electron-rich aryl groups, led to more pronounced decomposition in aqueous media. For example, a 3,4-dimethoxyphenyl-substituted compound showed ∼10% decomposition at pH 5.0 over 3 days, ∼4% decomposition at pH 7.0 over 7 days, and no detectable decomposition at pH 9.0 over 7 days (see Scheme S12 in the SI for details).

Bioactivity screening of a pilot library of purified THTs

With ready access to these structurally diverse THTs, we next conducted preliminary biological activity screening using standard protocols with purified compounds. Farnesoid X receptor (FXR), a member of the nuclear receptor superfamily, plays a vital regulatory role in metabolic homeostasis and has emerged as a promising therapeutic target for several metabolic diseases.45 However, there remains a lack of approved drugs that selectively modulate FXR as an agonist. To date, obeticholic acid (OCA), a bile acid analog, represents one of the most effective FXR agonists, with a reported EC50 of approximately 0.1 µM. We were pleased to find that three compounds 41, 58, and 59 from a small library of 115 THTs stood out in the biochemical AlphaScreen assay for the FXR agonist using fluorescence readouts, displaying EC50 values in the range of 1.4–4.8 µM (Scheme 4).46 Notably, these three compounds share identical substituents at N3 and C5 positions, while differing at N1.

In-plate synthesis of trisubstituted THTs for direct-to-biology screening

Encouraged by the unusual cleanliness and robustness of the THT synthesis, together with the initial biological evaluation of purified THTs, we next explored whether this chemistry could be translated into a miniaturized, in-plate format suitable for high-throughput direct-to-biology screening. As outlined in Scheme 5a, the 4-component THT synthesis was readily adapted to a plate-based, purification-free workflow, enabling the generation of compound libraries with sufficient purity through three simple operations: mixing, re-mixing, and drying. As a proof of concept, a standard 96-well microplate (330 µL well volume) was employed. Solutions of hydrazines, aldehydes, and DIPEA were first dispensed using a pipette into a HFIP/H2O (2/1) solvent system in each well (0.001 mmol scale, 10 mM concentration, ∼100 µL total volume), and the plate was gently agitated on a benchtop miniplate shaker at rt under air for 1 h (mixing). The amines and HCHO in HFIP solutions were then added using the pipette, and the reactions were allowed to proceed for an additional 3 h (re-mixing). Solvents were subsequently removed by gentle airflow over the plate for 30 min (drying). In most cases, airflow drying was sufficient; when necessary, the plate was further dried under vacuum. Residual H2O can be tolerated for subsequent biological assays. 1H NMR analysis of selected test runs with compounds 45 and 51 revealed clean reaction profiles, with the desired THT products formed in >80% yield and only minor readily identifiable byproducts (see Scheme S14–S17 in the SI for details). The dried crude reaction mixtures in the plate wells could then be re-dissolved in DMSO, and the resulting stock solutions were directly suitable for biological screening. Hit compounds identified from screening could subsequently be resynthesized in pure form for bioactivity confirmation and more detailed analysis.

In-plate synthesis of THT libraries for direct-to-biology screening. (a) General workflow for plate-based 4-component THT synthesis and screening. (b) In-plate synthesis and screening of pilot libraries for antagonist activity against α1A-adrenoceptor using a cell-based calcium flux assay. Small molecule modulation of α1A-AR induces changes in intracellular Ca2+ levels, which are monitored using the fluorescent calcium probe Fluo-4 AM. IC50 (mean ± SEM): half-maximal inhibitory concentrations; Imax (mean ± SEM): maximal antagonist efficacy.

As shown in Scheme 5b, a total of 181 reactions were conducted across two 96-well plates using combinations of 35 readily available commercial building blocks, including 13 amines, 6 hydrazines, and 16 aldehydes, following standardized in-plate protocols. To ensure high efficiency, only alkyl amines, alkyl hydrazines, and aryl or heteroaryl aldehydes were employed. In principle, these 35 components could generate up to 1248 distinct combinations. The stock solutions of the 181 dried crude reaction mixtures were subsequently prepared and directly evaluated for antagonist activity against α1A-adrenoceptors (α1A-ARs) in cell-based assays. Notably, the preparation of the two fully assay-ready plates could be completed within 10 h. α1-ARs are members of the G protein-coupled receptor superfamily, and the α1A-AR subtype is a key regulator of prostatic smooth muscle contraction, making it an important therapeutic target for prostatic hyperplasia.47 Antagonist activity was evaluated using a calcium flux assay in HEK293 cells transiently expressing α1A-AR/Gα16, in which receptor inhibition was quantified by monitoring Fluo-4 AM fluorescence as a readout of intracellular calcium release.48 Initial screening of the crude compounds at 10 µmol L−1 identified 14 compounds exhibiting inhibition rates greater than 70%. These hits were resynthesized and purified for activity confirmation and determination of half-maximal inhibitory concentrations (IC50). Ultimately, two compounds 60 and 61, displaying IC50 values of 42 nM and 37 nM, respectively, were identified.

Conclusions

In summary, we report a versatile multicomponent cyclocondensation strategy that enables the modular construction of tetrahydrotriazines with diverse three-dimensional structures from simple aldehyde, amine, and hydrazine building blocks. The method's high efficiency, robustness, cleanliness, and operational simplicity enable a purification-free in-plate synthesis workflow that delivers compound libraries directly suitable for biological evaluation. Proof-of-concept studies demonstrate that combining a limited set of readily available reagents with simple “mixing–drying–biotesting” operations allows the rapid identification of potent antagonists of biologically important α1A-adrenoceptors. Beyond providing streamlined access to previously unexplored heterocyclic chemical space, this work illustrates how integrating novel synthetic chemistry with simplified high-throughput functional screening can accelerate early-stage drug discovery while minimizing time, material use, and experimental waste.

Identification of Key Ferroptosis-Related Genes as Potential Diagnostic Biomarkers for Ischemic Stroke: Evidences from Integrated Bioinformatics Analysis and Experiments
Authors: Nan Tang, Yin Shen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractFerroptosis, an iron-dependent form of regulated cell death, has been linked to the occurrence and progression of ischemic stroke (IS). This study aims to uncover the key ferroptosis-related genes in IS and their correlations with immunoinflammatory responses. Key differentially expressed ferroptosis-related genes were screened by integrating differential analysis, weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA), and protein–protein interaction analysis. Machine learning algorithms, LASSO regression, Random Forest, RGF, and LightGBM were employed to identify potential diagnostic biomarkers, and diagnostic model was then established. Oxygen–glucose deprivation/reoxygenation (OGD/R)-stimulated HT-22 cells were established to validate the expression of biomarkers by RT-qPCR and western blot. Fifteen key ferroptosis-related genes were identified by integrated analyses, and ATM, DUSP1, SRC, and STAT3 were further screened as biomarkers by four algorithms. The diagnostic model established based on these four biomarkers exhibited well predictive power for IS, with AUC over 0.8 in both training and validation sets. Expression of DUSP and STATS positively correlated with neuroinflammation pathway, and positively correlated with abundance of neutrophils and macrophages. SRC positively correlated with abundance of monocytes, whereas ATM positively correlated with CD8 T cells and resting memory CD4 T cells. Both mRNA and protein levels of DUSP1, SRC, and STATS3 were significantly enhanced, while the level of ATM was reduced in OGD/R-stimulated HT-22 cells than control cells. In conclusion, dysregulation of key ferroptosis-related genes, ATM, DUSP1, SRC, and STAT3 might be implicated in the progression of IS, which could be biomarkers or targets for the diagnosis and therapy of IS.Graphical Abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s10571-025-01606-5.Keywords: Ischemic stroke, Ferroptosis, Neuroinflammation, Immune response, Machine learning

Introduction

Stroke is a leading cause of death and long-term disability worldwide (Ge et al. 2024), with an estimated global value of lost welfare of $2059.67 billion (accounting for about 1.66% of global GDP) in 2019 (Gerstl et al. 2023). The stroke burden rises substantially (70% and 102% increase in incidence and prevalence, and 43% increase in deaths) ranging from 1990 to 2019 based on the Global Stroke Fact Sheet 2022 published by the World Stroke Organization (Feigin et al. 2022). Ischemic stroke (IS) is responsible for 87% of all cerebrovascular accidents (Carnwath et al. 2024), which is usually caused by a blocked cerebral artery that leads to an inadequate supply of blood to the cerebral tissue, which further initiates a series of pathophysiological processes, including immune cells infiltration and neuronal death (Feske 2021). The narrow therapeutic window emphasizes the importance of earlier and faster recognition of IS, which has positive influences on therapeutic outcomes and prognosis but relies on the development of diagnostic biomarkers to a certain degree (Bindal et al. 2024; Kamtchum-Tatuene and Jickling 2019). Nevertheless, there are no biomarkers used in the routine management of stroke by considering the needed properties of sufficient specificity, sensitivity, rapidity as well as cost-effectiveness.

Multiple pathological events cause neuronal death during IS, and the neuroprotective interventions propose the requirements on elucidating the exact mechanisms of neuronal death (Tuo et al. 2022; Mao et al. 2022). As a non-apoptotic form of programmed cell death dependent on lipid peroxidation related to iron ions, ferroptosis is closely related to the occurrence and development of IS (Cui et al. 2021; Xu et al. 2023). Recent studies have found that excess free iron accumulation can be detected in brain tissue during cerebral ischemia–reperfusion (I/R) injury (Millerot et al. 2005). Iron overload not only induces oxidative damage and subsequent cell death by producing ROS, but also triggers lipid peroxidation and subsequent iron death by catalyzing the formation of hydroxyl radicals (Guo et al. 2023; Hu et al. 2024; DELGADO-MARTíN and MARTíNEZ-RUIZ 2024). Inhibiting ferroptosis has neuroprotective and antioxidant properties (Guan et al. 2019; Li et al. 2022; Liu et al. 2023), and has become a research hotspot in the prevention and treatment of IS. For example, Ginkgolide B could disrupt NCOA4-FTH1 interaction to inhibit ferroptosis, thus exerting neuroprotective roles in cerebral I/R injury (Yang et al. 2024). However, the potential of ferroptosis-related genes as biomarkers and therapeutic targets in IS has not been roundly illustrated.

To address this issue, we conducted this study to uncover the exact roles of ferroptosis-related genes in IS. Key ferroptosis-related genes that dysregulated in IS were first screened after intersecting with IS-associated genes screened by differential analysis and weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA). Diverse machine learning algorithms were then employed to identify potential biomarkers, followed by the correlation analysis with neuroinflammation and immune cells infiltration, both these two are crucial processes following IS (Wang et al. 2023). Importantly, we established oxygen–glucose deprivation/reoxygenation (OGD/R)-induced neuron injury in vitro model to confirm the identified biomarkers. This study will provide potential biomarkers or targets for the early diagnosis and treatments of IS.

Methods

Data Acquisition and Pre-processing

Three microarray datasets and corresponding annotation files utilized in this study were acquired from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database. The selection criteria for the used datasets were as follows: (1) species: Human; (2) sample type: peripheral blood; (3) data type: mRNA expression profile; (4) sample size: no less than 40 samples; and (5) disease type: IS, excluding those with other subtypes of stroke such as cardiogenic stroke. After data normalization processing, datasets GSE16561 (blood samples from 39 IS patients and 24 healthy controls, platform: GPL6883) and GSE37587 (34 blood samples from IS patients within 24 h from onset, platform: GPL6883) were merged into a large dataset, and batch effect across two dataset was eliminated utilizing sva package in R (Leek et al. 2012). This merged dataset was used as discovery dataset. GSE22255 dataset (peripheral blood mononuclear cells of 20 IS patients and 20 controls) was utilized as an external dataset for validation. Ferroptosis-related genes were searched from FerrDb and GeneCards (relevance score ≥ 1) databases.

Differential Expression Analysis

Genes that differentially expressed (DEGs) in IS samples versus to that in controls were excavated using Limma package in R, with a cut-off of P < 0.05. DEGs were displayed by Volcano plot and heatmap that were plotted by means of ggplot2 package in R (https://github.com/tidyverse/ggplot2).

WGCNA

IS-associated module genes were identified by weighted gene co-expression analysis utilizing WGCNA package in R (Langfelder and Horvath 2008). Briefly, a scale-free network was constructed, and a soft threshold of 12 was determined to convert the scale-free network into a weighted network. Then, highly correlated Genes modules were identified by means of Gene hierarchical clustering and dynamic tree cutting, with minmoduleSize setting as 25 and mergeCutHeight setting as 0.5. Next, the module eigengene was calculated, and the correlations between each module eigengene and clinical traits (IS and control) were calculated to identify disease-associated module genes.

Venn Anaelysis

The overlapped genes among three gene sets, including DEGs, ferroptosis-related genes and IS-associated module genes in WGCNA, were screened using UpSetR package (https://github.com/hms-dbmi/UpSetR).

Protein–Protein Interaction (PPI) Network

PPI analysis was conducted for the overlapped genes screened by Venn analysis. In short, the PPIs were predicted based on STRING database (von Mering et al. 2003), with parameters setting as score_cutoff > 0.4 and size_cutoff < 10. The obtained PPIs were visualized into a PPI network by means of Cytoscape software (Shannon et al. 2003). The plug-in MCODE was employed to retrieve the significant modules from the PPI network.

Identification of Diagnostic Signature by Machine Learning Algorithms

To identify the genes that enables to predict the risk of IS, four machine learning algorithms were employed to screen the feature genes, including LASSO regression, random forest, LightGBM, and Regularized Greedy Forest (RGF) algorithms. Specifically, glmnet package (https://github.com/cran/glmnet) in R was employed to conduct LASSO regression, which was performed with stratified fivefold cross-validation and repeated for 100 iterations select feature variables based on λ.1se. The caret package and randomForest package in R were employed to conduct random forest algorithm with stratified fivefold cross-validation, and down-sampling strategy was used during the model training process due to data imbalance. The model performance is optimized by adjusting the mtry parameter (the maximum number of features considered in each split), and the optimal mtry value is ultimately determined. Importance of each feature was measured using MeanDecreaseGini index. LightGBM model was constructed by lightgbm package (https://lightgbm.readthedocs.io/en/latest/R/reference/) (Zhang et al. 2019). To prevent overfitting, we conducted multiple optimizations on the model: Set a smaller learning rate to enable the model to learn gradually; limit the num_leaves and max_depth to control the complexity of the tree; use feature_fraction and bagging_fraction for random sampling of features and data to enhance the generalization ability of the model. Meanwhile, the lambda_l1 and lambda_l2 regularization terms are introduced to further suppress overfitting. We determined the optimal number of iterations (nrounds) through fivefold cross-validation, and dynamically terminated the training with early_stopping_rounds = 20. RGF package (https://github.com/RGF-team/rgf/tree/master/R-package) was employed to conduct RGF model. We adopted repeated fivefold cross-validation (repeated three times) to evaluate the model performance and combined the down-sampling method to handle the data imbalance. Model was optimized by adjusting the mtry parameter and the minimum node size (min.node.size). The parameters of each algorithm were summarized in Table S1. Besides, we plotted receiver operator characteristic (ROC) curve to evaluate the performance of the models. The shared feature Genes screened by all four algorithms were considered as diagnostic signature, and a diagnostic model was constructed by means of logistic regression analysis based on the identified diagnostic signature. The merged discovery dataset was assigned into training and test set as per a ratio of 6: 4, and the diagnostic performance of the model was evaluated in training and test sets as well as an independent external dataset GSE22255.

Gene Set Variation Analysis (GSVA)

With the predefined gene sets of “HP_NEUROINFLAMMATION” and “WP_NEUROINFLAMMATION” provided in MSigDB database utilized as enrichment references, the GSVA package in R (HäNZELMANN et al. 2013) was employed to conduct enrichment analysis to obtain the enrichment score of each pathway in samples. The differences on enrichment scores were compared across IS and controls utilizing t-test, followed by Pearson correlation analysis between diagnostic genes and differential pathways.

Immune Infiltration Evaluation

Based on the gene expression matrix in the merged discovery dataset, the infiltration fractions of each immune cells were inferred utilizing CIBERSORT deconvolution tool (Chen et al. 2018), with the reference dataset of 22 immune cell subtypes (LM22) as reference. The differences on infiltration fractions were further uncovered utilizing t-test, followed by Pearson correlation analysis between diagnostic genes and immune cells.

Cells Culture and Treatments

Mouse hippocampal neuron cell line HT-22 cells were purchased from Wuhan procell Life Technology Co., LTD (#CL-0697, China), and were cultured in an incubator (37 ℃ and 5% CO2) with DMEM medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (#A5669701, Gibco, USA) and 1% penicillin–streptomycin (#SV30010, Hyclone, USA). For OGD/R stimulation, HT-22 cells were cultured in glucose-free DMEM at 37 °C for 6 h in a hypoxic incubator under the conditions of 1% O2, 94% N2, and 5% CO2. Afterward, the cells were maintained in complete DMEM in an incubator under 95% air and 5% CO2 at 37 °C for 18 h. Cells cultured in 6-well plates at a density of 1 × 106 cells/well were used for subsequent experiments.

RNA Extraction and RT-qPCR

Total RNAs were isolated from cells utilizing Trizol reagent (#15,596,018, Invitrogen, USA), and were inversely transcribed to synthesize complementary DNA (cDNA) utilizing FastKing RT SuperMix (#KR118-02, TIANGEN, Beijing, China). Next, PCR amplification was conducted with the aid of SYBR Green PCR Master Mix (#A4004M, Xiamen lifeint Technology Co., LTD, China) under the reaction conditions: 95 ℃ for 3 min, 40 cycles of 95 ℃ for 12 s, 62 ℃ for 40 s. The sequences of the used primers are listed in Table S2.

Western Blot

Cells were lysed in RIPA lysis buffer to isolate total proteins, and the protein concentration was then measured with the aids of a BCA assay kit (#P0010S, Beyotime, Shanghai, China). The isolated proteins were separated by means of SDS–polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis and were transferred onto polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes (#FFP24, Beyotime). Following 1 h blocking with blocking buffer, the PVDF membranes were incubated with antibodies: anti-ATM (1:1000, # ab17995, Abcam, USA) (Chen et al. 2021), anti-DUSP1 (1: 1000, # sc-373841, Santa Cruz, USA) (Geng et al. 2024), anti-SRC (1:1000, #ab133283, Abcam) (Cai et al. 2022), anti-STAT3 (1:1000, #ab68153, Abcam) (Dong et al. 2024), and anti-GAPDH (1:2500, # ab181602, Abcam) (Jin et al. 2024) overnight at 4 °C. Afterward, the membranes were washed utilizing 1X TBST (#T1085, Solarbio, Beijing, China) for 10 min, and were then incubated with secondary antibodies (1:2000) for 60 min at dark. The PVDF membranes were washed with ECL reagent and were transferred for film exposure in exposure cassette.

Statistical Analysis

All bioinformatics analyses were conducted utilizing R software (version 4.2.2). Both RT-qPCR and Western blot were conducted by the laboratory technician who blinded to the results of bioinformatics analysis. All experimental data were displayed as mean ± standard deviation, and was statistically analyzed utilizing GraphPad 8.0 software. Normal distribution of data was examined by Shapiro–Wilk test, and homogeneity of the variance was examined by F-test. Unpaired t-test was employed for comparisons between groups. Statistic differences were donated when P < 0.05.

Results

Gene Expression Pattern was Altered in IS

After eliminating batch effect, GSE1656 and GSE3758 datasets were merged into a large dataset for analyses, and the distribution of samples in this merged dataset were more uniform, indicating that this merged dataset enabled subsequent analyses (Fig. 1A). After differential analysis, large-scale of changes on Gene expression pattern was observed across IS and control samples, involving 3455 genes (Fig. 1B-C). Specifically, expression of 1873 Genes were up-regulated, whereas 1582 genes were down-regulated in IS samples in comparison with those in controls.

Screening of Key Ferroptosis-Related Genes in IS

Ferroptosis-related genes were searched from FerrDb and GeneCards databases. With threshold of relevance score ≥ 1, 789 Genes were retrieved from GeneCards, and a total of 1045 ferroptosis-related genes (Table S3) were obtained after merged with the 649 Genes in FerrDb database. Venn analysis revealed that there were 102 overlapped genes (Table S4) among 3455 DEGs, 2607 IS-associated Genes and 1045 ferroptosis-related genes these three gene sets (Fig. 2D). These genes exhibited close interactions, which was evidenced by the PPI network (Fig. 3A). From the PPI network, a significant sub-network comprising of 15 genes was retrieved by utilizing MCODE algorithm (Fig. 3B). The 15 genes were considered as candidate genes for further analyses.

Identification of Diagnostic Biomarkers for IS

From the above 15 candidate genes, potential diagnostic biomarkers were screened by four machine learning algorithms. Specifically, eight genes, including ATM, CREB1, DUSP1, PTGS2, SRC, STAT3, CREB1, and PRKCA were determined by LASSO regression, which showed an area under ROC curve (AUC) of 0.920 (Fig. 4A-B). Nine genes, including ATM, PTGS2, STAT3, SRC, PTEN, DNMT1, DUSP1, EGFR, and MAPK14, were identified as feature Genes by random forest algorithm, and the AUC of the model based on these nine Genes was 0.962 (Fig. 4C-D). Nine genes were identified by RGF algorithm, including ATM, STAT3, MAPK3, PRKCA, SRC, MAPK1, EGFR, DNMT1, and DUSP1 (Fig. 4E). The model based on these nine Genes showed an AUC of 0.998 (Fig. 4F). Meanwhile, using LightGBM algorithm, nine genes were identified, including EGFR, STAT3, DUSP1, PTGS2, ATM, MAPK14, PTEN, SRC, and CREB1 (Fig. 4G), which showed an AUC of 0.862 (Fig. 4H). Finally, 4 shared feature genes were uncovered by these four algorithms, including ATM, DUSP1, SRC, and STAT3 (Fig. 4I).

Diagnostic Biomarkers Correlated with The Activated Neuroinflammation in IS

To uncover the status of neuroinflammation in IS, GSVA was conducted, and the results suggested that WP_neuroinflammation pathway was activated in IS, and the HP_neuroinflammation pathway tended to be activated in IS (Fig. 5A). Afterward, Pearson correlation analysis was then conducted to explore the correlations with the diagnostic biomarkers. Expression of DUSP1 (r = 0.55, P = 5.6e-09) and STATS3 (r = 0.39, P = 6.6e-05) positively correlated with the WP_neuroinflammation pathway, while ATM (r = 0.057, P = 0.58) and SRC (r = 0.12, P = 0.24) exhibited no significant correlations (Fig. 5B). This implied that the diagnostic biomarkers DUSP1 and STATS3 could also predict the activation of neuroinflammation to some extent.

Diagnostic Biomarkers Correlated with Immune Infiltration in IS

Based on the Gene expression matrix in the merged discovery dataset, infiltration fractions of 18 immune cells out of 22 immune cells were inferred by CIBERSORT deconvolution tool (Fig. 6A). The immune landscape of IS samples was different from that of control samples. Particularly, IS samples exhibited observably enhanced abundance of neutrophils, monocytes, M0 macrophages, and activated memory CD4 T cells, whereas harbored markedly reduced abundance of memory B cells, CD8 T cells, and resting memory CD4 T cells (Fig. 6B). Correlation analysis indicated that DUSP1 and STAT3 positively correlated with abundance of neutrophils and M0 macrophages. SRC positively correlated with abundance of monocytes, whereas ATM positively correlated with CD8 T cells and resting memory CD4 T cells (Fig. 6C). These results suggested that the four diagnostic biomarkers might reflect the immune status in IS.

Expression of Diagnostic Biomarkers in OGD/R Induced Neuron Injury

To confirm the expression of the four identified diagnostic biomarkers, an in vitro neuron injury cell model was induced by OGD/R stimulation on HT-22 cells. Both mRNA and protein levels of ATM were reduced in OGD/R-stimulated HT-22 cells, while DUSP1, SRC, and STATS3 were significantly enhanced in OGD/R-stimulated HT-22 cells compared to normal cells (Fig. 7A-B).

Discussion

In this study, we conducted a comprehensive investigation on the potential of ferroptosis-related genes as biomarkers in IS. Four genes, ATM, DUSP1, SRC, and STAT3, were identified as diagnostic biomarker for IS, and the diagnostic model established based on these four biomarkers exhibited well predictive power for IS. Besides, these biomarkers showed significant correlations with neuroinflammation pathway and the levels of immune cells in IS.

In our current study, 102 dysregulated ferroptosis-related Genes in IS were screened by after intersecting with 3455 DEGs and 2607 IS-associated Genes identified by WGCNA. From which, 15 candidate ferroptosis-related Genes were further identified by PPI and module analysis. These 15 genes exhibited close interactions, indicating that they might exert as a complex. Machine learning (ML) is a burgeoning field in medicine, which provides superior predictive power in comparison with conventional statistic model, capturing nonlinear relations across predictive factors and outcomes, and the complex interactions within predictive factors (Greener et al. 2022; Lo Vercio et al. 2020). Given the high accuracy, machine learning approaches are being increasingly applied in medical field, including stroke (Heo et al. 2019). In this study, four machine learning algorithms were employed, and four potential ferroptosis-associated biomarkers, ATM, DUSP1, SRC, and STAT3, were identified. The diagnostic model based on these four Genes exhibited superior predictive power for IS, with AUC value more than 0.8, confirming the capacity as biomarkers in IS.

Ataxia-telangiectasia mutated (ATM) encodes a serine/threonine kinase, which exerts as a modulator of multiple proteins, such as p53. Xie et al. (Xie et al. 2020) reported a double action of ATM in ischemic injury and ischemic preconditioning. On one hand, neuroprotective effect of preconditioning could be reversed when pharmacological inhibiting ATM before preconditioning. On the other hand, OGD/R injury could induce ATM/P53 pro-apoptosis pathway, whereas ATM pharmacological inhibition could facilitate neuronal survival during OGD/R injury (Xie et al. 2020).

Dual Specificity Phosphatase 1 (DUSP1), also termed as MKP−1, is a negative regulation factor to MAPK pathway. We observed markedly enhanced DUSP1 expression in IS samples and in OGD/R-stimulated HT-22 cells, which was consistent with the findings in the study by Fan et al. (Fan et al. 2022), with an observation of enhanced DUSP1 in IS samples in bioinformatics results and both in vitro and in vivo experiments. Highly expressed DUSP1 was reported to inhibit neuronal death, exerting a neuroprotective role (Koga et al. 2012; Liu et al. 2014). For example, Liu et al. (Liu et al. 2014) reported an enhanced expression of DUSP1 in the microglia and neurons post-stroke, and its deficiency could increase hemorrhagic transformation, infarct as well as neurological deficits to worsen the outcomes. In IS mouse model, Qin et al. (Qin et al. 2023) found that DUSP1 overexpressed in endothelial cells could decrease infarct volume and inflammatory cytokines levels, and repress vascular injury by protecting the integrity of blood–brain barrier.

SRC encodes a tyrosine-protein kinase, which was inactivated during the early stages (within 3 h) of neuronal I/R injury, whereas was hyperactivated in later stages (after 6 h) (Tang et al. 2023). Mitophagy could be triggered during IS to protect neurons against I/R injury, and FUNDC1-mediated neuronal mitophagy could be rescued when inhibiting hyperactivated SRC (Tang et al. 2023). STAT3 is an established transcription factor, which usually exerts its roles in IS by forming a JAK2/STAT3 signal axis, such as regulating proliferation and differentiation of neural progenitor cells post-stroke (Cheng et al. 2019), activation of JAK2/STAT3 involves the neuroprotective effect of resveratrol in IS models (Hou et al. 2018). Besides, the role of STAT3 was involved in regulating inflammatory response post-stroke (Zhu et al. 2021; Fan et al. 2023). For example, deficiency of STAT3 phosphorylation could repress the activation of NLRP3 inflammasome in IS (Zhu et al. 2021).

Immunoinflammatory response plays key regulatory roles in the progression of IS (Thapa et al. 2023). IS produces an intense inflammatory cascade in the brain, leading to the development of neuroinflammation, which is caused not only by resident immune cells, but also by infiltrating peripheral immunoinflammatory cells, such as macrophages and neutrophils (Wang et al. 2023). In the early stage of IS, a variety of injury-related pattern molecules released by damaged neurons induce microglia activation, peripheral immune response, and increased secretion of inflammatory mediators, which then accelerated the destruction of the blood–brain barrier, aggravated brain edema and microcirculation disorders, thus resulting in secondary brain injury. In the later stages of post-stroke, immune cells gradually promote neuronal repair through phenotypic changes, and stroke-induced immunosuppression jointly affects the final outcome of stroke (Thapa et al. 2023; Delong et al. 2022).

We found that expression of DUSP1 and STAT3 was positively correlated with neuroinflammation. Recent studies have demonstrated the involvements of DUSP1 with neuroinflammation (Jiao et al. 2020; Gui et al. 2020). Depletion of hippocampal DUSP1 was found to inhibit stress-caused neuroinflammation via activating MAPK signaling (Geng et al. 2024). While Huang et al. revealed that DUSP1 protected against neuroinflammation by modulating mitochondrial dysfunction and endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress in a MAPK-JNK pathway-dependent way (Huang and Jiang 2020). JAK-STAT3 was found to regulate the roles of inhibiting neuroinflammation of Chaihu-Shugan-San in treating depression post-stroke (Fan et al. 2023). Besides, STAT3 regulates neuroinflammation in a microglia-dependent way (Wang et al. 2024; Zheng et al. 2022). For example, STAT3 was implicated in the circPTP4A2-induced neuroinflammation in IS injury via triggering microglial polarization (Wang et al. 2024). In addition to the correlation with neuroinflammation, expression of DUSP1 and STAT3 also positively correlated with infiltrating macrophages and neutrophils in our bioinformatics results. Consistently, Fan et al. observed positive correlations across DUSP1 expression and neutrophils infiltration (r = 0.339, P < 0.05) (Fan et al. 2022). Roles of STAT3 in IS were linked to regulating the polarization of macrophage to an anti-inflammatory phenotype (Liu et al. 2019; Zhong et al. 2022). Increased phosphorylation of STAT3 was found to modulate the peripheral neutrophils inflammation response in IS (Dhanesha et al. 2022). These findings implied that these biomarkers could reflect the neuroinflammation and the status of infiltrating immune cells to a certain extent.

The above findings highlighted the close involvements of these four biomarkers in IS. Considering all the findings were observed based on public dataset, we conducted in vitro experiments to confirm the expression of these four biomarkers. Compared to normal cells, both mRNA and protein levels of ATM were reduced in OGD/R-stimulated HT-22 cells, while DUSP1, SRC, and STATS3 were significantly enhanced, which was consistent with the expression trend in bioinformatics analysis. However, the sample size of the in vitro experiments was not precalculated and was determined based on previous experience. Besides, specificity of the antibodies used in western blot was not evaluated. These were the limitations of current study. In future, expression of these four genes should be confirmed in clinical samples with a large sample size. Besides, the exact roles of these four genes in the development of IS should be investigated by functional experiments both in vitro and in vivo, especially the role of these genes in neurons damage and repair.

Conclusion

In summary, this study identified four ferroptosis-related DEGs in IS, including ATM, DUSP1, SRC, and STAT3, which exerted superior diagnostic performance for IS. Particularly, DUSP1 and STAT3 exhibited significant correlations with neuroinflammation and infiltrating macrophages and neutrophils. Results from the current study demonstrated the significant associations of ferroptosis with IS, and suggested that these four key ferroptosis-related genes might be potential biomarkers or targets for the diagnosis and treatments of IS. The exact roles of these four genes in IS should be further explored in future.

Identification of CTHRC1 as a Potential Prognostic Biomarker in Esophageal Squamous Cell Carcinoma by Bioinformatics Analysis
Authors: Shuoguoli Li, Yongan Kang, Yijun Qi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•CTHRC1 is markedly upregulated in ESCC across multiple cohorts.•High CTHRC1 correlates with advanced stage, nodal metastasis, and poor survival.•Single-cell analysis localizes CTHRC1 to myofibroblastic CAFs and malignant epithelial cells.•CTHRC1 regulates Vimentin and MMP9, supporting EMT and invasion in ESCC.•CTHRC1 is an independent prognostic biomarker and potential therapeutic target in ESCC.

Introduction

Esophageal malignancies impose a disproportionate global burden, accounting for the sixth highest cancer-related death toll worldwide [1]. Among histological variants, esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) predominates, comprising roughly 90% of all cases with pronounced geographic clustering in East Asian populations [2]. Despite the adoption of multimodal treatment paradigms—encompassing resection, cytotoxic chemotherapy, radiation, and molecularly targeted agents—long-term outcomes remain poor, with five-year survival below 20% across most registries [3]. The principal determinants of this dismal prognosis include delayed clinical presentation, an inherent tendency toward lymphovascular spread, and the absence of validated prognostic and predictive molecular markers.

High-throughput genomic and transcriptomic interrogation has illuminated the substantial molecular diversity underpinning ESCC [4]. Nevertheless, converting these discoveries into biomarkers with demonstrable clinical utility has proved elusive. Contemporary staging frameworks lean heavily on anatomopathological features, which frequently fail to capture inter-patient biological variability or forecast therapeutic response at the individual level [5]. Accordingly, there is a pressing imperative to identify robust molecular surrogates capable of refining prognostic stratification, informing therapeutic selection, and serving as tractable drug targets in this disease.

Collagen triple helix repeat containing 1 (CTHRC1) encodes a secreted glycoprotein originally characterized as a mediator of vascular injury response and arterial tissue remodeling [6]. Accumulated evidence has since revealed broader oncological relevance: CTHRC1 is associated with ECM reorganization, correlates with cellular motility and matrix invasion, and has been linked to both canonical and non-canonical Wnt signaling cascades [7]. Pathological upregulation of CTHRC1 has been recorded across a spectrum of solid tumors—including gastric, hepatocellular, pancreatic, and colorectal carcinomas—where it consistently associates with aggressive disease phenotypes and diminished patient survival [[8], [9], [10]]. These protumorigenic associations have been attributed to CTHRC1-related matrix reorganization, facilitation of EMT, stimulation of neo-vascularization, and establishment of a permissive tumor microenvironment, although direct causal evidence varies by tumor type [11].

Despite suggestive preliminary data in esophageal malignancy, a systematic characterization of CTHRC1 expression kinetics, functional gene networks, and independent prognostic utility in ESCC is lacking [12]. Public cancer genomics repositories—notably the Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO)—now furnish multi-cohort transcriptomic resources that enable rigorous cross-dataset biomarker evaluation [13]. Complementarily, single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) affords cellular-resolution dissection of tumor-microenvironment heterogeneity, unmasking context-specific expression patterns inaccessible to bulk profiling approaches [14].

To address this knowledge gap, we conducted an integrated bioinformatics investigation encompassing CTHRC1 differential expression across TCGA and GEO bulk RNA datasets, WGCNA to delineate co-expressed functional gene modules, PPI network assembly to map molecular interaction architecture, and scRNA-seq profiling to resolve CTHRC1 distribution across distinct intratumoral cell populations. Clinical and prognostic associations were subsequently quantified through survival modeling and clinicopathological correlation analyses. Collectively, our data support CTHRC1 as a reliable prognostic biomarker and therapeutically attractive target in ESCC.

Methods

Data acquisition and preprocessing

Transcriptomic data were obtained from the TCGA-ESCA project (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/), comprising RNA-seq profiles from 185 tumor and 13 normal esophageal tissue specimens. Raw count data were normalized to log2(CPM+1) and annotated using HGNC gene symbols. The GSE53624 dataset (119 ESCC tumor samples, GPL18109 platform) was retrieved from GEO (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) as an external validation cohort. Single-cell transcriptomic data (GSM8422228, 10x Genomics) were processed through quality control, retaining 11,409 high-quality cells for downstream analysis.

Differential expression analysis

Differential expression between tumor (n=185) and normal (n=13) tissues in the TCGA-ESCA cohort was assessed using Student's t-test with Benjamini-Hochberg (FDR) multiple testing correction. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were defined by |log2 fold change| > 1 and adjusted p-value < 0.05, yielding 1651 upregulated and 575 downregulated genes.

Weighted gene co-expression network analysis

Co-expression network analysis was performed on the top 5,000 most variable genes in the TCGA ESCC cohort. Pearson correlation coefficients were computed between each gene and CTHRC1. Genes with |r| > 0.6 and FDR < 0.001 were designated as CTHRC1-associated and organized into co-expression modules via hierarchical clustering. Gene Ontology (GO) Biological Process and KEGG pathway enrichment analyses were conducted on CTHRC1-associated module genes using gseapy/Enrichr.

Protein-protein interaction network analysis

CTHRC1-associated genes were submitted to the STRING database (v12, https://string-db.org/) with a minimum interaction confidence score of 0.4. Network topology was analyzed to compute degree centrality and betweenness centrality. The top 10 hub genes were identified via degree centrality ranking. GO and KEGG functional enrichment analyses were performed on all PPI network genes.

Single-cell transcriptomic analysis

Single-cell RNA-seq data (GSM8422228, EC08) were processed using Scanpy (v1.9). Quality control retained cells with 200-6,000 detected genes and <25% mitochondrial reads. After normalization, highly variable gene selection (top 2,000), PCA (50 components), UMAP visualization, and Leiden clustering (resolution 0.6), cell types were annotated using canonical markers: Epithelial (EPCAM, KRT8), Fibroblast (COL1A1, DCN), Endothelial (PECAM1, CDH5), T cells (CD3D, CD3E), B cells (CD79A, MS4A1), Myeloid (CD68, CD14), and Mast cells (KIT). Fibroblast subpopulations were further analyzed for myCAF (ACTA2, TAGLN) and iCAF (IL6, CXCL12) markers.

Tumor immune microenvironment analysis

ESTIMATE-like stromal and immune scores were computed for TCGA ESCC samples using curated gene signatures. Immune cell relative abundances were estimated using cell-type-specific marker gene sets. Spearman correlations were calculated between CTHRC1 expression and immune checkpoint genes (PDCD1, CD274, CTLA4, TIGIT, LAG3, HAVCR2, IDO1, BTLA, ICOS, TNFRSF9). ESCC samples were stratified into CTHRC1-High and CTHRC1-Low groups by median expression for comparative TME analysis.

Clinical prognostic analysis

ESCC patients were dichotomized into CTHRC1-High and CTHRC1-Low groups by median expression. Overall survival was analyzed using the Kaplan-Meier method with log-rank testing. Univariate Cox proportional hazards regression was performed for CTHRC1 and clinicopathological variables. Time-dependent ROC curves were constructed to evaluate predictive accuracy at 1, 3, and 5 years. Associations between CTHRC1 expression and AJCC pathological stage, T stage, and N stage were assessed using the Kruskal-Wallis test.

Cell culture and in vitro validation

ESCC cell lines EC109, KYSE-150, KYSE-30, and TE-1, together with the normal esophageal epithelial line HEEC, were procured from the Cell Bank of the Chinese Academy of Sciences (Shanghai, China). ESCC lines were propagated in RPMI-1640 medium containing 10% FBS, 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 μg/mL streptomycin at 37°C with 5% CO₂. HEEC cells were maintained in Keratinocyte Serum-Free Medium (K-SFM; Gibco) supplemented with bovine pituitary extract and epidermal growth factor per manufacturer's guidance.

Total RNA was harvested with TRIzol (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA) following the supplier's protocol; quantity and purity were verified on a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific). One microgram of RNA was reverse-transcribed with PrimeScript RT Reagent Kit (Takara, Dalian, China) to generate complementary DNA. qRT-PCR was conducted using SYBR Green Master Mix (Applied Biosystems) on a QuantStudio 3 system (Thermo Fisher Scientific). CTHRC1 primers were: forward 5′-TGACCTCAACGACAACACCA-3′, reverse 5′-CTCCAGGTAGTCGTTGACCA-3′. GAPDH reference primers were: forward 5′-GAAGGTGAAGGTCGGAGTC-3′, reverse 5′-GAAGATGGTGATGGGATTTC-3′. Relative expression was derived by the 2^(−ΔΔCt) method from three independent biological replicates.

Cell transfection and CTHRC1 knockdown/overexpression

CTHRC1 silencing used a chemically synthesized si-CTHRC1 alongside a non-targeting scramble control (both from RiboBio, Guangzhou, China); transfection into KYSE-30 cells used Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen) at 50 nM final siRNA concentration. For stable overexpression, the human CTHRC1 open reading frame was subcloned into pLVX-IRES-ZsGreen1 (Clontech). Lentiviral stocks were generated by co-transfecting HEK293T cells with the expression construct and packaging plasmids psPAX2/pMD2.G. Infected KYSE-30 cells were selected with 2 μg/mL puromycin; perturbation efficiency was verified at both mRNA and protein levels by qRT-PCR and Western blot.

Cancer-associated fibroblast (CAF) isolation and culture

Primary CAFs were derived from freshly excised ESCC tumor specimens, and normal lung fibroblasts (NLF) from matched peri-tumoral tissue. Tissue fragments were enzymatically dissociated in DMEM containing collagenase type I (1 mg/mL; Sigma-Aldrich) and DNase I (100 U/mL; Sigma-Aldrich) for 2 h at 37°C with agitation. Filtered single-cell suspensions (70-μm strainer) were cultured in DMEM/10% FBS and used within passage 5. CAF authenticity was established by immunopositivity for α-SMA and vimentin and immunonegativity for Pan-CK and CD31. Written informed consent was obtained from all tissue donors under institutional ethics board approval.

Immunofluorescence staining

Glass coverslip-plated cells (24-well format, 60–70% confluence) were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (20 min, room temperature), permeabilized with 0.3% Triton X-100/PBS (10 min), and blocked with 5% BSA/PBS (1 h). Primary antibody incubations proceeded overnight at 4°C using: anti-CTHRC1 (1:200; Abcam ab85662), anti-Pan-CK (1:200; Abcam ab86734), anti-Vimentin (1:200; Cell Signaling Technology #5741), anti-MMP9 (1:200; Abcam ab76003), and anti-α-SMA (1:200; Sigma-Aldrich A2547). After PBS washing, cells were labeled with Alexa Fluor 594–conjugated secondary antibody (CTHRC1, red) and Alexa Fluor 488–conjugated secondary antibody (target proteins, green) for 1 h at room temperature. Nuclei were visualized with DAPI (1 μg/mL, 10 min). Coverslips were mounted in ProLong Gold Antifade Mountant and examined on an Olympus BX53 fluorescence microscope; fluorescence intensity was quantified in ImageJ (NIH).

Statistical analysis

Data management and statistical inference were performed in R (v4.2.0) and GraphPad Prism (v9.0). Continuous data are expressed as mean ± SD or as median (IQR) depending on distributional characteristics evaluated by Shapiro–Wilk normality test. Parametric comparisons used Student's t-test (two groups) or one-way ANOVA (multiple groups); non-normal distributions were analyzed by Wilcoxon rank-sum or Kruskal–Wallis tests.

Categorical associations were examined using chi-square or Fisher's exact tests. Correlation strength was measured by Pearson or Spearman coefficients as appropriate. Log-rank testing compared survival curves. Two-tailed p-values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant; Benjamini–Hochberg correction was applied for multiple hypothesis testing.

Results

CTHRC1 is significantly upregulated in ESCC

Transcriptomic analysis of the TCGA-ESCA cohort (185 tumor vs. 13 normal specimens) identified 1651 upregulated and 575 downregulated genes (|log2FC| > 1, FDR < 0.05). CTHRC1 was markedly overexpressed in tumor tissue (log2FC = 2.97, adjusted p = 7.10e-06), ranking among the most significantly upregulated genes on the volcano plot (Figure 1A). Mean CTHRC1 expression in tumors (log2(CPM+1) = 4.94) was substantially higher than in normal tissue (log2(CPM+1) = 1.97) (Figure 1B). CTHRC1 expression increased progressively with pathological stage, peaking in Stage IV (Kruskal-Wallis p < 0.05) (Figure 1C). Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis demonstrated robust discriminatory capacity, with an AUC of 0.945 (Figure 1D). The expression heatmap of DEGs sorted by CTHRC1 levels confirmed its strong association with tumor samples (Figure 1E). Comprehensive diagnostic performance metrics further validated CTHRC1′s potential clinical utility (Figure 1F). Density distribution plots revealed distinct expression patterns between tumor and normal groups (Figure 1G). Module-trait correlation analysis mapped the relationships between gene co-expression modules, CTHRC1, and clinical traits (Figure 2F). Scatter plot analysis confirmed a strong linear relationship between CTHRC1 and its top co-expressed gene (Figure 2G). The top 10 hub genes by degree centrality were: COL1A1, COL1A2, COL3A1, FN1, POSTN, among others (Figure 3B). Notably, all hub genes encode extracellular matrix structural or matricellular proteins, consistent with CTHRC1′s established role in ECM remodeling. Hub genes exhibited extensive co-expression, as shown by the correlation matrix (Figure 3C). Expression heatmaps of hub genes across ESCC samples revealed coordinated upregulation patterns (Figure 3D). KEGG enrichment of PPI network genes prominently featured ECM-receptor interaction, focal adhesion, and PI3K-Akt signaling (Figure 3E). The CTHRC1-centric subnetwork highlighted direct interactions with COL1A1, POSTN, SPARC, and MMP2/MMP9 (Figure 3F). GO Biological Process waterfall analysis further demonstrated enrichment in ECM organization and collagen fibril assembly (Figure 3G). Differential analysis between CTHRC1-positive and CTHRC1-negative fibroblasts demonstrated significant upregulation of collagen and matrix-related genes in CTHRC1+ fibroblasts (Figure 4H). Clinical association and prognostic significance of CTHRC1

CTHRC1 expression increased progressively with AJCC pathological stage (I-IV) (Kruskal-Wallis p < 0.05) (Figure 6A). Stratification by T and N stage revealed significantly elevated CTHRC1 in patients with advanced locoregional disease (Figure 6B). Kaplan-Meier analysis compared overall survival between CTHRC1-High and CTHRC1-Low groups (Figure 6C). Stage-stratified survival curves provided clinical context for prognostic evaluation (Figure 6D). Univariate Cox regression forest plot displayed hazard ratios and 95% confidence intervals for CTHRC1 and clinicopathological variables, with CTHRC1 HR > 1 indicating association with adverse prognosis (Figure 6E). Time-dependent ROC curves demonstrated CTHRC1′s discriminatory capacity at 1, 3, and 5 years (Figure 6F). CTHRC1 expression differed significantly between deceased and surviving patients (Figure 6G). Spearman correlation analysis with continuous clinical variables (age, stromal score, immune score) revealed additional dimensions of CTHRC1′s clinical relevance (Figure 6H). CTHRC1 mRNA expression is upregulated across multiple ESCC cell lines

CTHRC1 mRNA was consistently upregulated across all four ESCC cell lines relative to HEEC, with fold-increases ranking KYSE-30 (8.47-fold) > TE-1 (7.91-fold) > KYSE-150 (6.23-fold) > EC109 (5.18-fold; all p < 0.001; Figure 7A), and three independent siRNAs achieved robust knockdown in KYSE-30 cells (Figure 7B). Functionally, CTHRC1 silencing suppressed migration, invasion, and proliferation, while overexpression produced the opposite effects (Figures 7C–F), a pattern confirmed by Transwell quantification showing significantly reduced cell counts upon knockdown and increased counts upon overexpression (Figures 7G–H; all p < 0.001). Discussion

The present multi-layered bioinformatics and experimental investigation establishes CTHRC1 as a consistently overexpressed gene in ESCC with demonstrable and independent prognostic value. By harmonizing bulk transcriptomic profiling, co-expression network architecture, protein interactome mapping, single-cell transcriptomics, and in vitro cellular validation, we construct a comprehensive molecular portrait of CTHRC1 in ESCC pathobiology and patient outcome.

The reproducibility of CTHRC1 upregulation across three independent cohorts profiled on different platforms (TCGA RNA-seq, GSE53624 microarray, GSE23400 microarray) materially strengthens inferential confidence. Diagnostic AUC values exceeding 0.85 in each dataset indicate that transcriptomic CTHRC1 abundance alone distinguishes tumor from normal tissue with high fidelity, positioning it as a candidate molecular diagnostic marker pending prospective screening validation.

WGCNA co-expression data situate CTHRC1 within a gene module dominated by ECM homeostasis, collagen biosynthesis, and EMT—consistent with co-expression patterns documented for CTHRC1 in gastric, pancreatic, and colorectal cancer models [15,16]. It should be noted that WGCNA identifies co-expression relationships rather than regulatory causality; the pathway associations described here are correlative and require dedicated functional studies to establish causal roles in ESCC. The presence of PI3K-Akt and focal adhesion pathways within CTHRC1-correlated KEGG categories offers a potential mechanistic hypothesis linking CTHRC1 overexpression to cell survival, proliferative signaling, and matrix-directed motility in ESCC, warranting experimental validation.

PPI network topology discloses a hub architecture centered on structural collagens and matricellular proteins (COL1A1, COL1A2, COL3A1, POSTN, SPARC) that are well characterized as effectors of cancer-associated fibroblast activation, desmoplasia, and pro-invasive matrix remodeling [17,18]. The predicted STRING interactions between CTHRC1 and MMP2, MMP9, and MMP14 are based primarily on co-expression and text-mining evidence (combined confidence scores 0.71–0.83) rather than experimentally validated physical binding; these associations suggest a potential role in proteolytic ECM degradation, but direct protein–protein interaction in ESCC remains to be confirmed experimentally.

Cellular-resolution scRNA-seq data afford a particularly nuanced view of CTHRC1 biology by revealing its preferential enrichment in myofibroblastic CAFs within the ESCC tumor microenvironment [19]. The myCAF–iCAF expression dichotomy positions CTHRC1 as a mechanobiological rather than inflammatory mediator in the tumor stroma, with therapeutic implications: targeted CTHRC1 disruption might selectively impair ECM stiffening and biomechanical tumor-stromal coupling without broad suppression of inflammatory CAF functions.

Elevated CTHRC1 in a malignant epithelial subcluster introduces an autocrine dimension to its signaling repertoire. The paracrine (CAF-derived) and autocrine (tumor cell-intrinsic) production modalities may synergize to sustain a CTHRC1-rich pericellular milieu, reinforcing mutual tumor–stroma activation through ECM receptor engagement as captured in our CellChat analysis.

The tight statistical linkage of CTHRC1 with advanced T stage, lymph node positivity, and poor histological differentiation is biologically plausible: CTHRC1-associated ECM remodeling and EMT-related gene co-expression patterns may contribute to local invasion and lymphovascular permeation, though causal mechanisms require in vivo validation [20]. The particularly robust N-stage association is clinically consequential, since nodal burden governs both surgical eligibility and adjuvant treatment decisions in ESCC.

Multivariable Cox modeling confirms that CTHRC1 contributes prognostic information orthogonal to pathological stage and nodal status—the cornerstones of current prognostic algorithms. An adjusted HR of 1.78 represents a clinically meaningful risk gradient. Prognostic nomogram analysis demonstrated that incorporating CTHRC1 alongside conventional staging variables improved the C-index from 0.698 to 0.742, quantifying the incremental benefit of molecular biomarker integration.

The serial increase in time-dependent AUC from 0.682 at 1 year to 0.748 at 5 years suggests that CTHRC1’s prognostic signal strengthens over longer follow-up horizons, possibly reflecting its sustained role in processes such as metastatic niche preparation and late-stage disease evolution that accumulate over time. It should be acknowledged that these AUC values reflect moderate rather than exceptional discriminatory performance; CTHRC1 alone is unlikely to replace established staging systems, and its principal clinical value lies in incremental risk refinement within stage-defined subgroups. Integration into multi-marker panels incorporating complementary molecular features will likely be necessary to achieve clinically actionable predictive accuracy.

In vitro, the multi-dimensional experimental evidence obtained in this study directly corroborates and extends the bioinformatics predictions. qRT-PCR established consistent mRNA upregulation across all ESCC lines tested, and IF co-staining with Pan-CK confirmed cancer-selective protein overexpression. Loss- and gain-of-function IF experiments in KYSE-30 cells linked CTHRC1 abundance functionally to Vimentin and MMP9 regulation—supporting CTHRC1’s role in EMT and matrix invasion programs in this cellular model, though transcriptional regulatory mechanisms remain to be elucidated. Primary CAF IF further validated the scRNA-seq prediction of myCAF-dominant CTHRC1 expression, and the contrasting absence of α-SMA in CTHRC1-high ESCC cells delineated the dual stromal and epithelial origins of CTHRC1 in the tumor microenvironment.

The characteristics of CTHRC1 as a therapeutically tractable target merit consideration. Its extracellular secreted nature renders it susceptible to antibody-based neutralization strategies without intracellular delivery challenges. Its expression is largely restricted to injury-response contexts in normal adult physiology [21], suggesting an acceptable therapeutic index. By simultaneously being associated with ECM remodeling, EMT, angiogenesis, and tumor–stromal crosstalk, CTHRC1 inhibition could exert pleiotropic anti-tumor effects. Preclinical evaluation of monoclonal antibodies targeting secreted CTHRC1 in ESCC models represents a logical translational extension; identification of the cognate cell-surface receptor(s) mediating CTHRC1 signaling on ESCC cells is an important prerequisite for rational small-molecule targeting strategies. Beyond therapeutic targeting, the secreted nature of CTHRC1 raises the possibility of circulating protein detection in serum or plasma as a non-invasive biomarker strategy. Although the present study focused on tissue-level transcriptomic evidence using existing public resources, serum ELISA measurement of circulating CTHRC1 in prospective ESCC cohorts represents a compelling liquid biopsy avenue. ESCC is accessible to endoscopic surveillance, and combined tissue/circulating biomarker strategies may ultimately offer greater clinical utility than tissue-only approaches, particularly for post-treatment monitoring and minimal residual disease detection.

Beyond therapeutic targeting, CTHRC1 expression may serve as a patient-selection biomarker in trials of agents directed against the tumor microenvironment or ECM remodeling pathways, enabling biomarker-enriched study designs that maximize treatment response signal.

This study carries several inherent limitations. The retrospective computational design necessitates prospective clinical validation. Protein-level evidence from immunohistochemistry or Western blot in primary tissue specimens would strengthen translational confidence beyond the mRNA and cell-line data presented. The functional mechanistic inferences drawn from WGCNA and PPI analysis are correlative by design; dedicated in vitro and in vivo experiments are required to establish causal relationships. The prognostic nomogram achieved an incremental C-index improvement (0.698 to 0.742) over stage-only models, but external validation in an independent cohort is required before clinical deployment can be considered. The scRNA-seq analysis relied on only four ESCC specimens from GSE160269; while myCAF-enriched CTHRC1 expression was consistent across all four donors, the limited sample size and possible under-representation of ESCC molecular subtypes (basal vs. classical) constrain generalizability. The STRING-derived CTHRC1–MMP interactions represent computational predictions rather than experimentally confirmed physical associations, and the cognate cell-surface receptor(s) mediating CTHRC1 signaling on ESCC cells have not been identified. Finally, the scRNA-seq cohort size and composition may not exhaustively capture CTHRC1 expression heterogeneity across the full range of ESCC molecular subtypes and clinical stages.

Priority avenues for future inquiry include: (1) prospective biomarker validation in large multi-institutional ESCC cohorts with standardized clinical endpoints; (2) mechanistic dissection of CTHRC1 oncogenic functions using genetically engineered mouse models and patient-derived organoids; (3) assessment of CTHRC1 as a predictive biomarker for emerging anti-stromal and immune-based therapies; (4) development of CTHRC1-directed therapeutics for preclinical efficacy testing; and (5) construction of multi-marker prognostic panels integrating CTHRC1 with complementary molecular features.

In summary, the present study furnishes multi-dimensional evidence—spanning genomic co-expression networks, protein interaction topology, single-cell transcriptomics, and cellular experimentation—that CTHRC1 is a robustly upregulated, prognostically relevant molecule in ESCC. Its convergent associations with ECM remodeling, tumor–stromal communication, lymph node metastasis, and diminished survival nominate CTHRC1 as both a clinically actionable prognostic biomarker and a mechanistically grounded therapeutic target, offering a substantive foundation for translational studies directed toward improving outcomes in this aggressive malignancy.

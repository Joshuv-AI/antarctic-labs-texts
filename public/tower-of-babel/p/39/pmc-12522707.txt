Prediction of intraductal cancer microinfiltration based on the hierarchical fusion of peri-tumor imaging histology and dual view deep learning
Authors: Guoyan Yao, Yan Huang, Xiaojing Shang, Lei Guo, Jieping Feng, Zhongqiu Lai, Weikang Huang, Jianye Lu, Lijun Chen, Minan zheng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThe aim of this study was to develop a multimodal fusion model for accurate risk prediction and clinical decision support for ductal carcinoma in-situ (DCIS).MethodBy integrating deep learning (DL), radiomics and clinical features, this study constructed the combined model and validated its performance in a multicenter cohort containing 232 patients (103 in the training set, 43 in the validation set and 86 in the external test set).ResultsUnimodal DL models showed significant overfitting in external tests (e.g., DenseNet201 training set AUC = 0.85 vs. test set 1 AUC = 0.47), whereas the multimodal fusion model achieved optimal predictive performance across cohorts through heterogeneous data synergy (training set AUC = 0.925, test set 2 AUC = 0.801) with the DeLong test confirmed that it significantly outperformed the unimodal model (P < 0.05). Grad-CAM visualization showed that the model focus region was highly consistent with the radiologist annotation (81% overlap, Cohen’s κ = 0.68). Calibration curves (Hosmer-Leeshawn test P > 0.05) and decision curve analysis (DCA) validated the model prediction reliability (error < 5%) with a net clinical benefit advantage (net benefit difference of 7% to 28% at thresholds of 5% to 80%).ConclusionThe multimodal fusion strategy could potentially mitigate the limitations of unimodal models, presenting a potential solution with promising high accuracy and interpretability for individualized diagnosis and treatment of DCIS.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-025-15054-3.

Introduction

Ductal Carcinoma in Situ (DCIS) is an early non-invasive form of breast cancer, but the presence of microinvasion (MI) has been shown to significantly affect clinical treatment decisions and prognostic assessment [1–3]. Although pathological biopsy is the gold standard for the diagnosis of microinvasion, its invasiveness, sampling error and time cost limit its clinical application [4]. DCIS was selected as the focus of this study due to its unique clinical challenges: while it is non-invasive, the presence of MI upstages the disease to invasive carcinoma, necessitating more aggressive treatment (e.g., sentinel lymph node biopsy). However, accurately identifying MI preoperatively remains difficult. Mammography, the primary screening tool, has limited sensitivity (60–70%) for detecting MI, often leading to either underdiagnosis (missing MI) or overtreatment (overtreating pure DCIS) [5, 6]. This clinical dilemma underscores the urgent need for a non-invasive, high-precision predictive tool specifically for DCIS with MI. Furthermore, compared to invasive breast cancers (e.g., invasive ductal carcinoma), where invasion is already established, DCIS presents a critical window for early intervention and risk-adapted therapy. Its heterogeneity and the subtlety of microinvasion make it an ideal model to test the capability of advanced multimodal AI approaches in capturing nuanced imaging and biological signatures. Consequently, there is an urgent need to develop high-precision, non-invasive predictive models to enhance the diagnostic and treatment strategies for DCIS.

In recent years, radiomics has provided a systematic perspective for revealing tumour heterogeneity through high-throughput extraction of morphological, textural and functional features of tumor regions [7, 8]. Research has demonstrated that imaging features in the peritumoral region (PTR) can serve as a reflection of dynamic changes in the tumour microenvironment, including angiogenesis, immune infiltration and stromal remodeling, which are closely related to tumor invasiveness [9–11]. Lee et al. found that peritumoral texture features could effectively predict lymph node metastasis in breast cancer [12]. However, the majority of extant studies have focused on intratumoural features, and systematic mining of the peritumour region is still in its infancy and lacks synergistic integration with deep learning [13–15]. Conversely, the utilization of deep learning (DL) has demonstrated notable advantages in the classification of mammography images [16]. The joint analysis of dual views (mid-lateral oblique (MLO) and craniocaudal (CC)) can reduce the interference of overlapping anatomical structures in a single view and improve the lesion detection rate [17, 18]. However, traditional Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) models tend to rely on intratumorally regions of interest (ROIs) and ignore information about spatial and temporal heterogeneity in the peritumour region. Furthermore, single-modality approaches (e.g., utilizing only imaging or clinical data) are inadequate in fully capturing the complexity of tumour biological behaviours, emphasizing the urgent need for multimodality fusion strategies to enhance model robustness.

Although several models for predicting DCIS prognosis and invasion risk have been developed, they predominantly rely on single-modality data, which limits their predictive accuracy and clinical applicability. For instance, clinical models based on traditional biomarkers often lack sensitivity, while radiomics models focusing solely on intratumoral features may overlook critical biological information from the peritumoral microenvironment. DL models, particularly those using single-view mammography, have shown promise but are often hampered by overfitting and poor generalizability to external datasets. Crucially, there is a lack of models that systematically integrate peritumoral radiomics, dual-view deep learning, and clinical parameters in a hierarchical manner to leverage their complementary strengths. This gap in the literature highlights the need for a robust, interpretable, and multimodal framework to improve the preoperative prediction of MI in DCIS.

Based on this, this study proposes an innovative multimodal hierarchical fusion framework, aiming to address the following key issues by systematically integrating peri-tumour imaging histology, dual-view deep learning and clinical parameters: firstly, to address the limitations of peri-tumour dynamic information mining, by defining peri-tumour extended region (3–5 mm annular area), extracting its imaging histological features (including shape features, texture features, wavelet energy and LoS transform, etc.), and screening key features with biological relevance by combining with Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) regression to quantify the heterogeneity of the tumour microenvironment; secondly, to solve the problem of inconsistent feature expression in dual views (MLO and CC), a dual-channel architecture is designed to be constructed based on pre-trained networks (ResNet50, DenseNet121, and DenseNet201), and by dynamically Finally, in order to maximize the complementarity of multimodal data, an “early-intermediate-late” hierarchical fusion strategy is proposed, in which peri-tumour histological features and clinical parameters are spliced into the logistic regression model at the early fusion stage, and the fully connected layer is used to achieve the deep learning feature expression consistency problem in the middle fusion stage. In the mid-term fusion stage, a fully connected layer is used to achieve the interaction mapping between deep learning features and histological features, and in the late-stage fusion stage, a stacking integration algorithm is used to optimize the prediction probability of the early-stage and mid-term models, which significantly improves the sensitivity of the models to microinvasive markers. This framework provides a systematic approach to the systematic synergy of peritumour genomics, dual-view deep learning and clinical features, which not only provides a high-precision tool for non-invasive prediction of DCIS microinfiltration but also lays a methodological foundation for the scalability and clinical translation of multimodal analysis of medical images with the layered fusion paradigm.

Materials and methods

Data sources and groupings

This study was conducted in accordance with the ethical standards of the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Guangdong Provincial Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine (Approval No. ZE2021-111). As this study was retrospective in nature, the requirement for informed consent was waived. Mammography images from local tertiary care hospitals were included, and all cases were pathologically diagnosed as DCIS and labelled with MI status. Patients were excluded if they had a history of prior breast cancer treatment (e.g., surgery, radiotherapy, or chemotherapy), significant comorbidities (e.g., active infectious diseases or other malignancies), or incomplete clinical or imaging data. The training set (70%) and the internal validation set (30%) are randomly divided in a 7:3 ratio, and two independent external test sets are introduced to verify the model generalizability. All datasets are strictly partitioned at the patient level, ensuring no overlap of patients or images across training, validation, and test sets. All data processing processes ensure that the patient identity information is untraceable through desensitization techniques. The inclusion criteria comprised a histopathological confirmation of a diagnosis of DCIS, with image quality that met the requisite diagnostic standards (absence of motion artefacts, resolution ≥ 0.1 mm/pixel).

Image annotation was conducted by two radiologists, working independently and utilizing ITK-SNAP software to delineate the intratumoural and peritumoral ROIs for both the MLO and CC views. Discrepancies in annotation were resolved through consensus discussion and arbitration by senior radiologists, with a minimum of 20 years of experience in the field. For other imaging sequences, while automatic alignment was performed by ITK-SNAP, manual refinement was also performed by professional radiologists to ensure high accuracy and alignment consistency of the imaging data. The annotations by the two radiologists yielded a Dice coefficient of 0.956, indicating substantial inter-rater agreement.

Preprocessing and ROI segmentation

Firstly, the images were normalized using parameters derived exclusively from the training set. Specifically, the percentile range (0.5% to 99.5%) for intensity clipping and the mean and standard deviation for Z-score normalization were computed from the training cohort and then applied to all other sets to prevent data leakage. For ROI processing, representative slices containing the largest lesion area were selected for each patient, and the smallest rectangular area containing the ROI was retained based on the minimum bounding rectangle cropping technique with cropping parameters determined from the training set. The cropped images of the MLO and CC views were combined into a two-channel input. In the data augmentation strategy, real-time enhancement techniques (including random horizontal/vertical flipping and ± 10% translation cropping) were applied only during the training phase. During testing, only the normalization transformation (using training-set-derived parameters) was applied without any augmentation, ensuring no information from the test set was used during model development.

Feature extraction and model construction

In this study, a transfer learning strategy is adopted to make full use of the generalization ability of the pre-trained model. To this end, a dual-channel network is constructed based on ResNet50, DenseNet121 and DenseNet201 architectures to process the input images of the MLO and CC views independently. The common features across the views are extracted by sharing the underlying convolutional layers while preserving view specificity information. To address the issue of feature misalignment, which is caused by the disparity in spatial location of dual-view ROIs, a Spatial Attention Module has been developed. This module is designed to calculate the spatial weight distribution of the feature maps of the MLO and CC views. The purpose of this calculation is to align and match the cross-view ROI regions through the weighted fusion of the feature maps. This process serves to enhance the consistency of the feature expression of the multi-views. For further technical details, please refer to the supplementary material. In summary, the joint prediction probability of the two-channel network is output through the SoftMax layer. This is defined as the DL Signature, which provides a comprehensive characterization of the morphological and deep semantic features of the lesion. This provides a highly discriminative basis for subsequent multimodal fusion.

Crucially, our approach differs from existing multimodal studies not only in the types of inputs fused (peritumoral radiomics, dual-view DL, clinical) but, more importantly, in the methodologies developed for fusion and feature alignment. Beyond simple input concatenation or late fusion, we introduce a dedicated Spatial Attention Module to address the critical challenge of feature misalignment between MLO and CC views, an issue often overlooked in prior work. Furthermore, we propose a hierarchical “early-intermediate-late” fusion strategy, which is architecturally distinct from common fusion paradigms. This strategy is meticulously designed to maximize complementary information extraction at different feature abstraction levels—addressing semantic gaps between handcrafted radiomics and deep features through interactive mapping—rather than treating all modalities uniformly.

In the radiomics module, the Radiomics tool was first used to systematically extract multidimensional imaging histological features of the peri-tumour area (defined as the circumference of 3–5 mm outside the tumour boundary), including morphological features (e.g. sphericity, surface area), first-order statistics (e.g. skewness, kurtosis), textural features (based on grey-scale covariance matrix (GLCM) and grey-scale size region matrix (GLSZM)), wavelet transform energy and local binary patterns. GLSZM), wavelet transform energy and local binary patterns, Subsequently, features significantly associated with microinfiltration status (significance threshold P < 0.05) were screened using LASSO regression coupled with a 10-fold cross-validation method, and highly specific radiomics features were constructed. In the clinical model construction, clinicopathological parameters such as patient age, BI-RADS grading, hormone receptor status (ER/PR) and HER-2 expression were integrated, and the Clinical Signature was trained using the Random Forest algorithm to capture the predictive potential of traditional biomarkers. (See supplementary material.)

In order to achieve the synergistic optimization of multimodal information, this study proposes a layered fusion strategy: in the early fusion stage, radiomics features are directly spliced with clinical signatures and fed into a logistic regression model to generate preliminary joint prediction probabilities; in the middle fusion stage, a fully-connected layer is used to achieve the interactive mapping between the deep learning features (from a dual-channel ResNet) and radiomics features to mine the cross-modal potential associations; and finally, in the later stage, a Stacking integration approach is used to integrate the clinical signatures. fusion stage, XGBoost is used as a meta-model to integrate the early fusion probabilities with the mid-term interactive features to generate a Combined Signature with comprehensive predictive capabilities, thus maximizing the sensitivity and robustness of the model to microinfiltration markers.

Model validation and interpretability

In the context of model performance assessment, this study employed multidimensional metrics to validate the predictive efficacy of the model. The primary assessment metrics encompassed the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy, which were utilized to quantify the overall discriminative ability of the model. With regard to clinical utility, the net benefit of the model was calculated by DCA for varying risk thresholds to assess its clinical decision-making value, and a calibration curve was employed to test the consistency between the predicted probability and the true microinfiltration risk to ensure the reliability of the probability output. In order to enhance the interpretability of the model, gradient-weighted class activation mapping (Grad-CAM) was used to generate heat maps to visualize the regions of interest of the deep learning model in the dual-view images and verify its spatial correlation with pathological microinfiltration markers. Meanwhile, the contribution of radiological features to the prediction results was analyzed based on Shapley Additive Explanations (SHAP) values, combined with logistic regression. Then combined these with the OR value and confidence interval of the logistic regression model.

Statistical analyses

Normality of clinical characteristics was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Continuous variables were analyzed by t-test or Mann-Whitney U-test depending on their distribution, while categorical variables were assessed by Chi-square (χ²) test. Model comparisons were made by the DeLong test (AUC difference). P-values < 0.05 were considered significant. All data analyses were performed on the OnekeyAI platform (version 3.9.1) using Python (version 3.7.12). Statistical analyses were performed using Statsmodels (version 0.13.2) and radiomics feature extraction was performed using PyRadiomics (version 3.0.1). Deep learning models were developed using PyTorch (version 1.11.0) and optimized using CUDA (version 11.3.1) and cuDNN (version 8.2.1) to maximize computational efficiency (Fig. 1).

Results

Baseline characteristics of the study cohort and data distribution

A total of 232 patients with pathologically confirmed DCIS were included in this study, and the data were obtained from a multicenter collaborative project, which was divided into a training set (103 cases, 44.40%), an internal validation set (43 cases, 18.53%), and two independent external test sets in a ratio of 7:3 (test set 1: 49 cases, 21.12%; test set 2: 37 cases, 15.95%). The mean age of the patients was 51.23 ± 10.23 years, with no significant differences between the groups (P = 0.818). The mean length of lactation was 8.28 ± 8.07 months with no significant difference (P = 0.96). A total of 71.12% (165/232) of patients had no family history of breast cancer, and 44.83% (104/232) had a history of one pregnancy, with no significant differences in distribution between the groups. The surgical rate was 80.60% (187/232), and 63.79% (148/232) of patients showed no abnormal signs on palpation examination. In terms of imaging features, 72.41% (168/232) of patients showed dense glandular tissue (72.82% in the training set and 74.42% in the validation set), whereas the proportion of sparse glands was significantly higher to 21.62% in test set 2, suggesting potential heterogeneity in the distribution of glandular density between the different cohorts. Performance advantages of multimodal fusion models

Among the individual models, the DL signature consistently achieves the highest AUC values in both validation and test cohorts. In the validation cohort, the DL signature attains an AUC of 0.751 (95% CI: 0.6050–0.8973), outperforming the Clinical (AUC: 0.627) and Radiomics (AUC: 0.731) signatures. This trend continues in the external test cohorts, where the DL signature achieves AUC of 0.735 (test1) and 0.717 (test2), significantly higher than the Clinical and Radiomics signatures. However, the Combined signature, which integrates the DL, Radiomics, and Clinical models, demonstrates the best overall performance. In the training cohort, the Combined signature achieves an AUC of 0.925 (95% CI: 0.7861–0.935), surpassing all individual models. This superiority is maintained in the validation (AUC: 0.858) and test cohorts (AUC: 0.751 in test1 and 0.801 in test2), with narrower confidence intervals indicating greater reliability. The results highlight the superior performance of the DL signature compared to the Clinical and Radiomics signatures, particularly in validation and external test cohorts, where it demonstrates higher AUC values and robust generalization capabilities. However, the Combined signature, which leverages the strengths of all three models, achieves the best overall performance, with the highest AUC values and more precise confidence intervals across all cohorts. This suggests that integrating multiple data modalities and modeling approaches significantly enhances predictive accuracy and reliability. As shows in Table 3 and Fig. 3.

Discussion

In this study, we developed and validated a multimodal fusion model for the preoperative prediction of MI in DCIS. Our approach features a hierarchical integration of peritumoral radiomics, dual-view deep learning, and clinical features, which to our knowledge has not been extensively explored for this specific clinical challenge. We systematically explored the synergistic effects of DL, radiomics and clinical features in the risk prediction of DCIS through multimodal fusion model construction and validation and deeply evaluated the clinical translational potential of the model. The study found that the combined model integrating DL, radiomics, and clinical features demonstrated significantly better predictive performance than the unimodal model in the training, validation, and external test cohorts, and the Delong test further confirmed that the difference in AUC was statistically significant (P < 0.05). This result is consistent with several recent studies. Li et al. found that localization accuracy for tumour segmentation and risk stratification for survival prediction could be improved by multimodal data [19]. And the core mechanism lies in the complementary nature of the heterogeneous data sources - the DL features are good at capturing the higher-order nonlinear patterns in images (such as the spatial distribution of microcalcification clusters), radiomics quantifies interpretable textural features such as tumour heterogeneity, and clinical features integrate patient phenotypic and medical history information, which together cover a multidimensional information space ranging from microimaging features to macroscopic clinical risk factors [20–22]. Notably, the unimodal DL models (e.g. DenseNet201) in this study exhibited plummeting performance on external test sets (training set AUC = 0.85 vs. test set 1 AUC = 0.47), suggesting a possible over-reliance on training set-specific features (e.g., imaging device-specific noises), whereas the multimodal fusion, by constraining the complexity of the sub models and optimizing the integration weights, the significantly mitigated such overfitting risks (36% to 58% reduction in test set AUC reduction). This finding provides important insights for clinical AI model development: the limitations of single data modality or modelling approaches can be systematically circumvented by cross-disciplinary feature fusion strategies.

Although DL models are often regarded as a “black box”, this study revealed a high degree of consistency between the decision logic of the multimodal fusion model and clinical perceptions through the high overlap between Grad-CAM visualizations and radiologists’ annotated ROIs (81%, κ = 0.68). The model’s focus on textural features at the edge of the lesion may reflect its ability to recognize the tendency of DCIS microinfiltration, while the internal heterogeneous activation areas may correspond to imaging markers related to histological grading. This result is consistent with other studies, which have shown that clinical adoption of AI models can increase by more than 40% when their activation patterns are consistent with physicians’ experience [23, 24]. In addition, calibration curves and Hosmer-Lemeshow tests (HL values did not reject the original hypothesis, P >0.05) showed that the model predicted probabilities were highly matched to the actual risks, especially in the key clinical decision thresholds (e.g., 20%−80% risk interval) with an error of less than 5%, which avoids the risk of over-treatment or under-diagnosis due to the distortion of probabilities. Together, this evidence supports the reliability of the model in real-life scenarios.

DCA showed that the net benefit of the combined model was significantly better than that of the “all treat” or “no treat” strategies (with a difference of 7%−28%) over a wide range of risk thresholds (5%−80%), suggesting that it can assist physicians in optimizing individualized treatment decisions. In low-risk DCIS patients (< 20% predictive probability), the model supported avoidance of unnecessary radical surgery, whereas in high-risk patients (>60% predictive probability). In addition, nomogram transform complex algorithms into actionable clinical tools by intuitively integrating multimodal contribution weights, allowing physicians to quickly estimate risk without relying on specialized software. This is in line with most of the rationale put forward for lowering the barrier to use by simplifying interaction design and accelerating the integration of AI into existing diagnostic and therapeutic processes [25, 26].

Despite the progress made in this study in terms of validation and interpretable analysis of multicenter data, the following limitations still need to be addressed: firstly, sample size and distributional bias, the relatively small sample size of the external test set (only 37 cases in test set 2) and the heterogeneity of the distribution of glandular densities between the cohorts (with 21.62% of glandular sparseness in test set 2), which may affect the performance of model generalization. This may affect the overall assessment of the generalization performance of the model. In the future, we need to expand multicenter data across regions and devices to enhance generalizability. Second, We observed notable performance fluctuations between the training and external test cohorts: Specificity decreased from 75.6% in the training set to 50.0% in test set 2, indicating a potential increase in false positives and reduced reliability when applied to new populations, The AUC declined from 0.925 (training) to 0.801 (test set 2), suggesting the need for cautious clinical interpretation and underscores the necessity for further validation on larger cohorts. The third, real-time validation is still lacking, the clinical utility of column mapping needs to be validated by prospective cohort studies, especially its long-term impact on the efficiency of physicians’ decision-making and the prognosis of actual patients.

Conclusion

This proof-of-concept study demonstrates that the multimodal fusion approach improves predictive performance over unimodal methods (DeLong test P < 0.05). While showing technical feasibility for clinical translation, further validation is required before implementation.

Exogenous proline regulates the antioxidation system and fruit quality of cherry tomatoes under chilling storage: Integrated physiological, metabolomic, and transcriptomic study
Authors: Suihua Huang, Quanqing Deng, Kaixin Bao, Jianneng Chen, Xuqiao Deng, Xu Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Cherry tomatoes fruits suffer from chilling stress during storage. Proline (Pro) is a vital amino acid to alleviate the stress. However, the biochemical mechanisms remained unclear. A field experiment was conducted with spraying distilled water (T0) or Pro solution during the growth period (T1); mature fruits were sampled at the date of harvest (D0) and stored at 4 °C for 20 d after harvest (D1). Results showed that the alanine content was increased by 1.7 %–8.0 % while the arginine content was reduced by 12.7 %–61.5 % under D1T1 compared with D1T0. The sugar acid ratio in D0T1 vs D1T1 was reduced to 65.9 % lower than that in D0T0 vs D1T0, although Pro treatment decreased the sugar acid ratio. Multivariate analysis revealed that PC1 accounted for 93.1 % in principal component analysis. The enzymatic activity of superoxide dismutase, ascorbate peroxidase, the amino acid content of glutamate and γ-aminobutyric acid were positively related to Pro content. Integrated metabolomic and transcriptomic analysis revealed that differentially expressed metabolites and genes were enriched in phenylalanine metabolism and lignin biosynthesis pathways. CCoAOMT was a putative gene responsible for the changes. Applications of Pro regulated the level of relevant amino acids and antioxidants, preserving the flavor quality after chilling storage in cherry tomatoes.

Introduction

Tomatoes are widely cultivated horticultural crops beloved worldwide for their delicious flavor and nutritional benefits; they come in a range of varieties, for instance, beefsteak tomato, roma tomato, grape tomato, cherry tomato, etc. (Candir et al., 2017; Sun et al., 2017), and each variety offers a unique taste and culinary experience. In recent years, it has been notable that consumers pay attention to the disparity of taste in modern commercial tomato varieties compared with the traditional tomato varieties (Wang et al., 2023). In China, cherry tomatoes dominated a 36 % share of global tomato production in 2023 (https://www.reportlinker.com). In addition, cherry tomatoes which were exceptional sweetness balanced with a hint of sourness, contained abundant and various substances that contributed to their nutritional profiles and distinctive taste, such as soluble solids, sugar, acid, amino acids, minerals, and volatile compounds, etc. (Jin et al., 2023). Ensuring the optimal storage condition and transportation of cherry tomatoes is vital to preserving their vibrant color, firm texture, freshness, and delectable taste (Liu et al., 2019).

Cold storage is commonly utilized for horticultural product preservation to extend the shelf-life. Cherry tomatoes are sensitive to low temperatures and suffer damage if stored below 12 °C (Li et al., 2016). Typically, refrigerators designed for fruit and vegetable preservation maintain a temperature of around 4 °C, which is lower than the suitable temperature for storing cherry tomatoes. Previous studies indicated that storing tomatoes at 4 °C led to changes in fruit quality and nutritional properties, including alterations in soluble solids content and antioxidant activities (Patanè et al., 2019). Chilling increased reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels in tomato fruits and diminished their nutritional traits (Li et al., 2023). To meet the evolving preferences of consumers, it is imperative to address the challenges posed by low temperatures and develop strategies to mitigate injury while preserving the quality and flavor of cherry tomatoes.

Proline (Pro) stands out as a pivotal amino acid that exerts a remarkable influence on the physio-biological process of plants, fulfilling a range of essential functions, including regulating osmotic adjustment, scavenging excessive free radicals, and maintaining redox balance (Alvarez et al., 2022). To date, considerable endeavors have been made that exposure to abiotic stresses such as drought, salinity, heavy metal, irrigation, and chilling could regulate the levels of Pro present in tomato plants (Ahammed et al., 2020; De la Torre-González et al., 2018; Jin et al., 2023; Li et al., 2016; Nazir et al., 2021). Previous studies have shown that endogenous Pro was regarded as the barometer of stress intensity (Claussen, 2005) while exogenous Pro could release the stress by salinity in tomatoes (Heuer, 2003) and abiotic tolerance in other crops (Hosseinifard et al., 2022). Besides, it is noteworthy that the flavor and taste of horticultural crops are intricately linked to the presence of specific amino acids responsible for taste perception (Wang et al., 2020). Specifically, Pro contributes to forming the desired taste profile of tomatoes by participating in interactions with other taste-active amino acids, like the umami amino acid glutamate (Glu) and the bitter amino acid arginine (Arg), thereby enhancing the overall flavor profile (Alvarez et al., 2022; Liao et al., 2022; Qian et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2020). However, there is still limited knowledge on whether exogenous Pro can enhance the postharvest storage and fruit quality.

The defense mechanism of cherry tomatoes against low-temperature storage involved various compounds and genetic factors (Wang et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2016). Recently, integrative technology of metabolome and transcriptome analysis was employed to reveal the color and taste development mechanism in cherry tomatoes (Li et al., 2022), which offered a practical approach to explore whether Pro modulated the antioxidant and amino acid level to promote the quality for better preservation in the present study. Therefore, the present study aimed to investigate whether the application of Pro could enhance the capacity of ROS scavenging in cherry tomatoes and to explore the response mechanisms of potential metabolites and related genes under chilling stress conditions. This study provided valuable insights into the effectiveness of Pro in optimizing storage practices and preserving the flavor and quality of cherry tomatoes for extended storage period.

Materials and methods

Experimental set-up

A field experiment was conducted in Dadaosha Village, Guangdong Province, China (22.9°N, 113.5°E), which enjoyed a climate of humid subtropical monsoon, from September to November in 2022. Two cherry tomatoes viz, Huangjinguo (HJG) and Zixia (ZX) were used as the plant materials, whose seeds were obtained by Panyu Xiyue Agricultural Development Co., Ltd. (Guangzhou, China). L-Proline powder (dry basis assay ≥98.5 %) was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich Trading Co. Ltd. (Shanghai, China).

The experimental treatments consisted of the cherry tomato plants with spraying distilled water (T0) or Pro solution during growth period (T1) and the cherry tomato fruits sampled at the date of harvest (D0) and stored at 4 °C for 20 d after harvest (D1). The sterilized seeds were sown in plastic trays on September 1st, 2022 and the 30-day-old seedlings were transplanted at inter-row spacing of 20 cm and intra-row spacing of 15 cm in an open filed. Each treatment was replicated three times with each plot covering an area of 6 m2. The cherry tomato leaves were sprayed with 1 g L−1 Pro solution (L-proline was dissolved by distilled water) on the 30th, 40th, and 50th day after transplanting. The cherry tomato fruits of similar sizes were harvested during the ripening stage on December 16th, 2022. The tomato cultivation and field management were recording to the regular cherry tomato cultivation procedures. The fruit peel and pulp of harvested and stored cherry tomatoes were liquid nitrogen frozen for 30 s and kept at −80 °C for the detection of the following parameters.

Quantification of amino acid

Fresh tissues (100 mg) were added in centrifuge tube with 1 mL distilled water, extracted in ultrasound bath for 30 min, and centrifuged at 12000 rpm for 10 min under 4 °C. The High Performance Liquid Chromatography-Mass Spectrometry (HPLC/MS, Shimadzu, Kyoto, Japan) were operated to detect the free amino acids by Shengyangbiotech. Co., Ltd. (Qingdao, China). The detailed instrument settings were shown in Supplementary Materials. The amino acids included glycine (Gly), valine (Val), tyrosine (Tyr), threonine (Thr), phenprobamate (Phe), serine (Ser), methionine (Met), lysine (Lys), leucine (Leu), isoleucine (ILE), glutamate (Glu), Pro, aspartic (Asp), arginine (Arg), alanine (Ala), cysteine (Cys), asparagine (Asn), glutamine (Gln), tryptophan (Trp), histidine (His), ornithine (Orn) and γ-aminobutyric acid (GABA). Three biological replicates were performed for each amino acid quantification.

Analysis of metabolome

The samples of cherry tomato fruits were sent to Gene Denovo Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Guangzhou, China) for metabolome analysis. Fresh tissues in triplicate were extracted with 1 mL 70 % aqueous methanol at 4 °C for 12 h. After centrifugation (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Bremen, Germany) at 10000 g for 10 min, the extraction was absorbed by 250 mg CNWBOND Carbon-GCB SPE cartridge (ANPEL, Shanghai, China) and filtrated by 0.22 μm organic phase nylon needle filter (ANPEL, Shanghai, China) before being analyzed by Liquid Chromatography-Tandem Mass Spectrometry (LC-MS/MS, SCIEX, USA) system. The instrument settings, the identification for metabolites, and the total ion chromatogram (TIC) were shown in Supplementary Materials. Positive ion mode (POS) and negative ion mode (NEG) were both used to detect metabolites and a variable importance in projection (VIP) score of Orthogonal Partial Least Squares Discriminant Analysis (OPLS-DA) model was applied to rank the metabolites that best distinguished between two groups. t-Test was used as a univariate analysis for screening differentially expressed metabolites (DEMs). Those with a p value of t-test<0.05 and VIP ≥ 1 were considered DEMs between two groups.

Analysis of transcriptome

Total RNA of fresh tissues in triplicate were extracted using Trizol reagent kit (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, United States). The quality was accessed by Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer (Agilent Technologies, Palo Alto, CA, United States). The cDNA fragments were purified with QiaQuick PCR extraction kit (Qiagen, Venlo, Netherland). The ligation products were sequenced using Illumina HiSeq2500 by Gene Denovo Biotechnology Co. (Guangzhou, China). The genes with the parameter of false discovery rate (FDR) below 0.05 and absolute fold change≥2 were considered differentially expressed genes (DEGs). Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis were performed in this study. The raw sequencing data was submitted to the NCBI Gene Expression Omnibus under project NO. CRA025298.

Measurement of hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) content and antioxidant enzymatic activities

After the freeze-dried tissues were grounded into powder, 500 mg of samples were mixed with 9 mL of 50 mM phosphate buffered saline (PBS, pH 7.8), centrifuged (Eppendorf, 5810R, Hamburg, Germany) at 8000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C, the supernatant was obtained. H2O2 content was measured according to Huang et al. (2023), approximately 200 μL of the above supernatant mixed with 0.5 μM ammonium ferrous sulfate, 200 mM sorbitol, 200 μM xylenol orange, and 50 mM sulfuric acid. The mixture was incubated in the dark for 45 min and read at 560 nm. The activities of superoxide dismutase (SOD), peroxidase (POD), and catalase (CAT) were determined by the methods of Huang et al. (2023). For SOD activity, 0.05 mL of the above supernatant was mixed with 0.3 mL of 130 mM L-methionine, 0.3 mL of 750 μM nitro blue tetrazolium (NBT), 0.3 mL of disodium edetate, 0.3 mL of 20 μM riboflavin, and 1.5 mL of 0.05 M PBS (pH 7.8). The mixture after 50 % inhibition in the initial rate of reduction of NBT was detected at 560 nm in a microplate reader (Biotek, Epoch, Vermont, United States). For POD activity, the mixture contained 1 mL of 0.05 M PBS (pH 7.0), 1 mL of 0.3 % H2O2, 0.05 mL of the above supernatant, and 0.95 mL of 0.2 % H2O2 was read at 470 nm for 1.5 min with an interval of 0.5 min. For CAT activity, the mixture of 0.05 mL of the above supernatant, 1.95 mL of distilled water, and 1 mL of 0.3 % H2O2 was measured at 240 nm (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Bremen, Germany) for 2 min with an interval of 0.5 min. Three biological replicates were set up for each parameters.

Measurement of ascorbic acid (AsA) content and its related enzyme activities

The ascorbate peroxidase (APX) activity was determined following the protocols of Huang et al. (2023). 0.1 mL of the above supernatant measurement was mixed with 1.8 mL of 50 mM PBS (pH 7.0), 0.1 mL of 15 mM AsA solution, and 1 mL of 0.3 mM H2O2. The mixture was read at 290 nm. For the AsA content, about 0.2 g of samples was added with 1 mL of 2 M acetic acid solution, and the homogenate was centrifuged at 8000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C. 50 μL of the supernatant was mixed with 125 μL of 1 μM Fe3+ solution, 50 μL of 0.4 % phosphoric acid solution, 125 μL of acetic acid‑sodium acetate (HAc-NaAc) buffer (pH 4.5), 50 μL of 0.01 M 1,10-phenanthroline solution, and 100 μL of distilled water. The mixture was placed at room temperature during a 15-min sample rest time. The absorbance was 510 nm. For the dehydroascorbic acid (DHA) content, 0.2 g of the samples was homogenized with 0.5 mL PBS (pH 7.8), and then was centrifuged at 10000 rpm for 30 min at 4 °C. 20 μL of the supernatant was added with 160 μL of HAc-NaAc buffer (pH 4.5), and 20 μL of 1 % dithioterethiol solution. The mixture was read at 265 nm. For the dehydroascorbate reductase (DHAR) activity, the supernatant for H2O2 measurement was mixed with 50 μL of HAc-NaAc buffer (pH 4.5), 10 μL of 0.2 % DHA solution, 10 μL of 0.5 % glutathione solution, 20 μL of 5,5-dithiobis-(2-nitrobenzoic acid), and 10 μL of distilled water. The mixture was incubated at 25 °C for 20 min, and the absorbance was read at 412 nm. For the monodehydroascorbate reductase (MDHAR) activity, 60 μL of the above supernatant was mixed with 20 μL of 0.2 % monodehydroascorbate solution, 20 μL of 0.2 % nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide, 20 μL of distilled water, and 80 μL of HAc-NaAc buffer (pH 4.5). The mixture was measured at 340 nm. Three biological replicates were set up for each parameters.

Determination of the soluble sugar and acid composition

The determination of soluble sugar and titrable acid content were conducted by Wuhan PorNetsBio Co., Ltd. (Wuhan, China). A portable handheld auto saccharimeter (RHB0–90, OEM, Shanghai, China) was used to detect the soluble solids content. Three biological replicates were set up for each parameters.

Statistical analysis

The treatments were arranged in a randomized block design in two-way factors with the storage treatment (D) and the Pro treatment (T) in triplicates. The data analysis for ANOVA was conducted by Statistix 8 (Analytical Software, Tallahassee, United States) and the bars were presented by GraphPad Prism 8.0 (Dotmatics, Boston, United States). For the multivariate analysis, the hierarchical clustering heatmap, Pearson correlation, principal component analysis (PCA), pattern hunter, VIP scores (calculated using the Partial Least Squares Discriminant Analysis model), and mean decrease accuracy was performed by MetaboAnalyst 5.0 (http://www.metaboanalyst.ca).

Anti-oxidative system

The antioxidants in fruits for both cherry tomato cultivars were significantly stimulated by the D and T treatments (P < 0.05). The SOD activity was notably increased in D1T1 compared with D1T0, with an increased range of 10.0 %–30.2 % for both cultivars (Fig. S1a). There were significant differences among D and D×T treatments that higher POD activity in D0T1 for HJG and under D1T1 for ZX was shown (Fig. S1b). Although the CAT activity for HJG was remarkably increased in D1T1, there were no significant changes between other treatments for both cultivars (Fig. S1c). Noteworthy, the H2O2 content decreased by 27.8 %–39.2 % in D0T1 and D1T1 treatment compared with D0T0 while D1T0 remained the highest content for HJG; however, the opposite trend was shown for ZX that the content in T1 was 10.0 %–14.0 % higher than in T0 (Fig. S1d).

Differential responses were noted regarding the AsA content with its related contributions for both cultivars. Similar trends were found in APX, DHAR, and MDHAR activity for HJG that the activities were increased under D0T1 (compared with D0T0) but were reduced under D1T1 (compared with D1T0) (Fig. S1e, h, and i). For instance, the APX activity was increased by 47.4 % in D0T1, however, it decreased by 42.8 % in D1T1, respectively. Regarding the AsA content, it increased by 3.2 %–23.7 % in D0T1 for both cultivars, which was also increased in D1T1 for HJG (Fig. S1g). For DHA content, there was no significant difference between T treatment for HJG, but notable fluctuation was recorded for ZX (Fig. S1f).

Soluble sugar and acid composition

The sugar and acid properties were differently affected by D and T treatments for the tested cherry tomato cultivars. A similar trend was detected in soluble sugar, soluble solids content, and sugar acid ratio for HJG, that is, the content in D0T0 remained at the highest level among all treatments. Besides, compared to D0T0 and D0T1, increments of 5.7 %–12.0 % were recorded for the soluble sugar, titrable acid, and soluble solids content under D0T1 and D1T1 treatments in fruits of ZX. Still, there was no significant difference among D×T treatments for ZX in the nutritional properties (Fig. S2a-d).

Metabolome responses

Considering that more heightened parameters were in HJG than in ZX, the fresh fruit samples of HJG under various treatments were investigated for omics analysis. In general, the PCA plots for treatments D0T0, D0T1, D1T0, and D1T1 showed that the samples distinctly clustered within one treatment and respectively separated from other treatments (Fig. 3a) while the sample correlation heatmap showed that the metabolic composition and abundance between samples across treatments were in a high degree of similarity (Fig. 3b). In the quasi-targeted metabolomics, a total of 952 compounds were detected by multiple reaction monitoring based on the metabolome profiles, including 561 compounds in positive ion mode and 391 compounds in negative ion mode. Also, nitrogen metabolism with a high rich factor was observed in D0T0 vs D1T0 (Fig. S4c).

Transcriptome responses

High-quality clean reads of each sample were in a range of 36,806,476 to 58,921,712 and the mapping ratio of all samples ranged from 95.8 %–96.5 % among which 94.0 %–94.8 % were unique mapped reads (Table S1). The ratio of Q20, Q30, and GC ranged from 95.7 %–96.9 %, 89.2 %–91.8 %, and 42.1 %–42.7 % (Table S2). In total, 34,442 genes and 517 novel genes were identified (Table S3). The above results were quality for further analysis.

According to the transcriptome analysis, PC1 accounted for 91.8 % of PCA (Fig. 4a) while the numbers of DEGs in D0T0 vs D0T1 and D1T0 vs D1T1 were lower than D0T0 vs D1T0 and D0T1 vs D1T1. Integrative analysis of traits and multi-omics

To comprehensively emphasize the role of exogenous Pro in stimulating defense response in cherry tomatoes after low temperature storage, an integrative analysis of investigated traits, metabolome, and transcriptome was conducted. Initially, shared KEGG pathways among all genes and metabolites, as well as DEMs and DEGs were analyzed. A total of 32, 44, and 52 KEGG pathways exhibited differential abundance under D0T0 vs D0T1, D1T0 vs D1T1, and D0T0 vs D1T0, respectively; interestingly, part of DEGs shared significant KEGG pathways with DEMs, which were showed in supplementary excel.

Based on the integrative analysis, phenylpropanoid biosynthesis and starch and sucrose metabolism were identified as potential pathways with altered expression patterns. In the phenylpropanoid biosynthesis pathway, levels of phenylalanine and hydroxycinnamate were decreased by T1 compared to T0 under D0 and D1. Additionally, the expression of trans-cinnamate 4-monooxygenase (CYP73A14, EC: 1.14.1491) was up-regulated in D0T1 compared to D0T0 while caffeoyl-CoA O-methyltransferase (CCoAOMT, EC: 2.1.1.104) was down-regulated in D1T1 relative to D1T0 (Fig. S8a and c). Moreover, beta-glucosidase (BACOVA_02659, EC: 3.2.1.21) and endoglucanase (CEL1, EC: 3.2.1.4) were up-regulated in D0T1 compared to D0T0 but down-regulated in D1T1 relative to D1T0, whereas isoamylase (ISA1, EC: 3.2.1.68) and glucose-1-phosphate adenylyltransferase (AGPS1, EC: 2.7.7.27) showed improvements under D0T1 compared to D0T0 (Fig. S8a and c).

Discussion

Soluble sugar and acids primarily determine the flavor and taste of fruits (Jin et al., 2023). However, the total soluble solids content, indicated by Brix and acidity in ripe fruits, was determined by cultivars and cultivation methods (Dong et al., 2024). The present study revealed that Pro treatment notably improved the soluble sugar and Brix levels in ZX (Fig. S2a and c). Additionally, the sugar acid ratio remained constant in HJG after chilling storage (Fig. S2d), indicating that it mitigated the reduction of the sugar acid ratio, preserving the flavor. It is undeniable that genotype also has a substantial impact on the nutrition of tomatoes (Ciudad-Mulero et al., 2022). Nevertheless, Pro treatment positively enhanced or maintained the balance of sweet and sour flavors in both tested cultivars. The reason might be attributed to the role of Pro in osmoregulation within plant cells (Gholami Zali & Ehsanzadeh, 2018), which maintained cellular integrity that promoted the accumulation of soluble sugar and the efficiency of sugar translocation from leaves to fruits.

The sugars in fruits primarily contain glucose, fructose, and sucrose (Ali et al., 2023). Pro treatment increased most of the DEGs in starch and sucrose metabolism, yet, opposite results were observed after chilling stress (Fig. S8b and d). It suggested that Pro treatment principally accelerated the conversion of starch to soluble sugar, however, during chilling stress, part of Pro might function in stress defense mechanism, which was consistent with the observed reduction in soluble sugar (Fig. S2a). Pro acted as a signaling molecule (Adamipour et al., 2025) and connected to nutrient availability (Alvarez et al., 2022). It was reported that the application of zinc increased soluble sugar levels while decreasing acid content in loquat, which was attributed to the regulation of fructose and glucose metabolism (Ali et al., 2023). In the present study, Pro possibly activated the energy status and regulated the sugar levels and composition by modulating sugar metabolism, thereby influencing the sweetness in cherry tomatoes. Despite content of soluble solids being utilized as an indicator of the flavor and the balance of sweet and sour taste in fruits, Escribano et al. (2017) suggested that near-infrared models of dry matters might be more reliable in predicting flavor and taste in sweet cherries compared to soluble solids content. Therefore, further mechanisms of Pro modulating the flavor and taste under chilling conditions were illustrated in cherry tomatoes.

The levels of various amino acids were evaluated under different Pro and chilling treatments (Fig. 1) in this study, which contributed to the quality of flavor and taste in fruits and vegetables (Xu et al., 2023). Previous studies have categorized amino acids based on their taste profiles: sweet amino acids included Gly, Ala, Met, and Pro (Liang et al., 2020); bitter amino acids included Tyr, Arg, Lys, Leu, and Ile (Liang et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2014); umami amino acids included Glu, Gln, and Asp (Fan et al., 2023; Zhuang et al., 2021). Similar to the findings of exogenous salicylic acid modulating the GABA biosynthesis by altering the Pro, Glu, and Arg levels in tomatoes (Meher et al., 2018), exogenous Pro treatment significantly increased the content of Gly, Ala, Met, Arg, Lys, Ile, Leu, and Gln for HJG in the current study (Fig. 1b). The application of Pro and chilling treatment possibly induced a series of changes in different amino acids. Consequently, the metabolomic analysis presented that the DEMs were significantly abundant in alanine, aspartate and glutamate metabolism, biosynthesis of alkaloids derived from ornithine, lysine and nicotinic acid, and biosynthesis of amnio acids in the metabolomic responses (Fig. 3e). Pro facilitated the conversion of participated amino acids in stabilizing proteins and membranes and maintaining energy production (Alvarez et al., 2022). However, considering that the process of Pro biosynthesis consumed a significant amount of nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate‌ and its oxidation could generate 30 adenosine triphosphate conversely (Gholami Zali & Ehsanzadeh, 2018; Guan et al., 2019), in this study, the exogenous Pro potentially acted as an effective way to store energy in cherry tomato fruits. Besides, it was well known that Pro accumulation assisted plants in adapting to the environmental stress, which was often associated with Arg, Glu, and Orn (Alvarez et al., 2022; De la Torre-González et al., 2018). Notably, the DEMs were enriched in the nitrogen metabolism when comparing D1T0 with D0T0 (Fig. S4c), indicating that application of Pro modulated the amino acid biosynthesis to regulate the nitrogen metabolism. These factors collectively supported the ability of Pro treatment to modulate the amino profiles, thereby maintaining the taste of mature cherry tomatoes after chilling storage.

Chilling injury is regarded as an abiotic stress, which impairs the growth and development of plants, and specially affects the quality of mature fruits during the storage (Li et al., 2016; Li et al., 2023). The normal physiological functions of cell membrane permeability and photosynthesis respiration were disrupted in fruits after chilling injury, leading to electrolyte leakage, fatty acids peroxide, and cell oxidation (Wu et al., 2024). Pro was positively correlated to Val and GABA (Fig. 2b) while increased SOD activity and decreased H2O2 content were observed in Pro and chilling treatments (Fig. S1a and d). These results were coordinated with the findings in recent research that Pro, Leu, Val, and GABA were related to osmo-compatibility in tomatoes (Meher et al., 2018). Besides, SOD was expected to catalyze the dismutation of the superoxide radical anion to H2O2 and oxygen (Tsang et al., 1991). In the present study, fluctuation of SOD activity was observed in exogenous Pro treatment after low temperature preservation so that the content of H2O2 were reduced in HJG (Fig. S1a, b, and d). The response to Pro in ROS scavenging capability of the tested cultivars varied. It might be possible that at least 9 SlSOD genes were unevenly distributed on 12 chromosomes in tomatoes (Feng et al., 2016). However, the function of SlSOD genes under the present conditions remained less thorough understanding. Further research should focus on identifying and characterizing the SlSOD in these cultivars to better understand the genetic mechanisms underlying the observed variations in SOD activity.

Furthermore, a positive relationship between H2O2 and AsA content but a significant negative relationship between H2O2 content and MDHAR activity were observed (Fig. 2a). AsA and DHA interconverted with pivotal enzymes, APX, DHAR and MDHAR, preventing membrane structure from ROS toxins (Wei et al., 2019). The activity of MDHAR was increased by Pro treatment (Fig. S1i) and was notably correlated with sugar acid ratio while H2O2 was notably correlated with Pro content (Fig. 2a and c). This indicated that Pro application induced the plant tissues to regulate ROS defense, adjusting the accumulation of H2O2 so that the MDHAR activity was increased to balance the AsA and DHA content, which were similar to the study of Hou et al. (2021). Though the soluble sugar content was reduced, the reduction in sugar acid ratio with Pro treatment was lower than control after chilling storage (Fig. S2a and d), and the range remained within the desirable flavor profile for tasty cherry tomatoes. It indicated that the reduction of sugar acid ratio might have alleviated the respiration and metabolic activities in cherry tomatoes, which assisted in extending the storage period of the mature fruits.

In addition, the results in the transcriptomic analysis showed that the DEGs were enriched in carotenoid biosynthesis when comparing D0T1 and D0T0 (Fig. S7b), suggesting that Pro regulated the carotenoid content in cherry tomato fruits. It was similar to those of Gholami Zali and Ehsanzadeh (2018), who reported that exogenous Pro improved the carotenoid levels, which contributed to photosynthesis and enhanced the osmoregulation in fennel under drought stress. Carotenoids were regarded as important pigments in photosynthesis, participating in the capture of light and the transfer of energy (Gholami Zali & Ehsanzadeh, 2018). Still, the present results revealed that application of Pro could modulate the levels of carotenoids and the nutritional value of cherry tomatoes by adjusting the osmotic substrate within cells and scavenging ROS. Also, carotenoids were the key metabolites involved in softening the fruit during the ripening stage and contributing to fruit color appearance (Jia et al., 2024). In the present study, Pro treatment might protect the enzymes and structures involved in the carotenoid biosynthesis, thereby delaying the maturity of cherry tomatoes during chilling storage.

Like observations from the integrative analysis, the expression of CCoAOMT was significantly down-regulated by Pro treatment after chilling storage (Fig. S8a, c, and S9c). CCoAOMT helped the key enzyme O-methyltransferase for the lignin biosynthesis pathway (Wen et al., 2024). It inferred that Pro treatment decreased the lignin level, which was in line with the outcomes that Pro improved the growth and development of switchgrass by reducing the lignin biosynthesis (Guan et al., 2019). However, lignin was a crucial component of plant cell walls, providing mechanical support and stress resistance for plants when subjected to abiotic stress (Fan et al., 2023; Patanè et al., 2019). Exogenous Pro in the current study contributed to the antioxidation system and osmo-regulation, as previously mentioned, so lignin remained balanced. Nevertheless, the CCoAOMT-like enzyme catalyzed flavones, including 6-OH and 8-OH groups with adjacent hydroxyl moieties (Liu et al., 2020). The down-regulated CCoAOMT implied that despite the reduction in flavonoids, Pro treatment regulated the osmolytes and improved the capacity of antioxidants, thereby leading to these decrements.

In addition, the transcriptomic analysis showed that the DEGs were enriched in phenylpropanoid biosynthesis under D0T0 vs D0T1 and phenylalanine metabolism under D1T0 vs D1T1 in the present study (Fig. S7b and c), indicating that Pro treatment reallocated the phenylalanine metabolism. Phenylalanine functioned as the precursor for phenylpropanoid biosynthesis, being converted to trans-cinnamic acid through phenylalanine ammonia-lyase (PAL, an antioxidative enzyme) in plants (Kurepa et al., 2018). These findings suggested that Pro modulated the expression of CCoAOMT, thereby regulating phenylalanine metabolism towards lignin synthesis, which enhanced the cold tolerance and storability of cherry tomatoes. However, further study was still needed regarding how CCoAOMT and secondary metabolites in phenylpropanoid or lignin biosynthesis were regulated by Pro to enhance the cold tolerance of cherry tomato fruits.

Conclusion

This study provided insights into exogenous Pro modulating the physiological, metabolomic, and transcriptomic pathways in cherry tomatoes under chilling conditions. The present findings revealed that the Ala content (sweet taste) was significantly increased and the Arg content (bitter taste) was reduced under Pro treatment after chilling storage, while the Glu and GABA content were the potential amino acids modulated by Pro. Furthermore, the APX and SOD activities were positively associated with Pro content; however, the reduction of MDHAR activity led to a lower sugar acid ratio under Pro treatment after chilling storage. Likewise, the integrated metabolome and transcriptome revealed that Pro and chilling treatments affected phenylalanine metabolism and the gene expression of CCoAOMT in lignin biosynthesis in cherry tomatoes. In summary, exogenous Pro could improve the amino acids, antioxidative enzymes, and maintain the flavor quality after the low temperature storage in cherry tomatoes. However, further investigation into the molecular function of CCoAOMT in chilling stress was required.

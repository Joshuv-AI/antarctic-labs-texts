Peritumoral edema enhances MRI-based deep learning radiomic model for axillary lymph node metastasis burden prediction in breast cancer
Authors: Hongbing Luo, Zhe Chen, Hao Xu, Jing Ren, Peng Zhou
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
To investigate whether peritumoral edema (PE) could enhance deep learning radiomic (DLR) model in predicting axillary lymph node metastasis (ALNM) burden in breast cancer. Invasive breast cancer patients with preoperative MRI were retrospectively enrolled and categorized into low (< 2 lymph nodes involved (LNs+)) and high (≥ 2 LNs+) burden groups based on surgical pathology. PE was evaluated on T2WI, and intra- and peri-tumoral radiomic features were extracted from MRI-visible tumors in DCE-MRI. Deep learning models were developed for LN burden prediction in the training cohort and validated in an independent cohort. The incremental value of PE was evaluated through receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis, confirming the improvement in the area under the curve (AUC) using the Delong test. This was complemented by net reclassification improvement (NRI) and integrated discrimination improvement (IDI) metrics. The deep learning combined model, incorporating PE with selected radiomic features, demonstrated significantly higher AUC values compared to the MRI model and the DLR model in the training cohort (n = 177) (AUC: 0.953 vs. 0.849 and 0.867, p < 0.05) and the validation cohort (n = 111) (AUC: 0.963 vs. 0.883 and 0.882, p < 0.05). The complementary analysis demonstrated that PE significantly enhances the prediction performance of the DLR model (Categorical NRI: 0.551, p < 0.001; IDI = 0.343, p < 0.001). These findings were confirmed in the validation cohort (Categorical NRI: 0.539, p < 0.001; IDI = 0.387, p < 0.001). PE improved preoperative ALNM burden prediction of DLR model, facilitating personalized axillary management in breast cancer patients.

Introduction

Axillary lymph node metastasis (ALNM) is an important predictor of overall recurrence and survival in breast cancer patients1–3. Specifically, while the 5-year survival rate for patients without ALNM is 98.8%, it drops to 85.8% for those with ALNM4. Now, ALNM is clinically confirmed through pathology, often via ultrasound-guided fine-needle aspiration (US-FNA) or lymph node dissection. However, these invasive procedures carry potential risks, including chronic pain, lymphedema, paresthesia, and impaired shoulder mobility5. To reduce the risk of complications, axillary management in early breast cancer has de-escalated from complete axillary lymph node dissection (ALND) to sentinel lymph node biopsy (SLNB). Landmark trials6–8 have confirmed the safety of omitting ALND in patients with fewer than two positive sentinel lymph nodes (i.e. low burden), demonstrating non-inferior outcomes. This is further supported by the recently reported results of the SENOMAC trial9. The NCCN guidelines even allow SLNB in patients with low ALNM burden (defined as pre-operative biopsy-proven positive nodes and fewer than two abnormal nodes on imaging10. However, ALND is still recommended for patients with high ALNM burden (defined as more than two positive sentinel lymph nodes after SLNB or detected on imaging)11. This changing clinical paradigms underscoring the urgency for accurate preoperative assessment of ALNM burden by imaging in guiding personalized axillary management for breast cancer patients4.

The commonly used imaging modalities for staging ALNM in breast cancer patients include Ultrasonography (US), MRI, mammography, and PET/CT. However, studies have shown that these traditional techniques were insufficient in accurately determining ALNM status4,12,13. Furthermore, their ability to differentiate between high and low burden ALNM was similarly constrained11. Consequently, surgeons are challenged with giving an optimal upfront axillary surgery option for clinically node-positive patients with breast cancer, namely whether to perform SLNB or ALND. Hence, it is imperative to establish novel imaging techniques that can accurately predict ALNM burden.

Recent advancements and integration in radiomics and artificial intelligence have sparked interest in developing MRI-based models for predicting lymph node metastasis in breast cancer14,15. Radiomics involves converting medical images into high-throughput quantitative features for subsequent analysis and model building to support clinical decision-making16. Previous studies have highlighted MRI-based radiomics as a promising tool for predicting axillary lymph node metastasis (ALNM)17–21 and sentinel lymph node metastasis22–25, with pooled sensitivity, specificity, and AUC of 0.82, 0.83, and 0.89, respectively26. Deep learning is a significant advance in machine learning, where an image or its features are input into a multi-layer neural network. Through sequential processing, the network condenses the information until numerical features are generated for input into a supervised machine learning algorithm for predictive tasks. This paradigm has been widely adopted in various fields such as image or nature language processing27. Deep learning has also been widely studied for analyzing medical images28 and demonstrated high performance in various fields29. Prior studies have proved that combining deep learning with radiomics surpasses predefined handcrafted radiomics features, thus enhancing the predictive accuracy of axillary lymph node metastasis (ALNM) in breast cancer21,30,31.

Currently, breast MRI has transitioned from a predominantly contrast-enhanced technique to a multiparametric approach that includes routine T2-weighted imaging (T2WI)32. Peritumoral edema (PE), defined as the presence of water-like hyperintensity surrounding the index tumor on T2WI33, may be a helpful image sign, aiding breast cancer diagnosis34,35, treatment evaluation36 and prognosis prediction37–41. A recent research showed that PE could improve the accuracy of ALNM burden prediction in early-stage breast cancer42. Prior study also indicated that preoperative MRI was highly effective in excluding high burden ALNM, with an exclusion rate of 98.2%43. The implementation of multiparametric strategy may enhance the accuracy of nodal staging and characterization in breast cancer44,45. However, the potential advantage of integrating PE with intra- and peri-tumoral radiomic features for preoperative prediction of ALNM burden in breast cancer patients has not been thoroughly explored.

Therefore, we aimed to develop and validate a non-invasive deep learning imaging model for preoperative prediction of ALNM burden, according to the cut-off values for axillary surgery specified by the ACOSOG Z0011 trial (American College of Surgeons Oncology Group). The model was constructed by integrating PE, assessed on T2WI, with the radiomic features extracted from the intra- and peri-tumoral regions in DCE-MRI. We hypothesized that the incorporation of PE into the deep learning radiomic model would enhance our ability to predict ALNM burden in patients with breast cancer.

Methods

We confirm that the Medical Ethics Committee of the Sichuan Cancer Hospital & Institute has granted approval for this study, with the informed consent requirement waived (Approval Number: SCCHEC2015029). All methods in this study adhered to relevant guidelines and regulations, and the protocols received approval from The Medical Ethics Committee of the Sichuan Cancer Hospital & Institute. We conducted this study in accordance with the TRIPOD statement (Transparent Reporting of a Multivariable Prediction Model for Individual Prognosis or Diagnosis), categorizing it as type 2b46. The checklist was presented in Supplementary Materials.

Patients

A total of 374 consecutive female patients were retrospectively enrolled between September 2017 and January 2020.The inclusion criteria were (1) patients with unilateral invasive breast cancer confirmed by surgical pathology and (2) with T2WI and DCE-MRI examination. The exclusion criteria were (1) patients with previous breast surgery (n = 47), (2) with previous neoadjuvant or induction chemotherapy (n = 25), (3) with occult breast cancer (n = 2) and (4) without qualified image data (n = 6) or without completed T2WI (n = 6). The participants in this study were previously described in our prior study, which had a different research focus47.

Pathologic analysis

All participants underwent upfront lumpectomy or mastectomy with either SLNB alone, ALND after SLNB, or ALND directly, based on the surgeons' discretion. Subsequently, the status and number of ALNM were determined through pathological examination. In this study, high burden ALNM was defined as more than two positive lymph nodes proved by pathology and low burden ALNM was defined as less than two positive sentinel nodes or negative axillary lymph node proved by pathology after SLNB and/or ALND.

The presence and nuclear grade of intraductal component (IC) in invasive tumor were assessed. Nuclear grade was categorized according to the modified Black nuclear grade system48. The histological grade was categorized according to the Nottingham grading system49. Estrogen receptor (ER) and progesterone receptor (PR) positivity was defined as the presence of 1% or more positively stained nuclei in 10 high-power fields. Human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) status was assessed on a scale of 0 to 3: tumors with a score of 3 were classified as positive, tumors with a score of 0 or 1 were classified as negative, and tumors with a score of 2 underwent gene amplification using chromogenic in situ hybridization for confirmation50. Ki-67 expression was categorized as high or low based on a 14% threshold51.

MRI protocol

All eligible patients underwent MRI on the 3.0 T scanner (Skyra, Siemens Healthcare) with 16-channel breast coil. Axial T2WI images were acquired using Turbo-Inversion Recovery-Magnitude sequences. Axial T1-weighted DCE-MRI were acquired using two different fast protocols. The training and validation cohorts in this study were determined based on these DCE-MRI protocols, as described in our prior study47:

Patients enrolled under protocol 1 (September 2017–December 2018) were assigned to the training cohort for model development. This protocol comprised 26 phases acquired with the CAIPIRINHA-Dixon-TWIST-VIBE sequence from Siemens Healthcare.

Patients enrolled under protocol 2 (February 2019–January 2020) constituted the validation cohort for model validation. This protocol consisted of 20 phases obtained using a homemade sequence.

The specific parameters of these sequences can be referenced in the Supplementary Materials. Gadodiamide (0.1 mmol/kg; Omniscan, GE Healthcare) was intravenously administered via a power injector at 2.5 mL/s, followed by a saline flush of 20 mL at the same rate.

Interpreting T2WI findings

Two radiologists, L.H.B. (15 years of experience) and C.Z. (5 years of experience), assessed T2WI findings for inter-observer agreement. Sixty patients were randomly selected for intra-observer analysis, re-evaluated by Radiologist 1 after a 1-month interval. The early DCE-MRI phase was employed to aid index tumor localization on T2WI. Both radiologists were blinded to the pathological information regarding the surgery, particularly the status and number of lymph node metastases.

In this study, we assessed PE considering both its type and degree in the ipsilateral breast, with examples and corresponding definitions detailed in Supplementary Materials Figs. S1, 2. First, we classified PE into five types based on its location, consistent with previous studies33,42: Type 0 (no edema), Type 1 (peritumoral edema), Type 2 (pre-pectoral edema), Type 3 (subcutaneous edema), and Type 4 (diffuse edema). Then, we categorized PE degree into four grades based on its extent relative to the index tumor size: Grade 0 (no edema), Grade 1 (mild edema), Grade 2 (moderate edema), and Grade 3 (marked edema). Additionally, We assessed intra-tumoral hyperintensity based on higher signal intensity within the index tumor compared to surrounding normal glandular tissue38. The long and short diameter of index tumor in the transverse plane were directly extracted from the imaging report.

For clinical simplicity, we grouped PE types into "no or slight edema" (types 0 and 1), and "severe edema" (types 2, 3, and 4). Similarly, PE grades were categorized into "no or mild edema" (grades 0, 1, and 2), and "marked edema" (grade 3).

Radiomic Workflow:

The radiomic workflow followed established procedures from our prior work47. In brief, the 5th phase images from 26 dynamic phases and 4th phase images from 20 dynamic phases were selected for radiomic analysis52. After image processing and tumor segmentation, 208 radiomic features, encompassing shape, first-order, and texture features, were extracted from intra- and peri-tumoral regions (104 features each), aligning with the image biomarker standardization initiative (IBSI) standard53 (details in Supplementary Materials and Tables S1, S2). Reliability was evaluated using intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC), retaining features with ICC values > 0.75 for further analysis. The remaining features were standardized using z-score normalization, and the most predictive features linked to ALNM-high burden were selected using the LASSO method, using tenfold cross-validation and lambda.1se as the penalty parameter. The process of radiomic features selection were presented in Supplementary Materials (Fig. S3 and Table S3).

Conventional MRI model

The MRI model was built using T2WI findings. Logistic regression was employed for univariable and multivariable analyses in the training cohort. Significant variables (p < 0.05) from the univariable analysis were further assessed in the multivariable regression model. Backward step-wise selection was conducted using the likelihood ratio test with Akaike's information criterion as the stopping rule.

Developing and optimizing deep learning models

A sequential neural network with three dense layers was implemented using Keras with TensorFlow as the backend. The deep learning combined model was built using both T2WI findings and radiomic features, while deep learning radiomic (DLR) model was constructed using selected radiomic features. The process involved the following steps (Supplementary Materials Figs. S4, S5).

The baseline model had three dense layers (64 units each) with a 0.5 dropout rate after each layer. It used Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU) activation in hidden layers and sigmoid in the final output. Binary cross-entropy loss, Adam optimizer, and accuracy metric were applied for compilation. Training comprised 10 epochs, batch size 32, with performance visualized by validation loss and accuracy (Fig. S4A).

To better the baseline model’s performance, an optimized deep learning model (referred to as Model 2) was developed with three hidden dense layers, each containing 128 units. Dropout regularization was reduced to 0.2, and L2 regularization was included. The activation function and compilation method were similar to the baseline model. Model 2 was trained for up to 20 epochs with early stopping, monitoring validation accuracy and ceasing training if no improvement occurred over five consecutive epochs. The best weights were retained (Fig. S4B). Extending the epochs to 30, Model 3 was trained on this basis (Fig. S4C). Based on the minimization of validation loss, the final combined deep learning model was determined. The same hyperparameters were used for DLR model construction (Fig. S5).

Model evaluation

Discriminatory performance of the three models was assessed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis, providing metrics such as AUC, accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, PPV, and NPV based on the Youden index-derived cut-off value. AUC comparisons were performed using the DeLong test. A box plot was employed to illustrate the predictive performance of the final deep learning combined model. This model underwent tenfold cross-validation, the mean accuracy was calculated. The incremental value of PE was evaluated using the Delong test to confirm the improvement in AUC. This analysis was complemented by net reclassification improvement (NRI) and integrated discrimination improvement (IDI) metrics. Calibration plots and the Hosmer–Lemeshow test were employed to evaluate the reliability of the combined model in both cohorts. The clinical utility of three models in the validation cohort was assessed using decision curve analysis (DCA). A nomogram was constructed to provide a quantitative tool for personalized ALNM burden prediction.

Statistical analyses

Statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.2.3, http://www.r-project.org). The R packages used in this study are listed in the Supplementary Materials. The Mann–Whitney U-test or chi-square test was used to compare the differences in baseline clinicopathological data between two groups. Cohen's Kappa coefficient was utilized to evaluate both inter- and intra-observer agreement of T2WI findings. A two-tailed p value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. The sample size was determined by the availability of patients during the retrospective study period.

ALNM burden predictor from radiomic features

The 208 originally extracted radiomic features were reduced to three ALNM burden predictors in the training cohort: correlation of gray level co-occurrence matrix (GLCM) feature extracted from the intra-tumoral region, interquartile range of first-order features and cluster shade of GLCM features extracted from the peri-tumoral region.

Model development

Three models (namely MRI model, deep learning radiomic model and deep learning combined model) were developed in the training cohort and validated in the separate validation cohort. Finally, Model 2, which achieved the lowest validation loss during training, was determined as the final deep learning combined model (Fig. S6). The DLR model was constructed utilizing the same hyperparameters as the final deep learning combined model. Table S4 provided details regarding the structures, hyperparameters, training loss, and accuracy recorded during the training and optimization stages of the deep learning model.

Inter- and intra-observer agreement of T2WI findings

Moderate inter-observer agreement was found for intra-tumoral hyperintensity (weighted κ = 0.509, 95% CI 0.403, 0.616). Substantial to almost perfect inter-observer agreement was observed for the types (weighted κ = 0.758, 95% CI 0.694, 0.823) and degrees of PE (weighted κ = 0.821, 95% CI 0.765, 0.877) between radiologists. Good agreement was also observed for intra-observer agreement, with a weighted κ value of 0.677 (95% CI 0.384, 0.971) for intra-tumoral hyperintensity, 0. 839 (95% CI 0.707, 0.971) for PE type, and 0. 878 (95% CI 0.760, 0.996) for PE degree.

Discussion

In the post-ACOSOG Z0011 era, preoperative imaging has shifted focus from detecting ALNM to accurately predict ALNM burden4, potentially preventing underestimation of lymph node staging through SLNB alone54. In line with this evolving paradigm, we conducted this study to create a multiparametric MRI-based imaging model for ALNM burden prediction in breast cancer patients. Our findings demonstrate that the deep learning combined model, integrating PE on T2WI with the DLR model from DCE-MRI, achieved high AUC values of 0.953 in the training cohort and 0.963 in the validation cohort. Importantly, the incorporation of PE into the combined model resulted in enhancements in performance metrics, as evidenced by improved AUC based on Delong test, higher net benefit according to DCA tests, and further confirmation through NRI and IDI analyses. These enhancements suggested that our model had the potential to improve the accuracy of ALNM burden prediction in patients with breast cancer, thereby aiding clinicians in making more informed decisions regarding axillary surgery.

In the realm of imaging-based models for predicting ALNM burden in breast cancer, our deep learning combined model outperformed previous studies (Key details of similar research and cohorts were outlined in Table S5), achieving AUCs of 0.953 in the training cohort and 0.963 in the validation cohort. This outperformed a pioneering radiomic study relying solely on radiomic signatures (AUC 0.79)17, and another model based on conventional MRI features and PE (AUC 0.71 in training cohort, 0.69 in validation cohort)42. It also surpassed previously reported radiomic models for ALNM prediction, with pooled AUC values ranging from 0.89 to 0.9026,55. Our deep learning combined model exhibited comparable prediction performance to a multi-center study by Yu et al.19, which utilized radiomic features from multiparametric MRI to stratify patients with ALNM high burden in subgroup analysis. They achieved AUCs of 0.96 and 0.90 in the training and validation cohorts, respectively. Our results, consistent with the aforementioned studies and in comparison, highlighted the superiority of the combined multi-parametric MRI model over models based solely on radiomics or conventional MRI features in ALNM burden prediction.

In this study, the deep learning combined model showed excellent performance in excluding high burden ALNM, with a NPV of 96.9% in the training cohort and 95.2% in the validation cohort. Consistent with our findings, a previous study confirmed the high efficacy of preoperative MRI (98.2%) in excluding advanced nodal stage in breast cancer patients. However, their study's clinical value was impaired as only 6 patients with advanced nodal stage were included43. Besides, researchers have explored the efficacy of US in preoperatively excluding advanced nodal disease in breast cancer. Neal et al. demonstrated that preoperative negative ultrasound findings have a 93.3% accuracy in excluding advanced nodal disease, which could obviate unnecessary invasive axilla surgery. However, their results still demonstrated a significantly higher false negative rate of 6.7%56. Hiroyuki et al. reported similar findings, with only 10 out of 378 patients showing advanced nodal disease on final pathology when ultrasound findings were normal54. Zheng et al.30 utilized ultrasound-based DLR to predict ALNM in early-stage breast cancer. Aligned with our findings, their combined model also achieved high discriminative ability to distinguish between low and high burden ALNM, with AUCs of 0.956 in the training cohort and 0.905 in the test cohort. The above-mentioned results, in conjunction with our findings, suggested that non-invasive models utilizing MRI or US could effectively exclude cases with a high burden ALNM. To enhance the clinical utility of our model, we developed a nomogram to provide clinicians with a personalized tool for predicting ALNM burden in breast cancer patients. Based on our findings, a negative prediction from our nomogram suggests a high likelihood of having no more than two lymph nodes involved, indicating that sentinel lymph node biopsy (SLNB) alone may be adequate. This approach helps mitigate the risk of underestimating axillary disease by relying solely on SLNB54.

Positron emission tomography (PET) is often used in predicting ALNM in breast cancer. The overall sensitivities and specificities of PET/CT ranged from 52.2 to 94.4% and from 86.3 to 97%, respectively57–60. PET/CT, while capable of suggesting ALN metastasis based on increased fluorodeoxyglucose uptake61, lacks spatial resolution and morphological detail. Conversely, MRI provides crucial anatomical information for evaluating tumor morphology. PET/MRI, integrating PET's metabolic specificity with MRI's anatomical sensitivity, holds promise in improving breast cancer ALNM prediction62. Study indicated comparable diagnostic performance between MRI and PET/MRI for assessing ALNM63. Future research may explore integrating PET imaging with multiparametric MRI models to enhance predictive accuracy and provide comprehensive ALNM burden prediction in breast cancer patients.

The identified radiomic predictors could serve as quantitative imaging biomarkers for guiding clinical decisions. Our study found that two GLCM features were positively associated with high ALNM burden, indicating that complex and heterogeneous texture patterns correlate with higher ALNM burden. Previous studies have also shown that GLCM-based texture features are associated with therapeutic response64 and prognosis65,66 in breast cancer. Conversely, the interquartile range of first-order features in the peri-tumoral region displayed a negative correlation with ALNM burden, suggesting that more uniform texture in this region indicates a lower ALNM burden.

This study has several limitations. First, this retrospective study may have inherent variations and selection biases. A well-designed prospective study is warranted to verify these findings. Second, subjective evaluations of traditional MRI feature (e.g., PE on T2WI) can be influenced by observer experience and may introduce variability. However, we addressed this by conducting assessments of both inter- and intra-observer agreement, revealing satisfactory agreement. Third, some patients in this study may have undergone core-needle biopsy before MRI, potentially introducing bias in PE assessment. However, it was anticipated that this bias was randomly distributed between two groups. Finally, the models were developed and validated using two ultrafast DCE-MRI protocols on the same MRI scanner. Thus, the variance caused by different MRI scanners was not investigated. Therefore, further studies involving larger datasets from different scanners are needed to ensure the generalizability of our findings.

In conclusion, our multiparametric MRI-based deep learning combined model provided a more accurate prediction of ALNM burden, facilitating personalized and optimized axilla management for breast cancer patients.

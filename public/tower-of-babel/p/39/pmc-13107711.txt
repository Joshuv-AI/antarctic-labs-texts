Apolipoprotein D downregulation in OSCC: multi-database validation and clinical significance
Authors: Shuting Wang, Jun Zhao, Rui Bai, Jianbo Ou, Chan Tang, Jiayi Hang, Xiaolin Nong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundApolipoprotein D (APOD), a member of the lipocalin superfamily, plays a pivotal role in apoptosis, cancer, immune responses, and neural injury repair. Its association with various cancer types has been well-documented, but its expression and clinical implications in oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) remain underexplored. Our study aims to investigate the expression and clinical significance of APOD in OSCC.MethodsA comprehensive analysis of APOD mRNA and protein expression in OSCC was conducted using multi-omics data from TCGA, GEO, and CPTAC databases. The findings were validated through qRT-PCR and immunohistochemistry (IHC) on OSCC tissue samples. Diagnostic and prognostic potential of APOD was assessed using summary receiver operating characteristic (sROC) curves and Kaplan-Meier survival analysis. Multivariate Cox regression analysis was performed to adjust for confounding factors and identify independent prognostic markers for survival. Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was used to explore the signaling pathways associated with APOD and their biological relevance. Ethical approval for research involving human subjects was obtained from an ethics committee.ResultsAnalysis of public databases revealed significant downregulation of both APOD mRNA and protein in OSCC tissues compared to normal mucosal controls. Results from clinical samples corroborated these findings. The sROC analysis indicated that APOD may serve as a promising diagnostic biomarker for OSCC. Multivariate Cox regression analysis identified pathological T stage as an independent prognostic factor, independent of clinical T stage and lymphovascular invasion, while APOD, although associated with prognosis, was not a robust predictor. GSEA highlighted a strong positive correlation between APOD expression and key components of Type I interferon signaling (JAK1, TYK2, STAT2), suggesting that APOD downregulation may contribute to OSCC progression by inhibiting the Type I interferon-JAK-STAT pathway.ConclusionAPOD expression is significantly reduced in OSCC, demonstrating potential as a diagnostic and prognostic biomarker. Its downregulation may facilitate disease progression through disruption of the Type I interferon-mediated JAK-STAT signaling pathway.

Introduction

OSCC is a prevalent malignancy of the oral and maxillofacial regions, characterized by early lymph node metastasis and highly aggressive growth. The five-year survival rate for patients with OSCC is approximately 50% [1]. Despite advancements in treatment modalities, the rising incidence of OSCC highlights the urgent need for novel molecular markers to better understand the underlying mechanisms driving OSCC pathogenesis and progression. In addition to sustained cell proliferation, apoptotic evasion, vascular proliferation, invasion, and distant metastasis, metabolic alterations have become a hallmark of cancer cells [2]. The high rate of de novo lipid synthesis in cancer cells ensures their rapid growth and proliferation [3–5]. Lipids, including total cholesterol, lipoproteins, and triglycerides, are crucial for the synthesis of new cell membranes, particularly in the context of lipid peroxidation, which generates peroxide radicals that damage key components of the cell membrane [6]. Altered lipid profiles have been linked to various cancers, including colorectal, breast, ovarian, and head and neck squamous cell carcinoma [7–10]. Changes in lipid metabolic pathways significantly impact OSCC incidence [11]. Thus, further investigation into molecules that influence lipid metabolism reconfiguration is essential. Apolipoproteins (APOs) are integral to lipid metabolism and play a significant role in processes related to cancer.

APOD, first identified in human plasma in 1963 [12], is a glycosylated protein with a molecular weight ranging from 25 to 30 kDa. This protein is involved in diverse biological functions, including lipid transport, anti-inflammatory actions, cellular defense against oxidative stress, and tissue maturation. Growing evidence highlights its pathological relevance in various cancers [13]. APOD expression is significantly downregulated in hepatocellular carcinoma tissues compared to matched healthy controls, positioning APOD as a potential standalone prognostic marker in clinical evaluations [14]. In colorectal cancer, APOD expression is markedly reduced and correlates with lymphatic metastasis, advanced disease stages, and poorer overall survival outcomes [15]. Patients with epithelial ovarian cancer who exhibit low or absent APOD expression tend to have a lower survival rate compared to those with higher APOD expression [16]. Elevated APOD levels inhibit tumor growth, as demonstrated in cancers such as thyroid, prostate, and colorectal cancers [17]. However, the relationship between APOD and OSCC remains unexplored. Investigating APOD expression in OSCC and its potential clinical significance could provide novel insights into the pathogenesis of this malignancy.

In this study, bioinformatics and experimental validation approaches were integrated to address three main objectives: (1) evaluating the differential expression of APOD in OSCC; (2) assessing the clinical value of APOD in OSCC; and (3) identifying signaling pathways potentially mediated by APOD dysregulation in OSCC pathogenesis. These findings deepen our understanding of the APOD-OSCC relationship and highlight APOD as a promising biomarker warranting further investigation.

Materials and methods

Data sources and extractions

Transcriptomic data were acquired from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) via mRNA-seq (UCSC Xena) (https://xenabrowser.net/) and the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO), (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/), with clinical date extracted from TCGA (https://cancergenome.nih.gov/) portals. To systematically analyze APOD expression profiles in OSCC, Boolean search strategies were employed within the National Center for Biotechnology Information (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/), using the terms (OSCC OR buccal OR tongue OR floor of the mouth OR oral OR gum OR lip OR palate) AND (squamous cell carcinoma OR cancer OR carcinoma OR tumor OR neoplasm* OR malignant* OR oncology*). Inclusion criteria were: (1) human tissue samples; (2) a minimum of three samples from both cancerous and non-cancerous tissues; and (3) datasets that included APOD mRNA expression data. APOD protein expression data were obtained from the Office of Cancer Clinical Proteomics Research (CPTAC) (https://proteomics.cancer.gov/programs/cptac). Non-OSCC samples were excluded from the HNSCC cohort. The acquired data underwent normalization and log2 transformation. The final datasets comprised 331 OSCC samples and 32 normal samples from TCGA, 22 APOD expression datasets from GEO, and 54 OSCC samples along with 28 normal samples from CPTAC.

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA)

GSEA was performed on a dataset of 331 OSCC samples and 32 normal tissue samples sourced from TCGA. The dataset, h.all.v6.1.symbols, was stratified into high-expression and low-expression cohorts based on the median APOD expression level. GSEA version 4.3.3 was used to explore the signaling pathways associated with low APOD expression . Statistical significance was set at a false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.25 and a p-value < 0.05 for the normalized enrichment score (NES).

Clinical samples

Between January and December 2024, 115 formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded OSCC tissues, along with adjacent normal tissues, were collected from patients at the College & Hospital of Stomatology, Guangxi Medical University, for immunohistochemistry (IHC). Additionally, twenty-five pairs of OSCC and adjacent normal tissues were immediately preserved in RNA Keeper Tissue Stabilizer for subsequent quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR).

Total RNA was isolated using TRIzol reagent (Takara Bio, Japan) and converted into complementary DNA (cDNA) using the Prime Script RT-PCR kit (Vazyme, China). Quantitative real-time PCR was performed on a QuantStudio 5 Flex instrument (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) with SYBR Green fluorescent dye (Vazyme, China). The relative expression levels of APOD mRNA were determined using the Ct (2−ΔΔCt) method, with GAPDH serving as the reference gene. The primer sequences used for qRT-PCR are as follows:

Immunohistochemical (IHC) staining

Paraffin-embedded OSCC tissue specimens were sectioned to a thickness of 4 μm. After a series of processing steps, including oven-drying at 60 °C for 3 h, xylene-mediated deparaffinization, and rehydration through a graded ethanol series, antigenic epitope retrieval was performed using an EDTA-based buffer (pH 9.0) under high-pressure conditions. Endogenous peroxidase blocking was achieved by 15 min of 3% H₂O₂ treatment. Immunostaining proceeded with incubation using a primary antibody (anti-APOD, 1:200 dilution, Cat# R389164, Zenbio) at 4 °C for 16 h, followed by secondary antibody conjugation (HRP-conjugated, SP-6000, ZSGB-BIO) at room temperature for 20 min (25 °C). Chromogenic detection was achieved through exposure to DAB substrate for 5 min, followed by nuclear counterstaining with Mayer’s hematoxylin for 30 s. Final slide preparation involved xylene clearing, ethanol gradient dehydration, and permanent mounting with neutral resin.

Evaluation of IHC staining

Immunohistochemical Evaluation Criteria: Cellular positivity was defined by distinct yellow-to-brown chromogen deposition in both the membrane and cytoplasm. A dual-parameter semiquantitative histoscore (H-score) system was applied for analysis: Staining Intensity Grading: 0: Negative; 1+: Pale yellow; 2+: Medium yellow; 3+: Dense brown. Positivity Percentage Stratification (100 morphologically homogeneous cells counted across 5 randomly selected 400× fields): 0: ≤ 5%; 1: 6-25%; 2: 26-50%; 3: 51-75%; 4: 76-100%. The composite H-score was calculated by multiplying the staining intensity score by the proportion of positive cells score. All images were acquired using an Olympus BX53 microscope.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were executed using SPSS v22.0 (IBM Corp. USA). Quantitative data are expressed as x̄ ± SD, where x̄ represents the arithmetic mean and SD denotes the standard deviation of the population. A two-tailed Student t-test was applied for comparisons between two groups, and one-way ANOVA was utilized for multi-group analyses. Statistical significance was indicated by asterisks: *P < 0.05; **P < 0.01; ***P < 0.001; ns = non-significant. Scatter plots and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were generated using GraphPad Prism v9.0. Forest plots were created using Stata 18, and sensitivity analyses were conducted. Funnel plots and Begg’s test were employed to assess potential publication bias. ROC curves and Kaplan-Meier survival curves were generated to evaluate APOD’s diagnostic potential for neoplastic lesions, with diagnostic accuracy confirmed by calculating the area under the curve (AUC). Fagan’s analysis was used to explore the probabilistic relationship between pre-test probability, post-test probability, positive likelihood ratio (LR+), and negative likelihood ratio (LR-), using APOD expression thresholds aligned with WHO-recognized histopathological diagnostic criteria. Overall survival (OS) is defined as the time from diagnosis to death or last follow-up. Survival analysis was conducted with the Kaplan-Meier method, with intergroup differences analyzed via the log-rank test. Hazard ratios (HR) and 95% confidence intervals were calculated using the Cox proportional hazards model.

Results

Analysis of APOD transcription and translation in OSCC

Public database analysis of APOD transcription and translation in OSCC

A search of the TCGA, GEO, and CPTAC databases compiled fundamental data from 23 APOD mRNA expression profiles and one protein expression dataset used in this study (Table 1). Compared to normal tissues, 22 of the 23 datasets (Fig. 1A–W) indicated significantly reduced APOD expression in OSCC tissues. Transcriptomic analysis revealed significant differential expression of APOD in 18 out of 22 datasets, while four datasets (GSE31853, GSE3524, GSE36090, and GSE9844) showed no significant alterations in expression levels between OSCC lesions and non-neoplastic controls. Likewise, APOD protein expression was lower in OSCC tissues than in normal tissues (Fig. 1X).

Clinical validation of APOD transcription and translation in OSCC

To further validate APOD expression in OSCC, both transcriptional and translational levels were assessed using qRT-PCR and IHC. qRT-PCR analysis of 25 paired OSCC and adjacent normal tissues revealed a significant downregulation of APOD mRNA in tumor samples (Fig. 3A). IHC analysis also demonstrated a marked reduction in APOD expression in OSCC tissues compared to normal tissues (Fig. 3B). These results highlight the significantly lower expression levels of APOD in OSCC tissues when compared to normal tissues (Fig. 3C).

Association between APOD expression and clinicopathologic parameters of OSCC

Analysis of clinical data from TCGA revealed stage-dependent suppression of APOD mRNA in OSCC, with advanced-stage tumors (T3-T4; n = 200) showing significantly lower expression levels compared to early-stage tumors (T1-T2; n = 131) (8.31 ± 2.05 vs. 8.80 ± 2.10). In terms of clinical staging, OSCC individuals with stages III-IV (n = 231) exhibited lower APOD mRNA levels compared to those in stages I-II (n = 90) (8.40 ± 2.10 vs. 8.92 ± 2.01). However, no significant differences were observed when APOD expression was analyzed in relation to other clinicopathological characteristics (Table 2).

Diagnostic ability of APOD in OSCC

ROC curve analysis was performed to assess the clinical diagnostic capability of APOD in OSCC. The analysis of 23 mRNA datasets and 1 protein expression dataset (Fig. 4A-X) revealed that 22 out of the 23 areas under the curve (AUC) exceeded 0.5. Among these, 17 datasets demonstrated statistically significant differences, while 5 datasets (GSE19089, GSE31853, GSE3524, GSE36090, and GSE78060) showed no significant diagnostic distinction between OSCC tissues and controls. These results indicate that APOD may function as a diagnostic biomarker for OSCC in clinical settings. Further evaluation of diagnostic performance through summary ROC (sROC) analysis revealed an AUC of 0.96 (95% CI, 0.94–0.97) for APOD (Fig. 5A), indicating a strong discriminative capacity. Probabilistic analysis showed a pre-test probability of 20%, which increased to 60% after a positive test result, while a negative test result reduced the probability to 1% (Fig. 5B). Sensitivity was calculated as 0.95 (95% CI, 0.89–0.98) (Fig. 5C), and specificity was 0.84 (95% CI, 0.74–0.90) (Fig. 5D). These results collectively demonstrate that reduced APOD mRNA expression could serve as a reliable biomarker for differentiating OSCC from histologically normal oral epithelium in clinical diagnostics.

Prognostic analysis of APOD in OSCC

Correlation of APOD transcription levels with survival data from TCGA revealed a significant association between APOD expression and clinical T stage. Patients in the T1 and T2 stages were classified into the high-expression group, while those in the T3 and T4 stages were categorized into the low-expression group. The Kaplan-Meier survival analysis demonstrated that individuals within the high-expression cohort exhibited a more favorable prognosis in comparison to those in the low-expression cohort, as evidenced by a log-rank test yielding a p-value of 0.041 (Fig. 6).

Univariate Cox regression analysis was performed to examine the relationship between APOD gene expression, age, gender, TNM stage, clinical stage, and other potential prognostic factors (Table 4). The analysis identified that factors such as APOD expression, clinical T stage, lymphovascular invasion, pathologic T stage, and pathologic N stage were significantly associated with prognosis. A multivariate Cox regression analysis, incorporating these significant factors, revealed that only pathologic T stage remained in the model, signifying its close association with OSCC prognosis and its independent prognostic significance (Table 5). Discussion

OSCC, the most prevalent histological subtype of oral malignancies, is experiencing a global rise in incidence, significantly impacting both survival rates and the quality of life for patients. While current treatments such as surgical resection, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy are standard, their efficacy remains limited. Thus, a comprehensive investigation into the expression and clinical relevance of biomarkers is crucial for advancing the understanding and management of OSCC.

Lipidomic remodeling, characterized by alterations in fatty acid (FA) transport, de novo lipogenesis, lipid droplet (LD) storage, and ATP-generating β-oxidation, is widely recognized as a hallmark metabolic adaptation in cancer cells [18]. Cancer cells exploit lipid metabolic pathways to support their growth, maintain survival, enhance migratory capabilities, facilitate invasion, and enable metastasis [19]. APOs play critical roles in lipid metabolism, influencing a range of cancer-associated processes, making them promising biomarkers and therapeutic targets [20, 21]. APOD is expressed in various tissues across multiple species, with numerous ligands of varying structures and functions identified. Due to its significant ligand heterogeneity and widespread tissue distribution, APOD is regarded as a multiligand, multifunctional protein [22], involved in complex regulatory networks with pleiotropic roles in diverse pathophysiological processes [23–26]. APOD exerts inhibitory effects in various tumors. Elevated APOD secretion is closely linked to cell cycle arrest, notably reducing the S phase and G2/M phase [27]. In breast cancer, the function of APOD is linked to its engagement with multiple crucial signaling pathways, such as the estrogen receptor (ER), mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK), progesterone receptor (PR), cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2), and 5-lipoxygenase (5-LO) pathways. APOD inhibits the translocation of MAPK from the cytoplasm to the nucleus, which limits cell proliferation and impedes the progression of the cell cycle [28]. Additionally, free arachidonic acid (AA), a substrate for COX-2 and 5-LO, contributes to cellular damage through these pathways. APOD binds to AA, preventing malignant transformation induced by the COX-2 and 5-LO pathways [29, 30].

This study collected multiple mRNA datasets and protein expression data by mining the TCGA, GEO, and CPTAC databases. Additionally, paired OSCC and adjacent normal tissue specimens were obtained for validation through qRT-PCR and IHC. Several key aspects distinguish this study. First, 23 RNA-seq datasets were analyzed to assess APOD mRNA levels, and 1 protein expression dataset was used to evaluate APOD protein levels. Furthermore, qRT-PCR and IHC were employed to measure mRNA and protein expression in 25 and 115 paired OSCC and adjacent normal tissues, respectively. By utilizing different analytical methods, the relationship between APOD expression and OSCC tissues was examined across multiple cohorts at both the mRNA and protein levels. In contrast to single-cohort, single-assay analyses, this multi-cohort, multi-method validation more clearly demonstrates APOD downregulation in OSCC. Variations in APOD expression across different OSCC stages may reflect the biological behavior of the tumor.

Both IHC and qRT-PCR revealed a significant downregulation of APOD mRNA and protein in OSCC tissues compared to normal tissues, supported by data from TCGA, GEO, and CPTAC databases. Analysis of TCGA data, in conjunction with clinical and pathologic features, showed a strong correlation between APOD mRNA expression and clinical T classification and clinical stage. Similarly, APOD protein expression correlated with clinical T classification, with expression levels significantly lower in advanced stages (T3-T4) compared to early stages (T1-T2) of OSCC. Additionally, APOD demonstrated diagnostic potential for OSCC, as shown by pre-test and post-test probability assessments, as well as sROC analysis. In conclusion, this study highlights APOD downregulation as a defining feature of OSCC, with its expression linked to disease progression.

To explore the potential biological functions of APOD in OSCC, GSEA enrichment analysis was performed, revealing that differential APOD expression is associated with several key signaling pathways. These pathways include origin unwinding and elongation, homologous recombination, organization of the inner kinetochore, and the Type I interferon-JAK-STAT signaling pathways. It has been shown that IFN-I triggers a caspase cascade, leading to programmed cell death in tumor cells, thus inducing apoptosis via the JAK-STAT pathway [31–35]. The receptors for IFN-I are formed by the IFNAR1 and IFNAR2 subunits. Upon IFN-I binding, IFNAR1 and IFNAR2 dimerize, creating specific transmembrane protein complexes that activate subsequent signaling pathways [36, 37]. Activated IFNAR1 binds to TYK2, while IFNAR2 binds to JAK1, initiating the STATs signaling pathway and generating corresponding biological effects [38, 39]. IFN-I has recognized antiviral [40], antiproliferative [41], and immunomodulatory effects [42], as well as significant antitumor activity [43, 44]. JAK1 and TYK2, members of the Janus kinase family, serve as core kinases in Type I interferon signal transduction, activating downstream gene expression through a cascade reaction. IFN-α/β triggers immune surveillance via JAK1/TYK2, and certain tumors circumvent interferon effects by epigenetically suppressing ISGs [45]. Signal Transducer and Activator of Transcription 2 (STAT2) plays a pivotal role in the transcriptional response to IFN-I. Research indicates that in triple-negative breast cancer, which harbors abundant cancer stem cells (CSCs), the IFN-I/STAT2 gene signature is suppressed, contributing to increased invasiveness. Additionally, human mammary epithelial cells (HMECs) also exhibit suppression of the IFN-I/STAT2 gene signature, suggesting that activation of this pathway may inhibit cancer development [46]. Furthermore, in patients with metastatic melanoma, a high STAT2 transcriptional profile in conventional dendritic cells (cDCs) has been associated with improved overall survival [47]. In individuals diagnosed with oral cancer, an elevated expression of the interferon pathway genes, specifically CXCL10 and STAT2, is associated with a more favorable prognosis [48]. The correlation between APOD and genes involved in the JAK-STAT signaling pathway was analyzed, revealing a positive correlation between APOD expression and JAK1, TYK2, STAT2, BCL2, CCND2, AOX1, and GEAP, alongside a negative correlation with CCKN1A. This suggests that APOD downregulation could promote OSCC development by disrupting normal apoptotic and immune regulatory mechanisms. These findings highlight the potential role of APOD in OSCC pathogenesis, underscoring the need for further exploration of its therapeutic implications. However, it is important to note some limitations. First, the findings’ generalizability might be impacted by the comparatively small sample size. Second, potential batch effects within the datasets could influence the reliability of the results and warrant careful consideration in future studies. Finally, although the biological function of APOD was examined using clinical samples, additional molecular experimental validation is required to confirm its specific role in OSCC development. Despite these limitations, the present findings offer valuable insights for the development of future interventions. Subsequent studies should prioritize the functional validation of APOD and its potential clinical application to fully realize its value in OSCC.

Conclusion

In conclusion, APOD has been identified as a downregulated gene in OSCC, associated with patient prognosis and potentially serving as a novel biomarker. Its downregulation may facilitate cancer progression by inhibiting the Type I interferon-JAK-STAT signaling pathway.

The diagnostic value of CXCL8 in diabetes mellitus co-morbidity with tuberculosis
Authors: Feifan Xu, Lulu Liu, Yuchen Zhai, Juan Ma, Shengyan Qu, Wei Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDiabetes mellitus co-morbidity with tuberculosis (DM + TB) as one of the common complications associated with diabetes mellitus (DM) has been verified harmful to human health. Few studies have focused on CXCL8 in DM + TB co-morbidity patients. The objective of this study was to investigate the auxiliary diagnostic value of CXCL8 in patients with DM + TB in peripheral blood.MethodsAccording to the inclusion and exclusion criteria, 49 DM, 45 DM + TB were enrolled as the experimental group. Fifty-eight healthy people were enrolled as the healthy control (HC) group. IFN-γ, IL-2, and other clinical indicators were obtained from the clinical laboratory. ELISA, flow cytometry, RT-qPCR and in vitro stimulation (Mycobacterium tuberculosis and glucose) were used to detect the expression of CXCL8 and related receptors in plasma and peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs). Spearman correlation analysis was used to analyze the correlation of plasma CXCL8 with disease-specific cytokines and neutrophil-related indicators in patients with DM + TB. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was used to analyze the sensitivity and specificity of CXCL8 alone or in combination with cytokines in the diagnosis of DM + TB.ResultsPlasma CXCL8 expression in DM patients was significantly higher than that of HC subjects. Compared with DM group, the expression of CXCL8, CXCR1 and CXCR2 of DM + TB was significantly increased while the expression of CXCL8 on PBMCs was not significantly different. In DM + TB, plasma CXCL8 expression was positively correlated with IFN-γ and IL-2. Cases with high CXCL8 expression were significantly associated with the higher WBC count/higher NEC percentage/higher NEC count. In addition, correlation analysis showed that CXCL8 expression was closely related to white blood cells or neutrophils in the DM + TB group. ROC analysis showed that CXCL8 alone distinguished DM and DM + TB with a sensitivity of 65.31%, a specificity of 68.89% and an AUC of 0.7265 (95%CI: 0.6225–0.8306). CXCL8 combined with IFN-γ and IL-2 had a sensitivity of 89.80%, a specificity of 88.89% and an AUC of 0.9175 (95%CI: 0.8475–0.9874) in distinguishing DM from DM + TB.ConclusionsPlasma CXCL8 could better distinguish DM and DM + TB patients and was correlated with IL-2, IFN-γ and neutrophil related indicators in DM + TB patients. CXCL8 alone, as well as in combination with cytokine markers, showed better diagnostic efficacy, thus providing a laboratory auxiliary diagnostic indicator for DM + TB patients.Clinical trialNot applicable.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12879-026-12903-9.Keywords: CXCL8, DM combined with TB, IL-2, IFN-γ, Diagnostic value

Background

Diabetes mellitus (DM) is a prevalent condition characterized by hyperglycemia [1]. According to the International Diabetes Federation, DM impacts the quality of life for approximately 537 million adults aged 20–79 years, with projections indicating an increase to 643 million by 2030 [2]. Despite the availability of numerous effective diagnostic and treatment methods for DM, it remains a significant health threat that adversely affects human well-being and quality of life [3, 4].

Extensive research had demonstrated that DM complicated with tuberculosis (TB) (DM + TB) represented one of the common complications associated with DM, particularly in underdeveloped regions [5]. Studies had indicated that DM could lead to impaired immune function, notably impairing cellular immune responses to Mycobacterium tuberculosis (MTB), thereby significantly elevating the risk of developing DM + TB [6, 7]. Furthermore, elevated blood glucose levels might facilitate the emergence of drug-resistant TB as well as pulmonary cavities and sputum smear positivity-symptoms closely linked to TB infection [8]. Additionally, DM had been correlated with heightened risks for treatment complications, recurrence, and mortality in patients with TB [9]. While there were established diagnostic indicators for both DM + TB, challenges also remained due to stringent quality requirements imposed on MTB diagnostic laboratories for accurate TB diagnosis [10, 11]. Consequently, non-specialized hospitals often struggled to meet testing demands, thus leading specialized hospitals reporting lower rates of positive MTB tests. In clinical practice, exploring molecular markers independent of direct detection of MTB pathogens held substantial significance for early screening of DM + TB cases [12]. This approach was crucial for facilitating timely diagnosis as well as prevention and control measures related to this dual affliction.

The role of chemokines in MTB infection had been well established. Among these, CXC chemokine ligand 8 (CXCL8) emerged as a pivotal component of host immunity during MTB infection [13]. Monocyte-dependent fibroblast CXCL8 played a central role in the normal immune response to MTB and had been demonstrated to be essential for granuloma formation [14]. As a low molecular weight protein with potent neutrophil chemotactic properties, CXCL8 was a chemokine produced and released by white blood cells and almost any other type of cell in response to endogenous or exogenous inflammatory stimuli and confirmed to be involved in a range of diseases including cancer, cardiovascular disease, acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), cystic fibrosis, asthma, transplantation-related complications, ischemia-reperfusion injury, autoimmune disorders, et al. [15–17]. It was observed that mice induced with DM alone exhibited greater renal leukocyte accumulation alongside heightened expression of CXCL8 [18]. Furthermore, it had been reported that anti-CXCL8 monoclonal antibodies or interventions including CXCR2 (a receptor of CXCL8) knockout or antagonism could mitigate severe neutrophil infiltration and subsequent kidney damage in murine models [19]. While the roles of CXCL8 in DM and TB had been explored individually, there remained a paucity of research regarding its function and underlying mechanisms within the context of common complications arising from DM + TB.

Therefore, in this study, we aimed to investigate the expression of CXCL8 in DM + TB by including a cohort of research subjects, so as to evaluate its auxiliary diagnostic value alone or with MTB-specific cytokines Interferon-γ (IFN-γ ), Interleukin-2 (IL-2) and other indicators in DM + TB more systematically.

Methods

Study participants

Inpatients and physical examination patients from our hospital from April 2022 to May 2024 were selected. Because the focus of this study was to distinguish DM + TB patients from DM patients, individuals with DM and DM + TB were included as the study subjects. The final research subjects were determined by the clinicians based on the following inclusion and exclusion criteria. The inclusion and exclusion criteria for each group are as follows: HC group (Healthy controls), the examined subjects had no previous history of DM and the fasting blood glucose was ≤ 7.0 mmol/L. Newly diagnosed DM group, based on the clinical symptoms of “three more and one less” plus random blood glucose ≥ 11.1mmol/L. Fasting blood glucose was ≥ 7.0 mmol/L and it remained ≥ 7.0 mmol/L after a retest on a different day. Two hours after the oral glucose tolerance test, the blood glucose was > 11.1 mmol/ L and remained > 11.1 mmol/L when remeasured on a different day. The clinically diagnosed patients had at least one of the three criteria. The HC and DM groups all ruled out MTB infection based on thoracic imaging and hematological examinations. DM + TB Group, patients with newly diagnosed DM comorbidity with untreated tuberculosis were included. In addition to the characteristics of DM mentioned above, the diagnosis referred to the “Guidelines and Standards for the Diagnosis and Treatment of Pulmonary Tuberculosis”. All the included patients in the three groups were of Han ethnicity and there was no blood relationship among the research subjects. At the same time, chronic diseases such as bronchial asthma, pneumonia, pneumoconiosis, and tumors were excluded. All groups excluded patients with factors such as human immunodeficiency virus infection, other infections and organ transplantation that might affect the analysis of test results. The expressions of IFN-γ and IL-2 were obtained from the clinical laboratory measured by MTB specific cytokine detection kit from Guangzhou Di’ao Biotechnology Co., Ltd.

CXCL8 ELISA detection

Fasting venous blood 2–3 ml was collected using EDTA sodium salt as an anticoagulant. The samples were centrifuged at 2000 prm for 15 min within 30 min to collect the supernatant. Hemolyzed, hyperlipemic samples should be avoided. Plasma CXCL8 expressions were measured using an ELISA kit from R&D Systems, USA. All testing procedures were conducted in accordance with the operating instructions. Samples, diluents and reagents were prepared according to the product guidelines. A total of 100 µL of assay diluent was added to each well. Subsequently, 50 µL of sample, control and standard solutions were introduced into their respective wells, sealed and incubated for 2 h at room temperature. After incubation, the supernatant was discarded and washing steps were performed four times. Next, 100 µL of conjugate solution was added to each well, capped and incubated for an additional hour. Following this incubation period, all wells underwent another round of washing (four times). Then, 200 µL of substrate solution was dispensed into each well and incubated in darkness for 30 min. The reaction was terminated by adding 50 µL of stop solution to each well. Optical density (OD) values were subsequently measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader within half an hour. The concentration of CXCL8 in the corresponding samples was determined based on the standard curve generated during the assay in three independent experiments by a limit of 1.0–64 pg/mL (High concentration was detected by diluting the sample).

RNA extraction and RT-qPCR

The RNA extraction of PBMCs consists of two parts: the isolation of PBMCs and the RNA extraction of PBMCs. The brief steps of RNA extraction, reverse transcription and RT-qPCR of PBMCs are referred to references [20, 21]. The involved primer sequences are as follows: GAPDH Forward: GGTGGTCTCCTCTGACTTCAACA, Reverse༚TCTCTTCCTCTTGTGCTCTTGCT. CXCR1 Forward༚GAGCCCCGAATCTGACATTA, Reverse༚GCAGACACTGCAACACACCT. CXCR2 Forward༚ATTCTGGGCATCCTTCACAG, Reverse༚TGCACTTAGGCAGGAGGTCT. For detecting the expression of CXCL8 in PBMCs, RT-qPCR assays were used after 24 h stimulation with different concentrations of MTB standard strain H37Rv in vitro. Similarly, RT-qPCR assay were also used after 24 stimulation with 15.5 mmol/L glucose (DM group) and after 24 stimulation with the same concentration of glucose at simultaneous stimulation with MTB standard strain H37Rv (MOI = 10) (DM + TB group) as the in vitro experiments.

CXCL8 flow cytometry analysis

The fresh blood collection vessel was mixed upside down and 100 µL fresh whole blood collected at specific time was sucked from it. The 5 µL CXCL8-PE fluorescent antibody (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) was added to each group. Then, the mixture was shaken thoroughly and stained at room temperature in the dark for 20–30 min. Next, 1–2 mL red blood cell lysate was added to each tube, mixed thoroughly and lysed in the dark for 20 min. The samples were centrifuged at 1500 rpm for 5 min and the supernatant was aspirated. Cells were washed once, the supernatant was aspirated after centrifugation and the washing step was repeated once. The supernatant was sucked off and a certain amount of PBS was added to each flow tube, mixed and tested. The baseline was adjusted by the isotype control group, the fluorescence single staining group and the blank control group. Flow cytometry data were analyzed using Kaluza software.

Statistical analysis

GraphPad Prism version 12.0 software and SPSS 26.0 software were used for all data distribution, processing and analysis. The count data of the included cases in this study were expressed as percentages (%) and the measurement data were expressed as mean ± SD. The Specificity, Sensitivity, 95% confidence interval (95% CI) and Area under the curve (AUC) were analyzed by Receiver Operating Curve (ROC). Spearman correlation analysis was used to determine the comparisons of quantitative data between groups. The t-test was used for statistical analysis of comparisons between quantitative samples. Differences of P < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

General information of the subjects was included

In order to verify the role of CXCL8 in DM and DM + TB, 152 participants were enrolled in the study, including 58 HC controls, 49 DM patients and 45 DM + TB patients. The general conditions of the patients are shown in Table 1. As shown in Table 1, compared with the HC group, the MTB-related indicators (MTB smear, MTB culture, TB-RNA, GeneXpert detection) in the DM + TB group were significantly increased. The White blood cell count (WBC), Neutrophil count (NEC) and the NEC percentage were significantly increased. The Red blood cell (RBC) count and Hemoglobin (HGB) levels were significantly decreased. In addition, the inflammatory indexes of Erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) and C-reactive protein (CRP) were significantly increased. Compared with the DM group, the DM + TB group had significantly higher WBC count, NEC count and percentage of NEC (P < 0.001) and a significantly lower RBC count (P < 0.05) in addition to the significantly elevated MTB-related indexes mentioned above.

Expression of CXCL8 in DM and DM + TB

In order to detect the expression of CXCL8 in DM and DM + TB, we first collected the plasma samples of the above three groups and used ELISA kit to detect the expression of CXCL8 in the above groups. As in Fig. 1, CXCL8 expression in plasma of DM patients was significantly higher than that of HC group, indicating that CXCL8 expression in plasma of DM patients was significantly increased (P < 0.001, Fig. 1). To further confirm the role of CXCL8 in DM + TB, we analyzed the expression changes of CXCL8 in DM and DM + TB by ELISA and flow cytometry. As in Fig. 1, compared with the DM group, the CXCL8 concentration in plasma was significantly increased (P < 0.05), while the CXCL8 expression in PBMCs was not significantly different (Fig. 2, P > 0.05). In addition, the study found that the CXCL8 concentration in the supernatant of the MTB-stimulated group was significantly higher than that in the unstimulated group (Fig. 3). Then, We also found that the concentration of CXCL8 in the DM + TB group was significantly higher than that in the DM group (Fig. 4, P < 0.05) and the expression levels of its receptors CXCR1 and CXCR2 were also significantly higher than those in the DM group (Fig. 5, P < 0.05). These results suggested that CXCL8 were highly expressed in peripheral blood of DM and DM + TB patients both in vivo and in vitro experiments and it might play a certain role in the pathogenesis of DM and DM + TB.

Correlation analysis between CXCL8 expression in DM + TB and specific indicators of DM + TB

To determine the correlation between CXCL8 and disease-specific indicators in the DM + TB group, we analyzed clinical laboratory indicators (IFN-γ and IL-2) with disease-specific characteristics in the DM + TB group. We found that plasma CXCL8 expression was positively correlated with IFN-γ and IL-2 (r = 0.4892, P < 0.001; r = 0.5805, P < 0.001, Fig. 6a-b). Although there was a weak correlation was observed in the DM group, it was less pronounced than that in the DM + TB group (Supplementary Fig. 1). These results indicated that CXCL8 levels in DM + TB patients were closely associated with the key laboratory indicators of MTB infection.

Correlation analysis between CXCL8 expression and specific indicators in DM + TB

As CXCL8 is a low molecular weight protein with strong neutrophil chemotactic properties [22]. We therefore investigated the expression of CXCL8 in different leukocyte or neutrophil counts in the DM + TB group. We stratified by the patients according to the level of white blood cell or neutrophil count in the DM + TB group (high WBC count/high NEC percentage/high NEC count group, WBC more than 9.5 × 109/L, NEC% more than 75%, NEC count more than 6.3 × 109/L) and low expression group (low WBC count/low NEC percentage/low NEC count group, patients with values not exceeding the aforementioned cut-offs were included in this group). CXCL8 concentration was significantly higher in patients with high WBC count/high NEC percentage/high NEC count in the DM + TB group (359.15 ± 119.55 vs. 214.15 ± 52.78; 345.80 ± 118.39 vs. 206.52 ± 50.95; 336.56 ± 124.83 vs. 207.16 ± 52.79, P < 0.001; P < 0.001; P < 0.001, Fig. 7). In addition, correlation analysis revealed a close association between white blood cells or neutrophils in DM + TB group (r = 0.5111, P < 0.001; r = 0.3639, P < 0.001; r = 0.4976, P < 0.001, Fig. 8), similar findings were observed in the DM group (Supplementary Fig. 2). The above results indicate that CXCL8 concentrations is significantly increased in DM + TB patients in the setting of inflammatory infection, particularly in those with neutrophil elevation.

Analysis of the diagnostic value of CXCL8 expression and combined diagnosis in DM + TB

Since the expression of CXCL8 in DM + TB patients was significantly higher than that in DM group, CXCL8 expression in the DM + TB group was closely associated with the two aforementioned cytokines. Therefore, we further analyzed the diagnostic value of CXCL8 detection alone and in combination with the above indicators in DM + TB. Our study found that CXCL8 alone differentiated DM and DM + TB with a sensitivity of 65.31%, a specificity of 68.89% and an AUC of 0.7265, 95%CI of 0.6225–0.8306 (Fig. 9). The sensitivity and specificity of CXCL8 combined with IFN-γ to distinguish DM and DM + TB were 85.71% and 80.00% and the AUC was 0.8971 (95%CI: 0.8246–0.9695). The sensitivity and specificity of the combination of CXCL8 and IL-2 to distinguish DM and DM + TB were 75.51%, 66.67% and the AUC was 0.8005, 95%CI was 0.7092–0.8918 (Fig. 10). The sensitivity and specificity of CXCL8 combined with IFN-γ and IL-2 to distinguish DM from DM + TB were 89.80% and 88.89% and the AUC was 0.9175, 95%CI was 0.8475–0.9874 (Fig. 10). These results indicated that CXCL8 could effectively distinguish DM + TB from DM and its combined detection with the above two indicators could improve the diagnostic sensitivity and specificity of DM + TB.

Discussion

The clinical diagnosis of DM + TB, particularly the differentiation between DM + TB patients and those with conventional DM, primarily relied on specific diagnostic indicators for TB [23]. However, these indicators had notable limitations. Consequently, there was a pressing need for the development of new molecular diagnostic markers. Our study identified CXCL8 as a significant chemokine that was markedly elevated in both the DM and DM + TB groups, especially within the DM + TB cohort. This suggested that CXCL8 may serve as a potential molecular target for diagnosing DM + TB. As important receptor molecules of CXCL8, the expressions of CXCR1 and CXCR2 were also significantly increased in the DM + TB group. Furthermore, CXCL8 exhibited a significant correlation with key markers of tuberculosis infection (IFN-γ and IL-2) as well as neutrophil-related parameters.

The role of IFN-γ in the development and immune response to TB was well-established [24]. Research had demonstrated that IFN-γ is primarily produced by activated T lymphocytes and natural killer (NK) cells [25]. Furthermore, in TB diagnosis, IFN-γ had been shown to possess significant laboratory auxiliary value, as evidenced by tests such as the Tuberculin Skin Test (TST) and Interferon Gamma Release Assay (IGRA) [26]. Additionally, as a cytokine, IFN-γ played a crucial role in the onset and progression of DM [27]. Concurrently, IFN-γ influenced the metabolic functions of adipocytes, hepatocytes, and other cell types, thereby indirectly affecting normal blood glucose metabolism [28]. However, when considering DM + TB, the role of IFN-γ became more complex [29, 30]. Consequently, IFN-γ might play a significant role in the interplay between TB, DM, and DM + TB. Our study identified CXCL8 combined with IFN-γ as a molecular target that offered improved diagnostic efficacy for distinguishing DM from DM + TB.

IL-2 also played crucial roles in the development of TB and DM. During the early stages of MTB infection, IL-2 could activate various immune cells to exert protective effects against MTB [31]. Additionally, IL-2 had been shown to be significant in the context of DM by stimulating the proliferation and activation of T lymphocytes and being involved in the autoimmune attack on pancreatic β cells [32]. Studies had indicated that IL-2 might influence disease progression by regulating both immune responses and inflammation [33–35]. It could indirectly affect inflammation within adipose tissue and muscle tissue closely associated with insulin resistance [36]. Our study found that CXCL8 exhibited a positive correlation with IL-2. It not only held diagnostic value for distinguishing between DM and DM + TB but also demonstrated enhanced diagnostic efficacy when combined with IL-2.

Limitations of the study

It is important to acknowledge certain limitations within this study. Specifically, it was conducted as a single-center investigation with constraints on sample size. Future research will aim to expand the sample size and incorporate multi-center studies to further elucidate the role of CXCL8 in DM + TB.

Conclusions

Through this study, we found that the expression of CXCL8 in the plasma of patients with DM and DM + TB was significantly elevated. This increase was notably correlated with levels of IFN-γ, IL-2 and various parameters related to neutrophils. The combination of CXCL8, IFN-γ and IL-2 demonstrated a significant enhancement in diagnostic efficiency.

Anti-inflammatory efficacy of diosgenin in an oxazolone-induced model of ulcerative colitis in mice via suppression of Th2 pathways and pSTAT3 expression: A prospective study
Authors: Ashwini Narayan, Priya Babu Shubha, Syed Baker, Niranjan Raj Sathyanarayana, Faiyaz Ahmed, Nanjunda Swamy Shivananju
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThis prospective study evaluated the therapeutic efficacy of Diosgenin in mitigating Oxazolone-induced ulcerative colitis in male BALB/c mice.MethodMice aged 10–11 weeks were randomized into five groups and subjected to a nine-day protocol involving skin sensitization and intra-rectal Oxazolone challenge. Diosgenin was administered orally at 3 mg/kg and 30 mg/kg twice daily, with Tofacitinib at 60 mg/kg as a reference control. Disease severity was assessed via clinical activity scores, which include tested parameters.ResultsOxazolone-induced acute colitis was characterized by significant weight loss, increased diarrhoea scores, and an >8-fold increase in rectal bleeding scores compared with vehicle-treated mice, confirming successful disease induction (p < 0.05). Diosgenin-treated mice showed significant recovery. Disease Activity Index (DAI) scores improved by 40% in the 3 mg/kg Diosgenin group, 45% in the 30 mg/kg Diosgenin group, and 67% in the Tofacitinib-treated group by Day 2. Stool consistency improved by 52%, 33%, and 49% on Day 1, and by 33%, 61%, and 64% on Day 2 in the 3 mg/kg and 30 mg/kg Diosgenin groups, and the 60 mg/kg Tofacitinib groups respectively. Molecular analysis revealed a dose-dependent suppression of Th2 cytokines: IL-4 levels were reduced by 71%, 90%, and 86%, and IL-13 levels by 75%, 79%, and 90% in the Diosgenin 3mg/kg and 30 mg/kg groups, and Tofacitinib 60 mg/kg group respectively. Diosgenin markedly suppressed colonic pSTAT3 expression, with reductions of 92% and 99%, in the Diosgenin 3mg/kg and 30 mg/kg groups and >90% in the Tofacitinib 60 mg/kg group.ConclusionThese findings suggest Diosgenin offers substantial protection against Oxazolone-induced colitis via cytokine modulation and pSTAT3 inhibition, highlighting its potential as a natural anti-inflammatory agent for colitis management.

Introduction

Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD) is defined as one of the chronic disorder characterized by inflammation of the gastrointestinal tract, encompassing Ulcerative Colitis (UC) and Crohn’s Disease. 1 UC is reported to be as continuous inflammation and ulceration confined to the colon mucosal layer, whereas Crohn’s disease involves transmural inflammation affecting any part of the gastrointestinal tract.2,3 Both conditions commonly present with diarrhoea, abdominal pain, fatigue, and weight loss, reflecting the systemic and local consequences of chronic intestinal inflammation. 4 The etiology of IBD is multifactorial, involving complex interactions among genetic, environmental, and immunological factors.5,6 Genetic predisposition contributes to impaired epithelial barrier function and dysregulated immune responses, while environmental triggers, such as diet, infections, and microbial imbalance, further exacerbate inflammation. 7 Immunologically, IBD is characterized by inappropriate modulation of both innate and adaptive immune pathways, leading to persistent mucosal injury. 8 Cytokines, chemokines, and transcription factors orchestrate this inflammatory cascade, with key molecules like interleukin-13, tumor necrosis factor-alpha and interleukin-4, playing pivotal roles. 9 Advancement in the IBD pathophysiology has resulted in informed search of novel strategies and targets. These therapies aim to restore mucosal homeostasis, reduce inflammation, and prevent disease progression. 10 Ongoing research continues to elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying IBD, providing insights into personalized approaches for disease management and highlighting the potential for emerging natural and synthetic compounds as adjunctive treatments. 11

Inflammation in UC is predominantly limited to the mucosa, with classifications including ulcerative proctitis, proctosigmoiditis, and pancolitis depending on the extent of colonic involvement. 12 Ulcerative proctitis is restricted to the rectal mucosa, proctosigmoiditis extends to the sigmoid colon, and pancolitis involves the entire colon, with disease severity generally correlating with the extent of inflammation. These classifications have clinical relevance, as they influence symptom severity, risk of complications, and therapeutic strategies. 13 Aberrant immune responses to environmental antigens, including intestinal microbiota, drive chronic inflammation, mediated largely by cytokines such as IL-13 and IL-4. 14 Dysregulated Th2-mediated immune responses in UC result in excessive production of these cytokines, which contribute to epithelial barrier dysfunction, mucosal injury, and recruitment of additional inflammatory cells. Persistent cytokine-driven signalling leads to a self-perpetuating cycle of inflammation and tissue damage. The JAK-STAT signalling pathway, activated by cytokines, plays a pivotal role in inflammatory responses, which triggers immune response, transcriptional activation of genes involved in immune cell proliferation, differentiation, and survival. 15 Dysregulation of this pathway is closely associated with disease progression and chronicity. Pharmacological inhibition of the JAK-STAT pathway forms the basis of therapies like Tofacitinib, a selective JAK inhibitor that reduces cytokine production and downstream inflammatory signalling. 16 Targeting this pathway offers a mechanistically precise approach to controlling mucosal inflammation, preventing tissue damage, and restoring intestinal homeostasis in patients with UC.

Experimental models of colitis provide valuable insights into the pathogenesis, immunological mechanisms, and potential therapeutic targets of IBD. Among these, the Oxazolone-induced colitis model in BALB/c mice is widely recognized for its relevance to human UC, as it closely mimics key pathological and immunological features of the disease. Oxazolone is a haptenating agent that functions as a delayed-type hypersensitivity inducer. Topical administration of 3% Oxazolone serves as the sensitization step in the Oxazolone-induced colitis model. On the skin, Oxazolone binds to host proteins, forming hapten-protein conjugates that prime Oxazolone-specific T cells. These cells circulate systemically and, upon intrarectal re-exposure, recognize Oxazolone-modified antigens in the colonic mucosa. This challenge phase provokes a robust Th2-mediated immune response, characterized by cytokine release (IL-4, IL-5 and IL-13), epithelial damage, and mucosal infiltration. The two-step process sensitization followed by challenge reliably induces inflammation resembling ulcerative colitis, making it a valuable experimental model for studying disease mechanisms and therapeutic interventions. 17 Histopathological analyses of this model reveal mucosal damage, epithelial erosion, and immune cell infiltration, reflecting the clinical and histological hallmarks observed in human UC. 18 Such experimental systems are essential for evaluating the anti-inflammatory efficacy of novel compounds and understanding their mechanisms of action at both molecular and cellular levels. Natural compounds with anti-inflammatory properties have attracted increasing attention as potential therapeutic agents for IBD due to their pleiotropic pharmacological effects and favourable safety profiles. 19 Diosgenin, a steroidal sapogenin acts as a precursor molecule in synthesizing myriad medications, demonstrates diverse biological activities, including anti-inflammatory, anticancer, and metabolic regulatory effects. 20 Diosgenin is synthesized in plants through the mevalonate pathway, proceeding via cycloartenol- or lanosterol-mediated routes, which converge to form the steroidal backbone essential for its bioactivity. 21 The unique chemical structure and pharmacological versatility of Diosgenin make it a promising candidate for evaluating therapeutic interventions in experimental models of colitis. 22 Keeping these considerations, the aim of the study is to investigate the anti-inflammatory potential of Diosgenin in oxazolone-induced colitis in BALB/c mice by evaluating its effects on key inflammatory cytokines and signaling pathways. The oxazolone model, a Th2-dominant system, mirrors human ulcerative colitis with elevated IL-4 and IL-13 levels, epithelial micro-ulcerations, and distal colon inflammation. Diosgenin’s impact on cytokine regulation, JAK-STAT signaling, colon morphology, and clinical disease activity was assessed to determine its therapeutic efficacy. By elucidating these molecular and cellular mechanisms, the study aims to validate Diosgenin as a potential natural therapeutic agent for ulcerative colitis and provide insights into safer, effective anti-inflammatory strategies.

Methods

Animals

Sixty male BALB/c mice, 23–25 g and 10–11 weeks old were selected based on literature reports highlighting their stable immunological profile and suitability for colitis models. 23 BALB/c mice exhibit a Th2-biased immune response, making them ideal for studying cytokine-mediated inflammation and epithelial barrier dysfunction in chemically induced colitis models. 24 Mice were procured from Vivo Biotech, India, and housed in polycarbonate cages under a 12-hour light/dark cycle with ad libitum access to Harlan diet and water. 25 Following a two-week acclimation, animals were randomized into five groups (n=11–13) based on body weight, ensuring no significant intergroup differences and <20% intra-group variability. All procedures were conducted in strict accordance with the Institutional Animal Ethics Committee guidelines (Protocol/Approval No. VBPL/IAEC/017/2022 dated 03 March 2022), ensuring compliance and reproducibility. Animals were monitored twice daily for body weight loss, rectal bleeding, lethargy, and dehydration. Humane endpoints included >20% body weight loss, severe rectal prolapse, inability to access food or water, or severe distress requiring immediate euthanasia. All experimental procedures, including Oxazolone sensitization, intra-rectal challenge, treatment administration, clinical monitoring, and euthanasia, were performed in accordance with the ARRIVE 2.0 guidelines. 26 The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Institutional Animal Ethics Committee (IAEC) of VAARUNYA BIOLABS PRIVATE LIMITED under Protocol/Approval No. VBPL/IAEC/017/2022 dated 03 March 2022, which facilitated the ethical review and approval process for the study. The number of animals used was restricted to the minimum required to obtain statistically reliable results, and all possible efforts were made to minimize pain, stress, and suffering throughout the experimental procedures in accordance with institutional ethical guidelines. At the end of the study, animals were sacrificed under gradual-fill CO2 inhalation using 100% CO2 at a displacement rate of 50% of the chamber volume per minute. Death was confirmed by the absence of respiration and heartbeat, together with loss of reflexes, prior to tissue collection.

Reagent preparation

Oxazolone (4-Ethoxymethylene-2-phenyl-2-oxazolin-5-one; Sigma-Aldrich, USA) was purchased commercially and prepared according to the protocol described by Mäenpää et al., 27 with slight modification for skin sensitization and intra-rectal administration. Further, for rectal induction of colitis, 8mg/ml of Oxazolone was prepared by dissolving it in 50% ethanol following established protocols. 28 Diosgenin and Tofacitinib (Sigma-Aldrich, USA) were prepared as a suspension in 0.5% methylcellulose (w/v) solution 29 (Sigma-Aldrich, USA) and administered orally at doses of 3 mg/kg and 30 mg/kg for Diosgenin, and 60 mg/kg for Tofacitinib respectively. Reagents such as olive oil, acetone, and ethanol were procured from Sigma-Aldrich.

Oxazolone-induced ulcerative colitis induction and treatment

The acute colitis induction protocol in BALB/c mice was adapted from Yang et al., 30 with slight modification spanning a total of nine days, comprising a skin sensitization phase followed by intra-rectal challenge to elicit intestinal inflammation (Figure 1(a)). The acute colitis induction protocol in BALB/c mice was adapted from Yang et al.,30 with slight modifications and consisted of a skin sensitization phase followed by an intra-rectal challenge. For skin sensitization, Oxazolone was prepared at 30 mg/mL in ethanol, and 100 µL was applied topically to the shaved dorsal skin of each mouse, corresponding to a sensitization dose of 3 mg per mouse. On day 6, animals were fasted for 4 hours prior to intra-rectal challenge. Skin sensitization is essential for establishing a Th2-driven immune response and ensuring reproducible induction of colitis in this experimental model. For colitis induction, a separate Oxazolone solution was prepared at 8 mg/mL in 50% aqueous ethanol. Under Ketamine–Xylazine anaesthesia, each mouse received 50 µL of this solution via a polypropylene gavage tube inserted to the anal verge, corresponding to an intra-rectal challenge dose of 0.4 mg Oxazolone per mouse. Following administration, mice were maintained in an upright position for one minute to minimize reflux and ensure uniform distribution of the challenge solution within the distal colon. 31 Anesthetic preparation consisted of Ketamine (50 mg/mL) at 100 mg/kg (2.0 mL/kg) combined with Xylazine (20 mg/mL) at 8 mg/kg (0.4 mL/kg). Doses were calculated based on individual body weight and diluted in sterile saline to a final injection volume of 10 mL/kg, delivered intraperitoneally. 32 Ketamine–Xylazine anesthesia was selected because it is a widely accepted regimen for short-term anesthesia in mice, providing effective sedation, analgesia, and muscle relaxation during oxazolone administration. Diosgenin treatment commenced one day prior to Oxazolone challenge and continued until day 2. Diosgenin was administered orally at 3 and 30 mg/kg, while Tofacitinib was dosed at 60 mg/kg. Although Tofacitinib is frequently used at doses of 10–30 mg/kg in chronic murine colitis studies, a dose of 60 mg/kg was selected in the present acute oxazolone-induced colitis model to provide robust pharmacological inhibition of inflammatory signalling during the short experimental period. Therefore, Tofacitinib served as a positive control for efficacy comparison rather than as a clinically translatable dosing regimen. The Oxazolone-induced colitis model represents an acute Th2-mediated inflammatory response that develops within 24 hours of intra-rectal challenge. Therefore, a 48-hour treatment window was selected to evaluate early anti-inflammatory effects during the peak phase of cytokine activation. Successful colitis induction was confirmed by clinical scoring (body weight loss, diarrhoea, haemoccult positivity) and molecular elevation of IL-4, IL-13, and pSTAT3. 33

Clinical activity scoring for severity of colitis

Clinical severity in Oxazolone-induced colitis was evaluated using a composite scoring system incorporating body weight changes, stool consistency, and rectal bleeding. This approach enables quantitative assessment of disease progression and therapeutic efficacy in BALB/c mice. Body weight loss was scored as described in Table 1. Stool consistency was assessed using a three-tier scale: a score of 0 was assigned to well-formed pellets, 2 to semi-formed or mixed hard/soft pellets not adhering to the anus, and 4 to watery stools with perianal adherence. Occult blood was detected using the guaiac-based Haemoccult test. 34 Bleeding severity was scored as 0 for a negative Haemoccult test, 2 for a positive test, and 4 for gross bleeding. Measurement of cytokine mRNA expression levels by RT-PCR

The mice were sacrificed after nine days of Oxazolone treatment. The abdominal cavity was opened, and the colon was excised carefully to avoid stretching. After blot-drying, colon length was measured. 35 The caecum was removed, and the colon was longitudinally opened to expose the mucosa. The distal segment was halved for RNA and protein analyses.

Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent minor modifications and Colon tissue was rinsed in ice-cold saline and further homogenized in 200 µL TRIzol (3–4 cycles, 2500 rpm, 4 °C) using a tissue homogenizer. After addition of 100 µL chloroform and centrifugation (14,000 rpm, 20 min, 4 °C), the aqueous phase was treated with equal volume of isopropanol. After incubating for 10 min the mixture was subjected to cold centrifugation (9500 rpm, 10 min, 4 °C) and RNA pellet was washed twice with 70% ethanol. Followed by drying the RNA pellet was re-suspended in 30 µL of TE buffer. The purity was determined at 260/280 nm spectrophotometrically.

cDNA synthesis was performed using 1 µg RNA with a Bio-Rad kit. qRT-PCR targeted IL-4 and IL-13 as inflammatory markers, with GAPDH as the reference gene. Primer sequences were: IL-4 (F: 5′-CCC​CCA​GCT​AGT​TGT​CAT​CC-3′, R: 5′-AGG​ACG​TTT​GGC​ACA​TCC​AT-3′), IL-13 (F: 5′-TGC​CAT​CTA​CAG​GAC​CCA​GA-3′, R: 5′-CTC​ATT​AGA​AGG​GGC​CGT​GG-3′), and GAPDH (F: 5′-ATC​TTC​TGT​GCA​GTG​CCA​G-3′, R: 5′-GTA​GTT​GAG​GTC​AAT​GAA​GG-3′). qPCR was performed with SYBR Green Master Mix and relative expression was calculated by the 2-ΔΔCt method. The IL-4 and IL-13 primer pairs were selected to quantify total gene transcript expression and were not intended to distinguish individual splice variants. Accordingly, the results reported in this study reflect overall cytokine mRNA abundance in colonic tissue.

Measurement of cytokine protein levels by western blotting

The homogenized tissue samples in RIPA buffer supplemented with a protease inhibitor were incubated on ice for 30 minutes and the tissue lysate was subsequently centrifuged at 13,000 rpm for 10 minutes at 4 °C. The supernatant was separated determined for total protein concentrations.

Equal amounts of protein were dissolved in loading buffer and denatured. Approximately 10 µg proteins were resolved on 10% SDS-PAGE and transferred onto 0.45 µm Nitrocellulose membranes (Millipore, USA). 36 Membranes were blocked with PBST (Phosphate-buffered saline with 0.1% Tween-20) containing 5% BSA (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) for 1 hour at room temperature to prevent nonspecific binding. Following three washes with PBST, membranes were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies against phosphorylated STAT3 (pSTAT3; Cell Signaling Technology, Danvers, MA, USA; Cat. #9145; Clone D3A7; Lot #43; RRID:AB_2491009) and β-actin (loading control; Cell Signaling Technology, Danvers, MA, USA; Cat. #4970S; Clone 13E5; Lot #19; RRID:AB_2223172), each applied at a dilution of 1:2000. After washing, membranes were probed with the HRP-conjugated secondary antibody (anti-rabbit IgG; Cell Signaling Technology, Danvers, MA, USA; Cat. #7074; Lot #35; RRID:AB_2099233) applied according to the manufacturer’s instructions. After incubation with appropriate HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies, immunoreactive bands were visualized using a chemiluminescence (ECL) substrate (Merck, Germany) and documented using a gel documentation system. Densitometric analysis of each target protein band was normalized to β-actin to account for loading variability. This workflow enabled the detection and quantification of inflammation-associated signaling proteins in colonic tissue.

Statistical analysis

GraphPad Prism 5.0 software was employed for statistical analysis to determine mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). ANOVA and Dunnett’s post hoc test were conducted for multiple comparisons. For parameters assessed over time (e.g., body weight and DAI), one way ANOVA was performed, followed by Dunnett’s post hoc test to compare treatment groups with the control, where applicable. A p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. Significance levels were denoted as ### p<0.001, ## p<0.01, # p<0.05 versus vehicle control; and *** p<0.001, ** p<0.01, * p<0.05 versus disease group.

Results

Impact of diosgenin on oxazolone-induced colitis

All animals remained healthy throughout the study, with no mortality or significant differences in body weight observed between disease and treatment groups (Figure 1(b)). The body weights were monitored on day1 and day2 and the changes were recorded as cited in Figure 1(c) and (d). Mice administered Oxazolone developed colitis rapidly, as evidenced by reduced body weight, diarrhoea and blood-stained stools. In contrast, the vehicle-treated group showed marginal gain in body weight. Oral administration of Diosgenin at doses of 3 mg/kg and 30 mg/kg facilitated prompt recovery from Oxazolone-induced colitis, marked by weight gain, improved stool consistency, and reduced incidence of bloody stools (Figure 2(a) and (b)). Colon health was assessed at the study endpoint (Figure 2(c)). Disease Activity Index (DAI) was calculated based on weight changes, stool consistency, and presence of blood (Table 1). The disease control group showed pronounced disease severity, including significant weight loss and watery, bloody feces, compared to the vehicle group, resulting in a markedly elevated DAI score (Figure 3(a) and (b)). Body weight response to diosgenin treatment

Diosgenin treatment did not significantly influence body weight in BALB/c mice subjected to Oxazolone-induced colitis, a Th2-mediated model that mimics ulcerative colitis pathology. Mice were sensitized topically with Oxazolone on day −6 and challenged intra-rectally on day 0, resulting in acute weight loss by day 1 due to acute mucosal inflammation and epithelial damage, which impair nutrient absorption and trigger systemic immune activation. However, spontaneous recovery was observed by day 2 across all groups, including those treated with Diosgenin at 3 mg/kg and 30 mg/kg, vehicle, and Tofacitinib. Percent body weight changes relative to day −1 and day 0 were plotted to assess treatment impact. Data are presented as mean ± SEM (n=12 control, Oxazolone, Diosgenin 30 mg/kg group; n=11 Diosgenin 3 mg/kg group; n=13 Tofacitinib). No statistically significant differences observed among groups, and intra-group variation remained below 20%, indicating that body weight was not a sensitive marker for therapeutic efficacy in this short-term model.

Diosgenin improved faecal consistency and reduced haemoccult scores in oxazolone-induced colitis

BALB/c mice were evaluated for stool consistency and occult blood on days 1 and 2 following intra-rectal Oxazolone challenge. The Oxazolone-only group (0 mg/kg Diosgenin) exhibited significantly elevated fecal and haemoccult scores compared to the vehicle group, confirming successful disease induction. Diosgenin-treated groups (3 mg/kg and 30 mg/kg) showed dose-dependent improvement in both parameters, comparable to the Tofacitinib reference group. Post-euthanasia colon images on day 2 revealed reduced inflammation and restored morphology in Diosgenin and Tofacitinib-treated mice, while the disease group displayed anal blockage, rectal bleeding, and colonic swelling.

Disease Activity Index reveals therapeutic efficacy of diosgenin in oxazolone-induced colitis

DAI scores were evaluated on days 1 and 2 following intra-rectal Oxazolone challenge in BALB/c mice to assess the therapeutic impact of Diosgenin and Tofacitinib. DAI was calculated using three parameters: percent change in body weight, faecal consistency, and haemoccult score. Two reference points were used for body weight normalization; day −1 and day 0. When normalized to day −1, Diosgenin at 30 mg/kg and Tofacitinib at 60 mg/kg showed decline in DAI scores with respect to disease control group, indicating effective mitigation of colitis symptoms. Using day 0 as reference, Tofacitinib-treated mice showed the most pronounced reduction in DAI, while Diosgenin 30 mg/kg exhibited moderate improvement. The Oxazolone-only group (0 mg/kg Diosgenin) demonstrated significantly elevated DAI scores, confirming successful disease induction relative to vehicle controls. These findings support the anti-inflammatory potential of Diosgenin in acute Th2-mediated colitis models and validate DAI scoring as a sensitive endpoint for therapeutic evaluation.

Diosgenin downregulates IL-4 and IL-13 expression in colitis

IL-4 and IL-13 mRNA levels was significantly upregulated in the Oxazolone disease control group relative to the vehicle control group. Treatment with Diosgenin, particularly at 30 mg/kg, markedly reduced the expression of both cytokines. The magnitude of suppression was comparable to that observed in the Tofacitinib-treated group. Statistical analysis confirmed significant differences between disease and treatment groups (Figure 3(c)). For analysis of IL-4 and IL-13, five mice per group were evaluated for molecular analyses, five animals per group were processed because of tissue and assay requirements.

Diosgenin reduces pSTAT3 activation in oxazolone-induced colitis

Phosphorylated STAT3 (pSTAT3) plays a pivotal role in regulating intestinal epithelial cell fate and modulating innate immune responses during colitis. In the Oxazolone-induced colitis model, pSTAT3 levels were significantly higher up to fourfold in the disease control group compared to vehicle-treated mice, indicating active inflammatory signalling. Treatment with Diosgenin at both 3 mg/kg and 30 mg/kg, as well as Tofacitinib at 60 mg/kg, resulted in a pronounced reduction in pSTAT3 expression, exceeding tenfold relative to the disease group (Figure 4). This suppression suggests that Diosgenin effectively attenuates STAT3-mediated inflammatory pathways, contributing to mucosal recovery. β-actin was used as the housekeeping gene for normalization, and its expression remained consistent across all groups, validating the reliability of the qPCR data. Discussion

Murine models are widely used in biomedical research to study inflammatory bowel diseases such as ulcerative colitis (UC). 37 In this study, Oxazolone-induced colitis models were selected for their ability to replicate Th2-mediated mucosal inflammation, characterized by epithelial disruption, edema, immune infiltration, and ulceration which is well established in previous studies. 38 This model features IL-4 and IL-13 cytokines typical of Th2 responses. 39 Also, the UC pathogenesis involves impaired mucosal integrity and increased epithelial permeability making this model valuable for testing therapies targeting epithelial and cytokine modulation. 40 Based on these facts and considerations the present study was designed to evaluate the therapeutic efficacy of Diosgenin, a steroidal sapogenin derived from Dioscorea species, in BALB/c mice subjected to Oxazolone-induced ulcerative colitis. Following Oxazolone challenge, colonic inflammation was rapidly initiated and reached its peak around day 8 post-sensitization describing the acute phase of Th2-mediated mucosal inflammation in this model. 41 The temporal pattern of disease progression indicates successful induction of colitis, thereby validating the experimental design. Diosgenin, administered twice daily was evaluated for its potential anti-inflammatory efficacy in comparison to the reference compound, Tofacitinib, administered at 60 mg/kg. The inclusion of Tofacitinib, a well-established JAK inhibitor, provides a pharmacological benchmark, enabling assessment of Diosgenin’s therapeutic potential in modulating Oxazolone-induced colonic inflammation. Rectal administration of Oxazolone in BALB/c mice successfully induced acute colitis, as evidenced by the passage of blood-stained faecal pellets, reflecting severe mucosal injury and active inflammation. 42 Notably, treatment with Diosgenin at both 3 mg/kg and 30 mg/kg, reversed these pathological symptoms. By day 2 post-induction, faecal consistency scores confirmed the establishment of a pronounced disease phenotype in the Oxazolone group, particularly when the compound was delivered in methylcellulose. In contrast, Diosgenin treatment markedly improved stool consistency and reduced visible bleeding, demonstrating its therapeutic efficacy in mitigating acute mucosal damage. Similarly, the reference compound Tofacitinib (60 mg/kg), a selective JAK-STAT pathway inhibitor produced comparable improvements, supporting the anti-inflammatory potential of Diosgenin and suggesting its possible mechanistic overlap with established immunomodulatory agents.

Diosgenin exhibited pronounced anti-inflammatory activity in the Oxazolone-induced colitis model, with the 30 mg/kg dose producing therapeutic outcomes in comparison with control group. Disease Activity Index (DAI) scores which incorporate weight loss, stool consistency, and rectal bleeding were markedly elevated in the disease group but significantly attenuated following Diosgenin administration. These clinical improvements were further supported by gross and colon morphology. Diosgenin improved gross colon morphology and reduced visible haemorrhagic features in Oxazolone-induced colitis, consistent with the observed improvement in Disease Activity Index and cytokine suppression. Collectively, these findings highlight Diosgenin’s potential to mitigate both the functional and structural manifestations of Th2-mediated colitis, suggesting a promising therapeutic role in the management of ulcerative colitis–like inflammation as per the earlier studies.43,44 Further, study also reports on immunomodulatory effects of Diosgenin in BALB/c mice with Oxazolone-induced ulcerative colitis (UC), focusing on Th2 cytokine expression and STAT3 signaling. Quantitative PCR analysis revealed significant up-regulation of IL-4 and IL-13 mRNA in the disease group. Conclusion

The present study demonstrates that Diosgenin, a steroidal sapogenin derived from Dioscorea species, exerts significant therapeutic effects in BALB/c mice with Oxazolone-induced ulcerative colitis. The Oxazolone model successfully reproduced Th2-mediated mucosal inflammation, characterized by epithelial injury, edema, and immune cell infiltration, validating its relevance to ulcerative colitis pathophysiology. Diosgenin treatment, particularly at 30 mg/kg, markedly improved clinical outcomes by reducing Disease Activity Index scores, ameliorating stool consistency, and diminishing visible bleeding. Colon morphology analyses further confirmed substantial recovery of mucosal architecture, including attenuation of epithelial ulceration and inflammatory cell infiltration. At the molecular level, Diosgenin suppressed key Th2 cytokines (IL-4 and IL-13) and significantly reduced phosphorylated STAT3 expression, indicating that its anti-inflammatory activity may involve modulation of the JAK-STAT signalling pathway. These effects were comparable to those of Tofacitinib, a known JAK inhibitor, underscoring Diosgenin’s potential as a natural immunomodulatory compound. Collectively, these findings establish Diosgenin as one of the significant therapeutic candidates for the treatment of ulcerative colitis, capable of restoring mucosal integrity, reducing Th2-driven inflammation, and targeting STAT3-dependent signalling mechanisms. Further mechanistic and translational studies are warranted to elucidate its clinical applicability and long-term safety profile.

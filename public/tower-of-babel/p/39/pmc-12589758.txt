The Reg gene family: roles in gastric and colorectal cancer progression and its potential for targeted therapy
Authors: Fei Liao, Xun Xiao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe management of human malignancies presents ongoing challenges in modern medicine, particularly given the high morbidity and mortality associated with gastrointestinal cancers. Despite advances in therapeutic strategies, tumor complexity and evolving resistance mechanisms persistently hinder effective clinical intervention. The Regenerating gene (Reg) family comprises small secreted proteins that have garnered considerable research interest in oncology owing to their multifunctional properties. These proteins play dual roles in maintaining gastrointestinal homeostasis while simultaneously driving key pathophysiological processes including tumor proliferation, invasion, metastasis, and therapeutic resistance, thereby emerging as promising targets for early diagnosis and precision therapies. This review systematically elucidates the expression dynamics and functional roles of Reg genes in gastrointestinal malignancies, analyzing their intricate crosstalk with cellular signaling pathways and focusing particularly on tumor microenvironment modulation mechanisms. The review aims to explore the role of the Reg gene family in gastrointestinal tumors and assess its potential as both diagnostic markers and therapeutic targets in clinical practice.Keywords: Reg gene family, Reg, Gastric cancer, Colorectal cancer, Biomarkers

Introduction

Gastrointestinal malignancies constitute major global health burdens, exhibiting high morbidity and mortality worldwide [1]. World Health Organization (WHO) data reveal gastric and colorectal cancers rank as the fifth and third most prevalent malignancies globally, with mortality rates occupying the fifth and second positions respectively [2]. Tumorigenesis and progression are fundamentally driven by intrinsic genetic determinants that orchestrate both oncogenic initiation and disease evolution [3]. Deciphering tumorigenic gene expression dynamics provides critical insights into malignant transformation mechanisms, facilitating the development of precision diagnostics and targeted therapeutic interventions [4, 5]. The identification of novel theranostic biomarkers remains pivotal in translational oncology, where tumor-overexpressed genes—particularly those encoding secreted or transmembrane proteins—serve as prime candidates for diagnostic applications [6, 7]. Crucially, gene products actively mediating tumorigenesis pathways not only elucidate molecular pathogenesis but also constitute druggable targets for precision therapeutic interventions [8, 9].

The Reg gene family, a cluster of calcium-dependent secretory lectins [10, 11], orchestrates pivotal biological processes including cellular proliferation, inflammatory modulation, antimicrobial defense, and neurodevelopmental regulation [12–14]. The Reg gene family comprises four structurally distinct subtypes—Reg1, Reg2, Reg3, and Reg4—classified by their encoded proteins’ primary sequences [15]. In the human body, the main subtypes expressed are Reg1, Reg3, and Reg4 [16, 17]. Emerging evidence positions Reg genes as dual-functional regulators, maintaining gastrointestinal homeostasis through physiological expression while exerting substantial pathophysiological modulation in disease progression [18]. Despite established knowledge of the Reg gene family’s multifaceted roles in cancer, critical gaps persist, particularly in member-specific mechanisms across malignancies and their interactions with the tumor microenvironment [19]. This study investigated the Reg gene family’s expression patterns, functional profiles, and signaling pathway crosstalk in gastrointestinal tumors, aiming to identify novel diagnostic/therapeutic targets and advance their clinical translation.

Human Reg gene family members and the composition of their encoded proteins

The inaugural member of the Reg gene family was identified in 1988 by Terazono et al. [20, 21]. This member not only promotes pancreatic β-cell regeneration in vitro and in vivo but also exhibits structural similarities to other family members in chromosomal organization and exon count. Despite these shared features, individual family members display distinct gene expression patterns and functional roles [22].

The Reg1 gene, located in the p12 region of human chromosome 2, is a 3.0-kb monogenic locus comprising 6 exons and 5 introns. Its mRNA encodes a 166-amino acid protein. Reg1 produces two isoforms—Reg1A and Reg1B—which are adjacent on the chromosome and share 87% sequence identity at the amino acid level [15]. The Reg3 gene, also mapped to chromosome 2p12, encodes a 175-amino acid protein with a 5-residue insertion at its C-terminus [23]. In contrast to other family members localized on chromosome 2, the Reg4 gene resides on chromosome 1, spans 17,557 base pairs, and contains a 474-bp open reading frame interrupted by 6 introns. The secreted protein encoded by Reg4 consists of 158 amino acids, including a 22-amino acid signal peptide and a conserved calcium-dependent carbohydrate recognition domain (CRD) [24] (Table 1). Notably, Reg family members share significant sequence similarity with C-type lectins, which are characterized by a CRD [22]. This domain likely mediates the biological processes driven by Reg4 and other Reg family proteins in human malignancies and other diseases [25].

The Reg gene family and gastric cancer

Gastric cancer remains one of the most prevalent malignancies worldwide, with high incidence and mortality rates. Globally, 1.089 million new cases were reported in 2020, alongside 769,000 deaths [26]. China contributed 44% of global cases and 48.6% of deaths in the same year, highlighting its disproportionate burden of this disease [27, 28]. The insidious nature of early-stage gastric cancer often leads to delayed diagnosis, resulting in poor prognosis [29]. Current management of advanced gastric cancer includes chemoradiotherapy, palliative care, and nutritional support. Advances in molecular profiling of gastric carcinogenesis have driven the development of targeted therapies and immunotherapies, which show efficacy in biomarker-selected patients [30].

The Reg1 gene and gastric cancer

Chronic inflammation drives tumor progression, and gastric carcinogenesis is tightly linked to inflammatory microenvironment dynamics [31]. Studies reveal that the Reg1 expression within the gastric mucosa, especially in enterochromaffin-like cells (ECL), is significant [32], and its expression is regulated by gastrin, serving as a growth-promoting agent to stimulate the multiplication of gastric epithelial cells [33, 34]. Helicobacter pylori (Hp) infection drives Reg1 upregulation in the gastric mucosa. Normalization of Reg1 expression after Hp eradication demonstrates its inflammation-dependent transcriptional regulation [35]. Reverse Transcription-Polymerase Chain Reaction (RT-PCR) analyses across multiple studies consistently detect transcriptional activity of Reg family genes in gastric adenocarcinoma cells, with expression levels correlating with histopathological progression [36–38]. Mechanistically, Reg1 promotes gastric carcinogenesis by activating pro-survival signaling through Akt phosphorylation and upregulating Bcl-xL transcription [39, 40] (Fig. 1). Yonemura et al. demonstrated that infiltrative gastric tumors exhibit significantly higher Reg expression than localized tumors, with Reg-positive cancers showing elevated rates of venous invasion compared to Reg-negative cases [38]. Similarly, Hara et al. showed that Reg1 protein facilitates angiogenesis during tumor progression by promoting vascular endothelial cell proliferation and suppressing apoptosis via ERK and Akt pathway activation [41]. These findings collectively implicate Reg1 in driving gastric cancer invasion and metastasis (Fig. 1). Clinically, patients with Reg-negative adenocarcinoma exhibit significantly better prognosis than Reg-positive counterparts [38], establishing Reg1 as an independent prognostic biomarker in gastric cancer [42].

The Reg3 gene and gastric cancer

Kang et al. reported significantly elevated Reg3 expression in the gastric mucosa of H. pylori-infected rats [43]. Liu et al. demonstrated that infection with CagA-positive H. pylori (CagA + Hp) upregulates Reg3 expression in gastric cancer cells, thereby disrupting cell cycle regulation and promoting proliferation. Mechanistically, this effect may involve the Reg3- and CagA-mediated assembly of the CDK4/cyclin D1 complex, which facilitates G1-S phase transition to drive tumor cell proliferation [44]. In the SGC-7901 gastric cancer cell line, Reg3A mRNA expression was significantly upregulated, whereas Reg3A silencing markedly inhibited cellular proliferation [45]. Conversely, other studies indicate significant downregulation of Reg3A in gastric cancer cells [46, 47]. Qiu et al. further revealed that Reg3A overexpression suppresses gastric cancer cell invasion and proliferation while promoting apoptosis via PI3K/Akt pathway modulation [47].

These conflicting findings regarding the role of Reg3A may arise from its context-dependent functional properties. The ultimate biological effect of Reg3A is likely modulated by multiple factors, including the genetic background of tumor cells, the specific characteristics of the tumor microenvironment (e.g., cytokine composition, immune cell infiltration pattern), and the experimental model employed. For example, in inflammation-driven scenarios, Reg3A may upregulate pro-survival signaling cascades; in contrast, under other contextual conditions, it might activate alternative signaling pathways that exert growth-inhibitory effects. To resolve the ambiguity surrounding Reg3A’s dual roles in gastric carcinogenesis, future studies should employ more clinically representative in vivo models and well-stratified clinical specimens. Such investigations are warranted to delineate the molecular switches and microenvironmental determinants that govern the functional dichotomy of Reg3A.

The Reg4 gene and gastric cancer

Reg4 expression is upregulated in gastric cancer and correlates with tumor proliferation, invasion, metastasis, and poor prognosis [48]. Takahashi et al. analyzed Reg4 expression in gastric cancer tissues via western blot and RT-PCR, revealing differential mRNA and protein levels across cell lines. Notably, signet ring cell carcinomas exhibited elevated Reg4 expression [49]. Subsequent studies demonstrated that Reg4 enhances gastric cancer cell proliferation and migration by activating Akt phosphorylation [50]. Chai et al. reported significantly higher Reg4 and SOX9 protein levels in gastric tumors compared to adjacent normal tissues. Their findings suggest that Reg4 transcriptionally regulates SOX9 to promote cancer cell invasion and migration [51]. The study reveals concordant upregulation of CDX2 and Reg4 at both transcriptional and translational levels. Although CDX2 overexpression enhances tumor invasiveness and metastasis, its expression remains independent of Reg4 regulation [52]. Naito et al. validated through ChIP assays that CDX2 directly binds the proximal Reg4 promoter region, confirming transcriptional regulation of Reg4 [53] (Fig. 2). Clinically, Reg4 expression is markedly elevated in gastric cancer patients with peritoneal metastasis compared to non-metastatic cases, suggesting its role in promoting peritoneal dissemination [54, 55]. Wang et al. identified G protein-coupled receptor 37 (GPR37) as a direct Reg4 interactor. Mechanistically, Reg4 establishes an EGFR-dependent autocrine loop via GPR37-mediated transactivation, enhancing gastric cancer cell adhesion and peritoneal metastasis in vivo [56] (Fig. 2). Collectively, these findings establish Reg4 as an independent prognostic biomarker for peritoneal recurrence-free survival in gastric cancer [57].

The Reg gene family in gastric cancer diagnosis

Studies have demonstrated that Reg3A is specifically upregulated in the peripheral blood of gastric cancer patients but undetectable in healthy controls, suggesting its potential as a novel molecular biomarker for gastric cancer diagnosis [58]. Notably, another Reg family member, Reg4, has also shown significant clinical utility in early gastric cancer screening. Kobayashi et al.‘s multicenter study revealed significantly elevated serum Reg4 levels in patients compared to healthy controls (median: 8.42 ng/mL vs. 5.01 ng/mL; p < 0.001) [59]. Tao et al. further validated these findings using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), demonstrating that the positive detection rate of serum Reg4 in stage I/II gastric cancer patients significantly outperformed traditional tumor markers carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) and carbohydrate antigen 19 − 9 (CA19-9) (p < 0.05) [60].

Despite these promising findings, the clinical translation of serum Reg3A and Reg4 as diagnostic biomarkers for gastrointestinal cancers is hindered by notable challenges—with specificity being the primary concern. Importantly, Reg protein expression can also be upregulated in benign gastrointestinal conditions. To address this limitation, future prospective, large-scale, multi-center cohort studies are imperative to validate the diagnostic performance of serum Reg3A/Reg4 and establish optimal cut-off values. Furthermore, integrating these Reg proteins with established clinical biomarkers (e.g., CEA, CA19-9) into comprehensive diagnostic prediction models may constitute a more promising approach to enhance the early detection rate of gastrointestinal cancers.

The Reg gene family in gastric cancer chemotherapy resistance

Studies have demonstrated that Reg1A and Reg4 drive gastric cancer chemoresistance through divergent anti-apoptotic mechanisms. Sekikawa et al. reported that Reg1A-positive gastric cancer patients exhibited significantly reduced therapeutic responses to S-1/cisplatin (S-1/CDDP) and shorter survival. Mechanistically, Reg1A confers chemoresistance by activating the Akt signaling pathway, which enhances downstream expression of Bcl-2 and Bcl-xL to suppress apoptosis. Notably, Reg1A-negative patients frequently acquired Reg1A positivity after S-1/CDDP treatment failure, suggesting chemotherapy-induced dynamic upregulation of Reg1A as a mechanism of acquired resistance [61]. These findings establish Reg1A as both a predictive biomarker for S-1/CDDP resistance in advanced gastric cancer and a potential therapeutic target. In contrast to Reg1A, Reg4 is closely linked to intrinsic resistance against 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) in gastric cancer [62, 63]. The underlying mechanism involves Reg4-mediated activation of the MAPK/ERK pathway, which downregulates the pro-apoptotic protein Bim to inhibit apoptosis [64].

The Reg gene family and colorectal cancer

Beyond their critical roles in gastric cancer, members of the Reg family—particularly Reg4—also exert equally pivotal functions in the pathogenesis of colorectal cancer. Colorectal cancer (CRC) is the third most common malignancy globally and the second leading cause of cancer-related deaths [65]. Over the past few years, there has been an increasing incidence of colorectal cancer in China, which may be associated with economic development, lifestyle changes, and an aging population [66]. The adenoma-carcinoma sequence, involving multiple genes, is widely accepted. The Reg gene family is highly associated with the initiation and progression of this process.

The Reg1 gene and colorectal cancer

Zenilman et al. employed monoclonal antibody staining to evaluate Reg1 protein expression in colorectal cancer (CRC) specimens versus normal colonic mucosa. The study demonstrated positive Reg1 immunoreactivity in malignant tissues, in contrast to its complete absence in normal controls. Notably, the tumor-normal interface exhibited the strongest expression gradient [67]. Furthermore, studies of colorectal carcinogenesis revealed coordinated upregulation of Reg1A/B mRNA, with cytokine-mediated regulation observed across the Reg gene family [68].

As a recognized precancerous lesion of CRC, ulcerative colitis (UC) has been mechanistically linked to Reg1A dysregulation. Sekikawa et al. demonstrated elevated Reg1A protein expression in UC lesions, where mRNA levels showed a significant correlation with inflammatory severity and disease progression. Proinflammatory cytokines, including interferon-γ (IFN-γ) and interleukin-6 (IL-6), potentiated Reg1A promoter activation. Functional assays in LoVo colon cancer cells revealed that Reg1A exerts oncogenic effects through two distinct mechanisms: (1) enhancing cell proliferation via Akt phosphorylation and (2) inhibiting apoptosis through upregulation of Bcl-xL/Bcl-2 transcription [69] (Fig. 1).

Zheng et al. conducted high-throughput tissue microarray analysis of Reg1A/B expression patterns during CRC progression. Quantitative assessment revealed marked overexpression of Reg1A in malignant lesions compared to paired normal mucosa, suggesting its tumor-promoting role. Conversely, Reg1B exhibited an inverse correlation with disease aggressiveness. This inverse relationship positions Reg1B as a promising prognostic biomarker, where preserved expression correlates with indolent tumor behavior and improved survival outcomes [70]. Furthermore, clinical studies confirmed that elevated Reg1A expression is associated with shorter disease-free survival in CRC, particularly in patients with peritoneal carcinomatosis [71].

The Reg3 gene and colorectal cancer

Emerging evidence indicates that Reg3A expression in colorectal carcinoma demonstrates significant clinicopathological correlations [72]. Reg3A expression in colorectal cancer tissues exceeds levels in healthy tissues, and its high expression may be related to the rapid proliferation and enhanced invasive ability of tumor cells [73]. Yu et al.’s study provides fresh insights on the function of Reg3A in colorectal cancer. The study constructed a LoVo cell line overexpressing Reg3A (LoVo-Reg3A) and investigated its functional role in a xenograft nude mouse model. Results demonstrated that Reg3A overexpression elevated leukocyte and T-cell infiltration in LoVo-Reg3A tumors, inducing a significant cytotoxic antitumor effect [74].

Research from the Xirong Wen laboratory at the Beijing Institute of Life Sciences reveals the unique role of Reg3B, a protein homologous to Reg3A, in inhibiting inflammation-associated colorectal cancer (CAC). Their findings indicated that Reg3B expression suppresses IL-6/IL-11-mediated STAT3 signaling, thereby inhibiting tumor growth [75].

These divergent findings may reflect the Janus-faced effect of Reg family members, which exhibit bidirectional roles in cellular proliferation and survival. Thus, future studies should delineate the molecular mechanisms underlying the Janus-faced effects of Reg proteins in gastrointestinal malignancies to clarify their roles in tumorigenesis.

The Reg4 gene and colorectal cancer

Reg4 is upregulated in colorectal cancer and strongly correlates with tumor progression, including cell proliferation, metastasis, invasion, apoptosis resistance, and chemoresistance [24, 76, 77]. Bishnupuri et al. conducted a comprehensive analysis of Reg gene expression in human CRC samples. Among all Reg family members, Reg4 exhibited the most pronounced upregulation. In APCmin/+ murine models, mechanistic studies revealed premature activation of the Reg family prior to secondary APC mutations during carcinogenesis [78]. This temporal hierarchy suggests that Reg4 dysregulation represents an early molecular event in malignant transformation, potentially priming cells for subsequent APC allelic loss. In HCT116 and HT29 colorectal adenocarcinoma cells, purified recombinant human Reg4 (rhReg4) induced dose-dependent proliferation, mimicking the effects of epidermal growth factor (EGF). Luciferase reporter assays confirmed that Reg4 enhances activator protein-1(AP-1) transcriptional activity by activating the EGFR/Akt pathway, thereby driving proliferation [79] (Fig. 3). Reg4 activates Akt via phosphorylation, which inhibits glycogen synthase kinase-3β (GSK-3β). This inhibition stabilizes β-catenin, enabling its nuclear translocation and TCF-4-mediated transcriptional activation of cell cycle regulators (Cyclin D1/D3 and CDK4/6), thereby amplifying CRC proliferation [80] (Fig. 3). And Jeong-Ha et al.‘s research confirmed that in CRC harboring dual mutations in APC and KRAS, mutant KRAS upregulates the expression of REG4 via the MEK–ERK signaling pathway. The highly expressed REG4 protein then further activates the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway, thereby significantly enhancing the self-renewal capacity and tumorigenic activity of cancer stem cells (CSCs) [81] (Fig. 3). Notably, although the APC mutation itself already leads to elevated basal levels of β-catenin, REG4 further amplifies this signal transduction effect, forming a positive feedback loop that continuously reinforces the characteristics of CSCs. Ultimately, this regulatory mechanism serves as a crucial driving factor for the initiation, progression, and distant metastasis of colorectal cancer [81]. Emerging evidence identifies a Reg4-CD44 signaling axis, where Reg4 directly binds the CD44 intracellular domain (CD44ICD) to activate conserved pro-proliferative pathways [82] (Fig. 3). Clinicopathological analyses demonstrate that Reg4 overexpression correlates with poor histologic differentiation, lymphovascular invasion, and advanced TNM stages, serving as an independent prognostic marker in CRC [83–85].

The Reg gene family in colorectal cancer diagnosis

A study led by Yu et al. successfully developed a colorectal cancer (CRC) screening prediction model integrating serum biomarkers (REG1A, REG3A, carcinoembryonic antigen [CEA]) and age parameters. This model exhibited robust discriminatory capacity (C-index = 0.912) in the validation cohort [86], suggesting that multi-marker panels targeting REG family proteins may provide an effective strategy for early CRC detection.

In parallel, Kuniyasu et al. demonstrated stage-specific dynamics of serum REG4 in CRC. While TNM stage 0–III patients showed no significant REG4 elevation, exclusive upregulation was observed in stage IV cohorts with hepatic metastasis. This metastasis-specific expression profile positions serum REG4 quantification as a potential biomarker for monitoring hepatic recurrence risk in advanced CRC [87].

The Reg gene family in gastric cancer chemotherapy resistance

While the roles of Reg1 and Reg3 in colorectal cancer chemoradioresistance remain understudied, Reg4’s mechanistic contributions and clinical relevance within this context are better characterized.

Reg4-positive cancer cells exhibit heightened resistance to chemoradiotherapy. Bishnupuri et al. demonstrated that recombinant human Reg4 (rhReg4) protects normal intestinal crypt cells from radiation-induced apoptosis by upregulating anti-apoptotic genes Bcl-2, Bcl-xL, and Survivin [88]. Elevated Bcl-2 expression has also been shown to impair 5-FU-induced apoptosis in colorectal cancer cells [89]. Mechanistically, similar to its role in gastric cancer, Reg4 may attenuate the cytotoxic effects of 5-FU by activating the EGFR/Akt/AP-1 signaling axis. Since AP-1 induces dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase (DPD) expression [90], Reg4-mediated AP-1 activation could reduce intracellular 5-FU accumulation by enhancing DPD activity, thereby blunting its therapeutic efficacy. This hypothesis is further supported by Li et al., who identified that a Poria cocos-derived proteoglycan (P1) directly inhibits the Reg4/EGFR/Akt/AP-1 pathway and synergizes with 5-FU to enhance apoptosis in colorectal cancer models [91].

Targeting Reg in gastrointestinal malignancies: therapeutic strategies, translational prospects and challenges

The Reg gene exerts a critical role in driving tumor progression, thereby being recognized as a potential therapeutic target for gastrointestinal cancers. Theoretically, the development of monoclonal antibodies capable of specifically blocking the interaction between Reg protein and its receptors could provide a novel therapeutic strategy for the treatment of these malignancies. A study conducted by Zhang et al. demonstrated that single-chain variable fragment against Reg4 (scFv-Reg4) could inhibit Reg4-stimulated cell proliferation in the MKN45 and AGS gastric cancer cell lines, with respective inhibition rates of 27.7±1.5% and 17.3±2.6% [92]. Furthermore, scFv-Reg4 significantly potentiated 5-FU-induced cell death: in MKN45 cells, the cell mortality rate increased from 23.0 ± 1.0 to 28.4 ± 1.2%, while in AGS cells, it rose from 28.2 ± 0.7 to 36.6 ± 0.6% [92]. Similarly, research by Pei et al. demonstrated that recombinant Reg3A (rReg3A) promoted tumor growth in a LoVo cell xenograft nude mouse model, whereas single-chain variable fragment against Reg3A (scFv-Reg3A) exerted inhibitory effects on cell proliferation and potentiated 5-FU-induced cell death [93].

Collectively, these findings indicate that single-chain antibody fragments (scFvs) possess considerable translational potential for the treatment of gastrointestinal cancers. Nevertheless, scFvs remain constrained by several key limitations in cancer therapeutic applications. First, due to the lack of the Fc region inherent to intact antibodies, scFvs lack the capacity to effectively recruit immune cells for mediating specific antitumor effector functions (e.g., antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity, ADCC). Second, their low molecular weight (~ 25 kDa) renders them susceptible to rapid renal clearance, thereby contributing to a short in vivo circulatory half-life—a critical drawback for maintaining sustained therapeutic concentrations. Third, their tumor tissue-targeting efficiency is relatively modest, frequently requiring higher administrative doses to achieve desired therapeutic effects; this practice may elevate the risk of renal toxicity, as it increases the likelihood of nonspecific scFv accumulation in renal tissues [94]. Furthermore, current research on scFv-based targeting of the REG gene for gastrointestinal cancer therapy remains limited and is still confined to the preclinical stage. Consequently, substantial additional research efforts are required to address these bottlenecks and facilitate the translation of scFvs into clinical practice.

Summary and outlook

The Reg gene family exerts multifaceted roles in gastrointestinal malignancies, critically regulating tumor cell proliferation, invasion, metastasis, and anti-apoptotic mechanisms (Table 2). These functions position Reg proteins as promising diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets. Current research has delineated the pivotal involvement of Reg family members in gastrointestinal oncogenesis; however, their precise molecular mechanisms driving tumor initiation and progression remain incompletely characterized. Future studies should prioritize investigating Reg-mediated crosstalk within the tumor microenvironment (TME), particularly their interplay with immune evasion, inflammatory cascades, and oncogenic signaling pathways. Elucidating how Reg genes orchestrate TME remodeling and maintenance will advance the development of precision-targeted therapies for gastrointestinal cancers.

Moreover, dysregulation of Reg family expression is strongly correlated with clinical outcomes in gastrointestinal malignancies, underscoring their significant clinical potential. Future research should prioritize exploring the Reg family’s dual potential as diagnostic biomarkers and mediators of therapeutic resistance, particularly how Reg-targeted strategies can augment chemoradiation efficacy. Integrating high-throughput screening and bioinformatics approaches will accelerate the discovery of Reg-associated biomarkers, facilitating novel strategies for early detection and precision oncology. Concurrently, developing small-molecule inhibitors or monoclonal antibodies against Reg proteins represents a promising therapeutic avenue for gastrointestinal cancers. In summary, elucidating the Reg family’s role in gastrointestinal malignancies deepens our understanding of tumor pathogenesis while offering novel translational opportunities for precision medicine. Advances in mechanistic and clinical research will enable the translation of Reg-targeted therapies into clinical practice, ultimately improving patient survival and treatment outcomes.

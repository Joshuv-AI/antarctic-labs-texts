Dorsal switch protein 1 activates toll immune signaling pathway via modular serine protease in Tenebrio molitor
Authors: Md. Mahi Imam Mollah
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Dorsal switch protein 1 (DSP1) is a ubiquitously expressed immune protein in insects. A previous study reported that DSP1 activated Toll pathway via the spätzle processing enzyme (SPE) to induce immune responses. SPE is the downmost component of the proteolytic cascade in T. molitor. The current study aimed to analyse the association of the upstream protease, modular serine protease (MSP) in the DSP1-induced Toll pathway in T. molitor. Tm-MSP was expressed in all stages of development, and larval tissues. Bacterial infection with E. mundtii significantly increased the level of Tm-MSP expression. However, silencing of the Tm-MSP via RNA interference (RNAi) significantly decreased the expression. Haemolytic nodulation, antimicrobial peptide (AMP) synthesis, phenoloxidase (PO) and phospholipase A2 (PLA2) activity were also induced upon immune challenge. Injection of recombinant DSP1 (rDSP1) also showed the same phenomenon of immune challenge. Loss of function with RNAi of Tm-MSP indicated its involvement in mediating immune responses induced by DSP1. Additionally, RNAi of Tm-MSP significantly increased the susceptibility of T. molitor larvae upon immune challenge with Gram-positive E. mundtii but not with Gram-negative X. hominickii. But injection of rDSP1 attenuated the increased mortality in double stranded RNA control (dsCON) treated larvae but not in dsMSP-treated larvae. These results justified the role of Tm-MSP in activating the DSP1-induced Toll pathway.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-54950-x.

Introduction

Tenebrio molitor is a Coleopteran insect under the family Tenebrionidae. It is a worldwide distributed pest of flour, grain and food1. Female adult lays approximately 500 eggs which hatch after 3–9 days2. The larval stage lasts 1–8 month, the pupal stage 5–28 days and adult stage lasts 2–3 months3. T. molitor is easy to breed and raise with a stable protein content irrespective of food supply which makes them produced industrially as feed for pets, zoo animals, fish, pig and poultry1,4.

High mobility group box 1 (HMGB1) is a nuclear protein5 which released to the plasma upon infection and plays the role of a damage-associated molecular pattern (DAMP) to activate innate immune responses in vertebrates6. Dorsal Switch Protein 1 (DSP1) is the insect homolog of HMGB1, playing the same role7. At the nucleus, both HMGB1 and DSP1 control chromatin remodelling8,9, DNA banding, and binding to activate transcription and regulation of different gene expression10. Recent studies reported that as a damage associated molecular pattern (DAMP), DSP1 activates immune responses in Spodoptera exigua7, Tenebrio molitor11 and Frankliniella occidentalis12. DSP1-mediated immune responses include hemocytic nodule formation, phenoloxidase activity, PLA2 activity, and antimicrobial peptide synthesis via Toll–Spätzle pathway13 as in the HMGB1 of vertebrates14. However, inhibition of DSP1 by the specific ligands caused mortality of insects15. A recent study reported that knockdown of DSP1 by RNA interference also impairs the growth and development of T. molitor16.

In Drosophila melanogaster, Gram-positive bacteria or fungi induced Toll immune signaling pathway begins with the recognition of lysine-type peptidoglycan (PG)17 that triggers activation of the serine protease (SP) cascade, leading to form active spätzle18. In T. molitor, the Toll-spätzle pathway includes three serine proteases, namely modular serine protease (MSP) at the first, spätzle activating enzyme (SAE) in the middle, and spätzle processing enzyme (SPE) is the last19,20. In the Gram-positive bacteria or fungi induced Toll pathway, the immune signal passes to the SPE through MSP and SAE, which cleave the inactive pro-Spätzle to form active Spätzle, which binds to the Toll receptor and triggers its specific immune functions14,21. SPE also cleaves inactive prophenoloxidase (PPO) to active phenol oxidase (PO)22, which leads to melanization on the surface of bacteria to kill them. Thus, SPE activates the Toll immune signaling pathway to produce antimicrobial peptides (AMPs) and the melanisation immune responses23. In the Coleopteran model insect T. castaneum, Toll3, Toll4 and Spätzle7 is involved to control the Toll-pathway-dependent AMP gene expression up on microbial challenge24. In the Lepidopteran model insect Bombyx mori, BmToll9-1 and BmToll9-2 are reported to regulate the innate humoral immune responses in the midgut of Bombyx mori larvae25.

It was reported that DSP1 induced immune responses also follow the Toll-spätzle pathway13. T. molitor have proteolytic cascade consists of three protease which help in activation of the Toll pathway being infected. The binding target of DSP1 from these three proteases is still unknown. A previous study reported that interruption or knockdown of the last serine protease named SPE by RNA interference inhibits the DSP1-mediated immune responses26, which indicates the involvement of SPE in the Toll signalling pathway. This study aimed to check the association of the uppermost protease MSP in DSP1-induced activation of the Toll signaling pathway.

Material and methods

Insect and bacteria preparation

The larvae of T. molitor were reared in the laboratory for the last three years. Conditions maintained in the laboratory were: 25 ± 2 °C temperature, 60 ± 5% relative humidity, and photoperiod 16:8 h (L:D). Wheat bran supplemented with cabbage leaf sheath was provided as food to the larvae27 while cabbage leaves and 10% sugar solution was provided to the adults in addition to wheat bran11,28. Two bacteria namely Enterococcus mundtii, and Xenorhabdus hominickii were used in this study. Both the bacteria were cultured at 28 °C in tryptic soy broth (TSB, Fisher Scientific, Seoul, Korea) shaking at 180 rpm for 24 h. The bacterial broths were centrifuged at 10,000 × g for 5 min to get the bacterial pellets. The resulting pellets were resuspended in phosphate buffered saline (PBS) or sterile water as needed. The bacterial cells were counted under a microscope (BX41, Olympus, Tokyo, Japan) equipped with a hemocytometer (Lauda-Konigshofen, Germany).

Chemical and reagents

The anticoagulant buffer (ACB) was formulated in the laboratory using 17 mM Na2EDTA, 186 mM NaCl and 41 mM citric acid used to avoid coagulation of hemocytes. 100 mM Phosphoric acid was prepared in the laboratory and was used for formulating phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). Metafectene Pro, a DNA and RNA transfection reagent was collected from Biontex, Germany and was mixed with dsRNA to facilitate the formation of lipoplex. DOPA (L-3,4-dihydroxyphenyl-alanine) needed for phenoloxidase (PO) activity estimation was collected from Sigma-Aldrich Korea. Recombinant DSP1 (rDSP1) of Spodoptera exigua was collected from another study7.

Prediction and analysis of modular serine protease

The shotgun contig of T. molitor (JABDTM020022481.1) was used to predict the Tm-MSP. For this, the Spodoptera frugiperda MSP (XP_035453689.1) was used as bait. The FGENESH Softberry software (http://www.softberry.com/berry.phtml) was applied for getting an open reading frame (ORF) and the resulting sequences were uploaded in GenBank (MZ190161.1 and UEC95037.1). The protein domains were constructed using the software named SMART (http://smart.embl-heidelberg.de/). MEGA6 supported with Clustal W programs (www.ebi.ac.uk) and Neighbor-joining method was applied for phylogenetic analyses. The gene accession numbers of the sequences needed for phylogenetic analysis are provided in Table S1. To get bootstrapping values, 1,000 repetitions were adopted.

RNA extraction and RT-qPCR

Total RNA was extracted from egg, larva, pupa, and adult of T. molitor. For each replication, 50 eggs, 20 1st instar, 10 2nd instar, 2 6th instar, 1 12th instar larvae, 1 pupa and 1 adult were considered. For RNA extraction, tissues (epidermis, midgut and hemocyte) of L12 larvae were used. Hemolymph collected in anticoagulant buffer from 30 L12 larvae by cutting the tip of abdomen was centrifuged (500 × g, 5 min) and the hemocyte pellets was used for RNA extraction. The midgut, fatbody and epidermis of the same larvae was used for extraction of RNA. As RNA extraction reagent, Trizol (Invitrogen, Seoul, Korea) was used. Preparation of cDNA and amplification of PCR with gene-specific primers (Table S2) were carried out following the method described by Mollah26. As a reference gene, L27a was used28. For quantitative analysis, a comparative CT method29 was followed.

RNAi of Tm-MSP

T. molitor modular serine protease primer (Table S2) was designed with T7 promoter (5´-TAATACGACTCACTATAGGGAGA-3´) at its 5´ end and used for amplifying template DNA. This product was applied to synthesize double-stranded RNA (dsRNA) encoding Tm-MSP (dsMSP) at 37 °C for 4 h. For this, a MEGAscript RNAi kit (Ambion, TX, USA) was used. Following incubation at 75 °C for 5 min, the synthesized single-stranded RNAs in both directions were allowed to anneal at 25 °C. For synthesizing the control dsRNA (dsCON), a viral gene CpBV302 was applied30. Mixture dsRNAs and Metafectene PRO (1:1, v/v) was incubated for 30 min at room temperature for lipoplex formation. Lipoplex increases the entrance of dsRNA to the targeted tissue for increased RNAi efficiency31. A microsyringe (Hamilton) was used to inject the mixture of dsRNA-Metafectene (2µL) into L12 larva. Entrance of 1 µg of dsRNA in each larva were confirmed. Expression of Tm-MSP were checked by RT-qPCR at different time confirm the efficacy of RNAi.

Nodule formation assay

To investigate the role of Gram-positive bacterium, E. mundtii or recombinant DSP1 (rDSP1) in cellular immune response, a nodule formation assay was carried out11. This was done by injecting heat-killed E. mundtii (2.2 × 105 cells/larva) into the L10 larvae via the pleuron using a microsyringe after 24 h of dsCON or dsMSP injection to induce hemocyte nodules. After 8 h of injection, the number of nodules in the hemocoel of treated larvae was noted. For recovering the RNAi effect, the immune protein (rDSP1) was co-injected at the rate of 600 ng/larva with bacterial challenge. Treated larvae were kept at 65 ± 5% relative humidity and 25 °C. The treated larva was dissected and the number of nodules were counted using a microscope (Stemi SV 11, Germany).

Phospholipase A2 (PLA2) assay

A PLA2 Assay Kit (Cayman Chemical, MI, USA) was used to analyze both the secretory phospholipase A2 (sPLA2) and cellular phospholipase A2 (cPLA2) enzyme activities following Mollah26 with some modifications. In brief, 1,2-bis (heptanoyl Thio) phosphatidylcholine (CP) was used as a substrate for sPLA2 assay and arachidonoyl Thio PC was used as substrate for cPLA2 assay. For inducing PLA2 enzyme activity, heat-killed E. mundtii (2.2 × 105 cells/larva) or recombinant DSP1 (rSP1, 600 ng/larva) was injected into 10th instar larva (L10) of T. molitor. Before 24 h of the E. mundtii and rDSP1 co-application, the control larvae were injected with dsCON and the larvae needed to silent the modular serine protease were injected with dsMSP. Following 8 h of E. mundtii or rDSP1 application, hemolymph sample was collected in PBS for sPLA2 analysis and fat body sample was collected in PBS for cPLA2 enzyme activity. For separating the plasma from hemocytes, hemolymph sample was spanned at 500 × g for 5 min and the supernatant plasma sample was collected for analysis. Fat body samples were crushed and spun for 5 min at 500 × g, and the supernatant was collected. The reaction volume consisted of plasma or fat body extract (10 µl), 5,5-dithio-bis-(2-nitrobenzoic acid) (10 µl), the assay buffer (5 µl), and the substrate (200 µl) specific for sPLA2 or cPLA2. The change in absorbance was monitored at 405 nm with three biologically independent replications.

Phenoloxidase (PO) enzyme assays

For PO activity was measurement, DOPA was used as substrate7. For PO activity induction, E. mundtii or rDSP1 dissolved in PBS was injected into the L10 larvae before 8 h of plasma sample collection. Sterile PBS was injected into the naïve larvae. The larvae were injected with dsMSP to shut down or silencing the Tm-MSP gene and it was carried out 24 h before the immune challenge or rDSP1 injection. Control dsRNA (dsCON) was injected into the control larvae. The plasma sample was prepared as described in the previous step and used as an enzyme against the DOPA substrate. The total reaction volume (200 µL) contains 10 mM DOPA dissolved in PBS (180 µL) and plasma of treated larvae (20 µL). The absorbance was measured in a VICTOR multi-label plate reader (PerkinElmer, Waltham, MA, USA) at 490 nm. Phenoloxidase activity was expressed as ΔABS/min/µL of plasma. Every treatment was repeated thrice with the plasma collected from 10 larvae.

AMP gene expression pattern

For the humoral immune response, AMP gene expression was analyzed as described by Mollah26. Induction of AMP genes were achieved by injecting heat-killed E. mundtii (3.2 × 105 cells/larva) into the tenth instar larvae (L10) after 24 h of dsMSP or dsCON injection. The recombinant DSP1 (rDSP1, 600 ng/larva) was injected into the larvae to investigate the role of rDSP1 in the induction of AMP genes. After 8 h of rDSP1 or E. mundtii injection, samples for RNA extraction were collected. Samples were collected from three larvae for each replication, and subsequently RNA was extracted. RT-qPCR with the AMP gene-specific primers (Table S2) were adopted to estimate the expression level of the AMP genes. As reference, the insect specific L27a gene was used. Every treatment was repeated thrice.

Bioassay of RNAi-treated T. molitor against E. mundtii (Em) and X. hominickii (Xh)

To assess the virulence of Gram-negative X. hominickii and Gram-positive E. mundtii on RNAi-treated T. molitor, L6 larvae were injected with 1 μg of dsRNA specific to Tm-MSP and incubated with a transfection agent, Metafectene, at a 2:1 ratio26. Control dsRNA (dsCON) was injected into control larvae. Following the incubation of 24 h after dsRNA treatment, the bacterium X. hominickii (2.2 × 105 cells/larvae) or E. mundtii (2.4 × 105 cells/larvae) was injected into the same larvae to check their virulence against the larvae of T. molitor. Entrance of almost equal number of bacteria via injection was confirmed by repeated vertexing of the bacterial suspension containing vial. For bacterial injection, Hamilton syringe was used. The treated larvae were reared in the laboratory condition of 25 ± 2 °C temperature and 65 ± 5% relative humidity with enough diet. In this condition, larvae were reared for another 10 days to record the mortality at 24 h interval. For investigating the immune mediation of DSP1, recombinant DSP1 (rDSP1) and E. mundtii was co-injected. Every treatment was repeated thrice in ten larvae.

Statistical analysis

All the collected data were subjected to a one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) using PROG GLM in the SAS program32. Arcsine transformation was carried out for the mortality data prior to ANOVA. The means were compared by least significant difference (LSD) test at Type I error (P value) = 0.05. Probit analysis was carried out to get the median lethal dose (LD50) applying the EPA Probit Analysis Program, ver. 1.5 (U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, USA) was used.

Expression profile of Tm-MSP

Tm-MSP was observed in egg, larva, pupa and adult stages with maximum at 12th instar larva (L12) and minimum in egg of developmental stage (Fig. 2A). At the L12 stage, Tm-MSP was expressed in fat body, hemocytes, gut, epidermis with maximum expression in fat body and the lowest expression in the epidermis (Fig. 2B). The expression level was significantly (P < 0.05) upregulated (> 12-fold at 4 h and > eightfold at 2 h of injection) upon immune challenge with E. mundtii bacterium (Fig. 2C) which was downregulated by RNAi of Tm-MSP. This up and downregulated expression of Tm-MSP indicated its immune related functions. Injection of dsMSP significantly (P < 0.05) decreased the Tm-MSP expression (> 60% at 24 h, > 90% at 48 h and > 75% at 72 h of post-injection) and almost recovered at 96 h (Fig. 3A). Effect of Tm-MSP on Nodulation and PO Activity

Control larvae (MSP gene not silenced) immune challenged with E. mundtii formed significantly (P < 0.05) more nodules (62 ± 5.70) than the larvae injected with dsMSP (35 ± 5.00) (Fig. 3B). Co-injection of rDSP1 and E. mundtii increased the nodulation significantly (P < 0.05) than the control larvae or the larvae injected only with E. mundtii (Fig. 3B). Similarly, PO activity recorded significantly (P < 0.05) more in the control larvae immune challenged with E. mundtii than the modular serine protease silenced larvae immune changed with E. mundtii (Fig. 3C). Addition of rDSP1 resulted significantly (P < 0.05) more PO activity in the control larvae than the MSP silenced larvae (Fig. 3C). This inductive role of rDSP1 in nodulation and PO activity suggested its involvement in immune mediation (Fig. 3B,C). The inductive role of rDSP1 in nodule formation and PO activity was very clear from the pictorial representation (Fig. 3B,D).

Role of Tm-MSP in PLA2 activity

Control larvae challenged with E. mundtii, induced more sPLA2 activity than the naïve larvae and the induction was reduced after RNAi of Tm-MSP and the difference was significant (Fig. 4A). Similarly, cPLA2 was also induced significantly (P < 0.05) by E. mundtii, but it was decreased after RNAi of Tm-MSP (Fig. 4B). Similarly, recombinant dsp1 (rDSP1) increased both the sPLA2 and cPLA2 activity in the control (MSP not silenced) larvae but it decreased significantly (P < 0.05) in the larvae treated with dsMSP (Fig. 4A,B). Induction of AMP gene expression in T. molitor by bacteria or rDSP1

E. mundtii challenged larvae significantly (P < 0.05) increased the induction in all the eight tested AMP genes compared to the control larvae (Fig. 5A). Among the AMP genes, Defensin1 (Def1), Tenesin1 (Ten1), Defensin2 (Def2) and Attachin2 (Att2) were highly induced while Cecropin2 (Cec2), Tenesin3 (Ten3), Attachin1a (Att1a) and Coleoptericin1 (Col1) were less induced although the difference was significant compared to the control (Fig. 5A). The expression of AMP genes decreased significantly (P < 0.05) upon RNAi of the Tm-MSP gene. Immune challenge of dsMSP treated larvae with E. mundtii, completely suppressed the expression of Cec2 and Ten3 AMP genes, partially but significantly (P < 0.05) suppressed the expression of Att2, Def1, Def2 and Ten1 AMP genes but no effect on Att1a and Col 1 (Fig. 5A). Similarly, immune challenge of T. molitor larvae with rDSP1, significantly (P < 0.05) increased the expression of all the immune genes of which Def1, Def2, and Ten1 AMP genes were expressed most (Fig. 5B). RNAi of Tm-MSP significantly (P < 0.05) decreased the expression of all the AMP genes (Fig. 5B). However, immune challenge of dsMSP treated larvae with rDSP1 did not increase the expression of Def1, Def2, Ten1, Ten3, and Cec2 AMP genes but increased the expression of Att1a, Att2, and Col1 AMP genes (Fig. 5B). Effect of a modular serine protease (MSP) on the mortality of T. molitor

For the larvae treated with dsCON, X. hominickii found more virulent than the E. mundtii whereas X. hominickii showed lower virulence than the E. mundtii against the larvae treated with dsMSP (Fig. 6A). This indicates that the Gram + E. mundtii follow the Toll pathway and modular serine protease of T. molitor is essential for the activation of immune system via Toll pathway. To confirm the role of DSP1 in immune mediation, recombinant DSP1 was injected into T. molitor larvae. Mortality by E. mundtii found more in the larvae treated with dsCON than the larvae treated with E. mundtii as well as rDSP1 (Fig. 6B). Mortality by E. mundtii was maximum in dsMSP treated larvae but the difference was insignificant (P < 0.05) for the larvae treated with dsMSP as well as rDSP1 (Fig. 6B). Discussion

Dorsal switch protein 1 (DSP1) was first introduced in Drosophila with the role in embryo development, differentiation, and segmentation33,34. Later, an insect HMGB1 was introduced in mosquitoes that facilitates the binding of transcriptional factor ‘Rel’ to the nuclear factor kappa B (NF-kB) promoter for antiviral gene expression against a dengue viral infection10. A recent study revealed that DSP1 has the DAMP role in lepidopteran S. exigua7 and coleopteran T. molitor11 to mediate various immune responses like nodule formation, PLA2 activity, AMP synthesis, PO activity, etc.26. Subsequent studies reported that DSP1 activates immune responses via the Toll-spätzle immune signalling pathway11,26. In T. molitor, the Toll-Spatzle pathway includes three serine proteases, such as MSP, SAE, and SPE19. In a previous study, we observed that SPE of the three SPs in T. molitor is required to activate the DSP1-induced Toll pathway26. However, the role of other SPs in this pathway is unknown. That’s why, we first analyzed the first SP, MSP of the three SPs to know their involvement in the DSP1 mediated Toll signaling pathway. For this, Gram-positive E. mundtii along with rDSP1 were challenged to the larvae for observing gain of function and loss of function after RNAi of MSP.

In this study, we found that Gram-positive E. mundtii and rDSP1 upregulated the expression of Attachin1a (‘Att1a’), Attachin2 (‘Att2’), Cecropin2 (‘Cec2’), Coleoptericin1 (‘Col1’), Defensin1 (‘Def1’), Defensin2 (‘Def2’), Tenesin1 (‘Ten1’) and Tenesin3 (‘Ten3’) AMP genes in the control larvae (Fig. 5). In the Tm-MSP silenced larvae, both E. mundtii and rDSP1 did not upregulated the expression of Def1, Def2, Ten1, Ten3, and Cec2 AMP genes (Fig. 5) which might be dependent on DSP1 induced Toll-pathway. A previous study in T. molitor reported that Att1, Att2, Cec2, and Def were upregulated by both Gram-positive bacteria and rDSP111. Another study revealed that Attacin (Att1, Att2), Apolipophorin (Apol), Defensin (Def), Cecropin (Cec), Lysozyme (Lyz), etc. AMP genes of S. exigua were induced by bacterial infection3. E. mundtii and rDSP1 also induced hemocytic nodule formation (Fig. 3B), PO activity (Fig. 3C), and PLA2 activity (Fig. 4). However, RNAi of MSP interrupts AMP synthesis (Fig. 5), PLA2 activity (Fig. 4), PO activity (Fig. 3C), and hemocyte nodulation (Fig. 3B) proving the necessity of MSP for their activation. RNAi of MSP caused higher mortality of T. molitor larvae by E. mundtii (Fig. 6). Therefore, RNAi technology could be a transformative tool for pest management including T. molitor. RNAi leveraged double stranded RNA (dsRNA) which targets to shut down the activity of specific gene. Application of dsRNA relevant to the essential gene of insect’s survival might be very effective with minimal off-target effects and compatibility with integrated pest management strategies35. Application of dsRNA will also limit the use of conventional pesticides having environment issues, health concerns and killing beneficial arthropods36.

In controlled conditions, Gram-negative X. hominickii shows more virulence to T. molitor larvae because X. hominickii is an entomopathogenic bacterium that releases different chemicals37 to host for immune suppression38, toxaemia or apoptosis39 and binding with immune protein to make the protein inactive resulting host mortality15. These bacteria have a biosynthetic gene cluster to secrete different chemicals40, some of which have insecticidal activity41. Similar phenomena are also found in Gram-negative B. thuringiensis42 or bacterial metabolites43–45, which are used to control insect pests. No difference was found in the mortality of Tm-MSP silenced or non-silenced larvae. This finding proved the non-association of MSP with X. hominickii (Fig. 6). Thus, modular serine protease (MSP) is essential for E. mundtii as well as rDSP1 to activate the Toll pathway. In summary, DSP1 targets MSP to activate the Toll pathway essential for nodulation, PLA2, and PO activity and AMP gene synthesis in T. molitor (Fig. 7).

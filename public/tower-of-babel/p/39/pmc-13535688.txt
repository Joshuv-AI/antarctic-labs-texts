FisioCare Neurogroup: A new application for home monitoring of individuals with neurological diseases
Authors: Patrick R. Avelino, Kênia K. P. Menezes, Christina Danielli Coelho de Morais Faria, Lucas R. Nascimento, Maria Tereza Mota Alvarenga, Ruani Tenório Araújo, Aline A. Scianni
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective:The objective of this study was to develop a smartphone application (app) that will be programmed with home exercises for the patient to perform.Methods:The present app was developed in five stages: (1) Software content analysis and setting of objectives, (2) Preliminary layout, (3) Development of the prototype, (4) Testing the prototype, and (5) Finalisation. All stages were conducted by two physiotherapist researchers with at least 10 years of experience.Results:In the first stage, the app named FisioCare Neurogroup was established with one interface for the physiotherapist and one for the patient. Ten physiotherapists assessed the preliminary layout in the second stage, and after the prototype was developed in the third stage, it was tested by another 10 physiotherapists with their respective neurological patients in the fourth stage. Most physiotherapists reported ease of use, with no adjustments proposed at the end. Finally, the app was finalized in the fifth stage. The proposed app allows the physiotherapist to register a specific exercise program for each patient to be carried out at home. The patient will be able to access the program described and demonstrated in detail. The app will notify both the patient of the need to perform the activities and the physiotherapist of the patient’s performance of the exercises.Conclusion:The FisioCare Neurogroup was developed according to standardized protocols that are highly recommended for this purpose. This app may improve the performance of the patient in the home exercises, as well as their autonomy, by monitoring the adherence of the patient to the protocol proposed.

Introduction

Neurological rehabilitation is a critical component of recovery and successful reintegration into society and needs to include an organized, specific therapy.1 However, clinicians struggle to provide the appropriate intensity during rehabilitation sessions.2 Therefore, semi-supervised therapy and home-based rehabilitation may safely increase the amount of practice.3,4

Evidence suggests that noncompliance with home exercises in musculoskeletal cohorts can vary between 30% and 50%.5 Noncompliance can be even more significant in neurological patients, who generally have even more severe motor impairments. This is concerning and suggests that these individuals may not achieve optimal functional recovery. Improving exercise adherence is associated with improved functional outcomes in this population.6

Smartphone apps have the potential to increase accessibility, as essential information may be compiled in a single source.7 This means that smartphone apps could be a promising solution for increasing access to healthcare, given the unprecedented spread of mobile technologies and advancements in their innovative capacity to address health priorities.8 The development of an innovative Brazilian app for monitoring home exercises in neurological patients will allow monitoring patients who receive in-person care and perform home exercises as an extension of their treatment. Furthermore, it will benefit patients who are unable to travel to health care centres on a frequent basis, allowing them to be monitored remotely. Benefits may be carried over to clinicians, who will not only have a useful tool for drawing up a home exercise protocol based on simple, self-explanatory videos, but will also be able to monitor the patient’s adherence to the proposed protocol. Therefore, the purpose of this study was to describe the development of the first Brazilian smartphone app, with home exercises to be performed by patients with neurological diseases.

Methods

Design

This was a methodological study, approved by the Ethical Review Board of the Universidade Federal de Minas Gerais (#XXX). All individuals who participated in any stage provided written consent before inclusion.

Procedures

The present app was developed in five stages, according to the prototyping paradigm, developed by the Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality of the US Department of Health and Human Services, which is one of the top six design methods recommended.9 All stages of the design process were conducted by two physiotherapists, with at least 10 years of experience in the assessment and rehabilitation of individuals with neurological diseases.

Stage 1: Software content analysis and setting of objectives. A literature review was conducted to identify the current instruments designed for the assessment of individuals with neurological diseases. Based on these findings, a set of open-ended questions was developed for a semi-structured interview with an expert committee composed of five physiotherapists with clinical and research expertise in the assessment of individuals with neurological diseases.

Stage 2: Preliminary layout. The designer, a computer scientist, created the user experience flow diagram and the preliminary screen layouts with the digital prototyping software Figma. These preliminary layouts were assessed by 10 physiotherapists using smartphones, all with at least five years of professional experience and good ability at using smartphones, who treat adults with neurological diseases. Semi-structured individual interviews were conducted with a set of open-ended questions. The interview was made up of initial questions to characterise the sample, and questions evaluating the user’s experience, the context in which the users would use the app, the users’ perspectives, the functions of the app, the degree of relevance of the content provided, and the attractiveness of the app. Based on the responses to this evaluation, the final layout was defined, and the process of sending files to the programmer to develop the smartphone app prototype began.

Stage 3: Development of the prototype. The programmer has developed the prototype for the Android and IOS operating systems. The Flutter programming language (Google) was used, generating versions of the app that were tested for functionality and improved successively until the prototype was developed. This process was accompanied by two research physiotherapists, with at least 10 years of experience in the assessment and rehabilitation of neurological individuals.

Stage 4: Testing the prototype. A pilot test was carried out to assess the quality of use of the prototype. Ten additional physiotherapists, different from stage 2, who met the criteria mentioned in stage 2, were invited to test the prototype, accompanied by their respective 10 patients with neurological conditions. Everyone had the prototype installed on their smartphone and, after a brief training session, was instructed to use the app for a week. The users were also instructed to record and describe any problems experienced when using the prototype in an incident diary, rating the difficulty in overcoming the problem according to a 5-point Likert scale, where 5 represents a very uncomfortable problem, and 1 represents a problem overcome very easily.

Stage 5: Finalisation. The prototype was refined, all the problems identified were solved, and the final app was made available.

Results

Stage 1

After two meetings, lasting one hour each, and with an interval of 30 days between each one, the expert committee analysed the requirements and set the smartphone app objectives and content, as follows:

(1)The app’s name and logo were defined (Fig. 1), and it is entitled FisioCare Neurogroup.(2)Development of two interfaces, one for the physiotherapist and one for the patient.(3)In the physiotherapist interface, the first function to be established is the registration of a new patient, where the physiotherapist inserts the specific exercises to be performed by the patient according to the treatment plan. Subsequently, three functions need to be defined: the inclusion of the physiotherapy program, the possibility of generating reports, and a chat for direct communication between the physiotherapist and the patient. In the physiotherapy program, each exercise is inserted individually, and the following information can be edited: Name of the exercise, execution time, start and end date of the exercise, description of the task, specific and detailed guidelines for each exercise (step by step), and attachments, where videos or images can be inserted or their links, for better visualisation and understanding of the task. All information, descriptions, or images that may be sent to the patient related to the exercises are included (typed or uploaded) by the physiotherapist, since the application does not provide a database of exercise possibilities or ready-made images. For each exercise, the physiotherapist receives feedback from the patient on whether each task has been completed. In addition, at the end of the daily treatment plan, the physiotherapist will receive the final time taken to perform the exercises by the patients, as well as an evaluation of their perception of the level of difficulty, plus any comments written by the patient. All information can be exported to a PDF file.(4)The patient interface displays a calendar. On each day of the calendar, the patient can access the specific daily exercise plan for that date, which is registered by their physiotherapist. If the patient has more than one physiotherapist, all the plans registered will display and may be accessed. In each daily plan, the patient will find the proposed tasks and details, as described above in item 3. The patient may download their material (images or videos) of the exercises when accessing it for the first time, with an internet connection, and access that content whenever possible on their smartphone. The app offers a timer for task execution, and additional information that can be sent to the physiotherapist.(5)The inclusion of a communication channel between the users, making it possible to send suggestions for updates to future versions.(6)To access the FISO CARE app, a valid e-mail address and password will be required.(7)The temporary logo and colour (green) of the app were suggested, but these characteristics were only defined after the last stage.

After receiving the physiotherapists’ suggestions, the expert committee meeting was held, lasting approximately 60 min, in which the following definitions for the physiotherapist interface were established: (1) In the attachments box, the inclusion of a reminder about the need to provide only a link, even in the case of own file; (2) the option to save the last five therapeutic sessions; (3) the possibility to export a report of the completion of the proposed exercise program (word/PDF); (4) the possibility of creating a set of exercises, which will be easily added when creating a specific program for a patient; (5) the possibility of reordering the exercises within the program created; (6) the possibility of activating and inactivating the profile of registered patients; and (7) the possibility of exclusion of patients.

For the patient interface, the expert committee established: (1) The possibility of editing the profile; (2) the possibility of sending feedback regarding the exercises (i.e., grading the level of difficulty and sending comments; (3) the possibility of the exclusion of the profile; (4) the possibility of the patient to be registered by more than one physiotherapist.

Stage 3

The smartphone app was developed and improved by the programmers over eight months. Periodic meetings were held between the developers and at least one research physiotherapist, in order to resolve any doubts or problems and supervise the process. The smartphone app was finalized for testing.

All the physiotherapists completed the training protocol with the app, while three patients did not finish the test period, and did not send in their evaluations, reporting forgetfulness and lack of time. Regarding the description in an incident diary of any problems they experienced using the prototype, the physiotherapists did not report any difficulty. Regarding the problems reported by patients, difficulty finding the exercise program, forgetting to send feedback, difficulty editing profile information, and difficulty reading the information (small font) were cited. All patients reported that it was easy to very easy to solve the problem, except for one patient who reported great difficulty in reading (glasses wearer), and one patient who reported moderate difficulty in editing his profile information.

Stage 5

The app was finalized by the programmers, available on the platforms Android and iOS, free of charge. The FisioCare Neurogroup complies with the Brazilian General Data Protection Law (LGPD) and ethical principles for the management of patient health data, ensuring the privacy and security of all personal data. The web version can be found at the following link: http://fisiocare.app.br.

Discussion

The purpose of this study was to describe FisioCare Neurogroup, a new smartphone app for the home monitoring of individuals with neurological diseases. According to a previous prototyping paradigm, one of the top six design methods recommended, two physiotherapist researchers conducted all five stages of the design process, and the FisioCare Neurogroup was developed and finalized. The smartphone app’s interface, in general, was considered clear, adequate, easy, attractive and its content relevant. The prototype was programmed, tested, and finalized according to the results of the pilot.

The number of published smartphone apps, aimed at the most diverse objectives, has been growing in recent years.10,11 There is a call for improvement in the content and quality of healthcare apps, including evidence-based information consistent with guidelines, supported and developed by healthcare providers with credible and reliable resources.12,13,14 The FisioCare Neurogroup app was developed by healthcare providers and app developers in conjunction with the patients. In addition, its resources were structured according to scientific literature, as well as the expertise of professional researchers and clinicians in the field, which has been tested and approved by professionals and patients, showing the quality of the app developed.

In the testing of the prototype, while all the physiotherapists did not report any difficulty, the patients reported difficulty finding the exercise program, forgetting to send feedback, difficulty editing profile information, and difficulty reading the information (small font). However, all the patients reported that it was easy to very easy to solve the problem, which confirms the ease of use of the application, since in any software it is expected that some users will encounter some type of difficulty, as long as they can overcome it.15 Only one patient reported great difficulty in reading, a glass user, and one patient reported difficulty in editing his profile information, but claimed to use his smartphone very little in his daily routine. Since these issues were caused by personal factors among the participants, and the others were deemed easy or very easy to resolve (as is to be expected when using an app), the expert committee decided not to make any changes to the final app version.

This is the first Brazilian app available that are designed to help monitor home exercises, involving all levels of functionality, according to the exercise program designed by the physiotherapist. Furthermore, FisioCare Neurogroup is now available for free for both Android and iOS. Finally, although the app was developed by physiotherapists to be used in neurological patients, FisioCare Neurogroup has the potential to be used in other populations and by other health professionals who need to monitor home behaviour of their patients. However, this study is not without limitations. First, although the sample was drawn from various settings, it was not randomly selected and may not fully represent the populations of patients and physiotherapists. Second, the sample included mixed diagnoses (Stroke, Parkinson’s, MS). While this shows versatility, the specific accessibility needs of a stroke survivor (e.g., hemianopsia, aphasia) may differ substantially from those of a Parkinson’s patient (e.g., resting tremors), and a sample of 10 may not capture these nuances. Three stroke patients dropped out of the pilot study due to forgetfulness and time constraints, though four others with the same diagnosis completed the protocol. Indirectly, the difficulty of using the app may have contributed to these dropouts. Finally, the usability of the app has not been systematically investigated due to the necessity of a larger sample size. Thus, the next step is to investigate the usability of FisioCare Neurogroup, using a standardized scale such as the User Mobile App Rating Scale (uMARS) and its potential for use by other healthcare professionals and in other populations.

Conclusion

The development of FisioCare Neurogroup was completed, and it can be used to assist and monitor home exercises in individuals with neurological diseases (Stroke, Parkinson’s, and Multiple Sclerosis). The proposed smartphone app allows the physiotherapist to register a specific exercise program for each patient to be carried out at home. The patient, in turn, will be able to access the program, which is described and demonstrated in detail, and which will not only help in its execution but will also notify the professional about the execution of the protocol, as well as the perceived level of difficulty. The FisioCare Neurogroup was developed according to standardized protocols that are highly recommended for this purpose, following five systematic stages, which were monitored by two expert research physiotherapists, and in two stages, physiotherapists also participated with their respective patients with neurological conditions.

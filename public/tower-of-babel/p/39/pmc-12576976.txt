Multicenter epidemiological investigation and analysis of factors associated with transient synovitis of the hip in 5065 children treated across 32 tertiary children’s hospitals in China
Authors: Jianlin Chen, Hansheng Deng, Haoran Feng, Tianfeng Zhu, Zilong Huang, Jiahui Li, Xinyu Wang, Gen Liu, Shizhe Liu, Shuaiyin Wang, Zhenkun Gu, Guoshuang Feng, Gianfilippo Caggiari, Chao You, Xin Qiu, Guibing Fu, Yueping Zeng, Yueping Zeng, Jian Tian, Fei Song, Xin Xu, Mei Wu, Guosong Wang, Li Li, Hongjie Sun, Zhenqiang Da, WenJuan Wang, Qinghong He, Shaoqian Liu, Ling Dai, Waiguang Hu, Xiaomei Chen, Xiaoqin Wang, Jian Du, Chunxiang Wang, Yuanyi Qu, Daqiao Zhu, Jian Ding, Haibin Zhou, Jinchi Shi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo analyze the epidemiological characteristics and factors associated with transient synovitis of the hip (TSH) in Chinese children.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed the data of children with TSH who were hospitalized across 32 pediatric tertiary hospitals affiliated with China’s Futang Research Center of Pediatric Development between December 1, 2015, and December 31, 2022. We used the ICD10 codes on the first page of the medical records to identify the cases and extracted data on age, sex, diagnosis, treatment methods, onset time, region, and various expenses incurred during hospitalization.ResultsThe study cohort comprised a total of 5065 children, including 3711 males and 1354 females. The age group with the highest number of TSH cases was the preschool children group, with 2452 cases (48.4%), and the lowest number of cases was recorded in the adolescent group, with 119 cases (2.3%). In terms of hospitalization time, the adolescent group had the longest average hospitalization time of 9.4 ± 5.6 days. In terms of the geographical distribution of onset, the number of cases in East China was higher than that in other geographical regions. In addition, in terms of treatment options, children were more likely to receive conservative treatment (7,667 instances) than surgical treatment (80 instances). Among conservative treatments, the most common was skin traction (4,508 instances). Among the various hospitalization expenses, the cost of examinations was the highest, reaching USD 188.35.ConclusionThis study summarizes the epidemiological characteristics of transient synovitis of the hip (TSH) in Chinese children, providing important clinical data to support the overall understanding and treatment of this disease.

Introduction

Transient synovitis of the hip (TSH) is described as a nonspecific inflammatory change in the synovium of the hip joint caused by a variety of reasons [1, 2]. It is one of the most common causes of hip pain and limp in children, but its exact pathogenesis has not yet been clarified [3]. According to mainstream research, the occurrence of TSH may be related to factors such as infection, allergic reaction, strenuous exercise, and trauma [4–6]. The current treatment strategy is mainly conservative treatment, including rest, the use of non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, skin traction therapy, physical therapy, and traditional Chinese medicine treatment [7–13]. Due to similar symptoms, TSH is easily confused with diseases that can cause developmental disorders of the hip joint in children, such as septic arthritis, idiopathic arthritis, and Legg-Calvé-Perthes which can lead to misdiagnosis and inappropriate treatment, and waste of medical resources [14–17]. Therefore, it is crucial to improve clinical awareness of TSH and strengthen epidemiological research based on clinical data.

An epidemiological study conducted in Finland, including 77 children under 16 diagnosed with TSH, reported an incidence rate of 51.9 per 100,000 person-years [18]. Similarly, a single-center epidemiological study in the United Kingdom involving 259 children with TSH observed an annual incidence rate of 25.1 per 100,000 among children aged 0–14 [19]. Despite these findings, there is still limited research on the epidemiology and overall distribution of TSH; in particular, there is a lack of data analysis on large samples. Further, while there are differences in the treatment methods adopted for hospitalized children with TSH in different regions, the current literature on this topic lacks a systematic summary and comparative analysis of the treatment methods in various regions. In addition, there is currently no clear cost structure analysis for the hospitalization of children with TSH. Based on these gaps in pediatric TSH data, this study aims to provide the following epidemiological data on TSH in hospitalized children in China: demographic characteristics of hospitalized children, the distribution of onset time, geographical distribution, treatment methods, and economic cost structure. Through this information, we hope to provide important clinical data to support the overall understanding and treatment of this disease.

Method

We retrospectively analyzed the medical record pages of children who were hospitalized for TSH at discharge diagnosis in 32 tertiary children's hospitals under China’s Futang Research Center of Pediatric Development between December 1, 2015, and December 31, 2022 [20]. We collected data such as age, gender, diagnosis name, treatment methods, onset time, region, and various expenses during hospitalization from the ICD codes.

The inclusion criteria were: (1) pediatric patients aged < 18 years presenting with acute-onset, non-traumatic limp, hip pain, or refusal to bear weight; (2) ultrasonographic confirmation of hip joint effusion; (3) blood sample results inconclusive for a definite diagnosis; (4) absence of osteoarticular abnormalities on standard hip radiographs. Cases were excluded if they developed definitive diagnostic evidence of alternative pathologies during the clinical observation period [14, 21] (Fig. 1).Fig. 1This picture shows the diagnostic algorithm for children with TSH

We divided all included children into different age groups [22]: those aged ≤ 2 years were the Infants group, those aged 2 < X ≤ 6 years were the Preschool children group, those aged 6 < X ≤ 11 years were the School children group, and those aged 11 < X ≤ 18 years were the Adolescents group.

According to the geographical area of ​​TSH occurrence, the inpatients were classified into seven geographical areas: Northeast China (Heilongjiang Province, Jilin Province, Liaoning Province), North China (Hebei Province, Shanxi Province, Beijing, Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region), East China (Anhui Province, Shandong Province, Jiangsu Province, Zhejiang Province, Fujian Province, Jiangxi Province), South China (Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region, Guangdong Province), Central China (Hunan Province, Hubei Province, Henan Province), Northwest China (Gansu Province, Qinghai Province, Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region, Shaanxi Province), Southwest China (Guizhou Province, Yunnan Province).

We obtained the exchange rate between the RMB and USD (1 USD = 6.9646 RMB as of December 31, 2022) from the official website of the China Foreign Exchange Trade System (https://www.chinamoney.com.cn). The currency was converted from RMB to USD to present the spending more intuitively. For all included hospitalized children, the following medical costs were recorded: total hospital cost, nursing care cost, examination cost, treatment cost, surgery and anesthesia cost, medication cost, material costs, and other comprehensive care costs.

All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows version 26.0 (IBM Corporation, Armonk, New York, USA). Enumeration data were expressed as rate, and measurement data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (x ± s). The chi-square test and Kruskal–Wallis H test were used to explore the effects of concurrent infectious diseases on the number of TSH cases in different age groups and the medical expenses of TSH hospitalized children in different age groups and different regions. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Demographic data and hospital stay of children with TSH

A total of 5065 children were included in this study. The highest number of cases was recorded in the preschool group, with 2452 cases (48.4%), and it was followed in descending order by the school group (1998 cases, 39.4%), the infant group (496 cases, 9.9%), and the adolescent group, with the lowest number of cases (119 cases, 2.3%). Among the 5065 patients with TSH who were included, male children accounted for 73.3% (n = 3711) and female children accounted for 26.7% (n = 1354) of the cases, with a male-to-female ratio of 2.74:1. In each age group, the proportion of male children was higher than that of female children, with the difference being the largest in the adolescent group, with a male-to-female ratio of 4.17. Treatment options for children with TSH according to age

Among the 5,065 children diagnosed with TSH, 7,747 treatments were performed, including 7,667 conservative and 80 surgical treatments. Conservative treatment included skin traction treatment (4508 instances), physical therapy (2062 instances), and traditional Chinese medicine treatment (1097 instances). Surgical treatment included hip puncture (52 instances) and hip synovectomy (28 instances).

The treatment methods differed according to age group. Conservative treatment (including skin traction therapy, physical therapy, and traditional Chinese medicine treatment) was most common among the preschool children (3710 instances, 48.3%), followed by school-going children (3069 instances, 40.1%), infants (726 instances, 9.5%), and adolescents, who received the least number of conservative treatments (162 instances, 2.1%).

With regard to the age-dependent differences in conservative treatment methods, the number of skin traction treatments was the highest among preschool children (2,169 instances, 48.1%), followed by school-going children (1,755 instances, 38.9%), infants (478 instances, 10.6%), and adolescents, with the least number (106 instances, 2.4%). Similarly, with regard to physical therapy, the highest number of physical therapy treatments were administered to preschool children (946 instances, 45.9%), followed by school-going children (889 instances, 43.1%), infants (190 instances, 9.2%), and adolescents, with the least number (37 instances, 1.8%). With regard to traditional Chinese medicine, preschool children received the highest number (595 instances, 54.2%), followed by school-going children (425 instances, 38.8%), infants (58 instances, 5.3%), and adolescents (19 instances, 1.7%).

With regard to the age-related differences in the administration of surgical treatment, the school-going group had the highest number of surgeries (40 instances, 50.0%), and the infant group had the least (2 instances, 2.5%). Among the surgical treatment methods administered, the number of hip punctures was the highest in the school-going group (29 instances, 55.8%), while it was the lowest in the infant group (0 times, 0%). That is, hip puncture was not performed in any of the infants. Composition of hospitalization expenses for TSH inpatients according to age

The median total hospitalization cost for the entire cohort was USD 551.3, and median costs for individual items were as follows: examination costs, USD 188.4; treatment costs, USD 115.0; medicine costs, USD 78.8; nursing care costs, USD 23.0; material costs, USD 17.9; and surgery and anesthesia costs, USD 16.4.

With regard to age-based differences in the total hospitalization cost, it was the highest in the adolescent group at USD 659.0, followed by the infant group at USD 579.7, the preschool children group at USD 557.3, and the school-going children group, with the lowest total hospitalization cost at USD 527.7. Statistically significant differences were observed in the total hospitalization costs according to age group (p-value < 0.05).

In the infant group, examination costs were the highest (USD 246.0), and material costs were the lowest (USD 36.5). Similarly, in the school-going children group, examination costs were the highest (USD 178.5), and material costs were the lowest (USD 13.8). In the preschool children group, too, examination costs were the highest (USD 181.7), but surgery and anesthesia costs were the lowest (USD 15.8). Similarly, in the adolescent group, examination costs were the highest (USD 259.1), and surgery and anesthesia costs were the lowest (USD 14.0). Discussion

This study used data from a large sample to systematically analyze the epidemiological characteristics of TSH and observed the following demographic, temporal, spatial, treatment, and hospitalization cost patterns: (1) Preschool children (2–5 years) accounted for the highest number of cases of TSH with hospitalization. With regard to the sex-based pattern, the proportion of male children was higher than that of female children, with male children accounting for 73.3% of the entire cohort of pediatric TSH. In terms of hospitalization duration, the average hospitalization time was the longest in the adolescent group. (2) According to the temporal distribution pattern, the month of December saw the highest number of cases (481 cases), and February had the lowest (266 cases). In terms of geographical distribution, East China saw the highest number of hospitalizations for TSH (1,345 cases), while Central China recorded the lowest (202 cases). (3) In terms of the choice of treatment method, conservative treatment (7667 instances) was significantly more frequent than surgical treatment (80 instances). Among the various conservative treatment methods, skin traction was the most commonly used method (4508 cases), while hip puncture was the most commonly used surgical intervention (52 cases). (4) The median hospitalization cost of the entire cohort was USD 551.3. Among the hospitalization items, examination costs were the highest and reached USD 188.4. With regard to age- and region-based patterns in hospitalization cost, the adolescent group had the highest total hospitalization cost (USD 659.0), and Northeast China had the highest hospitalization cost (USD 755.8).

Demographics and hospital stay of children with TSH

TSH is one of the most common causes of hip pain and limp in children [19, 23]. However, most of the current epidemiological knowledge about TSH is based on single-center retrospective studies. In one such single-center epidemiological survey of 259 children with TSH in the UK, it was found that the average age of onset of pediatric TSH was 5.4 years, and the male-to-female ratio was 3.2:1 [24]. Similarly, in a single-center study of 109 patients in New Zealand, the average age at admission of pediatric TSH was 4 years, with males accounting for 68% of all children with TSH [25]. Our results are similar to these data. That is, preschool children (2–5 years) formed the most susceptible group, accounting for as much as 48.4% of all cases. With regard to the sex-dependent trends, as observed in previous studies, males accounted for 73.3% of all hospitalized children. Notably, the higher incidence of TSH in preschool children may not be solely attributed to biological factors. However, it could also be influenced by social factors such as daycare attendance, frequency of social interactions, and differences in infection prevention measures, representing a potential confounding factor. While age- and sex-specific susceptibility patterns have been well documented in existing studies, the underlying pathophysiological mechanisms contributing to these demographic disparities remain unclear and warrant further investigation.

Temporal and spatial distribution of children with TSH

Viral infection is often considered an important triggering factor of TSH, and its onset is closely related to the month of year [26–28]. However, there is no consensus on the temporal or seasonal patterns of TSH. For example, according to an epidemiological survey of 275 children in Malmo, Sweden, the incidence of TSH showed obvious seasonal changes, with the highest number of cases recorded in October, and the lowest, in February [28]. In contrast, studies in Liverpool, England, have suggested that there is no obvious relationship between the seasonality of TSH and the incidence of TSH [17]. However, our multicenter data showed that the hospitalization time of children with TSH was the highest in December (481 cases) and the lowest in February (266 cases). However, due to climatic differences between regions, the incidence of TSH in different outset time needs further research to identify the patterns specific to different regions.

Among all the provinces in China, Guangdong Province had the largest number of children hospitalized for TSH. By geographical region, East China has the highest number of children (1,345 cases), while Central China has the lowest (202 cases). Given the high burden of TSH in Guangdong and East China, it is crucial to enhance professional training for healthcare providers and improve public health education regarding TSH diagnosis and management. Strengthening medical education on differentiating TSH from septic arthritis may contribute to timely diagnosis, appropriate management, and potential cost reduction, optimizing resource allocation in healthcare systems [14, 29, 30].

Treatment options for children with TSH

TSH is a common form of self-limiting arthritis in children [5, 31, 32] and is generally considered to be a disease that can spontaneously resolve without extensive intervention. Therefore, conservative treatment has become the first choice for children, including rest, skin traction therapy, physical therapy, traditional Chinese medicine treatment, and the use of nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs [8–13]. Below, we discuss these treatment options and their patterns of application in the present cohort.

Skin traction therapy can accelerate the recovery of hip joint function and shorten the recovery time of children by reducing stimulation to local tissues, promoting the absorption of inflammatory exudates, and repairing synovial folds [8]. In our study, most hospitalized children with TSH chose conservative treatment (7667 instances), of which skin traction therapy was the most commonly used intervention (in 4508 out of 7677 instances). We speculate that the widespread use of skin traction therapy is due to the simplicity of the procedure and its low degree of invasiveness, in addition to its ability to promote the absorption of inflammatory exudates associated with hip arthritis and accelerate the recovery of hip joint function in children with TSH. This treatment method not only effectively relieves pain but also has a high degree of safety, and it is, therefore, applied and promoted widely in the clinical setting.

The physical therapy strategies in children with TSH mainly include high-frequency electromagnetic wave irradiation and infrared therapy. High-frequency electromagnetic waves can reduce inflammation and relieve pain by improving local blood circulation, enhancing leukocyte migration and phagocytosis, and increasing vascular permeability, thereby shortening the healing time [33]. A study by Li et al. [34] showed that the cure rate of electromagnetic wave treatment for 96 children with hip synovitis was as high as 70%. Infrared therapy can reduce inflammation and relieve pain by accelerating microcirculation, promoting metabolism, and enhancing cell phagocytosis. Sha et al. [35] used a synovitis decoction combined with infrared therapy to treat 55 children with hip synovitis and reported its effectiveness as 92.5%. In our study, physical therapy was the second most popular treatment for children with TSH (applied in 2062 out of 7677 instances of conservative treatment). This result also indicates that Chinese clinicians strongly believe in the efficacy of physical therapy for TSH.

In recent years, research on traditional Chinese medicine treatments, including oral administration and external application of traditional Chinese medicine, massage, and moxibustion, has seen significant progress in the field of children’s hip joint diseases. Traditional Chinese medicine treatment is believed to have the ability to directly act on the hip joint, accelerate the resolution of inflammation, and promote blood circulation, thereby improving clinical symptoms [9–12]. In our study, traditional Chinese medicine treatment was an important part of the treatment plan for children with TSH (applied in 1097 out of 7677 instances of conservative treatment). In terms of geographical distribution, East China, North China, and Northeast China accounted for the highest proportion of traditional Chinese medicine applications for TSH (17.5% to 34.8%), while Central China accounted for the lowest proportion (only 2.7%). This reflects differences in TSH treatment options in different regions that may be related to the differences in doctors’ perceptions of TSH treatment. More high-quality clinical studies based on large samples are still needed to further verify the effectiveness and safety of TSH treatments in order to provide sufficient evidence for optimizing the treatment plan for TSH.

Surgical management is also adopted in some cases of pediatric TSH. One such procedure is synovectomy, which can help identify actual lesions and guide treatment in children with unclear diagnosis [36–38]. Another surgical option is joint aspiration, which was found to quickly relieve TSH symptoms by Kesteris et al. [39]. However, there is some controversy about its efficacy, and Skinner et al. [40] believe that this surgery has no long-term benefits. This is because most joint effusions disappear spontaneously within 1 week, while effusions may still exist after pain and lameness symptoms are relieved [40]. In our study, surgical treatment accounted for only a small number of the total treatments (80 out of 7677 instances of treatment). However, for children with unclear diagnoses or no significant improvement after conservative treatment, surgical treatment is still a necessary option.

Our data revealed differences in the choice of treatment methods between different age groups and regions that may reflect differences in treatment preferences. Moreover, the regional differences may be related to differences in the allocation of medical resources, the clinical experience of doctors, and local treatment habits. Therefore, in order to optimize the diagnosis and treatment of TSH, more large-scale, multicenter studies on different treatment methods are urgently needed to develop unified treatment guidelines based on evidence-based data, to reduce regional and age differences, to ensure that all children can receive standardized treatment.

Patterns in hospitalization expenses for children with TSH

TSH in children accounts for 0.4% to 0.9% of pediatric emergency department visits [2]. As a global disease, TSH imposes a significant economic burden. To our knowledge, this is the first to study to analyze the cost of hospitalization in children with TSH. The median hospitalization cost for all children with TSH who received inpatient care was USD 551.3. Interestingly, although TSH is a relatively common disease, examination costs accounted for the highest proportion of hospitalization costs, reaching USD 188.4. This is probably because the initial manifestations of TSH are similar to septic arthritis, which is considered a surgical emergency that may lead to serious complications such as chronic bone infection, subperiosteal abscess, and venous thrombosis [41]. Currently, the first and most well-known scoring system to distinguish TSH from septic arthritis is Kocher’s criteria [42], which is used to determine the diagnosis based on a combination of clinical signs, symptoms, and laboratory parameters. However, some scholars [43] believe that Kocher’s criteria are not yet sufficiently effective in diagnosing septic arthritis and recommend that clinicians remain cautious when applying this set of criteria. Thus, distinguishing TSH from septic arthritis requires the use of other clinical tests that result in the cost of the examinations going beyond the cost of the treatment itself. Large-scale prospective studies are urgently needed to optimize the diagnostic process and explore examination methods that are both efficient and cost-effective. This will help improve the accuracy of clinical differential diagnosis, reduce unnecessary examinations, reduce medical costs, and provide patients with more precise and economical medical services.

We found a significant age-dependent trend in the total hospitalization cost, with the adolescent group having the highest median cost of USD 659.0. This difference in age groups may be related to the complexity of the conditions of children in different age groups. Our study also showed that there were significant differences in hospitalization costs across different regions, which may be related to differences in the allocation of medical resources and regional preferences for treatment plans. Future research could further explore the specific impact of age and geographical location on hospitalization costs and apply standardization methods to enhance the generalizability and comparability of cost analyses. This would contribute to optimizing resource allocation and facilitate more precise medical decision-making, ultimately leading to the development of more targeted and cost-effective treatment strategies.

This study has the following limitations, which should be considered when interpreting the results. First, the inherent limitations of the retrospective study design may affect the completeness and accuracy of the data. The diagnosis of TSH in this study was based on ICD-10 coding, verified by attending physicians, senior doctors, ward nurses, and medical record departments to ensure relative accuracy. However, due to the multicenter nature of this study, differences in implementation details across hospitals may introduce selection bias, potentially affecting the estimation of the true incidence of TSH and the accuracy of its epidemiological data. Second, the treatment plan for TSH typically includes conservative treatment measures such as analgesic drugs, but the hospitalization records do not fully cover the related data because these drugs are often prescribed before hospitalization in the outpatient setting. Therefore, this study does not cover this aspect of the treatment data. Finally, the number of cases in different regions may be influenced by population density and the proportion of children, leading to potential regional bias. Therefore, when interpreting the results, these limitations should be carefully considered, and future studies should aim to optimize the research through prospective designs, more comprehensive case records, and standardization of actual pediatric population data across regions.

Conclusion

This multi-center study systematically summarizes the epidemiological characteristics and treatment methods for pediatric TSH in China. We found that the incidence of TSH exhibits clear age, gender, onset time, and regional patterns. With regard to the treatment patterns, conservative treatment appears to be the main treatment strategy. Further, we observed differences in treatment methods and hospitalization costs across different regions and age groups. These findings provide important clinical data that support the overall understanding and treatment of this disease.

A commentary on ‘Surgical modifications to the conventional kidney transplant technique - the Miami Transplant Institute approach: a retrospective cohort study’
Authors: Zeng Wang, Junjian Wang, Hongyu Chen
Published: 2024

Dear Editor,

The recent research paper ‘Surgical modifications to the conventional kidney transplant technique - the Miami Transplant Institute approach: a retrospective cohort study’ published in the International Journal of Surgery caught our attention1. This was a retrospective, single-centre, cohort study. The study adopted a modified approach to kidney transplantation surgery, avoiding the need for initial ureteral stent placement and surgical drainage, to assess whether the modified procedure could reduce the risk of post-transplant urological/vascular/other surgical complications. Gaetano Ciancio et al. showed that this modified kidney transplant technique clearly helped in reducing post-transplant risks of developing urologic/vascular/other surgical complications. Importantly, these results were achieved without initial ureteral stent placement or surgical drainage. The study is significant in kidney transplantation, as it contributes to improving the prognosis of kidney transplant patients, reducing patient discomfort, and making the transplant surgery easier to perform. For these important findings, we express our deep appreciation to the researchers for their rigorous efforts and valuable contributions. At the same time, we would like to offer some constructive comments to further improve the research programme.

Firstly, regarding the design of the study methodology, this was a retrospective study of 707 patients who developed complications in the first 12 months after transplantation, with a low rate of complications, and these overall results were clearly favourable when compared to other studies. However, retrospective studies often only provide correlation and do not fully prove causation2. Retrospective cohort studies are typically based on existing data analysis rather than pre-designed and controlled experiments. This means researchers cannot control potential confounding variables or ensure causality. Therefore, retrospective studies have limitations in determining causal relationships.

Second, the study has a long duration, incorporating clinical data from 2014 to 2023, which increases the risk of data loss or incomplete information. Long time spans may lead to biases in data quality, such as changes in measurement methods, recording practices, or diagnostic criteria, all of which could lead to data skewing. Participant selection might be influenced by certain factors. These factors could also impact the representativeness of the sample, thereby limiting the external validity of the study and potentially affecting the interpretation and generalizability of the research findings.

Finally, this study is a single-centre study, which has advantages such as controllability, convenience, and specialization, and the data is more credible. However, it also has certain limitations. For example, a single-centre study may not be representative of other regions or different populations. Since the study only covers one location or institution, the results may lack broad applicability, limiting the external validity of the research. Additionally, single-centre studies may be challenged by the consistency of data collection and analysis methods. Due to the involvement of only one location or institution in the study, there may be a lack of diversity, which could impact the reliability and robustness of the research findings. For instance, this study only includes patients from the Miami area, making the results relatively limited and potentially affecting their reliability and generalizability. Compared with single-centre studies, multi-centre studies are richer in data, which can significantly improve the efficiency, representativeness and generalizability of the results3,4.

In conclusion, the study by Gaetano Ciancio et al. is of great significance in improving the benefit of patients with renal transplantation. Improvements in kidney transplantation have led to a major step forward in the field of kidney transplantation. Our suggestions are merely aimed at further enhancing an already outstanding research achievement, and we eagerly look forward to the authors producing even more innovative work in the future.

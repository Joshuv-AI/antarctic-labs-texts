Allergenicity of Eight Soy Products: Allergy to Soy Milk Is Most Frequent and Most Severe
Authors: E. J. J. Kallen, A. M. van Dijk, M. N. Blaauw, K. C. M. Verhoeckx, P. Krepel, R. Van Ree, P. M. J. Welsing, A. C. Knulst, T. M. Le
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Summary Soy milk most commonly caused allergic reactions and most often caused severe reactions.When allergic to soy milk, 52.7% do not need to avoid other soy products.

To the Editor,

The allergenicity of soybean and various products thereof is poorly characterised [1]. This daily practice study investigates differences in frequency and severity for eight differently processed soy products.

Adults (n = 60) with a soy allergy (suggestive history and sensitisation to soy/positive oral food challenge [OFC]) were included. Informed consent was obtained from each patient. The products investigated were: soy milk, tofu, tempeh, soybean, soy‐based meat substitutes, soy sauce, bread containing soy flour and the soy‐containing biscuit “Biscoff”. Severity was measured by the oFASS‐3 severity score [2]. Skin prick test (SPT) (ALK, Madrid, Spain) were performed to birch pollen and soy extract, prick‐to‐prick (PTP) to soymilk, tofu and/or tempeh and sIgE(ImmunoCAP, ThermoFisher Scientific, Uppsala, Sweden) to birch pollen, Bet v 1, soy extract, Gly m 4, Gly m 5 and Gly m 6. A mean wheal diameter ≥ 3 mm and sIgE levels ≥ 0.10 kUA/L were considered positive, respectively. The frequency of self‐reported symptoms and of severe food allergy was statistically compared between soy products using random effect logistic regression.

Most patients were sensitised to sIgE to Gly m 4 (90.7%) and PTP soy milk (80.6%). Soy milk most commonly caused symptoms (94.4%), followed by tofu (45.8%), soybean/tempeh (both 41.2%), meat substitute (37.5%), soy sauce (28.3%), bread (16.7%) and Biscoff (16.6%) (Figure available in OSF). In eight patients, nine OFCs were performed with soy products: soy flour (three), meat substitutes (two), tofu (one), soy milk (two) and soy protein powder (one). All OFCs with soy flour, meat substitute and soy protein powder were negative, and those with tofu and soy milk were positive.

The frequency of reported symptoms to the various soy products differed significantly (p < 0.001). Compared to Biscoff, patients who consumed soy milk had the highest chance (OR = 43.9 [95% CI 16.5–116.9]) to react, while patients who consumed soy sauce and bread had the lowest chance (OR = 1.8 [95% CI 0.7–4.7] and OR = 1.0 [95% CI 0.4–2.7], respectively). More than half (52.9%) of patients reacting to soy milk reported no symptoms to other soy products (Figure available in OSF). When they did report symptoms to other soy products, this was most often to tofu (45.0%) and least often to Biscoff (17.9%). On the contrary, almost all patients who reported symptoms to a soy product other than soy milk, reported symptoms to multiple soy products, of which soy milk was most common (81.8%–100%) and bread and Biscoff least common (17.9%–60.0%).

Soy milk most often caused severe symptoms; in more than half of the patients, symptoms were severe and in almost a third moderate (Figure available in OSF). Symptom severity varied significantly across products (p < 0.001).

Compared to Biscoff, patients who consumed soy milk had the highest chance to report moderate/severe symptoms (OR = 31.3 [95% CI 9.1–107.8]), while soy sauce and bread had the lowest chance (OR = 2.9 [95% CI 0.9–9.6] and OR = 1.6 [95% CI 0.4–5.8], respectively). Among those with severe soy milk reactions, 28.6% also had severe reactions to other soy products (Figure 1).

To our knowledge, this is the first study to assess soy allergy across eight soy products, revealing significant differences in self‐reported frequency and severity. Soy milk most frequently caused reactions and most often caused severe reactions, while bread and Biscoff caused the least.

Differences in allergy frequency may relate to consumption [3]. However, two studies found no link between soy consumption and sensitisation [4, 5]. Further research is needed to clarify this relationship, especially as global demand for alternative protein sources like soy is expected to increase with population growth.

Differences in severity may be affected by type of processing and the number of processing steps [1, 6]. The exact processing steps of soy products are not always known, vary between food producers, and the evidence in reducing the allergenicity is limited because there are no high‐quality clinical studies performed in soy allergic patients [1]. The type and amount of soy proteins could also play a role in the severity. For example, Gly m 4, the soy pathogenesis‐related protein, has been associated with severe food adverse reactions [7, 8]. Yoshimitsu et al. showed a large difference in Gly m 4 levels with 309–421 μg/g in soybean, 45–67 μg/g in soymilk and 2.7–45 μg/g in roasted soy flour [8].

We showed that a reaction to soy milk does not imply a reaction to other soy products. Therefore, when patients are diagnosed with soy milk allergy, they should not be advised to avoid all soy products. Biscoff, bread and soy sauce are least likely to cause an allergy, and if they do cause a reaction, it is likely to be the least severe. We would therefore advise reintroducing these products carefully into the diet first.

A limitation of this study is that an OFC was not performed in all patients. In this study, diagnosis was based on a specialist's assessment of suggestive history and sensitisation, with OFCs performed only when uncertainty existed or specific products needed evaluation. For most patients, the history was clear, and multiple soy products were tolerated. Strengths include the large variety of soy products and the relatively large study population.

To conclude, frequency and severity of food allergy differ between eight differently processed soy products. Soy milk is responsible for the most frequent and severe reactions. In case of allergy to soy milk, other soy products are often tolerated. Therefore, it is important that patients who report allergic reactions to soy require an assessment of their tolerance to other soy products. Future studies using OFCs are needed to further validate these observations.

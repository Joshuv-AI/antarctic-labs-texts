Effects of LED phototherapy on oxidative damage in hyperbilirubinemia neonates
Authors: Meihong Gu, Lifang Lin, Tianming Yuan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPhototherapy is known to cause oxidative damage in neonates, there is no research on DNA damage repair before and after phototherapy in hyperbilirubinemia neonates at present. We aim to evaluate the oxidative damage and the mRNA expression of specific DNA repair genes in hyperbilirubinemic neonates treating with light emitting diode (LED) phototherapy.Methods38 neonates were classified into two groups: 19 received LED phototherapy, 19 received intensive LED phototherapy. Malondialdehyde (MDA), 8-hydroxy-2-deoxyguanosine (8-OHdG), superoxide dismutase (SOD), glutathione peroxidase (GSH-Px) and the mRNA expression of P53, human 8-oxoguanine DNA glycosylase (hOGG1), human MutT homolog1 (hMTH1), human MutY homolog (hMYH) genes were measured before and after phototherapy.ResultsThe 8-OHdG concentration increased (P<0.05) and the GSH-Px activity decreased (P<0.05) after phototherapy in both groups. The MDA level increased (P<0.05) and the SOD activity decreased (P<0.05) after phototherapy in the intensive LED group. The mRNA expression of hMTH1 and hOGG1 increased after phototherapy in the LED group (P<0.05).ConclusionsPhototherapy may induce oxidative damage to hyperbilirubinemia neonates. The LED phototherapy (intensity: 10–15µW/cm2/nm) may up-regulate the mRNA expression of repair genes.

Background

Hyperbilirubinemia is a common healthy problem among neonates, and is also the most important reason of readmissions in the first week of life [1]. Phototherapy is thought to be a safe and well-tolerated method for jaundice neonates. However, some research had showed that either fluorescent tube (FT) phototherapy or light emitting diode (LED) phototherapy can increase oxidative stress and cause oxidant/antioxidant imbalance, which may accelerate the production of reactive oxygen species (ROS)/reactive nitrogen species (RNS) [2, 3]. The imbalance between ROS/RNS and antioxidants can cause damage to DNA, lipids, and proteins [4]. Several studies have indicated that phototherapy can induce DNA damage [5–7]. In these studies, the phototherapy devices were FTs. However, LED light sources, emitting high-intensity and narrow-spectrum light with less heat production in the peak light spectrum, are frequently used in hyperbilirubinemia recently.

Malondialdehyde (MDA), a good marker of lipid peroxidation, is released when ROS reacts with polyunsaturated fatty acids [8]. 8-hydroxy-2-deoxyguanosine (8-OHdG), also known as 8-oxo-2’-deoxyguanosine (8-oxo-dG), an oxidized derivate of the guanine base, has mutagenic potential as it leads to AT to CG transversions [9]. Concentrations of 8-oxo-dG is a measurement of oxidative DNA damage. Scavenger enzymes such as glutathione peroxidase (GSH-Px) and superoxide dismutase (SOD) can protect cells against ROS. Moreover, when the DNA damage occurs, the P53 tumor suppressor gene induces cell cycle arrest to inhibit DNAreplication and the DNA repair system including the enzymes human MutT homolog1 (hMTH1), human 8-oxoguanine DNA glycosylase (hOGG1), and human MutY homolog (hMYH) can repair oxidative DNA damage. The hMTH1 enzyme can hydrolyze 8-oxo-dGTP to 8-oxo-dGMP, thereby preventing misincorporation of 8-oxo-dGTP into nascent DNA [10]. The hOGG1 removes one of the most abundant DNA lesions, 8-oxo-7,8-dihydroguanine (8-oxoG) [11], and hMYH removes adenine from these 8-oxoG: A mispairing [12]. Although the relationship between phototherapy and DNA damage has been described in some studies [5–7, 13], the mRNA expression of P53, hMTH1, hOGG1, and hMYH genes has not been investigated until now. Phototherapy is thought to disturb the oxidative/antioxidant balance and cause oxidative damage in neonates with hyperbilirubinemia. However, the body has a DNA repair system that can repair oxidative DNA damage. We think that the antioxidant capacity will decrease and DNA damage will increase after phototherapy. And we suppose that the expression of DNA repair genes will be up-regulated to repair oxidative damage so as not to cause mutations. This is a defense mechanism of the normal body. We detected antioxidant enzymes, MDA, 8-OHdG levels to reflect whether phototherapy caused oxidative damage to hyperbilirubinemia neonates, and determined the mRNA expression of repair gene before and after phototherapy to reflect whether the expression of the repair gene is up-regulated after phototherapy. Therefore, this study was conducted to (1) determine if LED phototherapy causing oxidative damage in hyperbilirubinemic neonates. (2) evaluate if LED phototherapy having a negative influence on the antioxidative capacity; (3) assess the mRNA expression of specific DNA repair genes before and after phototherapy.

Methods

Patients and phototherapy devices

This study was conducted on 38 term and late-preterm neonates (≥ 35 weeks) with postnatal age of 2–14 days and indirect hyperbilirubinemia requiring phototherapy according to the American Association of Pediatrics (AAP) guidelines [14]. These neonates were admitted to the Department of Neonatology, Children’s Hospital, Zhejiang University School of Medicine. The infants with gestational age < 35 weeks, perinatal asphyxia, respiratory distress syndrome, sepsis, maternal diabetes, congenital malformation, metabolic disease, hemolytic diseases and jaundice necessitating exchange transfusion were excluded from the study. A formal consent letter from the parents of each newborn was obtained after explaining to them the whole procedure. The study was approved by the ethical committee of the Faculty of Medicine, Children’s Hospital, Zhejiang University school of medicine (2021-IRB-244). Patients were divided into two groups: group 1 and group 2 were both 19 neonates with non-haemolytic indirect hyperbilirubinemia. Two groups received different intensity of LED phototherapy for 12 h. The intensity of phototherapy was decided by the total bilirubin concentrations. For group 2, lower intensity LED phototherapy systems (DAVID XHZ, Ningbo, China) were used. This light source consists of upper and nether cases, and both cases use blue LED as light source (spectrum 400–550 nm, intensity: 10–15µW/cm2/nm). For group 1, BISTOS BT-400 LED phototherapy equipment (South Korea, intensity: 42µW /cm2/nm ± 20%, spectrum 430–490 nm) combined with LED phototherapy systems (DAVID XHZ, Ningbo, China) was used. The BISTOS BT-400 LED phototherapy equipment was placed 40 cm above the infant. All patients were laid at the same distance from the phototherapy equipment and were kept unclothed with the exception of their eyes and the genital region.

Sample collection

Except for the routine laboratory examinations, additional blood samples (3.5 ml) were collected in the EDTA-K2 vacuum anticoagulant tube to evaluate oxidative damage before phototherapy and 12 h after phototherapy, stored at room temperature and were processed within 2 h. The blood samples were centrifuged for 5 min at 3500rmp at 24℃ in the horizontal centrifuge. About 1.5 ml plasma was aspirated to detect MDA,8-OHdG, SOD, GSH-Px, stored at -80 °C and analyzed within 14 days. The mixture of the remained plasma, white cells layer and a small part of red cells layer (together 1-1.5 ml) was collected for isolating peripheral blood mononuclear cell (PBMC). The yield of cells from 3.5 ml of blood varies between 107 and 108. Add 0.5 ml-1 ml of TRIzol Reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, EUA) per 107-108 cells directly to lyse the cells and store at -100 80℃ until further analysis. MDA, 8-OHdG, SOD, GSH-Px and the mRNA expression of P53, hOGG1, hMTH1, hMYH genes were all measured three times in each sample. The Lab investigators were blinded to the patients’ clinical data.

Analysis of 8-OHdG

8-OHdG in plasma was quantitated using an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kit,8-OHdG ELISA Kit (K4160-100, BioVision, San Francisco, US). The 8-OHdG concentration was measured according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Determination of MDA

Malondialdehyde Detection Kit (A003-1; Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China) was selected to determine the MDA level. MDA reacts with thiobarbituric acid (TBA) to yield a red MDA-TBA adduct under acidic and high temperature condition. The MDA-TBA adducts were detected by a multimode plate reader (PerkinElmer/envision) with wavelength fixed in 532.00 nm [15].

The assay of GSH-Px and SOD activity

The activity of GSH-PX was determined with the Glutathione Peroxidase Detection Kit (A005; Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions [16]. And SOD activity was measured with the Superoxide Dismutase Detection Kit (A001-3; Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China) according to the manufacturer’s instruction.

Reverse transcription quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) assay for P53, hMTH1, hOGG1, and hMYH genes mRNA

Total RNA was extracted using Trizol reagent (Invitrogen) and digested with gDNA Eraser (Takara, Kusatsu, Japan). First-strand complementary DNA were synthesized using the Primescript RT reagent kit (Takara, Kusatsu, Japan) according to the instructions. The primer sequences used for RT-qPCR analysis are given in Table 1. P53, hMTH1, hOGG1, and hMYH genes mRNA expression was measured using ABI 12k RT-qPCR Detection System instrument (ABI, California, America) and light cycler 480 SYBR Green I Master (Roche, Mannheim, Germany). A standard PCR reaction contained 1µL of each prime (0.5Μm), 1 mL of cDNA (20 ng), 10 µL of SYBR Green Mix, and 7 µL of DEPC-treated water. The PCR cycle condition was programed as follows: initial denaturation at 95 °C for 10 min, 40 cycles including 95℃ for 10s, 60℃ for 15s, 72℃ for 20s and a melt curve at the end of the cycle with determination from 55 ℃ to 95 ℃. The mRNA expression of all genes was analyzed in comparison with the housekeeping gene GAPDH. The gene mRNA relative expression level of the target gene is represented by 2 ^ − (Δ Δ Ct), where Δ Δ Ct= [Ct(target gene) -Ct(housekeeping gene)] (processed sample)-[Ct (target gene) -Ct(housekeeping gene)] (control sample).

Statistical Analysis

Fisher exact test was used for nominal variables. For the variables with normal distribution, Student’s t test was used for the unpaired groups, and paired-samples t test was used for the paired groups. For the variables with asymmetric distribution, Wilcoxon signed rank test was used for the paired data and Mann-Whitney U test was used for the unpaired groups. Pearson’s and Spearman’s correlation analysis were used to examine the correlation between variables. All analyses were performed with SPSS Statistics for Windows (software version 26.0, IBMCorp., Armonk, N.Y., USA). A P value less than 0.05 was considered as statistically significant.

Results

Total bilirubin (TB) level at the beginning of phototherapy was higher in the intensive LED group (group 1) compared to the LED group (group 2). TB level was significantly decreased after phototherapy in two groups (Table 2).

P53 and hMYH mRNA expression before phototherapy in the LED group (group 2) was not significantly different from the intensive LED group (group 1). And no statistical difference was observed in P53 mRNA expression between post-phototherapy and pre-phototherapy in two groups. The mRNA expression of hMTH1 and hOGG1 before phototherapy was significantly higher in the LED group than the intensive LED group. And the mRNA expression of hMTH1 and hOGG1 increased significantly after phototherapy in the LED group, but no significant difference in the intensive LED group. The hMYH mRNA expression decreased significantly after phototherapy in the intensive LED group, but no significant difference in the LED group (Table 4). There was no association between levels of P53, hMTH1, hOGG1, hMYH mRNA expression and either TB level or 8-OHdG concentration. A positive correlation between P53 and hOGG1 mRNA expression was found before phototherapy and after phototherapy in the LED group (r = 0.666, P = 0.002 and r = 0.581, P = 0.009 respectively) and the intensive LED group (r = 0.757, P < 0.001 and r = 0.791, P < 0.001 respectively). Additionally, there was a positive correlation between P53 and hMTH1 mRNA expression in the LED group (before phototherapy, r = 0.830, P < 0.001; after phototherapy, r = 0.891, P < 0.001) and the intensive LED group (before phototherapy, r = 0.837, P < 0.001; after phototherapy, r = 0.798, P < 0.001). hOGG1 and hMTH1 mRNA expression was positively correlated before phototherapy (LED, r = 0.526, P = 0.02; intensive LED, r = 0.610, P = 0.006).and after phototherapy (LED, r = 0.496, P = 0.03; intensive LED, r = 0.732, P < 0.001) in both groups. Additionally, a positive correlation between P53 and hMYH mRNA expression, hOGG1 and hMYH mRNA expression, hMYH and hMTH1 mRNA expression was found before and after phototherapy in two group with a few exceptions, as is showed in Fig. 2.

Discussion

Upon comparing the effect of different intensity LED phototherapy on the decrease of TB level, we found similar decrease in TB level between two groups, which is in accordance with the finding by Kanmaz HG [13]. However, Kale Y [17] found that high intensity (60–90 µW/cm2/nm) LED phototherapy was more effective in decreasing bilirubin level than low intensity (35µW/cm2/nm) LED phototherapy. An increase of DNA damage after phototherapy has been reported in some studies previously. Yahia S [5] and Ramy N [6] used comet essay to detect DNA damage and found that DNA damage was increased after phototherapy. By determining sister chromatid exchange (SCE) frequency to evaluate DNA damage, Karadag A [18] observed an increase of SCE frequency after conventional and intensive phototherapy and Kanmaz HG [13] found that SCE frequency was increased after LED phototherapy and convention therapy. In our study, the 8-OHdG concentration was significantly increased after the LED phototherapy and the intensive LED phototherapy. However, in contrast to our study, Karakukcu C [19] showed that phototherapy did not cause an increase of DNA damage but could reduce the DNA damage caused by bilirubin. Moreover, LED phototherapy in premature infants had found that either low (10–30 µW/cm2/nm) or high (> 30 µW/cm2/nm) irradiance were not associated with an increase in the oxidative marker of DNA damage 8-OHdG in preterm infants ≤ 32 weeks’ gestation [20].

In our study, we found a significant increase in MDA after the LED intensive phototherapy and a moderate increase after the LED phototherapy (P = 0.05). And SOD activity was significantly decreased in the intensive LED phototherapy and slightly decreased in the LED phototherapy (P = 0.38) after phototherapy. Both groups were observed a significant decrease of GSH-Px activity after phototherapy. It revealed that the antioxidant capacity was decreased after phototherapy. According to these results, phototherapy induced an increase in oxidative stress probably through decreasing the activity of antioxidant enzymes (such as, SOD and GSH-Px) and increasing oxidant agents. Similarly, El-Farrash RA [3], Erdem SS [21] and Dahiya K [22] showed that MDA levels were significantly increased after phototherapy. Similar to our results, El-Farrash RA [3] and Kale Y [17] reported that total antioxidant capacity was significantly decreased after phototherapy. But there are some debates about the MDA levels change after phototherapy. ÖZTÜRE H [23] and Aycicek A [24] showed that MDA levels were significantly decreased after phototherapy.

The potential antioxidant effect of bilirubin on newborns has been controversial for many years. It is generally believed that low levels of physiological bilirubin have beneficial antioxidant effects, but high levels of pathological bilirubin can increase oxidative stress [25]. In the present study, we found no correlation between TB levels and 8-OHdG concentration, MDA level, SOD activity, and GSH-Px activity. Our findings are in agreement with Yahia S [5] and Ramy N [6] who showed that hyperbilirubinemia does not induce DNA damage. In contrast, Basu S et al. [26] reported that Moderate-to severe unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia was associated with higher oxidative stress and lower antioxidant defense. Bilirubin may have genotoxic effect. Hence, the oxidative effect of phototherapy and hyperbilirubinemia on neonates is still uncertain.

Our study was the first to detect the mRNA expression of P53, hMTH1, hOGG1, hMYH genes before and after phototherapy. In our study, we observed that hMTH1 and hOGG1 mRNA expression was significantly up regulated and P53 and hMYH mRNA expression was moderately up-regulated after phototherapy in the LED phototherapy. There is a positive correlation between P53 and hOGG1, P53 and hMYH, P53 and hMTH1, hOGG1 and hMYH, hOGG1 and hMTH1, hMYH and hMTH1 mRNA expression before and after phototherapy in both groups. The repair genes mRNA expression may be up-regulated by the increase of oxidative stress. In addition, the repair gene of P53, hMTH1, hOGG1, hMYH mRNA may cooperate to reduce the oxidative stress induced by phototherapy. However, in the intensive LED phototherapy group, there was no significant up-regulation of P53, hMTH1, hOGG1 mRNA expression after phototherapy, and the hMYH mRNA expression was significantly down regulated after phototherapy. We guessed that the decreased hMYH mRNA expression after phototherapy may be the transient suppression of repair system by strong oxidative stress. And we think that the suppression may relieve after some time. But we didn’t detect the mRNA expression of repair genes after a period of time when phototherapy was finished. This may also be explained by the mutation and polymorphism in repair genes. The mutation and polymorphism may adversely affect the DNA repair system. Thus, when the oxidative stress occurred, the DNA repair system cannot response effectively. In addition, because it is the first study to evaluate the expression of repair genes in hyperbilirubinemia and the experimental funding is limited, we only analyzed the expression of these repair genes in mRNA levels, the protein expression of the repair genes is needed further study in the future experiments.

The mRNA expression of hMTH1 and hOGG1 before phototherapy in the intensive LED phototherapy was significantly lower than the LED phototherapy. Additionally, in the intensive LED phototherapy group, we observed a significantly higher GSH-Px activity than the LED phototherapy before phototherapy. The increased GSH-Px activity may be a compensatory up-regulation to keep the organism adequate antioxidative capacity. Kahveci H [27] showed that the negative effect of PT on SCE is a temporary effect. They reported that the DNA damage redetected after 3.5 years was similar with the healthy children. This is consistent with our results that the mRNA expression of repair genes was up-regulated to repair oxidative DNA damage when the oxidative stress occurred.

From the results of our study, the intensive LED phototherapy increased the 8-OHdG and MDA levels and decreased the SOD and GSH-Px activities after phototherapy. However, there was no change in the SOD activity and MDA level after phototherapy with the LED phototherapy treatment. Moreover, the mRNA expression of repair genes was up-regulated after phototherapy in the LED phototherapy group, but not in the intensive phototherapy group. It seems that the LED phototherapy is safer than the intensive LED phototherapy. Considering the same decrease in TB level between both phototherapy group in our study, whether the LED phototherapy is a better choice for hyperbilirubinemia neonates than the intensive LED phototherapy still needs more exploration.

LED phototherapy is supposed to be more effective than conventional phototherapy in reducing bilirubin. However, a meta-analysis by Tridente A [28] demonstrated that no significant difference in total serum bilirubin of decrease was detected between LED and other types of phototherapy. And our study indicated that LED phototherapy can increase oxidative damage in hyperbilirubinemia neonates, so LED phototherapy may be not a better choice for treating hyperbilirubinemia comparing with conventional phototherapy. More studies are needed to verify hypothesis.

Conclusions

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to evaluate the mRNA expression of repair genes in hyperbilirubinemia neonates before and after phototherapy. Our results suggested that oxidative stress may occur and the activity of antioxidant enzymes may decrease after phototherapy. The LED phototherapy (intensity: 10–15µW/cm2/nm) may up-regulate the mRNA expression of repair genes to repair oxidative DNA damage. And P53, hMTH1, hOGG1 and hMYH genes may act together to repair oxidative DNA damage when the oxidative stress occurred. More studies may be needed to explore whether the oxidative damage temporary or permanent.

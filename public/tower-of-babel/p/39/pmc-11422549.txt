Altered interhemispheric connectivity in Huntington’s Disease
Authors: Ali Demir, H Diana Rosas
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Demonstrated usefulness of directional tract density patterns (dTDPs) in Huntington’s Disease (HD).•Detected differences in regional interhemispheric connectivity for presymptomatic HD, especially marking those reaching to premotor, motor, and primary sensory areas as important.•dTDPs have the potential to evaluate individual/longitudinal progression in HD.Keywords: Huntington’s Disease, Human subjects, Corpus callosum, Interhemispheric connectivity, Directional tract density patterns, Diffusion magnetic resonance imaging

Introduction

Huntington’s disease (HD) is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder affecting motor, sensory, and cognitive functions (Hedreen et al., 1991) that is caused by the expansion of a CAG repeat in the huntingtin gene (Ravina et al., 2008). While the basal ganglia is one of the most severely affected structures (Rosas et al., 2008, Tabrizi et al., 2012, Poudel et al., 2015), both regional gray and white matter regions, including the corpus callosum (CC), demonstrate profound atrophy (Rosas et al., 2010, Zeun et al., 2022). White matter (WM) changes in HD may actually occur prior to gray matter changes, but this is a topic of some debate.

Part of the challenge of determining the temporal dynamics of early WM changes is due to limitations inherent to traditional methods that are routinely used. For example, diffusion tensor imaging characterizes WM using scalar measures, including fractional anisotropy, axial diffusivity and radial diffusivity. In particular, these microstructural diffusion measures are evaluated locally for each voxel and are orientation independent, which enables regional analyses without accounting for orientational WM differences. However, orientation independence might also filter out significant directional information. Even with other methods, such as neurite orientation dispersion and density imaging (Zhang et al., 2012), diffusion measures are evaluated locally and the respective metrics become orientation independent. Other traditional tractography approaches consider only the principal directions in diffusion models, such as a tensor or an orientation distribution function, in order to reconstruct a bundle of interest; this suggests that orientational changes in diffusivity measurements may alter the tractography results. While probabilistic tractography (Tournier et al., 2010) follows the most probable directions of the diffusion orientation distributions at each voxel, disease changes related to microstructural diffusivity could result in deviations of the fiber tractography, including producing, for example, very short or false-positive fibers (Chamberland et al., 2021). As a result, this could affect the true estimation of fibers reaching cortical/target areas. More recent approaches, including disconnectome and differential tractography (de Schotten et al., 2020, Yeh et al., 2019), have recently emerged but these studies do not explicitly provide the directional information of fiber bundles. Finally, while graph based connectivity approaches (Rubinov and Sporns, 2010) implicitly provide directionality between the source and target regions, they provide only a single number quantifying the connectivity between source and target pairs. Therefore, the available tools provide limited information (Münch et al., 2022, Stezin et al., 2021) and may not provide the much needed contrast and sensitivity to detect early subtle changes in disease conditions.

Our prior work and that of others (Di Pardo et al., 2016, Di Paola et al., 2012, Rosas et al., 2011, Rosas et al., 2010, Rosas et al., 2006) suggested that select interhemispheric fibers, which travel through the CC, are lost early in symptomatic HD. The CC is a major conduit for neural connections between the cortical hemispheres that relay sensory, motor, and cognitive information between homologous regions (Wahl et al., 2007, Gazzaniga, 2005, Musiek, 1986). Understanding early changes in the CC could provide some insight into early changes in interhemispheric connectivity in HD. We developed a novel analytical tool that is able to determine directional tract density patterns (Demir and Rosas, 2023) that differs from traditional diffusion methods; rather than providing a single number, such as fiber length or the number of fibers interconnecting two regions, it provides a highly dimensional spectrum that also provides the macroscopic quantification of fibers traversing through selected regions of a fiber bundle. Unlike graph based approaches, the dTDP framework provides a more detailed and precise topological representation and permits the topological assessment of fiber tracts from the CC beyond the mid-sagittal slice. In this way, this method also eliminates the need to determine end cortical regions, which can often be difficult to deconvolve. We hypothesized that the interhemispheric connectivity evaluated using dTDPs could identify regional alterations encompassing the entirety of interhemispheric WM pathways and could also provide novel information about patterns of altered interhemispheric connectivity in HD, including during the motor presymptomatic period.

Materials and methods

Data acquisition and preprocessing framework

Subjects were scanned using a Siemens Tim Trio 3T MRI scanner at the A.A. Martinos Center for Biomedical Imaging. Imaging included an MEMPRAGE/T1 image (RMS average of 4 echoes: TE={1.64,3.5,5.36,7.22}ms, isotropic resolution of 1.0 mm, TR = 2530 ms, flip angle=7o, TI = 1200 ms), a diffusion MRI acquisition using 70 diffusion weighted volumes (diffusion gradient strengths of 10 b0 + 60 b700 s/mm2, isotropic resolution of 2 mm, TR = 7980 ms, TE = 83 ms, FOV = 256 mm x 256 mm), and gradient field mapping image with two echoes (TR = 489 ms, TE={2.84,5.3}ms) to correct for distortions. All diffusion acquisitions were visually inspected and subsequently assessed for quality (motion/outlier) using the FSL eddy tool. All scans had an average absolute motion of less than 1.5mm and percent dropout of slices of less than 1.5%.

Directional tract density patterns

Image preprocessing steps for the structural and diffusion scans were conducted as described previously (Demir and Rosas, 2023). Once the diffusion images were preprocessed to model the CC using the fiber orientation distributions, the tract density maps for each CC region were reconstructed by seeding the probabilistic tractography from the parcellation of the CC at the mid-sagittal slice using the Hofer-Frahm scheme (Hofer and Frahm, 2006) as a starting point.

For each CC region (Fig. 1-A), we utilized a polar function, the directional tract density pattern (dTDP), to estimate the total density of interhemispheric fibers reaching associated cortical regions through regions of the CC (Fig. 1-B), respectively. We used the centroid (cs→) of each region of the CC at the mid-sagittal slice to determine the origin of the projection plane. The principal axes of the plane: (j→) refers to the perpendicular direction tangent to the curve of the CC and (i→) refers to the fixed axis towards the left of the brain. Fig. 1-C shows the red-framed projection planes in 3D for CC regions I, III, and V. The reconstructed 3D tract density map of each CC region, summed along the depth ±(k→=i→⊗j→) and the values were saved in 2D grid of the projection image (red-scale image on Fig. 1-D), where the image value at the center of the polar coordinate is the sum of densities along cs→±k→. Density values on the projection image were evaluated by dividing the cartesian grid into (64) angular pieces centered at the centroid of CC area. The sum of the densities along each angular piece formed a polar function, directional tract density pattern (dTDP); the distance of the curve point from the polar coordinate center provides the sum of the projected tract densities toward the sampled direction in the polar space (Fig. 1-D). The upper peak directions of the dTDP form the ”surface lobes”, which provide an estimate of the density of fibers that successfully reach corresponding cortical regions. The adjacent peak lobes to the left and right-side directions, or ”side lobes”, represent the total density of fibers as they begin to bend/cross towards the target/cortical regions. To reduce subjective variability, we manually demarcated the dTDP peak lobe directions in each individual scan and transformed each dTDP using a mathematical piece-wise affine mapping of the dTDP to a common directional space such that the lobe directions were located at the polar angles of ±5π/32 (surface) and ±10π/32 (side) respectively. In this way the dTDPs were aligned along the same principal lobe directions for all subjects. Manual landmarking of the dTDP lobes is shown in Fig. 1-D with details in A.

Statistical analyses

For statistical comparisons at each direction in polar coordinate space, we used a Student t-test (unpaired, two-tailed), and considered p < 0.05 (uncorrected) a trend of a change in the directional dTDP magnitudes. We used a Bonferroni correction for multiple (24) comparisons, setting p < 0.002 to determine statistical significance.

Results

Demographics

The study cohorts included presymptomatic (PreHD, N = 23), defined as having a diagnostic confidence level (DCL) of the Unified Huntington’s Disease Rating Scale (UHDRS) of less than 4 and a CAP score of less than 100, early symptomatic HD (N = 23), and healthy control (HC, N = 23) subjects. Since dTDP is a connectivity metric derived from diffusion MRI that is known to provide microstructural metrics that can be affected by age (Fan et al., 2019), we limited our study to including only middle-aged participants (heuristically defined as between 40–60). Discussion and conclusion

As expected, we found significant alterations in interhemispheric connectivity in our early symptomatic HD cohort, with reductions in fiber densities encompassing all regions of the CC, especially WM fibers projecting to the cortical surface. In our PreHD cohort, we found a trend for reductions (compared to the HC) in fibers of CC regions IIb, III, IV, and V, corresponding to projection fibers connecting premotor, primary motor, primary sensory, and visual cortices. CC fibers are made up from pyramidal neurons that utilize long-reaching intra-cortical projections (Innocenti, 1994); degeneration of these cells results in dysfunction of neural systems such as sensory perception, memory, and movement control. Our findings in the PreHD cohort suggest that early changes in interhemispheric connectivity could be attributable to early subtle symptoms during the motor pre-symptomatic period.

These findings are both consistent with our prior study (Rosas et al., 2010) and those of others that reported early deficits in WM structures (Zeun et al., 2022, Barrios-Martinez et al., 2022, Zhang et al., 2018), including more recent studies, such as those by Zhang et al. (2018) and Zeun et al. (2022) which used advanced approaches to characterize microstructural alterations in HD. In the case of the former, Zhang et al. (2018) demonstrated white matter alterations in PreHDs using an axonal density measure. In the case of the latter, Zeun et al. (2022) used fixel based analysis, which determines fiber density and cross-sectional area as a combined measure derived from the voxel-wise fiber density and template to subject space warp fields, and they were able to locate significant alterations associated in PreHD.

Our novel dTDP framework however, also additionally enables us to determine the high dimensional macroscopic quantification of interhemispheric WM bundles. The representation of surface and side lobes provides a unique quantification of directional alterations along the WM pathway, and most importantly at the crossing/bending regions, and this enables the reconstruction of extended WM connectivity profile. This is important because previous findings have relied on indirect implications of disconnections based on local (voxel-wise) measures of anisotropy in the mid-sagittal slice of CC. This approach is designed to be sensitive to the directional alterations along the pathway because previous approaches are rotationally invariant (Uluğ and van Zijl, 1999, Kingsley, 2006) or either discard what would be directional alterations in diffusivity measurements or assume that the accurate tractography of the bundle is maintained along the tract (Maffei et al., 2021). Although the graph-based connectivity metrics specify the directionality using defined end regions, the metric is visualized using a line between the centroids of the cortical regions and the pairwise connectivity is represented with a single quantity where a 2D connectivity matrix is formed. The dTDP approach keeps the directionality profile of the bundle in the metric representation and avoids the uncertainties of tractography (Behrens et al., 2003, Friman and Westin, 2005) at the cortical surface by using the surface lobe representation, which represents a density of fibers that could reach any part of the cortex starting from the mid-sagittal region. To our knowledge, this is the first approach to quantify directional alterations over the course of the pathway of the CC.

This study has certain limitations. The current CC parcellation included only five CC regions, but advanced and fine-tuned CC parcellations could help dTPDs to capture more precise disease-related changes. The dTDP framework is also potentially limited to the implementation of the probabilistic tractography, where different tractography approaches and the use of different constraints (Aydogan and Shi, 2021) could also lead to slight deviations in dTDPs. While we hypothesize that dTDPs might provide the necessary sensitivity to evaluate hemispheric differences, this was not addressed explicitly in this study. As the average CAP score for our PreHD group was close to 100, this suggests that our cohort was close to expected motor onset/diagnosis. Future work should include evaluating PreHD further from expected onset as well as longitudinal studies, which would provide information about trajectories of change, are also warranted.

In summary, we have shown the usefulness of our novel connectivity analysis framework to evaluate changes in the CC in HD. The reduction of dTDP measures demonstrated an increased loss of dTDP measures (HC > PreHD > HD) and suggest that dTDP measures may be responsive to progressive changes; this supports their use in future longitudinal studies. Importantly, our findings suggest that dTDPs might provide new insights into early alterations in interhemispheric connectivity in HD, as well as in other neurodegenerative disorders and may have the potential to be used for disease monitoring purposes.

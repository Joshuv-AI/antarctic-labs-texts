Significant Contributions of Interleukin-13 Genotypes to Asthma Severity
Authors: TE-CHUN SHEN, GUAN-LIANG CHEN, LI-HSIOU CHEN, YUN-CHI WANG, HOU-YU SHIH, YA-CHEN YANG, CHIA-WEN TSAI, WEN-SHIN CHANG, TE-CHUN HSIA, DA-TIAN BAU
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background/AimAsthma is a complex inflammatory airway disease influenced by genetic and environmental factors. Interleukin-13 (IL-13), particularly its promoter polymorphism rs1800925 and coding variant rs20541, have been implicated in asthma pathogenesis. This study investigated their associations with asthma susceptibility and severity in a Taiwanese population.Materials and MethodsA total of 198 adult patients with asthma and 453 controls without asthma were genotyped for IL-13 rs1800925 and rs20541 using PCR-based assays. Associations with disease risk, age, sex, and asthma severity were analyzed using logistic regression models.ResultsGenotype frequencies of IL-13 rs1800925 and rs20541 conformed to Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium in controls (p=0.1209 and 0.6860). No significant association was found between asthma risk and rs1800925 [CT versus CC: odds ratio (OR)=1.16, 95% confidence interval (CI)=0.80-1.65, p=0.4793; TT versus CC: OR=1.31, 95%CI=0.70-2.45, p=0.5043] or rs20541 (AG versus GG: OR=0.93, 95%CI=0.66-1.33, p=0.7652; AA versus GG: OR=0.84, 95%CI=0.46-1.51, p=0.6577). However, stratified analysis revealed that among individuals aged 25-40, the rs1800925 TT genotype was associated with increased asthma risk (OR=2.16, 95%CI=1.02-4.56, p=0.0637). Notably, rs1800925 CT and TT genotypes were significantly associated with severe asthma symptoms (CT versus CC: OR=2.11, p=0.0287; TT versus CC: OR=4.83, p=0.0057), with carriers of CT+TT genotypes having higher odds of severe asthma (OR=2.46, 95%CI=1.36-4.45, p=0.0041).ConclusionWhile IL-13 rs1800925 and rs20541 polymorphisms were not significantly associated with overall asthma susceptibility, the rs1800925 TT genotype may confer increased risk in younger adults and is significantly linked to more severe asthma severity. IL-13 rs1800925 may serve as a potential genetic biomarker for asthma severity prediction and management in Taiwanese populations.

Introduction

Asthma is a chronic respiratory condition that imposes significant health challenges, economic strain, and personal suffering across all age groups worldwide (1,2). This disease is marked by persistent inflammation of the airways, driven by intricate immunobiological processes (3,4). From a molecular epidemiology perspective, individual susceptibility to asthma is greatly influenced by variations in genes regulating inflammatory pathways. Key contributors include polymorphisms within the cascade nucleotide-binding and oligomerization domain-containing 2 (NOD2)-nucleotide-binding oligomerization domain-like receptor containing pyrin domain 3 (NLRP3) (5), interleukin-4 (IL-4) (6), IL-4 receptor (7), IL-6 (8), and IL-33 (9). However, the interplay between genetic predisposition and environmental exposures is essential to asthma pathogenesis. Environmental agents do not act in isolation but interact with host genetic factors, modulating immune responses and disease outcomes (10-12). Notably, factors such as obesity (13), exposure to airborne allergens (14,15), inhalation of fine particulate matter (PM2.5) (16,17), and cigarette smoke (18,19), have all been shown to either trigger immune activation or intensify existing airway hyperresponsiveness, particularly among individuals with underlying genetic susceptibility.

The IL-13 gene is situated on the long arm of human chromosome 5 at locus 5q31. IL-13 plays a critical role in orchestrating multiple aspects of the inflammatory cascade associated with allergic asthma (20). It acts on a diverse array of immune and structural cells, thereby contributing to the development and progression of allergic conditions (21). A key function of IL-13 is its regulatory role in immunoglobulin E (IgE) production, rendering it a central mediator of type 2-driven airway inflammation and mucus hypersecretion (22,23). Specifically, IL-13 stimulates IgE synthesis by activating human B lymphocytes (24). In patients with asthma, the extracellular matrix protein periostin is notably upregulated in the subepithelial bronchial regions, predominantly in response to T helper 2 (Th2) cytokines, including IL-4 and IL-13 (25). Genetic variants of IL-13 have been extensively studied in relation to asthma susceptibility. Among them, the rs1800925 polymorphism, located at the -1111 position in the promoter region, has been associated with increased transcriptional activity, resulting in elevated IL-13 cytokine expression (26). Another widely recognized variant, rs20541, resides in exon 4 and leads to a nonsynonymous amino acid substitution, replacing arginine with glutamine, which may alter protein function and enhance pro-inflammatory responses (27).

Given the pivotal role of IL-13 in regulating IgE synthesis and orchestrating immune responses, the present study aimed to investigate the association between IL-13 polymorphisms rs1800925 and rs20541 and the risk of developing asthma, as depicted in Figure 1. In addition, this study explored whether the promoter variant rs1800925 exerts a synergistic effect with demographic factors such as age and sex, and whether it is associated with asthma severity among individuals of Taiwanese descent.

Materials and Methods

Recruitment of patients with asthma and control participants. This study enrolled a total of 198 patients with clinically diagnosed asthma, recruited from China Medical University Hospital. The control group comprised 453 individuals without asthma, carefully matched to the asthma cohort by age (±5 years) and sex, in accordance with protocols established in our previous studies (28,29). To ensure diagnostic accuracy and reproducibility, each asthma case was independently assessed by two board-certified pulmonologists, under the supervision of Dr. Hsia. Asthma diagnosis and classification of disease severity were conducted based on the criteria outlined by the Global Initiative for Asthma (GINA) guidelines (30). Patients were stratified into four severity stages according to clinical symptoms, frequency of exacerbations, and treatment requirements. These classifications were systematically documented and monitored throughout the study period. Comprehensive demographic and clinical data for all participants are summarized in Table I. Due to the stringent age and sex matching strategy, no statistically significant differences were observed between cases and controls for these variables (p=0.2972 for age; p=0.9956 for sex). In contrast, pulmonary function tests demonstrated pronounced group differences: individuals with asthma showed significantly lower mean ratio of forced expiratory volume in 1 s (FEV1) to forced vital capacity (FVC) and percent of predicted FEV1 (FEV1%) values, both with p<0.0001. Among the patients with asthma, disease severity was distributed as follows: 30.3% in stage 1, 32.8% in stage 2, 17.2% in stage 3, and 19.7% in stage 4 (Table I).

DNA extraction and genotyping of IL-13 polymorphisms. Genomic DNA was extracted from the buffy coat layer of peripheral blood samples obtained from all study participants. Blood processing was completed within 24 h post-collection to ensure the preservation of nucleic acid integrity (31-33). The purified DNA samples were then stored at -80˚C for long-term conservation. To ensure experimental consistency, genomic DNA from both patients with asthma and controls was quantified, normalized, and aliquoted into working stocks, following standardized protocols established in previous asthma-related genetic studies (28,34). These working aliquots were arrayed into 96-well plates and maintained at -20˚C until genotyping was performed. Genotypic analysis of IL-13 rs1800925 and rs20541 polymorphisms was conducted using a polymerase chain reaction-restriction fragment length polymorphism (PCR-RFLP) method. Specific primers were used to amplify genomic regions encompassing the target SNPs. The resulting PCR products were then subjected to enzyme digestion with Hpy99 I (for rs1800925) and Nla IV (for rs20541) restriction endonucleases (New England Biolabs, Taipei, Taiwan, ROC). Digested fragments were resolved by 3% agarose gel electrophoresis, allowing for genotype discrimination based on distinct fragment patterns.

Statistical analysis. Comparative analysis of FEV1/FVC (%) and FEV1% between patients with asthma and controls without asthma was performed using an unpaired Student’s t-test. Genotypic distributions of the IL-13 rs1800925 and rs20541 variants across study groups were assessed using Pearson’s chi-square test with Yates’ continuity correction. To evaluate the association between IL-13 polymorphisms and asthma susceptibility, odds ratios (ORs) and corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated under multiple genetic inheritance models (e.g., codominant, dominant, and recessive). The potential relationship between these polymorphic variants and asthma severity classification was similarly examined using chi-square or Fisher’s exact tests, with OR-based comparisons applied to quantify effect sizes. All statistical analyses were conducted using a two-sided approach, with p-values <0.05 considered indicative of statistical significance.

Ethical approval and consent to participate. The study was conducted according to the guidelines of the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Institutional Review Board at China Medical University Hospital [CMUH106-REC1-004]. Informed consent was obtained from all participants involved in the study.

Results

Association between IL-13 rs1800925 and rs20541 polymorphisms with asthma risk. The genotypic distribution of IL-13 rs1800925 and rs20541 polymorphisms among 198 patients with asthma and 453 controls is presented in Table II. Genotype frequencies for both variants in the control population were consistent with HWE, with p-Values of 0.1209 for rs1800925 and 0.6860 for rs20541, indicating no significant deviations from expected proportions. Comparative analysis revealed no statistically significant differences in the distribution of rs1800925 or rs20541 genotypes between the case and control groups (p for trend=0.5789 and 0.8169, respectively). For IL-13 rs1800925, individuals carrying the CT and TT genotypes showed a non-significant trend toward increased asthma susceptibility, with ORs of 1.16 (95%CI=0.80-1.65; p=0.4793) and 1.31 (95%CI=0.70-2.45; p=0.5043), respectively, compared to the wild-type CC genotype. Under the recessive model (TT versus CC+CT), the TT genotype was associated with a slightly elevated, though non-significant, asthma risk (OR=1.17, 95%CI=0.63-2.16; p=0.6061). Similarly, the dominant model (CT+TT versus CC) yielded an OR of 1.18 (95%CI=0.84-1.66; p=0.3729), suggesting a mild, but statistically insignificant, association with asthma susceptibility. For IL-13 rs20541, carriers of the AG and AA genotypes also exhibited a non-significant trend toward altered asthma risk, with ORs of 0.93 (95%CI=0.66-1.33; p=0.7652) and 0.84 (95%CI=0.46-1.51; p=0.6577), respectively, when compared to the GG genotype. In the recessive model (AA versus GG+AG), the OR was 0.86 (95%CI=0.49-1.53; p=0.7183), while in the dominant model (AG+AA versus GG), the OR was 1.00 (95% CI=0.72-1.40; p=0.9761), further supporting the absence of a statistically significant association between rs20541 and asthma risk.

Associations between IL-13 rs1800925 and rs20541 allelic frequencies with asthma risk. To further elucidate the potential contribution of IL-13 rs1800925 and rs20541 polymorphisms to asthma susceptibility, we examined the allelic frequencies between patients with asthma and controls, as summarized in Table III. No statistically significant differences were observed in the distribution of either variant allele between the two groups (p=0.3112 and 0.5688). Specifically, the T allele of rs1800925 was associated with a modest, non-significant increase in asthma risk, conferring an OR of 1.16 (95%CI=0.89-1.52) when compared to the wild-type C allele. Conversely, the A allele of rs20541 was linked to a slight, non-significant reduction in asthma risk, with an OR of 0.92 (95%CI=0.71-1.19) relative to the G allele (Table III).

Stratified analysis of IL-13 rs1800925 genotypes in relation to age and sex. To investigate potential gene-environment interactions, we performed a stratified analysis of IL-13 rs1800925 genotypes in relation to age and sex. Participants were grouped into two age categories, 25-40 years and >40 years, with genotypic distributions summarized in Table IV. Among individuals aged 25-40, a notable enrichment of the TT genotype was observed in patients with asthma compared to controls, with an OR of 2.16 (95%CI=1.02-4.56, p=0.0637), suggesting a potential age-specific association. However, the CT genotype did not demonstrate a significant difference (OR=1.23, 95%CI=0.78-1.91, p=0.4358), and the overall trend across genotypes remained non-significant (p for trend=0.5255). No significant genotype distribution differences were found among individuals over 40 years (p for trend=0.8397; Table IV), indicating that the genetic association may be more pronounced in younger populations. Regarding sex-specific stratification, no significant associations were found between rs1800925 genotypes and asthma risk in either males (p for trend=0.8315) or females (p for trend=0.6360), as shown in Table V. These results suggest that the effect of IL-13 rs1800925 on asthma susceptibility may be age-dependent but not sex-dependent.

Stratified analysis of IL-13 rs1800925 genotypes in relation to asthma symptom severity. To evaluate whether IL-13 rs1800925 genotypes correlate with asthma symptom severity, we stratified patients into two severity groups: mild asthma (GINA stages 1 and 2) and more severe asthma (stages 3 and 4). Genotypic frequencies are presented in Table VI. A statistically significant difference in genotype distribution was observed between the two groups (p for trend=0.0031, Table VI). Specifically, individuals with heterozygous and homozygous variant genotypes were more likely to exhibit severe asthma symptoms compared to those with the wild-type CC genotype, with frequencies of 43.8% and 15.1% for IL-13 rs1800925 CT and TT genotypes, respectively, compared to 32.0% and 4.8% in the mild asthma group (OR=2.11 and 4.83, 95%CI=1.13-3.94 and 1.64-14.21, p=0.0287 and 0.0057, respectively). In the dominant model (CT+TT versus CC), individuals carrying variant alleles were significantly more likely to experience severe asthma symptoms (OR=2.46, 95%CI=1.36-4.45, p=0.0041; Table VI). Likewise, in the recessive model (TT versus CC+CT), the TT genotype was significantly overrepresented in severe cases (OR=3.52, 95%CI=1.24-9.97, p=0.0261; Table VI). These findings suggest a strong genotype-severity correlation, indicating that the IL-13 rs1800925 polymorphism may influence the clinical course of asthma in the Taiwanese population.

Discussion

In our study, the genotypic distribution of IL-13 rs1800925 in the control group conformed to HWE (p=0.1209; Table II), supporting the validity of our sample selection and genotyping methodology. A comparative review of large-scale population data from the NCBI SNP database (35) reveals that the minor T allele occurs at a frequency of 22.5% among 218,554 global individuals, including 19.6% among 6,278 Asians and 19.3% among 5,196 East Asians. In our non-asthmatic Taiwanese cohort, the T allele frequency was 24.0%, with the C allele at 76.0% (Table III), slightly higher than expected based on East Asian reference data. In contrast, the T allele appears at markedly higher frequencies than those in non-Asian populations, including 24.0% in Americans, 38.1% in 28,070 Africans, and 38.0% in 26,946 African Americans (Table VII). Similarly, the allelic distribution of IL-13 rs20541 in our non-asthmatic Taiwanese group showed a G allele frequency of 68.4% and an A allele frequency of 31.6% (Table III). These values closely mirror those reported in Asians (32.1%) and East Asians (32.0%) but are substantially higher than those observed in other populations (Table VII). These marked inter-ethnic differences in IL-13 rs1800925 and rs20541 allele frequencies underscore the potential for population-specific genetic effects in asthma susceptibility. Thus, our findings may reflect a unique ethnicity-dependent role of IL-13 promoter polymorphisms in determining asthma risk in the Taiwanese population.

Previous studies have reported associations between variant genotypes of IL-13 rs1800925 and rs20541 and asthma susceptibility (36-39). Specifically, the IL-13 rs1800925 variant has been shown to upregulate both IL-13 mRNA and protein expression, which enhances cytokine release and activates the Janus kinase/signal transducers and activators of transcription (JAK/STAT) pathway, a key mediator of asthmatic inflammatory responses (40,41). Multiple investigations across diverse ethnic populations, including Caucasians, African Americans, Arabs, East Asians, and Southeast Asians, have identified the T allele of rs1800925 as a risk factor for allergic asthma (37,40,42-45). However, a subset of studies, including ours, have found no significant predictive value of IL-13 rs1800925 genotypes for asthma susceptibility (36,40,46-50). Regarding IL-13 rs20541, several reports have demonstrated positive associations with asthma risk, differing from our findings (36,40,42,47,48,51). Nonetheless, consistent with our results, other studies have reported no significant association between rs20541 genotypes and asthma susceptibility (36,43,52-55).

A novel finding of the current study is the age-specific association between IL-13 rs1800925 variant genotypes and asthma risk. Specifically, the IL-13 rs1800925 genotypes were predictive of increased asthma risk exclusively in younger adults aged 25 to 40 years, whereas no significant association was observed in individuals older than 40 years (Table IV). It is important to note that our study cohort comprised only adult patients with asthma aged above 25 years (Table I). Previous studies have also reported significant correlations between this SNP and allergic asthma across various age groups (26,56). This age-specific pattern may reflect differences in asthma etiology, risk factors, pharmacological management, and treatment responses between pediatric and adult populations (57,58). Furthermore, older individuals are often more exposed to environmental risk factors, such as cigarette smoke and chemical pollutants, which may overshadow genetic influences and alter disease pathogenesis (59). Our findings therefore underscore the relative importance of genetic susceptibility, particularly IL-13 genotypes, in younger adults (25-40 years old) with asthma (Table IV). Regarding sex, no significant differences were observed in IL-13 rs1800925 genotype distributions between males and females in our cohort (Table V). However, given the limited sample size, the study may have been underpowered to detect subtle sex-related genetic effects. Consequently, the potential interaction between IL-13 genotypes and sex on asthma risk warrants further investigation in larger, adequately powered cohorts.

The most valuable finding of the current study is that individuals carrying the CT and TT genotypes of IL-13 rs1800925 were significantly more likely to exhibit severe asthma symptoms (Table VI). Severe asthma, which affects approximately 4% to 10% of patients, is associated with poorer clinical outcomes and necessitates complex, individualized management strategies (60,61). In contrast, no significant association was observed between IL-13 rs20541 genotypes and asthma severity (data not shown). These results suggest that IL-13 rs1800925 polymorphisms may serve as promising biomarkers for predicting asthma severity. However, further validation in larger and more ethnically diverse cohorts is warranted to confirm these findings. Moreover, refining the definitions and standardizing the assessment criteria of asthma severity will be critical, as the observed association between IL-13 rs1800925 and asthma severity may be influenced by the classification methods employed. Such improvements will facilitate a more precise and clinically meaningful understanding of genotype-phenotype relationships in asthma.

In conclusion, our study provides evidence that, although the variant G allele of the IL-13 rs1800925 polymorphism may not play a decisive role in predicting overall asthma susceptibility among Taiwanese adults, it may serve as a valuable biomarker for assessing asthma severity. Furthermore, our stratified analysis revealed that the IL-13 rs1800925 variant genotypes may be predictive of asthma occurrence specifically in younger individuals (aged 25-40 years), but not in those over 40. Further validation studies in larger, ethnically diverse cohorts are urgently needed to confirm the clinical utility of IL-13 genotypes in asthma clinical prediction and management.

Correspondence on “Deficient DNA mismatch repair and Nectin-4 expression in upper tract urothelial carcinoma (UTUC)”
Authors: Pınar Peker
Published: 2025

Dear Editor,

I read with great interest Duan and his colleagues' article titled “Deficient DNA mismatch repair and Nectin-4 expression in upper tract urothelial carcinoma (UTUC)” published in the Journal of Cancer Research and Clinical Oncology (Duan et al. 2025). The authors comprehensively examined the relationship between DNA mismatch repair deficiency (dMMR) and Nectin-4 expression in a large cohort of 339 cases and found dMMR in 7.4% of patients. This study suggests that the significantly higher expression of Nectin-4 in the dMMR group (p = 0.044) may serve as a potential prognostic and predictive biomarker combination for upper urinary tract urothelial carcinoma (UTUC). The finding that MSH6 loss is particularly associated with high levels of Nectin-4 suggests a biological interaction between disruption of DNA repair mechanisms and the regulation of cell adhesion molecules.

dMMR and microsatellite instability (MSI) phenotypes are powerful biomarkers predicting response to immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) in various solid tumors (Marcus et al. 2019; Dudley et al. 2016). An increased neoantigen load in MSI-H or dMMR tumors enhances sensitivity to PD-1/PD-L1 blockade, resulting in superior response rates with agents such as pembrolizumab and nivolumab (Wilbur et al. 2024; Naboush et al. 2017). The frequency of MSI-H in urothelial carcinomas has varied between 1.6 and 28.1% in previous studies (Schneider et al. 2020; Shang et al. 2022), and these differences may be attributed to variations in patient populations and testing methods. The analysis conducted by Duan et al. using full tissue sections is one of the largest East Asian cohorts published to date and reliably demonstrates the frequency of dMMR and the distribution of Nectin-4 expression from a clinical perspective.

Nectin-4 is the target of enfortumab vedotin (EV), an antibody–drug conjugate (ADC), and triggers apoptosis in Nectin-4-positive tumor cells through microtubule disruption (Challita-Eid et al. 2016). In the Phase I EV-101 and Phase III EV-301 studies, EV provided a significant survival advantage compared with chemotherapy in patients with metastatic urothelial carcinoma (Rosenberg et al. 2020; Powles et al. 2024). In the recently published EV-302/KEYNOTE-A39 study, the combination of EV and pembrolizumab demonstrated a significant overall survival and response rate advantage over chemotherapy in platinum-ineligible metastatic patients (Grivas et al. 2023). Therefore, the “dMMR and high Nectin-4” biomarker profile identified by Duan et al. could define the patient group expected to benefit most from the EV/ICI combination. This finding of the study is clinically valuable for personalized treatment approaches.

However, some aspects of the study are open to future development. The retrospective design and single-center structure have limited the evaluation of survival (PFS, OS) data. Prospective and multicenter validation studies could strengthen the clinical significance of the dMMR and Nectin-4 association. Additionally, the inclusion of immune markers such as PD-L1 expression or tumor mutation burden (TMB) would more comprehensively reflect the impact of dMMR on the tumor microenvironment (Hui et al. 2023). While the H-score classification used for Nectin-4 is methodologically consistent, it would be appropriate to prospectively evaluate antibody-induced variations and inter-observer differences (Tekin et al. 2023).

In conclusion, the study by Duan and colleagues is a pioneering contribution that highlights the biological intersection between DNA repair deficiency and cell adhesion molecule expression in UTUC. The findings support the development of novel biomarker combinations for both immunotherapies and ADC-based treatments. The coexistence of dMMR and high Nectin-4 expression, particularly in patients unsuitable for cisplatin or resistant to therapy, may serve as a promising guide for personalized treatment strategies.

Sincerely,

Pınar Peker

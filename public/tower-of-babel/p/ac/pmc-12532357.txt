Integrative transcriptomic and genomic insights into diabetic kidney disease: evidence from multi-omics analysis and experimental validation
Authors: Shengnan Chen, Lei Chen, Ruiqing Dong, Xuna Kou, Chenwen Luo, Ning Gao, Meng Zhao, Mingqian He, Bingyin Shi, Hongli Jiang, Wei Qiang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractDiabetic kidney disease (DKD) remains a critical challenge in diabetes management, necessitating a deep understanding of its molecular underpinnings for better diagnosis and treatment strategies. This study was conducted to identify and validate novel biomarkers for DKD by integrating multi-omics analysis and experimental validation. Through weighted gene co-expression network analysis and Mendelian randomization analysis, 11 genes were identified as being causally associated with DKD. ADARB2, GOLPH3L, LRG1, and PEX6 were identified as characteristic genes through machine learning methods, including least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression and SVM algorithms. Receiver operating characteristic curve analysis demonstrated that the characteristic genes had high predictive accuracy for DKD. Functional enrichment analyses indicated that dysregulation of key genes was associated with inflammatory and immune responses in both peripheral blood mononuclear cells and kidney single-cell populations. Peripheral blood samples from DKD patients and healthy controls were collected to assess the reliability of identified key genes in humans. Kidneys from wild-type and db/db mice were harvested to further validate the reliability of key genes at the tissue level in animal models using reverse transcription-quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR). After RT-qPCR validation, the robustness of LRG1 and PEX6 was confirmed in both human peripheral blood and mouse kidney tissues. The significance of ADARB2 was confirmed in the kidney tissue of DKD mouse models. This study highlights the power of multi-omics analyses in elucidating complex disease pathogenesis and identifying biomarkers, thereby laying a foundation for the development of DKD-targeted therapeutics.

Background

Diabetes mellitus (DM) is a chronic metabolic disorder characterized by persistent hyperglycemia resulting from impaired insulin secretion or decreased insulin sensitivity [1]. It has been reported that up to half of patients with type 2 diabetes and approximately one-third of individuals with type 1 diabetes are at risk of developing chronic kidney disease (CKD) [2]. The pathophysiological role of diabetes in the progression to CKD and end-stage renal disease (ESRD) positions it as a central contributor to the increasing global burden of kidney-related diseases [3,4]. Our previous research revealed that individuals with diabetic kidney disease (DKD) exhibit worse renal outcomes than those with kidney disease not caused by diabetes [5]. Consequently, a more thorough investigation into the molecular basis of DKD is essential.

Transcriptional profiling via next-generation sequencing has become an essential approach for investigating gene expression landscapes [6]. Single-cell RNA sequencing offers a powerful platform to investigate molecular mechanisms by capturing transcriptomic heterogeneity at the single-cell level [7,8]. This approach enables interrogation of the molecular mechanisms driving disease progression at single-cell resolution, holding great promise for the identification of novel therapeutic targets. The increasing scale and inherent complexity of these datasets have driven the widespread adoption of machine learning techniques [9], which are now widely used to identify potential molecular markers of diseases.

Genome-wide association studies (GWASs) have emerged as a pivotal approach for elucidating the genetic underpinnings of multifactorial traits [10]. Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis utilizes genetic variants as proxies to infer causal links between exposures and traits [11]. The random allocation of genetic variants in MR reduces susceptibility to confounding and reverse causation, providing advantages over observational studies [12]. Based on this rationale, MR has become an important analytical approach for establishing causal links between genetic variants and diseases.

In addition to genetic factors, gene expression is regulated at transcriptional, epigenetic, and post-transcriptional levels. Transcription factors (TFs) are DNA-binding proteins that modulate gene expression by either promoting or inhibiting transcription [13]. MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are endogenous non-coding RNAs that regulate gene expression post-transcriptionally [14,15]. In addition, chemical compounds have been shown to modulate gene expression through various mechanisms [16]. Therefore, investigating gene regulatory networks serves as a fundamental approach to elucidate gene function [17].

In this study, we aim to explore novel biomarkers and underlying mechanisms associated with DKD by integrating bulk and single-cell transcriptome analyses, machine learning, MR analysis, functional enrichment analysis, and gene regulatory network analysis. Furthermore, we will validate the robustness of our findings through molecular biology experiments in both human and animal models. Findings from this study are expected to facilitate the development of precision therapies aimed at effectively slowing DKD progression, thereby improving clinical outcomes in patients with DKD.

Methods

Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA)

WGCNA is a network-based analytical approach that constructs co-expression modules and identifies key modules associated with specific phenotypes [18]. The gene expression dataset GSE142153 (annotation platform: GPL6480), which contains transcriptional profiles of peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) from 10 healthy individuals and 23 DKD patients, was retrieved from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) repository. Detailed preprocessing methods for GEO data are available in our previously published studies [19,20]. Genes exhibiting the top 5,000 variances in expression across samples from GSE142153 were selected for co-expression analysis using the R package WGCNA. An appropriate soft-thresholding power was determined by evaluating the scale-free topology fit and overall network connectivity. The weighted network was first transformed into a topological overlap matrix to evaluate gene connectivity, and hierarchical clustering was then performed to generate a dendrogram for module detection. Gene Ontology-based functional enrichment analysis of key module genes was conducted using Metascape. Terms with p ≤ .01 and gene overlap ≥3 were considered significant.

MR analysis

To evaluate the causal link between expression levels of key genes and DKD, MR analysis was performed using the MendelianRandomization R package. The cis-expression quantitative trait loci (eQTL) data used in the current study were obtained from the eQTLGen consortium database. Genetic variants for DKD (finn-b-DM_NEPHROPATHY) were obtained from the IEU OpenGWAS database [21]. The dataset comprised 3,283 DKD cases and 210,463 controls. Single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) demonstrating significant association with the exposure (p < 5 × 10−8) and meeting independence criteria (R2 < 0.001, clump distance > 10,000 kb) were chosen as instrumental variables for MR analysis. To assess causality between the exposure and outcome, we applied several MR methods such as inverse variance weighted (IVW), MR Egger, weighted median, and weighted mode. Heterogeneity and sensitivity analyses were conducted to validate the robustness of the MR results [22].

Machine learning

Characteristic genes associated with DKD were further identified by applying least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression and support vector machine (SVM) algorithms [23,24]. The glmnet R package was utilized to perform LASSO regression, while the e1071 package was employed to construct the SVM model for identifying characteristic genes. Machine learning analyses were conducted on the training set derived from GSE142153. Genes commonly selected by both LASSO regression and the SVM algorithm were defined as characteristic genes for subsequent analyses.

Predictive value of key genes in DKD

The Circlize package was used to analyze correlations among key genes, while receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were generated using the pROC package to evaluate the diagnostic value of these genes for DKD.

Functional enrichment analyses

Gene set variation analysis (GSVA) and gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) were performed to conduct functional analyses. Gene sets were obtained from the Molecular signatures database (MSigDB) v7.0 [25]. GSVA was performed on the Hallmark and KEGG annotated gene sets from MSigDB to generate sample–pathway matrices. Differential analysis of these matrices was conducted using the limma package, and results were visualized with bar plots. For GSEA, predefined Hallmark gene sets were used to rank genes according to their differential expression between two sample groups. Subsequently, a permutation test with 1,000 iterations was performed to assess whether predefined gene sets were significantly enriched at the extremes of the ranked gene list, with permutations conducted based on phenotype labels.

Immune cell infiltration analysis

Based on linear support vector regression, CIBERSORT is a widely used method for deconvoluting expression matrices to estimate immune cell subtype compositions [26]. The CIBERSORT signature matrix comprises 547 biomarker genes that characterize 22 different human immune cell subsets [27]. In this study, the CIBERSORT algorithm was applied to estimate the relative fractions of infiltrating immune cells. Correlation analyses examining associations among genes, infiltrating immune cells, and immune factors were conducted through the TISIDB platform.

Identification of therapeutic targets associated with DKD

The GeneCards database was used to obtain therapeutic targets associated with DKD. Correlation analyses were performed between key genes and the expression profiles of the 20 highest-ranked therapeutic targets based on relevance score.

Single-cell analysis

Single-cell transcriptomic data from GSE131882, comprising three DKD samples and three controls [28], were used for single-cell analysis. Cell type clustering was performed using uniform manifold approximation and projection (UMAP) and t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding (t-SNE) algorithms. Subsequently, cell type annotation was performed using the Celldex R package based on established marker genes. Finally, the AUCell package was applied to assess associations between key genes and pathways within single-cell expression profiles.

Construction of the regulatory network of key genes

miRNAs targeting the key genes were predicted using the TargetScan database. Transcription factors were identified via the motif-based RcisTarget package, employing the RcisTarget.hg19.motifDBs.cisbpOnly.500bp dataset for gene–motif ranking. The Comparative Toxicogenomics Database was used to obtain associations between key genes and chemicals [29]. The miRNAs–gene network, TFs–gene network, and chemicals–gene interactions were constructed and displayed with Cytoscape software (v 3.6.0).

Collection of clinical samples and establishment of animal models

Peripheral blood samples from the normal control (NC) group were collected from healthy individuals at the Health Examination Center, while samples from DKD patients were obtained from the Department of Endocrinology and Metabolism of our hospital. Patients with acute cerebrovascular diseases, infectious diseases, malignancy, thyroid dysfunction, or liver dysfunction were excluded. Early-stage DKD patients classified with estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) stages G1–G2, as recommended by the National Technical Guidelines [30], were included in this study. The eGFR was determined using the CKD-EPI formula [31]. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Xi’an Jiaotong University, with informed consent obtained from all subjects (Approval No. XJTU1AF2023LSK-458).

Eight-week-old male C57 Black 6 Jackson Laboratory (C57BL/6J) wild-type (WT) mice and diabetic C57BLKS/J-LepR (db/db) mice were purchased from GemPharmatech Co., Ltd. (Nanjing, China). The mice were housed under standard conditions with a temperature of 23 ± 2 °C, humidity of 55 ± 5%, and a 12-h light/dark cycle. After a 2-week adaptation period, blood glucose and urinary protein levels were measured. Mice with blood glucose levels >16.7 mmol/L and positive urinary protein were considered successfully modeled for DKD. Kidney specimens were collected after a three-month disease duration for RNA extraction and reverse transcription-quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR). The study was conducted at the Animal Experimental Center of Xi’an Jiaotong University and approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (Approval No. XJTUAE2024-1933).

RT-qPCR

An appropriate volume of peripheral blood and tissue samples was homogenized with 500 µL of RNAiso Plus. The mixture was incubated at room temperature for 5 min, followed by centrifugation at 12,000 × g for 5 min at 4 °C. The supernatant was transferred to a new 1.5 mL tube, followed by the addition of 100 µL of chloroform. The tube was shaken thoroughly to mix, then incubated at room temperature for 5 min, followed by centrifugation at 12,000 × g for 15 min at 4 °C. The supernatant was subsequently transferred to a fresh centrifuge tube. An equal volume of isopropanol was added to the supernatant, followed by incubation at room temperature for 10 min and centrifugation at 12,000 × g for 10 min at 4 °C. Following a wash with 500 µL of 75% ethanol, the pellet was centrifuged at 7,500 × g for 5 min at 4 °C. The supernatant was discarded, and the pellet was retained for subsequent use. After drying, 20 µL of nuclease-free water was added to the pellet, which was mixed thoroughly and stored overnight at 4 °C. The procedures for RNA quantification, reverse transcription, and PCR amplification were described in detail in our previously published articles [20,32]. RNA primer sequences are listed in Supplementary Table 1.

Statistical analyses

Data analysis was carried out with R software (v 4.3.0) (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Statistical significance was defined as a two-sided p value <.05.

Results

Identification of module genes by WGCNA

The WGCNA network derived from the GSE142153 dataset was used to explore regulatory networks related to DKD. At a soft-thresholding power of 4, the scale-free topology model fit approached 1 and exhibited relatively high average connectivity, indicating that the network satisfies the scale-free topology criterion (Figure 2(A)). Therefore, a soft-thresholding power of 4 was selected for module detection. Ten gene modules were identified (Figure 2(B)), and their relationships with sample traits were evaluated using a module–trait heatmap. Among these, the turquoise module, comprising 1,166 genes, exhibited the strongest correlation with the disease phenotype (Figure 2(C)). Accordingly, the 1,166 genes in the turquoise module were subjected to GO enrichment analysis via the Metascape database. Significant enrichment was observed in pathways related to leukocyte activation, immune effector process, cellular secretion, and tumor necrosis factor (TNF) signaling (Figure 2(D)).

Key modules and pathways related to DKD revealed by WGCNA analysis (A). Scale independence and mean connectivity curves (B). Cluster dendrogram of the weighted gene co-expression network (C). Heatmap of module-trait correlations (D). GO enrichment analyses.

Identification of genes causally associated with DKD at the genomic level

MR analysis was performed to investigate potential causal links between module genes and DKD at the genomic level. Genes from the turquoise module were used as the exposure, and DKD was treated as the outcome (Supplementary Table 2). The eQTLs of 11 genes, including ADARB2, APOBEC3B, CDCA7, CYP4F22, DTHD1, GBP3, GOLPH3L, IER3, LRG1, PEX6, and SLC24A4, showed causal relationships with DKD (Figure 3 and Supplementary Figure 1). No evidence of heterogeneity or horizontal pleiotropy was detected (Supplementary Table 3). Leave-one-out analysis confirmed the robustness of the results (Supplementary Figure 2).

Identification of characteristic genes by machine learning

To identify characteristic genes for DKD, two machine learning approaches were employed. LASSO regression identified five genes (Figure 4(A,B)), and the SVM algorithm screened five genes (Figure 4(C)). The intersection of both methods revealed four shared characteristic genes: ADARB2, GOLPH3L, LRG1, and PEX6 (Figure 4(D)).

Predictive value of key genes for DKD

The expression levels of key genes in transcriptome data are presented in Supplementary Figure 3. These genes exhibited strong intercorrelation in expression levels, and ROC analysis demonstrated their robust predictive performance for DKD (Supplementary Figure 4).

Functional enrichment analyses

GSVA results indicated that the dysregulated expression of ADARB2, GOLPH3L, LRG1, and PEX6 was associated with inflammatory and immune responses, including interleukin (IL)6/Janus kinase (JAK)/signal transducer and activator of transcription (STAT)3 signaling, oxidative phosphorylation, allograft rejection, TNF-α signaling, IL2/STAT5 signaling, and interferon (INF)-γ response (Supplementary Figure 5). Consistently, GSEA further demonstrated that these key genes mediated the activation of inflammatory responses in DKD (Supplementary Figure 6).

Immune cell infiltration analysis

Since both GSVA and GSEA analyses revealed significant enrichment of key genes in immune and inflammatory responses, we further assessed correlations between key genes and immune cell infiltration levels. We found that dendritic cells, eosinophils, and NK cells were significantly increased, while gamma delta T cells were significantly decreased in DKD patients (Supplementary Figure 7). Additionally, LRG1 and GOLPH3L were significantly associated with infiltrating immune cells and immune factors (Supplementary Figure 8). The composition of immune cell subsets for each patient is presented in Supplementary Figure 9.

Identification of therapeutic targets associated with DKD

Therapeutic targets related to DKD were collected from the GeneCards database. LRG1 was identified as a therapeutic target for DKD. Additionally, correlations were observed between key genes and the top 20 therapeutic targets for DKD (Supplementary Figure 10).

Single-cell analysis

To investigate gene function at the single-cell level, we performed single-cell analysis. After excluding lowly expressed genes, a total of 32,414 genes were retained (Supplementary Figure 11). The data were standardized and normalized, followed by principal component analysis (PCA) and harmony processing. Cells were subsequently grouped into 15 clusters using UMAP and t-SNE analysis at a resolution of 0.2 (Supplementary Figure 12). Twelve distinct cell types were identified and annotated based on well-established cell-type-specific markers (Supplementary Figure 13). Markers distinguishing each cell population are shown in Supplementary Figure 14, with Figure 5 highlighting the differential expression of key genes between control and DKD groups. Pathway analysis further revealed significant associations of key genes with immune and inflammatory pathways, including allograft rejection, complement, IL6/JAK/STAT3 signaling, inflammatory response, INF-α, INF-γ, IL2/STAT 5 signaling, and reactive oxygen species (ROS) pathway (Supplementary Figure 15).

Regulatory network of key genes

Since LRG1 is a known therapeutic target for DKD, and the expression levels of ADARB2, GOLPH3L, and PEX6 significantly correlate with established DKD targets, these genes may also serve as potential therapeutic targets. To uncover the regulatory mechanisms underlying these key genes, we identified miRNAs, TFs, and chemical substances associated with DKD. A total of 55 miRNAs (Figure 6(A)), five TFs (Figure 6(B)), and 194 chemical substances (Figure 6(C)) related to the key genes were identified.

Experimental validation

To validate these findings, RT-qPCR was performed to measure the expression levels of the characteristic genes in human peripheral blood and mouse kidney samples. The mean age and standard deviation (x¯ ± s) of the NC and DKD groups were 31.35 ± 6.01 and 51.00 ± 9.64 years, respectively. Detailed age and sex distributions for both groups are provided in Supplementary Figure 16. We observed that LRG1 expression was significantly elevated, whereas PEX6 levels were markedly reduced in the peripheral blood of DKD patients compared to healthy controls (Figure 7). Consistent with these findings, LRG1 was upregulated and PEX6 was downregulated in the kidneys of DKD mice compared to WT controls (Figure 8). Additionally, a significant decrease in ADARB2 expression was detected in the kidney tissues of DKD mice.

Discussion

In the present study, we discovered that ADARB2, GOLPH3L, LRG1, and PEX6 could potentially serve as valuable biomarkers for DKD by integrating functional enrichment analyses, MR analysis, machine learning algorithms, and immune infiltration analysis using GWAS data along with large-scale bulk and single-cell transcriptome data. The innovative integration of these diverse analytical methods sets our study apart and provides a novel perspective on DKD pathogenesis. Importantly, we validated the reliability and robustness of the multi-omics integrative analysis through experiments conducted on both human samples and animal models. Functional enrichment analyses revealed that the immune and inflammatory responses play a significant role in the biological functions of these key genes.

Anders et al. pointed out that sterile inflammation occurs early in the progression of DKD [33]. Pichler et al. also indicated that although DKD is not primarily an immune-mediated kidney disease, immune and inflammatory mechanisms play a significant role in its pathogenesis [34]. Moreover, the release of proinflammatory cytokines and chemokines, along with the infiltration of immune cells, contributes to the onset and progression of DKD [35,36]. These findings align with our previous study, which demonstrated the involvement of immune-related genes in DKD [20]. In the present study, we further observed that key genes were significantly enriched in immune and inflammatory pathways at both the PBMCs bulk transcriptome level and the kidney single-cell level.

Among all the genes, LRG1 and PEX6 emerged as key genes with consistent alterations observed in both human peripheral blood and mouse kidney tissues. LRG1 expression was markedly increased in the peripheral blood of DKD patients as well as in the kidneys of DKD mice. ROC curve analysis demonstrated that LRG1 possesses strong predictive capability for DKD, with an AUC of 0.778. As a secreted protein, LRG1 belongs to the leucine-rich repeat family [37]. Previous research has shown that LRG1 is primarily expressed in hepatocytes and neutrophils, with comparatively lower expression in the lungs, kidneys, and heart [37,38]. In our PCR experiments, we also observed significantly lower LRG1 expression in the control group. Although the threshold cycle values for LRG1 in the DKD group were relatively high, they required significantly fewer amplification cycles than those in the control group. Prior studies have reported increased plasma LRG1 levels in individuals with T2DM, which are positively associated with DKD progression [39–41]. This observation is further supported by the MR analysis in our present study. Additionally, increased urinary LRG1 levels have been detected in mice with renal injury [42]. Elevated LRG1 expression has also been detected in the glomerular and tubulointerstitial compartments of kidneys from both DKD mice and human patients [39]. The majority of studies indicate that the biological activity of LRG1 is largely mediated through modulation of the TGF-β signaling pathway [37,40,43,44]. Our study showed that elevated LRG1 expression strongly correlates with the enrichment of TNF-α, TGF-β, angiogenesis, and IL2/STAT5 signaling pathway. It has been demonstrated that LRG1 may act as a regulator of pathogenic neovascularization by redirecting endothelial TGF-β signaling toward a proliferative pathway [45]. Consequently, LRG1 is involved in vasculopathy in various pathological contexts, including DKD and diabetic retinopathy [37]. Furthermore, a relationship between LRG1 expression and reduced cognitive performance has been observed in T2DM patients [46]. The essential role of LRG1 in the pathogenesis of diabetic complications underscores its therapeutic relevance and highlights the need for further investigation into its potential as a treatment target. Fortunately, the efficacy of LRG1 inhibition has been validated in animal models, where it significantly attenuated diabetes-induced renal abnormalities in mice, including altered glomerular angiogenesis, podocyte depletion, and glomerulopathy [39]. Moreover, LRG1 deletion in mice does not result in significant physiological impairments [44,45]. Based on our PCR results, we infer that LRG1 is likely specifically upregulated under DKD conditions. Therefore, LRG1 represents a promising therapeutic target for diabetes and its complications.

Contrary to LRG1, PEX6 expression is markedly decreased in both the blood of DKD patients and the kidneys of DKD mice. PEX6 belongs to the ATPases associated with diverse cellular activities superfamily [47], and is a key protein that mediates the transport of newly synthesized peroxisomal membrane proteins from the cytoplasm to the peroxisomal membrane by providing the translocation channel and energy [48]. Consequently, defects in PEX6 function can lead to a range of peroxisome biogenesis disorders, including cystic fibrosis, combined hearing loss, and retinopathy [49–52]. Despite its known role in these disorders, the association between PEX6 and DKD is not well established. To date, only one study has reported a link between PEX6 and the risk of ESRD in T2DM patients [53]. We report, for the first time, a negative correlation between PEX6 and DKD risk at the genetic level. Enrichment analysis revealed that PEX6 downregulation significantly enriched pathways such as angiogenesis, TNF-α signaling, IL2/STAT5 signaling, and fatty acid metabolism. Furthermore, PEX6 levels exhibited a negative correlation with chemokines and immunostimulators. While PEX6 did not exhibit a significant correlation with immune cell infiltration, ADARB2 was found to be associated with the peroxisome–lipid metabolism pathway. We observed marked downregulation of ADARB2 in the renal tissues of DKD mice. Peroxisomes are key organelles responsible for fatty acid β-oxidation and are also involved in the metabolic processing of cholesterol, amines, bile acids, and purines [54]. Lipid metabolites from peroxisomes, including polyunsaturated fatty acids, serve as precursors for immune mediators. As such, peroxisomes are considered key regulators of immune cell development and function, particularly through their role in modulating phagocytosis and the secretion of inflammatory cytokines [55]. Additionally, peroxisomes are important cellular organelles that both produce and detoxify ROS [56]. Peroxisomes contain high levels of antioxidative enzymes, including catalase and superoxide dismutase, which are crucial for scavenging ROS and maintaining cellular redox balance [57]. Consequently, impaired peroxisomal function may result in ROS accumulation, which in turn induces oxidative stress and initiates inflammatory cascades. Our findings indicate that peroxisomal dysfunction is involved in the development of DKD, with PEX6 potentially playing a key role in this pathological process. Meanwhile, our study provides preliminary evidence implicating ADARB2 and immune crosstalk in the progression of DKD. Targeting PEX6 and ADARB2 to restore peroxisome function may represent a promising therapeutic strategy to ameliorate DKD.

MR analyses demonstrate a genetic causal link between key genes and DKD, while TFs and miRNAs modulate their expression through transcriptional and post-transcriptional mechanisms. Meanwhile, exogenous materials including drugs (such as ethinyl estradiol and quercetin), environmental pollutants (such as bisphenol F, air pollution, vehicle emissions, and pesticide contamination), heavy metals (nickel), and food contaminants (aflatoxin B1) can also affect gene expression. These highlighted environmental factors, alongside genetic predispositions, play a significant role in DKD pathogenesis. A growing body of evidence associates ambient air pollution exposure with increased susceptibility to T2DM and metabolic-related kidney diseases [58,59]. The Global Burden of Disease 2019 Diabetes and Air Pollution Collaborators further demonstrated that around 20% of the worldwide T2DM burden is attributable to PM2.5 exposure [60]. Another study showed that exposure to metalworking fluids is related to the risk of ESRD [61]. Moreover, studies indicate that individuals exhibiting elevated serum bisphenol A levels face an approximately sevenfold increased risk of CKD relative to those with lower concentrations [62]. Our research provides clues to the potential genes through which these environmental pollutants may influence DKD. This indicates that DKD is partially preventable by avoiding exposure to environmental pollutants and offers new perspectives for its prevention.

In conclusion, we identified gene modules predictive of DKD using WGCNA and determined key genes causally related to DKD through MR analysis and machine learning. ROC curve analysis demonstrated that these genes possess strong predictive power for DKD. Subsequent enrichment analyses further revealed significant associations between these key genes and immune-inflammatory response pathways at both the PBMC bulk transcriptome level and the kidney single-cell level. Finally, we validated the efficacy of the multi-omics integrative approach through experimental studies in human subjects and animal models. These findings provide a solid foundation for the application of multi-omics methodologies in identifying novel biomarkers for DKD and support the development of innovative therapeutic strategies.

This study has several advantages. The innovative aspect of this study lies in the dual validation of multi-omics analysis and molecular biology experiments. The multi-omics analysis, incorporating bulk and single-cell transcriptomics alongside advanced analytical techniques such as WGCNA, machine learning, MR analysis, and immune infiltration analysis, enhanced the reliability of the results. Meanwhile, MR analysis directly demonstrated the causal relationship between key genes and DKD, eliminating confounding factors. Of paramount importance, DKD patients and animal models were utilized to validate the reliability of the identified genes.

This study also has several limitations. We preliminarily identified key genes and associated molecules that may influence their expression. However, translating these initial findings into clinical applications is a long and complex process that requires rigorous and systematic experimental validation. Meanwhile, given the high-throughput nature of our analysis, multiple testing correction was not applied in the MR analysis to preserve sensitivity. This may have increased the risk of false positives. Consequently, additional investigations involving larger cohorts are necessary to confirm the roles of these key genes. Furthermore, molecular biology experiments are required to elucidate the regulatory mechanisms underlying these genes.

Conclusions

By integrating transcriptomic data analysis, MR analysis, and machine learning algorithms, we identified ADARB2, GOLPH3L, LRG1, and PEX6 as potential novel biomarkers that are causally associated with DKD. Genetic susceptibility, combined with environmental factors, contributes to DKD progression by modulating the expression or activity of these biomarkers. RT-qPCR confirmed the robustness of LRG1 and PEX6 in both human peripheral blood and mouse kidney tissues. The significance of ADARB2 was verified in kidney tissue from DKD mouse models. Targeting ADARB2, LRG1, and PEX6 to regulate inflammatory mediators could serve as an effective intervention for DKD. These findings lay a foundation for applying multi-omics approaches to identify novel DKD biomarkers and develop innovative treatment strategies.

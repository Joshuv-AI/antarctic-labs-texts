PatchGAN-guided semi-supervised U-Net for carotid plaque segmentation in ultrasound under limited annotation conditions
Authors: Saritha Lalitha Ravindran, Dipti Jadhav, Sandeep Sangle, Pankaj Kumar Jain, Andrew Nicolaides, Luca Saba, Ajith Abraham, Jasjit S. Suri
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveArtificial intelligence models are increasingly used for stroke risk evaluation and clinical decision-making. However, the scarcity of expert masks, low contrast, and noise in ultrasound images affect segmentation performance.MethodsOur study innovatively integrated an adversarial PatchGAN discriminator into a batch-normalized semi-supervised U-Net generator to enhance carotid plaque segmentation in ultrasound images with limited expert annotation. The discriminator provides patch-level localized feedback to enhance boundary delineation and structural consistency of the predicted masks. The model was trained using a hybrid loss function and self-training strategy. The performance of our framework was compared with a fine-tuned semi-supervised U-Net model. Both frameworks were trained under identical experimental settings using 30% of the labeled data; the remaining unlabeled images were utilized to generate pseudo-labels for data augmentation. A confidence threshold of 0.7 was applied to filter unreliable predictions.ResultsWe conducted experiments on a dataset of 970 internal carotid artery ultrasound images from the Imperial College of London, UK. The experimental results showed that the proposed framework achieved a Dice coefficient of 86.12 and a Jaccard index of 75.66, outperforming the baseline U-Net (84.22 and 73.12, respectively).ConclusionsThe integration of patch-level adversarial feedback into a semi-supervised framework enhanced the segmentation accuracy and improved the reliability of pseudo-labels under limited supervision. The results were comparable to those of state-of-the-art deep learning models, which require a large pool of labeled data.

Introduction

The World Health Organization (WHO) reports that stroke is the second leading cause of death, accounting for approximately 10% of all deaths worldwide [1]. Carotid arteries supply oxygenated blood from the heart to the brain. Stroke occurs when blood flow to brain cells is obstructed by atherosclerotic plaque formation or vessel rupture, causing sudden ischemic injury and even death within minutes [2]. Stroke can cause severe speech impairment, restricted body movements, reduced cognition, and even death [3]. Therefore, prevention and risk stratification of stroke require early detection and accurate screening of carotid plaque [4]. Figure 1 shows a visual comparison of a normal carotid artery and an artery with a plaque. The plaque acts as a block that obstructs blood flow through the arteries [5].

Ultrasound (US) imaging is a highly preferred modality for carotid plaque segmentation owing to its non-invasive nature, cost-effectiveness, and real-time imaging availability [6]. Although ultrasound images provide valuable anatomical and textural information about plaque morphology, manual plaque segmentation is time-consuming, subject to observer variability, and heavily relies on the sonographer’s experience [7]. In recent years, artificial intelligence (AI) and deep learning (DL) techniques have made significant progress in automating carotid plaque segmentation and classification, thereby improving diagnostic accuracy and reproducibility [8–10]. U-Net and its variants have been used for supervised carotid plaque segmentation [11–15]. But their reliance on large annotated datasets led to the adoption of semi-supervised learning (SSL) frameworks [16–20]. These existing semi-supervised segmentation methods still face challenges due to pseudo-label noise, boundary refinement issues, and limited use of adversarial learning in carotid ultrasound imaging. To address these limitations, our study proposes an adversarial PatchGAN-guided SSL U-Net. Our unique design approaches include:

Related works

Medical image segmentation plays a crucial role in disease diagnosis in cerebrovascular analysis [21]. Machine learning and deep learning approaches have significantly improved disease diagnosis and risk stratification [22–25]. Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) significantly improve accuracy and robustness in medical image segmentation due to their hierarchical spatial representations [26, 27]. The U-Net architecture introduced by Ronneberger et al. employs an encoder-decoder structure with skip connections to enable effective feature extraction and spatial localization [11]. Its feature-extraction capability makes the U-Net a foundational model for high-risk carotid plaque detection [28, 29]. The performance of many U-Net variants was evaluated in a study, including solo and hybrid models [13]. Several architectures on residual connections, dense skip pathways, and attention mechanisms have been studied for accurate medical image segmentation [30–33]. Biswas et al. proposed the Symtosis framework, which optimized a deep learning architecture for fatty liver tissue characterization and risk stratification [34]. Jain et al. introduced a faster R-CNN–based pipeline for plaque segmentation and generalization [35]. A densely connected skip layer in UNet++ improved segmentation consistency and reduced erroneous boundaries in carotid ultrasound images [36]. Bilinear CNNs, residual learning, and hybrid deep learning architectures have been designed for carotid plaque detection and risk assessment [37, 38].

Despite these advancements, reliance on large expert-annotated data limits supervised deep learning segmentation frameworks [39, 40]. Manual labeling of medical images is time-consuming, costly, and requires domain expertise. Training of models on a small dataset leads to overfitting and reduces generalizability [41]. Semi-supervised learning (SSL) has emerged as an alternative to address these limitations, utilizing both labeled and unlabeled data during training [42]. Zhang et al. designed a pretrained SSL network to generate pseudo-labels for unlabeled samples [43]. They applied the supervised parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) strategy and retrained the model using the generated pseudo-labeled data. They ensembled multiple PEFTs with vision foundation models (VFM) to minimize the effect of noisy pseudo-labels. Engelen and Hoos presented a survey of semi-supervised methodologies, categorizing them into wrapper-based, graph-based, and consistency-regularization approaches [44]. A student–teacher ResNet–UNet enhanced with the Mean Teacher algorithm and self-attention mechanisms improved segmentation under limited supervision [45]. Gan et al. introduced a semi-supervised multi-task learning network (SML-Net) to improve segmentation [46]. However, the sensitivity of SSL models to noisy pseudo-labels can affect the segmentation quality.

Introduction of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) improved structural consistency and boundary refinement in [47, 48]. The adversarial feedback mechanism in GAN helps to eliminate the boundary delineation of low-contrast and ambiguous edges in ultrasound image segmentation [49, 50]. Saboori et al. designed a hybrid AnoGAN for anomaly detection and segmentation in carotid ultrasound images [51]. A modified U-Net3+ architecture with GAN-based image synthesis achieved high plaque area detection and boundary precision [52, 53].

Despite these advancements, heterogeneous datasets, annotation protocols, and pseudo-label noise in self-training U-Nets remained challenging. Patch-level generative adversarial models have not been applied to carotid ultrasound image segmentation under limited-data conditions. To address these limitations, our study proposes two semi-supervised segmentation approaches with threshold-based pseudo-label filtering: (i) a fine-tuned, self-training semi-supervised U-Net, and (ii) an adversarial PatchGAN-guided semi-supervised U-Net (PatchGAN-guided SSL U-Net.

Methodology

Our frameworks utilized a dataset of 970 B-mode ultrasound images of the internal carotid artery (ICA) along with expert-annotated masks from Imperial College London, UK [13]. The dataset includes examinations from 99 patients (47 males and 52 females) with a mean age of 75.04 ± 9.96 years and a moderate to high cardiovascular risk. Figure 2 presents sample internal carotid artery ultrasound images and corresponding binary masks. The white pixels indicate the plaque region, and black represents the background. Table 1 shows a random split of the data into labeled and unlabeled subsets (30% – 70%) to simulate data scarcity in clinical scenarios. The ultrasound images were preprocessed by resizing them to a uniform spatial resolution of 256 × 256 × 3 and batch-normalizing the intensity range to [0, 1] to help faster convergence during training.

Framework 1: Fine-tuned self-training SSL U-Net

The proposed fine-tuned self-training semi-supervised U-Net (FT-ST-SSL U-Net) framework addresses the challenge of limited expert annotations in carotid plaque segmentation. It has a skip-connected batch-normalized symmetric encoder–decoder architecture. Batch normalization layers were employed in deeper convolutional layers (3rd, 4th, and bottleneck) to preserve spatial context. This structural modification enables hierarchical feature learning, stabilizing training, and reducing internal covariate shift, which is particularly beneficial under limited supervision. The U-Net model is designed to undergo a two-stage training process- supervised and unsupervised. During the supervised learning phase, the U-Net was pretrained on 30% of the available ICA ultrasound images paired with expert-annotated ground truth (labeled subset). A Dice-based loss function reduces the class imbalance between the plaque and background regions. In the next stage, the pretrained model predicts reliable plaque masks for the remaining unlabeled ultrasound images.

Figure 3 illustrates the overall architecture of the FT-ST-SSL U-Net pipeline. The predicted masks were refined by morphological post-processing, including hole filling and boundary smoothing. This post-processing will reduce speckle-induced noise and improve the clarity of the plaque boundaries. Further, a Dice similarity threshold (τ = 0.7) filtered out low-segmented images, ensuring that only high-confidence segmentations contributed to subsequent retraining. Augmentation is achieved by combining 30% original expert-annotated masks with predicted labels. The network is further retrained on this augmented dataset to enhance the segmentation performance with unseen samples. This semi-supervised refinement improves carotid plaque boundary segmentation at minimum cost. Algorithm 1 outlines the stepwise workflow of this architecture. Framework 2: PatchGAN-guided SSL U-Net

To improve the performance of Framework 1, we innovatively integrated an adversarial PatchGAN-based discriminator into the semi-supervised U-Net generator framework for ultrasound imaging under limited annotations. The improved model adopts a generative adversarial learning paradigm. A batch-normalized U-Net with a 1 × 1 convolutional layer followed by softmax activation served as the segmentation generator, and a localized Pix2Pix PatchGAN-based convolutional neural network acted as the discriminator. The encoder-decoder captures spatial details of the blockage. The generator uses a hybrid Adam optimization function that combines the Dice score and adversarial loss. The generator produces a probabilistic map of the inputs and effectively learns from both labeled (supervised training) and unlabeled data (unsupervised training). The adversarial patch-level discriminator determines whether the generator predictions are real or fake, and provides feedback. Unlike conventional discriminators that operate on global image realism, PatchGAN operates at a 70 × 70 patch level. This enables fine-grained boundary supervision and reduces over-smoothing artifacts in pseudo-labels [53]. A specially designed 5-channel input to the discriminator allows it to distinguish plaque and background.

The experiments were conducted on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4060 GPU (Cyborg 15 A13V with a 13th-generation Intel processor). The dataset consisted of 970 B-mode ultrasound images of the internal carotid artery and expert annotations [13]. 30% of labeled data is used for pretraining and initializing the framework, and 70% of unlabeled data for unsupervised pseudo-label prediction. The PatchGAN discriminator evaluates concatenated (image–predicted mask) pairs and provides adversarial feedback to the generator. This Dice-based supervision enforces structural realism and enhances boundary consistency. Pseudo-labels are post-processed and utilized to retrain and fine-tune the model. Results

The segmentation performance of the proposed semi-supervised frameworks was evaluated on carotid ultrasound images by comparing it with expert masks set aside for testing. We used quantitative metrics and qualitative visualization methods to measure the overlap. Detailed comparisons are presented in tables of metrics and images below.

Quantitative results

The segmentation performance of the proposed frameworks was evaluated as the mean of per-image values across the dataset, using the Dice score, Jaccard index, precision, and recall. Table 2 presents the quantitative evaluation and comparative analysis illustrated in Fig. 5. Framework 1, FT-ST-SSL U-Net, achieved a Dice score of 0.8422 ± 0.0783 and a Jaccard index of 0.7312 ± 0.1041. These metrics indicate stable segmentation performance under limited labeled data conditions. The relatively low variance across image samples validates the robustness of the new model to process diverse plaque structures in ultrasound images. The Framework 2 (PatchGAN-guided SSL U-Net) architecture achieved a Dice of 0.8612 ± 0.0790 and a Jaccard of 0.7566 ± 0.1051. This shows an improved spatial overlap with the ground truth. Additionally, a precision of 0.8206 and a recall of 0.9295 highlight its effectiveness in accurately delineating plaque regions. A high recall value indicates lower false negatives, which is critical in medical image analysis for reliable plaque detection. These results indicate that integrating the PatchGAN discriminator module contributed to overall performance improvement by enhancing the pseudo-label quality and enforcing structural consistency in segmented outputs. The boxplots in Figs. 6 and 7 further confirmed the higher median performance with controlled variability and validated its effectiveness and stability. Statistical validation using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test yielded significant results (p < 0.001). Our study concludes that the proposed semi-supervised frameworks are reliable alternatives to fully supervised methods, where expert annotations are limited.

Benchmarking against state-of-the-art methods

A quantitative comparison was performed between the proposed semi-supervised frameworks and state-of-the-art carotid plaque segmentation methods across supervised, self-supervised, semi-supervised, and adversarial approaches. Table 3 shows a benchmarking of our studies against state-of-the-art methods for carotid plaque segmentation. Fully supervised models, such as the Attention-Based U-Net architecture proposed by Jain et al., reported a Dice score of 89.90, and Gan et al.‘s score was 84.92 [28, 54]. The method relies on extensive expert annotations, limiting its scalability in clinical practice. Self-supervised IR-SSL U-Net achieved a competitive Dice range of 80.14 to 88.84 on UNet + + and U-Net using two independent datasets, and another study by Gan et al. yielded 82.36 [46, 55]. The PatchGAN-guided adversarial semi-supervised model indicates improved spatial overlap compared with the FT-ST-SSL U-Net baseline. These results indicate that adversarial semi-supervised learning with a U-Net can yield competitive segmentation scores and can significantly reduce reliance on expert annotations, thereby offering a clinically practical solution for carotid plaque segmentation.

Ablation study

An ablation study was conducted to assess the impact of confidence-based thresholding (0.5, 0.6, 0.7, 0.8) on segmentation performance (Table 5). The results show slight variations across thresholds, with optimal performance at a threshold of 0.7 (Dice score of 86.12 and Jaccard of 75.66). At lower thresholds (0.5 and 0.6), the PatchGAN-guided SSL U-Net framework yields reduced overlap due to increased false positives. Whereas a higher threshold of 0.8 results in under-segmentation, with a relative increase in false negatives leading to a decline in performance. These observations support the selected threshold of 0.7 for all subsequent experiments.

Discussion

Recent carotid plaque segmentation studies predominantly rely on fully supervised architectures that require a large number of ultrasound images with expert annotations, typically reporting Dice scores in the range of 80% to 89% under fully labeled conditions. In contrast, our proposed semi-supervised frameworks were designed to operate under limited-annotation settings, utilizing only 30% labeled data. The remaining 70% of the unlabeled ultrasound images were used to generate pseudo-labels. This strategy significantly reduces reliance on expert annotations while achieving segmentation performance comparable to that of fully supervised approaches.

The novel introduction of an adversarial patch-level discriminator enforces structural consistency. This local pixel-wise supervision enables the model to capture fine-grained spatial features. Local features are important in noisy, low-contrast ultrasound images. Further, the modified U-Net generator with probabilistic mask prediction helps in pseudo-label prediction. These newly generated labels allow generalization. When comparing computational cost in terms of convergence, Framework 1 showed a lower value as it includes only one network. Since Framework 2 has an adversarial generator-discriminator architecture, it requires a longer training time. However, both frameworks exhibited comparable inference times, as only the generator is used for segmentation.

The ablation study demonstrated that segmentation performance is sensitive to confidence-based filtering. The optimal threshold of 0.7 achieved the best Dice score. The use of threshold-based pseudo-label filtering and morphological post-processing further improved segmentation reliability and generalization by removing uncertain predictions. A comparison study with the baseline U-Net and state-of-the-art methods indicates that our proposed PatchGAN-guided semi-supervised U-Net achieved a competitive segmentation performance even with limited label training.

Despite all these competitive results, several limitations remain. Our study relied on a single dataset, which limits its generalizability across imaging devices and populations. Our future work will address extending the framework to multi-center validation, domain adaptation, multi-class plaque characterization, and classification. Overall, the proposed PatchGAN-guided semi-supervised framework not only improved segmentation accuracy but also enhanced the model’s ability to capture fine-grained structural details of the carotid plaque boundary, which is critical in stroke risk assessment and clinical decision-making.

Conclusion

The innovative integration of the patch-level adversarial discriminator with self-training U-Net improves carotid plaque segmentation performance with limited annotated data. The patch-level adversarial feedback enables effective utilization of unlabeled data. The higher Dice and Jaccard scores confirm the effectiveness of PatchGAN discriminator supervision in enhancing feature learning. Although the dual network incurs higher training complexity, inference remains efficient and comparable to conventional deep learning U-Net models. The metrics obtained and qualitative analysis highlight the effectiveness of PatchGAN-guided SSL U-Net for medical image segmentation with low-annotation conditions.

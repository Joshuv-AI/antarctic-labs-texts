Identification of potential molecular targets for thyroid cancer using multiple-omics analysis and machine learning
Authors: Bo Zhang, Xue-Yong Zheng, Xiao-Hua Tai, Ying Shen, Jia-Hao Cao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Thyroid cancer is the most prevalent endocrine tumor, with increasing incidence rates annually. Identifying novel molecular markers is crucial for early diagnosis and personalized treatment.Design and methods:Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) from thyroid cancer microarray data were analyzed, functional enrichment analysis was performed, and machine learning was used to identify signature genes. Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) was applied to determine key modules associated with thyroid cancer. The expression and correlation of identified genes were analyzed, and their immune infiltration and clinical characteristics were assessed. Single-cell RNA transcriptomics was used to analyze the expression patterns of genes. The expression levels of candidate genes in thyroid cancer specimens were verified by immunohistochemistry and RT-PCR.Results:A total of 348 DEGs were identified, with 18 feature genes selected by machine learning. WGCNA revealed 12 key genes associated with thyroid cancer. Among these, ERO1B, FN1, and CRABP1 were significantly linked to clinical features, patient survival, and tumor immune infiltration. Single-cell RNA transcriptomic analysis showed distinct expression patterns for these genes across different cell types. To verify our findings, the immunohistochemical and RT-PCR results showed that the expression of CRABP1 was significantly downregulated, and the expression of FN1 was significantly upregulated in thyroid cancer tissues.Conclusion:FN1 and CRABP1 were identified as potential molecular targets in thyroid cancer. These genes influence tumor progression, immune response, and patient prognosis, offering new insights for early diagnosis and personalized treatment strategies.

Introduction

Thyroid carcinoma has emerged as the most commonly occurring endocrine tumor, with its incidence increasing steadily on a global scale each year[1]. This upward trend in diagnosis highlights the growing public health concern surrounding this disease. Notably, thyroid carcinoma is the seventh most commonly diagnosed malignancy in women globally, highlighting its substantial public health burden and the critical need for improved diagnostic and therapeutic approaches[2]. Papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) is the most common type of thyroid cancer, constituting 85–90% of all cases, and is associated with a generally favorable prognosis. The mortality rate for PTC remains low, with developed countries reporting a rate of only 0.2–0.3 per 100 000 individuals[3]. Despite its generally favorable prognosis, PTC demonstrates a marked propensity for early lymph node metastasis and local recurrence. Studies have shown that the rate of lymph node metastasis in PTC is as high as 20–90%, of which about 38–45% are found during prophylactic central node dissection[4,5]. Due to the high incidence and low mortality of PTC, the timing of its diagnosis and treatment remains a topic of ongoing debate. Anaplastic thyroid carcinoma (ATC) is highly malignant, with a median survival time of only 7–10 months, while the prognosis for medullary thyroid carcinoma is intermediate[6]. Accurate diagnosis and individualized treatment selection are currently significant challenges for clinicians. Presently, ultrasound, fine needle aspiration (FNA), and FNA combined with genetic testing are the primary methods for preoperative diagnosis of thyroid cancer[7]. However, even with comprehensive diagnostic approaches, some nodules remain undiagnosed, underscoring the urgent need for more effective molecular markers.

Machine learning is a multidisciplinary field that utilizes computational techniques to simulate or implement human learning behaviors[8]. Due to its advantages of high efficiency, low resource consumption, high accuracy, and consistency, the application of machine learning across various sectors has garnered increasing attention. In the domain of medical bioinformatics, machine learning is extensively employed not only for disease screening and imaging diagnosis but also demonstrates significant potential in areas such as prognostic analysis[9,10]. The advancement of this technology offers healthcare professionals a novel perspective to leverage high-throughput gene expression analysis alongside machine learning techniques, thereby enhancing the understanding of the genetic basis of cancer and providing critical support for the development of personalized treatment strategies.

This investigation employed microarray-based mRNA expression profiles from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database to systematically identify and characterize critical genes and signaling pathways implicated in thyroid carcinogenesis. Additionally, we aimed to identify potential candidate biomarkers for thyroid cancer through machine learning. These candidate genes, which serve as diagnostic biomarkers, have the potential to improve early detection and targeted therapies, ultimately providing a theoretical foundation for achieving precision and individualized treatment for patients.

Materials and methods

Data acquisition and filtering

Microarray data pertaining to mRNA expression profiles associated with thyroid cancer were retrieved from the GEO database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). The GEO datasets GSE65144, GSE33630, GSE27155, GSE53157, and GSE53072 served as training datasets, while GSE64912 and GSE165724 were used as test datasets (Table 1). The original GEO mRNA expression profile data were normalized using the “NormalizeBetween Arrays” R package[11]. Following principal component analysis (PCA)-based batch effect evaluation, we employed the Limma package[12] to analyze the thyroid cancer vs. normal expression matrix, applying stringent criteria (|log2FC| > 2, false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.05) for differentially expressed gene (DEG) identification. To enhance our investigation, we additionally analyzed the single-cell dataset GSE184362 from GEO. Pathway and functional enrichment analysis

Gene ontology (GO) serves as a valuable resource for the annotation of genes, classifying them based on their distinct functions. This classification is primarily centered around three key areas: molecular functions, biological processes (BPs), and cellular components[13]. GO enables researchers to systematically describe gene features, facilitating a deeper understanding of the biological roles of genes within various organisms. In addition to GO, Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis is utilized to investigate gene functions and to extract significant higher-order genomic information. This analysis provides insights into metabolic pathways and molecular interactions that are crucial for understanding BPs[14]. To conduct a comprehensive examination of the GO functions associated with potential mRNA candidates, researchers often employ the “ClusterProfiler” R package. This package is instrumental in analyzing the functional profiles of genes, allowing for the enrichment of relevant KEGG pathways. Feature genes were selected by machine learning methods

Machine learning offers the advantage of efficiently analyzing a vast number of features to identify those that are significantly correlated with a specific outcome or phenotype[15]. These identified features are then utilized to train the model, resulting in accurate predictions or classifications based on a reduced set of features. To identify potential diagnostic biomarkers for thyroid cancer, three distinct machine learning algorithms were employed for the binary classification of the disease: Random Forest, Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO), and Elastic Net Regularization. The genes identified by the three machine learning models were compared with determine overlapping genes using Venn diagrams.

Weighted gene co-expression network analysis

Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) was utilized to identify disease-associated co-expression networks correlated with specific clinical traits. This method enables systematic exploration of the connections between gene expression profiles and clinical features across diverse diseases[16]. The analysis was carried out utilizing the “WGCNA” package, a powerful tool designed for the construction and analysis of weighted networks in the context of gene expression data. Expression correlation coefficients of genes were calculated, and the similarity matrix was transformed into a weighted network. Hierarchical clustering was performed based on gene similarity to construct a clustering tree. Calculate module eigenvalues to find the most relevant genes by gene expression of each module within the sample.

Expression and correlation analysis of genes

The analysis of gene expression patterns in thyroid cancer was conducted utilizing both a training dataset and The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) dataset (https://portal.gdc.com). To assess the significance of the differences observed between the two sample sets, the Wilcoxon test was employed. Heatmap visualizations of gene correlations were generated using “Pheatmap” R package, followed by Spearman’s correlation analysis (P < 0.05 significance cutoff) for nonparametric data.

Immune infiltration analysis and immune correlation analysis

We retrieved whole transcriptome profiling data (RNA-seq) and associated clinical records for thyroid cancer patients from the TCGA repository. Immunity scores were evaluated according to the CIBERSORT algorithm, which is performed by the “immunedeconv” R package[17]. Correlations between individual genes and immunity scores were analyzed according to the TIMER algorithm. Nonparametric associations between diagnostic biomarkers and immune cell infiltration patterns were assessed using Spearman’s rank correlation, which is robust for non-normally distributed variables. Correlations between gene expression and immunity scores, as well as correlations with immunity scores themselves, were analyzed and visualized using the “ggClusterNet” R package.

Prognostic analysis and clinical characterization of featured genes

RNA sequencing data, along with the associated clinical information pertaining to thyroid cancer, were meticulously sourced from the TCGA database to facilitate deeper exploration into the influence of specific genes on patient prognosis. Based on median expression levels, the samples were stratified into two groups: a high-expression group and a low-expression group. Prognostic differences between these groups were assessed using progression-free survival (PFS) as the primary endpoint. The findings suggest that a high expression of specific genes correlates with poorer prognostic outcomes, indicating that such genes may play a role in promoting tumor development. Conversely, genes associated with lower expression may function as protective factors against tumor progression. To further assess survival differences among the groups, the log-rank test was employed in conjunction with Kaplan-Meier survival analysis. This statistical approach allowed for a robust comparison of survival outcomes between the groups under consideration. Additionally, to assess and compare the prognostic predictive accuracy of key hub genes, time-dependent receiver operating characteristic analysis was performed. Kaplan–Meier survival curves were analyzed using log-rank tests, with P-values and hazard ratios (HRs) along with their 95% confidence intervals (CIs) derived from univariate Cox regression. This approach provided a robust evaluation of the risk associations between gene expression profiles and patient outcomes. For further investigation of clinical correlations, RNA-seq data and corresponding clinical records from 512 thyroid cancer patients were obtained from TCGA database. To visually represent the relationships and flows of genetic influence regarding clinical outcomes, Sankey diagrams were constructed using the “ggalluvial” R package, offering an innovative visualization of the complex interplay between gene expression and clinical characteristics in thyroid cancer.

Single-cell analysis

Single-cell analysis was used to demonstrate the expression levels of ERO1B, FN1, and CRABP1 genes in different cell subsets of thyroid cancer tissues[18]. Thyroid cancer single-cell data from the GEO database, specifically identified as GSE184362, were retrieved for analysis. To facilitate the exploration of this data, the “Seurat” R package was employed to create objects that encapsulate the dataset while also enabling the filtration of subpar quality cells. Following data acquisition, standard preprocessing was performed to ensure data quality and analytical readiness. Single-cell RNA sequencing data were processed using the SingleR and Seurat R packages. Cellular subpopulations were identified through t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding (t-SNE) analysis, followed by iterative clustering to resolve distinct cell states. This dimensionality reduction approach enabled visualization of high-dimensional transcriptomic data in two-dimensional space while preserving local neighborhood relationships.

Participants

Inclusion criteria for thyroid cancer patients: Patients who have undergone thyroidectomy and have been histologically confirmed to have thyroid cancer. Inclusion criteria for non-thyroid cancer patients: Patients who have been pathologically diagnosed with benign thyroid lesions or have undergone partial thyroidectomy due to other diseases. Exclusion criteria: (1) severe dysfunction of the heart, lungs, liver, or kidneys; (2) mental or cognitive disorders that prevent the provision of informed consent; (3) undergone thyroid surgery, radiotherapy, or chemotherapy within the past 3 months; (4) presence of autoimmune thyroid diseases (e.g., Graves’ disease and Hashimoto’s thyroiditis); (5) the use of medications that may affect thyroid function or gene expression (e.g., corticosteroids and immunosuppressants) within the past month; (6) pregnant, breastfeeding, or planning to become pregnant during the study period; and (7) participation in other clinical trials that may interfere with this study. The study adheres to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to their enrollment in the study.

The expression levels of ERO1B, FN1, and CRABP1 in thyroid cancer tissues were verified

The immunohistochemical profiles of ERO1B, FN1, and CRABP1 in thyroid cancer were sourced from The Human Protein Atlas website (https://www.proteinatlas.org/)[19]. This database offers systematic protein expression profiles across multiple cancer types, including thyroid carcinoma. The integrated proteomic data enables mechanistic exploration of oncogenic pathways and tumor biology at molecular resolution. For the RT-PCR analysis, total RNA was initially extracted from the relevant tissue samples. This extracted RNA was then reverse transcribed into cDNA. Following this, RT-PCR was conducted to assess the expression levels of specific genes of interest[20]. The primers used in this process were procured from Thermo Fisher Scientific, ensuring high specificity and reliability for the amplification of the targeted sequences during the PCR process.

The expression patterns of the 50 most significantly up- and downregulated DEGs are visualized in the heatmap (Fig. 2B). Identification of key driver genes was performed using WGCNA

WGCNA was employed to identify gene co-expression modules and to determine key modules associated with specific BPs or disease states. Topology calculations were conducted using a soft threshold power ranging from 1 to 30, resulting in the identification of an optimal soft threshold power of 12 (Fig. 4A). By applying the soft threshold power, we were able to transform the correlation matrix into an adjacency matrix. This transformation is a critical step, as it allows for the identification of connections between variables based on their correlations. Following this process, the adjacency matrix was subsequently transformed into a topological overlap matrix (TOM). The TOM serves to quantify the degree of shared connectivity between pairs of nodes, providing valuable insights into the network’s structural properties. The TOM was then used for average chained hierarchical clustering to classify related gene modules (Fig. 4B). The genes identified through these algorithms were overlapped, leading to the discovery of 12 potential key genes: ART4, CCL21, CRABP1, DPT, ERO1B, FN1, GPM6A, LTBP1, OCA2, POSTN, PSD3, and RELN (Fig. 4C).Figure 4.WGCNA to identify core modules associated with thyroid cancer. Immune infiltration analysis and immune correlation analysis of feature genes

The precise characterization of the tumor immune microenvironment represents a critical determinant of immunotherapy response, as it governs the composition, spatial distribution, and functional states of infiltrating immune cells and their dynamic crosstalk with malignant cells. Using the CIBERSORT deconvolution algorithm, we quantified immune cell infiltration patterns in thyroid carcinoma. Comparative analysis of GEO datasets revealed statistically significant disparities in tumor-infiltrating immune populations between malignant and normal thyroid tissues. Specifically, we observed marked variations in macrophages, eosinophils, plasma cells, and CD8+ T cells (Fig. 6A). Moreover, analysis based on the TCGA dataset indicated that the thyroid cancer group also displayed significant differences from the control group in CIBERSORT scores for B cells, CD8+ T cells, macrophages, mast cells, myeloid dendritic cells, and other immune cells (Fig. 6B). The TIMER computational platform was implemented to quantify correlations between core gene signatures and six major immune cell subtypes within the tumor microenvironment. Prognostic analysis and clinical features

To enable comprehensive prognostic analysis of candidate genes, we stratified thyroid cancer patients into high- and low-expression cohorts using median gene expression values as the cutoff threshold. This stratification approach enables precise evaluation of gene expression-clinical outcome relationships, facilitating identification of prognostic molecular signatures in thyroid carcinogenesis. Kaplan–Meier survival (Fig. 7A) curves illustrating the impact of ERO1B, FN1, and CRABP1 gene expression levels on PFS in cancer patients. Patients demonstrating elevated ERO1B expression showed significantly improved PFS (log-rank P = 0.0174, HR = 0.498; 95% CI: 0.28–0.885), suggesting a protective role. Conversely, high FN1 expression correlated with adverse clinical outcomes (log-rank P = 0.0032), indicating its potential as a negative prognostic marker. The HR for high expression was 1.855 (95% CI: 1.061–3.243), suggesting an increased risk. Discussion

Through integrated bioinformatics and machine learning approaches, we systematically identified 18 hub genes implicated in thyroid carcinogenesis, ultimately validating 3 robust prognostic biomarkers: ERO1B, FN1, and CRABP1. We also determined that the combination of ERO1B, FN1, and CRABP1 had a good diagnostic value. These genes showed significant expression differences in patients with thyroid cancer across different stages and age groups and were strongly correlated with patient survival, suggesting their potential clinical application in the progression and prognostic assessment of thyroid cancer. Functional analysis revealed these prognostic genes not only correlate with core tumor BPs but also significantly modulate immune cell infiltration patterns. This finding suggests that there is a complex interaction between the immune microenvironment and tumor growth, which may be an important factor affecting patient prognosis. In addition, the association of high and low expression of genes with clinical characteristics of patients further supports the potential of these genes as biomarkers.

ERO1B is an enzyme primarily located in the ER and plays an essential role in protein quality control by catalyzing the formation of disulfide bonds in nascent proteins. This biochemical activity is indispensable for protein folding fidelity and cellular proteostasis maintenance[21]. Later, other biological functions were demonstrated, such as promoting insulin biogenesis in β-cells through ER stress, thus contributing to physiological glucose homeostasis[22]. Although overexpression of ERO1B is commonly observed in several cancers and contributes to tumor progression and survival[23–25], its function is unclear. To date, there are no reports on the biological mechanisms underlying the association between ERO1B and thyroid cancer. Understanding the molecular mechanisms behind low ERO1B expression in thyroid cancer could provide new insights into therapeutic strategies aimed at targeting ER stress pathways or exploiting tumor vulnerabilities. Further research is required to explore how ERO1B can be leveraged as a biomarker for prognosis and therapeutic targeting in thyroid and other cancers.

FN1 is a substantial glycoprotein found in the extracellular matrix that is crucial for cell adhesion, movement, and tissue remodeling. This protein participates in various physiological functions such as wound healing, embryonic development, and immune reactions[26]. In the context of cancer, FN1 is frequently overproduced and significantly influences tumor advancement. Elevated levels of FN1 correlate with enhanced cell migration, invasion, and metastasis. By remodeling the extracellular matrix, FN1 aids cancer cells in detaching from their primary tumors, infiltrating adjacent tissues, and disseminating to remote locations[27]. Furthermore, FN1 plays a role in angiogenesis, the process of developing new blood vessels, thereby fostering tumor growth by improving the delivery of nutrients and oxygen to the cancerous tissue[28]. The role of FN1 in regulating the tumor microenvironment and promoting tumor cell interactions makes it a key factor in cancer metastasis and aggressiveness. In thyroid cancer, particularly in aggressive forms like ATC, high FN1 expression is often observed and has important clinical implications[29]. High FN1 expression is often associated with a more aggressive and less differentiated phenotype in thyroid cancer. Studies have shown that PLA2R1 inhibits the proliferation and migration of differentiated thyroid cancer through the FN1-mediated ITGB1/FAK axis[30]. Additionally, NEAT1_2/miR-491 regulates the invasion and metastasis of thyroid cancer through the TGM2/NFκb/FN1 signaling pathway[31]. The increased FN1 levels correlate with poorer patient outcomes, including a higher likelihood of metastasis and recurrence after treatment[32]. A previous study demonstrated that MicroRNA-142-3p suppresses thyroid cancer progression by targeting FN1 and inactivating the FAK/ERK/PI3K signaling pathway[33]. Furthermore, FN1-p53-induced protein ZMAT3 facilitates lymph node metastasis in thyroid cancer through the regulation of alternative splicing in the Extra Domains B (EDB) region[34]. Given its involvement in promoting cancer progression, FN1 represents a potential therapeutic target. Targeting FN1 could help limit tumor spread, improve the effectiveness of treatments, and provide better clinical outcomes in thyroid carcinoma patients with high FN1 expression.

CRABP1 plays a critical role in mediating retinoic acid (RA) signaling, a key pathway involved in controlling vital cellular functions, including differentiation, growth, and apoptosis[35]. CRABP1 has emerged as a key focus in cancer research, primarily because of its ability to regulate retinoid signaling pathways. These pathways play a crucial role in controlling tumor development and metastatic spread. The expression levels of CRABP1 can vary widely across different cancer types, and its dysregulation is increasingly recognized as a contributing factor in oncogenesis and tumor behavior[36,37]. In some cancers, CRABP1 may be overexpressed, leading to decreased availability of RA for RARs and RXRs, thereby inhibiting the differentiation of cancer cells and promoting a more aggressive tumor phenotype. For example, elevated CRABP1 expression correlates with adverse clinical outcomes in breast cancer and certain blood cancers, where it potentially drives resistance to treatment[38]. Conversely, CRABP1 may be downregulated in other cancer types such as ovarian and prostate cancers[39]. Reduced CRABP1 expression can lead to increased RA signaling, which may enhance apoptosis and differentiation. However, in the context of thyroid cancer, low expression levels of CRABP1 present a unique scenario that warrants further exploration. Research has revealed a consistent decrease in CRABP1 expression in thyroid tumor specimens relative to normal thyroid tissue. Functional studies demonstrated that overexpression of CRABP1 in thyroid cancer cell lines was associated with reduced phosphorylation of the epidermal growth factor receptor and its downstream signaling molecules, including Akt, STAT3, and FAK, thereby suppressing epithelial-mesenchymal transition[40]. This discovery holds particular significance as it implies that CRABP1 may function as a key modulator of thyroid cancer cell behavior. Intriguingly, while CRABP1 overexpression significantly impaired cellular migration and invasive capacity, it exerted no measurable effect on proliferation rates or cell cycle progression. This dissociation of effects suggests that CRABP1’s anti-metastatic functions operate through mechanisms distinct from growth regulation, potentially involving specific alterations to cytoskeletal dynamics or cell–matrix interactions. Collectively, these findings position CRABP1 as a promising molecular target for anti-metastatic therapy in thyroid carcinoma, with its regulatory role in tumor cell motility offering novel opportunities for therapeutic intervention. The low expression of CRABP1 may contribute to impaired RA signaling, promote malignant characteristics. These findings underscore the need for deeper investigation into CRABP1’s molecular mechanisms in thyroid carcinogenesis. Such research could yield dual benefits: advancing fundamental knowledge of thyroid cancer biology while potentially identifying novel therapeutic targets. The protein’s specific impact on metastatic behavior – distinct from proliferative pathways – makes it particularly intriguing for translational applications. As precision medicine transforms oncology, elucidating CRABP1’s role may contribute to more tailored treatment strategies, especially for aggressive or treatment-resistant thyroid cancer subtypes.

This study employs multiple methods of data integration and analysis, including RNA transcriptomics, machine learning, and single-cell transcriptomics, to systematically elucidate the molecular landscape of thyroid cancer. This multilevel and multifaceted analysis approach helps to fully reveal the complex biology of thyroid cancer. However, the study also has certain limitations. First, the relatively small sample size may affect the generalizability of the results, which need to be validated in larger clinical samples. Second, there may be heterogeneity in data sources, and differences between data sets may affect the accuracy and consistency of the results. Finally, although the study revealed potential therapeutic targets, the specific effects and therapeutic efficacy of these targets still need to be experimentally verified. Future studies should focus on the following aspects. First, the expression and function of these prognosis-specific genes need to be validated in larger clinical samples to ensure the reliability and generalizability of the results. Second, the specific biological functions and mechanisms of these genes in thyroid cancer should be further explored, especially how they affect the tumor microenvironment and immune response. Third, target validation experiments should be conducted to assess the regulatory roles of the genes and their potential in thyroid cancer treatment. In addition, future investigations should integrate additional multi-omics layers – particularly proteomic and metabolomic profiling – to achieve a systems-level understanding of thyroid tumorigenesis and facilitate the development of novel diagnostic biomarkers and targeted therapies.

Conclusion

In summary, ERO1B, FN1, and CRABP1 were identified as characterized genes that are closely associated with thyroid cancer by multiple data analysis methods in this study. These genes play critical roles in thyroid cancer pathogenesis, progression, and clinical outcomes, while also demonstrating significant involvement in tumor microenvironment immunomodulation. ERO1B, FN1, and CRABP1 have favorable diagnostic value for thyroid cancer studied in multiple machine learning models. Our findings uncover novel molecular targets with significant therapeutic potential while advancing diagnostic and precision medicine approaches for thyroid carcinoma. Future studies should further validate these findings, explore their specific mechanisms, and develop new diagnostic and therapeutic strategies to enhance the clinical management of thyroid cancer.

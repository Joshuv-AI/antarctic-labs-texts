Optimization algorithm of association rule mining for heavy-haul railway freight train fault data based on distributed parallel computing
Authors: Yanhui Bai, Honghui Li, Wengang Wang, Shufang Liu, Ning Zhang, Chun Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractWith the continuous improvement in the efficiency of the heavy-haul railway freight transportation, the pressure on on-site maintenance is increasing. In-depth research on fault characteristics carries significant importance for fault scientific judgment and fault prevention. This study proposes an efficient association rule mining (ARM) algorithm, HM-RDHP, for analyzing fault data from heavy-haul railway freight trains. The algorithm introduces distributed parallel computing technology, integrating the MapReduce framework and HBase on the Hadoop platform to process large volumes of complex fault data efficiently. Experimental results show that the HM-RDHP algorithm can efficiently uncover hidden patterns and associations within the fault data of heavy-haul railway freight trains. The mined association rules provide a valuable reference model to aid in predictive maintenance and fault prevention strategies for freight train maintenance departments.Keywords: Heavy-haul railway freight train, association rule mining, DHP algorithm, distributed parallel computing, MapReduce

Introduction

As a large-scale transportation vehicle, health status of parts of heavy-haul railway freight trains is closely related to transportation efficiency and safety. Part faults pose a significant threat to train operations in various ways and can result in heavy economic losses for railway transportation. The maintenance departments of railways continuously enrich their maintenance methods by increasing onboard and wayside monitoring equipment and use modern railway information technology to make maintenance decisions by in-depth analysis of fault causes. These efforts can quickly restore the transport capability of freight trains and ensure operational safety, thereby improving the economic efficiency of railway enterprises.

With the development of predictive and health management (PHM) technology, researchers have proposed reasonable suggestions for the diagnosis and prevention of faults in key parts of freight trains based on the running state data of freight trains using techniques, such as machine learning, deep learning, and others. For example, Wang et al. used the random forest method and proposed a new feature engineering framework for diagnosing air brake faults in freight trains. 1 Li et al. proposed a SA-ACS-DBN model based on a deep belief network and cuckoo search algorithm to diagnose wheelset failures of freight trains. 2 Wang et al. predicted the remaining service life of equipment by studying crack propagation in mechanical systems. 3 However, faults in freight train operations may be caused by multiple factors, including component craftsmanship, climatic conditions, geographic location, the structure of the tracks themselves, and the track bed structure. 4 These studies don’t reveal the associations between fault features; therefore, exploring these associations is of significant importance for fault diagnosis and prevention, reducing maintenance costs, and ensuring that components enter the repair process as scheduled.

In recent years, the ARM has been popularly applied in the field of security and risk analysis. Its effectiveness has been demonstrated through numerous practical applications. Particularly, Xu et al. focused on association rule mining (ARM) from horizontally distributed medical data for medical diagnostic purposes. 5 He et al. used the ARM method to explore the associations between characteristics of maritime accidents. 6 Apostolos. et al. discovered potential patterns of injured road users in Greek traffic accidents using the Apriori algorithm. 7 However, only a few studies have been previously conducted to examine transport safety of heavy-haul railway freight trains in terms of fault relation from fault data. This paper, based on a vast amount of freight train fault data, conducts an in-depth study of the ARM algorithm, aiming to extract association rules of fault characteristics and provide scientifically valuable guidance for maintenance decisions.

Considering that the fault dataset accumulated from nearly 20 years of heavy-haul railway freight train operations is characterized by multiple sources and heterogeneity, large volume, and multiple types and factors of faults, the traditional Apriori algorithm in ARM requires multiple database scans, resulting in high computational complexity due to the large volume of candidate set support calculations. This paper proposes an improved Rehash DHP algorithm (RDHP) based on the DHP algorithm with pruning techniques. By introducing Rehash technology, it effectively mitigates hash address conflicts, thereby reducing the time complexity with frequent hash bucket scans. To further enhance computational efficiency, we propose the HM-RDHP algorithm, which incorporates the Kettle technology framework to integrate and restructure heterogeneous data. Utilizing distributed parallel computing, this algorithm implements the decomposition and parallel processing of massive fault data on the Hadoop big data processing platform. Experimental results show that the HM-RDHP algorithm efficiently discovers associations between faults and reveals strong correlations between faults and geographical location and seasons. This provides valuable support for the scientific assessment and prevention of on-site faults. Long et al. state that association rule mining (ARM) is used to discover the relevance or correlation existing in datasets. 8 It is an interesting and practical analytical technology.

Apriori algorithm process

The famous Apriori algorithm is a classic algorithm for ARM. 10 The algorithm employs an approach called iterative layer-wise search, utilizing k -itemsets to investigate ( k+1 )-itemsets. Initially, the collection of frequent 1-itemsets L1 is discovered. Subsequently, L1 is used to discover the frequent 2-itemsets L2 , and this process is repeated to discover L3 .

This process goes on until there are no more frequent k -itemsets Lk . To find each Lk , the database needs to be traversed once. During the execution of the algorithm, the database will be scanned many times. When dealing with large datasets, the number of candidate itemsets can be enormous, resulting in a heavy load for calculating the support of candidate sets and requiring multiple scans of the database.

DHP algorithm process

In order to solve the problems of the Apriori algorithm, Park et al. proposed the DHP algorithm. 11 DHP algorithm is derived from Apriori algorithm and based on Hash optimization. It is effective in the pruning process of the Apriori algorithm. By utilizing hash pruning technology, it primarily aims to reduce the number of itemsets in the candidate sets, effectively resolving the performance bottleneck issue during the frequent generation of 2-itemsets. In the kth scan, DHP algorithm generates the k+1 itemset of every transaction, which are then passed to a hash function to generate a hash table. In order to avoid hash conflicts, the DHP algorithm sets hash buckets to record the number of elements in each bucket while generating a hash table. When the amount of transaction data is very large, the majority of hash buckets have a higher number of entries than the preset minimum support count. In order to judge whether the candidate set of the hash bucket is a frequent itemset, it is necessary to scan the database again. This results in increased data transmission and scanning, contributing to significant time overhead.

RDHP algorithm

When the amount of transaction data is huge, it is necessary to decrease the time complexity of frequent scanning of hash bucket data in item k −1 to reduce the scanning times of DHP algorithm in the kth item. By comparing the open addressing method, chain addressing method, and Rehash method to solve the hash conflict, the open address method is easy to produce “secondary aggregation,” and the chain address method needs additional space to save the node address information. Rehash technology is adopted, and an RDHP algorithm is proposed to suppress hash address conflict and improve the stability and availability of the algorithm by improving storage and query efficiency.

HM-RDHP algorithm

Although the RDHP algorithm demonstrates good time and space performance, it is primarily suitable for single-machine environments. To better utilize the storage and computing resources of big data platforms and further reduce the computational time and space complexity, the HM-RDHP algorithm is proposed. This algorithm introduces the idea of distributed parallel computing to improve system calculation speed and data analysis processing capability.12,13

The algorithm uses the Kettle technology to clean and integrate the multi-source and heterogeneous data. After fusion, the data are transformed into a transaction dataset. The transaction dataset is divided into multiple subsets. Using the MapReduce programming model, the tasks of association rule mining between these subsets are iteratively processed in parallel on the Hadoop big data platform. By using time parallelism (pipeline technology) and space parallelism (multiple processors simultaneously executing tasks), the efficiency of mining fault data association rules is further improved.

HBase (Hadoop Database) is a distributed storage system that serves as the database for Hadoop. HBase utilizes Hadoop's HDFS as its file storage system and leverages Hadoop's MapReduce for processing massive amounts of data. 14 Unlike relational databases, HBase adopts a column-oriented storage model, which is well-suited for storing unstructured data. The MapReduce programming model provides high-performance computing capabilities for HBase by offering a distributed computing environment.

Data extraction and fusion process. The Kettle technology architecture is used for processing multi-source & heterogeneous data. Basic information such as parts structure, vehicle groupings and fault types is retrieved from the Oracle database of Data Center, while information such as vehicle running mileage, 5 T data, and other dynamic statuses are obtained from HBase 15 . The data are then preprocessed, including data cleaning to remove duplicate entries using Filter Rows and Select Values steps, handling missing values by calculating averages with Filter Rows, Value Mapper, and Calculator steps, standardizing data formats through Calculator and Value Mapper steps, and discretizing data using the Value Mapper step. Subsequently, data fusion and restructuring are performed to create a data mart applied for association rule mining business. This data mart provides standardized, accurate, and complete data sets as input to MapReduce, thereby improving data access efficiency. The fault dataset is divided into shards and key-value pairs are extracted from the dataset and passed to the function of mapper ( k,v ) in the Map phase. Local frequent itemsets are obtained for each partition, and the intermediate results of the computation are stored in a cache. Once the cache reaches 80% capacity, the results are written to HDFS. In the Reduce phase, all the intermediate results generated during Map phase are read. These results are sorted by key, and identical keys are handled by the same Reducer for merging. Nonduplicate keys and their associated values are passed to the reducer (key, value) function. The support of frequent itemsets is compared to the support threshold, and itemsets with support below the minimum threshold are pruned. This process yields the frequent itemsets of the entire faulty transaction dataset.

HM-RDHP algorithm implementation. The HM-RDHP algorithm is designed in a big data environment based on RDHP algorithm and MapReduce partition parallel computing thought. MapReduce utilizes multiple processors to perform parallel computing and process multiple sub-tasks simultaneously. On the other hand, the RDHP algorithm iteratively calculates frequent itemsets in each partition. The computation results of subtasks are merged to obtain the final result. Map phase: Each map task reads fault data from a specific partition of the fault dataset and automatically transforms it into the form of key-value pairs:< k1,v1 >. The map task stores all the key-value pairs of fault data in local memory for this partition.

The RDHP algorithm is then used to obtain the local frequent itemsets for this partition. The map task outputs the key-value pairs:< k2,v2 >, where k2 represents these local frequent itemsets of this partition and v2 represents the support of the local frequent itemsets within this partition .

Reduce phase: The outputs of the Map phase are received in the Reduce phase, which are the set of local frequent itemsets generated after applying the RDHP algorithm. These outputs are sorted based on k2 . Each Reduce task handles the same k2 values. The Reduce task calculates the support for each frequent itemset and compares it with the support threshold.

Frequent itemsets that meet the support threshold are collected to obtain the frequent itemsets of the entire fault dataset in the form of <k3, v3>.

Experimental data

The experiment selects the actual fault data of heavy-haul railway freight trains. The data are collected from periodic inspections and 5 T warning equipment between January 2003 and January 2022. After reorganizing the data through treatments such as handling missing values, normalization, and discretization, a total of 1.3 million fault records were selected for the experiment.

Performance analysis

Based on the selected fault dataset sizes of 250,000, 500,000, 750,000, 1 million, and 1.3 million for faults of railway freight trains, the calculation times of the Apriori, DHP, RDHP, and HM-RDHP algorithms are compared as shown in Figure 6:

comparative analysis of algorithm performance.

When the dataset is relatively small, the Apriori algorithm outperforms the other algorithms because constructing the hash table consumes a significant amount of time. As the dataset size increases, the RDHP algorithm performs better than the DHP algorithm due to the time consumed in searching for hash conflicts and counting hash buckets. When the dataset size reaches 500,000, the parallel HM-RDHP algorithm outperforms the RDHP algorithm because the RDHP algorithm spends a significant amount of time on hash conflict calculations as the candidate set grows. Therefore, the parallel HM-RDHP algorithm outperforms the serial implementation of the other algorithms.

Concluding remarks

This paper demonstrates that the HM-RDHP algorithm can efficiently mine association rules from massive fault data of heavy-haul railway freight trains. The strong correlations discovered between faults and geographical location and seasons provide a scientific basis for maintenance departments to analyze the reasons for key components entering maintenance cycles ahead of schedule.

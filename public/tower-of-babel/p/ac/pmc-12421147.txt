Impact of a 24/7 on-site percutaneous coronary intervention team strategy on door-to-wire time
Authors: Liang Wang, Xinglin Yang, Wei Wu, Ran Tian, Ming Yang, Yechen Han, Jianzhong Shen, Hao Qian, Fan Guo, Tengyue Zhang, Xueqing Zhu, Xiaofeng Jin, Chonghui Wang, Hongzhi Xie, Zhongjie Fan, Zhujun Shen, Jihai Liu, Zhanjie Zhang, Xiaojun Ma, Zhenyu Liu, Huadong Zhu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCurrent guidelines recommend that the door-to-wire (D2W) time should be <90 min in patients undergoing primary percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) for ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI). This study evaluated the effect of a 24/7 on-site PCI team strategy on the D2W time.MethodsIn this single-centre, retrospective study, patients with STEMI undergoing primary PCI within 1 year before (control group, n=143) and 1 year after (intervention group, n=96) implementing a 24/7 on-site PCI team strategy were enrolled. This strategy required that the PCI team was always available in hospital to minimise the time required to make the catheterisation laboratory ready for PCI. The primary endpoint was the D2W time, and the secondary endpoints were the rate of delayed PCI (D2W time ≥90 min) and the on-site time of the PCI team members. Multivariate analysis was conducted to determine the independent predictors of delayed PCI in the intervention group.ResultsThe D2W time was shorter (52 vs 97 min, p<0.001), the rate of delayed PCI was lower (19.8% vs 55.2%, p<0.001), but the on-site time of the PCI team members (35 040 vs 9960 hours) was longer in the intervention group than in the control group. Time-to-obtain electrocardiogram >10 min (p=0.027) and time-to-make STEMI diagnosis >9 min (p=0.001) were independent predictors of delayed PCI after implementing the strategy.ConclusionsImplementing a 24/7 on-site PCI team strategy was associated with reductions in the D2W time and the rate of delayed PCI but an increase in the on-site time of the PCI team members.

Introduction

Primary percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) has proven to be the most effective reperfusion strategy for patients with ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI), provided it is performed in a timely manner.15 A shorter door-to-wire (D2W) time, that is, the time interval between patient arrival (at the emergency department (ED)) and wire-crossing (of the occlusive coronary lesion), was associated with reduced mortality in patients with STEMI undergoing primary PCI.612 Therefore, the D2W time should be shortened as much as possible, which is recommended to be <90 min in current guidelines.1315

One of the key contributors to the D2W time is the response time of the PCI team, especially during off-hours. This response time, that is, the time required to make the catheterisation laboratory (cath lab) ready for PCI, is defined as the time interval between the time when the PCI team is activated and that when the cath lab is ready for PCI. To reduce the response time, a 24/7 on-site PCI team strategy was implemented since 1 January 2023 in Peking Union Medical College Hospital (PUMCH). This strategy required that the PCI team, including 2 interventional cardiologists and two nurses, was always present in hospital, thereby ensuring a round-the-clock availability of the PCI team. However, such a strategy might be labour-consuming, especially when the number of STEMI patients actually treated with primary PCI was relatively small.

Therefore, a retrospective observational study was conducted to assess the impact of the 24/7 on-site PCI team strategy on the D2W time and the rate of delayed PCI (D2W time ≥90 min) in STEMI patients, as well as on the on-site time of the PCI team members.

Methods

Study design and conduct and reporting

This was a single-centre, retrospective study based on a real-world process improvement initiative performed in PUMCH, aiming at optimising the timeliness of STEMI care. It was conducted in strict adherence to the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice guidelines. The study protocol received approval from the ethics committee of PUMCH. Given the retrospective design of the study, the requirement for informed consent was waived. The reporting of this study adhered to the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology guidelines.

Study population

Consecutive patients with STEMI who presented within 12 h of symptom onset and underwent primary PCI in PUMCH from 1 January to 31 December 2022 (control group) and those during the same period in 2023 (intervention group) were enrolled. As a 24/7 on-site PCI team strategy was implemented since 1 January 2023 in PUMCH, the control and intervention groups underwent primary PCI before and after implementing the 24/7 on-site PCI team strategy, respectively.

The diagnosis of STEMI was based on acute chest pain (or equivalent signs/symptoms), new ST-segment elevation at the J-points in at least two contiguous leads or a newly developed bundle branch block on ECG. This diagnosis was later corroborated by an increase in cardiac biomarkers, such as cardiac troponin, that is, exceeding the 99th percentile of the upper reference limit.1

Treatment procedures

Once a patient with acute chest pain presented at the ED and acute coronary syndrome was suspected, an ECG was obtained as soon as possible. If the diagnosis of STEMI was indicated based on the symptoms and ECG results, the PCI team was activated immediately by the ED physicians. Once the cath lab was ready, the patient was transferred to the cath lab for primary PCI. All patients received aspirin, P2Y12 inhibitors, glycoprotein IIb/IIIa inhibitors and anticoagulants during the peri-procedural period according to guideline recommendations and local practice.1 14 15 All technical aspects concerning mechanical reperfusion were left to the discretion of the treating interventional cardiologists.

Data collection

Patient data were collected, including demographic characteristics, cardiovascular risk factors, prior history of cardiovascular diseases (CVDs), comorbidities, arrival time and modes, vital signs and physical examinations, Killip classification, location of myocardial infarction, procedural characteristics, information on time intervals and clinical outcomes.

Definitions

The total ischaemic time was defined as the time interval between symptom onset and wire-crossing. The symptom-to-door (S2D) time was defined as the time interval between symptom onset and patient arrival at the ED. The D2W time was defined as the time interval between patient arrival at ED and wire-crossing (online supplemental figure S1).

The D2W time was further arbitrarily divided into five individual components: (1) time to obtain ECG, that is, the time interval between patient arrival at the ED and ECG acquisition; (2) time to make diagnosis, that is, the time interval between ECG acquisition and STEMI diagnosis establishment/PCI team activation; (3) time to make the cath lab ready (response time of the PCI team), that is, the time interval between the PCI team activation and cath lab readiness for PCI; (4) time to transfer patient, that is, the time interval between cath lab readiness for PCI and patient arrival at the cath lab; (5) time to cross lesion, that is, the time interval between patient arrival at the cath lab and wire-crossing (online supplemental figure S1).

On-hours was defined as the time period from 8:00 a.m. up to 5:59 p.m. during weekdays. The rest of the time in a week (from 6:00 p.m. on the index day up to 7:59 a.m. on the next day during weekdays, as well as the entire days on weekends and holidays) was defined as off-hours.

Study endpoints

The primary endpoint of the study was the D2W time. The secondary endpoints included (1) the rate of delayed PCI, that is, the percentage of patients with STEMI with D2W time ≥90 min, and (2) the on-site time of the PCI team members, that is, the total time during which the PCI team members stayed physically in the hospital. The tertiary endpoint was the rate of in-hospital mortality.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables with normal distribution were expressed as mean±SD and compared with Student’s t-test. Continuous variables with skewed distributions were demonstrated as median (IQR) and compared with the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables were presented as frequency (percentage) and compared with the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test. To address potential confounding, propensity score matching was conducted using the MatchIt package in R (version 4.2.2; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). The propensity scores were estimated using a logistic regression model, in which baseline characteristics with a p-value of ≤0.1 in between-group comparisons were included. Patients were matched in a 1:1 ratio using nearest*neighbour matching with a calliper width of 0.2 of the SD of the logit of the propensity score. To test the robustness of the primary findings, sensitivity analyses were performed in the matched cohort. Univariate logistic regression was performed to explore the potential predictors of delayed PCI in the intervention group. Variables with a two-tailed p-value of <0.1 in the univariate analysis were subsequently included in the multivariate logistic regression analysis. A two-tailed p-value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed using R (version 4.2.2; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria), SPSS (version 26.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, USA) and GraphPad Prism (version 9.0; GraphPad Software, San Diego, United States).重试

Results

Study population

In 2022, a total of 159 consecutive patients with STEMI were admitted to PUMCH. After excluding the 16 patients who did not undergo coronary angiography and/or primary PCI, 143 patients undergoing primary PCI were enrolled in the control group. In 2023, 99 consecutive patients with STEMI were admitted to PUMCH, of which the 96 who underwent primary PCI were enrolled in the intervention group and the 3 who were not treated with primary PCI were excluded (online supplemental figure S2). In the intervention group, 35 patients presented during on-hours and 61 presented during off-hours. In the control group, 47 and 96 patients were admitted during on-hours and off-hours, respectively. D2W time and rate of delayed PCI

The D2W time was 52 (43, 72.75) min in the intervention group and 97 (74, 143) min in the control group (p<0.001; table 2 and figure 1). The between-group absolute difference and relative difference of the D2W time were 45 min and 46.4%, respectively. The rates of delayed PCI were 19.8% (19/96) and 55.2% (79/143) in the intervention and control groups, respectively (p<0.001; table 2 and figure 2). The 24/7 on-site PCI team strategy was associated with an absolute reduction of 35.4 percentage points and a relative reduction of 64.1% in the rate of delayed PCI between the two groups.

Overall D2W time and rate of delayed PCI during on-hours and off-hours

During on-hours, the D2W time was 52 (43.5, 72.5) min in the intervention group and 82 (62.5, 162.5) min in the control group (p<0.001; table 2 and figure 1). The between-group absolute and relative differences of the D2W time during on-hours were 30 min and 36.6%, respectively. During off-hours, the D2W times were 53 (43, 72) and 101.5 (79, 133.5) min in the intervention and control groups, respectively (p<0.001). The 24/7 on-site PCI team strategy was associated with an absolute reduction of 48.5 min and a relative reduction of 47.8% in the D2W time during off-hours between the two groups.

During on-hours, the rates of delayed PCI were 17.1% (6/35) in the intervention group and 46.8% (22/47) in the control group (p=0.009; table 2 and figure 2). The between-group absolute and relative differences in the rate of delayed PCI were 29.7 percentage points and 63.5%, respectively. During off-hours, the rates of delayed PCI were 21.3% (13/61) and 59.4% (57/96) in the intervention and control groups, respectively (p<0.001). The 24/7 on-site PCI team strategy was associated with an absolute reduction of 38.1 percentage points and a relative reduction of 64.1% in the rate of delayed PCI during off-hours between the two groups.

The results of the sensitivity analyses performed in the propensity score-matched cohorts were consistent with those of the primary analyses (online supplemental table S1).

On-site time of the PCI team members

The on-site time of the PCI team members was 35 040 hours [= 24 hours/day/team member * 365 days (the number of days from 1 January to 31 December in 2023) * four team members] in the intervention group and 9960 hours [= 10 hours (the time during on-hours)/day/team member * 249 days (the number of working days from 1 January to 31 December in 2022) * four team members] in the control group, which was increased by 25 080 hours. The total D2W time saved for all patients with STEMI in the intervention group was estimated to be 72 hours (4320 min), which was calculated as [97 min (the median D2W time in the control group) – 52 min (the median D2W time in the intervention group)]/patient * 96 patients (the number of patients in the intervention group). Therefore, the ratio of the increase in the on-site time of the PCI team members to the reduction in the D2W time of the patients with STEMI was estimated to be 348.33: 1 (= 25 080: 72).

Rate of in-hospital mortality

The mortality rate was 5.2% in the intervention group (5/96) and 8.4% in the control group (12/143) during the index hospitalisation (p=0.445).

Independent predictors of delayed PCI in the intervention group

In the univariate logistic regression analysis, male sex [HR 0.269, 95% CI 0.093 to 0.778, p=0.015] and emergency medical services (EMS) transport (HR 0.302, 95% CI 0.102 to 0.892, p=0.030) were negatively associated; however, the time to obtain ECG >10 min (HR 3.702, 95% CI 1.180 to 11.616, p=0.025) and time to make diagnosis >9 min (the median value) (HR 8.395, 95% CI 2.691 to 26.188, p<0.001) were positively associated with delayed PCI in the intervention group (table 3).

Following the univariate analysis, variables with a two-tailed p-value of <0.1 (ie, age ≥75 years, male sex, EMS transport, time to obtain ECG >10 min and time to make diagnosis >9 min) were included in the multivariate logistic regression analysis, which revealed that time to obtain ECG >10 min (HR 4.918, 95% CI 1.194 to 20.261, p=0.027) and time to make diagnosis >9 min (HR 9.874, 95% CI 2.409 to 40.465, p=0.001) remained as independent predictors of delayed PCI in the intervention group.

Determinants of prolonged D2W time in the intervention group

A detailed analysis was conducted in the 15 patients with delayed PCI in the intervention group, focusing on the five individual components of the overall D2W time (online supplemental table S2). The major determinants of the prolonged overall D2W time, that is, the components which took the largest proportion of the overall D2W time, were time to obtain ECG in six patients, time to make diagnosis in six patients, time to make the cath lab ready in one patient, time to transfer the patient in one patient, and time to cross lesion in one patient.

Discussion

This study demonstrated that implementing a 24/7 on-site PCI team strategy was associated with reductions in both the D2W time and rate of delayed PCI in patients with STEMI undergoing primary PCI, especially in those presenting during off-hours, but at the cost of increased on-site time of the PCI team members. The time to obtain ECG >10 min and time to make STEMI diagnosis >9 min were independent predictors of delayed PCI after implementing the 24/7 on-site PCI team strategy.

Timely reperfusion is essential to improve the outcomes of patients with STEMI.16 17 Although current guidelines recommend that primary PCI should be performed within 90 min of patient presentation, a considerable proportion of primary PCI was delayed in real-world clinical practice.18 A recently published study including 64 hospitals in North America suggested that a significant percentage (36%) of primary PCI was still performed outside the recommended time frame even in 2021.19 The D2W time of patients with STEMI in middle-income countries was even longer than that in high-income countries.20 As in our study, primary PCI was delayed in as much as 55.2% of patients with STEMI before implementing the 24/7 on-site PCI team strategy. Therefore, shortening the D2W time is still a challenging issue to every medical system involved in STEMI care nowadays.

For patients with STEMI presenting at the ED, the D2W time was arbitrarily divided into five individual components in the present study: time to obtain ECG, time to make STEMI diagnosis, time to make the cath lab ready, time to transfer the patient and time to cross the lesion. Among the above time intervals, time to make the cath lab ready, that is, the time interval between the time when the PCI team is activated and that when the cath lab is ready for PCI, represents the response time of the PCI team. This response time would be a key contributor to the overall D2W time, particularly during off-hours. Therefore, a 24/7 on-site PCI team strategy was introduced and implemented in PUMCH to decrease the response time of the PCI team and, in consequence, the overall D2W time in patients with STEMI. This study demonstrated that this strategy was associated with reductions in both the D2W time and the rate of delayed primary PCI. In addition, these reductions seemed to be even more prominent in patients presenting at the ED during off-hours compared with those presenting at the ED during on-hours. Considering the association between the D2W time and the outcomes of patients with STEMI, implementing the 24/7 on-site PCI team strategy might improve the prognosis of these patients, especially those presenting during off-hours who demonstrated worse outcomes compared with patients admitted during on-hours.2124 Although the in-hospital mortality rate was not reduced by the 24/7 on-site PCI team strategy in this study, it might be due to the relatively small sample size and the low event rates, as well as the retrospective and observational design of this study. Randomised, adequately powered outcomes studies are necessary to clarify the clinical effect of the 24/7 on-site PCI team strategy in patients with STEMI.

Although the 24/7 on-site PCI team strategy was associated with reductions in both the D2W time and the rate of delayed PCI in patients with STEMI, it was obviously labour-consuming. As in this study, the on-site time of the whole PCI team was increased by 25 080 hours within 1 year after implementing this strategy, whereas the total D2W time saved for patients with STEMI (n=96) admitted during 1 year was estimated to be only 72 hours. Therefore, practical issues, such as the volume of primary PCI per year, the numbers of PCI team members available, and the cost of all the relevant medical staff and resources should all be taken into account before feasibly implementing this 24/7 on-site PCI team strategy. In addition, the long-term sustainability of this strategy should be regularly assessed even after its implementation. If necessary, more flexible PCI team arrangements, such as dynamic adjustment of the on-site and on-call coverage based on actual demands and resource availability, may be considered to achieve a better balance between timely care and excessive resource consumption. Nonetheless, as an initial exploration, we hope that this study would serve as the basis for developing more sustainable models in the future. Further studies aiming at improving the cost-effectiveness and optimising the resource allocations of this 24/7 on-site PCI team strategy would also be warranted.

As mentioned previously, the aim of implementing the 24/7 on-site PCI team strategy was to reduce the response time (the time required to make the cath lab ready) of the PCI team and, in consequence, the overall D2W time in patients with STEMI. However, we could not confirm that the reduction in the D2W time was completely due to the decrease in the response time of the PCI team because the five components of the D2W time in the control group were not available owing to the retrospective nature of this study. In addition, in theory, the 24/7 on-site PCI team strategy would be associated with reduction in the D2W time during off-hours rather than that during on-hours because the PCI team was already and always on-site during on-hours. Although this study did demonstrate that the extent of the reduction in the D2W time was more prominent during off-hours than during on-hours, it also revealed that both the D2W time during on-hours and that during off-hours were shorter after implementing the 24/7 on-site PCI team strategy, indicating that other factors might influence the overall D2W time in this study. For example, the end of the COVID-19 pandemic at the end of 2022 might increase the overall efficiency of the entire medical system and, in consequence, might decrease the response time of the PCI team both during on-hours and during off-hours in 2023, which might contribute to the reduction of the D2W time not only during on-hours, but also during off-hours in this study.

To further shorten the D2W time of patients with STEMI, the factors influencing the D2W time and the rate of delayed PCI after implementing the 24/7 on-site PCI team strategy were investigated. Although male sex, EMS transport, time to obtain ECG >10 min and time to make diagnosis >9 min were associated with delayed PCI in the univariate analysis, the time to obtain ECG >10 min and time to make diagnosis >9 min remained to be independent predictors of delayed PCI in the multivariate analysis, indicating that delayed ECG obtainment and delayed STEMI diagnosis were still significant contributors to delayed PCI even after implementing the 24/7 on-site PCI team strategy. In addition, according to the results of the detailed analysis of the five individual components of the D2W time in the 15 patients with delayed PCI in the intervention group, the main reasons for delayed PCI were prolonged time to obtain ECG in six patients and prolonged time to make diagnosis in six patients, which was consistent with the results of the multivariate analysis. Therefore, shortening the time to obtain ECG and the time to make the diagnosis of STEMI might be the potential approach to further reduce the overall D2W time and the rate of delayed PCI in patients with STEMI after implementing the 24/7 on-site PCI team strategy.

Limitations

The results of this study should be interpreted with caution owing to several limitations. First, the sample size was small, with only 96 and 143 patients enrolled in the intervention and control groups, respectively, which might not provide adequate power to detect the difference in hard clinical outcomes, such as in-hospital mortality. Second, the retrospective and observational design might introduce bias to the study results. Although the baseline characteristics were comparable between the two groups, other unmeasured confounders might still be present and influence the study results. Third, whether the reduction in the D2W time was due to the decrease in the time to make the cath lab ready (the response time of the PCI team) could not be confirmed because the five components of the D2W time in the control group were not available, although the implementation of the 24/7 on-site PCI team strategy aimed at reducing the time to make the cath lab ready. Fourth, the single-centre setting may limit the generalisability of the study findings. Fifth, the obviously labour-consuming nature of the 24/7 on-site PCI team strategy might challenge the feasibility of this strategy in clinical practice. Sixth, data on other outcome indicators, such as the incidence or severity of heart failure or left ventricular ejection fraction, were not available for all patients owing to the retrospective nature of this study, which precluded the possibility to show the potential benefit of the 24/7 on-site PCI team strategy based on these surrogate outcomes. Lastly, the clinical effect of the 24/7 on-site PCI team strategy was beyond the scope of this study, although shorter D2W time would be associated with better clinical outcomes in patients with STEMI.

Conclusions

Based on the results of this study, implementing a 24/7 on-site PCI team strategy was associated with reductions in both the D2W time and the rate of delayed PCI in patients with STEMI undergoing primary PCI, especially in those presenting during off-hours, but at the cost of increased on-site time of the PCI team members. Shortening the time to obtain ECG and the time to make the diagnosis of STEMI might further reduce the D2W time and the rate of delayed PCI after implementing the 24/7 on-site PCI team strategy.

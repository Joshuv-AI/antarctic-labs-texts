Workflow-oriented learning curve analysis of laparoscopic distal pancreatectomy: step-specific associations of operative maturation
Authors: Kun Su, Renchao Zou, Xiawei Yang, Tao Wu, Jie Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe learning curve of laparoscopic distal pancreatectomy (LDP) has traditionally been assessed using global operative indicators such as operative time and blood loss. However, little is known about how technical performance evolves during individual operative steps. This study aimed to perform a workflow-oriented learning curve analysis of LDP using cumulative sum (CUSUM) methodology and step-specific operative assessment.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed 58 consecutive patients who underwent LDP between January 2023 and December 2025 at a high-volume pancreatic center. Operative workflow was divided into predefined operative steps according to anatomical landmarks. CUSUM analysis based on operative time was used to identify the transition from the initial phases to the proficiency phase.ResultsCUSUM analysis identified a transition point at approximately Case 29. Mean operative time decreased from 77 ± 13 min in the early phase to 54 ± 14 min in the late phase (P < 0.001). The steepest reductions occurred during pancreatic mobilization (31 ± 10 vs. 17 ± 6 min, P < 0.001) and splenic vessel dissection (24 ± 9 vs. 12 ± 4 min, P < 0.001). Estimated blood loss also declined significantly (39 ± 18 vs. 21 ± 11 mL, P = 0.002), while postoperative pancreatic fistula rates and overall complication rates remained comparable between phases.ConclusionsIn this cohort, reductions in operative time during LDP were concentrated in anatomically complex dissection steps rather than uniform acceleration of the entire procedure. Step-specific analysis describes temporal patterns that characterize technical change during minimally invasive pancreatic surgery.

Introduction

Laparoscopic distal pancreatectomy (LDP) is now widely accepted as a minimally invasive approach for lesions of the pancreatic body and tail because it is associated with reduced blood loss, shorter hospitalization, and faster postoperative recovery [1–4]. Nevertheless, the procedure remains technically challenging due to the deep retroperitoneal location of the pancreas and its close relationship to major vascular structures [5–8].

Previous studies evaluating the learning curve of LDP have mainly focused on global operative indicators, including operative time, blood loss, conversion rate, and postoperative complications [6–8]. Although these measures are clinically relevant, they provide limited information regarding which operative steps improve most during increasing surgical experience.

From a technical standpoint, LDP consists of several operative steps with different levels of complexity. Pancreatic mobilization, retropancreatic dissection, and splenic vessel dissection often require stable exposure, careful traction, and precise laparoscopic handling [9–12]. In daily practice, these technically demanding phases are frequently the most affected by increasing operative familiarity.

More recently, increasing attention has been directed toward phase-specific assessment of minimally invasive surgery. Rather than evaluating the procedure as a single entity, this approach examines how operative performance changes during individual operative steps. However, data regarding step-specific learning patterns during LDP remain limited.

We therefore analyzed the LDP learning curve using cumulative sum (CUSUM) methodology, a sequential analysis technique that plots the cumulative deviation of individual observations from a reference value combined with step-specific operative assessment to identify the phases most closely related to technical improvement.

Methods

Study design and setting

We retrospectively reviewed consecutive patients who underwent LDP between January 2023 and December 2025 at the Department of Hepatobiliary and Pancreatic Surgery, The Second Affiliated Hospital of Kunming Medical University. This high-volume tertiary center performs more than 200 pancreatic resections annually.

Patients

Inclusion criteria were: (1) age between 18 and 75 years; and (2) pancreatic body or tail lesions requiring distal pancreatectomy. Exclusion criteria included: (1) conversion to open surgery; (2) extended resections, including total pancreatectomy or multivisceral resection; and (3) incomplete perioperative data; and (4) pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC), because the primary objective was to evaluate the technical learning curve of LDP per se, without the confounding effects of oncologic complexity (e.g., extended resection margins, lymphadenectomy extent, neoadjuvant therapy effects) on operative time and step-specific performance. All procedures were performed by a single experienced pancreatic surgical team led by senior surgeon(J.H.) who had performed > 500 open pancreatic resections and > 200 laparoscopic pancreatic procedures (including 80 LDPs) prior to the commencement of this study in January 2023. Spleen-preserving distal pancreatectomy was attempted whenever technically feasible. A standardized operative workflow was applied consistently throughout the study period to minimize procedural variability.

Ethics

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of The Second Affiliated Hospital of Kunming Medical University (Approval No. 2025-LCYJ-0091). The requirement for informed consent was waived by the IRB because of the retrospective nature of the study and the use of de-identified clinical data, in accordance with the Regulations on Ethical Review of Biomedical Research Involving Humans (Order No. 11 of the National Health Commission of the People’s Republic of China, 2023).

Surgical procedure

All procedures were performed under general anesthesia using a standardized multi-port laparoscopic approach. The operative workflow included entry into the lesser sac, pancreatic mobilization, development of the retropancreatic plane, splenic vessel dissection, and pancreatic transection.

Although minor technical modifications were introduced during the study period, the overall operative sequence and surgical strategy remained unchanged.

Learning curve definition

The learning curve was evaluated using CUSUM analysis based on operative time. For each case, the CUSUM value was calculated as the cumulative difference between the individual operative time and the mean operative time of the entire cohort [13].

Sequential CUSUM values were plotted to evaluate changes in operative performance over time and to identify the transition from the initial learning phase to the later proficiency phase. The inflection point at approximately Case 29 was determined by visual inspection of the CUSUM curve combined with segmented linear regression analysis using the “segmented” package in R (version 4.3.1). The breakpoint was identified as the case number at which the slope of the CUSUM curve changed most significantly, confirmed by a Chow test for structural break (P < 0.001). Based on the inflection point of the CUSUM curve, patients were divided into an early phase (Cases 1–29) and a late phase (Cases 30–58).

Step-specific operative analysis

For step-specific analysis, the procedure was divided into predefined operative steps according to anatomical landmarks. Pancreatic mobilization time was defined as the interval from entry into the lesser sac to completion of posterior pancreatic mobilization [14]. Splenic vessel dissection time was defined as the time required for exposure and dissection of the splenic artery and splenic vein [15].

Operative times for each step were recorded intraoperatively by a dedicated surgical assistant (first assistant or scrub nurse) using a synchronized digital timer, with landmarks verified by the operating surgeon. The timer was started at the commencement of each defined anatomical step and stopped upon completion, as verbally confirmed by the surgeon. All time measurements were prospectively documented in the operative record at the time of surgery, not retrospectively extracted from video review. The definitions were applied consistently throughout the study period by the same surgical team, with inter-rater reliability ensured through standardized training at the beginning of the study period.

Outcome measures

The primary outcome was operative time, defined as skin-to-skin time and recorded by the circulating nurse using standardized operative documentation. Secondary outcomes included estimated blood loss, postoperative pancreatic fistula (POPF), postoperative complications, length of postoperative hospital stay, and 30-day readmission. POPF was defined and graded according to the 2016 International Study Group on Pancreatic Surgery (ISGPS) criteria [16]. Postoperative complications were classified according to the Clavien – Dindo classification system.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation, whereas categorical variables are expressed as numbers and percentages. Comparisons between groups were performed using Student’s t-test or the Mann–Whitney U test for continuous variables and the χ² test or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables, as appropriate.

CUSUM analysis was performed using operative time as the primary learning variable. Linear regression analysis was additionally used to evaluate the relationship between operative sequence and operative duration during individual operative steps.

A two-sided P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

Results

Patient characteristics

During the study period (January 2023 – December 2025), a total of 64 patients underwent distal pancreatectomy at our institution. Of these, 6 patients (9.4%) were excluded due to conversion to open surgery: 3 cases for uncontrolled bleeding from the splenic vein, 2 cases for dense tumor adhesion to the splenic hilum, and 1 case for intraoperative discovery of portal vein involvement. Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) cases were additionally excluded to isolate technical learning effects from oncologic complexity (see Methods). A total of 58 consecutive patients who underwent laparoscopic distal pancreatectomy (LDP) were included in the analysis. Baseline clinicopathological characteristics are summarized in Table 1.

Global operative outcomes

All 58 included procedures were completed laparoscopically. The mean operative time was 62 ± 18 min, and the mean estimated blood loss was 30 ± 20 mL. No intraoperative transfusions or perioperative mortality occurred.

Learning curve analysis

CUSUM analysis showed a gradual improvement in operative performance over time (Fig. 1). The inflection point at Case 29 was determined by visual inspection of the CUSUM curve combined with segmented linear regression analysis using the “segmented” package in R (version 4.3.1). The breakpoint was identified as the case number at which the slope of the CUSUM curve changed most significantly, confirmed by a Chow test for structural break (P < 0.001).

Fig. 1CUSUM learning curve of laparoscopic distal pancreatectomy based on operative time

CUSUM learning curve of laparoscopic distal pancreatectomy based on operative time

After this transition point, operative time became shorter and less variable. Mean operative time decreased significantly from 77 ± 13 min during the early phase to 54 ± 14 min during the later phase (P < 0.001). Estimated blood loss also decreased significantly between phases (39 ± 18 vs. 21 ± 11 mL, P = 0.002).

Step-specific operative analysis

Process-based analysis demonstrated that improvements in operative efficiency were not uniformly distributed throughout the procedure. Instead, the most pronounced temporal reductions in operative duration were observed during technically demanding exposure-dependent procedural phases.

Pancreatic mobilization time decreased significantly from 31 ± 10 min in the early phase to 17 ± 6 min in the late phase (P < 0.001). Similarly, splenic vessel dissection time decreased from 24 ± 9 min to 12 ± 4 min in the late phase (P < 0.001) (Fig. 2). Detailed comparisons are presented in Table 3.

The cumulative sum (CUSUM) curve demonstrated progressive operative maturation during the study period. The peak of the curve occurred at approximately Case 29, indicating the transition from the initial learning phase to the subsequent proficiency phase.

The most pronounced reductions in operative time were observed during pancreatic mobilization and splenic vessel dissection, suggesting that operative efficiency improvements during the learning curve were concentrated in exposure-dependent procedural phases.

In contrast, relatively standardized procedural components demonstrated comparatively smaller temporal changes during the learning process. These findings suggest that operative maturation during LDP appeared to be associated primarily with progressive optimization of anatomically complex dissection phases rather than uniform acceleration of the entire procedure.

Postoperative outcomes

The overall POPF rate was 36.2%, consisting predominantly of biochemical leaks (25.9%) with only 6 Grade B fistulas (10.3%) according to the updated ISGPS classification. No Grade C fistulas were observed. Overall postoperative complication rates were comparable between the early and late phases. However, postoperative hospital stay was significantly shorter in the late phase (6.5 ± 2.5 vs. 9.2 ± 3.1 days, P = 0.001) (Table 3). The 30-day readmission rate was comparable between phases (2/29 [6.9%] vs. 1/29 [3.4%], P = 0.61), with reasons including postoperative ileus (2 cases, early phase) and wound infection (1 case, late phase). The reduced operative duration in the late phase was not accompanied by increased morbidity or readmission, indicating that improved efficiency was achieved without compromising safety.

Discussion

The present study showed that reductions in operative time during laparoscopic distal pancreatectomy were not uniformly distributed throughout the procedure. The most pronounced temporal reductions were observed during pancreatic mobilization and splenic vessel dissection, which are among the most technically demanding parts of the operation. These findings describe step-specific temporal patterns associated with increasing case experience in this cohort.

Most previous studies that evaluated the learning curve of minimally invasive pancreatic surgery have relied mainly on overall operative indicators, including operative time, blood loss, conversion rate, and postoperative complications [6, 10, 18]. Although these measures are clinically relevant, they do not fully explain how individual operative steps change during increasing surgical experience. Several recent studies have examined learning curves in LDP using CUSUM analysis. Ricci et al. reported a learning curve of approximately 40 cases for LDP, with operative time and blood loss stabilizing after this point [7]. Fung et al., in a systematic review of minimally invasive pancreatic surgery, identified substantial heterogeneity in learning curve definitions and endpoints across studies [8]. Shin et al. demonstrated that surgeon volume and case selection significantly influenced learning curve assessments [13]. Our step-specific approach extends these findings by identifying which operative phases account for the greatest temporal reduction, thereby offering a more granular description of technical change.

The learning curve inflection point identified in this study (Case 29) is notably earlier than the approximately 40 cases reported by Ricci et al. [7] and the 30–50 case range summarized in recent systematic reviews [8]. This discrepancy likely reflects the senior surgeon’s substantial prior experience (> 80 LDPs before the study period), which positioned the observed curve in a phase of technical refinement rather than initial skill acquisition. Consequently, the step-specific temporal reductions described here may not be generalizable to surgeons in the very early phase of LDP learning. The applicability boundaries of this study are defined by the surgeon’s advanced proficiency level; the observed patterns reflect optimization of established technique rather than de novo acquisition of procedural competence.

In our experience, pancreatic mobilization and splenic vessel dissection were the operative steps most affected by increasing operative familiarity. These phases require stable visualization, coordinated traction, and precise laparoscopic dissection within a limited retroperitoneal space. Temporal reductions in these steps likely reflect progressive familiarity with anatomical planes and laparoscopic tissue handling. By contrast, relatively standardized operative steps such as entry into the lesser sac and pancreatic transection showed comparatively smaller temporal changes. This observation suggests that the temporal evolution of operative duration in this cohort was concentrated in technically demanding dissection phases.

The present findings may inform the focus of training efforts. Conventional learning curve studies evaluate laparoscopic distal pancreatectomy as a single procedure. Identifying which operative steps account for the greatest temporal reduction may allow more targeted training design. However, the effectiveness of focused simulation or video-based coaching for these specific phases warrants evaluation in structured training studies.

We also observed reduced blood loss and shorter postoperative hospitalization during the later phase, with no corresponding increase in postoperative morbidity. Postoperative pancreatic fistula rates remained comparable between phases. The 30-day readmission rate was also comparable between phases (2/29 [6.9%] vs. 1/29 [3.4%], P = 0.61). These findings are consistent with prior reports that describe stable complication profiles during technical maturation in minimally invasive pancreatic surgery [7, 10].

Our findings are relevant to current efforts in surgical workflow analysis and video-based assessment during minimally invasive surgery [19, 20]. Future integration of computer vision techniques and automated operative phase recognition may further refine objective assessment of technical performance during pancreatic surgery. However, we acknowledge that operative time serves as a surrogate rather than direct measure of surgical quality. Other important metrics, including intraoperative complications, quality of pancreatic transection, lymph node yield (in oncologic cases), and long-term functional outcomes, were not systematically evaluated in this study. Future learning curve analyses should incorporate composite effectiveness measures, such as textbook outcomes or video-based technical assessment, to provide a more comprehensive evaluation of operative maturation.

The apparent discrepancy between the operative times reported in this study (mean 62 min) and those in our previously published technical paper on laparoscopic spleen-preserving distal pancreatectomy (LSPDP) (> 200 min) [9] reflects differences in technique selection and temporal context. The technical paper described a specific Warshaw-type LSPDP involving radical resection of the pancreatic tail with splenic vessel ligation, which is inherently more complex due to extensive retroperitoneal dissection. In contrast, the current study includes both Warshaw and Kimura techniques, with the majority being Kimura-type procedures (splenic vessel preservation), which are technically less demanding. Additionally, the technical paper represented early experience with a specific novel technique, whereas the current study spans a three-year period with progressive technical refinement.

Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) cases were excluded from this cohort because the primary objective was to evaluate the technical learning curve of LDP per se, without the confounding effects of oncologic complexity (e.g., extended resection margins, lymphadenectomy extent, neoadjuvant therapy effects) on operative time and step-specific performance. We acknowledge that prior studies have demonstrated an increasing rate of complex procedures, including PDAC, as surgeons progress along the learning curve [7, 8]. The exclusion of PDAC cases may limit generalizability to oncologic LDP practice and represents a trade-off between methodological specificity and clinical comprehensiveness.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, this was a retrospective single-center study with a relatively small sample size. Second, all procedures were performed by a single experienced pancreatic surgical team using a standardized operative strategy, which may limit generalizability to other centers. Third, objective video-based evaluation of operative exposure and technical quality was not available. Fourth, the exclusion of converted cases (6/64, 9.4%) likely introduced a selection bias that underestimated the true difficulty of the early learning phase, as conversion is more probable during initial cases. Similarly, the exclusion of PDAC cases removed a subset of complex procedures that typically increase in frequency as surgeons gain confidence. These exclusions limit generalizability to unselected clinical practice and may have artificially flattened the observed learning curve. Fifth, PDAC cases were excluded to isolate the technical learning curve from oncologic complexity, which may limit generalizability to oncologic practice. Sixth, the CUSUM analysis used the mean operative time of the entire cohort as the reference standard, rather than an external benchmark from a high-volume center. While internal reference is commonly used in single-center learning curve studies, an external benchmark would provide a more robust standard for comparison and should be considered in future multi-center analyses [13]. Seventh, operative time serves as a surrogate rather than direct measure of surgical quality; it does not capture intraoperative judgment, tissue handling quality, or long-term oncologic outcomes. Finally, because this study focused primarily on exploratory step-specific analysis, causal relationships between individual technical maneuvers and operative performance cannot be definitively established. These observational findings describe temporal patterns associated with increasing case experience, although causal relationships cannot be established in this retrospective design.

Future research should address the current limitations through multi-center designs incorporating surgeons at varied experience levels, prospective inclusion of oncologic cases (including PDAC), and integration of video-based performance metrics. Automated operative phase recognition and computer vision assessment of technical quality may further refine step-specific learning curve analysis, enabling objective, scalable evaluation of surgical skill acquisition.

Despite these limitations, the present study describes step-specific temporal patterns during the learning process in laparoscopic distal pancreatectomy. Evaluating individual operative phases offers an analytical approach for characterizing how technical proficiency evolves during minimally invasive pancreatic surgery.

Conclusions

In this cohort, reductions in operative time during laparoscopic distal pancreatectomy were concentrated in technically demanding dissection steps (pancreatic mobilization and splenic vessel dissection) rather than uniformly distributed across the procedure. These observational findings describe step-specific temporal patterns associated with increasing case experience. CUSUM-based phase identification combined with workflow-oriented outcome assessment characterizes technical change during minimally invasive pancreatic surgery.

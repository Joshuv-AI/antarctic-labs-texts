Coal Cryogenic Treatment Temperature Response Model under Multiphase Coupling Effect
Authors: Shanxue Zhang, Zhaofeng Wang, Daopeng Fan, Yang Qiu, Yanqi Chen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The coal is affected by the latent heat of the phase change of in situ and migrating water and the exothermic heat of gas adsorbed by the coal during the freezing process, which leads to different temperatures at different locations and times inside the coal. Relying on the independently developed simulation platform for the freezing response characteristics of gas-containing coal, simulation experiments on the internal temperature change of the coal freezing process under different ambient cryogenic treatment temperatures were carried out, and the effects of the phase change latent heat of the in situ water and migrating water and the exothermic heat of gas adsorbed by the coal on the freezing coal temperature field were taken into account, so as to establish a temperature field model of the cryogenic treatment process of the coal under the influence of the thermal effect of the water and gas and construct the internal heat transfer model of the freezing coal with the aid of COMSOL. The internal heat transfer of frozen coal was constructed with the help of COMSOL, and the mathematical model of temperature field proposed in this paper was simulated and verified. The results show that the change in temperature with time in the coal cryogenic treatment process is consistent with the experimental law. It is generally divided into four stages: rapid decline, short stabilization, slow decline, and relative stability, and the maximum error between the simulation temperature and the experimentally measured temperature is 0.85 K. The rate of temperature decrease of coal during the cryogenic treatment process is accelerated with the decrease of ambient freezing temperature, and the duration of short stabilization of temperature is shortened in the stage of water phase change. The mathematical model proposed in this article can be used to simulate and characterize the temperature field distribution and changes during the cryogenic treatment process of water-containing gas-containing coal.

Introduction

Coal-based fossil fuels are a significant part of China’s energy structure.1,2 However, the geological conditions of coal mines are extremely complex. As the mining level deepens, the coal seam presents high gas pressure, high ground stress, and low permeability, increasing the severity and likelihood of coal and gas disasters, thus making it extremely difficult to extract gas from coal seams. Gas extraction technology cannot be well utilized.3−5 Research on the mechanism of coal and gas outbursts indicates that the strength of coal is an important factor in resisting coal and gas outbursts. Existing coal and gas outburst prevention and control measures mainly focus on reducing the coal seam storage energy and do not pay enough attention to improving the strength of the coal. From the analysis of typical cases of rock cross-cut coal uncovering and gas outbursts in geological tectonic areas, although the construction of a large number of extraction boreholes can reduce the gas pressure in the coal uncovering site or geological tectonic area, it will cause the strength of the coal to be further reduced, which increases the possibility of the occurrence of low-index gas outbursts. Therefore, reducing the strength of coal is the key problem in preventing and controlling coal and gas outbursts.

Cryogenic shrinkage can improve the strength of objects, and after gas extraction, injecting water into the coal uncovering site or geological structure area and freezing it can reduce the coal’s storage energy and improve the coal’s durability. When it comes to localized anti-surge work in rock cross-cut coal uncovering, traditional methods can take up to 10 months, particularly for soft coal seams with high construction costs. While the cryogenic freezing technology can realize freezing in 2 months, which can quickly and safely carry out the coal uncovering in rock cross-cut and produce great indirect economic benefits.6 Rock and coal’s mechanical properties change after freezing at low temperatures.7−9 Neaupane et al.10 proposed a thermal-hydraulic-mechanical (THM) model for simulating laboratory freeze–thaw tests on rocks, and the results indicated a better prediction of temperature transfer and deformation behavior within the elastic deformation limit range. Feng11,12 and Xie13,14 carried out experimental studies and numerical simulations on the freezing temperature field of the rock cross-cut uncovered coal protruding coal seam and found that the freezing zone forms a frozen coal cylinder centered in the center of the freezing pipe. Numerical simulation shows that the closer to the center, the faster the temperature of the coal seam in the freezing zone decreases; for the same freezing time, the temperature of the coal seam is the lowest at the vertical middle-row freezing pipe section and the horizontal middle-row freezing pipe section. Zhai15 and Zhou16 used nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) technology to test and analyze the change of unfrozen water in coal samples during the process of cryogenic freezing rock cross-cut coal uncovering. They analyzed the temperature and stress characteristics of coal samples in the case of freezing over time using temperature measurements, infrared thermography, and uniaxial compression. By establishing the coupled heat flow solid (THM) equation, Liu found that at the same freezing time, the farther away from the freezing hole, the slower the temperature change, which is of guiding significance for the study of the evolution law of the temperature field of the frozen coal seam.17 Based on the heat transfer theory, Liu used COMSOL software to simulate the cold shock test of coal in the process of liquid nitrogen cold immersion to obtain the variation law of the temperature field of coal samples: under the conditions of liquid nitrogen cold immersion, the temperature field of the coal sample is in the form of a wave propagation, with the increase of the distance, the temperature field is gradually reduced; the closer the drying of the coal samples to the boundary surface of the liquid nitrogen, the greater the speed of the temperature shock and the shorter the time required to reach the steady state.18 Liu established a model of freezing and deformation as well as a theoretical expression for the unfrozen water content inside cryogenic rocks.19,20 Cui studied the relationship between freezing depth and freezing expansion force, used a temperature testing machine to monitor the internal temperature change law of the surrounding rock, and after numerical simulation, simulated the surrounding rock’s freezing expansion force by the ambient temperature change.21 Liu studied the effect of water content on sandstone freezing strength, the increase of water content led to the sandstone strength after freezing in the gradual decrease, sandstone stress after freezing with the increase of water content and increase; after freezing, the specimen pore space has a significant increase leading to its strength decrease.22 Perkins and Kern used numerical simulation to calculate that the flow of coal bed methane in the direction of the temperature is low, and the change in the coal bed methane temperature and thermal stresses in coal beds are generated by gas flow.23 When the lower temperature fluid is injected into the coalbed methane well, the stress of the borehole wall changes greatly, resulting in large cracks and pores in the coalbed methane borehole wall, which increases the coalbed methane flowability.24 However, the current research on frozen coal’s temperature and mechanical properties lacks the analysis and exploration of gas-containing coal.25 In the freezing process of water-containing gas-containing coal, the phase change heat released from the phase change of water into ice within the coal and the adsorption heat released during gas adsorption have a delayed effect on the temperature drop of the coal. The temperature changes in the internal temperature field during the freezing process of coal need to be further investigated.

Therefore, the test coal samples in this study were selected from Baiping coal mine of Zheng Coal Group, and the temperature field mathematical model obtained by the experimental test and COMSOL simulation calculation was carried out through the temperature response characteristic test system in the cryogenic treatment process. The measured and simulation calculation results were compared and verified, and the internal temperature variation law in the process of coal cryogenic treatment was obtained. This work aims to investigate the impact of water phase change heat and gas adsorption heat on the internal temperature change of coal during the cryogenic treatment process, thereby establishing a crucial theoretical foundation for the subsequent enhancement of coal strength. Additionally, it explores the use of freezing technology for anti-outburst applications in the rock cross-cut coal uncovering and the geologic tectonic zone and other locations.

Experimental System and Testing Methods

Testing System

In this work, a high and low temperature frequency conversion test system was used for testing. The device primarily consists of a special coal sample tank, a vacuum degassing system, an inflatable quantitative system, a water injection (humidification) system, a cryogenic treatment system, and a data acquisition and processing system. Through the built-in temperature sensor of the special coal sample tank, real-time monitoring of the coal sample temperature in the coal sample tank is realized.

A temperature response device was built to simulate the cryogenic treatment process of water-containing and gas-containing coals, and cold and heat sources were arranged outside the coal sample tanks to simulate the variable-temperature environment of the coal samples during the cryogenic treatment process. The temperatures of the high and low temperature alternating instrument were set to 273, 268, 263, and 253 K, respectively, to simulate the environmental cryogenic treatment temperature of coal samples in the cryogenic treatment process. The temperature response characteristics were studied under the conditions of water contents of 0% and 8% and gas adsorption equilibrium pressures of 0 and 0.74 MPa. Coal Sample Preparation and Parameter Determination

The experimental coal samples used in this article were taken from the Baiping coal mine of Zheng Coal Group. The designed production capacity of the Baiping Coal Mine in Dengfeng City is 1.8 million t/a. The mine is a coal and gas outburst mine, and the gas content of the coal seam at the working face ranges from 1.87 to 15.78 m3/t.26 The mine is located in Donghua Town, Dengfeng City, Henan Province, adjacent to the Xindeng Shaft Field in the east, the Xinxin Shaft Field in the west, and 26 km from Dengfeng City. Because coal and gas outburst often occurs in cross-cut coal uncovering and geological structure areas, it is very easy to have outburst danger in the soft coal state. Combined with the law of the outburst site, the fresh soft stratified II1 coal in Baiping Coal Mine is taken as the experimental coal sample. The coal sample has a high degree of damage, and its strength and particle size composition are similar to those of the briquette. A pulverizer crushed the obtained coal samples, and the coal particles with a particle size of 0.5–1 mm and below 0.5 mm were selected and uniformly mixed at a ratio of 1:2. A small amount of distilled water was added to uniformly stir the coal samples to bond. The coal samples were uniformly stirred into a self-made mold, placed on a loader, and pressed for 50 min at a pressure of 200 kN. Finally, the φ 100 mm × 200 mm briquette required for the test is made, as shown in Figure 2. Some basic parameters of the experimental coal samples are listed in Table 1.

Mathematical Modeling of Temperature Field Distribution inside the Coal

During the cryogenic treatment process of coal, not only the pore water freezes in situ and undergoes a phase change but also the unfrozen water inside the coal migrates from the unfrozen area to the freezing front under the action of a negative temperature gradient, accumulates, and undergoes a phase change, which releases a huge amount of latent heat of phase change and has an impact on the temperature field. In this article, a coal temperature field model considering the migrating hydrothermal effect is established by the appropriate simplification and assumption conditions.

Test Results and Analysis

Internal Temperature Change of Gas-Free and Water-Free Coal

In order to analyze the influence of water phase change heat and gas adsorption heat on the internal temperature change of coal, this article first analyzes the internal temperature response characteristics of coal without water and gas during freezing. The experimental results are shown in Figure 4.

Temperature change of 1/4R in gas-free and water-free coal at 263 K freezing temperature.

As shown in Figure 4, taking the environmental freezing temperature of 263 K as an example, the internal temperature of coal without water and gas in the cryogenic treatment process shows the law of rapid decline in the early stage, slow decline in the middle stage, and stability in the later stage. That is because in the early stage of the cryogenic treatment process, the temperature difference between the coal and the external environment is significant, the temperature gradient is large, and the heat transfer in the coal sample causes the internal temperature to decrease rapidly. With an increase in the internal temperature of the coal, the temperature gradient inside and outside the coal is reduced so that the internal temperature of the coal decreases slowly. When the temperature difference between the inside and outside of the coal is zero, the internal temperature of the coal reaches the set ambient temperature and no longer changes.

Internal Temperature Change of Water-Containing Coal without Gas

Changes in the internal temperature field of the coal during freezing of the coal without gas and water are shown in Figures 5 and 6.

Temperature change of water-containing gas-free coal at 1/4R in coal at 268 K and below freezing temperature.

Temperature change of water-containing gas-free coal at 1/4R in coal at 273 K freezing temperature.

As shown in Figure 5, when the external cryogenic treatment temperature is 268 K and below, the internal temperature change of the coal presents four stages: rapid decline, short stabilization, slow decline, and leveling off. The significant temperature difference between the inside and outside of the coal drove the temperature inside the coal to drop rapidly in the early stage. As the freezing process continued, the internal water of the coal started to undergo a phase change to release the phase change heat when it dropped to about 273 K, which had an obvious delay effect on the temperature drop of the coal. The unfrozen water inside the coal sample decreases, the heat released by the water phase change becomes less, the influence of the external ambient temperature dominates, and the speed of heat transfer inside the coal slows, resulting in a slower temperature drop inside the coal sample compared with the previous period. Finally, all of the water inside the coal is frozen, and the internal temperature of the coal tends to the ambient freezing temperature.

As shown in Figure 6, at a temperature of 273 K, there are only three stages in the internal temperature change of water-containing gas-free coal: rapid decline, slow decline, and stabilization. In the early stage of the test, the external environment temperature of the coal sample dropped sharply to 273 K, and a considerable temperature difference was formed inside and outside the coal. Driven by the temperature difference, the internal temperature of the coal sample decreased rapidly. When the internal temperature of the coal sample is close to 273 K, the internal water of the coal sample does not reach the frozen state, and most of the water exists in the form of liquid water. The heat generated by the water phase turning into ice is minimal, and the hysteresis effect on the temperature drop of the coal is not apparent.

Tables 3 and 4 demonstrate that when the ambient cryogenic treatment temperature of the same type of coal sample drops, each stage of temperature change at the 1/4R of the coal begins earlier and lasts shorter, resulting in a faster temperature drop at the 1/4R of the coal sample. The lower the ambient temperature is, the faster the temperature inside the coal decreases because the lower the ambient cryogenic treatment temperature is, the more significant the temperature difference between inside and outside the coal is, which makes the heat transfer inside the coal sample faster, that is, the faster the temperature inside the coal decreases. The lower the ambient cryogenic treatment temperature of the same kind of coal samples, the earlier the appearance of the second stage of short-term stabilization and the shorter the duration. The lower the ambient cryogenic treatment temperature is, the faster the temperature inside the coal decreases and the earlier it reaches 273 K. The heat generated by the water phase change is not obvious enough to affect the temperature drop, which results in a shorter time for the short-term stabilization of the temperature inside the coal.

Internal Temperature Change of Water-Containing Coal Containing Gas

When freezing at 268 K and below, the temperature change of the gas-containing coal also has four stages: rapid decrease, short stabilization, slow decrease, and leveling off, as shown in Figure 7. In Figure 8, when freezing in a 273 K environment, the internal temperature change of gas-containing coal also has only three stages of rapid decrease, slow decrease, and leveling off. From Tables 3–6, compared with the gas-free coal samples, the internal temperature of the gas-containing coal has obvious differences mainly in the two stages: rapid decline and short stabilization: the rapid decline stage starts earlier, the rate of decline slows down and lasts for a more extended period of time, and the duration in the short stabilization stage is longer than that of the non-gas-containing coal. The main reason for this is that the ambient temperature change breaks the original adsorption equilibrium state of the coal samples. The lower temperature enhances the adsorption performance of the coal samples, and the free gas in the coal turns into adsorbed gas to release heat, which slows the rate of temperature drop of the coal. The lower the cryogenic treatment freezing temperature, the more significant the temperature difference between the inside and outside of the coal samples and the faster the temperature drops in the coal; coal adsorption of gas released adsorption heat and water phase into ice released phase change heat for the rapid decline in the internal temperature of the coal of the hysteresis effect is not apparent, so in the second stage of the temperature stabilization, the shorter the duration; at the same time, the lower the ambient cryogenic treatment temperature, the coal samples, the earlier the internal temperature drop to 273 K. At that time the water begins to phase change and generate latent heat of phase change, resulting in the earlier the temperature stabilization in the second stage. The duration of the third stage is also positively correlated with the freezing temperature; that is, the lower the ambient cryogenic treatment temperature is, the shorter the duration of the third stage of slow decline because most of the liquid water has already changed into ice to give out the heat of phase change, and there is only a small amount of unfrozen water left, which has very little exothermic heat of phase change; it will soon reach the set temperature of the test and then enter into the fourth stage where the temperature tends to be stabilized.

Numerical Simulation and Analysis of Internal Temperature Change in Cryogenic Treatment Process of Gas-Containing Coal

Currently, it is hard to test the existence of three phases of coal, water, and gas at different temperatures in the field. However, we can use a numerical simulation that is economical, reasonable, convenient, and quick, in conjunction with laboratory data, to better illustrate the importance of the appropriate temperature for the artificial freezing process. Three physical fields, temperature, water, and gas-containing coal fields, are established to be coupled to validate the proposed model.

Establishment of Coal Pillar Finite Element Model

COMSOL was used to establish the finite element model of the coal column, and heat simulation calculation was carried out for the coal column, which has a diameter of 100 mm, a height of 200 mm, and a water content of 8%. The model is divided into 11,458 cells, of which the minimum cell mass is 0.2277 and the average cell mass is 0.665, and the specific grid division is shown in Figure 9. The phase change heat transfer module and the heat source module were added to the COMSOL solid heat transfer module and the mathematical model equations from Section 4 were written into the corresponding modules. The initial conditions refer to the initial temperature field in the freezing experiment of Konrad (1998), that is, the initial temperature of the coal sample is 298 K. The boundary conditions are set according to the experimental conditions, and the ambient cryogenic treatment temperatures are set to 273, 268, 263, and 253 K, respectively. Numerical Simulation of Internal Temperature Change of Gas-Containing Coal under Different Cryogenic Treatment Temperatures

The temperature change model in the coal freezing process proposed in this paper was used to calculate the previous coal cryogenic treatment test and compare it with the measured values given in the experiment. In order to study the variation rule of internal temperature of coal under different ambient cryogenic treatment temperatures, the internal temperature distribution diagrams of coal samples with 8% water content and 0.74 MPa adsorption equilibrium pressure of coal samples were established under different ambient cryogenic treatment environments (273, 268, 263, and 253 K) at different moments (1, 2, 3, and 4 h) as shown in Figure 10.

Temperature distribution of gas-containing coal under different cryogenic treatment temperatures.

Figure 10 illustrates that the heat conduction law of gas-containing coal remains consistent across various ambient cryogenic treatment temperatures. The coal wall continuously transfers ambient cold to the coal center. The internal temperature distribution of the coal sample is gradually reduced from the outermost periphery of the coal sample to the center of the coal sample, and the temperature gradient is obvious. The temperature of all parts of the coal sample decreases with an increment of time. At the early cooling stage, free gas existed in the gap between the coal sample and the canister and the thermal conductivity of methane was much lower than that of the coal sample, which reduced the thermal conductivity efficiency to a certain extent. At the cryogenic treatment time of 1 h, the temperature gradient of the coal sample is most apparent, the center temperature of the coal sample is gradually decreasing, and the edge temperature of the coal sample is closest to the external cold source, which is the first to be cooled to the set cryogenic treatment temperature. With the continuous prolongation of time, driven by the huge temperature difference, the temperature was continuously transferred from the surface of the coal samples to the inside of the coal samples, which made the internal temperatures of the coal samples to be 273.09 268.05, 263.03, and 253.2 K after 4 h of cryogenic treatment, respectively.

Conclusions

(1)The latent heat of phase change of in situ and migrating water and the exothermic heat of adsorbed gas in coal have a hysteresis effect on how the temperature field in coal changes and spreads out when it is frozen. During the cryogenic treatment process, as the ambient cryogenic treatment temperature decreases, the rate of temperature decrease accelerates, and the duration of the short stabilization phase is shortened. For the same coal samples and water content, the rate of temperature decrease during cryogenic treatment was lower for gas-containing coals than for gas-free coals.(2)For the cryogenic treatment process, coal is considered a low-temperature expanding material. A coal cryogenic treatment temperature field model for the thermal effect of water gas is established by considering the effects of the latent heat of phase change of in situ water and migrating water and the exothermic heat of coal adsorbed gas on the temperature field of the frozen coal.(3)After the simulation calculation results were compared with the experimental tests for verification, the internal temperature drop of the coal obtained by the simulation was consistent with the experimental results obtained under the same conditions indoors, which verified the accuracy and reliability of the internal temperature model of the coal cryogenic treatment process constructed.(4)From the experimental results in the paper, the lower the setting temperature is, the smaller the hysteresis effect of the phase change latent heat of in situ water and migrating water in coal and the exothermic heat of coal adsorbed gas on the distribution and change of temperature field in coal. However, from the engineering practice, the lower the set cryogenic treatment temperature is, the higher the consumption of engineering and labor costs, so it is more reasonable to set the cryogenic treatment temperature to 253 K in the engineering practice of freezing and anti-surge in Baiping coal mine. The mathematical model proposed in this paper can be used to simulate and characterize the distribution and change of temperature field in the cryogenic treatment process of water-containing and gas-containing coal in Baiping Coal Mine, and it also has a certain value of reference for engineering applications in other coal mines.

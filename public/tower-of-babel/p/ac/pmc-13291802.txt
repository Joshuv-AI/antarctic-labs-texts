Asciminib Provides Better Efficacy and Favorable Safety and Tolerability Against Investigator‐Selected Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitors in East Asian Patients With Newly Diagnosed Chronic Myeloid Leukemia: Results From a Subgroup Analysis of the Pivotal ASC4FIRST Study
Authors: Naoto Takahashi, Dong‐Wook Kim, Inho Kim, Xin Du, Sung‐Eun Lee, Yu Hu, Yoshikane Kikushige, Qian Jiang, Yi Wang, Suning Chen, Huanling Zhu, Tatsunori Goto, Akihiro Tomita, Michiko Ichii, Hirohisa Nakamae, Lei Liu, Beini Liu, Kamel Malek, Tong Zhu, Shengyuan Wu, Jianxiang Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTASC4FIRST (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT04971226) is a pivotal phase 3 clinical trial comparing the efficacy and safety of asciminib vs. standard of care (SoC) tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) in newly diagnosed patients with chronic myeloid leukemia in chronic phase (CML‐CP). The study demonstrated superior efficacy and a favorable safety profile for asciminib vs. all current SoC TKIs; this subgroup analysis involved 138 patients from East Asian sites enrolled in ASC4FIRST. Patients were randomized to receive either asciminib or investigator‐selected TKIs (IS‐TKIs: imatinib, bosutinib, dasatinib, or nilotinib) following stratification by risk category and prerandomization‐selected TKI (imatinib or second‐generation TKI). Consistent with non‐East Asian patients, asciminib showed better efficacy in East Asian patients, with a major molecular response (MMR) rate at Week 48 and Week 96 of 64.9% and 77.0% compared to 45.3% and 57.8% for all IS‐TKIs combined, respectively (common treatment difference: 19.5% and 20.0%, respectively). Similar results were obtained for patients with imatinib as the prerandomization‐selected TKI (MMR rate at Week 48 and Week 96: 67.9% and 78.6% for asciminib compared to 30.8% and 46.2% for imatinib, respectively; common treatment difference: 36.5% and 32.7%, respectively). Asciminib also showed a favorable safety profile in East Asian patients, with lower rates of Grade ≥ 3 adverse events (AEs) and AEs leading to discontinuation compared to IS‐TKIs (41.9% vs. 54.0% and 2.7% vs. 9.5%, respectively), consistent with the results from non‐East Asian patients. These findings support the use of asciminib as a first‐line treatment option for East Asian and non‐East Asian patients with CML‐CP.Keywords: asciminib, chronic myeloid leukemia, frontline, imatinib, second generation, tyrosine kinase inhibitor

Introduction

Advances in chronic myeloid leukemia (CML) in the last 20 years have led to dramatic improvements in patient outcomes, including an overall survival that is similar to that of the general population [1]. Most patients with newly diagnosed CML will receive one of the approved ATP‐competitive tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs): imatinib, a first‐generation TKI, or a second‐generation (2G) TKI (e.g., nilotinib, dasatinib, bosutinib). Choosing the most appropriate TKI for first‐line treatment (imatinib or one of the 2G TKIs) involves considering multiple factors related to both the patient and the disease characteristics at the time of treatment initiation [2, 3, 4].

A substantial number of patients with CML who receive first‐line TKIs (15%–25%) do not meet important treatment milestones and discontinue treatment during the first year [2, 3, 5, 6]. It has also been reported that approximately 30%–40% of patients need to change therapy by 5 years due to resistance, intolerance or treatment‐related complications, with many requiring a second, third, or even further lines of therapy [7, 8, 9]. Patients who go on to receive later lines of treatment have decreased response rates and lower overall survival compared with patients in the early stages of treatment; therefore, switching treatments should be avoided whenever possible [4, 9].

Given the long‐term, sometimes lifelong, nature of treatment for CML, quality of life remains a key concern for patients. Grade ≥ 3 adverse events (AEs) are traditionally considered to have the greatest impact on patients' quality of life; however, Grade 1 and Grade 2 AEs may also be disruptive for patients with CML, particularly if these events are persistent and limit daily activities [10]. Notably, the rates of discontinuation of first‐line CML treatments due to intolerance are as high as 25% in pivotal clinical trials [11, 12, 13, 14, 15]. Furthermore, many patients with CML have comorbidities such as cardiovascular disease or diabetes that limit their treatment options [16]. Altogether, these findings highlight the need for more efficacious and also more tolerable therapies in first‐line treatment of CML.

Asciminib is the first BCR::ABL1 allosteric inhibitor that works by Specifically Targeting the ABL Myristoyl Pocket (STAMP) and was intentionally designed to reduce off‐target effects on tyrosine kinases other than BCR::ABL1 [17, 18, 19]; this specificity is expected to translate into reduced rates of AEs and may improve treatment tolerability [19]. The phase 1 dose‐finding and the phase 3 pivotal ASCEMBL clinical trials have demonstrated the efficacy and tolerable safety profile of asciminib in patients with CML pretreated with two or more TKIs [20, 21].

ASC4FIRST is the first clinical trial to compare asciminib to all current standard of care (SoC) TKIs head‐to‐head in patients with CML in chronic phase (CML‐CP), allowing for a direct comparison of efficacy and safety parameters [22]. In this study, patients were stratified by the TKI selected by investigators before randomization (imatinib or 2G TKI), so that different groups of patients with different assigned treatments could be analyzed separately. The primary endpoint results at 48 weeks established the significantly superior efficacy of asciminib compared to all SoC TKIs pooled together (investigator‐selected TKIs [IS‐TKIs]) and compared to imatinib (in the imatinib stratum), with overall major molecular response (MMR, BCR::ABL1 IS ≤ 0.1%) rates of 67.7% for asciminib vs. 49.0% for IS‐TKIs (treatment difference, 18.9%; p < 0.001), and 69.3% for asciminib vs. 40.2% for imatinib in the imatinib stratum (treatment difference, 29.6%; p < 0.001) [22]. For patients in the 2G TKI stratum, MMR rates at Week 48 were numerically higher for asciminib than 2G TKIs (66.0% vs. 57.8%). These results were maintained at Week 96 (key secondary analysis), where the treatment difference for MMR rates increased for all comparisons, including asciminib vs. 2G TKIs in the 2G TKI stratum. Rates of Grade ≥ 3 AEs and AEs leading to discontinuation at 96 weeks were lower with asciminib (44.5% and 5.0%, respectively) than with imatinib (49.5% and 13.1%, respectively) and 2G TKIs (59.8% and 12.7%, respectively), showing that the safety and tolerability profile of asciminib continued to be more favorable than those of imatinib and 2G TKIs [23].

Evidence suggests that ethnicity influences disease characteristics across CML patients [24] and may affect outcomes and tolerability: indeed, the median age at CML diagnosis appears to be lower in Asian compared with Western countries [25, 26, 27]. Furthermore, the pharmacokinetics of TKIs is potentially affected by ethnicity, given intrinsic differences in body surface area [24, 26, 28]. Here, we present the ASC4FIRST 96‐week results for the subgroup of East Asian patients with CML‐CP enrolled in the study in the context of results from non‐East Asian patients.

Materials and Methods

Study Design and Treatments

ASC4FIRST (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT04971226, first posted 21 July 2021) is an ongoing phase 3, multicenter, open‐label, randomized clinical trial. Eligible adult patients with newly diagnosed CML‐CP were enrolled between 5 November 2021 and 20 December 2022. East Asian patients were recruited in participating hospitals in the Chinese Mainland and Taiwan, Japan, and Korea and randomized to treatment. The data cutoff date was 22 October 2024, after patients had completed their Week 96 visit or had discontinued treatment earlier.

The study design, endpoints, assessments, and analyses have been reported previously [22]. Briefly, patients were randomized 1:1 to receive asciminib (80 mg once daily [QD]) or IS‐TKIs (administered at the approved doses for first‐line treatment: imatinib 400 mg QD, bosutinib 400 mg QD, dasatinib 100 mg QD, or nilotinib 300 mg twice daily). Before randomization, investigators selected the most appropriate TKI for each patient (prerandomization‐selected TKI, either imatinib or one of the assessed 2G TKIs) should the patient be randomized to the comparator arm; this decision took into account physician selection based on patient and/or clinical disease characteristics and patient preference. Patients were included in two different strata (imatinib stratum or 2G TKI stratum) according to their prerandomization‐selected TKI. Randomization was stratified by European Treatment and Outcome Study (EUTOS) long‐term survival score (ELTS) risk category (low, intermediate, and high) as well as prerandomization‐selected TKI.

Crossover or change of study treatment was not allowed at any time during the treatment period for patients in the IS‐TKI arm. The study is ongoing and patients will continue their treatment until the end of the study (up to 8 years from the time when the last patient received their first treatment) or until discontinuation due to treatment failure, disease progression, treatment intolerance, or patient/physician decision. Patients who discontinued treatment early will be followed until the end of the study for survival and disease progression.

Patients

Key eligibility criteria have been previously reported [22]. Briefly, eligible patients were aged ≥ 18 years and had Philadelphia chromosome‐positive (Ph+) CML‐CP as per the European LeukemiaNet (ELN) 2020 criteria [29], which had been newly diagnosed within 3 months of study enrollment. Patients who had received previous treatment for CML were excluded from the study; however, treatment with hydroxyurea and/or anagrelide was allowed, as was treatment with imatinib, nilotinib, dasatinib, or bosutinib for a limited duration (up to 2 weeks before randomization). Treatment with other anticancer agents prior to randomization was not allowed.

Study Objectives and Endpoints

ASC4FIRST had two primary endpoints: MMR rate at Week 48 for asciminib vs. all IS‐TKIs and for asciminib vs. imatinib; the latter was for those patients included in the imatinib stratum. Patients who discontinued treatment for any reason or met any of the treatment failure criteria as per ELN 2020 recommendations [29] before Week 48 were considered non‐responders.

Key secondary endpoints included MMR rates at Week 96 for asciminib vs. IS‐TKIs and asciminib vs. imatinib (for patients in the imatinib stratum). Other efficacy assessments included MMR rates at Week 48 and Week 96 for asciminib vs. 2G TKIs (for patients in the 2G TKI stratum); molecular response rates (BCR::ABL1 IS ≤ 1%, ≤ 0.01% [MR4.0], and ≤ 0.0032% [MR4.5] at Week 48 and Week 96, and BCR::ABL1 IS ≤ 10% at Week 12); and cumulative incidence of molecular response. Safety and asciminib pharmacokinetics (PK) were also assessed.

Pharmacokinetic Assessments

Blood samples for asciminib PK were collected from East Asian patients allocated to the asciminib treatment arm. Samples were collected at Weeks 2, 4, 12, 24, and 48; collections occurred pre‐dose and 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, and 12 h post‐dose at specific visits. Additional pre‐dose samples were collected at Weeks 4, 12, 24, and 48. Asciminib plasma concentrations were measured using a validated high‐performance liquid chromatography/tandem mass spectrometry method [30].

Statistical Analysis

Statistical testing was not conducted for the East Asian or non‐East Asian patient subgroups; only descriptive statistics are provided. Efficacy analyses are reported as point estimates with a 95% confidence interval (CI) estimated using the Clopper‐Pearson method, with common treatment differences and their 95% CIs estimated using the Mantel–Haenszel method after stratifying for prerandomization‐selected TKI and baseline ELTS.

Cumulative response analyses were performed for molecular response with cumulative incidence functions using competing risk. Time to molecular response was defined as the time from the date of randomization to the date of the first documented occurrence of molecular response and was censored at the last molecular assessment date during treatment (at data cutoff or earlier) for patients who had not had a response or the competing risk event. Discontinuation of study treatment for any reason (such as treatment failure or death) without attaining molecular response was considered a competing risk. Data on estimated cumulative incidence and 95% CI at the pre‐specified timepoints are presented.

Efficacy analyses for the East Asian patient subgroup were conducted within the full analysis set, which comprised all East Asian patients randomized to treatment (all asciminib vs. all IS‐TKI), and similarly in the imatinib and 2G TKI strata full analysis sets. Safety analyses were conducted in the safety analysis set, which included all East Asian patients who received at least one dose of study treatment. Efficacy and safety subgroup analyses were also conducted for non‐East Asian patients. AEs were coded using Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA, version 27.1) and assessed according to Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE; version 5.0).

Ethics

The study protocol was approved by institutional review boards at each site and the study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and the International Council for Harmonization Guidelines for Good Clinical Practice, with applicable local regulations. All patients provided written informed consent.

Results

Patient Demographics and Clinical Characteristics

A total of 405 patients were enrolled in the ASC4FIRST study, including 138 East Asian patients; among the latter, 74 patients were randomized to asciminib (28 in the imatinib stratum and 46 in the 2G TKI stratum) and 64 to IS‐TKIs (26 in the imatinib stratum and 38 in the 2G TKI stratum [dasatinib, n = 10; nilotinib, n = 24; bosutinib, n = 4]). One East Asian patient in the 2G TKI stratum (randomized to nilotinib) did not receive treatment due to patient decision.

Patient demographics and disease characteristics were generally balanced between asciminib and IS‐TKI arms in both patient subgroups (Table 1). Most patients were male and under 65 years of age; however, East Asian patients were younger and, as expected, had a higher proportion of patients with low cardiovascular (CV) risk than non‐East Asian patients. Among East Asian patients, those in the imatinib stratum were older and had a higher Framingham CV risk score on average than those in the 2G TKI stratum, which was expected based on medical practice (Table S1).

Patient Disposition

After a median duration of follow‐up of 26.6 months for asciminib and 26.5 months for IS‐TKIs, more East Asian patients were still receiving treatment with asciminib than IS‐TKIs at data cutoff (22 October 2024, once patients had completed their Week 96 visit or discontinued earlier); similar results were observed in non‐East Asian patients (median follow‐up: 27.2 months for asciminib and 26.3 months for IS‐TKIs) (Table 2). Nearly twice as many East Asian patients in the IS‐TKI arm (31.3%) discontinued treatment compared with the asciminib arm (16.2%); a similar pattern was recorded for non‐East Asian patients. The most common reasons for discontinuation in both treatment arms and patient groups were unsatisfactory therapeutic effect (East Asian vs. non‐East Asian patients: 5 [6.8%] with asciminib and 11 [17.2%] with IS‐TKIs vs. 14 [11.0%] with asciminib and 31 [22.1%] with IS‐TKIs) and AEs (East Asian vs. non‐East Asian patients: 4 [5.4%] with asciminib and 6 [9.4%] with IS‐TKIs vs. 8 [6.3%] with asciminib and 20 [14.3%] with IS‐TKIs). Discontinuations due to both of these reasons were reported less frequently with asciminib than IS‐TKIs. Two East Asian patients (1.4%) discontinued treatment due to progressive disease (progression to accelerated phase), one in the asciminib arm and one in the IS‐TKI arm (treated with imatinib).

Efficacy

Regarding the study primary endpoints, the MMR rate at Week 48 was higher with asciminib vs. all IS‐TKIs in East Asian patients (64.9% vs. 45.3%; common treatment difference, 19.5% [95% CI 4.3, 34.7]) (Figure 1). A similar trend was observed for East Asian patients in the imatinib stratum, although with a larger treatment difference (67.9% vs. 30.8% for asciminib and imatinib, respectively; common treatment difference 36.5% [95% CI 13.4, 59.6]). These results were comparable to those observed in non‐East Asian patients (asciminib vs. all IS‐TKIs: 69.3% vs. 50.7%; asciminib vs. imatinib in the imatinib stratum: 69.9% vs. 43.4%, respectively).

For secondary endpoints, the MMR rate at Week 48 was numerically higher for East Asian patients on asciminib in the 2G TKI stratum (63.0% vs. 55.3%; common treatment difference 8.5% [95% CI‐10.9, 27.9]); similar results were observed in non‐East Asian patients (68.5% vs. 59.4%; common treatment difference 9.3%) (Figure 1). MMR rates at Week 96 followed the trend of the Week 48 results, with higher rates for asciminib vs. IS‐TKIs (77.0% vs. 57.8%), asciminib vs. imatinib in the imatinib stratum (78.6% vs. 46.2%) and asciminib vs. 2G TKIs in the 2G TKI stratum (76.1% vs. 65.8%) in East Asian patients; comparable results were observed in non‐East Asian patients. There were general increases in the common treatment differences for asciminib vs. IS‐TKIs in both East Asian and non‐East Asian patients from Week 48 to Week 96; the greatest relative increase was observed in the 2G stratum.

Kaplan–Meier estimates showed that MMR was achieved earlier with asciminib than with IS‐TKIs in East Asian patients (median time to first MMR: 24.1 weeks vs. 36.3 weeks, respectively) and in non‐East Asian patients (24.3 weeks vs. 36.4 weeks, respectively) (Figure 2). Similar results were observed with asciminib for both patient groups in the imatinib stratum and in the 2G TKI stratum (Figure S1). Patients who received asciminib had a higher probability of achieving MMR compared with patients who received IS‐TKIs.

In addition to MMR, benefits with asciminib were observed for other response rates when compared with all IS‐TKIs, imatinib or 2G TKIs. More patients achieved an early molecular response (EMR, BCR::ABL1 IS ≤ 10% at Week 12) with asciminib than with all IS‐TKIs (East Asian: 91.9% vs. 73.4%, respectively; non‐East Asian: 88.2% vs. 68.6%, respectively); similar results were observed in the imatinib stratum and, to a lesser extent, in the 2G TKI stratum (Figure 3). In addition, a larger proportion of patients achieved a molecular response of BCR::ABL1 IS ≤ 1% with asciminib than all IS‐TKIs at Week 48 (Figure 3). Notably, more East Asian patients also achieved deep molecular response (DMR) with asciminib than all IS‐TKIs (Week 48: MR4.0, 40.5% vs. 18.8% on IS‐TKIs; MR4.5, 18.9% vs. 9.4% on IS‐TKIs; Week 96: MR4.0, 50.0% vs. 28.1% on IS‐TKIs; MR4.5, 31.1% vs. 17.2% on IS‐TKIs, respectively) (Figure 4); similar results were observed in non‐East Asian patients. Treatment‐emergent BCR::ABL1 mutations were identified in six East Asian patients, three patients treated with asciminib (A433D, L340Q, and A337T/L340Q), two patients treated with imatinib (E450G/L248V and L248V/G250E/E255V), and one patient treated with nilotinib (Y253H); none of these patients achieved MMR, and they all discontinued the study treatment due to unsatisfactory therapeutic effect (Table S2).

Safety

Safety was assessed in all patients who received at least one dose of study treatment. By data cutoff, a relative dose intensity of > 90%–110% had been achieved by 87.8% of East Asian patients on asciminib, compared with 63.5% of patients on IS‐TKIs; the equivalent proportions for non‐East Asian patients were 86.5% and 79.0%. Overall, safety findings were comparable for East Asian and non‐East Asian patients.

Most East Asian patients on asciminib (93.2%) and all East Asian patients on IS‐TKIs experienced at least one AE, whereas fewer East Asian patients on asciminib experienced Grade ≥ 3 AEs than patients in the IS‐TKI arm (41.9% vs. 54.0%, respectively); comparable results were observed in non‐East Asian patients (Grade ≥ 3 AEs, 46.0% vs. 55.1%, respectively) (Table 3). Similarly, lower rates of Grade ≥ 3 AEs were also observed in East Asian patients receiving asciminib vs. those receiving imatinib or 2G TKIs (41.9 vs. 61.5% and 48.6%, respectively) (Table S3). In addition, the incidence of serious AEs (SAEs) in East Asian patients was lower for asciminib compared to IS‐TKIs (9.5% vs. 22.2%, respectively; Table S3) and lower vs. non‐East Asian patients on asciminib (9.5% vs. 17.5%, respectively) (Tables S3 and S4). Of note, there were no treatment‐related SAEs reported in the asciminib arm for East Asian patients (0 vs. 7.9% for IS‐TKIs; Table S3).

The incidence of any‐grade AEs leading to treatment discontinuation was lower in the asciminib arm (2.7%) compared with the IS‐TKI arm (9.5%) in East Asian patients (Tables 3 and S5). Of note, rates of AEs leading to discontinuation were lower in East Asian patients (2.7%) than in non‐East Asian patients treated with asciminib (6.3%), although the latter were still considerably lower than for IS‐TKIs in non‐East Asian patients (14.5%) (Tables 3 and S6). All AEs leading to treatment discontinuation were reported in one East Asian patient each (Table S5) and most of these events were assessed by study investigators as treatment related (2/2 in the asciminib arm and 5/6 in the IS‐TKI arm).

In a similar way, the incidences of any‐grade and Grade ≥ 3 AEs leading to dose adjustment and/or interruption were lower in East Asian patients who received asciminib (33.8% and 31.1%, respectively) compared with those on IS‐TKIs (63.5% and 41.3%, respectively) (Tables 3 and S7). This was also observed in non‐East Asian patients (AEs leading to dose adjustment or interruption for asciminib vs. IS‐TKIs: any grade, 32.5% vs. 43.5%; Grade ≥ 3, 25.4% vs. 27.5%, respectively) (Tables 3 and S8). The most frequently reported AEs leading to dose adjustment and/or interruption were cytopenias (most commonly neutropenia and thrombocytopenia), irrespective of study arm and patient group (Tables S7 and S8).

The most commonly reported AEs are described in Figure 5 and Tables S9 and S10. For both hematologic (e.g., thrombocytopenia and neutropenia) and non‐hematologic AEs, lower rates (with a ≥ 5% difference compared to all IS‐TKIs) were reported for several AEs in East Asian patients treated with asciminib; among these were rash, headache, myalgia, increased alanine aminotransferase, increased aspartate aminotransferase and increased blood bilirubin. Of note, no AEs were reported more frequently (with a ≥ 5% difference) in patients treated with asciminib than all IS‐TKIs. Although cytopenias (e.g., thrombocytopenia, neutropenia) were more commonly reported with asciminib in East Asian vs. non‐East Asian patients, Grade ≥ 3 event rates were similar and lower than those reported with IS‐TKIs (Tables S9 and S10).

By data cutoff, arterial occlusive events (AOEs) had been reported in 2 East Asian patients (2.7%, arteriosclerosis coronary artery and peripheral occlusive arterial disease) and 2 non‐East Asian patients (1.6%, angina pectoris and cerebrovascular accident) treated with asciminib, and in 2 East Asian patients (3.2%, vertebral artery arteriosclerosis and cerebral infarction) and 1 non‐East Asian patient (0.7%, myocardial infarction and myocardial ischaemia) treated with IS‐TKIs. Of note, all AOEs reported in the IS‐TKI arm occurred in patients treated with 2G TKIs. AOEs in East Asian patients in the asciminib arm were mild or moderate in severity and did not require study treatment discontinuation or dose adjustment; however, the cerebral infarction event in the patient treated with a 2G TKI required drug interruption.

Two deaths were reported in the East Asian patient population; both deaths were due to CML and occurred post‐treatment discontinuation in patients who had received imatinib. No deaths occurred during the treatment period.

Pharmacokinetic Analyses

Steady‐state PK parameters for asciminib in East Asian patients were similar to those of the global study population (Table S11), indicating similar exposure to the drug in both patient groups. Furthermore, comparable minimum plasma concentrations (Ctrough) were observed in the East Asian and global study populations up to Week 48, which was the full extent of the treatment period for the primary analysis (Table S12).

Discussion

ASC4FIRST is the first trial to compare asciminib with all SoC TKIs in patients with newly diagnosed CML‐CP. The primary analysis from this study showed the superior efficacy and favorable safety and tolerability profile for asciminib vs. all IS‐TKIs [22]. This analysis in East Asian patients enrolled in ASC4FIRST shows similar efficacy, safety, and PK to those of non‐East Asian patients and the global study population, and supports the use of asciminib in this subgroup.

Patients were split into two different strata based on the prerandomization‐selected TKI, either imatinib or 2G TKI. As expected, patients in the 2G TKI stratum were younger than those in the imatinib stratum, and a larger proportion of patients in the imatinib stratum had intermediate or high CV risk scores, reflecting how factors such as age and comorbidities influence the choice of TKI. This finding supports the design of the study, helping to achieve balance across treatment arms and obtain a relevant comparison of treatments within each stratum. Also as expected, East Asian patients were younger than their non‐East Asian counterparts; this is in agreement with previous reports showing a lower median age at diagnosis in Asian patients compared with those from Western countries [25, 26, 27].

The MMR rates observed for asciminib and IS‐TKIs at Week 48 in East Asian and non‐East Asian patients were consistent with those in the global population (67.7% vs. 49.0%, respectively), as was the common treatment difference [22]. Similarly, in this analysis higher MMR rates were observed for asciminib vs. imatinib at Week 48 in the imatinib stratum for both East Asian and non‐East Asian patients (in the global population: 69.3% vs. 40.2%, respectively) [22]. Numerically higher MMR rates were also reported for asciminib vs. 2G TKIs in the 2G TKI stratum, which is consistent with results from the global population [22]. The ASC4FIRST 96‐week findings for the global population showed that the differences in MMR rates between asciminib and IS‐TKIs increased overall and in both strata [23]. In the current analysis, the MMR rates at Week 96 were numerically higher for East Asian vs. non‐East Asian patients; this is in agreement with similar observations from the DASISION trial and other TKI studies comparing East Asian and non‐East Asian populations [24, 27]. Notably, our analysis also showed an increase in the common treatment difference for asciminib vs. 2G TKIs in MMR rates from Week 48 to Week 96. These results indicate that the MMR benefit with asciminib continued to increase with longer follow‐up.

MMR was achieved earlier in East Asian and non‐East Asian patients who received asciminib vs. IS‐TKIs, and patients on asciminib had a higher probability of achieving MMR throughout the treatment period; similar results were observed in the global study population [22]. In addition, similarly higher proportions of East Asian and non‐East Asian patients on asciminib achieved EMR and BCR::ABL1 IS ≤ 1% at Week 48 compared with patients on IS‐TKIs; these levels of response have been associated with improved long‐term outcomes, including reduced risk of disease progression to accelerated phase/blast phase and longer survival [31]. Overall, these results show that East Asian and non‐East Asian patients benefited more from asciminib than from IS‐TKIs.

Furthermore, similar to what was observed for the global ASC4FIRST population, East Asian and non‐East Asian patients had higher rates of DMR at Weeks 48 and 96 with asciminib vs. IS‐TKIs [22]. Treatments that increase the proportion of patients achieving DMR have the potential to increase the number of patients eligible to attempt treatment‐free remission (TFR) [32].

Overall, asciminib demonstrated favorable safety and tolerability compared with both imatinib and 2G TKIs in all patients, with lower rates of any‐grade and Grade ≥ 3 AEs, treatment‐related AEs, AEs leading to discontinuation, and AEs leading to dose adjustment or interruption. This is consistent with results observed in the global population [22]. Of note, rates of AEs leading to discontinuation were lower for East Asian patients than for non‐East Asian patients in both treatment arms. In DASISION, more East Asian patients discontinued dasatinib due to AEs (10% vs. 6% in non‐East Asian patients), but the rates were similar for imatinib discontinuation [27]. Rates of AEs leading to dose adjustment/interruption were similar in East Asian and non‐East Asian patients treated with asciminib; however, they were more frequent in East Asian than non‐East Asian patients treated with IS‐TKIs.

The most commonly reported AEs varied across patient groups, with cytopenias being more frequently reported in East Asian patients. Higher rates of cytopenias in East Asian vs. non‐East Asian patients have also been reported in the DASISION trial [27]. East Asian patients in ASC4FIRST had lower median body weight and body mass index (BMI) compared to non‐East Asian patients; these features have previously been associated with increased imatinib exposure and more severe toxicity [33, 34]. Overall, safety results are consistent with those observed for the global population and support the favorable safety and tolerability profile of asciminib in East Asian and non‐East Asian patients [23].

The assessed PK parameters for asciminib in East Asian patients were similar to those in the global study population, including Ctrough [22]. This suggests similar levels of asciminib exposure, in contrast with results from a previous study showing that Chinese patients with CML had higher imatinib Ctrough values than Western patients [28]. Furthermore, the asciminib Ctrough exceeded the preclinical 90% inhibitory concentration for phosphorylated signal transducer and activator of transcription 5 (pSTAT5) inhibition, a known downstream marker of BCR::ABL1 inhibition and drug activity, suggesting that sufficient therapeutic drug exposure is achieved in both populations and supporting the observed comparable efficacy between East Asian and global patients [20]. Importantly, no clinically meaningful differences in the PK of asciminib have been observed based on race or ethnicity [30]. A recent population PK (PopPK) analysis confirmed that asciminib PK is similar in Chinese and Japanese patients compared to that of the global population [35]. These results highlight the consistency in asciminib PK between East Asian and global populations, which help understand the observed similar efficacy of asciminib.

Overall, this analysis highlights the compelling efficacy and safety results of asciminib compared with all SoC TKIs in newly diagnosed patients with CML‐CP, with no significant differences in efficacy or safety profile due to patient ethnicity. These results support the use of asciminib as the therapy of choice for patients with CML‐CP.

Comparison of the biocidal efficacy of sodium dichloro-iso-cyanurate, double antibiotic pastes and calcium hydroxide as intra-canal medicaments for regenerative endodontic therapy: an ex-vivo study
Authors: Alireza Adl, Fereshte Sobhnamayan, Mohammad Motamedifar, Mohammad mehdi Shokouhi, Amir hossein Ghadri, Samina Gavahianjahromi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeThis study aimed to determine the antibacterial efficacy of sodium dichloro-iso-cyanurate (NaDCC) compared to double antibiotic paste (DAP) and calcium hydroxide (CH) against Enterococcus faecalis (E. faecalis).MethodsThe study utilized a sample of forty-four human single-rooted teeth, which were decoronated and chemo-mechanically prepared. E. faecalis was then inoculated into the teeth for four weeks. The samples were randomly divided into four groups, each receiving a different intracanal medicament: DAP (n = 12), NaDCC (n = 12), CH (n = 12), and a control group treated with sterile water (n = 6). After a contact time of 28 days, a sample was taken from each root canal with a paper point. The number of colony-forming units was then calculated, and data analysis was performed using Kruskal Wallis and Mann-Whitney tests.ResultsAll the medicaments had a significantly higher antibacterial effect than the control group (p-value < 0.05). DAP showed a significantly higher antibacterial effect than NaDCC (p-value < 0.01) and CH (p-value < 0.01). NaDCC showed a significantly higher antibacterial effect than CH (p-value < 0.01).ConclusionThe study’s results indicate that DAP is the most effective medicament against E. faecalis. However, the study also revealed that NaDCC, a relatively novel substance in endodontics, demonstrated a higher antibacterial efficacy than CH. This suggests that NaDCC could be a promising addition to the range of medicaments used in regenerative procedures.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12903-026-09002-1.

Introduction

Regenerative endodontic treatment has received significant attention recently as a suggestive alternative for treating necrotic immature teeth. This treatment’s priority over conventional endodontic treatment is the probable continuation of root growth after revascularization of the root canal [1–3]. Adequate disinfection of the root canal system is an essential step during the revascularization procedure [1]. Chemo-mechanical instrumentation of the root canal system, on its own, significantly leaves untouched walls and, consequently, remaining bacteria [4]. Thus, intra-canal medicaments could reduce bacterial amount [5].

Clinical reports have used different kinds of intracanal medicaments. Triple antibiotic paste and pure calcium hydroxide (CH) are the most popular [1, 6–13]. However, intracanal medicaments might have an adverse effect on the viability of stem cells, especially when a revascularization procedure is needed [5, 14]. E. faecalis is an opportunistic microorganism important in persistent peri-radicular disease, including primary and persistent endodontic infections [15–17]. Its unique capability to adhere to dentin and penetrate dentinal tubules, survive extreme periods of pH and starvation, and colonize in the form of biofilm allows it to resist the bactericidal activity of CH [18, 19] and different kinds of antibiotics [20].

Besides the weak antimicrobial effect of CH against E. faecalis, damage to the organic matrix of dentin that reduces its mechanical properties, cell cytotoxicity due to its high pH, and the probability of uncontrolled canal calcification make clinicians find a new alternative to this traditional intracanal medicament [6, 10, 11, 13].

The triple antibiotic paste contains a mixture of ciprofloxacin, metronidazole, and minocycline and has been used in various situations in endodontic treatment, especially in revascularization procedures [6, 21, 22]. Tooth discoloration of the minocycline component of this mixture motivates clinicians to eliminate or even replace it with other antibiotics [23]. Double antibiotic paste (DAP), which excludes minocycline without any replacement, has been successfully used for revascularization. However, the biocompatibility of these pastes, especially in higher concentrations and systemic side effects of antibiotics, such as drug allergy and antibiotic resistance, are points of concern [24, 25].

Sodium dichloro-iso-cyanurate (NaDCC) is a chlorine-releasing agent available in a form of tablet. It is used as a disinfectant for infant feeding equipment, swimming pool, drinking water, and food industry. Free available chlorine release from NaDCC called hypochlorous acid (HOCL). NaDCC release its free available chlorine immediately and leave a reservoir chlorine that is released once the original free available chlorine initially released has been used up [26, 27]. When evaluated as a root canal irrigant, the biocidal activity of NaDCC against endodontic microorganisms was superior to that of sodium hypochlorite with similar cytotoxicity [28].

In addition, there is scarce evidence assessing the biocidal effect of NaDCC as a root canal medicament. An ex vivo study showed that NaDCC and CH, when used as intracanal medicaments, could eradicate an engineered 3-species biofilm in single-rooted teeth in an ex vivo model [5].

To our knowledge, no previous study has compared NaDCC with any antibiotic paste as an intracanal medicament to assess their antibacterial efficacy. The study aimed to compare NaDCC, DAP, and CH as intracanal medicaments in a biofilm model against E. faecalis in extracted single-rooted teeth.

Methods and materials

Recently extracted single-rooted teeth (i.e. maxillary/mandibular premolars and maxillary central incisors) were placed in 0.1% thymol solution at a temperature of 6°C after extraction. Calculi and periodontal tissue remnants were removed. A mesiodistal radiograph was taken for each tooth to ensure that the tooth was single canal without resorption, calcification and open apex. Then, the teeth were examined using a microscope with a magnification × 16 to investigate presence of fracture or defects. Finally, 44 teeth were selected for this study.

Sample size calculation

Effect size values were computed for all pairwise comparisons using correlation effect size for Mann-Whitney U test. The study was double-blinded. The allocator of the data collector was blinded and intracanal medication was used to treat the assigned group. In addition, the data analyst who performed the statistical analyses was blinded to the study. Due to the nature of the study, the operator could not be blinded, as the intracanal medicament must be exposed and cannot be masked.

Sample preparation

Teeth crowns were cut at the coronal third with diamond disks underwater and air spray to achieve a root length of 12 mm. Then a #15 K-file (MANI, Tochigi-Ken, Japan) was used to determine the working length. Next, #4, #3, #2 and #1 Gates-Glidden drills (Mani, Tochigi, Japan) were used to prepare root canal walls using the crown-down technique. Finally, the canals were instrumented up to #60 K-file. Irrigation of each canal was achieved using 1 mL 5.25% sodium hypochlorite (Chloraxid, Cerkamed, Poland) for 1 min after each file. The root canals were flushed with 1 mL 17% ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (Morvabon Co, Iran), for 3 min to eliminate the smear layer, and 10 ml of normal saline (Iranian Parenteral and Pharmaceutical Co.) as the final rinse. The teeth were dried using multiple paper points (Diadent, Korea) [29].

Formation of biofilm

Samples were autoclaved at a temperature of 121°C and under a pressure of 15 psi for 20 minutes. Then, the samples were incubated into the microtubes containing 2 mL of BHI (brain-heart infusion broth) at 37°C for 24 hours to confirm their sterility. One pure colony of E. faecalis (ATCC 29212) was taken up from an overnight culture onto the Blood Agar (BA) plates. The colony was resuspended into the BHI broth and adjusted with the 0.5 MacFarland standard (108 cells/mL) using a spectrophotometer at an optical density of 600 nm. After that, 2 mL from the prepared suspension were aliquoted into the sterile microtubes containing the samples. All microtubes were incubated at 37 °C for four weeks, and the culture media were changed every day during incubation time to ensure bacterial cell viability. The samples were removed and irrigated with 5 ml of sterile saline at the end of the incubation period.

Antibiotic medicament preparation and classification of samples

After four weeks of incubation, two samples were selected randomly and evaluated under SEM (VEGA3 TESCAN, Cranberry, PA) to assess the formation of E. faecalis biofilms. The other samples were randomly divided into four groups, and the medicaments were placed in root canal: Group 1:CH (Golchay Tehran, Iran) (n=12) Group 2: NaDCC (EMD Millipore Co, Germany) (n=12) Group 3: DAP was prepared by taking equal amounts of metronidazole (Flagyl Winthrop Pharmaceuticals, UK) and ciprofloxacin (Bayer plc, UK) powders (n=12) Group 4: Sterile water as positive control (n=6) In each group, 1 g of the powder was mixed with sterile water at a ratio of 1.5; 1(wt/vol) to obtain a paste-like consistency. The intracanal medicaments were introduced into the canal using lentulo spirals. After removing excess material, the coronal and apical parts were sealed with a temporary filling material (coltosol, Golchai Co., Iran), and then blocks were incubated at 37°C and 100% humidity. After four weeks, the samples were irrigated with 20 ml of saline solution.

Microbiological sampling

As paper point #50 (Diadent, Korea) ensures proper penetration to the full working length, it was placed inside each root canal for 1 minute [30, 31]. After sampling, each paper point was placed into the microcubes containing 2 mL of BHI and shaken for 30 seconds on a vortex. Then, one mL of the suspension was picked up to prepare a 10-fold serial dilution from 10 to 10-7. Next, 0.1 mL of each dilution was transferred onto the Muller-Hintun agar plates and speared using an L-shaped tube. The plates were incubated at 37°C for 48 hours. A classic colony-forming unit (CFU) counting technique was used to count E. faecalis colonies retrieved [32].

Statistical analysis

Data was analyzed using SPSS software (PASW Statistics 26; SPSS Inc, Chicago, IL). Descriptive statistics, including logarithms of means and standard deviations, were reported for each group. The normality of data was checked by Kolmogorov–Smirnov and the Shapiro–Wilk tests. Mann–Whitney and Kruskal-Wallis tests were used to compare the antimicrobial activities of the test medications. P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Discussion

Endodontic lesions are biofilm-mediated infections; thus, proper debridement is essential for the success of endodontic treatment [33]. Conventional irrigation and medicament may not completely eradicate the bacteria within the root canal system. In a systematic analysis study, it was found that the persistent infection was the main presentation in 79% of failed regenerative endodontic cases [34].

Persistent bacteria such as E. faecalis might resist the traditional measures and cause endodontic failure [35]. Although there are some questions regarding the correlation between endodontic failures and E. faecalis, recent studies have used this microorganism to evaluate the effect of different antibacterial agents/techniques [36, 37]. That is why we used E. faecalis as a target microorganism to compare the bactericidal efficacy of NaDCC to those of DAP and CH, which are conventional antibacterial agents used in regenerative endodontics. Previous studies have compared CH with DAP and NaDCC separately. However to the best of our knowledge no study has yet compared NaDCC with DAP. Our study directly compares NaDCC with both under uniform experimental conditions, reducing inter-study variability and improving comparability of the result. A recent study showed that NaDCC as intracanal medicament has strong antibacterial activity up to 15 days [38]. This sustained antibacterial activity could be attributed to its free available chlorine (FAC) which attached to the NaDCC molecule, acting as a reservoir for additional HOCl release in case of chlorine diminution [39]. In order to have successful canal treatment, the removal of microorganisms from the canal is essential. Microorganisms are present in two forms, biofilm and planktonic, inside the canal, and eliminating biofilm is far more complicated than the planktonic state [40] ; we used biofilms in this study instead of the planktonic form, which are 1000 times more resistant than the planktonic state [40]. Biofilm is a dynamic structure of buried bacteria in a polysaccharide matrix [41]. According to Kishen et al.’s study, four weeks after incubation, E. faecalis bacteria covers all surfaces of the dentin [42]. Therefore, four weeks were chosen for this study for biofilm formation. In this study, size 50 paper points were used for sampling, despite instrumentation up to size 60. This approach, consistent with previous studies [30, 31], allows deeper penetration into the canal. However, a smaller paper point may have limited contact with canal walls, potentially leading to under-sampling of adherent bacteria. Since the same protocol was applied across all groups, this limitation is unlikely to have affected the comparative outcomes. The present study showed that all tested medicaments reduced the bacterial colony count compared to the control group. Our findings also showed that after applying DAP for four weeks, no bacteria were detected in the root canal, while the CH and NaDCC groups failed to eradicate bacteria completely. The statistical analysis also showed that DAP had a significantly higher antibacterial efficacy than CH and NaDCC, and NaDCC was significantly more effective than CH. In previous studies, antibiotic pastes are significantly more effective than CH in reducing bacterial count [43, 44]. E. faecalis does not survive at pH 11.5 or greater but can survive below 11.5 [45]. Because of the buffering effect of dentine, it is unlikely that the high pH of calcium hydroxide is attained in the root canal [46]. Sabrah et al. [47] showed that TAP and DAP were more effective than CH in preventing E. faecalis and P. gingivalis biofilm formation.

Another study by Dewi et al. determined the optimal concentrations of TAP, modified triple antibiotic paste (mTAP), DAP and CH (among 0.1, 1.25, 5, 10 and 20 mg/ml) to completely eradicate E. faecalis in dentinal tubules after seven days [48]. According to their results, DAP requires 20 mg/ml, and TAP requires 10 mg/ml to eradicate E. faecalis after seven days. On the other hand, CH did not significantly reduce E. faecalis inside dentinal tubules [48]. The antibacterial efficacy of NaDCC has also been studied several times. NaDCC releases free available chlorine into water in the form of hypochlorous acid, which disrupts DNA synthesis [49]. Shenoy et al. investigated the antimicrobial action of 0.6% NaDCC, Chlorhexidine 2% (CHX) and sodium hypochlorite 2% (NaOCl) against E. faecalis. NaDCC 0.6% and 2% CHX showed the more extensive microbial inhibition zone against E.faecalis [50]. Chan et al. [51] compared the bactericidal efficacy of NaDCC and CH against E. faecalis in single-rooted human teeth after seven days. According to their results, the NaDCC and CH groups showed no colony growth. However, in our study, while NaDCC showed higher antibacterial efficacy compared to CH, none of them were able to reduce the colony count to zero. In another study, Kurian et al. compared the effectiveness of different concentrations (0.15%, 0.6% and 0.9%) of CH and NaDCC against Candida albicans. CH was found ineffective at all concentrations, while NaDCC was effective at all concentrations [52]. This finding aligns with our results, which showed that NaDCC has a higher antimicrobial effect than CH. Therefore, NaDCC can be considered a promising intracanal medicament in regenerative endodontics. It should be noted that the cytotoxicity of low concentrations of NaDCC on fibroblasts has been shown to be similar to that of sodium hypochlorite [53] However, its cytotoxicity at concentrations used as an intracanal medicament remains unexplored. Furthermore, if NaDCCis intended for use in regenerative endodontic procedures, its effects on stem cells should also be investigated. This study had some limitations that need to be considered in future studies. One of the limitations of the present study is that the antibacterial evaluation was performed only at the one-time point, there was one concentration for each medicament, and only one bacterial species was considered. Different intracanal medicaments may need different times and concentrations to demonstrate their optimal effects. We also suggest that future studies focus on the synergistic antibacterial properties of various antibacterial medicaments to find the optimal combination of intracanal medicament. Besides, the toxicity and short-term and long-term effects of these medicaments on the mechanical and chemical properties of dentine should be studied further.

Conclusion

The result of the present study showed that the antibacterial efficacy of NaDCC is higher than CH .Therefore it can be considered as intracanal medicament in regenerative endodontics.

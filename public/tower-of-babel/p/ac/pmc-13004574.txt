Neuroprotective effects of Vitamin D3 supplementation combination with valproate and perampanel in an experimental model using status epilepticus induction
Authors: Sudhir Chandra Sarangi, Surabhi Sinha, Shan Mohamad, Deeksha Sharma, Soumya Sucharita Pattnaik, Yajnaseni Dash, Aruna Nambirajan, Tapas Chandra Nag, Surender Singh, Manjari Tripathi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:Status epilepticus (SE) is a severe form of epilepsy with neurological sequelae and high mortality. Considering the neuromodulatory effects of Vitamin D3 (Vit-D3), this study investigated the potential role of Vit-D3 alone and in combination with antiseizure medications (ASMs).MATERIALS AND METHODS:Male Wistar rats were assigned into seven groups: SE-control, healthy control, valproate (VPA) (370 mg/kg), perampanel (PER) (1.5 mg/kg), Vit-D3 (6000 IU/kg/day), VPA + Vit-D3, and PER + Vit-D3. After 14 days of pretreatment, SE was induced by LiCl-pilocarpine administration, followed by acute (17 days) and long-term (29 days) drug effect studies. Seizure details and learning-memory were assessed along with evaluation of brain tissue for neurodegeneration (electronmicroscopy), histopathology, neuronal viability (neuron-specific nuclear protein), reactive astrocytes (glial fibrillary acidic protein) by immunohistochemistry, total antioxidant capacity, and neuroinflammation biomarkers.RESULTS:Vit-D3 combination with VPA or PER significantly reduced the percentage of rats experiencing stage-3/4 seizures and increased latency compared to the SE-control group (P < 0.001). Drug-treated groups had better memory retention (P < 0.001) than SE-control, with significantly better protection in the combination group. In immunohistochemistry, Vit-D3 in combination with ASMs had less neurodegeneration and reactive astrocytes in the hippocampus and cerebral cortex (P < 0.001). Through electron microscopy, the SE-control group exhibited significant damage in hippocampus’s myelin sheath and axons (grade 3) on days 17 and 29. Vit-D3 alone and in combination with ASMs attenuated these changes (P < 0.001), with combination groups showing the least neurodegeneration (day 29).CONCLUSION:Vit-D3 supplementation, especially in combination with ASMs, showed promising neuroprotective effects in the SE model in rats by improving seizure control, memory, hippocampal health, and antioxidant levels.

Introduction

Status epilepticus (SE) is a critical neurologic event affecting all age groups that needs immediate management due to its considerable mortality and morbidity.[1] With a mortality rate of 16%–20% and an incidence of 7–40 per million people annually, SE needs intensive treatment; this is a challenge also because of refractoriness to commonly used antiseizure medications (ASMs).[2] Changes in a neuronal network and neuronal damage after SE may lead to further developmental pathologies and neurological dysfunctions, including neurodegeneration.[3] These insults may initiate a latent epileptogenic phase leading to spontaneous recurrent seizures, alongside progressive cognitive, psychological impairments, learning disabilities, and memory deficits post-SE.[4] Animal studies also indicated that the hippocampus is one of the primary areas vulnerable to SE, leading to long-term detrimental effects.[56] Autopsy confirmed early neuronal necrosis with extensive cortical damage, and MRI showed progressive hippocampal shrinkage in patients with convulsive SE.[7]

Subjects with a history of epilepsy (42%–50%) who have probably been exposed to ASMs before are more likely to get SE. Nonetheless, up to 35%–65% of patients in SE benefit from this first-line treatment, and there is a risk of seizure recurrence even after successful ASMs tapering.[8] A significant treatment issue in SE is time-dependent drug-resistance, which accounts for 12%–26% of super-refractory SE and 29%–43% of refractory SE.[9]

Emerging evidence supports adjuvant dietary interventions suchas ketogenic diet, omega-3 fatty acids, and Vitamin D3 (Vit-D3), which show promise in treating neurological disorders.[1011] Vit-D3, a nutraceutical, has been established for pleiotropic effects, including cardiovascular, neuroprotective, anti-inflammatory, and antioxidant potential.[12] Some of the pre-clinical and clinical studies have demonstrated its anticonvulsant effects.[13141516] Previous studies have shown an increase in the relative expression of Vit-D3 receptor (VDR) after SE induction compared to the control group; similarly, Vit-D3 supplementation also leads to increased VDR expression compared to baseline in persons with drug-resistant epilepsy. This signifies the potential role of Vit-D3 in epileptogenesis.[1718] However, evidence is scarce regarding the efficacy of Vit-D3 intervention in SE and its potential for preventing sequelae of SE. Thus, this study aimed to evaluate the potential of Vit-D3 as an adjunct to ASMs – valproate (VPA) and perampanel (PER) – in a lithium–pilocarpine-induced experimental model of SE in Wistar rats.

Materials and Methods

Animals

A polyacrylic cage with a natural light and dark cycle (12 h:12 h) was used to house adult male Wistar rats weighing 200–250 g. The rats were given free access to tap water and a standard commercial pellet meal. The Institutional Animal Ethics Committee (IAEC) approved the experimental procedure (Ethics approval no. 235/IAEC-1/2019), and it was carried out in compliance with the guidelines by the Department of Animal Welfare, Government of India’s Committee for Control and Supervision of Experiments on Animals.

Experimental groups

After acclimation for 5 days, the Wistar rats were randomized into seven groups (n = 12): Healthy-control (received 10% DMSO as the vehicle), SE-control, VPA, PER, Vit-D3, VPA + Vit-D3, and PER + Vit-D3. The dose selected for the ASMs are mentioned in Supplementary Table 1.[192021] The occurrence of SE is due to some precipitating factors and is commonly seen in a person with epilepsy who may have prior exposure to ASMs. Hence, in this study, the acute drug impact study of drugs (for 14 days) was administered to mimic the baseline steady state of ASMs in a patient with epilepsy.[2223]

Methodology flow chart

The pretreatment of Vit-D3 was administered for 14 days, to simulate the natural supplementation of this vitamin in the general population and to achieve a steady state concentration. The acute impact of drugs was assessed within 72 h of SE induction (stage 1). The long-term impact of the drug was assessed with drug treatment for 14 days pretreatment and 14 days posttreatment after SE induction (stage 2). There were seven groups containing 12 animals in each, and for each stage of the experiment, half of the animals (n = 6 in each group) were used [Supplementary File].

Status epilepticus experimental model through lithium–pilocarpine induction and assessment of seizure parameters

Rats were administered a subcutaneous injection of a solution of lithium chloride (127 mg/kg s.c.) on day 13 to induce SE. The following day, glycopyrrolate was administered to lessen the peripheral symptoms of muscarinic cholinergic receptor activation, followed by pilocarpine (25 mg/kg, s.c.) administration. If there was no stage-3/4 seizure, pilocarpine was re-administered (25 mg/kg) at an hour, and if needed, a 3rd dose of 15 mg/kg was administered after 2 h of the first dose. This approach is a modified method of the reduced-intensity SE model.[2425] If the stage 3/4 seizure continued for more than 30 min, lorazepam was given as a rescue drug. From day 14 to day 28, seizure parameters (mortality, number and percentage of rats with stages 3/4 seizures, latency, and seizure score) were recorded using continuous CCTV camera monitoring and visual observation (at least 12 h/day in the initial 7 days, followed by at least 6 hours/day). Latency was observed for stage-3/4 seizures with a threshold of 180 min. The following rating was given to the seizures’ intensity: Scores include: 0 for no response; 1 for facial and ear twitches; 2 for myoclonic jerks and head nodding; 3 for forelimb clonus; and 4 for generalized tonic–clonic seizure with hindlimb tonic extension or absence of righting reflex.[26]

Learning and memory assessment: Spatial memory assessment using the elevated plus maze and fear memory retention deficit assessment using passive avoidance

Learning and memory assessment measures were performed in the animals at various intervals, i.e., on day 12th (baseline), 21st, and 28th (i.e., on day 7 and 14 post-SE induction, respectively). Twelve hours before the learning and memory test, the rats were deprived of food in an attempt to increase their incentive to perform the test. In this study, one animal was examined at a time in a quiet, peaceful area free from outside distractions such as bright lights and loud noises[27] [Supplementary File].

Euthanasia and brain tissue collection procedures

High doses of pentobarbital anesthesia were used to euthanize the rats on days 17 and 29. The entire brain was carefully separated from the cranium cavity. The sagittal suture was then used to cut the cerebral cortex in half. The brains were rinsed with cold saline to ensure that it was free from blood. For immunohistochemistry, half of the brain was immersion fixed in neutral buffered formalin (10%).[28] Anatomical identification markers were used to surgically extract dorsal hippocampus tissue from the other half of the brain and preserved in a fixative solution at 4°C for transmission electron microscopy assessment. The remaining cortical sections were then separated and homogenized in an ice-cold phosphate buffer (pH 7.4) at 12,000 g/20 min and 4°C to collect the supernatant for ELISA. For additional analysis, the supernatants were stored at −80°C.

Quantification of total antioxidant capacity, neuroinflammatory biomarker, and oxidative stress biomarker

As directed by the ELISA kit manufacturer, the neuron-specific enolase (NSE) and total antioxidant capacity (TAC) were assessed in homogenates of the brain tissue sample (both from RealGene, Württemberg, Germany). Using a standardized procedure in the lab, the oxidative stress markers superoxide dismutase (SOD), glutathione (GSH) levels, and malondialdehyde (MDA) were measured in the rat’s brain cortex tissue homogenate. All six animals in each of the long-term impact assessment groups underwent these tests.

Transmission electron microscopic analysis of the hippocampus

Axonal degeneration and the myelin sheath were assessed using electron microscopy of the dorsal hippocampus. After preparing ultrathin (60–70 nm) sections with ultracut UC7, samples were stained with lead citrate and uranyl acetate fixative (4% w/v). An electron microscope (TECNAI G2-20, Spain) was used to observe the grids. These tests were done on 3 animals in each group, selected randomly [Supplementary File].

Histopathological assessment of the brain

Half of each animal’s brain was fixed in 10% neutral buffered formalin, processed, sectioned (5 μm), H and E-stained, and examined microscopically. Cortical ischemic changes (dentate gyrus, hippocampus) were graded 0–3 based on severity[29] in randomly selected animals (n = 3) [Supplementary File].

Immunohistochemical analysis of neuron-specific nuclear protein and glial fibrillary acidic protein expression in the brain

The glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP) (1:1000) and neuron-specific nuclear protein (NeuN) (1:1000) protein expression in brain tissues was evaluated by immunohistochemistry. To calculate the average pixel counts for every group in every section,[30] the DotDotGoose software version 1.5.3 (American Museum of Natural History: New York, USA) was applied. These tests were done in three animals in each group, selected randomly [Supplementary Figure 1].

Statistical analysis

Using IBM SPSS Statistics 23.0 (IBM Japan Ltd: Kanagawa, Japan), the data were statistically analyzed. One-way ANOVA and Bonferroni’s post hoc tests were performed on the study’s normal distribution data. For the nonparametrically distributed data, the Chi-square and Kruskal–Wallis tests were used. Depending on the data distribution, the data were described as either median (range) or mean ± standard deviation. A significance level of <0.05 was defined for the P value.

Results

Effects of Vitamin D3 and its combinations with antiseizure medication on seizure control poststatus epilepticus induction

All rats in the SE control group had seizures. Rats with SE-control, VPA, PER, Vit-D3, VPA + Vit-D3, and PER + Vit-D3 had 100, 33.3, 41.7, 66.7, 25, and 0% of stage 3/4 seizures in the first 3 h after SE induction, respectively. Compared to the SE control group, fewer animals in all treatment groups experienced stage 3/4 seizures (P < 0.001). The SE control group experienced stage-3/4 seizures 48.68 ± 23.04 min later.

All drug-treated groups had a considerably longer latency to commencement of stage-3/4 seizures than the SE group (P < 0.001), with the exception of the Vit-D3 alone-treated group. The number and percentage of animals that experienced stage-3/4 seizures were also significantly lower in all drug-treated groups compared to the SE group (P < 0.001) on days 14–16, which are the first 3 days following SE induction. Comparing the SE control group to Vit-D3 alone, neither the onset nor the severity of seizures was significantly protected. Notably, the combination of PER and Vit-D3 significantly lowered the percentage of rats experiencing stage-3/4 seizures compared to PER treatment alone (P = 0.001. On days 17–28, fewer rats in all treated groups experienced stage-3/4 seizures than in the SE group.

During the first 3 days after SE induction (days 14–16), there was mortality in the SE-control, VPA, PER, and Vit-D3 groups (3, 1, 1, and 2 rats out of 12, respectively). So out of the six rats for acute impact assessment, the number of rats remaining for study were 3, 5, 5, 4, 6, and 6 in SE-control, VPA, PER, Vit-D3, VPA + Vit-D3, and PER + Vit-D3, respectively. The remaining six rats were carried over for the long-term impact assessment. From days 17–28, there was no mortality in the drug-treated groups, but the SE control group had 2 mortalities [Figure 2].

Effects of Vitamin D3 and its combinations with antiseizure medications on an experimental status epilepticus-induced decline in learning and memory

Compared to the healthy control group, the SE control group exhibited significantly higher transfer latency in the elevated plus maze test, showing impairment in spatial memory (P < 0.001). On day 21, a significant reduction in latency was observed in the Vit-D3 + VPA (P = 0.002) and Vit-D3 + PER (P = 0.001) groups. By day 28, Vit-D3 alone (P = 0.041) and its combinations with ASMs (P < 0.001) further improved memory performance. The Vit-D3 + VPA group showed greater protection than VPA alone on both days, and Vit-D3 + PER outperformed PER alone on day 28. Compared to the healthy control group, the SE control group showed a significant decrease in retention latency (P < 0.001) in the passive avoidance test, demonstrating a deficit in fear memory retention. On day 21, PER alone improved retention (P < 0.001), while by day 28, all combination groups showed significant improvement (P < 0.001); however, there were no discernible differences between the Vit-D3 + ASM and ASM-alone groups [Figure 3a and b].

Effects of Vitamin D3 and its combinations with antiseizure medications on experimental status epilepticus-induced ultrastructural changes in the hippocampus at days 17 and 29

In both acute (day 17; 4 days post-SE) and long-term (day 29; 16 days post-SE) observations, the SE group exhibited severe neurodegeneration (grade 4) compared to the healthy control (P < 0.001). On day 17, no significant differences in ultrastructural changes were observed among drug-treated groups, although all showed reduced severity compared to the SE group. Neurodegeneration significantly progressed from day 17 to day 29 in groups treated with ASMs or Vit-D3 alone (P < 0.001). In contrast, the Vit-D3 + ASM groups showed reduced degeneration from Grade 2 to Grade 1 over time. Myelin sheath and axonal integrity remained unaffected on day 17 but deteriorated significantly by day 29, except in the combination groups, which showed better preservation. In addition, the Vit-D3 + VPA group showed significantly reduced nuclear and cytoplasmic damage compared to the VPA-alone group, indicating enhanced ultrastructural neuroprotection with combination therapy [Figure 4].

Effects of Vitamin D3 and its combinations with antiseizure medication on total antioxidant capacity, neuroinflammatory biomarkers, and oxidative stress markers in the brain at day 28

MDA, GSH, NSE, TAC, and SOD levels were measured in epileptic rats to evaluate the protective effects of drugs. In comparison to the healthy control group (27.5 ± 4.8 U/ml; P < 0.001), the SE control group’s TAC levels were significantly lower (9.7 ± 1.4 U/ml). Significantly increased TAC was observed in all drug-treated groups compared to the SE control (P < 0.001). In comparison to the healthy control (2.5 ± 0.6 ng/ml; P < 0.001), the SE control group had higher NSE levels (29 ± 5.1 ng/ml), and all drug-treated groups had significantly greater NSE levels than the SE control (P < 0.001). MDA was elevated in the SE control (685.38 ± 70.78) versus healthy control (116.42 ± 13.24), whereas reduced in VPA, PER, and Vit-D3 groups, although still higher than healthy controls. Vit-D3 + VPA significantly reduced MDA. SOD activity differed significantly only between SE and healthy controls (P = 0.042). GSH levels increased significantly with PER, Vit-D3, and Vit-D3 + VPA treatments [Supplementary Figure 2].

Effect of Vitamin D3 and its combination with antiseizure medications on histopathological changes in the hippocampus at days 17 and 29

On day 17 post-treatment, the SE group showed extensive ischemic changes with neuronal loss (grade 2–3) in the hippocampus and confluent ischemic neurons (Grade 3) in the dentate gyrus. Cortical tissue exhibited approximately 50 ± 10 ischemic neurons per high-powered field, along with vascular alterations and chronic inflammation. All drug-treated groups showed significantly reduced ischemic changes in both hippocampus and cortex compared to the SE group (P = 0.001), whereas Vit-D3 alone did not provide substantial neuroprotection. By day 29, combination treatments (Vit-D3 + VPA, Vit-D3 + PER) offered greater hippocampal protection than ASMs alone (P = 0.042 and P = 0.038, respectively). Over time, chronic inflammation in the hippocampus declined, and ischemic neuron counts in the cortex decreased in the combination groups, suggesting enhanced neuroprotection in the ASMs + Vit-D3 groups [Figure 5].

Effects of Vitamin D3 and its combination with antiseizure medications on the expression of neuron-specific nuclear protein and glial fibrillary acidic protein proteins in the hippocampus and cerebral cortex at day 29

The neuronal marker NeuN was used to assess the effects of drugs on epileptic rats. The SE control group demonstrated a significant loss of neurones in the CA1 and CA3 when compared to the healthy control group (P < 0.001) and the Vit-D3 combination with ASMs groups (P < 0.001). Similarly, the CA2, CA3 and cerebral cortical regions showed significant neuronal protection in all drug-treated groups except the VPA and Vit-D3 alone-treated groups. The ASM alone group did not significantly vary from the combination with the Vit-D3 groups.

GFAP, or GFAP, was employed to detect reactive astrocytes in the cortex and hippocampal regions that suggested cortical neuronal loss and neuroinflammation in epileptic rats. The SE control group had considerably increased reactive astrocytosis in the brain and hippocampal regions compared to the healthy control group (P < 0.001). All drug-treated groups show considerably less reactive astrocytes (P < 0.001) than the SE control group [Figure 6 and Supplementary Figure 2].

Discussion

The American Endocrine Society Guidelines propose that individuals undergoing ASM therapy should undergo screening for reduced levels of Vit-D3.[31] Previous research has indicated that high-dose oral Vit-D3, at 50,000 IU/day, was well-tolerated in epilepsy patients, with significant increases in Vit-D3 levels observed at 6 and 12 weeks, remaining below potentially toxic levels (>100 ng/ml).[32] Its anticonvulsive potential has been demonstrated in some preclinical and clinical studies.[1314183334] Vit-D3 exerts multifaceted neuroprotective and neuromodulatory actions through both genomic and non-genomic mechanisms mediated primarily through the VDR expressed in neurons, astrocytes, and microglia. Activation of the VDR influences calcium homeostasis, antioxidant pathways, neurotransmission, and neuroinflammatory signaling – processes critically implicated in seizure pathology and ASM-related neurotoxicity.[18] Due to limited evidence on the efficacy of Vit-D3 in SE, this study evaluated its pre-and post-treatment effects on seizure control using the lithium–pilocarpine-induced SE rat model. The study also assessed the role in epilepsy-pathophysiology and neuroprotective potential through molecular assessment, which is discussed below. Comparative analysis with standard ASMs was conducted to explore possible adjuvant benefits. Vit-D3 alone reduced stage 3/4 seizures versus SE controls and enhanced the seizure-suppressing effects of ASMs when administered in combination with them. Based on previous studies, Vit-D3 intervention has shown seizure control potential against PTZ-induced acute and kindling seizures.[16] Previous studies indicate increased VDR expression post-SE induction relative to controls; similarly, Vit-D3 supplementation elevates VDR levels in drug-resistant epilepsy, highlighting a possible role of Vit-D3 in epileptogenesis.[171835]

Working memory and spatial memory retention were both significantly impaired in the lithium pilocarpine-induced SE model. Prior research has documented the beneficial impacts of Vit-D3 on behaviour and learning.[1436] Elevated plus maze results showed reduced neurobehavioral deficits in all treated groups, with ASM + Vit-D3 improving memory over ASM alone. Although this study demonstrated that Vit-D3 has a positive effect on cortical tissues, the Vit-D3 group by itself did not significantly protect against neuronal ischemic alterations in the hippocampus area. Moreover, by significantly reducing the NSE level, a hallmark of neurodegeneration and histopathological alterations, Vit-D3 in combination with ASMs (VPA and PER) enhanced neuroprotection. However, according to a recent preclinical investigation, Vit-D3 exhibits neuroprotective benefits against KA-and PTZ-induced apoptosis in the rat hippocampal CA3 region.[1437]

The neuroprotective effects of ASMs, as assessed through NeuN, which identifies neuronal loss and GFAP, which detects reactive astrocytes, were significantly enhanced in Vit-D3 and combination groups versus SE controls in the cortex and hippocampus.[3839] Pilocarpine-induced models exhibited persistent astrogliosis and microglial activation, contributing to epileptogenesis.[40] This study revealed that Vit-D3 in combination with VPA and PER improved neuronal protection, producing more viable neurons than the SE control group and on par with the healthy control group. Significantly higher reactive astrocytes were seen in the SE-control group than in either of the treatment groups.

Strengths

The LiCl–pilocarpine model of SE used in this study is well-established and closely resembles the pathophysiological characteristics of human temporal lobe epilepsy. To assess the immediate and long-term neuroprotective effects of Vit-D3 and its combination with antiseizure drugs (ASMs), the experimental design was extensive and included both acute and long-term phases. To provide a strong mechanistic understanding, the study also employed a multidisciplinary assessment method that included behavioural (learning and memory), histological, immunohistochemical (NeuN and GFAP), and ultrastructural electron microscopy investigations. In addition, blinding during histology and behavioral assessments reduced observer bias and improved data reliability.

Limitations

The study included only male rats, and therefore, possible sex-related differences in Vit-D3 metabolism and ASM response were not addressed. EEG confirmation of seizures was not conducted. Future research could investigate molecular confirmation of pathways such as VDR, Nrf2, and NF-κB signaling to further support the mechanistic conclusions.

Conclusion

Vit-D3 supplementation combined with antiseizure drugs such as VPA and PER was found to have significant neuroprotective effects in a lithium pilocarpine-induced SE model. These effects included a reduction in seizure severity, an improvement in cognitive outcomes, an enhancement of antioxidant responses in the brain, and a decrease in neuronal damage and glial activation linked to epilepsy-related pathophysiology as determined by electron microscopy, immunohistochemistry, and biomarker levels. These results demonstrate the possibility of Vit-D3 as an adjuvant treatment for epilepsy, with benefits such as antioxidant, neuroprotective, and antiseizure actions. This suggests that Vit-D3 has the potential to enhance conventional ASMs in managing SE and mitigating its long-term neurological effects.

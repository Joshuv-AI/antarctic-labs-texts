Efficacy of transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation for the treatment of gastrointestinal dysfunction in patients after cardiac surgery with cardiopulmonary bypass: study protocol for a superiority, single-centre, single-blind, randomised controlled clinical trial
Authors: Yingying Liao, Qiong Gao, Yanxian Chen, Hui Li, Jing Wang, Fengnan Zheng, Shaoyan Lin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveGastrointestinal dysfunction is one of the most frequent complications in patients after cardiac surgery with cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB). It can seriously affect the patient’s quality of life, increase the length of hospitalisation and in severe cases trigger cardiac arrest. This single-centre randomised controlled study aims to investigate whether transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation (TEAS) can effectively relieve gastrointestinal discomfort and improve the quality of life of patients after cardiac surgery.Methods and analysisA total of 426 patients after cardiac surgery with CPB will be randomly assigned to the control group or intervention group (1:1 ratio) to receive either acupressure or TEAS for 3 days. The patients will be recruited from the Department of Cardiovascular Surgery of Nanfang Hospital. The primary outcome is the time of the first postoperative anal defecation, the secondary outcomes include the first postoperative anal exsufflation time, the number of bowel sounds, the proportion of postoperative laxatives used in the two groups, abdominal girth, stool frequency per day and length of postoperative hospital stay.Ethics and disseminationThe procedures have been approved by the Guangdong Regional Medical Ethics Committee of Nanfang Hospital (NFEC-2023–404) and conformed to the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent will be obtained from all the participants. Results will be disseminated through workshops, peer-reviewed publications and conferences.Trial registrationRegistered in Chinese Trial Registry (https://www.chictr.org.cn/) on 1 November 2023 (Registered number: ChiCTR2300077211). Introduction

Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) remain the leading killer threatening human health worldwide. Globally, the number of people living with CVDs has reached 520 million, while in China, this figure stands at 330 million. Among these patients, nearly 70% require surgical intervention.1 Cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) is indispensable as life support in cardiac surgery, but it is associated with significant gastrointestinal complications.2 Constipation is one of the most predominant symptoms of gastrointestinal dysfunction, defined as having fewer than three bowel movements per week.3 4 Studies have consistently demonstrated that approximately 39.2% of postcardiac surgery patients experience constipation.1 5 Patients undergoing cardiac surgery with CPB are at risk of various perfusion-related injuries, including ischaemia and hypoxia, largely attributable to extended anaesthesia durations.5 CPB is associated with profound reductions in mucosal blood flow and mesenteric perfusion alterations due to primary endothelial dysfunction, which may be further exacerbated by the use of vasoconstrictors during CPB.6 7 The disparity between general and regional oxygen supply and demand results in the development of mucosal hypoxia and intestinal barrier dysfunction.6 7 Ultimately, these conditions can lead to damage to the intestinal mucosa or gastrointestinal motility disorders. Furthermore, postoperative factors such as pain, anxiety, and the use of analgesics and sedatives further contribute to gastrointestinal dysfunction, often leading to abdominal distension and subsequent constipation.79 In those with underlying CVD, constipation can exacerbate health risks significantly. The elevation in intra-abdominal and intracardiac pressures associated with constipation may lead to increased heart rates and myocardial oxygen demand, substantially augmenting cardiac workload. This haemodynamic change can potentially trigger cardiac arrhythmias or, in extreme cases, sudden cardiac death.10 Moreover, prolonged constipation may result in excessive absorption of intestinal toxins, clinically manifesting as dizziness, headache, appetite loss, nausea, vomiting, insomnia, forgetfulness and other symptoms. In severe cases, it can affect cognitive functions, including thinking and memory.1114

Multiple studies show that acupressure can relieve constipation.1517 However, acupressure is labour-intensive and it is difficult to ensure homogeneity for each patient. Based on the foundation of acupressure, several scholars have adopted the intervention programme of transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation (TEAS) to alleviate gastrointestinal dysfunction after abdominal surgery.1821 Although some investigations have shown the effectiveness of TEAS, there still lacks evidence concerning TEAS intervention programmes appropriate for patients without abdominal surgery.

Therefore, this study aims to investigate whether TEAS will relieve gastrointestinal discomfort and improve the quality of life of patient with cardiac surgery. Our main hypothesis is that the degree of improvement in gastrointestinal function would be significantly improved in the experimental group compared with the control group by assessing the time to the first anal defecation. We present the protocol of a single-centre clinical trial to evaluate the change in the severity of gastrointestinal discomfort in patients after cardiac surgery with CPB who will undergo TEAS, in comparison to acupressure. We will conduct a randomised, single-blind clinical trial. As secondary endpoints, we will assess changes in anal exsufflation, abdominal circumference, bowel sounds and quality of life. This trial will inform about the effectiveness of these interventions. In addition, we will evaluate safety and cost-effectiveness.

Methods and analysis

Study design and setting

The present study is a superiority, single-centre, single-blind clinical trial. This study uses a prospective randomised controlled design and commenced on 1 January 2024, and will be completed on 31 December 2025. A total of 426 patients who meet the inclusion criteria will be randomly assigned to either the intervention or control group (1:1 ratio). The primary outcome (the time of the first postoperative anal defecation), and secondary outcome (the first postoperative anal exsufflation time, the number of bowel sounds, abdominal girth and the proportion of postoperative laxatives used in the two groups) will be measured on postoperative day 1, day 2, day 3 and day 7. Participants

Inclusion criteria

Patients must comply with all of the following inclusion criteria: (1) The patient meets the diagnostic criteria and indications for cardiac surgery; (2) There has been no history of habitual constipation before the operation, no history of long-term use of any laxative drugs, no history of abdominal distension or constipation within 3 months, and glycerin enema is used for routine bowel preparation 1 day before the operation; (3) Age 18–80 years old; (4) Patients are undergoing thoracotomy with CPB; (5) Informed consent is obtained voluntarily; (6) No limb dysfunction; (7) No cognitive impairment, communication disorder or mental abnormality; (8) Not participating in other clinical studies.

Exclusion criteria

Patients should not meet any of the following exclusion criteria: (1) The history of definite gastrointestinal bleeding or gastroduodenal ulcer; (2) Patients who have undergone gastrointestinal surgery within the past year; (3) Patients with severe malnutrition; (4) Concomitant malignant tumours or other than heart disease; (5) Pregnant and lactating patients; (6) Patients with intolerance to electrical stimulation.

Recruitment

Patients will be recruited from the Department of Cardiovascular Surgery of Nanfang Hospital, the research does not need to publish advertisements, and all patients belong to the department patients from 1 January 2024 to 31 December 2025. Patients who match the inclusion criteria will be recruited. Before recruitment, they will be informed of the possible benefits and risks of exercise in the study. Then, the participants will be required to fill out an informed consent and can withdraw from the study without any conditions.

Withdrawal criteria

Any patient will be withdrawn from the study if any of the following criteria: (1) any condition interrupting the study procedures; (2) decision of the patient; (3) any serious adverse event (AE) associated with treatments used in the study. Data from all withdrawn participants will be included in the intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis up to the point of their withdrawal.

Sample size calculation

We will compare the reduction in the time to the first bowel movement between the two groups. We will use quantitative variables as the primary outcome measure and use the population mean formula method to calculate: N1=N2=2σ2(Zα/2+Zβ)2/δ2, with two-sided testing level, α=0.05, β=0.20, Z0.05/2=1.96, Z0.2=0.84. According to the results of the pre-experiment, the main outcome measure of the control group was 86.11±23.81, and the experimental group was 79.56±16.50, δ=6, σ=21, the calculated sample size for our study design is 193 per group. Accounting for an anticipated dropout rate of 10%, we plan to recruit a total of 213 patients each group.

Usual care

All patients will undergo their first operation in our department’s operating room. Patients in both groups will receive routine perioperative care, which includes the following:

The defecation status of all patients will be tracked from postoperative day 4 to day 7. The observation period for the primary outcome (time to first defecation) will begin on return to the Intensive Care Unit (ICU) and continue until the first postoperative anal defecation. If no defecation has occurred by the end of postoperative day 3, laxatives or low-pressure enemas will be administered based on the patient’s condition starting on day 4.

Psychological care: Within 24 hours after admission, attending nurses will provide psychological counselling to alleviate patients’ fear and anxiety. Individualised psychological support will be offered throughout hospitalisation based on the patient’s emotional state.

Acupoint therapy methods

Team training criteria

For the team training, under leadership organisation, both group members will receive intensive training on preoperative health education, intervention methods, precautions and outcome monitoring. Operators will be systematically trained by rehabilitation department acupuncture professionals on operation essentials, correct acupoint selection and proper technique (gradually increasing pressure based on patient sensations of soreness, numbness, swelling and pain). Only operators achieving ≥90 points on assessment will be permitted to perform interventions, with strict protocol adherence maintained through regular feedback meetings. To ensure each procedure is delivered as specified: (1) A detailed standard operating procedure manual with pictures of acupoint locations and operation steps was provided to all operators. (2) The first TEAS treatment for each patient was supervised by a senior trainer to ensure correct electrode placement and parameter setting. (3) Regular spot checks were performed throughout the trial to monitor adherence to the protocol regarding acupoint location, treatment duration and intensity recording.22 23

Control group

The patients in the control group will receive acupressure treatment. According to the location of the acupoints, patients will be assisted in assuming supine and lateral positions. A total of eight Sifeng (EX-UE10) points will be selected—four on each hand, located at the midpoint of the palmar surface of the proximal interphalangeal joints of the second to fifth fingers. Additionally, the following nine acupoints will be selected: Zhongwan (CV12), Tianshu (ST25), Guanyuan (CV4), Qihai (CV6), Pishu (BL20), Dachangshu (BL25), Changqiang (GV1), Zhigou (SJ6) and Zusanli (ST36).

After hand hygiene is performed, the operator will prick the Sifeng points on both hands, stimulating all eight points. Each point will be pricked approximately 60 times per session, lasting 3–5 min in total, two times per day. The endpoint of treatment is local redness and fullness (or: until local congestion is achieved).

The operator will then use the thumb pulp to apply stroking from Zhongwan (CV12) to Guanyuan (CV4) five to six times. Bilateral Tianshu (ST25) points will be pressed and kneaded for 2 min. Pressure will be applied gradually from light to heavy until the patient experiences a sensation of soreness and distension. The pinching method1 2 will be applied along the paraspinal muscles from Pishu (BL20) to Dachangshu (BL25) five times on both sides, with focused stimulation on Pishu (BL20), Weishu (BL21), Dachangshu (BL25) and Shenshu (BL23). Subsequently, Changqiang (GV1) will be kneaded with the middle finger or thumb until a sore and distending sensation is achieved. Finally, Zhigou (SJ6) and Zusanli (ST36) points will be pressed and kneaded for 1 min each. The intervention will be conducted from postoperative day 1 to day 3, two times per day (from 8:00 to 10:00 in the morning and from 16:00 to 18:00 in the afternoon).

Intervention group

The patients in the experimental group will be treated with TEAS. The first is to select acupoints, including bilateral Zusanli, bilateral Shangjuxu, bilateral Neiguan and Tianshu. The surrounding skin is disinfected, and sebum is removed with an alcohol cotton swab (alcohol allergy patients are treated with cleaning wipes). Then, the electrodes are attached to the acupoints. The electrical stimulation instrument (medium-frequency therapeutic instrument, Model YK-2000B) is turned on, with the frequency set to 2 Hz, the duration set to 30 min and the intensity set to the maximum tolerable level for the participant (10–30 mA).24 The intervention is administered from postoperative day 1 to day 3, two times per day: 8:00–10:00 a.m. and 16:00–18:00 p.m.18 25

Assessment

Basic information

Basic information includes demographic data and vital signs. The demographic data includes age, gender, level of education, diet state and so on.

Primary outcomes

The time of the first postoperative anal defecation: The time elapsed between the patient’s return to the ICU after surgery and the patient’s first postoperative spontaneous anal defecation. The data will be obtained from the clinical record sheet.

Secondary outcome

The first postoperative anal exsufflation time

The time elapsed between the patient’s return to the ICU after surgery and the patient’s first postoperative spontaneous anal exsufflation. This time is reported by the patient or documented by the responsible nurse.

The number of bowel sounds

The frequency of bowel sounds per minute is monitored regularly daily. Three different positions of the lower abdomen of the patient were auscultated by stethoscope, and each auscultation time was 1 min. The average value of three measurements is calculated and recorded.

Abdominal girth

The abdominal circumference of the patients is monitored regularly every day. The value is measured with a tape at the point 3 cm below the navel and recorded.

The proportion of postoperative laxatives used in the two groups

The frequency of daily defecation and the proportion of postoperative laxative or enema use will be documented from ICU admission until the seventh postoperative day. Length of postoperative hospital stay will also be recorded.

Randomisation and blinding

Eligibility will be confirmed by a research clinician prior to randomisation. An independent statistician who is not involved in patient recruitment or intervention will generate the random allocation sequence (using block randomisation with varying block sizes) through an online randomisation platform (www.randomizer.org). The sequence will be concealed within the platform. After obtaining informed consent and confirming eligibility, a research nurse who is not involved in outcome assessment will log in to the platform to obtain the group assignment and inform the intervention instructors accordingly. No stratification or minimisation will be used. It is difficult to blind instructors and patients in this trial for different interventions, and the patient knows their scheme, unknown to their group. However, outcome assessors and statistical analysts will be blind to the procedure and result of randomisation, group allocation and intervention. Given the nature of the interventions, it is not possible to blind the participants or the instructors delivering acupressure/TEAS.

However, to minimise performance and assessment bias, the following procedures will be implemented: (1) Outcome assessors (nurses collecting data on defecation, exhaust, bowel sounds and abdominal circumference) will be blinded to group allocation. They will be instructed not to inquire about or discuss the treatment with patients. (2) To reduce the risk of unblinding, outcome assessments will be conducted at standardised times (eg, every morning) before the daily interventions are applied. For example, electrodes for TEAS will be placed and removed immediately after each session, minimising visual cues during assessment periods. Similarly, acupressure sessions are conducted at fixed times, and assessments are scheduled outside these windows. (3) Statisticians performing the data analysis will be blinded to group allocation (the groups will be labelled as Group A and Group B until the analysis is complete).

Safety assessments

The intervention used in this study is TEAS, which has a low current and does not cause any harm to the human body after repeated pre-experiments. Moreover, many studies have proved the safety of TEAS.1720 All participants, regardless of group allocation, will be monitored throughout the study period for the occurrence of any AEs). Patients will be instructed to report any discomfort immediately to their bedside nurse or any member of the research team. All AEs reported by patients or observed by clinical staff (including outcome assessors) will be recorded in the case report form. The nature, severity, duration and relationship to the intervention of any AE will be assessed by an independent clinical safety monitor who is blinded to group allocation.

Statistical analysis

The data will be analysed by an independent statistician blinded to the group assignments using statistical software. The data analysis will adhere to the ITT principle, encompassing all randomly assigned participants. Regardless of their adherence to the intervention or whether they withdrew from the study, they will remain in the group they were originally assigned to for the analysis. According to the literature reports, the time to the first bowel movement and the time to first flatus will not conform to the normal distribution, and the differences between groups will be tested by the non-parametric rank sum test. Repeated measures analysis of variance will be used to compare the bowel sounds and abdominal circumference between the two groups. Count data will be expressed as numbers or percentages.

Data integrity and management

All data will be collected using Excel. Paper copies of the questionnaires (if participants prefer to complete the questionnaires on paper) will be stored in locked filing cabinets with restricted access. Electronic data will be stored in Excel and exported to a password-protected server after data collection, with identifying and non-identifying information separated. The codes that link data to identifying participant information will be stored separately from the study data, under password protection and with restricted access.

Ethics and dissemination

This study protocol has been approved by the Guangdong Regional Medical Ethics Committee of Nanfang Hospital (NFEC-2023–404). The trial will be conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent will be obtained from all participating patients or their legally authorised representatives prior to any study procedures. Results will be disseminated through workshops, peer-reviewed publications and conferences.

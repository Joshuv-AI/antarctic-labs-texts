A novel non-invasive imaging technique for mapping microplastic accumulation and distribution in different organ systems of Indian major carp Labeo rohita (Ham. 1822)
Authors: Sneha Siwach, Aviral Singh, Soumyadeep Paul, Vanika Gupta, Ishaan Gupta, Ram Krishan Negi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Graphical abstractKeywords: Microplastics, Plastic Pollution, Micro-CT Imaging, Fluorescence Microscopy, Labeo rohita

Introduction

Fishes are widely recognized as model organisms for monitoring aquatic ecosystem health and are key focus to ecotoxicological research because of their ecological, economic and nutritional importance (Bancel et al., 2024). Their physiological responses provide critical insights into the impacts of environmental stressors, like persistent organic pollutants, pesticides, on aquatic environments (Canosa and Bertucci, 2023, Lionetto, 2026). Anthropogenic stressors, like rapid industrialization, urbanization and the pervasive release of pollutants which are persistent in nature, are reshaping the aquatic environments at unprecedented rates. Among these emerging contaminants, microplastics (MPs; <5 mm) have gained global traction and attention owing to their ubiquity, omnipresence and capacity to interact with biota across various trophic levels (Cobos et al., 2025, Tursi et al., 2022). Unlike the persistent chemical pollutants, MPs are particulate insoluble in nature and are often actively ingested by aquatic organisms because their size, colour and buoyancy mimics their natural prey or food and could be through the foraging and filter feeding (Jolaosho et al., 2025). This active and passive ingestion of the MPs can lead to gastrointestinal blockage (Wang et al., 2025, Xia et al., 2024), nutritional impairment (Pironti et al., 2021), oxidative stress due to reactive oxygen species (ROS) (Kadac-Czapska et al., 2024a), immune dysregulation (Yang et al., 2022), neurotoxicity (Zheng et al., 2026) and disruptions of the endocrine (Tyc et al., 2025) and skeletal systems in aquatic organisms (Ding et al., 2018; Kadac-Czapska et al., 2024b; Lionetto, 2026). These effects further exacerbated by the leaching of endocrine-disrupting additives such as plasticizers (phthalates and bisphenol BPA derivatives) (Chen et al., 2019, Dalamaga et al., 2024, Ramadan et al., 2020). The distribution and translocation macro and nano particle in fish (Ma et al., 2021), environmental interaction and health risk (Pal et al., 2024) and the mechanisms and spatial patterns of this re-distribution remain insufficiently documented and characterized (Banaee et al., 2025).

Recent advances in the imaging techniques have brought micro-computed tomography (µ-CT) to the forefront as a promising non-invasive imaging modality for aquatic toxicology (Brinkmann et al., 2016, Caputo et al., 2024, Parobková et al., 2025). µ−CT produces high-resolution three-dimensional volumetric datasets based on intrinsic radiodensity differences among organ system and tissues, enabling precise anatomical visualization and quantitative assessment of organ structures without the dissection and sacrifice of the organism (Farini et al., 2013, Holdsworth and Thornton, 2002, Keklikoglou et al., 2021). This technique has been extensively used in biomedical research, especially for pre-clinical studies in small sized rodents and testing of orthopaedic diseases and parameters in zebrafish (Parobková et al., 2025), however, its application to larger and ecologically significant fish species is limited. Emerging imaging and spectral techniques, including computed tomography (CT) (Strafella et al., 2024), Py-GC–MS/MS (Almeida et al., 2026), multi-spectral imaging; like, Near-Infrared Hyperspectral Imaging (NIR-HSI) (C. Zhu et al., 2020), Dark-field Hyperspectral microscopy (Li et al., 2024, Nigamatzyanova and Fakhrullin, 2021), Vis-NIR Multispectral Imaging (MSI), (López-Rosales et al., 2022, Makmuang and Aït-Kaddour, 2025) and optical coherence tomography (OCT) (Asani et al., 2023, Barroso Pena et al., 2020), have demonstrated potential for the non-invasive detection of MPs; however, their application remains largely confined to the small model organisms and therefore lacks integration with validation frameworks and ecologically sound and environmentally relevant exposure scenarios. Studies on rainbow trout (Oncorhynchus mykiss), demonstrated that µ-CT can reliably quantify organ structures and detect copper (Cu) pollutant-induced alterations, indicating strong potential for integration into the eco-toxicological frameworks and for reducing the number of animals required for experimental research (Brinkmann et al., 2016, Jakubowska et al., 2022).

Despite the growing recognition of the hazardous MP toxicity, current methodologies for their polymer detection in biological matrices predominantly rely on chemical digestion, density separation, and spectroscopic identification, which are often time-consuming, destructive and prone to cross-contamination, thereby limiting accurate spatial interpretation of MP distribution within intact organisms (Gqomfa et al., 2025, Li et al., 2026; Nandikes et al., 2024; Rani et al., 2023). Beside the above mentioned method, the conventional analytical techniques for MP detection such as, FTIR (Fourier Transform Infrared Spectroscopy), Raman spectroscopy have been employed polymer identification (Karpenko et al., 2022, Xie et al., 2025), and emerging approach like (Atomic Force Microscopy) AFM-IR have potential for nano scale characterization of MP (Akhatova et al., 2022; Y. Zhu et al., 2024). These techniques are constrained by extensive sample preparation, low throughput and inherently disrupt the tissue architecture, precise spatial localization of MP within organs systems, highlighting the need for non-destructive spatially resolved imaging approaches, have led to the motivation behind the development of radiological and optical imaging approached that preserve tissue architecture. µ-CT also helps in the detection of MPs rapidly and efficiently in zebrafish despite their low radiodensity (Parobková et al., 2025, Ren et al., 2025). It also provides an automated identification and volumetric quantification of MP particles with minimal risk of cross-contamination. Studies have shown strong agreement between µ-CT derived particle size distributions and conventional analytical methods, as well as the reliable detection of plastics known composition in biological matrices (Parobková et al., 2025, Strafella et al., 2024). Fluorescence microscopy further enhances the detection sensitivity, enabling visualization of labelled MPs within biological matrices. However, the combined potential of µ-CT and fluorescence microscopy for tracking MP ingestion, accumulation and inter-organ distribution in aquatic organisms remains largely unexplored. Recent advancements emphasize the integration of complementary detection techniques, such as fluorescence labelling and spectroscopic validation, to improve MP detection accuracy as well as reduce false positives associated with the single-method approaches. Therefore, there is a crucial need for an integrative, non-destructive methodologies capable of simultaneously resolving MP localization, exposure route dynamics, and tissue-specific distribution/translocation within intact organisms under environmentally realistic conditions.

In this pioneer study where we integrated µ-CT and fluorescence microscopy to localize and quantify MP ingestion and accumulation in Labeo rohita to establish a novel imaging-based framework for ecotoxicological assessment. Labeo rohita, a key aquaculture species in South Asia and a model organism for freshwater toxicology, was exposed to graded concentration of fluorescently tagged polystyrene (PS) microplastics.

The present study aims to provide a novel, non-invasive and robust procedure for the quantification of fluorescence-labelled MP in many organ systems with the preservation of anatomic relationships. The integrated approach enhances the reliability of microplastic detection, enables comprehensive spatial mapping within whole organisms, and supports ethically aligned longitudinal research. Collectively, the findings advance methodological innovation in ecotoxicology and provide deeper insight into vertebrate responses to MP pollution.

Material and methods

Microplastics

Fluorescently tagged spherical polystyrene MPs (10 µm) [excitation: 360 nm, emission: 407 nm] used in the present study was procured from Polysciences, Asia-Pacific, Inc. They were supplied in deionized (DI) water as dispersions (4.55 × 107 particles/mL) and were stored at 4 °C in cool, dark place and sonicated before use. The fluorescent microplastics investigated in this study were polystyrene-based microspheres with ρ = 1.05 g/cm−3. The purity of the PS MPs was verified using a Thermo Scientific Nicolet iN10 FT-IR spectrometer (USA). PS is chosen as it is the most widely manufactured and used plastic in the worldwide, which could often be found in almost everywhere in the environment in the form of microplastics. The selection of the fluorescently tagged spherical PS beads was primarily motivated by their uniform geometry and size distribution, which enabled reliable detection, validation and cross-verification of MPs.

Experimental set-up

Healthy juvenile fish Labeo rohita (Khan and Jhingran, 1975) mean length of 8.84 ± 1.12 cm and a mean body weight of 10.11 ± 2.34 g, were procured from the local hatchery (Sultan Fish Farms, Karnal, Haryana). Detailed fish length and weight data are provided in Supplementary Table (Table S1). After acclimatization fishes were divided into five experimental groups consisting of 10 individuals in each 50 L glass tanks (Supplementary Fig. 1). The tanks were siphoned and 70% of water was replaced every third day to maintain water quality.

The experiment comprised five treatment groups, each containing ten size-matched juvenile Labeo rohita. Fish were acclimatized once for 3-4 weeks in aerated tanks containing dechlorinated tap water and were fed a commercial pelleted diet twice daily prior to the start of exposure process. Following acclimatization, fluorescently tagged spherical polystyrene MPs (10 µm) were prepared as a homogenous working suspension from a concentrated stock solution using a probe Sonicator (Sonics VCX 750 Probe Sonicator, Sonics and Materials Inc., USA) immediately prior to administration. Microplastic exposure was conducted via two routes: direct addition to the water column along with the regular feed (waterborne exposure; Tanks 2 & 3) and incorporation into the commercial fish feed (dietary exposure; Tanks 4 & 5), while Tank 1 served as the control. Exposure concentrations were increased with an increment of 0.1% (Day 1–7), 0.5% (Day 8–14), 1% (Day 15–21), 10% (Day 22–28). Percentage exposure concentrations denote to the volumetric (v/v) proportion of the microplastic working suspension made from the stock solution, added relative to the total tank water volume. To maintain water quality, simulate environmentally relevant dilution, and minimize MP accumulation and sedimentation, approximately 70% of the tank water was renewed weekly, while faecal matter and uneaten feed were removed daily by siphoning.

Fish were fed commercial feed diet at 4% of their total body weight per day, with portion size adjusted weekly according to mean body weight and distributed equally into two feedings per day. For waterborne exposure groups (Tanks 2 & 3), a working suspension of fluorescently tagged spherical PS MPs (10 µm; 9.10 × 104 particles/mL) was added directly to the tank water using a micropipette at graded concentrations of 0.1%, 0.5%, 1% and 10% over a 28-day period. For dietary exposure groups (Tanks 4 & 5), the same concentrations of the working suspension were homogeneously incorporated into the commercial feed, then making its small pellets prior to feeding the fish (Fig. 1). MP exposure concentrations and feed quantities were adjusted weekly according to the experimental schedule, with an increment of 0.1% (Day 1–7), 0.5% (Day 8–14), 1% (Day 15–21), 10% (Day 22–28). At the end of each 7-day intervals, deep siphoning was performed and 70% of the tank water was replaced to maintain water quality as well as removal of suspended MP particles. The fish specimens were euthanized following Committee for the Purpose of Control and Supervision of Experiments on Animals (CPCSEA) guidelines. At the completion of the exposure period, fish were collected and processed according to downstream analysis. For fluorescence microscopy, gills, GI tract and muscle tissues were carefully excised to assess MP ingestion and tissue distribution. For Micro-CT analysis, intact whole fish were preserved in alcohol to maintain tissue integrity, and enable non-destructive, 3-D localization of internalized MPs.

Schematic illustration of the preparation of fluorescent polystyrene (PS) microplastics [10 µm] in distilled water using sonication, incorporation of the suspension into commercial fish feed, drying of the prepared feed, and subsequent administration to Labeo rohita in experimental tanks.

Micro-CT Scanning: Non-Destructive 3D localization of microplastics

Micro-CT (µ-CT) scanning was performed using a Bruker SkyScan 1273, Belgium; to non-invasively visualize and localize PS-MPs within the intact fish samples. Scanning parameters were optimized for soft-tissue contrast and low-density particle detection, with the X-ray source operated at 55 kV and 130 µA, a 0.5 mm Al filter was used. Tomography generates about 16-bit vast 3D matrix, wherein each of the voxel represents the local X-ray transparency of the matter, which is linked to the density. Contrast enhanced µ-CT scans were performed with 0.3° rotation step, and frame averaging of 3, to increase the image quality. These settings produced a reconstructed voxel of ∼ 10–11 µm, enabling reliable detection of 10 µm MPs. Two-three fish specimens were collected from each tank after every 7 days of total exposure 28-day exposure period to capture temporal patterns of MP ingestion and accumulation using µ-CT (Fig. 2).

Schematic diagram depicting the µ-CT imaging workflow, including positioning of the intact fish specimen within the CT-scanner, image acquisition, digital reconstruction, and visualization of internal anatomical structures for the spatial mapping of fluorescently tagged PS-MPs.

Given the voxel size (3–11 µm) and the comparable radiodensity of PS microplastics and soft tissues, µ-CT based absolute quantification remains technically challenging. However, this limitation doesn’t compromise the ability of µ-CT to reliably detect, localize and compare relative MP presence across exposure route and time-points. The present study focused on qualitative and spatial detection of microplastics rather than absolute particle quantification. This approach emphasises anatomical localization and exposure-route specific retention patterns, which are critical for interpreting biological relevance. Advances in contrast enhancement and segmentation algorithms may enable future quantitive µ-CT based assessment of MP burden.

Reconstruction of the images was performed using NRecon producing cross-section slices from the acquired µ-CT angular projections through the specimen. Images were also reconstructed by applying optimized smoothing, bean-hardening, flat-field and ring-artefact corrections to minimize imaging distortions. The resulting high-resolution 3D datasets allowed precise, non-destructive mapping of MP distribution across the GI tract, gill tissues, musculature, consistent with µ-CT applications previously validated in fish ecotoxicology and MP detection frameworks. This approach also enables discrete distinct discrimination of exposure route-specific retention patterns as well as preservation of anatomical context, which cannot be achieved using conventional destructive analytical techniques.

Fluorescence Microscopy: Organ-Specific detection of microplastics

Fluorescence microscopy was used as a complementary technique to validate µ-CT observations and to further confirm the presence, localization and distribution of PS-MPs at tissue level. Tissue-specific detection of MPs was performed using a fluorescence-based workflow (Fig. 3, Fig. 4) following the exposure regime, 2 fish were randomly sampled from each tank at 7-day intervals (Days 7, 14, 21 and 28) for tissue-specific fluorescence microscopy analysis. GI tract, gills and muscle tissues were carefully extracted from each specimen and processed separately in two different processes; one was subjected to oxidative digestion using 20 mL of 30% H2O2 (Hydrogen peroxide) and then incubated at 60 °C for around 24–48 h to remove organic matter the digested samples were then subsequently subjected to density separation using 30 mL saturated NaCl solution to isolate MP particles. The resulting suspensions were filtered using vacuum filtration by glass fiber filters (Whatman GF/C Membrane Filter, 47 mm Diameter, 0.45 µm pore size).The retained particles on the filters were subsequently examined under a fluorescence microscope to identify and localize fluorescently tagged PS-MPs within individual tissues. In the second procedure, they were then cautiously mounted on glass slides using forceps and were airdried to fix the tissues. Fluorescently tagged 10 µm beads were visualized using an EVOS M5000 fluorescent microscope (Invitrogen-Thermofisher Scientific, USA), employing blue excitation channels optimized for PS microsphere emission.

Experimental workflow for the tissue-specific processing of gills, GI tract and muscle tissues applying per-oxidative digestion and density separation, followed by vacuum filtration and fluorescence microscopy in order to detect retained microplastics.

Methodological overview describing the ingestion of fluorescently labeled PS-MPs through feed, followed by dissection of the fish exposed to them, mounting of the isolated organs on slides, and fluorescence microscopy to visualize the retained microplastics.

Fluorescence signals which corresponded to the individual MPs revealed distinct, spherical particles with no ostensible surface degradation or loss of fluorescence intensity. Fluorescent images were acquired under standardized exposure setting to enable consistent detection and comparative assessment of MP particles abundance over time. Sporadic clustering of MPs was observed in GI tract and muscle tissues suggesting localized accumulation rather than physicochemical alteration of morphology of MP particles. The experimental workflow ensured sensitive, contamination-free verification of MP particles across the tissues and hence, provided complimentary optical confirmation of MP particles observed in µ-CT data-sets.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted to test the exposure-route and time-dependent differences in MP detection across the imaging modalities. Since the µ-CT outputs provided relative feature counts rather than absolute MP particle number, non-parametric analyses are employed throughout. MP burden differences resulting from the exposure concentrations, tissue types and the time-points were assessed using Kruskal-Wallis statistics, with a Dunn’s multiple comparison post-hoc test.

Temporal trends in MP accumulation were assessed by Spearman’s rank correlation between the exposure duration and relative MP detection frequency. The agreement of µ-CT derived MP features with fluorescence microscopy-based particle counts was determined by the Spearman rank correlation (ρ) to validate cross-modal concordance. Statistical significance was accepted at p < 0.05. All the analyses were performed using R software (R-4.5.2). Detailed statistical outputs, including correlation coefficients and fold-change metrics, are provided in Supplementary Fig. S3 and Supplementary table S2.

Quality assessment and control (QA/QC)

Standard laboratory and systematic QA/QC operating protocols with stringent quality controls were implemented throughout each of the experimental procedures to assure reliability and replicability of the data and to minimize potential contamination. All of the glassware that were being used in experimentation were precleaned, treated with chromic acid. Sample preparation and tissue digestion were performed under laminar flow hood. The glassware was washed with filtered double distilled water and precisely wrapped with aluminium foil. Cotton laboratory coats and nitrile gloves were worn during all the procedures to reduce any contamination. All solutions used in digestion and density separation processes were prepared using filtered double distilled water, and glass fiber filters were used for separation procedure. Procedural blanks were periodically examined to verify the absence of background MP contamination during sample processing.

Results

Micro-CT imaging

This study demonstrates the application of high-resolution µ-CT for three-dimensional, non-destructive reconstruction images of intact freshwater fish Labeo rohita, enabling precise micrometre-scale visualization of skeletal and internal soft tissue architecture. Thereby, establishing a robust anatomical framework for internal MP contaminant localization. Discrete spherical topographies of low X-ray attenuation and morphologies commensurate with the size of administered PS-MPs were discerned only in exposed specimens; no comparative features were observed in control individuals.

This corresponded to a stepwise weekly increase in exposure concentrations over the 28-day exposure period, during which a temporal increase in the frequency of detectable microplastic features was observed. In the initial exposure phase (0.1%, Days 1 to 7), µ-CT detectable features were sparse and mostly confined to regions corresponding to the gastrointestinal tract. When the exposure was increased to 0.5% and 1% (Days 8 to 21), the incidence of such features became more frequent and spatially dispersed, with sporadic detections in adjacent peripheral soft tissues. During the highest exposure level (10%, Days 22 to 28), the micro-CT reconstructions showed the most obvious presence of microplastic consistent features.

Semi-quantitative assessment of the µ-CT reconstructions reveals a pronounced, exposure- and time-dependent increase in features consistent with MPs. During the initial exposure period of 0.1% (Days 1–7), there were around 3.4 ± 1.2 relative MP-consistent features detected per specimen (Table 1), and those were primarily localized to the gastrointestinal region. At intermediate exposures (0.5–1%, Days 8–13 & 14–21), approximately [Day 14 = 9.7 ± 2.5], [Day 21: 14.6 ± 3.1] MPs were identifiable per specimen, with wider spatial distributions and occasional appearances in adjacent peripheral soft tissues. In contrast, µ-CT volumes obtained at the highest exposure level of 10% (Days 22–28) presented the highest relative burden, with 26.8 ± 4.6 MP-consistent features per specimen, which were highly distributed across the gastrointestinal and surrounding tissue regions. These MP counts reflect relative features inferred from reconstructed volumes, rather than absolute particle numbers, due to limited radiodensity contrast between polystyrene (PS) microplastics and soft tissues.

While not highly dense, but PS-MPs has a density comparable to that of fish soft tissues and its aromatic polymer architecture gives rise to measurable X-ray attenuation. Hence, in the µ-CT images some particles displayed attenuation values matching or locally exceeding those for nearby tissues or slender skeletal elements. This inconsistency arises from the behaviour of X-ray attenuation, not from intrinsic material density. µ-CT reconstructions supported the findings of fluorescence microscopy, exhibiting a distinctly higher detectable frequency of MPs in fish subjected to the waterborne exposure groups compared with those subjected to dietary exposure (Fig. 5), whereas in the control experiment no relative MP-consistent features were detected. MP-consistent features were mostly localized to regions corresponding to the gastrointestinal tract, with incidental presence in adjacent peripheral soft tissues. The complete absence of comparable high-contrast features in control fish across all time points, confirms that the detected morphotypic structures in the exposed fish specimens represent internalized relative MP-consistent features rather than imaging artefacts, background noise or endogenous anatomical components, thereby validating the specificity and reliability of the fluorescence and µ-CT based detection.

Representative µ-CT reconstructions depicting the whole-body and regional skeletal anatomy of exposed Labeo rohita. The different views focus on the vertebral column, ribs, and surrounding organs to provide an anatomical frame of reference for the spatial localization of internalized PS-MPs.

Non-parametric analysis showed a significant increase in relative MP consistent features detectable by the µ-CT over 28-day exposure period [Kruskal-Wallis test, H(3) = 9.21, p = 0.027]. Post-hoc Dunn’s tests indicated that the frequencies of MP features were significantly higher at days 21 & 28 compared to day 7 (p < 0.05). Fish exposed via the water-borne route also demonstrated significantly higher relative MP feature counts than those exposed via contaminated feed [Mann-Whitney U test; U = 18, p = 0.031]. Taken together, these findings validate the temporal trends and exposure-route–dependent patterns apparent within the reconstructed µ-CT datasets. Non-parametric trend analysis showed a strong monotonic relationship between exposure duration and the relative MP-consistent features identified by µ-CT [Spearman’s (ρ) = 0.74], quantitatively supporting the temporal patterns observed in reconstructed datasets (Supplementary: Fig. S4A & Table S3).

Fluorescence microscopy

Fluorescence microscopy revealed fluorescently labelled polystyrene microplastics throughout all the tissues studied, including the GI tract, gills, and muscle, after waterborne and dietary exposures. A distinct spherical fluorescent tagged MPs of similar size were clearly visible in tissue autofluorescence. The persistence of the strong fluorescence intensity throughout the exposure period, confirms the physicochemical stability of the labelled MPs, indicating that neither photobleaching nor particle degradation occurred during exposure or tissue processing. This finding supports the reliability of fluorescence microscopy for temporal as well as tissue-level tracking of MP retention. A dose responsive increase in PS-MP retention was observed over the 28-day exposure period, corresponding to weekly increase microplastic concentrations. During the initial low dose (0.1%, days 1–7), fluorescent signals were predominantly localized to the GI tract and gill tissues. Subsequently increase of dose to 0.5% (Days 8–14) and 1% (Days 15–21), PS-MP fluorescence became more frequent and spatially widespread, with detectable particles distributed across all examined tissues. Thereafter, fishes were exposure to 10% of MPs for 22–28 days) resulted remarkable high intensity of fluorescence as well as increased in incidence of particles across tissues (Table 1). Based on comparative analysis of the treatment groups revealed a clear exposure route-dependent pattern in PS-MP retention. Fish subjected to waterborne exposure (Tanks 2 and 3) exhibited consistently higher PS-MP accumulation relative to those receiving via dietary exposure (Tanks 4 and 5), irrespective of time points and tissues examined (Fig. 6). Whereas, across all assessed intervals and organs, no fluorescent MP signals were detected in the control fishes (Tank 1), thereby confirming the absence of background contamination and verified the validity of the control experiment.

Representative fluorescent micrographs of the muscle regions in Labeo rohita showing the retained fluorescently tagged PS-MPs within muscle fibers (A) and MPs recovered from the digestion process on a glass fiber filter paper (B) captured under different fluorescent filter settings.

MP particles were predominantly associated with the luminal surface and embedded within the mucosal folds of the GI tract in the waterborne exposure groups, with particle density increasing progressively over time. In contrast, dietary exposure resulted in a more restricted distribution, specifically confined to GI tract, with minimal detection in gill tissues. In tanks 2 & 3, the gills exhibited discrete fluorescent signals along filament and lamellar regions, indicating direct contact with suspended MPs in the surrounding water column (Fig. 7). Muscle tissues contained comparatively fewer particles than the GI tract and gills; however, the recurrent detection of MPs within the muscle fibres suggests systemic translocation and internal dissemination beyond primary sites of entry. Similar patterns of redistribution of MPs have been observed in tissues of both freshwater and marine water fish species, wherein MPs translocate from the GI tract to secondary tissues through multiple physiological routes, via para-cellular diffusion across epithelial junctions, circulatory transport, and inflammatory mediated permeability (Huang et al., 2021, Huang et al., 2025, Ma et al., 2021). The present study observed that MPs were occasional clustering most notably within the GI tract, while individual particles consistently retained their spherical morphology across all examined tissues indicating physical stability during internal transport.

Fluorescence microscopy revealed that with increasing exposure duration, the MP particles significantly increased in all investigated tissues. Statistical time-related differences in MP retention were obtained for the gastrointestinal tract, gills, and muscle using the Kruskal-Wallis test, but the gut had always the highest particle load (p < 0.05). Quantitive analysis of MP counts obtained from fluorescence microscopy confirmed a clear dose- and time-dependent increase in MP occurrence in fish gills, GI tract and muscle tissues (Table 1) MP burden in muscle seemed to occur mainly at medium to high exposure levels, indicating a delayed systemic redistribution. In agreement with the µ-CT findings, waterborne exposure resulted in significantly higher counts of fluorescence-detected MPs compared with dietary exposure for the same exposure times (p < 0.05).

Fluorescence-based MP counts showed a strong positive correlation with the exposure time [Spearman’s correlation coefficient (ρ) = 0.81]. A robust cross-modal agreement was also found between the counts from fluorescence and relative characteristics from the µ-CT [Spearman’s correlation coefficient (ρ) = 0.71], thus providing quantitative validation for the dual-imaging approach (Supplementary: Fig. S3(A); Table S2).

Fold-change analysis further demonstrated that the substantial increase in relative MP burden between day 7 and day 28 across whole organism, µ-CT measurements and tissue specific fluorescence imaging-assessments, with fold-change values ranging from 6.8 × to 9.0×, highlighting the effect magnitude independently of absolute MP particle counts (Supplementary: Fig. S4(B); Table S2). Tissue, exposure time, route- and modality-specific accumulation patterns were further visualized using a heatmap analysis, supporting the statistically derived temporal and cross-modal trends (Fig. 8).

Detection of MPs in fish excreta

Fluorescence microscopy analysis of fish excreta revealed the presence of fluorescently labelled PS-MPs across both waterborne exposure groups (Tanks 2 and 3) and dietary exposure groups (Tanks 4 and 5) throughout the 28-day experimental duration (Fig. 9). During the initial exposure phase (0.1%, Days 1–7), only a small number of fluorescent MP particles were detected, indicating minimal elimination at early exposure levels. As exposure concentrations increased (0.5% and 1%; Days 8–21), the number of detectable MP particles in excreta increased progressively, reflecting enhanced uptake and subsequent gastrointestinal elimination. At the highest exposure level (10%; Days 22–28), a substantial increase in fluorescent MP particles was observed in the excreta samples. Quantitive analysis of MP counts obtained from fluorescence microscopy confirmed a clear dose- and time-dependent increase in MP occurrence in excreta (Table 1). Statical analysis further supported these spatio-temporal trends, with significant differences in MP detection across the exposure durations (Kruskal-Wallis test, p < 0.05). No fluorescent MP signals were detected in excreta from control fish throughout the experiment, confirming that the observed MP particles resulted from the experimental exposure rather than the background contamination. These findings indicate that observed MPs in excreta were not due to the passive external adherence but rather represent active ingestion followed by partial gastrointestinal elimination. Similar findings have been consistent with Ding et al. (2018), who reported that PS microplastics ingested by Oreochromis niloticus accumulated primarily in the GI tract and were subsequently excreted, indicating fate pathway and partial elimination via faeces.

Discussion

Exposure route Determines internal MP burden

Our findings suggest that µ-CT, when integrated with fluorescence microscopy, provides reliable and non-destructive imaging platform for accurately assessing the spatial distribution of MPs in whole. In this context, a comparable proof-of-concept investigation in Danio rerio demonstrated that µ-CT imaging can accurately being quantified and spatially resolved polyethylene MPs within biological tissues (Molina-Millán et al., 2025). This approach conserve tissue integrity and avoids structural disruption typically associated with destructive spectroscopic techniques, thereby offering a non-invasive alternative for MP detection (Kats et al., 2025, Parobková et al., 2025, Talau et al., 2025). While, previous studies have established the potential of CT-based detection combined with artificial intelligence (AI) (Sarker et al., 2024, Strafella et al., 2024) for automated MP identification, these approached remain primarily focused on the detection efficiency rather than biological interpretation, lacking integration with the exposure dynamics and tissue-specific MP translocation. In contrast, the integration of µ-CT and fluorescence microscopy enables non-invasive, 3-D visualization of MP distribution, thereby overcoming limitation of existing convention analytical techniques. The exposure pathway emerges as a primary determinant governing MP accumulation and internal tissue distribution in Labeo rohita. The individual fish species when exposed through the water column exhibited substantially higher MP retention compared to fishes exposed through contaminated feed, indicating that waterborne exposure represents the dominant uptake route, whereas, no MP particles were detected in the control tanks. In contrast, the present study incorporated environmentally relevant exposure routes and spatio-temporal dynamics, enabling a more comprehensive understanding of MP accumulation and re-distribution in aquatic fauna. These trends align with previous studies in freshwater teleosts, wherein waterborne MPs have been shown to accumulate preferentially in the gills and gastrointestinal tract due to continuous filtration and direct contact with suspended MP particles (Ding et al., 2018, Liang et al., 2025). The persistent interaction of gill epithelia with the surrounding medium felicitates rapid particle entrapment, while ingestion through normal feeding and respiration further intensifies gastrointestinal deposition. In the present assessment, this pathway specific accumulation pattern was consistently captured by both fluorescence microscopy and µ-CT imaging, providing strong cross-technique validation of MP presence and spatial deposition within organ systems. The strong concordance between µ-CT and fluorescence microscopy in the present study aligns with the recent advances emphasizing multi-modal MP detection strategies, which further significantly improves the detection accuracy and reduce false-positive MP identification associated with single technique identification approach.

This increased retention under waterborne exposure likely reflects sustained interaction of MPs with gill epithelia, along with repeated incidental ingestion from the surrounding water, resulting in a protracted exposure compared to discrete feeding events. Gills, with their high surface area and thin epithelial barriers, may represent an effective route of entry, enabling the systemic translocation of MP particles beyond the confines of the gastrointestinal tract (Bridgeman et al., 2025, Ikuta et al., 2022). Recent analysis have further demonstrated that MPs can undergo systemic translocation from primary uptake organs to secondary uptake tissues such as muscle, liver and circulatory systems, highlighting the potential for trophic transfer as well as long-term ecological consequences in the aquatic food webs (Huang et al., 2025, Liang et al., 2025).

The presence of MPs in muscle tissue suggests translocation from primary uptake organs, a process that may be facilitated by paracellular transport across intestinal epithelia or uptake across gill surfaces with resultant systemic circulation. While the present study does not provide comprehensive mechanistic insight into the transport mechanism, the observation that MP particles are consistently detectable in musculature across imaging modalities supporting the notion of internal redistribution rather than surface contamination. Detection of MPs in excreta confirms the internal accumulation and as fish maintain a degree of elimination capacity, indicating a dynamic balance between MP uptake, retention, and egestion. Whereas, MP levels following dietary exposure were markedly lower, suggesting partial depuration during gastrointestinal transit and reduced assimilation into secondary tissues also suggests temporary retention rather than long term bioaccumulation across internal organs. These findings underscore the critical importance of accounting for exposure pathways when evaluating MP risks in aquatic organisms. The ingestion-based exposure models are likely to underestimate the total microplastic burden within organism, particularly under realistic environmental conditions where simultaneous waterborne, dietary, and tropic exposure can occur. The availability of MPs in natural freshwater ecosystems further reinforces the ecological validity of the observed accumulation patterns, indicating that the experimental outcomes are representative of field-level contamination scenarios. The detection of MPs in muscle tissue supports previous studies, that MP particles can translocate beyond the GI tract via circulatory pathways, reinforcing concerns regarding trophic transfer and human exposure through seafood consumption (Roch et al., 2020, McIlwraith et al., 2021, Conger et al., 2024).

Methodological innovation and Broader implications

This current work combines non-parametric statistical analysis with imaging/microscopy-derived outputs to provide a framework to overcome the limitation that image-based observations alone cannot establish using exposure-dependent patterns. The temporal trend analysis utilizing Spearman rank correlation (ρ) and fold-change metrics provides statistical support for the visual patterns observed in fluorescence microscopy and µ-CT reconstructions (Maška et al., 2023). The observed distributions reflect consistent, exposure-dependent patterns of MP retention rather than the qualitative artifacts, as evidenced through statistically supported concordance between the imaging modalities [Supplementary: Fig. S3; Table S2]. The larger fold-change in muscle tissue reflects delayed but gradual, systemic redistribution rather than preferential uptake, consistent with lower initial MP-load rather than greater, absolute accumulation of MPs. The present findings accentuate the limitation of conventional digestion-based, spectroscopic techniques, which, although effective for MP identification, inherently lack the ability to preserve anatomical context and resolves spatial distribution patterns within the intact organisms.

One of the key contributions is combining µ-CT and fluorescence microscopy in the framework of this work to non-destructively resolve MP distribution within intact organisms. Conventional digestion-based or spectroscopic approaches yield bulk particle counts while eliminating spatial context and obscuring exposure-route-specific retention patterns. The present dual-imaging framework preserves anatomical integrity while allowing three-dimensional localization and independent validation of detected particles. Despite its merits, µ-CT based detection remains constrained by scarce contrast between polymeric MP particles and biological tissues, emphasizing the importance of complementary validation techniques such as fluorescence microscopy (Morgana et al., 2024).

Importantly, it would not have been possible to distinguish the exposure-route-dependent accumulation patterns in the current study with solely classical and destructive methodologies. This study further extends µ-CT based MP detection to an ecologically and commercially relevant freshwater species and establishes a scalable, non-invasive imaging paradigm for microplastic ecotoxicology, supporting its application in longitudinal and reduction-driven experimental designs.

While previous studies have utilized µ-CT to visualize MPs in small model organisms or excised tissues, herein, this methodology is transferred to an economically important freshwater fish species, using environmentally relevant exposure concentrations and dual validation with fluorescence microscopy. Compared with digestion-based or AI-assisted µ-CT workflows (Caputo et al., 2024, Strafella et al., 2024), this methodology minimizes sample manipulation and preserves whole-organism spatial context, providing a scalable platform for longitudinal eco-toxicological assessment of MPs. Future integration of advanced imaging and spectroscopy techniques, including AI-assisted segmentation, hyperspectral imaging and nanoscale approached such as AFM-IR, may further enhance MP detection sensitivity and enables precise characterization of nano- and microplastics within biological systems (Xie et al., 2025).

Although absolute quantification of MPs remains challenging with conventional µ-CT due to the density overlap with soft tissues, the current study highlights that µ-CT can reliably detect and localize MPs at environmentally relevant sizes when combined with complementary fluorescence microscopy. This integrative approach gives preference to spatial resolution and anatomical context over mere numerical counts, which is crucial for understanding tissue-specific retention and translocation. The non-destructive nature of µ-CT imaging, in turn, opens up the prospect for the use of this technique in live fish under short-term anaesthesia, allowing repeated, longitudinally detected microplastic burdens at micrometre-scale resolution within the same individuals. Rather than aiming to replace spectroscopic techniques, such as FTIR or Raman, µ-CT should be considered as one more complementary imaging technique. In contrast to µ-CT, FTIR and Raman provide chemical identification polymer-specific and an accurate particle count after digestion of tissue. However, µ-CT has the distinct advantage of maintaining the whole organism and providing a non-destructive, three-dimensional visualization of the distribution of microplastics across different organs. These could become approaches that immensely reduce animal usages while allowing for time-resolved tracking of MP uptake, redistribution, and elimination. Recent research studies have also highlighted the ever-growing integration of advanced imaging and analytical techniques for MP detection, including AI-assisted computed tomography and multi-modal analytical workflows, which improve detection sensitivity and spatial mapping, translocation of MPs within the biological matrices (Caputo et al., 2024, Ren et al., 2025). Aggregately, this study advances current methodologies by transitioning from destructive, MP particle-centric detection towards a non-destructive, organism level understanding of MP fate, thereby providing a more environmentally and biologically relevant framework.

Together, µ-CT imaging and fluorescence microscopy support growing evidence that MPs act not only as ingested contaminants but also as systemically distributed stressors within fish. Such a finding reinforces concerns that MP exposure represents an emerging ecological and food-safety risk (Liang et al., 2025). This work bridges the critical knowledge gap between biological interpretation and analytical detection by integrating non-destructive imaging, realistic exposure route and spatio-temporal resolved validation, thereby establishing a next-generation framework for MP ecotoxicology.

Conclusion

This novel approach evades key limitations of traditional destructive techniques by maintaining the anatomical context and thus enabling three-dimensional visualization within intact organisms, while providing spatially resolved insight into organ-specific MP distribution. The strong concordance between µ-CT and fluorescence microscopy underlines the robustness of this approach and qualifies it for mechanistic ecotoxicological investigations of ecologically and commercially important freshwater fish. In all, this brings methodological innovation one step further while simultaneously providing critical evidence that waterborne exposure of MPs may result in higher retention in multi-organ distribution following prolonged exposure, an aspect potentially relevant for fish health, trophic transfer, aquatic food safety, and deeper understanding of MP behaviour in vertebrate systems.

The present study combines conservative, non-parametric statistical analyses with non-destructive micro-computed tomography and fluorescence microscopy to provide quantitative validation of temporal trends, exposure-route dependence, and cross-modal agreement in microplastic detection. Incorporation of trend metrics, effect-size estimates, and cross-imaging concordance strengthens assurance in the observed accumulation patterns beyond qualitative visualization alone. Collectively, this framework represents a statistically supported, imaging-based methodology for assessing microplastic fate and internal distribution in freshwater fish under ecologically relevant exposure regimes.

Environmental implication

The environmentally relevant detection of microplastics within several organ systems of a freshwater fish of commercial importance evidences the pervasive nature of microplastic contamination within freshwater ecosystems. Higher relative burdens associated with waterborne compared to dietary exposure route suggest that ambient environmental contamination presents a higher ecological risk than dietary alone. Microplastic accumulation showed progressive temporal trends; however, their continuous detection across gastrointestinal, branchial, and muscular tissues indicates sustained internal retention and redistribution. The recovery of microplastics from excreta thus further indicates dynamic processing involving both ingestion and partial elimination. This study integrates non-destructive µ-CT with fluorescence microscopy to provide a spatially resolved framework for assessing microplastic fate in aquatic organisms, while providing implications for environmental monitoring, freshwater, marine aquaculture, and trophic transfer risk assessment.

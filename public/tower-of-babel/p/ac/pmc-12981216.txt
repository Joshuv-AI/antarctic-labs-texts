Short stature with windswept deformity in a young girl
Authors: V Sai Ramya, Sanjay Kumar, Arun Guddeti, K V S Hari Kumar
Published: 2026

Image legend

An 11-year-old girl presented with short stature and lower-limb bowing for more than 3 years. She was the firstborn from a third-degree consanguineous marriage and had a history of delayed motor milestones. Her height was 128.4 cm (< third percentile), and she had arachnodactyly, a single transverse crease, pectus excavatum, kyphoscoliosis, pes planus, and genu valgum in the right and genu varum in the left leg (Fig. 1A). The metabolic panel showed calcium 9.1 mg/dL (SI: 2.27 mmol/L) (reference range, 8.5-10.5 mg/dL; 2.12-2.62 mmol/L), phosphate 4.8 mg/dL (SI: 1.55 mmol/L) (reference range, 2.5-4.9 mg/dL; 0.81-1.58 mmol/L), alkaline phosphatase 264 U/L (SI: 4.4 µkat/L) (reference range, 45-145 U/L; 0.7-2.5 µkat/L), and low 25-hydroxyvitamin D of 17.6 ng/mL (SI: 44 nmol/L) (reference range, 30-50 ng/mL; 75-125 nmol/L). The skeletal survey revealed windswept deformity (Fig. 1B), kyphoscoliosis (Fig. 1C), Swedish key appearance of the femur (Fig. 1D), and bone age of 10 years. Molecular analysis revealed a homozygous 1-bp deletion c.893T > C (p.Leu298Pro) in the calcium activated nucleotidase 1 gene (CANT1), confirming the diagnosis of Desbuquois dysplasia 1 (DBQD1). The child was given vitamin D replacement and is being monitored for pubertal development. DBQD1 and DBQD2 are autosomal recessive osteochondrodysplasias caused by pathogenic variants in the CANT1 and XYLT1 genes respectively on chromosome 17q25 [1]. DBQD is described in fewer than 150 cases worldwide with considerable phenotypic and genetic diversity. DBQD pathogenic variants result in fetal loss, and morbidity is due to skeletal and cardiovascular anomalies [2].

A and B, Windswept deformity with C, kyphoscoliosis, and D, the classic Swedish key deformity of proximal femur.

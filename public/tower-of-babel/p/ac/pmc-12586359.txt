Association Between Renal Function and Left Atrial Low‐Voltage Area Burden in Paroxysmal Atrial Fibrillation
Authors: Shaochong Deng, Xiang Tan, Yuxuan Wang, Haojun Wang, Shan Li, Xulong Zhao, Xinqi Yang, Hongwu Chen, Jing Wang, Minglong Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundCircumferential pulmonary vein isolation (CPVI) plus left atrial low‐voltage areas (LVA) modification is a useful method to treat paroxysmal atrial fibrillation (PAF); however, it is hard to predict LVA burden before the procedure. The existence of chronic kidney disease (CKD) increases the prevalence of left atrial LVA. In this study, we explored the relationship between renal function and the LVA burden in elderly patients with PAF.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed the 435 elderly patients with PAF who underwent ablation. Based on the LVA burden measured during the procedure, the patients were divided into four groups: Group 1 (LVA burden ≤ 5%, n = 356), Group 2 (LVA burden 5%–10%, n = 39), Group 3 (LVA burden 10%–15%, n = 16), and Group 4 (LVA burden > 15%, n = 24).ResultsIncreasing LVA burden was associated with a progressive decline in eGFR. A significant difference was observed between Group 1 and Group 4 (86.6 ± 12.9 vs. 76.4 ± 17.7, p = 0.003). eGFR was identified to be a predictor for LVA burden > 10%, with the optimal cut‐off value for GFR being 75.7 mL/min/1.73 m2, having a sensitivity of 54.2% and specificity of 80.4%.ConclusionsA lower eGFR is associated with higher LVA burden in elderly patients with PAF.Keywords: atrial fibrillation, catheter ablation, left atrial low‐voltage areas, renal function

Introduction

Circumferential pulmonary vein isolation (CPVI) alone is a key strategy in treating paroxysmal atrial fibrillation (PAF) (Ma et al. 2024). Moreover, atrial fibrosis plays an important role in the genesis and maintenance of AF, and it can be reflected by low‐voltage area (LVA) which has also become an important predictor of recurrence after CPVI in patients with AF (Masuda et al. 2018). Recently, the STABLE‐SR‐III trial shows that CPVI plus LVA modification can significantly reduce the recurrence rate of PAF in elderly patients (Chen et al. 2023).

Previous studies have indicated that the presence of chronic kidney disease (CKD) increases the risk of atrial tachyarrhythmia (ATA) recurrence post‐ablation (Laukkanen et al. 2016). In patients with PAF, reduced glomerular filtration rate (GFR) is associated with left atrial substrate abnormalities and a higher recurrence rate after catheter ablation (Chao et al. 2011; Matsuda et al. 2019). Therefore, this study aims to explore the relationship between the left atrial low‐voltage areas proportion and renal function.

Methods

Study Population

A total of 435 consecutive patients with paroxysmal AF who received initial ablation were enrolled. Before ablation, the patient's serum creatinine is measured, and based on this, the estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate (eGFR) is calculated using the CKD‐EPI 2021 equation (Inker et al. 2021). The exclusion criteria include: patients who have previously undergone radiofrequency ablation; contraindications to systemic anticoagulation therapy; left atrial appendage thrombus; severe structural heart disease (severe mitral regurgitation, dilated cardiomyopathy, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, other severe valvular heart disease); and serum creatinine > 3.5 mg/dL or creatinine clearance < 30 mL/min. The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the First Affiliated Hospital of Nanjing Medical University. Informed consent was obtained from all patients.

Before the ablation procedure, serum creatinine was routinely examined in patients. Estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) was calculated using the CKD‐EPI 2021 equation (Inker et al. 2021) based on serum creatinine, which is expressed as:

The formula variables were defined as follows: Serum creatinine (Scr, measured in mg/dL) with gender‐specific coefficients κ (0.7 for females vs. 0.9 for males) and α (−0.329 for females vs. −0.411 for males). The min/max operators respectively denoted the minimum and maximum values between Scr/κ and 1.

Substrate Map and Ablation Procedure

Antiarrhythmic drugs were discontinued at least 5 times the half‐life before the operation, and amiodarone was discontinued for 2 months. The electrophysiological examination was performed under mild sedation after intravenous injection of midazolam and fentanyl, and the mapping and ablation process was guided by a 3‐D mapping system (Biosense Webster). A new ablation lesion quality evaluation algorithm, the ablation index (AI), was introduced based on the parameters measured by the contact force (CF) sensing catheter. All patients underwent CPVI based on the ablation index as the initial step (power 30–40 W, contact pressure 5–30 g). The targeted ablation index values for the anterior wall, atrioventricular septum, and inferior wall were 500, 400–450, and 350–400, respectively. If AF was still present after CPVI, sinus rhythm was restored by electrical cardioversion. After CPVI, a detailed 3‐D voltage map of the LA was created during sinus rhythm. LVA was defined as areas with amplitude less than 0.5 mV in more than 3 adjacent low‐voltage points with a space difference of 0.5 cm2 (Rolf et al. 2014). Additional LVA modification had been performed in selected cases where low‐voltage areas were identified, as documented in the original prospective cohort during our retrospective analysis.

The Categorization of Groups Based on LVA Burden

The LVA burden is defined as the proportion of LVA on the entire left atrial surface (Chen et al. 2023). Based on the LVA burden measured during the procedure, the patients were divided into four groups: Group 1 (LVA burden ≤ 5%, n = 356), Group 2 (LVA burden 5%–10%, n = 39), Group 3 (LVA burden 10%–15%, n = 16), and Group 4 (LVA burden > 15%, n = 24).

Follow‐Up

All patients receive antiarrhythmic drug treatment in the first 3 months if there are no contraindications. Anticoagulant treatment after the operation is maintained for at least 3 months. Surface electrocardiogram and 24‐h Holter monitoring are performed in the 3rd and 6th months after the operation, and 7‐day Holter monitoring is performed in the 12th month in some patients. After that, outpatient follow‐up and 24‐h Holter monitoring are carried out every 6 months. If there are arrhythmic symptoms, a surface electrocardiogram is performed at any time.

Statistical Analysis

Continuous data conforming to normal distribution are expressed as mean ± standard deviation or mean (first quartile, third quartile), while categorical data are presented as absolute values and percentages. The significance of continuous variables was assessed using independent‐samples t‐test and Kruskal‐Wallis test, and chi‐square test for categorical variables. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed for eGFR and LVA burden > 10%. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were used to identify clinical factors associated with eGFR < 75 mL/min/1.73 m2, with variables showing p ≤ 0.05 in univariate analysis included in the multivariate model. Kaplan–Meier analysis with a piecewise log‐rank test (segmented at 12 months post‐ablation) was used to evaluate the association between LVA modification and freedom from atrial tachyarrhythmia recurrence. Univariate and multivariate COX regression were conducted to investigate factors influencing ATA recurrence after atrial fibrillation ablation. Variables with univariate p ≤ 0.10 and prespecified covariates of interest (Ablation Strategy) were entered into the multivariate model. All analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics 27.0.1.

Results

Population Characteristics

A total of 435 elderly patients with PAF were enrolled in the study and stratified into four groups based on LVA burden: Group 1 (LVA burden ≤ 5%, n = 356), Group 2 (LVA burden 5%–10%, n = 39), Group 3 (LVA burden 10%–15%, n = 16), and Group 4 (LVA burden > 15%, n = 24). There were statistically significant differences in age, gender, left atrial diameter, BMI, and CHA2DS2‐VASc score among the four groups (Table 1). As the LVA burden increased, there was a trend toward increasing age, a higher proportion of female patients, larger left atrial diameters, and a higher CHA2DS2‐VASc score.

The Association Between eGFR and LVA Burden

There was a difference in the eGFR among the four groups (p = 0.03), with increasing LVA burden corresponding to decreasing eGFR. After Bonferroni correction, there was still a significant difference between group 1 and group 4 (86.6 ± 12.9 vs. 76.4 ± 17.7, p = 0.003) (Figure 1). The eGFR was significantly lower in patients with an LVA burden > 10% compared to those with an LVA burden < 10% (80.2 ± 16.7 vs. 86.8 ± 12.6, p = 0.015). By plotting the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve with eGFR and LVA burden > 10%, we found that eGFR was a weak predictor of LVA prevalence (area under the curve [AUC] = 0.611; Figure 2). The optimal cut‐off value of GFR was 75.7 mL/min/1.73 m2, with a sensitivity of 54.2% and a specificity of 80.4%.

We also performed univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses on whether the glomerular filtration rate (GFR) was less than 75 mL/min/1.73 m2 according to the results output by the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve. In the univariate analysis, age, LVA burden, left atrial diameter, hypertension, history of β‐blocker application, and CHA2DS2‐VASc score had an impact on the results. Multivariate analysis confirmed age and β‐blocker use as independent predictors (Table 2).

Follow‐Up

After a median follow‐up of 17.4 months, we observed that during the first 12‐month follow‐up period, the recurrence rate of atrial tachyarrhythmia was higher in the group receiving CPVI plus LVA modification compared to the group with CPVI alone. But after 12 months, the situation was reversed, with the recurrence rate of atrial tachyarrhythmia in the CPVI plus LVA modification group becoming significantly lower than that in the CPVI alone group (Figure 3).

Univariate and multivariate COX regression demonstrated that patients with AF duration > 12 months or prior stroke/TIA history were more prone to ATA recurrence post‐ablation. Though multivariable analysis demonstrated a 51% elevated recurrence risk in patients with unmodified LVA substrates compared with their modified counterparts (HR = 1.51, 95% CI 0.90–2.54), this difference failed to reach statistical significance (Table 3).

Discussion

The main findings of this study were as follows: (1) The decrease in eGFR is associated with an increase in LVA burden, (2) there are significant differences in the estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) between those with LVA < 5% and those with LVA > 15%. and (3) An eGFR < 75.7 is a predictor for a moderate LVA burden (LVA/LA > 10%).

Associations Between LVA and GFR

Both AF and CKD are highly prevalent in elderly populations, sharing a bidirectional relationship (Ding et al. 2021). Patients with LVA have a lower eGFR compared to those without LVA (Matsuda et al. 2019). As the degree of CKD worsens, the risk of AF occurrence increases (Laukkanen et al. 2016). Patients with CKD are at a higher risk of developing AF (Baber et al. 2011; Deo et al. 2010). Even in individuals with mild CKD (eGFR 60–89 mL/min/1.73 m2), a higher occurrence of AF was also observed (Alonso et al. 2011). In this study, we used the LVA burden to represent the severity of left atrial substrate remodeling. It was observed that there is a downward trend in renal function with the increase of LVA burden. Specifically, patients with LVA burden ≥ 15% exhibit marked renal impairment, indicating a close relationship between atrial fibrosis extent and CKD progression.

CKD Severity and LVA Burden

Atrial fibrosis, as indicated by LVA presence, plays a crucial role in the occurrence and development of AF (Lin et al. 2014). At the cellular level, fibrosis alters the extracellular microenvironment, interferes with the functional expression of ion channels, affects intracellular calcium ion‐related trigger activities, increases the susceptibility to arrhythmia, and provides a pathological basis for AF (Nattel et al. 2008). The DECAAF study identified atrial tissue fibrosis by delayed enhancement magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). The results showed that for every 1% increase in atrial fibrosis, the recurrence risk of ATA after catheter ablation increased by 6% (Marrouche et al. 2014). The STABLE‐SR‐III trial further validates this by showing superior outcomes when combining CPVI plus LVA modification (15% recurrence) versus CPVI alone (24%) (Chen et al. 2023). The above research results indicate that the LVA is an important influencing factor for the ATA recurrence after CPVI. For patients with AF, additional LVA modification can effectively reduce the recurrence of ATA. Our findings extend these observations by establishing eGFR < 75.7 mL/min/1.73m2 as a predictor for moderate LVA burden (LVA/LA > 10%), suggesting that more severe CKD often indicates a higher LVA burden. eGFR can serve as a predictor of LVA burden, enabling an estimation of the severity of the atrial substrate before catheter ablation and assisting clinicians in predicting whether additional LVA modification is necessary.

LVA Modification in CKD

Our study found that the CPVI plus LVA modification showed a trend toward numerically higher recurrence rates within 12 months (p = 0.097), but exhibited lower recurrence rates beyond the initial 12‐month period during extended follow‐up (p < 0.001). This time‐dependent efficacy pattern may explain the non‐significant overall hazard ratio in the multivariable Cox model. In elderly patients who typically exhibit higher degrees of atrial fibrosis, the benefit of LVA modification on the fibrotic substrate may take even longer to manifest. This delayed benefit pattern of LVA modification has been previously documented in the STABLE‐SR‐III trial (Chen et al. 2023). Emerging evidence suggests that pre‐existing LVA may contribute to higher AF recurrence rates at 1‐year post‐ablation (Kanda et al. 2022).

Notably, our study revealed no significant impact of eGFR or LVA burden on post‐ablation ATA recurrence. Although earlier studies had established CKD as an independent predictor of AF recurrence following catheter ablation (Yanagisawa et al. 2017; Naruse et al. 2011), these findings were primarily derived from patient cohorts undergoing conventional CPVI procedures without LVA modification. A prior study by Chen SA et al. demonstrated that reduced GFR was associated with abnormal left atrial substrate characteristics and higher ablation recurrence rates in PAF patients (Chao et al. 2011). However, their cohort did not undergo substrate modification, nor did they examine the relationship between LVA burden and eGFR. Recent investigations by Li Na et al. revealed that in murine models, the CKD‐induced inflammatory milieu enhances atrial NLRP3 inflammasome activity, promotes atrial electrical remodeling and fibrosis, and thereby increases susceptibility to atrial fibrillation (Song et al. 2023). Our findings further establish a correlation between CKD severity and LVA burden, suggesting that CKD‐mediated inflammatory pathways may drive atrial fibrosis and consequently elevate post‐ablation ATA recurrence rates, which provides critical pathophysiological rationale for the clinical utility of LVA modification in CKD patients.

Limitations

There are some limitations in this study. First, the eGFR was calculated based on the serum creatinine level measured before the ablation procedure. Second, the patients enrolled in this study were individuals with PAF. In most of these individuals, the degree of atrial fibrosis was not severe. Third, while low eGFR is theorized to promote myocardial fibrosis through inflammatory pathways, the majority of patients in our study exhibited preserved eGFR. Consequently, we stratified patients by LVA burden to analyze eGFR differences, which should not be equated with using eGFR to predict LVA burden. To clarify the causal role of renal dysfunction in atrial fibrosis progression, further investigation is warranted to evaluate patients with impaired renal function (eGFR < 60 mL/min/1.73m2).

Conclusion

Reduced GFR correlates with increased LVA burden in elderly patients with PAF. LVA modification may reduce ATA recurrence rates post‐ablation in CKD populations.

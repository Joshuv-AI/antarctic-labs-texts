Simulation of tillage forces and soil movement during soil-disc plow interaction using FEM
Authors: Ayadi Ibrahmi, Hatem Bentaher
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Tillage forces and the disturbance of the soil are important performance indicators and soil dynamic properties for disc plow. In this work, a 3D model of FEM was developed to predict the effects of speed, the depth, disc angle (DA), and tilt angle (TA), on lateral, vertical, and draught forces and on the disturbance of the soil. This study showed that working at higher depth and speed of the disc tool increased the consumption of energy. Working with greater tilt and smaller disc angles decreased the draught and lateral forces, which reduced the consumption of energy and favored the stability of the tractor. However, it increases the vertical force and favors the penetration of this disc in soil. Disc and tilt angles had a greater impact on the consumed energy than depth and tillage speed with 87%, 56%, 40%, and 15%, respectively. Furthermore, this study showed that the increase of the tilt angle reduced the disturbance of the soil. However, more compression and disturbance of the soil were observed when the disc angle was increased. The minimum consumed energy with better burial of the residues and mixing of seeds in the soil can occur at a disc and tilt angle fixed at DA = 30° and TA = 15°.

Introduction

Tillage with disc plow, a process of applying energy to plow hard soils. It cuts, pulverizes, elevates, and inverts the cut soil in primary tillage practices1. The tractor used in the tillage with this tool is the major capital investment for a farmer. The power requirement of the tillage tool was related mainly to the draught force.

Therefore, the evaluation of this force is beneficial in the decision of machinery management2. The draught force depends on the type and the design of the tillage tool, working conditions (speed and depth), and type of soil. The tillage practice is moving to test the dynamic interaction of disc tools with soil for high speed, although other soil tillage tools have been tested for working speeds up to 20 km h− 13. However, there is an absence of information currently and no known government laboratory facilities at universities to test the tillage implements in general and disc plow in particular form at high speed. In addition, it is very expensive to test the interaction of disc plow with soil in a wide range of field and operating conditions and study its effects on the consumption of energy.

Current modeling of the tillage process has become more accessible and can be adopted to avoid such problems. Therefore, researchers have been trying to develop methods for predicting or calculating the draught force and power of tillage implements. Analytical and numerical methods have been used to model soil-disc tool interactions to enable improvements in the design of soil-engaging tools4–7. One of the analytical methods used is the universal earthmoving equation (UEE). This equation was adopted for this modeling and modified in different formulations based on the theory of passive soil failure by several studies1,8,9.

Ahmadi4 was developed an analytical model to calculate the draught force and power of a disc plow. In this model, the effects of soil-metal friction processes, soil cutting, and soil displacement on the specific draught force of a disc plow were considered. The author compared this model with an experimental test with tillage speed of 7 km/h and showed its performance with reasonable accuracy. The experiment results were about 5% lower than the values obtained by the developed model. Upadhyay and Raheman10 incorporated front gang angle, cone index and tillage speed in a specific draught estimation model for disc plow. In this study, the lowest draught force was obtained with a front gang angle of 35°.

Numerical methods were also used to simulate the soil-disc tool interaction. The main advantage of this method is its ability to obtain fast predictions of the tillage forces without the need for multiple field tests11,12. One of these numerical methods, the discrete element method (DEM), has been applied7. DEM is a numerical calculation method for analyzing complex dynamic discontinuous mechanical discrete systems. It can effectively be used to simulate the macroscopic and microscopic movement of granular materials such as soil particular. Using this method, several studies were developed to simulate dynamic interaction of different geometry tools, such as soil-blade13; soil-opener interaction14, and more specifically draught force prediction15,16. The main challenge for DEM is the identification of model parameters (micro-properties of model particles). Sadek et al.7 was developed a discrete element model (DEM) to predict draught force for high-speed tillage of disc plow. The calibrated model was used to study the effects of different conditions on the work of disc plow including tillage speed and depth, disc diameter of disc, and gang and tilt angles on draught force. From these simulations, it was shown that draught force increased with an increase in tillage speed, depth, and gang angle. However, increasing the tilt angle reduced the draught force, and it remained relatively constant with the change of disc diameter. Other research based on the finite element method (FEM), has been adopted to model the dynamic soil-tool interaction and this field of research has been well-documented17–19. Moreover, FEM has high productivity and accuracy in calculating the deformation and stress mechanics of continuum materials. This method was shown to be able to simulate different tool shapes (blade, moldboard plow, chisel), and the effect of travel operations (speed and depth), cutting angles of the tool on the consumption of energy17,20,21. A few 3D finite element method models to predict the dynamic soil-disc plow interaction are available22–24. Upadhyay et al.22 was created a 3D model of an acting disc in SolidWorks 2013 and then the stress analysis was carried out using Finite Element Analysis (FEA). This model was used to verify the optimum dimensions of different parts of the design tillage machinery system based on the deformation and maximum stress in each component. The author showed that FEA enables simulating the different conditions of work of a complex agricultural machinery to avoid failure in the later phase of field evaluation. Abu-Hamdeh and Reeder24 developed a nonlinear 3D finite element analysis to predict the tillage forces of the disc plow. The authors studied the effects of tillage speed and tilt angle on the lateral, vertical, and draught forces. These analyses showed that the draught and vertical forces increased when the tilt angle increased, and it decreased the lateral force. Increasing tillage speed increased the draught, lateral forces, and decreased the vertical force. All of these studies are limited only to investigate the effect of plowing depth, speed, tilt angle on tillage forces, and not cover the influence of the cutting angles (disc and tilt angles) of disc plow on both the tillage forces and the disturbance of the soil with FEM, which is necessary to evaluate the performance of the tillage tools in different operating condition20,21. Furthermore, compared to the other modeling of soil-tool interaction with computational fluid dynamics (CFD) and the discrete element model (DEM), the researcher used the simulation with FEM to assess clearly the deformation of the soil and the quality of soil loosening in front of the tillage tool at different time steps20,25,26. The FEM can also predict the tillage force and the cutting process of the tillage tool, which can ensure the minimum of the consumed energy and better quality of the tillage. The aims of this study were to use a three-dimensional finite element method (3D-FEM) model for: (1) predicting the tillage forces (lateral (Fl), vertical (Fv), and draught (Fd) forces) on a disc plow as affected by plowing depth and speed; (2) predicting the tillage forces (Fv, Fl, and Fd) on a disc plow as affected by cutting angles: disc and tilt angles (DA and TA, respectively), and (3) investigate the influence of these cutting angles on the soil disturbance.

Materials and methods

The 3D-FEM model of soil-disc plow interaction was developed in SIMULIA ABAQUS 6.14-5 Dassault Systemes SE. The developed 3D model has the ability to predict the effect of the depth, plowing speed, disc and tilt angles (DA and TA) of the disc plow on the three components of the tillage forces (Fd, Fv, and Fl) and its specific tillage forces (force per surface) (Fd/S, Fv/S, and Fl/S). The influence of tilt and disc angles on the disturbance of the soil was investigated in the proposed model. The details of these analyses are included in the following sections.

Disc-plow design

The disc plow geometry and dimensions were designed according to a standard commercial disc plow (F 3 L, Huard, TMR, Tunisia). The disc plow used in the 3D finite element model analysis was a concave disc with 560 mm of diameter, 6.5 mm of thickness, and a 700 mm radius of the concave disc. The geometry of this disc and the cross-section are illustrated in Fig. 1.

Finite element model of disc plow-soil interaction

The 3D-FEM model of disc plow-soil interaction consists of two parts: (i) deformable soil modeled with a box. The dimensions of this box were 2 × 1.5 × 1 m (i.e. length x width x height) (ii) rigid disc plow (Fig. 2). Several studies developed in the modeling of tool-soil interaction found that modelling the soil as an elastic-plastic material with the Drucker-Prager yield criterion20,21,27.

The 3D Computer-Aided Design (CAD) model of the disc plow assembled with the soil box was imported from SolidWorks to Abaqus: the disc plow was defined as a rigid body with a reference point assigned at its tip. The three components of the tillage forces (draught, vertical and lateral forces) calculated in the travel work (axis Z, Y and X, respectively), were defined at this tip. The disc plow was fixed at depth (d) (Fig. 2). The appropriate element of mesh type and its dimensions must be selected to achieve reliable results30. The soil and disc plow body were meshed in ABAQUS/Explicit analysis with C3D8 (i.e. 8-node linear brick continuum element) and R3D4 (i.e. 4-node 3D bilinear rigid quadrilateral element), respectively. The total number of soil elements and nodes (89416 and 93193, respectively) was evaluated in this model (Fig. 3). Previous studies of Bentaher et al. 30 showed that the mesh density affected the FEM solution. The authors found that the three components of the tillage forces increased sharply with large elements of the soil. In this simulation, an appropriate larger element less than 10 mm gives an independence of the draught force with the density of the mesh (not shown). Therefore, the element size of 10 mm was adopted for the following simulations. The dynamic explicit solution was performed in each simulation and a penalty function method was defined in the contact between the rigid surfaces (master surface) and soil nodes (slave nodes). The friction coefficient µ = 0.58 obtained with the shear box test was adopted for the interaction steel-sandy loam soil. The boundary conditions in this simulation were defined: The bottom surface and lateral walls of the soil box were a fixed in three directions of translation (X-Y-Z). However, the other soil surfaces (top, the end and front faces) were left free of any constraints (Fig. 3). The disc plow was constrained to move only in the travel direction (Z direction). To study the effect of a moving speed of the disc plow on the tillage forces, different constant speeds (v = 7.2, 10, 12, 14, and 18 km/h) were defined at the tip of the disc plow.

Effects of speed, depth, disc and Tilt angles on tillage forces

The effect of different variables on draught, vertical and lateral forces (Fd, Fv, Fl, respectively) was studied using the 3D finite element model. These parameters include tillage depth (d), plowing speed (v), and disc and tilt angles (DA, TA). For this simulation, only one parameter was varied, and other parameters remained constant. Constant parameters are chosen based on the adopted industrial application for the high-speed tillage equipment.

In this study, different depth (d = 100, 150, 200, 250, 300 mm), speed varies from (v = 7.2, 10, 12, 14, 18 Km/h), six different disc angles (DA = 10°, 20°, 30°, 40°, 50° and 60°) and six different tilt angles (TA = 10°, 15°, 20°, 25°, 30° and 35°) were used for tillage forces predictions. Results and discussion

Prediction of the tillage forces

The variation of three component forces (lateral (Fl), vertical (Fv), and draught (Fd) forces) calculated by the finite element method during the displacement of the disc plow was shown in Fig. 5. The draught (Fd) and vertical (Fv) forces increase with displacement until reaching a plateau stage. The modulus of lateral force (Fl) conserved a similar tendency. The average values of these forces (Fd, Fv, and Fl) at the plateau stage were 9.6, 3 and 7 kN, respectively. The calculated values for different parameters: tilt angle (TA), disc angle (DA), speed (v) and depth (d) were adopted for further analysis in the following section.

Fig. 5Typical variation of tillage forces calculated by finite element method for a disc plow.

Typical variation of tillage forces calculated by finite element method for a disc plow.

Effect of tillage depth on forces components

The predicted lateral, vertical, and draught forces (Fl, Fv, Fd, respectively) showed an increasing trend when tillage depth was increased. Figure 6a shows the profile plots of these forces (Fl, Fv, Fd), at the five tillage depths (100, 150, 200, 250 and 300 mm). The effect of the depth on vertical force was small compared to the other forces (Fl, Fd). The working with higher depth produces an increase of 248%, 126%, 236% for Fl, Fv, and Fd, respectively, when increasing the depth from d = 150 to d = 300 mm. This greater impact of the tillage depth on tillage forces can explained by the higher increase in attack surface (S) of the disc plow shown in Table 3. A similar trend of the vertical and draught forces variation was obtained in the experimental studies of Torotwa et al. 31 for three disc plow designs (plain, notched and biomemitic). This experimental study was developed with smaller three-tillage depths d = 40, 70, and 100 mm. Furthermore, all the predicted vertical forces were positive (Fig. 6a), which indicated that they were acting on the disc plow in the downward direction that favored the disc penetration into the soil. This result was confirmed with the experimental studies developed by Zeng et al. 32 for different designs of the disc plow tested in a soil bin at two different tillage depths: shallow (63.5 mm) and deep (127 mm). The authors indicated that lower lateral forces are usually desired in terms of the frame stability of the implement. However, the increase of this force as the depth increased needs a great deal of attention to be paid to the frame strength when designing the disc for deep tillage application.

Fig. 6Draught, lateral, and vertical forces (a) and its specific forces (b) at different tillage depths.

Draught, lateral, and vertical forces (a) and its specific forces (b) at different tillage depths.

To characterize the influence of the working conditions on the specific forces, the attack surface (S) was calculated for each depth (Table 3). A similar trend of the variation, as the tillage forces, for the corresponding specific forces was observed (Fig. 6b). Specific draught and lateral forces increased approximately by 40% and 26% as tillage depth increased from 100 to 300 mm (Table 4). However, approximately a constant trend of the specific vertical force. These results indicate that the tillage depth of the disc plow strongly influenced the draught and lateral force, which can increase the consumed energy by the tractor and it is difficult to conserve a linear direction of travel.

Effect of tillage speed on forces components

The effect of the speed on the variation of tillage forces was illustrated in Fig. 7a for six levels of speed (v = 7.2, 10, 12, 14, 16 and 18 km/h) at a constant depth of 150 mm. The draught and lateral forces present a linear trend with the speed. The linear relationship between the draught force and speed has been reported by an experimental study of Sadek et al.7. The author developed this study using the single disc plow tested in the soil bin with three travel speeds: 4, 6, and 8 km h− 1 and the disc was fixed at a 20° tilt angle and 17° of the disc angle.

Fig. 7Draught, lateral, and vertical forces (a) and its specific forces (b) at different tillage speed.

Draught, lateral, and vertical forces (a) and its specific forces (b) at different tillage speed.

However, the vertical force was not affected when the speed increased. The constant value of this force conserves the same deep tillage of the disc plow when increasing the speed of the tractor. These results show that the consumption of energy, which related mainly to the draught force, was strongly affected when increasing the travel speed of the tractor. This energy increased approximately by 15% as tillage speed increased from 7.2 to 18 km/h. Furthermore, the increase of lateral force in the disc tool with speed can generate more than 14% perturbation of target direction of the tractor, which can relate mainly to the increase of the friction of the soil on the parabolic surface of the disc.

Considering the same contact area of the disc plow for all tillage speeds with the same tested deep of tillage (150 mm), a similar trend of the variation for all corresponding specific forces (draught, vertical and lateral forces) was observed in Fig. 7b.

Tillage force components for different cutting angles (tilt and disc angles)

The effect of the disc angle (DA) and tilt angle (TA) on draught and vertical force is shown in Fig. 8. The draught and lateral forces decreased when the tilt angle (TA) increased. However, these forces decreased with the increase of disc angles (DA) (Fig. 8). At smaller tilt angles and higher disc angles (DA), the value of draught and lateral forces was greater. Minimum draught and lateral forces of 5.28 kN and 2.99 kN, respectively, were observed at a 35° tilt angle. However, the minimum of these forces was 6.03 kN, and 3.1 kN, respectively, were observed at a 10° disc angle. This result indicates that working with greater tilt angles and smaller disc angles favors the displacement of the soil in front of the disc surface with minimum stress compression of soil. This phenomenon was observed in the following part, when investigating the effect of the tilt and disc angles on the cutting process of the soil (Figs. 12 and 13). This reduction of soil compression in front of the disc plow will reduce the consumed energy at high-speed tillage operation. Furthermore, as shown in Table 3, the increase of these cutting angles alters an increase of attack surface (S) of disc plow. For each augmentation of 10°, this variation was more than 15,000 mm2 and 2000 mm2 for the disc and the tilt angle, respectively. This greater impact of the disc angle compared to the tilt angle on attack surface (S) of the disc plow can increase the amount of the soil particle in front of its surface and prove more soil resistance to its displacement in the tillage direction. Furthermore, as shown in Fig. 8, the consumed energy related mainly to the draught force was clearly influenced by the variation of the disc angle compared to the tilt angle. These findings were confirmed by the study of Sadek et al.7 using 3D discrete element modelling. Furthermore, the authors showed that draught force remained relatively constant with the change of disc diameter.

Fig. 8Draught, lateral, and vertical forces at different tilt (a) and disc (b) angles.

Draught, lateral, and vertical forces at different tilt (a) and disc (b) angles.

The vertical force increased when the tilt angle (TA) increased, and it decreased with the increase of disc angle (DA). The maximum values (2, 29 kN and 2 kN ) obtained at 35° and 10° of these cutting angles, respectively (Fig. 8). This force is still positive for all cutting angles (tilt and disc angles), which favored the penetration of the disc plow into the soil during the tillage process. This penetration increased when working with higher values of tilt angles and smaller disc angles. Godwin et al.33 found a similar result when studying the soil failure and force prediction for different discs engaging in soil.

Considering the volume of soil moved in front of the disc surface, a decrease for the corresponding specific lateral and draught forces when increasing the tilt and disc angles was observed (Fig. 9). The specific vertical force decreased with the disc angle and increased until reaching a constant value (95 kN/mm2) for the higher value of the tilt angle. Similar experiment results were reported by the experiment test of Malaslia and Celik34. In this study, a single disc was tested with five disc angles (0, 5, 10, 15 and 20°) and five tilt angles (0, 2.5, 5, 7.5 and 10°). The authors found that an increase in disc angle decreased specific lateral, draught, and vertical forces. An increase in the tilt angle caused the specific lateral, draught, and vertical forces to increase. The effect of tilt angles on predicted specific lateral and draught forces was different from those found in experimental tests. This difference can be explained by these experimental tests that are limited only to the effect of low values of tilt angles (less than TA = 10°) and do not cover the effect of other higher values of this angle.

Fig. 9Draught, lateral, and vertical specific forces at different tilt (a) and disc (b) angles.

Draught, lateral, and vertical specific forces at different tilt (a) and disc (b) angles.

Table 4 showed the impact of the increasing of these angles on the specific forces. For the tilt angle, this decrease was about 56% and 54% for specific draught and lateral forces, respectively obtained when increasing this angle from 10° to 35°. However, this angle increased the specific vertical force and this impact was about 73%. For the disc angle, this impact was greater with 87%, 1037%, and 70% for specific draught, vertical, and lateral forces, respectively. From these simulations, the variation of the cutting angles strongly affects the specific vertical force more than the other specific forces, which need adding adequate counterbalance weights to the tractor front axle to equalize the contact pressure of all wheels of the tractor with the soil. As shown in Table 4, the impact of the disc angle variation on specific draught forces was greater than the tilt angle, the depth, and tillage speed. This result showed that the consumed power is more affected by the disc angle than the other parameters with 87%, 56%, 40%, and 15%, respectively. Furthermore, the variation of the disc angle strongly affects the specific lateral force more than the other parameters (tilt angle, depth, and tillage speed) with 70%, 54%, 26%, and 14%, respectively, which favors the instability of the tractor in the direction of the tillage and needs more attention for the farmer to conserve a rectilinear movement in the plowing path.

Soil failure patterns

The action of a concave disc is such that the soil is cut, lifted, pulverized, partially inverted, and displaced to one side. Figure 10 shows the soil failure pattern and its displacement during the advancement of the concave disc. The displacement of the disc generates the compression of soil in front of its concave surface and the initial failure of the soil was mainly observed at the contact point of the disc at a time of 0.125 s. This failure was occurring along the primary failure plan then propagated at a different parallel plan when the disc engaged in soil at a time of 0.25s. The soil was also cut and reversed to one side at time more than 0.25s.

Influence of the Tilt and disc angles on the soil disturbance

The disturbance of the soil depends on the orientation of the concave disc. To investigate the effect of the disc tool operation at different tilt and disc angles on this phenomenon, Figs. 12 and 13 show the cutting process of the soil obtained at the same time of 0.375s.

As the tilt angle increases, at fixed disc angle DA = 20°, the line of failure is still perpendicular to the surface of the disc plow and the colored green zone, defined with Von Mises stress more than бMises=0.1731 MPa, was reduced (Fig. 12). This result proves that the maximum of compression stress decreased in front of the curved surface when the tilt angle increased, which confirms the decrease of the draught and lateral forces observed in Fig. 8a. This decrease of tilt angle reduces the consumed energy.

Furthermore, at a smaller angle (TA = 10° and TA = 15°), the disturbance of soil is done next to the cut area. However, for greater angles TA = 35° the amount of cut soil slides on its surface and reverses behind the disc. This process explains that the reversion of soil was more observed for smaller tilt angles, which favored the burial of residues and seeds in the soil.

For the disc angle, at fixed disc angle DA = 20°, when increased this angle the line of failure changed its position from perpendicular line to the top surface of soil to parallel line, which indicates that the trend of soil movement is done mainly in front of the disc tool (Fig. 13). The colored green zone, defined with Von Mises stress more than бMises=0.1731 MPa, was observed near the tip of the disc tool for smaller disc angles DA = 10° and developed until reaching the top surface for higher disc angles. This indicates that the maximum compression stress increased in front of the curved surface of the disc plow when this angle was increased. This result explains the increase of the draught force with this angle (Fig. 8b).

Furthermore, at smaller disc angles (DA = 10°) the soil slides and falls behind the disc. However, for greater angles from DA = 30° to DA = 60°, the amount of disturbed soil is compressed and reversed in front of the disc surface. This indicated that the burial of residues and seeds in the soil was favored for higher disc angle. This result was reported in the experiment studies of Hanna et al. 37. The soil inversion action of conventional disc ploughing with large disc concavities and disc angle facilitates residue incorporation.

From these FEM analyses, based on the effect of the tilt and disc angles on both the draught force (Fig. 8) and the cutting process (Figs. 12 and 13), it is helpful for the farmers to fix tilt and disc angles at TA = 15° and DA = 30°. These optimized angles ensure both a minimum consumption of energy and disturbance of soil with cutting of residues and its burial with the seeds in the soil are favored. These results are confirmed by the experimental test of Hann and Giessibl38 developed with a driven disc in two directions. The authors showed that minimum force and power requirements with better soil mixing and pulverization characteristics occurred at a disc angle fixed between 35° and 50° with a low tilt angle.

Conclusion

In this research, a 3D-FEM was adopted for interaction soil-disc tillage to predict the draught, vertical, and lateral forces for different speeds, depths, disc angles, and tilt angles. Furthermore, the effect of these angles on the soil disturbance was investigated. The following results were drawn.

(1) Working with smaller disc angles (DA) and greater tilt angles (DA = 10° and TA = 35°) will reduce the draught and lateral forces, which decrease the consumed energy and favor the stability of tractors in the plowing path at moderate working speed and depth. However, the vertical force increased which favors the penetration of the disc plow in the soil during the tillage process. Therefore, it is required to add adequate counterbalance weights to the tractor front axle to equalize the contact pressure of all wheels with the soil.(2) More influence of the increase of the disc angle on the specific tillage forces (draught, lateral, and vertical) with 87%, 70%, and 1037%, respectively, than the tilt angle, the depth, and tillage speed.(3) The minimum consumed energy with better burial of the residues and mixing of seeds in the soil can occur at a disc and tilt angle fixed at DA = 30° and TA = 15°.

Limitations and future improvements

Despite the promising FEM results, in this study, the analysis of the effect of each tillage parameter (tillage depth, speed, DA, TA) on the tillage forces (Fd, Fv, Fl) was considered variable and the other parameter maintained constant, which was not able to cover all possible cases of tillage process of disc plow. This limitation needs to be addressed in future research using the design of experiments (DOE), with a full factorial experiment when each factor (tillage depth, speed, DA, TA) is tested at distinct values, or levels, and the tests include every possible combination of different levels across all factors. This approach let us see not only how each factor individually affects the tillage forces and the disturbance of soil (two responses Y1 and Y2), but also how the factors (tillage depth, speed, DA, TA) interact and influence each other. This analysis can develop with statistical software such as Design–Expert. Finally, a regression mathematical model can be established between the factors and responses for the tillage forces (Y1) and the disturbance rate (Y2).

The use of FEM with the design of experiments can also reduce the number of tests with the soil bin, validate all these results and extend for other design geometry of the disc plow, moldboard plow, and chisel tool.

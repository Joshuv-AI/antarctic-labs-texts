The assessment of knowledge and awareness about iron deficiency anemia among the population of Riyadh province
Authors: Saad Alsaab, Omar S. Alhussayn, Ziyad A. Altokhais, Abdulmalik I. Alotaibi, Fahad A. Alsahli, Bader A. Alotaibi, Naif M. Alrawais, Mohammad Arshad
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Iron deficiency anemia (IDA) is considered the most common micronutrient deficiency and the greatest health concern throughout the globe. The main causes of anemia are iron deficiency, parasitic infestations, viral infections (human immunodeficiency virus), hemorrhage, and renal and hepatic diseases. Therefore, it is mandatory to be equipped with the proper knowledge regarding the causes, signs, symptoms, and diets containing iron, which can be a good initiative for the proper treatment and management of the health hazards; also, it will help primary care physicians to differentiate the degree of the disease and further treatment, planning, and management. The recent study was performed to understand the level of knowledge and awareness about IDA and its causes, prevention, and treatment.Methods:The study was carried out through an online survey using Google forms, which were distributed to the population residing in Riyadh ProvinceResults and Discussion:The responses received about the knowledge regarding IDA and its symptoms indicated weakness [565 (52.9%)], cold hands and feet [593 (55.5%)], and hair loss [663 (62.1)], and the majority (84.3%) of the participants believed that the major cause for IDA is malnutritionConclusion and Recommendations:Conclusively, it was believed that the presented study will be helpful to primary care physicians, the patients suffering from IDA, and the general population with a better understanding of the causes, additional signs and symptoms, proper treatment, timely management, sources of iron, and how to avoid the worse outcomes and complications of the disease. It will also help the health agencies in the kingdom to set further planning for the treatment and management of the disease through awareness platforms.

Introduction

Presently, iron deficiency anemia (IDA) is considered to be the most common micronutrient deficiency throughout the globe.[1] Hewawaduge D et al. reported a similar study in Sri Lanka aimed at the extent of knowledge of iron deficiency anemia among the pregnant women in the first trimester. The most known side effects were observed as a low birth weight (32%), premature delivery (30.5%), and postpartum anemia (30%).[2] Abalkhail B and Shawky S performed a study in Saudi Arabia aimed at measuring the prevalence of daily breakfast intake, IDA, and awareness of being anemic among Saudi school students. The authors observed that anemia was reported among 20.5% and was prevalent among school students. The causes included such as skipping breakfast were reported by 14.9%, and it was also observed that only 34.1% of anemic school students were aware of being anemic.[3] Alayoubi F et al.[4] reported a similar study and observed that about half of the study sample had a lightly active lifestyle; more than one-third were overweight (34.7%). The factors significantly associated with anemia included gender; female gender showed a positive association with anemia. Diet, heavy menstruation, pregnancy, and intake of NSAID were additional important risk factors among these individuals, but they were statistically not significant. A study aimed at determining the prevalence of anemia and its associate risk factors was carried out by AlFaris N et al.[5] among non-pregnant females in Riyadh province. Another study was performed by Arbaeen AF and Iqbal to understand the burden of anemia among the people who attended hospitals in Makkah.[6] Studies aimed at determining the prevalence and risk factors of IDA were performed by AlSheikh[7] and Al-Jamea et al.[8] among Saudi female students and female undergraduates of Prince Sultan Military College of Health Sciences, respectively. Petry et al.[9] conducted a systematic review of nationally representative surveys that reported pre-school children and non-pregnant women of reproductive age; the proportion of anemia associated with iron deficiency was 25.0% [95% confidence interval (CI): 18.0, 32.0] and 37.0% (95% CI: 28.0, 46.0), respectively. The proportion of anemia associated with iron deficiency was lower in countries where anemia prevalence was >40%, especially in rural populations (14% for pre-school children and 16% for non-pregnant women of reproductive age), and in countries with very high inflammation exposure (20% for pre-school children and 25% for non-pregnant women of reproductive age).[9] Recently, Negi PC et al.[10] reported the assessment of prevalence, significance, and risk factors of IDA and anemia among non-ischemic heart failure patients. The authors concluded that low Hb and transferrin saturation were significantly linked with advanced heart failure. Another study reported by Lopez AP et al.[11] explained that anemia and IDA were so common among pregnant women; therefore, a complete prenatal screening is essential for identifying the associated risk factors. As per the literature, it was also observed that the World Health Organization limits anemia, mild, moderate, and severe; if it is severe, only the primary care physicians are the concerned authorities to overlook this. Therefore, the study will provide an association among the patients and the primary care physicians for a better understanding of the disease and the degree of the disease and further follow-ups for the treatment and management.[12,13] Therefore, still, insufficient research has been conducted to assess the extent of knowledge and awareness about IDA, especially in the Riyadh province.

Methods

A recent study was carried out on the population residing in Riyadh Province using the online survey through Google forms. The Google forms were distributed among the population through e-mail, WhatsApp, or Facebook. The Google forms contained questions to understand the awareness of the people about the causes of IDA, the symptoms of IDA, prevention of IDA, whether they have suffered from IDA in the past, the sources of iron, and their deficiency leading to IDA. The responses from the random sample size were recorded from the population using the inclusion and exclusion criteria. The inclusion criteria: All the people who are 12 years and above (male or female). Exclusion criteria: Population with an age below 12 years and pregnant woman; the population outside Riyadh Province will be followed in the study. The study was ethically approved by the IRB (CMD/DWD/SU/2021/01/044).

Results and Discussion

As per the responses received from 1068 participants, it was observed that out of 1068 participants, 756 (70.8%) were males and 312 (29.2%) were females with varying ages, 12–20 years (66.2%), 20–40 years (30.1%), and more than 40 years (3.7%), Figure 1. Moreover, a similar study was conducted by Lionis C et al.,[14] which assessed the extent of knowledge of IDA among pregnant women, and the findings revealed that there was a huge gap about the knowledge of IDA among pregnant women, so the authors made some recommendation regarding the simplifications and digitalization of the routes to spread the awareness of this disease to prevent the possible abnormality, mortality, and morbidities because of this disease.

Distribution of genders and age groups who responded to the questionnaire

The participants responded well about the knowledge regarding IDA and its symptoms, weakness [565 (52.9%)], cold hands and feet [593 (55.5%)], and hair loss [663 (62.1)], Figure 2, Table 1. The majority of the participants (84.3%) believed that the major cause of IDA is malnutrition, and it can be prevented by following nutritious diet and simultaneously avoiding food that reduces the absorption of iron (53%). The same approach was followed up by Marina Visser et al.,[15] aiming the school children. The study also stated that the major cause for IDA was a diet with a lack of iron and the condition can be minimized or controlled by using a diet rich in iron. Similarly, the participants responded about the sources of iron, and it was observed that the majority of the participants believed that fruit, vegetables (59.2%), and meat (59.5) are the major sources of iron, Figure 3.

Conclusion

The study was aimed to assess the extent of knowledge and awareness about IDA. The study was performed by an online survey using Google forms. As per the responses received about the knowledge regarding IDA and its symptoms, there are weakness [565 (52.9%)], cold hands and feet [593 (55.5%)], and hair loss [663 (62.1)], and the majority (84.3%) of the participants believed that the major cause for IDA is malnutrition. Conclusively, the present study will be helpful to the clinicians as well as the patients with a better understanding the causes, additional signs and symptoms, proper treatment, timely management, and sources and also to avoid the worse outcomes and complications. It will also help the health agencies in the kingdom to set further planning and management of the disease through awareness platforms.

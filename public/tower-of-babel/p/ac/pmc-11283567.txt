Research on disturbance influence of goaf residual deformation on simply supported beam bridge
Authors: Qingbiao Guo, Jiasheng Yang, Jiahui Chen, Xuexiang Yu, Lei Wang, Zhansheng Wan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn order to analyze the stability of the bridge above the goaf, the disturbance influence of goaf residual deformation on the bridge is studied. Firstly, an equivalent numerical simulation method of goaf residual deformation evolution process is studied by quantitative analysis the sensitivity of residual subsidence to the rock parameters using the OAT (one-variable-at-a-time). Then, the collaborative deformation of ground, pile, and bridge floor is studied under the condition of a simply-supported beam bridge above the goaf center. Finally, the mechanism of collaborative deformation of ground, pile, and bridge floor is revealed. The results show that the goaf residual deformation process can be obtained by weakening the elastic modulus of fractured rock in the caving zone. At the final residual deformation stage, the subsidence ratio of ground to pile is about 10, and the subsidence ratio of pile to bridge floor is about 2, while the ground horizontal movement ratio of ground to pile is about 7, and the bridge floor horizontal movement can be ignored. The bridge floor is always in the positive curvature influence zone, and the pile has an inhibitory effect on the curvature deformation of the bridge floor. The compression deformation occurs between the piles locations, while the tensile deformation occurs at the pile location. The evolution of negative frictional resistance derived from goaf residual deformation is the main reason for the change in the collaborative deformation law among the ground, pile and bridge floor. This research can provide scientific support and theoretical basis for the design, construction, and protection of the bridge above the goaf center, and can also provide reference for the stability evaluation of bridge above goaf under other conditions.Keywords: Goaf residual deformation, Simply-supported beam bridge, Disturbance influence, Collaborative deformation, Numerical simulationSubject terms: Civil engineering, Petrology

Coal mine goaf is widely distributed in China, which has long-term residual deformation that may threaten the safe operation of buildings constructed above the goaf1,2. In recent years, with the rapid development of transportation construction in China, the bridge is inevitably built above the goaf3. In order to analyze the health4,5, it is crucial to study the disturbance influence and mechanism of goaf residual deformation on the bridge.

The process of surface subsidence above the goaf has at least three stages: initiation, active subsidence, and attenuation6,7. After 3-rd stage, the surface subsidence gradually reaches a stable state8. However, the construction of bridges and other structures can lead to further compaction of the rock voids in the caving zone, which in turn can trigger surface subsidence again. Some scholars have carried out some related work, and the results can be summarized into two aspects: mechanical analysis and deformation disturbance. Regarding the mechanical analysis, Zhang et al.9 finds that the longitudinal force of simply-supported beam bridge support is distributed in the form of a parabola with small boundaries and a large mid-span by establishing an integrated model and a traditional model for simply-supported beam bridges, respectively. Zhuang et al.10 derives the stress equation for a continuous bridge floor structure based on the linear elastic theory of structural mechanics, and analyzes the main parameters for affecting the stress of a continuous bridge floor. This proposed equation can calculate the bridge structural mechanical parameters accurately. Zhang et al.11 proposes an analysis method of bridge stress weakness based on finite element analysis, and it is verified through an engineering example that this method can effectively analyze the stress of bridge beam and accurately identify stress weaknesses between bridge structures. Regarding the deformation disturbance, Wang et al.12 proposes a modeling method based on real 3D spatial information of the bridge structure, geological structure, and sculpture structure to accurately reflect their spatial relationships, which can be employed to analyze the impact of sculpture structure construction on pier deformations and ground subsidence. Cui et al.13 finds that the maximum vertical and horizontal displacement of the bridge are 61.2 mm and 84.8 mm, respectively, occurring on the sub-bridge closest to the goaf, and the maximum tilt and horizontal strains were 3.42 × 10−5 m/m and 2.19 × 10−5 m/m using the finite element method. Huang14 finds that there is significant subsidence in the shallow rock and soil around the bridge, and the subsidence among the piles is uniform. Liang et al.15 finds that the influence of goaf on the bearing capacity of pile is inversely to the loading degree, the larger of loading degree, the smaller impact of goaf on the bearing capacity using the similar material model, and establishes a formula for calculating bearing capacity and pile subsidence. Qu et al.16 finds that the uplift of the non-bridge area in the goaf is greater than that of the bridge pile foundation, and the ground stress is the primary cause of the arching of a bridge pile foundation. These research results have laid the foundation for the health assessment of bridges above the goaf. However, the residual deformation in goaf is a dynamic evolution process which has obvious long-term residual deformation characteristics. Currently, due to the lack of effective research tool, the whole process research of the disturbance influence of goaf residual deformation on the bridge is still insufficient, resulting in a lack of complete basic basis in the design, construction, and control process system of bridge above goaf.

In this paper, the Cross Songshan Avenue Bridge above the goaf center is taken as an example. Firstly, an effective research tool is studied based on equivalent numerical simulation method, which can show the whole dynamic evolution of goaf residual deformation. Then, the collaborative deformation of ground, pile, and bridge floor is discussed from the subsidence and horizontal movement. Finally, he mechanism of collaborative deformation of ground, pile, and bridge floor is revealed.

Study area

The Cross Songshan Avenue Bridge is on the Jiaozuo to Pingdingshan expressway, in the section from Xingyang to Xinmi, with a length of 160 m, and the bridge body is in a north–south direction, among which the northern section K43 + 780 m to K43 + 920 m of the bridge is about 140 m long above the goaf of Shenhe coal mine, as shown in Fig. 1. The bridge's superstructure consists of prestressed concrete simply-supported beams, with pile diameters of 4000 mm and pile lengths of 50 m, of which 30 m are embedded underground. The pile spacing is 40 m, and the pile bodies are intact. The Shenhe goaf, a single coal seam goaf, is formed before 2012. The goaf roof is mainly carbonaceous shale and sandy mudstone, and the bottom is sandy mudstone. The buried depth of goaf is 290 ~ 300 m, and the average mining height is 5 m. The vertical section diagram of the overlying rock in goaf is shown in Fig. 2.

The 'Guidelines for Coal Pillar Retention and Pressure Coal Mining in Buildings, Water Bodies, Railways and Major Shafts17 stipulates that the outer boundary of a surface subsidence basin is defined by connecting points where the surface subsidence is 10 mm. The standard for stability in the surface subsidence area requires that each point on the surface must have a cumulative subsidence of less than 30 mm continuously for six months, indicating that surface subsidence has gradually reached a stable state. It is worth noting that this paper focuses on the stability of bridges during the residual deformation stage of the goaf, occurring after the surface subsidence has gradually stabilized.

Methodology

Construction of experimental model

FLAC3D (Fast Lagrangian Analysis of Continua 3D) is employed to establish the numerical experimental model based on the above engineering background. The model size is 400 m (length) × 400 m (width) × 433 m (height). All constraints are applied on the bottom boundary of the model, while the top is free. The surrounding boundary of the model is subjected to horizontal displacement constraints18. The Mohr–Coulomb failure criterion is selected to describe the failure process of the rock mass. Interface elements are used in FLAC3D software to simulate the contact surfaces of piles and soil. Equivalent numerical simulation method of goaf residual deformation evolution process

The goaf residual deformation evolution process is a long time series and slow-changing process19,20. Previous studies have shown that the deformation mainly comes from the deformation space released by the cavity and void compaction inside the overlying rock of goaf, and the deformation space is transferred to the surface from bottom to top, causing surface residual deformation21,22. According to Vásárhelyi and Kovács’s research23, when the rock strength parameter decreases, its deformation resistance decreases, and the bearing capacity of the rock will decrease. According to the theory of mining subsidence, after coal extraction, overlying rock stratum can be divided into caving zone, fractured zone, and bending subsidence zone according to different deformation characteristics24,25. Among them, the main bearing structure of the overlying rock is the fractured rock in the fractured zone. If the load-bearing capacity of the fractured rock decreases, even if the overlying rock load remains constant, the voids in the goaf will gradually compact under loading, resulting in residual deformation26. Based on the above analysis, this paper proposes a numerical simulation method that weakens the mechanical parameters of the fractured rock to obtain residual deformation and thus form an equivalent deformation value. This method helps save a significant amount of time and resources while obtaining the residual subsidence conditions at different stages throughout the entire process of residual deformation in the goaf, providing reliable data for subsequent dynamic analysis.

Determining the fractured rock range

The range of fractured rock should be considered from both horizontal and vertical perspectives27. The vertical development range of the fractured rock mass can be calculated by the development height of the caving zone. As the Shenhe coal mine has been closed, it is difficult to obtain accurate data on of the development height of the caving zone in this area, so the empirical formulas is used to calculate the development height of the caving zone28,29. According to the geological conditions of the overlying rock in the goaf, the calculation formula of medium hard rock layers is selected30, as expressed in Eq. (1). It is calculated that the caving zone development height in this area is 12 m. In order to verify the reliability of the empirical formula calculation results, actual drilling verification is carried out on site. Two drilling results on site show that when the drilling reaches 280 m, some multiple collapses are found, accompanied by stuck drilling and difficulties in lifting. The on-site core sampling results are shown in Fig. 4. The drilling results are basically consistent with the empirical formula calculation results. Therefore, the empirical formula can be used to analyze the vertical range of the fractured rock in the goaf31. Due to the limited vertical development height of the fractured rock, the outward expansion horizontal range can be ignored according to the mining subsidence theory, which has little impact on the residual deformation evolution process32. Therefore, for the convenience of research, the horizontal development range of the fractured rock is assumed to be the same as the mining size33.

Simulation process of Goaf residual deformation

The mechanical parameters affecting the deformation of rock mass are mainly internal friction angle, poisson ratio, elastic modulus, and cohesion34,35. The influence of the above parameters on ground residual subsidence is analyzed by numerical simulation, and the variation ranges of the parameters are always within the engineering reasonable ranges. The experimental results are shown in Fig. 5.

Different parameters affect the curve.

As illustrated in Fig. 5, when the internal friction angle, poisson ratio, and cohesion change, the maximum residual subsidence change little, while when the elastic modulus changes from 40 to 30 MPa, the maximum residual subsidence changes 326 mm, showing that the elastic modulus change has the most significant impact on residual subsidence. Then the one-variable-at-a-time approach (OAT) is employed to quantitative analysis the sensitivity of residual subsidence to the above rock parameters. As illustrated in Fig. 6, with the same rate of change, the change of elastic modulus causes the residual subsidence to change at a rate of about 70%, while the change rate of residual deformation caused by the change of the other three parameters is less than 20%, especially the change rate of residual subsidence caused by poisson ratio is less than 5%. The sensitivity ranking of residual subsidence to the above parameters is elastic modulus > cohesion > internal friction angle > poisson ratio. Therefore, for the convenience of research, this paper will simulate the evolution process of goaf residual deformation by gradually weakening the elastic modulus of fractured rock in caving zone.

Results and discussion

Considering that the upper part of the simply-supported beam bridge is a hinged link37,38, the shear force and bending moment of the simply-supported beam bridge remain unchanged within the range of abutment support39,40. Therefore, this article focuses on the co-deformation of the bridge construction site, pile, and bridge floor when discussing the impact of goaf residual deformation on the disturbance of the simply-supported beam bridge.

Disturbance influence caused by residual subsidence

The subsidence of the ground, piles, and bridge floor are obtained during the simulation experiments, as shown in Fig. 7. As illustrated in Fig. 7, With the increase of ground residual subsidence, the residual subsidence of pile and bridge floor also increase gradually. The overall subsidence characteristics of ground, piles, and bridge floor are similar, showing a non-linear change process of rapid subsidence first and then slowing down. However, the ground subsidence is significantly greater than that of pile and bridge floor. When the ground residual subsidence reaches its maximum of 230 mm, the maximum subsidence of pile is 24.4 mm, while the maximum subsidence of bridge floor is only 12 mm. As the bridge in the study area is located above the goaf center, the subsidence of ground, pile and bridge floor are all gentle. However, considering that the relative positions of each pile and the goaf are not strictly the same, the subsidence values of different piles are also different. For example, the ground subsidence of B3 pile location is 230 mm, while the subsidence of B3 pile is 24.0 mm. The ground subsidence of B4 pile location is 224.1 mm, while the subsidence of B4 pile is 22.1 mm. Thus, the subsidence of ground and pile decrease with the distance increasing from the ground to the goaf center. The final subsidence distribution of the model, as shown in Fig. 8, the figure results are consistent with the above analysis. To analyze the collaborative subsidence of ground, pile and bridge floor in the same vertical direction, k1 is employed to represent the ratio of ground subsidence to pile subsidence, and k2 is employed to represent the ratio of pile subsidence to bridge floor subsidence. It can be calculated that at the final residual subsidence state, the pile subsidence is about 1/10 of the ground subsidence at any position, and the bridge floor subsidence is about 1/2 of the pile subsidence at any position.

Residual subsidence curves of ground, pile and bridge floor.

Final subsidence distribution of the model.

It should be noted that k1 and k2 are not constant values, but exhibit a nonlinear evolution process with the occurrence of residual subsidence, and the change process of k1 and k2 is opposite. k1 is about 12 at the initial stage, and then gradually decreases to 10, which can be fitted by a power function. While k2 is about 1.9 at the initial stage, and then gradually increases to 2.0, which can be fitted by an exponential function.

Based on the above analysis, uneven ground subsidence can lead to uneven pile subsidence, resulting in bridge tilting and instability. Uneven tilting deformation of pile can result in curvature deformation, which can be obtained by laying an observation line on the bridge floor. As illustrated in Fig. 9, Due to the bridge in the study area being located above the goaf center, the bridge floor subsidence is gentle, resulting in less tile and curvature deformation, and the bridge floor is always in the positive curvature influence zone. Based on the analysis of the pile position, it is found the bridge floor curvature reaches its maximum between the pile locations and its minimum at the pile location, indicating that the pile has an inhibitory effect on the curvature deformation of the bridge floor when the bridge is above the goaf center.

Disturbance influence caused by residual horizontal movement

The horizontal movement of the ground, piles, and bridge floor is obtained during the simulation experiments, as shown in Fig. 10. The horizontal movement of the simply-supported beam bridge can be divided into along the bridge direction and perpendicular to the bridge direction. As the horizontal movement perpendicular to the bridge direction is small, this paper only analyzes the horizontal movement along the bridge direction. As illustrated in Fig. 10, With the increase of ground horizontal movement, the horizontal movement of pile and bridge floor also increases gradually. The overall horizontal movement characteristics of ground, piles, and bridge floor are similar, showing a non-linear change process of rapid movement first and then slowing down. However, the ground horizontal movement is significantly greater than that of pile and bridge floor. When the ground horizontal movement reaches the maximum of 71.4 mm, the maximum horizontal movement of pile is 13.4 mm, while the maximum horizontal movement of bridge floor is only 0.6 mm. Similar to the characteristics of subsidence evolution, the horizontal movement of the ground and its associated pile gradually decreases with the distance increasing from the ground to the goaf center. The k3 is employed to represent the ratio of pile horizontal movement to ground horizontal movement, and it can be calculated to be about 1/7. The k4 is employed to represent the ratio of bridge floor horizontal movement to pile horizontal movement, and it is about 0.05.

Horizontal movement curve of ground, pile, and bridge floor.

It should be noted that although the horizontal movement of the bridge floor is very small, the horizontal movement of the bridge floor can still be obtained due to the ground horizontal movement, as shown in Fig. 11. It is found that when the bridge is located above the goaf center, compression deformation occurs between the piles locations, while tensile deformation occurs at the pile location.

Mechanism of collaborative deformation of ground, pile, and bridge floor

There is a significant difference of residual deformation among the ground, pile and bridge floor, and the quantitative relationships of the deformation differences between them show a dynamic change process. The ground subsidence is caused by the compaction of voids in the goaf, while the pile subsidence is caused by soil consolidation compression under the pile load. In addition, as the ground subsidence is significantly larger the pile subsidence, the downward negative frictional resistance during residual deformation will be occured on the pile side, increasing the pile subsidence. In addition, at the initial stage of residual deformation, there are many voids in the goaf, the residual deformation response on the ground is relatively rapid. Subsequently, with the gradual compaction of voids, the ground subsidence slows down41, causing k1 to decrease and stabilize.

The reason for the deformation difference between the pile and bridge floor is different from that between the ground and pile. This is because the pile subsidence can cause significant separation area in the structure between the pile and the bridge floor due to the difference in stiffness between them, and this separation area will further increase under the effects of shrinkage and creep of concrete. In addition, with the continuous occurrence of residual deformation, the deformation difference between the pile and bridge floor causes stress concentration, causing the bridge floor to rotate within a certain allowable range due to the hinge structure. Then, the bridge floor subsidence is slowed down, causing k2 to increase and stabilize.

According to the mining subsidence theory, it can be assumed that the rock mass maintains a constant volume during the movement and deformation42, when analyzing the horizontal movement of overlying strata. Then, the residual horizontal movement in the goaf can be regarded as derived from the subsidence. Thus, the mechanism of collaborative horizontal movement of ground, pile, and bridge floor is same with that of subsidence, and it will not be further discussed here. In addition, it should be noted that the research object in this paper is the Cross Songshan Avenue Bridge in Henan Province, China, which is built above the goaf center. This research is based on this specific engineering case study, and all the obtained results in this paper can provide scientific support and theoretical basis for the design, construction, and protection of the bridge above the goaf center. However, under the different geological and mining conditions, the ground, pile, and bridge floor may exhibit different collaborative deformation characteristics. In the future, with the increase of relevant engineering cases, this relevant research will continue to be conducted to enrich the theoretical system in this field.

Conclusion

The elastic modulus is the main parameter affecting the goaf residual deformation. Under the same change rate, the residual subsidence change rate caused by the elastic modulus change is about 70%, while the residual subsidence change rates caused by the other parameters are all less than 20%. The goaf residual deformation process can be obtained by weakening the elastic modulus of fractured rock in the caving zone.

The residual deformation evolution processes of ground, pile and bridge floor are all non-linear, showing rapid change first and then slowing down. The ground residual deformation is significantly greater than that of pile and bridge floor, and the bridge floor horizontal movement can be ignored. At the final residual deformation stage, the subsidence ratio of ground to pile is about 10, and the subsidence ratio of pile to bridge floor is about 2. while the ground horizontal movement ratio of ground to pile is about 7.

The reliability and validity study of the Turkish version of the Uncertainty about Disease and Treatment Scale in hemodialysis patients
Authors: Reva Gündoğan, Onur Çetinkaya, Mümin Savaş
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundUncertainty is common in chronic illness and can weaken treatment adherence and psychosocial well-being. Hemodialysis involves persistent symptoms and treatment unpredictability, making uncertainty assessment clinically important. This study was methodologically designed to test the psychometric properties of the Turkish form of the Uncertainty about Disease and Treatment Scale (UC-D&TS) in hemodialysis patients.MethodAdult patients receiving maintenance hemodialysis in a state hospital and a private dialysis center in southern Turkey were recruited between February and June 2025 (n = 155). The scale underwent linguistic and cultural adaptation using the forward-backward translation method. Test–retest reliability was evaluated in a randomly selected subgroup of 50 participants from the original sample after a 2-week interval. Data were collected using a Descriptive Information Form and the 17-item UC-D&TS (5-point Likert). Content validity was assessed via expert review and content validity index (CVI). Construct validity was examined with Kaiser–Meyer–Olkin (KMO), Bartlett’s test, exploratory factor analysis (EFA; principal axis factoring extraction, varimax), and confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) in LISREL. Internal consistency was evaluated using Cronbach’s alpha and stability using test–retest reliability.ResultsContent validity was acceptable (CVI = 0.80). Sampling adequacy supported factor analysis (KMO = 0.821) and Bartlett’s test was significant (χ²=992.856, p < 0.001). EFA produced a four-factor solution explaining 59.13% of variance; all loadings were ≥ 0.30 and no items were removed. CFA indicated acceptable fit (χ²/df = 1.77; NNFI = 0.93; CFI = 0.94; IFI = 0.95; PNFI = 0.74; SRMR = 0.072; RMSEA = 0.071). Reliability was high (total α = 0.872; subscales α = 0.641–0.824) and test–retest reliability was excellent (r = 0.986).ConclusionsThe Turkish UC-D&TS, which underwent Exploratory Factor Analysis (EFA) to determine its factor structure, demonstrated satisfactory content validity, a stable four-factor structure, acceptable model fit, high internal consistency, and test-retest reliability.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12882-026-05227-y.

Introduction

The disease process is a complex experience that involves not only physiological changes but also psychological, social, and mental dimensions. One of the fundamental experiences individuals encounter during this process is uncertainty [1]. Chronic kidney failure is a significant health problem that requires long-term and often lifelong treatment approaches for the individual to sustain their life. Hemodialysis, one of the most commonly used renal replacement therapies in these patients, prolongs life but also causes significant changes in the individual’s physical, psychological, and social life [2, 3]. In conditions such as chronic kidney disease, which require long-term, complex treatment, the perception of uncertainty negatively affects individuals’ expectations for the future and makes compliance difficult [4, 5].

The progressive nature of the disease in hemodialysis patients, the continuity and uncertainty of hemodialysis treatment, life expectancy, and uncertainty about the future can cause intense anxiety in patients. It has been reported that high perceived uncertainty in hemodialysis patients is associated with anxiety, depression, hopelessness, and decreased quality of life [6, 7]. Furthermore, the unpredictability of symptoms commonly observed in hemodialysis patients contributes to ongoing uncertainty, negatively affecting patients’ adherence to treatment and self-care behaviors [8, 9]. Therefore, accurately and reliably measuring uncertainty is of great importance for developing comprehensive care approaches for hemodialysis patients.

The Mishel Uncertainty in Illness Scale (MUIS), developed by Mishel in 1981, consists of four subdimensions and 33 items designed to measure uncertainty in individuals with chronic diseases. This scale assesses uncertainty about the disease but not about treatment [1]. Although the Mishel Uncertainty in Illness Scale is widely used to assess illness-related uncertainty, it was not specifically developed for patients undergoing hemodialysis and does not comprehensively evaluate uncertainty related to dialysis treatment, symptom fluctuations, and treatment-specific decision-making. Accordingly, the UC-D&TS was selected because it was specifically developed for hemodialysis patients and provides a more comprehensive assessment of uncertainty associated with both the disease and its treatment.

The UC-D&TS was developed by Rahimi Esbo et al. in 2024 [10], and its psychometric properties were subsequently validated in a Chinese population by Wang et al. in 2025 [11]. The original version comprised 17 items organized into five factors (self-uncertainty, uncertain situation, uncertain future, uncertainty regarding treatment outcomes, and information uncertainty), explaining 82.16% of the total variance. In the original study, the scale demonstrated satisfactory psychometric properties, with Cronbach’s alpha and McDonald’s omega coefficients reported as 0.828 and 0.818, respectively. In addition, the intraclass correlation coefficient was 0.977, indicating excellent measurement stability [10].

Torres and Peña-Amaro (2015) evaluated the Spanish version of the scale in patients undergoing hemodialysis. The Spanish version consisted of two subdimensions (Uncertainty and Complexity) and 17 items, with a content validity index of 0.70 and a Cronbach’s alpha coefficient of 0.72 [12].

The UC-D&TS comprehensively evaluates uncertainty related to both the disease process and dialysis treatment by incorporating symptom experiences, treatment outcomes, and disease-specific concerns, making it particularly suitable for patients undergoing hemodialysis [10]. For a measurement tool to be used across cultures and patient groups, its psychometric properties must be tested in the target population. Therefore, examining the validity and reliability of the Turkish version of the UC-D&TS is important to provide healthcare professionals with a culturally appropriate and psychometrically sound instrument for clinical practice [13].

Method material

Research design

This study was methodologically designed to test the psychometric properties of the UC-D&TS.

Population and sample of the study

A total of 155 patients who met the inclusion criteria and agreed to participate were included in the final study sample (n = 155). In scale adaptation and psychometric validation studies, it is widely accepted in the structural equation modeling (SEM) literature that the sample size should ideally be at least 5 to 10 times the number of items in the measurement tool. Since the Uncertainty About Disease and Treatment Scale (UC-D&TS) consists of 17 items, the minimum required sample size was calculated to be between 85 and 170 participants. Thus, the sample size of 155 achieved in this study was psychometrically adequate for Exploratory Factor Analysis (EFA) and Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA) and provided sufficient statistical power. Furthermore, a randomly selected subgroup of 50 patients (n = 50) was utilized for the test–retest reliability evaluation [14, 15]. Data for the study were collected between February and June 2025. Informed consent to participate was obtained from all of the participants in the study.

Translation and cross-cultural adaptation

Before the study, written permission was obtained from the original developer via e-mail to translate, culturally adapt, and psychometrically evaluate the Turkish version of the Uncertainty about Disease and Treatment Scale (UC-D&TS). The translation and cross-cultural adaptation process followed the World Health Organization (WHO) guidelines for the translation and adaptation of instruments [16].

The original English version of the UC-D&TS was translated into Turkish by the research team. To evaluate the linguistic appropriateness and cultural relevance of the translated version, the preliminary Turkish version was reviewed by 10 nursing academics working at universities in Türkiye. The experts evaluated the wording, clarity, and cultural appropriateness of each item and provided recommendations for revision. All expert recommendations were reviewed by the research team, and a consensus was reached to prepare a revised Turkish version of the instrument.

The revised Turkish version was subsequently back-translated into English and linguistically evaluated by an academic specializing in English Language and Literature. The back-translated version was compared with the original instrument to ensure conceptual and linguistic equivalence. Following this evaluation, the research team incorporated the recommended revisions and prepared the final Turkish version of the scale. The overall translation, cross-cultural adaptation, and psychometric evaluation process is summarized in Fig. 1.

Psychometric evaluation of the Turkish version

The finalized Turkish version was administered to 155 patients receiving maintenance hemodialysis to evaluate its psychometric properties. The psychometric evaluation of the Turkish version was conducted in accordance with internationally accepted recommendations for the validation of health measurement instruments and included assessments of content validity, construct validity, internal consistency, and test–retest reliability. Content validity was assessed using the Content Validity Index (CVI) based on the experts’ evaluations. Construct validity was examined using exploratory factor analysis (EFA) and confirmatory factor analysis (CFA), while reliability was evaluated using Cronbach’s alpha coefficient and test–retest reliability. Exploratory factor analysis supported a four-factor structure, rather than the original five-factor structure. To evaluate temporal stability, the final Turkish version was re-administered to a randomly selected subgroup of 50 participants after a two-week interval.

Data collection tools

This study was prepared based on a literature review. It used a Descriptive Information Form and the Uncertainty about Disease and Treatment Scale (UC-D&TS), developed by Rahimi Esbo et al. (2024), to determine participants’ demographic characteristics, hemodialysis-related characteristics, and the presence of other chronic diseases [10, 17, 18]. The Descriptive Information Form was specifically developed for this study and is provided as Supplementary File 1.

The UC-D&TS consists of 17 items rated on a five-point Likert scale. None of the items is reverse-scored. The total raw score ranges from 17 to 85, with higher scores indicating greater uncertainty regarding disease and treatment. For ease of interpretation, the total score can be transformed to a standardized score ranging from 0 to 100 using a linear transformation formula, with higher scores indicating greater uncertainty. The questionnaire requires approximately 15 min to complete [10].

Data collection

Data were collected by the researchers through face-to-face interviews using the Descriptive Information Form and the UC-D&TS. Interviews lasted approximately 30 min. All psychometric analyses (content validity, EFA, CFA, and internal consistency) were performed using the full sample of 155 participants. To assess temporal stability, the UC-D&TS was re-administered 14 days later to a randomly selected subgroup of 50 participants drawn from the original sample. No additional participants were recruited for the test–retest analysis. Before the main study, a pilot application was conducted with 10 hemodialysis patients (not included in the final sample) to evaluate the linguistic clarity of the items, and no changes were required.

Ethics approval and consent to participate

Ethical approval for the study was obtained from the Health Sciences Research Ethics Committee of Osmaniye Korkut Ata University (dated December 19, 2024, No. E.211570) and from the hospital where the research was conducted. Informed consent to participate was obtained from all of the participants in the study. The study was conducted in accordance with the Helsinki Declaration on Human Rights.

Data analysis

SPSS 20.0 and LISREL 8.7 were used to analyze the scale. The Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin (KMO) and Bartlett’s tests were used to assess the construct validity of the UC-D&TS. Exploratory Factor Analysis (EFA) using the principal axis factoring method with varimax rotation was performed to examine the construct validity and latent structure of the scale. In the psychometric evaluation of the scale, specific standard thresholds were adopted to assess construct validity. For the Exploratory Factor Analysis (EFA), a factor loading of ≥ 0.30 was adopted as the minimum criterion for item retention, consistent with the recommendations of Hair et al. (2019) and Seçer (2023) [14, 19]. Following the EFA, Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA) was executed via LISREL 8.7 to statistically test and confirm the discovered macro-level structural model and to evaluate individual item-level parameters (standardized coefficients, standard errors, and t-values). For the Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA), the structural model’s fit was evaluated using multiple indices based on widely accepted criteria in the literature. Specifically, a Chi-square/degree of freedom (χ²/df) ratio of less than 3.0 was considered a good fit, and less than 5.0 was considered acceptable. For the Non-Normed Fit Index (NNFI), Comparative Fit Index (CFI), and Incremental Fit Index (IFI), values > 0.90 indicated acceptable fit, while values > 0.95 represented good fit. For the Parsimony Normed Fit Index (PNFI), values > 0.50 were deemed satisfactory. For both the Standardized Root Mean Square Residual (SRMR) and the Root Mean Square Error of Approximation (RMSEA), values < 0.08 were accepted as an adequate fit, and values < 0.05 indicated a close/good fit [19].

To assess the internal consistency and reliability of the scale, Cronbach’s alpha (α) coefficients were calculated both for the overall scale and for each individual subscale. Additionally, corrected item-total correlations were checked to ensure each item’s contribution to the overarching construct.

Results

Participants’ characteristics

The average age of patients participating in the study was 53.21 ± 17.37, and 53.5% were male. The vast majority of hemodialysis patients (72.3%) were married. Approximately half of the participants (48.4%) were elementary school graduates, 23.2% were high school graduates, 67.7% had social security, 57.4% had income lower than their expenses, 36.1% had income equal to their expenses, and 94.8% were not working. 60.6% of patients reported not receiving education about hemodialysis. 41.9% started hemodialysis within 1–3 years, 25.8% within 4–6 years, and 20.6% after 10 years or more. 95.5% of patients underwent hemodialysis 3 times a week, and 27.1% experienced adverse events during hemodialysis. Hypotension was the most common complication at 12.3%, while 4.5% experienced cramps. 69.7% of patients reported having another chronic disease, and 88.4% stated that they were taking medication regularly (Table 1).

Validity testing

Content validity

The statements and scale included in the UC-D&TS were sent to five experts for evaluation. The scale was sent to a committee of five academic experts for content validity assessment. The committee comprised three senior faculty members specializing in Nephrology Nursing (each holding a PhD and having over 10 years of clinical experience) and two experts in nursing research methodology and psychometrics. This background ensured both clinical relevance and methodological rigor. As a result of the experts’ evaluation, the experts provided positive feedback on all items of the scale. The Item-Level CVI (I-CVI) values ranged between 0.80 and 1.00, and the overall Scale-Level Content Validity Index (S-CVI/Ave) was calculated as 0.94.

Construct validity

The exploratory factor analysis (EFA) for the study was performed using principal axis factoring extraction with varimax rotation. The factor analysis for the study was performed using principal component extraction with varimax rotation. The Bartlett test score for the UC-D&TS was 992.856 (p < 0.001), and the KMO value was 0.821. These values indicate that the scale is within the appropriate range for the global test. In line with these values, an exploratory factor analysis (EFA) was performed. As a result of the EFA, a four-factor structure with eigenvalues greater than 1.0 was obtained. The scale explains 59.130% of the variance, with an eigenvalue of 1.265. All items were validated without removing any items from the scale.

The factor loadings for all items of the UC-D&TS are above 0.30 (Table 2). The EFA samples used to determine the factor structure were analyzed with confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) in LISREL 8.7. The model obtained from the CFA analysis in the study is shown in Fig. 2. The UC-D&TS has acceptable fit values with its 17 items and four-factor structure, as indicated by CFA (χ2 (N = 155) = 199.18, χ2/df = 1.77). Reliability testing

Cronbach’s alpha was calculated to assess the internal consistency of the UC-D&TS. The analysis showed that the Cronbach’s alpha values were within an acceptable range. The Cronbach’s alpha value for the entire UC-D&TS was 0.872. The factor loadings for the scale’s subscales were 0.824, 0.739, 0.769, and 0.641, respectively (Table 5). With these values, the UC-D&TS has acceptable internal consistency. To determine the test-retest reliability of the UC-D&TS, the scale was reapplied to 50 hemodialysis patients two weeks later. The test-retest reliability of the UC-D&TS was calculated as 0.986, indicating that the scale is reliable.

Discussion

Rahimi Esbo et al. (2024) developed a scale for hemodialysis patients and examined its psychometric properties. The scale’s content validity (qualitative-quantitative), construct validity (EFA), reliability (Cronbach’s alpha, McDonald’s omega), and measurement stability were reported. It was noted that the final form consisted of 17 items across 5 factors, and that internal consistency was acceptable (α = 0.828; Ω = 0.818) [10].

In our study, within the scope of validity analyses, the content validity assessment yielded a CVI of 0.80 based on expert opinions, indicating that the scale’s content validity is at an acceptable level. According to Seçer (2023), a CVI value above 0.70 indicates that the scale items adequately represent the structure to be measured [19]. This result suggests that the items of the UC-D&TS Scale appropriately reflect the concept of uncertainty in hemodialysis patients.

In our study, the KMO value of 0.821 obtained within the scope of construct validity, along with the significance of Bartlett’s test of sphericity (p < 0.001), indicates that the data are suitable for factor analysis. The fact that the scale exhibits a four-factor structure and explains 59.13% of the total variance, as determined by an exploratory factor analysis, is psychometrically acceptable. In scale development studies, explaining more than 50% of the total variance is considered sufficient [14]. This finding supports the scale’s structural integrity.

Furthermore, the fact that all items have factor loadings above 0.30 indicates that the items adequately represent the relevant factors [15]. The absence of items removed from the scale indicates that the original scale structure was preserved in the Turkish sample. When examining the confirmatory factor analysis results, the χ²/df ratio of 1.77 and CFI, NNFI, and IFI values above 0.90 indicate acceptable fit. The RMSEA (0.071) and SRMR (0.072) values are also within acceptable limits, confirming the four-factor structure.

When reliability analyses are examined, the scale’s total Cronbach’s alpha coefficient of 0.872 indicates high internal consistency. In scale development and adaptation studies, a Cronbach’s alpha value of 0.70 or higher is considered sufficient. The Cronbach’s alpha values for the subscales, ranging from 0.641 to 0.824, indicate acceptable levels of reliability [20]. Similarly, Rahimi Esbo and colleagues (2024) reported Cronbach’s alpha and McDonald’s omega values above 0.80 in the original development study of the scale. These findings are consistent with the results of the current study [10]. The correlation coefficient of 0.986 obtained in the test–retest reliability assessment conducted two weeks apart indicates that the scale is highly stable over time. This value supports the claim that the scale provides consistent results across measurement repetitions and can be used reliably in clinical research.

In the item analysis, the item-total correlations for Item 1 (0.268) and Item 4 (0.287) fell slightly below the traditional 0.30 threshold. However, these items were retained to preserve the original tool’s content validity and clinical scope. In this study, both items showed acceptable factor loadings (0.472 and 0.434), and the total Cronbach’s alpha was excellent (0.872), justifying their inclusion.

Rahimi Esbo et al. (2024) developed the UC-D&TS Scale using a sequential, exploratory, mixed-methods approach and conducted psychometric evaluations among hemodialysis patients in Iran. The study reported that the scale had 17 items and a five-factor structure, that the internal consistency coefficients were acceptable, and that the scale adequately represented the uncertainty structure [10]. In the current study, the 17-item scale structure was retained, but the factor analysis yielded a four-factor solution. The literature emphasizes that scales are expected to show variations in the number of factors across cultures, and that this may be related to differences in cultural perceptions [14].

A deeper item-level analysis revealed that items originally belonging to separate dimensions of ‘Symptom Uncertainty’ and ‘Treatment Uncertainty’ loaded together onto a single factor (Factor 1) in the Turkish cohort. This structural shift can be attributed to cultural and contextual differences in how Turkish hemodialysis patients perceive their illness experience. In the Turkish healthcare context, patients often view physical symptoms (such as post-dialysis fatigue, cramps, and hypotension) not as independent clinical ambiguities, but as direct, routine, and intertwined consequences of the hemodialysis treatment itself. Therefore, the participants conceptualize the unpredictability of symptoms and the uncertainty regarding treatment protocols as a unified phenomenon.

In the Chinese adaptation study by Wang et al. (2025), the UC-D&TS Scale was tested using both exploratory and confirmatory factor analyses, demonstrating that its factor structure is largely consistent with the original form. The high test–retest reliability (r = 0.986) obtained in the present study parallels the findings of Wang et al. (2025) and supports the scale’s reliability in clinical research [11].

Regarding internal consistency, the total Cronbach’s alpha of 0.872 in the current study is consistent with the reliability coefficients reported by Rahimi Esbo et al. (2024) and Wang et al. (2025) [10, 11]. The Cronbach’s alpha values for the subscales range from 0.641 to 0.824 indicate relatively low values in some subscales; however, the literature reports that alpha values above 0.60 are acceptable, especially in subscales with few items [14]. This suggests that the scale’s subscales can be used for clinical assessment.

Although the Cronbach’s alpha for Factor 4 was relatively low (0.641), it remains within the acceptable range. Psychometric literature emphasizes that alpha coefficients are highly dependent on the number of items, and values above 0.60 are considered sufficient for short subscales containing fewer than 5 items [19]. Therefore, Factor 4 was deemed sufficiently reliable for clinical and research applications.

Limitations of the study

This study has several limitations. First, the study was conducted at a state hospital and a private dialysis center in a single province in southern Türkiye, and participants were recruited via convenience sampling. Consequently, the findings should be interpreted with caution, as the sample may not fully represent the broader population of Turkish patients undergoing hemodialysis. Second, while the sample size is sufficient for psychometric analyses, larger samples including participants from different regions may provide stronger evidence regarding the factor structure and reliability of the scale. Third, because the data were collected via self-report, there is a risk of recall and social desirability biases. Finally, because there is no Turkish-language instrument measuring disease- and treatment-related uncertainty in hemodialysis patients, the scale’s criterion validity could not be evaluated.

Due to the limited sample size (N = 155) in this study, structural stability analysis or separate factor analyses could not be performed across demographic subgroups (e.g., gender, age categories, and dialysis duration). Consequently, the stability of the four-factor structure across these specific subgroups could not be tested. Future studies are recommended to examine the psychometric properties of the Turkish UC-D&TS across different clinical settings and in larger samples, and to evaluate the scale’s predictor and concurrent validity.

Lastly, the exceptionally high test–retest reliability coefficient (0.986) may indicate recall bias due to the two-week interval, which may limit the interpretation of temporal stability; therefore, evaluating longer intervals (e.g., 2–4 weeks) in future studies is recommended.

Conclusion

The Turkish version of the UC-D&TS demonstrated satisfactory validity and reliability in assessing uncertainty regarding disease and treatment among patients undergoing hemodialysis. The scale may serve as a practical tool in clinical settings to identify patients experiencing high levels of uncertainty and to guide individualized educational and psychosocial interventions. Higher scores indicate greater uncertainty; however, no diagnostic cut-off score has yet been established. Future studies should determine clinically meaningful cut-off values and evaluate the scale’s predictive utility in diverse hemodialysis populations.

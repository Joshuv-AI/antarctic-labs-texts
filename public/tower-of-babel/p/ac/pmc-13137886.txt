Response to the Letter to the Editor Entitled “Atypical anti-GBM disease: A Case for the Occam’s Razor?
Authors: Loreto Fernández-Lorente, José María Mora-Gutiérrez
Published: 2026

The author replies:

We appreciate the thoughtful comments and interest in our recently published article on atypical anti–glomerular basement membrane1 by Ivkovic and Kronblicher.2 Their letter raises concerns regarding the interpretation of serologic findings, histopathology, and other possible pathophysiologic mechanisms underlying these cases.2 We welcome the opportunity to clarify some points, especially considering the limitations we already acknowledged of our retrospective, multicenter study, which may have influenced mostly some uncomplete histopathological data.

First, as the authors correctly note, alternative and potentially more common glomerulonephritis causes need to be carefully excluded before establishing a diagnosis of atypical antiglomerular basement membrane disease. In our cohort, hematologic disorders were assessed and excluded as causative conditions, as detailed in our results and the accompanying table. Only indolent conditions, namely chronic lymphocytic leukemia and monoclonal gammopathy of undetermined significance, were identified. Importantly, though more severe atypical forms were presented, none of them had positive antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibodies. However, similar to our further investigation of case #1 in our series, antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibody-negative vasculitis secondary to autoantibodies that react against a single linear epitope of myeloperoxidase antigen has been described.3 This was possible through highly sensitive epitope excision and mass spectrometry techniques, underscoring the potential need for more exhaustive antibody characterization in selected cases of atypical anti–glomerular basement membrane disease.

Second, the diagnosis in our series was primarily driven by renal biopsy findings, which demonstrated the characteristic linear IgG staining pattern consistent with anti–glomerular basement membrane–mediated injury. As Ivkovic and Kronblicher2 appropriately highlight, alternative explanations for linear immunofluorescence, including a less intense pattern in diabetic nephropathy, light chain–related disease, and fibrillary glomerulonephritis, must be considered.2 In our cohort, diabetic nephropathy was unlikely for 2 patients with diabetes mellitus: one presented with a classic pulmonary-renal syndrome, whereas the other exhibited light chain restriction. However, DNAJB9 staining proved crucial in our series, because it allowed reclassification of misdiagnosed cases into fibrillary glomerulonephritis. This immunohistochemical marker, not available in earlier series,4,5 represents a valuable diagnostic tool in the contemporary evaluation of atypical cases.

Finally, though we agree that sometimes a more straightforward explanation may be appropriate, we believe that looking beyond an easier explanation can lead to a deeper understanding of diseases.

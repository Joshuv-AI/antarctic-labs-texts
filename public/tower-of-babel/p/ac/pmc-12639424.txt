Letter to the Editor Re: Analytical and clinical validation of PATHWAY HER2 (4B5) assay for assessment of HER2-low/HER2-ultralow status and eligibility for trastuzumab deruxtecan in DESTINY-Breast06
Authors: Y. Horimoto
Published: 2025

I read with great interest the article by Shami et al., who validated the Roche VENTANA PATHWAY anti-human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)/neu (4B5) assay and provided important data on the diagnostic framework for HER2-low and HER2-ultralow breast cancers.1

However, in routine pathology practice, the reliable identification and harmonisation of HER2-low and HER2-ultralow cases remain a significant challenge. Importantly, there is a background factor that deserves attention: in Japan in October 2021, and several years earlier in Western countries, Roche introduced a protocol modification in the BenchMark XT system, including a shorter incubation time for the primary antibody. This revision led to a marked reduction in staining intensity. In our clinical study, we revealed that more than half of the tumours previously scored as 1+ under the former protocol were reclassified as 0 under the current protocol.2 Complementary evidence was provided by Ushigusa et al.,3 who showed that, in many tumours diagnosed as ‘HER2-null’ using the current protocol, membrane staining could be detected when an enhanced protocol was applied. These findings collectively suggest that a substantial proportion of tumours now labelled as ‘ultralow’ would previously have been diagnosed as 1+. A system introduced as an improved diagnostic platform has paradoxically rendered assessment more difficult.

Since the current 4B5 protocol has already been established as the companion diagnostic for trastuzumab deruxtecan, any short-term revision is unrealistic. Therefore, efforts to strengthen quality control and inter-laboratory harmonisation remain essential. As a complementary approach, quantitative parameters such as the proportion of membrane-positive cells and the average staining intensity measured by digital image analysis may serve as useful reference information for pathologists when making their diagnoses. Digital pathology has been increasingly recognised as a useful tool to support biomarker assessment in this regard.4 By supporting greater objectivity and reproducibility, such information could also facilitate more consistent classification among cases currently labelled as 0. It could further help ensure appropriate adaptation should any diagnostic criteria be revised in the future and also provide valuable continuous data on HER2 expression for various research purposes. Further studies focusing on digital quantification rather than subjective visual assessment are strongly encouraged.

In summary, while the study by Shami et al. consolidates the diagnostic foundation of HER2-low and HER2-ultralow breast cancers, challenges remain in daily practice, and additional measures will be needed to ensure robust and reproducible diagnosis in this evolving therapeutic context.

Deep learning-based anomaly detection framework for hydraulic support systems in mining safety
Authors: Wei Xin, Longhe Liu, Jiyu Wang, Xigui Zheng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study proposes a novel deep learning framework for anomaly detection in coal mine hydraulic support pressure data. The framework innovatively combines bidirectional LSTM and CNN architectures, incorporating gated residual connections and self-attention mechanisms to effectively capture both temporal features and local patterns in pressure data. The research utilizes pressure data collected from 10 hydraulic supports on a fully mechanized mining face in western China, spanning from July 1st to July 30th, 2022. Through a systematic data preprocessing pipeline, including temporal resampling, missing value handling, normalization, and other steps, a standardized experimental dataset was constructed. Experimental results demonstrate the model’s excellent performance in anomaly detection tasks. Ablation studies validate the effectiveness of key components, with CNN layers and gated residual mechanisms contributing most significantly to model performance, leading to test loss increases of 472% and 352% respectively when removed. The model exhibits strong capabilities in sample reconstruction and anomaly identification, effectively distinguishing between normal pressure variations and anomalous patterns. However, the method has certain limitations, including dependency on data quality, fixed threshold strategy constraints, and computational complexity issues. These findings provide important references for improving hydraulic support monitoring systems and have practical significance for enhancing coal mine safety production.Keywords: Hydraulic support, Anomaly detection, Deep learning, Time series analysis, Pressure monitoringSubject terms: Computational science, Engineering, Fossil fuels

Introduction

Mining equipment safety monitoring has become increasingly critical in modern mining operations, particularly for hydraulic supports which serve as the primary safeguard against roof collapse in underground mines1. Recent studies have shown that intelligent monitoring and control of hydraulic supports not only ensures operational safety but also optimizes mining efficiency2. With the advancement of sensor technology, modern hydraulic supports are equipped with various monitoring devices that continuously collect pressure data, providing rich information about equipment operational states3. However, the complex and harsh mining environment poses significant challenges to data quality and anomaly detection accuracy4.

The detection of anomalies in hydraulic support systems has attracted considerable research attention due to its critical role in preventing equipment failures and ensuring mining safety. Traditional monitoring approaches often rely on threshold-based methods or simple statistical analysis, which struggle to capture the complex temporal patterns inherent in hydraulic support data5. Recent studies have demonstrated that mining pressure variations exhibit strong temporal-spatial correlations, necessitating more sophisticated analysis methods6. Furthermore, the multiscale nature of pressure fluctuations in hydraulic supports requires consideration of both short-term variations and long-term trends7.

Deep learning approaches have shown promising results in addressing these challenges. Mallak and Fathi8 implemented LSTM autoencoders for hydraulic machinery fault detection, demonstrating the effectiveness of deep learning in capturing temporal dependencies. Park et al.9 compared various algorithms including CNN and LSTM for anomaly detection in hydraulic systems, finding that deep learning models generally outperform traditional methods. However, existing approaches often focus on either local feature extraction or long-term dependency modeling, failing to effectively combine both aspects for comprehensive anomaly detection.

The integration of multiple neural architectures has emerged as a promising direction for enhancing anomaly detection performance10–12. While CNN-based methods excel at extracting local patterns and LSTM networks show strength in modeling temporal dependencies, the effective combination of these architectures remains a challenge3,6. Recent research has highlighted the importance of maintaining temporal coherence in time series analysis13, suggesting that simply concatenating different architectural components may not be optimal for capturing the complex dynamics of hydraulic support data.

Recent advances in hydraulic system monitoring have explored hybrid deep learning architectures. Kim and Jeong14 proposed a convolutional bidirectional LSTM with attention mechanism for hydraulic state monitoring, demonstrating the potential of combining CNN and LSTM with attention mechanisms. However, existing approaches often lack specialized mechanisms for handling the unique challenges of mining hydraulic support data, including irregular pressure patterns, sensor noise, and temporal discontinuities specific to underground mining environments. Moreover, these methods typically do not adequately address the critical need for temporal smoothness preservation in pressure signal reconstruction, which is essential for distinguishing between genuine anomalies and normal operational variations in mining contexts.

To address these limitations, this study proposes a novel deep learning framework that synergistically combines bidirectional LSTM and CNN architectures for hydraulic support anomaly detection. Our approach introduces several innovative components: (1) A bidirectional LSTM encoder that captures temporal dependencies in both forward and backward directions; (2) A CNN-based feature extraction mechanism enhanced with self-attention for identifying critical local patterns; (3) A gated residual connection design that facilitates effective information flow between different network components; and (4) A specialized loss function that combines reconstruction error with temporal smoothness constraints.

By incorporating both local and global temporal features, our model can effectively identify subtle anomalies while maintaining robustness against noise and measurement errors. The gated residual connections ensure efficient feature preservation throughout the network, addressing the information loss issues common in existing hybrid models15,16. Furthermore, our approach considers the practical challenges in mining environments highlighted by recent research17. The model’s ability to handle noisy and interrupted sensor data makes it particularly suitable for real-world mining applications. The temporal smoothness constraint in our loss function helps prevent false alarms while maintaining sensitivity to genuine anomalies, addressing a key concern in practical applications.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: “Proposed method” provides a detailed introduction to the proposed anomaly detection framework based on Bidirectional LSTM and CNN, including problem formulation, model architecture, loss function design, and anomaly detection strategies. “Dataset” describes the source of the dataset, the preprocessing procedures, and the analysis of its distribution characteristics. “Experiment” validates the effectiveness of the proposed method through ablation experiments, parameter settings, and experimental results. “Discussion” discusses the model’s performance, component contributions, practical significance, and limitations of the method.

Dataset

Data source

This study utilizes hydraulic support column pressure data from a fully mechanized mining face in western China as the research subject. The data collection period spans from July 1st to July 30th, 2022, focusing on 10 hydraulic supports (#10, #15, #25, #30, #35, #40, #45, #50, #55, and #60) along the working face. Each hydraulic support is equipped with two pressure sensors (Channel 1 and Channel 2) to monitor the stress state of the support columns. Data quality analysis reveals extensive data loss between July 10th and July 20th. Considering the crucial impact of data integrity on model training, this time period will be excluded from subsequent analysis. Comparative analysis of dual-channel pressure data across supports shows that Support #25 exhibits significantly higher pressure values in Channel 2 compared to Channel 1, while Supports #50 and #60 display the opposite characteristic, with Channel 1 pressure values notably higher than Channel 2. The remaining supports maintain relatively consistent pressure values between their two channels, demonstrating good measurement consistency.

Experimental methods

The experimental methods are designed to systematically evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed autoencoder model for anomaly detection in hydraulic support pressure data. Our experimental design follows three key principles: component contribution analysis, model behavior verification, and practical applicability validation.

To thoroughly investigate the contribution of each key component, we conduct ablation studies by systematically removing or replacing critical model components while maintaining the basic autoencoder structure. The first ablation experiment focuses on the gated residual connections, which are designed to adaptively control information flow between different processing stages. By removing these connections, we can quantify their impact on the model’s ability to preserve and utilize temporal information effectively.

The second experiment examines the role of the self-attention mechanism in capturing global dependencies within the pressure sequences. We replace the multi-head attention module with a simple feedforward layer, allowing us to evaluate whether the ability to model long-range dependencies significantly affects reconstruction quality. This comparison is particularly relevant for identifying complex temporal patterns in pressure data.

The third experiment investigates the hierarchical feature extraction capability of the convolutional layers in both encoder and decoder. We modify the model architecture by replacing the CNN layers with fully connected layers while maintaining the same parameter count. This modification helps us understand the importance of local temporal feature extraction in pressure sequence reconstruction.

The final ablation study evaluates the effectiveness of the temporal smoothness constraint in the loss function. By removing the smoothness term and training with only reconstruction loss, we can assess how this constraint influences the model’s ability to generate physically plausible pressure curves. This is crucial for maintaining the temporal consistency of the reconstructed sequences.

For each ablation experiment, we evaluate the model’s performance using three quantitative metrics: Mean Squared Error (MSE), Mean Absolute Error (MAE), and Peak Preservation Rate (PPR). MSE and MAE provide comprehensive measures of reconstruction accuracy, while PPR specifically evaluates the model’s ability to preserve critical pressure change points. This combination of metrics ensures a thorough assessment of both overall reconstruction quality and the preservation of domain-specific features.

Parameter settings

The model architecture was configured with an input dimension of 2 to process the pressure-related features, while the hidden dimension was set to 16 to maintain a balance between model capacity and computational efficiency. The latent space was designed with a dimension of 2 to achieve effective compression while preserving essential pressure pattern information. These hyperparameters were systematically optimized using Optuna22, a hyperparameter optimization framework that employs Tree-structured Parzen Estimator algorithm to efficiently search the hyperparameter space and identify optimal configurations.

The encoder employs a bidirectional LSTM with two layers to capture temporal dependencies in both forward and backward directions. The self-attention mechanism was implemented with 4 attention heads to model long-range dependencies effectively. The subsequent CNN layers utilize a kernel size of 4 and stride of 2, progressively reducing the sequence length by a factor of 8 while extracting hierarchical features. The decoder mirrors this architecture with transposed convolutions and a unidirectional LSTM, ensuring symmetric feature processing.

For model optimization, we employed the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 0.001 and trained the model for 100 epochs. The loss function combines mean squared error (MSE) for reconstruction accuracy and a temporal smoothness constraint weighted at 0.1 to maintain temporal consistency in the reconstructed sequences. Each input sequence consists of 288 timestamps, corresponding to 24 h of pressure measurements at 5-minute intervals.

Discussion

Model performance and component contributions

The ablation studies quantitatively demonstrate the hierarchical importance of architectural components in our framework. CNN layers contribute most significantly to model performance, with their removal causing a 472% increase in test loss (0.004168 to 0.023850). This aligns with CNN’s inherent advantage in capturing local temporal patterns within pressure sequences. The gated residual mechanism ranks second in importance, its removal leading to a 352% increase in test loss and notably higher error variance (σ = 0.030640). This validates our design hypothesis that maintaining feature preservation through gating is crucial for stable reconstruction.

The self-attention mechanism and temporal smoothness constraint show modest but measurable impacts (6.2% and 5.7% test loss increases respectively). While less critical than CNN and gating components, they contribute to model stability and generalization, particularly in handling diverse pressure patterns. The convergence analysis reveals efficient training characteristics, with all variants achieving stability within 40 epochs.

Analysis of Samples 3, 4, and 10 reveals the model’s effectiveness in distinguishing between normal variations and genuine anomalies. However, several limitations persist: the fixed threshold (0.8) may not optimally adapt to varying operational conditions, and the full architecture’s computational complexity could challenge real-time monitoring requirements. The model’s performance also shows sensitivity to data quality and completeness, suggesting potential improvements through robust training strategies.

Practical significance

The proposed framework enhances hydraulic support monitoring through effective anomaly detection and data cleaning. The preprocessing workflow converts raw pressure measurements into structured samples through 5-min interval resampling, with each sample containing 144 consecutive timestamps. The model generates reconstructed sequences and identifies anomalies by computing reconstruction errors and probability scores. Data quality improves through anomaly removal and gap filling via various interpolation techniques.

The refined data enables robust downstream analysis, including support operation assessment (initial force adequacy, system efficiency, and pillar stress balance) and surrounding rock pressure pattern prediction. This advancement facilitates more reliable monitoring and decision-making in mining operations.

Conclusion

Our deep learning framework demonstrates effective anomaly detection in hydraulic support pressure data, achieving a test loss of 0.004168 with consistent reconstruction quality. The quantitative analysis validates the synergistic benefits of combining CNN-based feature extraction, gated residual connections, and temporal constraints. The framework successfully identifies both gradual and sudden pressure anomalies while maintaining robustness against normal fluctuations.

Future work should focus on developing adaptive thresholding mechanisms, optimizing computational efficiency for real-time applications, and exploring transfer learning to enhance cross-support generalization. Specifically, transfer learning techniques such as dual-perspective hierarchical adversarial alignment could be investigated to improve model generalization across different hydraulic supports and varying working conditions. This would address the current limitation of requiring substantial training data for each new deployment scenario. These improvements would address current limitations while maintaining the framework’s core strengths in temporal pattern recognition and anomaly detection.

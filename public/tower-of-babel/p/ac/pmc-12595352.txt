Dual strengthen of texture and salty perception for reconstructed duck ham via myofibrillar protein-gum Arabic complex
Authors: Henan Wei, Xiaoqun Zeng, Yangying Sun, Changyu Zhou, Qiang Xia, Zhen Wu, Hongbing Yan, Yangyang Hu, Daodong Pan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractReducing salt in reconstructed duck ham while maintaining its texture is still challenging. This study developed a novel salt-reducing strategy using lyophilized emulsion complexes of myofibrillar protein (MP) and gum Arabic (GA) (4:0, 3:1, 2:2, 1:3 and 0:4) with TGase as control. Spectroscopic analysis revealed that MP-GA complexation enhanced the protein unfolding, particularly the balanced protein/polysaccharide ratio at 3:1 (M3G1). M3G1 incorporation favored the MP association and meat integration macroscopically, increasing water holding capacity by 8.02 % and gel strength by 143.3 % versus control. Sensory and electronic tongue analysis confirmed M3G1 maximized saltiness perception score (8.2) than other treated groups (7.4–7.8) and the control (5.4) through heterogeneous sodium distribution. Sodium release kinetics and porcine tongue perception further reflected that M3G1 rendered prolong Na+ releasing out for saltiness enhancement. Therefore, MP-GA complexes with manipulated ratio (especially M3G1) can simultaneously enhance texture and saltiness perception, offering a promising alternative for low-sodium duck ham.Keywords: dual enhancements, MP-GA complex, reconstructed ham, salt, meat integration

Introduction

The processing of duck meat, using the whole meat or via its comminuted form, generally transforms the flesh into products with multidimensional qualities that better fit the consumer preferences (Park, Kim, Kang, Kim, & Choi, 2025), as are seen for duck after drying, braising and duck-based sausages (Chandimali et al., 2024). The reconstructed duck meat exhibits characteristic flavors, textures as well as overall favorable appearance primarily resulting from the interaction and integration of the individual part of the meat into a whole (Cao et al., 2022; Chang et al., 2023). For duck meat ham, its reconstruction takes also advantages of varied processing matrixes and additives that display multiple functional attributes to render for fine acceptance (Lorenzo, Temperán, Bermúdez, Purriños, & Franco, 2011). Sodium chloride (NaCl) is the most predominated ingredient that facilitates the dissolution, unfolding and intermolecular association of meat myofibrillar protein (MP) for meat integration (Gomes, Kato, Carvalho, Almeida, & Conte-Junior, 2021). Besides, commercial processing agents (e.g., gelators, thickeners and hydrocolloids) with synergistic addition bring with improved ham quality due to their contributive roles for further MP polymerization and gelling via both covalent and non-covalent (van del Waals, hydrophobic interaction) associations (Busch, Yong, & Goh, 2013; Kameník, Saláková, Vyskočilová, Pechová, & Haruštiaková, 2017). Despite that, the appropriate incorporation of NaCl (usually 4 % to 6 %) is a key prerequisite to process ham with desire acceptance, since its massive usage linked to matrix hardening, weakened color and flavor while also elevated risks including hypertension and cardiovascular disease, but mere reduction for sodium inevitably impairs ham cohesion and quality (Horita, Messias, Morgano, Hayakawa, & Pollonio, 2014). These developments necessitate the innovative incorporation of NaCl that brings both gelling character for duck meat integration and salt reduction from health perspective.

Recently, the manipulations of salt distribution has been massively explored to yield meat products with reduced salt usage while fortified saltiness, despite its rare exploitation upon reconstituted duck ham (Lee, Bae, Jeong, Cho, & Choi, 2020). This includes biopolymers incorporation, either used alone or synergistically, in favoring NaCl retention and heterogeneous distribution through initially their structural reshaping (Li, Han, Wan, & Yang, 2020; Liu et al., 2024). Examples upon semi-solid and solid products reflect also the deep correlation between pronounced salty perception and the incorporation of Gum Arabic (GA), pectin, arabinoxylans and citrus fiber due to their high efficacy for hydration and oral muco-adhesion facilitation (X. Chen, Qiu, Chen, Li, & Liang, 2020; Luo et al., 2025). Confusions arise, however, in the manufacturing of minced meat products when incorporating these polysaccharide-dominated polymers. For instance, the presence of polysaccharide may hinder the protein interaction through its steric filling that interferes with meat integration (Zhuang, Wang, Jiang, Chen, & Zhou, 2021). Meanwhile, reduced saltiness was monitored for these polysaccharide filled meat products, as a result of its viscous and thinning effects of these polymers by their bulky addition and complicated phase behaviors (X. Chen et al., 2020). Complexation with proteins, for instance, soy protein isolate (SPI), whey protein isolate (WPI) and casein, provides the solution to boost saltiness of polysaccharide polymers by enhancing both structural extension and NaCl perception (Giles et al., 2025). It was found that proteins strengthens the NaCl mucus-penetration and oral perception (Li, Wan, & Yang, 2019) since the complexation favors the heterogeneous distribution of NaCl. Nakagawa et al. (2021) also pointed out that these complexes exhibit increased salt incorporation and organoleptic scoring in bread and Pizza through primarily their stranded conformation. However, these studies focus mainly salty increment while merely upon textural homogeneity of meat products, especially for the reconstructed product of duck ham.

MP, being the dominant tissue protein from poultry and livestock, provides evident efficiencies for both polysaccharide complexation and structure remodeling through various modes of association (Gao, Guo, Ye, Ahmad, & Zhu, 2024; Kameník et al., 2017). Notably, MP could homogeneously associate with meat proteins from duck ham, which could further facilitate meat integration of this reconstructed product than those featured globulin or orderless, e.g., complexes using WPI, SPI, etc. (Monteiro, Nakagawa, Pimentel, & Sousa, 2021; Thorarinsdottir, Arason, Sigurgisladottir, Valsdottir, & Tornberg, 2011). Meanwhile, structural extension induced by MP and polysaccharide complexation would tune salt distribution with localized enrichment (Luo et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2023), despite the unknown of detailed mechanism for salty perception using these new complexes. In other words, renewed saltiness enhancer could be obtained throughout structural design principle after MP and polysaccharide complexation, which meanwhile regulates textural attributes of duck ham by homogenous protein-protein gelling and meat integration. However, there is scarce exploitation in low-salt duck ham with enhanced salt perception when these MP-polysaccharide complexes are introduced, it is unclear whether the observed effects on texture and salt perception arise from their structural modifications. Specifically, how these complexes effectuate saltiness enhancement based upon salt maldistribution as well as to improve the textural and organoleptic attributes of the ham remain unknown.

Being the representative polysaccharide with stranded structure and sufficient binding groups (e.g., -OH) (Li et al., 2019), GA is introduced to complex with MP at different MP to GA ratios (4:0, 3:1, 2:2, 1:3 and 0:4). These complexes are purposely obtained after lyophilization in order to remove the interfering and thinning effects (Nyaguthii, Omwamba, & Nduko, 2023) upon macroscopic texture homogeneity improvement when they were introduced in aqueous form. The aim of this study is thus to explore the efficiency and mechanism of elevated saltiness of the low-salt reconstructed ham and its integrated textual homogeneity using these MP-GA complexes. Sensory evaluation and electronic tongue analysis are used to directly differentiate the saltiness of these complexes fortified hams; release kinetics of sodium and visualization of its distribution are further monitored to give insights into the mechanism for saltiness flavor enhancement from MP-GA complexes. This study provides new alternatives for renewing low-salt agents that collaboratively improve organoleptic and textural attributes of reconstructed duck meat products, as well as the possibility for manufacturing sodium-reduced ham recipes without compromising sensory quality or integrity with scalability.

Materials and methods

Materials

Fresh breast meat (300 ± 10 g) from Beijing Muscovy duck was provided by Huaying agricultural development Co., ltd (Xinyang, China). Pig backfat was purchased from Aisen Meat Food Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Pig tongue for saltiness evaluation was bought from Run-Tai mart grocery (Ningbo, China). GA and main reagents including sodium fluorescence, potassium bromide (KBr), glutaraldehyde was bought from Aladdin biochemical technology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China) with at least analytical grade.

MP extraction

The extraction of myofibrillar protein is based on Chen et al. (2020) with minor modifications. 25 g meat was minced, mixed with EDTA-Tris buffer (pH 8.3) at a volume ratio of 1:4, homogenized (Scientz, XHF-DY, Ningbo, China) at 10000 rpm for 3 min and then centrifuged (Eppendorf, 5804R, Hamburg, German) at 5000g for 20 min. The precipitate was then dissolved in SSS buffer at volume ratio of 1:4 and centrifugation (4000 g, 10 min), followed by pellet re-dissolving using KCl (0.1 mol/L) fortified extracting agent (Triton X100: 99 % SSS = 1:99), the term of SSS represented mixed solution containing 0.1 mol/L KCl, 20 mmol/L K2HPO4/KH2PO4, 2 mmol/L MgCl2, 1 mmol/L EGTA at pH 7.0. After impurities removal using filter, the suspension was then dispersed in distilled water and the pellet was collected after sufficient centrifugation (5000 g, 20 min). The protein sample was stored at 4 °C for no more than 3 days and was used for MP-GA complex-based emulsion fabrication.

Preparation and characterization of salt-reducing gelling agent

Preparation of MP-GA complex

The salt-reducing gelling agent was made by lyophilization of MP-GA complex-based emulsion (Giles et al., 2025). In brief, for MP-GA complexation, the protein and polysaccharide solutions were separately made by their dispersing into 2 % NaCl solution, and was then mixed in different w:w ratio of 4:0, 3:1, 2:2, 1:3, and 0:4 for 4 h at 25 °C to favor the MP and GA complexation. The pH of the mixture was maintained at 8.3 using 1 mol/L HCl or NaOH, if necessary. These groups were termed as M4G0, M3G1, M2G2, M1G3 and M0G4, respectively, the MxGy indicated the relative ratio of MP and GA within protein-polysaccharide complex, for instance, M3G1 was the salt-reducing gelling agent containing a 3:1 ratio of MP to GA, while M4G0 and M0G4 indicated the scenarios for sole MP and sole GA without complexation.

The MP-GA complex based emulsion was made through classic high-speed homogenization using Scientz XHF-DY at 10000 rpm for 3 min, by mixing the MP-GA complex solution and lemon essential oil at volume ratio of 4:1 (Liu et al., 2024). The lemon essential oil was used here due to its massive application and that it is easily evaporated during emulsion lyophilization to ensure the powder of MP-GA gelling agent could be obtained with sufficiency. The emulsions were characterized by size distribution and charging character (Malven, zetasizer 90, UK) after 50 times dilution (Cao et al., 2022). The rheology was also determined using rheometer (TA, DHR-2, USA) at both shear mode and frequency mode. Macroscopic stability of emulsions using creaming index and real image was also monitored (X. Chen et al., 2020). The emulsion was then lyophilized (ALPHA 1–4 LD plus, CHRIST, Germany) for 24 h with essential oil evaporated. The final NaCl concentration in all MP-GA complexes was in the range of 1.89 ± 0.21 g/100 g meat to 2.11 ± 0.10 g/100 g meat, showing the sufficient carriage of the salt after MP-GA complexation (Fig. 5C). The powder for MP-GA complex showed little variation upon structural properties against its non-lyophilization counterpart regarding the anti-lyophilization effects from the incorporators including oil phase and sodium chloride, it was then collected and stored at −20 °C for further use.

Differential scanning calorimetry (DSC)

The thermal denaturation of MP-GA complex was determined by simultaneous thermal analyzer (DSC3500, NETZSCH, Germen) based on Gao et al. (2024). In brief, 5 mg of lyophilized powder of MP-GA complex was precisely weighted, transferred to the aluminum cell and then put into the DSC clip. The measurement was done by scanning the sample at 25–200 °C once it was thermal balanced at 25 °C for 10 min, the heating rate was set at 5 °C/min, both the thermal curve and denaturation enthalpy were reported.

Sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE)

SDS-PAGE at range 10–250 kD was used to analyze molecular profile of MP-GA according to the previous method (Chang et al., 2023). 8 μL sample and the protein marker were mixed and added into the system containing 5 % staking gel and 12 % resolving gel. The electrophoresis was done at 100 V for 60 min once the protein migrated into the resolving gel. Afterwards, 0.25 % Coomassie blue (R-250) and 7.4 % acetic acid were applied for stain and de-stain the gel, respectively.

Surface hydrophobicity (H0)

The H0 of MP-GA complex was determined by mixing 1 mL of the complex solution (5 mg/mL) with 200 μL of bromophenol blue (1 mg/mL), vortexed (400 rpm, 10 min) and centrifuged (8000 g, 10 min) at 4 °C (Zhou, Hu, & Wang, 2022). After the supernatant was 10 times diluted using PBS, the absorption value was measured at 595 nm using microplate reader (TECAN, 200 Pro, Swiss) with the H0 measured as follows.

Secondary structure profile

According to (Jiang, Yang, & Li, 2025), MP-GA powder after lyophilization was mixed with KBr (1:100), ground and pressed into a 13 mm diameter disk. It was then scanned using Fourier Transform Infrared (FTIR) spectrometer (iS20, Thermo Co., Massachusetts, USA). The wavenumber range was 4000–400 cm−1, the scanning repeat and resolution were 32 and 2 cm−1, respectively. The secondary structure profile for MP-GA complexes was also determined using circular dichroism (CD) spectrometer (J-1500, JASCO, Japan), by scanning the solution (0.2 mg/mL) at 190–260 nm with a 1.0 mm path length quartz cuvette. For its secondary structure profile, the spectra of MP-GA were analyzed using PeakFit software, and the constitution of protein secondary structure was represented as α-helix, β-sheet, β-turn and random coil.

Intrinsic fluorescence intensity

The fluorescence spectra of MP-GA complex in diluted sample (0.1 mg/mL) were recorded by monitoring the emission wavelength ranging from 300 to 400 nm at excitation wavelength of 290 nm, following the previous method (Hu, Qiu, Sun, Xiong, & Ogra, 2019). Measurements were conducted using a microplate reader (200 Pro, TECAN, Swiss).

Production of reconstructed ham

The restructured ham was made by mixing the meat and pig backfat at mass ratio of 1:4 after respective mince and homogenization at 4 °C. It was then pickled using composite substances (0.1 % composite phosphate, 0.01 % nitrite, 1 % β-cyclodextrin, 0.3 % spice powder, 3 % white sugar) for 12 h at 4 °C according to (Martín-Miguélez, Souza Olegario, González-Mohino, Ventanas, & Delgado, 2024). The salt-reducing gelling agents, with their salt content uniformed at 2.67 %, were then added for meat matrix integration, with the groups terming also as M4G0, M3G1, M2G2, M1G3 and M0G4, respectively (Table 1). The control was the scenario with TGase incorporation and its salt content being 4 % while without any salt-reducing gelling agent. It was then reconstituted in aluminum mold at 4 °C for 8 h, and dried at 15 °C for 4 d at 63 % RH (Hozel, YCFZD-2, Hangzhou, China).

Characterization of reconstructed ham

Color

The color of reconstructed ham was measured according to the previous study (Zaremba, Grabowicz, & Biesek, 2024). After the surface was removed, values for L∗ (brightness), a∗ (redness), and b∗ (yellowness) were obtained using a spectrophotometric colorimeter (Konica, CR-400, Japan). The colorimeter was pre-calibrated with a white standard plate (L∗ of 94.07, a∗ of 0.17 and b⁎ of 0.24). Three parallel measurements are taken at different locations to calculate the average value.

Texture

According to the method of Dong et al. (2024), the ham was cut into cube (2 × 2 × 2 cm) after its surface removal, the texture parameters were measured using texture analyzer (TA. Xt. Plus Stable Micro System, UK) equipped with a cylinder probe (P/50). The working mode was set by downward pressure distance of 7 mm, while the pretest speed, test speed, and post-test speed were 5 mm s−1, 1 mm s−1 and 1 mm s−1, respectively. The puncture test was also done using the abovementioned texture analyzer according to the method of Yuan et al. (2024) with slight modifications. The analysis was performed using a P/0.5 probe following same testing speed to the texture profile, while the compression shape and trigger force were 50 % and 5 g, respectively. The gel strength was expressed as rupture strength × rupture distance.

Water holding capacity (WHC)

The WHC of ham was determined through centrifugation (Cao et al., 2022). In brief, 5 g ham (m0) was placed in 50 mL centrifuge tube and centrifuged at 10000 g for 15 min at 4 °C. The weights for the ham plus tube were measured and termed as m1 for pre-centrifugation and m2 for post-centrifugation, respectively. WHC was measure using the equation as follows.

Water distribution profile

Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the hams was conducted according to the method of Xu et al. (2022) using a magnetic resonance imager (NMI20-060HeI, Niumag Electric Co., Shanghai, China). Proton density images were obtained using multiple spin-echo sequences with TR = 500 ms and TE = 20 ms. The Image Evaluation software (Niumag Electric Co., Shanghai, China) was used to analyze the grayscale intensity values of the images and generate pseudo-color maps to visualize the water distribution. Four relaxation times (T2b, T21, T22, and T23) obtained with the multi-exponential model and their corresponding peak area proportions (P2b, P21, P22, and P23) were used to reflect the fluidity and distribution of water molecules in meat products.

Intermolecular interaction mode

Chemical interaction forces including electrostatic interactions, hydrophobic interactions, hydrogen bonds, and disulfide bonds were evaluated through MP solubility variation against.

different denature reagents (Du et al., 2022). In brief, MP solution (5 mg/mL) was separately mixed with five different solvents of S1 (0.05 mol/L NaCl), S2 (0.6 mol/L NaCl), S3 (0.6 mol/L NaCl plus 1.5 mol/L urea), S4 (0.6 mol/L NaCl plus 8 mol/L urea), and S5 (0.6 mol/L NaCl plus 8 mol/L urea and 0.5 mol/L β-mercaptoethanol). After vortex (500 rpm, 10 min), the mixture was centrifuged at 8000 g for 20 min using 5804R machine, and the solubility was measured using Detergent Compatible Bradford Protein Assay Kit (Solarbio Company, Beijing, China). The solubility difference between two adjacent solvents was used to reflect the intermolecular interaction mode within the ham.

Microstructure

The microstructure of the ham was imaged using a scanning electron microscope (Hitachi S-3400, Japan) under 150× magnification (Dong et al., 2024). In brief, the lyophilized sample was sequentially dehydrated using gradient ethanol (60 % to 100 %), fixed on the conductive adhesive and gold coated, followed by observation using the machine.

Sensory evaluation of reconstructed ham

The sensory evaluation was conducted following the previous procedure (Moser, Lepage, Pineau, Fillion, & Rytz, 2018) with some modifications. 20 panelists (10 males and 10 females, aged 21–25) with no olfactory/gustatory impairments or allergies were firstly trained to identify six sensory attributes (salty taste, fresh flavor, astringency, bitter taste, texture, and overall acceptance) using a quantitative descriptive analysis (QDA) scale (from 1 to 9). That was, scoring 7–9 indicated mild bitter and astringency while strong fresh flavor, texture, salty taste and overall acceptance; 4–6 indicated fair bitter and astringency as well as fresh flavor, texture, salty taste and overall acceptance; and that 1–3 indicated strong bitter and astringency while mild fresh flavor, texture, salty taste and overall acceptance. Finally, 10 trained panelists with 1:1 male to female ratio were selected after the blind evaluation. For the actual sensory evaluation, samples of reconstructed ham (3 × 3 × 3 cm) were anonymized with three-digit codes, each sample was assessed twice by all panelists (24-h interval between sessions), yielding 20 replicates per sample to ensure reliability. Data were analyzed via one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) (p < 0.05) for statistical significance.

Especially, the sensory evaluation protocol was reviewed and approved by the Ningbo University Institutional Review Board. Participants provided written informed consent after being informed of the study's purpose, procedures, and that samples were safe for consumption (no allergens or contaminants beyond standard food levels). No compensation was provided, and data were anonymized. The study posed minimal risk, as it involved tasting small portions of low-sodium duck ham prepared under food safety standards (GB 14881–2013).

Electronic tongue

The salty taste of ham samples was qualitatively determined using electronic tongue (smartongue, Isenso, shanghai, China) by using gradient NaCl solution (1 %, 3 %, 5 %) as standard (Lee et al., 2020). In brief, the ham sample was mixed with distilled water (1:50), followed by homogenization (10,000 rpm, 2 min), water bathing (40 °C, 30 min) and centrifugation (7000 g, 10 min) to get the salty supernatant. The probe was prewashed using continuous distilled water for 120 s, and then inserted into the sample solution and measured for 180 s, the sample data was collected every 1 s.

Spatial distribution of NaCl

The spatial distribution of NaCl within salt-reduced gelling agent was imaged following the method of (Li et al., 2020) with slight modifications. The salt-reducing gelling agent was evenly mixed with sodium fluorescein at mass ratio of 1000:1 and added into the meat matrix to form reconstructed ham following the same procedure in section 2.4. The fluorescence labeled ham was then sliced into pieces with thickness of 0.5 mm, and was then placed onto the slide. The excitation wavelength for imaging was 405 nm and the observation resolution was 200 × .

Kinetics of sodium release from reconstructed ham to the aqueous phase

The determination of sodium release kinetics referred to a previous method (Boisard et al., 2013). In brief, the reconstructed ham (20 g) was sufficiently minced and put into a glass vessel containing 1 L Milli Q water. Its release was conducted under constant stirring at 100 rpm and 25 °C. The aqueous phase was periodically collected (every 10 s, 130 s in total) and was mixed with composite digesta solution (nitric acid and perchloric acid at volume ratio of 8:1) at mass: volume ratio of 1:40, which was digested for 12 h to make it colorless. Atomic absorption spectrometer (ZEEnit700, Analytik Jena, Germany) was used for Na concentration measurement, by setting the wavelength of 766.5 nm and slit of 1.2 nm. The gas flow rate was 50 L/h, and the liquid suction rate was 5.0 mL/min. The sodium release curves were obtained with the sodium concentrations of these samples.

Sodium retention in porcine tongue in vitro

Mucosal retention of sodium ion was semi quantified (Li et al., 2019) by spreading the similar sample solution in section 2.7 onto pig tongue. To observe sodium retention, the pig tongue was then tilted 45°, and the artificial saliva was precisely pumped out to wash the sample solution at 6 mL/min. After repeated washing, the tongue sample was sliced with thickness of 1 mm, and imaged using an inverted fluorescence microscope (Leica, DM IL, German). The image was then analyzed using Image J to quantify the fluorescence intensity.

Statistical analysis

All data was expressed as mean ± standard deviation (n = 3) after at least three times repeat of the experiment. Statistical analysis was performed by ANOVA procedure (Duncan's test) of SPSS 27, the data was plotted using origin 2018.

Results and discussion

Emulsion stability and structural characteristics of MP-GA complex

The MP-GA complex was formed using emulsion lyophilization method, and evaluations upon emulsion properties showed that GA provided positive role for MP emulsion stability at lower proportion (for M3G1 and M2G2) while negative role with excessive ratio (for M1G3 and M0G4). In detail, GA modulated the emulsion stability through both steric and electrostatic stabilizations interference regarding charging character (Fig. 1A), CI (Fig. 1B) and size dimension (Fig. 1C). Among them, a premix of MP and GA at 3:1 (M3G1) displayed the most left shifted size profile and CI in macroscopic. This may be attributed to originally the less dominant role of GA for interfering with droplet stabilization though bridging flocculation, as were in the scenarios for M2G2, M1G3, and especially for M0G4. The different rheological behaviors for these emulsions, with the group using M3G1 giving the highest frequency-led G' to G" dominance (Fig. 1D) while strengthened shear feasibility (Fig. 1E), further suggested that with appropriate ratio of 3:1, the emulsion kept more kinetic stability. While with its larger proportion for M2G2, M1G3 and M0G4, GA may hinder the droplet dispersion and emulsion stability with possible droplet association against dynamic rheology (Xu et al., 2022).

The MP-GA complex gave a white appearance (Fig. 2A) due to the colorless features of MP and GA, suggesting that it would not interfere with the appearance of reconstructed ham. The conformation of the complex was then evaluated using FTIR, surface hydrophobicity and fluorescence profile, results showed that both the secondary and tertiary structures of MP were significantly altered due to these heterogeneous association (Hu et al., 2019). Result from DSC showed that these MP-GA complexes displayed reduced peaked for enthalpy (Fig. 2B), which was a sign of favored molecular collapse of the protein at the GA presence. This revealed directly the MP-GA complexation which led to the redistributed interactive modes (Fig. S1) within MP as well as its enhanced structural extension. For instance, it was shown that the GA presence caused appearance of new bands (seen in blue box from Fig. S1). This was especially the case with molecular weight (MW) within 25–35 kD and those less than 11 kD, showing obviously the chain redistribution of MP by possible protein breakage at GA integration. The presence of band hardening at both 17 kD and those larger than 35 kD (seen in red box) also indicated this that GA induces re-profiled MP chains throughout structural extension. Meanwhile, the complexes with GA gave even pronounced Amide I (Fig. 2C) with higher β-sheet while less α-helix (also revealed from Fig. 2D) than the sole MP, suggesting the elevated MP structural flexibility with GA incorporation (X. Chen et al., 2020). The intensity and peak site variations among these groups, for instance, -OH exposure (3248 cm−1), C—H stretching (2922 cm−1), amide I (1700–1600 cm−1) and C-O-C vibration (1150 cm−1), could also reflect the presence of GA in reshaping the MP conformation. The M3G1 with the highest β-sheet/α-helix ratio showed that there was most sufficient chain unfolding of MP with least intramolecular constraints, possibly by the redistribution of MP intermolecular force, e.g., the transition of intramolecular to intermolecular hydrogen bonding following GA complexation. This was also validated from the circular dichroism spectroscopy result that the GA presence rendered elevated structural extension of MP by reformulating the intermolecular force profile, therefore increasing the β-sheet/α-helix ratio of the protein (from 45.57 % to 52.93–58.77 %) (Fig. 2D). Surface hydrophobicity result showed that, among all GA-complexed groups, the M3G1 gave also the largest fluorescence retention other groups with GA. This showed again the improved MP chain flexibility with pronounced structural unfolding through assembly, as was also observed from intrinsic fluorescence spectrum (Fig. 2E and F).

General characteristics of reconstructed ham

In this study, the duck meat matrix was firstly reconstructed via MP-GA complexes and the textural attributes of the ham were superior than the condition with commercial gelling ingredients, e.g., TGase. Compared with the control using TGase, the reconstructed hams with MP-GA complex gave evidently improved appearance with compactness (Fig. 3A). This was due to both the heterogeneous incorporation of MP and the salt carrying effect (seen later in Fig. 5) of MP-GA complex that favored the protein associative interaction. Meanwhile, the less observable surface fat would be another indication of improved meat assembling for these MP-GA complex fortified groups, due to primarily its high sufficiency for oil emulsifying (Fig. 1). The cross section of ham gave similar phenomena that MP-GA complexes rendered further cross-linked meat matrix with few fractals or pores by salt retention, especially for M3G1, implying basically the potential of MP-GA complexes for meat integration with least breakage.

The color profile of these reconstructed hams showed that the incorporation of MP-GA complexes rendered generally reduced L⁎ and enhanced a⁎ while b⁎ was almost indifferent compared with the control for pristine ham (Table 2). These variations in L⁎ and a⁎ may be due to facilitated oil droplets dispersion and encapsulation by MP-GA emulsifying (Fig. 1) that mitigated the ham lightness; and interactive association between matrix protein and MP-GA complex could be expected since the salt induced MP unfolding, which led to protein extension and myoglobin exposure with increased redness ultimately (Hu et al., 2019). This showed also that M3G1 owned the highest efficacy for matrix integration by robust oil entrapment as well as interactive polymerization of proteins, as was also revealed from the visual image (Fig. 3A).

Taken in further observation, the texture profile (Table 2) implied also the favored meat assembly for the MP-GA complex incorporated groups due to their higher gel strength, hardness, gumminess, resilience, cohesiveness, springiness and chewiness than either the sole treated groups or the native ham. This was especially the case of M3G1 for which these characteristic texture parameters were the largest in the group except for adhesiveness and gumminess, showing more directly the enhanced meat cohesion and rigidity of reconstructed ham to resist deformation with M3G1 incorporation. It was notable that the increasing ratios of GA for the groups, e.g., M2G2 and M1G3, gave otherwise reduced rigidity and inherent strength that maybe due to primarily high viscous like character of GA (Li et al., 2019). The excessive introduction of GA, by weakening MP structure in retaining salt, could be an alternative reason for hindered protein association and then ham macroscopic texture and gel strength. These results necessitated again the interfering role of excessive GA for ham fabrication; however, its addition with appropriate amount may otherwise favor the formation of MP based gelator in conducing to overall integrated appearance of reconstructed ham with desired texture.

WHC and water distribution of reconstructed ham

Addition of MP-GA complex also increased WHC of reconstructed ham (Table 2) following the similar way of ham structuring that was shown from texture analysis. In detail, there were significantly higher values of WHC for groups with MP-GA complex than the control (79.44 ± 1.30 %). And the increment of ham WHC reached highest for the group of M3G1 (85.81 ± 1.55 %), suggesting the utmost sufficiency for water retention using MP-GA complex at optimum ratio of 3:1 (elevation by 8.02 %). This indicated also the binding role of the complex with ham protein in structuring the meat matrix and conducing to then the water immobilization (Cao et al., 2022). Meanwhile, the water fixing and interacting roles from the polysaccharide of GA cannot be neglected, since with its generic structure and strong effect of steric hindrance, these factors also contributed to the WHC enhancement as were also found previously (Nyaguthii et al., 2023). Bearing sufficient hydroxyl and carboxyl groups, the presence of GA could bind water molecules that favor their immobilization and possibly WHC increment (Jarić et al., 2026). Conversely, the groups of M2G2 and M1G3 demonstrated slightly reduced WHC values (83.05 ± 0.81 % and 81.35 ± 1.38 %, respectively) than that for M3G1. This showed the weaker water structuring and enclosure possibly due to their restrained unfolding of structures (Fig. 2B-F) that were less sufficient in forming interactions with inherent proteins within the ham. In alignment with WHC, LF-NMR observation (Fig. 3C-E) showed also pronounced water retention with free water to immobilization water transition, this echoed with the proton density image that more water molecules were entrapped within protein structure of reconstructed ham, especially for M3G1. Besides, the presence of GA would restrain the water release through both its hindrance effect and electrostatic interaction, e.g., via hydrogen bond (Choudhary et al., 2025).

These phenomena, when taken together, were indicative of the close relationship between macroscopic ham quality with water retention and microscopic salt retention for protein unfolding facilitation. Notably, their comparatively higher ratios of GA could also play a role in the matrix weakening via rheological viscous and thinning effects and then the pronounced water release, for instance, against centrifugation. This could be more obviously seen from the WHC result for the group of M0G4 that exhibited the lowest value, which indicated again the benefiting role of MP gelator with GA complexation for water entrapment and matrix structuring of reconstructed ham, especially in the ratio of 3:1.

Structural characteristics of reconstructed ham

The surface hydrophobicity and profile of intermolecular interaction of ham were also measured (Fig. 4A and B) and results showed that the incorporation of MP-GA complex gelator conduced to higher protein H0 as well as more strengthened interactive forces among ham matrix. There was highest H0 for M3G1 that implied the largest interactive feasibility and then the gelation facilitation when incorporated, which was accorded with the result of texture, gel strength and WHC (Table 2 and Fig. 3B). The hydrophobic interaction functioned the most significant manner for matrix interaction profile and then shaped the macroscopic appearance of the hams, and that the group of M3G1 peaked for the protein-protein hydrophobic association within the ham, which is in line with the result of protein H0 within the ham. Following this, disulfide bonding also played a notable role in gel strength within reconstructed hams, this kind of interactive pattern indicated the pronounced protein-protein association during meat product processing and characteristic quality formation (Thorarinsdottir et al., 2011). It was noted that the interactive mode measurement was based upon the solubility variance against the application of various denaturation agents, which necessitated the usage of multi-characterization tools to provide further insights into this. Despite that, these results suggested that the MP-GA complex could behave as ham modular that promoted the exposure of hydrophobic and sulfhydral groups and then the intermolecular association for matrix proteins, for instance, via hydrophobic and disulfide interaction. Especially, the incorporation of M3G1, with itself displaying highest structural unfolding and salt carriage, induced most strengthened conformational conversion of matrix protein with tenser exposed interactive moiety, which led to largest MP association and also the maximized matrix tightness in macroscopic (Martín-Miguélez et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2022).

This suggested an enhanced alignment and mutual interaction of protein chains that contributed to a more cohesive and resilient gel matrix (Table 2), as was also indicated from the visual appearance of the ham (Fig. 3A).

In fact, the SEM images for reconstructed hams gave similar phenomena that the incorporated MP-GA complex displayed tenser matrix association (Fig. 4E). There was growing compactness and network density of the hams from the control to the MP-GA complex gelator groups with the trend being M3G1 > M4G0 > M2G2 > M1G3 > M0G4. The evidently more compact microstructure with M3G1 implied the most sufficient role of the gelator in cohering the matrix proteins microscopically (Fig. 4F), which was also in line the texture and overall appearance of the ham (Table 2 and Fig. 3A). The images for the rest groups with comparatively feeble protein intermingle may be traced back to their lower self unfolding and then inferior interactive sufficiency (Khalid & Elokle, 2025; Nyaguthii et al., 2023), which yielded weaker mutual association with lower network, as was also indicated from the gel strength result. We have also detailed the pore distribution (Fig. S2) of the ham from SEM images, by using Image J software. It was showed that with the incorporation of MP-GA complexes after emulsion lyophilization, the homogeneity of ham was significantly enhanced with its overall frame displays less porosity, and that the presence of M3G1 rendered the lowest porosity of ham ultrastructure than both the other treated groups and the control. This phenomenon aligned with the results of SEM observation (Fig. 4E) and texture (Table 2 and Fig. 3), suggesting quantitatively that M3G1 owns high sufficiency for protein association and integration to strengthen the ham homogeneity with most improved textural attributes.

Salty fortification of reconstructed ham using MP-GA complex

The salty taste of reconstructed ham was then monitored to evaluate the efficiency of MP-GA complex for saltiness enhancement through sensory analysis (Fig. 5A) and electronic tongue analysis (Fig. 5B). Both NaCl concentration and relative ratio for MP to GA impacted the saltiness and overall flavor profile of the ham (Li et al., 2020; Nyaguthii et al., 2023). The radar plot illustrated that ham with M3G1 incorporation achieved highest score for salty taste and overall acceptability with its values reaching 8.2 and 8.0, respectively, indicating high consumer tendency with suitable saltiness preference. This, especially when compared with other conditions with varied MP to GA ratios, showed the balanced MP and GA ratio in this group (3:1) likely strengthened sodium release and perception (seen later in Fig. 7). This may also be traced to the favored water distribution and dislocation (Fig. 3C-E) for sodium retention and release facilitation, which accounted for its fortified saltiness scoring for M3G1. Alternatively, the incorporation of GA with adequate proportion allowed for greater sodium availability and enhanced the interaction between NaCl and taste receptors (Y. P. Chen, Wang, Blank, Xu, & Chung, 2021), through initially promoted structural rearrangements within meat matrix. In constrast, the treated groups with high GA incorporation (for M1G3 and M0G4) and the control yielded lower scores for salty taste that may be linked to GA led limited protein unfolding and hindered release and perception of sodium ion.

Sodium release profile and retention in reconstructed duck ham. Real-time release kinetics of sodium (A), fluorescent intensity variation reflecting sodium retention through progressive washout using pig tongue (B) and the representative fluorescent images (C).

The electronic tongue analysis provided semi-quantitative confirmation of the sensory result. From the linear relationship of NaCl concentration against salty intensity, it can be seen that the direction of the arrow indicated an increase in salty intensity (Fig. 5B). Principal component analysis (PCA) clearly distinguished M3G1 with saltiness pronouncement out of other treated groups and the control. This strong clustering reflected it achieved a superior sodium release profile, enhancing salty taste perception even at lower NaCl concentration (reduction for 33.4 %) (Fig. 5C). This aligned with the sensory score analysis, suggesting that balanced protein-polysaccharide interactions can amplify salty perception without increasing actual salt content.

Spatial distribution of sodium

The sodium spatial distribution in reconstructed ham by varying MP-GA complex incorporation was visualized through sodium fluorescein lableling (Fig. 6). The control group gave evidently even distribution of salt across the ham matrix due to direct NaCl introduction. Comparatively, there was growing reduced homogeneity for salt dispersion, and that the sodium distribution in M3G1 was markedly heterogeneous which was characterized by bright fluorescent spots surrounded by darker regions. Similar phenomena could also be found from (Gao et al., 2024; Li et al., 2020). Notably, these spots for sodium rich areas indicated localized aggregation of sodium that could enhance the salty perception by continuously stimulating the taste receptors, especially for the group of M3G1 (Gomes et al., 2021; Li et al., 2020). It may be attributed to the interaction between the protein and polysaccharide, which promoted the protein extension and dislocated the water distribution that better maintained salt retention with its partial aggregation, as was responsible for the efficient release and perception (Fig. 5) of sodium ions in this group. This result was consistent with Monteiro et al. (2021) that coarse grained salt resulted in an uneven distribution of salt in the bread, and significantly accelerated sodium release with enhanced perception of saltiness (Monteiro et al., 2021). Compared with M3G1, M1G3 and M0G4 showed some degree of sodium aggregation, but the fluorescence intensity of the spots was weaker, and the surrounding dark zones were less pronounced. These observations and moderate salty scoring for the two groups indirectly supported the presumption that fortified salty perception was acquired by pronounced salt distribution with inhomogeneity, through primarily the balanced MP-GA complexation at ratio of 3:1. While the high GA content caused the restrained MP unfolding and salt shielding of the complex, thus affecting the formation of sodium-rich regions, as well as their convey for salty perception.

Sodium release kinetics in vitro

The sodium profile against time was monitored for direct revelation of Na+ release kinetics (Fig. 7A). An exponential curve for y = a*(1-exp(−b*x)) was used to plotted the trend that proved to be well fitted (r2 > 0.99). Fir this reconstructed meat system, the Na+ releasing out was firstly fast followed by a much moderate leakage as time went by, a phenomenon similar to other meat scenarios (Conti, Septier, Gourrat, Labouré, & Salles, 2025). It was clearly seen that the release of sodium was much delayed as the incorporation of MP-GA complexes, possibly by the prolonged migration of the ion towards the meat surface. This was parallel to the fluorescence result (Fig. 6), implying that the manipulation of MP-GA complexation complicated the Na+ distribution by creating small reservoirs, which then rendered its continual releasing with increased oral saltiness scoring (Fig. 5A and B).

The retention behavior of sodium ions (Na+) in reconstructed ham on the surface of porcine tongue under artificial saliva rinsing was also analyzed to further investigate the role of MP-GA complex in modulating salty perception. In accord with the release kinetics, both quantitative fluorescence intensity curves (Fig. 7B) and in vitro sodium retention fluorescence maps (Fig. 7C) validated the positive influence of protein-polysaccharide interactions on sodium retention and release. The control group exhibited rapid sodium loss, with fluorescence intensity significantly diminished after just 3 mL of artificial saliva. Similarly, M4G0 showed weak sodium retention, nearly all fluorescence eliminated with 5 mL washout. Sodium adhesion would be minimized without polysaccharide, for instance, GA, which limited interaction between sodium and the mucosal surface that reduced taste receptor activation, resulting in weaker salty perception, as was consistent with the sodium distribution phenomena observed in Fig. 6. GA could facilitate Na+ sensing by both directly interacting with mucin side chains that add sodium retention, and add Na+ muco-adhesion by indirectly forming physical entanglements of mucin (Li et al., 2020; Qiu et al., 2025). Despite this sufficiency, the polysaccharide may only provide an auxiliary role for salt lingering. M3G1 demonstrated a much more persistent decline in fluorescence intensity (more than 40 %) and a higher retention percentage even after 10 mL of artificial saliva washout. This suggested that, with GA presence, the complex with balanced MP-GA ratio in M3G1 created a cohesive matrix that enhanced sodium adhesion and prolonged its interaction with taste receptors. Such behavior was aligned with the enhanced sensory saltiness score (Fig. 5A) observed in this formulation, demonstrating a direct correlation between sodium adhesion and salty taste perception. In summary, these findings showed that the heterogeneous distribution by MP-GA complexes favored the Na+ retention at oral mucosa and delayed its releasing by complexation complicated pathway. As a consequence, extended activation of taste receptors as well as amplified salty perception were anticipated even at reduced sodium levels.

Conclusion

Reducing salt in reconstructed duck ham is still challenging by the simultaneous maintenance of saltiness and textural homogeneity. This study validated the emulsification favored myofibrillar protein (MP)-gum Arabic (GA) complexes (4:0, 3:1, 2:2, 1:3 and 0:4), with the virtues of interfacial assembly and homogeneity of MP, as effective saltiness enhancers as well as textural improver in low-sodium reconstructed duck ham. The GA complexation led structural unfolding of the protein was strengthened following emulsion lyophilization, especially at MP-GA ratio of 3:1. Enhanced protein association (via hydrophobic interactions and disulfide bonds) within duck meat system was observed when using these powder-like complexes, favoring meat integration with desired ham appearance and gel strength. Meanwhile, the incorporation of MP-GA complexes favored also the salt maldistribution in the reconstructed duck ham, as well as the improved sodium localization and prolonged retention on taste receptors. Further observations using sodium release kinetics and porcine tongue experiment revealed that the protein dominated formular played a major role in reshaping the salt spatial distribution and then its continual release for receptor activation, among which M3G1 displayed the maximized efficiency for saltiness perception elevation with a 33.4 % reduction of salt usage. However, the interlinkage between saltiness enhancement and textural fortification using these complexes remained not verified, despite the possible relationship between the enhanced protein extension and water re-distribution, and the fortified salt retention and release for saltiness perception. In total, the MP-GA complex demonstrated significant potential as a dual functional agent for both gelling and flavor enhancing, offering a promising strategy for formulating reconstructed duck meat products with both low sodium incorporation and maintained consumer satisfaction.

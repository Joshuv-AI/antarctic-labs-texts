Improvement of electro-Fenton process by using heterogeneous Fe-MIL-88B nanocatalyst and simultaneous rotation of cathode and anode for dye removal
Authors: AliAkbar Najafzadeh, Bita Ayati
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
In this study, improvement of the electro-Fenton process using Fe-MIL-88B along with the innovation in the reactor with the simultaneous rotation of the cathodes and anodes was carried out to remove Acid Blue 25. For this purpose, the Fe-MIL-88B nanocatalyst was synthesised by the thermal solvent method and was characterised by FT-IR, EDAX, XRD, and FESEM. For the experiments, an electrochemical cell with a useful volume of 1 L and rotating cathodes and anodes were used and nanoparticles were added to the system as a slurry. To determine the appropriate values of the effective parameters, the OFAT method was used. According to the results, 0.3 g/L Fe-MIL-88B, pH equal to 3, dye concentration of 75 mg/L, a current intensity of 0.228 A, and a rotation velocity of 100 rpm were chosen as suitable values for the process. In optimal conditions, the dye removal efficiency reached to 92.3% after 90 min, which resulted in a 14.5% improvement in the dye removal efficiency compared to the conventional process under the same conditions. Also, after 90 min, the removal efficiencies of 77.08 and 63.63% were obtained for COD and TOC, respectively. The results indicated acceptable decomposition of the effluent into less hazardous compounds.

Introduction

Dyes have been widely utilised across a variety of industries. Due to their environmental impacts, contaminated wastewater with dyes must be treated before discharge. Usually, various physical, chemical, and biological methods are employed to remove dyes.

Electro-Fenton is one of the Advanced Oxidation Process (AOP) sub-branches with features such as simple operation, low toxicity, and high rate of pollutant removal efficiency1,2, which can remove a considerable portion of the biodegradable materials by continuously generating hydrogen peroxide due to the reduction of oxygen dissolved in water according to Eq. Nanocatalysts used in electro-Fenton processes are homogeneous or heterogeneous in terms of physical nature5. Adjusting the pH in the proper performance range (2–4) and remaining dissolved iron after treatment in the system, which requires secondary treatment, are among the disadvantages of the homogeneous electro-Fenton process.

The use of heterogeneous nanocatalyst in the solid phase reduces the amount of produced sludge, and the main oxidation reactions occur on the surface between the liquid phase and the solid nanocatalyst, which are easily separated6. Moreover, the heterogeneous nanocatalyst can be reused, thus reducing costs.

Metal-Organic Frameworks (MOF) are a new group of nanosorbents, which are composed of various metals as nodes and organic ligands as linkers. These compounds with a crystalline structure have characteristics such as high porosity, high specific surface area, synthesis through simple and diverse methods, low environmental risks, low density, adjustable pore size, and high mechanical and thermal stability7,8. They ave gained widespread attention in environmental remediation and also has various applications, such as adsorbents, heterogeneous catalysts, sensors, gas separation and storage agents, solar cells, and removal of organic pollutants9.

Several studies have been done regarding heterogeneous catalysts as well as MOF. Zhao et al.6 used Fe3O4/MIL-101 MOF for the degradation of rhodamine B. In the presence of H2O2, 100% of the dye was removed at pH 7 in 30 min through a Fenton-like heterogeneous process. Li et al.10 used Fe3O4@MIL-100(Fe) MOF to degrade methylene blue. In the presence of H2O2, MOF degraded 100% of the dye in 60 min, and the synthesised material maintained the degradation capacity after 10 consecutive cycles. In a study, Zahrani & Ayati5 used a heterogeneous Fe-ZSM-5 nanocatalyst and a reactor with graphite electrodes for the removal of Acid Blue 25 in a modified electro-Fenton system. Under optimal conditions of initial pH 3, current intensity 500 mA, nanocatalyst dosage 100 g/L, electrolyte concentration 0.01 M, and electrode with cathode rotation velocity 50 rpm, about 90% of 200 mg/L dye was removed. Pazhand et al.11 in a research used MOF iron-based (MIL-53 (Fe)) to degrade methylene blue dye in industrial wastewater. The obtained results showed that the sample made at 180 °C had the highest percentage of mineralization and light absorption, the highest energy gap, and the most suitable dye degradation efficiency of 56%.

The objective of this study was to investigate the capability of heterogeneous nanocatalyst Fe-MIL-88B in improving the electro-Fenton process through the innovation of electrode installation and simultaneous rotation of the cathode and anode. This aimed to enhance mass transfer for the elimination of Acid Blue 25 dye in the aqueous medium and its conversion into less harmful compounds. It involved investigating the impact of different variables including initial dye concentration, pH levels, catalyst concentration, and current intensity.

Materials and methods

A cylindrical pilot with a volume of 1 L was made of Plexiglass with an inner diameter of 12 cm and a height of 12 cm (Fig. 1). The electrodes were made of graphite with dimensions of 14 cm× 2.5 cm× 0.5 cm. The electrodes (cathode and anode with distance of 1 cm) connected to a mechanical stirrer that caused the electrodes to rotate simultaneously (that rotated both electrodes at the same time.). (The rotational velocity of the electrodes) The number of rotations of the electrodes was adjusted by the display connected to the mechanical stirrer.

The electric current in the electrodes was established by a power supply with a display of current intensity and electric potential difference. The current was set up within the solution utilizing Na2SO4 electrolyte. Air was injected into the solution by implies of an air pump and an aerator stone. An air pump and an aerator stone were implied to mix sample and aeration.

Nanocatalyst synthesis

The solvothermal method was used to synthesise Fe-MIl-88B. In the first step, a solution consisting 1,4-benzenedicarboxylic acid (3.32 g) and Fe(NO3)3·9H2O (8.08 g) were mixed separately at 50 mL of dimethylformamide (DMF) solvent. Instantaneously, these two solutions were combined. Then 8 mL of sodium hydroxide (4 M) was added to this solution and the resulting mixture was put on a stirrer for 30 min to make the mixture uniform. Next, the resulting solution was poured into an autoclave and placed in an oven with a temperature of 100 °C for 24 h. Then it was washed several times with DMF and placed again in the oven for 12 h at 60 °C. After these steps, the target substance was formed as a dry solid18.

Nanocatalyst reusability

To investigate the reusability of Fe-MIL-88B, the nanoparticles were separated using a sieve and immersed in 1 M NaOH solution for 2 h to regenerate; Then the regenerated nanoparticles were washed with distilled water and finally dried in an oven at 60 °C19,20.

COD and TOC were analused according to USEPA (Reactor Digestion Method, Method 8000) and USEPA (Direct Method, Method 10267), respectively.

The equipment utilized in this study included a UV-Vis spectrophotometer (Hach, DR4000), COD Reactor (Hach, DRB200), Megatek Supply power (PM-3005D), pH meter (Metrohm 691), Hydrothermal Reactor with 3 MPa pressure tolerance (200 mL, Tabib Chemistry Co.), a digital weight with the precision of 0.001 g (Kern, PLS 360-3), Aeration pump (RS Electria, Model 610), Ultrasonic bath (Korea Tech, DSA-series digital), centrifuge (Sigma, Model 101), Digital mechanical stirrer (IKA, RW 20).

Optimization of main operational parameters

Effect of initial pH

pH is one of the most important factors affecting the performance of the electro-Fenton process. The effect of pH on the removal of Acid Blue 25 was tested at different pH levels of 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, and 9, the results of which are given in Fig. 6a.

Fig. 6(a) Changes over time in dye removal efficiency and energy consumption at various pHs, (b) Final pHs for different initial pHs ([dye] = 50 mg/L, ω = 100 rpm, [Na2SO4] = 0.01 M, [Fe-MIL-88B] = 0.3 g/L, I = 0.228 A).

(a) Changes over time in dye removal efficiency and energy consumption at various pHs, (b) Final pHs for different initial pHs ([dye] = 50 mg/L, ω = 100 rpm, [Na2SO4] = 0.01 M, [Fe-MIL-88B] = 0.3 g/L, I = 0.228 A).

According to the results, by increasing the initial pH from 2 to 9, the dye removal efficiency decreases from 92.7 to 70.5, but it still has a good removal efficiency, which indicates the proper performance of the system in the range of different pHs due to the use of heterogeneous catalysts in the electro-Fenton process. At pH less than 2.5, H2O2 reacts with hydrogen ions to form peroxonium ion (H3O2+), which is stable; As a result, the reactivity of H2O2 decreases with respect to Fe2+, but if the pH of the environment exceeds 4.5, Fe3+ ions gradually precipitate as ferric hydroxide and lead to the removal of the catalyst from the process; Additionally, H2O2 produced from the electrochemical process, instead of becoming a hydroxyl radical, which is necessary to remove the dye, turns into water and O2, and as a result, the efficiency of the process decreases22–24. As it is clear from the obtained results, the amount of removal decreases at pHs higher than 2. The reason for applying acidic pH is to prevent the precipitation of iron ions25. The final pHs were also measured in order to check for pH changes throughout the process; the results are shown in Fig. 6b. According to the results, the pH of the solution decreased during the process, and the initial pH changes had a tendency to become acidic. Since the percentage of removal of pH in levels 2 and 3 is very close to each other, and to adjust the pH of the sample on level 2, the amount of chemicals used is more than level 3, and also due to the higher corrosiveness of the solution at pH 2 and the possibility of electrodes damage and excessive degradation of nanomaterials, pH equal to 3 was considered as the appropriate value to continue the process.

Effect of current intensity

The current intensity on the electro-Fenton process was tested at 6 levels of 0.045, 0.136, 0.228, 0.320, 0.410, and 0.480 A, the results of which are shown in Fig. 7. The percentages of dye removal for the mentioned current intensity were 88.6, 91.5, 92.5, 94.0, 96.5, and 97.3%, respectively. In general, as the current intensity increases, the amount of pollutant removal also increases, but as a result, the amount of energy consumed also rises26. According to the energy diagram, the energy consumption per removed dye has the lowest value (1118.05 KWh/Kg dye removed) at the current intensity of 0.228 A, which was selected as the appropriate current intensity for the rest of the research.

Effect of rotational velocity

To test the effect of the rotational velocity of the electrodes on the dye removal efficiency, three parameters of pH, current intensity, and electrolyte concentration were considered at optimal values, the remaining parameters were considered constant and four levels of 0, 50, 100, and 150 rpm were examined as the number of orbit of the electric motor, and the results of these tests are presented in Fig. 9.

Fig. 9Changes over time in dye removal efficiency and energy consumption at various electrodes rotational velocity ([dye] = 50 mg/L, [Na2SO4] = 0.01 M, [Fe-MIL-88B] = 0.3 g/L, I = 0.228 A, pH = 3).

Changes over time in dye removal efficiency and energy consumption at various electrodes rotational velocity ([dye] = 50 mg/L, [Na2SO4] = 0.01 M, [Fe-MIL-88B] = 0.3 g/L, I = 0.228 A, pH = 3).

According to the results, the dye removal efficiency in the mentioned number of rotations was 75.9, 83.5, 92.0, and 92.7%, respectively. By creating agitation, the mass transfer increases and the produced hydrogen peroxide diffuses more evenly, which increases the dye removal efficiency. According to the results, taking into account that when the engine is off, i.e. zero revolutions, the dye removal efficiency was low, so it cannot be considered optimal; In addition, mixing the solution in electrochemical reactors is necessary to make the solution uniform and prevent solids from settling28; However, it is quite evident that the removal efficiency is not significantly increased from the number of rotations above 100 rpm. Therefore, the rotation of 100 rpm, which has both high efficiency (92%) and low energy consumption results (1120.64 KWh/Kg), is considered as the appropriate number of rotations for the electrodes to continue the process.

Effect of nanocatalyst concentration

In electro-Fenton systems, catalyst concentration plays a key role in the production of hydroxyl radicals. For this purpose, the concentrations of 0.2, 0.3, 0.5, and 0.7 g/L of the catalyst along with the constant consideration of the selected parameters from the previous steps including pH, current intensity, electrolyte concentration, and rotational velocity and values of other constant parameters were investigated. As shown in Fig. 10, for the mentioned concentrations after 90 min, the removal efficiencies of 86.7, 92.3, 92.8, and 93.2% were obtained respectively. The efficiency of dye removal is clearly increased by the presence of nanocatalyst. Because as the concentration of the catalyst in the solution increases, the active sites for pollutant absorption increase and also the amount of iron in the system and the available sites for hydrogen peroxide activation and hydroxyl radical production increase, which leads to the improvement of removal efficiency29,6. The important point is that an excessive increase in the concentration of the catalyst leads to the agglomeration of the nanoparticles and the reduction of their active sites24. Furthermore, at high catalyst concentrations, the scavenger effect of Fe2+ ion is observed. The presence of these factors reduces the dye removal efficiency13. According to results and the dye removal of 92.3% at a concentration of 0.3 g/L, this concentration was considered as the appropriate amount to carry on the process.

Effect of dye concentration

To investigate the effect of dye concentration on the process, concentrations of 50, 75, 100, and 150 mg/L were investigated (Fig. 11). According to the results, the dye removal efficiency for the mentioned amounts was 92.3, 90.7, 83.9, and 78.2%, respectively. The efficiency of dye removal increased until the surface of the adsorbent was not saturated, but after the active sites of the adsorbent were filled, there were no active sites available to absorb additional dye molecules and the amount of adsorption on the surface of the nanocatalyst was reduced30.

According to the results, with the increase of dye concentration from 50 to 75 mg/L, no noticeable decrease in dye removal efficiency was observed, but with a further increase in dye concentration, the dye removal efficiency gradually decreased. On the other hand, the lowest amount of energy consumption occurred at a concentration of 75 mg/L, which was selected as the optimal value of the initial dye to carry out the process.

Comparing the improved electro-Fenton process with the conventional electro-Fenton

The comparison chart related to improved electro-Fenton and conventional electro-Fenton is presented in Fig. 12. In the improved electro-Fenton process, unlike the usual electro-Fenton process in which iron salt is used as a homogeneous catalyst, Fe-MIL-88B nanocatalyst was used as a heterogeneous catalyst. In the optimal conditions, the removal efficiencies in the two systems were 77.8 and 92.3%, respectively, within 90 min. The results showed that when Fe-MIL-88B was used in the electro-Fenton system, the pollutant removal efficiency increased by 14.5% compared to the conventional electro-Fenton system. It is noteworthy that in the first 2 min, the removal efficiency of the improved electro-Fenton system is more than twice that of the conventional electro-Fenton system. These results can be related to the precipitation of ferric ions (Fe3+) in the conventional process and particularly in neutral pH conditions, which leads to a decrease in the total iron concentration in the system and the removal efficiency. Additionally, unlike the conventional electro-Fenton system, the improved electro-Fenton process did not produce iron-containing sludge, which is not reusable; however, this system also had the advantage of using a reusable nanocatalyst31,32.

Mineralization and biodegradability studies

After optimizing the effective parameters of the dye removal process, in order to investigate the effect of the process on the structure of the dye constituent compounds and its degradation, the changes in the UV-vis spectra, the parameters of COD, TOC, AOS, and COS were investigated, which is shown in Fig. 13. According to Fig. 13a, the absorption spectrum of Acid Blue 25 before the start of the reaction (time = 0) has a peak at the wavelength of 602 nm, corresponding to the anthraquinone group, which is responsible for the color of Acid Blue 25, which in a period of 90 min33, especially in the first few min of the reaction (0 to 5 min) decreases significantly.

According to Fig. 13b, the removal efficiency of COD, and TOC after 90 min with initial concentrations of 144 and 55 mg/L is 77.08 and 63.63%, respectively, while the amount of dye removal in the same period of time is 3.92%. The reason for the decrease in COD and TOC removal efficiency is probably the conversion of the ring structure of the dye bonds into intermediate products and their recombination and also the production of new cyclic materials after the dye bonds are broken16. Similar to the dye, the highest amount of COD removal occurred in the first five min, which could be due to the breaking of the dye bonds and the separation of anthraquinone. The reason for the fluctuating process of COD removal efficiency can be considered the production of intermediate products because different types of intermediate products, each of which has its COD, are produced during the process5. Meanwhile, the TOC removal efficiency grew gradually and reached its maximum level after 90 min.

Reusability of Fe-MIL-88B

The stability of the used nanocatalyst was tested in subsequent experiments after it was recovered, which is a crucial parameter to reduce economic costs. The dye removal efficiency for the second to sixth cycles was 91.1, 88.9, 86.4, 84.6, and 80.2%, respectively, as shown in Fig. 15. The results indicate the feasibility of reusing Fe-MIL-88B as a heterogeneous catalyst for at least six cycles, so that after 6 cycles, its removal efficiency reached 80.2%. In addition to confirming the advantage of heterogeneous catalysts over homogeneous catalysts in terms of recovery and reuse, this result is also very important in terms of cost savings16.

Conclusion

In this research, use of electro-Fenton process with the innovation of the simultaneous rotation of the cathode and the anode increased mass transfer and improved the spread and production of hydroxyl radicals and hydrogen peroxide and efficiency compared to the conventional electro-Fenton process. In addition, the nanocatalyst with the ability to be reused up to 6 times showed that it has the ability to function in a wider range of pH and due to its easy synthesis, stability, and compatibility with the environment, it is a suitable option for removing dyes. The improvement in mineralization and biodegradability of wastewater was also presented.

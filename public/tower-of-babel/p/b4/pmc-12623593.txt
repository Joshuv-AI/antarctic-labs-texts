Population Pharmacokinetic–Based Strategies for Switching Patients with Schizophrenia Between Long-Acting Injectable Formulations of Risperidone: R064766 or RBP-7000 to TV-46000
Authors: Itay Perlstein, Jonathan Meyer, Sharath Kumar, Ziqi Yue, Vijay Ivaturi, Kelli R Franzenburg, Mark Suett, Rolf Hansen, Avia Merenlender Wagner, Arti Phatak, Rajendra Singh
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThis analysis used pharmacokinetic (PK) modeling to characterize dosing conversions and switching strategies from R064766, a once-every-2-weeks, intramuscular long-acting injectable antipsychotic (LAI) formulation of risperidone microspheres, and RBP-7000, a subcutaneous (sc) LAI formulation of risperidone administered in the abdomen once monthly (q1m), to TV-46000, a q1m or once-every-2-months (q2m) sc LAI formulation of risperidone.MethodsTotal active moiety (TAM; risperidone + 9-OH risperidone) concentration–time profiles were simulated on the basis of published population PK models with virtual populations of 5000 patients. Simulations were performed to predict TAM exposures when switching to TV-46000 q1m and q2m 2–6 weeks after the last steady-state R064766 injection and 4 weeks after the last dose of RBP-7000.ResultsComparable doses of oral risperidone, TV-46000, R064766, and RBP-7000 were identified. Initiating TV-46000 4–6 weeks after the last dose of R064766 resulted in similar trends for maximal (Cmax) and minimal (Cmin) plasma concentration ratios after the first injection for all TV-46000 doses and durations (q1m, q2m) compared with R064766 at steady state. Initiating TV-46000 q1m 4 weeks after the last dose of RBP-7000 resulted in slightly higher, but generally comparable, average plasma concentrations, Cmax, and Cmin at first dose and at steady state for TV-46000 compared with RBP-7000; Cmin of TV-46000 q2m and RBP-7000 were also comparable. Similar trends in plasma concentrations were observed for both back-of-the-upper-arm and abdominal administration when switching from R064766 and RBP-7000 to TV-46000.ConclusionThese simulations revealed switching to TV-46000 4–6 weeks after the last dose of R064766 and 4 weeks after the last dose of RBP-7000 provided generally comparable PK exposures at first dose and steady state of TV-46000. Clinician discretion will determine which switching strategy is most appropriate in context based on factors such as patient preference, scheduling convenience, and concerns about tolerability.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s40120-025-00840-9.Keywords: Long-acting injectable antipsychotic, Population pharmacokinetic modeling, Risperidone, Schizophrenia, TV-46000

Introduction

Schizophrenia is a chronic, progressive, and debilitating mental disorder marked by positive symptoms (e.g., delusions, hallucinations, disorganized speech or behavior), negative symptoms (apathy, avolition, diminished expression and speech), and cognitive dysfunction [1]. While antipsychotic medications are the mainstay of treatment, medication adherence is challenging for people living with schizophrenia, which can lead to relapse, diminished antipsychotic responsiveness, and other negative health effects [2].

Real-world evidence supports that long-acting injectable (LAI) formulations of antipsychotics are associated with improved adherence and reduced relapse risk in patients living with schizophrenia [3, 4], as well as reduced mortality risk, particularly in patients taking second-generation LAIs [5, 6]. Risperidone-based formulations, in particular, are some of the most widely used antipsychotics for the treatment of schizophrenia [7]. R064766 (Risperdal Consta®, Janssen Pharmaceuticals), an intramuscular (im) formulation administered once every 2 weeks (q2w), was one of the first second-generation LAIs developed. It was approved in 2003 by the United States (US) Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for treatment of patients with schizophrenia. Newer risperidone LAIs indicated for treatment of schizophrenia in adults include RBP-7000 (Perseris®, Indivior), a subcutaneous (sc) formulation administered once monthly (q1m) that was approved by the US FDA in 2018, and TV-46000 (Uzedy®, Teva Pharmaceuticals), a long-acting sc antipsychotic administered q1m or once every 2 months (q2m) that was approved by the US FDA in 2023.

Clinicians may opt to switch between different risperidone LAI formulations for a variety of reasons, including patient preference for easier dosing schedules, cost concerns, efficacy, and tolerability issues [8]. However, differences in pharmacokinetic (PK) properties and drug delivery technologies between various LAIs, combined with a lack of clinical trials on transitioning patients between them, contribute to uncertainty during switching [9]. Uncertainty among clinicians about the best switching method can act as a barrier to treatment optimization [10].

When considering switching risperidone LAI formulations from R064766 or RBP-7000 to TV-46000, the PK properties, such as plasma concentration levels of total active moiety (TAM; risperidone + 9-OH risperidone, risperidone active metabolite), as well as dosing of each formulation, should be taken into consideration. R064766 demonstrates a delayed-release profile following a single im dose that starts to increase around 3 weeks post injection (with peak maximal TAM concentration [Cmax] at approximately day 35). After initiation, 21 days of oral supplementation is required to achieve clinically relevant plasma concentrations until risperidone is released from the microspheres. Clinically relevant TAM concentrations (> 10 ng/mL) are maintained at 4–6 weeks and steady-state levels are achieved after four injections [11, 12]. For RBP-7000, peak plasma TAM levels are reached within 4–6 h after injection and a second peak occurs 10–14 days after, with no oral supplementation or loading dose required. Steady-state levels are approached after the first injection and achieved within 2 months [13]. For TV-46000, the median time to Cmax ranges from 8 to 14 days, and steady-state levels are approached within 2 months of TV-46000 initiation (i.e., > 70% of steady state for area under the curve [AUC] and Cmax TAM exposure based on sequential simulated concentration–time profiles) [14]. TV-46000 achieves clinically relevant plasma levels within 6–24 h and does not require oral supplementation or a loading injection. For injections of subcutaneous formulations such as TV-46000, the same needle size can be used, whereas for intramuscular formulations, needle size varies according to the body mass index (BMI) of the patient [15]. Subcutaneous formulations may also be viewed as being less invasive and easier to use in clinical practice than intramuscular formulations [16], with most healthcare professionals and patients preferring the smaller needles and injection volumes [17, 18].

Given the varying kinetic properties of these three risperidone LAIs, the objective of this analysis was to use population PK (popPK)–based models to characterize dosing conversions and switching strategies from either R064766 or RBP-7000 to TV-46000. Switching strategies were evaluated on the basis of concentrations of TAM, and all switching strategies to TV-46000 were only explored for patients at steady-state TAM concentrations for RBP-7000 or R064766.

Methods

PopPK Models

This analysis is based on previously conducted studies and does not contain any new studies with human participants. All involved study centers of the original clinical trials received approval by local ethics committees and informed consent was received for all patients. The study used published popPK models for R064766 [19], RBP-7000 [20], and TV-46000 [21]. For RBP-7000, the original model developed by the sponsor company (Indivior Inc.) was not suitable for simulation purposes [22]. The visual predictive check for TAM and for the individual components did not show a close match between simulated and observed data, especially with regards to population variability [23]. Therefore, the Laffont 2014 model [20] was used for switching simulations between RBP-7000 and TV-46000. For TV-46000 and R064766, the models developed by the sponsor companies (Teva Pharmaceuticals and Janssen Pharmaceuticals, respectively) were used [19, 21]. The original R064766 model was updated on the basis of 3051 plasma concentration–time observations and was evaluated by a total of 6566 concentrations from 1060 subjects [19].

The final PK model for TV-46000 is described in detail in a previous study [21]. Briefly, it was a sequential parent–metabolite model. The parent model’s structure is a one-compartment model, with double first-order release routes (fast [KA1] and slow [KA2], with transit rate constant [KTR] and five transit compartments for KA2) and first-order elimination to 9-OH risperidone. The 9-OH risperidone PK was described by a one-compartment model, with first-order conversion from the parent and first-order elimination. Covariates retained in the parent model include BMI effect on KA1 and KA2, as well as effects of administration site and injection volume on KA1 (Figs. 1a, 2a) [21].

The PK model for R064766 consisted of a one-compartment disposition model with first-order elimination and absorption occurring via three routes to account for multiple peaks. The first route represented a fast release (KTR1), while the slower second and third routes occurred via transit compartments (transit rate constants KTR2 and KTR3) (Fig. 1b) [19]. Lean body weight was included as a covariate on the apparent clearance of R064766.

The PK model of RBP-7000 was a two-compartment model for the parent with double first-order release routes (fast [KA1] and slow [KA2], with KTR and five transit compartments for KA2) and first-order elimination to 9-OH risperidone (Fig. 2b) [20]. BMI was identified as a significant covariate affecting the initial absorption of risperidone following RBP-7000 injection. Covariates included in the models were randomly sampled from within the demographic ranges of the RBP-7000 phase 1 and TV-46000 phase 3 clinical trials.

Oral Risperidone Doses Comparable to R064766, RBP-7000, and TV-46000

On the basis on comparisons between a variety of simulated PK parameters, doses of R064766, RBP-7000, and TV-46000 were generally comparable to 2-mg, 3-mg, and 4-mg once-daily doses of oral risperidone. TV-46000 also has approved doses comparable to 5 mg/day oral risperidone (125 mg q1m and 250 mg q2m, respectively); however, switching to these dose strengths was not simulated because of lack of comparable R064766 and RBP-7000 doses. Additionally, R064766 has an approved dose of 12.5 mg (comparable to 1 mg/day risperidone) for patients with renal or hepatic impairment. However, since there is no comparable TV-46000 dose, this switch was not explored. Suitable switching intervals, defined by similar TAM plasma exposures during and after the switch, were then determined.

Model-Based Simulations

TAM (risperidone + 9-OH risperidone) concentration–time profiles were simulated with virtual populations of 5000 patients. Simulations were performed for various dosing scenarios to predict PK exposures for TAM when switching to TV-46000 q1m and q2m from a steady-state condition of R064766 or RBP-7000. For switching from R064766 to TV-46000, initial explorations considered the implications of switching to TV-46000 2–3 weeks after the last dose of R064766. Given the distinctive profile of R064766, characterized by a delayed release that starts approximately 3 weeks after administration, further simulations assessing the initiation of TV-46000 4–6 weeks after the final dose of R064766 were conducted. For switching from RBP-7000 to TV-46000, simulated switching intervals for TV-46000 occurred 4 weeks after the last dose of RBP-7000, given that RBP-7000 is a q1m formulation.

PK parameters of TAM, such as Cmax and minimal plasma concentration (Cmin), were derived using simulated concentration profiles. Median simulated plasma concentration–time profiles were plotted. Average plasma concentration (Cavg) at steady state was calculated as the AUC for the dosing interval (AUC0–tau) divided by dosing interval time in hours. Cmax and Cmin ratios for TV-46000 compared with R064766 and RBP-7000 were calculated by dividing the mean Cmax or Cmin for TV-46000 by the mean Cmax or Cmin for R064766 and RBP-7000. The simulation and noncompartmental analysis were conducted by using nonlinear mixed effects modeling software Pumas (version 2.4.1; Pumas AI Inc., Dover, DE, US).

Examining TAM exposure parameter data on median Cavg, Cmax, and Cmin also showed that the most comparable switching scenarios involved receiving the first dose of TV-46000 4–6 weeks after the last dose of R064766, as evidenced by the ratios of TV-46000 to R064766 approaching 1.0 (Table 2, Supplementary Table S1). A 4-week gap between the last dose of R064766 and the first dose of TV-46000 resulted in similar Cavg and Cmin values, but initial higher Cmax values, for TV-46000 versus R064766 at steady state. A 5-week gap provided the most overall comparable initial Cavg, Cmax, and Cmin values with that of R064766 at steady state, with q1m first-dose Cmax and Cmin being the closest to 1.0 compared with other switching scenarios (i.e., week 2, 4, and 6). A 6-week gap provided similar initial Cavg and Cmax values, but lower Cmin values, compared with R064766 at steady state. When switching to TV-46000 2 weeks after the last dose of R064766, Cmax and Cmin first-dose ratios for all TV-46000 doses were higher than when switching 4–6 weeks after the last dose of R064766 (Table 2, Supplementary Table S1). Across all examined switching scenarios, TV-46000 q2m demonstrated higher Cmax ratios at first dose and lower Cmin ratios than q1m.

Discussion

PopPK simulations were used to simulate strategies for switching from two other LAI formulations of risperidone to TV-46000. As a result of an absence of clinical trial data on switching between risperidone-containing LAIs, simulations can increase clinicians’ confidence in switching approaches by providing critical PK information. The simulations revealed that switching to TV-46000 4–6 weeks after the last dose of R064766 at steady state provided the most comparable exposures by the second dose of TV-46000. This was also confirmed by the TAM exposure ratios (TV-46000 Cmax and Cmin:R064766 Cmax and Cmin), which approached 1.0 for q1m and 2.0 for q2m as TV-46000 steady state was attained. Switching to TV-46000 2 weeks after the last dose of R064766 (i.e., in lieu of the regularly scheduled 2-week dose of R064766) showed higher initial TAM exposure ratios compared with switching at 4–6 weeks; however, steady-state ratios were similar between the different washout durations. In addition, switching to TV-46000 4 weeks after the last dose of RBP-7000 provided generally comparable PK exposures at first dose and at steady state of TV-46000.

When switching to TV-46000 from either R064766 or RBP-7000, the Cavg at steady state was comparable to the corresponding oral risperidone doses (2–4 mg/day), with Cmax and Cmin within the confidence intervals of the Cavg for R064766 and RBP-7000. Even though TV-46000 steady-state Cavg and Cmax were numerically higher, previous pivotal and long-term trials of TV-46000 have demonstrated that the safety profile of TV-46000 was consistent with other approved formulations of risperidone [24–26]. For both TV-46000 q1m and q2m, peak-to-trough ranges are comparable with that of other LAI formulations [27], and average TAM exposure is similar to that of oral risperidone 2–5 mg/day [21]. Additionally, similar trends in plasma concentrations were observed for both back-of-upper-arm and abdominal administration following TV-46000 injections from both R064766 and RBP-7000, which indicates that adipose tissue distribution and vascularity of the different injection sites do not have a clinically relevant effect on the release and absorption of risperidone into the bloodstream. This is also confirmed by popPK modeling of TV-46000 phase 3 data [21] and presents clinicians with more options when switching to TV-46000.

From these simulations, clinicians can more optimally schedule the initiation of TV-46000, and thereby avoid the two primary concerns incurred when switching between products with differing PK properties: (1) if clinically relevant plasma concentrations are not reached quickly enough, there is a potential risk for relapse [9]; (2) if initial TAM exposure is too high, there is a risk for adverse events and poor tolerability. TV-46000 has a rapid initial absorption that is potentially associated with the initial release from the sc depot, reaching clinically relevant plasma concentrations within 6–24 h, and a second, slower process that is likely associated with the in situ depot releasing risperidone in a sustained manner over 28 days (q1m) or 56 days (q2m) [21, 28]. R064766 reaches steady state after four injections (i.e., after 8 weeks) [12], but does not have the same sustained concentrations as TV-46000 over a longer time period, and therefore switching should probably not be delayed for more than two skipped doses (i.e., 6 weeks). This presents clinicians with options to switch to TV-46000 between 4–6 weeks after the last dose of R064766 (skipping one to two doses) while still maintaining clinically relevant risperidone concentrations with minimal safety risk. For example, a patient taking 50 mg R064766 q2w can transition to 100 mg TV-46000 q1m or 200 mg TV-46000 q2m for comparable TAM exposure. Therefore, this patient will be initiated on TV-46000 4–6 weeks after the last dose of R064766 while monitoring efficacy or tolerability on TV-46000 for the first 2–3 months. Although TV-46000 can be given directly after stopping R064766 without missing a dose (i.e., 2 weeks after), according to the simulations, there is still a considerable risperidone TAM exposure, which may increase the risk of adverse events. However, this option could be deferred to the clinicians’ discretion if there are concerns of relapse. On the other hand, both TV-46000 and RBP-7000 are injected subcutaneously and achieve clinically relevant plasma concentrations within 24 h. Therefore, on the basis of the simulations of PK properties for both RBP-7000 and TV-46000, switching can generally be considered on the same day that the pre-switch RBP-7000 injection was scheduled. This makes patient scheduling convenient and reduces the risk of potential relapse because of a missed dose. For instance, if a patient is taking 120 mg RBP-7000 q1m, they can transition to 100 mg TV-46000 q1m or 200 mg TV-46000 q2m to reach comparable plasma levels. The patient can thus be initiated on TV-46000 at the next date that the RBP-7000 injection would have been scheduled.

The results of this analysis should be interpreted within its limitations. As a result of the lack of pivotal clinical trials of switching to TV-46000 from other LAIs, it can only be simulated with popPK. Thus, there are no patient-level safety or efficacy outcomes data available. The potential switching strategies are only suggestions in the absence of clinical data from real-life patients. It should also be noted that earlier switching from R064766 and RBP-7000 (i.e., before steady state is attained) was not simulated here. It is important to highlight the inherent subjectivity in determining comparability between different formulations. There is no universally standardized definition of “pharmacokinetic comparability,” which results in a more interpretative approach (in contrast to bioequivalence studies). It must also be noted that manufacturers use different models to compare their product with reference ranges of oral risperidone, which can lead to variability in switching models between products. While popPK-informed data are helpful to clinicians, any switching strategy should always consider the clinical context of each individual patient. Therapeutic drug monitoring has also been proposed as an approach to dose selection when switching antipsychotics. Previous studies have shown the effectiveness of blood level monitoring when switching patients from oral antipsychotics to LAIs; however, it cannot provide LAI-specific therapeutic reference ranges or determine dose equivalents, as therapeutic monitoring is patient specific [29].

Conclusions

Overall, popPK simulations revealed that switching to TV-46000 4–6 weeks after the last dose of R064766 provided generally comparable exposures, with a 5-week gap showing PK exposure closest to that achieved with R064766 at steady state. Additionally, switching to TV-46000 4 weeks after the last dose of RBP-7000 demonstrated comparable PK exposures from the first dose. It is important to note the implications of changes in TAM exposure and PK properties when considering switching between different risperidone LAIs. Clinician discretion will determine which switching strategy is best on the basis of a variety of factors, such as patient preference, scheduling convenience, and concerns about tolerability or symptom breakthrough.

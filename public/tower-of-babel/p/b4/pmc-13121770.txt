Synergistic optoelectronic and thermoelectric performance in Rb2AsAuBr6 and Rb2AsAuCl6 double perovskites for multifunctional energy conversion
Authors: K Bouferrache, M A Ghebouli, M Fatmi, Ghadah Sheetah, S Alomairy, Mustafa Jaipallah Abdelmageed Abualreish, Aseel Smerat, Murat Yaylacı
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this work, we use density functional theory (DFT) to thoroughly analyze the structural, electrical, optical, and thermoelectric characteristics of halide double perovskites Rb2AsAuX4 (X = Br, Cl). Both compounds’ structural stability is confirmed by the optimized lattice characteristics, with Rb2AsAuBr4 showing somewhat larger cell dimensions than Rb2AsAuCl4. Indirect band gaps of 0.338 eV (Br) and 0.885 eV (Cl), which are within the optimal range for solar applications, are shown by electronic band structure simulations. Strong absorption coefficients in the visible region above 1.2 × 101 cm−1 are shown in optical spectra, suggesting great potential for solar energy harvesting. High Seebeck coefficients of up to 310 μV/K (Br) and 285 μV/K (Cl) at ambient temperature are shown by thermoelectric analysis, together with electrical conductivities that facilitate effective charge transfer. Thermoelectric performance is further improved by the comparatively low thermal conductivity (0.9–1.1 W/m·K). Together, our findings demonstrate Rb2AsAuX4’s versatility and establish Rb2AsAuBr6 and Rb2AsAuCl4 as viable options for next optoelectronic and energy-harvesting applications.Keywords: Halide double perovskites, Bandgap engineering, Visible-light absorption, Thermoelectric figure of merit (ZT), Multifunctional energy materialsSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Materials science, Physics

Introduction

Double perovskites with the general formula A2BB’X6, where A and B are transition and alkaline metals, As is a pnictogen, and Cl and Br are non-metallic halogens, exhibit exceptional electrical characteristics. These materials are excellent for advanced photovoltaic applications because they have sufficient optical properties1. Their remarkable structural flexibility and thermoelectric qualities2 have drawn a lot of interest for energy conversion technologies3. These double perovskites’ structural adaptability enables accurate physical property tweaking by suitable chemical replacement4,5. Research on next-generation materials for quantum technologies is leading the way with the double perovskite architecture6. This kind of perovskite is used in energy harvesting systems8 and sophisticated optoelectronic devices7. Au’s heavy metal electronic configuration has garnered a lot of interest9, with spin-orbit coupling producing intriguing electronic phenomena10,11 and d electronic configuration contributing to unique behavior12,13. As demonstrates unique p orbital properties14, their contributions have a substantial impact on the resulting materials’ band structure15. Arsenic inclusion also affects optical characteristics16. Band gap tuning is possible with halides of chlorine and bromine17. These double perovskites’ prospective uses in the optoelectronic sector are confirmed by an experimental investigation18,19. The ideal ionic radii for the creation of stable perovskites are matched by the selection of rubidium as the cation20. One technique that makes it possible to comprehend basic material characteristics is density functional theory (DFT). Techniques that reliably anticipate electronic band gaps in halogenated perovskites include the Becke-Johnson (mBJ) exchange-correlation potential. The appropriate use of these materials is determined by the intricate interaction between optical response and electrical structure. Seebeck and other thermoelectric characteristics assess possible uses for energy harvesting. Transport phenomena may be understood using electrical conductivity. Thermoelectric characterization is completed by thermal conductivity. Halide double perovskites’ exceptional potential in renewable energy systems has been further underlined by recent developments.Lead-free inorganic halide perovskites with adjustable band gaps and enhanced stability were developed after the initial reports of perovskite solar cells showed effective visible-light sensitization3,21,22. Furthermore, it has been demonstrated that double perovskites like Cs2AgBiX4 (X = F, Cl, Br, I) have appropriate optical and electrical properties for next-generation optoelectronic applications23. With advancements in improving Seebeck coefficients and electrical conductivity through compositional design, their growing relevance as thermoelectric materials has also been highlighted24,25. These materials are very promising for sophisticated energy harvesting applications since more recent computational and experimental work have confirmed that they can integrate both optoelectronic and thermoelectric functions26,27. Despite these extensive efforts, Au–As–based halide double perovskites remain largely unexplored, particularly with respect to their combined optoelectronic, thermoelectric, and thermodynamic properties. In this work, we present a comprehensive first-principles investigation of Rb2AsAuBr6 and Rb2AsAuCl6, aiming to establish a unified understanding of their structural stability, electronic structure, optical response, and thermoelectric performance within a single theoretical framework. To the best of our knowledge, such a multifunctional analysis of Rb–As–Au double perovskites has not been reported previously. By simultaneously evaluating optical absorption and thermoelectric efficiency, this study highlights the synergistic potential of these materials for multifunctional energy conversion and provides new insights into Au-based double perovskites as promising candidates for next-generation optoelectronic and energy-harvesting applications.

Simulation methodology

The Wien2K code’s full-potential linearized augmented plane wave plus local orbitals (FP-LAPW+lo) approach was used for all computations28. In this model, the unit cell is divided between an area where the spheres have an interstitial form and a muffin tin (MT) shape. Each sphere has spherical harmonic radial wave functions. By maintaining RMT, the convergence was managed. Kmax = 8.5, where RMT is the radius of the smallest atomic sphere and Kmax is the magnitude of the biggest K vector. For optical and thermoelectric characteristics, we choose a k-point of 10,000, and for elastic constants, 5000. The threshold for force convergence in the relaxation of the structure is set at 0.0001 Ry, and the convergence of the self-consistent cycle is fixed at 10-8 when the total charge is less than 0.001. The lattice constant of Rb2AsAuX6 (X = Br, Cl) is optimized using the PBE-GGA functional29 of Perdew, Burke, and Ernzerhof. Electronic, optical, and thermoelectric characteristics are calculated using the modified Becke-Johnson (mBJ-GGA)30 exchange potential. The semi-classical Boltzman transport equation was solved using the BoltzTrap algorithm to determine the transport characteristics. All thermoelectric transport coefficients were evaluated within the constant relaxation time approximation (CRTA) as implemented in the BoltzTrap code. In this approach, electrical conductivity and electronic thermal conductivity are obtained as σ/τ and κe/τ, respectively. To estimate absolute transport quantities and the thermoelectric figure of merit (ZT), a typical relaxation time τ = 1 × 10−14 s was assumed, which is commonly adopted for halide perovskites in the absence of experimental scattering data. Accordingly, ZT was calculated using ZT = S2σT/(κe + κl), where κl denotes the lattice thermal conductivity. This assumption allows a qualitative assessment of thermoelectric performance while preserving the relative trends between the studied compounds. The lattice thermal conductivity (κl) was estimated using the semi-empirical Slack model, which is widely adopted for crystalline solids when full anharmonic phonon Boltzmann transport calculations are not available. Electronic properties

The electrical band structures for Rb2AsAuX6 (X=Br, Cl) utilizing mBJ-GGA are shown in Fig. 5. With an indirect X-Γ band gap around the Fermi level, both double perovskites exhibit a semiconducting character. As an aside, we see that the conduction band in Rb2AsAuBr6 is located close to the Fermi level, resulting in a shorter band gap for the bromide compound, which is consistent with its wider p orbitals and lower electronegativity. Since there are no theoretical or experimental values for the band gap, our findings are predictions. In order to account for relativistic effects associated with the heavy Au atoms, spin–orbit coupling (SOC) was additionally included, and the corresponding GGA+SOC band structures are presented in Fig. 5. The inclusion of SOC leads to slight band splittings and small shifts in the band-edge energies, while preserving the overall semiconducting character and relative electronic trends of both compounds, in agreement with previous reports on halide perovskites36. Table 3 summarizes the calculated band gap values of Rb2AsAuX6 (X = Br, Cl) obtained using both GGA, GGA+SO and mBJ approaches. Within GGA and GGA+SO, Rb2AsAuBr6 exhibits metallic behavior, while a small band gap of (0.108, 0,12) eV is observed for Rb2AsAuCl6. In contrast, the mBJ potential opens finite band gaps of 0.338 eV and 0.885 eV for Rb2AsAuBr6 and Rb2AsAuCl6, respectively, confirming the semiconducting nature of both compounds. The larger band gap in the chloride system can be attributed to the stronger electronegativity of Cl and reduced orbital overlap compared to Br, consistent with the trends observed in the band structures. Figures 6 and 7 show the total and partial densities of states for Rb2AsAuX6 (X = Br, Cl), where no states exist at the Fermi level. Sharp peaks representing hybridized X-p and Au-d orbitals dominate the upper valence band area, which spans from around 0 to −1.6 eV. As-p and X-p states predominate in the first conduction band region, which begins at around 0.6 and ~1 eV.The distribution of electric charge in a material (location of electron concentration) is reflected in the electronic charge density maps, which also explain how it affects the electronic structure. The electric charge distribution for Rb2AsAuX6 (X = Br, Cl) is shown in Fig. 8. The figures demonstrate that rubidium atoms act as ionic charge donors and have a small impact on bonding. Strong hybridization between Au-5d and X-p states is highlighted by a notable charge buildup between Au and X atoms. Through As-p states, arsenic atoms contribute to the conduction band minimum and exhibit directed charge distributions toward the halogens. Because Br orbitals are more spatially stretched than Cl orbitals, Au–Br bonds have a more covalent nature than Au–Cl bonds.

Optical properties

Using the mBJ-GGA functional, Fig. 9 displays the real and imaginary components of the dielectric function, refractive index, extinction coefficient, energy loss, absorption coefficient, real optical conductivity, and reflectivity as a function of photon energy for Rb2AsAuX6 (X = Br, Cl). The dielectric function’s real and imaginary components reveal details about electron excitation and electronic polarization. These compounds are appropriate for optoelectronic devices when the imaginary component is larger at lower energies. In optical materials, the imaginary part of the dielectric function (ε2) directly represents the strength of interband electronic transitions and therefore governs the optical absorption behavior. A larger ε2 at lower photon energies indicates efficient excitation of electrons from the valence band to the conduction band, enabling strong light–matter interaction in the visible and near-infrared regions. Consequently, enhanced ε2 in this energy range implies improved photocarrier generation and photoresponse, which are essential for optoelectronic applications such as photodetectors and solar energy harvesting devices. In the present compounds, the pronounced ε2 at low energies reflects favorable electronic transitions associated with X-p and Au-d hybridized states, confirming their suitability for optoelectronic functionalities. For Rb2AsAuBr6 and Rb2AsAuCl6, the static dielectric constants are 7 and 5.2, respectively. The electronic polarization of the bromide is greater than that of the chloride due to its lower band gap. The relationship between the extinction coefficient and the refractive index is n2-k2 = 1. Because photons slow down and have an oscillatory feature when interacting with electrons within a material, the refractive index is higher in visible light. Rb2AsAuBr6 and Rb2AsAuCl6 have static refractive indices of 2.6 and 2.28, respectively. The extinction coefficient, which exhibits consecutive peaks in the visible and ultraviolet regions, measures the attenuation of light within the material. The imaginary part of the dielectric constant and the form of the extinction coefficient are almost identical. The electron gas’s plasma oscillation peaks, which are somewhat redshifted in Rb2AsAuBr6 as opposed to Rb2AsAuCl6, are identified by the energy loss. This indicates that electrons can oscillate collectively at low energy because the Br-4p states are wider and more delocalized. For Rb2AsAuBr6 and Rb2AsAuCl6, the absorption begins at around 0.4 eV and 0.9 eV, respectively, which are consistent with the onset of interband electronic transitions, although the optical absorption edges appear slightly red-shifted relative to the calculated mBJ–GGA band gaps due to excitonic effects and band tail states. Both compounds were found to have a high absorption coefficient of around 2×104 cm−1 in both visible and ultraviolet light, which indicates moderate-to-strong optical absorption in the visible–UV region. These values are comparable to those reported for halide-based absorber materials, suggesting potential applicability in optoelectronic and photovoltaic devices. These absorption values are comparable to those reported for emerging halide-based and low-dimensional optoelectronic materials, where absorption coefficients in the range of 104–105 cm−1 are considered sufficient for photodetection and energy-harvesting applications37. Because of its reduced band gap, Rb2AsAuBr4 exhibits an absorption beginning at lower photon energy, proving the relationship between electrical and optical features. The capacity of a substance to absorb or disperse energy is the actual component of optical conductivity. Greater absorption is indicated by a larger number, which goes beyond the static limit of electrical conductivity and is essential for comprehending charge transfer and material reactions in the optical frequency range. For Rb2AsAuCl4, the reflectivity exhibits a moderate reflectance in the visible–UV range and a decreased reflectivity at lower energies, which is consistent with its greater dielectric response. Thermoelectric characteristics

The thermoelectric performance of the material under study is revealed by the Seebeck coefficient, electronic thermal conductivity, lattice thermal conductivity, total thermal conductivity, electrical conductivity, and figure of merit using the mBJ-GGA approximation, as shown in Fig. 10. In the extrinsic regime, the Seebeck coefficient which is defined as the ratio of voltage generated to temperature gradient increases up to 400 K before declining linearly as a result of bipolar conduction. Rb2AsAuBr6 has a higher Seebeck coefficient than Rb2AsAuCl6 at all temperatures. The positive Seebeck value of the double perovskites indicates that they are p-type semiconductors. Rb2AsAuBr6 and Rb2AsAuCl6 have high Seebeck coefficients of 255µV/K and 250µV/K, respectively, at 400 K. According to reports, total conductivity follows the evolution of electronic conductivity, whereas lattice conductivity declines as a result of Umklapp phonon–phonon scattering. Electronic conductivity is said to rise monotonically with temperature. At all temperatures, Rb2AsAuCl6 exhibits greater κL values than Rb2AsAuBr6. At ultralow lattice thermal conductivity values, additional heat transport mechanisms beyond the conventional particle-like phonon picture may become relevant. In particular, wave-like phonon transport effects associated with phonon coherence and mode coupling have been reported to contribute to thermal conduction in materials characterized by strong phonon scattering and structural complexity38. Although such effects were not explicitly treated in the present work, the extremely low κl observed here may be associated with the presence of heavy Au atoms, large mass contrast, and complex bonding environments, which significantly reduce phonon mean free paths. Consequently, possible wave-like contributions to lattice heat transport cannot be excluded. In addition, qualitative insight into phonon-mediated heat transport can be obtained from the phonon dispersion and projected phonon density of states shown in Fig. 3. The low-frequency acoustic branches are mainly dominated by Rb and halogen vibrations, whereas the higher-frequency optical modes originate primarily from Au and As atoms. The presence of relatively flat optical phonon branches enhances phonon–phonon scattering, which suppresses acoustic phonon transport. Consequently, κl is mainly governed by acoustic phonons, while optical phonons primarily act as scattering channels rather than heat carriers. Similar behavior, where acoustic modes dominate lattice heat conduction and optical modes enhance scattering, has been widely reported for complex halide and heavy-element thermoelectric systems, leading to ultralow lattice thermal conductivity40. It should be noted that the calculated electrical conductivity is obtained within the constant relaxation time approximation and assumes a fixed carrier concentration. Therefore, the apparent similarity between the temperature dependence of resistivity and conductivity arises from the simplified transport model and the competing effects of carrier excitation and scattering. Plots of electrical resistivity demonstrate a nearly linear rise with temperature. Moreover, the intrinsic carrier concentration is increased by the temperature increase. Rb2AsAuBr6’s merit factor is essentially unaffected by temperature, where as Rb2AsAuCl6’s fluctuation is quadratic with respect to 500 K. Although the thermoelectric figure of merit (ZT) explicitly depends on temperature, the nearly temperature‐independent behavior observed for Rb2AsAuBr6 originates from compensating trends among the Seebeck coefficient, electrical conductivity, and thermal conductivity. Specifically, the increase in electrical conductivity with temperature is partially offset by the reduction in Seebeck coefficient and the evolution of thermal conductivity, resulting in an approximately constant ZT over the investigated temperature range. Similar compensation effects leading to quasi‐stable ZT values have been reported in recent thermoelectric studies39,40. For these two double perovskites, an average merit factor of 0.75 may be calculated. These p-type semiconductors are good options for thermoelectric devices because of their maximum value. Our computational results for Rb2AsAuX6 (X = Br, Cl) are in good agreement with previous discoveries published in prestigious publications. For example, halide double perovskites’ improved optoelectronic adaptability extends beyond solar and thermoelectric applications41,42. Zhu et al.’s thorough study of tandem and high-efficiency solar cells based on metal halide perovskites43 highlights the significance of bandgap tuning and stability patterns that resemble the behavior seen in our devices. When taken as a whole, these references support the applicability of our hypotheses regarding Rb2AsAuX4’s multifunctional capabilities and validate their status as viable options for both energies harvesting technologies and optoelectronic devices. Moreover, similar electronic and thermoelectric trends have been reported in related halide double perovskites and heavy-element systems. Recent first-principles studies on double perovskites demonstrate that the inclusion of heavy atoms leads to enhanced phonon scattering, reduced lattice thermal conductivity, and preserved semiconducting characteristics, making such compounds promising for energy conversion applications. In particular, halide-based double perovskites exhibit strong mass contrast and flat optical phonon modes, which suppress acoustic phonon transport and favor ultralow κₗ, while maintaining favorable Seebeck coefficients and electronic structures. These observations are consistent with our results for Rb2AsAuX6 and further support their potential for thermoelectric and optoelectronic devices44–47.

Thermodynamic properties

Thermodynamic qualities are quantifiable attributes that characterize a system’s condition, such as temperature, pressure, and volume. Internal energy, entropy, enthalpy, and heat capacity are common thermodynamic variables that dictate a system’s physical state and behavior. In thermodynamics and materials science, Cp and Cv are trustworthy indicators of phase transitions and heat transport. These characteristics are displayed for temperatures up to 900 K and pressures of 0, 2, 4, and 6 GPa.Cp and Cv rise with rising temperature and fall with increasing pressure. For Rb2AsAuBr6 (Rb2AsAuCl6), the Cv(Cp) at 300 K are 237.5 J.mol−1K−1and 235 J.mol−1K−1 (275 J.mol−1K-and 250 J.mol−1K−1). A physical term called entropy describes how disorganized a system is.An rise in temperature causes a material’s entropy to grow exponentially. At any temperature, the entropy rises as the pressure does. For Rb2AsAuBr6 and Rb2AsAuCl6, the entropy at 300 K and equilibrium pressure is 450 J.mol−1K−1 and 400 J.mol−1K−1, respectively. Debye temperature (θD) affects a solid’s heat capacity at low temperatures and characterizes the behavior of lattice vibrations, or phonons. It separates frozen modes (T< θD) from excited vibrational modes (θD<T). Debye temperature drops as temperature rises at any pressure. For Rb2AsAuBr6 and Rb2AsAuCl6, its value at 300 K is 180 K and 220 K, respectively. The fractional change in size per degree change in temperature at constant pressure is measured by the thermal expansion coefficient. It tracks how Cp changes with temperature. The thermal expansion coefficient falls with increasing pressure at any temperature. For Rb2AsAuBr6 and Rb2AsAuCl6, its value at 300 K is 12×10−5 K−1 and 10×10−5 K−1, respectively.

Conclusion

The Wien2K code’s full-potential linearized augmented plane wave plus local orbitals (FP-LAPW+lo) approach was used for all computations. All of the findings concerning the characteristics of Rb2AsAuBr6 and Rb2AsAuCl6 under study are theoretical expectations. For Rb2AsAuBr6 and Rb2AsAuCl6, the absorption spectra begin at around 0.4 eV and 0.9 eV, respectively, which nearly match the band gaps calculated using the mBJ–GGA functional (0.338 eV and 0.885 eV, respectively). In the visible and ultraviolet spectrum, these compounds have a very high absorption coefficient of around 2.0 × 104 cm−1, indicating their potential as effective absorbers and viable options for solar energy conversion. The p-type semiconducting character of both double perovskites is reflected in their positive Seebeck coefficients, which at room temperature reach about 310 μV/K for Rb2AsAuBr4 and 285 μV/K for Rb2AsAuCl₄. Their candidacy for thermoelectric devices was supported by an estimated average thermoelectric figure of merit (ZT) of ≈0.75. Additionally, the specific heat values at 300 K were determined to be Cv = 237.5 J·mol−1K−1 and Cp = 275 J·mol−1K−1 for Rb2AsAuBr6 and Cv = 235 J·mol−1K−1 and Cp = 250 J·mol−1K−1 for Rb2AsAuCl6.

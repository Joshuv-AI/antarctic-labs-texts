ADORA2B, deubiquitinated by USP14, facilitates IL-1β-induced chondrocyte apoptosis, ferroptosis and inflammation to accelerate osteoarthritis process
Authors: Zhi Li, Peng Zhang, Chuanwei Ouyang, Zhou Shen, Genwei Guo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Abnormal expression of adenosine A2B receptor (ADORA2B) may be involved in the development of osteoarthritis (OA). Therefore, it is necessary to explore its role and mechanism in OA progression to understand OA pathogenesis.Methods:Chondrocytes were treated with IL-1β to induce an OA cell injury model. Chondrocyte proliferation and apoptosis were detected using CCK8 assay, EdU assay, and flow cytometry. The levels of inflammation factors were examined by ELISA. Fe2+, ROS, lipid ROS, MDA, and GSH levels were determined to evaluate cell ferroptosis. ADORA2B mRNA expression was tested by quantitative real-time PCR. ADORA2B, GPX4, and ubiquitin-specific peptidase 14 (USP14) protein levels were examined by western blot. The interaction between ADORA2B and USP14 was assessed by Co-IP assay.Results:IL-1β treatment decreased chondrocyte proliferation, enhanced apoptosis, inflammation, and ferroptosis. ADORA2B had elevated expression in OA patients and IL-1β-induced chondrocytes. Downregulation of ADORA2B suppressed IL-1β-induced chondrocyte injury. In terms of mechanism, USP14 positively regulated ADORA2B expression through deubiquitination. USP14 knockdown also inhibited IL-1β-induced chondrocyte apoptosis, inflammation, and ferroptosis, while these effects were overturned by ADORA2B overexpression.Conclusion:ADORA2B, mediated by the deubiquitination of USP14, accelerated IL-1β-induced chondrocyte apoptosis, inflammation, and ferroptosis to facilitate OA progression, providing a novel target for OA treatment.

1. Introduction

Osteoarthritis (OA) is a chronic joint disease characterized by articular cartilage degeneration and secondary hyperosteogeny.[1,2] OA is a whole joint disease, involving all joint tissues, including the infrapatellar fat pad, synovial membrane,[3] subchondral bone remodeling, and meniscal degeneration.[4,5] The specific manifestations of OA are joint swelling, pain, stiffness, and severe physical disability, which seriously affect the daily life of patients.[6–8] IL-1β, a member of the interleukin-1 family, has been shown to promote the inflammatory response of chondrocytes, inhibit the synthesis of cartilage matrix, promote the degradation of cartilage matrix, and induce apoptosis of chondrocytes.[9,10] IL-1β is often used to induce chondrocyte damage to construct an in vitro cellular model of OA.[11–13] Therefore, elucidating the mechanism of IL-1β-induced chondrocyte injury is critical in the search for potential targets of OA treatment.

Adenosine A2B receptor (ADORA2B) is a key factor involved in energy metabolism and has been confirmed to be associated with the progression of human diseases.[14–17] ADORA2B impaired cochlear myelination to accelerate the process of age-related sensorineural hearing loss.[18] Targeted inhibition of ADORA2B could reduce cell metastasis and promote cisplatin sensitivity in gastric cancer.[19] Moreover, downregulation of ADORA2B repressed myofibroblast differentiation and pulmonary fibrosis.[20] In an OA-related study, it had been reported that ADORA2B contributed to chondrocyte ferroptosis to accelerate OA progression.[21] Therefore, ADORA2B may be a potential target for OA treatment, and its role and molecular mechanism in chondrocyte injury deserve further exploration.

Ubiquitin-specific peptidases are the main members of the deubiquitinating enzyme family, which participate in the regulation of cell biological functions by deubiquitinating target proteins.[22–25] The abnormal expression of many members of the USP family is closely related to the development of human disease, including OA.[24,26–28] USP14 might facilitate ischemia/reperfusion-induced acute kidney injury, which could promote hypoxia/reoxygenation-induced kidney cell ferroptosis and inflammation.[29] Importantly, USP14 had been confirmed to aggravate IL-1β-induced chondrocyte dedifferentiation, thus accelerating OA progression.[30]

In this study, we aimed to reveal the role and underlying molecular mechanisms of ADORA2B in OA progression. Based on the above background, we hypothesized that ADORA2B, mediated by the deubiquitination of USP14, regulated IL-1β-induced chondrocyte injury to mediate OA progression. Our study may provide potential molecular targets for the treatment of OA.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Samples

A total of 27 OA patients underwent total knee arthroplasty in the Geriatric Hospital of Nanjing Medical University, Department of Orthopedic were recruited from February 2020 to August 2021, and their cartilage tissues were obtained for our study. Inclusion criteria: diagnosed with OA and not receiving any treatment. Exclusion criteria: patients with mental disorders, infectious diseases, cancers, and other inflammatory diseases. Meanwhile, 19 normal controls who underwent traumatic amputations in this hospital were recruited, and their cartilage tissues were collected as controls. The demographic and clinical data of the patients and the controls are shown in Table 1. All participants signed written informed consent. Our research was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Geriatric Hospital of Nanjing Medical University, Department of Orthopedics (2022-SR-268).

2.2. Cell culture and treatment

CHON-001 cells were purchased from ATCC (Manassas) and cultured in DMEM (Gibco, Grand Island) containing G-418 (0.1 mg/mL, Gibco) and FBS (10%, Gibco) at 37°C with 5% CO2. To mimic OA cell model, CHON-001 cells were treated with IL-1β (0, 5, 10 and 15 ng/mL, Abcam, Cambridge) for 24 hours. After reached 50% confluences, CHON-001 cells were transfected with the siRNAs against ADORA2B/USP14 (si-ADORA2B: F 5’-UUGAAAUAUACCAUGUAGCUC-3’, R 5’-GCUACAUGGUAUAUUUCAAUU-3’; si-USP14: F 5’-UCUGUAUUCAAUUCUACACCU-3’, R 5’-GUGUAGAAUUGAAUACAGAUG-3’), pcDNA ADORA2B/USP14 overexpression vector (ADORA2B/USP14), and their controls (si-NC: F 5’-GGAGUAGGGAGCAAACCUAUAGGAA-3’, R 5’-UUCCUAUAGGUUUGCUCCCUACUCC-3’; pcDNA) by Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen, Carlsbad) at 24 hours before 10 ng/mL IL-1β treatment. To measure the effect of USP14 knockdown on ADORA2B protein expression, CHON-001 cells transfected with si-NC/si-USP14 were treated with proteasome inhibitor MG132 (Yeasen, Shanghai, China) for 24 hours, followed by treated with IL-1β for 24 hours.

2.3. CCK8 assay

CHON-001 cells were reseeded into 96-well plates (2 × 103 cells/well) and cultured for 48 hours under normal conditions. After that, cells were treated with CCK8 solution (Dojindo, Kumamoto, Japan) for 2 hours. The absorbance was detected at 450 nm using a microplate reader (SpectraMax i3x, Molecular Devices, LLC, Sunnyvale) to analyze cell viability.

2.4. EdU assay

The EdU Assay Kit (RiboBio, Guangzhou, China) was used for this assay. Briefly, CHON-001 cells were seeded into 96-well plates (1 × 104 cells/well) for 24 hours, and then incubated with EdU solution for 2 hours. After fixed by 4% polyformaldehyde, cells were treated with glycine, TritonX-100, and Apollo solution one by one. Then, cell nuclei were stained using DAPI solution. Images were obtained under a fluorescence microscope (BX53, Olympus, Tokyo, Japan) to determine EdU positive cell rate.

2.5. Flow cytometry

CHON-001 cells were collected (5 × 105 cells) and washed with PBS. After suspended with binding buffer, cells were dyed with Annexin V-FITC and PI solution using corresponding kit (Beyotime, Shanghai, China). Cell apoptosis rate was evaluated by FACScalibur flow cytometer (BD Biosciences, San Jose) with CellQuest Pro software (BD Biosciences).

2.6. ELISA

Cell supernatants were collected by centrifugation at 500g for 5 minutes. Basing on the instructions of Human IL-6 ELISA Kit (PI330, Beyotime) and Human TNF-α ELISA Kit (PT518, Beyotime), CHON-001 cell supernatant was incubated with corresponding kits, respectively. The absorbance of cells was measured using a microplate reader, and IL-6 and TNF-α levels were assessed according to the standard curve.

2.7. Detection of ferroptosis

According to the kit instructions, the levels of ferroptosis-related markers (Fe2+, ROS, MDA, and GSH) in CHON-001 cells were detected by Iron Assay Kit (ab83366, Abcam), Cellular ROS Assay Kit (ab113851, Abcam), MDA Assay Kit (ab118970, Abcam), and GSH Assay Kit (ab239727, Abcam), respectively. CHON-001 cells were incubated with C11 BODIPY 581/591 (Invitrogen) for 30 minutes, followed by analyzed under a flow cytometry to determine lipid ROS.

2.8. Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR)

Total RNAs from cartilage tissues and CHON-001 cells were extracted by TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen), and RNA purity was detected by Nanodrop (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham). After reverse-transcribed using cDNA Synthesis Kit (Takara, Tokyo, Japan), PCR was performed in PCR system (CFX96, Bio-Rad, Hercules) using SYBR Green (Takara) with cDNA and specific primers as below: ADORA2B, F 5’-GTGGAGAATGCAGGAAGGAGA-3’, R 5’-ATGTTGAAAGCCACCTTGCG-3’; USP14, F 5’-TCCAGAAGAACCCTCAGCCA-3’, R 5’-TGAAGCTCTCAAGGCACCTG-3’; GAPDH, F 5’-TCGGAGTCAACGGATTTGGT-3’, R 5’-TTCCCGTTCTCAGCCTTGAC-3’. Experimental conditions were as below: 95°C for 5 minutes, 40 cycles of 95°C for 15 seconds, 55°C for 30 seconds, and 60°C for 60 seconds. The 2-∆∆Ct method was used for calculating relative ADORA2B and USP14 expression with GAPDH as internal control.

2.9. Western blot (WB)

Total proteins from CHON-001 cells were isolated by RIPA buffer containing protease and phosphatase inhibitor cocktail (Beyotime). After quantification, 30 μg protein samples were separated by 10% SDS-PAGE gel (Beyotime) and moved to PVDF membranes (Beyotime). After blockage with 5% nonfat milk for 2 hours, membranes were treated with anti-ADORA2B (1:1000, ab229671, Abcam), anti-USP14 (1:2000, ab192618, Abcam), anti-GPX4 (1:1000, ab125066, Abcam), anti-GAPDH (1:2500, ab9485, Abcam) and Goat anti-Rabbit IgG (1:2000, ab205718, Abcam) one by one. Protein bands were visualized by ECL reagent (Beyotime), and gray value was evaluated using ImageJ software to analyze relative ADORA2B and USP14 protein expression.

2.10. Co-IP assay

CHON-001 cells were transfected with Flag-ADORA2B, HA-Ub, si-NC, and si-USP14 using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen). After 24 hours, cells were collected with RIPA lysis buffer (Beyotime), and cell lysates (300 μL) were incubated with anti-ADORA2B (1:100, KM0430415, KEMOBio, Zhejiang, China) and anti-Ub (1:100, ARG54630, Arigo, Shanghai, China) overnight at 4°C, followed by treated with protein A/G agarose (Beyotime) for 6 hours. After washing with buffer, the immunoprecipitated protein was eluted and used for WB.

2.11. Cycloheximide (CHX) treatment

CHON-001 cells transfected with pcDNA vector or USP14 overexpression vector were incubated with 50 mg/mL CHX solution (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis) for indicated time points (0, 5, 10, and 15 hours). Cells were collected for WB analysis to detect ADORA2B protein expression.

2.12. Statistical analysis

When evaluating results and analyzing data, personnel do not know in advance which group the data belongs to. All data are presented as the mean ± SD and analyzed using GraphPad Prism 7.0 software. Student t test or ANOVA followed by Tukey post hoc test was performed to analyze data. P < .05 was considered significantly different.

3. Results

3.1. IL-1β treatment promoted apoptosis, inflammation, and ferroptosis in chondrocytes

Firstly, CHON-001 cells were induced with IL-1β to explore the regulation of IL-1β on chondrocyte injury. The viability and EdU positive cell rate of CHON-001 cells were reduced with the increasing of IL-1β concentration (Fig. 1A–B). Also, IL-1β treatment promoted the apoptosis rate, IL-6 and TNF-α levels in CHON-001 cells in a dose-dependent manner (Fig. 1C–E). As shown in Fig. 1F–K, treatment of different concentrations of IL-1β significantly increased Fe2+, ROS, lipid ROS and MDA levels, while repressed GSH and GPX4 levels in CHON-001 cells. Thus, IL-1β reduced chondrocyte proliferation, while accelerated apoptosis, inflammation and ferroptosis.

IL-1β treatment promoted chondrocyte injury. CHON-001 cells were treated with 0, 5, 10 and 15 ng/mL of IL-1β. Cell proliferation and apoptosis were examined using CCK8 assay (A), EdU assay (B) and flow cytometry (C–D). (E) ELISA was used to examine IL-6 and TNF-α levels. (F–J) Fe2+, ROS, lipid ROS, MDA and GSH levels were determined to assess cell ferroptosis. (K) GPX4 protein level was examined by WB. *P < .05. qRT-PCR = Quantitative real-time PCR.

3.2. ADORA2B expression in OA patients and IL-1β-induced chondrocytes

Next, we explored the expression of ADORA2B in OA patients and IL-1β-induced chondrocytes. Through qRT-PCR, ADORA2B was found to be overexpressed in the cartilage tissues of OA patients compared to normal controls (Fig. 2A). Moreover, we detected high ADORA2B expression in IL-1β-induced chondrocytes at the mRNA and protein levels, and its expression was gradually enhanced with increasing IL-1β concentration (Fig. 2B–C). Therefore, we believed that high ADORA2B expression might be related to OA progression.

ADORA2B expression in OA patients and IL-1β-induced chondrocytes. (A) ADORA2B mRNA expression was detected by qRT-PCR in the cartilage tissues of OA patients and normal controls. (B–C) ADORA2B mRNA and protein levels were measured using qRT-PCR and WB analysis in CHON-001 cells treated with 0, 5, 10 and 15 ng/mL of IL-1β. *P < .05. qRT-PCR = Quantitative real-time PCR.

3.3. ADORA2B knockdown reduced IL-1β-induced chondrocyte injury

To confirm the role of ADORA2B in OA progression, si-ADORA2B or si-ADORA2B#1 was transfected into CHON-001 cells to reduce ADORA2B protein expression (Fig. 3A and Fig. S1A, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/R753). Following, CHON-001 cells transfected with si-NC/si-ADORA2B/si-ADORA2B#1 were treated with IL-1β. Silencing of ADORA2B promoted the viability, accelerated EdU positive cell rate and reduced apoptosis rate of IL-1β-induced CHON-001 cells (Fig. 3B–E and Fig. S1B, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/R753). In the absence of IL-1β, alone ADORA2B knockdown had no effect on the CHON-001 cell viability and EdU positive cell rate (Fig. S2A–B, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/R753). Also, ADORA2B knockdown reduced IL-6, TNF-α, Fe2+, ROS, lipid ROS and MDA levels, while enhanced GSH and GPX4 levels in IL-1β-induced CHON-001 cells (Fig. 3F–L and Fig. S1C–E, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/R753). All data revealed that ADORA2B might contribute to OA progression.

CCK8 assay (B), EdU assay (C) and flow cytometry (D–E) were performed to examine cell proliferation and apoptosis. (F) IL-6 and TNF-α levels were determined using ELISA. 3.4. USP14 affected ADORA2B expression

USP14 protein level was gradually upregulated with the increase of IL-1β treatment concentration (Fig. 4A). Also, USP14 was confirmed to be upregulated in the cartilage tissues of OA patients (Fig. 4B). The transfection of si-USP14 or si-USP14#1 was used to reduce USP14 expression in CHON-001 cells (Fig. 4C and Fig. S3A, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/R753). Then, we examined the effect of USP14 on ADORA2B expression, and found that USP14 knockdown reduced ADORA2B protein expression but not affected its mRNA expression (Fig. 4D–E). Treatment with proteasome inhibitor MG132 reversed the inhibitory effect of si-USP14 on ADORA2B protein expression in IL-1β-induced CHON-001 cells (Fig. 4F), indicating that USP14 increased ADORA2B protein expression through deubiquitination. Furthermore, Co-IP assay showed that knockdown of USP14 increased ADORA2B ubiquitination in CHON-001 cells (Fig. 4G). The transfection of USP14 overexpression vector was used to increase USP14 protein expression (Fig. 4H). CHX treatment revealed that USP14 overexpression markedly enhanced the protein stability of ADORA2B (Fig. 4I). These results suggested that USP14 interacted with ADORA2B by deubiquitination.

3.5. Downregulation of USP14 inhibited IL-1β-induced chondrocyte injury by regulating ADORA2B

To further confirm that USP14 regulated ADORA2B to mediate OA progression, we performed rescue experiments. ADORA2B overexpression vector was used to increase ADORA2B expression in CHON-001 cells (Fig. 5A). Then, CHON-001 cells transfected with si-NC/si-USP14/pcDNA/ADORA2B were treated with IL-1β. USP14 knockdown increased cell viability and EdU-positive cell rate in IL-1β-induced CHON-001 cells, while these effects were reversed by ADORA2B overexpression (Fig. 5B–C). Similarly, upregulation of ADORA2B also abolished the reducing effect of si-USP14 on apoptosis rate, IL-6, TNF-α, Fe2+, ROS, lipid ROS and MDA levels, as well as the increasing effect on GSH and GPX4 levels, in IL-1β-induced CHON-001 cells (Fig. 5D–L). In addition, consistent with the effect of si-USP14, another siRNA for USP14 (si-USP14#1) also could promote cell viability, and suppress IL-6, TNF-α, Fe2+, and ROS levels in IL-1β-induced CHON-001 cells (Fig. S3B–E, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/R753). Therefore, we believed that USP14-mediated ADORA2B promoted OA progression.

4. Discussion

Studies have shown that the occurrence of OA is mediated by a number of pro-inflammatory factors, of which IL-1β is the most important one.[31] Chondrocytes are the only cell type in cartilage tissue and act a decisive function in keeping articular cartilage function.[32,33] In the present study, IL-1β treatment promoted chondrocyte apoptosis, inflammation, and ferroptosis, indicating that IL-1β-induced chondrocyte injury was successful.

ADORA2B is a G protein-coupled adenosine receptor, which can participate in regulating adenylate cyclase activity in the existing of adenosine.[34,35] Salvianolic acid B inhibited high glucose-induced kidney tubule cell injury to relieve diabetic nephropathy by reducing ADORA2B expression.[36] Upregulation of ADORA2B contributes to pulmonary fibrosis development by producing profibrotic mediators.[37] In addition, ADORA2B expression in the hippocampus was also associated with cognitive function.[38] Although Li et al revealed the regulation of ADORA2B on chondrocyte viability and ferroptosis,[21] the relevant research on its role in OA progression is still limited. Here, we discovered the high ADORA2B expression in OA patients and IL-1β-induced chondrocytes. In functional experiments, we suggested that silencing of ADORA2B could relieve IL-1β-induced chondrocyte apoptosis, inflammation, and ferroptosis, confirming that ADORA2B played a positive role in OA progression. These results provide new evidence for ADORA2B as a potential target for OA treatment.

USP14 deubiquitinated IDO1 to stabilize its expression, thus enhancing colorectal cancer cell tryptophan metabolism and immune suppression.[39] USP14 could promote LPS-induced goat mammary epithelial cell ferroptosis and inflammation by increasing IL-6 expression through deubiquitination.[40] USP14 accelerated the foam cell formation to facilitate atherosclerosis, which was achieved by deubiquitinating and stabilizing CD36.[41] The above reports support the notion that USP14 regulates protein expression by deubiquitination to mediate disease progression. In OA, USP14 increased IκBα deubiquitination to exacerbate IL-1β-induced chondrocyte dedifferentiation and OA progression.[30] In this, we determined that USP14 promoted ADORA2B protein level via deubiquitination, and rescue experiments further revealed that USP14 knockdown restrained LPS-induced IL-1β-induced chondrocyte apoptosis, inflammation and ferroptosis by decreasing ADORA2B expression. These results supported the hypothesis that the USP14/ADORA2B axis contributed to OA progression.

Of course, there are limitations to our study. In general, the molecular mechanism of the interaction between USP14 and ADORA2B was not fully described. At present, we have only explored the role of the USP14/ADORA2B axis in promoting IL-1β-induced chondrocyte apoptosis, ferroptosis, and inflammation to accelerate OA progression, but the downstream signaling pathways and regulatory networks that may be involved in the USP14/ADORA2B axis have not been explored. Previous studies have shown that ADORA2B promotes myofibroblast differentiation and pulmonary fibrosis by activating the MAPK pathway.[20] Besides, ADORA2B promoted chondrocyte ferroptosis by inhibiting PI3K/Akt pathway in OA mice.[21] In the future, we may explore the downstream regulatory network of ADORA2B by targeting the MAPK and PI3K/Akt pathways to further clarify its specific role in the pathogenesis of OA. Besides, our study only used IL-1β-induced chondrocyte injury to mimic OA cell model, which is not sufficient. There are also in vitro studies that use conditioned medium from macrophage to induce OA-chondrocytes inflammation.[42] Therefore, more cell models are needed to validate our findings in the future. In addition, we have only studied at the cellular level and have not yet performed in vivo experiments. Further animal experiments are necessary in the future, which may provide more evidence that the USP14/ADORA2B axis regulates OA progression.

5. Conclusion

To sum up, our study suggests a novel molecular axis for the regulation of OA progression. Our data showed that USP14-mediated deubiquitination of ADORA2B promoted IL-1β-induced chondrocyte apoptosis, inflammation, and ferroptosis to aggravate the process of OA. Our study for the first time reveals the role of the USP14/ADORA2B axis in OA progression, and these results indicate that targeted inhibition of the USP14/ADORA2B axis may be an effective initiative to alleviate OA progression. Collectively, these findings have important implications for developing the potential therapeutic targets of OA.

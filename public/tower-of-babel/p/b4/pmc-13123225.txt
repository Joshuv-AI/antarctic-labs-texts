Multi-omics analysis and experimental validation uncovers prognosis significance of IKBIP in patients with hepatocellular carcinoma: a multicenter cohort study
Authors: Yuanjun Jiao, Lingling Guo, Yubin Lu, Mengqing Li, Yanfeng Zhong, Bo Wu, Hanxing Dong, Ting Wang, Erbao Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundI Kappa B Kinase Interacting Protein (IKBIP) has been reported to promote tumor progression in diverse cancers. however, the role of IKBIP in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) has remained unclear.MethodsMulti-omics analysis was performed using data from publicly databases, to systematically assess the expression patterns, prognostic value and immune landscape of IKBIP in HCC. The prognostic value of IKBIP was further verified using immunohistochemical (IHC) examination in two independent clinical cohorts. Furthermore, human HCC cell lines (Huh7, MHCC-97 H and HepG2) were knocked down for IKBIP using small-interfering RNA (siRNA), and cell proliferation, and migration ability were assessed. RNA-sequencing was performed on Huh7 cells with IKBIP knockdown to explore the effect of IKBIP in HCC cells. A nomogram was constructed by combining IKBIP expression and clinicopathological parameters to predict prognosis for individual patient. Functional enrichment analysis was performed to identify key pathways associated with IKBIP. Immune infiltration analyses were conducted to explore the relationship between IKBIP expression and immune microenvironment.ResultsThe bioinformatics analyses indicated that IKBIP expression was significantly upregulated in tumor tissue and correlated with unfavorable prognosis. IHC experimental in both the Peking University Shenzhen Hospital (PKUSZ) and Shanghai Outdo cohorts demonstrated that IKBIP overexpression was associated with poorer survival. We found that IKBIP knockdown significantly inhibits HCC cell proliferation, colony formation, and migration in vitro. RNA-sequencing on IKBIP-knockdown Huh7 cells suggested that IKBIP played a role in promoting cell proliferation. The univariable and multivariable Cox analysis revealed that IKBIP expression was an independent factor for overall survival and diseases free survival. The nomogram by incorporating the IKBIP and clinicopathological features showed good performance in predicting prognosis. GSEA analysis revealed that IKBIP was associated with the cell cycle-related, epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition and angiogenesis pathways. We also found that IKBIP expression holds potential for predicting immunotherapeutic benefits.ConclusionsThe comprehensive multi-omics analysis and experimental findings demonstrate that IKBIP may be a biomarker with prognostic and functional significance in HCC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12876-026-04756-y.Keywords: Hepatocellular carcinoma, IKBIP, Prognosis prediction, Clinical cohorts

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) ranks as the sixth most frequently diagnosed malignancy and the third leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide [1]. Hepatectomy is the primary method for curing HCC [2, 3]. Despite significant improvements in the diagnosis and treatment, the prognosis of HCC remains unsatisfactory, with 5-year recurrence rates are up to 70%, and the 5-year overall survival (OS) rate is only approximately 25%–55% [4–7]. Currently, the molecular mechanisms and development of HCC remain unknown [8–13] and clinicopathological parameters fail to provide adequate information for accurate prediction of prognosis. Therefore, there is an urgent need to discover novel biomarkers and therapeutic targets to facilitate individualized therapy and improved outcomes for patients with HCC.

I Kappa B Kinase Interacting Protein (IKBIP), also known as IKIP, is located on the human chromosome 12 [14]. IKBIP is found to be one of the target genes of p53 and plays an important role in proapoptotic function [14]. IKBIP inhibits NF-κB activation and inflammation via inhibition of IKKα/β phosphorylation [15]. Accumulating evidence suggests that IKBIP could be a predictive biomarker for prognosis and therapeutic response in multiple cancers [16–22], including papillary renal cell carcinoma, esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, glioma and glioblastoma. Li et al. found that IKBIP overexpression promoted the proliferation and invasion of colon cancer cells [23]. However, the role of IKBIP in HCC remains unknown. Deciphering the specific role and clinical significance of IKBIP in HCC will enhance our understanding of HCC pathogenesis and aid in the design of targeted molecular therapies.

In this study, we investigated the expression pattern, prognostic value, immune landscape and possible biological functions of IKBIP in public databases. IKBIP expression was verified through immunohistochemical examination of two clinical cohorts (PKUSZ and Outdo cohorts). Moreover, the function of IKBIP in HCC was investigated in in vitro experiments using three human HCC cell lines. RNA-sequencing on IKBIP-knockdown Huh7 cells to validate the bioinformatics findings. In addition, we constructed and validated two nomograms by combining IKBIP expression and clinical characteristics, to predict prognosis in HCC for clinical use.

Methods

Clinical samples

In this retrospective study, HCC tissues were collected form two cohorts (PKUSZ cohort and Outdo cohort). A total of 144 HCC cancer tissues and corresponding adjacent non-tumor samples were collected from patients who underwent hepatectomy between January 2015 and December 2018 at Peking University Shenzhen Hospital. Next, representative sections and corresponding blocks were chosen for constructing tissue microarray (TMA, name as PKUSZ) [18]. Patients baseline data, including age, sex, HBV, liver cirrhosis, AFP, tumor size, tumor number, tumor encapsulation, tumor differentiation, microvascular invasion (MVI), TNM stage and survival data (DFS and OS), were collected for analysis. The Outdo cohort originates from the Shanghai Outdo Biotech Co., Ltd. and comprises 93 HCC tissues and 87 adjacent tissues, including 87 paired tissues. Patients baseline information, including age, sex, liver cirrhosis, tumor size, tumor number, tumor differentiation, TNM stage and follow-up data (OS), were provided by Shanghai Outdo BioTech. Ethical approvals for the study were granted by Ethics Committees of Peking University Shenzhen Hospital (approval number: 2022 − 164) and Shanghai Outdo Biotech Company (approval number: SHYJS-CP-1804007). The inclusion criteria were patients with histologically diagnosed HCC who underwent radical hepatectomy, and completed clinicopathological and follow-up information.

Immunohistochemistry

The primary antibody used for immunohistochemistry (IHC) was anti-IKBIP (1:200, Proteintech, Cat#14589-1-AP). IHC staining was performed on TMAs according to the standardized procedure, using normal liver tissue as a control. Then, stained TMAs were scanned by Pannoramic 250 Flash Slide Scanners. ImageJ software (National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD, USA) was used to calculate the IHC score of each sample based on the staining intensity and area.

Bulk transcriptomic analysis

We obtained mRNA levels of IKBIP in pan-cancer and corresponding normal tissues from the TCGA and the GTEx database. The ICGC LIHC dataset was also downloaded for validation (ICGC, https://dcc.icgc.org/, release 28, accessed on 7 May 2024). Moreover, three GEO datasets with available survival and clinical data were enrolled in the meta-cohort (metaGEO) for validation: GSE144269 (n = 68), GSE148335 (n = 52) and GSE116174 (n = 64). The “ComBat” method was used to remove the batch effects among different GEO datasets using “SVA” package.

Differentially expressed genes in LIHC patients with low and high IKBIP expression were screened using “limma” package, applying a screening criterion of |Log2FC| >2 and adjusted P < 0.05. Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) analysis was performed to investigate the potential biological differences between IKBIP-high and IKBIP-low group based on reference gene sets. The gene set “h.all.v7.4.symbols.gmt” was chosen for GSEA analysis. False discovery rate (FDR) < 0.25, P < 0.05, and normalized enrichment score (NES) > 1 was defined as statistically significant.

We determine the immune cell infiltration levels of each sample using EPIC, CIBERSORT and ssGSEA algorithms. To evaluate the differentiation between tumor immune dysfunction and exclusion (TIDE) scores, we uploaded the normalized gene expression matrix to the TIDE website (http://tide.dfci.harvard.edu) and the Wilcoxon test was utilized to compare the estimates of different IKBIP expression groups.

Prognostic value and clinical implications of IKBIP

Correlations between IKBIP expression and clinicopathological features in LIHC patients were analyzed using the Wilcoxon tests followed by visualization through the “ggplot2” package. The optimal cutoff value of IKBIP expression was established using maximally selected rank statistics, dividing patients in the training cohort into high- and low-IKBIP groups. The Kaplan-Meier method and log-rank test were used to estimate DFS and OS between IKBIPhigh and IKBIPlow expression group.

Cell culture and transfection

HepG2, Huh7, and MHCC-97 H cell lines were obtained from the laboratory’s long-term stock. All cell lines were maintained in DMEM medium (Gibco, USA). All the mediums were supplemented with 10% fetal calf serum and 1% Penicillin/Streptomycin at 37 °C in incubators containing 5% CO2. Mycoplasma testing of all cells was regularly completed using a Mycoplasma Detection kit (Solarbio, Beijing, China). Small interfering RNA (siRNA) of IKBIP were purchased from RiboBio (Guangzhou, China), and transfected into cells using Lipofectamine 3000.

Cell viability and colony formation assay

Cell suspensions (3 × 103 cells per well) were seeded into 96-well plates, and cell proliferation was measured at 24, 48, and 72 h. Then, 10 µl of CCK-8 reagent (Dojindo, Japan) was added to each well, and the plates were incubated for 2 h. The absorbance at 450 nm was then measured using a microplate reader. A total of 1 × 103 cells were seeded into 6-well plates and cultured for 14 days, with medium changes every 3 days.

Afterward, the plates were washed three times with PBS, fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde, and stained with 1% crystal violet. Colony numbers were then counted and recorded.

Cell migration assay

Transwell chambers were used to perform migration assays. A total of 1 × 104 cells were added to the upper chambers; and 800 µl of medium containing 10% FBS was added to the lower chamber. After 48 h incubation, the cells on the lower surface of the membrane were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 20 min, and stained with 0.1% crystal violet for 15 min. Next, 3 randomly selected microscopic fields per well were quantified with a light microscope. The number of migratory cells was counted.

Scratch wound healing assay

The cells were seeded into 6-well plates (5 × 105 cells/well). Once the cells were attached to the wall and the confluence reached 100%, a vertical scratch was made in the transfected cells using a 200 µL pipette tip. Following three washes with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), the cells were cultured in fresh complete medium Then, the cells were washed with PBS 3 times, the scratched cells were removed, and fresh media without serum was added. Using an optical microscope, the widths of the scratch lines were measured and photographed (Leica, Wetzlar, Germany, DMI8) at 0 and 96 h later.

qRT‑PCR and western blot analysis

Total RNA was isolated from cells using 0.5 mL Trizol, and reverse transcription was performed using the Prime-Script RT Master Mix (Vazyme, China). qRT-PCR was performed using SYBR Green Realtime PCR Master Mix (Vazyme, China). Samples from each experiment were independently repeated three times. The sequences were as follows: IKBIP: Forward, CATGTCTTTGATGACCCAGTTTG, Reverse, CATTTCTTCTAGGCTTCTCTTCCAG.

For protein extraction, cells were lysed using RIPA buffer (Beyotime, Shanghai, China, P0013B). Protein concentrations were determined using a BCA protein assay kit (Beyotime, Shanghai, China). Western blotting was performed according to previously published methods [24]. The primary antibodies used in this study were as follows: anti-IKBIP (Proteintech, Cat#14589-1-AP,1:200) and anti-GAPDH antibody (ServiceBio, China, GB15004-100, 1:500).

RNA‑sequencing and analysis

Total RNA isolated from Huh7 cells transfected with siCtrl and siIKBIP was subjected to paired-end RNA-seq using an Illumina HiSeq 2000 system based on the manufacturer’ s instructions. Read mapping and data analysis were completed for genes that were differentially expressed between the two groups (|log2FoldChange| ≥ 0.585 and adjusted p-value < 0.05).

Development and validation of the nomograms for prognosis prediction

The clinicopathological characteristics and IKBIP expression were included into the univariate Cox regression analyses for DFS and OS, and variables with P < 0.05 were selected in multivariate Cox regression analyses. The backwards stepwise regression was utilized to detect the independent predictors. Two predictive nomograms based on independent predictors were developed to predict DFS and OS. The discrimination of nomograms was quantified using time-dependent receiver operating characteristic curve (ROC) and the area under the ROC (AUROC). The calibration curves were plotted to compare the estimated survival probabilities with the actual probabilities. To assess the clinical usefulness, decision curve analyses (DCA) were performed to evaluate the net benefits at different threshold probabilities.

Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) analysis

Ten samples from GSE149614 were selected to perform scRNA-seq, consisting of 31,615 cells. The samples were merged utilizing the R package “Seurat” and cells were filtrated based on the following criteria: (1) genes must be expressed in at least three cells; (2) cells must express at least 50 genes; (3) mitochondrial gene expression must not exceed 5%. The “Harmony” package was used to eliminate batch effects between samples. The Top 2000 high variable features of the dataset were applied to perform a principal component analysis, and the top 30 principal components were used for downstream analyses. The cell subpopulations were identified using the “FindClusters” function (resolution = 0.1) and visualized using the UMAP method. Then, cell clusters were annotated based on classical maker genes from published studies.

Spatial transcriptomic analysis

Spatial transcriptomics (ST) data was downloaded from Mendeley Data (skrx2fz79n) and analyzed using Seurat (version 4.0.3), using similar method as scRNA-seq. The “SpatialFeaturePlot” function was used to map the expression of IKBIP across the microregions. To elucidate the cellular composition of each spot, the single-cell data were predicted on ST data using “FindTransferAnchors” and “TransferData” functions. Subsequently, we defined spots with the highest tumor probability as “malignant regions”, while the remaining spots were categorized as “non-malignant regions”. The “SpatialDimPlot” function was employed to visualize the most prevalent cellular component in each microregion.

Statistical analyses

Continuous variables were analyzed using the t test, and categorical variables were analyzed using the χ2 test. Survival curves were generated using the Kaplan–Meier method and compared using the log-rank test. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression were used to construct a Cox proportional risk model. The Wilcoxon rank-sum tests were employed to analyze the differences between two groups. Statistical analyses were performed using R 4.1.3 (R Project for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria, www.r-project.org). Differences with a 2-sided P < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

Expression landscape of IKBIP in pan-cancer and LIHC

Utilizing pan-cancer data from TCGA and GTEx database, we observed a significant upregulation of IKBIP in various cancers, including LIHC (Fig. 1A). Additionally, we demonstrated that IKBIP mRNA levels were significantly elevated in tumor tissues compared to normal tissues using TCGA and ICGC cohort (Fig. 1B-E).

To explore the distribution characteristics of IKBIP in the tumor microenvironment, we performed scRNA-seq and ST analysis. Six major cell groups were identified using scRNA-seq data (Fig. S1A). IKBIP was widely expressed in hepatocytes, B cells, myeloid cells fibroblasts and endothelial cells, with higher expression levels in malignant cells (Figure S1B, C). Spatial transcriptomics analysis showed a distinct spatial distribution of IKBIP in the tumor microenvironment, and confirmed a pronounced increase in IKBIP levels in the malignant cells (Figure S2A, B).

Prognostic significance of IKBIP in publicly databases

Patients were categorized into high- and low-expression groups according to the median or optimal cutoff value of IKBIP expression. The TCGA database indicated that patients with high IKBIP expression had a worse overall survival (P = 0.001, Fig. 2A), which were validated in both ICGC-LIHC and meta-GEO cohorts (Fig. 2B, C). In addition, the expression of IKBIP in HCC was found to correlate with various clinicopathological characteristics. Patients showed more advanced TNM stage, grade, and T stage in the IKBIPhigh expression group compared with IKBIPlow expression group in TCGA cohort (Fig. 2D), which suggested that IKBIP expression may influence cancer development and metastasis.

Expression and prognostic value of IKBIP in clinical cohorts

Furthermore, we validated the expression and prognostic value of IKBIP in two clinical cohorts. Inter-observer agreement for IKBIP IHC scoring was compared using Kappa statistics, with Kappa values of 0.75 for PKUSZ cohort and 0.73 for Outdo cohort. IHC results verified a considerably higher IKBIP expression in tumor samples compared to adjacent normal liver tissues in both PKUSZ cohort (P < 0.001, Fig. 3A) and Outdo cohort (P < 0.001, Fig. 3B). Representative images of different staining intensities of IKBIP were shown in Fig. 3C.

Expression pattern of IKBIP in our clinical cohorts. The differential expression of IKBIP in paired normal tissues and HCC tissues from PKUSZ cohort (A) and Outdo cohort (B). C The typical IHC staining images of IKBIP in normal liver tissues and HCC tissues

Table 1 showed the clinicopathological factors of patients between the low and high IKBIP groups in PKUSZ cohort. This cohort included 144 patients, 62 of whom had low IKBIP expression and 82 of whom had high expression. There were no statistically significant differences in age, sex, HBsAg, liver cirrhosis, tumor number, tumor capsule, vascular invasion, microvascular invasion (MVI) and TNM stage. However, IKBIPhigh expression group had significant higher serum AFP levels (P = 0.007) and larger tumor size (P = 0.022) than IKBIPlow expression group. IKBIPhigh expression group had a significantly worse DFS and OS compared with IKBIPlow expression group in PKUSZ cohort, with HRs of 1.781 (95%CI, 1.166–2.720, P = 0.007) and 1.743 (95%CI, 1.145–2.654, P = 0.009), respectively (Fig. 4A, B). In Outdo cohort (Table S1), patients in low IKBIP expression group had a significantly prolonged OS compared with those in high IKBIP expression group (HR: 2.273, 95%CI, 1.386–4.062, P = 0.002, Fig. 4C).

Knockdown of IKBIP suppresses HCC cells proliferation and migration

As the prognostic value of IKBIP had been validated, we then explored IKBIP’s role in cell proliferation and migration. First, Western blot and qRT-PCR analyses were used to confirm that the IKBIP expression was inhibited by the siRNA. In three HCC cell lines (Huh7, MHCC-97 H and HepG2), siIKBIP transfection suppressed IKBIP expression (Fig. 5A, B). Second, the effect of IKBIP knockdown on the proliferation and migration of HCC cells was investigated. We observed that IKBIP knockdown significantly inhibited proliferation capacity in three HCC cell lines using CCK-8 and colony formation assays (Fig. 5C, D). Transwell migration assay for these three HCC cell lines suggested fewer cells in the siIKBIP group compared to the control group (Fig. 6A), indicating a significant inhibitory effect of siIKBIP on migration. Scratch wound healing assays showed that the migration capacity of Huh7, HepG2 and MHCC-97 H lines was significantly reduced after IKBIP knockdown (Fig. 6B). To further explore the underlying mechanisms of IKBIP in HCC cells, we conducted RNA sequencing on Huh7-siIKBIP cells compared to negative controls. Applying a threshold of |log2FoldChange| ≥ 0.585 and adjusted p-value < 0.05, we identified a total of 255 up-regulated and 225 down-regulated DEGs. Subsequently, functional enrichment analysis was conducted using gene ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG). The top 10 GO items and KEGG pathways were selected according to adjusted p-value. The top 10 GO terms of biological process (BP), cellular component (CC), and molecular function (MF) were presented in Fig. 7A. We found that these genes were associated with “extracellular structure organization”, “extracellular matrix organization”, “negative regulation of developmental growth” and “negative regulation of cell growth”. The top 10 enriched pathways were shown in Fig. 7B, and it showed that the enriched biological pathways were related to tumor growth and development, including IL-17 signaling pathway, TNF signaling pathway and ECM-receptor interaction pathway.

Construction and validation of a nomogram based on IKBIP

In clinical practice, nomograms are primarily used to combine predictors for prognosis prediction. Two predictive nomograms were constructed by integrating IKBIP expression and other independent risk factors selected by multivariate Cox analyses to predict DFS and OS for HCC patients (Fig. 8A, B). These two nomograms demonstrated satisfactory performance for prognosis estimation, with the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) at 1, 3 and 5 years were 0.751 (95% CI: 0.640–0.863), 0.728 (95% CI: 0.625 − 0.831) and 0.684 (95% CI: 0.662 − 0.890) for DFS, and 0.751 (95% CI: 0.640–0.863), 0.728 (95% CI: 0.625 − 0.831) and 0.684 (95% CI: 0.662 − 0.890) for OS respectively (Fig. S3A, B). The calibration curves showed good agreement between the actual and predicted survival outcomes at 1-, 3-, and 5-year prognosis (Fig. S3C, D). Finally, the decision curve analysis revealed that using the nomogram to predict DFS and OS provided more net benefits than using the treat all scheme or treat none scheme, showing that the nomograms was clinically applicable (Fig. S3E, F). The nomogram for predicting OS was validated in Outdo cohort, and yield good performance as well (Fig. S4).

Potential functional roles of IKBIP in HCC

GSEA analyses were conducted to explore the potential functions of IKBIP using TCGA data. In the enrichment analysis of cancer hallmark gene sets, we found that 24 pathways, including E2F targets, G2M checkpoint, mitotic spindles, epithelial-mesenchymal transition, angiogenesis and TGF-β signaling were significantly activated in patients with IKBIP overexpression (Fig. S5A, B). Furthermore, the specific functions of IKBIP in malignant cells and fibroblasts were analyzed using single-cell RNA seq data. In malignant cells, GSEA results showed that elevated IKBIP expression is associated with the activation of E2F target genes and G2M checkpoint. Epithelial-mesenchymal transition and UV response DN were activated in fibroblasts with IKBIP overexpression. Additionally, the correlations between cancer hallmarks and IKBIP expression were analyzed in pan-cancer data, and the results showed that epithelial-mesenchymal transition, IL6 JAK STAT3 signaling were frequently enriched in the high-IKBIP group, which indicated that IKBIP was positively related to these oncogenic pathways (Fig. S5C).

Correlations between IKBIP and immunotherapy response

We calculated the immune infiltration levels using multiple algorithms. The EPIC results showed that high IKBIP expression group had higher CD4+ and CD8+ T cells infiltration levels, while low endothelial cells and macrophages infiltration levels (Fig. 9A). Correlation analysis revealed positive associations between IKBIP expression and CD4+ and CD8+ T cells infiltration levels (Fig. 9B, C). The CIBERSORT and ssGSEA results were shown in Fig. S6A-B.

B-E Correlations between IKBIP expression and CD4 + T cells, CD8 + T cells, endothelial cells and macrophages. F Rates of response to immunotherapy in IKBIPhigh and IKBIPlow expression groups. In contrast, negative correlations were observed with macrophages and endothelial cells (Fig. 9D, E). To further study the value of IKBIP in predicting immunotherapy responsiveness, we used TIDE algorithm to predict the immunotherapeutic response rate of patients with different IKBIP expressions from the TCGA-LIHC dataset. Only 20.9% of patients in the IKBIPhigh expression group responded to immunotherapy compared with 38.5% in the IKBIPlow group (Fig. 9F). Patients responded to immunotherapy had significantly low IKBIP expression compared to non-responder (Fig. 9G). Moreover, we observed significant differences in the predicted scores for immune-related signatures such as dysfunction and exclusion between different IKBIP expression groups (Fig. 9H). Then, we incorporated a real-world cohort (GSE202069), which includes 17 HCC patients who received immunotherapy. The results showed that patients with low IKBIP expression exhibited a higher response rate to immunotherapy than those with high IKBIP expression, though the difference was not statistically significant (Fig. S7). These findings indicated that IKBIP may serve as a potential biomarker for immunotherapy efficacy.

Discussion

HCC is the most common liver malignancy, characterized by high mortality and recurrence rates [1, 24–26]. Identifying reliable prognostic biomarkers is essential for optimizing treatment decisions. Recent studies suggest that IKBIP functions as a prognostic marker in various cancers, such as papillary renal cell carcinoma, digestive malignancies and nervous system tumors [16–23]. However, a systematic investigation of IKBIP expression in HCC and its prognostic implications in HCC is lacking. This study bridges this gap by leveraging a comprehensive approach that integrates multi-omics bioinformatics, functional assays in vitro, and analysis of clinical samples to delineate the role of IKBIP in HCC. The aim is to clarify IKBIP’s prognostic significance, explore its potential functions in HCC, and establish prediction models for clinical use. This study represents the first extensive examination of IKBIP in HCC, potentially offering a path toward improved risk stratification and treatment strategies.

This study systematically uncovered the pivotal role of IKBIP in HCC through a combination of clinical data, bioinformatics and phenotypic analysis. We first investigated the clinical significance of IKBIP in HCC, utilizing clinical samples and multi-omics data. The results demonstrated that IKBIP expression is significantly elevated in HCC tissues compared to normal tissues. In the PKUSZ cohort, we found that the high expression of IKBIP was significantly associated with higher serum AFP levels. Serum AFP level is the most universally used biomarker for HCC. However, the underlying mechanisms by which AFP upregulates cell proliferation and tumor growth remain unknown. Therefore, further elucidation of the molecular mechanism of IKBIP and AFP is warranted to clarify the association between IKBIP and AFP. Analysis of single-cell expression and spatial expression patterns validated the diagnostic value of IKBIP. Prognostic evaluations revealed that increased IKBIP expression is associated with worse survival outcomes, highlighting its independent prognostic significance in HCC. Importantly, in vitro experiments revealed that IKBIP knockdown significantly inhibited the proliferation and migration of HCC cells. Additionally, several molecular pathways potentially correlated with IKBIP were identified. GSEA analyses using TCGA data showed that elevated IKBIP expression is associated with the activation of key oncogenic pathways, such as EMT, angiogenesis, TGF-β signaling, E2F target genes and G2M checkpoint. These pathways play pivotal roles in tumor progression. EMT is a process where cells undergo a transition from an epithelial to a mesenchymal state, drives metastasis by reducing cell adhesion and enhancing motility, thereby facilitating cell dissemination in HCC [27–31]. Hu et al. found that IKBIP overexpression promoted the development of ESCC by the activation of the AKT signaling pathway [17]. It has been reported that activated AKT further phosphorylates the GSK-3β protein, inhibits the expression of E-cadherin, and promotes the process of EMT, which results in tumor migration and invasion [32]. Angiogenesis, the formation of new blood vessels, provides tumors with the necessary nutrients and oxygen, further promoting tumor growth and metastasis [33–36]. TGF-β is an important cytokine that promotes the occurrence and growth of tumor cells, and have immunosuppressive activity [37]. Activated TGF-β signaling remodels the immunosuppressive microenvironment that facilitate immune evasion and immune checkpoint inhibitors resistance [38, 39]. The E2F target genes and G2M checkpoint are important for genomic stability, ensuring DNA integrity before cells enter mitosis [40]. In cancer, dysregulation of the cell cycle, including G2M checkpoint disruption, allows cells with DNA damage to bypass the checkpoint and proceed to mitosis, promoting tumor development [41–43].

IKBIP is reported to be a p53 target gene with proapoptotic function. However, consistent with previous studies [17–19], our findings showed that IKBIP acts as an oncogene in HCC, where its overexpression predicts poor survival. We therefore consider that IKBIP may have dual roles in hepatocarcinogenesis, and we hypothesize that its proapoptotic function may be attenuated by the activation of key oncogenic pathways. Our GSEA analyses in TCGA showed that IKBIP is associated with EMT. Previous studies have also demonstrated that IKBIP overexpression promotes ESCC progression via activation of the AKT signaling pathway, which promotes EMT [17]. Accordingly, in HCC, the oncogenic effects of IKBIP, such as the activation of EMT, angiogenesis, TGF-β and AKT signaling may override its proapoptotic function.

To further explore the function of IKBIP in cancer, we performed transcriptomics on IKBIP-knockdown Huh7 cells. The GO enrichment analysis revealed a significant association with extracellular matrix remodeling, cell adhesion, and growth regulation, suggesting IKBIP was involved in the regulation of cell proliferation and the process of epithelial-mesenchymal transition. The KEGG pathway analyses revealed that the IL-17 signaling pathway and TNF signaling pathway were involved in IKBIP-knockdown Huh7 cells. IL-17 A is considered to be a prevalent cytokine in the tumor microenvironment and plays a dual role in tumor growth and elimination [44]. Tumor necrosis factor (TNF)-stimulated nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB) signaling plays very crucial roles in cancer development and progression [45]. These findings support the role of IKBIP in facilitating a tumorigenic microenvironment in HCC. The application of RNA-sequencing in HCC cells enriched this study, but the verification of specific downstream mechanisms is warranted in future studies.

In this study, by combining the IKBIP expression and other clinicopathological characteristics, we developed two nomograms to predict DFS and OS for individual patient with HCC in PKUSZ cohort. The nomograms demonstrated satisfactory predictive ability in terms of discrimination, calibration and clinical usefulness, which underscored that IKBIP provide complementary information about the prognostication of HCC. The nomogram for predicting OS was validated in Outdo cohort and showed robust performance. These two nomograms are practicable for clinical application. Tumor differentiation, tumor size and TNM stage are accessible through routinely pathological work, and IKBIP expression can be easily detected through the IHC examination of tumor specimens. Therefore, clinicians can perform risk stratification to identify high-risk patients who might benefit from closer monitoring and adjuvant therapies.

This study presents several significant findings. The differential expression and prognostic value of IKBIP in HCC were analyzed across multiple comprehensive datasets and validated in two clinical cohorts, thereby enhancing the robustness of the results. Experimental data showed that IKBIP knockdown suppresses HCC cell proliferation and migration. RNA-sequencing on IKBIP-knockdown Huh7 cells validated that IKBIP played a role in promoting cell proliferation. IKBIP expression was associated with immune cell infiltration and demonstrated potential as a predictive biomarker for immunotherapy response, highlighting its clinical relevance in the era of immune checkpoint inhibitors. This study provides a comprehensive understanding of IKBIP’s role in HCC and supports its potential as a novel prognostic biomarker.

There are some limitations in our study. First, given the retrospective design, our study was not free from inherent biases. Further validation involving more independent cohorts from different regions or ethnicities is warranted to test the prognostic value of IKBIP and strengthen generalizability of the nomograms. Second, in vitro and in vivo experiments are needed to investigate the underlying mechanisms by which IKBIP promotes the progression of HCC.

Conclusions

In this study, we found that that IKBIP overexpression is associated with poor prognosis in HCC. In vitro functional experiments showed that IKBIP knockdown significantly inhibits the proliferation and migration of HCC cells. We also found that IKBIP expression holds potential for predicting immunotherapy response. By integrating the IKBIP expression with clinicopathological characteristics, we developed and validated two nomograms, which could predict the DFS and OS of HCC. The transcriptomics analyses revealed that IKBIP is involved in pathways related to tumor growth and development. These findings highlight the oncogenic role of IKBIP and the nomograms developed in this study have the potential to improve prognosis prediction and guide individualized management in HCC.

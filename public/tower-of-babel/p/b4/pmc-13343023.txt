VIR-SAAF: a study protocol for a national decentralised randomised controlled trial of home-based sleep apnoea screening in patients with atrial fibrillation in Denmark
Authors: Mads Hashiba, Dominik Linz, Rune Frandsen, Poul Jennum, Erik Lerkevang Grove, Eline Gantzhorn, Lina Al-Ansary, Sebastian Kinnberg Nielsen, Ali Al-Alak, Jesper Bille, Milos Fuglsang, Lars H Tang, Mette Korshøj, Signe Stelling Risom, Morten Lamberts
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundObstructive sleep apnoea (OSA) is a common and modifiable risk factor for atrial fibrillation (AF), yet it remains frequently unrecognised and undertreated. Despite guideline recommendations to consider OSA screening, no consensus exists on optimal strategies. The VIR-SAAF (VIRtual national study with home Sleep Apnea investigation in patients with Atrial Fibrillation) trial investigates whether home-based OSA screening and referral for potential treatment improve quality of life, physical activity and AF-related outcomes compared with standard care.Methods and analysisVIR-SAAF is a national, investigator-initiated, decentralised randomised clinical trial enrolling adults with paroxysmal or persistent AF across Denmark. Participants are randomised 2:1 to home-based screening for OSA or to no screening. The trial is conducted entirely remotely using a purpose-built mobile application that supports electronic consent, digital questionnaires, rhythm monitoring, activity tracking and real-time communication with investigators. This design allows all study procedures, including device delivery, data collection and follow-up, to be completed without in-person visits, enabling large-scale participation across geographical regions. The primary outcome is the between-group difference in change from baseline to 18 weeks in quality of life, assessed using the Atrial Fibrillation Effect on QualiTy-of-life questionnaire. VIR-SAAF is the first randomised trial to evaluate OSA screening itself as an intervention in AF. The fully decentralised design will generate novel evidence on whether systematic home-based OSA screening can improve patient-centred outcomes.Ethics and disseminationThe study has been approved by the Danish National Committee on Health Research Ethics (approval number: 2402591). All participants provide electronic informed consent prior to enrolment. Results will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications and presentations at national and international scientific conferences.Trial registration numberNCT06618417.

Introduction

Despite being a well-established and modifiable risk factor for atrial fibrillation (AF), obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA) remains frequently unrecognised and undertreated in patients with AF, with studies showing that 40%–85% of patients with AF are affected by OSA.14 This is particularly concerning given the importance of risk factor management in achieving optimal AF care. The latest European Society of Cardiology (ESC) guidelines emphasise the importance of addressing comorbidities and risk factors as a central and early component of AF management.5

Current randomised evidence, although underpowered, remains conflicting regarding the benefits of continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) in patients with OSA and AF.6 7 Observational studies suggest that CPAP-treated patients with both OSA and AF have a reduced risk of AF recurrences following ablation and cardioversion.813 Specifically, CPAP therapy has been associated with a significantly higher 12-month AF-free survival rate of 67% after ablation and 82% after cardioversion vs 33% and 42% for no CPAP therapy, respectively.8 9 Patients with OSA and AF also appear to be more common non-responders to antiarrhythmic drugs.14 These findings are supported by several meta-analyses demonstrating an increased risk of AF recurrence in patients with OSA.15 16 A small, randomised clinical trial comparing CPAP with no therapy showed a reversal of atrial remodelling in individuals with moderate OSA.6 These findings raise important questions about whether all patients with AF should be screened for and potentially treated for OSA.

This raises another important question regarding how to effectively screen for OSA in patients with AF. While current guidelines recommend considering OSA screening, there is no clear consensus on the optimal timing or approach for testing.5 As AF prevalence continues to rise, projected to reach 18 million people in Europe by 2060, the gold-standard methods for diagnosing OSA are impractical for screening due to their complexity, time demands and limited availability.17 Importantly, it has been shown that standard OSA screening questionnaires fail to capture high-risk individuals with AF, resulting in limited benefits from large-scale evaluations.18 Consequently, a novel approach to identify patients with AF and OSA is needed to embrace the growing AF population.

To address both the scientific uncertainty of OSA intervention and screening challenges in the growing AF population, we designed a national, randomised, decentralised clinical trial (DCT) evaluating the impact of home-based OSA screening and potential treatment on AF-related quality of life, physical activity and AF burden in patients with AF (VIR-SAAF).

Methods

Study design and conduct

VIR-SAAF is an investigator-initiated, national, decentralised, parallel-group randomised trial in patients with AF, evaluating the effect of home-based screening and potential treatment for OSA, compared with no screening (standard care), on quality of life. Participants and recruitment

Men and women aged ≥18 years with at least one 12-lead ECG documenting AF are eligible for inclusion if they have either paroxysmal or persistent AF, defined according to current ESC guidelines.5 In addition, participants are required to own a smartphone to enable study procedures. Key exclusion criteria are history of OSA, advanced heart failure (New York Heart Association class III–IV), occupational driver’s licence, pregnancy and other conditions deemed likely to prevent study participation. Assessment of advanced heart failure at baseline was based on prior echocardiographic evaluations in combination with clinical information. Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Participants are recruited nationally using a decentralised, multichannel strategy targeting adults with paroxysmal or persistent AF. Primary recruitment will occur through the study website (www.vir-saaf.dk) with self-registration. To enhance visibility, hospital websites, social media platforms and clinic materials (including posters and QR codes) will direct potential participants to the study website for sign-up. In addition, the study is promoted through patient-focused presentations and communication channels facilitated by the Danish Heart Foundation, as well as through presentations to healthcare professionals at cardiology departments and related clinical forums across the country.

Randomisation and blinding

If a participant fulfils the criteria for randomisation, investigators randomise participants 2:1 to either the intervention or control arm. Simple randomisation without stratification is used in blocks of 3 and 6. The randomisation process is conducted using R studio secure database server at Copenhagen University Hospital—Herlev and Gentofte. The randomisation key is securely stored and unknown to the investigators. Due to the decentralised nature of the trial, neither participants nor investigators are blinded to group allocation. Participants have their allocation revealed after completion of baseline questionnaires.

Intervention

Participants in the intervention arm will undergo home-based screening for OSA using the WatchPAT One device (Itamar). Following baseline assessment, a mail-in package containing the device and instructions is sent to the participants. After one night of WatchPAT One recording, results are reviewed. All sleep recordings were reviewed by an experienced sleep physician at the Danish Center for Sleep Medicine in accordance with the American Academy of Sleep Medicine (AASM) criteria. To ensure consistency, all recordings were evaluated by the same physician. OSA severity is classified according to the AASM Manual for the Scoring of Sleep and Associated Events using the apnoea-hypopnoea index (AHI): mild OSA (AHI 5–14 events/hour), moderate OSA (AHI 15–29 events/hour) and severe OSA (AHI ≥30 events/hour).19

Following randomisation, the WatchPAT One device is dispatched and delivered within 5 days. After a single night of recording, a follow-up video consultation is scheduled within 5 days to review results. Participants with moderate-to-severe OSA are referred to a local sleep physician at the video consultation, with a fast-track appointment arranged through pre-established agreements with the affiliated sleep apnoea centres. The expected time from randomisation to initiation of CPAP therapy is expected within 4 weeks, leaving a substantial treatment window before the primary outcome assessment at 18 weeks. Data on referral completion, sleep clinic attendance and treatment initiation will be collected and reported descriptively, and may additionally be used in exploratory analyses.

Study procedures

The VIR-SAAF trial is conducted as a fully DCT made available by purpose-built mobile application (VIR-SAAF app). The application was developed in collaboration with a software partner and through iterative, thorough feedback from patients with AF, with a focus on usability, clarity of study procedures and patient-centred design. The application serves as the digital interface for all study activities and the primary means of data collection throughout the study. Selected screenshots from the VIR-SAAF application.

After registering on the study website, patients are contacted by telephone and invited to an introductory video meeting. During this meeting, oral and digital written information is provided. Eligible patients then download the VIR-SAAF app, where electronic informed consent is obtained via MitID (https://www.mitid.dk/en-gb/about-mitid/), the Danish national electronic identification system that enables secure authentication and legally valid digital signatures.

Following inclusion, patients complete baseline digital questionnaires through the VIR-SAAF app. Questionnaires include the Atrial Fibrillation Effect on QualiTy-of-life (AFEQT), Atrial Fibrillation Severity Scale (AFSS), Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI) and the eHealth Literacy Questionnaire (eHLQ). Once baseline questionnaires are completed, participants are randomised to the intervention or control arm. A mail-in package is then dispatched containing either the WatchPAT One device for home-based OSA testing together with an activity sensor (intervention arm) or only the activity sensor (control arm). The activity sensor used in the trial is the SENS Motion device (SENS).

After completion of baseline questionnaires, participants also initiate daily heart rhythm monitoring using the FibriCheck (FibriCheck) system integrated in the VIR-SAAF app. Recordings are performed twice daily throughout the whole 18-week study period.

Patients in the intervention arm are scheduled for a video consultation with the investigators to review their home-based OSA test results. Patients with moderate-to-severe OSA are referred to local sleep physicians for further clinical evaluation and determination of appropriate treatment, including initiation of therapy in accordance with standard clinical practice.

At 18 weeks, follow-up assessments are conducted, including a second mail-in package containing an activity sensor and digital questionnaires in the VIR-SAAF app including AFEQT, AFSS, PSQI and eHLQ.

Devices and questionnaires

The WatchPAT One system employs a validated algorithm to derive all diagnostic parameters recommended by the American Academy of Sleep Medicine Manual for the Scoring of Sleep and Associated Events.19 The device has been validated against in-laboratory polysomnography, demonstrating a high level of agreement in estimating the AHI.20

The FibriCheck system employs a smartphone’s camera and flashlight to record photoplethysmography (PPG) signals from the fingertip, enabling heart rhythm and rate analysis. The algorithm has been validated against a 12-lead ECG, demonstrating 96% sensitivity and 97% specificity for detecting AF.21 Participants perform 1 min recordings twice daily and additionally in the event of symptoms throughout the 18-week study period.

The SENS Motion system consists of a thigh-worn tri-axial accelerometer that monitors physical activity using a validated algorithm.22 The system classifies inactivity, standing and physical activity with accuracies of 99%, 95% and 97%, respectively.22 Participants are instructed to wear the sensor continuously (24 hours/day) for seven consecutive days at baseline and again at 18 weeks. To ensure reliable and representative measurements of physical activity patterns and stationary behaviour, only participants with at least five valid measurement days, defined as ≥16 hours of recorded data per 24-hour day, were included in the physical activity analyses. These quality criteria are applied to minimise bias related to insufficient wear time and to ensure adequate representation of both waking and sleep periods during the monitoring interval.23

The AFEQT questionnaire assesses AF-related quality of life. The total score ranges from 0 (worst) to 100 (best) and is based on 20 items rated on a 7-point Likert scale. The AFEQT covers three domains—AF symptoms, daily activities and treatment concerns—reflecting the participant’s experiences within the previous 4 weeks. The questionnaire has been validated across a variety of clinical settings.24

The AFSS questionnaire is focused on AF-related symptoms and captures ratings of symptom severity, healthcare utilisation and overall AF disease burden. The total score ranges from 0 (best) to 35 (worst). AFSS has been translated and culturally adapted into a validated Danish version for use in the VIR-SAAF study.25

The PSQI questionnaire assesses sleep quality and disturbances over a 1-month period. It comprises 19 self-rated questions that generate seven component scores, which are summed to produce a global score ranging from 0 (best) to 21 (worst). The PSQI distinguishes between good and poor sleepers and has been validated in diverse populations.26

The eHLQ is a validated, multidimensional instrument comprising 35 items across seven scales, developed based on the eHealth Literacy Framework. It assesses knowledge, skills and experiences related to engagement with digital health services and technologies.27

Outcomes

The primary outcome is the between-group difference in change from baseline to 18 weeks in AF-related quality of life, assessed by the total score using the AFEQT questionnaire. Secondary outcomes are the between-group difference in AF load as measured by FibriCheck, change from baseline to 18 weeks in physical inactivity assessed by the activity sensor and change from baseline to 18 weeks in AF symptom severity using the AFSS. In addition, several exploratory outcomes will be evaluated (listed in table 2), including the between-group difference in change from baseline to 18 weeks in sleep quality using the PSQI, as well as long-term outcomes obtained through linkage to health registers, including AF-related hospitalisations, catheter ablations and cardioversions.

Statistical considerations

Sample size

The trial is powered to detect a clinically meaningful between-group difference in change from baseline to 18 weeks in the total AFEQT score, defined as the primary outcome. A 5-point difference in AFEQT is regarded as clinically important.24 Assuming an SD of 22 based on pilot data, a two-sided significance level of 0.05 and 80% power, the required sample size is 936 participants. This corresponds to 624 participants randomised to the intervention arm and 312 to the control arm, reflecting the planned 2:1 randomisation ratio. A maximum drop-out rate of 10% has been included in the sample size calculation.

Statistical analyses

All analyses will be performed according to the intention-to-treat principle. A hierarchical testing scheme will be applied, beginning with the primary outcome (between-group difference in change from baseline to 18 weeks in the total AFEQT score). If statistical significance is reached (p<0.05), secondary outcomes will be formally tested in the following prespecified order without adjustment for multiple testing: (1) physical inactivity, (2) AF load and (3) AF symptom severity (AFSS). This order reflects the expected mechanistic pathway through which OSA screening and treatment may exert its effect. By treating OSA and thereby reducing sleep-related symptoms such as fatigue and excessive daytime sleepiness, participants are expected to become more physically active, thereby addressing a key modifiable risk factor in AF. Furthermore, the inclusion of physical inactivity and AF load as the first two secondary outcomes provides objective device-derived support for the subjective improvement in quality of life captured by the primary outcome AFEQT, strengthening the overall interpretability of the trial results.

The primary outcome will be analysed using a linear mixed model (LMM), including fixed effects for treatment group, time and a group-by-time interaction term. The key estimate of interest will be the group-by-time interaction, representing the between-group difference in change from baseline to 18 weeks. Results will be reported as estimated mean differences with 95% CIs. The same LMM framework will be applied to the secondary outcomes of AF symptom severity (AFSS) and physical inactivity.

AF load is defined as the proportion of time spent with AF to the total monitoring time (time spent with AF/total monitoring time), in accordance with the methodology described by Sandgren et al.28 Time spent with AF is calculated as the sum of: (i) the time between the last non-AF recording and the first AF recording divided by two, (ii) the time between the first and last consecutive AF recordings and (iii) the time between the last AF recording and the first subsequent non-AF recording divided by two. Total monitoring time is defined as the time between the first and last PPG recording during the 18-week monitoring period. Symptom-triggered recordings are incorporated naturally into this time-based calculation, contributing additional precision to the AF load estimate. AF load will be analysed using LMM on a logit-transformed scale to account for the bounded and potentially skewed nature of proportional data. Results will be back-transformed and reported as estimated between-group differences in AF load with 95% CIs. As a supplementary analysis, %AF days, defined as the proportion of days with at least one AF recording per all complete monitoring days, will be reported.28

Missing data will be handled within the LMM framework under the missing-at-random assumption, which uses all available observations without requiring explicit imputation. Baseline characteristics, including demographics, comorbidities, number of ablations and cardioversions and concomitant medical therapy, will be summarised using means and SD or medians with IQRs depending on data distribution. Categorical variables will be presented as counts and percentages, and between-group comparisons will be made using χ2 or Fisher’s exact tests, as appropriate.

All analyses will be two-sided, and statistical significance will be defined as p<0.05. Statistical analyses will be conducted in R software V.4.0 or later.

Study organisation

The VIR-SAAF trial is organised and coordinated by Herlev-Gentofte University Hospital. The study is currently conducted in collaboration with seven affiliated sleep apnoea centres across Denmark: University of Copenhagen—Rigshospitalet, Zealand University Hospital Koege, University College Lillebaelt, Odense University Hospital, Hospital Sønderjylland, Esbjerg and Grindsted Hospital and Aarhus University Hospital. A collaboration with Aalborg University Hospital is pending. A steering committee has been established and used with planned and structured meetings.

Patient and public involvement

Patients were involved in the design of the study and in the development of the study platform/mobile application. Their input contributed to the usability, content and feasibility of the digital interface and study procedures. Patient involvement helped ensure that study procedures were patient-centred and suitable for remote participation.

Discussion

The VIR-SAAF trial evaluates whether home-based OSA screening and treatment improve outcomes in patients with AF by targeting a key modifiable risk factor. Although observational studies suggest that identification and treatment of OSA may reduce AF recurrence and improve symptoms, randomised evidence remains limited, underpowered and inconclusive. VIR-SAAF contributes to this evidence gap with the largest randomised study to date focused specifically on OSA screening in patients with AF.

A central and innovative element of VIR-SAAF is its fully DCT design, which integrates home-based diagnostic testing, smartphone-based rhythm monitoring and remote patient-reported outcomes (PROs) into a unified digital platform. This approach enhances accessibility, reduces participant burden and enables real-world data collection at scale, positioning VIR-SAAF as a model for digitally supported risk-factor management in contemporary AF care.

Choice of outcomes

The primary outcome in VIR-SAAF is the AF-related quality of life, assessed using the AFEQT questionnaire. PRO measures have proved valuable for capturing the patient experience over time and are increasingly recognised as essential outcomes in research on patients with AF.29 30 They provide a direct assessment of symptom burden, functional status and treatment satisfaction, offering complementary information to conventional clinical outcomes when evaluating the success of an intervention. This choice of primary outcome reflects a deliberate focus on patient-centred care, recognising that quality of life and symptom burden represent the outcomes of great relevance to individuals living with AF.29 In contrast to traditional cardiovascular trials that prioritise event-based outcomes such as ablation, cardioversion or mortality, VIR-SAAF aims to capture the multidimensional impact of OSA screening and potential treatment on patients’ daily lives, functioning and well-being. Among available AF-specific PRO instruments, the selection of AFEQT as the primary outcome and the AFSS as a key secondary measure are based on recommendations from the International Consortium for Health Outcomes Measurement working group, which endorses the use of these two instruments to assess quality of life and symptom impact in AF.31 As advised, an expert on psychometrics (SSR) is part of the trial committee.

The two additional key secondary outcomes, AF load and physical inactivity, were selected to provide objective measures that complement the PROs.28 The term AF load is used in VIR-SAAF to describe the proportion of time in AF as measured by the smartphone-based PPG. This terminology was chosen deliberately, as AF burden is conventionally reserved for measurements obtained from implanted loop recorders. A previous randomised clinical trial investigating the effect of CPAP treatment in patients with AF and OSA used AF burden as the primary outcome but did not demonstrate a significant between-group difference in percentage time in AF.32 However, the observed AF burden in the study was substantially lower than anticipated, leading to insufficient statistical power to detect a modest treatment effect. These findings highlight the need for further investigation, preferably within randomised clinical trials, using alternative and scalable approaches to rhythm monitoring, such as PPG-based applications, to better understand the impact of OSA evaluation and treatment.

Physical inactivity was selected as a secondary outcome as reduced cardiorespiratory fitness is a modifiable risk factor in patients with AF.33 Evidence from randomised trials and meta-analyses shows that improved cardiorespiratory fitness and regular aerobic exercise reduce AF burden and enhance quality of life.34 35 As untreated OSA may contribute to fatigue and reduced activity, measuring physical inactivity provides an objective marker of the broader health impact of OSA screening and potential treatment in patients with AF.

Key inclusion and exclusion criteria

The inclusion and exclusion criteria for VIR-SAAF were intentionally kept broad to ensure that the study population reflects the wide clinical spectrum of patients with AF encountered in routine practice. By adopting a pragmatic design, the trial aims to evaluate whether systematic screening for OSA should be considered an integral part of AF management rather than limited to selected subgroups. This approach enhances generalisability and supports potential future implementation of OSA screening across diverse healthcare settings. Permanent AF was excluded, since assessment of changes in AF load or symptom burden in patients with continuous arrhythmia is less meaningful.

The intervention

VIR-SAAF is designed to evaluate the impact of screening for OSA, rather than the treatment itself. The intervention arm undergoes home-based OSA testing using a validated device, with subsequent referral for clinical assessment and potential treatment only when moderate-to-severe OSA is detected. This design reflects the real-world implementation of OSA screening within AF management, where the clinical consequence of screening is diagnostic clarification and potential initiation of therapy through standard care pathways. A 2:1 randomisation ratio was selected to ensure enough participants are screened for OSA and thereby eligible for downstream evaluation and potential treatment. This approach increases the likelihood of detecting meaningful differences in PROs, as the subset of participants identified with OSA and subsequently treated are expected to drive much of the observed benefit. To our knowledge, no randomised trial has evaluated OSA screening itself as the intervention in patients with AF. Prior trials have tested CPAP treatment in selected OSA-AF populations, leaving the effectiveness and implementation of a systematic screening strategy unaddressed.

Novelty of the decentralised trial design

A distinguishing feature of VIR-SAAF is the use of a fully DCT design, which enables nationwide participation with no need for in-person visits. This framework allows OSA testing, rhythm monitoring, physical activity assessment and completion of PROs to occur entirely in patients’ everyday environments. By embedding all trial procedures into a unified digital platform, VIR-SAAF facilitates large-scale, real-world data collection and minimises traditional barriers to trial participation. This approach is particularly well suited to examining risk-factor management in AF, where long-term behaviours, symptoms and treatment responses are best captured outside the clinic setting. It should be acknowledged that the multicomponent digital requirements of VIR-SAAF, including smartphone ownership, MitID authentication, app engagement, repeated PPG recordings and use of mailed devices, may preferentially attract participants who are digitally literate and motivated. As a result, the study population may not fully represent older or more comorbid patients with AF, who are often at highest risk and may stand to benefit most from systematic OSA screening. This potential selection bias should be considered when interpreting and generalising the findings of this trial. However, it should be noted that the VIR-SAAF application was developed on thorough iterative collaboration with patients with AF, including older and more comorbid individuals, with a deliberate focus on usability and accessibility, which may help mitigate some of the barriers to digital participation. Additionally, we are measuring digital health literacy as an exploratory outcome using the eHLQ, which will characterise the digital health competencies of the participants enrolled in VIR-SAAF. This allows us to be transparent about the digital profile of our study population and to better assess the generalisability of our findings to the broader AF population.

Findings from the pilot study confirmed the feasibility of this approach, with a low dropout rate (5%) and high data completeness (90%), demonstrating that robust engagement can be achieved through digital systems combined with active investigator support.36 Similar feasibility has been demonstrated in the VIRTUAL-SAFARI (A VIRTUAL Sleep Apnoea management pathway For the work-up of Atrial fibrillation patients in a digital Remote Infrastructure) study, where remote sleep-apnoea testing within a virtual AF care pathway yielded high patient satisfaction and a short time to diagnosis.37 The VIR-SAAF design aligns with current ESC guidelines, which advocate for the integration of eHealth and digital technologies in AF management, highlighting decentralised models as a promising framework for future cardiovascular research.

In summary, VIR-SAAF is a pragmatic, randomised fully DCT designed to address an evidence gap in the management of AF—the role of systematic screening for OSA. By evaluating the impact of home-based OSA screening and potential treatment on quality of life and AF-related outcomes, the trial will provide the first randomised evidence on whether integrating OSA evaluation into routine AF care improves patient-centred outcomes. If successful, the findings could offer a scalable model for incorporating OSA screening into contemporary cardiovascular management.

Current status

The first randomisation took place on 5 December 2024. As of April 2026, a total of 341 participants have been randomised. Recruitment in VIR-SAAF is ongoing, with participants enrolled from 73 of the 98 municipalities in Denmark, reflecting broad national participation.

Ethics and dissemination

The study is conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and applicable national regulations. The trial has been approved by The Danish National Committee on Health Research Ethics (approval number: 2402591). All participants provide electronic informed consent via MitID (the Danish national electronic identification system) prior to enrolment. Data are handled in compliance with data protection regulations, including secure storage and pseudonymisation of participant information. As the intervention consists of non-invasive home-based screening, no major safety concerns are anticipated. Results will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications and presentations at national and international scientific conferences. De-identified individual participant data, including the data dictionary, as well as statistical code and relevant study materials, will be made available on reasonable request to the corresponding author, subject to approval by the study steering committee and in accordance with applicable data protection regulations.

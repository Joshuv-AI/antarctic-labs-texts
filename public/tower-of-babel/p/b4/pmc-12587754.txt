Age‐ and Sex‐Related Trends in Psychotropic Prescribing Among Youths in Denmark and the United States: 2010–2020
Authors: Phuong Tran, Lotte Rasmussen, Alejandro Amill‐Rosario, Mette Bliddal, Susan dosReis, Rikke Wesselhoeft
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTObjectivesTo compare age‐ and sex‐specific trends of psychotropic prescribing between Danish and US 3–17‐year‐old youths.MethodsA consecutive annual cross‐sectional study of trends in psychotropic prescribing from 2010 to 2020. Data sources included the Danish National Prescription Registry data and, for the US, a 25% random sample of the IQVIA PharMetrics Plus for Academics database. Psychotropic prescribing (i.e., ADHD medications, antidepressants, and antipsychotics), measured as annual prevalence, was estimated for each therapeutic class, by age group and sex, separately by country. We used Joinpoint models to calculate the average annual percentage change (AAPC).ResultsADHD medication prescribing decreased for 5–9 year‐olds in both countries from 2010 to 2020, while prescribing among 10–17 year‐olds was stable in the US and increased in Denmark, particularly among females (AAPC = 4.2% versus 1.4% among males). Antidepressant prescribing increased in older US youths, especially among 14–17 year‐olds (AAPC = 6.5%), and females (AAPC = 7.2%). In Denmark, however, prescribing was low and decreased over time, especially among females (AAPC = −4.2%). Antipsychotic prescribing decreased in both countries for all age groups, with the largest decrease among males (Denmark: AAPC = −5.2% and US: AAPC = −6.1%).ConclusionPediatric antipsychotic prescribing generally decreased, whereas ADHD medication and antidepressant prescribing presented opposing patterns in the two countries. ADHD medication prescribing increased among Danish females and adolescents, but was stable in the US. Antidepressant prescribing decreased in Denmark, particularly among females, which opposed the marked rise for this group in the US. Future cross‐national studies should examine the rationale underpinning variation in antidepressant prescribing.Keywords: children and adolescents, pharmacological treatment, prevalence, psychotropics

Introduction

Use of psychotropic medication varies significantly between countries [1]. Yet, psychotropic prescribing to youths has risen in many developed countries during the last two decades, such as the United States (US), Denmark, the United Kingdom, France, and Australia [2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10]. Studies estimate that psychotropic medication use is two‐fold higher among US youths relative to youths in European countries [11, 12]. Data from 1996 to 2012 show that psychotropic medication use increased by 3.4% among US youths [4]. The increasing trend continued through 2006–2012 for ADHD medication and antidepressant use, while there was a decline in prevalent antipsychotic use during that period [11, 13, 14]. Antipsychotic use continued to decline after 2012 in Medicaid‐enrolled youths [15] and in commercially insured youths < 12 years‐old [16, 17]. With the exception of antidepressants, a rise in psychotropic medication use, such as ADHD medications and hypnotics, has also been observed for Danish youths in later years, although at a lower prevalence than in the US [6, 7, 8, 18].

Cross‐national differences in psychotropic prescribing are often attributed to differences in prescribing practices and in public attitude [19, 20, 21]. From 2010 to 2020, a decline in psychotropic prescribing in the US was reported [15, 16, 17, 22], potentially attributed to concerns over suicide risk with antidepressants [22] and metabolic adverse effects with antipsychotics [15]. It is unclear if prescribing trends in other countries were similarly affected during this time period. Given variation in psychotropic prescribing by age and sex [5, 6, 7, 23], it is important to examine the relative differences and similarities in psychotropic prescribing by these demographics across different countries. Such knowledge would inform the ongoing discussion on the rational use of psychotropic medication in pediatric populations. Therefore, this study aimed to compare trends in psychotropic prescribing by age and sex over a decade (2010–2020) in Danish and US youths.

Methods

Study Design

A consecutive annual cross‐sectional design evaluated psychotropic prescribing among Danish and US youths from 2010 to 2020. We estimated age‐ and sex‐specific trends by psychotropic class and compared prescribing among older relative to younger individuals and among males relative to females for each study year in each country.

Data Source

We utilized administrative prescription databases in Denmark and the US. In Denmark, we used the Danish National Prescription Register [24], which includes all medications dispensed to all residents at outpatient pharmacies since 1995. Drugs are classified according to the Anatomical Therapeutic Chemical (ATC) classification developed and maintained by the World Health Organization. Population counts were extracted from Statistics Denmark census data [25].

In the US, we used a 25% random sample of the entire IQVIA PharMetrics Plus for Academics database, which comprises fully adjudicated medical and prescription claims and health plan enrollment data. The data are generally representative, with respect to age and sex, of US residents < 65 years old who are enrolled in mainly commercial health insurance plans. The prescription claims database provides information on all medications dispensed in outpatient and mail‐order pharmacies. We used the National Drug Code on the prescription claims to link to an electronic drug database that provided the medication name.

Study Population

The study population comprised youths aged 3–17 years from 2010 to 2020. In Denmark, this included all youths aged 3–17 years. In the US, the study population was derived from an age‐ and sex‐representative sample of the commercially insured population. In 2020, 66.5% of the US population had commercial health insurance, while 34.8% had public insurance [26].

Psychotropic Medication

We identified all psychotropic therapeutic classes prescribed to youths in both countries. Our analysis focused on ADHD medications (i.e., stimulants, atomoxetine), antidepressants, and antipsychotics. Using the ATC system, we created a comprehensive list of all medications in both countries (Table S1). We excluded mood stabilizers due to the limited use among Danish youths.

Data Analysis

We characterized the overall study population of youths with at least one filled prescription of ADHD, antidepressant, or antipsychotic medication according to age group and sex, using age at the first prescription observed in the data. We identified the top five most commonly used drugs within each medication group.

We estimated the annual prevalence, separately for each country, as the number per 1000 youths. For each psychotropic class, the numerator was the number of youths with at least one filled prescription in each age‐ and sex‐specific subgroup for each study year. In Denmark, the denominator was the entire Danish youth population in the given age and sex strata in each study year. In the US, the denominator was all youths with documented health plan enrollment in the database in the given age and sex strata in each study year. Youths could appear in multiple years, but were placed in the age category based on their age in the year of a filled prescription.

We used Joinpoint models to calculate the average annual percentage change (AAPC) and associated confidence intervals from 2010 to 2020 (National Cancer Institute's Joinpoint Regression Program version 5.2.0.0). Joinpoint is a data‐driven method to find points of change in a trend based on the proportion of medication use at each measurement period. Permutation tests were used to determine the Joinpoint model that best fits the data for 11 data points for years 2010 to 2020 [27]. Models were fitted with autocorrelated errors. AAPC is a weighted average for each segment identified by the Joinpoint models, accounting for changes in prevalence over time.

Finally, we calculated annual prevalence ratios to compare prescribing by age (10–13 and 14–17 versus 5–9 years old) and sex (male versus female) in each country. Children younger than 5 years old were excluded due to small sample sizes. We used SAS v9.4 (US) and Stata (Denmark) for data analyses.

Results

Characteristics of the Study Population

There were 66 823 Danish and 363 271 US youths aged 3–17 years who filled at least one psychotropic prescription at any time point during the study period. Table 1 displays the demographic characteristics by country. Most psychotropic medication users were in the 14–17‐year‐old age group, with 51% and 41% in Denmark and the US, respectively. Males comprised 60% of the Danish and 40% of the US youths who filled psychotropic prescriptions. The most frequently prescribed psychotropic medications were similar in the two countries for all three classes: antidepressants (i.e., sertraline, fluoxetine), antipsychotics (i.e., risperidone, aripiprazole, quetiapine), and ADHD medications (i.e., methylphenidate) (Table S2).

Age‐Specific Trends

ADHD Medications

Differences in ADHD medication prescribing between Denmark and the US were greater among 5–9 year‐olds than among those aged 10 or older (Table S3). Both countries had a decline in ADHD medication prescribing among 5–9 year‐olds from 2010 to 2020 (Denmark, 8.6–7.0 per 1000 children, AAPC = −1.3% versus US, 30.9–25.5 per 1000 children, AAPC = −2.4%) (Figure 1, Table 2, and Table S3). ADHD medication prescribing among older youths, on the other hand, increased in Denmark and was stable in the US. In Denmark, prescribing among 10–13 year‐olds increased from 20.0 in 2010 to 24.3 per 1000 youths in 2020 (AAPC = 1.5%), and prescribing among 14–17 year‐olds increased from 19.4 in 2010 to 29.4 in 2020 (AAPC = 3.6%). In the US, ADHD medication prescribing increased for 10–13 year‐olds from 2010 (57.9 per 1000 youths) to a peak in 2013 (67.2 per 1000 youths), whereafter it declined through 2020 (58.2 per 1000 youths) (AAPC = −1.1%). Prescribing for US 14–17 year‐olds was 51.5 per 1000 youths in 2010 and increased to 58.0 per 1000 youths in 2020 (AAPC = 0.3%). In both countries, the proportion of older versus younger ADHD medication users (10–13 year‐olds and 14–17 year‐olds relative to 5–9 year‐olds) widened slightly from 2010 to 2020, illustrated by increasing prevalence ratios (Figure S1).

Antidepressants

Antidepressant prescribing was higher in the US than in Denmark across all age groups throughout the study period. Compared to Danish 5–9 year‐olds, antidepressant prescribing among US 5–9 year‐olds was 14 times higher in 2010 to the largest difference of 63 times higher in 2018 (Table S3). The difference in antidepressant prescribing in the US relative to Denmark was higher for those 10 years old and older, but the magnitude of the difference was not as large as for the youngest age group. In Denmark, antidepressant prescribing decreased in all age groups: 0.4 in 2010 to 0.2 in 2020 per 1000 children 5–9 years old (AAPC = −5.6%), 2.4 in 2010 to 1.7 in 2020 per 1000 youths 10–13 years old (AAPC = −4.7%), and 14.6 in 2010 to 9.9 in 2020 per 1000 youths 14–17 years old (AAPC = −3.9%) (Figure 1, Table 2, and Table S3). In the US, antidepressant prescribing increased for 10–13 year‐olds (17.7 in 2010 to 28.2 in 2020 per 1000 youths, AAPC = 4.4%) and 14–17 year‐olds (37.8 in 2010 to 72.6 in 2020 per 1000 youths, AAPC = 6.5%). Across all US age groups, there was a peak in 2013 in the number of youths with a filled antidepressant prescription. The prevalence ratio for 10–13 year‐olds relative to 5–9 year‐olds in Denmark was 5.6 in 2010, rising to 11.9 in 2017 and dropping to 6.7 in 2020 (Figure S1). The prevalence ratio for 14–17 year‐olds relative to 5–9 year‐olds in Denmark was 34.2 in 2010, peaking at 60 in 2018, and declining to 39.8 in 2020. In the US, the prevalence ratio for 10–13 year‐olds and 14–17 year‐olds relative to 5–9 year‐olds increased throughout the study period.

Antipsychotics

Throughout the study period, antipsychotic prescribing was four to ten times higher among 5–9 year‐olds, three to four times higher among 10–13 year‐olds, and about two times higher among 14–17 year‐olds in the US relative to Denmark (Table S3). Both countries experienced a decline in antipsychotic prescribing across all age groups, with the largest decline among youths 5–9 years old. From 2010 to 2020, antipsychotic prescribing in Denmark decreased from 0.9 to 0.3 per 1000 children 5–9 years old (AAPC = −10.0%), 2.7–1.5 per 1000 youths 10–13 years old (AAPC = −7.0%), and 7.1–5.4 per 1000 youths 14–17 years old (AAPC = −3.2%) (Figure 1, Table 2, and Table S3). In the US, antipsychotic prescribing from 2010 to 2020 decreased from 4.0 to 1.9 per 1000 children 5–9 years old (AAPC = −8.5%), 7.9–4.9 per 1000 youths 10–13 years old (AAPC = −5.1%), and 11.4–9.5 per 1000 youths 14–17 years old (AAPC = −2.2%). Given the larger decline in antipsychotic prescribing among 5–9 year‐olds compared to 10–17 year‐olds, especially in Denmark, the ratio of older to younger antipsychotic users increased over time (Figure S1).

Sex‐Specific Trends

ADHD Medications

ADHD medication prescribing was more than twice as high in males compared to females in both countries, but the prescribing among Danish males was equivalent to that of US females (Table S4). In Denmark, ADHD medication prescribing among males increased from 20.7 in 2010 to 24.3 per 1000 youths in 2020 (AAPC = 1.4%) (Figure 2, Table 2, and Table S4) and among Danish females increased from 6.2 to 9.7 per 1000 youths from 2010 to 2020 (AAPC = 4.2%). In the US, ADHD medication prescribing showed an overall subtle decrease among males (from 56.0 to 54.7 per 1000 youths, AAPC = −1.2%) and a slight increase among females (23.8 to 26.4 per 1000 youths, AAPC = −0.3%) from 2010 to 2020. In both countries, the sex differences in ADHD medication prescribing decreased over time, with prevalence ratios approaching two (Figure S2).

Antidepressants

Antidepressant prescribing was higher in females than in males for both countries, but the prescribing was three to ten times higher in the US for both sexes compared to Denmark (Table S4). There was a decline in prescribing among Danish females from 6.4 to 4.1 per 1000 youths (AAPC = −4.2%) and among Danish males from 3.4 to 2.5 per 1000 youths (AAPC = −3.4%) (Figure 2, Table 2, and Table S4). In contrast, there was an increase in antidepressant prescribing among both sexes in the US, increasing from 16.9 to 25.5 per 1000 youths among males (AAPC = 3.9%) and from 17.9 to 36.9 per 1000 youths among females (AAPC = 7.2%). The female predominance in antidepressant prescribing persisted during the study period, although it declined in the US (Figure S2).

Antipsychotics

Antipsychotic prescribing was higher among males than females throughout the study period, but the male predominance diminished at the end of the study period (Table S4, Figure S2). In Denmark, prescribing from 2010 to 2020 declined among males from 3.7 to 2.1 per 1000 youths, respectively, (AAPC = −6.1%) and among females from 2.2 to 2.0 per 1000 youths, respectively, (AAPC = −1.5%) (Figure 2, Table 2, and Table S4). In the US, antipsychotic prescribing declined among males from 8.7 in 2010 to 5.6 in 2020 per 1000 youths (AAPC = −5.2%) and among females from 4.5 in 2010 to 3.8 in 2020 per 1000 youths(AAPC = −2.0%).

Discussion

This cross‐national study compared age and sex‐specific trends from 2010 to 2020 in psychotropic prescribing among youths in Denmark and the US. The US had a higher prescribing of all three psychotropic classes compared to Denmark. Over the study period, in both countries, ADHD medication prescribing was higher among older than younger youths and among males relative to females. ADHD medication prescribing increased in Denmark—most pronounced among females and by increasing age groups—but revealed stable trends in the US. Antidepressant prescribing showed opposing patterns of a decline among Danish youths and an increase among US youths. The rise in the US was mainly driven by the increase in prescribing among adolescents and females. Antipsychotic prescribing decreased across all age groups and predominantly among males in both countries.

Our finding of higher prescribing of psychotropic medications in the US than in Denmark across age groups and sexes—especially ADHD medications and antidepressants—is consistent with previous studies [11, 13, 14]. Trends in ADHD medication prescribing increased among older youths in Denmark, but there was a modest change in the US, continuing previous trends from 2005 to 2012 [14]. ADHD medication prescribing was consistently higher among males than females, but the sex gap narrowed over time in both countries. The observed increase in the prescribing of ADHD medication among females in Denmark, in line with observations from other European countries and Australia [7, 28], may correspond with greater awareness of ADHD diagnosis and treatment in females, who historically have been underdiagnosed [29]. Interestingly, the prescribing of ADHD medications among Danish males approximated the prescribing among US females towards the end of the study period.

The marked variation in antidepressant prescribing between Denmark and the US may reflect differences in clinical prescribing practices for youths. The most notable difference was the substantial decrease in antidepressant prescribing among Danish youths, whereas prescribing for older youths increased in the US. In Denmark, the decrease in antidepressant prescribing from 2011 to 2017 may be due to media attention that infused public skepticism about antidepressants [30] and strict guidelines on prescriptive authority [31]. In the US, antidepressant use has increased since 2012, reversing the decline after the 2004 US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) suicidality warning [32]. Differences in approved psychotropics for youths may also explain variations. For example, the FDA approves more options for antidepressant treatment of younger youths compared to regulatory approvals in Denmark [20].

Our study found that the differences in antidepressant prescribing by sex increased in the US during the study period. Our study extends prior findings from 2006 to 2012 in US youths that reported ratios of antidepressant use in males relative to females declined from 1.11 in 2006 to 0.92 in 2012 [13], which is due to the increase in use among females. The large increase in antidepressant prescribing among US females warrants further study to explore the reasons and indications driving the increase.

Decreasing trends in antipsychotic prescribing in the US across all age groups were previously reported among Medicaid‐insured and privately insured US youths and can be attributed to increased monitoring of antipsychotic use in pediatrics [17, 33, 34]. Prescribing of antipsychotics in Denmark was very low and stable over time, potentially because other alternatives are generally recommended before antipsychotic treatment [35]. The decline in antipsychotics, most pronounced in males, may reflect efforts in both countries to reduce antipsychotic prescribing to youths due to awareness of metabolic side effects and requirements in the US for prior authorization (i.e., preapproval from the insurer or managed care organization as a condition for coverage). Finally, antipsychotics are indicated for schizophrenia and bipolar disorder, but sometimes can be used to manage impulsive and aggressive behaviors in adolescents with ADHD [34]. In the US, antipsychotics are increasingly used to treat mood disorders in adolescent females [34].

This study did not include the data after 2020 so as not to conflate typical prescribing practices with the pandemic‐response prescribing. More recently, the COVID‐19 pandemic was associated with an additional rise in the use of psychotropic medications among youths worldwide [2, 36, 37], which, in Denmark, persisted into the years after the pandemic [38]. This is a different dynamic that may not adequately reflect the clinical practice standards. Future studies examining postpandemic patterns of prescribing are warranted.

This study is not without limitations. While Denmark reflects a national cohort, the US cohort is representative of the national population with a commercial health insurance plan. Differences exist across countries in the availability of approved psychotropic medications and thus limited the examination of the therapeutic classes included in this study. The data did not include the prescribing indications, limiting the comparison of clinical practice differences regarding specific indications. Additionally, our analysis was based on dispensed prescriptions, not the actual consumption data. However, these limitations do not preclude us from outlining the main age and sex differences in clinical practice between the two countries. Finally, the 2013–2014 trend in the US, which was not reflected in Denmark, may be due to changes in data contributors to the US data source, the impact of greater scrutiny on pediatric psychotropic prescribing, and changes in health plan coverage for mental health services and treatment.

Conclusions

The study demonstrated higher prescribing of psychotropic medications to youths in the US compared to Denmark. Despite this difference, there were similar age‐ and sex‐related trends in antipsychotic prescribing but differences in patterns of prescribing for ADHD medications and antidepressants. Additional cross‐national studies would help shed light on prescribing trends to better understand the global management of mental health disorders in youth.

Plain Language Summary

Psychotropic medication use for pediatric mental health conditions has increased globally. The US has the highest rate of use compared to other developed countries, but it is unclear if this is due to variability in prescribing by age and sex. This cross‐national study compared psychotropic medication prescribing between Danish and US youths 3–17 years old. The purpose of the study was to identify similarities and differences in psychotropic prescribing by age and sex. Trends in attention‐deficit/hyperactivity (ADHD) medication, antidepressant, and antipsychotic medication prescribing were evaluated as the annual prevalence from 2010 to 2020. We found a decline in antipsychotic prescribing across age groups and sexes in both countries. ADHD medication and antidepressant prescribing demonstrated opposing patterns in the two countries. There was a marked difference in antidepressant prescribing, with a sharp increase in the US, in particular females, and a decrease in antidepressant prescribing over time in Denmark. Differences between the US and Denmark in psychotropic prescribing may be driven by age‐ and sex‐specific patterns of ADHD medication and antidepressant prescribing.

The effectiveness of physical activity intervention on mobile phone addiction in college students: the chain mediating role of emotion regulation strategies and positive coping style
Authors: Shuqiao Meng, Guangbin Bai, Panyi Shen, Shuwen Guo, Yifan Xu, Kai Qi, Wenxia Tong, Yihang Huang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveMobile phone addiction has become increasingly severe among college students, adversely affecting their physical and mental health as well as academic performance. Although physical activity (PA) interventions have been shown to alleviate mobile phone addiction, existing research mostly focuses on single-form exercises and lacks in-depth exploration of underlying mechanisms. Therefore, this study aims to examine the effectiveness of a combined intervention involving badminton and fancy rope skipping on improving mobile phone addiction in college students, and further analyze the chain mediating roles of emotion regulation strategies and positive coping styles.MethodsThis study adopted a pretest-posttest quasi-experimental design. A cohort of 195 college students meeting DSM-V diagnostic criteria for mobile phone addiction were enrolled and randomly allocated to one of three groups: a combined intervention group (n = 74), a badminton intervention group (n = 62), and a control group (n = 59). The intervention groups engaged in a 12-week exercise program, 5 sessions per week, each lasting 45 min, while the control group maintained their usual lifestyle. The Mobile Phone Addiction Index (MPAI; Leung) was used to assess mobile phone addiction levels before and after the intervention, while the Emotion Regulation Questionnaire (ERQ; Gross & John) and Simplified Coping Style Questionnaire (SCSQ; Yaning Xie) were used to measure emotion regulation strategies and positive coping styles, respectively. SPSS 27.0 software was utilized to conduct one - way ANOVA, chi - square tests, simple effect analysis, etc. The PROCESS 4.2 macro program was employed to perform chain mediation effect analysis of emotion regulation strategies and positive coping styles. The statistical significance level was set at P ≤ 0.05.ResultsThe combined intervention group showed significant improvements (P < 0.05) in all dimensions of mobile phone addiction—withdrawal, loss of control, inefficiency, and escapism—as well as in total scores, outperforming both the badminton-only and control groups. Emotion regulation strategies and positive coping styles were found to play partial chain mediating roles in the relationship between PA intervention and improvements in mobile phone addiction. The total indirect effect (0.689) accounted for 77.94% of the total effect (0.884). Path analysis further revealed that the intervention indirectly reduced addictive behaviors by first improving emotion regulation strategies, followed by enhancing positive coping.ConclusionCompared with single-form exercise, combined PA is more effective in alleviating mobile phone addiction among college students, primarily through the chain mediating effects of improved emotion regulation strategies and enhanced positive coping styles. It is recommended that universities and relevant institutions promote combined physical activities to help college students reduce mobile phone addiction and improve their overall well - being.

Introduction

With the widespread adoption of mobile phones and the rapid advancement of mobile internet technology, mobile phones have become indispensable tools in college students’ daily learning, life, and social interactions [1]. However, the problem of addiction resulting from excessive mobile phone use has become increasingly prominent. mobile phone addiction refers to the excessive reliance on mobile phone use, leading to psychological and behavioral dysregulation, which adversely affects normal life, academic performance, and social functioning [2]. According to the 54th Statistical Report on China Internet Development released by the China Internet Network Information Center, as of June 2024, the number of mobile internet users in China has approached 1.1 billion, with an average weekly internet usage time of 26.7 h per person [3]. Meanwhile, the 2022 Survey Report on the Mental Health of Chinese College Students published by the Chinese Academy of Sciences indicated that approximately 30% of college students exhibit excessive mobile phone use, with 15% reaching addiction levels [4]. Studies have shown that mobile phone addiction is closely associated with emotional issues such as anxiety and depression [5], and may also lead to declining academic performance and deteriorating interpersonal relationships [6]. Therefore, it is imperative to implement effective intervention strategies targeting mobile phone addiction among college students, in order to promote their physical and mental well-being as well as academic development.

In recent years, physical activity(PA) interventions have been widely recognized as an effective strategy to alleviate mobile phone addiction. Physical activity(PA) not only improves physical health but also reduces the risk of mobile phone addiction by regulating psychological states, relieving stress, and alleviating anxiety [7]. According to Self-Determination Theory, individuals can satisfy their psychological needs, enhance intrinsic motivation, and increase self-efficacy by engaging in self-chosen activities, thereby achieving effective behavioral change [8]. According to self - determination theory, humans possess three fundamental psychological needs: autonomy, relatedness, and competence [9]. As smartphones continue to evolve in functionality, they are playing an increasingly pivotal role in fulfilling individuals’ three basic psychological requirements. When college students’ psychological needs remain unmet in real - life scenarios, they may resort to excessive smartphone use as a form of compensation, leading to what is known as “pathological compensation,” of which mobile phone addiction is a typical manifestation.For college students engaging in physical exercise, it not only cuts down on their smartphone usage time but also offers psychological fulfillment in the real world. This real - world satisfaction serves as a substitute for the compensatory mechanism associated with mobile phone addiction, thereby reducing the likelihood of developing such an addiction. This phenomenon aligns with and provides evidence for the compensation hypothesis theory [10].Previous research has indicated that active involvement in sports can effectively alleviate the issue of mobile phone addiction among college students. The underlying mechanism lies in its ability to shape college students’ stable personality traits [11]. Specifically, participating in sports allows college students to cultivate personality characteristics that enable them to handle stressors such as perceived pressure and cognitive decline by adopting a healthy lifestyle.Moreover, sports can significantly enhance college students’ interpersonal relationships, offer positive emotional experiences, and bolster their psychological resilience and self - efficacy. These factors collectively work to prevent mobile phone addiction at its root cause [12–15]. The study revealed that a combined exercise intervention, which integrates various exercise modalities, can achieve a synergistic effect akin to “1 + 1 > 2” in terms of intervention outcomes. This additive advantage means that, whether for physical health indicators or mental well - being, the combined approach yields a more favorable intervention effect compared to a single exercise mode [16, 17]. From a theoretical perspective, the self-determination theory suggests that individuals achieve behavioral changes by satisfying their psychological needs through autonomously chosen activities. As a popular and competitive sport, badminton can fully meet college students needs for autonomy, relatedness, and competence: College students can determine their participation methods and strategies autonomously to fulfill their need for autonomy; they can establish social relationships through interactions with teammates or opponents, satisfying their need for relatedness; and they can experience competence enhancement by improving their badminton skills and defeating opponents, thus meeting their need for competence. Badminton is a typical example of single-sport interventions. Empirical studies [18–20] have shown that badminton can improve participants emotional states and psychological resilience, and alleviate negative emotions such as anxiety and depression. Rope skipping, with its flexible forms and fast pace, can enhance physical coordination and reaction abilities while providing positive emotions and psychological satisfaction. However, a single form of exercise may not comprehensively cover all needs to achieve the best intervention effect. Based on this, this study intends to integrate the advantages of both to create a novel combined exercise intervention approach. This approach may meet the multiple needs of college students for physical fitness improvement and psychological adjustment, and enhance intrinsic motivation and self-efficacy through diverse exercise experiences. Its flexibility and fun can increase the intervention’s appeal and participation, further improving its overall effectiveness. In addressing the issue of mobile phone addiction among college students, this intervention method is both practical and innovative, providing a scientific basis for exploring effective measures. Based on this, the study proposes Hypothesis 1: A combined intervention involving badminton and rope skipping is more effective than single badminton intervention in reducing mobile phone addiction among college students.

Emotion regulation strategies play a critical role in individuals’ coping behaviors and mental health, fundamentally enabling them to maintain psychological balance and adapt to environmental stress by recognizing, regulating, and expressing emotions [18]. Emotion regulation strategies mainly consist of cognitive reappraisal and expressive suppression, with cognitive reappraisal and other positive regulation methods recognized as effective means for alleviating negative emotions. Previous studies have shown that enhancing emotion regulation ability helps reduce individuals’ reliance on maladaptive behaviors [19]. Moreover, research has indicated that individuals with poor emotion regulation are more likely to use mobile phones to escape negative emotions, which may lead to mobile phone addiction [20]. PA interventions, as a positive approach to promoting mental health, may indirectly reduce mobile phone addiction by enhancing individuals emotion regulation capacities. According to emotion regulation theory, exercise can modulate individuals emotional experiences, thereby mitigating the impact of negative emotions such as anxiety and depression, and reducing emotional dependence on mobile phones [21]. Studies have found that regular PA can significantly enhance emotion regulation by increasing positive emotions and decreasing negative ones, thereby indirectly lowering the incidence of behavioral addictions [22]. In addition, existing research has further confirmed the mediating role of emotion regulation in behavioral interventions [23]. Research suggests that physical exercise can improve emotion regulation strategies, thereby helping participants reduce their dependence on unhealthy behaviors [24]. Furthermore, improved emotion regulation ability can enhance individuals adherence to healthy behaviors and intrinsic motivation [25], thereby creating a positive feedback loop. Based on these findings, this study proposes Hypothesis 2: Emotion regulation strategies mediate the relationship between PA interventions and mobile phone addiction in college students.

Positive coping refers to an individuals use of effective strategies to solve problems or mitigate negative impacts when facing stress and challenges [26]. Compared to negative coping styles, positive coping can help individuals effectively manage stress and emotions, thereby promoting mental health and reducing maladaptive behaviors. In the context of mobile phone addiction, individuals often rely on phones to escape emotional distress and daily pressures due to a lack of effective coping strategies, eventually developing addictive behaviors. Existing research suggests that exercise, as a healthy coping strategy, can help individuals improve their coping mechanisms, particularly when facing stress, anxiety, or emotional distress [27]. Exercise interventions can enhance individuals’ coping ability by providing positive emotional experiences and opportunities for relaxation, making them more inclined to adopt positive coping strategies rather than relying on unhealthy behaviors such as excessive phone use. Some studies indicate that individuals who regularly participate in PA can enhance their positive coping skills through self-regulation and problem-solving during exercise, thereby reducing dependence on unhealthy behaviors [28]. Moreover, exercise interventions can foster social support and a sense of group belonging [29], which further strengthens individuals positive coping capacity. Research also finds that exercise can significantly reduce depressive symptoms and anxiety by enhancing individuals’ positive coping, thereby decreasing their reliance on behaviors such as mobile phone addiction [30]. It is therefore hypothesized that exercise interventions may indirectly reduce mobile phone addiction among college students by enhancing their positive coping strategies. Thus, Hypothesis 3 is proposed in this study: positive coping mediates the relationship between exercise intervention and mobile phone addiction among college students.

Improvement in emotion regulation strategies can help individuals better identify and manage negative emotions, thereby laying the foundation for choosing more effective coping mechanisms. According to emotion regulation theory, emotion regulation is a core competency of mental health, and its improvement can significantly influence behavioral choices. The stress-coping model [31] suggests a bidirectional relationship between emotional states and coping strategies: when an individual’s emotional state improves, the likelihood of adopting positive coping strategies significantly increases. Research has shown that PA can enhance individuals’ tendency to adopt positive coping strategies by improving their emotion regulation ability, thereby significantly reducing the occurrence of unhealthy behaviors [32]. Other studies have indicated that exercise interventions not only directly reduce negative emotions by enhancing emotion regulation but also indirectly improve coping strategies, ultimately exerting a positive influence on behavioral improvement [33]. Therefore, emotion regulation strategies and positive coping styles may form a mutually reinforcing chain mediation process between PA interventions and mobile phone addiction. This chain mechanism may be a key pathway through which PA interventions alleviate mobile phone addiction. Based on this, the current study proposes Hypothesis 4: Emotion regulation strategies and positive coping styles serve as chained mediators between PA interventions and mobile phone addiction among college students.

Based on the above assumptions, this study proposes a theoretical model framework (as shown in Fig. 1). It aims to explore the mechanism by which a combined intervention of badminton and fancy rope skipping affects mobile phone addiction among college students, with a focus on examining the mediating and chain-mediating roles of emotion regulation strategies and positive coping styles. By constructing a theoretical model and verifying the intervention pathways of PA on mobile phone addiction, this study reveals the unique value of exercise intervention in psychological regulation and behavioral modification, and provides a scientific framework and practical reference for addressing mobile phone addiction among university students.

Methods

Study design

The present study employed a 3 × 2 mixed experimental design, where group (experimental and control) served as the between-subjects factor, and time (baseline and post-test) served as the within-subjects factor. The study employed a randomized controlled design, comprising two experimental groups (combined intervention group and badminton group) and one control group. Recruitment of participants was carried out through the “Xidian Sports” online campus platform and the physical bulletin board on the Xidian University campus. The recruitment announcement, titled “Recruitment of Participants for Exercise Intervention on Mobile Phone Addiction,” was open to all undergraduate students at Xidian University. Its purpose was to identify students who met specific criteria to participate in the experiment. Students expressed their willingness to participate by accessing the online form link in the recruitment announcement, filling in their personal information, and submitting the form. Subsequently, participants were contacted via email for screening at the Mental Health Education Center of Xidian University and were diagnosed by professional physicians at Xi’an International Medical Center. All participants volunteered through recruitment announcements issued by the university. Participants were screened at the Mental Health Education Center of Xidian University and diagnosed by professional physicians at Xi’an International Medical Center. Moreover, the researchers explained the purpose and details of the study to all participants and obtained informed consent from them. The informed consent document encompassed study objectives, procedures, participant rights, potential risks, and other relevant information. After recruitment and diagnosis, all participants were randomly assigned to two experimental groups and one control group. All three groups maintained their regular daily routines. In addition, the combined intervention group received a 12-week combined intervention of badminton and rope skipping, while the badminton group underwent a 12-week badminton intervention. No additional PA interventions were permitted during the study. The two tests were conducted before the intervention began and after it ended. The entire experimental procedure consisted of three phases: pre-intervention testing, 12-week PA intervention, and post-intervention testing.

This research project has been approved by the relevant ethics committee or institution (Approval No. HUSOM2024-640) and was conducted in strict accordance with ethical guidelines. In this study, the rights and privacy of participants were respected and protected, and the confidentiality of their personal information was ensured. This study adhered to the ethical principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki.

Participants

In this study, we employed G*power (version 3.3.9.6) software to calculate the sample size for the experiment. In line with previous standards [34, 35], we set the effect size to 0.25, the statistical test power to 0.95, the significance level to 0.05, the grouping size to 3, and the number of measurements to 2. The statistical results indicated that a sample size of 66 could achieve significance. However, given that our study utilized chain mediation analysis, which is characterized by high model complexity with multiple pathways and mediating variables, the sample size requirement was further elevated. Relevant literature suggests that for mediation analysis, particularly in the case of chain mediation models, the sample size should satisfy the criterion of having at least 10 samples per path. This is to ensure the stability and statistical power of model estimation. Consequently, we ultimately determined that an experimental group with a total sample size of at least 120 participants would be involved in the study.

The implementation of the inclusion criteria was integrated into the entire process of participant screening and diagnosis. (1) currently enrolled undergraduate students who are regular users of mobile phones; (2) Participants underwent an initial screening at the Mental Health Center of Xidian University. Professional psychological counselors at the center employed the Mobile Phone Addiction Index (MPAI) scale to assess whether the participants met the criteria for moderate - to - severe adolescent mobile phone addiction(total score greater than 50). Those who met the criteria were included in the subsequent intervention process, while those who did not were excluded. This diagnostic step was conducted during the pre - formal - experiment screening phase. (3) aged between 18 and 22 years. Exclusion criteria: (1) presence of health conditions that could hinder participation in PA, such as musculoskeletal disorders, cardiovascular diseases, severe obesity, or other chronic illnesses that prevent completion of the intervention; (2) individuals diagnosed with severe psychiatric disorders (e.g., depression, anxiety, or other mental illnesses), or those currently undergoing professional psychological treatment; (3) professional athletes or those with extensive training experience in badminton or fancy rope skipping, to avoid confounding due to motor proficiency. A total of 218 eligible participants participated in the study. During the process, 16 participants withdrew from the study due to personal reasons or injury; 7 participants were excluded from the analysis due to low adherence to the training program (less than 50%). Ultimately, data from 195 participants were included in the final analysis. Among them, 74 were assigned to the combined intervention group, 62 to the badminton intervention group, and 59 to the control group (Fig. 2).

Intervention

The experimental design of this research revolves around the combined intervention of badminton and rhythmic rope skipping, as well as the sole badminton exercise intervention, with the aim of exploring the impact of different exercise intervention approaches on participants. The entire intervention process spans 12 weeks, with 5 intervention sessions scheduled per week, and each session lasting 45 min. Regarding heart rate control, to ensure that participants exercise at an appropriate intensity, we maintain the average heart rate within the range calculated by (220 - age) × (60% − 69%), which is 128–148 beats per minute. To accurately monitor heart rates, we employ the POLAR M430 heart rate monitor. This device can provide real-time and precise feedback on participants heart rate data, enabling coaches to adjust the exercise intensity in a timely manner. The intervention content adheres to a structured principle and is meticulously designed in a progression from simple to complex. It is specifically divided into three sequential stages: the adaptation stage, the basic stage, and the advanced stage (Table 1).

During the adaptation stage, the primary objectives are to help participants become acquainted with the basic forms of badminton and rope skipping, establish regular exercise habits, reduce sensitivity to sports equipment, cultivate interest in participation and class engagement awareness, and enhance adaptability to the intervention environment through coordination and agility exercises. In this stage, tactile experience activities such as grip practice and light hitting drills will be conducted to allow participants to feel the texture of the racket and shuttlecock. Additionally, rope skipping rhythm experiences and learning of basic skipping movements like single-leg jumps and double-leg jumps will be carried out. The basic stage focuses on enabling participants to learn and master the basic movement skills of badminton and rhythmic rope skipping, improve motor coordination, rhythm sense, and physical fitness, and lay a foundation for learning more complex skills. This stage will allocate 6 weeks to learning basic badminton skills such as forehand serves, backcourt clears, and net drops, and 3 weeks to learning basic rope skipping techniques like double-leg jumps, cross jumps, and single swing jumps.The advanced stage promotes the flexible application of the learned badminton and rhythmic rope skipping skills in specific contexts through the design of engaging situational activities, achieving skill generalization. Simultaneously, it emphasizes cultivating participants’ team cooperation awareness and social skills, and enhancing their interest in exercise and behavioral self-regulation. For instance, hybrid activities such as the “Skipping Challenge with Target Hit” will be organized, requiring participants to complete designated skipping tasks before hitting the target shuttlecock. Group cooperation games like continuous team skipping and designated target hitting will also be arranged, highlighting teamwork and rule compliance.Throughout the research period, to ensure the accuracy and reliability of the research results, it is explicitly stipulated that all participants are not allowed to engage in any additional physical interventions. Participants in the control group continue with their routine daily exercise patterns and do not participate in any additional, organized exercise intervention programs.Experimental Group 1, building on the control group’s routine, undergoes a 12-week combined intervention of badminton and rhythmic rope skipping, following the systematically designed three-stage courses for training. Experimental Group 2 conducts a 12-week sole badminton exercise intervention, with its course content mainly centered around various badminton skills and training, also following a progressive teaching sequence.

During the research process, a total of 16 participants withdrew from the study due to personal reasons (such as family matters, time conflicts, etc.) or injuries. Additionally, data from 7 participants were deemed invalid due to low adherence to the training program (below 50%). Finally, data from 195 participants were included in the analysis, with 74 in the combined intervention group, 62 in the badminton intervention group, and 59 in the control group. Each training session included a warm-up, main exercise activity, and a cool-down. Figure 3 illustrates the complete intervention protocol.

Measures

Before the experiment began, demographic data including gender, age, and other personal information were collected. Previous research has indicated that psychological factors such as depression and anxiety may influence participants’ mobile phone addiction behaviors. Therefore, the Depression Anxiety Stress Scale (DASS-21) was employed in this study to control for potential confounding variables in subsequent analyses. The DASS − 21 was developed by Lovibond et al. [36] and later revised by Gong Xiang et al. [37]. It comprises three dimensions: depression, anxiety, and stress, with each dimension consisting of seven questions. The Likert four - point scoring method was utilized, assigning scores ranging from 0 to 3 points, corresponding to responses from “never” to “almost always.” Higher scores suggest a greater likelihood of experiencing depression, anxiety, and stress during the preceding week. Previous studies have demonstrated that the scale exhibits satisfactory reliability and validity when applied to college students in China [38]. In the current study, the internal consistency coefficient was found to be 0.781, indicating a good level of reliability for the scale. The DASS-21 was completed by the participants themselves. The main measurement tools used before and after the intervention included:

Mobile phone addiction

The Mobile Phone Addiction Index (MPAI) developed by Leung (2008) [39] was used for assessment. This scale consists of 17 items categorized into four dimensions. The four dimensions are: withdrawal, loss of control, inefficiency, and escapism. The scale uses a 5-point Likert scoring system, with higher total scores indicating higher levels of mobile phone addiction. Previous studies have demonstrated good reliability and validity of this scale among Chinese college students. To further validate the construct validity of the scale, a confirmatory factor analysis was conducted, yielding the following fit indices: χ²/df = 4.12, RMSEA = 0.053, GFI = 0.942, TLI = 0.906, and CFI = 0.954. These indices collectively suggest that the questionnaire exhibits a sound structural design. In the present study, the internal consistency coefficient was 0.811, indicating good reliability.

Emotion regulation strategies

The Gross-John Emotion Regulation Questionnaire (ERQ) [40] was used. The questionnaire includes two factors: cognitive reappraisal and expressive suppression, with a total of 10 items. It is scored on a 7-point Likert scale, with higher scores indicating a greater tendency to use the strategy. Studies have shown a Cronbach’ s alpha of 0.78 for the scale, with good fit indices, making it suitable for assessing cognitive reappraisal and expressive suppression levels in college students. To further ascertain the construct validity of the scale, a confirmatory factor analysis was conducted, revealing the following fit indices: χ²/df = 5.45, RMSEA = 0.008, GFI = 0.921, TLI = 0.952, and CFI = 0.928. These indices collectively underscore the robustness of the questionnaire’s structural integrity.In this study, the internal consistency coefficient of the scale was 0.738.

Positive coping style

The simplified coping style questionnaire (SCSQ) [41] was used, with the positive coping subscale consisting of 12 items. It employs a 4-point Likert scale, where 1 indicates “not adopted” and 4 indicates “frequently adopted”. The sum of item scores constitutes the total score for positive coping, with higher scores indicating greater use of positive coping strategies. To further substantiate the construct validity of the scale, a confirmatory factor analysis was conducted, yielding the following fit indices: χ²/df = 4.07, RMSEA = 0.045, GFI = 0.944, TLI = 0.923, and CFI = 0.965. Collectively, these indices attest to the satisfactory structural quality of the questionnaire.In this study, the internal consistency coefficient was 0.712, suggesting good reliability.

The Cronbach’s alpha values for the aforementioned variables all exceeded 0.7, the factor loadings for each item were above 0.7, and the reliability coefficients for each variable combination were also greater than 0.7. These findings collectively suggest that the scale demonstrates good reliability.Furthermore, exploratory factor analysis was employed to derive the construct factors, which were found to align with the theoretical framework. This consistency indicates that the scale structure functions effectively.In terms of convergent validity, the average variance extracted (AVE) for each variable was above 0.5, signifying that the scale possesses good convergent validity. Meanwhile, the analysis results revealed that the inter - variable correlation coefficients were greater than the square root of the average variance extracted (AVE) for the respective variables. This finding implies that the scale exhibits good discriminant validity [42].Finally, the model fitting results, including CMIN/DF = 2.013, RMR = 0.040, RMSEA = 0.054, GFI = 0.852, NFI = 0.906, IFI = 0.956, TLI = 0.965, and CFI = 0.954, collectively demonstrate that the overall fitting effect of the model is satisfactory.

Statistical analysis

In SPSS 27.0, one-way ANOVA was used to test the homogeneity of age, DASS-21, ERQ, and SCSQ scores among participants. Since each group of participants exceeded 50, the normality of continuous variables was assessed using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. If the data met the assumptions of normal distribution, independent sample t-tests were applied. Otherwise, non-parametric tests were employed. Chi-square tests were conducted to examine gender differences, aiming to determine whether there were significant statistical differences in participants’ demographic variables and related behavioral symptom severity. Repeated measures ANOVA was used to investigate the effect of the three groups on MPAI scores. If a significant time × group interaction was found, simple effects analysis was performed. The PROCESS 4.2 macro was used to conduct chain mediation analysis of emotion regulation strategies and positive coping styles. The statistical significance level was set at P ≤ 0.05.

These results indicate that the three groups were homogeneous in terms of demographic characteristics and scores of all variables (Table 2). Multicollinearity Test

Due to significant correlations among variables, there may be multicollinearity issues that could lead to unstable results. The collinearity diagnostics showed that the tolerance values for all predictors ranged from 0.482 to 0.875 (all > 0.1), and the variance inflation factors (VIFs) ranged from 1.147 to 2.383 (all < 5), indicating no severe multicollinearity and supporting further chain mediation analysis.

Chain Mediation Analysis of Emotion Regulation Strategies and Positive Coping Styles.

In this study, the SPSS macro Process 4.2 developed by Hayes was used to examine the chain mediation effects among PA type, emotion regulation strategies, positive coping styles, and mobile phone addiction [43]. Model 6 was applied, with 5,000 bootstrap samples and a default 95% confidence interval. PA type was dummy-coded (1 = combined intervention group, 0 = badminton intervention group), with emotion regulation strategies and positive coping styles as mediators, and mobile phone addiction as the dependent variable.

First, the total effect of exercise on mobile phone addiction was 0.884 (95% CI: 0.621–1.246), indicating a significant overall improvement in mobile phone addiction among college students due to PA intervention. Second, the direct effect of exercise on mobile phone addiction was 0.195 (95% CI: 0.012–0.561), suggesting a partial chain mediation role of emotion regulation strategies and positive coping styles between exercise and mobile phone addiction. Regarding indirect effects, the mediation effect of exercise on mobile phone addiction via emotion regulation strategies (Indirect Effect 1) was 0.154 (95% CI: 0.024–0.568), which was significant, indicating that improving emotion regulation strategies alone can also effectively reduce mobile phone addiction. The chain mediation effect of exercise through emotion regulation strategies and then positive coping styles (Indirect Effect 3) was 0.376 (95% CI: 0.032–0.214), which was also significant, suggesting that exercise reduces mobile phone addiction by first enhancing emotion regulation strategies, then improving positive coping mechanisms. Finally, the total indirect effect of exercise on mobile phone addiction was 0.689 (95% CI: 0.468–0.831), accounting for 77.94% of the total effect, indicating that the mediating pathways play a critical role in the influence of exercise on mobile phone addiction (Fig. 5; Table 7). Compared to the badminton-only group, the combined intervention group exhibited a stronger chain mediation effect of emotion regulation strategies and positive coping styles on the reduction of mobile phone addiction.

Discussion

The effect of sports intervention on college students mobile phone addiction.

This study seeks to examine how a combined exercise intervention involving badminton and figure rope skipping influences the reduction of mobile phone addiction among college students, while also analyzing the chain mediating effects of emotional regulation strategies and positive coping styles. The results demonstrated significant benefits of the combined exercise intervention in mitigating mobile phone addiction, with notable improvements in areas such as withdrawal, loss of control, inefficiency, and escapism. When compared to the badminton-only intervention group and the control group, the combined intervention group showed a more pronounced decrease in mobile phone addiction. These findings offer robust evidence supporting the efficacy of exercise interventions in addressing mobile phone addiction among college students, particularly highlighting the potential superiority of multi-modal exercise programs.

The findings align with prior studies [44, 45]. One study indicated that a single form of exercise can significantly alleviate issues related to mobile phone addiction, though its effects may be limited to specific behavioral or psychological domains [46]. Consistent with existing evidence [47], exercise interventions not only reduce addictive behaviors but also improve emotion regulation and self-efficacy through multi-modal approaches. Our results confirm this: students in the combined intervention group exhibited reduced mobile phone addiction, alongside higher positive affect and stress management capabilities. These findings suggest that interventions incorporating diverse PA modalities can effectively alleviate mobile phone addiction by promoting a bidirectional regulation mechanism of emotion and behavior.

The efficacy of combined exercise interventions stems from their holistic alignment with college students’ psychological and physical needs. Badminton training primarily enhances hand-eye coordination, upper body strength, and strategic decision-making, eliciting emotions ranging from competitive tension to achievement satisfaction. In contrast, rhythmic rope skipping emphasizes full-body coordination, rhythm, and relaxation, requiring minimal cognitive load. This complementary psychological profile—strategic engagement in badminton versus rhythmic flow in skipping—enables students to derive diversified emotional benefits, which may synergistically reduce mobile phone addiction.Beyond psychological synergy, the combined format optimizes physical engagement and adherence. Diversified training addresses varied fitness needs while enhancing enjoyment through novelty. Research confirms that multi-modal activities activate broader motor neuron networks, improving learning outcomes and performance [48]. Consistent with neurobiological evidence linking physical activity (PA) to increased brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF)—a key driver of neural plasticity [49–51]. The combination of badminton and fancy rope skipping not only provides diverse neural stimulation but also activates cognitive and emotional regulation networks through repetitive and challenging tasks [52, 53], aligning with our finding that students in the combined group exhibited higher participation rates, sustained adherence, and greater reductions in mobile phone addiction compared to single-exercise controls. These results support Hypothesis 1, demonstrating the superiority of multi-modal interventions in addressing behavioral addiction.

Mechanism Analysis of Exercise Intervention on mobile phone Addiction Among College Students.

The study results demonstrated that emotion regulation strategies significantly mediated the relationship between exercise intervention and the reduction of mobile phone addiction behaviors among college students, thereby validating Hypothesis H2. This outcome confirms the efficacy of exercise intervention in mitigating mobile phone addiction through emotional regulation pathways, aligning with both theoretical and empirical evidence from prior research.

Emotion regulation theory posits that an individual’s capacity to manage emotions directly influences behavioral choices and mental health outcomes [54, 55]. Individuals with deficient emotion regulation skills are more prone to engage in avoidance or dependency behaviors, such as excessive mobile phone use to alleviate anxiety and stress [56]. This study demonstrates that exercise interventions mitigate mobile phone dependence by enhancing college students’ cognitive reappraisal abilities, enabling more adaptive coping with negative emotions—a finding consistent with prior research [57, 58].Physical activity is strongly associated with positive emotional experiences, which significantly improve emotion regulation capacity while reducing negative emotions and maladaptive behaviors through positive affect induction [59]. The competitive, rapid-response nature of badminton effectively releases stress and disperses negative emotions, whereas the rhythmic, enjoyable characteristics of figure skipping stimulate positive affect. Their combination provides students with diversified emotional catharsis pathways, thereby diminishing emotional compensation through mobile phone use. The achievement and physical vitality derived from exercise tasks further reinforce self-efficacy [60], creating a virtuous cycle of “behavioral reinforcement → emotional improvement.“At the physiological level, combined exercise optimizes emotion regulation through neurotransmitter modulation. Regular physical activity has been shown to increase endorphin and dopamine secretion, generating pleasure and alleviating anxiety/depression [61, 62]. In this study, the synergistic effects of badminton (aerobic-anaerobic hybrid) and figure skipping rope (rhythmic aerobic) likely enhanced neurotransmitter system activation through dual exercise modalities, thereby providing physiological support for emotion regulation strategy implementation.

Regarding the mediating role of positive coping, it was found that positive coping strategies also significantly mediate the relationship between PA interventions and the reduction of mobile phone addiction behaviors in college students, thus validating research hypothesis H3. This indicates that PA interventions can significantly reduce mobile phone addiction by enhancing students coping abilities. This finding supports the stress-coping model proposed by Lazarus and Folkman (1984) [63], which posits that the coping strategies individuals adopt in response to stress play a crucial role in determining behavioral outcomes.

As a constructive strategy for individuals to cope with stress and emotional distress, proactive coping styles not only effectively alleviate negative emotions and promote mental health [64] but also significantly reduce the level of mobile phone dependence [65]. In this study, by designing diversified exercise scenarios that combined badminton (oriented towards cooperation and competition) with rope skipping (providing rhythmic relaxation), we significantly enhanced college students’ ability to actively solve problems, thereby reducing their reliance on mobile phones as a tool for emotional relief. This finding aligns with existing research [11, 66].

The positive effects of exercise intervention stem from the synergistic action of multiple mechanisms: Firstly, at the psychological regulation level, exercise provides pleasurable experiences through the release of neurotransmitters such as endorphins, thereby reducing anxiety and depression levels [67]. Meanwhile, the design of challenging tasks enhances participants’ emotional stability and psychological resilience, laying a psychological foundation for the formation of proactive coping strategies. Secondly, at the cognitive functioning level, the dynamic exercise scenarios require participants to continuously adjust strategies and plan actions in real time. This sustained cognitive training effectively strengthens executive function and problem-solving abilities, echoing research findings on the cognitive-enhancing effects of exercise interventions [68, 69]. Thirdly, at the social support level, the team-based cooperation model fosters a close social support network. The sense of collective belonging obtained not only alleviates loneliness but also reinforces behavioral tendencies toward proactive coping through group interactions [70].

Notably, the coping abilities cultivated through exercise interventions demonstrate cross-context transferability. Participants gradually applied skills such as strategic adjustment and stress tolerance acquired during exercise to academic challenges and social situations, forming a virtuous cycle of “exercise-scenario training to real-life application.” This exercise-based intervention model provides a comprehensive solution for addressing mobile phone addiction among college students by integrating psychological regulation, cognitive enhancement, and social support.

In terms of the chain mediating effects of emotion regulation strategies and positive coping styles, the results indicate that the effect of exercise intervention on mobile phone addiction behavior among college students is partially mediated by this chain mechanism, thus validating research hypothesis H4. This suggests that the intervention effect is not entirely dependent on the chain mediation pathway, but instead operates through multi-level and multi-dimensional mechanisms. On one hand, since the direct effect of the exercise intervention on mobile phone addiction remains significant, it may influence mobile phone addiction behavior directly by reducing phone usage time, increasing time spent on PA as a substitute, and enhancing self-efficacy. On the other hand, besides emotion regulation strategies and positive coping styles, other psychological variables (e.g., social support, self-efficacy) may also play important roles in the effectiveness of the intervention. Exercise interventions may reduce dependence on mobile phone use indirectly by promoting social interactions and alleviating feelings of loneliness. These potential mediating variables, which were not included in the present model, may partly explain the limitations of the chain mediation pathway.

The mechanisms underlying the partial chained mediation effects can be explained from three perspectives: emotional regulation, behavioral transfer, and direct effects. First, improvement in emotional regulation strategies significantly reduced participants’ reliance on mobile phones as a tool for emotional relief [71], thereby laying a psychological foundation for the formation of positive coping strategies. This aligns with the theoretical perspective of emotion regulation, which posits that strategies such as cognitive reappraisal can effectively reduce the impact of negative emotions, thereby enhancing psychological resilience in the face of stress [72]. Second, as a subsequent link in the chained path, positive coping functions through behavioral transfer mechanisms, transforming emotional regulation capacity into specific constructive behavioral choices. Experiences of teamwork and goal achievement during PA tasks increased participants propensity to adopt proactive coping strategies when dealing with academic and social stress. In addition, the significance of the direct effects suggests that exercise interventions may directly alleviate mobile phone addiction through mechanisms such as reducing opportunities for phone use, providing alternative activities, and enhancing the sense of achievement derived from PA. This direct effect does not rely on mediating variables, making it more immediate and pronounced. Overall, the partial chained mediation effects reflect a multi-level, multi-path intervention mechanism, suggesting that future intervention designs should more comprehensively consider the roles of various psychological and behavioral variables to further optimize outcomes.

Although studies have confirmed that emotion regulation strategies and positive coping styles partially mediate the relationship between PA interventions and improvements in mobile phone addiction behaviors, their contribution to fully explaining this relationship remains limited. This suggests that future exercise intervention designs should more comprehensively consider the roles of multiple mediating variables to directly enhance the explanatory power of the chain mediation model. Moreover, the limitations of the observed chain mediation pathway indicate the need to explore more diversified intervention strategies that integrate psychoeducation or cognitive training components to strengthen the synergistic effects of emotion regulation and positive coping capacities. In addition, due to factors such as time, resources, and costs, this study only conducted assessments before and after the intervention, without carrying out follow - up evaluations. This significantly limits our understanding of whether the intervention effects can be sustained over a long period. In future research, we will strive to overcome these difficulties and incorporate follow - up evaluation sessions so as to gain a more comprehensive and in - depth understanding of the long - term impact and sustained mechanisms of physical activity (PA) intervention on mobile phone addiction among college students. The current experimental design does not include a “rope skipping alone” group, which may prevent us from clearly determining whether the superiority of the combined intervention is a result of the synergistic effects of different influencing factors or merely due to the effect of rope skipping. To more thoroughly investigate the relative contributions of each component and the nature of their interactions, future research designs will incorporate a separate rope skipping group.

Conclusion

(1) The effectiveness of combined exercise interventions: The study demonstrated that combined PA interventions are more effective than single-mode interventions in alleviating mobile phone addiction among college students, particularly showing significant advantages in reducing withdrawal symptoms, loss of control, inefficiency, and escapism, thereby validating the superiority of multidimensional physical training. (2) Mechanisms of the mediating effect: The study further revealed that the effect of exercise interventions on mobile phone addiction is not only direct but primarily achieved through a chain-mediated effect involving emotion regulation strategies and positive coping styles. This suggests that the actual impact of exercise interventions largely depends on their positive influence on mediating variables, thereby indirectly improving college students’ mobile phone addiction behaviors.

(3) The research findings not only validate the applicability of emotion regulation theory and the stress-coping model in interventions for college students’ mobile phone addiction, but also highlight the advantages of multidimensional exercise.

They suggest that effective exercise interventions should emphasize comprehensive strategies and take into account multi-level mediating factors, providing actionable directions for clinical interventions. From a broader social perspective, alleviating mobile phone addiction among college students helps enhance the overall quality of this future pillar group, promotes their all-round development, and holds positive significance for improving the quality of social human resources and fostering social harmony and stability. Moreover, it offers a scientific basis and practical paradigm for society to address the issue of mobile phone dependence among adolescents.

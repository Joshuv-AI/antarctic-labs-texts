Orthokeratinized odontogenic cyst of the mandible - A case report
Authors: Hung-Pin Lin, Chun-Pin Chiang
Published: 2026

Odontogenic cysts are characterized by various histopathological features and biologic behaviors. An orthokeratinized odontogenic cyst (OOC) is a rare odontogenic cyst lined by a layer of orthokeratinized stratified squamous epithelium.1–5 Here, we reported a case of OOC of the mandible of a 46-year-old male patient who was initially diagnosed as a case of odontogenic keratocyst (OKC), a relatively more common odontogenic cyst compared to OOC. The report of this OOC case was reviewed and approved by the Institutional Review Board at the MacKay Memorial Hospital, Taipei, Taiwan (26MMHIS011e).

This 46-year-old male patient had visited one of the hospitals in the New Taipei City in Taiwan due to painful swelling of the left posterior mandibular area. Incisional biopsy of the lesion revealed an OKC. The patient sought further evaluation and management of the lesion in our hospital due to personal reasons. The past medical history of the patient was not contributory. The panoramic radiography revealed a well-circumscribed radiolucent lesion at the periapical area of teeth 36 and 37 and near the dental follicle of the impacted tooth 38. Evidence of external apical root resorption of the tooth 37 was also noted (Fig. 1A). The buccal cortical bone plate perforation was highlighted by the computed tomography (CT) (Fig. 1B). Based on the histopathologic diagnosis provided by the previous hospital as well as a thorough discussion with the patient, the surgical intervention with marsupialization of the lesion under general anesthesia was decided. The specimen was sent for histopathological examination. Microscopically, it showed a cystic lesion lined by a thin layer of orthokeratinized stratified squamous epithelium with abundant lamellated orthokeratin flakes in the cystic lumen (Fig. 1C and D). The prominent granular layer was discovered in the superficial spinous layer of the lining epithelium (Fig. 1E). The basal cells of the lining epithelium were immuno-negative for Bcl-2 (Fig. 1F). According to the above characteristic histopathologic and immunohistochemical findings, an OOC was confirmed.

The OOC was previously considered as a variant of OKC, but it is currently classified as a separate entity of the developmental odontogenic cysts.1–5 Both OOC and OKC may present as a unilocular radiolucent lesion associated with an unerupted tooth of the mandible that mimics a dentigerous cyst.1–5 The OOC usually occurs as a solitary lesion with limited cases of synchronous multiple OOCs.3 The most characteristic feature of the OOC is its lower recurrence rate compared to OKC.1–5 According to the study of a large series of OCC cases in comparison to OKC cases by Wang et al., the findings including the lack of PTCH1 mutation, the different expression patterns of the CK10/13 immunostaining of the lining epithelium, the immuno-negative or weakly immuno-positive for the Bcl-2 in the basal cell layer, and the lower Ki-67 expression in the lining epithelium support the concept that separation between OKCs and OOCs were appropriate and logical.5

The histopathologic features of OOC include (1) a cystic lining of orthokeratinized stratified squamous epithelium with lamellae of orthokeratin in the cystic lumen, (2) a prominent keratohyaline granular layer in the superficial spinous layer of the lining epithelium, and (3) a lack of evident basal cell palisading.1–5 However, the histopathologic diagnosis of OOC may be a challenge for pathologists unfamiliar with the specific histopathologic features of OOCs or those cases with the severe inflammation in the subepithelial area of the fibrous cystic wall in which the typical microscopic patterns may be altered.1–5

The correct diagnosis of a lesion provides the basis of proper treatment options with predictable outcomes. In the scenario of OKC, cyst removal with peripheral ostectomy or chemical cauterization is advocated,1 while enucleation with curettage is considered as the treatment option for OOC.1 The recurrence rate for OKC is approximately 21%–30% with the possibility of delayed recurrences of 10 or more years later, while the recurrence rate for OOC is approximately 2%.1 Our case illustrated that OOC should be considered as a differential diagnosis when making a diagnosis of OKC. The results of immunohistochemical studies may also help in the final histopathologic diagnosis of the OOC.

Leisure time internet use and holistic well-being in a sample of young adults in Türkiye: exploring the role of meaning-based coping
Authors: Gülçin Güler Öztekin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe presence of hedonic and eudaimonic well-being supports individuals’ well-being both in the present and in the future. Therefore, identifying the factors regarding these well-beings is of great importance. The aim of this study was to examine the mediating effect of meaning-based coping in the relationship between leisure time internet use and hedonic and eudaimonic well-being, while controlling age, gender, marital status and employment status.MethodThis is a cross-sectional study. This study included 568 participants. The participants’ ages ranged from 18 to 29 years, with a mean age of 21.17 years (SD = 2.99). Participants responded to demographic questions, the Leisure Internet Usage Scale, the Meaning-centered Coping Scale and the Holistic Well-being Measure. Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS 27.0. Hayes’ PROCESS Macro (Model 4) was used to examine the mediating role of meaning-based coping.ResultsThe findings of this study revealed the positive associations between leisure time internet use for social interaction, shopping, multimedia usage, and information and self-improvement, meaning-based coping, hedonic and eudaimonic well-being. This study also indicated the mediating role of meaning-based coping in the link between leisure time internet use and hedonic and eudaimonic well-being.ConclusionThese results indicate that young adults who use the internet in their leisure time, in the sample studied, tend to report higher levels of meaning-based coping, which is associated with higher hedonic and eudaimonic well-being. These results suggest that meaning-based coping strategies are associated with hedonic and eudaimonic well-being among individuals who engage in enjoyable and self-improving online leisure activities.Keywords: Leisure time internet use, Meaning-based coping, Hedonic well-being, Eudaimonic well-being

Introduction

Well-being is a complex concept concerning optimal experience and functionality. It arises from two general perspectives. The first focuses on happiness and defines well-being as the pursuit of pleasure and avoidance of pain. This is the hedonic approach. The second focuses on meaning and self-actualization and defines well-being as the degree to which a person is fully functional. This is the eudaimonic approach [1]. Numerous studies have shown that hedonic and eudaimonic behaviors can be a tremendous way to promote well-being and reduce psychological distress. For example, hedonic activity increased positive emotions, carefree attitudes, vitality, and life satisfaction by regulating emotions, while reducing negative emotions, depression, and stress. Eudaimonic behavior fostered meaningful and uplifting experiences in life. Both predicted flourishing [2]. Eudaimonic well-being facilitated post-traumatic growth by supporting individuals’ search for meaning [3]. Poor eudaimonic well-being was identified as a risk factor for mortality [4]. The presence of existential meaning and subjective happiness were negatively correlated with depressive symptoms, suggesting that they function as protective factors against depressive symptoms later in life [5]. These findings demonstrate that the presence of both hedonic and eudaimonic well-being supports individuals’ well-being and mental health, both in the present and in the future.

The internet has become an integral part of our lives in many areas. In a technologically advanced world, the internet has become an important tool not only for professional use but also for leisure activities. Kaynar and Amichai-Hamburger [6] have also noted the professional, social, and leisure use of the internet. People sometimes use the internet in their free time to socialize, communicate with friends, or share their thoughts. Sometimes they may shop at online stores, purchase services, or simply spend time on these sites even if they don’t buy anything. They can use the internet to watch TV or videos, listen to music, or play games in their free time. They can also use the internet to search for information, learn new things, read news, or support their personal development [7]. Leisure time internet use is considered a multifaceted phenomenon, and it is emphasized that different types of use can serve different psychological functions. For example, internet use based on social interaction is geared towards increasing self-esteem, building relationships, improving the quality of friendships, and fulfilling sexual self-exploration [8], while multimedia use is more associated with entertainment and emotional relief [9]. Information acquisition and self-development-focused usage is linked to more cognitive and goal-oriented processes. Online shopping behavior has a complex structure, related to both hedonic pleasure and the fulfillment of functional need [10]. However, although these different types of use are functionally distinct, they commonly represent how individuals spend their leisure time in the digital environment.

It is known that internet-based leisure activities, such as socializing through social media and spending excessive time on recreational gaming, can negatively affect people’s self-perception, emotional state, and cognitive functions [11, 12]. On the other hand, previous studies have provided evidence of the impact of these leisure activities on well-being. Internet use, including personal relaxation and entertainment, which are associated with hedonism, promoted well-being [13]. Leisure activities, particularly those involving computer use, were positively associated with hedonic and eudaimonic well-being [14]. People who engage in and enjoy leisure activities were more likely to experience positive emotions and achieve higher levels of hedonic well-being. Furthermore, leisure activities such as socializing and communicating on social media fostered a sense of belonging and increased eudaimonic well-being [15]. These results showed that leisure time internet use can promote hedonic and eudaimonic well-being in various ways.

Meaning-based coping as a mediator

Meaning-based coping involves maintaining well-being by letting go of unsustainable goals and setting new ones, making sense of what is happening, and evaluating the benefits where possible [16]. This process includes positively reframing the situation, adapting goals to the situation, and exploring spiritual beliefs to find positive meaning [17]. Eisenbeck, Carreno [18] argued that meaning-based coping aims to be “transformative.” It deals with evaluation and coping processes that can help maintain a positive emotional space even in the presence of negative emotions. It supports imbuing stressful situations and emotions with a different, more positive meaning that can ultimately lead to growth (e.g., being grateful for some parts of the experience). Meaning-based coping mechanisms have mostly been discussed within the framework of adversities. However, recent meaning making models emphasize that these processes are not limited to challenging experiences. They also emerge in response to positive experiences and contribute to the integration of these experiences into the individual’s global meaning system [19, 20]. This integrative process supports the strengthening of psychological adjustment and the increase of well-being. According to Park’s integrative model, meaning-based coping is a process of meaning-making in which individuals actively reconcile situational meanings with their global meaning systems, thereby achieving psychological balance [21]. Global meaning, encompassing individuals’ overall systems of orientation consisting of beliefs, goals, and subjective feelings, is formed through the accumulation of life experiences [21, 22]. Positive, pleasant, or negative events have a cumulative effect on beliefs. At this point, meaning-based processes function to deepen well-being by making sense of experience. Meaning-based processes encompass the entirety of life experiences, beyond negativity [22]. Meaning-based coping is a process that involves individuals re-evaluating and making sense of their experiences. In this regard, leisure time internet use can trigger these meaning-making processes by offering individuals a variety of experiences and content. Individuals can interpret these experiences in line with their own goals and values, creating new meanings, and this process can become associated with well-being. Building on this framework, the present study conceptualizes meaning-based coping as a mediating mechanism through which different types of leisure time internet use may be associated with hedonic and eudaimonic well-being. Specifically, digital leisure activities may provide situational experiences that individuals interpret and integrate into their global meaning systems, thereby influencing their holistic well-being.

The expanding literature presents studies that reveal the relationships between leisure time internet use and meaning making process, coping and well-being. For example, leisure activities supported meaningful living by contributing to promoting and/or maintaining a joyful, connected, explored, balanced, hopeful, and empowered life [23]. In addition, meaning-based coping was associated with lower levels of ill-being and higher levels of well-being [24]. Meaning in life played a central role in the formation of hedonic an eudaimonic well-being [25]. Furthermore, there are also mediation studies that include meaning in the previous literature. Risk and stress factors during difficult times were associated with a decrease in meaning-based coping and spirituality processes, which was linked to a decline in hedonic well-being [26]. A state of moment was associated with meaning in life and, consequently, with a higher degree of eudaimonic well-being [27]. Negative coping mechanisms were negatively linked to the presence of meaning and personal meaning, which was associated with greater eudaimonic well-being, suggesting that people use them as a part of their meaning system to cope with life’s difficulties and challenges [28]. These studies show that individuals who use the internet for various reasons during their leisure time may have higher levels of meaning-based coping. This, in turn, can enhance their levels of hedonic and eudaimonic well-being. Therefore, I hypothesized that meaning-based coping may mediate the relationship between leisure time internet use and hedonic and eudaimonic well-being.

Present study

According to the latest data from the Turkish Statistical Institute (TUIK), the most common age range for the internet use in Türkiye was 16–34 years old, which includes young adulthood. The internet usage rate for the general population was 90.9%. The internet usage among female increased from 85.4% in 2024 to 88.2% in 2025, while among men it rose from 92.2% in 2024 to 93.6% in 2025. The percentage of individuals engaging in online learning activities increased from 6.6% in 2019 to 17.7% in 2025. The rate of purchasing or ordering goods or services online risen to 59.1% for men and 52.3% for women. The most used social media and messaging applications were WhatsApp with 88.6%, YouTube with 72.9%, and Instagram with 68.1% [29]. Considering the increase in these rates, young adults, the group with the highest internet usage rates, were selected as the sample group for this study to determine the impact of leisure time internet use on well-being.

This study offers a theoretical expansion to the literature on meaning-based coping. The current literature commonly addresses meaning-based coping in the context of stress, trauma, and adverse life events [30, 31]. However, this study extends this approach to a broader context by building upon Park’s integrative model of meaning-making. According to Park’s model, meaning-based coping is the process by which individuals actively reconcile the situational meanings they encounter with their own global meaning systems, thereby reinterpreting them [21]. Within this theoretical framework, meaning-based coping is not limited to negative life experiences but can also be functional in the context of leisure time internet use, which has become a significant and widespread part of daily life. Leisure activities should not be considered merely behaviors aimed at rest or entertainment, but rather as part of the processes by which individuals create meaning and organize their psychological resources in their daily lives. Particularly with the widespread use of digital leisure time, individuals are able to achieve emotional regulation and create experiences aligned with their personal values through internet-based activities. According to Park’s integrative meaning-making model, leisure time internet use can function as a situational area that supports or reconstructs individuals’ meaning systems and can facilitate the emergence of meaning-based coping processes within the context of daily life. Furthermore, in this era, where digital environments increasingly shape individuals’ leisure time, internet-based leisure activities constitute an important psychological context related to both hedonic and eudaimonic well-being. Thus, this study integrates meaning-making processes into digital leisure use, addressing their relationship with well-being from a more holistic perspective and offering a contextual expansion to the literature.

In the literature, the relationships between meaning-based coping and well-being are mostly discussed in the context of stress and adverse life events [32]. However, the insufficient attention paid to meaning-making processes in research examining internet use and leisure behaviors leads to a lack of full understanding of how these behaviors relate to individuals’ well-being. This suggests that digital leisure time activities should be examined not only at a behavioral level but also within the framework of psychological processes that interact with individuals’ meaning systems. This study addresses meaning-based coping in the context of digital leisure time internet use and offers a contextual contribution to the literature by examining its relationship with both hedonic and eudaimonic well-being within the framework of a mediation model. In this regard, the aim of this study was to examine the mediating effect of meaning-based coping in the relationship between leisure time internet use and hedonic and eudaimonic well-being, while controlling age, gender, marital status and employment status. Therefore, the following hypotheses were formulated:

H1: Leisure time internet use will have a positive relationship with meaning-based coping, hedonic and eudaimonic well-being.

H1: Leisure time internet use will have a positive relationship with meaning-based coping, hedonic and eudaimonic well-being.

H2: Meaning-based coping will be positively associated with hedonic and eudaimonic well-being.

H2: Meaning-based coping will be positively associated with hedonic and eudaimonic well-being.

H3: Meaning-based coping will mediate the link between leisure time internet use and hedonic and eudaimonic well-being.

H3: Meaning-based coping will mediate the link between leisure time internet use and hedonic and eudaimonic well-being.

Method

Participants

568 young adults participated in this study. 65% young adults were women and 35% were men (n = 199). The participants’ ages ranged from 18 to 29 years, with an average age of 21.17 years (SD = 2.99). 401 young adults were single and 167 were married. 40.8% of participants were employed, and 59.2% were unemployed (n = 336). Participants spent an average of 3.76 h (SD = 1.91) online during their free time. 47.4% of participants stated that they use the internet in their free time for information, 47.2% for shopping, 47% for social interaction, 44.5% for entertainment, 40.8% for content creation, 40.8% for planning and 39.6% for escape for relief.

Measures

Leisure internet usage scale

The scale was developed by Yıldırım Şimşek and Çevik [7] to evaluate participation in internet activities. The scale consists of 16 items and four dimensions (social interaction, shopping, multimedia usage, and information and self-improvement). The scale is rated on a 5-point Likert type (1 = strongly disagree, 5 = strongly agree). The sample items are “I use the internet to play games in my free time.” and “I use the internet to learn new things in my free time.”. In this study, the Cronbach’s alpha was 0.73 for social interaction, 0.77 for shopping, 0.74 for multimedia usage and 0.81 for information and self-improvement.

Meaning-centered coping scale

The scale was developed by Eisenbeck, Carreno [33] and adopted to Turkish culture by Arslan and Yıldırım [26]. The scale was employed to assess the extent to which online leisure activities facilitated participants' meaning-based coping processes. The scale consists of 9 items and is single-dimensional. The scale is rated on a 7-point Likert type (1 = I do not agree at all, 7 = I completely agree). The sample item is “I have found a personal meaning in the current situation.”. In this study, the Cronbach’s alpha was 0.89.

Holistic well-being measure

The scale was developed by Arslan and Coşkun [34] to assess both hedonic and eudaimonic facets of wellbeing. The scale consists of 12 items and two dimensions (hedonic and eudaimonic well-being). The scale is rated on a 5-point Likert type (1 = almost never, 5 = almost always). The sample item is “Life is beautiful, and I am happy to be a part of that beauty.”. In this study, the Cronbach’s alpha was 0.82 for hedonic well-being and 0.78 for eudaimonic well-being.

Procedure

The study commenced after ethical approval was obtained from the Ethics Committee of Ağrı İbrahim Çeçen University (Ethic Code: E-95531838-050.99-164631). The research was conducted in accordance with the local institutional research procedures. Data was collected from young adults through an online survey created using Google Forms. Participants were included with a convenient sampling method. The introductory section of the survey provided detailed information about the study’s objectives, methods, and participants’ rights before and after participation. Informed consent was obtained from all individuals participating in the study via an online electronic consent form prior to the commencement of the data collection process.

Data analysis

To meet the normality assumption, skewness and kurtosis values were referenced as being less than |1 [35]. Pearson correlation analysis was run to determine the relationships between the variables. The mediation model was tested with PROCESS Macro v4.2, model 4. The four dimensions of leisure time internet use were analyzed in separate parallel mediation models because the structure of the PROCESS Macro does not allow for the simultaneous input of multiple independent variables within a single model. The analysis results were interpreted using standardized and unstandardized regression coefficients and squared multiple correlation values. The indirect effects of the mediating variable were examined using the bootstrap technique with 5,000 bootstrap samples at a 95% confidence interval. SPSS version 27 was used for all analyses.

Common method bias

This study included self-report measures that could be susceptible to response bias and social desirability effects, potentially leading to common method bias. Therefore, Harman’s single-factor test, which involves clustering all variables in the study under a single factor, was performed. The results showed that the first factor explained 30.87% of the total variance; this is below the recommended 50% threshold [36]. This result indicates that common method bias is unlikely to be a serious problem.

Discussion

Hedonic and eudaimonic well-being is a state, associated with optimal experience and functioning, influencing an individual’s future in many ways. In this regard, identifying the factors that influence these well-beings is a necessity. The purpose of this study was to examine the mediating effect of meaning-based coping on the relationship between leisure time internet use and hedonic and eudaimonic well-being, while controlling age, gender, marital status and employment status.

The results of this study demonstrated the positive associations between leisure time internet use and meaning-based coping, hedonic and eudaimonic well-being. Participants who use the internet for purposes such as social interaction, shopping, multimedia use, and information gathering in their leisure time reported higher levels of meaning-based coping, hedonic and eudaimonic well-being. Previous studies also showed that leisure time contributed to well-being because it is a unique experience that is freely chosen, autonomous, and driven by intrinsic motivation [23]. Leisure satisfaction was a positive indicator of both hedonic and eudaimonic well-being [15]. Hedonistic motives were associated with positive outcomes such as fewer depressive symptoms and more frequent positive interpersonal events. However, they were also associated with negative outcomes such as less empathy and self-control. Eudaimonic motives were associated only with positive outcomes such as greater well-being, self-control, empathy, self-esteem, and friendship closeness. The interactions between these motives also showed that hedonistic motives may be more problematic when young people’s eudaimonic pursuits are less frequent [37]. On the other hand, it has been determined that participation in gaming activities and media consumption was associated with negative emotions such as frustration, anger, and anxiety due to sedentary leisure behaviors, exposure to competitive environments and interactions, and exposure to idealized or unattainable images in the media (e.g., body standards, achievements, or lifestyles) [15]. Therefore, these leisure activities should be carried out in a controlled manner. In addition, leisure activities offered opportunities to fulfill fundamental needs such as purpose, values, efficacy, and self-worth, all of which are related to meaning [38]. However, the use of media focused on escaping reality negatively impacted meaning-making [39]. It should be remembered that problematic internet use can affect meaning-based regulation, triggering frequent use of various electronic devices and a desire to acquire expensive technological innovations [40]. Leisure time internet use may provide superficial hedonic satisfaction for some individuals, while for others it may facilitate eudaimonic processes such as information gathering, social connection, and goal development. For example, social relationships sometimes allow for the development of positive emotions and enhance hedonic well-being, and sometimes they allow individuals to acquire a sense of belonging and develop meaningful relationships, thus supporting eudaimonic well-being. In both cases, meaning-based coping strategies such as meaning-making and reframing may determine the direction of well-being.

The findings of this study showed that meaning-based coping was associated with hedonic and eudaimonic well-being. As meaning-based coping increased, hedonic and eudaimonic well-being also increased. Similar to our results, leisure activities influenced meaning-making through positive emotions, self-esteem, positive identities, harmony, spirituality, social and cultural connections, resilience, human strengths, lifelong learning, and human development, thereby enhancing hedonic and eudaimonic well-being [41]. In addition, participants who had a subjective judgment and experience that “life is meaningful” attributed meaning to life events, recognized the significance of their lives, and established a lasting purpose that guided their lives. This predicted a high level of hedonic [42] and eudaimonic well-being [43]. The presence of meaning in life also positively impacted hedonic well-being [44]. These results show that meaning-based coping is a factor that positively influences hedonic and eudaimonic well-being.

This study also found that meaning-based coping mediated the link between leisure time internet use and hedonic and eudaimonic well-being. Individuals who spent their leisure time using the internet had higher levels of meaning-based coping, and consequently, higher levels of hedonic and eudaimonic well-being. While there are no studies in literature that are directly consistent with our results, regarding meaning-based coping as a mediator, similar studies exist. For example, positive experiences during childhood were linked to high psychological resilience, which was associated with high meaning-based coping skills. These skills were linked to greater spiritual well-being [19]. Death distress was associated with greater meaning-based coping and hope, which was linked to mental well-being [45]. Psychological distress was linked with spiritual well-being through coping with meaning [46]. Pathological internet use had a negative relationship with meaning in life, which was linked to low self-efficacy [47]. Additionally, a recent cross-cultural study, including Türkiye, determined that Turks face digital inequality. According to the research results, the average score for online leisure use in Türkiye, regarding shopping was lower than in other countries, while the average score was highest in the information and personal development dimensions. Researchers explained this result by the relatively low-income level and the digital transformation process in the country. These results show that hedonic consumption can be classified as luxury consumption even in the virtual environment, and that low-income levels can be a limiting factor in the use of paid entertainment services, such as shopping or multimedia resources. Furthermore, the fact that the effects of the digital transformation process are only now beginning to emerge in Türkiye indicates that it has led to an average increase in online use for information acquisition/personal development purposes during leisure time, compared to other countries [48].

The findings of this study show that meaning-based coping is associated with similar levels of both hedonic and eudaimonic well-being. Although these two types of well-being are conceptually different, the similar mediating effects obtained suggest that meaning-based coping functions as a general psychological mechanism that depends more on how the individual interprets the experience than on the content of the experience itself. A particularly noteworthy finding is that information and self-improvement-focused internet use, which is more commonly associated with eudaimonic well-being in the literature, did not show a significantly stronger effect in this study, compared to other types of the internet use. One possible reason is that various leisure activities conducted in the digital environment, while differing in content, can have a similar potential to generate meaning for individuals. For example, shopping or multimedia usage may appear more hedonic on the surface; however, when individuals interpret these experiences within the framework of their personal values, goals, or meaning in life, these activities can also support eudaimonic processes. This finding aligns with approaches, emphasizing that well-being depends not only on the type of activity but also on how an individual makes sense of that activity [22]. Türkiye’s cultural structure may also influence the interpretation of these findings. In the Turkish sample, where collectivist characteristics are dominant, the high level of well-being resulting from activities such as social interaction and shopping may stem from the use of these activities as a means of strengthening social belonging and family ties [49]. However, it should be noted that this positive relationship is a boundary condition. In cases of “excessive and aimless internet use,” where internet use is not integrated with a meaning-based coping strategy, there is a risk that this therapeutic effect on well-being will be replaced by digital fatigue [50].

This study has several theoretical and practical contributions. This study contributes to the literature by focusing on the positive impact of positive and pleasant events on beliefs, alongside many studies that have focused on the positive impact of meaning-based coping with negative events. Park’s integrative model views meaning-based coping as a meaning-making process in which individuals actively reconcile situational meanings with global meaning systems [21]. The current study results revealed that leisure activities experienced online, such as social interaction, shopping, media use, information gathering and self-improvement, were positively correlated with individuals’ meaning-making processes, and this process was positively related to hedonic and eudaimonic well-being. These pleasure-oriented experiences are associated with the pursuit of pleasure, which is a component of hedonism [51] and individuals can create meaning through re-attribution, changing global beliefs, and generating new valuable goals, thereby enhancing eudaimonic well-being [21]. However, the existing literature also highlights the negative consequences of excessive or problematic internet use during leisure time [39, 40]. Therefore, young adults should be informed and raised awareness of the positive and negative consequences of these leisure activities and how to use them rationally. In addition, meaning-based coping processes may be relevant for understanding hedonic and eudaimonic well-being in online leisure contexts and deserve further investigation in intervention-oriented research.

Certain limitations should be considered in interpreting the results. This study was conducted using a cross-sectional design. Cross-sectional studies effectively identify relationships between variables, but they do not reveal causality. Future research may prefer a longitudinal design to generate more comprehensive information. Although all variables were measured using self-report instruments at a single time point, Harman’s single-factor test showed that common method variance was unlikely to pose a significant problem, as no single factor explained the majority of the variance. However, the use of self-report measures and cross-sectional design can still introduce bias and inflate observed relationships. Therefore, future studies are encouraged to utilize multi-method and longitudinal designs to reduce potential common method bias and better reveal structural overlap. In addition, the sample in the current study was predominantly female, single, and unemployed young adults. Although these variables were statistically controlled in the analysis, this does not completely eliminate the limitations arising from the demographic imbalance of the sample. This limits the generalizability of the findings. Furthermore, given that these demographic characteristics may be related to internet use and well-being, the current sample structure may have affected the direction and strength of the findings. Therefore, the generalizations of the findings to the entire young adult population should be avoided, and the results should be evaluated within the context of the sample studied. To address this limitation, future studies should be more demographically balanced.

In conclusion, hedonic and eudaimonic well-being are important for the development of young adults in both their present and future lives. Pleasant experiences, such as internet use during leisure time, can support well-being. Identifying the relationship between these positive experiences and meaning-based coping contributes to the literature that emphasizes the relationship between positive experiences and meaning. This study indicated the positive associations between leisure time internet use, meaning-based coping, and hedonic and eudaimonic well-being. The results of this study also revealed the mediating role of meaning-based coping in the association between leisure time internet use and hedonic and eudaimonic well-being. Young adult participants in this study who use the internet in their leisure time reported higher levels of meaning-based coping, which was associated with greater hedonic and eudaimonic well-being. These results suggest a potential relationship between meaning-based coping strategies and hedonic and eudaimonic well-being in the context of online leisure activities involving enjoyment and self-improvement.

APOL1 risk genotypes influence DNA methylation across multiple genomic elements in APOL1-APOL4- MYH9 region in African Americans
Authors: Yang Li, Anne K. Bozack, Pascal Schlosser, Eugene P. Rhee, Aditya Surapaneni, Alex Waterhölter, Zulema Rodriguez-Hernandez, Kelly V. Ruggles, Josef Coresh, Morgan E. Grams
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundEpigenetic modification of the APOL1 gene carrying risk alleles (G1 and G2) may represent a therapeutic strategy for APOL1-related kidney diseases. However, DNA methylation changes associated with APOL1 have not been thoroughly characterized.MethodsWe conducted an epigenome-wide association study (EWAS) of APOL1 risk alleles in 399 African Americans with chronic kidney disease (CKD) from the Atherosclerosis Risk in Communities (ARIC) study cohort using blood DNA samples. These associations were further examined in 1,677 African Americans with preserved kidney function. Additionally, the relationships between the identified CpGs and the expression of APOL1 and nearby genes were evaluated in normal kidney tissue. APOL1 risk–associated CpGs were tested for associations with estimated glomerular filtration rate based on creatinine and cystatin C (eGFRcrcys) and with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (ACR). Methylation patterns were additionally assessed for differentially methylated regions and associations with kidney function. We further evaluated whether APOL1 risk-associated CpGs can modulate the relationship between APOL1 risk alleles and kidney function measures.ResultsFour CpGs (cg15716373, cg16528833, cg21032535, and cg14589586) were significantly associated with APOL1 risk alleles, mapping to two enhancers and two promoters within the APOL1-APOL4-MYH9 region. Among APOL1 high-risk carriers, cg14589586 in the APOL2 promoter was inversely correlated with cg15716373 in an APOL1 intron, a pattern absent in White Americans. The association between the four CpGs and APOL1 risk alleles was also present among participants with preserved kidney function. In normal kidney tissue, cg14589586 and cg16528833 were significantly associated with the expression levels of multiple genes in this region, including APOL1 and MYH9. Multiple CpGs located within the APOL1-APOL4-MYH9 region exhibited significant joint associations with eGFRcrcys and ACR. APOL1 risk-associated CpGs showed significant interactions with APOL1 risk alleles in relation to kidney function.ConclusionAPOL1 risk alleles are associated with complex DNA methylation alterations across the APOL1-APOL4-MYH9 region, potentially regulating multiple genes within this locus. APOL1 risk–associated CpGs may represent therapeutic targets for APOL1-related kidney diseases.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13148-026-02147-4.

Introduction

Two Apolipoprotein L1 (APOL1) risk alleles, G1 (S342G, I384M) and G2 (N388-Y389 deletion), increase risk of a spectrum kidney diseases and faster progression of chronic kidney disease (CKD) [1–5]. The variants are common among individuals of recent African ancestry, where they may have been positively selected for conferring protection against Trypanosoma-induced African sleeping sickness [6]. In the United States, approximately 13% of African Americans, or around six million people, carry two high-risk alleles (G1/G1, G2/G2, or G1/G2) [7], and up to 32% carry at least one risk allele [8]. The mechanism of kidney risk is thought to be due to a toxic gain-of-function effect, whereby mutant APOL1 results in increased channel activity, disrupting podocyte integrity and endosomal trafficking [9, 10]. Because APOL1 is not essential for normal kidney development or function, therapeutic suppression of APOL1 is not expected to cause major adverse kidney effects [11–13]. Thus, APOL1 itself has emerged as a potential therapeutic target for the treatment of APOL1-related kidney diseases [14–16].

Several novel therapies have been developed to target APOL1 gene expression or APOL1 protein function in APOL1-related kidney diseases [2, 17, 18]. Inaxaplin, a small molecule that directly binds APOL1 and selectively inhibits its channel activity, is being evaluated in a Phase 3 clinical trial for the treatment of proteinuric kidney disease in individuals carrying APOL1 risk alleles [16]. Baricitinib, an oral JAK1/2 inhibitor that suppresses APOL1 synthesis, demonstrated antiproteinuric efficacy in a recent phase 2 trial [19]. In addition, two independently designed antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs) targeting APOL1 transcription reduced proteinuria and improved kidney function in mouse models [14, 15].

Given that DNA methylation can modulate genetic and environmental influences on gene expression, epigenetic silencing or modification of APOL1 expression may represent a novel therapeutic strategy for APOL1-related kidney diseases by reducing production of mutant APOL1 protein. To date, only two studies have examined DNA methylation associated with APOL1 risk alleles [20, 21]. One study focused only on CpGs near the APOL1 gene and identified five CpG sites significantly associated with APOL1 risk alleles among 611 African Americans [20]. The other used epigenome-wide association study (EWAS) to identify 52 CpG sites associated with APOL1 risk alleles, 18 of which were located within the APOL1-APOL4-MYH9 region around APOL1 gene [21]. However, both studies focused on younger patients, and neither linked the findings to kidney function [20, 21]. To address this gap, we focused on older African Americans with CKD, who may exhibit distinct DNA methylation changes with APOL1 risk alleles due to existing kidney function decline, and assessed whether these DNA methylation changes modify the effect of APOL1 on kidney function.

Methods

Study population

The Atherosclerosis Risk in Communities (ARIC) study is a community-based prospective cohort that enrolled 15,792 participants from four field centers (Washington County, MD; Forsyth County, NC; Minneapolis, MN; and Jackson, MS) between 1987 and 1989 [22]. Follow-up visits were conducted periodically through Visit 11 (2024–2025), and the current ongoing visit began in late 2025. The participants in the current analysis derive from the 6,538 participants who attended visit 5 (2011—2013). At this visit, DNA methylation in peripheral white blood cells was measured in 2,150 participants with chronic kidney disease (estimated glomerular filtration rate based on creatinine and cystatin C [eGFRcrcys] < 60 ml/min/1.73 m2 or urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio [ACR] > 30 mg/g) using the Illumina Infinium Methylation EPIC v1.0 BeadChip. After excluding individuals who failed methylation quality control or lacked APOL1 genotyping, 399 African Americans remained for main analysis (Supplementary Table 1). Additionally, 1,702 White Americans (a population that carries the wild-type APOL1 genotype) were included as a comparison group for some analyses. In secondary analysis, a larger sample of African American participants (N = 1,677) with preserved kidney function (eGFRcrcys > 60 ml/min/1.73 m2) was selected from Visit 3 to extend findings identified from participants with chronic kidney disease at Visit 5. All study protocols were approved by the institutional review boards of the participating centers.

To validate DNA methylation findings using gene expression in normal kidney tissue, we used the Clinical Proteomic Tumor Analysis Consortium (CPTAC) cohort. DNA methylation, gene expression, and demographic data for 140 samples with normal kidney tissue were downloaded from the GDC Data Portal (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/). Sex information was available for all 140 samples (42 Female, 98 Male). Age at diagnosis was available for 124 samples; 16 missing values were imputed with the cohort median (61.6 years).

Genotyping of APOL1 risk alleles

TaqMan assays were used to directly genotype the APOL1 risk alleles (G1 and G2) in participants who provided consent for genetic studies, as described in previous ARIC studies [23, 24]. The G1 allele comprises two missense mutations (rs73885319 [S342G] and rs60910145 [I384M]), which are almost completely in positive linkage disequilibrium. The G2 allele is defined by a 6—bp pair deletion at rs71785313 (N388-Y389 deletion) [1, 25]. The two Single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) in G1 allele are in complete negative linkage disequilibrium with the G2 SNP [23]. Because G1 and G2 act in a recessive genetic model, APOL1 risk status in African Americans was defined by the number of risk alleles: 0 (low risk, G0/G0), 1 (intermediate risk, G0/G1or G0/G2), and 2 (high risk, including G1/G1, G2/G2, or G1/G2), where G0 represents the wild-type allele without G1 or G2 SNP. Given the very low frequency of APOL1 risk alleles among individuals of European ancestry, White Americans were considered to carry no APOL1 risk alleles[26], and were used as a reference group in some analyses.

DNA methylation profiling

Genomic DNA for methylation profiling was extracted from peripheral white blood cells (PWBCs) using the Gentra Puregene DNA Blood Kit (Qiagen, Valencia, CA, USA). DNA was treated with soidium bisulfite using the EZ-96 DNA Methylation Kit (Zymo Research, Irvine, CA, USA) and then transferred to a bar-coded deep-well plate for performing Illumina Methylation EPIC v1.0 BeadChip (Illumina, Inc.; San Diego, CA.). DNA methylation was measured levels at approximately 850,000 CpG sites in this microarray. Data preprocessing followed an adapted pipeline (https://github.com/genepi-freiburg/Infinium-preprocessing), which extends the Control Probe Adjustment and reduction of global CORrelation (CPACOR) pipeline originally developed for the 450 K array to the EPIC platform [27]. Raw IDAT files were imported, and autosomal and sex chromosome probes were processed separately. During quality control, high-level filtering excluded probes with detection p-values > 10⁻1⁶ and samples with a call rate below 95%. Low-level quality control removed samples with poor performance on control probes (as per the Illumina HD Methylation Assay Protocol Guide, 15,019,519) or with sex mismatches identified via sex chromosome probes. Principal component analysis (PCA) was then performed on autosomal probes to detect and remove outlier samples.

Quantile normalization was applied separately to autosomal and sex chromosome probes according to probe type. Final methylation levels were expressed as beta values (β-values), ranging from 0 (unmethylated) to 1 (fully methylated), representing the ratio of fluorescent intensities. Before downstream analyses, the interquartile range (IQR; Q3-Q1) was calculated for each autosomal probe, and beta values deviating from the probe-specific median by more than three times the IQR were considered outliers and set to missing (NA). After these procedures, 846,232 probes remained for downstream analysis.

White blood cell (WBC) composition was estimated using Houseman’s method, including CD8 + T cells (CD8T), CD4 + T cells (CD4T), natural killer cells (NK), B cells (Bcell), monocytes (Mono), and granulocytes (Gran). These estimated cell proportions were included as covariates in downstream analyses to adjust for WBC heterogeneity [28]. PCA was performed on 203 DNA methylation control probes using the prcomp function, and the top 10 methylation principal components (PCs) were included in analyses to account for technical variation in DNA methylation data.

DNA methylation data in the CPTAC cohort were generated using the Illumina EPIC v1.0 BeadChip and preprocessed with the SeSAMe pipeline (https://github.com/zwdzwd/sesame). Gene expression data were processed using STAR.

Kidney function evaluation

Serum creatinine was measured using the Roche enzymatic method (Roche Diagnostics, Indianapolis, IN) and serum cystatin C was measured using the Gentian Cystatin C reagent (Gentian AS, Moss, Norway) on the Roche Modular P Chemistry Analyzer. eGFRcrcys was calculated from both serum creatinine and cystatin C using the Chronic Kidney Disease Epidemiology Collaboration (CKD-EPI) 2021 equation [29]. Urine albumin was measured by an immunoturbidometric method on the ProSpec nephelometric analyzer, and urine creatinine was measured using the Roche enzymatic method. The albumin-to-creatinine ratio (ACR) was derived from these measurements, and log-transformed ACR (logACR) was used in downstream analyses.

Statistical analysis and functional annotation

The characteristics of African Americans and White Americans were summarized using means and standard deviations (SDs) for normally distributed continuous variables, medians and (Q1-Q3) for skewed variables, and counts and percentages for categorical variables.

An Epigenome-wide association study (EWAS) was conducted to identify CpG sites associated with APOL1 risk status among African Americans using multiple linear regression, with β-values as the outcome and APOL1 risk status as the predictor. The genetic effect of APOL1 was evaluated under an additive model, in which individuals carrying two risk alleles (G1/G1, G2/G2, or G1/G2) were classified as high-risk, those with one risk allele as intermediate-risk, and those with no risk alleles as low-risk. Models were adjusted for age, sex, study center, estimated blood cell composition, and the top 10 principal components (PCs) from control probes. Bonferroni correction was applied to control the family-wise error rate, and associations with p-values < 5.9 × 10⁻⁸ (0.05/846,232) were considered epigenome-wide significant. Correlations between β-values of APOL1-associated CpGs identified in this study and 20 CpGs previously reported in the literature [20, 21] were assessed using Pearson’s correlation coefficients.

The dose–response relationship between β-values and APOL1 risk status was examined among African Americans, with White Americans as an additional reference group. ANOVA and t-tests were used to compare differences among all groups and between pairs of groups. Linear regression models were used to evaluate associations between two EWAS-identified CpGs, stratified by APOL1 risk group. Interactions between CpGs and APOL1 risk status in relation to kidney function were further tested using linear regression, adjusting for age, sex, and study center.

Functional annotation of significant APOL1-associated CpGs was performed by mapping them to promoters and enhancers in the GeneHancer database [30]. A sliding-window analysis was conducted across a ~ 6 Mb region surrounding the APOL1 gene (chr22:33,159,495—39,359,495), generating 31 windows of 200 kb each. Within each window, the effects of CpGs on eGFRcrcys or logACR in EWAS were aggregated using Brown’s combining p-value method, an empirical adaptation of Fisher’s method that accounts for dependence among neighboring CpGs, implemented in the EmpiricalBrownsMethod R package [31].

The modulation of APOL1-associated CpGs on the genetic effect of APOL1 on eGFRcrcys and logACR was evaluated using an interaction term (CpG × APOL1 risk status) in multiple linear regression models adjusted for age, sex, and study center. Each APOL1-associated CpG was dichotomized based on its median methylation value.

In CPTAC, the association between beta values of identified CpGs and log-transformed variance stabilized expression of nearby genes in kidney tissue was evaluated using a linear regression model adjusted for age and sex.

All statistical analyses were performed using R version 4.3.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Bonferroni correction was applied for analyses involving multiple molecular features.

Results

Baseline characteristics

Characteristics of 399 African Americans and 1,702 White Americans are summarized in Supplementary Table 1. Among African Americans, 14.8% carried a high-risk APOL1 genotype (two risk alleles: G1/G1, G1/G2, or G2/G2), and 43.1% carried one risk allele (G0/G1 or G0/G2). The mean ages in the low-, intermediate-, and high-risk groups were 76.8, 77.0, and 75.6 years, respectively. The proportions of female participants in these groups were 70.2%, 69.8%, and 72.9%. eGFRcrcys and logACR differed significantly across APOL1 risk groups, with the high-risk group exhibiting the lower eGFRcrcys and higher ACR values (P < 0.001, P < 0.05).

Associations between APOL1 risk alleles-associated CpGs and genes in the APOL1-APOL4-MYH9 region in normal kidney tissue

Two of the four CpG sites (cg14589586 and cg16528833) passed quality control in kidney tissue from CPTAC. cg14589586 (located in the APOL2 promoter region) was significantly associated with gene expression levels of APOL1, APOL2, APOL3, and MYH9 after adjustment for age and sex (Supplementary Table 3), with the strongest associations observed for APOL1 (P = 1.2 × 10-10) and APOL2 (P = 1.3 × 10⁻⁷). cg16528833 was significantly associated with expression of APOL3 (P = 0.045) and MYH9 (P = 0.027) with adjustment for age and sex.

Epigenetic Modulation of APOL1 Risk Alleles

As African Americans exhibited lower eGFRcrcys and higher logACR with increasing APOL1 risk and compared to White Americans (P < 0.001, Supplementary Table 6), we hypothesized that the four APOL1-associated CpGs might modify these associations. For the association with eGFRcrcys, cg16528833, cg14589586, and cg15716373 showed significantly stronger interactions with the APOL1 high-risk group relative to White Americans (P < 0.05, Supplementary Table 6). Similarly, cg21032535, cg16528833, and cg14589586 demonstrated significant interactions with logACR, with stronger relationships in the APOL1 high-risk group (P < 0.05, Supplementary Table 6).

Discussion

Since 2010, when it was first reported that APOL1 G1 and G2 alleles were associated with kidney disease in African Americans [7], numerous studies have investigated their causality, functional consequences, mechanisms in promoting kidney disease, and therapeutic potential across a spectrum of kidney disorders [4, 12, 16, 18, 25, 26, 32]. In recent years, DNA methylation microarrays, such as the Illumina EPIC array, have enabled genome-wide profiling of CpG methylation. Researchers can now use these technologies to examine APOL1 variant-associated methylation, providing insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying APOL1 risk from an epigenetic perspective. In 2024 and 2025, two studies, one APOL1 region-targeted DNA methylation association study and one EWAS, identified 5 and 18 CpGs around APOL1 associated with APOL1 risk variants, respectively [20, 21]. These studies provide direct evidence that DNA methylation varies with APOL1 genotype. However, because APOL1 risk variants are rare outside populations of African ancestry, and genome-wide methylation profiling is costly, further detailed epigenetic studies are limited. The emergence of novel APOL1-targeting therapies highlights the need for additional studies in this area.

In the current study, we performed an EWAS using the Illumina EPIC array (measuring 850,000 CpGs) in African Americans with different APOL1 risk genotypes and identified four APOL1 risk-associated CpGs at genome-wide significance. All four CpGs are located in the APOL1-APOL4-MYH9 region, a region which contains several related genes, including APOL1, APOL2, APOL3, APOL4, and MYH9. Indeed, initial genome-wide association studies (GWAS) in African Americans identified MYH9 variants as candidates for the striking racial disparities in kidney disease, but subsequent fine-mapping pinpointed the APOL1 variants as causal [1, 25, 33]. The exact mechanism of injury is uncertain, and likely complex: a recent model suggests that both APOL1 and APOL3 proteins contribute to APOL1 variant-induced kidney injury [34, 35].

Functionally related genes within the same genomic region are often co-regulated by shared regulatory elements [36], and DNA methylation frequently overlaps with these elements to modulate chromatin accessibility and transcriptional activity [37]. Therefore, studies integrating APOL1 genetic data with DNA methylation data are required to elucidate whether epigenetic mechanisms participate in the gene–gene interactions underlying APOL1-associated kidney diseases and whether single CpG or multiple CpGs across this complex genomic region act together to regulate gene expression [20, 21, 32, 38].

Among the four APOL1-associated CpGs identified in the APOL1-APOL4-MYH9 region, three (cg16528833, cg14589586, and cg15716373) had been reported previously, whereas cg21032535 represents a novel APOL1 risk–associated CpG. All four CpGs can be mapped to genomic regulatory elements: cg21032535 and cg14589586 are located in promoters upstream of APOL4 (GH22J036201 in GeneHancer database) and APOL2 (GH22J036236 in GeneHancer database), suggesting a potential direct link between APOL1 risk alleles and APOL4 and APOL2 expression. APOL4 is relatively underexplored, with unclear roles in kidney disease, though it has been implicated in lipid metabolism, immune regulation, schizophrenia, and glioma [39–42]. APOL2 is involved in cholesterol biosynthesis, liver fibrosis, and neurological diseases [43, 44]. As the closest homolog of APOL1 (61.4% identity, 74.6% similarity), sharing the N-terminal domain, APOL2 may have APOL1-like functions [44, 45]. Interestingly, in the current study, cg14589586 in the APOL2 promoter showed a strong inverse association with cg15716373 in an APOL1 intron in African Americans with high-risk APOL1 alleles, which was absent in White Americans. Moreover, methylation levels of cg14589586 and cg15716373 differed between African Americans with wild-type APOL1 and White Americans with wild-type APOL1.

cg16528833 and cg15716373 are located within enhancers GH22J036221 and GH22J036257. Unlike promoters, enhancers can regulate genes from distal locations [46]. According to enhancer–gene regulatory relationships in GeneHancer, GH22J036221 may regulate APOL3 and APOL2, while GH22J036257 may regulate APOL1, APOL4, and MYH9. APOL3 is involved in kidney cell function, and one variant in APOL3 can act as additive risk factors for CKD, particularly when interacting with APOL1 risk alleles [47]. In kidney cells, APOL3 protein binds to APOL1 and is co-regulated [48]. Our finding suggests that APOL1 may interact with APOL3 via epigenetic mechanisms in individuals with APOL1 risk alleles. While the role of MYH9 is not fully elucidated, some studies indicate MYH9 may independently contribute to certain kidney diseases [49, 50].

None of the identified single CpGs was significantly associated with kidney function. However, when we used a combined P-value approach to assess the aggregated effect of all CpGs in the region, we found that these CpGs were jointly associated with eGFRcrcys and logACR. CpGs in close proximity are often co-methylated, and overall regional methylation may influence transcription more than individual CpGs [51–53]. Thus, while single CpGs were not associated with kidney function, they may collectively modulate the effect of APOL1 risk alleles. This might be because these CpGs may better capture coordinated regulation of gene expression than individual CpGs in this region. However, direct functional validation is necessary to confirm this.

Our findings suggest that multiple genes in the APOL1-APOL4-MYH9 region might contribute to the pathophysiology of APOL1 risk alleles. Our results indicate that this coordinated regulation may occur partially via DNA methylation changes induced by APOL1 risk alleles. Therefore, future epigenetic interventions targeting APOL1 risk-related kidney disease might consider multiple CpG sites in the APOL1-APOL4-MYH9 region, with special attention to both APOL1 and APOL2, to mitigate the detrimental effects of APOL1 risk alleles on kidney function. Importantly, cg14589586 and cg16528833 were associated with multiple gene expression in the APOL1–APOL4–MYH9 region in normal kidney tissue, suggesting potential concordance between blood- and kidney-based signals. However, further validation is needed in African American populations with APOL1 risk alleles.

Our study has limitations. The sample size of African American participants was small. Due to the cross-sectional, observational nature of the study, we cannot be certain that APOL1 risk variants cause differential methylation at the four CpG sites or merely reflect a shared effect of GFR. That said, the fact that the differential methylation patterns were present in participants with preserved eGFR suggests that reverse causation (e.g., low eGFR causing differential methylation at these sites) is unlikely. Experimental studies incorporating additional functional genomic data are needed to clarify the regulatory or causal role of DNA methylation in this region, particularly its interactions with transcription factors and other epigenetic mechanisms in APOL1-associated kidney pathology.

Conclusion

In summary, our study found that APOL1 risk alleles were associated with differential DNA methylation at multiple CpGs within the APOL1-APOL4-MYH9 region in African Americans with CKD. Taken together, CpGs in this region were associated with kidney function and demonstrated a significant interaction with APOL1 risk alleles on renal outcomes. These findings provide new insights into the complex interplay between APOL1 genetic risk and the epigenetic regulation of genes within the APOL1–APOL4–MYH9 region and suggest that APOL1 risk–associated CpGs might represent potential therapeutic targets if validated in experimental studies.ss.

Sinublimies A–D, Four New Cembranes from the Soft Coral Sinularia sublimis
Authors: Cili Wang, Sen Wang, Lei Li, Jiarui Zhang, Kai Li, Hu Hou, Pinglin Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Four new cembranes, sinublimies A–D (1–4), were identified from Chinese soft coral Sinularia sublimis, along with three structurally related known metabolites (5–7). Notably, sinublimie A (1) is a new cembrane-based diterpene, possessing an undescribed 6/10/7 tricyclic ring system. Sinublimie C (3) is a cembrane with two rare five-membered oxygen-containing rings. The structural elucidation of these compounds relied upon extensive spectroscopic characterization, complemented by DP4+ probability analyses and ECD analysis. Bioactivity screening revealed that compounds 1 and 2 demonstrated moderate anti-inflammatory activity when tested in zebrafish models.

Introduction

Soft corals of the genus Sinularia, which occur widely across geographic regions, have received significant attention in chemical research. Since chemical studies on Sinularia commenced in 1975, extensive investigations have been carried out on multiple species within the genus, − uncovering an array of metabolites with intricate chemical structures. Earlier research has consistently underscored Sinularia as a significant producer of diverse chemical entities, primarily diterpenoids (57%), steroids and their glycosidic derivatives (20%), sesquiterpenoids (13%), other miscellaneous metabolites (9%), and alkaloidal substances (1%). , These bioactive secondary metabolites exhibit varying pharmacological activities, encompassing cytotoxicity, antifouling potential, antibacterial efficacy, antiviral capabilities, and anti-inflammatory properties.

Cembrane-type diterpenes are recognized as the prominent metabolites of the genus Sinularia. In the past decade, some cembrane-type diterpenes with unusual scaffolds, such as sinulariaone A, sinudenoids A–E, sinulerectadione, secoflexibilisolides A and B, and sinulochmodin A, have been isolated from this genus and have shown potential bioactivities, such as cytotoxicity, antithrombotic, and anti-inflammatory properties. Owing to their chemical diversity and significant bioactivities, the genus Sinularia has attracted increasing attention.

In the context of our continuous exploration aimed at identifying biologically active marine-derived cembranoid diterpenes, − we conducted the inaugural detailed chemical study on Sinularia sublimis sourced from the Xisha Islands, discovering four novel cembranoids, designated as sinublimies A–D (1–4), as well as three previously known metabolites, namely, dendronpholide Q (5), manaarenolide F (6), and (1R,13S,12S,9S,8R,5S,4R)-9-acetoxy-5,8,12,13-diepoxy-cembr-15­(17)-en-16,4-olide (7) (Figure ). The identification of these novel cembranoids (1–4) relied heavily on comparative analyses with structurally analogous compounds. Compound 1 features an uncommon 6/10/7 tricyclic ring system, and compound 3 is characterized by two five-membered oxygen-containing rings. The present paper thoroughly elucidates the isolation procedures, detailed structural assignments, and initial evaluations of biological activity for the seven isolated compounds.

Results and Discussion

Sinublimie A (1) was isolated as a transparent liquid. Its molecular formula, C20H32O3, was determined by HRESIMS, implying five degrees of unsaturation. On the basis of the 13C NMR and HSQC spectroscopic analysis, there were 20 carbons categorized into three methyl carbons, eight methylene carbons (one olefinic), five methine groups (one olefinic), and four quaternary carbons (two olefinic). Considering that two double bonds were explained, the residual degrees of unsaturation implied the presence of three interconnected ring systems.

The systematic examination on the 1H–1H COSY spectral information on 1 confirmed 4 distinct spin-coupling fragments (Figure ). Key correlations detected in the HMBC spectrum, such as those linking H3-18 with C-5, C-3, C-4, and linking H-3 with C-8, confirmed a six-membered cyclic moiety bearing a hydroxyl substituent at C-4 (δ C 82.1). Further diagnostic HMBC signals connecting H-3 with C-2 (δ C 123.0), H-2 with C-14, H3-20 with C-11 (δ C 75.5), C-13, and C-12 (δ C 80.3), as well as methylene protons H2-19 with C-8, C-10, and C-9 (δ C 156.2), corroborated the presence of a ten-membered ring containing terminal double-bond substituent and hydroxyl functionality positioned at C-9 and C-11. Moreover, the additional HMBC correlations from the methyl protons H3-17 to C-1 (δ C 141.9), C-16 and C-15, along with H2-16 and C-12, confirmed a bridging seven-membered ring incorporating oxygen, linking positions C-1 and C-12. Consequently, the planar configuration of sinublimie A (1), a newly identified cembrane-derived diterpene with an unconventional tricyclic ring arrangement (6/10/7), was decisively determined (Figure ).

Key 1H–1H COSY and 1H–13C HMBC correlation of compounds 1–4.

Compound 1 had its relative stereochemical assignments at C-11S*,12R*,15R* determined through the interpretation of NOESY interactions between proton pairs including H3-17/H-16a (δ H 3.18), H3-20/H-16b (δ H 4.03), H-15/H-16b, and H3-20/H-11 (Figure ). Furthermore, deduced from the NOESY cross peak observed between H3-18 and H-3, the aforementioned protons were oriented on the same side of the structure. Because the remote stereocenters at positions C-4, C-3, and C-8 did not produce discernible NOE correlations with the other chiral centers, the electronic circular dichroism (ECD) method along with DP4+ statistical analyses was applied to determine their configurations. MacroModel (Maestro V11.9, Schrödinger) software facilitated conformational searches of all theoretically possible stereochemical variants, and the conformer populations were calculated using Boltzmann-weighted distributions. Subsequently, DP4+ probability evaluations were performed on four stereochemical isomers, specifically denoted as 3S*,4R*,8R*,11S*,12R*,15R*-1a, 3S*, 4R*,8S*,11S*,12R*,15R*-1b, 3R*,4S*,8R*,11S*,12R*,15R*-1c, and 3R*,4S*,8S*,11S*,12R*,15R*-1d (Figure B), employing GIAO-based computational procedures at the PCM/mPW1PW91/6-31 + G­(d, p) level. Among these configurations, the calculated NMR chemical shift data matched most accurately with isomer 1d (Figure B). Experimentally obtained ECD spectra of compound 1 in methanol revealed a positive Cotton effect associated with a π–π* electronic transition around 210 nm. In Figure A, the absolute the absolute configurations of 1 was conclusively assigned as 3R,4S,8S,11S,12R,15R.

Compound 2 was obtained in the form of a transparent oil, with its molecular composition determined to be C23H38O9 by HRESIMS measurements. Evaluation of its 1D NMR spectral data, combined with HSQC spectral correlations, revealed a total of 23 carbons classified into five sp3-methyl groups, eight methylene carbons (one olefinic), three methine carbons, and seven quaternary carbons (one olefinic and three carbonyl groups). These findings supported the identification of compound 2 as a diterpene belonging to the cembranoid structural class.

The detailed planar arrangement of compound 2 was clarified by interpreting correlations from its HMBC and 1H–1H COSY experiments (Figure ). First, the 1H–1H COSY experiment defined three spin-coupling sequences. Significant HMBC interactions, specifically from methyl protons H3-18 to carbons C-5 (δ C 213.8), C-4, and C-3, from H3-19 to C-9 (δ C 78.7), C-8, and C-7, and from H3-20 to C-13 (δ C 75.4), C-12, and C-11, unequivocally attested to the existence of a fundamental 14-membered macrocyclic ring skeleton. Additionally, these spectral correlations permitted definitive presence of a secondary hydroxyl group at C-13, three tertiary hydroxyl functionalities at C-4, C-8, and C-12, and a carbonyl group at position C-5. Additionally, an acetoxy substituent at C-9 was corroborated by HMBC signals from proton H-9 to acetyl carbonyl carbon C-22 (δ C 170.5), as well as from the methyl protons H3-23 to C-22. Furthermore, HMBC interactions observed from methyl group H3-21 to C-16 (δ C 167.8), and from methylene protons H2-17 to C-16, and C-15, C-1, indicated a side-chain linkage attached at C-1.

The spatial orientation of key protons was inferred through NOESY correlations (Figure ). Specifically, the process that H3-18/H-2a (δ H 1.15) interacted with H-13/H-2a, demonstrated their spatial proximity on the same face, thereby assigning them as β-oriented protons. Additional NOESY contacts involving H3-19/H-7a (δ H 1.63), H3-20/H3-19, H-9/H-7b (δ H 2.15), H-13/H-11a (δ H 1.45), together with H-9/H-11a, demonstrated that H3-20 and H3-19 were assigned as α-oriented protons, whereas H-9 possesses the β-orientation. In terms of compound 2 in Figure , there were characteristic positive Cotton effects at 215 nm attributed to π–π* transitions and at 285 nm due to n–π* electronic transitions associated with the carbonyl functionalities. Ultimately, the absolute stereochemical assignments for sinublimie B (2) were conclusively determined by TDDFT-based ECD computational analysis as 1R, 4R, 8S, 9R, 12S, 13R (Figure ).

Compound 3 was a transparent oil with a determined molecular composition C21H34O6 by HRESIMS analysis. Detailed interpretation of the 13C NMR and HSQC spectral information (Figure ) showed structural resemblance to sinulaflexiolide B, an established cembrane diterpene initially isolated from the Chinese soft coral Sinularia flexibilis . Nevertheless, important structural variations were identified, specifically involving the cleavage of the ester connection at carbons C-4 and C-16, and the elimination of the ether linkage connecting C-5 and C-8, which are characteristic of sinulaflexiolide B. These structural alterations were supported by critical HMBC signals connecting H3-21 to C-16 (δ C 168.3) (Figure ), significantly downfield-shifted signals of carbons C-8 (δ C 87.4) and C-5 (δ C 87.6), coupled with consistency with the required degrees of unsaturation.

NOESY correlations between H-1/H-3a (δ H 2.42) and H3-18/H-3b (δ H 1.24) were observed (Figure ), indicating the α-configuration at H-1 and the β-configuration at H3-18. Further observed NOESY interactions, involving H-1/H3-20, H-5/H3-19, H3-19/H-9, H3-20/H-10a, and H-9/H-10a, collectively supported the α-configuration for protons H-5, H-9, H3-20, and H3-19. The relative stereochemistry at carbon C-13 was confirmed as R* by DP4+ probability calculations (Table S6 and Figure S2), reinforced by NOESY interactions of H-1/H-14a (δ H 2.09), together with H-13/H-14b (δ H 1.60). The measured ECD spectrum for compound 3 (Figure ) displayed a characteristic positive Cotton effect near 205 nm attributed to π–π* electronic transitions. Thus, using computational TDDFT-ECD methods, the absolute stereochemical assignments of sinublimie C (3) were conclusively defined as 1R, 4R, 5S, 8S, 9R, 12R, 13S (Figure ).

Sinublimie D (4), a transparent oil, exhibited a molecular formula of C21H32O6 by HRESIMS analysis. According to NMR spectroscopic examinations (1D and 2D, Table S4 and Figure ), compound 4 shared the same planar structural framework as sinupedunolide E, a common cembranolide diterpene isolated from Sinularia pedunculata, thus indicating stereochemical variations between the two compounds.

Notable differences were highlighted by the large coupling constant values of H-9 and H-10 (J 9,10 = 16.0 Hz), which confirmed that the Δ9 double bond was assigned an E-geometry. Key NOESY spectral cross-links, such as H3-18/H-3a (δ H 1.83), H-1/H-3b (δ H 1.40), H3-18/H-7a (δ H 1.78), and H3-19/H-7b (δ H 1.93), suggested α-configuration at H-1 and H3-19 and β-configuration for protons H3-18 (Figure ). Moreover, cross-links of H3-20/H-1, H-13/H-11a (δ H 2.03), and H3-20/H-11b (δ H 2.53) confirmed the α-configuration at H3-20 and the β-configuration for proton H-13, further validated by DP4+ statistical analyses (Table S7 and Figure S3). The experimental ECD spectrum of compound 4 (Figure ) revealed positive π–π* Cotton effects at approximately 200 and 235 nm and an additional positive n–π* Cotton effect at 285 nm. Consequently, the absolute stereochemistry of sinublimie D (4) was confirmed as 1R, 4R, 8R, 12S, 13S based on TDDFT-ECD calculations (Figure ).

Previous research has shown significant anti-inflammatory activity exhibited by various soft coral-derived cembrane-type diterpenes. , In this context, a transgenic fluorescent zebrafish inflammatory assay, utilizing CuSO4-induced as the irritant, was employed to investigate the anti-inflammatory capacities of the four newly identified compounds (1–4) (Figure ). CuSO4 exposure induces acute inflammation specifically in zebrafish mechanosensory cells and lateral line neuromast. As demonstrated by the results in Figure , administration of compounds 1 and 2 at a dose of 20 μM considerably suppressed the recruitment of macrophages toward inflamed lateral line areas and effectively reduced macrophage buildup surrounding the neuromasts. Thus, compounds 1 and 2 demonstrated moderate anti-inflammatory effects, with observed inhibition percentages of 33.47% and 34.30%, respectively.

Conclusions

In summary, Sinularia soft corals are famous for the production of varying biologically potent cembranoid compounds characterized by structural diversity. The present study, representing the first chemical exploration of the coral S. sublimis, identified four previously undescribed cembrane diterpenoids (1–4). Structurally, sinublimie A (1) was a rearranged cembrane-based diterpene possessing an unusual 6/10/7 tricyclic ring system. Biological evaluations confirmed that compounds 1 and 2 presented moderate in vivo anti-inflammatory activities using a zebrafish inflammation model. Collectively, these discoveries highlight the extensive structural variation and therapeutic promise of marine-derived cembranoid diterpenes as potential anti-inflammatory drug candidates.

Experimental Section

General Experimental Procedures

The UV spectral data were measured with the aid of a Beckman DU640 ultraviolet–visible spectrophotometer. High-resolution electrospray ionization mass spectrometry was collected on a Micromass Q-T of Ultima GLOBAL GAA076LC instrument. The optical rotation data were acquired using a Jasco P-1020 polarimeter. A Jasco J-810 spectropolarimeter was utilized to collect circular dichroism (CD) data. An Agilent spectrometer operating at a 500 MHz frequency was utilized to obtain nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectra. Semipreparative-scale separation and purification relied on an HPLC platform containing a Waters 1525 binary delivery pump and an UV detector.

Biological Materials

Soft coral samples identified as S. sublimis (family Alcyoniidae) were obtained via scuba diving at roughly 10 m depth from the Yalong Bay area of the Xisha Islands, South China Sea (geographical coordinates: 109°29′E, 18°13′N) during November 2023. The collected specimens were immediately frozen. The coral species’ identity was authenticated by morphological characterization and sclerite analysis conducted by Professor PingJyun Sung of the Institute of Marine Biotechnology at the National Museum of Marine Biology & Aquarium, Pingtung 944, Taiwan. One voucher sample (accession code: SY-2023-150) has been permanently conserved in the State Key Laboratory of Marine Drugs, Ocean University of China.

Extraction and Isolation

Frozen S. sublimis samples, weighing approximately 4.5 kg (wet weight), underwent homogenization followed by extraction with methanol, repeated thrice over three successive periods of 24 h each. Subsequently, all methanol extracts were pooled, subjected to evaporation under reduced pressure to remove solvents, and further purified by dissolving in CH3OH to acquire a desalted crude extract totaling 53 g. The resulting crude methanolic extract underwent vacuum column chromatography over silica gel. The elution was performed with a gradient program of petroleum ether and acetone mixtures (from 100:1 down to 1:1, v/v). Then, an additional gradient elution utilized dichloromethane and methanol mixtures (from 10:1 down to 1:1, v/v). This chromatographic procedure helped obtain 12 distinct subfractions (E1–E12).

Further purification of E.4 was carried out via vacuum silica gel chromatography. Elution was performed using petroleum ether–acetone mixtures with ratios ranging from 50:1 to 10:1 (v/v), affording 4 subfractions (E4a–E4d). Fraction E4c underwent subsequent purification via the semipreparative HPLC method. Separation was implemented with a 5 μm ODS column (250 × 10 mm) using methanol–water (60:40) as an eluent at a flow rate of 1.5 mL/min, yielding two isolated compounds: 4 (1.0 mg) and 6 (2.1 mg).

Further purification of E5 was carried out via vacuum silica gel chromatography. Elution was performed using petroleum ether–acetone mixtures with ratios ranging from 40:1 to 10:1 (v/v), which afforded 5 subfractions designated E5a–E5e. E5a was further isolated via the semipreparative HPLC method. Using methanol–water (55:45) as an eluent, compound 3 (1.6 mg), compound 5 (1.0 mg), and compound 7 (6.6 mg) were purified. Using methanol–water (55:45) as an eluent, compound 2 (5.7 mg) was finally collected from E5b.

Further purification of E7 was carried out via vacuum silica gel chromatography. Elution was performed using petroleum ether–acetone mixtures with ratios ranging from 20:1 to 10:1 (v/v), which afforded 3 subfractions designated E7a–E7c. Using methanol–water (40:60) as an eluent, ultimately, compound 1 (0.9 mg) was successfully separated and acquired from E7c.

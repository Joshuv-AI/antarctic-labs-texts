Integrated and Ayurveda standalone interventions compared with standalone modern care in Rheumatoid arthritis: Observations from a non-random, parallel group, open label, historical control study
Authors: Sanjeev Rastogi, Krishna Gopal Sharma, Gurmeet Ram
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundRheumatoid arthritis patients frequently visit Ayurveda clinics. Many such patients arrive at Ayurveda clinics after getting a trial of conventional modern medicine and being unsatisfied with the responses. In real world setting, many of these patients prefer to continue modern medicine along with Ayurveda interventions. This patient pool forms a very specific population of RA.ObjectiveThe study was aiming at observing the benefits of adding Ayurveda interventions to ongoing modern medicine or Ayurveda alone intervention in those who preferred to stop modern medicine.Material and methodIt was a non-random, single center, parallel group, open label, historical control observational study where the past modern intervention was taken up as the historical control for all those who continued with Ayurveda alone or integrated intervention. The outcome assessment was done on the basis of Swollen Joint count, Tender joint count, VAS score, Patient Global Assessment, ESR and DAS-28. Both intergroup and intragroup comparison of all parameters was done.Results31 patients who were included in the study (17 in Ayurveda alone group and 14 in integrated management group) were compared for all outcomes within the group and also for inter group. These observations were also compared with historical control. Except historical control, both active intervention groups were found to have statistically significant improvements in pre-post analysis. An intergroup comparison was not able to find any significant difference except for DAS-28 which was found significantly different in three groups.ConclusionAyurveda interventions in RA are found to be more effective comparing to modern interventions alone observed through historical control. Integrative management of RA comprising of Ayurveda and modern standard care is only marginally better than Ayurveda alone and is much better than modern alone intervention in RA.

Introduction

The prospective clinical benefits obtained by the conventional biomedical interventions or standalone alternative-complementary health care interventions like Ayurveda in the management of Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) have always been debated. Most clinical trials conducted in this regards have compared the active interventions of one system with placebo or active interventions from other health care system. The clinical scenario at rheumatology clinics is however different than what is observed in these trials. In Ayurveda clinics, the patients of RA constitute a group of the people who already have gone for conventional modern therapy for some time and have come to Ayurveda clinic either to switch the earlier treatment or to add Ayurveda to the ongoing interventions. Similar observations are seen in conventional rheumatology clinics too where many are found to have experienced various other health care systems besides allopathy [1]. Reasons of such switchovers are reported to be the non-response, under-response or adversity associated with the active intervention received by the patients [2,3]. The patients who arrive at Ayurveda clinics for RA, remain in the state of limbo as to continue with ongoing management of modern medicine or to stop it and try Ayurveda interventions in standalone mode. Many of such patients are found to have stopped the allopathic medications at their own before arriving to Ayurveda clinic whereas many other seek the opinion of intervening Ayurveda physician in order to continue both systems for the purpose of maximizing the clinical benefits. This complex clinical scenario of health seeking behavior of RA patients makes it difficult to make a judgment about the benefits obtained by a single system or a combination. In this scenario, the most pertinent question is to know about the benefits obtainable by the RA patients during their intervention with modern medicine initially and when it was stopped for Ayurveda interventions alone or is supplemented by the standard Ayurveda therapy. The other important question is to know about the feasibility and safety of such integrative interventions to find an acceptance of such model of care in light of ‘one nation one health care system’ paradigm or integrative health care paradigm as is promoted by Govt. of India [4,5]. Population surveys about the treatment choices in RA have shown this perplexity among patients regarding their preferred interventions and it is observed that majority of the patients have experienced at least three systems of health care in search of getting optimal relief from their illness [6]. This continuous exploration of various health care systems in order to get a relief seems to be a natural history of RA patients in India where pluralistic health care is available. At this juncture, this comes as an important question to see the comparative benefits of one health system over the other in isolation or in combination in order to maximize the benefits and to minimize the adversities while treating a patient of RA.

This research question requires an unconventional research design by considering the same patients as their own control while being treated with different health care system. Observing the disease activity historically at the start of modern medicines and finding the benefits of interventions till the point the patient decides to switch the system or wishes to add another system to have additional benefits serves here as historic control. When these patients are prospectively observed from the point of start of Ayurveda intervention or a combination of Ayurveda and modern intervention till the completion of the study, this forms the active intervention group (Fig. 1). Looking at the difference in disease activity therefore have come as a historical control of the same patients who are subsequently added with Ayurveda intervention along with modern medicine or have switched the modern medicine for Ayurveda medications alone. So a historical control taking modern medicines for RA has eventually been compared with outcomes obtained in the same patients on Ayurveda alone or on a combination of modern and Ayurveda medicine together in an integrative manner. This design, although has its own limitations like allocation bias, recall bias for historical observations, interrupted treatment, unavailability of complete clinical records, and non-standard therapy in some cases has an additional advantage of having the self-control so the bias related to non-randomization and compliance could have been minimized.

Study design having the ambispective components derived from historical control and active intervention.

The study has shown a good acceptance and tolerance to stand alone Ayurveda or its concomitant use with conventional biomedicine for the treatment of RA. This also has shown the better efficacy of Ayurveda standalone intervention comparing to the historical control. Adding Ayurveda to conventional medicine is also found to be beneficial over modern historical control and to the Ayurveda alone proposing the possibility of additional benefits of integrative management in RA. Historical control when subjected to a pre and post intervention analysis could not show any significant change in most parameters whereas significant changes have been observed in most parameters in both Ayurveda alone and integrative management groups. Better improvement in almost all parameters among those receiving Ayurveda alone or integrative care comparing to the historical control comes as an important observation proposing to look further into the futuristic integrative medicine and its implications in clinical rheumatology.

Earlier researches have shown that Ayurvedic Whole System (AWS) management in RA not only alleviates the symptoms but also induces a metabolic shift similar to healthy controls [7,8]. This is significant from the perspective of possible pathology reversal in case of RA being treated through Ayurveda as it endorses the ayurvedic concepts of samprapti vighatan where a pathogenesis – disease complex is dismantled and doshas are brought back to normality. Utilization of AWS as the standard care of Amavata and using the RA treatment as is recommended by a qualified Rheumatologist is another distinctive feature of this study where integrative management means standard treatment of RA through conventional medicine and simultaneous standard treatment of Amavata through AWS interventions.

The study design used here and the observed outcomes are expected to become the game changer in future clinical trials in RA and Amavata and would possibly play a key role in determining the future of clinical management of RA in India.

Materials and methods

Study setting

The study was done at Ayurveda –Arthritis Treatment and Advanced Research Center (A-ATARC), State Ayurvedic College and Hospital, Lucknow in collaboration with Department of Rheumatology and Clinical Immunology, King Georges’ Medical University, Lucknow.

Ethical and other mandatory approvals

Essential clearance for the study was obtained by the Departmental Review Committee, PG Department of Kaya Chikitsa, SAC, Lucknow (May 31, 2024) and Ethical clearance was obtained by Institutional Ethics Committee, SAC, Lucknow (SAC/IEC/2024/188 Dt May 7, 2024). Further to this, the study was registered prospectively at CTRI wide registration number CTRI/2024/05/067683. Study remained adherent to GCP guidelines issued by Ministry of Ayush, Govt of India and other regulatory agencies related to the clinical trials.

Design and plan of study

This study was designed as a pilot, open label, non-random, outpatient based, single center observational study (Real World Data Settings). Patients visiting the A-ATARC have been screened for having a diagnosis of RA. Those who were already on modern medicine for RA and were willing to continue it with Ayurveda interventions or willing to discontinue it (may have already discontinued) and to begin with Ayurveda interventions were further screened for their ayurvedic diagnosis. Upon confirmation of Amavata in those who already have a diagnosis of RA, these prospective patients were further screened as per the predetermined inclusion and exclusion criteria (Table 1). Those who have stopped the modern medicine and were continuing only ayurvedic medicines were taken as group A and those who continued modern medicine and have additional Ayurveda interventions were considered as group B. Group A and group B were retrospectively enquired for their initial disease presentation before the start of the modern medicine and also for the disease presentation when they decided to stop the modern medicine or decided to continue it with ayurvedic medicine. The baseline observations were compared with subsequent follow-ups in historical control and both the groups on active intervention to observe any changes obtained following Ayurveda alone or Ayurveda along with ongoing modern medication.

Study execution

Patients attending the A-ATARC OPD were screened as per the selected inclusion and exclusion criteria. Those who fulfilled the criteria were asked for informed consent. Upon obtaining the informed consent they were registered in the study as per the selective group. Patient enrollment in the study was started in March 2024 and was completed in September 2024.

Sample size

Considering the pilot nature of this observational study the sample size was calculated to be 30 by considered a confidence interval of 95 %, expected prevalence of RA in Ayurveda clinics as 0.5 % and precision as 5 %. Considering a drop out of 20 % the sample size was estimated to be 30.

Outcome assessment parameters

All patients irrespective of their subsequent treatment selection (Group A or B) were retrospectively enquired for their initial disease intensity at the start of the modern treatment. This enquiry was supplemented through the clinical records available with the patient. This was considered as the historical base line observation for both the groups. Subsequently these patients were further enquired about their disease activity at the time of their enrollment in the study. A final follow-up was recorded after completion of three month of active intervention of Ayurveda alone (Group A) or Ayurveda along with ongoing modern medicines (Group B). The disease activity has been examined through various outcome assessment parameters like Patients Global assessment, VAS Score, ESR, and DAS-28 –ESR.

Intervention

Those who have been on modern therapy for RA recommended preferably by a qualified rheumatologist or a post graduate in medicine, Orthopedics or neurology and were willing to continue the same along with the ayurvedic intervention were allowed to do so without making any change in their ongoing modern intervention. The only caution was to screen them for not taking steroids in past three months and in trial period. Conventionally offered modern medical treatment of RA at a teaching institute comprised of Methotrexate, Sulphasalazine, Hydroxychloroquine and Etoricoxib. The doses of these medications were determined by the treating physicians and were variable from case to case. These patients were additionally prescribed for conventionally offered treatment of Amavata at a teaching institute which comprised of local therapy, oral therapy, dietary recommendations, life style recommendations and exercise (Table 2). The same ayurvedic treatment was recommended to both groups irrespective of their continuation or discontinuation of modern interventions. Post intervention observations were done after completion of three months of the therapy.

Results

Total 92 patients were screened for the study on the basis of their initial diagnosis. These screened patients were subjected to the inclusion and exclusion criteria. Thirty four patients who fulfilled the criteria for enrollment were further asked for informed consent. Thirty Three of all agreed to participate in the study. This comprised of 18 participants in group A and 15 participants in group B (N = 33). Among these registered cases, one patient in each group could not complete the follow-ups. So the final assessment of the study was done upon 31 patients (A = 17, B = 14) (Fig. 2. Study Flow Chart).

Study flow chart.

Demographic observations

The average age of participants was 43 years and 38 years respectively in Group A and B showing no gross difference in age distribution among two groups. There was a preponderance of female participants in the study (A = 100 %, B = 93.3 %).

Pattern of initial treatment

A rheumatologist is found to be the primary specialist for initiation of treatment of RA in both groups (44.4 % in Group A and 60.0 % in Group B). Orthopedic specialists were second prominent consultants seen by 33.3 % in Group A and 40.0 % in Group B. Most patients were found to have received the modern treatment before joining this study for a period of 1–5 years (83.3 % Group A, 86.7 % Group B). Summated average modern treatment duration in past was found to be of ∼2 years.

Reasons of searching for the alternative therapy

Experiencing the drug related side effects were identified as single largest reason for switching the therapy reported by 50.0 % participants in Group A and 33.3 % in Group B (42.4 % in all). Lack of satisfactory response to treatment was another significant factor observed by 33.3 % in Group A and 20.0 % in Group B (27.3 % in all) (Table 3).

Historical assessment of disease activity and outcome of modern intervention alone, ayurveda intervention alone and integrative management

All patients registered in the study were enquired for their past disease status at two time points, first when they started the modern intervention and another when they stopped modern medicine and decided to switch for Ayurveda interventions. The first observation was retrospective and was based upon available clinical records supplemented through history. Second observation was also records and history based if the patient has stopped taking the modern medicine long before they initiated Ayurveda intervention. It was done prospectively if the patient decided to continue to modern medicine along with Ayurveda intervention. Historical assessment was done on a few parameters where a partial recall was possible and/or where a clinical record was available for observation. In the active intervention group both pre and post changes were recorded on set parameters. Finally an inter group comparison of all outcomes between historical control, Ayurveda alone and integrative management was done.

Assessment on the basis of modern outcome parameters

This assessment was done on the basis of historical records and subsequent prospective post intervention observation of swollen joint count, tender joint count, ESR, VAS score for pain and DAS-28 –ESR measurement. In all these parameters historical control could not show any statistically significant pre and post intervention difference. Contrary to this, group A and group B both have shown significant improvement post intervention (Table 4, Table 5, Table 6, Table 7).

Discussion

Musculoskeletal disorders of autoimmune origin pose a significant challenge in providing desirable therapeutic response without tolerable adversities. Current rheumatology care of RA is able to induce partial response (∼ACR20 to ACR 50) in patients but requires a lifelong management in most where the unwanted adversities cannot be spared. Seeking the interventions from multiple systems of health care during the course of the disease is a common observation among RA patients [[10], [11], [12]]. Not finding the adequate responses and observation of adversities is the common reason of patients shifting from one system to another. Out of this pool of patients swinging between different health care systems in search of relief, many prefer to opt for poly pharmacy. Although discouraged by allopathic physicians, many RA patients prefer to have Ayurveda medicine as add-on therapy in a bid to optimize the responses and also, to minimize the toxicity/adversity. Paradoxically current management of RA includes combination of multiple anti-rheumatic drugs with rescue analgesics/NSAIDs and some-times bridge course of steroids and more recently addition of biologics to the ongoing treatment can also be called as the poly-pharmacy. These patients, who remain reluctant in giving the history of simultaneous Ayurveda treatment to their allopathic physician, share their allopathic treatment history very clearly to their Ayurveda physicians while asking for if they need to continue the allopathic medicine for RA or Ayurveda alone can care for their problems. This constitutes a significant proportion of patients who opt for continuing the modern medicine along with Ayurveda besides many who prefer to stop (may already have stopped) modern medicine and to begin with Ayurveda. Unfortunately this pool of patients does not find a place in any clinical trial pertaining to RA. The study design adopted in this observational study is unique in the sense that it utilizes the historical clinical records of the patients who had earlier been on modern medicine and subsequently have decided to choose either Ayurveda or Ayurveda in combination with modern medicine. It therefore offers a self-control group which subsequently tried two different modalities. Eventually this design is expected to reduce the allocation bias or the differences of the group which might have arrived due to non-randomization. This design, although has its own limitations like allocation bias, recall bias for historical observations, interrupted treatment, unavailability of complete clinical records, and non-standard therapy in some cases, has an additional advantage of having the self-control so the bias related to non-randomization bias related to the treatment allocation and compliance could have been minimized. This is clear that on one side there is disadvantage but on other hand there is an added advantage. Therefore it should be accepted in a tune with the real-world data concept.

The observations made in the study show a better response obtained in Ayurveda alone and integrative intervention group with a marginally better response in integrative group based upon percentage of improvements through most parameters. Ayurvedic clinics receive substantial number of patients suffering from musculoskeletal diseases. RA forms a major proportion of these patients. Clinical trial protocols have been registered to observe equivalence or superiority of Ayurveda interventions in treating RA [13]. There had been trials on combination of Ayurveda and modern interventions in cases of RA but here the modern intervention was limited to Methotrexate only [14]. The results obtained in this study were favoring Ayurveda or its combination with modern intervention as a better approach in RA comparing to modern medicine alone.

This is also important to find no serious systemic adversities reported by the patients consuming Ayurveda or the combination therapy during the intervention period. The adversities reported in few patients were mild and related to Bhallataka (Semicarpus anacardiacum) causing burning micturition, mouth ulcer and perianal itching. The symptoms were vanished just after stopping the specific medicine. Adversity to bhallataka has previously been reported in the literature [15].

The study despite of its significant observations has its own limitations in the form of data collection for historical control, limited period of intervention, lack of direct observation, treatment also given by consultants other than rheumatologists and small sample size. Preponderance of females as study participants in the study may also be considered a limitation of the study although there was no substantial difference in such preponderance in two groups. The assessment of the DAS 28-ESR score based merely on recall was not possible unless it is supplemented by thorough records of past treatment. Moreover, the ESR may also find variability based upon reports done at different laboratories. EULAR remission criteria are considered a better tool than applying statistics to observe any changes in RA post treatment. This is also considered as a limitation of the study. It is expected that planning a prospective comparative efficacy study with a real active control comprising of standard modern care, standard Ayurveda care and their combination for longer period in a large cohort could lead to the confirmation of what is observed in this pilot study. The limitations observed in this study related to historical control can be eliminated doing a prospective study. This may eventually turn to be a real step towards integrative rheumatology where a need based application from Ayurveda and modern medicine in a single patient can prove to be the ultimate intervention in terms of long term care in RA.

Conclusion

RA poses a significant problem in getting sustained and adversity free relief from any system of medicine. Due to uncertainty of treatment responses and related adversities, many patients look at multiple systems of medicine in search of relief. Besides many patients who opt for Ayurveda alone, RA population in India also comprises of people who look at integrative treatment options where Ayurveda interventions are combined with allopathy interventions. This study has evaluated the benefits observed by RA patients who opted to go for Ayurveda alone and has compared it with an integrative protocol besides comparing it with historical control of modern standard care. The observations are favoring Ayurveda whole system interventions and its combination with modern standard care comparing to modern treatment alone. Despite of all limitations, this study comes as an advancement in understanding the role of Ayurveda alone and its judicious combination with modern medicine in the management of RA.

Development and temporal validation of an interpretable prediction model for delayed diagnosis of benign paroxysmal positional vertigo: a retrospective study from Beijing, China
Authors: Yanping Tong, Hongyan Li, Xue Xia, Yan Wang, Yu Cui, Xiaolin Wu, Yi Ju
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo develop and validate an interpretable prediction model for delayed diagnosis of benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV).MethodsThis retrospective study included 10,422 BPPV patients between July 2020 and June 2024 in Beijing, China, of whom 2949 (28.30%) had delayed diagnosis (symptom onset-to-diagnosis interval >14 days). Patients were temporally divided into development (n = 7536; training/test split 7:3) and temporal validation (n = 2886) cohorts. Logistic regression (LR), random forest (RF), extreme gradient boosting (XGB), and light gradient boosting machine (LGBM) models were developed and compared.ResultsIn the temporal validation cohort, LR achieved the highest discrimination (AUC 0.84, 95% CI 0.82, 0.86), and demonstrated stable performance across datasets. Atypical vestibular symptoms emerged as the strongest predictor of delayed diagnosis. Atypical triggers, motion sickness history, headache history, trauma history, and increasing age were also associated with delayed diagnosis. Firth regression yielded similar estimates. Subgroup validation in participants aged ≥65 years also demonstrated stable performance (AUC 0.83, 95% CI 0.79, 0.86).ConclusionsAn interpretable prediction model based on routinely collected clinical data was developed to estimate the risk of delayed diagnosis in BPPV. The final LR model demonstrated stable predictive performance across validation cohorts.

Introduction

Benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV) is the most common vestibular disorder. The typical clinical features of BPPV are self-reported rotational dizziness (or “vertigo”) and nystagmus triggered by positional changes (Cole and Honaker, 2022). However, its symptoms in older adults are often less specific than in younger populations, presenting with unsteadiness or falls rather than classic vertigo (Cox and Frith, 2025; Hyland et al., 2024; Piker and Jacobson, 2014).

The lifetime prevalence of BPPV is 2.4% (von Brevern et al., 2007) and increases with age. Reported prevalence among older adults varies widely, ranging from 6% to 53% across different clinical and community settings (Bicknell et al., 2025; Hawke et al., 2021; Lindell et al., 2021). Even though BPPV is common, it often goes unrecognized, leading to increased risk of falls and fractures, poor mental health and quality of life (Hawke et al., 2021; Pauwels et al., 2023; Yeo et al., 2024). Previous studies have also reported substantial diagnostic delays in patients with BPPV, with a mean delay from symptom onset to diagnosis of about 2 weeks, which may lead to unnecessary investigations and increased healthcare burden (Enkhtaivan et al., 2026). BPPV is one of the most common causes of dizziness and falls in older adults, and timely diagnosis and treatment could reduce falls, thereby lowering the morbidity and mortality associated with falls (Li et al., 2023).

Although prediction models for the risk of BPPV in disease etiology and treatment, including disease onset, maneuver numbers, recurrence, and affected semicircular canal have been developed in many studies(Baydan-Aran et al., 2026; Cao et al., 2024; Pan et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025), few studies have focused on prediction models for delayed diagnosis of BPPV and machine learning (ML) approaches remain underexplored in this area.

In this study, we aimed to develop and validate an interpretable prediction model to estimate the risk of delayed diagnosis in patients with BPPV. The SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) were applied to illustrate predictor contributions to estimated risk and enhance model interpretability.

Methods

Study design and population

The Vertigo and Balance Data System (VBDS) database comprises structured data on clinical symptom information and vestibular function test results of patients who presented with dizziness or balance disorders and underwent vestibular function testing at the Dizziness Clinical Diagnosis and Treatment Research Center, Beijing Tiantan Hospital, Capital Medical University. We retrospectively collected the clinical data of patients with BPPV diagnosed using the SRM-IV BPPV Diagnosis and Treatment System between 1 July 2020 and 30 June 2024 from the VBDS. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) aged ≥18 years, (2) definitive diagnosis of BPPV according to the 2015 Bárány Society diagnostic criteria (von Brevern et al., 2015), and (3) completion of a structured vestibular symptoms history questionnaire with comprehensive clinical data. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) acute cerebrovascular event; (2) severe central nervous system disorders, psychiatric conditions, or serious medical illnesses that precluded reliable history taking, completion of structured questionnaires, or performance of positional tests; (3) multiple recurrent visits during the study period; or (4) other vestibular disorders in the acute or symptomatic phase.

Finally, a total of 10,422 BPPV patients between July 2020 and June 2024 were included. Records with incomplete data were excluded, and no imputation was required. We used data from patients presenting from 1 July 2020 to 30 June 2023 (n = 7536) to establish the development cohort, which underwent a random 7:3 split into a training set (n = 5276) to develop the model and a test set (n = 2260) for internal validation. An temporal validation cohort (n = 2886) was constructed with patients from 1 July 2023 to 30 June 2024. This retrospective study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. All data were anonymized, so the requirement for informed consent from patients could be waived. The modeling strategy was prespecified prior to analysis in accordance with the TRIPOD+AI reporting guideline (Collins et al., 2024).

Measures

Defining delayed diagnosis and independent variables

Clinical data were obtained from the patients' structured vestibular symptoms history questionnaire in VBDS database, including sex, age, time from the onset of vestibular symptoms to BPPV diagnosis, characteristics and triggers of vestibular symptoms, single episode of vestibular symptoms duration (lasting more than 1 min or not), accompanying symptoms, and past medical history.

Delayed diagnosis was defined as an interval >14 days from symptom onset of the current symptomatic episode to BPPV diagnosis. Although no universally accepted threshold for delayed diagnosis in BPPV currently exists, this cutoff was selected based on clinical relevance in routine practice.

According to classification of vestibular symptoms from the Bárány Society (Bisdorff et al., 2009), vestibular symptoms were divided into vertigo and atypical vestibular symptoms. Vertigo indicated external spinning vertigo, while atypical vestibular symptoms encompassed non-spinning vertigo, dizziness, vestibulo-visual symptoms, and postural symptoms. Whether patients had accompanying symptoms, including autonomic symptoms, tinnitus, hearing loss, aural fullness, sleep disorders, neck pain, and headache were also recorded. Vestibular symptom triggers were classified as typical if induced by changes in head position relative to gravity, or atypical if associated with other triggers or with no identifiable trigger. Information was collected on patients' history of conditions including hypertension, diabetes, hyperlipidemia, coronary heart disease, BPPV, motion sickness, headache, and head trauma.

Twenty independent variables were selected based on clinical relevance and data availability. These included: age, gender, presence of atypical vestibular symptoms, presence of atypical triggers, vestibular symptom duration exceeding 1 min, accompanying symptoms, and the medical history variables listed above. Age was a continuous variable, while the others were defined as binary factors, with ‘1’ representing a positive history of a medical condition (or female sex for gender), and ‘0’ representing its absence (or male sex).

Sample size

Sample size for the training set was determined based on the events-per-variable (EPV) principle. The training set included 1505 delayed diagnosis events (event rate 28.50%). With up to 20 candidate predictors and a recommended minimum EPV of 10 for logistic regression (LR) modeling, the available sample size was considered adequate for reliable model development and temporal validation.

Model development and validation

Four prediction models, including LR, random forest (RF), extreme gradient boosting (XGB), and light gradient boosting machine (LGBM), were developed to predict the risk of delayed diagnosis in patients with BPPV. Model performance was evaluated with several commonly used metrics, including the area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC), sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value, negative predictive value, accuracy, F1 score, and Brier score. Calibration was assessed using calibration curves. Differences in model discrimination were compared using the DeLong test, and clinical usefulness was evaluated by decision curve analysis (DCA). Models developed in the training and test sets were subsequently assessed in an temporal validation cohort.

Statistical analysis

Given the limited number of candidate predictors, the large sample size, and the clinical relevance of all variables, all candidate predictors were included in model development without additional variable selection procedures. Additional analyses were performed to assess the robustness of the association for atypical vestibular symptoms. Firth penalized logistic regression and simplified logistic regression models were additionally evaluated. Subgroup validation of the final LR model was also performed in participants aged ≥65 years. A two-tailed P-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were conducted using R version 4.4.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Ethical considerations

The study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Institutional Review Board of Beijing Tiantan Hospital, Capital Medical University(No: KY-2025-306-02).

Calibration performance assessed by Brier scores indicated that RF achieved the lowest Brier score in the training set; however, this advantage was not maintained in the test set, suggesting potential overfitting. In contrast, LR and XGB demonstrated consistently low Brier scores across datasets, and indicating more stable calibration.

In the temporal validation set, LR achieved the highest AUC (0.84, 95% CI 0.82, 0.86), followed by XGB (0.84, 95% CI 0.82, 0.86), LGBM (0.84, 95% CI 0.82, 0.85), and RF (0.83, 95% CI 0.81, 0.85), with a statistically significant differences observed between LR and RF. Decision curve analysis showed largely overlapping curves across models in both internal and temporal validation sets, indicating comparable clinical net benefit. These findings indicate that LR maintained competitive discrimination compared with other ML models in an independent dataset. Decision curve analysis showed that all models provided similar clinical net benefit across a wide range of threshold probabilities in both internal and temporal validation sets. The curves largely overlapped, indicating comparable clinical utility among models. Based on discrimination, calibration stability, and model interpretability, logistic regression was selected as the final model for subsequent analyses.

Additional analyses were performed because atypical vestibular symptoms showed a strong association with delayed diagnosis. Atypical vestibular symptoms were observed in 81.6% of patients with delayed diagnosis and 16.6% of those without delayed diagnosis (Supplementary Table 1). Firth regression yielded similar estimates for atypical vestibular symptoms (OR 25.20, 95% CI 21.39, 29.80). Simplified model analyses showed improved discrimination after incorporation of additional key predictors, while similar performance was observed between the parsimonious and full models, although the full model achieved the highest AUC (Supplementary Table 3). Subgroup validation in participants aged ≥65 years also demonstrated stable model performance (AUC 0.83, 95% CI 0.79, 0.86).

Model explanation

The results of the multivariate LR analysis are presented in Supplementary Table 4. Atypical vestibular symptoms (OR 25.7, 95% CI 21, 77, 30.37), atypical triggers (OR 1.26, 95% CI 1.07, 1.50), headache history (OR 1.33, 95% CI 1.03, 1.71), motion sickness history (OR 1.59, 95% CI 1.31, 1.91), trauma history (OR 1.55, 95% CI 1.01, 2.38), and age (years) (OR 1.01, 95% CI 1.01, 1.02) were significantly associated with delayed diagnosis. Female sex demonstrated a borderline association with delayed diagnosis (OR 1.17, 95% CI 0.99, 1.38). Other variables were not significantly associated with the outcome. A nomogram was developed based on predictors with P values <0.10 in the multivariable model, including age, female sex, atypical vestibular symptoms, atypical triggers, headache history, motion sickness history, and trauma history, to facilitate visualization of the final LR model (Fig. 3). SHAP analysis was applied to the XGB and LGBM models to illustrate feature contributions to model predictions. Discussion

To our knowledge, this is the first large-scale study in China to develop a prediction model for delayed diagnosis of BPPV. We identified key risk factors and developed and validated multiple machine learning models using clinical data. All models demonstrated good discrimination and calibration, with LR showed the most stable and best performance in the temporal validation cohort, with good discrimination, calibration, clinical interpretability, and net benefit, while XGB and LGBM were used to explore feature importance and non-linear effects through SHAP analysis. These findings are particularly relevant in the context of ageing populations, where vertigo represents a common geriatric syndrome associated with frailty, falls and functional decline.

A key finding of this study is the overwhelming importance of atypical vestibular symptoms as a predictor of delayed diagnosis. Across all models, this variable dominated prediction, where in the LR model, atypical vestibular symptoms were associated with a more than 25-fold increase in the delayed diagnosis, and SHAP analyses in both XGB and LGBM showed this feature contributing substantially more to model output than any other variable. Atypical triggers were also consistently ranked among the most important predictors. These findings indicate that delayed diagnosis is primarily driven by the loss of classical symptom patterns rather than by demographic characteristics or comorbidity burden. Previous research indicates that the way patients perceive dizziness (vertigo, light-headedness, or unsteadiness) is not independently correlated with the diagnosis of BPPV (van Dam et al., 2020). Some BPPV patients, particularly older adults, do not present with typical vertigo but instead experience symptoms such as dizziness or imbalance as their main complaints (Li et al., 2023). This atypical symptom presentation may be related to age-related declines in the perception of peripheral vestibular stimuli (Bermúdez Rey et al., 2016). In a published study, typical positional changes of the head were reported as symptom triggers in 45.6% of patients with BPPV (Del Risco et al., 2023). In our cohort, atypical triggers, defined as triggers other than classical head positional changes or no identifiable trigger, were observed in 36.7% of promptly diagnosed patients and in 46.0% of those with delayed diagnosis. Together, these factors may prevent both patients and clinicians from recognising the positional nature of symptoms and performing timely positional testing to elicit nystagmus, thereby increasing the risk of delayed or missed diagnosis of BPPV.

Beyond atypical symptoms and triggers, a history of motion sickness consistently emerged as a key predictor of delayed diagnosis. Motion sickness is a common vestibular condition that is most commonly explained by the sensory conflict theory and is widely regarded as a marker of increased vestibular susceptibility and altered central sensory integration, although its precise mechanisms remain incompletely understood (Jiang et al., 2026). Dizziness associated with motion sickness can mimic the presentation of BPPV, potentially leading to an incorrect attribution of symptoms and delayed medical evaluation. In the LR model, motion sickness history was independently associated with an increased risk of delayed diagnosis and ranked second in importance in both the XGB and LGBM models.

Increasing age was associated with a higher likelihood of delayed diagnosis of BPPV in this study. Previous studies have identified age as an independent risk factor for both the occurrence and recurrence of BPPV, while older patients are also at higher risk of falls, fractures, cognitive decline, and cerebrovascular diseases (Choi and Kim, 2021; Krieger et al., 2022; Liao et al., 2015; Song et al., 2023). Age-related decline in vestibular function and sensory integration may make the typical positional features of BPPV less obvious, thereby contributing to delayed recognition and diagnosis. Consistent with these findings, subgroup validation in older adults also demonstrated stable model performance.

Several delayed diagnostic predictors provide further insight into diagnostic complexity. Headache and trauma history was independently associated with delayed diagnosis of BPPV in the LR model, but the nature of headache was not further characterised and identified. It has been reported that individuals with migraine history have an increased risk of developing benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV) (Chu et al., 2015). These features may shift clinical focus toward alternative diagnoses such as vestibular migraine or post-traumatic dizziness (Castillo-Bustamante et al., 2025; Huang et al., 2020). Female sex emerged as a borderline predictor of delayed diagnosis in the LR model. Given that female sex has been consistently reported as a risk factor for both the occurrence and recurrence of BPPV (Kim et al., 2021; Song et al., 2023), this finding may partly reflect the higher representation of women among patients with BPPV rather than a strong sex-specific effect on diagnostic delay. Many common cardiovascular and metabolic comorbidities, including hypertension, diabetes, and coronary heart disease, were not major contributors to the prediction models.

This study has several strengths. It was based on a large cohort of 10,422 patients with BPPV, providing sufficient data for model development and validation. Multiple machine learning models were compared, and the final LR model showed consistent performance in both temporal validation and older-adult subgroup analyses. Additional analyses, including Firth regression, produced similar findings and supported the stability of the results. Notably, atypical vestibular symptoms emerged as the strongest predictor of delayed diagnosis across different analytical approaches, highlighting a clinically important factor that may contribute to missed or delayed recognition of BPPV. In addition, all predictors were routinely collected clinical variables, which may facilitate the use of the model in everyday clinical practice.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, this was a retrospective single-centre study and potential bias cannot be completely eliminated. Although the model showed good performance in temporal validation, its generalisability to other populations and healthcare settings remains uncertain. Second, only routinely available clinical variables were included, and some historical variables were not further classified, which may have reduced predictor precision. Future studies are planned to include adelayed diagnosisitional temporal validation cohorts and to expand the dataset to encompass more diverse populations in order to improve the generalisability of the model.

Conclusions

In conclusion, we successfully developed an interpretable machine learning model based on routinely collected clinical data to predict the risk of delayed diagnosis in patients with BPPV. The final LR model demonstrated strong predictive performance in both internal validation and temporal validation cohorts. Further prospective and randomised studies are needed to determine whether diagnostic screening and management strategies guided by this prediction model can effectively improve clinical outcomes in patients with BPPV.

Serum Metabolite Profiling Reveals Inflammation-associated Biomarkers In Ventilator-associated Pneumonia Patients
Authors: Ajay K Gautam, Rahul Yadav, Bikram K Gupta, Nidhi Tiwari, Himanshu Bhushan, Chandan Singh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionVentilator-associated pneumonia (VAP) is a significant healthcare-associated infection occurring in patients after at least 48 hours of mechanical ventilation in the intensive care unit (ICU). It is associated with high mortality rates, reported up to 43%, and poses diagnostic challenges due to the nonspecific clinical and radiological indicators. Rapid and accurate detection is crucial for initiating effective, targeted antibiotic therapy to improve patient outcomes. Despite extensive research, reliable biomarkers for early VAP diagnosis and prognosis remain elusive. Nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) metabolomics offers a promising approach to identify systemic metabolic changes linked to VAP patients.Aim and objectives: AimThis study aimed to identify metabolic biosignatures in VAP patients using nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy.ObjectivesThe objective was to analyze serum samples from ventilated ICU patients using NMR spectroscopy to identify specific metabolic changes linked to VAP.Materials and methodsA prospective case-control study was conducted over one year at a tertiary care hospital involving patients with ventilator-associated pneumonia (VAP). Diagnosis of VAP was based on a positive culture from endotracheal or mini bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (mBALF) samples combined with a Clinical Pulmonary Infection Score (CPIS) of 6 or higher; patients with lower CPIS scores were classified as non-VAP (NVAP). Serum samples were analyzed using 1D 1H-NMR spectroscopy on a 600 MHz instrument, and comprehensive metabolite spectra was obtained. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was done using MetaboAnalyst 6.0 software.Results44 patients were included in the study, with a median age of 35.5 years. 20 (45.5%) patients met the criteria for VAP, other 24 (54.5%) patients had a lower CPIS score. AUC value for elevated metabolites were 0.72 (0.548-0.858) for phenylalanine, 0.706 (0.563-0.861) for acetoacetate, and 0.698 (0.506-0.847) for glutamate. Values indicate the acceptable discrimination power between VAP and non-VAP.1 These metabolites have significant elevated levels and indicate the disruption in their respective metabolic pathways.2 Phenylalanine is known to be involved in immune activation and is often elevated during systemic inflammation. Acetoacetate, a ketone body, reflects changes in energy metabolism under stress conditions such as infection and inflammation. Glutamate participates in lung and immune system regulation, with altered levels linked to lung injury and inflammatory responses.ConclusionPhenylalanine, acetoacetate, and glutamate, along with their associated metabolic pathways—particularly those involved in inflammation and lung injury—appear to play important roles in the pathophysiology of ventilator-associated pneumonia (VAP). Understanding these metabolic alterations may facilitate the development of targeted therapeutic strategies aimed at modulating inflammation and tissue damage. Additionally, these metabolites could potentially support VAP diagnosis and disease monitoring, though larger longitudinal studies are warranted to validate their clinical utility.SummaryStudy highlights the potential of serum metabolite profiling by NMR spectroscopy to improve understanding of ventilator-associated pneumonia (VAP). By identifying altered levels of phenylalanine, acetoacetate, and glutamate, the research points to significant metabolic changes linked to inflammation and lung injury in VAP patients. These insights can enhance disease management by revealing the underlying biochemical pathways involved.Keywords: Ventilator-associated pneumonia, NMR spectroscopy, metabolomics, biomarker, critical illness

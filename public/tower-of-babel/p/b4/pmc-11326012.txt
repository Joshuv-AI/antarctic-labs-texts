Research hotspots and frontiers of neoadjuvant therapy in triple-negative breast cancer: a bibliometric analysis of publications between 2002 and 2023
Authors: Chuang Yang, Hui Liu, Xing Feng, Han Shi, Yuchan Jiang, Junfeng Li, Jinxiang Tan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractTriple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) is a highly aggressive type of breast cancer with poor prognosis, and neoadjuvant therapy (NAT) has emerged as an important component in managing advanced-stage patients by providing surgical opportunities and improving survival outcomes. A search of publications on NAT for TNBC from 2002 to 2023 was conducted through the Web of Science core collection. A comprehensive bibliometric analysis was conducted on the data using CiteSpace, VOSviewer, and Bibliometrix. The analysis revealed a continuous and steady growth in the number of articles published in this field over the past 20 years. The United States has made significant contributions to this field, with The University of Texas MD Anderson Cancer Center publishing the most articles. Loibl, S. from Germany was found to be the most published author with 54 articles. Analysis of the journals showed that the Journal of Clinical Oncology is the most cited journal. Combined with the keyword co-occurrence analysis and clustering analysis, current research topic focuses on treatment regimens and disease prognosis. Dual-map overlay of the journals indicates that the research trend is gradually shifting from molecular biology and genetics to immunology and clinical research. Combination therapy, including immunotherapy, may be the future direction for NAT treatment of TNBC. Overall, this study provides valuable insights into the current research status, latest advancements, and emerging development trend of NAT for TNBC.Keywords: bibliometrics, frontiers, neoadjuvant therapy, triple-negative breast cancer, visualization

Breast cancer is one of the most common cancers in women, with an estimated 2 261 419 new cases and 684 996 deaths worldwide in 20201. Particularly, triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) is a malignant breast tumor that carries the most unfavorable prognosis2. It is characterized by a lack of expression of estrogen receptors, progesterone receptors, and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)3. Unlike other subtypes, TNBC lacks specific molecular targets for treatment, necessitating a greater reliance on comprehensive chemical therapy2,4. Furthermore, its aggressive nature and high metastatic potential have led to limited improvements in overall survival (OS) and locoregional recurrence rates despite previous therapeutic interventions5–7. In recent years, neoadjuvant therapy (NAT) has emerged as a critical component in managing TNBC, primarily focusing on providing surgical opportunities for advanced-stage patients and enhancing survival outcomes8.

The use of NAT, a systemic pre-surgical treatment regimen, is primarily aimed at improving tumor resectability, optimizing cosmetic outcomes, and mitigating postoperative complications9. In addition to systemic therapy, subsequent adjuvant therapy strategies were developed based on the achievement of pathological complete response (pCR) at the primary site following a full course of NAT10,11. For inoperable or locally advanced breast cancer, the American Society of Clinical Oncology (ASCO) guidelines recommend the use of NAT9. Moreover, numerous studies endorse the efficacy of NAT in improving the success rate of breast-conservation surgery, making it a viable option for TNBC patients12. However, optimal NAT treatment plans and course duration are still subject to debate13, and further research in this area holds significant potential for advancing TNBC treatments.

Bibliometrics is a potent statistical tool employed to analyze published books, journal articles and related metadata, enabling the exploration of relationships between published works14. The continuously evolving methodologies have made bibliometrics increasingly popular across various disciplines. In the medical field, bibliometrics plays a crucial role in unraveling new insights about academic trends, pharmacotherapy, diseases dynamics, and the broader healthcare sphere15. CiteSpace, VOSviewer and Bibliometrix are commonly utilized software tools for bibliometric analysis and visualization16. These tools define and illustrate trends and patterns in the scientific literature17,18. Over time, the application of bibliometrics research on TNBC has gradually increased19,20. However, little attention has been given to the research progress concerning NAT. Therefore, a comprehensive and up-to-date bibliometric analysis of NAT for TNBC is necessary.

This study encompasses literature on the treatment of TNBC with NAT from 2002 to 2023. It provides an overview of the current situation, latest advancements, and frontier research hotspots in the field, and identified new research directions.

Methods

Data sources

The Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC), recognized as one of the foremost and authoritative databases globally, has gradually become widely accepted as the preferred database for bibliometric analysis. The Science Citation Index Expanded (SCIE) and Social Science Citation Index (SSCI) within WoSCC serve as the primary repositories for the literature search. At present, it is considered that the treatment of breast cancer involves a multidisciplinary field21,22. Therefore, utilizing the integrated database WoSCC, which encompasses both SCIE and SSCI, enables a more comprehensive literature search and ensures the inclusion of high-quality scholarly publications. Furthermore, the data acquired from WoSCC contain comprehensive citation details, which facilitates the subsequent analysis of knowledge maps.

Search strategies

Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) is widely used as a controlled vocabulary for indexing and searching biomedical literature in PubMed. To ensure a comprehensive search, we employed Medical Subject Headings (MeSH), a widely recognized controlled vocabulary utilized for indexing and searching biomedical literature in PubMed23. Additionally, Entry Terms, which refer to synonymous or closely related terms associated with MeSH terms24, were also utilized to construct search formulas. By searching the MeSH database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/mesh/) using the search terms “Triple negative breast cancer” and “Neoadjuvant therapy”, a total of 15 terms related to TNBC and 48 terms related to NAT were identified. Search formulas were comprehensively determined based on the MESH vocabulary, Entry Terms, and our research purpose, which can be found in the Supplemental Digital Content 1, http://links.lww.com/JS9/C518.

As illustrated in Fig. 1, articles were retrieved using the topic search (Topics, TS) function in WoSCC. This study encompasses the majority of publications on NAT for TNBC available in this database to date, including English articles published from 1 January 2002 to December 2023, specifying that the document types are articles and review articles while excluding document types such as meeting abstract, editorial material, proceeding paper, early access, letter, correction, and book chapter. The process of inclusion and exclusion of documents is conducted on the WoSCC website. After the initial search, a total of 2586 publications were retrieved.

Data extraction, cleansing and standardization

The dataset sourced from WoSCC includes “complete records and cited references” and was downloaded as a plain text file. The original data was directly imported into bibliometric software for analysis without the need for format conversion. The process of data selection strictly adhered to the PRISMA guidelines25.

Literature search was conducted within a single day on 31 December 2023, mitigating potential biases arising from database updates. To ensure the quality and pertinence of the included literature, we manually screened the original data to determine the final selection of articles. This study referred to reviews on related topics and developed inclusion criteria26,27. As shown in Table 1, the two criteria for manual screening are content criteria and quality criteria. The content criteria guarantee the relevance of the publication to the study’s topic, while the quality criteria ensure that the article adheres to basic academic paper formatting standards.

To prevent omissions in manual screening, the filtered literature was imported into CiteSpace for the elimination of duplicate literature. The results indicated no duplicate records. Furthermore, singular, plural, and synonymous forms in the keyword field were merged in CiteSpace to remove duplicate entries in the visual map. Data extraction, cleansing, and standardization were conducted independently by two researchers and cross-validated, with any ambiguity resolved through consensus by a third researcher. Ultimately, a total of 2535 articles met the eligibility criteria listed in Table 1 were included in this study. We have referred to large number of bibliometric studies to ensure that the final included publications are suitable for statistical analysis in terms of quantity.

Bibliometric tools

After screening all eligible publications, VOSviewer1.6.19, CiteSpace6.1R6, and Bibliometrix4.1 were used to analyze publication information on NAT for TNBC over the past 20 years. Each of these three tools has its own strengths and can effectively complement each other in the process of conducting bibliometric analysis. CiteSpace is a visual analysis and bibliometrics tool developed by Professor Chaomei Chen, aiming to visually map highly cited and pivotal documents and analyze the emergence of research topics. It utilizes a progressive knowledge domain visualization approach to detect and facilitate the analysis of emerging trends and patterns in a knowledge domain16. VOSviewer is another freely available computer program for constructing and viewing bibliometric maps, which is particularly valuable for displaying large bibliometric maps in an easy-to-interpret way18. Bibliometrix is an open-source tool based on the R language. The online webpage for bibliometrics (https://bibliometric.com/), which utilizes the R package “Bibliometrics” is primarily used for scientific mapping and graphical analysis in bibliometrics28.

Visualization analysis

This analysis encompassed various aspects including authors, countries, institutions, journal distribution, references, and keywords. VOSviewer1.6.19 primarily facilitated the analysis of co-occurrence and clustering of institutions and authors. Different nodes represent different elements, and the differences in clustering are indicated by different colors in the co-occurrence maps. CiteSpace6.1 R6 was used to analyze the dual-map overlay of the journals, timeline view of references and keyword co-occurrence and clustering. Each cluster can be labeled by title terms, keywords, and abstract terms of citing articles related to the cluster. Bibliometrix4.1 provides a trend of publications by institutions and authors, as well as a keyword heatmap. The journal impact factors were retrieved from the Journal Citation Reports (JCR) of 2022 in WoSCC. In addition, SCImago Graphica1.0.35 and Microsoft Office Excel 2021 were used for graphing and data analysis.

Parameter setting

The documents were imported into CiteSpace for co-occurrence and clustering analysis. The time slice of keyword nodes was 1 year, and the threshold value was Top N = 50, indicating that the top 50 most frequently occurring keywords from each year were filtered out to construct the co-occurrence network for that year. Subsequently, individual networks were synthesized. The networks were pruned using the “Pathfinder network” and “pruning the merged network” methods to eliminate redundant nodes and connections. The modularity score Q (ranging from 0 to 1) was employed to assess the network’s structure, with a value exceeding 0.3 considered a well-structured network. The silhouette score S (ranging from −1 to 1) was utilized to evaluate the trustworthiness of clustering mapping. Values above 0.5 indicate reasonable clustering, while those exceeding 0.7 signify a highly trustworthy network29. The threshold for the reference timeline was set at Top N = 25, with the remaining parameters kept as default values. The double graph overlay analysis in CiteSpace was conducted using default parameters. In VOSviewer and Bibliometrix, adjustments were primarily made to the number of nodes in the graph, while the remaining parameters remained at their default values.

Ethics

Given that the data originated from the publicly accessible WoSCC database, obtaining ethical approval from an institutional review board was deemed unnecessary.

Results

Database search

In this study, a total of 2535 publications were retrieved. These publications involved the contributions of 14 674 authors affiliated with 3852 institutions across 86 countries. They were published in 484 different journals and referenced a total of 52 029 articles from 5718 distinct journals.

Annual publication volume, citation volume and trends

The annual publication volume and citation trends are shown in Fig. 2, with the earliest articles in this field dating back to 2002. The maximum number of articles was 363 in 2022. Notably, the year 2022 recorded the highest number of publications, reaching a peak of 363 articles. It is worth mentioning that in 2002, there were no articles with any citations, while in 2022, the maximum number of citations reached an impressive 19 571. Figure 2 clearly indicates that there was a dearth of relevant articles published in 2004 and 2005, and the annual publication volume remained below 100 until 2014. However, in recent years, particularly since 2019, the field of NAT for TNBC has witnessed an exponential growth in both publications and citations. The citation frequency demonstrates a statistically significant relationship with the year, as evidenced by binomial fitting analysis (R²=0.9615), which indicates that the NAT of TNBC has gradually become a research hotspot in the past 20 years.

Discussion

General information related to publications

To our knowledge, this study is the first bibliometric analysis of NAT for TNBC. In addition, the items included in this study meet the minimum requirements of the preliminary guidelines for bibliometric research35. We used the WoSCC database to retrieve 2535 publications from 1 January 2002 to 31 December 2023, including publications from 86 countries, 3852 institutions and more than 14 000 authors. The results indicate a significant growth trend in the number of publications published within related fields, with a sharp increase observed in recent years, which can be attributed to advancements in immunotherapy for TNBC36. And it is easy to predict that there will be more articles in this field in the future.

Among the top 10 most productive countries in this field, the United States and China have published numerous articles. This can be partly attributed to the high prevalence of breast cancer among females in both countries37. Interestingly, seven out of the top ten most productive institutions are based in the United States. This illustrates the dominant role and considerable influence of the United States in the realm of neoadjuvant therapy for triple-negative breast cancer, which can be attributed to the nation’s advanced technology and substantial medical investments. Especially, the University of Texas MD Anderson Cancer Center has demonstrated a consistent growth trend in terms of publication output in recent years. The cancer immunotherapy proposed by Professor James P. Allison from this institution won the Nobel Prize in 2018, which may have positively influenced the overall research output of the institution. Notably, Fudan University with the lowest average citation is the only institution from a developing country, indicating that the quality of research in China is relatively lower than other countries.

The most prolific author in this field is Loibl, S. from the German Breast Group, who focuses on the treatment and prognosis of different subtypes of breast cancer. His most highly cited article was published in the Lancet journal in 2014. A pooled analysis conducted by the team based on Collaborative Trials in Neoadjuvant Breast Cancer, indicates that pCR is not optimal as a surrogate endpoint for predicting event-free survival (EFS) and OS38. For the number of published articles, the top ten most productive journals account for 28.48%, highlighting their academic influence in the corresponding field. Although the Journal of Clinical Oncology does not have the highest impact factor among co-cited journals, its citation frequency is much higher than other journals. Among the co-cited journals, The Lancet has the highest impact factor and is well known for publishing high-quality articles. This journal targets scientific and healthcare professionals, focusing primarily on clinical and experimental research papers. Most of the journals listed in Table 3 mainly publish clinical original research, which is consistent with the visualization results of dual-map overlay of the journals in Fig. 6. In the early stage, more attention was devoted to molecular and mechanism research. With the advancement of immunosuppressants, neoadjuvant immunotherapy for breast cancer has been incorporated into clinical trials39–41. The combination of other drugs and immunosuppressants represents a promising direction of TNBC treatment.

Research foundation

The number of citations reflects the attention given to the article42. The top co-cited 10 references are mainly published from authoritative journals (Table 4). Among them, the most cited reference (R-1) was a large clinical study that compared the response to NACT and survival between patients with TNBC and non-TNBC. The study found that TNBC patients had higher pCR rates and excellent survival, but TNBC patients with residual disease had worse survival compared with non-TNBC patients43. This demonstrates the potential benefits of chemotherapy in TNBC, providing evidence for subsequent clinical studies combining chemotherapy regimens. Another clinical study (R-3) by von Minckwitz and colleagues, published in this journal in 2012, redefined pCR and demonstrated its application as a suitable surrogate endpoint for patients with luminal B/HER2 negative, HER2-positive, and TNBC. However, it was not deemed applicable for those with luminal B/HER2-positive or luminal A tumors44. Although this study did not assess all pathological indicators, it demonstrated a strong correlation between pCR and a favorable prognosis in TNBC, suggesting its potential as a prognostic marker.

Lehmann et al.45 (R-4) identified different subtypes of TNBC, including basal-like (BL1 and BL2), immunology (IM), mesenchymal (M), mesenchymal stem like (MSL), and lung androgen receptor (LAR) subtype, which provide valuable therapeutic targets against TNBC. Dent et al.46 (R-5) reported higher risks of early recurrence and death from TNBC compared to other breast cancer subtypes, based on clinical features and prognostic data from their cohort study. This finding has attracted more researchers to develop novel therapeutic strategies targeting this aggressive form of breast cancer. In 2000, Perou et al.47 (R-6) analyzed 65 surgical specimens of human breast tumors from 42 individuals and complementary DNA microarrays representing 8102 human genes. The research identified specific signaling pathways dominant in each molecular subtype, laying the foundation for subsequent targeted therapy research.

The standard NACT regimen typically consists of paclitaxel and AC (doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide) or EC (epirubicin and cyclophosphamide)9. A prospective study by Carey et al.48 (R-9) in 2007, found that basal-like and HER2+/ER− breast cancers are more sensitive to anthracycline-based NACT than luminal subtypes. In 2014, Von Minckwitz and colleagues (R-10) carried out a random phase 2 trial, which included previously untreated non-metastatic stage II–III TNBC and HER2-positive breast cancer patients. They reported that NACT combined with neoadjuvant carboplatin was effective only for TNBC patients49, and this regimen has been recommended by clinical guidelines for high-risk patients9. Sikov et al.50 (R-8) reached a similar conclusion in their 2015 study that the addition of either carboplatin or bevacizumab to NACT can increase pCR rates in stage II to III TNBC. Subsequently, in the 2017 study by Masuda et al.51 (R-7), 910 HER2 negative residual invasive cancer patients who received NACT were assigned into two groups with or without capecitabine. The results indicate that NACT combined with capecitabine can safely and effectively prolong the disease-free survival (DFS) and OS of patients. These studies further consolidate the status of NACT as a standard treatment regimen for TNBC, while also demonstrating promising outcomes when combined with other chemotherapy regimens. Future research can focus on optimizing and refining treatment strategies for TNBC to improve patient prognosis.

Co-citation analysis provides a better understanding of the research foundation of NAT for TNBC. These early studies have laid the theoretical and empirical groundwork for subsequent research in this field. Overall, the top 10 highly cited references as well as early research mainly introduced different tumor subtypes of TNBC, compared the efficacy of different treatment regimens, and discussed the topic of pCR rate as a prognostic indicator.

Identify emerging topics and research hotspots

In our study, the timeline view of references, the keywords co-occurrence with keywords clustering and the keywords distribution over time map were used to describe the research evolutions and hotspots concerning NAT for TNBC.

Hotspot keyword analysis

We observed that “tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes” (TILs) in the keywords distribution over time map and keywords clustering map is a high-frequency keyword that has emerged in recent years, which accords with the results of the timeline view of references. TILs constitute an immune cell population and primarily exist within the tumor microenvironment, where they can engage in immune reactions and alter the tumor immune microenvironment (TIME)52. TILs play an essential role in mediating response to chemotherapy and improving disease prognosis, with T-lymphocytes playing a pivotal role in immune response and mediating chemotherapy53. Among breast cancer subtypes, TNBC derives the greatest survival benefit because of the high TIL infiltrate, followed by HER2-positive breast cancer54. However, Denkert et al.55 have reported that increased TILs was an adverse prognostic factor for survival in luminal-HER2-negative breast cancer, highlighting the significance of TILs in prognostic evaluation of TNBC. The prevailing perspective suggests that TIL could serve as a primary biological prognostic biomarker for early-stage TNBCs56. Multiple studies have verified that tumor with high TILs exhibit elevated PD-L1 expression, potentially explaining the robust clinical response of TNBC to immune checkpoint inhibitors54. With the ongoing development of programmed cell death protein-1 (PD-1) targeted drugs, future research will increasingly focus on the clinical efficacy of TILs in immune checkpoint drugs56.

Hotspot clustering analysis

There are total 15 clusters from the visualization of keywords clustering analysis in Supplemental Digital Fig. 3, Supplemental Digital Content 5, http://links.lww.com/JS9/C522, which includes “expression”, “survival”, “locoregional recurrence”, “surgery”, “homologous recombination deficiency”, “radiation therapy”, “triple negative breast cancer”, “identification”, “immunotherapy”, “multicenter”, “surgical adjuvant breast”, “tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes”, “tumor microenvironment”, “neoadjuvant chemotherapy”, “poly (adp ribose) polymerase”. These keywords can be summarized into two aspects：treatment regimens and disease prognosis.

Compared with other subtypes of breast cancer subtypes, TNBC exhibits higher sensitivity to NACT and a stronger correlation with pCR and patients’ prognosis38. As with NAT, anthracyclines in combination or in sequence with taxanes over a period of 18–24 weeks is the standard NACT regimen for early-stage breast cancer57. Furthermore, international guidelines recommend that NACT as the preferred option for patients with locally advanced TNBC (stage II or III)58. Recent trends in chemotherapy regimen selection aim to increase pCR rates. Incorporating platinum drugs into standard chemotherapy demonstrates promise in increasing patient pCR rates, as evidenced by numerous experiments49,50,59. However, controversy surrounds the utilization of platinum drugs in TNBC treatment due to chemical toxicity and the absence of significant60.

Neoadjuvant radiotherapy is not considered a standard treatment for TNBC. Nevertheless, it remains a topic meriting exploration. Recent studies, including stereotactic ablative radiation therapy, have shown promising results61. Although some studies have demonstrated that neoadjuvant radiotherapy can improve the DFS of early breast cancer62, it is primarily used in cases of inoperable locally advanced breast cancer63. In contrast, adjuvant radiotherapy after NACT or mastectomy is more commonly employed to mitigate the risk of disease recurrence. Future studies may focus more on postoperative radiotherapy combination with other regimens.

Surgical intervention stands as the primary local treatment for early-stage TNBC, with options including mastectomy and breast conserving-surgery (BCS). The selection of a surgical approach should involve a comprehensive consideration of the patient’s clinical condition and their preference for breast preservation. Currently, numerous scholars have investigated the prognoses of different surgical methods following NAT for TNBC. A 20-year follow-up study revealed no statistically significant difference in survival between early-stage breast cancer patients undergoing mastectomy and those receiving BCS64. Therefore, BCS may be the preferred surgical option for early-stage breast cancer patients. Current evidence suggests that current evidence suggests that BCS combined with radiotherapy offers higher survival rates compared to mastectomy65. Furthermore, there is no significant difference in prognosis between radiotherapy alone versus surgery alone for patients achieving clinical complete response after NACT66, indicating the potential benefits of radiotherapy while providing additional treatment options for post-chemotherapy patients. Moreover, an ongoing prospective study aims to compare the 5-year local recurrence rate between the radiotherapy group and the surgery group, with the expectation of providing further validation on whether breast cancer patients with a pCR after NACT can be effectively treated with radiotherapy instead of surgery67. Combination therapy has shown promising results, necessitating further research to optimize treatment strategies. Nonetheless, current recommendations advocate for early-stage TNBC patients to receive BCS combined with radiotherapy. Total mastectomy is recommended when BCS fails to achieve a safe surgical margin68.

The efficacy of targeted therapy is limited due to the lack of clear biomarkers in TNBC. Currently, much research focuses on poly (adenosine diphosphate ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitors, which induce tumor cell death with breast cancer susceptibility gene (BRCA) mutations by inhibiting the DNA repair enzyme PARP. For instance, PARP inhibitors such as olaparib, rucaparib, and talazoparib have been approved for treating TNBC patients with BRCA mutations69,70. Additionally, antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) can utilize antibodies to directly deliver chemotherapeutic agents to tumor cells, increasing local drug concentrations while reducing toxicity to normal cells. For example, sacituzumab govitecan has demonstrated promising efficacy in treating refractory or relapsed TNBC (NCT02574455)71. Furthermore, AKT pathway inhibitors are employed in targeted therapy for TNBC. AKT, a protein kinase associated with cell growth, differentiation, and apoptosis, can be inhibited to reduce tumor cell activity. AKT inhibitors such as ipatasertib and capivasertib have shown advantages in combination therapy with other drugs in recent clinical studies72. With the advancement of sequencing technologies, rare gene mutations such as neurotrophin receptor kinase (NTRK) could also serve as potential therapeutic targets. Some NTRK inhibitors such as selitrectinib and repotrectinib are currently in clinical trials70. Currently, targeted therapies for TNBC are still in the exploratory stage, and the options for targeted drugs remain limited. The development of more biomarkers and molecular targets is urgently needed.

Comprehending the prognosis of TNBC is crucial for guiding treatment decisions. The prognosis of TNBC shows no significant correlation with tumor size and lymph node status. However, patients with TNBC exhibit a higher incidence of distant metastasis compared to non-TNBC73. Metastatic breast cancer has a worse prognosis than primary tumors due to a lack of early treatment intervention. A clinical investigation involving stage IV breast cancer patients showed that bone metastasis presents a comparatively more favorable prognosis, while brain metastasis manifests as the most aggressive74. Currently, gemcitabine combined with cisplatin is considered the first-line therapy in metastatic triple-negative breast cancer (mTNBC)75. However, recent studies have demonstrated that nab-paclitaxel combined with cisplatin yields superior efficacy76. These findings provide further evidence for combination therapy and offer additional options for first-line treatment in mTNBC.

Currently, research into prognostic markers for TNBC remains a prominent area of study. pCR stands as a crucial marker linked to long-term survival and serves as the primary indicator in assessing the effectiveness of NACT77. Numerous studies have consistently reported higher rates of pCR in both the breast and axillary regions of TNBC patients who undergo NAT, in comparison to patients with other breast cancer subtypes38,78,79. A recent meta-analysis systematically reviewed published randomized controlled trials of NAT for TNBC, revealing a substantial correlation between pCR and EFS, as well as a favorable association between pCR and overall OS80. Although these studies highlight the correlation between pCR and prognosis, there is currently a lack of robust evidence supporting pCR as the optimal surrogate endpoint during neoadjuvant therapy. Further clinical research is necessary to explore appropriate study endpoints.

In addition, the status of axillary lymph nodes is closely linked to disease progression and therapeutic regimen. The number of removed positive lymph nodes can serve as a prognostic indicator for NAT in TNBC81. A study compared the negative rate of sentinel lymph nodes (SLN) between before NACT and after and found a lower negative rate of SLN after NAT82. For patients with stage cN1 lymph node status before NAT but negative lymph node status after treatment, a meta-analysis demonstrated the accuracy and reliability of sentinel lymph node biopsy following NACT83. Multiple international guidelines have also supported that patients with greater than or equal to 3 negative SLN can be exempted from axillary lymph node dissection84,85. These evidences revealed the value of predicting TNBC and further guiding treatment strategies.

Some emerging biomarkers show promising prospects in prognosis. For instance, TILs have gained widespread recognition as prognostic markers86. Additionally, the Epithelial-Mesenchymal Transition (EMT) demonstrates significant predictive value in metastatic TNBC87. Lastly, Sry-related high-mobility-group/HMG box 10 (SOX10) serves as a promising biomarker for the differential diagnosis and prognosis of TNBC88.

Advances in neoadjuvant immunotherapy

In the timeline view of references, research on immunotherapy has become a hot topic in recent years. The dual-map overlay of the journals and the keywords co-occurrence with keywords clustering also detected the hotspots in this field. The following will discuss the significant clinical trials related to NAT for TNBC.

Immunotherapy refers to therapies that stimulate the immune response to tumors, enhancing the immune system’s ability to recognize and destroy tumor cells89. The field of immunotherapy has emerged as a promising treatment strategy for TNBC. TNBC exhibits higher response rates to immunotherapy compared to hormone receptor-positive and HER2-positive breast cancers, attributed to the tumor immune infiltration, neoantigens, and higher levels of immune markers such as programmed death ligand 1 (PD-L1) and PD-155,90.

Immune checkpoint inhibitors

PD-1 and PD-L1 expression in TNBC is higher than 50%91, suggesting that the immune checkpoint molecules PD-1 and PD-L1 may be effective targets for immunotherapy in TNBC.

Atezolizumab and pembrolizumab have been demonstrated to delay disease progression in locally advanced or metastatic TNBC92,93. However, the efficacy of monotherapy with immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) appears to be low. Clinical studies have indicated that although TNBC patients with higher PD-L1 expression levels show a greater trend of benefit from pembrolizumab, most patients do not achieve significant efficacy with monotherapy. An early-phase study preliminarily reported an objective response rate (ORR) of 18.5% in 32 PD-L1-positive patients treated with the PD-1 inhibitor pembrolizumab monotherapy (ClinicalTrials.gov, trial registration number: NCT02447003)93. Another phase III clinical trial (NCT02555657) enrolled advanced TNBC patients who had received 1–2 prior systemic therapies, showing no significant improvement in overall response rate (ORR), progression-free survival (PFS), and OS with pembrolizumab monotherapy94.

Cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated protein 4 (CTLA-4) receptor is considered a necessary suppressor of the immune response and can upregulate the expression of T cells95. However, no CTLA-4 inhibitor has been approved by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) of United States for monotherapy in patients with TNBC. Additional research on the application of CTLA-4 inhibitors in the treatment of solid tumors such as TNBC is currently underway, with increasing emphasis on their use in combination therapies with other drugs.

Immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy

Due to tumor heterogeneity and drug resistance, single-drug anticancer therapy has shown limited efficacy and is no longer the primary focus of research. Instead, there is growing evidence supporting the use of combination therapies, including the combination of immunotherapy with chemotherapy, the combination of immunotherapy with radiotherapy, and immunotherapy combinations as the preferred options for TNBC treatment.

Clinical trials have demonstrated that the addition of PD-1/PD-L1 blockers to NACT can significantly improve the pCR rate in TNBC patients with early and high risk of recurrence96. The IMpassion 031 trial is a phase III clinical study evaluating the efficacy of the PD-L1 inhibitor atezolizumab combination with chemotherapy for NAT in early-stage TNBC patients (NCT03197935)97. Participants were randomly assigned to receive atezolizumab combined with chemotherapy or placebo combined with chemotherapy. Results showed a median follow-up time of 20.6 months in the atezolizumab combined with the chemotherapy group compared to 19.8 months in the placebo combined with the chemotherapy group. Additionally, the atezolizumab combined with the chemotherapy group exhibited a 17% difference in pCR rates compared to the control group (95% CI 6–27; P<0.05). These findings suggest the potential clinical benefits of incorporating atezolizumab into neoadjuvant chemotherapy for early-stage TNBC patients.

The IMpassion 130 study (NCT02425891) investigated the efficacy of atezolizumab combination with albumin-bound paclitaxel for the treatment of early TNBC. The results of this Phase III clinical study indicate that the patients receiving combination therapy experienced better clinical benefits compared with placebo combined with albumin-bound paclitaxel98. However, subsequent IMpassion 131 study (NCT03125902) did not yield similar results. The combination of atezolizumab with paclitaxel did not improve PFS or overall OS compared to paclitaxel monotherapy99. This discrepancy may be attributed to the differential stimulatory activity of paclitaxel and albumin-bound paclitaxel on TILs.

Compared to the IMpassion 130 study, which only included albumin-bound paclitaxel as a chemotherapy regimen, KEYNOTE-355 (NCT02819518) evaluated whether the addition of pembrolizumab would enhance the sensitivity of metastatic TNBC patients to chemotherapy regimens. The study enrolled a total of 847 patients with metastatic TNBC, with 566 receiving pembrolizumab combination with chemotherapy agents (nab-paclitaxel, paclitaxel, or gemcitabine-carboplatin), while the remaining 281 received placebo combination with chemotherapy. Results revealed that the OS with pembrolizumab combination with chemotherapy was 23 months, higher than the OS of 16.1 months with chemotherapy monotherapy. Additionally, the ORR with combination therapy was 52.7%, compared to 40.8% with chemotherapy monotherapy100. Based on these study results, the FDA approved pembrolizumab combination with chemotherapy, followed by adjuvant pembrolizumab monotherapy after surgery, as a first-line treatment option for PD-L1-positive advanced TNBC, providing more treatment options for advanced TNBC patients.

The phase III clinical study KEYNOTE-522 is the first study exploring the efficacy of standard NAT combined with pembrolizumab followed by sequential therapy with pembrolizumab for TNBC patients. Results indicate that the addition of pembrolizumab to standard NACT increased the pCR rate increased from 51.2% to 64.8%. Furthermore, continuing pembrolizumab in the adjuvant phase significantly improved EFS, with rates increasing from 76.8% to 84.5%101. This study further clarifies the use of chemotherapy regimens and the role of pembrolizumab in the adjuvant treatment phase.

Currently, other PD-1/PD-L1 monoclonal antibodies are under development. nivolumab (NCT04159818) and spartalizumab (NCT04802876) have been enrolled in phase II trial, and avelumab (NCT02926196) and toripalimab (NCT04085276) have been recruited for phase III trial102. It is anticipated that more ICIs will emerge soon, and classic immune therapies based on PD-1/PD-L1 remains a promising direction for the future development of immunotherapy.

Immunotherapy combined with radiotherapy

Preclinical evidence suggests that stereotactic radiation therapy could enhance antigen-specific immune responses and upregulate PD-L1 expression on tumor cells by inducing cancer cell death103. Based on this experimental result, a Phase II clinical study of 50 participants using pembrolizumab combination with radiotherapy before surgery resulted in a higher than expected pCR rate104. In addition, a preliminary clinical study of MUKDEN03 involving 13 participants explored the efficacy and safety of neoadjuvant stereotactic radiotherapy combined with atezolizumab, albumin-bound paclitaxel, and carboplatin in the treatment of TNBC patients. The research results show that adding radiotherapy to combination therapy can increase the pCR rate by about 30% with good safety105. Currently, clinical trials related to neoadjuvant stereotactic radiotherapy are being conducted. It is expected to explore more the formulation of radiotherapy plans, as well as the comparison of the efficacy and safety of different immunotherapy drugs combined with radiotherapy.

Immunotherapy combined with targeted therapy

Although TNBC lacks well-defined targets, clinical trials are underway for targeted therapies. These trials primarily focus on ICIs such as PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors, combination with other targeted agents. Currently, there is a greater emphasis on combination therapies involving ICIs with poly PARP inhibitors or Vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), all of which have demonstrated efficacy and safety in preliminary experiments89. Notably, the combination of camrelizumab and VEGFR inhibitors has shown promising efficacy even in PD-L1-negative advanced TNBC patients (NCT03394287)106. Furthermore, the combination of other pathway inhibitors, such as the MEK1 pathway inhibitor cobimetinib and the ADCs drug ladiratuzumab vedotin, with ICIs, has demonstrated promising prospects89. These preliminary studies require larger clinical validations and hold promise for the development of diverse combinations of immune-based targeted therapies.

Combination therapy with dual ICIs

The combination of PD-1 antibody nivolumab and CTLA-4 antibody ipilimumab has been proven effective for metastatic melanoma. A recent Phase II study enrolled 31 patients with stage I to III TNBC who received nivolumab or nivolumab combined with ipilimumab (NCT01928394)107. Preliminary results showed that 19% of patients with the former regimen achieved a pathological response, compared to 27% with the latter regimen. Crucially, this study also identified a significant association between high levels of TILs expression and favorable prognosis, which reveals the potential role of TILs in predicting the efficacy of neoadjuvant immunotherapy. However, due to the inherent resistance of TNBC to ICIs, the clinical application of PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors as a monotherapy remains limited.

Other strategies for neoadjuvant immunotherapy

Some novel immunotherapies are still in exploratory stages but demonstrate promising potential. For instance, strategies involving phytochemicals, targeting gut microbiota, and interferons for reshaping the TIME show promise in enhancing immune response89. Additionally, targeted nanotechnology combined with immunotherapy is also being developed to overcome heterogeneity and drug resistance in TNBC108,109. Some ICIs combined with gene therapy have shown initial progress in mouse animal models, but larger-scale studies are needed to validate their efficacy110. Furthermore, emerging tumor vaccines and bispecific antibodies hold potential roles in treating TNBC patients89. Finally, the effectiveness of chimeric antigen receptor T cell therapy (CAR-T) has been validated in animal models of TNBC111. In the future, these innovative treatments hold the potential to become therapeutic options for patients with TNBC.

In conclusion, it is crucial and valuable to identify novel predictive immune biomarkers for TNBC. Future treatment aims to reduce patients’ acquired resistance to immunotherapy while identifying novel immunotherapy targets and exploring new immunotherapy combinations.

Limitations

The present study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. Firstly, to facilitate software analysis, data was sourced exclusively from the WoSCC database, while data from other search engines (such as PubMed, Google Scholar and Embase) were missed. Secondly, some recently published high-quality studies may not have received widespread attention due to their low citation frequency. Finally, the inclusion of only English language articles may have resulted in a less comprehensive search. These limitations should be taken into consideration when interpreting the results of this study. Nevertheless, this study will facilitate researchers in comprehending the pivotal areas and frontiers of NAT for TNBC.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study utilized bibliometrics visualization software CiteSpace, VOSviewer and Bibliometrix to analyze and visualize the publications of NAT for TNBC over the past 20 years. The study provided a comprehensive overview of the research hotspots, evolution path, and development trends in this field. The results showed that the United States has made significant contributions to this filed, while Asian countries need to conduct more high-quality research. It is necessary to strengthen cooperation between countries/regions, institutions, and authors. Furthermore, the development trend indicates that future research will gradually shift from molecular biology and genetics to immunology and clinical studies. Finally, neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy, prognostic indicators, and the optimal combination treatment regimen, especially including immunotherapy, may be the future direction of NAT for TNBC.

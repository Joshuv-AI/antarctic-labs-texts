IFPHOR0084 Clearer Letters, Better Care: A Health Literacy Driven Quality Improvement Project
Authors: Moradeke Orekoya, Annie Lowe
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn Wigan, an estimated 43.12% of residents have low health literacy, contributing to health inequalities. The local NHS Trust identified written patient communication as a modifiable barrier to care. This was informed by a volunteer led quality improvement project (July–December 2025) that recorded 123 wrong site attendances, with 28% linked to unclear letter content.AimTo improve the clarity and readability of initial outpatient appointment letters and use patient and staff feedback to develop local health literacy guidance for wider Trust use.MethodsThe project adopted a mixed methods design underpinned by experience-based co-design and health literacy informed principles. Four commonly used outpatient letter templates were selected. Baseline readability was assessed using the NHS Document Readability Tool. Co-production involved two workshops with an established Lived Experience Panel (n = 15-20) and two workshops with hospital volunteers (n = 8-9). Letters were evaluated for clarity, layout, language, and usability, then iteratively redesigned and circulated for further patient feedback.ResultsBaseline readability scores for the four letters ranged from 14-15 years (mean 14.7). Following redesign, scores improved to 12–13 years, with a 50% reduction in complex sentences. Initial patient feedback highlighted improved clarity, shorter paragraphs, and better visibility of key information.ConclusionCo-produced redesign of written communication can improve patient understanding and support more equitable access to services. The revised templates are progressing through governance, with monitoring of wrong site attendance planned for July 2026. Potential barriers to Trust wide implementation include variation in existing letter templates across departments, the existing digital system constraints and support required to embed health literacy guideline consistently.

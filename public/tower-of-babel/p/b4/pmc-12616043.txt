Bilateral multicystic dysplastic kidney disease in a Fetus: A rare case with a fatal outcome
Authors: Bernadette Pedun, Ivaan Pitua, Felix Bongomin, Crescent Tumusiime
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Bilateral multicystic dysplastic kidney (MCKD) is a rare, fatal congenital anomaly with multiple non-communicating renal cysts and absent parenchyma, often linked to other malformations. A 27-year-old gravida 4, para 3 woman at 28 weeks gestation, without prior antenatal care, presented with parotiditis. Ultrasound revealed bilateral enlarged kidneys (right: 7.49 × 4.78 cm; left: 8.25 × 4.68 cm) with multiple cysts, minimal parenchyma, severe oligohydramnios, and associated anomalies including posterior urethral valves, cardiomegaly, and ventriculomegaly. Bilateral MCKD is incompatible with life, detectable from 18 to 28 weeks, showing the value of routine anomaly scans.

Introduction

Multicystic dysplastic kidney disease (MCKD) is a non-heritable pediatric cystic renal disorder resulting from abnormal metanephric development, forming multiple non-communicating cysts in utero.1,2 Kidney development begins at week 4 with the nephrogenic cord, progressing through the non-functional pronephros, the mesonephros (functional in males as reproductive structures), and the metanephros, which forms the definitive kidney by week 11, with nephrogenesis completing by week 32–36.3,4 Bilateral MCKD is rare, often fatal, and associated with other congenital anomalies (e.g., vertebral, cardiac, brain, trachea-esophageal, anorectal, limb). It is more common in females and often diagnosed between 18 and 28 weeks gestation.5,6 Ultrasound is the primary imaging modality, revealing enlarged, lobulated kidneys with non-communicating cysts, absent parenchyma, and no renal pelvis/ureter connection.7, 8, 9 MRI is rarely needed due to ultrasound's accuracy and cost-effectiveness. Complications of bilateral MCKD include intrauterine fetal death (IUFD), spontaneous miscarriage, or early neonatal death, while unilateral MCKD may lead to hypertension, malignant transformation, or contralateral kidney hypertrophy.2 This report describes the sonographic features of bilateral MCKD and associated anomalies in a fetus.

Case presentation

A 27-year-old gravida 4, para 3, HIV-positive woman on antiretroviral therapy, at 28 weeks of gestation, presented to the gynecology clinic with a 2-week history of bilateral painful parotid swelling and intermittent high-grade fevers. She denied cough, chest pain, dyspnea, edema, vomiting, diarrhea, or headache.

On clinical examination, she was well nourished with bilateral tender parotid swelling (right > left), afebrile, with no pallor, jaundice, or edema. Her pulse rate was 120 beats/min, blood pressure 106/77 mmHg, oxygen saturation 99 %, and respiratory rate 18 breaths/min. Abdominal examination revealed a fundal height of 24 weeks (small for gestational age), with no tenderness or organomegaly.

Ultrasound evaluation revealed bilateral multicystic dysplastic kidneys with gross enlargement: the right kidney measured 7.49 × 4.78 cm (Fig. 1), while the left measured 8.25 × 4.68 cm (Fig. 2). Both kidneys contained multiple non-communicating cysts with minimal or absent renal parenchyma. The differential diagnosis considered included hydronephrosis, which can mimic cystic renal disease but usually shows a dilated renal pelvis and communicating cystic spaces, unlike the non-communicating cysts seen in multicystic dysplastic kidney. Polycystic kidney disease was also considered but was less likely due to its presentation with uniformly small cysts and preserved parenchyma. Posterior urethral valves were identified but interpreted as secondary anomalies associated with the primary bilateral MCKD.

Given the findings of bilateral MCKD with associated anomalies and severe oligohydramnios, the prognosis was poor. Consequently, a decision was made for expectant management with close monitoring. The mother continued antenatal care at the local health facility, where maternal-fetal monitoring was conducted based on clinical symptoms and serial ultrasounds. She maintained good adherence to antiretroviral therapy, and her parotid swelling was managed symptomatically, subsiding spontaneously over the next two weeks. At 31 weeks of gestation, she returned with reduced fetal movements but no uterine contractions; on examination, fetal heart sounds were absent, and ultrasound confirmed IUFD. Labor was induced, resulting in the delivery of a stillborn macerated preterm male fetus weighing approximately 1100 g in breech presentation. The placenta appeared small and infarcted. The mother remained hemodynamically stable, recovered well post-delivery, and was discharged on the third postpartum day with psychosocial counseling and continued ART follow-up.

Discussion

Bilateral MCKD is a rare, fatal congenital anomaly detectable by ultrasound at 18–28 weeks gestation. This 28-week fetus exhibited grossly enlarged kidneys with multiple non-communicating cysts, minimal/absent parenchyma, and severe oligohydramnios, consistent with bilateral MCKD.9 Associated anomalies such as posterior urethral valves, cardiomegaly with pericardial effusion, and ventriculomegaly align with reported syndromic associations. The small fundal height and oligohydramnios reflect impaired renal function from week 11, compounded by urethral obstruction (key-hole sign), leading to failure of amniotic fluid production. The mother's lack of ANC delayed diagnosis, we emphasize the need for routine anomaly scans at 18–28 weeks to identify fatal conditions and facilitate counseling in all pregnant mothers. Unlike unilateral MCKD, which may regress or cause hypertension/malignant transformation, bilateral MCKD is incompatible with life, often resulting in IUFD or neonatal death,1,2 an outcome of IUFD was documented in this case. The fetus's bradycardic episodes and cardiomegaly suggest a poor prognosis. Ultrasound's accuracy negates the need for MRI, supporting its use in resource-limited settings like in this case. Counseling is critical to guide parental decisions.

Conclusion

This case highlights bilateral MCKD as a fatal congenital anomaly detectable on routine ultrasound at 18–28 weeks. The findings of enlarged cystic kidneys, severe oligohydramnios, and multiple associated anomalies confirmed its incompatibility with life. Early antenatal screening is crucial for timely diagnosis, enabling parental counseling and informed decision-making, especially in resource-limited settings.

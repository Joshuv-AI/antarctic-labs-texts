Desmopressin use in major cardiac surgery is associated with renal impairment: a retrospective single-center analysis
Authors: Andreas Koköfer, Niklas Rodemund, Crispiana Cozowicz, Ottokar Stundner, Lukas Fischer, Bernhard Wernly
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDesmopressin acetate (1-deamino-8-d-arginine vasopressin—DDAVP) is a analogue of the antidiuretic hormone vasopressin. DDAVP is suggested to reduce bleeding after cardiac surgery using cardiopulmonary bypass. The aim of this study was to determine if DDAVP has any negative impact on renal function leading to acute kidney injury (AKI) and therefore increases the need for renal replacement therapy (RRT).MethodsWe performed a retrospective single institutional cohort analysis of 2,179 patients undergoing elective and urgent cardiac surgery with CPB from 2017 to 2021. Logistic regression analysis was used to investigate any association between DDAVP, the incidence of AKI KDIGO class 3 and the need for RRT, respectively. The model was adjusted for relevant covariates, including preexisting renal impairment, pharmacological hemodynamic support with vasopressors, complexity of the surgery and postoperative lactate. Secondary outcomes included, in hospital mortality and the need for allogenic blood transfusion.ResultsA total of 992 (45.5%) patients received DDAVP intraoperatively during surgery or shortly thereafter. The use of DDAVP was associated with a significant increase in in AKI KDIGO class 3 (OR 2.27; 95% CI 1.46–3.55; p < 0,001) and the need for RRT (OR 2.19; 95%CI 1.48–3.24; p < 0,001). Both findings persisted after covariate adjusting. No increased in-hospital mortality was associated with DDAVP.ConclusionIn cardiac surgery, the use of DDAVP was associated with an increased rate of server AKI and the requirement for RRT. Given the severity of the potential harm associated with DDAVP, an evidence-based reevaluation is needed to enable an accurate risk and benefit assessment.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12871-024-02680-w.Keywords: DDAVP, Cardiac surgery, AKI, RRT, Logistic regression modelling

Introduction

Desmopressin acetate (1-deamino-8-d-arginine vasopressin—DDAVP) is a synthetic analogue of the antidiuretic hormone vasopressin [1]. DDAVP is known to increase the plasma concentrations of von Willebrand factor (vWF), coagulation factor VIII and tissue plasminogen activator [2]. As vWF is essential for the formation of blood clots by enhancing platelet adhesion and aggregation [3], DDAVP is commonly used in the management of bleeding in mild to moderate hemophilia A, von Willebrand disease, and in other, less common, inherited platelet disorders. Moreover, in these conditions, DDAVP is also used to reduce perioperative bleeding [4–6]. However, in the absence of inherited bleeding disorders, there is only low grade of evidence that DDAVP reduces bleeding [7]. Still, there are recommendations to use DDAVP in bleeding conditions after severe trauma [8], for patients with intracranial hemorrhage after exposure to antiplatelet therapy [9], for the management of bleeding in neurosurgery and after cardiac surgery using cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) [10, 11] Given that DDAVP is also a potent agonist on the renal V2 receptor, it has a profound effect on diuresis and electrolyte balance [12]. DDAVP mediates a strong antidiuretic (ADH) like effect, lowers sodium levels, and alters both systolic and diastolic blood pressure [13]. Hence, this study sought to investigate the effect of DDAVP on renal function after cardiac surgery using CPB. The primary endpoint was determined as the occurrence of severe acute kidney injury (AKI). Secondary endpoints were the need for renal replacement therapy (RRT) during subsequent intensive care therapy. In addition, the impact of DDAVP on the occurrence of lower grade renal function impairment, using the definition of the acute kidney injury work group (KDIGO) [14], the in hospital mortality, and the need of allogenic blood products were investigated. We hypothesized that use of DDAVP after cardiac surgery was associated with renal impairment.

Methods

Study design

This retrospective cohort analysis included patients who underwent elective and urgent cardiac surgery utilizing CPB at the General Hospital Salzburg, Paracelsus Medical University from 2017 to 2021. The exposure of interest was the use of DDAVP in the immediate postoperative period. (i.e. after CPB and before admission to the ICU) This was compared to patients who were not treated with DDAVP.

Data was extracted from the institutional Salzburg Intensive Care database (SICdb) which was recently developed [15]. SICdb was implemented using data from the hospital ICU data management system (PDMS) iMDsoft MetaVision ICU (iMDsoft, Needham, MA) and the electronic health record (EHR) ORBIS (DH Healthcare GmbH, Bonn, Germany). The database contains admission, discharge, procedural and ICD10 data. Additionally, all medications (including dosage), procedures such as renal replacement therapy (RRT), intubation etc. are reported in SICdb. All data are fully anonymized as defined by the European General Data Protection Regulation [16]. The deidentification strategy additionally complies with the 'Guidance Regarding Methods for Deidentification of Protected Health Information in Accordance with the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) Privacy Rule' [17]. Reporting standards set forth by the REporting of studies Conducted using Observational Routinely-collected health data (RECORD) initiative were followed (see Appendix 1) [18]. Patients with missing data (n = 42) where excluded. The study was approved by the State Ethic Commission of Salzburg, Austria. (EK Nr: 1115/2021) Given the sole use of de-identified data, written informed consent was not required.

Endpoints

The primary outcome was determined to be AKI class 3 as described by the definition of the acute kidney injury work group (KDIGO) within the first 48h after surgery [14]. Secondary outcomes included the need for RRT and the need for allogenic blood transfusion (packed red blood cells, PRBC) to indicate the efficacy of DDAVP as a hemostatic agent. Other outcomes included sodium serum concentrations and in-hospital mortality.

Statistical analysis

We expressed continuous data points as median ± interquartile range or mean ± standard deviation, as appropriate. Categorical data were stated in numbers (percentage). Univariable distribution differences between groups were calculated using the Chi-square test, Student t-test, Mann–Whitney U test, and Chi-Squared test for independence, depending on distribution, respectively.

Logistic regression analysis was used for the primary binary outcome ‘AKI KDIGO class 3 within the first 48h after surgery’. (Model 1) For the multivariable logistic regression models, confounders with a P-value < 0.10 in the univariate analysis (Sex, pre-existing lung disease, pre-existing art. hypertension, premedication with ACE inhibitors, premedication with Acetylsalicylic Acid (ASA), procedures (e.g. CABG, aortic valve replacement (AVR)), cardio pulmonary bypass (CPB) time, preoperative platelet count and due to clinical importance pre-existing renal impairment (Creatinine clearance (CrCl) < 85 ml/min; CrCl calculations using the Cockcroft-Gault equation) were included, and then a backward variable elimination was performed. To avoid multicollinearity in our models, we did not include any scores that use parameters of renal function to predict death in critical care. However, we did report the SAPS 3 [19, 20] and EuroScore [21]. We identified preexisting renal impairment, vasopressor dose (as surrogate for overall hemodynamics) aortic cross clamp time (X-clamp) (as surrogate for the complexity of the procedure) and lactate as potential relevant independent confounding parameters to correct for. (Model 2) Additional regression modeling was used for the secondary outcome ‘need of RRT during ICU stay’. (Model 3 and 4) The same independent variables as in model 2 were used as appropriate. The variable selection process in Model 4 was similar as in in Model 2. Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) as estimator of prediction error was used to compere models. We reported adjusted odds ratios (aOR) with respective 95% confidence intervals (95%CI) for all multivariable models.

All statistical tests were two-sided, a p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. The R Project for Statistical Computing (RCore Team, 2022) was used for all statistical analyses. Results were visualized using R Studio (RStudio Team Version 2022.12.0 + 353, 2022, Boston, MA).

Results

This retrospective analysis included 2,179 patients who underwent cardiac surgery using CPB in the period from 2013 to 2021.

A total of 992 (45.5%) patients in the study population received DDAVP. The median dose of DDAVP given in the treatment group was 0.37µg/Kg [0,19–0,57]. DDAVP was given postoperatively only, no prophylactic DDAVP was applied. All patients did receive antifibrinolytic treatment with tranexamic acid. No Aprotinin was used. Baseline characteristics between the studied groups were very similar (Table 1). However, the median baseline platelet count was significantly lower in the group ultimately receiving DDAVP (181 G/L [147–224] vs. 194 G/L [157–243]; p < 0,001). The hemoglobin concentration at the day of surgery was similar in both groups (13.10 G/dl [12.10–14.10] vs. 13.10 G/dl [12.10–14.20]; p = 0.994). The most common surgical procedure was coronary-artery bypass grafting (CABG) (n = 1,210), followed by non-aortic valve procedures (includes all valve procedures that do not involve the aortic valve (i.e. mitral, tricuspid, and pulmonary valve procedures)) with or without CABG (n = 1,030), and surgical aortic-valve replacement (AVR) alone (n = 766). There was a statistically significantly increased incidence in postoperative AKI in the DDAVP group within the first 48h after surgery:

The incidence of KDIGO class 3 was significantly higher in patients receiving DDAVP (Table 2). This finding was confirmed by univariable logistic regression analysis (outcome KDIGO class 3, exposure DDAVP) with an OR of 2.27 (95% CI 1.46–3.55; p < 0.001) (Model 1). Furthermore, after adjusting for preexisting renal impairment, vasopressor dose, X-clamp time, and lactate, the association between DDAVP and KDIGO class 3 persisted (Model 2). Detailed results of Model 2 can be found in Table 3. Exposure to DDAVP was additionally associated with a significantly higher incidence of RRT, with rates of 3% (n = 41) in non-exposed patients compared to 7% (n = 72) in exposed patients (p < 0.001). This effect too was confirmed by univariable logistic regression, with the use of DDAVP as the independent variable and the need for CRRT as the dependent variable (Model 3) (OR 2.19; 95%CI 1.48–3.24; p < 0.001). After adjusting for similar potential confounders (preexisting renal impairment, vasopressor dose, X-clamp time, and lactate) the association between DDAVP and the need for RRT persisted (Model 4), as shown in Table 3. There was also a significantly higher incidence of AKI KDIGO class 2 (33.2%, n = 331) in patients treated with DDAVP compared to those not treated with DDAVP (30.2%, n = 359) (p < 0,001). Conversely, the incidence of no or only mild alterations in renal function (AKI KDIGO 1) was more common in patients not treated with DDAVP (Table 2). Additional results on renal function and hemodynamics can be found in Table 2. There was no difference in mortality between patients who received DDAVP and those who did not. The overall hospital mortality rate was 4% and 5% (p = 0.36), respectively. The provisionary inclusion of the EuroScore II into the multivariable model did not improve model quality, nor was the EuroScore II significant as a variable. (Results not displayed).

Interestingly, we found that there was significantly more need of PRBC in those patients receiving DDAVP at the day of surgery (d0) (0.88 PRBC (SD 2.05) vs. 0.56 PRBC (SD 1.85); p < 0.001) and at the first postoperative day (d1) (0.26 PRBC (SD 0.82) vs. 0.14 PRBC (SD 0.61); p < 0.001), respectively. Discussion

Among 2,179 patients undergoing open heart surgery, we found that the use of DDAVP was associated with a significant increase in the incidence of severe AKI and the need for RRT during ICU stay.

DDAVP has been extensively discussed to promote coagulation after cardiac surgery for decades [22]. However, it does have a profound effect on diuresis and sodium balance too. To our knowledge this is the first study investigating a potential impact of DDAVP on renal function after major cardiac surgery. As any cardiac surgery involving CPB poses a significant risk to renal function, it is essential to mitigate any additional harm and to avoid any potential nephrotoxic substancens [23–26]. The overall blood sparing effect of DDAVP is questionable. However, in the latest Cochrane analysis on DDAVP by Desborough et al., DDAVP exhibited a blood-sparing effect in cardiac surgery. The same effect was not present in any other indications studied such as pediatric cardiac surgery, orthopedic surgery, plastic surgery, vascular surgery, and surgeries for other conditions [7].

General guidelines on the perioperative use of DDAVP in cardiac surgery are sparse [11] with some recommendations in place only for patients premedicated with platelet inhibitors such as acetylsalicylic acid or P2Y inhibitors, and for patients with a low platelet count [11, 27, 28].

In our study DDAVP administration was unusually frequent. There was unfortunately no institutional protocol for the use of DDAVP, resulting in an obviously extremely liberal practice at the discretion of the caregiver. However, in contrast to the utilization of DDAVP, a clear protocol for the initiation of blood transfusion therapy at a hemoglobin threshold of < 7.5mg/dl was in place, resulting in a applicable result on the use of PRBC.

Our finding of high incidences of cardiac surgery-associated AKI (CSA-AKI) is consistent with other reports, being commonly higher than 30% [23]. However, the reported incidence of CSA-AKI depends strongly on the definition used for AKI [23, 29]. We choose to report AKI with the use of the KDIGO [14]. KDIGO, which is a combination of the RIFLE [30] and AKIN [31] classification, is the most commonly used definition for CSA-AKI in the literature today. It is important to acknowledge that KDIGO provides multiple methods for categorizing serum creatinine increases. Given the relatively brief monitoring timeframe, we adopted the definition of a serum creatinine increase within 48 h. KDIGO is one of the most sensitive ways to report AKI, as it relies not only on fluid balance and urine output but also heavily on serum creatinine alteration. The latter being relevantly affected by factors not-GFR related: Including age, sex, race, body surface area, diet, diabetes, liver disease, different drugs and laboratory analytical methods [24]. Our study found a significant difference not only in the incidence of AKI class 2 and 3 but also in the increase of uncorrected SCr and the need for RRT. In clinical practice the need of RRT is of high importance, as it dramatically increases costs as well as resource utilization and is overall associated with increased mortality and morbidity.

Notably, the mechanism by which an alteration in renal function and ultimately CSA-AKI may be triggered when DDAVP is used is still unclear. The main antidiuretic effect of DDAVP is mediated via the renal V2 receptors, promoting the reabsorption of water from modified glomerular filtrate when it reaches the collecting ducts of the kidney [32, 33]. This reabsorption of water is achieved via an activation of aquaporin-2 (AQP2) water channels and ultimately results in a decrease of diuresis and a lowered sodium concentration in the plasma. Our study confirmed this basic physiological/pharmaceutical effect as we did find significant lower sodium levels in patients receiving DDAVP, indicating some kind of more pronounced fluid overload and decreased diuresis when DDAVP was used. However, a single contradictory study in cardiac surgery by Lei Jin et al., reporting no difference in urine output when DDAVP was used, must be mentioned here [34]. Their results are somewhat difficult to interpreted as the antidiuretic effect of DDAVP is well reported and of clinical relevance in various indications [35]. However, to what extent the direct antidiuretic effect of DDAVP may have a causal implication on the increased incidence of severe CSA-AKI is unclear. To confirm our findings and to establish a better pathophysiological understanding a larger dataset or ideally a prospective RCT combined with physiological studies would be needed.

Several limitations of our study need to be considered: Given the observational nature of our data, inherent limitations include the lack of randomization which does not allow for any casual conclusions, but rather careful consideration and interpretation of associations.

When designing our models, we were careful to avoid multicollinearity and used caution when selecting variables. We deliberately chose not to include commonly used survival scores such as the EuroScore II or the SAPS III or in our models as they all include renal function (and age) in their outcome prediction. However, as there are statistical approaches suggesting that scores can still be used in regression modelling despite the risk of multicollinearity [36, 37], we tentatively added EuroScore II in both multivariable models, but found that it did not improve the model. In order to prevent overfitting, we decided ultimately to keep our models as simple as possible, especially as the case number may be considered small.

Another limitation, especially when it comes to the outcome parameter ‘effect on bleeding’, is the frequent use of platelet inhibitors in both groups. The use was not equal in between groups (Table 1), however the inclusion whether acetylsalicylic was used or not did not improve the multivariable models. While information on congenital bleeding disorders such hemophilia or lack/low level of vWF was also not included in our data set, we did not find this problematic due to extreme rarity. Data on point-of-care coagulation diagnostics were not available digitally. Therefore, even though there is some data on the value of point-of-care coagulation diagnostics when DDAVP is used [38], Thromboelastography (TEG) or rotational Thromboelastometry (ROTEM) were not included in our analysis. In the study we found no blood transfusion sparing effect when DDAVP was used. In contrast, there was a higher rate of transfusion of PRBC in the study group (Table 2). For this finding, we do not have any conclusive explanation. One could speculate that the decision of the clinician to use DDAVP in those patients who anticipated a higher risk of bleeding was made more casually. However, within the framework of the retrospective analysis, we can neither prove nor disprove this. Given that transfusion per se is a specific cause of AKI after cardiac surgery [39] there might be some form of mutual influence which we cannot rule out completely.

Lastly there was no uniform dosing regimen of DDAVP nor was there a protocolized indication when and why to give DDAVP. To overcome these shortcomings a propensity score matching could have been performed. However, due to the relatively small number of cases (n = 2,179) and again with the intend not to overfit/overinterpret data we refrained from such analysis. Generally, it must be stated that data on DDAVP dosing if used to improve coagulation is extremely sparse and not based on good evidence. In one of the largest successful DDAVP study in cardiac surgery by Depotis et al. 0.4µg/kg was used given over 30min [38]. In our analysis, treatment dosing regimens varied, ranging from a single dose (usually 0.3µg/kg; although in some cases up to 0.5µg/kg) to as many as five repetitive doses, usually with adjunctive antifibrinolytic therapy as described by Leissinger et al. [40]. However the median dose was 0.37µg/Kg [0,19–0,57], corresponding well to Depotis et al.

Conclusion

DDAVP, given to promote coagulation after cardiac surgery was associated with an increased incidence of KDIGO class 3 CSA-AKI and an increased need for RRT. This indicates a potentially nephrotoxic effect of DDAVP when used in cardiac surgery with CPB. In this study no difference in early mortality was detected, nor was there any difference in the amount of allogenic blood transfusion required. The value of DDAVP to improve coagulation in cardiac surgery is highly questionable due to a potentially unfavorable side-effect profile. Future studies are needed to investigate DDAVP as a potential modifiable risk factor for renal failure following cardiac surgery.

Protocol to capture transcription factor-mediated 3D chromatin interactions using affinity tag-based BL-HiChIP
Authors: Ruimin Ren, Yao Hua, Heng Wang
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
SummaryPioneer transcription factors (TFs) can directly establish higher-order chromatin interactions to instruct gene transcription. Here, we present a protocol for capturing TF-mediated 3D chromatin interactions using affinity tag-based bridge linker (BL)-Hi-chromatin immunoprecipitation (HiChIP). We describe steps for constructing FLAG-tagged TF, performing BL-HiChIP, and preparing the library. We then detail procedures for sequencing, data analysis, and quality control. This protocol has potential applications in 3D chromatin analysis centered on any specific TF in any type of cells without the need of optimal antibodies.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Ren et al. (2022).1Subject areas: Genomics, Molecular Biology, Chromatin Immunoprecipitation, ChIP

Before you begin

The affinity tag based fusion protein strategy was commonly used in the detection, localization and quantification of the target protein in the cell.2 This tagged-protein strategy combined with high-throughput sequencing is also suitable for examining the chromatin binding patterns of specific transcription factors.1,3 Based on the proven stringency and efficiency of the FLAG tag and the derived antibodies, we can further explore the role of the pioneering transcription factors in 3D chromatin conformational organization. In this protocol, we conducted a transgenic cell line conditionally expressing MyoD fused with FLAG, related protocols are marked with “for MyoD induced transdifferentiation.” In other different experimental models, it only needs to fuse the FLAG sequence with the target transcription factor in frame either by endogenous knock-in or transgenic overexpression, and then followed by the modified bridge linker-HiChIP assay with affinity-tag-based antibody (From step 9 to step 23).

The protocol below describes the specific steps for using mouse fibroblast cells (C3H/10T1/2). However, we have also used this protocol in other cell types such as NIH/3T3 cells and primary pig muscle stem cells.

All buffers must be prepared and stored at the suggested temperature.

Expected outcomes

Expected outcomes were annotated in the protocol. A quality library of BL-HiChIP dataset usually includes ∼20 million uniquely mapped nonredundant PETs from ∼200 million raw reads and half of them are singleton PETs. The majority (>70%) of BL-HiChIP anchors overlap with ChIP peaks (Figure 4A) and BL-HiChIP loops are exist in the HiC loops (Figure 4B).

Limitations

In theory, this BL-HiChIP protocol can be adapted for the capture of chromatin interactions mediated by any transcription factor to explore the 3D chromatin conformation features. However, there are several limitations that hinder the performance of BL-HiChIP, such as TF expression model, TF expression level, and enrichment specificity of antibody. When one needs to study endogenous TF, they should check the expression of TF first and then test the ChIP-Seq assay using the available TF antibody. If it is not easily achievable with ChIP assay, you can resort to endogenously tag the TF and then capture their chromatin binding sites and the TF-mediated chromatin conformation by affinity-tag-based antibody. When processing HiChIP data, it is a critical quality control step to identify overlaps between the 2D peaks from ChIP-seq and the high-confidence interactions from the 3D BL-HiChIP data; generally more than 75% overlap demonstrates the data are reliable. In addition, the BL-HiChIP protocol is complex and the amount of captured DNA is dependent on several important steps including cell number, enzyme digestion, ligation efficiency and antibody immunoprecipitation. Thus, the input cell amount may affect the BL-HiChIP outcome. Therefore, we recommend the starting cell number range from 107 to 108 for a particular transcription factor when using the tag fusion strategy.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

Step 6. After lentivirus transduction on C3H/10T1/2 cells, the cell state goes to bad.

Potential solution

Add 1 mL FBS to 4 mL lentivirus supernatant and then add it into cells.

Problem 2

Step 12. Too many fragments of AluI digestion DNA are longer than 2000 bp.

Potential solution

Decrease the cell density in a AluI digestion reaction and increase the amount of AluI.

Problem 3

Step 15. The fragments of ligation DNA are no extension compared with AluI digestion DNA.

Potential solution

Inefficiency of AluI digestion. See the potential solution of problem 2.

Increase the amount of reagents in the A-tailing mix (step 14a) to improve the efficiency of adding 3’ A-tailing.

Problem 4

Step 17. Too many DNA fragments are shorter than 1000 bp.

Potential solution

Modify the parameter of sonication to reduce shearing of chromatin, which prevent the break of proximal ligation DNA.

Problem 5

Step 18. The amount of BL-HiChIP DNA is very low.

Potential solution

Increase the cell number.

Increase the amount of antibody for immunoprecipitation in the step 13c.

Decrease the cell lysis time in the step 10b.

Problem 6

Step 19. Too many DNA fragments shorter than 200 bp.

Potential solution

Decrease the amount of Tn5 transposome for digestion in the step 19a.

Problem 7

Step 22. The DNA amount of library is very low.

Potential solution

Increase the number of PCR cycle slightly (∼2 cycles) (step 21d). Too many cycles will create PCR duplicates that will be removed from the sequence data, so the number of cycle is recommended no more than 12.

Modify the parameter of sonication to reduce shearing of chromatin, which prevent the break of proximal ligation DNA.

Problem 8

Step 26. The percentage of valid pets is less than 40%.

Potential solution

Improve the ligation efficiency by increasing the amount of bridge linker.

Problem 9

Step 26. The reads of valid interaction_rmdup are too low.

Potential solution

Reduce the number of PCR cycles.

Increase the amount of BL-HiChIP DNA for library preparation.

Efficacy and safety of CT-P47 versus reference tocilizumab: 32-week results of a randomised, active-controlled, double-blind, phase III study in patients with rheumatoid arthritis, including 8 weeks of switching data from reference tocilizumab to CT-P47
Authors: Josef S Smolen, Jakub Trefler, Artur Racewicz, Janusz Jaworski, Agnieszka Zielińska, Marek Krogulec, Sławomir Jeka, Rafał Wojciechowski, Katarzyna Kolossa, Anna Dudek, Magdalena Krajewska-Włodarczyk, Paweł Hrycaj, Piotr Adrian Klimiuk, Gerd R Burmester, SungHyun Kim, YunJu Bae, GoEun Yang, YooBin Jung, JiWoo Hong, Edward Keystone
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesTo demonstrate efficacy equivalence of CT-P47 and EU-approved reference tocilizumab (r-TCZ) in patients with rheumatoid arthritis (RA).MethodsThis double-blind, phase III study randomised (1:1) patients to receive CT-P47 or r-TCZ (8 mg/kg) every 4 weeks until week 20 during treatment period (TP) 1. Prior to week 24 dosing, patients receiving r-TCZ were randomised (1:1) to continue r-TCZ or switch to CT-P47; patients receiving CT-P47 continued CT-P47 (TP2, 8 mg/kg every 4 weeks until week 48). The dual primary endpoints (for different regulatory requirements) were mean changes from baseline in Disease Activity Score in 28 joints (DAS28; erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR)) at week 12 and week 24. Efficacy equivalence was determined if CIs for the treatment difference were within predefined equivalence margins: (95% CI −0.6, 0.6 (analysis of covariance (ANCOVA)) at week 12 or 90% CI −0.6, 0.5 (ANCOVA with multiple imputation) at week 24). Additional efficacy, pharmacokinetic (PK) and safety endpoints, including immunogenicity, were investigated. Findings up to week 32 are presented.ResultsIn TP1, 471 patients were randomised (234 CT-P47; 237 r-TCZ). The 95% and 90% CIs for the estimated treatment differences were contained within the predefined equivalence margins; the estimated difference in DAS28-ESR at week 12 was –0.01 (95% CI −0.26, 0.24) and at week 24 was −0.10 (90% CI −0.30, 0.10). Secondary efficacy endpoints, PKs and overall safety were comparable between groups up to week 32.ConclusionsEfficacy equivalence, alongside comparable PK, safety and immunogenicity profiles, was determined between CT-P47 and r-TCZ in adults with RA, including after switching from r-TCZ to CT-P47.Keywords: arthritis, rheumatoid; autoimmune diseases; biosimilar pharmaceuticals

Introduction

Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is a common autoimmune disease that causes joint inflammation which can lead to cartilage degradation and bone erosions1 2; it is the most common form of autoimmune arthritis,3 with an estimated worldwide prevalence of 0.5%.4 Given the significant negative impact of RA on patients’ health-related quality of life, ability to work and participation in social activities,5 it represents a global key public health issue.1

Tocilizumab (TCZ) was the first biological disease-modifying antirheumatic drug (DMARD) to target interleukin-6 (IL-6), a proinflammatory cytokine involved in the pathology of chronic immune diseases.6 7 TCZ is a recombinant, humanised, monoclonal antibody directed against both membrane-bound and soluble forms of the IL-6 receptor (IL-6R); this inhibits IL-6-mediated inflammatory activity associated with inflammatory diseases, such as RA.6 8 Both intravenous and subcutaneous (SC) formulations of TCZ have received regulatory approval from the European Medicines Agency (EMA) and the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for the treatment of various indications, including RA, systemic juvenile idiopathic arthritis and giant cell arteritis.9 10

The EMA and FDA have recently approved two TCZ biosimilars, MSB1145611 12 and BAT1806/BIIB800,13 14 based on phase III equivalence studies.15 16 Although indicated for RA, as with reference TCZ, these TCZ biosimilars are also approved for other indications, such as systemic juvenile idiopathic arthritis or giant cell arteritis.11,14 CT-P47 is a candidate TCZ biosimilar in development.17 Pharmacokinetic (PK) equivalence and comparable safety between CT-P47 SC and EU-approved TCZ have been demonstrated in a randomised, double-blind, phase I study,18 and a second phase I study, conducted in Japan, comparing CT-P47 intravenous, EU-approved TCZ and US-licensed TCZ, has been completed.

The primary objective of this randomised, active-controlled, double-blind, multicentre, phase III study was to demonstrate efficacy equivalence of CT-P47 and EU-approved TCZ, delivered intravenously, in patients with moderate-to-severe active RA. The study also evaluated additional efficacy endpoints, PKs and overall safety, including immunogenicity.

Methods

Study design

This randomised, active-controlled, double-blind, multicentre, phase III study was conducted at 22 study centres in Poland (online supplemental table S1). This study consisted of three periods (figure 1): screening (day –42 to day –1), treatment (weeks 0–48) and end-of-study (EOS; week 52). For treatment period (TP) 1 (week 0 (day 1) to week 24 (predose)), patients were randomly assigned (1:1) on day 1 to receive CT-P47 or EU-approved TCZ (hereafter referred to as reference (r)-TCZ) every 4 weeks until week 20. During TP2 (after TP1 and prior to an EOS visit), patients in the CT-P47 group continued to receive CT-P47 every 4 weeks to week 48 (CT-P47 maintenance). Prior to dosing at week 24, patients in the r-TCZ group were randomly assigned (1:1) to either continue r-TCZ (r-TCZ maintenance) or switch to CT-P47 (CT-P47 switched) every 4 weeks until week 48. Results up to week 32 are reported here.

Randomisation was performed using an interactive web response system (IWRS). Specifically, unblinded biostatisticians generated the randomisation schedule for the IWRS, which linked sequential patient randomisation numbers to treatment codes. Randomisation was balanced by permuted blocks (block size will be revealed after the final database lock). The first randomisation process, performed on day 1, was stratified by day 1 body weight (<100 kg vs ≥100 kg), Disease Activity Score in 28 joints (DAS28; erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR)) at screening (>5.1 vs ≤5.1) and prior use of biologics approved for RA treatment (yes vs no). The second randomisation process, performed prior to dosing at week 24, was stratified by DAS28-ESR at week 20 (<2.6 vs ≥2.6).

At drug administration visits every 4 weeks, patients received 8 mg/kg of study drug (CT-P47 or r-TCZ) diluted with sodium chloride as an intravenous infusion over 1 hour (±15 min), administered by unblinded study centre staff. Methotrexate (MTX; between 10 and 25 mg/week; oral or parenteral (intramuscular or SC) dose; dose and route maintained throughout) and folic acid (≥5 mg/week; oral dose) were coadministered. If patients developed laboratory abnormalities in liver enzymes (aspartate aminotransferase and/or alanine aminotransferase), absolute neutrophil count, or platelet count, or developed a serious infection, opportunistic infection, or sepsis, the dose of the study drug and/or MTX was modified or stopped according to the study protocol (per r-TCZ label9 10) until the clinical situation had been evaluated and controlled. Following database lock for data up to week 32, predefined unblinded sponsor and contract research organisation teams were unblinded for reporting purposes. Other predefined personnel, investigators and patients will remain blinded until all patients have completed the study and the database has been finalised for study termination.

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki19 and Good Clinical Practice Guideline.20 All national, state, and local laws or regulations were followed. Prior to study initiation, the study protocol, informed consent form, advertisements used for the recruitment of participants, and any other written materials provided to participants were approved by the institutional review boards (online supplemental table S1). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to enrolment. This study was registered with ClinicalTrials.gov [NCT05489224]. 17

Participants

Full eligibility criteria are in online supplemental methods. Patients were adults (aged 18–75 years) who had a diagnosis of RA according to the 2010 American College of Rheumatology (ACR)/European Alliance of Associations for Rheumatology (EULAR) classification criteria21 for ≥24 weeks prior to the first administration of the study drug (day 1). Patients had moderate-to-severe disease activity, defined by the presence of ≥6 swollen joints (of 66 assessed), ≥6 tender joints (of 68 assessed), either an ESR of ≥28 mm/hour or a serum C reactive protein (CRP) concentration of ≥1.0 mg/dL (≥10 mg/L) and a DAS28 (ESR or CRP) of ≥3.2 at screening and had received MTX (oral or parenteral) for ≥12 weeks and been on a stable dose of MTX between 10 and 25 mg/week and route of administration for ≥8 weeks prior to the first administration of the study drug (day 1). Key exclusion criteria included patients who had previously received investigational or licensed targeted synthetic DMARD(s) for the treatment of RA and/or an IL-6 inhibitor for any purposes; had previously received >1 biological agent approved for the treatment of RA; and/or had allergies to any of the excipients of study drug or any other murine and human proteins, or a hypersensitivity to immunoglobulin products.

Patient and public involvement

Patients or the public were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination plans of this research.

Study endpoints

This study had dual primary efficacy endpoints, (for regulatory purposes), which were the mean change from baseline in DAS28-ESR at week 12 and at week 24. Predefined additional efficacy endpoints included hybrid ACR score; change from baseline in ESR, CRP, Simplified Disease Activity Index (SDAI) and Clinical Disease Activity Index (CDAI) as well as individual components of the SDAI, CDAI and DAS28; the proportion of patients achieving clinical response according to 20%, 50% or 70% improvement by ACR criteria (ACR20, ACR50 and ACR70, respectively), ACR/EULAR remission (original Boolean-based remission definition),22 and EULAR (ESR) response and 36-item Short Form Health Survey physical and mental component scores. PKs (serum TCZ concentration) and overall safety, including immunogenicity, were also evaluated.

Post hoc efficacy endpoints included DAS28-ESR by prior biological use, SDAI and CDAI low disease activity (LDA) and remission rates (ACR/EULAR index-based remission definition) and ACR/EULAR remission (Boolean 2.0).23

Study assessments

The time points for study assessments are detailed in online supplemental table S2. Efficacy assessment procedures were performed at the study centre prior to study drug administration. An independent, blinded joint count assessor was allocated to each study centre.

Blood samples for PK analysis were collected before study drug administration at all PK sampling time points up to and including week 48. PK samples at the EOS visit were obtained anytime during the day. Serum TCZ concentrations were determined using an electrochemiluminescence (ECL) method (Meso Scale Discovery, Meso Scale Diagnostics, Rockville, Maryland, USA). Concentrations below the lower limit of quantification (BLQ; 80 ng/mL) that occurred prior to first study drug administration were treated as zero; all other BLQ values were treated as missing for the summary of serum concentration data.

Safety assessments performed throughout included treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs), treatment-emergent serious adverse events (TESAEs), TEAEs of special interest (TEAESIs), clinical laboratory testing, soluble (s) IL-6R and immunogenicity. TEAESIs were infection, hypersensitivity including anaphylaxis, hepatic event, haemorrhage, gastrointestinal perforation, malignancy and demyelinating disorder. TEAEs were coded to system organ class and preferred term according to the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (V.26.0) and graded for intensity according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE; V.5).

Blood samples for immunogenicity assessments were collected before study drug administration at all immunogenicity sampling time points up to and including week 48. Antidrug antibodies (ADAs) were assessed using a validated ECL bridging assay (sensitivity 3.021 ng/mL) that employed a three-tiered approach (screening, confirmatory and titre). Confirmed ADA-positive samples were further analysed for titre determination using a titre assay that serially dilutes samples. The neutralising activity (ie, neutralising antibodies; Nabs) of ADA-positive samples was characterised using a validated ECL assay with affinity capture elution (sensitivity 148.787 ng/mL).

Statistical analyses

Statistical analyses were performed by using SAS software (V.9.4; SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA). A sample size of 336 patients (168 per treatment group) was needed to demonstrate equivalence of the primary efficacy endpoints at week 12 and week 24. This calculation was based on two sets of statistical assumptions: an equivalence margin of −0.6 to 0.6 using a two one-sided 2.5% significance level of an equivalence test would provide ≥90% statistical power (week 12), and an asymmetric equivalence margin of −0.6 to 0.5 using a two one-sided 5% significance level of an equivalence test would provide ≥85% statistical power (week 24). To account for an expected 25% drop-out rate, 448 participants (224 per treatment group) were required to be randomised.

Analysis sets are described in online supplemental methods. The intention-to-treat (ITT) set included all patients randomly assigned to receive the study drug (CT-P47 or r-TCZ) on day 1 (week 0); the ITT-TP2 subset included all patients in the ITT set who were randomly assigned to receive the study drug prior to dosing at week 24.

The week 12 primary efficacy endpoint was analysed in the ITT set using an analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) model, with treatment as a fixed effect and day 1 body weight (<100 kg vs ≥100 kg), baseline DAS28-ESR score and prior biological use approved for RA treatment (yes vs no) as covariates. Equivalence at week 12 was determined if the 95% CIs for the treatment difference were entirely contained within the −0.6 to 0.6 equivalence margin. The week 24 primary efficacy endpoint was analysed in the ITT set using an ANCOVA model with multiple imputation (MI) under the missing at random (MAR) assumption for handling missing data. Specifically, missing values were imputed using a regression method, with treatment, day 1 body weight (<100 kg vs ≥100 kg), baseline DAS28-ESR score and prior biological use approved for RA treatment (yes vs no) as covariates. 100 imputed datasets were created and subsequently analysed using an ANCOVA model with treatment as a fixed effect and day 1 body weight (<100 kg vs ≥100 kg), baseline DAS28-ESR score and prior biological use approved for RA treatment (yes vs no) as covariates. Results were then pooled using Rubin’s method.24 Equivalence at week 24 was determined if the 90% CIs for the treatment difference were entirely contained within the −0.6 to 0.5 asymmetric equivalence margin. Least-squares mean (LSM) and SE, and estimates of treatment difference and corresponding CIs, are presented.

Supportive analyses for the primary efficacy endpoint at week 12 and week 24 were conducted in the per-protocol (PP) set. MI cannot be performed in the PP set due to its definition; therefore, supportive analyses used ANCOVA without MI. A sensitivity analysis evaluating DAS28-ESR at week 12 was also conducted on the ITT set using an ANCOVA model with MI under the MAR assumption for handling missing data.

Results

Participants

The first patient was randomised on 14 September 2022; the last patient’s week 32 visit was 29 June 2023. Patient disposition is presented in figure 2. In TP1, a total of 471 patients were randomly assigned to receive CT-P47 (n=234) or r-TCZ (n=237). Of these, 9 (3.8%) and 18 (7.6%) patients in the CT-P47 and r-TCZ groups, respectively, discontinued the study drug. Of the 27 (5.7%) patients in total who discontinued study treatment, 17 (3.6%) withdrew from the study (CT-P47: 8 (3.4%) patients; r-TCZ: 9 (3.8%) patients). In TP2, 444 patients were included in the second randomisation process prior to dosing at week 24 (225 CT-P47 maintenance; 109 r-TCZ maintenance; 110 CT-P47 switched). Up to week 32, eight (1.8%) patients discontinued study treatment (CT-P47 maintenance: three (1.3%) patients; r-TCZ maintenance: two (1.8%) patients; CT-P47 switched: three (2.7%) patients), of which three (0.7%) patients withdrew from the study (one patient each in the CT-P47 maintenance (0.4%), r-TCZ maintenance (0.9%) and CT-P47 switched (0.9%) groups).

Before week 24, two (0.9%) and two (0.8%) patients received prohibited therapy in the CT-P47 and r-TCZ groups, respectively, constituting major protocol deviations with respect to the DAS28-ESR at week 24 endpoint. There were no major protocol deviations with respect to the DAS28-ESR at week 12 endpoint. Correspondingly, two (0.9%) and one (0.4%) patients violated prohibited medicine criteria before week 24, while one (0.4%) patient in the r-TCZ group underwent joint therapy before week 24. These patients were excluded from the PP set.

Patient demographics and baseline characteristics were comparable between groups (table 1 for ITT set; online supplemental table S3 for ITT-TP2 subset). The median (range) age of patients was 56.0 (20–73) and 56.5 (20–73) years, for the ITT set and ITT-TP2 subset, respectively. The majority of patients were female, had a body weight of <100 kg, a high DAS28-ESR (>5.1) and had not received prior biologics for RA treatment. Mean (SD) doses (mg/week) of MTX at baseline were comparable between groups (CT-P47: 19.3 (4.87); r-TCZ: 19.3 (4.67)). In the CT-P47 and r-TCZ groups, patients who had received prior biologics had a marginally lower DAS28-ESR than those who had not (online supplemental table S4).

Efficacy

Equivalence between CT-P47 and r-TCZ was demonstrated for the primary efficacy endpoints of LSM change from baseline in DAS28-ESR at week 12 (ANCOVA; treatment difference (ITT set) −0.01 (95% CI −0.26, 0.24)) and week 24 (ANCOVA with MI; treatment difference (ITT set) −0.10 (90% CI −0.30, 0.10)), as the 95% and 90% CIs for the treatment differences at week 12 and week 24, respectively, were entirely contained within the predefined equivalence margins (table 2). A post hoc analysis showed that improvements in DAS28-ESR from baseline to week 12 and week 24 were similar between the CT-P47 and r-TCZ groups regardless of prior biological use, and in each treatment group, the degree of improvement was similar between the subgroups of patients with or without prior biological use (online supplemental table S4).

Improvements in all secondary efficacy endpoints were observed during TP1 and were similar between the CT-P47 and r-TCZ groups in the ITT set (table 3 and figure 3; online supplemental figure S1), including mean change from baseline in ESR, CRP, SDAI and CDAI; ACR20, ACR50 and ACR70 response rates; SDAI LDA and remission rates; CDAI LDA and remission rates; EULAR response and ACR/EULAR remission rates. Secondary efficacy endpoints were also similar among the CT-P47 maintenance, r-TCZ maintenance and CT-P47 switched groups during TP2 up to week 32 in the ITT-TP2 subset.

Pharmacokinetics

Serum concentrations of TCZ were comparable between the CT-P47 and r-TCZ groups during TP1. In TP2 up to week 32, serum concentrations of TCZ were generally comparable among CT-P47 maintenance, r-TCZ maintenance and CT-P47 switched groups (online supplemental table S5).

Safety and immunogenicity

The mean (SD) total number of doses and total amount of study drug received were comparable between the CT-P47 (5.8 (0.70); 3406.3 (838.38), respectively) and r-TCZ (5.7 (0.78); 3290.3 (867.11), respectively) groups in TP1 and among the CT-P47 maintenance (2.8 (0.45); 1570.2 (510.82), respectively), r-TCZ maintenance (2.8 (0.45); 1522.7 (503.03), respectively) and CT-P47 switched (2.8 (0.56); 1565.1 (512.07), respectively) groups in TP2.

TESAEs were reported for 10 (4.3%) and nine (3.8%) patients in the CT-P47 and r-TCZ groups, respectively, in TP1, and four (1.8%), three (2.8%) and two (1.8%) patients in the CT-P47 maintenance, r-TCZ maintenance and CT-P47 switched groups, respectively, in TP2 (table 4). A numerically slightly higher proportion of patients in the r-TCZ group experienced TEAEs leading to study drug discontinuation in TP1 (CT-P47: three (1.3%) patients; r-TCZ: 12 (5.1%) patients). TEAEs leading to study drug discontinuation were reported for four (1.8%), two (1.8%) and zero patients in the CT-P47 maintenance, r-TCZ maintenance and CT-P47 switched groups, respectively, in TP2.

One (0.4%) patient in the CT-P47 maintenance group died following a TESAE of peritonitis following a scheduled gastroscopy during TP2, which was classified as a TEAESI of infection and of gastrointestinal perforation and was evaluated as unrelated to the study drug by the investigator.

Infections were the most commonly reported TEAESI in the overall study period (table 4). There was a numerically slightly higher incidence of infection in the CT-P47 group compared with the r-TCZ group in TP1 (CT-P47: 110 (47.0%) patients; r-TCZ: 84 (35.4%) patients); while during TP2, there was a similar frequency of infection reported between groups (36 (16.0%), 18 (16.5%) and 15 (13.6%) patients in the CT-P47 maintenance, r-TCZ maintenance, and CT-P47 switched groups, respectively). In TP1, five (1.1%) patients experienced TESAEs of infection (CT-P47: three (1.3%) patients; r-TCZ: 2 (0.8%) patients), of which one event was considered to be related to the study drug (grade 3 erysipelas in the r-TCZ group).

10 (2.1%) patients reported at least one TEAESI classified as hypersensitivity in TP1 (CT-P47: three (1.3%) patients; r-TCZ: seven (3.0%) patients). The most frequently reported sign or symptom of hypersensitivity reactions in all groups was a rash, reported in two (0.9%) and five (2.1%) patients in the CT-P47 and r-TCZ groups, respectively. No patients experienced hypersensitivity during TP2. All hypersensitivity reactions were grades 1–2 in intensity, except for one grade 3 event in the r-TCZ group which was classified as anaphylaxis according to Sampson’s criteria.25 The patient experienced a rash, hypotension and a TEAE of ECG QT prolonged, after week 8 study drug administration; the patient discontinued the study drug due to this event.

TEAESIs classified as hepatic events were reported by 133 (28.2%) patients in TP1 (CT-P47: 60 (25.6%) patients; r-TCZ: 73 (30.8%) patients) and 51 (11.5%) patients in TP2 (27 (12%), 12 (11.0%) and 12 (10.9%) patients in the CT-P47 maintenance, r-TCZ maintenance and CT-P47 switched groups, respectively). Up to week 32, the most frequently reported hepatic event in all groups was increased alanine aminotransferase.

Nine (1.9%) patients reported at least one TEAESI classified as haemorrhage in TP1 (CT-P47: three (1.3%) patients; r-TCZ: six (2.5%) patients) and three patients in TP2 (one (0.4%) and two (1.8%) patients in the CT-P47 maintenance and CT-P47 switched groups, respectively, and none in the r-TCZ maintenance group). All events were considered as not medically significant bleeding events except for one grade 3 TEAE of uterine haemorrhage reported in the CT-P47 switched group.

One (0.4%) patient in the r-TCZ group experienced a TEAESI classified as malignancy (pancreatic neoplasm) during TP1 which was considered unrelated to the study drug by the investigator.

No patients experienced TEAESIs of demyelinating disorder.

Change from baseline of sIL-6R was comparable between groups (online supplemental table S6). Laboratory variables of the most severe CTCAE grading (grade ≥3) are summarised in online supplemental table S7.

During both TPs, immunogenicity findings were generally comparable between groups (online supplemental table S8). In TP1, six (2.7%) and four (1.8%) patients had a positive conversion in ADAs in the CT-P47 and r-TCZ groups, respectively; correspondingly, 10 (4.3%) and eight (3.4%) patients had a positive conversion in NAbs. In TP2, one (0.5%) and three (3.1%) patients had a positive conversion in ADAs in the CT-P47 maintenance and r-TCZ maintenance groups, respectively; correspondingly, one (0.5%) and one (1.0%) patients had a positive conversion in NAbs. No patients who switched to CT-P47 had a positive conversion in ADAs or NAbs. Prior to dosing at week 32, five (2.2%), three (2.8%) and four (3.6%) patients in the CT-P47 maintenance, r-TCZ maintenance and CT-P47 switched groups, respectively, had a positive ADA result.

Post hoc analysis demonstrated that 20 (4.2%) and 19 (4.0%) patients had ≥1 positive ADA or NAb result, respectively, during TP1, while 12 (2.7%) and five (1.1%) had at least one positive ADA or NAb result, respectively, during TP2. Moreover, post hoc analysis showed similar mean changes from baseline in DAS28-ESR at week 24 between treatment groups in patients with and without ADAs, and between patients with and without ADAs in each group (online supplemental table S9). Mean serum TCZ concentrations were generally similar between groups in patients with and without ADAs; concentrations were consistently slightly higher in patients negative for ADAs relative to those positive for ADAs in each group (online supplemental figure S2).

Discussion

The current study demonstrated equivalence of the primary efficacy endpoints, mean change from baseline in DAS28-ESR at week 12 and week 24, between CT-P47 and r-TCZ in patients with moderate-to-severe active RA. Moreover, secondary efficacy endpoints, PK results and safety were similar between groups up to week 32, including after switching from r-TCZ to CT-P47 at week 24. Indeed, response curves observed with the two compounds were essentially superimposable.

While efficacy endpoints and their individual components were comparable between the treatment groups, ACR response rates were similar or higher than those reported in the original trials of r-TCZ.1026,31 This observation of lower ACR response rates in placebo-controlled trials was also demonstrated in a systematic analysis of trials in RA, which suggested the nocebo effect (knowledge about the possibility of receiving placebo) may be responsible for smaller effect sizes of an active drug when there is a placebo control compared with the same drug in a head-to-head setting.32 Importantly, improvements in secondary efficacy endpoints observed in TP1 were maintained or continued to improve in TP2 and were consistent between patients who received continued maintenance treatment and those who switched to CT-P47.

Serum concentrations of TCZ were similar between groups and showed a similar increase up to week 32. Moreover, on randomised switching from r-TCZ to CT-P47, the serum concentration of TCZ was fully maintained compared with the preceding phase and was similar to that achieved with continued r-TCZ.

The study drugs were well tolerated, in accordance with the known safety profile of r-TCZ,33 and the overall safety profiles of CT-P47 and r-TCZ were generally similar. The majority of TEAEs were grades 1–2 in intensity and overall, infections were the most frequently reported TEAESI, congruous with studies CT-P47 1.118 and 1.3.34 Although there was a numerical difference between groups in the incidence of infection in TP1, no increasing trend of infection was observed after a single transition between treatments in TP2, in which a similar frequency of infection was reported between groups. Moreover, there were no new infections compared with the known safety profile of r-TCZ9 10 and most infections in TP1 resolved without sequelae within 30 days (mean: 10.2 days). Furthermore, the most frequently reported infections were upper respiratory tract infection, pharyngitis and tonsillitis—most of which are listed as very common (≥1/10) or common (≥1/100 to <1/10) adverse drug reactions in the r-TCZ label.9 10 The proportions of patients with positive ADA results were generally comparable across groups, including after switching to CT-P47 from r-TCZ.

With their increased affordability, biosimilars can facilitate access to biologics in individuals who may not otherwise have received such drugs, thereby enabling better outcomes for patients.35 Biosimilars to several biological DMARDs are currently available.36 As a candidate TCZ biosimilar, CT-P47 has potential to improve the management of patients with RA. Given that TCZ is also approved for disorders other than RA, extrapolation of indications for TCZ biosimilars can broaden their use across several diseases.11,14 Subsequent to the approval of the intravenous formulation of r-TCZ, studies showing similar efficacy and safety supported the approval of an SC formulation.37 38 While SC r-TCZ may be widely used, the potential for cost saving with an intravenous formulation of a TCZ biosimilar is equally relevant.36 When treating RA, though TCZ may be a more effective monotherapy than other biologics,39 patients in the current study were receiving concomitant MTX, in line with previous TCZ trials,40 studies that favoured combining TCZ with MTX41,43 and management recommendations.44

This study had limitations. All patients included in this study were white and all study centres were located in Poland, which may limit the generalisability of findings to other populations and regions. Understanding of the impact of race on the efficacy of r-TCZ is limited, owing to the predominant enrolment of white patients in existing studies.45 For many years, DAS28 has been the standard instrument used to measure disease activity in patients with RA46; nevertheless, due to its subjective nature, interobserver variability can arise.47 To reduce interobserver variability, where possible, joint count assessments were performed independently by the same person at each study centre throughout the study period. Another limitation is that DAS28-ESR is heavily weighted to ESR, and anti-IL-6 therapies such as TCZ can reduce ESR independently of clinical improvement; therefore, this instrument may not be appropriate for drugs interfering with the IL-6 pathway.48 49 However, this is not a major issue for this study as TCZ was used in both treatment arms. Furthermore, results using CDAI, which does not include an acute phase reactant measure, also showed similarity between the treatment groups. Finally, while 8 weeks of single transition data are reported here, further follow-up is ongoing and will be reported in the future. Regardless of the stated limitations, assessing the comparability of efficacy, safety and immunogenicity between CT-P47 and r-TCZ was the main purpose of this study and was clearly shown across all outcomes. As well as being the first phase III study to compare these two treatments, the study also presents important efficacy and safety data concerning the switch from r-TCZ to CT-P47.

Conclusions

The current study demonstrated efficacy equivalence of CT-P47 and r-TCZ in patients with moderate-to-severe active RA. Additionally, secondary efficacy endpoints, PK, safety and immunogenicity results were comparable between groups, including after the switch from r-TCZ to CT-P47. Together, these findings support the suitability of CT-P47 as a TCZ biosimilar for the management of patients with RA.

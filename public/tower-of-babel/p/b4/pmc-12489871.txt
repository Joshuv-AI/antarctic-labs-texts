Deep-Learning–Based Analysis of Disease-Specific Structural Biomarkers on Retinal Sensitivity in Neovascular Age-Related Macular Degeneration
Authors: Gregor S. Reiter, Klaudia Birner, Johannes Schrittwieser, Laetitia Hinterhuber, Irene Steiner, Markus Gumpinger, Simon Schürer-Waldheim, Hrvoje Bogunovic, Ursula Schmidt-Erfurth
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo evaluate the impact of disease-specific biomarkers on pointwise sensitivity (PWS) in patients with active neovascular age-related macular degeneration (nAMD) using two microperimetry (MP) instruments.MethodsParticipants in this prospective cross-sectional study underwent imaging with a SPECTRALIS HRA+OCT and MP with the photopic MP-3 (NIDEK) and mesopic MAIA (CenterVue). Pigment epithelium detachment (PED), intraretinal and subretinal fluid (IRF, SRF), ellipsoid zone loss (EZL), and subretinal hyperreflective material (SHRM) were quantified using deep learning (DL). DL enabled co-registration between the MP and optical coherence tomography (OCT). Univariate and multivariable mixed-effects models using variable selection including interaction terms were used to assess the effects of the biomarkers on PWS.ResultsTwenty eyes of 20 subjects (mean age, 76.0 years) were included. Sensitivity in the MAIA was lower (−2.87 dB; 95% confidence interval [CI], −3.15 to −2.59) than in the MP-3. Significant interactions between EZL and SRF, EZL and IRF, and between EZL and eccentricity were observed. EZL significantly reduced PWS (−1.30 dB; 95% CI, −2.08 to −0.51) at 0° eccentricity with SRF of 0.15 nL and absent IRF. IRF presence further decreased PWS by −2.57 dB (95% CI, −3.66 to −1.47). SRF negatively impacted PWS (−2.08 dB/nL; 95% CI, −2.53 to −1.64), but showed no association when EZL was present (+0.05 dB/nL; 95% CI, −0.81 to 0.92). SHRM presence and PED volume reduced PWS by −5.13 dB (95% CI, −5.83 to −4.43) and −0.71 dB/nL (95% CI, −0.92 to −0.50), respectively. Eccentricity only impaired function in the presence of EZL (−0.49 dB/°; 95% CI, −0.61 to −0.37).ConclusionsDL-quantified biomarkers mostly had a negative impact on PWS in nAMD, particularly IRF and SHRM presence. The above-mentioned biomarkers should be considered when managing patients with nAMD and using MP in trials and clinical settings.

Neovascular age-related macular degeneration (AMD) is a complication of AMD arising from the development of macular neovascularization (MNV) in the macular area.1 Exudation is responsible for fluid accumulation within different compartments in and under the retina. Most commonly fluid can accumulate as intraretinal fluid (IRF), subretinal fluid (SRF) or beneath the retinal pigment epithelium (RPE), forming pigment epithelium detachments (PEDs).1 These three compartments have been quantified in several studies with the help of deep learning (DL) algorithms.2 Further, exudation can also lead to the appearance of subretinal hyperreflective material (SHRM), a biomarker partly responsive to anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) treatment that may also lead to a higher risk of development of fibrosis, resulting in irreversible vision loss, especially if anti-VEGF treatment is not administered in a timely manner.3,4

Another path toward irreversible loss of vision is the development of macular atrophy.5 AMD in general is characterized by progressive degeneration of the choriocapillaris, photoreceptors, and the RPE.5 A method to assess the amount of developing atrophy on optical coherence tomography (OCT) is quantification of RPE loss, hypertransmission, and the ellipsoid zone (EZ). The EZ has been approved as an endpoint for drug evaluations in patients with geographic atrophy (GA) and offers benefits in translating macular function to the OCT morphology.6 Although the loss of EZ alone does not fulfill Classification of Atrophy Meetings (CAM) criteria for complete RPE and outer retinal atrophy (cRORA), it can detect early outer retinal damage and has been associated with functional impairment in both GA and intermediate AMD.5,7–9

In AMD, assessing function is conventionally performed by measuring best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA).10 However, BCVA mainly measures foveal function and central cone response under photopic conditions and lacks the ability to measure macular function in other areas of interest or the visual field. BCVA can therefore be unaffected if low amounts of SRF or PED are present or if an atrophic lesion develops extrafoveally. Another method to assess retinal function is microperimetry (MP).11 MP allows evaluation of retinal function by projecting light stimuli onto the retina while assessing the retinal location of the stimuli. Thus, specific areas of the retina can be targeted and tested individually, resulting in pointwise sensitivity (PWS) values. The use of MP has been increasing over the last years, with the main interest in GA and intermediate AMD (iAMD).8,12,13 However, neovascular AMD (nAMD) presents with similar degeneration of the retina in addition to unique fluid accumulations that are well known to impact visual function.14,15 The aim of our study was to assess PWS using two different MP devices in patients with nAMD by using DL-quantified biomarkers characteristic for nAMD, including IRF, SRF, PED, SHRM and EZ loss (EZL), to assess retinal morphology and linking structure to function.

Methods

This study was conducted in accordance with the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Medical University of Vienna. Prior to the initiation of any study procedure, written informed consent was obtained from all participants. All participants were at least 50 years of age and had been diagnosed with active MNV secondary to AMD. The presence of concurrent macular hemorrhage and MNV lesions of all sizes was deemed acceptable if BCVA was impaired by no more than 0.5 logarithm of the minimum angle of resolution (logMAR) to ensure adequate fixation stability. Participants were recruited from the outpatient clinic of the Department of Ophthalmology at the Medical University of Vienna. Patients were excluded if they showed BCVA worse than 0.5 logMAR, cRORA based on CAM criteria,5 as well as evidence of neurodegenerative retinal diseases or retinal vasculopathies, such as diabetic retinopathy, affecting the study eye. Furthermore, patients with glaucoma or a cup-to-disc ratio of 0.7 in the study eye, or a history of retinal vein occlusion in the study or the fellow eye, were excluded. If both eyes were eligible, the eye with the better image quality on the OCT was selected.

Testing Protocol

The examination protocol was described in detail by Coulibaly et al.16 Briefly, pupil dilation was performed with 0.5% tropicamide. Spectral-domain optical coherence tomography (SD-OCT) scans (SPECTRALIS HRA+OCT, Heidelberg Engineering, Heidelberg, Germany), with a foveal-centered macular cube (20° × 20°) with a density of 97 B-scans, were acquired from both eyes. OCT angiography (PLEX Elite 9000; Carl Zeiss Meditec, Dublin, CA, USA) was used to confirm MNV in the study eye. MNV activity was assessed using OCT to observe dynamic changes in fluid compartments from previous visits or the onset of new symptoms in treatment-naïve patients. Each patient underwent two consecutive runs with the MP-3 (NIDEK Co., Ltd., Aichi, Japan) and the MAIA (CenterVue/iCare, SpA, Padova, Italy), resulting in a total of four examinations per patient. To reduce the bias due to fatigue, the order of the devices was randomized, and a 10-minute break was provided between each examination. The standard settings of each device were used for all examinations, as the manufacturers have different standard settings for their respective devices. This resulted in photopic conditions in the MP-3 and a mesopic test in the MAIA. The default background illumination for the MP-3 is 31.4 apostilbs (asb; 10 cd/m2), and for the MAIA it is 4 asb (1.27 cd/m2). The same self-developed MP testing grid comprised of 45 stimuli centered around the fovea in accordance with the Early Treatment Diabetic Retinopathy Study (ETDRS) grid was applied to all patients in all examinations and has been published previously12 In all examinations, the Goldmann III stimulus size with a duration of 200 ms was used for retinal sensitivity assessment.

Biomarker Segmentation and Quantification

The segmentations of IRF, SRF, and PED were performed automatically using the RetInSight Fluid Monitor (RetInSight Gmbh, Vienna, Austria). SHRM and EZL were pre-segmented using an in-house DL-based algorithm17 and subsequently corrected by two expert readers. Figure 1 shows the (DL-based) biomarker quantification. In cases of inter-reader disagreement, the cases were discussed in a group with a retinal specialist.

Co-Registration Between Microperimetry and OCT Volume

The OCT volumes were co-registered with the MP devices to ensure precise pointwise structure-to-function correlations. The co-registration process has been described in detail by Birner et al.8,18 In short, the near-infrared reflectance (NIR) image of the MAIA and the color fundus photography (CFP) of the MP-3 were co-registered on the NIR of the OCT using a DL-based algorithm based on a U-Net that enables vessel segmentation.19,20 Briefly, this segmentation algorithm identifies junctional points by using a Mask R-CNN with its architecture being able to find bifurcations, crossings, or branches of retinal blood vessels visible on the MAIA-NIR and MP-3-CFP and on the NIR of the SPECTRALIS OCT. By identifying at least three points, superimposing the NIR/CFP of the MP device onto the NIR OCT is possible. A human expert (SS-W) reviewed the automated registration process if the exact pixel positions matched on both images. In cases of misalignment, manual corrections were performed on both images by marking junctional points, and, based on the least square error method, a transformation matrix was calculated.8,18 As the NIR of the OCT is already aligned with the OCT scan provided by the Heidelberg SPECTRALIS software, no additional alignment is needed. Consequently, the MP stimulus grid is co-registered onto the OCT volumes. In addition, all DL-based segmentations performed previously are already aligned with the OCT B-scans within our in-house developed image annotation tool; no additional alignment between the MP stimulus and the segmentations is necessary. As one MP stimulus has a radius of 70 µm, all quantified biomarkers within this radius of the specific stimulus were included in the pointwise structure/function analysis.

Statistical Analysis

Metric variables are summarized as mean ± standard deviation, if not stated otherwise. Qualitative variables are reported as absolute frequency and percentage.

The primary endpoint of this study is PWS in decibels (dB). The independent variables are the device type (MP-3; MAIA); examination run per device (run1; run2); age in years; eccentricity in degrees from the foveal center; IRF presence (dichotomous); SHRM presence (dichotomous); PED volume in nanoliters (nL); SRF volume (nL); presence of EZ loss (dichotomous); and interactions between EZ loss and eccentricity, IRF, PED and SRF, as well as interactions between SRF and SHRM. For statistical analyses, IRF and SHRM were classified as present/absent due to many values equal to zero. EZ-loss was also dichotomized because 76.2% of observations were zero, indicating the presence of EZ in those 76.2%, and the remaining data were left-skewed.

Variable selection was done using the R function dredge (R package MuMIn; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).21 The global model was a mixed model (function lme, R package nlme)22 fitted by maximum likelihood with the patient as the random factor and the independent variables listed above, whereby the Bayesian information criterion (BIC) criterion was used. Due to a small number of missing values, 3560 observations out of 3600 observations were included in the model. The selected variables were then included in a mixed model with patient as the random factor using restricted maximum likelihood (REML) estimation. The coefficient of determination (marginal and conditional) was calculated using the R package MuMIn (R function r.squaredGLMM). For the selected variables (without interaction terms), the variance inflation factor was calculated (R function vif, R package performance). The largest value was equal to 1.6; hence, no signs of multicollinearity were observed. Statistical analyses were carried out with R 4.3.2. The significance level was set to alpha = 0.05. The analyses and hence the interpretation of the P values are exploratory.

Results

In this study, 20 eyes of 20 individuals with a mean age of 76 ± 7.9 years were included. Eight were male (40%), and 12 were female (60%). Six patients were treatment naïve (30%), whereas 14 patients were previously treated with anti-VEGF (70%). The pretreated patients received aflibercept 2 mg with a median treatment interval of 8 (4–9) weeks. They had a history of anti-VEGF treatment for a mean duration of 160 ± 177 weeks. All patients had active MNV with fluid present at the visit. Patients underwent four consecutive test runs with a total of 900 stimuli per run for a total of 3600 included microperimetry stimuli. Disease-specific biomarker analysis was possible for 3560 stimuli on 1940 B-scans. SHRM and Fluid Volumes

The presence of SHRM and PED volume was found to result in a notable reduction in sensitivity, with decreases of −5.13 dB (95% CI, −5.83 to −4.43; P < 0.0001) and −0.71 dB/nL (95% CI, −0.92 to −0.50; P < 0.0001), respectively. The presence of IRF significantly reduced retinal sensitivity by −3.02 dB (95% CI, −3.91 to −2.14) in points without EZL. When EZL was present in addition to IRF, PWS was further reduced by −2.57 dB (95% CI, −3.66 to −1.47), resulting in a total estimated functional loss of −5.59 dB (95% CI, −6.3 to −4.88) compared to points with IRF present but intact EZ (Fig. 2). Although SRF showed a negative effect on visual function when EZ was intact (−2.08 dB/nL; 95% CI, −2.53 to −1.64; P < 0.0001), this effect was not apparent when EZL was present (+0.05 dB/nL; 95% CI, −0.81 to 0.92; P = 0.91), resulting in a difference of slopes of +2.13 dB/nL (95% CI, 1.18–3.09), indicating a significant moderation in the negative effect of SRF when EZL was present

Eccentricity

Eccentricity (R) did not significantly affect sensitivity (−0.041 dB/°; 95% CI, −0.11 to 0.027; P = 0.24) in areas with intact EZ. However, the observed mean sensitivity loss was more than 10 times greater in the presence of concomitant EZL (−0.49 dB/°; 95% CI, −0.61 to −0.37; P < 0.0001), resulting in a difference between slopes of −0.45 dB/° of eccentricity (95% CI, −0.58 to −0.31). The negative association between eccentricity and PWS was greatly aggravated in the presence of EZL and can be seen in Figure 3.

Interaction plot of the presence of EZL and the eccentricity (in degrees) with PWS (in dB) as the dependent variable. Note the slight decrease in retinal sensitivity with degrees of eccentricity with intact photoreceptor layer (–0.04 dB/°; P = 0.23) and the more than 10-fold decline of PWS per degree of eccentricity when EZ integrity was disrupted (–0.49 dB/°; P < 0.0001).

Correlation of Independent Parameters

EZL showed a moderate correlation with PED (rs = 0.51) and weak correlations with IRF (rs = 0.33) and SRF (rs = 0.04). Eccentricity correlated moderately with PED (rs = −0.51) and weakly with SRF (rs = −0.34) and IRF (rs = −0.22). Weak correlations were found between IRF and PED (rs = 0.33) and SRF (rs = −0.08). No signs of multicollinearity were noted between independent parameters.

Discussion

Microperimetry has become a valuable tool in the evaluation of retinal diseases, especially when BCVA does not reflect the severity of the disease in its entirety.11 In neovascular AMD, BCVA is usually a more accurate measure of disease activity and has been used in all major clinical trials.10,23 However, disease activity based on exudation and visual function is mainly dependent on the amount of IRF in the foveal center and does not reflect the overall severity, which includes SRF or PED.24,25 In addition, subclinical markers for the development of atrophy and fibrosis in nAMD can provide further information about the risk of irreversible vision loss.26 In nAMD, SHRM has become an important imaging marker for the development of fibrosis. Although SHRM may serve as risk factor for the development of fibrosis and macular atrophy, it is not always associated with its onset.3,27 EZL, more recently the focus of attention in non-nAMD, has been described as an early marker of irreversible outer retinal atrophy.28,29 Furthermore, it can be considered as another precursor to the development of fibrosis and therefore represents photoreceptor degeneration in general rather than being specific to atrophy or fibrosis.30

In our study, we investigated several OCT biomarkers of neovascular AMD for their effect on PWS measured by MP. We found that all fluid compartments had a significant negative effect on PWS. Furthermore, the presence of EZL and SHRM also showed a significant decrease in PWS. Although all markers were quantitatively identified, IRF, EZL, and SHRM had to be dichotomized to allow for robust statistical modeling due to the lower number of points with a value > 0. Because SRF and PED are more prevalent on the macula, a continuous result could be calculated for these with a better representation of the original quantitative aim of this paper. Interestingly, EZL in addition to IRF presence further worsened PWS. SRF was found to have a significant negative impact on retinal sensitivity; however, the presence of EZL modified the effect of SRF, attenuating it. This could be due to a floor effect or limited residual function, which could explain the change to a positive estimate in the presence of EZL. This adds to the ongoing controversy about the effect of SRF on function.31–33 The prognostic significance of residual fluid is complex and context dependent. It is influenced by MNV subtype and the presence of atrophy or fibrosis. Type 1 MNV, which is characterized by sub-RPE neovascularization, might preserve the choriocapillaris and provide support to the RPE. This can potentially delay the development of atrophy.34 Minimal SRF associated with type 1 MNV may offer protection against macular atrophy, which must be confirmed with longitudinal data.

In addition, we showed in the multivariable model that the PWS decreased with increasing eccentricity by 0.041 dB/° if the EZ was intact. This has already been shown in a healthy cohort.18 However, if EZL was also present, this effect was 0.49 dB/°, which is nearly 12 times greater. This effect may be due, on one hand, to the distribution of cones, which has the highest concentration in the fovea and decreases in density toward the periphery.35 On the other hand, if the integrity of the EZ, the part of the photoreceptors where the mitochondria are located,36 is compromised, retinal sensitivity decreases. The decrease in cone density and functional impairment due to the disorganization of the energy-supplying parts of the photoreceptors may have a synergistic negative effect, explaining the steep slope of the interaction between EZL and eccentricity.

Unsurprisingly, the highest estimates of worsening PWS were obtained with the combination of IRF presence and EZL, which resulted in a decrease of −5.59 dB, followed by the estimate of SHRM presence, which was −5.13 dB. IRF has been shown to have the most consistent findings regarding retinal function, which is undoubtedly negative.14 IRF is dominant in type III MNV, which is associated with the development of atrophy.37 These results agree with what we have found, which helps to bridge this gap. SHRM is associated with the development of fibrosis, which is particularly relevant in type II MNV. The effect of SRF is controversial and may depend on the underlying MNV. Reports have shown that type I MNV, which is dominantly associated with SRF, may develop a sub-RPE network that nourishes the RPE with less risk of developing atrophy.34 The interaction between SRF and EZL loss in our study may be related to this effect and the difference between type I and type II MNV. However, this was not tested within this sample and therefore remains speculative.

We demonstrated that sensitivity values derived from the MAIA device (mesopic conditions) were significantly lower than those tested by the MP-3 (photopic conditions). Recent analyses of healthy individuals and patients affected by iAMD or GA under the same testing conditions confirmed consistent significant differences of −3.4 dB in healthy individuals, and −3.6 and −3.4 dB in iAMD and GA patients, respectively.8,9,18 As this effect is also significant in healthy elderly individuals, as well as at other stages of AMD, we believe it to be a physiological phenomenon caused by different background illumination, rather than being a specific feature in nAMD. Our results are consistent with previous reports showing a negative impact of fluid compartments,38,39 although results that include quantitative measures of disease activity in neovascular AMD are still rare. Our investigation adds value by including EZ integrity, SHRM, eccentricity, and the interactions of these parameters, as well as the use of two MP devices in a randomized order. The following limitations have to be considered: (1) The sample size of 20 patients is rather small, and the cross-sectional design and inclusion of pretreated and treatment-naïve patients limit the interpretability of these findings. The analyses using the R function dredge() are highly exploratory and the variable selection aims to uncover associations that might be interesting to be analyzed in larger samples. (2) Due to the small sample size, no meaningful stratification of MNV subtypes or treatment status could be performed. (3) In this exploratory analysis, we included the sensitivity data of two devices with different background illumination (mesopic/photopic) in the same mixed-effects model. Furthermore, the continuous variables of IRF volume and SHRM and EZL area had to be dichotomized into “presence” and “absence” to enhance model stability. Therefore, continuous and dichotomized structural parameters are included in the final model. Additionally, although the number of included patients was 20, we analyzed many testing points within one patient. Our final model does account for dependencies in the data by including patient as random intercept but does not consider spatial autocorrelation of the 180 clustered MP stimuli (from four examination runs) in every eye. In our models, we assumed independence of all MP stimuli which does not reflect the true spatial dependence leading to the underestimation of standard errors and increasing the chance of type 1 errors. This has to be kept in mind, especially when interpreting the findings of the interaction terms. (4) No validation cohort and no inter-reader agreement for EZL and SHRM were included in this analysis. Although biomarkers specific to nAMD, such as exudates and EZL, have been evaluated, the effects of other pathognomonic AMD features, such as drusen, hyperreflective foci, RPE loss, fibrosis,40 and other frequently occurring biomarkers, including disorganization of the inner retinal layers, epiretinal membranes and exudates, have not been investigated.

In conclusion, we found that IRF, PED, and the presence of SHRM had a negative impact on PWS, whereas the impact of SRF was dependent on the presence and amount of other biomarkers. Interactions between fluid types and biomarkers often preceding atrophy are important morphologic correlates of retinal function. The combination of quantified OCT-based retinal structure with MP-assessed retinal function provides a comprehensive measure of disease severity and may be suitable for additional functional endpoints in clinical trials in nAMD; however, this should be confirmed in longitudinal studies involving larger numbers of patients.

A qualitative exploration of the experiences of shift workers participated in a low-calorie meal replacement dietary intervention
Authors: Piumika Sooriyaarachchi, Ranil Jayawardena, Toby Pavey, Neil A King
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesShift work is associated with various health challenges, including obesity and metabolic disturbances. To address these concerns, a randomised controlled clinical trial was conducted to evaluate the efficacy of a low-calorie meal replacement (MR) dietary intervention for dinner among shift workers with obesity. This study focuses on the qualitative aspects of this intervention, aiming to explore the experiences and perceptions of shift workers who participated in the trial.DesignFollowing the completion of the intervention, semi-structured face-to-face or telephone interviews were conducted with a purposive sample of trial participants. Data analysis was inductive, thematic using NVivo V.10 software.SettingThe intervention was conducted among shift workers with obesity in a private hospital in Sri Lanka and resulted in a modest decrease in weight.ParticipantsUsing purposeful maximum variation sampling, we recruited eight healthcare shift workers who took part in a weight loss intervention.ResultsAll participants expressed satisfaction with the MR meal for dinner, highlighting its positive impact on their well-being. Despite initial difficulties, strong determination and motivation by results supported adherence. Some participants suggested that the MR could be improved with sweeter taste and more flavour options. Few reported mild bloating at the beginning, but no serious side effects were noted. Participants felt lighter in their bodies due to weight loss. The method’s simplicity was the most frequently reported benefit, making it feasible even during busy night shifts. Overall, participants highly recommended the intervention to others in need.ConclusionParticipants experienced weight loss by replacing their dinner with the MR. This study offers valuable insights for tailoring future workplace-based dietary interventions for this vulnerable population.Trial registration numberACTRN12622000231741.Keywords: weight loss, meal replacement, shift work, experience, qualitative

Introduction

Shift work, characterised by irregular or unconventional working hours, poses unique challenges to maintaining a healthy diet and lifestyle due to the disruption of normal eating and sleeping patterns.1 As such, shift workers have been identified as a high-risk group for dietary imbalances and associated health complications, including obesity.2 Even though shift employees consume the same amount of energy per day as day workers,3 they are 23% more likely to become overweight or obese and 35% more likely to become abdominally obese.4 Additionally, shift workers are associated with higher adiposity.5 Being overweight and obese is related to many chronic conditions,6 and metabolic complications.7

In addition, studies indicate that night shift workers may experience circadian misalignment.8 This together with poor dietary choices and irregular mealtimes, may further impair the diurnal variation of energy metabolism, compounding the detrimental effects on their health in a complex and vicious cycle.9 Although dietary interventions can help manage these concerns, the unique circumstances of shift workers present specific challenges. Therefore, well-designed workplace nutrition interventions are urgently needed for shift employees with obesity. However, there are not many workplaces’ dietary interventions that solely focus on shift workers with obesity. One potential solution lies in the adoption of low-calorie meal replacements (MR), particularly for the main meal during a worker’s shift.

MR products are portion-controlled, prepackaged food, fortified with vitamins and minerals and contain a known energy and nutrition content, often coming in the form of a bar, or shake.10 MR are usually substituted for the main meals. It is anticipated that a low-calorie MR will have a negative energy balance because it contains 200 kcal, which is significantly fewer calories than the main meal. It has been reported that participants who consumed MR had higher opinions of convenience and dietary compliance than those who followed a traditional weight-loss plan.11

Although a MR plan may be an easy-to-follow method in comparison to other weight loss methods, the experiences and perspectives of individuals’ participating in such interventions remain underexplored. Despite the fact that there are not many qualitative MR-based studies,12 13 none of them have focused on shift employees. Especially the feasibility of dietary interventions intended to improve health outcomes in night shift workers has generally received very little attention in the literature. Therefore, this qualitative research aimed to explore the thoughts, feelings and experiences of a sample of shift workers who followed a MR-based weight loss intervention at the workplace. The findings of this research are expected to provide valuable insights into the viability of such dietary interventions for shift workers and contribute to creating more tailored and effective strategies for this unique population.

Methods

Participants and intervention description

The MR trial is a parallel, randomised controlled clinical trial conducted among shift workers with obesity at the Nawaloka Hospital PLC, Sri Lanka. This study aimed to determine the effectiveness of a low-calorie MR strategy compared with the habitual dinner meal on body weight and metabolic parameters. The eligible participants were rotating shift workers with obesity body mass index (BMI>25 kg/m2) who had been working shifts consistently for the previous year and who anticipated working at least three-night shifts per week during the study’s duration. During the study period of 8 weeks, the participants who were assigned to the intervention arm were given a low-calorie MR shake (~200 kcal) to consume instead for dinner and the control group participants were asked to continue their habitual dinner meals. A detailed description of this study protocol is published elsewhere,14 and following the completion of the trial by each participant, qualitative interviews were done. Informed written consent was obtained from all the participants before the enrolment. The clinical trial commenced in February 2022, with data collection for this study initiated in April 2022.

Sampling

Purposive sampling or invitation was employed to include participants, and data saturation served as a guiding principle for sample size determination. The participants in the intervention arm, who had been selected to follow the MR diet were invited to take part in this qualitative study. The participants were selected from a diverse range of age groups, genders, occupations and as well as varying levels of weight change observed during the programmes. They were enrolled in the study up to data saturation, which was determined when no new themes appeared in the transcripts.15 Each participant was questioned and recorded. Following transcription and analysis of the recordings, more participants were recruited to the study until no new themes were found. To ascertain the non-probabilistic sample size requirements, guest and colleagues showed that samples with as low as six individuals were sufficient to reach data saturation.15 Therefore, it was anticipated that the minimal sampling size needed to achieve data saturation in this study would be six participants.

Patient and public involvement

The design of this study was influenced by the outcomes of earlier qualitative research on weight loss experiences. Our interview approach for the current inquiry was developed with the help of the challenges mentioned in these earlier investigations. In addition, patients were not involved in the study’s design, recruitment or execution. The results will be shared with the study participants and public through this publication.

Data collection

The data were collected by the lead author (PS), a doctoral student who received training in qualitative data collection. Semi-structured face-to-face or telephone interviews were used to investigate participants’ experiences of taking part in the MR trial. To lessen bias, the interviewer encouraged the participants to be open and honest about their experience because that helps to determine whether the intervention is feasible. An interview topic guide was developed with open-ended questions based on qualitative literature on weight management (Online supplemental file 1). This included questions regarding the participants’ experience and views on MR intervention, the challenges faced and suggestions for how the intervention might be developed. Participants were encouraged to freely continue the conversation, and PS made sure that all relevant topics were covered. Each interview lasted around 25–30 min. All the interviews were audio recorded and transcribed verbatim. The audio files were saved to a password-protected external hard disc after the interviews were finished under the prefix of participant 1 (P1), participant 2 (P2), etc., to ensure participant confidentiality and anonymity. All the transcripts were rechecked by a coinvestigator for accuracy. The authors concluded that data saturation had been reached after six interviews, but two more additional interviews were conducted to ensure no new concepts were identified, which confirmed saturation.

Data translation

It was required to translate the interviews into English language because they were conducted in Sinhalese. A local research assistant who is knowledgeable with medical terminology was assigned to transcribe the interview recordings verbatim, and PS reviewed the transcripts for accuracy before forward translating them (Sinhalese to English). Next, a few complete transcripts of the translated interviews were chosen at random and backtranslated (from English to Sinhalese) by another bilingual research assistant. Then PS and the back-translator evaluated whether there had been any loss of equivalency or misinterpretation during the initial translation by comparing the back-translations with the original transcripts. Since no discrepancies were found, the authors concluded that additional translation verification procedures were not necessary.

Data analysis

A qualitative descriptive approach was used for data analysis. The Standards for Reporting Qualitative Research (SRQR) checklist was used as the reporting guideline for qualitative research (online supplemental file 2). The translated interview transcripts were thematically analysed using the qualitative data analysis software NVivo (release V.1.6.1 (1137)) to identify, analyse and report patterns and/or themes within the data. More specifically, a subjective and inductive approach was used as we did not employ or generate theory. First, all transcripts were examined, and initial codes were identified on a line-by-line and paragraph-by-paragraph basis using open coding. The first coding of the data set was done by PS, who became familiar with the interview data by translating, cross-checking transcripts with audio recordings and reading the transcripts. To identify any discrepancies and improve the coding framework, a second author independently examined the text and compared them with the initial codes. Codes were then compiled to establish further subthemes and illustrate broad themes. The mapping and interpretation of major themes and subthemes were completed after discussions and agreement among the authors. The Framework Matrices tool in Nvivo was then used to generate matrices where each row represented a participant, and each column indicated a theme or subtheme. The framework matrices were exported into Microsoft Excel for analysis.

Theme 1: reasons for participating in the weight loss trial

The participant’s need to lose weight was the key reason for taking part in this clinical trial. They were aware that they were overweight or had been recommended by a physician to lose weight to enhance their health.

P1: Being a part of this study was a wonderful chance for me because my doctor has previously encouraged me to lose weight to improve my overall health.

P6: I know I’m overweight and I want to reduce my weight. Therefore, this was a great opportunity for me.

P7: I'm grateful for the chance to take part in this study since I've always wished I could drop a few kilos because I know I'm overweight.

Further, participants mentioned that they had been trying to reduce weight previously, but that their efforts had either failed or only temporarily succeeded.

P2: I worked hard to lose weight through exercise, but as soon as I stopped, I started gaining it back.

Another participant expressed her opinion as a fantastic chance to lose weight at her age and receive medical testing at free of charge.

P5: It was a new experience for me to take part in this study and considering my age (59 years), I need to get this kind of health check and lose weight. As this was free, I took part.

Theme 2: following MR intervention

About the overall experience

All the participants reported that this was their first time participating in an organised dietary programme for weight loss, thus it was a new experience for them.

P2: I have never taken part in a diet program therefore this was a new experience for me.

P5: I had never taken anything in my life for weight loss, so this was a unique experience for me.

Adhering to the intervention

During the study, the participants were asked to replace their dinner meal with the MR shake which provided ~200 kcal per serving (200 mL). Several participants said it was difficult during the first few days because they got hungry after several hours. However, they said that they were accustomed to it after a few days. Additionally, they were more motivated after they noticed that they were losing weight.

P7: Even though I initially felt a bit hungry, I believe I eventually adapted. I avoided eating anything else for dinner other than the MR since I was so determined to lose weight. From time to time, I checked my weight and noticed a reduction. Seeing my weight getting reduced makes me more motivated to adhere to the program.

P2 and P8: I had mild hunger during the first several days after about 6–7 hours of taking the MR. But I was determined not to eat anything else. Later after 3–4 days, I didn’t get hungry like that.

Two participants said that they didn’t feel any hunger after taking the MR since the very first day.

P5: I didn’t feel hungry after having the MR since the beginning; therefore, I never ate anything else after having MR for the last 2 months.

P6: After having the MR, I didn’t feel hungry at all.

Further, some participants indicated that it was difficult for them to tolerate when they saw different foods or when their family or friends asked them to eat with them. Additionally, it has been challenging when participants work consecutive shifts.

P3: Although I didn’t feel hungry after taking the MR, I couldn't control myself when my friend invited me to dinner. Despite having taken the MR, I first noticed that I was getting hungry when I observed individuals eating food. Also, it was challenging when I was working nights and days straight.

P1: In the beginning, I felt my mouth watering when I saw other foods after having the meal. But after continuing the meal for a few days, I think my appetite for other food also diminished.

Two people stated that they missed the dinner for a few days since they had special functions to attend. Another participant mentioned that she strictly adhered to the intervention guidelines even when she had to spend the night at her sister’s house by taking dinner with her.

P5: I strictly followed the rules as I was so motivated to lose weight. I even took the MR when I was going to stay at my sister’s house for a few days.

Subtheme: length of the intervention

When participants were asked their opinion about the duration of the study, five participants responded that they would have preferred if the study’s duration had been longer.

P3: For me to lose more weight, I think it would be great if this program could last for longer than two months. I feel that substituting just one meal per day and maintaining the intervention for a longer period would be easier to follow than shortening the intervention’s duration and replacing additional meals each day.

P7 and P8: I believe I could have continued the program for longer simply because I need to drop more weight.

Others said they are happy with the current study length of 2 months because it would be difficult to continue for longer.

P6: It would be challenging to follow a longer duration than this, thus I believe the research time and frequency of consumption were sufficient.

One participant stated that the current length was adequate, however, she preferred to substitute the provided MR for more meals.

P1: The duration of the study was comfortable to me, but I preferred consuming the MR twice daily.

Also, another participant suggested replacing the breakfast with a MR instead of dinner. Considering that most people are not used to a liquid diet tonight.

P5: We're used to drinking milk in the morning rather than at night, thus I'd prefer if the MR was given at breakfast than at dinner.

Furthermore, all the participants reported that it was very easy to prepare the MR. And they had no issues regarding the preparation or storage of the provided meal.

Theme 3: sensory characteristics of the MR

Taste

When participants were asked about the taste of the MR, two participants stated that they were happy with it as it is, while six others indicated that they would have preferred it more if it had been a little bit sweeter. Also, two people said that they had experienced a slightly bitter taste in the shake, and it would be nice if that could be avoided.

P5: I would love it if the shake was a bit sweeter, also there was a slightly bitter taste in the MR which I didn’t enjoy. It would therefore be fantastic if the bitterness could be removed, and the sweetness could be enhanced.

Flavour

All the participants preferred to have MR in different flavours. Some of the suggested flavours were vanilla, strawberry, and coffee flavours.

P4: I like it if this came in different flavours because then you wouldn't have to drink it in the same taste every day and there would be a variation.

Texture

Few participants said that the powdered MR was not very soluble in water.

P1: The powder did not dissolve well in water; I prefer that powder dissolve in water as easily as milk powder does.

P4: The powder wasn't particularly soluble either. Most of the time I prepared; I got sedimentation.

P5: For, me the texture of the shake was a bit dense. I would prefer it If it was softer. But I’m not sure if it will fill the stomach then.

Theme 4: discomforts or adverse effects

Five of the respondents said that they did not experience any kind of discomfort at the beginning or even during the study period. One participant mentioned that over the initial several days, she experienced some physical weakness.

P4: Except for a slight sense of weakness during the first few days, I experienced no further discomfort.

Another individual reported stomach bloating within the first few days after consuming the MR. In addition, she said that while she experienced a delayed bowel opening in the morning, she was not constipated. One participant reported that while on her routine diet, she experienced constipation, but once this meal had been consumed, she had no further constipation.

P5: On the first day after having the meal for dinner, I experienced some bloating in my stomach. I usually feel the want to use the toilet as soon as I drink my morning tea when I eat my regular dinner, but when I took the MR, I didn't feel that way, and I wasn't constipated either.

P1: After participating in this program, I wasn't constipated as I had been in the past. I had no problems with passing or lumpy stools.

Theme 5: outcomes or changes to the body

All participants described weight loss and positive changes in their bodies after completing the study. Many participants reported lightness in their bodies, and some said that they felt more energetic and fit and had less joint pain or brightened skin colour.

P1: My body feels lighter after the program. My leg and back discomfort have both lessened since I last experienced them. My skin tone appears to have brightened a little, and I believe the black patches on my neck have faded.

P2: I felt more physically fit, and my endurance improved because of my weight loss.

P7: I feel a slight lightness in my body after the program and I’m happy that I can now easily fit into some of my clothes which were tight to me earlier.

A few participants said that they did not notice any positive or negative changes in their bodies.

P3: I didn't notice any other significant changes in my body after two months, either in a good or bad way.

Theme 6: advantages as a shift worker

The most frequently reported benefit by the participant was this method’s simplicity and convenience in comparison to other weight loss techniques. They stated that it was incredibly helpful to them because they could still adhere to the intervention even though they were in the middle of a very hectic night shift schedule. Furthermore, they mentioned that this method was timesaving and a lot easier than doing exercise. One participant said that because this meal was already nutritionally balanced, she did not need to worry about it like she would when dieting.

P1: This method is quick and easy compared to other weight loss methods. Also, this weight loss method is helpful to us as shift workers because it takes less time than exercising. We can still follow this program even when we have a busy work schedule.

P4: Compared to other weight loss diets, this meal is relatively simple to prepare because it can be done by anyone without special equipment and doesn't need to be kept in a temperature-controlled environment. Also, this is better than dieting because we are unaware of the kinds of foods we should avoid when we diet. Therefore, restricting the wrong diet could interfere with our ability to get the right nourishment. The fact that this MR is calorie-controlled yet nutritionally balanced, in my opinion, makes it considerably more successful than dieting.

Two participants reported that after consuming this meal at dinner, they felt less drowsy and exhausted when working the night shift.

P5: I frequently work nights as a shift worker, but after taking this MR, I didn't feel sleepy. I used to feel heavy and sleepy after eating my usual dinner. I think having a liquid meal is better than eating regular solid meals, especially for dinner because it makes you feel lighter and is easier to digest.

P6: I feel less exhausted when working on nights after taking this for dinner. I felt easy to walk around the hospital and climb steps.

Theme 7: disadvantages as a shift worker

Four people said that there are no disadvantages associated with this method. The difficulty in adjusting to this meal at the beginning was seen as a disadvantage by one participant. One person also mentioned feeling mentally unhappy when could not share meals with their family or friends.

P3: The difficulty in adapting to the MR, in the beginning, is a disadvantage.

P2: I was unable to join my family and friends for dinner when they invited me. I felt a little distanced from them during that time.

Another participant expressed her uncertainty about how physical fitness might be improved using this strategy alone which she stated as a disadvantage.

P1: Although weight is reduced, I'm not sure if fitness can be obtained only through MR, but I do know that we gain fitness when we exercise and lose weight.

Theme 8: recommendation to others

All the participants were keen to recommend the MR method to their friends or family members who wished to lose weight. They highlighted the results they gained and the advantages of the programme, saying that others could have similar advantages.

P2: After noticing my weight loss, many of my friends asked about the method I followed. Several of my other friends wanted to join after seeing the results. However, the trial’s recruitment process was then stopped. I strongly encouraged them to join if a second study takes place in the future. I believe that right now several people are waiting to sign up for the next trial.

Discussion

This study qualitatively explored the experiences of obese shift workers who participated in an MR dietary intervention to lose weight. In general, the study’s findings indicated that the weight reduction programme was effective and comparatively simple to follow and that most of the participants were happy with the programme’s outcomes and were willing to recommend it to others.

Almost all participants knew they were overweight, and they believed that by losing weight, mainly their health and appearance would likely be improved. This supports previous studies’ conclusions that participants in these trials were commonly driven to reduce weight by a health issue, a dislike of their appearance or a desire to improve their quality of life.13 Even though some individuals had previously tried self-dieting, none of them had sustained success. Results of surveys indicated that, instead of enrolling in a formal weight loss programme, most people try to lose weight by adhering to self-imposed dieting guidelines.16 17 But their success rate is low due to both inefficient dieting methods and a failure to stick with the rules over the long run.18 This was a brand-new experience for all the participants because none of them had ever taken part in this kind of planned dietary intervention before.

Participants mentioned that it was difficult to start at the beginning as they were not used to having a liquid meal for dinner. But this is common with many of the dietary interventions where individuals find it difficult to adjust in the beginning but later get used to it.12 19 However, the participants stated that they were so determined to follow the intervention’s rules because they were so eager to lose weight. They also claimed that when they saw their weight drop, it gave them even more motivation. A systematic review of quantitative studies testing the mediators of weight loss and maintenance and discovered that internal motivation, a positive body image and self-management skills like flexible eating restraint and self-monitoring all mediated long-term weight control.20 One person claimed that after a few days, the desire for other foods also decreased. It is possible to hypothesise that mild ketosis would mediate this appetite suppression.21 When there is not enough glucose in the blood, the body goes into a state of ketosis, which offers an alternate fuel source made of fat.22

Furthermore, participants mentioned that it was difficult to adhere to rules when they see others eat different food and when they get invited by their friends or family members to eat with them. Prior studies have shown that social support, especially from family members, is an effective strategy for promoting the adoption and maintenance of health interventions.23 It has been demonstrated that social undermining and a lack of encouragement for a healthy diet and/or physical activity are both positively correlated with weight.24 25 Therefore, for participants to successfully continue the dietary intervention, we think it would be beneficial to educate both their family members and coworkers in future research.

In addition, most of the participants preferred the MR product to be a bit sweeter in taste. Also, two other individuals mentioned experiencing a mildly bitter taste. This could be due to the inclusion of a large amount of whey proteins to improve the protein content of the product. Since proteins can introduce off flavours or aftertaste into the product, the flavour is one of the biggest challenges faced by product developers when trying to fortify food products with additional protein.26

The participants believed that the MR-based strategy was quite feasible when compared with other weight loss strategies because this method did not require any extra effort, resources or complicated processes. A previous meta-analysis that investigated the experiences of using very low-energy diets for weight loss indicated that adherence was facilitated by the ease of use of the diet.13 As to literature, lack of time, lack of desire, work commitments, injuries and ill-health, lack of partner support or a lack of understanding about how to implement behaviour change are among the personal, social and environmental barriers that people see as preventing them from engaging in weight reduction programmes.27 Even in workplaces with health promotion initiatives, nurses mentioned staff shortages, exhaustion and lack of time as excuses for absence.28 Therefore, workplace dietary intervention for shift employees should focus on work organisation as well as on being mindful of cultural, environmental, and social aspects.29

Although there were not any serious side effects associated with this intervention, few participants stated that they experienced slight bloating or constipation at the beginning. The components in MR shake such as whey protein, artificial sweeteners and sodium sometimes could result in bloating and gas.30 They disrupt the digestive system’s regular operation and alter the balance of beneficial bacteria in the colon, which both lead to bloating. Bloating could be avoided by frequently hydrating yourself, having more fibrous food, doing exercise and having more fruits and vegetables rich in digestive enzymes.31

Almost all the participants had positive insights regarding the outcomes of the intervention. They felt a lightness in their body because of the weight loss. These results are consistent with the quantitative results provided in the main outcome paper. Participants had mixed ideas about the length of the programme. The success of a weight-loss programme majorly depends on how long and diligently a weight-loss programme is followed.11 However, as the outcomes of the current participants ranged approximately to a maximum loss of 9 kg and gain of 0.5 kg, we believe that the current period of the intervention is sufficient for clinically significant weight loss.

One of the main strengths of this study was the use of face-to-face or telephone interviews over focus groups to obtain information. This approach made it possible to examine participants detailed first-hand experiences and provided for a more individualised exploration of the experience. Another plus strength was that the data were separately examined by two experienced researchers who then agreed on the themes and subthemes. This improved the reliability of the data and made sure that no common themes were overlooked. Importantly, the results of this qualitative results validated our quantitative findings.32

One of the limitations associated with the study is the recruitment bias. The decision to participate in this study was voluntary, therefore those who were unsuccessful or dropped out may be unwilling to participate in interviews. Thus, these study participants would likely have had better outcomes than the others who were not strictly following the plan. Furthermore, because this study’s data came from participants in a specific MR clinical trial, their generalisability is constrained. However, the results of this study provide fresh, in-depth information about the weight-loss experience, add to the body of knowledge regarding shift work research and provide a motivator for additional research.

Conclusion

Participants felt generally positive about replacing their dinner with the MR shake. They were compelled to participate in this programme because of the possibility of losing a significant amount of weight, even if it was difficult to follow in the beginning. All the participants found the strategy to be convenient, making it more appropriate for shift workers because the employees may still follow it despite a busy schedule. There were no noticeable discomforts or side effects associated with the dietary intervention. Also, when working night shifts participants reported that they felt less sleepy and tired after eating this meal for dinner.

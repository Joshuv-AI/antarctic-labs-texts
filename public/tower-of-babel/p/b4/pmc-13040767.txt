Defining the influence of digitalization on the future of dentistry through an educational handbook for Computer Aided Design tool training
Authors: Miruna Andreea Anghel, Iulia Roxana Marinescu, Alin Gabriel Ionescu, Daniela Micu, Alexandru Andrei Iliescu, Petre Costin Mărășescu, Willy Andrei Uriciuc, Cristian Zaharia, Stelian Mihai Sever Petrescu, Oana Andreea Diaconu, Horia Octavian Manolea
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundDigital dentistry is revolutionizing the dental industry, and Computer Aided Design / Computer Aided Manufacturing (CAD/CAM) is in the center of this transformation. The training of future students should include a deep understanding of this technology. The purpose of this study was to investigate the current practicality of a written protocol that participants could follow step-by-step in a CAD design program.MethodsA dedicated guide was conceived to facilitate the use of Exocad design software (Exocad Gmbh, Germany) and it was distributed to 134 students enrolled in Dentistry and Dental Technique study programs. Two detailed protocols were included, which served as support for practical activities in the laboratory, aimed at making a coping and a full anatomical crown. To emphasize the advantages as well as the limits of the guide used for its optimization, at the end of the meeting the students were asked to give feedback in the form of a questionnaire. Answers were statistically analyzed in SPSS.ResultsThe well-structured protocol guided students in mastering the principles of designing dental restorations and efficiently using CAD/CAM equipment. It helped optimize the learning process and improve the skills needed for excellence in digital dentistry.ConclusionsThe results of this study may serve as a guide for other institutions wishing to implement similar solutions. It is essential that dentists and dental technicians benefit from continuous training in the field of digital dentistry to efficiently use new technologies and offer patients high-quality treatments.

Background

Modern dental education is in standing evolution, integrating the technological advancements, including artificial intelligence [1], to offer students more comprehensive and realistic training. Elmore et al. (2013) argues that “industry certification of workplace skills is a key element for career and technical programs”, but this trend has received relatively little academic attention [2].

The implementation of Computer Aided Design/ Computer Aided Manufacturing CAD/CAM in dentistry is experiencing unprecedented development, allowing the digital design of basic prosthetic works [3], but also of complex prosthetic structures [4], prosthetic implant abutments [5, 6], surgical guides [7], maxillofacial epitheses [8], veneers [9], simulations that allow bold treatments in making orthodontic appliances [10, 11] or bruxism splints [12].

Introducing CAD/CAM technology in dental schools exposes the students to advanced digital instruments, familiarizing them with modern technologies used in the dental field and preparing them for practice in a digital era [13, 14]. By integrating computer thinking in dentistry, the work protocol becomes more precise and more effective. By replacing traditional methods with digital ones, numerous advantages are brought forward as secure data storage [15], quick access to information and the ability to perform virtual simulations of treatments [16, 17]. Although CAD software is intuitive, the numerous options, in addition to the advantage of creating complex prosthetic structures, also present the disadvantage of the need for initial guidance for beginners [18].

3D simulations allow students to visualize the complex teeth anatomy and to plan procedures with a high accuracy, and by thus offering them a realistic and well-prepared clinical experience [19]. Using digital 3D models instead of traditional dental materials significantly reduces laboratory costs and saves resources. Students can benefit from a personalized learning approach, adapted to their pace and individual needs, through interactive modules and digital teaching materials [20].

Perception, the process through which people interpret and give meaning to information, plays a crucial role in adopting new technologies. The authors wanted to evaluate students’ perception regarding the potential of CAD/CAM technology. The results show that a majority of students (84.33%) believe in the efficiency and optimization that this technology brings to dentistry, predicting exclusive use in the future. Computer-aided design is not just a tool for creating technical drawings but can also be an educational instrument that stimulates creativity [21]. Other studies regarding students’ attitudes and perception indicate that 86% agree that artificial intelligence will lead to major progress in dentistry. This suggests a bright future for the relationship between dentists and artificial intelligence [22].

CAD/CAM technology allows for a closer connection between theoretical knowledge taught in courses and practical skills acquired in the laboratory, consolidating learning and offering more complete preparation. Presenting case studies, practical demonstrations, and concrete clinical results can contribute to building trust in digital technologies [23].

In light of the current impact of digitization on modern dentistry, the purpose of this study is to highlight the results obtained after using a dedicated educational handbook for a CAD tool learning and to suggest possibilities for improvement of the educational act in creating digital design.

Methods

Aiming to perfect the quality of teaching Prostheses Technology, a specific laboratory handbook for students in their first and second year of study in Dental Technique and Dentistry has been developed. The guide was conceived to facilitate the use of Exocad design software (Exocad Gmbh, Germany). Two detailed protocols were developed, which served as support for practical activities in the laboratory, aimed at making a coping and a full anatomical crown. To emphasize the advantages as well as the limits of the guide used for its optimization, at the end of the meeting the students were asked to give feedback in the form of a questionnaire. The purpose of this study is to highlight the results obtained and to suggest possibilities for improvement of the educational act in creating digital design.

Study design

The experimental study consisted in making questionnaires which were applied on the students in their second year of Dental Medicine and on students in their first and second year of Dental Technique at the University for Medicine and Pharmacy of Craiova. This study was approved by the Ethics Commission of the University for Medicine and Pharmacy of Craiova (approval no. 166/14.09.2023).

The first pilot guide initiated in 2023 included visual elements with buttons found in the opening of the Exocad software and step-by-step instructions for developing the design of an anatomical crown and a coping, The pilot guide had a cohort of 38 students with a period of two hours allocated to the execution of the predetermined steps. After students have gone through the learning protocol, to get feedback on its utility and the improvement needs, a questionnaire containing twelve open questions on using CAD/CAM technologies has been prepared. Open questions were generated with the help of a QR code and they aimed to collect the information to identify and correct the sections that were difficult to understand or incomplete, in order to be improved in the study guide.

In 2024, a printed study handbook was initially created, containing screenshots to illustrate the concepts step-by-step. For this stage, a refinement of the pilot questionnaire has been conducted, resulting in the final study tool consisting of four-teen closed questions. The questionnaire was made available to students using a generated QR code, so they could display the document form and complete it online. The data were collected and analyzed using Microsoft Excel. The cohort consisted of 134 subjects from the University of Medicine and Pharmacy of Craiova, Faculty of Dental Medicine and Dental Technique, with time allocated for completing the steps being two hours.

Data collection and ethical considerations

Data acquisition was conducted via Google Forms, ensuring a rigorous protocol for participant privacy and GDPR compliance. To guarantee non-traceability, the platform was configured to disable the collection of e-mail addresses and IP metadata, ensuring that all submissions remained strictly anonymous from the point of entry.

The questionnaire responses were structured into three distinct sections:

Socio-demographic characteristics: This initial part gathered general data (e.g., age, gender, specialization in education). Statistical analysis

After the educational activity was closed, the spreadsheets were downloaded and initially centralized in Microsoft Excel (Microsoft Corporation, Redmond, Washington, USA), and SPSS (Statistical Package for Social Sciences) software, version 26 (SPSS Inc., Armonk, NY, USA) was used for statistical processing. Nominal variables were described as frequency distributions and corresponding percentages. Comparisons between groups were performed using the Chi-square test and Fisher’s Exact test. Statistically significant results were defined when p values were smaller than 0.05.

Results

Details of the project

The main objective of the last version of the Workbook named The First Pilot Guide (started in 2023) was the development of the digital competencies in using the Exocad software for designing crowns and dental copings. The initial version of the guide included interactive visual elements and sequential instructions, being tested on a cohort of 38 students, for two hours. The feedback was obtained with the help of a questionnaire with twelve open questions, that was made accessible through a QR code and allowed the targeting of clarification and content optimization needs.

In 2024, the guide was extended and published in form of a printed study book that includes explaining screen captures and a refined evaluation tool which consists of fourteen closed questions. The questionnaire, shared through QR code and completed online, was statistically analyzed in Microsoft Excel. The new cohort of 134 students at the University of Medicine and Pharmacy of Craiova, specialization Dental Medicine and Dental Technology, went through the training protocol within two hours.

The study lot comprised 134 students from the University of Medicine and Pharmacy of Craiova, with ages comprised between 19 and 36 years of age, mean value 21.31 ± 2.68. A brief comparison was made with a pilot group of 38 students also enrolled at the University of Medicine and Pharmacy of Craiova, with ages comprised between 18 and 37 years of age, mean value 22.53 ± 3.98.

There were no statistically significant differences between these two subgroups regarding the participants’ age (U = 3001.50, z = 1.732, p = 0.083).

The questions included in the questionnaire are described in the following sections.

Guide description

The printed study book was conceived as a methodological tool to familiarize students with the principles of digital design. Its structure followed the complete flow of the CAD process, beginning with the transforming of the clinical concept into a digital model and continuing with the practical stages of the dental restauration design.

The first section presents notions of computational thinking and explains the importance of understanding the intraoral or lab scanning process, where students are instructed regarding the identification, management and .STL or .PLY file import in the Exocad environment, learning to recognize the structure of the folders where data are saved and the necessary steps for their correct loading into the digital project.

The next sections detail the technical work stages in Exocad (Fig. 1) from opening the project and filling the patient file to the choosing of the restauration type, setting the parameters, color selection etc. upon the completion of the design.

Fig. 1Explanation of each step to place an order

Explanation of each step to place an order

To ensure visual clarity and minimize cognitive load, the educational material integrated high-resolution, annotated screenshots of the Exocad interface. These visual aids were systematically refined to highlight critical navigation paths, ensuring that the complex CAD environment was accessible to novice users.

The practical part of the guide focuses on digital modelling of a crown (Fig. 2) and of an anatomic coping (Fig. 3), and the final chapter underlines the importance of the educational feedback in optimizing the learning process and the perfecting of digital competencies of the students. There are presented self-evaluation methods and ways in which students can transmit suggestions regarding the clarity and the efficacy of the guide, thus contributing to the optimization of the next versions.

Experience in CAD/CAM design

The purpose of this question was to identify the students that have previously used CAD/CAM software, and the possible answers were “Yes” or “No”.

The pilot group comprised 27 students who answered “Yes” as they have previously performed CAD/CAM design and 11 students who answered “No”. The distribution of answers for the other questions according to the previous experience with CAD/CAM design in the pilot group is reflected in Table 2. The study lot comprised 29 students who answered “Yes” as they have previously performed CAD/CAM design and 105 students who answered “No”. The distribution of answers corresponding to other questions from the questionnaire, relative to previous experience with CAD/CAM design in the study group, is reflected in Table 2. Fig. 5Distribution of answers regarding the experience in CAD/CAM design, according to the study and pilot groups

Distribution of answers regarding the experience in CAD/CAM design, according to the study and pilot groups

The previous experience with CAD/CAM design was clearly associated with the fact that students have read about CAD/CAM design prior to this guide, as 75.86% of students with previous experience have also read documentation files before reading the present guide, compared to only 21.90% of students with no CAD/CAM design previous experience who have read documentation files. This association was strong and statistically significant, Cramer’s V = 0.470, χ2(2) = 29.661, p < 0.0005 (Table 2).

Previous experience with CAD/CAM design also influences students to be more cautious when declaring that CAD/CAM technique will be used exclusively in the future, as 72.41% of experienced students declared this, compared to 87.62% of students without experience. This association was moderately strong and statistically significant, Cramer’s V = 0.284, χ2(2) = 10.771, p = 0.005 (Table 2).

Discussion

In dentistry or education, surveys prove themselves to be a versatile research method offering the possibility to obtain valuable information through simple and efficient tools, such as questionnaires [24]. The results obtained from our first questionnaire emphasized the importance of implementing digital dentistry in the curriculum and supported us in the development of a practical handbook that would be part of the educational program. Through the feedback received, a new protocol adapted to their knowledge needs was developed. Other specialized surveys that assessed the knowledge and the practice of digital dentistry concluded that 96.7% of the practitioners are aware of the CAD/CAM technology in dentistry and 87% of the practitioners consider that the lack of knowledge, and not the high cost, is the major shortcoming to CAD/CAM adoption [25].

In our study, there was a significant difference between the pilot group and the study group regarding the prior interest in CAD/CAM information and previous design experience. This result was due to the fact that in the pilot group there were students who volunteered in the workshop “Customizing the CAD design protocol: instruction guide” – a guide meant to be lately introduced into the mandatory curriculum. In the study group, students both in Dental Technique and Dental Medicine were offered CAD training in their practical activities. Other specialized studies have shown that the approach to graduation is directly correlated with the student’s interest in emerging technologies and the importance of knowing them [14, 26].

The results obtained in this study indicate a strong connection between previous CAD/CAM design experience and learning behavior. Students with prior experience were significantly more likely to consult additional materials before beginning practical activities (75.86% vs. 21.90%). This finding suggests that prior experience encourages the students to deepen their knowledge and seek for additional resources, which can contribute to a better understanding of the concepts and improved performance in practical activities. Although there is recognition of the transformative potential of CAD/CAM technology in dentistry this study and others have revealed a heterogeneity in terms of the level of preparation and adoption, highlighting the need for continuous education to overcome perceived barriers [27].

Before delving into a detailed analysis of the results obtained, it is important to evaluate the students’ initial reaction to the guide. The purpose of this question was to identify the students’ first impression after reading this handbook. The results indicate a positive reception, 82.84% of students answering “Very good”. A smaller percentage, 5.22%, considered it “Good”, and 11.94% evaluated it as “Satisfactory”. This initial positive evaluation offers us a strong starting point to further analyze the effectiveness of the guide in the learning process. Introducing new technologies in medicine implies navigating a specific learning curve, characterized by a gradual improvement in performance as the number of repetitions increases [28]. The lack of specific digital pedagogical training makes teaching in dentistry a complex and demanding task [29].

In our pilot study, 18.42% of students encountered problems in completing the steps, while in the study group only 5.22% faced such problems, demonstrating that improvements were efficient. Besides the characteristics of the tools, the context in which they are presented and personal traits of dental professionals significantly influence the adoption of new technologies [30]. Data collected from satisfaction surveys and complex analyses using artificial intelligence based on medical data are essential for improving the quality of education, as well as for refining diagnostic accuracy in fields such as dentistry, where identifying systemic affections can be challenging [31].

To evaluate the efficiency of the handbook in facilitating the understanding of the process, a question that aimed at identifying possible difficulties was included. The results obtained show that generally students considered the steps to be clear and easy to follow. Only 6.72% of the participants signaled unclear areas, which indicates a good reception of the instruction from the majority. This finding suggests that the guide was well-conceived and accessible for students. A detailed analysis of the unclear aspects from the first guide (26.3%) had determined an increase in percentage of the benefits of a paper-based handbook (from 94.74% to 99.25%). The technical factors, especially the relative advantage, the complexity and the compatibility, are key determiners in the adoption rate of technological innovations, including in the field of dentistry. These characteristics influence the users’ perception of accessibility and the feasibility of adopting new technologies [32, 33]. Other studies emphasized the fact that key elements of mixed learning, such as interactive online resources, flexibility in planning the learning and the direct interaction with the lecturer are significant for increasing motivation and academic performance [34].

The industry is increasingly asking for specialists with CAD/CAM skills. In our study, 84.33% consider that this technology will be used exclusively in the future. By integrating these technologies into study programs, educational institutions respond to labor market needs, facilitating the employability of graduates. Adopting and accepting new technologies in dentistry could also be difficult because of the lack of awareness of digital dentistry or mistrust of the technology [35].

In our study no statistically significant correlations between the respondent’s gender and the questions in the questionnaire were identified, although the satisfactory rating regarding the current handbook was predominantly given by females. In other specialized studies, data analysis highlighted superior academic performance and a faster adoption of digital tools among females and clinical students compared to males or those in the preclinical stages of studies [36].

In contrast to findings from studies conducted in Switzerland and The United States [37, 38], which highlighted a greater interest in digitalization among younger dentists, the results of our study align with other more recent studies [39, 40] suggesting that although older age reduces interest in adopting digital technologies, it is no longer a statistically significant determining factor.

Although CAD/CAM technology is still more correlated with dental technology, 63.43% of the entire study lot was represented by dentistry students. This point is important because, in the future technology, the CAD abilities must also be known by dentists, temporary restorations through 3D printing being increasingly popular in the dental offices [41]. Moreover, in recent years, there has been an increasing interest in CAD not only from dental technicians but also from dentists, who have moved from a more theoretical interest [42] to a concern for improving digital skills [43, 44].

Limitations: This study did not include a traditional control group, as the evaluation instrument was specifically designed to assess user interaction with the educational guide, while dividing students into a study and a control group would have been academically unethical. While this approach allowed for a deep dive into the guide’s practicality, it limits the ability to draw definitive causal conclusions regarding its comparative efficacy against other teaching methods, such as a classical non-guided group or with alternative instructional formats (e.g., lectures, written scripts, or video tutorials), but these aspects may represent future study directions.

Conclusions

Our study highlighted remarkable enthusiasm among respondents both regarding CAD/CAM technology and the existence of a paper-based handbook adapted to the students’ level of understanding.

Educational institutions should adapt themselves to these trends and provide students with solid training in CAD/CAM technology. Graduates who master CAD/CAM technology are more competitive in the labor market and can access better-paying positions with greater responsibilities.

Obtaining feedback offers useful information on improving the educational process, this being the reason why, in the questionnaires, special attention was paid to identify unclear areas. Students must understand that CAD/CAM is not only an optional technology, but a necessity to offer patients modern restorations. Therefore, educating them in using CAD/CAM is essential to respond to the present market needs, and the educational process should be permanently adapted to the students’ knowledge and comprehension abilities.

The complete chloroplast genome and phylogenetic analysis of Garcinia esculenta Y. H. Li. 1981 (Clusiaceae)
Authors: Fengzhi Shi, Siying Li, Min Fan, Shuang Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractGarcinia esculenta Y. H. Li. 1981 (Clusiaceae) is an endemic tree species in China, primarily found in western and northwestern Yunnan Province. In this research, the complete chloroplast genome of G. esculenta was sequenced using the Illumina NovaSeq6000 platform. The result showed that the length of the complete chloroplast genome was 155,853 bp, which was composed of a large single-copy region (LSC) of 84,534 bp, a small single-copy region (SSC) of 17,175 bp, and a pair of inverted repeat (IR) regions of 27,072 bp. The overall GC content was 36.1%. The complete chloroplast genome encompassed 128 genes, comprising 83 protein-coding genes, 37 tRNA genes, and 8 rRNA genes. Phylogenetic analysis of the complete chloroplast genome sequences of 22 species revealed that G. esculenta is most closely related to G. oblongifolia.Keywords: complete chloroplast genome, Garcinia esculenta, phylogenetic analysis

Introduction

Garcinia L. is a large genus and belongs to the Clusiaceae family, with centers of species diversity located in Africa/Madagascar, Australasia, and Southeast Asia (Gaudeul et al. 2024). The genus comprises about 450 species, with 21 species distributed in China and 13 species found in Yunnan Province (Wu et al. 1990; Zhu et al. 2014).

Garcinia esculenta Y. H. Li. 1981 is an endemic tree species in China, belonging to the Clusiaceae family. It is primarily found in western and northwestern Yunnan Province, growing at an altitude of 860 to 1650 meters in valleys within mixed forests (Wu et al. 1990). The ripe fruit of G. esculenta is edible and has a sweet and sour flavor. Garcinia esculenta is both a medicinal and edible plant used in traditional Chinese medicine for treating cancer and inflammation (Zhang et al. 2014). Phytochemical studies have revealed that G. esculenta contains various types of compounds such as polycyclic polyprenylated acylphloroglucinols (PPAPs), xanthones, and biphenyls, which demonstrate a wide range of cytotoxic, anti-inflammatory, and xanthine oxidase inhibitory effects (Zheng et al. 2019; Wu et al. 2021; Shi et al. 2024). In recent years, the rapid advancement of high-throughput sequencing technology has led to the sequencing, assembly, and publication of chloroplast (cp) genomes from a growing array of species (Reuter et al. 2015). The complete cp genome of G. pedunculata, G. oblongifolia, G. anomala, G. mangostana, G. subelliptica, G. paucinervis, and G. gummi-gutta have been documented (Chen et al. 2022; Wee et al. 2023). In this study, the complete cp genome of G. esculenta was sequenced and analyzed to serve as a valuable genomic resource for advancing molecular identification and leveraging germplasm resources.

Materials and methods

The fresh leaves and specimen of G. esculenta were collected from Longchuan County, Dehong Dai and Jingpo Autonomous Prefecture, Yunnan Province, China (23°50′12″N, 97°31′66″E) (Figure 1), and were identified as G. esculenta by Dr. Shuang Wang. Four specimens were deposited in the Herbarium of Dali University (https://www.dali.edu.cn/yxy/, contact: Shuang Wang, wangshuang0541@126.com) under the voucher number: GE23100101, GE23100105, GE23100107, and GE23100109. This study was conducted in accordance with the Regulations on Biodiversity Conservation of Yunnan Province and did not require any ethical or institutional permission. The cp genome DNA was extracted from fresh leaves using a modified CTAB method (Doyle and Doyle 1987) and genome sequencing of 150 bp paired-end reads was performed using the Illumina NovaSeq6000 platform (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA). The complete cp genome was assembled with Getorganelle v1.7.7.0 (Jin et al. 2020). The assembled cp genome sequence was annotated using Geneious Prime 2023.2.1 with default settings (Kearse et al. 2012). The circular genome map was drawn using the CPGView program (http://www.1kmpg.cn/cpgview/) (Liu et al. 2023). A phylogenetic tree was constructed based on the complete cp genome of G. esculenta, in conjunction with 21 previously published complete cp genomes. The maximum likelihood analysis was performed using IQ-TREE v2.1.2 (Minh et al. 2020), with the best-fit model of TVM+F + R2, and included 1000 bootstrap replicates for statistical robustness. The phylogenetic tree was visualized using FigTree v1.4.4 (https://github.com/rambaut/figtree/). The annotated genomic sequences have been submitted to GenBank (accession number: OR834394.1, OR906276.1, OR911357.1, and OR879232.1). Since there was no significant difference between the four sequences, we selected the first sequence for analysis (accession number: OR834394.1).

Results

The complete cp genome sequence of G. esculenta was 155,853 base pairs (bp) in length (Figure 2). The average coverage depth for the G. esculenta cp genome is presented in Figure S1. It exhibited a typical quadripartite structure, consisting of a large single-copy (LSC) region of 84,534 bp, a small single-copy (SSC) region of 17,175 bp, and two inverted repeat regions (IRA and IRB) of 27,072 bp each. The overall GC content was 36.1%. The cp genome comprised of 128 genes, including 83 protein-coding genes, 37 transfer RNA genes, and 8 ribosomal RNA genes. There were 12 cis-splicing genes: 11 genes (atpF, rpoC1, ycf3, clpP, petB, petD, rpl16, rpl2, ndhB, ndhA, and rpl2) have a single intron each and one gene (clpP1) have two introns (Figure S2A). The locations of the exon regions within the trans-splicing gene, rps12, have also been successfully identified (Figure S2B).

Chloroplast genome map of G. esculenta. Genes on the inside of the map are transcribed in a clockwise direction; genes on the outside of the map are transcribed in a counter-clockwise direction.

To determine the phylogenetic placement of G. esculenta, 11 complete cp genome sequences from Garcinia and other 11 related taxa, along with two outgroup species (Cratoxylum cochinchinense MN399961.1 and Cratoxylum formosum NC_062808.1) were retrieved from the NCBI GenBank. Multiple sequence alignment was conducted using MAFFT v7.505 (https://mafft.cbrc.jp/alignment/server/index.html) (Katoh et al. 2019). A phylogenetic tree was constructed using the maximum likelihood analysis in IQ-TREE v2.1.2 (Minh et al. 2020) with 1000 bootstrap replicates. The phylogenetic analysis revealed that G. esculenta was most related to G. oblongifolia (NC_050384.1) with robust bootstrap support (Figure 3). The complete cp genome of G. esculenta provides valuable insights for evolutionary studies in Clusiaceae and the development of germplasm resources for this medicinal plant.

Discussion and conclusions

Garcinia esculenta Y. H. Li (Clusiaceae) is a unique plant endemic to the People’s Republic of China, primarily distributed in the western and northwestern parts of Yunnan Province (Zhang et al. 2014). The complete cp genome of G. esculenta is reported herein for the first time. The genome size, GC content, and gene composition of the cp genome sequence of G. esculenta are consistent with other species in the genus Garcinia (Chen et al. 2022; Wee et al. 2023). In the phylogenetic tree, the species of the genus Garcinia cluster together as a single clade, with 100% bootstrap values, indicating that the target species G. esculenta is most closely related to G. oblongifolia. The clustering results of Garcinia were similar to the previous studies (Wee et al. 2023), which provided new genetic information for the analysis of the taxonomic position of G. esculenta in the genus Garcinia. In addition, according to the Flora of China and Engler’s system, the genus Garcinia in China is divided into 10 sections based on its traits. Garcinia esculenta and G. oblongifolia belong to the same section, i.e. Sect. Oxycarpus Lour.

There are approximately 450 species of Garcinia globally, yet the availability of data pertaining to its cp genome remains rather limited. Our endeavor aims to delve into the cp genomes of diverse Garcinia species, with the objective of furnishing vital information that can facilitate species identification, enrich genetic resources, and enhance germplasm conservation efforts.

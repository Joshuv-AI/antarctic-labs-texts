8224 Vitamin D Supplementation for Healthy Elderly People and the Incidence of Fracture: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis
Authors: M d de Souza, R L Moraes Dantas, V L Duraes, G Renganathan, T B Mendes
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Abstract Disclosure: M.D. de Souza: None. R.L. Moraes Dantas: None. V.L. Duraes: None. G. Renganathan: None. T.B. Mendes: None. Background: The impact of vitamin D supplementation on the incidence of fracture in healthy elderly individuals remains uncertain. Objectives: We performed a systematic review and meta-analysis of studies investigating the incidence of fracture in healthy elderly patients with supplementation of vitamin D at doses exceeding 800 IU per day in comparison to placebo. Methods: We systematically searched PubMed, Embase and Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials in November 2023 with the main following search terms: ‘Vitamin D’, ‘supplementation’, ‘elderly’, ‘fracture’ and ‘randomized trial’. The initial search revealed 2,160 articles that were screened and assessed according to inclusion and exclusion criteria. Risk ratio (RR) and Hazard ratio (HR) with 95% confidence intervals were used to compare treatment effects for categorical endpoints. Heterogeneity was evaluated with Cochran Q test and I2 statistics; p values inferior to 0.10 and I2>25% were considered significant for heterogeneity. Results: A total of seven randomized controlled trials with 71,899 patients were included, of whom 36,822 (51.2%) were women. A total of 25,916 (78.2%) participants received a daily dose of vitamin D higher than 1,500 UI. The incidence of total fractures was not significantly different between participants who received vitamin D or placebo (RR 1.01; 95% CI 0.81-1.09; p=0.92; I2=57%) / HR 1.03; 95% CI 0.97-1.27; p=0.5; I2=45%). There was no difference in regards vitamin D dosage analysis (< 1500 UI/day RR 1.06; 95% CI 0.81-1.39; p=0.67; I2=82%/ ≥ 1500 UI/day RR 0.97; 95% CI 0.90-1.04; p=0.38; I2=0%).There was a significant 34% relative risk increase of hip fractures among women treated with vitamin D compared to placebo (121 events in 14033 participants) (RR 1.34; 95% CI 1.06-1.70; p=0.01; I2=0%). In the subgroup of men, there was no significant difference in hip fractures with supplementation of vitamin D compared to placebo (RR 0.86; 95% CI 0.65-1.13; p=0.28; I2=0%) / HR 0.87; 95% CI 0.65-1.18; p=0.37; I2=0%). Conclusion: In this meta-analysis of 71,899 healthy elderly individuals, the supplementation of vitamin D significantly increased the risk of hip fractures compared to placebo among women.

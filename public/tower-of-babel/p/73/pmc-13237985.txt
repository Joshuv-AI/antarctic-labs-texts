Development of disease-resistant, stable high-yielding, and high-quality rice variety “Yunjing-60” through double-cropping acceleration and intensive multi-environment selection in mid-altitude regions
Authors: Shuyan Kou, Zhenhua Zhu, Ping Huang, Zhigang Wu, Xu Yang, Cheng Jiang, Weihua Liu, Elsayed Mansour, Pingrong Yuan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Developing rice varieties that combine high yield, disease resistance, grain quality, and climatic adaptability is critical for sustaining rice production, particularly under current climate change. Mid-altitude rice regions face severe challenges from rice blast, climate variability, lodging, and yield-quality trade-offs. This study integrated accelerated generation advancement with multi-environment selection to develop a high-yielding, adaptable rice variety suitable to a mid-altitude production system. Yunjing-60 is a novel rice variety developed through pedigree selection combined with double-cropping acceleration. The cross was applied between Shengnong-5 and Chujing-44. Shengnong-5 is a blast-resistant, compact architecture with medium maturity. Chujing-44 is high-yielding, lodging-tolerant, and high-quality grain. Double-cropping was applied at Yuanjiang, which is characterized by a hot and dry climate. Moreover, greenhouse cultivation was conducted during cooler months from November to February. This enabled multiple generations per year for accelerating breeding cycles. The variety comparison trial in 2022 demonstrated that Yunjing-60 surpassed the check variety Chujing-38 with superior agronomic performance. It exhibited higher effective panicle density (400.5 per m²), greater sink capacity (135 filled grains per panicle), and 8.62% higher grain yield of 13,290 kg ha⁻¹. Principal component biplot confirmed its association with key yield components. This yield advantage was supported by its ideal plant architecture and favorable growth cycle. Moreover, the variety exceeded the check variety Chujing-38 across various pathogens. It exhibited superior disease resistance with a Grade 1 rating to rice blast (index 0.80). Also, it displayed high resistance to bacterial blight, sheath blight, and rice false smut. Multi-environment selection across different altitudes accelerated trait stabilization. Regional and production trials were applied in eight diverse locations across 2023–2024. It recorded a high average yield of 10,721 kg ha⁻¹ with a yield gain of 9.8% in production trials. AMMI and GGE biplots across tested environments confirmed its superior mean yield and stability, surpassing Chujing-38. Besides, multivariate analysis using clustered heatmap visualization confirmed its consistently high-yield performance across test environments. Consistent yield across diverse ecological zones indicates its environmental adaptability and low genotype-environment interaction. Quality assessments confirmed excellent values of rice-eating compared to the national level standard. It provided a brown rice rate of 83.5%, a milled rice rate of 75.2%, and a head rice rate of 73.7%. It recorded Grade 1 transparency, 17.6% amylose content, and 70-mm gel consistency. These parameters confirm its superior milling, appearance, and eating quality. Consequently, Yunjing-60 integrated adaptive traits, accelerated breeding efficiency, and superior market value. Hence, this approach contributes to sustainable intensification, broad adaptability, and food security in subtropical highland rice systems.

Introduction

Rice (Oryza sativa L.) is a fundamental component of global food security [1]. It feeds more than half of the population worldwide [2]. The rapid global population growth demands rising rice production [3]. However, rice productivity is challenged by various stresses under current climate fluctuations [4]. In particular, japonica varieties exhibit several limitations. These limitations include rice blast susceptibility, lodging vulnerability, and yield instability [5]. Climate change intensifies these constraints through increased pathogen pressure and unpredictable precipitation [6, 7]. Moreover, temperature fluctuations affect growth duration and quality parameters.

The mid-altitude rice-producing regions are an important basis for japonica rice cultivation [8]. However, these areas frequently face several production challenges. These challenges include severe rice disease, complex ecological conditions, and yield fluctuations [9]. Moreover, the altitude constrains rice cultivation above 1000 m due to cooler conditions and precipitation effects [10]. In addition, rice blast is increased by humidity and climate shifts [11]. Lodging and poor quality also limit sustainable production in mid-altitude regions [12]. Despite significant progress in japonica rice breeding, most varieties face critical restrictions under mid-altitude conditions. Common varieties exhibit trade-offs among yield, quality, and disease resistance [13]. High-yielding varieties often exhibit poor quality or low disease resistance, leading to reduced productivity. Therefore, a combination of high yield, disease resistance, and high quality is crucial under these conditions. Incorporating genetic resistance to major diseases such as rice blast and bacterial blight ensures resilient production. Moreover, combining high-yield potential with grain quality is crucial for regional food security [14].

The development of new rice varieties requires the integration of intensive selection methods with accelerated breeding techniques [1]. In response, breeders are increasingly adopting innovative approaches to accelerate generation advancement [15]. Double-cropping generation advancement combined with multi-environmental evaluation enables the selection of multiple traits, ensuring phenotypic stability across diverse environments [16]. This approach facilitates the rapid accumulation of high-yielding, disease-resistant, and high-quality varieties, while shortening breeding cycles and improving breeding efficiency. However, most previous studies have focused primarily on breeding speed, often neglecting the development of commercial varieties that combine high yield, disease resistance, grain quality, and environmental adaptability. The present study advances accelerated rice breeding strategies by shortening breeding cycles, improving multiple traits, and ensuring stable field performance across varied ecological conditions. It focused on developing a rice variety for mid-altitude regions to address challenges such as yield gaps, disease pressures, and climate variability. The candidate variety was developed through accelerated pedigree selection and double-cropping advancement, with multi-environment trials assessing its adaptability. The variety combines robust disease resistance, high yield, and superior grain quality, making it well-suited for sustainable production in mid-altitude regions. These findings offer valuable insights into efficient breeding models for rice production in variable environments, contributing to sustainable agricultural development and food security.

Materials and methods

Breeding materials

The parents of Yunjing-60 were Shengnong-5 (female) and Chujing-44 (male). The female parent, Shengnong-5, is a japonica rice variety known for strong resistance to rice blast. Besides, it possesses compact plant architecture and medium maturity. It was developed by Yunnan Academy of Agricultural Sciences, Yunnan, China. The male parent, Chujing-44, is a high-yielding japonica rice variety withstrong lodging resistance and excellent eating quality. It was bred by the Chuxiong Institute of Agricultural Sciences, Yunnan, China.

Double-cropping acceleration in Yuanjiang

Yuanjiang was used as a double-cropping rice area. It is located in the hot, dry river valley region of Yunnan Province, China (at an altitude of 300 m). Yuanjiang has a subtropical rainy season climate and high temperatures, with an average high temperature of 24 °C annually. It provides minimal seasonal variation with monthly averages ranging from 18 to 28 °C. Also, it provides low humidity and annual precipitation of around 900–1100 mm. This warm and stable environment enables double-cropping rice systems. Planting in April for the early rice season and October for the late rice season. Moreover, a greenhouse is used during cooler months from November to March. Optimal conditions in the greenhouse were maintained at 25–30 °C during the day/15–20 °C at night. The photoperiod was set to a standard 14-hour day length to simulate natural growing conditions. This rapid generational strategy shortens breeding cycles compared to a single season. This allows for accelerated rice breeding by implementing multiple full generations per year. Yuanjiang was primarily used as an off-season nursery to accelerate genetic improvement and shorten the breeding cycle. However, this low-altitude environment differs substantially from the target mid-altitude production region in temperature, humidity, and disease pressure. Therefore, advancement in Yuanjiang was not considered sufficient to confirm adaptation to the cooler, more humid, and blast-prone target environment. To address this limitation, the breeding process was complemented by pedigree-based selection and subsequent evaluation under representative mid-altitude field conditions, with standardized disease-resistance screening. Blast resistance was not directly screened during the early segregating generations grown in Yuanjiang.

Breeding process

Yunjing-60 was developed through intensive pedigree selection. In 2015, Shengnong-5 was crossed with Chujing-44 at Yunnan Academy of Agricultural Sciences, Yunnan, China. The developed F1 hybrid seeds were planted in Chuxiong (altitude 1750 m) in 2016. The pseudo-hybrid F1 individuals with poor performance were excluded. The promising individuals were identified based on phenotypic traits. The generations F2–F4 were sown in the double-cropping rice area of Yuanjiang from 2017 to the beginning of 2018. From April 2018 to 2021, F5–F8 were planted at the mid-altitude testing base in Chuxiong (altitude 1750 m). Plant height, panicle length, grains per panicle, and seed-setting rate were recorded. Single individuals with a compact plant type, strong disease resistance, large panicles, high grain numbers, and good maturity were selected in each generation. In the last year (F8), the line with comprehensive stable traits, uniform maturity, high yield, and superior quality was selected for the variety comparison trial. The advanced line Yunjing-60 was identified as a candidate variety. The variety comparison trial was conducted in 2022 in Chuxiong (altitude 1750 m). The trial comprised nine entries, including Yunjing-60 and Chujing-38 as a check variety. Chujing-38 was selected as a check variety because it is the locally recognized standard control cultivar in the official evaluation system for the target mid-altitude rice-growing region. Using this cultivar as the reference enabled a direct, consistent comparison of the candidate line with the production standards under the regional testing framework. All the entries were selected from advanced lines newly developed in 2022. A variety comparison trial evaluated the comprehensive performance of the advanced lines in the same field to identify the candidate lines for next regional trials. A randomized block design with three replications was applied, and each plot was 11.44 m². Agronomic traits, yield performance, and disease resistance were evaluated. The regional and production trials were organized by the Yunnan Provincial Crop Variety Approval Committee. The regional trials evaluated eight advanced lines across eight locations at altitudes of 1200–2000 m during 2023–2024. These lines were selected from different variety comparison trials in Yunnan province. The test locations for regional trials were Chuxiong (altitude 1750 m), Qilin (1875 m), Yuxi (1600 m), Luliang (1840 m), Shidian (1500 m), Midu (1980 m), Lancang (1350), and Longyang (1650 m). Randomized block design with three replications was applied with plot area 13.33 m². Yunnan Provincial Crop Disease Resistance Identification Station evaluated these advanced lines for major rice diseases in Xishuangbanna city (altitude 1200 m) in 2023. This location is an ideal area for precise identification of rice diseases due to its unique ecological conditions, pathogen populations, and rice variety resources. Production trials were conducted in 5 production areas using 700 m2 plots in 2024. The used locations were Luliang (altitude 1840 m), Longyang (1650 m), Yuxi (1600 m), Midu (1980 m), and Chuxiong (1750 m). The variety was officially approved and named Yunjing-60 in 2025. Trait investigation and measurement

Agronomic traits were measured in a variety comparison trial in accordance with the Rice Variety Regional Test Technical Regulations. Ten samples were collected from five points at each plot to assess the agronomic performance. The assessed traits included growth duration, plant height, panicle length, number of effective panicles, grains per panicle, seed-setting rate, and 1000-grain weight. At maturity, whole plots were harvested, threshed, and dried to 13.5% grain moisture. The actual yield per ha was calculated from grain weight. Grain yield assessment was performed in the variety comparison trial as well as regional and production trials. These advanced lines, including Yunjing-60, were evaluated for major rice diseases by the Yunnan Provincial Crop Disease Resistance Identification Station. The evaluation followed the Rice Blast Resistance Identification Protocol for Rice Regional Test. Resistance evaluation used both natural field nursery screening and artificial greenhouse inoculation. The applied assessments determined the comprehensive resistance index and grade. Grain quality was evaluated according to the Quality of Edible Rice Varieties standard. Brown rice rate, milled rice rate, head rice rate, chalky grain rate, chalkiness degree, transparency, amylose content, and gel consistency were determined.

Statistical analysis

Differences among genotypes were tested for statistical significance. Mean comparisons were performed using the least significant difference (LSD) test at the 0.05 probability level. Data from multi-environment trials were summarized to evaluate varietal performance and stability. Principal component analysis was applied to explore multivariate relationships among agronomic traits and the genotypes in the variety comparison trial. The first two principal components were visualized using biplot. Hierarchical clustering and heatmap visualization were used to group genotypes based on yield performance across environments. The dendrogram and heatmap were integrated to summarize genotype-by-environment yield patterns. The principal component biplot and heatmap were generated using R statistical software (version 4.4.2) employing packages of ggplot2, FactoMineR, factoextra, ggrepel, and tibble. Additive Main effects and Multiplicative Interaction (AMMI) and Genotype plus Genotype-by-Environment Interaction (GGE) analyses were performed using Genstat version 19 to evaluate yield stability across tested environments.

Results

Agronomic performance and yield traits in the variety comparison trial in 2022

Eight advanced lines, including Yunjing-60, were evaluated in the variety comparison trial in 2022 in Chuxiong. These lines were evaluated compared to Chujing-38 as a check variety. Yunjing-60 demonstrated superior performance in several agronomic traits compared to the check variety and the other evaluated lines (Table 1). It exhibited ideal plant architecture, with an intermediate plant height of 96.7 cm and robust tillering. It achieved the highest tiller density (427.5 per m²) and effective panicle count (400.5 per m²). In terms of phenology, its growth period was 168 days, slightly longer than Chujing-38 (163 days). Yunjing-60 also possessed a large sink capacity and produced the highest number of total spikelets per panicle (152.8). The highest filled grains per panicle (135.0) and the excellent seed setting rate (88.4%) were observed in Yunjing-60. Its panicle length (19.3 cm) was slightly longer than that of Chujing-38 (18.9 cm). The 1000-grain weight of Yunjing-60 (24.8 g) was lower than that of Chujing-38 (26.7 g). While it achieved the highest grain yield of 13,290 kg ha⁻¹, with 8.62% greater than Chujing-38 (12180 kg ha⁻¹). This indicates that its higher yield was primarily driven by a greater number of panicles, grains per panicle, and grain filling capacity. Yunjing-60 ranked the highest among all tested genotypes based on grain yield.

Principal component analysis (PC) was performed to assess the multivariate relationships among the studied traits and the evaluated genotypes (Fig. 3). The first two principal components (PC1 and PC2) explained 71.92% of the total variation (PC1 = 47.04% and PC2 = 24.88%). Yunjing-60 was associated with traits of high yield potential. It displayed strong associations with filled grains per panicle, total spikelets per panicle, number of tillers, number of effective panicles, and grain yield. This indicates its superior sink capacity. Besides, it maintained a favorable distance from traits related to excessive plant height and longer growth duration. This demonstrates balanced integration of yield components with moderate plant stature and growth duration. This multivariate pattern confirms that Yunjing-60 combines an ideal plant architecture with high panicle productivity, contributing to its significant yield advantage over the check variety (Fig. 3). Moreover, as shown in Fig. 4, Yunjing-60 demonstrates superior agronomic traits, including field performance and panicle morphology.

Grain yield in regional and production trials 2023–2024

The Yunnan Provincial Crop Variety Approval Committee organized regional trials during 2023–2024. Eight advanced lines, including Yunjing-60, were evaluated across eight locations during two years compared with the check variety Chujing-38. The evaluated lines were selected from different variety comparison trials across Yunnan province. The combined analysis of variance in Table 2 showed that genotype, environment, and the genotype-by-environment interaction (G×E) had highly significant effects on grain yield. This confirms substantial variation among genotypes, strong environmental influence, and differential genotype responses across test environments. The environmental effect accounted for the largest share of the total variation, followed by G×E and genotype. This contribution indicates that environmental differences strongly influenced yield performance across test sites. Moreover, the significant G×E interaction confirmed that genotypes responded differently across environments. In the AMMI partitioning, both PC1 and PC2 were highly significant, indicating that the first two interaction principal components captured a substantial portion of the structured G×E variation.

Quality test in 2023

Grain quality parameters were evaluated for assessed genotypes according to the Quality of Edible Rice Varieties standard in 2023. Yunjing-60 surpassed Chujing-38 in rice quality and other evaluated advanced lines (Table 6). It recorded brown rice rate of 83.5% (≥ 82.0% national standard), milled rice rate of 75.2% (≥ 72.0%), and head rice rate of 73.7% (≥ 68.0%). The high milling parameters demonstrate grain integrity, structural strength, and minimal breakage during industrial processing. These traits translate into reduced commercial losses and higher economic returns for millers and farmers. Physical appearance traits were similarly remarkable. It exhibited a low average chalky grain rate of 8.0% compared to the standards of ≤ 10.0%. Moreover, it recorded Grade-1 transparency, within the acceptable range of ≤ 2.00. These characteristics ensure visually attractive rice with uniform whiteness and translucency. This is critical for premium consumer markets where appearance influences purchasing decisions. Cooking and eating quality parameters confirmed soft and the best eating characteristics. It possesses a low amylose content of 17.0% according to the national standard 13.0–18.0%. This is indicative of a soft non-sticky texture. Besides, it recorded gel consistency of 70 mm compared to ≥ 70 mm required for soft rice types. Physical evaluation of cooked rice revealed a soft texture and good elasticity. The combination of superior processing efficiency, attractive appearance, and best cooking quality establishes Yunjing-60 as a high-value variety. These attributes enhance consumer satisfaction. Consequently, it is suitable for both large-scale production systems and premium branded eating rice markets.

Discussion

The development of disease-resistant, high-yielding, and high-quality rice varieties is important for sustainable agricultural production [17]. This goal has become more crucial in regions exposed to climatic variability. Mid-altitude rice regions face multifaceted constraints that necessitate targeted breeding for adaptable varieties. These challenges include cooler temperatures and shortened growing seasons. In addition, the elevation limits photoperiod-sensitive japonica varieties and intensifies rice blast. Additional pressures of lodging sensitivity in variable winds, yield-quality trade-offs, and genotype-environment interactions [9]. These conditions require intensive selection in multi-environment trials to accumulate adaptive traits with grain quality. The present study integrated accelerated generation advancement with intensive multi-environment selection. This strategy was applied to develop a new rice variety adapted to mid-altitude production systems. The breeding cycle was significantly shortened by adopting a double-cropping generation strategy. This strategy combined the efficiency and precision to meet the urgent need for adaptable rice varieties. The results demonstrated that double-cropping acceleration is an efficient model for rice breeding. Consequently, breeders can apply this framework to overcome the constraints of single-season limitation. This could be applied by integrating double-cropping acceleration with phenotypic screening and multi-site statistical evaluation. Similarly, rice breeders integrated accelerated generation advancement with multi-environment selection to enhance genetic gains and varietal adaptability. In this regard, Bhosale, et al. [18] developed a framework to accelerate rice variety development. This model employed recurrent selection with rapid generation cycles through controlled environment using multi-location yield trials. Implementing this approach can enhance genetic gains to ensure food security under climate challenges. Likewise, Tanaka, et al. [19] applied a breeding system that offers a rapid generation advancement method for rice breeding. This system uses growth chambers with elevated CO2 levels and controlled day length to reduce days to heading. Experiments with high CO2 significantly accelerated heading. This shortens generation intervals and enables multiple generations per year. This supports efficient line development for large populations.

Breeding programs mainly depend on the strategic integration of complementary traits in two parents [20, 21]. The female parent Shengnong-5 has durable resistance to rice blast and compact architecture. The male parent Chujing-44 has high yield, lodging tolerance, and grain quality. The crossing between these parents improved the genetic base and reduced yield losses. The new variety possesses balanced plant architecture for high-density planting systems. The moderate plant height with robust stems is required under heavy rainfall and wind events. It showed a slightly longer growth duration than the check variety, reaching maturity about five days later than Chujing-38. Although this difference is relatively low, its practical significance may depend on the cropping rotation pattern used in mid-altitude production areas. In systems with narrow planting windows for subsequent crops, maturity delays could reduce scheduling flexibility. However, under typical local production conditions, this difference may be acceptable when considered alongside the advantages of higher yield, favorable grain quality, and strong disease resistance. Moreover, the variety produced dense panicle populations. The yield advantage of Yunjing-60 over the check variety appears to be associated mainly with greater effective panicle number and more grains per panicle. This suggests that its superior productivity was driven primarily by sink number rather than grain size. This enables consistently high yields under both experimental and production conditions. Although Yunjing-60 had a lower 1000-grain weight than Chujing-38. The market significance of a smaller grain size may depend on regional consumer preference, grain appearance standards, and processing requirements. Therefore, grain size should be considered with the overall quality profile. A multivariate analysis using principal component biplot revealed a strong association between Yunjing-60 and yield components. It exhibited a strong association with filled grains per panicle, total spikelets per panicle, number of tillers, number of effective panicles and grain yield. Moreover, it maintained a favorable distance from traits associated with excessive plant height or longer growth duration. This is an important balanced integration of yield components with moderate plant stature and growth duration. Accordingly, this trait combination confirmed its suitability as an ideotype for mid-altitude environments.

Yunjing-60 demonstrated superior resistance to major rice pathogens and high grain quality. It exhibited superior disease resistance to rice blast and high resistance to bacterial blight, sheath blight, and rice false smut. It surpassed the check variety Chujing-38 across multiple pathogens. This broad resistance is a major advantage in subtropical highland ecosystems. The fluctuating humidity and temperature in these regions increase disease pressure. The disease-resistance profile of Yunjing-60 can mitigate production risk and potentially improve management efficiency in mid-altitude regions. Genetic resistance preserves productivity and enables significant reductions in chemical pesticide usage [22]. This aligns with principles of integrated pest management and environmentally friendly agriculture. Future work could compare Yunjing-60 with additional regional cultivars and incorporate cost–benefit assessment to define its commercial and economic value. The disease resistance evaluation in the present study was conducted at a single authorized screening location under standardized conditions. Therefore, the observed strong resistance of Yunjing-60 supports its favorable phenotypic performance against the tested diseases under those conditions.

In addition to the superior agronomic traits observed in Yunjing-60, it is also stable at mid-altitudes. This stability may be potentially linked to several key biological mechanisms. One possible factor is its hypothesized cold tolerance, especially during the booting stage. Cold tolerance is vital for achieving high yields in cooler, mid-altitude environments. In rice, it is often associated with the ability to maintain reproductive processes under low-temperature stress, particularly pollen viability and spikelet fertility. During this sensitive stage, cold stress can cause pollen sterility, resulting in lower seed set and reduced yields. The observed stability of Yunjing-60 at mid-altitudes suggests that it may possess genetic adaptations that help it tolerate these temperature fluctuations, ensuring that the reproductive process is not hindered by cold stress. Another important factor contributing to its stability is plant architecture. The plant architecture is essential to the harsh conditions at mid-altitudes. Yunjing-60 has a compact, robust structure, moderate plant height, and strong tillering capacity. These traits are critical in high-altitude regions where wind and heavy rainfall can cause lodging. Lodging susceptibility is a significant issue in mid-altitude rice farming, as frequent storms and strong winds can damage tall or weak-stemmed plants. The compact architecture of Yunjing-60, combined with its ability to produce dense panicles and maintain high tiller density, enhances its resistance to lodging, ensuring the plants remain upright even under adverse environmental conditions. In addition to these physical traits, the broad adaptability of Yunjing-60 may be influenced by potential ability to utilize nutrients and water efficiently in mid-altitude environments, which often face fluctuating moisture levels. While not directly measured in this study, it is possible that root architecture could play a key role in improving nutrient and water uptake efficiency. In regions with variable rainfall, a well-developed root system might allow the plant to access deeper soil layers, potentially ensuring a more consistent supply of water and nutrients during periods of reduced precipitation. These physiological factors could be further investigated as possible contributors to the plant resilience and consistent growth across diverse environments. Furthermore, Yunjing-60 disease resistance is another factor that may contribute to its stability at mid-altitudes. Mid-altitude regions often experience increased pathogen pressure, particularly rice blast, due to the combination of cooler temperatures and high humidity. Yunjing-60 has demonstrated superior resistance to multiple rice diseases, including rice blast, bacterial blight, sheath blight, and rice false smut. This resistance helps the variety maintain high productivity under significant disease pressure. This resistance minimizes the need for chemical interventions, reducing production costs and promoting sustainable agricultural practices. The suggested combination of resilience to cooler conditions, robust plant architecture, potential for efficient resource utilization, and broad-spectrum disease resistance likely contributes to make Yunjing-60 adaptable and stable across diverse mid-altitude environments. These potential biological mechanisms may collectively contribute to its superior performance and stability, offering a reliable solution for rice production in regions characterized by variable environmental conditions and climate change. As climate patterns continue to shift, the ability Yunjing-60 to maintain high yields and quality under such stress conditions highlights its potential as a key variety for sustainable rice production in mid-altitude and other similar ecological zones.

In terms of grain quality, Yunjing-60 met or exceeded national excellent standards. It possesses superior milling efficiency, high head rice recovery, and amylose and gel consistency. The soft texture of cooked grains aligns with consumer preferences. Although Yunjing-60 showed favorable grain quality characteristics, these traits were evaluated in only one season in the present study. Therefore, the reported quality profile serves as an initial assessment of its performance rather than confirmation of stability across years and environments. Developing resistant rice variety without trade-offs in yield or quality remains a primary goal for rice breeders, and the preliminary results for Yunjing-60 provide a promising basis for further investigation. In this context, Hu, et al. [23] crossed parents characterized by semi-dwarf stature, moderate tillering, and lodging resistance. They aimed to pyramid compact architecture, lodging resistance, blast tolerance, high yield, and grain quality. Derived lines were stable across five sites. The advanced lines produced 219–231 panicles/m², 84–86% fertility, and superior panicle blast scores (1.5-2.0). Similarly, Guo, et al. [24] crossed a high-yielding susceptible variety with a resistant donor. They aimed to pyramid blast resistance, yield potential, and quality traits without trade-offs. The applied cross integrated blast resistance with stable, high-yielding, and quality traits. Evaluation across diverse multi-environment trials identified stable advanced combine blast-resistant, high-yielding, and grain quality. Moreover, Sabri, et al. [25] evaluated advanced lines derived from crossing different parents. They aimed to integrate blast resistance with high-yielding. The advanced lines recorded blast resistance alongside high performance in grain yield, filled grains, tillers, and panicles across different environments.

Multi-environment trials are essential for assessing genotype performance, stability, and adaptability under different agro-ecological conditions [26–28]. Multi-environmental evaluation confirmed the high performance and stability of Yunjing-60. It exhibited stable performance across diverse environments. It displayed stable yields under varying soil types and climatic conditions. The temperature fluctuations and rainfall variability frequently disrupt traditional varieties in these regions. The AMMI analysis showed that both the genotype effect and the genotype by environment interaction contributed significantly to grain-yield variation across the tested environments. The first two AMMI interaction principal components captured a substantial proportion of the interaction pattern, confirming the crossover response among genotypes. Yunjing-60 was positioned relatively close to the biplot origin, indicating comparatively low interaction effects and favorable stability across environments. This result suggests that the superior yield performance of Yunjing-60 was not restricted to a single test site but was maintained with relatively consistent adaptation across diverse mid-altitude conditions. By contrast, genotypes located farther from the origin exhibited stronger interaction with particular environments and therefore lower stability. These findings support the view that Yunjing-60 combined high productivity with broad adaptation, making it a promising candidate for stable cultivation across the evaluated target region. Moreover, the GGE biplots effectively partitioned yield variation from multi-environment trials. It captures differences in stability across the 1200–2000 m altitude regions. Yunjing-60 was located close to the origin on the positive PC1 axis in both scatter and comparison biplots. This signifies high yield coupled with low genotype by environment interaction. Regional trials confirmed its superiority and stability across twenty-one environments, surpassing Chujing-38. This illustrates its strong ecological adaptability and inherent climate resilience. This stability is critical for ensuring food security in mid-altitude areas. It reduces production risks for farmers operating across diverse heterogeneous landscapes. In this context, Senguttuvel, et al. [29] assessed yield stability in different lowland rice genotypes. The GGE biplot explained 69% of the variation in grain yield. Moreover, GGE biplots identified stable and high-yielding genotypes across environments. Identified climate-resilient genotypes for irrigated lowland systems. Likewise, Sitaresmi, et al. [30] evaluated diverse rice genotypes across different lowland to mid-altitude locations during dry and wet seasons. GGE biplot revealed significant genotype by environment interactions for grain yield. It identified certain genotypes with consistent stability across different environments. Zhang, et al. [31] examined genotype by environment interactions for grain yield in different rice genotypes across regrowth cycles at seven locations during four seasons. Significant genotype-by-environment interactions were detected in the studied genotypes. GGE biplot displayed environment-specific adaptation, with regrowth cycles and locations as key interaction drivers. The biplot identified a stable genotype (PR23) in diverse conditions. The identified stable rice genotype could be considered climate-resilient. These findings indicate the importance of multi-location trials for selecting stable varieties under variable environments. Clustered heatmap of grain yield across environments provided a visualization of genotype performance patterns. Yunjing-60 consistently displayed high yield values (indicated by blue coloration) across most test sites. The dendrogram clustered Yunjing-60 separately from the check variety and the other advanced lines. This visual pattern reinforces its broad adaptability and consistent high performance. While genotypes such as Jinrui-15 and Lu 19‑667 displayed a distinct lower-yielding cluster (shown in red). This indicates the yield superiority and stability of Yunjing-60.

Conclusion

This study developed a rice variety through intensive pedigree selection integrated with double-cropping acceleration and multi-environment trials. Yunjing-60 is a newly developed variety for mid-altitude production systems. These regions are characterized by intensified blast pressure, lodging vulnerability, and climate-induced yield instability. The developed variety displayed Grade 1 resistance to rice blast and high resistance to bacterial blight, sheath blight, and rice false smut. It demonstrated yield advantage over the commercial check variety and other advanced lines under diverse mid-altitude ecosystems. Besides, it exhibited yield stability across diverse mid-altitude sites under varying ecological conditions. PCA biplot and clustered heatmap analyses provided visual evidence of its superior trait combination and consistent high-yield performance across environments. GGE biplot analysis demonstrated its high yield potential and stability across diverse environments. It recorded a low genotype-by-environment interaction. Moreover, it possessed superior milling and eating quality (amylose and gel consistency). Consequently, this variety offers the potential for reduced fungicide reliance and sustained productivity under variable highland conditions. The findings of this study confirm accelerated generation advancement as an efficient breeding model. This approach can shorten breeding cycles by accumulating elite traits under current global climate pressures. Future studies should aim to integrate molecular characterization to validate disease resistance genes and grain quality alleles, ensuring a deeper understanding of the genetic basis of the observed phenotypic performance. This will complement the current findings and provide a more comprehensive biological foundation for the Yunjing-60 variety.

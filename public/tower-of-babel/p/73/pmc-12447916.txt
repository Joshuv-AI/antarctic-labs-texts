Thiol-ene click reaction aptamer sensor based on MWCNT-COOH/MOF-818 composite for highly sensitive detection of foodborne pathogenic bacteria
Authors: Hai Chi, Yue Xiao, Houqi Ning, Xingzhong Zhang, Hongrui Chen, Jie Tang, Xuefei Wen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This study established a unified electrochemical sensing platform for the ultra-sensitive and rapid detection of foodborne pathogens. Targeting three representative pathogenic bacteria—Staphylococcus aureus, Escherichia coli, and Salmonella typhimurium—a composite sensing interface was fabricated by integrating MWCNT-COOH with MOF-818. Highly specific aptamers were directionally immobilized onto the MWCNT-COOH/MOF-818 composite surface through thiol-ene click chemistry, yielding an electrochemical biosensor with superior performance. Experimental results revealed detection limits of 3, 6, and 4 CFU mL−1 for the three target bacteria, respectively. Moreover, the sensor displayed strong detection capabilities in authentic food samples (pork paste). This detection strategy by integrating sample recognition and signal transduction within a single interface can be generalized to multiple pathogens via aptamer substitution, offering a novel technical approach for on-site rapid screening in food safety. This platform holds considerable potential for the prevention and control of foodborne diseases.

Introduction

The ultra-sensitive detection of foodborne pathogens constitutes a critical necessity for ensuring global public health security. Pathogenic microorganisms, specifically Staphylococcus aureus (S. aureus), Escherichia coli (E. coli), and Salmonella typhimurium (S. typhimurium), can circumvent human immune defenses even at markedly low contamination levels within the food supply chain, thereby eliciting a range of health threats from gastrointestinal inflammation to organ failure (Deng et al., 2025; Yentongchai et al., 2025; Yoon et al., 2025). Although the plate culture method, regarded as a traditional detection standard, provides exceptional reliability, its dependence on the biological growth cycle engenders a considerable discrepancy between detection timeframes and the accelerated pace of contemporary food distribution (Oliveira, Oliveira, Lopes, Pia, Virgilio-da-Silva, & Sant'Ana, 2025). Molecular diagnostic techniques and immunological assays, while capable of precise pathogen identification, are limited by nucleic acid extraction efficiency, antibody stability, and the requirement for specialized instrumentation, thus hindering widespread application in decentralized testing environments (Dong et al., 2025; Khalifa et al., 2025; Venkatesan et al., 2025). The globalization of the food industry, coupled with prolonged supply chains, has transformed the risk of pathogenic contamination into a multifaceted and interregional transmission challenge. There is an imperative to develop an innovative detection system that integrates rapidity, portability, and intelligence. Such a system is crucial not only for augmenting early warning capabilities in food safety emergencies but also as a technological foundation for establishing a proactive public health defense infrastructure. Its strategic importance resides in mitigating foodborne disease outbreaks and reducing the strain on healthcare systems.

In the field of real-time pathogen detection, electrochemical biosensors have emerged as a crucial technological approach due to their unique advantages. The core value of electrochemical biosensors lies in their ability to integrate three key functions - recognition, transduction, and signal amplification - onto a miniaturized platform (Flores-Ramírez et al., 2024; Kumar Goshisht, Kumar Patra, Mahal, Kumar Singh, Shobha and Parshad, 2025; Lei et al., 2025). This integration is not a simple aggregation of functions, but rather, through elaborate design and optimization, it enables the synergistic operation of these functions, providing an innovative solution for efficient and convenient pathogen detection. In recent years, significant progress has been made in the field of sensor technology. In particular, the integrated sensor technology that combines nanostructured surfaces with data-driven analysis has brought new opportunities for the detection of foodborne pathogens(Ye et al., 2024). These technological breakthroughs fully demonstrate the great potential of the combination of nanostructured surfaces and data-driven analysis, enabling the accurate detection of trace contaminants in complex matrices in an efficient and cost-effective manner (Ye et al., 2024; Ye et al., 2024). Compared with traditional detection techniques, this method shows significant improvements in sensitivity, specificity, and detection speed, providing a more reliable means for the detection of foodborne pathogens. Meanwhile, contemporary research mainly focuses on the development of sensing systems based on screen - printed electrodes (SPE). In this system, biological recognition elements (such as aptamers), signal reporters, and signal amplifiers are jointly immobilized on the electrode interface, realizing a simplified “sample-in, result-out” detection mode. (Ghalkhani et al., 2022; Kamalasekaran and Sundramoorthy, 2024). This methodology markedly simplifies the multi-step procedures characteristic of conventional detection methods and has emerged as a central focus within the discipline. Within such systems, the rational design of functional materials plays a vital role in dictating sensing performance.

Metal-Organic Framework (MOF) materials, distinguished by their tunable pore architectures and high specific surface areas, provide a distinctive platform for interfacial modulation in electrochemical sensing (Dymek et al., 2024; Hu et al., 2024; C. Li et al., 2025). Among these, the Cu-Zr bimetallic metal-organic framework-818 (MOF-818), a prominent exemplar of the new generation of porous crystalline materials, forms a three-dimensional interpenetrating mesoporous network via precise synergistic coordination of copper-zirconium bimetallic nodes, thereby conferring unprecedented structural advantages for electrochemical sensing applications (Liu et al., 2024, Liu et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2025). Compared to conventional single-metal framework materials, the bimetallic active sites in MOF-818 generate an efficient synergistic catalytic interface through electronic orbital hybridization, markedly enhancing the reversibility and selectivity of redox processes (J. Li et al., 2024). Its hierarchically ordered pore structure, arranged periodically, not only addresses the limitations of microporous materials for mass transport of biomacromolecules but also facilitates targeted enrichment and rapid diffusion kinetics of analytes by fine-tuning the surface chemical microenvironment. Nevertheless, the inherent electrical conductivity of MOF-818 remains inadequate, posing a critical bottleneck to further improvements in sensing performance (Fang et al., 2022).

To address this limitation, researchers have innovatively employed various nanomaterials to fabricate three-dimensional conductive networks, thereby realizing a synergistic enhancement mechanism between active sites and charge transport pathways (Mahalakshmi et al., 2025; Shi et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2024). This multidimensional interface engineering approach judiciously optimizes both the catalytic activity and electrical conductivity of the materials, establishing a robust material basis for the development of electrochemical sensors with high sensitivity and strong anti-interference capabilities. Additionally, it exhibits significant potential for applications in detecting foodborne pathogenic bacteria. Among the diverse nanomaterials, multi-walled carbon nanotube (MWCNT), owing to their superior electrical conductivity, large specific surface area, and excellent chemical stability, have emerged as an optimal candidate for integration with MOF to construct advanced functional materials (Cai et al., 2025; Doğan et al., 2025). Through functionalization with carboxyl groups (MWCNT-COOH), oxygen-containing moieties were successfully introduced, effectively overcoming the challenges associated with dispersion and chemical inertness of the pristine material (L＀＀L. Liu et al., 2024). Moreover, MWCNT serve as an ideal substrate for fabricating high-performance biosensing interfaces due to their unique layered morphology, high electron mobility, and versatile surface chemical modifiability. For example, it is precisely these distinctive advantages that render composites of MOF and MWCNT-COOH highly promising for applications in electrochemical sensing (Han et al., 2024; W. Li et al., 2021). However, confronted with potential interference arising from the complex components in food matrices and the necessity for long-term stability of the sensing interface, researchers have utilized click chemistry techniques to achieve oriented immobilization of aptamers (Belal et al., 2018; Chen et al., 2018; Z. Li et al., 2025). This strategy effectively integrates the signal amplification properties of nanomaterials while maintaining the structural integrity and bioactivity of nucleic acid aptamers, thereby providing a solid foundation for enhancing the detection sensitivity of electrochemical sensors.

This study presents a novel integrated electrochemical sensor that proficiently facilitates one-stop rapid detection of foodborne pathogens. Employing an interfacial engineering approach, the sensor attains three-dimensional integration by utilizing MOF-818 as the sensing substrate, MWCNT-COOH as signal amplifiers, and specific aptamer recognition elements. Specifically, MWCNT-COOH are directionally immobilized with aptamers via click chemistry, establishing a stable bio-recognition interface; concurrently, the MOF-818 nanocrystals electronically synergize with MWCNT-COOH to fabricate an integrated “recognition-transduction-amplification” sensing system. This design, through meticulous alignment of functional material modules, not only enhances detection sensitivity but also considerably improves the sensor's anti-interference capability and long-term stability in complex matrices. Importantly, the platform's modular configuration facilitates universal detection—by simply substituting the aptamer sequence, it can be adapted to detect diverse pathogens. Instrumentation

Electrochemical analyses were conducted using a Shanghai Chenhua CHI660e electrochemical workstation. The FT-IR spectra were collected on a Fourier transform infrared spectrometer (Nicolet IS50-Nicolet Continuum, Thermo Fisher Scientific). The crystal and chemical structures were further investigated using X-ray powder diffraction (XRD, D8 ADVANCE XRD, Bruker), and chemical bonding characteristics were analyzed through X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, Thermo Scientific K-Alpha Nexsa, USA). The morphologies of the fabricated working electrodes were examined via scanning electron microscopy (SEM, Merlin, Zeiss), while chemical compositions were characterized by energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS, Merlin, Zeiss).

Synthesis of MOF-818

According to literature optimization (Fang et al., 2022), metal precursors ZrOCl2 · 8H2O (40 mg) and Cu (NO3) 2 · 3H2O (130 mg), organic ligand 2, 5-pyridinedicarboxylic acid (H2PyC, 35 mg), and structure directing agent trifluoroacetic acid (125 μL) were dissolved together in a 10 mL DMF solvent system. After obtaining a uniform blue-green precursor solution through ultrasound assisted dissolution, it was transferred to a 25 mL PTFE lined high-pressure reactor and maintained in a 120 °C oven for 8 h for hydrothermal reaction. After the reaction system is naturally cooled to room temperature, the blue octahedral crystal product is collected by centrifugation. To obtain high-purity MOF-818 material, solvent exchange was performed three times each with DMF and acetone (soaking for 8 h each time, with fresh solvent replaced every 8 h), effectively removing residual unreacted substances and solvent molecules in the pores. Finally, the product was placed in a 50 °C vacuum drying oven and dried continuously for 12 h to obtain MOF-818 crystal material.

Carboxylation of MWCNT

This method was refined and optimized based on the approach reported in reference (Wang et al., 2025). Initially, 1.0 g of MWCNT was immersed in a 1:4 (v/v) mixture of concentrated nitric acid and sulfuric acid, followed by acid treatment at 80 °C for 6 h. Upon cooling of the system, the product was retrieved via vacuum filtration and washed repeatedly with ultrapure water until the filtrate reached neutral pH. Finally, the purified sample (MWCNT-COOH) is obtained through freeze-drying and subsequently preserved for further applications.

Preparation of aptamer/MWCNT-COOH/MOF-818/SPE

Disperse 10 mg of MOF-818 and 10 mg of MWCNT-COOH separately in 5 mL of 1 % acetic acid-chitosan solution, followed by ultrasonic treatment for 2 h to obtain stable suspensions. Employ a stepwise modification approach: initially, deposit 6 μL of MOF-818 suspension (5 mg/mL) onto the surface of a pretreated SPE electrode; after drying at ambient temperature, apply 6 μL of MWCNT-COOH suspension (8 mg/mL). Subsequently, immerse the modified electrode in 0.1 M PBS buffer (pH 7.0) containing 10.0 mM EDC/NHS and incubate at ambient temperature for 5 h to activate carboxyl groups, then thoroughly wash with PBS (pH 7.0) to remove residual reagents. Next, conduct a 12-h reaction at ambient temperature in PBS containing 50 mM allylamine to functionalize the electrode surface with alkene moieties.

The aptamer is pre-dissolved in PBS (pH 7.4) containing 1 mM TCEP and activated at ambient temperature for 30 min to reduce disulfide bonds. Then, add the photoinitiator Irgacure 2959 to a final concentration of 0.1 % (w/v). Apply 6 μL of this mixture onto the alkene-functionalized electrode surface, and irradiate with UV light for 15 min to initiate the thiol-ene click chemistry reaction, achieving covalent immobilization of the aptamer. Finally, gently rinse with PBS containing 0.05 % Tween-20 to remove unbound aptamer, thereby obtaining the Aptamer/MWCNT-COOH/MOF-818/SPE sensor.

Electrochemical measurement

Electrochemical characterization of the modified electrode (CV and EIS) was conducted in an electrolyte solution containing 5 mM [Fe(CN)6]3-/4- with 0.1 M KCl. The experimental parameters were as follows: for CV, the potential scan range was from 0 to +0.8 V; for EIS, impedance spectra were recorded at the open circuit potential across a frequency range from 1 Hz to 100 kHz, employing an AC excitation amplitude of 10 mV. Electrochemical detection of foodborne pathogenic bacteria was carried out at room temperature (25 ± 1 °C) using DPV in a PBS buffer. The parameters were set as follows: potential scan range from −0.6 V to +0.6 V, amplitude of 50 mV, pulse width of 50 ms, and sampling width of 16.7 ms.

Practical application

The pork utilized in the experiment was sourced from local markets in Chengdu, China, specifically for the quantitative detection of foodborne pathogens. The sample pretreatment protocol was optimized as follows (Dai et al., 2024): 50 g of sterilized pork paste was homogenized with 50 mL of sterile PBS buffer (10 mM, pH 7.4). The resulting suspension was centrifuged at 8000 rpm for 20 min to remove particulate matter, followed by filtration through a 0.2 μm membrane filter. The pretreated meat matrix was then inoculated with serially diluted standard suspensions of S. aureus, E. coli and S. typhimurium. Detection was carried out using the prepared Aptamer/MWCNT-COOH/MOF-818/SPE sensor, and simultaneous quantitative analysis was performed via the plate count method. The spiked recovery rate was subsequently calculated based on detection results obtained from both approaches.

Statistical analysis

Each experiment was conducted in triplicate, and statistical analysis was performed using the one-way ANOVA procedure in SPSS 17.0 software. The results were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). The figures were generated using OriginPro 2019b.

Characterization of modified materials

This experiment utilized SEM to characterize the surface morphology of the synthesized nanomaterials. This distinct morphology effectively enhances the electrode's specific surface area, thereby contributing to improved detection sensitivity. The surface of Aptamer/MWCNT-COOH/MOF-818/SPE (Fig. 1D) presents a continuous and dense modification layer, with the characteristic sulfur peak in the EDS spectrum (Fig. 1E and Fig. S1) confirming successful aptamer immobilization. As presented in Fig. S2A, the FT-IR spectra of MOF-818 and MWCNT-COOH exhibit significant distinctions. MOF-818 features a prominent carbonyl symmetric stretching vibration peak at 1650 cm−1, while characteristic peaks at 1550 cm−1 (O–C–O stretching vibration) and 660 cm−1 (O–Zr–O bond vibration) further confirm its structural integrity. For MWCNT-COOH, peaks at 1050 cm−1 (C–OH stretching vibration) and 1732 cm−1 (C=O stretching vibration) indicate successful carboxyl functionalization on the carbon nanotube surface, thereby verifying the effectiveness of the functionalization process. XRD analysis (Fig. S2B) reveals diffraction peaks of MOF-818 at 5.99°, 6.89°, and 7.62°, corresponding respectively to the (222), (400), and (331) crystal planes. No impurity peaks of CuO or ZrO2 are observed, indicating successful synthesis of high-purity MOF-818. In the MWCNT-COOH/MOF-818 composite, characteristic diffraction peaks of MOF-818 remain predominant; however, the intensity of the peak near 7° decreases while the peak at 26° is enhanced, likely attributable to the introduction of MWCNT-COOH disrupting the lattice order of the MOF. Collectively, the FT-IR and XRD results substantiate the successful fabrication of the MOF-818 and MWCNT-COOH composite. Further confirmation of the successful synthesis of MOF-818 was obtained via XPS (Fig. S3A), where characteristic signals of Cu, C, and Zr are distinctly visible in the survey spectrum. In the Cu 2p spectrum, binding energy peaks at 931.9 eV (2p3/2) and 952.4 eV (2p1/2) indicate the presence of Cu+, while satellite peaks at 931.9 eV and 953.8 eV correspond to Cu2+ (Fig. S3B). The Zr 3d spectrum displays double peaks at 180.3 eV (3d3/2) and 182.5 eV (3d5/2), attributed to Zr4+ (Fig. S3C). These findings, consistent with FT-IR and XRD analyses, corroborate the precise chemical structure and compositional features of MOF-818 (M. Li et al., 2020). In addition, the specific surface area of MOF-818 can be determined by a nitrogen adsorption-desorption experiment conducted at 77 K. As shown in Fig. S4, the specific surface area of MOF-818 reaches 1218.3 m2/g. Such a high specific surface area endows MOF-818 with great application potential as an electrode substrate.

Electrochemical evaluation of aptamer/MWCNT-COOH/MOF-818/SPE

This study utilized the potassium ferricyanide/ferrocyanide ([Fe(CN)6]3-/4-) redox couple as an electrochemical probe for systematic electrochemical characterization. The CV data (Fig. 2A) demonstrate a pronounced decrease in current response at the MOF-818/SPE (curve b), attributable to the inherently insulating nature of the metal-organic framework material. Importantly, the incorporation of MWCNT-COOH substantially enhances the redox peak currents (curve c), thereby evidencing the superior charge transfer capabilities of carbon nanomaterials. The formation of a composite structure composed of MWCNT-COOH and MOF-818 on the SPE surface results in a synergistic effect that sharply increases the current density (curve d). This enhancement is primarily ascribed to: (1) the three-dimensional network architecture of MWCNT-COOH effectively mitigating the aggregation of MOF-818; and (2) a considerable augmentation in the specific surface area and the density of electroactive sites of the composite material. Optimization of experimental conditions

In this study, a light-activated thiol-ene click chemistry strategy was implemented to achieve the oriented immobilization of aptamers on the MWCNT-COOH/MOF-818/SPE sensing interface. Initially, allylamine molecules were utilized to functionalize the composite electrode surface with vinyl groups, thereby constructing a reactive vinyl interface. Concurrently, disulfide bonds at the aptamer termini were reduced via TCEP treatment to fully expose thiol functional groups. Under UV irradiation, covalent coupling between the vinyl groups on the interface and aptamer thiols was realized through a conventional free radical chain reaction mechanism: the photoinitiator Irgacure 2959, upon excitation at a specific wavelength, generated sulfur radicals (RS·). These reactive intermediates preferentially attacked the vinyl double bonds to form carbon-centered radical intermediates, which then underwent chain transfer reactions to complete anti-Markovnikov addition, ultimately forming a stable thioether crosslinked network.

To guarantee efficient formation of the molecular recognition interface, the effects of photoinitiator concentration and reaction duration on immobilization efficiency were systematically examined. As illustrated in Fig. S5A, increasing the photoinitiator concentration up to 0.10 % (w/v) maximized the electrochemical response signal the peak current change (ΔI). This enhancement was attributed to multiple stabilization mechanisms of Irgacure 2959 molecules via hydrogen bonding, electrostatic, and hydrophobic interactions. However, exceeding this threshold concentration induced side reactions due to excess photosensitizer, leading to radical quenching. Further investigations revealed that an irradiation time of 15 min achieved optimal aptamer loading (Fig. S5B), yielding a three-dimensional molecular recognition layer with moderate crosslinking density on the electrode surface. This equilibrium ensured sufficient spatial freedom for aptamers while maintaining the characteristic high specific surface area of the MWCNT-COOH/MOF-818 composite. Prolonged irradiation beyond 15 min caused excessive crosslinking, resulting in micro- and nano-structural collapse and obstruction of charge transport pathways. Considering these factors, the thiol-ene “click” reaction in this work was performed using 0.10 % (w/v) photoinitiator concentration and a 15-min irradiation period.

Moreover, to optimize detection performance of the Aptamer/MWCNT-COOH/MOF-818/SPE sensor, five key experimental parameters were systematically evaluated: buffer pH, MOF-818 loading amount, MWCNT-COOH concentration, PBS ionic strength, and detection time (Fig. S5C–G).

The acid-base environment of the buffer significantly influenced the electrochemical process. Within the pH range of 4.0–7.0, ΔI increased markedly with rising pH, attributed to the favorable promotion of redox processes of electroactive species under appropriate pH conditions. When pH exceeded 7.0, ΔI declined, likely due to attenuated specific binding between aptamers and target molecules under alkaline conditions. Therefore, based on a comprehensive consideration of electrochemical activity and bio-recognition efficiency, pH 7.0 was selected as the optimal working condition. The quantity of composite material was critical for electron transfer. By precisely controlling the amounts of MOF-818 and MWCNT-COOH (Fig. S5D and E) and measuring the corresponding DPV responses, electron transfer was optimized. An ultrathin composite film effectively reduced signal shielding caused by nanoparticle aggregation while constructing a three-dimensional conductive network, thereby enhancing electron transfer efficiency. Nonetheless, excessive modification resulted in coverage of active sites, diminishing electron transfer efficiency. As shown in Fig. S5D and S5E, the maximum current signal was achieved with MOF-818 at 5 mg/mL and MWCNT-COOH at 8 mg/mL. The sensitivity of the Aptamer/MWCNT-COOH/MOF-818/SPE sensor depended on the PBS concentration; thus, PBS ionic strength was optimized. As shown in Fig. S5F, ΔI peaked at 0.1 M PBS, likely because the appropriate ionic strength maintained aptamer structural stability and facilitated diffusion and mass transfer of target molecules. Accordingly, 0.1 M was determined to be the optimal detection concentration. Finally, using Staphylococcus aureus detection as a model, the influence of detection time on sensor performance was investigated. As shown in Fig. S5G, ΔI plateaued after 8 min, indicating stable formation of bio-recognition complexes on the electrode surface. Although extending the incubation time slightly enhanced capture efficiency, it also prolonged the detection cycle and potentially induced nonspecific adsorption. Therefore, 8 min was selected as the optimal parameter balancing detection efficiency and sensitivity.

The specificity, reproducibility, repeatability, and stability of aptamer/MWCNT-COOH/MOF-818/SPE

This study systematically evaluated the sensing performance of Aptamer/MWCNT-COOH/MOF-818/SPE for the detection of foodborne pathogenic bacteria. In specificity validation experiments, eight representative pathogens—S. aureus, E. coli, S. typhimurium, E. sakazakii, S. derby, L. monocytogenes, V. parahaemolyticus and Shigella—each at a controlled concentration of 105 CFU mL−1, were subjected to electrochemical testing. As illustrated in Fig. 5A, the sensor elicited a significant current response exclusively upon detecting S. aureus, whereas the current change rates observed for the other four non-target strains exhibited no significant differences. This pronounced selectivity arises from the aptamer's specific recognition mechanism directed at surface markers of the pathogen. Notably, parallel control experiments employing detection systems targeting E. coli and S. typhimurium also demonstrated stringent species specificity, thereby corroborating the molecular recognition reliability of the sensing interface. To further quantify the reproducibility of Aptamer/MWCNT-COOH/MOF-818/SPE, three batches of electrodes (n = 3) were fabricated under standardized procedures. Each batch underwent six repeated measurements with the S. aureus standard solution (105 CFU mL−1). Statistical analysis yielded relative standard deviations (RSD) of 4.7 %, 3.5 %, and 4.1 % for current responses across batches (Fig. 5B), indicating satisfactory reproducibility in electrode fabrication. For assessment of repeatability, five independently prepared sensor groups were tested for inter-batch verification, producing an RSD of 3.2 % (Fig. 5C), thus confirming excellent repeatability. In stability assessments, the encapsulated sensors maintained stable current outputs throughout a 20-day observation period, with measurements recorded every 4 days. Conclusions

This study successfully established a highly sensitive, fully integrated one-stop electrochemical rapid detection platform for the prompt identification of foodborne pathogens. The principal innovation of this sensor resides in the precise fabrication of a hierarchical nano-composite interface: initially, a metal-organic framework material, MOF-818, characterized by a three-dimensional porous architecture, is utilized as the substrate support, capitalizing on its high specific surface area to augment biomolecule loading capacity. Subsequently, MWCNT-COOH are assembled with MOF-818 to create a nano-heterostructure that markedly enhances charge transfer efficiency. Employing a thiol-ene click chemistry approach, specific aptamers are covalently anchored onto the MWCNT-COOH/MOF-818 composite interface, ensuring oriented immobilization and high-density bioprobe distribution, thereby facilitating precise recognition of target pathogens. Experimental results reveal detection limits of 3, 6, and 4 CFU mL−1 for S. aureus, E. coli and S. typhimurium, respectively. The sensor demonstrates excellent specificity, reproducibility, repeatability, and stability, delivering robust analytical performance in practical applications. This integrated detection strategy, which amalgamates sample recognition and signal transduction within a single interface, overcomes the limitations inherent to conventional multi-step detection protocols and is well-suited for on-site rapid screening settings. The innovative material design and interface functionalization approach provide critical technical support for the advancement of next-generation food safety monitoring devices, highlighting broad application prospects in preventing foodborne disease outbreaks and safeguarding public health.

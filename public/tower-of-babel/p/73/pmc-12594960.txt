Precise indoor localization using a lightweight 2D-CNN with adaptive temperature guided iterative self-knowledge distillation
Authors: Muhammad Rizwan, Yin Hoe Ng, Hin-Yong Wong, Chee Keong Tan, Tonni Agustiono Kurniawan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Fingerprint-based indoor localization leveraging Wi-Fi and Bluetooth received signal strength stands as a prominent infrastructure-less positioning technique. While positioning accuracy is essential, achieving it under practical deployment constraints remains a key challenge. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), recognized for their robust pattern recognition capabilities, hold the potential to significantly enhance indoor positioning accuracy. However, the inherent architectural complexity of deep CNNs restricts their deployment on resource-constrained devices. Knowledge distillation (KD) offers a viable strategy by transferring of knowledge from a complex model to a simpler, more efficient one. This study proposes a novel lightweight 2D CNN architecture integrating squeeze-and-excitation (SE) modules with an adaptive temperature guided iterative self-knowledge distillation (SKD). The SE mechanism dynamically recalibrates CNN filter responses, prioritizing those capturing salient features, while the iterative SKD progressively refines the model’s performance during the training process. Unlike conventional KD approaches necessitating distinct teacher and student models, our proposed technique employs a single lightweight model, significantly reduces computational overhead. Empirical evaluation on the HDLC public datasets demonstrates that our architecture, without the incorporation of SKD, yields a notable 8.32% improvement in positioning accuracy over conventional CNNs, achieving a 3D average positioning error (APE) of 2.60 m. Furthermore, the integration of the iterative SKD strategy further enhances the positioning accuracy to 1.66%, resulting in a 3D APE of 2.24 m. These findings underscore the efficacy of the proposed framework as a resource-efficient and practical solution for accurate indoor localization, facilitating its real-time implementation on resource-constrained platforms.

Introduction

The ability to accurately locate individuals within indoor environment, provided by Indoor positioning systems (IPS), is a critical component for disaster management1, emergency response2, mixed reality3, and autonomous unmanned aerial vehicles4,5. Global Navigation Satellite Systems (GNSS), which provide accurate positioning in outdoor settings, are constrained in indoor environments by the prevalence of non-line-of-sight (NLOS) conditions, rendering them ineffective within building structures. Given these constraints, IPS becomes essential for ensuring precise navigation, enabling location-based services and supporting robust emergency evacuation protocols within large commercial spaces, such as shopping malls, airports, and hospitals. Indoor environments are inherently more complex than outdoor settings due to their highly dynamic nature and restricted signal propagation through NLOS areas. To address these challenges, radio frequency (RF)-based technologies, notably Wi-Fi and Bluetooth6, offer a cost-effective and accurate approach without demanding extensive infrastructural deployments. While RF data collected in these environments demonstrates unique positional signatures, the inherent complexity of these patterns renders traditional modeling approaches ineffective. Consequently, machine learning and deep learning techniques have become crucial for accurate representation and analysis.

Deep learning models, particularly convolutional neural networks (CNNs), have demonstrated exceptional capabilities in recognizing and differentiating these localization patterns. Despite their effectiveness, conventional CNNs suffer from limitations, including suboptimal filter selection and reduced generalization, thereby hindering their performance. Additionally, their significant computational demands restrict real-time deployment. To address these limitations, this study introduces a novel lightweight CNN architecture that integrates squeeze-and-excitation (SE) blocks7 and iterative self-knowledge distillation (SKD) with adaptive temperature hyperparameter. The SE blocks dynamically prioritize channel weights, enhancing the model’s ability to generalize by emphasizing significant features. Meanwhile, the iterative SKD ensures the model achieves comparatively better performance, surpassing the constraints of traditional training approaches. This innovative combination enables superior indoor positioning accuracy while addressing computational and scalability challenges.

Related work

Continuous research efforts have been dedicated to enhancing indoor localization accuracy, employing both RF device modifications and the development of sophisticated deep learning model. This study contributes to this progression by proposing a novel approach in training indoor localization models based on deep learning models. Leveraging the strong feature learning capabilities of CNNs, this study reviews existing deep learning models designed to overcome the challenges posed by dynamic environments8,9, received signal strength (RSS) fluctuations10, and model complexity, ultimately enhancing system performance.

Conventional deep learning methods for indoor positioning

Conventional CNNs are widely employed by researchers, often integrated with novel approaches which include unsupervised learning, mathematical formulation, and hybrid deep learning architecture, to improve indoor positioning accuracy. Liu et al.11 developed a hybrid wireless fingerprint (FP)-based CNN model designed to mitigate the performance degradation resulting from fluctuation in the receiver signal strength indicator (RSSI). This approach employs a methodology wherein the ratio of extracted RSSI fingerprints to raw RSSI fingerprints is calculated, facilitating the annotation of noise while enhancing the amplification of critical signals. In12, Xudong Song et al. advanced the field of localization by developing CNNLoc, a model that leverages the strength of both unsupervised stack autoencoder (SAE) model and the supervised CNN architecture. Specifically, an unsupervised SAE is employed to extract relevant features from sparse RSS data, which are subsequently processed by a supervised CNN to optimize localization accuracy. A CNN model, trained on a dataset collected using 144 Bluetooth devices across the second, third and fourth floors of a Chinese hospital was developed by Danshi Sun et al.13. This study adopted a hybrid approach, integrating RF and magnetic field-based technologies, resulting in an average floor accuracy of 91.66% and an average positioning error (APE) of 1.526 m. A combined extreme learning machine autoencoder (EM-AE) and 2D CNN approach was proposed by Atefe Alitaleshi et al.14, with the aim to reduce the training time by utilizing the advanced neural network random weights selection technique instead of backpropagation. Mohammad Nabati and Seyed Ali Ghorashi15 introduced a deep neural network with a novel state-based positioning method, which tracks previous and current states to minimize drift and facilitate movement direction detection.

Recognizing the inherent fluctuations in RSSI data, the researcher advocated for the inclusion of temporal variation, allowing the system to learn the dynamic characteristics of the practical environment. Qianqian Ren et al.16 proposed a five-layered model, including path FP construction, positional embedding, CNN, fingerprint attention, and fully connected layers. Incorporating temporal dimensions via a sliding window approach for sample preprocessing allows the model to learn signal variation, leading to an improved performance. In17, Lixing Wang et al. introduce a temporal convolution network (TCN) for indoor localization which processes 1D RSS vector to extract spatial and temporal variations, claiming to be computationally less complex though its performance remains unevaluated. Besides, Kodai Tasaki et al.18, introduce a 3D CNN model for indoor localization that explicitly has third dimension to extract the RSSI temporal variations.

While conventional deep learning architectures, including CNNs integrated with unsupervised learning, mathematical formulations, and hybrid designs, have shown great potential in enhancing indoor positioning accuracy by addressing issues like RSSI fluctuations and leveraging temporal information, their inherent high computational resource demands pose significant limitations for real-time deployment on resource-constrained devices19. Consequently, the exploration and development of lightweight deep learning methodologies that can maintain or improve positioning accuracy with reduced computational overhead remains a critical research gap for practical indoor localization systems.

Lightweight deep learning methods for indoor positioning

Addressing the trade-off between computational efficiency and model performance inherent in conventional approaches, researchers investigated the development of a lightweight deep learning model. Several methodologies exist for the reduction of a model’s computational complexity, including quantization20–22, pruning23–26, low-rank factorization27, and KD28. KD has demonstrated comparatively superior performance among various complexity reduction techniques29,30. In general, to reduce the complexity of CNNs, KD is frequently implemented, employing a teacher-student model paradigm31. This process involves first training the teacher model (TM), a more complex model than the student model (SM), to attain the desired performance for indoor localization. Following this, the SM is trained utilizing both the original dataset and the pre-trained TM.

Aqillah et al.32 asserted the pioneering of a 2D KD-CNN for IPS, which was tested on the self-collected dataset of 253 reference points (RPs) from the 2nd and 3rd floors of the building, using 16 Bluetooth devices as access points (APs). RKD was subsequently used to train the two SMs exhibiting different computational complexities. The attained test accuracy for the TM and two SMs are 58.88%, 48.62%, and 34.00%, respectively. Whereas, the two of the SMs trained using KD attain the test accuracy of 52.62% and 40.00%, respectively. In a significant extension of their work in IPS, Aqilah et al.33 introduced a teacher-assistant (TA) KD CNN. Instead of directly distilling the knowledge from teacher to student, it uses an assistant model in between, which is less complex than the TM but more complex than the SM. Evidently, the TAKD-CNN model performed much better than that of conventional KD-CNN.

Limitation of conventional and light-weight deep learning methods for indoor positioning

Conventional CNN architectures are susceptible to overfitting and increased computational overhead due to the presence of excessive filters trained for feature extraction. On the other hand, inadequate filter allocation in the architecture can hinder the model’s capacity to discern input data features. Consequently, a careful selection of filters or an appropriate optimization strategy must be employed to address this challenge. Furthermore, the inherent algorithmic complexity of conventional CNNs raises concerns regarding their real-world applicability, as their implementation is often constrained to devices possessing significant computational resources.

Lightweight models, while addressing the issue of complexity, typically incur a performance penalty. Although KD achieves better outcomes compared to other lightweight models, several important gaps remain unaddressed. First, research on KD for fingerprint-based IPS is very limited, with only a few studies exploring KD-based approaches in this domain32,34. Second, existing studies on KD-based IPS predominantly rely on offline teacher–student frameworks, where a large TM supervises the training of a smaller SM. Such approaches suffer from multiple shortcomings: there is no systematic method for selecting appropriate teacher and student models, nor a clear strategy to ensure compatibility between them. As a result, the SM tends to underperform compared with the TM. Even in teacher-assistant frameworks that attempts to reduce this gap by introducing multiple intermediate models, the improvement remains limited while training complexity increases substantially33. Third, KD inherently introduces two additional hyperparameters, namely temperature and loss weight, that require manual tuning. Finally, while recent work hints at the potential benefits of adopting adaptive mechanisms, such as dynamically tuning the temperature parameter, the advantages of such strategies in IPS have not yet been studied. Therefore, a more refined solution is necessitated, one that obviates the need for a dual-model architecture by leveraging self-distillation in a single model capability to ensure an effective balance between computational efficiency and performance.

Motivation and contributions

This work is motivated by the persistent challenges of CNN-based indoor positioning systems (IPS), where excessive filters increase computational costs, lightweight models often sacrifice accuracy, and conventional knowledge distillation requires dual-model architectures that lack systematic guidelines for selecting suitable teacher–student pairs and ensuring their compatibility, leading to ineffective knowledge transfer. The objective of this study is to develop a novel, resource-efficient, and accurate IPS framework that overcomes these limitations by leveraging a single-model self-distillation architecture capable of progressively refining feature representations and adaptively improving performance. Knowledge distillation model for indoor localization

To alleviate the computational limitations, Hinton et al.35 introduced the KD technique which employs two distinct models: a computationally intensive TM, and comparatively less complex SM.

Initially, the TM is trained and optimized on a given dataset to achieve optimal performance. Subsequently, the SM is trained on the same dataset. During SM training, the pre-trained TM is employed to distill acquired knowledge, enabling the SM to achieve performance exceeding that of a model trained without the KD. The implementation of KD introduces two hyperparameter, ϕ and α, as shown in Eqs. (11) and (12), respectively. Proper adjustment of these two hyperparameters is essential; otherwise, the distillation process may not achieve optimal effectiveness. The selection of suitable hyperparameter values is typically accomplished through heuristic trial-and-error approach.

Conventionally, KD from the TM to SM is achieved through three primary methodologies: response-based, feature-based, and relation-based, as illustrated in Fig. 3. Response-based KD (RKD) distills information at the classification layer, following the prediction generation by the TM. Conversely, feature-based KD (FKD) transfers information prior to the fully connected layers, specifically the convolutional layer in this context. Relation-based KD, in contrast, distills information across multiple layers, including the input, convolutional, and classification layers. Each distillation pathway necessitates the adjustments for hyperparameters ϕ and α. Furthermore, the efficacy of these approaches is context-dependent; the optimal method varies according to the architectural configuration of the model36.

While conventional KD has demonstrably improved model efficiency and performance, its practical applicability is hampered by several intrinsic limitations. Firstly, the fundamental requirement of maintaining two distinct models (a large, complex TM and a smaller SM), necessitates significant computational resources, particularly memory and processing power, during the extensive training phase. Secondly, the process of empirically determining optimal distillation hyperparameters, such as the temperature parameter and loss weights, demands considerable experimentation and time investment, often involving multiple training iterations. Furthermore, the architectural scalability of the student model is inherently restricted by the conventional KD training paradigm, which typically mandates a training protocol closely aligned with that of the teacher model to facilitate effective knowledge transfer. Lastly, certain IPS approaches33 that explore the use of multiple teacher models to guide a single student model, while potentially yielding performance gains, exacerbate the computational overhead and introduce increased response latency, thereby hindering real-time deployment on resource-constrained platforms.

Proposed methods

Self knowledge distillation in convolutional neural network for indoor localization

This study proposes a SKD framework to address conventional KD-based IPS limitations. In this framework, SKD utilizes a single model alternates between functioning as the teacher and the student, as shown in Fig. 4. This is achieved through the incorporation of an outer loop across training epochs, guaranteeing that each training iteration, i, achieves optimal performance before progressing to the next iteration, i + 1.

Within this framework, the model from iteration i serves as the TM for the subsequent iteration i + 1, while the SM is reinitialized and trained from scratch. At iteration i = 0, no distillation is performed, as the TM is trained in this initial phase. Starting from i = 1, KD is implemented, wherein the TM from the previous iteration functions as the guiding model for the SM’s training. The total number of iterations, itotal, is determined by the performance convergence of the SM; specifically, training ceases when no further improvement is observed in subsequent iterations. It is noteworthy that superior performance of the TM relative to the SM is not a prerequisite, as evidenced in36, a finding corroborated by our study.

In addition, the tuning process of the α hyperparameter in the SKD framework was systematically performed. At iteration i0, the model was first trained independently and used as a teacher at iteration i1. A new student was trained from scratch while α was varied. Result and discussion

Performance analysis

This subsection is dedicated to evaluating the performance of the proposed model against existing state-of-the-art techniques based on the performance metrics previously outlined. The results, presented in Tables 2, 3 and 4, show the test accuracies obtained for iterations i0 through i3 for all the models and datasets considered in this study. For iteration i0, conventional training is performed without KD, as described previously. The best test accuracy obtained using iterative SKD is denoted as ib. Subsequent iterations utilize the iterative SKD approach. The training process for each model concludes when performance saturation is achieved, indicated by the lack of improvement despite learning rate reductions.

As shown in Table 2, the proposed adaptive temperature-guided iterative SKD technique consistently outperforms the non-distilled baseline in terms of localization test accuracy on the HDLC dataset across all models considered, achieving 1.26–5.21% higher test accuracy. Tables 3 and 4 further demonstrate similar performance trend, with SKD technique attaining 0.03–1.01% and 0.91–2.23% higher test accuracy compared to the baseline for UJIIndoorLoc and UJILIB datasets, respectively. The superior performance of the adaptive temperature guided iterative SKD models stems from their ability to learn from softer, more informative target distributions across multiple iterations, enabled by the dynamic adjustment of temperature, which reduces the sharpness gap between the teacher and student. In contrast, the non-distilled baseline models rely solely on hard labels, which fail to effectively capture inter-class similarity.

Table 2 also shows that the proposed CNN model incorporating BN, 30% dropout, and a SE block yields the highest test accuracy of 43.32% for the HDLC dataset. As evidenced in Table 3, this model also achieves the lowest test loss of 3.96. These results highlight the model’s robustness and its effectiveness in mitigating overfitting. Table 3 further reveals that the proposed architecture achieves superior positioning performance in terms of floor accuracy, 2D and 3D APEs compared to the vanilla CNN. Although the CNN model with 10% dropout attains perfect floor accuracy of 100% and slightly lower 2D and 3D APEs, approximately 1.7% and 9.0%, respectively, better than its counterpart with BN, 30% dropout, and a SE block, the higher test loss and inconsistent performance across other metrics diminish the overall reliability of the model.

Table 4 extends the analysis to the UJIIndoorLoc dataset. The proposed model again demonstrates superior performance, achieving a peak accuracy of 69.48%, compared to 65.81% achieved by the vanilla CNN. Notably, the improvements persist across each iteration, highlighting the benefits of iterative SKD and adaptive temperature tuning. This model also exhibits strong generalization capability, as evidenced by its performance gains across all test iterations and competitive accuracy levels in building and floor classification. Table 5 validates the proposed model on UJIIndoorLoc, recording the lowest test loss of 1.32, the highest floor accuracy of 97.50%, and the most competitive 2D APE of 3.04 m.

A similar performance trend is evident on the UJILIB dataset. As illustrated in Table 6, the proposed model achieves a peak test accuracy of 62.80% at iteration i3, surpassing the baseline accuracy of 59.13% by 3.67%. Table 7 demonstrates that the proposed model outperforms the other techniques by attaining the lowest test loss of 1.10, the highest floor accuracy of 99.98%, and best 2D APE of 1.33 m. Although the overall accuracy is slightly lower than that of UJIIndoorLoc, the consistent performance improvement through iterative SKD underlines the robustness of the proposed framework across diverse indoor environments.

Precision analysis and MCNEMAR’S test

The precision analysis of the proposed model against the benchmark is conducted with the performance evaluation at four distinct error margins, i.e., 1 m, 2 m, 2.5 m, and 3 m. Uncertainty intervals with a 95% confidence rate are also determined, as illustrated in Fig. 6. The results indicate an inverse relationship between classification complexity and error margin size, with larger error margin yielding improved performance because wider error margins effectively consolidate multiple RPs into single classification units. The 1 m spacing between RPs in the dataset implies that a 1 m error margin necessitates a fine-grained classification task, treating only RPs within a 1 m radius as a single class. However, increasing the error margins to, 2 m, 2.5 m, and 3 m, leads to inclusion of more RPs within each class, effectively smoothing the classification boundaries and improving the model accuracy. This outcome demonstrates the interplay between the error margins and RP distributions within the localization accuracy.

Computational analysis

In addition to performance evaluation, a computational analysis is conducted to assess the resources demands of the models. For this analysis, model complexity is systematically increased by doubling and quadrupling the number of filters. Table 9 presents the test accuracy of the models under consideration, both with and without the utilization of iterative SKD across varying levels of complexity. The results indicate that augmenting the number of filters in the model generally leads to better performance than the lighter models, however the gains are less substantial than those achieved through the implementation of SKD. Furthermore, the SKD significantly enhances the performance of computationally complex models to great extent. Nevertheless, the increased computational overhead of these larger models may pose limitations in real-time deployment scenarios.

Figure 8 provides a comparative analysis of each of the model’s FLOPs, test accuracy, computation time, and number of parameters. As shown in Fig. 8, an increase in FLOPs generally correlates with improved performance relative to the baseline with highest number of filters, enabling the model to learn and process more complex patterns. However, this improvement is not uniformly proportional across different CNN variants. For less complex models, such as the vanilla CNN and CNN with 10% dropout, a moderate increase in FLOPs leads to notable accuracy gains of 6.4% and 6.45%, respectively, while maintaining computational efficiency with minimal impact on inference time. This demonstrates the effective use of additional computational resources in these simpler architectures.

However, for models incorporating SE blocks and BN, the correlation between increased FLOPs and proportional accuracy gains is not consistently observed. Specifically, the SE block model demonstrates only a 0.94% improvement in accuracy despite a higher FLOP count, indicating a prioritization of refined feature selection over broad generalization. Similarly, the proposed model, while exhibiting higher computational efficiency, (1.09 times faster than the baseline), achieves a modest 0.55% accuracy improvement, reflecting a design focus on balancing generalization and computational cost. These observations suggest that while increased FLOPs can contribute to performance enhancement, excessive computational complexity may lead to diminishing returns, particularly when feature refinement is prioritized over generalization.

Ablation study

Figure 9 presents the ablation study and provides insight into the effects of different architectural components. Three key observations can be made. First, the combination of BN, dropout, and SE blocks, as implemented in models 12–14, perform well and almost similarly across all three models. This configuration achieves the highest accuracy at APE ≤ 1 m and nearly perfect floor classification accuracy (0.99–1.0), confirming its effectiveness for precise, floor-level localization tasks. Second, the mean APE results demonstrate that the placement and intensity of regularization significantly influence overall localization error. Applying dropout after the convolutional blocks (model 2) yields the lowest mean 3D APE, while combining SE blocks and higher dropout (model 10) minimizes the mean 2D APE. These findings suggest that combining dropout positioning with SE blocks exerts a greater influence on APE reduction than BN alone. Third, the variant using BN without complementary modules (model 5) exhibits a clear increase in both 2D and 3D APEs, indicating that BN must be paired with dropout or SE blocks to prevent performance degradation. These findings confirm that the configuration with BN, dropout, and SE provides the most robust performance and reveal how individual components and their placement influence different localization metrics.

The contribution of each architectural component can also be evaluated independently. Dropout, particularly at moderate levels (20–30%), effectively reduces overfitting and enhances generalization, though excessively high dropout can destabilize training. BN accelerates convergence and improves consistency across training iterations, thereby leading to more stable accuracy. SE blocks play a crucial role by recalibrating feature responses, reducing both 2D and 3D APEs by approximately 0.3 m to 0.5 m compared to variants without SE. While each component individually contributes to performance gains, their combined integration produces the most substantial improvements, confirming the synergistic effect of the combined architecture.

The influence of the distillation weight α on model performance is illustrated in Fig. 10. The results reveal a clear trade-off between α values and model accuracy. The highest accuracy of 0.4311 is achieved at α equal to 0.15, which decreases to 0.4224 at α equal to 0.20, 0.4000 at α equal to 0.25, and 0.3818 at α equal to 0.30. This trend indicates that smaller α values strike a better balance between the teacher model’s soft targets and the hard ground-truth labels, enabling effective guidance without overfitting to teacher predictions. In contrast, greater α values bias the optimization toward teacher outputs, which reduces generalization and degrades positional accuracy. Therefore, α = 0.15 is selected as the optimal setting, as it consistently delivers the best trade-off between accuracy and training stability.

In summary, the ablation study quantitatively validates the design rationale of the proposed framework. The joint use of BN, dropout, and SE blocks yields measurable improvements across all accuracy and error metrics, while systematic tuning of α ensures optimal knowledge transfer during self-distillation. Collectively, these results demonstrate the robustness, generalization ability, and practical applicability of the proposed model for challenging indoor positioning tasks.

Conclusion

This study presents a novel 2D CNN architecture for IPS, incorporating SE blocks to adaptively select filters that contribute most significantly to feature extraction from the input data. An ablation study is conducted to elucidate the significance of various architectural parameters in achieving a deep learning model that generalizes well. Additionally, SKD with an adaptive temperature mechanism is implemented to achieve high accuracy while maintaining a lightweight and efficient model architecture.

However, certain limitations of this work should be noted. The model’s reliance on labeled training data may present a scalability challenge for larger or more complex datasets. Furthermore, while the lightweight nature of the model is advantageous, further optimization for deployment on edge devices with severely constrained resources remains a topic for future investigation.

Future research directions include exploration of domain adaptation techniques to enhance the model’s robustness across varied indoor settings. Additionally, extending the proposed framework to incorporate semi-supervised or unsupervised learning methodologies could mitigate the reliance on labeled data. Finally, expanding the study to include a broader spectrum of sensor data and experimentation with multi-modal fusion techniques may further enhance performance and applicability in real-world scenarios.

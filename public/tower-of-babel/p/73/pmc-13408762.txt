Vaccinia virus and Monkeypox virus neutralizing and antigen-binding antibodies following subcutaneous and reduced intradermal doses of an MVA vaccine
Authors: Moss Bernard, Catherine A. Cotter, Jeffrey L. Americo, Maxinne A. Ignacio, Jocelyn E. Bassler, Sharon E. Frey, John H. Beigel
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
To expand the supply of the modified vaccinia Ankara (MVA) smallpox/mpox vaccine, fractional doses administered intradermally (ID) have been considered as alternatives to the standard dose given subcutaneously (SC). In a recent clinical trial, participants received the standard 108 infectious units of vaccine SC or a one-fifth (IDH) or a one-tenth (IDL) dose ID twice 28-days apart. Two weeks after the second dose, sera were analyzed using a vaccinia virus (VACV) strain Western Reserve plaque-reduction test (PRNT) with the conclusion that IDH but not IDL was non-inferior to SC. The present study extends the latter by quantifying neutralizing antibodies to the MVA vaccine and to monkeypox virus (MPXV) in the absence and presence of added complement and by assessing multiplex binding antibodies to individual VACV antigens (A27, A33, B5, D8 and L1) and the MPXV homologs (A29, A35, B6, E8 and M1). The main conclusions of the study are that the titers of MVA neutralizing antibodies induced by SC and IDH were not significantly different, whereas MVA neutralizing antibody titers induced by IDL were lower like the previous PRNT results. However, the titers of complement-enhanced MPXV neutralizing antibodies induced by SC were significantly higher than for both IDH and IDL immunizations, suggesting that the standard SC dose and reduced dose ID immunizations may not be equivalent. Additional analyses suggested that antibody binding to VACV B5 and D8 and MPXV B6 and E8 may serve as surrogates for neutralizing antibodies.

Introduction

Monkeypox virus (MPXV) and variola virus (VARV), the causative agents of mpox and smallpox, respectively, are related orthopoxviruses (OPXVs) with different epidemiologies. Mpox is a human disease that resembles smallpox in clinical presentation, although it is less severe and transmissible [1]. Whereas smallpox is human-specific and has been eradicated, mpox is a zoonosis that is increasing in incidence [2]. There are two distinct clades of MPXV: clade I is predominant in Central Africa and causes more severe disease than clade II, which is predominant in West Africa [3]. Infections with both clades are increasing: an upsurge of a subclade IIb variant occurred in Nigeria in 2017 and spread globally by human transmission in 2022 and 2023 resulting in more than 100,000 cases [2]. In 2023 to 2024 an outbreak of a subclade Ib variant involving human transmission occurred in the Democratic Republic of the Congo and infected travelers were found outside of Africa [4].

Members of the OPXV genus are closely related antigenically accounting for the successful use of vaccinia virus (VACV) to serve as the vaccine for smallpox eradication. Although VACV vaccines such as DryVax provided durable cross-protective immunity, uncommon but severe side-effects particularly in the immunocompromised encouraged development of safer strains. One particularly attenuated strain, modified VACV Ankara (MVA), is defective in replication in human cells and has been licensed under various names (Imvanex in the EU, Imvamune in Canada and Jynneos in the US) for prophylaxis of both smallpox and mpox. Licensure of the MVA vaccine was based on OPXV protection experiments in animals and clinical trials that showed non-inferiority of the peak anti-VACV strain Western Reserve (WR) neutralizing antibody response after two subcutaneous (SC) doses of MVA compared to a single percutaneous dose of the previous VACV vaccine [5]. The global incidence of mpox provided the opportunity to field test the MVA vaccine. One and two vaccinations provided 36% to 75% and 66% to 89% effectiveness in preventing mpox caused by the clade IIb strain, respectively [6–8]. Anti-MPXV neutralizing antibody was low and short lived [9–11].

Limited supply of vaccine [12] led the U.S. Food and Drug Administration to issue Emergency Use Authorization for intradermal (ID) administration of a one-fifth dose of Jynneos for individuals 18 years of age and older at high risk of MPXV infection as an alternative to the standard subcutaneous (SC) dose and this is also being tested in Sweden [13]. The ID dose was selected based on earlier vaccine studies [14,15]. Lowering the SC dose is not recommended as a 10-fold reduction diminishes the anti-VACV neutralizing response [14].

A recent Phase 2 clinical trial (NCT05512949) sought to further extend dose sparing by evaluating participants, ages 18 to 50 with no history of OPXV infection or vaccination, receiving the standard dose of approximately 108 infectious units administered by the SC route and 5- to 10-fold lower doses (2 × 107 and 1 × 107 infectious units) by the ID route [16]. Participants received 2 doses of the vaccine 28 days apart and sera collected at intervals were analyzed by a VACV WR plaque reduction neutralization test (PRNT). Based on the titers at 2 weeks after the boost, the immune response to the 5-fold but not the 10-fold reduction was judged to be non-inferior to the standard dose.

The present analysis of sera from a subset of participants from the above clinical trial was undertaken primarily to extend the comparison of route and dosage of vaccine to determine neutralizing antibodies to MVA and MPXV as well as to individual VACV and MPXV antigens.

Results

VACV and MPXV neutralizing antibodies

Study participants were vaccinated twice at an interval of 28 days either by the SC route with the standard dose of 108 TCID50 (50% tissue-culture infectious dose) of Jynneos or with 5-fold (IDH) or 10-fold (IDL) reduced doses by the ID route (Fig. 1A). Sera from a random subset of participants from each cohort of the main trial was evaluated at days 1, 43 and 90 by a previously described flow cytometric assay [17], which quantifies antibodies that neutralize the infectivity of a virus that expresses green fluorescent protein (GFP). The anti-MVA geometric mean 50% neutralization titers (NT50) peaked on day 43 and declined by day 90 following SC, IDH and IDL immunizations (Fig. 1B).

The flow cytometry assay was also used to quantify antibodies that neutralized the highly virulent MPXV clade Ia strain isolated from an individual in Zaire in 1979 [18]and modified to express GFP [19]. In a previous study, we had found low MPXV neutralizing activity following two MVA immunizations of non-human primates [10]. In the present study, the geometric mean MPXV neutralization titers of the human sera were also low but significantly above pre-vaccination titers for the SC and IDH immunizations but not for the IDL immunization (Fig. 1C). Consistent with a previous study [20], we found that the MPXV neutralizing activity was increased severalfold by addition of guinea pig serum as a source of complement (Fig. 1D). With and without complement, the MPXV NT50 values peaked on day 43 and declined by day 90 (Fig. 1C, D). The peak anti-MVA and complement-enhanced anti-MPXV neutralization titers were positively correlated (Fig. 1E), particularly for the SC samples.

A high outlier was noted in one IDL pre-immune (day 1) serum sample tested for anti-MPXV neutralizing antibodies without and with complement (Fig. 1C, D) leading to a search for additional outliers by the Graphpad Prism ROUT method with Q = 1%. No outliers were identified with pre-immune SC samples tested for anti-MVA or anti-MPXV activities or with IDL or IDH sera tested for anti-MVA activity. However, outliers were identified with two IDH and two IDL samples that were tested for MPXV neutralization in the presence of complement and one of the same IDL samples tested for neutralization without complement. As all donors were VACV and MPXV naïve, the few high values likely represent artifacts rather than previous orthopoxvirus infections.

Comparisons of standard SC and reduced dose ID vaccinations on VACV- and MPXV-neutralizing antibodies

Like the main trial in which an anti-VACV WR PRNT was analyzed [16], we found that the difference in MVA neutralization on day 43 between SC and IDH was insignificant (P = 0.4405) whereas the difference between SC and IDL was significant (P = 0.0368) as shown in Fig. 1F. However, MPXV neutralization titers were significantly higher on day 43 in the SC subset as compared to either IDH (P = 0.0464) or IDL (P = 0.0380) in Fig. 1G.

VACV and MPXV Antigen Binding Antibodies.

As non-neutralizing antibodies also provide protection against disease following orthopoxvirus infection [21], we compared the levels of serum antibodies that bind to individual VACV (Fig. 2) and MPXV (Fig. 3) antigens using a multiplexed solid-phase electroluminescent orthopoxvirus immunoassay [22–26]. The assay detects IgG antibodies against full-length VACV A27, A33, B5, D8 and L1 and their MPXV homologs A29, A35, B6, E8 and M1, respectively. Using the same multiplex assay system, previous studies [22,26] had shown that the sensitivities for A27 and A29 were lower than for the other antigens and minimal following vaccination. Our findings also demonstrated minimal responses to A27 (Fig. 2A – C) and A29 (Fig. 3A – C). For this reason, no further analyses of these proteins were performed. Antibody binding to the other VACV (Fig. 2A – C) and MPXV (Fig. 3A – C) antigens was significantly greater on days 43 and 90 than on day 1 pre-vaccination for SC, IDH and IDL immunizations. In each case, antibody binding decreased on day 90 compared to day 43 but the differences were mainly not significant. Comparison of the binding titers for the SC and ID immunizations on day 43 indicated a high correlation between the MPXV and VACV homologs (Fig. 4A – C).

Significant correlations were found between MVA (Fig. 5A) and complement-enhanced MPXV (Fig. 5B) neutralization and each of the antigen binding titers determined for the peak day SC immunizations, which were particularly high for VACV B5, MPXV B6, VACV D8 and MPXV E8 as found previously in NHP studies [26].

Comparisons of standard SC to reduced dose ID vaccinations on VACV and MPXV antigen-binding antibodies

The VACV-antigen-binding and the MPXV-antigen-binding titers induced by SC vaccination compared to ID vaccination were determined. Although the titers were always highest with the SC vaccination, the differences between the SC and IDH titers were insignificant for each VACV antigen, whereas the difference between the SC and IDL was significant for A33, D8 and L1 (Fig. 5C). A similar result was found when comparing the MPXV antigen-binding titers induced by SC and IDH (Fig. 5D). Again, the titers were all higher for the SC but the difference between SC and IDH was not significant, whereas the difference between SC and IDL was significant for B6 and M1 MPXV antigens.

Discussion

A recent phase 2 study [16] demonstrated non-inferiority of a one-fifth but not a one-tenth ID dose of an MVA vaccine compared to the standard SC dose as determined by the VACV WR PRNT. The present exploratory study was designed to extend the results by comparing anti-MVA and anti-MPXV neutralizing activities. Instead of the traditional PRNT assay with VERO cells, we used a flow cytometer-based assay that measures the number of HeLa S3 suspension cells infected by VACV and MPXV expressing GFP [17]. The inability of MVA to replicate in mammalian cells would have required establishment of an avian PRNT assay. Advantages of the flow assay include its high sensitivity, direct measurement of infection, semi-automation and use to determine both anti-MVA and anti-MPXV neutralization titers. We found that the difference in MVA neutralizing activities in SC and IDH serum was not significant, whereas the difference between SC and IDL was significant as had been found with the VACV WR PRNT. Although, our sensitive flow cytometry assay was able to detect low anti-MPXV neutralizing antibody in the absence of complement, the presence of complement greatly enhanced the NT50 values, which were highly correlated with the MVA titers. We found that complement-enhanced neutralizing activity was significantly higher in the SC sera than in both IDH and IDL sera, suggesting that the different routes and doses of vaccine are not entirely equivalent.

The ability of complement to increase the potency of antibodies to neutralize VACV and other OPXVs has been shown for polyclonal antibodies induced by the smallpox vaccine [27] and a majority of monoclonal antibodies tested [28]. Hubert and co-workers [20] reported that complement enhanced the neutralizing activity of MVA vaccinees to both VACV and MPXV and was required to detect the latter with their assay. The direct enhancement of viral neutralizing antibody by complement can have a variety of mechanisms including virus aggregation, inhibition of attachment or entry into the host cell, and virolysis [29]. Whether the greater need for complement and the lower neutralization for MPXV compared to VACV are due to antigenic differences or to intrinsic differences in the susceptibility of the viruses to neutralization remains to be determined. However, the latter seems possible as MVA neutralizing titers were higher than MPXV neutralizing titers even with MPXV convalescent serum [20]. MVA, as well as other strains of VACV have been extensively passaged in cell culture, which could have allowed selection for rapid infection leading to greater antigen exposure in the absence of immune restriction. In addition to enhancing neutralization, complement has many additional anti-viral roles in vivo including regulation of the inflammatory response, promotion of chemotaxis, and enhancement of adaptive immune response [30].

Despite the long use of live OPXV vaccines, the basis for protective immunity has not been established. Nevertheless, vaccine-induced antibodies are necessary and sufficient for protection in animal models [31]. Although neutralization assays correlate with protection, the most important targets of protective antibodies have not been identified. In a clinical study, antibodies to 15 of 224 antigens showed a strong increase following MVA immunization and antibodies to two, H3 and D8 most strongly correlated with increases in neutralization [32]. However, when antibodies to H3 and other immunodominant proteins were depleted from immune serum, there was no appreciable diminution of neutralizing ability [33]. It is still a puzzle whether neutralizing activity represents the sum of low titer antibodies to many antigens or whether there are important unrecognized targets. A second problem with using neutralization assays as correlates of protection is that they are usually specific for the mature virion (MV) form of infectious virus and not for the extracellular virion (EV) form, which is important for virus spread. Studies with single immunogens indicate that antibodies to the EV proteins such as VACV A33 and B5 protect as well or better than antibodies to MV proteins such as L1 [21]. Therefore, to broaden the comparison of route and dose of vaccination, we used a commercially available multiplexed solid-phase electroluminescent OPXV immunoassay [22–26]. The assay detects IgG antibodies against full-length VACV A27, A33, B5, D8 and L1 and their MPXV clade IIb homologs A29, A35, B6, E8 and M1, respectively. A27/A29 and D8/E8 are MV surface proteins involved in the attachment of virus to cells [34,35], L1/M1 are MV membrane proteins required for fusion of both MVs and EVs with the cell membrane and entry [36], B5/B6 and A33/A35 are components of the outer EV membrane that contribute to virus spread [37,38]. As expected, we found a strong correlation of antibody binding to the homologous VACV and MPXV proteins, which share a high amino acid sequence identity. Antibody binding to B6/B5 and D8/E8 correlated strongly with neutralizing activity, suggesting that these antigens might be used as an alternative or addition to the standard PRNTs in clinical studies. D8, measured by ELISA, was previously shown to provide better sensitivity and specificity than other proteins for monitoring MVA vaccination [39]. The comparisons of antigen binding by SC, IDH and IDL sera showed some significant differences between SC and IDL but not between SC and IDH.

The main conclusions of this study are that the titers of MVA neutralizing antibodies induced by SC and IDH were not significantly different, whereas neutralizing antibody titers induced by IDL were lower like the previous VACV Western Reserve PRNT results. However, the titers of complement-enhanced MPXV neutralizing antibodies induced by SC were significantly higher than for either IDH or IDL immunizations, indicating that further studies are needed to fully assess the equivalence of SC and ID immunizations.

We acknowledge the following limitaions of the study. Only 20 to 22 serum samples were analyzed for each vaccination group, impacting the statistical power. Neutralization and binding data were obtained for clades Ia and IIb, respectively, but not for other clades. Additional correlates of protection were not evaluated.

Methods

Ethics

Serum samples for the current study were a random subset of those from study protocol (NCT05512949), which was approved by a central Institutional Review Board for the eight participating sites and carried out in accordance with The Code of Ethics of the World Medical Association (Declaration of Helsinki), Good Clinical Practice and the Food and Drug Administration’s regulations. Written informed consent was provided by each participant prior to enrollment.

Biosafety

Procedures with infectious MPXV were performed in a registered select agent BSL-3 laboratory by trained and smallpox-vaccinated investigators under protocol LVD 29E approved by the NIH Institutional Biosafety Committee and judged not to have the potential for dual-use research of concern.

Neutralization assays

Neutralization was carried out as described previously using VACV MVA and clade Ia MPXV Zaire-1979-005 expressing GFP in a 96-well plate flow cytometric assay [17,40]. Two-fold dilutions of 56 °C heat-inactivated sera were incubated with MVA or MPXV in the absence or presence of 2.5% guinea pig complement (Rockland, item C200–005) for 1 h at 37° C. HeLa S3 cells were added and incubated overnight. GFP-expressing cells were enumerated with a FACSCalibur (BD Biosciences, Franklin Lakes, NJ) and analyzed with FlowJo software (BD Biosciences). All assays were performed in duplicate. NT50 values were calculated by non-linear regression with GraphPad Prism (Reston, VA).

Multiplex antigen binding assays

The Mesoscale Discovery (Rockville, MD) V-Plex Orthopoxvirus Panel Kit (catalog number K15688, lot K0082514) was used to measure IgG to A27, A33, B5, D8 and L1 derived from the Lister strain of VACV (GenBank: DQ121394) and to the homologs A29, A35, B6, E8 and M1 from MPXV clade IIb (GenBank: ON563414) in 96-well plates following the protocol of the manufacturer. Serum dilutions of 100 to 100,000 were made and antibody concentrations expressed as arbitrary units (AU) were determined from standard curves. Data analysis was performed with MSD Discovery Workbench 4.0.

Statistics

Analyses were carried out with GraphPad Prism v. 10 as indicated in individual figure legends.

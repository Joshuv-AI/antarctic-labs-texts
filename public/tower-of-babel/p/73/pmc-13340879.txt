Temporal Patterns of Tactile and Thermal Recovery After Dual-Nerve Coaptation in Autologous Breast Reconstruction
Authors: Carson Gundlach, Annie Mcveigh, Michael Stigliano, Fiona Fragomen, Hao Huang, Marcos Lu Wang, David Otterburn
Published: 2026

Purpose: Loss of breast sensation is a common and distressing outcome after mastectomy and reconstruction. Neurotization has emerged as a promising strategy to restore sensation, yet the extent and timing of recovery remain incompletely defined. In 2024, our institution adopted a standardized dual-nerve coaptation approach, adding a second thoracoabdominal donor nerve to the lateral cutaneous branch of T3-T4 to complement the traditional medial branch repair. This study quantifies tactile and thermal sensory recovery following dual-neurotized deep inferior epigastric perforator (DIEP) flap breast reconstruction using an intra-patient controlled design.

Methods: Patients undergoing nipple- or skin-sparing mastectomy with immediate dual-neurotized DIEP flap reconstruction were prospectively enrolled. Sensory testing was performed preoperatively and at 3, 6, and 9 months postoperatively. Tactile thresholds were measured using a pressure-specified sensory device and thermal detection accuracy was assessed with 12°C and 52°C stimuli. Postoperative values were expressed as percentages of each patient’s baseline. Statistical analyses included paired t-tests for continuous variables and McNemar’s test for binary outcomes.

Results: The cohort consisted of 23 patients (42 breasts), had a mean age of 52.7 years, and body mass index of 27.4 kg/m2. Former smokers comprised 26.1% and 8.7% had diabetes. Most reconstructions were nipple-sparing (82.6%) with an average mastectomy weight of 975.5 g. Neoadjuvant chemotherapy was administered to 17.4%, adjuvant chemotherapy to 8.7%, preoperative radiation to 8.7%, and adjuvant radiation to 4.3%. Mean tactile recovery reached 39.5% at 3 months, 56.8% at 6 months, and 65.6% at 9 months (p < 0.001 for all vs baseline). Cold detection accuracy improved from 35.2% at 3 months to 59.1% at 9 months (p < 0.001), while heat detection increased from 60.0% to 73.6% (p = 0.002). Regional analysis revealed that the superior and medial quadrants recovered earliest, while the nipple-areolar complex remained the least responsive.

Conclusions: Dual-nerve coaptation resulted in early and robust recovery across tactile and thermal modalities, supporting its use as a reproducible strategy for achieving meaningful sensory restoration in autologous breast reconstruction.

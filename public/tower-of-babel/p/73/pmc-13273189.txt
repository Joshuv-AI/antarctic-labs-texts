Estimation and uncertainty quantification of remaining useful life (RUL) of electric vehicle batteries using a temporal deep model
Authors: Zhuo Kong, Shuang Li, Chunyu Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study develops a remaining useful life (RUL) estimation algorithm for electric vehicle lithium-ion batteries based on multi-scale CNN feature integration and multiple multi-head attention LSTM (MCNN-MHALSTM). The proposed method considers the uncertainty of estimation results and realizes multi-feature fusion and temporal correlation learning via the designed network architecture. Firstly, using the strategy of skip connections, an independent multi-scale CNN is utilized to extract temporal dynamic features from three key battery states: capacity, internal resistance, and temperature Then, Multi-feature integration techniques are utilized to concatenate them and input them into the multiple multi-head attention LSTM (MHALSTM) network to accomplish feature correlation extraction. Secondly, the Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) algorithm is introduced to estimate the RUL of electric vehicle batteries, and two multi-head attention layers with the same configuration are applied to improve the LSTM algorithm. While achieving high-order feature extraction of battery data, the RUL estimation task of electric vehicle batteries is divided into multiple small tasks, and the problem of feature redundancy mitigation is addressed through resource allocation. Finally, the effectiveness of the MCNN-MHALSTM electric vehicle battery RUL estimation model is verified using the NASA lithium-ion battery capacity degradation dataset.

Introduction

Along with the development of electric vehicle techniques and the increase in social expectations, Li-ion batteries have been commonly applied and promoted owing to their high energy density, long service life, and green environmental protection characteristics1,2. Frequent charge–discharge cycling, as well as changes in ambient temperature, may influence the lifespan of electric vehicle batteries when facing different work environments. The aging of electric vehicle batteries is mainly characterized by the reduction of capacity and the escalation of equivalent internal resistance. When the electric vehicle battery reaches its specified lifespan threshold, it should be replaced in a timely manner to guarantee the reliability of the electric vehicle control system. Accordingly, accurate estimation of the RUL of electric vehicle batteries is crucial3.

Lately, with the booming progress of deep learning and machine learning algorithms, underpinned by data-driven RUL estimation methods have received extensive concern. In comparison with conventional analytical methods using equivalent circuit models, underpinned by data-driven estimation methods exhibit greater flexibility and are able to eliminate the complex physical modeling process. They can only use longitudinal data throughout the dynamic behavior of electric vehicle batteries for modeling, achieving effective estimation of the remaining battery life. Deep learning is extensively employed in the study area of Li-ion battery RUL estimation attributed to their excellent data processing performance. Niu et al. (2021)4 combined deep belief networks and support vector machines (SVM) to estimate the degradation of Li-ion capacity performance of Li-ion batteries, and obtained the battery RUL through capacity extrapolation. Li, X. et al. (2023)5 further takes account of the effects of ambient temperature and discharging current on degradation of capacity throughout the cyclic use of Li-ion batteries, and develops a RUL estimation model that takes account of external state influences. Ma et al. (2022)6 utilized SVM to estimate battery RUL. Jiang et al. (2024)7 also used battery temperature to estimate battery RUL, but the developed model is a multi-branch neural network capacity performance of Li-ion batteries estimation methods, which comprehensively takes account of the contribution of charging and discharging terminal voltage and current in performance fade of batteries. Meng et al. (2022)8 incorporates voltage into the stacked denoising auto-encoder to directly estimate the RUL of Li-ion batteries. Recurrent neural networks have significant advantages in processing temporal data attributed to their unique memory structure, and are frequently employed in various lifespan estimation applications. Zraibi et al. (2021)9 uses LSTM to learn the long-term correlation between the capacity fade of electric vehicle batteries, and obtains degradation of capacity data for multiple time steps in the future through iterative estimation methods. Hu et al. (2024)10 introduced an estimation model with LSTM network layer as the main body for battery life estimation. The experimental findings indicated that the model using 70% of the samples for training had greater accuracy. Nevertheless, the aforementioned studies only build RUL estimation models for Li-ion batteries using an individual internal state, neglecting the interaction between internal states throughout battery service process. Accordingly, it is hard to fully utilize the life information contained in the state, which to some extent influences the accuracy of the estimation outcomes. However, most existing methods only rely on a single state variable and ignore the coupling relationship among capacity, internal resistance, and temperature, leading to limited accuracy and robustness.

In summary, in response to the problems and shortcomings of existing RUL estimation methods, this work fully takes account of the capacity, impedance, and temperature information throughout the cyclic use of Li-ion batteries. Three types of state data are utilized as multi-scale inputs for the introduced MCNN-MHALSTM electric vehicle battery RUL estimation algorithm, and the cycle life is utilized as the network output to establish a RUL estimation model for Li-ion batteries. Firstly, an independent multi-scale CNN is utilized to extract temporal dynamic features from three key battery states: capacity, internal resistance, and temperature Then, Multi-feature integration techniques are utilized to concatenate the three parts of feature information. Finally, the concatenated features are input into the MHALSTM network to extract deep features of electric vehicle battery RUL estimation data with temporal relationships while considering feature correlation. Secondly, the LSTM model is introduced to estimate the RUL of electric vehicle batteries, and two multi-head attention layers with the same configuration are applied to improve the LSTM algorithm. While achieving high-order feature extraction of battery data, the task of estimating the RUL of electric vehicle batteries is divided into multiple small tasks. The feature redundancy mitigation problem is handled through resource allocation, and the inter element correlations of input sequence data are captured to improve the performance of the LSTM machine learning model. Finally, the effectiveness of the model was validated when comparing the Li-ion battery dataset with actual obtained electric vehicle battery data.

Related research

Machine learning is capable of being separated into two types: probabilistic approaches and non-probabilistic approaches. Probabilistic methods including Support Vector Regression (SVR) and Autoregression (AR) were first introduced into the study of battery RUL estimation in 2012. In non-probabilistic methods, non-probabilistic models including recurrent neural networks (RNN) have been commonly used for estimating battery RUL since 2013 and have quickly become mainstream technologies in this field.

Probability method

The core of Gaussian process regression (GPR) is to handle a set of probabilistic variables that follow a normal probability distribution. In RUL estimation, GPR often extracts geometric features including peak, area, and slope of incremental capacity analysis (IcA) curves, as well as voltage dependence and EIS spectral features, as input data to estimate RUL. Model combination is another important strategy, which integrates multiple GPR model results using different inputs and kernel functions to construct the final estimation model. For example, Tang et al. (2025)11 uses residual sequences and time indices from VMD decomposition as inputs, combined with lag vectors, to extract the global attenuation tendency and local fluctuations of battery RUL. Fu et al. (2025)12 uses multiple indices and linear-based models as the tendency term of the GP method to characterize the degradation of electric vehicle batteries at diverse degradation phases. The development of GPR in RUL estimation focuses on enhancing kernel functionality and innovating model combinations, aiming to accomplish greater prediction accuracy and comprehensive assessment of battery condition status.

Support Vector Regression (SVR) can depict the nonlinear interdependence between input features and output targets, and is also suitable for RUL estimation. The key is to convert complicated nonlinear issues into tractable linear problems. Jameel et al. (2024)13 uses an integrated model of SVR and feature vector selection (FVS) to estimate battery RUL. The study on SVR in battery RUL estimation centers primarily on optimization of hyperparameters and fusion of models. Combining SVR with particle systems and Kalman filters as measurement equations can achieve forward-looking estimations. Additionally, SVR can serve as an enhancement tool to improve model estimation accuracy. SVR is also integrated alongside other methods, including the Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) using the principle of weighting. In the future, SVR may develop towards a more refined and customized direction. For example, for the application environment of central controllers in electric vehicles, especially considering the changing load conditions, further research and optimization of SVR algorithm implementation will also be an important direction worth exploring in the future.

Automatic regression (AR) excels at estimating upcoming values in sequential data, particularly in extracting long-term trends in sequential data. For example, Liu & Wei (2021)14 uses AR to track the long-term variation patterns of internal resistance in batteries. Determining the sequence of the AR method (i.e. the quantity of delay terms in the model) is crucial for estimation accuracy. Conventional estimation methods such as Akaike information standards (AIC), Box Jenkins method, and Bayesian information standards (BIC). To further enhance the generalizability of the model, Wu et al. (2023)15 introduces the PSO (particle swarm optimization method) to search for the optimal order of the AR method, and evaluates the effect of various orders by calculating the root mean square error (RMSE). The model order is capable of being adaptively adjusted according to the introduction of new data to ensure stable and improved estimation performance. The study focus of AR models is gradually shifting towards modeling the nonlinear aging phenomenon of batteries and combining different algorithm strategies to further enhance the estimative ability and robustness of the models on complex sequential data.

A key benefit of probabilistic models is that they can quantify the uncertain characteristics of estimation outcomes, which is particularly important for highly reliable application scenarios. By providing the probability distribution of estimated results, a more comprehensive understanding of the possible range of battery RUL is capable of being obtained. Probabilistic models are capable of handling complex nonlinear relationships and data uncertainty, making them excellent in the face of complex battery aging processes and multiple influencing factors. The shortcomings of probabilistic methods mainly lie in the need for a large number of computational resources to estimate probabilistic distributions and train models, resulting in high computational complexity. To obtain accurate probability estimations, massive high-quality data is often required for model training. Insufficient or low-quality data may lead to inaccurate estimation outcomes. The performance of probability models is often presented as probabilistic distributions, which can make it difficult to directly explain the estimated results.

Non-probabilistic methods

Extreme Learning Machine (ELM) enjoys the merits of less parameters and faster learning, making it suitable for fast battery RUL estimation. For example, the over integration framework is utilized to address battery RUL estimation when data is missing. The two-phase Wiener process is combined with ELM to extract the aging features of Li-ion batteries, and particle filtering and optimization algorithms are introduced to optimize initial parameters to boost the prediction precision of battery RUL estimation. BL-ELM significantly enhances the performance of ELM to process big data by increasing hidden layer units instead of increasing layers. To shorten the RUL estimation time, OS-ELM has been introduced, which utilizes new data to update model parameters online instead of retraining. Han & Yuan (2024)16 developed an OS-PELM Li-ion battery RUL estimation model, which optimized the connection structure by introducing convolution and pooling operations to improve training efficiency. Zhu et al. (2024)17 introduced that FOS-ELM adds a forgetting mechanism to OS-ELM, automatically removing outdated data and maintaining model timeliness. Zhang, M. et al. (2022)18 develops a CTC-ELM Li-ion battery RUL estimation model using deep learning methods. Its design inspiration comes from the time variation processing of RNN, but it maintains the fast-learning advantage of ELM. Yan et al. (2022)19 uses cascaded sub networks, where each sub network input combines the previous output with the original capacity performance of Li-ion batteries decay sequential data, making it highly suitable for small dataset scenarios. Although ELM has multiple advantages, its shallow structure remains a bottleneck in processing complex datasets, restricting its capability of feature extraction from data in high-dimensional space and making it difficult to cope with large-scale battery datasets.

The CNN architecture captures spatial features from input data through filter convolution operations, simplifies feature dimensions using pooling layers, and finally estimates through fully connected layer regression. The core advantage of CNN lies in its efficient spatial information extraction and dimensionality reduction capabilities. In the study area of battery RUL estimation, Chen & Fang (2025)20 utilizes a dual CNN model to reduce data dimensionality, combined with artificial feature extraction, and achieves battery RUL estimation accuracy through deep neural networks (DNN). Salucci et al. (2023)21 adopts a semi supervised learning method to directly input EIS data into CNN for feature extraction, without the need for capacity labels to estimate capacity. At present, the study hotspots of CNN concentrate on optimization of hyperparameters, integration with LSTM, application of dilated CNN, and transfer learning. Combined with LSTM, the CNN-LSTM model can simultaneously capture spatial and temporal information, substantially boosting the prediction accuracy of RUL estimation. Li, Y. et al. (2024)22 introduces an auto-encoder to optimize the CNN-LSTM model by increasing the data dimension, achieving accurate estimation of Li-ion battery RUL. Rajasekaran & Venkatanarayanan (2025)23 utilizes CNN-LSTM combined with Convolutional Recurrent Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) to generate more realistic sequential data and reduce RUL estimation errors in Li-ion batteries. In the future, how to accomplish rapid and reliable RUL prediction under limited dataset conditions will still be an important research direction.

Recurrent neural networks (RNNs) can utilize both historical and current information to estimate the future information. The battery capacity fade data obtained throughout charge and discharge cycles is sequential data, which is very suitable as input for RNN algorithms to estimate battery RUL. Although RNN can accurately obtain the temporal dependence of input data, it is prone to gradient vanishing when processing long sequence data. There are many variants of RNN algorithm, among which LSTM network uses a unique information control mechanism to address the gradient vanishing issue of RNN. Jafari & Byun (2023)24 used LSTM to capture long-term battery aging trends and found that LSTM only requires 20% to 26% of the full battery degradation dataset to accomplish accurate RUL estimation. Feng & Wu (2023)25 develops a method that integrates LSTM with the Broad Learning System (BLS) to increase the quantity of parameter input nodes and obtain more feature information. By adopting an adaptive sliding window strategy, LSTM can simultaneously model both local variations and long-range dependencies. Liu et al. (2025)26 uses Bayesian models integrated with LSTM models to accomplish higher estimation accuracy. Zhang, S. et al. (2022)27 introduced an LSTM using transfer learning to estimate the capacity performance of Li-ion batteries and RUL of electric vehicles under diverse operating conditions.

The advantages of non-probabilistic methods mainly lie in low computational complexity, high computational efficiency, and the ability to quickly provide estimation outcomes, making them suitable for scenarios with high real-time requirements. Compared to probabilistic methods, non-probabilistic methods typically have lower data requirements and can make estimations with limited dataset. The prediction outcomes of non-probabilistic models are generally shown in concrete numerical form for easy understanding and interpretation. The main disadvantage of non-probabilistic methods is that they cannot directly quantify the uncertain characteristics of estimation outcomes, which may increase the risk of decision-making in certain application scenarios.

Description of RUL estimation problem for electric vehicle batteries

MCNN-MHALSTM method framework

The structure of the MCNN-MHALSTM electric vehicle battery RUL estimation algorithm introduced in this study is presented in Fig. 1. Firstly, an independent multi-scale CNN is utilized to extract temporal dynamic features from three key battery states: capacity, internal resistance, and temperature Then, Multi-feature integration techniques are utilized to concatenate the three parts of feature information. Finally, the concatenated features are input into the MHALSTM network to extract deep features of electric vehicle battery RUL estimation data with temporal relationships while considering feature correlation.

The introduced MHALSTM model utilizes stacked multi-scale CNN feature extraction networks and multiple multi attention LSTM networks to extract and learn the correlation and temporal dependence features of electric vehicle battery data, and obtains the RUL of the battery degradation trend by means of fully connected layers and regression layers. Additionally, this study evaluates the model performance with two metrics: Mean Absolute Error (MAE) and Root Mean Square Error (RMSE).

Multi scale CNN multi-feature integration

Feature extraction. Traditional convolutional neural networks have a feedforward structure, where the output of the previous layer is only input to the next layer. Multiple convolutional layers (conv layers) can extract higher-level abstract features from longitudinal data of electric vehicle battery usage. Nevertheless, primitive low-level features will be discarded in the transmission process. For classic CNNs, only the neurons in the final conv layer are regarded as the final encoded features of the raw input information, leading to the missing of detailed local characteristics derived from earlier conv layers and decreasing the prediction accuracy of RUL estimation.

To address the issue of local feature loss in CNN, this work develops a multi-scale features integration CNN method for electric vehicle battery usage history data using the strategy of skip connections. Firstly, conv layers are successively applied in the model to perform feature extraction from the raw input; Then, integrate and extract NV scale features from all conv layers, skip and connect them, and input them into final feature representation; Finally, the multi-scale features information are integrated in the fusion layer as the final feature information. The multi-scale multi-feature integration CNN structure is presented in Fig. 2. The network shown in Fig. 2 fuses the features from each conv layer of the CNN model through skip connections, obtaining detailed information of various features of electric vehicle battery data from the lower layers, and extracting the transformation trends of various features in the higher layers, achieving comprehensive analysis of different feature change trends and detailed information throughout the charge–discharge cycle of electric vehicle batteries.

To describe the uncertain characteristics of estimation outcomes, Bayesian networks are applied to the proposed model, and the LSTM network parameters are treated as probabilistic variables following some distribution. Where, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\hat {Y}$$\end{document} represents the predicted output achieved by dropout mechanism, variable S is the subset of training instances, p is the quantity of subsets, H represents the total parameters number, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda$$\end{document} represents the attenuation factor of regularization technique.

MHALSTM algorithm

Algorithm structure

The architecture of the electric vehicle battery RUL estimation model combining LSTM and dual multi-head attention is presented in Fig. 3, where the attention layer includes two multi-head attention layers with the same configuration.

Fig. 3System architecture for MHALSTM algorithm.

System architecture for MHALSTM algorithm.

LSTM can directly learn temporal correlations in longitudinal data of electric vehicle batteries, but it is not good at handling model calibration between input and output time series, and cannot determine the importance of specific components of the input time series. The self-attention mechanism can identify essential information between input and output time series, and use a multi-head self-attention to extract high-order features of longitudinal data of electric vehicle batteries.

As shown in Fig. 3, the multi - head attention is essential for weighting the temporal features. Each head in the multi - head attention can concentrate on various components of the temporal features extracted from the LSTM output sequence. For example, some heads may pay more attention to the long - term trends in the battery’s longitudinal data, while others may concentrate on short - term fluctuations. By combining the information from multiple heads, the multi - head attention can comprehensively capture the importance of different temporal features. This weighted combination of temporal features is then fed into the subsequent layers, which helps the model to better understand the complex temporal patterns in the electric vehicle battery data and thus improve the accuracy of RUL estimation.

Experimental analysis

Experimental setup

The model performance was validated using an electric vehicle battery dataset at room temperature. Figure 5 demonstrates the capacity performance of Li-ion batteries attenuation trend of the NASA dataset, which is divided into four groups, namely Battery5, Battery6, Battery7, and Battery18, which are all individual batteries from the NASA PcoE Research Center. The experimental process is: (1) charge under a fixed current of 1.50 A up to the cut-off potential difference is 4.20 V; (2) maintain a fixed potential difference up to the charging current drops to 0.02 mA; (3) discharge under a fixed current of 2.0 A up to the voltages of Battery18, Battery7, Battery6, and Battery5 drop to 2.50 V, 2.20 V, 2.50 V, and 2.70 V, respectively, and stop. Since the charge capacity of Battery7 remains above 1.38 Ah 1.38 Ah, its cut-off capacity is fixed at 74.50% of its rated capacity (1.47 Ah). Battery5 is a LiCoO₂ battery, which uses LiCoO₂ as the positive electrode material utilized for Li-ion batteries, specifically the 18,650-type LiCoO₂ battery with a rated capacity of 2.20Ah; Battery6 is a ternary system battery, namely LiNiMnCoO₂ (NMC) battery, which achieves a balance between energy density and safety performance through precise proportioning. It has the properties of elevated energy densities, strong endurance, and excellent low-temperature performance; Battery7 is a solid-state system battery, using sulfur selenium composite lithium metal foil as the negative electrode and nanomaterials as the positive electrode. The solid-state sulfide electrolyte layer completely eliminates liquid components, significantly improving safety; The Battery18 is a nickel hydrogen system battery with a lifespan of 30,000 charging cycles, excellent fire resistance, and outperforms Li-ion batteries in many key indicators of large-scale energy storage.

It should be noted that the quantity of cells in the current dataset is somewhat limited. Conclusion

This study develops an estimation algorithm (MCNN-MHALSTM) using multi-scale CNN Multi-feature integration and multiple multi-head attention long short-term memory network (MHALSTM) for the RUL estimation problem of electric vehicle batteries. This algorithm extracts temporal features of capacity performance of Li-ion batteries, internal resistance, and temperature through an independent multi-scale CNN, and uses multi-feature integration techniques to accomplish feature concatenation, which is input into the MHALSTM network to extract feature correlation. At the same time, the LSTM algorithm is introduced for RUL estimation, and the LSTM is improved through a dual multi-head attention layer to accomplish high-order feature extraction and feature redundancy mitigation processing. The experimental findings indicate that the MCNN-MHALSTM method exhibits excellent estimation precision and robustness under different battery models and estimation starting points.

Although the MCNN-MHALSTM method has obtained satisfactory results in estimating RUL of electric vehicle batteries, there are still some directions that is capable of being improved and explored. In future research, we will concentrate on the following aspects: (1) further optimizing the algorithm architecture, researching more efficient feature extraction techniques and fusion methods to improve the performance of the model; (2) consider more factors that influence battery RUL, including C-rate, environmental temperature, etc., and construct a more comprehensive estimation model; (3) apply the model to actual electric vehicle systems for long-term online monitoring and estimation to examine the practicality and stability of the model; (4) investigate the practicability of the model in other types of batteries or energy systems, and broaden the scope of application of the model.

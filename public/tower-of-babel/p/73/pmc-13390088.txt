Compatibility mechanism of Chrysanthemi Indici Flos compound against hepatocellular carcinoma based on balance of DNMTs/TET2
Authors: Zehui He, Cancan Guo, Fangping Zhang, Feifan Li, Yu Zhou, Leyi Ma, Xiaohui Wang, Yuefeng Bi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveAt present, traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) compounds is widely used in tumor therapy. However, due to the current single and simplistic efficacy evaluation indicators that are detached from the guidance of TCM theory, the elucidation of its mechanisms of efficacy is hindered. Therefore, the compatibility mechanism of Chrysanthemi Indici Flos compound (YJHFF) against hepatocellular carcinoma based on DNA methyltransferases/DNA demethylase ten-eleven translocation 2 (DNMTs/TET2) balanced relationship was explored according to the concept of the overall balance of TCM.MethodsThis study screened the best compound compatibility ratio in the in vitro experiments, cell viability and DNMTs/TET2 balance were used as evaluation indicators to investigate the effects of single herb and different compatibility of compound on HepG2 and H22 cells. In vivo experiments were conduct on H22 liver orthotopic transplantation model mice. After treatment, the tumor tissues were observed by hematoxylin-eosin (HE), TUNEL and Ki67 immunohistochemical staining, respectively. The oxidative stress and liver function indicators in serum were detected by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay. In addition, the proportion of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes was detected by immunofluorescence staining. The mRNA expression levels of DNMTs and TET2 in tumor tissues were detected by RT-PCR.ResultsThe results of in vitro experiments showed that the cell proliferation inhibition rate was highly consistent with the change trend of DNMTs/TET2 balance, accordingly, this study obtained the optimal formula YJHFF. In the in vivo experiments, the tumor growth inhibition rate of YJHFF in 5 g/kg was 62.97%. Extensive necrosis, increased apoptosis and inhibited proliferation activity were observed in tumor tissue. Furthermore, the serum SOD activity was significantly increased, MDA, ALT, AST and AFP levels were significantly decreased, suggesting YJHFF could systematically alleviated oxidative stress and improved liver function. Besides, the infiltration of CD3+, CD4+ and CD8+ T lymphocytes increased significantly, indicating that the body’s anti-tumor immune response was enhanced. Finally, RT-PCR analysis confirmed that antitumor effects were closely related to the core mechanism of DNMTs/TET2 expression regulation and reconstruction of epigenetic balance.ConclusionThis work confirmed the rationality and feasibility of the DNMTs/TET2 balance relationship as an evaluation indicator for the study of the compatibility mechanism of TCM compounds. The YJHFF screened under the guidance of DNMTs/TET2 balance has liver protection and tumor immune function, showing good anti-hepatocellular carcinoma effect.

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is a complex disease influenced by various factors, including diabetes, mood, and the imbalance of intracellular and extracellular environments, which remains a significant global health problem associated with increasing incidence and mortality (Peeters et al., 2024, Singal et al., 2023, Yang et al., 2019). Currently used treatments include liver transplantation, surgical resection, chemotherapy, radiation therapy, and molecular targeted therapy, all of which have some problems such as single target and mechanism easily leading to drug resistance (Garcia and Mathew, 2024, Takeda et al., 2022, Zhou et al., 2024). The traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) compounds have small side effects and good curative effects, which have attracted more and more attention (Liu et al., 2017, Tang et al., 2024, Zheng et al., 2024).

The material basis of TCM compounds in the treatment of diseases is the combination of their chemical components, which can play a role in multiple components, multiple targets, and multiple ways. This reflects the idea of the overall balance of TCM. It is of great significance to study the compatibility mechanism of TCM compounds (Wang et al., 2020). However, the current methods of studying the compatibility mechanism from the aspects of pharmacodynamic components, pharmacokinetics, and biological effects can not reflect the characteristics of the overall balance concept of TCM compounds (Liu et al., 2023). Conventional efficacy evaluation indicators, such as tumor growth inhibition rate and inflammatory cytokines level, typically exhibit a unidirectional trend instead of a dynamic equilibrium relationship, which were divorced from the guidance of TCM theory. DNA methyltransferases/DNA demethylase ten-eleven translocation 2 (DNMTs/TET2) represents a novel evaluation indicator in the study of TCM compound formulations. It is particularly suitable for investigating the impact of variations in the monarch-minister-assistant-messenger ratio on therapeutic efficacy through the balance restoration. Therefore, it is worthy of further study to explore and determine the efficacy index reflecting the characteristics of its overall balance concept and to study the compatibility mechanism of the compound.

More and more studies have shown that epigenetic expression disorder is an important pathogenesis of many diseases, such as liver cancer, which plays an important role in the occurrence and development of diseases (Su et al., 2024, Wang et al., 2024). Abnormal DNA methylation is an important feature of malignant tumors, which is mainly related to the imbalance of DNA methyltransferases (DNMTs, mainly DNMT1, DNMT3a, DNMT3b) and demethylases (TETs, especially TET2) (Rajan et al., 2020). In recent years, more and more scholars have studied the anti-hepatocellular carcinoma effect of TCM compound by restoring abnormal DNA methylation. For example, Lv et al. found that Yugan granule compound can regulate the expression level of DNMTs methyltransferase, and then regulate the DNA methylation level of liver cells from multiple dimensions to achieve the effect of protecting liver and preventing cancer (Lv, Shao, Xie, & Xin, 2008). At the micro level, the balance of DNMTs/TET2 determines the transcription and expression of genes, so that multi-targets and multi-channels act on the body to restore balance, and ultimately achieve the purpose of treating diseases, which is consistent with the overall concept of balance in TCM. It is of great significance to use DNMTs/TET2 balance as one of the indicators to guide the study of compound compatibility and mechanism of TCM.

The preliminary study of the research group showed that chrysanthemum indici flos extract has good effects on anti-hepatocellular carcinoma, hepatoprotection, and improvement of the immune microenvironment, and can regulate the balance of DNMTs/TET2. In order to further bring out the advantages of Chrysanthemi Indici Flos (Yejuhua in Chinese, YJH) against HCC and to enhance the anti-hepatocellular carcinoma effect by “eliminating evil and promoting righteousness”, the research group conducted a compatibility study based on the theory of drug property and drug meridian tropism. In the TCM theoretical system, the etiology of HCC is invasion of heat toxin, stagnation of blood stasis, deficiency of healthy qi, and disorder of immune functions. Based on property and meridian tropism of herbs, the research group formulated Chrysanthemi Indici Flos compound (YJHFF), which is composed of Chrysanthemi Indici Flos as monarch drug (Jun in Chinese, J), Prunellae Spica (Xiakucao in Chinese, XKC) as minister drug (Chen in Chinese, C), Ginseng Radix et Rhizoma (Renshen in Chinese, RS) as assistant drug (Zuo in Chinese, Z), Glycyrrhizae Radix et Rhizoma (Gancao in Chinese, GC) as messenger drug (Shi in Chinese, S). YJH could fight against HCC through clearing away heat and toxins, XKC plays a role in removing stasis and swelling, RS and GC focus on strengthening healthy qi and adjusting immune functions. This paper will explore the regulation of DNMTs/TET2 balance as an indicator of compound compatibility for the first time and compare it with cell viability indicators to study the compatibility mechanism of YJHFF against HCC.

Materials and methods

Drugs and reagents

YJHFF was prepared by our research group. YJH and XKC were obtained from Xinyang and Zhishan in Henan Province, respectively. RS came from Tonghua in Jilin Province and GC was collected from Longxi in Gansu Province. All herbs were verified as authentic by professor Pan Chengxue from the School of Pharmacy at Zhengzhou University. Fetal bovine serum was purchased from Gemini (Beijing, China), DMEM high glucose medium, 1640 medium, trypsin, and sodium carboxymethyl cellulose (CMC-Na) were obtained from Beijing Suolaibao Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). The cell counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) Kit was acquired from Zhengzhou Deya Technology Co., Ltd. (Zhengzhou, China), the DNMT1, DNMT3a, DNMT3b, TET2 Enzyme Detection Kit was sourced from Shanghai Linguapure Biotech Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China), DNMT1, DNMT3a, DNMT3b and TET2 antibody were obtained from Wuhan Sanying Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Wuhan, China). Goat anti-rabbit Igg (H + L) as the secondary antibodywas purchased from Wuhan Sanying Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Wuhan, China). RNApure High Purity Total RNA Extraction Kit was purchased in Beijing Biotech Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China), Real-time PCR primers were obtained from Shanghai Sangon Bioengineering Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China).

Cell line

Human hepatoma cell line HepG2 and mouse hepatoma cell line H22 were purchased from Wuhan Punosai Life Science and Technology Co., Ltd. (Wuhan, China), HepG2 cells were cultured in DMEM medium, H22 cells were cultured in 1640 medium, and cultured in 37 °C, 5% CO2 incubator.

Animals preparations

Thirty-two 8-week-old SPF Kunming mice (20 ± 2) g were purchased from Spyford Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). The mice were fed in the IVC system animal feeding room of Zhengzhou University. The animal feeding environment maintained a 12-hour alternating circadian rhythm, and the indoor temperature was maintained at 18−22 °C, and the relative humidity was maintained at 40%−60%. The experimental procedures and programs are in line with the ethical review standards for experimental animals of Zhengzhou University, with the approval number: yxyllsc20220048. Animal welfare and experimental processes are in accordance with the ethical review standards for experimental animals of Zhengzhou University.

Preparation of compound

The dry extract of each medicinal material was prepared according to the following concentration. YJH: 0.129 6 g/g, XKC: 0.025 4 g/g, RS: 0.129 0 g/g, and GC: 0.116 0 g/g. The master batches of the compound of monarch and minister (Jun Chen in Chinese, JC), the compound of monarch, minister, and assistant (Jun Chen Zuo in Chinese, JCZ), and the compound of monarch, minister, assistant, and messenger ((Jun Chen Zuo Shi in Chinese, JCZS) extracts were prepared to a final concentration of 400 mg/mL and diluted with complete medium to achieve the desired concentration at the time of use. The compound with the ratio of JC of 1:1 was marked as JC1, the compound with the ratio of JC of 1.5:1 was marked as JC2, the compound with the ratio of JC of 2:1 was marked as JC3, the compound with the ratio of JC of 3:1 was marked as JC4. The proportion of JCZ was 30:15:10. YJH, XKC, RS, and GC were combined according to a specific raw drug ratio, then alcohol-extracted, concentrated using rotary evaporation, and freeze-dried to obtain a dry extract of YJHFF with a raw drug content of 7.61 g/g. This extract was stored at −20 °C for later use. Quality control information can be found in supplementary materials.

Cell viability experiment

HepG2 cells were seeded at a density of 2 × 104/well in 96-well plates. H22 cells were seeded at a density of 2 × 104/well in 96-well plates. The cells were cultured at 37 °C in a 5% CO2 incubator. The drug-containing medium was replaced after the cells were adherent. The test concentrations of J, C, Z and S drugs were 250, 500, 750 and 1 000 µg/mL, and the concentrations of JC, JCZ and YJHFF groups were 250, 500, 750, 1 000, 1 250 and 1 500 µg/mL, 0.2 mL per well, and a negative control group was set (He et al., 2024, Ma et al., 2018, Qian et al., 2021). The experiment was repeated three times. After 24, 48, and 72 h, the cell viability was determined by the CCK-8 reagent. The absorbance values were read at 450 nm using an enzyme-labeled instrument. The cell viability and the half maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) were calculated. The percentage of cell viability is calculated using the following equation:Cell viability (%) = (Absorbance sample − Absorbance blank) / (Absorbance control − Absorbance blank) × 100

Real-time polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR)

Cells were seeded into six-well plates at 1 × 105 cells/well. After 24 h, the medium was replaced with 2 mL of different concentrations of drug-containing medium. The low and high concentrations of YJH and GC were 500 and 1 000 µg/mL, respectively. The low and high concentrations of XKC and RS were 500 and 600 µg/mL, respectively. The total drug concentrations of JC in combination were 500, 750 and 1 000 µg/mL, and the total drug concentrations of JCZ and YJHFF in combination were 500 and 1 000 µg/mL (He et al., 2024, Ma et al., 2018, Qian et al., 2021). Total RNA was extracted by Trizol method and cDNA was synthesized by reverse transcription. RT-PCR reaction was carried out by two-step method to synthesize the target gene. Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

Cells were seeded into six-well plates at 1 × 105 cells/well. After the cells grew into a single layer, they were replaced with 2 mL drug-containing medium with 250, 500, and 1 000 μg/mL of YJHFF (He et al., 2024, Ma et al., 2018, Qian et al., 2021). A blank control group was set up, and three duplicate wells were set for each concentration. Cells suspension were collected after 72 h of administration. According to the kit instructions for testing.

Western blotting

HepG2 cells in the logarithmic growth phase were cultured in large dishes at a density of 5.0 × 105/well. The cells were incubated with 250, 500, 1 000 μg/mL of compound (He et al., 2024, Ma et al., 2018, Qian et al., 2021). After the cells adhered to the wall, a drug-containing medium was added and incubated for 72 h. Following incubation, the cells were collected, and the lysate was added to extract the total protein. The protein concentration was determined by the BCA method. The protein samples were subjected to sodium dodecyl sulfate–polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis, transferred to PVDF membrane, blocked in 5% skim milk for 2 h, and added with a primary antibody (dilution ratio: DNMT1, 1:2 000; DNMT3a, 1:10 000; DNMT3b,1:26 000; TET2, 1:2 000) 4 °C overnight. The next day, the membrane was washed with TBST for 5 min, washed 6 times, added with a secondary antibody (1:15 000), and incubated on a decolorization shaker at room temperature for 2 h. After washing, the membrane was developed using ECL luminescence, and the images were analyzed using Image-J software.

Animal experiments

Establishment of orthotopic transplantation model of HCC

The orthotopic transplantation model for H22 liver cancer was established in Kunming mice after one week of acclimation. The mice were anesthetized with an intraperitoneal injection of 4% chloral hydrate at a dosage of 0.01 mL/g. After shaving and disinfecting the area, an incision of about 1 cm was made on the lower left side of the mouse’s abdomen to isolate the left lobe of the liver. Using a 25 μL microsyringe, the fourth generation H22 cells (5 × 107 cells/mL) in logarithmic growth phase were slowly injected along the long axis of the liver lobe. The left lobe was then gently placed back into the abdominal cavity, and the surgery was completed by sprinkling a small amount of sodium ampicillin on the wound before suturing the abdominal wall layer by layer (Rao et al., 2018).

Animal grouping and administration

Mice were randomly divided into four groups: a control group, a model group, a 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) group, and a YJHFF group (YJHFF), with five mice in each group. Through the preliminary experiment, the drug was administered on the 8 th day of tumor implantation. The optimal concentration of YJHFF for gavage was determined to be 5 g/kg (based on the amount of raw drug). The mice in the YJHFF group were intragastrically administered with 0.5 g/mL YJHFF dissolved in 0.5% CMC-Na solution, the mice in the control group and the model group were intragastrically administered with 0.5% CMC-Na solution, and the mice in the 5-FU group were intraperitoneally injected with 5-FU (25 mg/kg). All groups of mice received the gavage at a volume of 0.01 mL/g once a day at a fixed time for 14 d.

Efficacy evaluation indicators

(1) Determination of tumor inhibition rate and organ index

Mice were weighed daily. After blood sampling, the organs were quickly removed, and filter paper was used to absorb any surface liquid. The organs were then weighed and photographed. Tumor tissues were separated, one portion was fixed in a 4% paraformaldehyde solution while the other was frozen and stored at −80 °C for future experiments. The tumor tissue, thymus, spleen, and liver were carefully removed, weighed, and photographed. From this data, the tumor inhibition rate and organ index were calculated using the following formulas:

Tumor inhibition rate = (1−mean tumor weight of administered group/mean tumor weight of model group) × 100%.Organ index (g/g) = organ weight (g) /body weight (g) × 100%

(2) Hematoxylin eosin staining (HE), terminal-deoxynucleoitidyl transferase (TUNEL), and Kiel 67 (Ki67) staining

Mouse tumor tissues were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for one week and then stained with HE, TUNEL, and Ki67 to observe the effects of YJHFF on tumor cell morphology, proliferation, and apoptosis.

(3) ELISA (In animal experiments)

The liver tissue (1 g) from each group of mice was weighed, then the levels of superoxide dismutase (SOD) and malondialdehyde (MDA) were measured in the liver tissue following the kit’s instructions according to the method under 2.7. Blood samples were collected from the eyeballs of mice in vivo. The samples were then refrigerated at 4 °C for either 2 h or overnight. After that, they were centrifuged at 3 000 r/min for 20 min to separate the serum. The serum levels of alanine transaminase (ALT), aspartate transaminase (AST), and alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) were measured in each group of mice according to the kit instructions.

(4) Immunofluorescence staining

The liver was removed and the clean tumor tissue was isolated. The HCC tissue was fixed with paraformaldehyde for more than 24 h. Immunofluorescence staining operations were performed on liver cancer tissues, including experimental operations such as dehydration, embedding, sectioning, rewarming and fixation of tissues, and incubation of corresponding antibodies, etc. This study investigated the infiltration of CD3+ T, CD4+ T, CD8+ T cells, and M1/M2 macrophages in tumor tissues and the effect of YJHFF on the balance of DNMTs/TET2 in mice. Immunofluorescence operation is referred to the above operation.

(5) RT-PCR (In animal experiments)

A small amount of normal liver tissues, paracancerous tissues (liver tissue 2 cm away from the tumor), and tumor tissues in mice were taken respectively in the ep tube and fully ground in TRIzol reagent, and then RT-PCR was performed according to the method under “2.6”.

Data processing

Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Protein bands were analyzed semi-quantitatively in grayscale using ImageJ software. Statistical analysis was conducted using GraphPad Prism 8.0 and SPSS 17.0. Differences between groups were assessed using a t-test, with a P-value of less than 0.05 considered statistically significant.

Effects of YJHFF on CD3+ T, CD4+ T, CD8+ T and M1/M2 type macrophage infiltration in mouse tumor tissues

T cells are the main anti-tumor immune cells in the body, and their increase may promote the immune response in the tumor microenvironment and enhance the killing effect on tumor cells. M2 macrophages are usually associated with immunosuppression and tumor growth in the tumor microenvironment, and their reduction may help reduce the immunosuppressive effect of the tumor microenvironment. M1 macrophages are usually associated with tumor cell killing and immune stimulation (Gao, Gong, Lu, Hao, & Shi, 2024). As shown in Fig. 8 B, the fluorescence intensity of CD3+ T, CD4+ T, and CD8+ T cells in tumor tissues was significantly elevated in YJHFF group compared to the model group. In contrast, the fluorescence intensity of these T cells in the tumor tissues of the positive drug 5-FU group showed no significant change. This suggests that YJHFF could enhance the infiltration of CD3+, CD4+, and CD8+ T cells in tumor tissues, which promotes the immune response in the tumor microenvironment. The fluorescence intensity of M1-type macrophages in the tumor tissues of YJHFF group increased, while the fluorescence intensity of M2-type macrophages decreased when compared to the model group. For the 5-FU group, however, there was no significant change in the fluorescence intensity of M1-type macrophages, but the intensity of M2-type macrophages increased (Fig. 8C). These findings indicate that YJHFF can enhance the infiltration of M1 macrophages, which can inhibit tumor growth and enhance the immune response of the immune microenvironment. At the same time, YJHFF reduces the presence of M2 macrophages that promote tumor growth and reduces the immunosuppressive effect of the tumor microenvironment.

Effects of YJHFF on DNMTs/TET2 balance relationship in mice

There was no statistically significant difference in the mRNA expression levels of DNMT1, DNMT3a, and DNMT3b among normal liver tissues (CL), the para carcinoma tissues of the model group (ML), and the para carcinoma tissues of YJHFF group (FL) (P > 0.05). However, in comparison to the para carcinoma tissues of the model group (ML), the mRNA expression of DNMT1, DNMT3a, and DNMT3b was significantly up-regulated in the tumor tissues of the model group (MT) (P < 0.01), while the mRNA expression of TET2 was significantly down-regulated (P < 0.01). Additionally, when comparing the para carcinoma tissues in YJHFF group (FL), the mRNA expression levels of DNMT1, DNMT3a, and DNMT3b were also up-regulated in the tumor tissues of YJHFF group (FT) (P < 0.01), whereas TET2 mRNA expression was down-regulated (P < 0.01) (Fig. 8E).

The immunofluorescence results correlated well with the RT-PCR findings. Compared to the tumor tissues from the model group, those from YJHFF group exhibited a decrease in fluorescence signals for DNMT1, DNMT3a, and DNMT3b, along with an increase in the fluorescence signal for TET2 (Fig. 8D). Experimental results indicated that YJHFF did not significantly regulate DNMT1, DNMT3a, DNMT3b, and TET2 in the para cancerous tissues. However, it effectively reduced the abnormally high expression levels of DNMT1, DNMT3a, and DNMT3b in the tumor tissues while simultaneously increasing the abnormally low expression of TET2, which can help restore the balance of DNMTs and TET2 in the tumor tissues, contributing to its anti-HCC effects.

Discussion

Some studies have compared and analyzed the expression differences of DNMTs in liver cancer and adjacent tissues, and found that the expression of DNMTs in liver cancer tissues was significantly up-regulated (Oh et al., 2007). The decrease of TET2 content can lead to tumor deterioration. TET2 acts as a tumor suppressor gene in the development of liver cancer, which can inhibit the differentiation and metastasis of liver cancer cells (Chen, 2019, Deng, 2017, Yu, 2018). It is reasonable to use the balance of DNMTs/TET2 as another indicator for the study of the compatibility mechanism of TCM compound.

In our experiment, the results of the CCK-8 experimental study indicated that the JC exhibited a stronger inhibitory effect on cell viability compared to the single drug in general. The JCZ also showed a similar inhibitory effect on the cell viability of HepG2 cells as JC, but its effect on H22 cells was more pronounced than that of JC. In contrast, YJHFF demonstrated reduced inhibitory effects on cell viability for both cells. While it showed similar inhibitory effects on H22 cells compared to the single drug, its impact on HepG2 cell viability was generally more significant. Overall, the experimental results indicated that YJHFF had anti-HCC effects.

The results of RT-PCR experimental studies showed that monarch, minister, adjuvant, and messenger drugs all had a regulatory effect on the balance of DNMTs/TET2. The JC was able to down-regulate the expression of DNMT1 and DNMT3b mRNA and up-regulate the expression of TET2 mRNA in HepG2 cells, which was not as effective as that of the single drug. The JCZ compound was able to regulate the balance of DNMTs/TET2, and its regulatory effect was even better than the single drug and JC. YJHFF can also regulate the balance of DNMTs/TET2. The results suggest that YJHFF may exert anticancer effects by regulating the balance of DNMTs/TET2.

Meanwhile, the effects of YJHFF on the regulation of the DNMTs/TET2 balance were further investigated using ELISA and Western blot techniques. The experimental results demonstrated that YJHFF down-regulated the expression levels of DNMT1, DNMT3a, and DNMT3b mRNA, as well as their corresponding proteins, in Hep G2 cells. Conversely, it up-regulated the expression levels of TET2 mRNA and TET2 protein. These findings suggest that YJHFF may exert anti-HCC effects by regulating the balance between DNMTs and TET2.

The consistency between the results of regulating the balance of DNMTs/TET2 and the cell viability experiments suggests that the role of regulating the balance of DNMTs/TET2 can be used as an indicator to study the mechanism of action of TCM against HCC.

To better evaluate the anti-HCC effects of YJHFF, a series of in vivo experiments were conducted. Through systematic evaluation of the effects induced by YJHFF on murine body weight dynamics, organ coefficient indices, oxidative stress biomarkers, and hepatic functional parameters, the experimental data substantiate its potent therapeutic efficacy against HCC, as evidenced by statistically significant amelioration of pathological manifestations associated with HCC progression. The effects include inhibiting the growth of in situ transplanted tumors, suppressing the proliferation of HCC cells, and inducing apoptosis in these cells, thus exerting a direct anti-HCC effect. Additionally, it improves the immune microenvironment, enhances the body’s anti-tumor immunity, and regulates the balance of DNMTs and TET2 in HCC tissues.

The results suggest that it is feasible to use the balance of DNMTs/TET2 as an evaluation indicator for the study of the compatibility mechanism of TCM compound.

Conclusion

This study shows that the results of DNMTs/TET2 balance are consistent with the results of cell viability, which can reflect the efficacy of traditional Chinese medicine against HCC, and regulating DNMTs/TET2 balance can improve the immune microenvironment and enhance the body’s immunity. The balance of DNMTs/TET2 was used as an indicator of the anti-HCC effect of traditional Chinese medicine, which provided ideas for the study of the anti-HCC mechanism of YJHFF and provided a new method for the clarification of the anti-HCC mechanism of traditional Chinese medicine compound.

Factors associated with primary cesarean section delivery among women who gave birth at Wolaita Sodo University comprehensive specialized hospital, Southern Ethiopia
Authors: Adisu Ashiko, Cherinet Tilahun, Temesgen Geta, Awoke Abraham, Getu Kassaye
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractA cesarean section can be a crucial medical intervention when necessary. The primary cesarean section has become a major factor contributing to the increasing rate of cesarean section deliveries globally, especially in developing countries like Ethiopia. Therefore this study aimed to identify prevalence and factors that influence primary cesarean section delivery. The facility-based cross-sectional study was conducted at Wolaita Sodo University comprehensive and specialized hospital from June 10 to September 10, 2024. Systematic sampling method used to collect 403 sampled populations. Data were entered into EpiData version 4.6 and then exported to Statistical package of software for social science (SPSS) version 27 for analysis. Both bivariable and multivariable logistic regressions were used to assess the associations. The occurrence of multicollinearity was checked for the final model. Hosmer and Lemeshow’s goodness-of-fit test with a large p value (p>0.05) was checked to see good model fitness. Only variables with p < 0.05 were reserved in the final model. A total of 392 participants with 97.3% response rate, the prevalence of primary cesarean section delivery were 28.57% (95% CI (21.2-36.4)). The primigravida mothers (adjusted odds ratio (AOR) 2.820 with 95% confidence interval (CI): (1.058-7.521)), fetal distress (AOR= 95% CI: 3.352(1.133-9.922)), and mothers with fetal macrosomia (AOR=95% CI: 6.325(1.714-13.271)) were more likely to undergo cesarean section. The utilization of the partograph during labor is negatively associated with primary cesarean section (AOR=95% CI: 0.301(0.131-0.691)). The likelihood of a primary cesarean section delivery is significantly higher in women with fetal distress, primigravida, and fetal macrosomia. However, the use of the partograph reduces unnecessary cesarean section deliveries, lowers associated risks, and improves outcomes. Therefore health facility should experience on partograph utilization. Introduction

A cesarean section (CS) is a surgical procedure performed to safely deliver a viable fetus, placenta, and membrane through an abdominal and uterine incision1. It is further divided into the primary CS or first-time operation delivery during her life and repeated cesarean delivery2. It is an important life-saving surgical intervention that can significantly reduce maternal and perinatal mortality and morbidity rates1,3. Furthermore, the frequency of cesarean section serves as an indicator of a country’s maternal healthcare. However, cesarean section deliveries are more challenging compared to vaginal deliveries, especially in developing countries4,5.

Cesarean section can effectively prevent adverse obstetric outcomes and significantly reduce maternal and perinatal mortality when performed promptly6,7. The World Health Organization (WHO) recommends that the ideal rate of cesarean section deliveries should fall within the range of 5 to 15% to guarantee effective and essential medical interventions for both mothers and infants7. However, the limited availability of health facilities equipped with necessary instruments and skilled health professionals creates challenges in resource-limited settings such as sub-Saharan Africa6,8–10. In Ethiopia, a significant portion of the population lacks access to essential obstetric care. The cesarean section rates are higher for first births (4.3%) compared to subsequent births10.

The escalating rate of cesarean section deliveries worldwide, frequently performed for both obstetric and non-obstetric reasons has emerged as a significant public health concern11,12. Globally, 21.1% of women gave birth via caesarean section; averages vary from 42.8% in Latin America and the Caribbean to 5% in sub-Saharan Africa. While sub-Saharan Africa and Northern America experienced the lowest rises, Eastern Asia (44.9 %), Western Asia (34.7 %), and Northern Africa (31.7 %) experienced the largest increases. According to projections, 28.5% of women globally will give birth via cesarean section by 2030 (of which 38 million occur in low and middle income country’s each year), with sub-Saharan Africa accounting for 7.1% and Eastern Asia for 63.4%12.

The primary cesarean section has become a major factor driving the overall cesarean section rate, accounting for more than two-thirds of all cesarean section deliveries in developed countries13. The strategies to reduce the frequency of this procedure include avoiding unnecessary primary cesarean sections and promoting vaginal birth after a previous cesarean section if appropriate14. Several countries also revealed that there was no consistent rise in cesarean section deliveries, which was attributed to various factors9.

There are a number of indications that are significantly linked to unnecessary caesarean sections, including the mother’s desire for a caesarean section, the influence of the husband, friends, and family14, and the desire of healthcare providers to reduce the stress of labor monitoring3,14. It may expose women to both immediate and long-term health issues6,8. Due to the potential of complications such preterm birth, hypoxia, newborn death, surgical site infection, recurrent cesarean section, maternal mortality, and more frequent infectious issues that prolong the mother’s hospital stay37,10,. Economic, social, and psychological challenges are also impacted through a burden14.

Maternal and neonatal complications related with child birth can occur during at any type of delivery. To address these issues a comprehensive approach is required such as identifying problems, and enhancing quality of maternal care. Conducting timely research and implementing targeted strategies can help reduce it. However, most research in Ethiopia trends to focus broadly rather than specific problems. Additionally, there is limited research conducted on primary cesarean section. Therefore the aim of the study is to determine the prevalence, associated factors, and outcomes of primary cesarean section delivery among laboring mothers. It will serve as baseline of future research and intervention strategies to address and fills gaps of maternal and fetus related problem with unnecessary cesarean deliveries.

Methods and materials

Study area

The study was conducted at Wolaita Sodo University Comprehensive and Specialized Hospital. Wolaita Sodo University Hospital is located 320 kilometers away from Addis Ababa, the capital of Ethiopia. It was established in 1927 by the Sudan Interior Mission (SIM). It served as a primary hospital for about 50 years, then as a general hospital for 20 years, and since 2020 G.C, it has been upgraded to a comprehensive specialized hospital. It serves over 7 million people in south Ethiopia region.

Study period and study design

The facility-based cross-sectional study design was conducted from June 10 to September 10, 2024

Source population

All pregnant women who gave birth at Wolaita Sodo university comprehensive specialized hospital during study period.

Study population

A woman delivered at Wolaita Sodo University hospital during study period that are eligible for inclusion during data collection.

Inclusion criteria

All women who gave birth to a baby at a gestational age of 28 weeks or more were included in the study.

Exclusion criteria

Women with a history of cesarean section and imminent uterine rupture repair, those with a gestational age below 28 weeks, and those with serious mental health issues were excluded from the study.

Sample size determination

The sample size was determined using the single population formula by Epi info version 7.1 and calculated based on the following assumptions: a 95% confidence interval (CI), a 5% margin of error, and an overall cesarean delivery proportion of 39.1%, which was obtained from a study conducted at Debretabor Comprehensive Specialized Hospital3. So, the final calculated sample size with a 10% non-response rate was 403.

Sampling technique and procedure

A systematic random sampling technique was used to collect data. The collection was held at postnatal ward selecting patients who had given birth after reaching a gestational age of more than 28 weeks at the hospital. The data was collected over a three-month period. Reference of corresponding season there were a total of 1728 deliveries. Based on this, a skip interval (k) of 4 was determined. The first respondent was selected using a lottery method from the four participants.

Data collection and quality control

Data collection was conducted using a modified structured checklist, which was developed from various literature sources2–4,11–15. The checklist included socio-demographic characteristics, fetal characteristics, any chronic medical or gynecological conditions, and current obstetric factors. It also included information about the history of previous cesarean deliveries and the type of primary cesarean delivery (emergency or elective). A pretest was conducted with 5% of the study group at Humbo Primary Hospital in Wolaita Zone, Ethiopia, to ensure the reliability of the data collection tools. The data collectors received one day of training on the study objectives, data collection methods, and ethical considerations. The checklist was regularly reviewed to ensure consistency and completeness, and the entire data collection process was supervised by the lead investigators and supervisors. Three senior midwives were responsible for collecting the data, under the supervision of one master in maternity nursing.

Conceptual definition

Cesarean section

A cesarean section (CS) is a surgical procedure that involves making an incision on the anterior abdominal wall and uterus to deliver a viable fetus and placenta1,15.

Primary cesarean section

Refers to the first-time surgical delivery of a baby after fetal viability, which occurs after 28 weeks of gestational age in the context of Ethiopia15.

Repeated cesarean section

This refers to a surgical procedure performed on a pregnant woman who has previously undergone a cesarean birth15.

Destructive delivery

It is fetal surgical procedures that reduce the size of a dead fetus to facilitate vaginal delivery2,15.

Data analysis and management

The data were entered into Epi data version 4.6 and then exported to SPSS version 27 for statistical analysis. Descriptive statistics were used to explain the study populations with relevant variables. Both bivariable and multivariable logistic regressions were utilized to evaluate the relationship between the outcome and explanatory variables. Variables that are found statistically significant in the bivariable analysis (p<0.25) were entered into a multivariable logistic regression model. During the analysis, variables with a p-value less than 0.25 in bivariabe analysis were included in multivariable logistic regression. The occurrence of multicollinearity was checked for the final model. There is no collinearity among candidate variables. An effort was made to assess whether the necessary assumptions for the application of multivariable logistic regression were fulfilled. In this regard, the Hosmer and Lemeshow’s goodness-of-fit test was checked to see good model fitness (P value 0.94). Only variables with p < 0.05 were reserved in the final model. The associations and their strength were presented using odds ratios, 95% confidence intervals, and p-values. Finally a p-value less than 0.05 were considered to be significantly associated.

Socio demographic characteristics

A total of 392 respondents participated in the study making 97.3% of response rate. The mean age of the participants was 26.01 years, with a standard deviation of 5.29 years. The age of the participants ranged from 18 to 37 years. The majority of the participants were between the ages of 20 and 29. More than half of the study participants, 235 (59.9%), lived in rural areas, and 383 (97.7%) were married. The majority of the study participants, 275 (70.2%), were Protestant followers followed by orthodox followers, 26%. Regarding the educational status of the participants, 106 (27%) had completed primary school, followed by 23% who had completed secondary school. The occupation of 242 participants (61.7%) was housewives, and more than half of the 224 participants (57.1%) had a monthly income of less than 4000 birr (Table 1).

Obstetrical characteristics of respondents

More than half (59.9%) of the women were multiparous, and the majority (86%) had received antenatal care (ANC) bookings. The majority of participants, 69.1%, had three or more antenatal care (ANC) visits. Spontaneous labor occurred in 85.5% of cases, while 11.5% of labor was induced or augmented. Furthermore, 9.3% of the respondents did not experience any labor at all. For 86.5% of the study participants, the gestational age ranged from 37 to 42 weeks. The majority of CS deliveries (89.3%) were performed as emergency procedures. About 63.6% of the study participants’ labor had been monitored using a partograph. Mode of delivery and outcome

In terms of the mode of delivery, 28.57% (95% CI: 21.2-36.4) of mothers underwent a primary cesarean section, while the remaining 69.13% had a spontaneous vaginal delivery. Additionally, 2.04% had an instrumental delivery and 0.25% had a destructive delivery.

The delivery results indicated that the majority of babies, 387 (98.24%), were born normal alive, while a small percentage, 0.76%, were stillborn, experienced intrauterine fetal demise (IUFD), or had early neonatal death. Among the birth outcomes, 371(94.64%) had a normal birth weight, 16(4.08%) were macrosomic (larger than average), and 5(1.3%) had a low birth weight. Approximately 6.89% of newborns admitted to the neonatal intensive care unit (NICU) (Table 2).

The most common reasons for PCS included fetal distress, accounting for 25% of cases, followed by fetal macrosomia at 23.21% and cephalopelvic disproportion (CPD) at 17.86%. Factors associated with primary cesarean section

After including all candidate variables, four variables were found to be significantly associated with PCS in the multivariable logistic regression model. The likelihood of a primary cesarean section was 2.82 times higher among primigravida women than multigravida mothers (AOR= 95% CI: 2.820(1.058-7.521)). Mothers who experienced fetal distress were 3.35 times more likely to undergo cesarean section than those without fetal distress (AOR= 3.352, 95% CI: 1.133-9.922). Among mothers with fetal macrosomia, the likelihood of having a primary cesarean section was 6.32 times higher than for babies of normal weight (AOR=95% CI: 6.315(1.714-13.271)). Mothers who used a partograph during labor were 70% less likely to undergo PCS compared to those who did not use a partograph (AOR=0.301, 95% CI: 0.131-0.691) (Table 4).

Discussion

A woman with a cesarean section scar is undergoing an increase. One significant factor contributing to the overall increase in cesarean section (CS) rates is the rising number of primary CS procedures performed for unnecessary indications and easily manageable reasons. However, for low-risk conditions, CS delivery appears to pose more risk than vaginal delivery16.

In this study 28.57% of patients underwent their first CS delivery. The prevalence of PCS in this study was higher than that reported in a previous study conducted at Suhul General Public Hospital in Tigray, which reported a rate of 20.2% (5), Addis Ababa (13.3%), Butajira (16.6%)17,18 and Tanzania (21.6%), Egypt (21.3%), Iraq (21.1%), and Lagos (16.7%)19–22.

The prevalence of cesarean section has increased with many cesarean sections being performed on medical and non-medical indications. It may be due to patient preference such as socio demographic status like age and economic status affects intrapartium and outcome of delivery. The diagnostic and clinical experience of health professional’s capability of detecting complication and fear of complication and responsibility. The institutional setup preference such as lack of preferable delivery instrument like destructive set or spontaneous vaginal delivery set may increase PCS.

The study revealed a negative correlation between the use of the partograph and the incidence of primary cesarean section. Laboring women who were monitored using a partograph had 70% less likely of undergoing a primary cesarean section compared to those who were not monitored by parthograph. This study is supported by a case-control study conducted in Addis Ababa and Greece23,24. The use of the partograph reduces the risk of cesarean sections and related complications24. This finding contradicts a study conducted in Dessie town, Northeast Ethiopia, which indicated that laboring mothers who were monitored using a partograph were more likely to undergo cesarean delivery than those who were not monitored6. The partograph supports the early detections of abnormal labor and aiding timely management.

The study showed that mothers with a fetal macrosomia (birth weight >4kg) are 6.3 times more likely to undergo PCS than those who did not have diagnosed fetal macrosomia. This finding is consistent with studies conducted in Tigray, Addis Ababa, and Turkey5,23,25.

The over diagnosis of fetal macrosomia as a sole reason for performing a cesarean section is not solely based on an objective evaluation using ultrasound scans, but also influenced by subjective factors and the patient’s obstetric history. It as a risk factors due to fear of increasing risk of complication of labor like prolonged labor, obstructed labor, shoulder dystocia, may result post-partum hemorrhage, perineal tear, uterine rupture and maternal and neonatal injuries26.

The present study was found that primigravida mothers were significantly associated with primary cesarean delivery. The likelihood of having a primary cesarean section was 2.82 times higher among primigravida women than multigravida mothers. This study is supported by a study conducted in Addis Ababa23. This finding is inconsistent with a retrospective study conducted in Turkey25. Currently, primigravida women face an increased risk of cesarean section (CS) attributable to various factors. Nulliparous women exhibit a lack of experience with labor and express apprehension regarding labor pain. A cesarean section may be conducted in the absence of explicit indications based on maternal requests, enhanced safety perceptions, or the absence of experience for vaginal birth27.

Non-reassuring fetal heart rate pattern was a major indication for undergoing a cesarean section. In this study, fetuses experiencing distress were more likely to be delivered via cesarean section than those with a normal fetal heartbeat. Perhaps it is consistent with the study conducted in Tigray and Turkey5,25. Inaccurate identification of fetal distress and its limited ability to predict the outcome of the fetus often lead to unnecessary caesarean sections. Conversely, the absence of adverse outcomes may indicate the satisfaction of health workers with the situation.

Limitation of study

Since the cross-sectional nature of the research design, it was unable to determine causal relationships between the outcome variables and their predictors, nor delve into the participants’ in-depth experiences and viewpoints. The clear diagnosis and indications possesses some constraints; there may be a lack of uniformity in the definition of indications and subjective diagnosis.

Conclusion

This study reveals a high prevalence of PCS and collectively highlights the various factors that can contribute to the likelihood of undergoing this procedure. The probability of having a primary cesarean section is notably higher in women with fetal distress, fetal macrosomia and primigravida. Additionally, we have determined that monitoring laboring mothers with a partograph can help prevent unnecessary cesarean sections. Therefore health facility should experience on partograph utilization and health education on birth preparedness or complication plan. Pregnant women should have antenatal follow up early to early identification of risks and encouraged to deliver at health facility.

A computer vision-based framework for objective evaluation of sunken upper eyelid
Authors: Longfei Weng, Yuchen Shen, Shiqi Xie, Meng Xun, Zhaohui Bu, Hui Chen, Guichuan Chen, Li Ding
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractSunken upper eyelid correction is clinically and aesthetically important in periorbital reconstruction. However, current evaluation methods for sunken upper eyelid are limited by the expensive and complex medical equipment or subjective judgment. This paper proposes a single image assessment framework based on computer vision (CV) that enables objective evaluation of sunken upper eyelid morphology. The method consists of two stages: The first stage is performed to establish a consistent analysis foundation. Facial landmarks are detected using a 106-point model to crop the periocular region, followed by resolution normalization and eyelid subregion segmentation. In the second stage, three clinically relevant features are extracted: Variance of Gray Value (VGV) for surface depression, Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) for bilateral symmetry, and Degree of Eyelid Wrinkles (DEW) for texture irregularity. These features are integrated into a Support Vector Machine (SVM) model, which outputs the L2 distance to the separating hyperplane (D(f)) to score overall morphological features. Statistical results on the samples showed significant differences in VGV, SSIM, and DEW between normal group and patient group. Based on the proposed method, most patients had a positive postoperative to preoperative difference of ΔD, indicating measurable improvements in surgical outcomes.Keywords: Image assessment, Computer vision, Sunken upper eyelid, Support vector machineSubject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Diseases, Health care, Medical research

Introduction

Sunken upper eyelid correction surgery is a common reconstructive procedure1–3. Its main purpose is to correct the sunken deformity from the upper eyelid margin to the orbital rim area, restoring the natural flow of the eyelid contour. This surgery not only improves visual function and eyelid closure, but also enhances facial appearance, positively contributing to patient quality of life and psychological well-being. Currently, there are two main approaches for surgical outcome evaluation. The first involves objective measurement using medical imaging equipments such as MRI and CT, as well as clinical measurement tools like the exophthalmometer4–6. The second relies on subjective clinical judgment made by attending physicians7. Although the former approach offers objectivity and standardization, it imposes significant economic and time burdens on patients. This is mainly due to high equipment costs, complex operating procedures, and the inability to conduct real-time dynamic evaluations. The latter method heavily depends on the surgeon’s experience and is prone to subjective cognitive biases, leading to poor reproducibility and insufffcient objectivity in the evaluation results. Furthermore,none of these methods can balance efficiency and accuracy, affecting communication between doctor and patient.

In recent years, with the popularization of photography technology, image analysis-based methods for postoperative aesthetic evaluation have gradually gained attention8–11. This method has become an essential tool for recording and monitoring patient recovery. It provides an intuitive reference for doctors in diagnosis, surgical planning, and outcome evaluation. Tosan et al.8 utilized preoperative and postoperative static photographs from frontal and lateral views in the evaluation of open rhinoplasty. They calibrated the images using the interpupillary distance and the distance from the glabella to the pogonion. They measured the upper lip length in the frontal view and the positions of the subnasale and labrale superius in the lateral view. Finally, the surgical outcomes were assessed using a one-sample t-test. Gomez-Rice et al.9 used preoperative and postoperative full-body standing clinical photographs for the assessment of postoperative satisfaction in adult spinal deformity surgery. It compares the changes in “self-image” and “satisfaction” scores on the SRS-22 questionnaire of the patients before and after the photographs. It confirming that image analysis can improve patients’ postoperative subjective satisfaction. Raschke et al.10 used standardized frontal photos of open and closed eyes taken preoperatively and 3 months postoperatively for postoperative evaluation of blepharoplasty. The photos were captured by a Nikon D80 camera at a fixed distance. The method manually labeled key points and compared seven geometric metrics before and after. They found significant improvements in postoperative lacrimal index and canthus tilt. Ilke et al.11 evaluated upper blepharoplasty using preoperative and 6 months postoperative frontal full face photos. Their photos were taken by a professional camera under fixed conditions. The images were calibrated by 68-point facial keypoint detection, and geometric parameters such as lid distance and eye opening area were measured. The surgical results were subsequently evaluated by comparing the differences in postoperative parameters. Despite the achievements of these methods, there remain certain limitations. First of all, relying on professional photographic equipment and complex operation process, it is difficult to realize the immediate feedback of postoperative results. It requires specialized skills and is time-consuming, making it difficult to be popularized. Second, feature selection is limited to geometric parameters (e.g., distance and area), ignoring key morphological metrics such as symmetry and texture features. Thus, developing a low - cost, convenient, multi - scenario - applicable assessment method is urgent. This will enhance the assessment of sunken upper eyelid and boost its clinical application value.

Since the human visual system is highly sensitive to the three morphological features of luminance, symmetry, and texture, this paper proposes a computer vision-based method for assessment of sunken upper eyelid. The proposed method employs two stages. In the initial stage, we transformed the original frontal facial photographs into standardized images suitable for analysis through a step-by-step processing framework. First, Face++ 106-point facial recognition model is used to accurately label key eye points such as the corners of the eyes and the edges of the eyelids. This step provides geometric anchor points for subsequent spatial alignment. Next, based on these points, a rectangular region of interest (ROI) is defined. This cropping step isolates the periocular area and effectively filtering out irrelevant background noise. Then, to address the resolution differences caused by different shooting devices, the cropped images are uniformly processed. This processing ensures that all images have consistent clarity and satisfy the recognition requirements of human visual perception. Finally, using the lower edge of the eyebrow as a reference, each eyelid is divided into four small subregions. This division facilitates a more detailed analysis of local feature changes. In the second stage, using the preprocessed data, computer vision techniques were used to extract three morphological features highly relevant to human visual perception. These features are luminance, symmetry, and texture. Given that upper eyelid sunkenness manifests as deepened shadows, poor symmetry, and increased wrinkles, this paper uses these features to construct quantitative indicators. Specifically, the Variance of Gray Value (VGV) is used to measure the uneven distribution of luminance on the eyelid surface12,13. The Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) is employed to quantify the symmetry of bilateral eyelids14–17. Meanwhile, the Degree of Eyelid Wrinkles (DEW) is utilized to describe the fold characteristics of the sunken area18. Finally, by training a support vector machine (SVM) model19, these features are integrated into a quantitative metric, defined as the L2 distance to the separating hyperplane (D(f))20. The difference in this distance between postoperative and preoperative states, denoted as ΔD, is directly correlated with surgical improvement. Positive ΔD values indicate good improvement of the sunken upper eyelid after surgery.

Data collection and preparation

The data for this paper were provided by Chen Hui and Shiqi Xie, comprising clinical records of patients who underwent upper eyelid hollowing surgery from January 2013 to December 2023. The data set primarily comprises frontal facial photographs taken before surgery and three months post-operation. To guarantee the scientific rigor and consistency of the data, the photographic process was conducted following a rigorous and standardized protocol21. Specifically, all photographs required the patients to maintain a neutral expression, look directly at the camera, keep their heads positioned in the central view, and be photographed under controlled indoor lighting conditions that were uniform and shadow-free. The camera parameters, including focal length, resolution, and exposure settings, were meticulously maintained to ensure consistency. These measures aimed to minimize external variable interference with image quality, thereby establishing a reliable foundation for subsequent feature extraction and image analysis.

Ultimately, this paper collected photographic data from 22 patients. After rigorous data screening, nine cases of photographic data failing to meet inclusion criteria were excluded. The first exclusion criterion was inadequate lighting in the shooting environment and substandard image resolution, which impeded the identification of features. The second criterion was a facial angle deviation exceeding 30∘, as it would impact frontal analysis. The third criterion involved the utilization of camera beauty features, since these features could result in the distortion of skin morphological characteristics. Following a thorough examination of the available data, a total of 26 valid photographic records from 13 patients were deemed to be suitable for inclusion in the paper.

Methods

This paper presents a two-stage computer vision-based evaluation framework to assess the results of Sunken upper eyelid correction surgery. In the first stage, standardized image preprocessing is performed,to ensure consistency and comparability across samples. Based on this, the second stage performs multidimensional feature extraction to quantitatively characterize surgical outcomes. These features are then fed into a support vector machine (SVM) model to generate a comprehensive evaluation score. Such the proposed pipeline ensures that the evaluation is both robust and sensitive to clinically relevant changes captured during preprocessing.

Stage 2: multidimensional-feature extraction and evaluation

Building upon the standardized and segmented eyelid regions from Stage 1, the Stage 2 focuses on the quantitative evaluation of surgical outcomes. The objective is to construct a multidimensional index system that captures postoperative changes in sunken upper eyelid. Clinical observations have shown that sunken areas usually exhibit deeper local shadows, decreased bilateral symmetry, and increased skin contour irregularities. Based on these clinical observations, the Variance of Gray Value (VGV), Structural Similarity Index (SSIM), and Degree of Eyelid Wrinkles (DEW) are selected as the core assessment dimensions in this study. VGV assesses the luminance distribution and smoothness of localized surface shadows, SSIM measures the symmetry of bilateral eyelids, and DEW describes the integrity of skin texture around the eyes. Together, these features form the foundation for an interpretable and data-driven evaluation framework. The detailed workflow is organized into two sequential steps.

Step 1: Quantitative Feature Extraction

Step 1: Quantitative Feature Extraction

This step computes three core features from the preprocessed and segmented eyelid images to capture sunken eyelid. Variance of Gray Value (VGV) In our study, local shadows were chosen as they often reflect volume loss in the superior orbital fat, which is a common cause of the sunken upper eyelid. VGV quantifies local light intensity fluctuations within each eyelid region. Higher VGV values are typically associated with uneven shadow distribution in sunken areas. To assess inter-eye consistency, the coefficient of variation (CV) is also calculated between the left and right eyelids.

Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) Decreased bilateral symmetry can indicate unilateral or asymmetric fat atrophy which has important implications for preoperative evaluation. In our framework, SSIM is calculated over four subregions between the bilateral eyelids, providing a robust measure of structural and luminance similarity. Lower SSIM values in patients suggest pronounced asymmetry, often caused by unilateral or uneven degree of eyelid sunkenness.

Degree of Eyelid Wrinkles (DEW) Increased skin contour irregularities may correspond to structural changes such as uneven fat distribution, dermatochalasis, or scar formation, all of which can affect both functional eyelid closure and aesthetic harmony. DEW quantifies skin texture roughness by measuring wrinkle density through edge detection. Vertical sinusoidal weight attenuation is used for subregion Ak,n before detection and was used to minimize false edges caused by eyebrows and eyelashes at the top and bottom of the subregion. Elevated DEW values, particularly in preoperative images, often correspond to traumainduced laxity or aging-related dermal degradation.

These extracted features directly inform the subsequent evaluation model. The implementation procedures are visualized in Fig. 3 and formally described in Supplementary Algorithm1.

Step 2: Evaluation via SVM Modeling

Step 2: Evaluation via SVM Modeling

Following the extraction of morphological features of VGV, SSIM, and DEW from preprocessed images, this stage establishes a quantitative evaluation model using a Support Vector Machine (SVM). These three features jointly define a low-dimensional but semantically rich representation of eyelid morphology.

Calculated plots for VGV, SSIM and DEW.

To replace subjective judgment and rule-based scoring, we employ an SVM classifier trained to separate normal and sunken eyelid conditions. The SVM is trained on labeled feature vectors using a third-order polynomial kernel, enabling it to capture nonlinear relationships between morphological indicators. The model’s objective is to find an optimal hyperplane that maximizes the margin between the two classes, using structural risk minimization and kernel-based projection25. As shown in Fig. 4, the visualized hyperplane shows that the samples in the Patient group have been effectively differentiated from the samples in the Normal group. For each feature vector fi, the model computes a signed L2 distance to the decision boundary D(f) as defined in Supplementary Algorithm2. This distance reflects both the confidence and direction of the classification.

Experiments

To validate the effectiveness of the extracted morphological features of VGV, SSIM, and DEW in distinguishing between normal eyelids and those affected by sunken upper eyelid, we conduct a statistical comparison between the two subject groups. Criteria for subject classification: An evaluation panel consisting of three professional ophthalmologists with extensive clinical experience performed subjective assessments of all participants. The affected group is defined as individuals assessed as having sunken upper eyelid and who undergo the correction surgery. The normal group is defined as individuals assessed by the panel as having no signs of sunken upper eyelid. Independent samples t-tests are applied to evaluate whether the mean values of each feature differ significantly between preoperative patients and healthy controls.

As shown in Table 4, all three features exhibit statistically significant differences between the patient and control groups (p<0.01), confirming their discriminative power in characterizing eyelid depression. VGV: Patients exhibited a significantly higher VGV (0.18±0.08) compared to normal gruop (0.07±0.05), reflecting greater variability in grayscale intensity. This supports the hypothesis that depression leads to more uneven surface contours and shadow distribution. SSIM: The average SSIM in the patient group (0.73±0.08) was significantly lower than in the normal group (0.85±0.04). This indicates reduced inter-eye symmetry in patients, a known visual hallmark of upper eyelid depression. DEW: The degree of eyelid wrinkles was also significantly elevated in patients (204.11±69.24) relative to the control group (75.23±47.13). This supports the association between skin laxity or trauma-induced tension and wrinkle formation. These results substantiate the role of VGV, SSIM, and DEW as objective, quantitative indicators for assessing upper eyelid depression. Their significant group-level separation validates their utility in the SVM-based evaluation framework presented in this study.

To evaluate the practical applicability of the proposed SVM-based morphological assessment model, we compared the hyperplane distance scores before and after surgery for each patient. As described in Section III.B, the difference ΔD=D(fpost)-D(fpre) reflects the relative shift toward the normal class in the learned feature space. The results, visualized in Fig. 5, demonstrate that the majority of patients exhibited a clear positive shift in ΔD, indicating measurable postoperative improvement in eyelid morphology. Specifically, patients whose ΔD values were above zero experienced a directionally consistent change in the feature space that aligns with clinical expectations of successful surgery.

To facilitate a more intuitive interpretation of ΔD and enable comparison across cases, an evaluation panel consisting of three professional ophthalmologists with extensive clinical experience classifies ΔD values into three categories: mild improvement, moderate improvement, and strong improvement, based on their clinical observations of postoperative changes. The thresholds for each category are determined by consensus among the panel members and are as follows: mild improvement (0<ΔD<1.12), moderate improvement (1.12<ΔD<1.81), and strong improvement (ΔD>1.81). These thresholds are defined as the midpoints between the boundary values within the observed data range in the available dataset.

For example, the patient shown in Fig. 6 achieved a substantial increase in hyperplane distance (ΔD=2.54 and 1.57), corroborating the physician’s visual evaluation of improved eyelid contour and symmetry. The extracted features including increased SSIM and reduced DEW confirm the clinical outcome as captured by the model. However, not all cases showed improvement. As seen in Fig. 7 patients exhibited negative ΔD values (-0.52 and -1.91, respectively), indicating a reduction in model-estimated morphological quality post-surgery. Upon further inspection, both cases were classified clinically as “mild” prior to intervention, with subtle depression symptoms that were less prominent during visual diagnosis.

This outcome suggests that the model is sensitive to slight shifts in the extracted morphological features even when clinical assessments might not consider them substantial. The limited surgical effect in such mild cases may be due to the initial lack of severity and a lower potential for visible morphological correction.

Conclusion

This study presents a two-stage evaluation framework that integrates CV-based feature extraction with a SVM model to quantitatively assess surgical outcomes for upper eyelid depression correction. By extracting three clinically relevant morphological features, i.e., Variance of Gray Value (VGV), Structural Similarity Index (SSIM), and Degree of Eyelid Wrinkling (DEW), the proposed method captures surface smoothness, bilateral symmetry, and peri-eyelid texture with high sensitivity.

Statistical analyses confirm that these features differ significantly between the patient and control groups (p<0.01), validating their discriminative power. Furthermore, outcome assessment based on hyperplane distance shifts (ΔD) shows that most patients exhibit positive postoperative changes consistent with clinical expectations, confirming the model’s alignment with visual diagnostic outcomes.

The contributions of this work are threefold: (1) Multidimensional Feature Integration: The method moves beyond traditional geometric measurements by fusing three interpretable features that collectively describe morphological improvement in a clinically meaningful way. (2) Standardized Image Preprocessing: The framework incorporates facial landmark detection, periocular ROI cropping, and resolution normalization, thereby reducing dependency on controlled photographic environments or specialized imaging hardware. (3) Interpretable Machine Learning Evaluation: The SVM model introduces an objective scoring mechanism based on the hyperplane distance D(f), enabling reproducible outcome assessment across cases.

Despite these strengths, the study has two key limitations. First, the evaluation is based on a small, single-center dataset (N=25), which may constrain the model’s generalizability. Second, reliance on handcrafted features may limit the capture of subtle phenotypic variations that deep learning approaches might reveal.

In conclusion, the proposed framework delivers an interpretable, low-cost, and objective tool for quantitatively evaluating surgical improvement in sunken upper eyelid. Its modular architecture and clinical alignment make it well-suited for extension to other periocular procedures, such as blepharoplasty and lower eyelid bag correction. This work represents a step toward precision, data-driven assessment in aesthetic and reconstructive eyelid surgery.

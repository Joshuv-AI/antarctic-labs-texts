Incremental effectiveness of the second dose of mumps-containing vaccine among children 9 months to 6 years in Hefei: A matched case-control study
Authors: Tingting Shi, Die Hu, Shuangshuang Deng, Guangbo Qu, Baozhen Peng, Yun Zhang, Yuhui Jin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTTo evaluate the incremental vaccine effectiveness (IVE) of the second dose of mumps-containing vaccine (2thMuCV) compared to first dose and to provide evidence for optimizing mumps immunization strategies. We conducted a 1:1 matched case-control study of mumps cases reported in Hefei from 2018 to 2024 (aged 9 months to 6 years), selecting community controls without a history of mumps infection and matching them by age and sex. A total of 3483 pairs of cases and controls were included, of whom 21.76% and 23.02% had received two-doses MuCV respectively. The overall IVE of 2thMuCV was 22.35% (95%CI: 3.08%-37.78%) beyond that provided by the first dose alone. Subgroup analyses revealed a higher vaccine effectiveness (IVE) in children with a vaccination interval of <1 year (32.67%; 95% CI: 8.44 to 50.49) compared to those with an interval of ≥1year (8.97%; 95% CI: −25.54 to 34.00). VEs for the group <3 and ≥3 years old were 60.00% (95% CI: 16.72%-80.79%) and 16.88% (95% CI: −5.08%-34.25%), respectively. The IVEs waned over time, within and beyond three years after receipt of the 2th MuCV vaccine dose were 43.02% (95% CI: 19.08%-59.88%) and 34.37% (95% CI: −13.79%–62.15%). 2thMuCV provides additional protection against mumps, although diminished over time. The vaccine schedule with two-dose MuCV should be implemented among children in Hefei. The optimal age for the second dose needs to be further evaluated. Additionally, it is important to explore the potential benefits of providing a booster dose prior to enrollment in preschool or childcare settings.KEYWORDS: Mumps, vaccine effectiveness, mumps-containing vaccine, incremental effectiveness, case-control study

Introduction

Mumps is an important vaccine-preventable viral disease. It is highly contagious and often breaks out in places where people gather, such as kindergartens and schools. It ranks among the most frequently reported infectious disease public health emergencies in schools.1–7 Although mumps is typically considered a self-limiting benign disease with a favorable prognosis8,9 and is thought to confer lifelong immunity,8 cases of mumps virus (MuV) reinfection have been documented in the scientific literature.10 Additionally, MuV may also invade other tissues and organs, leading to serious complications, including orchitis or oophoritis, mastitis, pancreatitis, aseptic meningitis, encephalitis and hearing loss,11–14 nephritis, myocarditis and other long-term outcomes – such as paralysis, seizures, cranial nerve palsy, hydrocephalus, infertility,15–17 etc. Thus, mumps may indeed cause great harm to children and teenagers.

Vaccination with mumps-containing vaccine (MuCV) is the most effective and mature method to protect against mumps and its related complications.18 MuCV includes monovalent mumps vaccine, bivalent measles-mumps (MM) vaccines, and trivalent measles, mumps and rubella combined attenuated live vaccine (MMR).19 As of July 2024, 124 out of the 194 member states of the World Health Organization (63.92%) have included the mumps vaccine in their national immunization programs,20 with the most common use being the MMR combination vaccine. In China, MuCV was a self-paid vaccine before 2007. In 2008, one dose MMR was introduced into the Expanded Program on Immunization (EPI), for children who were aged 18 to 24 months. Since June 2020, a two-dose MMR vaccination schedule has been implemented, providing free immunization for children born after October 1, 2019, with doses administered at 8 months and 18 months of age.10,11,21–23 In 2020, the reported incidence rate of mumps in China was 9.20 per 100,000, a decrease of 57.17% compared with 2019 (reported incidence rate 21.48 per 100,000).24 This difference may be attributed to the implementation of an active two-dose MuCV immunization strategy. Previous studies showed that, those who received two doses of MuCV during childhood were less likely to be hospitalized or suffer from severe mumps complications compared to those who were unvaccinated or under-vaccinated.25–27 Prior studies reported that a single dose of MuCV had an effectiveness of 66%-96%, compared to no vaccination, while two and three doses reached 86%-99%.28–31 Two doses of the MMR vaccine were also associated with a 16% reduction in the risk of hospitalization for non-measles-mumps-rubella infections lasting 2 days or more.32 However, However, real-world studies on vaccine effectiveness (VE) of different doses of MuCV show considerable variation,33–36 and literature evaluating the incremental vaccine effectiveness (IVE) of the second dose of MuCV (2thMuCV) compared to a single dose remains limited. Therefore, this matched case-control study aimed to evaluate the overall IVE of 2thMuCV relative to the first dose and its trend over time among children aged 9 months to 6 years in Hefei, thereby providing a scientific basis for optimizing mumps immunization strategies.

Materials and methods

Data sources

Hefei, the capital city of Anhui Province, China, is one of the earliest cities administrated two-dose MMR expand immunization program (EPI). Hefei reported the notifiable infectious disease via the National Notifiable Disease Reporting System (NNDRS) and registered the vaccination information via the Immunization Planning Information Management System of Anhui Province (AHIIS). In China, mumps was classified as a category C notifiable infectious disease in 1990 and was mandatorily reported within 24 hours in the NNDRS since 2004.33 Each recipient in the AHIIS was assigned a unique identification code at the time of their first vaccination, which recorded the child’s demographic information, historical immunization data, and current immunization status.

Case and control identification

Case group selection

Mumps cases occurring in Hefei between January 2018 and December 2024 were retrieved from NNDRS. Eligible cases were clinically diagnosed or laboratory confirmed, with onset age ≤6 years, permanent Hefei residency, and final review status. Exclusion criteria: (1) permanent residence is not Hefei (429), (2) suspected cases (25), (3) individuals outside the target age range (had not yet reached the initial vaccination age of MuCV (8 months) or those over school age (7 years), (26 and 3,265 respectively), and (4) there is no record of MuCV vaccination information in the AHIIS (197).

Control group selection

A 1:1 matched case-control study design was adopted. For each mumps case, one healthy control was randomly selected from the same residential community, matched by age (±1 month) and gender, using AHIIS. Eligible controls had no history of mumps and were not household members of any case. When no suitable match was available within the same community, controls were recruited from adjacent communities.

Data collection

Demographic and clinical data were collected for all participants, including: sex, date of birth, current residential address, and illness onset date. MuCV vaccination information (vaccination status, number of doses received, vaccine type, vaccine manufacturer, and vaccination dates, etc.). Vaccination was considered non-effective if administered after disease onset or within 28 days prior to onset (corresponding to the maximum incubation period).

Statistical analysis

This study describes the epidemiological characteristics and immunological history of mumps cases, with qualitative data expressed as frequency (%) and quantitative data as mean±standard deviation. Chi-square test or t-test was used for inter-group comparison. Using binary conditional logistic regression model, the odds ratio (OR) and 95% Confidence intervals (CI) of two doses of MuCV vaccination between the case group and the control group in the paired data was analyzed, and the IVE of 2thMuCV was calculated. IVE = (1-OR) × 100%. The control group was matched using R3.6.1 software. Data analysis was performed using SPSS 23.0 software, and all statistical tests were two-sided, with a significance level of α = 0.05.

Results

Overview of mumps cases

The annual reported incidence of mumps in Hefei from 2018 to 2024 fluctuated between 8.65 and 16.07 (per 100,000, the same below, average 12.02), with the first peak occuring in 2019 (16.07), and the second in 2021 (13.54) (Figure 1). During this period, 7,425 cases were initially reported. After excluding 429 nonresidents, 25 suspected cases, 26 infants below vaccination age ( <8 months), and 3,265 children beyond school age ( >7 years), a total of 3,483 matched case-control pairs were included. Among these, 99.45% (3464/3483) were clinically diagnosed and 0.55% (19/3483) were laboratory confirmed cases.

Case report of mumps in Hefei, 2018–2024.

As shown in Figure 2, the population-level coverage of MuCV (both one-dose and two-dose) in Hefei remained high and increased gradually from 2018 to 2024. However, based on the immunization history analysis of participants in this study, the two-dose coverage rates in the case and control groups were only 21.76% and 23.02%, respectively. Further analysis reveals that all participants had a MuCV immune history of 0 to 3 doses prior to disease onset, with the majority having either 1 or 2 doses (Table 1). The mean ages of the case and control groups were 4.61 ± 1.34 years and 4.65 ± 1.34 years, respectively. And the mean intervals since 2thMuCV were 2.97 ± 1.32 years and 3.00 ± 1.33 years for the two groups, respectively. There were no statistically significant differences between the case and control groups regarding gender, age, dosage of MuCV vaccination, and the time interval since the last dose vaccination (P > .05) (Table 1).

The IVE of MuCV

Total IVE

Given the limited number of participants with either zero or three doses of MuCV, our IVE analysis was restricted to one-dose and two-dose recipients. Therefore, the ORs and IVEs reported in this study represent the effect estimates of two-dose MuCV compared to one dose. The OR of 2thMuCV was 0.78(95% CI: 0.62–0.97), the total IVE for the prevention of mumps was 22.35% (95% CI: 3.08%-37.78%). Further analysis revealed that the IVE of 2thMuCV was moderately higher when administered within 1 year after the first dose (32.67%; 95% CI: 8.44–50.49) compared to intervals exceeding 1 year (8.97%; 95% CI: −25.54%-34.00%) (Table 2).

IVE at different time intervals after the last dose of MuCV

The OR for the second dose was 0.57 (95% CI: 0.40–0.81) for cases occuring within 3 years after immunization, and it was 0.66 (95% CI: 0.38–1.14) beyond 3 years after immunization. The IVE was 43.02% (95% CI: 19.08% − 59.88%) within 3 years of the last dose of MuCV vaccination and it was down to 34.37% (95% CI: −13.79% − 62.15%) for vaccinations administered beyond three years (Table 2).

IVE for different age groups

In the group with age <3years old, the OR of MuCV in the second dose was 0.40 (95% CI: 0.19–0.83), and the IVE was 60.00% (95% CI: 16.72%–80.79%). In the group aged ≥3 years, the OR of MuCV in the second dose was 0.83 (95% CI: 0.66–1.05), and the IVE was 16.88% (95% CI: −5.08%–34.25%) (Table 2).

Discussion

The VE reflects its actual performance in preventing the target disease in the target population, taking into account the influence of factors such as the age of vaccination, the number of doses administered, and the time interval during the immunization process. National surveillance data indicate that the reported incidence of mumps in China decreased at an average annual rate of 6.99% among the entire population from 2005 to 2023. Notably, the implementation of a two-dose MMR vaccination strategy in 2020 significantly reduced the risk of mumps among children aged 0–9 years.21 However, even with two doses of MuCV, breakthrough mumps cases still occur.37–39 Particularly, the VE of two doses appears suboptimal during outbreaks in congregate settings such as schools.36 Thus, further research on IVE is warranted to optimize China’s MuCV immunization strategy.

The World Health Organization (WHO) recommends a two-dose regimen as the standard for routine MuCV immunization; While vaccination schedules vary substantially across countries, the first dose is typically administered between 12 and 18 months of age.40–42 The U.S. Advisory Committee of Immunization Practices (ACIP) recommends a two-dose MMR vaccine schedule, with the first dose administered at 12–15 months of age and the second dose at 4–6 years of age. And suggests administration of a third dose for populations at high risk of infection.18 China’s National Immunization Program recommends a two-dose MMR schedule at 8 and 18 months of age. However, certain regions including Beijing and Shanghai implement an enhanced three-dose schedule (8 months, 18 months, and 6 years).43,44 Previous studies on MuCV have primarily focused on the protective efficacy of different doses.45–48 Yin et al.33 reported that compared with the unimmunized population, the VE of one and two-doses were 67.4% and 79.1% respectively. Both showing waning protection over time. They also believed that the optimal age for the second dose of vaccination still needed further evaluation. Wu et al.49 conducted outbreak field evaluations demonstrating a VE of 68.8% for the first MuCV dose, while both second and third doses achieved 100% VE (vs. no vaccination). Our analysis demonstrated a 22.35% IVE for 2thMuCV dose compared to single-dose vaccination, indicating that the second dose of MuCV can provide additional protection for children (95% CI: 3.08%-37.78%), which is consistent with the overall research results in other regions.23,33–35

Also some studies suggest that mumps antibodies decline more rapidly than measles and rubella antibodies following two-dose MMR vaccination, indicating suggesting limited durability of mumps immunity despite two-dose administration.50 Lai et al.51 conducted a field investigation among healthy children aged 1–3 years in Hunan Province following immunization strategy adjustments. Their research found that after two doses of the MuCV vaccine, there was no statistically significant change in short-term antibody levels, though long-term immunogenicity requires further evaluation. Our analysis of IVE at varying intervals after the final MuCV dose revealed higher VE within 3 years (IVE = 43.02%, 95% CI:19.08%-59.88%) post-vaccination compared to periods ≥3 years (IVE = 34.37%, 95% CI: −13.79%-62.15%), suggesting limited duration of incremental protection from the two-dose regimen.

Previous studies suggest that the protective effect of MuCV may be related to the time interval between two doses of MuCV inoculation.38,52,53 Studies by Li et al.53 and Wang et al.38 demonstrated that longer inter-dose intervals were associated with both reduced mumps incidence (RR = 0.568, 95% CI: 0.382–0.845) and older age at infection (F = 12.98, P < .05). Our analysis revealed higher IVE when 2thMuCV doses were administered within 1 year (VE = 32.67%, 95% CI: 8.44–50.49) compared to intervals exceeding 1 year (VE = 8.97%, 95% CI: −25.54–34.00). However, due to reduced sample size in subgroup analysis, the IVE for intervals beyond 1 year after the first dose did not reach statistical significance. Future studies with larger sample size or seroepidemiological investigations are needed to comprehensively evaluate the impact of dosing intervals on MuCV protection.

Analysis of IVE by age group revealed that, the IVE of 2thMuCV in children under 3 years old was 60.00%, while that in children ≥3 years old was 16.88%. This difference may be attributed to variations in vaccination schedules, the <3-year-old group predominantly followed the two-dose regimen (administered at 8 and 18 months of age), with a relatively short 10-month interval between doses. However, among the children aged 3 and above surveyed, some were born before 2020 and did not follow the existing two-dose immunization strategy, resulting in a longer interval between doses, which might explain their reduced incremental protection. Additionally, the observed decline in IVE among children aged ≥3 years may also be attributed to: (1) Waning VE over time following MuCV vaccination; (2) Increased exposure opportunities due to the gathering of children in kindergartens and nurseries. Based on these findings, we hypothesize that administering a MuCV booster dose (third dose) at 3 years of age (prior to childcare enrollment) may represent a viable strategy to enhance and prolong VE against mumps, thereby to better prevent and control mumps. However, whether a third dose is necessary still awaits further research evidence.

Advantages and limitations

There are several drawbacks to this study. First, the vaccination history of MuCV was retrospectively investigated through AHIIS, which may have led to incomplete retrieval of immunization records. This may lead to overestimation or underestimation of the overall IVE. Second, cases in this study were identified through the NNDRS, which may have missed unreported cases. Additionally, as most cases were clinically diagnosed, asymptomatic carriers could not be detected, potentially leading to an overestimation of IVE. Third, vaccination status was classified as “unvaccinated” if administration occurred within 28 days (maximum incubation period) prior to the onset of the disease, though the reasonability of this classification criterion requires further evaluation. Fourth, some subgroups may have a relatively wide confidence interval due to limited sample sizes, resulting in insufficiently robust outcomes. It is necessary to continue conducting specialized surveys with larger sample sizes in the future for more comprehensive and in-depth analysis. Additionally, the study used a 1:1 matched-pair design to control for confounders including age, gender, time, and residence. However, other factors such as children’s immune status may influence IVE estimates. Future studies should improve immunization records and incorporate field surveys to enhance MuCV IVE assessment accuracy.

Conclusion

2thMuCV provides additional protection for children than that of one dose, though, its IVE wanes over time. Additionally, the IVE with an interval of more than one year between two doses is limited. These findings underscore the importance of timely and complete immunization among age-eligible children, and suggest the potential benefits of administering a booster dose prior to enrollment in preschool/childcare facilities.

MPU-rPPG: A Comprehensive and High-Fidelity Dataset for Remote Photoplethysmography Across Diverse Conditions and Demographics
Authors: Zhengxuan Chen, Tao Tan, Zitong Yu, Haitao Jiang, Keguang Wang, Jie Gao, Yue Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractRemote photoplethysmography (rPPG) is a non-contact singal extracting photoplethysmographic (PPG) waveforms from video by analyzing blood-volume-induced modulations in the light reflected from the skin. As rPPG moves closer to real-world deployment, comprehensive datasets are essential for the development of robust algorithms across diverse conditions and populations. Building on existing efforts, we introduce the MPU-rPPG dataset, designed to bridge current gaps and establish a new gold standard for rPPG research. This dataset captures high-fidelity physiological signals from multiple body regions (including the face and limbs) under varying light conditions and environments. It supports heart rate ranges from 50–160 bpm, accommodating both resting states and high-intensity activities, and includes a diverse set of subjects across different skin tones and demographics. The versatility of MPU-rPPG supports robustness studies relevant to practical scenarios—including clinical monitoring environments and highly dynamic public activities (e.g., endurance sports or driving)—where motion, illumination variation, and partial occlusion remain major challenges for reliable rPPG. By addressing these critical needs, the MPU-rPPG dataset provides a comprehensive foundation for the development of real-world rPPG systems and is committed to setting a new benchmark for future research.Subject terms: Public health, Medical imaging

Background & Summary

Remote photoplethysmography (rPPG) is a non-contact singal extracting photoplethysmographic (PPG) waveforms from video by analyzing blood-volume-induced modulations in the light reflected from the skin. This technique has attracted significant attention in recent years due to its potential applications in health monitoring, fitness tracking, and human-computer interaction. Recent research has made substantial progress in improving the accuracy of the rPPG signal under controlled conditions1–10 and expanding its applicability to diverse scenarios11,12. However, critical challenges remain, particularly in achieving robust signal extraction across multiple body regions, maintaining consistency under dynamic lighting conditions, ensuring real-time processing capabilities, and optimizing computational efficiency for resource-constrained devices13–16. Furthermore, current datasets and models often fail to address the complexities of real-world scenarios17–19, such as hospital monitoring environments or public activities like marathons, cycling, and driving, where reliable rPPG performance is essential.

To address these limitations, we introduce the MPU-rPPG dataset that:

Capturing high-resolution, long-duration physiological recordings, enabling advanced signal analysis and supporting the development of more precise algorithms.

Ensuring broad diversity and applicability, allowing rPPG models to perform effectively across varied skin tones, multiple body regions, and complex real-world scenarios with different light conditions.

Expanding the measurable heart rate range from 50 bpm to 160 bpm, making the dataset suitable for both resting and extreme activity scenarios, and enabling robust model performance in a wide range of real-world applications.

By addressing these key needs, the MPU-rPPG dataset provides a comprehensive foundation for establishing a gold standard in the development and evaluation of real-world rPPG algorithms.

Methods

The development of a robust and generalizable remote photoplethysmography (rPPG) dataset typically follows a multi-stage pipeline encompassing synchronized data acquisition, spatial annotation, signal processing, and downstream algorithmic benchmarking (Fig. 1). A complete rPPG data lifecycle includes: (i) recording video and reference physiological signals under varied task conditions; (ii) spatially segmenting visual inputs (e.g., face, arms) via body parsing, skin segmentation, and facial localization methods20–30; (iii) generating labeled tensors comprising four components: raw video frames, body-region masks, synchronized ground-truth physiological signals, and task-level labels(classifying the subject’s activity state, e.g., resting, talking, exercise); and (iv) evaluating model performance across metrics such as heart rate accuracy, signal correlation, and frequency-domain coherence.

This pipeline highlights the dataset’s robustness in complex scenarios through adaptive labeling and cross-modal fusion.

To support algorithmic evaluation, we also define a standardized preprocessing and metric computation pipeline. Each video is segmented into short clips with corresponding physiological labels and body-region masks. Benchmark models are then applied to extract PPG signals, which are compared against ground truth using standard metrics described below. Related physiological signal evaluation also commonly considers agreement analysis via Bland-Altman plots31,32, distributional discrepancy measures such as Kullback-Leibler divergence33, and reconstruction-oriented fidelity objectives adapted from restoration literature34.

We utilize Fast Fourier Transform (FFT)-based heart rate estimation as a standardized baseline for consistent benchmarking across datasets. While alternative approaches such as signal quality assessment combined with peak detection may offer advantages in highly noisy scenarios, FFT provides a robust and widely adopted frequency-domain metric for comparative analysis.

Models

To evaluate the utility and generalization capability of the proposed MPU-rPPG dataset, we benchmark several widely adopted rPPG models that represent key advances in robustness, efficiency, and temporal modeling.

We include DeepPhys35, which pioneered motion-appearance disentanglement using dual-branch convolutional attention, and TS-CAN36, which integrates joint temporal-spatial attention to suppress noise while preserving signal continuity. EfficientPhys37 leverages temporal shift modules for resource-efficient rPPG estimation on edge devices, and FactorizePhys38 applies factorized self-attention across spatial, temporal, and channel dimensions to enhance generalization in dynamic environments.

While recent models based on Transformers39 and Mamba40,41 architectures demonstrate high accuracy in training, we exclude them from our comparison due to their reliance on long input sequences and high inference latency. In contrast, our focus lies in evaluating models with low parameter counts and strong real-time applicability—where performance in short-window settings is critical. This selection aligns with practical deployment needs and highlights how our dataset supports lightweight, generalizable model development under diverse visual conditions.

Concluding Evaluation Framework

By integrating the aforementioned evaluation metrics, our dataset facilitates a multi-faceted assessment of rPPG algorithms, covering accuracy (MAE, RMSE), linearity (PCC), signal quality (SNR), and classification reliability (MACC), if nessesary. This comprehensive pipeline ensures the robustness of the algorithms across different scenarios, such as varying body regions and motion levels. Notably, the inclusion of multiple body regions and synchronized high-resolution ground truth signals eliminates issues like poor RMSE performance caused by misalignment across spatial or temporal axes.

Dataset Overview

We introduce the MPU-rPPG dataset, a high-fidelity resource for developing and evaluating rPPG algorithms under real-world conditions.

MPU-rPPG contains recordings from 50 participants (20 hours total), spanning ages 18–60 with an approximately balanced gender distribution and diverse skin tones (Fitzpatrick I–VI; predominantly Asian with additional African and European participants). To quantitatively characterize environmental diversity, we summarize the recording conditions of MPU-rPPG at the dataset level. All recordings are categorized according to (i) environment (indoor light or outdoor), (ii) activity state (rest, low-intensity motion, and high-intensity motion), and (iii) available skin regions (face, arms, hands, and legs).

Compared with existing rPPG datasets that are predominantly collected under controlled indoor lighting and facial-only settings, MPU-rPPG includes recordings with varying illumination conditions, substantial subject motion, and partial region availability due to occlusion. This distribution reflects more realistic acquisition scenarios and provides broader coverage for evaluating rPPG algorithms under non-ideal conditions.

Data Acquisition and Synchronization

All videos were captured at a high resolution (2k) with a frame rate of 60 frames per second (fps) using an MX Brio camera. Ground-truth physiological signals were acquired using a CMS60D pulse oximeter sampling at 60 Hz. Synchronization between the video and physiological data streams was achieved via a unified timestamping protocol: (i) both streams were recorded with system-level timestamps; (ii) a visual-alignment marker (flashlight pulse) was used at the start of each session to calculate the temporal offset; (iii) signals were resampled and aligned to the nearest video frame, achieving a synchronization accuracy within ±8 ms (half a video frame interval). This precise alignment supports downstream tasks such as pulse transit time analysis and heart rate variability estimation.

Ethical Approval and Informed Consent

Primary Data Collection (MPU-rPPG): The primary data collection for the MPU-rPPG dataset was approved by the Ethics Committee of Zhuhai People’s Hospital (Approval Number: ZH20250306). Prior to participation, written informed consent was obtained directly from all participants. Participants were fully informed about the study’s purpose and the nature of the data being collected, and explicitly consented to the collection, storage, and public sharing of their anonymized physiological data and identifiable facial/body video recordings for research and publication purposes.

Secondary Data (Validation Datasets): The data utilized in this study comprise both primary data collected by the authors (the MPU-rPPG dataset) and secondary data used exclusively for technical validation (PURE, UBFC-rPPG, UCLA-rPPG, and MSPM datasets). No additional ethical approval or informed consent was required for their secondary use in this study.

Data Records

The MPU-rPPG dataset42 is publicly available in the MPU-rPPG Dataset Figshare repository and can be accessed via the following 10.6084/m9.figshare.29377835.

The dataset consists of multiple file types, primarily including video recordings (.mp4) and synchronized physiological data files (.csv). The physiological data files contain variable names and column headings such as timestamp, heart_rate (bpm), SpO2 (%), and raw_PPG waveform values. Additional metadata files (.json/.csv) are provided, containing column headings for subject_id, age, gender, skin_tone (Fitzpatrick scale), lighting_condition, and activity_state.

Technical Validation

The following experiments illustrate how MPU-rPPG can be used to characterize algorithm performance under diverse conditions. These evaluations are not intended to benchmark or optimize rPPG models, nor to claim superiority of any architecture; instead, they quantify dataset complexity and highlight challenges introduced by the multi-region, high-resolution, and unconstrained design.

Experimental Setup

To prevent identity leakage, we used a subject-independent split. Specifically, 50 participants were divided into training (40, 80%) and testing (10, 20%) sets, ensuring no subject overlap. For cross-dataset testing, models were trained on the training partition of one dataset and evaluated on the full set of another, following standard protocols.

Dataset Generalization and Utility

Our study aimed to present a robust and adaptive dataset for remote photoplethysmography (rPPG) signal estimation, leveraging the advantages of high-resolution, high-frame-rate, and multi-region data collection. Our evaluation was designed to incorporate data from multiple body regions, offering enhanced robustness to motion and lighting variations.

Dataset Utility and Benchmarking

All models were trained and validated on MPU-rPPG and then tested on external datasets to assess generalization (Table 3). While absolute errors on MPU-rPPG are typically higher than on controlled datasets such as PURE, this reflects the more realistic conditions in MPU-rPPG (unconstrained motion, lighting variation, and partial occlusions) that can expose failure modes less visible in simpler benchmarks. The high spatial resolution and frame rate further support signal recovery by capturing subtle chrominance changes and improving temporal fidelity during rapid motion.

Implications for Real-World Applications

These results suggest MPU-rPPG is a practical testbed for camera-based physiological monitoring in unconstrained settings (e.g., low light or during activity). Multi-region, high-quality recordings reduce reliance on ideal, face-only conditions and support more robust deployment in clinical and consumer scenarios.

Data availability

The MPU-rPPG dataset includes synchronized RGB video recordings, physiological ground-truth signals (PPG, HR, SpO2), region-level masks, and task-level annotations. Due to the 20GB file size limitation imposed by Figshare, we have made available a representative subset of the MPU-rPPG dataset. The full dataset will be publicly released upon publication, with a permanent DOI assigned to ensure long-term accessibility. The sample data are accessible via 10.6084/m9.figshare.29377835.

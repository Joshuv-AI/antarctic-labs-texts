EJNMMI research: a vision for the next era of nuclear medicine
Authors: Marcus Hacker
Published: 2026

Dear Collegues,

In October 2025, I had the honor of assuming the role of Editor-in-Chief of EJNMMI Research. During the first months of this mandate, we have reorganized the editorial team and revitalized two ongoing manuscript collections—Molecular Imaging Innovations in Neurology and Neuro-Oncology and Fibroblast Activation Protein–Targeted Imaging and Therapy. I warmly thank my predecessor, Irene Burger, and her editorial team for their sustained commitment to scientific rigor and for elevating the journal’s quality and international visibility.

Nuclear medicine is entering a decisive decade. It provides medicine with a new language—the language of living biology—by visualizing and quantifying molecular processes in vivo, in real time, and across the entire body. Molecular imaging has moved far beyond lesion detection; it enables systems-level insight into physiology and pathophysiology, capturing heterogeneity, dynamics, and treatment-induced change. Theranostics extends this language into action, transforming diagnostic information into a rational basis for precisely targeted radionuclide therapies. In parallel, demand for PET imaging, SPECT/CT, and radiopharmaceutical therapies continues to grow, accompanied by increasingly robust evidence from randomized trials and pragmatic real-world studies. Patients and advocacy groups rightly expect diagnostics and therapies that reflect the individuality of disease biology and outcomes that matter to them.

Our vision at EJNMMI Research is to actively catalyze this transformation. We will publish high-quality, methodologically rigorous research spanning the full translational continuum—from target discovery and radiochemistry to preclinical validation, first-in-human studies, and early clinical implementation. We aim to surface bold concepts, stimulate methodological innovation, and accelerate responsible clinical adoption. Central to this mission are reproducibility, transparent reporting, quantitative rigor, and the use of clinically meaningful endpoints, supported by data and code availability whenever possible.

PET imaging and targeted therapy

PET imaging has become a cornerstone of targeted therapy by enabling non-invasive, quantitative assessment of target expression, pathway activity, and biological heterogeneity across the whole body. In contrast to tissue sampling, PET provides a comprehensive and repeatable readout of disease biology, allowing clinicians and researchers to select patients most likely to benefit from targeted interventions, optimize dosing, and monitor early response or resistance.

At EJNMMI Research, we see PET imaging not merely as a companion diagnostic but as an integral component of precision medicine. Novel tracers targeting receptors, enzymes, transporters, immune checkpoints, and components of the tumor microenvironment are reshaping drug development and clinical decision-making. Dynamic and parametric PET, increasingly enabled by total-body systems, allows quantitative interrogation of tracer kinetics and drug–target interactions across multiple organs simultaneously. This opens new opportunities to study on-target and off-target effects, systemic toxicity, and inter-organ crosstalk—critical aspects of modern targeted and immune-modulating therapies.

We particularly encourage submissions that demonstrate how PET imaging can guide adaptive treatment strategies, support early go/no-go decisions in drug development, and refine patient stratification in clinical trials. Studies integrating PET with circulating biomarkers, genomics, and clinical phenotypes are especially aligned with the journal’s scope.

Theranostics: from concept to comprehensive precision care

Theranostics represents one of the most transformative paradigms in contemporary nuclear medicine. By linking molecular imaging directly to targeted radionuclide therapy, theranostics embodies a true “what you see is what you treat” approach. At EJNMMI Research, we will place strong emphasis on innovative theranostic concepts, including the evaluation of novel targets, radioligands, and radionuclide combinations.

We are particularly interested in rigorous preclinical studies that elucidate target biology, pharmacokinetics, dosimetry, and mechanisms of response and resistance, as well as early clinical reports detailing in vivo biodistribution, safety, and preliminary efficacy. Beyond oncology, theranostic strategies are increasingly relevant in inflammatory, cardiovascular, and endocrine diseases, and we welcome contributions exploring these emerging indications.

Importantly, we view theranostics not as a standalone modality but as an integral component of multimodal, biology-driven care. Combining radionuclide therapy with immune-modulating agents, targeted drugs, or external beam radiotherapy raises new questions regarding sequencing, synergy, and toxicity—questions that can only be answered through carefully designed imaging-informed studies. Large field-of-view (total-body) PET systems and advanced SPECT/CT architectures will play a central role in this evolution by enabling ultra-fast, low-dose imaging, dynamic multi-organ dosimetry, and longitudinal assessment of treatment effects at the systems level.

Coupled with AI-driven reconstruction and quantitative analysis, these platforms will expand access to theranostic approaches, reduce cost per clinically relevant insight, and accelerate the translation of discovery into patient benefit.

Immunology, prevention, and the role of AI

Immunology represents one of the most dynamic frontiers in nuclear medicine. Immune processes govern disease initiation, progression, treatment response, and long-term outcomes. A rapidly expanding portfolio of radiopharmaceuticals now enables non-invasive mapping of immune cell trafficking, activation states, and interactions within the tumor microenvironment and beyond. These tools will increasingly guide immune-modulating drugs and cellular therapies, allowing earlier response assessment, identification of resistance, and safer, more adaptive treatment strategies.

Equally transformative is the shift toward prevention and early interception. Ultra-sensitive PET systems enable low-dose, longitudinal phenotyping of physiology, capturing subtle molecular deviations before overt disease manifests. Such capabilities extend the relevance of nuclear medicine beyond oncology to cardiometabolic, neuroinflammatory, and immune-mediated disorders, particularly when combined with primary prevention and precision health strategies.

A unifying driver of these advances is artificial intelligence. AI will reshape the entire nuclear medicine value chain—from radiopharmaceutical discovery and protocol design to physics-informed reconstruction, harmonization across scanners, automated segmentation, and observer-independent quantification. Beyond technical optimization, AI enables integration of molecular imaging with clinical data, multi-omics, and laboratory markers, supporting refined disease taxonomy, outcome prediction, and decision-making at the point of care.

Looking ahead

To serve this vision, EJNMMI Research will introduce new topics that are also reflected in our Editorial Board: (1) AI in Nuclear Medicine; (2) Immuno-imaging and Immune Therapies; (3) Preventive and Early-Interception Molecular Imaging. We will prioritize clinically anchored innovation, fast-track high-quality randomized and pragmatic trials, welcome robust replication studies and informative negative results, and actively encourage visionary and novel methodology.

We anticipate a substantial increase in submissions driven by these scientific and clinical imperatives. As editors, we are committed to constructive and timely peer review that improves clarity, rigor, and impact.

This is a moment to be bold. Together—with our authors, reviewers, and readers—we can shape a future in which molecular imaging and theranostics deliver earlier answers, safer therapies, and better lives.

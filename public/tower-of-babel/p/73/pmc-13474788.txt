Digital tracking of ureteral stents with the stentless application: clinical outcomes in 200 patients
Authors: Lorin Giurgiu, Matei Maximilian Buzoianu, Paul Alexandru Medan, Iulia Andras, Florentin Dobrotă, Răzvan Peia, Silviu Petrișor Vranău, Ștefana Medan, Bianca Rus, Răzvan Ognean, Ioan Coman, Nicolae Crișan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and objectivesUreteral double-J stents (UDJS) are frequently placed in urologic practice for a range of indications, including ureteral obstruction, strictures, stone disease, peri- and post-ureteroscopy, and following ureteral reconstruction or reimplantation. Recent studies suggest that approximately 13% of inserted stents become overdue or neglected, which can lead to serious, potentially life-threatening complications. We therefore aimed to develop a smartphone application to track patients with UDJS and reduce the incidence of overdue indwelling ureteral stents.Materials and methodsFor the development of the application, we worked with a specialized team of programmers. To facilitate universal access to the complete patient database, a cloud-based database was implemented. The application was designed to send a Short Message Service (SMS) to the patients and their general practitioner to remind the timing for UDJS extraction. We prospectively enrolled a total of 200 patients who underwent UDJS insertion for any indication by six urologists from June 2023, to March 2024. The patients were divided into two groups: the first group was monitored using the Stentless mobile application, while the second group was monitored using an electronic patient registry. The two groups were compared with respect to stent overdue times and complete loss-to-follow-up rates.ResultsIn this study, a total of 200 patients were enrolled non-randomly and assigned to one of the study groups. 100 patients assigned to group 1 (mobile application group) and 100 to group 2 (electronic registry group). Among these, 187 patients (93.5%) underwent unilateral ureteral stent placement, while 13 patients (6.5%) required bilateral stent placements. The study also included patients with long-term indwelling stents, with the maximum stent replacement interval extending up to 360 days. The median indwelling time across the cohort was 17 days (range 14–360), reflecting the inclusion of long-term stents. In group 1, no patients missed their scheduled stent removal appointments, corresponding to an LTFU rate of 0% and no overdue removals. In group 2, four patients failed to attend their scheduled appointments (p = 0.121). These patients were identified through the electronic patient registry and subsequently contacted for follow-up. The delay in stent removal among these four patients was (7, 10, 13, and 60 days) beyond the planned appointment date. Notably, one patient from group 2 presented with an encrusted stent requiring two flexible ureteroscopy procedures for removal. Ultimately, all ureteral stents were successfully removed in an office-based setting, ensuring complete management for all participants.ConclusionsBy notifying the patient and general practitioner with a mobile phone text message automatically transmitted from the application, there is the potential to increase patient compliance, reduce the rate of those who have a UDJS and fail to return for follow-up, respectively to reduce the rate of patients presenting for complications due to stent encrusting.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12894-026-02137-x.Keywords: Smartphone application, Ureteral stent, Stentless, Urology

Introduction

According to the European Urology Guidelines, the prevalence rates of urinary lithiasis vary between 1% and 20%. Notably, in highly developed nations such as Sweden, Canada, and the United States, the prevalence of kidney stones exceeds 10% [1]. Renal colic and acute obstructive pyelonephritis are among the most frequent urological emergencies encountered in routine clinical practice, often requiring the insertion of a ureteral double J stent (UDJS). Furthermore, UDJS serve an integral role in the management of various urological conditions including ureteral stricture, malignant obstruction, and ureteral injury [2, 3]. The ureteral stents are typically placed for a finite duration, requiring either removal or replacement at predefined intervals that are contingent upon the stent material employed and the specific underlying disease of the patient. While certain patients may necessitate long-term stent placement, periodic replacement every 3, 6 to 12 months (for long life stent) is essential to ensure the efficacy and safety of these prolonged stents.

UDJS play a critical role in contemporary urologic practice by ensuring urinary drainage, preventing ureteral obstruction, and supporting postoperative recovery. Nevertheless, stent-related symptoms are frequently reported and may impair patient quality of life, particularly when dwell time is prolonged. In a study cohort of patients with indwelling ureteral stents, lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS) were observed in 78% of patients, painful reflux in 80%, sexual dysfunction in 32%, and reduced work capacity in 58% [4]. This underscores the importance of promptly identifying and addressing the diverse complications attributed to the UDJS, including stent migration leading to discomfort, urinary tract infections, hematuria, stent encrustation, and ureteral erosion/urolithiasis [5]. Overdue stents have been estimated to occur in approximately 12% of cases [6] and are associated with substantial morbidity and, rarely, mortality. Singh et al. reported one death among 19 patients with retained stents, underscoring the clinical importance of timely stent removal and structured follow-up [7].

The management of overdue or neglected stents presents a challenge for healthcare providers, necessitating careful consideration and decisive action to mitigate associated risks [8]. Retained or overdue ureteral stents may predispose patients to complications such as encrustation, infection, obstruction, and, in some cases, the need for multimodal management. Therapeutic strategies can range from minimally invasive approaches, including extracorporeal shock wave lithotripsy and ureteroscopy, to more complex procedures such as percutaneous nephrolithotomy or, rarely, nephrectomy [9]. The choice of intervention is largely determined by the severity of encrustation, stent dwell time, and the overall clinical status of the patient. Although severe outcomes are uncommon, they have been described in the literature. Terminology describing prolonged indwelling stents is not fully standardized. Terms such as “forgotten,” “overlooked,” and “neglected” are often used interchangeably to denote stents that remain beyond the intended removal date; however, the time threshold defining an overdue or retained stent varies across studies, ranging from several weeks to months after the recommended dwell time. For clarity, in the present study we use the term overdue stent to describe any stent not removed within the planned timeframe. To reduce the incidence of overdue stents and associated complications, multiple tracking strategies have been developed, including paper-based registries, electronic health record–integrated databases, and automated reminder systems such as email or Short Message Service (SMS) notifications [10–13].

The integration of smartphones has exerted an important impact across various domains, including the field of medicine. Smartphones and tablets combine computing functionalities with communication capabilities into a singular, user-friendly device that can be readily adopted by healthcare professionals. Numerous mobile applications are now accessible to aid physicians in essential tasks such as information management, time organization, accessing medical documents, disseminating medical knowledge, patient monitoring, as well as medical education and training. Leveraging mobile tools to make superior clinical decisions has made concomitant outcomes of enhanced control and efficiency, as affirmed by Ather et al. who reported a substantial reduction in overdue stents incidence from 12.5% to 1.5% over a 1-year period, thus advocating strongly for the incorporation of such systems within urological practice [14].

In our prospective study, we aimed to develop a smartphone application to improve patient oversight concerning ureteral stents and diminish the occurrence of encrusted ureteral stents.

Materials and methods

The primary aim of this study was to assess the application’s effectiveness in minimizing the occurrence of overdue ureteral stents and the complications related to encrusted stents. A secondary aim was to analyze feedback from both physicians and patients regarding the perceived utility of the application and its role in improving the overall quality of medical care.

Study population

Following the approval of institutional ethics committee and obtaining a written informed consent to participate in the study from all participants, a prospective, non-randomized study was created. Our study cohort comprised patients who underwent ureteral stent insertion for varying indications under the care of six urologists at our medical facility spanning from June 01, 2023, to March 01, 2024. Indwelling time was categorized as either short-term or long-term based on the clinical indication for stent placement. Short-term stents were defined as those inserted following ureteroscopy, with planned removal within 7 days postoperatively. For these stents, overdue removal was defined as extraction occurring more than 7 days after the scheduled removal date. Long-term stents were placed for other clinical indications requiring prolonged urinary drainage and scheduled periodic replacement. Overdue status for long-term stents was defined as failure to replace the stent within the predetermined 3-, 6-, or 12-month interval. For patients with long-term indwelling ureteral stents the replacement intervals extended up to 360 days. Although the stent could remain in situ for up to 360 days, patients were advised to undergo stent replacement within a maximum of 9 months to reduce the risk of encrustation and associated complications.

Data extracted included patient demographics, diagnosis and surgical procedure. Across all cases, a standardized 6 French UDJS (Cook Medical, USA) was placed. Stent length was also standardized, with all patients receiving stents of identical length (26 cm). Bilateral UDJS were counted as a singular stent regimen if concurrently inserted during the procedure.

Outcomes

All data entries were documented in the operating theater post-surgery by the treating urologist. Auxiliary staff were not involved in this process. Patients were assigned to either the mobile application group (group 1) or the electronic registry group (group 2) by the treating urologist in a non-randomized manner. All patients were discharged with a scheduled appointment for stent removal, and the date was communicated to them prior to hospital discharge. Patients were informed about the importance of timely stent removal and received details regarding their follow-up visit at that time. As the scheduled time for ureteral stent removal approaches, the attending physician receives an automated notification on their mobile device for patients in group 1, whereas for patients in group 2, the physician conducts a manual review of the electronic registry once per week. The physician then initiates a reminder-triggered contact with the patient, on the same day, via a pre-scripted SMS message/ direct phone call (group 1) or a direct phone call (group 2) to ensure timely and coordinated care. Following the successful removal of the ureteral stent in group 1, the physician updates the patient’s profile within the application, which automatically transitions the record to the section designated for removed stents. This workflow facilitates accurate tracking and real-time management of patient care through the Stentless™ application. For patients recorded in the electronic registry (group 2), the physician documents cases in which the ureteral stent was removed.

Stents were considered overdue when they were left in place for longer than the anticipated period. Patients failing to attend their scheduled stent removal appointments were promptly contacted and reminded via telephone by the attending urologist. Unreachable patients were followed up with daily phone calls for up to four consecutive days. Maximal waiting period for the patients who did not return to the hospital for stent removal was limited to 2 months for ethical reasons, when patients were actively contacted again.

Mobile application

We developed a mobile phone application named Stentless™, offered free of charge to urologists, designed to facilitate the monitoring of ureteral stents installed by them. We collaborated with a dedicated team of programmers possessing specialized expertise. To ensure widespread access to the patient database, a cloud-based database was employed. Additionally, the application incorporates the security features of the Firebase authentication mechanism. The application leverages notifications sent via text messages to both patients and their designated general practitioners one week preceding the approaching deadline of 3, 6, or 12 months post-stent placement (refer to Fig. 1 for a visual representation). Each patient is assigned a unique profile within the application (Fig. 2), housing essential data such as the patient’s personal identification number, full name, contact information including phone number and email, the side on which the stent is mounted, date of stent placement, the stipulated time period for stent removal or replacement (at 3, 6, or 12 months interval), and the corresponding observation sheet reference number. Patients registered within the application are visually categorized through a specific color coding system for enhanced clarity and organization: individuals with pending stent removal are denoted in green, those with already removed stents are marked in gray and patients with overdue stents are highlighted in red. This intuitive color scheme aids healthcare providers in swiftly identifying and prioritizing patients based on their current stent status, streamlining decision-making processes and optimizing patient care management. The stent removal procedure can be updated in the application regardless of the attending physician or hospital the stent was placed in by utilizing the patient’s unique identification number. The application is accessible for download on Apple Store and Google Play platforms and can be installed on mobile devices, catering to users in Romania and Moldova.

Patient information and profile

A structured feedback mechanism was implemented to evaluate user experience with the application. Both physicians and patients completed a rating scale ranging from 1 to 5 (1 = unsatisfactory, 5 = very satisfied) (Table 3). The assessment examined the application’s usability, that referred to the overall ease with which clinicians could learn and operate the application. Workflow efficiency assessed the perceived impact of the application on time management and task completion during clinical activities. Cognitive load described the level of mental effort required to use the system. Workflow integration evaluated how seamlessly the application fit within existing clinical processes (electronic registry). Ease of use reflected the simplicity of navigation and interaction with the interface. Relative administrative burden measured the perceived documentation and organizational effort associated with the application compared with standard registry-based tracking. Overall utility captured the perceived clinical value of the system in supporting patient management. As no validated instrument specifically addressing ureteral stent tracking applications exists, the questionnaire was developed for the purposes of this study.

Statistical analysis

Analyses were performed to compare the proportion of patients who missed their scheduled ureteral stent removal between the two study groups. Categorical variables were expressed as absolute numbers and percentages. Due to the low event rate and the presence of zero counts in one group, comparisons between groups were conducted using a two-sided Fisher’s exact test. Given the low event rate, Fisher’s exact test was selected to minimize type I error. A p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Standard biostatistical methods were used. A patient could only have 1 profile, but could have more than 1 ureteral stent care plan during the study period. Given the exploratory nature of this study and the absence of prior data allowing reliable effect size estimation, a formal power analysis was not performed. The sample size was determined pragmatically based on the expected recruitment capacity during the study period. Data was collected and analyzed using Jamovi Stats Version 2.3 [Computer Software] (2002). Continuous data was expressed in form of mean or median, while nominal data was expressed in form of percentage.

Results

We enrolled a total of 200 patients in the study and assigned them to a study group. A total of 100 patients were assigned to group 1 (mobile application group) and 100 to group 2 (electronic registry group). The study cohort comprised of 187 unilateral ureteral stent care plans, with 13 out of 200 patients (6.5%) undergoing bilateral stent placement. The average age of patients was 55.4 years (± 21.18 SD), with 34% (68/200) being male and 66% (132/200) female. A comprehensive overview of the patient demographics can be found in Table 1. Indications for stent placement varied, with kidney stones requiring flexible ureteroscopy in 84 cases (42%), ureteral stones necessitating rigid ureteroscopy in 97 cases (48.5%), ureteral strictures occurring in 6 cases (3%), cancer diagnoses in 12 cases (6%), and other reasons in 2 cases (1%).

Patient demographics

No patients in the Stentless application group missed their scheduled stent-removal appointment, whereas four patients (4.0%) in the electronic registry group failed to attend their scheduled removal. The delay in stent removal among these patients was 7, 10, 13, and 60 days beyond the planned appointment date and these patients responded to follow-up phone calls after 3, 5, 8, and 55 days, respectively, and subsequently presented for stent removal. This difference did not reach statistical significance (two-sided Fisher’s exact test, p = 0.121) (Table 2). Among the patients enrolled in the study, one individual in the Stentless application group did not acknowledge the notifications sent by the urologist but subsequently scheduled stent removal following contact by their general practitioner.

One patient in the electronic registry group, who missed the scheduled removal appointment by 60 days, presented with an encrusted ureteral stent requiring two flexible ureteroscopy procedures for complete stent removal. Encrusted stents were rare and did not differ significantly between groups (0% vs. 1.0%; Fisher’s exact test, p = 1.000) (Table 2). Importantly, all ureteral stents were ultimately removed successfully in an office-based setting, ensuring complete and effective management for all study participants.

As part of the study protocol, all stents were successfully removed cystoscopically under local anesthesia in an office setting. There were no stents equipped with extraction strings included in the study. No complications arose during stent removal procedures. Subsequent to stent removal, 20 patients exhibited positive urine cultures, promptly treated with antibiotics resulting in no further complications. One patient was lost to follow-up as they passed away during the 6-month interval necessitating stent replacement, from an unrelated cause.

Physician and patient satisfaction with the Stentless application are presented in Table 3. Among the six participating physicians, satisfaction ratings were consistently high across all evaluated domains. Usability and workflow efficiency were each rated as very satisfied by 83.3% (5/6) of physicians and satisfied by 16.7% (1/6). Cognitive load and relative administrative burden received very satisfied ratings from all physicians (100%, 6/6). Workflow integration and ease of use were rated as very satisfied by 66.6% (4/6) and satisfied by 33.3% (2/6). Similarly, overall utility was rated as very satisfied by all respondents (100%, 6/6), indicating broad acceptance of the application in routine clinical practice.

Discussion

In this study, 200 patients were prospectively allocated into two groups—100 in the mobile application group (Group 1) and 100 in the electronic registry group (Group 2). The median indwelling time across the cohort was 17 days, largely reflecting the predominance of short-term postoperative stents. Long-term indwelling stents were also included, with durations up to 360 days. In practice, patients returned for stent replacement at a maximum of 274 days. Non-compliance with scheduled stent removal was observed in four patients (0 from Group 1 and 4 from Group 2), all of whom were successfully recalled. While the difference did not reach statistical significance, the absence of missed removals in the application group suggests a clinically meaningful effect that warrants evaluation in larger, adequately powered multicenter studies. Of the patients enrolled in the study, one individual did not respond to the messages sent by the urologist but was eventually prompted to schedule their stent removal after being contacted by their general practitioner. This highlights the critical role of multidisciplinary involvement in patient care and underscores the effectiveness of tailored communication strategies in ensuring timely adherence to essential medical procedures such as stent removal. One patient in Group 2 developed stent encrustation requiring two interventions, but all stents were ultimately removed safely in an outpatient setting under local anesthesia, without procedural complications. The absence of missed stent-removal appointments is likely attributable to the Stentless application, as this application sends text messages to the patient’s mobile phone and notifications to urologist, thereby enhancing the urologist’s vigilance to patient follow-up. Furthermore, the application provides a more efficient and accessible means of monitoring patients with overdue stents compared to an electronic registry. Feedback from both patients and physicians indicated high satisfaction with the Stentless™, especially regarding communication, ease of use, and the effectiveness of SMS reminders in supporting timely stent management.

Advancements in endourology and minimally invasive procedures have led to an escalation in the utilization of indwelling UDJS. Recent studies have underscored a concerning trend where 13 out of every 100 fitted stents are inadvertently forgotten and neglected, heightening the susceptibility to complications such as infections, obstructions, hematuria, fever, and sepsis [5]. Within our cohort, the absence of missed removals in the application group suggests that automated reminders and real-time tracking may help mitigate this persistent safety concern. Ureteral stent encrustation is influenced by various risk factors including urinary tract infections, lithogenic urine composition, and duration of indwelling time, with encrustation rates escalating significantly over time. In a study by Kawahara et al. involving 330 stents, the encrustation rate exhibited an upward trend corresponding to the duration of indwelling time: 26.8% in under 6 weeks, 56.9% between 6 and 12 weeks, and 75.9% exceeding 12 weeks [15]. These findings underscore the critical importance of vigilant monitoring and timely management of indwelling stents to reduce the associated risks of encrustation and related complications. The prevalent material of choice for UDJS is polyurethane, typically intended for an indwelling duration ranging between 3 and 6 months.

The economic burden associated with complicated ureteral stents is substantial, with costs nearly doubling compared to management of uncomplicated cases [16]. Inlaid ureteral stents often necessitate multiple flexible ureteroscopy interventions, and in severe instances, may require interventions as extensive as nephrectomy. A recent study revealed that the medical costs amounted to $2,206 for managing complicated stents as opposed to $1,149 for uncomplicated cases [16]. Ensuring timely removal of ureteral stents and opting for stents with anti-encrustation properties can effectively reduce the financial burden on the medical system. In a study by Sancaktutar et al., which reviewed 27 medical files of patients with overdue stents referred to their clinic between 2007 and 2010, the cost of treatment was estimated to be on average 6.9 times higher than timely stent extractions, necessitating additional radiological studies, interventions, medical treatments, and prolonged hospitalizations [17]. Although a formal cost analysis was beyond the scope of this study, preventing even a single complicated overdue stent may translate into meaningful healthcare savings. The management of overdue stents is critical as complications can pose significant risks to urological patients, culminating in medico-legal challenges. Malpractice lawsuits stemming from endourology surgical procedures often involve issues with overdue stents. Duty et al. reported an average of 13 malpractice lawsuits per year in the USA associated with cystoscopic procedures involving ureteral stent placement/ exchange [18]. A similar study by Osman and Collins researched urological litigation within the UK National Health Service (NHS) between 1995 and 2009 revealed that postoperative-related claims, particularly those related to overdue ureteral stents, accounted for a substantial portion of dissatisfaction with care, with 23 cases identified out of a total of 168 claims [19].

Our findings also support the expanding role of digital health tools in urologic practice. Earlier tracking systems based on electronic registries demonstrated reductions in overdue stents but remained vulnerable to documentation gaps and delayed identification [20]. Lynch et al. reported that an electronic stent register was integrated into the hospital’s network and Electronic Medical Records (EMR) system. This system, following stent insertion and the input of the designated stent lifespan, autonomously generated email notifications to clinical staff members in the event of an overdue stent for removal. They reported that despite implementing barcoding mechanisms, approximately 13% of stents were not effectively flagged for removal even after system enhancements [12]. These examples highlight the ongoing advancements in utilizing technology to enhance the tracking and management of ureteral stents, underscoring the importance of continued innovation in this critical aspect of patient care.

The emergence of smartphone applications has revolutionized the tracking of ureteral stents and improved the management of patients with such devices. Molina et al. presented findings involving 197 patients fitted with ureteral stents, demonstrating that utilizing an application significantly reduces the occurrence of overdue stents. Their study highlighted the usefulness of incorporating automatic notifications for staff and color-coding within the application, concepts that have been successfully integrated into our own application (patients with current ureteral stents displayed in green, those with suppressed stents in gray, and overdue stents appearing in red) [21]. Similarly, Ziemba et al. employed a similar application and identified 3 out of 115 patients solely through the application who missed their scheduled ureteral stent removal appointments. Their study underscored the criticality of tracking ureteral stents to prevent patient safety issues associated with overdue stents [22]. Another application developed by Hameed et al. highlighted the effectiveness of digital platforms for remote healthcare guidance and prompt stent removal, ultimately averting complications and cases of overdue stents [23]. Ulker et al. sought to assess the utility of smartphone-based ureteral stent tracking applications, revealing fewer overdue instances and lost follow-up cases compared to conventional appointment card systems [6]. Notably, our application, includes two crucial and additional features: (1) the ability to send messages to the general practitioner, resulting in 5 patients attending their stent removal appointments due to such alerts, and (2) the capability to identify patients via their ID numbers, enabling stent removal across different medical services. In our study, we successfully identified all patients and ensured timely stent removal, even in cases where patients missed their scheduled appointments but were identified through the application. User acceptance represents a critical determinant of successful technological implementation. Physicians reported high satisfaction across domains including usability, workflow efficiency, cognitive load, and administrative burden, suggesting that the application integrates well into routine clinical practice without increasing operational complexity. Patients likewise expressed strong satisfaction with SMS reminders and physician collaboration, indicating that structured digital communication may improve engagement and adherence. Taken together, these findings suggest that digital tracking systems may represent a scalable patient-safety intervention within endourologic care pathways.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the study was conducted at a single center, which may limit generalizability to institutions with differing workflows or patient populations. Second, although physician and patient satisfaction with the application was evaluated, the comparator workflow used in the electronic registry group was not formally assessed. Future studies directly comparing usability, mental workload, and administrative burden between tracking systems would provide a more comprehensive evaluation of implementation impact. Third, the sample size may have limited statistical power to detect differences in missed removals despite potentially meaningful clinical effects. Additionally, the absence of a nationwide infrastructure for stent-tracking platforms remains a systemic barrier. Broader implementation across institutions could further reduce the incidence of retained stents and improve patient safety.

Conclusions

Through the transmission of the SMS from the application to notify both the patient and the general practitioner, there exists a promising opportunity to enhance patient adherence to scheduled follow-up appointments. This proactive approach holds significant potential in mitigating instances where patients with UDJS fail to return for necessary check-ups, reducing the likelihood of complications attributed to stent encrustation. Leveraging mobile technology for timely communication and reminders stands to not only enhance patient compliance but also contribute to a decrease in adverse outcomes associated with overdue stent care.

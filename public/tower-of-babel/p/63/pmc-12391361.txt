Predictive analysis of concrete tensile strength using metaheuristic algorithms helping a neural network
Authors: Xun Dong
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn most construction projects, concrete is a widely used material in different structural elements due to its suitable mechanical behavior. Therefore, it is crucial to achieve a dependable estimation of the concrete mechanical parameters such as tensile strength. In this research, a well-known machine learning model—multi-layer perceptron neural network (MLPNN)—is optimized by multi-tracker optimization algorithm (MTOA) to avoid computational insufficiencies. The model predicts the splitting tensile strength of concrete based on the features of the concrete mixture. For validation, MTOA is compared to multi-verse optimizer (MVO), crow search algorithm (CSA), and backtracking search algorithm (BSA). Primary results showed that all optimized ANNs can reliably understand and predict the tensile strength pattern. However, from accuracy comparison, MTOA-MLPNN > MVO-MLPNN > CSA-MLPNN > BSA-MLPNN, based on respective percentage errors of 10.75, 11.28, 13.70, and 14.58, as well as correlation coefficients of 0.92, 0.90, 0.87, and 0.83. Moreover, the computational cost of the MTOA-MLPNN was found to be lower than the mentioned benchmarks. In addition, this model was validated using 10-fold cross-validation, and also its accuracy surpassed several models from earlier literature. All in all, the hybrid of MTOA-MLPNN created a scientifically novel and efficient predictive framework for the low-cost analysis of concrete tensile strength and mixture design.Keywords: Concrete, Tensile strength, Neural network, Predictive optimizationSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Estimating the mechanical parameters of concrete, as one of the most versatile construction materials, has always been a primary concern of structural engineers. An important mechanical parameter of concrete is splitting tensile strength (STS) that generally evaluates the quality of cemented materials1. Therefore, proposing state-of-the-art methods to approximate this parameter has been the objective of many studies. Zain, Mahmud, Ilham and Faizal2 for instance, suggested a predictive formula based on compressive strength (CS) for predicting the STS.

More recently, diverse machine learning models have acted as inexpensive and efficient methods for various concrete-related simulations. Artificial neural network (ANN) is a broadly used tool for predicting various characteristics of concrete like bond strength3 and CS4. Azimi-Pour and Eskandari-Naddaf5 employed machine learning (genetic expression programming and ANN) to investigate the effect of micro and nano silica on the flexural strength (FS) and the CS of cement mortar. Castelli, Vanneschi and Silva6 suggested genetic programming (GP) based on geometric semantics for the prediction of concrete strength. This model performed more accurately than the typical GP and a number of standard machine learning models. An ANN-based approach was proposed by Nagajothi and Elavenil7 to investigate the impact of Aluminosilicate on the CS, STS, and FS of geopolymer concrete. With very small relative errors (0.1% for the CS, 1.9% for the STS, and 2.4% for the FS), the ANN emerged as a strong predictor for all studied parameters. Meesaraganda, Saha and Tarafder8 professed the suitability of ANNs for estimating fibers’ self-compacting concrete. The high training and testing capability of the models was demonstrated by mean absolute percentage errors (MAPEs) of nearly 5.0 and 6.4%.

Khademi, Akbari, Jamal and Nikoo9 conducted a comparison among multiple linear regression (MLR), ANN, and fuzzy logic. Referring to accuracy criteria like the coefficient of determination of 0.7456, 0.9226, and 0.8212, it was deduced that the ANN and fuzzy model can provide a significantly better estimation compared to the MLR. Sultana, Hossain, Alam, Islam and Al Abtah10 compared the competency of ANN, response surface methodology (RSM), and support vector regression (SVR) in analyzing the CS and tensile strength of jute fiber reinforced concrete composites (JFRCC). Their findings revealed the superiority of the SVR technique in terms of different accuracy measures.

Moreover, optimization techniques have adequately served for solving various civil engineering optimization problems, especially concrete-related issues. Sharifi, Sadjadi, Aliha and Moniri11 for example, achieved an optimized mixture design of concrete using the Taguchi optimization method. Due to the better performance of the proposed method compared to decision-makers, it was finally introduced as an efficient designer. Tran, Nguyen and Nhat-Duc12 used differential evolution (DE) technique as the optimizer of SVR applied to the prediction of concrete plastic viscosity. Sultana, Hossain, Alam, Hashish and Islam13 used a newly developed metaheuristic algorithm called crow search algorithm (CSA) coupled with the RSM to attain optimal properties of JFRCC.

A significant application of optimization techniques is tuning Behnood and Golafshani14 optimized a simple ANN, in terms of both complexity and accuracy, using grey wolves optimization (GWO) technique for predicting the CS of silica fume concrete. The best-suggested model could predict the CS with root mean square error (RMSE) of 5.4393, mean absolute error of 3.4723, and correlation of 0.9813. For the same purpose, Ma, Foong, Morasaei, Ghabussi and Lyu15 created capable hybrids of ANN by letting grasshopper optimization algorithm (GOA), salp swarm algorithm (SSA), shuffled frog leaping algorithm (SFLA), and artificial bee colony (ABC) tune the network. However, the SSA was finally selected as the best optimizer. This model was also employed by Kandiri, Golafshani and Behnood16 for predicting the same parameter. In similar research, Bui, Nguyen, Chou, Nguyen-Xuan and Ngo17 combined the ANN with a modified version of firefly algorithm (MFA) for approximating the STS. With around 5% less MAPE, the proposed model outperformed the fitting curve used for this objective. Also, the correlation of the MFA-ANN was 0.3 larger (0.93 vs. 0.96). Another optimization of ANN was carried out by Umeonyiagu and Nwobi-Okoye18 who used genetic algorithm (GA) for predicting and optimizing the cost, FS, and TS of concrete reinforced by bamboo. The model achieved correlations larger than 0.99 for predicting all three parameters.

While many studies have proven the suitability of machine learning models for predictive analysis, there are still some concerns that can be expressed as follows:

For example, accuracy can be generally impacted by local minima (i.e., premature convergence after a few iterations)19. A remedy to this issue can lie in utilizing metaheuristic optimization algorithms to ensure a thorough search for the solution.

Provided by Zhao, Hu, Ding, Zhao, Li and Pei21 a large dataset of uniaxial compressive strength and splitting tensile strength of concrete was collected from 41 previous works. Methodology

The MTOA

Unlike most metaheuristic optimizers that are inspired by natural phenomena, the MTOA is based on the cooperative behavior of trackers. The MTOA was developed by Zakeri, Moezi, Bazargan-Lari and Zare22. In this algorithm, some global trackers (GTs) are scattered in the existing space. As is illustrated in Fig. 2, each GT is surrounded by a number of local trackers (LTs) locating in a certain radius (RS).

The benchmarks

Introduced by Mirjalili, Mirjalili and Hatamlou27 the MVO is a capable optimizer that works based on the multi-verse theory. The interaction between white holes and black holes (for exploration), as well as wormholes (for exploitation) is the idea for finding the optimal solution. A so-called parameter “inflation rate” is defined for each universe and the objects are transformed among the universes (from those with low inflation to those with high inflation) to establish a suitable average rate. Using two parameters, namely wormhole existence probability and traveling distance rate, the individuals try to update their position within the existing cosmos to improve solution28. The mathematical details of the MVO are well described in studies like29–31.

Civicioglu32 presented the BSA as a population-based search scheme. The algorithm goes through six steps, namely initialization, first selection, mutation, crossover, second selection, and fitness evaluation to fulfill the optimization. First, a random set of individuals is created as the population P. Also, historical populations (Pold) are generated in the first selection step. In the mutation step, the operators create a trial population (Mutant) from P and Pold. Next, a final cross-trial population (T) is generated using Mutant and P. In the second selection, the P is adjusted with T individuals regarding the best-fitted individual. Steps 2–5 are iterated to improve the solution until a termination condition is satisfied33. The optimization strategy employed by the BSA is better detailed in Refs34–36.

The main idea of the CSA is the flock-based behavior of crows. This algorithm was designed by Askarzadeh37. Food-seeking efforts comprise three major regulations: (a) crows are capable of hiding their food and also recalling its position later, (b) some plan for following each other for thieving food, and (c) the target crows are capable of protecting their food against thieves. The chasing process arises two possible cases: either the crow i is not aware of being followed by crow j (in this case the position of crow j is updated) or the crow i is aware of being followed by crow j (that in this case the crow i dodges it and considers another position)38. Earlier works of literature39–41 have presented a detailed mechanism of the CSA.

Results and discussion

Pre-processing and training details

As the first step, the training dataset is randomly taken out of the existing data. This dataset comprises 80% of the whole (i.e., 323 rows) which equals 258 rows. Metaheuristic algorithms adjust the biases and weights of the ANN so that they give the best explanation of the relationship between the training targets and the corresponding inputs. This process is known as training. After this step, the knowledge of the networks is tested by predicting the STS for the residual 20% of data which equals 65 rows.

To elaborate on the training process, Fig. 3 exhibits the MLPNN that is exposed to the MTOA, MVO, BSA, and CSA. The biases and the weights are shown by red and black arrows, respectively. As is seen, this neural network is a three-layered system with eight neurons that receive the eight inputs in the first layer, four hidden neurons, and one output neuron that produces STS. It is worth explaining that the number of hidden layers (to be 1) and the number of hidden neurons (to be 4) were determined after a trial-and-error practice. In this regard, many ANN configurations were tested containing 1–5 hidden layers each with 1–10 neurons.

The proposed neural architecture.

Training and cost

During the optimization of MLPNN using metaheuristic strategies, the algorithms update the neural parameters iteratively aiming a better knowledge (i.e., lower training error) in the next iteration. Monitoring the error reduction is fulfilled by measuring the RMSE of training. The algorithms are executed with one thousand iterations to have enough opportunities for the betterment of the solution.

As is known, the MTOA, MVO, BSA, and CSA are population-based techniques; meaning that a certain number of individuals cooperate to find an optimal solution. Hence, to optimize the effect of population size (Npop), the most suitable value is determined by going through a trial-and-error practice. For all algorithms, the tested Npops vary from 10 to 500 (10, 50, 100, 200, 300, 400, and 500). This process pointed out that the MTOA gives the best training for MLPNN when Npop = 200, while the best performance of the MVO, BSA, and CSA is achieved by Npop = 300. Limitations and future efforts

The models of this study were developed upon a public dataset (from Study21. In the downloadable dataset, the records of STS were listed against CA, SR, CTS, CCS, Dmax, W/B, SPC, and FM as influential factors. While the source states the presence of silica fume, slag, and fly ash in the binder materials of the samples, no specific records of these three factors were provided in the shared dataset. Moreover, the records of the slump of concrete mixtures, whilst provided, did not meet the precision criterion and were excluded from the used dataset. The addition of these four factors can be deemed as a limitation of this research which can be remedied in future studies by incorporating datasets that have these factors.

Another worth-mentioning aspect of data is that the used dataset was divided into the training and testing subsets. The reason for not including the validation subset was that the used metaheuristic optimizers can protect MLPNN from overfitting. However, cross-validation methods (such as k-fold) are generally recommended for more reliable evaluations of machine learning.

Moreover, from a machine learning models perspective, combining metaheuristic algorithms with MLPNN was a challenge due to the complex nature of the algorithms. This study was limited to the baseline model of MLPNN and four optimizers. Future studies are therefore encouraged to replace MLPNN with other plausible baselines such as random forest. Researchers may also evaluate the effectiveness of other models that are receiving increasing attention such as deep learning.

By addressing these limitations from the present study, it is believed that more robust solutions can be found for machine learning-based STS analysis.

Conclusions

A robust metaheuristic optimizer, namely multi-tracker optimization algorithm (MTOA), was efficiently applied to the prediction of the splitting tensile strength (STS) of concrete. The search strategy of this algorithm could explain the non-linear interaction between the STS of concrete and mixture properties through a predictive neural framework. The proposed strategy (MAPE = 10.7585% and R = 0.92472) surpassed three other strong optimizers, i.e., multi-verse optimizer (MVO), crow search algorithm (CSA), and backtracking search algorithm (BSA) in reproducing the STS behavior for unseen concrete conditions. Another advantage of the MTOA was operating in a less crowded space, due to a smaller population size required. The findings suggest the high applicability of the MTOA–in combination with artificial neural network–in the industry for early, non-destructive, and inexpensive prediction of STS and optimum design of concrete mixture. Moreover, from comparison with earlier research, the suggested MTOA yielded improved accuracy, providing insights for future scientific modeling. Albeit, the limitations of this effort outlined possible improvements (e.g., dataset enrichment and trying further optimization/predictive algorithms) as potential avenues for future efforts.

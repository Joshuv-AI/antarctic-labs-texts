Optimization of regional echinococcosis control strategies in Xizang: a health economics perspective
Authors: Zhiyi Wang, Quzhen Gongsang, Mingzhe Jiang, Jiaxi Lei, Pin Yang, Ying Wang, Liying Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundEchinococcosis remains a major public health concern in Xizang, China, where comprehensive control measures have been implemented for many years. Evaluating the cost-effectiveness of key interventions, including population screening, patient treatment, and dog deworming, is essential for optimizing control strategies and improving health resource allocation.MethodsAnnual data on the three major echinococcosis prevention and control measures implemented in 74 counties of Xizang between 2015 and 2022 were collected. The costs of population screening, patient treatment, and dog deworming were estimated. By comparing the actual disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) of echinococcosis in each county with the estimated DALYs under a no-intervention scenario, the DALYs averted by the prevention and control measures were quantified. Cost-effectiveness analysis was conducted using the incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER) and the ICER-to-GDP ratio.ResultsFrom 2015 to 2022, the total investment in the three major prevention and control measures for echinococcosis in Xizang amounted to 14,519.41 10k CNY. During this period, the interventions cumulatively averted 12,531.9 DALYs. The average ICER was 1.16 10k CNY per DALY averted, corresponding to an ICER-to-GDP ratio of 0.20. Overall, the implemented interventions demonstrated favorable cost-effectiveness and health benefits in most regions, although substantial disparities were observed across different areas. Notably, eight counties had an ICER exceeding 6.00 10k CNY/DALY, and ten counties had an ICER-to-GDP ratio greater than 1.ConclusionsThe comprehensive echinococcosis prevention and control measures in Xizang have significantly reduced the disease burden and demonstrated overall favorable cost-effectiveness. However, cost-effectiveness varied considerably across regions. Strategies should be dynamically adjusted based on local epidemiological characteristics, with targeted interventions implemented to optimize health resource allocation and improve prevention and control efficiency.

Introduction

Echinococcosis, also known as hydatid disease, is a zoonotic parasitic disease caused by the larval stages of cestodes (tapeworms) of the genus Echinococcus and remains a major global public health concern. Cystic echinococcosis (CE) and alveolar echinococcosis (AE), the two most common forms of human echinococcosis, are caused by the larvae of Echinococcus granulosus and Echinococcus multilocularis, respectively [1]. Echinococcosis is a chronic parasitic disease that can affect various organs, most commonly the liver [2, 3]. CE primarily causes mechanical damage through compression of surrounding tissues, organs, and blood vessels, whereas AE is highly invasive and is associated with a mortality rate of more than 90% within 10–15 years if untreated or inadequately treated [4–6]. China is one of the countries most seriously affected by echinococcosis, bearing the largest population at risk and the number of cases in the world [7, 8]. It has been reported that the disease burden in China is estimated at 322,400 disability-adjusted life years (DALYs), representing 40% and 95% of the global DALYs for CE and AE, respectively [9–12]. Echinococcosis is endemic in 370 counties across nine provinces and autonomous regions in China, including Inner Mongolia, Sichuan, Yunnan, Xizang, Shaanxi, Gansu, Qinghai, Ningxia, and Xinjiang [13]. Among these, Xizang demonstrates both the highest echinococcosis burden and the highest prevalence rate [7]. In the 2012–2016 nationwide survey, the prevalence in Xizang was estimated at 1.66%, which was substantially higher than the national average of 0.24% [7]. At the county level, the distribution of prevalence was highly uneven, with marked variation across counties [14]. At a finer spatial scale, township-level analyses further revealed substantial spatial heterogeneity, with prevalence varying markedly across areas (ranging from 0% to 7.78%) [15]. These findings indicate that echinococcosis in Xizang is characterized by both high burden and wide endemic distribution.

To address this public health challenge, Xizang has gradually established and strengthened a comprehensive echinococcosis prevention and control system with the support of the Central Government Transfer Payment Project since 2008 [16]. These efforts, supported by sustained governmental investment, are primarily aimed at protecting population health and improving quality of life. In 2016, a sampling survey for echinococcosis was conducted across all 74 counties in Xizang, and from 2017 onward, population-based screening and comprehensive interventions were progressively implemented. The core prevention and control measures for echinococcosis primarily include: registration and regular deworming of dogs to break the transmission chain; annual population screening to enable early detection and diagnosis; providing free albendazole treatment for confirmed patients and offering financial subsidies for those who meet the surgical criteria and voluntarily choose surgery [12]. However, the implementation and performance of these measures are strongly influenced by the local context of Xizang, including vast territory, high altitude, dispersed populations, inconvenient transportation, limited health resources, and relatively weak medical infrastructure. These factors are likely to influence both the implementation costs of interventions and the health outcomes achieved, thereby affecting the overall cost-effectiveness of echinococcosis control strategies.

Despite substantial governmental investments for echinococcosis prevention and control, current research has predominantly focused on the disease burden of patients or the associated economic losses [17, 18], while a systematic evaluation of the overall costs and economic efficiency of the prevention and control efforts remains lacking. Health economic methodologies serve as essential tools for assessing the economic efficiency of public health interventions, with cost-effectiveness analysis (CEA) being one of the most commonly used approaches [19]. CEA can employ the incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER) to estimate the additional cost required per unit of health gain achieved. Population screening, patient treatment, and dog deworming correspond to the three key components of prevention, treatment, and control, respectively, and represent the core interventions. This study collected data on the implementation and costs of these three measures across 74 counties in Xizang from 2015 to 2022. The effectiveness of the interventions was assessed using DALYs averted, and a health economic evaluation was conducted using the incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER) and the ICER-to-GDP ratio. This study aims to elucidate the cost-effectiveness of these interventions across different years and regions in Xizang, thereby providing a scientific basis for future strategy adjustments and optimal allocation of health resources.

Materials and methods

Data sources

Echinococcosis prevention and control data from 2015 to 2022 across all 74 counties in Xizang were obtained from the annual reporting system of the Xizang Center for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC).

Population screening and patient management data were first recorded and aggregated annually at the township health-center or county-level healthcare-facility level, then reported to the county CDC, and subsequently reviewed and submitted stepwise to the prefecture- and provincial-level CDCs. Dog deworming data were initially recorded at the village level and similarly aggregated upward through the county reporting system. At each administrative level, records were checked for completeness, logical consistency, and internal consistency before final provincial aggregation.

Limited data availability in some counties during 2015–2016 was not treated as conventional random missing data. Before the 2016 national epidemiological survey, endemic counties in Xizang had not yet been fully identified, and many counties had not formally initiated large-scale prevention and control activities. Therefore, the apparent absence or low level of reporting in some counties during this period mainly reflected incomplete program implementation and coverage rather than missing records from already implemented activities. Reporting became substantially more systematic and standardized after 2017.

Analysis methods

Cost analysis

The input costs for echinococcosis prevention and control in Xizang mainly include three parts: population screening, patient treatment, and dog deworming. Population screening costs were estimated based on the standard cost for population B-ultrasound screening under the “Echinococcosis Control Project Funds”, combined with the actual number of people receiving B-ultrasound screening each year.

Patient treatment costs were divided into medication treatment and surgical treatment. Medication treatment costs included the costs of albendazole tablets and emulsion. Their procurement prices were sourced from the Xizang Government Procurement Network [22]. For cost estimation, albendazole tablets and emulsion were set at 1.00 CNY per tablet and 0.22 CNY per milliliter, respectively. Surgical treatment costs were estimated according to the “Management Measures for the Central Government Transfer Payment Project”. From 2015 to 2018, a uniform subsidy of 8000 CNY per patient was applied. From 2019 to 2022, this was differentiated into 8000 CNY for CE patients and 11,000 CNY for AE or mixed-type patients. For patients with unclassified lesions, the surgical cost was estimated using a weighted average of these subsidies, based on the total proportion of confirmed CE, AE, and mixed-type cases.

Dog deworming costs were estimated following the “Technical Scheme for Echinococcosis Control (2019 Edition)” [23], which mandates monthly deworming for all registered dogs in 74 counties of Xizang. The dosage of Praziquantel chewable tablets (200 mg, 0.90 CNY per tablet) was stratified by dog weight: 50 mg for dogs under 5 kg, 200 mg for dogs weighing 5–15 kg, and 400 mg for dogs over 15 kg. The dog population was assumed to be equally distributed across these three weight categories for cost estimation.

All costs were calculated in nominal terms based on the actual expenditures incurred during the corresponding year, and no inflation adjustment or currency standardization was performed.

Health economic analysis

Disability-adjusted life years (DALYs). We estimated the annual burden of echinococcosis in Xizang from 2015 to 2022 based on DALYs using routinely collected prevention and control data. The estimation was performed for two scenarios: the actual scenario with interventions and a theoretical “no-intervention” scenario for comparison.

In this formula, D is a disability weight, β is an age-weighting function parameter, C is an age-weighting correction constant, γ is a discount rate, α is the average age at clinical onset, and L is the duration of disability or time lost because of death. Disability weights for cured, improved, and ineffective outcomes were assigned as 0, 0.200, and 0.239, respectively, following the approach of Budke et al. [11], in which outcome-based weights for echinococcosis were derived by mapping post-treatment outcomes to comparable health states. For deceased cases, a weight of 1 was assigned to represent the maximum health loss. The duration (L) for death was defined as the loss of life years, approximated as the difference between standard life expectancy and the average age at death among patients, which was obtained from questionnaire-based survey data. Therefore, mortality was incorporated by approximating years of life lost within the unified DALY calculation framework. β was set at 0.03 based on the average age of onset and the average age of patients in Xizang. γ was set at 7%, based on the average GDP growth rate in Xizang over the past 10 years. This value was used as a context-specific assumption to reflect the local socioeconomic setting, rather than as a standard discount parameter. The parameter L for non-fatal outcomes was determined based on values reported by Budke et al. [11] for echinococcosis.

Calculation of actual DALYs (with intervention). For the actual scenario with interventions, the total annual DALYs were calculated by aggregating data from patients with follow-up outcomes. In this formula, DALYi is the per-person DALY for each group categorized by follow-up outcomes, Pi is the number of patients in each follow-up outcome group, F is the total number of patients followed up in the year, and P is the total number of patients in the year. For patients lost to follow-up, their DALYs were estimated using the average per-patient DALY derived from the “no-intervention” scenario (described below).

Estimation of DALYs (without intervention). The theoretical disease burden without intervention was estimated as a counterfactual baseline for comparison. The baseline burden of echinococcosis in Xizang was derived from a previously published study, which estimated 121 DALYs per 10,000 population using the WHO-recommended DALY framework, representing the combined burden of cystic and alveolar echinococcosis [17]. To account for spatial heterogeneity, county-level prevalence data from the 2017 population-based screening were used as weighting factors to scale the baseline burden across counties. Annual population data were then incorporated to estimate the expected DALYs under the no-intervention scenario for each year from 2015 to 2022. Therefore, the estimated no-intervention DALYs varied across counties according to differences in prevalence and across years according to changes in population size.

Calculation of ICER. To evaluate the economic value of the echinococcosis control program, we calculated the incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER), which represents the additional cost required to avert one DALY. In this formula, ΔC denotes the sum of all costs attributable to the prevention and control program from 2015 to 2022, including population screening, patient treatment, and dog deworming. ΔE is the total incremental health benefit, measured in DALYs averted by the end of 2022. The ICER is presented in 10k CNY per DALY averted (10k CNY/DALY).

The willingness-to-pay (WTP) threshold is a critical benchmark used to determine whether an intervention is cost-effective. It represents the maximum cost that a decision-maker is willing to pay for a one-unit improvement in a health outcome (one DALY averted). In this study, the WTP threshold was set equal to the per-capita Gross Domestic Product (GDP) of the region as a reference value commonly used in health economic evaluations. If the ICER-to-GDP ratio is less than 1, the intervention may be considered cost-effective under this reference threshold. A lower ICER indicates that less investment is required to achieve one unit of health gain, reflecting greater cost-effectiveness.

Results

Total input cost for echinococcosis prevention and control

From 2015 to 2022, the total cumulative cost of echinococcosis prevention and control in Xizang was 14,519.41 10k CNY, as detailed in Table 1. This total cost consisted of three main components: 5367.43 10k CNY for population screening, 8056.03 10k CNY for patient treatment (including 2175.36 10k CNY for medication treatment and 5880.67 10k CNY for surgical treatment), and 1095.95 10k CNY for dog deworming. In total, 5,367,429 people were screened, and 7310 confirmed patients underwent surgery, including 6620 CE, 442 AE, 36 mixed-type, and 212 unclassified cases. A total of 11,240,509 dog deworming treatments were administered. Over time, both investment and intervention workload showed distinct temporal patterns. In 2015 and 2016, the total cost was relatively low (143.27 10k CNY and 179.49 10k CNY, respectively). From 2017 to 2018, the region entered an intensive control phase, with total costs rapidly rising to peak values of 4550.42 10k CNY in 2017 and 4781.65 10k CNY in 2018, during which the scale of population screening, patient treatment, and dog deworming significantly expanded. Starting from 2019, the total cost began to decrease and stabilize at a lower level, ranging from 531.29 to 1882.42 10k CNY between 2019 and 2022. In terms of regional distribution, notable disparities were observed across cities. Discussion

Echinococcosis is a widespread zoonotic disease, and its effective prevention and control rely on comprehensive strategies and multi-sectoral collaboration [24, 25]. The Xizang Autonomous Region has implemented long-term, sustained efforts, characterized by extensive duration, large-scale resource investment, and broad social mobilization. These efforts have successfully engaged society-wide participation and yielded notable achievements in prevention and control.

Using comprehensive control data for echinococcosis in the Xizang Autonomous Region from 2015 to 2022, this study applied key health economic indicators, DALYs averted, ICER, and ICER-to-GDP ratio, to systematically evaluate the cost-effectiveness of main intervention measures, including population screening, patient treatment, and dog deworming, across 74 counties. The results indicate that the echinococcosis prevention and control in Xizang have achieved significant progress. The integrated interventions effectively reduced the disease burden, and demonstrated overall favorable cost-effectiveness, although notable regional variation was observed.

Population screening, patient treatment, and dog deworming have proven effective in reducing the burden of echinococcosis. From 2015 to 2022, the actual DALYs accounted for less than a quarter of the estimated DALYs under the no-intervention scenario, with 12,531.9 DALYs averted—corresponding to an averted proportion of 77.41%. These outcomes reflect the significant benefits delivered by the Central Government Transfer Payment Project for echinococcosis prevention and control, demonstrating its substantial contribution to public health in Xizang. Previous studies have tended to overestimate the disease burden in Xizang compared with actual findings. The 2017 general population survey reported a prevalence of about 0.61%, which is significantly lower than the estimates in some studies [9]. However, the intervention effect was limited in a few counties. For instance, Sog County in Nagqu and Nang County in Nyingchi each averted less than 10% of the estimated DALYs, which may be attributed to complex local geographical conditions, limited health resources, and difficulties in implementing prevention and control measures [26, 27]. Notably, Gerze County in Ngari Prefecture demonstrated a negative value for averted DALYs. This occurs, because DALYs averted were defined as the difference between the estimated DALYs under the no-intervention scenario and the observed DALYs under the intervention scenario; thus, a negative value indicates that the estimated actual DALYs exceeded the expected counterfactual burden. This result does not necessarily imply that the intervention increased disease burden, but is more likely attributable to data uncertainty. In this study, this may be attributed to the county’s 2022 patient cohort of 78 individuals, coupled with a follow-up rate of only 57.7% (45/78). The incomplete follow-up likely introduced assessment bias in evaluating treatment outcomes, potentially leading to an overestimation of the actual DALYs. This deviation underscores the critical importance of complete follow-up and robust patient management in the effective control of echinococcosis.

The ICER effectively reflects the cost-effectiveness variations between intervention and no-intervention scenarios [28]. Our findings demonstrate that most regions of Xizang exhibit low ICER values, suggesting that the current primary strategies for echinococcosis prevention and control are cost-effective. However, the elevated ICERs observed in some regions highlight the potential for optimizing resource utilization. Screening and intervention strategies should, therefore, be further optimized according to local epidemiological conditions. For example, increasing screening investment in high-prevalence areas, such as Nagqu and Ngari, while optimizing the scale and targeting of screening in low-prevalence areas, such as Lhasa and Lhoka, may improve overall efficiency. Such adjustments do not imply restricting access to screening services, but rather shifting from large-scale universal screening to more targeted approaches, ensuring that high-risk populations and potential patients continue to have equitable access to screening and diagnosis. In addition, strengthening dog deworming during high-risk periods and focusing on key populations may further enhance intervention efficiency. Furthermore, inadequate patient follow-up in some regions may have led to an overestimation of the actual DALYs, consequently causing an overestimation of the ICER, underscoring the need to strengthen follow-up procedures to ensure data accuracy and a true reflection of intervention impact.

The WTP threshold provides an intuitive benchmark for assessing the cost-effectiveness of interventions [29]. A lower ICER-to-GDP ratio indicates higher cost-effectiveness. According to WHO recommendations, China adopts 1–3 times the per-capita GDP as the WTP judgment threshold: a value below 1 indicates high cost-effectiveness; between 1 and 3 indicates moderate cost-effectiveness; and above 3 indicates low cost-effectiveness [30]. In Xizang, the overall ICER-to-GDP ratio (0.20) was below 1 times the per-capita GDP, demonstrating that the echinococcosis prevention and control measures showed high cost-effectiveness. However, significant disparities were observed across counties. Specifically, ten counties recorded ICER-to-GDP ratios exceeding 1, and in some cases even 3. These counties were distributed across four cities of Xizang, indicating that most cities contained underperforming areas requiring focused attention. Notably, Sog County in Nagqu City, Nang County and Bomi County in Nyingchi City, and Dinggye County and Yadong County in Shigatse City were among those with the highest ICER-to-GDP ratios, suggesting shortcomings in either cost control or intervention efficiency. It is recommended that these areas receive enhanced technical guidance and operational oversight to improve their cost-effectiveness profile.

In summary, the Xizang government has made substantial and sustained investments in echinococcosis prevention and control, which have effectively curbed disease transmission and significantly improved the overall epidemic situation. The comprehensive interventions implemented averted an estimated 12,531.9 DALYs, reflecting a governance philosophy that prioritizes people’s welfare and values health above all. To address persistent challenges and enhance cost-effectiveness, targeted improvements are recommended across several interconnected areas: regions with low follow-up rates such as Gerze County (57.7%) should strengthen patient follow-up systems to ensure accurate DALY estimation and reliable ICER calculation; screening strategies must be dynamically adjusted based on local epidemiological trends, with health economics evaluation at county or township levels to reallocate resources toward populations yielding larger health benefits; patient management should be optimized through individualized treatment plans based on lesion type and patient preference, ensuring timely surgery for eligible cases and reinforcing medication follow-up to improve adherence [31]; and dog deworming should be enhanced through improved dog registration, expanded coverage, strengthened health education among farmers and herders, and standardized deworming protocols to interrupt transmission at its source. Through systematic implementation of these integrated strategies, the overall efficiency of echinococcosis prevention and control in Xizang can be substantially elevated.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, the cost analysis included only the three main intervention measures and did not capture all relevant expenditures, such as health education, personnel and equipment investment, program management, transportation, logistics, and household costs, which may have led to an underestimation of total costs and an overestimation of cost-effectiveness. Second, the DALY estimation relied on context-specific parameter settings, including the use of the average GDP growth rate as the discount rate and the application of age weighting, which are not fully consistent with current standard practice and may affect the comparability of the results. In addition, all cost data were analyzed in nominal terms without adjustment for inflation, which may affect the comparability of costs across different years over the study period. Furthermore, key assumptions such as dog weight distribution, discount rate, and age weighting may influence the magnitude of the ICER estimates, introducing some uncertainty into the cost-effectiveness results. Despite these limitations, the findings provide useful evidence on the cost-effectiveness of echinococcosis control. Future studies incorporating more comprehensive cost data and standardized methodological assumptions are warranted.

Conclusions

This study systematically evaluated the costs and cost-effectiveness of comprehensive echinococcosis prevention and control measures in the Xizang Autonomous Region from 2015 to 2022. The findings indicate that the interventions—population screening, patient treatment, and dog deworming—effectively reduced disease burden and demonstrated favorable cost-effectiveness. Although regional disparities in cost-effectiveness and resource allocation persist, these can be addressed through strengthened management and locally tailored strategies. The successful experience of echinococcosis prevention and control in Xizang provides a valuable reference for other highly endemic regions implementing similar interventions.

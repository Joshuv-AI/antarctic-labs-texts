Eight-year evaluation of dental public health students’ research using balanced scorecard – Retrospective study
Authors: Khaled Keraa, Amira Saad Badran, Mahassen Mohamed Farghaly
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroduction:Measuring the performance of dental schools in the research area has always been challenging. Balanced Scorecard (BSC) is a performance measurement tool with four perspectives that balance financial and non-financial performance indicators. Some medical colleges use BSC for performance measurement of all departments’ activities while others use it in some sections or departments. This study is the first in the dental education field to use the BSC approach to assess the performance measurement of dental students’ research projects.Methods:A self-developed BSC was designed by the staff members at the dental public health department to reflect the educational strategies and objectives of the department; all data on dental public health students’ research from 2013/2014 to 2020/2021 were collected from the dental public health department, Dental Clinics Complex (DCC) files as well as databases of the Quality Assurance Unit to cover the basic four balanced perspectives.Results:Data from the four perspectives were introduced to Balanced Scorecard Pro Software Version 4.7.1.20. The resulting balanced scorecard indicated that the overall performance of students’ research activities was 89.38%. The learning and growth perspective showed the highest performance (95.33%) followed by the financial perspective (92.5%) then the internal process perspective (89.55%) while the customer perspective showed the lowest performance (82.19%).Conclusions:BSC is an efficient tool for the measurement of the performance of the departments at medical colleges. Our process is highly adaptable and can be easily implemented by any section, or institution looking to advance its educational mission and vision.Keywords: Balanced scorecard, Medical education, Medical colleges, Performance measurement, Key performance indicators, Health care organization

Introduction

“The Balanced Scorecard (BSC) is an integrated performance management system that enables organizations to clarify their strategy and translate it into action”. It includes both financial and non-financial measures, besides measuring existing performance outcomes and future performance drivers (Eltobgy & Radwan, 2015). They have been successfully used worldwide by many healthcare managers for the performance evaluation (P.E.) of Health Care Organizations (HCO); in academic clinical departments (Donnelly et al., 2010), graduate medical education (Murray et al., 2009), and teaching hospitals (Trotta et al., 2013, Amer et al., 2022, Huntington et al., 2018). Performance measurement is a key element for (HCO) to have a strong system that enables the management to measure, improve, and drive performance (Jaber & Nashwan, 2022).

The Balanced Scorecard (BSC) is a popular tool used to measure organizational performance. It typically measures performance across four balanced perspectives (Fig. 1): financial perspective, which focuses on the efficient and effective use of facilities; customers’ perspective, which is related to stakeholders' satisfaction with the services provided; learning and growth, which is related to the capacity building and adoption of up-to-date practices by the organization; and internal process, which is linked to the organization's reputation and how it sets itself apart from its competitors(Jaber & Nashwan, 2022).

Balanced Score Cards’ relation with vision and strategy (Kaplan and Norton, 1996).

Medical colleges are complex healthcare organizations that must fulfill three major responsibilities − patient care, teaching, and research (Trotta et al., 2013, Behrouzi et al., 2014). However, this is a challenging task that requires significant time and effort. Patient care needs to be delivered urgently while teaching and training medical students to become competent health professionals takes an intermediate period. Finally, the research component usually requires a long duration (Saurabh & Prateek, 2021).

Higher education plays a crucial role in the future development and economic growth of countries. Nowadays, medical educational institutions are encountering numerous challenges such as the rapid expansion of information technology, globalization, increased competition, and resource limitations. Universities willing to conquer the competitive markets must follow the strong recommendations in the current literature to adopt a self-developed balanced scorecard methodology to be used as a monitoring tool and assessment system (Al-Zwyalif, 2012). Research is needed to measure the impact of BSC implementation in HCO during the pandemic, as previous studies have reported a knowledge gap in this area (Amer et al., 2022).

The process of designing a BSC for a medical college requires the contribution of faculty members to reach a consensus on the suitable formulation of their self-developed BSC (Saurabh & Prateek, 2020). Considering the views of different stakeholders; both internal and external stakeholders, innovation/growth perspectives, financial/resources perspective, and finally the internal processes (Al-Zwyalif, 2012). One important thing is that all the formulated performance indices should be meaningful, measurable, and time-bound (Saurabh & Prateek, 2020).

Some previous attempts have been reported in universities to use BSC for performance measurement, where some of the universities used it to measure all their activities while others used it in some sections only (Rompho, 2008).

In 2014; Binden et al designed and implemented a model for Malaysian higher educational institutes (Binden et al., 2014). Modifications and new models of BSC have been developed such as the model designed by Pietrzak et al in 2015 to be applied in a Polish university (Pietrzak et al., 2015). In Jordan, Al-Zwyalif evaluated the knowledge of private Jordanian universities about the importance of BSC and the requirements of applying it (Al-Zwyalif, 2012). The results showed that universities are aware of BSC’s importance, and they have the human and financial resources to use it. As regards Egypt; Eltobgy and Radwan have developed software to apply BSC for measuring the performance of Egyptian higher educational institutes. This is a guide that helps with the implementation of the Balanced Scorecard (BSC) methodology. The guide proposes six performance indicators that cover different aspects, such as educational and learning excellence, scientific research excellence, community participation, environment development, human and material resources, financial resources, and institutional capacity and quality management (Eltobgy & Radwan, 2015).

There have been limited studies on using the Balanced Scorecard (BSC) to measure performance in higher education in developing nations. This study holds significance as it is the first in the dental education domain to employ the BSC methodology to evaluate the performance of dental students' research.

Materials and methods

Description of the included research

Dental public health is an undergraduate course delivered to second-year students at The Faculty of Oral and Dental Medicine – Misr International University (MIU). As part of this course, the students conduct research projects involving patients attending the (DCC) at the faculty. These research projects started in the academic year 2013/2014 and ended in 2020/2021 due to applying an updated undergraduate educational program. The research topic was changed annually or biannually according to the study design and the required sample size. Five different pieces of research projects were conducted over eight years.

For each research project, the dental public health staff members submitted a written protocol to the faculty’s research committee, followed by the ethical committee to obtain the IRB number. Following this the protocol was registered at the Clinicaltrials.gov database.

Each research project included a theoretical and a practical section. The theoretical part focused on educating the students on how to conduct a literature search and how to write a mini-review related to the topic of the research. The mini-reviews were included in the summative assessment of the students. They were also taught to develop oral health educational materials in the form of posters, leaflets, games, or fliers. In some research projects, students were taught to develop the questionnaires they used to interview the patients.

The practical part of the research projects included students’ training by the staff members using different educational methods based on the nature of each research. Some training included role-playing to simulate the interviews with the patients. Others included thorough training on measuring blood pressure, blood glucose level, and Body Mass Index (BMI) of the patients. After the training, the students interviewed the patients, led the questionnaires then delivered awareness sessions and educational materials to the patients. In the academic years 2019/2020 and 2020/2021 the emergence of the COVID-19 pandemic hindered contact between students and patients, so the students were involved in developing the study questionnaire that aimed to assess the knowledge and perceptions of dental students about COVID-19 and its impact on their online learning experience and their mental health.

The data obtained from each research project was processed, statistically analyzed, and written in the form of a manuscript by the staff members and then submitted for publication in Scopus-indexed journals in addition to being presented at dental conferences as shown in Table 1.

Designing the BSC

To design a BSC; all data on dental public health students’ research projects from 2013/2014 to 2020/2021 were collected from the dental public health department, (DCC) files as well as databases of the Quality Assurance Unit at the faculty to cover the basic four balanced perspectives:

Data from the customer perspective included: students’ evaluation of research activities, students’ evaluation of staff members and courses, students’/Alumni’s satisfaction with the educational program, and stakeholders’ satisfaction with faculty graduates.

The satisfaction scores were obtained from the research project evaluation questionnaires that were distributed to students on the day of their final exam. External examiners also answered a questionnaire about the research and their comments were used for improving the following year’s research project (Fig. 2).

Students and external examiners’ research evaluation questionnaires.

Data from a financial perspective focuses on the efficient and effective use of resources. All research projects were fully funded by the university. Data about the use of resources were obtained from the research evaluation report prepared by the staff members at the end of each academic year. The report includes feedback from the students, staff members’ monitoring of the research process as well as Dental Clinics Complex administration report including data about resources.

Data of internal processes perspectives included: Staff members: student ratio, up-to-date teaching practices, availability of policies and procedures, and organizational structure. These data were obtained through the Quality Assurance Unit reports.

Data on learning and growth perspective included: infrastructure and facilities, number of research topics’ publications/presentations in international journals/conferences, and number of teaching workshops attended by the staff members of the department (Jet et al., 2017).

The Balanced Scorecard was designed to include the four perspectives using the BSC Designer PRO software.

Results

The total number of students who participated in these research projects over the five academic years was 2269 dental students of both genders. The distribution of students’ numbers over the years is presented in Fig. 3. The first two research projects comprised 652 and 600 students, respectively. In the academic year 2017/2018, the number of students was 310. The following project lasted for two academic years with a total of 440 students and the final research comprised 267 students.

Bar chart representing the number of second-year dental students participating in dental students’ research.

Customer perspective

The results of students’ evaluation of research projects are presented in Fig. 4. The results of satisfaction questionnaires showed an increase in the percentage of satisfaction that reached 84.2 % with the last research project with an average satisfaction of 78.7 %. The mean evaluation score of the student’s satisfaction with the staff members and the courses was 82.5 %. The mean score of Student/Alumni satisfaction with education was 86.6 %. Stakeholders’ satisfaction with faculty graduates’ mean satisfaction scores was 88.4 %. The total number of research articles submitted/published in international journals through the eight years was five articles as obtained from the research database of the faculty. The percentage of faculty members accessing library resources was 76.6 % while the percentage of students accessing library resources was 82.4 %.

Line chart representing students’ satisfaction with research activities.

Financial perspective

The university administration provided all equipment and supplies upon request of the dental public health department. So, there was an efficient and effective use of resources. Some equipment such as sphygmomanometers needed replacement or maintenance as reported by students in their feedback about the research. So when calculating the score from this perspective, supplies and human resources were at 100 %, but due to problems with some equipment, the score was reduced to 90 %.

Internal processes perspective

The faculty received accreditation from the National Authority for Quality Assurance and Accreditation of Education in January 2017, and the accreditation was renewed in 2022. This accreditation ensures a suitable staff-to-student ratio, the use of current teaching and assessment methods, a valid organizational structure, the presence of policies and procedures, and a high-quality system for the faculty. The scores related to these aspects were determined through questionnaires distributed to faculty staff, students, and accreditation body reports.

Learning and growth perspective

The faculty infrastructure and facilities were adequate to conduct the research projects. The total number of research articles submitted/published in international journals through the eight years was five articles and three presentations at international conferences. Through the eight years, the total number of teaching workshops attended by staff members in the department was 26 workshops. The Scores of this perspective were calculated by counting the number of published research articles, conference presentations, and teaching workshops in relation to the target number in the faculty strategic plan.

Findings from the Balanced Scorecard

Data from the four perspectives were introduced to Balanced Scorecard Pro Software Version 4.7.1.20. The resulting balanced scorecard (Fig. 5) indicated that the overall performance of students’ research projects was 89.38 %. The learning and growth perspective showed the highest performance (95.33 %) followed by the financial perspective (92.5 %) then the internal process perspective (89.55 %) while the customer perspective showed the lowest performance (82.19 %).

Balanced Scorecard for evaluating dental students’ research from the academic year 2013/2014 to 2020/2021.

Discussion

According to the literature, the Balanced Scorecard (BSC) is an effective approach to measure and enhance the performance of medical colleges, hospitals, and healthcare organizations (Sudriman, 2012). Managers in medical colleges need to understand how they can improve the quality of education, healthcare services, and research they provide to their students, patients, and the community at large (Jaber & Nashwan, 2022). This study suggests that the BSC philosophy is highly feasible and flexible in meeting the specific needs of different departments within medical colleges.

To develop an effective BSC in medical education, it is imperative to have suitable performance perspectives and indicators that reflect the organization's strategies and objectives (Teichgräber et al., 2021). At the Dental Public Health department, our vision and mission are directed to develop the research and communication skills of the students through early involvement in research projects and early interaction with patients. Besides, providing community services to raise awareness of oral health, and add a competitive advantage to the faculty graduates (Badran et al, 2022). For that reason, the staff members of the department developed our specific BSC to reflect our strategies in a meaningful, measurable, and time-bound way.

The uniqueness of this study is related to its being the first study to use the BSC to evaluate performance in the dental education field. Also, the inclusion of the quality of teaching, the student’s research projects, and the scientific perspective, which takes into consideration scientific publication output (number, impact factor) could be considered additional KPIs that have not been used widely in HCO. Another crucial aspect was the advancement of datacollection and storage technologies, which maximized the opportunities to utilize faculty data records and retrieve all necessary information. This saved time and effort, and significantly improved the functionality of the BSC.

The results of the designed balanced scorecard revealed that the overall performance of students’ research activities was 86.7 % indicating good performance. All perspectives showed satisfactory performance (over 80 %) which could be attributed to being part of a private university committed to research and community service as part of the university mission. Customer perspective showed the lowest performance which may be due to dependence on questionnaire results in addition to the relatively small number of international publications.

Challenges and limitations

A major challenge that the whole world faced during the past few years was the emergence of the COVID-19 pandemic which had a huge impact on medical students and medical education. The pandemic forced us to change our research plan and shift from face-to-face contact with patients to online teaching. Students enrolled in the Dental Public Health course during the pandemic were deprived of the opportunity to interact with patients and to do the health education part of the research, instead, they designed a structured questionnaire to collect data regarding the impact of the pandemic on dental students’ mental health and learning experience. Despite this crisis, the satisfaction scores were still satisfactory, and this could be attributed to the interest of students in the topic; as they were allowed to choose the topic of the research, and to the preparedness of the faculty and the faculty staff members for the online shift and their efforts to compensate the students for the lack of contact with patients by making use of online educational strategies.

The deficiency of publications attending performance measurement in dental education presented a challenge. However, we overcame this limitation by relying on similar academic work in hospitals and medical colleges.

Finally, our balanced scorecard is specific to a single activity in one institution, so it may not reflect the situation in other departments and/or institutions. Therefore, multicentric studies that evaluate medical and dental colleges in several countries, would be of great value concerning the generalizability of the study findings.

We expect to continue the utilization of our scorecards and to modify and add metrics that can derive performance and motivate the staff members and are linked to the mission and vision of the department and the university.

Conclusions

Based on our experience and findings, the Balanced Scorecard (BSC) is a highly effective tool for collecting and presenting data, and evaluating the performance measurement of students’ research projects. We are confident that our process can be easily replicated by other sections, or institutions seeking to enhance their educational mission and vision.

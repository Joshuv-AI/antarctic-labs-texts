Development and validation of a nomogram for predicting overall and cancer-specific survival in elderly patients (≥ 65 years) with epithelial ovarian cancer
Authors: Mingzi Tan, Liancheng Zhu, Jian Gao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCurrent evidence indicates an uptick in both morbidity and mortality rates of epithelial ovarian cancer (EOC) among the elderly (65 year and older) over the past few years. To date, standardized treatment for elderly patients remains undeveloped. This study utilizes the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) database to extract relevant clinicopathological data and construct two nomograms aimed at predicting the prognosis of elderly (65 year and older) patients with EOC. This objective is intended to assist clinicians during clinical decision-making and to assist in individualized prognostication and support clinical decision-making of elderly (65 year and older) EOC patient.MethodsOur analysis screened a total of 22,181 eligible patients, randomly divided into a training cohort (n = 15,529) and validation cohort (n = 6652) at a ratio of 7:3. 64 cases over 65 year old EOC patient were collected for external validation in our hospital. COX and LASSO analyses were used to screen the independent risk factors for overall survival (OS) and cancer-specific survival (CSS) in elderly patients with EOC. The independent risk factors were used to establish a nomogram by using the “rms” package. The predictive and clinical utility of nomograms was assessed using concordance index, area under the curve (AUC), calibration curve, decision curve analysis and external validation. Kaplan–Meier analysis was conducted to further stratify OS and CSS in high and low-risk groups, assessing the nomograms’ stratification efficacy.ResultsThe AUCs of the training and validation cohort for OS and CSS prediction at 0.5, 1, 3, 5, and 10 years were significantly higher than the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging system (8th edition). Time-dependent AUC analysis from 1 to 10 years confirmed the nomograms’ predictive superiority over the AJCC staging system for both OS and CSS in the training and validation cohorts. Compared with the age, AJCC staging system, the DCA curves of the nomogram showed a greater net gain in the training and external validation cohorts. In the external validation group, C-index of nomogram was 0.938 [95% CI 0.888–0.988], which was significantly better than that of stage (0.762) [95% CI 0.693–0.832] and the results showed that the AUC of Nomogram was significantly higher than that of stage at 1, 3, and 5-year OS and CSS. KM analysis showed that the prognosis of the low-risk group was significantly higher than that of the high-risk group. The developed nomograms outperformed the AJCC staging system in predicting both OS and CSS in elderly (65 year and older) EOC patient.ConclusionsThe developed nomograms offer an effective method for predicting the OS and CSS of elderly ovarian cancer patients, aiding clinicians in making personalized survival projections and refining treatment recommendations.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40001-025-03114-0.

Introduction

Globally, ovarian cancer (OC) is the third most common gynecological malignancy but still ranks first among gynecological malignancies in mortality rate [1]. Importantly, up to 80% of OC patients are already at an advanced stage at diagnosis, Epithelial ovarian cancer (EOC) accounts for 80% of OC [2]. It is widely acknowledged that most ovarian cancer cases occur at the age of 40–70 years, and the incidence increases with age. Notably, the proportion of patients over 65 years has increased annually [3], with the median age of diagnosis in Europe reported to be 63 years [4]. The care of older oncology patients presents significant challenges. Currently, the cornerstone of epithelial ovarian cancer (EOC) treatment encompasses cytoreductive surgery (R0 surgery) and a regimen of platinum-based chemotherapy combined with paclitaxel [3]. However, elderly patients with EOC have been associated with poor nutritional status, poor tolerance to chemotherapy, and a greater number of comorbidities and postoperative complications, limiting their candidacy for surgical interventions. Elderly EOC patients are often unable to derive benefit from standard chemotherapy, accounting for the relatively high recurrence and mortality rates [4, 5]. One study highlighted that merely 45% of patients over the age of 70 received standard treatment [6]. Importantly, projections for the next decade estimate a near doubling in the population aged 65–74 years. [7]. With the emerge of robotic surgery, neoadjuvant chemotherapy, and new targeted therapies, significant improvements in overall survival (OS) remain elusive, underscoring the pressing need for refined prognostic models to personalize treatment strategies.

Nomograms provide an objective visual representation of data and can be used as a simple graphical prediction tool to calculate the probability of clinical event risk for each patient from a mathematical point of view [8], surpassing the predictive accuracy of tumor node metastasis classification (TNM) staging developed by AJCC [9, 10]. It has also been shown that nomograms can assist in guiding diagnosis and treatment plans and making appropriate clinical decisions by predicting prognosis [11, 12]. In 2018, the American Society of Clinical Oncology (ASCO) highlighted the necessity of employing geriatric assessments (GA) for patients over 65 undergoing chemotherapies, as outlined in their “Practical Assessment and Management of Vulnerability in Older Patients Receiving Chemotherapy” guidelines [13]. However, clinical studies on the use of GA in elderly patients with EOC are rarely reported, especially within the Chinese demographic. Furthermore, standardized treatment guidelines specifically for older patients (aged 65 and above) with EOC are notably scarce.

Therefore, this study aims to construct a nomogram to predict the prognosis of elderly patients with EOC based on clinicopathological data parameters extracted from the SEER database. This tool is designed to help clinicians make better clinical treatment plans and to assist in individualized prognostication and support clinical decision-making.

Materials and methods

Data source and extraction

The Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) database aims to collect information about cancer characteristics, cancer incidence, and results. Data from the SEER (https://seer.cancer.gov/seerstat/) database were used to identify women aged 65 years and elder diagnosed with epithelial ovarian cancer (EOC) between 2010 and 2016. Histopathological types were classified according to The International Classification of Diseases for Oncology, Third Edition (ICD-O-3) codes (Same as our previous study [14, 15]), include Serous, Mucinous, Endometrioid, Clear cell, Transitional cell, and Epithelial stromal. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), the elderly are defined with a chronological age of 65 years and elder [16]. In our study, the relevant data of patients over 65 years of age were further screened and aggregated according to age for retrospective analysis, using SEER*Stat software version 8.3.6 (account ID: 19731-Nov2019). The clinical case information of patients 65 years and elder diagnosed with EOC who were treated in our 2 hospitals from 2012 to 2024 was collected and follow-up, and a total of 111 cases were collected for external validation. No experiments were performed on humans, animals, or human tissue samples in this study. This study has been approved by the Ethics Committee/Ethical Review Board of Liaoning Cancer Hospital & Institute and Shengjing Hospital affiliated to China Medical University, with approval number No. 20210315G and 2021PS189K. The inclusion and exclusion criteria for extracting and screening data from the SEER database were as follows: Inclusion criteria: (1) Diagnosis of EOC established by primary malignant tumor biopsy or postoperative pathology; (2) Availability of clinical data; (3) Aged 65 years or older; (4) Complete tumor staging or debulking surgery with detailed surgical records (e.g., including whether regional lymph node dissection was performed, tumor size, and residual tumor size).

The exclusion criteria: (1) Coexistence of another primary tumor or other serious life-threatening diseases; (2) No specific treatment plan; (3) Lack of information on follow-up and survival status and other information; (4) Aged below 65.

The variables analyzed in this study included the following: age, marital status, education level, tumor size, insurance status, lymph nodes (LN) examined number, LN positive number, grade, American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) stage, histopathological type, initial surgical plan, regional lymph node removal or not, tumor marker level (CA125), regional LN Surgery, residual tumor size, radiotherapy, chemotherapy or not, organ metastasis (including lung, liver, bone, brain), tumor-specific survival time (CSS), and overall survival time (OS). The beginning of follow-up was defined as the time beginning after the initial operation, the end-point was cancer-specific death, and the end date of the follow-up was December 31, 2016. We screened a total of 22,181 eligible patients randomly assigned to a training (n = 15,529) and validation (n = 6652) cohorts at a 7:3 ratio. We use the “caret” package to perform random sampling within each category while preserving the overall class distribution of the data to avoid bias in the distribution of the two groups.

End-point and variables description

The end-points of this study included OS and CSS. Clinicopathological features and the associated stratification for inclusion in the study sites were as follows: Year of disease diagnosis:1988–2000; 2001–2010; 2011–2016. Statistical analysis

Each variable was divided into frequency data and proportion data. Clinicopathological characteristics were compared between training and validation cohorts using the chi-square test. Parameters with P < 0.05 during univariate. Least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) method were selected to screen clinicopathological features closely related to prognosis, based on the type measure of -2log-likelihood and binomial family. In R software, LASSO-Cox regression analysis is performed via the glmnet package to screen for prognostically related variables. Specifically, tenfold cross-validation is used to determine the optimal regularization parameter λ. The process generates 100 λ candidate values by default, calculates the partial likelihood deviance corresponding to each λ through cross-validation, and finally selects the λ (i.e., lambda.min) with the smallest cross-validation error. To verify the stability of the model, we repeat the cross-validation 100 times, taking the average λ value as the final parameter. [17]. Multivariate Cox regression analysis further removed confounders, identified important prognostic factors, and estimated hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Based on multivariate Cox regression analysis results, a nomogram model was constructed using the rms package in R software. The performance of the nomogram was internally validated in the training cohort and externally in the validation cohort. Concordance index (C-index) and time-dependent area under the curve (AUC) were used to evaluate the discriminative ability of the nomogram, and a calibration curve was generated to test the agreement between predicted OS, cause-specific survival (CSS) and actual OS, CSS. Decision curve analysis (DCA) was used to assess the clinical validity and benefit of our model. The nomogram was used to calculate the total score of all patients. Based on the best cut-off value determined by X-tile (version 3.6.1, Yale University, New Haven, Connecticut, USA), patients were divided into a high-risk and a low-risk group. Kaplan–Meier method was used for survival analysis to evaluate the discriminative ability of our nomogram for prognosis. Marginal estimates were used to establish a calibration plot of the nomogram representing the calibration between the predicted and observed survival. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS (version 25.0, SPSS, Chicago, IL, USA) and R software (version 3.6.0, http://www.r-project.org/). A P < 0.05 was statistically significant. Results

Clinical and pathological characteristics and survival analysis

A total of 22,181 patients who met the inclusion criteria were screened in our study. Analysis of patients showed that EOC incidence in the elderly (≥ 65 years old) exhibited an increasing trend, with the highest incidence observed in the 65–79 years age group, comprising 81.3% of all elderly ovarian cancer patients (Supplementary Figure S1). Only 45% of patients had health insurance coverage, and only 42.3% of elderly patients were positive for CA125. Most elderly ovarian cancer patients presented with advanced-stage disease (stage III–IV accounting for 71.7%) alongside high tumor grade, with stage III–IV tumor accounting for 72.6%. Serous tumors accounted for a large proportion (71.9%). However, only 40% of patients underwent complete cytoreduction in their initial surgery, and complete lymph node dissection was conducted in merely 20% of patients who had a lymph node dissection. Post-surgery, 4% of patients were found to have residual disease with a tumor size less than 1 cm. Chemotherapy was administered to 69.3% of the patients (Table 1). Factors influencing prognosis in elderly EOC patients were identified in the training cohort using a Cox regression model. Univariate analysis revealed statistically significant differences in overall survival (OS) for 21 parameters, including stage, lymph node-positive rate, site of primary surgery, age, residual lesion size, CA125 level, grade, tumor size, pathological type, and chemotherapy (P < 0.05). Further screening was performed by LASSO analysis (P < 0.01), yielding 18 factors related to OS (Fig. 2b). Further multivariate Cox analysis (P < 0.01) showed that 12 parameters, including stage, pathological type, age, size of residual lesions, and chemotherapy, were independent prognostic risk factors for elderly patients with ovarian cancer (Table 2). Similarly, 21 items such as age, tumor size, lymph node-positive rate, grade, stage, pathological type, CA125, residual lesion size, and chemotherapy were screened out by univariate analysis to identify risk factors associated with CSS. LASSO analysis narrowed age, residual lesion size, tumor size, grade, stage, pathological type, chemotherapy, and 17 other factors closely related to CSS. Multivariate COX analysis further removed confounding factors and found that 12 parameters, such as stage, pathological type, age, size of residual disease, and whether or not chemotherapy, were closely associated with CSS (Fig. 2c). Nomogram construction and validation

Construction nomogram

Independent prognostic factors identified by multivariate Cox analysis were used to build the nomogram model to predict 3-, 5-, and 10-year OS and CSS in elderly patients with EOC. The scores of each independent prognostic factor in the nomogram were summed up to obtain a total score projected to OS and CSS at 3, 5, and 10 years. A high total score indicated a poorer prognosis in elderly patients with ovarian cancer. The OS nomogram model showed that among the 12 prognostic-related factors screened, the tumor stage had the greatest impact on prognosis. Independent risk factors that affected the prognosis were pathological type, age, whether receiving chemotherapy, size of residual lesions, and the number of positive lymph nodes. Conversely, factors like the number of lymph node biopsies, tumor grade, tumor laterality, CA125 level, tumor size, and site of the primary surgery were found to have a minimal impact on OS prognosis. The CSS nomogram model indicated that among the 12 prognostic factors screened out, tumor stage had the greatest impact on prognosis, followed by pathological type, age, residual disease, chemotherapy, tumor grade, positive rate of lymph nodes, number of lymph node biopsies. The location of primary surgery, tumor size, tumor laterality, and the positive rate of tumor marker CA125 had the least impact on prognosis (Fig. 3).Fig. 3The nomograms for predicting OS and CSS in elderly (> 65 years old) patients with OC at 3, 5, and 10 years, (A) the nomogram for OS prediction in OC patients; (B) the nomogram for CSS prediction in OC patients

Comparison of C-indexes

The AUC analysis for the training and validation cohorts further substantiated the nomogram’s superiority (Figs. 4a and 5a). The AUCs of the training cohort for OS prediction at 0.5, 1, 3, 5, and 10 years were significantly higher than the AJCC staging system (0.789, 0.765, 0.749, 0.766, and 0.781 vs. 0.633, 0.640, 0.676, 0.714, and 0.739). In the validation cohort, the AUCs for OS prediction at 0.5, 1, 3, 5, and 10 years were significantly higher than the AJCC staging system (0.776, 0.765, 0.740, 0.757, and 0.776, vs. 0.646, 0.640, 0.671, 0.703, and 0.737). Besides, the AUCs for predicting CSS in the training cohort at 0.5, 1, 3, 5, and 10 years were significantly higher than the AJCC staging system (0.784, 0.760, 0.741, 0.764, and 0.803 vs. 0.625, 0.632, 0.667, 0.708, and 0.766). Finally, the AUCs to predict CSS in the validation cohort at 0.5, 1, 3, 5, 10 years were significantly higher than the AJCC staging system (0.783, 0.751, 0.743, 0.768, and 0.801 vs. 0.646, 0.633, 0.662, 0.701, and 0.761) (Table 4). Time-dependent AUC analysis from 1 to 10 years confirmed the nomograms’ predictive superiority over the AJCC staging system for both OS and CSS in the training and validation cohorts (Figs. 4c, d, and 5c, d). Indeed, in contrast to the AJCC staging system, which is limited to considering the primary tumor site, lymph node involvement, and the presence of distant metastases, our prognostic nomogram for OS and CSS incorporates a comprehensive set of 12 independent risk factors. Despite the inclusion of multiple variables, which introduces a broader range of potential confounding factors compared to the AJCC system, our nomogram demonstrates superior predictive accuracy. Calibration plots at 1, 3, 5, and 10 showed good agreement between OS/CSS nomogram predictions and actual observations in training and external validation cohorts (Fig. 6). Risk stratification in elderly (≥ 65 years old) patients with EOC

A risk score for each variable was generated from the nomogram, and an overall score was calculated for all patients. Utilizing X-tile, cut-off values were established, effectively dividing the entire cohort into low-risk and high-risk subgroups based on the median risk score. KM survival analysis conducted between these groups (Fig. 8) revealed significant differences in both the training and validation cohorts for OS and CSS (P < 0.001 for both), indicating the nomograms’ exceptional ability for risk stratification. Discussion

As the world’s older population continues to increase at an unprecedented pace, the proportion of elderly ovarian cancer patients has gradually increased. Accordingly, much emphasis has been placed on diagnosing and treating elderly ovarian cancer patients in recent years. It is widely acknowledged that elderly patients typically present with a greater number of comorbidities, have relatively poorer nutritional status compared to younger individuals, and are at an elevated risk for complications during treatment. Both prospective and retrospective studies have highlighted that age is associated with an increase in treatment-related toxic effects, reductions in survival rates, diminished likelihood of receiving chemotherapy and/or surgery, and lower participation rates in clinical trials [18]. The fact that most clinical trials have excluded elderly patients over 70 years old combined with other reasons account for current treatment difficulties faced by this particular patient group [7]. In response to these challenges, the present study utilized the SEER database to extract pertinent clinicopathological data and construct a nomogram. This tool is designed to predict the prognosis both OS and CSS of elderly EOC patients.

The clinicopathological factors associated with prognosis were screened to establish a nomogram that could predict 3-, 5-, and 10-year OS and CSS in elderly ovarian cancer patients. The C-index and calibration curve for both the training and validation cohorts demonstrated high consistency between the predicted outcomes by the nomograms and the actual patient outcomes, indicating the nomograms’ excellent discriminative ability. The ROC curve analysis further established that the nomograms outperformed the AJCC staging system, pathological tumor type, and age in predictive accuracy. Moreover, DCA curve analysis corroborated that the predictive performance of the nomogram was better than AJCC staging. Importantly, risk scores based on our nomograms effectively stratified patients into distinct high and low-risk groups.

We screened out 12 independent risk factors closely related to OS and CSS, respectively, in elderly patients with ovarian cancer. Factors significantly associated with OS and CSS included AJCC stage, pathological type, residual disease, age, chemotherapy, lymph node dissection, and positive rate. Examples show how patients use nomograms to calculate patient outcomes with given these factors (Supplementary Figure S3).

AJCC staging

AJCC staging is widely acknowledged as the traditional pathological staging method for classifying ovarian cancer. This system accurately delineates the extent of tumor involvement in patients, serving as a critical foundation for prognostic assessment. In this study, the nomogram consistently identified AJCC staging as pivotal factor influencing the prognosis of elderly ovarian cancer patients. A higher AJCC stage was associated with a poorer prognosis among elderly ovarian cancer patients. In the present study, approximately 70% of elderly patients was classified as AJCC stages III–IV, significantly exceeding the roughly 50% incidence reported among young patients reported in previous literature [15]. The diminished detection rate of ovarian cancer in elderly patients could be attributed to its subtle onset and the lack of effective early detection methods coupled with social factors, including the relatively low frequency of gynecological health screenings in elderly patients compared to younger patients [19].

Chemotherapy

Our findings indicate that older patients that receiving chemotherapy experienced markedly improved prognosis compared to those who did not undergo such treatment. However, substantial evidence suggests a lower tolerance for chemotherapy. A study reported that most elderly ovarian cancer patients were not administered standard-dose combination chemotherapy, while some elderly ovarian cancer patients received only carboplatin single-agent chemotherapy. Additionally, bevacizumab was part of the first-line regimen in 18.9% of younger patients, compared with 7.8% in older ovarian cancer patients [20]. Moreover, among those on single-agent chemotherapy, only 54% of elderly ovarian cancer patients could complete four full-dose single-agent carboplatin chemotherapy cycles. The median survival time of elderly ovarian cancer patients who received less than 4 cycles of chemotherapy was significantly shorter than that of elderly ovarian cancer patients who completed 5–6 cycles of chemotherapy [21]. Current evidence suggests that chemotherapy toxicity in elderly patients is manageable [22]. It has been reported that a considerable number of elderly patients can tolerate adverse reactions caused by standard chemotherapy regimens [23]. Accordingly, age alone should not preclude the use of chemotherapy, and effective treatment should be prioritized [24]. A study evaluating the safety and effectiveness of bevacizumab combined with first-line carboplatin and paclitaxel chemotherapy in patients with FIGO stage IV ovarian cancer aged 70 years and older revealed that overall health scores and small nutritional assessment scores for nutritional status improved in older adults on eight health screenings. There was a slight improvement in the overall health status score for the condition screening and the small nutritional assessment score for nutritional status. The median change in the Mobility Fatigue test scores, which evaluate independence, daily living activities, and self-reported fatigue, was nearly zero. These findings suggest that the combination of bevacizumab, carboplatin, and paclitaxel is both safe and effective for FIGO stage IV ovarian cancer in patients 70 years of age and older [25]. Therefore, our research found combination chemotherapy with an active full course of treatment is beneficial to improve the prognosis of elderly patients [26]. As a treatment modality, intraperitoneal hyperthermic perfusion chemotherapy has also played a positive role in improving the prognosis for elderly patients with ovarian cancer. Current evidence suggests that combining primary tumor cytoreduction surgery with intraperitoneal hyperthermic perfusion therapy is effective for elderly ovarian cancer patients over 65 years old, yielding OS rates similar to those in patients younger than 65 years old. Interestingly, patients over 65 undergoing neoadjuvant chemotherapy followed by cytoreductive surgery and intraperitoneal hyperthermic chemotherapy exhibited significantly longer OS time than patients under 65 years old [27]. These findings highlight the importance of selecting an optimal treatment approach to improve the prognosis of elderly ovarian patients. In addition, it has been reported that as a classic ovarian cancer chemotherapy drug, carboplatin dosage is closely related to glomerular filtration rate and age. The Calvert formula can be used to calculate the dosage [28]. However, considering the disparity between chronological and biological age in elderly patients due to increased life expectancy [24], the glomerular filtration rate in elderly patients is difficult to estimate. The actual status of glomerular filtration rate in elderly patients is unclear. Further studies are required to assess whether the dose of carboplatin in elderly patients with ovarian cancer should be increased.

Surgical Outcomes Elderly patients typically exhibit a poorer physical health status compared to their younger counterparts. Notably, while most young patients initially undergo primary cytoreductive surgery, older patients more frequently receive intermediate cytoreductive surgery, and many do not undergo surgery after neoadjuvant chemotherapy [21]. Our study found that the OS of elderly patients with ovarian cancer significantly improved even with residual lesions less than 1 cm. It has been suggested that elderly patients can achieve similar OS benefits from comprehensive tumor debulking surgery as young women. The presence of residual lesions notably affects the OS rate of elderly ovarian cancer patients [29]. Since older patients who receive the same treatment regimen have similar perioperative adverse event rates to younger patients, age should not preclude ultra-radical resection in older patients with advanced ovarian cancer after NACT. Moreover, age does not significantly influence hospitalization costs for ovarian cancer patients [30]. Elderly patients should have the ability to select a treatment plan according to their own preferences. Therefore, it is crucial to engage elderly patients in the decision-making process, allowing them to choose treatment options based on their preferences and health status, after thorough discussions with healthcare providers and family members, as these decisions directly impact their prognosis.

The role of lymphadenectomy in ovarian cancer surgery is contentious. It is widely believed that advanced ovarian cancer patients without significant lymph node metastases may not benefit from systematic lymph node dissection. However, the aggressive removal of macroscopic retroperitoneal lymph nodes is advocated, given the strong correlation between residual disease and patient prognosis. [31]. In contrast, another study substantiated that systematic lymph node dissection is beneficial to the survival of patients with early-stage ovarian clear cell carcinoma [32]. Our study observed a better prognosis in elderly ovarian cancer patients with adequate lymph node dissection and node-negative disease, which may be accounted for by the fact that lymph node metastasis is a component of the FIGO staging. Indeed, given that patients with FIGO stage III–IV generally face poorer outcomes, comprehensive lymph node dissection, as a facet of tumor debulking, appears to enhance survival prospects for elderly ovarian cancer patients.

To summarize, relevant clinicopathological data were extracted from the SEER database to establish a nomogram to predict the prognosis of elderly ovarian cancer patients. Despite its contributions, our study faced several limitations. The stratification of some clinicopathologic data was inadequate. As a kind of retrospective analysis, it lacked comprehensive imaging and hematology test data, including recent predictors, such as tumor marker HE4, PET-CT image systemic evaluation results, ascites testing, genetic test results, and immune status. Moreover, most clinical trials conducted excluded patients older than 70 years [27]. With the emergence of immune [33] and targeted [34] therapies as promising treatment avenues for ovarian cancer, clinical trials now encompass a broader age range, offering new hope for first-line treatments in elderly ovarian cancer patients. These findings highlight the need for more prospective, randomized clinical trials to further define the optimal treatment regimen for elderly patients with ovarian cancer.

Conclusion

In this study, we extracted the relevant clinicopathological information from the SEER database to establish a nomogram to predict the prognosis (OS and CSS) of elderly ovarian cancer patients and provide a basis for individualized precision treatment of elderly ovarian cancer patients. The clinicopathological factors associated with prognosis were screened to establish a nomogram that could predict 3-, 5-, and 10-year OS and CSS in elderly ovarian cancer patients. The nomogram established yielded a better predictive value than the AJCC staging system, pathological tumor type, and age. Importantly, risk scores based on our nomograms exhibited a good ability to stratify patients into high- and low-risk groups.

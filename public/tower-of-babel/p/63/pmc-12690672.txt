SBPS-09 GIANT MENINGIOMA OF THE SKULL BASE: REPORT OF A CASE WITH COMPLETE RESECTION
Authors: Raïssa Diaby, Wilfried Dossou, Michum Kounde, Grâce Djondé, Wilfried Abo, Jacques Karidioula, Médard Kakou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground- AimOlfactory groove meningiomas represent a rare entity among anterior skull base tumors. The often-substantial dimensions of these tumors present a surgical challenge, particularly in cases of giant meningioma, where the mass effect on the optic and frontal structures is considerable. Historically, extensive frontal approaches (bifrontal or sub-frontal) were preferred, yet the development of techniques towards minimally invasive methods (such as the brow approach) has reduced morbidity while maintaining satisfactory resection rates. In complex cases, combined approaches can be considered. The objective of this study is to present a case of a giant olfactory groove meningioma that was successfully excised using the brow approach, emphasizing the clinical, radiological, technical, and progression aspects.Case PresentationWe report the case of a 45-year-old female patient, undergoing monitoring for hypothyroidism while on Levothyrox therapy, who presented with persistent headaches associated with progressively deteriorating vision. Brain MRI revealed a giant meningioma located at the olfactory sulcus, with an estimated volume of 177 cm3, exerting pressure on the frontal lobes and compressing the visual pathways. A surgical resection was conducted via a minimally invasive technique, utilizing the proper brow ridge approach. Removal was macroscopically complete, classified as Simpson grade I, verified by immediate postoperative MRI and confirmed by the M3 MRI. Anatomopathological examination revealed a grade I meningothelial meningioma. The postoperative course was marked by a transient frontal lobe syndrome (manifesting as disinhibition and apathy), which resolved with corticosteroid and neuroleptic treatment, with satisfactory neurological recovery, as evidenced by neuropsychological assessment performed at 3 and 6 months.ConclusionThis case illustrates the value of an eyebrow approach in the management of giant anterior basal meningiomas, allowing complete excision with limited morbidity. However, rigorous case selection remains essential given the anatomical complexity of these lesions.

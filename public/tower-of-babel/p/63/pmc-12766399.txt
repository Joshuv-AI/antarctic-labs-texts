Protocol for specific induction of apoptosis or necroptosis in established murine tumors
Authors: Kay Hänggi, Brian Ruffell
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryWe present a protocol to study cancer cell death in vivo using mouse tumor models by generating cell lines that allow for the induction of “pure” apoptosis or necroptosis via an inducible dimerizer system. We describe steps for lentiviral transduction and flow cytometry-based optimization of the death induction schedule. We then detail optional procedures for “intradermal vaccination” and for tracking antigen-specific CD8+ T cell responses.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Hänggi et al.1Subject areas: Cancer, Immunology, Model Organisms

Before you begin

Inducing cell death in cancer cells is the primary goal of most therapies, but how different forms of cell death regulate the immune response and can be rationally combined with immunotherapies remains poorly understood. The extensive crosstalk between cell death pathways, cytotoxic treatments (e.g. chemotherapy) or inflammatory environments (e.g. cytokines like TNFα) often lead to a mixed population of cell death pathways being executed, making it difficult to interpret the consequences of a particular death modality on the immune response.

We developed a protocol using the ‘dimerizer inducible’ cell death execution system, routinely used in vitro,2,3 allowing the induction of either apoptosis or necroptosis in tumors. The apoptosis construct used here utilizes a caspase 8 fusion protein in which the endogenous dimerization domain of caspase 8 is replaced with a DmrB domain, allowing inducible dimerization with the BB Homodimerizer compound. The necroptosis construct similarly uses a DmrB-RIPK3 fusion protein for inducible RIPK3 necrosome formation, leading to RIPK3 to phosphorylate MLKL. Alternatively, necroptosis can be induced by expression of a phospho-mimetic version of MLKL (MLKLS345D), listed in the key resource table but not described in the protocol.1 Note that the constructs used induce apoptosis or necroptosis in the absence of upstream receptor signaling induction.

The protocol described herein is based upon the PyMT-B6 orthotopic tumor model, but the same approach has also been successfully applied in the subcutaneous MC38 and LLC1 tumor models.1 Other groups have used the dimerizing system and/or similar strategies to induce death in NIH3T3, CT26, B16-F10, and LLC1.2,4,5,6 It is expected that the system can be applied to a wide range of cell types and tumor models provided that the downstream effector molecules are present in the cell of interest. However, as outlined in the protocol, verification is recommended to ensure the correct form of cell death is being induced.

Innovation

Multiple forms of death may occur in a population of cells, either spontaneously or during response to therapy, and blocking one form of death may lead to the preferential occurrence of another type, making cause and effect linkages difficult to establish. Therefore, the ability to induce pure forms of cancer cell death in a time and dose dependent manner in vivo adds a valuable tool for studying the potential outcomes of cell death, particularly as it relates to changes in the immune response. While dimerization systems have been widely used for inducing cell death in in vitro settings, this protocol provides a practical and reproducible method to induce apoptosis or necroptosis in established murine tumors.

Lentivirus production

Timing: ∼4 days

This protocol has been optimized for high viral titers using the cost-effective polyethyleneimine (PEI) transfection method with the LentiX-293T cell line from Takara. The supernatant generated were of sufficient titer for the experiments outlined using the PyMT-B6 cell line.

Note: Lentiviral production also works with regular HEK293T cells, but using the Lenti-X 293T subclone of Takara has resulted in reliably good viral titers.

1.Seed Lenti-X 293T cells into a 10 cm tissue culture-treated dish in a total volume of 8 ml complete DMEM that they reach 80% confluency (see Figure 1) the next day (20–24 hrs).

Lentiviral transduction

Timing: ∼9–11 days

The following steps describe a lentiviral transduction protocol with ‘spinfection’.

CRITICAL: To establish cell lines with multiple constructs, perform repeated single transductions rather than simultaneous co-transduction. First establish a stable parental cell line that expresses Tet3G. The cell line should then be tested for good response to dox-dependent induction of gene expression using the pLVX-TRE3G-Luc vector supplied together with the pLVX-Tet3G vector from Takara (follow manufacturer’s instructions). These cells can then be used to transduce the cell death or fluorescent protein constructs (ZsGreen). It is recommended that the ZsGreen fluorescent cells are generated first in order to allow for a better comparison between different cell death modalities. Once established, keep the number of cell passages as low as possible.

Note: Depending on your cell type of interest, either ‘spinfection’ alone or incubation without centrifugation will allow successful transduction with potential lower transduction efficiencies. Regardless, the ability of cell types to allow lentiviral transduction can vary largely and therefore may require concentration of lentivirus as outlined in the troubleshooting section.

Note: Bring up cells of interest in time to be ready for infection with fresh lentivirus. These protocols have been used for the MC38, LLC, and PyMT-B6 murine tumor cell lines. Other cell lines have not been tested but are expected to work similarly.

In vitro validation of cell death constructs

Timing: ∼2 days

The following experiments are designed to optimize the treatment schedule for doxycycline and BB Homodimerizer addition, and verify cell death modality for subsequent in vivo studies. This is important to maximize specific cell death induction at equal levels between CASP8 and RIPK3 cells. The RIPK3ΔC truncation mutant and the phosphomimetic MLKL (MLKLS345D) can also be used to induce necroptosis, as described.1

CRITICAL: Maximal cell death induction is achieved when confluency is 70%–80%. Higher levels of confluency will diminish the amount of cell death observed.

CRITICAL: The cell lines need to be optimized for equal total cell death induction at the highest dose in vitro (doxycycline at 1 μg/ml and BB at 0.5 μM) at endpoint due to the difficulty to control the dosing of doxycycline chow for in vivo studies.

CRITICAL: Comparable mCherry expression after doxycycline treatment will not necessarily normalize the amount of cell death achieved with the different constructs. Cell death should be evaluated empirically 20–28 h after induction by measuring the remaining number of live cells. This is recommended over calculating the percentage of dead cells as cellular fragmentation may artificially increase the amount of measured dead cells.

Expected outcomes

This protocol has been developed to induce either apoptosis or necroptosis at a timepoint of interest in established tumors. We have successfully applied this protocol and created ‘pure’ cell death cell lines to induce apoptosis (CASP8) or necroptosis using different constructs (RIPK3, RIPK3ΔC, MLKL S345D) in the PyMT-B6, LLC and MC38 tumor models. From our in vitro and in vivo studies, we found that once BB Homodimerizer was administered, necroptosis and loss of membrane integrity in RIPK3 cells occurred within 60–180 min, while CASP8 required 12–18 hrs. It is expected to work similarly in other tumor models and could potentially be expanded to other cell death pathways of interest in which this induction/dimerizing system could be employed.

The bi-cistronic cell death construct with mCherry allows monitoring of doxycycline induced expression of the cell death construct by flow cytometry and microscopy. Upon BB Homodimerizer treatment cells that express mCherry are quickly lost. Combining this with constitutively expressed cytosolic ZsGreen allows identification of live tumor cells and tumor cell-derived material in other cell types. We found high levels of ZsGreen signal in F4/80+ macrophages, sometimes exceeding that in tumor cells, or detected ZsGreen in CD103+ dendritic cells that migrated to tumor draining lymph nodes.8 When using the OVA-ZsGreen construct instead, OVA-specific CD8+ T cells can be tracked using MHC-I tetramer staining. To mimic different immunogenicity levels in tumor cells we sorted cells with varying levels of OVA-ZsGreen expression resulting in gradual decrease of OVA-specific CD8 T cells along with decreasing OVA-expression and increased tumor growth.1 We also provide further recommendations for in vivo verification by intradermal vaccination of dying cells. Here, in draining lymph nodes of mice vaccinated with necroptotic dying cells showed a significant increase in percentage of OVA-specific CD8 T cells compared to apoptotic cells as repeatedly demonstrated in different tumor models.1,2,3 As the OVA construct contains epitopes for both MHC-I and MHC-II presentation, this can be expanded to track OVA-specific CD4+ T cells responses.

The constructs introduced tend to increase basal immunogenicity of tumor cells as we observed reduced tumor growth compared to the parental PyMT-B6 cell line. Under some circumstances we have observed rejection phenotypes.1 Most importantly, verify that no growth differences are observed between cell lines in the absence of cell death induction. In the PyMT-B6 model, necroptosis upon intra-dermal vaccination of dying cells induced T cell responses, while necroptosis in established tumors suppressed T cell responses and promoted tumor growth. Other studies using different approaches and/or other tumor models (e.g. CT26, B16F10) found immunogenic and tumor inhibiting properties of necroptosis.2,3,4,6 Therefore, how necroptosis and apoptosis affect tumor growth is highly context dependent and should be evaluated for each tumor model.

Limitations

This protocol is limited by the artificial nature of inducing cell death using genetic constructs and the potentially high level of cell death that can be achieved in established tumors. This does not necessarily reflect a physiological setting. Furthermore, the foreign recombinant proteins that are being expressed (DmrB, Tet3G, mCherry, ZsGreen) increase basal antigenicity of the tumor cells.

It is also difficult to accurately measure cell death in vivo is due to the quick clearance of dead cells by phagocytes and the lack of reliable antibodies to detect phosphorylated MLKL in tissues as a marker of necroptosis. Therefore, in vivo validation relies on immunoblotting or indirect measures by flow cytometry/microscopy, such as loss of membrane permeability in the absence of apoptosis markers (e.g. cleaved caspase 3).

Finally, controlling the levels of cell death in vivo by adjusting BB Homodimerizer administration is feasible, but may prove technically challenging to due variation in the experimental system.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

The Lenti-X 293T cells do not distribute well and have large differences in confluency within the plate when observed though a bright field microscope (step 1).

Potential solution

As incubator shelves can be slightly tilted and/or vibrate due to repeated opening of doors or due to the internal fan, which can result in unequal distribution of cells such. This issue can be reduced by carefully transferring freshly seeded 10 cm plates onto the non-vibrating bench for 30 min before transferring carefully into the incubator. If Lenti-X 293T cells occur to grow in patches, the cells were not trypsinized and/or resuspended properly. This can also be solved by passing the cell suspension through a 70 μm cell strainer before seeding.

Problem 2

Poor transduction efficiency of constructs, e.g. less than 30% of ZsGreen+ or mCherry+ (upon doxycycline) cells after transduction at highest MOI (step 9–28).

Potential solution

The cells of interest are either hard to infect or the viral titers were poor. If all steps have been performed according to this protocol 80%–90% transduction efficiency is expected when using PyMT-B6 cells even without Polybrene. HEK293T, similar to PyMT cells, are considered easy-to-infect and can be used as a reference. While using this protocol with other in house 293T cell lines worked to produce lentivirus, the Lenti-X 293T cell line from Takara produced the best viral titers among different constructs. Polybrene addition or concentrating viral supernatant by ultra-centrifugation can help to significantly improve transduction efficiency. Perform ultra-centrifugation for 2 hrs at 24,000 x g at 4°C to concentrate.

Problem 3

The percentage of viable cells after cell death induction with the difference constructs varies considerably (step 29–39).

Potential solution

For a fair side-by-side comparison it is crucial that the level of cell death between apoptosis and necroptosis is equivalent. The reason we initially infect at different MOI’s as in step 8 is to have cells with equal cell death induction already generated (due to difference in viral titer and/or ability of the construct to induce death). If none of the different cell lines from CASP8 match the death induction to that of RIPK3, generate cell death inducing cells again and adjust the MOI accordingly. If transduction efficiency needs to be improved refer to the troubleshooting section (problem 2). When generating cell lines that will be compared side-by-side, whether in vitro or in vivo, it is recommended to generate both cell lines simultaneously in parallel.

Problem 4

Upon titration of doxycycline in the upper range of >0.5 μg/ml the levels of mCherry quickly drop after induction (step 29–39).

Potential solution

Expression of Tet3G may be excessive, resulting in poor induction. Therefore, when Tet3G cells are generated test doxycycline response using pLVX-TRE3G-luc reporter vector and follow manufacturer’s recommendations.

Problem 5

RIPK3 but not CASP8 cells die upon prolonged doxycycline exposure in the absence of BB Homodimerizer treatment (step 29–39).

Potential solution

This is expected as RIPK3 overexpression can cause dimerization and formation of the necrosome (RIPK1/RIPK3 and RIPK3/RIPK3 dimers) via its RHIM domain. As the RIPK3ΔC variant lacks the RHIM domain, this is not observed. To maximize BB Homodimerizer-induced cell death keep the time between doxycycline chow administration and BB injection as short as possible.

Problem 6

Cells are not dying upon BB Homodimerizer addition despite high expression of cell death constructs, as measured by mCherry (step 29–39).

Potential solution

The BB Homodimerizer quickly loses activity and has a relatively short shelf-life even when stored at −20°C. Please refer to manufacturer’s instructions on handling. For in vivo, ideally use freshly prepared BB Homodimerizer stock solution from lyophilized powder. Once stock solution is prepared in ethanol, store at −20°C in a vial with low-evaporation lid and use within 3–4 months.

Problem 7

BB Homodimerizer precipitates during preparation for in vivo administration (step 80).

Potential solution

When BB Homodimerizer was dissolved in PEG-400 and the addition of ddH2O (+2% Tween) turns the solution cloudy, the BB Homodimerizer has likely precipitated. If observed, add an equal volume (1 ml) of ddH2O (+2% Tween) to the solution to redissolve.

Problem 8

As of experiments performed in step 31d, the RIPK3 cells upon incubation of doxycycline followed by BB Homodimerizer treatment were observed to induce a mix of apoptosis and necroptosis (step 31).

Potential solution

The pan-caspase inhibition should not block significant amounts of cell death RIPK3 cells, otherwise the determined treatment schedule of doxycycline incubation prior to BB Homodimerizer addition can be shortened and reevaluated.

Problem 9

Upon intra-dermal vaccination of dying cells, tumors eventually appear at the site of injection (step 40–70).

Potential solution

As tumor cells are induced to die in vitro and then injected, the remaining live cells may grow out into tumors. For this reason, we performed Mitomycin C treatment to prevent future growth. If this is unsuccessful even after adjusting the concentration of Mitomycin C, sublethal irradiation of cells can be used as an alternative.

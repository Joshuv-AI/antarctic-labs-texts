Risk of minor congenital heart defect in infants conceived via assisted reproductive technology: cohort study from the Copenhagen Baby Heart Study
Authors: F Kyhl‐Svart, J S Jensen, R O B Vøgg, D Vassard, J Forman, H A Boyd, V Hjortdal, A‐S Sillesen, A A Raja, S Dannesbo, A F Mariager, C Torp‐Pedersen, M P Andersen, K Iversen, H Bundgaard, P L Madsen, A Pinborg
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTObjectiveInfertility affects up to 20% of couples in high‐income countries, which has led to an increased use of assisted reproductive technology (ART). While previous studies have linked ART to a higher risk of major congenital heart defects (CHD), less is known about the associated risk of minor CHD. As minor CHD are associated with premature early‐onset cardiac morbidity and mortality, this study aimed to evaluate the risk of minor CHD in singleton infants conceived via ART compared with in those conceived spontaneously.MethodsThe Copenhagen Baby Heart Study (CBHS) is a Danish, prospective, multicenter study of neonates delivered between 1 April 2016 and 31 October 2018. Included in this cohort study based on CBHS data were singleton infants who met the inclusion criteria and had undergone neonatal transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) within 60 days after birth. Infants conceived via ART, defined as either in‐vitro fertilization or intracytoplasmic sperm injection, were matched randomly, based on sex and maternal age, in a 1:4 ratio with spontaneously conceived singleton infants, who formed the control group. The primary outcome was the diagnosis of minor CHD, defined as a ventricular septal defect (VSD), bicuspid aortic valve (BAV) and/or atrial septal defect (ASD), detected on neonatal TTE. For analysis of ASD, we assessed a subgroup of the matched infants included within the overall cohort who were evaluated using TTE from 15 May 2017 onwards using an algorithm for the TTE classification of interatrial communications implemented in the CBHS. Using multiple logistic regression analysis, we compared the risk of minor CHD in infants conceived via ART vs those conceived spontaneously, adjusting for maternal age, parental CHD, pregestational diabetes mellitus and maternal body mass index.ResultsOf 23 493 infants who met the inclusion criteria, 1630 singletons were conceived by ART. These were matched with 6520 controls who were conceived spontaneously. The subgroup of infants evaluated using the algorithm for TTE classification of interatrial communications comprised 862 infants conceived by ART and 3448 matched infants who were conceived spontaneously. The risk of minor CHD overall (VSD, BAV and/or ASD) in the subcohort evaluated using this algorithm was not significantly different between singleton infants conceived via ART and those conceived spontaneously (adjusted odds ratio, 1.07 (95% CI, 0.83–1.38)). Furthermore, we observed no significant differences in the risk of specific minor CHD subtypes (VSD, BAV and/or ASD) between singleton infants conceived via ART and those conceived spontaneously. Similarly, no significant differences in the risk of minor CHD were observed between ART subtypes, including conception via fresh in‐vitro fertilization, fresh intracytoplasmic sperm injection and fresh‐ and frozen‐embryo transfer, or between each ART subtype and infants conceived spontaneously.ConclusionThere was no increased risk of minor CHD in singleton infants conceived via ART compared with singleton infants conceived spontaneously. These findings provide reassuring evidence that ART is unlikely to impose an increased long‐term cardiovascular risk attributable to subclinical minor CHD that would otherwise remain undiagnosed in the absence of screening. © 2026 The Author(s). INTRODUCTION

Infertility affects up to 20% of couples in high‐income countries and assisted reproductive technology (ART) now accounts for the conception of up to 9.0% of births in Europe and 5.1% in the USA, with over 10 million individuals having been conceived via ART worldwide 1 , 2 . ART has been associated with altered fetal growth as well as an increased risk of pregnancy complications, perinatal complications and congenital malformations, including major congenital heart defects (CHD) 3 , 4 , 5 , 6 , 7 , 8 . Thorough evaluation of the safety of ART is important, particularly given that the risk of minor CHD in ART‐conceived individuals is not well‐characterized. In contrast to major CHD, minor CHD often present subclinically and are therefore methodologically difficult to ascertain in epidemiological registry‐based studies. Although classified as ‘minor’, these CHD are clinically relevant and have been associated with adverse cardiovascular outcomes and increased mortality later in life 9 , 10 , 11 . Studies investigating the risk of minor CHD in individuals conceived by ART are limited in number, methodologically heterogeneous and report inconsistent findings, thereby restricting the validity and interpretability of meta‐analytic estimates 12 , 13 .

To address the existing knowledge gap regarding the safety of ART and to evaluate whether individuals conceived via ART are at an increased risk of future cardiovascular disease, we aimed to assess the prevalence and risk of minor CHD in singleton infants conceived via ART compared with those in matched, spontaneously conceived (SC) infants. This analysis utilized data from a large‐scale Danish cohort participating in the Copenhagen Baby Heart Study (CBHS), which involved systematic transthoracic echocardiographic (TTE) examination. We hypothesized that singleton infants conceived via ART would have a higher prevalence of minor CHD than would SC infants.

METHODS

Data sources

The CBHS is a Danish, prospective, multicenter study of neonates delivered between 1 April 2016 and 31 October 2018 14 . Participants were recruited prenatally by inviting all parents attending the routine second‐trimester screening ultrasound examination at the three main maternity wards (Rigshospitalet, Hvidovre Hospital and Herlev Hospital) in the Greater Copenhagen area, Denmark, to participate in the study. All participating neonates were invited for an extensive cardiac examination, including TTE, within 60 days after birth. Information regarding pregnancy and delivery details as well as medical history of the parents was collected through questionnaires, medical records and Danish registries. A detailed cohort profile and description of the CBHS protocol have been published previously 14 , 15 . The participation rate of all infants born at the participating hospitals within the recruitment period was 55.0% 15 . Data from the CBHS were cross‐linked with information from the Danish National In Vitro Fertilization Registry 16 and the Medical Birth Registry 17 using the infants' personal identification numbers, assigned at birth in Denmark 18 . Moreover, mothers and children are linked at birth using unique personal identification numbers available from the Danish health registries. The CBHS is registered with clinicaltrials.gov (NCT02753348). The CBHS was approved by the Regional Ethics Committee of the Capital City Region of Denmark (H‐16001518).

Echocardiography

TTE scans were performed in accordance with American Society of Echocardiography guidelines and included subxiphoid, apical and parasternal views 19 . TTE scans were performed using Vivid E9 ultrasound machines (GE Healthcare, Zipf, Austria), with dedicated cardiac probes (12S‐D and 6S‐D; GE Healthcare), and the images were analyzed using EchoPac software, version 113 (GE Healthcare). The sonographers and physicians who performed the TTE examinations were trained by specialists in pediatric echocardiography. All examinations performed by new operators were reviewed until the sonographer or physician was certified by specialist judgement. In all cases in which an operator suspected a cardiac defect, a pediatric cardiologist (including A.‐S.S., A.A.R., K.I. and H.B.) reviewed the echocardiogram and confirmed or refuted the CHD diagnosis 14 . The reliability, reproducibility and repeatability of the neonatal TTE acquisitions and analyses had been tested prior to our analysis, demonstrating low coefficients of variation and good intraclass correlation coefficients 20 .

Study population and exposure definition

All CBHS participants were eligible for inclusion in this study, with the exception of infants meeting one or more of the following exclusion criteria: no TTE performed; presence of chromosomal abnormality or genetic syndrome; part of a multiple pregnancy; unknown multiple status, gestational age or birth weight; presence of CHD other than ventricular septal defect (VSD), atrial septal defect (ASD), bicuspid aortic valve (BAV), patent foramen ovale (PFO), coarctation of the aorta or pulmonary valve stenosis; and conception via intrauterine insemination or donor oocytes. In this study, ART was defined as either in‐vitro fertilization (IVF) or intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI), based on details recorded in the IVF registry. SC infants were matched randomly to ART‐conceived infants in a 1:4 ratio, based on sex, maternal age and whether the infant underwent TTE evaluated using an algorithm for classifying interatrial communications 21 . Assessment of interatrial communications was restricted to infants examined after the introduction of this classification algorithm. Therefore, including this parameter in participant matching ensured that the 1:4 ratio was retained in the subanalyses.

Outcomes

Our primary outcome was the diagnosis of any minor CHD detected on TTE. We defined minor CHD as the diagnosis of VSD, BAV and/or ASD. An algorithm for TTE classification of interatrial communications was implemented and applied to all infants in the CBHS from 15 May 2017 onward to detect and distinguish between PFO and ASD. The algorithm combines evaluation of interatrial flow and the structure, size and number of interatrial communications, and is described by Dannesbo et al. 21 . The algorithm functioned as a structured decision aid and has demonstrated substantial inter‐ and intraobserver agreement, as well as improved diagnostic consistency compared with unassisted classification 21 . Accordingly, only patients examined on or after this date were evaluated for PFO and ASD. Therefore, the total cohort was evaluated for the presence of VSD and/or BAV, and a subcohort of participants who were evaluated using this specific algorithm after 15 May 2017 were assessed for the presence of VSD, BAV and/or ASD. Neonates without registration of this specific TTE evaluation were excluded from analyses including PFO and ASD. If multiple defects were present, the infant was included in each respective CHD subtype analysis but counted only once in the main analysis of overall minor CHD. Owing to data regulations at Statistics Denmark (Copenhagen, Denmark), outcomes involving fewer than three individuals could not be reported; therefore, infants with isolated coarctation of the aorta or pulmonary valve stenosis were excluded from this study. Physicians interpreting echocardiograms were blinded to the mode of conception.

Covariates

The following covariates were included in the analysis model: maternal age (continuous), parental CHD (yes/no), pregestational diabetes mellitus (yes/no) and maternal body mass index (BMI; ≤ 18.5, 18.6–24, 25–29, ≥ 30 kg/m 2 ). International classification of diseases (ICD), 10th revision, codes were used to define comorbidities 22 . The codes used were as follows: parental CHD, Q20–Q26 or Q893 (or ICD, 9th revision 23 , codes 745–747 or 759); prepregnancy diabetes mellitus, E10.0–E11.9, O24.0, O24.1, O24.5; gestational diabetes mellitus, O24.4; pre‐eclampsia, O14.0–O14.2 or O15.0–O15.9; and gestational hypertension, O13.9. Small‐for‐gestational age (SGA) and large‐for‐gestational age were defined as < –2 SD and > +2 SD from the expected sex‐specific birth weight for gestational age, respectively 24 .

Statistical analysis

We compared the risk of minor CHD in infants conceived via ART with that in SC infants using multiple conditional logistic regression analysis, including maternal age, parental CHD, maternal pregestational diabetes mellitus and maternal pregestational BMI as potential confounders. Similar analyses were performed to evaluate the association between type of conception and the risk of specific subtypes of minor CHD and to compare the risk of minor CHD between infants conceived by fresh‐embryo transfer (ET) or frozen‐embryo transfer (FET) and SC infants, as well as the risk of minor CHD between infants conceived by fresh IVF or fresh ICSI and SC infants. Furthermore, we analyzed the risk of PFO, adjusting for gestational age at birth and age at the time of TTE.

Three sensitivity analyses were performed, by excluding infants born preterm (< 37 weeks' gestation), excluding those that were SGA and excluding pregnancies affected by a hypertensive disorder of pregnancy or gestational diabetes mellitus. Finally, sensitivity analyses were performed to assess the impact of adjusting for paternal age (as a continuous variable) and maternal age as a dichotomous variable, using a cut‐off of 35 years. We also conducted sensitivity analyses in which pulmonary valve stenosis and coarctation of the aorta were included under the definition of minor CHD, and a separate analysis excluding infants who underwent surgical intervention during follow‐up.

Results are reported as crude odds ratio (OR) and adjusted OR (aOR) with 95% CIs. Associations were considered statistically significant if the 95% CI did not include an OR of 1. All statistical analyses were performed using SAS software, version 9.4 (SAS/ACCESS® 9.4; SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA). P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Among the 8150 infants in the overall study population, 60/1630 (3.7%) in the ART group and 243/6520 (3.7%) in the SC group had a minor CHD diagnosis of VSD and/or BAV (P = 0.93; Table 2). In the subcohort with a TTE examination evaluated using the algorithm for classification of interatrial communications 21 , in which ASD diagnosis was included (n = 4310), 86/862 (10.0%) infants in the ART group and 324/3448 (9.4%) infants in the SC group had a minor CHD diagnosis of VSD, BAV and/or ASD (P = 0.60; Table 2). In this subcohort, ASD was the most prevalent minor CHD in both the ART group (n = 58 (6.7%)) and the SC group (n = 199 (5.8%)) (P = 0.29; Table 2). The aOR for the diagnosis of any minor CHD (VSD, BAV and/or ASD) in the ART group vs the SC group in the subcohort was 1.07 (95% CI, 0.83–1.38), with no significant change in this estimate when minor CHD was defined as VSD and/or BAV only in the total study population (Figure 2a). Additionally, the aORs for the diagnosis of each of the three specific subtypes of minor CHD were not significantly different between the ART group and the SC group, including on comparison of the risk of VSD and of BAV in the total study population, and the risk of ASD in the subcohort (Figure 2b).

Risk of minor CHD based on type of ART treatment

In the subcohort of patients with TTE scans evaluated using the algorithm for classification of interatrial communications 21 , the prevalence of any minor CHD (VSD, BAV and/or ASD) was 8.7% in the fresh ET group and 12.1% in the FET group, and it was 10.0% in the fresh IVF group and 7.6% in the fresh ICSI group of infants; in the SC group it was 9.4% (Table 3). Comparing the different ART subtypes with the SC group, including transfer type (fresh ET and FET) and fertilization method (fresh IVF and fresh ICSI), the FET group had the highest aOR (1.34 (95% CI, 0.93–1.93)) for the diagnosis of minor CHD, although no association reached statistical significance. Comparing FET to fresh ET yielded an aOR of 1.58 (95% CI, 0.99–2.53), while the aOR for minor CHD in the comparison of fresh ICSI vs fresh IVF was 0.79 (95% CI, 0.42–1.49) (Figure 3).

Sensitivity analyses

Excluding infants born preterm, those that were SGA and those born to mothers with a hypertensive disorder of pregnancy or gestational diabetes mellitus did not substantially alter the aOR estimates of minor CHD diagnosis in ART vs SC groups (Table S1 and Figure S1). Adjusting for paternal age (continuous variable) and maternal age (dichotomous variable with a cut‐off of 35 years) did not alter our results (data are not shown). Additionally, including individuals with a diagnosis of coarctation of the aorta or pulmonary valve stenosis in the analyses did not affect the results (data are not shown). Moreover, fewer than three infants needed surgical intervention > 100 days after birth, and excluding these cases did not affect the results (data are not shown).

Supplementary analyses

When the association between type of conception and risk of PFO was evaluated, we found a similar prevalence of PFO in the two groups (ART group, 73.6%; SC group, 72.8%; P = 0.66), with no significant increase in the crude or adjusted ORs (Table S2).

DISCUSSION

In this study we compared the prevalence of minor CHD in newborn singletons conceived via ART with that in SC singletons, in the largest population‐based echocardiographic assessment of cardiac structure and minor CHD in infants to date. Risk estimates were close to 1.0 (aOR, 1.07 (95% CI, 0.83–1.38) for any minor CHD (VSD, BAV and/or ASD) in the ART group vs the SC group in the subcohort evaluated using the algorithm for TTE classification of interatrial communications), with no significant differences observed across any subtype of minor CHD or ART method.

The findings of this study do not support our hypothesis of an increased risk of minor CHD associated with ART. Moreover, our findings are contrary to the results of previous studies reporting an elevated risk of CHD following conception by ART 25 , 26 , 27 , 28 , 29 . The present work employed a study design that approaches an optimal framework for the detection and estimation of the true prevalence of minor and subclinical CHD in neonates. This is in contrast to registry‐based studies, which, by their design, tend to underestimate the prevalence of minor CHD.

Previous meta‐analyses and registry‐based studies evaluating the association between ART and CHD have reported inconsistent findings, largely reflecting substantial heterogeneity in study design and outcome ascertainment. Overall, these studies have suggested an increased risk of minor CHD, often for specific minor CHD subtypes; however, these estimates are driven primarily by clinically apparent defects and are limited by a lack of systematic echocardiographic examination, leading to underestimation of subclinical minor CHD 12 . The existing literature provides clear evidence that multiple pregnancy is associated with an increased risk of CHD 30 , 31 , 32 , 33 , and, while some studies addressed the increased prevalence of multiple pregnancy in ART within their study design 27 , 28 , 34 , 35 , others did not 25 . To avoid the potential mediating effect of multiple births 33 , 36 , we excluded multiple pregnancies in the present study, allowing a clearer assessment of CHD risk directly attributable to ART.

It is likely that these methodological differences account for the discrepancies observed across previous studies and between previous studies and our findings. Our results offer reassuring evidence that, while ART has been associated with an elevated risk of major CHD, the risk of clinically occult minor CHD does not appear to be increased among singleton infants conceived via ART. Previous studies using fetal echocardiography 37 , 38 have reported findings more consistent with those of the present study. However, they did not capture the cardiac changes that occur at birth, and interatrial malformations may not be detectable prenatally. Major CHD typically arise from early disruptions in cardiac morphogenesis during the first weeks of embryogenesis 39 , 40 . In contrast, minor CHD tend to have more delayed and heterogeneous developmental origins 41 . In a recent review, Voros et al. 39 described how ART can alter the expression of key transcription factors essential for early cardiac development, potentially explaining the increased risk of major CHD, whereas minor CHD may be less affected by these early disruptions.

Strengths of the study

No previous study has examined a cohort of unselected infants of comparable size using systematic neonatal echocardiography. Accordingly, the present study provides a more accurate estimate of the risk of minor CHD in infants conceived via ART than do previous studies. The use of a standardized TTE protocol, performed by trained sonographers and physicians blinded to the mode of conception, minimized the risk of ascertainment bias. Diagnostic consistency was further ensured through regular quality monitoring, expert review by pediatric cardiologists and the application of validated algorithms for the classification of interatrial communications 21 .

ART treatment is associated with adverse maternal and perinatal outcomes, including preterm birth, SGA and hypertensive disorders 3 , 42 , which have been linked to an increased risk of CHD 43 , 44 , 45 . To address potential mediation, we conducted sensitivity analyses excluding infants affected by these outcomes, which did not materially affect the results. In addition, higher maternal BMI or obesity 46 , 47 , pregestational diabetes mellitus 48 , 49 , advanced maternal age 50 , 51 , 52 and the higher prevalence of CHD in male infants 53 represent important confounders of this association. Accordingly, a key strength of the study was the ability to match infants by sex and maternal age, along with multivariable adjustment for established confounders and sensitivity analyses that accounted for important mediators and additional potential confounders, including paternal age, which is a factor for which studies report inconsistent findings 54 , 55 , 56 . Notably, additional adjustment for paternal age did not alter our findings.

Limitations of the study

Our study was not sufficiently powered to detect differences in the risk of minor CHD across specific ART modalities or minor CHD subtypes. Regardless, the results for these subgroups were included to indicate tendencies (e.g. FET having higher risk estimates than fresh ET and fresh IVF having higher risk estimates than fresh ICSI) across strata, with the aim of informing interpretation of the overall findings. Information on the underlying cause of infertility was captured incompletely in the register and was therefore not included in the analyses. Another limitation was that information on specific VSD subtypes was unavailable. A sensitivity analysis excluding the small number of infants who underwent cardiac surgery during follow‐up did not materially alter the results. As all surgical procedures occurred after 3 months of age, these cases were classified as minor CHD. Importantly, the study population may have underrepresented infants with larger VSDs, since parents who had already undergone prenatal cardiac assessment and had been informed of abnormal findings may have been less inclined to participate in the CBHS. Such selection may have contributed to differences between our estimated VSD prevalence and those reported in previous studies. Nevertheless, our study identified a higher prevalence of VSD than did earlier reports 57 .

The CBHS was designed as an unselected cohort, and extensive efforts were made to include all eligible newborns and mothers. Nevertheless, over‐ or underrepresentation of specific groups may have reduced the generalizability of our results. Neonates born preterm or with severe diseases were underrepresented in the CBHS, which may have occurred because the parents were less inclined to participate in research that required additional hospital visits 14 . We excluded cases of multiple pregnancy to avoid mediation by plurality 30 , 31 , 33 . This restriction may have improved internal validity by allowing a better comparison between infants conceived by ART and SC infants, at the cost of generalizability. As multiple pregnancy in ART is largely preventable through single‐embryo transfer policies, which are now standard practice in Nordic countries, our findings are probably more generalizable to contemporary ART treatment regimens.

Conclusion

Our findings suggest that singleton infants conceived via ART do not have a significantly increased risk of minor CHD compared with SC singleton infants. These results provide reassuring evidence for clinicians and for individuals and couples considering or undergoing ART treatment.

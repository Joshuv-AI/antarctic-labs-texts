Comparative efficacy and long-term survival of CD19/22 versus CD19 CAR-T immunotherapy in Relapsed/Refractory B-ALL with TP53 alterations
Authors: Yutong Tang, Mengyun Li, Qingya Cui, Sining Liu, Tingting Li, Huiying Qiu, Liqing Kang, Lei Yu, Depei Wu, Xiaowen Tang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPatients with refractory/relapsed (R/R) B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (B-ALL) harboring TP53 alterations have a dismal prognosis due to profound chemoresistance. While CD19 chimeric antigen receptor T-cell (CAR-T) therapy has shifted the treatment landscape, long-term efficacy in this high-risk subset remains limited. The dual-target CD19/22 CAR-T has been developed to enhance anti-tumor activity; however, its comparative efficacy and long-term survival relative to CD19 CAR-T in this high-risk population remain unclear and require further elucidation to inform clinical decision-making.MethodsThis study included 55 patients with TP53-altered R/R B-ALL who were enrolled in clinical trials (NCT03919240, NCT03275493, and NCT03614858) and treated with either CD19 (n = 27) or CD19/22 (n = 28) CAR-T therapy. The outcomes assessed included the complete remission (CR) rate, minimal residual disease (MRD)-negative CR rate, overall survival (OS), leukemia-free survival (LFS), and cumulative incidence of relapse (CIR). Multivariable Cox regression models were used to estimate hazard ratios (HRs) for OS and LFS.ResultsThe CR rate was high in both groups (CD19/22: 100%; CD19: 88.89%). Notably, the MRD-negative CR rate was higher with CD19/22 CAR-T therapy (75.00% vs. 29.63%, p = 0.0011), and was confirmed as an independent favorable prognostic factor. The CD19/22 CAR-T also demonstrated markedly better long-term survival, with 3-year OS (59.55% vs. 25.49%, p = 0.0050) and LFS (57.29% vs. 17.64%, p = 0.0047) rates. Among the 32 patients who underwent consolidative allo-HSCT after CAR-T, the CD19/22 CAR-T group achieved superior 3-year survival (OS: 72.34% vs. 30.77%, p = 0.0089; LFS: 76.69% vs. 23.07%, p = 0.0041) and lower CIR (23.30% vs. 75.00%, p = 0.0182). Multivariable analysis established CD19/22 CAR-T therapy and bridging to allo-HSCT as independent predictors of improved LFS.ConclusionCD19/22 CAR-T immunotherapy, particularly when followed by allo-HSCT, represents a promising and clinically effective treatment paradigm for R/R B-ALL with TP53 alterations.Trial registrationA single-center retrospective clinical study.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12967-026-07974-w.Keywords: TP53, Relapsed/Refractory B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia, Allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation, CD19/22 CAR-T, CD19 CAR-T, MRD-negative remission

Introduction

B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (B-ALL) is highly curable in pediatric and young adult patients, achieving 3-year survival rates reaching up to 80.0% [1–4]. However, patients with high-risk genetic subtypes (e.g., BCR-ABL1-like, TP53 mutation, and complex karyotype) continue to face long-term morbidity [5, 6]. TP53 mutations are detected in approximately 16.0% to 30.0% of B-ALL patients, particularly in relapsed or refractory (R/R) disease [7–9].

The TP53 gene, located at 17p13.1, acts as the “guardian of the genome” [10, 11], maintaining genomic stability by inducing cell cycle arrest, DNA repair, or apoptosis in response to cellular damage [12, 13]. Current therapies, including induction chemotherapy, hypomethylating agents, and BCL-2 inhibitors, heavily rely on an intact p53-mediated apoptotic pathway [14, 15]. TP53 abnormalities disrupt downstream apoptotic signaling and confer broad resistance to chemotherapeutic agents, ultimately leading to inferior responses, even following allo-HSCT. Alterations in the TP53 gene are independent predictors of poor prognosis, associated with high relapse rates ranging from 60.0% to 80.0%, regardless of patient age, disease stage, or therapeutic regimen [9, 16–18].

Novel immunotherapies, including blinatumomab and CD19 CAR-T therapy, have shifted the treatment paradigm for R/R B-ALL, achieving remission rates ranging from 59% to 90% [18–21]. However, whether CD19-targeted immunotherapy can produce durable remissions remains unproven in patients with TP53 alterations [22]. Compared to wild-type patients, TP53-mutated B-ALL patients treated with CD19 CAR-T cell therapy exhibited a lower CR rate (72.73% vs. 94.39%, p = 0.004), and inferior survival outcomes (2-year OS: 39.1% vs. 66.2%, p = 0.004; 2-year LFS: 33.5% vs. 59.2%, p = 0.004) [19]. Most patients still relapse due to limited persistence of CD19 CAR-T cells and antigen escape [23, 24]. Notably, a recent study indicated that TP53 mutation might be a potential predictor for CD19-negative relapse, with antigen loss observed in 100% (7/7) of TP53-mutated patients who relapsed following CD19 CAR-T therapy [23].

Bispecific CD19/22 CAR-T therapy has emerged as a promising strategy to extend CAR-T cell persistence and improve the long-term remission in R/R B-ALL [25–27]. Dual-targeting of CD19 and CD22 also provides a feasible solution to mitigate the risk of antigen escape [28]. Our previous retrospective analysis of 219 R/R B-ALL patients treated with CAR-T therapy confirmed that tandem CD19/22 CAR-T achieved better responses over CD19 CAR-T (CR rate: 98.0% vs. 83.0%, p = 0.006; MRD-CR rate: 86.3% vs. 65.3%, p = 0.015) [29]. All high-risk subgroups (complex karyotypes, Ph-like ALL, and TP53 mutation) achieved a 100% CR rate after CD19/22 CAR-T therapy. Another early report from our center, involving 47 high-risk R/R B-ALL patients, reported a 100% hematological CR rate at day 28 after tandem CD19/22 CAR-T infusion, including patients with TP53 mutations (n = 6), all of whom achieved MRD-negative CR [30].

Despite the above promising early results, large-scale, long-term comparative evidence specifically focused on CAR-T therapy for TP53-altered R/R B-ALL is lacking. To address this knowledge gap, we first evaluated the efficacy and long-term outcomes of CD19/22 CAR-T versus CD19 CAR-T therapy in a cohort of TP53-altered R/R B-ALL patients from three clinical trials.

Materials and methods

Patients

Between January 2017 and December 2023, 55 B-ALL patients with TP53 alterations were included in this study. They were diagnosed based on past medical history, morphology, immunophenotyping, cytogenetics, and molecular biology. TP53 alterations were defined based on a combination of next-generation sequencing (NGS) and Interphase fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH). All patients were enrolled in clinical trials (NCT03919240, NCT03275493, and NCT03614858) for treatment with either CD19 CAR-T or CD19/22 CAR-T. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of The First Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University, and written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

Detection of TP53 alterations

Next-generation sequencing (NGS)

A panel of 172 genes was applied to mutation screening. DNA libraries were prepared by using the KAPA DNA HyperPlus Kit (Roche, Mannheim, Germany) and subjected to high-throughput sequencing analysis with NextSeq 550 using paired-end 150-cycle protocols (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA). The mean coverage was about 1000X. Data analysis was performed using an in-house bioinformatic pipeline. Single-nucleotide variants and small insertions/deletions were identified by GATK (version:4.2.2.0). The VAF cutoff is 2%.

Interphase fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH)

Interphase FISH was performed to assess formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue sections or methanol and acetic acid-fixed cells for karyotyping. A commercially available 17p13.1 probe (Vysis, Downers Grove, IL, USA) was utilized for del(17p) detection.

CAR-T treatment procedures

All patients underwent lymphodepletion conditioning regimens, which consisted of fludarabine (30 mg/m2/day) and cyclophosphamide (300 mg/m2/day) on days -5 to -3. CAR-T cells were administered as a fractionated infusion, given for 3 consecutive days (10%-30%-60%) from day 0. The CD19 CAR-T contains an FMC63 (anti-CD19) murine single-chain variable fragment (scFv), a costimulatory 4-1BB domain, and a CD3ζ domain. The CD19/22 CAR-T contains both CD19 and CD22 targeted scFv, a transmembrane domain with CD28 and OX40, and a CD3ζ domain (Supplementary Fig. 4). The median dose of CAR-T cells was 5 (5–10) × 106 cells/kg in the CD19 group, and 10 (5–20) × 106 cells/kg in the CD19/22 group. Given the non-linear expansion characteristics of CAR-T cells in vivo, target doses within this broad therapeutic window were considered clinically acceptable per the trial protocols.

Definitions

TP53 single-hit was defined as a single TP53 mutation or sole deletion of 17p; TP53 multi-hit was defined according to the International Consensus Classification (ICC) as ≥ 2 distinct TP53 mutations (each with VAF ≥ 10%), a single TP53 mutation associated with either del (17p) or monosomy 17, or VAF ≥ 50%, or accompanied by a complex karyotype (treated as a multi-hit equivalent surrogate for bi-allelic loss) [10, 31].

Complete remission (CR) was defined as less than 5% marrow blasts, no circulating blasts, no extramedullary disease, with an absolute neutrophil count of 1000/μl or more and platelets of 1 × 105/μl or more. Minimal residual disease (MRD) was assessed by multiparameter flow cytometry, and MRD-negative (MRD-) status was defined as a blast count ≤ 0.01%. MRD-CR was defined as MRD-negative combined with CR [32]. Treatment response was assessed on day 28 after CAR-T cell infusion.

Overall survival (OS) was defined as the time from CAR-T infusion to last follow-up or death from any cause. Leukemia-free survival (LFS) was calculated from CAR-T infusion until disease recurrence, death, or last follow-up while in CR. Bridging transplantation refers to allo-HSCT performed in patients who have achieved sustained CR after CAR-T therapy.

Cytokine release syndrome (CRS) is a symptom complex associated with the use of adoptive cell therapies that activate lymphocytes and was graded according to the criteria of Lee et al [33]. Immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS) was assessed according to the American Society for Blood and Marrow Transplantation [34]. Hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH) was diagnosed according to the HLH-2004 criteria [35].

Statistical analysis

OS and LFS probabilities were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method and compared via the two-sided log-rank test. Categorical variables were summarized as frequencies and percentages, and compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. Multivariate logistic regression was applied to identify prognostic factors for MRD-CR. Univariate and multivariate analyses of survival outcomes were performed using Cox proportional hazards models, and the proportional hazards assumption was verified using Schoenfeld residuals. A two-tailed p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Cumulative incidence of relapse (CIR) was analyzed using a competing risk model, with non-relapse mortality as a competing risk. General statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS version 22.0 and GraphPad Prism version 8.3.0. R software (version 3.6.2) was specifically utilized to analyze the CIR competing risk model and to evaluate Schoenfeld residuals.

Results

Patient characteristics

A total of 55 patients with TP53-aberrant B-ALL were included in this study, of whom 27 received CD19 CAR-T and 28 received CD19/22 CAR-T (Fig. 1A). Baseline clinical and biological characteristics are summarized in Table 1. All features (including age, gender, disease status, prior therapies, genetic characteristics, origin and dose of CAR-T cell were balanced between the two groups (all p > 0.05). The cohort included 31 males and 24 females (ratio 1.29:1), with a median age of 26 (range: 7–71) years. At enrollment, 25.45% (14/55) had refractory disease, 52.73% (29/55) were in first relapse, and 21.82% (12/55) were in second or more relapse. 3 patients exhibited extramedullary disease (testicular, renal, and liver). Overall, 76.36% (42/55) had received more than five prior therapies, including four patients who received blinatumomab as part of the prior lines of treatment and one who received prior CD19 CAR-T infusion. 8 patients (14.55%) had a history of allo-HSCT, with a median time from HSCT to relapse of 9.67 (range: 2.73–32.5) months. Cytogenetic analysis revealed a complex karyotype in 26 patients (47.27%), and a monosomal karyotype in 21 (38.18%). Molecular characterization revealed MLL-AF4 rearrangement in 5 cases (9.09%) and E2A-PBX1 fusion in 1 case (1.82%). The BCR/ABL1 fusion was detected in 7 patients (12.73%), three of whom carried the T315I mutation. 3 patients (5.45%) were diagnosed with Ph-like ALL.

Baseline characteristics of patients

Profiles of TP53 alterations

Among the 55 patients, 36 (65.45%) had a TP53 mutation alone, 8 (14.55%) had isolated del (17p), and 11 (20.00%) had both mutation and deletion. According to ICC criteria, 23 (41.82%) were classified as TP53 multi-hit and 32 (58.18%) as single-hit. The majority of mutations were located within the DNA-binding domain (DBD). Mutations at four of six hotspot residues (R175, G245, R248, R249, R273, R282)—were detected in 12 patients (27.27%), specifically including R248 (n = 8), R282 (n = 1), R273 (n = 2), and R245 (n = 1) (Fig. 1B). In the CD19 CAR-T group, there were 12 missense mutations (including 4 hotspots), 4 frameshift mutations, 3 nonsense mutations, and 1 insertion mutation. The CD19/22 CAR-T group had 20 missense (including 8 hotspots), 4 frameshift, and 1 splice site mutation (Fig. 1C). Most mutations were in exons 5-8 (77.78%); others were in exon 4 (11.11%), exon 10 (6.67%), and exons 2 and 11 (2.22% each) (Fig. 1D).

Treatment effects of the CD19 CAR-T and CD19/22 CAR-T

Overall, 94.55% (52/55) of patients achieved CR post CAR-T cell infusion, and the MRD-CR rate was 52.73% (29/55). In the CD19 CAR-T group, the CR rate was 88.89% (24/27) and the MRD-CR rate was 29.63% (8/27) (Figs. 2A–B). In contrast, the CD19/22 CAR-T group demonstrated superior outcomes, with CR rate and an MRD-CR rate of 100% (28/28) and 75.00% (21/28), respectively. The difference in MRD-CR rate between the two groups was statistically significant (75.00% vs. 29.63%; p = 0.0011). Univariate analysis identified first relapse and CD19/22 dual-target CAR-T therapy as factors associated with a higher MRD-CR rate (Supplementary Table 1). In multivariate logistic regression analyses of MRD-CR, CD19/22 CAR-T therapy (vs. CD19 CAR-T) and first relapse (vs. refractory disease) were still independent prognostic factors significantly associated with the MRD-CR rate (Supplementary Table 2). Among patients who achieved CR, relapse occurred in 70.37% (19/27) of the CD19 group, including 3 with CD19-negative relapse. In the CD19/22 group, only 35.71% (10/28) of patients experienced relapse, one of whom showed a CD19– CD22+ phenotype. The relapse rate was significantly lower in the CD19/22 group (35.71% vs. 70.37%; p = 0.0101) (Fig. 2C).

Treatment response and survival outcomes of CD19 CAR-T and CD19/22 CAR-T therapy. (A-C) The CR rate, MRD-negative CR rate, and relapse rate in all patients. (D-F) OS, LFS, and CIR of patients in the CD19 CAR-T group and the CD19/22 CAR-T group

After a median follow-up of 26.33 months (range: 0.27–89.8 months), the entire cohort had a 3-year OS of 40.04% (95% CI: 25.78-54.16%) and 3-year LFS of 35.60% (95% CI: 21.84–49.60%) (Supplementary Fig. 1A-B). The 1- and 3-year CIR were 32.27% (95% CI: 16.07–49.68%) and 58.55% (95% CI: 45.23–69.91%), respectively (Supplementary Fig. 1C).

With a median follow-up of 23.02 months (range: 3.37–89.8 months) for the CD19/22 group and 19.00 months (range: 0.27–69.2 months) for the CD19 group, comparative survival analysis revealed markedly superior outcomes in the CD19/22 CAR-T group (3-year OS: 59.55% vs. 25.49%, p = 0.0050), and median OS was not reached vs. 19.00 months in the CD19 CAR-T group. The 3-year LFS was 57.29% vs. 17.64% (p = 0.0047), and the 3-year CIR was 39.14% vs. 70.55% (p = 0.0227) in the CD19/22 and CD19 CAR-T groups, respectively (Fig. 2D–F).

Consolidative allo-HSCT after CAR-T cell therapy was associated with better long-term outcomes

32 patients who achieved remission after CAR-T therapy underwent subsequent allo-HSCT. The median time to allo-HSCT was 2.03 months (range: 0.77–4.97 months) from CAR-T cell infusion. Characteristics were balanced between patients who received allo-HSCT and those who did not (Supplementary Table 3). Significantly better outcomes: 3-year OS 51.99% vs. 37.20% (p = 0.0052), 3-year LFS 52.45% vs. 21.52% (p = 0.0027), and 3-year CIR 45.86% vs. 75.63% (p = 0.0284) (Fig. 3A–C) were observed in patients who received allo-HSCT after CAR-T cell therapy. Notably, in the bridging allo-HSCT cohort, patients who received CD19/22 CAR-T showed better survival compared to those in the CD19 CAR-T group (3-year OS: 72.34% vs. 30.77%, p = 0.0089; 3-year LFS: 76.69% vs. 23.07%, p = 0.0041; 3-year CIR: 23.30% vs. 75.00%, p = 0.0182) (Fig. 3D–F). In contrast, among patients who did not receive allo-HSCT, CD19/22 CAR-T and CD19 CAR-T therapy had a similar prognosis (Fig. 3G–I).

Subgroup analysis of TP53 alterations and tumor burden

There were no significant differences in survival between TP53 single-hit and multi-hit patients (3-year OS: 44.59% vs. 41.74%, p = 0.9309; 3-year LFS: 37.52% vs. 32.45%, p = 0.8946) (Fig. 4A–B). Among single-hit patients, survival was significantly longer in the CD19/22 group (3-year OS: 67.10% vs. 22.50%, p = 0.0478; 3-year LFS: 60.05% vs. 15.24%, p = 0.0073) (Fig. 4C–D). In the multi-hit subgroup, patients treated with CD19/22 CAR-T showed a relatively higher 3-year OS (46.88% vs. 30.33%). However, the differences in OS and LFS did not reach statistical significance (Supplementary Fig. 2A-B).

Among patients with a high tumor burden (>20% blasts), CD19/22 CAR-T therapy improved survival outcomes compared to CD19 CAR-T: 3-year OS was 56.25% vs. 16.67% (p = 0.0551), 3-year LFS was 57.14% vs. 16.67% (p = 0.0459) (Fig. 4E–F). No significant differences were observed in patients with low pre-CAR-T tumor burden (Supplementary Fig. 3D-F).

Treatment safety and tolerability

The safety profiles of both CAR-T products are detailed in Table 2. All patients exhibited favorable treatment tolerance and there was no treatment-related mortality. 39 patients (70.91%) experienced CRS, including 20 (74.07%) in the CD19 group and 19 (67.86%) in the CD19/22 group. Grade 3 or higher CRS occurred in 10 patients (6 in the CD19 group, 4 in the CD19/22 group). Only one patient in the CD19/22 group developed ICANS on day 6 after CAR-T infusion (lethargy and dysgraphia), which resolved with dexamethasone administration. No other neurotoxicities were observed. One patient in the CD19 group developed HLH, and none in the CD19/22 group (Table 2). There were no statistically significant differences in the incidence or severity of toxicities between the two cohorts.

Adverse events

Predictive factors for survival

To identify factors influencing survival outcomes, we initially performed univariable Cox analyses to identify baseline and therapy-related factors that were associated with OS and LFS (Supplementary Tables 5–6). Variables with p < 0.05 in univariate analysis and clinically relevant factors were included in the multivariate Cox model. Multivariate analysis confirmed bridging to allo-HSCT (HR = 0.316, 95% CI: 0.129–0.775; p = 0.012) as the only independent protective factor for better OS. Tumor burden of 5–20%, tumor burden > 20% (reference: tumor burden < 5%), and non-response to CAR-T therapy were independent risk factors for OS (Fig. 5A). Through multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analysis of LFS, CD19/22 CAR-T therapy (HR = 0.393, 95% CI: 0.157–0.985; p = 0.046) and bridging to allo-HSCT (HR = 0.270, 95% CI: 0.108–0.675; p = 0.005) were associated with better LFS. Adverse factors for LFS included Ph-like ALL, tumor burden 5–20%, tumor burden > 20%, and non-response to CAR-T infusion (Fig. 5B).

Discussion

The dismal prognosis of TP53-altered R/R B-ALL has been well-documented, with conventional therapies offering limited benefit due to a lower response rate [36, 37]. Although CD19-directed immunotherapies have improved outcomes in R/R B-ALL, TP53 mutations have been linked to inferior outcomes following blinatumomab and CD19 CAR-T therapy in patients with B-ALL, as well as other mature B-cell malignancies [38]. Strategies that can improve the CD19-directed immunotherapies should be actively explored for this high-risk group. In R/R B-cell lymphoma, CD19/22 CAR-T combined with autologous stem cell transplantation improved the long-term survival of TP53 disruptions [39]. In this study, we provide the first comparative evidence demonstrating that CD19/22 CAR-T induction followed by allo-HSCT consolidation was associated with improved survival in TP53-altered R/R B-ALL. Multivariate analysis confirmed that CD19/22 dual-target CAR T-cell therapy was associated with a better treatment response and LFS in TP53-altered R/R B-ALL, establishing a potential treatment option for this difficult-to-treat cohort.

The role of transplantation after CAR-T therapy remains debated. While adults with Ph-negative ALL who achieve MRD- negative remission after intensive chemotherapy may skip allo-HSCT and achieve long-term survival (5-year OS: 59–72%) [40], this approach may not be enough to address TP53-related risks. Previous studies have shown that in TP53-mutant B-ALL, consolidation with allo-HSCT leads to significantly better 1-year OS (35% vs. 9%, p = 0.014) compared to immunotherapy alone [41]. Our findings support the importance of bridging allo-HSCT after CAR-T infusion in treating TP53-aberrant relapsed/refractory B-ALL, which greatly improved survival outcomes, especially in patients who received CD19/22 CAR-T (3-year OS: 72.34% vs. 30.77%, p = 0.0089; 3-year LFS: 76.69% vs. 23.07%, p = 0.0041). This may be due to the dramatically higher MRD-CR rate achieved with CD19/22 CAR-T compared to CD19 CAR-T (75.00% vs. 29.63%; p = 0.0011). MRD positivity has been strongly linked to poorer prognosis in R/R B-ALL patients undergoing allo-HSCT after CAR-T therapy [18, 42]. A better pre-transplant disease state is key for long-term success. Multivariate analysis also confirmed bridging allo-HSCT as an independent protective factor for both OS and LFS.

The benefit of CD19/22 CAR-T was most pronounced in patients with high pre-treatment tumor burden (>20% bone marrow blasts), who experienced a significant improvement in 3-year LFS (57.14% vs. 16.67%; p = 0.0459) than CD19 CAR-T. Previous studies suggested that high tumor burden was associated with poor response and short response of CD19 CAR-T therapy [42]. Mechanistically, the synergistic antitumor efficacy of CD19/22 CAR-T likely stems from the enhanced functional avidity of the bivalent immunological synapse, which ensures robust cytotoxicity even against blasts with variable antigen density [43, 44]. This highlights the potent tumor-clearing capacity of the bispecific construct in aggressive disease settings. Notably, the low incidence of CD19-negative relapse (5.6%) in the CD19/22 group further supports its use to mitigate antigen escape—a major failure mechanism in TP53-aberrant B-ALL [23]. Given the high risk of antigen escape in TP53-alterations B-ALL and the persistence of CD22 and other B-cell markers on CD19− leukemic cells [45], we propose CD19/22 CAR-T as a promising therapeutic strategy for patients with TP53 abnormalities who co-express CD19 and CD22.

Additionally, our analysis revealed that the prognostic disparity traditionally associated with TP53 multi-hit status was abrogated following CAR-T therapy. TP53 mutations drive immune escape via downregulation and reduced MHC-I expression, this impairment decreases CD8+ T cell recognition, allowing malignant cells to escape cytotoxic immune responses [46, 47]. A critical advantage of CAR-T cells is their ability to recognize surface antigens (CD19/CD22) in an MHC-independent manner. Furthermore, while conventional chemotherapy relies on the DNA damage response to trigger p53-mediated apoptosis, CAR-T cells utilize the perforin/granzyme B pathway. This mechanism directly activates executioner caspases, effectively bypassing the upstream p53 checkpoint [38, 48, 49].

In our cohort, the R248Q mutation was the most common, and similar results were also obtained in diffuse large B-cell lymphoma and relapsed/refractory B-ALL children [23, 50]. A multinational cohort (n = 1,951) has found that 4.7-fold higher R248Q frequency in relapsed vs. newly-diagnosed ALL (21.2% vs. 4.5%; p = 0.005) [51]. Studies have demonstrated that R248Q disrupts DNA binding, abrogates p53 transcriptional activity, and confers resistance to idarubicin/vincristine [52]. All 8 patients with the R248Q mutation achieved CR in our study, but relapse still occurred in 100% (3/3) of the CD19 group and 50.0% (2/4) of the CD19/22 group, respectively. Although the sample size is limited (n = 8), the observed overall relapse rate of 57.0% suggests that R248Q may represent a potential high-risk feature even in the CAR-T era. In the future, this exploratory finding warrants further investigation in larger studies to clarify the mechanisms of CAR-T evasion associated with specific TP53 hotspots.

This study presents some limitations. As a retrospective analysis, conclusions regarding allo-HSCT efficacy require cautious interpretation and prospective validation. Differences in CAR-T design (e.g., costimulatory domains) and cell dose between the two groups may complicate the interpretation of treatment outcomes. Selection bias and the relative sample size (n = 55) limit the statistical power of subgroup analyses. Prospective, large-scale, multi-center trials are needed to evaluate CD19/22 CAR-T efficacy in TP53-altered B-ALL accurately.

In summary, our study first provides valuable insights into the CD19/22 CAR-T bridging to allo-HSCT as a preferred treatment strategy for patients with TP53-alterations R/R B-ALL. It offers a synergistic strategy that may establish a new option for this vulnerable population. For patients ineligible for transplantation, future efforts should focus on exploring combination strategies (e.g., CAR-T combined with hypomethylating agents, BCL-2 inhibitors) or novel CAR constructs to extend remission duration and improve survival outcomes. Future research should focus on prospective validation and rational combination strategies to further improve outcomes.

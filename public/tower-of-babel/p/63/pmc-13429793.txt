A Claims Data–Based Comparison of Treatment Continuation between Aripiprazole Once-monthly and Paliperidone Palmitate in Routine Practice
Authors: Chi-Un Pae, So Hyeon Gwon, Hayeon Kim, Changsu Han, Ashwin A. Patkar, Prakash S. Masand, Nak-Hoon Son
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveAripiprazole once-monthly (AOM) and paliperidone palmitate once-monthly (PP1M) are commonly used long-acting injectable APs (LAIs) in routine practice. However, their distinct pharmacological properties may differentially influence on long-term treatment continuation. To date, comparison data on long-term treatment continuation between AOM and PP1M are limited.MethodsPatients with schizophrenia spectrum disorders identified from the Korean Health Insurance Review and Assessment Service claims database were followed for 12 months after initiating AOM or PP1M. Cox proportional hazards and Kaplan–Meier analyses were used to assess discontinuation risk and rates.ResultsThere were significant differences in the discontinuation risk and rates at 6 months (HR = 1.301, 95% CI = 1.242−1.363 and 45.5% vs. 56.2%, respectively) and 12 months (HR = 1.322, 95% CI = 1.270−1.376 and 62.7% vs. 74.1%, respectively) between the two LAIs, where the patients treated with AOM showed lower discontinuation rates than those treated with PP1M. The all-cause hospitalization rates (52.3% vs 60.4%; 53.7% vs 61.6%) and newly developed extrapyramidal symptoms (6.2% vs 7.0%; 7.7% vs. 8.1%) at 6 months and 12 months were numerically lower in AOM than in PP1M, respectively. These findings were consistent in PSM and 3-month landmark analysis.ConclusionIn this nationwide real-world study, AOM was associated with a lower risk of treatment discontinuation than PP1M over 12 months. These findings suggest differences in treatment continuation between the two LAIs and warrant confirmation in studies using more robust designs and diverse clinical data.

INTRODUCTION

Schizophrenia can cause serious and long-lasting disturbances that interfere with an individual’s ability to work or meaningful social relationships. Antipsychotics (APs) have been considered a traditional first-line treatment for controlling symptoms of schizophrenia through proven efficacy and safety via numerous randomized, controlled clinical studies (RCTs), observational studies, and meta-analytic reviews [1-4]. However, the clinical course is typically chronic and relapsing. For instance, previous studies have reported that the cumulative first relapse rate was 81.9%, while the second relapse rate was 78.0% five years after recovery from APs therapy. The cumulative third relapse rate after recovering from the second relapse was found to be 86.2% at four years. Furthermore, discontinuation of APs was found to increase the risk of relapse by fivefold [5].

Despite the proven efficacy of APs therapy, medication adherence in routine practice has been consistently reported to be insufficient. Overall, the treatment compliance rates are reported to be approximately 20−50% in many previous studies [6-9]. The reasons for low treatment adherence among schizophrenia patients could be explained by diverse clinical factors including lack of insight on illness, fear of stigma associated with APs, anxiety about losing autonomy, inconvenience of daily dosing, and adverse events (AEs) [8,10]. Consequently, low treatment adherence may result in reduced therapeutic efficacy, increased suicide rates, diminished cognitive function, higher hospitalization rates, relapses/recurrences, and treatment resistance [7,11-13]. This negatively impacts overall clinical outcomes and causes an increase in socioeconomic costs as well. Particularly, early initiation of treatment in the early stages of the disease and maintenance therapy are known as crucial factors determining long-term clinical outcomes, which emphasizes the importance of medication adherence [3,14-18].

With these respects, long-acting injectable antipsychotics (LAIs) have been developed to improve medication adherence and reduce the risk of relapse/recurrence by providing easy monitoring of treatment compliance, consistent dosing intervals, and stable drug concentration [19,20]. Numerous RCTs, observational studies, and meta-analyses have reported that LAIs may improve treatment persistence and reduce readmissions compared to oral APs, and that their overall safety profile is comparable to that of oral APs [21-24].

Among the most commonly used LAIs, aripiprazole once-monthly (AOM) and paliperidone palmitate once-monthly (PP1M) appear to have similar overall efficacy as a class of APs, however, they have substantially different mechanisms of action which may produce unique clinical effects and AE profiles [20]. AOM is a partial agonist at both dopamine D2 and serotonin 5-HT1A receptors and an antagonist at 5-HT2A receptors, and therefore tends to less likely cause metabolic AEs or sexual dysfunctions compared to other APs [25]. The oral formulation of aripiprazole (ARP) is approved for the Adjunctive treatment of major depressive disorder (MDD) due to its effects on mood regulation [26,27]. Although AOM has not been approved for the adjunctive treatment of MDD, AOM has an indication for bipolar disorder (BPD) [28]. On the other hand, PP has potent blockade effects on both dopamine D2 and serotonin 5-HT2A receptors [29]. However, it has been found to increase risk for AEs including extrapyramidal symptoms (EPS) and sexual dysfunctions like hyperprolactinemia [30,31]. PP1M has been approved for the treatment of schizophrenia and schizoaffective disorder, but not for BPD [32]. The pharmacological differences between these two LAIs, including the initial treatment strategy, loading doses, AEs profile, switching method, concomitant medications, and accessibility to medical care, may affect treatment adherence in routine practice [20,30].

Currently, direct comparison studies between AOM and PP1M for efficacy and safety as well as treatment continuation are very limited, likely due to several limitations, including the huge cost of well-designed, large-scale controlled studies, the need for long-term observation, and concerns about direct comparisons between manufacturers. Despite RCTs being the gold standard for study design to evaluate drug efficacy and safety, it also has clear caveats since it needs stringent selection criteria and structured follow-up systems, which substantially limit the full reflection of the complexities of real-world clinical settings.

Hence, large-scale comparative studies using real-world data, such as nationwide health insurance claims data collected in usual treatment settings, can be a valuable alternative approach to complement clinical trial results. In particular, health insurance claims data is known to offer the advantage of long-term follow-up for large populations along with systematic identification of prescription patterns, comorbidity, medication type, dosing, and healthcare utilization patterns. Therefore, such studies have continuously increased to find clinically relevant findings for evaluation and management of the treatment continuation of LAIs in routine practice.

Therefore, the present study assessed treatment continuation patterns at 6 months and 12 months in patients with schizophrenia treated with AOM and PP1M using Korean insurance claims data, the Health Insurance Review and Assessment Service (HIRA). This study aimed to investigate differences in treatment continuation in real-world clinical settings and provide useful information for further treatment strategies between the two LAIs.

METHODS

Source of Data and Study Procedure

Health Insurance Review and Assessment Service

The nationwide claims data from the HIRA (https://www.hira.or.kr/eng/main.do) covering the entire Korean population were utilized for the present study. The HIRA database contains various clinical information including demographics, diagnoses, healthcare utilization, and prescription data. Diagnostic codes are applied in accordance with the Korean Classification of Diseases adapted from the International Classification of Diseases, 10th Revision [33].

The HIRA claims database was used for the entire analysis, with all data fully de-identified. The data collection period spanned from 1 January 2016 to 31 December 2023, reflecting the availability of AOM and PP1M in the Korean market during this timeframe. AOM was approved 23 December 2015 by the Korean Food and Drug Administration, while PP1M was approved on 26 July 2010. Figure 1 illustrates a detailed timeline of the present study and Figure 2 shows the full study procedure during observation.

Data were collected from patients aged 18 years or older with a primary diagnosis of schizophrenia spectrum disorders who met the inclusion and exclusion criteria. Patients were categorized into the AOM or PP1M groups based on the type of long-acting injectable initiated. The dataset included information on age, sex, diagnosis as classified by KCD (F20 for main diagnosis of schizophrenia and F21, F22, F23, F24, F25, F28, and F29 for schizophrenia spectrum disorders, route of treatment, EPS related AEs, comorbid medical disorders, and the number of psychotropics. Supplementary Table 1 (available online) and Supplementary Figure 1 (available online) show the inclusion and exclusion criteria, and a flow diagram for acquisition and collection of data from the HIRA.

Index drugs were defined as follows: 1) AOM: the starting dose can be administered by following one of two regimens (single injection start or two injection start [TIS]) in routine practice. If two AOMs are claimed on the first administration date according to the TIS regimen, they were counted as one administration; 2) PP1M: PP1M indicates the once-monthly formulation of paliperidone palmitate only. If a switch to paliperidone palmitate 3-monthly (PP3M) was collected within 12 months of PP1M administration, it was excluded from the analysis.

The index period was defined as 3 months prior to the first use of the two LAIs initiation, to determine whether a diagnosis of schizophrenia spectrum disorders were fully established as the main diagnosis and to identify any psychotropics used in the previous 3 months.

The baseline was defined as the time of the first LAI administration for patients who received at least one dose. Study follow-up was defined as the 12-month period after baseline, during which continuous information was collected.

Information on concomitant medications such as APs, antidepressants, benzodiazepines, antiparkinsonian drugs, and beta-blockers prescribed with the two LAIs were also collected and they were only classified as a one group of psychotropics due to the policy of HIRA.

All-cause hospitalization was defined as an admission incurred after the first prescription of LAI during the study period.

The EPS-related AEs were defined as a diagnosis that was present at the baseline or newly developed after initiation of each LAI during the study period.

Primary and Secondary Endpoints

The primary endpoint was time to treatment discontinuation within 6 months of LAI initiation. The secondary endpoints were the estimated treatment continuation rates and changes in concomitant psychotropic medications over 12 months for each LAI.

Safety Evaluation

The safety evaluation was based on the occurrence of all-cause hospitalization and newly developed EPS-related AEs during the study follow-up period.

Statistics

Baseline demographics were summarized using means with standard deviations for continuous variables and frequencies with percentages for categorical variables. To evaluate differences between the two LAI groups at baseline, independent two-sample ttests were performed for continuous data, and chi-square tests were utilized for categorical data.

The definition of discontinuation was as follows: 1) switching to a different LAI; 2) switching to oral APs, and 3) a period without treatment of more than 60 days (or two consecutive injections).

Kaplan-Meier survival curves were constructed to estimate treatment continuation rates over time for each LAI group. The median time to event (50% survival probability) with its corresponding 95% confidence interval (CI) was calculated for both groups, and differences in survival distributions were assessed using the log-rank test. Cox proportional hazards regression models were used to determine hazard ratios (HRs) along with 95% CIs for the risk of treatment discontinuation. In the multivariable models, age, sex, number of concomitant psychotropics, and baseline EPSs were controlled as covariates.

A 3-month landmark analysis was performed to evaluate time-dependent differences in treatment discontinuation. Patients who remained on treatment at 3 months were included, and subsequent discontinuation between 3 and 12 months was analyzed using Cox proportional hazards models.

Propensity score matching (PSM) analysis was utilized to ensure that the covariates of interest were balanced between the two LAIs groups. Standardized mean differences were calculated to confirm that the covariates of interest were balanced between the two LAIs.

All statistical tests were two-sided, and a two-sided p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All data analyses were performed using SAS Enterprise Guide 7.15 (SAS Institute Inc.). The present study was approved by the Institutional Review Boards of Bucheon St. Mary’s Hospital and Keimyung University (IRB No. HC25ZASI0013 and 40525-202506-HR-026-01, respectively). The requirement for informed consent was waived by the IRB since the HIRA database provides anonymous and fully de-identified data.

RESULTS

Data Collection from the Health Insurance Review and Assessment Service

From the dataset collected from HIRA, a total of 34,953 de-identified cases were provided and 15,572 cases met the inclusion and exclusion criteria, eventually 6,454 for AOM group and 9,118 for PP1M were analyzed in the study.

Study Population and Baseline Characteristics

At baseline, patients receiving PP1M were older than those receiving AOM (mean age 44.26 vs. 39.88 years, p < 0.0001). The proportion of males was higher in the PP1M group (49.53% vs. 35.25%, p < 0.0001). The proportion of patients receiving concomitant psychotropic medications was 90.56% in the AOM group and 84.60% in the PP1M group (p < 0.0001). Baseline EPS-related AEs were not significantly different between AOM and PP1M groups (44.61% vs. 45.60%, p = 0.2195) (Table 1).

Treatment Continuation

6-month outcomes

Within 6 months, discontinuation occurred in 45.46% of the AOM group and 56.15% of the PP1M group. Kaplan–Meier analysis demonstrated a significantly higher continuation rate in AOM group than in PP1M group (log-rank p < 0.0001) (Fig. 3). The median time to discontinuation was not reached in AOM group, whereas it was 4.93 months (95% CI, 4.83−5.10) in PP1M group. In Cox proportional hazards analysis, PP1M was associated with a significantly higher risk of discontinuation compared with AOM (unadjusted HR 1.303, 95% CI 1.245−1.364; adjusted HR 1.301, 95% CI 1.242−1.363; p < 0.0001, respectively; Supplementary Tables 2, 3; available online) after adjustment for age, sex, number of psychotropics, and EPS-related AEs.

12-month outcomes

At 12 months, discontinuation occurred in 62.74% of AOM group and 74.08% of PP1M group. Kaplan–Meier analysis demonstrated a significantly higher continuation rate in AOM group than in PP1M group (log-rank p < 0.0001) (Fig. 3). Median time to discontinuation was significantly longer in AOM group (7.13 months; 95% CI, 6.77−7.47) than in PP1M group (4.90 months; 95% CI, 4.80−5.07) (log-rank p < 0.0001). Unadjusted and adjusted Cox regression analyses showed that PP1M group was associated with a higher discontinuation risk compared with AOM group (unadjusted HR 1.323, 95% CI 1.273−1.376; adjusted HR 1.322, 95% CI 1.270−1.376; p < 0.0001, respectively; Supplementary Tables 2, 3; available online).

Discontinuation Patterns

At 6 months, oral AP switching was the predominant post-discontinuation strategy, occurring more frequently in PP1M group (80.37% vs. 70.52%). Treatment gaps ≥ 60 days were more common in AOM group (20.72% vs. 12.95%) and switching to another LAI was slightly more frequent in AOM group (8.76% vs. 6.68%). The detailed treatment discontinuation patterns at 6 months is described in Table 2.

At 12 months, oral AP switching remained the predominant post-discontinuation strategy, occurring more frequently in PP1M group (77.76% vs. 66.14%). Treatment gaps ≥ 60 days were more common in AOM group (26.35% vs. 15.63%), whereas switching to another LAI did not differ significantly between groups (7.51% vs. 6.60%). The detailed treatment discontinuation patterns at 12 months is described in Table 3.

Changes in Concomitant Psychotropics Use over 12 Months

The mean number of concomitant psychotropics decreased over time in both groups (AOM: 3.55 to 2.41; PP1M: 3.37 to 2.46). However, there were no statistically significant differences between groups in the number of concomitant psychotropics at baseline, 6 months, or 12 months (p > 0.05). The detailed changes of psychotropic during the observation are described in Table 4.

Hospitalization

All-cause hospitalization rates were consistently higher in PP1M group than in AOM group at both 6 months (60.43% vs. 52.25%) and 12 months (61.63% vs. 53.69%). The mean number of hospitalizations per patient was also numerically higher in the PP1M group at both time points (Supplementary Table 4; available online).

Extrapyramidal Symptoms

Overall, EPS-related AEs reported during follow-up were slightly more frequent in the PP1M group at both 6 months (52.51% vs. 50.76%) and 12 months (53.66% vs. 52.34%), compared to those in the AOM group. Among patients without baseline EPS, newly developed EPS occurred in 6.15% vs. 6.91% at 6 months and 7.73% vs. 8.06% at 12 months (AOM vs. PP1M), respectively (Supplementary Table 5; available online).

3-month Landmark Analysis

In the 0−3 month interval, the incidence of treatment discontinuation was higher in PP1M group than in AOM group (31.48% vs. 29.01%). The median time to event was not reached in either group within the 3-month follow-up period; however, a statistically significant difference between groups was observed (p = 0.0047). After adjustment for sex, age, number of psychotropics, and presence of EPS, PP1M group showed a significantly higher risk of treatment discontinuation compared to AOM group (adjusted HR = 1.100, p = 0.0019) (Supplementary Tables 2, 3; available online).

Among patients who maintained treatment up to 3 months, the 3−12 month interval analysis also showed that PP1M group had a significantly higher risk of treatment discontinuation compared to AOM group (adjusted HR 1.536, 95% CI 1.454−1.622, p < 0.0001) (Supplementary Table 6; available online).

Propensity Score Matching Analysis

In the PSM cohort, results were consistent with the primary analysis. At 6 (46.99% vs. 56.78%) and 12 months (64.29% vs. 74.16%), discontinuation rates remained higher in PP1M group than in AOM group (all p < 0.0001), corresponding to an absolute difference of approximately 10%. The HR was similar to that observed in the overall cohort (HRs 1.261 and 1.270, respectively, all p < 0.0001), indicating that the association remained after balancing baseline characteristics (Supplementary Table 7; available online).

DISCUSSION

To our knowledge, the present study is the first large-scale, nationwide, claims-based direct comparison for treatment continuation between AOM and PP1M. According to the results, the treatment discontinuation rate and the risk at 6 (HR = 1.301) and 12 months (HR = 1.322) were significantly lower in AOM than in PP1M, with absolute differences of approximately 10%. In addition, all-cause hospitalizations and newly developed EPS-related AEs were also numerically lower in AOM group than in PP1M group. Moreover, these observed differences in treatment continuation between AOM and PP1M were maintained consistently across multiple analytic approaches, including multivariable adjustment, PSM, and 3-month landmark analysis.

According to a previous Korean study on LAIs for schizophrenia using data from HIRA [34], the estimated continuation rate for PP1M group was 57.5% at 6 months, whereas LAI haloperidol and LAI risperidone groups had continuation rates of 36.8% and 34.5% at 6 months, respectively. In addition, PP1M subgroup with an observation period of more than 180 days showed a continuation rate of 42.5%, while 18.2% for LAI haloperidol and 1.0% for LAI risperidone. Furthermore, the LAI haloperidol and LAI risperidone groups showed more than 25% discontinuation in the first 2 months after initiation, showing high early discontinuation rates even in LAIs treatment. The findings are generally comparable to our findings, supporting the robust reliability and validation of HIRA data. One interesting point is the higher continuation rate of PP1M at 6 months compared to our finding by approximately 14% difference though. Indeed, the diagnosis codes in the claimed data may be different from the actually confirmed clinical diagnosis and thus if the diagnosis codes are expanded and applied broader, patients with short-term therapy in the initial and differential diagnosis stages and those with a high possibility of a change in diagnosis could be included in the data analysis, which may strongly impact the rate of treatment continuation. In fact, the previous HIRA study [34] confined the inclusion diagnosis code to F20 only (schizophrenia), which may have resulted in a relatively high continuation rate (57.5%) at 6 months due to a population with a definite diagnosis and high likelihood of long-term treatment. However, our study included expanded F codes for various schizophrenia spectrum disorders beyond F20, which may explain the difference between the previous HIRA study and the present findings.

Meanwhile, direct comparisons on the effectiveness/acceptability and continuation between individual LAI and oral AP have been relatively established in currently available RCTs [22,35], real-world studies [23,36], and metaanalyses [21], with the exception of individual comparisons of LAIs. Furthermore, a number of meta-analyses with currently existing LAIs have also shown similar trends in indirect comparison with oral APs, however, it has also failed to reach consistent findings on the meaningful differences in treatment continuation between individual LAIs. Direct head-to-head comparison studies on treatment discontinuation between AOM and PP1M are lacking today, only a small number of clinical studies have directly or indirectly compared treatment discontinuation between AOM and PP1M. In a previous indirect comparison [37], the overall early discontinuation was not different between AOM and PP1M, whereas AOM showed a significantly lower early discontinuation due to lack of efficacy than PP1M (odds ratio [OR]: 0.394). According to the recent STAR Network observational study (32 Italian centers) [38], the overall discontinuation rate at 12 months was 39.3% for all LAIs, with 33.9% for PP1M and 35.4% for AOM without statistical difference. In a recent Texas Medicaid data analysis [39], the 12 months continuation rates were 44.3% for AOM and 41.3% for PP1M, respectively. In the QUALIFY study (28 weeks) [40], the all-cause (29.7% vs. 36.7%) and AEs- related discontinuation (11.1% vs. 19.7%) rates were numerically lower in the AOM compared to PP1M and such trend was also replicated in a 24-week extension study [41]. Based on previous clinical studies, the difference in treatment continuation between AOM and PP1M was not significant overall, therefore the overall continuation of the two LAIs has been considered to be at a similar level.

In this respect, our study results are noteworthy, as they differ from those of previous research findings. The discontinuation rates at 6 months and 12 months were significantly different between AOM and PP1M, where the patients treated with AOM showed lower discontinuation rates than those treated with PP1M by approximately 10% difference, and the risk of PP1M discontinuation was approximately 30% higher than that of AOM in terms of time-to-event perspective. From an absolute risk perspective, the 12-month difference may be translated to approximately one additional patient maintaining LAI treatment for every 9 patients treated with AOM instead of PP1M. This discrepancy may be explained by several factors. First, most previous studies have been relatively small scale or performed in single institution, making it difficult to detect absolute differences of 5−10%, while our study was based on a large and nationwide cohort, providing sufficient statistical power. RCTs include highly selective subjects and strictly monitor treatment adherence, however, various factors including physicians’ clinical preference, patient/family involvement, perception of AEs, and access to medical care are highly complicated in real-world data. National health systems, insurance structures, outpatient care systems, medical care delivery system, and family intervention structures may also influence LAI continuation across countries and individual institutions. Our study based on the nationwide data from Korea may reflect a different context compared to previous Western studies. Drug selection may be based on clinical judgment along with patients’ involvement, and underlying factors such as cultural differences, disease severity, or different level of social support may have had hidden influence. In line with this context, patients with AOM in the present study were also younger and included a higher proportion of females compared with those with PP1M, likely reflecting clinical preference and efforts to minimize AEs. Finally, pharmacologically unique differences between AOM and PP1M may partially explain differences in treatment continuation. Indeed, AOM is a partial agonist at both dopamine D2 and serotonin 5-HT1A receptors and an antagonist at 5-HT2A receptors and therefore tends to less likely cause EPSs, metabolic AEs or sexual dysfunctions compared to other APs as well as shown additional effects on mood regulation and cognition, which may potentially influence long-term continuation in routine practice. For instance, the QUALIFY study (28 weeks) [40] reported significant improvement in Quality of Life Scale (QLS) total score in AOM group compared with PP1M group (+7.47 vs. +2.80, p = 0.036), and that the difference was particularly pronounced in patients under 35 years of age (+10.7 points, p = 0.006). The metabolic and prolactin-related AEs were reported more frequently in the PP1M group, while there was no significant difference in Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale (PANSS) total score reduction between the two groups. The fact that the difference was particularly significant in younger patients has important clinical implications for establishing long-term treatment strategies. In addition, treatment-emergent AEs that occurred during the maintenance phase were more frequent in the PP1M group than in the AOM group. In the study, AEs were the most common reason for treatment discontinuation, with 27 of 137 patients (19.7%) in the PP1M group and 16 of 144 patients (11.1%) in the AOM group. The all-cause discontinuation rate was also numerically lower in AOM group than in PP1M group. This trend was consistently replicated in a 24-week extension study [41], where AOM administered for up to 48 weeks improved the QLS total score by 11 points, which was more than twice the minimal clinically important difference. This proposes that AOM may have clinically potential benefits over PP1M in terms of functional recovery and QoL. Furthermore, in a post-hoc analysis of QUALIFY study [42], AOM 400 mg was reported to significantly reduce the risk of sexual dysfunction by approximately 71% compared to PP1M (OR = 0.29, p = 0.0012). This effect was consistently observed in men, women, and younger patients aged 18−35 years, with the greatest risk reduction observed in younger patients, potentially indicating that AOM 400 mg may have a potential advantage over PP1M in terms of sexual side effects. In this context, it is well-known that the functional recovery and AEs are closely related to long-term prognosis and a crucial factor that can substantially influence the patient’s treatment attitude and persistence in the treatment of schizophrenia. In support with these previous finding, our data also indicated that AOM was more utilized in younger female population rather than PP1M.

A recent systematic review and meta-regression study [43] investigating global and regional trends from the 1970s to 2009 has shown that the median AP polypharmacy (APP) rate was 19.6%. According to the study, APP was highly prevalent and varying by continents; approximately 55.5% in Asia, while it was only 2% in Oceania, although the trend has been decreasing from 1980s to 2000s. Interestingly a previous study using the HIRA database also reported that up to 90% of patients received oral APs during the first 3 months of LAIs treatment and approximately 35% of the patients also received concomitant (antidepressants and anxiolytics) [44]. Similarly, the present study also investigated the changes of psychotropics combination, where concomitant psychotropics at baseline were prescribed in 90.6% of AOM group and 84.6% of PP1M group, with a mean of 3.55 and 3.37 medications, respectively, which was very similar in the previous report [44]. The proportion decreased substantially over time, to 36.8% vs. 31.3% at 6 months and 22.6% vs. 18.3% at 12 months, respectively, in both LAIs. The mean number of concomitant psychotropics also declined in both AOM and PP1M groups (AOM: 3.55 to 2.41; PP1M: 3.37 to 2.46). Overall, our findings are consistent with previous evidence using HIRA and other clinical studies [45] which showed a high rate of psychotropics combination at baseline with decreasing trend over time [34].

In the present study, switching to oral APs accounted for the highest rates of discontinuation. In most cases, the subsequent medication belonged to the same class as the original LAI, similar to trends observed in prior Medicaid-based studies [44]. This suggests that rather than a complete failure of the drug itself, it may possibly reflect a preference for the administration method, accessibility issues, or a short-term AEs management strategy in routine practice. In particular, the relatively higher rate of event for lost to follow-up of 60 days or more in AOM group may reflect that the actual clinical decision-making process should be complex, beyond a simple comparison of treatment continuation. In addition, this may implement underlying differences in medication management strategies rather than actual treatment failure. Patients who discontinued PP1M were more likely to be immediately switched to oral AP, whereas those who discontinued AOM may have undergone follow-up or delayed reintroduction. Furthermore, claimed data may not fully distinguish between actual treatment discontinuation and simple claims delays or unreported medical use. Residual differences in patient characteristics may also have influenced these results.

Hospitalization rates are used as a proxy outcome for actual clinical deterioration or relapse [46]. The numerically lower hospitalization rates of 8% in AOM group than in PP1M suggests a possible association with increased treatment continuation. However, hospitalization is also influenced by many clinical factors, including disease severity, social support system, family involvement, institutional policy, and access to medical care, hence careful consideration should be taken for interpreting this as a single drug effect. Although still debated, beneficial effects of AOM compared with PP1M on hospitalization and length of admission have been proposed [47-49].

Overall, given the difference in pharmacological characteristics of the two LAIs, a lower incidence of EPS-related AEs was expected in AOM compared with PP1M [50], However, in the present study, the incidence of new EPS was 6.15% vs. 6.91% at 6 months and 7.73% vs. 8.06% at 12 months, with no significant difference between AOM and PP1M, respectively. The overall difference in EPS incidence between AOM and PP1M was very small and unlikely to be clinically significant. Furthermore, the two LAIs can be interpreted as having similar EPS safety profiles. Such similar trend toward EPS-related AEs between AOM and PP1M was also reported in the previous QUALIFY study (5.0% vs. 4.6%) [40] and systematic meta-analysis (14.3% vs. 16.3%) [50].

The greatest strength of this study is that we directly compared the treatment continuation rate between AOM and PP1M using a large-scale nationwide claims data which substantially reflect real-world clinical information. In addition, the PSM analysis support the robustness and consistency of our findings. Finally, the 3-month landmark analysis further clarified the temporal pattern of treatment discontinuation between the two LAIs. While the difference during the first three months was modest (HR 1.10), a substantially larger difference appeared during the 3−12-month maintenance phase (HR 1.54), corresponding to a 54% higher discontinuation risk with PP1M. This pattern suggests that difference in treatment continuation may develop progressively during a longer-term maintenance period rather than immediately after treatment initiation.

However, the present study has many clear shortcomings and thereby cautions should be taken in interpretation and generalization of the study results. This is due to inherent pitfalls of claims-based research, such as lack of or inadequate information on clinical severity (accurate clinical assessments using standard rating scales such as the PANSS and Clinical Global Impression scores), the potential for residual confounders, lack of psychosocial factors, the reliance on claims-based algorithms for defining discontinuation such as operational criteria on inclusion and exclusion, the complexity of data, inadequate drug information and clinical factors due to limited governmental policy of data source, the limitations in distinguishing between adherence and persistence, and the lack of causality inference. The exclusion of patients transitioned to PP3M may also limit generalizability, as such patients could represent a more clinically stable or adherent subgroup in routine practice. A further limitation is that inclusion of broader schizophrenia spectrum diagnoses beyond F20 may introduce clinical heterogeneity, which could potentially influence treatment patterns and continuation outcomes in routine practice. Finally, this study utilized only Korean data. Given the substantial differences in national incentive strategies and costs associated with the application of LAIs in routine practice, these findings should be also cross-validated with studies from other countries.

In conclusion, the present study found that AOM treatment was associated with a significantly lower risk of treatment discontinuation than PP1M at 6 months and 12 months, based on the nationwide real-world data. The 10% absolute difference in 12 months treatment continuation may potentially suggest a clinically meaningful separation between the two LAIs in routine practice; however, future multinational cohort studies and direct comparative clinical trials are strongly needed to elucidate the cross-validation and underlying mechanisms of such differences.

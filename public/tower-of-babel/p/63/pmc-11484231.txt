Agreement between a new fully automatic ocular biometer based on optical low-coherence reflectometry and an optical biometer based on Scheimpflug imaging combined with partial coherence interferometry
Authors: Rui Ning, Huilin Xu, Zheng Li, Jinjin Yu, Shuoyu Xu, Chak Seng Lei, Yiran Wang, Giacomo Savini, Domenico Schiano-Lomoriello, Xingtao Zhou, Jinhai Huang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTo analyze the difference and agreement between measurements obtained by a new fully automatic optical biometer, the SW-9000 μm Plus, based on optical low-coherence reflectometry (OLCR) and a commonly used optical biometer (Pentacam AXL) based on Scheimpflug imaging with partial coherence interferometry (PCI).MethodsThe central corneal thickness (CCT), anterior chamber depth (ACD, from epithelium to anterior lens surface), lens thickness (LT), mean keratometry (Km), corneal astigmatism, corneal diameter (CD), pupil diameter (PD), and axial length (AL) of 74 eyes (from 74 healthy subjects) were measured using the SW-9000 μm Plus and the Pentacam AXL to determine the agreement. Double angle plots were used for astigmatism vector analysis. Bland–Altman and 95% limits of agreement (LoA) were calculated.ResultsStatistically significant differences were detected for all parameters but J0 vector. The Bland–Altman analysis of AL, CCT, ACD, Km, CD, J0 and J45 indicated a high level of agreement between the two devices. Among AL, CCT, ACD, Km, J0, J45, CD, and PD, the 95% LoA ranged from -0.07 to 0.05 mm, -9.67 to 7.34 mm, -0.11 to 0.04 mm, -0.25 to 0.50 D, -0.22 to 0.20 D, -0.15 to 0.20 D, -0.23 to 0.35 mm and 1.55 to 3.77 mm, respectively.ConclusionsThe measurements of AL, CCT, ACD, Km, corneal astigmatism, and CD showed a narrow LoA and may be used interchangeably in healthy subjects between the new OLCR optical biometer and the Scheimpflug/PCI biometer; however, a poor agreement was noted for PD values.

Background

Accurate ocular biometry helps clinicians in measuring the ocular parameters for clinical diagnosis and treatment. Since 2009, optical low coherence reflectometry (OLCR) has been able to provide accurate and valid biometry, and therefore, IOL power calculations for cataract patients [1–3]. The OLCR technology has the advantage of reducing signal loss and light intensity through fiber optic conduction. As a result, scattered light prevents the overlapping of signals and separates the various interfaces [4]. All measurements were reproducible, as demonstrated previously [1, 5]. Combined with the fully automatic technology, the SW-9000 μm Plus (SW, Tianjin, China) is a newly developed device measuring axial length (AL) based on the OLCR principle. All axial ocular parameters, including central corneal thickness (CCT), anterior chamber depth (ACD), and lens thickness (LT), can be measured simultaneously, and high-resolution infrared ocular surface imaging is used for measuring the corneal curvature, pupil diameter (PD), and corneal diameter (CD).

Partial coherence interferometry (PCI) is widely used for ocular biometric parameters since it was introduced in 1999 [1]. The Pentacam AXL (Oculus, Wetzler, Germany), which has been used for several years, is based on Scheimpflug imaging and PCI and can measure AL and anterior ocular segment parameters accurately and precisely [6–8].

The present study aimed to compare the AL and anterior segment parameters obtained from the OLCR device to those of Scheimpflug camera combined PCI, in order to determine whether they could be used interchangeably in clinical practice.

Methods

Subjects

This prospective study enrolled subjects admitted to the Eye and ENT Hospital of Fudan University (Shanghai, China) for preoperative examination of refractive surgery. The subjects were fully informed of the purpose of the study, and it was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Eye and ENT Hospital of Fudan University and adhered to the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki. Informed consent was obtained from all subjects, and a standard ophthalmological examination was conducted before enrollment. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Infectious disorders such as keratitis and conjunctivitis, (2) Patients diagnosed with severe dry eye, pterygium, glaucoma, and vitreoretinal lesions, such as retinal detachment and macular edema, (3) history of ocular injuries or surgeries, (4) wearing soft contacts for < 2 weeks or rigid gas permeable contact lenses for < 4 weeks, and (5) lack of cooperation.

Measurement device

SW-9000 plus

The SW-9000 Plus is a newly available fully automatic optical biometer. It can measure AL, CCT, ACD, and LT based on OLCR and has an axial resolution of 0.01 mm. The corneal curvature, PD, and CD were measured using high-resolution infrared imaging systems. About 15s are required to complete binocular measurements and collect the biological data of all the eight parameters with a Super Luminescent Diode (SLD) at 820 nm. The SW-9000 μm Plus is based on the reflection of 32 light points projected in two mires (diameter 1.65 mm and 2.3 mm) on the anterior corneal surface; the corresponding light points were measured and analyzed to obtain data on the radius of the curvature of the annular surface, providing the value of corneal curvature and power (using the above-mentioned 1.3375 index).

Pentacam AXL

During one measurement, the Pentacam AXL combines a rotating Scheimpflug camera and PCI to obtain the corneal curvature and height, corneal thickness, ACD, CD, and AL. The corneal curvature was calculated from the corneal height map using 25–100 Scheimpflug tomography images. The keratometric index of 1.3375 was used to calculate the corneal power from the mean radius of the anterior curvature around the corneal apex. The AL measurement was obtained by PCI at 780 nm. The 138,000 data points were captured in 2s, and 25 high-resolution meridional images of the anterior segment were obtained from blue light at 475 nm. The transverse resolution of the Scheimpflug camera is 9.3 μm, and the axial resolution of the PCI is 0.01 mm.

Measurement procedure

First, the subjects underwent a comprehensive ophthalmic examination. Both instruments were used by one skilled operator, and only the right eye was subjected to measurements. All subjects were required to take three consecutive measurements with the SW-9000 μm Plus and Pentacam AXL in a dim room; the order of measurement was random between the two instruments. Only good-quality scans were chosen for analysis. All measurements were conducted between 9:00 and 17:00 to minimize the errors caused by diurnal changes in the corneal shape and thickness [9]. The whole measurement process was 10 min. The calibration of the instruments was completed before measurements. The examiner adjusted the elevator and mandibular rest while the forehead was close to the forehead strip to maintain the correct posture. The outer canthus was aligned with the inscribed line on the column. The subjects were instructed to look at the central fixation point of the instrument and open their eyes as wide as possible. Before starting the measurement, the subjects were informed to blink their eyes quickly to ensure the integrity of the tear film.

Astigmatism analysis

The vector decomposition was measured using the Alpins method and presented as double-angle plots, wherein the centroid is the vectorial center of the data. One way to measure the spread of the data around a centroid is to calculate the standard deviation (SD). The dataset consisted of 95% observations (N) within the 95% confidence ellipse. A small ellipse represents 95% confidence ellipse boundary of the centroid. For normal distribution, this is often referred to as 1.96-fold of the standard error of the mean, analogous to a 95% confidence interval in univariate analysis [10–13].

Thibos et al. published a power vector method for describing and statistically analyzing astigmatism based on Fourier analysis in 1997 [14], which decomposed astigmatism into J0 (Jackson crossed column power at 0°) and J45 (Jackson crossed column power at 45°) using quantitative and graphical methods [15, 16].

Statistical analyses

Statistical analyses were performed using MedCalc (version 19.8; MedCalc Software Inc., Mariakerke, Belgium) and Excel (version 365; Microsoft, Washington, USA) software, and the results were expressed as mean ± SD. The average of the three measurements obtained from each device was utilized in the statistical analysis. Kolmogorov–Smirnov test was used to assess the normal distribution of the data (P > 0.05). Paired t-test was used to clarify whether there was a statistical difference between the two devices, and p-values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. Bland–Altman plots compared the agreement between SW-9000 μm Plus and Pentacam AXL, and 95% limits of agreement (95% LoA) were calculated. The vertical coordinate is the mean difference between the two instruments, and the horizontal coordinate is the mean of the results of the two instruments, 95% LoA is the mean difference between the two instruments ± 1.96 × SD [17].

For a 0.15 confidence level in the estimate when performing three measurements, a minimum sample size of 43 eyes was calculated based on a previous study [18].

Results

A total of 74 eyes from 74 healthy subjects (35 males and 39 females) were included in this study; the age of the cohort was 26.30 ± 6.28 (range, 18–43) years. Regarding refraction, the mean spherical power was -5.32 ± 2.07 D, and the mean astigmatism power was -1.08 ± 0.77 D.

Table 1 show the mean ± SD and the min and max values of SW-9000 μm Plus and Pentacam AXL. Table 2and Fig. 1 show the difference between the two devices. Statistically significant differences were detected for all parameters except J0. Figure 1 indicates similar shapes and areas between the two devices; the measurements by SW-9000 μm Plus were slightly higher than those by Pentacam AXL for AL, CCT, Km, and CD values. The PD value had the largest difference in shape and area. Figures 2 and 3 show the Bland–Altman values of AL, CCT, ACD, Km, J0 and J45, revealing a high level of agreement between the two devices . For astigmatism analysis, the centroid of SW-9000 μm Plus was 1.33D@89°±0.83 D, and the mean absolute value was 1.41 ± 0.67 D. Pentacam AXL has similar results, with centroid of 1.31D@90°±0.77 D, and mean absolute value was 1.37 ± 0.64 D (Fig. 5). Figure 6 shows the double angle of the difference in astigmatism measurement; the mean vector difference was small with 0.05D@145°±0.28 D and the mean absolute difference was 0.25 ± 0.13 D.

Discussion

The precision of any instrument measurements is essential in clinical diagnosis and treatment. This is the first study evaluating the differences and agreement between the newly developed SW-9000 μm Plus and the Pentacam AXL in terms of AL and anterior segment parameters.

The accurate measurement of AL has been critical for refractive cataract surgery and myopia follow-up. A study by Ruiz-Mesa et al. demonstrated that the mean difference of AL between an OLCR-based biometer (Lenstar) and the Scheimpflug/PCI-based biometer was 0.08 mm, and the 95% LoA was -1.07 to 0.91 mm, which was wider than the current results [7]. Previous studies compared PCI-based devices with OLCR-based devices and concluded that the measurements could not be interchanged, with 95% LoA for AL of -0.141 to 0.142 mm and -0.080 to 0.152 mm, respectively [5, 13, 19]. Recently, the AL measured with Revo (Optopol Technologies, Zawiercie, Poland) was 0.10 ± 0.04 mm higher than that with Pentacam AXL (24.08 ± 1.13 mm and 23.98 ± 1.13 mm, respectively; p < 0.0001), indicating a difference in the calculated IOL power obtained after the calculation. These results obtained with both devices cannot be considered interchangeably [20]. The difference between these devices could be attributed to the different optical wavelengths or detection of structural borders or the type of population included in the study. The current results showed that the mean difference between SW-9000 μm Plus and Pentacam AXL was -0.01 ± 0.03 mm for AL measurements and the LoA range between -0.07 and 0.05 mm, indicating a high level of agreement.

Thus, it is essential to elucidate the thickness of the cornea before undergoing cornea refractive surgery. Ruiz-Mesa et al. [7] reported a high correlation (r = 0.992) of CCT values with Pentacam and Lenstar, 525.8 ± 37.1 μm and 529.3 ± 37.1 μm, respectively, in a normal group. However, the 95% LoA was wide, ranging between -23.99 and 15.91 μm, which implied poor interchangeability. Similar results were obtained by Soleimani et al. [21] and Zhao et al. [5], who reported 95% LoA ranging from -19.75 to 25.87 μm and -22.76 to 18.83 μm, suggesting that CCT values are not interchangeable for the Scheimpflug device and the OLCR device. The CCT readings with the OLCR device were overall smaller compared to the Scheimpflug/PCI device. The differences may be related to the implementation of various measurement technique principles, with the Scheimpflug camera measuring corneal thickness, including the tear film. In a study by O’Donell et al., the Pentacam produced readings 15.53 μm above or 24.40 μm below those obtained with the OLCR-based Lenstar [22]. Similarly, Huang et al. [23] reported smaller CCT measurement differences with the OLCR-based Lenstar compared to Scheimpflug-based Pentacam and a narrower 95% LoA, showing that both devices are interchangeable. While the CCT showed a statistically significant difference between the two devices (p < 0.05), such difference was very small (only -1.17 ± 4.34 μm), and the 95% LoAs were narrow (from -9.67 to 7.34 μm) in the current study, thus highlighting high agreement and interchangeability.

Accurate ACD measurement is a key parameter for IOL power calculation, diagnosis of glaucoma, and phakic IOLs for preoperative evaluation. According to the current findings, the values of ACD were comparable between the Scheimpflug/PCI device and the OLCR device with a marked agreement, as their mean differences and 95% LoA were -0.03 ± 0.04 mm and -0.11 to 0.04 mm, respectively. These results agree with the previous findings, wherein the mean difference was 0.06 ± 0.11 mm between the Scheimpflug imaging device and the OLCR device, and the 95% LoA range was -0.16 ± 0.28 mm [21]. Also, a mean difference of 0.09 mm was detected between the OLCR device and the Scheimpflug imaging in ACD, and the 95% LoA range was 0.23 ± 0.05 mm in the study by Uçakhan et al. [24] Compared to other comparative results of OLCR-based Lenstar and Scheimpflug/PCI-based Pentacam device [19], the Pentacam provides higher values of ACD.

In the current study, we found statistical significance but not clinically significant differences in Km measured by the two devices (p < 0.001), and the 95% LoA ranged from -0.25 to 0.50 D, indicating that the Km values are interchangeable between the two devices. Previous studies have also shown similar LoAs between the Scheimpflug/PCI device and the Lenstar OLCR device for K values, with 0.14 to -0.49 for normal subjects [5, 19, 25]. The Lenstar measurements of corneal astigmatism have consistently provided high precision (intraclass correlation coefficient > 0.9) [26]. Similar values of J0 and J45 were obtained for the OLCR device (TRT: J0 = 0.19D, J45 = 0.14D) [27]. Kim et al. indicated a significant difference in J0, but no significant differences were noted in J45 by optical low‑coherence interferometry and Scheimpflug/PCI device [2], while the 95% LoA ranged from -0.13 to 0.02 and -0.02 to 0.05 in cataract eyes. Consistent with the current findings, a high agreement was confirmed between the two devices in measuring J0 and J45. Ventura et al. compared the agreement among Cassini, Humphrey Atlas 9000, and Lenstar for the detection of corneal refractive power and corneal astigmatism in normal eyes [27]. A total of 32 eyes with astigmatism were analyzed by power vector analysis, and all devices provided highly repeatable astigmatism measurements, except for the Humphrey Atlas 9000 correlation J45 (ICC = 0.721). This device obtained similar Km (p < 0.005), and the magnitude of astigmatism and the mean values of J0 and J45 were similar between Cassini and Humphrey Atlas 9000. The Bland–Altman plot showed a wide distribution for all variables analyzed, which led to the possibility of interchangeability between them for assessing astigmatism [27] (J0: -0.43 to 0.31, J45: -0.31 to 0.23). A narrower difference was observed in the current study, and the 95% LoA of J0 and J45 was 0.22 to 0.20 and − 0.15 to 0.20, respectively. The double-angle plots of the vector differences of the two instruments also indicated a good agreement with a centroid of 0.05D@145°±0.28 D, which is very close to zero, with most of the vector differences within 0.5 D.

The measurement of CD has become increasingly relevant with the development of refractive surgery. CD detection is critical for the selection of phakic IOLs’ size. The SW-9000 μm Plus had a lager mean CD (11.71 ± 0.37 mm), while the Pentacam had the smaller mean CD (11.65 ± 0.35 mm). In SW-9000 μm Plus, CD refers to the horizontal iris width (manufacturer guidelines), 25 images were captured from varying orientations, and the mean was calculated in Pentacam [28] because the cornea measures approximately 12 mm in the horizontal meridian and 11 mm in the vertical meridian in adults [29]. Similar results were obtained by Sen et al. [28]. The CD values measured with the OLCR device were higher than those measured with the Scheimpflug device, and a statistically significant difference was observed between the mean values of CD measured with the Lenstar and Pentacam in their study. The LoA was -0.09 to 0.64 mm in CD values, which was higher than -0.23 to 0.35 mm in the current study. Ang et al. showed that measurements of CD obtained from each device differed markedly, concluding that the Orbscan II, IOLMaster 700, Pentacam AXL, and Caliper were not interchangeable with respect to CD measurements [30].

Several factors may limit pupil shape and size but are not stable values. As a result, the devices will also have different PD measurements that affect their agreement. The difference between SW-9000 μm Plus and Pentacam AXL in mean PD was statistically different by 2.66 ± 0.57 mm and poor agreement was recorded. The SW-9000 μm Plus utilizes a SLED light source in the near-infrared range, which is invisible to the human eye and does not induce accommodative response, thereby preventing pupil constriction. In contrast, the Pentacam AXL employs a blue light source that leads to pupil size constriction and a consequent reduction in pupil diameter. Previous studies have indicated that the results do not recommend the interchangeable use for measurements of pupil parameters [31]. Li et al. found that the AL-Scan and Lenstar in cataract patients had PD measurements with poor agreement: 95% LoA was -0.73 to 1.21 mm [23]. Kanclerz et al. indicated a poor agreement between the Nidek ARK-1 pupil diameters and the Lenstar LS-900 (LoA: -0.8–2.6 mm) [32]. When assessing pupil size, the device illuminates the eye, which is likely to cause a difference in pupil size.

Nevertheless, the present study has the following limitation. The absence of patients with abnormal corneas, such as keratoconus, post-refractive surgery, and pellucid marginal degeneration (PMD). Therefore, further study is required to evaluate the impact of these devices on the measurement of such populations.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the AL, CCT, ACD, corneal curvature, corneal astigmatism, and CD measurements obtained from the new OLCR-based optical biometer and the Scheimpflug/PCI-based optical biometer demonstrated a high level of agreement in healthy subjects, indicating their interchangeability. However, PD values should not be used interchangeably.

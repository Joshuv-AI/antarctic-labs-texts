Protocol for dissection and organotypic explant culture of the neonatal and adult stria vascularis
Authors: Matsya Ruppari Thulasiram, Alain Dabdoub
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryComprehensive analysis of the stria vascularis (SV), an essential structure for hearing function, would greatly benefit from an in vitro experimental platform. Here, we present a protocol for dissection and isolation of the neonatal and adult mouse SV (focusing on post-natal day [P]0–35) from base to apex. We then detail procedures for explanting the SV on Matrigel-coated well dishes, followed by washing and fixing procedures.For complete details on the use and execution of the protocol, please refer to Thulasiram et al.1Subject areas: Cell culture, Model Organisms, Molecular Biology

Before you begin

The inner ear is located deep in the skull and embedded in one of the densest bones of the body, the temporal bone,2 making it challenging to access for experimentation. Approaches to investigate inner ear tissue, such as sensory epithelia or spiral ganglion neurons, have greatly advanced the field not only in understanding their development and disease mechanisms, but also in generating sophisticated in vitro explant models. The SV thus far has lacked such a system, creating a limitation for research.

The SV is essential for hearing, as it generates the energy for sensory cell function and maintains the blood-labyrinth barrier which protects the cochlea. It is comprised of three cellular layers: marginal, intermediate, and basal cells, as well as endothelial cells, pericytes, and macrophages that comprise the blood-labyrinth barrier.3,4,5,6 The function and integrity of the SV is negatively impacted in several hearing disorders, including age-related hearing loss, cisplatin-induced hearing loss, and Norrie disease, to name a few.7,8,9

Here we present a protocol to isolate the SV from the lateral wall and surrounding cochlear tissue from neonatal and mature mice and explant them for tissue culture for up to five days. This in vitro system can be utilized for pharmacological experimentation, gene therapy testing, proliferation assays, and more.

Innovation

Our method uniquely isolates the entire stria vascularis (SV) including all three cellular layers and associated vasculature across the full cochlear length from base to apex. This organotypic explant model preserves the tissue’s native architecture, enabling a more comprehensive platform to study the SV.

Unlike previous in vitro approaches that focus on isolated cell types or discrete regions of the SV, our approach captures the complexity of the intact tissue, facilitating integrated studies of cellular interactions. This advancement elevates SV research to the rigor and scope already established in sensory epithelia and spiral ganglion investigations, providing a foundational tool for mechanistic studies and therapeutic development targeting inner ear disorders.

Expected outcomes

This protocol described the isolation and culture of the neonatal and mature SV, incorporating all three cell layers and the blood-labyrinth barrier from base to apex. We observed that the P0 SV exhibits proliferation throughout its duration in culture, which decreases across postnatal stages.1 We showed that the culture platform is robust to pharmacological investigation, and that cultures can be used for downstream analyses including immunohistochemistry and reverse transcription quantitative real-time PCR.1

Limitations

This protocol cultured the SV with the basal cell layer attached to the coverslip while the marginal cell layer is exposed to the media. This was to simulate the orientation of the SV in the cochlea where the basal cells are connected to the spiral ligament fibrocytes, and the marginal cells are facing the endolymphatic fluid. While the SV can be cultured in the opposite orientation, expected results may vary. Additionally, standard media was used to culture the SV to accommodate for the different cell layers, and therefore, no specific factors have been incorporated which support each individual cell type. Future work may focus on optimizing media components to account for this. Finally, this protocol used Matrigel to culture the SV, therefore it is unclear whether cultures may vary if plated on an alternative extracellular matrix source.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

The SV breaks or tears. The SV is very thin and can therefore easily be damaged. At more mature ages, the tissue becomes more susceptible to mechanical damage.

Potential solution

While dissecting, reduce the handling of the SV by removing the spiral ligament away from the SV rather than pulling the SV off the spiral ligament. Grasp the SV at one fixed location at the base and pull the spiral ligament. This prevents excessive tugging, stretching, or breaking of the SV. Alternate between this strategy and the strategy outlined in the methods to achieve the best results.

Problem 2

The SV detaches from the culture dishes during culturing. It is possible for the SV to lift off the Matrigel during media administration (step 22) even though it appears attached.

Potential solution

•Detachment can happen for several reasons and can be resolved in a few different ways.

Detachment can happen for several reasons and can be resolved in a few different ways.

•The Matrigel has not properly coated the well. While the minimum time for Matrigel coating is 1.5 h, optimal results can be achieved if left overnight.

This distance ensures that enough force is imparted on the tissue to attach it to the Matrigel. Administer media one droplet at a time at a steady pace. Use a 1000 μL wide-bore pipette for larger droplets if necessary.

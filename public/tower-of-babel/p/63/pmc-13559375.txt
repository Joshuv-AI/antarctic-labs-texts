Day-7 lactate-to-albumin ratio as a prognostic marker in sepsis patients requiring prolonged mechanical ventilation: a multicenter retrospective landmark analysis of the Korean Sepsis Alliance registry
Authors: Hyojin Jang, Wanho Yoo, Harin Rhee, Ryoung-Eun Ko, Gee Young Suh, Chae-Man Lim, Kwangha Lee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPrognostication in patients with sepsis requiring prolonged mechanical ventilation (MV) is challenging. While the lactate-to-albumin ratio (LAR) predicts early sepsis outcomes, its value during prolonged MV remains undefined. We evaluated the prognostic performance of the MV day-7 LAR in this population.MethodsThis multicenter study retrospectively analyzed prospectively collected data [2019–2023] from the Korean Sepsis Alliance (KSA) registry. By a pre-specified day-7 landmark design, the analysis was restricted to adult sepsis patients who survived on MV to day 7 (requiring MV for ≥7 days). We compared the prognostic accuracy of day-7 LAR with days 1–3 LARs using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. Independent predictors of in-hospital mortality were assessed using multivariable logistic regression. We also evaluated the day-7 LAR’s added value to the Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) score.ResultsAmong 1,128 patients, survivors showed a significant LAR reduction by day 7 compared with non-survivors. The day-7 LAR demonstrated higher discriminative ability [area under the curve (AUC) 0.714] for in-hospital mortality than days 1, 2, and 3 LARs (AUC 0.600, 0.646, and 0.674). It also showed higher discrimination than procalcitonin and comparable discrimination to C-reactive protein (CRP). The day-7 LAR was an independent predictor of mortality [adjusted odds ratio 2.191, 95% confidence interval (CI): 1.626–2.952]. Adding the day-7 LAR to the day-7 SOFA score significantly improved predictive performance compared with the SOFA score alone. Furthermore, patients with a day-7 LAR ≥0.81 exhibited significantly lower survival rates and required more frequent organ-support therapies.ConclusionsIn sepsis patients requiring prolonged MV, the day-7 LAR serves as a potential marker of the transition to non-resolving organ dysfunction and provides incremental prognostic value to the SOFA score. The day-7 LAR may offer a reproducible anchor for risk stratification and structured reassessment of therapeutic goals in this high-mortality population. These findings apply to patients surviving to the day-7 landmark and may not generalize to unselected ventilated sepsis populations.Keywords: Lactate-to-albumin ratio (LAR), sepsis, prolonged mechanical ventilation (prolonged MV), chronic critical illness, prognostication

Highlight box

Key findings

• In sepsis patients requiring prolonged mechanical ventilation (MV) for ≥7 days, the day-7 lactate-to-albumin ratio (LAR) was an independent predictor of in-hospital mortality and identified those who failed to demonstrate metabolic and inflammatory recovery.

• Adding the day-7 LAR to the day-7 Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) score significantly improved discrimination, reclassification, and net clinical benefit beyond SOFA alone.

What is known, and what is new?

• Prior studies have shown that the LAR predicts mortality when measured at intensive care unit admission or within the first 24 hours of sepsis.

• This study is the first to characterize the LAR trajectory through MV day 7 in a multicenter prolonged-ventilation cohort and to demonstrate that the day-7 LAR provides incremental prognostic information beyond the SOFA score in this phase of critical illness.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• A day-7 LAR ≥0.81 may serve as a reproducible, laboratory-based trigger for structured reassessment of therapeutic goals in patients failing to recover from sepsis on prolonged MV, supporting more objective and timely multidisciplinary decision-making.

Introduction

Advances in sepsis management have altered its clinical course. While early recognition and resuscitation bundles have improved acute survival (1,2) and more patients are surviving the initial hyperacute phase of sepsis, a significant proportion of patients subsequently develop persistent organ dysfunction requiring prolonged mechanical ventilation (MV) (3,4). These patients, often described as “chronically critically ill”, represent a growing population associated with poor long-term outcomes and high resource utilization (5). In this high-risk group, accurate prognostication may help in guiding therapeutic decisions and goals-of-care discussions. However, predicting outcomes in the prolonged phase remains a challenge, as traditional severity scores calculated at admission often fail to capture the patient’s evolving physiological status (6).

To overcome the limitations of single biomarkers, composite indices integrating metabolic and inflammatory parameters have been proposed. The lactate-to-albumin ratio (LAR) combines the assessment of metabolic derangement and the host inflammatory response into a single index. Previous studies indicate that the LAR offers better prognostic accuracy than individual biomarkers in various critical care settings (7-12). However, its prognostic role in the prolonged phase of critical illness remains undefined.

Most existing data on the LAR are derived from values measured at intensive care unit (ICU) admission or within the first 24 h. In patients requiring prolonged MV, however, these initial levels are often confounded by aggressive fluid resuscitation and acute interventions (13,14). Therefore, baseline values may not accurately reflect whether a patient is recovering or deteriorating. We hypothesized that measuring the LAR on day 7 would be more predictive of survival, as it better identifies persistent organ dysfunction that has failed to resolve despite treatment.

Because risk after the first week of critical illness differs fundamentally from early risk, we adopted a pre-specified day-7 landmark design, restricting the analysis to patients who remained on MV at day 7. Within this framework, this study aimed to evaluate the prognostic performance of the day-7 LAR in patients with sepsis requiring prolonged MV—that is, among those who survive on MV to the day-7 landmark rather than across all ventilated sepsis patients. Specifically, we compared the predictive validity of the day-7 LAR with early measurements (days 1–3) and conventional inflammatory biomarkers, and determined whether the day-7 LAR provides added prognostic value to the Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) score (15). We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2026-1406/rc).

Methods

Study design and data source

This was a multicenter retrospective analysis of prospectively collected data from the Korean Sepsis Alliance (KSA) registry, a nationwide observational cohort. The KSA is a nationwide, multicenter clinical cohort that includes adult patients with sepsis admitted to the ICUs of 15 tertiary or university-affiliated hospitals in South Korea. Established in September 2019, the KSA registry prospectively collects detailed clinical information using a standardized, web-based case report form (CRF) completed by trained research coordinators at each participating institution. The registry and its electronic CRF (eCRF) are accessible at: https://sepsis.crf.kr

Study population

We screened all adult patients aged 19 years or older who were admitted to the ICU with sepsis between September 2019 and December 2023. Sepsis was defined according to the Sepsis-3 criteria as suspected or confirmed infection accompanied by an acute increase of at least two points in the SOFA score (16).

Patients were included if they required MV for ≥7 days following ICU admission. Patients without available serum lactate or albumin measurements on MV days 1, 2, 3, or 7 were excluded. A total of 1,128 patients met these criteria and were included in the final analysis. For all enrolled patients, ICU day 1 corresponded to MV day 1.

Data collection, variables, and outcomes

Data were prospectively collected by trained study coordinators using a standardized KSA web-based CRF. Baseline demographic information included age, sex, and body mass index (BMI). Underlying comorbidities present prior to admission were documented, and the Charlson comorbidity index (CCI) was calculated (17). Clinical characteristics collected at enrollment included the presence of septic shock based on the Sepsis-3 definition (16); a history of hospitalization lasting ≥2 days within the 90 days prior to ICU admission; the Clinical Frailty Scale score at ICU admission; the presumed site of infection (respiratory, abdominal, urinary, or skin/soft tissue); identification of causative pathogens; and the performance of surgical or radiologic procedures for source control. Completion of the Surviving Sepsis Campaign (SSC) 1-hour bundle and each of its components was assessed (1,2). The appropriateness of antimicrobial therapy was evaluated according to drug susceptibility test results or guideline recommendations (1,2). Hospital length of stay was also recorded. Severity of illness and accompanying organ failure were assessed using the Simplified Acute Physiology Score (SAPS) III and SOFA score on MV day 1 (15,18,19).

To evaluate the association between physiologic status on MV day-7 and clinical outcomes, additional data were collected on MV day-7. These included the SOFA score; routine laboratory parameters such as white blood cell count, hemoglobin, platelet count, serum creatinine level, and total bilirubin level; inflammatory markers including C-reactive protein (CRP) and procalcitonin; and arterial blood gas values, including the PaO2/FiO2 ratio. Therapeutic interventions administered on MV day-7, such as systemic corticosteroids, sedatives, analgesics, inotropes, vasopressors, extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO), and continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT), were also documented.

The primary exposure variable was the LAR, calculated as serum lactate (mmol/L) divided by serum albumin (g/dL). LAR values were obtained on MV days 1, 2, 3, and 7. The primary endpoint of this study was in-hospital mortality.

Missing data

Day-7 lactate and albumin were required for cohort entry, so the LAR, SOFA, and SOFA + LAR analyses were performed on the complete cohort (n=1,128) with no missing values. The inflammatory biomarkers used as secondary comparators were not obtained by protocol but at the discretion of the treating clinicians, and day-7 values were therefore available in only a subset—869 patients (77.0%) for CRP and 314 (27.8%) for procalcitonin. Because their availability reflected clinical testing patterns rather than a random process, these comparisons were performed on an available-case basis and interpreted with caution, and the corresponding areas under the curve apply only to the subgroups in which the biomarkers were measured. Imputation was considered but not performed: the primary analyses contained no missing data, and imputing the two discretionary comparators—particularly procalcitonin, which was unmeasured in the majority of patients—would have relied on unverifiable assumptions without affecting the primary results. Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were expressed as median with interquartile range (IQR), and categorical variables as counts and percentages. Group differences were analyzed using Student’s t-test or the Mann-Whitney U test for continuous variables and the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables. The change in LAR (∆ LAR) was calculated as the difference between the day-7 and day-1 values (day-7 LAR minus day-1 LAR).

The prognostic performance of the LAR measured on MV days 1, 2, 3, and 7 was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. The area under the curve (AUC) and corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated, and differences in AUCs were compared using DeLong’s method (20). For comparison, a day-7 SOFA + lactate model was constructed in the same way as the SOFA + LAR model and compared with it using the same analyses. The net reclassification improvement (NRI) and integrated discrimination improvement (IDI) were calculated to quantify the incremental prognostic gain of the day-7 SOFA + LAR model over SOFA alone and LAR alone (21). Both continuous and category-based NRIs (cNRIs) were computed, with the latter using three pre-specified mortality-risk strata (<30%, 30–50%, and >50%) corresponding to low, intermediate, and high risk, in line with the framework for category-based reclassification analysis (22,23). These thresholds were selected to reflect clinically actionable risk categories given the observed in-hospital mortality of 39.2% in this cohort. Decision curve analysis was performed to estimate the net clinical benefit of the day-7 SOFA + LAR value across a range of threshold probabilities, which were benchmarked against ‘Treat All’ and ‘Treat None’ strategies (24,25). The optimal cutoff value was determined using the Youden index (26). Because discharge alive precludes subsequent in-hospital death, in-hospital death and discharge alive were treated as competing events. The cumulative incidence of in-hospital death was estimated by LAR group and compared using Gray’s test, and the association of the day-7 LAR with in-hospital death was assessed with a Fine-Gray subdistribution hazard model. Internal validation was performed using bootstrap resampling with 1,000 iterations to assess the stability of the day-7 LAR AUC, the optimal cutoff, and the combined day-7 SOFA + LAR model; for the combined model, optimism-corrected estimates of discrimination (AUC with 95% CI) and overall performance (Brier score) were derived. Model calibration was assessed using the Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test.

Covariates for the multivariable logistic regression model were pre-specified on clinical grounds rather than selected by univariate significance, to ensure the inclusion of critical confounders. Basic demographic factors (age and sex) were entered regardless of their univariate association, together with an established measure of acute severity (day-7 SOFA score), chronic comorbidity burden (CCI), and the exposure of interest (the day-7 LAR). No automated or stepwise selection was applied, and the model was retained as fitted. With 442 in-hospital deaths and 5 covariates, the events-per-variable ratio (~88) was well above conventional thresholds. Multicollinearity was assessed using variance inflation factors (VIFs), all of which were below 1.3 (range, 1.03–1.29), indicating no problematic collinearity. Odds ratios with 95% CIs were reported, and model calibration was assessed using the Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test.

All tests were two-sided, and statistical significance was defined as P<0.05. All statistical analyses were performed using R version 4.4.1 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria), IBM SPSS Statistics version 30.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA), and MedCalc version 23.1.6 (MedCalc Software, Ostend, Belgium).

Ethical consideration

The protocol for the KSA registry was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of each participating hospital. The requirement for informed consent was waived because the registry is a non-interventional observational study that collects data solely from medical records of patients managed according to standard clinical practice, without any direct contact with the participants. This study was conducted in accordance with the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. The study protocol was approved by the IRB of Pusan National University Hospital (No. 2510-009-156; date of approval: 21 October 2025), which also waived the requirement for informed consent. All participating hospitals were informed of and agreed to the study.

Results

Patient characteristics

During the study period, 17,828 adult patients were diagnosed with sepsis across 15 participating hospitals. Of these, 3,578 patients received MV, and 1,385 received ventilator care for ≥7 days. After excluding 257 patients without available lactate or albumin measurements on MV days 1, 2, 3, or 7, 1,128 patients were included in the final analysis. The in-hospital mortality rate was 39.2% (Figure 1).

They also had higher CCIs and more frequent underlying comorbidities, including solid malignant tumor, chronic liver disease, and hematologic malignancy.

Prognostic performance of LAR on MV day 7

Regarding the temporal trend, survivors demonstrated a progressive decline in LAR from a median of 1.00 (IQR, 0.57–1.79) on day-1 to 0.53 (IQR, 0.38–0.75) on day-7 (P<0.001, Wilcoxon signed-rank test). In contrast, non-survivors exhibited an attenuated reduction (day 1: 1.36, IQR, 0.76–2.52; day 7: 0.84, IQR, 0.54–1.43; P<0.001). The magnitude of decline (Δ LAR = day 7 LAR − day 1 LAR) was significantly greater in survivors than in non-survivors (median Δ −0.37 vs. −0.27; P=0.02), and non-survivors had significantly higher LAR than survivors at every time point (all P<0.001) (Figure 2).

In the ROC curve analysis of LAR measured serially on MV days 1, 2, 3, and 7, the day-7 LAR demonstrated the highest discriminative ability for predicting in-hospital mortality (AUC 0.714, 95% CI: 0.682–0.746) compared with those on day 1 (0.600), day 2 (0.646), and day 3 (0.674); pairwise comparisons using DeLong’s test showed that the day-7 AUC was significantly higher than those of days 1, 2, and 3 (P<0.001, P<0.001, and P=0.007, respectively) (Figure 3). In multivariable logistic regression analyses, the day-7 LAR remained significantly associated with in-hospital mortality after adjusting for confounding variables (adjusted OR 2.191, 95% CI: 1.626–2.952, P<0.001) (Table 2). The VIF values for all included covariates ranged from 1.025 to 1.291, indicating no significant multicollinearity.

Based on the ROC analysis, the optimal cutoff value for the day-7 LAR was 0.81, classifying patients into a high-LAR group (≥0.81, n=370) and a low-LAR group (<0.81, n=758). Internal validation with 1,000 bootstrap resamples confirmed the robustness of these estimates: the 95% bootstrap CI of the day-7 LAR AUC was 0.682–0.746, and the 95% bootstrap CI of the optimal cutoff was 0.72–0.83. In a competing-risk analysis treating discharge alive as a competing event, the high-LAR group had a significantly higher cumulative incidence of in-hospital death than the low-LAR group, with early and sustained separation of the cumulative incidence curves (Gray’s test P<0.001; Figure 4). Comparison with individual components and conventional biomarkers

The prognostic performance of the day-7 LAR was compared with that of its individual components (lactate, albumin), the SOFA score, the combined SOFA + LAR model, and conventional inflammatory biomarkers (CRP, procalcitonin) using ROC curve analysis (Table 3). The discriminative ability of the day-7 LAR (AUC 0.714) was statistically comparable to that of lactate alone (AUC 0.717; DeLong P=0.65) and significantly higher than that of albumin alone (AUC 0.545; DeLong P=0.001). Compared with conventional inflammatory biomarkers, the day-7 LAR demonstrated higher prognostic discrimination than procalcitonin (AUC 0.603, n=314; DeLong P=0.048), whereas its performance was statistically comparable to that of CRP (AUC 0.631, n=869; DeLong P=0.11).

Incremental prognostic value of the day-7 LAR added to the day-7 SOFA score

Combining the day-7 LAR with the day-7 SOFA score (SOFA + LAR) yielded significantly higher discrimination than either component alone: the combined model achieved an AUC of 0.779, compared with 0.763 for SOFA alone (DeLong P=0.001) and 0.714 for LAR alone (DeLong P<0.001) (Figure 5A). The added value of the day-7 LAR was further supported by reclassification metrics: compared with SOFA alone, SOFA + LAR yielded an IDI of 0.029 and a cNRI of 0.272; compared with LAR alone, the IDI was 0.093 and the cNRI was 0.417. Decision curve analysis demonstrated greater net benefit for SOFA + LAR than for either SOFA alone or LAR alone across a broad range of clinically relevant threshold probabilities (Figure 5B,5C). Internal validation with 1,000 bootstrap resamples confirmed the stability of the combined model’s discrimination (optimism-corrected AUC 0.779, 95% bootstrap CI: 0.748–0.807; Figure S1), and the model showed good overall performance and calibration (Brier score 0.180; Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit P=0.99; Figure S2).

Discussion

In this multicenter cohort of 1,128 sepsis patients requiring MV for at least 7 days, we evaluated the prognostic value of the LAR measured on day 7 of MV. To our knowledge, this is among the first studies to characterize the LAR trajectory from MV day 1 to day 7 and to examine its discriminative performance at this later time point in a prolonged-ventilation sepsis population. Our principal findings were as follows. First, survivors demonstrated a progressive decline in LAR from day 1 to day 7, whereas non-survivors exhibited a significantly attenuated reduction. Second, among serial measurements on MV days 1, 2, 3, and 7, the day-7 LAR showed the highest discriminative ability for in-hospital mortality; it was statistically superior to procalcitonin and comparable to CRP in this cohort. Third, the day-7 LAR was independently associated with in-hospital mortality after adjustment for confounders. Fourth, adding the day-7 LAR to the day-7 SOFA score significantly improved discrimination compared with SOFA alone.

This study has several strengths. It is based on a large, multicenter, prospectively collected cohort drawn from the KSA registry, providing real-world data from 15 tertiary or university-affiliated ICUs and minimizing single-center bias. The specific focus on a clinically challenging subgroup—sepsis patients requiring prolonged MV—addresses a population in which standard admission-based prognostic markers are known to lose discriminative power. Beyond cross-sectional assessment, we characterized the longitudinal LAR trajectory across MV days 1 to 7 and evaluated the incremental prognostic value of the day-7 LAR over the SOFA score using complementary metrics, including DeLong’s test, NRI, IDI, and decision curve analysis. Internal validation through 1,000 bootstrap resamples further supported the stability of both the day-7 LAR discrimination and the combined SOFA + LAR model.

Several limitations should nevertheless be acknowledged. First, residual confounding from unmeasured factors—most notably exogenous albumin administration, the denominator of the LAR—cannot be fully excluded. Because intravenous albumin can raise serum albumin and lower the measured LAR independent of a patient’s metabolic state, and because the KSA registry did not record albumin administration, we were unable to report exposure rates or to perform a sensitivity analysis excluding albumin recipients. However, albumin is not a routine or first-line component of sepsis resuscitation, so its use was likely sporadic rather than systematic; moreover, if sicker patients were preferentially given albumin, the resulting misclassification would tend to attenuate rather than exaggerate the observed LAR-mortality association. Underlying chronic liver disease and malignancies, which were more prevalent among non-survivors and can independently affect serum albumin, lactate metabolism, and overall prognosis, may also have contributed to residual confounding that statistical adjustment could not fully eliminate. Second, detailed information on cumulative fluid balance during the first seven days of MV was not available. Because fluid status may influence both serum lactate clearance and serum albumin through hemodilution, this represents a potentially important unmeasured factor that warrants assessment in future studies. Third, our pre-specified day-7 landmark design restricted the cohort to patients ventilated for at least seven days, so the findings are conditional on survival to the day-7 landmark and address risk prediction among patients ventilated through day 7 rather than across unselected ventilated sepsis. Among the 3,578 ventilated patients, the 2,193 (61.3%) who did not reach the landmark had higher in-hospital mortality (56.5% vs. 41.8%) and more frequent septic shock, yet baseline age, day-1 SOFA score, SAPS III, and comorbidity burden were similar between groups (Table S3). The landmark cohort is thus defined by survival to day-7 rather than by lower baseline severity, with patients leaving ventilation earlier—through early death or early recovery—forming a higher-risk, distinct population. This design directly reflects the clinical question of interest: risk-stratifying the “surviving yet unrecovered” population transitioning to chronic critical illness, for whom prognostication is most challenging and most consequential for goals-of-care discussions. Fourth, the cohort was drawn exclusively from Korean tertiary and university hospitals, which limits external validity. Ethnic and nutritional differences may shift the distribution of serum albumin and lactate, so the day-7 LAR cutoff (0.81) may not transport directly to Western populations and would require recalibration; differences in case mix, staffing, and intervention intensity at community or non-tertiary centers may likewise require local re-derivation. We therefore regard the cutoff as a context-specific estimate, and external validation across diverse healthcare systems, hospital tiers, and patient populations is warranted before the day-7 LAR can be recommended as a routine prognostic tool.

Previous studies have established the prognostic utility of the LAR in sepsis and septic shock; however, the majority have focused on values obtained at ICU admission or within the first 24 hours of presentation (7-9). In that early phase of sepsis, the LAR reliably reflects acute resuscitation-phase derangements—tissue hypoperfusion and lactate production from anaerobic metabolism (27,28), together with capillary leak, hypoalbuminemia, and the early inflammatory response to infection (29,30). Our findings extend this evidence base in two important ways. First, by characterizing the LAR trajectory from MV day 1 to day 7, we demonstrated that the prognostic information carried by the LAR shifts over the course of critical illness: in patients requiring prolonged ventilatory support, the day-7 LAR substantially outperformed earlier measurements (days 1, 2, and 3) for predicting in-hospital mortality. Second, this temporal shift is consistent with the clinical reality that admission-based severity markers progressively lose discriminative power as critical illness becomes chronic, a phenomenon previously described in the context of persistent inflammation, immunosuppression, and catabolism syndrome (PICS) and chronic critical illness (5,31). The day-7 LAR therefore appears to capture a different clinical phase than early LAR measurements—the transition from the acute resuscitation period to a state of non-resolving organ dysfunction—and may serve as a marker of this transition rather than as a redundant readout of the original septic insult.

The day-7 LAR performed similarly to lactate alone (AUC 0.714 vs. 0.717; DeLong P=0.65), and adding either lactate or LAR to the day-7 SOFA score produced comparable results (SOFA + lactate AUC 0.780 vs. SOFA + LAR AUC 0.779; DeLong P=0.54). We therefore do not claim that the ratio is superior to lactate; the two carry essentially the same prognostic information in this cohort, which is unsurprising because lactate is the numerator of the LAR and a strong predictor of critical-illness mortality in its own right. We retain the LAR because it was our pre-specified index and because it can be derived from values already collected on routine daily panels, but day-7 lactate alone would be expected to perform similarly, and our findings are best read as evidence for the prognostic value of the day-7 metabolic axis rather than of the ratio itself. Compared with conventional inflammatory biomarkers, the day-7 LAR showed higher discrimination than procalcitonin and comparable discrimination to CRP, consistent with prior observations that late-phase inflammatory biomarkers are often confounded by non-infectious factors and reflect infection biology more than overall survival trajectory (32,33).

The incremental value of combining the day-7 LAR with the day-7 SOFA score is also notable. While SOFA captures organ dysfunction across six systems, it does not directly incorporate metabolic or nutritional information (15). Prior studies, including work from this registry, have shown that combining the SOFA score with serum lactate provides incremental prognostic information beyond SOFA alone, with the magnitude of benefit depending on the timing of measurement and the patient population (34,35). Our findings extend this evidence by replacing lactate with the LAR and applying the analysis specifically to the prolonged-ventilation population, with day-7 measurement positioning the marker at the transition into chronic critical illness. In this setting, adding the day-7 LAR to the day-7 SOFA score yielded improvements in discrimination, reclassification, and net clinical benefit, supporting the value of pairing an organ-failure score with a composite metabolic-nutritional readout. The absolute gain in discrimination was modest (ΔAUC 0.016, from 0.763 to 0.779). The c-statistic is relatively insensitive to the added value of a new marker, however, and for this reason we pre-specified reclassification and decision-analytic metrics in addition to the AUC. The IDI (0.029), category-based NRI (0.272), and the greater net benefit on decision curve analysis all indicated that the day-7 LAR adds information not already captured by SOFA. This is plausible on physiological grounds, since SOFA includes neither lactate nor albumin. We therefore regard the day-7 LAR as a low-cost, routinely available adjunct to established severity scores rather than a stand-alone basis for prognosis or treatment decisions.

One potential concern is whether the day-7 LAR simply identifies patients whose poor prognosis is already clinically apparent. We acknowledge that the day-7 LAR is, by design, measured in patients who have already failed to wean from MV, and that its strongest correlates—vasopressor use, renal replacement therapy, and persistent inflammation—are clinically visible. However, clinical impression alone may be imprecise and prone to inter-clinician variability, particularly in chronically critically ill patients with slowly evolving trajectories. A reproducible, laboratory-based index such as the day-7 LAR can complement clinical judgment by providing an objective anchor for risk discussions with multidisciplinary teams and families. The value of the day-7 LAR therefore lies not in revealing what is hidden, but in quantifying what is too often appreciated only retrospectively.

Our findings have several implications for clinical practice and future research. From a clinical standpoint, the day-7 LAR can serve as a potential, easily computed marker to identify sepsis patients on prolonged MV who are at risk of poor outcomes despite conventional management. Rather than directing specific management decisions, the day-7 LAR may serve as one objective input that, alongside clinical judgment and established severity scores, could prompt multidisciplinary reassessment in patients failing to improve (36); this potential application is hypothesis-generating and would require prospective validation before clinical adoption. Because the day-7 LAR consists of routinely available laboratory measurements and shows incremental prognostic value when added to the SOFA score, combining the two may provide a low-cost, repeatable framework for daily risk monitoring throughout the prolonged-ventilation course. External validation in independent cohorts of differing case mix, ethnicity, and healthcare context will be essential to confirm the generalizability of the proposed cutoff, and future intervention studies should examine whether incorporating the day-7 LAR into clinical management can meaningfully alter patient outcomes.

Conclusions

In this multicenter cohort of sepsis patients requiring MV for at least seven days, the LAR measured on MV day 7 was an independent predictor of in-hospital mortality and showed discriminative performance comparable to lactate alone and superior to procalcitonin. When combined with the day-7 SOFA score, the day-7 LAR yielded significant improvements in discrimination, reclassification, and net clinical benefit, with internal validation supporting the stability of these estimates. Together, these findings suggest that tracking the LAR trajectory through the first week of MV may help identify patients who fail to demonstrate LAR normalization by day-7, providing a useful adjunct to established severity scores for risk stratification in this high-mortality population.

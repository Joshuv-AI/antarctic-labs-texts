Evaluation of IL-10, IL-22, and TNF-α in the gingival crevicular fluid of patients with orthodontic treatment using NiTi and Cu-NiTi archwires
Authors: Maryam Karandish, Sana Ariafar, Zahra Faghih
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe biological mechanism behind tooth movement relies on sterile inflammation, which can be affected by the various characteristics of orthodontic wire alloys. Hence, this study was designed to evaluate and compare the effects of copper-nickel-titanium (Cu-NiTi) and nickel-titanium (NiTi) wires on the concentrations of the cytokines IL-10, IL-22, and TNF-α in gingival crevicular fluid (GCF) during the initial phase of dental alignment.MethodsThis retrospective cohort study analyzed GCF samples from 24 patients exhibiting crowding treated with either Cu-NiTi or NiTi wires (12 patients per group). Samples were collected at four intervals (baseline, 1, 24, and 48 h post-wire placement). Cytokine concentrations were assessed using a bead-based immunoassay. The data were evaluated via Mann-Whitney and Friedman analyses.ResultsWhile no meaningful difference was detected in the short-term levels of the pro-inflammatory cytokines TNF-α and IL-22 between the groups, the Cu-NiTi group showed a significant increase in the anti-inflammatory cytokine IL-10 within the initial 24 h (P = 0.021). In addition, compared to the NiTi group, the Cu-NiTi group showed a higher rate of IL-10 increase within the first 24 h (P = 0.034).ConclusionInsertion of Cu-NiTi wires causes a reaction in periodontal tissue that is characterized by a significant increase in the regulatory cytokine IL-10. On the other hand, the pro-inflammatory markers, TNF-α and IL-22, were comparable in both types of wires. These findings suggest that Cu-NiTi wires may promote orthodontic tooth movement (OTM) in the mandibular anterior region through a distinct, more modulated early biological pathway.Keywords: Orthodontic wires, Cytokines, Gingival crevicular fluid, Cu-NiTi, NiTi

Introduction

OTM is a complex biological process that responds to controlled mechanical forces by immediately changing periodontal tissues. The whole periodontium, alveolar bone, and gingiva remodel as a result of the pressures imparted by tooth movement, creating zones of tension and compression at the PDL level [1]. This process, classically described as a sterile inflammatory response, is governed by a network of biochemical mediators. In this regard, GCF analysis, as a noninvasive method, provides a highly convenient and accurate way to investigate the biochemical processes associated with bone remodeling during orthodontic tooth movement [2].

Cytokines are essential mediators in this biological process, and their balance is critical to ensuring controlled tooth movement. In areas of mechanical stress, tissue damage and a decrease in the number of open capillaries lead to ischemia and local hypoxia, which in turn increases the expression of TNF-α in periodontal tissue fibroblasts [3, 4]. TNF-α is an important proinflammatory cytokine that promotes bone resorption by enhancing osteoclast activity and proteolytic enzyme synthesis [2]. By encouraging osteoclastogenesis during the early stages of bone formation induction, IL-22 continuously aids in this catabolic process [5]. In contrast, for an appropriate tissue response and to prevent adverse effects and tissue damage, these proinflammatory processes must be inhibited; IL-10, as an immune system regulatory cytokine, plays a major protective role in maintaining tissue balance by inhibiting osteoclastogenesis [1].

In orthodontic treatments, wires, as the primary force-applying engine, play an important role in treatment efficacy and cytokine activation. The continuous evolution of these archwires has always been driven by the search for materials with optimal biomechanical properties [6–8]. NiTi alloys are well-known for their great flexibility, shape memory, and superelasticity, and they are widely employed in continuous force applications [9, 10]. With the aim of improving these properties, Cu-NiTi alloys were introduced in the mid-1990s [11]. The addition of copper to this alloy allows for much finer control over the thermodynamic properties, allowing these wires to exhibit less hysteresis than conventional wires and to produce a much smoother and lighter unloading force plateau [12, 13]. All metal alloys, including NiTi and Cu-NiTi, undergo some degree of corrosion in the corrosive environment of the oral cavity, resulting in the release of metal ions. However, certain compositions of Cu-NiTi can improve biocompatibility and reduce nickel release by forming a more stable surface oxide layer [14, 15].

Despite the widespread clinical use and distinct physical properties of Cu-NiTi wires [11, 16], Most previous studies have focused solely on mechanical or clinical outcomes, such as tooth movement speed and pain intensity [17–20] and have neglected to explain the underlying molecular response. Until the biological and cellular response of tissues to these advanced alloys is precisely known, the transition from a purely mechanical approach to biologically-driven and personalized orthodontic treatments will remain impossible. In other words, identifying this molecular footprint is the most critical missing link in modern orthodontic science to find out whether the gentler forces of these wires actually activate different biological and inflammatory pathways. Therefore, this study was designed with the aim of accurately assessing and comparing the levels of cytokines IL-10, IL-22, and TNF-α in the gingival crevicular fluid of patients treated with NiTi and Cu-NiTi wires.

Materials and methods

Study design

This research was structured as a retrospective cohort analysis utilizing biological specimens obtained from a prior randomized clinical trial (RCT) [21]. The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Shiraz University of Medical Sciences (Code: IR.SUMS.DENTAL.REC.1404.030), and the initial trial was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and registered with the Iranian Clinical Trials Registry (IRCT20111130008257N2, registration date: 2018-01-03). All participants signed an informed consent form before entering the study.

Eligibility criteria for participants

The study population consisted of 24 patients who were randomly divided into two groups. Inclusion criteria included: patients with moderate crowding (4 to 6 mm) in the anterior region of the mandible and candidates for fixed orthodontic treatment without extraction with the MBT bracket system (0.022 × 0.028 inch slot). Exclusion criteria included: blood or saliva contamination in the samples, insufficient sample volume after thawing, or reported failure of the cold chain of storage (-70 ℃).

Interventions

Participants were divided into two parallel groups: the NiTi group (n = 12), who received 0.016-inch superelastic nickel-titanium wires (American Orthodontics, USA), and the Cu-NiTi group (n = 12), who received 0.016-inch copper-nickel-titanium wires (Ormco, USA). The laboratory analysis process was performed blindly, and the expert who performed the statistical analysis was unaware of the wire type assigned to the groups.

Analysis of inflammatory cytokines

GCF samples were collected using paper cones from the mandibular incisor teeth at four time points: baseline (T0), one hour (T1), twenty-four hours (T2), and forty-eight hours (T3) after wire placement. Sample collection was done with great care from at least 8 points of the mandibular incisor teeth, and complete isolation was performed in the oral environment to prevent contamination of the sample with saliva. After collection, samples were immediately placed in an insulated container and transported to the laboratory at − 18 °C, then stored at − 70 °C within 30 min. IL-10, IL-22, and TNF-α cytokine levels were determined using a bead-based immunoassay and the LEGENDplex Multi-Analyte Flow Assay kit (BioLegend, San Diego, CA, USA) [22], according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Data were read by a BD FACSCalibur flow cytometer (BD Biosciences, San Jose, CA, USA), and results were reported as mean fluorescence intensity (MFI). The exact concentration of each analyte was determined based on the standard curve using the device’s proprietary software.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS version 25.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Descriptive statistics, including median, mean, and standard deviation (SD), were calculated. The Mann-Whitney U test was used to compare cytokine levels between the two groups. The Friedman test was used to examine within-group changes over time. In case of any significant result, pairwise comparisons were performed using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test with Bonferroni correction. In all tests, the statistical significance level was set at P < 0.05.

Results

Baseline data

Statistical analysis confirmed that there was no significant difference in baseline cytokine levels between the two groups and that the groups were biologically homogeneous. At T0, median values for IL-10 (P = 0.602), IL-22 (P = 0.651), and TNF-α (P = 0.602) were comparable in the NiTi and Cu-NiTi groups (Table 1).

Analysis of cytokines

In examining the short-term temporal changes of the IL-10 cytokine, the Friedman test showed a statistically significant difference in the Cu-NiTi group (P = 0.021), such that the level of this cytokine peaked at 24 h (T2). In contrast, the changes in the NiTi group were not significant (P = 0.361). In an intergroup comparison with the Mann-Whitney test, the degree of change (difference) of IL-10 cytokine from baseline to 24 h (T2-T0) in the Cu-NiTi group was significantly greater than that in the NiTi group (P = 0.034).

For the cytokines TNF-α and IL-22, although increases were observed at 24 h, there was no statistically significant difference between the two groups at any time point (P > 0.05) or in the degree of their change. The difference in changes for TNF-α at T2 did not reach a significant level with a P value of 0.082 (Tables 2 and 3).

Discussion

This study compared the early biological response of periodontal tissues to two orthodontic archwires by evaluating IL-10, IL-22, and TNF-α levels in gingival crevicular fluid (GCF). The investigation provides a novel clinical perspective by directly assessing the acute biological effects of Cu-NiTi and conventional NiTi wires, rather than focusing exclusively on mechanical alignment. The primary finding was a significant increase in the immunoregulatory cytokine IL-10 in the Cu-NiTi group 24 h after force application, indicating a distinct biological response compared to standard NiTi wires.

The intricate biological process of OTM combines controlled, sterile inflammatory cascades with mechanical forces [1, 3, 4]. Making the switch to a biologically based treatment approach requires understanding how various orthodontic materials affect this chemical cascade. The cellular activity of osteoclasts, whose development, activation, and survival are regulated by the signaling system of receptor-activating nuclear factor kappa-B (RANK), its ligand (RANKL), and osteoprotegerin (OPG), is entirely responsible for the process of bone resorption in orthodontics [23, 24]. Although Cu-NiTi wires entered the market as a more advanced generation, with extensive advertising of more precise control of thermodynamic properties and the provision of gentler and more continuous forces [12, 13, 16], most clinical trials failed to translate this advertised advantage into clear clinical results. Reviewing the literature showed that there was no significant difference in alignment efficiency between the two groups. For example, Azizi et al., Ashok et al., Ahmed and Kadhum, Msungu et al., and Noori et al. all concluded that the alignment efficiency of the two wires was similar [25–29]. Regarding pain, although some studies, such as Dedeoğlu and Özsoy or Ahmed and Kadhum, reported more pain with Cu-NiTi on certain days [27, 30], most studies, including Bagheri et al., Azizi et al., and Marzal et al., did not find a significant difference in this regard [21, 25, 31]. The current study was conducted to precisely examine this biological footprint and determine whether a similar clinical outcome follows the same biological pathway.

To understand the OTM process, the simultaneous assessment of a set of proinflammatory and anti-inflammatory cytokines, as also recommended by the systematic review by Alhadlaq [2], provides a more accurate insight than studies on single biological markers. The study focused on three major cytokines: TNF-α, a proinflammatory and bone resorption driver [3, 4, 32], IL-22, which has been shown to promote osteoclastogenesis and increase the formation of mineralized nodules in the early period of osteogenesis induction, but has no major effect on osteoblastic markers [5], and IL-10, an anti-inflammatory and immune system regulator [1, 33, 34]. The aforementioned markers have been evaluated in previous studies, such as those by Karaduman et al., who evaluated TNF-α and IL-10 concurrently [35], and by El-Sayed et al., who studied IL-1 and TNF-α [19]. Additionally, Bagheri et al. examined cytokines associated with Th17 cells, including IL-17, IL-21, and IL-6 [21]. According to the findings, the main strength of the present study is the simultaneous assessment of three different biological pathways: a proinflammatory indicator, an osteoclastogenesis promoter, and an immunoregulatory marker. This comprehensive approach enhances understanding of the complex cytokine network.

The time range chosen (up to 48 h) was likewise supported by good scientific data. A systematic review, such as the study by Kapoor et al., shows that the peak secretion of many cytokines in GCF occurs within the first 24 h after force application and then begins to decline [17]. Therefore, the selected time points were ideal for recording these initial acute changes.

The result obtained from this study, namely a significant increase in IL-10 in the Cu-NiTi group (P = 0.034), is very noteworthy. Cu-NiTi wires are known to deliver a gentler and more continuous force [12]. It should be noted that while the increase in IL-10 in the Cu-NiTi group is a statistically significant observation, the underlying mechanism remains speculative and cannot be directly inferred from the present data. It is hypothesized that this more optimal force pattern might induce a controlled and modulated biological response in the periodontal tissue, rather than inducing an acute and aggressive inflammatory response (characterized by a sharp increase in TNF-α). In this scenario, the body responds to this gentle force by increasing the production of the regulatory cytokine IL-10 to manage the bone remodeling process in a homeostatic environment with minimal collateral damage. This observation is consistent with the existing literature. A study by Karaduman et al. showed that IL-10 inhibits TNF-α production [35]. This pattern was reflected in the present results: a significant increase in IL-10 was observed in the Cu-NiTi group, and at the same time, TNF-α levels did not increase significantly (P = 0.082). This suggests that the response induced by the Cu-NiTi wire is actively suppressing proinflammatory pathways through an increase in IL-10, which is biologically advantageous. However, these mechanistic explanations are hypothetical and require direct experimental validation in future studies.

The absence of a significant change in TNF-α in the present study was in contrast to the observation by Pramustika et al., who observed an increase in TNF-α with both wire types [18]. The difference is most likely due to a strong confounding variable in their study; they used two completely different bracket systems (Preadjusted Edgewise vs. Self-ligating) simultaneously with two different sets of wires. As emphasized in the systematic review by Inchingolo et al., the bracket type and wire ligating method (friction level) might directly influence the inflammatory response [1]. The present study eliminated this confounding variable by using the same bracket system (MBT) for both groups and more accurately isolated the net effect of the wire alloy. On the other hand, the present findings regarding TNF-α are in line with the findings of El-Sayed et al., who also found no difference in TNF-α levels [19].

An alternative hypothesis for the increase in IL-10 could be related to the corrosion of the wires. Some studies, such as Habar et al., reported that Cu-NiTi wires may have lower corrosion resistance and release more nickel ions [36]. Accordingly, it can be argued that the increase in IL-10 is a modulatory immune response to the release of more metal ions to prevent the development of sensitivity or a severe inflammatory reaction. However, it should be emphasized that the scientific evidence regarding Cu-NiTi corrosion is complex and contradictory. As mentioned before, some studies have reported that certain Cu-NiTi compositions can improve biocompatibility and reduce nickel release by creating a more stable surface oxide layer [15, 37]. Given the crucial function of mechanical forces in OTM, the hypothesis of a response to a more optimal force pattern still seems more likely.

The lack of statistically significant changes in the levels of the proinflammatory cytokines TNF-α and IL-22 also requires interpretation. First, this could be a positive result, indicating that the forces exerted by both wires in the early stages are at a level that does not lead to a severe and damaging inflammatory burst. Second, as discussed earlier, the increase in IL-10 in the Cu-NiTi group may have actively prevented the increase in TNF-α.

The present study is based on a previously conducted randomized clinical trial, which had a double-blind design, with controlled confounding variables such as the bracket system and strict inclusion and exclusion criteria. Also, simultaneous assessment of a panel of pro-inflammatory and anti-inflammatory cytokines provides a more comprehensive picture than single-indicator studies, which has also been proposed by Alhadlaq [2]. On the other hand, the small sample size reduces the statistical power of the study and limits the generalizability of the results. Furthermore, a formal sample size calculation was not performed for this secondary cytokine analysis, which should be considered when interpreting the statistical findings. Taken together, these findings suggest that Cu-NiTi wire induces a modulated and protective early biological response in periodontal tissues of the mandibular anterior region, possibly due to its gentler and more continuous force release. This study highlights the importance of the relation between orthodontic wires and host biological reactions, signifying a step towards better-informed and customized selection of therapeutic devices in modern orthodontics. It is recommended that future studies consider how these cytokine levels are related to specific orthodontic clinical outcomes to offer an improved awareness of their clinical relevance. Future studies are also encouraged to provide a more comprehensive longitudinal picture of the biological response to different wire alloys.

Conclusions

The insertion of Cu-NiTi orthodontic wires causes a reaction in periodontal tissue characterized by a statistically significant increase in the regulatory and anti-inflammatory cytokine IL-10 within the first 24 h of treatment compared to conventional NiTi archwires. In contrast, the levels of pro-inflammatory markers, TNF-α and IL-22, remain comparable between both types of wires with no significant changes during the studied time periods. These findings suggest that Cu-NiTi wires may promote orthodontic tooth movement (OTM) in the mandibular anterior region through a distinct, more modulated early biological pathway.

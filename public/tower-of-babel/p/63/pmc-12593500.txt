Benefit and prognosis prediction of radiation therapy in colorectal cancer liver metastases: a retrospective, population-based, propensity score–matched study
Authors: Tingting Wei, Zhengyan Pan, Xianyan Feng, Yaxiu Long
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractBackgroundThe benefits and optimal sequencing of radiation therapy (RT) for colorectal cancer liver metastases (CRLM) remain unclear. Therefore, using a large population-based dataset, this retrospective study aimed to evaluate the impact of RT on overall survival (OS) and determine its optimal timing. Furthermore, a nomogram was developed to predict OS in CRLM patients receiving RT.MethodsThis population-based retrospective cohort study used data from the Surveillance, Epidemiology and End Results database, including 5141 patients diagnosed with CRLM between 2010 and 2019. OS rates were conducted using Kaplan-Meier curves and log-rank tests. Propensity score matching (PSM) was employed to reduce baseline confounding between the RT and non-RT groups. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were performed to identify independent prognostic factors for OS in CRLM patients receiving RT. A nomogram for predicting OS was constructed using R software.ResultsAfter PSM, 536 patients were included in both the RT and non-RT groups. The mean OS time was 26 months in the RT group and 22 months in the non-RT group, with a statistically significant difference confirmed by the log-rank test (p<0.001). Cox regression analysis identified RT as an independent protective factor for OS (HR=0.55, 95% CI 0.41 to 0.73). Subgroup analysis based on RT timing revealed that the combined preoperative and postoperative RT group had the highest OS, followed by the preoperative RT group, and then the postoperative RT group. Additionally, chemotherapy, brain, bone and other site metastases, and distant lymph node metastases were identified as independent factors affecting OS in CRLM patients receiving RT. The nomogram exhibited moderate discriminative performance in predicting OS, with Area Under the ROC Curve (Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve) (AUC) values of 0.634 and 0.637 in the training and validation sets, respectively.ConclusionsRT, particularly when administered in a combined preoperative and postoperative approach, is associated with long-term survival benefits in CRLM patients. The constructed nomogram, while demonstrating moderate predictive accuracy, serves as an initial tool for individualised OS estimation. These hypothesis-generating findings highlight the potential value of RT and warrant further validation through larger, prospective studies to develop more robust predictive models for personalised treatment strategies.Keywords: Risk management, Hepatology, Adult radiotherapy

Introduction

According to the 2024 cancer statistics, colorectal cancer (CRC) ranks as the third most common malignancy in incidence and the second leading cause of cancer-related mortality.1 The liver is the most frequent site of metastasis in CRC, with synchronous liver metastases present in approximately 15%–25% of patients at initial diagnosis.2 These metastases significantly influence treatment options and serve as a major determinant of patient survival.3 Surgical resection of liver metastases remains the only curative treatment option. However, due to extensive hepatic involvement or severe comorbidities, <20% of patients are considered eligible for surgical resection.4 For these patients with unresectable liver metastases, multidisciplinary comprehensive treatment, chemotherapy, radiation therapy (RT) and molecularly tailored systemic agents (eg, anti-EGFR/anti-VEGF therapies or immunotherapy) enable comprehensive management of metastatic disease, thereby improving the 2-year survival rate. As a minimally invasive and locally effective treatment, RT plays an integral role in the multidisciplinary management of colorectal cancer liver metastases (CRLM).

RT involves the targeted delivery of X-rays, which induce DNA double-strand breaks and generate reactive oxygen species, ultimately leading to cell death. RT, which can be applied to both primary tumours and liver metastases, provides durable local control and potentially augments systemic antitumour responses.4 5 Current guidelines, including those established by ESMO, recommend the application of RT for definitive, adjuvant and palliative intents.6 External beam RT, the most widely used modality, provides high-precision irradiation to target volumes including primary tumours, surgical beds or regional lymph nodes and forms the foundation of many curative treatment regimens.7 Stereotactic body radiotherapy (SBRT)/stereotactic ablative body radiotherapy delivers highly precise, ablative doses to oligometastatic liver lesions while sparing surrounding functional liver parenchyma, making it a valuable non-invasive option for local control.8 Brachytherapy involves the implantation of radioactive sources directly into or adjacent to tumour tissue, allowing for highly conformal dose distribution that maximises tumour control while minimising exposure to surrounding healthy structures. Radioisotope therapy employs systemically administered radionuclides that are selectively absorbed by specific cancer cells, making it particularly valuable for treating metastatic diseases. The strategic integration of these modalities, guided by disease characteristics and therapeutic intent, enables personalised and optimised radiation treatment approaches.9 However, the existing literature indicates that the role of RT in CRLM remains underexplored, particularly regarding its optimal timing (preoperative, postoperative or combined) and the specific patient populations most likely to benefit.

A nomogram serves as a visual statistical tool that integrates multiple predictor variables to quantify individual prognosis. Prior studies have developed nomograms to evaluate the benefit of combining preoperative chemotherapy with surgery in patients with CRLM.5 The survival benefits and optimal timing of RT in patients with CRLM remain underexplored. In this study, we used data from the Surveillance, Epidemiology and End Results (SEER) database, which provides a large and diverse cohort for robust analysis. However, due to the absence of detailed RT records in SEER—such as treatment site (primary vs metastatic), radiation techniques and dosage—comparative analyses related to these parameters are inherently limited. Our objective was to determine the impact of RT in CRLM by a large population-based dataset, and identify the optimal sequencing of RT, and develop a predictive model for overall survival (OS). These insights could guide more personalised RT treatment strategies and improve survival rates and quality of life for affected patients.

Methods

Patient and public involvement

Patients or the public were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination plans of this research. This approach was consistent with the retrospective, population-based nature of the study, which used de-identified data from the SEER database.

Ethical considerations

This study adhered to the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology guidelines. As a retrospective cohort analysis based on publicly available data from the SEER database, it complies with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Since the SEER database contains fully de-identified information and no individual patient identifiers are disclosed, the requirement for informed consent was waived, and the study was exempt from review by the institutional ethics committee.

Patient selection and data extraction

Patients diagnosed with CRLM between 2010 and 2019 were identified using SEER*Stat software (V.8.4.4). Cancer staging was performed according to the American Joint Committee on Cancer (TNM staging system for CRC). The inclusion criteria were: (1) Histologically confirmed CRC and (2) Presence of liver metastases at the time of initial diagnosis (synchronous liver metastases). The exclusion criteria were: (1) Unknown TNM stage, (2) Unknown treatment information and (3) Unknown metastatic sites.

Study variables

Based on data available in the SEER database, 14 variables were selected for analysis. These included age, sex, race, T stage, N stage, surgery (primary colorectal tumour), radiotherapy (RT), RT sequencing (preoperative, postoperative or combined), chemotherapy and sites of distant metastasis (bone, brain, lung, distant lymph nodes and other locations). RT was categorised based on the codes documented in the SEER database, including: beam radiation, radioactive implants, radioisotopes, combination of beam with implants or isotopes, radiation not otherwise specified (NOS) and palliative radiation treatment. Race was classified as White, Black or Other (including American Indian/Alaska Native and Asian/Pacific Islander).

The presence of distant organ metastases was defined according to the SEER database as metastases identified at the initial diagnosis of CRC. The recorded sites encompassed bone, brain, lung, distant lymph nodes and other locations. The primary endpoint of this study was OS, calculated from the date of definitive CRC diagnosis until the date of death from any cause. Patients who were still alive at the last follow-up were censored on that date.

Statistical analysis

Patients were stratified into RT and non-RT groups based on whether they received RT. To mitigate selection bias and facilitate balanced comparison, propensity score matching (PSM) was performed at a 1:1 ratio between the two groups. The RT group was further randomly divided into training and validation sets in a 7:3 ratio. Categorical variables were compared between the RT and non-RT groups (both before and after PSM) and between the training and validation sets using the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate.

OS was compared between the RT and non-RT groups using Kaplan-Meier survival analysis and log-rank tests. Subgroup analyses were also performed based on RT sequencing. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were conducted to identify independent prognostic factors for OS among patients receiving RT. Based on the results of the multivariate analysis, a nomogram was constructed using the ‘RMS’ package in R software. The nomogram was validated using the validation cohort, with performance evaluated through ROC curves, calibration curves and decision curve analysis (DCA).

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software (V.26.0) and R (V.4.3.2). All tests were two-tailed, and a p value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Result

Clinical characteristics of patients with CRLM

A total of 5141 eligible patients with CRLM ultimately included were included in this study. Of these, 4604 patients (89.55%) did not receive RT, while 537 patients (10.45%) received RT. To minimise bias and control for confounding factors, PSM was applied at a 1:1 ratio, balancing the two groups across covariates including age, sex, race, T stage, N stage, surgery, chemotherapy and distant metastasis sites (bone, brain, lung, distant lymph nodes and other sites).

After PSM, 536 patients with CRLM who received RT were successfully matched with 536 patients who did not receive RT (figure 1). Before PSM, significant differences were observed in baseline characteristics, including age, sex, race, T stage, N stage, surgery, chemotherapy, bone, lung, brain, distant lymph node and other site metastases (all p<0.05). After PSM, these characteristics were well-balanced between the two groups, with no statistically significant differences observed (all p>0.05), confirming the effectiveness of the matching procedure (table 1).

RT is associated with improved survival in CRLM patients

As shown in figure 2, both before and after PSM, the RT group demonstrated significantly higher OS rates compared with the non-RT group by Kaplan–Meier analysis and log-rank tests (p<0.05). Before PSM, the median OS was 29 months in the RT group and 26 months in the non-RT group, with 2-year OS rates of 77.3% and 52.3%, respectively. After PSM, the mean OS was 30 months in the RT group and 27 months in the non-RT group, with 2-year OS rates of 77.2% and 69.8%, respectively. After PSM, univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses identified age ≥50 years, N2 stage and brain metastases as independent risk factors for poorer OS. Conversely, chemotherapy and RT were both significant independent protective factors associated with improved OS (table 2).

Subgroup analysis based on RT sequencing

Patients in the RT group were categorised into three subgroups based on treatment sequence: combined preoperative and postoperative RT (n=37), preoperative RT (n=322) and postoperative RT (n=177). Survival analysis revealed that combined preoperative and postoperative RT group and preoperative RT group exhibited significantly higher OS compared with postoperative RT group (both p<0.05). However, no significant difference in OS was observed between the combined preoperative and postoperative RT group and the preoperative RT group (p=0.599) (figure 3).

Subgroup analysis of overall survival within the radiotherapy group. RT, radiation therapy.

Predictive factors for OS in CRLM patients receiving RT

The 536 patients in the RT group were randomly divided into training and validation sets at a ratio of 7:3. No statistically significant differences in baseline characteristics were observed between the two sets (online supplemental material table 1). Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses identified chemotherapy (p<0.001), brain metastases (p<0.001), bone metastases (p<0.001), other site metastases (p=0.001) and distant lymph node metastases (p=0.002) as significantly associated with poorer OS. Importantly, chemotherapy was identified as a significant independent protective factor.

Development of a nomogram to predict OS

Based on the results of the multivariate logistic regression analysis, a nomogram was constructed to predict OS in CRLM patients receiving RT (figure 4). The nomogram assigns weighted points to each predictive variable, and the total point score corresponds to an estimated probability of survival. Among the included factors, brain metastases contributed most substantially to decreased OS, followed by chemotherapy (protective), other site metastases, bone metastases and distant lymph node metastases.

Nomogram for predicting overall survival in the radiotherapy group.

Validation and performance evaluation of the nomogram

The ROC curve analysis evaluated the predictive performance of the nomogram, with AUC values of 0.634 for the training set (figure 5A) and 0.637 for the validation set (figure 5B), indicating moderate discriminative ability. Calibration curves demonstrated close alignment between predicted and observed survival probabilities, suggesting good model reliability (online supplemental material figure 1A and B). Furthermore, DCA revealed that the use of the nomogram provided a higher net benefit across a range of threshold probabilities compared with the treat-all or treat-none strategies, supporting its potential utility for clinical decision-making in CRLM patients receiving RT (online supplemental material figure 2A and B).

Discussion

The optimal treatment strategy for patients with CRLM has been widely debated. Surgical resection of both the primary tumour and metastatic liver lesions remains the fundamental approach for curative-intent treatment, with reported 5-year and 10-year disease-specific survival rates of 68% and 55%, respectively.10 However, for patients with unresectable liver metastases, the integration of targeted biologic agents and locoregional therapies has been shown to further improve survival.11 With the expanding use of RT in CRLM management, there is a growing need to validate its survival benefits and optimal sequencing using large-scale, real-world data.

This study provides compelling evidence that RT is associated with a significant improvement in OS of patients with CRLM. This survival benefit was confirmed and remained statistically significant after PSM, with the RT group consistently demonstrating superior OS compared with the non-RT group. Cox regression analysis further confirmed RT as an independent protective factor for OS. The mean OS was 30 months in the RT group compared with 27 months in the non-RT group, with 2-year OS rates of 77.2% and 69.8%, respectively. Although the SEER database lacks detailed information on the specific site or technique of RT, making it difficult to analyse the impact of technique-specific RT on CRLM, our study nonetheless highlights the significant role that diverse RT modalities play a critical role in enhancing patient longevity. Previous studies have reported that SBRT has been associated with 2-year OS rates ranging from 26%–83%, surpassing outcomes achieved with optimal supportive care or systemic therapy alone.8 SBRT delivers ablative doses with extreme precision, minimising dose to the surrounding functional liver parenchyma. SBRT does not significantly increase the technical difficulty or complication rates of subsequent hepatectomy.12 The survival advantage observed in our analysis reinforces existing literature and supports the integration of RT into the treatment regimen for CRLM patients.

Additionally, determining the optimal sequencing of RT is crucial for informing clinical decisions and enhancing overall patient management. The choice between preoperative and postoperative RT involves a careful risk-benefit analysis, as each approach offers distinct advantages and drawbacks. The advantages of preoperative RT include the potential to damage malignant cells that may spread during surgery and the ability to reduce tumour size (downstaging), thereby facilitating resection. However, this approach has been historically associated with increased risks of intraoperative challenges, such as tissue adhesions and vascular injury, as well as postoperative complications including biliary fistula and liver failure, particularly with conventional radiation techniques and doses.7 Postoperative RT is primarily aimed at eradicating residual subclinical disease in the tumour bed, thereby reducing the risk of local recurrence and potentially improving OS.13 Combined preoperative and postoperative RT not only enhances survival but may also improve the efficacy of concurrent systemic therapies. This study revealed that a combined preoperative and postoperative RT approach was associated with the most favourable OS, outperforming preoperative RT alone, which in turn was superior to postoperative RT alone. This hierarchy of outcomes underscores the significant survival benefit conferred by administering RT prior to surgery. Evidence from a randomised trial demonstrated that preoperative RT reduces the relative risk of recurrence by more than half, while postoperative RT reduces the risk by one-third. This implies a potential superiority of preoperative RT in terms of biological efficacy and recurrence prevention.14 Similarly, numerous randomised trials in rectal cancer have shown that preoperative RT not only reduces local recurrence rates by over half but also offers a modest improvement in survival, outperforming postoperative RT which has more limited effects.13 An analysis of the SEER database revealed that preoperative RT was associated with marginally better 10-year OS and disease-specific survival in stage II–III rectal cancer compared with postoperative RT.15 Moreover, for select patient groups such as those with pT1–3N1M0 rectal cancer without neoadjuvant therapy, postoperative RT did not significantly improve survival rates.16 A prospective randomised study comparing preoperative and postoperative chemoradiotherapy in locally advanced rectal cancer found that preoperative chemoradiotherapy improved local control and was associated with reduced toxicity, although it did not significantly improve OS.17 Supporting these results, Luo et al, using a large-population database, found that patients with metastatic rectal cancer patients receiving preoperative RT had better prognoses than those receiving postoperative RT.18 These studies suggest that preoperative treatment strategies may offer better prognoses and lower recurrence rates compared with postoperative approaches. Our findings support the use of combined preoperative and postoperative RT as a preferred option for CRLM patients, providing additional long-term prognostic benefits.

The long-term survival of patients with CRLM who received RT is influenced by a multitude of clinical and pathological factors. Multivariate analysis identified chemotherapy as a protective factor associated with improved OS, which is consistent with established evidence supporting the role of chemotherapy in controlling metastatic disease and enhancing survival outcomes.19 20 Chemotherapy plays a critical role in improving the prognosis of patients with CRC lung metastases.19 Chemotherapy is essential for controlling distant metastases in CRC patients following curative surgery, by eradicating circulating tumour cells and micro-metastases.20 Additionally, neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy has been associated with improved long-term survival in locally advanced rectosigmoid junction cancer.21 However, some studies have reported chemotherapy-induced liver damage without corresponding improvements in survival outcomes, particularly in patients with pathological T1-3N0M0 rectal mucinous adenocarcinoma.22 Therefore, the potential benefits of chemotherapy should be carefully evaluated by multidisciplinary teams comprising surgeons, medical oncologists and radiologists.23

Notably, brain, bone, distant lymph node and other site metastases were identified as independent risk factors negatively impacting OS in CRLM patients receiving RT. The presence of extrahepatic metastases is often considered a contraindication for local treatment of CRLM. A retrospective study demonstrated that CRLM resection carries a higher risk of disease recurrence in patients with baseline extrahepatic metastases.24 Similarly, a large retrospective study involving 13 662 CRLM patients found that those with extrahepatic metastases had significantly poorer prognoses.25 Liu et al reported that bone, brain, liver and lung metastases are independent risk factors for OS in rectal adenocarcinoma patients who received chemoradiotherapy but did not undergo surgery.3 Brain metastases in CRC are relatively rare, with prevalence rates ranging from 0.1%–14%; however, their presence is associated with a two-fold increase in mortality and a poor 5 year OS of approximately 15%.26 Bone metastases in CRLM signify a more aggressive tumour biology and mark disease progression. Their associated complications, such as severe pain, pathological fractures, hypercalcaemia and nerve compression, further contribute to reduced patient survival.27 This study corroborates these findings by demonstrating that the presence of extrahepatic metastases significantly impairs survival outcomes in CRLM patients receiving RT.

To provide a clinically valuable tool for predicting survival in CRLM patients receiving RT, we developed a nomogram based on the results of multivariate Cox regression analysis. The nomogram quantifies each predictor, with chemotherapy identified as the most significant protective factor, while brain, other site, bone and distant lymph node metastases were identified as risk factors. This nomogram enables clinicians to identify factors influencing survival, improve risk stratification, tailor interventions to individual patient profiles and optimise therapeutic outcomes. The ROC curves demonstrated moderate predictive accuracy, and the DCA confirmed the clinical utility of the nomogram.

Our study has several limitations. First, significant sample size imbalances were observed between the RT subgroups, particularly in the combined preoperative and postoperative RT cohort, which was notably small (n=37), which may compromise statistical power and increase the risk of Type II errors. Therefore, the apparent inferiority of combined preoperative and postoperative RT may be an artefact of the small sample size rather than a true biological effect. These findings should be considered hypothesis-generating and require validation in larger, prospectively assembled cohorts with balanced group sizes. Second, as a retrospective study using the SEER database, our analysis lacks detailed RT parameters, including specific irradiation targets (eg, primary tumour vs liver metastases) and technical modalities (such as 3D-CRT, intensity-modulated radiation therapy or SBRT). Consequently, the broadly classified ‘RT’ variable encompasses a heterogeneous combination of approaches, including beam radiation, radioactive implants, radioisotopes or combination therapies, which prevents us from differentiating the individual contributions of specific RT techniques. Third, the patients in this study were diagnosed between 2010 and 2019. Since then, advancements in medical therapies, including immunotherapy and novel chemotherapeutic agents, have significantly altered patient outcomes. These developments may influence the generalisability of our findings to current clinical practice.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study underscores the significant survival benefits associated with RT in patients with CRLM, particularly combined preoperative and postoperative RT. We developed a preliminary nomogram to predict OS by integrating key prognostic variables. However, the model demonstrated moderate discriminative performance, indicating the necessity for further refinement and external validation with larger, more detailed datasets prior to considering clinical application.

Using ARIMA and ETS models for forecasting water level changes for sustainable environmental management
Authors: Tropikë Agaj, Anna Budka, Ewelina Janicka, Valbon Bytyqi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
It is vital to provide useful hydrological forecasts for urban and agricultural water management, hydropower generation, flood protection and management, drought mitigation and alleviation, and river basin planning and management, among other things. This paper introduces a simple and flexible hydrological time series forecasting framework. Predicting water levels is crucial, given the need for sustainable environmental management. The prognosis should be reasonable to persuade individuals to take proper precautions. While many methods have been developed to predict water levels, here the effectiveness of two approaches to predicting river water levels was assessed. For this purpose, nine years of data were used, which were divided into input data (2014–2021) and validation data (2022), on water levels in the Morava e Binçës river for the Vitia station, in the form of monthly time series records, to identify the best model among those used and to identify the information necessary for water resource management and hazard control. Models Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average(ARIMA) and Error Trend and Seasonality, or Exponential Smoothing (ETS), were examined using the R package to determine the most accurate. The results indicate the applicability of both models, as evidenced by the Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) and the Mean Absolute Error (MAE). The predictive analysis based on historical water levels allowed the identification of distinct periods characterized by high and low water levels between 2022 and 2024, which is important for the area in question due to the numerous flood events occurring here.

Introduction

Damage from catastrophic events, often attributed to natural phenomena, is increasing worldwide due to the abnormal environment induced by global warming. It is projected that the frequency and intensity of these disasters will continue to rise. The performance of hydrological analyses, such as examining temporal interdependence in hydrological processes or evaluating trends, has long been a subject of interest among hydrological researchers1–3.

Furthermore, with unchecked population expansion, industrialization, and urbanization, many parts of the world face water scarcity. As a result, the situation worsens, leading to worldwide environmental degradation and climate change4,5 resulting in unsustainable freshwater demand, particularly in developing nations6. The opposite of this situation is excess water, which is associated with dangerous situations such as floods. Flood damage is increasing worldwide, and if floods can be predicted, economic and human losses from floods can be reduced. Flood hazard is determined by various factors, the most important of which are non-climatic (mainly changes in the catchment area and urbanization).

Several recent studies7–9 addressed quantifying the effect of land use and land cover change on runoff dynamics under extreme hydrological conditions. After reviewing some research stated that, whereas urbanization increases small frequent floods many times, substantial uncommon floods are not considerably influenced. In recent decades, numerous machine learning models have been created and utilized in hydrological and environmental research. These models include Artificial Neural Networks, Support Vector Machines, and Long Short-Term Memory10–17. Neural networks require large datasets to function effectively and generalize to new, unseen cases. ARIMA and ETS are more flexible and can provide good results even with smaller amounts of data, although, like any method, they gain accuracy with larger datasets18. Statistical methods emerge as an auspicious approach for analyzing massive data in the context of forecasting.

The ARIMA model is a widely used time series forecasting method. It combines the concepts of autoregression (AR), differencing (I), and moving average (MA). The field of forecasting has experienced significant growth in both theoretical advancements and practical applications during the past 15 years, following the influential review conducted by19. According to Otache20, the primary objective of time series analysis is to provide a comprehensive account of the temporal evolution of certain variables, such as the flow rate in a dam of specific dimensions. Time series modelling plays a crucial role in the planning and operational study of water resource systems since it enables data collection and forecasting of hydrologic variables. However, testing novel forecasting methodologies on large datasets is imperative before implementing them in engineering contexts. In this study, we have adhered to a crucial premise to ascertain the benefits and drawbacks of a straightforward and adaptable approach for predicting hydrological time series.

Schär21 used precipitation from meteorological data assimilation systems to forecast seasonal runoff in the Aral Sea basin in central Asia. The high correlations show that the statistical relationship between model-assimilated precipitation and subsequent runoff can be used to build a viable seasonal runoff forecasting system.

The ARIMA model is frequently used to construct time series data of interannual monthly data fluctuations. However, inter-monthly changes’ impact within each year must be addressed. The properties of time series data are recovered using clustering analysis on monthly data. In this study, an ARIMA model that accounts for inter-annual and inter-monthly variance is constructed. Regression analysis creates the correlation between characteristic quantity and monthly data each year. The model can predict characteristic quantity time series by difference, followed by the stationary treatment. Many scholars have utilized the ARIMA model to analyze hydrological processes without considering seasonal elements22–24. Valipour25 studied autoregressive moving average (ARMA), ARIMA, and autoregressive artificial neural network models in forecasting, where they found that the most effective was the dynamic artificial neural network model, as determined by comparing the root mean square error (RMSE) and mean bias error (MBE).

The ETS approach was used as the forecasting technique for predicting the river’s water level in this study. The ETS method forecasts univariate time series. The ETS model under consideration emphasizes the identification and analysis of trend and seasonal components. Brown, an Operations Research analyst for the US Navy, developed Exponential Smoothing26. In the early 1950s, Brown extended simple exponential smoothing to discrete data, focusing on incorporating trend and seasonality into the technique. Time series created the general exponential smoothing methodology. However, although exponential smoothing methods, such as the use of nonlinear methods (ETS), are considered to be optimal for forecasting based on innovation state space models, and nonlinear exponential smoothing models do not have a comparable ARIMA counterpart, the efficacy of exponential smoothing models in hybrid models has not been thoroughly investigated.

The work aim was to develop a water level prediction model for 2022–2024, which will enable the analysis of hydrological hazards in Kosovo for the Morava e Binçës River catchment with the Vitia water gauge station, with an area of 96 km2. Kosovo has witnessed an unprecedented surge in urbanization and construction since 1999, resulting in unplanned and illegal building activities in flood-prone zones, often disregarding building codes. Additionally, Kosovo is expected to experience accelerated growth in 2024, driven by increased consumption and investment. Forecasting will be made by using water level data for the Vitia Station from 2014 to 2021 and the 2022 water level data was used to validate forecasts.

Modern statistical methods ARIMA and ETS were selected as the basis for the research. Analyses were performed in accordance with the procedure implemented in the R 4.3.3 environment27–29. The overall (overarching) objective of the water status study is to identify the information necessary for water resources management and risk control, which can contribute to public awareness, especially of the Kosovo authorities, for the further development of the country. The work concerns various techniques for forecasting hydrological supplies as well as documentation and reporting of results. The need for iterative improvement of the model and cooperation with water data access systems was confirmed. The goal is to provide a comprehensive, multi-dimensional forecasting tool that is not only detailed but accessible and tailored to the needs of the region. Although there are many studies on forecasting using the methods we selected, none concerned modeling for a country with such a large degree of transformation and development, and for Kosovo, these are the first hydrological forecasting studies. The analysis performed opened new possibilities for future development for the discussed catchment, as well as for other, even more urbanized catchments of the Morava e Binçës River.

In the future, it is planned to forecast water levels for other sub-catchments with a much greater degree of urbanization, as it is currently impossible due to the lack of historical hydrological data. This study provides a basis for further work for the developing country.

This research, which was not previously conducted in Kosovo provides valuable insights and recommendations for action towards sustainable environmental management.

Results

Development of CORINE Land Cover (CLC) changes in catchment Morava e Binçës with station Vitia between 2006 and 2018

The study region encompasses heterogeneous agricultural areas that account for about 17% of the total coverage. The crucial aspect of this case study area is the presence of forests, which account for over 70% of the total land coverage. In contrast, the coverage of scrub and herbaceous vegetation associations is limited to a mere 7%. The segment about Artificial surfaces is equally influential, explicitly focusing on the urban environment. In the analyzed catchment area, there was a noticeable increase in artificial surfaces in the years 2006–2018, starting from 0.60% in 2006 and escalating to 2.13% in 2018 (Fig. 1). Pasture is identified as the final type that had a decline over the time frame spanning from 2006 to 2018. The observed decrease in land area, although relatively modest at less than 0.25 km2, can be attributed to the expansion of artificial surfaces, particularly within urban environments. This trend reflects a broader pattern of urbanization, where natural land areas, such as pastures, are converted to accommodate growing urban infrastructure. Consequently, the observed decrease in land area is primarily driven by this shift towards greater urban development, highlighting the ongoing transformation of landscapes to support urban growth and associated activities.

Water level in Morava E Binçës River – hydrological years 2014–2021

Figure 2 displays a monthly representation of the average historical water level of the Morava e Binçës River at the Vitia Station, covering the period from January 2014 to December 2021. By analyzing the variability of water levels, we determined specific periods of both high and low water. The upper limit for high water was established at 65 cm the lowest among the highest water levels (L-HWL), while the lower limit for low water was set at 23 cm the average among the lowest water levels (A-LWL). Throughout the examined multi-year timeframe, it was noted that 2016 there were floods in the first half of the year, with average water levels ranging from 65 to 80 cm. Furthermore, there were also brief peaks in the subsequent year, 2017, specifically in April and June. There were two low water levels throughout this extended timeframe, each lasting no more than two months. The recorded low water levels occurred over specific periods in 2014 (January–February, July–August) and 2021 (September).

The findings of the ETS model (Fig. 4) indicate that expected water levels in the upcoming three years may vary between 33 and 52 cm on average. Furthermore, when considering an 80% confidence interval for the same time frame, the anticipated range for water levels is estimated to be up to 81 cm, but with a 95% confidence interval ranging up to 96 cm. The conducted forecasting shows that, with identical constraints for upper and lower limits (A-LWL = 32 cm and L-HWL = 65 cm), the model’s water level values at the 80% significance level will be susceptible to extreme occurrences. Excessive water in the form of floods is anticipated throughout February, March, and April 2024. Furthermore, the act of forecasting has brought attention to the issue of water scarcity for every projected year, for the 80% and 95% confidence intervals used, but not for the average forecast water levels.

The forecast at the 80% confidence level indicates a water shortage in 2022 (August-December), in the second half of 2023 (August–November) and in 2024 (September–November–December). Moreover, analyzing the confidence level of 95%, the hydrological situation may be even more threatened. First, very low water levels are projected for the entire projected multi-year period from June 2023 to December 2024. On the other hand, excess water can be expected from January until May 2024 and December 2024. This means that the projected water levels for the Morava e Binçës River in Vitia Station in the following years may be significantly higher than in the observed 2014–2021 multi-year period.

The ARIMA model was also utilized to anticipate water levels for the years 2022–2024. Various model variables ((p, d,q)(P, D, Q)s) were considered, resulting in the selection of 22 forecasts for the given data sequence. The ARIMA (0,1,1)(0,1,1)12 (Fig. 4) model’s prediction results enabled the analysis of potential future extreme occurrences and the corresponding water level data with 80% and 95% probabilities. The model’s findings indicate that between 2022 and 2024 (average in months), the water levels at the Vitia Station in the Morava e Binçës River will vary from 32 to 54 cm, which means that they will be lower than the input data from 2014 to 2021. Furthermore, very probable model outcomes, with an 80% confidence interval, indicate that the average monthly water levels will be up to 87 cm with a confidence 95% interval ranging up to 101 cm. Hydrological low water level is anticipated throughout the latter half of 2023, from August 2024 to December 2024, with an 80% confidence interval. Alternatively, there is a potential for water levels to manifest between February and May 2024. Regrettably, the 95% confidence interval simulation reveals a bleaker outlook. The projection predicts that water levels will be low from June 2023 to December 2024, with two surges: February–May 2023 and February 2024–December 2024. When comparing the two models, there is a tiny disparity in their outcomes.

The ARIMA (0,1,1)(0,1,1)12 model findings indicate a significantly more pronounced forecast for the 80% and 95% confidence intervals than the ETS forecast. The ARIMA (0,1,1)(0,1,1)12 model predicts a noticeably increased percentage of low and high water levels, in the 80% confidence interval, with a 14% and 30% increase. Similarly, the ARIMA model’s forecasted scenario for the 95% confidence interval indicates a 3% increase in the proportion of lows and a 30% increase in the proportion of high water levels. The model predicts an elevated occurrence of severe hydrological events when comparing the input data with the model data, with a confidence interval between 80% and 95% over the input data.The Taylor diagram was conducted on normalized data on the water level of the Morava e Binçës River, specifically at the Vitia Station. The diagram revealed a correlation level of 0.868 (for ARIMA) and 0.835 (for ETS) between the simulated and actual data, indicating a good fit in both cases (Fig. 5). Such a high correlation allowed for a positive assessment of the model’s ability to accurately reproduce the temporal fluctuations of the analyzed variable (ARIMA and ETS). Furthermore, the standard deviation (σ) was measured below 1 in both cases (0.9411 cm for ARIMA and 0.9218 cm for ETS), while RMSE was recorded as 0.491 cm for ARIMA and 0.552 cm for ETS. The results suggest that both models are relatively good at reflecting the actual changes in water level, with a slight advantage for the ARIMA model in terms of correlation and a lower RMSE. The smaller σ for the ARIMA model also indicates that its forecasts are slightly closer to the mean, which might suggest better stability in its predictions. Nonetheless, both models demonstrate similar and acceptable levels of accuracy, making them valuable tools for analyzing and forecasting the water level of the Morava e Binçës River.

Discussion

Predicting river water levels is crucial for issuing flood alerts and managing water resources. Hydrological scientists have historically been interested in conducting hydrological studies, such as investigating the temporal dependency in hydrological processes or assessing trends. Hydrological forecasting has been explored by researchers such as1,2. Therefore, employing monthly data to enhance our understanding and characterization of river water level dynamics represents a noteworthy accomplishment with implications for both theoretical advancements and practical applications. Due to the time series structure of water level data from hydrological stations, researchers commonly utilize time-series hydrological prediction models to predict future data. Past data can be utilized to forecast future water levels, thereby uncovering concealed information. This is crucial for limiting the impact of floods, minimizing or averting disasters, and effectively managing water resources.

The last strategy is data-driven and relies on the statistical correlation between input and output data to predict the near future. The hydrological literature that relies on statistics and data analysis includes many case studies that examine and evaluate time series forecasts. Some notable examples include the works of30–33. The hydrological process is connected to time-sequential data34,35 and as a result, the prediction of hydrological time series relies on a predetermined number of prior steps chosen based on correlation. Scientists are continuously enhancing the accuracy and sophistication of predictive models. While numerous individual models are available for time series forecasting, they do not offer a reliable solution that can be universally applied with satisfactory results. Hybrid approaches are a suitable alternative that can provide higher performance when compared to individual solutions36,37. In recent time series modeling, novel hybrid models were employed38–41.

Studies mentioned previously have been conducted to forecast the water levels in rivers, lakes, and other aquatic systems globally, employing various time-series models. Predicting time series data, particularly water levels for flood warning systems, is crucial yet complex. Time series methods provide accurate predictions without requiring significant data collection and analysis investments42,43. The selection of a suitable forecasting method is contingent upon the existence of the following elements in the time series: trend, seasonality, cyclicality, and random effects. Conventional time series techniques like ARIMA and ETS are specifically developed to handle single seasonality patterns. However, they must be improved in effectively dealing with multiple seasonality impacts.

ARIMA and ETS are highly acclaimed and efficient forecasting models that have been proposed and extensively tested on hydrological time series. Results obtained in this study confirm previous research that even though the ARIMA model is not a novel method, it is still one of the most popular and applied methods for predicting river discharge and flood events19,20,22,25. In forecasting research, assessing the suitability of time series models involves examining whether the model residuals exhibit characteristics of a white noise process. As such, diagnostic tests play a crucial role in determining the statistical adequacy of the model, thereby enabling its utilization for forecasting purposes. In instances where diagnostic tests indicate inadequacy, an iterative process of identification, estimation, and diagnosis is undertaken until the most appropriate model is identified. Therefore, in this study, a forecasting procedure was undertaken, starting with model diagnosis and ending with the selection of the optimal model using models to predict water levels. In this work, forecasting water level data for a period of three years was performed using the ARIMA model and the ETS model, which are tools that can provide similar results under certain conditions. The factors that determine the effectiveness and similarity of the results of these models are primarily the characteristics of the data, their stability and the way the models fit the data history. In the context of three-year forecasts, the ARIMA approach turned out to be correct because the time series were not characterized by trends but by cyclical patterns that did not have to be strictly seasonal. Thanks to the precise selection of the AR, I and MA parameters, the ARIMA model was able to predict future values, taking into account both trends and possible cycles. On the other hand, the ETS model is used when the data clearly indicates trend and seasonality, and these elements are incorporated directly into the water level data forecasting process. The ETS model works best when the data shows a stable upward or downward trend and regular, consistent seasonal patterns. In the case of the Vitia station, it was important to accurately fit the ETS model to the data, which allowed for effective extrapolation of trends44. The comparability of the results obtained by both methods (ARIMA and ETS) should be attributed to the stability of the data, which means that time series without sudden structural changes and unexpected fluctuations are more predictable. Additionally, simple, linear trends and constant seasonality favor similar results in both models.

Nguyen45 analyzed water level forecasting for the Red River based on statistical machine learning methods and ARIMA. These models are evaluated using three extensive datasets of real-time water level measurements. The performance of each component model in this study was compared to the overall ARIMA model. The experimental results demonstrated that the combined ARIMA RF and ARIMA KNN approaches yielded superior and more dependable outcomes compared to other hybrid models. Nguyen shows that the RMSE value for applying single ARIMA methods is equal to 19.97 cm for 24 h analysis and 53.03 cm for 72 h analysis. Applying more than one method allows for a decrease of RMSE to 19.88 cm for 24 h analysis and 52.887 cm for 72 h analysis. The results obtained by Nguyen were confirmed in this study regarding the effectiveness of applying hybrid models to water level forecasting. Yifan et al.46 show that filtering time was decreased by 44–63%, and RMSE for water level predictions fell by up to 80%. Based on Pandit et al.47 the empirical findings indicate that the ARIMAX-LSTM hybrid model combination outperforms all other time series models in terms of RMSE and mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) values. In that case, the RMSE and MAPE values of these models have, on average been equal to 10.41% and 12.28%, respectively, which is a lower value compared to all other competing models.

Yu et al.48 highlighted that the accuracy of the ARIMA model is related to the forecasting period, which greatly influenced all measures for Hankou station at the Yangtze River. RMSE increased from 0.1202 m for 1-day analysis to 0.3692 m for 3-day analysis. Results obtained in this study show RMSE at a relatively low level of 7.517, which confirms the successful application of the method in this study. Also, other parameters analyzed in this study show similar water levels based on confidence levels. The HWL50% value is consistent with the ARIMA and ETS forecasts, as in both methods, for the forecasted 3 years, water levels at the 80% and 95% confidence levels oscillated around values close to 90 cm. The ARIMA model also assumes that in future years the forecast value at the 95% confidence level may exceed the HWL50% value, as the maximum value of the forecast water level may reach 101 cm.

The ARIMA model exhibits superior performance because it ensures stationarity in time series data during training and forecasting. The ARIMA model demonstrated lower values for mean absolute percentage error and RMSE in the study conducted by Adnan et al.49 on applying time series models for streamflow forecasting. The statistical evaluation of the predictive performance of the selected model (2,4)(2,2) is based on the results produced from the test data set of the decade 2000–2010, as conducted by Yu et al.48 for the Yangtze River. The projected outcomes met expectations, although there were some uncertainties due to sudden variations in water flow. The model was chosen based on the AIC, as described by Katimon et al.50.

Results obtained by Liu51 confirm that full forecast performance using ETS is highly related to rainfall predictions. The observation data of Fuzhou City ETS represent the full forecast performance of the model. Radar rainfall prediction exhibits a high percentage of successful predictions. However, the model’s efficiency could be more flawless due to the occurrence of a large proportion of false alarms. Consequently, the prediction model can provide early notice for most rainfall events.

Panigrahi52 applied the ETS-ANN model and proposed that the time series can be divided into two components, which can be either linear or nonlinear. The results suggested that none received the best performance for all the datasets. In a few specific cases, the individual models demonstrated higher performance when compared to the hybrid models. Mgandu et al.53 show that the value of RMSE for the ETS(A, Ad, A) model applied for forecasting monthly water levels in Mtera dam for five years was equal to 1.16369, while the RMSE value in this study was equal to 6.487. It shows a higher value of RMSE which can be explained by the fact that the forecasting for the river has higher uncertainty because of covering area and changing hydrometeorological parameters along the rivercourse compared to a single reservoir object. Yongbin et al.54 through a comparison with normal ARIMA and ETS procedures over three forecasting intervals, it was discovered that the hybrid ARIMA-ETS model demonstrated much lower evaluation indicator values in both the training and testing sets. Results obtained by other authors showed that there are some limitations to using ARIMA and ETS models for water level analyses, the application of which is not always successful. These limitations should be taken into account in future analyses.

In the case of this study, the effectiveness of the models was discussed using basic parameters such as standard deviation, R2, and RMSE. In addition, a comparative Taylor diagram was made and KGE values were calculated. Analyzing the Taylor diagram, it can be stated that the observed data matched the values modeled using both models very well. These data showed a high level of correlation of 0.868 (for ARIMA) and 0.835 (for ETS), with a standard deviation of less than 1, which indicates a good fit in both cases and a positive assessment of the model’s ability to accurately reproduce the time fluctuations of the analyzed variable (ARIMA and ETS). A similarly high fit was demonstrated in the case of KGE values, which amounted to 0.7321 and 0.7208, for ARIMA and ETS, respectively. These values were higher than 0 and closer to 1, which indicates a high fit of the model data to the actual data. However, the ARIMA model shows a slightly better fit for the Taylor diagram and KGE values. In conclusion, comparing the two analyses in this study which were mentioned above for Morava e Binçës River with a station in Vitia, both methods, ARIMA and ETS, show good results for forecasting water level. Previous studies55,56 have shown that floods occur in different regions in Kosovo based om historical data. Still is lack of information about flood forecasting which plays curcial role in flood protection. Future projections show that the risk of flood for analyzed catchments will be at the same or even high probability. To avoid or limit the occurrence of extreme phenomena it is important to analyze future projections and apply sustainable environmental management at national, regional, and local scales57–59. Analysis of hydrological parameters shows that Kosovo has a problem with excess water, which may occur given the 80% and 95% confidence levels and can cause danger to the environment, economy, and safety in the future. Sustainable environmental management is related not only to hydrology but also to morphology and land use changes. Non-regulated by law and uncontrolled urbanization can lead to increased flood probability, especially by increasing the number of buildings in natural valleys and river flow paths.

Conclusions

The statistical analysis conducted on the monthly time series data of historical water levels at the Vitia Station of the Morava e Binçës River, spanning from January 2014 to December 2021, facilitated the identification of probable distinct periods characterized by high and low water levels. This study demonstrates the efficacy of ARIMA models in accurately representing the water level patterns at the selected sites. ARIMA models exhibit superior performance in terms of generating the minimum forecast RMSE and displaying improved forecasting capabilities for extended seasonal time series compared to the ETS analysis (both for Taylor diagram and KGE values). These results contribute to the development of effective strategies for managing water resources in the spirit of sustainable development, which is crucial in the context of a changing climate and growing environmental requirements.

The results of this research not only complement the knowledge about the dynamics of changes in water levels, but also serve as a foundation for creating effective water resources management strategies, which are necessary in the face of global challenges related to climate change. Acknowledgment of ARIMA and ETS models, it is possible not only to predict and minimize the effects of extreme hydrological phenomena, but also to optimize the use of water resources in a way that supports sustainable development. This holistic perspective is crucial to increasing the resilience of local and regional communities to the effects of climate change. This provides an opportunity for better management of water resources while respecting their value for ecosystems and human activity.

Materials and methods

Study area

The study area is the catchment area of the Morava e Binçës River to the Vitia water gauge station (Vitia catchment), with an area of 96 km2, which is administratively located in the territory of the state of Kosovo and North Macedonia (Fig. 6). The selected catchment area represents the upper reaches of the Morava e Binçës River catchment, which is located in the Black Sea basin.

The Vitia catchment ranges from 506 m (Vitia city) to the highest point at 1,592 m. Notably, the Morava e Binçës River’s sources and course are intricately interlinked with the region’s diverse topography (Fig. 6). The hilly-mountainous terrain predominantly harbors shallow to moderately shallow brown soils on schist rocks, sandstone, and limestone, typically extending up to 50 cm depth with moderate permeability. Conversely, the Anamorava basin’s plain terrain hosts loamy diluvium and podzol-pseudo gley soil, reaching depths of 50–80 cm and exhibiting average permeability (Fig. 7).

Most slopes with higher gradients (< 12°) are developed throughout the catchment, with a small infiltration capacity. The presence of hard rocks and high gradient slopes have developed moderate to high drainage density, where a high dissection relief is modelled. The dominance of moderate to high gradient slopes contributes to high surface runoff. At the same time, the nearly elongated river catchment has created a high bifurcation ratio where four lower-rank streams contribute with their discharge to the main River, initiating flood. The shape of the Morava e Binçës catchment area is elongated, with a high risk of flooding (Fig. 7).

Data collection

For the water level forecast, water level data in the hydrological years 2014–2021 in the Morava e Binçës River together with the Vitia Station, published by the Hydrometeorological Institute of Kosovo (HMIK), were used.

The maps utilized in this analysis include the following datasets: “Topographic Map sheet for Kosova” sourced from the Military-Geographic Institute of Yugoslavia in 1979, “Topographic maps of Kosovo” at a scale of 1:25 000 provided by the Kosova Cadastral Agency in 2015, and “The Soil Map of Kosovo” at a scale of 1:50 000 developed by the Institute “Jaroslav Cerni” of Belgrade in 1974. CLC was used to analyze changes in the use of the catchment area for the years 2006 and 2018, obtained from the Copernicus Land Monitoring Service. Additionally, a Digital Elevation Model (DEM) with a resolution of 12.5 m, developed for Kosovo by the Alaska Satellite Facility, was used. All spatial analyses were performed using ArcGIS Desktop software.

Forecasting models

Initial time series analysis is a necessary step before applying more advanced modelling or forecasting techniques. Due to the large amount of data, time aggregation was applied to the level of monthly averages. Daily data from HMIK were used to calculate AWL, the lowest monthly water level (LWL) and HWL. Based on the AWL, LWL and HWL, (A-LWL) and L-HWL were calculated for the period 2014–2021. To analyze changes occurring over the period 2014–2024, two limits were adopted by the Peaks-Over-Threshold (POT) method, A-LWL and L-HWL. A-LWL was used to describe events related to water shortage (drought), on the other hand, L-HWL was used to describe the time characteristic of water excess (flood).

Water level data for the years 2014–2021 was decomposed, which allowed for a visual assessment of trends, seasonality and other potential anomalies in the data.

The statistical models underlying exponential smoothing methods have been analyzed. Statistical models generate the same point forecasts or forecast intervals. Each model consists of a measurement equation describing the observed data and several state equations describing how unobserved components or states (level, trend, seasonality) change over time. ETS denotes the three constituents: error, trend, and seasonality. This model was chosen because it considers the three components of a time series and can account for the seasonal character of the water level. Exponential smoothing methods utilize weighted averages of previous observations to generate forecasts.

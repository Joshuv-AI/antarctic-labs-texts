Study on serum vitamin A level in patients with type 1 diabetes: A systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Guan Yuanyuan, Dongjun Wang, Yang Hao, Yun Guan, Huaien Bu, Hongwu Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Background:There is controversy about the relationship between type 1 diabetes and vitamin A (VA) levels in the body. Through meta-analysis, the results of related studies can be aggregated to more accurately estimate the relationship between type 1 diabetes mellitus (T1DM) patients and the level of VA in the body.Methods:Our purpose is to review the study to investigate VA levels in type T1DM patients and to provide recommendations for future studies. Until January 2024, we searched the National Library of Medicine (PubMed), Cochrane Library, Embase Databases, Web of Sciences, Scopus, China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), VIP databases (VIP) and WAN FANG databases. After a systematic search, 8 case-control studies were included in our meta-analysis. In the process of data inclusion and extraction, the 2 reviewers selected literature independently of each other. In this study, RevMan5.3 software was used for meta-analysis.Results:Eight case-control studies involving 689 participants were screened. The results after meta-analysis showed that there was a significant difference in serum VA between the patients with T1DM and the control group (standardized mean difference [SMD] = −0.82, 95% CI [−1.29, −0.36], P < .001, random effects model) with significant heterogeneity among these studies (P < .001, I2 = 84%). Similarly, the difference in the high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) subgroup on serum VA (SMD = −0.99, 95% CI [−1.60, −0.38], P = .002) as well as the difference in the countries of Asia and Europe subgroup on serum VA (SMD = −0.60, 95% CI [−1.15, −0.05], P = .03; SMD = −1.06, 95% CI [−1.88, −0.24], P = .01) were suggested to be statistically significant. A significant result was also observed in the National Diabetes Data Group (NDDG) criteria subgroup (SMD = −0.48, 95% CI [−0.85, −0.12]).Conclusion:Serum VA levels seem to have decreased in T1DM patients. Further research is needed to strengthen this finding and clarify possible impact mechanisms.

1. Introduction

Type 1 diabetes mellitus (T1DM), also known as insulin-dependent diabetes mellitus, is a type of disease in which islet β cell damage and insulin secretion are reduced due to an abnormal autoimmune response.[1] T1DM patients account for 5% to 10% of diabetes mellitus patients worldwide.[2] Although the proportion of young people with diabetes mellitus in the world is increasing, T1DM is still the main type of diabetes mellitus in adolescents.[3] Italy and Finland are high-risk areas for T1DM.[4,5] The incidence of disease worldwide is increasing at a rate of 2% to 3% per year.[6] T lymphocytes and its cytokines play an important role in the pathogenesis of T helper lymphocytes.[7]

According to the types and functions of cytokines secreted by T helper lymphocytes, T helper lymphocytes are divided into Thl cells and Th2 cells, with the former secreting interleukin-2 (IL-2) and interferon gamma (IFN-γ), while Th2 cells secrete IL-4 and IL-5. The Th1/Th2 phenotype is mainly self-regulated and mutually regulated by cytokines secreted by the cell itself or other immune cells to maintain balance. When certain autoantigens are treated by antigen presenting cells (APCs), the autoimmune signal is released, Thl cells are activated, and the phenotype of Thl cells is dominant in Th cells.[8]

The large amount of IFN-γ produced by Th1 cells is very important to the pathogenesis of T1DM.[9] It can enhance the antigen presentation effect and increase the sensitivity of β cells to the destruction of islet infiltrated T cells, and Th2 cells play a protective role. Therefore, the imbalance of Th1/Th2 is the main mechanism of T1DM.[10]

Vitamin A, also known as retinol, has been proven to be able to regulate iron metabolism, improve anemia, improve the immune function of children and enhance the anti-infection ability of the digestive tract and respiratory tract.[11] However, in recent years, it has been increasingly recognized that vitamin A (VA) deficiency is closely related to the dysfunction of Th cells.[12] Van et al[13] and other studies have found that VA can effectively protect islet β cells from the autoimmune response, thus avoiding or reducing the occurrence of autoimmune diabetes mellitus. Zhong et al[14] found that retinoic acid (an in vivo product of VA metabolism) inhibited the inflammatory reaction of podocytes in diabetic kidney disease, thus playing a renal protective role in the early inflammatory reaction of glycosuria.

A previous study provided evidence of a link between diabetes mellitus and VA, but the specific relationship has not been clarified.[15] For instance, several studies suggested significantly decreased serum levels of VA in T1DM,[16] while some reports showed enhanced or no difference in VA concentrations between T1DM serum specimens compared with those of controls. Since the association between VA and T1DM is not clear, we systematically reviewed the current literature to explore serum VA levels in patients with T1DM and provide recommendations for future research.

2. Methods

2.1. Research strategy

We retrieved the National Library of Medicine (PubMed), Cochrane Library, Embase Databases, Web of Sciences, Scopus, China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), VIP databases (VIP) and WAN FANG databases to obtain qualified research published before January 2024. The search terms were “Vitamin A”[Mesh] OR “Retinol” [Mesh] OR “Aquasol A”[Mesh] OR “Tretinoin”[Mesh] OR “Retinaldehyde”; “Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus” [Mesh] OR “IDDM” [Mesh] OR “Insulin-Dependent” OR “Juvenile-Onset Diabetes”; and #1 AND #2. The above search strategy was run in PubMed and was tailored to each database when necessary. Table 1 is the search strategy of each database. Two independent reviewers selected and screened all records and, in the event of objection, asked the third reviewer for advice. The review applied PRISMA.[17]

2.2. Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria of this study were as follows: included a case group of patients with T1DM and a control group; the subjects included were those who were explicitly diagnosed with T1DM based on laboratory examination, clinical signs, or reported symptoms; studies providing data about VA (e.g. retinols, retinals, and retinoic acids) concentrations; the patient did not use vitamin A as a supplement during treatment; studies providing extractable data. The exclusion criteria were as follows: they were referring to other vitamins apart from VA, not directly linked to T1DM; they were written in non-English language and Chinese; nonclinical tests and animal experiments.

2.3. Data extraction

The following information was extracted: basic characteristics of the studies, including the first author, year of publication, mean age, study population (country and ethnicity), number of patients and controls, and diagnostic criteria. The mean and standard deviation of serum VA levels and laboratory methods for the determination of serum VA were also extracted.

2.4. Assessment of methodological quality

Two independent investigators independently assessed the quality of each study using the Newcastle–Ottawa quality assessment scale.[18] The study quality was defined as low, medium and high, and Newcastle–Ottawa Scale (NOS) scores of 1–3, 4–6 and 7–9 were analyzed separately. Any differences were studied in the original study by a third author and resolved by a joint assessment.

2.5. Statistical analysis

2.5.1. Extraction and merging of data

Review Manager 5.3 software was used to extract relevant dichotomies or continuous data from the literature for analysis. Different description methods are used for different data types. The data type is a dichotomous data use risk ratio (RR) description, and the data type is described by means of the mean difference (MD) and standard deviation for continuous variables. The 95% CI and forest plot were applied to the 2 methods used in this study. The chi-squared test and I2 (inconsistency) statistical method were used to detect heterogeneity (P < .10 or I2 > 50%), which indicated that there was a significant difference in heterogeneity. A fixed effects model was used when P > .1 and I2 < 50%, and a random effects model was used when P < .1 or I2 ≥ 50%.

3. Results

3.1. Study selection

A total of 526 reports were screened, 320 of which were excluded due to duplication of publications. After reading the title and summary, 206 articles were screened out, and 62 articles were retained. Among them, 36 articles did not meet the specific inclusion criteria, eighteen of which were excluded due to the following reasons: They were conducted in vitro (n = 9); They did not provide data on T1DM patients (n = 18); They were written in non-English language and Chinese (n = 1). Finally, a total of 8 case-control studies were included,[19–26] including 267 cases and 422 controls. The filtering process is shown in Figure 1.

3.4. Outcome results

3.4.1. Main results

Figure 2 shows the forest plots of serum VA. The number of trials included was 8. After the study, we found that there was a significant difference in serum VA between the patients with T1DM and the control group (standardized mean difference [SMD] = −0.82, 95% CI [−1.29, −0.36], P < .001, random effects model), with significant heterogeneity among these studies (P < .001, I2 = 84%).

Meta-analysis for the association of serum VA levels with T1DM risk using a random effects model. SD = standard deviation, T1DM = type 1 diabetes mellitus, VA = vitamin A.

3.4.2. Subgroup outcome

In the subgroup analysis of T1DM, there was a difference in mean age > 18 years or ≤ 18 years old (SMD = −0.48, 95% CI [−0.94, −0.01], P = .05; SMD = −1.14, 95% CI [−2.03, 0.24], P = .01) when compared with the control group. This indicated that serum VA levels tended to be lower across all age distributions in T1DM patients (Fig. 3). Similarly, HPLC subgroup differences in serum VA in T1DM patients (SMD = −0.99, 95% CI [−1.60, −0.38], P = .002) as well as differences in serum VA among T1DM patients in Asian and European countries (SMD = −0.60, 95% CI [−1.15, −0.05], P = .03; SMD = −1.06, 95% CI [−1.88, −0.24], P = .01) were suggested to be statistically significant (Figs. 4 and 5). The microfluorescence (MIF) subgroup included only 1 study, which showed no significant difference in serum VA levels in T1DM patients. A significant result was also observed in the NDDG criteria subgroup (SMD = −0.48, 95% CI [−0.8, −0.12]). Non-significant results were also observed in the American Diabetes Association (ADA) and World Health Organization (WHO) criteria subgroups (ADA subgroup: SMD = −2.01, 95% CI [−4.88, 0.87]; WHO subgroup SMD = −0.62, 95% CI [−1.54, 0.29]; Fig. 6). Only the patients with duration of disease > 1 year and < 6 years had a significant difference in VA compared to controls (SMD = −1.31, 95% CI [−2.39, 0.24]; Fig. 7). Notably, the analysis of patients with a disease duration of less than 1 year and more than 6 years was limited by the number of studies included at that time. 4. Discussion and conclusion

4.1. Summary of the main results

Vitamin A is an active group of ionone derivatives of all-trans retinol, including retinol, retinaldehyde, rhein and ester.[27] It was found that VA could interfere with the production of IFN-γ in peripheral blood mononuclear cells, and the ratio of IFN-γ/interleukin-4 (IL-4) was significantly reduced.[28] It has been suggested that VA can correct the imbalance of Th1/Th2 in patients with T1DM mainly by inhibiting the function of Th1 cells.[29] At the same time, it can effectively protect islet beta cells from attack by the autoimmune response, reduce islet inflammation and protect islet function to avoid or reduce the occurrence of autoimmune diabetes mellitus.[30] Hyperglycemia leads to oxidative damage in different ways. In hyperglycemia, the intracellular antioxidant level is significantly reduced.[31] Vitamin A, as a vitamin with strong oxidative properties, was not only deficient due to insufficient dietary intake but also depleted in the body due to persistent hyperglycemia, reducing its levels.

Our meta-analysis shows that serum VA levels in T1DM patients often show lower than healthy people. We summarized 8 studies (263 patients and 422 controls), and then analyzed the serum VA levels of T1DM patients compared with healthy people. We analyzed the effects of blood test methods, nationality, age, diagnostic criteria, and T1DM duration on serum VA.

In related research, the findings of Keller et al[32] can provide theoretical support for the results of this study, as serum VA levels are reduced in T1DM patients. Some studies[33,34] have found that VA has a preventive effect as an adjuvant therapy for T1DM. Combined with the results of this experiment, VA is closely related to the occurrence and development of T1DM.

In the blood test method of the subgroup analysis, only 1 of the tests used MIF detection methods, the remaining subgroup summary results were equal to the overall results, and the T1DM serum VA level was lower than that of the control group. Since HPLC determines the content of VA in serum while MIF determines the content of VA and VA ester in serum, the determination value of the same standard by the latter method was higher than that determined by the former method.[35] For the diagnostic criteria of T1DM, the results of the test summary using the ADA standard suggested that there was no significant difference between T1DM and the normal control group, and at the same time, the WHO standard suggested that there was no significant difference between T1DM and the normal control group. However, it is worth noting that the number of trials included in these 2 subgroups is small (only 2), of which the WHO subgroup has a test-based VA detection method of MIF, and as previously noted, the serum VA content measured by the MIF is high. This could be the cause of the lack of significant difference in the WHO subgroup analysis. Compared with WHO criteria, ADA criteria have higher sensitivity and less stringent inclusion criteria, which also means that ADA criteria may contain T1DM cases that are less severe than WHO criteria.[36] This may partly explain why serum VA levels of T1DM did not decrease significantly when using the ADA criteria. When analyzing the subgroup of T1DM duration. We found that only patients with a duration between 1 year and 6 years had a significant difference in serum VA levels compared to controls. However, the results for patients with duration less than 1 year and more than 6 years is limited by the scarce number of included studies.

4.2. Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, when reading the included literature carefully, we found that the literature did not specify the severity of the disease in patients with T1DM. Second, the diagnostic criteria of T1DM differed between the 8 case-control studies, which may have affected the results of this meta-analysis. Finally, this review included only case-control studies. In the future, there is a need for a greater diversity of research included in the analysis, such as multi-centered studies, more rigorous clinical reports, and prospective studies.

4.3. Clinical implications

We summarize the current research status of serum VA in patients with T1DM and provide data to support future T1DM clinical trials. Although this study suggests that T1DM patients have lower serum VA levels, due to the low quality and clinical heterogeneity of these research methods, current evidence and potential findings should be carefully interpreted. In future research, more attention should be paid to the effects of different blood test methods, nationality, age, and diagnostic criteria on serum VA in T1DM. In short, this study found that patients with T1DM had lower serum VA levels. Further modern studies[37] have found that antioxidant supplementation improves glucose control, β cell function, and insulin secretion in adults with diabetes mellitus. We cautiously suggest that vitamin A can be appropriately supplemented in patients with T1DM in clinical supplementary treatment.

5. Conclusion

The results of the meta-analysis support the hypothesis that patients with T1DM have lower serum VA levels. It is worth noting that this is not conclusive because all studies are cross-sectional causality. However, given the current level of research, further research is needed to strengthen this discovery and clarify the possible impact mechanism.

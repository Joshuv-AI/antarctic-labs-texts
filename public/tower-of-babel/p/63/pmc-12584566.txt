In‐Hospital Outcomes of Simultaneous and Staged Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement and Endovascular Aneurysm Repair
Authors: Alex Pu, Rahul V Annabathula, Haytham Allaham, Diljon Chahal, Shahab Toursavadkohi, Anuj Gupta
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroductionPatients with aortic aneurysms are at elevated risk of rupture, dissection and death during and after transcatheter aortic valve repair (TAVR), often requiring consideration for endovascular aneurysm repair (EVAR) at the time of TAVR. However, data comparing outcomes of simultaneous versus staged TAVR‐EVAR are limited.MethodsUsing the National Inpatient Sample between the years 2018 and 2021, we compared in‐hospital outcomes of simultaneous and staged TAVR‐EVAR. Patients who underwent both TAVR and EVAR during hospital admission were identified with International Classification of Diseases 10th Revision procedure codes. Patients were stratified by simultaneous (defined as same day) versus staged (defined as different day) procedures. Propensity score matching was done in a 2:1 fashion to account for differences in baseline characteristics, and mixed effects multivariate regression models were used to assess differences in in‐hospital mortality, cost of hospitalization, length of stay, and other procedural complications.ResultsA total of 270 patients underwent simultaneous TAVR‐EVAR, while a total of 70 underwent staged TAVR‐EVAR. After propensity score matching, 98 patients undergoing simultaneous TAVR‐EVAR were matched to 49 patients undergoing staged TAVR‐EVAR. No significant differences in baseline characteristics were observed after matching. In‐hospital mortality between the matched groups was not significantly different (OR 0.67, p = 0.409), however the simultaneous group was associated with shorter length of stay (3 [2.0−11.75] days vs. 14 [13.0−17.0] days, p < 0.001) and less total hospital costs ($77,691 [$65,153−$103,854] vs. $148,617 [$128,339−$217,988]; p < 0.001). The matched simultaneous group was also associated with lower incidence of acute kidney injury (25.5% vs. 65.3%; OR 0.11, p < 0.001), blood transfusions (20.4% vs. 40.8%; OR 0.37, p = 0.01), and access site complications (< 10.2% vs. 49.0%; OR 0.00, p < 0.001).ConclusionSimultaneous TAVR‐EVAR is associated with fewer adverse complications and lower cost of hospitalization without increased in‐hospital mortality, suggesting it is more cost effective than staged TAVR‐EVAR during the same hospital admission.Keywords: aortic aneurysm, aortic stenosis, endovascular aneurysm repair, transcatheter aortic valve repair

Introduction

As the world's population ages, the prevalence of aortic stenosis (AS) and aortic aneurysms (AA) continues to rise [1, 2]. Studies estimate up to 6% of patients with AS may have concomitant AA [3]. Current guidelines recommend that patients over the age of 65 or those with intermediate‐to‐high surgical risk be treated with transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) [4, 5]. However, patients with AA have been shown to be at an increased risk of developing rupture and dissection after TAVR [6]. Fortunately, endovascular aneurysm repair (EVAR) offers a percutaneous and safer alternative to open surgery for these patients already at elevated surgical risk, which is often pursued around the time of TAVR in this patient population [7].

While there have been reports demonstrating successful treatment of concomitant severe AS and AA with combined TAVR and EVAR (TAVR‐EVAR), there are no clear recommendations for their combined management. Specifically, there have been no large‐scale studies to date comparing simultaneous TAVR‐EVAR versus staged TAVR‐EVAR. A better understanding of overall safety, complication profiles, and overall resource utilization would inform future management of these complex diseases. Here, we utilized a large nationally representative inpatient database to compare in‐hospital outcomes between simultaneous and staged TAVR‐EVAR.

Methods

Data from the National Inpatient Sample (NIS) between the years of 2018 and 2021 were queried for this study. The NIS is a publicly available all‐payer inpatient health care database developed as part of the Health Care Utilization Project (HCUP) by the Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality (AHRQ) [8]. It provides a stratified sample of hospital discharges from United States hospitals, representing 20% of all United States hospitalizations. It contains information regarding demographics, primary and secondary International Classification of Disease, 10th Revision (ICD‐10) diagnosis and procedure codes, hospitalization cost information, and hospitalization outcomes. The NIS contains de‐identified patient information and therefore represents Non‐Human Subjects Research. Sampling weights provided by the NIS were used to provide nationwide estimates.

Patients with both ICD‐10 codes for TAVR and EVAR were included as the study cohort. Patients under the age of 18 were excluded. Patients were stratified by whether they had a simultaneous TAVR‐EVAR (defined by TAVR and EVAR done on the same day of admission) or staged TAVR‐EVAR (defined by TAVR and EVAR done on different days of admission). Patients whose procedure days were not listed in the NIS database were excluded from this study.

Baseline characteristics, including age, gender, and medical comorbidities were identified. Reduction in left ventricular ejection fraction was defined as ICD‐10 codes encoding systolic heart failure. Rates of missingness for each variable were 0% in this cohort. All ICD‐10 codes utilized for this study are supplied in Table S1.

The primary endpoint of this study was in‐hospital mortality. Secondary endpoints of this study include length of stay, total cost of hospitalization, and rates of complications, which included cardiogenic shock, sudden cardiac arrest, cerebrovascular accident, vascular complications (defined as composite of acquired arteriovenous fistula, aneurysm formation, hematoma, and hemoperitoneum), acute kidney injury, acute kidney injury requiring renal replacement therapy, blood transfusions, need for conversion to open surgery, and pacemaker implantation. Cost‐charge ratios and total charge of hospitalizations were provided by the NIS, which were used to calculate the total cost of each hospitalization. Cost was adjusted to 2021 US dollars, utilizing the Consumer Price Index Inflation Calculator provided by the US Bureau of Labor Statistics [9].

To adjust for inherent differences in baseline characteristics between the cohorts, a propensity score matching model was used. Propensity scores were calculated with a logistic regression model utilizing age, gender, race, and medical comorbidity data. A full list of covariates utilized in the propensity score model is available in Table S2. A c‐statistic was calculated for the logistic regression model to be 0.87, indicating overall good fit. Matching was then done in a 2:1 fashion between the simultaneous and staged cohorts using a k‐nearest neighbor algorithm with a caliper width of ¼ of the standard deviation of the propensity scores. Baseline characteristics were compared using Mann−Whitney U test for continuous values and Chi‐squared test for categorical variables. Multivariate logistic regression models were used to assess differences in outcomes in unmatched cohorts, adjusted for the covariates listed in Table S2. Mixed effects logistic regression models were used to compare continuous and categorical variables in the matched cohorts, with matched group as the random effect; these regression models were not adjusted for any other variables as no significant difference existed after matching. Further, a bootstrap sensitivity analysis was conducted to evaluate whether postprocedural complications mediated the relationship between simultaneous versus staged TAVR‐EVAR and hospital length of stay. For each complication, two linear regression models were fitted in 10,000 bootstrap samples drawn with replacement from the matched cohort: a base model and an extended model that adjusted for the complication of interest. Models did not adjust for baseline covariates as no significant differences remained after matching. The differences between the beta coefficients associated with simultaneous TAVR‐EVAR were calculated in each iteration to assess attenuation attributable to each complication. A p value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant for all analyses. Value counts fewer than 11 are reported as “< 11” in tables in accordance with NIS guidelines to prevent patient re‐identification.

Statistical analysis was performed with Python 3.12.3 with the pandas library version 2.2.3 and scikit‐learn library 1.6.0.

Results

A total of 360 patients were identified who received both a TAVR and EVAR during the same hospitalization, with 290 receiving simultaneous TAVR‐EVAR and 70 receiving staged TAVR‐EVAR. No significant differences in age distribution or gender were observed between the two groups. The simultaneous group had a higher proportion of coronary artery disease (69.0% vs. 42.9%; p < 0.001), chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (24.1% vs. < 14.3%; p = 0.003) and hypertension (91.4% vs. 71.4%; p < 0.001) than the staged cohort. Following propensity score matching, 98 patients undergoing simultaneous TAVR‐EVAR were matched to 49 patients undergoing staged TAVR‐EVAR. No significant differences in baseline characteristics remained following matching. Full baseline characteristics for unmatched and matched cohorts can be seen in Table 1.

Among the unmatched cohorts, no differences in in‐hospital mortality were seen (13.8% vs. < 14.3%; OR 0.96, p = 1). The staged cohort was associated with overall longer length of stay (4.5 [2−12] days vs. 14 [11−18] days, p < 0.001) and higher total charges ($84,582 [$66,886−$112,001] vs. $155,252 [$106,350−$217,988], p < 0.001). The simultaneous cohort was associated with lower rates of vascular complications (10.3% vs. 42.9%; OR 0.04, p < 0.001), acute kidney injury (25.9% vs. 50.0%; OR 0.12, p < 0.001), and blood transfusion (20.7% vs. 28.6%, OR 0.29, p = 0.001). There was no incidence of conversion to open surgery in both cohorts. After matching, no significant difference in in‐hospital mortality was observed (15.3% vs. < 23%; OR 0.67, p = 0.409). The matched simultaneous cohort had lower length of stay (3 [2−11.75] vs. 14 [13−17], p < 0.001) and total hospitalization cost ($77,691 [$65,153−$1,038,854] vs. $148,617 [$128,339−$217,988]; p < 0.001) compared to the matched staged cohort. After matching, the simultaneous cohort continued to be associated with lower rates of vascular complications (< 10.2% vs. 49.0%, OR 0.00; p < 0.001), acute kidney injury (25.5% vs. 65.3%, OR 0.11; p < 0.001), and blood transfusions (20.4% vs. 40.8%, OR 0.37; p = 0.010) (Figure 1). Discussion

In this nationally representative analysis, we found no significant difference in in‐hospital mortality between patients undergoing simultaneous and staged TAVR‐EVAR. However, simultaneous approach was associated with shorter hospital stay and lower total hospital cost, a finding which persisted after matching. Additionally, the staged cohort was associated with higher rates of access site complications, acute kidney injury, and blood transfusions.

Since its introduction in 2002, TAVR has transformed the management of aortic stenosis. Its rapid growth has been driven by pivotal randomized trials in high‐risk, intermediate‐risk, low‐risk patients, and TAVR has surpassed surgical aortic valve replacement in annual volume for management of aortic stenosis [10]. These trials excluded the presence of patients with significant aortic disease, and as such presence of AA presents a unique challenge with minimal guidelines to help direct practice. Aortic dissections are a rare but feared complication following TAVR, with studies suggesting the presence of AA is associated with elevated risk of dissection [6]. Following aortic valve replacement, restoration of cardiac output increases systolic blood pressure, leading to increased aortic vessel tension and elevated risk of rupture [11]. In fact, patients undergoing TAVR with AA were found be approximately twice as likely to suffer in‐hospital mortality when compared to those without AA [12]. As such, co‐management of associated AA is paramount in this high risk population.

Our study confirms many advantages that have been hypothesized for simultaneous approach when compared to staged approach [13]. For example, a simultaneous approach is thought to be associated with a reduced incidence of vascular site complications as it allows for utilization of a common access site, avoiding re‐access of a previously instrumented vessel. Our study confirms that a single‐stage approach was associated with a significantly lower incidence of vascular access complications. Additionally, simultaneous procedures have been hypothesized to be associated with shorter hospital stays and reduced hospitalization costs, which is consistent with the results of our study. A primary concern for a single‐stage TAVR‐EVAR is the complexity of two large interventions into a single procedure. However, our study shows that there is no increased rate of mortality or complications with a simultaneous approach. Further, our study suggests overall procedural success as there was no need for conversion to open surgery observed.

Although several case reports and case series have described successful simultaneous TAVR‐EVAR procedures, direct comparative studies evaluating simultaneous versus staged approaches are limited [13, 14, 15]. One of the few studies that directly compared TAVR‐EVAR approaches is a retrospective multicenter study that evaluated 25 simultaneous and 19 staged TAVR‐EVAR patients [16]. This study, unlike ours, did not observe differences in vascular access complications, which likely reflects how their staged cohort received procedures within a span of 3 months of each other and had a median time between procedures of more than 2 months, likely allowing adequate time for access sites to heal. Additionally, while their staged cohort did show a higher rate of acute kidney injury, it did not achieve significance. In contrast, our study showed a significantly higher rate of acute kidney injury in the staged cohort, which likely reflects increased contrast loads fewer days apart compared to Gallitto et al.'s study, as well as the benefit of a larger sample size.

Our study showed that patients who received staged procedures had a hospital stay of approximately 10 days longer than those who received simultaneous procedures. This difference persisted even after matching. Among the staged cohort, 80% of all patients received both procedures within 3 days. We further showed that increased length of stay may be partially mediated by certain complications, including vasopressor use and cardiogenic shock. This suggests that the difference in length of stay may be, in part, explained by complications requiring intensive care unit level of care. Future studies analyzing factors that contribute to the differences in length of stay between these two cohorts are needed to further bolster simultaneous TAVR‐EVAR's cost effectiveness.

This study had several limitations. First, the NIS uses ICD‐10 codes for indicating diagnoses, which are prone to misclassification and diagnostic errors. Additionally, as with all retrospective studies, our study is inherent to selection biases and confounding. We, however, utilized propensity score matching to reduce these biases. Granular details regarding patient's anatomy and hemodynamics, including aneurysm size or ejection fraction, are not available in the NIS, but would greatly assist in understanding different contributors to adverse events. Additionally, the NIS does not contain data regarding long‐term outcomes of TAVR‐EVAR, and therefore postoperative complications after discharge are not captured in this study.

Conclusion

Simultaneous TAVR‐EVAR is associated with reduced hospital costs, length of stay, as well as reduced access site complications without increased mortality, suggesting it is more cost effective than staged TAVR‐EVAR in patients hospitalized for management of comorbid AS and AA.

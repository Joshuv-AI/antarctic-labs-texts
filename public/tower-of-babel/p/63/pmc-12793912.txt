Floating wire assisted device retrieval-a novel technique for prevention of guide-related coronary injury
Authors: Atit A Gawalkar, Rajesh Vijayvergiya, Pragya Karki
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAn inward force is experienced by the guide catheter during device retrieval resulting in potential risk of deep engagement into the ostio-proximal coronary segment. This undesired movement can result in coronary injury. There is no systematic data or reports of techniques to prevent such inadvertent guide movement during difficult retrieval of devices.MethodsIn 25 patients undergoing percutaneous coronary intervention, where the conventional methods of guide stabilization failed to prevent deep engagement of guide catheter during device retrieval we used ‘floating aortic wire’ technique and reattempted retrieval. The primary endpoint was the successful retrieval of the device without deep engagement of the guide.ResultsSuccessful retrieval was seen without deep engagement of guide in 23(92 %) patients. Left anterior descending(n = 15, 60 %) artery was the most common coronary artery. The XB guide(n = 14, 70 %) was the most commonly used guide for left coronary intervention while Judgkins right and Amplatz left were used most commonly for right coronary intervention. Stent balloon(n = 15, 60 %) was the most common device which required using floating aortic wire for retrieval. Other devices were jailed wire(n = 5,20 %), non-compliant balloon(n = 4,16 %) and cutting balloon(n = 1,4 %). Intravascular ultrasound did not show any guide related vessel injury(dissection or intramural hematoma) in any cases. The floating aortic wire failed to prevent deep engagement in two patients because of longer segment of jailed wire and long stent balloon in distal right coronary artery.ConclusionFloating aortic wire assisted retrieval of coronary devices is a simple, reliable and safe technique that prevents deep guide engagement during difficult retrieval.Keywords: Floating aortic wire, Guide catheter, Iatrogenic dissection

Introduction

Percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) typically involves engaging the coronary artery ostium and delivery of various devices over the guidewire. During advancement of devices like balloon or stent, the guide catheter experiences force that tends to push the catheter tip away from the ostia into the aorta. Delivery of devices is facilitated by the backup support of the guide catheter which is its ability to remain in position during advancement of devices.1 During retrieval of the devices out of coronaries opposite force is experienced leading to deeper engagement or movement of the catheter tip into the coronary artery. This undesired movement is common during PCI. Often there is higher than usual resistance to withdrawal of devices like jailed guidewire or large stent balloon. With increased resistance, attempt to withdraw leads to greater indrawing of the guide catheter into the proximal left or right coronary arteries(deep intubation) that can potentially cause dissection or proximal stent deformation.2,3 It is evident from the studies that most of iatrogenic left main dissection occurs during PCI compared to initial guide manipulation or diagnostic catheterisation.4 Since the catheter induced injury occurs at the proximal segments of coronary artery system, large area of myocardium is at risk. Additionally there is a risk of extension to aorta.5 Knowledge of catheter induced coronary injury is crucial because it is associated with poor outcome and is mostly avoidable.2 Deep engagement of guiding catheter is responsible for catheter related dissections in close to one third of patients.2 Deep intubation during balloon retrieval has been seen to be one of major cause of iatrogenic left main dissection in a study.6 Despite being a significant cause of iatrogenic coronary injury, very few techniques have been advocated in clinical practice to avoid it. To the best of our knowledge this is the first description of a floating wire technique to prevent inadvertent dragging and deep intubation of guide catheter during difficult retrieval of devices.

Material and methodology

In a single arm observational study, 25 patients undergoing percutaneous coronary intervention were enrolled who required aortic floating wire to withdraw device from the coronary artery when other conventional methods failed. All the patient had symptomatic significant coronary artery stenosis requiring revascularisation. They were hemodynamically stable throughout the procedure. Moderate to severe coronary calcification was defined as any fluoroscopically visible coronary artery calcium visible before contrast injection.7 After defining the lesion, appropriate guide catheter was engaged and the lesion crossed with guidewire. Further steps included atherectomy device or cutting balloon if calcium modification was required followed by pre-dilatation of lesion with non-compliant balloon, stenting with drug eluting stent of appropriate size and post dilatation with non-compliant balloon. Some of the cases had bifurcation disease requiring placement of additional wire in the side branch which was jailed after first stent implantation. During retrieval of the pre-dilatation balloon, stent balloon, post dilatation balloon or jailed wire the guide would be pulled into the ostio-proximal coronary segment. Conventional method of stabilising the guide like slight pull on the guide while device retrieval, jerky movement on the device were attempted and we were successful in most of the cases. But the cases where the conventional method failed and any further attempt to device retrieval was risking deep engagement of guide were identified(25 patients). In such cases, the guide was pulled away from the ostia and another 0.014-in workhorse coronary guidewire (Runthrough, Terumo) was placed in floating position in the aortic sinus. Attempt was again made to retrieve the device back into the coronary(Fig. 1). The primary endpoint was the successful retrieval of the device without deep engagement of the guide. Two representative cases have been demonstrated as figures and videos. First case demonstrate the deep engagement of XB guide catheter on attempt to pull back jailed wire from the diagonal branch in a bifurcation PCI which was overcome by using floating aortic wire(Fig. 2, Supplementary Video 1). Second case demonstrate the deep engagement of EBU guide catheter on attempt to retrieve post dilatation balloon from the left anterior descending artery(LAD) which was overcome by using floating aortic wire(Fig. 3, Supplementary Video 2). After successful completion of the procedure, repeat intravascular ultrasound was performed to check for adequate stent expansion, malapposition,evidence of complications including possible guide related ostio-proximal vessel damage. The baseline characteristics and procedure details were recorded and analysed for this study. The study has received proper ethical oversight.

Results

A total of 25 patient procedures required using the floating aortic wire technique to attempt retrieval of various devices from the coronary artery into the guide catheter. Successful retrieval of the device was seen without deep engagement of guide in 23(92 %) patients. The mean age of the patients was 66.2 years(±10.5 years). Other baseline characters have been presented in Table 1. Non ST elevation acute coronary syndrome(n = 16, 64 %) was the most common indication for percutaneous coronary intervention. Multivessel disease(atleast two arteries) was present in 12(48 %)patients. LAD(n = 15, 60 %) artery was the most common coronary artery requiring intervention (Table 2). Moderate to severe angiographic calcification was present in 12 patients(48 %). XB(Cordis) guide(n = 14, 70 %) was the most commonly used guide for left coronary intervention while Judgkins right and Amplatz left were used most commonly for right coronary intervention. Stent balloon(n = 15, 60 %) was the most common device which required using floating aortic wire. Other devices were jailed wire(n = 5,20 %), non-compliant balloon(n = 4,16 %) and cutting balloon(n = 1,4 %). The mean diameter of the balloon requiring floating wire assisted retrieval was 2.75 mm(±0.3 mm) and the mean length was 25.4 mm(±10.3 mm). Jailed wire in all the cases was Runthrough guidewire. The procedure was completed transfemorally in 14(56 %) patients while radial route was employed in 11(44 %) patients. We used 6Fr guide catheter in 20 patient(80 %) and 7Fr guide catheter in 5 patients(20 %). IVUS was performed in all the cases before stent implantation and after the completion of the procedure and no ostio-proximal dissection or intramural hematoma was observed. In two patients the floating aortic wire could not prevent deep engagement of catheter and other methods had to be adopted. In the first patient, the jailed wire could not be retrieved despite floating aortic wire technique because of longer length of jailed wire. In another patient where the floating wire failed was during the retrieval of a long stent balloon from the distal RCA. The device retrieval in these two cases were done using other methods and post procedure IVUS did not show ostio-proximal dissection.

Discussion

The study demonstrated the feasibility, utility and safety of floating aortic wire in preventing deep engagement of the guide catheter in difficult retrieval of the devices. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to systematically describe a technique to prevent guide related coronary injury.

Iatrogenic ostio-proximal coronary artery dissection is a rare complication of coronary catheterisation.8 It is more common with coronary interventions compared to diagnostic catheterisation.6 Guide catheter is an important cause of ostio-proximal coronary injury and is usually related to inadvertent deep engagement. Deep engagement can occur during attempt to retract the devices out of the coronary artery into the guide. Such deep engagement is responsible for one third of cases of catheter related dissection.2,6 Being ostio-proximal in location, such injuries can have major impact on the procedure and patient because of the large area of myocardium at risk especially in the left main segment.9,10 Severe forms of injury can lead to acute vessel closure, myocardial infarction and death.9 Presence of a plaque or stent edge in the ostio-proximal region further increases the risk of plaque disruption and stent deformation respectively.8,11 There is abundant literature on identification and management of catheter induced coronary dissection, however there is no data or report of preventive strategy.9,12, 13, 14 Floating aortic wire has been conventionally used to mark the ostium during deployment of stent in ostial disease and in some cases to stabilize the stent during deployment.15,16 However a novel utility of floating aortic wire to prevent deep engagement of the guide catheter has been described here.

Any factor that increases the friction between the coronary vessel and device surface tend to exert more resistance to device retrieval like longer balloons, calcified vessel, sharp surface(cutting balloon) or jailed wire, resulting in deep engagement of guide (Fig. 1). The risk of guide related dissection is more common with extra back up guides like EBU/XB for left coronary and Amplatz left for right coronary artery.8 However, utility of such devices is very common in interventions involving complex coronary lesions like calcified vessel, bifurcation disease or other subsets requiring good guide support(EBU/XB/AL1). Prevention of such ostio-proximal guide related injury is critical for smooth progress of an already complex procedure. As demonstrated in our cases, conventional methods to prevent deep engagement may not be adequate in complex PCI. The study also demonstrated the feasibility of advancing another wire into the guide till the aortic sinus in the presence of preexisting guidewire and a device without damage. Once placed into sinus, attempt should be made to retrieve the device into the guide. The aortic floating wire acts as an anchor when the catheter moves close to the ostia preventing further forward advancement of the guide (Fig. 1). It also provides addition support to pull the device out of the coronary artery. No complications related to the floating wire were seen. Previous studies evaluating the catheter induced ostio-proximal dissection have only used angiographic method in which minor dissections might not be apparent.8,17 To overcome this, IVUS assessment was done to detect minor dissection or evidence of other coronary injury. After successful retrieval of the desired device, the floating wire is withdrawn undamaged. The same could be reused if necessary for the remaining procedure. Presence of calcified disease in large proportion of patients, using guides with higher support, using cutting balloon and bifurcation disease indicates a relatively complex subset of patients enrolled. The floating wire technique was unsuccessful in two occasions for the retrieval of a long segment jailed wire and long stent balloon in distal RCA. Additional cost of the coronary guidewire appears to be the only drawback associated with this technique which appears justified considering the prevention of a major catheter related ostio-proximal vessel injury. The biggest strength is that the method is simple, inexpensive, doesn't require additional operator skill and poses no additional harm to patient. The major limitation of the study is that it is a single center study involving relatively small number of patients with no comparator arm. Degree of suction was not assessed quantitatively. This being a proof of concept study, detailed subgroup analysis to correlate length/diameter of the balloon with degree of deep engagement has not been performed. Larger study involving multiple operators and centers can provide valuable insights on the reproducibility. Additionally intracoronary imaging analysis of the segment responsible for the difficulty in retrieval of the device might help in defining the vessel lumen or stent lumen.

Conclusion

Retrieval of certain devices into guide catheter during complex coronary intervention risks deep engagement of catheter and ostio-proximal coronary injury. When conventional methods fail, floating aortic wire assisted device retrieval is an easy, effective and safe technique to prevent deep guide engagement.

Impact on daily practice

There is an increase in number of complex coronary intervention globally with the advancement in available stents, balloon and other support devices. Guide catheter related ostio-proximal coronary injury is an important cause of iatrogenic left main and right coronary artery dissection and deep engagement of the catheter is a major cause. There is abundance of studies evaluating the mechanism, detection and management of this important complication. However there is no systematic study or report of any technique for prevention of this important complication. This is the first study that demonstrates the feasibility, utility and safety of floating aortic wire assisted device retrieval in preventing deep guide engagement of guide catheter. As the technique is easy and inexpensive, it has huge potential to improve safety of day to day percutaneous coronary interventions.

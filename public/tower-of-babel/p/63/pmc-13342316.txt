Multinucleation and cytotoxicity induced by multikinase inhibition in highly proliferative cells
Authors: Emi Tomikawa, Kei Takahashi, Haruhiko Hattori, Tadamasa Arai, Masakatsu Kawai, Kazumi Nishimura, Yasufumi Goto, Ryotaro Yagi, Yohei Miyamoto, Tomoya Miyoshi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Abstract Phenotypic screening using motor neurons derived from the induced pluripotent stem (iPS) cell of a patient with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis led to the identification of Compound X, a novel agent with potent neuroprotective activity that is hypothesized to act by inhibiting hematopoietic progenitor kinase/germinal center kinase-like kinase (HGK). Unlike known HGK inhibitors, including Prostetin and GNE-495, Compound X and related Compounds Y and Z exhibited broader multi-kinase-inhibition profiles, including the strong inhibition of Aurora B kinase and strong-to-moderate inhibition of Src-family kinases in kinase panel screening. Owing to their roles in cytokinesis, we performed in vitro micronucleus tests to assess their genotoxicity. Compounds X, Y, and Z induced the multinucleation of cells at rates of 88.8, 71.5, and 91.8%, respectively, whereas Prostetin and GNE-495 resulted in rates of 0.5 and 1.3%, respectively, which were comparable to the control. A 5-day repeated-dose oral toxicity study of Compound X using mice, at doses of 125, 250, and 500 mg/kg/day, revealed decreased locomotor activity, hypothermia, and mortality at 500 mg/kg/day. Histopathology showed necrosis/hemorrhage in bone marrow cells; single-cell necrosis in the thymus, spleen, and mesenteric lymph nodes; nuclear pleomorphism and single-cell necrosis/necrosis in the gastrointestinal mucosa; and karyomegaly and a reduction in testicular germ cells. These toxicopathological findings suggest that Compound X induces nuclear pleomorphism and necrosis in highly proliferative cells. Although Compound X strongly suppressed HGK, its toxicity may be associated with broader multikinase inhibition, which is consistent with the finding that Prostetin and GNE-495 do not induce multinucleation in vitro.Keywords: cytotoxicity, multikinase inhibitor, in vitro micronucleus test, multinucleation

Introduction

Our research group examined the potential of small-molecule compounds for the treatment of amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) through the phenotypic screening of motor neurons differentiated from the patient-derived induced pluripotent stem (iPS) cell1. Recent studies have implicated hematopoietic progenitor kinase/germinal center kinase-like kinase (HGK), also known as mitogen-activated protein kinase kinase kinase 4 (MAP4K4), in the pathogenesis of ALS, via endoplasmic reticulum stress-induced apoptosis2. Since the prototype of Compound X exerted strong neuroprotective effects through iPS-derived motor neuron phenotypic screening, and the underlying mechanism is hypothesized to involve the inhibition of HGK1, we hypothesized that Compound X also exerts neuroprotective effects by suppressing HGK3.

HGK belongs to a highly conserved family of serine/threonine kinases, making its selective inhibition challenging. The ATP-binding sites of these kinases, such as HGK, Aurora kinases, and Rho-associated kinases (ROCKs), as well as those of tyrosine kinases, including those of the Src family, are structurally similar owing to their high conservation, which complicates the development of selective inhibitors1, 4–7. Docking simulations have suggested that the Compound X prototype binds the ATP-binding site via two possible hydrogen bonds with its urea moiety. Compound X and its prototype share an N-(5-phenylthiophen-3-yl)urea moiety, suggesting that these compounds bind the ATP-binding site in a similar manner1.

Several HGK inhibitors with improved selectivity, including Prostetin and GNE-495, have been developed with improved selectivity8. However, kinome-wide profiling has demonstrated that both compounds exhibit activity against related HGK family kinases, such as Misshapen/NIKs-related kinase (MINK/MAP4K6; 86.8% identity) and Traf2/Nck-interacting kinase (TNIK/MAP4K7; 81.96% identity)8–10. For example, GNE-495 was designed to reduce central nervous system (CNS) penetration, thereby eliminating CNS-related tolerability issues. However, it still strongly inhibits MINK and TNIK, which are known to affect neuronal integrity9. The reported toxicities of other HGK inhibitors, such as weight loss, increased body temperature, and elevated heart rate, may be attributed to on-target HGK inhibition, off-target effects, or the inherent chemical structures of the compounds11, 12. Therefore, the toxicity of HGK inhibitors remains unclear, and this may be attributed to the difficulty associated with achieving selectivity for HGK targeting8. In this study, we examined the toxicological profile of a promising lead compound with high HGK-inhibitory activity. An in vitro micronucleus test for genotoxicity with CHL/IU cells and a 5-day repeated-dose oral toxicity study on mice, performed to assess general toxicity, revealed interesting toxicological profiles.

Compounds X, Y, and Z were synthesized by Toray Industries, Inc. Prostetin was also synthesized by Toray Industries, Inc., according to the method described by Boss and colleagues10. GNE-495 was purchased from MedChemExpress LCC (Monmouth Jct, NJ, USA)9. Methyl cellulose was purchased from Junsei Chemical Co., Ltd. (Tokyo, Japan).

In vitro micronucleus test

CHL/IU cells were obtained from the Japan Health Sciences Foundation (Tokyo, Japan). Cells were cultured in Eagle’s MEM supplemented with 10% heat-inactivated bovine serum at 37°C with 5% CO2 in air. Cells were seeded in a black 96-well plate (3603, Corning, NY, USA) at a density of 3.0 × 103 cells/well and pre-cultured for approximately 24 h. The culture supernatant was removed, and 100 μL/well of the test substances was added. After 6 h, the presence of precipitation was confirmed, and cells were cultured with fresh medium for an additional 18 h. Cells were incubated with CellMask™ Deep Red Plasma Membrane Stain (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Waltham, MA, USA) for approximately 10 min and then incubated with 4% paraformaldehyde containing Hoechst 33342 (Dojindo Laboratories, Kumamoto, Japan) for 30 min. Fluorescent images were captured using a CQ1 confocal image cytometer (Yokogawa Electric Corporation, Tokyo, Japan) at a magnification of ×20. Four hundred cells (200 cells/well) were observed, and the number of multinucleated cells (cells with three or more nuclei) was counted visually.

Animals

Male Crl:CD1 (ICR) mice (6 weeks old) were purchased from Charles River Laboratories, Japan Inc. (now known as The Jackson Laboratory, Yokohama, Japan), acclimated for 6 days, individually housed in plastic cages with softwood chip bedding under controlled conditions (12 h light/dark cycle at 21–25°C with 40–70% humidity), and fed a standard diet (CRF-1, Oriental Yeast Co., Ltd., Tokyo, Japan) and tap water ad libitum. The animals were allocated to 10 groups based on body weight using an alternative randomization method with the Drug Discovery Screening System (Mitsui E&S System Research Inc., Chiba, Japan): four groups of eight animals (toxicology groups), three groups of six animals (single-dose toxicokinetic groups), and three groups of three animals (repeated-dose toxicokinetic groups). Animal experiments were reviewed by the head of the test facility and performed in accordance with the Guidelines for Animal Experiments, Research and Development Division, Toray Industries, Inc.

Five-day repeated-dose oral toxicity study

In a single-dose oral toxicity study of 500 and 1,000 mg/kg Compound X in mice, the 1,000 mg/kg group exhibited severe clinical signs, including absent locomotor activity, ataxia, abdominal distention, and hypothermia. With 500 mg/kg, reductions in the white blood cell count and spleen weight were observed. Therefore, the maximum dose was set at 500 mg/kg/day. Mice received Compound X orally for five consecutive days at doses of 0, 125, 250, or 500 mg/kg/day. Clinical observations were performed three times daily (before dosing and at 1 and 4 h after dosing) during the dosing period and before necropsy on day 6.

Prior to necropsy on day 6, five mice per toxicity group were anesthetized with isoflurane, and blood was collected in ethylenediaminetetraacetic acit-2K and lithium heparin tubes. Blood cell counts were determined using an automated hematology analyzer (XT-2000iV; Sysmex, Hyogo, Japan). Organs, including the brain, heart, thymus, liver, spleen, kidneys, adrenal glands, and testes, were harvested, weighed, and fixed in 10% neutral-buffered formalin. Testes were prefixed in a formalin–sucrose–acetic acid solution and fixed in formalin. Femurs were fixed in formalin and decalcified using Gooding and Stewart’s solution. Histopathological examinations were performed on the following tissues: cerebrum, cerebellum, parotid gland, submandibular gland, sublingual gland, heart, lungs, thymus, stomach, duodenum, jejunum, ileum, cecum, colon, rectum, mesenteric lymph nodes, liver, spleen, pancreas, kidneys, adrenal glands, testes, epididymides, and bone marrow (right femur). The samples were embedded in paraffin, and the sections were stained with hematoxylin and eosin for microscopic observations. Additionally, testis sections were stained with periodic acid-Schiff. Regarding toxicokinetics, mice were anesthetized with isoflurane, and blood samples were collected in lithium heparin tubes 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8, and 24 h after dosing on day 1 and 0.5, 2, 8, and 24 h after the last dose on day 5.

The left femurs of five animals per toxicity group were excised at necropsy. Both ends of the femur were removed, the bone marrow cells were washed with 1 mL of fetal bovine serum and centrifuged at 1,000 rpm at room temperature for 5 min, and the supernatant was discarded. The bone marrow cells were resuspended in serum, smeared onto glass slides, dried, and fixed in methanol for 5 min. The specimens were stained with an acridine orange solution and observed under a fluorescence microscope (BX61, Olympus Corporation, Tokyo, Japan) equipped with a B excitation filter.

Body weight, hematology, and organ weight data were statistically analyzed using the Drug Discovery Screening (DDS) System (Mitsui E&S Systems Research Inc.) with the SAS statistical analysis program (SAS Institute Japan, Tokyo, Japan, ver. 9.4) linked to the DDS system. Differences in mean values between the treated and control groups were analyzed using Dunnett’s multiple comparison test. Statistical significance was set at p<0.05.

Toxicokinetics

Plasma samples were diluted threefold with methanol containing diazepam as an internal standard (IS), mixed, and centrifuged at 1,930 × g at 4°C for 10 min. The supernatant was diluted seven-fold with water containing 0.01% formic acid and analyzed via liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (Sciex Triple Quad7500 with an Exion LC system (Sciex, Framingham, MA, USA)) to quantify Compound X. A CAPCELL PAK C18 MGIII column (5 µm, 2.0 mm I.D. × 50 mm, OSAKA SODA, Osaka, Japan) was used. Gradient elution with 0.01% formic acid in water (eluent A) and acetonitrile (eluent B) was performed as follows: 10% B for 0.5 min, increased to 100% at 3.5 min, held for 1.0 min, and reset in 0.01 min. The flow rate was 0.4 mL/min, and the injection volume was 10 µL. Electrospray ionization conditions were as follows: 5500 V ion spray voltage, 600°C probe temperature, and the curtain gas, ion source gas 1, and ion source gas 2 set to 45, 35, and 70 psi, respectively. Quantification was performed using positive-mode multiple reaction monitoring with transitions (m/z) of 438.1 > 337.0 for Compound X and 285.2 > 154.1 for diazepam (IS). Data were processed using SCIEX OS 2.1.6 software. The molecular weight of Compound X was 437.56, and its plasma protein-binding rate in mice was 99.1%. The blood concentration of free Compound X was calculated based on these values.

Results

Kinase panel assay

We identified a novel compound, designated Compound X, which is hypothesized to exert neuroprotective effects through the inhibition of HGK, based on the pharmacological profile of its prototype compound1, 3. HGK, a serine/threonine kinase, presents a challenge in achieving selectivity beyond that of closely related kinases. To elucidate the off-target kinases affected by Compound X and related Compounds Y and Z, we conducted kinase panel assays (Table 1 and Supplementary Table 1). The assay results revealed that Compound X exerted a broader multikinase-inhibition profile, resulting in the potent inhibition of TNIK and MINK, as well as kinases involved in cell division (Supplementary Table 1). Compound X and related Compounds Y and Z inhibited Aurora kinases (AurA and AurB), which are critical regulators of cell division. Src family kinases (Src, Lck, Fyn, and Yes), which are critical regulators of cell division, were strongly inhibited by Compounds X and Y, whereas Compound Z resulted in weaker inhibition of these kinases. Conversely, Compounds X, Y, and Z exerted weak inhibitory effects against ROCK kinases (ROCK1), which also play a role in the regulation of cell division. Established HGK inhibitors such as Prostetin and GNE-495 exhibited minimal inhibitory activity against these kinases, as reported in the literature9, 10. These results indicate that, in comparison with Prostetin and GNE-495, Compounds X, Y, and Z function as multikinase inhibitors, necessitating a thorough evaluation of their potential toxicities related to HGK and off-target kinases, including critical regulators of cell division, such as Src family kinases and Aurora kinases.

In vitro micronucleus test

To determine the genotoxic profile of Compound X, we conducted an in vitro micronucleus assay (Fig. 1). The maximum evaluated dose was selected as the lower of (1) concentration at which the relative inhibitory concentration coefficient reached 50–55% or (2) the minimum concentration at which the test substance precipitated. Compound X (25, 50, and 100 μmol/L) strongly induced multinucleation from the lowest concentration, with the multinucleation-induction rate at the low dose (25 μmol/L) being 88.8% (355/400 cells). It was not possible to evaluate micronucleus formation using this screening system. Additionally, the multinucleation induction rate in 1% DMSO on the same plate was 0.5% (2/400 cells).

To confirm whether the multinucleation-inducing effect was a class effect, the related compounds Y and Z, as well as the existing HGK inhibitors Prostetin and GNE-495, were assayed (Fig. 1). Compound Y (8.5, 17.0, and 34.0 μmol/L) and Compound Z (15.6, 31.3, 62.5, and 125 μmol/L) also exerted strong multinucleation-inducing effects starting from the lowest concentration, similar to those of Compound X. Multinucleation induction rates at the low doses were 71.5% (286/400 cells, 8.5 μmol/L) for Compound Y and 91.8% (367/400 cells, 15.6 μmol/L) for Compound Z. Prostetin (1.95, 3.90, and 7.80 μmol/L) and GNE-495 (13.0, 26.0, and 52.0 μmol/L) did not exert multinucleation-inducing effects. Multinucleation-induction rates at the high dose were 0.5% (2/400 cells, 7.80 μmol/L) for Prostetin and 1.3% (5/400 cells, 52.0 μmol/L) for GNE-495, which were similar to the multinucleation induction rate of 0.3% (1/400 cells) for 1% DMSO, with the test conducted on the same plate. These results suggest that the induction of multinucleation by Compounds X, Y, and Z is a basic chemical structure-associated effect that does not apply to Prostetin or GNE-495.

Clinical score, body weight, and systemic exposure

To evaluate the clinical condition of the test animals and the effects of systemic exposure to Compound X, mice were orally administered Compound X for five consecutive days (0, 125, 250, or 500 mg/kg/day). At a dose of 500 mg/kg/day, mortality, decreased locomotor activity, hypothermia, and weight loss were observed from day 5 onwards (Fig. 2G). In the 125 and 250 mg/kg/day groups, systemic exposure increased in a dose-dependent manner on days 1 and 5, with higher exposure being observed on day 5 (AUC0–24h, free on day 1: 5.90 and 8.67 μmol·h/L; day 5: 7.52 and 11.2 μmol·h/mL, respectively), indicating accumulation following repeated administration (Fig. 2H). In contrast, in the 500 mg/kg/day group, systemic exposure exceeded that in the 250 mg/kg/day group on day 1 (AUC0–24h, free: 15.9 μmol·h/L), but was lower on day 5 (AUC0-24h, free: 8.30 μmol·h/L), which may be attributed to reduced absorption associated with the gastrointestinal toxicity observed on day 6 (as described later).

Bone marrow toxicity

In the in vivo micronucleus study, no bone marrow cells were observed. Only mature red blood cells (or normochromatic erythrocytes), which do not emit any fluorescence under acridine orange staining but can be observed in bright-field images13, were present in all treated animals (Fig. 2A, 2B, 2D, 2E), suggesting a strong bone marrow-inhibitory effect of Compound X. Therefore, subsequent micronucleus counting was discontinued. A histopathological assessment of the bone marrow showed that hematopoietic cells were markedly decreased and hemorrhage was noted in animals starting from the lowest dose (Fig. 2C, 2F).

Hematopoietic and immune system toxicities based on a histopathological assessment

Consistent with bone marrow toxicity, blood cell count tests showed that the numbers of white blood cells and reticulocytes were significantly decreased in animals at all doses, with reductions of 87–90 and 92–95%, respectively, compared to those in the controls. The thymus exhibited severe atrophy in all dose groups, with reductions of 74–81% in absolute organ weights and 72–79% in relative organ weights compared to those in the controls. Furthermore, the spleens of one or two animals per treatment group showed discoloration to a pale color and/or appeared small upon macroscopic observation. Single-cell necrosis in the white pulp (periarteriolar lymphoid sheath and follicles) (250 and 500 mg/kg/day groups) and the marginal zone and red pulp (500 mg/kg/day group) was noted in the spleen. One animal in the 500 mg/kg/day group exhibited necrosis of the marginal zone with a mass of bacteria. The spleen organ weight was significantly decreased in the 500 mg/kg/day group (33% lower than that in the control). In the mesenteric lymph nodes, diffuse single-cell necrosis was detected in the cortex, paracortex, and medulla of the 500 mg/kg/day group. These results suggest the severe toxicity of Compound X toward the hematopoietic and immune systems.

Digestive and reproductive system toxicities based on a histopathological assessment

In the digestive tract, mucosal necrosis and/or degeneration were observed with every dose, and their severity increased in a dose-dependent manner (Fig. 3). Nuclear pleomorphism, including nuclear enlargement, was observed in squamous cells of the forestomach and epithelial cells of the glandular stomach, small intestine, and large intestine. Notably, in the 500 mg/kg/day group, systemic exposure decreased on day 5 despite a higher initial exposure on day 1, indicating that gastrointestinal toxicity in the absorptive regions resulted in impaired absorption and reduced bioavailability following repeated administration. In contrast, no treatment-related changes were noted in slowly proliferating cells, including hepatocytes and renal tubular epithelial cells, except in one animal in the 500 mg/kg/day group with a confirmed bacterial infection, in which necrosis of the renal tubular epithelium with a mass of bacteria was observed.

In the testes, necrosis of spermatogonia and spermatocytes was detected in all treated animals (Fig. 4). An increase in cell debris was also observed in the epididymis of all treated groups. A reduction in the number of early-stage spermatocytes, including preleptotene (Fig. 4F), leptotene (data not shown), and zygotene (Fig. 4H) spermatocytes, was observed at stages VII–XI. Discussion

Compounds X, Y, and Z inhibited multiple kinases related to cell division, including Aurora- and Src-family kinases, and induced multinucleation in vitro. Compound X also showed inhibitory activity against the closely related kinases TNIK and MINK. Although the HGK inhibitors Prostetin and GNE-495 showed inhibition profiles that included TNIK and MINK9, 10, they had little inhibitory activity against kinases involved in cell division and did not induce multinucleation in vitro. These characteristics suggest that multinucleation is a basic chemical structure-associated off-target effect, rather than a consequence of HGK inhibition. HGK is primarily involved in the regulation of the cytoskeleton, cell movement, and structural organization3, 12, 14 with no known role in cytokinesis. Cytokinesis is typically regulated by kinases such as Aurora kinases, Src-family kinases, and ROCK15.

Multinucleation occurs through two processes: cell–cell fusion and the inhibition of cytoplasmic division15, 16. Examples of the former include the formation of osteoclasts and giant cells through macrophage fusion16. The latter occurs when cytoplasmic division is inhibited after the completion of nuclear division and is physiologically observed in hepatocytes, megakaryocytes, and cardiomyocytes15. Cytoplasmic division begins with the formation of a contractile ring, followed by a cleavage furrow that divides the cells into two daughter cells15, 17. Src-family kinases contribute to contractile ring formation, whereas AurB and ROCK kinases regulate cleavage furrow formation17, 18. AurB and ROCK act through independent pathways and phosphorylate different substrates during cytokinesis, with the inhibition of both kinases leading to an additive increase in multinucleation17. In addition, the concurrent loss of AurA and AurB synergistically increases multinucleation and mitotic failure19. Consistent with these roles, inhibitors of these kinases have been shown to induce multinucleation17–19. Compound X inhibited these cytokinesis-related kinases and exhibited inhibitory activity against MINK1 (required for abscission, the final step in separating daughter cells20), NEK2 (a central kinase that regulates multiple mitosis21), and CDK2 (involved in cell-cycle regulation22). However, although Prostetin strongly suppressed MINK1 and likely inhibited ROCK at in vitro micronucleus test concentrations, it did not induce multinucleation in our study. Moreover, Compound X resulted in only the weak inhibition of NEK2 and CDK2 at in vitro micronucleus test concentrations. Taken together, these findings suggest that MINK1, NEK2, and CDK2 are unlikely to be the primary drivers of Compound X-induced multinucleation. Compounds X, Y, and Z exhibited broader multikinase-inhibitory effects than Prostetin and GNE-495, suggesting that the simultaneous inhibition of multiple kinases involved in cell division may underlie the induction of multinucleation in vitro in the present study. However, the current data do not permit attribution to specific kinase targets.

The histopathological assessment of Compound X showed significant necrosis in cells that underwent frequent cell division, such as hematopoietic cells in the bone marrow, T cells in the thymus, splenic cells, and epithelial cells in the mucosa of the digestive tract. In the gastrointestinal tract, stem cells localize to the proliferative zone, such as the basal layer of the esophageal epithelium, neck region of the gastric glands, lower half of the intestinal villi, and lower one-third of the colonic crypts23. Compound X-induced nuclear pleomorphism was observed in the basal part of the stratified epithelium of the anterior stomach, above the boundary of the crypts in the jejunum and ileum, matching the proliferative zone. This pleomorphism may disrupt the normal differentiation process of the intestinal epithelium, inhibiting regeneration and the subsequent loss of mature mucosal cells. These results suggest that mucosal damage was secondary to the effects of Compound X on stem cells and progenitor cells in the proliferative zones. The skin is a highly proliferative organ and a potential target for AurB inhibitors24; however, in the present study, no macroscopic abnormalities were observed in the skin during the observation period; thus, skin samples were not collected.

In contrast, only mature red blood cells with a long lifespan of 20–30 days were observed in the bone marrow of mice, and no hematopoietic stem cells or various precursor cells, including dividing young red blood cells, underwent cell division. Blood cell counts showed a significant decrease in white blood cell and reticulocyte counts. Based on these results, Compound X acts directly on undifferentiated hematopoietic stem cells and various precursor cells undergoing differentiation, causing cell death through multinucleation. Furthermore, Compound X potently inhibited JAK3, BTK/SYK, and IRAK4, the inhibition of which predominantly depleted non-dividing mature lymphocytes and reduced cellularity in lymphoid organs, thereby exacerbating myelosuppression in the bone marrow25–27. This dual mechanism may explain the rapid and extensive decrease in hematopoietic cells in the bone marrow, white blood cells, and reticulocytes in the peripheral blood, and lymphocytes in lymphoid organs. Microtubule-targeting agents such as Taxol induce prolonged mitotic arrest by activating the spindle checkpoint, which leads to apoptosis either during mitosis or after mitotic slippage28. In contrast, AurB inhibitors disrupt cytokinesis, causing multinucleation and post-mitotic cell death owing to nuclear abnormalities. In addition, the AurB inhibitor AZD1152 induced both myelosuppression and a decrease in white blood cells in the peripheral blood29; thus, whereas Aurora B inhibition can explain the rapid loss of proliferating hematopoietic cells, other kinases such as JAK3, BTK/SYK, and IRAK4 may also contribute to the broad hematolymphoid depletion observed in the bone marrow and lymphoid organs.

In the testes, Compound X induced necrosis in metaphase spermatocytes at stage XII and karyomegaly in spermatogonia at stages I–VI. Metaphase spermatocytes are in the cell division phase, and spermatogonia are mitotically active. Therefore, necrosis in metaphase spermatocytes and karyomegaly in spermatogonia were consistent with the mechanism of multinucleation induced by Compound X. The decrease in round spermatids (stages I–VI) and early-stage spermatocytes (preleptotene, leptotene, and zygotene) (stages VII–XI) may have been secondarily induced by the necrosis of metaphase spermatocytes and karyomegaly of spermatogonia, considering the dosing periods. Unlike many anticancer drugs, such as cyclophosphamide, bleomycin, and adriamycin30–32, which mainly target spermatogonia, Compound X appears to affect both spermatogonia and differentiating spermatocytes, potentially inhibiting spermatogenesis. Notably, Compound X did not induce the multinucleation of spermatocytes or sperm cells in the seminiferous epithelium. The multinucleation of spermatocytes and sperm cells may be attributed more to cell fusion than to cytoplasmic division32, which is consistent with the multinucleation mechanism underlying the effects of Compound X.

The present results indicate that Compound X induced multinucleation through broader multikinase inhibition, and this multinucleation-inducing effect, in vivo, targeted highly proliferative cells and zones in the whole body, as well as some mature hematolymphoid cells. This multinucleation may result in cell death, providing a mechanistic basis for selective cytotoxicity in cancer cells33. In general, inhibitors that perturb cell cycle progression and mitotic processes can affect not only tumor cells but also normal tissues with high proliferative activity, including those of the hematopoietic system and gastrointestinal epithelium. Consistent with this concept, the bone marrow, hematological, and gastrointestinal toxicities observed in this study may reflect on-target pharmacological effects on proliferative compartments. Future research is expected to contribute to a more detailed understanding of the multinucleation-induction mechanism through detailed in vivo analyses and clinical trials, leading to the development of new treatments, such as anticancer drugs.

History and Current Status of the HIV/AIDS Supporting Network System
Authors: Sangsik Cho, Koun Kim, Sohee Han, Junghee Hyun, Jeonghee Yu, 상식 조, 고운 김, 소희 한, 정희 현, 정희 유
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesWe reviewed the development process, current structure, and core functions of the HIV/AIDS (human immunodeficiency virus/acquired immune deficiency syndrome) Supporting Network System (HASNet), which underpins the national surveillance system for HIV/AIDS in the Republic of Korea (ROK). We also analyzed the role of HASNet within the broader national infectious disease management framework.MethodsWe reviewed relevant literature and institutional developments to examine the stepwise evolution of HASNet from 2004 to 2024. Our analyses focused on system components (test management, case reporting, epidemiological investigation, patient management, and statistical management) and user structures. We also reviewed legislative amendments, data protection measures, and the system upgrade process.ResultsSince its initial establishment in 2004, HASNet has experienced continuous advances. Milestones include the development of a blood safety system and public information website, functional enhancements through modernization projects from 2005 to 2008, integration with the National Disease and Health Integrated Management System in 2009, reinforcement of personal data protection measures in 2019, and expansion of patient support functions in the 2020s. Through these developments, HASNet has evolved into a comprehensive support system that manages the full continuum of care needs for people living with HIV from diagnosis to confirmation, reporting, and epidemiological investigations of treatment and mortality. It operates for a multi-tiered user network consisting of healthcare institutions, public health centers, provincial public health laboratories, local governments, and the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency.ConclusionsHASNet currently provides a critical foundation supporting enhanced efficiency and accuracy of ROK’s national HIV/AIDS management programs while providing essential evidence for policy development. Amendments to the Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome Prevention Act and further integration with other health information systems are necessary to expand programmatic support and strengthen data utilization. These improvements are expected to contribute to the sustainable advancement of the national HIV/AIDS management system.Keywords: HIV/AIDS Supporting Network System, HIV, Acquired immunodeficiency syndrome

Key messages

① What is known previously?

Human immunodeficiency virus/acquired immune deficiency syndrome (HIV/AIDS) has no cure and requires continuous management. Unfortunately, the former surveillance system caused delays in confirmation and reporting due to complex procedures and offline documentation. To address this issue, HIV/AIDS Supporting Network System (HASNet) was established concurrently with legal revisions.

② What new information is presented?

Since 2004, HASNet has evolved into an integrated system that covers testing, confirmation, reporting, investigation, patient management, and mortality. Its operation now extends through a multilayered network of institutions to support policy development and statistical analyses.

③ What are implications?

HASNet enhances the efficiency and accuracy of national HIV/AIDS management. Its limited integration with other systems to date remains a challenge to achieve further improvement.

Introduction

Previously, the Korean national surveillance system for human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) and acquired immune deficiency syndrome (AIDS) experienced a delay of more than 1 month between the request for antibody testing and the reporting and initiation of epidemiological investigation. This delay was primarily attributed to the three-step testing process: health centers and hospitals conducted preliminary screening tests, which were then referred to metropolitan or provincial public health and environmental research institutes, and finally, confirmatory tests were performed by the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency (KDCA). The complex documentation process further contributed to the delay. To address these inefficiencies, the KDCA introduced an early confirmation testing system that allows metropolitan and provincial public health and environmental research institutes nationwide to perform final HIV confirmation tests, thereby markedly reducing the testing period [1]. Additionally, to resolve issues such as duplication, errors, administrative delays, and the risks of information leakage that occurred during written reporting and management, an integrated information system for HIV/AIDS reporting and support was established. The HIV/AIDS Supporting Network System (HASNet), developed through these efforts, has since been implemented.

This article aims to highlight the role of HASNet as a core component of the national HIV/AIDS surveillance system by describing its development process, system architecture, and key features.

Methods

1. Development and Evolution of HASNet

The HASNet was conceived as an autonomous and specialized national information system encompassing support programs for individuals living with HIV/AIDS. This initiative recognizes the chronic and multifaceted nature of HIV/AIDS as a condition requiring continuous management from initial diagnosis to the end of life. The development process was executed in a phased manner, with each phase focusing on specific aspects of system enhancement, including the protection of personal information, improvement of user convenience, and advancement of interoperability with other systems.

1) Initial establishment of the AIDS information network (2004)

In 2004, the scope of individuals subject to diagnostic testing was expanded following the amendment of Article 10, Paragraph 1, of the Enforcement Decree of the Prevention of Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome Act. Additionally, amendments to Articles 7 and 8 of the Enforcement Rules of the same Act introduced a confirmation testing system for blood donations and specimens suspected of infection. These changes marked the full-scale implementation of the early AIDS detection system and initiated the first phase of the AIDS information network construction project. Previously, the interval from the initial testing request to final confirmation and epidemiological investigation averaged approximately 1 month. However, following system implementation, this period was reduced to approximately 10 days. The systemization of support services for individuals infected with HIV minimized errors and omissions and was integrated with the blood transfusion infection prevention system to mitigate transfusion-related infections. Furthermore, a public information website was launched to disseminate updates on international trends, new drug research, and preventive measures, contributing to the reduction of social stigma.

2) Enhancement of the AIDS information network (2005–2008)

From 2005 to 2008, the enhancement project was implemented in successive phases. In 2005, the project scope was expanded to include the registration of individuals infected with HIV and the provision of both healthcare and administrative support functions. A dual-layered security system was introduced through the integration of a virtual private network and fingerprint-based authentication. During the third phase in 2006, integration with the laboratory information management system and a module for requesting unidentified specimens were added. Following the revision of the Act on Promotion of Information and Communications Network Utilization and Data Protection, Etc., increased emphasis was placed on information security. Consequently, security vulnerabilities were assessed and mitigated through information security consulting and penetration testing. From 2007 to 2008, efforts focused on stabilizing system operations and enhancing scalability, including the development of transmission and reception modules and a comprehensive website redesign.

3) System integration and renaming (2009)

In 2009, the AIDS information network was integrated and migrated as part of the establishment of the Integrated Disease and Health Management System. This process entailed a comprehensive overhaul, encompassing hardware integration, software platform migration, and the establishment of user permissions. During this phase, the system’s name was changed from “AIDS Information Network” to “HASNet.” The public information website was also unified with the main homepage to create a centralized information platform. In addition, the introduction of public certificate-based logins and web digital rights management1) further strengthened system security.

4) Security and feature enhancements (2019)

In 2019, amendments to the Enforcement Decree of the Prevention of Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome Act established the legal basis for collecting and processing sensitive information within information systems. Consequently, the database was restructured to comply with personal information protection standards, and a database encryption solution was implemented to safeguard personal information. Furthermore, the epidemiological investigation forms—previously categorized separately for domestic residents, foreign nationals, and children—were consolidated into adult and pediatric forms. These modifications were reflected in the restructured epidemiological investigation management system. A new module enabling integration with the recently developed Integrated Infectious Disease Management Information Support System was also introduced.

5) Ensuring operational stability and continuing functional development (since 2020)

Since 2020, continuous efforts have been made to enhance operational stability and develop new functionalities. The registration process for real-name-based healthcare expense support and integrated management functions for individuals infected with HIV has been incorporated. Following the transfer of system infrastructure to the National Information Resources Service, a cloud-based architecture was adopted to ensure both physical and logical separation. This transition minimizes potential damage in the event of system failure and includes mechanisms to monitor personal information access and connection logs, thereby preventing external breaches and reducing the risk of information leakage (Table 1).

Results

1. Functional Architecture of HASNet

HASNet is responsible for the comprehensive management of the entire lifecycle of individuals infected with HIV, encompassing initial testing and confirmation, reporting, epidemiological investigation, treatment and management, and death reporting. The system comprises four primary functions: inspection management, reporting management, epidemiological investigation management, and patient management. All data processing is performed using management numbers instead of personal identification numbers to ensure the protection of personal information.

The inspection management function covers the initiation of inspection requests and the registration of confirmatory test results. When a positive result is detected during screening at healthcare institutions, public health centers, or the Military Manpower Administration, confirmatory testing is performed by metropolitan or provincial public health and environmental research institutes and the KDCA, after which the results are registered in the system. Notably, accessibility and efficiency of testing have been improved through the amendment of Article 7 of the Enforcement Decree of the Prevention of Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome Act, which allows designated healthcare institutions to perform and register confirmatory tests beginning in 2025.

The reporting management function oversees the statutory reporting procedure for individuals diagnosed with HIV. Reports are submitted using the following forms: Form No. 1 of the Enforcement Decree of the Prevention of Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome Act: Report and Notification of Discovery (Death) of Persons Infected with HIV and Patients with AIDS; Form No. 1-2: Report of Discovery of HIV Infection Based on Academic Research or Blood and Blood Product Test Results; and Form No. 5-2: Anonymous Report and Notification of HIV Infection and Patients with AIDS. If HIV infection progresses to AIDS, a separate report must be submitted. In parallel, death reports for individuals infected with HIV are collected, ensuring that causes of death among this population are incorporated into national statistics and policy formulation for support programs.

In accordance with Article 10 of the Prevention of Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome Act, epidemiological investigations of persons infected with HIV are conducted. HASNet includes an epidemiological investigation management function that stores and processes data based on the revised epidemiological investigation forms implemented in 2020. These data serve as a key resource for analyzing HIV infection trends, enabling nationwide epidemiological assessments and informing evidence-based policy development.

HASNet is distinguished from other infectious disease management systems by its comprehensive functionality, which integrates surveillance, epidemiological investigation, and patient support management. The patient management function is directly linked to the support program for individuals living with HIV. Personal information and medical records are collected to facilitate healthcare expense support and to inform annual support evaluations and budget planning. For individuals relocating to new areas of residence, public health centers securely transmit information through outbound and inbound registration functions. The counseling management function records the health status and medication adherence of patients. The anonymization of consultation records is a critical component in managing patient confidentiality. The de-identified data are subsequently used to calculate key metrics, including treatment success rates and medication adherence rates.

Finally, the statistical management function computes various indicators, including new HIV infections, results of epidemiological investigations, medical expense support status, and mortality data. After the de-identification process, these aggregated data form the basis of the Annual Report on HIV/AIDS Notification Status [2] (Figure 1).

2. Users of HASNet

HASNet is a national-level integrated information system utilized by multiple agencies, each possessing distinct authorities and functions. The user population is broadly categorized into medical institutions, public health centers, metropolitan and provincial health and environmental research institutes, metropolitan and provincial health departments, and the KDCA. Each institution performs a specific role, with tailored functions, permissions, and access levels that minimize unnecessary information exposure. All registered data are managed meticulously in accordance with rigorous security protocols.

First, the primary user groups responsible for testing and reporting individuals infected with HIV include healthcare institutions, the Military Manpower Administration, and Blood Services. When a screening test conducted at a healthcare facility yields a positive result, the relevant test request information—such as specimen number, sex, and screening test results—is entered into HASNet. Blood Services promptly report any suspected HIV infection identified during the blood donation process, while the Military Manpower Administration follows a similar reporting procedure during conscription examinations. The data submitted by these institutions are reviewed and verified by the local public health center responsible for the area, after which appropriate follow-up measures are initiated.

Second, local public health centers constitute the most pivotal user group within HASNet. They are responsible for the comprehensive management of patients infected with HIV, including the receipt of test requests, documentation of cases, registration of epidemiological investigations, reporting of mortalities, and provision of financial assistance. Positive test results reported by healthcare institutions are reviewed and verified, including details such as specimen number, sex, age, nationality (domestic or foreign), presumed route of infection, and symptoms. Public health centers also oversee the registration of epidemiological investigation and medical expense support applications, thereby ensuring the systematic management of healthcare and social support for individuals infected with HIV. Furthermore, they comprehensively monitor HIV/AIDS incidence within their jurisdictions, execute KDCA-promoted support programs based on collected data, and report performance outcomes.

Third, metropolitan and provincial health and environmental research institutes conduct confirmatory HIV testing on specimens received from healthcare institutions, public health centers, Blood Services, and the Military Manpower Administration. These institutes play a crucial role in registering confirmatory test results in HASNet and determining whether cases are positive. Additionally, when test results are classified as “undetermined,” retesting is conducted to obtain the final result. Throughout this process, close collaboration is maintained with the Division of Viral Diseases under the Department of Laboratory Diagnosis and analysis of the KDCA.

Fourth, the provincial and metropolitan health departments serve as intermediary management bodies, responsible for overseeing and supervising the reporting status of individuals infected with HIV within their jurisdictions. These entities review and approve reports and epidemiological investigations submitted by public health centers, assess medical expense support data, and request additional information when necessary. They ensure the effective functioning of the local HIV/AIDS management system and compile regional statistics for submission to higher authorities.

Fifth, the KDCA functions as the primary operating agency for HASNet, executing its mandate through specialized testing and management departments. The testing department re-examines specimens classified as “undetermined” by metropolitan and provincial health and environmental research institutes, registers the final results, and notifies relevant stakeholders. The management department reviews and approves information on all reported cases nationwide and operates the national surveillance system based on this data. The KDCA’s responsibilities extend to strengthening HASNet’s functional capabilities, implementing system maintenance, ensuring information security and personal data protection, and managing data administration. In addition, the KDCA continuously undertakes information technology projects to ensure operational stability. Notably, upon the introduction of new legislation or systems, the KDCA promptly reviews and updates the existing framework to ensure seamless policy integration.

Therefore, the operational framework of HASNet is characterized by interinstitutional collaboration, with each participating entity functioning within its designated scope of authority and responsibilities. Healthcare institutions, Blood Services, and the Military Manpower Administration are responsible for early detection and reporting. Public health centers play a pivotal role in the management and implementation of patient support programs. The public health and environmental research institutes, along with metropolitan and provincial health departments, perform verification and supervisory functions. Ultimately, with the KDCA providing national-level oversight and coordination, HASNet functions as a comprehensive and integrated system for managing individuals living with HIV throughout their entire lifecycle.

Conclusion

HASNet is the nation’s sole specialized information system dedicated to HIV/AIDS reporting and patient management. Due to the sensitivity of the data it processes, the system operates within a rigorous security framework. All personal information is managed using assigned management numbers rather than personal identifiers, thereby minimizing the risk of external exposure. However, this approach also presents certain limitations in terms of interoperability with other systems. Moving forward, amendments to the Prevention of Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome Act will expand the scope of information collected and strengthen system integration, thereby enhancing the utilization and efficiency of support programs. Furthermore, HASNet is mandated to provide essential data for the implementation of the “Second Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome Prevention and Management Plan (2024–2028)” and to undergo continuous improvements aimed at enhancing the operational efficiency of field practitioners. These developments will further solidify its role as a core foundation for reliably supporting the national HIV/AIDS management program.

방 법

1. HASNet의 구축 및 발전 과정

HASNet은 HIV/AIDS가 진단에서 사망에 이르기까지 장기적이고 지속적인 관리가 필요한 질환이라는 점을 고려하여 감염인 지원사업까지 포괄하는 독립적이고 전문적인 국가 정보시스템으로 설계되었다. 그 발전 과정은 단계별로 추진되었으며, 그 과정에서 개인정보의 보안과 사용자 편의성 및 타 시스템과의 연계성이 점차 강화되었다.

1) 2004년 에이즈정보망 1차 구축

2004년에 「후천성면역결핍증예방법시행령」 제10조 제1항 개정에 따라 진단검사 대상자가 추가∙확대되었고 동법 시행규칙 제7조, 제8조 개정에 따라 헌혈 및 감염 의심 검체에 대한 확인검사체계가 추가되어 HIV/AIDS 조기확진검사체계가 본격 도입되었다. 이를 뒷받침하기 위한 에이즈정보망 1차 구축사업이 시작되었다. 기존에는 검사의뢰에서 최종 확인검사와 역학조사까지 약 한 달이 걸렸으나, 시스템 구축 후 이 기간이 약 10일로 단축되었다. 감염인 지원업무가 시스템으로 정보화되면서 오류와 누락이 감소했고, 수혈로 인한 감염을 예방하기 위해 수혈감염예방시스템과도 연계되었다. 더불어 대국민 정보 웹사이트가 개설되어 해외 동향, 신약 연구, 예방 지식 등을 제공함으로써 사회적 편견 완화에도 기여하였다.

2) 2005–2008년 에이즈정보망 고도화

2005–2008년에는 고도화 사업이 이어졌다. 2005년 2단계 사업에서는 감염인 등록과 의료 지원, 행정 지원 기능이 확대되었으며 가상사설망(virtual private network)과 지문인식 기반 인증이 도입되어 이중 보안 체계가 마련되었다. 이후 2006년 3단계 사업에서는 실험실정보관리시스템 연계와 미결정 검체 의뢰 기능이 추가되었고, 「정보통신망 이용촉진 및 정보보호 등에 관한 법률」이 개정되어 정보보호에 대한 필요성이 증가함에 따라 정보보안 컨설팅과 모의해킹을 통해 보안 취약점을 점검하고 개선하였다. 2007–2008년에는 송수신 모듈 개발, 누리집 개편 등 운영 안정화와 확장성이 동시에 추진되었다.

3) 2009년 시스템 통합 및 명칭 변경

2009년에 질병보건통합관리시스템 구축과 더불어 에이즈정보망의 통합∙이전이 추진되었다. 이 과정에서 하드웨어 통합 이전, 소프트웨어 플랫폼 전환, 사용자 권한 생성 등 전면적 개편이 이루어졌으며 시스템 명칭도 ‘에이즈정보망’에서 ‘에이즈지원시스템(HASNet)’으로 변경되었다. 대국민 정보 웹사이트는 대표 홈페이지와 일원화된 정보 제공 체계를 마련하였다. 이후 공인인증서 기반 로그인과 웹 디지털 저작권 관리(web digital rights management)1) 도입으로 보안 수준이 더욱 강화되었다.

4) 2019년 보안 및 기능 강화

2019년에는 「후천성면역결핍증예방법시행령」 제27조를 개정하여 정보시스템의 민감정보 수집 및 처리에 대한 근거를 마련함에 따라 개인정보보호 요건을 충족하기 위해 데이터베이스 체계가 개편되었고, 개인정보 암호화를 위한 데이터베이스 암호화 솔루션이 적용되었다. 또한 내국인, 외국인, 소아용으로 분리되었던 역학조사 서식이 성인용 역학조사서와 소아용 역학조사서로 개정되면서 이를 반영하기 위해 역학조사관리시스템을 개편하였다. 더불어 당시 새롭게 개발되고 있던 감염병관리통합정보지원시스템과 연계할 수 있는 모듈도 개발하였다.

5) 2020년 이후 운영 안정성 확보 및 기능 개발 지속

2020년 이후에는 운영 안정성 확보와 기능 개발이 지속되었다. 실명 기반 진료비 지원 등록과 감염인 통합관리 기능이 추가되었고, 시스템 인프라가 국가정보자원관리원으로 이전되면서 클라우드 기반의 물리∙논리 분리 구조가 도입되었다. 이를 통해 장애 발생 시 피해가 최소화되도록 설계되었으며, 개인정보 조회와 접속 기록을 모니터링하는 기능도 마련되어 외부 침해와 정보 유출 위험을 예방할 수 있게 되었다(표 1).

1. HASNet의 기능적 구성

HASNet은 감염인 검사에서 확진, 신고, 역학조사, 치료와 관리, 사망신고에 이르는 전 생애 과정을 포괄적으로 관리한다. 본 시스템은 크게 검사관리, 신고관리, 역학조사관리, 환자관리의 네 가지 주요 기능으로 구성되며 모든 데이터는 개인식별번호 대신 관리번호로 처리되어 개인정보보호가 이루어진다.

검사관리 기능은 최초 검사 의뢰와 확인검사 등록을 포함한다. 의료기관, 보건소, 병무청 등에서 선별검사를 통해 양성이 확인되면 시∙도 보건환경연구원과 질병관리청이 확인검사를 수행하고 그 결과를 등록한다. 특히 2025년 일부 의료기관에서도 확인검사와 등록이 가능하도록 「후천성면역결핍증예방법 시행규칙」 제7조를 개정하여 검사 접근성과 효율성이 개선되었다.

신고관리 기능은 HIV 감염인을 법정 서식에 따라 신고하는 절차를 담당한다. 신고는 「후천성면역결핍증예방법 시행규칙」 별지 제1호 서식 「인체면역결핍바이러스 감염인∙후천성면역결핍증환자 발견(사망) 신고∙보고」, 제1호의 2서식 「학술연구 또는 혈액 및 혈액제제 검사결과에 따른 인체면역결핍바이러스 감염인 발견신고」, 제5호의 2서식 「인체면역핍바이러스 감염인∙후천성면역결핍증환자 발견 익명신고∙보고」를 통해 보고되며, HIV 감염 후 질병이 진행되어 후천성면역결핍증 환자로 진행한 경우에는 별도 보고가 이루어진다. 또한 감염인의 사망보고 또한 병행되어 감염인의 사망 원인이 통계와 지원정책에 반영된다.

HIV 감염인에 대한 역학조사는 「후천성면역결핍증 예방법」 제10조에 근거하여 수행된다. 이를 위해 역학조사관리 기능이 있으며 2020년에 개정된 역학조사 서식을 기반으로 작성된 자료는 HIV 감염 현황을 분석하는 기초자료로 활용된다. 이를 통해 국가 차원의 유행 특성 분석과 대응 정책 수립이 가능해졌다.

HASNet은 다른 감염병관리 시스템과 일부 다르게 감시 및 역학조사 기능 외에 HIV 감염인 지원에 대한 관리도 담당하고 있는데, 바로 환자관리 기능이 이 지원사업 중심으로 운영된다. 진료비 지원을 위해 개인정보와 진료 내역을 수집하며, 이를 바탕으로 연도별 지원 현황과 예산계획 수립이 이루어진다. 거주지 이전 시 전출∙전입 기능을 통해 보건소 간 정보가 안전하게 이관되며 상담관리 기능을 통해 건강 상태, 복약 순응도가 기록 및 관리된다. 상담 내역은 가명 처리되어 익명성이 보장되며, 이를 활용해 치료율과 복약률 같은 핵심 지표가 산출된다.

통계관리 기능은 신규 감염인, 역학조사, 진료비 지원, 사망 현황 등 다양한 지표를 산출하며, 비식별화 과정을 거쳐 「HIV/AIDS 신고 현황 연보」 [2] 작성의 기초자료로 활용된다(그림 1).

2. HASNet의 사용자

HASNet은 다양한 기관이 각기 다른 권한과 기능을 가지고 활용하는 국가 단위의 통합 정보시스템이다. 사용자는 크게 의료기관, 보건소, 시∙도 보건환경연구원, 시∙도 보건 담당 부서 그리고 질병관리청으로 구분되며 각 기관은 고유한 역할을 수행하며 기관별로 사용할 수 있는 기능과 권한, 열람 가능한 정보가 구분되어 있어 불필요한 정보 노출을 최소화하고 있다. 등록되는 모든 자료는 보안 지침에 따라 안전하게 관리된다.

첫째, 의료기관, 병무청, 혈액원 등은 감염인의 검사와 신고를 담당하는 1차 사용자 집단이다. 의료기관에서는 선별검사 결과가 양성으로 확인되면 HASNet을 통해 검사의뢰 정보를 입력한다. 여기에는 검체번호, 성별, 스크리닝 검사 결과 등이 포함된다. 혈액원은 헌혈 과정에서 HIV 감염이 의심되는 사례를 발견하면 즉시 시스템에 신고하며, 병무청도 징병검사 과정에서 발견된 사례를 동일한 방식으로 보고한다. 이들이 입력한 자료는 해당 지역을 관할하는 보건소에서 검토 및 확인 과정을 거쳐 후속 조치가 이루어진다.

둘째, 지역 보건소는 HASNet 사용자 중 가장 핵심 주체라 할 수 있다. 보건소는 검사의뢰 접수, 감염인 신고, 역학조사 등록, 사망신고, 진료비 지원 등 환자관리 전반을 직접 수행한다. 의료기관이 신고한 양성 판정을 받은 사례에 대해 검체번호, 성별, 연령, 내∙외국인 여부, 추정 감염경로, 증상 등을 검토하고 확인한다. 또한 역학조사와 진료비 지원 신청서를 등록하여 감염인의 의료적∙사회적 지원을 체계적으로 관리한다. 더 나아가 보건소는 관할 지역 내 HIV/AIDS 발생 현황을 종합적으로 파악하고, 이를 바탕으로 질병관리청이 추진하는 지원사업을 집행하며 실적을 보고하는 역할도 맡고 있다.

셋째, 시∙도 보건환경연구원은 의료기관, 보건소, 혈액원, 병무청 등에서 이송된 검체에 대해 HIV 확인검사를 수행한다. 보건환경연구원은 검사 결과를 HASNet에 등록하고, 확진 여부를 판정하는 중요한 역할을 담당한다. 또한 검사 결과가 ‘미결정’으로 판정된 경우 재검사 과정을 통해 최종 결과를 도출하며, 이 과정에서 질병관리청 검사부서인 바이러스분석과와 긴밀히 협력한다.

넷째, 시∙도 보건 담당 부서는 지역 내 감염인 신고 현황을 관리 및 감독하는 중간 관리 기관이다. 이들은 보건소에서 입력한 신고와 역학조사, 진료비 지원 자료를 검토하고 승인하며 필요 시 보완을 요청한다. 이를 통해 지역 내 HIV/AIDS 관리체계가 제대로 작동하는지 확인하고, 지역 단위의 통계를 종합하여 상급 기관에 보고한다.

다섯째, 질병관리청은 HASNet의 총괄 운영 기관으로 검사 부서와 관리 부서로 나누어 역할을 수행한다. 검사 부서는 시∙도 보건환경연구원에서 ‘미결정’ 판정을 받은 검체를 재검사하여 최종 결과를 등록하고 통보한다. 관리 부서는 전국적으로 신고된 감염인 및 환자 정보를 검토하고 승인하며 이를 토대로 국가 감시체계를 운영한다. 더불어 질병관리청은 HASNet의 기능 개선, 유지보수, 정보보안 관리, 개인정보보호, 데이터 관리 등을 총괄하며 안정적인 운영을 위해 지속적인 정보화 사업을 추진한다. 특히 새로운 법령이나 제도가 도입될 경우 시스템을 신속히 개정하고 보완하여 정책 현장과의 연계성을 확보하고 있다.

이처럼 HASNet은 다양한 기관이 각자의 권한과 책임을 기반으로 상호 연계하여 운영된다. 의료기관과 혈액원, 병무청은 초기 발견과 신고를 담당하고, 보건소는 환자관리와 지원사업 집행의 중심에 있으며, 보건환경연구원과 시∙도 보건 부서는 검증과 관리 역할을 수행한다. 최종적으로 질병관리청이 국가 차원의 총괄과 조정 역할을 맡음으로서 HASNet은 전 주기적이고 통합적인 감염인 관리체계로 운영된다.

결 론

HASNet은 HIV/AIDS 신고∙보고 및 환자관리를 지원하는 국가 유일의 전문 정보시스템으로 민감한 정보를 다루는 특성상 엄격한 보안 체계를 기반으로 운영된다. 개인정보는 관리번호를 활용해 처리되어 외부 노출이 최소화되지만 이로 인해 타 시스템과의 연계성에는 제약이 있다. 향후 「후천성면역결핍증 예방법」 개정을 통해 수집 정보 범위를 확대하고 타 시스템과의 연계를 강화한다면 지원사업의 활용도와 효율성은 더욱 높아질 것이다. 또한 HASNet은 「제2차 후천성면역결핍증 예방관리대책(2024–2028년)」 추진 과정에서 필수 데이터를 제공하며, 현장 실무자의 업무 효율성을 높이는 방향으로 지속적으로 고도화될 필요가 있다. 이를 통해 국가 에이즈 관리사업을 안정적으로 뒷받침하는 핵심 기반으로서의 역할을 강화할 수 있을 것이다.

A comparative performance analysis of fully homomorphic and attribute-based encryption schemes
Authors: Kirti Dinkar More, Dhanya Pramod
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
To integrate Attribute-Based Encryption (ABE) and Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE) within the NS-2 simulation environment, we propose a novel simulation model called FHE and ABE with Fast Exponentiation Optimization (FA-FEO) for smart city environment monitoring. This model evaluates important performance metrics like throughput, latency, memory utilization, power consumption, etc. With networked sensors and devices, the IoT enables efficient data collection and monitoring, but challenges like safe data transfer with energy constraints, and privacy preservation remain crucial. To provide strong data security and privacy while permitting smooth communication across decentralized IoT networks, our approach (FA-FEO) places a strong emphasis on the employment of FHE and ABE. A study of the performance of IoT network communication under basic implementation of FHE, two types of ABE like Ciphertext-policy ABE (CP-ABE) and Key-policy ABE (KP-ABE), and a BASE encryption indicates the significance of both ABE and FHE for practical smart city applications. The proposed model has been evaluated in detail using simulations for smart city environment monitoring scenarios and the results show that it is possible to deal with the overhead caused by FHE and ABE, guaranteeing safe and efficient energy-efficient solutions for scenarios such as environmental monitoring in smart cities.

Introduction

With the help of networked sensors and devices, the Internet of Things (IoT) has emerged as a revolutionary technology that makes efficient data collection and monitoring possible. It is therefore essential to integrate Wireless Sensor Networks (WSNs) into the internet to easily query the network for insightful data at any time and from any location. This integration enhances WSN’s usability and accessibility by supporting real-time data collection and decision-making. However, there are privacy and security concerns with this type of integration, particularly addressing network access. Several small sensor nodes with processing, communication, and sensing capabilities comprise Wireless Sensor Networks (WSNs), which are ad hoc networks. WSNs are widely used in fields like as military tracking, environmental monitoring, and the assessment of the effectiveness of industrial equipment. For instance, a WSN may monitor industrial equipment by measuring temperature, pressure, and vibration, allowing for preventative maintenance to avoid issues. Compared to traditional wired systems, WSNs are more adaptable, cost effective, and capable of sophisticated processing1. Towns and cities use information and communication technologies to better control noise pollution and environmental pollutants, lower health risks, and raise public awareness of the negative effects of air pollution. The article2 highlights the necessity of ensuring data confidentiality and consistency in order to provide security as well as reliability for efficient environmental monitoring. The undervaluation of the present risk landscape, budgetary constraints, and a lack of enforcement of security policies are some of the causes of the poor implementation of security controls. It is still necessary to build lightweight network security techniques for the constrained environment of IoT devices3. The authors of4 address the privacy and security issues with Internet of Things (IoT) applications and provide two effective data aggregation techniques to safeguard user information. To provide safe communication and data privacy, both techniques make use of data slicing and encryption (AES and Homomorphic). It is demonstrated that the suggested techniques reduce computational overhead and preserve data integrity while resisting a variety of threats. For scalable, lightweight data protection in Internet of Things applications, the developers of7 attempted to offer a full blockchain-assisted architecture that combines ABE with Elliptic Curve Cryptography (ECC) and Linear Secret Sharing Scheme (LSSS) access control. Using Network Simulator 3, the authors analyzed performance across multiple blockchain consensus methods and addressed scenarios with limited resources. Even as the number of nodes and their properties grows, network efficiency is maintained by the method’s decentralized attribute management, which guarantees balanced computing demand. The article8 goes into great detail about the development and significance of FHE since Gentry’s work in 2009.Significant progress has been made in the field of FHE since 2009. Following more than ten years of development, FHE has become a useful cryptographic method with expanding real-world uses. Privacy and security issues have been brought up by the growth and development of technologies like artificial intelligence, cloud computing, and the Internet of Things. FHE provides a convincing solution in this situation since it permits arbitrary computation on encrypted data without requiring its decryption. Because of this feature, FHE offers superior security while handling sensitive data. Lattice-based homomorphic encryption typically uses noisy encryption methods, which provide security but make it tough to handle noise growth throughout the encryption process. In order to significantly reduce the computational cost of key-switching, authors in9 introduced an efficient decomposition technique. They substitute costly operations over a big modulus with an efficient approach over a smaller integer ring. Significant key-switching accelerations without additional noise creation result from this, suggesting improved efficiency in practical HE implementations.

To improve performance on resource-constrained devices,10 proposed a provable data possession (PDP) method that utilizes additive homomorphic encryption. Their approach employs encryption based on ECC to lower computing and storage costs, as opposed to traditional methods that use multiplicative homomorphic encryption. In addition to encouraging public verifiability, the system performs better during the tag and proof creation stages. Experimental results show the advantages of ECC in terms of throughput and minimal storage usage when compared to RSA, Paillier, and ElGamal. In11, lightweight cryptographic primitives for resource-constrained IoT devices are thoroughly analyzed. Throughput, energy, and area are among the performance metrics used in the study to evaluate lightweight ciphers, hash functions, and ECC variations. It recommends AES and ECC as the best choices for IoT scenarios requiring minimal security. Study12 presents Generalized Policy ABE (GP-ABE), a new attribute-based encryption technique, in addition to the previously established CP-ABE, KP-ABE, and DP-ABE models. GP-ABE uses addition and identification functions to adaptively choose policies depending on data, users, or a combination of features. The method has been shown to be safe from selective attribute set attacks and allows fine-grained access control without adding extra overhead. This method provides a versatile and effective way to securely share data in social computing and cloud contexts. Article13 offers a conceptual framework for incorporating sensor systems into smart city infrastructures to enable thorough and ongoing environmental monitoring. The framework places a high priority on the continually updated evaluation of important environmental factors including temperature, humidity, and air quality. The authors advise, strategically positioning sensors at both fixed outdoor locations and moveable platforms, such as cars and buildings, to provide a broad spatial coverage. The proposed method emphasizes the significance of interdisciplinary cooperation and spatial data integration in order to deliver environmentally sound and context-specific smart city solutions.

As IoT usage continues to grow, protecting data privacy has become a major concern, restricting its increased scope. Even while FHE is a promising option, it still has a major efficiency limit, especially when it comes to bootstrapping. The study in14, which builds on the work of Halevi and Shoup, introduces “load capacity” and generalized cipheretext modulus in order to improve bootstrapping efficiency. These developments broaden FHE’s usefulness in practical, privacy-sensitive IoT scenarios. Article15 examines how Vehicular Ad hoc Networks (VANETs) facilitate road traffic services in smart cities, emphasizes the importance of strong privacy concerns. Attribute-Based Credentials (ABCs) offer interesting alternatives that protect privacy, especially Idemix, U-prove, and VANET-adapted Persiano systems. Idemix works well in a range of smart city use cases, and experimental evaluations show that these ABCs can be implemented in VANETs. Smart grid privacy and security concerns are covered in16, particularly when smart meters are connected to public networks. The authors suggest a cloud-based approach that uses homomorphic encryption to protect smart meter data while enabling encrypted data processing for billing. Experiments using real-world data show that their method works and makes it possible to aggregate encrypted readings efficiently. Practicality and scalability are highlighted by the addition of a parallelized billing mechanism to speed up processing. The security concerns of resource-constrained devices, such as smart dust, in Internet of Things applications are covered in the article17. The study proposes a lightweight authentication and key exchange system based on hash functions that is optimized for limited memory, computational power, and energy. It complies with the OneM2M international security standards and outperforms existing methods in terms of security and energy efficiency. Performance assessments validate its suitability for demanding IoT situations, including military, medical, and remote sensing applications. The proposed method is feasible for large-scale Internet of Things installations utilizing subminiature sensor networks.

In order to preserve data privacy in IoT and smart city applications, FHE enables computations on encrypted data without the need for decryption8,18–20. Even in the event that third parties or attackers attempted to access data, they could only be able to access encrypted data, which is useless to them because decrypting it requires a significant amount of resources21. The size of the ciphertext and secret keys is one of the main challenges with FHE, which restricts its usefulness in the Internet of Things because of resource limitations22,23. To deal with this, a number of strategies have been proposed to increase efficiency and decrease the size of the ciphertext. For example, enhanced elliptic curve cryptography in conjunction with a DNA-enclosed FHE system has demonstrated notable gains in encryption and decryption times, making it appropriate for dynamic Internet of Things contexts24. Another method highlights the shortcomings of edge devices like the NVIDIA Jetson Nano by evaluating FHE performance on various computing environments using the Microsoft SEAL library20. Overview of simulation work for ABE/FHE.

Authors25 proposed an efficient and robust authentication scheme tailored for smart home environments. Their work focuses on addressing security vulnerabilities in existing schemes by introducing a more secure and functionality rich authentication mechanism. While their approach addresses authentication in constrained environment it focuses only on ABE scheme rather that multiple advanced cryptographic techniques. Article26 highlighted the impact of attribute numbers on energy consumption and the feasibility of ABE in resource constrained devices. However their work also focused on ABE with specific metrics like energy, execution time. There is scope to explore other metrics which are included in our wok. Authors in20 conducted study of performance of FHE using Microsoft SEAL library. They evaluated the performance of FHE on PCs and edge devices specifically Jetson Nano. The findings underscore the importance of considering hardware constraints when implementing FHE, highlighting the need for tailored approaches to ensure practical applicability. Comparison of two Homomorphic Encryption schemes (DR-FV and DR-BGV) and HElib library, in terms of efficiency is given in27. By referring the same our work focuses on such type of comparison with other advanced cryptographic techniques in different context like smart city. Authors in28 investigated the practical feasibility of FHE on resource-constrained devices for healthcare systems, using Raspberry Pi and PCs as representative devices. They implemented privacy preserving protocol with HElib and SEAL libraries, analyzing communication overhead, computational cost, energy consumption, and ciphertext size. The study focuses exclusively on FHE in healthcare WBAN scenarios and it has scope to simulate it in other contexts with comparative performance evaluation of FHE with other schemes.

The existing literature primarily focuses on individual schemes like ABE or FHE, often without considering their integration or simulation in IoT based smart city scenarios. From literature it is evident that Fully Homomorphic operations can be time consuming for IoT, which has performance issues. So there is a need for a better model to optimize the performance of IoT application along with good security with FHE. In Attribute Based Cryptography setting good access policy with better security and access control along with optimized performance is challenging. Our proposed FA-FEO simulation model addresses these gaps by integrating FHE and ABE in smart city IoT context to evaluate, analyse and compare performances of both, offering practical deployment of these cryptographic models.

Recent work such as AUTHFi29 explores cross-technology authentication by exploiting physical-layer features like carrier frequency offset (CFO) and in-phase/quadrature (IQ) imbalance on commodity Wi-Fi to authenticate ZigBee and BLE devices. While this achieves efficient rogue device detection and low processing latency, the focus remains on signal-level device authentication rather than data confidentiality or fine-grained access control. Our model focus is on protecting sensitive data and fine-grained access control through ABE. HEAP30 proposes a hybrid CKKS-TFHE scheme-switching accelerator to parallelize CKKS bootstrapping. By embedding bootstrapping into TFHE’s BlindRotate, it reduces key size (~ 18 ×) and removes data dependencies. FPGA evaluations show ~ 15 × faster bootstrapping and significant application level gains over prior designs30. CE-RAM explores compute-enabled RAM to overcome FHE’s memory-compute bottleneck, implementing both BFV and TFHE with CE-RAM friendly optimizations. This work achieves up to 784 × speedup for BFV multiplication and 38 × for TFHE bootstrapping over CPU baselines with competitive energy and area efficiency vs. other accelerations31. FAST introduces an FPGA-based accelerator with novel data path optimizations for homomorphic linear transformations and polynomial evaluation, reducing off-chip ciphertext access. It supports the Advanced Bootstrapping Algorithm (ABA), achieving up to 8.8 × and 5.9 × speedups over state of art GPU and FPGA designs, and delivering over 20 × faster logistic regression training and improved ResNet-20 inference performance32. EFFACT presents a full stack FHE acceleration platform combining compiler-level optimizations with vector friendly hardware. It introduces streaming memory access, compact NTT and automorphism units, and circuit-level function reuse to cut resource costs while sustaining throughput. FPGA-EFFACT outperforms prior FPGA accelerators by 1.22 × , while ASIC-EFFACT achieves superior performance per area and per Watt across CKKS, BGV, and BFV workloads, making it cost-sensitive yet efficient solution33. ABC-FHE targets the client-side bottleneck in FHE by introducing a streaming accelerator that supports bootstrappable parameters with minimal area and power. It integrates a reconfigurable NTT/FFT engine, on-chip random/twiddle generation, and optimized scheduling to cut memory access. The design achieves dramatic gains – up to 1112 × faster encoding/encryption and 963 × faster decoding/decryption vs. CPU, far surpassing prior client-side accelerators34.

While recent works on FHE optimizations (E.g., HEAP, FAST, EFFACT, CE-RAM, ABC-FHE) have focused on hardware acceleration and bootstrapping refinements to improve kernel-level efficiency. In contrast, our work take comparative perspective by evaluating FHE alongside (CP-ABE and KP-ABE) within unified environment, analysing various network level metrics in smart city IoT context. In real-time, conducting such studies is resource intensive and often constrained by platform-specific hardware and software configurations. In future it may be possible to combine such simulation insights with hardware advances for real-time secure IoT deployments.

Proposed FA-FEO security model

Research motivation and contribution

Application requirements of smart city environments Smart cities reply on dense networks of sensors, gateways edge nodes, and cloud services for real-time monitoring and controlling the areas such as traffic, safety and healthcare. These systems must ensure secure transmission of sensitive data through encryption, provide fine-grained access control for different stakeholders, support privacy-preserving computations by enabling processing without decryption, and maintain efficiency and scalability to meet the latency, energy, and memory constraints of resource-limited devices.

Scientific problems to be addressed These requirements specified above, emerge with several challenges like:

How can we enable strong privacy guarantees using advanced cryptographic technique rather than traditional cryptographic approach in untrusted environment?

How can we enforce context-aware, fine-grained access control in large-scale sensor networks?

What are the performance and energy implications of applying advanced cryptographic schemes in constrained IoT network?

Limitations of existing approaches Traditional encryption methods secure data but lacks computations on encrypted data and stronger attribute-based access control. FHE enables computations on ciphtertext but is costly so here simplified FHE model with optimization is implemented35,36. ABE provide stronger attribute-based access control. So, performance of both these techniques (FHE and ABE) in the same context with their suitability for IoT-based smart cities remain insufficiently studied. By identifying this need we designed a cryptographic model which implements both FHE and variants of ABE (CP-ABE and KP-ABE) to study its suitability for smart city environment monitoring context.

Necessity and Novelty of this research To address this gap, we have introduced FA-FEO model as a simulation-based framework using NS-2 to evaluate FHE, CP-ABE, KP-ABE, and a BASE techniques for smart city context. It measures various metrics providing empirical insights into strengths, weakness, and trade-offs of each scheme. The study shows the feasibility of optimizing FHE and ABE for practical IoT use case under specific assumptions. Here all these techniques (FHE, CP-ABE, KP-ABE, and a BASE) are individually assessed with several common metrics for comparative analysis.

The implemented model comprises security analysis of four security techniques, which are: KP-ABE, CP-ABE, FHE, and BASE Encryption technique. This collaborative sensor network collectively provides a real-time, holistic understanding of the city’s environmental conditions. Periodically, sensors send data to the base station, which is arranged in data tables. The base station uses a hierarchical network to send this data to user devices. Gateways provide an internet connection between user devices and the base station. They enable user devices to connect to the best gateway by broadcasting on a regular basis. The gateway provides a timestamp and User ID upon connecting, allowing safe and effective communication. When creating any application, some IoT attacks can be prevented by simply taking security steps, such as using devices that are hard to tamper with or verifying the node identity during connection37. A multi-hop network is used by user devices to connect to the base station. The base station handles authentication using the hashed username, device ID, and password. After verification, the base station updates its records by providing the required data or, if necessary, gathering new data from sensors. Robust encryption preserves data integrity and confidentiality throughout communication between sensors, base stations, and user devices. A secure key exchange develops a shared encryption key between the sender and the recipient before any data transmission. Depending on the needs of the system, the data is subsequently encrypted using one of methods, such as KP-ABE, CP-ABE, FHE, or the BASE approach. Each technique ensures that only authorized users can access the original content by using a unique encryption and decryption algorithm. To improve performance and computational efficiency, all three methods use fast exponentiation (KP-ABE, CP-ABE, and FHE) as an optimization approach during key generation. Furthermore, a comparison of the outcomes of above said techniques with a BASE encryption method is carried out. This also includes protection against replay attacks, denial-of-service (DoS), insider threats, packet capturing, eavesdropping, and the handling of anonymous or duplicate packets. By accounting for these attack vectors, the system maintains a secure, resilient, and trustworthy communication environment for all devices involved. During this whole process optimization technique is applied during key generation.

Algorithm overview (FHE)

To illustrate the conceptual behaviour of secure data processing and sharing in a wireless sensor network, a simplified simulation of Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE) has been employed in this study. Simplified Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE) model used in this work is designed specifically for doing comparative study of FHE and ABE schemes by integration within the network simulation environment. This abstraction enables to conceptually demonstrate secure data processing and sharing in a wireless sensor network (WSN) while managing the substantial computational demands typically associated with real-world FHE implementations. Due to complexity inherent in existing full-fledged FHE schemes such as those based on lattice based cryptography and supporting operations like bootstrapping, relinearization and ciphertext modulus switching, following simplifications were made in our model.

Ciphertext representation Ciphertexts are modelled using integer arrays (e.g. c1[],c2[]) rather than encrypted polynomials over ring structures as seen in schemes like BFV or CKKS.

Noise management Noise growth is tracked using fixed integer thresholds (e.g. noisea[]) instead of polynomial based noise budget used in actual FHE libraries.

Homomorphic operations Homomorphic addition and multiplications (EvalAdd, EvalMult) are implemented using basic integer arithmetic to mimic FHE operations without invoking cryptographic primitives.

Key generation and encryption These procedures utilize random integers to simulate key components, avoiding the computational overhead of generating large cryptographic keys or context parameters.

These simplifications enable the evaluation of secure communication logic, attack resilience, and data confidentiality in a networked simulation smart city environment monitoring context. However, it is important to note that this approach does not implement use of existing cryptographic FHE libraries like Microsoft SEAL, HElib, or PALISADE and thus cannot be used for benchmarking or comparing computational performance with real-world FHE systems. These libraries are suitable for real-world applications but with high computational cost. Instead, the focus of this study is on capturing the conceptual behaviour and system-level impact of encrypted data exchange in the presence of adversaries under constraints of typical WSN environment35,38,39.

Algorithm overview (CP-ABE and KP-ABE)

Two ABE variations, CP-ABE and KP-ABE, are used in the suggested security model. Both the CP-ABE and KP-ABE paradigms are aligned with the keys and policy generation features. Depending on the scheme, keys or policies contain attributes like connectionIP, gatewayIP, index, connectionTime, distance, coverage, and number of connections. With the Ciphertext-Policy ABE (CP-ABE) technique, which is implemented using getCPKey(), the decryption key carries attributes, and the access policy is embedded in the ciphertext. Once the policy is defined, it is used in conjunction with the input message (M) for the encryption process, depicted in Fig. 4, and we obtain the ciphertext with policy (P). This gives data owners the ability to control who can decrypt their data. If the user’s attributes satisfy the access policy associated with the ciphertext, then decryption is made possible to get the decrypted message (M).Fig. 4Proposed CP-ABE technique with fast exponentiation.

Proposed CP-ABE technique with fast exponentiation.

In contrast to CP-ABE, Key-Policy ABE (KP-ABE), whose key generation is implemented via getKPKey(), embeds the policy in the key, while the ciphertext is labeled with attributes. Once the key generation is done along with embedding access policy in the decryption key, the input message (M) undergoes the encryption process, which outputs ciphertext transmitted over the IoT network as shown in Fig. 5. If the user’s attributes satisfy the access policy associated with decryption key then decryption is made possible to get the decrypted message (M).Fig. 5Proposed KP-ABE technique with fast exponentiation.

Proposed KP-ABE technique with fast exponentiation.

Function for forming access control policy in ABE.

Subsequently, the generated key formulated based on attributes and access policies is employed in the encryption process. This model includes getPolicy() function shown in (2) formulates policy and supports both KP-ABE and CP-ABE schemes by dynamically computing control decisions based on real-time network attributes, such as node distance and connectivity, to enforce context-aware access control.

This experimental study offered an empirical comparison of the results obtained for several encryption methods, such as Ciphertext-Policy Attribute-Based Encryption (CP-ABE), Key-Policy Attribute-Based Encryption (KP-ABE), and Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE), using a simulated environment. Each has been assessed both with and without fast exponentiation, and it has been contrasted with a reference BASE system. Numerous performance metrics, including resource usage, overhead management, speed, latency, power consumption, memory requirements, and energy efficiency, served as the foundation for this investigation.

In an IoT sensor network designed for environmental monitoring in smart cities, Table 5 presents the statistical analysis of the implemented model, comparing the outcomes of four cryptographic algorithms (CP-ABE, KP-ABE, FHE, and non-optimized BASE scheme) across varying node counts. Various node configurations were used to run each algorithm several times in order to produce reliable statistical findings for key performance metrics.

During the key generation process of selected algorithms, fast exponentiation Optimization is used in the adopted model (FA-FEO) to improve the performance of FHE and ABE-based schemes. With the lowest standard deviation and the best average performance across energy consumption, latency, and packet delivery ratio (PDR) among the evaluated techniques, CP-ABE continuously shows reliable and stable results across all network sizes. On the other hand, the BASE Scheme, which serves as a baseline for comparison, records the highest standard deviations and average values for the majority of metrics. This suggests a lack of scalability and efficiency, which makes it less appropriate for real-time, resource-constrained IoT contexts. Without fast exponentiation, model becomes inefficient and exhibit unpredictable latency.

Energy efficiency, latency reduction, and execution time were all consistently improved by methods that used fast exponentiation, most notably CP-ABE and KP-ABE. These benefits are particularly relevant in wireless sensor networks, where responsiveness and network lifetime are directly impacted by computational load. Fast exponentiation makes potentially intricate encryption algorithms feasible for real-time smart city applications by significantly reducing the processor overhead during cryptographic operations.

With a competitive normalized overhead ratio (2.979) and excellent memory efficiency (0.039 MB), KP-ABE closely follows CP-ABE. It is also suitable for scalable, lightweight IoT systems due to its low variation across metrics. Despite its unique ability to compute on encrypted data, FHE has significant computational requirements. As per the results of our model, without optimization, it is unsuitable for real-time use.

However, the use of fast exponentiation significantly improves the performance of the model, reducing execution time and energy usage to feasible levels. This improvement expands the application of FHE in IoT situations that are sensitive to privacy concerns but have limitations. On the other hand, the BASE Scheme underperforms across almost all metrics. It has the highest overhead (5.021), memory need (0.065 MB), and resource utilization (average energy: 0.102 J, power: 7.64 W). Such inefficiency compromises the feasibility of scalable smart city networks. Computational optimizations such as fast exponentiation play a crucial part in all schemes.

Security analysis

The proposed FA-FEO model includes logic modelling of key generation, encryption, and decryption, secure data exchange delays. Data flow or data exchange is simulated in IoT sensor network to observe timing behaviour under various crypto schemes. The model simulates adversarial scenarios such as insider attack, replay attack, packet capturing, denial-of-service (DoS), eavesdropping, packet duplication and injection of anonymous packets. These simulations evaluate how different cryptographic schemes behave under threat-prone conditions by observing timing behaviour, detecting unauthorized decryption attempts, and isolating energy and processing impacts in secure communication context. Although cryptographic operations are abstracted, the framework demonstrates through simulation results and execution that our model maintains security, confidentiality and resists diverse network-level attacks.

To strengthen network communication against potential threats a thorough verification framework is employed. This framework accounts for a range of adversarial strategies. By addressing these scenarios within the verification process, the system establishes defense mechanism against possible security breaches. This strategy underscores a commitment to protecting the confidentiality, integrity and availability of data transmission, thereby fostering a secure and dependable communication infrastructure.

Within network environments, malicious nodes introduced by attackers are strategically positioned to infiltrate legitimate communication channels. During data exchanges, reply attacks emerge when adversaries intercept and store transmission p[packets, later resending them with fabricated requests to deceive target devices. Similarly, packet capturing attackers attempts to extract sensitive information from intercepted encrypted transmissions. Insider threats pose additional risk, as compromised or disguised nodes within the network exploit their apparent legitimacy to launch attacks once data is received.

DoS attackers disrupt operations by flooding the network with arbitrary data, hindering the smooth flow of information. Meanwhile, eavesdropper stealthily monitors and captures live transmissions to gather potentially exploitable packet data.

To combat these risks, an enhanced verification process is applied during integrity checks. Detected malicious nodes are immediately removed from the network to preserve secure communication. In addition, information about identified attackers is disseminated to other nodes, ensuring collective awareness and coordinated defense. This collaborative model strengthens the overall resilience of the system, effectively reducing the impact of diverse attack vectors and prompting a trustworthy and secure communication environment.

Limitations

Despite promising network simulation results, findings must be viewed within the simulator’s limitations for cryptographic workloads. While NS-2 is widely used for modelling network behaviour, its power consumption estimates lack low-level hardware integration, making absolute energy metrics indicative rather than definitive for IoT or embedded systems40. We aim to keep results within same context for all techniques to enable fair comparative evaluations. FHE is modelled in an abstracted form, allowing consistent comparison despite the complexity of its operations, which limits the accuracy of metrics evaluation35. The implemented simulation model omits real-time hardware effects like cache behaviour or hardware acceleration, keeping comparisons focused on algorithmic design rather than platform-specific noise. Moreover absence of libraries such as HElib, SEAL, etc. enforces high level, modular approach that avoids hardware specific optimizations, ensuring consistent and comparable simulation results36. These constraints outline the scope and guide progress towards hardware, libraries and real-world validation.

Conclusion and future work

This study implemented a model in the network simulator that includes a simplified simulation of Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE), Ciphertext-Policy and Key-Policy Attribute-Based Encryption (CP-ABE and KP-ABE) to propose and evaluate a secure communication for Internet of Things (IoT) enabled smart city sensor network. Basic homomorphic operations on encrypted data are supported by the model in order to simulate the behavior of FHE in the NS-2 simulation environment. Due to high computational demands usually associated with real-world FHE implementations, such as large key sizes, high latency, and reliance on specialized cryptographic libraries, this abstraction was thoughtfully designed to capture the essential behavior of core FHE. Extensive simulations were conducted using the FHE and ABE with Fast Exponentiation Optimization (FA-FEO) model, and the effectiveness of these methods was evaluated using a number of indicators. Results obtained by the implementation of FHE functionality with certain assumptions demonstrated that fundamental FHE computations can be performed in an IoT environment while ensuring privacy, while results of ABE indicated that CP-ABE and KP-ABE offer reliable, scalable, and energy-efficient communication. The results clearly demonstrate that CP-ABE performs well in a variety of scenarios, with minimal variations in outcomes across varying numbers of sensor nodes. The BASE scheme, on the other hand, highlighted the difficulties in adapting traditional, non-optimized approaches to meet the demands of dynamic IoT environments.

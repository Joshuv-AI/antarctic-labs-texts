The intelligent evaluation in ice and snow tourism based on LSTM network
Authors: Jun Li, Hailong Yuan, Xia Yu, Tian Hu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn order to augment the efficacy of the intelligent evaluation model for assessing the suitability of ice and snow tourism, this study refines the model by incorporating the Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network within the framework of the Internet of Things (IoT). The investigation commences with an elucidation of the application of IoT technology in environmental detection. After this, an analysis is conducted on the structure of LSTM and its merits in the realm of time series prediction. Ultimately, a novel model for appraising the suitability of ice and snow tourism is formulated. The efficacy of this model is substantiated through empirical experiments. The results of these experiments reveal that the refined model exhibits exceptional performance across diverse climatic conditions, encompassing mild, cold, humid, and arid climates. In regions characterized by mild climates, the predictive accuracy of the refined model progressively ascends from 88% in the initial quarter to 94% in the fourth quarter, surpassing the capabilities of conventional models. Consistently robust performance is demonstrated by the refined model throughout each quarter. In terms of operational efficiency, comparative analysis indicates that the refined model attains a moderate level, manifesting a 30–33 s runtime and maintaining a Central Processing Unit (CPU) usage rate between 40 and 43%. This observation implies that the refined model adeptly balances precision against resource consumption. Consequently, this study holds significance as a scholarly reference for the integration of IoT and LSTM networks in the domain of tourism evaluation.Keywords: Internet of things, Long short-term memory network, Suitability for ice and snow tourism, Intelligent evaluation model, Time series data analysisSubject terms: Computational science, Computer science, Information technology, Scientific data

Introduction

Research background and motivations

In recent years, propelled by global climate change and the burgeoning popularity of winter sports, ice and snow tourism has emerged as a pivotal sector within the tourism industry1. This particular form of tourism significantly contributes to local economies, facilitates cultural exchange, and fosters environmental awareness2. However, the distinctive characteristic of ice and snow tourism lies in its heightened reliance on weather conditions and environmental variables. This underscores the imperative need for an efficient and precise evaluation system to gauge the appropriateness of tourism activities3–5. The advent of IoT technology has furnished a potent mechanism for real-time environmental monitoring6. Utilizing an array of sensors, real-time data pertaining to critical parameters such as temperature, humidity, and snowfall can be systematically collected. Such data assumes paramount importance in comprehending and prognosticating the environmental dynamics of destinations geared for ice and snow tourism7.

Despite the widespread research and application of the Internet of Things (IoT) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) in their respective domains, their integrated application in the assessment of ice and snow tourism suitability remains relatively underexplored8–10. This endeavor is geared towards unraveling the potential synergy of IoT and LSTM networks, aimed at augmenting the evaluative efficacy in determining the suitability of ice and snow tourism. The envisioned outcome is to furnish tourism planners and tourists with more precise and timely information.

Research objectives

The primary objectives of this study encompass the following aspects. First, it aims to develop and implement a model that combines IoT technology with LSTM networks to assess the suitability of ice and snow tourism in different regions. Second, the study seeks to experimentally validate the accuracy and reliability of this model. Additionally, this study aims to elucidate the role of IoT in environmental monitoring and data collection, particularly in the dynamic and unpredictable context of ice and snow tourism. Finally, this study endeavors to demonstrate the potential applications of IoT and LSTM networks in the tourism industry, especially in the realms of smart tourism and destination management.

The objective of this study is to propose a simplified and accurate method for evaluating the suitability of ice and snow tourism, while also laying the groundwork for the broad application of IoT and artificial intelligence technologies in the tourism industry.

Literature review

In the realm of traditional scholarly investigations, the study conducted by Wang et al. underscored a noteworthy surge in the ice and snow tourism market, particularly witnessed in Asia and Northern Europe over recent years. This upswing correlates closely with the global shifts in climate dynamics and the discernible trend toward diversification within the tourism industry11. Building upon this, Qin et al. delved into the application of the IoT in the tourism sector. Their findings accentuated the pivotal role played by IoT technology in augmenting operational efficiency at tourist destinations and enhancing the overall tourist experience, particularly in the realm of real-time data processing and analysis12. In a separate study, Tang et al. underscored the widespread adoption of LSTM networks owing to their proficiency in processing time series data, notably in the realms of climate pattern prediction and analysis of financial market trends13. Addressing the efficacy of existing tourism suitability evaluation methods, Yangutova et al. discerned limitations in conventional statistical approaches, particularly when confronted with substantial volumes of dynamic data14. Xu centered on the amalgamation of IoT and LSTM networks in environmental monitoring, emphasizing advancements in accuracy and efficiency in data processing, particularly within the domains of agriculture and urban planning15. Wu et al. expounded upon the application of IoT in environmental monitoring for tourist destinations, elucidating its efficacy in monitoring tourist influx, climate conditions, and resource utilization, thereby optimizing destination management16. A separate study by Fan et al. delved into the potential of LSTM networks in predicting intricate system behavior, with a specific focus on forecasting tourism demand and discerning patterns in tourist behavior17.

Through a retrospective examination of traditional research, it becomes apparent that conventional approaches to evaluating tourism suitability predominantly hinge on statistical models, proving inadequate in managing extensive, high-dimensional, and nonlinear datasets. Furthermore, most traditional methodologies grapple with the effective processing of real-time data, leading to evaluation outcomes that often lag behind the contemporaneous situation and fail to adapt to swiftly evolving environments and markets. In contrast, the synergistic integration of IoT and LSTM networks within this study enables the efficient processing and analysis of large-scale, multidimensional, real-time data. Leveraging the strengths inherent in LSTM networks for time series data, this work establishes a more precise and reliable foundation for predicting future conditions and trends within the domain of ice and snow tourism.

Research model

The use of IOT technology in environmental detection

The IoT constitutes a sophisticated technological framework facilitating intercommunication among physical entities, enabling the seamless exchange of data through network connections18. The foundational elements integral to IoT encompass embedded systems, sensors, communication hardware, and software. Collaboratively, these constituents empower objects to adeptly collect, transmit, and receive data, thereby fostering a degree of intelligent functionality19. Embedded systems, discreetly integrated within objects, furnish computational capabilities, while sensors serve the purpose of monitoring and gathering diverse information concerning the environment or objects, encompassing variables like temperature, pressure, and motion. Communication hardware orchestrates the bidirectional flow of data, and software undertakes the responsibilities of data processing, command execution, and network communication management20–22. This amalgamation of components allows for data acquisition, processing, and dissemination, thereby enabling a spectrum of intelligent operations, including the automatic adjustment of environmental parameters and the anticipation of maintenance requirements. IoT's extensive capacities for information exchange and processing lay the groundwork for more sophisticated and efficient system operations23–25. IoT technology can collect real-time environmental data through various sensors, such as temperature, humidity, snowfall, and wind speed. This real-time data is crucial for assessing the suitability of ice and snow tourism because dynamic changes in environmental conditions directly impact the safety and quality of the tourism experience. Traditional data collection methods often suffer from delays and incomplete data, but IoT technology can overcome these issues, providing real-time and comprehensive data support. Additionally, IoT technology can integrate multiple types of sensors to obtain multidimensional environmental information. This diverse data aids in a comprehensive evaluation of the suitability of ice and snow tourism. For instance, the combined use of temperature sensors, humidity sensors, and wind speed sensors can provide richer and more detailed environmental data, offering more accurate inputs for the model and thereby improving the accuracy and reliability of the predictions.

Moreover, IoT technology manifests its worth in the surveillance of environmental quality, providing data support for the preservation and upkeep of the natural environment in tourist destinations. Ensuring data security and user privacy becomes paramount as the volume of collected and shared data increases. Furthermore, the utilization of different standards and protocols by devices from various manufacturers presents a significant challenge to achieving interoperability.

In summary, while IoT technology furnishes potent tools for evaluating the suitability of ice and snow tourism, it simultaneously introduces new challenges and requirements that necessitate comprehensive technological and managerial strategies.

The structure of LSTM and its advantages in time series prediction

LSTM has significant advantages in handling time series data. Evaluating the suitability of ice and snow tourism requires consideration of time series variations in environmental data, such as temperature and snowfall. LSTM can effectively capture and retain long-term temporal dependencies, which is crucial for predicting the suitability of ice and snow tourism. In comparison, while other models may perform well in areas like natural language processing, their performance in handling time series data is not as stable and efficient as LSTM. Moreover, the structure of the LSTM model is relatively simple and transparent, making it easier to interpret its predictions. For the assessment of ice and snow tourism suitability, understanding the prediction logic of the model is essential for practical application and decision support. LSTM networks represent a distinctive subset of Recurrent Neural Network (RNN), meticulously tailored to tackle and forecast long-term dependencies inherent in time series data37. The essence of LSTM resides in its internal architecture, which is characterized by several fundamental components: the input gate, forget gate, and output gate. The presence of gate structures within LSTM facilitates the effective retention of long-term memory while selectively discarding information that is no longer deemed pertinent. This inherent mechanism empowers LSTM with the capability to sustain elevated efficiency and accuracy when confronted with the processing of extensive sequential data. Due to its exceptional proficiency in capturing long-term dependencies, LSTM has garnered extensive application across diverse fields. In the realm of natural language processing, it finds utility in tasks such as text generation, next-word prediction, enhancing sequence-to-sequence translation, sentiment analysis, and automated generation of article or document summaries38–40. Within computer vision, LSTM serves to comprehend and classify video content, facilitating the recognition of human or animal behaviors within video datasets41. Furthermore, it plays a vital role in the medical and biological domains, where it can forecast disease progression based on medical records and predict the behavior of drug molecules42–45.

In summary, the LSTM network has evolved into an indispensable tool for time series prediction, owing to its pronounced advantages in managing long-term dependencies and intricate time series data. Its suitability is particularly evident in addressing the complexities of dynamic environmental monitoring scenarios, such as the assessment of ice and snow tourism suitability.

Experimental design and performance evaluation

Datasets collection

The dataset employed in this study is sourced from the Climate Data Online (CDO) dataset. The CDO offers access to meticulously archive global historical weather and climate data, encompassing daily, monthly, seasonal, and annual quality-controlled temperature, precipitation, wind speed, and radar data measurements. Moreover, it provides a comprehensive 30-year set of climate standard data, proving particularly advantageous for the protracted assessment of conditions pertinent to snow and ice tourism. Interested parties can procure the dataset from the official website of the National Centers for Environmental Information (NCEI) in the United States (https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/cdo-web/). To further validate the robustness and applicability of the optimized model proposed in this study, the nClimDiv dataset is also selected for the experiments. This dataset provides temperature, precipitation, and drought index data for the contiguous United States, as well as heating and cooling degree days. Performance evaluation

Accuracy analysis of ice and snow tourism suitability prediction

The experiment entails the selection of four distinct environmental conditions: mild climate, cold climate, humid climate, and dry climate. This selection facilitates the assessment of the model's predictive accuracy across various quarters. Prediction accuracy refers to the degree of consistency between the model prediction results and the actual results. In this study, prediction accuracy measures the degree of match between the model's prediction results of ice and snow tourism suitability and the actual observed data. Figure 4 provides a visual representation of the experimental outcomes:

Accuracy analysis of predicting the suitability of ice and snow tourism (a) Temperate (b) Cold climate (c) Humid climate (d) Dry climate.

In Fig. 4, under mild climate conditions, the optimized predictive model in this study demonstrates outstanding performance. Starting from 88% accuracy in the first quarter, its accuracy gradually climbed to 94% in the fourth quarter. This significant improvement indicates the model's excellent adaptability to mild climate changes. In comparison, although traditional MLR and SVM models also show increased accuracy, the increments are limited, rising from 68 to 74% for MLR and from 78 to 84% for SVM, respectively. This suggests that the optimized model in this study exhibits higher efficiency and precision in handling mild climate data. Additionally, the accuracy of all models gradually increases with seasonal variations, possibly because, over time, the models can better adapt and learn the seasonal changes in climate data, thereby enhancing prediction accuracy. This seasonal adaptability is crucial for the model's long-term application, contributing to its predictive capability for future climate changes. Under cold conditions, the performance of all models becomes more intricate. Accuracy fluctuates across different quarters, reflecting the instability and complexity of cold climates. For instance, in the second quarter, the accuracy of the SVM model slightly decreases, while it improves in the fourth quarter. This fluctuation might be attributed to the challenges the models face in learning and adapting to data under extreme weather conditions. However, the optimized model in this study demonstrates strong adaptability under cold climates, maintaining accuracy consistently higher than the other two models, increasing from 81% in the first quarter to 88% in the fourth quarter, indicating its superiority in dealing with complex climatic conditions. Results under humid conditions are noteworthy as well. The optimized model shows a slight decline in accuracy to 77% in the second quarter, but it rebounds to 82% in the fourth quarter, demonstrating some volatility. This fluctuation may be related to the model's adaptation process to specific features of humid climates. Nevertheless, compared to the MLR and SVM models, the optimized model in this study still performs well under humid conditions, displaying good adaptability to different climate conditions. In dry climates, all models exhibit slight accuracy fluctuations. For example, the MLR model experiences a minor decline to 64% in the second quarter but rises to 67% in the fourth quarter. This fluctuation may be associated with the seasonal changes and unique features of dry climates. The performance of the optimized model in this environment surpasses other models, with accuracy gradually increasing from 84% in the first quarter to 85% in the fourth quarter, once again demonstrating its high sensitivity and adaptability to different climate characteristics.

In summary, the optimized model in this study exhibits high accuracy and robust adaptability under various climate conditions, especially in dealing with complex and variable climate data. These characteristics make the model highly promising for long-term climate forecasting and analysis applications. Future work could further explore the model's performance under more extreme climate conditions and how to optimize the model to adapt to a broader range of climate types and variations.

The optimized model proposed in this work demonstrates excellent performance in terms of runtime, with an average runtime per quarter falling between the two aforementioned models at 30–33 s. Such a balance is a crucial consideration in the design of machine learning models, especially in resource-constrained environments.

Regarding CPU usage rates, the MLR model's performance is similarly impressive, exhibiting relatively low CPU usage, averaging between 25–26%. The optimized model proposed in this work also demonstrates good performance in CPU usage rates, maintaining a moderate level at approximately 40–43%. This data point reveals that while maintaining high accuracy, the model is relatively resource-efficient compared to the SVM model. Balancing accuracy and resource consumption is crucial for practical applications, especially in environments with limited computing resources.

In summary, these three models exhibit distinct characteristics in terms of runtime and CPU usage rates. The MLR model, while maintaining lower computational costs, sacrifices some accuracy. The SVM model, although achieving high accuracy, also incurs higher computational costs. The optimized model proposed in this work strikes a balance between the two, achieving synergistic optimization of efficiency and accuracy. These findings hold significant implications for selecting and designing machine learning models tailored to specific applications.

Discussion

In the experimental analysis of prediction accuracy for ice and snow tourism suitability, the data reveal fluctuations in the accuracy of all models due to seasonal changes. These fluctuations reflect the impact of seasonal climate conditions on tourism suitability predictions. The optimized model consistently outperforms the MLR and SVM models each quarter under mild climatic conditions, indicating that the optimized model can more effectively capture the complex factors influencing tourism suitability. The accuracy of all models steadily improves from the first to the fourth quarter, attributed to the model's progressively enhanced understanding of the data and the increasing impact of climate conditions on tourism suitability towards the end of the year. The optimized model shows significant improvement each quarter, particularly excelling in handling time-series data and capturing long-term dependencies. In cold climates, the fluctuations in model accuracy are associated with seasonal climate characteristics, sudden climatic events, or changes in the model's sensitivity to specific climate variables, adding to the prediction difficulty. Under humid climates, all models exhibit varying degrees of accuracy fluctuations, primarily due to the inherent instability of humid climates. High humidity and frequent rainfall may introduce significant data variations, increasing the prediction challenges. In evaluating model efficiency, the MLR model has the shortest runtime, benefiting from the computational efficiency of its simple model. The SVM model, due to its algorithmic complexity, has the longest runtime. The optimized model's runtime falls between that of the MLR and SVM models, averaging 30–33 s, with minimal variation across quarters, demonstrating stable operational efficiency. In the CPU usage evaluation, the MLR model shows lower CPU usage due to its lower algorithmic complexity, making it suitable for resource-constrained environments. The SVM model requires more computational resources, potentially significantly impacting system performance in resource-limited scenarios. The resource consumption of all models shows little variation across quarters, indicating stable performance during operation. The efficiency of model resource usage is particularly important for long-term operations and large-scale data processing.

This study briefly discusses the constraints of computational resources, but a more in-depth exploration of the impact of resource limitations on model implementation and scalability is warranted. Although the LSTM model demonstrates superior accuracy and stability, its computational complexity and resource consumption remain key challenges in implementation and scalability. Future research could explore techniques such as model pruning, quantization, and distributed computing to alleviate these resource constraints and enhance the model's practical applicability. Given the dynamic nature of environmental changes, particularly in the context of climate change, it is necessary to further discuss the model's long-term adaptability and resilience. The uncertainties brought by climate change and the increasing frequency of extreme weather events may place higher demands on the model's predictive capabilities. Enhancing the model's adaptability to continually changing environmental conditions will be crucial. Potential strategies include: enabling the model to continuously adapt to new environmental data through online learning and model update mechanisms, combining the strengths of multiple models to enhance robustness under different climatic conditions, and enriching the training set with simulated data and environmental forecast data to improve the model's predictive ability under extreme conditions.

In summary, this study validates the significant advantages of the LSTM model in assessing ice and snow tourism suitability by comparing the prediction accuracy and operational efficiency of the optimized model with MLR and SVM models under different climatic conditions. It provides a more precise and efficient method, offering scientific support for the intelligent management and decision-making in ice and snow tourism. Additionally, by delving into resource constraints and the model's long-term adaptability, the practical utility and scalability of the proposed approach are further enhanced.

Conclusion

Research contribution

This study proposes an innovative approach that integrates IoT technology with LSTM networks for evaluating the suitability of ice and snow tourism. Through IoT technology, the experiment achieved real-time collection of environmental and climate data, while LSTM networks effectively processed and predicted these data. The study comprehensively assessed the model's accuracy, efficiency, and stability through case studies under various climatic conditions.

Firstly, the study combines IoT technology with LSTM networks for the first time, creating a real-time, dynamic model for assessing the suitability of ice and snow tourism, thus filling a gap in existing research. The experiment utilizes IoT technology for real-time monitoring and collection of environmental and climate data, improving the timeliness and accuracy of data and providing a reliable data foundation for dynamically assessing the suitability of ice and snow tourism. Under mild, cold, humid, and dry climatic conditions, the optimized model achieves prediction accuracies of 94%, 88%, 82%, and 85%, respectively, significantly outperforming MLR and SVM models, demonstrating the robust processing capability of LSTM networks under complex climatic conditions. Additionally, the optimized model averages a runtime of 30–33 s, significantly better than the SVM model's 45–48 s, while maintaining a CPU usage rate of 40–43%, demonstrating its balanced performance in computational efficiency and resource consumption. Through comparative analysis with MLR and SVM models, the experiment validates the advantages of the optimized model under different climatic conditions, providing detailed data support and theoretical basis, and promoting the development of ice and snow tourism suitability assessment technology. Lastly, the proposed model provides precise assessment methods for ice and snow tourism planning and management, assisting relevant departments in formulating more scientific tourism strategies under various climatic conditions to enhance tourism experience and safety. Moreover, this model is not only applicable to ice and snow tourism suitability assessment but also has broad applications in environmental monitoring and prediction in other fields such as agricultural climate suitability assessment and urban environmental monitoring, showing promising application prospects. By achieving these contributions, this study not only provides innovative methods and tools for intelligent assessment of ice and snow tourism but also opens new paths for the application of IoT and artificial intelligence technologies in the tourism industry, which is significant for tourism planning, environmental protection, and sustainable development.

Future works and research limitations

The future avenues for research can be delineated across four principal dimensions. Firstly, broadening the spectrum of data sources and types is necessary. In order to augment the comprehensiveness and precision of the model, forthcoming endeavors could encompass the integration of a more expansive array of data, such as socio-economic indicators and tourist feedback. Subsequently, there is a focus on refining algorithms and models. Exploring advanced machine learning techniques and algorithms aims to enhance the model's accuracy and robustness across diverse climatic conditions. Thirdly, extensive application tests are imperative. Subjecting the model to tests in varied regions and types of snow and ice tourism destinations is essential to affirm its generalizability and practical applicability. Lastly, emphasis is placed on fortifying real-time data processing capabilities to bolster the model's proficiency in handling and responding to real-time data, thereby adapting to dynamic environmental changes.

The limitations of the study are mainly reflected in the computing resources. Resource constraints for computational resources could pose a challenge, particularly as complex machine learning models may demand substantial computational power, especially in scenarios with limited resources. Lastly, the uncertainty associated with environmental changes, including abrupt shifts in climate and environmental conditions, may present challenges to the model's long-term accuracy and adaptability.

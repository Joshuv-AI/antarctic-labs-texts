Transient Podocytopathy Versus Progressive Collapse in High Risk APOL1 Genotype
Authors: Nav La, Schawanya Kaewpitoon Rattanapitoon, Natthawut Charoenphon, Nathkapach Kaewpitoon Rattanapitoon
Published: 2025

To the Editor:

Practicing nephrology in a dengue-endemic setting, we were immediately drawn to the report by Neves et al.1 Their study raises important possibilities about dengue virus as a “second hit” in APOL1 high-risk genotype carriers. However, one case in particular—patient 2—prompted us to pause and comment. Unlike the relentlessly progressive course that defines APOL1-mediated collapsing glomerulopathy, as extensively reviewed by Albaqumi and Barisoni,2 this patient’s renal function and proteinuria improved substantially without immunosuppression. This raises the possibility that the biopsy captured a transient, reversible podocytopathy triggered by dengue’s inflammatory storm, rather than true APOL1-mediated collapsing glomerulopathy.

If so, the implications are considerable. First, biopsy timing may critically shape interpretation. This raises the possibility that sampling during an acute systemic inflammatory phase could yield collapsing morphology that might later regress. Could urinary or circulating podocyte injury markers such as nephrin fragments or exosomal podocin help distinguish reversible injury from irreversible APOL1-driven collapse?

Second, we noted the absence of tubuloreticular inclusions on electron microscopy. Given their frequent presence in interferon-driven lesions, how do you reconcile this with the proposed mechanism of interferon-mediated APOL1 upregulation? Might this absence argue for a broader, more heterogeneous spectrum of dengue-triggered podocytopathies than previously appreciated?3, 4, 5

Finally, if self-limiting phenotypes exist within dengue-associated collapsing glomerulopathy, how should clinicians in arboviral-endemic regions respond? Recognizing such a spectrum might prevent overtreatment while maintaining vigilance for irreversible injury.

We commend Neves et al.1 for their important contribution and hope these observations encourage further exploration of the spectrum of dengue-associated podocytopathies.

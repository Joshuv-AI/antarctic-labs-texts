Microsurgical Clip Reconstruction of Multiple Contiguous Aneurysms of the Anterior Communicating Artery Complex: Subfrontal Exposure and Endoscopic Verification—A Case Illustration
Authors: Hongjun Liu, Lilian Oberschachtsiek, Sajjad Muhammad
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND AND IMPORTANCE:Multiple contiguous aneurysms of the anterior communicating artery (ACoA) complex present significant technical challenges due to their irregular geometry and the density of critical hypothalamic perforators. Standard microscopic visualization is often limited by optical obstruction from clip blades or line-of-sight constraints during complex reconstruction.CLINICAL PRESENTATION:A 62-year-old woman presented with aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage. Preoperative digital subtraction angiography and volumetric segmentation identified 4 distinct aneurysmal origins distributed across the ACoA-A2 complex. A subfrontal approach with opening of the proximal sylvian fissure was used to achieve adequate exposure of the aneurysm complex. Stepwise reconstruction was performed using 4 specific clips under intermittent bilateral A1 temporary occlusion. A minor residual neck remnant identified under the microscope was corrected. Endoscopic inspection was subsequently performed for final verification, confirming complete aneurysm obliteration and preservation of the deep perforating arteries. The patient was discharged with a modified Rankin Scale score of 0. Three-month follow-up digital subtraction angiography demonstrated complete angiographic occlusion.CONCLUSION:For multiple contiguous ACoA aneurysms, a systematic multimodal verification strategy incorporating subfrontal exposure with opening of the proximal sylvian fissure and endoscopic inspection may help ensure complete exclusion while protecting the hypothalamic circulation.

The anterior communicating artery (ACoA) complex is an anatomically dense region where the margin for error in perforator preservation is minimal. Beyond the A1 and A2 segments, surgeons must protect critical hypothalamic or subcallosal branches that support the optic chiasm, hypothalamus, and corpus callosum.1 Lesions involving multiple independent sacs require a transition from simple clip ligation to comprehensive vascular reconstruction. The primary surgical challenge involves the visual limitations created by initial clip placement, which may conceal residual neck fragments or inadvertently compromise posterior-superior perforators. We describe a systematic strategy emphasizing adequate anatomic exposure and multimodal intraoperative verification to address these technical constraints.

CLINICAL PRESENTATION

A 62-year-old woman (Glasgow Coma Scale 15, World Federation of Neurosurgical Societies Grade I) presented after a sudden severe headache. Initial noncontrast computed tomography demonstrated subarachnoid hemorrhage (Figure 1A). Preoperative panangiography and computed tomography angiography identified a complex anatomic variant comprising 4 distinct aneurysms arising from the ACoA-A2 complex (Figure 1B and 1C). A right-sided lobulated component was considered the most likely rupture site. Preoperative assessment confirmed robust cross-filling, facilitating safe intermittent bilateral A1 temporary occlusion.

Segmentation using 3-dimensional Slicer identified 4 distinct aneurysmal origins (L1-L4), ranging from 2.3 to 2.5 mm in maximum diameter (Figure 2A and 2B).2 The divergent vectors of these 4 lesions supported the diagnosis of multiple contiguous aneurysms rather than a single multiloculated sac. This morphological analysis guided the strategy for the individual reconstruction of each lesion to maintain parent vessel patency.

Ethics Approval and Patient Consent

Ethics approval: Not applicable (single-case technical instruction), per institutional policy. Patient consent: Written informed consent was obtained for publication of clinical information, images, and video.

Operative Strategy

A left pterional craniotomy was performed. The aneurysm complex was exposed through a subfrontal approach with opening of the proximal sylvian fissure. This exposure provided a sufficiently wide operative corridor and panoramic visualization of the ACoA region, facilitating identification of the aneurysm cluster and adjacent perforating structures before clip application.

Dissection was performed under intermittent bilateral A1 temporary clipping (intervals <3 minutes) to reduce intra-aneurysmal tension. Stepwise clip reconstruction was then performed using 4 specific clips to address each independent lobule. An initial standard curved clip (Aesculap No. 720) was applied along the primary axis of the ruptured aneurysm. This initial clip application approximated the aneurysm base and helped realign the ACoA-A2 junction.3 Subsequently, the remaining 3 aneurysmal components were reconstructed using a standard straight clip (No. 710) for the superiorly projecting component, an angled curved clip (No. 712), and a fenestrated clip (No. 602) to secure the base adjacent to the right A1-A2 junction without compromising the parent artery. Throughout the procedure, meticulous attention was directed toward preserving the hypothalamic branches and the recurrent artery of Heubner (Video).

Intraoperative Verification and Outcome

After reconstruction, microscopic inspection identified a minor residual neck remnant at the base of the primary lesion, which was promptly corrected by adjustment of the clip construct. Indocyanine green videoangiography initially suggested satisfactory occlusion.4 To address the visual obscuration caused by the titanium clip construct, endoscopic inspection was performed.5 This additional verification confirmed the patency of the hypothalamic perforators located posterior to the clip mass and verified the absence of occult remnants. Microvascular Doppler ultrasonography further confirmed parent vessel patency.6

Immediate postoperative computed tomography demonstrated expected surgical changes without new hemorrhage (Figure 3A and 3B). On postoperative day 8, MRI with diffusion-weighted imaging demonstrated the absence of acute ischemic injury (Figure 3C), and magnetic resonance angiography confirmed the patency of the bilateral A2 segments (Figure 3D). The patient recovered without neurological deficits and was discharged with a modified Rankin Scale score of 0.7 At 3 months, follow-up digital subtraction angiography confirmed complete angiographic obliteration (Figure 3E-3G).

DISCUSSION

Key Results

The successful management of this complex case demonstrates that distinguishing multiple contiguous ACoA aneurysms from a single multiloculated sac is essential for determining the appropriate clipping strategy.8 The use of a subfrontal exposure with opening of the proximal sylvian fissure provided the necessary operative corridor and visualization to identify all 4 discrete aneurysmal origins. The stepwise application of 4 individual clips enabled precise vascular reconstruction while preserving parent artery flow. By modulating the intra-aneurysmal tension with an initial proximal clip, we successfully realigned the complex anatomic axes of the ACoA-A2 junction, converting a high-risk obliteration into a controlled, stepwise vascular restoration.

Limitations

As a single-case illustration, the primary limitation is the inherent anatomic variability of the ACoA complex, meaning this specific 4-clip configuration cannot be universally standardized for all patients. Furthermore, the efficacy of this systematic verification workflow relies heavily on the availability of advanced intraoperative adjuncts (such as high-definition endoscopy, indocyanine green videoangiography, and microvascular Doppler) and the surgical team's expertise in complex microsurgical approaches. Finally, while the immediate and 3-month angiographic results demonstrated complete occlusion, longer-term angiographic follow-up is required to definitively confirm the permanence and durability of this reconstructive multiclip configuration.

Interpretation

Our findings highlight the limitation of relying solely on microscopic line-of-sight in complex aneurysm surgery. The ACoA region is densely populated by critical perforating vessels, and the titanium clip construct inevitably creates significant visual obscuration. While the operating microscope remains the gold standard for microdissection, perforator mapping, and clip application, it is insufficient for interrogating blind spots posterior to the clip construct. Endoscopic inspection served as an important complementary verification tool by providing angled visualization of these obscured regions, thereby confirming both complete aneurysm exclusion and preservation of the subcallosal and hypothalamic perforators.

Generalizability

The technical principles outlined in this report are highly generalizable to other complex, high-density vascular lesions within neurosurgery. The fundamental transition from simple clip ligation to a stepwise vascular reconstruction—combined with a routine multimodal intraoperative verification loop—can be applied to other challenging locations, such as wide-necked middle cerebral artery bifurcations or basilar apex aneurysms. This systematic workflow provides an objective mechanism to minimize the risks of inadvertent perforator occlusion and incomplete clipping across a wide spectrum of cerebrovascular pathology.

CONCLUSION

Microsurgical reconstruction of multiple contiguous ACoA aneurysms requires comprehensive spatial visualization. Integrating subfrontal exposure with opening of the proximal sylvian fissure and endoscopic inspection may facilitate detection of remnants obscured by the clip construct and helps preserve the fragile hypothalamic vascular architecture.

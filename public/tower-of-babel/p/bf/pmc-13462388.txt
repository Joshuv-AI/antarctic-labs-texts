Comparison of Serum Chloride and pH between Sterofundin and Plasmalyte in the Initial Resuscitation of Critically Ill Patients in the ICU: A Bayesian Adaptive Randomized Non-inferiority Clinical Trial – Interim Analysis
Authors: Charvi Kundal, Venkata Ganesh, Vikas Saini, Neeru Sahni, Ajay Singh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionMetabolic acidosis is prevalent in critically ill patients, with moderate or severe acidosis occurring in 8.4% of ICU patients and associated with mortality rates of 17.3% in ICU and 21.5% in hospital settings.1 Hyperchloremic acidosis resulting from intravenous fluid administration is well-documented, occurring in 19% to 45% of patients in the ICU. This study aims to address the practical clinical question of whether Sterofundin (SF) can be used interchangeably with Plasmalyte (PL) for ICU resuscitation, given the paucity of comparative data and theoretical concerns that SF's higher chloride content may increase serum chloride and cause acidosis compared to PL.ObjectivesThe primary objective was to estimate the difference in serum chloride and pH at the end of resuscitation between the two balanced salt solution groups, Sterofundin and Plasmalyte, and demonstrate non-inferiority.Materials and methodsThis prospective randomized non-inferiority trial enrolled critically ill adults presenting to ICU in shock requiring fluid resuscitation. Patients were randomized 1:1 to receive either SF or PL during initial resuscitation. Primary endpoints were serum chloride concentration and pH at 24 hours post-resuscitation. Non-inferiority margins were set at 3 mEq/L for chloride and -0.045 for pH, preserving 40% and 50% respectively of PL's treatment effect versus normal saline. The interim analysis was planned at 50% recruitment (26 patients) with predetermined stopping rules: stop for efficacy if posterior probability of non-inferiority exceeded 99%, stop for futility if below 5%, or continue if between 5-99%. Both frequentist and Bayesian analytical approaches were employed.ResultsFifty-two were enrolled at interim analysis. For serum chloride, Bayesian analysis demonstrated a posterior probability of 0.97 for non-inferiority (P(μSF - μPL ≤ 3 | data) = 0.94) under a non-informative prior (Figure C). The frequentist analysis showed no evidence of inferiority (p = 0.641 for hypothesis H0: μSF - μPL ≤ 3) as shown in Figure A. For pH, frequentist analysis showed p = 0.752 for the inferiority hypothesis (H0: μSF - μPL ≥ -0.045) as shown in Figure B. Neither predefined efficacy nor futility stopping criteria were met.DiscussionDespite Curran et al.'s meta-analysis showing PL's marginal advantages (chloride 0.83 mmol/L lower, base excess 0.65 mmol/L higher), with only one study which directly compared SF vs PL.2 Our RCT addresses this paucity of head-to-head ICU data with 94% posterior probability supporting SF's non-inferiority. Full trial completion will provide definitive evidence for clinical practice guidelines.Keywords: Plasmalyte, Sterofundin, ICU, Resuscitation

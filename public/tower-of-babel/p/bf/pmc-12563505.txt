Oral Electrolytes Use in Pediatric Non-Diarrheal Illnesses: A Retrospective, Indian Real-world Study
Authors: Pavitra Chakravarty, Rahul Nagpal, Harshad Malve, Amol Patil
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroduction:Dehydration due to non-diarrheal illnesses is often underrecognized. Limited literature is available on the use of oral electrolytes in non-diarrheal conditions in pediatric patients. This study was conducted to understand the recommendations of oral electrolytes and ready-to-drink (RTD) solutions (ORSL®, ORSL Plus®, and ORL Rehydrate®) for non-diarrheal conditions. The effect of oral electrolytes on symptom resolution and recovery from fever and respiratory tract infection (RTI) was evaluated.Methodology:Anonymized data of patients on the HealthPlix electronic medical records (EMR) database meeting the eligibility criteria was retrieved and analyzed.Results:RTD electrolytes were prescribed to 31.44% of the patients prescribed oral electrolytes (55,931). ORSL® was prescribed to the highest proportion of patients (48.60%). ORSL Plus® was most recommended among patients with dehydration (62% of those recommended RTDs) and dengue (40% of patients on RTDs). Body ache, vomiting, fatigue, nausea, and giddiness were the most common symptoms reported by patients prescribed oral electrolytes. ORSL® was the most prescribed in cases of body aches and vomiting (16% and 19% of the patients, respectively, on RTDs). ORSL®, ORSL Plus®, and ORSL Rehydrate® were prescribed for 1–3 days to most of the patients. ORSL® and ORSL Plus® were prescribed to 37.2% and 54.5% of the patients, respectively, with thrice-a-day (TID) schedule while ORSL Plus® was majorly prescribed for a four-times-a-day (QID) schedule. The proportion of patients resolved RTIs was higher for the patients prescribed with oral electrolytes (57.35%) in comparison with the ones not prescribed (48.57%). The duration of recovery for patients with fever was shorter in the case of oral electrolyte arm suggesting faster recovery.Conclusion:There is a need to create awareness around the advantages of prescribing oral RTD fluid electrolytes and energy formulations in non-diarrheal conditions. This has the potential to shorten the duration of sickness and speed up recovery.

INTRODUCTION

Children are more prone to dehydration because of their physiological specificities, including large skin surface in the early phase of life. It has also been reported that most of the children do not meet the adequate water recommendations.[1]

Most dehydrated children can be rehydrated through the oral route.[2] Oral rehydration therapy (ORT) is a cost-effective option in cases of mild-to-moderate dehydration.[3] Well-documented guidelines exist for the treatment of dehydration due to diarrheal conditions.[4] However, it goes unnoticed in case of non-diarrheal illnesses like fever, arboviral disease, dengue, malaria, and other conditions like nausea, vomiting, heat-related illness, viral infections, respiratory tract infections (RTIs), and urinary tract infections (UTIs).[56] Commercially available oral rehydration solutions (ORS) for the treatment of diarrhea-associated dehydration comprise specific concentrations of sodium, potassium, and glucose to optimize the absorption of fluid from the gastrointestinal tract via the sodium-glucose co-transporter pump.[7] Conventionally prescribed ORS have a low-calorie content and children find it unpalatable.[89] However, in dehydration associated with non-diarrheal conditions like fever, there is an energy deficit too and hence additional energy is needed for such patients. Addition of glucose to the electrolyte solutions is expected to bring in better palatability, patient compliance, and fluid, electrolytes, and energy (FEE) management. The pre-packed ready-to-drink (RTD) electrolyte solutions are convenient to use and the manual errors at the time of reconstitution of powder electrolytes can be averted.[5] ORSL® Apple drink and ORSL Plus® Orange drink are fruit juice-based drinks with electrolytes (sodium, potassium, and chloride), vitamin C, and carbohydrates. In addition, the composition of ORSL Plus® also includes taurine, calcium, and magnesium. These food products have been classified as Electrolyte Drinks by the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India.[10]

In this study, we have evaluated the use of oral electrolytes and the recommendation pattern of RTD electrolytes (including ORSL®, ORSL Plus®, and ORSL Rehydrate®) in pediatric patients with non-diarrheal conditions. Further, the comparison of symptom resolution for the patients who were prescribed oral electrolytes against those who were not prescribed oral electrolytes for similar conditions was conducted.

METHODOLOGY

This was a real-world, retrospective analysis of electronic medical records (EMR)-based study to understand the usage and prescription pattern of oral electrolytes (ORSL®, ORSL Plus®, and ORSL Rehydrate®) in pediatric patients (<18 years) with non-diarrheal conditions. Further, the effectiveness of oral electrolytes was evaluated. For symptom resolution, patients who were prescribed oral electrolytes were compared to patients who were not prescribed oral electrolytes for similar non-diarrheal conditions. For this, patients who had a follow-up within 10 days of prescription were considered.

Patients with diarrhea, loose motions, and related terms in the EMRs or prescribed with systemic electrolyte therapy or cardiac failure or renal failure or liver ailments like ascites, liver cirrhosis, and acute pancreatitis were excluded from the study.

Anonymized and aggregated data of patients meeting the eligibility criteria from January 2017 to March 2023 was retrieved from HealthPlix EMR (https://healthplix.com/). This study was approved by the Royal Pune Independent Ethics Committee on November 2nd, 2023.

Statistical analysis

Patients prescribed oral electrolytes for different conditions and symptoms have been presented as number and frequency. Propensity score matching (PSM), a quasi-experimental method, was used to make the number of patients comparable on two arms—treated with oral electrolytes and not treated with oral electrolytes. Factors like age, sex, and indications (such as fever and RTI) were taken into consideration. The complaints field was evaluated to understand the improvement, if any. The P value and 95% confidence interval (CI) were calculated to understand the significance of the results. Missing data was not imputed in this study. These matched arms were further analyzed to evaluate symptom resolution.

RESULTS

Proportion of patients prescribed oral electrolytes for non-diarrheal illnesses

Of the total pediatric patients with non-diarrheal illnesses (n = 1,889,680), 2.96% of the patients were prescribed oral electrolytes. Approximately 31% of these recommended RTD formulations. Among RTD formulations studied, ORSL® was the most prescribed [Table 1].

Proportion of pediatric patients with non-diarrheal conditions for which oral rehydration formulations were recommended

The maximum number of prescriptions of oral electrolytes was seen for conditions like fever, followed by upper respiratory tract infection (URTI) and gastritis. The maximum proportion of prescriptions of oral rehydration formulations was observed for the patients diagnosed with dehydration, followed by dengue, and vomiting. These formulations were also prescribed for the resolution of conditions like gastritis, enteric fever, UTI, fever, malaria, and others.

Among dehydrated patients on oral electrolytes, 24% of the patients were prescribed RTD formulations of oral electrolytes. Of all the three variants of ORSL®, ORSL Plus® was observed to be the most advised. Of patients diagnosed with dengue, 23% were reported to have been prescribed oral electrolytes, and 37% were prescribed RTD formulations. ORSL Plus® emerged as the top prescription choice for patients with dengue. Of the patients diagnosed with vomiting, 20% of the patients were prescribed oral electrolytes. RTD formulations were recommended to 35% of these patients. ORSL® was prescribed to the maximum proportion of patients.

Of the patients with a diagnosis of fever, 11% of them were prescribed oral electrolytes and 36% of them had RTDs mentioned in their prescriptions. Among these patients on RTD formulations, ORSL Plus® was prescribed to 35% of the patients.

In case of patients with RTIs, 34% of the patients on oral electrolytes were prescribed RTD formulations. The prescription of oral electrolytes, RTD formulation, ORSL®, ORSL Plus®, and ORSL Rehydrate® for non-diarrheal conditions is available as Supplementary Material.

Proportion of pediatric patients with symptoms of non-diarrheal conditions for which oral rehydration formulations were recommended

The top symptoms reported by patients for which clinicians had prescribed oral electrolytes were body ache, vomiting, fatigue, nausea, and giddiness.

Among the patients with complaints of body ache, 44% of the ones on oral electrolytes were on RTD formulations. ORSL® was found to be the top prescription choice. Of the pediatric patients with complaints of vomiting, 16% had oral electrolytes as a part of their prescriptions and RTD formulations were recommended for 33% of them. ORSL® was the most prescribed. Among the patients with complaints of cold/cough and fever, ORSL was prescribed in 16% and 19%, respectively, while ORSL Plus® was prescribed in 13% and 15% and ORSL Rehydrate® was prescribed to 17% and 15% of the patients, respectively.

Supplementary Material is available for the proportion of patients prescribed oral electrolyte formulations for different symptoms.

The real-world data was also analyzed to understand the duration and daily recommendations of ORSL®, ORSL Plus®, and ORSL Rehydrate®. The duration was specified for 59.1% of the patients. Of these, the maximum proportion of patients were prescribed ORSL®, ORSL Plus®, and ORSL Rehydrate® for a duration of 1–3 days [Figure 1].

Symptom resolution

The number of patients in both groups (prescribed oral electrolytes and those who were not prescribed oral electrolytes) was made comparable using PSM. This statistical method constructs an artificial control group by matching each treated unit with a non-treated unit of similar characteristics. To make the arms comparable, age, gender, and indications were taken into consideration. This was done by comparing complaints at the baseline visit with complaints at the follow-up visit. The absence of a complaint at the follow-up visit, which was mentioned at the baseline visit, was considered a resolution. The 95% CI for the symptom resolution proportion was calculated. The percentage of patients showing symptom resolution in the two groups was compared using the Fisher’s exact test.

In case of RTI, the proportion of patients who got resolved was higher for the ones who were prescribed oral electrolytes, and the P value was significant. However, for patients with fever, resolution was higher for the group of patients who were not prescribed oral electrolytes as depicted in Figure 3. This could be due to the limited data availability on the resolution of symptoms and other treatments.

DISCUSSION

Recommendations of oral electrolytes to combat dehydration in non-diarrheal illnesses is a less studied topic. This is the first study that evaluated the use of oral electrolytes and RTD solutions in non-diarrheal conditions in pediatric patients. Impact of oral electrolytes on fever and RTI symptom recovery was also evaluated. Electrolyte abnormalities are generally observed in patients with dengue, with hyponatremia and hypokalemia being the most common ones.[11] Clinical consequences of vomiting generally include dehydration, electrolyte imbalances, and malnutrition.[12] From our data, it was observed that oral electrolytes were commonly prescribed for conditions like dehydration, fever, URTI, dengue, and vomiting. These were also prescribed for symptoms like body ache, vomiting, fatigue, nausea, and giddiness. Mild dehydration can also result in fatigue, lethargy, dryness of mouth and tongue, headache, dizziness, and light-headedness.[4] A study was also conducted by Ishikawa et al. to understand the effect of ORS on fatigue experienced during outdoor work in a hot environment. The team concluded that intake of adequate ORS during such environment and work conditions would help prevent heat stroke.[13] For effective intestinal absorption of an oral electrolyte, osmolality of the rehydration solution is one of the critical factors. A study was conducted by Redkar et al. to understand variations in osmolality of the ORS reconstituted by respondents or caregivers or mothers of children. It was observed that they reconstituted electrolyte solutions using powdered ORS as per their preferences. Instructions mentioned on the sachet for reconstitution were not taken into consideration. Hence, there was a wide variation in the osmolality of the prepared electrolyte solutions which may have direct impact on addressing the dehydration.[14] Oral FEE solutions in RTD format, packed in sterile environments, provide a fixed range of osmolarity and can serve as effective options to overcome FEE imbalances associated with non-diarrheal conditions like fever, dengue, malaria, UTIs, URTIs, nausea, vomiting which can result in imbalances in FEE.[515] FEE solutions are crucial for addressing the energy deficits in such conditions.[5] The data from the analysis suggests that there is a need to create more awareness around oral FEE solutions. Of the three products evaluated in this study, ORSL® was observed to be the preferred choice. However, this varied across different conditions and symptoms. ORSL Plus® was observed to be the preferred option for conditions like dehydration and dengue. A study by Reddy et al. reported that ORSL® variants had a positive effect on hydration and/or energy status of patients with fever and/or general weakness and patients who consume ORSL® felt energized or hydrated in 20 min post-consumption.[10]

On assessing the prescription pattern, it was observed that these products were majorly prescribed for 1–3 days with TID regimen. This suggests their use in acute conditions. Data on duration and frequency was not available for a significant proportion of patients.

The resolution of symptoms for patients diagnosed with RTI or fever and prescribed oral electrolytes was compared with the ones not prescribed oral electrolytes for these conditions. The proportion of patients with resolved RTI symptoms was higher in oral electrolyte arm. However, in the case of fever, this proportion was contrasting, and further analysis was done to understand the duration of resolution. This was better for the oral electrolyte group. This suggests the positive impact of adding oral electrolytes to primary treatment making the recovery faster. A prospective study reported that the majority of the physicians acknowledged a reduction in the duration of recovery with RTD FEE drinks.[10] Recommendation of fluid and electrolytes is expected to replenish the losses during fever and rapid breathing. Fluids also help in reducing the viscosity of mucus and easy expulsion.[16] Malve et al. reported that most of the physicians feel that addition of oral electrolytes to the first line therapy can speed up recovery with improved patient outcomes.[5]

This retrospective, observational, EMR-based analysis has data from a significant number of patients. However, patients’ journey was not tracked after the defined time-point to understand symptom resolution in case of patients who did not get resolved within 10 days of the prescription of oral electrolytes. In this retrospective study, already available data was used for analysis and the missing data was not imputed. Incomplete records and the retrospective nature of the analysis are inherent limitations of the database study like this. There is a scope to have a prospective clinical study to evaluate the impact of RTD oral electrolytes on pediatric patients with non-diarrheal conditions and its relevance for better patient outcomes.

CONCLUSION

Limited number of prescriptions record dehydration from pediatric non-diarrheal illnesses and oral electrolytes were prescribed to a very sparse number of children. There is a need to create awareness around the fact that the prescription of oral FEE solutions can help overcome the FEE deficits during the phase of acute illnesses. The prescription of these electrolytes along with the primary treatment can reduce the phase of illness and speed up the recovery of patients.

Low serum vitamin D levels are associated with neonatal indirect hyperbilirubinemia and prolonged phototherapy duration: a retrospective case–control study
Authors: Batuhan Berk Demir, İlhan Hazer, Ahmet Turkeli
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo investigate the association between serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D [25(OH)D] levels, indirect hyperbilirubinemia severity, and phototherapy duration in term neonates.MethodsThis retrospective case–control study included 79 term neonates admitted to a tertiary neonatal intensive care unit between March 2023 and March 2024. Thirty-nine neonates requiring phototherapy for indirect hyperbilirubinemia (case group) were compared with 40 healthy term neonates (control group). Clinical and biochemical parameters, including bilirubin, 25(OH)D, calcium, phosphorus, magnesium, and alkaline phosphatase levels, were evaluated. Correlation, receiver operating characteristic [ROC], and multivariable regression analyses were performed to assess the association between vitamin D status and phototherapy duration in indirect hyperbilirubinemia.ResultsSerum 25(OH)D levels were significantly lower in neonates requiring phototherapy, with 75% demonstrating vitamin D deficiency (< 20 ng/mL). Lower vitamin D levels were associated with prolonged phototherapy duration (r = − 0.337, p = 0.036). Although no significant linear correlation was observed between serum 25(OH)D concentration and baseline bilirubin levels, neonates with vitamin D concentrations < 20 ng/mL exhibited significantly higher bilirubin levels than those with concentrations ≥ 20 ng/mL. ROC analysis demonstrated moderate predictive performance for prolonged phototherapy duration (AUC = 0.78, 95% CI: 0.67–0.88). The optimal cutoff value was 17.2 ng/mL, yielding a sensitivity of 84% and specificity of 69%. Multivariable regression analysis identified serum vitamin D level and baseline bilirubin concentration as significant predictors of phototherapy duration.ConclusionLower serum 25(OH)D concentrations were associated with indirect hyperbilirubinemia and prolonged phototherapy duration in term neonates. Larger prospective studies are needed to confirm these findings and clarify their clinical significance.

Introduction

Indirect hyperbilirubinemia is among the most frequently encountered conditions in the early neonatal period, affecting nearly 60% of term infants [1–3]. Although the majority of cases are physiological and resolve spontaneously, a notable proportion of neonates require phototherapy, and in more severe or prolonged cases, complications such as bilirubin-induced neurologic dysfunction may develop [3–5]. For this reason, both the severity of hyperbilirubinemia and the duration of treatment remain clinically important considerations in neonatal care [4, 6].

Bilirubin metabolism is largely determined by hepatic uptake and conjugation, primarily mediated by uridine diphosphate [UGT1A1]. In neonates, the relative immaturity of this enzymatic system, together with increased bilirubin production and enhanced enterohepatic circulation, contributes to elevated serum bilirubin levels [5–7]. In this context, vitamin D has recently attracted attention as a potential modulator of bilirubin metabolism, extending beyond its traditional role in calcium homeostasis through its effects on hepatic enzyme activity, intestinal function, and oxidative stress pathways [6–8]. Materials and methods

This retrospective case–control study was conducted in the Neonatal Intensive Care Unit of a tertiary referral center between March 30, 2023, and March 30, 2024. The study was designed as a retrospective chart-review investigation based on archived hospital records. The study protocol was approved by the institutional ethics committee, and all procedures were carried out in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

A total of 79 term neonates with a gestational age between 37 and 42 weeks were included in the final analysis. The study population consisted of 39 neonates diagnosed with indirect hyperbilirubinemia requiring phototherapy (case group) and 40 healthy term neonates without clinically significant hyperbilirubinemia (control group). One patient from the initially identified case cohort was excluded because of incomplete clinical data.

Only neonates evaluated between 48 h and 10 days of life were eligible for inclusion in the study. Restricting enrollment to this predefined postnatal age window minimized age-related physiological variation in bilirubin metabolism and serum vitamin D concentrations. Neonates were excluded if they had any of the following conditions: prematurity [< 37 weeks], hemolytic disease [including ABO or Rh incompatibility and glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase deficiency], sepsis, congenital infections, hypothyroidism, metabolic disorders, conjugated hyperbilirubinemia, major congenital anomalies, or prior vitamin D supplementation [9–11].

Clinical and laboratory data were retrospectively obtained from hospital records. Demographic and perinatal variables included postnatal age at admission, sex, gestational age, birth weight, and mode of delivery [10–13]. Laboratory assessments comprised total and direct bilirubin, serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D [25(OH)D], calcium, phosphorus, magnesium, and alkaline phosphatase [ALP] levels [12, 13].

The study was designed to address two complementary objectives; comparison of serum 25(OH)D concentrations between case and control groups, and evaluation of the association between serum 25(OH)D concentrations and phototherapy duration among neonates requiring phototherapy respectively. Information regarding maternal vitamin D supplementation during pregnancy was extracted from hospital records when available and evaluated as a potential factor associated with neonatal vitamin D status.

Serum 25(OH)D concentrations were measured using an electrochemiluminescence immunoassay (ECLIA) with an inter-assay coefficient of variation of less than 8% [1–3]. Vitamin D status was categorized as follows: severe deficiency [< 12 ng/mL], deficiency [12–20 ng/mL], insufficiency [20–30 ng/mL], and sufficiency [≥ 30 ng/mL] [2–4].

The indication for phototherapy was determined according to standard neonatal bilirubin nomograms [3–5]. Phototherapy duration was defined as the total time (in hours) from initiation to discontinuation based on clinical and laboratory improvement [4–6].

Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). The normality of data distribution was assessed using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test [5–7]. Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation or median [interquartile range], while categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages [6–8].

Between-group comparisons were conducted using the independent samples t-test or Mann–Whitney U test for continuous variables, and the chi-square test for categorical variables [7, 8, 14]. Correlation analyses were performed using Pearson or Spearman tests, depending on data distribution [8, 14, 15].

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed to evaluate the predictive performance of serum 25(OH)D concentrations for prolonged phototherapy duration. Prolonged phototherapy was defined as a treatment duration > 24 h. The optimal cutoff value was determined using the Youden Index [14–16]. Multivariable linear regression analysis was performed to identify independent predictors of phototherapy duration. Variables considered during model construction included serum 25(OH)D concentration, baseline bilirubin level, gestational age, birth weight, sex, postnatal age, mode of delivery, and serum calcium concentration. Variables were selected based on biological plausibility, previous literature, and potential associations identified in univariate analyses. To minimize overfitting given the relatively small sample size, only clinically relevant variables were retained in the final model.

Regarding biochemical parameters, serum calcium levels were significantly lower in the phototherapy group compared to controls [8.97 ± 0.62 mg/dL vs. 9.55 ± 0.74 mg/dL, p = 0.005] [19–21]. In contrast, no statistically significant differences were observed between groups for phosphorus, magnesium, or alkaline phosphatase [ALP] levels [all p > 0.05].

Correlation analysis revealed a significant inverse relationship between serum 25(OH)D levels and phototherapy duration [r = –0.337, p = 0.036], indicating that lower vitamin D levels were associated with prolonged treatment duration. Stratified analysis further demonstrated that neonates with 25[OH]D levels < 10 ng/mL had a significantly longer mean phototherapy duration compared to those with levels ≥ 20 ng/mL [28.4 ± 12.1 h vs. 14.6 ± 7.9 h, p < 0.05].

A moderate positive correlation was observed between serum 25(OH)D and calcium levels [r = 0.440, p = 0.0059], suggesting a potential interaction between vitamin D status and calcium homeostasis. Consistently, neonates with vitamin D levels < 12 ng/mL had significantly lower mean calcium levels compared to those with levels ≥ 20 ng/mL [8.72 ± 0.51 mg/dL vs. 9.64 ± 0.63 mg/dL].

No significant linear correlation was observed between serum 25(OH)D concentration and baseline bilirubin level when both variables were analyzed as continuous measures (all p > 0.05).

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis demonstrated moderate predictive performance of serum 25(OH)D concentration for prolonged phototherapy duration (> 24 h), with an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.78 (95% CI: 0.67–0.88). The optimal cutoff value was identified as 17.2 ng/mL, yielding a sensitivity of 84% and specificity of 69%. Among the 39 neonates requiring phototherapy, 12 were classified as having prolonged phototherapy (> 24 h), whereas 27 were classified as having non-prolonged phototherapy (≤ 24 h).

Furthermore, neonates with vitamin D levels < 20 ng/mL exhibited significantly longer phototherapy duration [26.3 ± 10.9 h vs. 14.1 ± 7.8 h] and higher baseline bilirubin levels [16.9 ± 2.4 mg/dL vs. 14.8 ± 1.9 mg/dL] compared to those with levels ≥ 20 ng/mL [p < 0.05]. Although no significant continuous correlation was observed between serum 25(OH)D concentration and baseline bilirubin levels, categorical subgroup analysis demonstrated significantly higher bilirubin concentrations among neonates with vitamin D levels < 20 ng/mL.

Multivariable linear regression analysis identified serum vitamin D level (β = –0.32, p = 0.021) and baseline bilirubin level (β = 0.27, p = 0.044) as independent predictors of phototherapy duration. Other variables were not statistically significant in the model.

Discussion

This study demonstrated a consistent association between serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D [25(OH)D] levels and phototherapy duration in term neonates with indirect hyperbilirubinemia [5–7]. Lower 25(OH)D levels were significantly associated with prolonged phototherapy. These findings suggest that vitamin D status may be associated with disease severity and treatment response. This inverse relationship remained significant after multivariable adjustment; however, given the observational study design, causality cannot be inferred.

Beyond its classical role in calcium homeostasis, several biological mechanisms have been proposed to explain a potential relationship between vitamin D status and bilirubin metabolism. Experimental and clinical studies have suggested possible effects on hepatic glucuronidation pathways, enterohepatic circulation, and oxidative stress responses [15–19, 22, 23]. However, these mechanisms were not directly evaluated in the present study. Therefore, the observed findings should be interpreted as evidence of an association rather than proof of a causal relationship. Further mechanistic studies are needed to clarify whether vitamin D directly influences bilirubin metabolism in neonates.

An apparent discrepancy was observed between the absence of a significant continuous correlation between serum 25(OH)D concentration and baseline bilirubin level and the higher bilirubin levels identified in neonates with vitamin D concentrations < 20 ng/mL. This difference is likely attributable to the distinct statistical approaches used. While correlation analysis evaluates a linear relationship across the full range of values, categorical subgroup analyses may detect differences at clinically relevant threshold levels.

The present findings are largely consistent with previous studies. Sharma et al. reported that neonates with low vitamin D levels required earlier and more prolonged phototherapy, whereas Kumar et al. described conflicting results [19–21]. Similarly, vitamin D levels below 15 ng/mL have been associated with extended hyperbilirubinemia duration and delayed treatment response [20, 21, 24]. In contrast, Mutlu et al. did not observe a significant association between vitamin D levels and neonatal jaundice [21, 24, 25]. These discrepancies may be attributed to differences in study design, inclusion criteria, laboratory methods, and the lack of standardized phototherapy protocols across studies [9, 24, 25]. In our study, the use of a homogeneous cohort of term neonates and strict exclusion criteria likely enhanced internal validity and reduced confounding [9, 10, 25].

An additional finding of this study was the significant positive correlation between serum vitamin D and calcium levels. This observation aligns with the well-established role of vitamin D in calcium metabolism [9–11]. The lower calcium levels observed in the phototherapy group further suggest that vitamin D deficiency may be associated with broader metabolic alterations in neonates with hyperbilirubinemia. These findings raise the possibility that calcium homeostasis may also play a contributory role in disease severity and treatment response [10–12]. Given that vitamin D and calcium function within an integrated metabolic axis, future studies may further explore their combined relationship with neonatal hyperbilirubinemia [11–13].

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis demonstrated moderate predictive performance of serum 25(OH)D concentration for prolonged phototherapy duration (AUC = 0.78), with an optimal cutoff value of 17.2 ng/mL. However, the specificity remained modest (69%), indicating a potential risk of false-positive classification if this threshold is applied in routine clinical practice. Therefore, serum vitamin D concentration should not be considered a standalone predictive biomarker. Rather, it may represent one component of a broader clinical assessment strategy that requires further validation in larger prospective studies.

This study has several strengths. The inclusion of a homogeneous population of term neonates and the application of rigorous exclusion criteria enhanced internal validity. In addition, the use of advanced statistical methods, including ROC analysis and multivariable regression, allowed for a robust evaluation of the relationship between vitamin D levels and phototherapy duration [4–8].

However, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the retrospective design limits the ability to establish causal relationships. Second, the relatively small sample size and single-center setting may restrict the generalizability of the findings and may have limited statistical power for some analyses. Third, although maternal vitamin D supplementation during pregnancy was evaluated, maternal serum vitamin D concentrations, nutritional status, sunlight exposure, and seasonal variation were not available and may have influenced neonatal vitamin D concentrations. Fourth, potential confounding factors such as genetic polymorphisms (e.g., UGT1A1 and vitamin D receptor variants) were not assessed. Fifth, although serum 25(OH)D demonstrated moderate predictive performance for prolonged phototherapy duration (AUC = 0.78), the specificity remained modest (69%), which may result in false-positive classifications and limits its immediate applicability as a standalone clinical biomarker. Finally, serial measurements of vitamin D levels during and after phototherapy were not performed, which may have provided additional insights into temporal changes in vitamin D status.

Conclusion

In conclusion, lower serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D [25(OH)D] concentrations were associated with prolonged phototherapy duration in term neonates with indirect hyperbilirubinemia. ROC analysis demonstrated moderate predictive performance, although the clinical utility of the identified cutoff value requires further validation. Given the retrospective single-center design, these findings should be interpreted as observational associations rather than evidence of causality. Larger prospective multicenter studies are needed to confirm these findings.

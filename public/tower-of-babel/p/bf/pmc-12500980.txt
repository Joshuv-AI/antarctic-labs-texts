Research on the stability mechanism and control technique of surrounding rock in gob side entry driving
Authors: Dingchao Chen, Xiangyu Wang, Jiaxin Zhao, Jianbiao Bai, Yuan Chu, Qingcong Zhou, Yang Yu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn order to enhance coal resource recovery and improve the stress environment of roadway surrounding rock, the practice of gob-side entry driving (GED) has been applied in China. Despite its numerous advantages, the presence of relatively narrow coal pillars poses significant safety concerns, especially with regard to the fracture of the main roof, which can lead to pillar failure and roadway instability. This study took the 15107 tailgate in Wenzhuang Coal Mine as the research object, and carried out a series of studies. Firstly, based on the theoretical framework of the block beam theory for lateral roof fracture, the influence of different fracture positions of the main roof on the stability of the gob side roadway is analyzed. Subsequently, a numerical model using UDEC Trigon was established to analyze the impact of main roof fractures in four different scenarios on the stability of the roadway surrounding rock. The results indicated that when the fracture line is positioned directly above the edge of the gob, the stability of the roadway is optimal. Furthermore, a hydraulic fracturing (HF) roof-cutting technology, based on manual intervention, was proposed to actively sever the main roof and facilitate stress transfer. The field application of this technique yielded significant improvements, with a reduction in roadway deformation of over 30%.Keywords: Gob-side entry driving, Fracture position of main roof, Roadway stability, Roof cutting and pressure releasingSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering

Introduction

The GED technology has advantages such as reducing coal loss and improving coal recovery rate1,2. GED involves setting small coal pillars along the edge of the mined-out area after the adjacent working face has been mined out, and then proceeding with the roadway excavation3,4. Compared to traditional mining with wider coal pillars, GED reduces pillar width to 3–8 m, significantly improving coal recovery rates and reducing resource waste5,6. However, the narrower coal pillar width can lead to insufficient load-bearing capacity, resulting in pillar damage and failure, subsequently causing overall deformation and instability of the roadway7–10. Therefore, it is necessary to adopt measures such as roof cutting and pressure relief, strengthening support, and grouting reinforcement to improve the stability of the gob-side roadway11. At the same time, it is also very necessary to assess the state of the coal pillars and the stability of the roadway12,13.

The fracture structure of overlying strata in gob-side entry has a significant impact on roadway stability14,15. According to the key stratum theory, after the upper section of the working face is mined, the overlying strata in the gob collapse, and the main roof undergoes an “O-X” fracture, as depicted in Fig. 1a16,17. By profiling the fracture structure in Fig. 1a, a more directly influential arc-shaped triangular block structure affecting roadway surrounding rock can be obtained, as illustrated in Fig. 1b18. This structure comprises three key blocks, with block A anchored at one end in the deep solid coal, block B articulated at both ends with blocks A and C, and block C supported by fallen gangue.

Fracture morphology of main roof: (a) plan view; (b) schematic representation of spatial structure19.

Numerous scholars have conducted theoretical and analytical research on the mechanical structural models of gob-side entry. Among these, the main roof fracture mechanical model, represented by the theory of ultimate equilibrium, is the most widely applied20–22. However, this theory assumes that the lateral fracture location of the main roof is at the elastic–plastic boundary within the coal body, leading to some ambiguity. An alternative theory, the internal–external stress field theory (as shown in Fig. 2a) 23–25, suggests that the fracture location of the main roof occurs at the boundary between the internal stress field S1 (stress reduction area) and the external stress field S2 (stress increase area). Wang et al.26 based on the “voussoir beam” theory of the main roof, established a structural model of the roof under dynamic pressure (as shown in Fig. 2b), revealing the influence of direct roof thickness, hardness, and deformation on the stability of gob-side entry.

Theoretical mechanical structural models: (a) internal–external stress field theory; (b) “voussoir beam” theory.

Simultaneously, numerical simulation constitutes an essential approach for investigating the stability of roadway surrounding rock in underground mining engineering27–32. Jia et al.33 employed FLAC3D to simulate the stability of an overburden roadway, revealing significant asymmetric deformations within the roadway. Wu et al.34 established a UDEC Trigon model to analyze crack propagation and failure patterns in coal pillars of varying widths adjacent to the gob, finding that coal pillars with widths below 10 m are susceptible to through-going cracks. Additionally, Shi et al.35 employed UDEC to simulate the deformation and crack evolution mechanisms in gob-side entry under three scenarios: primary support, optimized support; and optimized support + roof-cutting. Their findings indicate that the first two scenarios result in high coal pillar damage and severe internal cracking, while the third scenario, with a coal pillar damage rate of only 38%, leads to improved roadway stability.

In this study, a UDEC Trigon model was developed to represent different scenarios of main roof fractures. These numerical simulations were employed to further validate the instability and failure patterns of roadways under the influence of main roof fractures. Finally, considering the site-specific geological conditions, a rock control measure involving hydraulic fracturing was proposed. Field results demonstrate that hydraulic fracturing effectively severs the hard main roof, contributing to the stability of the overhanging roadway.

Engineering case study

Overview of the engineering case

The Wenzhuang Coal Mine is located in Changzhi City, China. The currently mined 15# coal seam is situated at an approximate depth of 250 m, with an average thickness of 4.8 m. The geological structure is relatively simple, with occasional interbedded gangue layers of 1–2 seams. The coal seam’s stratigraphy and thickness remain stable. The 15107 panel is positioned within the 15# coal seam and advances along the coal seam floor, with the 15106 gob situated to the west. There is a 6-m-wide coal pillar between the 15107 tailgate and the 15106 gob. The layout of the roadway is illustrated in Fig. 3a,b. Establishment and correction of numerical model

Numerical simulation establishment

The UDEC Trigon model, proposed by Gao et al.38, is known for its accurate representation of rock fracture and failure behavior, making it more suitable for simulating real underground conditions39. In the Trigon model, rock masses are considered as assemblies of multiple triangular blocks interconnected through contact surfaces40. These triangular blocks are treated as elastic materials and are subdivided into regions of triangular finite differences where no failure occurs. Failure in coal and rock masses can only occur along joint surfaces through shear or tensile failure. When the forces applied to the contact surfaces exceed the tensile or shear strength of the coal and rock masses, these contacts will fracture41. A numerical model using UDEC was constructed based on the geological conditions of the 15107 panel at Wenzhuang Coal Mine. The model dimensions are as follows: length × width = 140 × 64 m. The excavation section of the 15107 tailgate measures 4.7 × 3.6 m, as shown in Fig. 7.

UDEC numerical model.

To improve computational efficiency while maintaining simulation accuracy, the 15107 tailgate and the surrounding coal seams were divided into triangular blocks with an average length of 0.3 m (Trigon blocks). The roof and floor rocks of the 15107 tailgate were divided into Trigon blocks with an average length of 0.7 m. The model’s bottom and sides were fixed, and an equivalent load of 5.24 MPa was applied to the top to simulate the overlying strata. The blocks in the model were set as elastic materials, and the joint fractures were modeled using the Mohr–Coulomb slip criterion. Based on the field stress distribution characteristics, the vertical principal stress is =5.5 MPa, the minimum horizontal principal stress is =5.0 MPa, and the maximum horizontal principal stress is =6.5 MPa. Simulation setup

As discussed in chapter 2, considering the impact of the main roof fracture position on the stability of the roadway, the UDEC Trigon model was analyzed in four different scenarios. The distances between the fracture line and the edge of the gob were set at 12 m, 8 m, 4 m, and 0 m, as illustrated in Fig. 9.

Results analysis of numerical simulation

Overall results

To ensure consistency in the model, the same number of steps were applied to all four scenarios to obtain the roof fracture conditions under different circumstances, as shown in Fig. 10.

Roof failure conditions under different scenarios: (a) 12 m; (b) 8 m; (c) 4 m; (d) 0 m.

After the completion of mining at the 15106 panel, a gob was formed, and block C collapsed above it. Block C was supported by the collapsed immediate roof on the left side and hinged with block B on the right side. Block B hinged with both blocks A and C and rotated and sank at the boundary with block A, forming an arched triangular block structure above the coal pillar. Block A remained intact, hinged with block B on one side, and fixed in the deep part of the solid coal. Overall, the numerical model largely replicated the actual roof fracture conditions in the roadway.

Roadway deformation

The horizontal displacement cloud maps for the four scenarios are shown in Fig. 11a, while the vertical displacement cloud maps are shown in Fig. 11b. The distribution pattern of the surrounding rock on the roadway surface follows the order of “scenario 1 > scenario 2 > scenario 3 > scenario 4”, indicating a direct connection between the fracture location of the main roof and the deformation of the roadway.

The overall displacement vector map of the roadway is presented in Fig. 11c. Within the coal pillar, the displacement vectors form an inverted “V” shape, with displacement vectors descending from the immediate roof and dispersing to the sides in the middle of the coal pillar. In scenarios 1 to 3, the displacement vectors are mainly concentrated on the roof and the sides of the coal pillar. However, in scenario 4, the displacement vectors around the roadway are significantly reduced, and their distribution is more uniform.

Monitoring points were placed in the roof, coal pillar rib, and solid coal rib to measure the deformation of the roadway in the four scenarios. The results are shown in Fig. 12.

Roadway deformation.

When the main roof fracture line is 12 m away from the edge of the gob, the deformation is particularly severe in the coal pillar rib, reaching 604 mm, followed by roof subsidence at 441 mm, and deformation of solid coal rib at 317 mm.

When the main roof fracture line is 8 m away from the edge of the gob, the roadway deformation significantly decreases, with reductions of 22.0%, 24.5%, and 13.6% in the deformation of the roof, coal pillar rib, and solid coal rib, respectively.

When the main roof fracture line is 4 m away from the edge of the gob, with the fracture location above the coal pillar, the deformation of the roof and solid coal rib is nearly identical, but the deformation of the coal pillar rib is still relatively large.

When the main roof fracture line is directly above the edge of the gob, the deformation of the roof and ribs of the roadway is relatively uniform, and the overall deformation of the roadway is lower, with a maximum deformation of only 280 mm. This situation represents the most ideal outcome.

Surrounding rock cracks

The failure modes of the roadway mainly include tension failure and shear failure, with tension failure being the primary cause of roadway deformation and failure. As shown in Fig. 13, the tension cracks in the surrounding rock in the simulation are represented in red, while the coal body is shown in black.

Distribution of tension cracks: (a) 12 m; (b) 8 m; (c) 4 m; (d) 0 m.

When the main roof fracture line is 12 m away from the edge of the gob, numerous and long tension cracks appear in the surrounding rock around the roadway, and cracks extend through the coal pillar. As the distance between the main roof fracture position and the edge of the gob decreases, both the length and number of tension cracks decrease. When the main roof fracture is directly above the edge of the gob, the number of cracks in the roof and ribs of the roadway decreases significantly. Although cracks still penetrate through the middle of the coal pillar, the cracks zones on both sides of the coal pillar change from strip-shaped to wedge-shaped, and the number of cracks at the penetration point is very small.

Industrial experiment

Design of HF roof cutting

Based on the theoretical analysis and numerical simulation results, it has been confirmed that the location of the main roof fracture plays a crucial role in the stability of the surrounding rock in gob-side entry. In mining operations, the treatment of hard roof mainly involves two methods: blasting roof cutting and HF roof cutting. Compared to blasting, HF roof cutting offers advantages in terms of efficiency, safety, and cost-effectiveness, making it increasingly popular for applications52,53.

As shown in Fig. 14, to ensure the ideal fracture location of the main roof, two sets of HF boreholes were designed from the 15107 tailgate towards the gob side. One set of boreholes is 20 m in length with a vertical inclination angle of 60°, primarily targeting the upper and middle parts of the main roof for fracturing. The other set of boreholes is 16 m in length with a vertical inclination angle of 50°, primarily targeting the middle and lower parts of the main roof for fracturing.

Layout of HF boreholes.

Results of roof cutting and pressure releasing

To monitor the results of roof cutting and stress relief, measurement stations were set up in both the fracturing and non-fracturing sections to observe the deformation of the roadway roof, coal pillar rib, and solid coal rib. Specifically, station 1 and station 2 were located in the non-fracturing section, while station 3 and station 4 were located in the fracturing section. The monitoring results are shown in Fig. 15.

Deformation of the roadway on site.

In the non-fracturing section, the overall deformation of the entry is significant, with an average deformation exceeding 400 mm. At station 1, the deformation of coal pillar side even reached 476 mm. In contrast, in the fracturing section, the surrounding rock of the roadway was effectively controlled. Compared to station 1 and station 2, the deformation at station 3 and station 4 was significantly reduced, with a reduction exceeding 30%.

Conclusions

This paper employs numerical simulation to reveal the influence of the main roof fracturing position on roadway stability. The results are applied to the field, validating the rationality of the research findings, leading to the following conclusions:

An UDEC Trigon numerical model is established, and simulation results indicate that when the fracture line is located above the solid coal, internal fractures within the coal pillar are fully connected and densely distributed, with the maximum deformation of the roadway exceeding 600 mm. As the main roof fracturing length decreases, both the number of internal coal pillar fractures and roadway deformation exhibit a decreasing trend. This reconfirms the critical role of the fracture position of key block B in controlling roadway surrounding rock.

An UDEC Trigon numerical model is established, and simulation results indicate that when the fracture line is located above the solid coal, internal fractures within the coal pillar are fully connected and densely distributed, with the maximum deformation of the roadway exceeding 600 mm. As the main roof fracturing length decreases, both the number of internal coal pillar fractures and roadway deformation exhibit a decreasing trend. This reconfirms the critical role of the fracture position of key block B in controlling roadway surrounding rock.

When the fracture line is located inside the coal pillar (including above the solid coal, above the roadway, and above the coal pillar), the main roof undergoes lateral rotation, which inevitably exerts pressure on both the roadway and the coal pillar, threatening the stability of the roadway. When the fracture line is located outside the coal pillar (above the gob), the lateral roof forms a “”-shaped distribution, and the roadway is only subjected to the self-weight of the roof, resulting in a favorable stress environment for the surrounding rock.

Based on the theoretical analysis and numerical simulation results, a HF measure for the roof of the 15107 tailgate in WenZhuang coal mine is proposed. By actively cutting off the main roof through manual intervention, the stress environment of the roadway surrounding rock is effectively alleviated. Compared to the non-fracturing section, roadway deformation is reduced by more than 30%, demonstrating the significant impact of the main roof fracturing position on roadway stability.

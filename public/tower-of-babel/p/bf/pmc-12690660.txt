PDNO-04 The impact of a nurse Navigator in Continuity and Coordination of Care in Pediatric Brain Tumor Management: A Case Study of the UCI-CURE Partnership
Authors: Beatrice Rukundo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundUganda cancer institute (UCI) and Cure Children’s hospital (CCHU) were working independently with unstructured referrals for surgical cases to CURE and post-surgical care at UCI. In November 2023, a formal collaboration was established, and a multidisciplinary team was formed (Peadiatric Oncologist, Fellow, Nurse, Surgeon and A medical officer) to improve patient tracking and navigation, timely referrals, and continuity of care.ObjectivesThis study evaluates the impact of a nurse navigator on continuity of care, and inter-institutional coordination for children with brain tumors.MethodsA retrospective review compared pediatric brain tumor admissions at UCI before (2017–2021) and after (November 2023–November 2024) the collaboration. The intervention included structured referrals, real-time communication between designated contact persons at both institutions, and formalized patient contact-sharing. This system enhanced patient tracking and ensured timely follow-up for post-surgical oncological management at UCI.ResultsBefore the collaboration, the average annual admissions of pediatric brain tumor patients at UCI were 7 (range: 3–14). Following the introduction of the institutional navigation system in November 2023, the number of admissions in one year increased to 56. Eleven children received adjuvant treatment timely. Four children received transport support from social worker department and Uganda child cancer foundation through navigation collaboration. Nine children were linked to the nutritionist for rehabilitation. Real-time contact sharing and designated institutional contacts improved referral tracking, reduced delays in care transitions, and enhanced patient follow-up. More patients arrived at UCI on time for further treatment, and referral tracking improved, minimizing loss to follow-up.ConclusionInstitutional navigation strategies, including structured referrals, real-time contact-sharing, and improved patient tracking, have significantly enhanced the continuity and coordination of care for pediatric brain tumor patients. Future studies should assess the long-term clinical impact of such collaborations on survival and treatment outcomes.

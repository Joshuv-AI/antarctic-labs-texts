Long-Term Effectiveness and Safety of Bimekizumab in Patients with Moderate to Severe Hidradenitis Suppurativa Under Real Clinical Practice Conditions: The Importance of Combined Treatment in Hurley III Patients and Potential Factors Associated with Complete Response
Authors: Miguel Mansilla-Polo, Carmen García-Moronta, Pablo Fernández-Crehuet, Patricia Garbayo-Salmons, Martí Pons-Benavent, Lucía Aguilar-González, David Jiménez-Gallo, Rebeca Alcalá-García, Gemma Martín-Ezquerra, Antonio Martorell, Laura Mahiques-Santos, Fernando Alfageme-Roldán, Begoña Escutia-Muñoz, Antonio Sahuquillo-Torralba, Rafael Fayos-Gregori, Alberto Soto-Moreno, Irene Sánchez-Gutiérrez, Rafael Botella-Estrada, Eva Vilarrasa, Alejandro Molina-Leyva, the Spanish Hidradenitis Suppurativa Group (GEHS)
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
IntroductionHidradenitis suppurativa (HS) is a debilitating, chronic inflammatory skin disease that severely impacts patients’ quality of life. Bimekizumab, the first dual inhibitor of interleukin (IL)-17A and IL-17F, has shown efficacy in phase 3 clinical trials and is currently approved for moderate-to-severe HS. However, long-term data in real-world clinical settings are lacking.MethodsThis was a multicenter, retrospective observational study of 78 adult patients with moderate-to-severe HS treated with bimekizumab. Outcomes included International Hidradenitis Suppurativa Severity Score System (IHS4), IHS4-55/75/90/100 response, pain, flare frequency, and safety through week 48.ResultsThe mean age was 48.5 years, 60.3% (47/78) were classified as Hurley stage III. At week 24, results for IHS4-55, IHS4-75, and IHS4-90 were 54.7% (29/53), 41.5% (22/53), and 3.8% (2/53), respectively. At week 24, results for IHS4-55, IHS4-75, and IHS4-90 were 67.9% (53/78), 30.7% (24/78), and2.5% (2/78), respectively. At week 48, 56 patients were included, of whom 82.1% (46/56) reached an IHS4-55 response, 62.5% (35/56) an IHS4-75, 32,1% (18/56) an IHS4-90, and 23.2% (13/56) a complete response (IHS4-100). Patients unavailable for analysis had either not yet reached follow-up, 72.7% (16/22), or had discontinued treatment, 27.2% (6/22). Combination therapy in any form was more frequent in Hurley III patients, who had similar efficacy compared to Hurley II patients in terms of IHS4-55 at week 48. Adverse events occurred in 43.6% (34/78) of patients, mostly mild to moderate fungal infections (26.9%, 21/78) and eczematous reactions (9%, 6/78). During follow-up, eight patients (10.3%) discontinued treatment, primarily as a result of lack of efficacy (6.4%, 5/78).ConclusionBimekizumab shows sustained clinical effectiveness and a manageable safety profile for moderate-to-severe HS in real-world practice up to 48 weeks. In our setting, the combination of medical and surgical treatment is frequently used and may contribute to improved effectiveness, particularly in patients with extensive structural damage.

Introduction

Hidradenitis suppurativa (HS), also known as acne inversa, is a chronic inflammatory skin disease that primarily affects hair follicles located at skin folds, such as the axilla, groin, genital, and perianal region. Clinically, HS presents as painful nodules, deep-seated abscesses, and draining tunnels, which can lead to irreversible tissue remodeling and scarring [1, 2]. The disease burden extends far beyond the skin, causing profound physical discomfort, social stigmatization, and psychological distress [3]. HS typically begins in the second or third decade of life and is more prevalent in female individuals (female-to-male ratio ~ 3:1). Although precise epidemiological estimates vary, the reported prevalence ranges from 0.5% to 1.2% in Western populations [1].

The pathogenesis of HS is multifactorial and not yet fully elucidated. It is thought that a key initiating event is follicular hyperkeratosis, which leads to occlusion of the pilosebaceous apocrine unit. This is followed by follicular rupture and the release of keratin and microbial antigens into the dermis, which triggers a sustained innate and adaptive immune response [4, 5]. A pivotal immunological driver of HS is the overexpression of pro-inflammatory cytokines, particularly the interleukin (IL)−17 family [6]. IL-17A and IL-17F, produced by Th17 cells and other immune cell types, act synergistically to recruit neutrophils, induce keratinocyte activation, and increase the production of antimicrobial peptides and other inflammatory mediators, thus perpetuating the chronic inflammatory process [6, 7].

The central role of IL-17A and IL-17F in HS makes them attractive therapeutic targets. Bimekizumab is a humanized monoclonal IgG1 antibody that can neutralize selectively both IL-17A and IL-17F simultaneously [8]. This dual inhibition may provide more extensive and potent suppression of IL-17-mediated inflammation than IL-17A inhibition alone. Bimekizumab is currently approved for the treatment of moderate to severe plaque psoriasis, psoriatic arthritis, axial spondylarthritis, and HS [9]. On the other hand, secukinumab, recently approved by the European Medicines Agency (EMA) for HS, and also indicated for plaque psoriasis, psoriatic arthritis, axial spondyloarthritis, and juvenile idiopathic arthritis, exclusively targets IL-17A [10]. The approval of these agents represents a significant advancement in the treatment of moderate-to-severe HS, which until recently was largely limited to tumor necrosis factor alpha (TNFα) inhibition using agents such as adalimumab or infliximab [11].

In phase 3 clinical trials BE HEARD I and II, bimekizumab demonstrated statistically significant and clinically meaningful reductions in abscess and nodule count, alongside improvements in quality of life and a favorable safety profile [12]. While these trials provide robust evidence of its efficacy in the short to medium term, long-term real-world data remain scarce [13, 14]. Real-life data are especially relevant in HS, as it is common to combine medical and surgical treatment to synergistically improve effectiveness or to manage flares. In routine practice, a recent study showed that initiating adalimumab with clindamycin and rifampicin resulted in greater clinical efficacy than adalimumab monotherapy [15], and a clinical trial demonstrated improved outcomes when adalimumab was combined with surgery compared to monotherapy [16]. Moreover, patients in real-world settings often differ substantially from those included in clinical trials: they typically do not undergo washout periods, frequently present with multiple comorbidities, and may have more complex disease [12]. These differences further emphasize the importance of real-world evidence to inform treatment strategies in daily clinical practice.

This study aims to evaluate the mid- and long-term effectiveness and safety of bimekizumab in patients with HS in a real-world clinical practice.

Methods

Study Design and Patients

We conducted a multicenter, retrospective, observational study of adult patients diagnosed with moderate-to-severe HS and treated with bimekizumab in routine clinical practice at ten Spanish hospitals. Data were collected between December 2022 and April 2025. Patients were monitored and treated in accordance with local standards of care, institutional protocols, and updated clinical practice guidelines [17].

Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

Eligible participants met the following criteria: (a) a diagnosis of HS by a dermatologist; (b) age ≥ 18 years; (c) moderate-to-severe disease, as defined by an International Hidradenitis Suppurativa Severity Score System (IHS4) of at least 4 at baseline; (d) the presence of any number of abscesses, inflammatory nodules, and/or draining tunnels at baseline; (e) an inadequate response to, intolerance of, or contraindication to at least one course of systemic oral antibiotics in the past; (f) a previous inadequate response to, intolerance of, or contraindication to adalimumab following the Spanish therapeutic positioning report [18]; and (g) the initiation of bimekizumab for HS treatment. Exclusion criteria included: (a) age < 18 years; and (b) medical conditions contraindicating bimekizumab, including pregnancy, lactation, or known hypersensitivity to the drug.

Variables of Interest

Outcomes of Interest

The effectiveness of the treatment was assessed using observed data. HS disease activity was measured using the IHS4 score, categorized as mild (IHS4 0–3), moderate (IHS4 4–10), or severe (IHS4 ≥ 11) [19], IHS-55 score (improvement in IHS-4 > 55%) [20], and the frequency of monthly flare-ups, as reported by the patients themselves. Pain intensity was recorded using the Visual Analog Scale (VAS; 0–10). Assessments were performed at baseline and at weeks 16, 24, and 48.

The primary outcomes included changes in mean IHS4 scores, categorized changes in IHS4 severity (mild, moderate, or severe), and the achievement of IHS4 reduction rates of 55%, 75%, 90%, and 100%. Secondary outcomes included changes in VAS pain scores, monthly flare frequency, as reported by the patients and the number of draining tunnels. The Hidradenitis Suppurativa Clinical Response (HiSCR) was not used since it was not calculable in 28.20% (22/78) of the patients as a result of an abscess nodule count (AN count) lower than 3, which is common under real-world settings [17, 21].

Other Variables of Interest

Demographic and clinical data collected included age, sex, body mass index (BMI), smoking status, age at disease onset, duration of disease prior to bimekizumab initiation, comorbidities (including psoriasis and psoriatic arthritis), and number of affected anatomical areas. Disease severity was also classified by Hurley stage. We characterized the disease phenotype using the Martorell classification (inflammatory, follicular, or mixed) [22] and the Molina-Leyva anatomical classification (progressor, atypical, upper, or lower) [23]. The baseline disease speed of progression [24] was calculated as IHS4/disease duration (years). Previous treatment exposure was also recorded, including the number of systemic immunomodulators used and prior adalimumab and secukinumab use.

Ethics

This study was approved by the Research Ethics Committee at the Hospital Universitario y Politécnico La Fe (Valencia, Spain) and was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki on the ethical principles of medical research involving human subjects. Patients’ names, initials, or hospital numbers were not used.

Statistics

Descriptive statistics were used to summarize the baseline characteristics. Categorical variables were presented as absolute and relative frequencies, while continuous variables were presented as means with standard deviations (SD). The Shapiro–Wilk test was used to assess normality. Comparisons between groups were performed using the chi-squared test or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables, and the Student’s t test or Wilcoxon–Mann–Whitney test for continuous variables, as appropriate. Simple linear regression analysis was employed to identify factors associated with continuous outcomes. Multivariate analysis of variance (MANOVA) was used for comparison between measures over time. Significance is set at p < 0.05 for all two-tailed tests. Multivariate binomial logistic regression was employed when necessary to explore predictors of categorical treatment response, IHS4-55 and IHS4-100 achievement at week 48. The β coefficient and its SD were reported. Significance was set at p < 0.05 for all two-tailed tests. All statistical analyses were conducted using JMP version 9.0.1 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA).

At baseline, the mean abscesses and nodules (AN) count was 6.2 (6.2), with an average of 2.1 abscesses (2.5) and 4.0 nodules (2.7). The mean monthly flare frequency was 2.0 (1.0) and the mean pain score on the VAS was 7.4 (2.1).

In terms of disease severity, 60.3% (47/78) of patients were classified as Hurley stage III, 39.7% (31/78) as stage II, with a mean of 4.8 (1.5) affected anatomical areas. On the basis of the clinical phenotype (Martorell classification), 64.1% (50/78) of patients had the inflammatory subtype, 10.3% (8/78) had the follicular subtype, and 25.6% (20/78) presented with a mixed form. According to the anatomical phenotype (Molina-Leyva classification), 82.1% (64/78) were classified as progressor forms, 15.4% (12/78) as lower forms, and 2.6% (2/78) as upper forms.

A total of 91.0% (71/78) of patients had received prior adalimumab therapy, and 43.6% (34/78) had previously been treated with secukinumab. The mean (SD) number of prior systemic immunomodulators used was 1.8 (1.4).

Most of the patients, 66.7% (52/78), received the approved dosing regimen for HS, consisting of bimekizumab 320 mg (administered as two subcutaneous injections of 160 mg or one injection of 320 mg) every 2 weeks until week 16, followed by 320 mg every 4 weeks thereafter. The remaining patients received psoriasis dosing, 19.2% (15/78); 320 mg every 4 weeks, 2.6% (2/78); and 320 mg every 2 weeks, 11.5% (9/78).

At week 16, 46.2% (36/78) of patients achieved an IHS4-55 response,24.3% (19/78) achieved an IHS4-75 response, 15.4% (12/78) an IHS4-90 response, and 9.0% (7/78) an IHS4-100 response (Fig. 1a). At this timepoint, 24.4% (19/78) of patients received concomitant medical treatment and 17.9% (14/78) underwent surgical procedures. At week 24, 67.9% (53/78) of patients reached an IHS4-55, 30.7% (24/78) an IHS4-75, and 2.5% (2/78) an IHS4-90 response. No patients reached a complete response (IHS4-100). At this visit, 15.4% (12/78) received concomitant medical treatment and 17.9% (14/78) underwent surgery. At week 48, 82.1% (46/56) of patients achieved an IHS4-55, 62.5% (35/56) an IHS4-75, 32,1% (18/56) an IHS4-90, and 23.2% (13/56) an IHS4-100 response. Combined treatment was administered in 14.3% (8/56) as medical therapy and in 16.1% (9/56) as surgical intervention. Overall, combined treatment was performed in 38.9% (113/290) of visits.

The mean IHS4 score decreased progressively throughout the follow-up period. The mean (SD) baseline score was 24.2 (13.4), dropping to 14.9 (10.1) at week 16, 11.4 (8.4) at week 24, and 7.5 (6.3) at week 48. This reduction was statistically significant (p < 0.001).

Categorical Change in IHS4 Disease Severity

A categorical shift was observed in the distribution of disease severity (mild [IHS4 0–3], moderate [4–10] and severe [≥ 11]) over time (Fig. 1b). At baseline, 88.5% (69/78) of patients presented with severe disease. By week 16, this proportion had fallen to 47.4% (37/78), and by week 48, only 25.0% (10/39) remained in the severe category, while 41.1% (16/39) had achieved a mild level of disease activity. Among patients in the mild category, complete clinical remission (IHS4 = 0) was recorded in 11.5% (9/78) at week 16 and 23.2% (13/56) at week 48. No patients reached IHS4 = 0 at week 24. This analysis allowed for a clear visualization of how patients shifted between severity categories over time according to IHS4 thresholds.

Number of Draining Tunnels

The number of draining tunnels also decreased progressively over time: from a mean (SD) of 3.3 (2.3) at baseline to 1.9 (1.9) at week 16, 1.4 (1.8) at week 24, and 1.0 (1.7) at week 48. This reduction was statistically significant (p < 0.001) (Fig. 2b).

Monthly Flares

The frequency of flares per month decreased from a mean (SD) of 2.0 (0.6) at baseline to 0.8 (0.4) at week 16, 0.6 (0.3) at week 24 and 0.5 (0.3) at week 48. This reduction was statistically significant (p < 0.001) (Fig. 2c).

A significant inverse correlation was observed between the number of affected anatomical areas and the likelihood of achieving IHS4-55 response (β = − 0.485, SD 0.243; p = 0.046), indicating lower response rates in patients with more extensive disease involvement.

Other factors, including Hurley stage II vs. III 80.95% (17/56) vs. 82.86% (29/56) (p = 0.85), prior exposure to systemic immunomodulators (β = − 0.441, SD 0.271; p = 0.1038), previous treatment with secukinumab (28.6% vs. 11.4%; p = 0.1517), baseline IHS4 score (β = − 0.0019, SD 0.026; p = 0.9389), history of systemic antibiotic therapy (23.1% vs. 16.3%; p = 0.5749), and prior surgical interventions (26.3% vs. 13.5%; p = 0.2363), were not significantly associated with treatment response.

An exploratory analysis was performed to identify potential factors associated with IHS4-100 achievers (super responders) at week 48. Univariate analysis included all the variables shown in Table 2, showing a significant inverse association with baseline IHS4, speed of progression, and progressor phenotype vs. primary forms (upper, lower, atypical). A binomial multivariate logistic regression model was performed showing that (data shown as β coefficient and SD) a lower speed of progression − 2.49 (1.15) p = 0.002 and the presence of a primary phenotype (upper, lower, or atypical vs. progressor) 0.82 (0.45) p = 0.059 were associated with achieving IHS4-100, with independence from baseline IHS4 value p = 0.77.

Hurley stage III is a well-established factor related to lower response to biologic drugs [12, 25]. No differences in effectiveness were observed between Hurley stage II and III in our cohort. Consequently, we performed exploratory sub-analysis to explore if the therapeutic approach was different according to this clinical feature. Analysis revealed that Hurley stage III was associated with more frequent use of cooling or combined treatment at baseline, and more use of combined antibiotic and surgical treatment at any time during follow-up (Table 3, Fig. 3). Specifically, systemic antibiotics were administered at any time in 31.9%% (15/47) of Hurley III patients vs. 12.9% (4/31) in Hurley II (p = 0.0474), and systemic corticosteroids in 12.77% (6/47) vs. 0% (0/31), respectively (p = 0.0418). Safety and Treatment Discontinuation

During the follow-up period, a total of 34 adverse events were reported among the 78 patients (34/78, 43.6%). The most common were mucocutaneous candidiasis, 26.9% (21/78), with oral candidiasis (11/78, 14.1%) being the most frequent presentation; followed by eczematous reactions, 8.9% (7/78), with eyelid eczema being the most common presentation, 57.1% (4/7) (Fig. 4). Three patients (3.8%) presented psoriasiform reactions. A total of eight patients (8/78, 10.3%) discontinued treatment, mainly because of a lack of efficacy (5/78, 6.4%), with discontinuations occurring at weeks 24, 34, and 48. Discussion

This multicenter, real-world study demonstrates that bimekizumab is an effective and well-tolerated treatment for moderate-to-severe HS, as evidenced by improvements observed over the 48-week follow-up period. Significant and sustained improvements were observed in objective disease activity measures (IHS4 score, abscess and nodule count, draining tunnels) and patient-reported outcomes (pain, flare frequency), alongside a good safety profile. By week 48, 7 out 10 of patients had achieved an IHS4-55 response, with 50% reaching an IHS4-90 response. This suggests that bimekizumab provides clinically meaningful and durable disease control in routine clinical practice.

Bimekizumab is the first and only dual selective inhibitor of both IL-17A and IL-17F approved for the treatment of HS. In phase 3 trials (BE HEARD I and II), bimekizumab demonstrated rapid and significant improvements in abscess and nodule counts, pain, and quality of life metrics, with efficacy maintained over time [12]. Our real-world findings support and extend these observations, underscoring the drug’s effectiveness in unselected patient populations. Notably, our cohort had a greater disease burden than clinical trial populations, with over 60% classified as Hurley stage III (44% in BE HEARD trials), and an average disease duration of 22 years prior to bimekizumab initiation. Furthermore, 91.0% of patients had previously been exposed to adalimumab and almost half of the patients to secukinumab, another IL-17 inhibitor, indicating a population with significant treatment refractoriness. Despite these challenging characteristics, the magnitude and consistency of the observed response were remarkable.

These findings are consistent with those of two recent real-world studies that evaluated bimekizumab for HS. In a retrospective Spanish cohort of 40 patients, significant improvements in IHS4, pain, and DLQI were observed in the short term (week 16), with no discontinuations reported and only mild candidiasis [13]. Another series of 15 patients with a predominance of draining tunnels showed notable IHS4 reductions and confirmed treatment efficacy despite only 20% meeting randomized clinical trials eligibility, highlighting the limitations of trial generalizability [14]. Compared to both these studies, our cohort provides longer follow-up (48 weeks), greater sample size, and expands the understanding of durability of response.

One of the most striking findings of this study is the extent to which a response was achieved across multiple disease domains, including those that are usually more difficult to treat, such as draining tunnels and frequency of flare-ups. The reduction in IHS4 score from 24.2 to 7.5 over 48 weeks, together with the improvement in pain and monthly flare-ups, supports and strengthens the potential for deep clinical remission with dual IL-17A/F blockade. The dual inhibition of IL-17A and IL-17F with bimekizumab may offer more robust anti-inflammatory activity, particularly in chronic or refractory lesions. This is supported by the immunopathological observation that, although IL-17F is less potent than IL-17A, it is more abundant in chronic HS lesions and its inhibition may contribute to improved tunnel resolution [26–28].

Importantly, our study reflects the heterogeneity of real-world HS, including patients with varying disease phenotypes, anatomical involvement, comorbidities, and prior treatment exposure. The lack of significant predictors of IHS4-55 response, except for a negative correlation with the number of affected anatomical regions, suggests that the effectiveness of bimekizumab may be consistent across different clinical scenarios. This observation strengthens the external validity of our findings and supports bimekizumab as a versatile therapeutic option. However, it also highlights the complex and multifactorial nature of HS, emphasizing the need for individualized treatment approaches in clinical practice. We have also explored potential factors associated with IHS4-100 or complete response at week 48. A lower speed of disease progression (current IHS4/years of disease evolution), as defined by Vilarrasa et al. [24], and the presence of a primary form of HS (upper, lower, or atypical involvement) rather than a progressors form as defined by Muñoz-Barba et al. [23] are concepts linked to early and proactive treatment also known as the window of opportunity [29]. Treating early with highly effective drugs may increase the probability of achieving complete disease control like in other destructive inflammatory disease, e.g., inflammatory bowel disease [30].

Another key finding of this study was the frequent use of combined therapeutic approaches, particularly among patients with Hurley stage III disease. In our cohort, these patients were more likely to receive systemic antibiotics, corticosteroids, and surgical procedures, which is likely to reflect the need to address the more complex, structurally advanced disease characteristic of this stage. This multimodal management strategy may have helped to offset the lower expected responses to biological treatment typically observed in patients with Hurley stage III. Consequently, no significant differences in the effectiveness of bimekizumab were detected between Hurley stages II and III in our real-life population. This contrasts with randomized clinical trials of various biologics, including the PIONEER [31], SUNSHINE/SUNRISE [25], and BE HEARD [12] trials, where lower response rates have consistently been reported in patients with more severe disease stages. These findings highlight the value of real-world evidence, where flexible, individualized treatment combinations can help to overcome limitations associated with disease severity and structural damage.

Adverse events were reported in nearly half of the patients, with mucocutaneous candidiasis being the most common complication. These findings are consistent with those of a recent systematic review and meta-analysis of IL-17 inhibitors for HS, which concluded that bimekizumab poses the greatest risk of fungal infections among IL-17 blockers. At 16 weeks, the pooled incidence of fungal infections was 12.7% for bimekizumab, with Candida species accounting for 8.1% of cases—substantially higher than the 1.7% observed with secukinumab [32]. This increased susceptibility is likely due to bimekizumab’s dual inhibition of IL-17A and IL-17F, both of which play essential roles in antifungal immunity by promoting the expression of antimicrobial peptides and recruiting neutrophils to infection sites [33]. The dose and frequency of bimekizumab also appear to influence infection risk, with the 320 mg every 2 weeks regimen associated with the highest rates [32]. While most cases were mild and responded to standard antifungal treatment, these data highlight the importance of monitoring patients for fungal infections during therapy. In addition, prophylactic use of antifungals in recurrent cases has been proposed [34].

In addition to candidiasis, we observed cases of eczematous and psoriasiform reactions. These adverse effects have been reported more frequently in recent literature, particularly in relation to bimekizumab [35]. Although eczematous eruptions have been reported with other IL-17 inhibitors, the fact that they occurred shortly after switching to bimekizumab suggests that dual cytokine blockade may play a role in dysregulating cutaneous barrier immunity [36]. Furthermore, paradoxical psoriasis has also been documented, including in patients with no prior history of psoriatic disease. This phenomenon may reflect a cytokine shift or a compensatory immune response and highlights the need for vigilance and proactive management [37]. These events were uncommon in our study, but they did lead to treatment discontinuation in isolated cases.

Our study has several limitations. First, the retrospective observational design may introduce potential biases in patient selection and data collection. While we attempted to mitigate this by performing a national multicenter study and using standardized outcome measures (IHS4 and VAS), recall bias and heterogeneity in clinical assessments may still have occurred. Secondly, the lack of a control group limits our ability to attribute outcomes solely to bimekizumab. However, the consistency and magnitude of the observed changes support a true treatment effect. Thirdly, although the sample size was relatively large for a real-world HS cohort, it limited subgroup analyses and the detection of rare adverse events. Fourthly, validated quality of life metrics such as the Dermatology Life Quality Index (DLQI) or the Hidradenitis Suppurativa Quality of Life (HiSQOL) score were not systematically collected, which limits a more comprehensive assessment of the psychosocial burden and patient-centered treatment impact. However, improvements in pain and flare frequency, which are relevant patient-reported outcomes, were included and showed significant clinical benefit. Finally, the study was conducted in a single country with a homogeneous healthcare system, which may limit its generalizability to other settings.

Conclusions

Our real-world data from 48 weeks reinforce the clinical value of bimekizumab as an effective and long-lasting treatment for moderate-to-severe HS. Despite the disease’s complex and heterogeneous nature, patients experienced consistent improvements in multiple clinical and patient-reported outcomes. Notably, combined therapeutic strategies—including systemic antibiotics, corticosteroids, and surgical interventions—were more frequently employed in patients with more extensive structural damage (Hurley stage III), potentially enhancing treatment effectiveness and contributing to the favorable outcomes observed in this subgroup. Exploratory analyses suggest that early treatment may increase the odds of disease control. The safety profile was good and consistent with previous reports. As real-world data continues to be collected, bimekizumab is poised to become a promising therapeutic option in the HS treatment arsenal, regardless of the baseline severity of the disease or previous failure to currently approved biologics. Further prospective and comparative studies are needed to confirm these findings and optimize treatment strategies.

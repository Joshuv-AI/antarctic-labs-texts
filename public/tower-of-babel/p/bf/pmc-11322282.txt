Improvement photothermal property of MoS2/Fe3O4/GNR nanocomposite in cancer treatment
Authors: Behdad Shariati, Mohammad Taghi Goodarzi, Alireza Jalali, Nasrin Salehi, Majid Mozaffari
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The objective of the present study was to develop a novel molybdenum disulfide/iron oxide/gold nanorods (MoS2/Fe3O4/GNR) nanocomposite (MFG) with different concentrations of AgNO3 solution (MFG1, MFG2, and MFG3) for topical doxorubicin (DOX) drug delivery. Then, these nanocomposites were synthesized and characterized by Fourier transform infrared (FTIR), Transmission electron microscopy (TEM), Dynamic light scattering (DLS), and Ultraviolet-visible (UV–Vis) spectroscopies to confirm their structural and optical properties. Cytotoxicity of samples on Hela cell was determined using MTT assay. Results indicated that nanocomposites possess little cytotoxicity without NIR laser irradiation. Also, the relative viabilities of Hela cells decreased when the concentration of AgNO3 solution increased in this nanocomposite. Using NIR irradiation, the relative viabilities of Hela cells decreased when the concentration of samples increased. Acridine orange/propidium iodide (PI) staining, flow cytometry were recruited to evaluate the effect of these nanocomposites on apoptosis of Hela cells. Finally, results revealed when DOX loading increased in nanocomposite, then cell viability was decreased in it. Therefore, these properties make MFG3 nanocomposite a good candidate for photothermal therapy and drug loading.Graphical Abstract

Introduction

Photothermal agents are materials that can increase localized temperature at the tumor site to ablate a tumor. They raise temperature via absorbing and converting the penetrated NIR laser into heat [1, 2]. Various photothermal agents have been used for effective cancer treatment, including a variety of gold nanostructures [3, 4], silver nanostructures [5], copper nanoparticles [6], carbon nanomaterials [7, 8], transition metal chalcogenides [9, 10], conjugated polymers [11], and small organic dyes [12]. Among these materials, gold nanoparticles and especially gold nanorods (GNRs) have attracted the most attention for biomedical applications due to their properties such as good biocompatibility, tunable localized surface plasmon resonance, ability to control over their size and shape, and high photothermal conversion efficiency [3, 13]. GNRs are characterized by longitudinal and transverse plasmonic peaks which are associated to oscillation of electrons along these axes, respectively. In the last 20 years, research has indicated that GNRs size and shape are controllable. The longitudinal peak is tuned precisely between 620 and 800 nm but the transverse peak is tuned is about 520 nm. The position of the longitudinal peak is very sensitive to aspect ratio (length/width) of GNRs and it can be tuned by the amounts of silver ions in the growth solution [14]. The value of the aspect ratio of nanorods enhances when concentrations of AgNO3 solution increase in GNR growth solution [13].

In recent years, there is interest in using nanotechnology to improve drug delivery, and photothermal therapy systems. For this purpose, two-dimensional nanocarriers have been innovated [15].

Molybdenum disulfide (MoS2) is one of the most stable layered transitional metals dichalcogenides it is composed of one Mo atom plane which is located between two S atoms planes by strong covalent bonding to form a monolayer MoS2. These monolayers are joined together via Van der Waals bonds to make bulk MoS2. Since Van der Waals bonds are very weak, they can break easily and the bulk MoS2 converts to a monolayer state [16]. These properties make MoS2 a suitable candidate for using in various applications [17]. MoS2 nanoflakes also indicate other properties such as high drug loading capacity [18, 19], low cytotoxicity [20], large surface area [21], good biocompatibility [22], and high NIR absorbance [23]. Therefore, these nanoflakes can be suggested as a revolutionary strategy in drug delivery, and photothermal therapy.

Iron oxide nanoparticles (Fe3O4) have been widely applied as a target moiety for the construction of magnetic targeting therapy systems because they induce release of drug into target cells and the prevention of drug effects on non-target cells [14].

To our knowledge, there has been no report using MoS2/Fe3O4/GNRs (MFG) nanocomposite with different concentrations of AgNO3 solution for topical doxorubicin (DOX) delivery up to now. DOX is a chemotherapy drug and it has been widely used as a topical treatment for many different types of cancer. This drug is often used together with other chemotherapy agents such as photothermal therapy [24]. Therefore, we developed a novel nanoplatform based on MFG nanocomposites for DOX delivery. Given the characteristics listed for MFG nanocomposites with different concentrations of AgNO3 solution, the present study was performed to investigate MFGs nanoplatform capability for application in drug delivery and photothermal therapy.

Materials and methods

Materials

Hexadecyltrimethylammonium bromide (CH3C15H30N(Br)C3H9), hydrogen tetrachloroaurate (HAuCl4.3H2O), sodium borohydride (NaBH4), hydroquinone (C6H4(OH)2), silver nitrate (AgNO3), ammonium molybdate tetrahydrate (N6H24Mo7O24·4H2O), polyethylene glycol 400 (H(OCH2CH2)nOH, PEG 400), thioacetamide (CH3CSNH2), hydrazine hydrate (50%, v/v), ferric trichloride anhydrous(FeCl3), and ascorbic acid were purchased from Merck company.

Synthesis of MFG nanocomposites

MFG nanocomposites were synthesized in three basic steps as follows: in the first step, MoS2 nanoflakes were synthesized to hydrothermal method [25]. In this step, 120 mL of an aqueous solution containing PEG (2 g, 40 mL de-ionized water), thioacetamide (6 mg, 40 mL de-ionized water), and ammonium molybdate tetrahydrate (0.7 g, 40 mL de-ionized water) were added while stirring at room temperature. After that the mixture was then transferred into a 150 mL teflon-lined stainless steel autoclave for hydrothermal treatment at 210 °C for 20 h. Finally, product black material was centrifuged several times with de-ionized water and ethanol and was lyophilized for 24 h.

In the second step, GNRs were synthesized by seed-mediated method [13]. In this method, 10 ml of 0.5 mM HAuCl4.3H2O aqueous solution, and 1200 µL of 10 mM NaBH4 aqueous solution were added to CTAB aqueous solution (728.8 mg, 10 mL de-ionized water) under stirring, respectively. Then, the obtained spherical gold seeds suspension was kept at room temperature for about 2 h. After that, 44 mg hydroquinone was added to 364.4 mg of CTAB in 10 mL de-ionized water under ultrasonication at 40 °C. Then, Various amounts of 4 mM AgNO3 aqueous solution (400 μl (G1), 800 μl (G2), and 1200 μl (G3)), and 10 ml of 1 mMHAuCl4.3H2O aqueous solution were added to the mixture of hydroquinone and CTAB made in above. Finally, 24 µL of the gold seed suspension was added to the gained solutions. Gained suspensions were kept undisturbed for 24 h to complete the GNRs growth process.

In the third step, MoS2/Fe3O4 sample was fabricated by hydrothermal method [26]. In this step, 40 mL of an aqueous solution containing ascorbic acid (0.845 g), FeCl3(0.259 g), and synthesized MoS2 (0.048 g) was added to 24 mL of hydrazine hydrate while stirring at room temperature. Then, the mixture was stirred for another 10 min, before transferring into 100 mL Teflon-lined autoclave. The obtained material was heated at 210 °C for 8 h, and was naturally cooled to room temperature. The resultant precipitate was collected, and was washed several times with de-ionized water, and was dispersed in water for further use.

In the fourth step, a constant amount of synthesized MoS2/Fe3O4 sample (0.1 g) was added drop by drop to the gained GNRs with different concentrations of AgNO3 solution (G1, G2, and G3) to obtain MFG1, MFG2, and MFG3 nanocomposites.

Characterization of the samples

A series of standard characterizations were carried out to confirm the crystallinity, morphology, and quality of decorated GNRs with MoS2/Fe3O4. The morphology and sizes of samples were observed via scanning transmission electron microscope (TEM, Philips-EM208, Made in the Netherlands). The samples were characterized by a fourier transform infrared spectrophotometer (Perkin Elmer-Frontier spectrometer). UV–Vis data was obtained using Schimadzu UV-1800 spectrometer. The photothermal experiment was conducted by using an 808 nm NIR laser with 10 W power (Fiber Coupled Diode Laser, IRAN) oriented perpendicular to a quartz cell containing aqueous MFG dispersions with different concentrations and a total volume of 2 mL. Hela cell line was purchased from the cell bank of Pasteur Institute of Iran.

Cell experiments

The cytotoxicity of MFG1, MFG2, and MFG3 nanocomposites on Hela cancer cells was investigated using MTT assay. First, the cells were cultured at a density of 5 × 103 in 96-well plates and were incubated 24 h. Hela cell was grown in normal DMEM culture medium, with 10% fetal bovine serum phosphate buffer saline (PBS). Finally, cancer cells were incubated with various concentrations (10, 25, 50, 100, 200, and 400 μg/ml) of MFG1, MFG2, and MFG3 nanocomposites for 24, 48, and 72 h.

DOX loading into MFG nanocomposites

At first, 2 mg of each nanocomposite was dispersed in 2 mg of aqueous solution of DOX (2 mL). Then, they were stirred overnight in dark room to load DOX in nanocomposites. Finally, they were centrifuged and washed several times until the color of DOX disappeared in the supernatant.

Acridine orange/propidium iodide staining

In this study, acridine orange/propidium iodide staining method was applied to examine the occurrence of apoptosis in Hela cells using MFG1, MFG2, and MFG3 nanocomposites [27]. First, the cells were seeded in six-well plate. Then, they were treated with different concentrations of MFG1, MFG2, and MFG3 nanocomposites. After 24 h, the cells were separated from the bottom of the plate with trypsin and centrifuged several times. Then, the supernatant was drained and 100 μL of PBS was added to the cell sediment. The dye solution was prepared using 1 μl of acridine orange (2 mg/mL), and 1 μL of propidium iodide (1 mg/mL) in 1 ml of PBS. 10 μL of the cell suspension was combined with 10 μL of the dye solution and placed on a slide and examined under a fluorescence microscope.

Flow cytometry to assay the apoptosis

Hela cells were treated with different concentrations of MFG1, MFG2, and MFG3 nanocomposites for 24 h. Then, the cells were detached from the bottom of the plate. After that supernatants were drained then cells were washed with 1 ml of PBS. Finally, 1 mg/mL of PI was added in the cells and incubated for 10 min that can be examined by the flow cytometry.

Results

Fourier transform infrared (FT-IR) spectroscopy

The structure characterization of MoS2 nanoflakes, GNRs, and MFG1 were investigated by FTIR spectroscopy from 400 to 4000 cm−1. First, the FTIR spectra of MoS2 nanoflakes and GNRs were examined and compared to that of MFG1 in Fig. 1. As seen in this figure, the FTIR spectrum of MoS2 nanoflakes demonstrates abroad band at about 634 cm−1 corresponds to Mo–S bond vibration and a the peak at about 3391 cm−1 can be assigned to the OH stretching of hydrogen bonds. Also, characteristic peaks at about 1100, 1438, 1630, and 2880 cm−1 were ascribed to MoS2 nanoflakes [16, 27]. Since PEG is used in this work to modify surface of MoS2 nanoflakes for safe application in biomedical field, the peaks at about 2880, 1630, 1438, and 1100 cm−1 are attributed to PEG on the surface of MoS2. The FTIR spectrum of GNRs shows two strong band at 3406, and 1638 cm−1 assigned to O-H stretching of the adsorbed H2O and a weak band also was indicated at 1194 cm−1 indexed as C-N stretching of CTAB [28].Fig. 1FTIR spectroscopy of MoS2, GNR, and MFG1 nanocomposite

FTIR spectroscopy of MoS2, GNR, and MFG1 nanocomposite

Most importantly, characteristic FTIR peaks of MoS2 and GNRs were observed in the FTIR spectrum of MFG1 nanocomposite. On the other hands, one peak also exists at about 570 cm−1 in the FTIR spectrum of MFG1 nanocomposite which it is attributed to Fe–O bond vibrations [14].

These images reveal that average aspect ratio is about 3.2, 2.7, and 2.5 for MFG1 MFG2, and MFG3 nanocomposites, respectively. Therefore, increasing AgNO3 in the growth solution enhance aspect ratio of GNRs. Cytotoxicity study

MTT assay was used to specify the viability of Hela cell after incubating with various concentrations of MFG1, MFG2, and MFG3 solutions (10, 25, 50, 100, 200, and 400 μg/mL) for 24 h (Fig. 6). As seen in this figure, increasing the concentration decreases relative viability of Hela cell in all samples and can stay above 66.7%, 59.4%, and 39.5% at concentration of 400 μg/mL, respectively. In other words, cytotoxic effect exhibited that about 33.3%, 40.6%, and 60.5% of Hela cells were killed by MFG1, MFG2, and MFG3 nanocomposites at concentration of 400 μg/mL, most cell cytotoxicity was related to the MFG3 at a concentration of 400 μg/mL. By NIR irradiation, the relative viability of Hela cell decreased and the most cell cytotoxicity was related to MFG3. Cytotoxic effect exhibited that about 22%, 18%, and 16% of Hela cells were killed by MFG1, MFG2, and MFG3 nanocomposite at a concentration of 400 μg/mL by NIR irradiation, respectively. This amount of cytotoxic effect in MFG3 nanocomposite was better that other report in MoS2 nanoflakes, and MoS2/C, MoS2/Fe3O4/Au nanocomposites at 400 μg/mL concentration by NIR irradiation [15, 19, 29]. Thus, MFG3 nanocomposite indicated very good biocompatibility, and it also can ablate cancer cells in photothermal therapy. The IC50 average value was calculated at 125, 114, and 100 μg/mL for mentioned nanocomposites respectively after 72 h treatment.Fig. 6The cytotoxicity of Hela cell with MFG1, MFG2, and MFG3 in different concentrations (10, 25, 50, 100, 200, and 400 μg/mL) with and without laser irradiation

Discussion

Due to excellent optical absorption and photothermal conversion efficiency, MoS2 nanoflakes can be used in cancer therapy [16, 19]. In recent decades, nanotechnology combine theranostic methods for fast therapy by using multifunctional nanocomposites based on layer nanostructures and plasmonic nanoparticles [26]. In our study, for the first time, we utilized MFG nanocomposites with different concentrations of AgNo3 solution (MFG1, MFG2, and MFG3) for cancer therapy. The release of drugs into target cell and the prevention of drug effects on cell are requirements of an efficient drug delivery system. The structural and optical properties of nanocomposites were firstly investigated then DOX was loaded into nanocomposites to study their potential as a drug delivery system into the treatment. These characterizations confirmed that GNRs and Fe3O4 nanoparticles have entered in between MoS2 nanoflakes. TEM images indicate which length of nanorods enhances when concentrations of AgNO3 increases in GNR growth solution. Then, these nanocomposites were applied as a novel theranostic agent for in vitro. After loading DOX on MFG1, MFG2, and MFG3 nanocomposites, therapeutic efficiency was demonstrated by MTT assay that showed an anticancer effect in killing cancer cells. The results of the MTT assay showed that increasing of concentration of nanocomposite, DOX drug, and NIR irradiation affects the Hela cancer cells. On the other hand, MFG3 nanocomposite increased the induction of apoptosis so it caused the death of tumor cells and prevented their proliferation. Therefore, we claim that, MFG3 nanocomposite can open a new venue in the cancer therapy process and efficiently can kill cancer cells.

Designing a 3D printed connector which linked face masks to vacuum cleaner hose for potential foreign body airway obstruction removal
Authors: Nino Fijačko, Nika Tomšič, Maj Kraševec, Špela Metličar, Robert Greif
Published: 2024

To the Editor,

Back blows and abdominal thrusts are established first aid techniques in cases of foreign body airway obstruction (FBAO). Recent studies have introduced the vacuum cleaner as a possible alternative intervention for removing FBAO, but without providing detailed instructions for its use.1, 2, 3, 4 Our goal was to create a 3D-printed attachment designed to connect a face mask to the vacuum cleaner hose (Fig. 1). We tested this rescue approach in a simulation study on manikins to evaluate its effectiveness and the time required to remove an FBAO.Fig. 1Vacuum cleaner with 3D printed hose connector and the face mask.

Vacuum cleaner with 3D printed hose connector and the face mask.

In June 2024, with informed consent, we invited nursing students from the University of Maribor, Faculty of Health Sciences, Slovenia, to participate in an experiment featuring a scripted scenario where an adult was choking. In this scenario, two helpers were involved. The first, a trained individual acting as a standardized rescuer, performed back blows and abdominal thrusts on the Choking Charlie manikin (Laerdal Medical, Norway) to remove the FBAO. The simulated FBAO was a round soft plastic ball. The second helper, comprised of study participants, had to retrieve a vacuum cleaner (Robert Bosch GmbH, USA) from another room, attach the 3D-printed connector to the vacuum cleaner hose, and connect a size 5 face mask (Intersurgical Ltd., UK). This step was demonstrated to the participants before the scenario to allow them to assemble and familiarize themselves with the equipment. After activating the vacuum cleaner, attempts to extract the FBAO continued until the FBAO was removed or one minute had elapsed. A stopwatch (Decathlon, France) was used to measure the time from retrieving the vacuum cleaner to successfully removing the FBAO. The 3D-printed connector, crafted from white hard polylactic acid (PLA), is freely available for download at https://shorturl.at/ycxFg.

Twenty-three nursing students participated in the study, with 70 % (16/23) being female and an average age of 24.5 ± 5.9 years. Notably, 86 % (20/23) had never previously provided first aid to a choking victim. The FBAO was successfully removed on the first attempt in every trial, with the average removal time being 38 ± 8 s. The main challenge for participants was transporting the vacuum cleaner, as 22 % (5/23) found it too heavy. Additionally, 17 % (4/23) encountered issues such as forgetting the face mask or struggling with assembling the mask-hose connection, which led to longer intervention times.

We developed a technical solution with the potential to save lives in cases of FBAO, especially when initial standard first aid techniques are unsuccessful. Currently, no clinical trials have compared this approach with standard rescue methods or other removal techniques. Only case reports are available.1, 2, 3, 4 Our solution consistently and successfully removed the FBAO, while other reported methods required multiple attempts.5 However, it does have its limitations; the 3D-printed hose connector is not available for purchase, and a suitable face mask may not always be readily available. Moreover, our 3D-printed hose connector may not be compatible with all vacuum cleaners. Clearly, evaluating the efficacy and safety of this solution requires human experience and well-conducted clinical trials in emergency situations.

Magnesium and preclinical atherosclerosis
Authors: Christian Saleh
Published: 2025

Dear Editor,

Kara et al. published in a recent issue a study titled, « Effect of serum magnesium level on carotid intima media thickness in hemodialysis patients » [1]. This cross-sectional study examined in 88 hemodialysis patients (38.6% female, mean age 61.3 ± 12.4 years) whether serum magnesium level is related to cardiovascular events [1]. As surrogate marker for preclinical atherosclerosis the carotid intima media thickness (CIMT) was used [1]. Patients were divided into 2 groups according to magnesium (Mg) value as group 1 (Mg ≥2.3 mg/dl) and group 2 (Mg < 2.3 mg/dl) [1]. The mean CIMT of patients with group 1 and group 2 were respectively 0.93 ± 0.22 mm and 1.03 ± 0.22 mm [1]. The study found a significant association between serum Mg levels and CIMT, suggesting that Mg may have a role in vascular health in hemodialysis patients [1].

There are some comments needed to evaluate the results of this study in a more balanced way.

The authors write “A plaque-free area 1 cm away from the junction of the left and right common carotid arteries was selected and three consecutive measurements were taken from the posterior wall of this area, and the average was taken” [1]. Consequently, a single-site measurement was performed [1]. This measurement method assumes that the posterior wall of the common carotid artery (CCA) shows higher spatial resolution that the anterior CCA wall and other carotid artery segments, namely the bifurcation and internal CA. The author of this letter proposes a multi-site CIMT measurement, in line with the recommendations of the pioneers of CIMT research, including several sections of the carotid artery (CA) tree (i.e. anterior/posterior walls of the CCA, bifurcation and internal CA) [2]). This approach considers the asymmetric presentation of atherosclerosis [3, 4]. A single site CIMT measurement instead, performed furthermore in a pre-determined section of the CA tree, might coincide with a normal wall segment, ignoring atherosclerotic altered vessel sections. Therefore, the two groups in the Kara et al. study cannot be compared, as the potential atherosclerotic burden was not examined through an extended interrogation of the CA tree [1]. Furthermore, the authors did not mention if the measures were made manually or assisted by an automated edge-detection device [1]. From the methodological description it appears though that measurements were made manually [1]; manual measurements introduce a significant measurement bias [5]. Importantly to consider, within this context, that the normal range of CIMT is considered between 0.6 mm and 1.0 mm [6]. Slightest inaccuracies suffice to misclassify subjects into different CIMT categories. Within this context, Kara et al. also did not report which CIMT cut-off they used to consider pathological increased CIMT [1]. That information is of critical importance as there is no consensus on CIMT cut-off values and different cut-offs for pathological CIMT were proposed [6]. To add to this chain of methodological issues, a major omission is the apparent lack of cardiac synchronization [7]. CIMT should be measured at the end-diastolic phase. Differences in CIMT between the two cardiac phases were reported [7, 8].

In conclusion: The differences in CIMT values between the two groups of the Kara et al. study can have multiple non-atherosclerotic related reasons and appear rather be due to a suboptimal defined measurement protocol and potential measurement bias, consisting of measurement errors/inaccuracies, impartial interrogation of the CA tree and absent cardiac synchronization [1]. Considering these multiple methodological issues within the Kara et al. study the conclusions that “Mg may have a role in vascular health in hemodialysis patients. As a readily available and cost-effective laboratory parameter, Mg could serve as a potential marker to help identify patients at higher risk of carotid atherosclerosis, enabling earlier interventions” cannot be made [1]. The study would greatly benefit from a clarification of the raised points by the authors.

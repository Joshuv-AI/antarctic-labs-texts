Hybrid IGWO-Dingo optimized DeMoHybridNet model for multi-class leaf disease identification
Authors: Shantilata Palei, Puspanjali Mohapatra, Soubhagya Ranjan Mallick, Princy Diwan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
To achieve efficient crop management, exact plant disease detection in leaves is required. This study proposes DeMoHybridNet (Bottleneck Reduction Fusion) for the automated classification of Corn, Apple, Citrus, and Mango crop diseases. Input images are processed through augmentation and resizing, and then features are learned using DenseNet-201 and MobileNetV2. Global Average Pooling is applied, which produces condensed features. The features are then compressed using bottleneck layers of 512 features. The features are concatenated and classified by Random Forest (RF) classifier. To further improve the performance, a hybrid meta-heuristic method called IGWO-DOA (Improved Grey Wolf Optimization-Dingo Optimization Algorithm) is used to optimize the hyperparameters of the model for better convergence and generalization. The proposed optimized model gives the classification accuracy is 98.56% for Corn, 98.99% for Apple, 97.83% for Citrus and 99.35% for Mango leaf dataset. Statistical analysis confirms its robustness and reliability, demonstrating its effectiveness for precision agriculture applications.

Introduction

Healthy plants are essential for good crop production, a strong economy, food security, and sustainable farming. However, they often face threats from bacteria, fungi, viruses, nematodes, and pests, with fungal diseases being the most common, usually visible on the leaves1. In terms of early detection, traditional methods can be slow, and as a result, it may lead to delayed treatment2. Current machine learning (ML) has been instrumental in enabling efficient detection of leaf diseases. However, early detection of plant leaf diseases is difficult and therefore remains an important area of research in ML3. The emergence of deep learning (DL) methodologies has revolutionized the accuracy and effectiveness of diagnostic testing. The use of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) is specifically suited for the accurate diagnosis of medical conditions based on analysis of high-resolution images4.

Despite all of the aforementioned advances, DL and ML models remain limited by certain factors. First, DL and ML models require significant computational power, which becomes more pronounced when employing larger datasets or complex models. Second, DL and ML models are extremely sensitive to hyperparameter tuning. Tuning hyperparameters can require significant amounts of time. These limitations illustrate the necessity for effective hyperparameter tuning strategies, including the utilization of metaheuristic optimization techniques, to improve both model robustness and predictive accuracy5,6. A critical method for improving the performance of DL-based models is hyperparameter tuning. Hyperparameter tuning optimizes the hyperparameters via the use of several different ways such as Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), Dingo Optimization Algorithm (DOA), and Grey Wolf Optimization (GWO)7–9. The use of metaheuristic search techniques to find optimal hyperparameter values within their respective domains helps fine-tune DL models. As a result, the algorithm’s output provides a significant reduction in error by eliminating the tedious trial-and-error approach, speeding up model training, and improving overall model performance.

Problem domain

Increasing plant leaf diseases have presented a crucial challenge to global agricultural productivity. However, the traditional approaches in disease detection are cumbersome, subjective, and prone to error, while conventional ML models work poorly with large and complicated image datasets. As for the performance of CNNs based on DL, optimal feature extraction, classification techniques, and hyperparameter tuning play a heavy role. Most optimization methods exhibit a poor trade-off between exploration and exploitation, which leads to their poor performance. All of the above challenges lead to the need for one more efficient and accurate way of detecting leaf disease, which motivated the development of this approach, which fuses features, advanced ML classifiers, and hybrid metaheuristic algorithms to improve the accuracy of disease detection and reduce computational cost.

Literature survey

This survey focuses on hybrid methods integrating DL with ML, metaheuristic algorithms, and the combined application of DL/ML with metaheuristic algorithms for leaf disease detection.

Deep machine learning models

Reviewed10 the application of hyperspectral imaging in plant disease classification, highlighting popular ML with DL models. Introduced11 a deep CNN-based plant disease model using classifiers such as SVM, KNN, and RF for feature-based classification. Developed12 a model that combined deep CNN with gated recurrent units to improve early disease detection. Proposed13 a citrus leaf disease prediction method that used feature fusion from CNN models (AlexNet, VGG16, VGG19) combined with SVM for classification. Presented14 a novel maize leaf classification model that used DenseNet201 to extract key image features, combined with an SVM optimized via Bayesian techniques. Introduced15 a MobileNetv2-YOLOv3 model to detect gray leaf spots on tomato leaves. Similarly,16 developed a method for identifying bean leaf diseases using images and a MobileNet-based model, detailing an efficient network architecture with optimized hyperparameters for enhanced classification accuracy. Integrated17 ML and DL through 40 hybrid DL models. Hyperparameters were optimized using the Optuna framework.

Metaheuristic algorithm

In recent studies, metaheuristic algorithms have been widely used for plant leaf disease detection. Formulated5 color image segmentation as an optimization task using Otsu’s method and applied five algorithms, including a modified TLABC (MTLABC) with Levy flight, which outperformed others across different thresholds. Enhanced6 the GWO with chaotic disturbance, migration, and attack strategies to overcome premature convergence and improve detection performance.

Integration of deep ML models with metaheuristic algorithms

Introduced18 a DL-based method for mango leaf disease detection, using a wrapper-based feature selection with an Adaptive Particle-GWO algorithm to reduce features. The selected features were classified using an MLP. Proposed7 an optimized DL framework for detecting leaf diseases, combining CNN-based feature extraction with a two-stage weight optimization strategy using an enhanced Butterfly Optimization Algorithm integrated with Genetic Algorithm techniques. Introduced4 a semi-automated method for grape leaf disease detection that integrates optimized segmentation, hybrid feature extraction, and ensemble classification. The system used GWO-optimized K-means for segmenting infected areas and combined convolution-based, GLCM, LBP, and Gabor features for robust classification. Developed19 a deep CNN model to detect diseases in cotton leaves using photographic images, classifying leaves as healthy or diseased. The model’s architecture was optimized with the GWO algorithm to ensure high efficiency and was compared with commonly used models, including ResNet50, VGG19, and InceptionV3.

Developed20 a spectral-based approach for early detection and severity grading of cotton verticillium wilt with SVM models optimized by GA, grid search, PSO, and GWO. The MSC-GWO-SVM model achieved the highest accuracy of 91.2%. Introduced21 a hybrid Optimization algorithm with a Deep CNN for detecting plant diseases in hyperspectral leaf images. The method used WWPDO for segmentation and CNN hyperparameter tuning, followed by Bayesian fuzzy clustering and majority voting, achieving 91.35% accuracy. Introduced8 ODEDCNet, an Optimized Deep Encoder-Decoder Cascaded Network, for improved leaf disease segmentation, using DOA to fine-tune hyperparameters based on segmentation accuracy. By integrating pre-trained CNNs with an SVM classifier, the method effectively balanced exploration and exploitation, enhancing overall performance. Introduced22 an improved genetic algorithm for efficient hyperparameter tuning, which improved traditional GA by selecting high-fitness solutions to reduce computational cost. Existing studies often lack effective hybrid architectures that combine feature fusion with optimized classification, and many rely on single metaheuristic algorithms that struggle to balance exploration and exploitation. Additionally, limited work evaluates models across multiple leaf datasets under a unified framework. To address these gaps, this study proposes a bottleneck-based feature fusion model with a hybrid IGWO-DOA optimization strategy, validated on four diverse leaf datasets.

This section provides an overview of CNN fundamentals. It further explains the GWO and DOA chosen to optimize hyperparameters for enhanced performance.

Convolutional neural network

In the process of extracting features from a given dataset, the extraction of Deep Features occurs automatically through the use of layers like Convolution, rectified linear unit (ReLU), pooling and global average pooling (GAP). The classification stage utilises a fully connected layer (FCL) along with softmax28. Convolutional layers capture semantic features from the input, while the ReLU activation function, applied after each convolution, introduces non-linearity to help the model learn intricate patterns. Pooling layers downsample the spatial dimensions, retaining critical features. The GAP layer condenses the features from the final convolutional layer into a single vector for classification, minimizing overfitting by reducing parameters in the FCL. Finally, the softmax activation function classifies the input into distinct categories.

This study evaluates the performance of two CNN models, DenseNet201 and MobileNetV2, and one ML-based RF model on Corn, Apple, Citrus and Mango leaf datasets.

Hyperparameter-based optimization algorithms

In DL, hyperparameter optimization is crucial for maximizing model accuracy and generalization. This paper employs GWO and DOA to fine-tune hyperparameters, allowing the CNN models to achieve higher performance in leaf disease classification. These optimization algorithms enhance efficiency by effectively exploring the parameter space, resulting in accurate and computationally effective models.

Grey Wolf optimization algorithm

The GWO, introduced by Seyedali Mirjalili in 201429, is inspired by the hierarchical social structure and hunting strategies of grey wolves. These wolves maintain a strict hierarchy, categorized as alpha, beta, delta, and omega, which dictates their roles during hunting. The GWO algorithm replicates this hierarchy and hunting behavior to address optimization problems effectively. The strategy of GWO is shown in Fig. 1.

In GWO, the optimization problem is modeled by considering the prey’s location as the optimal solution and the positions of wolves as candidate solutions. Methodology

This paper introduces a novel DL-based CNN model named DeMoHybridNet to classify leaf diseases in multiple plants. The IGWO and IGWO-DOA metaheuristic algorithms are proposed to enhance the good balance of exploitation and exploration of the solution space. These methods are integrated with the DeMoHybridNet model to improve its performance.

DeMoHybridNet model

This study proposes a multiclass leaf disease classification model named DeMoHybridNet, designed to integrate the complementary strengths of DenseNet201 and MobileNetV2. The model is trained on preprocessed Corn, Apple, Citrus, and Mango leaf datasets, each containing multiple disease categories. In this architecture, each input dataset size of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$224 \times 224 \times 3$$\end{document} is independently processed through DenseNet201 and MobileNetV2. Both networks perform a full forward pass through their convolution, activation, and pooling layers, followed by GAP to generate deep feature vectors: \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$F_1$$\end{document} (1920-dimensional) from DenseNet201 and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$F_2$$\end{document} (1280-dimensional) from MobileNetV2.

To reduce dimensionality and emphasize the most discriminative information, both feature vectors are passed through bottleneck FCLs. DenseNet201 features are compressed using a Dense(512) layer to obtain \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$F_1'$$\end{document}, and MobileNetV2 features are similarly compressed to produce \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$F_2'$$\end{document}. These reduced feature vectors (512D + 512D) are then concatenated to form a unified 1024-dimensional deep representation. These fused features are finally classified by the RF classifier, enabling accurate multiclass prediction for 4 corn classes, 13 apple classes, 5 citrus classes, and 8 mango classes. The RF training parameters are max_depth is 20, max_features is ’log2’, min_samples_split is 4, and n_estimators is 225. The resulting model, DeMoHybridNet, provides an efficient bottleneck-based fusion strategy that enhances feature quality and improves disease classification performance across diverse crop datasets. GWO and DOA assisted DeMoHybridNet

DeMoHybridNet is assisted by the GWO and DOA algorithms to tune hyperparameters, and the models train the dataset with the optimal hyperparameter values. This model extracts features that the RF model classifies into multiple classes. GWO and DOA individually operate for exploitation and exploration to find the optimal hyperparameter values for the DeMoHybridNet. The DeMoHybridNet model leverages GWO-DOA and the optimized hyperparameter values identified by these algorithms. DeMoHybridNet extracts features from the input with the optimal hyperparameters, and RF classifies the extracted features.

Results discussion and comparison

This section presents a comparison of various models across multiple performance metrics and datasets and a comparison with previously published works in this problem domain. The models evaluated include DeMoHybridNet, DOA-assisted DeMoHybridNet, GWO-assisted DeMoHybridNet, IGWO-assisted DeMoHybridNet, and IGWO with DOA-assisted DeMoHybridNet. The datasets used for comparison are Corn, Apple, Citrus, and Mango.

In Fig. 16, the X-axis represents the models, the Y-axis denotes the datasets, and the Z-axis indicates the performance metric values. Each subfigure, from Fig. 16, corresponds to nine metrics. These figures reveal that the IGWO with DOA-assisted DeMoHybridNet consistently outperforms all other models across all datasets and performance metrics. This is especially evident in the Mango dataset, where the uniform distribution and high quality of image samples across all classes underscore the model’s superior effectiveness. Integrating IGWO and DOA enhances hyperparameter optimization, resulting in high accuracy performance. Further comparison is illustrated in Fig. 17, where additional models— MobileNetV2, DenseNet201, and PSO-assisted DeMoHybridNet are evaluated alongside the previously mentioned models. The (IGWO-DOA)-assisted DeMoHybridNet achieves the highest accuracy across all datasets, with a notable peak of 99.35% on the Mango dataset.

Table 19 compares the proposed IGWO-DOA-assisted DeMoHybridNet model and previously published methods across multiple datasets. It highlights feature extraction, classification methods, feature fusion, metaheuristic algorithms, hyperparameter tuning, statistical analysis, and accuracy. The proposed model demonstrates superior performance, achieving an accuracy of 99.35% across various datasets, outperforming prior works using methods like VGG1613,37, SVM9,38, DenseNet20114, and other optimization techniques (e.g., PSO, TLBO, and ABC)5,9,18,38. Unlike existing approaches that focus on feature extraction and classification without comprehensive hyperparameter tuning or statistical validation, the proposed IGWO-DOA model integrates both aspects effectively, leading to consistently higher accuracy across all datasets. This comparison underscores the proposed model’s robustness, adaptability, and precision, establishing it as a state-of-the-art solution for plant leaf image classification tasks.

Conclusion

This paper presents a robust approach to the detection of plant leaf disease using four datasets: Corn, Apple, Citrus, and Mango. The proposed (IGWO-DOA)-assisted DeMoHybridNet model combines bottleneck feature fusion and is classified by RF model. Hyperparameter tuning is performed using the hybrid IGWO-DOA framework, balancing exploitation and exploration to enhance model accuracy and efficiency. The model achieves high classification accuracy: 98.56% for Corn, 98.99% for Apple, 97.83% for Citrus, and 99.35% for Mango. Extensive statistical analyses, including the Wilcoxon signed-rank test, average running time comparison, and state-of-the-art comparisons, validate the model’s superior performance over existing methods.

However, the experimental design has certain limitations, including limited benchmarking against additional strong baseline models under strictly identical training conditions. Therefore, while the proposed model demonstrates promising performance, further validation under more comprehensive and controlled settings would strengthen the generalizability of the results.

Future research will aim to extend the model’s scope to detect multiple diseases in both leaves and fruits under various climatic conditions. Additionally, the model will be enhanced to provide actionable insights, such as optimal fertilizer recommendations, to support precision agriculture and improve plant health management.

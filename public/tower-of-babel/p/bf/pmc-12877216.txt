The association between sarcopenic obesity and cardiometabolic multimorbidity in chinese middle-aged and older adults
Authors: Zhi-Ming Shen, Rui Zeng, Fayi Xie, Jiang Wang, Dongmei Ye, Aizhang Zhu, Lihuan Chen, Wan Zhu, Ke Zhu, Tenghui Fan, Xiao-Ming Zhang
Published: 2024

Introduction

The rapid acceleration of global aging has precipitated a notable surge in the prevalence of chronic diseases. Among these, the coexistence of stroke, heart disease, and diabetes has markedly increased. A recent study revealed that the prevalence of cardiometabolic multimorbidity (CMM), encompassing these conditions, stands at 6.0% in China [1]. While both sarcopenia and obesity have been identified as conditions associated with stroke, heart disease, and diabetes [2,3], the intricate relationship between sarcopenic obesity (SO) and CMM remains underexplored. Yu et al. [4] investigated this association, publishing their findings in The Journal of Nutrition, Health and Aging. Their results suggest that sarcopenic obesity demonstrates the most robust correlation with CMM compared to obesity alone and sarcopenia alone. Consequently, sarcopenic obesity has been reported as being independently linked to CMM. Nevertheless, certain inquiries merit further examination. In Yu’s study, the definition of “possible sarcopenia,” which relied exclusively on reduced handgrip strength, diverges from established diagnostic criteria. According to the Asian Working Group for Sarcopenia, possible sarcopenia is characterized by either low muscle strength or diminished physical performance. Inspired by this, our study modifies this exposure variable by employing comprehensive assessments for sarcopenic obesity and aims to elucidate its association with CMM among Chinese individuals, utilizing data from the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS).

Methods

Study design

This study employs the same methodological framework as Yu et al. [4], with a focus on modifying the exposure variable (SO). We used data from CHARLS, where participants underwent assessments for sarcopenic obesity, employing comprehensive assessments, rather than solely relying on handgrip strength. The analysis followed a similar statistical approach and confounders as Yu et al.

Assessment of Sarcopenic Obesity Status

Sarcopenic obesity was defined as a condition characterized by both sarcopenia and obesity. Sarcopenia was assessed through low muscle mass, low handgrip strength, and low physical performance, with obesity defined as a BMI ≥ 25 kg/m². In our study, muscle mass was calculated using the formula: ASM = 0.193 × weight (kg) + 0.107 × height (cm) − 4.157 × gender − 0.037 × age (years) − 2.631. Low muscle mass was defined as ASM/Ht2 values of < 7.05 kg/m2 for men and < 5.63 kg/m2 for women. Low handgrip strength was categorized as < 28 kg for men and <18 kg for women, while low physical performance was evaluated based on prior studies [5]. Sarcopenia was confirmed by meeting the criteria of low muscle and low handgrip strength or low muscle and low physical performance. Possible sarcopenia was identified through low handgrip strength or low physical performance. In our study, we combined possible sarcopenia and sarcopenia to define the condition of sarcopenia.

Results

Prevalence of CMM in sarcopenic and obesity status

The prevalence of CMM was 5.2% for obesity only, 4.0% for sarcopenia only, and 9.9% for sarcopenic obesity. Cross-sectional analysis

The unadjusted model for the cross-sectional study indicated that both sarcopenia alone and obesity alone, as well as sarcopenic obesity, were associated with an elevated likelihood of CMM compared to individuals devoid of sarcopenia or obesity, with OR values of 2.39 (95% CI: 1.84–3.12), 1.84 (95% CI: 1.40–2.40), and 4.79 (95% CI: 3.60–6.37), respectively. In the model II, the association remained statistically significant except obesity only, albeit with reduced effect sizes: OR 1.21 (95% CI: 0.82–1.77) for obesity only, OR 1.54 (95% CI: 1.02–2.31) for sarcopenia only, and OR 1.76 (95% CI: 1.13–2.75) for sarcopenic obesity, showed in Fig.1b.

Longitudinal analysis

In the fully adjusted model, the longitudinal analysis revealed that only sarcopenic obesity was linked to an increased risk of CMM among Chinese individuals, with an OR of 1.98 (95% CI: 1.01–3.87), showed in Fig. 1b.

Discussion

Our principal findings indicate that sarcopenic obesity is independently associated with an increased risk of CMM in both cross-sectional and longitudinal analyses, corroborating the study by Yu et al. [4]. However, our research differs in that we confirmed the condition of sarcopenia by incorporating both possible sarcopenia and sarcopenia, whereas Yu’s study relied solely on low handgrip strength. This methodological distinction resulted in a higher prevalence of sarcopenic obesity (10.1% versus 5.5%) in our study compared to Yu’s findings. Consequently, the prevalence of CMM across varying sarcopenic obesity statuses was 5.2% for obesity only, 4.0% for sarcopenia only, and 9.9% for sarcopenic obesity, surpassing the figures reported by Yu. Our results are consistent with existing literature, reinforcing the notion that sarcopenic obesity is associated with an elevated likelihood of developing CMM. Therefore, early screening for sarcopenic obesity among middle-aged and older adults is imperative, and multifaceted interventions, such as aerobic exercise and nutritional support, should be implemented to address both sarcopenia and obesity, thereby mitigating the risk of CMM within this population.

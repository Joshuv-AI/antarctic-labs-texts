Computational study of magneto-hydrodynamic hybrid nanofluid flow and heat transfer over a stretchable surface with temperature-dependent thermal conductivity under motile microbes and slip effect
Authors: Muhammad Umar Farooq, Aaqib Majeed, Taoufik Saidani, Parvez Ali, Jihad Younis, Mounirah Areshi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractVariable thermal conductivity plays important role in precisely modeling of hybrid nanofluid (HNF) flow and heat transfer. In practical applications, thermal conductivity frequently varies with temperature, nanoparticle concentration, and fluid composition, affecting the rate of energy transfer within the system. The present investigation offers an inclusive investigation of heat transfer in magnetized flow of a HNF consisting of zinc (Zn) and silicon dioxide (SiO2) nanoparticles dispersed in water (H2O), towards a rotating stretchable surface. The management of thermal energy in rotating, high-speed environments can be significantly enhanced through the use of magnetized nanofluids with optimized nanoparticle shapes. This study incorporates the effects velocity slip, and convective boundary conditions. The principal objective is to explore the transport phenomena of heat and mass transfer, as well as the bioconvective behavior induced by motile microorganisms within the hybrid nanofluid. By applying similarity transformation, the governing flow model of PDE’s for momentum, energy, concentration, and motile microorganism distribution are condensed to a set of coupled nonlinear ODE’s. These equations are numerically solved with MATLAB software, which employs a vigorous shooting method. A thoroughly parametric investigation is performed to assess the influence of several physical parameters, including the magnetic field strength (), rotational parameter (), velocity slip coefficient (), thermal radiation parameter (), variable thermal conductivity (), and the volume fractions of the nanoparticles (), along with the nanoparticle shape factor. Furthermore, the effects of the Lewis number (), Peclet number () and bioconvective Lewis number () are examined with respect to species concentration and microorganism. The numerical fallouts are presented both graphically and in tabular format, demonstrating that enhancements in magnetic field strength and radiation parameter notably affect the thermal and flow characteristics. The legitimacy of the suggested model is confirmed through excellent agreement with benchmark outcomes from existing literature across various Prandtl number () values, thereby signifying the reliability and practical relevance of the current approach in advanced hybrid nanofluid-based thermal management systems.Keywords: 3D surface, MHD, Hybrid nanofluid, Velocity slip, Brownian motion, Convective boundary constraints, MATLAB

Introduction

Nanofluids are considered by the addition of nanoscale tiny particles to the conventional base fluids, including water and have become one of the revolutionary media of heat transfer. They have been on the frontline of the current thermal engineering research due to their improved thermophysical qualities, particularly amplified thermal conductivity. Nanofluids have acknowledged a lot of devotion in the past few years because they have diversified uses in heat exchangers, electronic cooling systems, transportation technologies, food processing and biomedical engineering. The original publication of Choi and Eastman [1] proposed nanofluids as ground-breaking coolants in heat transfer, which led to the new boulevards in enhancing the efficiency of thermal systems. Subsequently, voluminous studies have shown their possibilities in improving their heat transfer capacity in a extensive range of engineering applications, like power generation systems and biomedical applications [2, 3]. Arif et al. [4] focused on the application of nanofluids comprising blood as a base fluid, which is also pertinent in bio-thermal processes. Abdul et al. [5] examined magneto-convective radiative nanofluid flow induced by a non-isothermal rotating cone with the combined influence of heat generation and chemical reaction. The behavior of Brinkman-Darcy-Forchheimer bioconvection nanofluid flow past a spinning non-isothermal cone with connected Soret and Dufour effects was investigated by Anjum et al. [6]. Peerusab et al. [7] used the Keller box method to examine how Darcy–Forchheimer drag affected nonlinear convection in Buongiorno nanofluids. A relative sensitivity analysis of magnetohydrodynamic nanofluid flow driven by Coriolis force and its contribution to enhancing turbine cooling performance was provided by Farooq et al. [8]. Farooq et al. [9] scrutinized the effects of the impact of Soret and Du on non-similar bioconvective MHD based micropolar nanofluid flow induced by a stretched sheet.

To further improve thermal performance, the concept of hybrid nanofluids has been proposed whereby two or more different nanoparticles, which are suspended in a single base fluid. Hybrid nanofluids have greater heat transfer possessions and enhanced thermophysical features as compared to nanofluids, and thus are more fruitful in more advanced thermal management systems. Initial experimental and theoretical attempts were made to make stable mixtures of various nanoparticles to maximize synergistic effects. The behavior of hybrid nanofluids has been extensively studied. Their thermophysical properties and how the hybridization of nanoparticles increases the potential of heat transport were examined by Sarkar et al. [10]. In the similar manner Yang et al. [11] gave an inclusive plan of the applications of hybrid nanofluids, comprising the environmental factors. Esfe et al. [12] accompanied inquiries on their use in machining processes and Takabi et al. [13] surveyed the laminar convective heat transfer using water-based suspensions of Al2O3 and Cu nanoparticles and showed substantial enhancements in thermal efficiency. The influence of velocity and thermal slip on MHD hybrid nanoliquid flow over a stretched surface under nth-order chemical reaction effects were examined by Lakshmi et al. [14].

Magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) flow that portrays the collaboration between conducting liquids and magnetic field has become an imperative area of study because of its applications in the many engineering and industrial technologies. Its consequence is particularly manifest in processes like metal casting, optical fiber production, fusion reactors, polymer extrusion, and crystal growth, where precise control of flow and heat transfer is essential. In recent years, a lot of research has been done on how MHD might improve thermal and flow properties under various physical situations. Babu et al. [15] focused on the complexities of nanofluid flow on the stretching surface under the influence of magnetic fields and thermal radiation. Venkateswarlu and Narayana [16] studied the flow of MHD on a porous flat plate, with slip boundary conditions to more effectively model realistic thermophysical behavior. Besides, Das et al. [17] investigated how viscous dissipation and Joule heating interact with the mixed convection MHD flow along an inclined surface and emphasized the role of energy interactions in a mixed convection system. Sadighi et al. [18] inspected the flow of CoFe2O4 and water nanofluid over an extending surface considering suction/injection processes and internal heating or cooling. Freidoonimehr et al. [19] contributed in MHD nanofluid flow, considering the case of flow towards a porous space. These initial studies have been built upon by the recent studies that have gradually developed to hybrid nanofluids, exploring their superior thermal conductivity and flow characteristics under the various geometries and multifaceted boundary conditions. In these developments, the increased importance of MHD hybrid nanofluid systems in enhancing high-efficiency thermal management technologies is underlined [20–32].

The conventional no-slip boundary condition which has been the cornerstone of fluid mechanics is no longer suitable in situations involving micro- and nanoscale flows or rarefied fluid regimes. When this happens slip boundary conditions are more realistic with the tangential velocity of the fluid at a solid surface proportional to the shear stress at the wall. Navier [33] was the first to develop this basic concept when he suggested the linear slip mathematical model to designate the collaboration between fluids and surfaces. This was later expanded on by Maxwell [34], who delivered an explanation in terms of a kinetic theory, which explained the velocity slip of a velocity gradient normal to the surface as well as molecular-level creep effects. The introduction of slip boundary conditions during the past years has greatly improved the accuracy of modeling complex fluid systems, especially in nanofluid and hybrid nanofluid flows. As an example, the axisymmetric radiative flow and heat transfer of a hybrid nanofluid were studied by Khan et al. [35] on a porous rotating surface that has the ability to stretch and shrink. In their work, they used the velocity slip condition of Maxwell, and temperature jump condition of Smoluchowski to more accurately capture microscale thermal fluid interactions. In a similar manner, Khashi et al. [36] dogged the consequence of slip and convective boundary conditions on the 3D hybrid nanofluid flowing over the stretching/shrinking surface, and establish that there were significant differences in the flow and heat transfer conditions.

Novelty and significance

Motivation of the current study is to advances the prior research by investigating three-dimensional stretching flow through the application of the Tiwari and Das [37], integrated with the enhanced thermophysical properties of hybrid nanofluids as proposed by Devi [42]. The current research reveals a notable gap in exploring the hybrid nanofluid over an elastic surface. To fill this gap, the present study provides a comprehensive detail of variable thermal conductivity impact on radiative magnetized tangent hyperbolic hybrid nanofluid flowing towards a rotating surface under motile microorganism, which has not been studied so for. The approach is unusual in that it concentrates on the combined impacts of complex boundary layer dynamics, convective boundary constraints, and velocity slip. Numerical solutions were computed using MATLAB’s bvp4c solver, with results expressed via similarity profiles in both tabular and graphical representations. The novelty of the key controlling parameters, specifically the velocity slip parameter and Biot number on friction factors, heat transfer rate, Sherwood number, and density number of motile microorganisms are elaborated in detail for both nanofluid and hybrid nanofluid scenarios. The current model is applicable for rotating machinery cooling and advanced heat exchanger; it also provides insights for thermal management in electric motor and porous media.

The resulting equations are then solved numerically using MATLAB tool along with shooting technique.

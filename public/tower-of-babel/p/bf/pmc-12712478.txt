Response to the Letter to the Editor Entitled “From Variant of Unknown Significance to Likely Pathogenic: Adult-Onset - Nephronophthisis Linked to NPHP4 p.T680M”
Authors: Jens Christian König, Mareike Dahmer-Heath, Martin Konrad
Published: 2025

The Author Replies: We thank Koç et al.1 for their letter. Herein, they describe a consanguineous family with 4 individuals affected by late-onset nephronophthisis (NPH) because of an NPHP4 variant. Their findings extend the clinical and molecular spectrum of NPHP4-associated NPH previously outlined by König et al.2 and others, while highlighting a broader point that in adults with unexplained renal insufficiency, a substantial proportion may have inherited disorders, with NPH remaining particularly underdiagnosed.

NPH typically follows an insidious course, with few symptoms, gradual decline in kidney function, and largely unremarkable urinalysis. Although impaired urinary concentrating is usually present from childhood, patients often consider this normal, delaying recognition. Compared with the more common NPHP1-associated cohort, NPHP4 variants have been linked to milder clinical trajectories, later onset of kidney failure, and infrequent extrarenal involvement. Such variability likely contributes to missed diagnosis and helps explain the growing number of adult-onset cases identified through genetic testing. Indeed, the broader application of genetic diagnostics in recent years has led to an increasing number of reports of adult-onset NPH, including cases associated with variants in NPHP1 and NPHP4.3, 4, 5

This report highlights the central role of molecular diagnostics in adults with unexplained, and particularly familial, chronic kidney disease. In patients presenting with advanced renal impairment but preserved diuresis and little or no proteinuria, hereditary tubulointerstitial disorders, including NPH and autosomal dominant tubulointerstitial kidney disease, should be considered. Definitive diagnosis, however, requires genetic confirmation. Beyond enabling accurate genetic counselling and reproductive guidance, molecular diagnosis has direct clinical relevance: it confirms the absence of recurrence risk after kidney transplantation and, where applicable, indicates the need for tailored monitoring for extrarenal manifestations.

In summary, the work of Koç et al.1 expands the phenotypic spectrum of NPHP4-related NPH and reinforces the importance of integrating genetic testing into the diagnostic evaluation for adults with unexplained chronic kidney disease.

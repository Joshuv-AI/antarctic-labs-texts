Catheter-Induced vs Spontaneous Plaque Rupture in a Patient With Acute Coronary Syndrome
Authors: Murat Gökalp, Semih Eren, Ayça Gümüşdağ, Özge Çebi, Gündüz Durmuş, Mehmet Baran Karataş
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPlaque rupture at the left main coronary artery (LMCA) ostium is a clinically important lesion that requires accurate assessment and tailored intervention.Case SummaryA 75 year-old woman with non–ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction underwent coronary angiography. Initial imaging suggested a catheter-induced dissection at the LMCA ostium. However, intravascular ultrasound (IVUS) revealed a lipid-rich plaque rupture. IVUS-guided percutaneous intervention was performed, with optimal expansion and apposition.DiscussionThis case illustrates the critical role of IVUS in differentiating between dissection and plaque rupture. IVUS provided detailed plaque characterization and ensured appropriate stent deployment, highlighting its superiority over angiography alone in complex LMCA lesions.Take-Home MessagesIVUS is essential for diagnosing and managing complex LMCA lesions. Early use of IVUS can clarify ambiguous angiographic findings and guide appropriate strategy.Key words: acute coronary syndrome, imaging, percutaneous coronary intervention

History of Presentation

A 75-year-old woman presented to the emergency department with chest pain. Her physical examination was notable for a blood pressure of 145/78 mm Hg and a heart rate of 71 beats/min. The electrocardiogram and transthoracic echocardiogram were both unremarkable. Her high-sensitivity troponin T level was elevated at 67 ng/L. The diagnosis of non–ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction was established, and she was taken to the catheterization laboratory.

Past Medical History

The patient had a history of hypertension, type 2 diabetes mellitus, and hyperlipidemia. She had no known coronary artery disease or prior cardiac intervention.

Differential Diagnosis

The differential diagnosis for the angiographic finding at the left main coronary artery (LMCA) ostium included 1) catheter-induced LMCA dissection, 2) spontaneous coronary artery dissection, 3) atherosclerotic plaque rupture, and 4) thrombus formation with endothelial trauma.

Investigations

A right femoral artery puncture was performed, and the LMCA was engaged using a 4.0 Judkins left diagnostic catheter. Initial views in the left caudal position (Video 1) did not reveal any critical lesions. However, during the second view in the right cranial position (Video 2), an image suggestive of a catheter-induced dissection at the left main coronary ostium was observed. The right coronary artery showed no atherosclerotic lesions, and the patient remained hemodynamically stable. Intravascular ultrasound (IVUS) was used to evaluate the lesion further. A 7-F left guiding catheter was used to re-engage the LMCA. Pullback IVUS from the left anterior descending artery (LAD) demonstrated an ulcerated, heterogeneous plaque with echolucent areas and no significant calcification, consistent with a lipid-rich lesion. Although the exact histologic content could not be confirmed by IVUS alone, the observed features aligned with previously described characteristics of fibrofatty plaque rupture1,2 (Video 3). The minimal lumen area was measured at 5.26 mm2 (Figure 1).

Management

A 4.0 × 13 mm drug-eluting stent was implanted in the LMCA, followed by postdilatation with a 5.0-mm noncompliant balloon. Poststent IVUS pullback of the LAD (Video 4) showed that the minimal stent area was 13.05 mm2 (Figure 2). Poststent IVUS pullback of the LAD showed complete lesion coverage, good apposition, and expansion of the stent, with no edge dissection noted. The procedure was concluded successfully.

Outcome and Follow-Up

The patient had an uneventful in-hospital course and was discharged with optimal medical therapy. At 1-year follow-up, she remained asymptomatic, with no emergency visits. She continues under regular medical observation.

Discussion

According to both European and American guidelines, intravascular imaging is mandatory for LMCA interventions.3,4 This case emphasizes the importance of IVUS in identifying and managing complex coronary lesions, particularly heterogeneous ulcerated plaque rupture at the LMCA ostium. Initially, angiography suggested catheter-induced dissection, a known complication of coronary angiography. However, IVUS revealed a plaque rupture with lipid-rich content rather than a dissection. This distinction is crucial, as the treatment strategies for dissection and atherosclerotic plaque rupture are entirely different. The presence of diabetes mellitus and hyperlipidemia are well-recognized risk factors for the development of lipid-rich plaques. These metabolic conditions promote endothelial dysfunction, inflammation, and lipid core expansion, which predispose to plaque rupture in high-risk coronary segments such as the LMCA.1

IVUS plays a vital role in detecting and guiding treatment for plaque rupture, as confirmed by several studies. Recent studies have shown that IVUS-guided percutaneous coronary intervention led to better outcomes for patients with left main coronary artery disease, especially those with complex lesions such as plaque ruptures or ulcerations.5 IVUS is particularly valuable in complex cases, offering superior resolution compared to conventional angiography and providing a detailed assessment of plaque composition and vessel morphology.

In this case, IVUS-guided intervention and the use of a drug-eluting stent ensured optimal stent expansion and apposition, minimizing the risk of restenosis and stent thrombosis. Postprocedural IVUS confirmed effective lesion coverage and good stent expansion, ensuring long-term procedural success.

Conclusions

IVUS is crucial for accurately diagnosing and guiding the treatment of complex coronary lesions such as plaque rupture, especially in high-risk areas such as the LMCA. This case highlights the significant role of IVUS in improving procedural outcomes and patient safety.

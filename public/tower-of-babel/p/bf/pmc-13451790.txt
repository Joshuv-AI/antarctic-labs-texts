Comment on “Deep learning model for identifying significant tricuspid regurgitation using standard 12-lead electrocardiogram”
Authors: Suresh Kumar Maddila, Vishal Bharadwaj Meruga, Phaneendra Kanakamedala
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We read with great interest the study by Chang et al [1]. developing an electrocardiogram (ECG)-based deep learning model to detect significant tricuspid regurgitation (TR).

Of particular concern is the external validation performance of the XGBoost comparator model reported in Table 2, which is not addressed in the discussion. While Model A's external sensitivity (0.644) was reasonable, the external validation sensitivity of the XGBoost model collapsed to 0.12, despite a specificity of 0.97 and an AUC of 0.71. A model that detects only 12% of true TR cases is clinically equivalent to near-complete failure as a screening tool, regardless of its discrimination statistic [2]; however, this finding has not been discussed or explained.

This is important because the paper's stated translational goal is screening utility, identifying significant TR using a widely available, inexpensive test. The clinical value of a screening tool depends overwhelmingly on its sensitivity, as missed cases receive no further workup [3]. The unexplained divergence between the AUC and sensitivity in this comparator Model A raises the question of whether threshold selection, calibration drift, or population differences between the internal and external cohorts produced this collapse [4], and whether Model A could be similarly vulnerable to specific population shifts not captured by AUC alone [5].

Clarifying why the XGBoost comparator failed so dramatically on sensitivity, despite reasonable discrimination, would meaningfully strengthen confidence in the robustness of the primary deep learning models proposed for clinical screening.

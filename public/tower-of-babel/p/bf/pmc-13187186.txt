Theoretical study on the pressure relief range of the upper protected coal seam in protective layer mining
Authors: Keliang Zhan, Bao Zhang, Mengjun Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe mining of protective layers is recognized as an economical and effective measure for pressure relief, anti-erosion and prevention of coal and gas outbursts. To explore the influence law of protective layer mining on the pressure relief range of the protected coal seam, this paper takes the No. 6 Mine of Pingdingshan Tian’an Coal Industry Co., Ltd. as an example. Through theoretical analysis, the theoretical pressure relief protection range of the upper protected layer caused by the mining of the protective layer of the coal seam is studied, and the theoretical pressure relief angle along the tendency, the theoretical pressure relief angle along the strike and the theoretical pressure relief range along the vertical direction are determined. The results show that: (1) The factors influencing the protective effect during the mining of the protective layer include the properties of the rocks between layers, the duration of the protective effect, and the relative interlayer distance. (2) When the lower protection range of the protected layer is 76.32 m away from the inner roadway of the Wu9−10-32020 machine, the inner offset distance is 16.23 m. The upper protection range is offset from the wind tunnel of Wu9−10-32020. When the layer spacing is 76.32 m, the offset distance is 10.73 m. (3) The protection range of the protected layer is offset from the cut eye and the stop-sampling line of Wu9−10-32020. When the layer spacing is 76.32 m, the offset distance is 44.07 m. (4) The maximum distance between the protective layer Wu9−10 coal seam and the protected layer Ding5−6 coal seam is 73.62 m < 100 m < 122 m, which is within the effective pressure relief range. It not only meets the pressure relief requirements for mining the protective layer but also cannot damage the mining conditions of the protected layer.Keywords: Protective layer mining, Coal seam pressure relief, Protection mechanism, Theoretical pressure relief angle, Pressure relief range, Field industrial experimentSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Environmental sciences

Introduction

With the rapid development of the national economy, coal resources, as an important energy source supporting the rapid development of the national economy, are in increasing demand. The mining of coal resources in our country has gradually shifted from shallow to deep mining1–3. However, with the continuous increase in the depth of coal resource exploitation, coal mining is affected by multiple factors such as high ground pressure, high osmotic pressure, high ground temperature, gas, and frequent mining dynamic stress. Deep coal and rock are prone to disastrous events such as impact deformation damage and gas outburst4–10. Therefore, in the process of coal mining, how to reduce the occurrence of disastrous events such as rock burst and gas outburst is one of the key factors for achieving safe and efficient coal mining.

At present, the mining of protective layers is recognized as an economical and effective measure for pressure relief, anti-erosion and prevention of coal and gas outbursts. Both at home and abroad, there have been in-depth studies on the pressure relief laws of the protected layers during the mining of protective layers, and fruitful research results have been achieved11–18. Liu explored the pressure relief effect of the protective layer under different mining parameters, and systematically analyzed the influence of mining height, layer spacing, and working face length on the vertical stress distribution law of the protected coal seam and the pressure relief degree of the floor. It is found that the increase in mining height and the reduction in layer spacing will lead to an improvement in pressure relief effect19. Wang conducted a study on the pressure relief and anti-impact protection effect of the protective layer mining in Hongyang No. 3 Mine through microseismic monitoring. They found that the mining of the protective layer significantly reduced the microseismic events in the roof and coal seam during the mining process of the protected layer, lowered the risk of impact on the working face, and at the same time, the mining of the protective layer had a stress reduction and pressure relief effect on the protected layer20. Liu studied the spatio-temporal evolution law of surrounding rock stress during the mining of upper and lower protective layers in coal seam groups and found that dual mining of upper and lower protective layers can effectively reduce the stress of the protected layer and increase the pressure relief range21. Qin conducted a study on the pressure relief protection range of the upper protective layer by means of numerical simulation calculation and on-site investigation. It was found that the vertical stress of the protected layer was symmetrically distributed along the central axis, and the tendency pressure relief area was nearly elliptical. Moreover, 30 to 60 m behind the working face of the protective layer was the optimal area for pressure relief and gas extraction of the protected layer22. Yuan employed physical experiments, numerical simulations, and field applications to study the stress variation law of the protected area during the advancement of the upper protective layer mining. It was found that after the upper protective layer was mined, the pressure relief degree of the protected layer was 50%, the expansion deformation was 0.391%, and the gas concentration in the return air of the working face of the protected layer decreased to 0.35%. When the protected coal seam is overlaid with hard and thick rock layers, the combination of upper protective layer mining and high-position directional long borehole drainage can achieve ideal pressure relief, permeability enhancement and gas drainage effects23. Gao conducted research on the evolution law of overburden fractures in long-distance lower protective layer mining, analyzed the distribution characteristics of the “two zones” on the roof and the gas flow law after long-distance protective layer mining, discussed the movement and stress distribution law of overburden on the roof in the working face mining, and designed a long-distance protective layer mining scheme24. Yang studied the pressure relief and permeability enhancement effect of long-distance mining of the lower protective layer and the gas drainage scheme. They found that the mining of the protective layer would produce longitudinal interlayer fractures extending to the protected layer, which would reduce the coal seam pressure and accelerate the gas flow, resulting in a significant pressure relief and permeability enhancement effect in the protected layer25. Ma studied the factors influencing the protection effect of long-distance lower protective layer mining, analyzing the protection effect of the key layer’s position in the overlying rock, the length of the working face, the inclination angle of the coal seam, and the thickness of the mined coal seam on the protected coal seam during long-distance lower protective layer mining. The closer the key layer is to the protected coal seam, the longer the working face is, the smaller the coal seam dip angle is, and the thicker the mined coal seam is, the better the protection effect of the protected coal seam will be26. Zhang studied the multiple pressure relief effects of medium and distant coal seam groups under the downward mining sequence by combining numerical simulation with physical similarity simulation. They found that the mining of the protective layer had a significant pressure relief effect on the lower protected layer. The superimposed pressure relief effect caused by the multiple mining of the protective layer of the coal seam group led to obvious superimposed changes in the displacement field of the overburden layer27. Wang investigated the influence law of the mining of the upper protective layer in close-range inclined coal seam groups on the left roadway along the void. It was found that when the working face of the upper protective layer is arranged above the left roadway along the void of the protected working face, it can better relieve the pressure on the surrounding rock of the left roadway along the void and improve the safety of the left roadway along the void during its service life28. Therefore, the mining of protective layers can play a very good protective and pressure-relieving role for the protected coal seams, and the impact of different engineering environmental protection layer mining on the protected coal seams will also vary.

In recent years, with the intensification of deep coal resource development, research on protective layer mining technology has ushered in new breakthroughs and challenges. The latest special research on Pingmei No.6 Mine shows that long-distance lower protective layer mining can reduce the maximum gas pressure of the protected coal seam by 0.52 MPa, expand the permeability coefficient by 200 times, and the maximum expansion deformation rate reaches 49.05‰. However, there are significant differences in the dip and strike pressure relief angles (83° for the upper dip, 77° for the lower dip, and 60° for the strike), and the quantitative accuracy of the protection range decreases significantly when the interlayer distance exceeds 70 m. China Coal Research Institute proposed the stress release rate as an indicator for judging the pressure relief degree, and found that the pressure relief degree at 10–30 m of the deep floor can reach 44%-85%, but the stress recovery rate accelerates with the increase of depth, making it difficult for traditional static parameters to adapt to the dynamic mining process. In terms of research on complex geological conditions, the practice of long-distance protective layer mining in Qianjin Coal Mine revealed that the pressure relief effect of the protected coal seam is directly related to the height of the “two zones” of the overlying rock. When the coal seam is in the bending subsidence zone, the extraction concentration drops to 3.8% within 65 days, but there are significant differences in the upper and lower caving angles (69°, 62°) compared with shallow mining areas, verifying the regulatory role of geological conditions on protection parameters. In addition, the influence range of the residual coal pillars from the upper protective layer mining of the Wu8 coal seam in Pingmei No.6 Mine reaches 8.5 m. Although the impact on the mining of the protected coal seam is small, the stress concentration factor in the coal pillar area still reaches 3.5, indicating that the delineation of the protection range under multi-factor coupling needs to consider the influence of residual structures .

Despite certain progress in existing research, there are still three core deficiencies: first, the limitation of applicable scenarios. Most achievements focus on medium-shallow or conventional interlayer distance (< 60 m) mining areas. For deep gently inclined coal seam groups such as Pingmei No.6 Mine with a depth exceeding 800 m and an interlayer distance of 68–76 m, there is a lack of quantitative research on the protection range considering the coupled effect of high ground stress and high gas pressure. The adaptability of existing pressure relief angle parameters (such as 60°-66° for the strike) in kilometer-deep mines has not been fully verified ; second, the fragmented research on influencing factors. Existing literature mainly focuses on the impact of a single factor (mining thickness, advancing speed), while lacking systematic analysis of the evolution law of the protection range under the coupled action of multiple factors including “interlayer rock properties - protective layer parameters - geological structure”, especially ignoring the regulatory effect of the characteristics of deep rock layers such as fast stress recovery and short fracture development cycle on the pressure relief effect ; third, the disconnection in engineering transformation. Traditional research relies heavily on numerical simulation or laboratory experiments, and there are few studies combining on-site measurement and theory for the specific occurrence conditions of Pingmei No.6 Mine (medium-hard roof, small normal faults development), resulting in deviations between the theoretically calculated protection range and the on-site actual situation (such as inner offset distance of 8.7–16.4 m), which is difficult to directly guide the layout of extraction boreholes and the optimization of mining schemes .

Based on this, this paper takes the Wu9-10-32020 working face of Pingmei No.6 Mine as the engineering carrier, aiming at the occurrence characteristics of deep gently inclined outburst-prone coal seam groups. Through the combination of theoretical analysis and on-site measurement, key parameters such as pressure relief angles and protection range under multi-factor coupling are quantified, making up for the quantitative gap of existing studies in deep specific scenarios and providing directly applicable engineering basis for similar mines.

Theory of the protective effect of protective layer mining

The mechanism of action of the mining protective layer

After the protective layer is mined, the surrounding rock strata and coal seams move and deform towards the goaf, causing the formation stress to be redistributed. A natural collapse arch is formed above the goaf, and the pressure is transmitted to the rock strata outside the goaf to bear29. For this reason, it will have a mining dynamic impact on the coal seams (including the protected layer) and rock strata around the mining layer, and the gas dynamic parameters of the protected layer will undergo significant changes. The failure, movement and stress distribution of overlying rocks are shown in Fig. 2.

Schematic diagram of overlying rock failure, movement and stress distribution.

Within the pressure relief zone delineated by the rock pressure relief angle, the formation stress decreases, and expansion deformation occurs in the direction perpendicular to the coal seam plane. Not only do new cracks form within coal seams and rock layers, but the existing ones also open up and expand, increasing the permeability of the coal seams by tens to hundreds of times. Due to the vertical expansion deformation of the rock layer between the protective layer and the protected layer after the rock layer is depressurized, some of the rock layers in the parallel layers contract and deform, causing the original rock layer to break through, providing a channel for the desorbed gas to flow into the goaf.

The pressure relief of the protected layer enhances the gas emission capacity. The continuous outflow of gas leads to a decrease in gas pressure and an increase in the mechanical strength of coal. The pressure relief effect serves as the basis for changes in other factors and plays a decisive role. However, when the vertical distance between layers is large, it is unfavorable for gas emission, so it should be combined with gas drainage. When the protective layer is exploited in combination with gas extraction, once the stress state, gas dynamic parameters and mechanical properties of the protected layer undergo significant changes, it is impossible to restore them to their original state. Therefore, the protective effect is an irreversible process and will not completely disappear over time.

For the depressurized gas flow, there are “three horizontal zones” above the goafe: the initial depressurized zone with increased permeability and flow, the fully depressurized zone with high permeability and high flow, and the zone with reduced permeability and flow after ground pressure recovery, as shown in Fig. 3. This “horizontal three zones” extend vertically at a certain angle to the curved zone. According to the tangential stress distribution characteristics of the working face, it can be seen that the stress reduction zone can be divided into the initial pressure relief increased permeability and flow zone and the fully depressurized high permeability and high flow zone, while the stress recovery zone is the ground pressure recovery reduced permeability and flow zone.

Schematic Diagram of Gas Flow Zoning in Overlying Strata. ①—Initial pressure relief zone with increased permeability and gas flow; ②—Fully pressure-relieved zone with high permeability and gas flow; ③—Ground pressure recovery zone with reduced permeability and gas flow.

Due to the large distance between the protected layers at a long distance and the protective layer, the amount of depressurized gas from the protected layer entering the protective layer’s mining face through mining fractures is relatively small. If the depressified gas in the protected layer cannot be effectively extracted, once the ground stress recovers, it will be re-adsorbed by the coal seam, causing the gas pressure in the coal seam to rise again and resulting in the protected coal seam still having the risk of coal and gas outburst. During the process of protective layer mining, the “extraction window period” should be effectively utilized. During the active period of gas flow, the depressurized gas should be effectively extracted to eliminate the potential dangers originally present in the protruding coal seam to the greatest extent.

The principle of anti-protrusion in the mining protective layer

Domestic and international investigation data prove that after the protective layer is mined, the stress and deformation state of the protected layer, the coal structure and the dynamic parameters of gas undergo significant changes. From the perspective of the time when changes occur, the pressure relief effect appears first. Some pressure relief processes even start 10 to 20 m ahead of the working surface of the protective layer, usually behind the working surface. The dynamic parameters of gas change significantly only when the speed of expansion and deformation accelerates. Therefore, the sequence can be expressed as follows: Mining the protective layer→rock stratum movement→pressure relief of the protected layer→reduction of ground stress and expansion deformation of the coal seam→Increase of permeability and desorption of gas→Increase of gas emission capacity of the coal (rock) layer→Increase of gas emission and gas flow in boreholes→reduction of gas pressure→Decrease of gas content→improvement of mechanical strength of coal→further reduction of stress→elimination or reduction of the risk of outburst. The principle of the protective layer in preventing coal and gas outbursts can be shown in Fig. 4.

Schematic diagram of coal and gas outburst prevention by protective layer mining.

The above analysis indicates that although the protective function of the protective layer is the result of the combined effect of pressure relief and gas emission, the pressure relief effect is the basis for changes in other factors, and pressure relief is primary and decisive. Therefore, as long as the protruding layer is subjected to a certain pressure relief effect, the coal structure and gas dynamic parameters will change in the above sequence. When the interlayer distance is relatively far (but within the effective vertical distance between layers) and there are hard rock layers in the middle, the pressure relief of the protruding coal seam will undoubtedly increase the coal seam and its permeability, but the natural discharge of gas will be more difficult. From the perspective of the changes in the first two factors, both are conducive to eliminating protrusion. Therefore, even if the risk of protrusion cannot be completely eliminated, it will still be reduced to some extent30.

The principle of anti-protrusion in the mining protective layer

Properties of interlayer rocks

Some foreign scholars, based on certain observational data and some simulation test results, believe that hard rock layers act as a barrier for pressure relief. The investigation of the protective effect of the protective layer at a long distance shows that the depressurization process of the protected layer starts 17 to 20 m ahead of the protective layer’s mining face and accelerates the expansion and deformation rate about 20 to 40 m behind the working face (0.3 to 0.5 times the layer spacing). At a distance of 80 to 120 m behind the working face (1 to 1.5 times the layer spacing), the large expansion deformation value reaches 3.1‰ to 5.8‰, and then gradually decreases, but still maintains the apparent pressure state. 300 m behind the working face, the expansion deformation value is 2.4‰ to 4‰. This indicates that under the condition of an interlayer vertical distance of 70 to 90 m, even if there is a hard flint limestone 16 to 18 m thick between layers (with a compressive strength of ≥ 300 MPa), it will not have a barrier pressure relief effect.

The duration of protective effect

The time factor has two kinds of influences on the protective effect; One advantage is that after the protective layer is shut down, the movement and deformation of the rock layer and coal seam will continue for a certain period of time. Therefore, after a certain period of time, the protective belt can be partially expanded. Another one is unfavorable. After several years of mining the protective layer, the stress gradually recovers. Some researchers believe that the protective effect will disappear over time.

The latest domestic research results show that for close-range or medium-range protective layers, the pressure relief and gas discharge of the protected layer are relatively thorough. With the extension of time, the gas state of the protected layer and the mechanical properties of the coal cannot return to their original state, and thus the protective effect will not disappear with the extension of time. There are both domestic and foreign cases where no protrusion occurs in the protected layer after 6 to 12 years of mining.

For long-distance protective layers, although the vertical distance between layers can be as high as 80 m, the protected layer still maintains a certain degree of expansion deformation (1‰ to 2‰) after being collected for 2.5 years. Based on the trend of the curve changes, it is likely that it will remain in a depressurized state for a considerable period of time. Considering the simultaneous extraction and pressure relief of gas, the dynamic parameters and mechanical properties of the protected coal seam gas cannot be restored to their original state, and the stress, which has been reduced due to gas emissions, cannot be restored to its original state either. Therefore, when mining the long-distance protective layer in combination with gas extraction, its protective effect is also an irreversible process. The protective effect will not disappear with the extension of time, but the ground stress borne by the protective layer will increase over time.

Relative interlayer distance

Practical experience has shown that the protective effect is closely related to the thickness of the protective layer and the distance between layers. Therefore, when studying the protective effect, the thickness of the protective layer and the vertical distance between layers must be considered in combination. The concept of relative interlayer distance is introduced, that is, the ratio of the vertical distance H between layers to the layer thickness (or construction height) M, and R = H/M. Obviously, the smaller the relative interlayer distance, the more sufficient the protective effect, and vice versa.

According to the experimental research, it was found that when mining the protective layer, the protected layer never protrudes when R < 130, and in most cases, it does not protrude when R ≥ 130. In some cases (geological damage zones), protrusion has also occurred. Therefore, the relative inter-layer distance can be used as a basis for selecting the protective layer, that is, R < 75 (upper protective layer) or R < 130 (lower protective layer).

Definition and Source Basis of Parameter “R”

Definition and Source Basis of Parameter “R”

The definition and calculation method of the relative interlayer distance (R) are not independently proposed in this study. Its core basis originates from two aspects: ① Explicit provisions in industry standards: Appendix A of the Technical Specifications for Protective Layer Mining (AQ/T 1050–2008) identifies the relative interlayer distance as a core indicator for protective layer selection, specifying that for lower protective layer mining in gently inclined coal seams, the requirement R < 130 must be met, and for upper protective layer mining, R < 75. This standard provides an authoritative basis for the application of parameter R; ② Academic research consensus: This parameter has been maturely applied in domestic and foreign studies on protective layer mining. Numerous scholars have verified its rationality through on-site investigations, numerical simulations, and other methods22,25,26. For instance, Ma et al.26 confirmed through measured data in their research on long-distance lower protective layer mining that the R value can effectively quantify the coupled influence of interlayer distance and protective layer thickness, providing key support for evaluating protection effects.

(2)Calculation Data and Process of R in This Study

Calculation Data and Process of R in This Study

The calculation of the R value in this study is entirely based on on-site measured geological and mining data from Pingmei No.6 Mine, with details as follows: ① Vertical interlayer distance (H): Derived from the actual interlayer distance between the Wu9-10 protective layer and the Ding5-6 protected layer. According to the mine’s exploration boreholes and working face excavation data, the vertical interlayer distance ranges from 68.27 m to 76.32 m. To ensure conservative and reliable results, the maximum value of 76.32 m is adopted for calculation; ② Thickness of the protective layer (M): Obtained from the measured mining height of the Wu9−10-32020 working face. Field mining records indicate that the average thickness of the coal seam in this working face is 2.8 m, and no top or bottom coal was discarded during mining, so the mining height is consistent with the coal seam thickness (M = 2.8 m); ③ Calculation result: Substituting the above data into the definition of relative interlayer distance, the R value in the study area is calculated to be approximately 27.26. This value is much smaller than the critical threshold for lower protective layers (R < 130) specified in the Technical Specifications for Protective Layer Mining (AQ/T 1050–2008), thus confirming that the Wu9−10 coal seam is technically feasible to serve as a lower protective layer.

(3)Verification and Reliability of the R Value

Verification and Reliability of the R Value

The reliability of the conclusions derived based on the R value is further verified through the “Field Industrial Experimental Study” in Chap. 5. Experiments show that the residual gas pressure of the Ding5−6 coal seam is ≤ 0.44 MPa, and the expansion deformation is ≥ 10.55‰, both meeting the effective pressure relief standards specified in the Detailed Rules for the Prevention and Control of Coal and Gas Outbursts. This confirms that the protection effect evaluation conclusion corresponding to R = 27.26 is consistent with on-site actual conditions, further validating the scientificity of applying this parameter.

Experimental studies have shown that for lower protective layer mining, the protected layer never experiences outbursts when R < 130; when R ≥ 130, outbursts rarely occur, but may happen in specific cases (e.g., geological damage zones). Therefore, the relative interlayer distance can be used as a criterion for protective layer selection, i.e., R < 75 for upper protective layers or R < 130 for lower protective layers.

Selection basis of protective layer

The Wu9−10 coal seam was selected as the protective layer based on three key criteria:

Stable thickness: The average thickness is 2.8 m (ranging from 1.70 to 3.60 m) with a recoverability index of 0.95, belonging to a relatively stable and fully recoverable seam;

Stable thickness: The average thickness is 2.8 m (ranging from 1.70 to 3.60 m) with a recoverability index of 0.95, belonging to a relatively stable and fully recoverable seam;

Reasonable interlayer spacing: The vertical distance between the Wu9−10 and Ding5−6 coal seams is 68.27 to 76.32 m, which is less than the effective protective vertical distance of 100 m for gently inclined lower protective layers (Technical Specifications for Protective Layer Mining, AQ 1050–2008);

Reasonable interlayer spacing: The vertical distance between the Wu9−10 and Ding5−6 coal seams is 68.27 to 76.32 m, which is less than the effective protective vertical distance of 100 m for gently inclined lower protective layers (Technical Specifications for Protective Layer Mining, AQ 1050–2008);

Qualified relative interlayer distance: The relative interlayer distance R = H/M (H = 76.32 m, maximum interlayer spacing; M = 2.8 m, mining thickness of Wu9−10) is calculated as 27.26, which is less than the critical value of 130 for lower protective layers. This meets the selection requirement that R < 13026, ensuring sufficient pressure relief effect.

Qualified relative interlayer distance: The relative interlayer distance R = H/M (H = 76.32 m, maximum interlayer spacing; M = 2.8 m, mining thickness of Wu9−10) is calculated as 27.26, which is less than the critical value of 130 for lower protective layers. This meets the selection requirement that R < 13026, ensuring sufficient pressure relief effect.

Field Industrial experimental study

Experimental objectives

Taking the Wu9−10-32020 working face of Pingdingshan Tian’an Coal Industry Co., Ltd. No.6 Mine as the research carrier, this field industrial experiment aims to: ① Verify the variation laws of gas parameters (pressure and content) in the upper protected Ding5−6 coal seam during the mining of the Wu9−10 protective layer through on-site testing, and quantify the pressure relief effect; ② Monitor the expansion deformation characteristics of the Ding5−6 coal seam under the mining influence of the protective layer to determine the spatial scope of effective pressure relief; ③ Analyze the efficiency of pressure relief gas extraction and verify the engineering applicability of the theoretical pressure relief angle and protection scope; ④ Provide measured data support for the optimization of protective layer mining technology under similar geological conditions.

Experimental site and geological background

The experimental area is located in the Wu9−10-32020 working face on the west wing of the No.3 Level Wu2 Mining Area of Pingmei No.6 Mine and the corresponding area of the upper Ding5−6 coal seam. The underground elevation of the working face ranges from − 759 m to -846 m, and the ground elevation ranges from + 150 m to + 170 m. It is designed with a strike length of 1939 m, a dip length of 300 m, and mines the Wu9−10 coal seam (average thickness of 2.8 m) with an average dip angle of 8°, belonging to a gently inclined coal seam.

The vertical distance between the Wu9−10 coal seam and the upper Ding5−6 coal seam is 68.27 ~ 76.32 m (maximum 76.32 m). The Ding5−6 coal seam has an average thickness of 3.43 m, being an unstable mostly minable coal seam with an original gas pressure of 0.75 ~ 1.06 MPa and an original gas content of 5.3164 ~ 6.3391 m³/t, which is an outburst-prone coal seam. The Wu9−10 coal seam is a non-outburst-prone coal seam, whose roof and floor are mainly composed of fine-grained sandstone and sandy mudstone (the immediate roof has a compressive strength of 38.93 MPa, and the main roof has a compressive strength of 46.46 MPa). The geological structure is dominated by small normal faults, with a total of 72 exposed faults, which have little impact on the experiment.

Experimental design and layout

Overall experimental scheme

The experiment adopts a comprehensive “borehole testing + deformation monitoring + extraction statistics” scheme, covering two stages before and after protective layer mining: ① Before mining: Arrange original parameter testing boreholes to measure the original gas pressure and content of the Ding5-6 coal seam; ② During mining: Arrange residual parameter testing boreholes and expansion deformation monitoring points to track parameter changes under mining influence; ③ After mining: Statistically analyze gas extraction data and verify the protection effect combined with test results.

Arranged in the Wu8-32020 machine lane and the machine lane high-position lane, with 1 borehole per group at 20 m intervals along the strike of the lane. A total of 240 cross-layer extraction boreholes are constructed, with the final holes all located in the middle of the Ding5−6 coal seam. The borehole dip angles range from 38° to 50°, the lengths range from 88 m to 94 m, and the axis of the boreholes forms an angle of 30°~45° with the coal seam plane to ensure coverage of the entire protection scope.

Layout of expansion deformation monitoring points

Three monitoring points (P1, P2, P3) are arranged in the Wu8-32020 machine lane high-position lane, with specific positions as follows:

P1: Located at 546 m from the cut of Wu9−10-32020, the final hole of the borehole is 8 m inside the theoretical protection boundary of the Ding5−6 coal seam, with a borehole dip angle of 42°, a length of 95 m, and a sealing depth of 68 m.

P2: Located at 1321 m from the cut of Wu9−10-32020, with the same layout parameters as P1 to ensure the symmetry of monitoring positions.

P3: Located at 1693 m from the cut of Wu9−10-32020, with the same layout parameters as P1.

Monitoring coverage: Each monitoring point covers a 300 m length interval along the strike of the lane, from “80m inside the monitoring point to 220m outside the monitoring point”, to fully capture the deformation process before and after the mining of the protective layer.

Layout of gob pipe extraction

Two upper corner extraction pipelines (diameter Φ200mm) are laid on one side of the Wu9−10-32020 air lane wall, with the pipeline port 5 m away from the working face coal wall, moving forward synchronously with the advancement of the working face. One gob extraction pipeline (diameter Φ150 mm) is arranged at the rear of the machine lane, with the port extending 15 m into the gob. Negative pressure extraction is adopted (negative pressure ≥ 13 kPa), and monitoring devices are installed at key nodes of the pipeline to record extraction concentration and flow data in real time.

Experimental equipment and testing methods

Gas pressure measurement

Equipment: ZMB-50/70 electric grouting pump, Y-100 pressure gauge (range 0 ~ 6.0 MPa, accuracy class 1.0), bag-type sealing device, Φ25mm pressure measuring tube.

Method: Implemented in accordance with “Direct Determination Method of Coal Seam Gas Pressure in Underground Coal Mines” (AQ/T 1047–2007). Bag grouting is used for sealing (sealing depth 45 ~ 73 m) to ensure that the sealed section is isolated from the air interference of the lane wall. The passive pressure measurement method is adopted, with an observation period of ≥ 30 days. When the pressure fluctuation is ≤ 0.02 MPa for 3 consecutive days, it is regarded as a stable value and recorded.

Gas content measurement

Equipment: DGC direct gas content determination device, sealed coal sample can (volume 1 L), aneroid barometer (model DYM3), constant temperature box (temperature control accuracy ± 1℃).

Method: Carried out in accordance with “Direct Determination Method of Coal Seam Gas Content Underground” (GB/T 23250 − 2009). When collecting coal samples on site, gangue is removed to ensure the coal sample weight is ≥ 380 g. After coring, the sample is immediately put into a sealed can and the lid is tightened to avoid gas leakage. Underground desorption is conducted for 30 min, and the desorption volume in the first 30 min is recorded once per minute. Subsequently, the sample is sent to the laboratory for atmospheric pressure natural desorption (lasting 2 h) and crushing desorption (crushed to particle size ≤ 0.2 mm), and the total gas content is calculated combined with proximate analysis data.

Expansion deformation measurement

Equipment: Special coal seam expansion deformation meter (range 0 ~ 50 mm, accuracy 0.01 mm), anchor-type base point fixator, displacement sensor, automatic data acquisition instrument (sampling frequency 1 time per hour).

Method: Anchor-type base point fixators are installed on the roof and floor of the Ding5−6 coal seam through boreholes respectively to ensure firm coupling between the fixator and the coal-rock mass. High-strength steel wire ropes are used to connect the roof and floor base points with the external displacement sensor. Monitoring is started when the protective layer working face advances to 50 m in front of the monitoring point. Data is manually calibrated once every 2 days in the early stage, and 4 times a day after the working face passes the monitoring point. The coal seam expansion deformation is calculated through the relative displacement of the roof and floor (deformation = displacement/coal seam thickness × 1000‰).

Gas extraction parameter monitoring

Equipment: Infrared gas concentration sensor (range 0 ~ 100%, accuracy ± 1%), orifice flowmeter (range 0 ~ 100 m³/min), negative pressure sensor (range 0 ~ 100 kPa), data logger.

Method: One group of monitoring points is arranged every 50 m along the extraction pipeline. Data is statistically analyzed on the 5th, 15th, and 25th of each month to calculate the monthly pure extraction volume (pure extraction volume = extraction flow × extraction concentration × time). Combined with the gas reserves in the protected area of the Ding5−6 coal seam (12.653 million m³), the extraction rate is calculated (extraction rate = cumulative pure extraction volume/gas reserves × 100%).

Experimental verification conclusions

The Wu9−10 coal seam, as a protective layer, has a significant pressure relief effect on the Ding5−6 coal seam: the residual gas pressure is ≤ 0.44 MPa, the residual gas content is ≤ 3.4871 m³/t, the expansion deformation is ≥ 10.55‰, and the extraction rate is 33.0%, all meeting the relevant requirements of the “Detailed Rules for the Prevention and Control of Coal and Gas Outbursts” and the “Technical Specifications for Protective Layer Mining”.

The theoretically calculated protection scope is verified by experiments: the inner offset distance on the air lane side in the dip direction is 10.73 m, the inner offset distance on the machine lane side is 16.23 m, the inner offset distance of the cut and stoping line in the strike direction is 44.07 m, and the effective vertical protection distance is ≤ 122 m, which is completely consistent with the on-site measured effective pressure relief scope.

Experiments confirm that the interlayer rock properties (sandy mudstone + fine-grained sandstone, hard rock content < 50%), protective layer thickness (2.8 m), and interlayer distance (68.27 ~ 76.32 m) are the key factors affecting the protection effect, which is consistent with the theoretical analysis conclusions, providing measured basis for the parameter design of protective layers under similar geological conditions.

Limitations and assumptions

This study adopts theoretical analysis combined with field industrial experiments to determine the pressure relief range and pressure relief angles of the protected coal seam. However, several limitations and assumptions should be clarified to improve the scientific rigor and generalizability of the conclusions.

First, this study assumes a uniform coal seam dip angle and approximately horizontal coal seam occurrence, which is consistent with the actual conditions of Wu9-10-32020 working face. If applied to steeply inclined or structurally complex coal seams, the protection range and offset distances may change significantly.

Second, the theoretical calculation adopts idealized rock mechanical behavior and simplified strata structure, ignoring the spatial heterogeneity of rock strength and local fault disturbance. In actual engineering, local structures may weaken or locally enhance the pressure relief effect.

Third, the pressure relief angles used in this paper are mainly derived from specifications and theoretical calculations, and on-site measured pressure relief angles are not obtained due to construction and monitoring conditions. The theoretical values are verified by gas parameters and deformation data, but direct measured data can further improve accuracy.

Fourth, the conclusions are established under the specific geological conditions of Pingmei No. 6 Mine: interlayer spacing 68–76 m, mining depth > 800 m, hard rock content < 50%, and long-distance lower protective layer mining. The quantitative parameters (e.g., offset distances of 16.23 m, 10.73 m, 44.07 m) are highly reliable for similar deep gently inclined outburst-prone coal seams, but need to be corrected accordingly for other mining and geological conditions.

These limitations do not affect the correctness of the main conclusions of this paper, but need to be fully considered when extending the research results to other mines and geological conditions.

Conclusions

After mining the protective layer, the surrounding rock strata and coal seams move and deform toward the goaf, leading to the redistribution of formation stress. A natural caving arch is formed above the goaf, and the pressure is transferred to the rock strata outside the goaf, thereby exerting a pressure relief effect on the protected coal seam and enhancing gas emission capacity. Field measurements indicate that the vertical stress in the pressure relief zone of the Ding5−6 coal seam is fully released, and its permeability is increased by tens of times compared with the original state, creating favorable conditions for gas extraction.

The protection effect of protective layer mining is influenced by the coupling of multiple factors, with core key factors including: interlayer rock properties (the immediate roof is sandy mudstone with an average compressive strength of 38.93 MPa, the main roof is fine-grained sandstone with an average compressive strength of 46.46 MPa, and the hard rock content is < 50%), protection duration (the long-distance protected coal seam still maintains an expansion deformation of 1‰~2‰ 2.5 years after protective layer mining), and relative interlayer distance (the relative interlayer distance R in the study area is approximately 27.26, which is much smaller than the critical value of 130 for lower protective layers).

Association of Charlson Comorbidity Index and Charlson Fracture Index With Surgical Complications in Distal Radius Fractures: A Retrospective Review
Authors: Amber M Parker, Nicholas Genovese, Alexy Ilchuk, Carl M Harper, Mario Keko, Monica M Shoji, Tamara D Rozental
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTo assess and compare the associative value of comorbidity fracture-specific index (CFI) versus the Charlson comorbidity index (CCI) in identifying patients at risk for early complications after open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF) of distal radius fractures (DRFs).MethodsWe performed a single-center retrospective cohort study including patients ≥40 years who underwent ORIF for closed DRF using a volar locking plate with a minimum of 1 year follow-up. CCI and CFI scores were calculated based on comorbidities over the prior 15 years. The primary outcome was 90-day postoperative complications. We used logistic regression to compare CCI and CFI and their association with complications, adjusting for age, sex, smoking status, and ethnicity. We used Pearson correlation and the intraclass correlation coefficient to assess the relationship and agreement between CCI and CFI.ResultsAmong the 317 patients, 16.7% (n = 53) experienced complications. There were no statistically significant differences in CCI or CFI in patients with and without complications. After adjusting for covariates, each increase by 1 in CCI and CFI, increased the odds of complications; however, these effects were not statistically significant. CCI and CFI were strongly correlated and showed high agreement.ConclusionsCCI and CFI scores did not significantly differ between patients with and without complications after DRF and were not significantly associated with postoperative complications after adjusting for covariates. The strong correlation and agreement between CCI and CFI suggest they capture a similar comorbidity burden in this patient population. Neither CCI nor CFI demonstrated good discriminative ability in identifying patients at risk for early complications after ORIF of DRF.Type of study/level of evidencePrognostic IV.Key words: CCI, CFI, Comorbidities, Complications, Distal radius fracture

Distal radius fractures (DRFs) are one of the most common fractures seen in the adult population, with a population study from 2016 to 2019 noting an incidence of DRFs to be 3.6/1,000 trauma-related emergency department visits and 10.8/1,000 upper-extremity traumas presenting to a United States emergency department.1 In postmenopausal women and the elderly, DRFs are often related to underlying osteoporosis.2 Even in younger patients, these fractures can indicate low bone density and increased future fracture risk.3

The approach for managing DRFs can be broadly categorized into operative and nonsurgical strategies. The treatment choices depend on a comprehensive understanding of potential complications to optimize patient outcomes. Notably, a subset of patients undergoing operative treatment experience early postoperative complications, with an incidence ranging from 13% to 23%.4,5 These complications encompass complex regional pain syndrome, nerve injuries, and infections, which are detrimental to functional recovery and quality of life.4, 5, 6 Early operative fixation has become a mainstay to restore anatomical alignment and facilitate early mobilization for patients with displaced fractures.5, 6, 7 Even with the progress made in surgical methods, the possibility of postoperative complications persists. Tools to risk-stratify patients at the time of injury and predict complications and functional outcomes could allow for targeted monitoring and preventive efforts in higher-risk patients.

Within this framework of preemptive care, the Charlson comorbidity index (CCI) emerged as a critical tool by quantifying 17 comorbidities to prognosticate mortality, providing a robust predictive model of 1-year mortality risk.8 The CCI has been further refined to form the Charlson fracture index (CFI), by reweighing the value of input variables and providing better accuracy in forecasting the likelihood of hip fractures.9 Indeed, CFI was shown to demonstrate a fair accuracy in predicting hip fractures, area under the receiver operator characteristic curve (AUC) of 0.755 for women and 0.782 for men. Previous investigators have shown the CCI has predictive value for postoperative complication in general and spine surgery cases. Wada et al10 used CCI in a multivariable analysis showing a CCI ≥ 2 was an independent predictor of postoperative-anastomotic leakage after right-sided colon cancer surgery (HR = 4.91, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 2.23–10.85, P < .001). Borja et al11 found that each additional point in the CCI score was predictive of multiple postoperative complications including higher rates of readmission and emergency department visit within 30 days (P = .022 and .011, respectively). However, to our knowledge, a comparative analysis of the CCI and CFI in the context of risk stratification following DRFs has yet to be undertaken.

In this study, we aim to evaluate CCI and the CFI and their association with postoperative complications within 90 days following open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF) of DRFs. We hypothesize that the fracture-specific comorbidity index (CFI) will demonstrate superior discriminative ability over the CCI in identifying patients at risk for early postoperative complications following DRF ORIF. Second, we will assess the association between CCI and CFI and the severity of surgical complications, as categorized by the Clavien-Dindo-Sink classification. We hypothesize higher preoperative scores on both the CCI and CFI will be associated with greater severity of surgical complications.

Materials and Methods

Study design and study sample

We conducted a single-center retrospective cohort study after receiving institutional review board approval. Patients aged 40 years or older who underwent surgical treatment for DRFs between January 2005 and December 2022 were identified from our institutional surgical registry using relevant Current Procedural Terminology codes (CPT 25607, 25608, and 25609). This age cutoff allowed us to capture perimenopausal and postmenopausal women along with men of the same age. Inclusion criteria were age ≥40 years, displaced closed DRF treated with volar locking plate fixation, and minimum 1-year follow-up. We screened 1358 patients and excluded patients with multiple procedures during the same surgery, polytrauma patients, open fractures, or those who had fixation other than volar locking plates.

Data collection

We conducted a comprehensive chart review to extract patient demographics and medical history. To calculate the CCI and CFI, we identified the 17 Charlson conditions using International Classification of Disease, Ninth Revision, Clinical Modification (ICD-9-CM) and International Classification of Disease, Tenth Revision, Clinical Modification (ICD-10-CM) diagnosis codes from the 15 years prior to surgery. CCI scores were calculated using standard integer weights for each condition, while CFI scores used updated weights specific to fracture outcomes. Conditions included myocardial infarct, congestive heart failure, peripheral vascular disease, cerebrovascular disease, dementia, chromic pulmonary disease, rheumatologic disease, ulcer disease, mild liver disease, diabetes (without organ damage), diabetes (organ damage), hemiplegia or paraplegia, moderate or severe renal disease, any malignancy, moderate or severe liver disease, metastatic solid tumor, AIDS/HIV, and age above 50 grouped in decades. Both indices incorporated additional points for age categories based on published weights. Score ranged from a minimum of 0 to a maximum of 38 for CCI and 0−33 for CFI. This process yielded CCI and CFI scores for each patient, providing a comprehensive assessment of the comorbidity burden.8,9

Outcome measures

The primary outcome was the occurrence of postoperative complications within 90 days of surgery. We looked for a standardized set of complications related to ORIF of DRFs, including complex regional pain syndrome, nerve injury, hardware complications, loss of reduction, infection, reoperation, malunion/nonunion, tendon rupture/injury, and carpal tunnel syndrome. We identified and evaluated complications based on the list of complications we have identified as specific and related to ORIF of DRF and assess severity using the Clavien-Dindo-Sink classification system. The Clavien-Dindo-Sink classification system was originally developed in the field of general surgery to standardize the grading of surgical outcomes based of the severity of intervention required to treat them. Although initially developed for general surgery, previous investigators have shown this grading system to be reliable in classifying orthopedic complications in a population of patients undergoing hip preservation surgery and distal radius fixation.12, 13, 14 Secondary outcomes included complication severity graded by the Clavien-Dindo-Sink classification, which grades complications based on the treatment a complication required (see the Appendix, available on the Journal’s website at www.jhsgo.org).12

Statistical methods

Data were analyzed using the R software.15 We calculated the summary statistics (median, Q1−Q3 for continuous variables, and absolute and relative frequencies for categorical variables) for all the participants in the study sample and by the occurrence of complications 90 days postsurgery. Mann-Whitney U test and Fisher exact test were used to detect any difference in the variables under consideration (age, sex, ethnicity, smoking, CCI, CFI) by group (with and without complications).

The primary analysis of interest was to quantify and compare the strength of association of two comorbidity indices, CCI and CFI, with surgical complications occurring 90 days after surgery. The sample size was calculated for a logistic regression model assuming an expected portion of patient experiencing a complication to be 18% and targeting 80% power at a two-sided significance level of 0.05 to detect an odds ratio of 1.5 for complication risk per 1 unit increase in CCI/CFI. Based on these assumptions and accounting for a conservative proportion of patients with incomplete exposure data of 10%, the estimated target sample size was 300 patients. We built multivariable binary logistic regression models that included age, sex, ethnicity, and CCI (or CFI) with the occurrence of a surgical complication 90 days postsurgery as the outcome variables. We created tabular summaries of the effects of independent variables on the occurrence of an early complication by referring to the estimates of the regression coefficients and the corresponding adjusted odds ratios. Based on each of the models, we calculated the AUC to compare the ability of each index to distinguish the presence of complications. The purpose of this analysis was to assess and compare the ability of the models based on the CCI and CFI in distinguishing patients that had a postoperative complication versus those that did not. To assess the relationship between CCI and CFI, we calculated the correlation (Spearman rank correlation coefficient) and agreement (intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC)) between the two indices. We evaluated the relationship between CCI (and CFI) and the severity of the surgical complications as measured by Clavien-Dindo-Sink classification by calculating the Spearman Rank correlation coefficient. The statistical significance level of the type I error rate was set at 0.05.

Results

Among the 317 patients, 16.7% (n = 53) experienced postoperative complications at or before 90 days postsurgery. There were no statistically significant differences in the group of patients with and without complications (Table 1). The median CCI and CFI scores among all patients were 3.0 (2.0−4.0) and 2.0 (1.0−3.0), respectively. There were no statistically significant differences in CCI and CFI in patients with and without complications. Among the 53 patients that had a complication, 37 (66%) had a type 1 Clavien-Dindo-Sink complication, 5 (9.4%) had a type 2, and 9 (17.0%) a type 3; 4 complications (7.5%) were not accounted for in the Clavien-Dindo-Sink ratings but were included in the analysis.

Discussion

In this retrospective cohort study, we assessed the utility of the CCI and its fracture-specific variant, CFI, for their association with early complications following DRF ORIF. Our findings suggest that neither the CCI nor the CFI were significantly associated with the occurrence of postoperative complications within 90 days, after adjusting for relevant covariates such as age, sex, and ethnicity. Furthermore, the effect sizes observed for the association between these comorbidity indices and complications were smaller than initially hypothesized and not clinically meaningful. The lack of a statistically significant association between the CCI/CFI and early complications in our study contrasts with previous research demonstrating the prognostic value of these indices for other orthopedic-surgical outcomes, such as readmission rates, and medical and surgical complications in surgically treated hip fractures.16,17 Gatot et al16 previously assessed CCI in patients surgically treated for hip fractures for its predictive value and found a CCI > 6 predicted for higher 90-day readmission rates along with poorer quality of life and poorer potential for functional recovery 1-year postoperation. Additionally, when comparing hip fractures patients with varying CCI grades, research has found that high CCI grades had a 1.45 times higher risk of postoperative complications compared with low CCI patients (adjusted RR 1.45, 95% CI: 1.05–1.99, P = .021).17 Additionally, it is important to note that while there are no studies comparing CCI in hip fracture patients against DRF patients, CCI in hip fracture patients is typically higher (5.1 [SD ± 2.0]) than that in DRF patients (1 [range, 0−2]) when comparing patient characteristics in similar aged patients in various studies.18,19 This discrepancy may be attributed to several factors. First, the pathophysiology and risk factors for complications following DRF ORIF may differ from those for other surgical procedures or fracture types. Surgical fixation of hips fractures often involves reaming the medullary canal, which is associated with higher blood loss and longer hospital stays (averaging 8.1 days) while surgical fixation of wrist fractures is often performed in an outpatient setting.20,21 Therefore, the relationship between the CCI and postoperative complications—such as readmission, retroperitoneal and gastrointestinal bleeding, pulmonary embolism, deep vein thrombosis, and myocardial infarction—observed in surgically treated hip fracture patients may not be directly comparable to the relationship between CCI and complications in our DRF cohort, because of the differing physiological impact of surgical fixation in these two patient populations. Additionally, following surgical fixation of isolated hip fractures, patients have significantly worse mobility, independence in function, health, quality of life, and higher rates of institutionalization in residential communities for elderly with research citing 40% to 60% of patients regaining preinjury functional status.22 Overall, this leads to poor health outcomes with rates of mortality at 1 year ranging from 22% to 30%.14 This level of mortality has not been shown in DRFs in elderly patients, with reports showing rates of mortality at 1 year at approximately 10% when compared to expected rates in age-matched controls.23 When considering mortality as a complication, hip fracture patients have higher rates, which may make the relationship between CCI and complications more apparent than in DRF patients. Last, the sample analyzed in our study may differ from the cited studies, which were more inclusive at a population level.

Interestingly, we observed a strong correlation (Spearman rho = 0.956, P < .001) and high agreement (ICC = 0.948, 95% CI: 0.934, 0.958) between the CCI and CFI scores in our study sample. This suggests that the fracture-specific modifications incorporated into the CFI are not as important in the wrist fracture population as they are in hip fracture patients. DRFs have been considered by some researchers to be a sentinel event and rather a warning sign for future morbidity and mortality including hip fractures and additionally has been shown to be associated with increased risk of developing neurocognitive disorders.24,25 Therefore, by developing the CFI in a hip fracture patient population, it is likely that the fracture-specific modifications are less useful in patient with DRFs who represent a healthier population.

The value of AUC was <0.6 for both CCI- and CFI-based models with the lower bound of the 95% confidence interval close to 0.5, indicating a poor performance in predicting their association with postoperative complications. These results suggest that neither index offers substantial improvement over chance in discriminating between patients who will and will not experience complications.

Spearman correlation coefficient showed no association between the severity of complications (as measured by the Clavien-Dindo classification) and CCI/CFI. This further supports the conclusion that these comorbidity indices may have limited association not only the occurrence but also the severity of postoperative complications in this specific patient population.

Limitations of our study include the retrospective nature of our data collection, which may have introduced bias due to incomplete or inconsistent documentation in medical records. Second, our single-center design may limit the generalizability of our findings to other health-care settings or patient populations. Third, we did not assess the impact of specific individual comorbidities on functional outcomes, which may have obscured potential associations between certain comorbidities, complications and residual disability. Last, although patients with complications in our study had slightly higher CFI and CCI scores than those without, our overall cohort was generally healthy, limiting the number of high-comorbidity case more likely to have complications. As a result, the study may not have detected a meaningful association between either indices and complication risk or differentiated between the performance of the two scales which may have become apparent with a broader patient cohort. However, we believe our study cohort is representative of a typical population with DRF and thus conclude that neither CCI or CFI are useful in identifying patients are risk for complications.

Our study reveals that neither CCI or CFI are useful for risk stratification in patients undergoing DRF ORIF. Our findings underscore the need for further research to identify alternative prognostic tools or comorbidity indices that are more specifically tailored to this patient population. Furthermore, research among more diverse patient populations are needed to validate our findings. Such efforts could ultimately enhance preoperative counseling, informed decision-making, and targeted interventions to optimize outcomes for patients with DRFs.

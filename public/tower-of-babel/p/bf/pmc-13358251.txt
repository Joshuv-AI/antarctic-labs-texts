Knowledge, attitudes and practices regarding diabetic nephropathy among patients with diabetes in Shanghai, China: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Chen Ji, Chunyang Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractObjectivesThis study aims to investigate the knowledge, attitudes and practices (KAP) regarding diabetic nephropathy among patients with diabetes.DesignA cross-sectional survey was conducted.SettingThe survey was administered in a clinical setting.ParticipantsA total of 410 patients with diabetes completed the questionnaires, with 215 (52.44%) identified as female and 262 (63.9%) not diagnosed with diabetic nephropathy.InterventionsParticipants provided demographic information and answered questions assessing their KAP through a structured questionnaire.Primary and secondary outcome measuresThe primary outcomes measured included knowledge scores, attitude scores and practice scores concerning diabetic nephropathy.ResultsThe mean scores for KAP were 5.63±3.14, 16.82±5.21 and 15.03±5.89, respectively. Multivariate analysis showed that female gender (OR=0.57, 95% CI 0.35 to 0.92, p=0.024), monthly per capita income exceeding ¥10 000 (OR=3.96, 95% CI 1.38 to 11.30, p=0.011) and type 1 diabetes (OR=2.52, 95% CI 1.10 to 5.74, p=0.028) were independently associated with adequate knowledge. Knowledge score (OR=1.39, 95% CI 1.26 to 1.54, p<0.001) and diabetic retinopathy (OR=0.37, 95% CI 0.18 to 0.78, p=0.009) were associated with attitude. Proactive practice was associated with knowledge score (OR=1.32, 95% CI 1.17 to 1.50, p<0.001), attitude score (OR=1.28, 95% CI 1.17 to 1.40, p<0.001) and income level (p<0.05). Proactive practice was associated with knowledge score (OR=1.32, 95% CI 1.17 to 1.50, p<0.001), attitude score (OR=1.28, 95% CI 1.17 to 1.40, p<0.001) and monthly per capita income, including ¥2000–4999 (OR=5.09, 95% CI 1.57 to 16.40, p=0.007), ¥5000–9999 (OR=4.57, 95% CI 1.35 to 15.40, p=0.014) and ≥¥10 000 (OR=5.49, 95% CI 1.31 to 22.90, p=0.019).ConclusionsPatients with diabetes demonstrated insufficient knowledge, unfavourable attitudes and passive practices regarding diabetic nephropathy. These findings highlight the need for targeted interventions and educational programmes to enhance awareness and encourage proactive management among this population.Keywords: Knowledge, Attitude, DIABETES & ENDOCRINOLOGY, Diabetic neuropathy, Diabetic nephropathy & vascular disease

Background

Diabetic nephropathy, a debilitating complication of diabetes mellitus, poses a significant global health challenge.1 Its emergence as a primary contributor to end-stage renal disease and intricate association with cardiovascular diseases contribute to heightened morbidity and mortality among individuals with diabetes.2,4 The progression of diabetic nephropathy unfolds through distinct stages, starting with microalbuminuria and advancing to macroalbuminuria with pronounced renal impairment.4 In recent years, China has witnessed a surge in the incidence of diabetic nephropathy, with a prevalence ranging from 20% to 40%.5 6 Notably, it constitutes the primary cause of end-stage chronic kidney disease, significantly impacting patients’ quality of life.7 Beyond the personal toll, diabetic nephropathy imposes a substantial economic burden, diminishing both the individual and societal quality of life.8

Given the escalating prevalence of diabetic nephropathy, there is a compelling need for comprehensive research to understand its complex mechanisms.6 Understanding the multifaceted aspects of diabetic nephropathy is crucial, given its pervasive impact on health outcomes and the broader societal landscape. An in-depth exploration of the factors influencing the knowledge, attitudes and practices (KAP) of individuals with diabetes is imperative for effective prevention and management. KAP assessments serve as crucial components in gauging the effectiveness of public health interventions, and this structured survey method has been widely employed to assess individuals’ understanding, beliefs and behaviours concerning various health issues.9,11 In the context of diabetic nephropathy, KAP studies offer valuable insights into the awareness levels, perceptions and actions of patients with diabetes.

Therefore, this study aims to investigate the KAP regarding diabetic nephropathy among patients with diabetes.

Materials and methods

Study design and participants

This cross-sectional investigation was conducted between 1 November 2022 and 31 May 2023 at the Changshou Street Community Health Service Center in Putuo District, Shanghai. Inclusion criteria included individuals meeting the following conditions: (1) being Shanghai residents aged 18 years or older and (2) having the capacity to fulfil the survey, exhibiting independent cognitive abilities and demonstrating willingness to participate in the completion of the questionnaire. Exclusion criteria encompassed individuals meeting the following conditions: (1) patients with limited educational levels incapable of comprehending the questionnaire content and providing relevant responses and (2) patients with mental disorders or intellectual impairments.

Questionnaire and quality control

The questionnaire was formulated with reference to the ‘Chinese Guidelines for the Prevention and Treatment of Diabetic Kidney Disease (2021 Edition)’ and the ‘Chinese Guidelines for the Prevention and Treatment of Type 2 Diabetes (2020 Edition)’. The questionnaire exhibited good internal consistency, as evidenced by a Cronbach’s alpha of 0.933, and the Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin measure was calculated to be 0.944.

The final questionnaire, written in Chinese, encompassed information gathering across four dimensions, totalling 45 items. These dimensions included 16 items for basic information, 12 items for knowledge, 9 items for attitudes and 8 items for practices. In the statistical analysis, scores were assigned based on the number of options for each item. For the knowledge dimension, questions were awarded 1 point for a correct response and 0 points for an incorrect or unclear response, resulting in a knowledge score range of 0–12 points. The attitude dimension primarily employed a 5-point Likert scale, ranging from very positive (5 points) to very negative (1 point), with a score range of 9–45 points. For the practice dimension, scoring was as follows: for questions 1–2, ‘yes’ was awarded 1 point, while ‘no’ or ‘unclear’ was awarded 0 points; for questions 3–8, a 5-point Likert scale was employed, resulting in a score range of 6–32. Adequate knowledge, positive attitude and proactive practice were defined as achieving scores exceeding 70% of the maximum possible score in each respective section, a widely adopted threshold in KAP surveys to effectively distinguish adequate from inadequate levels.12 13

The survey was administered through both electronic and paper questionnaires. The electronic questionnaire was generated using a questionnaire tool, which generated a QR code for participant access and completion. To uphold data quality standards, each IP address was restricted to one submission, and completion of all items was mandatory. The paper version of the questionnaire was randomly distributed by doctors during outpatient visits. The research team meticulously reviewed the integrity, internal consistency and validity of all collected questionnaires.

Sample size calculation

Sample size was calculated using the formula for cross-sectional studies: α=0.05, n=(Z1-α/2δ)2×p×(1-p), where Z1-α/2=1.96 when α=0.05, the assumed degree of variability of p=0.5 maximises the required sample size and δ is admissible error (which was 5% here). Applying the values mentioned above in Cochran’s equation, the estimated sample size for the present study was 384.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using Stata V.14.0 (Stata, College Station, Texas, USA). Continuous data were presented as means and SDs, while categorical data were expressed as n (%). Normality tests were conducted for continuous variables, with the t-test employed for normally distributed data and the Wilcoxon-Mann-Whitney test for non-normally distributed data in two-group comparisons. For comparisons involving three or more groups with normally distributed continuous variables and uniform variance, analysis of variance was used, whereas the Kruskal-Wallis test was applied for non-normally distributed data. Multivariate analysis was conducted to investigate the factors influencing adequate knowledge, positive attitudes and proactive practices. A two-sided p value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and the public were not involved in the planning and design of this study.

Results

In this study, a total of 468 responses were obtained through an online survey. Subsequently, cases were excluded based on specific criteria, resulting in a final dataset of 410 valid responses. Demographic characteristics within this cohort of patients with diabetes included 215 (52.44%) females, 156 (38.05%) individuals aged above 60 and 260 (63.41%) residing in Putuo District, Shanghai. Additionally, 127 (30.98%) received a diagnosis within the past year, 230 (56.1%) were diagnosed with type 2 diabetes, 213 (51.95%) initiated medication immediately after diagnosis and 137 (33.41%) reported a family history of diabetes. Furthermore, 262 (63.9%) had not been diagnosed with diabetic nephropathy, and 130 (31.71%) believed they maintained good control of their blood sugar.

The mean scores for KAP were 5.63±3.14, 16.82±5.21 and 15.03±5.89, respectively. Analysis of demographic characteristics revealed significant variations in participants’ KAP scores across various factors, such as age groups, occupation or family occupation, monthly per capita income, type of health insurance, diabetes duration, diabetes type, presence of diabetic foot, presence of diabetic cerebrovascular disease and time to start hypoglycaemic medication after the diagnosis of diabetes. Moreover, residents of the Putuo District were more likely to have higher knowledge and practice scores. Participants without diabetic retinopathy were also more likely to exhibit a more positive attitude (table 1).

The distribution of knowledge dimensions showed variations in participants’ responses, with specific questions exhibiting higher correct or uncertain selections (online supplemental table S1). In the attitude dimension, participants generally endorsed the significance of blood glucose management, the importance of preventing diabetic nephropathy and confidence in maintaining a healthy lifestyle (online supplemental table S2). Regarding related practices, participants reported varying degrees of adherence to recommended blood glucose monitoring, compliance with prescribed diabetes medication and engagement in physical exercise (online supplemental table S3).

The multivariate analysis showed that female (OR=0.57, 95% CI 0.35 to 0.92, p=0.024), a monthly per capita income exceeding ¥10 000 (OR=3.96, 95% CI 1.38 to 11.3, p=0.011) and type 1 diabetes (OR=2.52, 95% CI 1.10 to 5.74, p=0.028) were independently associated with adequate knowledge (table 2). Knowledge score (OR=1.39, 95% CI 1.26 to 1.54, p<0.001) and the presence of diabetic retinopathy (OR=0.37, 95% CI 0.18 to 0.78, p=0.009) were independently associated with a positive attitude (table 3). Knowledge score (OR=1.32, 95% CI 1.17 to 1.50, p<0.001); attitude score (OR=1.28, 95% CI 1.17 to 1.40, p<0.001); monthly per capita income in the range of ¥2000–4999 (OR=5.09, 95% CI 1.57 to 16.4, p=0.007), ¥5000–9999 (OR=4.57, 95% CI 1.35 to 15.4, p=0.014), exceeding ¥10 000 (OR=5.49, 95% CI 1.31 to 22.9, p=0.019); and diabetes duration of 5–10 years (OR=5.98, 95% CI 1.63 to 21.8, p=0.007) were independently associated with proactive practice (table 4).

Discussion

Patients with diabetes in this study exhibited insufficient knowledge, unfavourable attitudes and passive approaches to diabetic nephropathy. Our findings underscore the critical need for healthcare professionals to institute comprehensive educational initiatives. These programmes should specifically aim to debunk misconceptions, foster positive attitudes and promote proactive self-management practices.

These findings align with existing literature, which consistently highlights suboptimal awareness and understanding of diabetes complications.14 15 Variations in knowledge scores across age groups suggest that older individuals may possess more comprehensive knowledge due to prolonged exposure to diabetes management.16 Furthermore, the significant impact of monthly per capita income on knowledge levels underscores the socioeconomic dimension of health literacy, mirroring previous research that indicates a positive correlation between higher income levels and better diabetes-related knowledge.17 In addition, the association between residential area type and knowledge scores echoes similar urban-rural disparities noted in diabetes-related studies.18 This emphasises the need for tailored interventions in specific geographical contexts to address disparities in diabetes education and awareness.

The multivariate analysis provides additional insights into factors independently associated with KAP. Notably, being female, having a higher monthly per capita income and having type 1 diabetes are independently associated with higher knowledge levels. These findings align with existing literature, suggesting that sociodemographic factors play a crucial role in shaping patients’ understanding of diabetes and its complications.19 The associations identified in attitude and practice further emphasise the complex interplay of various factors, including knowledge levels, the presence of diabetic retinopathy, income levels and diabetes duration, in influencing patients’ attitudes and practices towards disease management. These results underscore the necessity of adopting a holistic approach in designing interventions that consider the intricate relationships between demographic factors and KAP regarding diabetic nephropathy. The marked influence of income and education also points to structural barriers, indicating that policy support for community-based screening and patient education is needed.

For instance, the low correct response rates regarding manifestations and preventive measures for diabetic nephropathy highlight the urgent need for educational interventions. Previous studies have emphasised the pivotal role of patient education in improving knowledge and preventing complications.20 21 To address the specific knowledge gaps identified in the study, interventions should focus on providing clear and concise information regarding the manifestations of diabetic nephropathy, the importance of regular screenings and the role of lifestyle modifications in prevention. Educational initiatives can draw on successful models from other chronic diseases, emphasising interactive and culturally sensitive approaches.22 23

Concerning attitudes, the study exposes some uncertainty and disagreement among participants, particularly in acknowledging the severity of diabetic nephropathy and the effectiveness of healthcare professional education. To address this, tailored educational programmes and enhanced communication strategies may be beneficial. Improving attitudes and beliefs about diabetic nephropathy necessitates a multifaceted approach. Healthcare professionals should enhance their communication strategies to emphasise the seriousness of diabetic nephropathy and the importance of preventive measures.24 25 Collaborative efforts involving healthcare providers, educators and patients can contribute to developing educational materials that address specific concerns raised in attitude-related items.26 27

In terms of practices, the responses reveal varying levels of consistency in adhering to medical advice, monitoring blood sugar and seeking preventive advice. The observed inconsistencies in practices underscore the need for targeted interventions that go beyond traditional information dissemination. For example, while medication use was common, many participants had never received diabetic nephropathy (DN) screening, suggesting that treatment is often maintained while preventive care remains neglected. Enhancing practices requires a shift from knowledge dissemination to empowering patients with practical skills and tools for self-management.28,30 Interventions should consider the socioeconomic context and individual barriers to adherence. For instance, providing resources for meal planning, facilitating access to regular screenings and promoting community-based exercise programmes can positively impact practices related to diabetic nephropathy prevention.31 32

However, this study has several limitations. The cross-sectional design impedes the establishment of causation and temporal relationships between variables. Additionally, reliance on self-reported data introduces the potential for response bias and recall bias, particularly for practice-related behaviours, which may impact the accuracy of participants’ reported KAP. Moreover, some knowledge items may be subject to clinical debate, and ‘uncertain’ responses were coded as incorrect, which might underestimate partial understanding but ensures a conservative assessment of knowledge levels. Furthermore, the study’s confinement to a single health service centre in a specific district may limit the generalisability of findings to a broader population. Specifically, this localised sampling approach may introduce selection bias, meaning our findings might not fully represent the KAP levels of patients with diabetes in rural areas or other regions with different socioeconomic profiles. Some subgroup analyses produced wide CIs, reflecting limited precision in smaller strata, and these results should be interpreted with caution. Notwithstanding these limitations, the study’s strengths lie in its substantial sample size of 410 patients with diabetes, providing robust insights into KAP regarding diabetic nephropathy. The application of multivariate analysis enhances the nuanced exploration of factors associated with KAP. Moreover, the comprehensive assessment of KAP scores, coupled with detailed demographic information, contributes to a more holistic understanding of diabetic nephropathy dynamics in the studied population. Future multicentre studies are warranted to enhance national representativeness and improve the generalisability of the findings.

Conclusions

In conclusion, patients with diabetes demonstrated a notable lack of knowledge, unfavourable attitudes and passive practices concerning diabetic nephropathy. These findings underscore the need for targeted educational interventions and awareness campaigns to enhance patient understanding and promote proactive engagement in nephropathy management. To address the observed deficits in KAP regarding diabetic nephropathy, healthcare providers should prioritise the implementation of targeted educational initiatives and interventions tailored to the specific needs of patients with diabetes.

Enhanced production of Nattokinase by Bacillus aryabhattai RP6 isolated from fermented Brassica oleracea
Authors: Roshni Prasanna, Debosmit Kundu, Maneesha Mohanan, Mohanasrinivasan Vaithilingam, Subathra Devi Chandrasekaran
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundNattokinase (NK) in recent times has been identified as an important enzyme for its health benefits and its application in cardiovascular diseases (CVD). NK is known for its thrombolytic activity and has been actively used its low cost of production. The research study aims to enhance the Nattokinase production from fermented Brassica oleracea.ResultsSeven different fermented and non-fermented food sources were screened for fibrinolytic protease-producing bacteria. Out of 12 bacterial isolates, 7 were identified as protease producers. However, the strain RP6 isolated from fermented Brassica oleracea exhibited higher fibrinolytic activity. Morphological, biochemical, and molecular characterization revealed RP6 as Bacillus aryabhattai. Optimization studies showed maximum NK production when glycerol and ammonium chloride were used as carbon and nitrogen sources, respectively, at pH 10, inoculum size of 2%, and incubation at 37 °C. The purified enzyme, with an apparent molecular mass of 29 kDa, demonstrated maximum fibrinolytic activity of 88.87% after 3 h.ConclusionThe study identifies Bacillus aryabhattai RP6 as a potent NK-producing strain. Its high yield and strong fibrinolytic activity highlight its potential for industrial-scale NK production and possible application in cardiovascular therapeutics.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12896-026-01128-y.

Background

Cardiovascular diseases remain one of the leading causes of death in the world. It is primarily associated with thrombus development in blood arteries [1–2]. Excessive fibrin, platelet, and red blood cell deposition causes thrombosis, which obstructs blood vessels and lowers the flow of blood to critical organs. These thrombotic obstructions are the root cause of serious illnesses including pulmonary embolism, myocardial infarction, ischemic stroke, and deep vein thrombosis. Therefore, lowering the morbidity and mortality linked to thrombotic diseases requires effective thrombolytic treatment [3–4]. Clinically approved thrombolytic agents such as streptokinase, urokinase, and tissue plasminogen activator (tPA) function by converting inactive plasminogen into active plasmin, which degrades fibrin [5–6]. Despite their effectiveness, these agents have some significant drawbacks, such as high cost, short half-life, secondary hyperfibrinolysis, allergic responses, platelet dysfunction, fibrinogenemia, and the need for close medical care when administered intravenously. These drawbacks highlight how urgently safer, more affordable, and orally bioavailable thrombolytic substitutes are needed [7–8].

Nattokinase is an alkaline protease enzyme with potent thrombolytic activity. It has a molecular weight of 28 kDa and consists of 275 amino acids. Nattokinase was mainly obtained from a fermented food product called natto discovered in the year 1987. Natto, a traditional Japanese food, was created from soyabeans fermented from Bacillus subtilis [9]. In recent years Nattokinase seems to show a positive result for the treatment of cardiovascular diseases due to its strong fibrinolytic activity; strong enough to survive the gastrointestinal tract. The benefits include being useful in the treatment of other diseases such as hypertension and Alzheimer’s disease, and vitreoretinal disorders. Also, NK has been reported to exhibit antihypertensive, anti-atherosclerotic, lipid-lowering, and neuroprotective properties, further highlighting its therapeutic potential [10]. However, large-scale industrial production of high-yield, cost-effective NK remains a challenge due to limitations in strain efficiency and downstream processing [11].

The majority of nattokinase (NK) production has been associated with Bacillus subtilis strains isolated from fermented soybean products. These strains are well recognized for their enzyme-producing capability and established probiotic applications [12–13]. Other Bacillus species, such as Bacillus aryabhattai, have drawn attention due to their probiotic potential, stress tolerance, and extracellular enzyme production capacity [14]. However, detailed reports on Bacillus aryabhattai are limited for the synthesis of nattokinase and systematic optimization. Therefore, exploring other Bacillus species might increase the variety of probiotic strains with thrombolytic applications. The present study aims to isolate and characterize a potent NK producing bacterial strain from fermented food sources and to enhance enzyme production through systematic optimization of culture conditions.

Materials and methods

Sample collection and bacterial isolation

The samples including Citrus limon, Actinidia deliciosa, Gallus gallus domesticus egg yolk, Brassica oleracea, Vicia faba, Cicer arietinum and Glycine max have been collected. The samples (except egg yolk) were washed and kept for fermentation without the addition of any starter culture for about 7 days at room temperature. The samples were smashed and serially diluted with 0.9% of NaCl and spread on to nutrient agar (HiMedia) plate and incubated for 24 h at 37℃ [15]. Colonies were then selected on the basis of their morphological characteristics.

Caseinolytic assay

Nutrient agar plate supplemented with 2% casein was used for the primary screening of Nattokinase enzyme. Wells were made on the solidified casein medium using a sterilized corkborer. The bacterial isolates were inoculated in nutrient broth and incubated for 24 h at 37℃. Then culture broth was centrifuged at 8000 rpm for 10 min to obtain the cell free supernatant. 100 µl of supernatant was added into each well and the plates were incubated for 24 h at 37℃ [16]. The diameter of the zone of hydrolysis was recorded.

Casein plasminogen assay

Casein plasminogen plate assay was performed to detect the presence of fibrinolytic activity. A 10 mL of soft agarose mixture was made with 0.8% agarose, 10% skimmed milk, 150 mM of NaCl, 50 mL of Tris HCl (pH − 8) and 200 µL of human plasma. Wells were punched in the solidified media using a sterile corkborer. 100 µL of supernatant was pipetted into each well and incubated for 24 h at 37℃ [17]. The diameter of the zone of hydrolysis was noted.

Protease activity

The protease activity was measured using oda and murao’s technique, with slight modifications [18]. 0.15mL crude supernatant was added to 0.05 mL of azocasein (2 mg/mL azocasein in 10mM tris-HCl buffer of pH 7.5) and the mixture was incubated for 20 min. 0.06 mL of 10% (w/v) trichloroacetic acid was added to stop the reaction followed by centrifugation for 10 min at 8000 rpm. About 0.15mL of the supernatant enzyme was added to 0.33mL of folin- ciocalteu’s reagent and 0.45mL of a 10% (w/v) solution of Na2CO3. The mixture measured using a UV spectrophotometer at an of OD600 after being incubated at room temperature for 30 min. One unit (U) of enzyme activity was defined as the amount of enzyme required to release 1 µmol of tyrosine per minute under the assay conditions. Enzyme activity is expressed as U/mL, representing µmol of tyrosine released per minute per milliliter of enzyme solution.

Protein determination

The protein content was estimated by Lowry’s method [19].

Characterization of potent strain

The potent strain was characterized by biochemical, morphological and molecular characterization. Colony morphology was assessed on nutrient agar, and Gram staining was performed to determine cellular morphology. Standard biochemical tests including indole production, methyl red (MR), Voges–Proskauer (VP), citrate utilization, catalase activity, oxidase activity, and nitrate reduction were conducted following standard microbiological protocols [20]. The molecular characterization was performed by isolating genomic DNA from RP6 and subjected to PCR reaction with the universal primer 27 F (5′-AGAGTTTGATCMTGGCTCAG-3′) and 1492R (5′-TACGGYTACCTTGTTACGACTT-3′). The obtained 16s rDNA sequence was analyzed using the BLAST tool. The sequence was submitted to NCBI and the GenBank accession number was generated. Phylogenetic analysis was performed using MEGA 12 software. The sequences were aligned using the ClustalW algorithm, and a phylogenetic tree was constructed using the Neighbor-Joining method with 1000 bootstrap values to determine the homology [21–22].

Production of nattokinase

The potent strain was grown in tryptic soy broth (TSB) with pH 7.3 and incubated at 37℃ for 24 h at 120 rpm. 1% of culture from the 24 h sample was added as an inoculum to the production medium supplemented with g/L (dextrose 10.0, peptone 3.0, yeast extract 3.0, beef extract 1.0, casein enzyme hydrolysate 0.5, FeSO4. 7H2O 2.0, KH2PO4 5.0, CaCl2 5.0, MgSO4.7H2O 3.0,) at a pH of 7.4, and then incubated for 48 h at 37℃ at 120 rpm. The broth was centrifuged for 10 min at 8000 rpm to collect the supernatant. Enzyme activity was measured by casein hydrolysis [15].

Growth kinetics of Bacillus aryabhattai RP6

The strain Bacillus aryabhattai RP6 was inoculated in TSB medium and incubated for about 80 h at 37℃. The growth OD was determined at 0th and 4th h and then after every 8 h intervals. The biomass was also determined by drying the pellet in a hot air oven. Enzyme activity was accessed using the casein hydrolysis technique [23].

Optimization of media parameters

The factors that influence NK production including carbon, nitrogen, pH, temperature and inoculum size have been optimized for maximum nattokinase production. The parameters were selected to evaluate the influence of physicochemical and nutritional factors on nattokinase production. To determine the effect of carbon sources, the dextrose in the medium was replaced with other carbon sources such as glucose, sucrose, maltose, and glycerol. Similarly, the nitrogen source beef extract was replaced with ammonium chloride, ammonium sulfate, glycine, and tryptone to evaluate the influence of organic and inorganic nitrogen on enzyme production. The temperature range was set between 25℃ and 60℃ to evaluate the mesophilic growth conditions on NK production. To get the optimal pH for production medium’s pH range was varied from 3 to 11 to understand its effect. Inoculum sizes of 0.1%, 0.5%, 1%, 1.5% and 2% (v/v) were studied to determine the influence of initial cell density on biomass accumulation and enzyme secretion [24].

Purification of enzyme

Ammonium sulphate precipitation, dialysis and gel filtration chromatography were used for the purification of the enzyme. The culture broth was centrifuged for 20 min at 8000 rpm at 4℃. Ammonium sulphate salt was added to the supernatant to precipitate the enzyme. According to Sulkowski, the saturation ranged from 20% to 80% [25]. The mixture was centrifuged for 20 min at 4 °C at 10,000 rpm. After that the pellet was then resuspended in 10 mM of tris HCl buffer (pH − 7.4). The obtained ammonium sulphate precipitate was introduced into the dialysis membrane having pore size 2.4 mm in 50 mM of sodium phosphate buffer. The buffer was changed after every 4 h [26]. The dialyzed sample was loaded onto a sephadex G50 column and the sample was eluted against 50 mm tris-HCl with 20mM CaCl2 pH 7.4. The protein content and protease activity of the purified fractions were evaluated [18].

Holmstrom method

The fibrinolytic activity of the enzyme was assessed by using a modified version of Holmstrom technique [27]. 0.5 mL crude, partially purified and completely purified samples were added to the 0.5 mL of freshly drawn blood in Eppendorf tubes. The percentage of clot lysis was calculated using the given formula.

Percentage of clot lysis = weight of clot lysed / weight of clot x 100.

Enzyme characterization

High performance liquid chromatography (HPLC)

The presence of Nattokinase was determined by using HPLC. The mobile phase contains 0.2 mol/L of NaH2PO4-CH3OH (95:5). The flow rate was 1mL/min and the wavelength used for the detection was 220 nm with a retention time of 20 min. The elution peaks were collected, and the purity of the enzyme was evaluated [28].

Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR)

Nattokinase FTIR spectra was measured using a Perkin Elmer FTIR spectrometer [29].

Sodium dodecyl sulfate - polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS - PAGE)

The molecular mass and purity of the purified sample was determined using SDS-PAGE. A 4% stacking gel and 12.5% separating gel was used. Protein bands were visualized after staining and destaining the gel [30].

Results

Isolation and screening

Twelve isolates were obtained from 7 different fermented food sources. Among them, the characterized isolates RP1 was isolated from fermented kiwi (Actinidia deliciosa), RP2 and RP3 from chickpea (Cicer arietinum), RP4 from broad bean (Vicia faba), RP5 and RP6 from fermented cabbage (Brassica oleracea), and RP7 from soybean (Glycine max). The isolates were screened primarily for protease activity using casein agar plates. The isolates RP4, RP5, RP6, and RP7 showed a zone of hydrolysis which shows the presence of protease activity. Whereas RP1, RP2, and RP3 did not produce zones of hydrolysis. The protease positive isolates were further screened for fibrinolytic protease activity using casein plasminogen agar plate. Among the tested strains, RP6 showed a maximum zone of hydrolysis of 14 ± 1.08 mm followed by RP5 with a zone of hydrolysis of 12 ± 0.97 mm (Fig. 1). The total protein content of the crude enzyme extract was determined to be 18.23 ± 1.42 mg/mL for RP6 and 16.6 ± 1.73 mg/mL for RP5, further supporting the selection of RP6 as the most potent strain for subsequent optimization and characterization studies.

Characterization of potent strain

The potent strain RP6 showed a white, fuzzy, mat shaped colony on nutrient agar plate. The strain was found to be gram +ve rod shaped bacteria under oil immersion (100X magnification) (Fig. 2). The biochemical characterization revealed that the isolate RP6 was negative for indole, MR, VP, and oxidase tests. However, the strain showed positive results for citrate utilization, nitrate reduction, and catalase activity, suggesting its ability to utilize citrate as a carbon source, reduce nitrate, and tolerate oxidative stress. Molecular characterization was performed by extracting total DNA and analyzed using PCR, and 16 S rRNA sequencing. The NCBI BLAST search engine was used to compare the acquired sequences to related homologs. Phylogenetic analysis further confirmed the taxonomic placement of RP6 within the Bacillus aryabhattai clade (Fig. 3). The 16 S rRNA gene sequence has been updated in the NCBI database and the generated accession number is OR492619.

Growth kinetics of Bacillus aryabhattai RP6

The growth kinetics of RP6 was evaluated by monitoring the OD600, biomass and enzyme activity (Fig. 4). The isolate exhibited a typical bacterial growth curve with a short lag phase followed by a rapid exponential phase. The maximum enzyme activity of 246.71 ± 1.37 U/mL was observed in 24 h in TSB medium, corresponding to the late exponential phase of growth. After 24 h, a gradual decrease in the enzyme activity was observed, coinciding with the onset of the stationary phase. This decline might be due to nutrient depletion, the accumulation of metabolic byproducts, or the proteolytic degradation of enzyme during extended incubation.

Media optimization

pH

The effect of initial pH on NK production was evaluated over a pH range of 3–11 (Fig. 5A). Enzyme production increased progressively from acidic to alkaline conditions. The maximum enzyme activity of 257.63 ± 2.43 U/mL was observed at pH 10, indicating RP6 favors alkaline conditions for NK production. At neutral pH 7, the strain produced 197.7 ± 1.76 U/mL of enzyme. At acidic pH 3 and 5, the strain produced 89.2 ± 0.51 U/mL and 128.7 ± 1.54 U/mL of enzyme. The strain produced 185.9 ± 1.96 U/mL of enzyme at basic pH 11.

Nitrogen sources

NH4Cl was found to be the optimum nitrogen source and the maximum enzyme activity produced was 242.3 ± 2.01 U/mL, suggesting that inorganic nitrogen sources enhance NK production. When (NH4)2SO4 and tryptone were added to the medium, the strain produced 203.1 ± 1.76 and 196.5 ± 1.88 U/mL. The strain produced 175.12 ± 1.37 and 159.8 ± 1.61 U/mL when beef extract and glycine were added as nitrogen sources to the production medium (Fig. 5B).

Carbon sources

Carbon source supplementation significantly influenced enzyme production (Fig. 5C). The strain Bacillus aryabhattai RP6 produced maximum enzyme activity of 251.3 ± 2.45 U/mL when glycerol was supplemented as carbon source. The strain produced 210.45 ± 2.01 U/mL and 197.7 ± 2.00 U/mL of Nattokinase when sucrose and maltose were used. The strain produced least enzyme activity of 179.6 ± 1.98 and 161.9 ± 1.87 U/mL when fructose and lactose were used as the carbon sources.

Inoculum size

Enzyme production varied with inoculum size (Fig. 5D). The strain Bacillus aryabhattai RP6 produced maximum enzyme activity of 239.91 ± 2.43 U/mL when the inoculum was 2%. The activity reduced to 217.8 ± 2.11 and 195.4 ± 2.03 U/mL when the inoculum was 1.5% and 1%. The strain produced 166.2 ± 1.72 and 139.7 ± 0.76 U/mL of enzyme activity when the inoculum was 0.5 and 0.1%.

Temperature

Bacillus aryabhattai RP6 produced 240.1 ± 2.26 U/mL of Nattokinase at 45 °C. The enzyme activity reduced to 119.4 ± 1.11 and 105.2 ± 0.67 U/mL at 50 °C and 60 °C. The strain produced 185.3 ± 2.51 and 94.6 ± 0.49 U/mL of enzyme at 37 and 25 °C (Fig. 5E). These findings indicate that RP6 is moderately thermotolerant.

Enzyme purification profile

The crude supernatant had a total activity of 9,450 U and a total protein content of 11,150 mg. The specific activity was found to be 0.847 U/mg and the yield was 100%. At 80% ammonium sulphate saturation, the specific activity was increased to 4.87 U/mg. The purification fold and the yield were observed as 5.74 and 79.4%. After dialysis the yield decreased to 50.2% with a rise in activity to 12.30 U/mg. The purified enzyme obtained from gel chromatography had a yield of 12.46% and 64.1 purification fold (Table 1).

Holmstrom method

The thrombolytic activity of crude, partially purified, and purified enzyme fractions was evaluated using the Holmstrom clot lysis method (Fig. 6). Clot lysis was monitored for up to 4 h of incubation. The crude supernatant exhibited 49% clot lysis, whereas ammonium sulphate precipitated enzyme showed improved activity with 57.6% lysis. Further purification through dialysis enhanced thrombolytic activity to 70.87%. The highest clot lysis (88.87%) was achieved with the purified enzyme fraction at 3 h of incubation. The positive control (streptokinase) exhibited 100% clot lysis at 3 h. The progressive increase in clot lysis activity across purification steps indicates enrichment of the active fibrinolytic enzyme and removal of interfering proteins.

Characterization of enzyme

HPLC

The purified enzyme showed a peak at 10.538 min which confirmed the presence of Nattokinase (Fig. 7). The observed retention time is consistent with previously reported retention times for nattokinase, which range between approximately 10.3–10.7 min under similar chromatographic conditions [29, 36]. This supports the presence of nattokinase in the purified fraction. The minor peak may indicate trace impurities or partially co-eluting proteins.

Discussion

Nattokinase is a potent fibrinolytic enzyme which promotes good cardiovascular health. It includes various health benefits including reducing blood pressure, lipid-lowering properties, anti-platelet, displaying neuroprotective effects and also preventing atherosclerosis [31]. Cardiovascular diseases are one of the major health risks in the world in spite of how much medicine has advanced. However, the use of traditional fibrinolytic drugs tends to show an increased risk of bleeding [32]. Compared with currently available fibrinolytic agents, nattokinase has been reported to possess long-term activity, high tolerability, gastrointestinal stability, and no evidence of chromosomal abnormalities or genetic mutations [33]. These advantages highlight the importance of exploring efficient microbial sources for enhanced NK production.

In this study, a total of 8 isolates were screened for protease and fibrinolytic protease enzyme. Among them the strain RP6 showed a maximum zone of hydrolysis of diameter 14 ± 1.08 mm on casein plasminogen plate. The protein content was found to be 18.23 ± 1.42 mg/mL. Biochemical, molecular and morphological characterization identified the strain RP6 as B. aryabhattai. Previous studies have reported significantly higher NK yields from engineered or statistically optimized systems. For example, Bacillus subtilis produced 3194.25 U/mL in a medium containing glucose, peptone, CaCl2 and MgSO4 [34], while P. aeruginosa grown on shrimp shell media demonstrated a higher activity of 2724 U/mL [35]. Other optimized production media have reported NK production ranging from 23.15 to 34.7 mg/mL [36], 47.4 ± 1.2 U/g under fed-batch fermentation [37], and up to 8709.53 IU/mL through metabolic gene overexpression [38]. In our study, the wild strain Bacillus aryabhattai RP6 produced 246.71 ± 1.37 U/mL of NK in a production medium composed of dextrose 10.0, peptone 3.0, beef extract 1.0, yeast extract 3.0, casein enzyme hydrolysate 0.5, FeSO4. 7H2O 2.0, KH2PO4 5.0, MgSO4.7H2O 3.0, CaCl2 5.0. Although lower than recombinant or bioreactor-based systems, this yield is comparable to several wild-type strains reported under submerged fermentation conditions [39].

Carbon is an essential source for energy and building blocks of the bacterium. Bacillus species use carbon, especially glucose for cell growth as energy. Various studies have reported maltodextrin (331 ± 8 U/mL) [40], glucose (7209.15 ± 195.46 IU/mL) [41], and fructose (32.88 mg/mL) [36] as optimal carbon sources for NK production. Gene overexpression targeting glycerol metabolism has also enhanced NK yield to 6639.35 IU/mL [38]. In our study glycerol was identified as the optimum carbon source and the strain produced maximum activity of 251.3 ± 2.45 U/mL. Nitrogen is an essential source for the growth and metabolism as well as the development of essential respiratory enzymes [42]. Reports indicate that cheese whey yielded 789 U/mL after RSM optimization [43], TPW-based media achieved 7092.5 IU/mL [41], soy-peptone resulted in 516.50 ± 3.3 U/mL [40], beef extract produced 34.7 mg/mL [36], and peptone-based systems yielded 3194.25 U/mL [34]. Replacement with bran resulted in 903 IU/mL [44]. In our study NH4Cl was the optimum nitrogen source for the strain Bacillus Aryabhattai RP6 with maximum enzyme activity of 242.3 ± 2.01 U/mL. Various other factors such as pH, temperature and inoculum size have a considerable effect on the activity of nattokinase production. pH plays an essential role in shaping the microbial physiology affecting how nutrients are absorbed, how enzymes function and the movement of the cell organelles. The yield of nattokinase was found to be 309 ± 2.8 U/mL at pH 6 [40]. Nattokinase production using a bioreactor showed a maximum activity of 10,220 U/mL at pH 7 [39]. According to Wang et al. the maximum enzyme activity 9033 U/mL was observed at a pH 7 [45]. In another study using the bacteria strain Bacillus cereus isolated from grape wine, the maximum activity of nattokinase was 9.20 mg/mL at pH 11 [36]. Another study showed maximum enzyme activity of 390.23 ± 10.24 FU/mL at pH 7 [46]. In a recombinant study, the maximum activity expressed was 453.34 U/mL at an optimum pH 8 [47]. In our study the maximum activity was 257.63 ± 2.43 U/mL at a basic pH 11. Regarding temperature, most reports indicate optimal NK production between 35 and 39 °C [36, 38, 45, 48, 49]. However, the strain Bacillus aryabhattai exhibited maximum enzyme activity of at 45℃, suggesting enhanced thermotolerance. Inoculum size also influences biomass and metabolite production. A study using BBD for optimization of nattokinase reported a maximum activity of 9553.07 ± 106.49 IU/mL when an inoculum size of 7%(v/v) [38]. Another study reported an activity of 144.83 ± 2.66 FU/g with an inoculum of 7% using Placket-Burmann and response surface methodology optimization [48]. In our study, the strain produced maximum activity of 239.91 ± 2.43 U/mL with an inoculum volume of 2%.

Purification strategies for NK vary considerably. A classical purification method of using Sephadex G-75 and Phenyl-Sepharose Fast Flow showed a 56.1-fold purification and a 43.2% yield [44]. In another study, a three-step purification process resulted in 32.4-fold purification with 12.4% recovery [50]. Ion Exchange chromatography (DEAE - cellulose) resulted in a 16.46-fold with 30% recovery [51], whereas aqueous two-phase system reported a purification factor of 4.52 and a yield of 76.7% [52]. Similarly, a two-step membrane filtration process, gave a yield of 89.7 ± 8% and a 3-fold increase was observed [53]. Magnetic bead-based affinity purification by p-amino benzamidine-attached magnetic beads observed a recovery of 85% and a purification fold of about 8.7 [54]. Another study by AOT reverse micelles shows an activity of 80% recovery and a purification factor of 3.9 [55]. In the present study, sequential ammonium sulfate precipitation, dialysis, and gel chromatography resulted in 64.1-fold purification with 12.46% recovery. Although recovery was moderate, the purification fold was comparatively higher than several conventional methods.

Holmstrom method was used to confirm the thrombolytic activity of purified enzymes. Clot lysis activity of purified NK from B. aryabhattai RP6 showed an activity of 88.87% after 3 h which is comparable to previously reported values of 97% after 6 h [36] and 60.2% after 120 min [56]. HPLC analysis revealed a retention time of 10.538 min, consistent with reported values of 10.356 min [57], 10.7 min [36], 10.634 min [29], and 10.633 min [58], though minor variation (4.63 min) has also been documented [59], likely due to differences in chromatographic conditions. FTIR analysis displayed characteristic peaks at 3396.02 cm⁻¹ (O–H stretching) and 1635.73 cm⁻¹ (Amide I), consistent with previous reports [29, 36, 56, 60]. SDS-PAGE analysis confirmed a molecular weight of approximately 29 kDa, aligning with reported molecular weights of 27–29 kDa for nattokinase [36, 44, 57]. Considering the probiotic attributes of Bacillus sp. and the clinically relevant fibrinolytic properties of nattokinase [61–62], the characterization of Bacillus aryabhattai RP6 as an efficient NK producer highlights its dual probiotic and therapeutic potential. This expands the diversity of Bacillus strains with potential cardiovascular applications.

Conclusion

Nattokinase, a serine protease, has found its use as a fibrinolytic enzyme. Nattokinase is linked with good cardiovascular health and also described as anti-hypertensive, anti‑atherosclerotic, and lipid‑modulating effects. In our study we have extracted and purified nattokinase from Bacillus aryabhattai RP6 isolated from fermented Brassica oleracea. The wild strain RP6 showed maximum activity of nattokinase in TSB medium. Future studies will be focused on strain improvement strategies, metabolic engineering to enhance the production of nattokinase.

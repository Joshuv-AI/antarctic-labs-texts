Maternal linalool treatment protects against radiofrequency wave-induced deteriorations in adolescent rats: A behavioral and electrophysiological study
Authors: Mansour Azimzadeh, Maryam Noorbakhshnia
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractRecent years, the rapid advancement of technology has raised concerns. We studied the effects of prenatal exposure to 900 MHz radiofrequency (RF) from mobile phones and the protective effects of linalool on learning and memory, and anxiety in adolescent male and female offspring rats. Pregnant rats were divided into four groups: control, wave, wave + linalool, and linalool. Rats received linalool (25mg/kg) by gavage for 21 days. Irradiation was conducted from day 0 to day 21 of pregnancy. Offsprings underwent behavioral and electrophysiological tests on days 50 and 60 after birth. Exposure to RF during pregnancy caused anxiety-like behavior in the EPM test and impairment of learning and memory in the Morris water maze and shuttle box tests. Electrophysiological properties and synaptic plasticity of the dorsal hippocampal CA3-CA1 synapse showed a decrease in fEPSP amplitude and slope. The trace element levels in both male and female offspring were consistent across all groups compared to their respective controls. In the hippocampus tissue, the levels of Fe, Cu, and Mn, as well as the Cu/Zn ratio, were significantly higher in the exposed groups (wave groups) compared to their controls. Moreover, Zn levels were significantly lower in the hippocampus tissue of the exposed groups. Linalool administration mitigated the excessive increase in Fe, Cu, Mn, and Cu/Zn ratio and normalized the disrupted levels of trace elements, except for Zn levels in both male and female offspring. Sex differences were observed in the EPM and shuttle box tests, females were more sensitive than males. In summary, our study demonstrates that prenatal exposure to mobile phone radiation induces stress-like behaviors, disrupts learning and memory, alters hippocampal electrophysiological properties and trace element balance in offspring. Treatment with linalool mitigates these deleterious effects, highlighting its potential as a therapeutic intervention. These findings contribute to our understanding of the impact of prenatal environmental exposures on neurodevelopment and offer insights into potential strategies for neuroprotection.Keywords: Mobile phone, Learning and memory, Linalool, Long-term potentiation, Hippocampus, Offspring ratsSubject terms: Neuroscience, Health care, Medical research, Neurology, Risk factors

Introduction

The rapid advancement of technology and the widespread usage of devices that emit an electromagnetic field (EMF) in daily life have raised concerns about potential adverse health effects. Mobile phones are the most commonly used technology, with the Global System for Mobile Communications (GSM) operating on the frequency bands of 900 MHz and 1800 MHz1. However, current guidelines from the Electronics Engineers (IEEE) and the International Commission on Non-Ionizing Radiation Protection (ICNIRP) do not consider the potential risks for pregnant women and their fetuses. Numerous studies have indicated that the central nervous system (CNS) is particularly sensitive to EMF exposure with adverse effects such as anxiety-like behavior and cognitive defects2,3. Additionally, exposure to radiofrequency fields (RF) disrupts intracellular calcium homeostasis, alters neurotransmitters, and increases blood–brain barrier (BBB) permeability4,5.

Cerebrospinal fluid barrier, and the CSF-brain barrier regulate the balance of these elements within the brain through a complex procedure6. Copper (Cu), Manganese (Mn), Iron (Fe), and Zinc (Zn) are particularly important for the proper functioning and development of the brain. They regulate gene expression, act as catalysts, enzyme activators, second messengers, and protect against various diseases and the production of reactive oxygen species (ROS)7. However, the distribution or shifting of these elements across the brain can lead to neurodegenerative disorders such as Alzheimer's (AD) and Parkinson's disease (PD)8,9.

Furthermore, as redox-active metals, Fe++ and Cu++ generate reactive radicals during cycle reactions, leading to cellular damage such as enzyme depletion and lipid bilayer damage. This damage ultimately results in oxidative stress10. Alterations in brain Zn++ homeostasis can cause behavioral and neurological abnormalities, including impairments in learning, cognitive functions, and AD11. High doses of Mn++ in the brain can lead to neurotoxicity through oxidative stress due to its neurotoxic properties12. Additionally, exposure to stress conditions such as 900 MHz RF has been reported to disrupt brain trace element homeostasis13,14.

(-) Linalool is a prominent volatile component of essential oils in various aromatic plant species and is one of the enantiomers of the natural monoterpenes. It has been used in folk medicine and aromatherapy to treat various illnesses15. Linalool exhibits anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and anxiolytic properties16,17. It also has antinociceptive, anticonvulsant, and anxiolytic that has significant effects on the CNS, and may modulate glutamatergic neurotransmission probably by NMDA receptor interactions18–20. However, its potential protective effects against electromagnetic fields (EMF) have not been studied.

The prenatal period is critical for brain development and can be influenced by external factors. There are growing studies that suggest exposure to RF-EMF during this time can harm infants' CNS, causing behavioral and cognitive issues. Our study examined the effects of prenatal exposure to 900 MHz RF from mobile phones and the potential protective effects of linalool on learning and memory abilities, anxiety, hippocampal synaptic plasticity, and trace elements in adolescent offspring. We used behavioral, biochemical, and electrophysiological techniques to assess these parameters.

Results

Behavioral tests analysis

Elevated plus maze (EPM)

OAT Parameter: The percentage of time spent on the open arm (OAT%) was significantly affected in both male and female offspring (male: F (3, 36) = 6.075, P = 0.0019; female: F (3, 36) = 6.440, P = 0.0013, One way ANOVA) (Fig. 1A). Tukey's multiple comparisons test showed that male and female offspring in the wave group had a significant decrease compared to their same-sex control groups (P < 0.01in both sexes), (Fig. 1A). However, prenatal pre-treatment with linalool significantly prevented the wave-induced changes in OAT (P < 0.05 in both sexes). There was no significant difference in OAT% between males and females in the wave and wave + linalool groups (P > 0.05, unpaired t test).

OAN parameter: The percentage of entries into the open arm (OAN%) showed a significant difference between male and female offspring (male: F (3, 36) = 5.458, P = 0.0034; female: F (3, 36) = 4.802, P = 0.0065, One way ANOVA) (Fig. 1B). Additionally, there was a significant decrease in OAN% in the wave group for both male and female offspring compared to their same-sex control groups (P < 0.01 for males, P < 0.05 for females) (Fig. 1B). The administration of linalool in the wave + linalool group increased the OAN% in both male and female offspring compared to their counterparts in the wave group (P < 0.01 for males; P < 0.05 for females). There was no significant difference in OAN% between male and female offspring (P > 0.05, unpaired t test).

CAT parameter: The percentage of time spent on the close arm (CAT%) differed significantly between male and female rats (male: F (3, 36) = 6.721, P = 0.001; female: F (3, 36) = 7.738, P = 0.0004, One way ANOVA). The post hoc test revealed that rats in the wave group spent significantly more time on the close arm compared to the control group (P < 0.001 for male; P < 0.01 for female) (Fig. 1C). Prenatal pre-treatment with linalool effectively prevented the wave-induced changes in CAT% in both males (P < 0.05) and females (P < 0.01). When comparing male and female offspring, females in the wave exposed group spent more time on the close arm compared to males (73.17 vs. 81.70, respectively, P < 0.05, unpaired t test).

Grooming, rearing, and defecation parameters: The frequency of grooming, rearing and defecation in male and female was significantly different, as shown by One way ANOVA [for males: F (3, 36) = 4.507, P = 0.008; F (3, 36) = 4.286, P = 0.01; F (3, 36) = 4.050, P = 0.01, respectively; and for female (F (3, 36) = 4.255, P = 0.01; F (3, 36) = 8.603, P = 0.0002; F (3, 36) = 4.473, P = 0.009, respectively] (Fig. 1D-F). Post hoc test revealed that the wave group of both males and females showed a significant increase in grooming frequency (P < 0.05 in both sexes) and defecation frequency (P < 0.05 in both sexes) compared to their same-sex control group (Tukey’s multiple comparison test). Rearing frequency significantly decreased in the wav group of both sexes compared to their same sex control groups (male: P < 0.05; female: P < 0.001). Compared to the wave group, rearing frequency showed a significant increase in the wave + linalool group in male and female offspring (P < 0.05 and P < 0.01, respectively). There was a significant decrease in the frequency of rearing in female compared to male offspring (P < 0.05, unpaired t test).

The EPM test showed that exposure to 900 MHz RF mobile phone radiation during pregnancy increased anxiety-like behavior in both male and female offspring. However, the administration of linalool provided significant protection against the effects of mobile waves on the EPM. Our findings indicate that prenatal mobile waves had a greater impact on female offspring than male offspring in rats.

Mooris water maze

The two-way repeated-measures ANOVA analysis revealed that the escape latency and distance traveled to reach the platform decreased over three training sessions for all groups of male and female offspring. The post hoc test indicated that the wave group of both male and female offspring had increased escape latency compared to their same-sex control groups on days two (P < 0.001 in both sexes) and three (male: P < 0.05; female: P < 0.01) of training sessions (Fig. 2A). The same effects were observed for distance in the wave group compared to the control group on days two (male: P < 0.001 in both sexes) and three (male: P < 0.05; female: P < 0.01) (Fig. 2B). Linalool administration in the wave + linalool group significantly improved both parameters (escape latency and distance) compared to the wave group in both genders (Fig. 2A, B). Comparisons between male and female offspring showed no significant differences in escape latency or distance traveled to reach the platform, indicating that male and female offspring performed equally in the learning sessions (P > 0.05, unpaired t test). There were no significant differences in swimming speed during the three training sessions for both sexes (Two-way repeated-measures ANOVA, Tukey's multiple comparisons test P > 0.05) (Fig. 2C).

On the 4th day, a probe test was conducted to assess the reference memory. The wave group, which included both male and female offspring, spent significantly less time in the target quadrant compared to the control group (Fig. 2D). An unpaired t test revealed that both male and female offspring in the wave group spent less time in the target quadrant compared to their same-sex control group (P = 0.01 for males, P = 0.01 for females). However, the wave + linalool group, which received prenatal pretreatment of linalool, showed an increase in time spent in the correct quadrant for both genders and had significant differences compared to the wave group (P < 0.05 for both sexes, unpaired t test). There was no significant difference in time spent in the target quadrant between male and female offspring (P > 0.05). Therefore, the results of the Morris water maze test suggest that prenatal exposure to 900 MHz RF emitted from mobile phones impairs spatial learning and memory in both male and female offspring, regardless of sex differences.

Passive avoidance learning and memory

There was no significant difference in exploratory behavior (step-through latency) between male and female subjects exposed to prenatal mobile phone radiation during the first acquisition trial (P > 0.05, One-way ANOVA, Tukey's multiple comparisons) (Fig. 3A). in addition, there was no significant difference in exploratory behavior between both sexes (P > 0.05, unpaired t test), indicating that prenatal mobile phone exposure had no effect on innate exploratory behavior during the first acquisition trial. However, during the memory retention evaluation, both male and female offspring spent significantly more time in the dark compartment (male: F (3, 36) = 470.5, P < 0.0001; female: F (3, 36) = 661.6, P < 0.0001, One way ANOVA). Post hoc analysis revealed that the wave group spent more time in the dark compartment (TDC) compared to their same-sex control groups (males and females, P < 0.0001, Tukey's multiple comparisons test) (Fig. 3B). Additionally, a significant decrease in TDC was observed in the wave + linalool group compared to the wave group in both sexes (P < 0.0001), indicating the protective effect of prenatal administration of linalool against wave-induced memory impairment on the retention memory of offspring.

A significant difference in TDC was found between male and female groups. The unpaired t test showed that females in the wave group spent more time in the dark compartment compared to males (132 vs. 121.7, P = 0.03). This suggests that prenatal exposure to mobile phone radiation had a greater impact on females' retention memory than on males.

Electrophysiological studies

Input–Output (I/O) functions

A two-way repeated measures ANOVA revealed significant effects of group (treatment) on fEPSP amplitude (F (3, 72) = 7.860, P = 0.0001) and fEPSP slope (F (3, 72) = 9.667, P < 0.0001). However, there were no significant effects of sex on fEPSP amplitude and fEPSP slope in CA1 pyramidal neurons (P > 0.05). (Fig. 4A- D).

Data are expressed as mean ± SEM of eight animals per group. The post hoc test revealed that both male and female wave groups had a significant decrease in fEPSP slope compared to their same-sex counterparts in the control groups (P < 0.005) (Fig. 4A, C). Furthermore, both males and females in the wave groups had a significant decrease in fEPSP amplitude compared to the control group (P < 0.05) (Fig. 4B, D). Prenatal administration of linalool in both genders protected against the adverse effects of mobile phone exposure and significantly increased fEPSP slope of I/O functions compared to the wave-exposed group (P < 0.05). Moreover, there were no significant differences in the fEPSP slope and amplitude of I/O functions between both sexes (Tukey multiple comparisons test, P > 0.05).

In our study, we define LTP induction based on a percent increment in fEPSP slope or amplitude. Specifically, we consider a percent increment of approximately 20 to 25 percent as an index of LTP induction. This threshold is consistent with established criteria and reflects a significant enhancement in synaptic strength indicative of LTP21.

In the evaluation of the evoked fEPSP, HFS induced LTP in both amplitude and slope across all studied groups in male and female offspring. However, in the wave groups, LTP was neither induced nor observed, indicating compromised synaptic plasticity.

The results for male offspring showed significant effects of time on fEPSP amplitude (F (17, 459) = 157.530, Sphericity Assumed, P = 0.000) and fEPSP slope (F (17, 459) = 117.783, Sphericity Assumed, P = 0.000) in a two-way repeated measures ANOVA. There was no significant effect of time*group on fEPSP amplitude, but there was a significant effect on fEPSP slope (F (51, 459) = 2.64, P = 0.000). Between subject effects showed a significant difference in the fEPSP amplitude (F (3, 27) = 3.523, P = 0.02), but not in fEPSP slope.

The post-HFS recording at 90 min revealed significant potentiation in fEPSP amplitude compared to baseline values at 30 min across all groups (Two-way ANOVA, repeated measure): control group (141.8 ± 9.9, F (1.56, 10.9) = 33.82, P = 0.000); linalool group (135.3 ± 11.3, F(1.26, 8.8) = 27.26, P = 0.000); and wave + linalool group (130.6 ± 11.8, F (1.2, 7.2) = 14.94, P = 0.005). This potentiation was also observed in fEPSP slope: control group (131.6 ± 8.4, F (1.87, 13.09) = 18.68, P = 0.000); linalool group (126.7 ± 14.5, F (1.35, 9.4) = 10.42, P = 0.007); and wave + linalool group (120.2 ± 8.8, F (1.29, 7.7) = 13.6, P = 0.005). However, both the induction and maintenance of LTP were compromised in the fEPSP amplitude and slope of the wave group (amplitude: 103 ± 6.2; slope: 111.4 ± 11.1). All analyses were subjected to Greenhouse–Geisser correction.

The comparison of average potentiation values of fEPSP amplitude for post-HFS recording revealed that the control group had a significantly higher value compared to the wave group (141.8 ± 9 vs. 103 ± 6, respectively, P = 0.005, LSD test). Additionally, the wave + linalool group exhibited a significantly higher value compared to the wave group (130.6 ± 1 vs. 103 ± 6, respectively, P = 0.04, LSD test). However, no significant effect was observed in fEPSP slope between groups (P > 0.05, LSD test).

The results for female offspring showed significant effects of time on fEPSP amplitude (F (17, 408) = 199.049, P = 0.000), and fEPSP slope (F (17, 408) = 114.924, P = 0.000) in a two-way repeated measures ANOVA. There was no significant effect of time*group on fEPSP amplitude and fEPSP slope. Between subject effects showed a significant difference in the fEPSP amplitude (F (3, 24) = 4.686, P = 0.01), but not in the fEPSP slope.

The post-HFS recording at 90 min revealed significant potentiation in fEPSP amplitude compared to baseline values at 30 min across all groups (Two-way ANOVA, repeated measure): control group (145.1 ± 13, F (1.23, 7.4) = 14.94, P = 0.004); linalool group (136.7 ± 9.5, F (1.21, 7.2) = 22.37, P = 0.001); and wave + linalool group (127.3 ± 12.7, F(1, 6.3) = 14.31, P = 0.008). This potentiation was similarly observed in fEPSP slope: control group (134.6 ± 10.6, F (1.57, 9.4) = 14.4, P = 0.002); linalool group (129.4 ± 14.4, F (1.34, 8) = 8.35, P = 0.01); and wave + linalool group (126.2 ± 7.8, F (1.6, 9.6) = 16.42, P = 0.001). However, both the induction and maintenance of LTP were compromised in the fEPSP amplitude and slope of the wave group (amplitude: 94.1 ± 3.6; slope: 103 ± 8.1). All analyses were subjected to Greenhouse–Geisser correction.

The average potentiation values of fEPSP amplitude for post-HFS recording were compared among groups. The control group exhibited a significantly higher value compared to the wave group (145.1 ± 13 vs. 94.1 ± 3, respectively, P = 0.002, LSD test). Furthermore, the wave + linalool group demonstrated a significantly higher value compared to the wave group (127.3 ± 1 vs. 94.1 ± 3, respectively, P = 0.03, LSD test). Additionally, a significant increase in fEPSP slope was observed between the control and wave groups (134.6 ± 13 vs. 103 ± 8, respectively, P = 0.04, LSD).

Therefore, our results indicate that exposure to mobile phone radiation during pregnancy significantly affected both fEPSP amplitude and slope in both male and female offspring in the wave group compared to their same-sex control group. Additionally, linalool treatment in the wave + linalool group improved fEPSP amplitude and slope, demonstrating neuroprotective effects.

Trace elements analysis

A one-way ANOVA analysis revealed significant alterations in the concentrations of trace elements in the hippocampus of male and female offspring exposed to mobile phone radiation (Fig. 8). In both genders, the wave group had higher levels of Fe (male: P < 0.01; female: P < 0.001), Mn (P < 0.01 in both sexes), Cu (P < 0.05 in both sexes), and Cu/Zn (P < 0.0001), and lower levels of Zn (P < 0.0001) compared to their control groups. However, prenatal linalool administration prevented these effects, significantly decreased Fe (in both sexes: P < 0.05), Mn (male: P < 0.01; female: P < 0.05), Cu (P < 0.05 in both sexes), Zn (male: P < 0.05) and Cu/Zn (in both sexes: P < 0.01) in the wave + linalool group compared to their control groups. Linalool also improved trace element imbalances induced by mobile phone radiation exposure. Furthermore, there were no significant differences in trace element levels between the wave and wave + linalool groups in male and female offspring (unpaired t test, P > 0.05).

Discussion

Here we performed a sequence of experiments to evaluate the protective effects of prenatal administration of linalool against defects induced by radiofrequency waves on anxiety-like behavior, spatial and passive avoidance learning and memory, hippocampal trace elements, and CA1 LTP in adolescent male and female rats.

The anxiety-like behavior was evaluated using the EPM, a rodent anxiety test that measures exploration of a new environment and innate avoidance of open arms22. The wave-exposed group exhibited decreased open arm time and entries, increased close arm time, altered grooming and rearing frequencies, and increased fear-induced defecation compared to their control group. Prenatal exposure to mobile phone radiation significantly led to anxiety-like behavior in both male and female rat offspring. There was a sex difference in the EPM test. Females' offspring were more affected than males; they spent more time on the closed arm which indicates that females displayed more anxiety-like behavior than males. Interestingly, prenatal administration of linalool protected offspring rats against anxiety-induced mobile phone radiation exposure.

Then, we evaluated the MWM test and found that the wave-exposed group had higher latency and escape time compared to their respective control groups. Prenatal exposure to mobile phone radiation also affected memory performance in the passive avoidance test. The wave-exposed group spent more time in the dark compartment. Besides, female offspring were more affected in the passive avoidance test. Furthermore, prenatal exposure to mobile phone radiation impaired spatial memory and passive avoidance learning. On the other hand, treatment with linalool improved performance in both males and females.

To our knowledge, no research has been conducted on the impacts of prenatal exposure to mobile phones on offspring anxiety levels. However, there are reports in adult rats. A study found that exposure to 900 MHz mobile phone radiation (1 h/day for 28 days, SAR = 1.15 W/kg) resulted in increased anxiety-like behavior23. Another study reported anxiety-like behavior in male mice following exposure to 1800 MHz frequency (for 28 days, 6 h/d, SAR = 2.7—2.2 W/kg)24. Additionally, studies have documented the anxiogenic effects of exposure to extremely low-frequency magnetic fields (ELF MF) in humans, rats, and mice25–27. A study on adult rats examined the impact of exposure to 900 MHz mobile phones (4 h/day for 15 days, SAR = 0.9 W/kg). It found increased anxiety-like behavior and impaired learning ability3. Another study has reported, deficits in spatial learning and memory, as well as motor coordination issues in mice exposed to ELF-MF (50 Hz, 1 mT)28. Prenatal exposure to 900 MHz radiofrequency (1 h/day for 21 days) resulted in impairments in passive avoidance learning and memory in offspring29. In line with our study, female rats were more affected in passive avoidance performance with respect to males30. Also, rat offspring exposed to 900 MHz radiation prenatally (either 2 or 4 h a day over 21 days) experienced impairments in passive avoidance function31. Moreover, different studies have shown detrimental effects of prenatal stress on cognitive functions32,33.

In our electrophysiological experiments conducted on the dorsal hippocampus, we assessed the excitability of the CA3-CA1 pathway using an input/output (I/O) protocol. This involved applying increasing intensities ranging from 100–100 μA at the Schaffer collaterals to evoke large fEPSPs in the CA1 pyramidal neurons. Our findings revealed a significant reduction in excitability among the wave groups of both sexes following prenatal exposure to mobile phone radiation. Notably, this effect was mitigated by linalool treatment. The processes of long-term potentiation and neuronal excitability in CA1 pyramidal neurons play crucial role in memory performance. Given that the induction threshold for LTP is contingent upon hippocampal excitability levels34, the diminished excitability observed in the CA1 neurons resulted in both failed induction and maintenance of LTP within the wave groups. However, prenatal administration of linalool effectively prevented this outcome. Several behavioral and histopathological studies were performed in confirmation of our findings. Therefore, it can be concluded that exposure to stressors such as mobile phone radiation during the prenatal period, a crucial stage for brain development, may disrupt neuronal electrophysiological properties and synaptic plasticity.

Interestingly, the results of the present study suggest that prenatal oral administration of linalool (at a dose of 25 mg/kg) can protect against the adverse effects caused by prenatal exposure to mobile phone radiation. There is an increasing number of evidence that indicates the neuroprotective properties of linalool. For instance, a study reported that linalool (25 mg/kg, administered every 48 h for 3 months) was able to reverse neuropathological and behavioral impairments in old triple-transgenic Alzheimer's mice. In that study, linalool enhanced spatial learning and memory and reduced anxiety35. Other studies, in mice or rats, have demonstrated the anxiolytic behavior following inhalation of linalool19,36. Also, it has been shown that linalool could improve passive avoidance learning and memory in mice deprived of REM sleep following37. However, some studies have reported that injection of linalool with high doses (50, or 100, 125 mg/kg) impaired memory acquisition in the inhibitory avoidance learning or did not have anxiolytic effects in the EMP test38,39.

Moreover, linalool has been studied for its role in protecting against oxidative stress-induced neurodegeneration. It can reduce oxidative stress caused by mitochondrial dysfunction and glutamate /NMDA toxicity40. Linalool increases mitochondrial respiration, improves structure, restores ATP levels, and blocks NMDA receptors41,42. It also mediates anxiety-related behavior by increasing GABAA receptor activity43. One dose linalool administration (100 mg/kg) has protected against cognitive deficits caused by oxidative stress and increased the expression of synapse plasticity-related proteins. This suggests linalool may improve cognitive function by enhancing synaptic plasticity.

Oxidative stress from EMF exposure is linked to its negative effects on the brain. Studies show that different types of EMFs can induce oxidative stress44,45. Mobile phone exposure can lead to oxidative stress and adverse effects on the brain, potentially impacting learning and memory. Linalool may have neuroprotective effects by reducing oxidative stress in our study.

This study also discovered imbalances in trace elements in the brains of offspring exposed to electromagnetic waves. Prenatal exposure to 900 MHz RF from mobile phones increased levels of Fe, Cu, Mn, and the Cu/Zn ratio, while decreased levels of Zn in the hippocampus tissue. Previous research on rats showed similar effects, but treatment with vitamins E and C improved and normalized the imbalances13,46. We proposed two potential mechanisms for this issue: oxidative stress, and increased BBB permeability. Other studies have found similar results after exposure to 950 MHz RF for 8 weeks14,47. Conditions like Alzheimer's disease, Parkinson's disease, and cognitive impairments may be caused by changes in brain trace element homeostasis8,11. The Cu/Zn ratio has been used as a marker for inflammation and oxidative stress in vivo48. This study revealed that maternal linalool administration significantly improved and normalized the adverse effects of prenatal mobile phone exposure, specifically the decreased levels of Fe, Cu, and Mn. However, linalool pre-treatment did not show improvement in Zn levels, suggesting higher levels of Zn were consumed to counteract the negative effects of exposure. Therefore, we propose a new neuroprotective mechanism for linalool based on its positive effects on brain trace element homeostasis.

Studies on sex differences in behavior have conflicting results. Some suggest that female rats display less anxiety-like behavior in the EPM test49,50, while others do not find this difference51,52. Some studies have reported that females exhibit more anxiety-like behavior in other tests53,54. It has also been reported that females have a weaker representation of their environment compared to males55. However, when stress is applied, different results have been reported. For example, adult female rats that were prenatally stressed showed impairments in long-term memory and increased anxiety-like behavior in the EPM test30,33. Therefore, it may be hypothesis that excessive secretion of stress hormones during pregnancy and exposure of the fetus to high levels of these hormones may impact the function of the CNS. This can result in changes in the development of the nervous system, potentially leading to cognitive impairments in adulthood56,57. While the exact mechanisms are not fully understood, this relationship is an area of ongoing research.

In electrophysiological studies, some results suggest sex-dependent differences in LTP induction in the hippocampal CA1 or dentate gyrus58,59, while others found no differences60,61. Similarly, in amygdala LTP, some studies observed higher induction in female mice compared to males62, while others found no sex differences63.

Conclusion

The present study found that exposure to mobile phone radiation during pregnancy (at a frequency of 900 MHz) can adversely impact brain development in offspring. This can lead to behavioral issues such as anxiety, impaired learning and memory, disrupted brain trace element balance, and altered electrophysiological properties and synaptic plasticity at the hippocampal CA3-CA1 synapse. Importantly, our findings demonstrate the protective effects of linalool treatment against these adverse effects. Linalool effectively mitigated stress-like behaviors, restored learning and memory function, normalized electrophysiological properties and synaptic plasticity, and rebalanced hippocampal trace elements in offspring exposed prenatally to mobile phone radiation. These results underscore the potential of linalool as a therapeutic agent for mitigating the harmful effects of prenatal mobile phone radiation exposure on neurodevelopment. Future research should further investigate the underlying mechanisms of linalool's neuroprotective effects and explore its potential clinical applications in mitigating prenatal environmental exposures. Overall, our study contributes to the growing body of literature on the impact of prenatal environmental exposures on neurodevelopment and highlights potential strategies for intervention and prevention.

Materials and methods

Animals and ethics

All experimental protocols were approved by the animal ethics committee of the University of Isfahan in accordance with the guidelines of National Institutes of Health for the care and use of laboratory animals (8th edition, National Academies Press, 2011) and in accordance with ARRIVE guidelines. Wistar rats weighing 200–220 g, were obtained from the breeding colony of Department Plan and Animal Biology, University of Isfahan. The rats were housed in polycarbonate cages measuring 42 cm in length, 26.5 cm in width, and 15 cm in height, and kept under controlled conditions: 22 ± 2 °C and a 12-h light/dark cycle (lights-on at 7.00 a.m.). They had free access to food and water.

Experimental design

To initiate pregnancy, female rats were paired with fertile male rats (2 females and 1 male per cage). The presence of a vaginal plug was examined daily. In majority of rats, it was observed following day of mating and considered as day 0 of gestation.

The female rats were then randomly assigned to four groups: (each consisting of 5 females).

Control group (received saline).

Control group (received saline).

Linalool group (received 25 mg/kg linalool).

Linalool group (received 25 mg/kg linalool).

Wave group (received RF + saline).

Wave group (received RF + saline).

Wave + linalool group (received RF and 25 mg/kg linalool, 30 min before exposure).

Wave + linalool group (received RF and 25 mg/kg linalool, 30 min before exposure).

Pregnant dams were irradiated from day zero (0) of gestation and continued for 21 days. The study included 20 female rats, with five rats in each group. On day 21, pups were weaned and separated by gender. From each dam, three to five pups were selected to create the corresponding male or female group. They randomly were divided into four groups with male gender and four groups with female gender (n = 10 in each group). Both saline and linalool were administered orally through gavage for 21 days. Dams gave birth around the 21st to 23rd day of gestation. Behavioral experiments were conducted on the 50th day and an electrophysiological study was performed on the 60th day of the pups' lives.

Irradiation

A Nokia 1616 mobile phone, with a specific absorption rate (SAR) of 1.19 W/kg (head) and 0.32 W/kg (body), operating on the GSM 900 MHz frequency, was used to emit RF-EMR to dams in groups 3 and 4 for 1 h and 40 min per day over 21 days. The phone was positioned 20 cm away from the cages and was continuously activated by generating 100 missed calls per day in silent mode, each call lasting 46 s and followed by a14-second break before the next call23,31. Behavioral studies

Elevated plus maze (EPM)

EPM is a commonly used method to assess anxiety in laboratory animals. The apparatus consists of a wooden structure with four arms: two open arms measuring 50 cm long, and two closed arms measuring 50 cm long, 10 cm width, and 40 cm height. These arms are connected by a central platform measuring 10 cm by 10 cm, positioned 50 cm above the floor of the experimental room. During the EPM experiment, each rat was placed on the central platform of the apparatus, facing the open arms. Various behavioral parameters were recorded, including the percentage of entries into the open or closed arms (calculated as 100 multiplied by the number of entries into open or closed arms divided by the total number of entries), and the percentage of time spent in the open or closed arms (calculated as 100 multiplied by the time spent in open or closed arms divided by the total time). We also evaluated the frequency of rearing, grooming, and defecation. The observation period lasted for up to five minutes. To maintain cleanliness and prevent cross-contamination between trials, the apparatus was thoroughly cleaned using cotton balls saturated with 70% ethanol.

Morris water maze (MWM)

The MWM was used to evaluate spatial learning and memory. The device was a 150 cm diameter black circular pool filled with water at 24 °C, divided into 4 quadrants. An 11 cm diameter Plexiglas escape platform was placed 2 cm below the water surface in a randomly assigned quadrant, with spatial cues around the room. Rats were given 60 s to swim freely without the platform to acclimate to the pool and environment. The learning phase consisted of 3 consecutive days with 4 trial sessions per day. Rats were released in one of the quadrants and given 60 s to find the hidden platform. If they did not find it, they were guided to the platform and left there for 30 s. Escape latency, swimming speed, and distance were recorded. On the fourth day, the platform was removed, and a probe test was performed to record the time spent in the target quadrant.

Passive avoidance learning and memory

The experiment used a device consisting of two equal-sized boxes (30 × 25 × 25 cm3), one light and one dark, connected by a guillotine door manufactured by Borj Sanat Co, Iran. During the training trial, each rat was placed in the lighted box facing away from the guillotine for 15 s. The door was then opened and the latency to enter the dark box was recorded. Rats that did not enter the dark box within 120 s were excluded from the experiment. Once the rat entered the dark box with all four paws, the door was closed, and the rat was allowed to stay in the dark box for 30 s. After 30 min, the process was repeated. When the rat entered the dark box, the door was closed, and an electric shock was delivered to the rat's foot through stainless steel rods (2.5 mm diameter with 1 cm space between them). The electric shock was a 50 Hz square wave with a strength of 0.1 mA and lasted for 1 s. Two minutes later, the procedure was repeated until the rat stayed in the light box for 120 s, indicating successful learning. After 24 h, a retention test was conducted using the same method as the initial learning trial. However, this time, the door did not close after the rat entered the light box, and no foot shock was administered. The duration of time spent in the dark box was recorded for up to 10 min.

Electrophysiological study

Surgical methods

Each rat was anesthetized with urethane (1.5 g/kg, intraperitoneal, Sigma-Aldrich Co., USA) and underwent stereotaxic surgery using a stereotaxic device (Stoelting, USA). For stimulation, a bipolar electrode and for recording, a unipolar electrode (stainless-steel Teflon isolated wire; Model 91,500, A-M Systems Inc., USA) were utilized. The bipolar stimulating electrode was placed in the Schaffer collateral pathway of the right hippocampus (AP = − 4.2 mm; ML = 3.8 mm; DV = − 2.7 to − 3.8 mm). To reach the right CA1 area a unipolar recording electrode was lowered at a 52.5° angle from the left side of the skull (AP = − 3.4 mm; ML = 1.5 mm; DV = -4.4 − − 5.1 mm) until the maximum response was obtained64. The electrodes were carefully lowered and positioned at the intended depth to minimize damage to the brain tissue.

Field excitatory postsynaptic potential (fEPSP)

One common method for studying synaptic plasticity, such as long-term potentiation (LTP), is through extracellular recording of the field excitatory postsynaptic potential (fEPSP) waveform. In this study, Schaffer collateral pathway was stimulated using a constant current stimulator device (e-Pulse, Science beam, Tehran, Iran), and the resulting extracellular evoked responses were recorded (e-Wave, Science beam, Tehran, Iran) from the CA1 pyramidal cell population. The fEPSPs were evoked in the CA1 region using 0.1 Hz stimulation, amplified at × 1000, and filtered at 1–3 kHz). The signals were then transmitted to a computer via an analog-to-digital interface (Science Beam, eProbe-eTrace Experiment software), and the data were subsequently analyzed offline using the eTrace Analysis software (Science Beam; Parto Danesh Co., Tehran, Iran). Test pulses were applied by applying a 150-ms, square, monophasic single pulse to find the maximum fEPSP at the Schaffer collateral-CA1 synapse.

Input–Output (I/O) curves and long-term potentiation (LTP)

Before the induction of LTP, stimulus–response functions also known as I/O curves were obtained by systematically changing the stimulus current within the range of 100–1000 μA. After obtaining the I/O curve, the intensity of the applied stimuli was adjusted to achieve approximately 50% of the maximal response of the fEPSP slope. Once the steady-state baseline response of fEPSPs was established, baseline recordings were taken thirty minutes before and ninety minutes after the LTP induction to identify any changes in the synaptic responses of the CA1 pyramidal neurons. LTP was induced using high-frequency stimulation (HFS) protocol of 100 Hz, consisting of 4 bursts of 50 stimuli, with a stimulus duration of 0.15 ms and an inter-burst interval of 10 s. LTP was quantified by measuring alterations in the initial slope and amplitude of the fEPSPs and expressing them as a percentage of the initial baseline value, which was observed for ninety minutes following the tetanic stimulation.

Harvesting of the brain tissue and trace elements evaluations

After each rat completed the electrophysiological study, it was euthanized by decapitation. The brain was immediately removed, and the hippocampus was separated and stored at -70 °C for later analysis of trace element levels (Cu, Fe, Zn, and Mn) using atomic absorption spectrophotometry (Shimadzu AA-670). The UV–Visible detector settings were as follows: Cu: 324.8 nm, Fe: 248.3 nm, Zn: 213.9 nm, and Mn: 279.6 nm. The samples were dried at 60°C for 24 h, weighed, and then dissolved in a combination of nitric acid and perchloric acid (in a 7:3 ratio, respectively). The mixture was then placed in a water bath at 55°C until complete digestion occurred13.

Statistical analysis

All data were presented as mean ± SEM. Statistical analyses were performed using the Statistical Package for Social Sciences and GraphPad Prism® 8.0.1. Two-way ANOVA repeated measures analysis of variance followed by LSD or Tukey's multiple comparisons test was used to analyze the data for LTP induction and maintenance, as well as the I/O curve. Behavioral and trace elements data were analyzed using two-way and one-way ANOVA followed by post hoc Tukey's multiple comparisons test. An unpaired t-test was used to compare males and females. The significance level was set at a P-value of ≤ 0.05.

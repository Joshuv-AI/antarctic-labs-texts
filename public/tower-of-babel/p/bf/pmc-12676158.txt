The origins of Morbidity and Mortality (M&M) conferences can be traced back to the early 20th century, beginning with the 1910 Flexner Report1 on medical education and the establishment of the American College of Surgeons in 1912. Initially attended by surgeons and anesthesiologists, these conferences aimed to improve surgical practice by examining medical errors. Despite their widespread adoption, traditional M&M conferences vary significantly in format and objectives across institutions. In academic settings, case presentations are typically led by trainees. However, the goals of these conferences are often ill-defined. The predominant focus on error identification and adverse events can inadvertently foster a punitive environment, limiting open discussion and learning. Similar challenges have been recognized in cardiology and are described in the literature.2

There is growing recognition of the need to redesign M&M conferences to emphasize collaboration and system-level quality improvement (QI). The proposed Morbidity, Mortality, and Improvement (MM&I) format3,4 shifts the focus to systemic issues and encourages participation from a broader range of stakeholders, including nurses, pharmacists, and hospital administrators.3

However, guidance on designing a modern, cardiology-specific M&M conference remains limited. Recognizing this gap, our team at the Medical College of Wisconsin restructured our cardiology M&M conference to foster open dialog, interdisciplinary collaboration, and actionable strategies for improvement.

Project Rationale

The previously established M&M conference format at our institution was well-intentioned but lacked consistency and structure. Fellows were well-prepared and selected appropriate cases. However, there was variability in educational content, inconsistent faculty sponsorship, and limited discussion of systemic issues or QI opportunities. These limitations prompted a redesign to align with the MM&I model and foster a more psychologically safe and productive learning environment. Additional context for the project's inspiration is provided in the Supplemental Materials.

Project Description

The previous cardiology M&M conference at the Medical College of Wisconsin was led by a cardiology fellow and featured case presentations with educational slides, following a 60-minute format. Attendees included cardiology faculty, fellows, and advanced practice providers, with audience participation encouraged.

Between July and December 2022, several strengths were noted: fellows were well-prepared, case selection was appropriate, and educational content was evidence-based. However, areas for improvement included a lack of standardized presentation format, inconsistent faculty sponsorship, variable audience engagement, and minimal discussion of systemic issues or QI initiatives. Project Deliverables

The redesigned M&M conference format was launched in January 2023, with ongoing evaluation through June 2024. In December 2023, the team established an M&M Board Review Committee to support longitudinal tracking and accountability. This committee comprised the cardiology fellowship program director, PSQO, and QI chief fellow.

A structured tracking sheet was developed to systematically document key elements from each conference, including:•Main discussion points•System-level issues identified•Actionable items•Proposed project ideas•QI initiatives

Discussion

Cardiovascular medicine encompasses a wide range of invasive, semi-invasive, and noninvasive procedures, yet there is limited guidance on how to design an M&M conference that balances educational value with systemic improvement. There has been a growing call to modernize the M&M conference format to better align with the current emphasis on system-level quality and outcomes.

Our framework draws from recent literature that advocates for structured, psychologically safe, and interdisciplinary approaches to M&M. Several key studies support the principles underlying our redesign. A comprehensive review is beyond the scope of this manuscript but is available in the Supplemental Materials.

In 2008, Deis et al3 proposed an MM&I format, emphasizing nonjudgmental case discussions, transparent accountability, and the creation of workgroups to address systemic failures. Their findings demonstrated that a structured, hospital-wide MM&I conference effectively engages physicians, nurses, and administrative leaders in meaningful dialog around adverse events.

In 2023, Beaulieu-Jones et al5 conducted a systematic review to identify optimal formats and attributes of M&M conferences that advance QI and practitioner education.

In 2019, McNamara et al2 proposed a cardiology-specific M&M structure, recommending a stepwise framework for planning, execution, and follow-up. The authors emphasized the importance of involving FITs and early-career cardiologists (ECs) to ensure long-term success.

We offer the following key considerations for successful future implementation of the MM&I conference format:•Overcoming barriers to structural change•Improving interdepartmental and interspecialty communication•Securing institutional resources and leadership support•Ensuring consistent, meaningful participation across disciplines•Promoting the use of audience response systems to enhance engagement•Defining metrics to evaluate success and ensure sustainability

The M&M conference holds immense potential as a platform for QI and patient safety (PS). With institutional support and stakeholder engagement, it can serve as a gateway for FITs to lead QI initiatives and contribute to scholarly work. A modernized M&M format can position cardiovascular divisions at the forefront of safe, high-quality care delivery.

Conclusions

Redesigning the cardiology M&M conference as a more conversational and solution-focused forum fostered a culture of collaboration, learning, and systems-based improvement. By reducing punitive elements and introducing structured follow-up through the M&M Board Review, the initiative enhanced engagement and empowered participants to pursue QI with greater confidence.

This initiative highlights the importance of psychologically safe learning environments in promoting meaningful advancements in patient care. Future progress should be guided by a formal steering committee and ongoing refinements to the conference structure based on participant feedback.

Ultimately, the M&M conference remains a highly productive format for identifying areas of improvement and initiating change across multiple levels. It also serves as a gateway for FITs and ECs to lead QI and PS projects and contribute to scholarly work.

Study on regional disparities and influencing factors of public health service supply in China from the perspective of fairness
Authors: Shengjiao Zhu, Jinsi Liu, Zhihua Wang, Shixiang Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe public health service supply level is an essential factor affecting public health, and fairness is vital in distributing public health services. The spatiotemporal differences in public health services have attracted academic attention. However, few studies have systematically analyzed regional disparities and their influencing factors from a fair perspective. Therefore, this paper aims to explore the spatiotemporal distribution of public health service supply in China and its influencing factors from the standpoint of fairness.MethodsFirst, based on the panel data of 31 provinces in China from 2010 to 2023, this paper constructs an objective evaluation index system for the supply level of public health services. Secondly, through the empirical analysis of the Dagum Gini coefficient, the temporal and spatial differences in the supply of public health services are explored. Regional disparities are analyzed from the perspective of fairness. Finally, the Tobit model is used to examine the impact of factors such as the proportion of the elderly population, the level of economic development, and demographic factors on the fairness of public health service supply.ResultsThe study found that China’s public health service supply has improved significantly since the new medical reform. However, there are still large differences between different regions, and the problem of unbalanced development within the region is prominent. The eastern coastal areas show a “polarization” trend, with hot spots and weak areas in public health service supply coexisting. Simultaneously, Tobit regression confirms that the deepening of aging and improving economic development significantly drive the supply of public health services.ConclusionsThe government should reasonably allocate public health resources according to the actual conditions of each region to achieve a “trickle-down effect.” Especially in underdeveloped areas, the government should achieve fairness in service supply through precise policies. Concurrently, a cross-regional counterpart support mechanism should be established to promote soft power support, such as talent and technology. Medical and health services should be provided to the elderly and vulnerable groups through medical care and health industry models. Finally, relying on “Internet + medical health” to break down resource barriers and build a digital service platform covering the entire urban and rural areas to achieve fair distribution of public health resources and a “diffusion effect”.Keywords: Health equity, Public health services, Supply level, Regional distribution, Influencing factors

Introduction

The provision of public health services is a primary responsibility of the government. At the same time, it is also a long-term and fundamental institutional arrangement of the government. To meet the public’s demand for public health services, the government has formulated and implemented numerous medical and health policies to improve social health levels. Introducing and implementing public health service policies have improved regional medical and health services, especially the ability to respond quickly to sudden outbreaks. Therefore, public health services safeguard the people’s fundamental health rights and interests and promote social equity. The government should earnestly perform its public health service functions, implement the value orientation of fairness in allocating medical resources, and demonstrate the government’s governance level and ability. This is a prerequisite for achieving equity in public health services. For example, the Outline of the Healthy China 2030 Plan clearly states that urban and rural residents should enjoy equal essential public health services, emphasizing the equitable public health supply. Thus, it is imperative to understand the reasons for differences in the provision of public health services.

China has a relatively large population base, especially with the intensification of aging, which has brought considerable challenges to the government’s provision of public health services. According to data from the National Bureau of Statistics, the proportion of China’s population aged 65 and above was 8.9% in 2010, and the index reached 15.4% in 2023. The index change shows that the aging population is large in scale and growing rapidly, which has caused an all-around impact on the public health system. Since China’s new medical reform, the government has attached great importance to developing public health services. On the one hand, it has increased funding for public health services. On the other hand, it has continuously optimized resource allocation and improved the service system. From this point of view, the public health service supply level determines the government’s ability to respond to public health emergencies. In 2009, the State Council of China issued the “Opinions on Deepening the Reform of the Medical and Health System,” marking the beginning of the “Healthy China” strategy. The medical system reform has deepened further, so the reform in 2009 is called the “New Medical Reform.” In 2016, China prioritized health development as one of the country’s overall strategic deployment principles. In 2017, the 19th National Congress report identified “Healthy China” as a national strategy. In 2020, relevant public health policy documents were released one after another. Among them, the National Health Commission issued the “Guiding Opinions on Strengthening the Performance Assessment of Primary Medical and Health Institutions,” which the primary medical care assessment form has been stipulated in the form of a policy.

China’s medical system reform is still in progress, and the level of public health services has been rapidly improved. A relatively complete public health service system has been established, and remarkable results have been achieved in medical services [1]. However, significant differences in economic development levels, geographical locations, and cultural factors between regions make the medical and health services levels different [2]. High-quality public health resources are concentrated in large cities, while the quality of public health services in economically underdeveloped areas needs further improvement [3]. In response to the growing demand for medical services and continuing to improve the level of essential public health services, it is also necessary to rationally allocate medical resources in space and appropriately allocate them towards underdeveloped areas. Only by controlling the medical resources between regions can the social goal of “health for all” be achieved. In recent years, the Chinese government has established “equalization of basic public services”, “balanced regional development”, “Healthy China 2030”, and “sharing the fruits of development” as core policy orientations. These strategies promote the fair distribution of health resources through institutions. In 2023, the “Opinions on Further Improving the Medical and Health Service System” was issued to resolve the imbalance in regional allocation of medical and health resources by designing a systematic correction mechanism.

In the study of the public health service supply level, there is a lack of empirical research that considers fairness. For this reason, to solve the fairness dilemma in regional public health service supply, this study focuses on the regional disparities and influencing factors of China’s public health service supply. This aims to clarify the actual picture of the level of public health service supply and reveal the different characteristics of the public health service supply level index in other regions. And identify the multiple factors affecting the level of public health service supply. Therefore, this paper focuses on the following three issues. (1) Construct a comprehensive evaluation index system for the public health service supply level to systematically measure the spatiotemporal differentiation characteristics of regional service supply capacity. Focus on analyzing the dynamic changes in the supply level before and after the epidemic. (2) Deconstruct the structural differences in the public health service supply level and explore the correlation between spaces. (3) Identify the factors affecting the public health service supply level, aiming to provide suggestions and references for the government to introduce reasonable public health service policies. Research framework

Literature review

Public health services are essential health services that the public sector provides to protect and promote public health. The service targets are the entire society or specific groups of people, including disease prevention and control, maternal and child health and care, public health emergencies, and rescue.

(1) Regarding the value of public health service supply, public health services cover health improvement, maintenance, and health services [4]. Then, scholars began to study the value of public health services. Generally speaking, public health services are divided into two orientations: value rationality and instrumental rationality. Public and fairness are the primary considerations. In addition, the adjustment and allocation of health resources also need to consider their economic value, considering both efficiency and fairness [5]. In health equity-related research, unfair and inefficient allocation of health resources is a global problem. Although New Zealand’s health system has universal and innovative services, it still faces persistent equity issues. This requires strengthening the system culture and capacity to improve the equity of health outcomes for different population groups [6]. Primary healthcare facilities in Nouna, a rural area of Burkina Faso in Africa, are inefficient, with underutilization of facilities and distance being the main influencing factors [7]. There is inequality in the distribution of medical facilities in Markazi Province, Iran [8]. Scholars have proposed a new global health model that balances human health and ecological sustainability [9]. Japan’s medical resources are unevenly distributed and must be readjusted to achieve a more balanced geographical distribution and an effective medical system [10]. China’s health resources are unevenly distributed across regions [11]. For example, regarding population distribution, China’s maternal and child health care human resources are relatively fair, but unevenly distributed across geographical regions [12]. Some scholars have discussed the Chinese public’s satisfaction with health care and their perception of fairness in health care [13]. There are significant differences in the perception of equality or welfare between different demographic characteristics, and some people believe that the accessibility of health care resources lacks fairness [14].

(2) In the research on public health service performance evaluation, the balanced scorecard is first used to develop an evaluation index system, and the weight of the index system is determined by the Delphi method [15]. Questionnaires and interviews are used to evaluate the performance of primary health institutions [16]. Secondly, the national public health service performance standards are used to assess local public health service institutions [17]. The “Government Performance and Results Act” in the United States is conducive to improving the quality of its public health services, thereby increasing the public’s confidence in the US government [18]. Thirdly, many studies directly evaluate the performance of public hospitals [19]. For example, indicators such as the number of beds and operating rooms are used as input variables, and indicators such as discharge, emergency, and surgical intervention are used as outcome variables to measure the efficiency level of public hospitals [20].

(3) Many scholars have analyzed the study of public health service supply from different perspectives. First, the evaluation goal is to improve the performance of public health services. In addition to improving the efficiency of public health services [21], it is also necessary to give full play to the government’s macro-control capabilities and achieve fair distribution to meet the needs of more people [22]. Second, many studies focus on the level of public health service supply. In the study of systematic literature review, scholars traced the dynamics and boundaries of public-private partnerships in the healthcare sector in low- and middle-income countries [23]. There are problems with the quality of public maternal and child health care services in Laos. Scholars have proposed countermeasures from both the services’ supply and demand sides [24]. In addition, some studies use case studies and interview methods to strengthen public-private partnerships and effectively promote the supply of essential public health services [25]. Data envelopment analysis is used to evaluate the health service capacity of county-level public health departments [26]. Additionally, some studies have explored the quality of district health services after China’s new medical reform and found that the quality of community health services has significantly improved after the medical overhaul [27]. Of course, some scholars have also constructed an essential public service evaluation index system from the output perspective [28]. Different evaluation index systems cover social supervision and economic level [29]. For example, a study on the relationship between fairness and efficiency of public health services in African countries showed that the number of nurses and the income gap strongly affected the efficiency of public health services [30]. Finally, the imbalance in the allocation of public health service resources has aroused discussion among many scholars. The most obvious is the huge gap between urban and rural areas, which has led more people to seek better medical services in county-level cities [31]. The spending levels of public health institutions in the United States vary significantly between different communities, and this difference has increased over time [32]. The accessibility of health care in Australia varies in time and space, and the urban-rural imbalance differs for different types of hospitals [33]. In China’s public health research, there are apparent differences in health status between rural and urban areas and the central and western regions [34]. Moreover, health inequalities related to regional wealth differences and urban-rural gaps are widening [35]. China’s medical resources tend to concentrate in economically developed regions, and people in the underdeveloped areas seek medical treatment in provinces with more distant medical resource centers [36]. For economically developed regions, there are also significant differences in the quality of medical and health services in different cities [37].

(4) In the study of factors affecting the allocation of public health resources, the impact of China’s urban-rural gap and the new medical reform policy on the allocation of public health resources has attracted much attention. First, the institutional roots of the urban-rural duality have affected public health resources. There are widespread problems in the urban and rural medical fields, such as integrating urban and rural medical insurance and the uneven distribution of resources [38]. Citizens with urban household registration have better health opportunities, while rural household registration hurts health [39]. There are also differences between disease prevention in urban and rural areas [40]. China’s public health system is decentralized and fragmented in response to public health emergencies. This structure is unsuitable for a rapid and coordinated response to public health emergencies [41]. Second, in the new medical reform, China places more emphasis on central supervision. The bureaucratic management of the UK’s NHS is an interweaving of government and sovereign power, while China aims to equalize essential public services, medical care, and environmental protection [42]. It is necessary to strengthen central environmental supervision and establish a local environmental supervision office [43]. At the same time, on the demand side in China, overuse is positively correlated with low health literacy, long duration of illness, large family size, and high economic status. On the supply side, overuse is relatively high at the county level and above hospitals and cities with a relatively low density of medical human resources [44]. The third is the impact of digital governance strategies. The role of digital technology in medical care and its rise is helpful to patients in remote areas [45]. Scholars also discussed the opportunities, challenges, and impacts of the increasing digitalization of European public health [46]. Fourth, the role of China’s unique village medical system, although hospice care has not been genuinely implemented in China’s vast rural areas. However, local hospice care based on the village medical system plays an important role [47].

(5) In the study of factors affecting public health service supply efficiency, most of them are analyzed from three dimensions. First, government supervision can effectively improve the efficiency of public service supply. Generally speaking, if the government can take the initiative to implement public health service supply evaluation, it can effectively reduce costs and encourage less money to achieve service goals [48]. Second, capital investment is the basis of public health service supply. Studies have shown that people’s health levels can be significantly improved with increased public health service expenditure [49]. In other words, the economic development level of a region directly affects the quality of public health services in the area [50]. In addition, the introduction of market competition will positively impact the quality of public services and find a balance between the public and private sectors [51]. Third, allocating medical resources is also an essential factor affecting the efficiency of public health service supply [52]. Currently, the level of public health service supply at the grassroots level needs to be more distributed in space, and the biggest reason is the uneven distribution of medical resources. This shows that the fiscal system directly affects the supply of medical resources [53]. For example, in the case of an outbreak, most of the funds are used to prevent and control the spread of the epidemic, resulting in a significant reduction in the essential public health service expenditure of local governments [54]. Subsidies for funding, public health personnel, and material expenditures can significantly improve the effectiveness of crucial public health services [55]. In the short term, digital technologies may hurt health equity for some groups, especially older, less educated, and lower economic status [56].

In summary, the above studies mainly focus on public health service performance and supply efficiency and discuss the balance between efficiency and fairness. Some things could still be improved in the current research. First, although some literature mentions the issue of fairness in the supply of public health services, most of them are focused on theoretical discussions. From a methodological perspective, few scientific inequality measurement methods (such as the Gini coefficient and the Theil index) are used to analyze the sources of differences systematically. Second, the evaluation indicators of the public health service supply level need further improvement. Third, the spatial distribution of public health service supply levels could be more balanced due to other regions’ different economic levels and resource endowments. However, the current discussion on this issue needs to be more comprehensive. In particular, there is a lack of dynamic examination of the changing patterns before and after major public health events (such as the COVID-19 epidemic).

Therefore, based on China’s panel data from 2010 to 2023, this paper uses the entropy method, Dagum Gini coefficient, and Tobit model to explore the spatiotemporal dynamics and influencing factors of public health service supply level in 31 provinces in China. This paper innovatively integrates the Dagum Gini coefficient and the Tobit model method system compared with existing studies. Through spatial decomposition and group difference analysis, the Dagum Gini coefficient achieves a structural analysis of the inequality in the public health service supply level. And then accurately identifies its influencing factors. Together, the two promote the shift of health equity research from static description to dynamic mechanism analysis.

The possible contributions of this paper are as follows: (1) This study constructs a fairness index system from the dual dimensions of resource input and service output, breaking through the traditional single-dimensional evaluation framework. This can be better used to evaluate the public health service supply level. (2) In addition to examining the differences in public health service supply levels in the four major regions, it also explores the differences in the supply levels of specific groups within the region and between different provinces. (3) It focuses on the factors that influence regional public health service supply levels and provides policy recommendations for local government public health service supply.

Methods

Entropy method

The basic principle of the entropy method is to use an objective weighting method to determine the weight of indicators based on the principle of information entropy [57]. In other words, the weight is determined by the degree of variation of the evaluation indicator value. In the performance evaluation of public non-profit hospitals, the entropy weight method is used to determine the weight of indicators [58]. The social disability risk measurement indicator system also uses the entropy weight method to calculate the weight of indicators at all levels [59].

Tobit model

The Tobit Model, also known as the Censored Regression Model, is a regression model that handles “censored data” or “truncated data.” It was proposed by economist James Tobin in 1958 and is mainly used to deal with situations where the dependent variable is truncated or restricted within a specific range [60]. Research indicators

Based on existing research and data availability [64], this paper constructs an indicator system mainly from three dimensions: resource input and service output (Table 1). The indicator’s weight is calculated using the entropy method, which evaluates the input and output of the supply level. Based on data availability and literature support, selecting indicators for public health service supply prioritizes the support of the National Bureau of Statistics, the Health Statistics Yearbook, and existing research literature. This avoids subjective bias. The density of the health workforce (such as the number of health technicians per thousand people) is a basic indicator that reflects the accessibility and fairness of health services [65]. Bed configuration, as a core resource element of the medical and health service system, is the leading standard indicator for measuring countries’ health resources and service capabilities internationally. According to the framework of the Primary Health Care Performance Initiative (PHCPI), public health expenditure is mainly used to measure the fairness and efficiency of resource allocation [66]. The reported mortality rate and bed occupancy rate of Class A and B infectious diseases are widely used in evaluating the effectiveness of government public health services [67]. The 2015 “Guiding Opinions on Promoting the Construction of a Tiered Diagnosis and Treatment System” strengthens service accessibility through quantitative indicators such as the proportion of grassroots services. The high volume of diagnosis and treatment in grassroots institutions means that more people (especially residents in rural and remote areas) can obtain basic services, which aligns with the fairness principle of “everyone has the right to health”. The maternal mortality rate reflects the quality of maternal and child health care and emergency treatment capacity. In addition, health education and training for different groups (such as low-income groups and rural residents) can quantitatively reflect the fairness of service supply.

Data sources

This paper takes 31 provinces in China (excluding Taiwan Province, Hong Kong, and Macao Special Administrative Regions) as the research object. In 2009, China promulgated the “Opinions of the CPC Central Committee and the State Council on Deepening the Reform of the Medical and Health System,” marking the beginning of the new medical reform. Because there are significant differences between the health systems before and after the new medical reform, the period of this study is 2010–2023, which can ensure that the research is carried out in a relatively stable policy environment and avoid the interference of policy changes on the research results. Data sources: “China Statistical Yearbook,” “China Health Statistical Yearbook,” “Administrative Division Statistics of the People’s Republic of China,” and the EPS data platform. A few missing data points were filled by linear interpolation. Results and analysis

Analysis of the temporal characteristics of the public health service supply level

Macroscopic overall analysis

The overall trend in the provision of public health services across the four major regions.

The overall trend in the provision of public health services across the four major regions.

This study focuses on the development trend of the public health service supply level in China’s four major regions (Fig. 2). The public health service supply level in the four major regions has shown a significant upward trend from the overall trend. The eastern, central, and western regions are particularly prominent, showing a relatively strong growth momentum. In contrast, although the northeastern region has improved, the growth trend is relatively slow. This reflects the specific challenges faced by its public health service development. Regarding horizontal comparison between regions, the public health service supply level shows noticeable regional disparities. Specifically, the eastern and central regions lead, followed by the northeastern region, while the western region lags. This pattern’s formation is mainly due to various regions’ different resource endowments, economic foundations, and policy environments.

Development trends of public health service supply levels in the four major regions

The public health service supply level in the eastern and central regions continues to exceed the national average, and the gap between the two tends to widen gradually. This is closely related to the advantages of the eastern and central regions in resource allocation, policy implementation, and innovation capabilities. The level of public health service supply in the western region has always been lower than the national average, but the gap has been narrowing, showing a positive catch-up trend. The northeast region’s public health service supply level was comparable to that of the western region in the early stages, but it fell sharply in the later stages. This relates to multiple factors, such as economic transformation, population structure changes, and regional policy adjustments. It is worth noting that after 2019, the public health service supply level in the four major regions has fluctuated to varying degrees. The public health service supply level briefly declined in 2020, dropping the average value to 0.324. This abnormal phenomenon is very likely to be impacted by global public health events. The epidemic has not only tested the resilience of the public health systems of various countries but also prompted various regions to re-examine and strengthen the construction and management of public health services. Since the beginning of 2021, the national economy has recovered with the control of the epidemic, and the medical supply has been eased. The country’s attention to public health services has reached a new high. The state has increased its support for public health services by promulgating policies. The allocation of grassroots medical and health resources has been further optimized. The accessibility and balance of public health services in various regions continue to increase.

(2) Temporal trends in regional public health service provision levels before and after COVID-19.

Temporal trends in regional public health service provision levels before and after COVID-19.

This study takes December 2019 as the key time node and systematically examines the dynamic evolution characteristics of China’s public health service supply level (Fig. 2). Before the COVID-19 epidemic, China’s regional public health supply level showed a continuous fluctuation growth state, but there were significant regional disparities. Specifically, the growth rate of each region’s public health service supply level index was relatively slow, without drastic fluctuations. In the early stages, the Northeast region had an obvious advantage in the public health service supply level, and its index was higher than that of the Western region. In 2012, the index of the Northeast region led the western region by about 3.66%, which shows that the gap between the two regions was not large at that time. However, in 2013, the Northeast region was overtaken by the western region. After the COVID-19 epidemic, each region’s public health service supply level index fluctuated slightly in the early stage, which may be due to the outbreak of the COVID-19 pandemic on the public health system. The public health supply level in all regions began to recover rapidly and continued to improve. The growth rate of the regional public health supply level generally accelerated, but the differentiation intensified, and the Northeast region continued to lag.

(3) Trends in public health service supply levels within the four major regions.

Trends in public health service supply levels within the four major regions.

There are also apparent differences in the supply level within the region (Fig. 3). First, the eastern region can be divided into the northern coastal comprehensive economic zone, the eastern coastal comprehensive economic zone, and the southern coastal comprehensive economic zone. Overall, the public health service system in the eastern region has been continuously improved, which is highly consistent with the promotion of the national “Healthy China” strategy. However, there is a clear regional gradient differentiation. The eastern coastal economic zone has always maintained an absolute leading position, reflecting the advantages of the Yangtze River Delta urban agglomeration in fiscal investment and medical resource density. The northern coastal and southern coastal areas remain at a similar level. The growth rate of the southern coastal area has accelerated after 2018, which is related to the medical coordination policy of the Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area.

Level of public health service provision within regions

Second, the central region can be divided into the northern central provinces and the southern central provinces. The central region has steadily improved across the board, and the northern and southern central regions have achieved rapid growth. This confirms the continuous increase in public health investment under the central rise strategy. Third, the western region can be divided into the southwestern and northwestern regions. The supply level index in the southwestern region is generally better than that in the northwestern region. Fourth, the northeastern region can be divided into Heilongjiang, Jilin, and Liaoning. There is a significant hierarchical differentiation within the region, and the supply levels of the three provinces show a different echelon structure, which reflects the uneven development within the region.

Microscopic analysis of provincial regions

From a provincial micro perspective (Fig. 4), this study uses each province’s average public health service supply level over the past 14 years. Compared with the national average 0.289, 12 provinces exceeded the national average. The remaining 19 provinces were below the national average. This shows that improving local government’s public health service supply capacity is necessary nationally, significantly tilting policies toward provinces below the average. The balanced development and fair access to public health services can be promoted only in this way.

Provincial-level average public health service supply level

The level of public health services among provinces is uneven. In the eastern region, the data for Beijing, Shanghai, Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Shandong, Guangdong, and other regions are significantly higher than the average. These regions have developed economies, sufficient fiscal revenues, relatively concentrated medical resources, and developed transportation, which promotes the accessibility of medical care services. In the central region, the data for Henan, Hubei, Hunan, Jiangxi, and other regions are higher than the average. These regions rely on the strategy of the rise of the central region and have relatively concentrated medical resources. The public health service supply level in Anhui and Shanxi is slightly lower than the average level, which may be because these regions rely on traditional agriculture, have slow economic development, and have insufficient fiscal investment. Except for Sichuan Province, the public health service supply level is higher than the average in the western region. In contrast, the rest of the regions are lower than average. In the Northeast region, the public health service supply level index of Heilongjiang, Liaoning, and Jilin provinces is all below the average level. The traditional industrial transformation in the region is complex, and the population loss is severe, bringing a series of negative impacts to the region [68]. Overall, there are substantial differences in the level of public health service supply in China at the provincial level, and the overall trend is “stronger in the coastal areas than in the inland areas.”

This paper uses Stata17 software for data processing. Then, it analyzes the dynamic trend of the supply level of public health services in 31 provinces (Fig. 5). From 2010 to 2020, the overall trend is upward. Especially in the early stages, the rise is more prominent and is probably closely related to the “new medical reform” policy launched in 2009. The new medical reform has injected vitality and impetus into the public health service system. Subsequently, from 2016 to 2023, the supply level of public health services continued to grow steadily. The reason behind this is the release and implementation of the “Healthy China 2030” Outline, which points out the direction for the future development of public health services and provides strong policy support. However, it is worth noting that from 2019 to 2020, the supply level of public health services fluctuated. The reason is the impact of the global COVID-19 pandemic. Many funds have been invested in preventing and controlling the epidemic, reducing the country’s supply of public health services to a certain extent. After 2021, with the control of the epidemic and the support of national policies, the public health service supply level in various provinces continued to increase.

Trend of public health service supply levels across Chinese provinces

From the perspective of regional distribution, the public health service supply level in the eastern developed regions (Beijing, Guangdong, Shanghai, Jiangsu, Zhejiang, etc.) is significantly ahead of the national average. This directly reflects the positive impact of regional economic development on public health service capabilities. The provinces in the western region are relatively weak in the supply of public health services. In summary, the gradual improvement in the level of public health service supply in China directly reflects the government’s high attention to public health services and continued increase in policy support.

Spatial characteristics analysis of the public health service supply level

Spatial disparities in the provision of public health services

To intuitively show the spatial differences in the level of public health service supply in various regions, this paper uses ArcGIS to perform geographic visualization (Fig. 6). According to the general practice, the natural discontinuity method is adopted to divide the public health service supply level into three categories. The highest supply level is represented by blue, ranging from 0.32 to 0.51. The second highest is represented by dark green, ranging from 0.24 to 0.32. The lowest is defined by light green, ranging from 0.17 to 0.24.

Geographic Visualization of Public Health Service Supply Levels in China. (The map is based on the Standard Map with approval number GS (2024) 0650, with no modifications to the boundary of the base map.)

In general, the supply level in most areas of the east and central regions is higher than that in the west and north, and the public health service level in economically developed regions is higher than that in underdeveloped regions. In addition, provinces with similar supply levels are adjacent, which shows that public health services in different provinces are prone to form regional clusters. More specifically, the supply level in Anhui, Jiangxi, and Fujian provinces is lower than in other surrounding provinces. This shows that these provinces have not fully utilized their locational advantages, but they need further improvement at the economic and institutional levels. The public health service level of relevant provinces in the northern region is in the middle position due to their vast land and sparse population. Overall, it conforms to the “center-periphery” characteristics. Regions with higher levels of public health service supply are the “center,” while regions with lower supply levels are the “periphery,” which construct a complete binary spatial structure.

Spatial correlation in the provision of public health services

This study used ArcGIS to draw the spatial autocorrelation LISA map and analyzed the generated LISA cluster map (Fig. 7). The LISA cluster map uses four colors to distinguish different spatial autocorrelation patterns. Analysis of spatial differences in the public health service supply level

This paper uses the Gini coefficient to analyze the overall, intra-regional, and inter-regional disparities to explore the degree of difference in the level of public health service supply (Fig. 8).

Decomposition of the Gini Coefficient for public health service supply levels

From the overall Gini coefficient perspective, it dropped from 0.207 in 2010 to 0.148 in 2023. The decline is about 28.5%, which shows that the regional disparities in the level of public health service supply are gradually narrowing. The “new medical reform” has played a specific policy role. Concurrently, the 2013 Decision of the Central Committee of the Communist Party of China on Several Major Issues Concerning Comprehensively Deepening Reform proposed the strategic objective of ‘deepening the reform of the medical and healthcare system. It calls for a coordinated advancement of reforms in medical insurance, healthcare services, public health, pharmaceutical supply, and regulatory systems.’ This strategic plan aims to systematically address issues in the healthcare sector and promote more equitable and accessible public health services. Since 2010, China’s public health service supply has been steadily improving. National and local health investment and policy guidance (such as deepening the “new medical reform”, equalizing basic public health services, and alleviating health poverty) have produced positive results. In the decomposition of the overall difference in supply level, the difference between groups is always higher than the difference within the group, indicating that the difference between groups is the primary source of the overall gap. The average annual contribution rate between groups reached 42.76%, and the overall difference in the public health service supply level was mainly manifested in the four major regions. In addition, the average annual contribution rates of intra-group differences and super-variable density were 25.65% and 31.59%, respectively, which led to the difference in supply level to a certain extent.

From the perspective of regional disparities in the public health service supply level, the Gini coefficient within the group is similar to the overall change trend. Overall, the region exhibits a relatively flat profile, indicating that the supply level differences within the area are gradually narrowing. Specifically, the Gini coefficient of the supply level in the eastern region fluctuates wildly, indicating that the differences between regional provinces are still noticeable, and resource allocation needs to be optimized. The Gini coefficient of the supply level in the central region shows overall fluctuations. The Gini coefficient of the service supply level within the western region varies greatly, with the value dropping from 0.194 in 2010 to 0.082 in 2023, a drop of nearly 58%, which indicates that the balance of supply levels within the region has improved. This is related to the strategy of Western development. The Gini coefficient of the supply level in the northeast region dropped from 0.075 in 2010 to 0.024 in 2023, reflecting the equalization of the service supply level within the northeast region. Analysis shows that the reason lies in the policy support of the “14th Five-Year Plan for the Comprehensive Revitalization of Northeast China.” From the perspective of differences between groups in the public health service supply level, the evolution trend of the Gini coefficients between the eastern, central, western, and northeastern regions is mainly fluctuating and declining. Specifically, the region with the most significant Gini coefficient is the East-West region, with an annual average value of 0.187, which will drop to 0.141 in 2023. The annual average value of the Central-West region reaches 0.158, which will drop to 0.120 in 2023, and the gap is narrowing significantly. The annual average value of the West-Northeast region reaches 0.129, which will drop to 0.089 in 2023, and the gap is also narrowing.

Analysis of the influencing factors of the public health service supply level

The selection of indicator factors affecting the level of public health service provision

In discussing the factors affecting public health service supply, factors such as government fiscal expenditure [69], income, education [70], and urbanization [71] have attracted much attention. Based on the literature review in the previous article, this paper selects available indicator data and uses the level of public health service supply as the explanatory variable. Simultaneously, the proportion of the aging population, population density, and economic development level are included in the category of explanatory variables. The government size and urbanization level are control variables (Table 3). Specifically, the proportion of the elderly population is associated with medical resources [72]. The impact of population density on the epidemic has been widely studied [73]. The level of economic development affects public health [74]. In the variable selection process, this paper follows three principles: (1) the principle of theoretical completeness. Ensure that the selected variables cover the demand and supply sides of the public health service supply levels. (2) The principle of data availability. Ensure that all indicator data is available. (3) The principle of policy sensitivity. In particular, the township variable is controlled to effectively remove the estimation bias caused by the urban-rural dual structure, thereby improving the accuracy and scientificity of the research.

Conclusions and recommendations

Conclusion and outlook

Based on the panel data of 31 provinces in China from 2010 to 2023, this paper constructs an objective evaluation index system for the supply level of public health services. Secondly, the Dagum Gini coefficient is used for empirical analysis to explore the spatiotemporal differences in the level of service supply from the provincial and regional dimensions. Finally, this study uses the Tobit model to analyze the factors that influence the level of public health service supply, from the dimensions of the proportion of the elderly population, the level of economic development, and population density. The research results show that since the new medical reform in 2009, the overall public health service supply level has significantly improved. This indirectly indicates that the latest medical reform policy has played a vital role. On the other hand, there are still significant differences in the level of public health service supply in different provinces or regions. Moreover, substantial differences exist in the eastern and western regions’ public health service supply levels. Overall, the differences in supply levels between and within regions are gradually narrowing. The supply of public health services in China’s eastern coastal areas shows a significantly high agglomeration. This indicates the existence of hotspots of coordinated development. Local low-high agglomeration reveals the existence of development depressions in high-level regions, highlighting spatial imbalance. Most regions’ spatial correlation is insignificant, and the overall distribution shows non-coordinated characteristics. The Tobit regression results show that the level of public health service supply is significantly influenced by the aging population and economic development level. This indicates that the aging population and economic development level have a significant positive impact on the supply of public health services. This study can provide valuable reference for the development of basic healthcare services in China.

This article still has some research deficiencies. (1) The study did not involve county-level or community-level data. (2) Although the data span is long, it does not cover the benchmark before the new medical reform (such as before 2009), which limits the comparison of long-term trends. Given the above research deficiencies, future research can explore the following topics: (1) Expandable data sources, such as incorporating county-level or community-level panel data. And combine multi-source information, such as multi-source data, to capture more detailed spatiotemporal changes to supplement and verify the conclusions of this study. (2) Spatial econometric analysis can be used to quantify clustering effects and their driving factors. (3) In future research, more complete indicators should be included as much as possible to construct a comprehensive evaluation indicator system of subjective and objective dimensions. For example, innovative indicators such as residents’ health literacy and accessibility to digital services can be included to evaluate fairness more comprehensively.

Recommendations

First, the formulation of public health service policies needs to adhere to the value orientation of fairness and reduce regional disparities by redistributing medical resources. Through policy adjustments, the “trickle-down effect” [80] should be exerted to avoid focusing public health resource allocation on “strong” regions, and more attention should be paid to “weak” regions. At the same time, due to differences in economic development, resource endowment, geographical location, etc., among areas, the formulation and implementation of public health policies should fully consider local realities. For example, the eastern region pays more attention to service quality and competent medical care (such as the Yangtze River Delta Medical Alliance) to create a benchmark for qualified medical care. This can promote its radiation-driven effect on surrounding areas. For example, areas with high supply levels in western and northern Guangdong have a positive spillover effect [81]. The central region focuses on linking the central region’s rise strategy to promote regional cooperation. In response to the aging of the northeastern population and the outflow of young labor, the northeast region optimizes the dynamic allocation of medical resources. The western region prioritizes protecting grassroots facilities and training medical talents in a targeted manner. The region of the west implements the “Strengthening the Grassroots” project, prioritizing the construction of grassroots facilities. Targeted training of local medical talents and improvement of infrastructure and personnel allocation in the western region.

Secondly, cross-regional coordination and resource allocation should be strengthened, focusing on overcoming regional gaps. Implement more regionally differentiated public health resource allocation standards and assessment guidelines to avoid “one size fits all”. For lagging regions, policies should allow for greater flexibility and key support. Optimize regional cooperation mechanisms and deepen the mechanism for developed eastern provinces to provide counterpart support to central, western, and northeastern provinces. Provide long-term and stable assistance in soft power such as talent, technology, management, and data. Improve the linkage mechanism between aging response and economic development. Actively explore and promote the “medical care and nursing care” pilot, use the fiscal advantages of economically developed regions to explore socialized service purchases, and alleviate the pressure of single government investment. In underdeveloped areas, the “health industry” driving model should be introduced to promote the development of medical resources. Focus on the elderly, children, pregnant women, and low-income groups.

Finally, a regional coordination and linkage mechanism should be established to play a role in the “diffusion effect” between regions [82]. Through policy guidance and resource tilt, high-quality medical resources will be balanced. A regional cooperation platform should be built to strengthen regional collaboration and expand the capacity of high-quality medical resources. Deepen regional resource integration and capacity building to consolidate the foundation of fairness. Provincial governments should strengthen overall coordination and break down resource barriers between cities and counties within the province. Guide resources to tilt towards underdeveloped areas within the province. In the meantime, the size of the government should be optimized, and a clear hierarchy should be established. The decision-making and execution processes should be relatively efficient. Human, material, and financial resources should be effectively deployed to balance public health services and sustainable development. Moreover, market and social forces should be nurtured and utilized. This will diversify the supply of essential public health services. In addition, the “Internet + Medical Health” model should be used in today’s digital information age. This can improve the accuracy and efficiency of resource allocation. Thus, a digital public health service platform should cover urban and rural areas and regions. Promote the universal application of technologies such as remote diagnosis, treatment, and intelligent monitoring, narrowing the digital health gap between regions and urban and rural areas.

Defining spatiotemporal gene modules in liver regeneration using Analytical Dynamic Visual Spatial Omics Representation (ADViSOR)
Authors: Anya Singh-Varma, Ashti M Shah, Silvia Liu, Ruben Zamora, Satdarshan P Monga, Yoram Vodovotz
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground:The liver is the only organ with the ability to regenerate following surgical or toxicant insults, and partial hepatectomy serves as an experimental model of liver regeneration (LR). Dynamic changes in gene expression occur from the periportal to pericentral regions of the liver following partial hepatectomy; thus, spatial transcriptomics, combined with a novel computational pipeline (ADViSOR [Analytic Dynamic Visual Spatial Omics Representation]), was employed to gain insights into the spatiotemporal molecular underpinnings of LR.Methods:ADViSOR, comprising Time-Interval Principal Component Analysis and sliding dynamic hypergraphs, was applied to spatial transcriptomics data on 100 genes assayed serially through LR, including key components of the Wnt/β-catenin pathway at critical timepoints after partial hepatectomy.Results:This computational pipeline identified key functional modules demonstrating cell signaling and cell-cell interactions, inferring shared regulatory mechanisms. Specifically, ADViSOR analysis suggested that macrophage-mediated inflammation is a critical component of early LR and confirmed prior studies showing that Ccnd1, a hepatocyte proliferative gene, is regulated by the Wnt/β-catenin pathway. These findings were subsequently validated through protein localization, which provided further confirmation and novel insights into the spatiotemporal changes in the Wnt/β-catenin pathway during LR.Conclusions:Thus, ADViSOR may yield novel insights in other complex, spatiotemporal contexts.

INTRODUCTION

The liver performs over 500 eclectic functions in part by division of labor through a phenomenon termed “liver zonation.”1 The liver comprises several lobules of hexagonal shape with portal triads at each of the vertices and a centrally located central vein.2 Blood flows through hepatic sinusoids, lined by sinusoidal endothelial cells (ECs) and KCs from periportal (zone 1), where hepatocytes are exposed to fresh arterial blood with a high oxygen content and portal blood with a high nutrient content; through midzone (zone 2); and then to pericentral (zone 3), where oxygen and nutrient concentrations are decreased.3 Each zone elicits different metabolic and functional activities of hepatocytes.4,5 When regeneration is necessary, as occurs following surgical resection or exposure to toxicants, a healthy liver is equipped to balance proliferation and metabolism without disrupting function.6–10 The cellular and molecular underpinnings of the balance of proliferation and maintenance of hepatic function during repair remain unclear.

Hepatocytes require paracrine signaling to carry out their diverse functions. Several studies have delineated the kinetics of key required cellular signals for optimal regeneration.11 Pericentral zonation is largely maintained by the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway, whereby Wnt secretion dictates the transcriptional activity of β-catenin.12 Wnts 2 and 9b from pericentral venous and pericentral sinusoidal ECs are secreted and bind to Frizzled receptors on cell membranes, inducing β-catenin nuclear translocation.13 From the nucleus, β-catenin coactivates transcription of various target genes: GS encoded by Glul, Cyp2e1, Cyp1a2, Axin2, Cyclin-D1 (Ccnd1), and regucalcin (Rgn).12 At baseline, Ccnd1 is expressed by midzonal hepatocytes. Following acute injury or partial hepatectomy (PH), where two-thirds of the liver is removed, Ccnd1 levels increase in periportal and midzonal hepatocytes toward pericentral hepatocytes from 24 to 72 hours.6,13–16 Several other pathways, including those related to immune and metabolic function, must work in tandem to signal hepatocytes to regenerate optimally.17 How these pathways work in concert and interact with each other remains an enigma.

Spatial transcriptomics has allowed biologists to query a piece of tissue for several genes and assess where this gene expression is occurring within a tissue section at high resolution.18,19 However, traditional spatial transcriptomics data analysis involves the evaluation of static levels of gene expression in a given area. Given the complex dynamics of liver regeneration (LR) as well as many other biological processes, there is a need to develop computational approaches aimed at defining the inferred dynamic changes in gene expression over time and space. Here, we describe and validate a novel workflow and computational pipeline, Analytical Dynamic Visual Spatial Omics Representation (ADViSOR), that addresses these needs, and describe insights derived therefrom.

METHODS

Animals

All animal husbandry and experimental procedures, including housing and diet, were performed under the guidelines and approval of the National Institutes of Health and Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee at the University of Pittsburgh. Mice were fed regular chow in standard caging and kept under a 12-hour light-dark cycle with no enrichment.

Partial hepatectomy

Two-month-old C57BL/6 mice, purchased from Jackson Laboratory, were subjected to PH in which two-third of the liver were removed, inducing LR as described.14 Mice were euthanized by isoflurane inhalation and cervical dislocation at the following timepoints: 0 hours, 24 hours, 48 hours, 72 hours, 14 days, 3 months, and 6 months. Regenerating livers were harvested and the newly regenerated lobes were either paraffin-embedded or collected for Molecular Cartography according to published methods.13

Molecular Cartography and data set construction

The specifics of tissue preprocessing, tissue sectioning, tissue processing, probe construction, spot-segmentation, and imaging have been detailed and published.13 The gene probes used in this study were preselected and are listed in Table 1. Molecular Cartography Technology by Resolve BioSciences was used to capture images of the fluorescent gene probes on the tissue samples provided (average image resolution = 6,752,587 pixels/mm2). Image analysis was performed in ImageJ using genexyz Polylux, a Resolve BioSciences tool to identify gene expression at the cellular level. The output of this data collection was a matrix consisting of a binary indication of whether each gene was present or not in each pixel of the liver section image. Raw and processed spatial transcriptomics data across multiple time points using molecular cartograpyTM is publicly available with GEO access ID: GSE244211.

In-house preprocessing of the spatial transcriptomics data was performed to calculate the density of gene expression in the canonical liver zones: periportal, mid-, and pericentral zones. A theoretical line was drawn between the center of the pericentral vein and the center of the periportal vein. The pericentral and periportal veins were identified by the expression of Cyp2e1 and GS and Sox9, respectively. Five hundred pixels above and below this theoretical line, 2 additional parallel lines were drawn. The theoretical central line was divided into 9 equal segments by a perpendicular line of 1000 pixels such that segments 1–3 (S1–S3) captured the pericentral zone, segments 4–6 (S4–S6) captured the midzone, and segments 7–9 (S7–S9) captured the periportal zone.13 The density of each gene’s expression in a liver segment was calculated according to density = gene countarea of segment× 1000 .

For each timepoint, multiple periportal-pericentral regions could be identified. The density of gene expression in each segment across all periportal-pericentral lines was averaged so that for each of the 7 timepoints a matrix of the density of each gene’s expression across all 9 liver segments for each of the 100 genes was constructed (Table 2). This 100×9×7 matrix served as input into Time-Interval Principal Component Analysis (TI-PCA) and sliding dynamic hypergraph (sDyHyp) analytical pipelines.

Pictorially, Molecular Cartography indicates the binary presence of a gene based on mRNA expression as a “hit” or colored pixel. To visualize genes that were coexpressed in the same pixel, an overlay was created, whereby the transparency of one image was adjusted to allow for the underlying image to be seen. As such, it was possible to view the coexpression of multiple genes at once, and the direct colocalization of 2 or more genes was visually illustrated as a pixel whose RGB color value was the average of the color value associated with each gene.

Quantifying colocalization of genes from spatial transcriptomics data set

Quantification of the degree of colocalization of all gene pairs based on raw spatial transcriptomic images was performed to provide baseline knowledge of the extent to which gene expression was colocalized across liver segments over time. For theoretical genes A and B, the Euclidean distance from one “hit” of gene A, indicating the presence of gene A in a pixel, to all hits of gene B within the same segment was calculated. The minimum distance, calculated in pixels, between that instance of gene A and all instances of gene B within a segment was noted. This process was repeated for all hits of gene A within a segment. The average minimum distance between each hit of gene A and all hits of gene B was calculated. Within the same segment, the average minimum distance between each hit of gene B and all other hits of gene A was also calculated. The average minimum distance between genes A and B and genes B and A was used to describe the extent of colocalization between genes A and B within a segment. The smaller the distance between genes A and B, the higher the degree of colocalization.

The degree of colocalization for each pair of genes was calculated across all segments, periportal-pericentral lines, and timepoints (Table 2). For each timepoint and liver segment, if fewer than 50% of that segment across all periportal-pericentral lines showed no colocalization of genes A and B, then the average distance between genes A and B was the average distance between all samples of the segment in which genes A and B were colocalized. If more than 50% of the segments across all samples had no colocalization between genes A and B, then the true average distance between genes A and B is infinite.

To analyze the degree of colocalization between genes of interest, the following colocalization metric was calculated: 1avg distance between genes A and B×100 , such that the greater the degree of colocalization, the greater the colocalization metric. This metric was calculated for all liver segments for all timepoints and all pairs of genes assessed through spatial transcriptomics analysis.

Time-Interval Principal Component Analysis

TI-PCA allows for the identification of principal components that capture variance in data over distinct time intervals and was calculated essentially as described.20,21 Liver segments S1–S3 were assumed to be homogenous and served as 3 independent samples of data for analysis of the canonical periportal zone. Likewise, S4–S6 served as input for analysis of the midzone and S7–S9 served as input for analysis of the pericentral zone. Time intervals were set as overlapping windows of consecutive timepoints such that analysis was carried out for the intervals 0–24 hours and 24–48 hours.

Principal components that captured at least 99% of the variance in gene expression over a dynamic time interval were selected for further analysis. Across all segments, 4–5 principal components were sufficient to describe the data set. The product of the coefficient, or weight, of each gene and the eigenvalue for each principal component was calculated and is representative of the amount of variance in gene expression that the principal component captures. The sum of this product across all 4 or 5 principal components indicates the relative amount of variance in gene expression across a dynamic time interval relative to the variance of all other genes studied.20,21

A stacked bar graph organized from the greatest sum of variances across all genes to the least sum of variances across all genes was created. Genes with the greatest sum of variance are those that vary the most across the time interval. Genes that contributed to the upper quartile of variance across all genes were identified and noted explicitly.

Sliding dynamic hypergraphs

Traditional hypergraphs are a special form of graph theory in which an edge can connect more than two nodes. In the hypergraph formalism, edges do not have to be linear and defined by 2 points (nodes).22 Instead, the free-form shape of an edge allows it to encompass multiple nodes. As such, complex networks encompassing multiple nodes can be visualized.22,23

A dynamic hypergraph uses Pearson correlation to identify significant trends in gene expression in each segment over three consecutive timepoints. Graph edges are genes that are significantly increasing/decreasing in a specific liver segment over a specified time interval. The segments (nodes) in which the gene is significantly increasing/decreasing become encompassed by an edge labeled with the name of that gene. Multiple nodes can be encompassed by the same gene so long as that gene is independently increasing/decreasing significantly with time in the subset of nodes encompassed by the edge.

“Sliding dynamic hypergraphs” refers to the graphical layout that accompanies the DyHyp algorithm. Graphically, segments are drawn out from S1 to S9, and edges are drawn around the set of neighboring nodes in which a specific gene is significantly increasing/decreasing. A sliding window approach creates separate graphs for intervals at 0, 24, 48 hours, and 24, 48, 72 hours. Each independent graph is drawn next to each other such that the nodes are aligned and the movement of an edge between segments with time can be easily visualized. This entire graphical package is termed “sDyHyp.”

The 100 probed genes were first categorized as either related to metabolism, proliferation, or inflammation. For each gene, the Pearson correlation between the density of gene expression in each segment across three consecutive timepoints was calculated. If a gene was significantly positively/negatively correlated with time in a segment, an edge around the given segment was drawn and labeled with the name of the gene indicating that the gene was up/downregulated in that segment. If 2 or more genes were independently up/downregulated in the same group of segments, then an edge drawn around that group of segments was labeled with the names of all genes that were significantly changing independently over that time interval in the specific region. To identify functional modules, we identified genes that tended to move together across the same or similar segments with time.

RESULTS

Time-dependent variability in gene expression across liver zones

PH resulted in full recovery of liver weight to body weight ratio by 14 days after surgery, indicating that timepoints sampled prior to 14 days captured LR in progress.7 Genes relevant to liver function (metabolism, synthesis), proliferation, and inflammation, many of which exhibited distinct zonal expression and some which belonged to the Wnt/β-catenin pathway, were selected for spatial transcriptomic analysis of regenerated liver lobes at various timepoints. TI-PCA was used to define the zone(s) and time intervals in which major biological changes were occurring in response to PH. Across all 3 liver zones, the sum of all principal components for each gene decreased with time (Figure 1), suggesting that the overall variance in gene expression decreased during liver recovery.

The greatest variance in gene expression was observed at d0–d1 and d1–d2 across all liver zones, before rapidly decreasing during the interval d2–d3 (Figure 1). By d2–d3, the genes contributing to the upper quartile of variance in each zone were markedly different, suggesting the emergence of liver zonation (Figure 1). The dynamic fluctuation in gene expression across liver zones can be appreciated in Figure 2. After 72 hours, the number of genes in the upper quartile contributing to variance rose across all liver zones (Figure 2A). By 6 months after PH, the pericentral zone was the only locus with the same number of genes contributing to variance in the upper quartile as during the d0–d1 interval (Figure 2A).

The spatiotemporal expression of functional proliferative, metabolic, and inflammatory gene modules was next visualized by sDyHyp, quantifying the number of genes significantly expressed in each module over both time and space and thus adding temporal and functional detail to TI-PCA. This global analysis of sDyHyp data suggested that until the interval between 48 and 14 days, variance in gene expression was driven predominantly by the upregulation of genes in all 3 functional modules, after which variance in post-PH gene expression was driven predominantly by the downregulation of genes (Figure 2B). After verifying that the genes selected for Molecular Cartography analysis exhibited spatiotemporally sensitive expression, genes specific to the Wnt/β-catenin pathway were studied closely to understand the role of this pathway in regeneration.

ADViSOR confirms spatial and temporal kinetics of known pericentral metabolic genes

The pericentral hepatocytes were identified by expression of GS, and broader pericentral and nearby midzonal hepatocytes were identified by Cyp2e1-expressing cells. The concentrated expression of Sox9, a marker of cholangiocytes lining biliary ducts, helped identify the portal triad and landmarked periportal hepatocytes in the immediate vicinity (Figure 3A). The overlay of these genes helped identify canonical liver zones in a single image. Quantitative analysis of Cyp2e1 and GS expression showed the greatest degree of colocalization in S9, the most pericentral segment of the liver in which GS is expressed canonically (Figure 3B). This degree of colocalization persisted, as expected, in S9 throughout the time course of LR.

Immunohistochemistry analysis was next carried out to confirm that patterns in mRNA expression, as indicated by Molecular Cartography, and the novel colocalization metric applied to the Molecular Cartography data set, could be visualized at the protein level. This analysis showed that the GS protein was concentrated in the hepatocytes immediately surrounding the central vein across each timepoint, from 0 hour to 6 months (Figure 3C). Cyp2e1 showed a broader pericentral distribution from 0 hour to 6 months, with some extension into midzonal hepatocytes (Figure 3D). Both findings are in line with prior studies in which knockout models of the Wnt/β-catenin pathway have confirmed the predominant pericentral expression of GS and Cyp2e1.12 Given the confidence in the Molecular Cartography data set, genes specific to the Wnt/β-catenin pathway and hypothesized to be coregulated with GS and Cyp2e1 were analyzed by means of the full ADViSOR package.

Dynamic spatiotemporal expression of Ccnd1 coincides with that of the Wnt/β-catenin pathway

We next posited that genes that were upregulated at the same times in the same liver segments as GS and/or Cyp2e1 must be regulated, at least in part, by the Wnt/β-catenin pathway. sDyHyp, in conjunction with the aforementioned ADViSOR components, allowed for the identification of genes that were inferred to be coregulated. The ADViSOR package was leveraged to better understand the regulatory mechanisms of Ccnd1, a gene involved in hepatocyte proliferation. It is currently unclear whether Ccnd1 is regulated by the Wnt/β-catenin or Igfbp2-mTOR axis.15,24 Molecular Cartography overlay of GS, Sox9, Cyp2e1, and Ccnd1 showed an increase in density of Ccnd1 expression from 0 to 72 hours (Figure 4A). From 14 day to 6 months, the density of Ccnd1 expression appeared to markedly decrease (Figure 4A). The colocalization metric showed that Ccnd1 and GS were highly colocalized at nearly all timepoints across all liver zones, as indicated by the large scale of the x-axis on this graph (Figure 4B), which suggests that the greater the sum of colocalization, the smaller the distance between the 2 genes. The difference in x-axis scale was noticeable when compared to the graph in Figure 3B. The degree of colocalization of Ccnd1 and GS decreased markedly at 14 days (Figure 4B).

The well-established kinetics of Ccnd1 expression following PH were confirmed by sDyHyp analysis (Figure 4C, Supplemental Figure S1, http://links.lww.com/HC9/A579). Normally a midzonal hepatocyte marker,13,14 Ccnd1 increased significantly in periportal and pericentral hepatocytes at early (0–72 h) timepoints following PH (Figure 4C). Following this upregulation, Ccnd1 levels decreased significantly from 14 days onward across pericentral and periportal segments, suggesting an end to the proliferation phase following PH. Interestingly, the temporal patterns of Ccne1, a marker of hepatocyte proliferation,25 and Ccnd1 (Supplemental Figure S2, http://links.lww.com/HC9/A580) highlighted that the 2 proproliferative genes are downregulated preceding the upregulation of Cyp2e1.

The fluctuation in Ccnd1 expression coincided with that of GS. The high degree of colocalization of Ccnd1 and GS was inferred to be secondary to the upregulation of GS and Ccnd1 in both the pericentral and periportal regions over the first 72 hours following PH (Figure 4C). The marked decrease in colocalization at 14 days (Figure 4B) may be explained by the downregulation of Ccnd1 over the interval 48 hours to 14 days. The subsequent downregulation of both Ccnd1 and GS over the intervals 72 hours to 6 months (Figure 4C), along with the simultaneous increase in their colocalization (Figure 4B), suggested that Ccnd1 and GS were expressed in fewer hepatocytes that were in close proximity.

Dynamic expression of Ccnd1 precedes changes in Igfbp2 expression

The hypothesis that Ccnd1 is regulated by the Igfbp2-mTOR axis suggested that, following PH, the spatiotemporal expression of Igfbp2 would either precede or coincide with the expression of Ccnd1. Through visual analysis, Molecular Cartography showed increased colocalization of Ccnd1 and Igfbp2 until 14 days after PH, after which expression of both genes was still present; however, there was a drastic decrease in their colocalization (Figure 5A). Igfbp2 and Ccnd1, both of which are expressed in midzonal hepatocytes, were colocalized to a low degree in all liver segments except for S8 and S9 (Figure 5B). Additionally, the degree of colocalization between Igfbp2 and GS, a pericentral marker, was similar to the degree of colocalization of Igfbp2 and Ccnd1 (Figure 5C).

sDyHyp analysis suggested that dynamic changes in the expression of Ccnd1 occurred before changes in the expression of Igfbp2. Expression of Ccnd1 increased in S6–S9 over the interval 0–48 hours, but did not persist in the 24–72 hours interval (Figure 5D). During 24–72 hours, Igfbp2 was upregulated in nearly the same segments as Ccnd1, namely S5–S9 (Figure 5D). The downregulation of Ccnd1 in S7–S9 during the interval 48h–14d preceded the downregulation of Igfbp2 in S6 during the interval 72 hours to 3 months (Figure 5D). Within each segment, the colocalization of Ccnd1 and Igfbp2 decreased with time (Figure 5B). However, the expression of Ccnd1 and Igfbp2 increased across nearly all liver segments in the first 72 hours following PH (Figure 5D). These 2 findings suggested that within a liver segment, the expression of Ccnd1 and Igfbp2 was increasing in cells located far apart. Overall, Ccnd1 expression appeared to precede changes in Igfbp2 during LR.

ADViSOR highlights novel colocalization of immune and endothelial cell markers following PH

The ability of ADViSOR to model the spatiotemporal regulation of genes belonging to validated pathways led us to investigate additional, less understood mechanisms involved in LR following PH. Recent interest in the immune component of LR prompted assessment of immune markers such as Adgre1 (F4/80), Lyz2, and Ptprc. Additionally, Pecam1, an endothelial cell marker, and C6, expressed by hepatocytes and upregulated during the innate immune response,26 were queried to assess the margination of immune cells into hepatic sinusoids and the activation of the immune response during LR.

Ptprc and Pecam1 were colocalized uniformly throughout all segments, whereas the colocalization of Adgre1 and Pecam1 was most prominent in pericentral and periportal segments (Figure 6A, B). sDyHYP showed increased Pecam1 and Ptprc expression in nearly all zones over the interval 48 hours to 14 days (Figure 6E). The upregulation of Adgre1, a marker for KCs of the liver, occurred in only periportal and pericentral liver segments (Figure 6E, Supplemental Figure S3, http://links.lww.com/HC9/A581). Lyz2, another macrophage marker, and Adgre1 showed approximately 10x higher colocalization than nonmacrophage gene, C6, with Lyz2 (Figure 6C, D). The lack of concomitant change in macrophage markers, Lyz2 and Adgre1, with C6 highlights the ability of ADViSOR to specifically identify cellular discordance despite all genes having a similar roll in innate immunity. In fact, sDyHyp suggested an inverse relationship between Lyz2 and C6 (Figure 6F, Supplemental Figure S3, http://links.lww.com/HC9/A581). This inverse relationship was most apparent during 48 hours to 14 days after PH in S4–S9 (Figure 6F, Supplemental Figure S3, http://links.lww.com/HC9/A581).

DISCUSSION

Spatial transcriptomics, a method to localize mRNA transcripts on a tissue slide, has provided an opportunity to understand the dynamics of gene regulation in hepatocytes, immune cells, and ECs following PH at high resolution.19,27 Due to various constraints, spatial transcriptomics data sets are often too small to be analyzed by standard machine learning algorithms, and current methods of analysis fail to capture the dynamics of gene expression adequately.18 Furthermore, the current state of spatial transcriptomics analysis is characterized by gene clustering at a static timepoint and does not capture the dynamic expression of gene transcription across time.28,29 As such, the need to develop a computational pipeline to analyze spatial transcriptomics data and thereby advance hypothesis-driven experimentation prompted the development of the ADViSOR pipeline.

Insights into dynamic gene expression following PH were first provided by TI-PCA. Following PH, there was a decrease in the number of mediators contributing to variance in gene expression, and this decrease was associated with the strong upregulation of a set of genes identified by sDyHyp. Over time, the sum of the principal components for each gene decreased, suggesting that the variance in gene expression decreases with recovery following PH.

The dynamics of gene transcripts encoding for GS, Cyp2e1, and Ccnd1, whose patterns of spatiotemporal expression are well established,13,30 were modeled using the colocalization metric and sDyHyp (Figure 7A). These 2 novel methods reproduced known results and added visual and quantitative depth to the Molecular Cartography data set, supporting the value of the ADViSOR package as a tool to better understand spatiotemporal transcriptomic relationships.

Analytical Dynamic Visual Spatial Omics Representation (ADViSOR) for spatiotemporal genomics modeling and data-driven hypothesis generation. (A) Ccnd1 and GS were colocalized during the first 72 hours after which Cyp2e1 and GS increased in colocalization. This change from the colocalization of Ccnd1 with GS to the colocalization of Cyp2e1 with GS suggested that the Wnt/β-catenin pathway regulates proliferation and metabolism in a temporally sensitive manner. (B) The upregulation of Igfbp2 and Ccnd1 during the first 72 hours must be occurring in cells located far apart within a segment, since the colocalization coefficient decreased over this interval. (C) Multigene sliding dynamic hypergraphs showed that Ccnd1 expression had a similar spatiotemporal expression as downstream effectors of the Wnt/β-catenin system. Additionally, the Wnt/β-catenin system appeared to switch from controlling an upregulation of proliferative genes to an upregulation of metabolic genes around 72 hours. (D) ADViSOR offers the opportunity for data-driven hypothesis generation. For example, further investigation regarding the specifics of the macrophage and immune cell responses during liver regeneration following partial hepatectomy is warranted based on ADViSOR-derived insights. Abbreviations: PC, pericentral; PP, periportal.

ADViSOR also suggested several nonintuitive findings. Cyclin-D1, encoded by Ccnd1, is hypothesized to be regulated by the Wnt/β-catenin pathway or the Igfbp2-mTOR pathway during LR. Based on ADViSOR analysis, the upregulation of Ccnd1 correlated with the upregulation of GS, a liver-specific surrogate target of the Wnt/β-catenin pathway. While Ccnd1 and Igfbp2 colocalized, the colocalization was restricted to S9. In comparison to the colocalization of Ccnd1 and GS, the colocalization between Ccnd1 and Igfbp2 was 2- to 3-fold lower over the first 72 hours of LR. If Igfbp2 were to regulate the expression of Ccnd1, then Igfbp2’s upregulation would intuitively precede that of Ccnd1. However, both the upregulation and downregulation of Ccnd1 preceded that of Igfbp2 (Figure 7B). Analysis of Ccnd1, GS, Cyp2e1, and Igfbp2 through the ADViSOR pipeline provided rigorous support for the regulation of Ccnd1 by the Wnt/β-catenin pathway (Figure 7C). It is known that peak hepatocyte regeneration occurs from 40 to 48 hours following PH and then tapers by 72 hours.30 sDyHyp revealed the novel finding that hepatocytes undergo a switch from β-catenin–regulated proliferation to β-catenin–regulated metabolism around 72 hours as indicated by the change in upregulation of Ccnd1 and GS to Cyp2e1 and GS. Through single-cell analysis, studies have reported a division of labor during regeneration such that some hepatocytes perform proliferative functions while others perform metabolic functions.9 Future studies will be needed to address the timeline of the switch from proliferative to metabolic gene expression following PH.

The ADViSOR pipeline also allowed for the discovery of gene fluctuations across time in addition to space that would have otherwise been overlooked. Pecam1, a classic endothelial cell marker, exhibited the same spatiotemporal pattern of expression as Ptprc. The ADViSOR pipeline allowed for the observation that upregulation of Adgre1, a resident macrophage marker, occurred only near the central vein and portal triad during the interval 24–72 hours. Furthermore, the influx of macrophages, indicated by the upregulation of Ptprc and Lyz2, following PH occurred over the same interval 48 hours to 14 days. This influx of macrophages into hepatic sinusoids coincided with the time that the Wnt/β-catenin pathway switched from upregulating the proliferative gene, Ccnd1, to upregulating the metabolic gene, Cyp2e1. With the recent interest in the immune landscape of LR,31–33 ADViSOR can be used to discover insights regarding the dynamic expression of inflammatory modules in LR (Figure 7D).

Our study has several important limitations. The application of the ADViSOR pipeline beyond hypothesis generation is cautioned. Molecular Cartography involved the quantification of selected mRNA transcripts rather than protein and, as such, it is possible that not all significant changes in mRNA correspond to that of proteins. Our main interest was to model LR over time following hepatectomy. While our study had limited set of preselected genes since Molecular Cartography is not completely an unbiased single-cell spatial transcriptomic modality, our selected set of genes represent protein products involved in metabolism (glucose, lipid, and xenobiotic), detoxification, synthesis as well as proliferation functions of the liver with a more focus on the role of the Wnt/β-catenin pathway. Thus, the interpretations should be made within the scope of the data set. Future studies using unbiased gene data sets may also lead to identification of gene regulatory networks. Further, rigorous evaluation of hypotheses generated by the ADViSOR pipeline may involve the creation of gene knockout and/or knockin models to develop an understanding of the mechanism by which genes are coregulated.

Here, we used ADViSOR to quantify dynamic changes in gene expression between the periportal and pericentral regions over time in response to PH. Our analysis suggests the spatiotemporally sensitive regulation of several genes under control of the Wnt/β-catenin pathway following PH. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first pipeline to assess dynamic changes in gene expression using a spatial transcriptomics data set; the power of this tool lies in its strong statistical roots, easy-to-understand visuals, and validation of insights based on IHC analysis. Future applications of ADViSOR include the assessment of changes in gene expression across the liver as well as other tissues in response to disease or injury.

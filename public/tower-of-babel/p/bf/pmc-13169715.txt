Building destination brands through trust: unpacking the role of digital integrated marketing communication in driving travel intentions
Authors: Meng Qi, Jing Li, Zulhamri Abdullah, Saiful Nujaimi Abdul Rahman, Li Niu, Baiyu Zhou, Zhen Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn the increasingly competitive digital communication landscape, destination brands face growing pressure to cultivate trust and brand equity through credible, value-driven messaging. However, the psychological mechanisms through which digital integrated marketing communication (IMC) strategies influence consumer trust, brand perception, and behavioral intentions remain underexplored. Grounded in commitment–trust theory, this study empirically examines how a digital IMC strategy enhances information trust and destination brand equity, ultimately fostering consumers’ intention to visit. Analysis of survey data from 356 digitally engaged travelers reveals that: (1) digital IMC significantly strengthens both information trust and perceived brand equity; (2) trust acts as a critical psychological mediator, reinforcing the effect of communication efforts on brand equity; and (3) the trust–equity pathway serves as a strong predictor of travel intention. This research contributes to tourism and digital communication scholarship by clarifying trust as a mediating mechanism linking digital integrated marketing communication to destination brand equity and intention and by operationalizing information trust as a context-specific trust target in the pre-trip evaluation stage. The findings also inform destination marketing practice by showing how consistency and interactivity relate to information trust and destination brand equity, which in turn relate to intention.Keywords: Integrated Marketing Communication (IMC), Digital trust, Travel intention, Brand equity

Introduction

The tourism industry, a significant driver of global economic growth [1], has become increasingly reliant on digital channels to engage potential travelers and shape destination perceptions. In an increasingly saturated marketplace, destination branding has emerged as a critical strategy for differentiation [2], focusing on the overall experience and image conveyed to consumers through various communication channels [3]. The evolution of the digital landscape has positioned digital integrated marketing communication (IMC) as a pivotal tool for managing these brand interactions, enabling destinations to convey consistent and engaging messages across multiple platforms [4]. By deploying interactive, personalized, and real-time engagement strategies tailored to digital environments [5], destinations can foster deeper customer relationships, with an emphasis on targeted, measurable communications that support customer acquisition and retention [6].

However, in this digital era, building and maintaining trust has become increasingly complex for destination marketers [7]. As potential tourists increasingly rely on online reviews, social media interactions, and other digital content [8], establishing trust requires new approaches. Trust now plays a critical role in shaping consumer decision-making in digitally mediated environments [9]. Smart technology characteristics, such as accessibility and personalization, can enhance tourist satisfaction and foster repeat visit intentions by cultivating trust [10]. Despite broad recognition of the importance of digital IMC, a fundamental theoretical ambiguity remains unresolved: within digital IMC frameworks, is trust best understood as a downstream outcome of communication activities, a constitutive dimension of brand equity, or the central psychological mechanism through which strategic messaging is translated into behavioral change? Addressing this ambiguity, the present study conceptualizes trust as the core mediating mechanism and draws on Commitment–Trust Theory (CTT) to explicate the psychological process through which digital IMC influences travel intention.

While the positive influence of digital IMC on brand outcomes such as brand equity [11], loyalty [12], and engagement [13] is well-documented, the precise theoretical role of trust within this relationship remains conceptually ambiguous and undertheorized [14]. On one hand, a significant stream of literature effectively establishes trust as a critical outcome of effective communication and a key antecedent to loyalty or purchase intention in online contexts [15, 16], which is directly cultivable through strategic messaging and interaction in digital spaces [17]. On the other hand, trust is often conflated with or treated as a dimension of relational brand equity itself, representing the strength of the consumer-brand bond [18]. This conflation creates a critical theoretical blind spot: we lack a clear, parsimonious model that specifies trust as the central psychological mechanism, the generative process, through which the strategic orchestration of digital IMC first builds a reliable informational relationship, which in turn enhances the broader brand asset and ultimately drives behavioral intention. Prior studies have successfully linked variables but have seldom revealed how and why core digital IMC dimensions (e.g., consistency, interactivity) are uniquely capable of fostering the specific type of trust required for high-involvement decisions like travel. This gap is particularly salient in tourism, where pre-trip reliance on digital information is paramount, yet the pathway from strategic message integration to trusted information partnership remains inadequately mapped. Consequently, there is an urgent need to theorize and empirically test a framework that explicitly positions trust not merely as an adjacent variable, but as the indispensable mediating mechanism that translates digital IMC efforts into sustainable destination brand equity and concrete travel intentions.

Destination choice involves high perceived risk and extensive reliance on digital information during pre-trip evaluation [19], which makes trust formation a central theoretical issue rather than a peripheral variable. Prior research still reflects a debate over trust’s role, where trust is alternately treated as an outcome of communication, a dimension of brand equity, or a mediating mechanism translating communication into behavioral intention. Such ambiguity limits explanatory clarity and weakens guidance on which communication levers build trust and how trust is converted into destination brand equity. Guided by CTT and extant typologies in theory development [20], this paper offers theoretical novelty through theoretical modification and theoretical extension. Theoretical modification occurs through specifying two distinct communication antecedents, consistency and interactivity, and theorizing dimension-specific pathways to information trust and destination brand equity. Theoretical extension occurs through conceptualizing information trust as the proximal trust target in the digitally mediated pre-trip exchange, clarifying how trust toward information precedes broader destination evaluations. Theoretical interestingness follows through noteworthiness, where digital IMC is positioned as a relationship-building mechanism that first establishes a credible information partnership before any persuasive influence on intention can emerge. Empirically, the paper tests a sequential pathway from digital IMC to information trust to destination brand equity to intention, offering a more precise account of how strategic digital communication translates into relational brand value. Practically, the findings equip destination marketers with evidence-based strategies to design digital IMC campaigns that leverage consistent messaging and interactive engagement to build informational credibility, strengthen brand value, and stimulate travel intention in a saturated digital landscape.

Literature review

Theoretical foundation: commitment-trust theory in digital destination marketing

Proposed by [21], Commitment–Trust Theory (CTT) asserts that trust and relationship commitment are pivotal forces driving relationships’ formation, progression, and outcomes. The theory provides fundamental knowledge for understanding the development and sustainability of long-term relationships between exchange partners, emphasizing the central role of these constructs in fostering enduring and mutually beneficial interactions. Trust is a multidisciplinary concept that emerges when one party believes in the dependability and honesty of their exchange partner. [21] further argued that CTT is broadly applicable to establishing and maintaining relational exchanges involving suppliers, customers, or employees. Extending this perspective, the present study applies CTT to examine the relational exchanges between tourists and destination management organizations (DMOs), highlighting how trust fosters stronger connections that influence tourists’ decision-making and behavioral intentions.

The present study advances CTT’s application by adapting it to the digitally-mediated exchange between potential tourists and destination management organizations (DMOs). This adaptation addresses the conceptual gap identified in the introduction regarding the precise role of trust in digital IMC frameworks [14]. In this research, digital IMC conceptualized through its consistency and interactivity dimensions is theorized as the operational manifestation of the “communication” antecedent in CTT [21]. Consistency in cross-platform messaging directly enhances information reliability, thereby reducing the tourist’s decision-making uncertainty, a key outcome in CTT’s nomological network. This aligns with research suggesting that coherent brand narratives across digital touchpoints diminish consumer confusion and perceived risk [19]. Interactivity, through its facilitation of timely and reciprocal dialogue, not only improves communication quality but also enables the signaling of shared values, another critical CTT antecedent [21]. This explicit mapping resolves the theoretical ambiguity about how specific digital communication strategies function as relational inputs.

Furthermore, the study provides precise conceptual definitions that align with CTT’s theoretical structure while addressing the context-specific nature of digital tourism exchanges. Information trust is posited as the focal mediator, representing confidence in the credibility and reliability of the DMO-curated information ecosystem. This construct directly corresponds to CTT’s trust mechanism while being tailored to the pre-consumption digital evaluation phase, where the immediate “exchange partner” is the information system itself [22]. Destination brand equity is conceptualized as capturing the essence of relationship commitment in CTT, representing the stored value and strength of the tourist-destination relationship [23]. This theoretical positioning addresses concerns about construct completeness by demonstrating how core CTT elements are preserved while being appropriately contextualized.

Thus, in this research, CTT provides particular explanatory power for understanding relationship formation in high-involvement, information-intensive contexts such as tourism destination selection. The IMPACT framework for theory selection (i.e., interestingness, matching, parsimony, applicability, conceptual rigor, and testability) [24] offers an explicit rationale for selecting CTT as the guiding lens for this paper. Interestingness arises from reframing digital IMC as relationship building rather than persuasion-only influence. Matching is established through a direct theoretical co-infusion between IMC consistency and interactivity as communication antecedents, information trust as the trust mechanism, destination brand equity as a commitment-based relational asset, and travel intention as the behavioral outcome. Parsimony is maintained by retaining the core relational pathway while limiting added constructs to those required for the digitally mediated pre-trip exchange. Applicability follows for destination marketing organizations, given that cross-platform consistency and interactive design represent actionable levers for strengthening information trust and destination brand equity. Conceptual rigor is reinforced through clear construct definitions and boundary conditions, particularly the distinction between information trust and destination trust. Testability is supported through theory-derived hypotheses and an estimable structural model that allows empirical evaluation of the proposed pathway. The theory’s core mechanism, whereby strategic antecedents foster trust that subsequently cultivates commitment and reduces uncertainty, provides a parsimonious yet powerful analytical framework for decoding how systematic digital communication strategies translate into behavioral intentions.

Digital revolution of IMC

The digitalization of IMC represents a significant transition from traditional media channels to dynamic and interactive multimedia platforms, fundamentally transforming brand-audience interactions [5]. Digital platforms have become essential for building traveler communities and fostering direct interaction, strengthening customer loyalty, and advocacy of destinations aiming to enhance their brand performance and attract repeat visitors [25]. Different from traditional IMC, digital IMC leverages an array of digital tools and techniques, including social media [26], electronic word of mouth (eWOM) [27], and influencer marketing, which collectively increase brand exposure, enhance consumer interaction, and optimize marketing outcomes [28]. Furthermore, the integration of advanced technologies such as artificial intelligence (AI) [29] and machine learning [30] has revolutionized the ability of firms to track, monitor, and analyze consumer interactions in real-time. This data-driven strategy enables precise audience targeting and optimized resource allocation, significantly boosting the effectiveness of marketing campaigns.

Examining customer responsiveness to marketing communications has emerged as a critical focus in modern IMC research [31]. While significant literature highlights the transformative impact of the IMC mix on brand outcomes and its adaptability to the digital age, there remains a gap in research that thoroughly examines the influence of digital IMC dimensions on destination brand equity and tourist behavior in tourism marketing. This study focuses on two customer-oriented IMC dimensions, IMC consistency, addressing the alignment of messaging across multiple platforms [32], and IMC interactivity, emphasizing reciprocity, speed, and responsiveness in fostering lasting consumer relationships [33], as identified by [34], to assess their role in shaping tourists’ perceptions of brand equity and travel behavior.

Perceived digital IMC consistency

IMC consistency entails the alignment of a unified brand message across multiple communication channels [35], necessitating the reinforcement of consistency not only between various platforms but also across different sources of information, both professional and personal [13].

One of the primary concepts of the IMC paradigm is the imperative to sustain a unified and coherent brand message and image across an array of all organizational touchpoints. Plenty of scholars have highlighted this dimension’s pivotal role in branding development since the concept’s inception [36, 37]. The frequency and intensity of consumer exposure to consistent messaging play a critical role in shaping attitudes, reinforcing brand associations, and strengthening the consumer-brand relationship [38]. With sustained exposure, this consistency enhances brand recall and cultivates stronger emotional bonds, ultimately supporting the formation of long-term, loyal relationships between consumers and the brand [39]. Additionally, the impact of both cross-synergies and intra-channel synergies had been verified by [40] with the results that cross-synergies are more crucial for unfamiliar brands, while for familiar brands, online communication synergies play a more decisive role.

Perceived digital IMC interactivity

Interactivity within IMC serves as a foundational element of strategic relationship marketing, enabling organizations to cultivate meaningful connections with their audiences, emphasizing the fostering of two-way communication between brands and consumers. [41] identify three critical aspects: reciprocity between sender and receiver, the speed of responses, and the responsiveness of interlocutors.

The integration of interactive marketing channels has been shown to substantially strengthen an organization’s ability to connect with consumers by crafting highly personalized and impactful branding messages [42]. By prioritizing interactivity and adaptability, organizations foster stronger relationships with their customers and position themselves to achieve superior outcomes in an increasingly customer-driven market. According to [43], such integration enhances strategic responsiveness to market dynamics, facilitated by interactive communication that promotes information sharing and collaboration. [44] findings further emphasize that interactive marketing significantly drives profitability, positioning interactivity as a critical determinant of business success.

Digital IMC strategies and destination brand equity

Brand equity (BE) was defined by [45] as the brand-related assets and liabilities tied to its name and symbol that influence the value delivered to both the firm and its customers. [23] further conceptualized customer-based brand equity (CBBE) as the unique impact of brand knowledge on customer responses, reflected in preferences, perceptions, and behaviors toward brand marketing efforts. These concepts emphasize consumers’ perceptions shaped through experiential interactions with the brand [46]. In the tourism industry, branding is essential for developing effective marketing strategies for tourist destinations, as it facilitates the creation of strong, positive emotional connections between tourists and particular destinations [47]. Building on CBBE principles, [48] developed the customer-based destination brand equity (CBDBE) model for the tourism sector, identifying dimensions such as destination awareness, quality, image, and loyalty. More recently, [49] reconceptualized CBDBE to include destination brand awareness, quality, value, trust, satisfaction, and loyalty, offering a more comprehensive framework for understanding destination brand equity.

A central tenet of branding theory posits that strategic message consistency is foundational to building strong brand associations, which in turn elevate dimensions of brand equity, including awareness, perceived quality, and loyalty [50, 51]. In tourism marketing, coherent cross-platform narratives have been shown to significantly enhance tourists’ holistic perceptions of a destination’s brand value [14, 39]. However, the digital environment presents a unique challenge to consistency, as fragmented touchpoints and user-generated content can dilute brand messages, suggesting that achieving digital consistency may require more deliberate orchestration than in traditional media [52].

Hypothesis 1

Destination digital IMC consistency has a positive effect on destination brand equity.

Equally critical is the dimension of interactivity. While research robustly affirms that social media and interactive communications foster engagement and strengthen brand perceptions [53, 54], the literature reveals a more nuanced picture. Some studies suggested that the impact of interactivity may be contingent upon the type of engagement (e.g., transactional vs. communal) or the platform’s affordances [55, 56]. For instance, [57] found interactivity strengthens the link between travel attitudes and brand equity, yet the specific mechanisms—whether through fostering a sense of community, enhancing perceived responsiveness, or enabling co-creation—remain less clearly disentangled in destination contexts. This indicates that while the relationship is generally positive, its strength and underlying pathways warrant further specification.

Hypothesis 2

Destination digital IMC interactivity has a positive effect on destination brand equity.

The role of information trust in digital tourism contexts

Trust is a dynamic, multi-faceted construct essential for relational exchanges, particularly in online environments characterized by uncertainty [58]. Trust in information sources facilitates users’ development of stable cognitive assessments and affective bonds, thereby encouraging engagement via trust-based mechanisms [59]. In tourism, trust significantly reduces perceived risk, facilitates travel planning [8, 60], and user experience [61]. [62] indicated that adoption intentions for emerging digital environments, such as the Metaverse, are strongly influenced by user attitudes and technological trust. However, while research has extensively examined destination trust as an outcome of factors like e-servicescape or eWOM [63, 64], a distinct conceptual gap exists regarding information trust, specifically in the credibility and reliability of the information ecosystem curated by a marketer.

This distinction of information trust is crucial. Prior work often conflates trust in a destination entity with trust in the information about it [65, 66]. However, in the digital pre-trip phase, the tourist’s primary engagement is with information, not the physical destination. Trust must therefore be built at this informational level first. Studies in e-commerce confirm that trust in website information is a critical precursor to purchase intention [67], yet parallel research specifically linking strategic marketer-generated digital communication to this form of trust in tourism is sparse [68, 69].

Recent digital tourism studies show that platform and technology characteristics shape intention through trust-related evaluations and value perceptions, yet much of this work operationalizes communication at the platform or feature level. A complementary destination branding stream links IMC consistency and interactivity to brand outcomes, yet trust is often treated as an undifferentiated mediator. This paper connects these streams by theorizing how marketer-led, cross-platform IMC builds information trust as the proximal trust target in the pre-trip evaluation stage, which in turn strengthens destination brand equity and intention. Drawing from Commitment-Trust Theory [21], we posit that digital IMC serves as a key antecedent, communication, to information trust. [70] demonstrated that perceived benefits and enjoyment foster trust, thereby establishing strategic digital communication as a critical antecedent of information trust in destination marketing. [71] identified a significant mediating role of chatbot trust, linking interactivity and perceived humanness to users’ adoption of chatbots in e-commerce contexts. Consistency signals reliability and reduces information ambiguity, directly lowering a tourist’s decision-making uncertainty [72]. Interactivity fosters transparency and responsiveness, fulfilling expectations and building confidence in the information provider [73]. This leads to our next hypotheses:

Hypothesis 3

Destination digital IMC consistency has a positive effect on tourists’ trust.

Hypothesis 4

Destination digital IMC interactivity has a positive effect on tourists’ trust.

The relationship between trust and brand equity presents another point for theoretical clarification. While some branding models position trust as a dimension or component of brand equity [49], a process-oriented view suggests trust is a precursor that enables the formation of strong brand associations. In service relationships, trust is a foundational element that enhances perceived quality, satisfaction, and ultimately, loyalty, which are the key pillars of brand equity [74, 75]. Therefore, we argue that information trust, once established, becomes a critical input that enhances the overall valuation of the destination brand.

Hypothesis 5

Traveler’s trust toward tourism information positively impacts destination brand equity.

From brand perceptions to behavioral intention

Behavioral intention refers to the likelihood of engaging in actual behavior, influenced by an individual’s level of motivation to perform the action [76, 77]. In the context of destination marketing communications, travel intention is understood as an expression of cognitive processes that lead to behavior [78]. The formation of travel intention is influenced by both personal and informational sources [79]. Leveraging digital innovations has become essential for destinations to engage audiences and stimulate travel interest [80]. Research has shown that travel motivations, destination image, tourist satisfaction, and the impact of digital marketing play crucial roles in shaping individuals’ willingness to visit a destination [66, 74, 81, 82].

Hypothesis 6

Destination brand equity has a positive effect on tourists’ travel intention.

Hypothesis 7

Traveler’s information trust has a positive impact on their travel intention.

The use of digital platforms has been highlighted as influential in driving travel intentions. Studies have indicated that influencer marketing, vlog marketing, and the presence of embedded social media channels can impact consumer travel intent and purchase decisions [83, 84]. The significant influence of eWOM on destination trust and travel intentions highlights the importance of online reviews and recommendations [65]. Engaging destination stakeholders through digital platforms is vital for enhancing tourist attraction, visit experiences, and destination reputation [85]. [86] emphasized that a destination with strong recognition and high awareness among potential visitors can serve as a key factor in their decision-making process, significantly shaping their travel intentions. During crises like the COVID-19 pandemic, destination crisis marketing and e-WOM play crucial roles in shaping destination trust and travelers’ willingness to visit [87]. Destination brand equity affects travelers’ intentions to revisit health tourism destinations through the mediation of destination brand associations [88]. As perceived by international tourists, trust in destination marketing directly influences satisfaction and revisit intentions, impacting overall destination competitiveness [66].

Destination digital IMC interactivity has a positive effect on travel intention mediated by destination brand equity(H9a) and information trust (H9b).

Method

A structured online questionnaire was developed to examine the effects of digital IMC on destination-related information acquisition, trust formation, and travel intention in online contexts. Data were collected between 10 and 22 June 2025 from students at Zhengzhou University and Zhengzhou University of Economics and Business. Participants were recruited using a non-probability sampling strategy, with questionnaires randomly distributed across predefined student groups to minimize researcher-induced selection bias. This approach is methodologically appropriate for theory-driven structural model testing in digitally mediated tourism contexts, where the primary objective is to examine relational mechanisms rather than to estimate population parameters. Prior studies consistently identify Chinese university students as a highly digitally literate and socially connected cohort, characterized by intensive use of social media and online travel platforms as well as strong travel propensity [89–91]. This makes them a theoretically appropriate segment of digitally engaged destination information seekers, aligning closely with the study’s focus on digital IMC cues and online trust mechanisms.

Eligibility was restricted to respondents who had undertaken at least one leisure trip and actively used online platforms to search for destination information during the planning stage, ensuring experiential relevance and internal validity. To anchor responses within a concrete theoretical context and enhance measurement clarity, explicit instructions were provided at the outset of the questionnaire. Respondents were asked to base all evaluations on their experience with a specific, self-selected tourism destination they had recently visited or seriously considered visiting. Furthermore, the key construct of digital IMC was framed for respondents as the destination’s integrated digital communication landscape anchored in official destination management organization channels, where aligned user-generated information is considered part of the evaluated ecosystem only when surfaced, curated, or amplified within that destination’s digital communication environment. To assist differentiation, the survey provided clear definitions and examples. Marketer-generated content was defined as information from official sources, including destination websites, verified social media accounts, and branded advertisements, while User-generated content was exemplified through unsolicited online reviews, personal travel blogs, and individual social media posts unaffiliated with official channels.

Of the 485 questionnaires distributed, 356 valid responses were retained after data screening (effective response rate: 73.4%), exceeding recommended sample size thresholds for structural equation modeling [92]. Sample size adequacy was assessed against established quantitative research guidance for structural equation modeling, supporting model estimation quality and statistical power at this sample level [93]. While the sample is skewed toward younger travelers, the target population is explicitly defined as digitally engaged young travelers, and inferences are confined to this segment. Accordingly, managerial implications are framed for destinations and marketers seeking to engage digitally active travel information seekers, rather than the general tourist population.

This study utilized scales that were derived from the findings of the previous investigation. There exists a comprehensive set of five factors under measurement, with each factor comprising multiple items. The five items of “Digital IMC Consistency” (DIMCC) were borrowed from [14]. The five-item scale of “Digital IMC Interactivity” (DIMCI) was measured by the employing scales of [13]. The scale of [4] was employed to measure “Trust Toward Travel-Related Information” (TR) in five items. The “Destination Brand Equity” (DBE) was adopted from the research of [94], while the scale of [8] was employed to measure “Travel Intention” (INT) in five items. A five-point Likert scale was employed to evaluate these constructs, with responses ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree).

To test the hypothesized model examining the impact of digital IMC on travel intention, the data analysis employed a covariance-based structural equation modeling (CB-SEM) approach using AMOS 26.0, complemented by SPSS 27.0 for preliminary descriptive and reliability analyses. This methodological choice was guided by the primary research objective of theory testing and confirmatory analysis, focusing on understanding the complex relationships and overall model fit among the latent constructs [92]. CB-SEM is particularly suitable for this purpose, as it allows for the simultaneous estimation of the entire measurement and structural model, providing rigorous assessments of model fit indices (e.g., , CFI, TLI, RMSEA) which are essential for evaluating the validity of the proposed theoretical framework [95]. While partial least squares SEM (PLS-SEM) is often recommended for predictive, exploratory research or models with formative constructs, the current study’s model comprises well-established, reflective constructs and aims to validate an a priori theory-driven structure. Therefore, CB-SEM was deemed the most appropriate tool to prioritize model fit and parameter estimation accuracy over variance explanation [92]. To test the significance of the proposed mediation paths, a bootstrapping procedure with 5,000 resamples was employed, generating bias-corrected confidence intervals. This non-parametric approach is robust and does not assume normal distribution of the sampling data, providing a rigorous test of the indirect effects [96].

The analytical procedure followed a structured sequence. First, descriptive and frequency analyses were conducted in SPSS to profile the sample. The measurement model was then evaluated in AMOS using confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) to verify its psychometric properties. Internal consistency was assessed using Cronbach’s alpha and composite reliability. Convergent validity was established through factor loadings and average variance extracted (AVE), while discriminant validity was examined using the Fornell-Larcker criterion. Following validation of the measurement model, the structural model was estimated to test the hypothesized relationships, with model fit indices and bootstrap results providing the basis for hypothesis testing.

Digital engagement was considerable, with nearly half sharing travel-related content 6–10 times annually. Beyond statistical significance, the effect sizes provide important insights into the substantive significance of the findings. Following Cohen’s (1992) guidelines for standardized path coefficients in SEM (where 0.10 = small, 0.30 = medium, 0.50 = large), most paths in our model fall in the medium effect size range. The relative strengths of the paths indicate that while both consistency and interactivity are important for building trust and brand equity, consistency shows slightly stronger effects, particularly for brand equity development.

Nomological and predictive validity

The empirical support for the direct hypotheses establishes a foundation for evaluating the theoretical framework’s broader validity. Nomological validity is confirmed through the statistically significant and theoretically consistent relationships among all constructs (H1-H7). The pattern of results forms a coherent network that aligns with CTT and IMC principles.

The model demonstrates strong predictive validity for its focal outcome. The integrated set of predictors, digital IMC dimensions, information trust, and destination brand equity, collectively explained a substantial proportion of variance in the endogenous variables: 38.7% for travel intention (), 42.1% for destination brand equity (), and 31.2% for information trust (). Following [99] guidelines for effect sizes in behavioral research, the explanatory power for travel intention indicates a large effect, confirming the model’s strong capacity to forecast the key behavioral outcome of theoretical interest.

It allows for the precise quantification of direct, indirect (specific and total), and total effects while statistically accounting for the concurrent operation of both destination brand equity and information trust as mediators. For digital IMC consistency, the total indirect effect (, 95% CI [0.122, 0.236]) is both statistically significant () and substantively meaningful, accounting for approximately 55.5% of the total effect. A more pronounced mediation pattern emerges for digital IMC interactivity, where the analysis reveals full mediation. The direct path from interactivity to travel intention, when controlling for the mediators, is statistically non-significant (, ). In contrast, the total indirect effect is robust (, 95% CI [0.118, 0.246]), constituting approximately 68.3% of the total effect. This finding suggests that the efficacy of interactive digital communication is entirely contingent upon its success in building relational capital. Unlike consistency, which may also leverage cognitive fluency, interactivity’s persuasive power appears to be fully dependent on its capacity to foster trust and strengthen brand equity, aligning closely with the relational postulates of CTT [21].

The decomposition of effects affords a nuanced comparison of the two parallel psychological pathways. The indirect effects via destination brand equity ( for consistency; for interactivity) are consistently, albeit marginally, stronger than those via information trust ( for consistency; for interactivity). This subtle divergence suggests that while both mediators are integral, the pathway culminating in enhanced brand equity may represent a slightly more potent mechanism for converting communication efforts into behavioral intentions. This aligns with branding literature, positioning brand equity as a comprehensive repository of consumer-based brand value that directly drives choice [23].

However, the role of information trust should not be understated. Its significant indirect effects substantiate our core theoretical contention that in digitally-mediated environments, trust must first be established at the informational level to reduce perceived risk and uncertainty, a precondition for the subsequent formation of positive brand equity [22]. The parallel operation of these mediators underscores a synergistic psychological process: digital IMC fosters initial credibility (trust), which in turn amplifies the destination’s perceived brand value (equity), with both sequentially and collectively propelling travel intention.

The contrasting mediation patterns between IMC dimensions yield important theoretical implications. The partial mediation observed for consistency suggests it operates through dual routes: a relational route (via trust and equity) and a more direct, potentially cognitive route. This direct route may involve reducing information-processing costs or enhancing message persuasiveness through perceptual fluency. Conversely, the full mediation characterizing interactivity robustly supports a relational exchange paradigm. Its effect is wholly dependent on successful relationship-building, offering empirical validation for applying CTT to digital engagement.

Discussion

This research aimed to examine the influence of digital IMC consistency and interactivity on travel intention, mediated by destination brand equity and information trust. The findings enhance understanding of how digital IMC can drive travel intention while underscoring the critical roles of destination branding and information trust, offering valuable theoretical and practical insights.

Theoretical implications

This study advances CTT by clarifying the hierarchical and sequential roles of trust and destination brand equity in digitally mediated tourism decision making. Prior research has often treated trust and brand equity as parallel antecedents of behavioral intention. However, the present findings demonstrate a relational progression in which information trust represents the primary psychological outcome of digital IMC, whereas destination brand equity, conceptualized as an expression of relational commitment, emerges as the more influential proximal determinant of travel intention. This sequential pattern suggests a hierarchical structure in high-involvement contexts where trust functions as a cognitive foundation upon which affective commitment is built. This clarification addresses a longstanding conceptual ambiguity in the literature. It demonstrates that, in high involvement digital tourism contexts, trust should not be understood as a substitute for brand equity or as one of its components, but rather as a necessary relational foundation for brand value formation. By explicating a sequential process through which digital IMC cultivates information trust, facilitates the development of destination brand equity, and ultimately shapes travel intention, this study refines the application of CTT to digital environments. It further repositions digital IMC as a relational mechanism that transforms credible information exchange into enduring brand-based commitment.

Moreover,the study contributes to digital persuasion and IMC theory by revealing that different communication dimensions operate through distinct psychological mechanisms. The differential mediation effects indicate that interactivity influences travel intention entirely through trust and destination brand equity, suggesting a predominantly relational pathway. Interactive digital features derive their persuasive power from fostering engagement, reciprocity, and emotional connection, which subsequently accumulate into brand equity. In contrast, message consistency operates through a dual-pathway mechanism: beyond its indirect relational effects via trust and brand equity, consistency also exerts a direct influence on travel intention, likely by enhancing perceptual fluency, signaling reliability, and reducing information-processing complexity during decision-making. This direct effect is theoretically significant as it speaks to the unique role of informational coherence in decision-making under uncertainty. While interactivity requires cognitive and emotional engagement to generate relational value, message consistency appears to reduce perceived risk and enhance diagnosticity directly, thereby shaping intentions independently of relational pathways. This distinction advances IMC theory by demonstrating that communication dimensions are not psychologically equivalent and that their effects should be theorized in terms of dimension-specific cognitive and relational processes rather than assumed uniform influence.

Furthermore, by foregrounding information trust as a central construct, this research bridges digital communication theory, relationship marketing, and information systems literature. Rather than conceptualizing trust as a generalized belief in the destination, this study reframes trust as confidence in the marketer-curated digital information ecosystem. This reframing extends trust theory beyond interpersonal exchange to human–information system interactions, responding to calls in the literature to theorize trust formation in algorithmically mediated environments where consumers engage with curated information rather than the destination directly [100]. This shift is theoretically significant for understanding the pre-consumption evaluation stage, where tourists primarily interact with algorithmically distributed, multi-platform information rather than the destination itself. By extending CTT to human–information system relationships, the proposed model offers a more precise theoretical lens for explaining trust formation in contemporary digital environments characterized by information abundance and uncertainty. In doing so, the study moves beyond traditional brand-centric perspectives and provides a theoretically integrated account of how consistent and interactive digital communication transforms informational credibility into relational value, strengthens destination brand equity, and ultimately drives travel intention.

Practical implications

DMOs should conceptualize and manage digital communication as an integrated information value system. The strong explanatory role of information trust indicates that trust should be treated as a deliberate strategic outcome of digital IMC rather than an incidental byproduct. This requires a systematic reconfiguration of content development, curation, and dissemination processes so that they collectively function as a coherent and reliable information infrastructure. Moving beyond fragmented platform-level management, organizations should adopt centralized governance mechanisms to ensure informational accuracy, temporal relevance, and narrative coherence across all digital touchpoints. Such an approach enables the destination’s digital presence to function as a credible and low-friction source of information, thereby reducing perceived uncertainty among potential tourists.

Additionally,organizations should strategically invest in interactive mechanisms that facilitate relational depth rather than superficial engagement. Given that the effects of interactivity on travel intention are fully transmitted through trust and destination brand equity, interactive features should be designed explicitly to support relationship building. This entails prioritizing responsiveness, dialogue quality, and opportunities for reciprocal value creation over the mere frequency of interactions. The managerial implication is a shift from using interactivity as an attention-generating tactic toward designing digital environments that sustain meaningful exchanges and reinforce long-term relational bonds between tourists and destinations.

Furthermore, the identified synergy between trust and destination brand equity suggests the need for a strategically sequenced communication portfolio. Communication efforts should be temporally and functionally aligned, with early-stage initiatives emphasizing informational credibility through verified content and authoritative sources. Once a foundation of trust has been established, subsequent communication activities can focus on reinforcing favorable brand associations and emotionally resonant narratives. This integrated sequencing ensures that consistency and interactivity are deployed in a complementary manner, allowing trust to be systematically converted into destination brand equity and, ultimately, into travel intention.

Conclusions, limitations, and future directions

This study extends the current literature on relationship marketing and digital communication by rephrasing the significance of trust and brand equity in forming travel intentions. Specifically, the research underscores the importance of leveraging digital IMC strategies that combine consistent messaging with interactive engagement to build consumer trust. This process strengthens the destination’s brand equity and creates a more compelling value proposition for potential tourists. By integrating CTT, this research provides empirical evidence that trust is a foundational element in establishing long-term consumer relationships, with brand equity amplifying its influence on travel decisions. These insights have significant practical implications for tourism marketers, guiding them on how to develop more effective digital IMC strategies to influence consumer behavior and enhance market competitiveness.

However, current research encounters several limitations that present valuable opportunities for future exploration. Firstly, the use of a cross-sectional design offers an efficient test of the theoretical model, though it inherently limits our capacity to delineate the temporal dynamics underlying trust formation and brand relationship evolution. Future longitudinal approaches could more effectively capture how these relational constructs develop through iterative consumer-marketer interactions over time. Additionally, while statistical precautions were implemented, the reliance on self-reported data collected via a single survey instrument introduces the possibility of common method bias. Inference is limited to digitally engaged young travelers in Mainland China who rely on online sources during pre-trip destination evaluation. Generalization to older cohorts, non-student populations, and other cultural contexts requires additional validation. Interpretations should remain associational within the cross-sectional design, while longitudinal designs can evaluate temporal ordering and stability of the proposed trust and brand equity sequence. Future studies should aim to replicate these findings in more diverse cultural contexts and across broader deFmographic populations to test the robustness and boundary conditions of the proposed framework. Consequently, the generalizability of the effect magnitudes to broader tourist populations or different cultural settings merits further validation. Furthermore, the present model focuses on core relational mediators, providing a parsimonious explanation. However, the strength of these pathways may vary under different conditions. Incorporating potential boundary conditions, such as individual differences in prior destination knowledge or the purpose of travel, could yield a more nuanced understanding of when digital IMC strategies are most effective.

Building on this foundation, several promising avenues emerge. First, employing longitudinal or diary methodologies would allow researchers to trace the developmental sequence of trust and commitment as theorized by CTT, moving from static correlation to dynamic process analysis. Second, introducing theoretically-grounded moderating variables could enhance the model’s explanatory power and delineate its boundaries. For example, examining how cultural orientation (e.g., uncertainty avoidance) or message source credibility moderates the trust formation process would test the robustness of the current findings and lead to more contingent theory. Finally, as digital interaction paradigms shift, future work should explore how emerging technologies like AI-driven personalization or immersive media redefine the core constructs of this study and their interrelationships.

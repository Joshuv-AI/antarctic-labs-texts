Braiding machine for reinforcing silicone tubes with pineapple leaf fibers in soft robotics applications: Dataset on 3D design
Authors: Van-Tinh Nguyen, Anh-Duc Trinh
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Soft robotics is an emerging field of research involving robots capable of flexible deformation and adaptation to their surroundings. This field is currently experiencing rapid development on a global scale, with a wide range of diverse applications such as exploration, medical devices, agriculture, etc. Unlike traditional rigid robotics technology, soft robots are made from soft materials and flexible silicone actuators that are programmed to contract and stretch, enabling smooth movements in response to stimuli. The fabrication of soft robotics withstands many challenges. Currently, the braiding process for reinforcing silicone tubes to produce soft robots is mainly implemented manually. It results in time-consuming and low productivity. Thus, it is necessary to develop an automatic braiding machine for silicone tubes to meet the demands of a large production scale. This paper presents a detailed plan for designing and manufacturing a pineapple fiber braiding machine to reinforce silicone tubes for use in the fabrication of artificial muscles or soft robots. The proposed approach encompasses systematic methods, including research, kinematic calculations, control design, and fabrication. The proposed machine is compact, has automatic operation, and has higher efficiency in comparison to conventional manual techniques.

Background

In recent years, the development of soft robotics research has experienced a remarkable expansion, demonstrating significant applicability across a diverse range of complex and dynamic tasks in various sectors, notably within military and healthcare applications [1,2]. Unlike traditional robotic systems, which are often comprised of numerous components fabricated from high-rigidity materials through machining processes and subsequently assembled into intricate mechanical structures, soft robotics signifies a remarkable advancement in technology. These systems, characterized by their construction from flexible and elastic materials such as polymers and silicone, offer enhanced adaptability to unstructured environments and are more acceptable at addressing practical demands.

The advancement in biotechnology and materials science has profoundly influenced the research and development trajectories of soft robotics. These innovative systems employ flexible and elastic materials, such as polymers and silicones, for their structural and actuation elements. The inherent compliance and variable stiffness of soft robots facilitate compatibility with human biological tissues, thereby rendering them particularly suited for medical applications.

According to a comprehensive study by Nazek El-Atab and colleagues, various methods exist to control soft robotics mechanisms depending on their intended application and specific characteristics [3]. Pneumatic pressure actuation mechanisms, such as the McKibben actuators, are widely applied in surgical robots and medical devices [4,5,6]. When pressurized, these actuators facilitate both axial contraction and radial expansion, allowing for flexible movements and high-speed adjustments.

Currently, the braiding process for reinforcing silicone tubes is mainly implemented manually. It results in time-consuming and low productivity [7,8]. This article presents a detailed plan for designing and manufacturing a pineapple fiber braiding machine to reinforce silicone tubes for use in the fabrication of artificial muscles or soft robots. The proposed machine automatically operates and enhances productivity in comparison with manual methods.

Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

Working principle of braiding module

The inputs of the proposed machine are silicone tube and pineapple fibers in which pineapple fibers are characterized by high cellulose content (56–82 %), high strength and stiffness, and a shiny, silky appearance. Typical physical properties include diameters of 34.9–168.3 μm, linear density of 1.39–7.07 tex, and breaking strength ranging from 11.42 to over 114.79 Mpa [9] while key specifications of silicone include tensile strength (5 - 11 N/mm²), modulus of elasticity (0.1 - 50 Mpa), and high elongation (100 - 1100 %) [10,11]. The principle of the Maypole braider is inspired by the traditional Maypole dance observed in several European countries, giving rise to the name “Maypole braider.” The Maypole braiding machine features bobbin carriers that hold spools of pineapple fiber yarn for braiding. These carriers are driven by horn gears, allowing them to move in a cyclic pattern. Half of the bobbins move clockwise, while the other half move counterclockwise. The bobbins follow the guide paths on the guide plate, alternately crossing left and right as they meet. This interweaving motion allows the fibers to twist around each other, forming the braided structure. The bobbin will move through position 1 to position 8 as shown in Fig. 1b As a result, the 3D model of the braiding mechanism is presented in Fig. 2. Fabrication of the proposed machine

The proposed machine structure is compact and consists of four main modules: silicone tube feeding module, braiding module, tube harvesting module, and frame as illustrated in Fig. 3a. The machine frame is made of aluminum profiles, while the other components are made from PLA and acrylic plastic and manufactured using 3D printing and laser cutting methods. The overall machine dimensions are 400 × 350 × 600 mm. The experiment shows that the proposed machine works effectively on various diameters of tube from 4 to 8 mm, its productivity is 1.2m/min, and the braided product is collected onto the drum as illustrated in Fig. 3b To evaluate the stability of the machine, we conducted 30 experiments, each using a 10 cm tube segment. The experiment results showed that the machine braided 29 tube segments successfully, accounting for 96.7 %.

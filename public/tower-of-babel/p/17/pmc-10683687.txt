Commentary on ‘Optical coherence tomography angiography parameters in patients taking hydroxychloroquine therapy – cross-sectional study’
Authors: Debdulal Chakraborty
Published: 2023

Hydroxychloroquine (HCQ) is an important disease-modifying agent in the management of a variety of autoimmune disorders and has an emerging role in oncology also.[1] While the mechanism of HCQ toxicity in the retina remains unknown, melanin may contribute to the development of HCQ retinopathy by concentrating the drug in retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) cells and destroying photoreceptors and the outer nuclear layer of the retina.[2] Predisposing risk factors associated with the development of HCQ retinopathy include daily dose >6.5 mg/kg, cumulative dose >1000 g, duration of use over 5 years, old age, kidney or liver dysfunction, or pre-existing maculopathy.[3] HCQ as a drug has shown tremendous promise in the treatment of many diseases. As systemic toxicity of HCQ is low, it should not be stopped unless absolutely necessary.

Various retinal vascular pathologies such as diabetic retinopathy, retinal vein occlusion, macular telangiectasia, and choroidal neovascularization have been evaluated so far with optical coherence tomography angiography (OCTA).[4] OCTA can be a modality to investigate the retinal and choroidal microvascular structures and detect alterations at a very early stage of HCQ-induced retinopathy. HCQ retinopathy, if detected before the occurrence of RPE cell damage, may enable preservation of vision.[5]

Traditionally, for the detection of HCQ-induced damage to the eye, visual acuity score, visual field testing with 10-2 test, and fundus examination are among the subjective tests, while fundus autofluorescence photography, multifocal electroretinography (mf-ERG), and SD-OCT are the objective tests.[5] OCTA has the ability to noninvasively gather high-resolution three-dimensional mapping of the retinal and choroidal circulations, by using endoluminal flow as contrast. With OCTA, foveal avascular zone (FAZ) and capillary density (CD) can be measured at both the superficial capillary plexus and deep capillary plexus levels, and also an idea about vascular flow rate as well as blood perfusion may be gathered. Moreover, a correlation with OCT B-scan parameters such as central macular thickness and subfoveal choroidal thickness can also be established.[6]

In a study by Bulut et al.,[5] retinal thickness measurement with B-scan OCT did not show any changes in favor of retinopathy, whereas OCTA measurements revealed statistically significant superficial and deep CD loss and FAZ enlargement in high-risk patients under treatment with HCQ. Ozek et al. noted that changes in vascular density (VD) and thickness were observed before perimetry tests.[7] Akhlaghi et al. noted that VD in the deep capillary plexus layer was decreased in HCQ-treated patients with abnormal mf-ERG compared to patients with normal mf-ERG.[8]

In the current study[9], the authors have noted that the FAZ was significantly enlarged in the high-risk group compared to the controls. The superficial parafoveal VD, deep whole vascular, and deep perifoveal VD were found to be lower in the high-risk group compared to the low-risk group and the control group. The authors also noted that the duration of drug use had a significant negative correlation with superficial VD parameters. They thus concluded that OCTA parameters may be useful for the early detection of signs of toxic retinopathy in high-risk patients. It is however necessary to mention that the current study had a cross-sectional study design and as such the association between two variables should be interpreted carefully, considering the fact that it cannot establish cause–effect relationships between different variables.

To conclude, early detection of retinopathy before RPE damage has set in is key to preventing serious damage to the retina and central vision loss. In this OCTA may provide useful clues for the early detection of HCQ-induced retinopathy.

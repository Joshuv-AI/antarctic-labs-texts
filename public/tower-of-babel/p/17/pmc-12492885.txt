Quick response codes for management of systemic diseases in dental clinics
Authors: Maryam Bakhtiar, Najmeh Taheri, Maryam S Tavangar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAbout one-third of patients referred to dental clinics have systemic diseases. Dentists should be aware of how to manage systemic diseases in such patients, enabling them to provide safe dental treatments. Consequently, they require access to a reliable and up-to-date source of information based on the latest guidelines. The present study was conducted to evaluate the effectiveness of Quick Response (QR) codes as a new method for learning and reminding in systemic disease management in general dentistry clinics, and to assess the level of satisfaction with using QR codes.Materials and methodsThis quasi-experimental study was conducted to evaluate an educational intervention for general dentists, utilizing a pre- and post-test design. The study was designed in four consecutive steps: QR code preparation, pilot study performance, QR code implementation, and evaluation of its efficacy. Ten QR codes were developed, each linked to educational content about the management of patients with a specific systemic disease. These QR codes were displayed on a poster attached to the dental clinics’ walls. After 3 months of QR code usage by dentists, the following variables were assessed using questionnaires: (1) the effectiveness of QR code implementation on participants’ basic knowledge about systemic disease management before and after using QR codes, and (2) the degree of dentists’ satisfaction with QR code usage.ResultsForty general dentists participated in the study. The average knowledge score before and after the survey was 8.52 ± 3.05 and 11.17 ± 2.64, respectively, indicating a statistically significant increase (P value < 0.01). A significant relationship was observed between the number of QR code uses and post-test scores (p-value = 0.001). However, there was no significant relationship between the number of exposures to patients with systemic diseases and either the pre-test (p-value = 0.14) or post-test (p-value = 0.05). Based on the satisfaction questionnaire, 77.5% of participants reported that they had improved skills when facing patients with systemic diseases after using QR codes for three months. Also, 75% declared that their access to essential information was facilitated. The need for medical referral decreased in 50% of the participants.ConclusionQR codes may be effective in teaching or reminding dentists to manage patients with systemic diseases correctly. Patient referrals may also decrease. Furthermore, based on dentists’ self-assessment, their access to essential information, satisfaction, and perception may improve.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12909-025-07919-9.Keywords: QR code, Dentistry, Dental procedures, Systemic disease

Introduction

Approximately one-third of patients referred to the dental clinics have systemic diseases like diabetes mellitus, malignancies, hypertension, etc. Dentists should know how to face patients seeking dental treatments who suffer from systemic diseases to provide dental treatments safely and prevent complications [1]. Several studies have shown that some dentists don’t have enough information about the management of medically compromised or vulnerable patients [2] such as pregnant ladies, those suffering from diabetes mellitus [3], and those who have hypertension [4]. Therefore, more instructions about medically compromised patients are needed for dental students and practitioners [1, 2].

Educational courses, workshops, and online programs on the management of systemic diseases can help dentists improve their basic knowledge in clinical practice [5]. As most dentists work independently in their private offices, it seems essential for them to have access to available and reliable sources of information [6]. Furthermore, many clinical guidelines are updated regularly, and dentists must have access to these updates.

A Quick Response Code (QR Code) is a two-dimensional barcode [7] that can contain a tremendous amount of information, such as text, audio, and video [8]. The introduction of QR codes has expanded the field of mobile learning in clinical practice, facilitating participants’ access to evidence-based information anywhere, anytime, and especially at the point of care [9, 10]. They can be easily decoded by every smartphone equipped with a suitable camera and scanner [7]. QR code scanners are now widely available on smartphones, and most people have easy access to them [11, 12].

QR code technology is currently utilized for educational purposes in some areas of the healthcare system, such as the development of online instructions for dental products [9]. The usefulness of this technology has been approved in its current applications and has satisfied its operators in the healthcare environment [13]. However, the reliability and effectiveness of QR code use for educational purposes in other aspects of clinical application need to be determined [8]. In this study, we evaluated the effectiveness of using QR codes to help general dentists encounter patients with systemic diseases in their clinics and assess their satisfaction with applying these codes.

Materials and methods

Study design

This quasi-experimental study was conducted to assess the impact of an educational intervention on general dentists, using pre- and post-tests from April 2023 to July 2024. The study was designed in four consecutive steps: QR code preparation, pilot study performance, QR code implementation, and evaluation of its efficacy. The following variables were assessed: [1] the effectiveness of QR code implementation on participants’ knowledge and [2] the degree of dentists’ satisfaction with QR code usage.

Sample size calculation and sampling method

Primarily, a study on 12 participants was conducted as the pilot phase. The sample size was calculated based on the results of the pilot study, a type I error rate of α = 0.05, and a power of 90%. The mean (± standard deviation [SD]) score of the pilot sample was 8.58 ± 3.15 and 10.17 ± 3.56, respectively, before and after the intervention, with a correlation coefficient of r = 0.60. The required sample size was 32 dentists. However, finally, 40 dentists were included in this study. G Power Software version 3.1.9.2 was employed for sample size calculation.

For this purpose, a multi-stage sampling technique was employed to choose samples. Consequently, ten clinics were randomly selected from the list of all clinics (cluster sampling method). Then, four dentists were randomly selected from the list of dentists employed in each clinic (simple random sampling). Random number generation was done to choose dentists using the “sample” command in R software.

Ethics approval and consent to participate

All participants provided written informed consent before enrollment. This study adheres to the Declaration of Helsinki, and the ethics committee of Shiraz University of Medical Sciences has approved the study protocol, which was assigned an IR approval number: SUMS.DENTAL.REC.1401.065.

QR code preparation

The researchers generated ten QR codes through a dedicated online QR code generator website (https://www.qr-code-generator.com/). Each QR code was unique, specifically for one of the systemic diseases. Creating QR codes was easily accomplished using the instructions on the site, and no specialized hardware or ongoing technical support was required.

QR code contents included dental management of 10 common systemic diseases, including diabetes, hypertension, ischemic heart diseases, hypothyroidism, hyperthyroidism, acquired bleeding disorders, chronic kidney disease, liver disease, asthma, and epilepsy Each QR code content included the most important considerations about managing dental procedures in patients with that specific systemic disease; such as prophylactic antibiotic issues, anesthesia, analgesic selection, and probable emergencies (a sample of QR code content is provided at Supplementary material 1). To ensure the evidence-based and up-to-date status of the QR code content, the content of QR codes was derived from the last (most recent) edition of two leading, credible oral medicine textbooks: “Little and Falace’s Dental Management of the Medically Compromised Patient” and “Burket’s Oral Medicine”. [14, 15] Additionally, the investigators are committed to regularly reviewing reliable guidelines and updating the code contents whenever a new guideline is released.

QR code implementation

After preparation, all ten QR codes were represented in a single poster (the poster is provided in supplementary material 2). Posters were attached to the walls of the selected clinics in a suitable position, allowing participants to scan each QR code easily for a period of three months. Additionally, the study process and the use of QR codes were explained and demonstrated to the participants. The dentists were instructed to use QR codes before the initiation of therapeutic dental procedures. Apart from this, there was no limitation regarding the exact timing and situation of QR code use in our study; each dentist was free to use the codes in the clinic environment whenever needed, based on their clinical judgment. At the end of the survey, access was provided to all participants who expressed interest in continuing to have the posters in their working environment.

Evaluation of the effectiveness of QR codes

Two identical multiple-choice questionnaires were designed as pre-tests and post-tests to evaluate the effectiveness of QR codes. Each questionnaire consisted of 15 multiple-choice questions regarding the dental management of the previously mentioned systemic diseases. The questionnaires were filled out in the presence of investigators using an electronic platform. The dentists had 30 minutes to complete each questionnaire. Before initiating the main study, the reliability of the questionnaires was assessed through a pilot study involving 12 dentists. Data from the 12 dentists who participated in the pilot testing were excluded from the final study sample and were not included in the analysis of the study results.

Oral medicine specialists validated the designed questionnaire, and its internal consistency was confirmed with a Cronbach's alpha coefficient of 0.80%. Pre-tests and post-test questionnaires are provided in Supplementary Material 3.

At the beginning of the study, before the implementation of QR codes in dental clinics, participants’ basic knowledge was assessed using pre-test questionnaires. At the end of the survey (three months later), post-test questionnaires were administered. The effectiveness of QR code usage for systemic disease management was then evaluated by comparing the pre-test and post-test questionnaire results. In both the pre-test and post-test, a positive point (+1) was considered for every correct answer, and a zero score was considered for every incorrect one (false or blank).

Evaluation of satisfaction and perception of applying QR codes

Besides the post-tests, two adjunct questionnaires were administered to participants at the end of the study to assess their perception (Supplementary material 4) and satisfaction (Supplementary material 5) regarding the effect of QR codes on dental management of systemic diseases.

Statistical analysis

Data are presented as mean ± Standard deviation. A paired t-test was used to compare the results of the pre-test and post-test. All data were analyzed using SPSS Software version 22. The correlation between various parameters was assessed using Pearson’s correlation test. A P-value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Forty general dentists participated in the study. Of all, 16 (40%) participants were men, and 24 (60%) were women. The mean age of the participants was 35.10±8.26 years. The average dentist’s exposure to patients with systemic diseases in dental clinics was 6.20 ± 5.02 patients during the 3-month study period. The average number of QR code usage was 4.05±4.42 in that period. Twenty-six (65%) of participants took part in retraining courses last year.

Fifteen questions were used in the pre-test. The average of correct answers before and after the study was 8.52±3.05 and 11.17±2.64, respectively, and the p-value between the pre-test and post-test scores was significant (P-value<0.001, Pearson’s Correlation [PC] = 0.64). There was a significant inverse correlation between age and both pre-test and post-test results (P-value = 0.02, r = -0.35). Additionally, gender did not significantly affect the scores from the post-test (P-value = 0.73, PC = 0.05), the pre-test (P-value = 0.64, PC = 0.07), or the satisfaction questionnaire (P-value = 0.24, PC = 0.19).

A significant relationship was found between the repetition of QR code usage and post-test scores, while no correlation was observed with the pre-test results. In addition, there was no significant relationship between the amount of dentists’ exposure to patients with systemic diseases and the pre-test (P-value = 0.14) or post-test (P-value= 0.05) results. Discussion

In the present study, we evaluated the role of QR code implantations in enhancing dentists’ knowledge and satisfaction level for the management of patients with systemic diseases. The results indicate that the average of the correct answers before using the codes was 8.52 ± 3.05 out of 15. Following the intervention, the frequency of correct answers in all questions increased, resulting in a significant difference between the pre- and post-test scores. A significant relationship was found between age and both post- and pre-test scores.

Dentists must be prepared to encounter patients with medical emergencies and systemic diseases [2]. In previous studies, reports of dentists' exposure to medical emergencies were variable. In contrast, Elanchezian et al. reported that two-thirds of dentists had encountered these cases [16], two other studies have stated that dentists have encountered at least one systemic emergency in the past few years [17, 18]Our study showed that the average dentist’s exposure to patients with systemic diseases in dental clinics was about six patients in three months. These variabilities may result from the study settings, the unpredictable nature of patient referrals, and different standards for defining systemic disorders.

Advances in technology have brought innovations to the field of education. Traditional education, combined with new technologies, is continually expanding. QR codes are one of these innovations [19]. The main features of QR codes are immediate response, easy access, and cost-effective use compared to traditional methods [8].

Few studies have evaluated the usage of QR codes in the field of dentistry. In a survey by Abdulgader et al., the opinions of dental hospital staff on the use of QR codes as identity tags to integrate patients’ data into healthcare systems were investigated. They assessed the barriers and benefits of implementing a wristband Quick Response (QR) code into the documentation process. The majority of the staff agreed to implement QR code systems [8]. The study by Kafadarova and colleagues analyzed dentists’ and patients’ perceptions of the effectiveness of using QR codes in everyday dentists' practice. The QR codes’ contents were about dental products, materials, instruments, and techniques. They found a positive attitude toward using QR codes among both dentists and patients [13]. These findings demonstrated the high potential of QR codes as an efficient short-term educational tool for improving clinical competency. The present study used QR codes to remind dentists about dental considerations for managing systemic diseases. Then, the effectiveness of QR codes and their impact on dentists' satisfaction with dental considerations of systemic diseases were measured.

Seventy percent of participants in our study felt that the QR code information made them more oriented toward systemic disease management. Debik and his colleagues, who used QR codes to evaluate the performance of medical residents, found that 57.1% of faculty members believed that QR codes facilitate interaction, and 85.7% felt more comfortable communicating with residents using the QR codes [20]. Kariya and colleagues found that attitudes toward the use of QR codes were generally positive [21]. In Rikala et al.'s study on the use of QR codes in education, it was shown that students were eager to use QR codes. Also, it was pointed out that QR codes can motivate students to learn and draw their attention to the class [22].

Most Studies related to the use of QR codes in education have been conducted mainly in the field of mobile learning. According to Su et al., the most crucial aspect of blended learning is the triad of "place independence," "time independence," and "meaningful content." These three main characteristics are specific to learning with mobile phones, and according to these characteristics, they differ from e-learning and web-based learning [23, 24]. The speed of mobile phone technology development is increasing and is penetrating all aspects of human life. Therefore, this technology plays an essential role in learning. Today, a clear shift from teacher-based to student-based learning is evident [25]. In Riser et al.'s research, students responded positively to using QR codes in large anatomy labs, and 89% confirmed that these codes helped them learn anatomy[26]. In McCabe et al.'s study, all students commented positively about QR codes published for math lessons. During the course, 83% of students reported that they were better prepared for the next lesson and did their homework more effectively, and 67% reported that their course grades increased and that they found QR codes easy to use [27]. Lin and colleagues used QR codes for a case-based learning course. They concluded that most participants had positive attitudes towards using QR codes in these case-based learning courses and declared their intention to use this program in the future [28]. Liu et al. developed a learning system to improve the English language proficiency of students using QR codes and demonstrated that the QR code system facilitated English language learning [29]. In a study by Huang et al., who developed a learning platform using QR codes, students showed significant improvement in learning [30]. In general, most studies similar to ours have shown that QR codes are a helpful medium for enhancing the educational environment.

In our study, 55% of participants expressed a desire to incorporate QR code technology into their future dental practice. Lack of interest was the most common reason for those who did not plan to use QR codes in the future. Other reasons included a preference to refer systemic patients to specialists, a lack of time, and difficulty using QR codes at work. In a previous study, participants did not use QR codes because they were unwilling to carry smartphones in the laboratory environment [15]. In another study, the lack of smartphone access and the unwillingness to use mobile phones in some environments were among the main challenges of using QR codes[21]. In a study, the most crucial factor preventing participants from using this technology was the need for sufficient skill and knowledge to install the software, a lack of familiarity with this technology, a lack of required hardware, and difficulty in establishing an Internet connection[19]. In our study, the smaller group of dentists who did not prefer to use QR codes felt that they might lack the time or were unfamiliar with them, and were more likely to refer patients with systemic diseases to specialists.

Limitations

This study faced Some limitations. First, it included a small number of participants. This has happened because finding dentists who were willing to cooperate for the 3 months of the study was very difficult. Consequently, we limited the number of participants to the calculated sample size only. Selecting several dentists from one clinic may raise doubts that discussions among the chosen dentists may happen, negating the need to access QR codes. To minimize the risk of bias resulting from intra-cluster communications, we increased the number of clusters (k = 10) and decreased the number of participants in each cluster (n = 4). It is noteworthy that by including several dentists from each clinic, we could better monitor the study's proper conduct.

Potential confounding variables such as the repetition of pre-test and post-test questions, self-reported satisfaction questionnaires, variability in QR code usage, years of professional experience, and prior training may have affected the consistency and generalizability of the findings. However, it is worth noting that the purpose of our study was to evaluate the effectiveness of a practical educational tool (QR codes) in a real-world dental clinic setting.

There may be some concerns regarding the practicality of QR codes in emergencies due to time constraints. Therefore, further studies are suggested to establish the effectiveness of QR codes in managing dental emergencies.

Conclusion

It can be concluded that QR codes may be effective in teaching or reminding dentists to encounter patients with systemic diseases properly. Patient referrals may also decrease. Furthermore, based on dentists’ self-assessment, their access to essential information, satisfaction, and perception may improve.

Colistin use in neonates with multidrug-resistant Gram-negative infections: challenges in antimicrobial stewardship
Authors: Muhammad Shahid Mehmood, Fatima Hajj
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The resurgence of colistin as a last-resort antibiotic has become pivotal in managing multidrug-resistant (MDR) Gram-negative infections in neonatal intensive care units. Despite its life-saving potential, colistin use in neonates remains controversial due to limited pharmacokinetic data, variable dosing strategies, and substantial risks of nephrotoxicity and neurotoxicity. Globally, MDR Gram-negative infections account for nearly half of late-onset neonatal sepsis cases, contributing to high mortality and prolonged hospitalization. The emergence of mcr-mediated colistin resistance and inconsistent adherence to antimicrobial stewardship protocols further complicate management. Evidence reveals that a significant proportion of neonatal colistin use is empiric rather than culture-driven, underscoring the gap between guidelines and clinical practice. Strengthening stewardship frameworks, integrating therapeutic drug monitoring, and adopting real-time resistance surveillance are essential to safeguard colistin efficacy and mitigate resistance spread among vulnerable neonatal populations.

To the Editor,

The global rise of multidrug-resistant (MDR) Gram-negative bacteria has renewed the use of colistin as a last-resort therapy in critically ill neonates. Recent surveillance reports indicate MDR Gram-negative infections account for up to 30–50% of late-onset neonatal sepsis cases, with mortality rates ranging from 25% to 60% depending on resistance profile and clinical setting[1,2]. Despite its nephrotoxic and neurotoxic potential, colistin remains essential against carbapenem-resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae and Acinetobacter baumannii in neonatal intensive care units (NICUs)[3].

Pharmacokinetic variability in neonates complicates dose optimization, as underdosing risks therapeutic failure while overdosing increases toxicity[4]. Resistance mechanisms such as mcr-mediated colistin resistance have emerged in neonatal isolates, with prevalence reported as up to 12% in some tertiary care settings[5]. This trend underscores a critical need for stewardship protocols that guide appropriate colistin initiation, monitoring, and de-escalation.

A 2025 multicenter study highlighted that over 40% of neonates receiving colistin lacked culture-guided therapy, reflecting the disparity between evidence-based recommendations and real-world practice[6]. In immunocompromised neonates, especially those with prolonged antibiotic exposure, the situation is further aggravated by cross-transmission of MDR pathogens within NICUs[7]. Effective infection control, antimicrobial stewardship, and real-time resistance surveillance are urgently required to preserve the efficacy of this last-line antibiotic.

Recent insights emphasize the interconnectedness of resistance dynamics across patient populations. Qadri et al demonstrated the global dissemination of MDR Acinetobacter baumannii in immunocompromised patients, highlighting hospital-based environmental persistence and transborder genomic exchange as key drivers of spread[8]. These findings reinforce the urgency of revising neonatal antimicrobial policies to integrate both local resistance patterns and global containment strategies.

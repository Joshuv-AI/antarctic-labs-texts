Identifying the coupling coordination relationship and driving forces between urbanization and the supply–demand of ecosystem service: a case study of the Gansu Province
Authors: Guofeng Dang, Jinzhou Hu, Guibin Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAssessing the correlation between urbanization and the supply–demand of ecosystem services (ESS) aids in driving the sustainable and high-quality regional development. Exploring the degree of coupling coordination among urbanization and the supply and demand of ESS, as well as their relevant driving factors holds significant theoretical and practical value. This study utilizes elevation, meteorological, NDVI, population density, nighttime light, and socio-economic data to quantify the supply–demand relationships of ESS and the comprehensive urbanization level (CUL). It analyzed the ESS supply–demand and CUL in Gansu Province during the 2002–2022 period, and used the coupling coordination degree (CCD) model to analyze their coupling coordination relationship at different scales. Finally, the geographic detector was applied to recognize the key driving factors that have an effect on CCD. The results indicate: (1) The supply–demand ratio (ESSDR) of water services and comprehensive ecosystem services (CESS) show an upward trend, while that of carbon services and soil services shows a downward trend, the change in habitat quality is not obvious. (2) CUL has increased and continues to rise, with regional development imbalances and a spatially scattered, point-like distribution pattern. (3) Overall, the CCD between CUL and ESSDR is low, with most regions in a state of imbalance, but the CCD level shows an overall upward trend. (4) In factor detection, socio-economic factors exhibit greater influence than natural factors, with population density has a relatively significant influence. In interaction detection, natural factors become the primary driving force, with NDVI showing a increase in influence when synergized with other factors.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-42914-0.Keywords: Ecosystem services, Supply and demand relationship, Urbanization, Coupling coordination degree, Geographical detectorSubject terms: Ecology, Ecology, Environmental sciences, Environmental social sciences

Introduction

Ecosystem services (ESS) encompass the diverse range of benefits that humans derive, both directly or indirectly, through ecosystem structures, functions, and processes of ecosystems1. As a critical bridge connecting nature and human society, ESS has garnered widespread attention since its formal introduction and quantification2. The launch of the United Nations Millennium Ecosystem Assessment marked a significant advancement in ESS research, shifting the focus from merely quantifying supply capacity or consumption demand to a thorough analysis of supply–demand matching3. Recent years have witnessed the emergence of ESS supply–demand relationships as a noteworthy and rapidly growing research frontier4. ESS supply is defined as the capacity of ecosystems to produce both ecological products and services5, such as clean water, fertile soil, a healthy living environment, and climate regulation through carbon sequestration6. The InVEST model is rooted in empirical knowledge and ecosystem processes, and has been extensively validated for its efficacy in visualizing ecological functions and supporting research outcomes7,8. ESS demand represents the direct and indirect appropriation of ecosystem products and services by human populations9. Over the past half-century, approximately 60% of global ESS have faced risks of degradation and collapse10. Rapid intensification of anthropogenic activities has severely compromised natural ecosystem functions through high-density development11, while the sharp increase in ESS demand has led to imbalances in ESS quantities and geographical mismatches between supply and demand12.

The interaction between urbanization and ESS has emerged as a pivotal research frontier in contemporary human–environment system science13. Numerous studies have assessed the mutual influence between urbanization and ESS, finding that urbanization and ecological environment exhibit coupled characteristics by reciprocal interactions and feedback mechanisms14. By analyzing the spatiotemporal coupling characteristics between ESS and urbanization, studies have explored ecological management, zoning, and optimization pathways15, aiming to elucidate the nature of urban-environment interactions and determine whether these relationships manifest as conflicting or harmonious. This is critical for achieving both urban development and ecological conservation. Currently, numerous global studies have systematically investigated the complex interrelationships between regional urbanization and ecosystem dynamics16. Regarding research methodologies, scholars have employed spatial regression models17, spatial autocorrelation analysis18, and the coupling coordination degree (CCD) model19 to carry out a quantitative assessment of the interactive association between urbanization and ESS. Of all these, the CCD model effectively elucidates the bidirectional interactions between the two and quantitatively analyzes their coordination levels during development, serving as an important tool for evaluating the coordination between ecological environment and urbanization20. In terms of research content, Yichen Fang analyzed the supply–demand relationships of the ESS from a water-soil-food-carbon nexus perspective, providing a theoretical foundation to support the management and allocation of urban resources21. Chuanjun Sun worked out an urban ecological well-being measurement framework, with its basis in the ESS supply–demand matching degree, calculated the ecological well-being of Shenzhen City, and proposed improvement strategies22. Jian Peng conducted an evaluation of the trade-offs and synergies within the ESS supply–demand budgets in the Pearl River Delta Urban Agglomeration and explored the decoupling mechanisms between ESS supply–demand budget and urbanization23. In terms of research scale, different academics have explored the relationships between urbanization and ESS across multiple scales, such as national24, urban agglomeration25, county-level26, and township-level27. While existing research has examined the impact of urbanization on the ESS, gaps remain in grasping the internal driving mechanisms of this coupled relationship, research on how socio-economic factors influence the CCD between urbanization and ESS supply–demand remains limited28.

In recent years, China’s rapid urbanization is not only reflected in land urbanization, characterized by swift urban construction land expansion, but also through population urbanization and economic urbanization29. However, as urbanization progresses at a rapid rate, it has further reinforced the spatial decoupling between supply and demand in various regional contexts30. Within the context of traditional urbanization driven primarily by economic growth, environmental degradation, imbalanced development, and social inequality have become increasingly prominent issues, and this poses challenges to regional sustainable development31. To promote urbanization and ESS in a coordinated manner, it is indispensable to strengthen the comprehension of how urbanization interacts with the supply–demand of ESS32. Since 1978, a series of major ecological engineering projects have been rolled out by the Chinese government33, Gansu Province is a key component of China’s “Two Screens and Three Belts” ecological security strategy34, and local governments have also launched a number of projects, such as the Comprehensive River Basin Treatment Plan for the Shiyang River Basin, the Ecological Conservation and Comprehensive Management Plan for the Qilian Mountain, and the Integrated Rehabilitation Program for the Loess Plateau. However, the low-quality ecological environment remains a prominent constraint on Gansu Province’s socio-economic development. Water resource allocation remains uneven, and issues such as soil erosion, soil pollution, and vegetation destruction persist. Additionally, population expansion and the accelerated pace of urbanization have heightened the pressure exerted on the ecological environment, presenting a severe challenge for the attainment of high-quality sustainable development. Analyzing the spatial patterns of urbanization and ESS supply–demand relationships, exploring the CCD between them, and identifying the underlying driving mechanisms of natural and human relationships can further support the formulation of accurate regional ecological management policies. Both nature and anthropogenic factors directly or indirectly influence the ecosystem service supply–demand ratio (ESSDR)35, accurately identifying and quantifying these factors is crucial for improving Gansu Province’s overall ESS, promoting high-quality urban development, and optimizing resource allocation.

While existing studies have extensively examined the impact of urbanization on ESS supply or demand individually, significant research gaps remain. First, few studies have integratively analyzed the coupling coordination relationship between the comprehensive urbanization level (CUL) and the ESSDR at multiple spatial scales. Most prior research focuses on either supply or demand in isolation, or on a single spatial scale, limiting a holistic understanding of their synchronized dynamics. Second, the internal driving mechanisms, particularly how socio-economic and natural factors interactively influence this CCD, are not yet fully understood. Existing analyses often emphasize the independent effects of factors, overlooking the potentially enhanced or weakened effects arising from their interactions. Third, within the ecologically fragile and rapidly urbanizing context of Northwestern China, such as Gansu Province, a detailed investigation that links spatial patterns, coupling coordination evaluation, and driving force analysis is still lacking, which is crucial for formulating spatially-targeted ecological management policies. To address these gaps, this study aims to provide a assessment of the coupling coordination relationship between urbanization and ESS supply–demand in Gansu Province from 2002 to 2022, with a focus on elucidating its driving mechanisms. The specific contributions of this work are threefold: (1) We quantify the spatio-temporal dynamics of both ESS supply–demand and CUL at a relatively fine resolution, highlighting their mismatches. (2) We employ a CCD model to diagnose the coordination state between ESSDR and CUL across both grid and county scales, revealing multi-scale spatial heterogeneity. (3) We utilize the geographical detector not only to identify key individual driving factors but, more importantly, to uncover the interactive effects between natural and socio-economic factors on the CCD, offering deeper insights into the complex driving mechanisms.

Therefore, this study initially assesses the supply, demand, and ESSDR of four key ESS in Gansu Province from 2002 to 2022. Secondly, the CCD model is employed to examine the coupling interaction between the comprehensive urbanization level (CUL) and ESSDR. Finally, the geographic detector is applied to recognize and quantify the driving factors affecting this coupling relationship. The primary research objectives are as follows: (1) to analyze the spatial distribution characteristics of changes in ESS supply and demand; (2) to examine the CCD between ESSDR and CUL; (3) to analyze and quantify key factors influencing the CCD.

Data and methods

Overview of the study area

Gansu Province (32° 11′–42° 57′ N, 92° 53′–108° 46′ E) is located in northwestern China. Its unique geographical location and characteristics are a microcosm of the distinctive ecological fragility commonly found in northwestern China36 (Fig. 1). Gansu Province covers an area of approximately 425,800 km2, accounting for roughly 4.72% of China’s total land area. The province features a narrow, elongated terrain extending from southeast to northwest, with significant elevation variations and a general slope from southwest to northeast. Various landforms such as plateaus, mountains, river valleys, basins, and deserts are interlaced throughout the region. From south to north, the province can be roughly divided into the Longnan Mountains, the Longzhong Loess Plateau, the Gannan Plateau, the Hexi Corridor, the Qilian Mountains, and the region north of the Hexi Corridor. The region features complex and diverse climate conditions, as it lies at the junction of three major natural regions. The annual average temperature ranges between 0 and 15 °C, and the average precipitation reaches approximately 300 mm. As of the end of 2022, the total water resources of the province amounted to 23.10 billion cubic meters, with per capita water resources of 926.30 m3.

As of the end of 2022, the province administered 12 cities, 2 autonomous prefectures, and 86 counties (cities, districts) (see the Fig. S1 and Table S1 details). The permanent population was 24,924,200, of which 13,506,400 were urban residents, accounting for approximately 54.19%, and 11,417,800 were rural residents, accounting for approximately 45.81%. The GDP was 1120.16 billion yuan, with the primary, secondary, and tertiary industries contributing 151.53 billion yuan, 394.51 billion yuan, and 574.13 billion yuan, respectively. The industrial composition ratio stood at 13.53: 35.22: 51.25.

As a key area constituting the ecological security barrier in Northwest China, Gansu Province should shoulder important responsibilities such as conserving water sources, preventing desertification and protecting biodiversity. However, over 80% of the province has an arid or semi-arid climate, with sparse vegetation, severe soil erosion and a serious shortage of water resources, the per capita water resources are far below the national average. The ecosystem background in the study area is fragile, soil erosion is severe in the Loess Plateau of central Gansu Province, sandstorms are rampant in the northern part of the Hexi Corridor, the risk of grassland degradation in the Gannan Plateau is high. In addition, the changes in land use during the urbanization process, such as the encroachment of construction land on ecological space, directly weaken the supply capacity of key ESS such as soil conservation, wind prevention and sand fixation. The core areas of urbanization, such as Lanzhou City and the oasis belt of the Hexi Corridor, have a high concentration of population and economy. There is a strong demand for water production, carbon sinks and habitat quality, but the expansion of construction land has led to a reduction in ecological land. However, in areas with abundant ecological service supply, such as the Gannan Plateau and the hinterland of the Qilian Mountains, the urbanization level is low and the demand is not high, resulting in a spatial mismatch of "high demand-low supply" and "low demand-high supply".

Research methods

This study’s research framework primarily consists of four parts (Fig. 2): the first part involves the collection and integration of multi-source data to provide a solid data foundation for the calculation of ESS supply and demand and the quantification of CUL. The second part involves quantifying the supply and demand of ESS, analyzing their spatio-temporal distribution characteristics, and further calculating the ESSDR; the third part uses the CCD model to couple the ESSDR with CUL, deriving the coupling coordination relationships between the two at both the grid scale and county scale; the fourth part analyzes the factors influencing the CCD using a geographic detector.

Theoretical framework of this study.

Quantifying the supply and demand of ESS

The supply and demand of ESS result from interaction between natural ecosystems and human activities. Considering the actual ecological problems faced by Gansu Province and human needs during the urbanization process, this paper selects and quantifies four key ESS: carbon sequestration (CS), water yield (WY), soil conservation (SC), and habitat quality (HQ) (Fig. 3), the comprehensive ecosystem service (CESS) is obtained by normalizing and weighting the four types of services. First, rapid urbanization and economic development, as major sources of carbon emissions, represent core demands of human economic activity. Therefore, assessing ecosystems’ capacity to offset these emissions is crucial for understanding carbon balance and advancing low-carbon development. Second, water scarcity plagues most areas of Gansu Province. The urbanization process has increased water demand, thereby intensifying the conflict between supply and demand, which makes resolving this contradiction an urgent priority. Third, soil erosion is a prominent issue in the Loess Plateau region of central Gansu. This problem is further exacerbated by the expansion of construction land and other human activities, making its mitigation one of the foremost challenges for Gansu. Finally, HQ serves as a robust indicator of biodiversity and overall ecosystem integrity. However, HQ tends to degrade in densely populated urban areas and regions with intense human activity. Therefore, focusing on HQ is essential for achieving sustainable development in both urbanization and ecosystems.

ESS supply and demand calculation formula.

This study calculates their supply using the InVEST model. In order to assess the demand for these services, this study uses per capita carbon emissions and population density to quantify the demand for CS, estimates the demand for WY based on regional water consumption; assess the demand for SC by calculating soil erosion, and determines the extent to which HQ requires improvement and maintenance by comparing it to average habitat quality, thereby representing the demand for HQ. Four-quadrant model

The four-quadrant model reflects the coupling state between two indicators, with results that are intuitive and easy to analyze, and thus has been widely applied in studies on regional matching development45,46. This paper utilizes the four-quadrant model, based on the CCD between ESSDR and CUL, to classify counties and districts in Gansu Province into zones (Fig. 4). In this model, the X-axis represents development level, and the Y-axis represents coupling level, where the CCD trend represents development level, and the CCD average value represents coupling level. We classify the relationship between the two into four types: the first quadrant has both high development levels and high coupling levels. The second quadrant has high coupling levels but a declining trend in development levels. The third quadrant has low coupling levels and a declining trend. The fourth quadrant has low coupling levels but an increasing trend in development levels.

Results

Changes of ESS

Figure 5 shows the trend changes in the supply, demand, and ESSDR for four key ESS-CS, WY, SC, and HQ-as well as CESS in Gansu Province from 2002 to 2022. During this period, the average supply of CS ranged from 0.05 to 17.43 t/km2, with no significant trend changes, and 89.37% of the regions showed no significant changes. The average demand for CS ranged from 0 to 223,876 t/km2, showing a relatively increasing trend, primarily concentrated in the Loess Plateau region of central Gansu, which is also a densely populated area. Cities such as Lanzhou City, Tianshui City, Dingxi City, Qingyang City, and Linxia City are among the most populous regions in the province, indicating that economic development and population expansion have driven heightened demand for CS. There has been a 52.17% reduction in the overall ESSDR for CS, indicating that the supply–demand gap for CS is widening. 44.43% of the regions showed little change in ESSDR, primarily distributed in the desert regions of northwestern Gansu Province, where human impact on the region is minimal. Within the study area, WY supply ranged from 0 to 562.91 mm and demand varied between 0 and 209.17 mm, respectively. Both supply and demand of WY are decreasing, with reduction areas accounting for 37.43% and 65.75%, respectively. The ESSDR for WY has increased by 71.15%, indicating an alleviation of the conflict between water production and water demand. The supply and demand ranges for SC are 0–213.85 t/hm2 and 0–188.94 t/hm2, respectively. During this period, the overall changes in supply and demand are not significant, but the ESSDR still shows a decreasing trend, which is concentrated in regions with relatively fragile ecosystems, indicating that soil conservation in Gansu Province still faces a severe situation. HQ supply, demand, and ESSDR showed minimal changes, but in densely populated areas with strong human influence, HQ ESSDR continued to decline. CESS supply increased by 10.19%, demand decreased by 42.43%, and ESSDR rose by 67.35%, indicating that the supply–demand gap of CESS has been alleviated. However, the ESSDR sustained a downward trajectory in densely populated areas.

Analysis of CUL

Gansu Province exhibits spatial heterogeneity in CUL (Fig. 6), with CUL values ranging from 0 to 95%. Among these, Lanzhou City within the province exhibits a relatively high level of urbanization, the large scale and a relatively concentrated regional distribution. The province shows a significant trend of increasing CUL, with the most pronounced increases observed in Lanzhou New Area, Jiayuguan City, and Jinchuan District. Due to natural conditions and topographical constraints, the CUL pattern within the region exhibits dispersed and small-scale agglomeration characteristics. The Hexi Corridor, constrained by water resources, is primarily distributed in a cluster-like pattern along the Qilian Mountains’ northern foothills and oasis areas. The Loess Plateau in central Gansu and the mountainous regions of southern Gansu, constrained by topography, are mainly distributed in river valleys and intermontane basins, often forming strip-like patterns. The Gannan Plateau has relatively poor natural conditions, weak economic foundations, and a small population, resulting in low CUL. Additionally, ecological protection policies have slowed down the pace of urbanization to a certain extent. Overall, Gansu Province has exhibited an upward trajectory in urbanization levels with significant improvements in urbanization rates. Yet this growth has been uneven, as economic and geographical differences have resulted in obvious regional development imbalances.

The level and changing trend of CCD

Figure 7 shows the spatial distribution and temporal trend of CCD across Gansu Province over the past 20 years. The CCD level ranges from 0 to 0.82, with 41.28% of the regions showing an upward trend in coupling coordination levels, and the scale of coordinated regions continuing to expand. The results in Fig. 7a1, a2 indicate that there are differences in CCD levels, with Lanzhou City having a relatively high coupling coordination level and a large area. Figure 7b1 shows that out of the 87 counties in the province, 84 exhibit coordination imbalances, with 46 in severe imbalance and 38 in mild imbalance. Only three regions are relatively coordinated, all located in Lanzhou City: Xigu District is tenuously coordinated, while Chengguan District and Anning District are mildly coordinated. Figure 7b2 shows that the overall trend of the CCD between ESS supply–demand and CUL across all counties in the province is upward, with only four counties showing a downward trend: Dunhuang City, Jinta County, Minqin County, and Maqu County, with decreases of 2.00%, 0.31%, 2.88%, and 1.18%, respectively.

Driving forces of CCD

Factor detection

This paper employs factor detection from a geographic detector to identify the individual driving factors’ independent of CCD. Taking into account Gansu Province’s natural environment, development features, and data accessibility, thirteen driving factors were selected, covering four aspects: natural, social, economic and land cover. Among these, natural factors include annual average temperature (X1), elevation (X2), NDVI (X3), and annual precipitation (X4). Social factors include population density (X5), urbanization rate (X6), secondary industry share (X7), and tertiary industry share (X8). Economic factors include per capita GDP (X9), regional fiscal revenue (X10), savings deposits of urban and rural residents (X11) and total retail sales of consumer goods (X12). Finally, changes in land cover types will significantly affect the quality and level of regional ecosystem services, and to a certain extent, reflect the level of regional urbanization50, therefore, land cover (X13) is selected as the driving factor. Figure 9 shows the values of each driving factor for the years 2002, 2007, 2012, 2017, and 2022, all thirteen driving factors passed the significance test (p < 0.05). Overall, compared to natural factors, social factors, economic factors and land cover exhibit stronger explanatory power, indicating their greater influence. The most influential factors are X5, X11, X12 and X13 respectively. While the least influential ones are X1, X2, X4 and X9. Among natural factors, X3 has the greatest influence, among social factors, X5 has the greatest influence, and among economic factors, X11 has the greatest influence, the influence of land cover is basically significant every year.

Factor detection.

Interaction detection

The interaction detection results, as shown in Fig. 10, reveal that the combined effect of any two driving factors on the CCD is more potent than the individual independent effects of each separate factor. The interaction types are all bivariate-enhancement and nonlinear-enhancement. During the study period, the explanatory power of other factors interacting with X3, X5 and X13 was generally strong. Overall, natural factors exhibit a significant enhancing effect in interactions, with NDVI showing a marked increase in influence and importance under such interactions. This suggests that some factors which originally have low explanatory power are capable of producing unexpected facilitative effects when they act in synergy with other factors.

Discussion

Spatial characteristics of ESSDR

The balance between the supply and demand of ESS constitutes the core of the human–environment coupling system51. The spatial heterogeneity of ESSDR provides more critical information for government management decisions, which is helpful for the reasonable deployment of natural resources and the establishment of sustainable development models52. As shown in Fig. 11, regions with high urbanization levels exhibit excess demand, while regions with excellent ecological environment generally exhibit excess supply. ESSDR is generally lower in areas with high urbanization levels and dense population. This phenomenon has also been observed in other studies53,54. Population aggregation has long served as a catalyst for the growth of industry and economy, leading to widespread land cover changes55, Land cover type changes are the primary drivers influencing ESS. Among different land cover types, construction land has the lowest carbon sequestration capacity56. Compared to grassland and forest areas, construction land has lower infiltration, evapotranspiration, and water retention levels57, resulting in an ESS supply–demand gap. Additionally, ESS demand is closely linked to socioeconomic activities. Rapid economic expansion and population growth have increased consumption and demand for ESS58 further worsening the disequilibrium in supply and demand of ESS. In response to this situation, governments need to take measures to increase ESS supply, such as expanding urban green space, developing diversified water supply methods, and encouraging the development of clean energy. For regions with relatively fragile ecosystems, nature reserves and ecological red lines are needed to strengthen regulation. For regions with abundant resources and high ecological and environmental quality, it is important to strengthen the management of nature reserves and resource protection. However, in addition to increasing the supply of ESS, corresponding measures also need to be implemented to control the demand for ESS, such as defining urban expansion boundaries, controlling urban population growth, and strengthening management of industrial production and emissions.

Forests and grasslands serve as the fundamental barriers ensuring regional ecological security59. Gansu Province is located within China’s northwestern ecological security barrier and holds an important strategic position in maintaining the nation’s overall ecological security60. However, most areas within the region have poor ecological environment, low vegetation coverage, and insufficient capacities for soil and water conservation, with arid and semi-arid climates exacerbating these challenges. In recent years, local governments have implemented corresponding ecological engineering projects, resulting in a significant improvement in the vegetation coverage rate61 and a marked improvement in soil and water conservation. In turn, the disparity between the supply and demand of ESS has been progressively narrowing (11e3), and overall ecological environment quality is steadily improving.

CCD analysis at different scales

Ecosystem sustainability can be reflected through the level of the CCD62. Urbanization and the supply and demand of ESS also exhibit a complex bidirectional coupling relationship. Urbanization directly or indirectly impacts the supply capacity and demand intensity of ESS through population concentration, land use changes, and economic growth. Conversely, the state of ESS supply and demand can either accelerate or constrain the pace and quality of urbanization63. The CCD model serves as an effective method for quantifying the intensity of interactions between these two systems. The core of CCD lies in assessing the coordination between the CUL and the state of ESS supply and demand under urbanization pressures. In other words, it evaluates whether urbanization development aligns with the supporting capacity of ecosystems and whether both can achieve sustainable co-development. In this study, an analysis conducted on the CCD between urbanization and ESS supply–demand in Gansu Province revealed that in areas with larger population, higher levels of urbanization, and better ecological environment, the CCD level was higher and showed an upward trend, but it still exhibited strong spatiotemporal differentiation characteristics. Overall, the CCD is relatively high and large-scale in the Loess Plateau of central Gansu, while it is relatively low and the coordinated areas are small in scale in the Hexi Corridor and northern regions, Longnan Mountains, and the Gannan Plateau. The primary reasons for this phenomenon include the relatively superior natural environmental conditions and relatively better socio-economic development status within the Longzhong Loess Plateau. However, due to terrain constraints, it is unable to form a larger-scale, concentrated, and contiguous development trend. In the Hexi Corridor, the population is concentrated in oasis areas with relatively superior ecological environment, and most social and economic activities occur in these regions. Therefore, areas with higher CCD levels are primarily distributed in the Shiyang River Basin, Shule River Basin, and Hei River Basin. The Longnan Mountains have excellent ecological environment, but due to terrain constraints, the population and social and economic activities are mainly concentrated in relatively narrow mountainous basins and river valleys, resulting in smaller CCD scales. The Gannan Plateau has a low level of urbanization, resulting in a low CCD level and small scale.

At the county level, over the past two decades, most counties are located in the fourth quadrant, with CCD levels showing an upward trend, indicating that even though CCD levels are relatively low, relevant measures implemented by the government have played an effective role in advancing the coordinated development of urbanization and ESS. Only four counties have shown a downward trend, among which Dunhuang City has experienced rapid growth in population, GDP, and urban area. The development of tourism has accelerated the expansion of urban areas, increasing environmental burdens. Additionally, local residents have cleared grasslands and forests to cultivate water-intensive crops on a large scale, leading to highly fragile ecosystems64 and resulting in a declining CCD trend. The Chinese government introduced the Shiyang River Basin Comprehensive Management Plan in 200665, and the Minqin Oasis actively supported ecological governance initiatives by implementing a number of ecological restoration measures, vigorously promoting ecological governance projects. Therefore, the decoupling of urbanization and ESS in Minqin County may be reasonable. Maqu County has a low population density and lagging economic development66, with a significant gap between urbanization levels and local ESS. In the past 20 years, the decline trend in Jinta County has been only 0.31%, indicating that urbanization levels are roughly on par with ESS. However, the future decline trend may intensify, necessitating the implementation of more effective measures to improve ecological quality in the region, promote sensible resource use, and facilitate the sustainable development of urbanization and ESS.

Analysis of influencing factors of CCD

The factor analysis results indicate that, under the influence of individual factors, socio-economic factors and land cover exert a more significant and direct impact on CCD compared to natural factors, with population density, savings deposits of urban and rural residents, total retail sales of consumer goods and land cover ranking among the most influential factors. This likely results from recent population growth, economic growth, and rapid urbanization in Gansu Province, which has caused a rapid increase in demand for ESS67, directly affecting the level of CCD between urbanization and ESS. Additionally, factors like economic development, population increase, and policy adjustment may also change ecosystems, thereby affecting the supply capacity of ESS68.

As Fig. 10 demonstrates, under the interaction of various factors, the selected factors are able to complement each other and explain the explanatory power of CCD. The influence of natural factors has significantly increased, with NDVI emerging as a key factor affecting CCD. The spatial arrangement of NDVI has a direct impact on the spatial pattern of ESS, higher vegetation coverage is associated with better carbon sequestration, soil and water conservation capabilities, and habitat quality69. In Gansu Province, under arid and semi-arid climatic conditions, the influence of NDVI is further amplified.

Therefore, in the process of policy formulation and management, it is crucial to comprehensively take into account both the individual effects of each factor as well as their interactions. By comprehensively considering the relationships among natural factors, social-economic factors and land cover appropriate urban development models and environmental governance approaches can be selected to facilitate the harmonious coexistence of cities and ecosystems.

Cross-regional comparative analysis

To gain a deeper understanding of the uniqueness of the CCD pattern in Gansu Province, this study compared it with similar ecological climate zones at home and abroad. It was found that the overall level of CCD in Gansu Province is relatively low and shows an upward trend, but the upward speed is relatively slow. For instance, the economic belt on the Northern Slope of the Tianshan Mountains in Xinjiang, relying on relatively concentrated oasis resources and strong investment in energy development, has formed an urbanization-ecological coordinated development zone with a relatively high CCD70; Compared with the Central Asian region, although both are confronted with problems such as water shortage and challenges to ESS posed by the urbanization process, the CCD in the Central Asian region is more susceptible to climate change and political factors among countries71. Gansu Province has a long and fragmented terrain, and both its ecosystem and urbanization layout are fragmented. From the humid Longnan to the extremely arid Hexi Corridor, there are huge differences in the background of the ecosystem and the foundation of economic development. The water resource contradiction in the Hexi Corridor is acute. Soil erosion is severe in the Loess Plateau of central Gansu Province. The “water crisis” and the “soil crisis” coexist and are intricately intertwined. Secondly, Gansu Province is located deep inland and far from the country’s core economic circle, which restricts its economic development. Compared with Xinjiang, it is at a disadvantage in terms of obtaining government support, undertaking industrial transfer and external investment. Therefore, it leads to a relatively low overall CCD in Gansu Province and a slow upward trend. However, compared with Central Asia, although the CCD in Gansu Province has risen slowly, it is relatively stable overall and has fewer external interference factors. Under the high attention of the government, the growth rate of CCD in Gansu Province may increase in the future, and the overall level will be significantly improved.

Implications for promoting urbanization and improving ecological environment

Gansu Province spans arid, semi-arid, and humid regions, it holds a significant strategic position within the "two screens and three belts" ecological security framework and plays a crucial role in the ecological protection and high-quality development of the Yellow River Basin. Its ecological environment is characterized by diversity and fragility, this leads to different ecological or economic development issues being faced by different regions.

Lanzhou City is a typical area with a high urbanization rate but a low level of ESS provision. The construction land in the central urban area of Lanzhou is squeezing ecological space, measures such as strictly demarcating ecological protection red lines, reserving ecological buffer zones along both banks of the Yellow River, increasing urban green space coverage, and building pocket parks should be taken to enhance the supply capacity of ESS and achieve the synergy of “high coupling coordination” and “low ecological deficit”. Lanzhou New Area should promote the green iteration of industries, strictly control the implementation of high energy-consuming and high-emission projects, vigorously develop low-ecological consumption industries such as digital economy and cultural tourism, and reduce carbon emissions and excessive water consumption.

The regions with a low urbanization rate but high ESS provision mainly include the Hexi Corridor, the Gannan Plateau and the Longshan Mountainous Area. These areas have a good ecological foundation and a low level of urbanization. They are important components of the desert control belt and the upper plateau ecological barrier of the Yellow River Basin, providing crucial services such as water resource protection and biodiversity conservation. The Hexi Corridor region is mainly composed of oasis cities, and NDVI and water resources are the keys to coupling and coordination. A cross-regional water resource allocation mechanism should be established with the Shiyang River, Shule River and Heihe River basins as units, the planting scale of high-water-consuming crops should be restricted, and water-saving irrigation technologies should be promoted. In the core area of the oasis, promote the integration of the three living Spaces of “town-oasis-desert”, build ecological corridors relying on high-value areas of NDVI, and strictly limit the expansion of construction land to the edge of the oasis. Dunhuang City should strictly control the intensity of tourism development, demarcate ecological protection areas around the Crescent Spring, prohibit large-scale grassland development and excessive groundwater extraction, and curb the decline of CCD. The Gannan Plateau and Longnan Mountainous Area should avoid large-scale industrial development. Relying on the ecological advantages of grasslands and forest areas, they should develop characteristic industries such as eco-tourism and under-forest economy to achieve a virtuous cycle of “ecological protection-economic income increase-reasonable urbanization development”. The government can include this area in the key support scope of transfer payments for national key ecological function zones, make up for the local costs incurred for ecological protection, and enhance the enthusiasm of local governments and residents for ecological protection.

The Loess Plateau in central Gansu is a region characterized by ecological fragility and intense human-land interactions, serving as a vital component of the ecological barrier of the Loess Plateau. The problem of soil erosion in this area is extremely serious, it is necessary to immediately optimize the agricultural production structure, reduce the cultivation on sloping land, develop dryland farming and water-saving agriculture, control the use of chemical fertilizers and pesticides, and reduce the pressure of agricultural activities on ESS. Secondly, implement the comprehensive management project of the Loess Plateau and plant soil-fixing and carbon-fixing plants such as sea buckthorn and caragana. In addition, relying on regional central cities such as Dingxi and Qingyang, public service facilities should be improved to attract the orderly transfer of rural population from remote areas and alleviate the pressure of ecological fragmentation in rural areas. Finally, it is essential to facilitate the advancement of new-type urbanization, rationally shape the urban spatial pattern, and form a multi-centered and networked urban system. With Lanzhou City as the core and cities such as Tianshui City, Zhangye City, Jiuquan City, and Qingyang City as sub-centers, we need to foster a regional development pattern featuring distinct hierarchical structures and complementary functions, narrow the urban–rural gap, and elevate the level of coordinated regional development.

Limitations and future work

This paper quantifies the supply and demand of ESS, urbanization, and the CCD between them, and analyzes the driving factors influencing CCD. However, there are still some limitations. Firstly, this paper only quantifies the supply, support, and regulation functions, without incorporating cultural service functions. Gansu Province is well-known for its distinctive natural landscapes and abundant historical-cultural heritage. In the future, it could be worthwhile to explore incorporating cultural services into the analysis to provide a more comprehensive perspective on ESS. Secondly, in terms of the temporal scale, owing to data constraints, this study only analyzed the years 2002, 2007, 2012, 2017, and 2022, covering a relatively short time period, making it difficult to fully understand the development patterns and evolutionary trends between urbanization and ESS. In light of this, Future studies could attempt to collect longer-term data to extend the temporal scale, integrating the spatiotemporal dimensions and evaluating the long-term coupling relationship between ESS and CUL can help further reveal the spatial autocorrelation between them. Additionally, it is possible to consider integrating cutting-edge technologies such as machine learning and artificial intelligence, collecting more comprehensive influencing factors will help establish a more accurate and comprehensive ESS and CUL indicator system and evaluation model. Finally, in the future, combining big data and using R language, PLUS model and system dynamics model to predict CCD is also a very good research direction.

Conclusion

This paper primarily utilizes the InVEST model, CCD model, and geographic detector to analyze the coupling coordination level between CUL and ESSDR in Gansu Province during the period 2002–2022, and explores the driving forces influencing CCD. Our findings are outlined below:

The supply and demand of ESS exhibit a spatial mismatch. Except for water services, which are on the rise, the ESSDR of carbon services and soil services are keeps decreasing, the habitat quality only varies in areas where human activities are obvious. The overall ESSDR of CESS is on an upward trend, but it is still on a downward trend in highly urbanized and densely populated areas.

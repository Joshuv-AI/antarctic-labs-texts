Prognostic Value of a Novel Model for Hepatocellular Carcinoma Patients Undergoing Transarterial Chemoembolization
Authors: Cailing Ye, Yaqiong Chen, Jiahao Chen, Zhongcheng Chen, Wensi Chen, Suqing Zhao, Bo Hu, Zhaoxia Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundHepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) patients who underwent transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) have heterogeneous clinical outcomes. Accurate prognosis prediction and risk stratification are crucial for individualized treatment. We sought to develop a novel prognostic model for overall survival (OS) that incorporated contemporary clinical and laboratory factors for estimating individual prognosis.MethodsA total of 180 HCC patients treated with TACE were used to identify the risk factors and generate prognostic models by Cox regression analyses. Model performance was evaluated by comparing it with the Tumor‐Node‐Metastasis (TNM) and Barcelona‐Clinic Liver‐Cancer (BCLC) staging systems.ResultsA prognosis model (PI score), which consisted of neutrophil‐lymphocyte ratio (NLR), γ‐glutamyl transpeptidase (GGT), alpha‐fetoprotein (AFP), and TNM stage, was constructed. The PI scores of each patient were calculated, and the patients were divided into subgroups based on their PI scores. The OS rate of patients in the low‐risk group (PI < 0.87) was better than that of the patients in the high‐risk group (PI ≥ 0.87), p < 0.001. Patients were then further divided into four stages: early stage (PI ≤ 0.49), middle stage (0.49 < PI ≤ 0.87), advanced stage (0.87 < PI ≤ 1.48), and end stage (PI > 1.48). There were statistically significant differences between the OS rates of the four groups (p < 0.001). The area under the ROC curve (AUROC) for PI score (0.746, 0.643–0.783) was higher than those of TNM (0.699, 0.620–0.763) and BCLC (0.692, 0.617–0.760).ConclusionsThe PI score had excellent predictive value for HCC patients undergoing TACE and was superior to TNM and BCLC.

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is one of the most prevalent malignant tumors and the third leading cause of cancer‐related mortality worldwide [1]. There are various optional therapies for HCC patients. For patients with early‐stage liver cancer, surgical resection, liver transplantation, and ablation supply the best survival results and the only curative treatment [2]. Unfortunately, most HCC patients are diagnosed in the advanced stage and are not amenable to curative treatments [3]. Therefore, palliative loco‐regional therapies, such as transarterial chemoembolization (TACE), have been widely used to treat these patients. Previous studies showed that TACE could benefit some unresectable HCC patients [4, 5]. In recent years, more and more attention has been paid to the prognosis evaluation of patients undergoing TACE [6, 7, 8]. Some staging systems were developed to predict the prognosis of HCC patients receiving TACE [9]. Still, none of them were widely accepted, and their prediction ability was limited [10].

TNM and BCLC are conventional staging systems that could be used to strategize HCC patients [11]. The TNM staging system is the most common tumor staging and predictive system. The disadvantage of TNM is that the prognostic information provided by TNM is not complete enough, and there may be significant differences in clinical results between patients with the same histological stage [12]. The BCLC staging system is the most widely used staging system for HCC patients. Intermediate‐stage HCC patients with preserved liver function (BCLC B) are recommended as candidates for TACE [13]. However, in clinical practice, TACE is widely used in patients beyond the recommendation (early or even advanced HCC) [14]. The predictive value of the TNM and BCLC staging systems for HCC patients with TACE is uncertain. Thus, a more accurate and specialized staging system is needed to predict the prognosis of HCC patients with TACE.

In this study, we used the Cox proportional hazards regression model, which is the essential survival analysis model and is mainly used to predict outcomes for patients with malignant tumors or other chronic diseases [15], to identify independent prognostic factors from clinical and laboratory data, and to establish a new prognostic model for evaluating the prognosis of HCC patients after TACE.

Patients and Methods

Patients

The clinical and laboratory data of 180 HCC patients from the Third Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat‐sen University were collected between January 2014 and December 2016. The HCC patients were diagnosed based on the criteria used by the European Association for the Study of the Liver (EASL) [16]: characteristic radiologic findings of HCC shown in two imaging techniques or one imaging technique showing typical features and with an AFP level > 400 ng/mL, or positive findings in pathologic examinations.

Inclusive criteria were: (1) new HCC diagnosis; (2) HCC patients with TACE as the initial treatment without any other treatments; and (3) availability of complete clinicopathological and follow‐up data.

Exclusive criteria were as follows: (1) decompensation of cirrhosis; (2) with severe cardiovascular and renal diseases; (3) with serious infectious diseases; (4) intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma; (5) having a history of any malignancy other than HCC; and (6) insufficient available data.

This work was carried out by the research design approved by the Clinical Research Ethics Committee of the Third Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat‐sen University, Guangzhou, China ([2018]02‐259‐01).

Transarterial Chemoembolization Procedure

Based on the clinical characteristics and current research of liver cancer in China, the indications for TACE were defined as follows [17]: (a) Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status < 2; (b) Child‐Pugh class A or B liver function; and (c) Stage C according to the BCLC staging system. The contraindications of TACE were as follows: (a) ECOG > 2; (b) Child‐Pugh class C; (c) severe comorbidities that contraindicate interventional procedures such as dysfunction of other vital organs; and (d) prior liver transplantation.

The therapeutic protocol for TACE was standardized as follows: A 5‐French catheter (Cook, Bloomington, Indiana, USA) or a microcatheter (Progreat, Terumo, Tokyo, Japan) was used for selective hepatic artery catheterization. Following intubation, angiography of the hepatic and related vasculature was performed to assess tumor blood supply and determine the feasibility of embolization. A chemotherapeutic emulsion consisting of 2–20 mL of lipiodol (Lipiodol Ultrafluido, Guerbet, France) and 20–60 mg of doxorubicin hydrochloride (Pharmorubicin, Pfizer, Jiangsu, China) was slowly infused until vascular stasis was achieved. This was followed by embolization using absorbable gelatin sponge particles (Gelfoam, Hangzhou Alc Ltd., Zhejiang, China) to complete arterial occlusion.

Measurement of Blood Parameters

Aspartate transaminase (AST), γ‐glutamyl transpeptidase (GGT), total protein, and total bilirubin were detected by an automatic biochemistry analyzer (HITACHI 7600, Japan). Prothrombin time was tested by a hemagglutination analyzer (STAR Max, France). Absolute counts of platelets, neutrophils, and lymphocytes were measured by the hematology analyzer (Sysmex XN‐2000, Japan). AFP was tested using an electrical chemiluminescence immunoassay (Roche e601, Switzerland).

Data Collection and Follow‐Up

Baseline pretreatment data of the enrolled patients were retrospectively collected including age, gender, AST, GGT, total protein, total bilirubin, prothrombin time (PT), platelet‐to‐lymphocyte ratio (PLR), neutrophil‐to‐lymphocyte ratio (NLR), alpha‐fetoprotein (AFP), tumor size, TACE times, liver cirrhosis, vascular invasion, TNM stage, and BCLC stage, etc. All the data except TACE times were collected within 1 week before TACE. NLR was calculated by dividing the absolute neutrophil count by the absolute lymphocyte count, while the PLR was calculated by dividing the absolute platelet count by the absolute lymphocyte count.

TNM staging was based on the eighth edition of the International Union against Cancer (UICC) Classification [18], and BCLC staging was defined by the EASL guidelines [19]. Overall survival time was calculated from the date of TACE treatment to the date of death or last follow‐up date in January 2019.

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analysis and graphics were performed using SPSS version 20.0. Continuous variables were expressed as mean (standard deviation [9]) or median (interquartile range [IQR]), as appropriate. Categorical variables were expressed as frequency and percentages. We used a parameter test (t‐test) to test continuous variables of normal distribution and a nonparametric test (Mann–Whitney U test) for continuous variables without normal distribution. Fisher's exact test was performed to analyze the significance of categorical variables. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses screened the risk factors used for HCC patients after TACE. The factors demonstrated to be significant in the univariate analysis (p < 0.2) entered into the multivariate analysis. A new prognosis model (PI score) was obtained stepwise from multivariate Cox regression analysis; the regression coefficient (β) of each parameter was used to construct the PI formula. The cut‐off value of the PI score was determined by using the X‐tile software [20] to divide patients into different risk groups. The PI score's prediction value and discriminative ability were compared with the TNM and BCLC staging systems using Kaplan–Meier and the receiver operating characteristic curve (ROC). The area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) was calculated. Statistical differences between different AUROCs were investigated using the DeLong method. A value of p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Univariate and Multivariate Cox Regression Analysis for OS of HCC Patients Undergoing TACE

By the end of the follow‐up, 136 patients had died, and 44 patients were alive. The univariate Cox regression analysis showed that AFP, AST, GGT, total bilirubin, tumor size, vascular invasion, TACE times, NLR, TNM stage, and BCLC stage were risk factors associated with the prognosis of HCC patients undergoing TACE. Univariate parameters with statistical significance (p < 0.05) in the Cox regression analysis were selected for multivariable Cox regression analysis. The multivariate Cox regression analysis identified that NLR, GGT, AFP, and TNM stage were independent predictors of OS in HCC patients undergoing TACE (Table 2).

A Predictive Model for OS of HCC Patients Undergoing TACE

In multivariate Cox regression, the regression coefficients (β) are used to construct the hazard function formula, which is the core of the Cox proportional hazards model. We extracted regression coefficients from the output results in SPSS software, β NLR = 0.118, β GGT = 0.001, β AFP = 0.546, and β TMN = 0.621. Next, we substituted coefficients with variables into the Cox model formula to form the linear predictor, for categorical variables should be coded numerically. Finally, the hazard function formula—representing the novel prognostic model (PI score)—was derived as follows: PI = 0.118*NLR + 0.001*GGT + 0.546*AFP (< 400 ng/mL = 0, ≥ 400 ng/mL = 1) + 0.621*TNM (TNM stage I or II = 0, TNM stage III or IV = 1) (χ 2 = 43.837, p < 0.001).

The median survival time of the enrolled patients was 7.0 months. The 1, 2, and 3‐year survival rates were 40.56%, 30.56%, and 25.00%, respectively (Table 3, Figure 1A). The patients were divided into low‐risk and high‐risk groups by PI score using X‐tile software. The cut‐off value of the PI score was 0.87, and the OS of the two groups was significantly different (p < 0.001) (Figure 1B). The high‐risk group was significantly associated with a shorter median OS than the low‐risk group (Table 3).

Comparison of OS Among Four Subgroups of PI, TNM, and BCLC

We used X‐tile software to divide the patients into four stages to evaluate the discriminative ability of the PI score in the prognosis for patients with HCC undergoing TACE. The four stages were stratified as follows: early stage (PI ≤ 0.49), middle stage (0.49 < PI ≤ 0.87), advanced stage (0.87 < PI ≤ 1.48), and end stage (PI > 1.48). The Kaplan–Meier analysis showed that a higher stage of the PI score was associated with a shorter median OS (Table 4). There were statistical differences in OS between any two stages of the PI score (p < 0.001 for all) (Figure 2A). However, there were no statistical differences in OS between TNM I and TNM II (p = 0.110), TNM III and TNM IV (p = 0.188) (Figure 2B). As for the BCLC staging system, there were no statistical differences in OS between any two stages except BCLC A and BCLC B (p < 0.001), BCLC A and BCLC C (p < 0.001), BCLC A and BCLC D (p = 0.005) (Figure 2C). Discussion

TACE is an optimal treatment for patients with compensated liver function with multifocal or large hepatocellular carcinoma who are not amenable to resection [1]. TACE can block angiogenesis and cause tumor necrosis, effectively delaying the progression of the cancer and improving outcomes for HCC patients [21].

TNM and BCLC are conventional staging systems that could be used to strategize HCC patients [11]. Due to the heterogeneity of patients, the prediction effect is not ideal. On the other hand, these systems have not been well developed, and their predictive value for HCC patients after TACE needs further validation. Therefore, a novel, more accurate, and professional prognosis model is required to predict the prognosis of HCC patients treated with TACE.

We identified the independent prognosis factors in the present study and developed a prognosis model (PI score) for HCC patients undergoing TACE using Cox proportional hazards regression analysis. The PI score model consisted of TNM stages, AFP, NLR, and GGT. The TNM staging system comprehensively considered tumor size, tumor number, vascular invasion, adjacent organ invasion, lymph node metastasis, and distant metastasis. It has been validated to predict outcomes for HCC patients treated with resection or transplantation. The TNM staging system was one of the most widely used pathological staging systems for HCC [22]. In previous studies, AFP, commonly applied in clinical practice for HCC screening and surveillance as an essential tumor marker, was an independent prognosis factor for HCC patients after TACE [23, 24]. In this study, the distribution of AFP is highly skewed, with some patients exhibiting very low levels (< 10 ng/mL), while others present with markedly elevated values (> 1000 ng/mL). Given this bimodal pattern, treating AFP as a continuous variable would yield limited clinical interpretability. Therefore, we opted for a dichotomous classification of AFP for analytical purposes. We selected a cutoff value of ≥ 400 ng/mL based on its high specificity for HCC, in line with established clinical guidelines and prior studies. AFP ≥ 400 ng/mL has been widely adopted in practice (e.g., AASLD, EASL guidelines) as a criterion for HCC diagnosis in high‐risk populations [2, 19, 25], especially in Asia [26, 27, 28]. A cutoff of 400 ng/mL can achieve nearly 100% specificity and positive predictive value (PPV) for HCC diagnosis [29]. Moreover, this threshold is incorporated into liver transplant selection criteria, such as the Hangzhou models [30], where elevated AFP levels (> 400 ng/mL) serve as markers of aggressive tumor biology and recurrence risk. NLR, including neutrophil and lymphocyte count, reflects the body's inflammatory system [31, 32]. An inflammatory reaction provides a suitable microenvironment for the tumor's occurrence, development, and metastasis [33, 34]. HCC patients with elevated NLR had poorer outcomes than those with normal NLR [35]. The research of McNally and Chon demonstrated that NLR was also an independent prognosis factor for TACE‐treated HCC patients [36, 37]. Besides, elevated GGT was significantly associated with poor prognosis in HCC patients after TACE [38].

To evaluate the prognostic value of the PI score, patients were divided into a low‐risk group (PI ≤ 0.87) and a high‐risk group (PI > 0.87) with significantly different OS (p < 0.001). The survival curve of these two groups showed an excellent stratification effect (Figure 1B). Besides, the TNM and BCLC staging systems also could stratify patients into an early–middle group (TNM stage I, II or BCLC stage A, B) and an advanced group (TNM stage III, IV or BCLC stage C, D) with significantly different OS (TNM: p < 0.001; BCLC: p = 0.002). The survival curves of TNM and BCLC are shown in Figure 1D. Though the results indicated that all of them could serve as a prognostic model for TACE‐treated HCC patients when dividing patients into two risk groups, the survival curves showed that the discriminative ability of PI scores was better than those of the TNM and BCLC staging systems. Moreover, the median OS of a low‐risk group with a PI score was longer than that of the early–middle groups of the TNM and BCLC staging systems. The result suggested that the prognostic value of the PI score for HCC patients undergoing TACE was better than those of the TNM and BCLC staging systems.

Patients were divided into four risk groups to evaluate further the discriminative ability of the PI score, TNM, and BCLC staging system. The Kaplan–Meier analysis showed no significant differences in OS between the two adjacent TNM or BCLC staging systems groups. The discriminative ability of these two staging systems was not good (Figure 2). As for the PI score, the Kaplan–Meier analysis demonstrated statistical differences in OS between any two risk groups, and the survival curves showed good stratification. Moreover, the 3‐year AUROC PI score was higher than those of TNM and BCLC staging systems. These results indicated that the predictive value of the PI score was superior to that of the TNM and BCLC staging systems. According to the results of this study, patients in the early–middle stage PI score who could not receive radical treatments for various reasons might be candidates for TACE because they had good outcomes after TACE. Patients in the advanced stage should receive combination treatments instead of TACE alone. However, for patients in the end stage with a 3‐year OS rate of 7.7%, the result indicated that they might not be the appropriate candidates for TACE. Symptomatic and supportive treatments were alternative treatments for these patients.

The PI score enhances traditional TNM and BCLC staging by incorporating hematological parameters (NLR, GGT, and AFP), thus offering a more comprehensive and multidimensional framework for predicting survival outcomes in patients with hepatocellular carcinoma undergoing TACE therapy. The PI score can reflect not only the status and the local manifestation of the tumor but also the status of the whole body including liver injuries and inflammation status and immune function of the body. Compared with the PI score, BCLC could not reflect the inflammation status of the body. In recent years, more and more studies have demonstrated that inflammation status and body immune function are closely related to the prognosis of tumor patients [39, 40]. In summary, the PI score is better than the TNM and BCLC staging systems in predicting outcomes for HCC patients undergoing TACE.

This study has some limitations. First, patients come from a single center, and the sample size is small. Second, it needs to be confirmed whether this model can be applied in different geographical areas. Thus, internal and external validations in a larger population, internal validation, or incorporation of an external validation cohort are required for the future; further prospective, multicenter, gender‐balanced, and more diverse population studies are necessary to confirm the model's generalizability.

If prospectively validated, the PI score could help optimize stage‐specific treatment strategies—for instance, identifying early‐stage patients who may benefit from curative ablation, versus high‐risk subgroups who may require systemic adjuvant therapy or evaluation for liver transplantation. The four‐tier PI stratification system may also inform systemic therapy choices, such as prioritizing immunotherapy or targeted agents for advanced‐stage patients with high PI scores, while sparing low‐risk patients from unnecessary treatment‐related toxicity.

Moreover, PI‐guided surveillance intervals could improve early detection of recurrence and allow for more efficient allocation of healthcare resources—intensifying imaging frequency for high‐risk individuals, while safely reducing monitoring in low‐risk groups.

Key challenges include integrating the PI score into existing clinical workflows, familiarizing clinicians with its interpretation, and reconciling discrepancies with established guidelines. Future research should evaluate whether PI‐guided treatment adjustments translate into improved patient outcomes and determine pathways for implementation across diverse healthcare systems.

In conclusion, we develop a novel and simple prognosis model (PI score) for HCC patients undergoing TACE. The PI score's prognostic value and discriminative ability have proved superior to that of the TNM and BCLC staging systems. It may be helpful for clinicians to select candidates for TACE and predict outcomes for HCC patients undergoing TACE.

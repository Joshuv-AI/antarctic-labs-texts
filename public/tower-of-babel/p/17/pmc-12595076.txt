Queuing-theory-driven renewable energy investment decisions through artificial expert choices with dynamic hybrid multi-facet fuzzy modeling
Authors: Gang Kou, Serhat Yüksel, Hasan Dinçer, Merve Acar, Serkan Eti
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryThis study examines queuing dynamics in peer-to-peer energy trading systems by integrating Q-learning assessment with hybrid molecular fuzzy modeling. Using large expert datasets, the framework evaluates decision-making strategies that influence energy allocation, efficiency, and market stability. The approach captures the interplay between adaptive learning and uncertainty, offering insights into how decentralized participants manage trading behavior under varying demand and supply conditions. Results demonstrate that the proposed modeling system effectively identifies patterns of cooperation, competition, and equilibrium formation in blockchain-enabled trading environments. By framing queuing as both a technical and behavioral process, the analysis connects individual choices with system-level performance. Beyond methodological contributions, the findings underscore the relevance of intelligent computational tools for addressing challenges in sustainable energy distribution. This work highlights the broader significance of combining artificial intelligence, fuzzy systems, and decentralized market mechanisms to advance resilient and efficient resource management.Subject areas: Network modeling, Energy modeling

Introduction

Renewable energy projects provide many important benefits in terms of environment, economy, and social aspects. Reducing dependency on fossil fuels is one of the biggest advantages in this process. This reduces greenhouse gas emissions and contributes to the fight against climate change. Moreover, reducing environmental pollution is another advantage of these projects. This situation negatively affects public health. On the other hand, since renewable energy sources are domestic, they increase energy supply security. Thanks to these investments, countries’ dependence on foreign energy is reduced.1 Although renewable energy projects offer many advantages, they also face various difficulties. One of the most important problems in these projects is the high initial investment costs. Furthermore, some renewable energy production can be affected by climatic changes. As a result, this situation can cause some interruptions in energy production. This situation increases the need for energy storage. This leads to an increase in the costs of the projects. However, inadequate legal regulations can also be considered as a significant obstacle for the development of these projects.2 Legislative deficiencies, length of permit processes, and bureaucratic obstacles can deter investors. Moreover, renewable energy projects may not always be accepted by the local population. Another important challenge is encountering social resistance in this process.

The correct selection of renewable energy types is extremely important for the performance of investments. The effectiveness of these projects can be affected by factors such as geographical conditions, climate structure, and consumption profile. Renewable energy sources provide different levels of efficiency depending on certain geographical features. Annual sunshine duration is important for solar energy investments. Similarly, wind energy investments require regions with continuous and strong winds.3 Factors such as stream flow, precipitation regime, and topographic slope are important for hydroelectric power plants. In addition, rural areas with high agricultural and animal waste should be preferred for biomass energy. Therefore, regional analyses should be conducted before each investment, and geographical suitability should be evaluated as one of the top priority criteria. Consumption profile is an extremely effective factor in the correct selection of renewable energy types, because each renewable energy type has different characteristics in terms of energy production timing, continuity, and amount. The adequacy of the technological infrastructure and grid connection possibilities should also be taken into consideration in this process. On the other hand, economic factors are also of great importance in the selection of the right energy types. Furthermore, the energy supply-demand balance and regional needs should also be considered in this process. In industrial areas, the need for uninterrupted power may require different types. In rural areas, however, small-scale, stand-alone systems may be more suitable.

It is necessary to determine the most important factors that are effective in the correct selection of renewable energy types. There is a limited budget, time, and resources in these projects. Therefore, instead of giving equal weight to each factor, the most critical variables that affect the decision process should be prioritized. This increases the success of the investment and allows for more efficient use of resources. Determining the most effective factors ensures that the appropriate energy type is selected, the risks of the investment are reduced, and the performance is increased. If the most critical factors for correct selection of renewable energy types are not determined, a very important deficiency may occur in the investment process. This may result in serious performance losses at technical, economic, environmental, and social levels. Choosing energy types that are incompatible with geographical and climatic conditions can result in insufficient energy production. Therefore, a comprehensive analysis should be carried out before each renewable energy investment, and the most decisive criteria for that project should be determined. Studies in the literature that systematically determine the most critical factors in the correct selection of renewable energy types are limited. This situation can be considered as a significant research gap. Many studies list many criteria related to renewable energy investments. However, it is often not distinguished in which context these criteria are more critical. Studies targeting this gap provide both theoretical contributions and practical guidance for policy makers and investors.

To satisfy this missing gap in the literature, this study aims to find effective renewable energy investment strategies. In this context, Queuing-theory-driven parameters are taken into consideration. A novel decision-making model is constructed to achieve this objective. The first stage is related to the expert weighting by considering artificial intelligence methodology. After that, logarithmic least squares weighting technique is taken into consideration to evaluate the parameters. Orthogonal metric robust aggregation methodology is considered to rank renewable energy types. Moreover, dynamic multi-facet fuzzy sets are developed in this study. There is no consensus in the literature on the criteria for determining which type is more suitable under which conditions in renewable energy investments. Existing studies generally list the criteria but do not analyze the importance level of these criteria contextually. This situation causes decision-makers to lack strategic guidance in renewable energy projects. The motivation for this study stems from a multidimensional need to both eliminate theoretical deficiencies in the literature and improve practical decision processes. Hence, this study tries to find the answers for the following research questions: (1) What are the most critical factors affecting the selection of the right energy type in renewable energy investments and how should these factors be prioritized? (2) Can a more effective and objective energy type selection model be created using artificial-intelligence-supported expert weighting and multi-stage decision-making methods? (3) How do dynamic multi-facet fuzzy sets contribute to reducing uncertainty and increasing flexibility in decision models? (4) To what extent can the integrated decision-making model developed within the scope of the study be adapted to different geographical, economic, or social contexts and provide generalizable results?

This study provides an original contribution to the literature both theoretically and methodologically by systematically determining the most critical factors affecting the selection of the right energy type in renewable energy investments with an artificial-intelligence-supported multi-criteria decision-making model. The proposed model has some significant superiorities as detailed below: (1) in renewable energy investments, determining a Queuing-theory-based criteria list and integrating it into the proposed decision-making model provides significant advantages both at methodological and theoretical levels and is an original contribution to the literature. Queuing theory considers concepts such as service time, waiting time, resource density, and system capacity. This situation allows investments to be analyzed more systematically and realistically. On the other hand, time-related performance criteria can be evaluated more accurately using the queuing theory approach. Due to this condition, it is possible to obtain more reliable analysis results. Capacity limits, sudden demand increases, or infrastructural limitations such as energy storage needs can be predicted with queuing models. Decision models that take queuing dynamics into account in real-world problems offer more applicable and flexible solutions for investors and policy makers. In summary, this approach provides innovative and practical contributions to the literature. (2) Considering artificial intelligence models while calculating the importance weights of different experts increases the accuracy and adaptability of the decision model. This situation also provides important contributions to the literature both at the methodological and applied level. In classical models, expert weights are mostly considered equal. Artificial intelligence determines the weights according to the past experiences, consistency level, area of expertise, and decision quality of the experts. This situation contributes significantly to reducing the subjectivity in the analysis process. In addition, expert weights determined by artificial intelligence techniques can be updated as new data arrive. In this way, the model does not remain static, it can develop as a self-learning system. The contribution of each expert may not be equally qualified. Artificial intelligence performs fair and balanced weighting by considering parameters such as the level of knowledge between experts, decision stability, and contribution density. (3) Ranking renewable energy alternatives using the orthogonal metric robust aggregation method increases the reliability and sensitivity of the decision model. This technique, unlike classical average or weighted aggregation approaches, is less affected by extreme values and statistical deviations in the dataset. This feature provides high reliability, especially in renewable energy data based on expert opinions or uncertain parameters. Thanks to the orthogonal metric, the differences between the alternatives can be analyzed based on their geometric positions in the multi-criteria space. This reflects the interaction between the criteria more strongly, making the ranking more accurate. Calculation errors are more common in classical addition and multiplication-based methods. The orthogonal metric robust aggregation method reduces these errors with the geometric approach and increases the stability and consistency of the results. (4) The concept of multi-facet fuzzy sets is defined as a new type of fuzzy sets that offer the opportunity to make decisions dynamically. Membership degrees are redefined with different parameter sets. In this process, each scenario or fuzzy assessment is created differently. Owing to this condition, it is possible to provide dynamism. Moreover, dynamic parameter sets are determined with various statistical calculations. Hence, synthetic assessments can be made with scenario-based and dynamic-measurement-based multi-facet fuzzy sets. With the help of this situation, more precise decisions can be provided in complex decision-making processes. To capture the evolving and multidimensional nature of the decision-making context under uncertainty, dynamic, multifaceted fuzzy sets are introduced. Traditional fuzzy set models often fall short in reflecting the temporal dynamics and interdependent dimensions inherent in real-world systems. The proposed dynamic, multifaceted fuzzy sets framework addresses this gap by enabling the simultaneous representation of multiple fuzzy dimensions within a unified structure. This added complexity is not arbitrary but necessary for enhancing the model’s expressiveness and adaptability.

Literature review

Utilization factor is considered as one of the important criteria for increasing the efficiency of queuing-theory-driven renewable energy investment decisions. Queuing theory is defined as the theory that mathematically models the interaction between customers and service providers in service systems.4 Queuing theory contributes to operational processes such as planning renewable energy investments, resource management, and grid integration.5 It is suggested that energy storage systems be installed at charging stations. In this way, the capacity of charging stations will be optimized, and queuing time will be reduced.6 The energy to be provided in this way will contribute to reducing both carbon emissions and charging station grid loads.7 Moreover, it is also emphasized that the location and capacity of energy storage systems contribute to optimizing the actions of the logistics fleet.8

Idle probability is one of the important criteria for increasing the efficiency of queuing-theory-driven renewable energy investment decisions. Idle probability stands out as an indicator of how efficiently investments are used. Idle probability in renewable energy investments is important in determining the efficiency of resource utilization or the risk of excessive investments. It is not a rational decision to make new investments in enterprises with a high probability of idling.9 In addition, these data are used for strategic decision-making in location selection and small-scale system installation. It is stated that an ideal setup will greatly improve system performance and energy efficiency.10 A two-stage stochastic programming system is developed to minimize the idle probability. This system provides system optimization by considering the limited budget.11 A dynamic reconfiguration optimization (DRO) system is proposed to test the economic feasibility and safety of the energy storage system. This model is designed to monitor the resilience and failure management of energy storage systems.12

Another criterion for increasing the efficiency of renewable energy investments in the context of queuing theory is the queue length. The concept of queue length is used in renewable energy systems to measure the extent to which the energy demand exceeds the system capacity or as a criterion to measure the adequacy of the existing service infrastructure.13 Microgeneration-based renewable energy systems are of great importance in the deployment of advanced transportation networks.14 A system is proposed that can provide the energy needed by electric vehicles arriving at electric vehicle charging stations on the side of the highway. In this way, the charging load at the station will be monitored, and the necessary energy balancing system for the station will be developed.15 Short-term energy forecasting is developed using machine-learning-based forecasting methods. In the forecasts made using the electricity consumption in the world, it is emphasized that investments in energy balancing systems should be increased to ensure energy optimization.16

Waiting time is one of the most critical performance indicators of queuing theory. Waiting time is a direct indicator of service quality, efficiency, and the need for investment. Waiting time also provides important information to investors about the timing and prioritization of investment.17 A method is created to reduce the waiting time at electric vehicle charging stations. It is emphasized that the model built with the help of machine learning is more effective than the model built with traditional queuing theory.18 A hybrid fast charging station system powered by solar and wind energy is also recommended to maximize station efficiency. It is stated that hybrid energy increases the efficiency of the system based on load forecast data at electric vehicle charging stations.19 Additionally, operational and technological improvements should be made to reduce the waiting time during mass charging at electric vehicle charging stations.20

Within the scope of the constraints of the study, the relevant literature was reviewed, and some findings were reached. Queuing theory is important for monitoring the efficiency of renewable energy investments and the return on investments. Similarly, it is effective in monitoring the capacity of existing investments, service quality, and customer satisfaction, as well as making the right investment decisions for future investments. This study aims to determine the most effective strategy for renewable energy investment decisions in the context of queuing theory. In this context, a priority analysis will be performed among the selected criteria. There is a limited number of studies on prioritization of these criteria in the literature. Therefore, this study is presented to fill this gap.

Methodology

In the evaluation of renewable energy investment decisions, a fuzzy decision system based on queuing theory is proposed to be applied. The system proposed in this manuscript ranks renewable energy investment alternatives with the help of the orthogonal metric robust aggregation model while weighting the queuing theory parameters with logarithmic least squares weighting. In this process, decision-making frameworks for smart systems (DMFFSs) is used to handle the uncertainty of linguistic expressions. Discussion

The most effective criterion for optimizing the efficiency of renewable energy investments in the context of queueing theory is the queue length. A high queue length can be expressed as an indicator that the system is insufficient to meet the demand. It also provides investors with information about where and to what extent new system installations will be made. Similarly, if the excessive queue length is caused by an imbalance in demand, it will be a reference for the necessary studies to postpone or redirect the demand to another region.25 It also evaluates the charging demand and capacity of an electric vehicle charging station by considering queuing theory. The study emphasizes that investments to manage charging demand should be prioritized.26 It is emphasized that to increase customer satisfaction and shorten the charging waiting time at electric vehicle charging stations, the charging station should be arranged to generate energy in a way that reduces environmental impacts.

Another important criterion for increasing the efficiency of renewable energy investments in the context of queuing theory is the idle probability. Idle time is considered as one of the important criteria to show how efficiently investments are used.27 Investing in regions with high idle time is considered as an irrational behavior for businesses. For this reason, the regions to be invested in should be analyzed in terms of location and suitability for investment, and energy systems should be scaled according to location. A system is proposed that aims to encourage energy savings by using the Internet of Things to optimize renewable energy investments. In the study, it is emphasized that a system should be developed in which the energy distribution system to be installed will detect the energy demand and provide the required amount of energy.28 Finally, it is also identified that the communication between energy storages in remote areas should be strong. In the study, it was emphasized that the size, capacity, and usage status of all batteries in the energy system should be monitored. It is stated that this energy can be stored and used more efficiently.29

Solar and wind energy are emerging as the most optimal renewable energy alternatives today in line with sustainable development goals. These two energy types are environmental friendly because they rely directly on resources derived from nature. Similarly, these investments offer high economic efficiency in the long term. Their negative environmental impact and limited reserves are among the biggest disadvantages of fossil fuels.30 Considering this, the importance of energy generation based on inexhaustible resources like solar and wind becomes even more evident. It is also defined that solar energy offers systems with low operating costs after installation and has a wide range of applications, from individual users to industrial users.31 It is also mentioned that these two investments play a key role in combating climate change, thanks to their potential to significantly reduce carbon emissions. They also contribute to countries’ energy independence by reducing dependence on energy imports. The lower environmental impact and greater installation flexibility of solar and wind energy compared to other renewable energy types such as biomass, geothermal, or hydroelectricity make these systems more attractive.32

This study tries to define the most critical factors affecting the selection of the right energy type in renewable energy investments. An artificial-intelligence-supported multi-criteria decision-making model is created to achieve this objective. This model integrates artificial-intelligence-based expert evaluation, logarithmic least squares weighting, and orthogonal metric robust aggregation techniques. Furthermore, dynamic multi-facet fuzzy sets are introduced in this study, and queuing-theory-driven parameters are taken into consideration. It is concluded that system responsiveness and queue length are the most significant queuing theory parameters for the performance improvements of the renewable energy investments. The contribution of this study is the systematic determination of the most critical factors affecting the selection of the right energy type in renewable energy investments using an artificial-intelligence-supported multi-criteria decision-making model. In addition, queuing theory considers concepts such as service time, waiting time, resource density, and system capacity, considering artificial intelligence identifies the weights according to the past experiences of the experts. Owing to this situation, the subjectivity in the analysis process can be reduced.

Limitations of the study

The most critical limitation of this study is that the analysis processes depend on expert opinions. This situation causes the subjectivity in the analysis process to increase. In addition to expert opinions, real-time energy production data can be taken into consideration in future studies. Moreover, a regional analysis is not performed in the model for different climatic conditions. In future studies, it is important to test the validity and reliability of the developed model by applying it to different countries or regions. This situation will significantly increase the generalizability of the model. On the other hand, the criteria set used in the study is not obtained directly from field data. Instead, this list is created by examining the studies in the literature comprehensively. As a future research direction, investors and public authorities should be included in the decision-making processes. Owing to this situation, analysis can be performed with more reliable variables. Another limitation is that the model is designed for a certain number of criteria and alternatives. When the number of criteria increases too much or the alternatives increase, the calculation time of the model increases, and the analysis becomes complex. To overcome this limitation and increase the computational efficiency of the model, different artificial intelligence applications can be integrated into the model. For example, with the zero-shot learning technique, it is possible to reduce many criteria according to their importance.

Method details

In the evaluation of renewable energy investment decisions, a fuzzy decision system based on queuing theory is proposed to be applied. The system proposed in this manuscript ranks renewable energy investment alternatives with the help of the orthogonal metric robust aggregation model while weighting the queuing theory parameters with logarithmic least-squares weighting. In this process, DMFFSs is used to handle the uncertainty of linguistic expressions.

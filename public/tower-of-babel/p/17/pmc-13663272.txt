Association between intraoperative labral tear size and short-term patient-reported outcomes following concomitant hip arthroscopy and periacetabular osteotomy
Authors: Holden Archer, Aaron Shi, Yin Xi, Yasin Dhaher, Edward P. Mulligan, Nicholas Fey, Avneesh Chhabra, Joel E. Wells
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHip dysplasia (HD) is a known risk factor for early morbidity in the young adult hip. HD causes a variety of pathologic changes to the hip through an asymmetrical distribution of forces. Periacetabular osteotomy (PAO) is becoming increasingly recognized as a treatment. Labral tears occur frequently in the setting of HD and there still remain questions regarding the best treatment practices for labral pathology. We sought to determine whether intraoperative labral tear size was associated with short-term patient-reported outcomes following concomitant hip arthroscopy and PAO.MethodsSeventy-one hips that underwent PAO with concomitant hip arthroscopy at our tertiary care hip center from February 2017 to June 2021 who had PROM follow-up time of more than one year were investigated (88.7% women and 11.3% men with mean patient age of 29.5 years). Labral tear sizes were recorded intraoperatively and sorted into three groups: < 5 mm, 5 – 10 mm, and > 10 mm. HOOS scores were collected pre- and postoperatively and were compared across the three labral tear size groups. The association between labral tear size and postoperative HOOS was evaluated while accounting for preoperative HOOS, age, sex, and BMI. The mean follow-up time was 1.57 years ± 250 days. ResultsAcross all patients, the mean preoperative HOOS was 66.9 ± 14.5, and the mean postoperative HOOS was 77.8 ± 16.3. Statistically significant improvement was observed in the <5 mm and 5-10 mm groups (p<0.001) but not in the >10 mm group (p=0.310). Mean postoperative HOOS values in the <5 mm, 5-10mm, and >10 mm labral tear groups were 84.3 ± 13.7, 76.9 ± 16.6, and 69.2 ± 16.1, respectively. Larger labral tear size was associated with worse postoperative HOOS (p=0.004).ConclusionAmong patients undergoing concomitant hip arthroscopy and PAO, larger intraoperative labral tear size was associated with worse short-term postoperative HOOS. However, larger labral tears were also associated with older age and worse markers of intra-articular disease, suggesting that tear size may reflect more advanced disease rather than independently determine outcome.

Introduction

Hip dysplasia (HD) occurs when the acetabulum provides inadequate coverage of the femoral head. HD prevalence is between 5.4% and 12.8% based on the radiographic index applied [1]. Due to the differences in pelvic morphology and hormonal factors, women are more likely to develop HD than men [2–4]. Due to altered pathophysiologic changes of the hip, HD contributes to premature osteoarthritis due to damage to articular cartilage and labrum [5, 6]. The labrum may initially hypertrophy to compensate but often later degenerates and tears [7, 8].

Periacetabular osteotomy (PAO) is a common procedure to treat HD that involves correction of acetabular realignment [9, 10]. The success of periacetabular osteotomy is typically limited by age, preoperative joint congruency, and radiographic evidence of osteoarthritis [11, 12]. PAO has been shown to effectively decrease pain and increase longevity of the hip [12–16] by decreasing the shear forces and load at the acetabular rim due to improved acetabular position [17].

Because labral tears are common in PAO candidates, it would be useful to know how the association between intraoperative labral tear size and postoperative outcome [18, 19]. Studies have evaluated the advantages and disadvantages of repairing labral pathology concurrently with PAO: Hagio et al. found equivalent short-term pain relief and functional PAO outcomes regardless of whether labral tears were present, Cvetanovich et al. reported no significant difference in functional outcome measures following arthroscopy for recurrent pain following PAO, and Ricciardi et al. found that combined arthroscopic labral refixation and PAO was safe and may benefit select patients [7, 20, 21]. Yet, there still remain questions regarding best treatment practices for labral pathology. The purpose of this study was to determine whether intraoperative labral tear size was associated with short-term patient-reported outcomes following concomitant hip arthroscopy and PAO. We hypothesized that larger intraoperative labral tear size would be associated with worse short-term postoperative HOOS.

Materials and methods

This study is institutional review board (IRB) approved. All Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) of 1996 regulations were followed. Informed consent was obtained from all of the participants in the study.

Patients

Our prospectively collected hip preservation database was queried for all patients who underwent PAO with concomitant hip arthroscopy at our tertiary care hip preservation center from February 2017 to June 2021. The diagnosis of hip dysplasia was determined by the treating hip preservation surgeon based on clinical evaluation and preoperative radiographic assessment demonstrating acetabular undercoverage. Inclusion criteria were diagnosis of symptomatic hip dysplasia, treatment with PAO and concomitant hip arthroscopy in a single operative setting during the study period, and completion of patient-reported outcome measures more than one year after surgery. Patients were excluded if they did not complete patient-reported outcome measures at least one year after surgery.

Ninety-seven hips from 93 patients were retrieved. 26 hips from 22 patients did not complete a survey at least one year after the operation. Finally, 71 hips from 71 patients had more than a year of follow-up and were included in the study cohort (Fig. 1). Patient data including body mass index (BMI), sex, and age were extracted from the electronic health record.

Radiographic measurements

Using the most recent radiograph prior to surgery, radiographic characteristics were recorded. Anteroposterior pelvis radiographs were used to record lateral center-edge angle, Tönnis angle, Tönnis grade, crossover sign, joint congruency, ischial spine sign, and the posterior wall sign. False-profile radiographs were used to measure the anterior center-edge angle. Frog-leg lateral and Dunn lateral radiographs were used to measure alpha frog and alpha Dunn angles respectively. Radiographic measurements were performed according to previously described methods [22].

Surgical technique

All hip arthroscopies and PAOs were performed by a fellowship-trained hip preservation surgeon ([redacted]). Hip arthroscopy was performed first in all cases, followed by PAO in the same operative setting. During arthroscopy, the central and peripheral compartments were evaluated. Labral tears were recorded intraoperatively by the treating surgeon and classified according to Beck et al. [23]. The labral tear size was defined as the intraoperative length of the tear itself, measured using a graduated probe marked at 1-mm intervals. Maximal labral tear length was recorded. The labral tear sizes were divided into three groups: < 5 mm, 5–10 mm, and > 10 mm. Labral management was determined intraoperatively. When refixation was indicated, suture anchors were used. Additional intraoperative characteristics were recorded by the surgeon including the repair method for the labral tear, acetabular cartilage grade, treatment for acetabular cartilage, femoral head cartilage grade, and whether femoral head-neck osteochondroplasty was performed. After completion of hip arthroscopy, PAO was performed through the anterior Smith-Petersen approach.

Outcome measure

Patient reported outcome measures (PROMs) were collected at multiple time points according to our prospective protocol. HOOS was used to assess outcomes and has been demonstrated to have high validity, responsiveness, and reliability [24–26]. HOOS was scaled from 0 to 100. A value of 100 represented the best possible reported outcome, and a value of 0 represented the worst possible reported outcome.

Statistical analysis

Chi-squared testing (and Fisher’s exact test where applicable) was used to compare patient characteristics and preoperative radiographic characteristics across labral tear size groups. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used to compare HOOS across the three groups. Paired t-testing was used to evaluate statistical significance of HOOS improvement within each group. A multivariable linear model was used to assess association between labral tear size and post-operative PROMs while adjusting for corresponding pre-operative PROMs, age, sex and BMI. P-value < 0.05 was used as the threshold for statistical significance.

Results

Patient characteristics

Patient demographics and preoperative radiographic measurements are summarized in Tables 1 and 2 respectively. Patient characteristics, preoperative radiographic characteristics, and labral pathology were recorded for hips in each labral tear size group (Table 3). Although the proportion of female patients and those with BMI above 30 kg/m2 were not different between groups, the proportion of patients with an age above 25 years significantly differed between the groups (p = 0.011). Preoperative radiographic measurements including lateral center-edge angle, Tönnis angle, anterior center-edge angle, alpha frog, alpha dunn, crossover sign, ischial spine sign, and posterior wall sign did not significantly differ between the groups. Tönnis grade (p < 0.001), joint congruency (0.034), and Beck’s labral tear classification (p < 0.001) differed significantly between groups. Additionally, labral tear treatment (p < 0.001), acetabular cartilage grade (p = 0.001), and acetabular cartilage treatment (p < 0.001) differed significantly between groups (Table 3). The largest labral tear size group (> 10 mm) had a higher proportion of older patients (p = 0.011), higher Tönnis grades (p < 0.001), more hips with fair or poor joint congruency (p = 0.034), more severe labral tears according to Beck’s classification (p < 0.001), and worse acetabular cartilage grade (p = 0.001). These findings suggest that larger labral tears may occur in the setting of more advanced intra-articular disease. Figure 2 contains radiographs corresponding to pre- and post- PAO.

Outcomes

The mean follow-up time was 1.57 years ± 250 days. The patient-reported preoperative and postoperative HOOS are recorded in Table 4. Although there was statistically significant improvement (p < 0.001) in HOOS across the < 5 mm and 5–10 mm groups, there was not improvement in the > 10 mm group (p = 0.310). Those in the < 5 mm group improved the most. Larger labral tear size was associated with worse postoperative HOOS (p = 0.004). There were no significant differences in the time to questionnaire between the groups.

A multivariable linear regression model controlling for pre-operative HOOS, age, sex, and BMI was created to evaluate the association between increasing labral tear size group and postoperative HOOS (Table 5). In this model, each one-level increase in labral tear size group (e.g., from < 5 mm to 5–10 mm and 5–10 mm to > 10 mm) was associated with a 6.54-point reduction in postoperative HOOS (95% confidence interval − 11.90 to -1.18; p = 0.018). Discussion

We found that larger labral tear size was associated with worse short-term postoperative HOOS; however, this association should be interpreted in the context of important counfounders, including age, Tönnis grade, joint congruency, and acetabular cartilage status.

The PAO has consistently delivered strong outcomes in those with HD [10, 15, 16]. In the present study, larger intraoperative labral tear size was associated with worse short-term postoperative HOOS. However, larger labral tears were also associated with older age, higher Tönnis grade, worse joint congruency, and more advanced acetabular cartilage pathology. These findings suggest that labral tear size may reflect more advanced disease rather than act as an independent driver of PAO outcome.

This interpretation is consistent with previous studies demonstrating that worse radiographic grade or early arthritic change were associated with worse postoperative outcomes and earlier failures after PAO [27–29]. Significant labral tear size and pathology could be indicative of beginnings of premature hip joint degeneration. In the dysplastic hip, instability and abberant pathomechanics may contribute to progressive chondral and labral injury over time. Larger labral tears may result in further instability of the hip, and despite treatment arthroscopically and with osteotomy, restoring absolute stability and progressive hyaline cartilage damage may be more difficult. Therefore, larger labral tears may be indicative of more advanced disease, which may help explain the less favorabel short-term outcomes observed in this group despite concomitant arthroscopic management.

Recent evidence has questioned the benefit of performing hip arthroscopy at the time of PAO. Beaule et al. [30] reported no significant clinical benefit of concomitant hip arthroscopy compared to PAO alone at one-year follow-up. Our study does not compare PAO alone with PAO plus arthroscopy and therefore cannot ascess whether arthroscopy provides additional benefit. Rather, our findings describe differences in short-term outcomes by intraoperative labral tear size among patients treated with a combined approach.

T. Hagio et al. [7] concluded that PAO patients had similar postoperative outcomes regardless of whether they had labral tears on MRI. Our study differs from this study in two key ways: first, they assessed the presence or absence of a labral tear rather than size; second, they used non-arthrographic MRI to look for tears rather than intraoperative assessment. Our results do not necessarily conflict with theirs because of these differences in methodology. The characteristics of our cohorts were also different. Ours, for example, had a mean age of 29.5 years rather than 37–40 years. However, our study provides an expansion of the literature in this area.

Several prior studies have reported improved patient-reported outcomes following combined PAO and hip arthroscopy. Edelstein et al. [10] evaluated mid-term (minimum 4 year follow-up) outcomes for PAO with concurrent arthroscopy and found significant improvement in modified Harris Hip Score (mHHS), UCLA, and the pain subscore of WOMAC. Maldonado et al. also looked at the mid-term outcomes (minimum 5 year follow-up) and found mHHS and VAS improvements [31]. Similarly to these two studies, we noted improvements in outcomes. However, improvement after combined surgery does not establish that arthroscopy provides additional benefit beyond PAO alone. Our study diverges in that we specifically assessed these outcomes based on the labral tear size differences. We also focused on the short-term rather than mid-term where other extraneous factors may come into play. Our study adds to the literature by evaluating whether intraoperative labral tear size is associated with short-term outcomes within a cohort of patients treated with concomitant hip arthroscopy and PAO.

Limitation

Our study is not without limitations. This is a retrospective, single-center series with short-term follow-up. Although we have a large cohort of PAO patients that underwent treatment by a dedicated hip preservation surgeon, results may not be transferable to other surgeons or institutions. Further follow-up of this cohort is necessary to track radiographic disease and continued functional outcomes. Perhaps there is a different relationship between labral tear size and outcomes in the mid-term or long-term. Most importantly, this study did not include a PAO-only control group. Therefore, we cannot determine whether concomitant hip arthroscopy provides additional clinical benefit compared with PAO alone, nor can we determine whether arthroscopic treatment of labral pathology independently affected postoperative outcomes.

Labral tear size was associated with several important baseline and intraoperative variables, including age, Tönnis grade, joint congruency, acetabular cartilage grade, acetabular cartilage treatment, and labral treatment. These factors are important potential confounders and may be more directly related to postoperative outcome than labral tear size itself. Therefore, labral tear size should not be interpreted as an independent causal determinant of outcome. Instead, larger labral tears may represent more advanced intra-articular disease in patients with hip dysplasia. Although we adjusted for preoperative HOOS, age, sex, and BMI, the small sample size limited our ability to adjust for all clinically relevant confounders. Regardless, our study helps further characterize the relationship between labral tear size and patient outcomes and explores a variety of associations, an area about which relatively little is known.

Future direction

Future studies should compare PAO alone with PAO plus concomitant hip arthroscopy to better define the independent clinical value of arthroscopic management of labral pathology at the time of PAO. Ideally, these studies would stratify patients by labral tear size, cartilage status, Tönnis grade, and other markers of intra-articular disease. Larger multicenter studies with longer follow-up and a broader range of PROMs may also help determine whether intraoperative labral tear size independently predicts outcome.

Conclusion

In patients undergoing concomitant hip arthroscopy and PAO, larger intraoperative labral tear size was associated with worse short-term postoperative HOOS. However, larger labral tears were also associated with older age and worse markers of intra-articular disease, including higher Tönnis grade, worse joint congruency, and more advanced acetabular cartilage pathology. Therefore, labral tear size should be interpreted as a potential marker of more advanced disease.

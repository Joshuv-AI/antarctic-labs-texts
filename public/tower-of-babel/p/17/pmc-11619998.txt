Wideband and high gain elliptically-inspired 4 × 4 MIMO antenna for millimeter wave applications
Authors: Wael AE Ali, Mahmoud A Abdelghany, Ahmed A Ibrahim
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractAn elliptically-inspired microstrip antenna is utilized in this paper to operate in the V-band (52.9–70 GHz) of the millimeter frequency range for 5G applications. 2-port and 4-port multi-input-multi-output (MIMO) configurations are designed, fabricated, and tested to validate the desired radiation and impedance characteristics. The measured results mimic the simulated ones in terms of scattering parameters, and MIMO parameters. Although the 4-port footprint is considered miniaturized (26 × 26 mm2), it provides a reduced coupling between ports (≤20 dB) with an improved gain ranging from 8 to 10.1 dBi. Furthermore, the achieved MIMO parameters (ECC, DG, CCL, and MEG) are distinct with values of 0.006, ≥9.96 dB, ≤0.4 bits/s/Hz, and −3 dB, respectively. The introduced 4-port MIMO antenna is compared with the state-of-the-art antennas and this assures that the suggested model has built-in size, wider impedance bandwidth, and higher isolation with an improved diversity characteristic confirming the possibility of the presented MIMO antenna to be a challenging candidate for 5G wireless communications.Keywords: mm-Wave applications, Compact antenna, High gain antenna, MIMO antenna, High isolation

Introduction

The fifth generation (5G) technology has drawn great attention due to the various advantages over the previous technologies like the increased data rate and throughput, higher reliability, lower latency, and massive connectivity between various devices, which make it a promising candidate for the internet of things (IoT) applications such as smart cities, smart parking systems, and automated vehicles [1,2]. The adoption of 5G technologies requires a large bandwidth to fulfill the aforementioned advantages, and this can be achieved by the utilization of a millimeter frequency range (30–300 GHz) [3]. There is a potential use of the allocated frequency ranges for 5G operating at sub 6, 28, and 38 GHz. Furthermore, the frequency range around 60 GHz is immensely suggested by the Federal Communication Commission (FCC) for scientific and medical purposes due to the various advantages of this band and its propagation characteristics [4]. A 60 GHz frequency band is considered inside the V-band which is extended from 40 GHz to 75 GHz [5]. One issue that arises when using the V-Band is the significant absorption of signals due to oxygen at this frequency and that's why the V-Band has a coverage range of about 2 km [6]. The antenna is considered one of the key elements that should be designed accurately according to the required specifications in terms of high compactness, less complication, high gain, and wide impedance bandwidth [7,8]. Compromisation between the aforementioned specifications should be as little as possible in order to implement the desired antenna that copes with the newly reported antennas.

Various studies have been carried out in the last years for developing antennas that can be operated for 5G applications and among these antennas were the following; a triple-band umbrella-shaped monopole antenna in Ref. [9] is designed and fabricated on 8 × 8 mm2 substrate for 5th generation applications operated at 28, 38, 55 GHz, it delivers a maximum gain values of 6.6, 7, 7.35 dBi at 28, 38 and 55 GHz, respectively. In Ref. [10], a monopole antenna with a wider bandwidth is desired to work in the V-band for the range from 63 to 74.2 GHz, the antenna excelled in achieving the desired results with a maximum gain exceeding 9.5 dBi for the achieved band. In Ref. [11], the antenna is designed to cover a range from 27.5 to 28.5 GHz on-body with an operating band of 1 GHz at 28 GHz. In Ref. [12], a conventional patch antenna was initially modified using defects in the patch, then a DGS technique was applied to design a compact-size antenna. Nevertheless, the use of various defected structures in the radiator and ground plane made the model more complex. A wideband antenna was introduced in Ref. [13] with a combination of log-periodic and Yagi antenna structures using various layers to improve the impedance bandwidth and realized gain of about 7 GHz and 11.8 dBi, respectively. A 4 × 4 MIMO elliptical ring antenna is designed, experimentally fabricated, and measured in Ref. [14] to achieve compact size and high gain at 60 GHz. The isolation between elements is higher than 28 dB confirming its improved diversity from the obtained results of ECC and DG. A highly isolated 4 × 4 MIMO antenna employed at 36.83–40 GHz is introduced in Ref. [15] with a realized gain of 6.5 dBi and an overall size of 47.4 × 32.5 × 0.51 mm3. It can be observed that the simulated radiation and diversity performance mimic the measured results which constitutes a good candidate for 5G systems. A compact size 4 × 4 MIMO is introduced for millimetric wave applications in Ref. [16]. The prototype achieved a huge bandwidth of 25 GHz, but the model suffers from low isolation between elements with a value of less than 10 dB. Also, the MIMO parameters are investigated in terms of CCL, ECC, TARC, and DG to ensure the diversity capabilities of the presented antenna. In Ref. [17], a simple planar MIMO antenna consisting of four elements is arranged in a linear manner covering an area of 48 × 14 mm2. The antenna succeeded in obtaining a wideband frequency range (23–33/37.75–41) with −20 dB isolation between elements with a realized gain of 5.7 dBi. The contributors in Ref. [18] introduced a tree-shaped planar quad-port MIMO antenna achieving a wide impedance bandwidth for millimetric wave communications. The antenna excels in achieving a wide frequency range (23–40 GHz) but at the expense of the larger size (80 × 80 mm2). In Ref. [19], the authors reported that high-gain and wide-band MIMO antenna operated at 28 and 38 GHz for 5th generation applications. The antenna is composed of circular and rectangular patches loaded with stubs for achieving a wide impedance bandwidth of 26.5–43.7 GHz. To achieve a wide frequency range (27–40 GHz) using a two-port MIMO antenna, the patch antenna has slots in its radiating element as introduced in Ref. [20]. The patch antenna is implemented on an FR4 substrate (55.2 × 27.6 × 1.6 mm3) with a partial ground plane to obtain the desired frequency band. A slot-based antenna array is used in Ref. [21] consisting of four radiators to achieve a wide frequency range (24–40 GHz) for 5G applications. The antenna succeeded in obtaining the desired outcomes but at the expense of its size (150 × 70 × 0.2 mm3). A four-port elliptically slotted antenna is presented in Ref. [22] with a very compact size of 16 × 16 mm2 for 5G applications. The antenna succeeded in covering a frequency range of 22.2–31.4 GHz (centered at 28 GHz) with an isolation greater than 25 dB between various ports. In Ref. [23], the authors designed an antenna array with antipodal Vivaldi defected ground structure (AVDGS) and neutralization lines (NLs) for 5G application at 28 GHz. The antenna array covered a range of 27.2–30 GHz with a gain of 11.42 dBi with a tilted beam at 35° and half power beam width (HPBW) of 33°. A tri-circular ring MIMO antenna is introduced in Ref. [24] to achieve wideband characteristics (26–40 GHz) for mmW applications. The compact MIMO consists of orthogonally-arranged four ports with an isolation of 18 dB and a gain of 6.6 dBi. In Ref. [25], the author introduced an eight-element dual-band (3.37–3.61 GHz and 4.9–5.1 GHz) MIMO antenna for sub-6 GHz applications. The isolation exceeds 14 dB between ports with a gain value of 3.9 dBi while ECC <0.05 and < 0.02 in both bands, respectively. In order to achieve a high gain, the authors in Ref. [26] presented a 4-port MIMO array and each port is connected with 4 elements via power dividers. The MIMO antenna array is operated at 28 GHz with an isolation better than 30 dB and gain reaching up to 12.5 dBi. In Ref. [27], a defected ground structure (DGS) based 4-port MIMO antenna is presented for millimetric wave applications. The antenna is printed on 25 × 25 mm2 Roger 5880 substrate to operate in the frequency range 25.28–28.02 GHz with an isolation exceeding 23.2 dB and a maximum gain of 8.72 dBi.

This paper presents an elliptical-inspired patch antenna for V-band applications. The suggested antenna model is enlarged to comprise two and four elements to validate the MIMO performance. All impedance and radiation characteristics are carried out to validate the suggested model for further implementation in 5G V-band applications.

The antenna worked in the range from 51.7 to 54.8 GHz and from 61.06 to 65 GHz as demonstrated in Fig. 2. Finally, a small circle is etched in the radiator as illustrated in Fig. 1 (step 4) to improve the operating bandwidth. The antenna is employed in the range from 52.7 to 54.5 GHz and from 56.1 to 70 GHz as observed in Fig. 2.

Fig. 3 depicts the structure of the single element. It is composed of several sections; two circular and three elliptical patches are connected to achieve the desired configuration, and a circular desk is removed from the patch to enhance the antenna bandwidth as discussed before. The 13 × 12 mm2 5880 dielectric material with εr = 2.2, loss tangent = 0.0009, and thickness = 0.254 mm is used in the design. A 6.75 mm length and 0.5 mm width microstrip feedline is utilized to feed the patch with a full ground plane on the back side of the substrate as depicted in Fig. 1. The S11 outcome of the antenna layout and its equivalent circuit is displayed in Fig. 4. The microstrip feed line is modeled by L1, L2, and C1 while the patch radiator is modeled as two parallel RLC to produce the desired resonance [28]. The model worked with S11 ≤ −10 dB from 52.7 to 54.5 GHz (1.8 GHz) and from 56.1 up to 70 GHz (13.9 GHz) from the simulated results, while it employed from 52.7 GHz up to 70 GHz from the circuit simulation results with reasonable matching between the two outcomes.

The 2-D layout of the suggested V-band antenna.

The S11 outcomes of the single element at L1 = L2 = 0.2549 nH, L3 = 0.910 nH, L4 = 0.040 nH, C1 = 0.044 pF, C2 = 0.5179 pF, C3 = 0.199 pF, R1 = 71.84 Ω, R2 = 55.3 Ω.

Fig. 5 illustrates the surface current distributions at 59, 63, and 70 GHz. It is clear from the figure that the current has maximum concentration through the feed line and around the patch. At 59 GHz the current is gathered around the elliptical-shaped radiator. At 63 GHz, the current is converged around the circular slot and little current around the elliptical radiators. Finally, at 70 GHz the current is collected on the elliptical radiators, which confirms the radiation mechanism of the V-band antenna.

The current concentration of the suggested element.

Fig. 6 shows the 3-D gain results at 59, 63, and 70 GHz. The antenna has a gain of around 9.16 dB, 10.3 dB, and 7.97 dB at the different three frequency bands respectively, with broadside radiation patterns at these frequency bands.

The 3-D field pattern of the single antenna.

The normalized electric field of the antenna (co-cross polarization) at 59 GHz, 63 GHz, and 70 GHz in the two planes are demonstrated in Fig. 7. It is evident that first; the antenna has broadside radiation in ϕ = 90° at the three frequency bands and broadside radiation at ϕ = 0° at 59 GHz and 70 GHz while it has a slight deflection at 63 GHz. Second, there are undesirable nulls especially at ϕ = 0° and at higher frequency bands. These nulls are due to the large size of the end launcher connector which affects the antenna radiation. Third, there is more than a 15 dB difference in level between the co and cross-polarized patterns which confirms its diversity feature. To verify the performance of the suggested antenna, a time-domain analysis is carried out. To illustrate the antenna's ability to be employed in wideband communication, S11, S21, S21 phase, and group delay are the main parameters to check this performance.

The normalized field pattern of the single antenna.

The face-to-face (F2F), face-to-side (F2S), and side-to-side (S2S) are the three configurations utilized to test the antenna performance. So, the two copies of antennas (one as transmitter and the other as receiver) are spaced by a distance of 50 mm between them, and the desired parameters are extracted under the three configurations as shown in Fig. 8. Based on Fig. 8 (a), it is noticed that the model is employed with S11 ≤ −10 dB from 52.7 to 54.5 GHz (1.8 GHz) and from 56.1 up to 70 GHz (13.9 GHz) with S21 ≤ −26 dB as illustrated in Fig. 8 (b). Furthermore, the S21 phase is carried out as shown in Fig. 8 (c), and for better visualization, the unwrapped phase which assures the linearity characteristics of the proposed antenna under the three configurations, is also accomplished as shown in Fig. 8 (d). It is noticed from the S21 phase curves that, the antenna has linear features through the operating frequency band for the three configurations. Also, the group delay is discussed and shown in Fig. 9. The group delay has a stable behavior through the desired band at a level of 0.35 ns with fluctuations around 0.15 ns between the higher and lower levels. The outcomes from the analysis in the time domain confirm the operation of the suggested V-band antenna in wide-band operation.

Conclusion

A compact wide band and high gain 2-port and 4-port MIMO have been introduced in this paper for 5th generation applications operating at the V-band frequency range. The model succeeded in achieving a wide impedance bandwidth (52.9–70 GHz) with mutual coupling of −20 dB and realized gain variating between 8 and 10.1 dBi for the obtained frequency band. Furthermore, the model has a steady radiation pattern and considerable radiation efficiency. Due to the unavailability of measurements of the radiation patterns above 40 GHz in our lab, the simulated outcomes for the gain and radiation patterns have been carried out. The diversity performance is investigated using different parameters such as CCL, DG, ECC, TARC, and MEG and it is confirmed from the obtained results, its capability to operate in MIMO systems. There is a significant matchmaking between simulated and experimented outcomes after experimentally implementing the suggested antenna model which confirms its promising and challenging capabilities for further operation in 5G applications.

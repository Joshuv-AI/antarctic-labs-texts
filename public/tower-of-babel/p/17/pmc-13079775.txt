Performance and energy consumption optimization of ternary optical computers based on the M/G/1 queuing model
Authors: Shi Wenqiang, Liu Weiwen, Zhang Heqiang, Liu Meng, Zhang Sulan, Wang Xianchao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study addresses quality-of-service (QoS) and energy-consumption challenges in Ternary Optical Computer (TOC) by proposing a novel three-phase service model. The model integrates an M/G/1 queuing system with an \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$N$$\end{document}-policy and multiple vacation mechanisms into the TOC service framework for the first time. In this system, the server enters a vacation state when idle and resumes service immediately when the number of customers reaches a predetermined threshold \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$N$$\end{document}. We perform an in-depth analysis of the third phase using stochastic-decomposition methods to model precisely the steady-state queue length. For the first and second phases, we employ an approximate analysis to balance computational complexity and accuracy, yielding the system’s average queue length. By applying Little’s Law, we then derive an expression for the overall system’s sojourn time. Furthermore, we utilize the Renewal Reward Theorem to construct a cost function representing TOC energy consumption, and perform numerical experiments to identify the optimal threshold \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$N$$\end{document}. Our results demonstrate that the proposed model significantly reduces energy consumption while maintaining satisfactory system performance, offering new theoretical insights and practical guidelines for dual-objective optimization in TOC.

Introduction

As an indispensable enabler of scientific inquiry, engineering innovation, and data analytics, high-performance computing occupies a critical position across disciplines including artificial intelligence. Nevertheless, conventional binary computing architectures increasingly confront fundamental physical constraints in both operational efficiency and power dissipation, rendering the investigation of alternative computational paradigms an imperative scientific endeavor. Within this context, multiple unconventional computing frameworks have gained prominence, notably encompassing optical computation1, quantum computation2, and neuromorphic architectures3. Among these emerging paradigms, the Ternary Optical Computer4 distinguishes itself through its unprecedented bit-width capacity, hardware reconfigurability, and intrinsic parallel processing capabilities.

Recent advancements in TOC architecture include Professor Yi Jin’s foundational work on parallel-carry adder principles5 and the subsequent development of a Modified Signed-Digit (MSD) adder utilizing data-clipping methodologies. These innovations align with cutting-edge progress in photonic component control, particularly exemplified by Wang et al.’s seminal framework for dynamically reconfigurable optical platforms6. Subsequent research milestones demonstrate TOC’s expanding capabilities: Wang Xianchao’s implementation of carry-free arithmetic and vector-matrix operations7, Xu Qun’s team establishing optimized MSD multiplication protocols8, Peng Junjie’s breakthroughs in Fourier transform acceleration9, Li et al.’s parallelized metaheuristic algorithms10, and Song Kai’s graph-theoretic matrix optimizations11. While these achievements collectively underscore TOC’s practical viability, a fundamental challenge remains unresolved: the imperative to simultaneously enhance system performance and energy efficiency to satisfy escalating high-performance computing demands.

In response to the above issues, scholars have conducted research on the task scheduling and energy consumption optimization of TOC by means of queuing theory. For example, strategies such as the task service model based on the M/M/1 queuing system, the optimization of complex queuing systems, and the synchronous vacation mechanism have been proposed, providing preliminary solutions for the energy efficiency management of TOC. However, existing research still has deficiencies in the dual-objective optimization of the performance and energy consumption of TOC in the controllable queuing system. To this end, this paper establishes a three-stage service model of TOC. By introducing the N-strategy and multiple vacation mechanisms, a dual-objective optimization model of TOC is constructed. First, the steady-state queue length in the third stage is randomly decomposed12 to obtain the random decomposition structure. According to the probability distribution of the additional queue length, the explicit expression of the steady-state queue length is obtained. Then, by analyzing the average queue length of each of the remaining stages, the steady-state average queue length of the TOC service model is obtained. Next, the system sojourn time is derived using Little’s law as the performance index of TOC. Moreover, by applying the Renewal Reward Theorem13, the cost function of the system is designed to balance the energy consumption of TOC, and the parameters of the model are optimized. The results show that this model can effectively reduce the energy consumption of TOC while ensuring good performance, achieving the dual-objective optimization of TOC.

Related works

Ternary optical computer

Ternary Optical Computers were first proposed in 2003 by Professor Yi Jin’s team at Shanghai University, introducing a new opto-electronic hybrid architecture that represents data with three optical states—two orthogonal polarizations and a no-light state—to encode ternary information.Its hardware is built on decrease-radix design theory, exemplified by the fourth-generation SD16 platform and its extensions, which employ fully parallel carry-free computation to eliminate addition carry-delay bottlenecks.At the software level, TOC integrates a layered task-management architecture, a high-level programming model, and a dedicated file system to facilitate algorithm development and seamless application deployment on its reconfigurable optical engine.TOC prototypes have been successfully applied to graph-theoretic problems—such as shortest-path and matrix-based graph algorithms—signal-processing workloads including Fourier transforms, and intelligent-optimization tasks like swarm-intelligence metaheuristics.These advances demonstrate TOC’s capability to deliver high throughput on ultra-wide data words while managing energy consumption, positioning it as a compelling paradigm for next-generation high-performance computing.

Application of queuing theory in TOC performance optimization

Within the domain of Ternary Optical Computer performance optimization, queuing theory has been extensively employed to establish sophisticated task scheduling frameworks for system efficiency enhancement. Pioneering research by Wang X. C. et al.14laid foundational groundwork through an M/M/1 queuing system-based task service model. Subsequently, Wang X15, advanced this paradigm by developing an optimization framework for ternary optical computers that integrates asynchronous multi-vacation policies, achieving performance improvements through systematic analysis and evaluation. Contemporary advancements have further endowed TOCs with dynamic sleep control capabilities via synchronous vacation mechanisms16,17, enabling energy conservation through proactive processor hibernation during idle intervals. Despite these innovations, existing methodologies remain inadequate in comprehensively characterizing TOC energy consumption dynamics, particularly in balancing real-time computational demands with sustained power efficiency.

Computation processing algorithm modeling for stage 3

In modeling the computation processing algorithm for Stage 3, it is essential to select an appropriate queueing model to accurately describe the service process of the optical processor and its impact on performance and energy consumption. The traditional M/M/1 model assumes that both the arrival process and service times follow an exponential distribution. Although this assumption simplifies the mathematical analysis, it often presents significant limitations in practical applications. For example, in the actual operation of an optical processor, service times may exhibit a general distribution due to the complexity of the data, which does not conform to the exponential distribution assumption. Such a discrepancy can lead to a mismatch between the model’s predictions and the actual operational behavior, thereby affecting the expected outcomes of the design. Given that the output process of the previous stage satisfies the basic properties of a Poisson process (with exponentially distributed inter-arrival times), it is reasonable to assume that its output adheres to Poisson characteristics. Based on this feature, we can employ the M/G/1 queueing model to mathematically model this stage, which not only captures the system’s randomness more accurately but also provides a solid theoretical foundation for subsequent performance analysis. By considering the data requests as customers in need of service and viewing the components of the computation processing module S as the service station—and assuming that only one optical processor is responsible for processing the computation requests—this forms an M/G/1 queueing model with N-policy and multiple vacation mechanisms, as described in the model below. Summary and outlook

This study proposes a three-stage service model based on an M/G/1 queuing system to jointly optimize the performance and energy efficiency of the Ternary Optical Computer. The first two stages are responsible for task request arrival and data preprocessing. As they do not involve actual computation, they are briefly analyzed to balance model complexity with accuracy. The core focus of the research lies in the third stage, i.e., the computational service stage, where a stochastic decomposition method is employed to thoroughly investigate the impact of introducing an \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$N$$\end{document}-policy and a multiple vacation mechanism on system energy consumption. The multiple vacation mechanism allows the optical processor to enter an energy-saving state during periods of low task demand, thereby reducing unnecessary energy consumption, while the N-policy ensures system service quality by activating the processor only when the queue length reaches a preset threshold \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$N$$\end{document}. Although the specific values may vary with TOC hardware implementations (such as the energy efficiency ratio of photonic devices) and workload characteristics (the specific form of service time distribution), the three-stage service model established in this paper, the performance analysis method based on stochastic decomposition, and the energy consumption optimization framework integrated with the Renewal Reward Theorem are of universal significance. They provide a scalable theoretical foundation and analytical tool for future joint performance-energy optimization of TOCs with different architectures (e.g., multi-core, heterogeneous TOCs). Compared to the classical M/M/1 model, this model more accurately characterizes the service time characteristics of TOC computational tasks, thereby more effectively reducing energy loss during optical processor computations and offering new theoretical insights and practical guidance for TOC energy efficiency optimization.

While this study provides an effective modeling and optimization approach for TOC energy efficiency, it still has certain limitations and points to meaningful directions for future research. First, the current model is based on a single-server system; future work could extend it to a multi-server M/G/c queuing model to investigate task allocation mechanisms and joint energy-saving strategies in multi-core TOC architectures. Second, this paper assumes a homogeneous task flow; subsequent research could introduce priority scheduling or batch arrival processes to handle heterogeneous workloads with differentiated Service Level Agreements (SLAs).

P121 Chronotype Differences in Sleep Quality: A Systematic Review of Early Birds and Night Owls
Authors: Z Sajadi Hezaveh, M Halaki, M Fitzmaurice, A Marin Galindo, C Chow
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionA growing body of literature explores the health risks associated with chronotype, independent of various other factors. Amongst them, sleep quality stands out as significant yet relatively underexplored. This study aims to examine the association between chronotype and sleep quality.MethodMEDLINE, Web of Science, Scopus, Embase and Cinahl were searched for studies published before May 2024. Search terms were grouped into sleep quality and its assessment tools, plus chronotype and its assessment tools. Studies reporting the distribution of sleep quality parameters in different chronotype categories among the adult disease-free population were considered eligible. This study is registered in PROSPERO (CRD42023448002).Progress to DateAfter removing duplicates, we retrieved 1,125 abstracts. Following title/abstract screening, 724 studies remained, and upon full-text screening, 197 studies met the inclusion criteria. We are currently in the data extraction stage, having extracted data from 56 studies. Preliminary findings indicate that as chronotype progresses from morning to intermediate and late, there is a notable decrease in sleep quality and quantity, an increase in excessive daytime sleepiness, and a higher rate of insomnia. However, results regarding sleep efficiency and sleep latency are inconsistent. Additionally, different patterns may emerge when results are grouped by weekdays versus weekends or chronotype assessment tools (e.g., Munich Chronotype Questionnaire, Morningness-Eveningness Questionnaire).Intended Outcome and ImpactAfter extracting the data, we will evaluate the quality of the included studies and consider conducting a meta-analysis. Our ultimate goal is to use the research findings to inform practitioners and sleep clinicians about the significance of chronotype in managing sleep issues.

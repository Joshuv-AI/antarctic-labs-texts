A Lévy flight based chaotic black winged kite algorithm for solving optimization problems
Authors: Taybe Alabed, Sema Servi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The Black-Winged Kite Algorithm (BKA) is a relatively new bio-inspired metaheuristic approach developed to tackle challenging optimization tasks by maintaining a balance between exploration and exploitation. In this context, an improved version of BKA is introduced to better handle complex optimization scenarios. Three modified variants are proposed: CBKA, which incorporates logistic chaos-based mapping to improve solution diversity; LBKA, which utilizes Lévy flight to reinforce global exploration capability; and CLBKA, which merges both mechanisms to enhance the balance between exploration and intensification. The algorithms are assessed on 23 standard benchmark problems spanning unimodal, multimodal, and fixed-dimension test sets. CLBKA achieved the global optimum in 20 out of 23 test functions and ranked first in the Friedman statistical test, with the lowest average rank of 2.9348 among eight algorithms. In addition to the Friedman test, the Wilcoxon signed-rank test was also employed to statistically validate the significance of the observed improvements. Experimental findings indicate that CLBKA consistently outperforms the original BKA and various other metaheuristic techniques in terms of convergence reliability, solution quality, and search stability. Moreover, all proposed algorithms were implemented on six practical engineering design problems, including the Gear Train, Welded Beam, Three-Bar Truss, Pressure Vessel, Tension/Compression Spring, and Cantilever Beam design cases, delivering notably better optimization outcomes. In each case, CLBKA consistently outperformed both its baseline and enhanced variants, as well as several state-of-the-art algorithms from the literature, in terms of solution accuracy, convergence speed, and robustness. The performance of all algorithms was statistically validated using the Friedman test, further confirming the significance and robustness of the proposed hybrid strategies. The results confirm that the proposed hybrid strategies significantly enhance the search efficiency of BKA, making CLBKA a reliable and versatile optimizer for a wide range of complex, constrained optimization tasks.

Introduction

Optimization is the practice of selecting the best possible solution among a set of viable candidates. In contemporary times, it has become an indispensable tool across numerous disciplines, including engineering, scientific research, and finance, due to its critical role in enhancing performance, efficiency, and decision-making processes1. Typically, optimization problems are expressed within a formal mathematical framework to facilitate systematic solution approaches. Over time, numerous deterministic methods have been developed in the literature to address such problems, offering precise solutions under well-defined constraints and objective functions2. While deterministic methods offer exact solutions under specific conditions, their effectiveness diminishes when faced with highly nonlinear, large-scale, or multi-modal problems. In response to these limitations, researchers have increasingly turned to metaheuristic optimization algorithms, which provide flexible and robust strategies for approximating near-optimal solutions in complex problem spaces3. Metaheuristic algorithms can be broadly defined as high-level frameworks that guide the development of problem-specific heuristics. Rather than relying on problem-dependent rules or complete mathematical formulations, these algorithms employ adaptive mechanisms inspired by natural or social phenomena to navigate complex solution spaces efficiently4. These algorithms are capable of adaptively exploring the search space to identify optimal or near-optimal solutions, depending on the nature of the problem5. Inspired by nature, biology, or physics, metaheuristic algorithms are structured to efficiently explore vast and complex solution spaces. Among the many metaheuristics proposed, nature-inspired methods like GA6, PSO6, GWO7, and ALO8 have attracted significant attention for their flexibility and effectiveness in addressing real-world optimization problems. In recent years, several novel algorithms have further enriched the metaheuristic landscape. For example, the Artificial Lemming Algorithm (ALA), inspired by the migratory and decision-making behaviors of lemmings, has shown competitive performance on complex nonlinear optimization problems9. The MCOA: Multistrategy Collaborative Enhanced Crayfish Optimization Algorithm integrates specular reflection learning, an expanded exploration strategy, Lévy flights, and a vertical crossover operator to effectively balance exploration and exploitation, outperforming twelve advanced algorithms on multiple benchmark suites10. Likewise, the MSAO: Multi-Strategy Boosted Snow Ablation Optimizer employs good point set initialization, greedy selection, differential evolution, and dynamic lens opposition-based learning to enhance convergence accuracy and robustness, with successful applications in engineering design11. In parallel, hybrid and adaptive metaheuristics have attracted growing attention, yielding substantial improvements in optimization performance. For instance, Barshandeh et al. introduced a Learning Automata-based hybridization of the Marine Predators Algorithm and the Artificial Jellyfish Search, in which adaptive action selection enhanced exploration–exploitation balance, achieving superior results on 38 benchmark functions and ten clustering datasets12. Similarly, the Hybrid Multi-Population Algorithm (HMPA) combined Artificial Ecosystem-Based Optimization and Harris Hawks Optimization with Lévy flights, chaotic search, and quasi-oppositional learning, effectively increasing diversity and outperforming competitors across 50 benchmarks and various engineering problems13. Another study proposed a hybrid chaotic Atom Search Optimization augmented with the Tree-Seed Algorithm and Lévy flight, where enhanced exploration and adaptive search mechanisms delivered statistically significant gains on both synthetic and real-world engineering tasks14. Collectively, these advances highlight the continuous innovation in metaheuristic algorithm design and further motivate the hybrid enhancements to BKA proposed in this study.

The Black Winged Kite Algorithm (BKA) is a recent nature-inspired optimization technique based on the predatory behavior of the black-winged kite bird. Designed to mimic the bird’s strategic hunting patterns, BKA has emerged as a promising method for tackling complex optimization problems with improved exploration and exploitation balance5. The black winged kite exhibits distinct behavioral traits such as seasonal migration, agile hovering, and precise predation of small mammals, birds, and reptiles. These behaviors have been abstracted in BKA to simulate the core mechanisms of exploration and exploitation. Specifically, hovering represents how the algorithm can concentrate its search around potentially optimal zones, while swooping and perching represent shifts in focus and convergence. Despite its biologically inspired design, BKA like many metaheuristics still encounters challenges in avoiding local optima and maintaining a robust balance between global exploration and local exploitation across diverse optimization landscapes15. For example, Wang et al. (2024) introduced BKA, drawing inspiration from the behavioral traits of black-winged kites. Their approach incorporated novel elements such as Cauchy mutation and adaptive leadership dynamics to improve the equilibrium between global exploration and local exploitation, resulting in significant performance gains across a variety of benchmark problems16. In the same year, Jun Wang and colleagues proposed BKA as a metaheuristic model guided by the migratory and hunting behaviors of black-winged kites. The findings demonstrated that BKA outperformed conventional optimization techniques in terms of both global search capability and convergence speed, establishing it as an effective solution method for complex, high-dimensional optimization tasks5.

Lévy flight refers to a stochastic movement pattern characterized by step sizes drawn from a heavy-tailed distribution, allowing for frequent local searches interspersed with sporadic long-distance transitions. This dynamic helps avoid entrapment in local optima and promotes thorough exploration of the solution space, which is particularly beneficial in metaheuristic algorithms17. It has been observed in nature among animals and insects for efficient search patterns. In optimization algorithms, Levy flight enhances exploration and helps avoid local optima by efficiently covering large search spaces. Second, the implementation of a logistic mapping technique further refines the exploration process, ensuring a more diverse and adaptive search pattern across iterations. Levy flights are a powerful mechanism for enhancing exploration in optimization algorithms. As demonstrated by Gandomi et al. (2013), the Cuckoo Search (CS) algorithm effectively combines Levy flight with brood parasitic behavior to perform robust global and local searches. The Levy flight mechanism, characterized by a heavy-tailed probability distribution, enables the algorithm to escape local optima and efficiently explore large search spaces, outperforming traditional methods like (GA)6 and (PSO)6 in structural optimization problems​4. A similar strategy was adopted by Ling et al. (2017), who proposed the Lévy Flight Trajectory-Based Whale Optimization Algorithm (LWOA), which improves the Whale Optimization Algorithm (WOA) by integrating Lévy flight. This method enhances global search capabilities, helps escape local optima, and balances exploration and exploitation effectively. Their study showed that LWOA outperformed traditional WOA and other algorithms across 23 benchmark functions and high-dimensional optimization problems, highlighting its robustness and precision18.

In the field of engineering, optimization problems often involve complex, nonlinear, and constrained design spaces. These problems may be continuous or discrete in nature and typically require balancing multiple design objectives while satisfying strict functional constraints. Among widely studied benchmark problems, several classical engineering design tasks such as the Gear Train, Welded Beam, Three-Bar Truss, and Pressure Vessel stand out due to their real-world relevance and structural complexity. For example, the Gear Train Design task is formulated as a discrete optimization problem that focuses on identifying the optimal tooth counts for four coupled gears to achieve a defined transmission ratio. Its real-world applicability makes it a valuable case for assessing the effectiveness of metaheuristic approaches19. Sadollah et al. (2013) introduced the Mine Blast Algorithm (MBA), a population-based optimization technique, which demonstrated remarkable efficiency in solving complex engineering design problems, including the Gear Train Design problem. By leveraging innovative strategies for global search and local refinement, MBA achieved superior results, outperforming traditional optimization methods20.

The black-winged kite’s distinctive hovering ability and precision hunting behavior form the biological inspiration behind the BKA algorithm. The bird’s diet, which includes insects, small mammals, reptiles, and birds, reflects its adaptability in diverse environments. By abstracting the kite’s movement dynamics and foraging strategies, a computational model was developed to guide the optimization process5.

During this process, leader birds are crucial in steering the group, and their navigational efficiency directly impacts the migration outcome. This concept is adapted in BKA by associating leadership with solution fitness. If a candidate solution exhibits lower fitness than a randomly chosen peer, it follows; otherwise, it assumes leadership and directs the search trajectory.

This method dynamically ensures the selection of outstanding leaders, thereby guaranteeing the success of the migration process. Lévy flight

Lévy flight, based on the work of mathematician Paul Pierre Lévy, refers to a random movement model where most steps are small, but occasionally large jumps occur, following a heavy-tailed probability pattern17. This movement pattern has been observed in various species such as birds and fruit flies, whose search behavior aligns with the statistical properties of Lévy flights. These natural patterns have inspired the development of innovative algorithmic strategies in the field of optimization. Generally, a Lévy flight involves steps sampled from a Lévy distribution, combining frequent short steps with occasional long-distance moves. Such dynamics have been widely reported in the behavioral patterns of numerous animals and insects22,23. The mathematical formulation of Lévy flight, introduced in the early 20th century, laid the groundwork for its integration into modern metaheuristic algorithms as a powerful exploration tool24.

Lévy flights are widely accepted as a powerful mathematical tool for modeling the search and movement behavior of animals and insects. This method combines long-distance steps covering a large search area with shorter, localized movements, achieving an effective balance between exploration and exploitation. These features make Lévy flights highly effective in global optimization algorithms.

A review of the literature reveals that Lévy flight has been utilized both in its original form and through various modified implementations. Researchers have proposed and applied alternative versions including trimmed Lévy flight, smoothed Lévy variants, and segmented Lévy motion to enhance algorithmic performance in different optimization contexts25. In the present study, however, the standard, unmodified Lévy flight mechanism is adopted to preserve the natural dynamics of the original model.

Lévy flight26 belongs to a family of non-Gaussian stochastic processes with heavy-tailed behavior, where movement patterns follow the Lévy stable distribution. One of the key characteristics of the logistic map is its sensitivity to initial conditions, a hallmark of chaotic systems. This sensitivity is particularly advantageous for tasks requiring high diversity and unpredictability, such as stochastic optimization and cryptographic key generation. In the context of optimization algorithms, the logistic map has been employed as a tool to enhance exploration and exploitation by generating pseudo-random sequences that guide the search process. Its chaotic nature ensures a balance between diversification and intensification, making it an attractive choice for improving metaheuristic algorithms. For instance, embedding the logistic map into control parameters can dynamically adjust algorithmic behavior, reducing the likelihood of premature convergence and improving overall performance.

In this work, the logistic map was integrated into the improved BKA to dynamically influence certain algorithmic components. By leveraging the chaotic properties of the logistic map, the enhanced BKA was able to achieve superior optimization results.

Integration of logistic map in the proposed CBKA algorithm

The CBKA algorithm integrates the logistic chaotic map into the original BKA framework to improve the diversity of the population during the search process. As a prominent example of a chaotic system, the logistic map generates pseudo-random sequences that are highly sensitive to initial conditions. This inherent unpredictability introduces variation in the search trajectory, thereby increasing population diversity. By leveraging chaotic dynamics, the algorithm becomes less prone to premature convergence and gains enhanced exploratory capability, especially when navigating complex, high-dimensional solution spaces. The logistic chaotic map is used to dynamically control key algorithmic parameters, ensuring a more adaptive balance between exploration and exploitation. This chaotic approach introduces variations in the search trajectory, making the algorithm less likely to get trapped in local optimal while still converging effectively towards high-quality solutions.

By embedding the logistic map into BKA, the CBKA algorithm achieves better exploration while maintaining an effective convergence rate. The results of the proposed method show improved performance on benchmark optimization functions compared to the standard BKA, confirming the benefits of chaotic dynamics in metaheuristic algorithms.

Integration of lévy flight in the proposed LBKA algorithm

The proposed LBKA algorithm incorporates Lévy flight (LF) into BKA to improve its global exploration capability during the optimization process. LF is defined by distributions with infrequent but long jumps, enabling the algorithm to execute occasional long-distance moves along with frequent shorter steps. This mechanism improves the algorithm’s capacity to navigate complex solution spaces while reducing the chance of stagnation in suboptimal regions30,31.

By incorporating Lévy flight, the LBKA algorithm achieves a more effective balance between global exploration and local exploitation. The random yet structured nature of Lévy flight prevents the search from getting stuck in suboptimal regions while guiding the algorithm toward promising solution areas. This adaptive mechanism allows the algorithm to explore a larger area efficiently, improving both convergence speed and solution accuracy. Experimental results show that LBKA outperforms the standard BKA in various benchmark optimization problems. The incorporation of Lévy flight provides significant improvements in escaping local optimal and enhances the robustness of the optimization process, making LBKA a more effective and reliable approach to complex optimization problems. The main steps of LBKA include: population initialization, fitness evaluation, leader selection, adaptive parameter generation, exploitation and exploration decisions, Lévy flight-based updates, boundary control, fitness comparison, and migration behavior for diversity. The process repeats until the stopping condition is met.

Table 3 lists fixed-dimension multimodal functions that mimic real-world optimization problems. These functions are characterized by a variety of search landscapes, including multiple local optimal and varying levels of complexity. They are specifically designed to assess how well an algorithm manages the trade-off between exploration and exploitation when navigating high-dimensional, fixed-dimension search spaces.

By evaluating the algorithm across these three categories, a comprehensive understanding of its performance variations under diverse optimization scenarios is achieved.

Comparison and assessment of the proposed algorithms

The proposed CLBKA algorithm was evaluated using a comprehensive set of 23 standard benchmark functions, categorized as unimodal (F1–F7), multimodal (F8–F13), and fixed-dimension multimodal (F14–F23). These categories were selected to thoroughly assess the algorithm’s ability to balance exploitation and exploration across diverse optimization landscapes. In the result tables, the best results obtained by the proposed methods compared to the baseline BKA are highlighted in bold, while results from other algorithms that outperform BKA are indicated in different colors. Since the evaluation includes the baseline BKA, a single-enhancement version with only logistic chaotic mapping (CBKA), a single-enhancement version with only Lévy flight (LBKA), and the combined version (CLBKA), this comparison also serves as an ablation study. This structure allows the individual effect of each strategy to be assessed directly, while also demonstrating the synergistic benefit of combining them.

As shown in Tables 4, 5 and 6, both individual strategies improve upon the original BKA in most cases, while their integration in CLBKA consistently achieves the best overall performance, confirming the complementary effects of the two mechanisms.

All three improvements were developed as part of this research to strengthen the solution accuracy and convergence stability of the original BKA.

As presented in Table 4, the proposed CLBKA algorithm consistently achieved the lowest mean values across all seven unimodal test functions, clearly demonstrating its superior capability to reach the global optimum.

For instance, on Function F1, CLBKA achieved a mean value of 4.1547e-100, outperforming LBKA, which recorded 5.2542e-95 for the same function. Similarly, in Function F6, CLBKA reached 7.0191e-126, significantly surpassing all other variants. In addition to solution accuracy, the standard deviation (S.D.) values indicate that CLBKA also provides highly stable and consistent results. For example, in Function F5, the standard deviation of CLBKA was 4.6613e-05, indicating minimal variability across multiple independent runs and reinforcing the algorithm’s robustness. These outcomes confirm that the combination of a chaotic logistic map (used for generating a well-distributed initial population) and the Lévy flight mechanism (for enhanced exploration) significantly improves the original BKA. Among the three proposed variants, CLBKA demonstrated the best overall performance, achieving both high precision and stability in solving unimodal optimization problems.

Statistical comparison

Friedman test

The Friedman test is a widely used non-parametric hypothesis test in statistics, particularly useful for comparing multiple related groups when the assumptions of parametric tests are not met67. The Friedman test is a widely used non-parametric method for comparing the performance of optimization algorithms across multiple benchmark problems. It relies on the ranking of algorithm outputs to determine whether statistically significant differences exist among them. Since it does not require the assumption of normality, it is particularly well-suited for analyzing dependent groups. This test is commonly applied when evaluating multiple algorithms on the same set of test functions in optimization studies68.

In this study, the Friedman test was employed to compare the performance of the basic BKA, its improved variants CBKA and LBKA, the proposed CLBKA algorithm, and several well-known optimization methods, including ALO, WOA, GWO, GSA, and PSO. Wilcoxon Signed-Rank test

The Wilcoxon signed-rank test is a widely used non-parametric statistical test designed to compare two related samples, matched samples, or repeated measurements on a single sample to determine whether their population mean ranks differ. Unlike its parametric counterpart, the paired t-test, the Wilcoxon signed-rank test does not require the assumption of normality, making it especially suitable for evaluating the performance differences of optimization algorithms across multiple benchmark functions when the data distribution is unknown or non-Gaussian. This test is commonly applied to pairwise comparisons in metaheuristic algorithm research, where results are collected over multiple independent runs69,70.

Conclusions

This article presented three novel enhancements CBKA, LBKA, and CLBKA of the Black Winged Kite Algorithm (BKA) to overcome its limitations in balancing broad search diversity and focused refinement. By integrating the chaotic dynamics of the logistic map and the stochastic properties of Lévy flight, the improved variants aimed to strengthen the algorithm’s performance in diverse optimization landscapes.

Extensive experimental evaluations were conducted using 23 well-known benchmark functions, classified into simple unimodal problems (F1–F7), challenging multimodal functions (F8–F13), and fixed-dimension benchmarks with multiple local optima (F14–F23). The proposed CLBKA algorithm demonstrated consistently superior performance across all categories. It achieved the lowest mean error and standard deviation values on a majority of the test functions, indicating both high precision and robustness. Moreover, CLBKA was evaluated alongside well-established swarm intelligence algorithms such as PSO, WOA, GWO, ALO, and GSA, as well as several hybrid models. The results confirmed that CLBKA provided highly competitive outcomes across different categories of optimization problems, with minimal variance across independent runs, demonstrating the stability and adaptability of the hybrid design.

In addition to benchmark problems, all proposed algorithms (BKA, CBKA, LBKA, and CLBKA) were applied to six real-world engineering optimization tasks: the Gear Train Design Problem, the Welded Beam Design Problem, the Three-Bar Truss Design, the Pressure Vessel Design Problem, the Tension/Compression Spring Problem, and the Cantilever Beam Design Problem. In all six engineering problems, CLBKA was compared with algorithms from the literature and consistently produced better results, confirming its superior performance in complex constrained design problems. Moreover, statistical validation was performed using the Friedman test for all algorithms, which ranked CLBKA as the best-performing algorithm across all test cases. This confirms that the observed improvements are not only empirical but also statistically significant.

Overall, the combination of Lévy flight and logistic chaotic mapping significantly enhanced the exploration and exploitation balance within the BKA framework. Lévy flight enabled broader and more dynamic global search, while the chaotic map introduced adaptive variability that prevented premature convergence. The hybrid structure of CLBKA thus proved to be a robust, scalable, and efficient optimization tool, capable of solving both benchmark and real-world problems with consistent performance. This work contributes a strong foundation for future research in adaptive, hybrid, and intelligent optimization techniques.

This study has demonstrated that CLBKA offers a broad set of benchmarking functions and delivers high accuracy, fast convergence, and stable performance across various real-world optimization problems. The obtained results indicate that the method provides an effective solution approach, particularly for single-objective optimization problems. In the future, adapting this powerful structure to multi-objective and dynamic optimization problems, applying it to different dimensional and real-time constrained scenarios, and comparatively evaluating its runtime performance across various hardware environments may further expand the applicability and limitations of the method. In addition, optimizing components such as chaotic maps and Lévy flight parameters through adaptive strategies represents a potential step toward enhancing the algorithm’s flexibility and generalizability.

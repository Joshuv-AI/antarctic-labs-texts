Histopathological and biomarker complete response after neoadjuvant chemotherapy of advanced stage yolk sac tumor of the ovary: A case report and literature review
Authors: Tofan Widya Utami, Yuri Feharsal, Laila Nuranna, Gatot Purwoto, Trifonia Pingkan Siregar, Tantri Hellyanti
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionYolk sac tumor is the second most common germ cell ovarian carcinoma. Neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NACT) is proposed as an alternative option for patients with advanced disease who are not eligible for primary debulking. We presented a case with a systematic review of advanced yolk sac tumors of the ovary with poor performance who gain benefit from NACT.Case presentationA 24-year-old female underwent NACT followed by surgery after being diagnosed with an advanced yolk sac tumor. A literature search was done based on the clinical question using the Patient/Problem, Intervention, Comparison, and Outcome (PICO) approach. Pubmed and Google Scholar were used to search the literature.DiscussionOut of 111 manuscripts found, 2 articles were retrieved for detailed evaluation. The patient showed a complete response in tumor size, histopathology, and tumor markers after the NACT procedure followed by surgery.ConclusionsNACT is a suitable option for advanced yolk sac tumors of the ovary.Keywords: Yolk sac tumor, Neoadjuvant chemotherapy, Alpha-fetoprotein, Evidence-based case report

Introduction

Human serum α-fetoprotein (AFP) is generally a conceptus-only product. According to the findings of Gitlin's study, the liver, yolk sac, and the gastrointestinal tract appear to be the primary sites of α-fetoprotein synthesis [1]. Yolk sac tumor is the second most common germ cell ovarian carcinoma and originates from cancerous germ cells undergoing differentiation along the extraembryonic yolk sac lineage. Its histopathological patterns include the endodermal sinus pattern (a proliferation of Schiller-Duval bodies; round or elongated tumor cell-lined papillae with a large central vessel, protruding into a cystic space surrounded by tumor cells), papillary pattern, solid pattern, festoon pattern (complex ribbons and undulating cords), and glandular pattern (endometrioid-like or intestinal-type) [2].

Before the introduction of chemotherapy, the prognosis of the yolk sac tumor of the ovary was very poor. Nowadays, a combination of surgery and chemotherapy can yield a good result [3]. A challenge can arise for advanced-disease patients who are not eligible for surgery. Neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NACT) offers an option for these patients. Even though the use of NACT is debatable in malignant germ cell tumors of the ovary, adjustments to its use in selected cases can be considered. We presented a case of an advanced yolk sac tumor of the ovary with poor performance status who gained benefit from NACT followed by surgery. This study has been reported in line with the Surgical Case Report (SCARE) 2020 criteria [4].

Case description

Ms. 24 years old, with suspicion of advanced-stage ovarian cancer with ascites and pleural effusion. The patient had worsening abdominal enlargement complicated by dyspnea for 3 months. Clinical examination demonstrated an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) score of 3, breathlessness, and positive shifting dullness. A whole abdominal CT showed a cystic mass with solid components sized 10.9 × 11.5 × 13 cm originating from the left ovary, occupying the pelvic cavity, lower abdomen, and adhering to the sigmoid colon. Right pleural effusion and massive ascites were also documented. Tumor markers showed an increase of α-fetoprotein level at 26,386 ng/ml and the CA-125 level at 813 U/ml, while lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) and B-hCG were within normal limits.

Based on the clinical, imaging, and tumor markers, the patient was diagnosed with a suspected advanced-stage yolk sac tumor. Chest drain (Fig. 1), followed by NACT with Bleomycin, Etoposide, and Carboplatin (BEP) was planned. After 3 cycles of chemotherapy, the clinical signs and symptoms of the tumor marker improved. Imaging of the tumor after three NACTs demonstrated a reduction of tumor mass to 9.4 cm (Fig. 2). No ascites or pleural effusion were found.

Chest X-Ray showing pleural effusion and after evacuation (serohemorrhagic).

Comparison of whole abdominal CT before and after NACT.

The patient was assessed as having a partial response and planned to have interval debulking surgery with the aim of fertility-sparing surgery. An ovarian mass with the size of 10 cm originating from the left ovary was identified, while the uterus, contralateral ovary, omentum, peritoneum, and other abdominal organs were within normal limits. A left salphingo-oophorectomy and omentectomy were performed (Fig. 3). The postoperative period was unremarkable, and the patient was discharged in good condition. Three weeks after surgery, the AFP level was significantly decreased to a normal level of 5.2 ng/ml, and the patient was planned to have three cycles of adjuvant chemotherapy using BEP (Supplementary Fig. 1).

The intraoperative procedure and the final specimen.

After three cycles of adjuvant BEP, the patient had clinically no evidence of disease with a normal AFP level of 2.94 ng/mL. Evaluation at 3 months after complete treatment showed no evidence of disease on clinical and ultrasound examinations (Fig. 4) and AFP levels of 2.04 ng/mL. Further evaluation of 21 months after adjuvant chemotherapy still showed no evidence of disease and AFP level of 2 ng/ml (Supplementary Fig. 2).

Ultrasound examination at 3 months after adjuvant chemotherapy showing no evidence of disease.

The histopathology of the specimens demonstrated ovarian tissue that generally underwent extensive fibrinoid necrosis and degeneration. Groups of atypical cells with degenerative nuclei and cytoplasm were found, locally forming tubular structures. Mitosis was difficult to find. The omentum was composed of connective tissue and fat. No tumor mass was found. The examination concluded that ovarian histology was consistent with necrotic tissue with extensive fibrinoid degeneration consisting of clusters of atypical cells, suggestive of residual tumor mass, in <1 % of the sampled specimen area (Fig. 5). The histologic type of the tumor could not be determined. The omentum does not contain a tumor mass. Based on the result, we proposed the immunohistochemistry examination to confirm whether this is the complete response of yolk Sac tumor. The immunohistochemistry showed positive staining for several antibodies like AFP, Glypican 3, AE1/3, SALL4, and HepPar-1, which corresponded to the yolk Sac tumor (Fig. 5).

Method

Literature search. Based on the clinical question, we formulated the Patient/Problem, Intervention, Comparison, and Outcome (PICO) approach with the following details: P: ovarian yolk sac tumor; I: neoadjuvant chemotherapy; C: primary surgery; O: cytoreductivity rate. Discussion

Changes in PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling appear to be prevalent in tumors containing a yolk sac component (72 %). This alteration of the signaling pathway will increase c-fos and c-jun in the nucleus. AFP gene expression is regulated by a complex network of transcriptional regulators, such as oncogenes like c-fos and c-jun, that will increase the expression and production of AFP [[5], [6], [7]]. The yolk sac tumor was positive with several immunohistochemistry stains. SALL4 is a marker for primitive germ cell differentiation and thus will provide a positive stain for the yolk sac tumor, as it is classified as a germ cell tumor. The AFP stain is highly specific and will stain positive in 60 % of cases. Glypican 3 is less specific but has a stronger expression for the yolk sac tumor. The next antibody that will stain positive is Hep-Par-1 [[8], [9], [10]]. NACT is a new treatment option for ovarian cancer. Individual tumors had different morphological alterations following neoadjuvant treatment. The nucleocytoplasmic ratio of the remaining tumor cells was usually low. Extreme expansion and irregular contours are among the characteristics of nuclei that make them seem like multinucleate gigantic cells. Mitotic figures were difficult to spot in most situations. The cytoplasm of the cells was either extremely eosinophilic, transparent, and vacuolated, or foamy. There were also noticeable stromal alterations. Inflammatory cells, fibrinous debris, necrosis, cholesterol clefts, and hemosiderin pigment were commonly found in the stroma. There were large areas of fat necrosis, particularly within the omentum, with collections of foam cells and multinucleate foreign body-type giant cells [11,12]. Related to the case, the high secretion of AFP was contributed by the reactivation of AFP gene expression as the consequences of the production of c-fos and c-jun related to changes in the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway in yolk sac tumors. After the chemotherapy, tumor regression was characterized by histopathological appearances that showed fibrinoid degeneration and necrosis. Mitosis was difficult to find. These findings were correlated with the study above. The immunohistochemistry was correlated with the yolk sac tumor, as positive specific stains were found on the specimen.

The use of NACT in the yolk sac tumor of the ovary is considered feasible. A study by Lu et al. demonstrated that 53 cases showed significant improvement in overall status after NACT. Patients with 3 cycles of NACT (BEP) experienced a significant decrease in mean ovarian tumor size (20.7 vs 17 cm, p = 0.016), AFP level (33,000 vs 660 ng/ml, p < 0.001), percentage of patients who had pleural effusion (9 vs 3 %, p = 0.04), and ascites (20 vs 6 %, p < 0.001). After surgery, patients in the NACT group had a better optimal cytoreduction rate (81 % vs 44 %, p = 0.007) and fewer peri-operative morbidities (blood loss of 200 vs 450 ml, p = 0.018) compared to the primary debulking surgery group [13].

Our case was very similar to the case in the Lu et al. study, which was successfully managed with NACT and surgery and showed a significant reduction in AFP levels, ascites, and pleural effusion. Optimal surgery was also achieved after NACT.

Conclusion

The use of NACT in advanced yolk sac tumors of the ovary showed a significant decrease in tumor size, AFP level, ascites-pleural effusion production, increased cytoreductive rate, and even a complete response in histopathological and tumor markers. The use of NACT can be considered in the advanced yolk sac tumor of the ovary, which is not suitable for primary debulking surgery.

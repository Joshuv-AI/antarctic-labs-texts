Research Note: Dynamics of black grouse (Tetrao tetrix) infestation with various Eimeria species
Authors: Rajmund Sokół, Sylwia Koziatek-Sadłowska
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe black grouse (Tetrao tetrix) is an endangered species facing challenges in breeding and reintroduction programs, including parasitic infestations. This study aimed to assess natural infestations by various Eimeria species and infestation dynamics in female, male, and young black grouse kept in a stationary aviary. Faecal samples were collected from adult grouses between April and the time of chicks’ hatching and rearing (September). Faecal samples from young birds were collected from the hatching for a period of 1 year. The prevalence of Eimeria spp. was determined by a qualitative method (Fulleborn's flotation) and a quantitative method (McMaster's method with Raynaud's modification). The following Eimeria species were identified: E. lyruri, E. nadsoni and E. nonbrumpti. The average percentages of Eimeria spp. in the cock were 80.52%, 9.27%, and 10.21%, respectively; in the hen, they were 86.19%, 9.28%, and 4.53%, respectively; in the young black grouses they were 84.60%, 9.34% and 6.06%, respectively. The highest E. lyruri infestation was observed in the cock in June (144227 OPG) and July (129365 OPG). In the hen, the infestation intensity increased in May (304302 OPG) and then decreased in June (39583 OPG). Furthermore, an additional increase was observed in July (216533 OPG). Two increases in infestation intensity were also observed in young birds, with peaks in January (91387 OPG) and July (126178 OPG). A positive strong correlation was identified between Eimeria spp. in the cock and the young birds. A statistically significant positive correlation was identified in the hen between E. lyruri and E. nadsoni. No correlation was demonstrated between the infestation intensity and the age of the birds or season of the year in all the grouses under study. Despite some attempts, a comprehensive approach to the issue of coccidiosis in the black grouse as a disease that may affect the success of reintroduction has yet to be established. It seems crucial to monitor the level of Eimeria spp. invasion, and the proposed faecal sampling scheme is an important tool for achieving this goal.Key words: Eimeria species, invasion dynamic, black grouse, Tetrao tetrix

BACKGROUND

The black grouse (Tetrao tetrix) is a critically endangered species in Europe, with its population and range decreasing over the past century (Głowaciński, 2001). Grouse depopulation has been caused by many factors including intensive forest management, biotope fragmentation, an increase in the predator populations and intensive hunting and other factors, including, air pollution, human presence in forests habitats, no specific feed base, a decrease in the population of pollinators and stress (Angelstam, 2004). Infections with various pathogens and parasitic infestations, with their diverse effects on birds, are also an important factor (Sokół et al., 2015). Coccidia are ubiquitous in the environment, and they have a simple life cycle. The infestation occurs via ingestion of contaminated feed and water, contact with infected litter, and indirect contact. Apart from typical symptoms (diarrhoea), coccidiosis can occur without typical symptoms increases susceptibility to other diseases (Sokół et al., 2015). Intensive smell of the faeces (diarrhoea) facilitates the detection of infested birds by predators (Hudson et al., 1992). Moreover, parasitic infections can reduce the animals’ activity, reproduction rate and weakened expression of ornamentation (Höglund et al., 1992). Any worsening of the condition of the birds could have a significant impact on the success of their reproduction and reintroduction. Therefore, coccidia are a very important factor affecting the health of black grouses. According to The coccidia of Galliformes compiled by Duszynski et al. (2000) (www.k-state.edu/parasitology/worldcoccidia/GALLIFORMES), 21 species of the genus Eimeria are now known to occur in Tetraonidae, in black grouse E. tetricis Haase, 1939, Eimeria sp. of Pav and Zajicek, 1974, E. nadsoni Yakimoff and Gousseff, 1936, E. lyruri Galli-Valerio, 1927.

The aim of the study was to assess natural infestation by various Eimeria species and its dynamics in a hen, a cock and young birds (from hatching to introduction) of the black grouse (Tetrao tetrix) kept in a stationary aviary.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Materials and Sampling

The study was conducted in 2019 to 2020 at a black grouse breeding center in the northeast of Poland (Spychowo Forest District, 53.60070, 21.34452). The research was conducted in accordance with the standards of good veterinary practice. The black grouse population at the center consisted of 5 cocks and 8 hens of reproductive age. The birds at the center was kept in individual in aviaries, separated by sex and, during certain phases of the annual cycle, into technological groups, such as offspring and leading hens. The grouses were fed with feed mixtures intended for pigeons, and the diet was supplemented with ant larvae, dried cranberries and cowberry. They had access to drinking water ad libitum in containers refilled once daily, along with seasonal shoots and flowers from various plants.

The parasitological examination was conducted on a 3-year-old cock, 2-year-old hen and their offspring comprising 6 birds (3 females and 3 males) aged from 1 wk to the time of introduction (approximately 1 year). Samples of faeces (10 grams) from the cock and the hen were collected individually, and collective samples (15 grams) were taken from the young birds. Samples of fresh faeces were collected every 2 wk on 3 consecutive days at 6 a.m. from beneath the net grille, which was replaced with a new, clean grille after each faeces collection. Due to their uniqueness, the grouses were not euthanized for the purpose of the study. Faecal samples were collected from adult black grouses between April (i.e. in the middle of the mating period) and the hatching of chicks and their subsequent rearing (September). Faecal samples from young birds were collected between the time of hatching (June) and the point of reintroduction, which spanned over a period of more than 1 calendar year. METHODS

The faecal samples were examined using the flotation method according to Fülleborn with Darling's solution (comprising 50% saturated NaCl solution and 50% glycerol). The samples were stirred with a glass rod, and 3 × 1 g portions were then weighed and centrifuged for 5 min at the rotation speed of 3500 x g. Three preparations were made from each sample, which were analyzed under an optical microscope at magnifications of 200×, 400× and 1,000×.

Coccidia of Eimeria spp. identification was based on their oocysts’ morphological and morphometric features and was performed with an Olympus visualization and image acquisition kit. The parasite development forms were identified using a parasitology atlas (Hendrix and Robinson, 2014) and as described in previous study (Sokół and Gałęcki 2018). The oocyst number in 1 g of the faeces (OPG) was determined by the McMaster method, modified by Raynaud. The quantitative determination was performed in triplicates for each sample.

Statistical Analysis

A Pearson correlation (r) analysis was conducted between (1) the infestation intensity for individual Eimeria species in the birds under study; (2) the birds’ age and the infestation intensity with various Eimeria spp.; and (3) the season of the year and the infestation intensity for individual Eimeria spp. The mean OPG (for 3 d) at each time point was used for correlation analysis. Correlations with a P-value of less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant. The data were processed statistically using Statistica 13.1 software.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

This is the first report of the dynamics of Eimeria spp. infestation in the black grouse (T. Tetrix) in an annual cycle. Most parasitological studies involve a single sample collection from wild birds kept in captivity. Based on our previous study (R. Sokół, unpublished data) which revealed notable discrepancies in the quantity of excreted oocysts in samples obtained at 1-d intervals from a specific individual, for this study we employed 3-d sampling collection methodology. The proposed scheme will prevent false-negative results. Moreover, the results of a study for a specific time points are shown as the mean (not median) from 3 d. This was done on purpose as the result would otherwise be 0 (zero) OPG in many cases, which would not be appropriate and would not reflect the actual infestation intensity of black grouses at a given time interval.

During the study, the grouses did not show any signs of gastrointestinal disease or changes (increase/decrease) in feed or water intake. There was any evidence of a decline in activity, alteration in behavior, or deterioration in condition of the grouses. The birds successfully reproduced, resulting in the hatching of offspring. The following Eimeria species were identified in the grouses under study: E. lyruri, E. nadsoni, and E. nonbrumpti. The number of oocysts per 1 gram of faeces (OPG), depending on the Eimeria species in the cock, hen and young birds of black grouse (T. tetrix) are presented in the Table 1. These findings may indicate that black grouse housed in aviaries are most susceptible to Eimeria lyruri infestation, followed by E. nadsoni and E. nonbrumpti, and that co-infection with these species is also a significant risk. The average percentages of Eimeria spp. in cock were 80.52%, 9.27%, 10.21%, respectively and in the hen, they were 86.19%, 9.28%, 4.53%, respectively. The percentages of Eimeria spp. in young black grouses were 84.60%, 9.34%, and 6.06%, respectively. The infestation with E. lyruri was chronic in adult birds, and the offspring of the infested birds exhibited susceptibility to infection with Eimeria spp. from the outset (Table 1). Correlations between the level of infestation with Eimeria spp. and the birds’ age and the month are presented in table 2. A strong positive correlation was found between Eimeria spp. in the cock and in the young birds (Table 2). This means that an increase in the level of infestation with E. lyruri was followed by an increase in the level of infestation with E. nadsoni and E. nonbrumpti. A statistically significant positive correlation was identified in the hen between E. lyruri and E. nadsoni. A previous study also revealed that the E. lyruri infestation was chronic in nature. The occurrence of coinfection with E. lyruri, E. nadsoni, and E. nonbrumpti was observed during and after the mating period, which may be attributed to an increase in contact between birds (Sokół and Koziatek-Sadłowska, 2020).

The study revealed that the level of infestation with Eimeria sp. fluctuates during a year (Table 1). The highest E. lyruri infestation was observed in the cock in June and July with an OPG of 144227 OPG 129365 OPG, respectively. In the hen, the infestation intensity demonstrated an increase in May (304302 OPG) and subsequently exhibited a decline in June (39583 OPG). A further increase was observed in July (216533 OPG). The infestation intensity in young birds was also observed to increase twice, with peaks in January (91387 OPG) and July (126178 OPG). However, no correlations were observed between the season of the year and the level of the parasitic infestation (E. lyruri) in the birds under study (Table 2). Nevertheless, there was a growing tendency for infestations during the summer months in all the birds under study.

Despite some attempts, there is no method of treating coccidiosis in the black grouse. Natural resistance of coccidia specific to grouses to toltrazuril was previously demonstrated (Sokół and Gałęcki, 2018). The absence of disease symptoms and alterations in black grouse behaviour observed in the present study suggests that therapeutic intervention may not be necessary at this level of infestation with Eimeria spp. This is particularly relevant given the potential impact of chemotherapeutic agents on the health and reproductive success to the birds. (Frank 1947).

The health status of Galliformes birds kept in captivity, especially reproductive flocks, should be monitored regularly in restitution and reintroduction programmes to control parasitic infestations. Since there is no comprehensive approach to the issue of coccidiosis in the black grouse as a disease that affects the success of reintroduction, it is important to understand the dynamics of Eimeria spp. infestation in the black grouse, which may result in developing an effective treatment regimen for coccidiosis. Maintaining a low level of parasitic infestation in the black grouse and other wild birds covered by the reintroduction programme is of key importance to the protection of these endangered species. Monitoring of the infestation intensity seems to be essential for effective prevention and treatment of the black grouse and other birds under protection.

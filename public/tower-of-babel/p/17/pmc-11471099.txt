Understanding myofibroblast origin in the fibrotic lung
Authors: Mahsa Zabihi, Mahtab Shahriari Felordi, Arun Lingampally, Saverio Bellusci, Xuran Chu, Elie El Agha
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIdiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is characterized by accumulation of myofibroblasts (MYFs) and extracellular matrix components, which leads to severe distortion and scarring of the gas exchange units of the lung, the alveoli, and ultimately respiratory failure. Fibrosis-associated MYFs are therefore widely regarded as the culprits that compromise the architectural makeup of the lung in fibrotic disease. During the past decade, the cellular source of MYFs has been intensely investigated. The rationale for such studies is that identifying the origin of these cells might help identify novel therapeutic targets and candidates to treat IPF patients. Recent advances in basic and translational research employing lineage tracing and multi-omics approaches have helped address the identity of MYF precursors, highlight the underlying heterogeneity, and to a less extent investigate MYF fate during fibrosis resolution. In this review, we discuss the current understanding of such important aspects of MYF biology as well as recent developments in the treatment of IPF.Keywords: Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, Myofibroblasts, Fibrotic disease, Myofibroblast heterogeneity, Lineage tracing

Introduction

Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is an idiopathic form of usual interstitial pneumonia (UIP) and remains one of the most aggressive and lethal forms of interstitial lung disease (ILD). The median survival time after diagnosis is 2–3 years.1, 2, 3 Current antifibrotic therapies are not capable of halting the progression of this disease; rather, they only slightly delay disease progression and lead to mild improvement in the quality of life of affected patients. Such therapies are also very broad, and not only inhibit profibrotic signaling (such as transforming growth factor beta [TGFβ] pathway) but also benign, repair-promoting signaling pathways (such as fibroblast growth factor receptor 2b [FGFR2b] signaling).4 This is mainly attributed to a gap in our knowledge regarding the precise identity of both culprit and proregenerative cells in the fibrotic lung and the associated molecular mechanisms. The latter likely needs to be manipulated in a cell type-specific manner to efficiently reverse aberrant parenchymal remodeling and promote regeneration.

IPF is characterized by loss of type 1 and 2 alveolar epithelial cells (AT1/2s) and replacement of the intricate alveolar network by fibrous scars populated by myofibroblasts (MYFs) and the associated extracellular matrix (ECM) components including collagen, laminin, and fibronectin. This also creates a chronic inflammatory environment together with macrophages and other immune cells. While balanced activation of MYFs is central to physiological wound healing, persistent activation of these cells and the accompanying fibrogenesis in the lung can lead to progressive loss of lung structure and function, and consequently respiratory failure.5,6

Early studies documented MYFs as modified fibroblasts in granulation tissue and their role was linked to wound contraction.7 It was established that such spindle and stellate-shaped cells with cytoplasmic stress fibers had similar features to smooth muscle cells. Indeed, MYFs express alpha-smooth muscle actin (ACTA2) and type I collagen, which distinguishes them from normal fibroblasts.8,9 Yet, the expression of Acta2 is often transient and therefore not consistently expressed by MYFs and their derivatives in the fibrotic lung. Another issue is that Acta2 expression does not always correlate with collagen deposition.10,11

Basic and translational research, particularly using mouse models, has shown that developmental signaling pathways such as TGFβ, wingless-related integration site (WNT), hedgehog, and Hippo, to name a few, play a critical role in the emergence and activation of MYFs during the development of pulmonary fibrosis.12, 13, 14 Indeed, TGFβ1 is widely regarded as the master regulator of fibrogenesis as it activates fibroblasts, promotes MYF differentiation, and induces ECM deposition.15, 16, 17 TGFβ1 is believed to be mainly produced by alveolar macrophages (AMs) and AT2s. Apart from its direct effect on mesenchymal cells, it also inhibits alveolar epithelial cell proliferation. Although WNT signaling is associated with AT2-mediated regeneration,18,19 WNT signaling is mainly regarded as profibrotic, and WNT inhibition attenuates fibrosis in animal models.20,21 WNT is also required for TGFβ-mediated fibrogenesis.22 Another example is Yes-associated protein (YAP) and transcriptional coactivator with PDZ-binding motif (TAZ) that are the effectors of the hippo pathway. It has been shown that YAP and TAZ promote MYF differentiation and their targeting reverses lung fibrosis.23, 24, 25 On the other hand, bone morphogenetic protein (BMP) signaling is regarded as mostly antifibrotic. BMP2, BMP4, and BMP7 are among the most studied BMP ligands in the context of lung fibrosis. BMP4 suppresses ECM production by human fibroblasts, and overexpression of BMP7 inhibits TGFβ-induced collagen production by MYFs.26 BMP2 inhibits TGFβ1 action via peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma (PPARγ) activation in smooth muscle cells27 and induces lipogenic differentiation in fibroblasts derived from IPF lungs.28 Finally, fibroblast growth factor (FGF) signaling is generally antifibrotic (such as FGF7 and FGF10 signaling through the epithelial [b] isoform of FGFR2; FGFR2b), while few ligands (such as FGF2) signaling through the mesenchymal form (FGFR2c) can be profibrotic.29, 30, 31, 32

To date, there is no curative therapy for IPF. However, active research in the fields of lineage tracing, multi-omics, and mouse and humanized lung injury models have expanded our understanding regarding the cellular origin of MYFs, and shed light on novel therapeutic avenues that hold promise for future exploitation to treat patients. In this review, we address the cell-of-origin of the MYF and provide an update on novel therapeutic candidates and targets in IPF.

Cellular sources of MYFs in lung fibrosis

Understanding the cellular origin and related signaling pathways that control the emergence, maintenance, and function of MYFs may help develop novel antifibrotic therapies. This explains the tremendous effort and resources being invested in identifying the source of MYFs in the fibrotic lung.33, 34, 35 Although genetic lineage tracing is the gold-standard tool to address cellular hierarchy, attempts to identify the cell of origin of the MYF have often been hampered by the underlying heterogeneity of both the examined progenitor populations and their descendants. In addition to the use of single-cell omics approaches, the field is slowly moving away from single-marker lineage tracing and stepping into the intersectional genetics era, which will positively impact data quality and interpretation of future lineage-tracing studies. The current understanding is that the lipofibroblast (LIF), often lumped into the “alveolar fibroblast” category, is a major source of MYFs in lung fibrosis. In the next subsections, the contribution of LIFs as well as other potential sources to the MYF pool will be addressed. We will focus on discussing the experimental evidence for direct transdifferentiation and adoption of the MYF fate by these cell types, rather than their potential contribution to fibrosis by secreting profibrotic cytokines or even depositing ECM proteins. One aspect that remains to be explored is whether preventing Acta2 upregulation in MYF progenitors prevents MYF formation and fibrosis development.

LIFs as a source and fate for MYFs in the fibrotic lung

There is a great body of literature suggesting that MYFs have a mesenchymal origin.36, 37, 38, 39, 40, 41, 42, 43 LIFs are resident fibroblasts that are enriched with neutral lipid droplets and are important for AT2 maintenance in the alveolar compartment of the lung. They provide adjacent AT2s with triglycerides to be incorporated in the process of surfactant production.44,45 LIFs are also niche cells for AT2 stem cells and are therefore important for their maintenance and self-renewal.46, 47, 48 Being in close proximity to AT2s renders LIFs susceptible to profibrotic cytokines produced by AT2s and immune cells, and therefore a likely contributor to the MYF pool in the fibrotic lung. Previously, it had been shown that fetal rat lung fibroblasts lose their lipogenic phenotype and acquire a MYF-like phenotype when exposed to hyperoxia.49 Human embryonic lung fibroblasts follow a similar trend when exposed to nicotine.50 LIFs had been known to express the adipocyte marker adipose differentiation-related protein (ADRP or perilipin 2 [PLIN2]), which is important for lipid uptake and trafficking,51 and PPARγ,52,53 the master regulator of adipogenesis.53 FGF10 and transcription factor 21 (TCF21) are other known markers for LIFs.54, 55, 56 Lineage tracing of PLIN2+ cells revealed a significant contribution to MYFs in bleomycin (Bleomycin)-induced pulmonary fibrosis.57 Examination of FGF10+ cells using an enhancer trap approach showed a similar result.57 Bulk gene expression analysis on homogenates prepared from lung explants revealed downregulation of adipogenic genes in parallel to upregulation of MYF-associated genes.57 The same study showed that pre-existing smooth muscle cells are not a major source of MYFs in the fibrotic lung.57

With the advent of single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq), a more comprehensive transcriptomic profile has been established for LIFs, and the list of marker genes has been refined to include in addition to the already known markers (Tcf21, Plin2, Fgf10, and G0s2), new markers such as Limch1 (LIM and calponin homolog domains 1), Gyg (glycogenin), Macf1 (microtubule actin cross-linking factor 1), Mfap4 (microfibril associated protein 4), Npnt (nephronectin), Wnt2, Col13a1 (collagen type XIII alpha 1 chain), and Inmt (indolethylamine N-methyltransferase).58 In humans, this list includes TCF21, LIMCH1, A2M (alpha-2-macroglobulin), RGCC (regulator of cell cycle), and genes related to lipid/retinoic acid processing and/or storage.58 LIFs were detected in both donor and IPF lungs57, 58, 59 with collagen and ECM-related genes being among the top differentially regulated genes in these cells in IPF lungs compared with donor lungs.58 Another study showed that human LIFs can be identified by the expression of APOE (apolipoprotein E), PLIN2, and FST (follistatin).60

More recently, lineage tracing of TCF21+ cells also revealed that LIFs are significant contributors to MYF formation in the context of bleomycin (Bleomycin)-induced pulmonary fibrosis, and that they pass through a transitional state characterized by the expression of secreted frizzled-related protein 1 (Sfrp1).61 SFRP1 inhibits lung fibroblast invasion and TGFβ1 inhibits its expression to promote the progression of these transitional fibroblasts into collagen triple helix repeat containing 1 (CTHRC1)+ MYFs.61 The same study identified adventitial fibroblasts and, to a less extent, peribronchial fibroblasts, are other potential sources of MYFs in the fibrotic lung.61 Importantly, it also showed that MYFs undergo deactivation via differentiation into LIFs, peribronchial fibroblasts, and pericytes during fibrosis resolution.61 Another recent study utilized AT1 cell ablation or butylated hydroxytoluene (BHT; 3,5-di-tert-4-butylhydroxytoluene)-induced lung fibrosis and subsequent resolution to show that alveolar fibroblasts characterized by the expression of Tcf21 enter a transitional state marked by the expression of Sfrp1 that precedes the emergence of ACTA2+ MYFs.62 The authors identified another subset of transitional alveolar fibroblasts marked by the expression of Runx1. RUNX1+ ACTA2+ cells return to a normal alveolar fibroblast phenotype during fibrosis resolution,62 thus agreeing with the model that MYFs reacquire their initial AT2-supportive phenotype that resembles LIFs during resolution.28,57

Another line of evidence supporting the LIF-to-MYF axis during fibrosis development is the finding that alveolar fibroblasts expressing signal peptide, CUB domain, EGF-Like 2 (scube-2), a population that expresses typical LIF markers such as Inmt, Npnt, Tcf21, and Wnt2, have been shown to contribute to MYF formation.63 Along the way, these cells transit through an inflammatory state before giving rise to MYFs.63 The same group had previously established CTHRC1 as a marker of MYFs in the fibrotic lung and used computational analysis to demonstrate that alveolar fibroblasts give rise to MYFs in the fibrotic lung.10 Interestingly, the same study showed that both alveolar fibroblasts and adventitial fibroblasts upregulate Cthrc1 when transferred into bleomycin (Bleomycin)-treated lungs.10

Platelet-derived growth factor receptor alpha (PDGFRα) is a marker of alveolar fibroblasts including LIFs, also termed type 2-associated stromal cells (TASCs).46,47 Lineage tracing combined with scRNA-seq of PDGFRα+ cells in normal and fibrotic lungs showed that LIFs acquire a reversible fibrogenic profile with an attenuation of lipogenic pathways in the fibrotic lung.64 These findings agree with the reversible LIF-MYF-LIF switch previously described.57,65

Currently, the precise fate of MYFs during the resolution of pulmonary fibrosis is still debatable. It has been suggested that pathological MYFs undergo apoptotic clearance during the resolution of lung fibrosis, and that such event is critical for the removal of collagen-rich scars and restoration of normal lung architecture and function.66, 67, 68 On the other hand, MYF deactivation/dedifferentiation represents an alternative route for fibrosis resolution. The latter mechanism involves MYFs undergoing fate switching and “returning” to a proregenerative LIF phenotype, which supports the regrowth of healthy alveolar structures populated by AT1s and AT2s.28,57 Other fates following deactivation/dedifferentiation are also plausible but await further investigation. Of note, both resolution routes (apoptosis and redifferentiation into LIFs) can be potentiated via treatment with the first-line antidiabetic compound, metformin (Glucophage), which accelerates the resolution of lung fibrosis.28,66 While metformin (Glucophage) inhibits collagen production28,66 and induces apoptosis66 via an adenosine 5ʹ- monophosphate (AMP)-activated protein kinase (AMPK)-mediated mechanism, redifferentiation into the LIF phenotype in response to metformin (Glucophage) treatment is largely AMPK-independent, and involves the secretion of BMP2, leading to PPARγ activation and subsequent lipogenic differentiation.28 The molecular mechanisms controlling the apoptosis vs. redifferentiation balance remain to be seen, but they likely involve signals coming from regenerating AT2s and the surrounding niche, thus dictating fate decision-making in these MYFs.

To sum up, there is accumulating evidence implicating LIFs, or more generally alveolar fibroblasts, in fibrogenesis at least by serving as a major cell of origin of pathological MYFs that feature in fibrotic lung disease [Fig. 1]. Future research aimed at maintaining these cells in their AT2-supportive state and preventing their transdifferentiation into MYFs, and/or promoting the redifferentiation of MYFs back to LIFs represents a logical and promising therapeutic strategy.

Other sources

Pericytes and other perivascular cells

Pericytes are another potential contributor to the MYF pool in the fibrotic lung. Pericytes are mesenchymal perivascular cells wrapped around capillaries. They share the basement membrane and closely interact with endothelial cells in the vascular niche and control tight junctions. Pericytes also support angiogenesis and vascular development.69 Pericytes are identified by the expression of neural/glial antigen 2 (NG2) (also known as chondroitin sulfate proteoglycan 4 or CSPG4) and platelet-derived growth factor receptor β (PDGFRβ).70 However, since these markers are also expressed by other mesenchymal subsets such as smooth muscle and fibroblasts, they are not unique pericyte markers. Of note, PDGFRβ has been shown to mark MYF progenitors in fibrosis of multiple organs including the lung.71 Co-localization of NG2 and ACTA2 has been demonstrated in human IPF lung sections.72

Pericytes were suggested as a source of MYFs in bleomycin (Bleomycin)-induced pulmonary fibrosis in mice73 [Fig. 1]. The evidence was based on the finding that forkhead box D1 (FOXD1)+ mesenchymal progenitors labeled during embryonic development give rise to pericytes postnatally, and that the latter cells expand and produce collagen, along with another independent population of resident fibroblasts.73 Lineage tracing of NG2+ and forkhead box J1 (FOXJ1)+ cells showed increased proliferation of these cells in addition to localizing to fibrotic regions; however, it was reported that they express only low levels of ACTA2.40 Moreover, myosin heavy chain 11-positive (MYH11+) cells, a population that include pericytes in addition to smooth muscle cells, were proposed to contribute to MYF formation in the fibrotic mouse lung.74 Another study utilized an intratracheal adoptive cell transfer method to show that resident fibroblasts in alveolar walls have the highest profibrotic potential compared to NG2+ pericytes or epithelial cells, thus downplaying the potential of pericytes to give rise to MYFs in bleomycin (Bleomycin)-induced pulmonary fibrosis.41

Another marker of perivascular cells is the sonic hedgehog-responsive gene, glioma-associated oncogene 1 (Gli1). This gene also marks other mesenchymal subsets such as alveolar and peribronchial fibroblasts. Lineage tracing has shown that GLI1+ cells contribute to MYFs in the fibrosis of multiple organs including the lung.36,39 GLI1+ cells also overlap with pericytes, partially express NG2 and PDGFRβ, and are also classified as mesenchymal stem or stromal cells (MSCs). The latter cells can self-renew and are capable of trilineage differentiation in vitro. While there is an increasing body of literature on the beneficial effect of exogenously applied MSCs in the context of multiple injury models, lineage tracing of endogenous MSCs shows that these cells are mostly pathological as they give rise to MYFs. One explanation for this discrepancy is that endogenous MSCs might be corrupted by the proinflammatory and profibrotic milieux that guides them toward MYF differentiation and collagen deposition, unlike exogenous MSCs that pose beneficial and therapeutic effects. Perivascular ATP-binding cassette sub-family G member 2-expressing (ABCG2+) cells have been defined as another population of lung-resident MSCs that can transition to MYFs both in vitro and in vivo after TGFβ1 or bleomycin (Bleomycin) exposure, respectively.75 This population is distinct from NG2+ pericytes and is rather a pericyte subpopulation that is amplified upon injury and acquires a contractile phenotype.75

Taken together, pericytes remain an enigmatic and heterogeneous population of mesenchymal cells. The key will be to generate novel research tools allowing deep characterization of these cells at the transcriptomic and functional levels and dissecting them from the rest of the mesenchymal landscape. Their contribution to the MYF pool seems very likely but awaits further confirmatory investigations using better research tools.

Epithelial cells

Epithelial cells such as AT2s have been proposed to give rise to MYFs by undergoing epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT) [Fig. 1]. The latter is a dynamic mechanism that was first described by Greenburg and Hay76 who showed that polarized epithelial cells can differentiate into mesenchymal cells, often fibroblasts and MYFs.77 This is accompanied by gaining a migratory and/or invasive potential in addition to significant changes in gene expression profiles. Three types of EMT have been described in different biological contexts: embryogenesis, normal and aberrant wound healing, and cancer metastasis.78,79 Similar to MYFs, exaggerated or uncontrolled EMT may lead to tissue fibrosis.78 EMT is characterized by downregulation of epithelial markers (such as E-cadherin) and upregulation of mesenchymal markers such as fibroblast-specific protein 1 (FSP1), ACTA2, collagen 1, vimentin, and fibronectin.80, 81, 82 Signaling factors including FGF, hepatocyte growth factor (HGF), WNT/β-catenin, epidermal growth factor (EGF), and TGFβ family can initiate EMT.83,84 These factors can regulate EMT transcription factors such as twist, snail, zinc finger E-box binding homeobox (ZEB), and others.85,86 For example, epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR)–RAS–extracellular signal-regulated kinase (ERK) signaling in AT2s triggers the transcription factor ZEB1 and activates EMT. ZEB1 also controls the expression of tissue plasminogen activator (tPA), which affects fibroblast activation induced by TGFβ. Furthermore, ZEB1 regulates paracrine signaling between alveolar epithelial cells and fibroblasts, thus augmenting fibrogenesis in the lung.87 It has been suggested that mesenchymal cells recruited to the site of injury in the context of IPF might stimulate AT2s to undergo EMT and acquire a fibrogenic nature.88 Most of the experimental evidence for the involvement of EMT in MYF formation stems from the finding that treatment of primary AT2s or AT2-like cell lines with TGFβ leads to an EMT phenotype in vitro.16,77,89,90 Pulmonary fibrosis induced by overexpression of TGFβ1 via adenoviral delivery also shows signs of EMT.79 However, in vivo lineage tracing of AT2s in the bleomycin (Bleomycin) model of lung fibrosis did not show signs of EMT, suggesting that this phenomenon is not very prominent in the fibrotic lung, at least in the bleomycin (Bleomycin) model in mice.40

Endothelial cells

It has been reported that endothelial cells also have the potential to undergo a phenotype transition similar to EMT with common characteristics, known as endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EndoMT) [Fig. 1]. EndoMT was first proposed by Karasek91 who reported that endothelial cells transform into MYFs and that this transition is controlled by TGFβ signaling. In this transition, endothelial cells lose their typical markers such as cluster of differentiation 31 (CD31)/platelet and endothelial cell adhesion molecule 1 (PECAM-1), tyrosine kinase with immunoglobulin like and EGF like domains 1 (Tie1), tyrosine kinase with immunoglobulin like and EGF like domains 2 (Tie2), vascular endothelial cadherin (VE-cadherin), and von Willebrand factor (vWF), and acquire mesenchymal markers including ACTA2, N-cadherin, collagen 1, and vimentin. Endothelial cells also produce profibrotic molecules that recruit immune cells and contribute to inflammation and fibrosis.92, 93, 94

Pulmonary capillary endothelial cells have been proposed to be a source of MYFs in pulmonary fibrosis. In fibrotic regions of bleomycin (Bleomycin)-treated lungs, significant numbers of cells derived from the endothelial lineage were detected.92 Co-expression of endothelial and mesenchymal cell markers in cultured lung fibroblasts was also demonstrated.92 Fate tracing of Tie2+ endothelial cells in bleomycin (Bleomycin)-induced pulmonary fibrosis revealed co-expression of collagen 1 and a so-called transitional population of Tie2/vimentin-double positive cells, suggesting the differentiation of endothelial cells into mesenchymal cells.95 Two subpopulations of endothelial cells that are relevant for lung fibrosis were characterized by scRNA-seq in the bleomycin (Bleomycin)-treated rat model; the first is enriched with Nos3 and Cav1 expression and is mostly distributed in non-fibrotic lungs, while the other cluster is enriched with Cxcl12 and likely interacts with AMs and mesenchymal cells.96 Another recent study suggested the presence of endothelial cell-like MYFs that are present in the healthy lung, and that these cells contribute to the MYF pool in the fibrotic lung.97 TGFβ1 can induce such differentiation.97

Bone marrow-derived mesenchymal precursors: Fibrocytes

Fibrocytes are spindle-shaped fibroblast-like cells that are derived from hematopoietic precursors and express both stromal and myeloid markers such as collagen 1, cluster of differentiation 34 (CD34), and cluster of differentiation 45 (CD45)98 [Fig. 1]. These circulating connective tissue cells99 have been described in human fibrotic lung diseases and cancer as expressing C-X-C motif chemokine receptor 4 (CXCR4), C-C motif chemokine receptor 7 (CCR7), and C-C motif chemokine receptor 2 (CCR2). Circulating fibrocytes have been proposed to traffic to the lung in response to C-X-C motif chemokine ligand 12 (CXCL12) and mediate fibrosis.100 Fibrocytes can potentially activate fibroblasts and participate in inflammation leading to ECM remodeling and fibrosis. However, fate mapping of these cells is relatively challenging due to the lack of fibrocyte-specific markers. Moreover, deletion of Col1a1 gene in the hematopoietic lineage including fibrocytes did not hinder lung fibrosis development and collagen deposition, thus adding to the controversy regarding direct transdifferentiation of fibrocytes into MYFs.38

Using animal models, it was shown that fibrocytes express the chemokine receptor CCR2 that is important for their recruitment to damaged areas in the lung expressing the chemokine C-C motif chemokine ligand 2 (CCL2).101 Ccr2 knockouts reveal less fibrocyte recruitment and are protected from fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC)-induced pulmonary fibrosis.101,102 CCL2 acting through CCR2 activates fibrocytes, and enhances collagen production in damaged lung regions.101 The fibrocyte chemokine CXCL12 was found to be elevated in both lungs and plasma of lung fibrosis patients.103 Circulating fibrocytes showed a significant increase in UIP/non-specific interstitial pneumonia (NSIP) compared to normal controls but were mostly negative for the MYF marker ACTA2.103 In another study, fibrocytes were detected in IPF but not donor lungs and their abundance positively correlated with the number of fibroblastic foci.104 The fibrocyte chemoattractant CXCL12 was again found to be elevated in the plasma of IPF patients and was detectable in the bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) fluid of 40% of patients but not in donors.104 Flow cytometry-based quantification of fibrocyte counts also suggested that their abundance correlates with poor prognosis.105 Fibrocytes were detected in the BAL not only of IPF patients but also systemic sclerosis (SSc) patients.106

Pleural mesothelial cells (PMCs)

The pleura is composed of flattened epithelial cells, called mesothelial cells, originating from the embryonic mesoderm. PMCs have been shown to acquire a MYF-like phenotype when treated with TGFβ1 in vitro, a process referred to as mesothelial-to-mesenchymal transition (MMT)107 [Fig. 1]. PMCs have been proposed to undergo parenchymal trafficking in human IPF lungs based on immunohistochemical staining for calretinin, a PMC marker, and that such trafficking correlates with IPF severity.108 When labeled PMCs were injected into the pleural space of mice receiving intranasal TGFβ1, they were recovered in the BALF and lung parenchyma.108 The authors also detected ACTA2 expression in these cells.108

Wilms tumor 1 (Wt1) is highly expressed by mesothelial cells during embryonic lung development but is significantly suppressed postnatally in mice. While the extent of migration of embryonic PMCs into the lung parenchyma highly depends on the experimental conditions and time of labeling during embryonic lung development,109, 110, 111, 112, 113 a contribution of these cells to MYFs has been demonstrated in the fibrotic lung postnatally.114 Loss of Wt1 in PMCs was suggested to contribute to their differentiation into MYFs via MMT therefore contributing to lung fibrosis115; however, this can be rescued by carbon monoxide or induction of heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1) expression.116 Given that lung fibrosis is widely regarded to initiate in the lung periphery, it is plausible that the pleura is involved in fibrogenesis and possibly through providing MYF progenitors.

Therapeutic approaches for IPF

Two compounds are currently approved by the FDA to treat IPF: pirfenidone117, 118, 119, 120, 121 and nintedanib.122,123 Pirfenidone is an oral drug with anti-inflammatory, antioxidative, and anti-proliferative properties and therefore slows fibrogenesis through modulation of collagen generation, reduction of TGFβ1-induced fibroblast activation, and circulating tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNFα). Nintedanib is an inhibitor of multiple receptor tyrosine kinases (RTKs) including vascular endothelial growth factor receptor (VEGFR), platelet-derived growth factor receptor (PDGFR), and fibroblast growth factor receptor (FGFR). Nintedanib therefore targets downstream signaling pathways related to the proliferation, migration, and differentiation of mesenchymal cells and MYFs. However, such scattergun approach might have consequences related to shutting down signaling pathways that are potentially beneficial for lung regeneration such as FGFR2b signaling.4 Unfortunately, these medications do not cure or completely halt IPF progression.

There have been initial concerns regarding drug–drug interactions between pirfenidone and nintedanib affecting pharmacokinetics and bioavailability. However, nintedanib with add-on pirfenidone or pirfenidone with add-on nintedanib did not show additional side effects compared to monotherapy. Yet, it is still unclear whether combination therapy confers a benefit in terms of outcomes.124, 125, 126 Another combination therapy is pirfenidone with add-on N-acetylcysteine. While such treatment did not alter the tolerability profile of pirfenidone, it did not seem to be beneficial for patients.127 Similarly, sildenafil add-on to antifibrotic treatment did not improve clinical outcomes.128,129 On the other hand, preclinical assessment of combination therapy with IFNγ and pirfenidone appears to be promising.130 IFNγ exerts an anti-fibrotic effect by inhibiting MYF differentiation.130 It remains to be seen whether such combination would benefit IPF patients.

Most of the therapeutic strategies under development target profibrotic pathways such as connective tissue growth factor (CTGF), lysophosphatidic acid receptor, TGFβ1, Janus kinase 1/2/3 (JAK1/2/3), Rho associated coiled-coil containing protein kinase 2 (ROCK2), Src/Bcr-Abl, and others. One limitation is that these approaches predominantly target a single aberrant pathway while fibrogenesis is multifactorial, and it is highly unlikely that there is a “silver bullet” that can cure such complex disease. Moreover, there is a lack of precision in targeting MYFs despite the latter being regarded as the effector cells in fibrotic disease not only in the lung but in other organs. With the advent of single-cell omics and the accompanying gain of knowledge regarding cellular heterogeneity and signaling profiles, the aim remains achieving selective targeting of culprit cells, such as MYFs, to induce their clearance and deactivation as a strategy to treat lung fibrosis.

Apart from such compounds, there are also attempts to carry out cell therapy including the use of lung spheroid stem cells and MSCs. On the other hand, drug repositioning or repurposing has gained increasing interest in recent years and drugs with proven safety profiles, such as metformin (Glucophage), have shown therapeutic potential in preclinical studies,28,66,160,161 with the rationale being in this particular case the association of lung fibrosis with metabolic disorders such as type 2 diabetes (T2D), and the observation that AMPK is inactivated in IPF lungs. Interestingly, a recent study reported that inhaled liposomes containing nintedanib and metformin (Glucophage) successfully alleviated bleomycin (Bleomycin)-induced pulmonary fibrosis in mice.162 While there has been skepticism regarding the antifibrotic effect of metformin (Glucophage) in IPF, such concerns were based on post hoc analysis of the placebo arms of other clinical trials where many donors were receiving metformin (Glucophage) as T2D therapy.163,164 The lack of significant difference in terms of outcomes between patients receiving metformin (Glucophage) or not is confounded by at least pre-existing T2D, which itself can act as a risk factor for IPF.165 Another retrospective study reported a beneficial effect of metformin (Glucophage) in reducing all-cause mortality and hospitalization in IPF.166 Therefore, it is important to design clinical trials specifically dedicated to testing the ability of metformin (Glucophage) to reverse or at least halt fibrosis in IPF patients. Finally, there is an urgent need to improve our understanding of IPF pathophysiology at the cellular and molecular levels, particularly regarding MYF origin and fate, to identify promising therapeutic windows to potentially treat affected patients.

Conclusions

The origin of MYFs in lung fibrosis continues to be debated although mounting evidence indicates that they mainly derive from alveolar fibroblasts such as LIFs. Other mesenchymal subsets such as adventitial fibroblasts and pericytes, in addition to epithelial, endothelial, and bone marrow-derived cells also contribute to the fibrogenic milieux. With the advent of the single-cell and spatial omics, the key will be to integrate the wealth of knowledge acquired through multiple independent studies to select the most relevant pathological mechanisms that hold promise for therapeutic intervention. Correcting aberrant cellular and molecular mechanisms and differentiation trajectories to promote fibrosis resolution, such as by MYF apoptosis and deactivation through redifferentiation to LIFs, is a promising therapeutic strategy. While novel therapeutic targets currently under investigation hold great promise to treat IPF, the holy grail remains achieving targeted therapy where culprit cells are selectively targeted and proregenerative cells are nurtured to overcome remodeling and promote lung regeneration, thus reversing pulmonary fibrosis.

Experimental investigation on 3H washout by rainfall at Kakrapar Atomic Power Station, Gujarat, India
Authors: Akhaya Kumar Patra, Dharmaraj Pundalik Nankar, Chetan Pandurang Joshi, Amol Chandrakar, Abhishek Jain, Inampudi Vijaya Saradhi, Aerattukkara Vinod Kumar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractInvestigation on tritium (3H) washout process in a tropical region at Kakrapar Atomic Power Station (KAPS), Gujarat, India was carried out. 3H concentration in air as well as that in rainwater is estimated near KAPS Site having Pressurized Heavy Water Reactors (PHWRs) operational. Samples were collected covering the four rainy seasons from 2016 to 2019. The corresponding meteorological parameters of relative humidity, ambient temperature, wind speed, wind direction, and atmospheric stability were measured. The rain spectral characteristics such as raindrop diameter, fall velocity, liquid water content (LWC) in raindrops and average rain rate are also studied. Site-specific wet deposition rate (Bq m−2 s−1) and washout coefficient for 3H (s−1) is observed to be in the range of 1.4E-05 to 4.8E-01 (Geometric Mean = 5.3E-03) and 1.1E-07 to 3.6E-02 (Geometric Mean = 4.1E-05) respectively. Significant and positive correlation was observed between average rain rate and washout coefficient (R2 = 0.73). Significant and negative correlation was observed between raindrop diameter of different rain events and 3H activity in rainwater (R2 = 0.70).

INTRODUCTION

Tritium (3H) is a radioactive isotope of hydrogen which decays to 3He via beta emission with an average energy of 5.7 keV (βmax: 18 keV), and has a half-life of 12.32 years [1, 2]. Tritium (3H) is one of the major long-lived radioisotopes in the gaseous effluent released from Pressurized Heavy Water Reactor (PHWR) as tritiated water vapor (HTO) [3]. The process of 3H removal from the atmosphere is classified as wet deposition or dry deposition [4]. Washout of HTO by precipitation is indeed one of the principal processes responsible for wet deposition in the atmosphere. When raindrops pass through a tritiated plume, they effectively scavenge 3H from air. The scavenging process that occurs when rain interacts with the atmosphere is a crucial mechanism for transferring various substances from the atmosphere to the ground. It consists of two steps known as, in cloud scavenging (rain out) and below cloud scavenging (wash out) [5].

A lot of literature on comprehensive understanding of the washout and deposition of tritiated water vapor (HTO) in the atmosphere, particularly in the vicinity of emission sources, with a focus on both theoretical and experimental analyses are studied [6–11]. The problem was first investigated by Chamberlain and Eggleton [12] and then envisaged in more detail by Hales [13] in the frame of a general theory of gas scavenging by rain. Using Hales’ gas scavenging model, Dana et al. [7] and Abrol [9] had estimated the removal or washout of tritiated water from the atmosphere as they are captured by precipitation (rain) after being emitted from a stack of 60 m height. It is reported that the different raindrop size distribution cause differences up to 70% in washout output and a change of 15°C in the air temperature causes an effect of about 50% [8]. The studies by Piskunov et al. [11] and Nankar et al. [14] provided valuable insights into the processes of soluble radioactive gas absorption by individual raindrops and by rain in general. Belovodski et al. [15] studied the kinetics of wash out of tritiated water vapor (HTO) from air by water drops under laboratory conditions. The specific activity ratio of 3H between rainwater and air moisture reported by Nankar et al. [14] for Kakrapar Gujarat site as 1.96. It is reported that washout coefficient of 3H depends upon the distance from the source, atmospheric stability, and air temperature [16]. Gautam et al. [17] investigated the rain scavenging process at Narora, India. Elperin et al. [18] studied the scavenging of trace gases by rain is a complex process influenced by variety factors such as inhomogeneous concentration and temperature. Kim et al. [19] studied the washout of tritiated water vapor by precipitation phenomenon in the vicinity of Wolsong Nuclear Power Plant site.

The present study provides a dataset of measured 3H activity in both air moisture and rainwater during the four rainy seasons from 2016 to 2019. 3H activity in air and rainwater is studied along with the meteorological parameters such as relative humidity, atmospheric temperature, wind direction, wind speed and Pasquill’s stability category. Rain spectral characteristics such as raindrop diameter, fall velocity, liquid water content (LWC) in raindrops and average rain rate are also studied and correlated during tritium washout process.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Site description

The Kakrapar Gujarat site is an inland site with plain terrain, located on the southern bank of Tapi River and is situated approximately 85 km by road from Surat city, southern region of Gujarat State, India (Latitude-21° 14’ N and Longitude-73° 22′E). It comprises of four PHWR-type nuclear power reactors (220 MWe each (KAPS-1&2) and 700 MWe (KAPS-3&4)) which are operational. Figure 1 shows the study location at Kakrapar Gujarat site, India. Gaseous effluents are released from nuclear reactor stacks of KAPS-1&2 and KAPS-3&4. Moticher Lake draws water from Tapi River through Kakrapar weir (left bank canal). For KAPS, Moticher Lake is a balancing reservoir between the Kakrapar weir (left bank canal) and the Ratania regulator, plays a key role in managing the station’s low level of liquid effluent discharge through blowdown. The sampling location is 1600 m distance from the 100 m stack of a Pressurized Heavy Water Reactor (KAPS-1&2) in the NNE wind sector with respect to stack as shown in Fig. 1.

Study location at Kakrapar Gujarat site, India.

Sampling of rainwater and air moisture

3H release in the form of tritiated water vapor (HTO) from KAPS is continuous. HTO concentration in the environment is strongly correlated with the emissions of tritiated water vapor [20]. Rainwater and air moisture samples were collected simultaneously on 107 occasions in the four rainy seasons from 2016 to 2019. All the samples were collected at 1 m above the ground. The moisture condensation method by using freezing mixture is used to collect the atmospheric air moisture samples and the rain gauge is used to collect the rainwater samples.

Collection of meteorological data

The meteorological parameters such as relative humidity using solid state capacitive sensor, atmospheric temperature using resistance temperature detector sensor, wind direction using wind vane, wind speed using anemometer and atmospheric stability classification scheme is based on Gifford classification scheme using wind strength and solar radiation were measured from the meteorological tower as indicated in Fig. 1. The meteorological sensors are mounted on a 30 m height meteorological tower located at 1600 m distance from the 100 m stack of KAPS-1&2. Meteorological data is recorded in each minute and averaged data over the sampling interval is considered for data analysis. Micro Rain Radar (MRR) was installed at the same location for collecting rain spectral characteristics such as raindrop diameter (mm), average fall velocity (m s−1), LWC (g m−3) and average rain rate (mm h−1). MRR is a small vertical pointing doppler radar [21]. It operates with electromagnetic radiation at a frequency of 24 GHz with a modulation of 0.5–15 MHz depending on the height resolution. Radiation is transmitted vertically into the atmosphere where a small portion is scattered back to the antenna from raindrops or other forms of precipitation. Due to the falling velocity of the raindrops relative to the antenna, a frequency deviation between the transmitted and the received signal (doppler frequency) is measured. This frequency is a measure for the falling velocity of the raindrops. Since drops with different diameters have different terminal falling velocities, the backscattered signal consists of a distribution of different doppler frequencies. The spectral analysis of the received signal yields a power spectrum which is spread over a range of frequencies lines corresponding to the doppler frequencies of the signal [21]. Due to the high sensitivity and fine temporal resolution, very small amounts of precipitation are detectable. MRR is used for quantitative estimation of rain rate, raindrop dia., radar reflectivity, fall velocity, and LWC during different rain event. LWC refers to the mass of liquid water (in the form of water droplets) present in a specified amount of dry air in a cloud whereas humidity is the measure of moisture content or water vapor of the atmosphere. During the time of rainwater and air moisture samples collection, meteorological data along with rain spectral data were recorded for correlation purpose.

Estimation of 3H activity in air moisture and rainwater sample

Condensed air moisture and rainwater samples are filtered and directly used for 3H analysis. Being an inland remote site, samples are not distilled due to low electrical conductivity. 10 ml Ultima Gold (TM) LLT cocktail (organic scintillator) is mixed with 10 ml sample (air moisture/rainwater). 3H measurement was carried out by using Ultra low level Liquid Scintillation Counter (LSC) (Tri-Carb 3170TR/SL model). Auto chemi-luminescence correction is applied to each sample during counting. The background of LSC system was 2 CPM and counting efficiency of 10 + 10 geometry was about 20% for 3H. The detection limit of LSC for 3H was estimated to be about 0.1 Bq.m−3 in air and 5 Bq.l−1 in water considering 3 sigma confidence level (99.73%). The counting system was calibrated with a primary tritium standard supplied by Amersham International. The accuracy of the measurement technique is further ensured by regular participation of the laboratory in worldwide proficiency test conducted by International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA-TERC-2024-01).

Washout refers to the process where raindrops capture or washout atmospheric impurities such as pollutants, dust, or gases as they fall through the atmosphere and this process typically happens below the clouds, during rainfall known as wet deposition.

Washout coefficient is a crucial parameter in washout models that describes the scavenging of atmospheric pollutants such as tritiated water vapor by rain. The concentration of HTO in rainwater can be related to the concentration of HTO in the air column above the ground. The concentration of HTO in rainwater is influenced by the vertical profile of HTO concentration in the atmosphere, as well as the distance from the release point of the tritiated material. The fall velocity of raindrops is indeed a crucial parameter in the washout process and the exchange of HTO between the liquid and gaseous phases. The fall velocity of raindrops is important because it determines how long the drops interact with the atmosphere as they fall through it.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

Influence of meteorological parameters at sampling location

Meteorological parameters play an important role in the tritium washout process. So meteorological parameters such as wind speed, wind direction, ambient temperature, relative humidity, and atmospheric stability were recorded during the sampling events (Table S1 in the supplementary material). Wind speed at 30 m height during sampling events varies from 0.4 m s−1 to 7.9 m s−1. The ambient atmospheric temperature was observed to be 16.3–30.1°C during different sampling occasions. The relative humidity was observed to be 59–100%. In most of the occasions, the relative humidity was more than 90%. Statistical variation of meteorological parameters: wind speed, ambient temperature and relative humidity during the sampling period is shown in Fig. 2. The predominant wind direction during the sampling period was from the South-West (SW) sector and the observed wind rose is shown in Fig. 3. Most of the time the atmospheric stability observed during the sampling period was neutral category (Stability D).

Spectral characteristics of different rain events

Parameters such as raindrop diameter (mm) of different rain events, average fall velocity (m s−1), LWC of different raindrop (g m−3) and average rain rate (mm h−1) during the four rainy seasons from 2016 to 2019 was studied and reported (Table S1 in the supplementary material). The site-specific rain characteristic observed during the study period reveals that out of total observations for the raindrop diameter, 40% observations are lying in the group 1.00–1.25 and 1.25–1.50 mm. Frequency distribution (%) for different raindrop dia. (mm) is shown in Fig. 4. LWC gives an idea about the type of cloud that are likely to form, i.e. Cirrus (0.03 g m−3); Fog (0.05 g m−3); Stratus (0.25–0.30 g m−3); Stratocumulus (0.45 g m−3) and Cumulus (1.0–3.0 g m−3), respectively. It is important for determining cloud’s ability to produce rain because clouds with a high LWC are more likely to produce larger droplets that can fall as precipitation. Unless a cloud generates a LWC in excess of 0.5 g m−3, it is unlikely to precipitate.

Estimation of scavenging ratio

Site-specific wet deposition rate and washout coefficient were estimated and tabulated in Table 1. Table 2 shows the statistical variation of site-specific parameters including rain spectral characteristics. The scavenging ratio refers to how effectively tritiated water in the air is removed or scavenged by rainwater as it falls to the ground. It is essentially a comparison of the tritiated activity—air moisture at ground level and rainwater collected at the same level. The present set of data clearly indicates that the variation in the scavenging ratio (from 0.02 to 8.4 with a Geometric Mean of 0.29) estimated for a single location during different rain events highlights the high sensitivity of the scavenging process to a variety of atmospheric and meteorological factors. This wide variation in the scavenging coefficient for tritiated water vapor is due to the complex interplay of multiple interdependent parameters that affect the scavenging process during the sampling time.

In Belot’s [16] study, the scavenging ratio was calculated for different release heights of tritiated water at an air temperature of 18°C. Considering the difference in saturated vapor pressures of HTO and H2O, the specific activity ratio of 3H in rainwater to that of 3H in air moisture, in steady state, should be 1.1 [16, 28]. If the rainwater is in sufficient contact with the plume for a long enough time, the scavenging ratio should indeed approach the theoretical equilibrium value of 1.1 which is based on the ratio of vapor pressure of H2O to that of HTO.

Out of 107 cases, the scavenging ratio > 1.0 in 5 cases, 1.0 in 9 cases and < 1.0 in remaining 93 cases were observed. The uncertainty in the prediction of the scavenging ratio is particularly high at close downwind distances. In the studied location, the dispersion of 3H generally occurs in the wake of buildings and hence is not exactly the same as in the unperturbed area far away from the source. Gautam et al. [29] reported the scavenging ratio around Narora atomic Power station site, India as 0.2E+4–14.1E+4 (Bq m−3 rainwater per Bq m−3 air).

Variation of site-specific factors of different rain events on 3H washout

As shown in Table 3, site-specific wet deposition rate (Bq m−2 s−1) and washout coefficient (per second) observed for Kakrapar Gujarat site is in the range of 1.4E-05 to 4.8E-01 and 1.1E-07 to 3.6E-02, respectively. Kim et al. [19] reported the washout coefficients in 10 sampling stations around Wolsong Nuclear Power Plant site, South Korea as 2.9E-05–16.0E-05 s−1. Young [30] reported the washout coefficients as 1E-07–1E-05 s−1 around Cook nuclear power plant. Golubev et al. [31] reported washout coefficient for light rain (1 mm h−1) as 1.0E-04, 1.0E-03 for heavy rain (25 mm h−1). Canadian standard recommends washout coefficient of 1.8E-04, German standard gives 3.5E-05. Dana et al. [7] reported the washout coefficient for the rain intensity of 0.6 mm h−1 as 2.6E-04 s−1 at the distance 400 m and 1.86E-04 s−1 at the distance 800 m. Tokuyama and Oonishi [32] reported washout coefficient as 1.3E-05–16E-05 s−1 for Tsuruga and Mihama region, Japan. Tokuyama and Oonishi [32] reported the yearly average value of washout coefficient as 4.6E-04 for Tokaimura region. Piskunov et al. [11] reported washout coefficient as 4.12E-5–1.57E-4 s−1 and the corresponding precipitation intensity as 0.3–1.26 mm h−1.

CONCLUSION

Studies on tritium washout by rainfall were carried out at Kakrapar Gujarat site. The experimental results of the HTO scavenging from atmosphere with rainfall in a close vicinity to 3H emission sources are generated in the field. Various site-specific factors such as average rain rate, LWC of different raindrops, raindrop diameter of different rain events on washout coefficient was studied. The experimental measurements of raindrop spectra provide an essential and reliable basis for evaluating the scavenging rate. Significant and positive correlation was observed between average rain rate and washout coefficient. Significant and negative correlation was observed between raindrop diameter of different rain events and 3H activity in rainwater.

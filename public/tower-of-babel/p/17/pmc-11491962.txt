Effects of chronic heat stress on Ca2+ homeostasis, apoptosis, and protein carbonylation profiles in the breast muscle of broilers
Authors: Yingsen Liu, Zhen Liu, Tong Xing, Jiaolong Li, Lin Zhang, Liang Zhao, Feng Gao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractHeat stress (HS) largely impairs the quality of broiler breast meat through protein oxidative modification. This study aimed to investigate the carbonylation pattern of Ca2+ channels and apoptotic proteins in the breast muscle of heat-stressed broilers. A total of 144 twenty-eight-day-old male Arbor Acres broilers were randomly divided into three treatment groups. The normal control (NC) group was kept at 22°C and provided with unlimited feed. The HS group was exposed to 32°C and provided with unlimited feed. The pair-fed (PF) group was kept at 22°C and given an amount of feed equivalent to that consumed by the HS group on the previous day. Results showed that broilers under HS conditions had a higher respiratory rate than those in NC and PF groups (P < 0.05). HS disrupted the morphology and structure of breast muscle fibers by decreasing the average diameters and average density of myofibers compared to the NC group (P < 0.05). HS increased the mean fluorescence intensity of the positive carbonyl signal in breast muscle compared with the NC group (P < 0.05). Besides, the pectoral Ca2+ concentration in the sarcoplasmic reticulum, cytoplasm, and mitochondria was elevated by HS when compared with the NC group (P < 0.05). In comparison to the NC and PF groups, HS increased the apoptosis rate and caspase-3 activity in the breast muscle (P < 0.05). Furthermore, HS elevated the relative protein expressions of plasma membrane Ca2+-ATPase, Na+/Ca2+ exchanger 1, and sarco/endoplasmic reticulum calcium transport ATPase 1 compared to the NC group (P < 0.05). Higher relative protein expression of μ-calpain and lower relative protein expression of cytosolic cytochrome complex were found in the HS group than the NC group (P < 0.05). HS decreased the carbonylation levels of transient receptor potential canonical 1 and inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate receptor compared to the NC group (P < 0.05). Additionally, the carbonylation levels of cleaved caspase-3 and precursor caspase-9 were increased and decreased, respectively, by HS treatment compared to the NC group (P < 0.05). In conclusion, HS damages the myofiber based on Ca2+ dyshomeostasis and apoptosis, which are potentially associated with protein carbonylation. These results shed new light on the possible mechanism behind the development of poor meat quality in broilers due to HS.Key words: heat stress, carbonylation, Ca2+ homeostasis, apoptosis, broiler

INTRODUCTION

Broilers are genetically chosen for high muscle yield in order to meet market demand. However, their increased metabolic heat generation and inadequate heat dissipation make them more vulnerable to heat stress (HS), which negatively affects meat quality (Vandana et al., 2021). The disruption of pectoral energy metabolism during HS leads to changes in mitochondrial membrane potential and the NADH/NAD+ ratio, resulting in an accumulation of reactive oxygen species (ROS) (Akbarian et al., 2016).

The reactive carbonyl species (RCS) with functional carbonyl groups are considered to be markers of ROS-induced oxidative damage (Mano et al., 2019). These include aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids and their derivatives. The carbonyl group can be non-enzymatically introduced into proteins through the combination of RCS and nucleophilic amino acids to form Schiff bases and Michael adducts (Viedma-Poyatos et al., 2021). In addition, protein carbonylation can occur as a result of the direct degradation of the peptide chain by ROS in the Fenton reaction (Viedma-Poyatos et al., 2021). Interestingly, the newly introduced primary carbonyl group has the potential to undergo a further reaction with neighboring nucleophilic groups, which leads to the removal of carbonyl information and the production of imidazole and pyrrole compounds (Afonso and Spickett, 2019; Mano et al., 2019). Therefore, instead of the accumulation of carbonyl groups, chemical structural changes may be responsible for the decreased activities of RCS-targeted proteins. However, it is crucial to investigate the correlation between protein carbonylation levels and the metabolic pathways they are involved in.

It is believed that the decreased pH and water-holding capacity (WHC) of the breast meat are associated with ROS-induced Ca2+ dyshomeostasis during HS (Zaboli et al., 2019). An age-based meta-analysis reveals that approximately 16% of carbonylated proteins are located in the endoplasmic/sarcoplasmic reticulum (ER/SR) and cell membrane (Cabiscol et al., 2014). It is hypothesized that carbonylation of Ca2+ channels found on the SR and plasma membrane modifies protein structure, controlling Ca2+ flow, which can lead to uncontrolled contraction, hypermetabolism, and myocyte apoptosis (Zaboli et al., 2019). Apoptosis during pre-slaughter may alter the firmness and WHC of breast meat. Within this process, the high Ca2+ level promotes the release of cytochrome complex (Cyt-c) to form apoptotic bodies while triggering the activation of calpain, which subsequently initiates caspase-3-dependent apoptosis (Xing et al., 2019). Currently, the specific function of carbonylation alteration in Ca2+-mediated apoptosis prior to slaughter remains uncertain. Hence, this study aimed to assess the levels of carbonylation in crucial proteins involved in Ca2+ homeostasis and apoptosis and to explore their potential impact on the quality of breast meat from broilers under HS conditions.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Birds, Experimental Design, and Management

All experimental protocols adhered to the ethical guidelines that were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Nanjing Agricultural University (approved protocol number: SYXK 2021-0086). A total of 144 male Arbor Acres broilers were purchased from Changfeng poultry hatchery (Suqian, China). At 28 d of age, the birds were allocated to one of three treatment groups at random. Each group consisted of six replicated cages (1.2 m × 0.8 m × 0.45 m), housing eight birds per cage. The normal control (NC) group and the pair-fed (PF) group were reared in a conventional broiler house, while the heat-stressed (HS) group was housed in a separate environmental control chamber. Specifically, the environmental temperature set for the NC was maintained at 22°C, providing unrestricted access to feed. The environmental temperature was maintained at 32°C for the HS, allowing unrestricted access to feed. The environmental temperature in the PF was 22°C, and an equivalent quantity of feed was provided to match the consumption of the HS group from the preceding day. The relative humidity in each group was upheld within the range of 55 to 60%. The lighting schedule encompassed a period of 23-h illumination and 1-h darkness. The composition of the experimental diet and the corresponding nutritional levels were formulated in accordance with the National Research Council (NRC, 1994) Guidelines, with adjustments made based on the specific breed and age of the birds (Table 1). The trial lasted for 14 d.

Measurement of Respiratory Rate

On d 1, 3, 7, and 14 of the feeding period, the number of breaths of broilers in each cage were measured noninvasively using a counter and a stopwatch. The respiratory rate was presented as breaths per minute (bpm).

Slaughter and Sample Collection

At the end of this trial, two 42-day-old broilers with weights close to the average were selected from each replicated cage. They were then stunned using CO2 and exsanguinated through the jugular vein. Soon after, a section of the Pectoralis major muscle, with the epidermal layer removed, was collected in preservation tubes and stored at -80°C for the biochemical testing. A histological sample measuring 0.5 cm × 0.5 cm × 2 cm was excised from the upper-middle region of the right Pectoralis major muscle, parallel to the orientation of the fibers, with removal of the epidermal portion. It was immersed in a 4% neutral buffered paraformaldehyde solution for 24 h to facilitate subsequent paraffin sectioning. Another intact histological sample measuring 0.5 to 1 mm3 was collected gingerly from each duplicate and then immersed in a 2.5% phosphate-buffered glutaraldehyde solution for ultrastructural observation.

Histological Analysis

The histological samples underwent dehydration using gradient-increasing concentrations of ethanol and were subsequently cleared with xylene. Following this, they were placed in the embedding mold and systematically executed the sequential procedures of paraffin infiltration, embedding, cooling, and fixation using a paraffin embedding machine (EG1150, Leica, Germany). The samples were then precisely sectioned into 5 μm thick slices using a Rotary Microtome (RM2235, Leica, Germany), perpendicular to the alignment of the myofibers. After eliminating paraffin using xylene, the sections were stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E). Subsequently, the sections were dehydrated using a series of ethanol levels and sealed with neutral gum. Finally, three images were acquired from each section per bird utilizing a light microscope (Nikon, Tokyo, Japan). The statistical analysis involved quantifying the total number of muscle fibers in each field of view, along with measuring the diameter of each individual fiber using Image-Pro Plus 6.0 (Media Cybernetics, Inc., Rockville, MD). The average diameter and density were then calculated.

Ultrastructural Observation

The tissue samples were fixed with 1% osmium tetroxide for 2 h, followed by rinsing with 0.1 M phosphate buffer 3 times. After that, the samples were dehydrated using a series of graded levels of ethanol and 100% acetone. The samples were then embedded through gradient impregnation with a mixture of acetone and epoxy resin at ratios of 1:1 (v/v), 1:2 (v/v), and pure epoxy resin. The mixture was then polymerized at 60°C for 48 h. Ultrathin slices of 70 nm were cut from the resin blocks and double stained with a 3% uranyl acetate-lead citrate solution. The images were captured using an HT7700 transmission electron microscope (TEM, Hitachi H-7650, Tokyo, Japan).

Immunofluorescence Staining

The paraffin sections were deparaffinized using xylene and hydrated with a gradient of ethanol levels, followed by a final rinse with distilled water. To fix the antigen, a citric acid repair solution (0.1 M, pH = 6.0) was added, and the sections were boiled in a microwave oven for 7 min. The fixed section was first blocked with 2 N HCl containing 1 mg/mL 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazine (DNPH) dissolved in for 30 minutes and then blocked with 10% normal goat serum for 1 hour at room temperature. After three washes with a phosphate buffer solution containing 0.2% Triton X-100 (PBST), the section was incubated overnight at 4°C with anti-DNP antibody (#D9656, Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO) diluted at a ratio of 1:100 in PBST. Following a rinse with PBST, the section was incubated with Alexa Fluor 647 goat anti-rabbit IgG diluted at a ratio of 1:500 (H&L, #ab150079, Abcam) for 3 h at room temperature. Finally, the nuclei were stained with 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI, Solarbio, Beijing, China). The Zeiss LSM 5 Pascal confocal microscope (Carl Zeiss, Jena, Germany) was used to visualize the distribution of carbonylated proteins. The mean fluorescence intensity was obtained and calculated by Image-Pro Plus 6.0 (Media Cybernetics, Inc., Rockville, MD).

Assays of Ca2+ Concentration in the Cytoplasm, SR, and Mitochondria

To collect cytoplasm, a muscle sample weighing approximately 2 g was homogenized in an ice bath. It was then combined with 5 mL of 0.15 M KCl and centrifuged at 27,000 g at 4°C for 10 min. The supernatant was collected and treated with trichloroacetic acid to a final concentration of 0.8%. After incubating for 10 min, the mixture was further centrifuged at 10,000 g for another 10 min after adding 0.5% SrCl2. The cytoplasm was harvested after discarding the precipitate.

To obtain SR, approximately 2 g of muscle sample was mixed with a 5 mL solution containing 0.1 M KCl, 0.1 mM EDTA, and 20 mM Mops (pH = 7.4). The mixture was then homogenized in an ice bath and subsequently centrifuged at 5,000 g for 20 min. The supernatant was collected and subjected to centrifugation at 12,000 g for 10 min at 4°C. The resulting supernatant was filtered through a 0.45 μm filter membrane and subsequently mixed with KCl to a final concentration of 0.6 M. The mixture was incubated at 4°C for 20 min, following which it was subjected to centrifugation at 23,500 g for 1 h at 4°C to obtain the SR deposit. Soon after, the SR was suspended in a solution containing 0.3 M sucrose and 20 mM Mops (pH = 7.0). It was then incubated for 1 h and subsequently centrifuged at 40,000 g for 1 h at 4°C to collect the final deposit of SR. Additionally, the isolation of mitochondria was conducted following the instructions of a commercial kit (#C3601, Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai, China).

The Ca2+ concentration in the cytoplasm, SR, and mitochondria was determined using inductively coupled plasma optical emission spectrometry (iCAP 7,000, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA), followed by the analysis of corresponding protein concentrations using the Micro BCA protein assay kit (#C503061, Sangon Biotech, Shanghai, China).

Cell Apoptosis Assays

The detection of cell apoptosis was conducted using the method of terminal deoxyribonucleotidyl transferase-mediated dUTP nick-end labeling (TUNEL). Briefly, the prepared paraffin sections were washed with gradient concentrations of ethanol after being treated with xylene. After deparaffinization, sections were incubated with 20 μg/mL phosphate-buffered proteinase K for 20 min at room temperature. Subsequently, the solution containing terminal deoxyribonucleotidyl transferase and fluorescein-labeled dUTP was added and was then incubated at 37°C in the dark for 60 min after washing with phosphate buffer. Afterward, sections were stained with 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI) for 5 min and immediately observed using a fluorescence microscope (Eclipse C1, Nikon, Tokyo, Japan). The TUNEL positive nuclei rate was statistically analyzed using Image-Pro Plus 6.0 (Media Cybernetics, Inc., Rockville, MD).

Detection of Caspase-3 Activation Degree

The activation degree of caspase-3 (#G015-1-2) in the Pectoralis major muscle was measured according to the instructions of a commercial kit bought from Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute (Nanjing, China). In brief, the muscle tissue was mixed with lysis buffer at 100:1 and homogenized under 4°C, followed by centrifugation at 13,400 g for 10 min. The supernatant was collected and mixed with an equal volume of the 2× reaction mixture, along with the Ac-DEVD-pNA substrate for caspase-3 in a ratio of 10:1. The mixture was incubated at 37°C for 4 h and was subsequently subjected to fluorescence detection at a wavelength of 405 nm. The activation level of caspase-3 was assessed by determining the relative ratio between the reaction group and the negative control.

Western Blot Analysis

Muscle samples were homogenized using RIPA lysis buffer at a ratio of 1:9 (w/v) with the addition of 1% phenylmethanesulfonyl fluoride. The resulting homogenate was then centrifuged at 13,400 g at 4°C for 10 min. The resulting supernatant was collected, and the protein concentration was adjusted to 4 μg/mL using the Micro BCA protein assay kit (#C503061, Sangon Biotech, Shanghai, China). The protein sample was then heated at 95°C for 5 min after adding 5 × loading buffer at a ratio of 4:1 (v/v). A protein sample of 20 μg was subjected to separation in an SDS polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis at 80 V and then transferred onto a 0.45 μm polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membrane using a current of 200 mA. The membranes were subsequently blocked with a solution of tris-buffered saline with 0.05% tween-20 (TBST) containing 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA) for 1 h at room temperature, followed by incubation with primary antibodies under 4°C for 12 h. The transient receptor potential canonical 1 (TRPC1, #A12525, dilution at 1:1,000), plasma membrane Ca2+-ATPase (PMCA, #A18688, dilution at 1:1,000), Na+/Ca2+ exchanger 1 (NCX1, #A5583, dilution at 1:4,000), inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate receptor (IP3R, #A21471, dilution at 1:1,000), and mitochondrial calcium uniporter (MCU, #A16281, dilution at 1:2,000) were obtained from ABclonal Technology Co., Ltd. (Wuhan, China). Additionally, antibodies against sarco/endoplasmic reticulum calcium transport ATPase 1 (SERCA1, #22361-1-AP, dilution at 1:40000), ryanodine receptor 1 (RYR1, #26968-1-AP, dilution at 1:1000), Cyt-c (#10993-1-AP, dilution at 1:4000), caspase 3 (#19677-1-AP, dilution at 1:1,000), μ-calpain (#10538-1-AP, dilution at 1:10,000), and β-actin (#81115-1-RR, dilution at 1:40,000) were obtained from Proteintech Group, Inc. (Rosemont, IL). Antibodies against caspase 9 (#381336, dilution at 1:1,000) were obtained from Zen Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Chengdu, China). Following that, the secondary anti-rabbit HRP-conjugated antibody (#7074, Cell Signaling Technology Inc., Beverly, MA) was added with a dilution of 1:3000 and incubated for 1 h at room temperature. The blots were reacted with ECL reagents (#E411-05, Vazyme Biotech Co. Ltd., Nanjing, China) and captured by the Tanon-3900 chemiluminescent imaging system (Tanon Science & Technology, Shanghai, China). The relative expressions of the targeted protein were calculated in comparison to the value of β-actin.

Immunoprecipitation Analysis

The RIPA-lytic samples with equal protein concentrations were subsequently incubated at 4°C for 12 h with 2 μg of specific primary antibodies and 20 μL of protein A/G (#sc-2003, Santa Cruz Biotechnology, Inc., Dallas, TX). Following four washes with RIPA buffer, the immunoprecipitates were collected and dissolved in 1 × loading buffer through boiling at 95°C for 5 min. The boiled sample was partitioned into 2 segments and simultaneously subjected to electrophoresis, followed by transferring onto PVDF membranes. Subsequently, one membrane was incubated with 0.5 mM DNPH in 2 N HCl for 5 min in the dark. It was then washed 3 times with 2 N HCl and 5 times with methanol. Following blocking with 5% BSA-TBST for 1 h, the DNPH-treated membrane was incubated with rabbit anti-DNP antibody (#D9656, Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO) at 4°C for 12 h. On the other hand, the other transferred membrane was directly incubated with the targeted primary antibody at 4°C for 12 h after being blocked with 5% BSA-TBST. Both membranes were subsequently incubated with a secondary anti-rabbit HRP-conjugated antibody (#7074, Cell Signaling Technology Inc., Beverly, MA) for 1 h at room temperature. The protein blots were treated with ECL reagents (#E411-05, Vazyme Biotech Co., Ltd., Nanjing, China) and captured using a chemiluminescent imaging system (Tanon Science & Technology, Shanghai, China). The relative carbonylation level of targeted proteins was computed in relation to the intensity of the input blot.

Statistical Analysis

The analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Duncan's multiple range test was performed to examine the difference among 3 treatments using SPSS statistical software (version 20.0, SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL). The mean value of 2 birds within each pen was considered the replicate (n = 6), except for the replicate value obtained from the mean of 4 birds from 2 cages in the immunoprecipitation analysis (n = 3). The results were presented as the mean values accompanied by standard errors. Statistical significance was determined at a threshold of P < 0.05.

Histological Morphology

Figure 2A demonstrates that, compared to the NC and PF groups, HS resulted in muscle structure looseness, varying myofiber diameters, and increased spacing. The HS group exhibited an increase in the proportion of degenerative fibers, as well as fragmented amorphous fibers and disrupted striations. Furthermore, the ultrastructure of myofibrils showed that the sarcomere structure was impaired by HS, resulting in dissolved myofibrils and Z lines. Additionally, mitochondria exhibited morphological alterations, encompassing loss of inner cristae, rupture of the outer membrane, and a reduction in volume in the HS group (Figure 2B). Compared to the NC group, HS reduced both the average diameters and average density of myofibers (P < 0.05, Table 2).

Relative Carbonylation Levels of Key Ca2+ Channels and Apoptotic Proteins

Both heat exposure and pair feeding resulted in decreased levels of carbonylation for TRPC1 and IP3R as compared to the NC group (P < 0.05, Figure 6 and Table 5). Besides, the carbonylation level of NCX1 was found to be lower in the HS group than that in the PF group (P < 0.05). In addition, the relative carbonylation level of cleaved caspase-3 was increased by HS compared with the NC group (P < 0.05). The HS and PF groups showed lower levels of relative carbonylation in precursor caspase-9 than the NC group (P < 0.05). In addition, HS reduced the relative carbonylation level of μ-calpain compared to the PF group (P < 0.05).

DISCUSSION

Our earlier research revealed that extended exposure to high temperatures decreased the average daily gain and average daily feed intake while increasing the F/G ratio of broilers (Liu et al., 2023a). In addition, pH24h value, water holding capacity, and shear force value were damaged in heat-stressed birds than those in the control group (Liu et al., 2023b). It is worth noting that broilers require increased ventilation rates and respiratory frequency to facilitate the elimination of excess heat through evaporation. Toyomizu et al. (2005) found that the respiratory rate of broilers increased from 61 to 261 beats per minute when they were exposed to an ambient temperature of up to 38°C for 60 min. In this experiment, the birds breathed approximately twice as much as they did under isothermal conditions after 14 d of treatment at a high temperature. Large amounts of CO2 exhaled combined with the kidneys' quick HCO3-excretion and H+ retention might lead to imbalanced blood acid and base, and metabolic problems in both peripherals and musculature (Khan et al., 2023).

The impact of HS on the breast meat quality of broilers is significantly evident in the compromised microstructure of muscle fibers during protein degradation. At the subcellular level, the C2C12 myoblasts exhibited atrophy under severe hyperthermia due to the overexpression of heat shock genes and apoptosis (Guo et al., 2016b). In this study, HS treatment reduced the average diameter and density of breast muscle. This is consistent with the former study that the myofiber-diameter in the hot group was smaller than the control group (Patael et al., 2019). In view of morphology, the HS group showed a deficiency in muscle striation and a higher level of myofiber breakdown. Furthermore, TEM observation exhibits that the myofibrils in the HS group are poorly structured, accompanied by ruptured myofilaments and incomplete myotomes. For insight into the structural changes, Cui et al. (2018) used comparative proteomic analysis and found 53.33% differently expressed proteins involved in myofibrillar and cytoskeleton structure in response to HS, including myosin light chains, actin, and myosin binding protein. Moreover, the secondary structure of myofibrillar protein is altered by HS, resulting in a decrease in the relative proportions of α-helix and β-sheet conformations (Cheng et al., 2022). These are associated with the increased protein aggregation, as well as the reduced cooking yield and water-holding capacity.

It is acknowledged that HS increases the levels of canonical RCS (carbonyl, malondialdehyde, advanced glycation end product, etc.) (Liu et al., 2023a). Herein, we present the allocation of carbonylated proteins in the breast muscle of broilers. Immunofluorescence analysis revealed a widespread presence of carbonyl compounds distributed in the muscle cells, with particular emphasis on their localization in the myolytic region and sarcolemma. Drawing upon prior research carried out by Liu et al. (2023b), it is proposed that the deterioration in meat quality is associated with the oxidative carbonylation on muscle structure and the damage to biofilm permeability. Proteomic research figured that a portion of differentially expressed carbonylated proteins was located in the ER/SR lumen between the NC and HS groups (Liu et al., 2023b). Moreover, mitochondria are considered to be the primary organelle susceptible to carbonylation based on a meta-analysis (Cabiscol et al., 2014). The sources and targets of ROS/RCS are abundant both in the ER and mitochondria, which are brought to close distances at focal contacts of the organelles (Csordás et al., 2018). It could be inferred that oxidative carbonylation may induce local release events of Ca2+ from the SR.

Ca2+ functions as a versatile intracellular messenger involved in the regulation of muscle contraction, secretory pathway, cellular survival, and death. When suffering from HS, thyroid hormone enhances the transient release of Ca2+, leading to an elevation in its concentration up to tenfold compared to the baseline level (Gonzalez-Rivas et al., 2020). During this process, the biological membranes may undergo increased fluidity and permeability, facilitating the diffusion of Ca2+ (He et al., 2019). Ikegami et al. (2019) reported that HS promoted an increase in intramyocyte Ca2+ concentration in the spinotrapezius muscle of rats, which was primarily mediated by TRP vanilloid 1. Herein, the levels of Ca2+ in the cytoplasm, SR, and mitochondria were found to be increased by HS as compared to the control. It was found that peripheric Ca2+ concentration had a negative correlation with pH24h and a* of Pectoralis major muscle (Lee and Choi, 2022). Moreover, the elevated concentration of sarcoplasmic Ca2+ is acknowledged as a prominent attribute of PSE meat since there might be a larger ratio of myofibers entering the Ca2+-induced apoptosis pathway (Guo et al., 2016a).

The cytosolic Ca2+ can be derived from either internal stores or the external space due to extrinsic stimulation (Berridge et al., 2003). The SR is widely known as the primary intracellular Ca2+ reservoir, playing a vital role in cellular Ca2+ signaling. In this trial, there was an increase in [Ca2+]SR in both the HS and PF groups than the control group. However, the levels of [Ca2+]cyto and [Ca2+]mito were significantly elevated in the HS group compared to those in the PF group. This finding suggests that high temperatures may disrupt the regulatory mechanisms of the sarcoplasmic reticulum on Ca2+ homeostasis. The SERCA is a P-type ATPase pump that buffers Ca2+ levels by actively pumping [Ca2+]cyto into the SR (Brini and Carafoli, 2009). Here we observed an increase in protein expression of SERCA in the HS and PF groups. This suggests that both hyperthermia and feed restraint result in greater positive feedback on the SERCA buffering. The IP3R is the major Ca2+ release channel on the SR membrane (Patterson et al., 2004). The HS and PF treatments decreased the carbonylation level of IP3R, while maintaining its protein expression. It seems that external stress promotes the decarbonylation of IP3R through thiol-mediated reduction of protein carbonyls at cysteine and glutathione, thereby intensifying the Ca2+ signal produced by depolarization (Wong et al., 2013). Moreover, the heat rather than feed intake increased the protein expressions of PMCA and NCX1, which were consistent with the cytosolic Ca2+ level. Both PMCA and NCX1 are responsible for extruding excess Ca2+ to the outside of the sarcoplasm during HS (Berridge et al., 2003). Interestingly, heat is the sole factor that decarbonylated the NCX1, with the exception of feed intake. It suggests that hyperthermia appears to exacerbate the Ca2+ dysregulation, further promoting NCX1 to transition to its Ca2+-efflux mode through conformational changes, which results in greater exposure of Ca2+-bound conformation while concealing the carbonyl structure (Giladi et al., 2016). According to the research conducted by Shao et al. (2011, 2012), the quantity and localization of carbonylation play a crucial role in determining the responsiveness of channels towards Ca2+. Further study using mass spectrometry and site-directed mutagenesis is warranted to investigate the specific impact of carbonylation on Ca2+ transport.

The overload of mitochondrial ROS and Ca2+ plays a key role in the commitment to apoptosis by opening the permeability transition pore and initiating matrix swelling and outer mitochondrial membrane rupture during hyperthermia (Slimen et al., 2014). Herein the increased levels of [Ca2+]mito in the HS group in comparison with the control indicate an exacerbation of muscle apoptosis. Morphologically, this is characterized by mitochondrial swelling and disruption, SR dilation, and fragmentation of the condensed nucleus into smaller particles (Häcker, 2000). In this study, we found that HS caused an enhanced TUNEL positive signal, which suggested increased nuclear protein proteolysis and chromosomal DNA degradation. Consistently, the increased upstream apoptotic signals and the number of TUNEL- and caspase-3-positive cells in the HS group were found either in crossbred gilts (Ganesan et al., 2017) or Prochilodus lineatus embryos (Sales et al., 2019). It is worth mentioning that caspase-3 acts as the target executioner in the apoptotic signaling pathway triggered by mitochondria, death receptors, and granzyme B (Elmore, 2016). Our data showed that HS increased the activation degree of caspase-3 compared with the control. These results underlie the decisive effect of caspase-3 on the degradation of key myofibrillar and cytoskeletal proteins by mediating Ca2+ signaling, which directly contributes to breast meat tenderization (Zhang et al., 2021).

The mitochondrial pathway of apoptosis is initiated by the binding of Ca2+ to cardiolipin, causing the competitive dissociation of the Cyt-c from the inner mitochondrial membrane into the cytosol (Fox et al., 2021). The Cyt-c then binds and activates apoptotic protease activating factor-1 and precursor caspase-9 to form an apoptosome, a complex that could recruit and cleave precursor caspase-3 into its activated state (Malladi et al., 2009). It is noticeable that Cyt-c plays a fundamental role in ATP synthesis, which is recorded as being depleted with a decreased electron flow along the respiratory chain during HS (Zhao et al., 2006). Accordingly, we found that the protein expression of Cyt-c was decreased in the HS group compared with the control in this trial. It suggests that the low expression of Cyt-c leads to high dependency on the anaerobic metabolism, contributing to lactic acid accumulation and PSE-like formation (Zaboli et al., 2019). On the other hand, the assembly of the apoptosome may be disrupted in the event of a deficiency in Cyt-c (Bock and Tait, 2020). More importantly, a decreased protein expression of precursor caspase-9 was found owing to high temperature. It can be inferred that the executioner caspase-3 is scarcely produced through the apoptosome-induced mitochondrial pathway under chronic heat exposure. It is noteworthy that calpain is a Ca2+-activated non-lysosomal cysteine protease, which plays a direct role in the initiation of apoptosis by cleaving essential components in the apoptotic machinery (Pinton et al., 2008). Here we found an increased μ-calpain protein expression in the HS group, indicating that μ-calpain was involved in the activation of caspase-3. Additionally, the carbonylation level of μ-calpain was decreased by the HS group compared to that in the PF group. The catalytic domain of μ-calpain harbors numerous nucleophilic residues such as Cys, His, Asn, etc., rendering it susceptible to conformational modification by carbonyl compounds (Bhat et al., 2018). Conversely, the decarbonylated μ-calpain may exhibit an enlarged vestibule to facilitate substrate access upon Ca2+ combination, as demonstrated in this study (Goll et al., 2003). Interestingly, HS elevated the carbonylation level of cleaved caspase-3, which exhibited a high degree of activation. Previously, Biswas and Mano (2016) found that RCS increased the activity of caspase-3-like protease in a cell-free extract, indicating an allosteric regulation of carbonyl compounds to caspases. Based on the active site of caspase-3, it is speculated that carbonylation extends the formation of the substrate binding pocket (Crawford and Wells, 2011). This promotes the apoptosis of myofibrillar proteins, thereby contributing to the development of tenderization and exudation in breast meat.

CONCLUSIONS

Broilers have elevated respiratory rate, with impaired myofiber structure in the breast muscle under HS. There is an amplified carbonyl distribution in muscle fibers, along with elevated Ca2+ content at the subcellular level, which is associated with the increased relative protein expressions and decreased carbonylation degrees of key Ca2+ channels due to HS. Furthermore, HS extends the apoptosis rate, which is potentially regulated by enhanced expressions of μ-calpain and carbonylated caspase-3. Overall, these events contribute to the inferior meat quality of broilers suffering from HS.

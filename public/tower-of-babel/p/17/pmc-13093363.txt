Efficacy of the feed additive consisting of Clostridium butyricum FERM BP‐2789 (Miya‐Gold®) for chickens for fattening, chickens reared for laying and minor poultry species (excluding laying birds) Miyarisan Pharmaceutical Co. Ltd
Authors: EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP), Roberto Edoardo Villa, Giovanna Azimonti, Eleftherios Bonos, Henrik Christensen, Mojca Durjava, Birgit Dusemund, Ronette Gehring, Boet Glandorf, Maryline Kouba, Marta López‐Alonso, Francesca Marcon, Carlo Nebbia, Alena Pechová, Miguel Prieto‐Maradona, Katerina Theodoridou, Montserrat Anguita, Matteo L Innocenti, Jordi Ortuño
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFollowing a request from the European Commission, EFSA was asked to deliver a scientific opinion on the efficacy of the additive consisting of viable cells of Clostridium butyricum FERM BP‐2789 (Miya‐Gold®). The additive is currently authorised in the EU for use as a feed additive for chickens for fattening, chickens reared for laying, turkeys for fattening, turkeys reared for breeding, minor avian species (excluding laying birds), piglets (suckling and weaned) and piglets of minor porcine species. The authorisation for chickens and minor avian (excluding laying birds) is at a minimum level of 2.5 × 108 colony forming unit (CFU)/kg complete feed. The applicant has requested to lower this level to 1.25 × 108 CFU/kg feed. The applicant provided three efficacy trials to support the efficacy of the additive at the newly recommended dose. Based on the results of the efficacy trials submitted, the Panel concluded that the additive has the potential to be efficacious as a zootechnical additive in chickens for fattening when added to feed at 1.25 × 108 CFU/kg complete feed. This conclusion was extended to chickens reared for laying and extrapolated to minor poultry for fattening and reared for laying.Keywords: Clostridium butyricum FERM BP‐2789, efficacy, gut flora stabilisers, Miya‐Gold®, zootechnical additives

On 17.01.2023, the EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP), in its opinion on the safety and efficacy of a feed additive consisting of Clostridium butyricum FERM BP‐2789 (Miya‐Gold®) for chickens for fattening, chickens reared for laying and minor avian species (excluding laying birds), could not conclude on the efficacy of Miya‐Gold® at 1.25 × 108 CFU/kg feed for the target species. The European Commission gave the possibility to the applicant to submit supplementary information and data to complete the assessment and to allow a revision of EFSA's opinion. The new supplementary information and data have been transmitted by the applicant using the E‐Submission Food Chain Platform (application number FEED‐2023‐16506). 1

In view of the above and in accordance with Article 29(1)(a) of Regulation (EC) No 178/2002, the European Commission requests EFSA to deliver a new scientific opinion on a feed additive consisting of Clostridium butyricum FERM BP‐2789 (Miya‐Gold®) for chickens for fattening, chickens reared for laying and minor avian species (excluding laying birds) under the conditions of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, based on the supplementary information and data submitted by the applicant.

Additional information

The additive is based on viable spores of C. butyricum FERM BP‐2789. The additive is currently authorised for use in feed for chickens for fattening, chickens reared for laying, turkeys for fattening, turkeys reared for breeding, minor avian species (excluding laying birds), piglets (suckling and weaned) and piglets of minor porcine species (4b1830). 2

EFSA has issued six opinions on the safety and efficacy of the additive based on C. butyricum FERM BP‐2789 for different target species: (EFSA, 2009; EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2011, 2013, 2014, 2021, 2023).

Methodologies

The approach followed by the FEEDAP Panel to assess the safety and the efficacy of C. butyricum FERM BP‐2789 (Miya‐Gold®) is in line with the principles laid down in Regulation (EC) No 429/2008 5 and the relevant guidance documents: Guidance on the assessment of the efficacy of feed additives (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2024).

ASSESSMENT

The additive consisting of C. butyricum FERM BP‐2789, hereafter referred to by its trade name Miya‐Gold®, is authorised in the EU as a zootechnical additive (functional group: gut flora stabilisers) to be used in feed for chickens for fattening, chickens reared for laying, minor poultry species (excluding laying birds), piglets (suckling and weaned) and piglets of minor porcine species at a minimum proposed use level of 2.5 × 108 CFU/kg complete feed; and in turkeys for fattening and reared for breeding, at a minimum use level of 1.25 × 108 CFU/kg complete feed.

The present application is for the modification of the current authorisation to reduce the minimum use level in chickens for fattening, chickens reared for laying and minor poultry 6 species (excluding laying birds) from 2.5 × 108 CFU/kg to 1.25 × 108 CFU/kg complete feed.

In its previous opinion (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2023), the FEEDAP Panel could not reach a conclusion on the efficacy of the additive at the new proposed use levels as none of the studies submitted by the applicant were considered adequate, because the husbandry conditions in which the birds were kept during the in vivo trials were not in line with the welfare regulations in the EU.

The applicant provided three new studies to support the modification of the conditions of use.

In all trials, one‐day‐old male chicks were distributed in floor pens randomly allocated to two groups, which were fed either a non‐supplemented diet (control) or the control diet supplemented with Miya‐Gold® at 1.25 × 108 CFU/kg complete feed (confirmed by analysis; see Table 2). The animals received two basal diets (starter/grower) on ad libitum basis for 42 days. Animal health status and mortality/culling were monitored and recorded twice daily, with the most likely cause of death or reason for culling noted. The birds were weighed at the start of the trial, and thereafter, the body weight and feed intake were recorded on a pen basis for each phase (trial 1) or on a weekly basis (trials 2 and 3). The average daily weight gain, average daily feed intake and feed‐to‐gain ratio were calculated for the overall study period. All zootechnical parameters were corrected for mortality.

The experimental data were analysed using a one‐way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with diet as a fixed effect and pen as the experimental unit. The significance level was set at 0.05. The main results are shown in Table 3.

In all trials, the group supplemented with the additive at 1.25 × 108 CFU/kg complete feed showed an improvement in the zootechnical performance (better feed‐to‐gain ratio in trials 1 and 2; higher final body weight and average daily weight gain in trials 2 and 3) compared to the control group.

Conclusions on efficacy

Based on the results of the efficacy trials submitted, the Panel concludes that the additive has the potential to be efficacious in chickens for fattening when added to feed at 1.25 × 108 CFU/kg complete feed. This conclusion can be extended to chickens reared for laying and extrapolated to minor poultry species (excluding laying birds).

CONCLUSIONS

The additive has the potential to be efficacious in chickens for fattening, chickens reared for laying and minor poultry species (excluding laying birds) when added to feed at 1.25 × 108 CFU/kg complete feed.

A hybrid deep fuzzy clustering framework with fusion-based learning for robust SAR change detection
Authors: Chanchal Ghosh, Dipankar Majumdar, Bikromadittya Mondal
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Multi-temporal Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) images are essential in detecting changes in the environment, analyzing urban growth, and assessing disasters. Nonetheless, it is difficult to reliably detect meaningful changes because of speckle noise, radiometric variations, and inhomogeneous terrain features that severely impair the accuracy of the detection. This paper presents a hybrid change detection framework that integrates Fusion-based Difference Imaging (FDI) with a deep feature-guided clustering method. The proposed FDI module enhances sensitivity to subtle backscatter variations, which combines complementary difference representations to enhance the discriminability of changed and unchanged regions. These representations are refined through a deep clustering process that improves separability and reduces false alarms. Extensive experiments on benchmark multi-temporal SAR data sets indicate that the proposed approach has high detection accuracy and robustness with overall accuracy (PCC) of up to 99.97%, a Kappa coefficient of up to 98.30% and an F1-score of up to 99.59% across various datasets. The proposed approach consistently outperforms competing methods across evaluated datasets and different speckle conditions. The results show that fusion-based representation learning, with uncertainty-aware clustering, is a scalable, efficient, and flexible solution to robust SAR change detection in challenging imaging conditions.

Introduction

The ability of Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) change detection to provide high-resolution images regardless of weather and day or night has elicited considerable academic interest1,2. The discovery of temporal variations in SAR data is invaluable for the use of SAR data in urban monitoring processes3, disaster monitoring4,5, environmental monitoring6 and agricultural monitoring7. However, there are still limitations to the accuracy of detection due to speckle noise, geometric distortions, and the inherent scattering properties of SAR images8–11.

The fuzzy clustering methods in SAR analysis have been widely used due to its ability to maintain pixel-level details. However, the traditional fuzzy c-means (FCM)12 algorithm is highly sensitive to noise owing to the lack of spatial constraints. In order to address this weakness, spatially informed versions like FLICM13 and the neighbourhood-extended version14 thereof have been proposed. Subsequent developments include pixel relevance weighting15, adaptive neighbourhood weighting16 and patch similarity metrics17,18. Some of these advances include the fuzzy local patch similarity c-means (FLPSICM) algorithm, which uses contextual patch data to address the weaknesses of conventional FCM formulations.

Deep learning has become the leading paradigm for detecting SAR changes in a parallel direction, and unsupervised and semi-supervised approaches are often used to generate pseudo-labels. Recent research has implemented hierarchical clustering with neural refinement19 to enhance the feature representation of convolutional neural networks (CNNs), and multiscale transforms with attention mechanisms20,21 have been explored to supplement feature representation. Other studies have investigated joint spatial frequency learning22, shearlet texture enhancement23, and pseudo-labelling assisted by GANs, which is also known as robust learning by denoising autoencoders (RDA)24. Although these methodologies have proven effective, they are associated with high computational costs and low interpretability.

Recent advancements have also explored hybrid learning frameworks combining representation learning and uncertainty modeling. For instance, recent works25–27 have demonstrated the effectiveness of integrating multi-scale feature learning and robust optimization strategies for improved change detection and segmentation performance.However, existing methods struggle to simultaneously achieve robustness to speckle noise, preservation of structural details, and computational efficiency in heterogeneous SAR environments. To address these difficulties, this study presents a hybrid architecture that combines deep-learning-based clustering and a weighted fusion-based difference image (FDI) generation plan. The FDI highlights salient temporal variations, whereas the clustering module increases the accuracy of segmentation and false alarms. This unified architecture supplements the strength and computational performance of SAR change detection in noisy and heterogeneous settings.

Preclassification

Speckle Reducing Anisotropic Diffusion (SRAD)28

TThe issue of speckle noise reduction is familiar with synthetic aperture radar (SAR) image processing. The granular interference generated by coherent imaging systems is known as speckle, which can blur the features of fine structures and degrade image interpretability. This degradation affects the accuracy of the features obtained from the SAR images and degrades segmentation performance and anomaly detection. Thus, it can be seen that the successful suppression of speckles is an essential step to the remote sensing application of SAR data of any form to provide any degree of precision, interpretability, and sound usage.

Of the recent generation of denoising algorithms, the speckle-reducing anisotropic diffusion (SRAD) algorithm has demonstrated a high potential for enhancing SAR image quality. Compared to traditional diffusion filters that have the propensity to smooth out image details, the SRAD can smooth out homogeneous areas in a flexible manner as opposed to the edges between objects. Due to the multiplicative nature of speckle noise, suppression should be done differently. SRAD addresses this issue by taking advantage of the statistical characteristics of speckle to the extreme with the Instantaneous Coefficient of Variation (ICOV); therefore, it can effectively discriminate between structural and noise variations.

The SRAD can boost or cut the intensity of diffusion where needed locally to remove noise in homogenous areas while maintaining sharp object edges by solving a partial differential equation that considers image characteristics. This adaptive algorithm maintains both the geometric and textual content of the picture and removes most of the speckle. Consequently, SRAD generates images of higher quality that are not only optically satisfactory but also numerically satisfactory; it provides a sound basis for use in subsequent stages that extract the basic features of a scene or identify changes in an image. Diffusion coefficient

The diffusion coefficient c(x, y, t) is derived from the local statistical properties of the image and its speckle characteristics. Through local adaptive means, it minimises diffusion near object boundaries to preserve edges and simultaneously enhances the suppression of noise in homogeneous regions. By this method, SRAD is well balanced for edge conservation and effective speckle reduction, and works ideally for enhancing SAR images. The SRAD model includes a diffusion coefficient as a central control parameter that varies the properties of the diffusion process across an image. It controls the speed at which a pixel is smoothed based on the local image statistics; thus, pixels in a region of an image with a large change in intensity (e.g. an edge or an object boundary) will have little smoothing, and pixels in a region of the image with little intensity variation (e.g. a uniform area) will be smoothed more severely. Thanks to this adaptive behaviour, SRAD achieves efficient speckle noise attenuation without compromising salient structural and textural information.

Non-Local Means (NLM) denoising

Non-Local Means (NLM)29 denoising method is a powerful method used in enhancing the quality of images especially where noise is present. This method is particularly effective for images that have repetitive structures and textures.

This method, unlike more traditional schemes of local filtering, such as using Gaussian filters, median filters, or other schemes, uses the idea of non-local similarity, in which similar patterns can exist at different locations in the image. It takes advantage of this property to realise high noise suppression and maintaining fine structural and textual features.

This similarity-based weighting mechanism ensures that similar structural properties make greater contribution to the end estimate.

The NLM approach has been found to be highly beneficial in Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) image processing where a substantial amount of structural fidelity is needed in order to allow an appropriate interpretation to be made. Difference image generation

Traditional single method Difference Image (DI) generation methods, including the absolute difference or the log-ratio, have a number of interesting shortcomings. They are not good at revealing fine details in the scene, particularly noise, particularly speckle noise that makes up Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) images, and they are not always predictable in rough terrain. In order to overcome these inadequacies, a Weighted Fusion Difference Image Generation methodology is proposed. This method is a combination of several DIs obtained by different operators or features, thus making it more resistant to noise and more sensitive to salient changes.

Feature extraction and initial classification

The proposed model developed a hybrid convolutional neural network (CNN) to extract, encode, and decode spatial cues based on satellite imagery to detect changes using a Pyramid Scene Parsing (PSP)-inspired module. This composite format is such that the level of representation matches the level of semantic richness, allowing both fine-grained spatial details (e.g. edges and textures) and general contextual information (e.g. land cover). The model is computationally efficient and lightweight without losing the ability to encode the multi-scale spatial information of remote sensing data.

The entire network architecture comprises three elements, termed modules: Encoder – Hierarchical Feature Extraction: This module works with pre- and post-event images to recall low-to-mid-level spatial information. It learns with successive convolutional and pooling layers to detect the spatial gradient, texture, and boundary hints needed to distinguish between transformed and non-transformed regions.PSPNet-like Feature Extraction Module: The module was inspired by the Pyramid Scene Parsing (PSP) network, which uses contextual elements aggregated over a variety of spatial scales. Different levels of the pyramid are subdivided and partitioned on the feature map, adaptive pooling is used, and the feature representations are combined. This will allow the model to detect both local and global contextual information, thus providing strong feature encoding in mixed terrains.Decoder – Initial Classification and Output Generation: The decoder recreates a pixel-wise prediction map of the features encoded. It does so to generate an initial multiscale change map where change may be possible by combining the high-resolution spatial characteristics of the encoder with the multi-scale contextual characteristics of the PSP-inspired module. PSPNet-like feature extraction module

A PSPNet30-inspired feature extraction module was added to provide the network with multi-scale contextual awareness. Its goal is to allow the model to concurrently grasp the local visual or global scene semantics and the requirement for vigorous change detection in SAR or optical imagery. Adding the global context to the model mitigates its sensitivity to local-scale and intensity changes. The module was inspired by the Pyramid Scene Parsing Network (PSPNet)30.

Fuzzy Local Phase Semi-Iterative Clustering (FLP-SICM)

SAR images are strongly affected by multiplicative speckle noise, which limits the performance of traditional clustering methods such as FCM31 and even spatial extensions like FLICM6. To improve robustness and boundary preservation, the Fuzzy Local Phase Semi-Iterative Clustering Method (FLP-SICM) is adopted.The proposed FLP-SICM is conceptually based on FLICM and FLPSICM models but differs fundamentally from FLPSICM by introducing: (i) the addition of a local phase-based similarity constraint and (ii) semi-iterative optimization to make it computationally efficient.

The proposed FLP-SICM, opposed to the traditional FLPSICM that is based on patch similarity measurements, presents a local phase-based regularization term that aims at enforcing structural consistency in a neighborhood. In this case, phase is not the complex SAR phase but rather local patterns of variation of intensity which are an approximation of structural alignment between adjacent pixels.

Moreover, a semi-iterative optimization approach is also implemented to minimize computation cost to ensure the stability of clustering. Such a change improves the convergence performance over entirely iterative fuzzy clustering algorithms.

Thus, FLP-SICM may be seen as a continuation of spatially-aware fuzzy clustering tools, with patch similarity introduced in favour of phase-consistency modelling, and with selective iterative updates to enable the computationally-efficient implementation. The proposed FLP-SICM is based on spatially regularized fuzzy clustering approaches (e.g., FLICM), but has two major innovations: Objective Function. Comparison with FCM and FLICM The suggested FLP-SICM framework builds the classical fuzzy clustering methods with reference to the main limitations of FCM and FLICM with regard to SAR change detection.

The conventional Fuzzy C-Means (FCM) algorithm31 uses only the similarity based on intensity and fails to consider the spatial context. Consequently, it is very susceptible to speckle noise and tends to give fragmented and noisy clustering in SAR imagery.

To address this weakness, FLICM algorithm13 adds a local spatial regularization term, which strengthens the robustness through the addition of the neighborhood information. Although FLICM enhances noise tolerance, it mainly depends on the intensity difference and locality, which might not be adequate to resolve the intricate structural variations in non-homogeneous areas.

Conversely, the proposed FLP-SICM provides a local phase descriptor which is structure aware and therefore reflects the relative orientation of the intensity changes in a neighborhood. This allows the model to more accurately conserve edges and differentiate subtle variations that cannot be easily modeled with intensity. Moreover, FLP-SICM does not re-optimize all pixels in each iteration, unlike FLICM, and instead uses a semi-iterative optimization method, which refines cluster centers selectively in high-change areas, allowing it to be much more computationally-efficient than FLICM.

In general, FLP-SICM may be viewed as an extension of spatially regularized fuzzy clustering, in which similarity based on intensity (FCM) and spatial smoothness (FLICM) are further optimized by using structure-aware modeling and effective optimization. Morphological reconstruction

Morphological reconstruction is an image processing method that is a geodesic method, refining the results of segmentation or classification by a marker image, which has constraints on the mask image. It is effective in structural enhancement and noise reduction of images in comparison to the conventional morphological operations because unlike the latter, it does not change object shapes but modulates pixel values selectively.

Morphological reconstruction is often used as a post-processing which aims at refining binary change maps produced by clustering algorithms in SAR change detection. It eliminates false and isolated detections and enhances boundary continuity, thus providing clean and more dependable results.

Application in SAR change detection

Salt-and-pepper noise and tiny misclassified areas common in the preliminary change maps of SAR pipelines can be attributed to speckle effects. These outputs were then refined using morphological reconstruction, which eliminates single isolated artefacts, maintains the actual boundaries of change, and improves spatial consistency. Reconstruction that is used to open an image also removes tiny bright noise elements, and reconstruction that is used to close an image fills tiny holes without distorting the geometry of the region. This improvement step made the final SAR change map more structurally coherent and interpretable.

Dataset usage strategy

Dataset Usage Strategy

The suggested method adheres to a paradigm of a supervised learning scheme, in which ground-truth labels are used to train the deep learning part of the framework. In both datasets, the existing SAR image pairs (before and after the occurrence) are separated into training, validation, and testing groups to provide a reasonable and objective measurement.

The hybrid CNN is trained on labeled samples to learn discriminative feature representations of change and no-change classes. Hyperparameter tuning and model selection is done on the validation set and the final evaluation of performance is done only on the test set.

Change-detection results and analysis

To confirm the suggested approach,used four actual SAR picture collections and eight current state-of-the-art algorithms to create fine benchmarks. The comparison techniques are RUSACD36, PCAKmeans37, M2HGCD38, MRFFCM39, DDNet40, PCATLC41, CWNN20, and NRELM42. In the case of PCAKmeans, principal component analysis is conducted to obtain information that is contextual and subsequently, k -means clustering is applied to the resulting features. On the other hand, NRELM uses an extreme learning machine (ELM) framework of classification. RUSACD combines multi-scale superpixel reconstruction, centre-constrained clustering, CWNN, and a DCGAN-based augmentation strategy. M2HGCD represents complex relationships among spaces using a heterogeneous graph structure that uses multi-order and multi-level connections, thus, being sensitive to variations in changed and unchanged areas. The MRFFCM is a combination of fuzzy c-means clustering and a modified Markov random field energy function that alters pixel membership based on the local connection and reduces speckle noise. PCATLC is a fusion of PCA-based fusion of logarithmic and mean ratio difference images with Gabor feature extraction and a two-level clustering scheme (FCM and neighbour rule) to attain a better classification accuracy of various SAR datasets. CWNN applies a convolutional -wavelet neural network, which makes use of the dual-tree complex wavelet transform and virtual sample generation to reduce noise and solve the small-sample problem.For consistency across all visualizations, binary change maps represent changed regions in white and unchanged regions in black, while color-coded maps use red/yellow to highlight detected changes and green to denote unchanged or correctly classified regions.

Statistical significance analysis

Wilcoxon signed-rank test is used to determine the statistical significance of the performance improvements. It is a non-parametric test that compares matched observations without the assumption of normal distribution of the data.

Since there are paired differences in performance with the proposed method and a baseline across different samples, the test orders the absolute differences and tests whether the median difference is significantly non-zero.

Failure case analysis and limitations

Although it works well, the proposed method is limited in hard-to-detect situations like in low-contrast areas, textures, and speckle noise. Such cases can lead to false negatives (missed detections) and false positives (false alarms).

Conclusion and future scope

This paper presents a joint SAR change detection model that uses weighted fusion-based difference imaging and deep feature-guided fuzzy clustering. It is proposed to introduce SRAD-based despeckling, multi-operator difference image fusion, a PSPNet-inspired contextual encoder, and phase-constrained FLP-SICM clustering to effectively meet the challenge of speckle noise, nonlinear variation of backscatter, and heterogeneous terrain cases.

Experimental outcomes on benchmark SAR data, including Florence, Yellow River, Sulzberger, and Havana are presented, demonstrating that the suggested approach offers considerable enhancement in terms of accuracy (OA), Kappa coefficient, and error reduction in comparison to several state-of-the-art unsupervised methods and deep learning-based approaches. The significance testing indicates that the results are statistically significant and the significance testing also indicates that the observed improvements are not due to chance.

Uncertainty-aware clustering and fusion-based representation learning is introduced on top of the power of preserving the boundaries and the power of reducing the false alarms under varying imaging conditions. The results of the experiment prove that the method can be adjusted to the conditions of image acquisition SAR and is efficient since it does not assume the use of observed labelling information.

Further work will build on the framework to feature modelling based on transformers, self-supervised representation learning, and multimodal SAR–optical fusion to enhance the scalability and applicability of this solution to large-scale remote sensing tasks.

Lerich syndrome revealed by renal failure: a case report
Authors: Sara Anibar, Mohammed Elalami, Mounir Salek, Mohammed Bouchoual, Soukaina Wakrim, Marouane Jabrane, Mohamed Arrayhani
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractLerich syndrome, also known as aorto-iliac obliteration syndrome, is a specific entity among obliterative arteriopathies of the lower limbs, is defined as thrombotic occlusion of the aorto-iliac junction. We report a case of 60-yearold patient, chronic smoker with hypertension disease. He was admitted for an assessment of renal failure, in whom the diagnosis of chronic kidney disease was confirmed. Moreover, in the presence of renal asymmetry (4,9 cm) on renal ultrasound, a CT angiography was requested, confirming the diagnosis of Lerich syndrome with renal artery stenosis. A complementary assessment, including etiological one was performed, revealing no abnormalities. Therapeutically, the patient was placed on intermittent hemodialysis and curative anticoagulation. Our case represents a rare and fortuitous discovery of Lerich syndrome.

Introduction

Aortoiliac junction occlusion syndrome, also known as Lerich syndrome, is a distinct condition. It refers to thrombotic occlusion of the aorto-iliac junction. It is generally due to severe atherosclerosis [1]. Although relatively rare, Lerich syndrome can be clinically deceptive; however, its appearance on CT angiography is typically pathognomonic, as supported by our case and cases previously described in the literature [1, 2]. Such an incidental discovery during the assessment of renal failure is rarely reported in the literature.

Observation

Medical history

A 61-year-old male smoker (5 pack-years, quit 6 years ago) with a history of uncontrolled hypertension for 5 years, on calcium channel blocker (CCB). No history of diabetes or other systemic diseases was noted.

The current history

Two weeks before his admission, the patient developed sudden bilateral lower limb edema, complicated by dyspnea. The initial consultation revealed renal failure, for which the patient was referred to our department. On admission, the patient was hypertensive at 160 mmHg, tachypneic at 26 Beats per minute, tachycardic at 110 cycles per minute, with lower limb edema and crackles on pleuropulmonary auscultation. There were no clinical signs of heart failure and peripheral pulses were palpable. The initial assessment revealed severe renal failure (Urea at 2,1 g/l, creatinine at 11,7 mg/dl), classified as AKIN III (acute kidney injury classification). The renal ultrasound showed renal asymmetry with a 4 cm difference (right kidney measured 5.5 cm and left kidney measured 9.5 cm). An abdominal angio-CT scan was therefore requested, confirming the diagnosis of significant 98% stenosis of the renal artery, as well as a significant occlusion of 50% and 75% respectively in the abdominal aorta and its iliac branches (Fig. 1 and 2). Accordingly, the diagnosis of severe hypertension due to unilateral renal artery stenosis complicated by terminal chronic renal failure was retained, associated with Leriche syndrome.

Axial CT scan image during the arterial phase showing a defect in opacification at the infrarenal aorta (black star) as well as the ostium of the right renal artery (white arrow).

The additional assessment

After stabilizing the patient and initiating emergency hemodialysis, a complementary workup was requested to identify a treatable cause of Lerich syndrome, including cardiac evaluation which revealed a heart failure with an ejection fraction of 33% due to previous ischemic heart disease. The metabolic assessment showed a decrease in HDL (High-Density Lipoprotein at 0,4 mg/dl) with a correct glycemic assessment (Glycated hemoglobin at 5,4%). No abnormalities were found in the search for a systemic disease, antiphospholipid syndrome, and vasculitis (anti-glomerular basement membrane antibodies, anti-nuclear antibodies, anti-neutrophil antibodies, antiphospholipid antibodies and plasma protein electrophoresis). Likewise, the thrombophilia panel was negative (Protein C and Protein S levels were normal).

Sagittal (a) and coronal (b) reconstructions of the angiographic complement which showed a significant reduction in the caliber aorta with diffuse calcifications along abdominal aorta (different arrow).

Treatment and outcome

The patient was started on intermittent hemodialysis as a renal replacement therapy, based on his preference. To manage his blood pressure, a triple therapy regimen was prescribed, including calcium channel blockers, loop diuretics, and an ACE inhibitor. Following a cardiology consultation, beta-blockers and simvastatin were introduced, along with dual antiplatelet therapy for his ischemic heart disease. Regarding Lerich syndrome, anticoagulation therapy was initiated, initially with heparin, and later switched to an oral vitamin K antagonist (VKA). Given the asymptomatic nature of his condition, associated comorbidities and the patient's preferene, surgical intervention was deferred until his cardiac condition stabilized. Moreover, a good improvement has been observed, with a 9-month follow-up on hemodialysis and no significant vascular complications to date.

Discussion

Lerich syndrome was first described by Ms Leriche in 1923, hence its name. It is a rare form of arterial obstruction affecting the lower limbs. It’s about a complete occlusion located in the abdominal aorta below the renal arteries, extending to the common iliac arteries [1, 3].

Clinically, this syndrome presents as the combination of intermittent claudication of the thigh or hip, muscular hypotrophy, decreased femoral pulse and sexual impotence [1, 3]. Rare cases of the insidious form of this syndrome have been reported in the literature, as is the case of our patient. Some cases of resistant hypertension, sciatica, as well as cases of acute renal failure have also been observed [4–6].

Its association with stenosis of the renal arteries remains frequent, estimated at more than 70%, at least of one main renal artery in 15%–30% of cases, while bilateral lesions remain approximately 5%– 20% [7]. In line with this, Murvet et al. [8] described a case of resistant arterial hypertension, revealing bilateral renal artery stenosis in the context of Leriche syndrome, as seen in our patient.

Generally, this condition is seen in patients over 60 years of age with a high cardiovascular risk. Its occurrence in younger individuals should prompt an etiological workup [2, 3, 8]. Our patient had an atherosclerotic background due to his cardiovascular risk factors and ischemic heart disease. However, we still opted for an etiological workup to rule out a curable cause.

Radiologically, several lesions are possible, such as isolated stenoses of the common iliac arteries, lesions of the termination of the aorta and the origin of the common iliac arteries, extensive lesions of the abdominal aorta and iliac arteries or a complete occlusion of the infrarenal aorta [3].

Ultrasound with Doppler study can be used as an initial assessment, given its non-invasive and non-irradiating nature, it allows visualization of the abdominal aorta as well as its branches. However, it is an operator and patient dependent examination [9]. The reference method remains arterial angiography due to its ability to provide high-resolution images of the entire vascular network of the lower limb, and thus guide interventional procedures, but it’s still intrusive. Thus, CT angiography with 3D reconstruction is the most common examination, it offers the advantage of visualizing the entire surrounding area in search of possible additional pathologies, the occlusive extent, as well as the evaluation of the associated collateral circulation, which allows the confirmation of the diagnosis and the individualization of the therapeutic management [2, 9, 10]. MR angiography can be an alternative to CT angiography in the presence of contraindications.

Although the prognosis is reserved due to the severity of the lesion, the development of collateral circulation can, in some cases, compensate for the vascularization of the affected areas [9, 10], as seen in our patient.

The management is surgical. Different options are available such as thromboendarterectomy, aorto-bi-femoral bypass and percutaneous transluminal angioplasty (PTA) with or without stent placement. It should be noted that PTA was the treatment proposed for our patient, given the presence of comorbidities, but he preferred to undergo only medical treatment [7, 10].

Conclusion

Lerich syndrome is a rare condition, typically diagnosed by imaging. In reviewing the literature, it is one of the infrequent cases of Lerich syndrome discovered accidentally. Its association with renal failure seems to be one of the complications associated with accelerated atherosclerosis, rather than just a coincidence.

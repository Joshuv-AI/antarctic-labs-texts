Commencement of Hypofractionated Adjuvant Radiation Therapy in Intermediate-Risk Oral Cavity Cancer: A Phase 1 Study Protocol (CHARIOT-1)
Authors: Rohit Avinash Vadgaonkar, Mansi Chandwani, Chandrasekhar Pusarla, Sasi Krishna Kavutarapu, N Nageswara Rao, Sonali Susmita Nayak, Anupurva Dutta, Sreelakshmi KK, Raghvendra Hajare, Umesh Mahantshetty
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeOral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) contributes substantially to the cancer burden in India. The standard treatment involves surgical excision followed by adjuvant radiation therapy (RT) or chemo-RT, based on histopathological risk factors. Although conventionally fractionated RT remains the current standard, limited data exist on the role of hypofractionated RT (HFRT) in adjuvant setting. This trial aims to establish maximum tolerated dose and fractionation (MTDF) of adjuvant HFRT in OSCC. Secondary objectives include analyzing acute, consequential, and late toxicities associated with HFRT.Methods and MaterialsThis paper describes an ongoing single-institute phase 1 trial conducted at a tertiary cancer center in India. Eligible patients with primary OSCC who have undergone radical surgery and require adjuvant RT alone will be enrolled. The study consists of 2 parts: part A (dose escalation) to determine the MTDF using a modified rolling 6 design at 3 escalating dose levels, followed by part B (dose expansion) to assess the additional toxicity profile by randomizing patients between the MTDF (n = 12) and the preceding dose level (MTDF-1) (n = 12). Randomization will be performed using a minimization technique, and stratification factors include age, primary tumor location, and nodal disease burden. The sample size for the trial is 42 (18 in part A and 24 in part B). Adjuvant HFRT doses will be optimized using a biologically effective dose model, and all treatments will be delivered with image guided RT. Predefined stopping rules will be applied for dose-limiting toxicities, which will be monitored by an independent adjunct committee.

Introduction

Head and neck cancer (HNC) accounts for nearly one-third of the total cancer burden in India, with oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) representing more than half of all new cases.1, 2, 3 The recommended standard of care consists of aggressive loco-regional therapy in the form of surgical excision followed by adjuvant radiation therapy (RT) alone or chemo-RT based on histopathological risk factors.4, 5, 6 Adjuvant RT alone is typically recommended for cases with intermediate-risk features, whereas high-risk features require chemo-RT.5,7 Conventional fractionation (CFRT), delivering 1.8 to 2.0 Gy per fraction over 6 to 7 weeks to a total dose of 60 to 66 Gy, remains the standard adjuvant RT regimen.

Altered fractionation schemes, particularly hyper- and accelerated fractionations, have demonstrated improved survival but remain resource-intensive.8 In contrast, hypofractionation offers a biologically sound and resource-efficient alternative by reducing overall treatment time and mitigating tumor repopulation effects.9,10 In the radical treatment setting of HNC, hypofractionated RT (HFRT) has produced outcomes comparable to those of CFRT, without a substantial increase in toxicity.9,11,12 However, in the adjuvant setting, concerns about increased toxicity have historically limited the adoption of HFRT.8,13

In this context, the recent phase 1 study of 3-week hypofractionated postoperative radiation therapy for head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HYPORT) study13 demonstrated the safety of moderate adjuvant HFRT for predominantly human papillomavirus-positive pharyngeal cancers. In contrast, in India, human papillomavirus-negative OSCC is more common, and the majority present at loco-regional advanced stages.2,3 Moreover, the irradiated volumes in postoperative OSCC differ considerably from those in other HNC subsites. Therefore, the target volumes irradiated in the HYPORT study may differ significantly from those in a typical postoperative OSCC. In addition, another study assessing moderate HFRT for OSCC, the phase I/II study evaluating the feasibility and safety of delivering adjuvant hypofractionated radiotherapy in resected oral cavity cancers (HYPO-ART) trial, demonstrated the feasibility of HFRT; however, the associated toxicity of conventionally planned adjuvant HFRT was unacceptably high (grade 3 mucositis: 60.5%; nasogastric feeding tube placement: 71%; and inpatient admissions: 50%). Considering these contradictory observations, there is a clear need to evaluate adjuvant HFRT schedules in OSCC in a more systematic manner before advancing to a phase 3 trial.

Hence, this phase 1 single-center study was designed to assess the feasibility and safety of moderate HFRT for postoperative OSCC.

Methods and Materials

Trial design

This is an ongoing, investigator-initiated, single-center, phase 1 interventional study conducted at a tertiary cancer center in India. The trial has been approved by Institutional Ethics Committee of Homi Bhabha Cancer Hospital and research Centre, Tata Memorial Centre, Visakhapatnam. The trial is prospectively registered with the Clinical Trial Registry of India (CTRI/2024/09/073386) (http://ctri.nic.in) on September 5, 2024. Eligible participants include patients with histologically proven OSCC (American Joint Committee on Cancer Eighth Edition stages I-IVB) who have undergone curative resection and require adjuvant RT alone, as determined by established risk stratification (Table E1).4, 5, 6

The trial comprises 2 parts (Fig. 1):

1.Part A (dose escalation). A modified rolling 6 design will be used to determine the maximum tolerated dose and fractionation (MTDF) for HFRT.14,15 This design is adapted to accommodate radiation-specific toxicity assessment and the predefined dose-limiting toxicity (DLT) evaluation window. Up to 6 participants will be enrolled at each dose level until at least 5 of the 6 participants have undergone DLT evaluation. Dose escalation will proceed if none of the participants exhibit a DLT, or if only 1 of 6 exhibits a DLT (eg, 5/6 or 6/6 participants show no DLT). Conversely, dose de-escalation will be considered if 2 or more participants experience a DLT. The design targets an acceptable DLT rate of approximately 25% to 30%, consistent with phase 1 oncology trials. The threshold of ≥2 DLTs among 6 patients exceeds the predefined tolerability limit.

Adjuvant HFRT dose and fractionation

The adjuvant HFRT dose and fractionation will be determined using a biologically effective dose (BED) model16,17 with the formula: BED = nd(1 + d/[α/β]) − loge2(T − Tk/αTPOT), where n represents the number of fractions, d is dose per fraction, T is overall treatment time, Tk is the time delay for repopulation (21 days), TPOT is the potential doubling time (5 days), α/β is 10 for tumors and 3 for late-reacting tissues, and α is 0.3/Gy. RT treatment planning and delivery

All patients will undergo computed tomography simulation (2.5 mm slice thickness) with thermoplastic immobilization and intra-venous contrast. The clinical target volumes (CTV1 and 2) will be delineated according to standard guidelines.18,19 CTV1 encompasses the primary tumor bed and pathologically positive cervical nodal levels, and CTV2 covers the prophylactically treated neck regions.18,20, 21, 22 For both pathologically N0 and N1 disease, cranial coverage of the CTV, including level II nodal stations, will be limited to the level of the lowermost transverse process of the C1 vertebra.18,20,21 In cases where upper level II involvement with 1 or more lymph nodes is evident, the retrostyloid space will also be included in CTV1.20, 21, 22

When a metastatic node is located at the boundary of an adjacent nodal level not initially planned for inclusion in CTV1, CTV1 will be extended to encompass that level.21 Similarly, if level IV or Vb is involved, CTV1 will be expanded to include the supraclavicular fossa.21 In cases where flap reconstruction has been performed on the oral cavity or neck, the entire flap will be included.23 For extensive peri-neural invasion, the proximal course of the involved nerves will be contoured up to the infratemporal fossa or skull base.24 The planning target volume (PTV1 and 2) will be created by a uniform 3 mm expansion, cropped 3 mm from the skin.

Treatment plans will use 6 MV photons delivered via volumetric modulated arc therapy with a simultaneous integrated boost. PTV1 and PTV2 will be used for dose prescription at the appropriate dose fractionation levels outlined in Table 1. Dose constraints for various fractionation schedules are detailed in Table E2.25, 26, 27 Treatment plans will use 2 to 3 full- or partial-arc volumetric modulated arc therapy based on the extent of the PTV. Plans will be generated using 6 MV photons and the simultaneous integrated boost technique. The dose-volume constraints for the target volumes will require achieving a minimum dose of 98% of the prescribed dose for CTV1 and CTV2 (D98), and 95% of the prescribed dose for PTV1 and PTV2 (D95) at each dose level (0, +1, and +2). A volume receiving 107% of the prescribed dose to PTV1 and above will be accepted if it is ≤2 cm3 in the overall computed tomography, and this region should not lie outside the contours of PTV1. No more than 20% of PTV2 should receive >110% of the prescribed dose. No more than 1% of any PTV would receive <93% of the prescribed dose.28 Coverage of the CTVs and PTVs, as well as constraints on critical structures such as the spinal cord, brainstem, brain, mandible, and optic apparatus, will be strictly enforced. Failure to meet these constraints may prompt consideration of withdrawing the participant from the trial.

Pretreatment patient-specific quality assurance will be conducted to validate dose calculation accuracy using perFRACTION (fraction 0; Sun Nuclear Corporation) or portal dosimetry with an electronic portal imaging device, verifying fluence accuracy before plan approval.29,30 The gamma passing rate for pretreatment quality assurance of all plans will be maintained at ≥95% using criteria of a 3% dose difference and a 2 mm distance-to-agreement, as recommended by the American Association of Physicists in Medicine TG-218 guidelines.30 In vivo dosimetry will be performed for all fractions using perFRACTION to analyze daily variations and identify any systemic deviations that may require adaptive planning or setup corrections.29,30 Adaptive replanning may be considered at any point during treatment, at the discretion of the treating physician.

Stopping rules and safety oversight

In part A, dose escalation will cease if ≥2 participants experience any DLT at a given level; the preceding level will be designated as the MTDF. Protocol-defined DLTs include:

iii.An unplanned and unintentional treatment gap due to the management of acute HFRT-related toxicities during the course of HFRT for more than 3 working days.

An unplanned and unintentional treatment gap due to the management of acute HFRT-related toxicities during the course of HFRT for more than 3 working days.

In part B, if ≥33% of patients in any cohort develop CTCAE version 5.0 grade > 3 acute and consequential toxicity during HFRT or within 12 weeks posttreatment, the trial for that specific cohort will be discontinued. An independent adjunct committee comprising a team of 5 members, including clinicians and rehabilitation specialists involved in patient management, will oversee the evaluation of toxicities. None of the investigators will serve on the independent adjunct committee.

Assessment and follow-up

Baseline evaluations include quality-of-life and functional assessments using the European Organization for Research and Treatment of Cancer quality of life (QLQ)-C30 and QLQ-HN35 questionnaires,31 along with speech, swallowing, and nutritional evaluations. Patients will be reviewed twice weekly during treatment, then at 4 and 12 weeks post-HFRT for DLT assessment, followed by 3-monthly visits for 2 years, 6-monthly visits for 3 years, and annually thereafter.

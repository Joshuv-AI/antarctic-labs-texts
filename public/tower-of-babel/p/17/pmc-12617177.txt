OA2073. Understanding pediatric trauma in Moldova: Registry-based evidence
Authors: A Cazacu-Stratu, S Cociu, M Glavceva, S Cebanu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundRoad traffic accidents are a major public health issue and a leading cause of death and injury. In Moldova, they are a top cause of mortality in adolescents (14–18) and morbidity in children under 14. The study aimed to evaluate injury-related morbidity among children under 18 years old in the Republic of Moldova.MethodsData were retrospectively extracted from the iCREATE Injury Registry, piloted in 2018 in Moldova, Armenia, and Georgia. In Moldova, data from 2 hospitals were included, covering all injury cases in children aged 0–18 years over 12 months. Data analyses were performed with SPSS 20. Ethical approval was obtained.ResultsA total of 984 injury cases were recorded among children under 18. The highest incidence was among adolescents aged 15–18 years (44%) and 10–14 years (33%). Males represented 68.9% of all cases. Most injuries occurred in urban areas (87.7%), and 49.8% of children were admitted to the hospital, while 40.9% were observed without treatment. Most injuries were unintentional (93.4%). Children arrived mainly by private or public vehicles (49.8%), followed by ambulance (40.9%). The most common places of injury were the home (40.7%), school (9.0%), and public roads (9.8%). Falls are leading (64.6%), followed by cuts/piercings (5.7%) and road traffic incidents (3.8%). Most injuries occurred during vital activities (34.6%), leisure/play (22.5%), and education (7.4%). The most frequent injury types were fractures (37.3%), bruises (25.8%), and concussions (12.9%). The head (36.3%), lower arm (16.1%), and fingers (7.0%) were the most affected body parts.ConclusionsUnintentional injuries among children in Moldova are a significant concern, particularly among adolescents and boys. Targeted prevention efforts and improved safety measures are needed, especially in urban settings. Establishing a national injury registry is crucial for ongoing monitoring and effective intervention planning.Key messages• Unintentional injuries are common among children in Moldova, especially boys and teens. Falls and head injuries are the leading causes, mostly occurring at home and in urban areas.• Better injury prevention and safety strategies are urgently needed. A national injury registry is essential for tracking cases and guiding effective public health interventions.TopicPediatric Injuries, Injury Surveillance, registry.

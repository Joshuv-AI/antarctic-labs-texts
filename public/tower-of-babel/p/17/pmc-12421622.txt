Artificial intelligence in medical imaging empowers precision neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma
Authors: Jia Fu, Xiaoying Huang, Mengjie Fang, Xin Feng, Xu-Yao Zhang, Xuebin Xie, Zhuozhao Zheng, Di Dong
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractNeoadjuvant immunochemotherapy (nICT) has demonstrated significant potential in improving pathological response rates and survival outcomes for patients with locally advanced esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC). However, substantial interindividual variability in therapeutic outcomes highlights the urgent need for more precise predictive tools to guide clinical decision-making. Traditional biomarkers remain limited in both predictive performance and clinical feasibility. In recent years, the application of artificial intelligence (AI) in medical imaging has expanded rapidly. By incorporating voxel-level feature maps, the combination of radiomics and deep learning enables the extraction of rich textural, morphological, and microstructural features, while autonomously learning high-level abstract representations from clinical CT images, thereby revealing biological heterogeneity that is often imperceptible to conventional assessments. Leveraging these high-dimensional representations, AI models can provide more accurate predictions of nICT response. Future advancements in foundation models, multimodal integration, and dynamic temporal modeling are expected to further enhance the generalizability and clinical applicability of AI. AI-powered medical imaging is poised to support all stages of perioperative management in ESCC, playing a pivotal role in high-risk patient identification, dynamic monitoring of therapeutic response, and individualized treatment adjustment, thereby comprehensively advancing precision nICT.Keywords: Esophageal Cancer, Neoadjuvant, Immunotherapy, Computed tomography

Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) is a highly aggressive malignancy that is often diagnosed at a locally advanced stage due to its insidious onset, leading to a poor prognosis. The standard treatment for locally advanced ESCC consists of neoadjuvant chemotherapy or chemoradiotherapy followed by radical esophagectomy, aiming to improve surgical resectability and survival outcomes. However, long-term prognosis remains unsatisfactory, with high recurrence and metastasis rates, and the optimal neoadjuvant regimen is still under debate. For example, the CMISG1701 study1 showed that adding radiotherapy to chemotherapy did not further improve survival. Emerging clinical evidence has highlighted the potential of neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy (nICT) to improve pathological response and survival in ESCC. The KEEP-G 03 (neoadjuvant sintilimab and chemotherapy in patients with potentially resectable esophageal squamous cell carcinoma) study2 reported a pathological complete response (pCR) rate of 20.0% and a major pathological response rate of 50.0% with sintilimab plus triplet chemotherapy. Similarly, in the ESCORT-NEO (chemotherapy plus camrelizumab versus chemotherapy alone as neoadjuvant treatment for resectable esophageal squamous cell carcinoma) study,3 two nICT regimens incorporating camrelizumab yielded pCR rates of 28.0% and 15.4%, respectively, significantly superior to the 4.7% observed with chemotherapy alone. Despite these encouraging results, therapeutic responses to nICT vary significantly among individuals, and not all patients benefit. Furthermore, biomarkers such as microsatellite instability, PD-L1 expression, and circulating tumor DNA remain limited by insufficient large-scale validation. Accurately identifying patients who are most likely to benefit from nICT remains a major challenge in the clinical management of ESCC.

Medical imaging, particularly CT, provides information regarding tumor size, anatomical location, relationships with adjacent structures, and the status of regional and distant lymph node metastases. However, conventional imaging assessments rely heavily on subjective judgment and a limited set of quantitative indicators, such as tumor volume and CT values, which are inadequate for capturing the complexity and heterogeneity of tumors. The advent of artificial intelligence (AI) has opened new avenues for extracting high-dimensional and biologically meaningful features from medical images. Radiomics focuses on predefined features like texture and morphology, while deep learning enables the autonomous learning of abstract representations. Increasing evidence suggests that AI models can identify imaging phenotypes closely linked to ESCC diagnosis and prognosis.4 Building on this, AI holds the potential for non-invasive assessment of key biological information such as tumor microenvironment and immune status, thereby enabling accurate prediction of therapeutic response to nICT. Recently, Zhang et al5 published a multicenter study in the Journal for Immunotherapy of Cancer, enrolling 741 patients with ESCC who received nICT. They developed an AI model based on pretreatment contrast-enhanced CT images, aiming to predict pCR after nICT, and demonstrated excellent generalizability (AUC 0.83–0.92; accuracy 83–91% in independent validation cohorts).

The study by Zhang et al demonstrates a series of advanced technical strategies that collectively enhance the accuracy, robustness, and interpretability of AI-based prediction for response to nICT in ESCC. One of the core innovations lies in the construction of voxel-level radiomic feature maps, which compute and retain quantitative features at each voxel throughout the tumor volume. By preserving the spatial distribution of intratumoral heterogeneity, this approach enables a more comprehensive characterization of tumor biology than conventional vector-based radiomics. These feature maps, combined with raw pretreatment CT images, serve as multichannel inputs into the deep learning framework, allowing the model to jointly leverage structural and microstructural information in an end-to-end fashion. This design avoids the limitations of traditional feature concatenation methods and enhances the model’s capacity for non-linear, high-dimensional feature learning. At the architectural level, the authors employed a Vision-Mamba framework, which integrates state space model layers to capture long-range spatial dependencies in volumetric imaging data. Compared with conventional convolutional neural networks that are restricted to local receptive fields, Vision-Mamba introduces a bidirectional state space mechanism that effectively models global spatial relationships and anatomical context, thereby improving sensitivity to tumor morphology, distribution patterns, and peritumoral interactions. This architecture balances high representational power with computational efficiency, addressing a common trade-off in medical AI applications. The study also places strong emphasis on interpretability, which is essential for clinical adoption. Using SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP), the model provides feature-level attribution that quantitatively assesses the contribution of each input component to the final prediction. Results showed that both CT images and voxel-wise texture maps contributed almost equally (46% vs 54%), highlighting the importance of integrating both macroscopic anatomical structures and fine-grained textural features. Furthermore, SHAP heatmaps identified high-impact regions within necrotic tumor centers and invasive margins, consistent with known pathological correlates of treatment sensitivity and tumor aggressiveness. This level of interpretability not only increases clinician trust but also provides valuable insights into treatment response mechanisms.

Beyond technical performance, the clinical implications of accurate pCR prediction warrant careful discussion. For patients predicted with high confidence to achieve pCR, non-operative management strategies such as “watch-and-wait” may be considered to avoid unnecessary surgery and its associated morbidity, in line with the goal of organ preservation. In contrast, for patients predicted as non-pCR, therapeutic decision-making remains less defined. Although such predictions may not yet be sufficient to guide direct treatment modification in current practice, they can facilitate pre-treatment risk stratification and support response-adaptive strategies. For instance, patients at increased risk of treatment resistance or suboptimal immune activation could be managed within prospective trial frameworks incorporating early imaging reassessment, interim biomarker evaluation, or treatment adjustment—such as the addition of radiotherapy, extension of therapy duration, or modification of immunotherapy regimens. In parallel, prediction results may also inform downstream clinical decisions, including surgical planning, postoperative surveillance intensity, or selection into stratified therapeutic pathways.

It is important to recognize that while pCR prediction may contribute to individualized treatment planning in ESCC, its true clinical value depends on whether it can ultimately lead to improved long-term outcomes, particularly overall survival (OS). Although pCR is associated with better OS, this correlation alone is insufficient to establish pCR as a valid surrogate endpoint. Current evidence does not support its use as a reliable predictor of long-term prognosis in this patient population.6 In the study by Zhang et al, survival analyses showed that stratification based on predicted pCR status provided greater prognostic separation than actual pCR status. However, this trend did not reach statistical significance in the validation cohorts. This observation underscores an uncertainty. While deep learning models may uncover imaging features related to prognosis, it remains uncertain whether such predictions can translate into tangible survival benefits. To clarify this, prospective studies using OS as the primary endpoint are essential to determine whether early pCR prediction can meaningfully inform clinical decisions and improve long-term outcomes.

To further enhance the clinical applicability of AI models in ESCC, several strategic directions warrant in-depth investigation. First, improving model interpretability and facilitating effective human–AI interaction are essential prerequisites for clinical integration. Building on the existing predictive framework, the incorporation of large language models7 offers a promising avenue toward explainable AI. Specifically, the use of chain-of-thought reasoning and retrieval-augmented generation mechanisms can enable interactive, dialogue-based interpretability, allowing clinicians to probe model outputs, understand the rationale behind predictions, and explore alternative clinical scenarios in a transparent, context-aware manner.

Second, advancing from static, single-time point prediction to longitudinal, multimodal modeling may substantially improve the clinical relevance of AI outputs across the treatment continuum. For example, post-treatment endoscopic images have already demonstrated high accuracy in predicting pCR,8 providing complementary information to pretreatment CT. A future framework that intelligently integrates baseline CT scans with additional imaging modalities, molecular biomarkers, and clinical data across multiple time points could enable continuous monitoring of tumor response and support dynamic, adaptive treatment strategies—including escalation, de-escalation, or regimen modification. In this context, recent advances in foundation model architectures9 offer promising capabilities for learning unified representations across heterogeneous data sources, with improved scalability and generalization. By leveraging large-scale pretraining and flexible modality alignment, these models can facilitate more robust integration of spatial, temporal, and biological signals, thereby enhancing the precision and adaptability of pCR prediction. Such a paradigm shift would extend the utility of AI systems from isolated prediction tasks to comprehensive, temporally-aware decision support, encompassing pretreatment risk stratification, on-treatment response assessment, and post-treatment follow-up planning.

Third, a major challenge in advancing AI-based pCR prediction models lies in the complexity of real-world deployment. A recent randomized trial10 for ESCC detection in endoscopy demonstrated that even AI systems with strong retrospective accuracy may fail to improve clinical outcomes when applied prospectively, underscoring the disconnect between model development and clinical utility. Similarly, although Zhang et al’s study demonstrated promising multicenter performance, suggesting potential generalizability, broader deployment remains hindered by significant variability in CT acquisition protocols, reconstruction algorithms, and tumor delineation practices across institutions. In parallel, practical implementation is often technically demanding for hospitals, particularly those lacking dedicated AI infrastructure. To support adoption, future systems should prioritize ease of integration—for example, via lightweight, cloud-based services or zero-footprint web applications that can be embedded directly into radiology workflows without specialized hardware. Moreover, privacy regulations frequently restrict centralized data sharing, impeding the development of broadly generalizable models. Federated learning offers a practical alternative by enabling decentralized training without transferring raw patient data, thus preserving data privacy and institutional autonomy. When combined with secure parameter exchange and robust anonymization protocols, such frameworks offer a scalable path to cross-institutional collaboration while helping mitigate disparities in infrastructure and data governance.

In conclusion, AI in medical imaging is emerging as a key tool for enhancing the precision of nICT in ESCC. The approach proposed by Zhang et al, combining deep learning with voxel-wise radiomics, demonstrated strong generalizability in multicenter settings. Looking ahead, integrating multimodal data with scalable model architectures may further support individualized decision-making, provided that challenges in interpretability and clinical integration are effectively addressed.

Prognostic value of 3D mean CT attenuation in the solid component of resected part-solid lung adenocarcinomas
Authors: Ryohei Miyazaki, Masaya Tamura, Takashi Sakai, Naoki Furukawa, Yujiro Bunno, Marino Yamamoto, Hironobu Okada
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPreoperative prediction of recurrence in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) remains limited using conventional imaging parameters such as tumor size, consolidation-to-tumor (C/T) ratio, and 2D mean computed tomography attenuation (mCT) value. This study aimed to evaluate the prognostic utility of the 3D-solid mCT value in patients with surgically resected NSCLC.MethodsA total of 138 patients with part-solid adenocarcinomas who underwent complete resection between 2012 and 2018 were retrospectively analyzed. Preoperative CT data were processed using a 3D workstation to measure tumor volume and calculate the 3D-solid mCT value. Recurrence-free survival (RFS) was the primary outcome. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis was used to identify optimal cut-off values. Survival was analyzed using Kaplan-Meier and Cox proportional hazards models.ResultsPatients were divided into high (≥−106.0 HU) and low (<−106.0 HU) 3D-solid mCT value groups. The high value group showed significantly worse 5-year RFS (65.8% vs. 98.4%, P<0.001). The 3D-solid mCT value had the highest predictive accuracy for recurrence [area under the curve (AUC) =0.724] compared to the 2D-mCT value (AUC =0.643, P=0.02) and C/T ratio (AUC =0.598). Multivariate analysis identified high 3D-solid mCT value (hazard ratio: 9.22, P=0.02) and lymphatic invasion (P=0.002) as independent predictors of recurrence.ConclusionsThe 3D-solid mCT value is a strong, independent predictor of postoperative recurrence in NSCLC and showed better predictive performance than conventional 2D imaging parameters in ROC analysis. Preoperative assessment of this value may help identify high-risk patients and support personalized postoperative management. However, further studies with formal model comparison are warranted to confirm its superiority.Keywords: Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), 3D computed tomography analysis (3D-CT analysis), CT attenuation, recurrence prediction, lung adenocarcinoma

Highlight box

Key findings

• This study identified that the mean computed tomography attenuation (mCT) value of the solid tumor component measured via 3D CT is a strong independent predictor of postoperative recurrence in patients with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). A higher 3D-solid mCT value (≥−106.0 Hounsfield units) was significantly associated with lower recurrence-free survival.

What is known and what is new?

• Traditional two-dimensional CT parameters, such as tumor size, the consolidation-to-tumor ratio, and maximum standardized uptake value, have been used to estimate recurrence risk in NSCLC, but they are limited by interobserver variability and a lack of volumetric data.

• This study introduces the 3D-solid mCT value as a novel, objective, and quantitative imaging biomarker that outperforms conventional 2D indices in predicting recurrence risk. It also demonstrates the superior prognostic accuracy of this parameter with receiver operating characteristic analysis showing the highest area under the curve among evaluated markers.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• The preoperative assessment of 3D-solid mCT value offers a promising tool for stratifying recurrence risk in early-stage NSCLC, particularly in part-solid tumors. Incorporating this metric into clinical decision-making may guide the selection of high-risk patients for more intensive surveillance or adjuvant therapy. Prospective multicenter validation and integration into AI-based automated analysis systems could enhance its clinical utility and standardization.

Introduction

Background

Lung cancer remains the leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide, with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) accounting for approximately 85% of all cases (1). In recent years, the increasing use of chest computed tomography (CT) screening has facilitated the early detection of pulmonary nodules, particularly small, part-solid adenocarcinomas (2). This has led to a rise in curative-intent resections via minimally invasive surgery (3). Despite complete resection, a subset of patients with early-stage NSCLC experience recurrence and poor prognosis (4). Accurate preoperative risk stratification is thus essential to guide surgical decision-making and determine the need for adjuvant therapy.

Traditionally, radiological assessment of malignancy has relied on 2D CT parameters, such as tumor diameter, the consolidation-to-tumor (C/T) ratio, and maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax) on PET-CT, in addition to histopathological findings (5,6). However, these parameters have known limitations in sensitivity and interobserver variability.

Rationale and knowledge gap

With recent advancements in imaging technology, 3D-CT analysis allows for volumetric assessment and a more comprehensive characterization of tumor morphology. Specifically, the mean CT attenuation (mCT) value provides a quantitative metric reflecting intratumoral density, which may correlate with pathological aggressiveness (7). Although previous studies have explored the relationship between CT attenuation and tumor invasiveness, most have been limited to 2D measurements or mixed solid and ground-glass components. Furthermore, the prognostic significance of the mCT value of the solid component alone, derived from 3D volumetric data, remains unclear.

There is a critical need to identify reliable, objective, and quantifiable preoperative markers that can predict recurrence in patients with early-stage NSCLC. Evaluating the utility of 3D-derived solid-component mCT values as a predictor of recurrence has not been thoroughly investigated and could address this gap in current prognostic modeling.

Objective

This study aimed to investigate whether the mCT value of the solid component of lung tumors, as measured by 3D-CT (referred to as the 3D-solid mCT value), is associated with postoperative recurrence in patients with NSCLC. We evaluated the predictive performance of the 3D-solid mCT value in comparison to conventional radiological parameters and assessed its potential role as a novel preoperative prognostic marker to guide individualized treatment strategies. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2025-1790/rc).

Methods

Study design

This retrospective single-center study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Kochi Medical School (No. ERB-111042) and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. Informed consent was waived due to the study’s retrospective nature.

Patients

Between January 2012 and December 2018, 583 patients with primary lung cancer underwent complete surgical resection at Kochi Medical School. Cases with a C/T ratio of 0 or 100, or part-solid nodules with mCT below −300 Hounsfield Unit (HU) were excluded. A total of 138 patients met the inclusion criteria (Figure 1).

Flow chart of patient selection. C/T ratio, consolidation to tumor ratio; CT, computed tomography; HU, Hounsfield Unit; NSCLC, non-small cell lung cancer.

2D-CT and 3D-CT evaluation of primary tumors

Contrast-enhanced CT was performed using a 64-channel multidetector scanner (Light Speed VCT, GE Medical System, Milwaukee, WI, USA; slice thickness: 1.0 mm, reconstruction interval: 1.0 mm, reconstruction kernel: standard). The solid-part size was defined as the largest diameter of the solid component on the lung window. Ground glass opacity (GGO) was defined as hazy lung attenuation not obscuring vascular markings. Tumor diameter, full area, C/T ratio, and mCT values were assessed on preoperative CT. Volumetric analysis of the lung lesions was performed using a 3D image analysis software program, Synapse Vincent (Fujifilm Medical Co., Ltd., Tokyo, Japan). DICOM data from CT images were imported into the software, and lesion volumes were calculated using the semi-automatic segmentation function. Voxels with attenuation values above −300 HU were classified as solid components, whereas those between −300 and −800 HU were considered ground-glass nodule components. Volumes of the solid, non-solid, and total tumor were calculated (Figure 2). Two surgeons (R.M. and M.T.) reviewed and confirmed the results.

(A) High resolution CT image of part solid nodule. (B) Three-dimensional volume rendered images. Green: GGO portion; pink: solid portion. Data collection and follow-up

Clinical and pathological data (age, sex, TN stage, invasions, histology, tumor sizes, C/T ratio, mCT values, volumes, and SUVmax) were obtained from medical records. Postoperative follow-up included physical exams, blood tests, and chest X-rays every 3 months for 3 years, then every 6 months. Chest and abdominal CTs were performed at least annually, and FDG-PET or MRI was used when clinically indicated. Clinicopathological features were retrospectively analyzed. The primary endpoint was recurrence-free survival (RFS).

Outcomes and follow-up

The primary outcome was RFS, defined as the time from surgery to the first documented recurrence. Recurrence was confirmed by imaging, clinical findings, and histology when possible. Cases with second primary lung cancers, defined by modified Martini and Melamed criteria (8), were excluded.

Statistical analysis

Data were presented as medians with interquartile ranges or counts with percentages. RFS was defined from surgery to the first documented recurrence. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were constructed to determine optimal cutoff values for prognostic imaging parameters. The ROC analysis was performed using recurrence as a binary outcome (recurrence vs. no recurrence during the follow-up period), and the cutoff values were determined based on the Youden index. These cutoff-derived groups were subsequently compared for RFS using Kaplan-Meier analysis. Although time-dependent ROC analysis is generally more suitable for time-to event data, we used conventional ROC analysis due to the limited number of recurrence events and to maintain analytical stability. Survival was analyzed using the Kaplan-Meier method and log-rank test. Independent prognostic factors were identified via stepwise Cox proportional hazards regression with backward elimination. Variables with P<0.10 in univariate analysis were entered into the model, and those with P>0.10 were sequentially removed. Continuous variables such as 3D-solid mCT value, 2D-mCT value, tumor volumes and C/T ratio were dichotomized according to the optimal cutoff values determined by ROC analysis to reduce model complexity. To minimize the risk of overfitting given the limited number of recurrence events (n=25), only clinically relevant variables were retained in the final multivariate model. To assess potential multicollinearity among imaging variables (2D-mCT, 3D-mCT, 3D-solid mCT and 3D-GGO mCT values), we calculated the Variance Inflation Factor (VIF). Variables with VIF >10 were considered to have significant multicollinearity. Histological grade was also included as a covariate in the multivariate Cox model to adjust for pathological differentiation.

Results were reported with 95% confidence interval (CI). Analyses were conducted using JMP Pro (v12, SAS Institute), with significance set at P<0.05.

Results

Patient characteristics

Overall, 138 patients were involved in the analysis. The median follow-up period of patients was 61 (range, 6–133) months. Their clinicopathological and radiological data are summarized in Table 1. The median patient age was 73 (range, 35–86) years, 68 patients (49.3%) were male. Thirty-seven (26.8%), 55 (39.9%), 34 (24.6%) and 12 (8.7%) patients were diagnosed with clinical T1mi or T1a, T1b, T1c and T2a, respectively. The majority of patients (95.6%) were clinical N0 and diagnosed with adenocarcinoma (97.1%).

ROC curves of radiological parameters for recurrence

The ROC curves for recurrence as determined using 3D-solid mCT value, tumor volume, 2D-mCT value and C/T ratio are shown in Figure 3. After the curves were used for RFS prediction, the optimal cut-off was −106.0 for the 3D-solid mCT value, 3.47 for the tumor volume, −316.0 for the 2D-mCT value and 88.5 for the C/T ratio. The area under the curve (AUC) for the 3D-solid mCT value was the highest (0.724; 95% CI: 0.651–0.762) and was significantly larger than the AUC for the 2D-mCT value (P=0.02) in ROC analysis (Figure 3). Although this suggests a trend toward improved discriminative ability, a formal comparison of prognostic model performance was not performed.

Survival analysis based on 3D-solid and 2D-mCT value

The low 3D-solid mCT value (<−106.0) group included 76 patients (55.1%), while the high 3D-solid mCT value (≥−106.0) group included 62 patients (44.9%) (Table 2). The Kaplan-Meier method was implemented to perform survival analyses based on 3D-solid mCT value, which revealed a significantly worse prognosis in the high 3D-solid mCT value group than in the low group with regard to RFS (5-year RFS: 65.8% vs. 98.4%, P<0.001) (Figure 4A). The log-rank test results with the curves show that 2D-mCT value successfully divided the patients into high-risk and low-risk groups but its power was weaker than 3D-solid mCT value (P=0.01; Figure 4B).

Discussion

This study demonstrated that the mCT value of the solid tumor component, quantified using 3D-CT, is a significant predictor of postoperative recurrence in patients with NSCLC. Specifically, a higher 3D-solid mCT value was significantly associated with increased recurrence rates and shorter RFS.

Among various imaging parameters analyzed, the 3D-solid mCT value showed the highest predictive accuracy for recurrence in ROC analysis, suggesting potentially better performance than conventional 2D metrics such as the 2D-mCT value and the C/T ratio. However, since a formal statistical comparison between prognostic models (e.g., C-index or time-dependent AUC) was not conducted, this finding should be interpreted with caution. These findings suggest that 3D-CT-derived solid component density could serve as a novel and practical preoperative imaging biomarker for recurrence risk stratification in patients with early-stage NSCLC.

A major strength of this study lies in the use of volumetric 3D-CT analysis, which allows for a more accurate and comprehensive assessment of tumor density compared to traditional 2D-CT measurements. The study also evaluated a relatively homogeneous patient population undergoing complete resection for early-stage NSCLC, minimizing confounding factors such as advanced disease or histological heterogeneity.

However, several limitations must be acknowledged. First, the study was retrospective and conducted at a single institution, which may limit the generalizability of the findings. Second, despite using semi-automated software for image segmentation, manual corrections were necessary, potentially introducing observer bias. Third, the analysis was confined to radiological features and did not integrate molecular or genetic prognostic factors. Additionally, the possibility of vascular structures being included in the region of interest (ROI) may have led to overestimation of mCT values. Lastly, the use of contrast-enhanced CT in a small subset of patients might have introduced variability in attenuation measures. Furthermore, given that only 25 recurrence events occurred in our cohort, we acknowledge the potential risk of overfitting in the multivariate Cox regression model. To address this, we limited the number of covariates and used dichotomized variables based on ROC-derived cutoff. Validation in larger, prospective datasets will be necessary to confirm the robustness of our findings.

In addition, because the ROC analysis was based on binary recurrence status rather than time-dependent survival data, potential temporal effects on RFS could not be fully captured. Future studies with larger sample sizes should consider time-dependent ROC analysis to validate our findings. We did not perform a direct comparison of prognostic model performance (e.g., C-index) between models incorporating 2D- and 3D-mCT values, which limits our ability to conclusively determine superiority between the two parameters. Finally, because the number of recurrence events was relatively small, the multivariate Cox analysis might be subject to overfitting despite limiting the number of included variables. Future studies with larger sample sizes are warranted to confirm the robustness of our findings.

Previous studies have suggested that conventional imaging markers such as tumor size, C/T ratio, and SUVmax are useful for assessing tumor aggressiveness and recurrence risk in NSCLC (5,6,9-11). Our earlier work demonstrated that 2D-mCT value could predict tumor invasiveness and recurrence more sensitively than C/T ratio in stage IA NSCLC (12,13). However, these 2D measures often suffer from interobserver variability and fail to capture tumor heterogeneity in three dimensions.

The present study builds upon and extends previous findings by introducing a more refined metric: the 3D-solid mCT value. Unlike prior volumetric studies that focused primarily on tumor size or volume (14-16), our analysis incorporated tumor density—an imaging surrogate of histological features such as cellularity and fibrosis (2). Notably, our ROC analysis revealed that the 3D-solid mCT value had an AUC of 0.724, which was significantly higher than those of the 2D-mCT value and C/T ratio, underscoring its superior prognostic performance.

The strong association between high 3D-solid mCT values and recurrence may be explained by the pathological characteristics reflected in CT density. Higher attenuation values suggest increased tumor cellularity, fibrosis, or decreased aeration—features typically associated with invasive histologic subtypes and greater malignancy. This is consistent with our finding that elevated 3D-solid mCT values correlated with lymphatic and pleural invasion, which are well-established indicators of poor prognosis in lung cancer.

Additionally, volumetric analysis may capture subtle tumor heterogeneity that is often missed in 2D slice-based evaluations. By isolating and analyzing only the solid component of part-solid nodules, the 3D-solid mCT value provides a more biologically relevant metric for assessing tumor aggressiveness.

From a clinical standpoint, the integration of 3D-solid mCT value into preoperative evaluation protocols may enhance risk stratification and surgical decision-making in early-stage NSCLC. Patients with high 3D-solid mCT values could be considered for intensified postoperative surveillance or adjuvant therapy, even in the absence of other high-risk pathological features. Conversely, low-risk patients could potentially avoid unnecessary interventions, promoting more personalized and cost-effective care.

Future research should aim to validate these findings in prospective, multicenter cohorts and assess the additive value of 3D-solid mCT value when combined with molecular, histological, and radiomic features. Moreover, the development of fully automated, AI-based tools for CT attenuation analysis may further improve the reproducibility and clinical utility of this biomarker. Establishing standardized imaging protocols, including the consistent use of non-contrast CT when possible, will also be essential to minimize measurement variability.

Conclusions

This study suggests that the 3D-solid mCT value, obtained through preoperative 3D-CT analysis, may serve as a valuable marker for predicting postoperative recurrence risk in patients with primary lung cancer, particularly part-solid adenocarcinomas. Although 3D-solid mCT value showed better discriminative ability than 2D measures in ROC analysis, further validation with formal model comparison is needed to establish its prognostic superiority. Further prospective, multicenter studies are needed to validate the utility and generalizability of this parameter, with the ultimate goal of establishing a more precise and reliable prognostic model.

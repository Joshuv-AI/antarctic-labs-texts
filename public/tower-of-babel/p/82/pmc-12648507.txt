Inverse correlation between Lamin A/C and survival in ovarian cancer: implications for predicting responsiveness to taxane-based chemotherapy
Authors: Elizabeth R. Smith, Isaac R.L. Xu, Kathy Qi Cai, Sophia H.L. George, Matthew P. Schlumbrecht, Tian-Li Wang, Ie-Ming Shih, Xiang-Xi Xu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Paclitaxel and other taxanes are key components in the treatment of ovarian cancer.•Tumor Lamin A/C protein level and patient age independently predict overall survival.•Tumor Lamin A/C protein level inversely correlates with overall survival but not time to recurrence.•The results support the hypothesis that nuclear envelope malleability is a predictive factor of taxanes sensitivity/selectivity.

Introduction

Taxane drugs, including paclitaxel and docetaxel, are among the most effective anticancer agents commonly used to treat several major cancers, including breast, ovarian, prostatic, lung, pancreatic, and cervical cancers (Rowinsky and Donehower, 1991, Rowinsky and Donehower, 1995, Friedrich et al., 2004, Lemstrova et al., 2016 Dec, Bernabeu et al., 2017, Gallego-Jara et al., 2020). Currently, the carboplatin/paclitaxel combination along with debulking surgery is standard frontline treatment for epithelial ovarian cancer (Bookman, 2016, Bernabeu et al., 2017; 67,91). Recurrent and drug (platinum agent)-resistant ovarian cancer may be re-treated with paclitaxel as a single agent in a dose dense (weekly) schedule (Baker, 2003, Dunder et al., 2005, Baird et al., 2010 Oct, Jain et al., 2011 Jan).

The rate of initial response to paclitaxel in chemo-naïve patients is approximately 60 % (the reported range between 45 % to 85 %), but drops to around 30 % in recurrent, platinum-resistant ovarian cancer (Rowinsky and Donehower, 1991, Rowinsky and Donehower, 1995). Drug resistance unavoidably develops in repeated treatments in all cases (Horwitz et al., 1993; Mosca et al., 2021; Das et al., 2021). The lack of initial response in just under half of the patients and subsequent induced-resistance are barriers for the lasting success of taxane chemotherapy. To date, there are no predictive markers for paclitaxel effectiveness available in clinical settings.

Several possible mechanisms for resistance to paclitaxel are recognized (Horwitz et al., 1993, Barbuti and Chen, 2015, Das et al., 2021 Mar; Sazonova et al., 2021; Smith et al., 2022), such as overexpression of ABC transporters that promote drug efflux, altered expression of apoptotic genes or regulatory factors, and changes associated with microtubules that alter tubulin stability dynamics upon taxol binding. Paclitaxel resistance has also been proposed recently to be related to the structural malleability or fragility and stability of the nuclear envelope, which is influenced by the presence of the Lamin A/C proteins (Smith and Xu, 2021, Smith et al., 2022, Smith et al., 2022).

Generally, paclitaxel binds tightly to beta-tubulins within microtubules with an approximate Kd = 10 nM (Caplow et al., 1994). Microtubules are highly dynamic, but paclitaxel binding stabilizes the microtubule network and prevents the active turnover and regeneration that are inherent to microtubule function. It has been proposed that paclitaxel kills cells by binding to spindle microtubules and interfering with cell division, thereby inducing mitotic arrest and catastrophe (Schiff et al., 1979, Schiff and Horwitz, 1980, Jordan and Wilson, 2004), which subsequently triggers apoptosis in cancer cells (Wang et al., 1999, Weaver, 2014). However, efforts to develop additional anti-mitotic, cell-killing agents, which were inspired by the success of paclitaxel, have been unsuccessful, leading to doubts about the rationale for targeting mitosis and raising the possibility that mitosis might not be the primary mechanism of action for microtubule-targeting agents (Schimming et al., 1999, Komlodi-Pasztor et al., 2011, Komlodi-Pasztor et al., 2012, Mitchison, 2012).

Although the cancer killing mechanism of paclitaxel is not completely understood, the induction of micronuclei (a process termed micronucleation) may be important (Panvichian et al., 1998, Mitchison et al., 2017, Smith and Xu, 2021). The formation of multiple nuclear envelope fragments upon treatment of cancer cells with paclitaxel has been reported (Panvichian et al., 1998, Merlin et al., 2000, Weaver, 2014, Mitchison et al., 2017), though few studies have followed up this observation. Nearly all (>95 %) cancer cells treated with paclitaxel become micronucleated in cell culture (Smith and Xu, 2021), as well as in tumor spheres and in xenografts (unpublished). Thus, in addition to proliferation and mitotic rates, the malleability of the nuclear envelope appears to be an additional distinguishing feature of cancer cells—compared to benign cells—targeted by paclitaxel (Xu et al., 2024, Smith et al., 2022).

The nuclear lamina provides support and strength to the nuclear envelope, and the loss or reduction of nuclear lamina proteins, especially Lamin A/C, renders the nuclear envelope less structurally sturdy. In a previous study, Lamin A/C protein expression was observed to be low, misexpressed, or lost in about 47 % of ovarian cancer cells and tissues (Capo-chichi et al., 2011a, Capo-chichi et al., 2011b). Recently, we demonstrated that the expression of Lamin A/C is a key determinant of paclitaxel sensitivity in cultured cancer cells (Smith and Xu, 2021), which may be important to understanding mechanisms responsible for paclitaxel resistance (Xu et al., 2024, Smith et al., 2022).

Here, we sought to determine if the predictive ability of Lamin A/C expression for the sensitivity of cancer cells to paclitaxel could be extended to ovarian cancer patients undergoing chemotherapy. Tumor specimens from a cohort of cancer patients with known diagnostic and clinical outcomes were collected to examine the correlation between Lamin A/C expression, cancer recurrence, and overall survival.

All tissue samples and clinical information were collected with the written consent of the patients and in accordance with the guidelines of the respective institutional review board. Four TMAs were derived from speciments obtained from the surgical pathology repository of Johns Hopkins Hospital (Baltimore, MD), including a subset of high grade serous carcinomas (HGSC). Age at diagnosis and time to recurrence were provided for all four TMAs, whereas overall survival was also provided for three of these TMAs, as indicated in Table 1 and reported previously (see Table 1 in Ojalva et al., 2018). Complete deidentified patient clinical data were provided, including age, race, histologic subtype, treatments, time to recurrence, and overall survival data. Samples for this TMA were collected between 1995 and 2008. The sixth TMA was generated from tissues collected via the University Health Network (Toronto, Ontario, CA) and included 54 HGSC tumor tissues with mostly complete clinical data, as described previously (supplemental S-Table 1) (George et al., 2016); samples were collected from 1996 to 2008. For the sample cohort, optimal reduction in the primary surgeries varied between 50–65 % depending upon the hospital system, and the majority of patients were diagnosed at Stage III or IV (S-Table 1 and Ojalvo et al., 2018).

Immunofluorescence staining

For immunohistochemistry, paraffin sections of TMAs on glass slides were deparaffinized in xylene, then rehydrated through a graded series of ethanol, and underwent heat-induced antigen retrieval in 0.01 M sodium citrate, pH 6, in a boiling water bath for 20 min. Sections were blocked in 5 % BSA for 1 h at room temperature, then incubated with primary antibodies to Lamin A/C (CST#2032, 1:100) or Lamin A (Abcam ab26300, 1:100) diluted in 5 % BSA overnight at 4 °C in a humidified chamber. Secondary antibodies (Invitrogen A31572, Alexa Fluor 555 donkey anti-rabbit, 1:500–1:1000) were applied for 1 h at room temperature, and slides were washed extensively in PBS containing 0.1 % Tween-20 and counterstained with DAPI to visualize the nuclei. Slides were mounted in ProLong Gold antifade reagent (Invitrogen). Negative controls were treated similarly.

Slides were imaged using the 40 × objective on the VS120 Olympus slide scanner in the University of Miami Analytical Imaging Core Facility, and images were analyzed using QuPath (v0.4.1 with the Bio-Format extension). For each sample core, the analysis determined the number of positive cell counts, the percent of tumor versus stromal content of the tissue, and the staining intensity of Lamin A/C in the tumor area (Bankhead et al., 2017). Lamin A/C staining was scored according to a categorical scoring system from 0 (not detectable), 1 (weak intensity with limited focal staining), 2 (a medium percentage of cells staining positive with moderate staining intensity), to 3 (widespread staining with moderate to strong intensity). Scores from replicate sample cores were averaged and the mean scores recorded. Prior to sample identification, two persons, including a pathologist, read and scored the samples in a blinded manner. In case of discordance, the images and scores were reviewed and discussed to achieve a consensus. Cases were defined as “high Lamin A/C” if 50 % or greater of cancer cells were scored as having 2 + and 3 + Lamin A/C levels.

Analysis of data

Data from a total of 90 subjects was tabulated and analyzed using the survival and ggsurvfit package in R. Samples were grouped according to intensity of Lamin A/C immunostaining, either as “Tumor Lamin A/C Low” or Lamin A/C 1+ (n = 29) and or “Tumor Lamin A/C High” or Lamin A/C 2+/3+ (n = 61). The mean age of patients was 59.3 years; the median age was 61 years. Patients were subsequently grouped by age as either > 60 (n = 47) and < 60 (n = 43) years.

For the survival analysis, overall survival time (in months) was used as the primary endpoint, defined as the time from diagnosis to death or last follow-up. Overall survival and time to recurrence were assessed using Kaplan-Meier plots. Single-variable analyses employed the log-rank test, while multivariate survival analysis was performed using a Cox proportional hazards regression model to adjust for age as a covariate. Graphs and statistical analyses were generated using the survival and ggsurvfit packages in R.

Statistical testing

Survival differences between Lamin A/C expression groups were evaluated using the log-rank test and Welch two-sample t-tests. Spearman's correlation was used to explore the association between patient age and Lamin A/C expression. A p value < 0.05 was considered significant.

Results

Patient demographics

In laboratory experiments using cultured cancer cells, the level of Lamin A/C protein was determined to influence the sensitivity of the cancer cells to paclitaxel (Smith and Xu, 2021). To verify if such a correlation is present in human ovarian cancer, we set out to investigate Lamin A/C expression in a cohort of ovarian cancer patients for whom clinical information was available. After processing the TMAs for immunostaining, a total of 90 cases were determined to be suitable (having an intact tissue core that contained clear tumor features) for the assessment of nuclear envelope Lamin A/C level by IHC and immunofluorescence microscopy (Table 1, S-Table 1). Of these patient sample cores, a total of 81/90 (90 %) had associated time to recurrence data, and 83/90 (92 %) had overall survival information. The primary tumor tissues were the specimens from the initial debulking surgery, which were first used for pathological characterization and diagnosis, and were classified as high grade serous (68/90), poorly differentiated serous (18/90), and serous/endometrioid or other (4/90). The median age of the patients was 61 years (range 35–88). The majority of advanced ovarian cancer is treated with platinum or platinum/paclitaxel chemotherapy and surgery at initial diagnosis and paclitaxel is frequently used at the time of recurrent disease. Of those samples with known treatment data, the patients were principally administered a standard first line treatment regimen featuring platinum or platinum/paclitaxel (S-Table 1).

The overall survival probability was plotted for this cohort, with 50 % overall survival found to be 43.5 months (3.6 years), 20 % by 60 months (5 year survival), and only 5 % by 95 months (7.9 years) (Fig. 1A). Correlation between Lamin A/C and survival

We analyzed the correlation between Lamin A/C level and overall survival and determined that patients with low Lamin A/C (scored 0/1+) tumor correlated with a longer survival than those of high Lamin A/C (scored 2+ and 3+) (Fig. 4A). Median overall survival for Lamin A/C-high patients was 34.5 months (range 27–42 months) compared to 58 months (range 37–90 months) for the Lamin A/C-low group. The difference was statistically significant (p = 0.015), confirming a strong inverse correlation between Lamin A/C expression and overall survival (Fig. 4B).

In contrast, the time to recurrence was similar between the Lamin A/C low and high groups (Fig. 4C), where the median time to recurrence was 17.5 months and 18 months for Lamin A/C low and Lamin A/C high, respectively. A log-rank test also indicated no significant difference in time to recurrence between the Lamin A/C high and low groups (Fig. 4D).

There was some division in the probability of recurrence between 20 and 40 months between the low and high lamin A/C groups; however generally time to recurrence did not account for the differential survival probability between Lamin A/C high and low groups. Overall, low Lamin A/C expression correlated with longer OS in ovarian cancer patients.

Relationship between age and tumor Lamin A/C expression levels

Since we found that in this cohort that both age and Lamin A/C expression correlate with overall survival, we determined the relationship between age and Lamin A/C expression. A Welch two-sample t test comparing age between patients with low Lamin A/C expression and those with high expression revealed that the low Lamin A/C group was significantly younger (median age = 54 years) than the high Lamin A/C group (median age = 62 years; p = 0.04) (Fig. 5A). To determine whether Lamin A/C expression remained predictive of survival when accounting for the age factor, we performed a Cox proportional hazards regression including age as a covariate. The differences were still present: low Lamin A/C had an overall survival of 44 months at mid-point, whereas the high Lamin A/C group had 36 months (Fig. 5B). Thus, Lamin A/C expression is still a predictor of overall survival independently of age, and both age and Lamin A/C expression together influence overall survival. Surprisingly, however, when adjusting for age, we found that the low Lamin A/C group—despite having better overall survival—experienced slightly earlier recurrence compared to the high Lamin A/C group (median time to recurrence: 14 months vs. 20 months, respectively) (Fig. 5C). Thus, in this analysis, slightly shorter time for recurrence in the low Lamin A/C group correlated with longer survival. We speculate that tumors with low Lamin A/C expression may be more sensitive to paclitaxel-based treatment, allowing patients to respond better and survive longer.

Discussion

The current study found a strong correlation between Lamin A/C expression of the primary ovarian cancer and overall survival of the ovarian patients. Many of the patients were first treated with a carboplatin and paclitaxel combination, as per standard treatment protocol, and taxanes are very commonly used in the treatment of recurrent ovarian cancer. Drug resistance and non-responsiveness to treatment ultimately account for death of the patients. Although there may be several potential mechansms to explain the correlation, the increased resistance to paclitaxel in Lamin A/C-positive tumors is a compelling explanation.

Based on the findings, we suggest that the malleability of the cancer nuclear envelope, which is often a consequence of the reduction of the lamina structural protein Lamin A/C, is a second factor in cancer selectivity, after the high rate of proliferation of cancer cells (Xu et al., 2024). In tumors that Lamin A/C immunostaining and quantitation were completed, a significant correlation between Lamin A/C expression of the initial tumors from debulking surgeries and overall survival was observed, where high Lamin A/C expression in tumors predicted reduced overall survival.

Nuclear lamina structural protein Lamin A/C is often lost or reduced in breast and ovarian cancer (Capo-chichi et al., 2011b). Its loss leads to deformation of nuclear morphology found in malignant cells (Capo-chichi et al., 2011a,b), and promotes the generation of aneuploidy by nuclear envelope budding and micronuclei formation (Capo-chichi et al., 2011b, Capo-chichi et al., 2016). Our prior work demonstrated that paclitaxel-induced rigid microtubules cause the fragmentation/ breakage of the malleable nucleus of malignant cells (Smith and Xu, 2021, Smith and Xu, 2021). While mitosis enhances this effect, it is not essential (Smith and Xu, 2021). Based on preliminary studies in our lab, we suggest that paclitaxel induces nuclear breakage and the excessive formation of micronuclei in malignant cancer cells but not in normal cells that have a sturdier nuclear envelope (Xu et al., 2024). We found that the level of nuclear Lamin A/C protein inversely correlates with nuclear shape deformation and is a key determinant of paclitaxel sensitivity in cancer cells (Smith and Xu, 2021). Furthermore, cancer cells that survive paclitaxel treatment have high Lamin A/C expression, supporting a model where increased Lamin A/C may contribute to both intrinsic and induced drug resistance (Smith et al., 2022).

In this study, our findings support the conclusion that high Lamin A/C expression predicts reduced chemotherapeutic sensitivity and poorer survival. The lack of detailed individual treatment information prevents us from directly correlating lamin A/C expression with drug resistance. Thus, the conclusion may be further verified in future studies that directly determine the correlation between Lamin A/C expression to the response of the patients to chemotherapy treatment using paclitaxel or other taxanes.

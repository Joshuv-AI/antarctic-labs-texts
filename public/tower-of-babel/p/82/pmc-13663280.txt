Clinical randomized comparative study of Laifu Chengqi Decoction enema for treating postoperative peritonitis in children with complicated appendicitis
Authors: Guojing Zhang, Yaqin Wang, Shuhan Liu, Xiaoyan Wu, Hongmin Fu, Daqing Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundLaifu Chengqi Decoction (LF-CQD) is a traditional Chinese medicine enema rooted in classic heat-clearing and purgative formulas traditionally used to relieve abdominal distention, resolve stasis, and restore bowel motility. Its components (e.g., Laifuzi and Dahuang) provide plausible pro-motility and anti-inflammatory actions, supporting its culturally grounded use as a postoperative adjunct in pediatric perforated appendicitis. This study aimed to evaluate the clinical efficacy of LF-CQD enemas in the treatment of postoperative peritonitis in children.MethodsThis prospective randomized controlled trial included 118 children with perforated appendicitis complicated by peritonitis. The LF-CQD group received LF-CQD retention enemas for 5 days, whereas the control group was administered saline enemas. The primary outcome was time to first passage of flatus (a core marker of gastrointestinal recovery). Key secondary outcomes included time to bowel sound resumption, time to oral intake, preoperative and postoperative day (POD) 3 and 7 inflammatory marker levels, complication rates at 6-month follow-up, antibiotic use, and length of hospital stay.ResultsGastrointestinal function recovery was significantly faster in the LF-CQD group than in the control group [bowel sound resumption (p < 0.001), flatus (p < 0.001), and oral intake (p < 0.001)]. On POD7, the LF-CQD group exhibited significantly lower inflammatory marker levels than the control group (C-reactive protein level: p < 0.001). Exploratory post-hoc analyses showed greater relative reductions (ΔCRP/ΔWBC) in the LFCQD group at all timepoints (all p < 0.05). Complication rates for intra-abdominal abscess (8.5% vs. 25.4%, p = 0.008) and adhesive intestinal obstruction (5.1% vs. 22%, p = 0.003) were reduced, and antibiotic use duration was shorter (p < 0.001).ConclusionsLFCQD enema serves as a safe, well-tolerated adjuvant intervention for children with postoperative peritonitis secondary to complicated perforated appendicitis. It accelerates gastrointestinal function recovery and alleviates postoperative inflammation. However, being a single-center trial with a modest sample size, it yielded large treatment effects for intra-abdominal abscess, adhesive intestinal obstruction, and length of hospital stay; thus, these effect sizes warrant cautious interpretation and require validation in large-scale multicenter trials. Exploratory post hoc analyses also indicated reduced systemic inflammatory marker levels in the intervention group. We hypothesize that LFCQD may modulate inflammatory signaling cascades and promote gut microbiota homeostasis to drive these clinical improvements. However, as no direct assessment of these pathways or metagenomic profiling of the intestinal microbiome was performed during this trial, these mechanistic proposed mechanistic pathways remain speculative and unconfirmed. In conclusion, this study demonstrates clinical associations between LFCQD enema and improved postoperative outcomes, but does not establish definitive causal molecular mechanisms.Trial registrationInternational Traditional Medicine Clinical Trial Registry; ITMCTR2025001634. All primary and secondary outcomes, inclusion and exclusion criteria, and core study procedures were precisely predefined and finalized in 2019 at the study design stage, prior to the initiation of patient enrollment in January 2020. No post-hoc additions, deletions, or modifications to any trial outcomes were made after patient recruitment, data collection, or statistical analysis. Background

The perforation rate in pediatric acute appendicitis is significantly higher than that in adult acute appendicitis. In children, delayed surgical intervention increases the risk of progression to complicated appendicitis by 17% [1], with perforation rates reaching 42% in those under 5 years of age. While perforation rates vary globally (5–42%) depending on age and region [1–3], delayed presentation in our population contributes to higher complication rates. Postoperative peritonitis develops in approximately 19% of these cases [2], impeding the resolution of inflammation, delaying the recovery of intestinal function, prolonging hospitalization [4], and increasing the risk of long-term sequelae, such as postoperative intra-abdominal adhesions [5] and persistent gut dysbiosis [6], thereby increasing treatment complexity and contributing to greater socioeconomic burdens.

The current management of pediatric intra-abdominal infections predominantly relies on surgical debridement and antibiotic therapy. However, this approach is becoming increasingly limited by several critical challenges. The emergence of antibiotic resistance is particularly concerning, as the prevalence of carbapenem-resistant Enterobacteriaceae in pediatric intra-abdominal infections has increased markedly, from 8% in 2015 to 21% in 2022 [7]. Prolonged administration of broad-spectrum antibiotics further disrupts gut microflora homeostasis and predisposes patients to opportunistic fungal colonization, notably Candida overgrowth [8]. Postoperative intestinal dysfunction, commonly presenting as ileus, affects approximately 35% of pediatric patients and is positively correlated with elevated intraperitoneal concentrations of inflammatory cytokines, such as interleukin 6 (IL-6) and tumor necrosis factor-alpha [6]. In this context, the therapeutic use of conventional prokinetic agents often results in significant adverse effects in children. Furthermore, although laparoscopic techniques have reduced the incidence of wound infection, the use of electrocautery remains associated with the risks of thermogenic bowel injury and delayed intestinal perforation [2].

Laifu Chengqi Decoction (LF-CQD), a modified formulation derived from the classical Dachengqi Decoction described in the Treatise on Cold Damage Diseases (Shang Han Lun), has been adapted to simultaneously clear heat/fire stagnation and promote Qi circulation to relieve abdominal distension. The original Dachengqi formula—comprising Dahuang (rhubarb), Mangxiao (Glauber’s salt), Houpu (Magnolia bark), and Zhishi (Aurantium immaturus)—is used to treat Yangming Fu-organ excess syndrome through “vigorous purgation of heat accumulation and unblocking of the Fu organs.” In the LF-CQD formulation, Zhishi is replaced with Zhiqiao (Aurantium fruit), which exerts a stronger effect in regulating Qi and relieving chest and abdominal distensions. Several key herbs have been incorporated to improve efficacy: Laifuzi (radish seed) promotes Qi descent, facilitates bowel movements, and supports food digestion; Huaixinyu (Achyranthes root) guides the formula downward and tonifies the liver and kidney; and Muxiang (Aucklandia root) invigorates Qi circulation and relieves pain, thereby enhancing gastrointestinal motility. These additions aim to purge heat accumulation, resolve Qi stagnation, and eliminate food retention, thereby alleviating abdominal distension and pain, collectively rendering LF-CQD potentially more appropriate for addressing postsurgical inflammation, abdominal distension, and potential food stagnation in pediatric patients. Unlike hydration/analgesia which address systemic sequelae, LFCQD enemas locally modulate inflammation and motility via bioactive compounds (e.g., sulforaphane, rhein) with minimal systemic exposure.

Modern pharmacological studies of the individual active components of LF-CQD suggest that they may promote the resolution of intra-abdominal inflammation [9–11] and modulate the gut microbiota composition [12], supporting the biological plausibility of these effects for the formulation as a whole. Notably, the enema route is hypothesized to exert localized therapeutic effects within the inflamed peritoneal cavity and colon, potentially reducing systemic exposure while targeting the site of pathology; however, this localized action has not been directly verified in the current study.

Accumulating clinical evidence suggests that enema therapy may have value in related clinical scenarios. It has been reported to alleviate intestinal inflammation such as ulcerative colitis [13], improve pelvic inflammatory sequelae via regulation of inflammatory pathways and gut microbiota [14], reduce mechanical ventilation duration and ICU stay in patients with intraabdominal hypertension by suppressing inflammation and restoring intestinal motility [15], and prevent intestinal paralysis in severe acute pancreatitis as an independent protective factor [16]. In the clinical practice of pediatric surgery at our institution, children with postoperative peritonitis often experience refractory intestinal dysmotility, aggravated abdominal distension, and persistent intraabdominal inflammatory exudation despite standard Western medical supportive care. Traditional Chinese medicine enemas, as a localized intervention, offer unique advantages by directly targeting the pathological site, modulating local inflammation, and promoting gastrointestinal motility without significant systemic adverse effects. However, evidence supporting the use of LF-CQD enemas in the management of postoperative peritonitis in pediatric patients remains limited. Therefore, this study was conducted to evaluate the clinical efficacy of LF-CQD enema in the treatment of postoperative peritonitis in children.

Methods

Patient population and selection criteria

Children diagnosed with acute perforated appendicitis complicated by peritonitis and admitted to the Department of Pediatric Surgery at Tianjin Medical University General Hospital between January 2020 and April 2025 were included. The diagnosis of perforation and intra-abdominal pus was confirmed intraoperatively via emergency diagnostic laparoscopy performed within 24 h of admission. Postoperative pathological examination confirmed the presence of suppurative or gangrenous appendicitis. All clinical outcomes and follow-up records were prospectively recorded in a secure electronic case report form (eCRF), and the final data collation for statistical analysis was conducted retrospectively.

The inclusion criteria were as follows: age 3–18 years, perforation was confirmed intraoperatively by visual identification of fecal/purulent free fluid or visible wall defect. Appendicitis severity was histologically verified as suppurative/gangrenous by pathologists blinded to group allocation.

The exclusion criteria were as follows: requirement of conversion to open appendectomy; involvement of incidental appendectomy during other procedures; presence of major comorbidities, such as inflammatory bowel disease or immunodeficiency; preoperative antibiotic use exceeding 24 h; known allergy to any component of LF-CQD; severe hemodynamic instability or organ failure at admission; and loss to follow-up.

Group allocation

Patients were randomly allocated to two groups (LF-CQD and control) using a stratified blocked randomization design, with 60 participants initially randomized per group. An independent biostatistician not involved in patient recruitment or outcome assessment generated a computerized random sequence using SPSS 25.0 (IBM Corp.) with a random permuted block size of 4 to ensure balanced group allocation. Allocation concealment was achieved using serially numbered, sealed opaque envelopes prepared by the independent biostatistician, which were only opened after a patient met all inclusion criteria and was enrolled by a research nurse unaware of the random sequence.

Stratification was performed by age (3–7 years vs. 8–18 years) and preoperative CRP level (< 100 mg/L vs. ≥100 mg/L) to ensure baseline balance between groups. Due to distinct differences in color, odor, and texture between the LFCQD herbal enema and the normal saline solution, blinding of participants and bedside nursing staff was unfeasible. This limitation inherently introduces performance bias and detection bias for subjective gastrointestinal recovery endpoints. However, stringent assessor blinding was implemented to mitigate measurement bias: Laboratory technicians measuring inflammatory markers, radiologists/pathologists assessing complications and histopathological severity, and the biostatistician performing statistical analyses remained fully blinded to group allocation. Standardized protocols were applied to minimize data recording variability: All recovery timelines were documented exclusively within secure electronic case report forms without unstructured free-text commentary. Furthermore, statisticians analyzed fully de-identified datasets throughout all stages. Critically, our institutional pharmacy lacked the technical capacity to produce a perfectly matched placebo herbal enema devoid of odor and matching the color and texture of LFCQD. A total of 120 patients were initially randomized (60 per group) as the intention-to-treat (ITT) population. Two patients (1 in each group) were excluded post-randomization, and the final per-protocol (PP) population for analysis was 118 patients (59 per group).

The LF-CQD group received LF-CQD retention enemas, whereas the control group received retention enemas with an equivalent volume of normal saline (0.9% NaCl). This prospective randomized controlled trial was designed in 2019, and the initial ethical approval was obtained from the Ethics Committee of Tianjin Medical University General Hospital in December 2019 (Approval No.: IRB2019-KY-412). Patient recruitment was carried out from January 2020 to April 2025 in accordance with this initial approval. The approval (Approval No. IRB2025-KY-580) in July 2025 is a supplementary ethical approval obtained to meet the special filing requirements for traditional Chinese medicine clinical trials and facilitate the standardized publication of research outcomes. The trial was retrospectively registered at the International Traditional Medicine Clinical Trial Registry under the identifier ITMCTR2025001634 on July 24, 2025. We acknowledge that retrospective registration is a key methodological limitation of this work. The first patient was enrolled on January 1, 2020, and all primary and secondary outcomes were pre-specified at the study design stage (2019) prior to patient enrollment. No endpoints were altered after recruitment commenced. The registry entry is 100% concordant with the study design, population, intervention and outcomes reported in this manuscript (Supplementary Material 1). For all future TCM randomized controlled trials conducted by our team, we will complete prospective trial registration before participant enrollment to eliminate this limitation.

Intervention protocol

LF-CQD was freshly prepared daily under pharmacy supervision, following a standardized formulation: Dahuang (Rhubarb, 24 g), Zhiqiao (Aurantium fruit, 10 g), Laifuzi (radish seed, 15 g), Mangxiao (Glauber’s salt/sodium sulfate, 15 g—dissolved separately in the prepared decoction immediately before administration), Huainiuxi (Achyranthes root, 10 g), Houpo (Magnolia bark, 15 g), and Muxiang (Aucklandia root, 10 g). The raw herbs were boiled in purified water to obtain a final concentration of 100 mL per dose. The solution was sterilized by filtration and adjusted to 38 °C.

All enemas were administered by specialized nurses who strictly adhered to standardized protocols. The patients were positioned in the left lateral Sims position with the hips elevated. A lubricated soft silicone rectal tube (12–16 Fr) was carefully inserted 10–15 cm beyond the anal verge to ensure delivery to the sigmoid colon. The enema solution was instilled slowly by gravity (bag height < 30 cm H₂O) for 5–7 min to minimize intraluminal pressure and discomfort. The patients were subsequently encouraged to retain the solution for at least 30 min. The standardized enema volume was applied uniformly to all patients: 200 mL for those aged 3–7 years and 300 mL for those aged 8–18 years. Patient tolerance was assessed by nurses using a 3point scale (1: cooperative; 2: mild distress; 3: significant resistance requiring discontinuation). All administrations scored 1 (cooperative) or 2 (mild distress).

Regarding concomitant treatment, all patients underwent standardized laparoscopic appendectomy with peritoneal lavage and suction drainage if deemed necessary by the operating surgeon. Postoperative intravenous (IV) broad-spectrum antibiotics (either piperacillin-tazobactam or a ceftriaxone/metronidazole combination per local protocol) were initiated empirically and subsequently adjusted based on the intraoperative pus culture and sensitivity results. Supportive care, including analgesia and IV fluids, was standardized and administered according to the institutional guidelines. The routine 3-point clinical tolerance grading was embedded in standard nursing practice, was not based on a formally validated patient-reported outcome scale, and data were extracted retrospectively from medical records rather than collected prospectively using a dedicated research assessment framework.

Outcome measures

The primary outcome was the recovery of gastrointestinal function. The primary outcome was time to first passage of flatus (the most clinically meaningful and validated marker of gastrointestinal function recovery in pediatric postoperative ileus). Time to bowel sound resumption and time to tolerance of first oral liquid intake were defined as key secondary outcomes of gastrointestinal recovery. These included time to bowel sound resumption (assessed every 4 h via auscultation), time to first passage of flatus, as reported by the patient or attending nurse, and time to tolerance of first oral liquid intake (defined as intake without vomiting or significant abdominal distension within 4 h).

Secondary outcomes included inflammatory marker levels, postoperative complications, duration of antibiotic use, length of hospital stay (LOS), and treatment safety. Serum inflammatory markers included white blood cell (WBC, ×109/L) count and C-reactive protein (CRP, mg/L) levels, measured preoperatively and on postoperative day (POD) 3 and POD7. Postoperative complications were assessed during hospitalization and at the 6-month follow-up, including intra-abdominal abscess, surgical site infection (diagnosed according to the standardized criteria of the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, CDC), and adhesive intestinal obstruction. The clinical diagnosis of all complications was confirmed by abdominal ultrasound or computed tomography (CT). For intra-abdominal abscess, the clinical decision for interventional drainage was based on two criteria: (1) an abscess with the maximum diameter ≥ 2 cm on imaging; (2) presence of clinical symptoms including persistent fever, abdominal pain, or elevated inflammatory markers despite antibiotic therapy. Abscesses meeting neither criterion were managed with antibiotic therapy alone. All postoperative complications were classified according to the Clavien-Dindo Classification of Surgical Complications (Version 5.0). For intra-abdominal abscess, cases managed with antibiotic therapy alone were graded as Clavien-Dindo Grade II, whereas those requiring image-guided percutaneous drainage were graded as Clavien-Dindo Grade IIIa. Adhesive intestinal obstruction and surgical site infection were graded according to the standard Clavien-Dindo criteria for conservative management (Grade II).

LOS was measured from the day of surgery to the point at which the pre-specified standardized discharge criteria were met (all criteria must be satisfied): (1) Tolerance of a regular oral diet for ≥ 24 h without vomiting or significant abdominal distension; (2) Afebrile (body temperature < 37.5 °C) for ≥ 24 h; (3) Adequate pain control with oral analgesics only; (4) Normalized inflammatory markers (CRP < 10 mg/L, WBC 4–10 × 10⁹/L); (5) No evidence of active complications on clinical and imaging assessment. The standardized antibiotic discontinuation protocol was applied uniformly to both groups: empiric IV broad-spectrum antibiotics were adjusted only based on intraoperative pus culture and sensitivity results, with IV-to-oral switch when patients met discharge criteria (1)-(3), and antibiotic discontinuation only when all discharge criteria (1)-(5) were met.

Normalization of serum CRP and WBC was included as a mandatory discharge criterion in the present study. As LF-CQD enema possesses intrinsic anti-inflammatory activity, this creates an inherent circular relationship bias: the intervention itself may accelerate the resolution of inflammatory markers and thereby facilitate meeting laboratory-based discharge endpoints, potentially artificially reducing length of hospital stay. To formally address this methodological limitation, we predefined a sensitivity analysis that excluded inflammatory marker normalization from discharge criteria to verify the robustness of LOS comparisons.

Safety monitoring focused on potential enema-related adverse events, including rectal bleeding, anal tears, bowel perforation, anastomotic leak, electrolyte disturbances and hemodynamic instability. Predefined routine laboratory monitoring for electrolyte disturbances (sodium, potassium, chloride, bicarbonate) was performed on POD1, POD3 and POD5 for all patients, with additional measurements if clinically indicated (e.g., vomiting, diarrhea). Routine blood biochemistry panels (liver and kidney function) were conducted on POD1 and POD5 for routine safety evaluation, and electrolyte abnormalities were corrected per institutional pediatric electrolyte replacement guidelines. Follow-up was conducted at discharge and 6 months postoperatively via outpatient clinic visits and/or telephone interviews.

Data collection and management

Patient demographics, clinical characteristics, intraoperative findings, pathological results, laboratory values (WBC count and CRP level), gastrointestinal recovery timelines, antibiotic details, postoperative complications, and LOS were prospectively recorded in a secure electronic case report form. Follow-up data were recorded in a similar manner.

Statistical analysis

SPSS software (version 25.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) was used for statistical analyses. Normality of data distribution was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test, and homogeneity of variance was evaluated using Levene’s test. Continuous variables with normal distribution (e.g., time-to-event outcomes, laboratory values, LOS) are expressed as mean ± standard deviation and were compared between groups using the independent samples Student’s t-test. Non-normally distributed data are expressed as median (interquartile range) and were analyzed using the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical data (e.g., sex and complication rates) are expressed as frequencies and percentages and were compared using the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test when the expected frequencies were < 5. For the primary gastrointestinal function endpoints, statistical significance was defined as a two-sided α level of p < 0.05. Bonferroni correction was applied, where appropriate, to adjust for multiple comparisons. All analyses were conducted using an intention-to-treat approach with the complete dataset (n = 118).

A prespecified hierarchical statistical testing strategy was applied for secondary outcomes to avoid type I error: secondary outcomes were tested in the order of clinical importance (1) inflammatory marker reduction (CRP at POD3); (2) 6-month complication rates; (3) antibiotic use duration; (4) LOS; (5) other gastrointestinal recovery measures. Bonferroni correction (α = 0.05/3 = 0.0167) was applied only to the original three gastrointestinal recovery primary endpoints in the initial analysis, and no correction was required after redefining a single primary outcome.

Note: The analyses of relative reductions (Δ) in CRP and WBC (ΔCRP = postoperative CRP level - preoperative CRP level; ΔWBC = postoperative WBC count - preoperative WBC count) were conducted as exploratory post-hoc analyses in response to reviewer suggestions. These analyses are hypothesis-generating and were not pre-specified in the initial study protocol. These relative reduction values were calculated based on prospectively collected preoperative and postoperative (POD3, POD7) inflammatory marker data, which were obtained through standardized laboratory testing and recorded in the electronic case report form (eCRF) without modification. The statistical methods for comparing ΔCRP and ΔWBC between groups were consistent with those used for the absolute values of inflammatory markers (independent samples Student’s t-test for normally distributed data). All ΔCRP and ΔWBC results are exploratory in nature and solely serve to generate hypotheses for future validation studies, rather than providing definitive clinical evidence.

Results

Baseline demographics and clinical characteristics

A total of 156 children were screened for eligibility, 36 of whom were excluded based on the pre-specified criteria, and 120 patients were randomized to the LF-CQD group (n = 60) or control group (n = 60). Two patients were excluded post-randomization (1 per group), resulting in a PP population of 118 patients (59 per group) for primary efficacy analysis; intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis was performed on the full randomized cohort of 120 patients. This trial flow is presented in accordance with the CONSORT 2010 Statement (Fig. 1).

Postoperative inflammation markers

Although WBC count was observed to decrease markedly only on POD7, CRP levels were significantly lower in the LF-CQD group as early as POD3 (p < 0.001), with further improvement noted on POD7, than in the control group (p < 0.001). Additionally, the LOS was shorter in the LF-CQD group than in the control group (Table 3). The observed reduction in LOS should be interpreted with caution due to a potential circular relationship between the anti-inflammatory effect of LF-CQD and the inclusion of inflammatory marker recovery as a discharge requirement. Nevertheless, our pre-specified sensitivity analysis, which removed inflammatory marker normalization from discharge criteria and relied only on clinical symptomatic benchmarks, still confirmed a significantly shorter LOS in the LF-CQD group, indicating that the reduced hospitalization was a genuine clinical benefit rather than an artifact of discharge criterion definition. Relative reductions (Δ) in CRP/WBC were significantly greater in the LFCQD group at all timepoints (all p < 0.05) (Table 4). A sensitivity analysis excluding inflammatory marker normalization as a discharge criterion (LOS based only on clinical criteria: afebrile, oral diet tolerance, oral pain control) still demonstrated a significant reduction in LOS in the LF-CQD group compared with the control group (9.2 ± 2.8 days vs. 14.5 ± 2.1 days, p < 0.001).

Complications and antibiotic use duration

At the 6-month postoperative follow-up, the LF-CQD group had a significantly lower incidence of intra-abdominal abscess and adhesive intestinal obstruction than the control group; no surgical site infection was observed in either group during the study period. All cases of intra-abdominal abscess were diagnosed by abdominal ultrasound/CT, and the decision for drainage was based on standardized imaging (maximum diameter ≥ 2 cm) and clinical criteria. Similarly, the duration of postoperative IV antibiotic therapy was significantly shorter in the LF-CQD group than in the control group. Effect size analysis showed that LF-CQD enema significantly reduced the risk of intra-abdominal abscess (RR = 0.336, 95% CI: 0.138–0.815) and adhesive intestinal obstruction (RR = 0.232, 95% CI: 0.071–0.759) compared with normal saline enema (Table 5). All complications were graded per the Clavien-Dindo Classification (Version 5.0), with conservative management for adhesive intestinal obstruction (Grade II) and antibiotic-only treatment (Grade II) or image-guided drainage (Grade IIIa) for intra-abdominal abscess.

Safety assessment

No immediate or short-term adverse events directly attributable to enema administration, such as rectal bleeding, anal tears, bowel perforation, or anastomotic leakage, were observed in either group. Routine biochemical monitoring revealed no clinically significant differences or treatment-related abnormalities between the groups. No clinically significant electrolyte disturbances were observed in either group, and all electrolyte levels remained within normal pediatric ranges throughout the study (no corrective interventions required).

Discussion

Acute appendicitis in children frequently manifests with atypical early symptoms, such as vomiting and fever, often leading to misdiagnosis as gastroenteritis. Communication barriers and relatively weaker immune responses further contribute to delayed diagnosis, resulting in a significantly higher perforation rate than in adults and a markedly increased risk of progression to complicated appendicitis [1]. These factors not only delay intestinal functional recovery and extend hospitalization [4] but also increase the risk of postoperative complications, including intra-abdominal adhesions, abdominal abscesses, and gut dysbiosis [5, 6]. Although substantial evidence supports the efficacy of traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) enemas in managing postoperative ileus in adults, research on their use in pediatric populations remains limited.

In this comparative study of pediatric patients with acute perforated appendicitis, LF-CQD administered via enema accelerated the restoration of gastrointestinal function and earlier resumption of oral intake compared with control saline treatment. These clinical benefits associated with LF-CQD enema are hypothesized to be related to sulforaphane derived from Laifuzi, which has been shown to activate M3 receptors to promote gastrointestinal smooth muscle contraction, accelerate intestinal transit, and enhance motility in preclinical studies [11]; this provides a biologically plausible mechanism for the observed gastrointestinal function recovery, though it requires further verification. Serial inflammatory marker assessments revealed significantly lower CRP levels in the LF-CQD group on POD3 and POD7 than in the control group; however, a significant reduction in WBC count was observed only on POD3. This likely reflects the normalization of WBC count in both groups by POD7, rendering the between-group statistical difference clinically insignificant at this later time point. The observed anti-inflammatory effects are hypothesized to reflect mechanisms similar to those reported for other enema-administered formulations, such as the Sancao Lichang Decoction, which exerts therapeutic effects in diarrhea-predominant irritable bowel syndrome by inhibiting the TLR4/MyD88/NF-κB pathway, reducing inflammation, and improving intestinal mucosal barrier function [17]. A biologically plausible explanation for the reduced pro-inflammatory cytokines observed in this study is that rhein. Previous cellular and animal preclinical studies have suggested that rhein may suppress the TLR4/NF-κB inflammatory signaling pathway. However, we did not measure the expression levels of proteins related to this pathway in patient samples, so this anti-inflammatory pathway remains only a hypothesized mechanism [9]. Similarly, sodium sulfate (Mangxiao, Glauber’s salt), another key component of LF-CQD, is prposed to induce osmotic catharsis, alter intraluminal osmolarity, and reduce bacterial toxin absorption [10], a potential mechanism that requires further verification in the present pediatric cohort. Concurrently, the observed 1.9-day reduction in antibiotic use duration indicated decreased antibiotic dependency. Collectively, these effects contribute to shorter hospital stays, lower rates of secondary surgical interventions, and a substantial decrease in economic burden.

Analysis of long-term complications (6-month follow-up) revealed that the LF-CQD enema group exhibited significantly lower incidences of Clavien-Dindo Grade II/IIIa intra-abdominal abscess and Grade II adhesive intestinal obstruction than the control group (no surgical site infection was observed in either group), which may be associated with the accelerated resolution of intra-abdominal inflammation and early recovery of intestinal motility induced by LF-CQD enema. This finding aligns with prior preclinical research that supports the biological plausibility of a link between LF-CQD enema administration and a lower incidence of intra-abdominal adhesions [18]; this effect is hypothesized to be related to accelerated inflammatory resolution and early intestinal motility recovery, though these mechanisms were not directly investigated in the current study. Although external application of Yihuang San powder has been reported to reduce postoperative abdominal abscess formation and suppress intestinal adhesion formation [19], its effects on the gut microbiota and underlying immune mechanisms remain underexplored. Theoretical concerns persist regarding the potential of early postoperative enemas to increase the risk of anastomotic fistula; nevertheless, objective evidence confirming this specific risk in children is currently lacking. Notably, the overall incidence of long-term complications was relatively low, potentially introducing statistical variability.

It is important to acknowledge the clinical controversy surrounding enema use in pediatric postoperative peritonitis: enemas are not universally regarded as a routine intervention in contemporary clinical practice, which may limit the external validity of our findings. We acknowledge that the therapeutic effects of LFCQD enema observed in this study are most applicable to clinical settings with similar patient characteristics and unmet clinical needs to those at our institution—specifically, pediatric patients with postoperative peritonitis who experience refractory intestinal dysmotility, abdominal distension, and persistent local inflammation despite standard supportive care. To enhance the generalizability of enema interventions in this population, future research should focus on multicenter randomized controlled trials with standardized protocols, to identify optimal patient subgroups most likely to benefit from this intervention and verify its efficacy across diverse healthcare settings. Such efforts will help clarify the clinical applicability of enema therapy and strengthen the external validity of related evidence.

This study contributes valuable evidence to the key controversies surrounding the use of TCM enemas in pediatric populations. First, regarding safety, strict adherence to standardized enema protocols, including maintaining infusion pressure below 30 cmH2O and controlling rectal tube insertion depth to 10–15 cm, resulted in zero cases of intestinal fistulas. Second, regarding timing, initiating enema administration within 24 h postoperatively—significantly earlier than the 48-h timeframe observed in adult studies—did not increase the risk of anastomotic dehiscence. Finally, the observed reductions in CRP levels and postoperative adhesion rates provide indirect support for the biological plausibility of the gut microbiota–inflammation axis as a potential target of LF-CQD enema, and warrant further direct investigation in future research.

Overall, this study suggests that LF-CQD enema therapy promotes intestinal motility recovery, reduces inflammatory exudation, reduces antibiotic dependency, shortens hospitalization, minimizes long-term complications, and may reduce the economic burden associated with pediatric perforated appendicitis. However, some limitations warrant consideration, particularly regarding the potential methodological biases in the definition of key outcomes. First, this trial relied on retrospective trial registration, which represents an important methodological limitation that weakens internal validity. All trial endpoints were fixed before enrollment, but delayed registration remains a notable shortcoming that should be avoided in future TCM clinical trials. The inclusion of CRP and WBC normalization as core discharge criteria introduces an obvious circular relationship bias. LF-CQD enema exerts direct anti-inflammatory effects that can accelerate the return of inflammatory indices to normal ranges, which inherently aids patients in satisfying discharge laboratory benchmarks and may artificially shorten LOS. We have prominently acknowledged this methodological issue in the Methods, Results, and Table 3 footnote, rather than only describing it in the Discussion. The sensitivity analysis further verified that the between-group difference in LOS remained significant even after excluding inflammatory markers from discharge standards, supporting that the shortened hospital stay reflected true therapeutic benefits rather than structural bias in outcome definition. Second, as a single-center trial with a limited sample size, our study recorded substantial protective effects against intra-abdominal abscess and adhesive intestinal obstruction, concurrently with a marked reduction in hospital length of stay. These observed effect sizes may be inflated by the clinical homogeneity typical of single-center settings and constrained statistical power, limiting their generalizability to broader patient populations and healthcare contexts. Consequently, large-scale multicenter randomized controlled trials are warranted to validate and accurately quantify these treatment effects. Third, complete double-blinding was unattainable in this trial: only outcome adjudicators were blinded, while patients and nursing staff remained unblinded. Subjective gastrointestinal recovery metrics (bowel sound recovery, time to first flatus, oral feeding tolerance) rely on realtime clinical documentation by unblinded nurses, creating risks of performance bias and detection bias. Future trials should develop visually and sensorially matched placebo enemas to facilitate full doubleblind design and reduce subjective recording bias. Fourth, LF-CQD comprises a complex combination of multiple herbal components, and the current study only infers its potential mechanisms based on biological plausibility and preclinical research on individual components, rendering direct mechanistic exploration inherently challenging. Fifth, we only measured routine peripheral blood inflammatory markers and did not perform longitudinal gut microbiota metagenomic profiling or molecular quantification of TLR4/NFκB pathway mediators. All mechanistic claims regarding modulation of intestinal flora and inflammatory signaling are speculative, without direct biological evidence from our patient samples. Subsequent research should incorporate multiomics microbial sequencing and inflammatory pathway testing to definitively elucidate the pharmacological mechanisms of LFCQD enema. Sixth, patient tolerability and procedural distress data were retrospectively abstracted from routine nursing records rather than collected prospectively with a validated and standardized assessment tool. The lack of a unified quantitative rating scale and prospective research design introduces inherent observer bias and subjective documentation variability among nursing staff. Accordingly, these tolerability findings are limited to qualitative descriptive observations only, cannot support formal comparative inference on patient acceptability, and should be interpreted with caution. Future studies should employ validated patient-reported tolerability scales and standardized prospective data collection to objectively evaluate enema tolerance and acceptability in children. Sixth, the post-hoc analyses of ΔCRP and ΔWBC (conducted in response to reviewer feedback) are exploratory in nature and should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating findings rather than confirmatory results. Future studies should adopt validated patient-reported outcome (PRO) scales or standardized tolerability assessment tools to quantitatively evaluate patient experience and enema acceptability, with prospective data collection to minimize bias.

Conclusions

In summary, LF-CQD retention enema serves as a safe, well-tolerated and effective adjuvant intervention for children with postoperative peritonitis secondary to complicated perforated appendicitis. The intervention can significantly accelerate gastrointestinal function recovery, mitigate postoperative inflammatory response, reduce the duration of antibiotic therapy, shorten hospital stay, and lower the 6-month incidence of intra-abdominal abscess and adhesive intestinal obstruction.

Clinically, LF-CQD enema is particularly suitable for pediatric patients presenting with refractory abdominal distension, delayed intestinal motility recovery and persistent inflammatory exudation after conventional laparoscopic surgery and antibiotic treatment. LF-CQD enema provides a feasible traditional Chinese medicine adjunct option to alleviate antibiotic over-reliance and reduce long-term adhesive complications in pediatric abdominal surgery.

Mechanistically, the multi-ingredient herbal formula may exert therapeutic effects via regulating inflammatory cascade and intestinal motility; however, the exact causal signaling pathways and gut microbiota regulatory patterns remain to be elucidated. Future high-quality multicenter randomized controlled trials are warranted to validate the generalizability of our findings, optimize the treatment course and dosage regimen, and identify the most beneficial patient subgroups. Further dedicated mechanistic laboratory and clinical studies, including fecal metagenomic sequencing and quantification of TLR4/NFκB inflammatory pathway mediators, are required to validate the hypothesized regulatory effects of LFCQD on gut microbiota and inflammatory cascades, clarify its underlying pharmacological basis, and support standardized clinical application of LFCQD enema in pediatric surgical practice.

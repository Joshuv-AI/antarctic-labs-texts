Improved Glycaemic Control with IDegLira in Chinese Adults with Type 2 Diabetes in Real-World Settings: A Retrospective, Database Cohort Study
Authors: Shanshan Wang, Bei Sun, Dongdong Zhu, Zewei Shen, Xing Zhong, Liming Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionThis study aimed to investigate glycaemic control and safety in Chinese adults with type 2 diabetes (T2D) receiving fixed-ratio combination of insulin degludec and liraglutide (IDegLira) in real-world clinical practice.MethodsThe Tianjin Health and Medical Big Data Super Platform was utilised to assess clinical characteristics, glycaemic control, and safety of Chinese adults with T2D receiving IDegLira between March 5, 2022 and June 30, 2024. The primary endpoint was change in glycated haemoglobin (HbA1c) from baseline to day 180. Secondary endpoints included change in HbA1c by baseline characteristics; change in fasting plasma glucose (FPG), postprandial glucose (PPG), and daily insulin dose; and treatment persistence. Exploratory endpoints included hypoglycaemic events and gastrointestinal adverse events (GI AEs).ResultsEligible participants were included in the full analysis set and safety analysis set (both n = 12,103), and participants receiving IDegLira for 180 days were included in the effectiveness analysis set (n = 3062). The mean (SD) change in HbA1c, FPG, PPG, and insulin dose after 180 days from baseline was − 1.08% (1.87, p < 0.001), − 2.34 mmol/L (3.61, p < 0.001), − 2.10 mmol/L (4.86, p < 0.001), and − 4.95 U/day (21.93, p < 0.001), respectively. Subgroup analyses of HbA1c change demonstrated that IDegLira decreased HbA1c significantly, irrespective of prior treatment, age, most baseline HbA1c, daily insulin dose, and fasting C-peptide quartiles. Median treatment persistence duration was 141.67 days (95% CI 135.67–146.67). Low percentages of patients reported hypoglycaemia (0.7%) or GI AEs (11.1%).ConclusionsIDegLira statistically improved glycaemic control by reducing HbA1c, FPG, and PPG levels in the Chinese population with T2D, with a favourable safety profile.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s13300-026-01852-w.

Introduction

Diabetes is a global health concern, with approximately 830 million people living with diabetes worldwide in 2022, of whom approximately 95% have type 2 diabetes (T2D) [1]. With one of the fastest-ageing populations across the globe, the prevalence of T2D in China is rapidly increasing as a result of lifestyle-related, socioeconomic, and environmental risk factors [2]. A recent estimate reported a diabetes prevalence of 12.4% in 2018 among Chinese adults [3]. Despite recent treatment advancements, glycaemic management among Chinese people with T2D remains suboptimal, with only 50.1% of people with T2D achieving the glycated haemoglobin (HbA1c) target of less than 7.0% [3].

Glucose-lowering therapy is a cornerstone of T2D management, with insulin playing a crucial part for many individuals [4, 5]. In a recent study, oral antidiabetic drugs (OADs) achieved greater glycaemic control in 78% of people with T2D compared with 65% receiving early insulin therapy (p = 0.053) [6]. The American Diabetes Association (ADA), the European Association for the Study of Diabetes (EASD), and the Chinese Diabetes Society (CDS) guidelines recommend the use of combination treatments to achieve glycaemic targets in people with T2D who also have other considerations (e.g., weight-management goals, cardiovascular disease, or hypoglycaemia concerns) [4, 5, 7]. Initiation of glucose-lowering combination treatment with complementary mechanisms targeting multiple pathophysiological processes, such as with combination therapy of basal insulin with a glucagon-like peptide 1 receptor agonist (GLP-1 RA), can facilitate early and sustainable achievement of glycaemic control compared with monotherapy [5]. It can also contribute added benefits (e.g., weight control, cardiovascular disease risk reduction, and hypoglycaemia management), and simplify treatment with reduced medication burden and improved treatment persistence, adherence, and satisfaction [3, 5, 7–9].

When a greater HbA1c reduction is needed for people with T2D already receiving a basal insulin or GLP-1 RA, a combination treatment such as basal insulin with GLP-1 RA could be initiated to achieve HbA1c targets [4, 5]. Insulin degludec plus liraglutide (IDegLira) and insulin glargine plus lixisenatide (iGlarLixi) are two available fixed-ratio combinations of basal insulin and GLP-1 RAs [4, 5]. The guidelines for the Prevention and Treatment of Diabetes Mellitus in China state that such fixed-dose combinations can simplify treatment regimens, potentially lowering glucose and reducing adverse effects associated with insulin therapy, such as weight gain and risk of hypoglycaemia [7]. Among these combinations, IDegLira can be initiated when HbA1c is ≥ 7.0% after 3 months of lifestyle intervention and adequate OAD therapy [7]. In addition to these recommended combination treatment options, the guidelines for the Management of Diabetes Mellitus in the Elderly in China recommend a fixed combination of basal insulin with GLP-1 RA for elderly people with T2D for the initial insulin treatment, due to the need for fewer injections and the resultant simplified treatment regimen [9].

The efficacy and safety profile of IDegLira in managing T2D in global populations is well known (phase 3 DUAL I–IX trials) [10–18], with a larger proportion of participants reaching HbA1c targets, with a lower insulin dose, with fewer hypoglycaemia events, and less weight gain compared with basal insulin or GLP-1 RA monotherapy [10–18]. IDegLira was also found to be efficacious in Chinese people with T2D uncontrolled on basal insulin and OADs [19, 20]. Also, a study investigating fixed-ratio combination (including IDegLira) versus free uptitration of basal insulin and GLP-1 RA showed similar HbA1c reduction, fasting plasma glucose (FPG) target achievement, and hypoglycaemic risk [21].

Real-world studies can complement randomised controlled trials by providing valuable information relevant for clinical practice, such as long-term effectiveness and safety results, broader participant populations, and treatment use [22, 23]. Real-word studies evaluating the effectiveness of IDegLira in various clinical settings were conducted in several countries across the globe [24–44]. There are currently limited real-world data on IDegLira in the Chinese population. Therefore, this study utilised data from a large real-world database to investigate the glycaemic control and other clinical parameters after 180 days of treatment among Chinese adults with T2D who initiated or switched to IDegLira.

Methods

Study Design and Participants

Regional electronic health data from the Tianjin Health and Medical Big Data Super Platform were used to assess clinical characteristics, glycaemic control, and safety of Chinese adults with T2D who initiated or switched to IDegLira (Fig. 1). This database holds electronic medical records, laboratory results, and prescription and diagnosis data from 39 secondary hospitals, 43 tertiary hospitals, and the public health system within Tianjin, which allowed a large and diverse population of participants to be included in this study. In 2024, Tianjin had a population of 13.64 million people, the majority of whom were Han Chinese [45], and the age-standardised prevalence of diabetes was 25.78% in 2023 [46].Fig. 1Study design. Study design. FPG fasting plasma glucose, HbA1c glycated haemoglobin, IDegLira insulin degludec/liraglutide, PPG postprandial glucose, T2D type 2 diabetes

IDegLira was launched in China on March 5, 2022. Adults with T2D who were prescribed IDegLira between this date and June 30, 2024 were included (data extraction from January 1, 2019 and June 30, 2024), with a baseline period of 90 days prior to the first prescription of IDegLira (defined as the index date) for measurement of glycaemic parameters, and a 180-day period for consideration of prior treatment regimens and at any time from 2019 to index date for comorbidities. For T2D diagnosis within the database, International Classification of Diseases (ICD)-10 coding and a terminology list of all possible expressions of T2D was used. Participants aged ≥ 18 years were included in the study if they had at least one IDegLira prescription and a documented T2D diagnosis prior to the first prescription of IDegLira, without a T1D diagnosis. To ensure that standard-of-care practices and real-world evidence were assessed, exclusion criteria included pregnancy, gestational diabetes, and clinical trial participation.

This study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and the Guidelines for Good Pharmacoepidemiology Practices. The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of the Tianjin Medical University Chu Hsien-I Memorial Hospital, which was independent of the researchers and study sponsor. Participant-informed consent was not required for this database study. Database permission was granted to the Tianjin Healthcare and Medical Big Data Platform. The IRB number for this study is ZXYJNYYkMEC2025-10.

Endpoints and Assessments

The primary endpoint was change in HbA1c from baseline to day 180 (Fig. 1). Secondary endpoints evaluated from baseline to day 180 were change in HbA1c (by previous antidiabetic treatment regimen, baseline demographic characteristics, baseline clinical characteristics, and baseline treatment characteristics); change in FPG; change in postprandial glucose (PPG); the proportion of participants with HbA1c < 7.0%; change in daily insulin dose; and treatment adherence (at 3 and 6 months) and persistence (at 3 and 6 months). Exploratory endpoints assessed from baseline to day 180 were change in lipid profile (high-density lipoprotein cholesterol [HDL-C], low-density lipoprotein cholesterol [LDL-C], total cholesterol [TC], and triglycerides [TG]); the proportion of participants who reported hypoglycaemic events (defined as blood glucose ≤ 3.9 mmol/L or explicit documentation of hypoglycaemia in medical records); and the proportion of participants who reported gastrointestinal adverse events (GI AEs).

The insulin dose reduction was calculated from insulin-experienced patients, such as those who received basal insulin, premix insulin, or basal–bolus insulin, all with or without an OAD.

Treatment persistence was defined as no interruption during follow-up, where an interruption/discontinuation was failure to renew prescription within 30 days after exhausting the prior IDegLira dose. If a participant remained under follow-up 30 days after the last prescription (with medical records), treatment was considered interrupted/discontinued at the end of the last prescription.

Statistical Analysis

A minimum of 225 participants was required to achieve at least 90% power to test for the difference at 5% significance level using an assumed detectable difference of 0.5% and a standard deviation of 2.3%. Furthermore, considering that the proportion of available primary endpoint data was 8%, a minimum of 2813 participants was required.

Three analysis sets were used in this study. The full analysis set (FAS) included participants who met the eligibility criteria of the study and was primarily used for describing baseline demographic characteristics, comorbidities, and treatment, as well as adherence to and persistence with IDegLira treatment. The effectiveness analysis set (EAS) included FAS participants with at least 180 days of IDegLira treatment persistence, and was used for analysing changes in HbA1c, proportion of participants with HbA1c level < 7.0%, FPG, PPG, insulin dose, IDegLira dose, and lipid profiles. Finally, the safety analysis set (SAS) included FAS patients with at least one follow-up record post baseline and was used for analysing safety events.

Two-sided hypothesis tests were used throughout the analysis. Paired t test, McNemar’s chi-squared test, or a negative binomial mixed-effects model was used for baseline versus follow-up comparisons, and t tests, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), Wilcoxon rank-sum test, Kruskal–Wallis tests, or chi-square tests were used for comparisons across subgroups. A p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. The Wald 95% CIs of mean differences from baseline to day 180 of the relevant variables were calculated. All statistical analyses were performed using R software, version 4.2.0.

Results

Patient Population

A total of 15,108 participants had at least one prescription of IDegLira between March 5, 2022 and June 30, 2024, and 12,103 participants met the eligibility criteria and were included in the FAS and SAS of the study (Fig. S1). The EAS included 3062 participants, and the number of participants included in each effectiveness analysis was dependent on data availability for the study duration.

Baseline Characteristics

In the FAS, mean age (SD) was 52.4 (12.7) years, and more than half of the participants were male (57.9%). Mean (SD) HbA1c 8.9% (2.0), FPG 10.5 mmol/L (3.6), and PPG 13.9 mmol/L (5.2) (Table 1). Some participants were diabetes-drug-naïve (22.5%), and a few had only received OADs (9.8%) at baseline. Subgroup analysis by prior treatment at baseline was performed (Table S1). For the subgroups by prior treatment, the mean ages ranged between 48.5 and 54.6 years. Participants who were drug-naïve had the highest HbA1c (SD) at baseline (9.13% [2.03]) versus those who received other therapies (8.11–8.83%). Mean FPG (SD) and PPG (SD) were also the highest for patients who were drug-naïve (12.36 mmol/L [3.50]; 15.39 mmol/L [4.80]) and for patients who received basal–bolus insulin with or without OADs (11.65 mmol/L [3.88]; 15.49 mmol/L [6.11]), respectively, versus other treatment regimens. Fasting plasma C-peptide ranged between 0.68 and 0.86 nmol/L. Daily insulin (SD) dose was lowest for patients receiving basal insulin with or without OADs (20.50 U [8.44]), and the highest for patients receiving premix insulin with or without OADs. The mean number of OAD classes ranged from 2.5 to 3.

Change in HbA1c, FPG, and PPG

Participants experienced a significant reduction in HbA1c, FPG, and PPG after receiving IDegLira for 180 days. The mean (SD) change in HbA1c, FPG, and PPG from baseline to day 180 was − 1.08% (1.87, p < 0.001), − 2.34 mmol/L (3.61, p < 0.001), and − 2.10 mmol/L (4.86, p < 0.001), respectively (Fig. 2). Daily Insulin and IDegLira Dose

A significant reduction in insulin dose from baseline to day 180 was observed, with a mean (SD) change of − 4.95 U/day (21.93, p < 0.001) (Fig. 2d). Reductions in insulin dose were observed for all participants who previously received insulin in combination with or without OADs.

Mean daily IDegLira dose significantly increased from index date (19.96 dose steps [SD 7.16]) to day 180 (23.07 dose steps [SD 8.03]), with a mean (SD) change of 3.11 dose steps (5.87, p < 0.001).

Treatment Persistence and Adherence

The median treatment persistence duration was 141.67 days (95% CI 135.67–146.67), and the proportion of patients with treatment persistence was 64.9% at 3 months and 41.6% at 6 months (Table S2). The mean (SD) proportion of days covered (PDC) was 68.8% (33.91) during the study period, with 52.0% of participants achieving a PDC ≥ 80%. Mean PDC was 82.2% (24.77) at 3 months and 69.5% (31.61) at 6 months.

Change in Lipid Profile

Participants who received IDegLira had a significantly improved lipid profile from baseline to day 180 (p < 0.001) (Table S3). Mean (SD) change in HDL-C, LDL-C, TC, and TG was − 0.02 (0.22), − 0.21 (0.97), − 0.30 (1.35), and − 0.45 (3.71) mmol/L, respectively.

Safety Assessment

The number of participants who experienced hypoglycaemia events at baseline was 0.5% (n = 56), with 79 reported events, and during the observation period was 0.7% (n = 80), with 111 reported events.

GI AEs were reported during the observation period for 11.1% of participants receiving IDegLira (n = 1347), of which the most common were gastritis (7.5%), constipation (2.7%), and abdominal pain (1.4%) (Table S4).

Discussion

The uniqueness of this study was in leveraging a large-scale Chinese database, evaluating comprehensive clinical parameters (such as HbA1c, FPG, PPG, insulin dose, lipid profiles, and treatment persistence and adherence), and conducting sub-analyses from different perspectives (including prior therapy, HbA1c levels, age, fasting C-peptide, and daily insulin dose). This study confirmed that the glycaemic control of IDegLira in Chinese people with T2D reflects the efficacy reported in the DUAL series of randomised trials in international cohorts [10–18].

Importantly, the glycaemic control findings reported here were aligned with those reported with real-world studies performed in other countries [24–44]. IDegLira treatment has resulted in improvements in glycaemic control by reducing HbA1c, FPG, and PPG levels. Furthermore, the proportion of patients achieving HbA1c < 7.0% was 41.2%, which was within the previously reported range of 25% to over 60% [24–26, 28, 32, 33, 35, 37, 38, 44]. Achieving HbA1c < 7.0% is considered a reasonable target by most guidelines to see microvascular benefits [5]. The percentage of 41.2% from this real-world evidence study was at the lower end of the range from previous real-world studies, which may be due to factors relevant to this Chinese population such as real-world clinical practice in China, differing baseline HbA1c levels, and participant race.

The clinical significance and relevance of these glycaemic results from this study are reinforced when it is considered that glycaemic control improvements were achieved with a significant reduction in daily insulin dose in insulin-experienced patients. The reduction in daily insulin dose was likely to be because of the additive effects of the basal insulin and GLP-1 RA components of IDegLira on glycaemic control [5]. These findings were representative of previous real-world studies where insulin dose reductions were mostly observed with IDegLira compared with baseline [24–26, 28–31, 33, 35–37]. Mean daily IDegLira dose increased by a mean of 3.11 dose steps from index date and was considered to be low over a period of 6 months. This may have been due to treatment inertia. It should be noted that timely titrations with IDegLira are required to achieve optimal treatment outcomes [47]. Frequent self-titration may pose challenges for some patients, which could hinder glycaemic control [47]. In our study, more than half of the participants were persistent with IDegLira treatment after 3 months and 41.6% were persistent after 6 months. Treatment persistence was observed to be lower than in previously published studies [29, 41], which may be a result of differences in healthcare practices and data capturing, or study inclusion criteria. The simplicity and ease of use of IDegLira [7, 9] may have affected treatment persistence and adherence reported here, which could account for the changes in insulin dose from baseline. These factors are likely to be linked, and demonstrate that IDegLira could benefit patients by reducing overall treatment burden.

Subgroup analyses revealed that higher baseline HbA1c levels were associated with greater reductions in HbA1c. There was no association between baseline daily insulin dose and HbA1c reduction, nor between baseline fasting C-peptide and HbA1c reduction. However, across all baseline fasting C-peptide quartiles, patients had relatively poor β-cell function, which may have affected the interpretation of the results. Drug-naïve participants had higher baseline HbA1c, FPG, PPG, and younger age, and exhibited the greatest HbA1c reduction compared with participants who previously received antidiabetic treatment. The high baseline HbA1c values observed in patients receiving only OADs suggest a degree of clinical inertia in initiating insulin in clinical practice. Clinical inertia is common in T2D [48]. Significant HbA1c reductions were observed across subgroups, which demonstrates the applicability of IDegLira in different patient populations. This confirms that IDegLira could be used for improved glycaemic control in Chinese people with T2D whose current treatment may not be as effective as needed, with a tendency toward higher glycaemic control in insulin-experienced patients.

Outside of glycaemic control, significant improvements in lipid profiles in patients were observed. The improvement in lipid profiles with IDegLira was probably due to the GLP-1 RA component liraglutide, as it is known that liraglutide can have beneficial impacts on lipid variables [49]. Improving lipid profiles is important in those with T2D, as reduction of cardiovascular risk remains a top priority in this population [4, 5].

One limitation of this study was that data for weight/body mass index were largely missing, likely due to its infrequent documentation in routine diabetes management in China. Moreover, the incidence of hypoglycaemia was very low, as is customary in database studies [50]. Hypoglycaemia events were reliant on reporting within the database. In addition, there might be a selection bias in the analysis of HbA1c reduction by fasting C-peptide quartiles, as patients with higher glucose levels were more likely to undergo C-peptide testing. Limitations associated with using electronic database records commonly associated with real-word studies should also be considered when interpreting these results. Selection bias may have occurred, as data were primarily derived from northern China and may not, therefore, be generalisable to other regions. This study was also subject to information bias and confounding bias commonly associated with database studies. Nevertheless, extending the knowledge from previous studies performed in countries across the globe, this real-world database study substantiated the benefits of IDegLira in the Chinese population.

Conclusion

Our findings demonstrate that IDegLira was effective in improving glycaemic control by reducing HbA1c, FPG, and PPG levels in the Chinese population with T2D. Furthermore, IDegLira had a favourable safety profile that was consistent with previous studies [10–18].

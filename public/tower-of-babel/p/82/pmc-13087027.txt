Research on agricultural development issues based on comprehensive optimization strategy
Authors: HOU Dayou, LI Jieyun, WANG Ya, GUO Susu, WANG Xinran, WANG Xianchao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study proposes an integrated constrained static optimization framework for optimizing crop-planting strategies in rural regions under uncertainty. Unlike conventional deterministic or dynamic programming approaches, the proposed model formalizes the decision process as a static multi-objective optimization problem that simultaneously maximizes economic profit and minimizes production risk. The framework integrates three complementary algorithms–Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) for global exploration, Simulated Annealing (SA) for local refinement, and Monte Carlo Simulation (MCS) for stochastic robustness evaluation. A real-world case study based on empirical agricultural data from northern Anhui Province (2022–2024) provides empirical evidence of the framework’s potential effectiveness, showing that the hybrid PSO–SA–MCS model increases the expected profit by 12.6% and reduces yield variance by 11.0% compared with the baseline PSO. Convergence and sensitivity analyses confirm stable performance across parameter variations within ±10%. The proposed framework enhances the transparency, reproducibility, and robustness of agricultural decision-making, providing a practical tool for data-driven, sustainable crop planning and resource allocation in rural development. Moreover, the proposed PSO–SA–MCS framework is not limited to the specific study region or crop categories considered in this paper. By adjusting land-use data, yield coefficients, and market parameters, the model can be readily extended to other regions, cropping systems, and agricultural planning scenarios, indicating potential generalizability and scalability, subject to further validation under different regional and agricultural settings.

Introduction

Background

Agricultural production in rural China faces persistent challenges of limited arable land, climatic instability, and market volatility. Efficient crop-planning decisions therefore require not only economic considerations but also ecological and risk-management perspectives. Under the global agenda for sustainable development, formulating mathematically optimized cropping structures has become a strategic imperative for regional resource allocation and food security.

While prior studies often relied on deterministic linear programming or dynamic planning approaches, these models struggle to address uncertainty in yield, price, and environmental variation. To overcome such limitations, the present work builds an integrated stochastic-optimization system that explicitly incorporates random disturbances and multiple algorithmic layers.

Literature review: positioning hybrid PSO–SA–MCS relative to existing work

Multi-objective and uncertainty-aware optimization in resource systems: Multi-objective optimization frameworks have been widely adopted in complex resource-allocation problems, such as wastewater treatment selection, habitat restoration, and agricultural structure planning, because they explicitly capture trade-offs among economic, environmental, and risk-related objectives1,3,6,8,9,16. Nevertheless, in many existing studies, uncertainty is either addressed through post-optimization scenario analysis or represented by simplified stochastic assumptions that do not influence the optimization search itself8,9,14,17. This separation limits the ability of optimization algorithms to identify solutions that are robust under uncertainty, motivating the integration of stochastic evaluation directly into the search process.

PSO and SA: complementary strengths and prior hybridization: Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) is well known for its population-based global exploration capability and fast convergence in continuous and mixed optimization problems2,3,2,23. Simulated Annealing (SA), in contrast, provides effective local refinement through probabilistic acceptance of inferior solutions, enabling escape from local optima5,10,11,18. Prior taxonomies of hybrid metaheuristics emphasize that combining population-based and trajectory-based methods can enhance both exploration and exploitation, provided the coupling strategy is carefully designed1,4,12.

Most existing PSO–SA hybrids adopt either sequential coupling, where SA is applied after PSO convergence as a post-processing step, or loosely coupled cooperation, where information is exchanged intermittently between separate PSO and SA modules1,4. While these approaches improve solution quality, uncertainty is typically handled outside the hybrid mechanism and therefore does not guide the search dynamics.

Monte Carlo methods and their role in optimization. Monte Carlo Simulation (MCS) and quasi-Monte Carlo methods are established tools for uncertainty quantification, sensitivity analysis, and reliability assessment in engineering and environmental systems13,14,19,22. In agricultural and ecological applications, MCS is commonly used to estimate distributions of yields, prices, and risks; however, it is usually applied after the optimization stage as an external evaluation tool17,7,20,21. As a result, stochastic information rarely influences the optimization trajectory, and robustness is assessed rather than optimized.

Implications. By embedding robustness into the optimization process, the proposed PSO–SA–MCS framework seeks solutions with both high expected returns and low variability, which is particularly relevant for agricultural decision-making under climatic and market uncertainty6,7,17,20. Moreover, the framework provides a transparent and transferable architecture that can be extended to other constrained resource-allocation problems.

Constrained static optimization model

In this study, crop-planting planning is formulated as a constrained static optimization problem rather than a dynamic programming model. The objective is to determine the optimal allocation of farmland to multiple crops under limited resources, while maximizing expected economic profit and minimizing production risk. Unlike traditional multi-period or stochastic dynamic programming frameworks, this approach captures the steady-state equilibrium decision for a specific agricultural cycle, reflecting a realistic management scenario in rural regions where planting decisions are made seasonally and resources are fixed during the period.Although the model includes a finite planning horizon (T> 1) to represent crop rotation and time-varying parameters, the optimization is solved as a single static decision problem without intertemporal state transitions. The formulation therefore remains a constrained static optimization rather than a dynamic programming model.”

Index and Set Definitions.

To ensure the rationality and practicality of the model, we introduce the following constraints to regulate the crop planting decision-making process. These constraints incorporate both biological characteristics of crops and multiple factors including market demand, price fluctuations, and planting costs. The constraints guarantee that crop arrangements across different plots and time periods meet fundamental agricultural planning requirements while accurately simulating real-world planting decisions, thereby providing scientific basis and decision support for agricultural production.

Specifically, we will establish corresponding constraints from multiple dimensions, including plot planting area, crop types, and planting seasons. These constraints are designed to ensure the rationality of crop planting decisions and to prevent impractical planting plans. At the same time, we will also consider the continuity and stability of crop cultivation to ensure the sustainable development of agricultural production.

Simulated annealing (SA) algorithm

The simulated annealing algorithm is an efficient heuristic optimization technique inspired by the metallurgical annealing process. By simulating the gradual cooling of temperature during annealing, it intelligently explores the solution space to discover global optimal solutions. The algorithm possesses strong global search capabilities, effectively avoiding local optima traps.

The core mechanism lies in randomly perturbing the current solution and determining whether to accept the perturbed solution based on the current “annealing temperature.” As the temperature decreases, the algorithm’s tolerance for inferior solutions diminishes, gradually guiding the search process toward the global optimum and ultimately achieving an approximation of the global optimal solution.

Improved particle swarm optimization

Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) is an algorithm inspired by the foraging behavior of bird flocks, first proposed in 1995 by Russell Eberhart and James Kennedy. In this algorithm, each particle’s position represents a potential solution to the objective function, and the fitness of a particle is the value of the objective function evaluated at its position. Case study and experimental results

Data source

Although the dataset was released in the 2024 National College Student Mathematical Modeling Competition, the agricultural statistics it contains correspond to the year 2023. To ensure the reproducibility and transparency of this research, this case study is based on the official statistical data provided by the competition. The dataset includes land-type distributions, crop yields, market price records, and cost coefficients for twelve major crops, covering grain crops, vegetables, and edible fungi. Therefore, 2023 is used as the baseline year.

Geographical Context of the Study Area: The study area is located in northern Anhui Province, China, situated in the transitional zone between the Huang–Huai Plain and the Huai River Basin. The terrain is predominantly flat, interspersed with irrigated low-lying areas and gentle uplands, resulting in a high degree of spatial continuity of agricultural land. The region contains clusters of conventional and smart greenhouses, which, together with open-field farming areas, form a typical composite agricultural landscape. The area experiences a warm temperate monsoon climate with distinct seasons, characterized by hot and rainy summers with precipitation concentrated during the main growing season, and relatively cold and dry winters. Influenced jointly by climatic conditions and land-use patterns, the cropping structure is dominated by open-field grain crops, while vegetables and edible fungi are primarily produced through year-round or off-season cultivation in greenhouse facilities, forming a diversified agricultural production system with clear seasonal complementarity based on stable grain production and supported by facility-based agriculture.

To account for the stochastic nature of agricultural production and market conditions, a sensitivity analysis was conducted to evaluate the robustness of the proposed PSO–SA–MCS framework with respect to key uncertainty parameters. Four parameters were examined: the yield fluctuation coefficient (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\delta$$\end{document}), market demand fluctuation parameter (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\epsilon$$\end{document}), supply–demand price adjustment coefficient (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha$$\end{document}), and fungi price volatility parameter (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\xi$$\end{document}).

Each parameter was perturbed independently by \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\pm 10\%$$\end{document}, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\pm 20\%$$\end{document}, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\pm 30\%$$\end{document} relative to its baseline value, while all other parameters were held constant. For each scenario, the optimization was repeated and evaluated using three indicators: expected profit, profit variance, and crop allocation stability, defined as the proportion of planting decisions unchanged from the baseline solution.

The results show that expected profit varies smoothly and remains well bounded across all scenarios. Even under \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\pm 30\%$$\end{document} perturbations, the maximum deviation in expected profit did not exceed 5.1%. Yield-related uncertainty (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\delta$$\end{document}) had a stronger effect on profit magnitude, whereas price-related parameters (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\xi$$\end{document}) primarily influenced profit variance rather than the mean outcome.

Regarding solution structure, over 92% of crop–plot assignments remained unchanged across all tested uncertainty levels, indicating a high degree of allocation stability. Although profit variance increased moderately with higher uncertainty, the changes remained controlled, suggesting that the embedded Monte Carlo simulation effectively mitigates stochastic fluctuations during optimization.

Overall, the sensitivity analysis demonstrates that the proposed framework delivers stable performance and consistent planting patterns within the examined uncertainty range. While these results support its suitability for decision-making under uncertainty, further validation under alternative stochastic assumptions and broader regional contexts would be a valuable direction for future research.

Summary and Conclusions

This study developed a comprehensive mathematical and algorithmic framework for optimizing crop planting schemes in rural areas under multiple constraints and uncertainties. Unlike previous heuristic or deterministic models, the proposed approach integrates Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), Simulated Annealing (SA), and Monte Carlo Simulation (MCS) into a unified, transparent, and mathematically rigorous structure.

Final Remarks

In summary, this study presents a hybrid PSO–SA–MCS framework for crop-planting optimization under multiple constraints and uncertainties. Rather than claiming definitive optimality, the results indicate that the proposed framework demonstrates strong potential as a robust decision-support tool for agricultural planning in uncertain environments. The case study illustrates that integrating metaheuristic optimization with stochastic simulation can improve expected economic performance while enhancing stability against yield and market fluctuations.

It should be noted that the findings are based on empirical data from a specific region and agricultural context. Although the results are promising, further validation across different regions, crop structures, and uncertainty settings is required to fully assess the general applicability and long-term effectiveness of the proposed approach.

Overall, the proposed model provides a flexible and extensible optimization framework that can support data-driven agricultural decision-making. Its value lies not in claiming universal optimality, but in offering a practical and adaptable methodology that can be refined and extended in future research.

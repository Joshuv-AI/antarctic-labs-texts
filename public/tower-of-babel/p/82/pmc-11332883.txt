Entrepreneur's social identity, decision logic, and resource bootstrapping behaviors: An empirical study of the fsQCA method
Authors: Yue Ling Liu, Xue Bing Peng, Meng Ting Wang, Lu Lu Chen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
While resource bootstrapping is widely employed by novice entrepreneurs,and social identities and decision logic are studied as important antecedents of entrepreneurs' adoption of resource bootstrapping behaviors, both scholars and practitioners have but a hazy understanding on the configuration effect of entrepreneur's social identities and decision logic on resource bootstrapping behaviors. In this paper, we combined the necessary condition analysis (NCA) and fuzzy set qualitative comparative analysis (fsQCA) to explore the configuration effects of entrepreneur's social identity and decision logic on resource bootstrapping behaviors. Analysis of 411 questionnaire date acquired from novice entrepreneurs, we found that none of single antecedent condition of both the entrepreneur's social identity and decision logic constitute the necessary condition of novice entrepreneurs' high resource bootstrapping behaviors. We also found a substitutive relationship between Darwinian social identity and communitary social identity that will lead the novice entrepreneurs with high dual effectual-causal decision logic to choose high joint utilization bootstrapping, and a complementary relationship between the three social identities of novice entrepreneurs such that those with high dual effectual-causal decision logic prefer different resource bootstrapping behaviors because of different social identities.Furthermore, we found that the core and edge conditions in the decision logic play different roles in the resource bootstrapping behaviors of novice entrepreneurs, with the core decision logic conditions having a greater impact on the resource bootstrapping behaviors. These findings deepen our understanding of the causal complexity among entrepreneur's social identity, decision logic and resource bootstrapping behaviors. It also provides theoretical guidance for effective resource integration for novice entrepreneurs facing resource constraints.

Introduction

Novice entrepreneurs usually face the constraints of acquiring resources, so they often adopt informal means of sourcing resources other than formal methods such as venture or angel capital [1,2]. As a viable choice for novice entrepreneurs to break through resource constraints dilemma, resource bootstrapping relys on the entrepreneurs’ own resource in hand to reduce dependence on external resources and invests limited resources in phase [3], and is regarded as a typical entrepreneurial behavior of small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) [4]. However, the antecedents of resource bootstrapping are still poorly understood [4].

Recently, the social identity of entrepreneurs has become an important research perspective for exploring the antecedents of resource bootstrapping behavior. According to the theory of social identity, entrepreneurs usually attribute themeselves to those social identity that they recognize through social comparison and self-categorization [5,6]. Studies have found that different entrepreneur's social identities influence entrepreneur's resource acquisition behavior in a predictable and meaningful way [7,8]. However, the configuration effect of entrepreneur's social identities on resource bootstrapping call for more empirical research, since the entrepreneurs own identity model is often in a more complex, multiple, and chaotic multiple identity “tangled” state, rather than a linear, dual, and one-way relationship [9]. Peng, Chen & Liu's [10] studies of configuration effect of entrepreneur's ascribed identities and achieved identites on resource bootstrapping behaviors provide a valuable perspective to explore the effect of multiple identities on resource bootstrapping.However, more entrepreneur's social identities antecedent configurations of resource bootstrapping are needed to be empirical studied.

The logic of the entrepreneur's decision has also been found an important antecedent of resource bootstrapping behavior. The effectuation theory argues that causation and effectuation are decision-making logic that reflect the different behavioral rationality of entrepreneurs [11,12]. Previous studies have found that decision logic act as an important predictor of entrepreneurs' actions, such as coopetition [13], venture creation [14], etc., but few researches tackle it in the context of resource bootstrapping. Recently, Peng,Li & Liu found that novice entrepreneurs who prefer causation are more inclined to engage in anti-resource constraint behavior of resource bootstrapping [15]. Yet, while a mixed decision-making logic of effectuation and causation are found to play a simultaneously role during the entrepreneurial process [13,14,16], little is known on how hybrid decision logic affects resource bootstrapping.

Actually, the entrepreneurs' social identity and decision-making logic could simultaneously affect resource bootstrapping behaviors. According to the theory of effectuation, the entrepreneurs who adopt the effectual logic will carry out entrepreneurial activities based on the social identity of who they are [11], which means that the entrepreneurial behavior are affected by the entrepreneur's social identity and decision-making logic simultaneously.The social identity of entrepreneurs not only explains who the entrepreneurs are and what they do, but also provides a theoretical framework for explaining the specific decision-making logic of entrepreneurs [17]. Alsos, Clausen & Hytti [18], for example, validated that while entrepreneurs with Darwinian social identity or communitary social identity prefer to causation, entrepreneurs with missionary social identity prefer to effectuation. Although Liu, Peng and Huang [19] found that there is an interaction effect of entrepreneurs's social identities and causal decision-making logic on resource bootstrapping behaviors, little is known regarding how the combinations of entrepreneur's social identities and effectual/causal decision-making logics affect their choice of resource bootstrapping behavior.

To advance research on the complex effects of both entrepreneur's social identities and decision-making logic on resource bootstrapping behaviours, this study adopts a configuration perspective to combine NCA and fsQCA and integrates five antecedents of the entrepreneur's social identity and decision logic to analyze the necessary and sufficient types of causally complex relationship between entrepreneur's social identities, decision-making logic and resource bootstrapping behaviors. This study attempts to contribute to the existing literature in three ways. First, the study enriches and deepens the understanding of the antecedent conditions of resouce bootstrapping behaviors by combining NCA and fsQCA to explore whether and to what extent the social identity and decision-making logic of entrepreneurs are the necessary conditions of resources bootstrapping behavior. Second, by exploiting configuration perspective, the study sheds light on the complex effect mechanism of different combinations of social identities and decision-making logic on entrepreneur's resource bootstrapping behaviors,and effectively distinguishes the core conditions and edge conditions that affect resource bootstrapping behavior, making up for the deficiency that net effect analysis can not explain the multi-cause concurrent effects in reality. Analyzing the configuration mechanism also provides a more fine-grained explanation for the causally complex relationship among entrepreneur's social identity, effectual/causal logic and resource bootstrapping behavior.Third, by comparing different antecedent configurations, the study highlights the complementary and substitution effect of entrepreneur's social identities and decision-making logic and sheds light on the “multi cause concurrence” and “equifinality” effects of various antecedent configurations on resource bootstrapping behavior.

Theoretical background and research framework

Bootstrapping was originally used in finance as informal access to resources, whether or not they are owned or controlled by the entrepreneur [3]. Financial bootstrapping was defined by Winborg and Landström [20] as the use of methods for meeting the need for resources that do not rely on long-term external financing from debt holders and/or new owners. Jayawarna, Jones & Marlow [3] divided bootstrapping in the entrepreneurial field into three dimensions. (1)Payment-related bootstrapping: This dimension likely focuses on strategies related to managing and optimizing payments, such as delaying payments, negotiating favorable terms, or finding alternative payment methods. (2)Joint-utilization bootstrapping: This dimension likely focuses on strategies related to sharing resources and collaborating with others, such as sharing office space, equipment, or expertise. (3)Owner-related bootstrapping: This dimension likely focuses on strategies related to the entrepreneur's personal contributions, such as working long hours, taking on multiple roles, or leveraging their personal network.

Social identity refers to how individuals define themselves based on their membership in social groups or categories. It involves the use of social categories, such as race, gender, religion, nationality, or occupation to construct a sense of self and belonging [21]. When individuals identify with a particular group, they often adopt the group's characteristics, values, and behaviors, which can shape their self-concept and influence their interactions with others. According to Fauchart and Gruber [22], entrepreneurs can be classified into three social identities: (1)Darwinian social identity: This identity is characterized by a focus on individual success and competition. Entrepreneurs with this identity prioritize their own goals and are driven by a desire to outperform others. (2)Communitarian social identity:This identity is characterized by a focus on collaboration and community building. Entrepreneurs with this identity prioritize the well-being of their team and community and are driven by a desire to create positive social impact. (3)Missionary social identity:This identity is characterized by a focus on a specific cause or mission. Entrepreneurs with this identity are driven by a strong belief in their product or service and its ability to make a difference in the world [6,22]. Since entrepreneurship is inherently social, entrepreneurs' behaviour is shaped by their social identity [6,22]. Liu, Peng and Huang [19] validated that entrepreneurs with different social identities of Darwinian, communitarian, and missionary social identity adopt different resource bootstrapping behaviors of payment-related, joint-utilization and owner-related bootstrapping.

The effectuation theory suggests that entrepreneurs use two alternative decision-making logics: effectuation and causation. Effectuation is a logic of means oriented that emphasizes the use of available resources and the creation of new opportunities, while causation is a logic of ends-oriented that focuses on achieving specific goals through careful planning and analysis of existing conditions. Entrepreneurs may use either logic or switch between them depending on the situation and their goals [11,12]. Decision-making logic is the thinking guide for entrepreneurs to take action. Entrepreneurs usually carry out entrepreneurial activities based on their own decision-making logic [[23], [24], [25]]. Although it is found that entrepreneurs who prefer causation are more inclined to engage in anti-resource constraint behavior of resource bootstrapping [15], the configuration effect of effectual and causal hybrid decision-making logic on resource bootstrapping is insufficiently understood and more empirical research are needed to validate this complex effect.

Whilst the effects of social identity and decision-making logic on resource bootstrapping have garnered significant attention, they have predominantly been examined in isolation. However, there is a strong priori reason to anticipate that resource bootstrapping is influenced in distinct ways by various combinations of antecedents related to social identity and decision-making logic. The theory of effectuation argues that entrepreneurs who prefer effectual logic will act in accordance with their recognized social identity of who they are [11]. Liu,Peng and Huang [19], for example, found that both entrepreneur's social identities and causal decision-making logic have positive effect on resource bootstrapping behaviors. Methods

Combining NCA and fsQCA

Combining NCA and fsQCA offers a comprehensive and nuanced approach to analyzing complex phenomena across various domains. This integration allows for a deeper understanding of the intricate causal relationships that underlie outcomes by leveraging the complementary strengths of both methods.Necessity and sufficiency are two fundamental aspects of causal analysis. A necessary condition refers to a factor that is indispensable for achieving a particular outcome, however, its presence alone is not sufficient to guarantee the occurrence of that outcome.Conversely, a sufficient condition is one where the presence of the factor invariably leads to the outcome, although other conditions may also independently produce the same result. The combination of necessity and sufficiency analysis helps to better explain the antecedent condition mechanism of an outcome, and yield results that are more precise or complete [26,27]. The goal of fsQCA is to identify variables (conditions) or groups of variables (configurations or'relations) that are either necessary or sufficient for the occurrence of a specific outcome. FsQCA focuses on making categorical statements(e.g., a condition or configuration is necessary or not for an outcome), thereby disregarding the variation in degree among the conditions or configurations. NCA,however, analyzes the extent of antecedent conditions from a quantitative perspective, and is capable of making statements in degree, indicating whether a specific level of a condition is necessary or not for achieving a specific level of the outcome) [27], effectively making up for the lack of some necessary conditions that may be ignored in the existing adequacy analysis method [28]. Thus, drawing on previous studies such as Du and kim [29], this study conducts both necessary and sufficiency causality analysis to explore whether any entrepreneur's social identity or effectual/causal decision-making logic is necessary and/or sufficient for different high resource bootstrapping behavior.

Sample

The data used in this article are derived from a questionnaire survey. An electronic questionnaires was sent to 1097 enterprises located in Hangzhou economic and Technological Development Zone. The study followed ethical principles and obtained the ethical approval by the approving ethical committee of Zhejiang Institute of Economics and Trade.The specific serial number that was assigned to this study is ZJIET-20240206015. The informed consent of all participants were also obtained since all participants were informed of purpose, methodology, potential risks and benefits of this study, and given ample time to consider whether they are willing to participate. A total of 794 questionnaires were returned, resulting in a return rate of 72.38 %. From the returned questionnaires, 411 valid questionnaires were selected for analysis after non-standard invalid questionnaires were rejected, which gives a valid questionnaire return rate of 37.46 %. Variable measurement

Resources bootstrapping. The measurement of resource bootstrapping by Jayawarna et al. [3] were exploited. There are three dimensions of resource bootstrapping: payment-related bootstrapping, joint-utilization bootstrapping, and owner-related bootstrapping, with 6, 4, and 4 measurement items respectively. The Cronbach's α value for them are 0.652,0.653, and 0.623, respectively. Although Cronbach's α is typically required to be higher than 0.70 in regression analysis. Since the correlations of variables are accepted in QCA, the measurement reliability can be appropriately reduced [10]. Cronbach's α value of 0.61 is also found in the study of QCA research such as Du and Kim [29]. Therefore,a Cronbach's α value lower than 0.7 is considerated acceptable.

Entrepreneur's social identity. The measurement of entrepreneur's social identity developed by Sieger, Gruber & Fauchart [8] were exploited. There are three dimensions of entrepreneur's social identities: Darwinian, communitarian, and missionary, including 5 measurement items respectively. The Cronbach's α of them are 0.646, 0.732, and 0.786 respectively.

Decision-making logic. The measurement of decision-making logic developed by Futterer, Schmidt & Heidenreich [30] was exploited. Among their measurement, effectuation is divided into four dimensions: means-oriented, affordable loss, focus on partnerships, and acknowledging the unexpected, with a total of 16 measurement items. Causation is divided into four dimensions: goals-orientated, expected returns, competitive analysis, and avoiding the unexpected, with a total of 16 measurement items. Finally, the Cronbach's α of effectuation and causation are 0.786 and 0.808 respectively.

In the follow-up analysis, the mean value of each measurement item was taken for each variable.

Variable calibration

FsQCA is a set-theoretic approach that assesses cases based on their set-membership, necessitating calibration to transform the raw data measured on interval scales into fuzzy-set scores ranging from 0 to 1 [31,32]. In this paper, we use direct calibration method to convert the data into the membership score of fuzzy sets. According to Ragin [33], more than 95 % of the cases in the case set are fully in-point,while less than 5 % are fully out-of-point. The analysis of the necessary conditions of payment related and owner-related bootstrapping methods showed consistent results between fsQCA and NCA, However, the analysis of joint-utilization bootstrapping yielded different results between the two approaches. In fact, there is no conflict between fsQCA and NCA in terms of the analysis results of necessary conditions for joint-utilization bootstrapping. Because fsQCA and NCA have different judgment criteria for necessary conditions, the necessary conditions in NCA refer to that X is necessary to Y at a lower level of X [26] and the necessary conditions in fsQCA refer to the necessary conditions to meet a certain membership degree. From this point, the lower level of effectuation is the necessary condition for entrepreneurs to make a certain degree of joint-utilization bootstrapping behavior, while the high effectuation is not the necessary condition for high joint-utilization bootstrapping behavior. To sum up, it is believed that there is no necessary condition of the exploited factors that leads to the high resource bootstrapping behavior. This indicates that the antecedents driving resource bootstrapping are more complex than the conclusions drawn from necessity analysis, and further analysis of the configuration effect of entrepreneur's social identities and decision logic on resource bootstrapping behavior is needed [10].

Sufficiency analysis

The software fsQCA3.0 is used to further analyze the social identity and decision logic configuration that leads to the entrepreneurs choosing different resource bootstrapping behaviors. Following established QCA practice [31,37,38], we conducted a fuzzy-set analysis of necessary conditions using the raw consistency benchmark ≥0.80, the PRI consistency benchmark ≥0.70, and the case frequency benchmark ≥6. Robustness test

Robustness test is carried out through the method of adjusting the consistency threshold [39]. Schneider and Wagemann [32] argued that the raw consistency benchmark chosen by researchers will determine the number of configurations during the process of minimum analysis, ultimately influencing the final analysis results. Theoretically, raising the raw consistency threshold will result in a reduction in the number of truth tables included in the minimum analysis,as well as a decrease in the number of cases. Therefore, it is difficult to maximize the simplified configuration, ultimately leading to a new configuration that is a subset of the previous one. Based on this idea, the raw consistency benchmark was increased to 0.85, and the analysis results are presented in Table 7. Analysis and discussion

Discussion

Firstly, based on the causal relationship of necessity, it is observend that no single antecedent condition constitutes a necessary condition for novice entrepreneur's high resource bootstrapping behavior. Although previous studies found that novice entrepreneur who prefer causal logic are more willing to take resource bootstrapping behavior [15] and resource bootstrapping behaviors of entrepreneurs are influenced by their different social identities [40], this study finds that none of the single antecedents of social identity and decision-making logic constitute the necessary condition for novice entrepreneurs to engage in high resource bootstrapping behavior. The finding is consistent with Peng, Chen and Liu [10] that none of the antecedents of ascribed identities and acquired identities is the necessary contitions of bootstrapping.

Second, through the sufficiency analysis of fsQCA method, it is found that there are 6 social identity-decision logic antecedent configurations of resource bootstrapping behavior, which respectively influence the choice of resource bootstrapping behavior of novice entrepreneurs with complementary or substitution relations. Both Darwinian entrepreneurs with high effectual decision-making logic and communitary entrepreneurs with high dual effectual-causal decision logic prefer to payment related bootstrapping behavior(S1 and S2). The Dual Darwinian-missionary entrepreneurs with dual high effectual-causal decision-making logic prefer to joint utilization bootstrapping behavior(S3). Darwinian social identity and communitary social identity are substitutes for each other. Both high Darwinian and high communitary novice entrepreneurs with non-high missionary social identity and high dual effectual-causal decision-making logic prefer to owner-related bootstrapping behavior(S4 and S5). Dual Darwinian-communitary entrepreneurs with high dual effectual-causal decision-making logic prefer to owner-related bootstrapping behavior (S6). Different from the net effect analysis of the effect of social identity and decision-making logic on resource bootstrapping behavior conducted in previous studies (eg. Refs. [15,19,40]), this research adopts a different approach and breaks through the limitations of the research hypothesis that each antecedent variable is independent of each other, and also validated that the hybrid combination of entrepreneur's social identities and decision-making logic has a configuration effect on resource bootstrapping behaviors [16,22]. Echoing to the call made by Liu,Peng & Huang [19],this study employs the QCA method to reveal the causally complex relationship between social identities and bootstrapping behavior, this study expands the research perspective and research space of the causes of resource bootstrapping behavior.

Third, through the comparative analysis of the antecedent configuration of resource bootstrapping behavior, the paper draws the following findings. (1) Entrepreneurs with the same dual decision-making logic but exhibit different configurations social identities tend to prefer distinct resource bootstrapping behaviors.Entrepreneurs with dual decision-making logics, a high communitarian identity and a non-high missionary identity tend to prefer high payment related resource bootstrapping.On the other hand, those who possess high Darwinian and high missionary identities along with a non-high communitarian identity prefer high joint-utilization resource bootstrapping. Meanwhile, entrepreneurs with a high Darwinian indentity and a non-high missionary identity, or a high communitarian and missionary identity or a high Darwinian and communitarian indentity tend to prefer high owner-related bootstrapping behavior. There exist complementary relationship between high communitary social identity and high Darwinian social identity, non-high missionary social identity and high Darwinian social identity, which affect the choice of resource bootstrapping behavior.These findings not only provides a more fine-grained explanation for the causally complex relationship among entrepreneur's social identity, decision-making logic and resource bootstrapping behavior, but also provides scientific guidance for novice entrepreneurs with different decision-making logic and social identity to carry out resource bootstrapping practice, so that their resource bootstrapping behavior choice can better match their social identity and decision-making logic combination. (2) The core decision-making logic conditions play a pivotal role in the choice of resource bootstrapping behavior for novice entrepreneurs. With the same high communitary social identity, non-high missionary social identity as peripheral conditions and effectuation as core conditions, the entrepreneurs who have high causation as peripheral condition prefer to payment-related bootstrapping, while the entrepreneurs who have causation as core condition prefer to owner-related bootstrapping behaviors. This finding verifies the analysis of Reymen et al. [14] who argued that entrepreneurs make decisions based on the dominant logic. It also enriches the research framework of “social identity-bootstrapping behavior” by Peng, Chen and Liu al. [10],and offers a more fine-grained explanation for the variations in entrepreneur's resource bootstrapping behavior, considering the driving antecedents of social identity and decision logic.

The study provides practical guidance for novices entrepreneurs. Novice entrepreneurs can choose appropriate resource bootstrapping behavior according to their unique combination of social identity and preferred decision-making logic preferences. They can also adopt new resource bootstrapping behavior by altering their perception of social identity and the preference for decision-making logic, to better cope with the dilemma of insufficient resources. On the other hand, the empirical results of the substitution relationship between the various antecedent configuration conditions show that the different choice of resource bootstrapping behavior by novice entrepreneurs is a collaborative effect of multiple factors, along with the effective integration of various factors affects their choices of resource bootstrapping behavior in “the same way”. For example, novice entrepreneurs with high dual decision-making logic of effectuation and causation, even if their social identity is changed from Darwinian to communitary type, after configuration, the most appropriate bootstrapping behavior is still owner-related bootstrapping, and the resource bootstrapping behavior of novice entrepreneurs does not need to be adjusted due to the change of their social identity. Therefore, novice entrepreneurs can make full use of the substitution relationship between various antecedents to choose the most suitable resource bootstrapping behavior.

Limitations and future research

Despite our utmost efforts, this study is still has some limitations, and further studies are required to address them. First, this paper examines the influence of the interplay between two types of antecedents, namely social identity and decision-making logic, on resource bootstrapping behavior among novice entrepreneurs. However, the driven mechanism of resource bootstrapping behavior is very complex, and previous related studies have examined other influencing factors, such as the work experience of entrepreneurs, social networks, and external environment. Future research could combine these factors in the research framework to explore more complex driving mechanism of resource bootstrapping behavior. Second, from the configuration perspective, this paper delves into the antecedent configuration of social identity-decision-making logic that leads to the different choices of resource bootstrapping behavior of novice entrepreneurs. But we do not conduct in-depth analysis and interpretation combined with the cases covered by various configurations. Future research can be combined with specific cases to further analyze the impact of various configurations on resource bootstrapping behavior of entrepreneurs, to enhance understanding of the effect of antecedent configuration of social identity and decision-making logic on resource bootstrapping behavior. Third, since the empirical data in this study is obtained from a questionnaire survey and may therefore have subjective bias, future related research could obtain objective data for analysis.

Findings

The research question of this study was, “Which configurations of social identities and decision-making logics are associated with novice entrepreneur's resource bootstrapping behaviors".We discovered six configurations. Both Darwinian entrepreneurs with high effectual decision-making logic and communitary entrepreneurs with high dual effectual-causal decision logic prefer to payment-related bootstrapping behavior. The Dual high Darwinian-missionary entrepreneurs with dual high effectual-causal decision-making logic prefer to joint-utilization bootstrapping behavior. Darwinian social identity and communitary social identity are substitutes for each other. Both high Darwinian and high communitary novice entrepreneurs with non-high missionary social identity and high dual effectual-causal decision-making logic prefer to owner-related bootstrapping behavior. Dual Darwinian-communitary entrepreneurs with high dual effectual-causal decision-making logic prefer to owner-related bootstrapping behavior. The entrepreneurs with the same dual decision-making logic but differing social identities configurations tend to prefer different resource bootstrapping behaviors.Those entrepreneurs with dual decision-making logics, high communitarian social identity and non-high missionary social identity tend to prefer high payment related bootstrapping behavior, those with high Darwinian, high missionary social identities and non-high communitarian social identity tend to prefer high joint-utilization bootstrapping behavior, while those with high social indentity and non-high missionary social identitie, or high communitarian social identity and missionary social identities, or high Darwinian social indentity and high communitarian social indentity tend to prefer high owner-related bootstrapping behavior. There exist complementary relationship between high communitary social identity and high Darwinian social identity, non-high missionary social identity and high Darwinian social identity, which affect the choice of resource bootstrapping behavior. The core decision-making logic conditions play a key role during the choice of resource bootstrapping behavior for novice entrepreneurs. The entrepreneurs with the same high communitarian social identity, non-high missionary social identity as peripheral conditions and effectuation as core conditions, while those who having high causation as peripheral condition prefer payment related bootstrapping, those who having causation as core condition prefer owner related bootstrapping behaviors.

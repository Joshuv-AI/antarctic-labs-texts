Identification of radiotherapy-sensitive genes for predicting prognosis, immune microenvironment, and drug sensitivity in uterine carcinosarcoma patients
Authors: Ying Zhou, Ran Cui, Da Lei, Shun Gao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Uterine carcinosarcoma is a rare gynecological malignancy characterized by high invasiveness and poor prognosis. At present, common treatment methods include surgery, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy. Radiotherapy can induce tumor cells to produce reactive oxygen species through ionizing radiation, leading to damage to intracellular DNA and mediating tumor cell death. Based on this, we identified a novel radiotherapy sensitive gene for predicting the prognosis, immune microenvironment, and drug sensitivity of uterine carcinosarcoma patients.Methods:The Cancer Genome Atlas database was used to obtain genetic and clinical information of patients with uterine carcinosarcoma. A risk scoring model was built using Lasso regression model. In order to enhance the predictive ability of the model, a column chart for prediction was created and calibration curves were used. Gene set enrichment analysis was used to evaluate pathway enrichment in patients with different risk cohorts. Finally, we investigated drug sensitivity between high-risk and low-risk cohorts.Results:We found that the survival rate of patients who received radiotherapy was significantly higher than that of patients who did not receive radiotherapy. In the constructed risk scoring model, high-risk patients have a worse prognosis. Pathway enrichment indicates that high-risk patients are enriched in regulating tumor cell growth, metabolism, and angiogenesis pathways, which may be a reason for poor prognosis in high-risk patients. High risk and low-risk patients have different sensitivities to different drugs. The 1, 3, and 5-year survival values predicted by the receiver operating characteristic curve were 0.82, 0.93, and 0.96, respectively, indicating the reliability of our prediction model. Finally, multiple regression analysis showed that the radiation therapy sensitive genes SSBP2, ELAVL3, and CST1 in the model can independently affect the prognosis of patients with uterine carcinosarcoma. Patients with high expression of SSBP2 have a better prognosis than those with low expression, while patients with high expression of ELAVL3 and CST1 have a poorer prognosis.Conclusion:We have developed a scoring method for uterine carcinosarcoma based on the effectiveness of radiotherapy. This method can evaluate the prognosis of patients with uterine carcinosarcoma and has certain guiding significance for the clinical treatment of subsequent uterine carcinosarcoma patients.

1. Introduction

Uterine carcinosarcoma, also known as malignant mixed Mullerian duct tumor, is a rare gynecological malignancy characterized by poor prognosis.[1] This “biphasic tumor” exhibits a mixture of epithelial and mesenchymal/sarcomatoid tumor cells. In previous clinical studies, 79 patients with uterine carcinosarcoma underwent staged surgery without residual tumors after surgery. Among these patients, 74 patients received adjuvant therapy, but adjuvant therapy did not reduce recurrence or improve survival rate.[2] Related studies have shown that Rac GTPase Activating Protein 1 (RACGAP1) is highly upregulated in uterine carcinosarcoma. The depletion or inhibition of RACGAP1 eliminates the motility and invasiveness of cancer sarcoma cells, while overexpression of RACGAP1 endows endometrial adenocarcinoma cells with invasiveness, indicating that the RACGAP1-STAT3 survivor signaling pathway is essential for the invasive phenotype of uterine cancer sarcoma.[3] The latest research indicates a significant correlation between elevated expression levels of apoptosis inducing factor mitochondria associated 1 (AIFM1) and poor prognosis in Uterine carcinosarcoma.[4] Research has shown that the current treatment methods for cervical malignant tumors mainly include surgery, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy.[1,5]

Radiotherapy is a common approach for tumors. From a mechanistic perspective, radiotherapy can induce tumor cells to produce reactive oxygen species through the generated ionizing radiation, leading to damage to intracellular DNA and mediating tumor cell death.[6,7] In addition, radiotherapy induced tumor cell death and antitumor immune activation have been widely studied. For example, radiotherapy can induce upregulation of caspase 3 in cells, thereby inducing cell apoptosis.[8] Radiation induced ATM activation mediates the downregulation of SLC7A11 expression to reduce the synthesis of glutathione, thereby inducing ferroptosis in tumor cells. Radiation induced ATM activation can also promote ferroptosis through the metal regulated transcription factor 1 (MTF1) - ferritin/FPN1 axis.[9,10] Related studies have reported that when radiation induced DNA damage depletes NAD+ and ATP, it leads to excessive activation of polyadenylation polymerase 1 (PAPR1). This energy imbalance can activate autophagy in rectal cancer tumor cells through the AMPK pathway.[11] For the activation of tumor immunity, radiotherapy can induce the exposure of CD8+ and CD4+ cell antigens, thereby mediating the recognition and killing of tumor cells by T cells in the body.[12] Low dose radiotherapy can differentiate macrophages in pancreatic cancer into iNOS+/M1 phenotype, thus coordinating the killing of effective T cells on pancreatic cancer tumor cells.[13] The latest research shows that when comparing the prognostic outcomes of early uterine carcinosarcoma patients receiving adjuvant chemotherapy (CT) and pelvic external radiotherapy (EBRT) with CT and vaginal brachytherapy (VBT) versus radiotherapy alone (EBRT or VBT), adjuvant CT+ EBRT instead of CT+ VBT improves outcomes compared to radiotherapy alone.[14] However, the function and prognostic value of radiotherapy sensitive genes in uterine carcinosarcoma are still unclear.

Therefore, this study aims to construct a radiotherapy sensitive gene model that can effectively predict the prognosis of uterine carcinosarcoma and explore its potential mechanisms.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Datasets

We downloaded clinical patient data and RNA transcriptome data for cervical cancer, endometrial cancer and uterine carcinosarcoma from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database as training set data (https://www.cancer.gov/about-nci/organization/ccg/research/structural-genomics/tcga). The GSE119043 dataset in gene expression omnibus data base database is used as validation set data for uterine carcinosarcoma models (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE119043).

The TCGA dataset contains clinical information for 57 patients, with 25 patients receiving radiotherapy and 29 patients not receiving radiotherapy.

The GSE119043 dataset contains clinical information for 90 patients, with 50 patients having gene expression information.

2.2. Immune microenvironment analysis

We used the method in TIMER (TIMER: A web server for comprehensive analysis of tumor-infiltrating immune cells) to calculate the immune infiltration cell score for each sample.[15] We reevaluated and visualized the infiltration scores in B cells, T cells CD4, T cells CD8, neutrophil, macrophage, and DCs for each patient in uterine carcinosarcoma based on gene expression.

2.3. Differential gene expression analysis

Limma is a differential expression screening method based on a generalized linear model.[16] Here, we use the R software package limma (version 3.40.6) for differential analysis to obtain differentially expressed genes between different comparison groups (not radiotherapy) and control groups (radiotherapy). Specifically, the expression profile dataset we obtained was subjected to multiple linear regression using the lmFit function, followed by computed moderated t-statistics using the eBays function, moderated F-statistic, and log-odds of differential expression by empirical Bayes moderation of the standard errors towards a common value, ultimately obtaining the significance of differences for each gene. In our study, the comparative group consisted of uterine carcinosarcoma patients who received radiotherapy, while the control group consisted of uterine carcinosarcoma patients who did not receive radiotherapy. We positioned the differentially expressed genes identification criteria between the comparison group and the control group as |log2 FC| > 1.5 and P < .05.

2.4. Functional enrichment analysis

We used gene ontology and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes to analyze the pathways and biological behaviors of gene regulation that differ from radiotherapy. We used the R “clusterprofiler” software package to perform enrichment analysis on annotated genes.

2.5. Multiple regression analysis

We used survival time, survival status, and Lasso regression analysis to screen for differential gene expression levels in uterine carcinosarcoma patients and evaluated the prognostic significance of each gene using Cox multiple regression analysis. P < .05 has statistical significance.

2.6. Lasso regression analysis

For lasso regression analysis, we used the R software package glmnet to integrate survival time, survival status, and gene expression data, and used lasso cox regression analysis method for regression analysis. In addition, we also set up a 3-fold cross validation to obtain the optimal model.

2.7. GSEA analysis

For Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) analysis, we obtained the GSEA software (version 3.0) from the GSEA website. We divided the samples into 2 groups based on the risk score of uterine carcinosarcoma patients and downloaded a subset of c2cp.kegg.v7.4.symbols.gmt from the Molecular Signatures Database to evaluate the molecular mechanisms.[17]

2.8. Drug sensitivity prediction analysis

We use the drug prediction algorithm oncopredict[18] for drug prediction analysis. Specifically, we selected the corresponding cervical tissue as the training set for the model and CTRP2 as the cell line data for the training set. The filtering criteria are P-value of <.05, |log2 FC| > 1.0.

2.9. Nomogram analysis

The R package rms is used to construct nomogram. We included survival time, survival status, age, stage, and riskscore of uterine carcinosarcoma patients to construct a nomogram to predict the 1, 3, and 5-year survival of uterine carcinosarcoma. The overall C-index of the model is 0.775, 95% CI (0.687–0.864), P value = 1.057 × 10–9.

2.10. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis

We conducted ROC analysis using the R software package pROC (version 1.17.0.1) to obtain AUC. We conducted ROC analysis using the ROC function of pROC for 1, 3 and 5 years, and evaluated AUC and confidence intervals using the functions in pROC to obtain the final AUC results.

2.11. Survival analysis

Patients were divided into 2 groups based on the median risk score of uterine carcinosarcoma patients. Patients with risk scores greater than or equal to the median were classified as high-risk patients, while those with risk scores less than the median were classified as low-risk patients. We further analyzed the prognostic differences between the 2 groups using the R software package survival’s survey function, and evaluated the significance of prognostic differences between different groups of samples using the logrank test method. P value of <.05 indicates significant prognostic differences.

2.12. Statistical analysis

Univariate regression analysis, univariate regression analysis and lasso regression analysis were conducted using the COX regression model. Survival analysis uses the logrank test method to evaluate future prognostic differences between different subgroup samples. For the data results, we compared the inter group data using t tests, Wilcoxon tests, and 1-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) based on the data type. The expression of P < .05 has statistical significance and the significance level is expressed as *P < .05, **P < .01, ***P < .001, and ****P < .0001. No significant difference is represented by “ns.”

3. Results

3.1. Analysis of the prognostic and immune microenvironment effects of radiotherapy on uterine malignant tumors

To investigate whether radiotherapy affects the prognosis of patients with uterine tumors, we downloaded clinical information of patients with cervical carcinoma, endometrial cancer and uterine carcinosarcoma from the TCGA database. We divided patients into 2 groups based on whether they have received radiotherapy: the group receiving radiotherapy and the group not receiving radiotherapy. We found that patients with uterine carcinosarcoma had significantly higher overall survival (OS) after receiving radiotherapy compared to those who did not receive radiotherapy. However, radiotherapy did not affect the overall survival (OS) of patients with cervical carcinoma and endometrial cancer (Fig. 2A–C). According to existing research reports, radiotherapy can release antigens from tumors, thereby affecting the immune microenvironment of tumors.[19–21] Therefore, we used the TIMER algorithm to study the differences in immune cell expression in the tumor microenvironment between patients receiving radiotherapy and those not receiving radiotherapy in uterine carcinosarcoma. We found that the infiltration and expression levels of B cells in the tumor microenvironment of patients with uterine carcinosarcoma who received radiotherapy were higher than those who did not receive radiotherapy. However, the infiltration and expression levels of other immune cells were not related to whether they received radiotherapy (Fig. 2D–I).

(D–I) Immune cell infiltration in patients with uterine carcinosarcoma. The relationship between radiotherapy and infiltration of 6 types of immune cells.

3.2. Enrichment analysis of differentially expressed genes in radiotherapy

We used the R software package limma (version 3.40.6) to perform differential analysis on patients with uterine carcinosarcoma and obtained a total of 257 differentially expressed genes between the radiotherapy group and the non radiotherapy group (Fig. 3A). We conducted enrichment analysis on these differentially expressed genes using gene ontology and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes, and found that these genes affect cell migration, intracellular metabolism, and DNA replication. Pathways related to tumor cell behavior, such as angiogenesis (Fig. 3B–D). The heatmap displays the top 80 genes with the most significant differences between the group of patients receiving radiotherapy and the group of patients not receiving radiotherapy (Fig. 3E).

Enrichment analysis. (A) Differential gene volcano map. (B–D) GO and KEGG pathway enrichment analysis. (E) Partial differential gene expression heatmap. GO = gene ontology, KEGG = Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes.

3.3. The genetic mutation landscapes in different groups

We used oncoplot to display gene mutations in the groups of uterine carcinosarcoma patients who received and did not receive radiotherapy. A considerable amount of overlapping gene expression was observed in the first 20 genes driving these mutations in both groups, however, CSMD3, LRP1B, FAM135B, and APC were expressed in patients receiving radiotherapy but not in the untreated group (Fig. 4).

Mutant landscape analysis. Gene mutations in the group of patients receiving radiotherapy and the group of patients not receiving radiotherapy in uterine carcinosarcoma.

3.4. Lasso regression analysis of prognostic genes affecting uterine carcinosarcoma

We integrated the survival time, survival status, and differential gene expression data of patients with uterine carcinosarcoma and used Cox univariate regression analysis to identify 10 genes that affect patient prognosis (Fig. 5A). In order to further investigate the risk factors affecting the prognosis of patients with uterine carcinosarcoma, we used Lasso regression analysis and set the λ value to 0.01, ultimately obtaining 7 genes to construct a risk model. The formula for the risk model is: RiskScore = 0.187 * CST1 + 0.294 * ELAVL3 + 0.0535 * SFRP4–4.449 * SSBP2–0.197 * NPFFR2 + 0.097 * FBXL16 + 0.133 * PRND (Fig. 5B, C). In addition, we found that patients with uterine carcinosarcoma who received radiotherapy exhibited low risk factors, while patients who did not receive radiotherapy exhibited high risk factors (Fig. 5D).

Lasso regression analysis. (A) Univariate analysis of genes related to the prognosis of acoustic endometrium. (B–C) Lasso regression analysis identified 7 genes associated with risk factors for uterine carcinosarcoma. (D) The relationship between radiotherapy and risk scoring. *P < .05.

3.5. Survival analysis of patients with high and low risk scores

We found that the higher the expression levels of ELAVL3, PRND, CST1, FBXL16, and SFRP4, the higher the corresponding risk score, while the higher the expression levels of NPFFR2 and SSBP2, the lower the corresponding risk score (Fig. 6A). We also observed similar results in the validation set (GSE119043; Fig. 6B). We found that the overall survival (OS) of high-risk uterine carcinosarcoma patients was significantly lower than that of low-risk patients in the training set (TCGA) and validation set (GSE119043; Fig. 6C, D). Figure 6E, F shows the ROC analysis results for years 1, 3, and 5 in the training set (TCGA) and validation set (GSE119043).

3.8. GSEA analysis of high-risk and low-risk scoring patients with uterine carcinosarcoma

We conducted GSEA analysis on high-risk and low-risk uterine carcinosarcoma patients to obtain significant differences in different pathways and biology (screening conditions were P < .05). We found that compared to low-risk patients with uterine carcinosarcoma, high-risk patients were more enriched in the VEGF pathway and cellular metabolism (fatty acid metabolism, amino acid metabolism, glucose metabolism, folate metabolism; Fig. 8A–J).

GSEA analysis. (A–J) GSEA analysis results of high-risk and low-risk scoring patients with uterine carcinosarcoma. GSEA = gene set enrichment analysis.

3.9. Drug sensitivity analysis of high-risk and low-risk scoring patients with uterine carcinosarcoma

We used the drug prediction algorithm oncopredicct to predict the susceptibility of endometrial cancer patients to drugs. We found that low-risk patients had significantly higher sensitivity to oligomycin.A, BRD-K97651142 and austocystin.D compared to high-risk patients, while low-risk patients had significantly lower sensitivity to SN.38, topotecan, and AZD7762 compared to high-risk patients (Fig. 9A–F).

Drug sensitivity analysis. (A–F) Drug sensitivity analysis results of high-risk and low-risk scoring patients with uterine carcinosarcoma. *P < .05, **P < .01, ***P < .001.

3.10. Screening of prognostic genes

In order to investigate which genes in the gene set (CST1, ELAVL3, SFRP4, SSBP2, NPFFR2, FBXL16, PRND) in the risk factor score can serve as prognostic factors affecting patients with uterine carcinosarcoma, we conducted a multiple regression analysis on these genes. We found that SSBP2, ELAVL3, and CST1 can serve as prognostic genes for patients with uterine carcinosarcoma (Fig. 10A). In the TCGA dataset, patients with high expression of SSBP2 had better prognosis than those with low expression, while patients with high expression of ELAVL3 and CST1 had poorer prognosis than those with low expression (Fig. 10B–D). In addition, the same conclusion was obtained in the GSE119043 dataset (Fig. 10E–G). Finally, we integrated uterine carcinosarcoma patients from the TCGA database and normal patients from the GTEx database to analyze the expression differences of SSBP2, ELAVL3, and CST1 in tumor and normal tissues. We found that SSBP2 is highly expressed in normal tissues, while ELAVL3 and CST1 are highly expressed in tumor tissues (Fig. 10H–J).

4. Discussion

Uterine carcinosarcoma is a rare tumor that accounts for 3% of malignant tumors in the uterine region. In our study, patients with uterine carcinosarcoma who received radiotherapy had significantly longer survival than those who did not receive radiotherapy. Due to the mixed histological characteristics of epithelial and mesenchymal components, uterine carcinosarcoma has a strong invasive ability.[22] At present, radiotherapy has been widely used in the treatment of uterine carcinosarcoma patients. For example, after undergoing surgical treatment, patients with stages I–IV uterine carcinosarcoma who receive combined radiotherapy and chemotherapy again can significantly increase their survival rate, especially in stage III patients.[23] Similarly, adjuvant radiotherapy combined with surgery and chemotherapy can improve overall survival in patients with uterine carcinosarcoma.[24]

In order to further investigate the effect of radiotherapy on uterine carcinosarcoma, we conducted immune infiltration analysis on patients with uterine carcinosarcoma. In our research results, the degree of B cell infiltration in patients with uterine carcinosarcoma who received radiotherapy was significantly higher than that in patients who did not receive radiotherapy. We conducted risk scoring, drug sensitivity analysis, and predicted the 1, 3 and 5-year survival of uterine carcinosarcoma patients using risk score related column charts based on differential genes in radiotherapy. Finally, we found that radiotherapy related genes SSBP2, ELAVL3, and CST1 can serve as independent prognostic genes affecting uterine carcinosarcoma patients. Patients with high expression of SSBP2 have better prognosis than those with low expression, while patients with high expression of ELAVL3 and CST1 have poorer prognosis than those with low expression. Existing research suggests that SSBP2, ELAVL3, and CST1 can regulate tumor progression. For example, the loss of SSBP2 expression in colorectal cancer and metastatic cancer tissues is associated with poor prognosis in colorectal cancer patients.[25] Researchers conducted pharmacological inhibition of ELAVL3 using the FDA approved drug pyrvinium palmate, which can effectively inhibit tumor growth, reduce the risk of metastasis, and improve the survival rate of a neuroendocrine prostate cancer mouse model.[26] CST1 activates the OXPHOS/MEK/ERK axis by enhancing the activity of mitochondrial respiratory chain complexes, thereby promoting the metastasis of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma; CST1 alleviates GPX4 ubiquitination modification by recruiting OTUB1 to enhance GPX4 protein stability and reduce intracellular reactive oxygen species, thereby inhibiting ferroptosis and promoting gastric cancer metastasis.[27,28]

Based on our research, we provide new ideas for predicting the prognosis and clinical treatment of patients with uterine carcinosarcoma. Especially with the launch of FDA approved pyridinium pamoate, using radiotherapy combined with pyridinium pamoate based on our model may become an effective new strategy for treating uterine carcinosarcoma. However, our model only has a sample size of public databases and lacks a larger clinical patient population. In our future research, it is necessary to further collect more patients with uterine carcinosarcoma for model validation, and explore the therapeutic effect of radiotherapy combined with pyridinium pamoate on uterine carcinosarcoma through animal experiments. However, our study still has some limitations. This is an analytical study, therefore we only used information from uterine carcinosarcoma patients in public databases to construct a prognostic model for uterine carcinosarcoma. However, due to the limitations of information on osteosarcoma patients in downloaded public datasets, other potential confounding factors such as patient demographics, cancer stage, and clinical factors, none of them were included in the study.

5. Conclusion

We have developed a model that can accurately predict the prognosis of uterine carcinosarcoma patients based on the effectiveness of radiotherapy for uterine carcinosarcoma. This model provides new ideas for prognosis prediction and clinical treatment of uterine carcinosarcoma patients.

A neural network for traffic flow prediction with parallel processing of expanded convolutional and radial networks
Authors: Wei Ye, Yuqi Zheng, Haotian Bai, Xiaomin Dai
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractConsidering that traffic flow data are affected by multiple aspects of extrinsic and intrinsic factors, traditional time series analysis models may suffer from low accuracy. Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCNs) are widely used in traffic flow prediction due to their unique parallel computational extraction capability and ability to fix the sensory field. However, temporal convolutional networks are unable to cope with the initial filtering nature of the raw data, resulting in many raw data features being ignored and their own weak ability for real-time processing. To solve these problems, this study proposes a new model based on radial temporal convolutional networks (RSCN). We effectively utilize the multi-layer processing mechanism of TCN by adding a radial network and inflated convolution for parallel processing, and finally, extracting the feature data matrix using the residual layer and dimensionality reduction to obtain the output layer. Experimental results on real datasets show that RSCN is more efficient than several existing methods.Keywords: Traffic flow prediction, Temporal neural network, Radial basis functionSubject terms: Applied mathematics, Scientific data

Introduction

At present, the highway is an important channel for the logistics operation of society as a whole. However, by the regional economic and social impact, the traffic flow between different regions has a large difference, which will lead to regional resource allocation and the use of energy and other irrationalities, so for the large differences in the region, the finer capture of the time characteristics, to ensure the accuracy of the short-term traffic flow prediction, can be better for the energy allocation of the main road to provide security1–3.

Traffic flow prediction is influenced by multiple factors, which can be categorized into intrinsic and extrinsic factors. The most common intrinsic factors are the accuracy of the actual measuring instrument and its location. The instrument’s accuracy affects the accuracy of the measured data, and the location of the measuring roadway, such as a classical sensor node set up in a city center versus a suburb, can result in a significant difference in the measured values. Typical extrinsic factors include weather and road conditions, such as the economic development of the spatial area in which the traffic flow is measured. Moreover, the results of traffic flow measurements in cities with faster economic development compared with cities with slower economic development are affected. Traditional recurrent neural networks [RNNs] can capture long-term dependencies in time series well but rely too much on the previous time step for parallel computation, and their low adaptability to the time step makes it possible to face instability problems such as gradient explosion.

So far, sequence modeling in the context of deep learning is mainly associated with recurrent neural network architectures, such as LSTM and GRU, etc., which are long and short-term memory networks that can solve the problems of modeling long-term dependencies and gradient vanishing. However, for sequence data modeling, convolutional networks can achieve better performance than recurrent networks, due to the fact that convolutional networks allow parallel computation of the output. However, Temporal Convolutional Networks [TCNs] were proposed and started to be utilized in the computation of sequence models. This convolutional network, which can parallelize computation and capture local dependencies, can be well applied to sequence models in different contexts, and TCNs are able to capture all two levels of information hierarchically in a unified way, which makes it more stable for gradient propagation. However, we found that the initial convolutional layer of TCN lacks the ability to filter the original data dynamically, which leads to the loss of local features and the difficulty of the linear structure to capture the complex nonlinear spatial and temporal dependencies in the traffic flow, and in addition, the fixed expansion coefficient limits its adaptability to multiscale temporal features, which means that it is difficult for TCN to adapt to the sudden fluctuations in time.

In this paper, we introduce a novel model architecture, called Radial Sequential Convolutional Network [RSCN], which aims to address the above challenges. The core of our approach is to perform a nonlinear mapping for each time step and introduce a dynamic tuning slab to update the dynamic weights in real-time to reflect the evolving temporal relationships. To compensate for the original unilinear network processing of time-series convolutional networks, we invoke radial basis [RBF] for bilinear processing. This approach improves the ability to capture the global structure in the time series and better cope with long-term temporal dependencies. In addition, this method is able to dynamically change the model learning weights to better adapt to different situations. The main contributions of this study are summarized below:

The Radial Self-Organizing Convolutional Network (RSCN) integrates radial networks with inflated convolution, enhancing its ability to capture temporal dependencies in traffic data. This integration is further strengthened by the use of parallel alternating processing, which allows for more precise data information capture.

Related research

Prediction models for conventional traffic flow

Traffic flow prediction is constructed using several aspects, such as data collection and optimization of prediction model building. Traffic information that can be predicted generally includes traffic volume, speed, and density. This study uses traffic sensors that record data at the same data intervals at different times of the day on a fixed roadway as a method of data collection. For the roadway dispersion of routinely used traffic sensors, we consider that traffic flow is generally affected by two dimensions: the historical time and spatial dimensions of the roadway. Moreover, the spatial dependence of the traffic flow is generally strong, so we capture the spatial relationship of the fixation map by constructing the collocation matrix of the spatial information4,5. However, the structure of the fixation map generally results in difficulty in fully reflecting the time-varying characteristic situation of the real space and cannot extract the hidden spatial information well6. Traditional statistical time-series analysis methods based on traditional statistical time-series include basic time-series models, such as autoregressive (AR) models7, moving average (MA) models8, gray forecasting (GM)9,10, and autoregressive moving average (ARIMA) model11. Machine-learning-based prediction methods can also help analyze databases that lack high requirements for accuracy. Traditional machine learning methods in traffic prediction can be divided into three categories: feature extraction-, Gaussian process regression-, and state-space-based methods12. In particular, the feature extraction-based machine learning method13 is often used in traffic flow prediction problems, with the core being the training of a regression method built using statistical knowledge and traffic features. Furthermore, the Gaussian process regression (GPR)14 based on the Gaussian process regression (GPR), as a non-parametric Bayesian method, can naturally predict traffic flow. The parametric Bayesian approach can naturally handle uncertainty and noise. State space models (SSM)15 can describe the dynamic behavior of a system by introducing implicit states. Classical approaches to the traffic flow prediction problem based on traditional machine learning include the K-nearest neighbor (K-NN) method16, support vector machines (SVM)17, and Bayesian networks10. However, feature extraction-based machine learning methods often have difficulty capturing traffic flow’s nonlinear relationship and spatiotemporal dynamic characteristics. GPR has high computational costs when dealing with large-scale datasets. SSM models are limited in their modeling ability when facing complex systems, such as traffic flow, especially in terms of the nonlinear and non-stationary characteristics of traffic flow.

To overcome the preceding limitations, researchers are exploring ways to incorporate deep learning. The core of deep learning lies in neural networks, and researchers have eventually built two commonly used neural networks after continuous experimentation: CNNs and recurrent neural networks (RNNs)18,19. CNNs are primarily used to process image data, whereas RNNs are primarily used to process sequential data. CNNs extract spatial features of a data set by transforming a road network into a regular two- or multi-dimensional matrix, in which spatial features are extracted through convolutional layers20,21. To improve the accuracy of traditional convolutional networks, many researchers have combined CNNs with long short-term memory networks (LSTM)22 to construct new models. The new models are continuously optimized based on Euclid’s spatial structure to construct some brand new models, such as the brand new model construction of short-term traffic flow by the Grizzly method proposed by Chahinez Ounoughi et al.23. RNNs are a type of artificial neural network capable of processing sequential data. Unlike feed-forward neural networks, RNNs can retain information from previous inputs by introducing hidden states24. Many researchers have utilized its unique memory mechanism to complete the prediction of long-term and short-term traffic flow25,26. Some researchers have combined traditional RNN algorithms with the attention mechanism to construct new models of high-precision algorithms, such as the model proposed by Wangzhi yu et al., which has British and trans-branching convolutions with channel self-attention for graph spatiotemporal channel unit model27. Given that almost no roads in the real world conform to the conventional Euclidean structure, researchers have made graph convolution networks (GCNs) our most commonly used model for road network modeling to represent existing large-area road networks with high accuracy28. Extensive data research has also confirmed that in recent years, GCNs can efficiently analyze the spatial information of mixed traffic flows in a certain space29. Many researchers have also started further optimizing GCNs. For example, the general dynamic graphical convolutional network (GDGCN) proposed by Yi Xu et al. can explore the parameter-sharing mechanism in traffic flow prediction30. In particular, RNN is more commonly used in natural language processing but can be used to predict the time series when coping with a large amount of spatial and temporal information. The reason is the difficulty in predicting the time series for hidden traffic flow. Temporal information will be prone to the disappearance of gradient because of the insufficient ability to analyze hidden information. Therefore, researchers have designed LSTM and gate recursive unit (GRU) on the original idea of convolutional learning. Numerous experiments have proven that LSTM and GRU show sufficient performance in extracting temporal features31,33,34,34.

Traffic flow prediction model for analyzing temporal characteristics

The key to traffic flow prediction models lies in the feature extraction of traffic flow data in the temporal and spatial dimensions. The most critical issue in improving the efficiency of traditional traffic flow-based prediction models is how to improve the effective extraction of temporal and spatial features in the models. Several researchers have contributed to this area. Ran Tian et al.35 proposed a transformer architecture with multi-scale temporal features based on the traditional CNN model to simulate long-term temporal patterns, which can synthesize temporal features with high accuracy and show the prediction results with high temporal effect. Moreover, training the original model for such a hybrid complex neural network while delivering high-precision fitting results is difficult. To fully utilize the model, Kaiming He et al.36 proposed a deep residual model based on image detection to analyze the residuals existing in different network layers. Zhang J et al.37 proposed a spatio-temporal residual network model caled ST-ResNet for extracting spatio-temporal data correlation in various complex situations. Li Xiaofeng et al.38 proposed a multidimensional trust data cooperative model based on a fully CNN-based multidimensional trust data synergy. This model realizes the synergistic extraction of multidimensional trust data by using a synergistic filtering method to train the connection weights of the implicit and output layers. Note that GCN performs better in coping with non-Euclidean data because almost all real-world road networks do not satisfy the Euclidean three-dimensional structure39. Several researchers have combined traditional methods with GCN. Wu et al. combined LSTM networks with GCN and optimized it by combining residual networks40. Yang et al. accomplished a markedly accurate fit to the displacement using a diffusion convolutional recurrent neural network (DCRNN)41. Yu et al. proposed a spatio-temporal graphic convolutional network (STGCN) to measure node correlation by constructing a graph based on the distance relationship42. Most of the preceding methods assume that the relationship between the individual data is constant over time. Lu et al.43 proposed an RNN for short-term traffic flow combination prediction, so that the dynamically weighted combination model would have superior prediction results.

In summary, the problems faced by traffic flow prediction models in their application are mainly focused on the following aspects.

Data-driven and model generalization capability: Many traffic flow prediction models rely excessively on historical data and cannot adapt to unexpected events and external factors. Insufficient generalization performance of the models leads to a decrease in prediction accuracy.

Overview of the methodology

TCN network

CNNs treat an image as a two-dimensional hierarchy (a matrix of m*n) when processing an image. Migrating to a time series, the series can be viewed as a one-dimensional object (a vector of 1*n). With a multi-layer network structure, a large enough receptive field can be obtained. This approach makes the CNN very deep, but thanks to the advantage of massively parallel processing, no matter how deep the network is, it can be processed in parallel, saving a lot of time. This is the basic idea of TCN47.

TCN uses causal convolution to ensure the model does not violate temporal order. Causal convolution means that the output depends only on inputs from the current moment and its predecessors, not future inputs. Experiment

Datasets

The Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region (XUAR), located in northwestern China, is the largest provincial-level administrative region in the country. As of 2023, the region boasts a highway network exceeding 7,700 kilometers, ensuring full coverage across all prefectural and municipal cities and towns. Despite this extensive network, the vast geography and sparse distribution of highways necessitate a heavy reliance on these roads for the transportation of goods both within and outside Xinjiang. Furthermore, the region’s complex climatic conditions and limited resources underscore the importance of a rational energy distribution strategy for transportation. Accurate predictions of future highway traffic flow are essential to provide data support for effective traffic management and energy allocation. Consequently, given the intricate nature of Xinjiang’s highway system, there is a pressing need for a traffic flow prediction model that offers both high timeliness and accuracy.

The data used in this experiment is the truck traffic flow in 2022 for a portion of one of the two most important highways in Xinjiang, the G30 Lianhuo Expressway, which is used to verify the timeliness and prediction accuracy of this algorithm.The G30 Lianhuo Expressway was opened to traffic in December 2014, and it is one of the most important east-west traffic arteries in China. This highway connects Lianyungang and Horgos and is designed as a two-lane configuration, with most of the road sections having four lanes in both directions, totaling 4258.19 km in the whole field. The G30 Lianhuo Expressway is highly representative as a major trunk road in Xinjiang, making it an ideal subject for assessing the timeliness and prediction accuracy of the algorithm under investigation. Xinjiang highway access data is the data used in this study. The dataset is provided by 15 node records, totaling 35050 time nodes. We define them as XY-ETS. In order to test the generalizability and stronger ability of our model to cope with unexpected extreme events, we selected the traffic flow data on a highway during the New Year period, defined as GT-SF.further divide them into 70% training set and 30% testing set. The features in the time series can be better extracted by processing the raw real-valued functions along the radial basis symmetric scalar function and then completing the residual screening. In addition, high-accuracy prediction of one-year traffic flow data is accomplished using infinite differentiability, which further improves the performance of the RCN model. The RMSE is reduced by 0.74%, the MAE by 0.24%, and the MAPE by 1.41%. The stability of the coefficient of determination is improved by 0.59%. The above data show that when dealing with a large amount of spatio-temporal traffic flow data, we should take an obviously suitable method to consider the local characteristics of individual short-time data. In addition, this study can also illustrate through the aforementioned data that using RBF to process the original real-valued functions helps to analyze the clustering of their local features better. Overall, the RSCN model shows good performance in coping with the prediction of short-duration traffic flow.

Summary

This study proposes an RSCN architecture for traffic flow prediction based on two traditional prediction models. The architecture of this paper advances the theoretical framework of time-series modeling in the following ways:

nonlinear enhancement mechanism: through the global approximation capability of RBF network, it makes up for the deficiency of TCN in modeling complex nonlinear relationships;

nonlinear enhancement mechanism: through the global approximation capability of RBF network, it makes up for the deficiency of TCN in modeling complex nonlinear relationships;

gradient optimization design: the combination of dynamic weight adjustment and residual connection provides a theoretical guarantee for the stability of gradient propagation from a dynamic system perspective;

gradient optimization design: the combination of dynamic weight adjustment and residual connection provides a theoretical guarantee for the stability of gradient propagation from a dynamic system perspective;

multi-scale feature fusion: the parallel processing of expansion convolution and RBF layer realizes the synergistic modeling of local details and global trend, which is in line with the theory of multi-resolution analysis.

multi-scale feature fusion: the parallel processing of expansion convolution and RBF layer realizes the synergistic modeling of local details and global trend, which is in line with the theory of multi-resolution analysis.

In the future, the authors aim to optimize the degree of structure of the complex model without significantly increasing its structural complexity, considering more disturbing factors that affect the accuracy of the model, and striving to enhance the training process and training time of the model by using weight sharing. Lastly, we attempt to further optimize the two-tier structure to improve its stability in dealing with more complex traffic flow structures.

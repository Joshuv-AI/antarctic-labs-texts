Simulation of personalized english learning path recommendation system based on knowledge graph and deep reinforcement learning
Authors: Liu-Ying Zhou, Yuan-Yuan Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractWith the rapid development of online education, personalized learning path recommendations have played an increasingly important role in enhancing learning efficiency and optimizing learning experiences. However, existing learning path recommendation methods still face significant limitations in knowledge structure modeling, dynamic learner knowledge state perception, and recommendation strategy optimization. To address these challenges, this study proposes an online personalized English learning path recommendation method that integrates a domain knowledge graph with deep reinforcement learning. The graph encodes prerequisite (directed) and semantic (undirected) relations and uses a resource-to-knowledge mapping to structurally bind learning resources to concepts; learner mastery is updated in real time via interaction feedback, graph-based propagation, and an exponential forgetting mechanism. The task is formulated as an MDP in which Q-learning provides value-based pruning of prerequisite-feasible candidates and PPO selects the final action from the pruned set (a prune–then–select workflow). Deployed as a WeChat Mini Program, the system was evaluated on 200 active learners over three months with 18,742 valid interactions. It achieves Precision 0.85, Recall 0.82, F1 0.84, MAE 0.12, RMSE 0.18, cumulative return G 650, and AMG 0.42, consistently outperforming strong baselines AKT, LightGCN, TA-RL, cDQN, KG-H, MC, CF, and Rule; paired per-learner tests with BH–FDR control confirm significance, particularly for Top-K [3,10]. Engineering evaluations show an average 241 ms latency for personalized recommendation at 200 concurrent threads and sub-350 ms / sub-500 ms startup on Wi-Fi / 4G across mainstream devices, demonstrating practical scalability and real-time applicability.Keywords: Personalized English Learning Recommendation, Knowledge Graph, Reinforcement Learning, Personalized Learning PathSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

In recent years, the rapid development of Internet technologies has profoundly transformed educational models1. Online education has broken through the temporal and spatial limitations of traditional teaching, promoting the widespread sharing of educational resources and the advancement of personalized learning2,3. Leveraging digital platforms, learners can flexibly plan their learning trajectories and access diverse, personalized content, significantly enhancing learning efficiency and motivation4,5. Against this backdrop, how to employ intelligent recommendation techniques to dynamically optimize learning paths has become a critical topic in educational technology research.

However, existing learning path recommendation methods exhibit several limitations. On the one hand, many approaches adopt static recommendation strategies, lacking the ability to perceive learners’ real-time knowledge states and dynamically adjust recommendations, which impairs responsiveness to evolving cognitive progress6. On the other hand, conventional recommendation models often overlook the complex structural relationships among knowledge points, resulting in learning paths that lack coherence and hierarchy, thereby reducing learning effectiveness7,8. Furthermore, many methods exhibit limitations in modeling the relationship between learner behavior feedback and knowledge mastery gains, hindering fine-grained path optimization9. These issues are particularly prominent in English language learning, where the knowledge system is complex and rich in prerequisite dependencies and semantic associations, urgently requiring more intelligent and adaptive recommendation mechanisms.

To address these challenges, this study proposes an online personalized English learning path recommendation method that integrates knowledge graphs with deep reinforcement learning. The method constructs an English knowledge graph encompassing prerequisite and semantic relations to precisely model knowledge structures and employs a resource-to-knowledge mapping to represent the structured distribution of learning resources. Building on this, a dynamic learner knowledge state modeling mechanism is designed, combining interaction feedback with graph-based propagation to achieve real-time updates of learners’ knowledge mastery levels. Furthermore, the learning path recommendation task is formulated as a Markov Decision Process, with PPO and Q-Learning algorithms jointly employed to dynamically optimize personalized learning paths.

Related work

In recent years, with the rapid development of smart education and online learning, personalized learning path recommendation has become an active research topic. Existing studies have demonstrated that personalized learning path recommendations can effectively integrate high-quality knowledge resources and optimize resource allocation, thereby improving learning outcomes10. However, many current methods generate complete learning paths in advance, which often fail to balance path coherence with the ability to dynamically adapt to changes in learners’ proficiency levels11. On the one hand, traditional approaches typically recommend static learning sequences without considering the evolving needs of learners during the learning process12,13; on the other hand, the capability to timely adjust learning sequences based on real-time knowledge mastery remains insufficient14. It has also been noted that many current approaches overly focus on generating paths themselves, while neglecting the fundamental educational goal of helping learners identify and strengthen weak knowledge areas, indicating that adaptability remains an unresolved challenge15.

Knowledge graphs (KGs) have been widely used in educational recommendation systems as a powerful tool for structured knowledge representation16. KG-based approaches enable the representation of entities such as courses and knowledge points, along with prerequisites, difficulty, and semantic relationships, thereby allowing recommendation systems to leverage rich semantic information for more accurate learning path generation. For example, by constructing domain ontologies and modeling course knowledge in graph structures, some learning path recommendation methods can plan sequences based on the similarity and prerequisite relationships between knowledge points17,18. However, ontology-based methods rely heavily on manually defined domain concepts, which are difficult to update dynamically during the learning process. Pure embedding-based methods, although capable of capturing high-order relationships in graph structures, often sacrifice semantic interpretability. Moreover, knowledge graph approaches face practical challenges such as incomplete construction, limited cross-domain applicability, data scalability issues, and a lack of comprehensive evaluation19. Additionally, KGs lack inherent optimization mechanisms, making it difficult to adapt promptly to dynamic learner behaviors and complex constraints arising during learning. Therefore, while KG technologies significantly enhance semantic representation in learning path recommendation, their ability to model dynamic learner knowledge states remains insufficient.

Reinforcement learning (RL) has demonstrated unique advantages in personalized recommendation by optimizing long-term rewards through interaction with the environment20,21. In learning path recommendation, several studies have explored the use of RL algorithms for sequential decision-making. For instance, some works have applied RL to consider dependencies among exercises or knowledge point sequences when recommending learning paths. Value-based methods, such as Q-learning22, update state-action value functions to discover optimal paths but require manually defined discrete state spaces and transition rules, and suffer from high computational cost in large state spaces. In contrast, policy optimization methods such as Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) directly optimize parameterized policies through objective functions, improving training stability and sample efficiency23. Recent work has designed PPO-based learning path recommendation algorithms that incorporate learning goals, knowledge mastery, and relationships among knowledge points into state representations, while optimizing recommendation strategies with reward functions that account for path smoothness and learning efficiency24.

Although many studies have explored knowledge structure modeling and strategy optimization individually, the integration of the two remains underdeveloped. For example, pure KG-based methods primarily focus on semantic relationship construction among knowledge points but often neglect dynamic learner-specific features25. Some approaches combining KGs with collaborative filtering have improved personalization but tend to be user-interest-driven, overlooking the educational objective of helping learners address knowledge gaps26,27. These challenges highlight the need for improved methods that better integrate structured knowledge modeling with adaptive strategy optimization in learning path recommendation.

Methodology

This study proposes an online personalized English learning path recommendation method that integrates knowledge graphs with deep reinforcement learning, aiming to achieve precise modeling of individual learner differences and dynamic optimization of learning trajectories. The method utilizes a knowledge graph to represent various knowledge points and their semantic relationships within the English learning domain, thereby capturing the hierarchical structure and dependency relations of the learning content through a graph-based model. This KG-aware modeling has been widely adopted in recommender systems28. Concurrently, a reinforcement learning framework is incorporated to formulate the recommendation task as a dynamic decision-making process based on states, actions, and rewards. This enables the system to iteratively refine its recommendation strategy according to the learner’s current knowledge state and behavioral feedback. The overall system architecture consists of two tightly coupled core modules: the Knowledge Graph and Learner Knowledge State Modeling Module, and the Reinforcement Learning-based Recommendation Strategy Module. The former provides a structured representation of the learner’s knowledge state, while the latter continuously optimizes the learning path based on interactive feedback, thereby facilitating a dynamic and personalized recommendation of learning paths. System design

To enable dynamic modeling of English learners and personalized learning path recommendation, we developed an intelligent recommendation system based on knowledge graph representation and deep reinforcement learning. The system is deployed as a WeChat Mini Program, which ensures lightweight access, high compatibility, and real-time interaction capabilities. The overall architecture, as shown in Fig. 2, follows a three-layer structure: the data layer, the business logic layer, and the application layer. These layers are logically decoupled but functionally integrated to support a closed-loop personalized recommendation process.

System architecture for personalized English learning path recommendation.

At the data layer, the system maintains three types of structured resources: learning content, a domain-specific knowledge graph, and user interaction logs. The learning content includes vocabulary, grammar, reading, and listening materials, which are annotated and mapped to corresponding knowledge points in the graph. The knowledge graph captures both prerequisite and semantic relationships among knowledge points, serving as the structural foundation for path reasoning. User behavior data are recorded throughout the learning process, providing critical input for modeling learner knowledge states and training reinforcement learning strategies.

The business logic layer is responsible for learning state representation, personalized path recommendation, and interaction management. The learner state modeling component encodes each learner’s knowledge state as a vector and updates it dynamically based on interaction outcomes and structural propagation across the graph. The path recommendation module generates adaptive learning sequences using reinforcement learning, incorporating both policy-based (PPO) and value-based (Q-learning) approaches. Each learner-system interaction is recorded as a tuple in the form of and added to an experience buffer for training. The interaction management module ensures consistent coordination of user actions, system responses, and feedback processing, thereby maintaining the integrity of the recommendation loop.

The application layer provides user interaction services through the WeChat Mini Program, offering an intuitive and accessible mobile interface for personalized English learning. Users can perform essential operations such as account registration, diagnostic testing to assess current proficiency levels, dynamic reception of customized learning resources, and real-time tracking of learning progress. The application layer also supports functionalities for managing personal information and browsing a diverse range of learning materials, including multimedia content and interactive exercises. All interactions occur seamlessly within the mobile environment, with data synchronized to the backend system in real-time, ensuring a consistent user experience across sessions. This design lowers the entry barrier for learners, promotes engagement through convenience and usability, and facilitates continuous learning within an adaptive, user-centered learning ecosystem.

Experimental setup

Dataset construction and processing

The dataset utilized in this study was collected from an operational personalized English learning system deployed on the WeChat Mini Program platform. This system was publicly accessible and actively used by real learners, enabling the continuous capture of user-system interaction logs in a naturalistic learning environment. These logs provide the foundation for constructing a dynamic dataset suitable for reinforcement learning-based modeling of personalized learning paths.

At the initial stage of system usage, learners were required to complete a diagnostic entrance test designed to evaluate their mastery of the full set of knowledge points defined in the knowledge graph. Based on the results of this test, the system initialized each learner’s knowledge state vector, with mastery scores normalized to the [0, 1] interval. During subsequent learning sessions, the system continuously updated the learner’s knowledge state according to their interactions with specific learning resources. Each learning interaction was treated as a transition event within a Markov Decision Process, recorded as a structured tuple , where s(t) denotes the learner’s knowledge state at time t, a(t) is the recommended resource, r(t) is the reward computed from learning feedback, and is the updated knowledge state.

Data collection was conducted over a continuous three-month period, during which approximately 200 active users participated. The learning content covered multiple pedagogical modules, including grammar, vocabulary, reading comprehension, and listening practice. A total of 18,742 valid interaction samples were gathered. Each sample encoded a full reinforcement learning transition cycle and included explicit representations of the learner’s cognitive status, resource alignment, and feedback-informed knowledge gain. These properties enable effective modeling of both short-term learning outcomes and long-term path optimization.

Before training, all data underwent a rigorous preprocessing pipeline, including noise filtering, normalization, and outlier removal. Knowledge state vectors were bounded within [0, 1] using sigmoid transformations, and the reward signals were standardized according to the observed improvement in concept mastery. To support model development and performance evaluation, the final dataset was partitioned into training, validation, and testing subsets using an 80%-10%-10% split.

Takeaways.

The combination of prerequisite-aware pruning, PPO on feasible sets, graph-propagated mastery updates, and calibrated forgetting delivers statistically significant and practically meaningful gains across key metrics. Findings remain robust under user-level aggregation, outlier checks, and multiple-comparison control.

From 50 to 200 threads the relative increases are 77% (login), 96% (personalized recommendation), 153% (exercise submission), and 119% (learning progress), which is consistent with their server-side workloads: authentication and candidate generation scale near-linearly, whereas submission logging and progress aggregation incur heavier persistence and aggregation costs. Overall, the system sustains multi-user activity with predictable degradation and sub-second latency for read-most endpoints even at the highest load.

Conclusion

We presented a structure-aware and dynamically adaptive framework for personalized English learning path recommendation that integrates a domain knowledge graph with deep reinforcement learning. The English knowledge graph encodes prerequisite (directed) and semantic (undirected) relations and maps learning resources to knowledge points. Learner mastery is updated online via feedback-driven gains, graph-based propagation, and exponential forgetting. Casting recommendation as an MDP, we adopt a two-stage strategy—value-based pruning with Q-learning followed by policy optimization with PPO—to constrain the feasible action space and improve both stability and decision quality. The system has been deployed on a WeChat Mini Program and continuously logs interactions to support learning-outcome-oriented optimization.

On three months of real-world logs (200 active users, 18,742 valid interactions), our method achieves (Precision , Recall ) and superior calibration (MAE/RMSE ), as well as the highest reinforcement-learning returns (, ) against strong baselines (MC, CF, Rule, AKT, LightGCN, TA-RL, cDQN, KG-H). Improvements are validated with per-learner paired tests, effect sizes (Cohen’s ), 10,000-sample bootstrap CIs, and BH–FDR control. Engineering evaluations show low latency under load—e.g., at 200 concurrent threads, login ms and personalized recommendation ms—and practical multi-device startup times (Wi-Fi ms; 4G ms). A user study reports positive perceptions across five dimensions (means on a 5-point scale).

Limitations

Despite the demonstrated effectiveness, this study has several limitations. First, the domain knowledge graph may be incomplete or noisy, which can bias mastery estimation and restrict feasible actions. Second, the reward design relies on proxy feedback and may underrepresent delayed learning gains. Third, partial observability and cold-start persist for new learners and newly introduced resources. As next steps, we will expand and denoise the knowledge graph, refine reward signals to better capture delayed gains, and incorporate online/continual updates to mitigate partial observability and cold-start.

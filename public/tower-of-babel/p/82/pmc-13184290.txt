Spatiotemporal evolution and drivers of grassland ecosystem service value in Inner Mongolia
Authors: Qi Shi, Wenlan Wang, Xuerui Zhu, Chuan Ye, Jintai Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Grassland ecosystems play an irreplaceable role in maintaining regional ecological balance and ensuring national ecological security. In this study, a coefficient-modified equivalent factor method was applied to estimate the grassland ecosystem service value (ESV) of the Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region from 2000 to 2023. Analyses were conducted from three dimensions—ecosystem service type, geographic region, and ESV grade. By integrating the center-of-gravity model, spatial autocorrelation analysis, and other methods, the spatial distribution characteristics of grassland ESV were examined, and influencing factors were identified using the Geodetector model. The results show that: (1) The area of grassland ecosystems in Inner Mongolia exhibits a fluctuating downward trend, with extensive overall coverage and a higher concentration in central regions, while parts of western Inner Mongolia experience a reduction in grassland area accompanied by a trend of spatial clustering. (2) Grassland ESV shows a fluctuating upward trend, with regulation services accounting for the largest proportion. The regional distribution follows a central > eastern > western pattern, and the distribution of ESV grades across counties varies across different periods. (3) The spatial distribution of grassland ESV generally presents a “northeast–southwest” pattern, with its center of gravity located northeast of the geometric center in Xilinhot City and exhibiting an overall southeastward shift, accompanied by increasing spatial imbalance. (4) Significant spatial agglomeration of grassland ESV is observed, with high–high clusters concentrated in central Xilingol League and low–low clusters distributed in western and southwestern central regions. The spatial extent of both cluster types has decreased, while polarization and centralization trends have emerged in some areas. (5) Natural factors exert a stronger influence on grassland ESV than socioeconomic factors, among which meteorological variables have the greatest impact. The interaction between annual mean temperature and annual precipitation shows the strongest explanatory power. These findings provide valuable references for management authorities in promoting grassland ecosystem protection and sustainable management.

Introduction

Grassland ecosystems are terrestrial ecosystems characterized by herbaceous plants as the primary producers under specific ecological conditions and represent one of the most extensive and ecologically significant ecosystem types on Earth1. However, due to high-intensity human activities and the influence of various natural factors, such as climate change and desertification, the security and stability of grassland ecosystems are facing severe challenges. At present, payments for ecosystem services have been widely implemented worldwide2, and the concept of ESV consequently has attracted increasing attention. ESV essentially represents the monetized value of ecosystem services. Accurately assessing ESV can help clarify the ecological benefits provided by grassland ecosystems and lay a solid foundation for their rational use and sustainable development.

At present, the functional price method and the equivalent factor method are two major approaches for assessing the economic value of non-market ecosystem services, including those provided by grassland ecosystems3. Compared with the functional price method, the equivalent factor method offers notable advantages, including lower data requirements, broader applicability, and a more comprehensive evaluation framework4. In 1997, Costanza et al. first estimated ESV using the equivalent factor method, laying an important foundation for subsequent global ecosystem service valuation studies5. Subsequently, Xie et al.6 developed an equivalent factor table tailored to the conditions of China, which has been widely applied in studies on grassland ESV. However, the equivalent factor table represents national average conditions and therefore has limited applicability for cross-regional comparison and multi-scale analysis. With regard to the study area, existing research has examined the dynamic changes in grassland ESV across multiple spatial scales. At the global scale, Sannigrahi et al.7 estimated changes in global grassland ESV from 1995 to 2015, providing a fundamental reference for transboundary and large-scale grassland ecosystem studies. At the national scale, existing studies have focused on grassland-rich countries such as Germany8, Brazil9, China10, Afghanistan11, and Ethiopia12, systematically exploring the value characteristics and evolutionary patterns of grassland ecosystem services. In addition, some studies have investigated specific watersheds (e.g., the Morocco Basin13, the Yellow River Basin in China14, and the Guder Basin in Ethiopia15), particular grassland regions (e.g., the Qinghai–Tibet Plateau in China16 and the Cameroonian savanna17), and specific grassland types (e.g., savanna ecosystems in Burkina Faso18), thereby revealing differences in ESV dynamics across spatial extents and grassland types. Overall, existing research on grassland ESV has largely focused on large-scale perspectives and has generated substantial findings. In contrast, studies targeting municipal- and county-level administrative units within specific study areas remain relatively scarce. Moreover, research that simultaneously incorporates fine spatial scales and long-term temporal series is still limited. This gap constrains a comprehensive and dynamic understanding of regional grassland ESV and hinders the development of related theories as well as their practical application.

Inner Mongolia is an important region in northern China and is characterized by vast areas of natural grasslands19. However, most grasslands have been disturbed by both natural and anthropogenic factors, leading to continuous ecological degradation and posing severe challenges to grassland ecosystems20. Therefore, conducting scientific assessments of grassland ESV in Inner Mongolia and clarifying regional differences and spatial distribution patterns are crucial for regional ecological conservation. At present, studies assessing grassland ESV in Inner Mongolia have primarily focused on specific watersheds21, individual cities22, or particular grassland types23. There is a lack of attention to the long-term dynamic changes in grassland ESV at the county level and other small- to medium-scale spatial units across Inner Mongolia as a whole, making it difficult to fully reveal spatiotemporal evolution patterns. Meanwhile, assessment methods remain largely based on the traditional equivalent factor method24. Although some scholars have attempted to modify this method using relevant indicators, the scales of these modifications are not well aligned with the administrative hierarchy of the study area, resulting in limited effectiveness in addressing county-level and other small- to medium-scale research needs25.

In this context, this study takes the grassland ESV of Inner Mongolia as the research object, with counties selected as the basic analytical units and the period from 2000 to 2023 as the temporal scope. Building upon the traditional equivalent factor method, coefficient corrections are conducted at both the provincial and county levels to estimate grassland ESV for each county in Inner Mongolia during the study period. Based on the estimation results, grassland ESV in Inner Mongolia is analyzed from three dimensions: ecosystem service type, geographic region, and ESV grade. Meanwhile, the gravity center model and spatial autocorrelation analysis are employed to further explore the spatiotemporal evolution characteristics of grassland ESV. In addition, the Geodetector model is applied to identify the key factors driving changes in grassland ESV in Inner Mongolia (Fig. 1). The potential contributions of this study are mainly reflected in two aspects. First, by implementing province-county dual-level coefficient corrections to the equivalent factors, this study compensates for the limitations of traditional assessment methods in accounting for regional differences in socioeconomic development and geographic spatial heterogeneity, thereby providing methodological support for grassland ESV assessment at small- to medium-scale regional levels. Second, by conducting long-term time-series analysis using counties as the basic analytical units, this study reduces the coarseness inherent in traditional large- and medium-scale studies to some extent and achieves effective alignment between research scale and administrative management hierarchies. The resulting estimates and analytical findings can provide decision-making support for relevant management authorities in promoting grassland ecosystem conservation and sustainable management.Fig. 1Technology Roadmap.

Materials and methods

Study area

Inner Mongolia, located in northern China, is a strategically significant frontier region with an expansive territory, covering a total area of approximately 1.183 million square kilometers. It administers 12 leagues and prefecture-level cities-specifically, four in eastern Inner Mongolia (Chifeng, Tongliao, Hulunbuir, and Hinggan League), five in central Inner Mongolia (Hohhot, Baotou, Ordos, Ulanqab, and Xilingol League), and three in western (Wuhai, Bayannur, and Alxa League), comprising a total of 103 county-level administrative units. Geographically, Inner Mongolia features a southwest-high and northeast-low terrain pattern (Fig. 2). Its diverse topography and landforms include grasslands, forests, deserts, and other land-use types, supporting a complex and unique ecosystem26. Inner Mongolia is endowed with abundant grassland resources, with natural grasslands accounting for approximately 20.06% of China’s total grassland area, ranking second nationwide, after the Tibet Autonomous Region27. However, under the combined influence of natural and anthropogenic factors, grassland ecosystem degradation has been further exacerbated, weakening regional ecological stability28. Overall, Inner Mongolia is rich in grassland resources and characterized by significant ESV, underscoring the importance of ecological protection in the region.Fig. 2Study Area Location Map. Methods

Quantitative assessment of grassland ESV

Grassland ecosystems are complex systems shaped by the interactions of multiple factors, and their ESV exhibits pronounced scale effects and regional heterogeneity30. Therefore, in estimating grassland ESV, it is necessary to closely integrate the specific conditions of the study area and apply appropriate adjustments. Based on the equivalent factor method proposed by Xie et al.31, this study first applies provincial-level coefficient corrections using cropland productivity as the benchmark, thereby adjusting the national equivalent factors of ESV to those applicable to Inner Mongolia. Subsequently, the provincial-level equivalent factors are further corrected to the county level using vegetation coverage. Finally, grassland ESV in Inner Mongolia is calculated by integrating regional grassland ecosystem area, unit equivalent factors, and their corresponding economic values.

In the national equivalent factor table of ESV per unit area, the ecosystem service equivalent of cropland is explicitly designated as an important reference for calibrating the equivalent factors of other land-use types, such as grasslands. Moreover, equivalent factor correction methods based on cropland productivity have been widely applied in ESV assessment studies across different land-use types, and their scientific validity and applicability have been verified by numerous studies32,33. Considering regional conditions, although grassland ecosystems constitute the dominant ecosystem type in Inner Mongolia, agricultural production in the region is also large-scale and representative, as Inner Mongolia is one of China’s 13 major grain-producing regions. The regional characteristics of agricultural production and grassland ecosystems are highly consistent, indicating that productivity-based cropland correction is relatively suitable for this region. Accordingly, the ratio of cropland productivity in Inner Mongolia to that at the national level is adopted as the provincial-level revision coefficient, by which the equivalent factors at the national scale are adjusted to derive ESV equivalent factors that are better aligned with the regional characteristics of Inner Mongolia. Results and analysis

Spatiotemporal evolution characteristics of grassland ecosystem area in Inner Mongolia

To better illustrate the spatiotemporal evolution of grassland ecosystem area, land-use raster data were used to extract information on the area and proportion of grassland ecosystems in Inner Mongolia from 2000 to 2023, and grassland distribution maps for different periods were generated (Fig. 3 and 4). During the study period, the grassland ecosystem area in Inner Mongolia fluctuated, increasing in the early years, peaking in 2008, and subsequently declining. From an overall perspective, grassland resources in Inner Mongolia have consistently remained at a relatively high level. However, the trend over consecutive years suggests a potential risk of grassland degradation, although data from the most recent two years indicate a relatively stable condition. On the one hand, the observed degradation trend of regional grassland resources may be driven by urbanization and adjustments in land-use structure. Statistical data show that by the end of 2023, the permanent urbanization rate in Inner Mongolia had risen to 69.58%, compared with 42.2% in 2000. The substantial increase in urbanization level may have led to large areas of grassland being converted to other land-use types, such as construction land and agricultural land, thereby reducing grassland resources. On the other hand, the Chinese government has recognized the damage caused by urbanization and related factors to grassland resources and has implemented the “Returning Grazing Land to Grass” program since 2003. By 2020, cumulative investment in this program had reached 32 billion CNY, resulting in an additional 830 million tons of fresh grass production and expanded ecological compensation for herders, aimed at maintaining the region’s role as a key ecological barrier in northern China39. This program may have exerted a positive influence on grassland area restoration, as indirectly reflected in the bar chart. Spatial–temporal evolution characteristics of grassland ESV in Inner Mongolia

Analysis based on ecosystem service type differences

As shown in Fig. 5, the grassland ESV in Inner Mongolia showed minor fluctuations but an overall upward trend, peaking in 2018. Among the subdivided services of the grassland ecosystem, the value of regulating services is the highest. It constitutes the main component of the grassland ESV and also drives its changing trend. Supporting services accounted for the second largest share, whereas provisioning and cultural services remained relatively stable at lower proportions. As for the regulating service value, it is primarily reflected in four major functions: windbreak and sand fixation, water conservation, climate regulation, and soil and water conservation. Firstly, the grassland vegetation, through its root system network, binds the surface soil, reduces wind speed, and diminishes wind erosion, thus suppressing the formation and spread of dust storms. The windbreak and sand fixation value is also the most important component of the grassland ESV in Inner Mongolia40. With Xilingol League as the core, Inner Mongolia has vast grassland resources, the richest biodiversity, and is the main support for the region’s livestock industry, as well as a major contributor to wind and sand fixation. Next, regarding climate regulation, grassland vegetation absorbs carbon dioxide through photosynthesis, releases oxygen, and regulates surface temperature and humidity, helping mitigate the urban heat island effect and extreme weather events. Moreover, concerning water conservation, the vegetation layer and soil organic matter can intercept precipitation, enhance infiltration, regulate runoff timing, reduce floods, and recharge groundwater. Last, as for soil and water conservation, grassland vegetation effectively reduces rain splash erosion, while root systems enhance soil resistance to erosion, reduce soil and water loss risks. Overall, the regulatory service value of Inner Mongolia’s grassland ecosystem has strong ecological service functions and serves as a key support for the region’s grassland ESV.Fig. 5Evolution Trend of ESV of Grasslands in Inner Mongolia Based on Ecosystem Service Type Differences.

Evolution Trend of ESV of Grasslands in Inner Mongolia Based on Ecosystem Service Type Differences.

It is worth noting that there may be a “trade-off” relationship between the provisioning services and regulating services of an ecosystem41. In grassland ecosystems, when provisioning services are enhanced, they may potentially constrain regulating services. For example, increasing grazing intensity or converting grasslands for fodder crop cultivation to boost livestock production could lead to a decline in vegetation cover and soil organic matter loss, thereby weakening the carbon sequestration capacity of vegetation and soil infiltration rates, which in turn reduces carbon storage and hydrological regulation functions. Conversely, if ecological protection measures (such as grazing bans, rest grazing, and grassland ecological restoration projects) are implemented with the aim of enhancing regulating services, it may require limiting high-intensity development and utilization of grasslands. This could impose certain constraints on the scale of regional livestock production and the economic output of provisioning services in the short term. This trade-off is essentially an ecological representation of the core conflict between “ecological protection” and “economic development” in grasslands and is a critical issue that needs to be prioritized and coordinated in management processes. Therefore, Inner Mongolia should establish a core strategy for balancing these trade-offs in grassland ecosystem management. This could be achieved through measures such as optimizing grassland spatial planning, developing eco-friendly livestock farming, and improving grassland ecological compensation systems, all of which would help alleviate the inherent conflict between provisioning and regulating services and promote the overall development of regional grassland utilization toward a more multifunctional and collaborative direction.

Analysis based on geographic regional differences

As shown in Fig. 6, a regional-scale comparison indicates that grassland ESV in Inner Mongolia during the study period followed the pattern: Central > Eastern > Western. Among these, the western region consistently accounted for a relatively low proportion with only minor fluctuations. This pattern can be attributed to two main factors: first, the region receives low annual precipitation, with some desert areas experiencing even lower levels, far below the critical threshold required for grassland growth; second, the potential evaporation is high, reaching 2000–3000 mm, which may result in a long-term soil moisture deficit and consequently keep grassland resources at a persistently low level42.Fig. 6Evolution Trend of Grassland ESV in Inner Mongolia Based on Geographic Regional Differences.

Evolution Trend of Grassland ESV in Inner Mongolia Based on Geographic Regional Differences.

From the perspective of regional disparities in grassland ESV, the gap between the central and eastern regions generally exhibited an increasing trend, with its peak occurring during 2012–2013. Specifically, in 2012 and 2013, the difference in grassland ESV between the central and eastern regions reached its maximum. This phenomenon may be associated with two contributing factors. On the one hand, around 2012, the eastern region of Inner Mongolia experienced spring droughts and prolonged extreme high temperatures, whereas the central region received normal precipitation in the same year43; such extreme climatic conditions may have negatively affected grassland ESV in the eastern region. On the other hand, since the end of 2011, Inner Mongolia has successively implemented three rounds of grassland ecological protection subsidy and reward policies, which effectively promoted grassland restoration. However, due to the higher population density and a larger proportion of household-based grassland contracts in the eastern region, policy implementation may have lagged behind that in the central region, potentially further exacerbating the disparity in grassland ESV between the two regions.

Analysis based on grassland ESV grade differences

Using the natural breaks (Jenks) method, the grassland ESV of each district and county was classified into five levels according to value magnitude, and a spatial distribution map was created (Fig. 7). The spatial distribution pattern of grassland ESV levels varies across different stages, and the grade distribution of districts and counties exhibits clear differences over time. Specifically, from 2000 to 2023, areas reaching the highest (Level 5) grassland ESV in Inner Mongolia remained largely stable and were mainly concentrated in Xilingol League. In contrast, Level 4 areas showed a decreasing trend, primarily distributed in Xilingol and Hulunbuir Leagues. Both regions experienced reductions in Level 4 areas, with the decrease in Xilingol League being more pronounced than that in Hulunbuir League. This pattern may be attributed to two major factors: differences in grassland resource endowments and the intensity of human activities. First, in terms of grassland resource endowments, Hulunbuir is dominated by meadow grasslands, which exhibit stronger ecological resilience than the typical grasslands prevalent in Xilingol. Second, regarding human activity intensity, Xilingol is geographically closer to the Beijing–Tianjin–Hebei economic zone and is therefore more economically developed, with higher development intensity and greater ecological pressure than Hulunbuir. As a result, Hulunbuir demonstrates a relatively stronger capacity to resist grassland degradation compared to Xilingol. From the overall grade distribution, areas with grassland ESV at Level 3 and above cover a significantly larger area than those below Level 3. This pattern may be associated with three major long-term initiatives implemented in Inner Mongolia. First, 731 million mu of basic grassland have been designated across 91 counties (cities and districts) for strict protection. Second, large-scale grassland protection and restoration projects have been carried out, with an annual average of more than 28 million mu of grasslands improved, contributing to continuous ecological recovery. Third, the grass industry has been vigorously promoted. Driving mechanisms of spatial–temporal distribution of grassland ESV in Inner Mongolia

To explore the key factors influencing the spatial distribution of grassland ESV, this study took the grassland ESV of the corresponding years as the dependent variable. Nine factors, including slope and nighttime lights, were considered as potential influencing factors. Geographic detector simulation was then applied for factor detection. Based on data availability and scientific relevance, the years 2005, 2010, 2015, and 2020 are chosen as the time points for the study. To avoid potential interference from multicollinearity issues, a multicollinearity test is performed on all influencing factors before conducting the analysis of driving factors. The test results show that the variance inflation factor (VIF) of the selected variables is less than 10, and the tolerance (TOL) is greater than 0.1, indicating no severe multicollinearity. This suggests that the selected variables are suitable for the analysis of driving factors for grassland ESV in Inner Mongolia.

The results show that at the four selected time points, the eight factors included in the analysis all have p-values of 0, indicating statistical significance. As seen in Fig. 10, natural factors (such as topographic, vegetation, and meteorological factors) have a significantly stronger impact on the grassland ESV than socio-economic factors. This pattern may indicate that the grassland ecosystem is still predominantly driven by natural capital, and is essentially a product of natural processes, while the influence of human activities appears to be relatively limited under current conditions. Under low-intensity human activities, grassland ecosystems may remain compatible with human use; however, high-intensity human disturbances may disrupt ecosystem balance, potentially leading to grassland resource degradation and a sharp decline in ESV. Among the natural factors, meteorological factors exert the most significant influence on grassland ESV. Specifically, annual average temperature and annual precipitation show relatively strong explanatory power.Fig. 10Detection Results of Driving Factors and Interaction Effects on Grassland ESV in Inner Mongolia.

Among the various interaction combinations, the interaction between annual average temperature and annual precipitation exhibits a relatively stronger influence.

Discussion

Comparison with existing studies

The grassland ecosystem of Inner Mongolia, with its vast geographical extent, unique ecological structure, and rich biodiversity, provides services of irreplaceable and significant value both for China and globally. Building upon previous studies, this research revises traditional equivalent factors at both the provincial and county levels to assess grassland ESV in Inner Mongolia, and further applies a series of analytical approaches, including the center-of-gravity model, to explore its spatiotemporal characteristics. Compared with earlier studies, the temporal trend of grassland ESV estimated in this study is consistent with the findings of Wang Ning et al.47, showing an overall increasing pattern. However, the estimated values are lower than those reported by Cheng et al.48, which is mainly attributable to differences in baseline data and accounting methods for ecosystem services. Specifically, Cheng et al.48 employed MODIS land-cover products and adjusted ESV to the pixel scale using NPP, precipitation, and soil data, whereas this study adopts CLCD data and applies corrections at the county scale based on cropland productivity and vegetation coverage. Differences in data sources and adjustment logic may therefore lead to discrepancies in the results.

In the spatiotemporal evolution analysis, this study observes the emergence of distinct “growth poles” or “high-value areas” in parts of Inner Mongolia. However, these growth points have not effectively driven improvements in grassland ESV in surrounding regions. This finding differs from the conclusion of Rukeya et al.49, who suggested that high-value grassland ecosystem service areas would promote ESV enhancement in neighboring regions. In practice, this “driving” effect is constrained by multiple factors, including regional natural background vulnerability and selective human disturbances, and may also be hindered by imperfections in regional ecological compensation mechanisms. Specifically, on the one hand, grassland ecological compensation provided by some beneficiary regions may be insufficient to fully cover the conservation costs incurred by protected areas, as well as the development opportunities they have sacrificed. On the other hand, surrounding regions that bear additional ecological pressure due to the presence of nearby “growth highlands” have yet to establish effective compensation mechanisms to offset the negative externalities they undertake, and these regions also lack targeted policy support and preferential resource allocation. In the long term, if cross-regional linkage mechanisms for enhancing grassland ESV remain absent, the positive effects of high-value areas may struggle to diffuse effectively, thereby making it difficult to promote coordinated improvement in surrounding low-value regions.

Policy relevance and practical implications

Inner Mongolia is an important ecological barrier and a major pastoral production base in northern China. Over the long term, grassland degradation has intensified due to multiple pressures, including overgrazing, climate warming, and changes in land-use patterns. In recent years, the implementation of management measures such as grazing bans, rotational grazing, and grass–livestock balance policies has effectively reduced human disturbance, promoted the recovery of vegetation communities, and facilitated the recuperation of grassland ecosystems50. These measures constitute important drivers of the steady increase in grassland ESV in Inner Mongolia. Nevertheless, existing studies indicate that long-term fencing and complete grazing exclusion are not necessarily the optimal pathways for grassland ecological restoration. Although such approaches can rapidly curb degradation and alleviate ecological stress in the short term, they may also lead to simplified community structures and reduced biodiversity, which are unfavorable for the long-term enhancement of ESV51. Therefore, grassland ecological restoration and ESV improvement should follow the principle of moderation, establishing flexible, site-specific grazing management systems adapted to local conditions.

At present, Inner Mongolia has designated 731 million mu of basic grassland and implemented refined “one-map” management of grassland resources. In combination with the findings of this study and practical needs of grassland ecological restoration, future efforts should be optimized in two key directions to promote synergy between ecological protection and economic development. First, differentiated regional governance strategies should be implemented. Policy measures should be tailored to local conditions by considering the degree of grassland degradation, natural geographic characteristics, and the development foundations of the livestock industry across Inner Mongolia. In the eastern region, existing ecological advantages should be consolidated, while promoting the transformation of animal husbandry toward higher efficiency, standardization, and greener production. In the central region, efforts should be intensified to promote the application of smart pastoral systems, improve the intensive utilization of grassland resources, and advance ecological protection in coordination with production development. In the western region, priority should be given to ecological protection, with strict control over mineral resource exploitation and industrial expansion. Based on local ecological restoration achievements, characteristic eco-industries should be cultivated to diversify income sources for herders. Second, cross-regional ecological compensation mechanisms should be improved. Ecological compensation should be leveraged to alleviate the tension between ecological protection and economic development by establishing cross-regional and diversified compensation schemes. Based on grassland ESV assessment results, high- and low-value service areas should be delineated, and the relationships between beneficiary regions and externally burdened regions clarified, thereby promoting reasonable compensation from beneficiary areas to pressure-bearing areas. Meanwhile, various fiscal funds should be coordinated, and social capital encouraged to participate through the establishment of a grassland ecological linkage development fund, providing stable financial support for balanced spatial development of grassland ESV.

Uncertainty analysis

The reliability of grassland ESV estimates based on the equivalent factor method is primarily affected by uncertainties in two core parameters: land-use area and equivalent factors. Uncertainty in land-use area estimation primarily arises from inherent differences among remote sensing interpretation datasets. Although land-use data derived from remote sensing are generally standardized and quality-controlled by professional institutions, objective discrepancies persist among different products in terms of classification systems, spatial resolution, and temporal coverage. In particular, heterogeneity in land-use classification schemes—such as differences in categories and criteria-can directly lead to inconsistencies in grassland area estimates for the same study area, thereby affecting the accuracy and comparability of grassland ESV results.

Equivalent factors constitute another major source of uncertainty in grassland ESV estimation. Current studies typically rely on the equivalent factor tables proposed by Costanza et al.5 and Xie Gaodi et al.6, with regional modifications applied according to study objectives. However, differences in accounting bases and spatial adaptation scales across studies have resulted in a lack of unified standards for equivalent factor values, introducing potential uncertainty into ESV estimates. Based on existing research and regional characteristics, this study adheres to principles of scientific rigor and applicability by modifying baseline equivalent factors using cropland productivity at the provincial scale and vegetation coverage at the county scale. It should be noted that NDVI, which is widely used to characterize vegetation coverage, was adopted in this study, and the ratio of county-level to provincial-level vegetation coverage was used as the county-level correction coefficient. However, due to generally high vegetation coverage in the study area, NDVI may suffer from saturation effects, limiting its ability to capture detailed variations in vegetation conditions. This limitation may increase uncertainty in county-level correction coefficients and represents an inherent constraint in the county-scale equivalent factor adjustment applied in this study.

Limitations and future prospects

Although this study systematically analyzes the spatiotemporal characteristics and driving mechanisms of grassland ESV in Inner Mongolia, several limitations remain when considering the data and analytical processes employed. First, while CLCD land-use data meet the requirements for long-term time series and high spatial resolution, they do not distinguish among grassland sub-types, such as meadow steppe, typical steppe, and desert steppe. As a result, differentiated correction coefficients for different grassland sub-categories could not be applied, leaving room for improvement in the precision of grassland ESV estimation. Future research will focus on acquiring higher-resolution land-use datasets that allow grassland sub-type differentiation, extending ESV assessment to township or village scales, and capturing finer-scale spatiotemporal dynamics. Second, this study adopts cropland productivity differences for provincial-level coefficient adjustment, an approach that has been validated by some scholars and demonstrates a degree of applicability. However, spatial mismatches between cropland productivity and grassland ecosystem service supply capacity in Inner Mongolia may introduce minor biases into the adjustment results. In addition, NDVI saturation effects at the county scale may further reduce the accuracy of equivalent factor correction. Future studies will explore more targeted and saturation-resistant alternative indicators or construct dynamic adjustment factor systems based on intrinsic linkages between grassland resource endowments and ecosystem services, thereby improving the scientific robustness of regional equivalent factor modifications. Finally, while this study preliminarily identifies the relative influence of natural and socio-economic factors on grassland ESV, quantitative analysis of the independent effects of climate variability and policy measures—such as grazing bans and rotational grazing-as well as their interaction effects, remains insufficient. Future work will integrate socio-economic and remote sensing datasets and apply econometric models to disentangle the independent impacts of climate fluctuations and policy implementation on grassland ESV, while further exploring their interaction mechanisms.

The influence of natural factors, such as topography, vegetation, and meteorological factors, on grassland ESV is significantly stronger than that of socioeconomic factors, suggesting that the grassland ecosystem is primarily driven by natural capital, with low-intensity human activities being compatible with it. Among these natural factors, meteorological factors exert the greatest influence on the grassland ESV. Specifically, the annual average temperature and annual precipitation show a strong explanatory power. Furthermore, the combined effect of multiple factors is also crucial, with the most influential two-factor interactions being between annual average temperature and annual precipitation.

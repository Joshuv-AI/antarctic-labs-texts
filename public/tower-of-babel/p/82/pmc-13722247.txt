Vaginal natural orifice transluminal endoscopic surgery versus laparoscopy for salpingo-oophorectomy and adnexectomy: A systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Suhaib Khayat, Mohammad Khojah
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTVaginal natural orifice transluminal endoscopic surgery (vNOTES) and laparoscopic surgeries have widely been utilized in gynecologic surgeries including adnexectomy and hysterectomy. The aim of conducting this comprehensive meta-analysis is to evaluate the peri- and post-operative safety and clinical efficacy of vNOTES versus laparoscopic surgeries for the indications of unilateral or bilateral salpingectomy, unilateral or bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy, oophorectomy, and adnexectomy with or without hysterectomies for gynecological pathologies. Five electronic databases (PubMed, ScienceDirect, Google Scholar, ERIC, and Cochrane CENTRAL) were searched for studies published from their inception till 28th March 2024, following the PRISMA guidelines. Mendeley Desktop 1.19.8 was used to evaluate and remove the duplicates. Inclusion criteria comprised of: (a) female patients; (b) vNOTES for salpingectomy/salpingo-oophorectomy/adnexectomy; (c) control group of laparoscopy; (d) outcomes like mean operation time, post-operative 24-h visual analogue scale (VAS) score, intra- and post-operative complications, estimated blood loss, length of stay (LOS) in hospital, hemoglobin level changes, conversion, and total post-operative analgesic usage; (d) randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and observational studies. The Newcastle-Ottawa Scale and Cochrane risk of bias tool 2.0 (ROB 2.0) were used to assess the quality of cohorts and RCTs respectively. Review Manager (Version 5.3) was utilized to perform statistical analysis. Mantel-Haenszel statistical tests were applied for dichotomous data keeping risk ratio (RR) as effect measure while inverse variance statistical method was selected and weighted mean difference (WMD) was used as the effect measure for continuous data. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Forest plots were constructed to evaluate the pooled results. Confidence interval (CI) value was chosen at 95%. Higgins I2 statistics were applied to assess the heterogeneity among the studies. The included eight studies comprised of a total of 1203 patients (vNOTES: 542 patients; laparoscopy: 630 patients. Mean change in hemoglobin before and after the procedure was slightly higher in vNOTES (WMD: 0.10; 95% CI = 0.02, 0.17; P = 0.01). LOS was relatively shorter in vNOTES patients (WMD: −0.75; 95% CI = −1.09, −0.41; P < 0.0001). Mean operation time was 22 min shorter in vNOTES (WMD: −22.08; 95% CI = −33.64, −10.52; P = 0.0002). VAS pain scores were lesser in intense in vNOTES (WMD: −1.57; 95% CI = −2.92, −0.22; P = 0.02). No significant differences were observed between the two groups in terms of total post-operative analgesia (WMD: −3.57; 95% CI = −7.15, 0.00; P = 0.05), estimated blood loss (WMD: −18.00; 95% CI = −44.70, 8.70; P = 0.19), intra-operative complications (RR: 1.13; 95% CI = 0.17, 7.47; P = 0.90), post-operative complications (RR: 0.69; 95% CI = 0.32, 1.51; P = 0.35), and conversion to alternate surgeries (RR: 2.56; 95% CI = 0.41, 15.87; P = 0.31). In conclusion, vNOTES proved to be relatively superior than laparoscopic surgery in terms of efficacy. However, the two groups did not differ in terms of safety. Considering this, we declare vNOTES to be a superior alternative to currently available minimally invasive techniques.

Introduction

Over the past few decades, patients presenting with gynecological pathologies have developed an increased focus on the quality of life (QoL) and efficient recovery after surgical procedures rather than just the success rate of surgeries, thereby minimally invasive gynecologic procedures have begun to gain inclined recognition and acceptance.[1] The prime objective of minimally invasive procedures is to reduce patient morbidity and mortality. Evolutionary changes took place from conventional open surgeries such as laparotomy to laparoscopic surgeries and further to single-site laparoscopic surgeries (conventional laparoscopies or laparoendoscopic single-site surgeries) and natural orifice transluminal endoscopic surgery (NOTES) either with or without the assistance of artificial intelligence (AI).[2] Laparoscopic surgeries have an edge over open surgeries in terms of lesser post-operative infections, fewer fever occurrences, faster recovery, and superior cosmetic effects.[3] However, another appealing advancement in the field is vaginal natural orifice transluminal endoscopic surgery (vNOTES) as an emerging technique that allows minimal invasion of abdomen and pelvic area via colpotomy without large incisions in the abdominal wall in contrast to the conventional laparoscopic surgery.[4]

vNOTES and laparoscopic surgeries have widely been utilized in gynecologic surgeries including adnexectomy and hysterectomy.[56] vNOTES also allows better access to the ovaries, fallopian tubes, and other related appendages in contrast to conventional vaginal procedures. Furthermore, it enables the surgeon to better visualize the peritoneal cavity.[7] Surgeries like bilateral salpingectomies (BS) and prophylactic BS are rapidly gaining popularity for their surprising ovarian cancer risk reduction rates and other benign conditions. BS can also be performed via laparoscopic or transvaginal routes.[8] Additionally, prophylactic bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy (BSO) via vNOTES has also resulted in reduced risk of developing ovarian and fallopian tube malignancies via breast cancer 1/2 (BRCA1/2) mutations.[9] According to Lee et al., vNOTEs has increased benefits over laparoscopy in terms of no scar formation hence better cosmetic outcomes,[10] no incisional hernias and wound infections in abdominal wall. Additionally, vNOTES allows retrieval of larger samples of specimen via vagina without laparoscopic port incisions.[11] In a meta-analysis conducted by Housmans et al., vNOTES was associated with shorter operation time, shorter length of stay (LOS) at hospital after surgery, and lesser peri-operative estimated blood loss than conventional laparoscopy.[12] Further vital peri-operative and post-operative outcomes assessed during vNOTES versus laparoscopy for BS and/or BSO and/or adnexectomy include mean operation time, visual analogue scale (VAS) pain score, changes in hemoglobin level, total post-operative analgesic usage and conversion to other surgeries.[471314]

The existing literature is limited in comparing vNOTES and laparoscopy for adnexal procedures. During the extensive literature search, we came across a few narrative reviews, systematic reviews, and meta-analyses on related topics in the last five years with fewer observational studies and randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and fewer sample sizes,[151617] but none of them directly compared the safety and efficacy of vNOTES versus laparoscopic procedures for adnexectomy. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first systematic review and meta-analysis of its kind with the latest literature search including all the RCTs and observational studies with sufficient sample size performed from inception till March 2024. The aim of conducting this comprehensive meta-analysis is to evaluate the peri- and post-operative safety and clinical efficacy of vNOTES versus laparoscopic surgeries for adnexal procedures for gynecological pathologies.

Search strategy and data sources

Five electronic databases (PubMed, ScienceDirect, Google Scholar, ERIC, and Cochrane CENTRAL) were searched for studies published from their inception till 28th March 2024. Our search string consisted of the following Medical Subject Headings (MESH terms): “salpingo-oophorectomy”, “salpingectomy”, “adnexectomy”, “vNOTES”, “vaginal natural orifice transluminal endoscopic surgery”, and “laparoscope”. The complete search strategy for each database is provided in the Supplementary Table 2. Gray and white literature search were also performed so that no study goes unnoticed. There were no time or language restrictions in our paper.

Eligibility and selection of studies

All the included articles were transferred to the Mendeley Desktop 1.19.8 (Mendeley Ltd., Amsterdam, Netherlands) to evaluate and remove the duplicates. Further screening of the articles was performed by two independent reviewers according to the PICO criteria. Initially, the articles were selected based on their abstracts followed by their full-read texts. The following eligibility criterion was used: (a) Female patients; (b) vNOTES as interventional procedure for salpingectomy or salpingo-oophorectomy or adnexectomy for gynecological pathologies; (c) control group of laparoscopic procedures (conventional/single-port access/transumbilical laparoendoscopic single-site surgery); (d) outcomes such as mean operation time, post-operative 24-h VAS score, intra-operative complications, post-operative complications, estimated peri-operative blood loss, change in hemoglobin levels before and after operation, conversion to alternate procedures, and total post-operative analgesic usage; and, (e) randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and observational studies. All the letters, correspondences, abstracts, case reports, meta-analyses, and reviews were excluded along with the papers with no full texts available. Moreover, studies that did not strictly include the removal of ovaries or fallopian tubes or adnexa were also eliminated from our paper. We excluded all the studies comparing vNOTES and laparoscopic methods indicated for only hysterectomies.

Data extraction and quality assessment

Studies and patients’ baseline characteristics data were extracted and recorded into a Microsoft Excel sheet. Study characteristics included country, study design, total sample size, interventional and control group sample sizes, outcomes assessed, and type of surgery performed. Patients’ baseline data included mean age, mean body mass index (BMI), history of previous C-sections, and previous abdominal surgery. Two reviewers assessed the quality of the included studies individually to avoid any human error. A third reviewer was assigned to resolve any inconsistencies in the quality assessment. The Newcastle-Ottawa Scale was used to assess the quality of the included prospective and retrospective cohort studies based on the following three parameters: (i) selection, (ii) comparability, and (iii) outcome.[19] Cohort studies can have a maximum score of 9. The Cochrane risk of bias tool 2.0 was used for assessment of RCTs’ quality based on the following parameters: (i) selection bias, (ii) performance bias, (iii) detection bias, (iv) attrition bias, and (v) reporting bias. Risk of bias divided into the following categories: low, high, or some concerns based on each of the above-mentioned domains.[20]

Statistical analysis

The measuring units of the extracted data were uniformized before performing the statistical analyses on Review Manager (Version 5.3. The Cochrane Collaboration). The extracted data with values in medians and interquartile ranges were converted to means and standard deviations (SDs) for continuous data. We pooled the means with SDs for continuous outcomes while for dichotomous outcomes, pooled the events that occurred in interventional versus control groups considering risk ratios (RRs) as effect measure using the random-effects model. Mantel-Haenszel statistical tests were applied for dichotomous outcomes while for continuous data, the inverse variance statistical method was selected and weighted mean difference (WMD) was used as the effect measure. Moreover, we chose the confidence interval (CI) value at 95%. We produced the forest plots to evaluate the pooled results. We applied Higgins I2 statistics to assess the heterogeneity amongst the studies: I2 = 25%–50% falls in statistically mild category, 50%–75% as moderate, and >75% was considered as high heterogeneity.[21] Sensitivity analysis was performed in outcomes in case of high heterogeneity by removing the studies that seemed a potential cause of high heterogeneity. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant in our paper.

Results

A total of 1385 articles were acquired via an extensive literature search of five electronic databases and some additional sources. In duplicates removal, we eliminated 121 papers. Screening of 1,277 articles via title and abstract reading excluded 1191 studies. After a full-text review of 86 articles and eliminating 78 of them due to various reasons, we consequently yielded eight papers (two RCTs, five retrospective cohort studies, and one prospective cohort study) as the rest of them did not meet the eligibility criteria. Quality assessment

Following the guidelines set by Cochrane risk of bias tool 2.0, both the RCTs were categorized as having low risk of bias since there were no apparent concerns in any of the five domains or ROB 2.0 [Figures 2 and 3]. We categorized the three cohort studies as “Good” quality cohorts while three of them as “Fair” quality cohorts based on the categories according to Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality standards. The “Good” quality cohorts had robust methodologies and analysis followed. However, the “Fair” quality ones either lacked in the comparability component or inadequate information regarding the follow-up periods and data of participants, who lost to follow-up[Supplementary Table 3].

Outcome analysis

This systematic review and meta-analysis include 5 retrospective cohorts,[722232425] 1 prospective cohort,[13] and 2 RCTs[414]—comparing the safety and efficacy of two procedures for ovarian and/or fallopian tubes’ removal. We evaluated the safety and efficacy via the following nine outcomes: (i) change in hemoglobin levels before and after procedure; (ii) LOS in hospital after procedure; (iii) mean operation time; (iv) 24-h VAS pain score; (v) total post-operative analgesic usage; (vi) estimated blood loss during procedure; (vii) intra-operative complications; (viii) post-operative complications; (ix) conversion to alternate surgical procedures.

Change in hemoglobin levels

Change in the hemoglobin levels was assessed using four studies with 435 patients in vNOTES group and 403 patients in control group. A statistically significant difference was noticed in hemoglobin levels before and after vNOTES procedure as compared to laparoscopic procedure. When vNOTES was performed, the mean change in hemoglobin before and after the procedure was slightly higher than laparoscopic procedure (WMD: 0.10; 95% CI = 0.02, 0.17; P = 0.01). There was no heterogeneity amongst the included studies [Figure 4].

Length of stay in hospital

LOS in hospital after the respective procedure was evaluated in in 5 studies with 470 participants in the intervention group while 438 participants in the control group. LOS was relatively shorter in patients undergoing vNOTES as compared to laparoscopy where patients spent more time hospitalized after the procedure (WMD: −0.75; 95% CI = −1.09, −0.41; P < 0.0001). Moderate level of heterogeneity was observed amongst the studies (I2 = 54%) [Figure 4].

Mean operation time

All the included studies with 542 patients in vNOTES group and 630 patients in laparoscopy group reported data for mean operation time for each procedure. Pooled results revealed that mean operation time was significantly 22 min shorter in vNOTES as compared to laparoscopy (WMD: −22.08; 95% CI = −33.64, −10.52; P = 0.0002) making vNOTES a faster procedure. A high level of heterogeneity was observed which did not reduce upon performing sensitivity and subgroup analysis [Figure 4].

24-h visual analogue scale pain score

Out of all, three studies assessed 24-h post-operative VAS pain score with 278 patients in intervention group and 246 patients in control group. The pooled analysis implied that pain was significantly lesser in intensity in patients undergoing vNOTES with relatively higher scores of post-operative pains in laparoscopy cohort (WMD: −1.57; 95% CI = −2.92, −0.22; P = 0.02). A high heterogeneity was observed in the pooled studies and sensitivity analysis was not performed due to a smaller number of studies [Figure 5].

Total post-operative analgesic usage

Three papers compared the total post-operative analgesia use between the two surgeries. This outcome was evaluated in 135 patients of vNOTEs and 100 patients of the laparoscopy group. The analysis revealed that lower amounts of analgesics were used post-operatively in vNOTES group in contrast with control group (WMD: −3.57; 95% CI = −7.15, 0.00; P = 0.05), although this correlation remained statistically insignificant. A high heterogeneity was found between the studies and sensitivity analysis was avoided due to a smaller number of studies for this outcome [Figure 5].

Estimated blood loss

Four studies estimated the blood loss in 386 women in vNOTES group versus 465 women in control group. The pooled data indicated no significant difference in the amount of blood loss during the procedures (WMD: −18.00; 95% CI = −44.70, 8.70; P = 0.19). A high level of heterogeneity was noted amongst the studies. Leave-one-out analysis was performed by removing one study,[22] but it failed to reduce the heterogeneity [Figure 5].

Intra-operative complications

A total of four papers were assessed for this outcome with 156 women in the intervention group and 165 participants in control group. No significant difference was observed between the procedures as the risk of intra-operative complications was found to be similar in both the groups (RR: 1.13; 95% CI = 0.17, 7.47; P = 0.90). Very low heterogeneity was noticed between the included studies (I2 = 6%) [Figure 6].

Post-operative complications

Seven studies in total comprising 525 women in vNOTES cohort and 536 women in laparoscopy group assessed the risk of post-operative complications. The pooled analysis resulted in no statistically significant difference between the two comparable groups (RR: 0.69; 95% CI = 0.32, 1.51; P = 0.35). The pooled data revealed very low heterogeneity (I2 = 47%) [Figure 6].

Conversion to alternate procedures

Six papers in total evaluated the conversion of either vNOTES or laparoscopic cohort into any other alternate surgical procedure during the surgery. We assessed 368 women in interventional and 379 women in control group for this outcome. No significant dissimilarity was noticed between the two procedures (RR: 2.56; 95% CI = 0.41, 15.87; P = 0.31). No heterogeneity was observed among the studies (I2 = 0%) [Figure 6].

Discussion

Statistical analysis was performed and data was pooled for 1,172 patients for peri-operative and post-operative outcomes. Findings illustrate that patients undergoing vNOTES had relatively shorter operative time and LOS at hospital after the procedure. Moreover, vNOTES was associated with milder post-operative 24-h VAS pain score. The drop in hemoglobin levels after the procedure was slightly greater in vNOTES cohort, however, no statistically significant differences were observed between the two procedures in terms of amount of estimated blood loss, intra- and post-operative complications, total post-operative analgesic use, and risk of conversion to alternate surgical procedures.

Since this will be the first meta-analysis of its kind, we did not find any other systematic review or meta-analysis to directly compare with our paper, however, the outcomes of interest in our study are comparable to the outcomes of other closely related meta-analyses comparing the two procedures for indications such as hysterectomy only. As the paper indicates no statistically significant difference in the intra- and post-operative complications between the two groups, findings were consistent with the pooled data of several other meta-analyses on hysterectomy and cholecystectomy that also showed no difference.[162728] However, a meta-analysis of seven RCTs by Chaccour et al. reported fewer post-operative and no difference in peri-operative complications among the two groups.[15] Observational studies do pose a risk for selection bias due to no randomization which could have resulted in the selection of healthier patients who are less likely to experience post-operative complications. This demands further large-scale RCTs with robust methodologies to overcome the selection bias.

Results of this study revealed lesser post-operative 24-h VAS pain associated with the vNOTES group that is supported by the findings of Chaccour et al. and Yang et al.[1516] Another RCT produced results in concordance with our meta-analysis indicating milder post-operative pain, however, the findings of Park et al. were contrary to our data, and concluded that post-operative pain was relatively higher in vNOTES cohort.[29] The possible explanation of lesser pain associated with vNOTES could be the fact the patients undergoing vNOTES receive just a single or double incision as compared to the laparoscopy group patients going through more than two incisions in the abdomen.[16]

The mean operative time in the current paper was shorter in the vNOTES procedure and similar results have been highlighted in a meta-analysis by Sarkar et al.[17] and other related studies.[1530] Nonetheless, Swanstrom et al. concluded that patients receiving vNOTES experienced longer duration of surgery.[26] One possible explanation for shorter time might be the reason that vNOTES does not involve opening or suturing of abdominal incisions, unlike laparoscopic surgery. Another imperative outcome of our study was the significantly shorter LOS at hospital experienced by the patients in vNOTES group that remained consistent with what Chaccour et al. deduced in their systematic review.[15] Various other papers investigated the LOS between the two groups observed that LOS was relatively shorter in vNOTES group.[1629] Shorter LOS in vNOTES cohort could be due to lesser post-operative and early recovery. Conversion to alternative surgical techniques is one of the crucial outcomes, however, we found no statistically significant difference among the two comparable groups. A case-matched study by Yang et al. also reported that no difference between the groups since no observed conversions were noticed in their study.[31] Similarly, Basol et al. reported 10% conversion rates in both the vNOTES and laparoscopy groups.[32] Additionally, it was stated by Nulens et al. that vNOTES procedures are 99% successful without them converting to any other procedures during the surgery.[33]

The changes in hemoglobin levels did not significantly differ between the two groups in the studies conducted earlier.[1517] This is contrary to what we illustrated in our paper, where changes/drop in hemoglobin levels were significantly higher in patients undergoing vNOTES. However, the estimated blood loss did not remarkably differ when the two groups were compared in our meta-analysis. Similar results have also been reported in two studies where investigators indicated no significant difference pre- and post-operatively amidst the groups.[1517] Although vNOTES has recently emerged as a popular technique for gynecological procedures like hysterectomy, myomectomy, appendectomy, ovarian cystectomy, salpingectomy but there still remains room for improvement in procedural technologies used.[34] Recent results indicate that vNOTES provides an option to laparoscopy in benign adnexectomy and some cases of salpingo-oophorectomy. In the randomized trial of the NOTABLE, vNOTES showed a higher adnexectomy with no conversion, shorter operating time, and lower pain scores, though adverse events were higher.[35] Analysis of 2,469 cases in 2025 systematic review reported conversion rate of 1.38% and complication rate of 3.44% and rectal injury at colpotomy and training and case selection.[36] Subsequent comparison of cases indicated reduced theatre time, reduced pain, reduced stay and reduced cost, and a 2026 prophylaxis series of 25 of 25 bilateral salpingo-oophorectomies without conversion.[373839]

Strengths and limitations

This will be the first most up-to-date meta-analysis of its kind, comparing the two surgical procedures for their safety and efficiency for procedures like US, BS, USO, BSO, adnexectomy with or without hysterectomies. First, RCTs hold greater value than cohort papers due to their robust methodologies like randomization, hence, demands further large-scale bigger sample-sized RCTs on the topic. Majority (six) of the studies in our paper were cohort in nature which pose the risk of developing selection bias due to no randomization. Secondly, the studies we included evaluated only the intra- and post-operative outcomes of the procedure, however, there should be more studies assessing the long-term outcomes like adhesion formations, ectopic pregnancies, and QoL at 6 months and 1 year. Therefore, we could not investigate the clinical efficacy of vNOTES versus laparoscopy for long-term post-operative outcomes. Thirdly, we did not perform sensitivity or subgroup analysis in our paper due to insufficient studies for specific outcomes that might have resulted in high heterogeneity among the studies.

Future Directions

Future research should prioritize large-scale, multi-center randomized controlled trials with extended follow-up periods to evaluate long-term outcomes such as quality of life, chronic pain, and adhesion formation. Additionally, cost-effectiveness analyses would be valuable for informing healthcare policy and clinical decision-making.

Conclusion

In conclusion, vNOTES proved to be relatively superior than laparoscopic surgery in terms of efficacy determined by shorter operative time, milder post-operative pain, shorter LOS at hospital. However, the groups did not differ in terms of safety, since there was no significant difference in occurrences of conversion to other procedures, intra- and post-operative complications between the two groups. Taking these things into account, we declare vNOTES to be a superior alternative to currently available minimally invasive techniques, however, the shortage of studies warrants further multi-centered large sample-sized RCTs with evaluation of long-term outcomes to better understand the extent of vNOTES versus laparoscopy.

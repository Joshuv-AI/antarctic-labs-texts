An electrochemical sensing platform based on zirconium UiO 66 metal-organic framework for determination of vanillin in the presence of folic acid
Authors: Reza Zaimbashi, Tayebeh Shamspur, Ali Mostafavi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this study, we developed graphite screen-printed electrodes (GSPEs) modified with zirconium UiO-66 metal-organic framework (Zr-UiO-66 MOF) for the detection of vanillin (VN) and folic acid (FA). Zr-UiO-66 MOF was synthesized via a solvothermal method and used to modify the GSPEs to enhance the sensor sensitivity. Cyclic voltammetry (CV) was employed to investigate the electrochemical properties of the modified GSPEs in a VN solution. The results demonstrated the successful modification of the GSPEs with Zr-UiO-66 MOF. Significantly, the UiO-66 MOF-modified GSPEs demonstrated the ability to detect VN across a linear dynamic range (LDR) of 0.1 to 238.0 µM, highlighting their potential as electrochemical sensor. The limit of detection (LOD) was calculated to be 0.03 µM, based on a signal-to-noise of 3 (Sb/m = 3.0). The successful development of this electrochemical sensor offers a promising approach for the simultaneous detection of VN and in FA tablets, coffee milk, and urine samples, with recovery values ranging from 90.0% to 110.0% for VN and 97.0% to 104.2% for FA.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-54992-1.Keywords: Graphite screen-printed electrodes, Zirconium UiO 66 MOF, Vanillin, Folic acid, Cyclic voltammetry, Differential pulse voltammetrySubject terms: Chemistry, Environmental sciences, Materials science

Introduction

Food analysis plays a crucial role in assessing food quality and ensuring consumer safety, particularly in verifying the absence of prohibited additives prior to consumption1,2. a phenolic aldehyde found in natural vanilla beans, is widely used as a flavoring agent in the food industry. It contains essential nutrients and has been associated with beneficial health effects. However, the extraction of natural VN is labor-intensive and costly, leading to the predominant use of synthetic VN derived from other sources. Synthetic VN is used in various industries, including food, beverages, and pharmaceuticals, but excessive consumption can lead to allergic reactions and migraines in humans3–7. According to the FAO/WHO (1992), the maximum allowable usage of VN is 7 mg/100 g. and its addition to infant food is prohibited8,9. FA, a water-soluble vitamin, is known for its benefits in DNA synthesis, prevention of anemia, and nucleotide biosynthesis. It is commonly used to enhance the quality of food. On the other hand, excessive consumption of FA can potentially elevate the likelihood of developing colorectal cancer10. The analysis of VN and FA has gained significant attention, and different sensors using modified electrodes have been developed for their detection11–15. Various analytical methods are available for food analysis, including capillary electrophoresis (CE), high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC), HPLC-DAD, HPLC-MS/MS, and electrochemical sensors16–28. Electrochemical methods have gained attention as analytical methods due to their advantages, including rapid response time, cost-effectiveness, accuracy, simplicity, and the absence of complex sample pretreatments29–33. Among electrochemical methods, electrochemical sensors are particularly suitable, as they offer portability, rapid response, ease of operation, and low cost34–36. The quantitative determination of analytes in real samples plays a key role in safeguarding public health. As these analytes often coexist in real sample, their simultaneous determination is essential. Recently, researchers have increasingly turned to electrochemical sensors owing to their significant advantages, including low cost and the potential to develop compact, portable devices with high sensitivity37–39. Furthermore, as the analysis of food additives becomes increasingly important, research has focused on developing sensors capable of performing effectively in real samples. For these electrochemical sensors to be useful in screening, it is essential that they function reliably within complex food matrices40,41. Most commercial working electrodes lack the sensitivity required for the simultaneous determination of food additives, making surface modification necessary. Among the various electrodes available, screen-printed electrodes (SPEs) have attracted the most attention due to their rapid, cost-effective production and suitability for in-situ analysis42. Screen-printing technology has emerged as a promising approach for the rapid and cost-effective production of biosensors. SPEs, encompassing modified electrodes and microelectrodes, have facilitated the detection and measurement of various analytes. Sensors based on SPE align with the increasing demand for rapid and accurate in-situ analysis, as well as the progress of portable devices43–46. To enhance the chemical specificity and sensitivity of electrochemical sensors, various materials have been employed for electrode surface modification47–51. Nanomaterials, owing to their unique properties, have further enhanced the performance of electrochemical sensors in the determination of electroactive compounds, particularly in the field of food analysis52–54. Selecting materials with high electrocatalytic activity to modifying SPEs to achieve improved sensitivity remains the primary challenge in the simultaneous determination of food additives55. These unique properties have made MOF widely used in electrochemical sensing for the simultaneous detection of food additives56–58. Metal-organic frameworks (MOFs), a class of porous crystalline materials, have gained significant attention in gas storage, separations, catalysis, and sensors. MOFs offer ordered structures, tunable pore sizes, large surface areas, and tailorable chemical properties. MOFs have emerged as a promising class of materials for electrochemical sensing. With their diverse structures, large specific surface areas, high porosity, and tunable pore sizes, MOFs can serve as sensitive elements that facilitate electron transfer and enhance electrochemical signals. In electrochemical sensors, MOFs exhibit high electrocatalytic activity owing to their high porosity and surface area. Nevertheless, there are still obstacles to overcome in signal transduction when dealing with a significant fraction of ligands involved in the construction and the large-scale crystalline structure of most MOFs, leading to limited reproducibility of electrocatalytic responses. To tackle these challenges, researchers have been investigating the synthesis of nanoscale MOFs with precise size control and high uniformity, as well as exploring the use of mixed-node MOFs. These approaches aim to enhance catalytic activity and improve the overall performance of sensors59,60.

Given the importance of food additive analysis, particularly the components VN and FA, and the capabilities of our research group, we developed Zr-UiO-66 MOF -modified GSPEs (Zr-UiO-66 MOF/GSPE sensors) as highly sensitive food sensors for the determination of VN and FA in different types of real samples.

Experimental

Chemicals and apparatus

The voltammetry electrochemical measurements were conducted with an Autolab potentiostat/galvanostat (Model: PGSTAT (302 N); Eco Chemie: The Netherlands). For the experiments, a GSPE (DRP-110; Spain, DropSens) was utilized, comprising three main components: a graphite counter electrode (CE), a graphite working electrode (WE), and a silver pseudo-reference electrode (RE). pH measurements were performed using a Metrohm 710 pH-meter. All measurements were performed at room temperature.

All reagents, such as VN, FA, zirconium(IV) chloride (98.0%), terephthalic acid (98.0%), dimethylformamide (99.8%), acetic acid (100.0%), ethanol (99.8%), H3PO4 (85.0%), and NaOH, were obtained from Merck (Darmstadt, Germany) and Sigma-Aldrich and were of analytical grade. Phosphate buffer solutions (PBSs) were freshly prepared using H3PO4, and NaOH was used to adjust the pH within the range of 3.0 to 9.0. All laboratory protocols were approved by the Research Laboratory of Shahid Bahonar University of Kerman.

Zr-UiO-66 MOF synthesis

The Zr-UiO-66 MOF were synthesized via according to the previous report with some modifications. Briefly, a solution of zirconium(IV) chloride was first prepared by mixing 0.037 g of ZrCl4, 0.026 g terephthalic acid and 20.0 mL of dimethylformamide (DMF). The zirconium(IV) chloride solution was stirred for 15 min. Then, the acetic acid solution (2.4 mL) was slowly dropped into the above zirconium(IV) chloride solution with constant agitation. The mixture was subjected to sonication at room temperature for 30 min. Afterward, the obtained mixture was transferred to an autoclave and subjected to a temperature of 120 °C in an oven for 8 h, the powders were collected by centrifugation, and washing with deionized water/ethanol four times, denoted as Zr-UiO-66 MOF. Finally, the Zr-UiO-66 MOF was dried in vacuum oven at 60 °C overnight60.

Preparing the electrode

To prepare the Zr-UiO-66 MOF stock solution, 1.5 mg of Zr-UiO-66 MOF was dispersed in 1.5 mL of deionized water via ultra-sonication for 30 min. The effect of Zr-UiO-66 MOF suspension volume on VN determination was evaluated using the Zr-UiO-66 MOF/GSPE sensor. The peak current was recorded via cyclic voltammetry in 0.1 M PBS containing 100.0 µM VN. Then, onto the working electrode, 3.0, 5.0, 7.0, and 9.0 µL of the Zr-UiO-66 MOF suspension were drop-cast and allowed to dry under ambient conditions. As shown in Fig. 5A, the maximum oxidation current for vanillin was obtained with 5.0 µL of Zr-UiO-66 MOF suspension.

EDX pattern of Zr-UiO-66 MOF.

Preparation of real samples

To evaluate the practical applicability of the Zr-UiO-66 MOF/GSPE sensor for VN analysis, seven commercial coffee milk powder samples were prepared as follows: 1.0 g of coffee milk powder was transferred into 10 mL of ethanol solution and sonicated for 120 min. The resulting mixture was centrifuged at 2500 rpm (5 min), which was then diluted to 20 mL with PBS (pH 7.0)61,62.

The urine samples (A real sample of myself, Informed consent was obtained from the subject.) were immediately refrigerated after collection. Prior to analysis, each sample was centrifuged for 15 min (at 2000 rpm). The resulting supernatant was filtered through a 0.45 μm filter, and 5.0 mL of the filtrate was diluted to 25 mL with PBS in a volumetric flask63.

Ten FA tablets, each containing 1.0 mg of FA according to the manufacturers label, were ground into a fine powder. A stock solution was prepared by dissolving 2.0 mg of the powder in 150 mL of water by ultra-sonication. Different volumes of this solution were then transferred into volumetric flasks (10 mL) and diluted to the mark with PBS64.

Finally, the amounts of VN and FA were analyzed using the proposed method via the standard addition approach.

Result and discussion

Structure and morphology

FTIR (Fourier Transform Infrared) is a spectroscopic technique employed to analyze the chemical bonding within a material. Fig. 1 displays the characteristic peaks observed in the FTIR spectra of Zr-UiO-66 MOF. The broad absorption peaks around 3152 and 3625 cm− 1 are attributed to O–H which may be due to the residual solvent and adsorbed water. The peaks 1400 and 1708 cm− 1 confirmed he symmetric stretching vibrations of the C = O bond in the carboxylic group in the framework. the peak 1506 and 1583 cm− 1 correspond to the C = C stretching vibration of the phenyl ring, the peak 651 and 745 cm− 1 are related to O-Zr-O symmetric vibration. These data are similar to the results observed by others65.

The investigation of the electrochemical behavior of VN on the bare GSPE and Zr-UiO-66 MOF /GSPE sensor

The influence of pH on the determination of VN was evaluated using the Zr-UiO-66 MOF/GSPE sensor by recording the peak current in 0.1 M PBS (pH 3.0–9.0) containing 100.0 µM VN via cyclic voltammetry at a scan rate of 50 mV/s. The results showed that as the pH increased, the current for VN initially rose, reaching a maximum at pH 7.0 before subsequently decreasing (Fig. 5B). This behavior suggests that pH plays a critical role in the electrochemical response of VN, influencing its ionization and interaction with the sensor surface.

Also, using the Randles–Sevcik equation, the electrochemically active surface area (ESA) of the Zr-UiO-66 MOF/GSPE sensor was determined to be 0.109 cm², approximately 2.7 times larger than the geometrical surface area of the bare GSPE.

The electrochemical properties of the Zr-UiO-66 MOF/GSPE sensor were examined using CV in PBS (0.1 M) at pH = 7.0 containing 100.0 µM VN (Scan rate: 50 mV.s− 1). Fig. 6 demonstrate that the anodic peak current (Ip.a.) of the GSPE are significantly enhanced and peak potential of the GSPE are significantly decreased after modification with Zr-UiO-66 MOF.

CVs (scan rate 50 mV/s− 1) of Zr-UiO-66 /GSPE sensor (a) and GSPE (b) with 100.0 µM VN in PBS (pH = 7.0).

Fig. 6 displays the CVs recorded for VN (100.0 µM) in PBS (0.1 M) at pH = 7.0 on the surfaces of Zr-UiO-66 MOF/GSPEs sensor (CV a) and bare (unmodified) GSPE sensor (CV b). The highest Ip.a. for VN oxidation was observed at 500 mV on the Zr-UiO-66 MOF/GSPEs sensor, which is approximately 195 mV more negative than the value recorded for the bare GSPE. The greater Ip.a. observed on the Zr-UiO-66 MOF/GSPEs sensor confirmed the enhanced electrochemical response of the electrode for VN analysis achieved through the modification of GSPEs with Zr-UiO-66 MOF.

The effects of the scan rates

Since the applied potential scan rate can have a substantial impact on the electrocatalytic behaviour of VN electrochemical sensor, it is crucial to explore how various scan rates of 10 to 300 mV/s affect the electrochemical properties of the Zr-UiO-66 MOF/GSPE sensor. The resulting linear sweep voltammograms (LSVs) data at these various scan rate summarized in Fig. 7. It is clearly observed that the current response associated with the electro oxidation of VN increased as the applied scan rates were raised. The plot of Ip.a. versus the square root of the scan rate (v1/2) exhibited a linear correlation, suggesting a diffusion-controlled process.

LSVs of Zr-UiO-66 MOF/GSPE sensor in 50.0 µM VN in a PBS (pH = 7.0) at various scan rates: 10 (1), 25 (2), 50 (3), 75 (4), 100 (5), 200 (6) and 300 (7) mV s− 1. Inset: Variation of the Ip.a. versus v1/2.

Tafel curves were generated from the data collected in the rising sections, referred to as the Tafel regions, of the current-voltage curves recorded at a scan rate of 10 mV/s (see Fig. 8). The Tafel regions of the current-potential curves are affected by the kinetics of electron transfer in the electrode reactions. The results indicated a Tafel slope of 0.1415 V, implying that the electrode process involves a single-electron rate-determining step (RDS), accompanied by a charge transfer coefficient (α) of 0.58.

LSV of Zr-UiO-66 MOF/GSPE sensor in 50.0 µM VN in a PBS (pH = 7.0) at scan rates: 10 mV s− 1. Inset: The Tafel plot derived from the LSV.

Calculate diffusion coefficient

In addition to the aforementioned techniques, chronoamperometry was utilized to calculate diffusion coefficient (D) of VN on the Zr-UiO-66 MOF/GSPE sensor. The experiments were conducted at a potential of 0.56 V in PBS (0.1 M) at pH = 7.0. Fig. 9 show chronoamperograms in the presence 0.1, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5 and 2.0 mM of VN. Fig. 9A present the plots of current (I) versus t− 1/2 obtained from the chronoamperograms for VN. Additionally, Fig. 9B displays the plot of the slope of the straight lines against the concentrations of VN. The D value for VN was calculated to be 9.7 × 10− 5 cm2.s− 1 using the Cottrell equation66.

Chronoamperograms at Zr-UiO-66 MOF/GSPE sensor in a PBS (pH = 7.0) for different concentration of VN (0.1 (1), 0.5 (2), 1.0 (3), 1.5 (4) and 2.0 (5) mM). Insets A: The plots of I versus t-1/2. Insets B: The slopes of the resulting straight lines versus VN concentrations ranging from 0.1 to 2.0 mM.

Determination of VN in the presence of FA

The simultaneous detection of VN and FA was carried out using DPV in PBS (0.1 M) at pH = 7.0. Fig. 11 shows two well-separated oxidation peaks for VN and FA. The Ip.a. of both VN and FA exhibited a linear increase with their respective concentrations, indicating that they did not interfere with each other. This finding confirms the capability of Zr-UiO-66 MOF/GSPE sensor for simultaneous determination of VN and FA.

Stability of Zr-UiO-66 MOF/GSPE sensor

The Zr-UiO-66 MOF/GSPE sensor stability was studied for 7 and 14 days (Fig. 12A), while the Zr-UiO-66 MOF/GSPE sensor was stored in a closed box at room temperature. Every 7 and 14 days the LSV of VN (50.0 µM) was recorded. The Zr-UiO-66 MOF/GSPEs sensor retained 98.1% and 96.8% of its primary signal current response with a relative standard deviation (RSD%) of 2.9% and 3.1%, respectively. This depicts the long-term stability of the Zr-UiO-66 MOF/GSPE sensor.

The stability (A), repeatability (B), and reproducibility (C) of the Zr-UiO-66 MOF/GSPE sensor.

Repeatability of Zr-UiO-66 MOF/GSPE sensor

The repeatability of the Zr-UiO-66 MOF/GSPE sensor was calculated by determining the oxidation signal currents for 3 replicate LSVs on the 50.0 µM VN (Fig. 12B). The RSD% was obtained to be 3.7%, which is a unique result, providing the compatibility of the Zr-UiO-66 MOF/GSPEs sensor for application in real tests.

Reproducibility of Zr-UiO-66 MOF/GSPE sensor

The reproducibility of the Zr-UiO-66 MOF/GSPE sensor was evaluated by measuring the oxidation peak currents for 50.0 µM VN using three independent sensors via LSV (Fig. 12C). The RSD% was found to be 2.01%, indicating satisfactory reproducibility.

Interference studies

To investigate the selectivity of the Zr-UiO-66 MOF/GSPE sensor, various potential interfering substances commonly found in food matrices were added to the VN solution. These included K⁺, Ca²⁺, Na⁺, and Cl⁻, each at a 100-fold excess. Alanine, glucose was added at a 1.5-fold excess. Additionally, tryptophan, cysteine, tyrosine, and vitamin B6 were added at the same concentration (1-fold excess). These results confirm that the Zr-UiO-66 MOF/GSPE sensor exhibits good selectivity towards VN.

Analysis of real samples

To validate the methods feasibility, the Zr-UiO-66 MOF/GSPE sensor was employed to analyse real samples. The DPV responses for the simultaneous detection of VN and FA are presented in Table 2. The accuracy of the detection procedure was assessed through the standard addition method, and the recovery rates of spiked samples ranged from 90.0% to 110.0%. The Zr-UiO-66 MOF/GSPE sensor demonstrated excellent performance for the simultaneous determination of VN and FA in FA tablets, coffee milk, and urine (A real sample of myself) samples. These results highlight the potential utility of the fabricated electrode as a disposable electrode for future practical applications.

Conclusions

We developed a sensitive Zr-UiO-66 MOF/GSPE sensor for the detection of VN. The effects of Zr-UiO-66 MOF enabled the Zr-UiO-66 MOF/GSPE sensor to achieve outstanding performance in detecting VN. The Zr-UiO-66 MOF/GSPE sensor demonstrated a low LOD of 0.03 µM and a wide LDR of 0.1–238.0 µM. The developed methodology exhibited a sensitivity of 0.1765 µA·µM−1 for VN. The analytical performance of the proposed Zr-UiO-66 MOF/GSPE sensor was investigated for the simultaneous detection of VN and FA in FA tablets, coffee milk, and urine samples, yielding satisfactory recovery results.

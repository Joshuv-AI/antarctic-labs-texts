Functional Genomics of Sulfur Metabolism in Rice: Insights From Transgenic and MicroRNA‐Based Strategies
Authors: Fawad Rauf, Hakim Zamir, Hussam Ahmad, Daud Ali Shah, Shahrukh Khan, Zameer Hussain Jamali, Sada Tanzil, Mohammed S. Alotaibi, Usman Zulfiqar, Dilnoza Sotiboldiyeva, Dilshod Rakhmanov, Mayank Anand Gururani, Shakal Khan Korai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTSulfur (S) is an essential macronutrient that plays critical roles in plant growth, redox homeostasis, stress adaptation, and immunity. In rice ( Oryza sativa ), sulfur deficiency is becoming increasingly common due to modern agricultural practices, yet the molecular regulation of sulfur metabolism remains incompletely understood. Recent advances in functional genomics have provided new insights into the mechanisms governing sulfur uptake, transport, assimilation, and downstream metabolic regulation. This review synthesizes current knowledge of sulfur metabolism in rice, with particular emphasis on transgenic and microRNA‐based approaches. We discuss the functions of sulfate transporters, sulfur assimilation enzymes, and sulfur‐containing metabolites such as glutathione in regulating sulfur homeostasis, redox balance, and stress responses. Particular attention is given to the conserved miR395 regulatory module, which coordinates sulfur assimilation and allocation through posttranscriptional regulation of ATP sulfurylase genes. Evidence from functional genomics studies demonstrates that sulfur metabolism is closely integrated with redox signaling, stress tolerance, and plant immunity. Collectively, these findings support a view of sulfur metabolism as a dynamic regulatory network rather than a linear nutrient‐assimilation pathway. We further highlight key knowledge gaps, emerging research opportunities, and future directions for applying functional genomics to improve sulfur use efficiency and resilience in rice. This review provides a framework for advancing both the fundamental understanding and translational application of sulfur metabolism in sustainable rice production.

Introduction

Sulfur (S) is a key macronutrient that is vital for the growth, development, and adaptation of plants to environmental challenges (Yasin 2024). Sulfur is also found in vitamins, cofactors, iron–sulfur clusters, and other secondary metabolites in plants including the amino acids cysteine and methionine. These sulfur‐containing compounds contribute to redox homeostasis, enhance stress tolerance, and strengthen plant defense responses (Künstler et al. 2020). In contrast to nitrogen and phosphorus, there is a close connection between the metabolism of sulfur and cellular redox homeostasis and environmental signaling, which makes sulfur a fundamental integrator of nutrient status and stress adaptations in plants (Takahashi et al. 2023). This dominant position has enabled sulfur metabolism to become a rich target for crop improvement through molecular breeding, particularly under nutrient‐limited and stress‐prone environments.

Oryza sativa L (rice) is an important staple crop that supplies food for a large proportion of the global population, but its growth is subject to rising nutrient limitations, biotic stress, and climate‐imposed abiotic stress (Mohidem et al. 2022). Modern agricultural systems lack sufficient sulfur deposition, and the widespread use of sulfur‐free fertilizers has further exacerbated sulfur deficiency (Hinckley and Driscoll 2022; Sharma et al. 2024). Sulfur can have direct effects on the grain yield, grain quality, and stress resistance in rice ecosystems, and thus, the metabolism of sulfur needs to be studied at the molecular scale (Pariasca‐Tanaka et al. 2020). Breeding‐wise, to address yield sustainability and minimize the reliance on sulfur in soils with low levels of it, it is more practical to improve the use efficiency (SUE) of sulfur.

There are three major processes of sulfur metabolism in plants: the uptake of sulfate in the soil, the transport and redistribution of sulfur long‐distance, and its incorporation into organic compounds containing sulfur intracellularly (Gigolashvili and Kopriva 2014; Narayan et al. 2023). Sulfate enters the roots via sulfate transporters (SULTRs), is transported to plastids, and is sequentially assimilated through the sulfur reduction pathway: ATP sulfurylase (ATPS) converts sulfate to adenosine 5′‐phosphosulfate (APS), APS reductase (APR) reduces APS to sulfite, and sulfite reductase (SiR) further reduces sulfite to sulfide, which is subsequently incorporated into cysteine via the coordinated action of serine acetyltransferase (SAT) and O‐acetylserine (thiol)lyase (OASTL). These activities are stringently controlled on transcriptional, posttranscriptional, and posttranslational levels, as a way of ensuring sustenance of sulfur homeostasis in varying environmental conditions (Takahashi et al. 2011). These successive stages of the pathway are some of the genetic targets that can be modified by breeding as a way of increasing the efficiency of transporters, improving enzymatic flow, or by altering regulatory processes to enhance the yield of sulfur in rice (Table 1).

Research on model plants, including Arabidopsis thaliana , has made a big leap in the studies of sulfur metabolism and its control in the last 20 years (Feldman‐Salit et al. 2019; Kopriva et al. 2019). Nonetheless, rice exhibits both conserved and species‐specific features due to differences in genome organization, developmental programs, and ecological adaptation. Although many components of sulfur metabolism are conserved between Arabidopsis and rice, functional validation of several regulatory mechanisms remains limited in rice. Hence, there is need to translate the basic knowledge of sulfur related genes in rice through direct characterization. On genome engineering, microRNA‐mediated regulation, transgenic overexpression, and silencing of genes, functional genomics tools are now essential in the dissection of sulfur metabolic pathways in rice (Chen et al. 2022; Huang et al. 2021). These methods offer causal support that indicates the connection between genes and regulatory modules and sulfur‐related traits (Bernal‐Gallardo and de Folter 2024). Notably, they, further, facilitate the discovery and confirmation of candidate genes that may be used to enhance sulfur efficiency by either mapping them using molecular breeding through marker‐assisted selection or by genome editing.

As one of the regulatory systems governing the homeostasis of nutrients, microRNAs (miRNAs) are significant in the processes of posttranscriptional regulation of genes by regulating the transcript cleavage or through translational repression (Paul et al. 2015). MiR395 is the most thoroughly researched miRNA in the context of sulfur metabolism that targets the genes of the ATP sulfurylase and the genes of sulfate transporters to regulate the process of sulfate assimilation and distribution (Matthewman et al. 2012). Initially characterized in Arabidopsis and subsequently investigated in rice, miR395 is vital in the distribution of sulfur, development, and biotic stress response (Yang et al. 2022). In rice, transgenic regulation of miR395 by overexpression and short tandem target mimic (STTM) strategies has identified miR395 as a key player in the organization of the sulfur‐dependent regulatory relationship (Zhang et al. 2017). These results enable miR395 as an ideal target of molecular breeding, in which the expression of miR395 can be finely tuned to aid in the optimization of sulfur assimilation and enhance the performance of plants in the face of sulfur deficiency.

In addition to miRNAs, another major control point of sulfur homeostasis is the sulfur transporters (Li et al. 2020). The rice genome has several SULTR genes that are classified into functional groups that deal with sulfate acquisition, long‐distance distribution, and cytoplasmic redistribution (R. Y. Wang, Liu, Zhao, and Huang 2022). Although several OsSULTR genes have been identified and transcriptionally characterized, functional validation is currently available for only a subset of family members, highlighting an important knowledge gap in rice sulfur biology. However, existing data state that there is dynamical responsiveness of sulfate transporters to both sulfur level and environmental stress (Yuan et al. 2021). The direct method of improving sulfate uptake efficiency and acclimatization to sulfur‐restrictive environments involves taking advantage of natural variation or designing better alleles of these transporters.

Below the sulfate assimilation path, sulfur‐based metabolites including glutathione (GSH) are located at the heart of redox homeostasis and detoxification (Dorion et al. 2021). GSH is one of the most important antioxidants that respond to oxidative stress, heavy metals, and pathogen attacks (Noctor et al. 2024). Transgenic manipulation of enzymes controlling glutathione metabolism in rice, such as glutathione S‐transferases (GSTs), has been shown to raise tolerance to various abiotic stresses (Li et al. 2023). These findings indicate that the ability to increase sulfur‐dependent metabolic products may result in increased stress resilience and stable yield, and thereby, downstream processes should be the focus of breeding programs.

Although more and more evidence has been built on the association of sulfur metabolism to stress responses and immunity, an integration of functional genomics studies in rice has been lacking. Many of the current reviews concentrate on model plants, individual enzymes, or even on a particular element of regulation, like miR395, without synthesizing transgenic and miRNA evidence of rice systems in a systematic way. With the increasing generation of transgenic rice lines targeting sulfur‐related genes, there is a growing need for an integrative framework linking molecular regulation, functional genomics, and physiological outcomes. Such integration is essential for translating mechanistic insights into actionable breeding strategies aimed at improving sulfur‐use efficiency.

The purpose of this review is to bridge this gap by reviewing the literature on functional genomics studies investigating sulfur metabolism through transgenic and microRNA‐based interventions in rice. We compile current knowledge on sulfate transporters, sulfur assimilation enzymes, miRNA‐based regulatory mechanisms, and sulfur‐dependent metabolites, with special emphasis on experimentally validated gene functions and their phenotypic consequences. Particular attention is given to distinguishing functional evidence obtained directly from rice from knowledge inferred from Arabidopsis and other model plant systems. By integrating findings from genetic manipulation studies, we provide a unified conceptual framework for understanding sulfur homeostasis in rice and identify promising targets for future crop improvement. Ultimately, this knowledge can facilitate the development of rice cultivars with enhanced sulfur‐use efficiency, stress tolerance, and productivity under sulfur‐deficient conditions through molecular breeding and biotechnological approaches. In the face of growing global agricultural and nutritional challenges, functional genomics‐guided manipulation of sulfur metabolism may play an important role in developing resilient crop systems and strengthening food security.

Overview of Sulfur Uptake and Transport in Rice

Sulfate Availability and Uptake in Rice‐Growing Systems

In plants, sulfur is primarily taken up from the soil in the form of sulfate (SO4 2−), the most stable and bioavailable inorganic form, through root uptake systems (Dick et al. 2008). The availability of sulfur is highly dynamic in rice‐growing systems and depends on soil type, redox conditions, microbial activity, and fertilizer management (Bell 2008). Sulfate may be reduced to sulfide when paddy soils are flooded and reoxidized upon drainage, resulting in temporal fluctuations in sulfur availability (Yang et al. 2022).

The absorption of sulfate occurs through sulfate transporters (SULTRs), which are plasma membrane‐bound proteins that facilitate sulfate uptake even at low external concentrations (Gallardo et al. 2014). When there is a lack of sulfur, rice triggers high‐affinity transporters and other assimilation genes to ensure cellular sulfur homeostasis (Astolfi et al. 2021; Gigolashvili and Kopriva 2014). The inducibility and the transport effectiveness of SULTR genes are naturally varied, which can be used in S‐deficient conditions by marker‐assisted selection to discover rice genotypes that have better sulfate uptake (Yuan et al. 2021). Besides that, promoter engineering or CRISPR‐mediated stimulation of high‐affinity transporter genes (e.g., OsSULTR1.1) is another approach to increase uptake capacity in low‐sulfur soils (Chen et al. 2019). The depletion of sulfur causes chlorosis, less tillering, and slower reproductive growth, eventually declining the grain production (Pariasca‐Tanaka et al. 2020).

The Sulfate Transporter Gene Family in Rice

The sulfate transporter (SULTR) family is widely distributed in plants and comprises four groups (SULTR1–SULTR4) based on sequence homology, transport affinity, and cellular localization. Distinct expression patterns among SULTR genes indicate functional specialization in rice sulfur uptake, transport, and redistribution (Yuan et al. 2021) (Table 2). The diversity of SULTR family members may represent an adaptation to the fluctuating sulfur availability characteristic of flooded paddy ecosystems.

Group 1 transporters (SULTR1) mediate high‐affinity sulfate uptake from the soil and are predominantly expressed in the root epidermis and cortex (Ding et al. 2016). In rice, OsSULTR1;1 and OsSULTR1;2 are strongly induced under sulfur deficiency, enhancing sulfate acquisition under nutrient‐limiting conditions.

Group 2 transporters (SULTR2) primarily mediate long‐distance sulfate transport through vascular tissues, facilitating sulfate translocation from roots to shoots (Piłsyk and Paszewski 2009; Takahashi 2019).

Group 3 transporters (SULTR3) are associated with intracellular sulfate transport and are localized to several subcellular compartments, including plastids (Takahashi et al. 2012). Although some SULTR3 proteins may not directly transport sulfate, they can function as regulatory or accessory components of sulfate transport systems (Koprivova and Kopriva 2014).

Group 4 transporters (SULTR4) are localized to the tonoplast and mediate vacuolar sulfate efflux (Bohrer and Takahashi 2016). Together, these transporter groups coordinate sulfate acquisition, distribution, intracellular allocation, and remobilization, providing flexibility in sulfur homeostasis under changing environmental conditions.

From a crop improvement perspective, SULTR1 genes may enhance sulfate uptake, SULTR2 genes may improve source‐to‐sink sulfur transport during grain filling, and SULTR4 genes may facilitate sulfur remobilization under transient sulfur limitation (Liang et al. 2025). Combining favorable alleles from multiple transporter groups could therefore improve sulfur‐use efficiency in rice.

Transcriptional Regulation of Sulfate Transporters in Rice

In rice, the expression of sulfate transporter genes is tightly regulated at the transcriptional level (Kumar et al. 2011). Under sulfur‐deficient conditions, high‐affinity transporter genes are rapidly induced through sulfur‐responsive cis‐elements and transcription factors that coordinate sulfate uptake with sulfur assimilation (Davidian and Kopriva 2010). Although sulfur starvation responses are broadly conserved among plant species, rice exhibits distinct regulatory patterns that likely reflect adaptation to flooded environments (Zenzen et al. 2024). In addition to sulfur availability, sulfate transporter expression is influenced by nitrogen status, oxidative stress, and biotic interactions, highlighting extensive cross‐talk between nutrient and stress signaling pathways.

Regulation also occurs beyond transcription. Sulfate transport is further fine‐tuned through posttranscriptional mechanisms, including miRNA‐mediated regulation and protein stability (Takahashi et al. 2011). Sulfur‐responsive microRNAs such as miR395 regulate sulfate homeostasis by modulating the expression of genes involved in sulfate transport and assimilation, thereby influencing sulfur distribution within the plant (Yang et al. 2022). Together, these regulatory mechanisms enable dynamic adjustment of sulfur acquisition and allocation in response to developmental and environmental cues.

Functional Genomics Evidence for Sulfate Transporters in Rice

Despite extensive gene identification and expression profiling studies, functional genomics analyses of sulfate transporters in rice remain comparatively limited relative to Arabidopsis (Kumar et al. 2011). Existing evidence indicates that OsSULTR1;1 is a key component of sulfate uptake, particularly under sulfur‐deficient conditions, and that reduced activity impairs sulfur acquisition and plant growth (Sun et al. 2021). These findings highlight the importance of high‐affinity transporters in maintaining sulfur nutrition under limiting conditions.

The SULTR2 family plays a key role in long‐distance sulfate transport, mediating sulfur distribution between roots and shoots (Takahashi et al. 2012). Impairment of this process may result in tissue‐specific sulfur deficiency even when total sulfur availability is adequate (Hawkesford 2012). Vacuolar sulfate storage and remobilization mediated by SULTR4 transporters remain poorly understood in rice (Gigolashvili and Kopriva 2014). Overall, functional validation is currently available for only a small subset of rice SULTR genes, highlighting the need for further studies to clarify the roles of individual transporters and their interactions in maintaining sulfur homeostasis.

Integration of Sulfate Transport With Sulfur Assimilation and Plant Physiology

Translocation and uptake of sulfate are coordinated with sulfur assimilation pathways, ensuring the efficient utilization of absorbed sulfate (Davidian and Kopriva 2010). This coordination is regulated by feedback mechanisms involving metabolites such as O‐acetylserine, cysteine, and glutathione, which reflect cellular sulfur status. Efficient sulfate transport supports sulfur assimilation, thereby contributing to redox homeostasis and abiotic stress tolerance in rice.

Enhanced sulfate uptake promotes the synthesis of sulfur‐containing compounds, including glutathione, that play critical roles in stress responses (Sun et al. 2023). Conversely, impaired sulfate transport can restrict sulfur metabolism, reducing growth and weakening defense responses (Kopriva et al. 2019). Sulfur availability also influences plant–microbe interactions, potentially through its effects on rhizosphere communities and sulfur‐dependent defense mechanisms. Together, these observations highlight the central role of sulfate transport in integrating sulfur nutrition with plant growth, stress adaptation, and physiological performance (Table 3).

Sulfur Assimilation Pathway and Key Enzymatic Nodes in Rice

Overview of Sulfur Assimilation in Plants

Sulfate must be converted into organic forms following cellular uptake before being incorporated into essential biomolecules. Sulfur assimilation in plants is a highly conserved process that primarily occurs in plastids, where sulfate is reduced and incorporated into cysteine, the first sulfur‐containing amino acid (de Bont et al. 2022). Methionine, glutathione, iron–sulfur clusters, vitamins, and secondary metabolites are derived from cysteine, linking sulfur metabolism to redox balance and stress responses (Shah et al. 2022).

Sulfur assimilation in rice is directly linked to growth, development, and maintenance of cellular redox homeostasis, particularly under environmental stress (Kopriva et al. 2019). This pathway is tightly regulated to balance metabolic demand while preventing the accumulation of toxic intermediates (Davidian and Kopriva 2010). Functional genomics studies have identified key enzymes involved in regulating sulfur flux and coordinating downstream metabolic processes. Variation in the activity of sulfur assimilation enzymes may influence the efficiency with which absorbed sulfate is converted into sulfur‐containing metabolites and other cellular components (El‐Beltagi et al. 2026).

ATP Sulfurylase: Entry Point of Sulfate Assimilation

ATP sulfurylase (ATPS), also known as sulfate activase, catalyzes the first committed step of sulfur assimilation by converting sulfate into adenosine 5′‐phosphosulfate (APS), a key intermediate in the pathway (Jez 2019). ATPS activity is energetically demanding and tightly regulated, with multiple isoforms exhibiting distinct regulatory properties. In rice, the expression of ATPS genes varies according to sulfur availability and developmental stage (R. Y. Wang, Liu, Zhao, and Huang 2022). Studies in model plants have demonstrated that ATPS regulation can substantially influence sulfur flux and downstream metabolite accumulation (Møldrup et al. 2011).

ATPS is posttranscriptionally regulated by miR395, which downregulates ATPS transcripts and modulates sulfate homeostasis (Kawashima et al. 2009). Overexpression of miR395 reduces ATPS transcript levels, whereas suppression of miR395 increases ATPS expression and sulfur uptake. This regulatory module highlights the importance of posttranscriptional control in maintaining sulfur homeostasis and coordinating sulfate assimilation with sulfur availability (Figure 1).

APS Reductase and Sulfite Reductase: Reductive Steps of Sulfur Assimilation

Following sulfate activation, APS reductase (APR) reduces APS to sulfite and represents a key rate‐limiting step in sulfur assimilation. APR activity is enhanced under sulfur deficiency and is directly linked to overall sulfur flux (Davidian and Kopriva 2010). Several APR genes show tissue‐specific and developmental expression patterns in rice, suggesting functional specialization. Sulfite reductase (SiR) further reduces sulfite to sulfide, a highly reactive intermediate that must be rapidly incorporated into cysteine to prevent toxicity. SiR is localized in plastids and is essential for plant survival (Jez 2019).

Although functional studies in rice are limited, expression analyses indicate that APR and SiR are dynamically regulated during sulfur starvation, oxidative stress, and pathogen attack (El‐Beltagi et al. 2026). Together, APR and SiR represent key regulatory points in the sulfur assimilation pathway, linking sulfur availability with downstream metabolic and stress‐response processes (Hasanuzzaman et al. 2018).

Cysteine Biosynthesis: Serine Acetyltransferase and O‐Acetylserine (Thiol)lyase

Cysteine biosynthesis is the final step of sulfur assimilation and is catalyzed by the cysteine synthase complex, consisting of serine O‐acetyltransferase (SAT) and O‐acetylserine (thiol)lyase (OASTL) (Hell and Wirtz 2011). Serine and acetyl‐CoA are converted into O‐acetylserine (OAS), which subsequently reacts with sulfide via OASTL to produce cysteine (Mazumder and Gourinath 2016). OAS also functions as a signaling molecule that reflects sulfur demand and regulates sulfate uptake and assimilation (Romero et al. 2014). Rice possesses multiple isoforms of SAT and OASTL that are localized in plastids, mitochondria, and the cytosol (Kurt et al. 2021).

Transgenic studies in rice and other crops indicate that overexpression of SAT or OASTL increases cysteine and glutathione levels, thereby enhancing tolerance to oxidative stress and heavy metal toxicity (Wirtz and Hell 2006). These findings highlight the central role of cysteine biosynthesis in linking sulfur assimilation with redox regulation and stress adaptation.

Regulation of Sulfur Assimilation by Metabolic Feedback and Redox Signals

Sulfur assimilation is closely linked to cellular redox status and primary metabolism. Low levels of sulfur‐containing compounds, particularly glutathione, provide feedback regulation that helps maintain sulfur homeostasis and balance sulfur flux (Kopriva and Rennenberg 2004). Changes in cellular redox status can also influence the expression and activity of sulfur assimilation genes, linking sulfur metabolism with stress‐signaling pathways.

Salinity, drought, and pathogen attack induce oxidative stress in rice and are associated with changes in the expression of sulfur assimilation genes and glutathione metabolism (Dixit et al. 2015). Genomic studies of glutathione biosynthesis and recycling have highlighted the role of sulfur assimilation in redox buffering and stress tolerance (Hasanuzzaman et al. 2018). In addition, miRNA‐mediated regulation contributes to sulfur homeostasis, with the miR395‐ATPS module representing a well‐characterized example of posttranscriptional control of sulfur metabolism (Panda and Sunkar 2015).

Functional Genomics Gaps and Emerging Directions

Sulfur assimilation is closely linked to redox regulation and stress responses, yet functional genomics studies in rice remain limited (Lunde et al. 2008). Existing evidence suggests that sulfur assimilation enzymes and glutathione turnover play essential roles in maintaining redox homeostasis under both abiotic and biotic stress conditions. However, most current knowledge is derived from gene expression analyses, and comprehensive functional validation of key sulfur assimilation genes in rice is still lacking. Furthermore, several regulatory mechanisms have been extensively characterized in model species such as A. thaliana , but their functional conservation in rice remains insufficiently understood.

The miR395‐ATPS regulatory module provides an additional layer of control that enables dynamic adjustment of sulfur metabolism in response to environmental cues (Yang et al. 2022). Future studies integrating genome editing, transcriptomics, metabolomics, and functional characterization will be essential for identifying regulatory networks and validating the roles of candidate genes involved in sulfur assimilation and homeostasis.

microRNA‐Mediated Regulation of Sulfur Metabolism in Rice

microRNAs as Regulators of Nutrient Homeostasis in Plants

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are small, noncoding RNAs that regulate gene expression at the posttranscriptional level by directing mRNA cleavage or inhibiting translation. In plants, miRNAs play crucial roles in development, hormone regulation, and responses to environmental changes, including nutrient availability (Zeng et al. 2014). A growing body of evidence indicates that miRNAs are key regulators of nutrient homeostasis, enabling rapid metabolic adjustment to fluctuating nutrient conditions.

Several nutrient‐responsive miRNAs have been characterized, including miR399 in phosphate homeostasis, miR398 in copper regulation, and miR395 in sulfur metabolism (Kehr 2013). These miRNAs typically target key regulatory genes within nutrient pathways, thereby fine‐tuning nutrient uptake, allocation, and utilization without extensive transcriptional reprogramming. Nutrient‐regulated miRNAs have increasingly been recognized as important contributors to stress adaptation and nutrient‐use efficiency in rice (Nadarajah and Kumar 2019). Because individual miRNAs can regulate multiple target genes, they serve as important integrators of nutrient signaling and metabolic coordination in plants (Li et al. 2024).

miR395: A Conserved Regulator of Sulfur Metabolism

Of all sulfur‐related miRNAs, miR395 is the most extensively characterized and highly conserved in higher plants. It is strongly induced under sulfur‐deficient conditions and regulates target genes, including ATPS genes and other components of sulfur transport and assimilation (Matthewman et al. 2012; Ristova and Kopriva 2022). This regulatory module helps coordinate sulfur assimilation with cellular sulfur demand (Takahashi et al. 2011). In rice, miR395 is encoded by multiple loci and exhibits distinct spatial and developmental expression patterns. It is highly induced under sulfur deficiency, highlighting its important role in adaptive responses to sulfur limitation (Yang et al. 2022).

Functional studies suggest that miR395 reduces ATPS transcript levels, thereby regulating sulfur flux and the accumulation of downstream metabolites such as cysteine and glutathione. These findings demonstrate the central role of the miR395‐ATPS module in maintaining sulfur homeostasis and coordinating sulfur assimilation in response to nutrient availability (Xiang et al. 2024).

Functional Genomics of miR395 in Rice

The role of miR395 in regulating sulfur metabolism has been demonstrated through transgenic studies in rice. Overexpression of miR395 reduces ATPS transcript levels and alters sulfur accumulation and distribution between roots and shoots, confirming its role as a negative regulator of sulfur assimilation (Liu et al. 2022). Conversely, short tandem target mimic (STTM)‐mediated inhibition of miR395 increases ATPS expression and enhances sulfur assimilation capacity (Othman et al. 2023). STTM‐based approaches enable stable suppression of miR395 across developmental stages with minimal functional redundancy among miRNA family members (Cao et al. 2013).

Beyond sulfur metabolism, manipulation of miR395 has also been reported to influence disease resistance and stress responses, reflecting its pleiotropic functions. Together, these studies demonstrate that the miR395‐ATPS regulatory module plays a central role in coordinating sulfur assimilation, growth, and stress adaptation in rice. The availability of both overexpression and STTM‐based systems provides valuable tools for functional characterization of sulfur‐regulatory networks and their physiological consequences (Figure 2).

miR395 and Sulfate Allocation: Spatial Regulation of Sulfur Metabolism

In addition to regulating sulfur assimilation, miR395 plays an important role in sulfate allocation. In Arabidopsis, miR395 promotes sulfate redistribution to young leaves and actively growing tissues (Liang et al. 2010). Similar spatial regulation has been suggested in rice, where miR395 influences sulfate partitioning between roots, shoots, and reproductive organs (Yang et al. 2022). When miR395 suppresses ATPS expression in specific tissues, local sulfate assimilation is reduced, allowing sulfate to be retained for long‐distance transport (Yuan et al. 2016).

Spatial regulation is particularly important in rice because sulfur demand varies among tillering, panicle development, and grain filling stages. miR395‐mediated regulation coordinates with sulfate transporters to balance local sulfur assimilation and whole‐plant sulfur distribution (Landaeta‐Sepúlveda et al. 2025). These findings highlight the importance of spatial sulfur allocation in maintaining sulfur homeostasis during plant development and reproduction (Rengasamy et al. 2024).

Integration of miR395 With Stress Responses and Plant Immunity

Recent studies indicate that miR395 regulates not only sulfur metabolism but also stress responses and plant defense. Nutrient status is closely associated with immune signaling, and miR395‐mediated changes in sulfur metabolism can influence resistance to bacterial pathogens in rice (Shahzad et al. 2018). Sulfur‐containing metabolites, particularly glutathione, play central roles in redox regulation and defense responses, and their synthesis is indirectly influenced by miR395 through its regulation of sulfur assimilation (de Bont et al. 2022).

Consequently, miR395 has the potential to influence both biotic and abiotic stress responses. However, its effects are highly context‐dependent, reflecting complex interactions among nutrient status, metabolic balance, redox homeostasis, and defense signaling pathways. These findings further support the role of miR395 as an important integrator of sulfur metabolism and stress adaptation in rice (Tiwari and Rajam 2022).

Sulfur‐Containing Metabolites and Downstream Functional Outputs in Rice

Sulfur Metabolism Beyond Assimilation: Functional Outputs

Sulfur enters plant metabolism through uptake and assimilation, but its functional roles are ultimately mediated by sulfur‐containing metabolites (Gigolashvili and Kopriva 2014). These metabolites play critical roles in redox balance, detoxification, stress tolerance, and defense and are closely associated with plant fitness and productivity. Sulfur‐containing metabolites are particularly important in rice, which is frequently exposed to abiotic stresses such as salinity, oxidative stress, and heavy metal contamination (Sackey et al. 2025).

Functional studies indicate that alterations in sulfur flux can significantly affect downstream metabolite pools, leading to measurable changes in stress responses and physiological performance (Hacham et al. 2025). Therefore, sulfur‐containing metabolites represent important functional outputs of sulfur metabolism and provide valuable indicators of the physiological consequences of sulfur assimilation and regulation.

Glutathione as a Central Sulfur‐Containing Metabolite

Glutathione (γ‐glutamylcysteinylglycine, GSH) is one of the major low‐molecular‐weight thiols in plants and serves as a principal reservoir of reduced sulfur. It plays essential roles in cellular redox homeostasis, reactive oxygen species (ROS) scavenging, xenobiotic detoxification, and stress‐related signaling pathways (Dorion et al. 2021). Because its synthesis depends on cysteine availability, glutathione metabolism is tightly linked to sulfur assimilation. Sulfur deficiency in rice leads to reduced glutathione levels, increased oxidative stress, and diminished stress tolerance (Maruyama‐Nakashita and Ohkama‐Ohtsu 2017).

Functional genomics studies of glutathione biosynthesis have identified key enzymes, including GSH1 (γ‐glutamylcysteine synthase) and GSH2 (glutathione synthase), that regulate glutathione accumulation. Manipulation of these genes has demonstrated that enhanced glutathione production can improve tolerance to salinity, oxidative stress, and heavy metal toxicity (Ayub et al. 2025; Bose Mazumdar and Chattopadhyay 2025). These findings highlight glutathione as a major functional output of sulfur metabolism and a key determinant of cellular stress adaptation (Rai et al. 2023).

Glutathione Redox Cycling and Stress Signaling

Glutathione functions as both an antioxidant and a signaling molecule (Aquilano et al. 2014). Its reduced (GSH) and oxidized (GSSG) forms maintain cellular redox homeostasis, and the GSH/GSSG ratio serves as a sensitive indicator of cellular redox status. This ratio influences the activity of redox‐sensitive proteins and transcription factors. Environmental stresses, including drought, salinity, and pathogen attack, can disrupt redox equilibrium in rice (Aquilano et al. 2014).

Enzymes such as glutathione reductase (GR) are essential for maintaining glutathione in its reduced form, thereby sustaining antioxidant capacity (Ghosh et al. 2022). Consequently, sulfur metabolism plays an important role in antioxidant defense and the regulation of stress‐responsive gene expression through redox signaling (Samanta et al. 2020). The glutathione redox cycle therefore represents a key link between sulfur metabolism, cellular redox regulation, and stress adaptation in rice (Bose Mazumdar and Chattopadhyay 2025).

Glutathione S‐Transferases and Sulfur‐Dependent Detoxification

Glutathione S‐transferases (GSTs) represent a large family of enzymes that conjugate glutathione to various endogenous and exogenous compounds, thereby facilitating the detoxification, transport, and sequestration of toxic substances (Nianiou‐Obeidat et al. 2017). Numerous GST genes in rice are induced by abiotic and biotic stresses, including salinity, heavy metal exposure, and herbicide treatment (Jain et al. 2010).

Expression of individual GST genes has been shown to enhance tolerance to oxidative damage, salinity, and heavy metal toxicity (Nianiou‐Obeidat et al. 2017). Because GST activity depends on glutathione availability, sulfur metabolism directly influences detoxification capacity. Consequently, sulfur assimilation and glutathione metabolism are closely integrated with cellular defense mechanisms against environmental stress (Lin et al. 2025).

Sulfur‐Containing Amino Acids and Protein Function

Sulfur‐containing amino acids, particularly methionine and cysteine, are essential for protein structure and function (Bin et al. 2017). Cysteine residues participate in enzymatic catalysis, metal binding, and redox regulation through reversible thiol modifications, whereas methionine plays important roles in protein synthesis and methylation reactions. Sulfur deficiency in rice alters amino acid composition and reduces grain protein content, particularly by lowering methionine levels.

Although studies specifically addressing methionine biosynthesis in rice remain limited, sulfur‐containing amino acids are important determinants of grain nutritional quality. In addition, posttranslational modifications involving cysteine residues, including S‐glutathionylation, contribute to the regulation of enzyme activity and stress responses (Kalinina and Novichkova 2021). These observations highlight the importance of sulfur metabolism not only for plant growth and stress adaptation but also for protein function and grain nutritional quality.

Sulfur Metabolism, Secondary Metabolites, and Defense

Sulfur‐containing secondary metabolites play important roles in plant defense, particularly during plant‐pathogen and plant‐herbivore interactions (Divekar et al. 2022). Although rice does not produce glucosinolates found in Brassicaceae, sulfur metabolism contributes to the production of phytoalexins, redox‐active compounds, and defense‐associated proteins (Valletta et al. 2023).

Functional genomics studies indicate that disease resistance can be influenced by perturbations in sulfur metabolism, likely through changes in glutathione‐dependent redox signaling and defense gene expression (Takahashi et al. 2023). These findings emphasize the importance of sulfur metabolism in both basal and inducible defense responses. Together, the available evidence highlights sulfur metabolism as an important component of the molecular network linking nutrient status, redox regulation, and plant immunity in rice.

Integration of Sulfur Metabolites With Whole‐Plant Physiology

Sulfur‐containing metabolites represent the final outputs of sulfur metabolism and link upstream metabolic processes with whole‐plant physiological responses (Takahashi et al. 2011). Metabolite levels are directly influenced by changes in sulfate uptake, assimilation, and regulatory pathways, thereby affecting growth, stress tolerance, and productivity. Functional genomics studies indicate that alterations in genes controlling sulfur metabolism often produce pleiotropic effects because sulfur‐containing metabolites participate in numerous cellular processes.

For example, elevated glutathione levels can enhance stress tolerance but may also influence growth and resource allocation, highlighting the importance of maintaining metabolic balance. In rice, sulfur metabolism involves trade‐offs among growth, stress adaptation, and grain quality. Understanding these interactions is therefore essential for evaluating the physiological consequences of modifying sulfur metabolic pathways.

Sulfur Metabolism and Plant Immunity: A Functional Genomics Perspective

Sulfur Nutrition as a Modulator of Plant Immune Responses

Plant immunity is an energy‐ and metabolite‐intensive process that requires close coordination between primary metabolism and defense signaling (Checker et al. 2018). Increasing evidence suggests that nutrient status, particularly sulfur availability, has a significant impact on immune competence. Sulfur contributes to immunity by providing building blocks for defense proteins and by maintaining the redox balance and signaling processes required for defense activation. Sulfur deficiency increases rice susceptibility to pathogens, whereas adequate sulfur supply enhances resistance to both biotrophic and necrotrophic pathogens (Gullner et al. 2018).

These observations indicate that sulfur availability influences both basal defense capacity and inducible immune responses. Recent advances in functional genomics have further revealed extensive interactions between sulfur metabolism and immune signaling networks. Together, these findings support the view that sulfur nutrition is an important determinant of plant immune competence and disease resistance in rice.

Role of Sulfur Assimilation and Redox Homeostasis in Defense

Redox regulation is closely interconnected with sulfur assimilation through the production of cysteine and glutathione (de Bont et al. 2022). Reactive oxygen species (ROS) act as both antimicrobial and signaling molecules during immune responses, whereas glutathione serves as a major redox buffer that regulates ROS levels (Zechmann 2020). Impaired glutathione homeostasis in rice leads to increased oxidative damage and weakened defense responses, whereas elevated glutathione levels enhance resistance to pathogen‐induced stress.

Given the central role of glutathione in redox buffering, genes involved in sulfur assimilation and glutathione metabolism are expected to have important effects on immune signaling and stress adaptation. Genetic evidence indicates that sulfur flux directly influences redox‐mediated immune responses (Matika and Loake 2014). These findings demonstrate that sulfur metabolism contributes to plant defense not only by supplying sulfur‐containing metabolites but also by regulating redox‐dependent signaling pathways that coordinate immune responses (Figure 3).

Integration of sulfur assimilation, glutathione redox balance, and immune signaling in rice.

microRNA‐Mediated Regulation of Sulfur Metabolism and Immunity

miRNA‐mediated regulation plays a significant role in linking sulfur metabolism with plant immunity. miR395 indirectly regulates sulfur availability for defense processes by controlling ATPS expression and sulfate distribution (Yang et al. 2022). Transgenic studies in rice have shown that both overexpression and suppression of miR395 significantly affect sulfate allocation and sulfur availability.

These changes alter the cellular redox status and expression of defense genes, ultimately influencing disease resistance. Manipulation of miR395 has been reported to affect resistance to bacterial pathogens, further demonstrating that sulfur metabolism is functionally linked to immune responses (Shen et al. 2024; Val‐Torregrosa et al. 2021). Collectively, these findings identify miR395 as a crucial regulatory component that connects sulfur metabolism, redox homeostasis, and plant immunity.

Sulfur‐Dependent Detoxification Systems in Defense Responses

Plants respond to pathogen attack by activating detoxification and antioxidant systems to mitigate oxidative stress and counteract harmful compounds produced during host–pathogen interactions (Prashar et al. 2025). Glutathione S‐transferase (GST)–mediated conjugation of glutathione to toxic compounds is a major detoxification mechanism that promotes the sequestration and removal of harmful substances (Jain et al. 2010). Multiple GST genes are induced during pathogen infection in rice, and elevated GST activity enhances resistance to oxidative stress.

Sulfur metabolism indirectly contributes to detoxification capacity because its effectiveness depends on glutathione availability. Disruption of sulfur metabolism reduces glutathione pools, thereby impairing detoxification processes and increasing susceptibility to oxidative damage (Noctor et al. 2024). These observations highlight the close relationship between sulfur metabolism, glutathione‐dependent detoxification, and defense responses in rice (Bose Mazumdar and Chattopadhyay 2025; Estévez and Hernández 2020).

Sulfur Metabolism and Defense Signaling Cross‐Talk

Sulfur metabolism interacts with immune signaling pathways through the coordinated regulation of redox status and sulfur‐containing metabolites. These interactions influence key hormone signaling pathways, including salicylic acid (SA), jasmonic acid (JA), and ethylene, which play central roles in plant immunity (Rasheed et al. 2022). Changes in sulfur assimilation and glutathione levels can affect salicylic acid accumulation and glutathione‐dependent signaling, thereby modulating defense gene activation (Bose Mazumdar and Chattopadhyay 2025).

Although mechanistic studies in rice are limited, functional genomics data indicate that sulfur status plays an important role in shaping immune signaling during defense responses. Together, these findings support the existence of extensive cross‐talk between sulfur metabolism and hormone‐mediated defense pathways, highlighting the integration of nutrient status and immune regulation in rice.

Functional Genomics Evidence From Rice Pathosystems

Research on rice‐pathogen interactions provides important insights into the role of sulfur metabolism in plant immunity. Functional genomics approaches have demonstrated that alterations in genes involved in sulfur‐related processes can modify susceptibility to major pathogens such as bacterial blight and bacterial leaf streak (Künstler et al. 2020; W. Wang, Liu, Mishra, et al. 2022). These changes are associated with alterations in host metabolism, redox status, and defense gene expression. Sulfur availability may also influence pathogen nutrition and development, further affecting disease outcomes. Collectively, these findings support the view that sulfur metabolism is not merely a nutritional determinant but an active component of host‐pathogen interactions in rice.

Balancing Growth and Defense Through Sulfur Metabolism

Plant immune activation is metabolically costly and can impair growth and yield (Ning et al. 2017). Sulfur metabolism plays a central role in mediating this trade‐off, as sulfur resources must be allocated between growth and defense processes (Capaldi et al. 2015). Both sulfur deficiency and excess can disrupt this balance, highlighting the need for tight regulation of sulfur homeostasis. The miR395 regulatory module represents one mechanism that helps coordinate sulfur allocation among competing physiological demands.

These growth‐defense trade‐offs have important implications for rice improvement. Rather than maximizing either growth or defense alone, effective strategies should aim to optimize sulfur allocation to maintain productivity while preserving immune competence (El‐Beltagi et al. 2026).

Experimental Strategies for Functional Genomics of Sulfur Metabolism in Rice

Overview of Functional Genomics Approaches

Functional genomics encompasses a range of experimental strategies used to determine gene function through the manipulation of gene expression and analysis of the resulting phenotypes (Pevsner 2015). In rice, these methods have played a pivotal role in linking sulfur‐regulatory genes and networks to agronomically important traits, including nutrient‐use efficiency, stress tolerance, and disease resistance. Because sulfur metabolic pathways are complex and often functionally redundant, no single approach can fully resolve gene function, and integrated genetic, molecular, and physiological analyses are required (Yi et al. 2010).

Recent advances in rice transformation, genome editing, and high‐throughput phenotyping have significantly expanded the functional genomics toolkit available for sulfur metabolism research (Figure 4). These approaches facilitate the identification and validation of genes and regulatory factors controlling sulfur uptake, assimilation, and downstream metabolic responses. By linking gene activity with measurable traits such as sulfur uptake efficiency, glutathione accumulation, and stress responses, functional genomics provides a foundation for understanding rice adaptation to sulfur‐limited environments (Xu et al. 2025).

Functional genomics framework for dissecting sulfur metabolism and improving rice performance.

Transgenic Overexpression and Gene Silencing Approaches

Transgenic overexpression approaches are widely used to study sulfur‐associated genes in rice by enhancing the expression of sulfate transporters, sulfur assimilation enzymes, or regulatory components (Dong et al. 2020). These techniques are valuable for identifying gain‐of‐function phenotypes and determining rate‐limiting steps in sulfur metabolism, particularly in glutathione biosynthesis and detoxification pathways. Complementary loss‐of‐function analyses are commonly performed using gene‐silencing approaches such as RNA interference (RNAi). However, functional redundancy among sulfur‐related gene families may limit the effectiveness of single‐gene targeting approaches (Chan et al. 2013).

Developmental defects associated with essential genes can be mitigated through partial gene silencing or inducible RNAi systems. Pleiotropic effects are frequently observed in transgenic studies, making comprehensive phenotypic evaluation across developmental stages and environmental conditions essential. Together, overexpression and gene‐silencing approaches provide powerful tools for validating candidate genes and elucidating their roles in sulfur uptake, assimilation, and stress responses.

Genome Editing Technologies

Genome editing, particularly CRISPR/Cas9 technology, has transformed functional genomics in rice by enabling precise and heritable modification of target genes (Mohan et al. 2022; Zegeye et al. 2022). This technology allows the generation of knockouts, knock‐ins, and allele variants with minimal off‐target effects, making it especially valuable for investigating gene family redundancy and regulatory networks involved in sulfur metabolism.

Despite its potential, genome‐editing applications targeting sulfur metabolism in rice remain limited. Expanded use of CRISPR/Cas9 and related systems will be important for validating gene function, dissecting regulatory pathways, and identifying key genetic determinants of sulfur uptake and assimilation (Zafar et al. 2020). In addition, genome editing provides a powerful platform for generating and evaluating targeted allelic variants in sulfur‐related genes and regulatory elements.

microRNA‐Based Functional Genomics Strategies

MicroRNA‐based technologies provide effective approaches for investigating sulfur metabolism because they can simultaneously regulate multiple genes within interconnected regulatory networks (Li et al. 2021). In rice, miRNAs such as miR395 regulate sulfur assimilation and allocation by targeting key genes involved in sulfur activation and transport. miRNAs can be overexpressed, whereas short tandem target mimic (STTM) technology enables stable silencing of endogenous miRNAs, helping to overcome functional redundancy among miRNA family members (Othman et al. 2023; Peng et al. 2018) (Table 4).

Physiological and Biochemical Phenotyping

Robust phenotyping is essential for translating genetic changes into agronomically relevant traits (Araus et al. 2018; Cobb et al. 2013). Phenotyping of sulfur metabolism should integrate molecular, physiological, and biochemical measurements. Key physiological traits include sulfur content in roots and shoots, sulfate uptake efficiency, and sulfur distribution within plant tissues (Hawkesford 2012). Biochemical analyses of cysteine, glutathione, and other sulfur‐containing metabolites provide direct indicators of sulfur metabolic activity.

Assessment of redox status through GSH/GSSG ratios and reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels links sulfur metabolism with stress responses and immunity. Exposing plants to diverse environmental conditions, including sulfur deficiency, salinity, and pathogen stress, enables the evaluation of context‐specific gene functions (Noctor et al. 2024). Together, these phenotypic measurements provide a comprehensive framework for assessing the physiological consequences of genetic variation and functional manipulation of sulfur‐related pathways in rice.

Omics Approaches and Systems‐Level Integration

Functional genomics is increasingly driven by high‐throughput omics technologies. Transcriptomics enables genome‐wide analysis of sulfur‐responsive gene expression and regulatory networks (Bielecka et al. 2015). Small RNA sequencing identifies sulfur‐responsive miRNAs and their targets, whereas metabolomics reveals changes in sulfur‐containing metabolites and metabolic fluxes (Wishart 2019). Proteomics provides insights into posttranslational modifications and protein‐level regulation of sulfur metabolism (Friso and van Wijk 2015).

Integration of these datasets through systems biology approaches enables the construction of comprehensive models linking gene expression, metabolism, and phenotypic outcomes. Such analyses facilitate the identification of regulatory networks, key molecular hubs, and candidate genes that coordinate sulfur uptake, assimilation, and downstream physiological responses.

Future Perspectives and Implications for Rice Improvement

Emerging Questions in Rice Sulfur Biology

Although significant progress has been made in understanding sulfur uptake, assimilation, and regulation in rice, several key questions remain. In particular, the coordination of sulfur transport, allocation, and assimilation across tissues and developmental stages remains poorly understood. Although individual components of sulfur metabolism have been characterized, their integration into a unified regulatory network is still incomplete. A major challenge is the functional redundancy and specialization of sulfate transporters and sulfur assimilation enzymes, which often exhibit overlapping expression patterns and complicate functional analyses.

Future research should move beyond single‐gene studies and adopt integrative approaches that combine functional genomics, genome editing, transcriptomics, metabolomics, and physiological phenotyping to resolve gene‐specific and context‐dependent functions. Addressing these knowledge gaps will be essential for identifying key regulatory nodes governing sulfur homeostasis and for understanding how sulfur metabolism is coordinated with growth, stress adaptation, and immunity. A systems‐level understanding of these processes will provide the foundation for future efforts aimed at improving sulfur use efficiency and adaptation to sulfur‐limited environments.

Expanding Functional Genomics Beyond Model Paradigms

Current understanding of sulfur metabolism is derived largely from studies in A. thaliana . Although these studies have provided valuable mechanistic insights, rice possesses distinct physiological characteristics, developmental programs, and ecological adaptations that necessitate species‐specific validation. Consequently, regulatory mechanisms established in Arabidopsis cannot always be assumed to function identically in rice. Recent advances in rice transformation and genome‐editing technologies have greatly expanded opportunities for high‐resolution functional studies directly in rice systems.

Technologies such as multiplex CRISPR/Cas9, promoter editing, and inducible expression systems provide powerful tools for dissecting sulfur‐regulatory pathways while minimizing pleiotropic effects. These approaches enable the functional characterization of sulfur‐related genes and regulatory networks under environmentally relevant conditions. Future studies should prioritize the validation of rice‐specific functions of sulfate transporters, sulfur assimilation enzymes, and miRNA‐regulated pathways to distinguish conserved mechanisms from species‐specific adaptations. Extending functional genomics research beyond model systems will therefore be essential for translating mechanistic knowledge into practical strategies for improving sulfur metabolism in rice.

Integrating microRNA Regulation Into Crop Improvement Strategies

MicroRNA‐mediated regulation represents a promising yet underexplored approach for crop improvement. Unlike conventional transgenic strategies that target individual genes, miRNA‐based approaches can simultaneously regulate multiple components of interconnected metabolic networks, enabling coordinated control of nutrient fluxes.

The miR395 regulatory module exemplifies how miRNAs control sulfur metabolism through the regulation of sulfur uptake, assimilation, and allocation. Manipulation of miR395 has been shown to influence growth, stress responses, and disease resistance in rice. Notably, miRNA‐based strategies offer opportunities for fine‐tuning metabolic regulation while minimizing the adverse effects often associated with constitutive overexpression or complete knockout of essential genes. Future approaches may include the editing of miRNA loci or miRNA‐binding sites in target genes to achieve context‐dependent regulation of sulfur metabolism. Combined with genome editing and conventional breeding approaches, miRNA‐guided regulation provides a promising framework for improving sulfur use efficiency and stress resilience in rice.

Sulfur Metabolism as a Lever for Stress Resilience and Immunity

Abiotic and biotic stresses are becoming increasingly severe under changing climatic conditions, making stress resilience a major objective in rice improvement. Sulfur metabolism plays a central role in linking nutrient status with redox homeostasis, detoxification, and immune responses. Functional genomics studies indicate that manipulation of sulfur metabolic pathways can enhance tolerance to salinity, oxidative stress, and pathogen infection. However, because sulfur metabolism also influences growth and resource allocation, improvements in stress tolerance must be balanced against potential fitness costs.

Future research should focus on identifying regulatory nodes that enhance resilience while minimizing growth penalties, particularly under field conditions. Priority targets for functional validation and multiplex genome editing may include combinations of OsSULTR1;1 and OsSULTR1;2 to improve sulfate acquisition, ATPS and APR to enhance sulfur assimilation, and the miR395‐ATPS regulatory module to optimize sulfur allocation and homeostasis. Integrating these approaches with physiological and agronomic evaluation will facilitate the development of rice cultivars with improved resilience and productivity under sulfur‐limited and stress‐prone environments.

Toward Systems‐Level Models of Sulfur Metabolism

Because sulfur metabolism is highly complex and interconnected, integrative systems‐level models that combine genetic, transcriptomic, metabolomic, and physiological data are needed. Predictive modeling of sulfur‐regulatory networks can generate testable hypotheses and identify critical control points within these networks. Advances in computational biology, multiomics integration, and high‐throughput phenotyping now enable the modeling of sulfur fluxes and regulatory interactions at the whole‐plant scale.

Such models provide powerful frameworks for predicting plant responses to environmental conditions and for identifying key regulatory components of sulfur homeostasis. By integrating diverse datasets, systems‐level approaches can improve our understanding of how sulfur metabolism interacts with growth, stress adaptation, and immunity. Ultimately, these models will facilitate the rational design and evaluation of strategies aimed at improving sulfur metabolism and plant performance under nutrient‐limited environments.

Translational Potential and Agricultural Relevance

Functional genomics provides valuable insights into sulfur utilization processes that can be translated into practical agricultural applications. Improving sulfur use efficiency has the potential to reduce fertilizer inputs, lower production costs, and minimize environmental impacts. In addition, enhanced sulfur metabolism can increase the accumulation of sulfur‐containing amino acids, thereby improving grain nutritional quality.

The successful development of sulfur‐efficient rice cultivars will require the integration of functional genomics, molecular breeding, and agronomic evaluation. Approaches such as marker‐assisted selection, genomic selection, and genome editing provide complementary tools for incorporating beneficial sulfur‐related traits into elite germplasm. Ultimately, these strategies can contribute to the development of rice varieties capable of maintaining productivity, grain quality, and stress resilience across diverse agroecological environments, including sulfur‐deficient soils.

Conclusions

Sulfur metabolism is a central component of rice physiology, influencing growth, nutrient utilization, redox homeostasis, stress adaptation, and immunity. Functional genomics studies have demonstrated that sulfur homeostasis is controlled by an interconnected regulatory network involving sulfate transporters, sulfur assimilation enzymes, microRNAs, and sulfur‐containing metabolites. Among these, the miR395 regulatory module has emerged as a key regulator linking sulfur uptake, assimilation, and allocation.

Recent advances in transgenic technologies, genome editing, and multiomics approaches have substantially improved our understanding of sulfur metabolism in rice and revealed its broader roles in stress signaling and plant defense. These findings highlight sulfur metabolism as a dynamic regulatory system rather than a simple nutrient‐assimilation pathway.

Despite considerable progress, important questions remain regarding functional redundancy, tissue‐specific regulation, and the integration of sulfur metabolism with other signaling networks. Addressing these challenges will accelerate the translation of functional genomics knowledge into strategies for improving sulfur use efficiency, stress resilience, and sustainable rice production under changing environmental conditions.

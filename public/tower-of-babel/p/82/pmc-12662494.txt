Effect of single acupoint and acupoints compatibility on PSQI in patients with primary insomnia: A randomized controlled clinical trial
Authors: Di Wu, Xiao-Na Liu, Xin Jia, Li-Juan Ha
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:To observe the different effect of single acupoint and acupoints compatibility on the Pittsburgh sleep quality index (PSQI) of primary insomnia patients.Methods:60 patients with primary insomnia were randomly divided into 2 groups: single acupoint group and acupoints compatibility group, with 30 cases in each group. The single acupoint group electroacupuncture the “Shenmen (HT 7)” acupoint, the acupoints compatibility group with the “Sancai” acupoint. To observe the different effect of single acupoint and acupoints compatibility on the PSQI of primary insomnia patients.Results:After treatment, the effective rate was 86.67% and 66.67% respectively, and the compatibility group was significantly better than the single acupoint group. In the PSQI total score, there were significant differences in before and after treatment and follow-up period between the compatibility group and the single acupoint group, and the compatibility group was superior to the single acupoint group.Conclusion:Single acupoint and the acupoints compatibility are both effective in treating primary insomnia, and the efficacy of acupoint compatibility is superior to that of single acupoint, and the long-term acupuncture effect is better too.

1. Introduction

Insomnia refers to the subjective experience of patients who are not satisfied with the time and/or quality of sleep and affects daytime social functioning.[1] Epidemiological investigation have shown that approximately 10% of adults have varying degrees of sleep disorder.[2] The incidence of insomnia in our country is 45.4%, 32% to 35% in the United States, 10% to 14% in the United Kingdom, 11.9% in Finland, and 21% in Japan.[3] Insomnia can also lead to cognitive decline,[4] reduced work efficiency and quality of life, and increased risk of death in severe cases.[5]

Western medicine frequently employs sedatives and sleep-inducing medications to manage insomnia. While these drugs demonstrate certain therapeutic benefits, prolonged use may lead to adverse effects, such as drowsiness, memory impairment, and drug dependence. Additionally, the condition tended to relapse after discontinuation of the medication. This has prompted a growing interest in alternative therapies.[6] Currently, external treatment modalities of traditional Chinese medicine, including acupuncture, moxibustion, massage, auricular point stimulation, and acupoint application, have shown favorable clinical outcomes for insomnia management. These methods are characterized by minimal adverse reactions and high patient acceptance, thus receiving increasing attention in clinical practice. Among these acupuncture has emerged as a critical intervention for treating primary insomnia.

Acupuncture and moxibustion therapy have the characteristics of many methods, simple operation, and nontoxic side effects, and the efficacy of acupuncture and moxibustion therapy is positive and safe. Previous studies have demonstrated that acupuncture has a significant therapeutic effect on insomnia.[7] Kim SungA et al[8] conducted a Cochrane bias experiment to compare acupuncture with pharmacological treatment, revealing that acupuncture significantly improves insomnia symptoms. Hu et al[9] applied “Zhishen” acupuncture to treat insomnia caused by Yin deficiency and internal heat hyperactivity, which effectively alleviated depressive symptoms and enhanced sleep quality. Wang et al[10] used the Yuanluobeishu acupoint combination method to treat 66 patients with intractable insomnia. The results indicated that the PSQI and TCM symptom scores of the patients decreased post-treatment compared to pretreatment, achieving a total effective rate of 95.5%. As of December 2012, there were 1207 basic research and clinical reports on acupuncture and moxibustion for insomnia in China and 680 abroad. A meta-analysis was performed on 25 randomized controlled clinical studies (2193 patients) that met the inclusion criteria.[11] The results showed that the clinical efficacy of the acupuncture group was better than that of the drug group, and the efficacy rate of acupuncture with Chinese herbal medicine treatment was also better than that of Chinese herbal medicine treatment alone.

Ancient doctors have long reached a consensus on the clinical efficacy of acupuncture and moxibustion in the treatment of insomnia and have proposed many acupuncture methods and acupoint compatibility methods. Examples include the Yuan Shu acupoint compatibility method,[12] the 8 confluence acupoint compatibility method,[13] and the Sancai acupoint compatibility method.[14] However, how can acupoints be selected for clinical treatment of insomnia? Is acupoint compatibility better than a single acupoint? However, these questions do not have scientific basis. In this study, primary insomnia was the research subject, and a randomized controlled research method was used to treat primary insomnia using acupuncture at a single acupoint (Shenmen, HT 7) and compatibility (Sancai compatibility). The Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI) was used as an evaluation tool to evaluate the clinical efficacy of the treatment of primary insomnia with single acupoints and different acupoint compatibilities.

2. Methods

2.1. Ethical approval

This study was approved by the Changchun University of Chinese Medicine Affiliated Hospital and registered with ClinicalTrials.gov (No. NCT02448602). All eligible participants fully understood the requirements of the clinical trial, signed an informed consent form, and volunteered to participate in the study.

2.2. Study design

This study was a randomized, controlled clinical trial. All patients recruited were from Changchun University of Chinese Medicine Affiliated Hospital and 973 Scientific Research Clinic, Jingkai Hospital affiliated with the Changchun University of Chinese Medicine. Sixty patients with primary insomnia who met the inclusion criteria were enrolled in the study. They were randomly divided into 2 groups: a single acupoint group (Shenmen, HT 7), and a compatibility group (Sancai compatibility), with 30 cases in each group. All eligible participants fully understood the requirements of the clinical trial, signed an informed consent form, and volunteered to participate in this study. 2.3. Sample size calculation

This was a randomized controlled trial. The treatment group consisted of the combination group (Sancai compatibility), while the control group comprised a single point group (Shenmen). The efficacy rate served as an evaluation index for therapeutic effectiveness. Based on a review of the relevant literature and the efficacy rates of similar treatments, the efficacy rate for the treatment group was estimated at 87.2%, whereas that for the control group was 60%. With a 2-sided significance level α = 0.05 and power 1 - β = 0.9, the sample size calculation formula used was N=2p¯q¯(Zα+Zβ)2/(p1−p2)2. The total sample size was 55 patients. Accounting for a 10% dropout rate, a minimum of 60 participants were included in this study. Consequently, the sample sizes for the 2 groups were as follows: treatment (n = 30) and control (n = 30).

2.4. The source of the research object

All patients recruited in the study were from Changchun University of Chinese Medicine Affiliated Hospital and 973 Scientific Research Clinic, Jingkai Hospital affiliated with Changchun University of Chinese Medicine. Sixty patients with primary insomnia who met the inclusion criteria were enrolled in this study. They were randomly divided into 2 groups, a single acupoint group (Shenmen, HT 7) and a compatibility group (Sancai compatibility), with 30 cases in each group.

2.6. Randomization

Simple randomization was performed using a random number table. Participants were allocated into 2 groups: a single point group (Shenmen) and a combination group (Sancai combination), with 30 participants in each group. Random numbers were generated using the IBM SPSS Statistics software (version 27.0). To ensure allocation concealment, random numbers and corresponding group assignments were placed in sequentially numbered, sealed, opaque envelopes. Upon participant enrollment, the attending physician retrieved the envelopes in numerical order to determine the participant’s random number. Based on random numbers, participants were assigned to their respective groups to receive different treatment protocols.

2.7. Blinding

This study used a single-blind design. Participants were unaware of their group assignments to prevent behavioral or psychological responses from influencing the results. Although the experimental operators had access to grouping information to ensure operational safety, the data evaluators remained blinded to the grouping details throughout the entire process.

2.8. Intervention modality

This study established 2 groups: the treatment group (combination group: Sancai combination) and the control group (single point group: Shenmen). Two groups of self-control, before and after self-treatment, and parallel control between groups, were adopted.

Five days for one course of treatment, a treatment interval of 2 days, and an intervention of 5 courses. Four weeks after the end of the treatment, a follow-up session was performed.

2.9. Outcome measurements

The PSQI was used as the evaluation index in this study.[17] The evaluation was conducted before treatment, after 5 courses of treatment (after treatment), and at the fourth week of follow-up after the end of treatment (follow-up period). The PSQI consists of 19 self-evaluation items and 5 other evaluation items. The 19th self-evaluation item and 5 other evaluation items were not included in the scoring. The 18 self-evaluation items comprised 7 sleep components: sleep quality, time to fall asleep, sleep time, sleep efficiency, sleep disorders, drug hypnosis and daytime function. Each component is scored on a 0–3 likert scale, and the cumulative score of each component is the PSQI total score, which ranges from 0 to 21. The higher the score, the worse is the sleep quality. 0–5 points sleep quality is very good, 6–10 points sleep quality is OK, 11–15 points sleep quality is average, 16–21 points sleep quality is very poor.

2.10. Statistical analysis

The collected experimental data were used to establish a database using Excel, and statistical analysis was performed using the SPSS19.0 software. The classification data of the count were tested using x2 and x2 values, and P-values were recorded. The numerical data were measured using the t-test and expressed as mean ± standard deviation (x¯±S), and T-values and P-values were recorded.

4. Discussion

Many medical records of insomnia have been published since ancient times. Huangdi Neijing pointed out that the occurrence of sleep is closely related to the movement of Yin and Yang in the human body.[18] Based on this, Professor Wang Fuchun further elaborated on the pathogenesis of insomnia that “Yin and Yang are not in harmony, Yang does not enter Yin,” put forward the “theory of three types of causes” of insomnia,[19] and established the “Zhenjing Anshen acupuncture method” to treat insomnia.[14] The acupoint selection of “Zhenjing Anshen acupuncture method” is the compatibility group of “Sancai compatibility” applied in this study, which is also called the acupoint selection based on essence-qi-spirit. The Baihui (GV 20) acupoint was located along the route of the governor vessel. The governor vessel is directly connected to the brain and the branches are connected to the kidneys and heart. The spirit of the heart and brain is the core of insomnia, and the filling of essential qi in the kidneys is an important factor in ensuring sleep. The Shenmen (HT 7) acupoint is effective in the treatment of insomnia; therefore, acupuncture at the Shenmen (HT 7) acupoint can unclog the meridians of the heart and nourish the heart for tranquillization. The Sanyinjiao(SP 6) acupoint can regulate the liver, spleen, and kidney simultaneously, nourishing Yin to suppress hyperactive Yang.

The PSQI was compiled by Buysse et al, a psychiatrist at the University of Pittsburgh, in 1989.[17] PSQI is used to assess the overall sleep quality of the subjects in the last 1 month, which is suitable for the evaluation of sleep quality in patients with sleep disorders and mental disorders, and is also suitable for the evaluation of sleep quality in general. Currently, it has become a common scale for the clinical evaluation of psychiatry at home and abroad and is practicable in clinical practice and research activities in psychiatry. Martin et al. ‘s study on the reliability and validity of the PSQI showed that the total PSQI score was significantly correlated with the severity of obstructive sleep apnea (P = .261, P < .05).[20] The results showed that the PSQI scale has a high degree of internal consistency, reproducibility, and effectiveness and can be used as a sleep quality screening tool. In 2012, the Sleep Disorders Group of the Neurology Branch of the Chinese Medical Association invited a number of domestic experts to develop the “Chinese Adult Insomnia diagnosis and Treatment Guidelines,” in which the Pittsburgh sleep quality Index is listed as a necessary assessment tool in sleep diagnosis.[21]

Acupoints are the reaction points of the diseases and the stimulation points of acupuncture treatment. The compatibility of acupoints is a method based on the theory of traditional Chinese medicine,[22] under the guidance of acupuncture acupoint selection principles, combining the clinical and the characteristics of acupoints, selecting 2 or more acupoints with the same function for compatibility, and giving play to the synergistic effect of acupoints to achieve a specific therapeutic effect and improve the clinical curative effect. Ancient doctors have reached a consensus that acupoint compatibility can improve the clinical effects of acupuncture and have proposed many compatibility methods. Modern acupoint compatibility research has yielded results, related literature at home and abroad, and a review of 874 articles. However, there is no scientific basis for whether the effect of acupoint compatibility is superior to that of a single acupoint alone. In this study, primary insomnia was used as the carrier to observe the differences in the effects of a single acupoint and acupoint compatibility on the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index. The study showed that based on the PSQI index, both the compatibility and single acupoint groups could improve the symptoms of primary insomnia. The compatibility group performed better than the single acupoint group, and the long-term post-acupuncture effect was better.

This study provides a scientific basis for the clinical application of acupoint compatibility in improving PSQI in patients with primary insomnia. However, it is essential to acknowledge the inherent limitations of this study. For instance, during treatment, variations in acupuncture depth and operational techniques may influence therapeutic efficacy and prognosis, leading to diverse outcomes. Additionally, factors such as the patient’s physiological and psychological conditions as well as comorbidities can also affect the treatment results. Currently, numerous studies on acupuncture for insomnia treatment exist; however, these studies often suffer from drawbacks including relatively small sample sizes, lack of uniformity in observation indicators, and inconsistent therapeutic effect standards. Moreover, most clinical studies are single-center, small-sample investigations, introducing a degree of selection bias. Therefore, future research should involve multi-center, large-sample, randomized controlled trials to further explore and refine the theory of acupoint compatibility. This approach aims to provide a robust theoretical foundation and research direction for the acupuncture treatment of primary insomnia.

5. Conclusion

Both single acupoints and acupoint compatibility are effective for the treatment of primary insomnia. The efficacy of acupoint compatibility was better than that of a single acupoint, and the long-term post-acupuncture effect was better.

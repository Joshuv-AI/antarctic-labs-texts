Screening of kinase inhibitors in the triple negative KRAS G13D-mutated MDA-MB-231 breast cancer cell line
Authors: Sandra Stickler, Marie-Therese Eggerstorfer, Maximilian Rieche, Sinem Arslan, Maryana Teufelsbauer, Martin Hohenegger, Lukas Weigl, Gerhard Hamilton
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTriple negative breast cancer (TNBC) is more chemoresistant and has a poorer prognosis than other breast cancer types. A subset of TNBCs carries KRAS mutations or amplifications. MDA-MB-231 cells, which have a KRAS G13D mutation and express HER2-103, were previously shown to be sensitive to the KRAS G12D inhibitor MRTX1133 and ERBB2-targeting drugs. A kinase inhibitor library of 157 compounds was screened against MDA-MB-231 cells. Effective compounds and ERBB2-directed drugs were tested for synergistic antiproliferative effects in combination with two KRAS inhibitors and for their impact on cell migration. The BCR-ABL/SRC inhibitors Bosutinib and Dasatinib showed strong activity, while the SRC inhibitor Saracatinib was less effective. MDA-MB-231 lacks BCR-ABL but expresses c-Abl, as shown by Western blot and comparison with K562 cells. The tricomplex RAS(ON) inhibitor RMC-7977 was more effective than MRTX1133. Combinations of KRAS inhibitors with Bosutinib, Dasatinib, Saracatinib (to a lesser extent), and ERBB2 inhibitors Neratinib and Mobocertinib enhanced antiproliferative effects. These findings highlight c-Abl as a relevant target in MDA-MB-231 TNBC cells. C-Abl and ERBB2 inhibitors can enhance the efficacy of KRAS-targeted therapies, offering potential combination strategies to overcome resistance in KRAS-positive TNBC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-43207-2.Keywords: Triple negative breast cancer, MRTX1133, RMC-7977, KRAS, c-AblSubject terms: Cancer, Drug discovery, Oncology

Introduction

A small subset of breast cancers, namely the approximately 10–15% of triple-negative breast cancers (TNBCs), is known to frequently harbor mutations of the KRAS (Kirsten rat sarcoma viral homolog) oncogene in conjunction with other drivers1,2. Metastatic progression in TNBC patients seems to be dependent on KRAS-supported invasion3. TNBC is traditionally defined by the absence of the estrogen (ER) and progesterone (PR) receptors and absent or low expression of the human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (ERBB2/HER2) and is characterized by its high metastatic potential and elevated risk of recurrence4,5. Conventional cytotoxic chemotherapy based on anthracyclines and taxanes is the traditional treatment option for TNBC6. Neoadjuvant chemotherapy achieves a pathological complete response (pCR) in approximately 30% of patients with TNBC7,8. In patients lacking a pCR, this chemotherapy spares a subpopulation of chemotherapy-resistant tumor cells leading to a poor outcome9. Alterations in TP53 were identified in 72/81 (89%) of TNBC cases and ERBB2 amplification was found in 7/81 (9%)10. Furthermore, loss of CDKN2A, CDKN2B and Ras/MAPK alterations could be detected with amplifications comprising PIK3CA (49%), KRAS (32%), BRAF (30%), and EGFR (23%)11. Recent advances have led to the identification of a HER2-low TNBC phenotype that responds to treatment with the antibody-drug conjugate (ADC) Trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd)12,13. Given the established role of KRAS mutations in these tumors, the newly identified HER2 subtype offers novel treatment strategies14. Despite MDA-MB 231 cells being characterized as HER2-negative, several research groups reported a low HER2 expression in this cell line15–17. This discrepancy seems to be linked to the alternative expression of HER2 by a unique circular HER2 RNA (circ-HER2) that encodes a novel HER2-103 protein, while regular HER2 mRNA and the corresponding protein can hardly be found18. Circ-HER2/HER2–103 was detected in approximately 30% TNBC clinical samples and these TNBC patients have a worse overall prognosis compared to circ-HER2/HER2–103 negative patients.

In KRAS-driven oncogenesis, mutations at the G12, G13 and Q61 genomic hotspots drive constitutive activation of this G-protein and the downstream signaling pathways19. Although KRAS G12C-directed agents that bind covalently to the cysteine residue, such as sotorasib and adagrasib, are clinically available, no corresponding approved treatment currently exists for the KRAS G12D mutation and other KRAS genotypes20,21. MRTX1133 has been described as selective KRAS G12D inhibitor and was recently tested in a phase 1/2 clinical trial (NCT05737706) in patients with advanced solid tumors22. Although MRTX1133 showed promising results in vitro and in vivo xenografts, as well as cytotoxic effects in the TNBC MDA-MB 231 carrying a KRAS G13D mutation, the clinical trial was recently terminated after phase 117,23–25. Thus, panKRAS inhibitors, PROTACS or novel multi-specific agents are needed to target the majority of KRAS aberrations.

Therefore, a novel approach to target all types of KRAS mutations is the development of RAS inhibitors like the tool compound RMC-7977, a reversible, tricomplex agent that binds non-covalently; first to the chaperone cyclophilin A, then to active RAS(ON) and blocks proliferation and tumor growth in vitro and in vivo26. This orally bioavailable inhibitor was derived from the cyclophilin-binding compound sanglifehrin A, creates a groove between KRAS and cyclophilin A (CYPA) along the nucleotides G12, G13 as well as Q61 and exhibits low nanomolar median effective inhibitory concentrations for mutations at these hotspots and wildtype KRAS26. RMC-6236 is the clinically tested compound and differs from RMC-7977 by two minor side moieties. Both compounds are pan-RAS inhibitors that tackle different KRAS genotypes as well as wildtype/amplified KRAS. Despite this general KRAS-directed activity of the two RMC compounds, normal tissues revealed only a transient suppression with low toxicity. Holderfield et al. observed that pERK suppression in KRAS G12D pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma cell lines rebounded 48 h after MRTX1133 treatment, whereas the RAS(ON)inhibitor RMC-7977 maintained the suppression permanently26. The investigational clinical compound of the RAS(ON)inhibitor, RMC-6236, is currently being investigated as monotherapy and in combination with other RAS(ON)inhibitors against standard of care treatments in several clinical trials for lung and pancreatic cancer as well as other solid tumors (NCT06128551, NCT06162221, NCT06445062, NCT05379985, NCT06625320). Importantly, RMC-7977 also shows effectiveness against cancer models resistant to current KRAS G12C inhibitors, suggesting its potential to reverse resistance. Notably, the drug induced apoptosis in cancer cells while sparing normal tissues, underscoring its tumor-selective activity. The research on RMC-7977 also uncovered a potential resistance mechanism involving c-MYC copy number gain, which could be mitigated by combinatorial Transcriptional enhancer factor TEF-1 (TEAD) inhibition, providing a promising strategy to enhance RAS inhibition efficacy27. However, the broad-spectrum nature of RMC-7977, while advantageous for targeting diverse RAS mutations, may raise on-target toxicities in normal tissues that rely on RAS signaling for homeostasis. Additionally, the emergence of resistance mechanisms, such as c-MYC copy number gain, may require combination therapies to optimize treatment strategies.

Given that HER2 and its downstream effector KRAS can drive tumor proliferation and metastasis, other forms of dysregulated cellular tyrosine kinases may reinforce their oncogenic potential28–32. The partial inhibition of MDA-MB-231 cells by MRTX1133 indicates the presence and activity of other participating receptor/nonreceptor tyrosine kinases (RTK/nRTK) and here we report a search using a compound library comprising 157 kinase inhibitors against this TNBC cell line17. Reports have documented that Src, a key nRTK and oncogenic switch, plays a pivotal role in HER2- and EGFR-driven signaling and further facilitates breast cancer progression by promoting cell migration and invasion33,34. Src is present in nearly all breast cancer tissues and exhibits elevated protein expression and activity compared to normal breast tissue35. Furthermore, the increased Src activity in breast cancer suggests a potential link to the KRAS mutation36.

The contrast between promising preclinical results and the early termination of the MRTX1133 clinical trial for KRAS G12D-positive solid tumors underscores the need for adjunct strategies. New ways to target KRAS, as well as targeting multiple nodes within this signaling cascade, such as KRAS, HER2 and Src, could provide a more effective strategy in overcoming resistance and improving treatment outcomes in HER2- and KRAS-driven tumors. Consequently, our study aims to identify kinase inhibitors from a kinase screening library that may serve as a synergistic addition to future treatment regimens and the assessment of MRTX1133 as well as RMC-7977 combinations with these compounds for improved therapy of resistant TNBC cancers.

Methods

Cell lines

The triple negative breast cancer cell line MDA-MB-231 (ATCC HTB-26) carrying a KRAS G13D mutation and K-562 (ATCC CCL-243), a chronic myelogenous leukemia cell line harboring a Philadelphia chromosome, were obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC, Manassas, VA, USA). Both cell lines were maintained in 75 cm² tissue culture flasks (658170, Greiner Bio-One GmbH, Kremsmuenster, Austria) using RPMI-1640 medium (R8758, Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (SFBS-AU, Eximus, Catus Biotech, Tutzing, Germany) and antibiotics (P4458, Sigma-Aldrich, Darmstadt, Germany). The cell lines were cultured at 37 °C with 5% CO₂ and regularly passaged by trypsinization in case of MDA-MB-231. Cell counting was performed using a LUNA automated cell counter (Biozym, Vienna, Austria).

Compounds

The compounds Bosutinib, Dasatinib, as well as AZD 0156, AZD 7762, BX-912, GSK1059615, GSK2334470, CAY10621, Nilotinib, NH125 and LY2606368 were part of a Kinase Screening Library (10505, Cayman Chemical, Ann Arbor, MI, USA) that consists of 157 inhibitors at 10 mM in 50 µl aliquots in DMSO. MRTX1133, RMC-7977 and BAY-293 were procured from TargetMol (T9303, T81263, T5418, Wellesley Hills, MA, USA) and Neratinib as well as Saracatinib from Sigma-Aldrich (AMBH324A4D8B, AABH9A959735). The compounds were used as a 10 mM stock solution in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO).

Cytotoxicity assays

Cells were plated at a density of 1 × 10⁴ in 100 µl of RPMI-1640 medium (supplemented with 10% FBS and antibiotics) into the wells of 96-well flat-bottom microtiter plates (92096, Techno Plastic Products AG, TPP, Trasadingen, Switzerland) to determine the half-maximal inhibitory concentration values (IC50-value) of the test compounds. Seven two-fold serial dilutions of each of the 157 kinase library screening compounds ranging from 20µM to 0.31µM were added in duplicate to evaluate the in vitro cytotoxicity against the KRASG13D mutated MDA-MB-231. MRTX1133, as well as RMC-7977 were added in triplicate at concentrations ranging from 10µM to 0.15µM to evaluate their single-agent cytotoxicity, as well as together with selected Kinase Screening Library compounds in triplicate for combination experiments. All experiments were performed at least in duplicate for initial screening, and in triplicate for compounds exhibiting IC50-values below 20 µM. After four days of incubation under standard tissue culture conditions, viable cells were measured at 450 nm using a modified 3-(4,5-dimethylthiazolyl-2)-2,5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide (MTT) assay (EZ4U, Biomedica, Vienna, Austria).

Drug combinations

Drug combinations were evaluated using the Chou-Talalay method with the Calcusyn 2.0 software (Biosoft, Acropolis Computers Ltd, Cambridge, UK) and the Loewe method was used to confirm synergistic interactions. Combination Index (CI) -values were interpreted according to values < 0.95 having a synergistic effect, values between 0.95 and 1.05 an additive effect, and values > 1.05 an antagonistic effect. Regarding the Chou-Talalay method the fraction affected (FA) values below 0.5 were excluded, as they represent less than 50% inhibition and are therefore considered biologically less relevant. A non-fixed ratio combination design was chosen for RMC-7977 due to differences in the individual activities of the compounds. While RMC-7977 exhibits potent activity at 2 µM, the combination compounds demonstrate efficacy primarily at 10 µM. A fixed-ratio approach would not have accurately captured the interaction dynamics across their respective effective concentration ranges. A fixed-ratio approach was employed for MRTX1133, as it exhibits activity at 10 µM, similar to the combination compounds.

Western Blots

To analyze the protein expression of c-Abl and BCR-ABL in K-562 and MDA-MB-231, cells were lysed in Radioimmunoprecipitation assay (RIPA) buffer consisting of 50 mM Tris–HCl/pH 8.0, 150 mM NaCl, 0.1% SDS, 1% NP-40, 10 mM glycerol phosphate, 1 mM aprotinin, 1 mM leupeptin, 1 mM Pefabloc (R0278, Sigma-Aldrich), 1 mM NaVO3, 5 mM NaF and subsequently subjected to protein isolation. Total protein concentration was determined using the bicinchoninic acid (BCA) assay (A55861, ThermoFisher, Waltham, MA, USA). 40 µg of protein per well were resolved by SDS-PAGE for cell lysates of MDA-MB-231 and K-562, run on 8% polyacrylamide gels. To indicate protein size, molecular weight markers (RPN755E, Amersham, Buckinghamshire, UK) and Spectra™ Multicolor Broad Range Protein Ladder (#2664, ThermoFisher) were used. To test for the effects of treatment with bosutinib and dasatinib on MDA-MB-231 cells, c-Abl was analyzed using additional western blots using the same technique.

Following separation, proteins were transferred (Trans-Blot Turbo Transfer System, BIO-RAD, Hercules, CA, USA) to nitrocellulose membranes (Catalog No. GE10600002, Amersham) and incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary monoclonal antibodies targeting both c-Abl and BCR-ABL (Rabbit #2862, Cell Signaling Technology, Dunvers, MA, USA). The membranes were incubated for 1 h at 25 °C with horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary antibodies (Anti-rabbit IgG, HRP-linked Antibody #7074, Cell Signaling Technology). Finally, specific detection was achieved using WesternBright Sirius chemoluminescence substrate (Catalog No. K-12043-D20, Advansta Inc., San Jose, CA, USA). Western Blots were further analyzed using the C-DiGit Blot Scanner (LI-COR, Biosciences GMBH, Bad Homburg, Germany) and the Image Studio Software v6.0 (LI-COR, Biosciences).

Scratch assays

For scratch assays to evaluate migration capacity, MDA-MB-231 cells were seeded in six-well plates (No. 92096, Techno Plastic Products AG, Trasadingen, Switzerland) and cultured in 3 mL of medium until they reached confluency. Two perpendicular scratches were then introduced using a plastic pipette tip to remove the cells. Cultures were subsequently treated with either DMSO (control) or the respective inhibitors and incubated under standard tissue culture conditions. Cells were treated with either 2.5 µM Bosutinib, 0.25 µM Dasatinib, 2.5 µM Saracatinib, or 0.5 µM RMC-7977. Light micrographs were taken daily (24 h) for three consecutive days (magnification 40×), and the scratch areas not yet repopulated by cells were quantified at multiple positions using ImageJ software (version 1.52s, National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD, USA) (Supplement 1).

Statistics

The Origin 9.1 software (OriginLab, Northampton, MA, USA) was used to obtain dose-response curves and IC50-values. Differences in clear areas of scratch assays were evaluated using a paired student’s t-test to determine differences between initial control scratch and different days of treatment. These were conducted under the assumption of normality using a Shapiro-Wilk test and visual plots. P < 0.05 was considered significant. Artwork was created using Microsoft PowerPoint for Microsoft 365 and Origin 9.1 software.

Results

In this study, 157 kinase inhibitors of a compound library were tested for their cytotoxic effects on the KRAS G13D-mutated MDA-MB-231 cell line. The Chk1/Chk2-targeting agents AZD7762 and LY2606368, as well as the PI3Kα inhibitor GSK1059615, demonstrated the highest cytotoxicity in MDA-MB-231 cells, with no IC₅₀-values reached due to high cytotoxic effects observed even at the lowest concentration tested of 0.31 µM. Additionally, the eEF-2 kinase inhibitor NH125 showed strong cytotoxic activity against MDA-MB-231 cells. However, these compounds were not used for further experiments due to their low specificity. Among the two c-Abl/Src inhibitors, Dasatinib exhibited the lowest IC₅₀-value, followed by Bosutinib. Saracatinib was selected for further analysis due to its selective inhibition of Src kinases without affecting c-Abl (Table 1; Fig. 1). The K-562 cell line, which harbors a Philadelphia chromosome with the BCR-ABL fusion, was used as a control. Both Bosutinib and Dasatinib exhibited strong cytotoxic effects in K-562 cells, with IC₅₀-values < 0.31 µM. In contrast to MDA-MB-231 cells, where Saracatinib failed to reach an IC₅₀-value due to low cytotoxicity, K-562 cells were sensitive to Saracatinib, showing an IC₅₀-value of 0.13 µM (Fig. 1, Supplement 1).

Western blot analysis of c-Abl

Western blot analysis employing a c-Abl antibody clearly detected the BCR-ABL fusion protein, characteristic of the Philadelphia chromosome, in K-562 cells, which was absent in MDA-MB-231 cells. However, c-Abl is expressed in MDA-MB-231 (Fig. 2).

C-Abl and BCR-ABL intensity in cell lines MDA-MB-231 and K-562 evaluated by Western Blots with protein amounts applied equally. Band intensities were quantified as percentages of the total signal per lane. In K-562 cells, Bcr-Abl and c-Abl contributed approximately 35% and 36% of the total signal, respectively, while the remaining bands corresponded to degradation products. In MDA-MB-231 cells, the c-Abl band was defined as 100%, and all additional signals were interpreted as unspecific cross-reactions.

KRAS inhibitors—Kinase inhibitor combinations

Based on their IC₅₀-values and selectivity, active kinase inhibitors and ERBB2-directed agents were combined with the KRAS G12D-targeting agent MRTX1133 and the multi-specific RAS(ON) inhibitor RMC-7977, for the determination of possible synergistic effects. Additionally, the IC₅₀-values of MRTX1133 and RMC-7977 were assessed as single agents to evaluate their in vitro cytotoxicity in MDA-MB-231. Combinations of MRTX1133 and RMC-7977 with BAY-293, Bosutinib, Dasatinib, Mobocertinib, Neratinib, and Saracatinib enabled the calculation of combination index (CI) values. MRTX1133 combinations were initiated at concentrations of 10 µM for both agents. For RMC-7977 combinations, RMC-7977 started at 2 µM, while the second agent started at 10 µM. The SOS1-targeting inhibitor BAY-293 demonstrated synergistic CI values at higher concentrations (10–2.5 µM) when combined with MRTX1133. Similarly, Mobocertinib and Saracatinib showed synergistic interactions in the range of 10–5 µM. Neratinib displayed additive effects at 1.25 µM, while Bosutinib showed antagonism at 5 µM, however both agents demonstrated synergy at all other concentrations. Among all tested compounds, Dasatinib was the only agent to exhibit consistent synergistic effects across all concentrations when combined with MRTX1133 (Figs. 3 and 4A). Conversely, all compounds used in combinations tested with RMC-7977 showed synergistic interactions, regardless of their concentrations (Figs. 3 and 4B).

Migration assays

Scratch assays were conducted to evaluate the migratory potential of MDA-MB-231 cells under treatment with Bosutinib, Dasatinib, Saracatinib, and RMC-7977. In untreated controls, the scratch area was completely closed by the second day, while Saracatinib treatment resulted in only a small remaining gap after day 1. In contrast, both Bosutinib and Dasatinib effectively maintained an open scratch area over two consecutive days. The open area one day after treatment with Dasatinib did not differ significantly from the control. RMC-7977 delayed scratch closure on the first day but failed to sustain this effect by the second day (Fig. 5, Supplement 2).

Discussion

Since TNBC lacks hormone receptors and high HER2 expression, hormonal therapies and HER2-targeted treatments (trastuzumab/Herceptin) are largely ineffective. This cancer is more aggressive than other types of breast cancer and has a higher risk of recurrence, especially within the first 3–5 years after treatment37. Treatment consists of surgery, chemotherapy (neoadjuvant and/or adjuvant), radiation therapy, immunotherapy using atezolizumab or pembrolizumab for metastatic disease38. Standard cytotoxic regimens comprise Doxorubicin and Cyclophosphamide, followed by Paclitaxel (AC-T), Docetaxel, Cyclophosphamide and Carboplatin plus Paclitaxel. Chemoresistance in TNBC is characterized by initial chemosensitivity, that later turns into resistance resulting in recurrences and metastasis. Accumulation of resistant Cancer Stem Cells (CSC) within TNBCs may contribute to poor clinical outcomes39.

MDA-MB-231 represents a unique KRAS G13D mutated TNBC line that additionally expresses low ERBB2/HER2 and allows for both, the investigation of drug resistance and the interaction of KRAS and ERBB2. BET domain protein-directed agents such as the JQ1 inhibitor and the PROTACs MZ1 and ARV-771 inhibit growth and migration of the KRAS G13D-mutated MDA-MB-231 cells40.

The limited efficacy of conventional chemotherapy treatments may be overcome by direct targeting of the tumor oncogenic drivers, in the case of the MDA-MB-231 cell line mutated KRAS (G13D) and ERBB2/HER2. Our lab has demonstrated that MRTX1133 significantly inhibited the proliferation of MDA-MB-231 cells17. Similarly, migration of MDA-MB-231, but not that of MDA-MB-436, was retarded by this inhibitor. Furthermore, the SOS1 inhibitors that retard the GTP-GDP loading of KRAS, namely BAY-293, BI-3406 and MRTX0902, exhibited high antiproliferative activity against MDA-MB-231 cells. MRTX1133 binds to KRAS G12D in both its active state and inactive state41. However, the KRAS G12D inhibitor MRTX1133 has limited activity against the MDA-MB-231 KRAS G13D cell line and a clinical trial of this inhibitor was terminated after phase I (NCT05737706). Therefore, in this investigation a library of 157 kinase inhibitors was screened against the MDA-MB-231 cell line to test for kinases capable of inhibiting the malignant proliferation. The most active compounds found include BCR-ABL and c-Abl inhibitors comprising Bosutinib, Dasatinib and Nilotinib, besides inhibitors targeting Check1/2, ATM, PI3K and several others. Of course, the MDA-MB-231 cell line lacks a BCR-ABL Philadelphia chromosome aberration, but exhibits c-Abl expression as shown by comparison with the BCR-ABL positive K-562 cell line. In further experiments, Bosutinib and Dasatinib were combined with MRTX1133 to check for possible synergistic activity. The lower activity of Saracatinib compared to these two inhibitors indicates a minor role of SRC kinases.

C-Abl kinases (Abelson murine leukemia viral oncogene homolog) represent a family of non-receptor tyrosine kinases (nRTKs) consisting of two paralogs, c-Abl and ABL2 (ABL-related gene, ARG)42. Their expression and/or activity is increased in solid tumors and the inactivation of c-Abl impairs metastasis42. The c-Abl tyrosine kinases regulate diverse cellular processes during development and normal homeostasis and are aberrantly activated during tumor progression42. C-Abl kinases are activated by growth factors, adhesion receptors, chemokines, oxidative stress and DNA damage43. In solid tumors, c-Abl kinases are upregulated by oncogenic drivers, such as RTKs and chemokine receptors. Constitutive activation of c-Abl upon fusion with the breakpoint cluster region (BCR) generates the BCR-ABL oncoprotein responsible for driving several forms of human leukemia44. The increased expression of the c-Abl kinases in pancreatic, renal, colorectal, breast and gastric tumors is due to Abl amplification or somatic mutations45,46.

Importantly, the available ATP-competitive c-Abl tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) target multiple tyrosine kinases in addition to c-Abl and ABL2. Imatinib (Gleevec) was the first TKI developed against BCR-ABL that binds the ATP-binding site of c-Abl and inhibits both BCR-ABL and c-Abl resulting in inhibition of leukemic cells47,48. Several second and third generation TKIs targeting BCR-ABL include Dasatinib, Nilotinib, Bosutinib, and Ponatinib49. Dasatinib and Nilotinib are FDA approved as first and second line therapy and Bosutinib, as well as Ponatinib are used as second line therapy for patients with BCR-ABL mutations50. The potent dual Src/Abl inhibitor Bosutinib exerts anti-tumor effects on neuroblastoma (NB)51. In TNBC cells, combination treatment with c-Abl inhibitors and EZH2 (enhancer of zeste homolog 2) inhibitors elicited a synergistic decrease in cell survival52.

In the present study, combination experiments using the SRC inhibitor Saracatinib and MRTX1133 demonstrated only modest synergistic effects in MDA-MB-231 cells. Dasatinib exhibited a low synergy, whereas the strongest synergistic effect was observed with Bosutinib. Additionally, the SOS1 inhibitor BAY-293 acted as a modulator that enhanced the activity of MRTX1133 as published previously53.

Although the MRTX1133 G12D inhibitor exhibited significant effects against MDA-MB-231 cells, novel KRAS-directed agents with higher activity and broad reactivity to divers KRAS mutations are required. A new class of inhibitors exploit the steric blockade of diverse mutated KRAS proteins by formation of a tricomplex with cyclophilin A (CYPA) and sangliferin54. The tool compound RMC-7977 and the corresponding clinical compound RMC-6236 inhibit signaling of KRAS G12X and to a lesser degree KRAS G13X oncogenes.

The RAS inhibitor RMC-7977 was previously believed to solely block downstream signaling activation; however, it has since been shown to also possess GTPase-activating (GAP) activity, promoting the cycling of RAS to its inactive, GDP-bound state55,56. Cuevas-Navarro et al. reported that RMC-7977 acts also as a GAP, stimulating the hydrolysis of RAS bound GTP57. The tricomplex RAS multi-selective on-state inhibitors are active against most KRAS mutants, wild-type KRAS and both wild-type and mutant HRAS and NRAS proteins55. The CYPA-RMC-7977 binary complex maximally stimulated hydrolysis on G12X mutants and to an intermediate level on G13X mutants. RMC-7977 exhibits high inhibitory activity against MDA-MB-231 that could be increased in combination with c-Abl inhibitors. In contrast to MRTX1133, this KRAS inhibitor exhibited synergy with a SOS1 inhibitor, c-Abl inhibitors and ERBB2-directed agents. The anticancer activity of the ERBB2-directed inhibitors Mobocertinib and Neratinib was published before by our group and experiments were included here just to compare their efficacy in comparison to RMC-7977 and MRTX1133 c-ABL inhibitor combinations58.

Here, the effects of the KRAS-directed drugs and inhibitors were studied for their effects on MDA-MB-231 cell migration in a scratch assay. This cell line shows a high mobility that is inhibited by concentrations corresponding to the IC50-values of Bosutinib, Dasatinib and highest concentrations of Saracatinib. In contrast to previous findings for MRTX1133, the inhibitory effect of RMC-7977 on cell migration seems lost after two days of the scratch test most likely related to the inhibitory effect of this compound that is restricted to approximately 40% of controls40. These findings are important in respect to the rapid dissemination of TNBC cells in patients.

Bosutinib is clinically well established for the treatment of chronic myeloid leukemia59. Synergy of Bosutinib with KRAS G12C inhibitors was reported, and this drug was found to prevent development of resistance60.

Chemotherapy remains the standard of care for TNBC since many targeted therapies have been tested without significant survival benefits61. The low-level expression of HER2/ERBB2 in TNBC offers a new chance for antiproliferative approaches. However, targeting EGFR by TKIs or EGFR-antibodies revealed disappointing results in TNBC62. While the KRAS G13D mutation and its sensitivity to the G12D inhibitor and the multi-specific RMC compound proof the role of KRAS as oncogenic driver, the low HER2 expression of MDA-MB-231 suggests that additional receptor-mediated signaling is involved. In case of an active mutated KRAS, cells can bypass HER2-triggered growth but blockade of KRAS signaling will restore the cells in a HER2 sensitive state. Combination of MRTX1133 and the more potent RMC-7977 to inhibit signaling by KRAS and HER2 inhibitors enables a dual suppression of proliferation and migration of these oncogenic drivers. This study corroborated the expression of c-Abl as oncogenic driver in this unique KRAS G13D-driven cell line and identified c-Abl-directed inhibitors among compounds of a kinase inhibitor library, including Dasatinib, Bosutinib and related compounds already in clinical use or trial studies. Therefore, the novel RMC KRAS-directed drugs, HER2 inhibitors and c-Abl inhibitors may be used to improve the prognosis of the difficult-to-treat TNBC.

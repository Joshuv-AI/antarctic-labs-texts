Time aware cyclic queuing forwarding traffic scheduling algorithm considering reallocation and priority sorting (RPS-TACQF)
Authors: Yuanxun Shen, Xinjie Chen, Xuelian Ma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo meet the mixed traffic scheduling requirements of time-triggered (TT) streams and audio video bridging (AVB) streams in the industrial Internet, this paper proposes a time aware cyclic queuing forwarding traffic scheduling algorithm considering reallocation and priority sorting (RPS-TACQF) method. Combining time aware shaper (TAS) and cyclic queuing and forwarding (CQF) mechanisms, it dynamically adjusts the queue resource allocation and traffic forwarding order, and uses the dead time of CQF odd and even queue switching to schedule TAS, optimize the frame injection time slot and routing, while meeting their respective end-to-end delay and jitter requirements. The experimental results show that the RPS-TACQF algorithm significantly improves the scheduling success rate in scenarios with 50 TT data streams and 100–1000 AVB data streams, especially under high load conditions, where the scheduling success rate of AVB data streams is about 36.5% higher than other algorithms; The bandwidth utilization rate increased by about 36.5% under high load, and the algorithm execution time still saved about 31.4% compared to the TAMCQF-ILP solver algorithm under high load networks.Keywords: Industrial Internet, Mixed traffic scheduling, Time aware shaper, Circular queuing forwarding, Priority scheduling, Scheduling success rateSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Industrial Internet connects and communicates industrial equipment, production systems, sensors and other physical devices through Internet technology, and uses sensors, embedded systems, cloud computing platforms and data analysis technology1. Data upload and control command issuance require millisecond level latency and microsecond level jitter. Industrial Internet needs to simultaneously carry multiple traffic such as time-triggered streams and audio video bridging streams. For communication service quality requirements, TT data streams require strict latency determinism, while AVB data streams have relatively relaxed requirements. Hybrid scheduling requires ensuring that different data flows do not interfere with each other in a shared network, while meeting their respective end-to-end latency and jitter requirements. Traditional Ethernet transmission modes cannot meet these requirements.

Time sensitive networking (TSN)2 is a set of data link layer protocols developed by the IEEE 802.1 task force. It consists of a set of protocol clusters, including AS time synchronization, Qbv traffic scheduling, Qbu frame preemption, Qci per data stream filtering and supervision, and Qcc TSN configuration. It solves the three key problems of time synchronization, traffic scheduling, and system configuration. Qbv traffic scheduling is the core mechanism of TSN standard, which can meet the service quality requirements such as end-to-end latency and jitter of business traffic through technologies such as time synchronization, traffic shaping, and priority control. The TSN traffic scheduling mechanism is mainly implemented by traffic shapers such as the Time Awareness Shaper proposed by Qbv and the Circular Queuing and Forwarding proposed by Qch3. TAS achieves microsecond level traffic isolation through gate control lists (GCL), while CQF ensures fixed latency transmission through odd even cyclic queues. At present, the TAS gating list configuration for time sensitive networks is very complex, and CQF is difficult to solve the problem of mixed traffic scheduling. To address the above issues, researchers have proposed some new CQF extension schemes, such as Cycle Specific Queuing and Forwarding (CSQF) and Multiple CQF (Multi CQF)4,5. Among them, CSQF introduced a third queue on the basis of the original two queues in CQF to receive data frames that did not arrive on time. Multi CQF uses multiple CQF queues for scheduling, with each CQF queue using a different cycle period. Although CSQF and Multi CQF can achieve mixed scheduling of AVB data streams with multiple cycles, they are difficult to solve the problem of mixed scheduling of TT data streams and AVB data streams. At present, people mostly use the combination of TAS and CQF to achieve mixed traffic scheduling of different types of traffic. However, TT data flow will affect the scheduling of AVB data flow, and the centralized scheduling of high priority TT data flow will significantly reduce the scheduling success rate of AVB data flow. In addition, the scheduling algorithm under this model does not incorporate routing for scheduling, and as network load increases, the success rate of scheduling significantly decreases6.

Although the existing research has made some progress in solving the mixed traffic scheduling problem of TT and AVB in the industrial Internet, there are still some shortcomings, such as the complex configuration of TAS gating list, the difficulty of CQF in solving the mixed traffic scheduling problem, and the limitations of the model combining TAS and CQF in terms of scheduling success rate, load balancing and routing. In this regard, this paper proposes a time aware circular queueing and forwarding traffic scheduling (RPS-TACQF) method that considers reassignment and prioritization. Research shows that the proposed RPS-TACQF algorithm performs well in terms of scheduling success rate, bandwidth utilization and algorithm execution time, and provides an efficient and reliable traffic scheduling solution for the industrial Internet. In addition, with the development of Software Defined Networking (SDN) technology, its application in cloud computing and Fog IoT networks is becoming increasingly widespread, providing new ideas for task scheduling and load balancing. The centralized control and flexible scheduling capabilities of SDN make it possible to achieve more efficient task allocation and resource utilization at the network level7. Especially when dealing with complex and changeable industrial Internet traffic, combining SDN’s task scheduling and load balancing methods, such as the combination strategy based on the Eagle Optimization Algorithm (AO) and the Whale Optimization Algorithm (WOA)8, is expected to further improve network performance and quality of service. Although this article does not directly explore the specific implementation in SDN environment, the idea of RPS-TACQF algorithm proposed can provide reference and inspiration for research in related fields.

Related work

Ethernet has gone through three stages of development: traditional Ethernet, real-time Ethernet, and deterministic networks9. Among them, traditional Ethernet adopts a shared medium and best effort communication mechanism, which has problems such as network congestion and packet collision; Although real-time Ethernet can perform specified tasks well in specific fields, due to the different technical routes for solving real-time problems, they form automated islands between each other. In addition, standard Ethernet and real-time Ethernet of various automation systems cannot be directly connected, which poses interconnection and interoperability issues, affecting the industrial expansion of the solution. With the expansion of network technology and application scenarios, various TSN based network technologies that provide deterministic guarantees have emerged, such as Deterministic Networking (Deterministic Net)10, Deterministic IP Network (DIP)11, 5G time sensitive network12, WiFi time sensitive network13, etc. Drawing on the architecture, capabilities, and mechanisms of TSN, deterministic network technology focuses on integrating with different network types to adapt to a wider range of business scenarios. Between 2015 and 2021, key standards and drafts addressed critical issues in real-time communication and data transmission. Since 2017, in order to better meet the real-time communication and data transmission needs of different industries, TSNTG has developed a series of standards and drafts, focusing more on the definition and research of TSN in industrial applications. In 2019, TSNTG launched a technological update in the automotive field. These published and ongoing standards have formed a comprehensive standard system, covering various aspects such as TSN architecture, protocols, testing, and applications14,15.

The hybrid TAS/CQF scheduling and joint routing scheduling of TSN have been widely studied, such as CSQF/Multi CQF and other TAS assisted CQF designs. Among them, (1) CSQF and Multi CQF: CSQF introduces a third queue on the basis of the original two CQF queues to receive data frames that have not arrived on time, thereby alleviating the mixed scheduling problem to a certain extent. However, CSQF still struggles to effectively handle mixed scheduling of TT and AVB streams. Multi CQF uses multiple CQF queues, each with a different period. Although it achieves multi period mixed scheduling of AVB flows, it lacks strict delay deterministic support for TT flows. (2) TAS assisted CQF design: Some studies attempt to combine TAS and CQF mechanisms to achieve strict delay control of TT flow through TAS, while utilizing CQF to ensure relaxed delay requirements of AVB flow. However, these designs often face the problem that TT flows affect AVB flow scheduling, especially under high load conditions, centralized scheduling of TT flows will significantly reduce the scheduling success rate of AVB flows. Compared with the above design, RPS-TACQF not only utilizes the advantages of TAS and CQF, but also significantly improves the transmission efficiency of time sensitive data through dead time utilization strategy. In addition, under the proposed GCL/guard-band configuration and assumptions, RPS-TACQF achieves interference free coexistence of TT flow and AVB flow by dynamically adjusting queue resource allocation and traffic forwarding order, significantly improving scheduling success rate and bandwidth utilization.

In addition, compared with adaptive TSN scheduling methods (such as the recently proposed dynamic priority allocation based on reinforcement learning18 or SDN controlled flow scheduling19), adaptive TSN can cope with dynamic traffic, but it relies on offline training or centralized control, which is difficult to meet the millisecond delay requirements of the industrial Internet. RPS-TACQF achieves dynamic resource allocation without the need for global model training through online traffic recognition and dead time reuse. Its lightweight design is more suitable for resource constrained industrial equipment.

The RPS-TACQF algorithm mentioned in this article is based on a system architecture that combines centralized controllers with distributed switches. Among them, the centralized controller is responsible for monitoring the global network status, path calculation, time slot allocation, and GCL configuration, which are distributed to various switches as offline scheduling solutions. The switch dynamically adjusts queue resources and traffic forwarding order based on the received scheduling scheme using online data plane mechanisms (including matching action tables, ACL updates, and packet loops).

(1) Assumption: (1) All network nodes (including switches and terminal hosts) have achieved global clock synchronization through the IEEE 802.1AS protocol. (2) The centralized controller has sufficient computing power to handle the global network state and generate optimized scheduling schemes. (3) All switches support time aware shaper and cyclic queue forwarding mechanisms, and have the ability to dynamically update matching action tables and ACLs. (4) The traffic characteristics of all TT and AVB flows, such as period, maximum end-to-end delay, packet size, etc., are known before scheduling and provided as inputs to the centralized controller. (2) Output: The centralized controller generates and distributes scheduling information for each flow, including the complete path from source to destination, frame injection time slots for each switch port, and gate control list configuration for each port. (3) Perform the following operations online: (1) Update the matching action table to allocate queue resources. (2) Update ACL to identify latency sensitive traffic and throughput sensitive traffic. (3) Utilize packet looping mechanism to ensure correct queuing of data packets.

In the CQF model, the CQF odd even queue switches alternately. Figure 4a shows the dead time diagram of traditional CQF queue switching. As shown in Fig. 4a, consider the scenario where switch sends data frames to switch in the network. When a CQF odd even cycle ends and switches from an odd period queue to an even period queue, it will enable the reception of dual queue data frames and disable the reception of odd queue data frames. Dead time mainly includes transmission delay and propagation delay. By increasing the CQF cycle duration and reducing the switching frequency of odd even queues, the proportion of dead time can be reduced. However, the latency of the data stream under the CQF shaper is directly proportional to the duration of the CQF cycle, and excessively long CQF cycles will cause the data stream to time out. Therefore, in most CQF cycles, the dead time will occupy 5% to 10% of the time.

At the end of each CQF cycle, we precisely control the opening and closing time of the TAS gate to ensure that only TT flow is allowed to pass through during dead time, while AVB flow is blocked. This algorithm does not rely on preemption mechanism, but avoids conflicts through the following design: (1) strict temporal isolation. TT frames are only transmitted during the dead time interval, while AVB frames are only transmitted during the AVB transmission phase, with no overlapping timing between the two; (2) Queue priority isolation: TAS queue has a higher priority than CQF queue (/), even if TT frames and AVB frames arrive at the switch at the same time, TT frames are still processed first; (3) Protection band absorbs uncertainty: The duration of the protection band covers fluctuations in link transmission delay, ensuring that residual TT frames do not interfere with the AVB queue under the proposed GCL/guard band configuration and assumption.

RPS algorithm model

Algorithm framework

To detect the types and characteristics of different traffic, this article extracts key packet metadata from the data plane of the switch, such as timestamps and queue depths of packets at different processing stages. This article also utilizes stateful data processing capabilities to enable switches to track network flows and set response modes for specific events. Based on these characteristics, this article designs a TRPS traffic scheduling algorithm that considers traffic reallocation and priority sorting, as shown in Fig. 5. The traffic recognition module mainly distinguishes the size of traffic and groups flows based on their sensitivity to latency; The reallocation module is responsible for dynamically reallocating queue resources between different categories of traffic when necessary; The priority scheduling module ensures that latency sensitive traffic can bypass high latency queues, thereby reducing waiting time and improving QoS.

Structure of RPS traffic scheduling algorithm.

The combination strategy of RPS and TSN categories (TT, AVB-A/B) and TAS/CQF scheduling decisions is explained as follows: (1) metadata/features. Time sensitive identification identifies TT and AVB traffic through priority fields (such as PCP fields) in the packet header. TT traffic is usually marked as high priority, while AVB traffic is classified into Class A and Class B based on its latency requirements, marked as medium priority and low priority, respectively. (2) Queue reallocation mechanism. Based on the identified traffic types, the RPS algorithm dynamically adjusts queue resource allocation and assigns TT traffic to the highest priority queue to ensure its low latency and zero jitter requirements. AVB-A and AVB-B traffic are allocated to medium priority and low priority queues respectively to balance latency and bandwidth utilization. (3) Compatibility with TSN scheduling framework. The RPS algorithm is closely integrated with TAS and CQF mechanisms. During the odd even queue switching period of CQF, TAS scheduling uses dead time to ensure that TT traffic is not affected by AVB traffic, achieving interference free coexistence of TT and AVB traffic under the proposed GCL/guard-band configuration and assumptions. (4) Programmable exchange hypothesis. Assuming that the switch supports a certain degree of programmability, similar to the packet recycling mechanism in P4 language, it enables the switch to perform complex traffic identification and queue reallocation operations on the data plane, and verifies the performance of the algorithm using an NS-3 simulator that supports programmability.

Reassignment of modules

(1) Flow group update. The calculation of parameters required for most data flow classification can only be completed in the exit block. However, in order to ensure the correct queuing of data packets, it is necessary to know the type of traffic in advance in the entrance block. To this end, this article adopts the recursive function supported by the switch, which allows data packets to be redirected back to the entrance of the switch after being processed by the switch exit. This behavior triggered an update to the access control list (ACL) to allocate different types of traffic to the corresponding queues.

(2) Queue reallocation algorithm. This article utilizes the polling (RR) scheduling mechanism of switch standards to design a queue reallocation algorithm (as shown in Fig. 6) to ensure that different types of traffic can be allocated to different queues, thereby enabling delay sensitive data traffic to bypass long queue grouping that needs to be queued.

Queue reallocation algorithm.

(3) Traffic reallocation scheduling. Using the Matching Action Table (M-A), different types of traffic are directed and allocated to corresponding queues. A packet defined as a certain type will be looped back to the entrance, updating the ACL to indicate that the flow it belongs to is latency sensitive. The packets processed after belonging to that flow will be reassigned to their respective queues through the M-A table. The new stream labeled as throughput sensitive will also be assigned to another queue.

This mechanism implements packet loop back and forth through recursive functions to dynamically update ACLs and reallocate traffic, but additional computational overhead should be noted: (1) bandwidth usage. Packet cycling requires additional bandwidth at the entrance/exit ports. If a single packet is cycled N times, its total bandwidth consumption will increase by N times (original path bandwidth requirement × N). (2) Processing delay. The loop mechanism will cause a specific packet to go through two complete inbound/outbound processing flows, resulting in an end-to-end delay that nearly doubles (ignoring queue waiting time).

For the impact of TSN deterministic delay boundary, the following factors need to be considered: (1) utilization of dead time. The RPS-TACQF algorithm schedules TT traffic through the dead time (about 12 µs) of CQF parity queue switching, releasing the main time slot resources for AVB traffic to use, which can partially offset the cyclic overhead. (2) Priority isolation. TT traffic is always transmitted through the highest priority queue, and its delay certainty is strictly guaranteed by TAS gating, which is not affected by the loop mechanism. To further reduce expenses, future work may consider: (1) hardware acceleration. In programmable switches that support P4, ACL can be directly updated through Register Arrays without looping packets, reducing latency to a single inbound processing level. (2) Incremental update. Trigger ACL updates only for packets with significant changes in traffic characteristics to reduce invalid loops.

It should be noted that regarding the impact of the “packet recycling” mechanism on the internal latency of switch hardware, additional latency can be controlled through the following measures: (1) hardware acceleration optimization. In switches that support P4 programmability, ACL updates can be directly implemented through Register Arrays without the need for actual packet loops; (2) Incremental update strategy. Trigger ACL updates only for packets with significant changes in traffic characteristics to reduce the number of invalid loops; (3) Delay boundary guarantee. Absorb the uncertainty of link transmission and propagation delay through the Guard Band mechanism, ensuring that TT stream transmission is not affected by cyclic mechanism interference. Under reasonable design, significant hardware delay will not be introduced, and its microsecond level overhead can be fully absorbed by the deterministic delay control framework of TSN (such as TAS gating), meeting the strict requirements of industrial Internet for jitter.

Priority sorting module

Nowadays, ordinary hardware also supports priority classification and multiple queues. Therefore, this article designs three different priority queues for each output port to store and forward data packets: one for latency sensitive traffic, one for throughput sensitive traffic, and one for general traffic (i.e. traffic without any labels). The time interval for rate estimation is generally set within several round-trip time delays (RTT) to respond promptly to changes in flow rate. Throughput sensitive traffic with high speed and long existence time will be delayed or discarded, forcing it to reduce its sending rate. In long-term operation, various types of traffic tend to share bandwidth resources fairly. Different dynamic queue sizes can be set for different types of streams, and multiple decreasing alpha parameters can be set for each port queue to control the buffer ratio available for each port queue. The algorithm maps data packets to different parameter groups according to their respective streams, where delay sensitive traffic is mapped to queues with smaller alpha values, and throughput sensitive traffic is mapped to queues with larger alpha values. Throughput sensitive traffic can obtain a larger buffer limit, while latency sensitive traffic, due to its small data volume, can adopt a smaller buffer limit and ensure priority forwarding. The overall algorithm process is shown in Fig. 7.

RPS-TACQF algorithm process.

During the entire traffic scheduling process, if a certain packet has a sequence number of N, when it enters the high priority queue from the normal queue, the packets in the normal queue are not completely cleared, which will cause the packets in the high priority queue to be forwarded first, such as packets N + 1 and N + 2, while packet N is after packets N + 1 and N + 2, resulting in out of order. To solve this problem, the receiver usually designs a buffer to store out of order packets until all packets that arrive before them have arrived and been processed. When a packet arrives at the receiver, first check if its sequence number matches the expected next sequence number. If they match, process the data packet directly; Otherwise, store the data packet in the buffer. When the sequence number of a packet in the buffer matches the expected next sequence number, retrieve the packet from the buffer and process it, while updating the expected next sequence number.

This algorithm temporarily stores out of order data packets in the receiving buffer, but it is necessary to strictly ensure that the sum of buffer delay and network transmission delay does not exceed the delay constraint. Experimental tests have shown that in a CEV network topology (e.g., 14 switches, 31 terminals), the 99% percentile out of order delay is 8.5us. This value is determined by the following factors: (1) the dead time of CQF parity queue switching (approximately 12 µs) limits the out of order range caused by TT traffic bursts. (2) The strict gate control of high priority queues ensures that TT traffic does not block AVB traffic for a long time, thereby controlling buffer time. For extreme scenarios, the following safeguard measures can be taken to reduce the risk of out of order: (1) Adjust the buffer size according to real-time traffic load to avoid additional delay caused by buffer overflow (the buffer occupancy rate in the experiment was always < 65%). (2) By using TAS gating to precisely synchronize frame injection time slots, the burst interference of TT traffic on AVB queues is reduced (experiments have shown that the standard deviation of TT traffic burst intervals is reduced by 42%).

Collaborative mechanism of deterministic scheduling between RPS and TSN

(1) Global local collaborative architecture. RPS adopts a dual layer architecture of “centralized global planning+distributed local optimization”: (1) centralized controller. Generate a deterministic scheduling scheme based on the whole network topology and traffic characteristics, including strict slot allocation of TT flows, routing paths of AVB flows and CQF cycle parameters, to ensure that end-to-end delay and jitter meet the requirements of the industrial Internet (such as TT flow delay ≤ cycle, AVB flow delay ≤ 1.5 × cycle); (2) Distributed switch. Implement RPS dynamic adjustment mechanism, including traffic identification, queue reallocation, and priority scheduling, but all operations are constrained by centralized GCL (Gate Control List), without modifying global path and time slot allocation, and only optimizing resource utilization within a local scope. Therefore, the dynamism of RPS is only reflected in the local optimization of queue resource allocation and priority sorting, without disrupting global deterministic planning, and therefore will not affect the strict delay guarantee of TT flow.

(2) Deterministic guarantee mechanism. RPS ensures that local optimization does not compromise global determinism through the following design: (1) Time slot alignment constraints. The queue reallocation and priority scheduling of RPS are strictly executed within the centrally allocated time slots. That is, TT flows must be transmitted within the dead time interval specified by GCL, and RPS only adjusts the priority of AVB flows in the CQF queue to avoid occupying TT flow time slots; (2) Priority isolation. TT flows are always mapped to the highest priority queue (TAS queue), while AVB flows are assigned priority to medium/low priority AVB flows based on delay sensitivity, without affecting the priority transmission rights of TT flows; (3) The protective tape absorbs uncertainty. RPS uses protective tape to isolate the queue switching boundary between TT flow and AVB flow, ensuring that even if local queue adjustments cause brief delay fluctuations, they will not affect the end-to-end certainty of TT flow.

(3) Algorithm execution time: The time it takes for the algorithm to run and obtain scheduling results.

Analysis of TT data stream sorting experiment

In the TT data traffic scheduling algorithm of joint routing, TT data flows are scheduled one by one, and different scheduling orders of TT data traffic will have an impact on the scheduling results. Therefore, this article compared the maximum TAS queue opening time in the network after scheduling with three sorting algorithms. The three sorting algorithms are as follows. S1: Sort by cardinality based on the source and destination stations of the data stream. S2: Stream sorting algorithm in JRS-LB. This algorithm takes into account the maximum end-to-end latency, period, and packet size, and calculates weights based on these parameters for ranking. S3: Randomly select data streams.

In this experiment, the packet length of TT data stream was randomly generated within the range of 50-200B, and the period was randomly generated within the range of 500 µs, 625 µs, 750 µs, 875 µs, and 1000us. The CQF cycle period TCQF was set to 125 µs. The average results of 10 experiments are shown in Fig. 9.

Maximum TAS queue opening time.

In the sorting algorithm S1, data streams with the same source and destination stations are scheduled adjacent to each other, which makes it easier for data streams to choose routes with lower load but longer paths. Therefore, the TTAs under the S1 algorithm are maximized. S2 preferentially schedules data streams with lower load, which will make it easier to choose routes with low load but long path for data streams with higher load after scheduling. Under the routing algorithm in this article, the sorting algorithm S2 will also increase the maximum TAS queue opening time. S3 randomly selects data streams for scheduling, avoiding consecutive scheduling of similar data streams. Therefore, the TT data traffic scheduling algorithm for joint routing in this article is scheduled through S3. In addition, it should be noted that the core reason for S3’s excellent performance in the experiment is that it avoids path selection bias caused by structured sorting in S1 and S2 through randomness. However, in scenarios with strict deterministic requirements such as the industrial Internet, there may be limitations in relying solely on random sorting. Subsequent work will design a hybrid sorting algorithm and compare its performance with pure random sorting in complex topologies through simulation, with a focus on verifying its effectiveness in improving end-to-end delay stability of TT flows.

Conclusion

This paper proposes a time aware cyclic queuing and forwarding traffic scheduling (RPS-TACQF) method, which considers the reallocation and prioritization of mixed traffic scheduling requirements of time triggered (TT) streams and audio video bridging (AVB) streams in the industrial Internet. The experimental results show that RPS-TACQF algorithm is effective and superior in mixed traffic scheduling, and provides an efficient and reliable traffic scheduling solution for industrial Internet. Although the RPS-TACQF algorithm proposed in this article performs well in terms of scheduling success rate, bandwidth utilization, and algorithm execution time, there are still some issues that need to be addressed: (1) The current algorithm mainly relies on fixed priorities and rules when dynamically adjusting queue resource allocation and traffic forwarding order. In the future, we can explore more intelligent and adaptive dynamic adjustment mechanisms. (1) The algorithm in this article mainly focuses on optimizing single or several indicators, such as scheduling success rate, bandwidth utilization, and algorithm execution time. In the future, multi-objective optimization algorithms can be studied to comprehensively consider the optimal balance of multiple performance indicators. (3) This experiment is mainly conducted in small and medium-sized network topologies, and can be extended to larger and more complex network scenarios in the future. (4) Although the simulation results in this article show some improvements, the next step of research should include testing using real industrial traffic trajectories or standard benchmarks (such as TSN Industrial Overview) to enhance generality.

Regarding scalability in large data centers or cross regional industrial environments, although the current experiment is based on a medium-sized network topology (14 switches, 31 terminals), its design has reserved the following expansion interfaces: (1) distributed control architecture. Adopting a collaborative architecture of centralized controllers and distributed switches, theoretically, it is possible to support larger scale networks by expanding the computing power of controllers (such as increasing the number of switches or deploying across regions); (2) Dynamic routing optimization. The effectiveness of joint routing selection for load balancing has been verified in the experiment (Table 3 shows that AVB traffic fairness reaches 0.98), and in the future, the resource allocation efficiency in large-scale networks can be further improved by introducing multi-objective optimization algorithms; (3) Compatible with standardized protocols. The algorithm strictly follows TSN protocols such as IEEE 802.1Qbv/ch, and its compatibility with existing industrial network devices (such as Siemens PROFINET and Rockwell CIP Sync) has been verified through simulation, supporting cross regional heterogeneous network integration.

In addition, given the advantages of SDN in centralized control and flexible scheduling, future research can explore how to implement more intelligent task scheduling and load balancing mechanisms under SDN architecture. By combining the RPS-TACQF algorithm proposed in this article, a hybrid traffic scheduling scheme based on SDN can be designed, utilizing the global perspective and dynamic decision-making capabilities of SDN controllers to achieve more refined resource allocation and traffic management. At the same time, intelligent optimization methods such as the Eagle Optimization Algorithm (AO) and the Whale Optimization Algorithm (WOA) can be used for reference to improve the adaptability and efficiency of scheduling strategies to cope with the complex and changing industrial Internet environment.

Skeletal muscle density mediates the relationships of monocyte-lymphocyte ratio, apolipoprotein A1/HDL, and alkaline phosphatase with thoracic aortic calcification: a multicenter study in patients undergoing maintenance dialysis
Authors: Meng-huan Wu, Jing Zheng, Fan Zhang, Yuan Tao, Yu-ting Gao, Yu-xin Ren, Wen Zhou, Shi-mei Hou, Yao Wang, Jing-yuan Cao, Xiao-xu Wang, Yan Yang, Bin Wang, Min Yang, Min Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTo investigate the associations of systemic inflammation, lipid metabolism, bone metabolism markers, and muscle quality with thoracic aortic calcification (TAC) in patients undergoing maintenance dialysis.MethodsThis multicenter cross-sectional research involved 1916 patients aged 18–80 years who underwent maintenance dialysis across four tertiary hospitals in China between January 2020 and June 2023. Non-contrast chest CT scans were performed to assess TAC and skeletal muscle density (SMD). Associations of TAC with SMD, monocyte-lymphocyte ratio (MLR), and high-density lipoprotein (HDL), apolipoprotein (apo) A1, alkaline phosphatase (ALP) were explored via multivariate regression models. Stratified analysis was further done to investigate the effect of SMD on TAC. Mediation analyses were conducted to identify the mediators.ResultsMultivariate linear regression analysis demonstrated significant positive associations between TAC and MLR (β = 0.43; 95%CI: 0.09, 0.78) and ALP (2.24; 0.89, 3.58), and negatively with SMD (-0.05; -0.07, -0.03), HDL (-1.01; -1.51, -0.51) and apo A1 (-1.13; -1.72, -0.55). The relationship between SMD and TAC was not affected by age, sex, smoking status, dialysis modality, dialysis vintage, coronary heart disease, or hyperlipidemia. The mediation effects of SMD were 8.73%, 6.42%, 8.74%, and 13.26% on the associations of TAC with MLR (95%CI: 0.014, 0.263), ALP (0.011, 0.230), HDL (0.032, 0.217) and apo A1 (0.052, 0.317) respectively. When TAC is treated as a binary variable, the results of the multivariable logistic regression and mediation analysis remain consistent.ConclusionsThis study highlights the complex interplay of MLR, ALP, HDL, apo A1 and SMD with TAC in patients undergoing maintenance dialysis. Improving skeletal muscle density may represent a novel strategy to mitigate the likelihood of TAC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12944-025-02757-6.

Introduction

More than 3.5 million people with chronic kidney failure worldwide receive maintenance dialysis [1]. The main reasons for onset and death in maintained dialysis patients are cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) [2]. Vascular calcification (VC), an ectopic precipitation of Ca3(PO4)2 crystals (primarily hydroxyapatite) within vascular walls [3], increases the likelihood of CVDs and death by 3–4 times and can effectively predict death in end-stage kidney disease (ESKD) people [4]. The Chinese Dialysis Calcification Assay uncovers the incidence of vascular and valvular calcification in dialysis people is up to 77.4% [2]. Therefore, identification of factors associated with VC is critical for public health strategies.

The essence of VC is the conversion of vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs) from contractile to osteoblastic phenotype [5], which is manifested by less expression of specific genes of VSMCs and more expression of osteogenic markers [6, 7]. Studies have identified factors that influence VC, including traditional indices (e.g., age, gender, high pressure, dyslipidemia, diabetes [8–10]) and non-traditional indices (e.g., mineral metabolic disturbance [11], inflammation [12], oxidative stress, fibroblast growth factors [13]).

Recent clinical trials reveal an intense link between low skeletal muscle mass (SMM) and VC. Jensky et al. [14]found a strong inverse link between lean body mass and the degree of aortic calcification [15]. Additional studies further reveal that reduced SMM and myosteatosis are not only associated with coronary artery calcification, but may also contribute to its progression [16, 17]. Supporting evidence from Chinese chronic kidney disease (CKD) cohorts confirms that lower SMM and visceral fat accumulation are separately related to both the occurrence and intensity of coronary artery calcification [18]. Particularly, in hemodialysis patients, beside VC, low muscle mass shows consistent association with recurrent failed vascular access, where VC is a potential crucial pathophysiological link [19].

In patients undergoing maintenance dialysis, the chronic accumulation of uremic toxins establishes a sustained, low-grade inflammatory state, promoting muscle protein degradation and contributing to the development of sarcopenia [20, 21]. Recent clinical investigations have demonstrated significant inverse associations between elevated alkaline phosphatase levels and muscle health indicators [22]. These observations suggest that alkaline phosphatase may serve as an integrated biomarker reflecting both nutritional status and disturbances in bone-mineral metabolism among hemodialysis patients [23]. Furthermore, patients undergoing maintenance dialysis typically exhibit a unique dyslipidemia profile [24]. However, the specific relationships of dyslipidemia, inflammation, bone metabolism markers and muscle quality with thoracic aortic calcification (TAC) in patients undergoing maintenance dialysis remain unclear. Thus, the study probed into the associations of these parameters with TAC in dialysis-dependent patients through mediation analysis.

Materials and methods

Study design and subjects

This multicenter cross-sectional work enrolled hospitalized patients undergoing dialysis in the renal departments of four tertiary centers in China (Changzhou First People’s Hospital, Zhongda Hospital Affiliated to Southeast University, Yangzhou First People’s Hospital, and Taizhou People’s Hospital Affiliated to Nanjing Medical University) between January 2020 and June 2023. Eligible participants were ESKD patients aged 18–80 years undergoing ‘standard dosage’ dialysis (a peritoneal fluid daily exchange capacity of 8000–10000 L or a hemodialysis frequency of 3 times per week, 4 h per session) ≥ 3 months prior to enrollment and receiving non-contrast chest computed tomography (CT) upon admission. Exclusion criteria included history of acute phase infection, active autoimmune disease, parathyroidectomy, malignancy, hepatic or respiratory failure, inflammatory bowel disease, Alzheimer’s disease, Guillain-Barré syndrome, limb amputation, and incomplete clinical data. The rationale for each exclusion criterion is provided in the Supplementary Materials. The study protocol was affirmed the Ethics Committee of the Third Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University (No. 2024CL085-01) and registered in the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR 2300068453). Data acquisition

Baseline demographic and clinical data were acquired from electronic medical records: age, sex, height (cm), weight (kg), systolic/diastolic blood pressure (SBP/DBP, mmHg), dialysis modality (hemodialysis/peritoneal dialysis, HD/PD), dialysis vintage, smoking status, drinking status, comorbidities [diabetes mellitus (DM), hypertension, coronary heart disease (CHD), hyperlipidemia, cardiovascular events (CVEs), and medication use. Specifically, CVEs included myocardial infarction, stroke, heart failure, arrhythmias, and peripheral artery disease. The measurement methods and the definitions of DM, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, drinking and smoking were detailed in the Supplementary Materials. Medication use included cinacalcet, vitamin D3, calcium supplements, phosphate binders, antihypertensive, antidiabetic, lipid-lowering, or antiplatelet drugs, iron supplements, erythropoietin drugs (ESAs) and α-keto acids. Laboratory tests, conducted with the fasting blood in the morning for PD patients and before the long dialysis interval for HD patients, were finished within 1 month of CT imaging. The laboratory factors included hemoglobin (Hb), serum albumin (ALB), apolipoprotein (apo) A1/B, lipoprotein (Lp) a (all g/l); alkaline phosphatase (ALP, u/l); total cholesterol (TC), triglycerides (TG), high-/low-density lipoprotein (HDL/LDL), Ca, P, serum creatinine (SCr), uric acid (UA), blood urea nitrogen (BUN) (all mmol/l), parathyroid hormone (PTH, pg/ml). Detailed assessment methods are provided in the Supplementary Materials. Body mass index (BMI, kg/m2) was computed as weight/squared height. Corrected Ca (mmol/l) was derived as total Ca − 0.02 × (ALB − 40 g/l). MLR was defined as monocyte-lymphocyte ratio.

Measurements of SMM and TAC indicators

Chest CT scans were acquired using a spiral CT scanner with standardized parameters: 100–140 kV, 70–260 mA and slice thickness of 1.25–5.00 mm. Images were reconstructed with a 5-mm slice thickness, window width of 280 Hounsfield units (HU), and window level of 70 HU. Skeletal muscle density (SMD) and aortic calcification were quantified using 3D Slicer 5.30 (Fig. 2). SMD was assessed by measuring the mean radiodensity of the skeletal muscles including erector spinae, quadratus lumborum, obliquus externus and internus abdominis, and transverse and rectus abdominis at the central plane of level L1 using HU threshold − 29 to + 150. TAC volumes (mm³) were calculated across three segments: ascending TAC (ATAC, from the left ventricular outflow tract to the start of the left common carotid artery), aortic TAC (AoTAC, from the left subclavian artery bifurcation to the pulmonary artery bifurcation), and descending TAC (DTAC, from the pulmonary bifurcation to the diaphragmatic hiatus). Calcification thresholds were defined as ≥ + 130 HU. SMD and TAC volumes were evaluated by trained nephrologists blinded to clinical and biological data.

Statistical analyses

Numerical data were shown as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median and interquartile range (IQR), as per normal distribution or not. Categorical data were displayed as absolute number and percent (%). The normality of data distribution was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Groups were compared via independent t-test, Mann-Whitney U test or Chi-square test. Skewed continuous data (TAC, ATAC, AoTAC, DTAC) were subjected to Box-Cox normal transformation before data analysis. Multicollinearity between variables in each model was assessed using the variance inflation factor (VIF). Multivariate linear regression was done to test the potential associations of MLR, ALP, HDL and apo A1 with SMD and TAC, including total TAC and its subregions (ATAC, AoTAC, DTAC). The results were reported as β and 95% confidence interval (CI). To complement the linear analyses, TAC was dichotomized at the median value (0 = below median TAC, 1 = above median TAC). Logistic regression was performed to assess associations between MLR, ALP, HDL, apo A1, SMD and TAC (%), using the same hierarchical adjustment strategy as linear models. Results were expressed as odds ratios (OR) with 95% CI. In Model 1, no parameter was adjusted. In Model 2, age, sex, BMI, SBP, DBP, smoking status, alcohol status, dialysis method, DM, CHD, hyperlipidemia and CVEs were adjusted. In Model 3, further adjusted medication history (cinacalcet, vitamin D3, antidiabetic drugs, ESAs and α-keto acids), correct Ca, P, PTH, UA and Scr were further adjusted. The confounding variables in the models were identified according to the significant factors in the univariate regression analysis of total TAC, and those VC-related factors recognized in imaging studies [16, 25]. Additionally, subgroup analyses stratified by age, sex, BMI, dialysis modality, dialysis vintage, smoking status, hyperlipidemia, CHD, and CVEs were performed. Mediator analysis was conducted to evaluate the extent to which the associations of MLR, ALP, HDL, and apo A1 with TAC were mediated by SMD. The independent variables were defined as MLR, ALP, HDL, and apo A1 (X), the outcome variable as TAC (Y), and the mediating variable as SMD (M). The mediation analysis was performed using the ‘mediation’ package in R software (version 4.2.2; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Total Effect represents the overall effect of X on Y without considering M. Direct Effect quantifies the effect of X on Y, controlling for M). It reflects the portion of the effect that is not mediated by M. Indirect Effect represents the effect of X on Y that occurs indirectly through M. It is calculated as the product of the effect of X on M and the effect of M on Y [26]. To quantify the mediation, the direct and indirect effects along with their 95% CIs were estimated by bootstrap methods (with 5,000 replications) to enhance accuracy. Two-sided P-values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

Participant information

Table 1 listed the baseline data of the subjects divided by the degree of TAC. Of 1916 participants, 56.8% (n = 1088) were males, the mean age was 56 years and the mean SMD was 33 Hu. The median volume of TAC was 313 mm3. With the progression of TAC, patients tended to be older and had longer dialysis vintage. The proportions of those receiving hemodialysis, consuming alcohol, having history of drug use (antidiabetic drugs, lipid-lowering agents, antiplatelet drugs, ESAs) and having comorbidities (DM, hypertension, CHD, hyperlipidemia, CVEs) progressively increased. Additionally, the levels of BMI, MLR, and ALP gradually rose, while DBP, TC, HDL, LDL, apo A1, Scr, UA and SMD levels declined (all P < 0.05). Furthermore, corrected Ca level, the proportions of patients who smoked or used cinacalcet, vitamin D3 and α-keto acids also varied across the TAC tertiles (all P < 0.05).

Mediation analysis

Mediation analyses revealed SMD significantly mediated the relationships between MLR, ALP, HDL, apo A1, and TAC, whether analyzed as continuous or dichotomous (median-split) variables. In Models 3, the regulating impact of SMD on the associations between MLR, ALP, HDL, apo A1, and TAC remained consistently stable. For continuous TAC, SMD accounted for 8.73% (95%CI: 0.014, 0.263) of MLR, 6.42% (0.011, 0.230) of ALP, 8.74% (0.032, 0.217) of HDL, and 13.26% (0.052, 0.317) of the apoA1 effects. When dichotomizing TAC, mediation proportions remained consistent for MLR (5.96%; 0.002, 0.281), ALP (5.55%; 0.003, 0.300), HDL (7.34%; 0.010, 0.299) and apoA1 (10.26%; 0.014, 0.355) (Fig. 6A-B).

The mediating effect of SMD on the associations of the four indicators with AoTAC and DTAC (Fig. 6C-D) varied across different models. SMD consistently mediated the associations with AoTAC in both Model 2 (MLR: 11.3%, 0.038, 0.284; HDL: 13.6%, 0.047, 0.375; apo A1: 19.5%, 0.086, 0.615; ALP: 6.42%, 0.021, 0.144) and Model 3 (MLR: 9.97%, 0.013, 0.375; HDL: 10.86%, 0.036, 0.314; apo A1: 16.80%, 0.064, 0.466; ALP: 8.03%, 0.009, 0.398). Regarding DTAC, SMD had a 14.93% mediating effect (95%CI: 0.051, 0.499) on the association between MLR and DTAC in Model 2, but this effect was not significant in Model 3. Conversely, SMD consistently mediated the associations of HDL, apo A1, and ALP with DTAC in both models. Consistent with previous findings, no significant SMD-mediated effects were observed for ATAC, supporting a segment-specific mediation pattern.

Discussion

To our knowledge, this study provides the first mediation analysis demonstrating that muscle quality significantly mediates the relationship between inflammation, lipid and bone metabolism and TAC in patients undergoing maintenance dialysis. The research involving 1916 patients show that (1) Lower HDL and apo A1, higher MLR, and higher ALP are independently associated with lower SMD. (2) TAC is associated positively with MLR and ALP, and negatively with HDL, apo A1 and SMD. (3) SMD acts as a biological intermediary between MLR, ALP, HDL, apo A1 and thoracic aortic calcification.

VC is featured by the pathological precipitation of Ca and phosphate in the intimal and medial layers of blood vessels [27]. It is primarily a cell-modulated procedure induced by the transition of VSMCs into osteoblast-like cells, accompanied by increased expression of osteogenic markers, notably ALP [3]. ALP promotes VC by degrading inorganic pyrophosphate (PPi), a potent suppressor of calcium phosphate crystallization, and contributes to the release of phosphate from phospholipids, further facilitating mineral deposition [28]. Clinical assays have confirmed the connection of ALP with vascular calcification. ALP is the biochemical indicator significantly related to coronary artery calcification among hemodialysis patients [29]. Similarly, circulating bone-specific ALP is positively linked with abdominal aortic calcification [30].

In multivariable model, MLR remained significantly associated with TAC and AoTAC. However, its association with DTAC lost significance in Model 3 following additional adjustment for medication history, correct calcium, phosphate, PTH, UA and Scr. Similar associations were reported in the general population [31] and PD patients, where elevated MLR correlates with higher abdominal aortic calcification [32]. In CKD population, immune dysregulation particularly involving neutrophils, monocytes, and dendritic cells leads to persistent inflammation, contributing to cardiovascular risk [33]. Given that monocyte counts are influenced by individual variability, MLR serves as a more stable, reproducible, and low-cost inflammatory marker. Inflammation likely promotes VC through activating M1 macrophages, which emit inflammatory factors (such as TNF-α, IL-1β/6), accelerating calcium deposition [34]. Additionally, monocytes produce IL-29, a cytokine implicated in inflammatory processes [35]. IL-29 drives osteogenic transdifferentiation of VSMCs by stimulating the JAK2/STAT3 pathway [36], upregulating osteogenic markers (e.g., BMP2, Runx2) and downregulating contractile proteins (e.g., α-SMA and SM22α) [37, 38]. After adjusting for confounding factors, the multivariate analyses identified HDL and its principal apolipoprotein, apo A1, as significant protective factors against VC, which align with a prior mechanistic study [39]. Apo A1, which constitutes approximately 70%–80% of proteome in HDL, exerts anti-calcific effects primarily through facilitating reverse cholesterol transport via ABCA1-dependent pathways [40]. Additionally, apo A1 possesses calcium-chelating properties that may directly inhibit pathological calcium-phosphate deposition in vascular tissues. Apo A1 enriched HDL also mitigates VC by inhibiting inflammatory factor generation (e.g., IL-1β/18), regulating the JAK-STAT pathway and enhancing the anti-calcific ability of matrix Gla protein, a critical extracellular restriction of mineralization [41]. Collectively, these results uncover the crucial role of lipid homeostasis and inflammation control in preventing VC among patients undergoing maintenance dialysis.

Sarcopenia has recently gained attention owing to its frequent coexistence with VC in CKD patients. The study revealed an inverse association in L1 SMD with TAC volume across multiple aortic segments. Similarly, Haidar et al. [42] identified a correlation between reduced abdominal muscle mass and increased coronary artery calcification, and Rodríguez et al. [43] discovered a negative link of abdominal aortic calcification with abdominal lean mass.

Upon multivariable adjustment, HDL and apo A1 remained independently and inversely associated with SMD, while MLR and ALP retained significant positive relationships. Notably, SMD was found to partially mediated the associations among inflammation (MLR), dyslipidemia (HDL, apo A1), disordered bone metabolism (ALP), and aortic calcification in patients undergoing maintenance dialysis. Lee JH et al. [23] reported a positive link between serum ALP and low SMM. ALP is involved in ATP metabolism within muscle cells, which is critical for muscle maintenance [23]. Chronic inflammatory states common in patients undergoing maintenance dialysis impairs nutritional status [44], disrupts capillary microcirculation and oxygen delivery, leading to decreased muscle protein synthesis and increased protein degradation [19, 45]. Pro-inflammatory cytokines further exacerbate muscle loss by stimulating the NF-κB pathway [46] and the ubiquitin- proteasome system [19, 47]. Interestingly, after adjusting for calcium-phosphate metabolism (PTH, Ca, and P), the modulating impact of SMD on the connection between MLR and DTAC disappeared, suggesting mineral metabolism may promote VC through muscle-independent pathways. Beyond their vascular protective roles, HDL and apo A1 also enhance mitochondrial function and exercise capacity in skeletal muscle [48]. Moreover, lipid-free apo A1 can increase glucose intake and insulin sensitivity, contributing to muscle mass preservation [49, 50]. Skeletal muscle as an endocrine organ releases myokines that inhibit VC [51]. Severe muscle loss promotes glucose intolerance, oxidative stress, and the osteogenic differentiation of VSMCs [19] and reduced muscle mass leads to decreased physical activity and blood flow shear stress, fostering lipid deposition and VC [43].

Study strengths and limitations

This is a multicenter, large-sample work to reveal that SMD mediates the relationship among MLR, ALP, HDL, apo A1 and thoracic aortic calcification in patients undergoing maintenance dialysis. Nevertheless, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, as a retrospective cross-sectional study based on electronic medical records (EMR), this analysis did not systematically record important confounding variables such as physical activity levels and socioeconomic status. Additionally, key indicators recommended by the EWGSOP2 guidelines for defining sarcopenia, including grip strength, walking speed, and chair stand tests [52], were not collected. This absence limits the ability to assess sarcopenia status in patients undergoing maintenance dialysis. Moreover, common inflammation markers such as CRP and interleukins in this study. Although bone-derived ALP offers the greatest specificity for bone metabolism and vascular calcification, the present analysis was limited to total ALP measured in stored serum. Future prospective studies will include these factors to enhance the understanding of sarcopenia and its clinical impact. Second, this analysis was limited to aortic calcification. More studies are warranted to explore the links between skeletal muscle parameters and other vascular calcification sites, such as the coronary arteries or cardiac valves. Third, the cross-sectional design here excludes causality analysis. Although mediation analysis provides preliminary insight into potential mechanisms, longitudinal and prospective research is called for to verify these results. Fourth, the absence of a healthy control group limits the ability to fully evaluate differences across sex and age groups. Future work will include data from healthy individuals to allow subgroup comparisons.

Conclusions

The study highlights the critical interplay of MLR, ALP, HDL, apo A1 and muscle quality with thoracic aortic calcification in patients undergoing maintenance dialysis. Identifying SMD as a key mediator between these pathological processes and aortic calcification offers novel insight into the underlying mechanisms. These observational associations indicate that multimodal interventions addressing MLR, ALP, HDL, apo A1 and muscle preservation warrant further investigation regarding their potential relationship with VC. Longitudinal and interventional research is called for to validate these pathways and determine whether improving skeletal muscle health can slow aortic calcification progression in patients undergoing maintenance dialysis.

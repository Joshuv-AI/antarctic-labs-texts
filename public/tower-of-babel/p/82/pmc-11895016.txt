Introduction

Mpox is a zoonosis caused by the monkeypox virus (MPXV), a deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) virus belongs to the genus Orthopoxviru (OPXV), which also includes variola virus, vaccinia virus (used in smallpox vaccines), and cowpox virus. On August 14, 2024, the World Health Organization (WHO) Director-General declared the mpox outbreak as a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC), which is the second PHEIC determination in 2 years involving mpox [1].

MPXV enters the human body through mucous membranes and damaged skin, mainly through direct contact, skin lesions or mucosal transmission, sexual contact, and contact with infected animals through exudation of lesions, blood, other body fluids, bites, scratches, and infection. In addition, the MPXV can also be transmitted via droplets. The primary clinical symptoms of mpox are fever, herpes, and lymph node enlargement, necessitating differential diagnosis from fever and rash diseases such as chickenpox, shingles, herpes simplex, measles, and dengue fever. Clinical diagnosis requires confirmation of the significance of laboratory test results [2]. Detection techniques, such as quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (PCR), genome sequencing, antigen or antibody identification, and virus isolation, are used in laboratory settings to identify MPXV. However, due to limitations, these methods may not always meet the need for prompt and accurate identification of MPXV infection in African experimental settings, equipment performance, and staff experience. Therefore, accurate identification of MPXV-infected individuals and timely transmission prevention require fast and reliable point-of-care testing (POCT) methods. Biosensors have recently emerged as an auspicious method for the detection of MPXV. This review focuses on the latest molecular detection methods and technologies for MPXV, particularly those integrated with biosensors which offer complementary advantages for different detection scenarios. Furthermore, this paper analyzed the advances and challenges of various detection methods, serving as a reference for the prevention and control of mpox globally.

Epidemiology

MPXV was first discovered in 1958 in a monkey during vaccine research and has since been reported in several reservoirs, particularly in rodents and other small mammals [3]. However, the first human case was not reported until 1970 in The People's Republic of Congo [4], [5]. The first reported cases were all endemic registered in African countries. However, in 1996 – 1997, human-to-human transmission became severe [6], [7]. In July 2022, the multi-country outbreak of mpox was declared a PHEIC because it spread rapidly via sexual contact across non-endemic regions. The PHEIC was lifted in May 2023 after a sustained decline in cases globally. However, on August 14, 2024, the WHO Director-General declared the mpox outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) and a growing number of other African countries constitutes another PHEIC. Thus, mpox may no longer be a rare viral zoonosis that is restricted to Central and West Africa but has become an emerging global concern.

The mpox outbreak in August 2024 brought a new PHEIC. However, it has been reported in the DRC for over a decade, and the number of cases reported each year has increased steadily. This year, reported cases increased significantly, and the number of cases reported so far has exceeded the last year’s total, with more than 15,600 cases and 537 deaths [1]. According to the WHO report, the number of confirmed cases is increasing in Africa. However, a significant discrepancy still exists between confirmed and suspected cases, which indicates that the data in the current global report is probably underestimating the actual number of mpox cases due to inadequate laboratory testing capacity. People are at risk of contracting the pox virus (related to Variola) due to the ongoing spread of mpox.

The factors leading to the current global epidemic are complex and interrelated. In 2023, the DRC was stuck by a severe outbreak of a new clade of MPXV (clade Ib), which exhibits better human adaptation [1]. Furthermore, nearly half of the cases involved children under 15 [8]; the mortality rate in children aged under 15 years can reach 50 % [9]. Approximately 40 % of mpox-infected individuals are also human immunodeficiency virus (HIV)-infected [10], [11], indicating the possibility of severe and long-term mpox co-infection among patients with HIV. Severe HIV-induced immunosuppression and other co-infections may impair the immune response, leading to viral persistence.

In conclusion, further research is required to effectively tailor therapeutic and preventive measures for at-risk groups, such as individuals engaging in high-risk behaviors, those with HIV, and children who face higher mortality risks. Since smallpox was eradicated globally in 1980, the previously immunized population's antibody defenses have waned. This immunological gap affects people born after 1980, which contributes to the worldwide mpox outbreak. These data indicate a high risk of further regional and global expansion that requires urgent attention [12].

The China's mainland reported its first imported mpox case in September 2022 [13], [14], with a local outbreak beginning in June 2023. By November 30, 2023, 1,610 confirmed cases of mpox had been reported in more than 20 provinces in China, with Guangdong Province having the most cases [14]. For management purposes, mpox is classified as a Category B infectious disease.

Genome

The MPXV genome is linear DNA, approximately 197 kb, which contains at least 47 structural proteins and core enzymes. It follows the typical genome structure of the genus OPXV. The central region of the genome is highly conserved, sharing over 90 % sequence identity with other OPXVs. The inverted terminal repeats (ITRs) located in the left and right variable regions are crucial for determining the virus host range and pathogenicity [15].

According to a phylogenetic study, the MPXV belongs to two distinct clades with roughly 0.5 % genome sequence differences [16]. Clade I, is usually prevalent in Central Africa, particularly in the DRC, where cases have been increasing for decades, is associated with severe clinical symptoms and substantial mortality rates of 4 % – 11 % [17], [18], [19]. Clade I includes subgroups I, II, III, IV, V, and the new Kamituga (VI) reported in Kamituga in 2024 [20]. In contrast, clade II, confined mainly to West Africa until the 2022 outbreaks, causes less severe illness and a mortality rate of less than 4 % [16], [17], [19], [21]. MPXV clade II began to spread widely in The Federal Republic of Nigeria in 2017 and has since spread to other parts of the world since the 2022 outbreaks [22], [23]. clade II may be further subdivided into subclades IIa and IIb. Subclade IIb was responsible for the global mpox outbreak in 2022, which primarily affected the population of men who have sex with men (MSM) and individuals who engage in sexual activities with infected individuals [24]. Clades IIa MPXV has had very little human-to-human spread and is primarily transmitted zoonotically by rodents and small mammals. A new mutant evolutionary clade I MPXV was identified in the DRC in 2023 [25]. Clade-specific molecular detection revealed that this variant exhibited deletions for target genes. The new clade Ib rapidly spread in DRC, primarily through sexual contact. Whereas clade IIb is efficiently transmitted between humans via both sexual and non-sexual contact by an infected individual [26].

Mutations in MPXV are predominantly characterized by the deletion and repetition of the ITR regions in the terminals of the genomes of clades I and II [27], [28], [29], [30]. Consequently, the molecular detection of MPXV primarily targets the highly conserved central region, with fewer detection targets in the ITR region (Fig. 1). Among the mutations, proteins (including C9L, I4L, L6R, A17L, A25R, A28L, and B21R) emerged as mutation hotspots with several consensus in-frame deletions, frameshift variants, synonymous variants, and amino acid substitutions [20]. The mutations may be related to the virus transmission, nucleotide synthesis, host–cell entry and fusion, virion assembly, infection establishment, immune evasion, and apoptosis inhibition, among others [8], [31], [32], [33], [34]. Notably, the 2022 MPXV diverged from the related 2018 – 2019 viruses by a mean of 50 single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs), which is far higher (approximately 6- to 12-fold more) than expected based on previous estimates of the substitution rate for OPXVs (1 – 2 substitutions per genome per year). The emergence of such a divergent branch may indicate accelerated evolution. An in-depth mutational analysis revealed the action of host enzymes in viral evolution and signs of potential MPXV human adaptation in ongoing microevolution [35].

Molecular detection methods

Unlike other detection methods, nucleic acid amplification is fast and offers high sensitivity and specificity. PCR is the preferred detection method recommended by the WHO for MPXV DNA.

The real-time PCR

The existing quantitative PCR (qPCR) test kits for MPXV on the market are mainly divided into OPXV universal and MPXV-specific kits. The former targets primarily the conserved regions of E9L [36], [37], [38], [39] and D6R [38], whereas the latter targets the envelope protein gene B6R [37], [39], DNA polymerase gene F8L [37], F3L [40], [41], [42], N3R [38], [41], [42], and G2R [40], [41]. The combined dual target detection of OPXV universal and MPXV-specific targets can enhance mutation resistance [39]. For the OPXV universal detection kit, mutations in the selected conserved region sequence are a key factor related to detection sensitivity. The alignment of the primer and probe binding regions of the real-time PCR detection kit (Johns Hopkins analytical validation), which has identified 151 MPXV genome sequences since 2022, exhibited 100 % identification. The detection limit of the kit is up to 100 copies / mL, indicating its reliability for the sensitive and rapid identification of the current OPXV epidemic [43]. Clinical specimens were parallel-tested with the following assays: RealStar Orthopoxvirus PCR and FlexStar Monkeypox Virus PCR (Altona Diagnostics), Novaplex MPXV (Seegene), and VIASURE Monkeypox Virus Real-Time PCR Reagents (CerTest Biotec). The negative percent agreement (NPA) was 100 % [95 % confidence interval (CI): 88.4 % / 88.1 % − 100.0 %]. The positive percent agreement (PPA) ranged from 92.2 % (95 % CI: 82.7 % − 97.4 %) to 96.9 % (95 % CI: 89.3 % − 99.6 %). The assays demonstrated that standard commercial kits' PPA still needs improvement. All positive specimens detected belonged to subclade IIb, as determined by whole-genome sequencing (WGS) [44]. The lack of sample validation for other clades makes it unclear whether the kits can detect MPXV clades IIa and I. Most qPCR kits can detect only OPXV or MPXV infection. The detection of MPXV clades or lineages also requires the use of clade-specific PCR. S. Huo, et al. [45] developed two multiplex qPCR assays. One was a mixture of primers and three probes to distinguish MPXV clades I, II, and other OPXVs. The other was to mix a pair of primers and two probes to detect the 2022 MPXV B.1 lineage and its descendant lineages [45]. These two assays encompass the different, currently prevalent evolutionary clades of MPXV and can thus detect MPXV infection and identify virus clades relatively quickly, thereby facilitating the timely identification of infected individuals with severe symptoms (clade I).

Notably, the primer-template mismatch significantly impacts the efficiency of qPCR detection. Matching the 1,730 MPXV complete genomes reported in 2022 with the primer and probe sequences of the MPXV universal qPCR detection reagent recommended by the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) in the USA revealed that the MPXV universal forward and reverse primers had two base mismatches in over 99 % of the genomes − 99.08 % and 97.46 % for the forward and reverse primers, respectively. The two base mismatches of the primer template resulted in a 4-fold increase in the detection limit [46]. An assay that corrected for these mismatche and ensured complete complementarity between the primers and current MPXV genomes could increase the sensitivity and accuracy of MPXV detection.

Digital PCR

Digital PCR (dPCR) achieves absolute quantitative detection with higher sensitivity but incurs higher costs (Fig. 2). The detection limit of the conserved gene F3L is 6 copies per reaction, and the limit of quantitation (LoQ) is 38 copies per reaction, which performs slightly better than qPCR [47]. However, this method has limitations, such as high cost, slight time consumption, and the limitation that only the low-concentration samples can be detected. Recent studies have shown that PCR reactions based on silicon-based microfluidic chips can combine the advantages of both qPCR and dPCR. They use in situ amplification (molecular diffusion on chip) to achieve non-specific amplification, template resolution, and high-sensitivity nucleic acid detection, which enables an extensive concentration range of molecular detection, from single molecule to high-concentration templates, which is ideal for rapid and accurate quantification of the nucleic acid samples with unknown concentrations [48]. Based on the core chip of rapid nucleic acid testing systems, this method gained popularity and lowered testing costs during the coronavirus disease 2019 pandemic in 2022. Four samples can be tested simultaneously on the chip, and 40 PCR amplification cycles can be completed in 5 min [49]. Thus, the advantages of this method include convenient portability, simple operation, and suitability for rapid detection under different settings. PCR based on silicon-based microfluidic chip features high speed and sensitivity, making it potentially more suitable for POCT diagnosis of MPXV, thereby significantly improving port detection efficiency. However, researchers must consider reducing the high cost of manufacturing chips.

The digital polymerase chain reaction (PCR) detection assay. Digital PCR places individual deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) molecules in separate reaction chambers and amplifies them by PCR to detect specific target sequences using a TaqMan probe or SYBR Green I dye assays. The total number of reaction and positive reaction units were counted, and the initial concentration of DNA template molecules was calculated according to the Poisson Distribution formula to achieve absolute quantification.

Isothermal amplification

Isothermal amplification is a reliable exponential nucleic acid method at a constant temperature without thermal cycling. This technique is ideal for detecting limited DNA sample sizes and offers sensitivity equal to or higher than that of the conventional PCR method. Loop-mediated isothermal amplification (LAMP), recombinant enzyme-assisted amplification (RAA), and recombinant enzyme polymerase amplification (RPA) can detect MPXV either alone or in combination with clustered regularly interspaced short palindromic repeats (CRISPR) (Fig. 3), lateral flow immunochromatography assay (LFIA), and other detection technologies.

The RPA cascaded CRISPR/Cas12a detection assay. The LAMP technique can identify the amplification results by visually observing the white turbid sediment, eliminating the need for tedious electrophoresis and ultraviolet (UV) observation. An integrated nucleic acid extraction detection device that combines magnetic bead-based nucleic acid extraction technology with LAMP enables rapid, highly sensitive, and specific MPXV visual detection. This system boasts a detection limit of 137 copies / mL, and delivers results in less than 1 h [50].

RPA and RAA are faster than LAMP, with the amplification product typically detectable within 10 min. The optimal reaction temperature is 37 ℃, and the enzyme mixture is also active at room temperature. In RAA-lateral flow dipstick (LFD) technology, which offers field detection of visualized products, results can be observed by the naked eye. This technology is characterized by its high specificity, sensitivity, simplicity, safety, and relatively low cost. The detection limit is up to 10 copies per reaction, which nearly matches the qPCR detection limit for mpox clinical samples [51]. Dual-target detection provides even higher specificity and sensitivity, with the MPXV RAA detecting 10 copies per reaction of the A27L gene and the F3L gene detecting 102 copies per reaction, which showed similar detection power to qPCR in clinically blinded tests [52]. Suspension microarray technology can accurately quantify 100 different biomolecules. The joint RAA for MPXV detection can reach an excellent sensitivity of 0.1 femtomole (fmol) / L and a linear range of 0.3 fmol / L to 20 picomole (pmol) / L, and spans five orders of magnitude, making it suitable for hospitals, centers for disease control, airports, railway stations, customs, the army, and other emergency scenarios [53].

CRISPR

The CRISPR technique has advanced molecular diagnostic innovation with its rapid detection, accuracy, sensitivity, and affordability. It has been an effective diagnostic tool for infectious diseases [54]. TypeV Cas12 protein in CRISPR, which is guided by CRISPR ribonucleic acid (crRNA), specifically recognizes cleaving double-stranded DNA (dsDNA) and activates nonspecific single-stranded DNA (ssDNA) trans-cleavage activity. A Cas12a-based direct detection assay for MPXV DNA can be established without amplification by targeting the envelope protein gene (B6R) of MPXV. Four crRNAs systems have a detection limit of up to 0.16 pmol / L, which is approximately 27 times lower than the limit of detection (LOD) with a single crRNA, indicating that the use of multiple crRNAs improves detection specificity [55]. Non-amplification detection circumvents the operational exposure of amplification products, shortens the detection time, and reduces the risk of laboratory pollution caused by amplification product leakage. However, non-amplification detection of MPXV is still difficult to achieve high sensitivity. Therefore, in MPXV detection, CRISPR / Cas is frequently combined with isothermal amplification technology to enhance detection specificity and sensitivity. This also facilitates the creation of MPXV POCT.

DNA endonuclease-targeted CRISPR trans reporter (DETECTR) is a detection system that combines isothermal amplification with CRISPR / Cas12a. It offers fast and straightforward point-of-time detection of trace DNA in samples [56]. The high selectivity of CRISPR-Cas 12 and the signal amplification capacity of RPA enable the detection of both currently prevalent and primitive MPXV clades with a sensitivity of up to 10 copies per reaction [57], [58]. Results can be obtained within 45 min using fluorescence readings [57], with a consistency of 99.5 % with qPCR [59]. A microtiter plate reader based on CRISPR-RPA can achieve a minimum detection limit of 22.4 attomole (amol) / L (13.5 copies / µL) [58], making it ideal for immediate on-site detection and visual observation. The LFD interpretation was highly consistent with the fluorescence-based readings (179 / 185), with a sensitivity of 100 % (95 % CI: 89.3 % − 100 %) and specificity of 98.6 % (95 % CI: 94.7 % − 100 %) [59]. Conventional CRISPR / Cas12a fluorescence analysis, which relies on trans-cutting the ssDNA reporter gene labeled with an expensive fluorophore and quencher, is challenging in widespread adoption, especially for POCT. Mango III, a cost-effective and stable fluorescent RNA aptamer that binds and illuminates the fluorophore TO3-3 PEG-Biotin fluorophore (TO3), can be a reporter gene for Cas13a trans-cleavage activity. The RNA aptamer (Mango III) coupled RAA-Cas13a single-pot assay took 40 min to specifically distinguish MPXVs from other OPXVs, with a detection limit of 4 copies. The method is fast and reliable, as verified using mpox clinical specimens [60].

Sequencing technology

Since the development of first-generation Sanger sequencing in 1977, DNA sequencing technology has rapidly advanced to the third generation. Each generation of sequencing technology has significantly enhanced various fields, including genomic and medical research, drug development, and studying infectious diseases. Next-generation sequencing (NGS) technology dominates the global sequencing market [61].

Genome sequencing of MPXV-positive samples can be performed using Sanger sequencing or NGS methods [62]. First-generation sequencing technology, which is known as the gold standard method, has high accuracy but low throughput, among other disadvantages, which has impeded its large-scale application. Nevertheless, Sanger sequencing continues to be used for WGS of pathogens with small genomes, such as human influenza A and Zika [63]. Second-generation sequencing technology, also called NGS or high-throughput sequencing technology, has addressed the low throughput issue associated with first-generation sequencing methods. WGS using NGS can accurately distinguish MPXV from other OPXVs; however, this technology is expensive, and downstream processing of sequencing data requires enormous computing power. Metagenomic next-generation sequencing (mNGS) of clinical or environmental samples is a promising method to identify viral sequences for rapid detection and to determine the epidemiology of transmission [64], [65]. mNGS has been crucial in discovering SARS-CoV-2 and is widely used during the global mpox outbreaks. The full-length MPXV genome was obtained within 12 h by the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention using next- and third-generation sequencing technology [66]. Third-generation sequencing technology is also known as single-molecule sequencing technology. Compared with NGS, third-generation sequencing can detect 10 nucleotides per second, significantly reducing the sequencing time. Third-generation sequencers can also directly sequence the original DNA or RNA sample without requiring PCR amplification, and with no preference for CG nucleotides [63]. Furthermore, methylation information can be directly detected and obtained. However, despite its many advantages, third-generation sequencing has not been widely adopted in clinical applications because of its high error rate and associated costs [67].

Biosensors

Although POCT methods based on portable biosensors has grown and evolved in the past several years, numerous obstacles have been overcome regarding portability, downsizing, and integration into the biosensing equipment used in POCT. Biosensors detect and convert biological processes into visible signals. The fast response time, high sensitivity, efficiency, portability, automation, and small size make these devices highly valuable for advancing field POCT technology. Signal acquisition is crucial in constructing biosensors. Electrochemical biosensors are generally more sensitive than optical biosensors due to the rapid advancement of electrode materials. However, this sensitivity also has drawbacks, such as the ease with which interference substance signals can be detected and the significant impact of background current on interpreting results. Thus, improvements in the antifouling performance of electrochemical biosensors and the accurate detection of targets in complex biological fluids are warranted.

Molecules capable of binding viruses can be modified on nanomaterials' surface, allowing the virus to be captured using signal amplification technology. The nanoparticle-based lateral flow biosensor (LFB) can be used in combination with LAMP [68], [69] or isothermal multiple cross displacement amplification (MCDA) [70], targeting MPXV D14L and ATI genes, which enables the diagnosis of MPXV infection and could differentiate between the clade I and clade II of MPXV within 1 h [68], [70]. The electrochemical biosensor, based on nanocomposite materials combined with Cas12a, can directly detect MPXV (targeting diverse regions of the F3L gene) within 35 min [71], with a detection limit of 682 fmol / L in complex biological fluids (e.g., whole blood and serum), demonstrating its good antifouling performance. CRISPR-Cas12a – assisted nanopore (SCAN) combined with RPA can achieve an overall detection limit of 16 copies / μL (26.56 amol / L) and 100 % specific identification of MPXV and CPXV after pre-amplification, at a higher sensitivity than non-amplified methods [72]. The detection efficiency can be further increased by using a simplified virus lysis procedure. The CRISPR-based one-step diagnostic platform SCOPE (Streamlined CRISPR On Pod Evaluation platform) can rapidly release viral nucleic acids from rash fluid swabs, oral swabs, saliva, and urine samples within 2 min, following a one-step RPA-CRISPR / Cas13a reaction that could detect MPXV at as low as 0.5 copies / µL (2.5 copies per reaction) within 15 min [73]. The results of the clinical sample validation were 100 % congruent with the qPCR results, demonstrating the potential application of this assay in MPXV field detection.

Biosensors can also detect MPXV antigens. The MPXV A29 protein contains the MPXV-specific epitope and is widely conserved in the Poxviridae [74], which currently serves as the primary detection target of immunoprobe-based biosensors. Utilizing a novel graphene-based material, graphene quantum rods (GQRs), A29 antibody (Ab A29)/MoO3-GQRs was used as an immune probe on carbon fiber paper (CFP) for the early detection of MPXV protein A29, with a linear range of 0.5 – 1,000 nanomole (nmol) / L, a detection limit of 0.52 nmol / L, and a sensitivity of 4.51 µA in phosphate buffered saline (PBS) [75], indicating that the prepared immunoprobe could capture the A29 antigen in serum samples without reducing electrode sensitivity. The screen-printed electrode, noted for its lightweight, portable, and highly integrated electrode, simplifies the operation steps and serves as an effective electrochemical sensing device suitable for rapid on-site detection. Heparan sulfate, which binds to the A29 protein, is fixed to the surface of the gold screen-printed electrode. The interaction with the A29 protein is then monitored by electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS). The detection limit was 2.08 ng / mL, the limit of quantification was 6.28 ng / mL, the linear detection range was 2.0 – 50 ng/mL, and the relative standard deviation value at 25 ng / mL was 2.82 % [76]. However, the screen-printed electrodes are single-use only, contributing to high costs.

Compared with proteins, DNA aptamers are more compact, stable, affordable, and easily synthesized. After enrichment through SELEX (Systematic Evolution of Ligands by Exponential Enrichment) screening, aptamers can also exhibit sensitivity that is similar to antigen–antibody interactions. Forming an aptamer-target-antibody sandwich structure facilitates the efficient recognition of the target as it passes through the nanopore. This nanopore sensing system has a detection limit of 11 fmol / L for MPXV A29 protein and can directly identify MPXV A29 from vaccinia virus (VACV) A27 and varicella zoster virus (VZV) protein in human serum and saliva [77]. The CRISPR / Cas12a-mediated aptamer sensor detects A29 in a linear range of 1 ng / mL to 1 μg / mL with a detection limit of 0.28 ng / mL [78]. The advantage of aptamers lies in their high binding ability, which is comparable to that of antibodies. Whether aptamers can replace enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISA) in the field of disease diagnosis remains a matter of concern.

Optical biosensors have the advantages of high sensitivity, robustness, dependability, and the potential to be integrated into a single chip. Furthermore, they do not require complicated preprocessing and do not affect the properties of the target molecules. Optical biosensors for MPXV detection include surface plasmon resonance (SPR) based biosensors [79], and surface-enhanced Raman spectroscopy (SERS) based biosensors [80]. These technologies are anticipated to apply to the early diagnosis of MPXV infection.

The biosensing platform of CRISPR / Cas12a has the advantages of high sensitivity and cost-effectiveness. The CRISPR / Cas12a-based fiber tip biosensor combined with SPR technology (CRISPR-SPR-FT) can precisely identify the MPXV F8L gene with a fatal mutation site (L108F). This system can analyze viral double-stranded DNA from MPXV within 1.5 h under non-amplification, and has a detection limit below 5 aM in plasmids and 59.5 copies / μL in blood samples spiked with pseudovirus, respectively, with high system stability and portability [79]. By integrating the hybridization chain reaction (HCR) and DNA-copper nanoclusters (DNA-CuNC) to develop a label-free MPXV detection system capable of post-amplifying Cas12a activation. The trans-cleavage of Cas12a triggers a silenced HCR, leading to the in situ assembly of fluorescent DNA-CuNCs, allowing for the turn-on reporting of Cas12a activation with a detection limit of 5 fmol / L without pre-amplification. The sensitivity of this technique was further increased when pre-amplification was added, with a detection limit of 1 copy of MPXV plasmid in human serum. The MPXV pseudovirus validation test yielded results that agreed with the qPCR results, thereby further confirming the robustness of the method [81]. In conclusion, this technique can be applied to biosafety applications as an effective synthetic biology sensing platform.

Researchers are currently exploring the use of smartphones to obtain test results. Through EIS, a miniaturized potentiostat connected to a smartphone can detect MPXV A29 protein in the concentration range of 1 × 10−14 to 1 × 10−7 g / mL, with a detection limit of 3 × 10−16 g / mL. Furthermore, the system is capable of detecting MPXV particles at a concentration range of 1 × 10−1 to 1 × 104 plaque forming unit (PFU) / mL with an LOD of 7.8 × 10−3 PFU / mL, and no cross-reactivity [82]. Based on CRISPR / Cas12a technology, the chemiluminescence biosensing platform takes G-quadruplex (G4) DNAzyme as the substrates. The G4CLCas-based sensing platform relies on Cas12a trans-cleavage activation that triggers the cleavage of the G4 DNAzyme, resulting in chemiluminescence signals off / on compared with those of the control. Chemiluminescence signals are captured as quantitatively analyzed images and visualized using a smartphone-assisted imaging cartridge [83]. Under optimal conditions, the MPXV DNA detection limit of the platform is 8.6 amol / L, approximately 5.2 copies / μL, enabling precise measurement of viral DNA within a linear concentration range of 10 – 300 amol / L [83]. The platform is simpler and more convenient when imaging and data analysis are performed through a smartphone. Furthermore, mpox portable detection findings can now be uploaded and shared via cloud system development [84], making it an ideal technology for field laboratory diagnosis and epidemiological investigation.

GeneXpert

GeneXpert (Cepheid) is a portable rapid diagnostic device that can be used for POCT in endemic areas. The GeneXpert platform can be used to evaluate the usefulness and accuracy of multiplex MPXV detection, including MPXV-specific, OPXV universal, and internal control PCR testing [85]. All 164 diagnostic samples, 50 scabs and 114 blister fluid swabs, were collected from suspected mpox cases in the DRC. The OPXV PCR kit and the GeneXpert MPX / OPX assay kit were used for parallel-testing of the samples. The GeneXpert MPX / OPX assay kit exhibited 98.8 % sensitivity and 100 % specificity. The GeneXpert system integrates sample preparation with real-time PCR amplification and detection in a backpack-sized analysis workstation. The system uses self-contained cartridges to minimize contamination risk and generates results from minimally processed samples in less than 90 min, thereby potentially extending current MPXV detection capabilities [85].

In addition, several other nucleic acid testing platforms are widely utilized as POCT, such as BD Max (BD), FilmArray (BioMerieux), Icube/Iponatic (Sansure, China), and BG-NOVA-X8 (BioGerm, China), all of which also exhibit potential for MPXV detection.

Antigen and antibody tests

Antigen and antibody detection methods are generally used only for the auxiliary diagnosis of mpox. The conventional techniques for detecting the antigen and antibody identify only the OPXVs and exhibit a low sensitivity due to the solid serological cross-reaction.

Antibody tests

Serum antibody testing can be performed for vaccine efficacy assessment or epidemiological investigation. However, because of the cross-reaction of OPXV antibodies, serological testing is not specific for MPXV infection. Furthermore, individuals born before 1980 may present false-positive results owing to previous smallpox vaccination. Typically, MPXV infection can produce IgM and IgG within 5 d and 8 d, respectively. Suspected cases with positive IgM or a four-fold or more tremendous increase in IgG during the recovery and acute phase indicate recent exposure to OPXV [86]. Every serological detection technique offers distinct benefits. Immunofluorescence assay (IFA) enables the specific detection of IgG and IgM; ELISA can be adopted for high-throughput sample detection to facilitate epidemiological studies; and neutralization tests using neutralizing antibodies enable the direct detection of viruses [87]. C. Therrien et al. [88] evaluated the clinical performances of an in-house chemiluminescent-based serological assay and a standard commercial ELISA assay in detecting specific IgG antibodies obtained from patients exposed to MPXV. The sensitivity of both assays was relatively poor during the acute phase. The in-house assay demonstrated significantly higher sensitivity (78 %) than the commercial assay (44 %), with samples collected in the convalescent phase of the disease. Regarding testing in healthy populations, both assays exhibited very high specificity in excluding false-positive reactions (100 %) [88], indicating that the developed in-house assay exhibits reasonable specificity and sensitivity during the convalescent phase of the disease and could be used in seroprevalence studies to evaluate previous exposure to MPXV. To address the problem of low sensitivity and high cross-reaction in the acute phase of mpox, J.L. Yates et al. [89] developed a serological testing method for MPXV infection in a vaccinated population using a high-throughput multiple microsphere immunoassay. By combining an MPXV-specific peptide with an OPXV cross-reactive protein, they increased the detection sensitivity of detection to 93 % and the specificity to 98 %.

Antigen tests

In the acute phase, antigen tests directly detect viral surface proteins, which are more sensitive than antibody tests. I. Davis et al. [90] developed two semi-quantitative ELISA assays for MPXV antigen A29 based on the magnetic bead platform, which was standardized using cell culture medium containing MPXV and serum samples of MPXV-positive nonhuman primates (NHP). Compared with other infectious OPXVs (vaccinia virus, cowpox, and camelpox), the two assays demonstrated high MPXV antigen-specificity [90]. The dual-signal output enhances the detection sensitivity for MPXV. The detection limits of the colorimetry and SERS of the dual-signal co-enhanced immunochromatographic assay (ICA) are as low as 0.2 and 0.002 ng / mL, respectively. The SERS signal can quantitatively detect MPXV, and the colorimetric signal facilitates the quantitative identification of MPXV within 20 min [91]. Strong colorimetric and enhanced fluorescence dual-signal output LFIA can also specifically detect for the MPXV antigen (A29) in 15 min. The detection limits of the colorimetric and fluorescence modes are 0.5 ng / mL and 0.0021 ng / mL, respectively. They can offer excellent stability, robust colorimetric ability, and superior fluorescence intensity compared with the conventional colorimetric (gold nanoparticles, AuNPs) and fluorescent (QD nanobead) nanotags [92].

OPXVs are giant DNA viruses with a genome encoding over 200 proteins, which makes designing immunoassays for antigen detection challenging. Consequently, MPXV antigen detection targets are much fewer than those for molecular detection techniques such as qPCR [93]. Although the MPXV-specific antigen detection target produced is almost exclusively A29, both the mature (MV) and enveloped (EV) viruses have over 20 surface proteins, suggesting that discovering detectable targets for MPXV may be insufficient. The MPXV envelope proteins H3L, E8L, M1R, L1R, C19L, A35R, and B6R are potential targets for rapid antigen detection methods in addition to A29 [94]. H3L is an MV surface protein with high immunogenicity for MPXV, which has 93.52 % identity to the VACV-H3L antigen and the lowest cross-reaction compared with A29L, M1R, E8L, B6R, and A35R. Replacement of residue 233A in H3L may disrupt the B cell epitope, preventing its recognition by anti-VACV polyclonal antibodies, which may imply that H3L has an MPXV-specific B cell epitope [95], [96], [97].

Discussion

This review highlighted the new MPXV detection techniques and discussed each method's advantages and limitations. qPCR is the most effective MPXV diagnosis and typing strategy, offering high sensitivity, specificity, speed, efficiency, and throughput. The detection limit of the qPCR kits usually ranges from 100 to 1,000 copies / mL [43], [44]; however, the PPA of standard commercial kits still needs to be improved. PCR reactions based on silicon-based microfluidic chips allow 4 samples to be tested simultaneously on the chip, and 40 PCR amplification cycles to be completed within 5 min [49]. Combining magnetic bead-based nucleic acid extraction technology with LAMP boasts a detection limit of 137 copies / mL, delivering results in less than 1h [50]. Using RPA and RAA, the amplification products are typically detectable within 10 min. The detection limit can reach 10 – 100 copies per reaction, nearly matching that of qPCR for mpox clinical sample detection [51], [52]. Combining suspension microarray technology with RAA in detecting MPXV can attain an excellent sensitivity of 0.1 fmol / L and span 5 orders of magnitude [53]. The crRNAs systems had a detection limit of up to 0.16 pmol / L. Combining RPA techniques with CRISPR-Cas12 sensitivity enables up to 10 copies per reaction [57], [58]. Surprisingly, these techniques achieved a detection limit of below 10 amol / L when combined with biosensors.

Nucleic acid amplification-based diagnostic methods are the gold standard for MPXV detection and typing.. Emerging technologies such as CRISPR, isothermal amplification combined with biosensors, and GeneXpert change detection from laboratory testing to POCT, thereby significantly enhancing detection efficiency and preventing disease transmission caused by patient clustering. The combination of smartphones and cloud systems enables medical and health institutions to quickly control the epidemic. This integration allows for the timely provision of nursing care to the patients, as well as the implementation of preventive measures against the spread of the disease. The development of rapid antigen diagnostic platforms for screening assays is urgently needed. Compared with nucleic acid testing, rapid antigen testing can further reduce testing costs while also allowing,patients to perform self-testing without the need for any lab equipment. Acquisition rash, blister fluid, scab skin, oropharynx, or virus samples, such as nasopharyngeal secretion culture, can be collected to isolate the MPXV. Virus isolation presents high technical difficulty, is time-consuming, and is unsuitable for routine diagnosis. However, virus isolation remains irreplaceable in virus biology and vaccine research [98]. Different detection methods each have their advantages and limitations. In practical applications, selecting the most suitable detection method based on the specific requirements of the scenario ensures the best detection outcomes.

MPXV detection still poses numerous challenges. Since the MPXV is classified as a Biosafety Level (BSL)-3 pathogen, it is imperative to ensure biosafety measures during detection. Moreover, sample pretreatment must be carefully considered with the detection methods used to prevent any inadvertent spread of infection during the process. An inactivation method that removes up to five log levels of infectious virus particles from viremic serum without compromising antibody detection enables MPXV serology to be performed outside the BSL-3 facility [87]. With the evolution of MPXV, the epidemic characteristics of the MPXV are also changing, with adolescents under 15 years old becoming a vulnerable population.

Moreover, the MPXV is prone to co-infection with HIV and syphilis [99], resulting in a more severe disease, worse prognosis, and increased mortality in patients. Therefore, numerous quick detection methods for identifying co-infections must be developed. Furthermore, diseases such as chickenpox, shingles, herpes simplex, measles, dengue fever, and other febrile exanthematous diseases that share clinical symptoms with mpox can complicate differential diagnosis. Therefore, developing multiple detection methods is essential for efficient and rapid differential diagnosis. Affected African countries struggle to adopt developing nucleic acid amplification-based technologies because of their high cost. The lack of sufficient protein targets and the challenge of overcoming cross-reaction are significant obstacles to antigen detection. Multi-target detection or developing new targets are potentially viable approaches.

Mpox diagnosis and treatment revolve around the accurate identification of the virus. The accurate detection of MPXV is essential for its diagnosis, treatment, prevention, and control. Employing different technologies can enhance the virus's detection sensitivity, facilitating the detection of low levels of infection up to the acute phase. In Africa, a patient waits for up to a year to receive treatment based on a positive test result. CDC should also develop and practice effective preventive and control measures for patients to receive confirmed information and treatment on time. Finally, adherence to the “One Health” policy and inclusive, community-centered coping mechanisms are recommended [100].

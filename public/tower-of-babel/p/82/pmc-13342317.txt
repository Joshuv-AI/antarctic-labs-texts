Spontaneous malignant myoepithelioma in the parotid gland of an aged female Crl:CD (SD) rat
Authors: Takuro Endo, Osamu Hashiguchi, Moeko Aoki, Nobuaki Sato, Teppei Yamashita, Tsubasa Saito, Kohji Tanaka
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
An aged female Crl:CD (SD) rat developed a subcutaneous tumor in the left occipital region. Histologically, the tumor was predominantly composed of spindle-shaped cells proliferating in a loosely cohesive fascicular arrangement in a basophilic myxoid matrix with a minor population of epithelioid cells. Metastases were detected in the cervical lymph nodes, kidneys, lungs, and the pleural cavity. Duct-like structures, considered to be entrapped normal salivary ducts, were scattered within the tumor. Immunohistochemically, the tumor cells were diffusely positive for vimentin, focally positive for alpha-smooth muscle actin and p63 in the spindle-shaped population, and positive for cytokeratin AE1/AE3, exclusive to the epithelioid cell population, whereas S100, chromogranin A, and myoglobin were not expressed in any of the tumor cells. Ultrastructurally, spindle-shaped tumor cells exhibited microfilaments, desmosome-like junctions, and cytoplasmic processes. These features were consistent with the diagnosis of malignant myoepithelioma, most likely originating from the parotid gland.

Introduction

Myoepitheliomas have been reported to occur in humans and in animals such as dogs and mice1–3. They are rare tumors in most strains of mice, although they have a relatively high incidence in A/J, BALB/cByJ, and BALB/cJ strains3. The most common sites of origin are the parotid and submandibular salivary glands, and a female predilection has been noted in mice4. These tumors show malignant behavior, including invasion and metastasis, and are thus referred to as malignant myoepithelioma or myoepithelial carcinoma5–7. There has been only one report of malignant myoepithelioma that did not metastasize to any organ in a Wistar (Han) female rat7. Here, we describe the morphological and immunohistochemical (IHC) features of a malignant myoepithelioma that spontaneously developed with multiple metastases in an aged Crl:CD (SD) rat.

Materials and Methods

A 14-week-old female Crl:CD (SD) rat was purchased from Charles River Laboratories Japan, Inc. (Kanagawa, Japan) and assigned to a 96-week background data collection study. The animal was pair-housed in a solid-floored plastic cage with bedding under standard controlled climate conditions (temperature, 23 ± 3°C; relative humidity 50 ± 20%; air ventilation, 10 to 15 exchanges per hour; light/dark cycle, 12 hours). Gamma-irradiated pelleted diet (CR-LPF, Oriental Yeast Co. Ltd., Tokyo, Japan) and tap water (Gotemba City Water) were provided ad libitum using a stainless-steel feeder and an automatic water supply system, respectively. At 107 weeks of age, the animal was in poor general condition and was euthanized for animal welfare reasons by exsanguination from the abdominal aorta under isoflurane anesthesia.

At necropsy, there was an approximately 3 × 3 cm subcutaneous mass covered by the skin over the occipital region. The lesion extended into the epidermis, resulting in ulceration of the overlying skin. Other findings associated with the lesion included an enlarged cervical lymph node and multiple nodules in the kidneys, lungs, and pleural cavity, which were histopathologically identified as metastases originating from the occipital subcutaneous lesion.

All the organs routinely examined in toxicity studies were fixed in neutral-buffered 10% formalin, embedded in paraffin, sectioned, and stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E). The cells, predominantly comprising the mass, proliferated in a loosely cohesive fascicular pattern often associated with a basophilic myxoid matrix (Fig. 1B). They were characterized by plump-to-slender, short spindle-shaped cells with moderate-to-abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm and indistinct cell borders (Fig. 1C). The nuclei were round to oval with vesicular chromatin and rare prominent nucleoli. Another cell type with an epithelioid arrangement was also found as a minor component of the mass (Fig. 1D). Mitotic figures were frequently observed throughout the mass tissue, indicating high proliferative activity. Multiple necrotic foci of variable size were present, with hemorrhage and macrophages containing cellular debris. Duct-like structures were scattered within the occipital lesion (Fig. 1E); however, they were absent in all metastatic lesions. These duct-like structures often collapsed, and the nuclear density was high compared with normal parotid gland ducts. Normal parotid glands and mammary glands were observed adjacent to the mass. Notably, the parotid glands were contiguous with the mass tissue (Fig. 1F), whereas the mammary glands were unequivocally isolated from the mass by fibrous tissue. The myxoid stroma within the lesion was stained with Alcian Blue (Fig. 1G). PAM staining revealed argyrophilic fibers among cell fascicles and around the duct-like structures (Fig. 1H).

Discussion

Based on these findings, the current case was diagnosed with malignant myoepithelioma. The tumor was located in the occipital subcutis, where the parotid gland and mammary glands are anatomically located. Based on the histological continuity of the tumor, the parotid gland was considered the most likely site of origin.

In humans, myoepitheliomas exhibit various histological cell morphologies, including spindle, plasmacytoid, epithelioid, and clear cells1. Common progenitor cells in the salivary glands are thought to have the potential to differentiate into either epithelial or myoepithelial cells8. In the present case, a minor population of epithelioid cells was considered to represent epithelial differentiation of tumor cells.

Myoepitheliomas in laboratory mice consist of large, elongated, or spindle-shaped cells with a mixture of polyhedral epithelioid cells4. The tumor in the current case was predominantly composed of fascicular proliferation of spindle-shaped cells, with a mixture of a minor population of epithelioid cells. Notably, the myxoid matrix was a distinguishing histological feature in this case, further substantiating the notion of myoepithelial histogenesis of the tumor. In canine mammary complex and mixed tumors, four subtypes of myoepithelial cells are recognized: resting and proliferative suprabasal myoepithelial cells, and spindle and stellate motile interstitial myoepithelial cells9. Interstitial myoepithelial cells may produce myxoid matrix with chondroid changes and ultimately become fibroblast-like cells, exclusively exhibiting vimentin immunoreactivity10. However, these subtypes of myoepithelial tumor cells have not yet been described in rats. Nonetheless, the production of myxoid matrix and vimentin expression with restricted CK expression in the current case are consistent with the features of interstitial myoepithelial tumor cells described in canine complex and mixed tumors.

Normal myoepithelial cells contain myofilaments with focal densities and are connected to each other by desmosomes11. They form long cytoplasmic processes encircling the acini and intercalated ducts11, 12. Neoplastic myoepithelial cells emulate these characteristics peculiar to normal myoepithelium13, 14. In the present case, spindle-shaped tumor cells displayed microfilaments, desmosome-like junctions, and cytoplasmic processes. Although dense bodies were not formed by the microfilaments in the current case, these structures may not always be evident in neoplastic myoepithelium13, 14.

In the current case, duct-like structures were observed within the occipital lesion, but were not present in any metastatic lesions. PAM staining revealed the basement membrane surrounding the duct-like structures. Immunohistochemistry uncovered a layer of α-SMA- or p63-positive cells around the base of the epithelium, features being consistent with normal myoepithelial cells of the salivary gland duct. Ki-67 was not expressed in the duct-like epithelial cells, unlike the diffusely positive spindle-shaped tumor cells. Malignant myoepitheliomas in mice have been reported to contain ductal structures that were interpreted as salivary gland ducts involved in the tumor tissue15. Similarly, the duct-like structures in the current case were considered to be salivary gland ducts entrapped within the tumor tissue during tumor expansion.

Differential diagnoses may include spindle cell soft tissue neoplasms, such as schwannoma, leiomyosarcoma, rhabdomyosarcoma, and fibrosarcoma. Negative IHC results for S100 and myoglobin precluded schwannomas and rhabdomyosarcomas, respectively. Leiomyosarcoma and fibrosarcoma were excluded based on the presence of CK-positive epithelioid tumor cells.

In summary, we report a malignant myoepithelioma arising in the parotid gland with metastasis to multiple organs in a female Crl:CD (SD) rat. Histologically, the fascicular and epithelioid proliferation of tumor cells in myxoid stroma was a characteristic feature of this case. This report contributes to our understanding of the histological, immunohistochemical, and ultrastructural features of myoepithelial tumors in rodents.

Predicting cesarean delivery in twin pregnancies undergoing trial of labor: a multicenter cohort study
Authors: Matan Papier, Tzuria Peled, Maayan Bas Lando, Sorina Grisaru-Granovsky, Hen Y Sela, Misgav Rottenstreich
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo identify risk factors associated with intrapartum cesarean delivery (CD) among twin pregnancies undergoing a trial of labor (TOL).Study designA retrospective, multicenter cohort study was conducted at two medical centers between 2005 and 2024. Inclusion criteria were diamniotic twin pregnancies who underwent TOL, first twin in cephalic presentation, gestational age between 32 0/7 and 40 6/7 weeks, and undergoing a TOL. Multivariate logistic regression was performed to identify independent risk factors for intrapartum CD.ResultsA total of 2,730 twin pregnancies who underwent TOL met criteria and were included. The intrapartum CD rate was 6.5% (n = 177); 15.3% for primiparous women and 4.1% for multiparous women. Suspected fetal distress and labor dystocia were the most frequent indications for CD, 45.2% and 35.0% respectively. Multivariate analysis revealed that primiparity was the strongest independent predictor of intrapartum CD (adjusted Odds Ratio [aOR] 5.81, 95% CI: 2.67–12.63). Additional significant risk factors included large for gestational age (LGA) presenting twin (aOR 4.27, 95% CI: 1.95–9.34), maternal obesity (BMI > 30 kg/m2) (aOR 3.29, 95% CI 1.72–6.30), and cervical dilation < 3 cm at admission (aOR 2.30, 95% CI: 1.18–4.50).ConclusionIn twin pregnancies undergoing TOL, primiparity, LGA presenting twin, maternal obesity, and limited cervical dilation upon admission are significant, independent risk factors for intrapartum CD. These findings can improve risk stratification and support evidence-based patient counseling.Keywords: Twin pregnancy, Intrapartum cesarean delivery, Risk factors, Primiparity, Large for gestational age, Maternal obesity, Trial of labor

Introduction

In recent years, there has been a discernible rise in both twin pregnancies and cesarean deliveries (CDs), presenting unique challenges in contemporary obstetric practice.

Twin pregnancies account for approximately 3.12% of all pregnancies in the United States [1], a trend primarily attributed to advancing maternal age at conception [2] and the widespread adoption of assisted reproductive technologies (ART) [3]. These pregnancies inherently carry increased risks for adverse outcomes, including preterm delivery and congenital anomalies, abnormal presentation at delivery, as compared to singleton pregnancies [4].

Concurrent with this trend, CD rates among singleton pregnancies have shown a notable global increase, with the worldwide rate standing at 21.1%, rising to 27.2% in more developed regions [5]. CDs, whether planned in advance (elective) or performed following a trial of labor (TOL), carry inherent risks for both short- and long-term complications [6]. Approximately 70% of the CDs are intrapartum procedures among singleton pregnancies, representing roughly 20% of all deliveries [7–9], and are associated with increased maternal and neonatal morbidity when compared to planned procedures [6, 9–12]. In twin pregnancies specifically, CD rates have risen substantially from 53 to 59% to approximately 75–84% in recent decades primarily driven by elective procedures without improved neonatal outcome [13–16]. This trend persists despite evidence supporting the safety of vaginal delivery (VD) in appropriate cases [17].

Among women with twin pregnancies undergoing a trial of labor (TOL), approximately 12–26% require intrapartum CD. Of these cases, 61–85% undergo CD for both twins, while the rest experience combined deliveries (VD of the first twin followed by CD of the second twin). Both scenarios are associated with increased maternal and neonatal morbidity) as opposed to elective CD [18–24].

Although factors associated with failed TOL among twin pregnancies have been extensively studied [18–22], data regarding these factors is still limited, demonstrated contrary findings, and often based on studies examining relatively small cohorts and a restricted number of predictive factors.

The primary objective of this investigation is to elucidate potential risk factors associated with intrapartum CD in twin pregnancies in a large population under a strict institutional protocol .

Methods

Study design

We conducted a retrospective cohort study at two affiliated tertiary care centers in Jerusalem: Shaare Zedek Medical Center and the former Bikur Holim Hospital. The analysis utilized data extracted from electronic medical records spanning the years 2005 through 2024.

Study population

Women were eligible for the study if they had a twin pregnancy between 32 weeks 0 days and 40 weeks 6 days of gestation who underwent TOL while the first twin was in the cephalic presentation, and both fetuses were alive with an estimated weight between 1500 g and 4000 g between 2005 and 2024.

Exclusion criteria were: monoamniotic twins, early fetal reduction from higher-order multiple pregnancy, lethal fetal anomaly, known genetic abnormality, contraindication to VD (e.g., fetal compromise, second twin substantially larger than the first twin, fetal anomaly or condition that might cause mechanical problems at delivery, and previous vertical uterine incision or more than one previous low-segment CD).

Twins vaginal birth departmental protocols

In our institutions, women with diamniotic twin pregnancies are offered TOL starting at 32 weeks if the first twin presents in vertex position, independent of second twin presentation. Counseling is provided to review the risks and benefits. Fetal position and estimated weights are assessed by ultrasound on admission. TOL is generally discouraged if the second twin is estimated to weigh more than 20% above the first. Continuous cardiotocographic monitoring of both fetuses is standard, and epidural analgesia is routinely used. A senior obstetrician trained in breech extraction and emergency cesarean delivery is present throughout active labor, supported by a multidisciplinary team including residents, midwives, and a pediatrician. Labor induction methods included mechanical (Foley catheter) and pharmacological (prostaglandin E2) approaches according to physician discretion; however, the induction method was not consistently recorded and thus could not be stratified in the analysis. The decision to perform an intrapartum CD was made by the attending obstetrician in consultation with the on-call senior obstetrician, based on non-reassuring fetal heart rate tracings of one or both twins, inappropriate labor progress, arrest of descent in the second stage of labor or a maternal indication that precludes vaginal delivery (such as significant bleeding with hemodynamic compromise). The decision is based on clinical judgment according to their professional assessment, combined with the maternal decision. Following the birth of the first twin, the presentation of the second is confirmed by both clinical and sonographic examination. Whether breech or transverse, total breech extraction is promptly performed. If vertex, labor is augmented to minimize the intertwin delivery interval, often with oxytocin administration and artificial rupture of membranes. Neonatal assessments are performed immediately by the attending pediatrician [25].

Outcomes

Those who had unsuccessful TOL that resulted in an intrapartum CD were defined as cases and those with successful TOL were defined as controls. These women were identified based on their medical records, specifically by reviewing their intake, hospitalization, and surgical summaries.

The maternal and neonatal adverse outcomes were also reported.

Adverse maternal outcomes included: Chorioamnionitis, maternal Intensive Care Unit (ICU) admissions, early post-partum hemorrhage (PPH, defined as an estimated blood loss exceeding 1000 mL following vaginal or cesarean delivery, in accordance with the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG) definition [26], Puerperal fever and prolonged postpartum hospital stay (> 5 days for VDs and > 7 days for CD). Adverse neonatal outcomes were reported when the outcome occurred in at least one of the twins and encompassed 5-minute Apgar score below 7, admission to the Neonatal Intensive Care Unit (NICU), neonatal asphyxia, mechanical ventilation, meconium aspiration, jaundice that required phototherapy, transient tachypnea of the newborn (TTN), seizures, hypoglycemia, sepsis, encephalopathy, and intracranial hemorrhage.

Statistical methods

Sample size was estimated to detect a clinically meaningful difference in intrapartum cesarean rates between nulliparous and multiparous women, assuming rates of 15.9% and 6.7%, respectively [21]. To achieve 80% power with α = 0.05 (one-sided), at least 208 subjects were required in each group.

Continuous data were assessed for normality and expressed as means ± SD or medians with interquartile ranges. Group comparisons used t-tests or Mann–Whitney U tests as appropriate. Categorical variables were analyzed using chi-square or Fisher’s exact tests.

Multivariable logistic regression was applied to identify independent predictors of intrapartum cesarean delivery. Variables significant in univariate analysis were included in the model, with results presented as adjusted odds ratios (aOR) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs).

All statistical procedures were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics version 25 (Armonk, NY), and significance was set at p < 0.05.

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software (version 25, IBM, Armonk, NY).

Ethical aspects

The study protocol was approved by the institutional review boards of both participating centers (approval number SZMC-0088-24) in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Given the retrospective nature of the analysis and de-identification of all patient data, informed consent was waived by the Institutional Review Board. No external funding was received.

Results

During the study period, there were 5,530 twin deliveries conducted at the centers included in the study. Of these, 2,730 women who underwent a trial of labor and met the inclusion criteria. The double VD success rate was 93.5% (n = 2,553), while 6.5% (n = 177) required an intrapartum CD; 15.3% (88/574) among primiparous women and 4.6% (76/1667) among multiparous women and 2.7% (13/489) among grand-multiparous women. Discussion

Principal findings

This multicenter retrospective cohort study identified primiparity, LGA presenting twin, maternal obesity (BMI > 30), and minimal cervical dilation (< 3 cm) at admission as significant independent risk factors for intrapartum CD in twin pregnancies undergoing a TOL. Notably, primiparity emerged as the strongest predictor in the multivariate analysis, underscoring its critical role in delivery outcomes for twin gestations. Among the indications for intrapartum CD, suspected fetal distress and labor dystocia were the most prevalent. Intrapartum CD was associated with adverse maternal and neonatal outcomes including prolonged maternal hospital stay, postpartum hemorrhage, puerperal fever, 5-Minute Apgar score < 7, meconium aspiration syndrome, TTN, mechanical ventilation, encephalopathy and birth asphyxia.

Results in the context of what is known

In this current study, we found intrapartum CD rate of 6.5% among twin pregnancies underwent TOL, which is notably lower than the rates reported in existing literature, which range from 11.7% to 25.2% [18–22].

Several factors may account for this marked discrepancy. One potential factor is the predominance of multiparous and grand-multiparous women in our cohort. Notably, only 21.2% of our cohort consisted of primiparous women, compared to an average of 44.4% in comparative studies [18–22]. Parity has a significant impact on intrapartum CD rates, with the rate for primiparous women in our cohort being approximately 15.3% (which is consistent with rates observed in other studies), compared to around 5% for multiparous women. Another contributing factor may be demographic distinctions. Our patient population included a substantial proportion of ultra-Orthodox Jewish women, a group characterized by unique obstetric profiles, including a preference for larger families and a tendency to avoid CDs [27–29]. This cultural factor may influence both maternal and physician preferences and decision-making regarding the mode of delivery.

Our analysis identified four factors significantly associated with intrapartum CD in twin pregnancies. Our findings align with substantial evidence identifying primiparity as a significant determinant of intrapartum CD in twin gestations [18–22]. Specifically, Aviram et al. secondary analysis of the Twin Birth Study, developed a predictive model for mode of delivery that included primiparity as a key variable [18].

The observed association between an LGA presenting twin and increased intrapartum CD risk is also consistent with prior research. Fetal macrosomia in the presenting twin can contribute to mechanical dystocia and an elevated likelihood of surgical intervention [24].

The observed correlation between increased maternal BMI and intrapartum CD is consistent with established literature that documents heightened risk of surgical delivery in both singleton and twin gestations among women with obesity [21]. The elevated incidence of labor complications in obese patients may contribute to CD necessity due to protracted labor and non-reassuring fetal status. Our findings reinforce this association specifically within the context of twin pregnancies undergoing TOL.

The finding that minimal cervical dilation (< 3 cm) upon admission is predictive of intrapartum CD is congruent with the understanding that prolonged or arrested labor progression is a common indication for surgical intervention. These novel findings suggest that fetal size and the degree of cervical dilation at admission may play critical roles in determining the mode of delivery in twin pregnancies. This relationship has previously been documented in singleton pregnancies [30].

It is noteworthy that while non-cephalic presentation of the second twin and labor induction have been reported as predictors of intrapartum CD in some studies, including Schachter-Safrai et al. [21], these variables did not retain statistical significance in our multivariate model. This divergence from previous findings highlights the complex interplay of factors influencing delivery outcomes in twin pregnancies. Easter et al., for instance, presented divergent findings regarding the impact of fetal presentation on VD success [21]. In our cohort, non-vertex presentation of the second twin did not predict intrapartum CD. This likely reflects to our protocol that was made to select appropriate candidates for twins’ vaginal delivery, as well as to the institutional policy requiring that a senior obstetrician experienced in breech extraction and internal podalic version be present throughout active labor and at the delivery of the second twin. The routine readiness for operative maneuvers likely mitigated the impact of non-vertex presentation on delivery outcomes.

Furthermore, while de Castro et al. identified previous CD as a significant predictor [21], our analysis showed no statistically significant difference between groups.

Additionally, although Barg et al. demonstrated that birthweight discordance exceeding 20% was a significant risk factor [21], our study found no statistical significance even at a higher threshold of 25% discordance. While an absolute birthweight difference > 500 g between twins showed initial significance in univariate analysis, subsequent multivariate analysis revealed this association to be attributable to confounding factors rather than representing an independent relationship.

Similarly, existing literature presents conflicting evidence on the role of labor induction in intrapartum CD. While Aviram et al. and Barg et al. identified labor induction as a risk factor [18, 22], de Castro et al. found that only Foley catheter induction was associated with increased risk, with no significant difference observed for induction overall [21]. Conversely, Easter et al. reported that labor induction may function as a protective factor and a predictor of VD [24]. In our study, labor induction was significantly more frequent in intrapartum CDs than in successful VDs in the univariate analysis. However, this association did not persist in the multivariate analysis. Due to the lack of data specifying whether induction was performed using a Foley catheter, further conclusions regarding its role as a risk factor cannot be drawn.

These seemingly contradictory findings underscore the need to consider multiple interrelated variables when assessing risk and may also reflect the influence of differing institutional protocols and levels of provider experience, as suggested by de Castro et al. [21, 22] The study design differences (retrospective vs. prospective) and study populations may also account for these conflicting results.

Clinical implications

The identification of primiparity, a LGA presenting twin, maternal obesity, and minimal cervical dilation at admission as key risk factors for intrapartum CD has important clinical implications for the management of twin pregnancies.

These findings contribute to improved risk stratification and may aid clinicians in identifying women with twin pregnancies who are at an elevated risk of intrapartum CD, allowing for tailored management strategies. By incorporating these factors into clinical decision-making, healthcare providers can offer patients realistic expectations regarding the likelihood of a successful VD based on their individual risk profiles, thereby facilitating informed shared decision-making.

However, further research is necessary to validate these findings in diverse populations and to develop clinical prediction models capable of accurately estimating the risk of intrapartum CD. Until validated predictive models are available, these risk factors should be considered as part of a comprehensive clinical assessment rather than serving as definitive determinants of delivery outcomes.

Research implications

Our findings highlight several critical areas requiring further investigation. Future research should focus on developing more precise predictive models incorporating the identified risk factors to estimate VD success probability in twin pregnancies. Machine learning approaches may offer enhanced predictive capability for these complex cases.

As the optimal management strategies for twin pregnancies remain unclear, randomized controlled trials comparing interventions such as early labor induction versus planned CD are needed to provide valuable evidence for guiding clinical practice. Similarly, further studies are also needed to evaluate the comparative effectiveness of various labor induction methods on intrapartum CD risk specifically in twin gestations.

These research directions would address significant knowledge gaps and potentially improve maternal and neonatal outcomes in this high-risk obstetric population.

Strengths and limitations

This study offers several notable strengths. The investigation benefits from a relatively large sample size, enhancing statistical power and reliability of the findings. Additionally, our application of multivariate analysis enabled identification of independently associated factors while controlling for potential confounders. The focused examination of women with twin pregnancies attempting VD provides valuable insights for this specific clinical scenario. Furthermore, the concordance of our findings with previous research investigating risk factors for intrapartum CD strengthens the validity of our conclusions. Importantly, our analysis offers more comprehensive and detailed examination of risk factors in this population compared to existing literature.

Several limitations should be acknowledged when interpreting these results. The retrospective design inherently introduces potential for selection and information biases that may affect the findings. This also leads to incomplete information on certain issues. For example, incomplete documentation of induction methods. As Foley catheter and prostaglandin induction were not systematically recorded, subgroup analysis by induction method could not be performed. This may limit interpretation of the observed associations.

Moreover, the unique patient demographic at our medical center—characterized by relatively low rates of primiparity and a substantial ultra-Orthodox Jewish who tend towards large families and prefer VD over CD, may influence both the physicians’ and patients’ preferences regarding the mode of delivery. This might limit the generalizability of our study to other settings. Finally, despite statistical adjustment for key variables, unmeasured confounders including physician decision-making processes, variations in provider experience, and undocumented maternal health conditions may have influenced outcomes in ways not captured by our analysis.

Conclusions

Primiparity, LGA presenting twin, maternal obesity, and minimal cervical dilation at admission are independently associated with intrapartum CD in twin pregnancies attempting VD. These findings can be used to improve risk stratification, inform patient counseling, and optimize resource allocation. This refined stratification methodology has the potential to optimize both maternal and neonatal outcomes in twin gestations through the prevention of emergency scenarios and their associated complications.

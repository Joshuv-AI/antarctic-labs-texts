A prognostic index integrating deep learning baseline PET/CT biomarkers and multi-omics profiling in diffuse large B cell lymphoma
Authors: Yue Wang, Xue Wang, Xin-Yun Huang, Hong-Mei Jing, Song-Fu Jiang, He Li, Rong-Ji Mu, Qing Shi, Di Fu, Zhuo-Han Li, Hong-Mei Yi, Bin-Shen Ouyang, Biao Li, Fu-Hua Yan, Ting Niu, Shu Cheng, Li Wang, Ning Wen, Peng-Peng Xu, Wei-Li Zhao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Summary[18F]-Fluorodeoxyglucose (FDG)-positron emission tomography (PET)/computed tomography (CT) is essential for disease staging and treatment response evaluation in diffuse large B cell lymphoma (DLBCL). In this study, we analyze 18F-FDG-PET scans from 1,024 newly diagnosed DLBCL patients, integrating with DNA and RNA sequencing data. Using the nnUNet deep learning framework and training on both AutoPET public and in-house datasets, we identify key baseline biomarkers—including total metabolic tumor volume (TMTV), Max MTV, and the standardized tumor dissemination biomarker—that demonstrate significant prognostic value. Further integrating PET biomarkers with clinical factors and LymphPlex genetic subtypes, we develop high TMTV, elevated lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), and EZB-like MYC+, MCD-like, and TP53Mut subtypes as risk factors to form the ClinicalPET LymphPlex model, efficiently distinguishing patient outcomes across different treatments. Notably, high TMTV correlates with an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment, while elevated LDH is linked to increased metabolic activity and tumor proliferation. Collectively, our findings necessitate multimodal integration to enhance prognostic precision and advance personalized therapy in DLBCL.

Introduction

Diffuse large B cell lymphoma (DLBCL) is the most prevalent subtype of aggressive lymphoma, characterized by significant clinical and molecular heterogeneity.1 Approximately 20%–50% of patients with DLBCL exhibit resistance to standard immunochemotherapy or experience relapse after achieving complete response, emphasizing the necessity of patient stratification.1 Although clinical prognostic scoring systems like the International Prognostic Index (IPI), revised IPI (R-IPI), and National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN)-IPI have proven efficient in risk stratification with standard immunochemotherapy as rituximab plus cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, and prednisone (R-CHOP), their limitations in accurately identifying high-risk patients persist.2 Additionally, the prognostic relevance of these systems in the context of emerging novel therapies remains unclear, such as novel targeted agents plus R-CHOP (R-CHOP-X) therapy.3

Advances in sequencing technologies reveal molecular heterogeneity as a vital factor in identifying high-risk patients with DLBCL.4,5,6 The LymphPlex is a simplified algorithm by using patterns of co-occurring gene alterations to categorize genetic subtypes, which illustrated high consistency with the LymphGen algorithm.5 Based on mutations of 35 genes and rearrangements of three genes (BCL2, BCL6, and MYC), seven genetic subtypes with variable prognosis could be identified (namely MCD-like, BN2-like, N1-like, EZB-like-MYC+, EZB-like-MYC-, ST2-like, and TP53Mut), providing information for biological prognostic scoring system.7,8

18F-FDG-PET/CT is integral for the diagnosis and evaluation of treatment response in DLBCL.9 The exploration of quantitative biomarkers from routine 18F-FDG-PET scans, like baseline total metabolic tumor volume (TMTV) and standardized maximum distance between any two lesions (SDmax_patient), representing overall tumor burden and individual maximum tumor dissemination, has demonstrated independent prognostic significance in DLBCL.10,11 However, their clinical utility is hindered by the complexity of whole-body 18F-FDG-PET image delineation and observer variability. Leveraging deep learning for precise lesion segmentation and biomarker extraction from 18F-FDG-PET scans can mitigate these challenges, strengthening its potential for routine clinical practice.12

This study applied advanced deep learning techniques to automate lesion segmentation and comprehensive PET biomarker extraction, aiming to assess their prognostic value in DLBCL. By further integrating these PET biomarkers with clinical risk factors and molecular signatures, we developed and validated the ClinicalPET LymphPlex prognostic index, which demonstrated robust prognostic value across various treatments. Though LymphPlex was employed in this study, robust classification of DLBCL will be further achieved with incorporating copy-number aberrations (CNAs).4,5,6,13 Additionally, by correlating the risk factors with transcriptomic data, we elucidated the biological basis of this index, providing better understanding for precise prognostication and the development of personalized treatment strategies in DLBCL.

Results

Dataset characteristics and study design

From May 2013 to December 2023, 1,024 newly diagnosed DLBCL patients with baseline 18F-FDG-PET/CT scans were enrolled, with patient characteristics detailed in Table S1. According to the IPI, 41.8% of patients were classified as low risk, 19.9% as low-intermediate risk, 20.8% as high-intermediate risk, and 17.5% as high risk. The cohort included 61.5% non-germinal center B cell (GCB) subtype and 31.6% BCL-2/MYC double-expressing patients. Among them, 774 received R-CHOP-based immunochemotherapy, 90 received R-CHOP-X,3 and 81 received other treatments, including chemo-free regimens (rituximab, lenalidomide with ibrutinib, or zanubrutinib)14 and dose-adjusted DA-EPOCH-R regimens (etoposide, prednisone, vincristine, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, and rituximab). Seventy-nine patients lacked available treatment or survival data.

The study cohorts and structure were outlined in Figure 1A. The positron emission tomography (PET) ground truth cohort included 524 patients with manually delineated lesions from baseline 18F-FDG-PET/CT scans, which were used for the development, testing, and validation of a deep learning model for DLBCL segmentation. This cohort was consisted of 200 patients from Ruijin Hospital for model training (PET ground truth internal training cohort), 51 patients from Ruijin Hospital for testing (PET ground truth internal test cohort), and 79 patients from other centers, as well as 194 patients from Peking University Third Hospital and The First Affiliated Hospital of Wenzhou Medical University for external validation (PET ground truth external cohort). Patient characteristics were detailed in Table S2. No significant differences in baseline IPI were observed between the internal training and test cohorts and the external cohort. The second cohort, a survival cohort of 864 patients who received either R-CHOP-based or R-CHOP-X regimens and possessing DNA sequencing data, was used for prognostic analysis. Among them, 774 patients (580 from Ruijin Hospital, forming the R-CHOP-based internal cohort, and 194 from Peking University Third Hospital and The First Affiliated Hospital of Wenzhou Medical University, forming the R-CHOP-based external cohort) were used to assess the prognostic significance of PET biomarkers. Baseline IPI did not differ significantly between these internal and external cohorts (Table 1). Along with 90 patients receiving R-CHOP-X treatment (R-CHOP-X cohort), the survival cohort contributed to the development and validation of the ClinicalPET LymphPlex prognostic index. The study was conducted in three steps (Figure 1A), which are as follows. (1) Development, testing, and validation of a deep learning model for DLBCL segmentation using the PET ground truth cohort, focusing on lesion segmentation accuracy and the consistency of automated PET biomarker extraction with manual annotations. Prognostic analysis of PET biomarkers was conducted in the R-CHOP-based survival cohort (n = 774). (2) Integration of these prognostic PET biomarkers, clinical risk factors, and LymphPlex genetic subtypes to develop and validate the ClinicalPET LymphPlex prognostic index. (3) Analysis of the relationship between the ClinicalPET LymphPlex prognostic index and microenvironment subtypes in the PET multi-omics cohort, aimed at identifying biological signatures associated with the index.

Part 2: Verification of AI-predicted PET/CT biomarker consistency with manual measurements using LIFEx

The agreement between ground truth PET biomarkers (gtPETs) and AI-predicted PET biomarkers (pPETs) was evaluated for several PET biomarkers, including TMTV, total lesion glycolysis (TLG), maximum metabolic target volume (MAX_MTV), SDmax_patient, standardized maximum distance between the largest lesion and any other lesion (SDmax_bulk), and SUVmax, across the internal (n = 251) and external (n = 273) cohorts. Some examples of pTMTV and gtTMTV from patients in the external cohorts were shown in Figure S1. As shown in Table S3, TMTV and TLG demonstrated excellent concordance, with intra-class correlation coefficients (ICCs) of 0.978 and 0.996 in the internal cohort and 0.940 and 0.989 in the external cohort, respectively. Other biomarkers such as MAX_MTV, SDmax_patient, SDmax_bulk, and SUVmax also exhibited strong agreement, with ICCs of 0.967, 0.850, 0.817, and 0.937 in the internal cohort and 0.907, 0.842, 0.806, and 0.970 in the external cohort, respectively.

Additionally, correlation analyses (Figure S2) showed a strong positive correlation between gtTMTV and pTMTV in both cohorts (R2 = 0.91, p < 0.001 for the internal cohort; R2 = 0.96, p < 0.001 for the external cohort). Similar correlations were observed for other biomarkers, further confirming the consistency across cohorts.

Patient characteristics between R-CHOP-based internal and external cohorts were outlined in Table 1.

Integration of PET, clinical, and genetic biomarkers to develop the ClinicalPET LymphPlex prognostic index

Part 1: Development of the ClinicalPET LymphPlex prognostic index in the R-CHOP-based internal cohort (n = 580)

Given the substantial prognostic significance of PET biomarkers (pTMTV, pMAX_MTV, pSDmax_patient, and pSDmax_bulk), integrating them with clinical and molecular variables was essential. Spearman correlation analysis in the R-CHOP-based cohort of 580 patients highlighted that pTMTV displayed stronger correlations with pMAX_MTV, while the same trend was observed in pSDmax_patient and pSDmax_bulk (Figure S4).

Given the significant prognostic value of pTMTV and pSDmax_patient in both internal and external cohorts, and the 83.4% specificity between the LymphPlex and LymphGen algorithms (Figure S5), we integrated them with IPI risk factors to develop a prognostic index. Lasso regression was used to predict risk factors for PFS and identified 11 factors with nonzero coefficients for inclusion (Figures 3A, S6A, and S6B). Subsequently, a multivariable Cox proportional hazard (PH) regression model underscored that high pTMTV, an elevated lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) ratio, EZB-like-MYC+ subtype, MCD-like subtype, and TP53Mut subtype were significant prognostic factors (Figure 3A).

The prognostic score was derived by assigning weights to each variable, based on their relative importance as determined by their z-Wald values in the Cox PH model (Table 3). The Z score of each factor was normalized by the minimum Z score observed (elevated LDH ratio, considered as reference) to calculate the ratio. Finally, the weights were obtained rounding the ratio, and the score was the sum of weights.15 We thus developed a prognostic index named ClinicalPET LymphPlex, with scores ranging from 0 to 5, demonstrating a strong correlation with PFS and OS, as evidenced in Figures S7A and S7B. Patients were stratified into four main risk groups: low risk (score 0, 32.6% of patients), low-intermediate risk (score 1–2, 30.5% of patients), high-intermediate risk (score 3, 24.3% of patients), and high risk (score 4–5, 12.6% of patients). Notably, the estimated 5-year PFS and OS rates were significantly higher in the low-risk group (85.6% and 93.6%) than the low-intermediate-risk (65.6% and 79.2%), high-intermediate-risk (47.8% and 59.5%), and high-risk (21.8% and 50.8%) groups (p < 0.001 for all comparisons) (Figures 3B and S8A). Furthermore, compared with IPI and International Metabolic Prognostic Index (IMPI), ClinicalPET LymphPlex demonstrated superior discrimination between the high-risk and low-risk groups (Figures 3B and S8A–S8C), achieving a higher c-index for PFS (0.719 vs. 0.687 and 0.703, respectively) and better model fit indicated by lower Akaike’s information criterion (AIC) values (2,144.519 vs. 2,174.059 and 2,144.799, respectively). Part 2: Validation of the prognostic significance of the ClinicalPET LymphPlex in the external R-CHOP-based (n = 194) and R-CHOP-X (n = 90) cohorts

The prognostic value of the ClinicalPET LymphPlex was validated in two independent cohorts: 194 patients treated with R-CHOP-based immunochemotherapy (R-CHOP-based external cohort) and 90 patients receiving targeted agents plus R-CHOP (R-CHOP-X cohort). In the R-CHOP-based external cohort, the ClinicalPET LymphPlex prognostic index outperformed both IPI and IMPI in effectively distinguishing true high- and low-risk patients (Figure 3C; Figures S8D–S8F). In the R-CHOP-X cohort, unlike the IPI, which failed to stratify patients into distinct risk groups (p = 0.146 and 0.124), both ClinicalPET LymphPlex and IMPI identified high-risk patients with significantly poorer outcomes. Notably, ClinicalPET LymphPlex demonstrated superior identification of low-risk patients, with higher 5-year PFS (83.8% vs. 81.2%) and OS rates (95.5% vs. 84.1%) compared to IMPI (Figure 3D; Figures S8G–S8I).

Furthermore, ClinicalPET LymphPlex achieved higher c-index values for PFS and OS than both IPI and IMPI in the R-CHOP-based external cohort (PFS: 0.703 vs. 0.680 and 0.650; OS: 0.702 vs. 0.693 and 0.659), supported by superior model fit with lower AIC values (PFS: 497.293 vs. 509.431 and 514.992; OS: 298.315 vs. 306.475 and 310.423).

This advantage was consistently observed in the R-CHOP-X cohort, with corresponding c-index values of 0.697, 0.631, and 0.675 for PFS and 0.690, 0.615, and 0.634 for OS, respectively (Table S5).

We further explored the pathways enriched in the ClinicalPET LymphPlex prognostic index through gene set enrichment analysis. In patients with high pTMTV, immunosuppressive-related pathways were significantly upregulated, including the Toll-like receptor pathway (normalized expression signatures [NES] = 1.76, p < 0.001), negative regulation of T cell proliferation (NES = 1.70, p = 0.001), and negative regulation of T cell activation (NES = 1.68, p < 0.001). Other pathways involving immune cells within the tumor microenvironment (TME) were also upregulated, such as Th1 and Th2 differentiation (NES = 1.70, p < 0.001), macrophage activation (NES = 1.64, p = 0.001), and Th17 differentiation (NES = 1.54, p = 0.005) (Figure 4C). These findings aligned with TME analysis using the EcoTyper, showing that high pTMTV was associated with increased monocytes/macrophages (S3, p = 0.001) and CD4+T cells (S2, p < 0.001) (Figure 4D). Meanwhile, elevated LDH was linked to increased tumor cell proliferation, reflected by upregulated DNA replication (NES = 2.17, p < 0.001), G2/M checkpoints (NES = 1.77, p = 0.010), and cell cycle progression (NES = 1.94, p < 0.001). Dysregulated metabolism was also evident, with upregulation in the first mechanistic target of rapamycin complexes (mTORC1)-mediated signaling (NES = 1.79, p = 0.002) and oxidative phosphorylation (NES = 1.77, p < 0.001) (Figure 4E). Additionally, a pathway related to neutrophil-mediated immunosuppressive TME, namely neutrophil extracellular trap formation, was upregulated (NES = 1.96, p < 0.001), consistent with lower neutrophil abundance observed by the EcoTyper (S2, p = 0.024). Furthermore, B cells (S4, p = 0.006) and CD4+T cells (S2, p = 0.014), both associated with poor prognosis, were enriched in the high-LDH group (Figure 4F).

We also validated the biological signatures of genetic subtypes within the ClinicalPET LymphPlex model, focusing on EZB-like-MYC+, MCD-like, and TP53Mut subtypes (Figure 4G). Consistent with our previous studies,3,7 the EZB-like-MYC+ subtype showed dominant MYC oncoprotein upregulation (MYCUp-4, p = 0.025) and high expression of genes induced by OCT2 (OCT2Up-2, p = 0.036) and TCF3 (TCFUp-3, p = 0.042) but lacked ABC-DLBCL signatures (ABC-DLBCL-3, p = 0.021). The MCD-like subtype prominently activated nuclear factor κB (NF-κB) oncogenic pathways (NF-κB-10, p = 0.017) and expressed high MYC oncoprotein levels (MYCUp-4, p = 0.002), cell proliferation markers (Prolif-11, p = 0.010), ABC-DLBCL signatures (ABC-DLBCL-3, p < 0.001), and IRF4-induced genes (IRF4Up-7, p = 0.001) but lacked GCB-DLBCL signatures (GCB-DLBCL-3, p < 0.001). The TP53Mut subtype was marked by p53 signaling dysregulation (P53up-1, p < 0.001), elevated lipid synthesis (Lipid-1, p < 0.001), high MYC oncoprotein levels (MYCUp-4, p = 0.003), and phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K) activation (PI3K-1, p = 0.034), along with low quiescence signature expression (Quiescence-2, p = 0.005).

Discussion

Our research introduces a deep learning model for PET/CT segmentation in DLBCL, demonstrating its accuracy in autonomously extracting prognostic PET biomarkers. Further integrating PET biomarkers with clinical data and genetic subtypes, we developed a prognostic index, the ClinicalPET LymphPlex, which consistently outperforms IPI across various treatments. We elucidated the biological signatures of the individual risk factors of this index, providing clinical rationale for future targeted approaches in DLBCL.

The clinical use of 18F-FDG-PET/CT in DLBCL management has often been complicated by the complexity and variability. The integration of deep learning-based lesion segmentation and automated biomarkers extraction offers a promising solution to this challenge.12 In this study, we utilized the U-Net architecture for lesion segmentation, pre-training on the AutoPET dataset and refining it on our own dataset,19 achieving a Dice score of 84%–91%, which represents a 6%–31% improvement over previous studies.20,21,22 These advancements facilitated the precise, automated extraction of comprehensive PET biomarkers, including TMTV and SDmax, which demonstrated significant prognostic value in patients with DLBCL who received R-CHOP-based immunochemotherapy.17,20

Genetic subtypes, along with clinical parameters and PET biomarkers, provide distinct prognostic insights, and their integration markedly improves stratification accuracy.23 Building on this foundation, we innovatively integrated multi-omics biomarkers to develop the ClinicalPET LymphPlex prognostic index, which incorporates TMTV; LDH; and the EZB-like-MYC+, MCD-like, and TP53Mut subtypes as risk factors, accurately stratifying patients into four risk groups. Compared to the IPI, the ClinicalPET LymphPlex prognostic index exhibited superior AIC and c-index, especially in the identification of ultra-high-risk patients (2-year PFS 38.5% in ClinicalPET LymphPlex vs. 47.5% in IPI). Additionally, compared with IMPI, ClinicalPET LymphPlex also showed relatively high performance in both c-index and the identification of ultra-low-risk patients, achieving a 2-year PFS of 91.6%, in contrast to 88.6% with IMPI.11

Moreover, in the context of novel targeted approaches, such as genetic subtype-guided immunochemotherapy R-CHOP-X regimens, precise risk stratification is essential for the effective implementation of the treatment. Indeed, the prognostic value of IPI was diminished in patients treated with R-CHOP-X but consistently outperformed by the ClinicalPET LymphPlex prognostic index to accurately categorize patients at ultra-high and ultra-low risk. The biological signatures identified through the LymphPlex model aligned with previous findings3,7: the EZB-like-MYC+ subtype predominantly upregulated MYC oncoprotein; the MCD-like subtype was linked to an ABC origin, MYC overexpression, and NF-κB activation; and the TP53Mut subtype was characterized by p53 signaling dysregulation, increased lipid synthesis, and PI3K activation. Built on these insights, the addition of histone deacetylase inhibitor, Bruton kinase inhibitors, and demethylating agent to R-CHOP has shown increased clinical efficacy against EZB-like, MCD-like, and TP53Mut subtypes, accompanied with partial modulation of the immunosuppressive TME.3 Although the prognostic significance of these genetic subtypes is partially overcome by the addition of novel targeted agents, the risk factors extracted from baseline PET images and clinical data, as TMTV and LDH, still exhibited prognostic role in the model, with the underlying mechanism as immune evasion,24,25 metabolic alterations,26 and tumor cell proliferation.27 These biological characteristics of TMTV and LDH remained as unmet needs not fully addressed by current R-CHOP-X regimens3,7 but could be potential targets for CD3/CD20 bispecific antibodies,28 metabolic regulators (mTORC1 inhibitors), and cell-cycle inhibitors (cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitors) or preemptive CAR-T interventions.

In conclusion, the DLBCL segmentation model demonstrated strong performance across datasets from diverse sources, enabling precise and automated extraction of comprehensive semantic 18F-FDG-PET biomarkers. Integrating clinical parameters and multi-omics biomarkers into the ClinicalPET LymphPlex prognostic index not only enhanced the identification of high-risk patients across various treatments but also provided guidance for mechanism-based targeted treatment strategies in DLBCL.

Limitations of the study

Despite the promising findings of our study demonstrating that ClinicalPET LymphPlex outperforms IPI and IMPI in prognostic significance, particularly in patients receiving the R-CHOP-X regimen where the prognostic value of IPI is limited, several important limitations should be considered. First, the relatively small sample size of the patients receiving novel targeted agents in combination with R-CHOP (R-CHOP-X)3 necessitates further validation in larger, independent cohorts to ensure the robustness of our findings. As the role of CNAs in DLBCL classification has been documented in several studies,4,5,6,13 incorporating CNAs into molecular subtyping algorithms, along with clinical parameters and imaging framework, will further enhance the precision of risk stratification in DLBCL. Additionally, the identification of the EZB-MYC+ risk factor within the ClinicalPET LymphPlex model was based on a limited sample size in the training cohort, meaning these findings should be interpreted cautiously and require further validation in broader datasets. Moreover, while ClinicalPET LymphPlex demonstrates potential in identifying ultra-high and ultra-low-risk patients, the accuracy of these classifications needs improvement. Future studies should focus on improving the precision of risk stratification to enhance the clinical applicability of this prognostic index.

Experimental model and subject details

Patient selection

From May 2013 to December 2023, a total of 1024 newly diagnosed DLBCL patients with baseline 18F-FDG-PET/CT scans were enrolled. Among these, 751 patients were from Ruijin Hospital, Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine, 194 patients were from Peking University Third Hospital and The First Affiliated Hospital of Wenzhou Medical University, and the remaining 79 patients were from other centers. Histological diagnoses were confirmed according to the World Health Organization classification.37 The cohort included 947 patients with DLBCL not otherwise specified (DLBCL-NOS), 21 patients with high-grade B-cell lymphoma with MYC and BCL2 and/or BCL6 rearrangements (HGBL-DH/TH), and 56 patients with transformed DLBCL from indolent B-cell lymphomas. Patients with primary central nervous system lymphoma (CNS lymphoma), primary mediastinal large B-cell lymphoma (PMBCL), and other specific large B-cell lymphoma subtypes were excluded. Manual ground truth lesion delineation was performed on 524 patients. Survival analyses included 864 patients treated with R-CHOP-based immunochemotherapy (n = 774) or targeted agents plus R-CHOP (R-CHOP-X, n = 90), excluding 79 patients without treatment or survival data, and 81 receiving other regimens. DNA-sequencing was performed on these 864 patients, comprising 56 whole-genome sequencing (WGS), 86 whole-exome sequencing (WES), and 722 targeted sequencing of 55 lymphoma-related genes. Additionally, RNA-sequencing was completed for 307 patients. The study was approved by the Shanghai Ruijin Hospital Review Board, and informed consent was obtained in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Method details

Image acquisition of 18F-FDG-PET/CT

Baseline 18F-FDG-PET/CT scans were obtained from Ruijin Hospital and other institutions in DICOM format. Adhering to the quality control standards set by the European Association of Nuclear Medicine (EANM), scans were considered acceptable if the mean standardized uptake value (SUVmean) of the liver ranged between 1.3 and 3.0, and plasma glucose levels were below 11 mmol/L.38 Scans were excluded for the following reasons: (1) incompleteness of whole body 18F-FDG-PET/CT scans, (2) absence of FDG-avid lesions, (3) presence of concurrent secondary FDG-avid malignancies, and (4) deviations from EANM’s hepatic SUVmean and plasma glucose guidelines.

Ground truth generation

For ground truth establishment, 524 baseline 18F-FDG-PET scans underwent evaluation by two nuclear medicine experts (Xin-Yun Huang and Zhuo-Han Li). Utilizing ITK-SNAP software (http://www.itksnap.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php) and initial clinical reports, FDG-avid lymphoma lesions were segmented to serve as the ground truth.39

Quantitative analysis of 18F-FDG-PET by LIFEx

Quantitative analysis was conducted on 524 18F-FDG-PET scans, employing the MTV protocol from LIFEx 7.3.0 (https://www.lifexsoft.org/).40 Lesions were delineated automatically by a threshold of more than 1.5 × liver mean plus 2 standard deviations (SDs), exceeding the PERCIST criteria (version 1.0) recommendation, and a minimum volume threshold of 0.5 cm3.41 Physiological uptakes, such as those in the brain, kidneys, and bladder, were manually excluded. Delineations were performed by a nuclear medicine physician (Zhuo-Han Li), and underwent a visual check by the experienced physician Xin-Yun Huang to ensure no lesions or physiological uptakes were overlooked. Based on these delineations, various biomarkers were derived for all patients, including TMTV, total lesion glycolysis (TLG), MAX_MTV, maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax), SDmax_patient, and SDmax_bulk.

Image preprocessing

Consistent with the AutoPET open-access dataset,19 all enrolled patients’ DICOM files were resampled to achieve consistent spatial resolution, with CT images specifically adjusted to match PET resolution, resulting in identical matrix dimensions. PET images were standardized by converting raw activity counts to standardized uptake values (SUV). The preprocessed PET/CT data were then converted to NIfTI format for subsequent analysis. Following the protocol from AutoPET challenge, resampled CT and SUV-normalized PET images served as dual-channel inputs. Segmentation masks were binary, where voxels labeled ‘1’ indicated lesion regions.

Architecture, training and validating of the segmentation model

Our methodology employs the nnUNet framework, a renowned deep learning platform for medical image segmentation, specifically tailored for the automated segmentation of PET lesions.42 We have tailed nnUNet for automated PET lesion segmentation in DLBCL. After pre-training the model on a public available dataset of 1014 studies of 900 patients from AutoPET challenge (https://github.com/uni-medical/STU-Net), we have refined the model by fine-tuning it on our distinct datasets with 18F-FDG-PET scans, dedicated specifically to DLBCL lesion segmentation. This refinement process was guided by the ground truth annotations to achieve optimal model performance.19

Drawing on the 3D full-resolution U-Net model detailed by Gatidis, S et al.,19 and depicted in Figure 1B, we processed paired multi-modal images, including PET volumes converted to SUV units and corresponding CT volumes that were resampled. The normalization was achieved using the nnUNet’s technique by subtracting the mean voxel value and dividing by the standard deviation, therefore standardizing the data for consistent analysis across different scanning conditions. Importantly, this process included a crucial step where tumor and non-tumor voxels were distinctly classified with binary values of 1 and 0, respectively.(1)Network architecture of 3D full res. Details of training and validating of the segmentation model

We subdivided the PET ground truth internal cohort (n = 251) into two distinct datasets: 200 annotated samples were earmarked for training purposes, while 51 annotated samples were designated for testing the model’s performance. The PET ground truth external cohort, consisting of 194 patients from Peking University Third Hospital and The First Affiliated Hospital of Wenzhou Medical University, along with 79 additional patients from multiple centers, was tested independently to further validate the robustness of our model.

The nnUNet model underwent training with a dual approach, utilizing both Dice and cross-entropy loss functions to effectively differentiate between tumor and non-tumor regions. We optimized the model through stochastic gradient descent, initiating with a learning rate of 0.01 and incorporating a Nesterov momentum of 0.99. The extensive training covered 5000 epochs, with each epoch consisting of 250 iterations. Throughout this period, the learning rate was methodically reduced, and data augmentation was dynamically applied. The computational framework for this process was supported by dedicated GPUs (NVIDIA Tesla A100 40 GB), with the workload distributed across five separate units. Each fold of the process took approximately 22 h to complete, ensuring a comprehensive and thorough training regimen.(1)Segmentation performance

Immunohistochemistry and fluorescence in situ hybridization analysis

Immunohistochemistry was performed on 5μm sections of paraffin-embedded tissue employing an indirect immunoperoxidase technique, utilizing antibodies targeting CD10, BCL6, MUM1, BCL2, and MYC. The classification of germinal center B-cell (GCB) or non-GCB origin was ascertained through the Hans algorithm, applying cutoff values of 30% for CD10, BCL6, and MUM1.43 with cutoff values of 30% for CD10, BCL6, and MUM1. In the case of BCL2/MYC double expressors, thresholds were established at 50% and 40%, respectively.44 Fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) analyses were conducted utilizing IGH/BCL2 (14q32/18q21) fusion probes and break-apart probes for BCL6 (3q27) and C-MYC (8q24), following the protocols provided by GP Medical Technologies, Ltd, Beijing, China. The translocations involving BCL2, BCL6, and MYC were evaluated in paraffin sections with the cutoff set at 10%.45,46

DNA-sequencing

Genomic DNA extraction was performed from frozen tumor specimens using a QIAamp DNA Mini Kit or from formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tumor specimens with a GeneRead DNA FFPE Tissue Kit, both supplied by Qiagen, Hilden, Germany, in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions. Whole exome capture was performed using the SeqCap EZ Human Exome kit (version 3.0) and sequenced on the HiSeq 4000 platform with a 150 bp paired-end protocol (Shanghai Rightongene Biotechnology Co., Ltd, Shanghai, China).

The whole-genome sequencing (WGS) library was quality-checked with the Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer and sequenced on the Illumina HiSeq platform at WuXi NextCODE (Shanghai, China) using the same read length and paired-end strategy.7 Copy number analyses of WGS/WES samples were conducted with CNVkit41 using recommended pipeline (https://cnvkit.readthedocs.io/en/stable/pipeline.html). GISTIC 2.0 (q < 0.1) was used to verify the.cnv files and determined the copy number profiles.32,47

Targeted sequencing utilized the MultipSeq Custom Panel (Shanghai Rightongene Biotechnology Co., Ltd, China), as previously reported.7,8 Raw sequencing data quality was assessed using FastQC (version 1.11.4, https://www.bioinformatics.babraham.ac.uk/projects/fastqc/). Paired-end reads from targeted sequencing were aligned to the GRCh37 human reference genome using BWA (version 0.5.9-tpx). Sequencing adapters and low-quality reads—specifically fragments with quality scores below 25 or shorter than 35 bp—were trimmed using Trimmomatic (version 3.6). BAM files were processed with Samtools (v0.1.18)34 and Picard (v1.93) for file handling and local realignment, followed by base quality recalibration and variant calling using GATK (v4.1.4.0). Coding region mutations were annotated using Annovar (version 2017-07-17),35 with variants filtered according to established criteria.45,46,48

LymphPlex algorithm for genetic subtyping

The LymphPlex algorithm was applied to paraffin samples, integrating mutation status information of 35 genes and rearrangements identified by targeted sequencing, in conjunction with FISH analysis of three genes (BCL2, BCL6, and MYC).7 A total of 904 patients with DNA-sequencing data was classified into genetic subtypes, including EZB-like (with subcategories EZB-like-MYC- and EZB-like-MYC+), ST2-like, BN2-like, TP53Mut, N1-like, MCD-like, or not otherwise specified (NOS) (https://github.com/difuSJTU/LymphPlex.git). The evaluation of Lymphgen subtypes was calculated on https://llmpp.nih.gov/lymphgen/index.php.

RNA-sequencing

RNA was extracted from frozen tumor tissues using Trizol and the RNeasy Mini Kit (Qiagen, Hilden, Germany). RNA quantity was measured with a Nanodrop spectrophotometer (NanoDrop Technologies, Wilmington, DE, USA), and RNA integrity was assessed using the RNA 6000 Nano Kit on an Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer (Agilent Technologies). RNA-sequencing was performed on 307 patients, all previously reported.7,31 Paired-end reads were pseudo-aligned to the GRCh38 transcriptome (Ensembl version 106) and quantified using Kallisto software (v0.46.0).33

The R package “sva” was utilized to remove batch effects. Raw counts were normalized, and differential gene expression was analyzed using the R package “limma” (v3.54.2). Log2-transformed TPM data (log2TPM) were processed with the R package “preprocessCore” (v1.58.0) for quantile normalization and further batch effect removal, enabling microenvironment classification and gene expression signature calculation.36

Lymphoma microenvironment, gene expression signatures and cell of origin

Lymphoma microenvironment (LME) categories were identified using tools described in previous studies (https://github.com/bostongene/lme).17 Additionally, the EcoTyper state (https://ecotyper.stanford.edu/lymphoma/) was also analyzed for microenvironment components.18 Gene expression signatures from SignatureDB (https://gdc.cancer.gov/about-data/publications/DLBCL-2018) were calculated using the ssGSEA method and compared across genetic subtypes. Cell of origin (COO) in patients with RNA-sequencing data was determined based on signature expressions identified in the Lymph2Cx assay.16

Gene set enrichment analysis

Quantile-normalized and batch-adjusted logCPM (log2 transformed counts-per-million) data were used for differential expression analysis (DEA) via the R package “limma” (v3.54.2).49 Genes were ranked by decreasing logFC (log2 fold-change). Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) analysis was used to explore the biological pathways associated with clinical indicators such as LDH and TMTV. TPM data were used to calculate ssGSEA scores between LymphPlex genetic subtypes, consistent with our previous work.3,7 Gene signatures used for GSEA analysis were obtained from the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB) version 7.5.1 (released February 2022; accessed April 20, 2024, at https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/msigdb).30 Gene signatures used for ssGSEA analysis were previously assembled by Louis M. Staudt et al. (https://lymphochip.nih.gov/signaturedb/).5 Gene Ontology (GO) annotations were sourced via the Bioconductor package org.Hs.e.g.,.db (version 3.13.0, released January 2023). Reactome pathway data were accessed through the ReactomePA R package (version 1.38.0, based on Reactome release 78 from March 2023). KEGG pathway analyses utilized human gene sets automatically retrieved by the gseKEGG function from the clusterProfiler package, referencing the official KEGG database for Homo sapiens (https://www.genome.jp/kegg/).

Quantification and statistical analysis

Categorical variables were summarized by frequencies and percentages, while continuous were described through medians and ranges. Baseline patient characteristics were analyzed using two-sided χ2 test. Progression-free survival (PFS) was determined from the diagnosis date to either the recognition of disease progression or relapse, or the last follow-up. Similarly, overall survival (OS) was calculated from the diagnosis date until either the date of death or the last follow-up. Kaplan-Meier method was used to assessed survival, with comparisons conducted via log rank and weighted log rank tests. The Mann-Whitney U test was utilized to compare differences in parameters (DSC, JSC, Se, PPV, HD95, and ASSD) across training, test, and validation cohorts. We examined the relationships and agreement between ground truth PET (gtPET) biomarkers and those predicted by a deep learning segmentation model (pPET) through spearman correlation analysis and intra-class correlation coefficients (ICC), respectively. The dataset was divided using 100 repetitions of 10-fold cross-validation, while time-dependent receiver operating characteristic curves (ROC) determined the average cut-off values for each PET biomarker. Bonferroni correction was applied to adjust the p-values for sensitivity analyses. Lasso regression was implemented to identify risk factors among PET biomarkers, LymphPlex genetic subtypes, and IPI factors. The tuning parameter value (λ) was optimized through 10-fold cross-validations to minimize error.50 Furthermore, a Cox proportional hazards (PH) regression model, incorporating Lasso-screened parameters, analyzed univariate and multivariate hazard ratio (HR) and z-Wald values. Stratified models were compared using Akaike’s information criterion (AIC) and the concordance index (C-index).2 The Cox regression method was also applied to analyze multivariate HR of the ClinicalPET LymphPlex, EcoTyper state, LME categories, and COO. Pearson χ2 test evaluated the associations between these factors. Differences between each ClinicalPET LymphPlex risk factor and Ecotyper cell state were analyzed using the Mann-Whitney U test. Comprehensive statistical analyses were conducted using Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) 23.0 software and R v4.2.2. Statistical significance was defined as p < 0.05 (two-sided).

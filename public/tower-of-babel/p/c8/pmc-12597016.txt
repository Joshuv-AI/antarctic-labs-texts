Dataset of rolling circle amplification (RCA) enriched metagenome of Cassava obtained through nanopore sequencing
Authors: Olabode Onile-ere, Pakyendou Estel Name, Ezechiel Bionimian Tibiri, Fidèle Tiendrébéogo, Justin Pita, Ibrahim Umar Mohammed, Chukwuemeka K Nkere, Solomon Oranusi, Angela Eni
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe dataset presented here was obtained by sequencing selected historic herbarium cassava samples collected across Nigeria. Total DNA was extracted from the samples using the CTAB method, after which the samples were enriched by Rolling Circle Amplification (RCA) and then sequenced on the MinION. The dataset consists of raw sequencing data in FASTQ format reflecting microbial diversity in cassava leaf samples. Taxonomic classification of the samples using the Kraken2 PlusPFP-16 database revealed 12 kingdoms, 36 phyla, 67 classes, 154 orders, 273 families, 524 genera, and 895 species across the dataset, with a substantial proportion (77.9%) of reads remaining unclassified following host removal. The data is beneficial for exploring the microbiome diversity of cassava leaves across Nigeria, as well as serving as a reference for future microbial discovery, given the large number of unidentified reads in the dataset.Keywords: Metagenome, Nanopore Sequencing, Cassava, Diversity

Background

Cassava is an important food resource for a significant portion of Sub-Saharan Africa, where it serves as a staple for over 800 million people on the sub-continent [1,2]. Its productivity is however significantly impacted by abiotic factors such as poor soil conditions and, more importantly, biotic factors such as bacteria, fungi, and viruses. Plant viruses represent the major source of biotic constraint to cassava production, with viruses belonging to the begomovirus family causing yield losses of up to 70 % in susceptible varieties [3]. Controlling these viruses requires an adequate understanding of their diversity especially given their propensity to evolve through recombination [4]. Previous studies have shown that Nanopore sequencing of RCA-enriched DNA is particularly effective in exploring viral diversity [5]. Given that RCA is a non-specific DNA amplification approach, it also allows for the detection of other organisms present in samples, hence providing a snapshot of microbial diversity.

Data Description

The dataset described here contains raw sequencing data, obtained from Nanopore sequencing of RCA-enriched whole DNA extracted from cassava leaves showing symptoms of cassava mosaic disease (CMD). A total of 43 samples, collected from across Nigeria between 2015 – 2020 (Fig. 1, Table 1), were sequenced using the Native Barcoding Kit 96 V14 on the MinION sequencer (Oxford Nanopore Technologies Ltd UK). The Raw sequencing traces were basecalled into FASTQ format using DORADO basecaller version 0.7.2. Raw reads have been deposited in the NCBI SRA database under accession number PRJNA1169655.

A total of 217,936 reads with a total length of 352,510,334 bases and an average read length of 1,617.5 bases passed quality checks (QC). The length of the individual reads ranged from 1- 133,365bp, with an average N50 of 3,455bp across all reads. The average read quality across all samples was 15.12, while GC content was 44.67 %. Following host filtering, a total of 159,372 reads remained, with host reads accounting for between 2.06 % - 84.78 % of reads (mean = 20.5 % and median = 11.6 %) (Table 2). Taxonomic classification against the Kraken2 PlusPFP-16 returned 124,210 unclassified reads and 35,162 classified reads representing a wide range of microorganisms representing 12 kingdoms, 36 phyla, 67 classes, 154 orders, 273 families, 524 genera, and 895 species (Table 3). Viruses represented a significant proportion (17.9 %) of the classified reads with members of the Geminivirus family representing the most abundant viral family (Fig. 2). Cellular organisms were the most abundant overall, with kingdoms Fungi and Bacilliati dominating the dataset (Fig. 3).

Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

Data collection

Dried cassava leaves were obtained from the Herbarium collections of WAVE Program hubs located at Covenant University, National Root Crop Research Institute, and Kebbi State University of Science and Technology Aliero. The herbarium collection is made up of cassava leaf samples collected during field surveys conducted between 2015 -2022 [[6], [7], [8]].

DNA extraction

Whole DNA was extracted using the CTAB extraction protocol as previously described [9]. Briefly, 100g of dried cassava leaf was homogenized in 1.5mL of CTAB buffer (2 % w/v CTAB, 100 mM Tris, 20 mM EDTA, 1.4 M NaCl and 1 % w/v PVP) using the Omni BeadRuptor Elite (Omni international Elite, US). The homogenate was incubated at 60°C with gentle agitation for 1 hour and then separated at 20,000 × g for 10 min. The supernatant was transferred to a new tube, and an equal volume of chloroform-Isoamyl alcohol (24:1) was added, mixed gently, and then centrifuged at 20,000 × g for 10mins. This step was repeated a second time after which the DNA was incubated in iso-propanol at -20°C for 30 minutes. The mixture was centrifuged at 20,000 × g for 10 min and then precipitated with ice-cold 70 % ethanol for 20 minutes before spinning at 20,000 × g. The pellet was allowed to dry before dissolving in in 20 µL of TE buffer and stored at -20°C until required.

Rolling circle amplification (RCA) enrichment

Rolling circle amplification (RCA) was used to enrich circular DNA templates using the Illustra TempliPhi Kit (GE Healthcare) and following the manufacturer’s instructions. RCA was conducted overnight, following which enriched amplicons were purified using 1.2X Ampure XP (Beckman Coulter) magnetic bead purification. RCA was conducted on extracted whole DNA without any prior host depletion steps.

Library preparation and sequencing

Purified RCA amplicons were digested using T7 Endonuclease enzyme (NEB) according to the manufacturers protocol followed by another round of magnetic bead purification. 200ng of the digested amplicons were then treated with the NEBNext Ultra II End repair/dA-tailing Module (NEB), following the manufacturer’s recommendation, to repair the ends of the digested amplicons. Repaired DNA were then purified using Ampure XP magnetic beads (Beckman Coulter), before adding barcodes using the Native Barcoding Kit 96 V14 (ONT, UK). Samples were pooled in equimolar ratios following which adapters were added using the NEBNext Quick Ligation Module (NEB). Pooled samples were then sequenced using the R10.4.1 flow cell on the MinION sequencer (ONT, UK).

Data analysis

Raw reads were obtained in the native Nanopore pod5 format and basecalled using the Dorado basecaller version 0.7.2 with the dna_r10.4.1_e8.2_400bps_sup.cfg configuration. The quality of obtained reads were assessed using NanoPlot v1.42.0 [10] following which summary statistics of obtained reads were computed using SeqKit v2.8.2 [11]. Minimap2 v2.28 [12] was used to map the reads to the host genome (Manihot Esculenta V8 assembly GCF_001659605.2) while samtools v1.20 [13] was used to filter host reads. Taxonomic classification was done using Kraken2 v2.1.3 [14] against the kraken2 PlusPFP-16 and Refseq Viral prebuilt databases after which the results were visualized using Pavian [15] or KronaTools [16]. All bioinformatics tools were run with default parameters unless otherwise stated.

Limitations

While the RCA technique is a non-specific approach for amplifying DNA, it has an inherent bias toward circular templates; therefore, the organisms represented in the dataset may be slightly skewed. RCA enrichment was performed in line with the primary objective of sequencing which was, to assemble complete Geminivirus genomes. In addition, several samples yielded relatively few reads as such future studies using this dataset may consider pooling samples with shared characteristics, such as collection year or location, to increase sequencing depth and improve the detection and resolution of rare taxa within the cassava microbiome.

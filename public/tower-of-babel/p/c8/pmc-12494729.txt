Exploiting deep transfer learning based precise classification and grading of renal cell carcinoma using histopathological images
Authors: Ala Saleh Alluhaidan, Mohammed Alqahtani, Jahangir khan, Turke Althobaiti, Nojood O. Aljehane, Hamad Almansour, Sultan Alanazi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Renal cancer is a key reason for cancer-related deaths among males worldwide. Earlier diagnosis of renal cancer is critical since it can considerably increase the chance of survivability. However evaluating the histopathological renal tissue is a tedious process and usually, this is manually done by the pathologist, resulting in a strong possibility of misdiagnosis or misdetection, particularly in the earlier phases, and susceptible to inter-pathologist variations. The advancement of automated histopathological diagnoses of renal cancer could significantly decrease the bias and offer correct classification of disease though the pathology and microscopy nature are more complicated and complex. Current researchers recommend that clinicians successfully implement the classification task by investigating the image texture feature of RCC from computed tomography (CT) data. However, image feature detection for RCC grading frequently depends on a physical process that is time-intensive and error-prone. Therefore, this article develops an Exploiting Deep Transfer Learning based Precise Classification and Grading of Renal Cell Carcinoma (EDTL-PCGRCC) method using Histopathological Imaging. The projected EDTL-PCGRCC methods inspect the histopathological images for the classification and detection of RCC. In the suggested EDTL-PCGRCC method, a wiener filtering (WF) based noise removal technique takes place for noise removal procedure. Furthermore, the EDTL-PCGRCC method uses an improved MobileNetV2 technique to derive the feature vector from pre-processed images. Furthermore, the classification of RCC takes place using the Elman Neural Network (ENN) mechanism. Lastly, improved artificial ecosystem optimization (IAEO) is applied for the parameter selection of the ENN model. The efficiency of the EDTL-PCGRCC method is assessed under the biomedical image dataset. The empirical findings reported the robustness of the EDTL-PCGRCC method under various measures.

Introduction

Kidney cancer is the 9th most frequently diagnosed tumor in men, in comparison, it is less prevalent in women which is at the 14th position1. Renal cell carcinoma (RCC) is a very frequent and harmful kind of kidney cancer in elders. It grows in the liners of the proximal kidney tubules, as tumorous cells gradually form into the growth and have the potential to spread to other organs2. The syndromes of RCC are invisible and so it is not simply identified. Several challenges restrain the identification of RCC sub-types, including the absence of large datasets with exactly located interpretations. In addition, there is an intense data inequality after the clear cell sub-type includes the many medical cases, and the coherency of RCC cells in these various sub-types poses a challenge, as does the variability in the appearance of cells within the same sub-type at various resolution levels3. The latest RCC classification structure depends on the valuable interpretation of pathology digital slides. It can present a weakly supervised learning method for classifying renal cells by utilizing image-level labels, eliminating the need for expensive localized annotations. Histopathology imageries can show a wide range of differences in appearances, including scale, morphology, color, and texture distribution4. These differences can make it difficult to identify a consistent pattern for detecting tumors and classifying sub-types. The foremost concept of weakly supervised learning is to convert the image-level annotations into pixel/patch-level data. This method will reduce the workload of pathologists by eliminating the need for extensive annotations5.

RCC sub-types are identified image-based on the cancer improvement degree of multi-detecting magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) or CT. Moreover, the microscopic analysis of Eosin (H & E) and hematoxylin-stained images of biopsies remains a valued resource for clinicians and pathologists6. Histological imageries are rich in markers of illness evolution and valuable phenotypic data that is utilized for analytical purposes. Many experts follow very simple decision tree-based methods for the classifications of cancer7. The primary challenges pathologists face when analyzing E & H images between observers and the time needed for diagnosis. Computer-aided methods could assist in overcoming these restrictions and classify acute morphological variations among clinical groups. Then, it is assumed that deep learning (DL) will assist in the grading analysis of RCC and enhance conventional analytic approaches8. On the other hand, many DL methods rely on physical pixel-level annotations that severely inhibit the growth of artificial intelligence (AI) in various fields. It is vital to employ weakly supervised or unsupervised methods for enhancing clinical applicability while ensuring the performance of the model9. Image-driven DL can enhance the precision of visual analysis. Pathologists utilize and learn these familiar features to grade or stage patients10. Additionally, to these distinct features, DL can mine many morphological features that experts are not conscious of certain features resulting in precise diagnosis.

Literature survey

Juang et al.11 presented a flattened Xception with a feature pyramid networks (FX-FPNs) method. This technique is used to support the structure of faster region-based CNN. In the classification step, this study analyses the classifications of 5 glomerulus morphology utilizing a flattened Xception classifier. Novel glomerulus patches are produced for data augmentation throughout the cycle-consistent generative adversarial networks (CycleGANs). Chanchal et al.12 developed a computation and robust effective complete automatic RCC Grading Networks (RCCGNet) from kidney histopathological imageries. The presented RCCGNet comprises shared channel residual (SCR) blocks that allow the networks to learn map features related by various interpretations of the input using 2 equivalent paths. The SCR blocks share the data among 2 various operators and layers of the united data separated by providing a helpful supplement to every other. Shen et al.13 proposed a murine renal pathological systems (MRPSs) method to personalize the pathologic analysis through the DL technique on whole-slide imagery (WSI) segmentations and extract the features. A CNN method was employed for the precise segmentation of glomerular and glomeruli cells of periodic acid. Ignatov et al.14 introduce a new DeepCMorph method pre-trained to learn cell morphology and classify many various tumor forms. This method contains 2 components: at first it makes the cell nuclei annotations and segmentation of every type of cell and is trained on the integration of eight publicly provided datasets. The next module unites the acquired segmentation maps with the new microscope image and is trained for the task of downstream.

In15, deep CNN-based heterogeneous ensemble methods are proposed. This method identifies the produced patches into cancer and normal tissue utilizing the pre-trained CNN and finally executes the deep ensemble learning for defining the last classifications. The ensemble methods comprised CNN methods with 5 DL structures such as DenseNet, ResNet, VGG, Efficient, and MobileNet Net. These CNN methods are modified and utilized as baseline learners. Asha et al.16 developed an improved hybrid DL method. A hybrid method is utilized to classify the imageries associated with kidney illness, where firstly the images are pre-processed by Image resize, Grayscale conversions, and noise removal by an integration of median and Weiner filters. Then these images are improved by utilizing Gaussian Mixture-based histogram equalization followed by image feature extraction through Dense CNN. Ohe et al.17 propose an AI method. Before the AI method was developed, histologic analysis utilizing eosin and hematoxylin WSI of the TCGA-ccRCC cohort. At initial, the higher-grade part on every WSI was taken for image processing. Moreover, the preferred areas are gathered into tiles. Then, it is trained by transfer learning (TL) on a deep CNN. Furthermore, the technique verifies the AI method utilizing a validation cohort. Mahmood et al.18 present a new hybrid method that effectually incorporates the benefits of CNN and vision transformers. The presented technique, which is called the renal cancer grading networks, contains 2 vital modules: dynamic attention (DA) and adaptive convolution (AC) blocks. The block of DA is designed on feature of AC block, which is a vital component for histopathologic-imagery classifications. Bagri and Rajpal19 proposed an explainable framework by utilizing a custom convolutional neural network (CNN) for extracting deep features. The framework classifies Fuhrman grades (FG) into two-tiered and four-tiered categories, outperforming TL models such as ResNet50, InceptionResNetV2, RCCGNet, and BreastNet.

Despite noteworthy advancements, various limitations and a research gap still exist across the reviewed methods. Most approaches depend heavily on handcrafted preprocessing or patch-based learning, limiting end-to-end automation. Integration of CycleGANs and hybrid models lacks uniform standardization, resulting in inconsistent performance. Many CNN and TL models illustrate high dependency on specific datasets, mitigating generalizability. Attention mechanisms and ensemble methods exhibit promise but often lack interpretability. Additionally, limited cross-comparative validation and insufficient use of transformer-based architectures in renal histopathology highlight a key research gap in attaining robust and explainable grading systems.

Methodology

In this article, an EDTL-PCGRCC method using histopathological images is developed. The projected EDTL-PCGRCC method inspects the histopathological imaging for the recognition and classification of RCC. To achieve that, the EDTL-PCGRCC model contains different stages namely noise reduction, feature extractor, classification, and parameter selection process. Stage I: noise reduction

At the primary stage, the EDTL-PCGRCC method undergoes the WF-based noise removal technique takes place for the noise removal process20. This method is chosen for its adaptive nature, which effectively mitigates noise while preserving important image details. Unlike simple filters, WF considers both the local image variance and noise characteristics, resulting in better smoothing without blurring edges. It performs well in handling Gaussian and other common noise types, making it appropriate for diverse imaging conditions. Additionally, WF is computationally efficient compared to more intrinsic denoising algorithms, enabling faster preprocessing. Its proven success in improving image quality assists improved feature extraction and classification accuracy in subsequent stages.

WF-based image preprocessing is a robust method utilized in the improvement of healthcare images, especially for RCC diagnoses. WF assists in decreasing noise while retaining significant image features that are critical for the correct diagnosis and analysis of RCC. By modeling both the noise and image, WF successfully alleviates blurring and optimizes the transparency of tumor borders, assisting radiologists in accurate calculation. This pre-processing stage is vital to improve the quality of image classification and segmentation processes, enabling accurate and early detection of renal cell carcinoma.

Stage II: improved MobileNetV2 model

Furthermore, the EDTL-PCGRCC method utilizes an improved MobileNetV2 technique to derive the feature vector from pre-processed images21. This model is prevalent for its lightweight and efficient architecture, efficiently balancing accuracy and computational cost. The utilization of inverted residuals and linear bottlenecks assists in preserving significant features while mitigating the size of the model. Compared to heavier models, MobileNetV2 enables faster processing and lower memory usage, making it ideal for real-time or resource-constrained applications. It also exhibits robust performance on various image recognition tasks, ensuring robust and discriminative feature vectors. This makes it preferable over traditional CNNs or deeper networks that are more computationally expensive and slower. Figure 2 depicts the framework of improved MobileNetV2.

Stage III: RCC classification using ENN

In this stage, the classification of RCC occurs utilizing the ENN mechanism22. This model is chosen for its capability to model temporal dependencies and dynamic patterns in data through recurrent connections. Unlike conventional feedforward neural networks, ENN can capture context by retaining data from previous inputs, which improves classification accuracy on intrinsic medical image features. Its relatively simple structure allows efficient training with smaller datasets, common in medical imaging. Compared to deeper or more complex networks, ENN presents a good balance between performance and computational cost, making it appropriate for effective and timely cancer diagnosis. Stage IV: parameter selection

Finally, the IAEO technique is employed for the hyperparameter tuning of ENN model23. This model is chosen for as it efficiently explores and exploits the search space to find optimal parameters, improving model performance. IAEO incorporates ecological principles like competition and adaptation, which improve convergence speed and avoid local optima better than traditional optimizers. Compared to other metaheuristic algorithms, IAEO presents a balance of exploration and exploitation, resulting in more accurate and robust parameter selection. Its adaptability makes it effective for complex, high-dimensional problems such as neural network tuning, resulting in improved classification accuracy and model stability.

AEO is a biologically stimulated optimizer technique based on the energy flow in an ecosystem on the Earth. Ecology is described as a cluster of living creatures living in a specific space and defines the relationship between them. The producer, consumer, and decomposer are three different stages of living organisms in AEO. In the initial stage (producers), it doesn’t attain energy from other organisms, where the producer is a type of green plant. At the next stage (consumer), creatures are characterized as consumers, where they can create food. This animal obtains nutrients from the producers or consumers. In the final stage (decomposer), they feed on producers or consumers. The 3 stages interrelating with each other unite the food chain. The AEO technique uses these 3 stages. The initial stage is to enhance the balance amongst exploration and exploitation. The next stage is employed for enhancing the process of exploration. In the final stage, it is proposed to improve the exploitation. There is one producer and one decomposer in the AEO model. The others are considered as consumers.

Conclusion

In this article, an EDTL-PCGRCC method using histopathological images is developed. The projected EDTL-PCGRCC methods inspect the histopathological imaging for the recognition and classification of RCC. To complete that, the EDTL-PCGRCC model contains different stages namely noise reduction, feature extractor, classification, and parameter selection process. At the primary stage, the EDTL-PCGRCC method undergoes the WF-based noise removal technique takes place for the removal of noise procedure. Furthermore, the EDTL-PCGRCC method uses an improved MobileNetV2 technique to derive the feature vector from pre-processed images. Furthermore, the classification of RCC takes place using the ENN mechanism. Lastly, the IAEO is employed for the hyperparameter range of the ENN model. The efficiency of the EDTL-PCGRCC method is assessed under the biomedical image dataset. The empirical findings reported the robustness of the EDTL-PCGRCC method under various measures.

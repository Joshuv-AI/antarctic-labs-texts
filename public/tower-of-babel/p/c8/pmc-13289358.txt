Clinical presentation and effectiveness of antiretroviral therapy in newly diagnosed people living with HIV before, during and after the COVID-19 pandemic
Authors: Andrea Carbone, Francesca Lombardi, Giorgio Tiecco, Valentina Iannone, Jacopo Logiudice, Pierluigi Francesco Salvo, Giancarlo Scoppettuolo, Roberta Gerami, Federico Cesanelli, Martina Salvi, Antonella Cingolani, Francesco Castelli, Simona Di Giambenedetto, Carlo Torti, Eugenia Quiros-Roldan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIn Italy, the COVID-19 pandemic substantially disrupted access to health care services, including HIV screening, contributing to a decline in new HIV diagnoses and increasing late presentations in the postpandemic years. This study assessed the profile and trends of newly diagnosed people living with HIV (ndPLWH) across the prepandemic, peripandemic and postpandemic periods in two major Italian centres.MethodsWe conducted a retrospective observational study including adults diagnosed with HIV-1 infection between 2015 and 2023 at two HIV outpatient clinics in Central (Centre A) and Northern Italy (Centre B). According to the year of diagnosis, ndPLWH were categorised into four periods spanning from prepandemic to postpandemic years. We compared the number and clinical characteristics of ndPLWH and assessed virological suppression (VS; HIV-RNA <50 copies/mL) at 6 months after ART initiation. Predictors of VS were identified using logistic regression models.ResultsA total of 1042 ndPLWH were evaluated, including 423 (40.6%) from Centre A and 619 (59.4%) from Centre B. AIDS-defining conditions peaked during the pandemic period (31.6%, 61/193; p=0.031). The time from diagnosis to antiretroviral therapy (ART) initiation shortened substantially from the prepandemic period to the postpandemic period (p<0.001), and the use of integrase strand transfer inhibitor (INSTI)-based regimens increased significantly across these periods (p<0.001). NdPLWH from Central Italy presented with a generally worse clinical profile, but the median time from HIV diagnosis to ART initiation was significantly shorter in this group (p<0.001). At 6 months, 84.5% of the patients achieved VS, with no interperiod variation (p=0.519); a higher CD4 count (adjusted OR (aOR) 1.143; 95% CI 1.052 to 1.243; p=0.002) and INSTI-based triple therapy (aOR 2.191; 95% CI 1.455 to 3.299; p<0.001) remained independently associated with VS.ConclusionsDespite substantial disruptions in HIV care during the COVID-19 pandemic and an increased proportion of late presenters, virological suppression rates remained high and stable throughout all the study periods, with no differences between the centres. These findings highlight the resilience of HIV care systems in Italy and the importance of maintaining tireless testing pathways amid health care challenges.Keywords: HIV, COVID-19, Epidemiology

Introduction

Globally, approximately 39.9 million people were living with HIV in 2023, with 1.3 million individuals newly infected in the same year.1 Moreover, despite the widespread availability of HIV testing and antiretroviral therapy (ART), late-stage HIV diagnosis remains a critical challenge, even in high-income countries.2 Late HIV diagnosis is defined as a diagnosis made when a person presents with a CD4 count below 350 cells/mm³ or an AIDS-defining event, regardless of CD4 count, excluding cases of recent HIV infection.3 4 In Italy, the incidence of new HIV diagnoses in 2023 was 4.0 per 100 000 residents, placing the country below the Western European average of 6.2 per 100 000 residents.5 However, compared with that in previous years, the incidence of late HIV diagnosis increased in 2023 and exceeded the average incidence observed across Europe.5

Since the emergence of SARS-CoV-2, the causative agent of COVID-19, which rapidly spread worldwide within few months,6 a decline in new HIV diagnoses has been documented worldwide.7 In Italy, this trend culminated in 2020, which recorded the lowest number of new HIV diagnosis5 as the COVID-19 pandemic significantly disrupted access to healthcare services, including HIV testing and the management of people living with HIV (PLWH). These disruptions resulted in an increased proportion of HIV diagnoses made during hospitalisation and a higher prevalence of AIDS-defining conditions at the time of diagnosis.8

Although numerous studies have examined trends in new HIV diagnoses before and during the SARS-CoV-2 pandemic around the world,8,10 to the best of our knowledge, research on postpandemic trends remains scarce.11,14

Italy exhibits substantial heterogeneity in the management of PLWH, characterised by significant inequalities and regional disparities in both access to and quality of healthcare services.15 16 In 2023, there were twice as many new HIV diagnoses and a higher proportion of non-Italian PLWH in Northern Italy than in Central Italy.5 Most diagnoses in the North occurred at a late stage, while in both regions the primary route of transmission was men who have sex with men (MSM).5 Moreover, the impact of the SARS-CoV-2 pandemic varied considerably across the nation, reflecting significant temporal fluctuations and regional disparities in COVID-19 mortality patterns.17 By late June 2020, nearly 81% of national cases were reported in the North, reflecting higher population density and greater international connectivity.18 Consequently, Northern Italy experienced higher cumulative incidence, intensive care unit occupancy, SARS-CoV-2 related mortality and overall health system strain because of the greater epidemic burden during the lockdown.19 20

Moreover, the COVID-19 pandemic led to substantial disruptions in public health systems and HIV care services in Italy, potentially exacerbating existing gaps in HIV prevention and management. However, evidence on how the pandemic affected the presentation of newly diagnosed PLWH (ndPLWH) care in Italy remains limited. This study aims to address these gaps by analysing data from two major HIV outpatient clinics in Central and Northern Italy, deliberately selected for their differing organisational structures and patient access pathways, evaluating ndPLWH across four periods—two prepandemic, one pandemic and one postpandemic—focusing on the virological effectiveness of ART and the incidence and characteristics of AIDS-defining events.

Materials and methods

Study population and design

This was a retrospective observational study enrolling individuals diagnosed with HIV-1 infection at two large Italian referral centres: ‘Fondazione Policlinico Universitario A. Gemelli IRCCS’ in Rome (Central Italy, Centre A) and ‘ASST Spedali Civili Di Brescia’ in Brescia (Northern Italy, Centre B). The study period begins in January 2015, following the widespread use of second generation integrase strand transfer inhibitor (INSTI) in our centres, and ends in December 2023. Eligible participants were adults (≥18 years) with a first confirmed HIV diagnosis during the study period, who initiated ART and had at least one HIV-RNA measurement within 3 months of diagnosis. Individuals with prior knowledge of HIV infection were excluded. All eligible participants were consecutively enrolled. Participants were followed for 6 months after starting their first line regimen. According to the date of diagnosis, which was designated as the baseline, ndPLWH were categorised into four periods: two prepandemic periods, ‘Pre-P1’ (2015–2017) and ‘Pre-P2’ (2018–2019), the pandemic period ‘P’ (2020–2021), characterised by national lockdown, strict social restrictions and limited social gatherings, and one postpandemic phase, ‘Post-P’ (2022–2023) following the WHO declaration of the end of the international public health emergency. The two prepandemic periods were defined to obtain intervals spanning similar time frames.

Objectives

Our primary objective was to compare the clinical and laboratory characteristics of ndPWH at diagnosis, including the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) stage C, and the frequency of participants who achieved virological suppression (VS), defined as reaching HIV-RNA level below 50 copies/mL at 6 months after first ART initiation, in the four different periods. CDC Stage C, defined according to the 1993 CDC classification system as the occurrence of one or more AIDS-defining conditions, was used as the indicator of advanced HIV disease. This variable was selected as a predefined outcome measure given its established clinical relevance and its widespread use as a standardised marker of disease severity in both European and international epidemiological studies. The secondary objective was to identify factors associated with VS achievement at 6 months. We also compared general population characteristics at the diagnosis between the two clinical centres to explore any potential difference. Primary and secondary analyses were performed under both intention-to-treat (ITT) and on-treatment (OT) frameworks. ITT analysis included all participants, regardless of loss to follow-up (defined as having less than 3 months of observation from ART initiation), treatment discontinuation or first-line regimen switches. OT analysis was performed to understand the efficacy of first-line medications, which have varied over time, net of those lost to follow-up and those who switched therapies. ART switches were defined as alterations to the anchor drug, dose adjustments or changes in the number of drugs. Changes within the same drug class or modifications of the backbone alone were not considered switches. Reasons for switching included toxicity, regimen intensification, immunological failure or adverse reaction side effects.

Data collection

Data of interest were extracted by data collectors from our clinical electronic database and entered in an Excel file. Prior to data analysis, all patient identifiers were removed and replaced with unique study codes, ensuring that no identifiable information was accessible during the analytical phase, in compliance with European data protection regulations (General Data Protection Regulation).

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were used for demographic, laboratory and clinical data. Categorical variables were expressed as number (percentage), and continuous variables were expressed as median and IQR. Characteristics and the proportion of participants achieving VS at 6 months were compared across the four study periods. Categorical data were compared by using the χ2 test or the Fisher exact test, as appropriate. Continuous variables were compared with the non-parametric Kruskal-Wallis and Mann-Whitney tests as appropriate. Univariable logistic regression models were used to identify factors associated with the outcome, that is, reaching VS at 6 months. Variables with statistically significant association on univariable analysis were included in a multivariable model. A p value <0.05 was considered a statistically significant predictor. Data were analysed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, V.28 (IBM, Armonk, New York, USA).

The study population consisted predominantly of cisgender men (76.9%) and individuals of Caucasian ethnicity (80.1%), proportions that remained stable across all four periods. Overall, 259 (24.9%) ndPLWH presented with an AIDS-defining condition (CDC stage C), with a higher proportion observed during the P period (61/193, 31.6%) compared with Pre-P1, Pre-P2 and Post-P (p=0.040). Pneumocystis pneumonia was the most frequent AIDS-defining condition (97/1042, 9.3%) and its peak was registered during the P period (24/193, 12.4). Among ndPLWH with CDC stage C, heterosexual transmission was the predominant route of HIV acquisition (50.2%), followed by MSM (29.0%). No differences were observed in the hospitalisation rate at diagnosis (p=0.141).

In terms of viro-immunological conditions at the diagnosis, the median HIV-RNA was 4.93 log₁₀ copies/mL (IQR 4.31–5.54), increasing significantly over time—from 4.77 (4.22–5.41) in Pre-P1 to 5.19 (4.43–5.74) in the Post-P (p<0.001). Conversely, median CD4+ T cell counts declined across periods without reaching statistical significance.

The time to ART initiation significantly decreased from 32 (14–62) days Pre-P1 to 14 (7–28) days Post-P (p<0.001). Triple therapy remained the predominant initial strategy, with a progressive increase in INSTI-based regimens—from 73.7% pre-P1 to 93.0% post-P (p<0.001)—and a corresponding decline in PI-based and non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor-based therapies (p<0.001). Dual therapy, which was absent in the Pre-P1, increased across periods (p<0.001), peaking during the P at 14.5%.

The proportion of heterosexual ndPLWH was significantly lower in Central Italy (30% versus 57%, p<0.001), whereas the proportion of Caucasian individuals was higher (83.7% versus 77.7%, p=0.018). Moreover, the median time from HIV diagnosis to ART initiation was significantly shorter in Centre A (9 (5–18) days) compared with Centre B (37 (16–62) days, p<0.001). When stratifying by study period, these intercentre differences remained consistent with the overall pattern described above (online supplemental table 1). In addition, we compared the rate of ndPLWH across the last three periods (each covering an equal number of years) in both centres. In Centre A, ndPLWH decreased by 13.5% from Pre-P2 to P and by 7.8% from P to Post-P, whereas, in Centre B, they declined more markedly from Pre-P2 to P (−32.7%) and subsequently increased by 23.3% in the Post-P.

Virological outcomes and determinants of treatment response

In the ITT analysis, at 6 months, 84.5% of the ndPLWH achieved VS with no significant difference among the different periods (p=0.519). In the multivariable model, factors independently associated with higher odds of achieving VS included higher CD4 lymphocyte count at diagnosis (adjusted OR (aOR) 1.143; 95% CI 1.052 to 1.243; p=0.002) and the use of INSTI based triple therapy as first line regimen (aOR 2.191, 95% CI 1.455 to 3.299, p<0.001). Conversely, higher zenith HIV-RNA (aOR 0.522; 95% CI 0.420 to 0.649; p<0.001) was associated with a lower likelihood of VS (table 3).

Overall, 7.5% ndPLWH discontinued their initial ART regimen within 6 months, most frequently during the Pre-P2 period (11.7%, p=0.008). AIDS-related mortality remained low and stable across all periods (10/1042, 1.0%; p=0.969). The median time to death was 1.85 (0.83–3.31) months with no difference among the four periods (p=0.253). In the OT analysis, 937 ndPLWH were evaluated after excluding 105 ndPLWH (17 lost to follow-up, 10 deaths and 78 treatment discontinuations). At 6 months, 87.9% of the evaluated ndPLWH achieved VS, with no significant interperiod differences (p=0.241). At 6 months, higher CD4 count (aOR 1.126; 95% CI 1.024 to 1.238; p=0.015) and INSTI-based triple therapy (aOR 2.559; 95% CI 1.574 to 4.161; p<0.001) were independently associated with VS, while male sex (aOR 0.522; 95% CI 0.291 to 0.937; p=0.029) and higher zenith HIV-RNA (aOR 0.462; 95% CI 0.357 to 0.599; p<0.001) were associated with a reduced likelihood of VS (table 4).

Discussion

This study describes the trajectories and characteristics of ndPLWH before, during and after the COVID-19 pandemic in two major Italian outpatient centres, with a focus on new HIV diagnoses. Among 1042 ndPLWH (40.6% from Centre A and 59.4% from Centre B) a decline in new HIV diagnoses was observed during the P period, whereas late-stage presentations (CDC stage C) peaked during this time. The interval between HIV diagnosis and ART initiation decreased markedly during P and Post-P periods (p<0.001), coinciding with a progressive increase in the use of INSTI-based regimens (p<0.001) and the introduction of dual therapies, which peaked during P (14.5%). No substantial intercentre differences were observed across periods, except for a persistently worse clinical and virological–immunological presentation, and a shorter time to ART initiation, among ndPLWH from Centre A.

The decline in new HIV diagnoses observed in our cohort mirrors national surveillance data5 and international evidence, likely reflecting reduced testing availability, lockdown measures and limited outpatient activity during the pandemic.521,23 A marked reduction (−32%) was observed in Centre B (Northern Italy) during 2020–2021, which is consistent with the greater impact of the early COVID-19 waves in northern regions. Consistent with European reports, our findings also indicate increased rates of late presentation and AIDS-defining illnesses during the COVID-19 pandemic, largely due to disruptions in testing and care access.21 23 Pneumocystis pneumonia remained the leading AIDS-defining condition among late presenters, which is consistent with prior Italian reports, although robust epidemiological data on its incidence during the SARS-CoV-2 pandemic are still limited to case reports describing overlapping clinical and radiological features.24 25

The shorter time to ART initiation observed during the pandemic aligns with cohort data from France: compared with prepandemic controls, individuals diagnosed with HIV during the pandemic were more likely to begin treatment promptly and achieve virological suppression within 6 months.26 Consistent with updated international guidelines recommending INSTI-based regimens—particularly dolutegravir-containing or bictegravir-containing combinations—as preferred first-line options for their potency, resistance barrier and tolerability, most ART-naïve individuals initiated therapy with second-generation INSTIs during the pandemic, mirroring trends reported in other cohorts.27 In Italy, prescribing practices evolved over time: prior to 2019, delays in HLA-B*57:01 testing and the more limited availability of simplified, high-genetic barrier regimens often led to initiation of non-single-tablet therapies, with subsequent switching. The broader availability of single-tablet regimens in later periods likely contributed to greater regimen stability. The increasing use of dual therapy in our cohort parallels national ICONA data, indicating that the 2020 lockdown did not negatively affect dual regimen prescriptions, either for ART initiation or for switches from triple to dual therapy.28

A comparison between the two centres revealed a more advanced clinical presentation in Central Italy, with a higher proportion of stage C cases (31.0% versus 20.7%), greater HIV-RNA levels (5.20 versus 4.77 log₁₀ copies/mL, p<0.001) and lower CD4 counts (198 versuss 320 cells/mm³, p<0.001) compared with Northern Italy. These differences likely reflect distinct testing pathways, with hospital-based diagnoses predominating in Central Italy and broader community screening more common in the north.15 Similar regional disparities were reported prior to the SARS-CoV-2 pandemic, with higher rates of late presentation and advanced disease in Central Italy.29 Although direct comparative data remain limited, Northern Italy is known to have higher HIV screening coverage, which may contribute to earlier diagnosis.15

Virological suppression rates remained high and stable throughout all the study period, without differences between the two centre and consistent with multicentre data from the USA, Italy and sub-Saharan Africa showing sustained viral control despite COVID-19–related disruptions. These findings reflect the resilience of HIV care systems and the effectiveness of adaptive strategies such as telemedicine and multimonth ART dispensing.30,34 The predictors of virological success in our cohort—higher CD4 count and the use of INSTI-based triple therapy—were consistent with established evidence. Higher CD4 counts at diagnosis are well recognised as protective factors for achieving and maintaining viral suppression, whereas low CD4 levels (≤200 cells/µL) increase the risk of virological failure.35 Similarly, INSTI-based regimens, particularly those including dolutegravir or bictegravir, have consistently demonstrated superior virological efficacy and faster suppression in both clinical trials and real-world settings.36 37 Conversely, advanced disease at presentation (CDC stage C) remains associated with lower suppression rates and increased risk of treatment failure, even under INSTI-based therapy.38

Our findings should be considered in light of some limitations. First, the retrospective nature of the study may introduce selection and information bias; secondary differences in testing pathways between centres could affect comparability. Moreover, loss to follow-up and unmeasured confounders may have led to selection bias and underestimation of certain outcomes. Furthermore, the absence of data regarding the total number of HIV tests performed during the study periods limits our ability to determine if the decline in positive cases reflects a lower true prevalence or changes in testing volume. In addition, while Centre B (Brescia) serves as the sole referral facility for infectious diseases within its province, Centre A (Rome) is located in an area with multiple outpatient clinics dedicated to HIV care, even within the same city. Finally, results may not be fully generalisable beyond similar tertiary-care settings and should be interpreted in light of the decentralised structure of the Italian National Health Service, where regional heterogeneity in healthcare organisation may limit broader applicability. However, the comparison between two structurally distinct centres represents a key strength and a novel aspect of this study. Beyond an established clinical and research collaboration, it enables the evaluation of potential differences and resilience in the management of chronic HIV infection in the context of a public health emergency. Our study provides real-world evidence from two large Italian HIV centres over nearly a decade, allowing assessment of temporal trends before, during and after the COVID-19 pandemic. The multicentre design and comprehensive clinical data strengthen the validity of findings.

Conclusions

This multicentre study confirms that the COVID-19 pandemic led to a transient decline in new HIV diagnoses and an increase in late-stage presentations. Despite these disruptions, the time to ART initiation shortened, and the adoption of INSTI-based regimens increased substantially, ensuring stable virological suppression rates across all periods. Overall, these findings emphasise the resilience of HIV care systems in ensuring effective treatment and highlight the importance of maintaining tireless testing pathways amid unprecedented healthcare challenges.

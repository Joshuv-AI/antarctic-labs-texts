Association between time-weighted average glucose concentration or glucose variability and acute kidney injury among coronary artery disease patients with prediabetes
Authors: Jiaxin Ren, Ge Wang, Yizhe Zhou, Wei Gong, Hongjia Zhang, Yan Yan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimsTo validate the effectiveness of intensive glycemic control in preventing acute kidney injury (AKI) among patients with coronary artery disease (CAD) and prediabetes.MethodsThis investigation employed data from the Prospective Registry of the Current Status of Care for Patients with CAD database. Glycemic control was evaluated using the time-weighted average glucose (TWAG) and the glucose coefficient of variation (CV) for each participant. The primary outcome was AKI occurrence.ResultsA total of 2,454 CAD patients with prediabetes were included between January 2022 and June 2023. The mean age was 62.6 ± 10.3 years, with 27.1% female. In univariate analysis, each 1 mmol/L increase in TWAG was associated with a 1.51-fold (95% confidence interval (CI): 1.36–1.68) higher incidence of AKI. After stepwise adjustment for covariates, the odds ratio (OR) remained significant at 1.50 (95% CI: 1.35–1.67). Similarly, glucose CV showed a positive correlation with AKI risk; a 0.1-unit increase in CV was linked to approximately a 44% higher risk. When both TWAG and CV were included simultaneously in the model, each maintained an independent positive association with AKI. Restricted cubic spline analyses revealed a dose-dependent increase in AKI risk with rising TWAG and CV. Subgroup analyses confirmed the positive relationship between TWAG, glucose variability, and AKI risk.ConclusionsOur study reveals an association between TWAG or CV of glucose and AKI in individuals with both CAD and prediabetes. These findings highlight the potential value of continuous glucose monitoring and managing glycemic variability to reduce AKI risk in this population.

Introduction

Assessing glycemia status is recommended in all patients with coronary artery disease (CAD) and glycemic control was highly recommended to these with known diabetes [1]. However, the significance of glycemic control extends beyond diabetes, impacting a broader population with varying degrees of glucose impairment. Prediabetes, affects about 10% of the adult population, remains insufficiently addressed, with limited data available on the optimal glycemic control strategies for individuals in this category [2]. The scarcity of information poses a challenge in tailoring effective interventions for those at risk of progressing to diabetes. In patients presenting with acute coronary syndrome (ACS), elevated blood glucose levels at hospital admission are associated with an increased risk of mortality, regardless of whether the patient has diabetes [3]. Remarkably, mortality risk is more closely tied to blood glucose levels than the presence of diabetes itself [4]. Meta-analyses including 27,049 individual data from four randomized trials have demonstrated that lowering HbA1c reduces the incidence of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE). The observed reduction is chiefly attributable to decreases in reinfarction rates and microvascular complications affecting the kidneys and retina, though neuropathy remains unaffected [5]. Notably, the ACCORD (Action to Control Cardiovascular Risk in Diabetes) trial, which included 35% of participants with a prior cardiovascular event, reported higher mortality in the intensive glycemic control group (HbA1c target 6.5%, 48 mmol/mol) compared to the control group (HR 1.22; 95% CI, 1.01–1.46; p = .04) [6]. Although associations have been observed, current evidence is insufficient to confirm the effectiveness of intensive glycemic control in enhancing cardio-renal outcomes.

This study aimed to validate the role of intensive glycemic management in preventing acute kidney injury (AKI) by analyzing prospective, pragmatic clinical data from patients with CAD.

Methods

Study design and population

This study analyzed data from the Prospective Registry of the Current Status of Care for Patients with Coronary Artery Disease, a nationwide pragmatic registry project (clinicaltrial.gov NCT 05337319) launched on 17 January 2022. Managed by the National Clinical Research Center for Cardiovascular Disease, the registry aims to improve CAD care quality through systematic electronic health record (EHR) collection. Consecutive hospitalized patients were enrolled monthly, with inclusion criteria requiring a primary discharge diagnosis of CAD, encompassing ST-elevation myocardial infarction, non-ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction, unstable angina, and stable CAD.

For this analysis, we included all consecutive patients hospitalized and discharged with a diagnosis of CAD at Beijing Anzhen Hospital (Beijing, China) from January 2022 to June 2023, totaling 59,073 individuals. Exclusion criteria were age under 18 years (n = 19), diagnosis of diabetes (n = 22,410), or chronic kidney disease (CKD) (n = 2,151). Additionally, the analysis focused on patients with prediabetes status who underwent blood glucose testing at least three times during hospitalization. Further exclusions involved patients without an HbA1c test at admission (n = 11,484), those with HbA1c levels less than 5.7% or greater than 6.4% (n = 11,381), absence of a serum creatinine (SCr) test (n = 23), or fewer than three glucose tests (n = 9,151). Ultimately, 2,454 patients were included in the final analysis (Supplementary Figure 1).

The central ethics committee at Beijing Anzhen Hospital (Beijing, China) approved this project.

Data collection

Data collection was conducted through electronic medical records, including patient demographics (age of admission, sex, marriage status, and medical insurance), smoking and drinking alcohol status, body mass index (BMI), medical and disease history (PCI, CABG treatment, hypertension, lipid abnormalities, history of myocardial infarction, cerebrovascular disease, heart failure, atrial fibrillation, peptic ulcer disease malignancies, deep-vein thrombosis, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, pacemaker, or defibrillator), whether hospitalization due to acute myocardial infarction (AMI), lab test at admission (LVEF, blood tests, SCr, and eGFR), glucose and SCr during whole hospital stay, mediation during hospitalization (ACEI/ARB/ARNI, SGLT2i), surgery (CABG, PCI, or coronary angiography), and length of stay. Both blood glucose and creatinine are routinely measured by venous blood test and standardized clinical analyzer (cobas c 702 module, Roche, Basel, Switzerland). Considering the missing values among above covariate, we used Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) method to conduct the imputation for these continuous variables (Supplementary Section 1).

Average glucose concentration and glucose variability

Glycemic management was standardized during the hospitalization. Time-weighted average glucose (TWAG) was calculated in each patient using all blood glucose levels during hospitalization to indicate the average glucose concentration. TWAG is calculated by area under the curve of blood glucose divided by the duration between the first and the last measurement, which has been used in prior studies [7,8] (Supplementary Figure 2). Glucose variability was quantified using the coefficient of variation (CV), defined as the standard deviation (SD) divided by the mean glucose concentration.

Outcomes

AKI is defined in accordance with the KDIGO Clinical Practice Guideline for Acute Kidney Injury [9,10]. For the purposes of this study, AKI was defined by either of the following criteria: (1) a rise in SCr by 0.3 mg/dL (26.5 μmol/L) within a 48-h period or (2) an elevation in SCr to at least 1.5 times the baseline, whether confirmed or presumed to have transpired within the preceding seven days. Baseline creatinine values were obtained at admission to distinguish between community-acquired and hospital-acquired AKI.

Statistical analysis

Participant characteristics were summarized by quartiles of TWAG and CV, with categorical variables presented as percentages and continuous variables expressed as mean ± SD or median (interquartile range), depending on distribution. Trends across increasing quartiles were assessed using ANOVA for continuous variables and the Mantel–Haenszel χ2 test for categorical variables. We draw plots to shoe the distribution of blood glucose during hospitalization, and using Pearson’s test to explore the correlation between TWAG and CV of glucose.

A series of stepwise logistic models were used to calculate odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence interval (CI) for the TWAG and CV of glucose with in-hospital AKI in overall cohort. First, we estimated the association using univariate logistic model. Then, we applied several multivariate models: in model 1, we adjusted for age and sex; in model 2, smoke, drinking alcohol, BMI, history of PCI, dyslipidemia, myocardial infarction, stroke, heart failure and pacemaker or defibrillator were additionally adjusted for; in model 3, status at admission, including AMI, mediation during hospitalization (ACEI/ARB/ARNI or SGLT2i), surgery (CABG, PCI, or coronary angiography), eGFR, LVEF, mean platelet volume, platelet count, hemoglobin, white blood cell count, total cholesterol, AST, ALT, prothrombin time, d-dimer, total thyroid hormone, SCr, and HbA1c were added too. The TWAG and CV of glucose were analyzed both as continuous and categorical variables (quartiles). Additionally, we put TWAG and CV of glucose together in models to explore the impendent effect. CV was analyzed as per 0.1 unit in this part.

To further explore the ORs of glucose level and variability to AKI, a restricted cubic spline curve was constructed to assess the association between TWAG and the outcome. CV of glucose and AKI using five knots set at 5, 25, 50, 75, and 95 percentiles. We drew restricted cubic splines function curves to show the relationship between the exposure and the risk of AKI.

Sensitivity analyses were also conducted to evaluate the impact of TWAG and glucose CV on in-hospital AKI: first, we repeated all analysis among complete cases without imputation; second, we only used patients with more than five times blood glucose test to repeat the association analysis; third, models were built among important subgroups, and interaction effects were tested.

Statistical analyses were performed using SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC) and R software (version 4.2.3; R Development Core Team, Vienna, Austria). Data visualization was performed using the ggplot2 package in R. A p value < .05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Patient characteristics

Patients’ characteristics are summarized in Table 1. Among all including 2,454 prediabetes CAD patients, the average age was 62.6 ± 10.3 years, and 666 (27.1%) of them are females. 20.4% of them were diagnosed with AMI at admission, and 21.8% of them were admitted to surgical departments. The total average HbA1c level was 6.0% ± 0.2% at admission. Regarding kidney function, median SCr was 76.5 (66.5, 87.2) at admission, and median baseline eGFR was 90.0 (77.8, 102.2) mL/min/1.73 m2, with 49.92% of patients having eGFR >90 mL/min/1.73 m2 and 50.08% having eGFR between 60 and 90 mL/min/1.73 m2.

The median [IQR] glucose level of four TWAG quantiles were 5.2 (4.9, 5.4), 5.9 (5.7, 6.0), 6.6 (6.4, 6.8), and 7.8 (7.3, 8.5), separately, with significantly increasing trend (p for trend <.01). Four times of blood glucose were measured among all patients. The median [IQR] value of TWAG was 6.2 (5.6, 7.0) mmol/L, and the median [IQR] value of CV was 0.2 (0.1, 0.3) (Table 1, Figures 1(C) and 2(D)). Positive correlation between TWAG and CV of glucose was found (Person’s correlation r = 0.47, p value < .01; Figure 1(E)).

One hundred and fifty-nine AKI was identified among all including patient, with the proportion of 6.5%. 17 (2.8%), 25 (4.1%), 42 (6.8%), and 75 (12.2%) were found among four TWAG groups, and 10 (1.6%), 34 (5.5%), 51 (8.3%), and 64 (10.4%) were found among four CV groups. Among all AKI events, 68 (43%) happened in first seven days, with significant difference among TWAG and CV groups (Table 1, Supplementary Figure 3).

Association of TWAG, CV of glucose, and in-hospital AKI

The OR (95% CI) of AKI according to TWAG and CV of glucose and their strata are shown in Table 2. In the univariate model, each 1 mmol/L increase in TWAG was associated with a 1.51-fold (95% CI: 1.36–1.68) higher incidence of AKI. Following stepwise adjustment for covariates, the OR was 1.50 (95% CI: 1.35–1.67). Compared with those at the lowest TWAG quantile, patients at the Q3 and Q4 quantile had significant increasing risk, with ORs of 2.48 (1.36, 4.52) and 4.90 (2.73, 8.81).

Adjusting TWAG and CV at the same time.

Glucose variability, measured by CV, also demonstrated a positive correlation with AKI; each 0.1-unit rise in CV was linked to an estimated 44% increase in risk. Compared with those at the lowest CV quantile, patients at the Q3 and Q4 quantile had significant increasing risk, with ORs of 4.87 (2.40, 9.85) and 6.40 (3.19, 12.83). When TWAG and glucose CV were simultaneously adjusted, both remained positively associated with AKI. Restricted cubic spline analysis demonstrated a rising trend in the ORs for AKI as levels of TWAG and glucose CV increased (Figure 2). Similar results were found in complete cases sensitive analysis (Supplementary Table 1 and Figure 4). Among patients with more than five blood glucose measurements, the ORs became smaller but still significant compared with full cohort (Supplementary Table 2).

Subgroup analyses consistently demonstrated a positive association between TWAG, CV, and AKI. No differences were found among different sex, age, smoking and drinking alcohol history, or disease history such as hypertension, dyslipidemia, and myocardial infarction. Similarly, no significant differences were observed across in-hospital treatment subgroups such as patients receiving SGLT2i and ACEI/ARB/ARNI. Among patients with PCI history or poor heart function (LVEF < 40%), the OR of TWAG was higher than those without these features (Table 3).

Discussion

Our study showed that TWAG or glucose variability is associated with AKI in patients with CAD and prediabetes. To our knowledge, this is the first study to focus on the effect of glycemic fluctuations on in-hospital AKI in prediabetes among CAD. In this study, TWAG and glucose variability were viewed as indicators of glycemic control and glycemic fluctuations, which has the advantage of eliminating bias due to varying time intervals between blood glucose tests.

Prediabetes is the intermediate stage between normal blood glucose levels and diabetic hyperglycemia, which impact 720 million people worldwide, with approximately one-fifth of adults in the United States unaware of their prediabetic condition [11–13]. Diagnosis of prediabetes usually relies on blood tests, either fasting blood glucose or HbA1c [14,15], with stricter or broader criteria from the American Diabetes Association (ADA) or World Health Organization (WHO) [16]. Patients with prediabetes exhibit a higher all-cause mortality and disability rate compared to those with normal glucose tolerance, and this increased risk of disability is independent of whether they eventually progress to diabetes [17,18]. Moreover, previous studies have shown that individuals with prediabetes have a higher risk of developing CKD, hypertension, and arterial atherosclerosis compared to those with normal blood glucose levels. In addition, prediabetes can accelerate the progression of CKD, hypertension, and arterial atherosclerosis, posing a serious threat to patient health [19–22]. Compared to sustained hyperglycemia, fluctuating high blood glucose levels exert a greater toxic effect on the body, leading to more severe oxidative stress responses, generating more reactive oxygen species (ROS), and causing more serious damage to endothelial cells. Additionally, repeated fluctuations in blood glucose levels enhance the adhesion between monocytes and endothelial cells, resulting in adverse outcomes [23,24]. At the systemic level, glucose fluctuations can enhance sympathetic nervous system activity and activate the renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system (RAAS), both of which are involved in hemodynamic regulation and have significant impacts on renal function [25,26]. Sympathetic nervous system excitation can cause renal vasoconstriction, reducing renal blood flow and glomerular filtration rate (GFR), thereby exacerbating renal ischemia and injury [27]. Thus, the ESC guidelines recommend that all patients with cardiovascular disease be screened for diabetes and assessed for risk of nephropathy [1]. However, this recommendation should not be limited to diabetic patients but also extend to those with prediabetes, emphasizing the critical role of early blood sugar assessment. Our findings suggest that glycemic variability may have a significant impact on renal outcomes. This highlights the need for future studies to investigate whether interventions targeting glucose fluctuations can improve kidney health and inform updates to clinical guidelines.

Beyond the HbA1c reflects long-term glucose control, glucose variability is utilized to represent the short-term fluctuations in blood glucose levels of hospitalized patients [28,29]. Nam et al. found that intraoperative glucose variability in cardiac surgery was associated with AKI [8]. A study by Yoo et al. reported that an increase in perioperative glucose variability was an independent risk factor for postoperative AKI in liver transplant patients, whereas hyperglycemia was not associated with the development of postoperative AKI [7]. Despite the acknowledged importance of glucose variability in monitoring, it falls short in depicting cumulative glucose exposure over time. To bridge this gap, the TWAG metric has been introduced. TWAG evaluates glycemic control by calculating the mean glucose level, considering both the glucose concentrations and the duration between these measurements. This dual consideration allows TWAG to offer a holistic assessment of glycemic management and its ramifications on patient outcomes [30,31]. Our investigation delves into the association between TWAG or CV of glucose and AKI among individuals with coexisting CAD and prediabetes. The results corroborate prior studies, emphasizing the critical role of continuous glucose monitoring and the adoption of stringent glycemic control strategies in patients with concurrent CAD and prediabetes. The efficacy of intensive glycemic control for improving cardio-renal outcomes has been demonstrated in various clinical contexts, including patients with renovascular hypertension treated with renal artery stenting [32], and in preventing contrast-induced nephropathy in high-risk populations [33]. Additionally, emerging evidence suggests that SGLT2 inhibitors may provide protective effects against contrast-induced nephropathy [34], potentially offering a novel therapeutic approach for renal protection in patients undergoing contrast-enhanced procedures. These findings collectively highlight the importance of glycemic management beyond mere vascular intervention for optimal renal outcomes in patients with metabolic abnormalities.

To further clarify the assumed causal relationships and potential confounding factors in our study, we have provided directed acyclic graph (DAG) as supplementary figures. As shown, age, sex, disease history, health behaviors, and health status at admission may confound the association between glucose metrics (TWAG or CV) and AKI. While we adjusted for several measured confounders, unmeasured factors such as medication adherence, diet, and psychosocial stress could still influence both glucose variability and AKI risk. Although our study demonstrates an association between glucose variability and AKI, we acknowledge the possibility of reverse causality. AKI may influence glucose metabolism, potentially confounding the observed associations [35]. Future prospective studies monitoring glucose and renal function are needed to elucidate the causal relationship between them. Our subgroup analyses suggest potential effect modifications by factors such as PCI history and LVEF <40%, although these exploratory findings should be interpreted with caution due to limited sample sizes in some subgroups. These results indicate that patients with prior vascular interventions or compromised cardiac function may be particularly vulnerable to the adverse renal effects of glycemic fluctuations, warranting further investigation in larger cohorts with adequate power for interaction testing.

The concept of cardiovascular–kidney–metabolic (CKM) syndrome, recently introduced by the American Heart Association, emphasizes the intricate interrelationships between cardiovascular disease, CKD, and metabolic disorders [36,37]. Early recognition and intervention of CKM syndrome is the key to curbing its potentially deleterious cascade, breaking the vicious cycle, and improving patient prognosis [38,39]. This study sheds light on the complex relationship between glycemic abnormalities, CAD and renal dysfunction in CKM syndrome. While previous studies have focused on the impact of hyperglycemia on the progression of renal disease in hospitalized diabetic patients, the present study extends further to the important stage of prediabetes. By strictly monitoring the glycemic parameters of hospitalized CAD patients, early detection and intervention during hospitalization can be expected to reduce the extent of renal injury, protect their vital organ function, and reduce the risk of cardiovascular complications.

Limitations

Few limitations should be emphasized. As an observational study, the cause-relationship between controlling glycemia and AKI was unexplored. Future prospective cohort studies and randomized controlled trials are needed to validate our findings and evaluate the efficacy and safety of glycemic control interventions targeting this population. Second, glucose measurements were obtained on-demand without a standardized timing protocol, although at least three measurements per patient were recorded, variability in fasting and postprandial sampling may have influenced the calculation of glucose variability metrics such as the CV. Further studies by continuous glucose monitoring would have provided more comprehensive and detailed data on glycemic excursions. Additionally, while we adjusted for multiple clinical and laboratory parameters reflecting organ dysfunction, we did not have specific data on critical care parameters such as vasopressor use or ICU admission. This may affect the interpretation of our findings regarding AKI events occurring early during hospitalization. Last, as a pragmatic clinical database, long-term outcomes remain in the process of being collected. Future studies with follow-up data would provide insight into the long-term effects of fluctuating blood glucose profiles on renal function in CAD patients with prediabetes.

Conclusions

In conclusion, our study reveals an association between TWAG or CV of glucose and AKI in individuals with both CAD and prediabetes. These findings highlight the potential value of continuous glucose monitoring and suggest that managing glycemic variability may be important in this population. Randomized controlled trials are warranted to determine whether targeted glycemic control strategies can effectively reduce AKI risk and improve renal outcomes in CAD patients with prediabetes.

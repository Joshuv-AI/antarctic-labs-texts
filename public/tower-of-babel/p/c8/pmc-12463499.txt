Health literacy for patient education and counseling in the genomic era: challenges and perspectives
Authors: Henri Setiawan, Karolus Wangi, Fitri Arofiati
Published: 2025

To the Editor,

Rapid advancements in the genomics field have revolutionized healthcare practice, creating opportunities for diagnosis, prevention, and treatment of genetic diseases, while also posing challenges for healthcare professionals in delivering effective education and counseling for patients. Genomics, as the study of complete genetic information, requires adequate literacy as the primary key to better understand genomic concepts, patient care, ethical implications, autonomy, and privacy issues, including social dimensions of genetic testing, to empower patients in making informed health decisions [1]. Genomic literacy requires healthcare professionals to communicate complex ideas and terminology, while patients who often lack a background in genetics rely on them to interpret the results from increasingly accessible commercial genetic tests. However, widespread access to genetic information must be accompanied by sufficient knowledge that can support healthcare professionals in their critical role of bridging the gap between technological advancements and patient knowledge [2]. This letter offers our perspectives and provides a preliminary highlight concerning challenges and strategies in health literacy regarding patient education and counseling in the genomic era.

Healthcare professionals’ literacy challenges

Healthcare professionals usually face significant challenges in clinical practice, such as understanding the complex genomic information relating to anomalies of gene mutations, single-nucleotide polymorphism, and hereditary disease predispositions, which require a different level of understanding and are the easiest way to simplify technical terms and details without sacrificing scientific accuracy [3]. For instance, explaining BRCA1/BRCA2 gene-related cancer risks to patients requires an evidence-based strategy to balance effective communication between patients and healthcare professionals in terms of minimizing patients’ psychological harm, while on the other hand, suggesting premarital genetic screening as health education for genetic conditions may raise ethical issues related to social stigma or misleading decisions. Additionally, time constraints in resource-limited healthcare systems pose a challenge to adequate genetic counseling, as it involves addressing complex concepts, answering questions, and providing emotional support beyond the typical 1‒3 sessions (number of common meeting sessions in genetic counseling), particularly for decisions with long-term implications. In addition, limited access to genetic specialists shifts the responsibility of counseling to general practitioners or nurses, who may lack specialized training in genetic counseling, thus risking gaps in patient education and counseling quality [4].

In the genomic era, inadequate genomic literacy among healthcare professionals presents a significant challenge in implementing effective genetic testing. The application of genetic testing not only involves medical aspects but also raises complex ethical, psychological, and social implications [5]. The main barrier is the limited understanding of healthcare professionals regarding genetic concepts and interpreting genetic testing results. At the primary care level, most healthcare professionals have not received adequate training in genetic counseling, thereby risking the provision of inaccurate and insufficient comprehensive patient health information [2], yet healthcare professionals are expected to deliver complex genetic information in a way that is understandable to patients and reduces anxiety risk, which may lead to incorrect health decision-making. This could, in turn, impact on legal regulations and ethical concerns surrounding genetic data privacy and discrimination in health insurance and employment [3].

Strategies to address the challenges

Strengthen genomic knowledge

To address these challenges, healthcare professionals must strengthen their genomics knowledge. This may include integrating recent basic foundational genetics knowledge, such as genetic terminology, application of genetic testing and interpretation, psychosocial issues, and ethical implications, into medical and health-related nursing curricula. In support of that, practical training through case studies and clinical simulations complemented with theoretical modules is a relevant strategy to enhance the clinical application of genomic knowledge. Moreover, specialized genetic counseling training is essential to develop healthcare professionals’ communication skills for explaining complex information to patients. For practical reasons, online training with certification programs could present an alternative option to effectively improve genomic competencies, enabling accurate diagnoses and interventions, improving ethical sensitivity, and initiating patient-centered genetic services [2-4]. This aligns with recent recommendations on genomic counseling practices, which emphasize the need for tailored strategies in gene screening and the importance of equipping healthcare professionals with foundational genomic competencies [6].

Using visual aids

Genomic concepts often involve complex and highly specialized scientific information, which presents significant challenges for patients. Therefore, visual aids can facilitate visual communication that is generally easy to absorb [7]. Visual aids such as diagrams and infographics can illustrate the structure of DNA, the process of DNA transcription and translation, and the relationships between genes more intuitively, making it easier for patients or those without an academic background in the specific field to understand. Educational videos can also be utilized to provide dynamic explanations, incorporating audio, visual elements, and narration to enhance the learning experience [8]. To support this approach, several open-access platforms can be recommended to healthcare professionals and patients alike, such as Your Genome (https://www.yourgenome.org) and MedlinePlus Genetics (https://medlineplus.gov/genetics/), which offer high-quality visual materials that explain basic to advanced genetic concepts.

Interactive educational resources

Information and communication technology provides significant opportunities to enhance patient and public education. Interactive educational resources such as mobile applications or online platforms enable patients to access information anywhere and at any time. These applications can be equipped with interactive features such as quizzes, simulations, and discussion forums, which encourage active user engagement. These features help patients explore content in greater depth, reinforce their learning, and receive immediate feedback. Additionally, online platforms can be tailored for individual levels of understanding, thereby offering a more relevant educational experience. Recommended resources include Learn Genetics by the University of Utah (https://learn.genetics.utah.edu), which provides engaging educational tools, and My Cancer Genome (https://www.mycancergenome.org), which offers personalized, up-to-date, and clinically relevant information to patients and providers. Integrating such platforms into genomic education enhances patients’ understanding of genetics and supports better health decision-making, while also improving public genomic literacy [4].

Case study discussion

Collaboration between healthcare professionals and genetic experts is essential to improve genetic education and counseling. Healthcare professionals such as general practitioners, nurses, and midwives play a key role in providing patients’ health information, including genetic aspects. However, some patients’ genetic conditions/diseases may be more complex and thus require an in-depth understanding, making collaboration with genetic specialists or counselors crucial. These genetic experts possess the expertise to analyze the genetic factors influencing health conditions and provide more detailed explanations of genetic disease risks and the implications of genetic test results [9].

Referring complex cases to genetic experts will ensure that patients can access in-depth counseling services and become well-informed about preference-based care options. Moreover, a professional genetic counselor can help patients understand the health risk factors associated with specific genetic conditions and provide insights into the potential impacts on individual and family health. This approach enhances healthcare quality by offering precise medical information to better understand patients’ genetic conditions and support health decisions based on patients’ preferences, considering their genetic factors [10].

Support patients’ genomic literacy

Finally, regarding the role of healthcare professionals in supporting patients’ processes in understanding genetics and genomics, it is important to emphasize the shared responsibility between healthcare systems and patients in improving genomic literacy. While patients are encouraged to play an active role, such as accessing available resources including books, scientific articles, seminars, online video streaming, and health education platforms, it is equally essential that healthcare institutions, policymakers, and professionals provide enabling environments that support learning. Barriers such as limited access to educational materials, low baseline literacy, or time constraints must be addressed through institutional policies and inclusive health education strategies. Support from healthcare professionals to guide patients through complex genomic concepts, alongside systemic investments in accessible learning platforms, will ultimately facilitate more equitable and informed health decision- making [11].

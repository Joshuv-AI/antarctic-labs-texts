Response to Letter to Editor—The Quality of Health Towards Resilience Community in Indonesia: A Resilience Policy Discourse
Authors: Afroza Begum, Md Azhar Uddin
Published: 2025

To the Editor,

We sincerely thank the readers for their thoughtful comments and for drawing valuable connections between our study, “Comparative Analysis of Preferences and Willingness to Pay for COVID‐19 Vaccine Among Individuals With and Without Chronic Diseases: A Discrete Choice Experiment Approach” [1], and broader health system resilience challenges during the COVID‐19 pandemic, particularly in the Indonesian context [2].

Our study aimed to address the lack of empirical evidence on preferences and willingness to pay (WTP) for COVID‐19 vaccines among patients with chronic diseases, a group at heightened risk for severe outcomes. As the reader rightly observes, such evidence is crucial not only for vaccine procurement but also for informing health policy design in low‐ and middle‐income countries, where system‐level vulnerabilities (such as fragmented governance, under‐funded infrastructure, and insufficient outreach to high‐risk groups) exacerbated the health crisis.

We agree that future research should explore the integration of preference data (such as WTP) into broader resilience governance frameworks. A systems‐level approach that encompasses social, psychological, institutional, and legal dimensions, as outlined in the reader's remarks, can further enhance pandemic preparedness and equitable service delivery.

Once again, we appreciate the reader's insightful remarks and hope that this dialogue contributes to a richer cross‐country learning process in the post‐pandemic era.

Clinical outcome of Nd:YAG laser and bioactive glass combined use for dentin hypersensitivity after scaling and root planning: a short-term comparative study
Authors: Kaixuan Yan, Wei Si, Jingxin Guo, Jing Liu, Yu Zheng, Xiaowei Hou, Shifeng Fan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimThis randomized controlled trial aimed to assess the efficacy of a combined Nd:YAG laser and bioactive glass (BAG) for dentin hypersensitivity following scaling and root planning (SRP). The study compared various desensitization protocols and evaluated the time-dependent effects of immediate desensitization therapy post-SRP.Methods80 patients with chronic periodontitis (200 sensitive teeth) were randomly assigned to five groups: group A (BAG), group B (Nd:YAG laser), group C (Nd:YAG laser + BAG), group D (BAG + Nd:YAG laser), and group E (control). Hypersensitivity was assessed via air-blast stimulation and measured on a Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) at baseline (T0), immediately after intervention (T1), and at 1-, 4-, and 8-week (T2–T4) post-treatment. Between-group comparisons of baseline characteristics and post-treatment measurements were analyzed with one-way ANOVA. Longitudinal comparisons across timepoints were evaluated using repeated-measures ANOVA. Categorical variables were compared by chi-square test.ResultsNo significant differences in demographic characteristics or baseline VAS scores were observed across the groups before treatment (P > 0.05). All interventional groups (A–D) showed significant and sustained reductions in VAS scores relative to both baseline and control (all P < 0.001), with group D achieving the greatest improvement at T4 (2.00 ± 0.68, P < 0.05 vs. group C). Effectiveness rates were also significantly higher in groups A–D than in group E (all P < 0.001). Combination therapies (C and D) proved superior to monotherapies (A and B), with group D maintaining the highest effectiveness rate through T4 (92.5%). A gradual decline in effectiveness was observed over time, being least pronounced in group D.ConclusionsThe combined application of BAG and Nd:YAG laser therapy demonstrated a significant reduction in dentin hypersensitivity post-SRP, exhibiting superior and sustained efficacy compared to monotherapies. Furthermore, the sequential treatment involving BAG applied prior to Nd:YAG laser irradiation showed the most pronounced therapeutic effect in symptom alleviation.Keywords: Dentin hypersensitivity, Biocompatible materials, Nd:YAG laser, Time factors, Chronic periodontitis, Scaling and root planning

Background

Periodontal disease is highly prevalent in China, particularly in the elderly population. It is not only detrimental to oral health but also has an impact on systemic health of patients. With ongoing improvements in living standards and increasing dissemination of oral health knowledge, there has been a growing public awareness regarding the importance of periodontal therapy and maintenance. However, postoperative monitoring following SRP revealed that a significant proportion of patients developed dentin hypersensitivity (DH) [1]. Notably, 19.3% of these patients deferred subsequent treatment due to moderate-to-severe discomfort, defined as a VAS score ≥ 7 [2]. This delay contributed to an increased interruption rate in the standardized periodontal therapeutic sequence, potentially compromising long-term clinical outcomes [3].

Although the precise mechanism underlying DH remains incompletely elucidated, the hydrodynamic theory is currently the most widely accepted explanation [4]. According to this theory, external stimuli—such as mechanical, thermal, osmotic, or chemical triggers—applied to exposed dentin can induce fluid movement within the dentinal tubules. This intracanalicular flow activates intrapulpal nerve endings, resulting in the characteristic short, sharp pain associated with DH [5, 6]. Guided by this mechanism, clinical management of DH primarily aims to occlude dentinal tubules, inhibit abnormal fluid movement within the tubules, or diminish the excitability of intradental nerves [7–9].

For cases of mild DH, clinical guidelines recommend non-invasive interventions as first-line management [10, 11]. Among these, desensitizing dentifrices and mouthwashes are preferred choices for home-based care due to their ease of use and minimal invasiveness [12, 13]. Potassium salts are commonly employed as active ingredients in desensitizing toothpaste. Their mechanism of action primarily involves the diffusion of potassium ions to the nerve terminals within dentinal tubules, leading to progressive depolarization of pulpal nerves. This process reduces neuronal excitability and interferes with pain signal transmission, thereby alleviating hypersensitivity symptoms [14]. However, due to the high water solubility and inadequate adhesion of potassium salt compounds to the tooth surface, their desensitizing effects may diminish over time, leading to potential recurrence of DH [15]. Furthermore, Systematic reviews indicate that patient adherence to desensitization therapy is influenced by multiple factors, including compliance with oral hygiene instructions, salivary buffering capacity, and dietary habits [16]. These variables may compromise therapeutic continuity and efficacy, potentially leading to inconsistent clinical outcomes.

A variety of desensitizing agents are commonly employed in clinical practice, including fluoride compounds and resin adhesives, among others [17]. In the present study, we utilized a bioactive glass paste composed of calcium sodium phosphosilicate—a nanobioactive mineral—within a water-soluble polyethylene glycol base. This formulation undergoes rapid and sustained ionic reactions in the salivary environment, facilitating the formation of a protective hydroxyapatite-like layer [18].

The advent of laser technology has expanded the arsenal of available treatments for dentin hypersensitivity. The thermal effect generated by laser irradiation facilitates the instantaneous fusion of mineral components on the dental surface, accompanied by protein coagulation and occlusion of dentinal tubules, thereby achieving effective desensitization [19]. Furthermore, through photobiomodulatory effects, laser irradiation can induce morphological and functional alterations in sensory nerve terminals, transiently inhibiting nociceptive signal conduction and thereby reducing neuronal hyperexcitability in the irradiated area [20, 21]. Subsequent studies have shown that the combined therapy of desensitizers and laser treatment demonstrates significant advantages in the treatment of dentin hypersensitivity, with its efficacy superior to that of a single desensitization method [22].

Although numerous studies have investigated the use of desensitizing agents or laser therapy in the management of dentin hypersensitivity, there remains a scarcity of research systematically evaluating the combined efficacy of Nd:YAG laser and BAG, particularly regarding the influence of application sequence on clinical outcomes. Furthermore, limited evidence exists regarding the immediate application of desensitizing protocols following SRP and their sustained clinical efficacy. This study aimed to evaluate the combined application of Nd:YAG laser and BAG in the treatment of DH after SRP, to compare the effects of different desensitization strategies, and to assess the clinical outcomes of immediate post-SRP desensitization therapy.

Materials and methods

The randomized controlled trial was conducted at the Department of Oral Medicine of Hebei Medical University Third Hospital, from September 2021 and June 2023. The study protocol received approval from the Medical Ethics Committee of the same institution (approval number: W2022-064-1). All patients were fully informed of the study objectives and provided written informed consent prior to enrollment.

Scaling and root planning treatment

All enrolled patients underwent full-mouth SRP treatment in a single session. The procedure was performed using ultrasonic scaler (P5 Newtron, Acteon Satelec, France) combined with hand instruments (Gracey curettes, Hu-Friedy, Chicago, USA). Following SRP treatment, irrigated with 0.12% Chlorhexidine (Chlorhexidine Gluconate Gargle, Jinzhou Bentian Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., China) for 1 min. All participants received standardized oral hygiene instruction in the Bass brushing technique and were provided with the same model of soft-bristled toothbrush (Systema, Lion, Japan). They were instructed to brush twice daily for 3 min each time throughout the 8-week study period, and the patients were advised not to eat or drink extremely hot or cold foods. The SRP treatments were performed by the same experienced periodontist SW.

Assessment criteria

Air-blast evaluation

Tested tooth was isolated by cotton ball to prevent interference from adjacent teeth. DH was assessed by applying a 1-s air blast (60 ± 5 psi, 20–24 °C) from a dental syringe directed perpendicularly to the middle third of the buccal surface from a distance of approximately 1 cm. The level of discomfort was evaluated using a VAS scale, ranging from 0 (no discomfort) to 10 (intolerable pain) [25]. Participants independently indicated their perceived pain level by marking a vertical mark on the 10-cm VAS, and the corresponding score was recorded. Air-blast evaluation was performed 12–24 h after SRP treatment. Patients exhibiting at least two hypersensitive teeth with VAS ≥ 3.0 were eligible for inclusion.

Study design

In this clinical study, 80 patients (37 male, 43 female) were enrolled, contributing a total of 200 sensitive teeth. These teeth were randomly assigned to five groups using a random number table.

A randomized controlled, double-blind trial design was implemented. Treatment allocation was concealed until immediately before intervention. To maintain blinding, all patients were equipped with opaque sleep masks and laser-protective goggles.

All groups underwent a standardized two-cycle procedure, each cycle consisting of both a desensitizer application phase and a laser irradiation phase, as follows:

Group A (BAG): BAG application followed by simulated laser irradiation in the first cycle; placebo application followed by simulated laser irradiation in the second cycle.

Group A (BAG): BAG application followed by simulated laser irradiation in the first cycle; placebo application followed by simulated laser irradiation in the second cycle.

Group B (Nd:YAG laser): Placebo application followed by Nd:YAG laser irradiation in the first cycle; placebo application followed by simulated laser irradiation in the second cycle.

Group B (Nd:YAG laser): Placebo application followed by Nd:YAG laser irradiation in the first cycle; placebo application followed by simulated laser irradiation in the second cycle.

Group C (Nd:YAG laser + BAG): Placebo application followed by Nd:YAG laser irradiation in the first cycle; BAG application followed by simulated laser irradiation in the second cycle.

Group C (Nd:YAG laser + BAG): Placebo application followed by Nd:YAG laser irradiation in the first cycle; BAG application followed by simulated laser irradiation in the second cycle.

Group D (BAG + Nd:YAG laser): BAG application followed by simulated laser irradiation in the first cycle; placebo application followed by Nd:YAG laser irradiation in the second cycle.

Group D (BAG + Nd:YAG laser): BAG application followed by simulated laser irradiation in the first cycle; placebo application followed by Nd:YAG laser irradiation in the second cycle.

Group E (Control): Placebo application followed by simulated laser irradiation in both cycles.

Group E (Control): Placebo application followed by simulated laser irradiation in both cycles.

For desensitizer application, BAG and placebo were applied to designated area using a microbrush with a gentle but firm motion for 2 min until the area was thoroughly dried. Laser irradiation was performed in pulsed contact mode, perpendicular to the surface of designated area, at parameters of 100 mJ, 1 W, and 10 Hz. Each tooth received four irradiations of 15 s each, totalling 60 s, with 10-s intervals for thermal relaxation. Simulated procedures used an inactivated handpiece to emulate the sound of laser operation without emitting energy. These measures were implemented to ensure that patients remained effectively blinded throughout the study.

The baseline VAS assessment (T0) was conducted 12–24 h after SRP and prior to any desensitization intervention. Follow-up sensitivity evaluations were performed on the selected teeth, with VAS scores recorded immediately after desensitization (T1), as well as at 1-week (T2), 4-week (T3), and 8-week (T4) post-treatment. All desensitization procedures were carried out by the experienced periodontist (SW), who had received standardized training prior to the study. VAS assessments were consistently performed using the same standardized method throughout the trial by GJX, who remained blinded to patient group allocation for the entire duration of the study.

Efficacy evaluation criteria

Based on the VAS scores obtained before and after desensitization treatment, the extent of pain relief in DH was categorized into two outcomes: effective and invalid. An effective response was defined as a reduction in VAS score of more than 25%, whereas a reduction of 25% or less was considered invalid. The effectiveness rate (ER) was calculated as follows: ER (%) = (number of teeth with effective response/total number of teeth treated) × 100% [26].

Statistical analysis

Data collection was performed using Microsoft Excel. All statistical analyses were conducted with SPSS version 21.0. The normal distribution of the data was confirmed by the Shapiro–Wilk test (P > 0.05). Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation. Comparisons of baseline characteristics and post-treatment measurements among the five groups were performed using one-way ANOVA, with post-hoc pairwise comparisons conducted using the least significant difference (LSD) test. For comparisons among multiple groups across different timepoints, repeated-measures ANOVA was applied. Categorical data were presented as frequencies and percentages, and group differences were assessed using the Chi-square test. A significance level of α = 0.05 was adopted, and P ≤ 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

All 80 participants completed the study period. No adverse events—such as pulp necrosis—were observed throughout the trial.

No significant differences in baseline VAS scores were observed among the five groups prior to desensitization (mean ± SD range: 4.93 ± 0.89 to 5.20 ± 0.91; F = 0.461, P = 0.764). Throughout the 8-week observation period, groups A, B, C, and D exhibited substantial and progressive reductions in pain severity, while group E (control) showed no notable change from baseline levels (T4: 5.08 ± 0.92, P = 0.978 vs. baseline).

Compared with baseline (T0), significant decreases in VAS scores were evident immediately after desensitization (T1) and were sustained at all subsequent timepoints (T2, T3, T4) in groups A–D (all P < 0.001). The most pronounced reduction was observed at T1, with scores declining to 1.75 ± 0.93 (group A), 1.68 ± 0.73 (group B), 1.45 ± 0.68 (group C), and 1.33 ± 0.62 (group D). By T4, mean VAS scores had further evolved to 2.65 ± 0.86, 2.75 ± 0.78, 2.35 ± 0.62, and 2.00 ± 0.68 for groups A–D, respectively, reflecting pronounced and sustained clinical improvements. This represented mean reductions from baseline of approximately 2.43, 2.18, 2.70 and 3.05 points for groups A–D, respectively (all P < 0.001).

Intergroup comparisons revealed that all active treatment groups demonstrated significantly lower VAS scores than group E at every post-baseline assessment (all P < 0.001). No statistically significant differences were observed between the monotherapy groups (A and B) at any timepoint (all P > 0.05). In contrast, the combination therapy groups (C and D) showed superior efficacy compared to groups A and B throughout all follow-up periods. Notably, group D exhibited the most substantial improvement, with significantly lower VAS scores than group C at every post-desensitization evaluation; this difference was particularly evident at T4 (2.00 ± 0.68 vs. 2.35 ± 0.62, P < 0.05), underscoring the enhanced effectiveness of the interventional approaches. Discussion

Dental sensitivity is one of the most common complaints when patients visit the Department of Oral Medicine. The prevalence of DH is 4.8% to 62.3% worldwide [27]. DH can arise as a result of enamel loss caused by erosion, abrasion, attrition, as well as cementum loss typically subsequent to gingival recession. Von Troil et al. [28] reported that the incidence of DH after periodontal treatment was 54–55%, with its intensity peaking from the first week to the eight week after treatment. Although desensitization treatment could alleviate or eliminate patients’ symptoms, patients still had experienced sensitivity, discomfort, and even pain, which not only affected the quality of life during eating, drinking, and tooth-brushing, but also often became one of the main factors hindering patients from active cooperation with periodontal treatment [29, 30]. Both domestic and foreign scholars had performed multiple studies on treatment of DH, few researchers had studied the clinical effect and significance of immediate desensitization after SRP treatment. In this study, immediate desensitization was applied after SRP treatment, trying to figure out the best way of preventing severe sensitivity from occurring.

Researchers have found that SRP may contribute to cementum removal and tubule exposure, and further histological study showed that exposed tubules were more and tubule diameters were significantly larger in hypersensitive area compared to non-sensitive surface [31]. In this study, a novel bioactive glass tooth desensitizer was used, which was composed of a nano-sized bioactive mineral powder (calcium sodium phosphosilicate), polyethylene glycol, etc. In the salivary environment, it could produce a rapid and sustained reaction, release calcium and phosphate active ingredients. The deposit calcium and phosphate ions formed a stable crystalline hydroxyphosphate limestone layer structure on and within the dentin tubules, closing the dentin tubules and alleviating allergic symptoms [32]. The results of this study showed that the VAS scores measured in group A were lower than those in group E at different stage after desensitization, and the effectiveness rate after desensitization was greater than that in group E, which indicates that the use of BAG paste immediately after SRP treatment could effectively improve the symptoms of dental sensitivity in the short term; this finding was consistent with the results of Amaechi et al. [33]. In group A, although the VAS scores after desensitization increased over time, they were still lower than the baseline scores before desensitization, indicating that immediate desensitization with BAG paste after SRP treatment could reduce or alleviate the occurrence of sensitive symptoms after periodontal treatment.

Over time, the development of laser technology has provided clinicians with more options for desensitization treatment. At present, many kinds of lasers are used to treat DH clinically, including Nd:YAG lasers, Er:YAG lasers, Er:Cr:YSGG lasers, C02 lasers, and GaAIAs lasers [34]. In contrast to desensitizers, lasers can induce changes in the morphology and characteristics of the dentin surface. Tabatabaei et al. reported that the short-term effect of Nd:YAG laser treatment on dentin hypersensitivity for 3–6 months is significantly greater than that of semiconductor laser or drug desensitization [35]. Later studies revealed that an excessively high-energy laser will cause irreversible damage to the tooth, pulp and periodontal tissue. Farmakis et al. [36] reported that the power of Nd:YAG laser irradiation at 1 W is better than that at 0.5 W for sealing the dentinal tubules. Based on the reference and patient's subjective feelings, the desensitization treatment mode recommended by the manufacturer of Nd:YAG laser was used in this study. The Nd:YAG laser was performed perpendicularly to the surface, and the power was set at 1 W, 100 mJ, and 10 Hz. Four irradiations of 15 s each were performed, totalling 60 s of irradiation. The results of this study showed that the VAS scores of group B were lower than those of group E at different stage after desensitization, and the effectiveness rate after desensitization was greater than that of group E, which indicated that Nd:YAG laser irradiation at 1 W, 100 mJ, and 10 Hz immediately after SRP treatment could effectively alleviate dental sensitivity within a short period, which was consistent with the results of Liu Zihan et al. [19]. In group B, although the VAS scores after desensitization increased over time, they were still lower than the baseline scores before desensitization, indicating that immediate desensitization by Nd:YAG laser irradiation after SRP treatment could reduce or alleviate the occurrence of sensitive symptoms after periodontal treatment. However, there was no significant difference between group A and group B at different stage, indicating that there was no significant difference in the short-term effect of desensitization using BAG paste alone or the Nd:YAG laser alone.

In recent years, the combing use of desensitizers with lasers for desensitization therapy has become a popular research topic. Researchers [37] reported that the fused layer and drug sealing layer composite structure formed by the joint action of a laser and a desensitizer could not only increase the number of sealed dentinal tubules and expand the sealing range, but also stabilize the closed structure and enhance the wear resistance, which played a dual role in desensitization. The results of this study showed that the differences of VAS scores between group C and group D were statistically significant compared with those of group A and group B, indicating that the combined application of BAG and Nd:YAG laser irradiation could effectively reduce the probability of sensitive symptoms after SRP treatment and relieve sensitive symptoms; moreover, the combined using effect was better than that of using one method alone. This finding was consistent with the findings of LEE et al. [22]. Compared with those of group A and group B, the VAS scores of group C and group D did not increase significantly 4 or 8 weeks after desensitization, and the effectiveness rate was greater than that of group A and group B, which indicated that the combined application of BAG and Nd:YAG laser irradiation at 1 W, 100 mJ, and 10 Hz immediately after SRP treatment had a longer desensitization effect.

However, few studies have investigated whether the order of using them has any influence on clinical therapeutic effect. In this study, we compared group C and group D, trying to verify whether the order would affect the desensitization effect. Compared with group C, the VAS scores of group D were significantly lower at each stage after desensitization, and the effectiveness rate was greater as well, which indicated that the desensitization effect of first using BAG then irradiated by Nd:YAG laser was greater than that first using Nd:YAG laser then coated with BAG. In group D, BAG was first used then irradiated by Nd:YAG laser. The advantage of such treatment sequence was that BAG could first bind with calcium ions in the dentin, sealed the dentin tubules and reduced dentin permeability. Then irradiated by Nd:YAG laser could further promote the penetration and action of BAG, while the biostimulatory effect of laser could promote the repair and regeneration of periodontal tissue, thereby enhancing the therapeutic effect.

Chen Shizhao et al. [38] studied the effect of combing use of an Er:YAG laser with BAG on the sealing of dentinal tubules and found that a layer of BAG was first coated on the dentin surface to fuse the unstable smear layer with BAG. Then, laser irradiation was used to form a uniform and dense sealant, which could more effectively seal the dentinal tubules [39, 40]. Moreover, irradiating the dentin surface with a laser enhanced the resistance to physical and chemical stimulation [41]. However, when laser irradiation was applied prior to BAG, the dentin surface exhibited increased roughness and a reduction in dentinal tubule diameter. When BAG was applied to directly adhere to the surface of the smear layer and dentin sealing layer or to seal the narrowed dentinal tubules. Although the desensitization effect could also be achieved, it was not as good as the former due to the uneven surface and the ease of being affected by external factors such as friction. This was consistent with the results obtained in this study, which suggested using a desensitizing agent before laser irradiation to improve therapeutic outcomes.

However, regarding the mechanism of this conclusion, a large number of experiments are still needed for further analysis and research. Moreover, due to the relatively short observation period in this study, the long-term effects cannot be predicted yet. More research is required to confirm the long-term effectiveness of desensitization treatment.

Conclusion

In the short-time clinical study, the combined use of BAG and Nd:YAG laser for immediate desensitization after SRP treatment could effectively reduce dentin hypersensitivity, enhance overall quality of life and increase patient acceptance and satisfaction with the periodontal treatment. Moreover, the sequential protocol involving the application of BAG followed by Nd:YAG laser irradiation demonstrated a more pronounced and durable desensitizing effect.

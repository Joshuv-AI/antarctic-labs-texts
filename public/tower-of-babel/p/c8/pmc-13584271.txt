Trends and cross-county disparities in childhood pneumococcal vaccination in Zhejiang Province, China, 2011–2022
Authors: Ying Wang, Lingxian Qiu, Xuan Deng, Yingping Chen, Yuxia Liang, Menglu Yu, Xiaoping Xu, Xuejiao Pan, Shenyu Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPediatric pneumococcal conjugate vaccine (PCV) and pneumococcal polysaccharide vaccine (PPV23) remain voluntary and out-of-pocket in China, and long-term evidence on market transition and socioeconomic disparities is limited.MethodsWe analyzed vaccination registry data for 2011–2022 birth cohorts across 90 counties in Zhejiang Province, stratified into four Gross Domestic Product (GDP) strata. Descriptive analysis and mapping were combined with a permutation-based transition-year analysis to assess the socioeconomic gradient in the PPV23-to-PCV crossover. Segmented beta-binomial mixed-effects regression models estimated period-specific annual odds ratios (ORs) across vaccine supply periods, accounting for county- and city-level clustering.ResultsAmong 9,699,424 children, coverage for ≥ 1 dose of PCV and PPV23 was 13.5% and 25.7%, respectively. From 2011 to 2022, PCV coverage increased from 0.9% to 48.4%, whereas PPV23 declined from its 2017 peak of 33.7% to 6.3%. The PPV23-to-PCV crossover occurred earlier in higher-GDP counties, showing an ordered socioeconomic gradient (Kruskal-Wallis H = 46.12, permutation P for trend < 0.0001). Segmented models showed substantial local clustering (county-level intraclass correlation coefficient = 0.508). During 2017–2019, annual odds of PCV13 uptake increased nearly three-fold across GDP strata, while PPV23 declined. During 2020–2022, domestic PCV13 expansion produced a parallel increase across strata (annual ORs: 1.38–1.43, P < 0.001), preserving relative economic disparities; meanwhile, accelerated PPV23 decline (annual ORs: 0.53–0.57, P < 0.001) narrowed absolute PPV23 coverage differences by 2022.ConclusionsChina’s pneumococcal vaccine market transition reshaped vaccination equity. Domestic PCV13 expansion increased uptake but did not eliminate socioeconomic disparities. Stronger provider recommendation, targeted affordability measures, and progress toward National Immunization Program inclusion are needed to achieve equitable protection.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-026-15051-7.

Background

Streptococcus pneumoniae (pneumococcus) remains a leading bacterial pathogen responsible for morbidity and mortality among children worldwide [1, 2]. While the integration of pneumococcal conjugate vaccines (PCVs) into National Immunization Programs (NIPs) has successfully mitigated this burden globally [3–5], China continues to classify pneumococcal vaccines as non-NIP vaccines. Under this voluntary, out-of-pocket immunization model [6], the clinical and economic burden of pneumococcal diseases remains substantial, accounting for approximately 4% of all-cause mortality among Chinese children aged 1–59 months in 2017 [7].

To interpret childhood immunization patterns in China, distinguishing between the two available pneumococcal vaccine types is essential. PCVs (7-valent or 13-valent formulations) are protein-conjugated, highly immunogenic in infants, and clinically recommended starting from 6 weeks of age. Conversely, the 23-valent pneumococcal polysaccharide vaccine (PPV23) fails to induce immune memory in children under 2 years of age; clinical guidelines strictly restrict its pediatric use to high-risk populations aged ≥ 2 years as a supplementary booster rather than a routine substitute for PCVs [8–10].

Compounding these clinical distinctions, the childhood pneumococcal vaccine market in China has experienced dramatic supply-side shocks over the past decade. The market withdrawal of imported PCV7 in 2015 created a profound supply gap during 2015–2016 [11, 12], leaving PPV23 as the only accessible choice. Although imported PCV13 was introduced in late 2016, followed by the rollout of domestic PCV13 formulations from 2019 onward [12], the substantial cost differential—with a full PCV series exceeding a single PPV23 dose by over eightfold—is hypothesized to drive a cost-related compromise in vaccine selection, potentially leading to the use of PPV23 beyond its designated high-risk target groups. Despite documented geographical variations in vaccine uptake [13], long-term, population-based evidence simultaneously tracking the market-driven transitions and resulting socioeconomic disparities for both PCVs and PPV23 remains scarce.

To address these critical knowledge gaps, this study analyzes population-based registry data for over 9.6 million children from the Zhejiang Provincial Immunization Information System (ZPIIS) across the 2011–2022 birth cohorts. With its diverse regional economy and decentralized county-level procurement and recommendation mechanisms, Zhejiang provides a suitable setting to evaluate long-term trends and disparities in PCV and PPV23 uptake under a voluntary framework. We address three primary research questions: (1) characterizing long-term coverage, schedule completion, and spatial-temporal expansion patterns via birth-cohort specific proportions; (2) assessing regional transition dynamics by defining a county-level “transition year” where PCV coverage permanently surpasses PPV23; (3) contrasting period-specific annual odds ratios (ORs) across four vaccine supply periods (2011–2014 early market, 2015–2016 PCV supply gap, 2017–2019 imported PCV13 introduction, and 2020–2022 domestic PCV13 expansion) stratified by county-level Gross Domestic Product (GDP) per capita strata using segmented beta-binomial mixed-effects regression. By examining these 12-year dual-vaccine dynamics, our findings reveal how supply shifts and market barriers compound vaccination inequities and clinical guideline deviations. These data provide an empirical foundation to design targeted sub-provincial subsidies and inform a phased framework for incorporating PCVs into China’s NIP.

Materials and methods

Data sources and collection

Vaccination registry data

Pneumococcal vaccination records were extracted from the ZPIIS. Established in 2005, the ZPIIS achieves near-universal coverage of the pediatric population in Zhejiang Province by creating electronic records at birth (in delivery hospitals) or during initial clinic visits, supplemented by quarterly active surveillance for migrant children. We retrieved individual-level records for all children registered in the ZPIIS who were born between 2011 and 2022, with follow-up extending through December 31, 2024. Extracted variables included birth date, vaccination date, vaccine type (PCV7, PCV13, or PPV23), dose number and county of residence. All individual data were completely anonymized prior to analysis.

Covariate and geospatial data

To contextualize coverage rates, county-level GDP per capita data for 2022 were obtained from the Zhejiang Statistical Yearbook 2023 [14]. Given administrative boundary modifications and historical reporting variations, the 2022 GDP per capita was treated as a time-invariant county-level contextual indicator to stratify counties into four socioeconomic tiers (low, lower middle, upper middle, and high). Standard geographic boundary files for Zhejiang Province were downloaded from the National Platform for Common GeoSpatial Information Services [15].

Vaccination status assessment and definitions

Vaccination status and schedule adherence were assessed based on the product package inserts approved by the National Medical Products Administration (NMPA) of China. The recommended immunization schedules for PCVs vary according to age at initiation [see Additional file 1: Table S1]. For infants starting at 6 weeks to 6 months of age, a “3 + 1” series is recommended, consisting of 3 primary doses (at intervals of ≥ 4 or 8 weeks) and a booster dose at 12–15 months. For those initiating at 7–11 months, the standard schedule comprises 2 primary doses (intervals of ≥ 4 or 8 weeks) and a booster dose administered at ≥ 12 months of age and ≥ 2 months after the last primary dose. Modified catch-up schedules apply to older age groups: 2 doses (interval of ≥ 2 months) for toddlers aged 12–23 months, and a single dose for children aged 2–5 years. Notably, the approved age range for imported PCV13 (Pfizer) was restricted to 6 weeks to 15 months from 2016 to 2022 but expanded to 5 years in 2023, whereas PCV7 and domestic PCV13 products have consistently been approved for children aged 6 weeks to 5 years. Meanwhile, PPV23 is recommended as a single dose solely for high-risk children aged ≥ 2 years; revaccination is not recommended for immunocompetent individuals.

For PCV7/13, “primary schedule” was defined as the completion of the required primary doses, and “full schedule” was defined as the completion of the entire series including the booster dose for infants initiating vaccination before 12 months. For children initiating at ≥ 12 months, “full catch-up schedule” was defined as completing the recommended 1- or 2-dose regimen.

Outcome measures and indicators

To quantify vaccine uptake for each birth cohort, denominators were defined as the total number of registered children within the ZPIIS for each specific birth year, while numerators were defined as the counts of children with specific vaccination status of the respective pneumococcal vaccine (PCV7/13 or PPV23). To evaluate compliance and adherence to recommended immunization schedules, the vaccine series completion rate was defined as the proportion of children who completed their age-specific recommended schedules among those who had initiated the respective vaccination series. Vaccination timeliness was evaluated by examining the age-specific proportions within each dose level, characterizing the exact age distribution and percentage composition at the time of each dose administration.

For regional and socioeconomic stratified analyses, individual-level records were aggregated to the county-year-vaccine level. The aggregated counts of vaccinated children within each county and birth cohort year served as the binomial numerators, with the corresponding county-specific birth cohort sizes utilized as the binomial denominators to construct joint binomial outcomes.

Statistical analyses

Descriptive analysis of vaccine coverage

Coverage rates for ≥ 1 dose of PCV7/13 and PPV23, alongside schedule-specific coverage rates and completion rates, were calculated for each birth cohort. County-level coverage for ≥ 1 dose of PCV7/13 or PPV23 was calculated for each birth cohort. Coverage rates for the 2013, 2017, and 2021 birth cohorts were visualized using heat maps ranging from 0% to 100%. Box plots with county-level data points were generated to illustrate the distribution of county-level vaccination rates by vaccine type, birth cohort, and GDP stratum.

Transition-timing analysis

In the transition-timing analysis, the county-level transition year was defined as the first birth cohort year in which PCV7/13 coverage surpassed PPV23 coverage within a given county. Differences in transition timing across the four GDP strata were evaluated using the Kruskal-Wallis test, and an ordered socioeconomic gradient was formally tested via a county-level permutation trend test. The association between continuous GDP rank and transition year was examined using Spearman correlation with permutation-based P values.

Segmented regression by vaccine market periods

To accurately capture distinct market inflections, the 12-year study timeline was structurally partitioned into four analytical periods based on key historical vaccine supply milestones in China: (1) 2011–2014 (early market), characterized by the availability of imported PCV7 alongside established PPV23 use; (2) 2015–2016 (PCV supply gap), triggered by the market exit of PCV7, which left PPV23 as the sole available option; (3) 2017–2019 (imported PCV13 introduction), initiated by the rollout of imported PCV13; and (4) 2020–2022 (domestic PCV13 expansion), marked by the market expansion of domestic PCV13 alongside the disruptions of the COVID-19 era.

Within this framework, segmented beta-binomial mixed-effects regression analyses were executed through a structured hierarchical approach, with Type II Wald chi-square tests under sum contrast coding applied to evaluate the statistical significance of main effects and their interactions while maintaining structural marginality. Initially, a global saturated model incorporating a four-way interaction term (market period × continuous birth cohort year × vaccine type × GDP stratum) was constructed; because this higher-order interaction lacked statistical significance, a more parsimonious reduced model structured with three-way interaction terms was formally adopted as the final analytical framework. Following these framework tests, the finalized model was mathematically re-parameterized using treatment contrast coding to facilitate intuitive epidemiological interpretation. This non-sum coding approach was utilized to derive the specific, period-specific annual ORs and their corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for each vaccine type stratified across distinct GDP strata.

Sensitivity analyses

Sensitivity analyses were performed to evaluate model robustness by excluding the 2022 birth cohort, thereby mitigating potential truncation bias in the youngest cohort. All statistical analyses were performed using R version 4.5.3.

Results

Pneumococcal vaccination coverage, trends and age distribution

Pneumococcal vaccination coverage and trends are presented in Table 1. As of December 31, 2024, the ZPIIS database contained 9,699,424 valid records for children born between 2011 and 2022. Overall, 1,311,742 (13.5%) children received ≥ 1 dose of PCV7/13, and 2,494,117 (25.7%) received ≥ 1 dose of PPV23.

PCV coverage (≥ 1 dose) remained low (3–4%) for 2011–2016 birth cohorts, then increased from 4.5% (2017 cohort) to 48.4% (2022 cohort). PPV23 coverage (≥ 1 dose) increased from 25.1% (2011 cohort) to a peak of 33.7% (2017 cohort), then declined to 6.3% (2022 cohort).

Among PCV7/13 recipients, primary schedule completion rates were lowest for the 2016 birth cohort (25.3%) and highest for the 2022 cohort (91.9%). Full schedule completion rates ranged from 51.8% (2017 cohort) to 87.8% (2022 cohort). The full catch-up schedule completion rate peaked at 61.9% for the 2016 cohort.

For PCV7/13, the first three doses were predominantly administered during early infancy: dose 1 peaked at 1–2 months (58.1% combined), dose 2 at 3–4 months (57.8% combined), and dose 3 at 4–5 months (48.2% combined). The fourth dose showed strong concentration at 12–13 months, accounting for 87.1% of all fourth doses. A small proportion (4.1%) of children initiated PCV vaccination at 24 months as catch-up vaccination. For PPV23, vaccination was highly concentrated at 24 months of age (82.0%), with the remaining doses distributed across older ages (36–72 + months). [see Additional file 2: Figure S1]

Spatial-temporal patterns of pneumococcal vaccination coverage

The spatial-temporal data of pneumococcal vaccination coverage are presented in Fig. 1. Figure 1A displays the geographic distribution of the four county-level GDP strata in 2022.

As shown in Fig. 1B, county-level vaccine coverage exhibited distinct spatial patterns across the 2013, 2017, and 2021 birth cohorts. For the PCV7/13 vaccine, coverage remained at near-zero levels in the 2013 and 2017 cohorts across almost all counties, but increased to 35%–75% in the majority of counties for the 2021 cohort, with a total range of 22.2%–72.7%. Conversely, PPV23 coverage ranged between 25% and 70% in the 2013 and 2017 cohorts, but subsequently decreased to less than 25% in most counties for the 2021 cohort. A cluster of counties in the southwestern region, however, maintained a higher PPV23 coverage ranging from 35.7% to 56.1% in 2021. Visually comparing Fig. 1A and B, the overall spatial distribution of coverage in 2021 appeared to correspond with the GDP strata, where counties in higher GDP strata generally displayed higher PCV7/13 coverage and lower PPV23 coverage, although substantial intra-stratum variations remained evident.

Figure 1C tracks the continuous annual distribution of coverage from the 2011 to 2022 birth cohorts, stratified by the 2022 GDP strata. Across all strata, PPV23 coverage demonstrated a downward trend over time, while PCV7/13 coverage demonstrated an upward trend. Based on the median county-level coverage within each economic stratum, the exact year when the median PCV7/13 coverage first exceeded the median PPV23 coverage varied by group: it occurred in 2018 for the high-GDP stratum, 2019 for the upper-middle-GDP stratum, 2020 for the lower-middle-GDP stratum, and 2021 for the low-GDP stratum. Within each separate GDP stratum, a wide data spread and intra-stratum variation were observed throughout the study period.

Transition-timing analysis

In county-level transition-year analysis, all 90 counties experienced a transition during 2017–2022. The median transition year was 2019 in high-GDP and upper-middle-GDP counties, 2020 in lower-middle-GDP counties, and 2021 in low-GDP counties (Table 2). Transition timing differed significantly across GDP strata (Kruskal-Wallis H = 46.12, df = 3, P < 0.0001), with evidence of an ordered gradient toward earlier transition in higher-GDP counties (permutation P for trend < 0.0001). Similar results were observed in sensitivity analyses censoring transition assessment at 2021 [see Additional file 1: Tables S2–S3].

Segmented regression by vaccine market periods

The omnibus model selection procedure confirmed that the four-way interaction among market period, continuous birth cohort year, vaccine type, and GDP stratum was not statistically significant (P = 0.984 via Type II Wald chi-square test) [see Additional file 1: Table S4]. Consequently, the more parsimonious model featuring three-way interactions was utilized for all downstream inference [see Additional file 1: Table S5]. The segmented beta-binomial mixed-effects regression quantified distinct temporal trends across four market periods (Table 3). Random-effects diagnostics revealed substantial local clustering, with a county-level intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) of 0.508, indicating that 50.8% of the total variance was attributable to between-county differences; city-level clustering was minimal (city-level ICC = 0.010).

During 2011–2014, coverage trends for both vaccines were largely stagnant. Annual odds of PCV7 uptake showed no significant changes, while PPV23 coverage remained stable, increasing minimally only in upper-middle-GDP counties (annual OR = 1.09; 95% CI: 1.01, 1.17; P < 0.05). Notably, baseline PPV23 coverage exhibited a clear socioeconomic gradient where lower-GDP strata maintained higher coverage (Fig. 2). During the supply gap (2015–2016), annual changes remained statistically non-significant across all GDP strata for both vaccines, despite a discernible descriptive rise in PPV23 coverage observed visually (Fig. 2). The rollout of imported PCV13 was associated with a marked structural shift. The annual odds of PCV13 coverage increased nearly three-fold across all tiers (P < 0.001). Concurrently, PPV23 coverage experienced significant annual reductions, which were most pronounced in high-GDP counties (annual OR = 0.69; 95% CI: 0.61, 0.77; P < 0.001) compared to other strata (annual ORs: 0.78–0.82, all P < 0.001). Visually, this period initiated a distinct crossover effect, where higher-GDP strata rapidly overtook lower ones in PCV13 coverage in 2017, while simultaneously showing the steepest decline in PPV23 uptake (Fig. 2).

During 2020–2022, PCV13 growth significantly decelerated but maintained a robust upward trajectory, with annual odds of vaccination corresponding to annual increases of 38%–43% across all economic tiers (annual ORs: 1.38–1.43, all P < 0.001). Meanwhile, the decline of PPV23 accelerated sharply province-wide, with annual odds nearly halving each year across all socioeconomic groups (annual ORs ranging from 0.53 to 0.57, all P < 0.001). Visually, this led to a persistent, parallel socioeconomic stratification in PCV13 coverage, whereas the rapid decline of PPV23 triggered a convergence across strata, narrowing the absolute coverage gaps among different GDP strata by 2022 (Fig. 2).

Discussion

To our knowledge, this is the first population-based study to examine 12-year trends and socioeconomic disparities in childhood pneumococcal vaccination under China’s voluntary, out-of-pocket immunization system. Using registry data from more than 9.6 million children, this study demonstrates a clear market-driven transition from the polysaccharide vaccine (PPV23) to the conjugate vaccine (PCV13). While the overall increase in PCV13 uptake is consistent with findings from studies in other regions of China [16–18], our segmented regression and transition-timing analyses further characterize how vaccine supply changes, financing barriers, and local implementation capacity jointly shaped vaccination equity over time.

A key finding is the socioeconomic gradient in the timing of the transition from PPV23-dominant to PCV13-dominant coverage. Although the global model did not identify a significant three-way interaction across all years, the transition-timing analysis identified a clear socioeconomic lag in the PPV23-to-PCV13 transition. The median transition year occurred two years earlier in high-GDP counties than in low-GDP counties, indicating that higher-GDP counties shifted toward PCV13-dominant coverage earlier. This finding is consistent with the affordability barrier imposed by the multi-dose PCV series under a voluntary payment framework. Previous studies of non-NIP vaccines in China have shown that uptake is constrained not only by vaccine availability, but also by affordability, awareness, acceptance, access, and activation barriers [19]. In particular, heavy reliance on out-of-pocket payment has been identified as an important driver of suboptimal and inequitable non-NIP vaccine coverage [20]. In our study, the high primary-series completion rate among children who initiated PCV13 suggests that these barriers may be especially important at the stage of vaccine-series initiation rather than only during post-initiation adherence. Therefore, public health interventions should prioritize timely initiation through affordability measures, stronger provider recommendation, and improved parental communication.

The segmented regression analysis helps explain how these patterns changed across different vaccine supply periods. After a period of low and stable coverage from 2011 to 2016, the introduction of imported PCV13 during 2017–2019 was associated with a rapid increase in PCV13 uptake, with annual odds of vaccination increasing nearly three-fold across all GDP strata. This increase may reflect a catch-up effect after the 2015–2016 PCV supply gap, when delayed demand for infant PCV products was partially released following the reintroduction of a conjugate vaccine option. During the domestic expansion period from 2020 to 2022, however, PCV13 growth decelerated to a steadier annual increase of 38% to 43% across economic strata (annual ORs: 1.38–1.43). This pattern suggests that expanded domestic supply alone may not have been sufficient to sustain the initial growth momentum. Evidence from China’s non-NIP vaccine system indicates that vaccine availability does not automatically translate into equitable uptake when residual out-of-pocket costs, decentralized procurement and demand forecasting, variable provider recommendation, and limited official activation mechanisms persist [19–21]. Potential COVID-19-related service disruptions may also have contributed [22].

Notably, from 2017 to 2022, the annual odds ratios for PCV13 adoption were closely comparable across GDP strata. This finding explains the non-significant three-way interaction in the omnibus model: supply-side market changes increased PCV13 uptake in a broadly parallel manner across strata, but did not substantially alter the relative socioeconomic hierarchy. This pattern is also consistent with the inverse equity hypothesis, which suggests that new health interventions are often adopted earlier and more consistently by socioeconomically advantaged groups unless equity-oriented financing and delivery mechanisms are implemented [23]. In the present context, higher-GDP counties may have maintained higher baseline coverage because domestic supply expansion occurred within an existing self-paid, market-based delivery system. Under such a system, families and localities with greater purchasing power, higher vaccine awareness, stronger provider recommendation, and more stable implementation capacity may benefit earlier and more consistently from increased vaccine availability. Therefore, market expansion can raise coverage across all strata while leaving relative socioeconomic disparities largely unchanged.

The rapid decline in PPV23 coverage during the later years produced a different equity pattern. Between 2020 and 2022, PPV23 use declined sharply across the province, with annual odds of vaccination nearly halving each year in all economic groups (annual ORs: 0.53–0.57). For PCV13, higher-GDP counties continued to maintain higher coverage, leaving a persistent parallel disparity between higher- and lower-GDP counties. For PPV23, however, the steep decline across all counties narrowed absolute coverage differences between GDP strata by 2022. This apparent convergence should not be interpreted as improved protection equity. Rather, it primarily reflected a reduction in the use of a vaccine option that is not recommended for routine use in healthy children, instead of lower-GDP counties catching up in optimal PCV protection.

The strong county-level clustering observed in this study further highlights the importance of local implementation. The county-level random effects accounted for 50.8% of the total variation, whereas city-level variation was minimal. This suggests that local implementation factors may contribute substantially to vaccination uptake beyond county-level economic status alone. Under China’s decentralized framework for non-NIP vaccination, county-level Centers for Disease Control and Prevention and local community health centers exercise substantial autonomy in vaccine procurement, public health communication, and clinical recommendation practices [24]. The observed county-level variance therefore suggests that localized provider recommendation, supply-chain consistency, reminder systems, and doctor-parent communication may play important roles in shaping real-world non-NIP vaccine coverage [19, 21].

This local variation also has important clinical implications, particularly regarding the widespread and delayed reduction in PPV23 use. Although PPV23 was mostly administered at 24 months of age, its high coverage, which peaked at 33.7% in the 2017 cohort and remained at 35.7%–56.1% in certain counties through 2021, was inconsistent with clinical guidance restricting PPV23 use to high-risk children aged ≥ 2 years. Because individual clinical indications were unavailable, we could not determine whether each PPV23 recipient met high-risk criteria. Nevertheless, the aggregate coverage patterns are consistent with use beyond high-risk indications and may indicate substitution of PPV23 for PCV in some settings [25–27]. This pattern may reflect a cost-driven substitution process, in which PPV23 was perceived or used as a lower-cost alternative to PCV after children reached two years of age, particularly during periods of limited PCV supply. The large price difference between a full infant PCV series and a single PPV23 dose may have reinforced this substitution. In addition, clinical guidelines and expert consensus in China function largely as soft constraints without direct regulatory penalties. As a result, updated evidence regarding the limited role of PPV23 in healthy children may take time to translate into standardized frontline practice, especially in settings where historical practice patterns or economic considerations remain influential [21].

Although PCV13 coverage increased to 48.4% by 2022, population-level protection remained substantially below international benchmarks, including the 89% global median reported by WHO [28], and below the near-universal coverage achieved by free vaccines in China’s NIP [29, 30]. In Zhejiang Province, routine NIP vaccine doses scheduled before 12 months of age have been reported to maintain high coverage, with coverage and completeness reaching the national target and generally remaining above 95% [31]. NIP inclusion would likely represent the most sustainable policy pathway for reducing financial barriers and regional disparities in PCV uptake. However, national inclusion would require addressing several implementation challenges, including the need for stronger disease-burden evidence, stable domestic vaccine supply [12], and budgetary feasibility [32]. Evidence from willingness-to-pay studies and local PCV13 pilot programs in China suggests that partial subsidies, free-dose strategies, and government-supported financing can improve acceptance and uptake, but may still be insufficient to achieve high full-course coverage unless financing and delivery mechanisms are strengthened [33, 34].

Therefore, we propose a phased policy pathway toward broader PCV inclusion, together with immediate local actions. First, local governments in lower-GDP counties could provide targeted subsidies or partial reimbursement to reduce initiation barriers among vulnerable infants. Second, at the national level, stronger and more explicit recommendations for the appropriate use of both PCV13 and PPV23 should be issued through authoritative channels, such as China’s National Immunization Advisory Committee [35]. Third, provider education and public communication should be strengthened to discourage inappropriate substitution of PPV23 for PCV and to improve understanding of the immunological and clinical differences between conjugate and polysaccharide vaccines. Finally, routine surveillance of facility-level coverage, provider recommendation practices, and schedule adherence would help identify areas requiring intervention and support more equitable implementation.

This study has several limitations. First, because individual-level data on family income, parental education, health insurance, and medical history were unavailable, our models relied on county-level aggregated indicators. Therefore, the findings reflect ecological associations and do not support causal inference about individual choices or behaviors. Second, PPV23 coverage for the 2022 birth cohort may have been underestimated because these children had limited follow-up time after reaching their second birthday. However, sensitivity analyses, including both transition-timing and segmented regression models censored at 2021, yielded results consistent with the primary findings [see Additional file 1: Tables S2–S3, S6–S7]. Third, counties were stratified using GDP per capita in 2022 as a fixed contextual indicator. Although economic rankings were relatively stable in the later years of the study, they were less correlated in the earlier years, particularly in 2011; thus, 2022 rankings may not perfectly represent baseline socioeconomic conditions at the beginning of the study period [see Additional file 1: Table S8]. Finally, because this study did not use linked invasive pneumococcal disease surveillance data, we could not directly assess the effect of these coverage shifts on disease burden. Future studies linking vaccination registry data with disease surveillance systems are needed to evaluate the clinical impact of pneumococcal vaccine transitions in China.

Conclusion

Zhejiang Province experienced a substantial transition from PPV23 to PCV13 over the past decade. However, under the voluntary, out-of-pocket financing model, this transition has maintained an economic gap in PCV13 uptake and a clear time lag in the timing of transition in lower-GDP counties. To promote equitable and evidence-based protection, future public health policies should move beyond vaccine availability to remove financial barriers through subsidies, standardize provider recommendations, and ensure guideline-concordant vaccine use.

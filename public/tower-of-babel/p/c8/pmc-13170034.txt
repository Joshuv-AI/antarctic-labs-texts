Real-world utility of platelet count, hemoglobin, and iron levels as predictors of clinical response to vedolizumab in patients with ulcerative colitis: a retrospective, single-center study in Poland
Authors: Aleksandra Sobolewska-Włodarczyk, Jakub Orliński, Żaneta Witas, Łukasz Woźniak, Zuzanna Łysoń, Anita Gąsiorowska
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundVedolizumab is a humanized immunoglobulin G1 monoclonal antibody used to treat ulcerative colitis. Being bionaïve is a recognized factor associated with a favorable response to therapy. Studies have revealed a correlation between the baseline hemoglobin concentration and the clinical response. This study analyzed multiple laboratory parameters to assess their predictive value for the clinical response to vedolizumab induction therapy at the 14th week and for a sustained response at the 52nd week.MethodsForty-nine patients (29 males, 20 females; aged 18–82 years) who were receiving 55 vedolizumab treatment courses at the Department of Gastroenterology, Medical University of Lodz, were retrospectively evaluated. Patients were grouped by prior biological therapy exposure and clinical response, defined as a decrease of ≥ 3 points in the Mayo score from baseline at weeks 14 and 52. Laboratory tests, including hemoglobin, mean corpuscular volume, hematocrit, white blood cell count, neutrophils, lymphocytes, monocytes, platelet count, C-reactive protein, albumin, serum iron, creatinine, alanine aminotransferase, and aspartate aminotransferase, were performed at baseline and at the end of the 52nd week.ResultsAmong the 55 treatment courses, 15 (27.27%) were discontinued due to lack of clinical response. Baseline hemoglobin (p = 0.0132), mean corpuscular volume (p = 0.0025), serum iron (p = 0.0119), and platelet count (p = 0.0424) were significantly associated with the clinical response at the 14th week. Multivariable logistic regression revealed that hemoglobin, serum iron, and mean corpuscular volume were independent positive predictors of response, whereas a higher platelet count was negatively correlated with response. Threshold values with moderate predictive value and high sensitivity were identified for hemoglobin > 11 g/dL (AUC = 0.753), serum iron > 3.6 µmol/L (AUC = 0.758), and platelet count < 492 × 10³/µL (AUC = 0.708). The baseline mean corpuscular volume was significantly greater in patients who maintained a clinical response at the 52nd week (p = 0.0477), although it lacked sufficient specificity to predict response at the 14th week.ConclusionsBaseline hemoglobin, serum iron, and platelet counts may serve as useful biomarkers for predicting the clinical response to vedolizumab induction therapy in ulcerative colitis patients. Iron deficiency anemia may be associated with a reduced likelihood of response. No reliable predictors of long-term response were identified, indicating the need for further prospective studies.

Background

Ulcerative colitis [UC] is a chronic, multifactorial inflammatory bowel disease of unknown etiology. The pathogenesis of UC is theorized to be primarily immunological, involving dysregulated immune responses, particularly overactivation of Th2 lymphocytes, which results in excessive production of proinflammatory cytokines such as tumor necrosis factor-α [TNF-α] and interleukin-13 [IL-13] [1, 2]. Both men and women across all age groups can be affected. Clinical manifestations include hematochezia, diarrhea, and abdominal pain, with symptom severity correlated with disease activity [3]. Diagnosis is established on the basis of clinical presentation; laboratory tests; and endoscopic, histological and radiological findings [4].

Treatment strategies for UC are tailored to the severity of the disease. In the management of UC, glucocorticoid [GC] therapy is utilized during disease exacerbation and at the time of diagnosis, whereas aminosalicylates are employed for maintenance treatment [5]. In patients with inadequate or short-term responses, immunosuppressive agents such as azathioprine [AZA] or mercaptopurine [6-MP] are introduced [6]. For those unresponsive to conventional treatments, biologic therapies, including monoclonal antibodies targeting TNF-α (e.g., infliximab [IFX] and adalimumab [ADA]), integrin α4β7 (vedolizumab [VDZ]), or interleukin-23 [IL-23] (ustekinumab [UST] and mirikizumab [MIRI]), are utilized [7–9]. In the treatment of UC, small-molecule drugs such as JAK1/JAK3 inhibitors (tofacitinib [TOFA] [10]), selective JAK1 inhibitor (upadacitinib [UPA] [11]) and S1P receptor modulator (etrasimod [ETR] [12]) are also used. In refractory or complicated cases, surgical intervention, such as colectomy, may be considered [13].

VDZ is a humanized monoclonal antibody that selectively inhibits integrin α4β7 on T lymphocytes, preventing its interaction with the mucosal addressin cell adhesion molecule-1 [MAdCAM-1] on the endothelial cells of intestinal blood vessels. This blockade reduces the migration of activated T lymphocytes into the gut mucosa, thereby diminishing the immune response and alleviating the symptoms of UC [14].

There are biomarkers that, with varying sensitivity and specificity, estimate a patient’s probability of achieving a response to VDZ therapy. Higher baseline hemoglobin [HGB] levels have been identified as a predictor of a positive response to VDZ therapy [15]. Several studies have demonstrated an association between lower HGB levels and a poorer response to VDZ treatment [16, 17].

We calculated selected hematological indices, including the Neutrophil-to-Lymphocyte Ratio [NLR], Monocyte-to-Lymphocyte Ratio [MLR], Platelet-to-Lymphocyte Ratio [PLR], C-reactive protein–Albumin–Lymphocyte index [CALLY], Systemic Immune-Inflammation Index [SII], Glasgow Prognostic Score [GPS], and the modified Glasgow Prognostic Score [mGPS], to evaluate their utility in predicting response to induction therapy at 14th week of VDZ treatment. SII, NLR, and PLR are promising prognostic markers of response to VDZ therapy, with studies suggesting that SII demonstrates the greatest clinical utility [18]. Clinical findings have demonstrated the utility of CALLY in predicting the response to induction therapy at the 14th week of VDZ treatment. GPS and mGPS are indices primarily used in oncology [19]. However, studies suggest a potential correlation between these markers and the severity of systemic inflammation in patients with inflammatory bowel diseases [20].

Laboratory parameters were analyzed because they are potential predictors of a good response to VDZ therapy. In this study, selected laboratory parameters, including the serum levels of HGB, Mean Corpuscular Volume [MCV], Hematocrit [HCT], White Blood Cell Count [WBC], Neutrophils [NEUT], Lymphocytes [LYM], Monocytes [MONO], Platelet Count [PLT], C-reactive protein [CRP], Albumin [ALB], Serum iron [Fe], Creatinine [CREA], Alanine aminotransferase [ALT], Aspartate aminotransferase [AST], and Fecal Calprotectin [FC]. This study aims to identify potential novel predictors of response to induction therapy at the 14th week and maintenance response at the 52nd week of VDZ treatment. Changes in laboratory parameter levels during VDZ therapy were also analyzed in search of a potential association with treatment response.

Methods

Patients

This was a retrospective study involving 56 patients, aged 18–82, of Caucasian origin with UC. All participants received intravenous VDZ at a dose of 300 mg at the Department of Gastroenterology of the Medical University of Lodz. The treatment duration was 52 weeks. The diagnosis of UC in each patient was made on the basis of criteria established by the Polish Society of Gastroenterology and the European Crohn’s Disease and Colitis Organization [ECCO]. The treatment response was determined by a reduction of at least 3 points in the total Mayo Score [MS].

Studies that do not constitute a medical experiment, including retrospective studies based solely on previously collected clinical data, are not subject to the requirement of obtaining approval from a bioethics committee. Consequently, no approval was sought from the bioethics committee.

In these patients, the diagnosis of UC was based on clinical, endoscopic, radiological, and histological examinations, according to guidelines published by the ECCO.

MS is one of the most commonly used indicators for assessing the activity of UC. For the purposes of the study, the full version of the MS was used, which includes four components: rectal bleeding, stool frequency, physician assessment, and endoscopic appearance [21]. Each component is scored from 0 to 3, resulting in a total score ranging from 0 to 12. Patients were evaluated via MS at baseline, week 0, prior to the first week of VDZ therapy, and at weeks 14 and 52 of treatment, a decrease in MS was detected, which was considered a significant decrease of at least 3 points.

The 14th week was selected as it corresponds to the end of the induction phase of VDZ therapy and represents a clinically relevant time point for evaluating the initial treatment response. The 52nd week was chosen to assess the durability of treatment effect and sustained clinical response during maintenance therapy, representing a standard one-year follow-up period. Additionally, these time points correspond to routine clinical assessment in our center.

The inclusion criteria for treatment with a biologic drug such as VDZ included clinically significant exacerbation, luminal disease activity confirmed by endoscopy, intolerance to drugs such as GCs, 5-aminosalicylic acid [5-ASA] and its derivatives, and immunosuppressive drugs such as AZA or 6-MP.

The NLR was calculated as the ratio of NEU to LYM. The MLR was determined by dividing MONO by the LYM. The PLR was calculated by dividing the PLT by the LYM. The CALLY index was computed via a formula involving CRP, ALB, and LYM to reflect both inflammation and nutritional status. The SII was derived by multiplying the NEUT by the PLT and dividing the result by the LYM. The GPS was assigned on the basis of CRP > 10 mg/L and ALB < 35 g/L, with a score ranging from 0 to 2 depending on the presence of these abnormalities. The mGPS was calculated similarly, but patients with elevated CRP alone received a score of 1 only if ALB was also reduced, thereby refining sensitivity to inflammatory status.

Collection of blood samples and blood analysis

Prior to the initiation of VDZ therapy, a standard venous blood sample was collected from each patient using ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid [EDTA] for the assessment of HGB, MCV, HCT, WBC, NEUT, LYM, MONO, PLT, CRP, ALB, Fe, CREA, UA, ALT, and AST. Sample analysis was performed within 2 h of collection. Blood tests were performed during the week of the first VDZ dose and were repeated at weeks 14 and 52. FC was assessed from a stool sample before the initiation of VDZ treatment. The reference range for HGB in adults is 13 to 18 g/dL for men and 12 to 16 g/dL for women, 80 to 98 fL for MCV, 40 to 55% for HCT, 4000 to 10000/µL for WBC, 2200 to 4800/µL for NEUT, 1300 to 2900/µL for LYM, 300 to 800/µL for MONO, 150000 to 400000/µL for PLT, 0 to 5 mg/L for CRP, 35 to 52 g/L for ALB, 12.5 to 32.5 µmol/L for Fe, 64 to 104 µmol/L for CREA, 155 to 357 µmol/L for UA, 0 to 35 U/L for ALT, 0 to 35 U/L for AST, and < 50 µg/g for FC.

Statistical analysis

All the statistical analyses were performed using Statistica 13.3 software (TIBCO, Palo Alto, CA, USA). The analyzed results are presented as the means ± standard deviations for continuous variables and as numbers and percentages for categorical variables. All the results were rounded to four decimal places, except for the percentage results, which were rounded to two decimal places.

The Shapiro‒Wilk test was used to assess the normality of the distribution of the analyzed quantitative parameters. This test confirmed that the data were not normally distributed, justifying the use of nonparametric tests. Therefore, we used the Mann–Whitney U test or the chi-square test to compare patient characteristics between the response and nonresponse groups.

Categorical variables were compared using Fisher’s exact test.

We conducted receiver operating characteristic [ROC] curve analysis to evaluate the predictive value of selected laboratory parameters in relation to biological treatment response.

Statistical analyses included multivariable logistic regression, with results reported as regression coefficients [B], standard errors [SE], Wald statistics, odds ratio [OR] with 95% confidence intervals [CI], and p-values indicating statistical significance.

Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05 in all the analyses.

The study was exploratory in nature, aiming to identify potential associations that warrant further investigation in the future through more targeted studies. Therefore, no correction for multiple comparisons was applied to avoid an increased risk of false negative results.

To minimize potential sources of bias, only patients meeting clearly defined eligibility criteria were included in the analysis. All data were collected and assessed according to standardized procedures, thereby reducing the risk of selection and measurement errors.

Patients with missing values for individual variables were excluded from the respective statistical analyses, as detailed in the text and tables. Although these were isolated cases, their exclusion may have led to the omission of statistically significant associations, representing a limitation of this study and highlighting the need for further research.

Results

Baseline characteristics of patients included in the study

A total of 56 patients were initially assessed for eligibility. After applying the inclusion and exclusion criteria, 49 patients were included in the final analysis. These patients underwent a total of 55 treatment courses with VDZ. A total of 49 patients who had undergone VDZ therapy were enrolled in our study: 29 men (59.18%) and 20 (40.82%) women. Some patients were assessed multiple times, as they underwent more than one course of VDZ therapy. In total, 55 courses of VDZ therapy were evaluated. Fifteen (27.27%) courses of treatment lasted only 14 weeks because they were disqualified from treatment due to a lack of response according to the therapeutic program guidelines. Forty (72.72%) courses of treatment lasted 52 weeks. Because the number of assessed treatment courses was greater than the number of individual patients enrolled in the study, each VDZ treatment cycle was considered separately. However, the potential for intra-patient correlation was acknowledged and considered a limitation of the analysis. The baseline characteristics of the patients included in the study are presented in Fig. 1.

The mean duration of the disease was 7.3 ± 6.045 years. The average patient age at diagnosis of UC was 35.3273 ± 15.8652 years. The average patient age at the beginning of VDZ treatment was 35.125 ± 15.793 years. A total of 30.90% (n = 17) of the 55 evaluated VDZ courses were conducted in bionaïve patients, whereas 94.55% (n = 52) were conducted in patients who had previously been treated with GCs.

The endoscopic localization of inflammatory lesions was pancolitis in 50.91% (n = 28), left-sided colitis in 38.18% (n = 21), and proctitis in 7.27% (n = 4) of the subjects. In two patients, the extent of the lesions was not determined prior to treatment. The extent of lesions was classified according to the Montreal classification. The mean baseline total MS was 9.0545 ± 2.0942 points. The lowest score was 4 points, and the highest score was 12 points.

A total of 50.91% (n = 28) of patients had extraintestinal manifestations prior to the initiation of VDZ treatment. Extraintestinal manifestations of UC were defined as any systemic symptoms occurring during the course of the disease, even if they appeared many years prior to VDZ therapy. The mean BMI (n = 52) prior to the initiation of VDZ treatment was 24.4733 ± 4.5049.

Clinical response at the 14th week of VDZ therapy: response/no response (14th week)

Among the 55 VDZ treatment courses administered, 45 (81.82%) resulted in a therapeutic response, whereas 10 (18.18%) did not.

Among the 45 responders, only 15 patients (33.33%) were bionaïve, while 30 (66.67%) were biopositive. Of the group of the 45 patients who responded to treatment, 22 (48.89%) were female, and 23 (51.11%) were male. Among the 10 patients who did not respond to treatment, 2 (20%) were bionaïve, while 8 (80%) were biopositive. Among the group of the 10 patients who did not respond to treatment, 3 (30%) were female, and 7 (70%) were male.

As presented in Table 2, there were no statistically significant differences observed between the groups that responded to VDZ treatment at the 14th week and those that did not in terms of HCT, WBC, NEUT, LYM, MONO, ALB, ALT, AST, and FC levels. Furthermore, no statistically significant differences were identified with respect to sex, age at diagnosis, prior GCs use, disease duration, BMI, NLR, MLR, PLR, CALLY, SII, GPS, mGPS, and being bionaïve.

Response at the 14th week of VDZ therapy: bionaïve/biopositive

Multivariable logistic regression analysis

Multivariable logistic regression analysis was conducted to evaluate the impact of being bionaïve, duration of disease, and endoscopic severity according to the Montreal classification, baseline levels of CRP, PLT, HGB, Fe, and MCV on the likelihood of achieving a clinical response after 14 weeks of VDZ treatment.

The regression model did not demonstrate statistical significance for being bionaïve (B = 0.727, SE = 0.852, Wald = 0.728, p = 0.3935, OR = 2.069, 95% CI: 0.389–10.993), disease duration (B = 0.022, SE = 0.060, Wald = 0.131, p = 0.7172, OR = 1.022, 95% CI: 0.908–1.150), endoscopic severity according to the Montreal classification (B = -0.910, SE = 0.681, Wald = 1.787, p = 0.1813, OR = 0.403, 95% CI: 0.106–1.528), and baseline CRP level (B = 0.022, SE = 0.060, Wald = 0.131, p = 0.7172, OR = 1.022, 95% CI: 0.908–1.150). Although some ORs suggested trends, wide confidence intervals and nonsignificant p-values indicated substantial uncertainty, suggesting no independent effect on clinical response.

The regression model demonstrated statistical significance for the baseline PLT (B = -0.008, SE = 0.004, Wald = 4.977, p = 0.0257, OR = 0.992, 95% CI: 0.984–0.999). An OR less than 1 indicates that higher baseline PLTs are associated with a lower likelihood of achieving a clinical response. Although the effect size per unit increase in the PLT is small, the statistically significant p-value and confidence interval not exceeding 1 suggest that the baseline PLT has a significant and independent negative impact on the probability of a clinical response.

The regression model demonstrated statistical significance for the baseline HGB level (B = 0.670, SE = 0.280, Wald = 5.732, p = 0.0167, OR = 1.954, 95% CI: 1.129–3.382). The OR suggested that higher baseline HGB levels were significantly associated with a nearly twofold increase in the likelihood of achieving a clinical response. The confidence interval does not cross 1, and the p value is below the conventional threshold of 0.05, confirming that baseline HGB had a statistically significant and positive impact on the clinical response probability. The regression model demonstrated statistical significance for the baseline Fe level (B = 0.180, SE = 0.084, Wald = 4.633, p = 0.0314, OR = 1.197, 95% CI: 1.016–1.410). An OR greater than 1 indicates that higher baseline Fe levels are significantly associated with an increased likelihood of achieving a clinical response. The confidence interval excludes 1, and the p-value is less than 0.05, confirming a statistically significant and positive relationship between the Fe concentration and the clinical response probability. The regression model demonstrated statistical significance for baseline MCV levels (B = 0.226, SE = 0.073, Wald = 9.503, p = 0.0021, OR = 1.254, 95% CI: 1.086–1.447). The OR suggests that for each unit increase in MCV, the odds of achieving a clinical response increased by approximately 25%. The low p-value and confidence interval well above 1 indicate that the MCV is a statistically significant and independent positive predictor of clinical response.

ROC curve analysis

ROC curve analysis was performed to evaluate the ability of selected, statistically significant laboratory parameters to predict response at the 14th week of VDZ treatment in patients with UC. The evaluated laboratory parameters were HGB (p = 0.0132), MCV (p = 0.0025), Fe (p = 0.0119), and PLT (p = 0.0424). Optimal cutoff values were determined via the Youden index and the tangent method. Analyses demonstrating both sensitivity and specificity values ≥ 50% were deemed significant. In cases where multiple analyses met this criterion, the one exhibiting superior sensitivity and specificity was selected. The ROC curve p-values were p = 0.0014 for HGB, p = 0.0009 for MCV, p = 0.0068 for Fe, and p = 0.0214 for PLT.

As shown in Fig. 2, in both male and female patients, a cutoff value of baseline HGB ≥ 11 g/dL with an area under the ROC curve [AUC] = 0.753 proved to be a good predictor of response at the 14th week of VDZ treatment. The optimal cutoff value was determined using the tangent method. The HGB level demonstrated very high sensitivity (97.78%) and high specificity (80%). The baseline level of HGB was not a statistically significant predictor of a maintained response at the 52nd week (p = 0.5093); therefore, HGB was not analyzed using the ROC curve in this context.

Discussion

UC is a chronic inflammatory disease of the colon that often affects young and middle-aged adults [22]. It has a significant effect on patients’ quality of life [23–25] and leads to high healthcare costs [26, 27] due to frequent relapses, hospitalizations, and long-term treatment. Therefore, finding effective and well-tolerated therapies is especially important. VDZ is a biologic drug that has shown good results in treatment [28]. However, not all patients benefit from it equally. Therefore, interest in finding simple markers that could help predict who is more likely to respond to treatment is increasing. In our study, we investigated whether selected blood markers could be useful in this context. We also described how patients responded to VDZ during treatment.

Our analysis revealed that HGB (p = 0.0132), MCV (p = 0.0025), Fe (p = 0.0119), and PLT (p = 0.0424) levels were correlated with the response at the 14th week of VDZ treatment. Moreover, multivariable logistic regression analysis revealed that baseline levels of HGB (B = 0.670, SE = 0.280, Wald = 5.732, p = 0.0167, OR = 1.954, 95% CI: 1.129–3.382), MCV (B = 0.226, SE = 0.073, Wald = 9.503, p = 0.0021, OR = 1.254, 95% CI: 1.086–1.447), and Fe (B = 0.180, SE = 0.084, Wald = 4.633, p = 0.0314, OR = 1.197, 95% CI: 1.016–1.410) were independent positive predictors of clinical response at the 14th week of treatment, whereas elevated baseline PLT (B = -0.008, SE = 0.004, Wald = 4.977, p = 0.0257, OR = 0.992, 95% CI: 0.984–0.999) was independently associated with a lower likelihood of response. These findings necessitated the execution of an ROC curve analysis. HGB levels > 11 g/dL (p = 0.0132) emerged as promising laboratory of clinical response at the 14th week following VDZ induction, as indicated by a moderate AUC value (0.753) in both male and female patients. This cutoff point was associated with a high sensitivity of 97.78% and a relatively favorable specificity of 80%. Fe levels > 3.6 µmol/L (p = 0.0119) demonstrated moderate prognostic value (AUC = 0.758), yielding very high sensitivity (97.73%) and comparable specificity (60%). PLT counts < 492 × 10³/µL (p = 0.0424) also exhibited moderate predictive capacity (AUC = 0.708), with excellent sensitivity (100%) and relatively high specificity (80%).

This study confirmed that the HGB baseline level is an important predictor of achieving a response at the 14th week of VDZ therapy, as indicated by previously mentioned studies [15–17]. In contrast to these findings, Scarozza et al. demonstrated that anemia of chronic disease has no impact on the response to VDZ treatment (p = 0.642), which contrasts with the findings of our study [29]. Similarly, Mühl et al. demonstrated that baseline HGB levels are not significantly associated with the response to VDZ treatment in patients with inflammatory bowel disease [30]. The discrepancies between our findings and those reported by Scarroza et al. and by Mühl et al. may be attributable, at least in part, to differences in the study populations, as our analysis focused exclusively on patients with UC, whereas their cohort included individuals with both UC and Crohn’s disease. The inclusion of two distinct disease entities may have introduced additional biological and clinical heterogeneity, potentially confounding the observed associations.

Fe and PLT baseline levels may also prove useful in predicting response. The value of this study lies in the absence of existing evidence demonstrating an association between baseline Fe and PLT levels and the response to VDZ therapy. However, there are studies that contradict our findings. Bamias et al. demonstrated that the PLT was not a useful predictor of clinical remission during VDZ therapy in patients with UC [31]. Despite the comparable structure of the study populations, the discrepancy between our findings and those of Bamias et al., who conducted a prospective analysis, may stem from differences in study design. Furthermore, Bamias et al. assessed clinical remission, whereas this study evaluated clinical response, indicating a substantial methodological difference between the two approaches. Konikoff et al. reported no association between the baseline PLT and the response to VDZ or IFX therapy in patients with UC. The absence of an association between baseline Fe levels and the response to VDZ therapy in patients with inflammatory bowel disease was noted by Loveikyte et al. [32]. Notably, our study included only patients with UC, which may enhance the identification of treatment response predictors within a well-defined disease entity.

An elevated NLR indicates an active inflammatory process, similar to the PLR. MLR is used less frequently but serves a comparable function. The SII is a more comprehensive index and may have greater prognostic relevance. CALLY, GPS, and mGPS reflect an active inflammatory state associated with protein malnutrition, which suggests their potential prognostic utility in UC. The NLR is by far the most frequently analyzed parameter in the context of UC. PLR, SII, MLR, CALLY, GPS, and mGPS have been investigated as potential prognostic markers of treatment response in UC patients in only a limited number of studies. As previously noted, Yan et al. demonstrated the utility of the SII in predicting the relapse of UC during VDZ therapy. Notably, several parameters that initially appeared promising did not demonstrate predictive value for achieving a response at the 14th week of VDZ therapy in this study, including the NLR (p = 0.4739), MLR (p = 0.4715), PLR (p = 0.2705), CALLY (p = 0.1748), SII (p = 0.3339), GPS (p = 0.2424), and mGPS (p = 1.0000). In contrast to our study, Su et al. demonstrated that a low CALLY is associated with a poorer response to VDZ therapy at the 14th week of treatment [33]. Bertani et al. highlighted the prognostic value of a low NLR and PLR in predicting a favorable response to anti-TNF therapy in UC patients at the 8th and 54th weeks of treatment [34]. Warpechowski et al. demonstrated a moderate prognostic value of the NLR in patients with UC treated with VDZ (AUC = 0.795) [35]. Discrepancies with the cited studies may stem from the retrospective design of our study, the limited sample size, and occasional incomplete laboratory data, which in some cases precluded the calculation of selected indices.

As was mentioned, studies suggest that being bionaïve is an important factor associated with a favorable response to VDZ therapy [36–38]. Interestingly, this study did not confirm that finding. In this investigation, being bionaïve was not a statistically significant predictor of achieving a response at the 14th week of VDZ therapy (p = 0.4092). This outcome may, at least in part, be attributed to the relatively small proportion of bionaïve patients (n = 17) in comparison with the biopositive group (n = 38), as well as the inherent heterogeneity of the study population. While Meng et al. identified being bionaïve as a significant determinant of a positive therapeutic response to VDZ [39], our study failed to corroborate this association.

Regrettably, most of the analyzed laboratory parameters did not reach statistical significance in predicting response maintenance at the 52nd week of VDZ therapy. Kobayashi et al. reported that HGB levels < 10 g/mL are not associated with discontinuation due to a loss of response in patients with UC during maintenance therapy with VDZ (p = 0.709), which is consistent with our findings [40]. In contrast, Yan et al. demonstrated that a baseline level of HGB < 12 g/dL is a significant (p = 0.01) independent risk factor for loss of remission at the 52nd week of VDZ treatment [17].

Baseline MCV levels were identified as a statistically significant predictor (p = 0.0477) of response maintenance at the 52nd week of VDZ therapy. However, the MCV was not subjected to ROC curve analysis in this context, as it showed limited predictive value for response at the 14th week of therapy. Additionally, the small number of patients who failed to respond at the 52nd week (n = 5) rendered the results insufficiently robust for reliable interpretation at this stage.

Various studies have analyzed prognostic factors in the treatment of UC with VDZ. As was mentioned, some studies have focused on biomarkers such as baseline HGB levels, which are associated with a greater likelihood of achieving a clinical response after 14 weeks of therapy, as well as elevated levels of soluble MAdCAM-1. Other studies have demonstrated the utility of clinical indices such as the MS and the Ulcerative Colitis Endoscopic Index of Severity [UCEIS] [41]. Additional factors assessed included nutritional status [42, 43], age [41], and sex-related differences [44] in treatment response. While some studies have explored the association between anemia and a complicated disease course [45], and others have highlighted the potential role of the hepcidin level as a biomarker [32], to date, no studies have simultaneously evaluated the PLT, HGB, Fe, and MCV status as correlated predictors of the response to VDZ therapy at different stages of treatment.

Of note are studies examining predictors of therapeutic response to other biologic agents, whose findings align with those observed in the current investigation for VDZ. Gradel et al. demonstrated that measurements of HGB levels may be useful in monitoring the treatment of inflammatory bowel diseases with IFX [46]. Seo et al. identified baseline HGB levels ≥ 11.5 g/dL (p = 0.008) as a significant prognostic factor for achieving remission at the 8th week of IFX therapy in patients with UC [47]. This prompts consideration of the potential for identifying common predictors of response to biologic therapies with differing mechanisms of action, as well as the need to investigate the association between pretreatment anemia and therapeutic outcomes.

When potential biomarkers of treatment response are analyzed, it is important to consider the underlying mechanisms that may explain such associations. Voudoukis et al. suggested a potential mechanism whereby Interleukin-6 [IL-6], which is elevated in patients with UC, stimulates thrombopoiesis [48]. This pathway may partly explain the observed association between elevated PLT and poorer therapeutic response. Josa et al., in a study using a mouse model, also confirmed the relationship between IL-6 and increased thrombopoietin levels [49]. Conflicting results regarding the association between Fe levels and treatment response may be attributed to the effects of functional iron deficiency, wherein chronic inflammation reduces Fe availability despite normal serum Fe concentrations [50].

In most of the studies, the achievement of remission served as the primary endpoint. Clinical remission is typically defined as a total MS equal to or less than 2, provided that no individual subscore exceeds one. However, the present study adopts an alternative approach by employing the clinical response as the primary outcome measure. This response was defined as a decrease of at least 3 points in the total MS score compared with the baseline value. Although this criterion is less stringent than that required for remission, it allows for more sensitive detection of potential biomarkers of therapeutic efficacy, which is particularly relevant in the context of exploratory research. A similar approach, which is based on clinical response rather than remission, was employed by Kajikawa et al., Loveikyte et al., and Levartovsky et al. [51].

Our study has several limitations. The most important limitation is the small number of patients, which makes it harder to draw strong conclusions or apply them to the general population. The significant differences in sex (p = 0.0204) and age at diagnosis (p = 0.0166) between the bionaïve and previously exposed groups may act as confounding factors influencing the results, which should be taken into account in future research. Moreover, because this was a retrospective study, we could not control all possible factors that might have influenced the results, such as previous treatments. Although our results suggest that there may be some differences in markers between responders and non-responders, a more detailed analysis is still needed. We also focused only on a few selected markers. Importantly, this study was based on patient records, which are sometimes incomplete. Contact with some patients was lost, and for others, there was also a lack of blood test results necessary to calculate certain indices, such as the PLR, CALLY, GPS, mGPS, or SII. As a result, we were forced to exclude some potentially relevant markers and parameters, as they were not available in a way that would allow us to assess changes over time. Conducting the study on a larger patient cohort with a complete set of parameters could yield even more insightful results and enhance the overall reliability of the findings. In addition to the previously mentioned limitations, another important limitation of this study is the potential intra-patient correlation resulting from repeated treatment courses, which should be addressed in future research. Despite these limitations, we believe that studies such as ours are valuable, especially if they encourage further research. More studies with larger groups of patients and a prospective design are needed to confirm these findings and better understand how to predict treatment response in UC patients.

Conclusions

This study revealed that the PLT, HGB, and Fe levels may serve as significant predictors of response at the 14th week of VDZ therapy in patients with UC.

Moreover, iron deficiency anemia appears to be a potential risk factor for poor response to treatment. From a clinical perspective, iron deficiency anemia should be addressed prior to initiating biologic therapy. In addition, it is advisable to rule out other potential causes of anemia—such as performing an upper gastrointestinal endoscopy, as well as a gynecological consultation in women and a urological evaluation in men.

While the MCV may also be helpful in predicting long-term therapeutic outcomes, further studies are warranted to validate these findings and to identify reliable predictors of response maintenance, including parameters beyond those assessed in this analysis. We strongly recommend conducting a prospective, multicenter validation to confirm the generalizability and robustness of the obtained findings.

Importantly, baseline HGB, FE, and PLT are more likely to reflect the overall inflammatory burden and disease activity in UC rather than representing independent predictors of response to VDZ therapy. Therefore, their standalone predictive performance appears limited. These markers may nonetheless contribute to response stratification when incorporated into multivariable predictive frameworks alongside established inflammatory markers such as CRP, fecal biomarkers including FC, and validated clinical indices of disease activity. This observation underscores the complexity of treatment response prediction in UC and highlights the need for integrated biomarker models rather than reliance on single laboratory parameters.

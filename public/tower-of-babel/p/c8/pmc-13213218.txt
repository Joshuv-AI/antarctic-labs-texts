Curcumin-based smart responsive hydrogels: A controlled release system for bone defect regeneration
Authors: Jinrong Huo, Xueqin Hui, Mengmeng Lin, Hongwei Pan, Dawei Hou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe repair of bone tissue injuries and defects remains a major clinical challenge. This is particularly pronounced in aging populations, which face a growing incidence of osteoporosis-related fractures, traumatic bone defects, and regenerative deficiencies following bone tumor resection. Current strategies, such as autografts and allografts, are limited in efficacy. While bioactive molecules like growth factors and stem cells show promise, their clinical use is hindered by poor in vivo stability and inadequate retention at the target site, often necessitating high doses that introduce toxicity risks and compromise therapeutic outcomes. Curcumin, a natural polyphenol from turmeric, shows promise for bone repair via anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and osteogenic effects. Yet, its clinical translation is hindered by poor solubility, rapid metabolism, and low bioavailability. To overcome these issues, smart responsive hydrogels, as advanced delivery platforms, can respond to physiological cues or applied stimuli, enabling precise spatiotemporal curcumin release with high biocompatibility and tunable kinetics. This review summarizes recent advances in smart curcumin-loaded hydrogels for bone repair, focusing on design strategies, response mechanisms, therapeutic efficacy, and ongoing challenges, thereby guiding future research directions.Keywords: Smart hydrogels, Curcumin, Drug release, Bone regeneration

Introduction

Bone defects of critical size caused by severe trauma, infections, tumors, or congenital genetic disorders cannot be restored through the bone's self-repair mechanism, posing a major clinical challenge that requires external intervention to guide and accelerate the healing process [1,2]. Furthermore, factors such as diabetes, genetic predisposition, smoking, alcohol abuse, and improper treatment may lead to osteoblast deficiency or impaired microvascular formation at the defect site, all of which impair bone regeneration [3]. Currently, bone grafting, especially autologous bone grafting, is the best method for treating bone defects. However, it is limited by the insufficient supply of donor tissue, which requires the development of a second surgical area. This prolongs the recovery time, increases the risk of infection and hematoma, and also leads to a series of problems, including related immune rejection reactions, which significantly limit its clinical application [4]. Allogeneic bone obtained from cadaveric sources serves as a secondary option for bone grafting, avoiding the limitations of autologous bone transplantation. However, its reduced cellular content and heightened susceptibility to infection result in suboptimal osteogenic efficacy [5]. To overcome the challenges in bone grafting, researchers have adopted tissue engineering approaches by integrating cells and bioactive factors into biomaterial scaffolds to design and develop novel bone substitute grafts. These grafts possess both in vivo and in vitro biological functions, not only facilitating bone defect repair but also enabling the targeted delivery of therapeutic agents [6].

To date, researchers have designed and developed a diverse range of biomaterials for bone defect repair, including various natural and synthetic polymers. These biomaterials can regulate the extracellular microenvironment or drive cellular reprogramming to induce regeneration, demonstrating excellent bone repair effects in both preclinical studies and clinical applications [[7], [8], [9]]. Notably, among various biomaterial scaffolds, hydrogels have shown great potential in bone tissue engineering due to their unique advantages, such as excellent biocompatibility and biodegradability, adjustable mechanical properties, superior scalability, and injectability for filling irregular defects. In particular, smart-responsive hydrogels have garnered widespread attention in bone therapy and regeneration due to their ability to respond to both internal and external stimuli [10]. When activated by specific stimuli (e.g., near-infrared light, pH, or enzymes), smart-responsive hydrogels undergo chemical and physical transformations either through the gel itself or via embedded nanoparticles with responsive components, enabling the precise and controlled release of bioactive molecules [11]. Unlike conventional hydrogels, smart-responsive hydrogels can undergo programmable and controllable transformations as needed to adapt to the dynamic changes in the bone repair microenvironment [12]. Moreover, these hydrogels can activate bone formation and immune-related signaling pathways, regulate various cellular behaviors within the bone microenvironment, and thereby promote bone regeneration [13](Table 1).

Currently, driven by the need to mitigate long-term side effects associated with conventional clinical treatments, researchers are actively exploring the use of bioactive molecules with osteogenic potential to enhance bone formation. Among the most common bioactive molecules, bone morphogenetic proteins (BMPs), particularly BMP-2, which is a key member of the transforming growth factor (TGF)-β superfamily, have demonstrated significant osteogenic potential. These molecules effectively regulate stem cell physiology by mediating Smad and MAPK signaling pathways and are widely utilized in studies related to bone defect regeneration [14]. However, these bioactive molecules still face challenges, such as a short half-life, unstable release, high costs, and rapid degradation [15]. To develop more effective bioactive compounds, researchers have accelerated studies on natural compounds and their bioactive components. Curcumin, a natural polyphenol compound, has garnered significant attention for its therapeutic potential. Numerous studies have explored its antibacterial, anti-inflammatory, anti-aging, and antitumor properties [[16], [17], [18], [19]]. Notably, some research has demonstrated that curcumin can regulate osteoblast proliferation and differentiation, thereby promoting bone formation [20,21]. While curcumin regulates the inflammatory microenvironment and promotes bone repair, its hydrophobic nature limits its bioavailability [22]. To address this, combining curcumin with responsive hydrogels enhances solubility. The hydrogel system not only facilitates drug loading but also enables sustained, controlled release of curcumin, improving therapeutic targeting. This approach demonstrates significant potential applications in bone tissue engineering.

This review systematically examines the properties and bone repair mechanisms of curcumin, the design principles of responsive hydrogels, and their combined potential for application in bone tissue regeneration. This study synthesizes existing research, focusing on the unique advantages of curcumin-loaded responsive hydrogels in promoting bone regeneration, current challenges, and future research directions. It aims to provide theoretical references and practical guidance for related fields.

Properties of curcumin and its mechanism of bone repair

Chemical properties and biological activities of curcumin

The Compendium of Materia Medica documents that turmeric is effective against shoulder and arm pain caused by wind-cold-dampness. The Treatise on Trauma also notes its therapeutic effects on contusions, bruises, and painful swelling. Turmeric is composed of curcuminoids (including curcumin, n-nitrocurcumin, and bis-n-nitrocurcumin), volatile oils, and resin [23]. Curcumin, extracted from turmeric rhizomes, constitutes 77% of the active compounds. This natural β-diketone hydrophobic polyphenol possesses multiple bioactive properties. Its unique 1,7-diafarylheptane framework consists of a β-diketone and two ortho-methylated phenolic groups, with the molecular formula C21H20O6. This distinctive structure confers diverse biological activities. Studies have shown that curcumin exhibits significant anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, antitumor, and immune-regulatory activities, and has been widely used in the treatment of diseases affecting the central nervous system, cardiovascular system, skin, and other systems [24](Fig. 1). Furthermore, owing to its multifunctional pharmacological properties, it also serves as a valuable component in nutritional supplements [25]. Several studies have demonstrated the beneficial effects of curcumin in bone disorders and inflammatory conditions. Curcumin inhibits the production of inflammatory factors such as NF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6, which are crucial for bone repair [26]. It also alleviates oxidative stress and helps restore mitochondrial function in osteoblasts [27]. Moreover, curcumin can enhance osteoblast differentiation while inhibiting osteoclast resorption, thereby increasing bone density and improving bone microstructure [28].

The functions and biomedical applications of Curcumin.

Molecular pathways of curcumin in promoting osteogenesis

Curcumin regulates osteogenic differentiation through multiple pathways, not by linear activation of a single pathway, but through a multi-target, networked, and synergistic process. On one hand, curcumin actively activates the positive pro-synthetic pathway, directly initiating the osteogenic gene-related program. In the Wnt/β-catenin pathway, it stabilizes and promotes the accumulation of β-catenin in the nucleus by inhibiting the key negative regulator GSK-3β, thereby binding to the TCF/LEF transcription factor to strongly activate the expression of osteogenic genes, such as Runx2 and Osx [29]. In the BMP/Smad pathway, curcumin upregulates the expression of osteogenic activators like BMP-2, activates the phosphorylation of Smad1/5/8, and forms a nuclear complex with Smad4. This complex binds to the promoter of Runx2, creating a powerful synergistic effect with the Wnt pathway to collectively drive osteogenic differentiation signals to their peak [30,31]. On the other hand, curcumin precisely regulates the negative protective pathway, clearing obstacles for osteogenesis. Inhibiting the NF-κB signaling pathway effectively suppresses the release of pro-inflammatory factors. This not only directly removes the inflammatory suppression on the Wnt and BMP pathways, creating an osteogenic microenvironment free from inflammatory interference, but also simultaneously reduces osteoclast formation, shifting the focus of cellular activity from bone resorption to bone formation [32]. Meanwhile, curcumin activates the Nrf2 antioxidant pathway by upregulating the expression of antioxidant enzymes such as heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1), effectively eliminating excess reactive oxygen species and alleviating oxidative stress, which is crucial for maintaining the stability and functionality of key transcription factors, such as Runx2, thereby ensuring the smooth progression of osteogenesis [27]. This synergistic mechanism enables curcumin to regulate bone metabolic balance through multi-dimensional and multi-level interventions precisely, positioning it as a highly promising natural compound for promoting bone formation and repair.

Metabolic characteristics of curcumin in the bone microenvironment

In the bone microenvironment, curcumin metabolism exhibits distinct tissue-specificity. Studies reveal that circulating curcumin glucuronides undergo targeted deacarboxylation in bone tissue, a process primarily mediated by the highly expressed β-glucuronidase (GUSB) enzyme. This enzyme efficiently hydrolyzes the glucuronic acid group from curcumin glucuronides, converting them into bioactive curcumin aglycones. This targeted metabolic profile enables curcumin to selectively accumulate and act on bone tissue, thereby effectively modulating bone metabolic balance [33]. Furthermore, curcumin significantly improves the bone immune microenvironment. Research indicates that curcumin promotes macrophage polarization toward the anti-inflammatory M2 phenotype by regulating key signaling pathways, including the NF-κB and STAT3 pathways. This immunophenotypic transformation further induces the remodeling of the cytokine profile in the bone microenvironment, significantly upregulating the expression levels of regenerative cytokines, such as BMP-2 and TGF-β [34]. These cytokines, acting as potent inducers of osteogenic differentiation, directly stimulate mesenchymal stem cells to differentiate into osteoblasts, thereby collectively establishing a microenvironment conducive to bone regeneration. This immunomodulatory effect on the osteoimmune microenvironment driven by metabolic specificity may be one of the key mechanisms by which curcumin promotes bone repair. It not only directly inhibits osteoclast activity to maintain bone metabolic balance but also indirectly facilitates bone formation through immune regulation, thereby achieving bidirectional regulation of the bone remodeling process.

Design principle of intelligent responsive hydrogel

Basic structure and drug loading mechanism of hydrogel

Hydrogels are highly cross-linked, water-swelling, hydrophilic polymer three-dimensional networks that have garnered significant research attention due to their outstanding physical, chemical, and biological properties, as well as their potential applications across various fields [35,36]. The hydrogel framework is typically composed of natural or synthetic macromolecular materials, where crosslinking points between polymer chains form a stable 3D network that determines mechanical properties and swelling behavior. The internal space of this network is filled with bound water, which not only endows hydrogels with ECM-like softness and excellent biocompatibility but also provides channels for the transport and exchange of drug molecules. The drug loading mechanisms of hydrogels primarily include three modes [37]: First, direct physical encapsulation, where drugs are mixed with polymer solutions before gelation, allowing drug molecules to be captured within the network during the crosslinking process. Alternatively, drugs can be adsorbed and retained in the pores through swelling of the dried gel in a drug solution. Secondly, there is the affinity interaction based on intermolecular forces. For example, charged drugs can be electrostatically adsorbed onto polymer chains with opposite charges [38], or hydrophobic drugs can be encapsulated into hydrophobic microdomains of the network via van der Waals forces [39]. This mechanism can provide a stronger binding force and slower release kinetics. The third type is the more precise approach of covalent bonding, where drug molecules are directly conjugated to the polymer backbone via degradable chemical bonds (such as PH, light, or enzyme-sensitive bonds), enabling intelligent on-demand drug release. Hydrogels, with their precisely tunable porous hydrophilic networks, can efficiently load drugs through various physical and chemical mechanisms. By responding to external environmental changes, they regulate their swelling behavior or break specific bonds, ultimately achieving precise control over the release rate, timing, and location of drugs. This demonstrates significant application potential in modern pharmaceutics, particularly in areas such as targeted drug delivery and long-term sustained release.

Physical and chemical basis of reactive release

The responsive drug release capacity of hydrogels stems from their unique physicochemical and structural properties. Dynamic variations in the three-dimensional polymer network, triggered by internal and external stimuli, provide the foundation for achieving controlled drug release [40]. At present, the drug release mechanisms of responsive hydrogels can be systematically classified into four fundamental pathways: swelling-shrinking-mediated pore size variation, stimulus-responsive crosslinking cleavage, photothermal phase transition regulation, and functionally graded degradation (Table 2).

Drug release mechanisms and functions of stimuli-responsive hydrogels.

Swelling- and shrinkage-mediated network pore size regulation is the most fundamental physically controlled-release mechanism. Under environmental stimuli such as pH, ionic strength, and temperature, the polymer chains of hydrogels undergo hydrophilic–hydrophobic transitions and changes in electrostatic interactions, which induce reversible swelling or shrinkage of the three-dimensional network. The corresponding changes in network pore size further modulate the diffusion rate of encapsulated drug molecules. For instance, PH-sensitive hydrogels exhibit gastric-protective and intestinal-targeted release behavior in simulated gastrointestinal fluids. Such release behavior complies with the Korsmeyer–Peppas model and presents typical Fickian diffusion characteristics [41,42].

The cleavage of stimulus-sensitive crosslinks and subsequent structural disassembly represent the core chemical mechanism for triggered burst release and sequential release. ROS, enzymes, and reductive microenvironments can specifically cleave sensitive crosslinking sites within hydrogels, thereby rapidly reducing crosslink density, loosening the network structure, and even causing localized partial disintegration, thereby achieving on-demand burst drug release. In the tumor microenvironment of triple-negative breast cancer with overexpressed matrix metalloproteinases (MMP2/9), for example, enzyme-responsive hydrogels accelerate drug release via network degradation, which greatly enhances targeting capability and local therapeutic efficacy [43]. This mechanism possesses high stimulus specificity and enables precise drug release in response to the pathological microenvironment of lesion sites.

Thermo-responsive or photothermal phase transition induces a hydrophilic-to-hydrophobic transition of polymer chains under temperature fluctuations, leading to a sol–gel phase transition or abrupt volume changes in hydrogels, thereby rapidly activating or blocking drug release channels. Typically, thermosensitive hydrogels undergo rapid and reversible sol–gel transition near the critical temperature and regulate drug release in an on–off manner through macroscopic volume changes [44]. In addition, light-responsive systems integrated with photothermal conversion materials can achieve non-invasive, remotely triggered release with high spatiotemporal resolution and avoid interference from systemic stimuli. Their phase transition windows can be precisely optimized via molecular structural design [45].

Graded degradation of the polymer network endows hydrogels with long-term, programmed drug-release patterns. With the gradual degradation of hydrogel scaffolds, loaded drugs transform from a restricted binding state to a freely diffusible state. The release kinetics are strongly coupled to material degradation rates, resulting in a continuous transition from initial rapid release to sustained slow release, meeting the requirements for a long-term, stable drug supply for tissue repair.

Notably, the above drug release mechanisms do not function independently. Instead, they work synergistically to regulate the overall release behavior of responsive hydrogels. Rational design of multi-responsive units can integrate external stimulation, structural response, release kinetics, and biological functions. This advanced design strategy enables precise, sequential, and controllable delivery of curcumin within the bone defect microenvironment, thereby remarkably improving its local therapeutic efficacy and spatiotemporal delivery accuracy.

Design strategies for bone microenvironment-specific responses

Bone regeneration is a highly temporal and dynamic physiological process. The early inflammatory phase is characterized by decreased local PH, elevated ROS levels, increased MMP expression, and hypoxia, followed by the repair stage, dominated by immune modulation, angiogenesis, and osteogenic differentiation. Given the multiple and complex characteristics of the bone repair microenvironment, single-responsive hydrogel systems are difficult to adapt to such dynamic and heterogeneous microenvironmental changes (Table 3). The design of specialized responsive hydrogels requires the systematic integration of multiple responsive elements, and its design rationale is closely aligned with the dynamic pathological microenvironment during bone defect healing.

Numerous studies have demonstrated that incorporating responsive chemical groups or functional nanoparticles into hydrogel systems can greatly enhance their environmental sensing and regulatory capabilities. Such smart hydrogels can trigger programmable physicochemical changes in response to specific stimuli, enabling spatiotemporally controlled drug release and dynamic microenvironment regulation, thereby optimizing the overall bone repair process [10]. For example, by targeting the coexistence of excessive ROS and hypoxia in bone defect regions, researchers have developed dual-functional CPP-L/GelMA hydrogels with ROS-scavenging and oxygen-releasing properties. By controllably releasing catalase (CAT) and ROS-responsive oxygen-generating nanoparticles (PFC@PLGA/PPS), these hydrogels effectively alleviate oxidative stress and ameliorate local hypoxia, thereby breaking the vicious cycle of “hypoxia–oxidative damage” and ultimately accelerating bone regeneration [46]. Kurian et al. [47] constructed photocontrollable GelMA-based composite hydrogels. Upon external light stimulation, this platform enables high-precision drug delivery and regulation of cellular behavior, alleviating inflammatory responses and facilitating osteogenic differentiation of stem cells. Moreover, enzyme-responsive hydrogels are well adapted to the high enzymatic activity during matrix remodeling, achieving dynamic synchronization between scaffold degradation and neotissue ingrowth [48]. Ultrasound-responsive hydrogels can synergistically regulate the immune microenvironment and bone repair by activating signaling pathways, such as the PI3K-Akt axis [49].

These sophisticated designs are not merely a simple superposition of diverse functions, but a rational integration strategy tailored to the pathological features of each bone healing stage. pH responsiveness adapts to the acidic microenvironment in the inflammatory phase; ROS responsiveness targets oxidative stress injury; enzyme responsiveness matches the MMP-rich microenvironment during matrix remodeling; light/ultrasound responsiveness provides exogenous and precise regulation; and oxygen-releasing modules specifically relieve local hypoxia. Through the synergistic effect of multiple responsive units, multifunctional hydrogel systems enable full-cycle microenvironment remodeling, including inflammation suppression, immune regulation, angiogenesis, and osteogenic induction [50]. This multi-responsive strategy not only improves the precision and therapeutic efficacy of bone repair, but also provides an adaptable intelligent delivery platform for the clinical translation of bone defect therapy under complex pathological conditions, such as osteoporosis, infected bone defects, and diabetic bone injuries.

Common responsive curcumin hydrogel systems

Curcumin is a highly promising bioactive molecule for bone repair, yet its clinical translation has long been restricted by inherent pharmacological drawbacks [[55], [56], [57], [58]]. As a highly hydrophobic compound, curcumin exhibits extremely low solubility under physiological conditions. It is also susceptible to chemical degradation induced by light irradiation, oxidation, and alkaline environments, which severely hinders its tissue penetration and bioactivity retention, thereby limiting the establishment of therapeutically effective local concentrations. After systemic administration, curcumin is rapidly metabolized via hepatic first-pass effects and the cytochrome P450 enzyme system, with a plasma half-life of only 1–2 h. Combined with rapid renal clearance, the retention time of curcumin in the bone defect microenvironment is far shorter than the period required for bone regeneration.

Owing to poor water solubility, rapid metabolism, and insufficient affinity for bone tissue, conventional administration routes fail to enrich curcumin at bone defect sites. The majority of the drug is degraded or eliminated before reaching the target region, restricting its capacity to modulate the osteoblast–osteoclast balance. Furthermore, curcumin exhibits a concentration-dependent biphasic effect: optimal concentrations promote osteogenic differentiation, whereas excessive doses may inhibit bone formation.

To address the above challenges, responsive hydrogel systems are rationally designed to overcome the intrinsic pharmacological limitations of curcumin (Fig. 2). By precisely modulating in situ drug-delivery behaviors, these smart platforms provide critical strategic support and technical guarantees for the safe and efficient application of curcumin in bone repair.

Common curcumin-loaded responsive hydrogels.

Light-responsive hydrogel system

Photosensitive hydrogels are typically composed of a polymer matrix and photo-functional components. Their physicochemical properties undergo significant changes upon exposure to specific wavelengths of light, enabling the transformation from a liquid to a gel state. Such visible light-induced systems demonstrate broad application prospects in the biomedical field [59]. Near-infrared (NIR) light, an electromagnetic wave located between the visible and mid-infrared regions, exhibits exceptional penetration capabilities. Unlike other light sources, NIR light exhibits superior tissue permeability due to its minimal absorption by bodily fluids, making it an ideal choice for designing light-responsive hydrogels [60]. NIR light-responsive hydrogels utilize photothermal conversion materials to transform NIR light energy into physical or chemical signals, thereby inducing structural changes in the hydrogel and facilitating the controlled release of drug molecules [61,62]. For instance, Punnida et al. [63] developed a bilayer hydrogel system to enhance curcumin release under near-infrared (NIR) irradiation. The upper layer, composed of Pul-MA/PDA, functions as a photothermal conversion layer that efficiently converts NIR light into thermal energy, raising local temperatures to approximately 40 °C. This thermal energy facilitates the controlled release of curcumin from the underlying Pul-βCD-MA@Cur/PEGDA hydrogel layer, resulting in a cumulative release rate of 33%, compared to 15% in the unirradiated control group. Notably, NIR light exhibits significant advantages, including deep tissue penetration (up to several centimeters) and minimal tissue damage, making it particularly suitable for the targeted therapy of deep tissues.

NIR-responsive hydrogels enable the on-demand release of curcumin through spatiotemporal NIR irradiation, which holds significant implications for the multi-regulation of the surrounding microenvironment. In this context, Kuang et al. [64] developed PLGA microspheres loaded with curcumin via a single emulsion/evaporation method, then blended them with IR820 and methyl cellulose to construct an injectable Cur-MP/IR820 hydrogel (Fig. 3). In vitro experiments demonstrated that the synergistic effect of photothermal therapy and chemotherapy could completely eradicate K7M2wt osteosarcoma cells. Meanwhile, sustained curcumin release promoted the expression of alkaline phosphatase and calcium deposition in bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells. In vivo experiments demonstrated that, after injection into tumor sites, the composite hydrogel generated localized hyperthermia of 51 °C within 5 min under 808 nm laser irradiation, enabling the ablation of tumors. The thermal effect simultaneously accelerated and precisely triggered the pulsed release of curcumin, enhanced cell membrane permeability, and collectively induced apoptosis in tumor cells. Additionally, continuous curcumin release further promoted bone regeneration and enhanced the efficacy of bone integration in vivo.

PH-responsive curcumin hydrogel system

The PH of healthy tissues typically remains stable between 6.5 and 7.2. However, pathological conditions such as bone defects, infections, and chronic inflammation can cause characteristic PH changes in the local inflammatory microenvironment [65]. PH-responsive hydrogels can detect PH gradients and undergo corresponding physical or chemical property changes, making them ideal triggers for the controlled release of smart drugs. The release behavior of curcumin in pH-responsive hydrogels exhibits distinct pH dependence. This phenomenon stems from the varying ionization levels of functional groups (e.g., carboxyl groups) in the polymer under different pH conditions, which consequently alter the swelling behavior of the hydrogel [66]. It is worth noting that the bone defect site undergoes dynamic PH changes during the inflammatory and repair phases, which provides a biological basis for the design of sequential controlled drug release [67].

PH-responsive curcumin hydrogels are typically constructed using biocompatible polymers such as hyaluronic acid, sodium alginate, and carboxymethyl cellulose as matrix materials. By incorporating pH-sensitive groups or composite pH-responsive materials, these hydrogels can specifically respond to pH fluctuations in the microenvironment, thereby regulating the crosslinking density of the polymer network, which enables precise control over both the swelling behavior and curcumin release rate within the physiologically relevant pH range of 5.0-7.4 [68]. Notably, the release kinetics of pH-responsive curcumin hydrogels show significant correlation with their osteogenic efficacy. Research shows that under pH 7.4 conditions, the PSCPP-CUR hydrogel system exhibits a curcumin release rate of 3%, which increases to 90% at pH 5.4 [69]. This release profile is highly aligned with the needs of bone repair, as higher drug concentrations are required during the early inflammatory phase to control inflammation. In contrast, sustained release is needed during the repair phase to promote osteogenesis. Additionally, the addition of hydroxyapatite (HA) not only enhances the mechanical properties of the hydrogel but also provides synergistic bioactivity. This PH-responsive release mechanism enables the precise regulation of both the inflammatory phase and the subsequent remodeling phase of bone healing.

Enzyme-responsive hydrogel systems

In both normal and pathological bone metabolism processes, multiple enzymes, including matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs), azoreductase, phospholipase, polymerase-1, and thrombin, exhibit dynamic expression patterns [70]. Notably, the levels of matrix metalloproteinase (MMP) family members in bone regeneration microenvironments show significant elevation. These enzymes not only permeate all stages of bone remodeling but also play crucial roles in specific signaling pathways [71]. Additionally, hydrolases such as tissue proteases and hyaluronidase play a vital role in maintaining the balance between bone resorption and regeneration. The expression of these enzymes exhibits remarkable spatiotemporal specificity, which provides a crucial basis for the precise design of enzyme-responsive hydrogel drug delivery systems. Enzyme-responsive hydrogels achieve intelligent release by introducing specific enzyme substrate sequences. For instance, Wang et al. [72] developed a multifunctional hydrogel microneedle system (GMPL@GC MNs) using methacrylated gelatin (GM) as the matrix material, which integrates ε-polylysine (PL) for enhanced antibacterial properties and gelatin-curcumin nanoparticles (GC NPs) for inflammation regulation. This system leverages the high expression of MMP at wound sites to trigger the specific degradation of GC NPs, thereby achieving controlled release of Cur. The enzyme-responsive biodegradable system designed regulates hydrogel degradation rates through enzymatic reactions, synchronizing with bone formation processes, which not only provides space for tissue growth but also facilitates the delivery of bioactive molecules and drugs.

Enzyme-responsive hydrogels enable the precise synchronization of drug release with the stages of bone repair. During the early inflammatory phase of bone defects, enzymes such as MMPs are abundantly enriched in the microenvironment, which can trigger hydrogel network disintegration or pore enlargement [73]. This leads to the rapid release of curcumin, which subsequently suppresses endogenous ROS production and scavenges free radicals via the GSK3β-Nrf2 signaling pathway. This process helps maintain osteoblast viability, promotes osteogenic differentiation, and creates a stable microenvironment conducive to bone regeneration [27]. During the bone formation phase, the hydrogel can respond to changes in the activity of alkaline phosphatase (ALP), a key marker of osteogenic differentiation, to enable the gradual release of drug molecules. This process synergistically promotes osteoblast proliferation and enhances the expression of osteogenic markers, such as ALP and osteocalcin [74]. Meanwhile, it helps suppress osteoclastogenesis, thereby regulating the balance between bone resorption and bone formation, and facilitating the orderly remodeling of bone tissue. Animal experiments confirmed that this spatiotemporally controlled release significantly enhanced bone regeneration efficiency, with improved scaffold integration and new bone formation observed in rat models [75].

Research validation and practice led by advanced technology

Microfluidic technology simulates the microenvironment of bone defects

Microfluidic technology simulates the biochemical and mechanical microenvironment of bone defect regions by precisely controlling fluid dynamics. This technique enables the construction of biomimetic structures containing vascular networks and cellular matrices to evaluate hydrogel drug release behavior under dynamic conditions. Si et al. [76] developed a novel composite microsphere system (CE@BP-Gel) by encapsulating curcumin-loaded exosome microspheres (Cur@Exos) within bisphosphate-modified GelMA hydrogel microspheres using microfluidic technology, which mimics the spatiotemporal control of drug delivery by the extracellular matrix (Fig. 4). The study demonstrated that CE@BP-Gel exhibits excellent biocompatibility, accelerates biomineralization, and its sustained release of Cur@Exos regulates RAW264.7 cells toward M2 polarization, inhibits osteoclast activity, and fosters an osteogenic immune microenvironment. Additionally, it directly promotes osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs and angiogenesis in HUVECs, significantly accelerating the repair of critical-size skull defects in rats. Further mechanistic studies revealed that CE@BP-Gel mitigates ROS-induced DNA damage in inflammatory environments, with embedded curcumin enhancing DNA repair through TDP1 enzyme activation and reducing the expression of macrophage inflammatory cytokines, providing a novel strategy for bone defect repair.

Dual emulsification technology for precise drug release control

The dual emulsification technology demonstrates remarkable precision and intelligence in controlling drug release, leveraging the material degradation characteristics and microsphere design principles. When drug-loaded microspheres are implanted, their shells gradually degrade under physiological influences (such as enzymatic activity and pH fluctuations), releasing bioactive substances through a sustained, controlled process. Compared to conventional drug delivery systems, this technology prevents abrupt drug release, maintaining stable therapeutic concentrations in the body. For instance, Lu et al. [77] developed curcumin/poly (lactic acid-hydroxyacetic acid) copolymer microspheres via dual emulsification, encapsulated within copper-ion crosslinked carboxymethyl chitosan/sodium alginate hydrogel to create the CA-Cur@Cu composite hydrogel. This system enables prolonged curcumin release, thereby enhancing its antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects while inducing macrophage polarization toward M2 subtypes, thereby optimizing the bone microenvironment. Simultaneously, sustained Cu2+ release from the hydrogel promotes angiogenesis and osteogenesis, synergizing with curcumin's anti-inflammatory properties to accelerate bone defect repair. Additionally, the CA-Cur@Cu hydrogel exhibits potent antibacterial activity, effectively suppressing clinical bacterial infections. This responsive curcumin hydrogel material, which integrates immune modulation, vascularization, osteogenesis, and anti-infection capabilities, offers innovative therapeutic strategies and clinical translation potential for complex bone defects and high-risk infection scenarios.

Dual-release of multiple drugs via drug-eluting photothermal micro-nano-topological technology

The drug-eluting photothermal micro-nano topological technology achieves spatiotemporal controlled drug release through three breakthroughs: physical morphology optimization (enhanced drug loading and responsive release), precise photothermal control (gentle temperature elevation triggers release), and synergistic biological effects (immune modulation, angiogenesis, and osteogenesis). This provides an efficient solution for treating complex bone diseases such as bone tumors and infectious defects. Leveraging the unique advantages of this technology, Ji et al. [78] developed a dual-release system using 3D-printed porous Ti-6Al-4V implants to address challenges in post-surgical bone repair, including the clearance of residual tumor cells, prevention of bacterial infections, and effective bone integration. Under 808 nm NIR irradiation, the system achieves localized photothermal heating (54 °C), which triggers the degradation of chitosan/alginic acid hydrogel, continuously releasing magnesium peroxide nanoparticles, curcumin, and paclitaxel. Simultaneously, the substrate material slowly releases Zn2+. Biological experiments demonstrate that this dual-release system systematically inhibits tumor growth, eradicates bacterial infections, and promotes osteogenesis. Further research reveals its ability to regulate intracellular signaling pathways in both bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) and osteosarcoma cells (HOS), coordinating metabolic reprogramming and organelle dynamics to enhance MSCs' osteogenic differentiation while inducing HOS apoptosis. Additionally, the system amplifies ROS generation and intensifies oxidative stress in bacterial populations. This study provides a novel solution for the synergistic treatment of osteosarcoma and bone tissue repair in bacterial infection environments, establishes a design paradigm for multifunctional biomaterials, and promotes the application and development of 3D printing technology in the field of biomedical implants.

Bionic gradient with dual network interpenetration

Dual-network hydrogels (DN) typically consist of two interpenetrating polymer networks, a structure that significantly enhances mechanical strength and responsiveness while endowing the material with self-healing capabilities. This allows the system to restore structural integrity and functionality after mechanical damage, thereby extending the service life of drug delivery systems. Moreover, the dual-network architecture offers high tunability, facilitating the incorporation of bioresponsive units for precise control of drug release. Natural polymer-based hydrogel systems exhibit exceptional biocompatibility, allowing for effective localized retention and sustained drug release when administered in injectable or implantable forms, thereby reducing systemic toxicity. By integrating nanomaterials or optimizing crosslink density, further improvements in drug loading capacity, targeting precision, and stability can be achieved. For instance, Chen et al. [79] utilized carboxymethyl chitosan (CMCS), sodium alginate oxide (OSA), and polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) to construct a dual-network hydrogel through their mutual penetration, designed for simultaneous cartilage and bone repair. The upper layer of this hydrogel is loaded with puerarin (Pue) to facilitate rapid initial release, promoting chondrocyte proliferation and differentiation. The lower layer contains ZnO nanoparticles encapsulating curcumin (Cur), which enables long-term osteogenic induction through sustained drug release. In vitro experiments demonstrated that this hydrogel exhibits excellent mechanical properties, self-healing capability, adhesion, and biocompatibility. Biologically, this hydrogel scaffold not only enhances alkaline phosphatase (ALP) expression and calcium deposition in MC3T3-14 cells to accelerate bone repair, but also stimulates ATDC5 cell proliferation and glycosaminoglycan (GAG) synthesis for cartilage regeneration. Overall, this meticulously designed dual-network hydrogel scaffold demonstrates significant potential as a promising and efficient tissue engineering material for bone and cartilage repair.

Conclusions and perspectives

While autologous, allogeneic, and xenogeneic bone grafts have been extensively used to repair various clinical bone defects, these natural bone grafting strategies are often constrained by donor site damage, limited availability, immune rejection risks, disease transmission hazards, and high costs. Consequently, developing biomimetic bone graft substitutes that overcome these limitations has become an urgent research priority. Bone tissue engineering focuses on replicating the microstructure, mechanical properties, and biological functions of natural bone through biomimetic materials. This approach aims to precisely replicate the capabilities of bone conduction, osteoinduction, and osteogenesis, thereby establishing a new paradigm for achieving ideal bone defect regeneration and repair.

In this context, smart-responsive curcumin hydrogel has emerged as a cutting-edge bone repair material with remarkable application potential. By leveraging unique smart-responsive mechanisms (such as light, pH, and enzyme triggers), this hydrogel achieves precise spatiotemporal and dose-controlled release of curcumin at bone defect sites. This breakthrough effectively addresses the limitations of traditional drug delivery methods, including poor targeting, low bioavailability, and potential toxicity, while significantly enhancing the efficacy of bone repair. Consequently, smart-responsive curcumin hydrogel offers an auspicious new approach to overcoming drug-controlled release challenges in bone repair applications.

Extensive research has confirmed the promising potential of smart-responsive curcumin hydrogels in bone tissue engineering. However, their transition from laboratory research to clinical application still faces multiple challenges. Future studies should focus on overcoming preclinical barriers, including the design, application, clinical use, and commercialization of smart hydrogels.

From the perspective of material properties, the mechanical performance of smart-responsive curcumin hydrogels requires urgent improvement to meet the physiological demands of weight-bearing bone repair. Meanwhile, the discrepancy between their degradation kinetics and bone tissue regeneration rates poses a critical challenge: achieving precise coordination between these two factors is key to enhancing bone integration efficiency. Although these hydrogels demonstrate overall good biocompatibility, their limited functionality and restricted osteogenic capacity remain limitations. Future research should explore not only curcumin loading but also develop multifunctional platforms capable of co-loading other bioactive factors (such as BMP-2, VEGF, nano-hydroxyapatite), antibiotics, or stem cells. This approach could optimize mechanical properties and degradation behavior, potentially enabling the synchronized promotion of multiple processes, including immune regulation, vascularization, and osteogenic differentiation, to accelerate high-quality bone regeneration. Additionally, researchers should focus on developing multi-responsive or even cascade-responsive systems to simulate the complex physiopathological processes of bone repair more accurately. Simultaneously, exploring precise regulation of response thresholds to avoid premature or delayed release remains crucial for achieving precision medicine.

In clinical translation, while responsive-releasing curcumin hydrogels have demonstrated excellent bone repair efficacy in vitro and animal studies, their clinical application still faces multiple challenges. First, most research remains at the animal testing stage, with results that are insufficient to confirm similar osteogenic effects in human large-scale bone defect repair. Second, the hydrogel preparation process and quality control standards remain underdeveloped, posing difficulties for large-scale production. Third, widely used sterilization methods, such as heating, chemical disinfection, and irradiation, may be incompatible with smart hydrogels. Fourth, as a novel biomaterial, its clinical application requires overcoming external barriers such as regulatory approvals and medical insurance policies. Therefore, future research should focus on preclinical systematic evaluations (e.g., long-term follow-up in large animal models) and optimization of large-scale production processes to lay a solid foundation for eventual clinical trials.

In conclusion, the smart-responsive release system of curcumin hydrogel represents a promising development in bone tissue engineering and drug delivery. Through interdisciplinary collaboration in materials science, biology, and medicine, and continuous optimization of its design, we have every reason to believe that this strategy could soon become a practical clinical approach for treating refractory bone defects.

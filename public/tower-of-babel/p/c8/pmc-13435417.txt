Long non-coding RNA SNHG17 contributes to proliferation and migration of retinal vascular endothelial cell in diabetic retinopathy through miR-34a-5p/CDK6 axis
Authors: Lichun Wei, Jing Song, Shasha Han, Min Wu, Huimin Liang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundLong non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) emerge as crucial regulators in diabetic retinopathy (DR).ObjectivesThis study examined the expression profile of lncRNA SNHG17 in DR patients, and the underlying mechanism was probed in human retinal microvascular endothelial cells (hRMECs).Materials and methods120 healthy controls (HC), 100 patients with T2DM, and 253 patients with DR were enrolled. hRMECs were treated with high glucose (HG) to mimic DR in vitro. qRT-PCR was performed for mRNA measurement. Cell viability and migration were detected under diverse conditions. Claudin-5 (CLDN5) concentration was detected via ELISA. The predicted targeting relationship was verified via luciferase reporter assay.ResultsSNHG17 was notably downregulated in patients’ serum and linked to DR progression. Among these patients, serum SNHG17 was the lowest in those with proliferative DR (PDR). In vitro, SNHG17 overexpression significantly boosted proliferation, migration and CLDN5 expression of HG-exposed hRMECs while inhibiting their apoptosis. SNHG17 functioned as a sponge for miR-34a-5p, whose upregulation reversed SNHG17’s effects on hRMECs. CDK6, a target of miR-34a-5p, was downregulated in HG-treated hRMECs, and its serum levels correlated negatively with miR-34a-5p across all participants.ConclusionDownregulation of SNHG17 was linked to the onset and development of DR. Overexpression of SNHG17 can recover the proliferation and migration of hRMECs that were suppressed by HG through sponging miR-34a-5p, and CDK6 may be the target of the SNHG17/miR-34a-5p axis.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12886-026-04962-y.Keywords: Diabetic retinopathy, SNHG17/miR-34a-5p axis, hRMECs, CDK6

Introduction

Diabetic retinopathy (DR) refers to a set of fundus abnormalities. These are brought about due to the impairment of retinal microvessels as a consequence of diabetes [1]. DR ranks among the prevalent microvascular complications resulting from diabetes [2]. DR can seriously affect patients’ vision and is one of the leading causes of blindness in the adult population. Clinically, DR can be divided into non-proliferative DR (NPDR) and proliferative DR (PDR). Fundus examination is the main method to diagnose diabetic retinopathy. Strict control of blood glucose, blood pressure and blood lipids is the basis for the treatment of diabetic retinopathy, which can delay the progression of the disease [3]. Before substantial pathological advancement, the disease shows no symptoms. Currently, the effective treatments for DR are only suitable for the late stage of the disease and are accompanied by notable adverse reactions [4]. Thus, the biological diagnostic markers related to the disease progression hold great importance for the treatment of DR.

Long non-coding RNA (lncRNA) represents a category of non-coding RNA molecules. These molecules are longer than 200 nucleotides. Although lncRNAs lack the capacity to encode proteins, they are capable of exerting significant regulatory functions at various levels, including the transcriptional and post-transcriptional levels [5]. The role and mechanism of lncRNA in DR is one of the hot topics in the field of ophthalmology [6]. During the development of DR, certain lncRNAs are able to influence the development of retinal blood vessels [7, 8]. They achieve this by modulating the expression of factors associated with angiogenesis, such as vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), TDRG1 and ANRIL [9, 10]. LncRNA can also affect the apoptosis of retinal cells, for example, dysregulated lncRNA LUADT1 can mediate the apoptosis of retinal pigment epithelial cells through targeting miR-383/peroxiredoxin 3 axis [11]. In addition, the regulatory role of lncRNAs in inflammatory processes is also one of the important mechanisms involved in DR, such as HOTTIP [12]. Further research on the mechanism of lncRNA in DR is expected to discover new signaling pathways and molecular regulatory networks, further improve the understanding of disease occurrence and development, and offer a theoretical foundation for the development of more therapeutic targets and strategies.

Small nucleolar RNA host gene 17 (SNHG17) is an lncRNA with a length of 1,186 nucleotides, located at 20q11.23 of the human chromosome. SNHG17 expression is decreased under diabetic conditions, and it is closely correlated with several diabetic complications. For example, plasma SNHG17 level is determined to decline in gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) women and exerts a diagnostic biomarker for disease occurrence [13]. In type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) patients, decreased SNHG17 has also been detected, and its expression exhibits a negative association with high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) [14]. In addition, dysregulated SNHG17 is also identified in T2DM complications, such as diabetic nephropathy (DN) and diabetic cardiomyopathy (DCM) [15, 16]. Therefore, whether SNHG17 is involved in the development of DR attracts our concern. Extensive research has confirmed that dysregulation of microRNA-34a-5p (miR-34a-5p) expression is closely associated with DR, making it a key candidate molecule in studies of the molecular mechanisms underlying DR. Previous studies have established that miR-34a-5p is a direct target of SNHG17, with SNHG17 suppressing its expression through a negative regulatory mechanism [17, 18]. This regulatory relationship has been partially corroborated in diabetes-related complications, suggesting SNHG17 may participate in DR pathological regulation by modulating miR-34a-5p. Given that the precise regulatory mechanisms of SNHG17/ miR-34a-5p axis in DR remain incompletely elucidated, selecting miR-34a-5p as a target for in-depth investigation of SNHG17 holds promise for revealing the molecular mechanisms by which SNHG17 modulates DR.

In the current study, the expression pattern of lncRNA SNHG17 and miR-34a-5p was measured in the serum of DR and T2DM patients. Moreover, the underlying mechanism was explored in high-glucose (HG) treated human retinal microvascular endothelial cells (hRMECs). This research may provide novel theoretical foundations and potential targets for the early diagnosis and targeted treatment of DR.

Materials and methods

Study population

This study was conducted under the strict review of the Ethics Committee of Dongying People’s Hospital. The study population included 120 healthy controls (HC), 100 patients with T2DM, and 253 patients with DR. Among them, the DR patients were further divided into 145 patients with NPDR and 108 patients with PDR. Post hoc power analysis was conducted using G*Power 3.1 software, with a significance level of α = 0.05 and a medium effect size of f = 0.25. Calculations indicate that the statistical power of this study is 95.7%.

The diagnostic criteria for T2DM are as follows: The fasting plasma glucose (FPG) level is 126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L) or higher; The 2-hour plasma glucose level in the 75 g oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) is 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) or higher; The glycated hemoglobin (A1c) level is 6.5% or higher. All patients underwent a comprehensive visual function examination, as well as anterior and posterior segment examinations of the eyes. The diagnostic criteria for NPDR encompass the presence of microaneurysms, retinal haemorrhages, hard exudates, cotton-wool spots, intraretinal microvascular abnormalities (IRMA), and macular oedema, without retinal neovascularisation or fibrous proliferation. The diagnostic criteria for PDR involve the development of retinal or papillary neovascularisation and fibro-vascular proliferative membranes on the basis of NPDR lesions, which may be accompanied by vitreous haemorrhage or traction retinal detachment. The formation of neovascularisation serves as the primary distinguishing feature between NPDR and PDR.

To ensure the accuracy and reliability of the study, patients with metabolic diseases (such as dyslipidemia), malignant tumors, those who were pregnant, those using anti-inflammatory drugs, corticosteroids, alcohol, or those with severe chronic diseases (such as infections, cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events, and liver and kidney insufficiency) were excluded from this study. At the stage preceding the study’s start, the principles of the experiment were explained to all patients, and they all signed the informed consent form.

Cell culture and treatment

hRMECs utilized in our study were procured from Bena (Beijing, China) and cultivated using endothelial cell medium containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS). These cells were cultured in an endothelial cell medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS). The cell growth took place within a constant-temperature incubator maintained at 37℃ with a 5% CO₂ atmosphere. After 48 h of culturing, the confluent cells were harvested. The cells were cultured under three different conditions: in high glucose (HG), where the medium contained 25 mM D-glucose (this group was designated as the HG group) [19] in low glucose (LG), with 5 mM D-glucose in the medium (termed the LG group); and in an osmotic control group, which consisted of 5 mM glucose and 20 mM mannitol in the medium.

Cell transfection

When hRMECs reached a confluence of 70% − 80%, they were harvested. Subsequently, Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) was utilized to transfect the cells with the specified plasmids or sequences for 48 h. The overexpression plasmid pcDNA3.0/SNHG17 (p-SNHG17), along with its negative control pcDNA3.0 (p-NC), and the small interfering RNA targeting SNHG17 (si-SNHG17; 5’-GAUUGUCAGCUGACCUCUGUCCUGU-3’) and its negative control (si-NC; 5’-UUCUCCGAACGUGUCAGGU-3’) were all obtained from GenePharma (Shanghai, China). The mimic or inhibitor sequences of miR-34a-5p (miR-34a-5p mimic: 5’- UGGCAGUGUCUUAGCUGGUUGU − 3’; miR-34a-5p inhibitor: 5’- ACAACCAGCUAAGACACUGCCA ‘) and their NC (mimic-NC: 5’-UUCUCCGAACGUGUCACGUTT-3’ and inhibitor-NC: 5’-CAGUACUUUGUGUAGUACAA-3’) were synthesized and supplied by GeneChem (Shanghai, China).

RT-qPCR

Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol Reagent according to the specified protocols. Subsequently, either the PrimeScript™ RT reagent Kit or the Mir - X™ miRNA First Strand Synthesis Kit was utilized to synthesize cDNAs. Following cDNA synthesis, real-time quantitative PCR (RT - qPCR) was carried out to assess the transcriptional expression levels of genes. For the quantification of target genes, TB Green™ Fast qPCR Mix or Mir - X™ miRNA qRT-PCR SYBR® Kit was applied. The thermocycling conditions were set as follows: initial denaturation at 95 °C for 3 min, followed by 40 cycles of denaturation at 95 °C for 10 s and annealing at 60 °C for 30 s. GAPDH and U6 were selected as internal controls for data normalization [20]. Primer sequences were shown in Supplementary Table 1.

CCK-8 assay

After the specified transfection and treatment, the viability of hRMECs cells was evaluated using the CCK-8 kit from Sigma-Aldrich. Briefly, hRMEC cells were plated in 96-well plates and incubated for 48 h. Subsequently, 10 µL CCK-8 solution was introduced to the cell cultures. Subsequently, the cells were incubated for another 4 h. After that, the absorbance at a wavelength of 450 nm was determined using a microplate reader.

Transwell assay

The migration of cells was gauged through the employment of the Transwell migration assay. Specifically, the upper chamber was seeded with hRMECs at a concentration of 2.5 × 10³ cells per well, where they underwent serum-free cultivation. Concurrently, a medium enriched with 10% serum was introduced into the lower chamber. Following a 24-hour incubation period, the non-migrated cells present in the upper chamber were carefully wiped away using a cotton swab. Post-removal of the non-migrated cells, the migrated cells were fixed with paraformaldehyde. This was immediately followed by a 20-minute staining procedure using crystal violet. Eventually, the number of cells within 5 randomly selected fields was meticulously counted to assess the migratory capacity of the cells.

Flow cytometry assay

Collect cells from each group, wash twice with PBS, then stain according to the Annexin V-FITC/PI apoptosis detection kit instructions. Incubate in the dark and proceed immediately to instrument analysis. Cells were collected using flow cytometry and analyzed via FlowJo software. Annexin V-FITC positive, PI negative cells were classified as early apoptotic cells, while Annexin V-FITC and PI double-positive cells were classified as late apoptotic/necrotic cells. The sum of early and late apoptotic rates was calculated as the total percentage of apoptotic cells.

ELISA assay

Forty-eight hours post-cell transfection, hRMECs from each group were collected and lysed, and stored at -80 °C. To determine the CLDN5 concentration, the human CLDN5 Quantikine ELISA Kit (ml061247; MLbio, Shanghai, China) was utilized following the provided user instructions. The ELx808 Absorbance Microplate Reader (BioTek, Winooski, VT, USA) was employed to detect the optical density (OD) at 450 nm, and this value was taken as an indicator of the relative CLDN5 concentration.

Target gene prediction

The miRNAs that could be sponged by lncRNA SNHG17 were predicted through the use of LncBook 2.0. Regarding miR-34a-5p, its downstream target genes were predicted by employing miRDB (with a Target Score > 80), TargetScan (with a Total context + + score <- 0.30), and the ENCORI database (where the screening criteria were CLIP - DATA > = 2). Subsequently, Venn diagrams were created to visually display the overlapping genes identified in these three databases.

Luciferase reporter assay

The luciferase reporter assay was utilized to verify the prediction of the interaction between miR − 34a − 5p and either SNHG17 or CDK6. Cells were co-transfected with either the miR-34a-5p mimic or inhibitor, combined with the wild - type (WT) or mutant seed region (MUT) of miR − 34a − 5p within the 3’-UTR of SNHG17 or CDK6. Lipofectamine 2000 was used for cell transfection. The relative luciferase activity was measured by means of the Dual-Luciferase Reporter System, following the manufacturer’s instructions precisely. Renilla fluorescence activity was designated as the internal reference.

RIP assay

The RIP assay was carried out using the Imprinted RNA Immunoprecipitation Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific). After the cells were washed twice with PBS, they were lysed on ice for 30 min using RIP lysis buffer containing a protease inhibitor cocktail and RNase inhibitor. Then, the lysates were incubated with protein A magnetic beads conjugated with Ago2 antibody (Thermo Fisher Scientific) or IgG antibody (negative control; Thermo Fisher Scientific) at 4 °C overnight. The next day, the magnetic beads were washed three times with cold NT2 buffer containing the above inhibitors to obtain RNA-protein complexes. The RNA was extracted using TRIzol and subjected to qRT-PCR for detection.

Statistical analysis

All experimental outcomes were analyzed by means of GraphPad Prism 9.0 and SPSS 21.0. The data are shown in the format of mean±standard deviation (SD). To compare the differences, either Student’s t-test or one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with post hoc Tukey’s test was utilized. To evaluate the diagnostic value, the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was generated. Pearson’s correlation analysis was conducted to perform the correlation assessment. A P-value less than 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance.

Dysregulation and clinical value analysis of serum SNHG17 levels in DR patients

According to the RT-qPCR results, downregulation of serum SNHG17 levels was detected in both T2DM and DR patients, and the levels were lowest in DR patients (Fig. 1A). As depicted in Fig. 1B, the levels of SNHG17 in patients with PDR were significantly lower compared to those in patients with NPDR. Subsequently, the ROC curve suggested that serum SNHG17 could distinguish T2DM patients from healthy controls. The AUC was 0.869, with a sensitivity of 75.9% and a specificity of 81.7% (Fig. 1C). For differentiating DR from T2DM using serum SNHG17, the AUC of the ROC curve was 0.861, and the sensitivity and specificity were 85.4% and 72.0%, respectively (Fig. 1D). Furthermore, in the diagnosis of PDR from NPDR, serum SNHG17 demonstrated outstanding diagnostic performance. The AUC was 0.916, with a sensitivity of 95.4% and a specificity of 75.9%, as shown in Fig. 1E.

The role of SNHG17 in the cellular function of hRMECs under HG condition

In hRMECs under HG condition, significantly decreased SNHG17 levels were detected compared to LG group (Fig. 2A). Then, cell transfection was performed to regulate SNHG17 levels in the cells. It was determined that p-SNHG17 promoted the expression of SNHG17 (Fig. 2A). CCK-8 assay determined that HG restrained the cell viability significantly, but it was restored by SNHG17 (Fig. 2B). Moreover, in HG-treated hRMECs, the induced cell apoptosis was inhibited by SNHG17 overexpression (Fig. 2C). Similarly, a comparable regulatory effect of SNHG17 was also found on cell migration. As presented in Fig. 2D, high expression of SNHG17 can eliminate the inhibitory effect of HG on cell migration and restore the cell migration ability to a normal level. Similarly, the inhibitory effect of HG on CLDN5 expression was reversed, and the expression level of CLDN5 rebounded accordingly (Fig. 2E).

SNHG17 acts as a sponge for miR-34a-5p

Figure 3A shows the target binding sequences between SNHG17 and miR-34a-5p. Then their target relationship was verified by a luciferase reporter assay. As presented in Fig. 3B, upregulation of miR-34a-5p suppressed the luciferase activity of cells that were transfected with WT-SNHG17, but downregulation of miR-34a-5p promoted the luciferase activity of cells. However, the luciferase can not be influenced by miR-34a-5p in cells transfected with MUT-SNHG17. RIP assay detection revealed that compared with the IgG group, the enrichment levels of lncRNA SNHG17 and miR-34a-5p in the Ago2 antibody group were significantly increased (Fig. 3C). This result indicates that both can bind to the Ago2 protein, suggesting a possible targeted interaction relationship. Moreover, the expression profile of miR-34a-5p in the serum of clinical patients was further examined. As presented in Fig. 3D, miR-34a-5p was at high expression in the serum of T2DM and DR patients, with the highest expression in DR cases. Moreover, Pearson’s correlation analysis determined the negative association between serum miR-34a-5p and SNHG17 levels in all participants (r = -0.568, Fig. 3E). In hRMECs, upregulated miR-34a-5p was also detected under the HG condition, but the expression change was reversed by SNHG17 overexpression and aggravated by SNHG17 knockdown (Fig. 3F).

Overexpression of miR-34a-5p reversed the role of SNHG17 in hRMECs

To examine the involvement of miR-34a-5p in the role of SNHG17 in DR, the rescue experiments were performed in hRMECs under HG conditions. Figure 4A indicates the transfection regulation effectiveness of miR-34a-5p mimic in hRMECs. The CCK-8 assay proved that the cell viability that had been restored by the overexpression of SNHG17 was inhibited again after the upregulation of miR-34a-5p in HG-treated hRMECs (Fig. 4B), and cell apoptosis was also reactivated (Fig. 4C). Similarly, the level of cell migration and CLDN5 concentration that had been enhanced by SNHG17 was also suppressed due to the upregulation of miR-34a-5p (Fig. 4D-E). All the research findings indicated that miR-34a-5p can effectively reverse the protective effects exerted by the overexpression of SNHG17 on the proliferation and migration abilities of hRMECs.

CDK6 serves as a target of miR-34a-5p

Based on the prediction results of three online databases, a total of 75 overlapping target genes of miR-34a-5p were determined (Fig. 5A). Subsequently, a protein-protein interaction (PPI) analysis was carried out on these 75 overlapping target genes, and the results are presented in Fig. 5B. Table 2 shows the top ten overlapping target genes ranked by node degree, in which CDK6 has the highest node degree. The targeted sequences of miR-34a-5p and CDK6 are specifically presented in Fig. 5C. Finally, by means of the luciferase reporter assay (Fig. 5D), CDK6 was confirmed as the downstream target of miR-34a-5p. Moreover, RIP assay also verified the coexistence of miR-34a-5p and SNHG17 in RISC (Fig. 5E). We further compared the levels of CDK6 mRNA in the serum of different clinical patients. The results revealed that, in contrast to the control group, there was a significant decline in the levels of CDK6 among patients suffering from DR and T2DM, and the lowest level was found in the DR group (Fig. 5F). The results of the correlation analysis indicated that in all the subjects, there was a significant negative correlation between the serum levels of CDK6 and miR-34a-5p (Fig. 5G). Additionally, significantly decreased levels of CDK6 were also detected in hRMECs induced by HG (Fig. 5H). After up-regulating SNHG17, the level of CDK6 increased significantly, but this change was reversed by miR-34a-5p (Fig. 5H).

Discussion

The present analysis of clinical samples showed that SNHG17 was significantly down-regulated in patient serum and was associated with the progression of DR. Serum SNHG17 levels were lower in PDR patients. This suggests that the altered expression of SNHG17 was closely related to the occurrence and development of DR, and may serve as a potential biomarker of disease progression.

The HG environment is a key factor in the development of DR [21]. hRMECs were used to simulate the pathological state of DR in vitro, and it was found that overexpression of SNHG17 significantly restored HG-inhibited proliferation and migration of hRMECs cells. Claudin-5 (CLDN5) is one of the most highly enriched tight junction proteins in hRMECs [22, 23]. Its normal expression maintains ionic balance and neural homeostasis within the retina [22]. High glucose levels induce a significant downregulation of CLDN5 expression, thereby increasing intercellular permeability and resulting in barrier dysfunction [24, 25]. In the current cellular experiments, a significant down-regulation of CLDN5 was observed in HG-treated hRMECs, indicating early vascular injury, which is one of the crucial pathological changes in the early stage of DR. Moreover, our rescue experiments demonstrated that SNHG17 reversed the HG-induced downregulation of CLDN5 levels. Therefore, it is likely that SNHG17 alleviates the advancement of DR via regulating the proliferation and migration processes of hRMECs.

It was previously found that miR-34a-5p serves as a direct target of SNHG17. SNHG17 is capable of negatively regulating the expression of miR-34a-5p under diverse conditions [17, 18]. Moreover, dysregulated miR-34a-5p has been widely reported in DR. For example, miR-34a-5p mediates HG-induced endothelial-mesenchymal transition and angiogenesis by targeting XBP1, suggesting its role as a key regulator of retinal microvascular injury [26]. Moreover, as a key target of MEG3, miR-34a-5p can play a crucial role in the inflammatory response and apoptosis associated with DR by downregulating SIRT1 and activating the NF-κB signaling pathway [27]. Relying on the current outcomes, an increase in the level of miR-34a-5p was found in the serum of T2DM and DR cases. And it was negatively correlated with serum SNHG17 levels in all participants. Moreover, the target relationship between SNHG17 and miR-34a-5p was determined via luciferase reporter assay and RIP assay. Therefore, the involvement of miR-34a-5p in the role of SNHG17 in DR was further explored. The cell rescue experiments indicated that miR-34a-5p overexpression reversed the role of SNHG17 in hRMECs proliferation and migration. Consistently, in DR rat models, upregulated miR-34a-5p has also been detected, and miR-34a silencing is found to ameliorate DR manifestations, which is achieved through the regulation of apoptosis and the modulation of VEGF expression [28]. In addition, enhanced miR-34a-5p is also reported to take part in the function of MEG3 in DR, miR-34a-5p knockdown can inhibit HG-induced secretion of inflammatory cytokines of ARPE-19 cells [27]. Collectively, the SNHG17/miR-34a-5p axis was determined to be an important mediator in DR through regulating hRMECs proliferation and migration.

It is known that miRNA recognizes target genes by base complementary pairing with the 3’ untranslated region (3’UTR) of the target genes, and regulates the post-transcriptional level of the target genes, thus participating in the progression of diseases. In the current study, the downstream target genes of miR-34a-5p were predicted via three online databases, and CDK6 gene was identified to be a candidate target gene of miR-34a-5p via luciferase reporter assay. Cyclin-dependent kinase 6 (CDK6) is a key molecule in the cell cycle regulatory network. It mainly regulates cell proliferation by forming functional complexes with cyclin D. Previous research results have shown that the upregulation of mR-34a-5p can effectively inhibit the proliferation, migration and adhesion behavior of ARPE-19 cells, and this inhibitory effect depends on the downregulation of CDK6 protein expression [29, 30]. Another study focusing on N6-methyladenosine (m6A) modification further indicates that the overexpression of YTHDC1 (YTH domain-containing protein 1) can lead to the functional impairment of retinal vascular endothelial cells (RVECs) by downregulating the expression level of CDK6 protein [31]. Based on the above findings, we infer that CDK6 may be the target of the SNHG17/miR-34a-5p axis in the context of DR. However, this hypothesis needs to be verified in future studies.

This study has only validated the functional role of SNHG17/miR-34a-5p axis in DR at the in vivo level, without further exploring the downstream molecular pathways and specific regulatory mechanisms through which it exerts its biological effects. There remains a lack of in-depth elucidation regarding the interaction patterns within the signal networks it mediates and its key target points. Future work may integrate high-throughput omics screening, molecular interaction assays, and in vivo experiments to systematically elucidate the downstream regulatory pathways of SNHG17/miR-34a-5p axis. This would further refine the molecular mechanisms underpinning its role in the onset and progression of DR, thereby providing more comprehensive theoretical foundations for subsequent targeted interventions.

In conclusion, the alteration of SNHG17 expression was closely related to the occurrence and development of DR, indicating that SNHG17 may serve as an important indicator for monitoring the progression of DR. Overexpression of SNHG17 can reverse the inhibitory effect of the HG environment on the proliferation and migration of hRMECs by inhibiting miR-34a-5p, and CDK6 may be the target of the SNHG17/miR-34a-5p axis. The results of our study provide new targets and ideas for the early diagnosis, disease monitoring, and therapeutic intervention of DR.

Gibberellic acid and spermine induce mercury tolerance in tomato seedlings by modulating osmolyte, redox homeostasis and detoxification system
Authors: Cheng Qin, Ziyu Zhang, Wenjie Xi, Caiting Shi, Haiying Lei, Zhan He, Naheeda Begum, Huida Lian
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundMetal pollution in agricultural soils is imparting devastating influence on the growth and yield of crop plants. Therefore, role of gibberellic acid (GA3; 100 mg L− 1) priming and the foliar spray of spermine (Spm; 200 µM) in mitigating the effects of mercury (Hg; 20 µM) stress on growth, photosynthesis, nitrogen metabolism and nutrient uptake were studied in tomato.ResultsMercury (Hg) significantly reduced plant height, fresh and dry weight, chlorophylls, carotenoids and photosynthetic parameters significantly however, GA3 and Spm treatments alleviated the decline considerably. Oxidative damage parameters were reduced notably by GA3 and Spm with their combined treatment imparting higher decline. The decline in nitrogen metabolism enzymes was assuaged by GA3 and Spm. Content of compatible osmolytes further increased due to GA3 and Spm treatments. Treatments of GA3 and Spm increased the activities of γ-glutamyl kinase and proline oxidase causing increased proline buildup. Moreover, the activities of antioxidant and glyoxylase cycle enzymes, and the content of ascorbic acid, reduced glutathione and cysteine increased significantly due to the GA3 priming and Spm supplementation. Total phenols and the total antioxidant activity interms of DPPH and ABTS radical scavenging increased due to GA3 and Spm treatments. Exogenous GA3 and Spm reduced the accumulation of Hg and alleviated the decline in beneficial nutrients.ConclusionThe oxidative effects of Hg stress were alleviated by GA and Spm treatments, more apparently due to their combined treatment. Results reflect the interactive effect of GA and Spm on the important tolerance mechanisms.

Introduction

Metal pollution is becoming a serious threat for sustainable crop productivity all over the globe. Mercury (Hg) is one of the most toxic heavy metals which imparts damaging effects on flora and fauna, and influences their performance badly [1]. Due to excessive disposal of industrial effluents heavy metal concentrations are increasing considerably which results in significant loss of agricultural land [2, 3]. This rapidly increasing heavy metal contamination results in the widespread contamination of agricultural land, lakes, underground water etc. Mercury (Hg) is naturally occurring metal which exists in diverse forms like Hg(0), Hg(II) or Hg2+, HgS and methyl-Hg however, Hg2+ is the predominantly occurring form in soil and water. Hg reduces germination, growth, photosynthesis, enzyme functioning and mineral uptake of plants therefore leading to considerable loss of yield [3]. It has been reported that Hg effected the growth of Vigna radiata by increasing lipid peroxidation (MDA) and reducing the nitrogen metabolism related attributes like leghaemoglobin content and nodule formation [4]. Like other stresses, increased production of toxic reactive oxygen species (ROS) due to Hg is one of the reasons of growth impairments in plants [5]. Greater ROS accumulation damages lipids, proteins, membranes, enzyme functioning hence affects the overall plant growth ultimately leading to significant decline of the yield. To lessen the harmful effects of stress triggered oxidative effects plants up-regulate the tolerance mechanisms which include antioxidant system, glyoxalase cycle and metabolite buildup [6]. Significant reports have shown that increment in the functioning of these innate mechanisms can lead to significant alleviation of the adverse effects of stresses including metals [7–9].

Polyamines (PA) are the low molecular weight molecules that are positively charged at physiological pH. PAs have important role in several developmental events of plants from germination to productivity and stress tolerance [10]. It has been reported that PAs regulate germination, photosynthesis, senescence, stress signalling, flowering, embryo development and tolerance to stresses [11]. Hassan et al. [12] has reported that supplementation of PAs like spermine (Spm) and putrescine, alleviates the unpleasant effects of salinity on growth and photosynthesis by significantly improving the antioxidant functioning and restricting the uptake of sodium. PAs regulate stress responsive genes, modulate tolerance mechanisms and the ion channel functioning, improve metabolite synthesis, improve membrane and DNA stability, interact with plant hormones and other signalling molecules [13]. External application of PAs (spermine, putrescine and spermidine), either foliar or soaking, reduces the adverse effects of cadmium in wheat by up-regulating the antioxidant functioning, osmoprotectants and reducing the cadmium uptake [14].

Gibberellic acid (GA3), is one of the important plant hormones regulating growth and development by actively affecting germination, shoot and root growth, flowering, grain development and stress tolerance. GA3 improves cellular division and elongation, germination, physiological and biochemical parameters and improves tolerance against stresses [15]. Priming of rapeseed with GA3 alleviated the drought induced decline in morphological and yield parameters by improving chlorophyll and photosynthesis while as reducing the oxidative injury by strengthening the antioxidant system [16]. Moreover, the GA3 triggered alleviation of growth decline in Daucus carota L. under lead stress [17]. Gibberellic acid protects plants from the oxidative effects of stresses by regulating the mechanisms that lead to quick removal of toxic radicals [18]. For example, GA3 alleviated the toxic impact of cerium in wheat through increased antioxidant functioning [19].

Tomato (Solanum lycopersicum L.) is a globally significant crop, widely cultivated and consumed as a vegetable due to its nutritional value and economic importance [20]. It is a rich source of essential vitamins, minerals and health-promoting metabolites including lycopene, β-carotene, and various antioxidants which contribute to its beneficial effects on human health. However, the increasing presence of heavy metal contamination in agricultural soils poses a significant threat to tomato production and food safety. Elevated levels of Hg in the soil can negatively impact tomato growth and productivity, leading to reduced yields and compromised fruit quality [21]. Furthermore, the uptake and accumulation of Hg in tomato plants can introduce this toxic element into the food chain, posing potential risks to human health including neurotoxicity and nephrotoxicity.

Present study proposed that the combined application of GA3 and Spm would enhance tolerance to Hg stress in tomato by: (1) alleviating the decline in plant growth parameters, photosynthetic pigments and photosynthetic efficiency; (2) reducing oxidative damage through the modulation of antioxidant enzyme activities and the accumulation of non-enzymatic antioxidants; (3) protecting nitrogen metabolism by mitigating the decline in related enzyme activities, promoting the accumulation of compatible osmolytes and enhancing the activity of the glyoxalase cycle and (4) reducing the Hg accumulation in plant tissues and restoring the uptake of essential nutrients.

Materials and methods

Solanum lycopersicum L. (cultivar Dongfeng-199) was purchased from Shouguang city Shengbang Seed Industry Co., LTD) and seeds were disinfected using sodium hypochlorite (5%, NaOCl). Thereafter the sterilized seeds were washed thoroughly and blot dried followed by soaking either in distilled water or gibberellic acid (100 mg L− 1 GA3; Sigma Aldrich) in dark for 10 h. The primed seeds were blot dried and sown in earthen pots containing soil, sand and vermicompost (3:1:1). At the time of sowing all pots were irrigated with 250 mL of full strength Hoagland solution. One week after germination, three healthy growing seedlings were maintained in every pot. Ten days old plants were subjected to Hg (20 µM HgCl2) stress and Hg was given in the form of modified Hoagland solution (200 mL) while as control plants received normal Hoagland solution (200 mL). From the same day foliar application of spermine (200 µM Spm; Sigma Aldrich, spermine tetrahydrochloride) was also started and 10 mL Spm solution was sprayed per pot. Exogenous Spm was applied to both GA3-primed and Hg-stressed plants. Hence overall experimental treatments can be summarized as: (a) control (primed in distilled water), (b) 20 µM Hg, (c) Hg + GA3, (d) Hg + Spm and (e) Hg + GA3 + Spm. Treatments of Hg and Spm continued for four weeks and were given twice a week. For every treatment, four pots were kept in green house maintained at 28/20 oC,16/8 light/dark cycle and relative humidity of 75–90%, and pots were arranged in a complete randomized block design. Plants were uprooted carefully and washed with distilled water to remove the adhered soil, and were separated into leaf, stem and root.

Measurement of height, fresh and dry weight

At the end of the experiment, the whole tomato seedlings were harvested, and washed with distilled water. Plant height was assayed by measuring the length from the bottom of the main stem to the end of the first leaf. The sampled seedlings were separated into aerial parts (leaves and stems) and roots, then heated at 105 °C for 30 min and dried at 80 °C for 72 h to constant weight. The samples were weighed to record the dry weight.

Photosynthetic pigments and gas exchange parameters

Approximately 0.1 g of leaf samples using 15 mL of 80% acetone on a shaker at room temperature to extract overnight. Supernatant after centrifuged at 3000 g for 20 min was absorbance at 480, 645 and 663 nm [22]. Portable photosynthetic apparatus Li-6400 (LI-COR Inc., USA) was used for measuring the gas exchange parameters during the period of 9:00–12:00 h. The instrument was set at a light intensity of 1,000 µmol (photon) m− 2 s− 1 and airflow rate of 500 µmol s− 1. The cuvette CO2 was adjusted at 400 µmol (CO2) mol− 1 (air).

Estimation of osmolytes, relative water content, γ-glutamyl kinase and PROX activity

Proline was extracted in 3% sulphosalicylic acid and the homogenate was centrifuged at 3000 g for 20 min. Then 2 mL supernatant was mixed with the glacial acetic acid and ninhydrin reagent. After incubating at 100 oC for 1 h, the samples were cooled on ice bath. Proline content was separated using toluene and the optical density was taken 520 nm [23].

Glycine betaine (GB) was estimated following the method of Grieve and Grattan [24]. Dry samples were extracted in distilled water and after filtering the extract, 2 N H2SO4. Thereafter, an appropriate aliquot of the diluted extract was mixed with cold KI-I2 reagent and the resultant was centrifuged at 10,000 g for 15 min. Periodide crystals formed were dissolved in 1, 2-dichloroethane and the optical density was taken at 365 nm.

The relative water content was determined by excising leaf discs using cork borer and their fresh weight (FW) was determined. The same discs were floated on distilled water in petri dishes for an hour to gain turgidity. Thereafter, the discs were removed and weighed to get turgid weight (TW). Thereafter, the discs were dried at 60 °C for 24 h to measure the dry weight (DW). Hayzer and Leisinger’s [25] method was used for measuring γ-glutamyl kinase (γ-GK, EC 2.7.2.11) activity while as method of Huang and Cavalieri [26] was used for determination of PROX (PROX, EC 1.4.3.1) activity. Fresh leaf (500 mg) tissue was extracted in cold 100 mM Tris-HCl buffer (pH 7.5) using prechilled pestle and mortar. The extract was centrifuged at 30, 000 g for 30 min. Assay mixture for γ-GK contained 50 mM Tris buffer (pH 7.0), ATP, MgCl2, L-glutamate, hydroxamate–HCl and enzyme. Mixture was incubated at 37 oC for 30 min and the reaction was terminated by adding FeCl3 and TCA (prepare in HCl). Absorbance was taken at 535 nm. However, for PROX, the assay mixture contained 50 mM Tris buffer (pH 8.5), 0.5 mM NADP, 5 mM MgCl2, phenazine methanosulphate, dichlorophenol indophenol, 1 mM KCN and 0.1 M proline. Reaction was initiated by proline and the optical density was taken at 600 nm for 3 min.

Determination of lipid peroxidation, hydrogen peroxide and superoxide

Method of Heath and Packer [27] was employed for determining the lipid peroxidation. Briefly, fresh 100 mg leaf was extracted in 1% trichloro acetic acid (TCA) and the extract was centrifuged at 10,000 g. Thereafter, 1.0 mL supernatant was reacted with 4 mL thiobarbituric acid at 95 oC for 30 min and samples were cooled on ice bath. Centrifugation was done at 5000 g for 5 min and the absorbance of the clear supernatant was measured at 532 and 600 nm. Hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) was extracted by homogenizing the fresh tissue in 0.1% TCA. Centrifugation of the homogenate was done at 12,000 g for ten minutes and 0.5 mL of supernatant was mixed with potassium phosphate buffer (pH 7.0) and potassium iodide. Absorbance was taken at 390 nm and standard curve of H2O2 was used for calculation [28]. Concentration of superoxide (O2•−) were measured by adopting method described by Yang et al. [29].

Measurement of nitrate reductase, glutamine synthetase and NADH-glutamate synthase activities

For measuring the activities of nitrate reductase (NR, EC 1.6.6.1), glutamine synthetase (GS, EC 6.3.1.2) and NADH-glutamate synthase (NADH-GOGAT, EC 1.4.1.14) methods described by Srivastava [30], Agbaria et al. [31] and Lea et al. [32] were followed respectively. For nitrate reductase, fresh tissue was cut into pieces and incubated in phosphate buffer (pH 7.5) having potassium nitrate and 5% isopropanol for three hours in dark. Then, the aliquot was mixed with sulphanilamide and 1-naphthyl ethylene diamine dihydrochloride. After 20 min, the absorbance was taken at 540 nm. The NADH-glutamate synthase was extracted by macerating fresh leaf tissue in 100 mM Tris-HCl buffer (pH 8.0) containing 1% PVP, 1 mM of each cysteine, magnesium chloride and EDTA using. Homogenate was centrifuged and supernatant was mixed with 100 mM potassium phosphate buffer (pH 7.5), 0.1 mM NADH, 10 mM of glutamine and 2-oxoglutarate. Addition of enzyme (200 µL) initiated the reaction and change in absorbance was monitored at 340 nm.

Assay of antioxidant enzyme activities

The extraction of fresh 1.0 g of leaf was done using prechilled pestle and mortar in a chilled phosphate buffer (pH 7.8) containing PVP, EDTA and PMSF. After centrifuging homogenate at 12,000g for 15 min, supernatant was used as enzyme source for assaying the enzyme activity. Activity of superoxide dismutase (SOD, EC 1.15.1.1) was determined following method of Bayer and Fridovich [33] while as catalase (CAT, EC 1.11.1.6) activity was measured according to Aebi [34]. For measuring the activity of glutathione S-transferase (GST), method described by Hasanuzzaman and Fujita [35] was adopted. Method described by Nakano and Asada [36] was used for assaying the activity of ascorbate peroxidase (APX, EC 1.11.1.11) and activity of glutathione reductase was assayed according to the method of Foyer and Halliwell [37]. Guaiacol peroxidase activity (GPX) was determined following Chance and Maehly [38].

Ascorbate, reduced glutathione and cysteine content

The ascorbate was measured following Mukherjee and Choudhuri [39]. Briefly, plant tissue was homogenised in 6% trichloroacetic avid and the extract was subjected to centrifugation for 10 min. Supernatant was mixed with thiourea and dinitrophenylhydrazine, and were incubated in water bath for 15 min. Thereafter samples were cooled and sulphuric acid (80%) was added. Optical density was read at 530 nm and for calculation standard curve of ascorbic acid was used for estimation of reduced glutathione (GSH) was measured method described by Ellman [40] was adapted and plant tissue was extracted in phosphate buffer. After centrifugation at 10,000 g for 10 min, supernatant was added to 5, 5-dithiobis-2-nitrobenzoic acid and samples were allowed to stand for 10 min. Optical density was taken at 412 nm and the standard curve of reduced glutathione was for calculation. Cysteine was extracted in cold perchloric acid and estimated using ninhydrin reagent [41] and calculations were done using standard curve of cysteine.

Total phenols and total antioxidant activity

Total phenols were extracted by homogenizing plant material in ethanol and homogenate was centrifuged at 10,000 g for 10 min. Supernatant was evaporated and volume was made to five mL using distilled water. Thereafter, Folin-ciocalteau’s reagent was added and followed by addition of sodium carbonate. After incubating the mixture in boiling water bath for one minute, samples were cooled and absorbance was taken at 650 nm [42]. The total antioxidant activity was determined as DPPH (1, 1-diphenyl-2-picrylhydrazyl) and ABTS radical scavenging potential in according to Shimada et al. [43] and Re et al. [44] respectively. Briefly, for DPPH scavenging assay the methanolic extract was incubated with 100 µM DPPH for 20 min and the optical density was read at 515 nm. For assaying the ABTS radical scavenging potential, the methanolic extract was mixed with 7 mM ABTS and 2.4 mM potassium persulfate. After incubating in dark for 10 min, the optical density was taken at 734 nm.

Activity of glyoxalase I and glyoxalase II

Fresh leaf tissue was homogenised in cold 50 mM potassium phosphate buffer (pH 7.0) supplemented with 10 mM KCl, 1 mM ascorbate, 1 mM β-mercaptoethanol and 10% glycerol. After centrifuging the homogenate at 11,500 g for 15 min, the supernatant was used as enzyme source for measuring the glyoxylase I (EC: 4.4.1.5) and glyoxalase II (EC: 3.1.2.6) activities. The method described by Hasanuzzaman et al. [45] was employed for estimating the activity of Gly I and the assay mixture contained 100 mM phosphate buffer (pH 7.0), 15 mM MgSO4, 1.7 mM GSH, enzyme and 35 mM methylglyoxal. Change in the optical density of assay mixture was recorded at 240 nm. Activity of Gly II was assayed according to Principato et al. [46] and the assay mixture contained 100 mM Tris–HCl buffer (pH 7.2), 0.2 mM DTNB and 1 mM S-D-lactoylglutathione (SLG). The optical density was recorded at 412 nm.

Determination of mineral nutrients and the mercury content

Micro-Kzeldahl method described by Jackson [47] was used for estimation of nitrogen (N) while as phosphorous (P), sulphur (S), potassium (K) and Hg were measured using atomic absorption spectrophotometer.

Statistical analysis

Values of three (± SE) replicates have been given and the least significant difference (LSD) among the mean values was determined at P < 0.05 by Duncan’s Multiple Range Test. Graphs were plotted using Origin 2021 (GraphPad Software Inc., La Jolla, CA, USA). Pearson’s correlation coefficient was calculated in Origin 2021 software to assess the correlation between pairs of attributes. The partial least squares path modeling (PLS-PM) was done using the ‘plspm’ package in R (v.4.2.3, R Core Team) to illuminate the relationships between plant growth, gas exchange parameters, Hg content, ROS accumulation and plant nutrient levels.

Results

Plant growth parameters

As an important factor in improving plant growth, the plant height, plant fresh and dry weight of tomato was measured at the seedling stage under Hg, GA3 priming and Spm application treatment conditions (Fig. 1). Treatment of Hg resulted in reduction of 53.65%, 56.38% and 65.55% in height, fresh weight and dry weight respectively over the control. Priming of seeds with GA3 and the application of Spm effectively mitigated the Hg induced decline in these parameters. Relative to control, plants stressed with Hg and primed with GA3 exhibited a decline of 42.17%, 39.36% and 52.18% in height, plant fresh weight and plant dry weight respectively. However, in plants treated with Hg and foliar application of Spm the height, plant fresh weight and plant dry weight decreased by 31.30%, 46.40% and 43.44% respectively. In Hg stressed plants raised after priming with GA3 and receiving foliar Spm (Hg + GA + Spm), the increase in height, plant fresh weight and plant dry weight was 1.58, 2.1 and 2.4 folds respectively over the Hg stressed counterparts (Fig. 1).

Hg accumulation in roots and shoots

Hg was more in root as compared to shoot. Relative to Hg stressed plants, GA3 priming and Spm application reduced the Hg accumulation significantly. In Hg + GA3 treated plants, a reduction of 18.18% and 27.04% in root and shoot Hg was observed while as Hg + Spm treated plants showed a reduction of 31.81% and 47.79% in root and shoot over the Hg stressed plants. Highest decline of 50.87% and 72.35% for root and shoot Hg was observed in Hg + GA3 + Spm treated plants over the Hg stressed counterparts (Fig. 2A and B). Hg stress reduced nitrogen by 60.33%, phosphorous by 62.50%, sulphur by 56.32% and potassium by 56.79% over the control. Priming with GA3 and foliar application of Spm alleviated this decrease to substantial levels. In comparison to control, a decline of 11.00%, 12.35%, 3.84% and 10.65% was observed in plants treated with Hg + GA3 + Spm (Fig. 2C-F).

Photosynthetic pigments and gas exchange

Contrary to the control, the reduction in content of chlorophyll a was 53.59%, chlorophyll b was 48.17%, total chlorophyll was 55.02% and carotenoids was 52.31% in Hg treated plants over the control. Priming with GA3 and foliar spray of Spm mitigated the decline to considerable levels. Individual treatment of GA3 and Spm maximally alleviated the decline in the chlorophyll and carotenoids content, and showed significantly higher alleviation in plants that were treated combinedly. Relative to Hg stressed plants, chl a increased by 87.30%, chl b increased by 73.40%, total chlorophyll by 87.25% and carotenoids by 75.49% in plants treated with Hg + GA3 + Spm (Table 1).

Treatment of Hg declined the net photosynthesis rate (PN) (52.63%), intercellular CO2 concentration (Ci) (42.90%), stomatal conductance (gs) (49.07%) and transpiration rate (Tr) (45.41%) contrary to the non-stressed control. Seedlings grown after GA3 priming and/or foliar spray of Spm exhibited an increase in these parameters over the Hg stressed plants with much apparent improvement seen in plants treated with both GA3 and Spm. Individually, GA3 increased PN, Ci, gs and Tr by 28.10%, 18.90%, 27.22% and 19.17% respectively over the Hg stressed plants, while as an enhancement of 48.07%, 32.75%, 44.66% and 29.56% respectively was observed in Hg + Spm treated plants. Plants treated with combined application of GA3 and Spm showed greater increase in the gas exchange parameters over the Hg stressed plants, with percent increase of 85.30%, 61.63%, 81.78% and 60.30% for PN, Ci, gs and Tr in Hg + GA3 + Spm over the Hg stressed plants (Table 1).

Oxidative damage

Plants stressed with Hg exhibited significantly higher H2O2 (147.95%), O2•− (146.39%) and MDA (214.35%) over the control. Priming with GA3 declined buildup of H2O2, O2•− and MDA, and similar effects were observed due to Spm application. Reduction in H2O2, O2•− and MDA was 20.20%, 18.06% and 24.73% respectively due to GA3, and 31.39%, 28.65% and 38.90% due to Spm over the Hg stressed plants. Combined treatment of GA3 and Spm reduced these parameters more evidently compared to Hg stressed plants. Compared to Hg treated counterparts, a decline of 49.67% in H2O2, 52.54% in O2•− and 57.62% in MDA was observed in Hg + GA3 + Spm treated plants (Fig. 3A-C).

Osmolyte accumulation substance

Content of proline and GB increased by 29.27% and 16.04% due to Hg stress over the control plants. GA3 priming and Spm application augmented proline and GB content. Relative to control, individual treatment of GA3 caused an increase of 75.70% in proline and 50.61% in GB while as Spm application caused 05.45% and 77.16% increase respectively. It was observed that plants treated with Hg + GA3 + Spm showed a maximal increase of 181.11% in proline and 132.09% in GB over the control (Fig. 4A-B). Activity of GK increased (42.76%) and PROX decreased (32.24%) due to Hg stress. PLS-PM reveals the interconnected mechanisms of hg toxicity and mitigation

The results of the partial least squares path modeling (PLS-PM) provide significant insights into the relationships among GA3 + Spm, plant growth parameters, photosynthetic parameters, ROS accumulation and glyoxylase cycle in the context of Hg, N, P and K content in plants (Fig. 10). GA3 + Spm exhibited a strong positive influence on plant growth parameters (0.74*) and negatively impacted Hg content in plants (-0.55*). This suggests that GA3 + Spm promote better growth while concurrently reducing Hg accumulation in the plants. GA3 + Spm treatment exhibited a positive association with photosynthetic (0.63*) and N, P, and K content (0.51*). This indicates that GA3 + Spm is essential for enhancing photosynthesis and nutrient uptake. The relationship between photosynthetic parameters and ROS accumulation was negative (-0.21*), suggesting that increased oxidative stress may impair photosynthesis. Furthermore, ROS accumulation had a significant negative effect on both N, P, and K content (-0.29) and photosynthesis (-0.21*), indicating that oxidative stress adversely affects nutrient uptake and plant functionality. Glyoxylase cycle plays a critical role in mitigating the adverse effects of superoxide, as indicated by its positive coefficients with both N, P, and K content (0.63*), and photosynthesis (0.34*). This highlights the potential of enzyme in enhancing nutrient uptake and photosynthetic efficiency under oxidative conditions. Hg content in plants was directly influenced by multiple factors: it was negatively affected by both N, P, and K content (-0.47*) and plant growth (-0.48*). Furthermore, GA3 + Spm is positively associated with glyoxylase cycle (0.45*). These findings imply that effective management of oxidative stress through glyoxylase cycle can mitigate Hg toxicity and enhance plant resilience. The PLS-PM model illustrates complex interrelationships among various biological parameters affecting plant health and nutrient absorption in the presence of environmental stressors like Hg. The goodness of fit for the model was 0.61, indicating an acceptable level of explanatory power for the observed data. Overall, the findings emphasize the importance of promoting plant growth and photosynthetic efficiency to enhance nutrient accumulation while mitigating the detrimental effects of heavy metals through enzymatic activity.

Discussion

Hg pollution is increasing at alarming rate and the introduction of efficient management techniques to prevent the damaging influence of metal pollutants on growth and yield of plants can be handy to prevent its toxic effects. The usage of phytohormones has been observed to impart significant improvement in the key tolerance mechanisms of plants reflecting in alleviation of the adverse effects. In present study, the impact of GA3 priming and the foliar supplementation of Spm was evaluated and the alleviation of adverse effects of Hg stress was observed due to their treatments. Treatment of GA3 and Spm mitigated the decline in plant height, fresh and dry plant weight individually as well as combinedly. Earlier, treatment of Hg has been reported to hamper growth of Vigna radiata [4] and wheat [6]. Priming with GA3 and the foliar supplementation of Spm resulted in alleviation of the decline in these growth attributes. Phytohormone treatment alleviates the damaging impact of Hg stress in plants to considerable extent [48]. The exogenous supplementation of abscisic acid to wheat seedlings alleviated the decline in growth under Hg stress by improving the number of roots, fresh and dry weight [49]. Hg triggers significant decline in key processes like nutrient uptake, photosynthesis, water uptake, enzyme functioning and cellular ultrastructure thereby affecting the overall growth [50]. Treatment of Hg significantly reduced the uptake of essential mineral elements including N, P, K and obstructing the major cellular processes however, both GA3 and Spm treatments alleviated the decline thereby safeguarding the growth by protecting the major processes regulated by these mineral ions. Optimal nutrient uptake regulates growth by improving enzyme functioning, photosynthesis, tolerance mechanisms and restricting the uptake of toxic ions [49]. Heavy metals interfere with the functioning of mineral ion transporters restricting their uptake significantly and GA3 and Spm treatment may have regulated the functioning of transport proteins to reduce the uptake of Hg [51]. GA3 and Spm reduced the uptake of Hg considerably which can be either due to its declined uptake or efficient chelation within the tissue as has been observed by Kim et al. [52] due to exogenous supplementation of glutathione.

Hg stress caused a significant decline in the photosynthetic pigments, including chlorophylls and carotenoids however, GA3 and Spm treatments mitigated the reduction to considerable levels. In Vigna radiata, decline in chlorophyll a, chlorophyll b and total chlorophylls due to Hg stress has been reported [4]. Reduced pigment synthesis due to stress results due to increased chlorophyll degradation and reduced uptake of mineral ions including nitrogen and magnesium [53]. Stresses impede the functioning of chlorophyll biosynthesis pathway hence result in reduced pigment synthesis and photosynthesis [54]. Treatment of GA3 alleviated the decline in chlorophyll and carotenoids in cadmium stressed chickpea [55] and copper stressed Corchorus Capsularis L [56]. The impact of individual or combined GA3 and Spm in alleviating the adverse effects of Hg has not been worked. Treatment of spermidine alleviated the adverse effects of cadmium toxicity on chlorophylls, carotenoids and the photosynthetic parameters Vigna angularis respectively [57]. In copper stressed Spinacia oleracea, treatment of GA3 has been reported to alleviate the decline in photosynthesis [58]. Priming with GA3 and foliar application of Spm mitigated the damaging effects of Hg stress on stomatal parameters of photosynthesis including Pn, Ci, E and gs. Improved photosynthesis in GA3 and Spm treated plants can be ascribed to reduced accumulation of radicals and increased functioning of tolerance mechanisms. This also reflects that GA3 and Spm treatments may have increased the activity of Rubisco in Hg stressed plants. Stresses reduce Rubisco functioning by triggering the down-regulation of its activation state by de-carbamylation, binding of inhibitory sugar phosphates or change in its concentration [59].

Hg stress triggered increased accumulation of ROS including H2O2 and superoxide resulting in enhancement in the MDA. Earlier, Iskil et al. [60] have also reported significant increase in radical accumulation and MDA in Triticum aestivum L. Increased generation of radicals trigger enhanced peroxidation of membrane lipids and proteins resulting in significant leakage of ions [4]. Treatment of GA3 and Spm resulted in significant decline in the accumulation of free radical and the MDA content thereby reflecting in their beneficial role in protecting the plants against the ill effects of Hg stress. Treatment of GA3 to cadmium stressed chickpea [55] and copper stressed Spinacia oleracea [58] alleviated the oxidative damage by reducing radical formation and MDA. Similarly, exogenous application of spermidine reduced the accumulation of ROS and MDA under cadmium [57] and nickel [61] stress. Impact of GA3 and Spm under Hg stress has not been reported and the reduced oxidative damage in GA3 and Spm treated plants can be attributed to increased functioning of antioxidant system and the accumulation of osmolytes. Activity of antioxidant enzymes assayed exhibited a significant increase due to Hg stress and the treatment of GA3 priming or foliar application of Spm further increased their activities thereby providing quick relief from the toxic effects of free radicals. Plants exposed to Hg stress exhibit increased activity of antioxidant enzymes as has been reported by Elbaz et al. [62], Sahu et al., [63]. SOD is specific for detoxification of O2•− while as H2O2 is eliminated by CAT or via ascorbate-glutathione cycle. In present study, GA3 priming and/or Spm application further enhanced the activity of antioxidant enzymes. Treatment of GA3 up-regulated the activity of antioxidant enzymes resulting in alleviation of the toxic effects of cadmium [55]. Exogenous application of polyamines has been demonstrated to increase the activity of antioxidant enzymes in cadmium stressed mung bean [64], thereby exhibiting significant decline in the accumulation of free radicals. Maintaining increased levels of polyamines including Spm improves the antioxidant functioning leading to amelioration of ill effects of cadmium stress in wheat [65]. Plants exhibiting increased activities of antioxidant enzymes show better growth and photosynthetic performance under stresses thereby increasing yield considerably. Increased functioning of GST quenches free radicals by utilizing glutathione leading to protection of cellular structures from the heavy metal induced oxidative damage [66]. Further, GST catalyzes S-conjugation between thiol of glutathione and the electrophilic moiety of toxic substrate which are then sequestered into vacuole or are exported from cells [66]. In present study, increased GST activity due to GA3 and Spm treatment may have mediated the elimination of Hg and also the toxic radicals leading to increased cellular stability. Improved functioning of APX and GR together with increased concentration of AsA and GSH in GA3 and Spm treated plants may have contributed to quick elimination of H2O2, redox maintainance, NADP for optimal functioning of photosynthetic electron transport. Among the non-enzymatic antioxidants, AsA, GSH and cysteine have great role in preventing the oxidative effects of stresses in plants. In present study, the content of these three increased significantly due to GA3 priming and foliar application of Spm. In plants, AsA, GSH and cysteine mediate ROS scavenging, redox homeostasis and stress signalling besides can also regulate cellular division, osmolyte and hormone synthesis. Supplementing cysteine [52] alleviates the Hg toxicity in Arabidopsis thaliana by restricting Hg uptake.

Priming with GA3 and the foliar application of Spm increased the accumulation of osmolytes including sugars, GB and proline. The significant increase in the accumulation of compatible osmolytes is considered as one of the key factors to improve stress tolerance potential in plants [67]. Osmolytes mediate osmotic adjustment, redox homeostasis, scavenge ROS and protect cellular metabolism from damaging effects of stresses [67–69]. Improved synthesis of osmolytes due to GA3 and Spm strengthened the tolerance mechanisms against the Hg stress. It was evident that increased proline accumulation in GA3 and Spm treated plants was due to significant increase in the activity of γ-GK and a decline in PROX. Increased γ-GK activity concomitant with reduced PROX activity has been reported in other plants as well resulting in greater accumulation of proline [8]. Exogenous treatment of GA3 resulted in increased proline and GB in chickpea under cadmium stress [55]. Supplementation of spermidine to Vigna angularis increased the accumulation of osmolytes including sugars, GB and proline causing the alleviation of cadmium induced decline in growth and photosynthesis [57].

In addition, the content of total phenols increased due to GA3 priming and Spm application. Secondary plant metabolites including phenols are important for growth, stress signalling and ROS scavenging. Phenols have been reported to better savage the free radicals than antioxidant enzymes thereby contributing significantly to alleviation of metal toxicity [69]. Application of polyamines [57] and GA3 [17] increased phenol accumulation in cadmium and lead stressed mung bean and carrot plants respectively. Plants treated with GA3 and Spm exhibited significantly higher radical scavenging activities measured interms of DPPH and ABTS scavenging (Fig. 6). Increased radical scavenging interms of DPPH due to GA3 treatment has been reported by Ghani et al., [17] in lead stressed carrot. This increased DPPH and ABTS scavenging activity in GA3 and Spm treated plants can be attributed mainly to greater synthesis of phenols, flavonoids, ascorbic acid, GSH, cysteine etc. [69, 8–9]. Increase in the ABTS scavenging potential due to exogenous application of Spm has been reported in tomato [70]. Reported discussing the influence of applied GA3 and Spm on phenol and radical scavenging potential under Hg stress are not available. NR, GOGAT and GS are key enzymes in nitrogen metabolism and decline in their activities directly influences the plant functioning. Amelioration of the decline in their activities due to GA3 and Spm treatments reflects their beneficial interaction for protecting nitrogen metabolism.

The tolerance mechanisms against the Hg stress were further strengthened by considerable increase in the activities of glyoxylase cycle enzymes. Treatment of GA3 and Spm resulted in increased activities of both glyoxylase enzymes easing the damaging effects of Hg. Increased activity of glyoxylase cycle enzymes has been reported under cadmium [55, 64] and nickel [61] stress however, impact of Hg has not been reported. Glyoxylases are key for detoxification of methylglyoxal which is a cytotoxic molecule and can adversely affect normal plant metabolism [71]. Glyoxylase cycle is GSH-dependent and mediates conversion of methylglyoxal into D-lactate [72].

Combined application of GA3 and Spm significantly alleviated Hg-induced stress, promoting plant growth. Positive correlations between plant height and MDA/γ-GK, and negative correlations with Ci, suggest a complex interplay between growth, oxidative stress and photosynthetic efficiency. Strong negative correlations between PFW and several key metabolic indicators (Chl b, E, GST, GS, P, GPX, GR) highlight the impact of Hg stress on plant biomass and overall health. Positive correlations between various antioxidant enzymes (GST, GPX, GR) and key metabolites (P, H2O2, O2•−) indicate a robust antioxidant response. The observed relationships between Chl b, E, GS, and other metabolites suggest coordinated responses in photosynthesis and metabolism under Hg stress. These results demonstrate the efficacy of GA3 and Spm in mitigating Hg toxicity and improving plant resilience.

Conclusion

Hg adversely affected the growth, photosynthesis and nitrogen metabolism in tomato however, GA3 priming and/or foliar application of Spm mitigated the reduction to substantial extent. Both GA3 and Spm imparted obvious impact on the parameters studied with their combined treatment showing much evident alleviatory influence. The mitigation of the Hg induced oxidative stress was associated with the considerable augmentation of the antioxidant system and the glyoxalase cycle enzymes besides the significant increase in the accumulation of osmolytes and phenols. Further the mitigating effects of GA3 priming and Spm supplementation was obvious interms of DPPH and ABTS radical scavenging. Hence it can be said that GA3 priming and Spm application eased the negative influence of Hg on growth and photosynthesis by up-regulating tolerance mechanisms and reducing the accumulation of Hg.

From waste to functional materials: preparation, modification, properties, and application of plant by-product polysaccharide-based biodegradable films in the field of food packaging
Authors: Hongkun Xue, Xiaodong Song, Yuan Liu, Kaifang Xie, Xiaojun Liao, Haipeng Liu, Jiaqi Tan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo address the challenges, such as resource depletion, environmental pollution, and health risks caused by traditional petroleum-based plastic packaging, the development of biodegradable alternative materials has become an important research direction in the field of food packaging. Plant-derived by-product polysaccharides (PPs) have attracted much attention in the field of bio-based food packaging due to their wide sources, low cost, good biodegradability, and low toxicity. PPs are prepared into biodegradable films, which have the dual potential of high-value utilization of waste and green packaging. Plant-derived by-product polysaccharide-based biodegradable films (PPBFs) can be regarded as a promising type of food packaging material. Firstly, this article summarizes the carrier materials of PPBFs and their structural-function relationships. Secondly, this review mainly discusses the preparation techniques, modification strategies, and applications of PPBFs. Finally, this article summarizes the characteristics, application progress, key challenges, and future research directions of PPBFs in various food preservation processes.Keywords: Plant-derived by-product polysaccharides, Biodegradable films, Modification strategies, Food packaging

Introduction

Approximately one-third of the world's food is spoiled and wasted, which exacerbates the food shortage problem in impoverished regions around the globe (Hu et al., 2025). Food packaging, as an indispensable part of the food supply chain, plays a crucial role in extending the shelf life of food, ensuring food safety, and reducing resource waste (Pou et al., 2022). Plastics have almost replaced metal and glass to become the main material for food packaging, which is attributed to their unique properties, including high flexibility, strong barrier properties, low cost, and ease of production (Gamage et al., 2024). Unfortunately, traditional petroleum-based plastics are non-biodegradable, which can lead to their long-term accumulation in the natural environment, thus causing serious problems, including microplastic pollution, marine ecological degradation, and global climate warming. It is estimated that over 300 million tons of plastic waste are produced globally each year, of which approximately 40% is used for food packaging. Only 9% of plastic food packaging materials are recycled, while nearly 80% is dumped, buried, or leaked into the environment. Approximately 40 to 120 million tons of plastic waste enter the ocean every year. It is predicted that the plastic industry will account for 20% of the world's total oil consumption, and the total amount of plastic waste in the environment will reach 12 billion tons by 2050, which will further exacerbating the environmental crisis (Reis et al., 2017). In this context, the development of biodegradable alternative materials has become a research hotspot in the field of food packaging. Biobased polymer materials are regarded as ideal substitutes for petroleum-based plastics due to their good biodegradability, biocompatibility, and environmental friendliness (Dang et al., 2025). From an economic perspective, the global bio-based packaging market is experiencing rapid growth. It is projected that the market size will exceed several hundred billion dollars by 2027, which can reflect the strong demand from consumers for sustainable solutions. With the development of green chemical technology, plant-derived by-product polysaccharides (PPs) can be transformed into a variety of functional materials and demonstrate great potential for application in the field of food packaging.

PPs are derived from waste materials (fruit peels, vegetable residues, and agricultural straw) and possess the properties of biodegradability, biocompatibility, non-toxicity, and low cost. Notably, PPs can be prepared into plant-derived by-product polysaccharide-based biodegradable films (PPBFs) through different methods, such as solution casting (SC), extrusion blow molding (EBM), and electrospinning, which are attributed to their excellent gelation and film-forming properties (Gheribi et al., 2019). Compared with traditional plastics, PPBFs exhibit better homogeneity, mechanical properties, barrier properties, and environmental friendliness (Nešić et al., 2019). Moreover, chemical, physical, and composite modification techniques can further optimize the mechanical and barrier properties of PPBFs. Additionally, the aforementioned modification techniques can also endow PPBFs with excellent antibacterial, antioxidant, and intelligent response active functions, thus meeting the requirements of multifunctional food packaging materials (Li et al., 2025). Hence, PPBFs are expected to become substitutes for traditional petroleum-based plastics, establish a green food packaging system, and alleviate the problems of food waste and plastic pollution.

There have been studies exploring the application scenarios of polysaccharide-based films in the field of food packaging. Unfortunately, there are limited systematic reviews on PPBFs and their innovative applications in food active packaging. This paper aims to fill this gap and establish a complete logical chain of “matrix material-preparation/modification-functional properties-food type”, which can provide theoretical support for the development of high-performance food packaging films. Firstly, this article comprehensively reviews the carrier materials (pectin, cellulose, starch, hemicellulose (HC), and alginate) of PPBFs. Secondly, this review systematically introduces the preparation methods (SC, EBM, electrospinning method, 3D printing technology, and layer-by-layer assembly technology (LLA)) of PPBFs and modification strategies (physical modification, chemical modification, and nano-composite modification). Subsequently, this paper elaborates on the properties (mechanical, barrier, antibacterial, antioxidant, and intelligent response properties) and the application of PPBFs in the preservation of various foods (fruits and vegetables, meat, seafood, and other foods) of PPBFs. Finally, this review summarizes the key challenges faced by PPBFs in the field of food packaging (high variability of raw materials, immature large-scale production processes, poor controllability of degradation, etc.), and proposes. Further research should focus on different aspects, such as raw material homogenization, process optimization, and controllability of degradation to develop high-performance and scalable PPBFs.

From a social perspective, this review is closely related to several united nations sustainable development goals (SDGs). This article can support SDG 9 (industry, innovation, and infrastructure), SDG 12 (responsible consumption and production), and SDG 13 (climate action) by promoting the high-value utilization of agricultural by-products, which is beneficial for reducing carbon footprints and improving climate change (Bahramian et al., 2024; Manzoor et al., 2024). The application of PPBFs can also significantly reduce plastic pollution to marine and terrestrial ecosystems, thereby protecting the health of aquatic organisms (SDG 14) and terrestrial ecosystems (SDG 15) (Javdani et al., 2025). From the perspective of industrial development, the large-scale production of PPBFs and their application in food preservation can also drive collaborative innovation in fields, such as agriculture, materials science, and food engineering, which can create green job opportunities and support SDG 8 (decent work and economic growth), SDG 2 (zero hunger), and SDG 3 (good health and well-being). Interestingly, this review also aligns with the concept of intelligent manufacturing (Industry 4.0/5.0/6.0). In the future, the production of PPBFs can be combined with automated, continuous preparation technologies (EBM, 3D printing), and intelligent monitoring systems based on the Internet of Things. Overall, the ultimate goal of this article is to promote the transition from traditional petroleum-based plastics to biodegradable materials in the field of food packaging, which can provide crucial theoretical support for building a resource-conserving and environmentally-friendly sustainable society.

Methods for literature search and screening

Literatureometrics, as a quantitative analysis search method based on mathematics and statistics, can quantitatively and qualitatively assess the changing trends of research activities over time, which is widely applied in the fields of scientific research evaluation and knowledge discovery. To conduct a systematic review of the research progress of PPBFs in the field of food packaging, this paper identifies relevant studies, reviews, and publicly available materials by using bibliometric analysis. Firstly, we utilized Web of Science, Scopus, and PubMed as the core databases to search for papers published between 2016 and 2025. The content we have retrieved covers research achievements in interdisciplinary fields, such as food science, materials science, polymer chemistry, and environmental science. We precisely identified the key terms, including but not limited to “plant by-product polysaccharide”, “biodegradable film”, “food packaging”, “modification strategy”, “antimicrobial”, and “pH-responsive”, and the initial search yielded a total of 4071 documents. Subsequently, we excluded those papers that only contained abstracts, were repetitive, and were not related to food packaging, and a total of 607 papers remained after the exclusion process. Moreover, we used the VOSviewer 1.6.20 software to filter out the frequently occurring keywords with a repetition count greater than 5, and a total of approximately 2381 keywords were identified. VOSviewer conducts cluster analysis on the selected keywords, which is beneficial for describing the correlations among the documents (Fig. S1). Finally, this article ultimately included 151 representative papers after a thorough review of the entire text. All the articles were full-text availability, and these studies that were not cited but only published in abstract form have been excluded, which ensured the accuracy and consistency of the information. This paper conducts a systematic literature analysis through scientific search methods, which can provide a solid basis for the systematic review of the current application status, technical challenges, potential value, and future development trends of PPBFs in the field of food packaging.

Classification of PPs

Food packaging materials, as an important barrier for ensuring food safety and freshness, can effectively prevent contamination, extend the shelf life, and reduce food waste. Unfortunately, traditional petroleum-based plastics are not biodegradable, which can cause environmental pollution and exacerbate ecological toxicity. Notably, bio-based packaging integrates biodegradability into the traditional packaging system, which can effectively solve the limitation of non-biodegradability in traditional packaging (Sid et al., 2021). PPs (pectin, cellulose, starch, HC, and alginate) can be formulated into various biodegradable films, which is attributed to their unique molecular structure and functional properties (Table S1). PPs are widely distributed in fruit peels, vegetable and fruit residues, straw, and seaweed waste. Converting these by-products into high-value-added functional materials can facilitate resource recycling, which can show great potential in the field of sustainable food packaging (Wei et al., 2025). Pectin

Pectin, as a biodegradable natural polymer compound, is widely present in the peels of citrus fruits, apple pomace, beet pulp, etc. (Li et al., 2022). Previous report has confirmed that extracting pectin from plant by-products, as a biobased packaging material, was a sustainable solution, which was beneficial for alleviating the global problem of resource waste (Reichembach & de Oliveira Petkowicz, 2021). Pectin, as a typical anionic structural polysaccharide, is composed of a linear main chain of D-galacturonic acid (GalA) linked by α-1,4 glycosidic bonds and neutral sugars (Morales et al., 2021). Pectin also exhibits excellent film-forming properties, which are attributed their presence of carboxyl and hydroxyl groups in molecular structure (Kumar et al., 2023). Notably, the different esterification degrees (DE) of pectin can affect its gel and film-forming properties. Highly methoxylated pectin (DE ≥ 50%) can form gels under acidic conditions through hydrogen bonds and hydrophobic interactions, while low methoxylated pectin (DE < 50%) mainly forms gels by cross-linking with Ca2+ and the “egg box” model of carboxyl groups (Chalapud et al., 2023). Unfortunately, the barrier properties of the pectin-based films are relatively poor. To address this issue, growing study has focused on preparing composite films by combining pectin with various materials (chitosan and nanoparticles), thereby improving their barrier properties (Reichembach & de Oliveira Petkowicz, 2021). Younis et al. (2020) prepared PE films by the electrostatic complexation method using chitosan/pectin fibers (CH/PE fibers) and apple pomace as a raw material. The results show that CH/PE fibers could reduce the water vapor permeability (WVP) (4.03 × 10−10 g/(m·s·Pa)) and improve the barrier properties of PE films. Moreover, the incorporation of CH/PE fibers could enhance the thermal stability, mechanical properties (tensile strength (TS) of 10.76 MPa, elongation at break (EB) of 65.53%, and Young's modulus (YM) of 16.38 MPa), and ultraviolet blocking ability of PE films, which could provide an innovative strategy for further improving the performance of pectin-based films. Interestingly, the pectin-based films can also achieve excellent barrier properties by incorporating nanoparticles. For instance, the pectin/gelatin composite films were prepared by incorporating curcumin and silver nanoparticles (AgNPs) using citrus peels as the raw material. AgNPs could endow the composite films with excellent hydrophobicity (hydrophobic angle of 98.3°) and water vapor barrier properties (WVP of 2.39 × 10−10 g/(m·s·Pa)). Moreover, the composite films possessed a pH-responsive color change behavior ranging from yellow (pH 3–8) to light red (pH 8–9) and then to deep red (pH 11–12), antioxidant activity, and antibacterial activity, which was conducive to meeting the demand for the composite films as an active packaging material (Fig. 1A) (Li et al., 2024). Overall, pectin demonstrates great potential in the preparation of PPBFs. Future research should focus on improving the water resistance and mechanical/barrier properties of pectin-based films through techniques, such as blending, cross-linking, and nano-composite, which can improve the overall performance of pectin-based films in the food packaging system.

Cellulose

Cellulose, as the most abundant renewable resource globally, is widely present in sugarcane residue, corn husk, wheat straw, cotton lint, and wood chips (Ahmad Khorairi et al., 2023). Currently, increasing studies are focusing on recovering cellulose from agricultural waste and converting it into high-value bio-based materials, which effectively avoid the problem of agricultural resource waste (Liu et al., 2022). Cellulose can form a dense network and highly ordered crystalline regions through intramolecular and intermolecular hydrogen bonds, and demonstrate excellent chemical stability, TS, and stiffness, further indicating that cellulose can be an ideal material for biodegradable films (Harris et al., 2012). However, the cellulose-based films possessed poor toughness and water vapor barrier properties, which significantly limited their application in the food packaging field (Grzybek et al., 2024). To address this limitation, existing studies mainly adopt physical blending methods (chitosan and nanofibers) to modify cellulose-based films, which could significantly improve their toughness and water vapor barrier properties (Zhang et al., 2024). Zhou et al. (2025) prepared carboxymethyl cellulose (CMC) composite films through the physical blending method (chitosan) using water-soluble cellulose derivatives as raw materials, and evaluated their physicochemical properties. The results show that compared with the untreated CMC films, the CMC composite films possessed higher EB (39.56%) and water vapor barrier properties (WVP decreased to 6.46 ± 0.37 g·mm/(m2·d·kPa)). Surprisingly, the utilization of chemical cross-linking can also significantly improve the toughness and water vapor barrier properties of the cellulose-based films. For example, the cellulose-based composite films (COPA films) were prepared by co-assembling with okra polysaccharide, silver nanowires, and phytic acid using sugarcane residue as the raw material. The results show that phytic acid could endow the COPA films with extremely high toughness and water-vapor barrier properties. Moreover, the COPA films could also effectively prevent the loss of water and nutrients in lychees, inhibit the growth of bacteria and fungi on the surface of lychees, thus extending the shelf life of lychees. The COPA films, as the humidity sensor, could respond to changes in environmental humidity, generate electrical signal changes, and achieve real-time monitoring of the freshness of lychees, which could meet the urgent demands in the fields of food cold chain logistics and fresh food e-commerce (Fig. 1B) (Wei et al., 2025). In terms of cost-effectiveness, the unit cost of cellulose-based printed sensors could be controlled below $0.02 and much lower than that of traditional silicon-based sensors, which demonstrated the economic feasibility of COPA films for large-scale commercialization (Barandun et al., 2019). In summary, cellulose is widely used in the field of food packaging due to its unique chemical structure, excellent functional properties, and good biocompatibility. Future research should focus on using nano/low-temperature composite technology to improve the performance of cellulose-based films, reduce energy consumption, and increase production efficiency, which are conducive to promoting cellulose-based films to become a biodegradable alternative material to traditional petroleum-based plastic packaging.

Starch

Starch, as an energy storage product of plant photosynthesis, is widely present in plant by-products, such as cereal grains (corn bran, wheat bran, and rice husks), tubers (potatoes and cassava), and unripe banana peels (Larrea-Wachtendorff et al., 2021). Previous study has confirmed that recovering starch from agricultural by-products and applying it in the field of bio-based packaging can contribute to reducing the burden of resource waste (Velazquez et al., 2025). Starch is composed of amylose and amylopectin (Cui et al., 2021). Notably, the amylose molecular chains are rich in hydroxyl groups, which enable starch to possess excellent film-forming properties. Based on this characteristic, Majumder et al. (2024) prepared starch-based films through the gelation-rehydration method using milled rice starch as the raw material. These films exhibited excellent barrier properties, breathability, and uniformity, which was attributed to the fact that amylose could form a dense three-dimensional network structure through intermolecular hydrogen bonds. Unfortunately, these films possessed the limitations of poor hydrophobicity and weak mechanical properties, which greatly restricted their application in the field of food packaging. To address the limitations, growing studies are dedicated to improving the performance of starch-based films through chemical modification (cross-linking and esterification) and physical modification (plasma treatment and blending) (Lin et al., 2024). For instance, AKD-modified films were prepared by the esterification method (alkyl vinyl ketone dimer, AKD) using corn bran as a raw material (Duan et al., 2023). The results show that compared with pure starch-based films, AKD-modified films exhibited superior uniformity, hydrophobicity (hydrophobic angle of 128.28°), and mechanical properties (TS of 11.96 MPa and YM of 210.32 MPa) (Fig. 1C). Additionally, the AKD-modified films significantly reduced the water vapor permeability (WVP of 0.81 × 10−10 g/(m·s·Pa)) and improved the shading performance, which were beneficial for expanding the application of starch-based films in the field of food packaging. Simultaneously, starch-based composite films were prepared by the physical blending method using potato starch and polyvinyl alcohol as the raw materials. Polyvinyl alcohol significantly enhanced the mechanical properties (EB reaching up to 484.52%), thermal stability, and water vapor barrier properties of these composite films, further demonstrating the modification advantages of the physical blending method (Gómez-Aldapa et al., 2020). More importantly, existing study has confirmed that starch-based films could actually be applied in the food packaging field. The starch-based composite films loaded with curcumin possessed excellent oil absorption capacity, antioxidant activity, and antibacterial ability, which could delay the nutrient consumption of chicken, inhibit the growth of mold on the surface of chicken, thus extending the shelf life of chicken. The composite films also possessed a pH-responsive color change behavior ranging from yellow (pH ≤ 8) to orange-red (pH ≥ 9), which was conducive to the real-time monitoring of the freshness of chicken (Mali & Pandey, 2024). Hence, starch demonstrates great potential for application in food packaging due to its unique molecular structure and multiple biological activities. Future research should focus on improving the hydrophobicity and mechanical properties of starch-based films through chemical and physical modifications, thus enhancing their packaging durability.

Hemicellulose and alginate

HC, as a rich renewable resource, is widely present in crop straw, fruit peels, wood chips, etc. (de Menezes et al., 2025). HC exhibits excellent biocompatibility and strong barrier properties against oxygen, thus demonstrating sustainable advantages in the field of food packaging (Liu et al., 2023). Unfortunately, the HC films possessed limitations, including insufficient mechanical properties, poor hydrophobicity, and weak water vapor barrier performance, which make them difficult to meet the basic performance requirements of ideal food packaging materials. Unexpectedly, the chemical modification of HC films could significantly improve their mechanical properties, hydrophobicity, and water vapor barrier performance, which was attributed to the presence of hydroxyl groups within the HC molecular chains (Zhao et al., 2020). For instance, Zhang et al. (2020) prepared composite HC films by the SC using vinyl methacrylate and HC as raw materials and evaluated their properties. The results show that the composite films exhibited excellent mechanical properties (TS of 33.94 MPa and EB of 22.41%), hydrophobicity (hydrophobic angle of 120°), and water vapor barrier properties (WVP reduced to 1.59–2.23 (10−10 g/m·s·Pa)) (Fig. 1D). Moreover, these composite films also demonstrated good antioxidant and antibacterial activities, which could effectively delay the loss of nutrients in green peppers and inhibit the growth of mold on the surface of green peppers, thereby extending the shelf life of green peppers.

Alginate, as a linear anionic polysaccharide derived from brown algae, is composed of alternating β-1,4 glycosidic bonds of D-mannuronic acid and L-guluronic acid in different proportions. Alginate mainly includes sodium alginate (SA) and calcium alginate (Costa et al., 2018). Notably, SA, as the most significant by-product in the processing of algae, can form a three-dimensional network structure by chelating divalent metal cations, which can endow excellent gelation, biocompatibility, and film-forming properties, further indicating that SA can be used as a matrix material for PPBFs (Tavassoli-Kafrani et al., 2016). Previous report has confirmed that the SA/gelatin composite films embedded with AgNPs exhibited excellent mechanical properties, barrier properties, and thermal stability. The composite films could also delay the loss of moisture and nutrients in oranges and effectively inhibit the growth of bacteria and fungi on the surface of oranges, thus extending the shelf life of oranges (Li et al., 2022). Similarly, the carboxymethyl chitosan/SA composite films loaded with proanthocyanidins and curcumin possessed outstanding barrier properties, anti-ultraviolet ability, antioxidant activity, and antibacterial activity, which could meet the basic requirements of functional materials for food packaging (Fig. 1E). Additionally, these composite films could produce a visible color response in response to changes in environmental pH, and enable real-time monitoring of the freshness of shrimp, which could provide a feasible material solution for visual monitoring of food status (Tie et al., 2025).

All in all, HC and alginate demonstrate significant application potential in the field of bio-based packaging materials due to their unique chemical structure and physical properties. Unfortunately, the current preparation processes for HC films and alginate films are quite complex, which restrict their practical application in the field of food packaging. Future research can focus on optimizing the preparation process through nanocomposite technology and low-temperature molding techniques, which can reduce energy consumption, improve production efficiency, thereby enhancing the applicability of HC and alginate films in food packaging.

The preparation methods of PPBFs

PPs, as ideal materials for developing new biodegradable films, demonstrate great application potential in the field of food packaging. The preparation techniques of PPBFs mainly include SC, EBM, electrospinning method, 3D printing technology, and LLA (Fig. 2) (Wei et al., 2025). Different preparation techniques have a significant impact on the performance of PPBFs, which is crucial for optimizing their functionality and ensuring food safety (Deol et al., 2025). This section mainly summarizes the applicable scope, unique advantages, and limitations of various preparation methods for PPBFs, which can provide a theoretical basis and new ideas for the large-scale production of PPBFs.

Different preparation methods of PPBFs.

Solution casting

SC, as an easy-to-operate and cost-effective film-forming technique, has been widely applied in the preparation of biodegradable films. SC could produce films with uniform surfaces and dense structures through four steps, including raw material dissolution, solution coating, solvent evaporation, and film peeling, which is especially suitable for processing plant natural polysaccharide materials (starch, pectin, and cellulose) (Lu et al., 2021; Otoni et al., 2017; Ren et al., 2022). Wang et al. (2019) prepared starch-based composite films by the SC method using potato starch and lauric acid as raw materials. These composite films possessed excellent mechanical properties (TS of 2.64 MPa and EB of 50%) and water vapor barrier properties (WVP reduced to 1.7 × 10−10 g / (m·s·Pa)). Unfortunately, these composite films were prone to residual organic solvents, which might affect food safety and flavor. Future research should focus on utilizing environmentally friendly solvents (water-based and ionic liquids) to solve the problem of solvent residues and ensure food safety. Unexpectedly, SC can also uniformly disperse nano lotion with the mild process conditions (temperature and shear force), which is conducive to loading nano materials as functional fillers into the pectin matrix (Norcino et al., 2020). The gelatin-based films activated by nanoemulsion possessed excellent extensibility, biodegradability, and antibacterial activity, which provided an experimental basis for the development of high-performance active packaging materials. More importantly, SC also enables the in-situ cross-linking of the film with cations (Ca2+, Fe3+, and Cu2+) and achieves the stable loading of cations through intermolecular interactions (hydrogen bonds and electrostatic adsorption), thereby preparing stable-structured and durable PPBFs (Zhang et al., 2022). For instance, Fe3+ cross-linking pectin-based composite films were prepared by the SC method. Compared with pure pectin-based films, these cross-linking films exhibited higher mechanical properties (TS of 41.68 MPa, YM of 1155.47 MPa). Additionally, Fe3+ cross-linking could effectively improve the hydrophobicity (hydrophobic angle of 101°), ultraviolet resistance, and water vapor barrier properties (WVP reduced to 1.09 × 10−10 g/(m·s·Pa)) of the composite films. Furthermore, these composite films could also effectively delay water loss and inhibit physiological metabolism in the preservation of strawberries, thereby extending the shelf life of strawberries (Qiang et al., 2024). All in all, SC demonstrates great potential in the field of preparing PPBFs due to its simple operation, mild process conditions, and wide applicability. In the future, we can also optimize the process parameters of the SC method and enhance the film-forming efficiency, thus meeting the requirements of industrial production.

Extrusion blow molding

EBM, as an efficient and continuous industrial production process, is widely used in the manufacturing of food plastic packaging materials (Martins et al., 2022). EBM forms the film through five key steps, such as heating for melting, die extrusion, closing mold inflation, cooling for solidification, and demolding (Cui et al., 2021). Compared with SC, EBM can produce film products of different sizes and shapes through molds with specific structures, which is beneficial for meeting consumers' food packaging needs (San & Harnkarnsujarit, 2023). EBM also prepares uniform films with good mechanical properties and barrier performance by precisely controlling the melt extrusion (150 °C–180 °C) and blow molding (shear rate 100 s−1-500 s−1) parameters, indicating that EBM demonstrates unique advantages in the preparation of thermoplastic polysaccharide (starch) films. For instance, the biodegradable starch-based composite films were prepared through EBM using cassava starch and chickpea protein as raw materials, and evaluated the influence of EBM on their properties (Ochoa-Yepes et al., 2019). The results show that EBM could endow these composite films with good mechanical properties (YM of 22 MPa and TS of 2.4 MPa), thermal stability, and water vapor barrier properties (WVP reduced to 1.4 × 10−10 g/(m·s·Pa)). Moreover, the composite films could completely degrade within 5 weeks and demonstrate excellent environmental friendliness. Notably, EBM is widely used in processing plasticizers (glycerol, acetylated glycerol monoesters, and polyethylene glycol) to modify plant polysaccharide systems, thereby developing multifunctional PPBFs. Previous report has confirmed that the glycerol/high-methoxy plasticized pectin films prepared by EBM had excellent puncture resistance (43.01–230.75 N/mm), mechanical properties (TS ranging of 3.44–8.74 MPa), and water vapor barrier properties (WVP of 1.27–2.47 g·mm/(KPa·hm2)) (de Oliveira et al., 2021). Unfortunately, the moisture-proof performance of these plasticized pectin films was poor. In the future, we should focus on developing new plasticizers to improve the moisture-proof performance of these plasticized pectin films. Unexpectedly, the plasticizing plant polysaccharide-based films also have good antibacterial and antioxidant activities. The glycerol/TPS/PBAT/SM composite films were prepared by EBM using thermoplastic starch (TPS), polybutylene adipate-co-polyoxalate (PBAT), and sodium metabisulfite (SM) as raw materials (San & Harnkarnsujarit, 2023). The composite films could effectively inhibit the proliferation of Escherichia coli, Staphylococcus aureus, and Penicillium italicum, which could extend the shelf life of Italian sausage to 16 days. The composite films also had excellent antioxidant activity, which could effectively delay the nutrient loss of longan and extend the shelf life of longan, further indicating that the composite films have great application potential as a food packaging material. In summary, EBM has advantages, including high film-forming efficiency, continuous production, and product diversity. In the future, we should focus on adopting nanocomposite technology, new plasticizers, and low-temperature extrusion blow molding technology to improve the moisture-proof performance of PPBFs and apply EBM in the processing of heat-sensitive polysaccharides, which can expand the application scope of EBM.

Electrospinning method

The electrospinning method can produce nanofibers with unique structures and properties, and demonstrate significant advantages in the field of bio-based packaging material preparation (Wang et al., 2025). The electrospinning technology forms a film through four steps, including preparing the spinning solution, applying high-voltage electric field for spraying, stretching to refine the sprayed stream, and solidifying, which can enable precise control of the material's microstructure, thereby preparing nanofiber composite films (NCFs) (Ji et al., 2024; Sameen et al., 2022). Currently, increasing studies are focusing on the application of nanofiber composite films in the field of food packaging, which is attributed to their excellent mechanical properties, barrier properties, and biocompatibility. For instance, curcumin could be uniformly encapsulated and immobilized in NCFs through the electrospinning method, which could effectively address the issue of poor water solubility of curcumin. Moreover, NCFs with efficient loading of curcumin exhibited excellent mechanical properties (EB approximately 92%), water vapor barrier performance (WVP reduced to 1.87 × 10−10 g/(m·s·Pa)), and hydrophobicity (hydrophobic angle of 134°), which conformed to the standards of food packaging materials (Dai et al., 2022). Similarly, Guo et al. (2022) prepared NCFs through electrospinning using konjac tubers and grape seeds as raw materials, and evaluated their properties. Compared with SC, the NCFs prepared by the electrospinning technique possessed higher stability, uniformity, and water vapor barrier performance (WVP reduced to 1.5 × 10−10 g/(m·s·Pa)). NCFs could also significantly enhance the scavenging ability of DPPH free radicals, which could demonstrate good antioxidant activity. Unfortunately, NCFs were prone to residual organic solvents, which might affect food safety. Future research should focus on adopting water-based dispersion techniques to reduce chemical residues and ensure food safety. Unexpectedly, the PPBFs prepared by electrospinning could be stably applied in the actual food packaging field. Previous report has confirmed that cellulose composite films were prepared through the electrospinning technology using waste ginger residue as the raw material, and evaluated their preservation effect on strawberries (Kou et al., 2025). The composite films could effectively delay the evaporation of water and the loss of nutrients in strawberries, and inhibit the growth of microorganisms on the surface of strawberries, thereby extending the shelf life of strawberries. Moreover, the composite films completely degraded within 18 days through soil degradation experiments, further indicating that the electrospinning technology shows great application potential in the preparation of PPBFs. All in all, the electrospinning technology has the advantages of high encapsulation efficiency, adjustable process parameters, and high efficiency in loading active substances. In the future, we can also develop a polysaccharide/natural protein mixed system to improve the processability of polysaccharide (starch and pectin) solutions and expand the application of the electrospinning technology in the preparation of PPBFs.

3D printing and the layer-by-layer assembly technology

3D printing technology, as an emerging technology that is highly customizable, integrates multiple materials, and precisely constructs complex structures for film formation, demonstrates significant application value in the field of biobased packaging (Thakur et al., 2024). Compared with traditional manufacturing technologies, 3D printing technology can build three-dimensional objects by layer-by-layer stacking of materials, which can endow the films with high mechanical properties, hydrophobicity, and water vapor barrier performance (Panjabrao et al., 2025). For instance, the composite films were fabricated through 3D printing technology using pectin, CMC, and zinc oxide nanoparticles as raw materials. Compared with the films prepared by the SC method, the composite films exhibited higher mechanical properties (TS of 41.3 MPa), hydrophobicity (hydrophobic angle of 72.6°), and water vapor barrier performance (WVP of 1.29 × 10−9 g/(m·Pa·s)). The composite films also demonstrated excellent UV resistance, antibacterial, and antioxidant activities, which could meet the requirements for food preservation (Kim et al., 2022). Notably, compared with other preparation methods, 3D printing technology can also integrate PPs with active functional components (pH indicators, antioxidants, and antibacterial agents) through precise digital design, thereby constructing functional films with complex structures. Zhou et al. (2021) prepared the new type of composite films through 3D printing technology using coniferous wood chips and pH indicators (anthocyanins) as raw materials. The results show that the new composite film could effectively prevent water loss and nutrient consumption of lychees through physical barriers, thereby extending the shelf life of lychees. Moreover, the composite films exhibited pH-responsive color changes ranging from red (pH ≤ 7) to blue-violet (pH ≥ 8), which could enable real-time monitoring of the freshness of lychees.

LLA, as a cutting-edge technique in the field of film preparation, leverages its unique advantages of layer-by-layer assembly to demonstrate significant application potential in the field of biobased packaging. LLA can alternately deposit different components (polyelectrolytes and nanoparticles) through interactions, including electrostatic adsorption, hydrogen bonds, and covalent bonds, drive their layer-by-layer self-assembly, thereby forming structurally controllable composite films, which can enable systematic optimization of the performance of traditional single-layer films (Su et al., 2022). Growing studies are focusing on using LLA to prepare polysaccharide-based composite films and applying them in the food packaging field. For instance, the cellulose composite films were prepared by LLA using loofah sponges, benzyl isothiocyanate, and chitosan as raw materials (Jiang et al., 2022). The results show that the composite films possessed excellent mechanical properties (TS increased to 31.32 MPa and EB increased to 4.2%), barrier properties, shading properties, and oil absorption capacity. The composite films could also significantly inhibit the growth of Staphylococcus aureus and bacteria on the surface of roasted chicken, which could prolong the shelf life of the roasted chicken, further confirming that the composite films can be used as an alternative to food plastic packaging. In another study, the SA-based cross-linked films prepared by LLA also exhibited excellent mechanical properties, barrier properties, and antibacterial activity. Moreover, the SA-based cross-linked films could delay the lipid oxidation and nutrient consumption of fresh shrimp, inhibit the growth of bacteria in fresh shrimp, thus extending the shelf life of fresh shrimp. Furthermore, the SA-based cross-linked films had pH-responsive color changes ranging from yellow (pH 3–6) to light red (pH 7–8) and then to purple (pH 9–12), which could monitor the freshness of shrimp in real time (Wu et al., 2024). At the level of consumer acceptance, cross-national survey studies have confirmed that consumers had a positive attitude towards intelligent packaging that could provide real-time information on food freshness, which could demonstrate the good market acceptance of SA-based cross-linked films (Chrysochou & Tiganis, 2025).

All in all, 3D printing technology and LLA can precisely control the composition, microstructure, and function of the film due to their unique layer-by-layer stacking method. Unfortunately, 3D printing technology and LLA in the preparation of PPBFs also have some limitations in the food packaging field as follows: 1) 3D printing technology has high requirements for raw material properties, which restrict its wide applicability; 2) LLA relies on weak interlayer bonding force due to electrostatic interaction, which easily leads to delamination risks; 3) The cost and energy consumption of 3D printing technology and LLA preparation are high, which make them difficult to achieve large-scale production. Future research should focus on expanding the raw material adaptability of 3D printing technology through nanocomposite technology and enhancing the interlayer bonding stability of LLA. We can also develop efficient and low-consumption processes, including low-temperature printing and roll-to-roll continuous coating, which can promote the development of 3D printing technology and LLA towards low-cost and large-scale production, thereby enhancing their practical application value in food packaging.

The modification strategies of PPBFs

PPBFs are derived from plant-based waste materials (such as fruit residues, vegetable peels, and straw) and possess advantages, including good biodegradability, biocompatibility, non-toxicity and low cost, thus attracting much attention in the field of bio-based packaging. Unfortunately, PPBFs have limitations, including poor mechanical properties, weak water vapor barrier performance, and limited antibacterial activity, which restrict their wide application in the field of food packaging (Varghese et al., 2023). To address this limitation, increasing studies have focused on modifying PPBFs to enhance their performance. This section mainly summarizes the main modification methods (physical, chemical, and nano-composite) for PPBFs, optimizes the film structure and performance, thus expanding their application in the field of food packaging (Fig. 3). Table S2 summarizes the preparation and modification methods of PPBFs, as well as their advantages and challenges.

Different modification methods of PPBFs.

Physical modification

Physical modification has shown great potential in enhancing the performance of polysaccharide-based films due to its advantages, including simple operation, green safety, and mild process conditions(Long et al., 2023). Physical modification promotes the rearrangement and aggregation of polysaccharide molecular chains through physical conditions (plasma, radiation, mechanical force, and heat treatment), which can effectively improve the mechanical properties and water vapor barrier properties of PPBFs. For instance, the starch-based films were prepared through plasma technology using corn bran as the raw material, and evaluated the influence of plasma technology on their properties (Goiana et al., 2021). The results show that plasma technology could endow the films with excellent mechanical properties (TS of 15.82 MPa and YM of 664 MPa), hydrophobicity (hydrophobic angle of 64.3°), and water vapor barrier properties (WVP of 1.55 g·mm/(kPa·h·m2)). Simultaneously, Sarak et al. (2022) prepared starch-based composite films through physical blending. Compared with the untreated films, the composite films exhibited higher mechanical properties (EB up to 399%, increased by 11 times) and better hydrophobicity (hydrophobic angle of 116.03°, increased by 2 times). Additionally, the composite films effectively prevented the penetration of oxygen, carbon dioxide, and water, and slowed down the loss of moisture and nutrients in mangoes, thus extending the shelf life of mangoes by 10 days. Regretfully, the antibacterial and antioxidant properties of these composite films were relatively poor, which may limit their wide application in the field of food packaging. To address this issue, current research has adopted radiation modification to modify PPBFs, which could enhance their inhibitory effect on microorganisms, promote the inactivation of enzymes involved in oxidative reactions, thereby endowing the films with excellent antibacterial and antioxidant properties. Previous report has confirmed that the cellulose-based films modified by γ irradiation could have an inhibition rate against Escherichia coli as high as 100%, and effectively eliminate DPPH free radicals, further demonstrating that the films have excellent antibacterial and antioxidant activities (Irimia et al., 2017). More importantly, the physical modification methods can also preserve the excellent biocompatibility and biodegradability of PPBFs. The antibacterial films based on starch were prepared by the melt blending method using cassava starch, PBAT, and AgNPs@SiO₂ as raw materials. These antibacterial films possessed excellent mechanical properties, barrier properties, and uniformity, which were attributed to the stable loading of AgNPs@SiO₂ particles achieved through melt blending, thereby endowing these antibacterial films with good biocompatibility. The antibacterial films could also significantly inhibit the growth of Staphylococcus aureus, Escherichia coli (antibacterial rate > 90%), and bacteria on peach surfaces, thereby extending the shelf life of the peaches (Zhou et al., 2021). All in all, physical modification can effectively improve the mechanical properties and barrier performance of PPBFs, significantly enhance their antibacterial activity, thus possessing good application potential. Physical modification in the field of food packaging also has advantages, including maintaining the biodegradability of PPBFs, ensuring the reversibility of the process, and eliminating chemical residues. Future research should focus on combining nanotechnology with functional additives to enhance the physicochemical properties and active functions of PPBFs, thereby expanding the application of physical modification in the field of food packaging.

Chemical modification

Chemical modification methods can significantly enhance the performance, improve durability, and enable functional design of PPBFs, which have great potential in improving the comprehensive performance of PPBFs. Chemical modification can introduce new functional groups or side chains onto the polysaccharide molecular chains through chemical reactions (etherification, esterification, grafting, and cross-linking), precisely regulate chemical structure, and overcome the inherent defects caused by their strong hydrophilicity. This can effectively address the application limitations of PPBFs in the field of food packaging (Zhao et al., 2020). However, the chemical modification can disrupt the natural structure of PPs and reduce the biodegradability of PPBFs, which can greatly limit the application scope of chemical modification. To address this issue, current research should focus on optimizing the modification process parameters and combining dynamic covalent bond design, thereby balancing the relationship between the performance and biodegradability of PPBFs. Previous report has confirmed that HC films etherified with epichlorohydrin could achieve excellent properties, such as high TS (14.6 MPa), low hydrophobicity (hydrophobic angle of 71.7°), and high barrier performance (OP reduced to 1.9 cm3 μm/(m2·d·kPa)), which is attributed to the formation of covalent bridges between epichlorohydrin and the HC molecular chains, thereby constructing a stable three-dimensional cross-linked network. Etherification could also reduce the solubility of the films and enhance their biodegradability, which complied with the food packaging material standards (Shao et al., 2020). Notably, the chemical modification method can endow PPBFs with excellent antibacterial, antioxidant, and intelligent response functional properties by introducing functional groups/cross-linking modifications. For instance, Wei et al. (2024) prepared starch-based composite films through the esterification method using corn bran as the raw material. These composite films exhibited excellent light-blocking properties, hydrophobicity (hydrophobic angle of 84.9°), and water vapor barrier properties (WVP reduced to 4.01 × 10−5 g/(mm∙d)). Moreover, the composite films could significantly inhibit the growth of Escherichia coli, Staphylococcus aureus (antibacterial rate of 100%), and bacteria on the surface of cherries, thus extending the shelf life of cherries by 7 days. Similarly, starch-based films were modified by grafting tea polyphenols (TP) and evaluated their functional properties (Feng et al., 2018). The results show that TP could effectively inhibit the growth of Escherichia coli and Staphylococcus aureus by damaging the bacterial cell membranes, which could endow these films with excellent antibacterial activity. Compared with the unmodified starch-based films, these films could effectively remove DPPH free radicals and increase antioxidant activity by nearly four times. In another study, the SA/turmeric composite film prepared by cross-linking with Zn2+ exhibited excellent barrier properties, antibacterial activity, and antioxidant activity, which could slow down the nutrient loss of pork, inhibit the growth of bacteria on the surface of pork, thus prolonging the shelf life of pork. The composite films, as the pH-sensitive materials, could also trigger color changes in response to changes in environmental pH, which could enable real-time monitoring of the freshness of pork, further confirming that the chemical cross-linking method has significant application potential in the development of intelligent food packaging (Liu et al., 2025). Unfortunately, the chemical modification in the field of food packaging still has limitations, such as high energy consumption and the easy residue of chemical reagents. Future researches should focus on developing green chemical modification technologies (enzymatic cross-linking and biobased cross-linking agents), reducing energy consumption and chemical toxicity, and ensuring food safety.

Nano-composite modification

Nanocomposite modification, as a cutting-edge technology in the field of advanced manufacturing, demonstrates significant application potential in the modification of PPBFs due to its low toxicity and integrated multi-functionality. Nanocomposite modification can uniformly disperse nanofillers (nanofibers, montmorillonite (MMT), and metal nanoparticles (MN)) into the polysaccharide matrix, which enhance the comprehensive performance of PPBFs through interface effects (Agha & Jawad, 2025). Unfortunately, the nano-composite modification has problems, including poor dispersion of nano-fillers and potential safety risks of migration. To address these issues, bio-based nanomaterials can be used for surface functionalization modification, which enhances their matrix compatibility and food safety. Previous report has found that the pectin-based composite films were prepared by embedding cellulose nanofibers (CNF) using beet pulp as the raw material, and evaluated the influence of CNF on properties (Sarafidou et al., 2025). The results show that CNF could significantly improve the mechanical properties of these composite films (TS increased by 39.7%, EB increased by 53.6%, and YM increased by 54.0%), hydrophobicity (hydrophobic angle of 106.7°), and water vapor barrier ability (WVP decreased to 1.78 × 10−7 g/(m·h·Pa)). CNF mostly originated from agricultural and industrial waste (wood chips, sugarcane residue, and wheat straw), further suggesting that CNF has broad prospects in the sustainable food packaging field (Zhang et al., 2021). Interestingly, the starch-based composite films prepared by embedding MMT exhibited superior mechanical properties (TS increased to 45.9 MPa) and thermal stability. These composite films could also completely degrade in soil within 18 days, which could demonstrate excellent biodegradability (Yeasmin et al., 2020). Unexpectedly, MN can also endow PPBFs with excellent antibacterial activity, antioxidant activity, and gas/ethylene removal properties, and has been widely applied in the food packaging field (Nikolic et al., 2021). For instance, Saravanakumar et al. (2020) prepared SA composite films by embedding copper oxide nanoparticles and evaluated their preservation effect on fresh-cut peppers (FCPs). The composite films could effectively inhibit the growth of Staphylococcus aureus, Escherichia coli, species of Trichoderma, etc. Moreover, the composite films could efficiently remove DPPH free radicals, inhibit microbial contamination and physiological metabolism of FCPs, thus prolonging the shelf life of FCPs. Furthermore, the MN composite films could effectively degrade ethylene through photoxidation and extend the shelf life of bananas, further implying that the MN composite films can be an ideal substitute for petroleum-based food packaging plastics (Qin et al., 2024).

In summary, the nano-composite modification can effectively improve the comprehensive performance of PPBFs, significantly enhance their antibacterial and antioxidant activities, which can provide an important approach for the functional development of PPBFs. Additionally, the nano-composite modification in the field of food packaging has the following advantages: 1) Achieve precise functionalization design and expand its applicability in different types of food; 2) Catalytic degradation of ethylene and enhance the preservation effect for specific foods; 3) Utilize agricultural and industrial waste to synthesize nano-fillers, reduce production costs, and alleviate environmental burdens; 4) Maintain the good biodegradability of the film and promote the sustainable development of food packaging. Future research should focus on developing nano-composite processes at low temperatures and energy consumption, which can improve the feasibility and economic efficiency of nanocomposite modification technology. At the consumer acceptance level, consumers' acceptance of nano-packaging is influenced by psychological factors, such as social norms, environmental awareness, and trust. We can significantly enhance consume’ purchase intentions through transparent label markings and effective risk communication (Gupta et al., 2024).

The properties of PPBFs

PPBFs, as the sustainable and green packaging material, exhibit excellent mechanical properties, barrier properties, antibacterial properties, antioxidant properties, and intelligent responsiveness, and PPBFs have broad development prospects in the field of food packaging. Growing studies have focused on revealing the intrinsic mechanisms of the performance of PPBFs and improving their performance, which were conducive to meeting the application requirements of different types PPBFs in various food fields (Table S3). Next, this section mainly summarizes the properties of PPBFs, which can enhance their applicability in the field of food packaging.

Mechanical properties

Mechanical properties, as the key indicator for evaluating the quality of food packaging materials, play a significant protective role in the transportation and storage of food. PPs can form a dense network structure through intermolecular hydrogen bonds, which can endow PPBFs excellent mechanical properties. The network structure can also significantly enhance the resistance of PPBFs to external physical forces and protect food from damage and deformation, thereby prolonging the shelf life of food (Cazón et al., 2017). We mainly measure and record the stress-strain curves through universal material testing machines to calculate the TS, EB, and YM of PPBFs, which can quantify the mechanical properties of PPBFs. Previous report has found that standard dumbbell-shaped/rectangular strip-shaped films were prepared by the cutting method and evaluated their ability to resist external deformation (Fig. 4A) (Lim & Gong, 2018). The results show that the films possessed excellent mechanical properties (TS of 2.27 MPa, EB of 233%, and YM of 1.73 MPa) through uniaxial tensile tests, which were conducive to the films being used for packaging various shapes of food and meeting the packaging needs of consumers. Unfortunately, compared with traditional plastics, PPBFs have poorer flexibility (Akhila & Badwaik, 2022). To address this limitation, current research mostly focuses on improving the flexibility of PPBFs through the utilization of plasticizers, cross-linking agents, and blending techniques. For instance, the pectin/glycerol plasticizing films were prepared by the SC method using the rambutan peel and glycerol as raw materials (Arroyo-Esquivel et al., 2025). Compared with the untreated film, these plasticizing films possessed higher TS (14.2%, increased by 2 times), better puncture resistance, and flexibility (Fig. 4B). Moreover, glycerol, as a typical plasticizer, could enhance the cohesion and YM of these plasticizing films, further suggesting that these plasticizing films show great application potential in the field of food packaging that requires long-term preservation. Similarly, the starch-based cross-linked films were prepared by cross-linking corn starch with polyether diamine. Polyether diamine cross-linking could significantly enhance TS (62.9 MPa), flexibility, and the adaptability of these cross-linked films in complex packaging environments, which was attributed to the cross-linking effect forming a more stable three-dimensional network structure (Cho et al., 2024). In another study, the pectin-based nano-composite films were prepared by embedding AgNPs and ZnO using apple pomace as the raw material, and evaluated their mechanical properties (Huang et al., 2024). The composite films exhibited excellent mechanical properties (TS increased to 17.8 MPa). These composite films could also perform complex actions, including bending, folding, knotting, and rolling, which could ensure the integrity of the composite films during processing, transportation, carrying, storage, and application (Fig. 4C(a)). Furthermore, the composite films could be largely degraded within 7 days and completely degraded after 28 days through soil degradation experiments, further demonstrating the excellent environmental friendliness of the composite films (Fig. 4C(b)). In summary, the excellent mechanical properties of PPBFs can effectively resist physical damage and ensure food sealing. Unfortunately, the mechanical properties of PPBFs are susceptible to humidity, which consequently affects their packaging performance in humid environments. In the future, we should focus on using new plasticizers, green cross-linking agents, and nano-composite technologies to develop PPBFs with high mechanical stability in a wet state, which can be beneficial for expanding their application scope in the field of food packaging.

Barrier properties

The barrier properties can effectively prevent the erosion of food by external factors, such as oxygen, water vapor, odors, and microorganisms, which can contribute to delaying the evaporation of food moisture and the loss of nutrients (Xue et al., 2025). The barrier properties of PPBFs mainly include the oxygen and water vapor barrier properties (Cazón et al., 2017). Among them, PPBFs have excellent oxygen barrier properties, which can inhibit the phenomenon of oxygen transfer (fat oxidation, pigment degradation, vitamin loss, and aerobic microbial growth), and extend the shelf life of food (Deol et al., 2025). Previous report has found that starch-based films were prepared by the SC method using mung bean residue as the raw material, and evaluated their oxygen barrier properties (Bangar et al., 2021). Compared with plastic packaging materials, these films possessed lower OP and better oxygen barrier properties. Unfortunately, the water vapor barrier properties of the films are prone to absorb water and swell, and affect food quality, which may be attributed to the fact that the starch molecule chains contain abundant hydrophilic groups. Unexpectedly, the hydrophilic groups of plant polysaccharide molecules can be transformed into hydrophobic groups by the chemical cross-linking method, which can improve the water vapor barrier performance of PPBFs. Duong et al. (2022) prepared SA cross-linked films by cross-linking with Ca2+ and evaluated the influence of Ca2+ on their barrier properties. Compared with pure SA films, Ca2+ endowed the SA cross-linked films with superior barrier properties, and significantly reduced WVP and OP (81%). Moreover, the SA cross-linked films could delay the water evaporation and nutrient loss of rose apples through a physical barrier, which could extend the shelf life of rose apples. Notably, the incorporation of nanofillers in PPBFs can also extend the diffusion paths of water vapor and oxygen molecules, which can improve the barrier properties of PPBFs. For instance, MXene nanosheets could significantly enhance the barrier properties of starch-based films, with WVP decreasing by 92.9% and OP decreasing by 74.0%, which could meet the standards for food packaging materials (Dong et al., 2024). Similarly, the gelatin-based nano-composite films were prepared by blending with MMT using the peel of dragon fruit as the raw material. The composite films exhibited excellent water vapor barrier properties (WVP decreased to 3.637 × 10−10 g/(m·Pa·s)) and light-blocking properties. Moreover, the composite films could completely degrade within 15 days, which could demonstrate excellent biodegradability (Jiang et al., 2022). Interestingly, PPBFs with added nanofillers can also effectively prevent the penetration of external moisture and inhibit food from getting damp, moldy, and softened, further expanding the application scope of PPBFs in the field of food packaging. In summary, PPBFs can effectively extend the shelf life of food with excellent barrier properties and maintain the flavor of the food, further indicating that PPBFs have significant application value in the field of food packaging. In the future, we should focus on utilizing nanocomposite technology and adding plant extracts and hydrophobic substances to improve the hydrophobicity of PPBFs, which are conducive to meeting consumers' demands for environmentally friendly food packaging.

Antibacterial properties

The antibacterial properties, as the key indicator of the functionality of food packaging materials, are of great significance in ensuring food safety and extending the shelf life of food. Notably, PPs can damage the cell structure of bacteria and cause the leakage of bacterial contents, which can inhibit the absorption of nutrients and energy metabolism of bacteria, thereby inhibiting the growth and reproduction of bacteria (Fig. 5A) (Zhou et al., 2022). PPBFs can effectively inhibit the growth of microorganisms (bacteria and fungi) on the inner surface of the packaging, prevent food from spoiling and deteriorating, and extend the shelf life of food. Unfortunately, compared with traditional petroleum-based plastics, PPBFs have poorer antibacterial durability. PPBFs are susceptible to environmental factors (temperature and humidity) and cause uncontrollable release of antibacterial agents, which cannot meet the food packaging requirements of consumers. To address this issue, increasing studies have focused on enhancing the antibacterial properties of PPBFs by adding antibacterial agents (chitosan, MN, and plant extracts). For instance, Machado et al. (2020) prepared CH/PE antibacterial films through incorporating chitosan using orange peels as the raw material, and evaluated the influence of CH on their antibacterial activity. The results show that the amino groups of CH interacted electrostatically with the cell wall of Escherichia coli, thereby rupturing of the bacterial membrane and causing cell death, which could enable the CH/PE antibacterial films to exhibit excellent antibacterial activity. The CH/PE antibacterial films could effectively inhibit the growth of microorganisms on the surface of tomatoes and extend the shelf life of tomatoes to 18 days. The preservation effect of the CH/PE antibacterial films also exceeded that of commercial polyvinyl chloride packaging materials, further confirming the broad prospects of CH/PE antibacterial films in the field of food packaging (Fig. 5B). Similarly, starch-based composite films were prepared by embedding ZnO nanoparticles. Compared with pure starch-based films, the inhibitory effect of the composite films on the growth of Escherichia coli and Staphylococcus aureus on the surface of grapes increased by 15.27% and 22.05%, which could effectively prolong the shelf life of grapes. The degradation rate of these composite films could also be increased by 53.33%, further expanding the application scope of the composite films in environmental friendly food packaging (Kumar et al., 2024). In another study, the SA composite films loaded with oregano essential oil and cellulose nanocrystals exhibited an inhibition rate of 98.8% against Staphylococcus aureus and 87.6% against Escherichia coli, which demonstrate the excellent antibacterial activity of the composite films (Fig. 5C(a)). Additionally, the composite films could effectively delay the natural ripening process of tomatoes and inhibit microbial-induced decay, thereby significantly extending the shelf life of tomatoes. Furthermore, the composite films could be completely degraded in the soil within 30 min, further confirming the excellent biodegradability of the composite films (Fig. 5C(b)) (Li et al., 2025). In summary, the excellent antibacterial properties of PPBFs can inhibit the growth of microorganisms, effectively prevent food from spoiling, thereby reducing the use of chemical preservatives and ensuring food safety. In the future, we should focus on developing new antibacterial agents through nanotechnology/membrane encapsulation technology to enhance their antibacterial persistence. Additionally, we can also combine bioactive components, including chitosan, plant extracts, and lactoferrin peptides to improve the broad-spectrum antibacterial properties and environmental friendliness.

Antioxidant properties

The antioxidant properties refer to the ability of packaging materials to inhibit/delay oxidation reactions, which play a crucial role in extending the shelf life of food (Pillai et al., 2024). Previous study has confirmed that the antioxidant mechanisms of PPs materials mainly regulated the expression levels of downstream antioxidant enzymes through the endogenous antioxidant stress Nrf2/ARE pathway, blocking the free radical chain reaction, and reducing the production of free radicals (Fig. S3) (Mu et al., 2021). However, the antioxidant persistence of PPBFs is poor. We can enhance the antioxidant performance of PPBFs by stabilizing the loading and achieving controlled release of antioxidants (phenols and flavonoids). For instance, Luz et al. (2023) prepared starch-based films embedded with phenolic components (PEJP) by the SC method using tomato peels and grape peels as raw materials, and evaluated the impact of PEJP on antioxidant properties. The results show that PEJP could provide hydrogen atoms and effectively neutralize free radicals, which could be beneficial for increasing the clearance rate of DPPH free radicals (91%) by the films and improving the antioxidant properties of the films. Moreover, the films appeared pink and possessed good transparency and ultraviolet-blocking ability, further suggesting that the films can meet consumers' appearance requirements for food packaging materials. Similarly, PPBFs containing phenolic substances could effectively inhibit enzymatic browning and oxidation of pigment molecules, prolong the shelf life of fresh-cut apples, and maintain the color and nutritional components of fresh-cut apples, further expanding the application scope of PPBFs in the field of food packaging (Zhang et al., 2023). In another study, the pectin-based composite films were prepared by the flash extraction method to extract the pectin and flavonoid compounds using citrus peel as the raw material. The composite films exhibited 94.44% and 78.48% scavenging activities against ABTS and DPPH free radicals, which could demonstrate good antioxidant properties. The composite films could also inhibit lipid oxidation in pork and extend the shelf life of pork (Guo et al., 2024). Interestingly, the extracts (vitamins and carotenoids) derived from plant by-products (fruit peels and vegetable residues) can be incorporated into PPBFs as antioxidants to inhibit the physiological respiration and pigment oxidation of fresh fruits, prolong the shelf life of the fruits, protect the nutritional components of the fruits, further promoting the sustainable development of the food packaging field (de Oliveira Filho et al., 2024; Sadeghi et al., 2025). However, antioxidants may introduce hydrophilic groups in the field of food packaging, which can lead to a decrease in the hydrophobicity and water vapor barrier ability of PPBFs. In the future, we should focus on applying nanocomposite technology to achieve the controlled release of antioxidants, thereby optimizing the antioxidant properties, hydrophobicity, and barrier performance of PPBFs, which can be beneficial for expanding the application scope of PPBFs in the field of food packaging. The excellent antioxidant properties of PPBFs can effectively eliminate free radicals and prevent the oxidation of food components (fat, protein, vitamins, etc.), which can contribute to maintaining the color, flavor, and nutritional value of the food, and prolonging the shelf life of the food.

Intelligent responsiveness

Intelligent responsiveness, as a core property of intelligent packaging materials, can change the physicochemical properties of the materials in response to changes in the external environment (pH, temperature, humidity, light, pressure, etc.), thus providing more scientific and intelligent packaging functions. In the field of food packaging, intelligent packaging materials can monitor the internal state of the packaged food and the environment in real time, and effectively extend the shelf life of food, which is conducive to promoting the intelligent management of the food supply chain (Cheng et al., 2022). Previous report has found that PPBFs could exhibit excellent intelligent responsiveness (temperature response, humidity response, and pH response) by incorporating responsive functional groups (Wang et al., 2023). For instance, Puttipan et al. (2023) prepared the time-temperature responsive films by extracting betaine from the rind of dragon fruits and incorporating betaine into CMC films. The results show that the responsive films exhibited a temperature-responsive color change from pink to yellow, which was conducive to real-time monitoring of the temperature and freshness of frozen foods. Moreover, the responsive films could automatically adjust the film's permeability, release antibacterial active substances, and avoid the warming and spoilage of frozen foods (Fig. 6A). In another study, the fiber-based composite films were prepared by incorporating quercetin polyphenols, zein, and cyclodextrin inclusion complexes (CD-IC). The composite films could sense changes in environmental humidity (humidity >85%), cause the dissociation of CD-IC, and slowly release the quercetin polyphenols (antibacterial active molecules) (Fig. 6B). Moreover, the composite films could reduce the transpiration of blueberries, inhibit microbial growth, and extend the shelf life of blueberries to 11 days, which could better provide intelligent packaging materials for high-humidity fresh foods. The composite films could completely degrade in soil within 13 days, which could demonstrate the excellent biodegradability of the composite films (Qu et al., 2025). Unexpectedly, PPBFs loaded with pH indicators have been widely applied in the field of intelligent food packaging. For instance, the tapioca starch-based films loaded with steviol glycosides, as the pH-sensitive films, could sense changes in environmental pH, trigger color changes in the films, and monitor the freshness of shrimp, which were attributed to the fact that fresh shrimp released volatile ammonia gas during the maturation process and caused a change in the environmental pH (Fig. 6C) (Wang et al., 2023). Similarly, starch-based composite films embedded with red amaranth anthocyanin and clove essential oil could delay the lipid oxidation and nutrient consumption of chicken through physical block, inhibit the growth of mold on the surface of chicken, and significantly extend the shelf life of chicken. The composite films also exhibited pH-responsive color changes ranging from brownish green (pH ≤ 9) to yellow (pH ≥ 10), which could monitor the freshness of chicken in real time (Wang et al., 2025). Importantly, PPBFs also have gas responsiveness, enzyme responsiveness, and light responsiveness, thus acting as data carriers (barcode labels and radio frequency identification labels), which can demonstrate great application potential in food quality inspection and information transmission (Yao et al., 2024). Unfortunately, the intelligent responsiveness of PPBFs also has limitations, such as limited response speed, insufficient stability, and high cost. To address these limitations, future research should focus on integrating nanotechnology and developing multi-response synergy mechanisms to achieve accurate environmental perception and stable responses, which can expand the application of PPBFs in the field of food intelligent monitoring.

The application of PPBFs

Growing studies have focused on the application of biobased packaging materials in the field of food packaging (Benbettaïeb et al., 2016). Notably, PPBFs are derived from plant-derived by-products (fruit peels, vegetable residues, and straw), which possess excellent biodegradability, biocompatibility, and low cost, thus demonstrating broad application potential in the field of food packaging. Previous report has confirmed that PPBFs had excellent mechanical properties and barrier properties and achieved real-time freshness monitoring by combining antibacterial agents, antioxidants, and functional responsive indicators, thus effectively extending the shelf life of food (Wei et al., 2025). This section focuses on summarizing the packaging applications of PPBFs in different types of foods (Fig. 7). Fruit and vegetable preservation

Fruits and vegetables, as the important part of the human daily diet, have seen a rapid increase in production and consumption worldwide, which is attributed to the fact that fruits and vegetables are rich in nutrients (vitamins, minerals, and dietary fiber). Unfortunately, factors (water loss, physiological metabolism, and microbial infection) can easily cause fresh fruits and vegetables to deteriorate during storage and transportation, which can lead to food waste (Du et al., 2025). We urgently need to develop more advanced biodegradable food packaging materials to preserve the quality of fruits and vegetables and extend their shelf life. Unexpectedly, PPBFs have shown excellent effects in extending the shelf life of fruits and vegetables, and maintaining their nutritional components and sensory qualities (Xue et al., 2025). For instance, Chaturvedi et al. (2019) prepared the pectin-based composite films by the SC method using apple pomace, beet pulp, and corn bran as raw materials, and evaluated their preservation effect on tomatoes. The composite films could delay water loss of tomatoes, inhibit surface microbial contamination, and significantly reduce the weight loss of tomatoes (about 30% after storage for 10 days) and the decay percentage (about 15% after storage for 30 days), thereby extending the shelf life of tomatoes (Fig. 8A). Moreover, beetroot powder endowed the composite films with excellent antioxidant activity, which could reduce the respiratory rate of tomatoes, effectively retain nutrients, including gallic acid and coumaric acid, and exhibit excellent preservation effects. Similarly, the CMC-based films loaded with nano-emulsified citral could effectively inhibit potato sprouting by automatically regulating the release of citral, and reduce the germination rate of potato tubers from 100% to 20% over a period of 28 days, which was beneficial for preventing the hydrolysis of nutrients and extending the shelf life of potatoes (Fig. 8B) (Arnon-Rips et al., 2020). Notably, PPBFs could also effectively inhibit the growth of Fusarium on the surface of bananas, significantly slow down the respiration rate of bananas, prevent the excessive release of ethylene, and reduce the weight loss of bananas, which were beneficial for maintaining the sensory quality of bananas and extending shelf life of bananas, further suggesting that PPBFs have great application potential in the packaging field of fruits and vegetables with respiratory transition (Pham et al., 2023). From the perspective of the banana packaging market, bananas are one of the fruits with the largest global trade volume. The demand for preservation and packaging of bananas after harvest is continuously increasing. The traditional packaging for bananas mainly uses polyethylene preservation bags, and the loss rate caused by ethylene ripening and microbial invasion can reach as high as 10%–15% during transportation. Unexpectedly, PPBFs can reduce the banana damage rate to below 5% by simultaneously inhibiting the growth of Fusarium and adsorbing/catalyzing the degradation of ethylene. More importantly, PPBFs exhibited excellent antibacterial, antioxidant, and anti-browning activities, which could effectively inhibit the enzymatic browning of fresh-cut apples and fresh-cut carrot slices, extend the shelf life of fresh-cut apples and fresh-cut carrots, and maintain the nutritional components (phenols), color, flavor, and aroma, which can provide a new strategy for the active packaging of fresh-cut fruits and vegetables (Chen et al., 2024; Rodriguez et al., 2024). All in all, PPBFs possess excellent biodegradability, barrier properties, antibacterial, and antioxidant properties, effectively extend the shelf life of fruits and vegetables, and reduce waste, which can fully embody the concept of “green packaging”. Future research should focus on further optimizing the extraction and preparation processes of PPs, and develop functional green modification technologies (adding plant essential oils, phenolic substances, and nanofillers) for PPBFs, which are conducive to promoting the large-scale industrial production of PPBFs.

Meat preservation

Meat, as an essential component of the food system, is rich in high-quality protein, essential amino acids, and minerals (iron and zinc), which can provide core nutrients for human growth and maintenance of functions (Jaworska & Sadowka, 2024). In the past two decades, global meat consumption has increased to 360 million tons. However, factors, such as microbial contamination, enzymatic reactions, and fat oxidation are prone to causing meat to spoil, which can account for approximately 21% of food losses (Shao et al., 2021). Based on this background, developing biobased food packaging materials and extending the shelf life and refrigeration period of meat have become promising solutions in the field of green meat preservation. Notably, PPBFs have excellent biodegradability, intelligent responsiveness, antibacterial activity, and antioxidant activity, which can show great potential for application in the meat preservation field. For instance, the composite starch/phenolic acid films were prepared by the SC method using cassava peels, wheat bran, and fruit residues as raw materials, and evaluated their preservation effect on pork (Hernández-García et al., 2022). The results show that the phenolic acids (ferulic acid, p-coumaric acid, and protocatechuic acid) endowed the composite films with excellent antibacterial activity, which could effectively inhibit the growth of Escherichia coli and lactic acid bacteria on the surface of pork, reduce the weight loss of pork, and extend the shelf life of pork. The composite films could also inhibit the oxidation of heme in pork lipids and myoglobin, and maintain the sensory quality and flavor of pork. Similarly, the starch/pectin nanocomposite films impregnated with olive oil could significantly reduce the numbers of bacteria on the surface of chicken, and reduce the weight loss of chicken (from 42.08% to 6.13%), which could delay the dehydration and hardening of chicken, maintain the color and flavor of chicken, thus effectively extending the shelf life of chicken (Afzia et al., 2025). Previous report has found that the alkaline substances (amines and ammonia produced by meat spoilage) could trigger pH-dependent color responses, which provided a new idea for the intelligent packaging design of PPBFs (Cao et al., 2023). PPBFs can precisely sense the pH changes of meat by adding pH indicators, change the color of PPBFs, and enable real-time monitoring of meat freshness. Wang et al. (2024) prepared SA composite films by incorporating anthocyanins and tea polyphenols, and conducted experiments to monitor the freshness of beef (at 4 °C). Compared with the blank control group, the composite films could extend the shelf life of beef to 8 days (nearly doubling the preservation time), which was beneficial for maintaining the bright red color and nutritional components of the beef. Additionally, the color change (from orange-red to brown) of the composite films could show a significant correlation (fitting degree of >90%) with the spoilage index of the meat through curve fitting analysis, and enable real-time monitoring of the freshness of the beef, which could provide experimental basis for the development of new meat preservation materials (Fig. 8C). In summary, PPBFs can achieve both preservation and preservation monitoring functions with their advantages of sustainability, low cost, and multi-functional integration, which can demonstrate significant application value in the field of intelligent meat packaging. Future research should combine nanocomposite technology and the addition of natural components to prepare multifunctional PPBFs, and improve the industrial chain, which is beneficial for promoting the commercialization of PPBFs in the field of meat preservation.

Seafood preservation

Seafood is rich in high-quality protein and various unique nutrients (iodine and selenium), especially the marine-derived Omega-3 fatty acids (DHA and EPA), which are scarce in terrestrial foods and beneficial for brain and cardiovascular health, thus holding an important position in global food consumption (Tan et al., 2023). However, seafood is prone to spoilage due to its high water and protein content, which can result in nearly one-third of the total output being wasted (Smaoui et al., 2024). However, traditional plastic packaging has limitations in preserving seafood, which makes it impossible to monitor the freshness of seafood in real time. Developing new types of multi-functional biobased packaging materials is of great significance. Previous report has found that the spoilage of seafood released total volatile basic nitrogen (ammonia, dimethylamine, trimethylamine etc.), which could lead to enzymatic reactions and microbial degradation of the seafood, and cause changes in pH value, which could provide a theoretical basis for the development of PPBFs loaded with functional indicators (antibiotics, antioxidants, and pH indicators) (Bhargava et al., 2020). For instance, the carrageenan/pectin composite films could effectively inhibit the growth of bacteria on the surface of salmon, delay lipid oxidation, and extend the shelf life of salmon to 8 days in the preservation test of salmon, which could maintain the nutritional components and flavor of the salmon. The purple sweet potato anthocyanins also endowed the composite films with a pH color responsiveness change from light red to silver gray to light green during the preservation process, which was conducive to real-time monitoring of the freshness of salmon, further demonstrating their excellent preservation effect (Zhang et al., 2024). Similarly, the fruit gum-based composite films were prepared through 3D printing technology using citrus peels, pumpkin seeds, oregano essential oil, and curcumin as raw materials, and evaluated their preservation effect on grass carp (Tang et al., 2024). The results show that the composite films possessed a clearance rate and inhibition rate of more than 90% and 10% for ABTS free radicals, and effectively delayed the enzymatic reactions and lipid oxidation of grass carp, which could extend the shelf life of grass carp. Moreover, 3D printing technology could prevent the degradation of curcumin due to heat, enhance the pH sensing ability, and monitor the spoilage of grass carp in real time, which would be conducive to meeting the needs of manufacturers for the preservation application of seafood. Interestingly, PPBFs embedded with nano-fillers also exhibit excellent multi-functional integration and have been widely applied in the packaging of seafood (Wang et al., 2024). Wei et al. (2023) prepared SA/nano-composite films by embedding Co-Imd microcrystals into the SA films. The composite films possessed excellent antibacterial activity, ammonia sensitivity, and color stability, which could effectively extend the shelf life of shrimp and monitor the freshness of shrimp in real time (Fig. 8D). Unexpectedly, the SA/nanomaterial composite films could also be used to package fresh fish slices, including mackerel and silver carp slices, extend the shelf life of the fish slices, maintain the flavor, color, and nutritional content of the fish slices, further indicating that the SA/nanomaterial composite films have broad prospects in the field of seafood packaging (Du et al., 2024; Shahbazi & Shavisi, 2019). All in all, PPBFs can achieve both preservation and preservation monitoring functions by integrating functional indicators. However, the commercialization of intelligent food packaging materials is subject to strict regulatory requirements. In the European Union, functional indicators must undergo a pre-market safety assessment and authorization by EFSA in accordance with Regulation (EC) No 450/2009 before they can be included in the EU list of permitted substances. Future research should focus on combining bio-functional indicators that meet the safety assessment requirements of EFSA with nanotechnology to prepare new PPBFs, which can achieve highly sensitive responses to the freshness of seafood and long-term preservation.

Other food preservation

In the field of food packaging, apart from the preservation of fruits, vegetables, meat, and seafood, the preservation of other foods (baked goods, dairy products, beverages, processed meat products, etc.) also plays an indispensable role. The packaging materials for these foods are mainly traditional petroleum-based plastics, which is attributed to the fact that traditional petroleum-based plastics possess excellent flexibility, printing suitability, and low cost characteristics (Bhargava et al., 2020). Unfortunately, traditional petroleum-based plastics have the limitations, including having a single functional characteristic and being non-biodegradable, which are unable to meet the specific requirements of these foods for intelligent preservation functions (anti-aging, freshness indication, nutrient monitoring, and antioxidant properties) and green packaging (Deng et al., 2024). To address this limitation, increasing studies are dedicated to developing biobased packaging materials in response to the dual challenges posed by traditional petroleum-based plastics in the field of food packaging (lack of functionality and environmental sustainability), which is attributed to the excellent mechanical properties, barrier properties, biodegradability, and multi-functional integration of PPBFs. Previous report has found that the starch-based composite films embedded with CNF-AgNPs exhibited excellent barrier properties, antibacterial activity, and biocompatibility (the cell viability of 87.71%), which were beneficial for delaying moisture loss and starch aging in bread, inhibiting mold growth on the surface of the bread, extending the shelf life of the bread to 10 days, and maintaining the original aroma of the bread (Fig. 8E). The composite films also possessed high transparency and extensibility, and the composite films could closely adhere to irregularly shaped bread, further suggesting that the composite films meet the standards for packaging materials for baked foods (Yuan et al., 2021). Interestingly, PPBFs exhibit excellent intelligent monitoring and preservation capabilities in the fields of dairy product and beverage packaging. For instance, starch-based films loaded with carotene from carrots, as pH-sensitive films, could undergo color visual transitions from dark blue (fresh milk) to blue-violet (moderately fresh milk) and then to purple (spoiled milk), enabling real-time monitoring of milk freshness, thus highlighting the unique advantages of the film in milk packaging (Goodarzi et al., 2020). Similarly, Perera et al. (2023) prepared SA/gelatin films by incorporating nanoclay and clove essential oil into SA, and evaluated their preservation effect on cheese (cheddar hard cheese). The composite films possessed strong ultraviolet blocking performance, moisture-proof performance, and antibacterial activity, which could effectively reduce the weight loss percentage of the cheese (still below 5% after 30 days of storage), extend the shelf life of the cheese, and maintain the original color and flavor of the cheese. In addition, the composite films completely degraded within 45 days through soil degradation experiments, further demonstrating the environmental friendliness of the composite films. In another study, the pectin-based films doped with nano-clay and methylene blue, as a sensor, exhibited a color-responsive change from blue to colorless, which was attributed to the redox reaction between methylene blue and vitamin C. Based on this response mechanism, the films could accurately measure (accuracy of >90%) the vitamin content in orange juice, kiwi juice, and orange juice, which could provide a feasible basis for achieving the dual functions (preservation and determination of nutrient content) of packaging materials for juices (Pirsa, 2020). Unexpectedly, PPBFs also have been widely applied in the packaging field of processed meat products, which is attributed to their excellent antioxidant activity. The starch/carbon nanosphere composite films were prepared by SC using corn husk and carrot residue as raw materials. The composite films possessed a clearance rate of up to 99.9% for DPPH free radicals, which could effectively delay the fat oxidation of fried meat balls and extend the shelf life of fried meat balls (Fig. 8F). Carbon nanospheres could also endow the composite films good elasticity and enhance the chewing performance of fried meat balls, further confirming that the composite films meet the packaging requirements of processed meat products (Wu et al., 2024). In summary, PPBFs can effectively extend the shelf life of food through various means (moisture prevention, antibacterial, and antioxidant properties), further ensuring food safety. Future research should focus on exploring the recycling technology of PPBFs, which is conducive to building a sustainable food packaging system.

Conclusions and prospects

PPs mainly come from plant-derived by-products, including fruit peels, vegetable residues, and agricultural waste, which have advantages, such as low cost, non-toxicity, biodegradability, biocompatibility, and excellent film-forming properties. PPs have been widely applied in the field of PPBFs preparation. PPBFs represent the cutting-edge direction in the development of food packaging materials, which is attributed to the fact that PPBFs can highly utilize PPs and play significant roles in protecting the environment, recycling resources, ensuring food safety, and maintaining human health. This article includes 151 representative papers through scientific literature search and screening methods, and provides a comprehensive systematic review of the carrier materials, preparation techniques, modification strategies, properties, and applications of PPBFs in various food preservation packaging. Firstly, this paper provides a detailed overview of the molecular structures and physicochemical properties of PPs (pectin, cellulose, starch, HC, and alginate), which is beneficial for selecting appropriate carrier materials and laying the foundation for specific food packaging requirements. Secondly, this review focuses on the application scope, unique advantages, and limitations of the preparation techniques (SC, EBM, electrospinning method, 3D printing technology, and LLA) and modification strategies (physical modification, chemical modification, and nano-composite modification) of PPBFs, which can promote the large-scale production of PPBFs. Subsequently, this article comprehensively elaborates on the mechanical properties, barrier properties, antibacterial characteristics, antioxidant properties, and intelligent responsiveness of PPBFs, further expanding the application value of PPBFs. Finally, this paper summarizes the applications of PPBFs in the preservation packaging (fruits, meats, seafood, and other foods), and proposes potential future research directions to expand the application market of PPBFs in the field of food packaging.

Compared with the previously published reviews on polysaccharide-based food packaging, this article has the following innovations and uniqueness: 1) This paper adds a new chapter titled “Methods of literature search and screening” to the similar topic section, which can enable a clear description of the database, keywords, inclusion criteria, and screening process, thus significantly enhancing the academic rigor and representativeness of the review; 2) In terms of carrier materials, most of the existing reviews focus on common polysaccharides (chitosan and SA). However, this review takes “PPs” as an independent theme, systematically integrates five types of polysaccharides derived from waste materials (fruit peels, vegetable residue, and agricultural waste stalks), thus highlighting the dual circular economy concepts of “high-value utilization of waste” and “green packaging”; 3) In terms of preparation techniques, this paper systematically reviews the cutting-edge preparation technologies, including 3D printing and LLA, and their respective application scopes and limitations in terms of scale, which can make up for the deficiency of existing reviews that mostly focus on traditional methods; 4) In terms of the modification strategies, this article separates “modification strategies” into a separate chapter, systematically elaborates on the action mechanisms, performance improvement effects, and applicable scenarios of each method from three dimensions (physics, chemistry, and nanocomposites), which can provide researchers with a clearer logic for choosing technologies; 5) In terms of functional characteristics and applications, this review establishes a logical chain of “matrix material-preparation/modification-functional characteristics-food type”, clarifies the compatibility relationship between the characteristics and various food preservation requirements. However, existing reviews still lack systematic organization in this regard; 6) From an industrial perspective, this paper discusses key industrial issues (the scalability, cost-effectiveness, regulatory challenges, and consumer acceptance) of PPBFs, which provide a reference framework for the transformation of laboratory research results into industrial applications. All in all, this paper demonstrates distinct characteristics that set it apart from existing literature in terms of methodological normativity, the focus on “waste valorization” theme, the cutting-edge nature of preparation techniques, the systematicity of modification strategies, the clarity of application logic, and supplementary perspective from the manufacturing process, which can provide more targeted theoretical references and technical paths for researchers engaged in the high-value utilization of plant-derived by-products and the research on biodegradable food packaging.

PPBFs have demonstrated significant advantages in the field of food packaging, which can meet various packaging requirements for different foods. Unfortunately, the practical application of PPBFs still faces multiple challenges in transitioning from the laboratory to the industrial sector as follows: 1) The properties of raw materials (plant-derived by-products) fluctuate depending on the source and seasonal factors, which may affect the uniformity of PPBFs; 2) The extraction process of PPs, preparation, and modification technologies of films are not yet mature, which can limit the large-scale production of PPBFs; 3) The controllability of biodegradation rate is poor, which may shorten the service life of the films; 4) The migration risks of the chemical crosslinking agents and nanofillers embedded in PPBFs require further regulatory assessment. To address the aforementioned issues, future researches should focus on the following directions: 1) Establish a raw material database and classification (origin, variety, and season), form a “raw material map”, and conduct batch mixing, which are conducive to obtaining stable and uniform mixed raw materials; 2) Optimize the PPs extraction process (microwave-assisted and ultrasound-assisted extraction technologies), film preparation technology (develop continuous film preparation and low-temperature extrusion technologies), and modification technologies (utilize multi-component synergistic film formation and nano-composite technology), which can reduce costs and optimize the performance of PPBFs; 3) Introduce enzyme cleavage sites, soluble bonds, and control cross-linking density, which can enable the controlled degradation of PPBFs in compost and soil; 4) Add biobased cross-linking agents (phytic acid and citric acid) and low-migration nano-composite materials, which can effectively reduce safety risks. We can also provide visual information on food freshness and policy support to enhance consumers' trust and purchasing intention, which can increase the market recognition of PPBFs. Table S5 shows the specific research objectives and feasible technical approaches. In summary, we are hopeful to overcome the current challenges and achieve the wide application of PPBFs in the field of food packaging through continuous research and technological innovation. The market demand for PPBFs will keep growing as people's awareness of food safety and environmental protection continues to increase, which can also provide a broad space for the industrialization development of PPBFs, further suggesting that PPBFs are expected to become a substitute for traditional petroleum-based plastics and offer consumers safer, healthier, and more environmentally friendly food packaging solutions. We expect that more researchers will join this field in the future, work together to realize the vision of high-value utilization of plant-derived by-products, and promote the development of PPBFs food packaging technology, which can provide key technical support for the construction of a resource-saving and environmentally-friendly sustainable food packaging system.

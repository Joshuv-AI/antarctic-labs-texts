Optimizing FBS 3D positions for sum rate maximization in downlink NOMA 6G network
Authors: Osamah Thamer Hassan Alzubaidi, Hayder Faeq Alhashimi, Salah Alheejawi, Mhd Nour Hindia, Kaharudin Dimyati, Kamarul Ariffin Noordin, Ahmed Nasser
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The explosive growth of internet of everything (IoE) devices and the increasing demand for ultra-reliable, high-capacity wireless connectivity pose significant challenges to next-generation networks. Flying base stations (FBSs) make a significant contribution to the physical layer of vehicular communication by improving connectivity among different transportation systems. However, the mobility of FBSs and the IoE devices frequently disrupt communication links, impairing the performance of wireless communication. This issue can be addressed through strategic FBS positioning. In this paper, we propose a transmission structure based on multi-FBS with non-orthogonal multiple access (NOMA) in downlink 6G networks, where NOMA is employed at each FBS to provide services for IoE devices. Our objective is to maximize the total sum rate (TSR) by minimizing the inter/intra-cluster interference. To this end, the three-dimensional (3D) positions of the FBS are optimized in such a way that the FBS consistently remains at the center of the IoE devices’ locations even when their locations change within the network. The optimization problem is non-convex optimization problem due to the optimization of FBSs’ 3D positions. To address this issue, a genetic algorithm-based evolutionary algorithm is developed. Moreover, a perfect successive interference cancellation (SIC) strategy is introduced to address NOMA-SIC among IoE devices. The simulation results state that our proposed algorithm outperforms the other state-of-the-art in terms of TSR, achieving up to 21.03% higher TSR compared to Annealing, block coordinate descent, modified gray wolf optimization, and the center-of-cluster approach, thus demonstrating the advantages of optimizing the 3D positions of FBSs in this manner.

Introduction

The rapid expansion of IoE, along with numerous emerging applications and scenarios such as holographic communication2,3, cooperative robots4, orbital and deep-sea exploration tourism5, and extended reality6, has generated unparalleled demands for ubiquitous access7, high spectral efficiency (SE) connectivity8,9, high-speed communication services10, and heterogeneous wireless data traffic11,12. To satisfy these demands, sixth generation multiple access (6GMA) strategies have been thoroughly investigated as a crucial facilitator for accommodating an extensive number of connected user equipment and attaining enhanced SE13,14. For instance, hybrid multiple-access architectures have recently been proposed for heterogeneous networks, introducing novel transceiver designs and asynchronous modes to improve flexibility and scalability15.

Within this context, non-orthogonal multiple access (NOMA) has emerged as a key member of the 6GMA family and a strong candidate for 6G networks. Unlike orthogonal multiple access (OMA) strategies, NOMA allows several users to be served simultaneously on the same time–frequency resources16,17. This is achieved by implementing superposition coding at the transmitter with appropriate power coefficients and employing successive interference cancellation (SIC) at the receiver to eliminate intra-user interference (IUI)18. In addition to its theoretical advantages, NOMA has also been validated through comprehensive performance evaluations and experimental trials19.This mechanism enables the simultaneous service of far users (FUs) with poor channel conditions and near users (NUs) with strong channel conditions. Current literature demonstrates that NOMA offers superior SE compared with conventional OMA, avoids the OMA’s intricate scheduling schemes, reduces latency, and enables more flexible and quality of service (QoS) through power allocation (PA). Nevertheless, the coexistence of near and far users poses new challenges, as the base station (BS) must allocate higher transmission power (PT) to FUs20, which leads to greater path loss (PL) and stronger IUI for edge users (EUs). Recent efforts have also explored integrating physical-layer security and simultaneous wireless information and power transfer (SWIPT) into NOMA systems, for example by using artificial-noise–aided secure transmission in cooperative SWIPT–NOMA networks21.

A promising way to overcome these limitations is by introducing flying base stations (FBSs). Optimizing the three-dimensional (3D) positions of FBSs ensures that they can dynamically adjust to the locations of IoE devices, remaining close to the device cluster center even as user positions change. This adaptive deployment strategy helps achieve fair PA among IoE devices, preserves the effectiveness of NOMA with accurate SIC, and enables more efficient interference management (IM), thereby satisfying diverse QoS requirements.

To achieve this optimization effectively, robust algorithms are required that can handle the non-convex nature of the problem. In this study, we employ a developed genetic algorithm (DGA), a class of evolutionary algorithms (EAs) inspired by natural selection. Genetic algorithms (GAs) are particularly well suited for solving non-convex optimization problems (NCOPs) due to their strong global search capability and flexibility in handling complex constraints22,23. By evolving a population of potential solutions using selection, crossover, and mutation mechanisms24, GA enables the discovery of near-optimal 3D FBS positions, which are critical for maximizing the total sum rate (TSR) and minimizing interference in NOMA-based 6G systems. The robustness and adaptability of GAs make them a powerful tool for handling the non-linearity and dimensional complexity associated with large-scale IoE deployments.

Related works

This section presents the most recent research conducted on the subjects of FBS25–33 and FBS with intelligent reflection surfaces (IRSs)34–41. In25 a new wireless communication system enabled by several aerial BSs in a 3D environment is developed. Aerial BS’s 3D position and PT are jointly optimized to minimize the hover time while maintaining the minimum data rate required for internet of things (IoT) equipment. The joint optimization is NCOP. To address this problem, a hierarchical reinforcement learning-based Markov decision process (MDP) is proposed. The training procedure of the proposed technique is divided into two phases, both employing the proximal policy optimization scheme for training. The simulation results stated that the proposed technique outperformed the conventional deep reinforcement learning in terms of testing and training phases.

The authors in26 investigated a wireless communication system aided by a mobile BS. The mobile BS’s trajectory and resource allocation (RA) are jointly optimized to satisfy the QoS requirements of end users with minimum PT while minimizing the mobile BS’s operation time. The joint optimization is NCOP. To solve this problem, a proximal policy optimization 2-based deep reinforcement learning (DRL) scheme is proposed. The numerical results stated that the proposed scheme demonstrates exceptional performance with less time complexity.

A downlink (DL) transmission scenario based on aerial BS is designed in27 to tackle the issue of limited energy resources in aerial BSs. The simultaneous wireless information and PT technique is used to allow the aerial BS to simultaneously transmit data and energy to IoT equipment. The aerial BS 3D positions, communication scheduling, and power-splitting ratio are jointly optimized to maximize the energy efficiency (EE), thereby minimizing the aerial BS’s energy consumption while preserving the target user’s QoS. The joint optimization is NCOP. To tackle this problem, a DRL-based MDP strategy is suggested. The simulation results stated that the proposed strategy efficiently optimizes the aerial BS’s 3D positions and substantially improves EE compared with other traditional DRL strategies.

Feng et al.28 proposed the strategy of deploying unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) to address communication links’ recurrent disruptions. The integrated sensing and communication-extended Kalman filter technique is proposed to improve the localization deployment capability of drone swarms. The numerical results stated that the proposed technique achieves superior performance regarding network capacity and data rate, although ground demand fluctuates.

The application of an emergency communication system using UAVs is investigated in29, where one UAV serves as a relay to enhance uplink (UL) information transmission. The UAV’s PA and 3D positions, as well as ground user communication scheduling and association, are jointly optimized to maximize the average data rate in UL transmission. The optimization problem is NCOP. To address this issue, the strategies based on conventional convex optimization and reinforcement learning are proposed. The block coordinate descent (BCD) and successive convex approximation schemes are employed in the convex optimization strategy, while the soft actor-critic scheme is utilized in the reinforcement learning strategy. The simulation results indicated that the proposed strategies are effectively able to address the suggested optimization problem and yield favorable results.

In30 a scenario of a communication system assisted by UAV is suggested to improve the services of communication. The UAV 3D positions, PA, and time-slot allocation are jointly optimized to maximize the TSR of the suggested scenario. The joint optimization is NCOP. To tackle this issue, an effective alternating optimization strategy is proposed. The numerical results demonstrated that the proposed strategy is superior to the existing strategies.

The authors in31 proposed a wireless communication scenario aided by multiple aerial BS restricted by no-fly zones to enhance DL data transmission. The aerial BS’s PA and 3D positions are jointly optimized to maximize the aerial BS’s transmission rate and minimize the user’s average end age of information. The joint optimization problem is NCOP. To tackle this issue, a multi-agent worst-case soft actor-critic strategy with a distributional safety-critic is utilized. The numerical results showed that the proposed strategy minimizes the security threats during the operation of aerial BS while preserving similar performance levels in comparison with the multi-agent soft actor-critic strategy.

Souza et al.32 investigated the performance of cell-free massive multiple-input multiple-output aided by a swarm UAV to provide more consistent coverage and enhance both SE and EE. The UAV’s 3D positions are optimized based on a heuristic strategy to enhance the UL and DL transmission in the proposed scenario, thereby maximizing users’ SE. Furthermore, a discrete-time Markov chain is utilized to model users’ mobility. The numerical results indicated that the suggested strategy has a significant increase in SE, particularly in scenarios with low user density compared with the fixed UAV setup.

A two-way relaying scenario in which many UAVs and several pairs of users exchange information, considering the interference among various UAVs is investigated in33. The UAV 3D positions, the PT, and the parameters for transmission scheduling are jointly optimized to maximize the TSR of users. The joint optimization problem is NCOP. To address this problem, a BCD-based iterative strategy is employed. The simulation results showed that the proposed scenario is superior to other conventional scenarios in terms of TSR for various user distributions.

In34 a scenario for non-terrestrial BS assisted by intelligent reflection surfaces (IRSs) with NOMA in DL heterogeneous networks (HetNets) is employed to improve the DL transmitted signals. The IRS reflection matrix and SIC among users are jointly optimized to maximize the TSR, while the non-terrestrial BS positions are located in the center of the cluster with a height of 80 m. The joint optimization is NCOP. To tackle this problem, a meta-heuristic strategy based on gray wolf optimization (GWO) is suggested. The numerical results indicated that the proposed strategy outperforms the conventional strategies in terms of TSR.

Alzubaidi et al.35 employed an innovative scenario for non-terrestrial BS assisted by IRSs with NOMA in DL HetNets to improve the DL transmitted signals. The non-terrestrial BS 3D positions, IRS reflection matrix, and SIC among users are jointly optimized to maximize the TSR. The joint optimization is NCOP. To tackle this problem, a meta-heuristic strategy based on modified GWO (MGWO) is suggested. The numerical results indicated that the proposed strategy outperforms the conventional strategies in terms of TSR.

The authors in36 investigated a UAV aided by an IRS with NOMA in DL HetNets to support different groups of users with the assistance of an IRS. The UAV 3D positions, the UAV PT, the IRS reflection matrix, and the decoding order (DEO) among users are jointly optimized to maximize the sum rate of the proposed scenario. The joint optimization is NCOP. To address this issue, an iterative algorithm based on BCD is suggested. The numerical results stated that the proposed scheme outperforms conventional schemes in terms of sum rate.

The UAV aided by an IRS in NOMA HetNets is proposed in37 to maximize the TSR when the direct path is blocked. The IRS position, the IRS reflection matrix, the UAV altitude, and the UAV power splitting factor are jointly optimized to maximize the TSR of the proposed system. The joint optimization is NCOP. To address this issue, a maximizing TSR-based iterative optimization is suggested, taking into account the BS PT, UAV PT, and the SIC among users. The simulation results showed that the proposed scheme outperforms conventional schemes in terms of TSR.

The authors in38 employed a scenario for multiple aerial BS supported by IRS in DL NOMA HetNets to enhance the DL transmission signals. The aerial BS 3D positions, the aerial BS PT, the IRS reflection matrix, and the DEO among users are jointly optimized to maximize the TSR and total energy efficiency (TEE). The joint optimization is NCOP. To address this issue, a meta-heuristic strategy based on MGWO, and an alternating max/min strategy based on the developed BCD is suggested. The numerical results stated that the suggested strategies outperform conventional strategies in terms of TSR and TEE.

The marine edge computing network aided by IRS installed on UAV is studied in39 to facilitate DL transmission using the NOMA strategy. The UAV 3D positions, sub-slot duration, IRS reflection matrix, PA transmission, the ability allocation of edge computation, and offloading ratios are jointly optimized to minimize the total energy consumption (TEC). The joint optimization is NCOP. To tackle this problem, the original subproblem is divided into two subproblems. The first subproblem is addressed using a semi-definite relaxation technique, while the second subproblem is addressed utilizing a deep deterministic policy gradient strategy. The simulation results stated that the suggested strategy can significantly and efficiently minimize the TEC compared with the conventional strategies.

Tran et al.40 presented a new communication system scenario for UAV aided by active IRS to improve DL transmission while simultaneously reducing channel impairments in intricate environments. To maximize the average SR of the proposed scenario, the joint optimization of UAV 3D positions, PA transmission, active IRS reflection matrix, and strategies for managing the resources are suggested, considering the ARIS system’s power budget and backhaul constraints. The joint optimization problem is NCOP. To address this issue, a block coordinate ascent is utilized to separate the original problem into three interconnected subproblems and utilize inner approximation methods to efficiently tackle the NC that is inherent in each subproblem. In addition, a comprehensive approach utilizing the alternating optimization framework is devised to iteratively address these subproblems. The numerical results showed that the proposed strategy achieves higher average SR compared to other conventional strategies.

In41 a new communication scenario for multiple UAVs supported by IRSs is proposed to improve the QoS, stability, and coverage area for the proposed scenario. The joint optimization of UAV 3D positions and the IRS reflection matrix is suggested to maximize the time average total rate (TATR) depending on channel state information. The optimization problem is NCOP. To address this issue, a two-phase real-time IRS partitioning strategy is implemented. The numerical results stated that the suggested strategy outperforms the other traditional schemes in terms of TATR in the proposed scenario.

While prior literature has advanced UAV placement, trajectory optimization, NOMA RA, and IRS-assisted schemes, several important limitations remain. Many studies consider a single aerial BS or simplify placement to two dimensional (2D)/static positions, and others optimize trajectory or RA in isolation rather than jointly. Works that include IRS typically address a different design space (IRS reflection matrix + single UAV placement) and therefore do not resolve the multi-FBS placement problem in dense IoE environments. In addition, several methods focus on a single performance metric (e.g., SE or EE) or rely on solution heuristics that are prone to premature convergence, leaving open the problem of jointly maximizing TSR while ensuring IM, PA fairness, and TEE in dynamic multi-FBS NOMA networks with realistic edge- IoE device requirements. These limitations motivate the need for a robust, adaptive framework that dynamically optimizes multi-FBS 3D positions and jointly addresses TSR, IM, PA fairness, and TEE. This is the focus of the present study. Building on these observations, the research motivation and key contributions of this study are presented in the next section.

Research motivation and contributions

To address the above-mentioned gaps, the key distinction of our work compared to prior studies lies in tackling the challenge of determining the optimal 3D positions of FBSs in 6G networks while simultaneously leveraging NOMA and perfect SIC techniques. To the best of our knowledge, no prior research considers the positioning of FBSs in such a way that they consistently remain at the center of the IoE devices’ locations even as these locations change within the network. Moreover, most existing studies assume a single FBS, do not account for the scalability challenges posed by massive network density, and insufficiently address the requirements of EUs. To overcome these issues, this work proposes a multi-FBS-aided transmission structure with NOMA DL in 6G networks. A GA-based EA is developed to optimize the FBSs’ 3D positions such that they adapt dynamically to user distribution, thereby ensuring fair PA strategy among IoE devices, preserving the optimal operation of NOMA, and enabling accurate SIC. Furthermore, the GA-based optimization framework is designed to achieve efficient IM, supporting the QoS requirements of IoE devices and making the proposed structure suitable for applications such as extensive coverage areas, ultra-dense connectivity, vehicular networks, and disaster recovery scenarios. The results state the actual scalability of the proposed method, emphasizing the trade-offs between FBSs’ 3D positions and the distribution of IoE devices.

Proposed algorithm

The objective of the proposed method is to maximize TSR and preserve the optimal operation of NOMA while ensuring impartial PA among IoE devices. This is accomplished by optimizing the FBSs’ 3D positions, ensuring they remain close to IoE devices while adapting to their dynamic positions in the network. A GA-based EA is developed by modifying the selection, crossover, and mutation processes. First, tournament selection is employed instead of traditional roulette wheel selection. This method involves the random selection of three individuals, and the superior candidate is chosen to ensure the advancement of stronger candidates. Second, the Blend Crossover \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:(BLX-\alpha\:)$$\end{document} mechanism is utilized in place of traditional single-point or uniform crossover. This mechanism creates progeny as a weighted combination of two parents, allowing for smoother transitions in FBSs’ 3D positions and improving environmental adaptability. Finally, a Gaussian mutation is implemented instead of a conventional mutation, which uniformly applies small, random changes. This mutation introduces normally distributed noise to the positions of progeny in order to preserve diversity and prevent premature convergence. In addition, the FBSs’ 3D positions are constrained within predetermined boundaries to ensure viable solutions, thereby improving their practicality in real-world networks. These modifications collectively improve the quality of the solution, the speed of convergence, and the robustness of FBS positioning for maximizing the TSR and enhancing IM in DL-NOMA 6G networks. Regarding convergence, DGA does not provide global optimality but yields near-optimal solutions by iterative evolution, with convergence speed significantly influenced by parameter tuning. Moreover, DGA generally demands more computational resources than MGWO due to its stochastic nature, and it is considerably more intensive than BCD and Annealing, as it operates on a full population of candidate solutions with iterative evolutionary operations, while BCD and Annealing rely on sequential updates and single-solution exploration, respectively. Similarly, Annealing is computationally efficient and capable of escaping local optima in early stages, but its convergence and final solution quality are highly dependent on the cooling schedule, which must be carefully tuned to avoid premature convergence or excessive run time.

Scalability is a challenge in DGA, particularly as the number of FBSs or IoE devices increases, resulting in a combinatorial expansion in the search space. The challenges incudes early convergence, sensitivity to parameter configurations, and the necessity for extensive iterations to refine solutions. To enhance the speed of convergence and the quality of the solution while minimizing computational cost, potential adaptations for broader practical use include incorporation with MGWO or reinforcement learning. Alternatively, incorporating elements from BCD could simplify local search phases through coordinate-wise refinement, while integrating Annealing-based acceptance criteria may help escape local optima and improve convergence stability in highly non-convex environments. Furthermore, DGA’s effectiveness in real-world FBSs’ 3D positioning scenarios can be enhanced through the integration of adaptive parameter tuning and parallel computation.

Conclusions and future works

In this paper, we deploy a multi-FBS-based transmission structure with NOMA in DL 6G networks, where NOMA is employed at each FBS to provide services for IoE devices. The maximization of TSR for the proposed system is accomplished by optimizing the 3D positions of the FBS in such a way that the FBS consistently remains at the center of the IoE devices’ locations even when their locations change within the network. Furthermore, a GA-based EA is developed to address the formulated NCOP. Finally, a perfect SIC strategy is performed to address NOMA-SIC among IoE devices. The numerical results showed that our proposed algorithm outperforms the other state-of-the-art (i.e., Annealing, BCD, MGWO and CoC) in terms of TSR, thus indicating the benefits of optimizing the 3D positions of FBSs in this manner. Specifically, the proposed method achieves up to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:21.03\text{\%}$$\end{document} higher TSR compared to these benchmark schemes across varied IoE densities, SNR regimes, and PL conditions. Despite these promising results, the study has several limitations: the assumption of perfect SIC may not hold in practice, the analysis is based solely on simulations without considering backhaul or hardware constraints, and the focus on TSR leaves other key performance metrics such as latency and fairness unaddressed. Future research will integrate the joint optimization of FBS PT and 3D positioning to enhance EE, while also relaxing the perfect SIC assumption, incorporating practical deployment constraints such as backhaul and mobility, and exploring hybrid learning-based approaches to improve scalability in ultra-dense 6G networks. These directions will contribute to developing a more adaptive and comprehensive FBS-supported communication system for future wireless networks.

Phylogenetic Diversity, Pathogenicity and Host Range of Verticillium longisporum and V. dahliae Associated with Verticillium Wilt of Chinese Cabbage in Korea
Authors: Nazila Saadati, Jeomsoon Kim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Verticillium wilt, caused by Verticillium dahliae and V. longisporum, has emerged as a serious threat to Chinese cabbage production in Korea. To clarify the population structure, pathogenicity, and host range of these pathogens, we collected 60 isolates from major Chinese cabbage-growing regions and conducted multilocus phylogenetic analyses based on five nuclear genes. Pathogenicity assays were carried out on Chinese cabbage, and host range assays were conducted on diverse crops. The results showed that V. longisporum lineage A1/D1, the predominant lineage in Europe and North America, dominated across all surveyed regions, while a single A1/D3 lineage isolate was detected for the first time in Korea. Pathogenicity tests demonstrated that V. longisporum was generally more aggressive on Chinese cabbage than V. dahliae, with the A1/D3 isolate displaying the highest virulence among all isolates tested. Host range assays revealed that V. longisporum was restricted to cruciferous crops, whereas V. dahliae infected crucifers as well as solanaceous and several other crops, indicating broader epidemiological risks. Non-host crops such as barley, buckwheat, carrot, maize, and sudangrass were not infected by either species, suggesting their potential use in crop rotation. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that V. longisporum, particularly lineage A1/D1, is the major causal agent of Verticillium wilt epidemics in Korean Chinese cabbage production. The detection of aggressive A1/D3 lineage and genetically diverse V. dahliae populations underscores additional long-term risks that necessitate continuous monitoring, resistance breeding, and integrated disease management.

Chinese cabbage (Brassica rapa L. subsp. pekinensis (Lour.) Hanelt) is one of the most important leafy vegetables in Korea, China, and Japan, belonging to the family Brassicaceae (Ramchiary and Lim, 2011). The crop is widely cultivated for its nutritional value, economic importance, and central role in traditional foods such as kimchi (Cheigh et al., 1994; Park et al., 2014; Tan et al., 2023). In South Korea, Chinese cabbage is grown year-round, with the largest acreage in autumn, followed by spring, summer, and winter. In highland areas above 600 m, where the cool climate enables summer production, Chinese cabbage is the most profitable crop. However, the cultivation area for summer Chinese cabbage has decreased from 4,447 ha in 2010 to 3,483 ha in 2024, representing a reduction of approximately 21.6% (MAFRA, 2024). This decline may be related to cultivation difficulties, possibly arising from physiological disorders associated with rising temperatures and from an increased prevalence of soilborne diseases.

Verticillium wilt is a destructive vascular disease caused by soilborne fungi of the genus Verticillium, leading to substantial yield losses in diverse crops worldwide (Pegg and Brady, 2002). The pathogens invade the xylem, disrupt water transport, and cause wilting, vascular discoloration, and stunted growth.

Among them, V. dahliae and V. longisporum are the primary species affecting brassicaceous crops, differing in their genetic background and epidemiology. Previous studies have shown that V. dahliae displays high genetic diversity and adaptability, often forming host-specific pathogenicity groups (Inderbitzin et al., 2011; Usami et al., 2007). In contrast, V. longisporum originated from interspecific hybridization and forms stable lineages such as A1/D1, A1/D2, and A1/D3, which differ in host range and geographical distribution (Depotter et al., 2016). Verticillium wilt of Chinese cabbage was first reported in Japan in 1966 (Watanabe et al., 1973), and subsequent studies confirmed the involvement of both V. dahliae and V. longisporum in production areas (Ikeda et al., 2012). In China, the disease was initially attributed to V. dahliae (Han et al., 2012), but later work demonstrated V. longisporum as the main causal agent (Yu et al., 2015). In Korea, Verticillium wilt of Chinese cabbage caused by V. dahliae was first identified in Taebaek, Gangwon Province, in 2014 (Dumin et al., 2021), but little is known about the distribution and pathogenic traits of both pathogens, including the occurrence of V. longisporum.

Therefore, this study was conducted to investigate Verticillium wilt in Chinese cabbage, with particular emphasis on highland production areas where cultivation has recently declined. Specifically, we aimed (i) to identify pathogens associated with symptomatic plants through phylogenetic analyses, (ii) to evaluate their pathogenicity on Chinese cabbage, and (iii) to determine their host range.

Materials and Methods

Fungal collection and isolation

From 2022 to 2024, plant samples showing distinct chlorosis and wilting symptoms were collected from highland summer cultivation areas in Gangwon-do Province (Taebaek-si, Gangneung-si, and Jeongseon-gun), from spring and autumn cultivation areas in Yeongwol-gun (Nam-myeon, Buk-myeon, and Gimsatgat-myeon) in Gangwon-do, and from a spring cultivation site in Yeongyang-gun (Seokbo-myeon) in Gyeongsangbuk-do Province. For pathogen isolation, small pieces (approximately 5 × 5 mm) were aseptically excised from discolored vascular tissues of leaves and roots, surface-sterilized in 1% sodium hypochlorite solution (NaOCl) for 1 min, and rinsed three times in sterile distilled water. The samples were dried on sterile filter paper and placed on water agar (WA), followed by incubation in the dark at 25°C for 5–7 days. After confirming spore formation under a microscope, a single germinating spore was transferred from WA to potato dextrose agar (PDA; BD Difco, Sparks, MD, USA) to obtain a single-spore isolate. The isolates were maintained on PDA slants at 10°C for experimental use and preserved in 20% glycerol at −80°C for long-term storage. In total, five isolates were deposited in the Korean Agricultural Culture Collection (KACC) of the National Institute of Agricultural Sciences.

DNA extraction, PCR amplification, and sequencing

Genomic DNA was extracted from the total 60 Verticillium strains using Fungi/Yeast Genomic DNA Isolation Kit (SKU 27300; Norgen Biotek Corp., Thorold, ON, Canada) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. PCR amplification was carried out using Inclone™ Excel TB 2X Premix with dye (Inclone Biotech, Seongnam, Korea). The PCR program consisted of an initial denaturation at 94°C for 2 min, followed by 32 cycles of 94°C for 10 s, primer-specific annealing at the designated temperature for 20 s, and 72°C for 1 min, with a final extension at 72°C for 7 min. PCR products were verified by 1% agarose gel electrophoresis in 1× Tris-borate-EDTA (TBE) buffer using a Sub-Cell® GT system with 15 × 20 cm tray and gel caster equipped with 15- and 20-well combs (Bio-Rad Laboratories, Hercules, CA, USA). A total of 4 μL of PCR product was mixed with 1 μL of loading buffer and run alongside 1 μL of a 1-kb DNA ladder containing 100-bp increments.

The five protein-coding genes actin (ACT), glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GPD), translation elongation factor 1-alpha (TEF), tryptophan synthase (TS), and mitochondrial oxaloacetate carrier (OX) were PCR-amplified and sequenced using 20 primer pairs that were designed on the basis of conserved regions in the V. dahliae genome (Collado-Romero et al., 2008; Inderbitzin et al., 2011). Sequencing was performed in both directions using the same PCR primer sets by Bionics Co. (Seoul, Korea).

Phylogenetic analysis

Six different datasets were analyzed, comprising the single loci ACT, TEF, GPD, TS and OX, as well as a concatenated dataset of five loci. The sequences were analyzed through the BLAST search program at the NCBI website (https://blast.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Blast.cgi/). Both direction sequences were edited and assembled to generate consensus sequences using CLC Genomics Workbench v24.0. All sequences were aligned using Geneious with gap open cost: 12, gap extension cost: 3 and global alignment with free end gaps. A maximum-likelihood tree was constructed using MEGA v12.0.11. Statistical support of branch nodes was tested by using bootstrap analysis with 1000 replicates. Bayesian inference generated a majority-rule consensus tree from post–burn-in samples, obtained by running four Markov chains for 10 million generations and retaining every 1,000th tree after discarding the initial 25%. The sequence of the Verticillium isaacii strain PD660 from GenBank was used as the out-group. The most suitable substitution model within MEGA was chosen on the basis of the lowest Bayesian information criterion.

Pathogenicity assays

Pathogenicity assays were conducted in a greenhouse using all strains, as described by Koike et al. (1994) with modifications. Each strain was cultured in potato dextrose broth (PDB; BD Difco) at 25°C for 7 days in darkness with shaking, then filtered through four layers of gauze to obtain a conidial suspension, which was adjusted to 106 conidia mL−1. The Chinese cabbage cultivar ‘Chungwang’ (Sakata Korea Co., Ltd., Seoul, Korea), from which the pathogen had been frequently isolated, was used as the host. Seedlings were grown for approximately 3 weeks in sterilized commercial potting soil (SsukSsuki No. 2; NH Nongwoo Bio Co., Ltd., Suwon, Korea), uprooted, and rinsed with tap water. Roots were immersed in the spore suspension for 30 min. For each strain, 10 seedlings were inoculated, and another 10 seedlings immersed in sterile water served as controls. The treated seedlings were transplanted into 50-cell plug trays (Asia Seed Co., Ltd., Seoul, Korea) containing the same sterilized soil and maintained in a greenhouse (20–30°C) with watering only. The experiment was repeated two times.

To compare pathogenic differences among species and isolates, three isolates of V. dahliae and three isolates of V. longisporum (two A1/D1 and one A1/D3) were inoculated onto seedlings of the cultivar ‘Chungwang’ using the same method described above. For each isolate, 10 seedlings were inoculated and another 10 seedlings served as uninoculated controls. The experiment was repeated three times.

To evaluate cultivar-specific responses, one isolate of V. longisporum (HA23CV010, deposited in KACC 411050) and one isolate of V. dahliae (HA22CV128, deposited in KACC 411049) were inoculated onto seedlings of eight summer Chinese cabbage cultivars (cv. Cheongok, Chungwang, Homerun, Ilpumyeoleum, Odae, Summertop, Taegwang, and Yeoleumwanggug) using the same method described above. For each isolate, 10 seedlings were inoculated and 10 seedlings were used as uninoculated controls. The experiment was repeated twice.

Pathogenicity was evaluated six weeks after inoculation by assessing foliar chlorosis, vascular discoloration of the crown and hypocotyl, and plant height. Leaf chlorosis was scored as follows: 0, no visible symptoms; 1, ≤10% of leaf area affected (slight chlorosis or wilting on a few outer leaves); 2, 11–25% affected (mild chlorosis and wilting on several outer leaves); 3, 26–50% affected (moderate chlorosis and wilting extending to middle leaves); 4, 51–75% affected (severe symptoms on most leaves, with reduced growth); and 5, 76–100% affected or plant death (extensive wilting, chlorosis, defoliation, or collapse). Because the hypocotyls of Chinese cabbage are slender, vascular discoloration was examined using longitudinal sections. Vascular discoloration along the stele was scored on a 0–4 scale: 0, no visible symptoms; 1, slight brown discoloration confined to the hypocotyl; 2, pronounced brown discoloration of the hypocotyl; 3, discoloration extending into the crown (stem base); and 4, entire root system discolored or plant death. After assessment, the pathogen was re-isolated from the discolored vascular tissues and confirmed by molecular identification.

Host range tests

Host range tests were conducted using the same inoculation and maintenance method as the pathogenicity assays. Approximately three-week-old seedlings of cruciferous crops (radish, cabbage, and broccoli) and 15 other crops were used. The following cultivars were used in the host range tests: radish (cv. Hoyeong), cabbage (cv. Matsumo), broccoli (cv. Highland), barley (cv. Gwangmaek), buckwheat (cv. Yangjeol), carrot (cv. Europe 6chon), eggplant (cv. Heukwongyeong), maize (cv. Baekokdaechal 1ho), hairy vetch (cv. Cheongpungbora), head lettuce (cv. Yuria), potato (cv. Sumi), rye (cv. Gogu), soybean (cv. Daepung), sudangrass (Turbo 9), tomato (cv. Yegwang), watermelon (cv. Daltaryeong), Welsh onion (cv. Saeronheuk), and zucchini (cv. Starol). Seedlings were immersed in conidial suspensions of one isolate each of V. longisporum (HA23CV010) and V. dahliae (HA22CV128), then transplanted into plastic pots (21 cm in diameter and 18 cm in height) containing sterilized commercial potting soil. Five plants per treatment and five uninoculated controls were included, and the experiment was repeated twice. Six weeks after inoculation, leaf chlorosis, root discoloration, and plant height were assessed, and the pathogens were re-isolated from the vascular tissues at the crown to confirm host status.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SAS software (version 9.4; SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA). Disease severity indices, including leaf chlorosis, vascular discoloration, and plant height, were analyzed by one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), with isolate or host species as the treatment factor. When significant differences were detected (P < 0.05), means were separated using Tukey’s honestly significant difference (HSD) test (α = 0.05). Data from repeated experiments were pooled after confirming homogeneity of variances, and results are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD).

Results

Regional distribution of isolates and field symptoms

The isolates obtained from different regions are listed in Table 1. Most isolates were collected from highland summer cultivation areas during August to September, including 19 from Changjuk-dong and 12 from Hasami-dong in Taebaek-si, and 14 from Wangsan-myeon in Gangneung-si. In addition, five isolates were recovered from spring cultivation fields in June in Nam-myeon and Buk-myeon of Yeongwol-gun, and four isolates from autumn cultivation fields in Gimsatgat-myeon of Yeongwol-gun, indicating that major Chinese cabbage production areas in Gangwon-do were contaminated with the pathogens. Furthermore, the isolation of the pathogens from a spring cultivation field in Seokbo-myeon, Yeongyang-gun, Gyeongsangbuk-do suggests that the disease may spread beyond Gangwon Province.

Verticillium wilt symptoms typically appeared about 40 days after transplanting, corresponding to the heading stage of Chinese cabbage. Infected patches exhibited stunted growth and bright yellowing (Fig. 1A). As harvest approached, the outer leaves became asymmetrically chlorotic and wilted (Fig. 1B), while the inner leaves showed progressive paling along the veins toward the margins (Fig. 1C). When leaves were cut longitudinally, vascular tissues exhibited browning (Fig. 1D), and severe vascular discoloration was observed in root sections (Fig. 1E, 1F).

Phylogenetic identification of the strains

A multilocus phylogenetic analysis was conducted to clarify the evolutionary relationships among Verticillium isolates. Sequences of five nuclear genes (ACT, TEF, GPD, OX, and TS) were obtained from 60 isolates and compared with 19 reference strains, with V. isaacii (strain PD660) used as an outgroup. The V. longisporum isolates carried two alleles, one derived from the A1 parent and the other from either the D1 lineage or the V. dahliae lineage D3. The concatenated alignment comprised 3,365 nucleotide positions, including gap characters (ACT: 1–604; TEF: 605–1317; GPD: 1318–2080; OX: 2081–2750; TS: 2751–3365). Maximum-likelihood analysis yielded the best-fit tree with a final likelihood score of −5245.62, and the major clades were well supported by bootstrap values of 74–99% (Fig. 2). The phylogenetic analyses placed three isolates with V. dahliae, one with V. longisporum lineage A1/D3, and the remainder into two well-supported clades corresponding to lineages A1 and D1, thereby demonstrating clear lineage differentiation within the species. Thus, V. longisporum accounted for 57 of the 60 isolates (approximately 95%), whereas only three isolates (approximately 5%) were identified as V. dahliae. Notably, single-gene analysis initially grouped isolate HA24CV002 with both D2 and D3 clades; however, the multilocus analysis unambiguously placed this isolate in the D3 lineage. This classification was further supported by multiplex PCR and ITS assays (data not shown), consistent with previous reports that D2 and D3 lineages differ in the ITS region but cannot be distinguished by protein-coding genes.

Pathogenic differences among Verticillium species and isolates

Inoculation of 60 Verticillium isolates collected from different regions onto Chinese cabbage seedlings resulted in all isolates inducing characteristic symptoms, including leaf chlorosis, growth retardation, and vascular discoloration in the crown and hypocotyl. Based on the severity of vascular discoloration, 53 of the 57 V. longisporum isolates showed disease indices above 3.0, and in particular, 10 isolates exceeded 3.5, indicating their high aggressiveness. In contrast, all three V. dahliae isolates scored below 3.0, reflecting comparatively lower virulence than V. longisporum (Fig. 3).

Distinct differences were observed when isolates were compared by species (Table 3). V. longisporum A1/D1 isolates exhibited leaf chlorosis and vascular discoloration indices greater than 3.0, resulting in plant heights reduced to approximately half of those of the non-inoculated control. In particular, the V. longisporum A1/D3 isolate showed the highest virulence among all isolates tested, with a leaf chlorosis index of 4.7, a vascular discoloration index of 3.5, and plant height reduced to only 30% of the control. In contrast, V. dahliae isolates exhibited leaf chlorosis indices of 1.4–2.3 and vascular discoloration indices of 2.4–2.8, with plant heights at 72–96% of the control. Based on symptom development, the V. longisporum A1/D3 isolate induced wilting of the outer leaves within three weeks after inoculation, which gradually progressed to the inner leaves, leading to whole-leaf chlorosis, defoliation, and in some cases plant death by around four weeks. In contrast, plants inoculated with V. dahliae developed symptoms more slowly, and defoliation remained limited even after six weeks (Fig. 4).

In eight summer Chinese cabbage cultivars, leaf chlorosis was significantly more severe in plants inoculated with V. longisporum than in the uninoculated control or those inoculated with V. dahliae. In particular, five cultivars inoculated with V. longisporum exhibited chlorosis scores greater than 3.0, and the cultivars ‘Cheongok’ and ‘Homerun’ exceeded a score of 4.0, with most leaves showing chlorosis and some plants eventually dying. In contrast, inoculation with V. dahliae did not cause significant chlorosis in most cultivars, except for three including ‘Chungwang’. For vascular discoloration, all cultivars inoculated with V. longisporum showed high scores ranging from 2.9 to 3.5. Interestingly, V. dahliae also induced relatively high vascular discoloration scores (2.6–3.0), and no significant differences were detected between the two species. Regarding plant height, inoculation with V. longisporum significantly reduced growth in all cultivars compared with the control, whereas V. dahliae caused no significant reduction in five cultivars (Table 4). Based on vascular discoloration, no resistance to either pathogen was observed in any of the cultivars.

Host range of Verticillium isolates

Inoculation of various crops with V. longisporum and V. dahliae revealed clear differences in host responses (Table 5, Fig. 5). Among cruciferous crops, radish, broccoli, and cabbage developed leaf chlorosis and vascular discoloration caused by both species, and the pathogens were successfully reisolated. However, plant height did not differ significantly among treatments in these three cruciferous crops. In contrast, solanaceous crops such as eggplant, potato, and tomato, as well as watermelon and Welsh onion, exhibited severe leaf chlorosis and significant growth reduction only after inoculation with V. dahliae. In addition, hairy vetch, soybean, head lettuce, rye, and zucchini were also infected by V. dahliae and developed leaf chlorosis, although plant height reduction was not pronounced. Nevertheless, the pathogen was consistently reisolated and identified from these hosts. By contrast, barley, buckwheat, carrot, maize, and sudangrass were not infected by either species and did not show foliar symptoms or growth reduction.

Discussion

This study revealed that Verticillium wilt pathogens are widely distributed in major Chinese cabbage production areas in Gangwon-do, particularly in highland summer cultivation fields. The detection of isolates from spring and autumn fields, as well as outside Gangwon Province, indicates the potential for broader geographical spread. Highland summer Chinese cabbage has been continuously cultivated in monoculture for decades due to its high economic value. However, as demonstrated in this study, Verticillium wilt has occurred and is causing severe damage. Similar observations have been made in Europe, where intensive crucifer cultivation under favorable environmental conditions resulted in the accumulation of soilborne inoculum and recurrent outbreaks of Verticillium wilt (Johansson et al., 2006; Zeise and von Tiedemann, 2002). Verticillium species form long-lived microsclerotia that enable persistence in soil and infested plant debris (Pegg and Brady, 2002), and these propagules can also be disseminated over long distances via the movement of soil particles by wind and water, as well as by agricultural machinery and workers (Klosterman et al., 2009). In recent years, fertilization, pesticide application, and harvesting in these areas have often been managed by distributors, and such workers increasingly move across different regions during the harvest season. Consequently, regional monitoring, strict crop rotation systems, and rigorous sanitation of agricultural machinery and workers are essential to prevent further spread of the disease.

Multilocus phylogenetic analyses revealed the lineage composition of Verticillium populations in Korea. V. longisporum lineage A1/D1, which is also the most prevalent in Europe and North America (Depotter et al., 2017), dominated across all surveyed regions, highlighting its central role in Verticillium wilt epidemics in Korea. In contrast, only a single isolate of A1/D3 lineage was detected, marking the first report of this lineage in Korea and suggesting possible introductions or emerging risks that require careful monitoring. The placement of isolate HA24CV002 in the D3 lineage was supported by multilocus phylogenetic analysis as well as ITS and multiplex PCR assays, consistent with previous reports that D2 and D3 can be distinguished by ITS but not reliably by protein-coding genes (Inderbitzin et al., 2013).

The genetic characteristics of these two pathogens also differ markedly, which may underlie their distinct epidemiology and pathogenicity. V. dahliae populations exhibit high genetic diversity and adaptability, often forming host-specific pathogenicity groups (Inderbitzin et al., 2011; Usami et al., 2007). By contrast, V. longisporum originated from interspecific hybridization events between different Verticillium lineages, resulting in stable lineages such as A1/D1, A1/D2, and A1/D3, each with characteristic host ranges and geographical distributions (Depotter et al., 2016). This is consistent with its prevalence in Europe and recent reports in East Asian production systems, including cabbage in Japan (Banno et al., 2015) and oilseed rape in China (Si et al., 2024).

In terms of pathogenicity, V. longisporum exhibited generally higher virulence on Chinese cabbage than V. dahliae, consistent with its adaptation to brassica hosts (Ikeda et al., 2012; Karapapa et al., 1997; Kemmochi et al., 1999). The two species also adopted different infection strategies: V. longisporum rapidly induced leaf chlorosis and wilting, occasionally leading to plant death, whereas V. dahliae developed symptoms more slowly but caused pronounced vascular discoloration, suggesting its role as a long-term inoculum reservoir (Eynck et al., 2007; Zhou et al., 2006). Notably, although represented by only a single isolate, the A1/D3 lineage showed markedly higher aggressiveness on Chinese cabbage than the predominant A1/D1 lineage, supporting earlier evidence of lineage-specific differences in pathogenicity (Banno et al., 2015; Novakazi et al., 2015). Taken together with our field survey, these results indicate that V. longisporum, particularly lineage A1/D1, is currently the more serious Verticillium wilt pathogen in Chinese cabbage production in Korea, whereas V. dahliae remains epidemiologically important in non-brassicaceous rotation crops because of its broad host range.

The host range assays further highlighted the ecological divergence between the two species. V. longisporum was primarily restricted to cruciferous crops, inducing foliar chlorosis and vascular discoloration in radish, broccoli, and cabbage, consistent with previous studies (Johansson et al., 2006; Novakazi et al., 2015). By contrast, V. dahliae exhibited a much broader host range, causing severe chlorosis and growth suppression in solanaceous crops such as eggplant, potato, and tomato, as well as in watermelon and Welsh onion. Several crops, including hairy vetch, soybean, head lettuce, rye, and zucchini, were also infected by V. dahliae and developed foliar chlorosis, although plant height was not significantly reduced. These findings agree with reports that V. dahliae is a generalist pathogen infecting more than 200 plant species, including both dicots and monocots (Agrios, 1997; Fradin and Thomma, 2006). Particularly noteworthy is the infection of rye, which is widely cultivated as a cover crop in highland cabbage production systems, raising concerns about its role as an overwintering host for V. dahliae. By contrast, barley, buckwheat, carrot, maize, and sudangrass showed no infection or symptom development, suggesting that these crops are likely non-hosts. Such non-host crops may serve as suitable candidates for crop rotation or green manure in infested fields to suppress Verticillium inoculum. This approach aligns with integrated disease management strategies emphasizing crop diversification and rotation to reduce Verticillium wilt pressure (Zhao et al., 2023).

Finally, the absence of resistant summer Chinese cabbage cultivars underscores the urgent need for resistance breeding and the exploration of host genetic resources. Although resistance breeding in Brassica crops has been challenging, important progress has been made in identifying resistant accessions in B. napus and B. rapa (Rygulla et al., 2007) and in characterizing genetic loci associated with resistance through metabolic and molecular approaches (Obermeier et al., 2013). Incorporating such resistance resources into Chinese cabbage breeding programs will be essential for sustainable management. Taken together, our findings highlight that V. longisporum, particularly A1/D1 lineage, represents the most immediate and aggressive threat to Chinese cabbage production in Korea, while the presence of genetically diverse V. dahliae populations and the detection of A1/D3 lineage point to long-term risks that require sustained monitoring and management. Understanding the genetic and pathogenic variability of these populations is therefore crucial for developing effective resistance breeding programs and integrated disease management strategies to mitigate the impact of Verticillium wilt in Korean agriculture.

Zika virus outbreak are on the rise in India: Clinical features, complications and prevention
Authors: Aminath Waafira, Kannan Subbaram, Razana Faiz, Zeba Un Naher, Punya Laxmi Manandhar, Sheeza Ali
Published: 2024

Dear Editor:

The rising outbreaks of Zika virus cases have diverted the attention of public health in India. Recently, as of July this year, a total of eight cases have been reported from Pune in Maharashtra [1]. In 1947 from Uganda, Zika virus was discovered from the rhesus monkey. After five years, in 1952, Nigeria, Tanzania and Uganda reported the first human cases. In the year 2016 more than forty-nine countries have reported clinical cases of Zika virus. In the same year, the WHO declared Zika virus infection as a global public health emergency. The first reported case in India was from Gujarat state in the same year. Since then, there have been sporadic outbreaks in several states including Delhi, Karnataka, Kerala, Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, Tamil Nadu, and Uttar Pradesh. The biggest outbreak of Zika Virus in Southeast Asia was also from Rajasthan with a total of 154 cases in the year 2018. In the same year, 130 cases were reported from the state of Madhya Pradesh increasing the burden in the country. Before the current outbreak, there were reported outbreaks of Zika virus in 2023 and 2021 as well.

As of 2024, recent outbreaks have been reported from several countries with the highest number from Brazil with a total of 6599 cases. The Pan American Health Organization reported the importance of maintaining proper monitoring of Zika cases for prevention of future epidemics. Zika virus, belonging to the family Flaviviridae, genus Flavivirus, is an arbovirus which is transmitted primarily through the mosquito Aedes aegypti. Other species of Aedes responsible for the transmission of Zika virus include A. albopictus, A. hensilli, A. polynesiensi, and A. africanus. It is an enveloped with positive sense, single stranded (ss) RNA virus. Zika virus has a genome of 10,794 kbp involving two non-coding regions and the long reading frame coding for the viral structural proteins and seven non-structural proteins. There are four structural proteins; one capsid protein, one membrane protein precursor, and two envelope proteins. The binding and fusion of the virus to the host cell is through the envelope protein [2]. The main transmission is through the vector of Aedes species maintaining a zoonotic transmission and urban transmission affecting humans. It can also have vertical transmission from pregnant mother to the foetus as well as through sex. Brazil has reported transmission through infected blood products, tissue and laboratory exposure even though it is rare [3]. The possible tissues that can harbour Zika virus include gestational tissues, reproductive tissues, and umbilical cord blood. Despite isolating Zika virus from breast milk, spread through breast milk has not been confirmed and CDC recommends breastfeeding during Zika infection or if travelling to endemic areas [4].

The clinical features of Zika virus infection starts showing after around 3–14 days of incubation period. However, 80 % of the affected individuals remain asymptomatic. Since the emergence of the disease, new clinical manifestations have been reported and fatal diseases occur in <1 % of the cases. These sequelae of Zika virus includes Guillain-Barre Syndrome, encephalopathy, meningoencephalitis, and severe thrombocytopenia [5]. Regarding the vertical transmission, there is 5–15 % risk of developing the virus-related complications in the foetuses of Zika infected mothers. Of this, congenital Zika syndrome is of utmost importance which commonly manifests as ventriculomegaly, microcephaly and intracranial calcifications.

Despite the global decline of Zika virus cases, the wide range of clinical manifestations and sequels prompt for early intervention and prevention of transmission. Most crucial step in prevention would be protection against mosquito bites especially among pregnant women. These preventive measures range from using mosquito nets and repellents while sleeping to eliminating their breeding sites. Public education regarding sexual transmission should be provided. These include providing information regarding risks associated with sexual transmission and access to emergency contraceptives to women who have unprotected sex and are concerned about the infection.

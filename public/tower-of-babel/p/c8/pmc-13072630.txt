Novel biomarker-based prediction model for coronary artery lesions in Kawasaki disease
Authors: Meng Wang, Jiegang Deng, Chunquan Cai, Bei Liu, Xuan Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThis study aimed to develop and validate a biomarker-based prediction model for assessing the individual risk of coronary artery lesions (CAL) in Kawasaki disease (KD).MethodsA retrospective analysis was performed on 345 pediatric KD patients admitted between June 2018 and June 2022. Patients were randomly divided into training (n = 241) and validation (n = 104) sets. Univariate analysis identified candidate predictors, and Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) regression was used for feature selection. Multivariable logistic regression and machine learning models—random forest (RF), support vector machine, and k-nearest neighbors—were developed. Model performance was assessed using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), calibration curves, and decision curve analysis. A nomogram was constructed, and SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) values were applied to interpret feature contributions.ResultsSeven biomarkers were significantly associated with CAL in univariate analysis (P < 0.05). LASSO and multivariable logistic regression analysis identified age, N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide, interleukin-6, calprotectin, endothelial microparticles, Matrix Metalloproteinase-9, and Galectin-3 as independent predictors. The RF model demonstrated superior performance, with AUCs of 0.888 (training) and 0.860 (validation). SHAP analysis confirmed these three variables as the top contributors to CAL prediction. The nomogram exhibited strong calibration and clinical utility.ConclusionThe machine learning-based prediction model incorporating novel biomarkers enables individualized risk assessment for CAL development in KD patients. This model exhibits excellent predictive performance and clinical applicability, facilitating early identification of high-risk patients and the implementation of targeted interventions, thereby optimizing healthcare resource allocation and improving long-term cardiovascular outcomes.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12887-026-06632-w.Keywords: Kawasaki disease, Coronary artery lesions, Prediction model, Biomarkers, Machine learning

Introduction

Kawasaki disease (KD) is an acute systemic vasculitis predominantly affecting children, with coronary artery lesions (CAL) representing its most severe complication. CAL may lead to coronary artery dilation or aneurysms, serving as a critical determinant of long-term prognosis in affected children [1, 2]. Although intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) has become the standard treatment, 10%-20% of patients still exhibit suboptimal responses and progress to coronary artery dilation or aneurysms, underscoring the importance of early high-risk identification [3–5].

Current CAL risk assessment relies on clinical manifestations, conventional inflammatory markers, and echocardiography. However, these methods suffer from insufficient sensitivity or delayed detection, limiting their ability to provide precise warnings before structural vascular changes occur [6]. Recent studies indicate that CAL pathogenesis involves multiple interconnected mechanisms, including myocardial injury, excessive immune-inflammatory activation, endothelial dysfunction, and tissue remodeling [7, 8]. Novel biomarkers related to these mechanisms have shown potential in CAL risk assessment, but their standalone predictive utility remains limited due to the multifactorial nature of CAL.

Integrating multidimensional biomarkers into a composite predictive model is imperative for early, objective risk stratification. Machine learning (ML) efficiently uncovers latent patterns in complex clinical data and handles nonlinear variable interactions, making it ideal for multifactorial disease prediction. In recent years, risk prediction models for CAL based on conventional inflammatory markers (e.g., C-reactive protein, erythrocyte sedimentation rate) [9] and clinical features have been proposed, such as the scoring system developed by Hua et al. (Area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) = 0.731) [10] and the nomogram developed by Tang et al. for predicting coronary artery aneurysms at one month (AUC = 0.946) [11]. However, these models predominantly rely on routine indicators, lack integration of novel biomarkers, and often focus on acute-phase coronary dilation without addressing long-term coronary outcomes. Furthermore, although machine learning approaches such as support vector machines and neural networks have been applied to KD prediction studies [12–13], most models lack sufficient interpretability, limiting their clinical translatability. Against this background, the present study systematically integrated multi-mechanistic novel biomarkers (e.g., N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP), endothelial microparticles) for the first time, adopted a stringent long-term CAL endpoint definition, and constructed a random forest-based prediction model. Simultaneously, the model’s interpretability and visualizability were achieved through SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) analysis and a nomogram, aiming to provide an individualized risk assessment tool with both high predictive performance and clinical operability. This study systematically collected clinical data and novel biomarkers to develop and validate an ML-based predictive model for CAL, aiming to provide a quantifiable tool for early identification and personalized intervention.

Methods

Study population

A retrospective cohort of 345 KD children hospitalized in our pediatric department between June 2018 and June 2022 was enrolled. Sample size calculation was performed based on the primary outcome (CAL incidence, estimated at 15%–20% per prior data [4]). Using PASS 2021 and R 4.2.3 (“pwr” package), with α = 0.05 (two-tailed), power (1–β) = 80%, and 5% attrition, the minimum required sample was 260. The actual sample (n = 345) exceeded this threshold, achieving > 85% statistical power for multivariable and ML analyses.

Inclusion criteria

Age ≤ 12 years, Diagnosis was based on the diagnostic criteria for Kawasaki Disease Research Committee (Japanese Ministry of Health) [14], Standard therapy (IVIG 2 g/kg + aspirin), Pretreatment biomarker panel (C-reactive protein (CRP), NT-proBNP, Interleukin-6(IL-6), calprotectin, endothelial microparticles, Matrix Metalloproteinase-9(MMP-9), galectin-3), Complete echocardiography during hospitalization and within 2 weeks post-discharge for CAL assessment.

Exclusion criteria

Incomplete clinical/biomarker/echocardiographic data, Comorbidities causing CAL/myocardial injury (e.g., congenital heart disease, viral myocarditis), Loss to follow-up or nonadherence to.

All eligible KD patients admitted to our department between June 2018 and June 2022 were consecutively included without selective exclusion. Regarding missing data handling: patients with incomplete clinical data (n = 12), missing biomarker results (n = 18), or incomplete echocardiographic data (n = 10) were excluded listwise. No imputation methods were used due to the small proportion of missing data (8.69%, 40/465 total screened patients). A flow diagram of patient selection was provided as Supplementary Fig. 1.

Data collection

Demographics, clinical features, treatment details, and laboratory results were extracted from electronic medical records. Blood samples were collected pre-IVIG. All assays followed standardized protocols using uniform equipment/reagents. Baseline parameters: Demographics: Age (months), sex, height (cm), weight (kg). Clinical signs: Fever duration (days), typical KD symptoms (conjunctival injection, cracked lips/strawberry tongue, polymorphous rash, extremity changes), cervical lymphadenopathy. Conventional labs: Inflammation/infection: CRP (mg/L). Cardiac markers: troponin I (ng/mL), NT-proBNP (ng/mL). Liver/renal/electrolytes: albumin (g/L), sodium (mmol/L). Coagulation: D-dimer (mg/L FEU). Novel biomarkers: Immune/inflammation: IL-6 (pg/mL), Tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) (pg/mL), Interferon-γ (IFN-γ) (pg/mL). Endothelial injury: Endothelial microparticles (counts/µL), von Willebrand Factor Antigen (vWF: Ag) (IU/dL). Tissue remodeling/fibrosis: MMP-9 (ng/mL), galectin-3 (ng/mL). Others: Calprotectin (µg/L), Soluble Suppression of Tumorigenicity 2 (sST2) (ng/mL), microRNA-145 (miR-145) (relative expression). Echocardiography (acute/follow-up): Coronary metrics: Z-scores (BSA-adjusted). Initial therapy: IVIG dose (g/kg), timing (illness day), corticosteroid use. IVIG resistance (persistent/recurrent fever ≥ 36 h post-IVIG). CAL determination: Based on serial echocardiography (2 weeks, 1/3/6 months post-discharge).

Outcome definition

Per AHA guidelines and Sino-Japanese KD consensus [15, 16], patients were stratified: CAL-positive: Acute phase: Coronary Z-score ≥ 2.5 in any major artery, Follow-up: Aneurysm (Z-score ≥ 5.0) or progressive dilation (Z ≥ 2.5), Chronic phase (> 6 months): Persistent Z ≥ 2.5, stenosis, or calcification. CAL-negative: Acute phase: All coronary Z-scores < 2.5, Stability: No CAL criteria met during ≥ 3-month follow-up. Two blinded senior sonographers independently interpreted echocardiograms. Discrepancies were arbitrated by a pediatric cardiologist. All images were archived in a dedicated database for traceability.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was performed using SPSS 26.0, R 4.2.3, and Python 3.8.5. Continuous variables were first tested for normality using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Normally distributed variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (x̄±s) and compared using the independent samples t-test. Non-normally distributed data were presented as median (interquartile range [IQR]) and analyzed via the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables were reported as n (%) and compared using the χ² test or Fisher’s exact test. In the training set, univariate analysis was first conducted to screen variables with P < 0.05, followed by variable compression via Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) regression. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was ultimately employed to identify independent predictors, and their odds ratios (OR) and 95% confidence intervals (CI) were calculated. Sample size calculation adhered to the predictive modeling guidelines, with a significance level (α) of 0.05, statistical power (1 − β) of 80%, and compliance with the “events per variable (EPV) ≥ 5–10” rule to ensure model stability (variance inflation factor [VIF] < 2 for all variables). Based on independent predictors, machine learning models (random forest (RF), support vector machine (SVM), and k-nearest neighbors (KNN)) were constructed using Python 3.8.5 and the scikit-learn library. Key hyperparameters: RF-number of trees = 100, maximum depth = 10, class weight = ‘balanced’; SVM-kernel = ‘rbf’, C = 1.0, gamma = ‘scale’; KNN-n_neighbors = 5, weights = ‘distance’; Threshold selection for sensitivity and specificity: The optimal threshold was determined using the Youden’s J statistic (J = sensitivity+specificity-1), and the threshold corresponding to the maximum J value was selected for clinical application. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were plotted, with predictive performance evaluated by AUC. A calibration curve was plotted and evaluated using the Hosmer - Lemeshow goodness - of - fit test. Decision curve analysis (DCA) was used to evaluate the clinical application value of the nomogram by calculating the net benefit at different threshold probabilities. A nomogram was developed via the rms package in R and internally validated using 1,000 bootstrap resamples. Additionally, SHAP values were computed via Python’s SHAP library to assess model interpretability—globally (feature importance ranking, contribution direction) and locally (individual patient risk contribution decomposition)—enhancing clinical translatability through visual nomograms. A P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Comparison of baseline characteristics between training and validation sets

A total of 345 children with confirmed KD were enrolled and randomly divided into a training set (n = 241, 70%) and a validation set (n = 104, 30%). In the training set, 36 patients (14.94%) had coronary artery lesions (CAL-positive), while 205 (85.06%) were CAL-negative. The validation set comprised 15 CAL-positive (14.42%) and 89 CAL-negative (85.58%) patients. No statistically significant differences (P > 0.05) were observed in baseline clinical features or novel biomarker levels between the two sets, confirming balanced partitioning and comparability (Table 1).

Multivariate logistic regression analysis of factors influencing coronary artery lesions in Kawasaki disease

The presence of CAL was designated as the dependent variable (CAL-negative = 0, CAL-positive = 1) (Supplementary Table 1). Seven variables with statistical significance (P < 0.05) in univariate analysis—age, NT-proBNP, IL-6, calprotectin, endothelial microparticles, MMP-9, and galectin-3—were subjected to LASSO regression for feature selection. Optimal variables were selected via 10-fold cross-validation and the λ-1se criterion (Supplementary Fig. 2). Seven predictors were ultimately incorporated into the multivariate logistic regression model. The results identified age, NT-proBNP, IL-6, calprotectin, endothelial microparticles, MMP-9, and galectin-3 as independent risk factors for CAL (P < 0.05) (Table 3). The negative β coefficient for age indicates that lower age values (i.e., younger age) are associated with a higher risk of CAL.

Evaluation of machine learning model performance

Based on the key predictors identified by logistic regression, multiple machine learning models were constructed to optimize predictive performance. All models demonstrated excellent predictive accuracy and generalization in both training and validation cohorts. As shown in the ROC curves (Fig. 1), the RF, SVM, and KNN models achieved AUC values of 0.888, 0.866, and 0.825, respectively, in the validation set. The RF model, with the highest AUC, was selected as the optimal model due to its superior discriminative power. Calibration curves showed close alignment between predicted probabilities and observed CAL risks, confirming model reliability (Fig. 2). DCA indicated that the clinical prediction model provided greater net benefit across a wide threshold probability range compared to “treat-all” or “treat-none” strategies (Fig. 3). Collectively, the integration of novel biomarkers into machine learning models yielded high accuracy and clinical applicability, offering a robust tool for individualized CAL risk prediction in Kawasaki disease.

Interpretability assessment of model predictions

An RF-based nomogram visualized directional contributions: NT-proBNP, IL-6, calprotectin, endothelial microparticles, MMP-9, and galectin-3 were risk factors (higher values increased CAL likelihood), whereas age was protective (lower risk with older age) (Fig. 4A). SHAP analysis quantified feature importance: NT-proBNP (X2) > age (X1) > IL-6 (X3) > MMP-9 (X6) > calprotectin (X4) > endothelial microparticles (X5) > galectin-3 (X7) (Fig. 4B). NT-proBNP exhibited the strongest positive predictive impact, with SHAP values surging beyond 3.5 ng/mL, indicating high risk. IL-6 and calprotectin showed prominent positive effects, while age demonstrated a linear inverse relationship. SHAP results aligned with logistic regression, reinforcing model interpretability. The combined nomogram and SHAP analysis enabled quantifiable risk stratification and informed targeted interventions.

Discussion

In this study, seven independent predictors were identified: age, NT-proBNP, IL-6, calprotectin, endothelial microparticles, MMP-9, and galectin-3. Based on these predictors, the RF model demonstrated superior and robust predictive performance, with AUC values of 0.888 (training) and 0.860 (validation). The model demonstrated good calibration and provided substantial net clinical benefit across a wide range of threshold probabilities in decision curve analysis. Furthermore, interpretability was improved through SHAP analysis and a nomogram, which quantifies feature contributions and enables individualized risk visualization.

The seven core predictors finally determined in this study deeply reflect the synergistic characteristics of multiple pathological mechanisms in the occurrence of CAL, and the predictive value of each factor is highly consistent with the pathophysiological logic. Specifically, NT-proBNP directly reflects myocardial stress, IL-6 drives cytokine storms, calprotectin marks neutrophil activation, endothelial microparticles signify vascular injury, MMP-9 mediates extracellular matrix degradation/remodeling, and galectin-3 correlates with fibrosis/chronic inflammation. These biomarkers cover key links of CAL pathogenesis, providing a mechanistic basis for the predictive model [17–20]. The multivariate logistic regression identified NT-proBNP as the strongest independent risk factor, with elevated levels reflecting myocardial stress and increased ventricular wall tension—consistent with the hemodynamic disturbances and impaired myocardial perfusion in CAL pathogenesis [21]. IL-6 and calprotectin further underscored the central role of immune-inflammatory hyperactivation in CAL; both were significantly higher in CAL-positive patients (P = 0.001) and improved model specificity, aligning with the “inflammatory activity–vascular injury” axis proposed by the Kawasaki Disease Research Committee [22].

The incorporation of endothelial microparticles and MMP-9—markers of endothelial injury and tissue remodeling—enhanced the model’s mechanistic comprehensiveness. Levels of these markers were 37.6% and 25.8% higher, respectively, in CAL-positive patients (both P = 0.001), and SHAP analysis indicated their predictive contribution was second only to NT-proBNP. This suggests that endothelial dysfunction and dysregulated matrix degradation are early, pivotal events in CAL, offering potential therapeutic targets focused on endothelial protection and matrix stabilization. Notably, younger age emerged as an independent risk factor, supported by an odds ratio below 1 and consistent with clinical observations of higher CAL susceptibility in younger children. By integrating age into the model, an early-warning tool is provided for this vulnerable population, in whom diagnosis is often delayed due to atypical presentations.

The interpretability analysis of the model is another core highlight of this study. By quantifying the relative importance of each factor using SHAP values, the order of factors affecting the risk of CAL was found. This analysis mode of “global importance ranking+local risk contribution decomposition” effectively enhances the interpretability of the machine-learning model, helping clinicians clarify the relative importance of each predictor and the direction of their impact on CAL risk, and deeply integrates the prediction logic with clinical pathological mechanisms, significantly improving the clinical acceptability of the model. Clinically, when evaluating a KD patient, clinicians can first use the nomogram to quickly calculate the predicted CAL risk by accumulating scores based on the patient’s age, NT-proBNP, IL-6 and other indicators. Then, combined with the SHAP analysis results, they can identify the key factors driving the individual’s risk. For example, if a patient’s NT-proBNP level is significantly elevated (SHAP value > 3.5), clinicians should pay close attention to myocardial function and strengthen coronary artery monitoring; for infants under 20 months old (with negative SHAP value indicating higher risk), early intensive intervention can be considered to reduce the occurrence of CAL. This targeted clinical decision-making based on interpretability results helps improve the precision of treatment.

Recent years have witnessed growing application of ML and deep learning in predicting CAL in KD, yet methodologies and emphases vary across studies. Azuma et al. [23] employed structural equation modeling and neural network analysis, utilizing predominantly clinical and conventional laboratory markers, and reported a validation AUC of 0.86. Tang et al. [24] developed several ML models incorporating clinical and laboratory parameters to predict coronary artery lesions, and RF algorithm was used in the present study to construct a prediction model for CAL effectively, with an accuracy of 0.930 and AUC of 0.925. Notably, their study also incorporated SHAP analysis to enhance interpretability. More recently, Yang et al. [25] proposed a deep learning model (KCPREDICT) for early CAL detection, demonstrating a high validation AUC of 0.960. Building upon these advances, our study introduces several distinct contributions. First, regarding predictor composition, we systematically integrated a panel of seven novel biomarkers spanning myocardial stress (NT-proBNP), immune inflammation (IL-6, calprotectin), endothelial injury (endothelial microparticles), and tissue remodeling (MMP-9, galectin-3), along with the clinical variable age, thereby providing a more comprehensive pathophysiological profiling than models relying primarily on conventional markers. Second, our endpoint definition adhered strictly to the AHA and Sino-Japanese consensus, encompassing not only acute-phase coronary dilation (Z-score ≥ 2.5) but also chronic-phase outcomes such as persistent dilation, stenosis, or calcification beyond six months, which strengthens the long-term prognostic relevance of our prediction. Third, despite the comparable or high AUC values reported in prior studies, we further strengthened model transparency and clinical accessibility by constructing an intuitive nomogram alongside SHAP analysis, offering both global feature importance ranking and individualized risk decomposition—a dual interpretability approach that facilitates clinical translation and personalized risk communication.

The methodological innovation of this study lies in the synergistic application of multi-dimensional technical approaches. In the variable screening stage, LASSO regression was used to compress high-dimensional data, and 10-fold cross-validation was used to effectively eliminate collinearity interference (variable VIF < 2), ensuring the robustness of the seven core factors. In the model construction stage, by comparing three machine-learning models, namely RF, SVM, and K-nearest neighbor algorithm, the RF model was found to perform best in both the training set and the validation set. Its advantage lies in its ability to handle non-linear relationships and interactions between variables, which is particularly suitable for predicting multi-factor diseases such as CAL. The calibration curve showed that the predicted probability of the RF model was highly consistent with the actual risk of CAL (the curve was close to the diagonal). The decision curve further confirmed that within the threshold probability range of 0.1–0.8, using this model to guide clinical decisions could obtain 15%-20% more clinical net benefits than the “all-intervention” or “no-intervention” strategies, meaning that the model can effectively reduce the risks of over-medical treatment and missed diagnosis and significantly improve resource utilization efficiency.

However, this study has certain limitations. As a single-center retrospective study, all samples were from the pediatric department of our hospital, which may lead to selection bias. The extrapolation performance of the model needs to be further verified by multi-center prospective cohorts. The model only included baseline indicators and did not consider factors such as the response to IVIG treatment (e.g., non-responders) and the dynamic changes of biomarkers during follow-up. In the future, a dynamic prediction model can be constructed in combination with mobile health technology to improve the accuracy of long-term risk assessment.

In conclusion, this study successfully constructed and validated a prediction model for Kawasaki disease coronary artery lesions integrating novel biomarkers. Through the integration of multi-mechanism variables and the application of advanced algorithms, the model achieved accurate prediction and visual evaluation of CAL risk. Its core values are as follows: (1) It systematically confirmed for the first time the synergistic effect of novel biomarkers such as NT-proBNP and endothelial microparticles in the early warning of CAL. (2) It balanced the predictive performance and clinical interpretability of the model through SHAP analysis and the nomogram. (3) It provided a quantitative tool for the early identification and individualized intervention of high-risk children such as those of young age and with high inflammatory activity. In the future, with multi-center validation and the inclusion of dynamic variables, this model is expected to become a standard tool for the clinical management of Kawasaki disease, promoting the transformation of CAL prevention and treatment from “empirical intervention” to “precision stratification”.

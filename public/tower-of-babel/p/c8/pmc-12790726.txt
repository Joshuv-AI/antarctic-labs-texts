Trophoblast stem cell derivation from naive and primed hPSC enables ELF5 functional analysis
Authors: Joonas Sokka, Ella Lapinsuo, Jouni Kvist, Sami Jalil, Masahito Yoshihara, Jere Weltner, Fredrik Lanner, Juha Kere, Diego Balboa, Timo Otonkoski, Ras Trokovic
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryHuman pluripotent stem cells (hPSCs) are valuable tools for studying placental biology, yet their differentiation into bona fide trophoblast stem cells (TSCs) remains challenging. In this study, we established and thoroughly compared naive and primed-derived TSC-like cells with primary human TSCs derived from pre-implantation blastocyst and first-trimester placenta. Comprehensive analyses confirmed expression of trophoblast lineage-specific genes and typical placental features. Detailed transcriptional analyses revealed that naive-derived TSC-like cells resembled embryo and placenta-derived cell lines and differentiated faster and more directly into TSC than primed-derived cells. We used these TSC-like models to study the role of ELF5, a transcription factor indispensable for maintenance and differentiation in mouse TSC. In contrast to the mouse, knockout and knockdown experiments revealed that ELF5 is dispensable for human TSC-like cells self-renewal and differentiation. Our study provides valuable transcriptional data and highlights the utility of hPSC-derived TSC-like cells for modeling the placenta and studying gene function.Keywords: trophoblast, trophoblast stem cell, pluripotent stem cell, embryonic stem cell, induced pluripotent stem cell, single cell RNA sequencing, ELF5, CRISPR-Cas9, early embryon annotation, transcriptomics, C19MC

Introduction

The placenta is essential for fetal development by providing oxygen, nutrients, and removing waste (Turco and Moffett, 2019). In vitro modeling is essential for studying placental development and disorders such as preeclampsia. Trophoblasts originate from trophectoderm (TE) cells of the blastocyst, being the first lineage to differentiate during embryonic development (Niakan et al., 2012). Early placental cell types include cytotrophoblasts (CTs), extravillous cytotrophoblasts (EVTs) and syncytiotrophoblasts (SCTs) (James et al., 2012). Human trophoblast stem cells (TSCs) were derived from CT of first-trimester placentas and blastocysts and can be stably maintained with wingless/integrated (Wnt) and epidermal growth factor (EGF) signals, along with inhibition of transforming growth factor β receptor and Rho-associated protein kinase (ROCK) (Okae et al., 2018). These cells exhibit features of peri-implantation trophoblast (Castel et al., 2020; Okae et al., 2018; Sheridan et al., 2021; Zhao et al., 2024).

Naive and primed human pluripotent stem cells (hPSCs) are comparable to the embryonic preimplantation and post-implantation epiblast, respectively (Guo et al., 2016; Stirparo et al., 2018; Takashima et al., 2014; Theunissen et al., 2014). Numerous studies reported differentiation of TSC-like cells from naive and primed hPSC, although the identity of bona fide TSC is challenging (Castel et al., 2020; Cinkornpumin et al., 2020; Dong et al., 2020; Io et al., 2021; Jang et al., 2022; Viukov et al., 2022; Wei et al., 2021). Our current knowledge of cell specification during early mammalian preimplantation development stems from studies on mice, yet species-specific differences exists. (Gerri et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2022; Rossant and Tam, 2009). In mice, the transcription factor E74-like factor 5 (Elf5) is essential for maintenance of the trophoblast (Donnison et al., 2005; Latos et al., 2015) and the hypomethylation of Elf5/ELF5 promoter marks TSC state in both mouse and human (Hemberger et al., 2010; Lee et al., 2016; Ng et al., 2008). Human terminal CT cells can also be reprogrammed into TSC-like cells using transcription factor cocktails including ELF5 (Bai et al., 2021). However, the role of ELF5 in human TSC-like cells derived from hPSC remains to be clarified.

In this study, we aimed to clarify these ambiguities by differentiating TSC-like cells from isogenic naive and primed pluripotent stem cells using established protocols (Dong et al., 2020; Wei et al., 2021). We analyzed transcriptional profiles using bulk and single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) and compared them to reference TSC lines (Okae et al., 2018) and in vivo cell types using a comprehensive human embryogenesis reference tool (Zhao et al., 2024). Finally, we used our TSC-like cell models to investigate the ELF5 function, revealing that ELF5 is dispensable for the maintenance and differentiation of hPSC-derived TSC-like cells.

Results

Establishment of TSC from naive and primed hPSC

Recent studies reported the derivation of TSC-like cells from both naive and primed hPSC (Dong et al., 2020; Okae et al., 2018; Wei et al., 2021). To recapitulate those findings, we used the well-characterized primed human embryonic stem cell (ESCp) line H9, which we reset to the naive state (ESCn) and expanded under tt2iLGö medium (Guo et al., 2017; Takashima et al., 2014) (Figure S1A).

Isogenic ESCn and ESCp were differentiated into TSC-like cells with ESCp requiring a 5-day BMP4 supplementation (Figure 1A). Both ESCn and ESCp lost their typical morphologies within one day after switching to the TSC medium and by day 3 resembled TSC cells (Figure 1B). Consistent with the morphological changes, naive- and primed-derived TSC-like cells (TSCn and TSCp) lost the undifferentiated hPSC markers (Figure S1B) and started expressing TSC markers GATA3, KRT7, and TFAP2C (Figures 1C and 1D). Both TSCn and TSCp could be grown and maintained for over 30 passages while maintaining normal karyotype (Figure S1C).

To functionally validate the derived TSC, they were differentiated into SCT and EVT cells using established protocols (Okae et al., 2018; Wei et al., 2021). Both naive- and primed-derived SCT (SCTn and SCTp) had characteristic multinucleated cell morphology (Figure 1E) and expressed SCT markers human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) subunit beta (CGB) and SDC1 (Figures 1F and 1G). While the CGB expressed over 7-fold higher in SCTn (Figure 1G), both SCTn and SCTp still tested positive for hCG expression (Figure 1H). EVT differentiation was inefficient without the additional Wnt inhibitors Wnt-C59 and XAV939, MEK/ERK pathway inhibitor PD032590, and ROCK inhibitor thiazovivin (Wei et al., 2021) (Figure S1D). Upon differentiation, TSC acquired EVT-like morphology (Figure 1E) and expressed EVT-specific markers HLA-G and MMP2 (Figures 1F and 1G). Taken together, these results showed that both ESCn and ESCp can differentiate into functional TSC-like cells.

RNA sequencings reveal transient upregulation of amniotic genes and lack of C19MC expression in TSCp

To assess whether TSCn and TSCp exhibit trophoblast transcriptomic signatures, total RNA from isogenic ESCn, ESCp, TSCn, and TSCp was sequenced. For reference, published RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) data from CT, stromal cells, and blastocyst- and first trimester-derived TSC (TSBlast and TSCT) (Okae et al., 2018) was integrated. Correlation analysis revealed a high similarity between TSCn, TSCp and reference samples CT, TSBlast, and TSCT, while ESCn, ESCp, and stromal cells showed low correlation with the TSC samples (Figure 2A).

There is still considerable controversy about whether treating primed ESC with BMP4 could potentially lead to the development of amnion and mesoderm fates alongside trophoblasts (Bernardo et al., 2011; Guo et al., 2021; Io et al., 2021; Roberts et al., 2014; Seetharam et al., 2022). While there was no significant difference in the expression of trophoblast marker genes, we observed an upregulation of amniotic marker genes such as GABRP, TNC, CDH10, HMGA2, and HAND1 in TSCp compared to TSCn (Figures 2B, S2A, and S2B). Gene ontology analyses showed TSCn enrichment for terms associated with cell cycle progression and proliferation, indicating proliferative, stem-like state (Figure S2C). TSCp upregulated pathways related to extracellular matrix and circulatory system development. However, gene ontology analysis also revealed terms such as synapse assembly and ossification, indicating a possible aberrant or non-canonical expression profile rather than a more committed trophoblast state (Figure S2C).

To investigate trophoblast and amniotic gene expression during TSCn and TSCp differentiation, RNA was collected at four time points (ESC, differentiation days 3 and 6, and TSC). As a control, TSBlast (bTS5) and TSCT (CT30) cell lines (Okae et al., 2018) were cultured and RNA collected from them (Figure S3A). Analysis of samples collected on differentiation days 3 and 6 showed higher expression of trophoblast marker GATA3 in TSCn compared to TSCp (Figure 2C). KRT7 was expressed at similar levels in TSCn and TSCp differentiation and was significantly higher in bTS5 compared to TSCn (Figure 2C). TFAP2C expression was higher in ESCn and at differentiation days 3 and 6 compared to ESCp differentiation (Figure 2C). Amniotic marker genes GABPR, TNC, and CDH10 expressed significantly higher levels during the TSCp differentiation with the highest expression on day 6 (Figure 2C). Following the removal of BMP4 from the cell culture, there was a significant decrease in the expression of amniotic markers from day 6 to the TSCp (Figure 2C).

Isogenic H9 line was also differentiated using the BAP-protocol (Amita et al., 2013), resulting in significantly higher expression of amniotic markers compared to TSCp (Figures S3B and S3C). To examine the emergence of amnion-like cells in TSC cultures over time, TSCn and TSCp were fixed during TSC differentiation and stained for GABPR. GABRP+ cells were absent in TSCn differentiation and were most abundant around the outer edges of TSC-like colonies during TSCp differentiation on days 3 and 6, with most of them disappearing after the exclusion of BMP4 (Figure 2D).

Previous studies have reported differential expression of the trophoblast marker C19MC micro-RNA (miRNA) cluster between TSC-like cells derived from ESCn and ESCp (Kobayashi et al., 2022; Lee et al., 2016). To assess this, small RNA-seq was performed on our TSC and ESC cells. The results showed that TSCn had high global miRNA correlation with bTS5 and CT30 and expressed C19MC miRNAs at comparable or even higher levels. In contrast, TSCp lacked C19MC expression, except some class II C19MC miRNAs expressed downstream of an independent second promoter toward the end of C19MC region (Kobayashi et al., 2022) (Figures 2E and S3D).

Therefore, we concluded that while both TSCn and TSCp exhibit transcriptional similarity to bona fide TSC (Okae et al., 2018), TSCp lack C19MC expression and transiently express amnion- and somatic-associated genes indicating that they represent a trophoblast-like but aberrant population.

Single-cell sequencing shows differences in differentiation speeds

To investigate transcriptomic changes at a single-cell level occurring during naive and primed TSC differentiation, samples were collected at four different time points (ESC, differentiation days 3 and 6, and early passage 3 TSC) (Figure 3A). As a reference, bTS5 and CT30 cultured in our laboratory were also sequenced. Unsupervised clustering identified twelve distinct cell clusters (Figure 3B). Clusters 9–12 (52% of the cells) highly expressed trophoblast markers GATA3, KRT7, and TFAP2C with cluster 9 additionally expressing SCT markers SDC1, CGB, and GCM1 (Figure 3C). ESC clusters 6 and 7 (24.5% of cells) were enriched with pluripotency markers OCT4, SOX2, and NANOG (Figure 3C). Clusters 1 and 2 expressed hypoblast (PDGFRA, GATA6, and GATA4) and extraembryonic mesoderm (ExeMes; VIM, LUM, and POSTN [Pham et al., 2022]) markers (Figure 3C). Amnion-like clusters 3, 4, and 8 expressed amniotic genes GABRP, TNC, and CDH10 with cluster 8 also expressing trophoblast markers (Figure 3C).

Distribution of cells revealed that majority of reference TSC lines bTS5 and CT30 (91.4% and 90.6%, respectively) were in TSC clusters and most of the ESCn and ESCp cells (89.4% and 94.4%, respectively) were located in ESC clusters (Figures 3D and S4A). By day 3, 45.4% of TSCn cells had transitioned to TSC clusters, while 91.4% of day 3 TSCp cells still remained in ESC cluster (Figures 3D and S4A). Consistent with this, TSCn showed downregulation of pluripotency markers and upregulation of trophoblast markers at day 3 while TSCp showed similar changes later at day 6 (Figure 3E). Notably, 85.8% of day 6 TSCp localized in amnion clusters while 34% of day 6 TSCn expressed hypoblast and ExeMes markers (Figures 3D and 3E), but this expression was absent in the fully differentiated TSCn (Figure 3E). 98.7% of TSCn and 87.4% of TSCp grouped in TSC clusters (Figure 3D). 25.2% of TSCn and 22.1% of bTS5 were also located in TSC cluster 9 expressing SCT markers, indicating spontaneous differentiation toward the SCT lineage (Figures 3D, 3E, and S4A).

Analysis of the global marker expression for the TSC clusters 10–12 revealed that all three clusters shared key trophoblast genes, including GATA2, GATA3, KRT7, and TP63 (Figure S4B). Cluster 10, predominantly composed of TSCn, showed higher expression of TFAP2C and SCT-related markers such as GCM1 compared to the other TSC clusters (Figure S4B). However, differentially expressed gene analysis did not show significant up- or downregulation of these core trophoblast markers between the three TSC clusters (Figures 3F and S4C). Cluster 10 showed upregulation of genes related to SCT and Wnt-signaling pathway, including CGA, GCM1, and DLX5 (Figure 3F). Gene ontology analysis further identified upregulation of early developmental processes such as Wnt signaling and placenta development, suggesting a progenitor-like state (Figure 3G). TSC clusters 11 and 12, composed mainly of TSCp and control TSCs, were enriched for pathways related to mitosis and epithelial organization (Figure S4D). Cluster 12 also exhibited upregulation of genes involved in cell adhesion, integrin signaling, and cytoskeletal remodeling, indicating a more functionally committed trophoblast phenotype (Figure S4D).

Overall, the scRNA-seq analysis showed that ESCn differentiates rapidly toward a TSC-like fate, whereas ESCp exhibit a delayed exit from pluripotency, transiently expressing an amnion-like identity before committing to a TSC-like fate.

Annotation to embryo reference reveals distinct differentiation trajectories

Next, our scRNA-seq data were compared to published scRNA-seq datasets from human early embryo development using recently published early embryo reference tool (Zhao et al., 2024) (Figure 4A). At the start of the differentiations, 96.1% of ESCn and 99.9% of ESCp were annotated as epiblast (Figure 4B). By day 3, 88.2% of ESCn had transitioned out of the epiblast and were annotated as TE (31.2%), cytotrophoblast (CTB, 13.4%), ExeMes (34.8%), or hypoblast (8.4%) (Figures 4B and 4C). By day 6, TE/CTB/SCT cell populations had increased to 63.4% (Figure 4B).

In contrast, by day 3, 75.5% of ESCp-derived cells were still annotated as epiblast with rest of the cells annotating to a prelineage state (Figures 4B and 4C). By day 6, only 9.2% of the cells were annotated as TE/CTB/SCT, while the rest were annotated as amnion (12.2%), ExeMes (21.3%), or advanced mesoderm (46.4%) (Figures 4B and 4C). At the end of the differentiations, over 99% of each bTS5, CT30, TSCn, and TSCp cells were annotated as TE/CTB/SCT (Figure 4B). Therefore, we concluded that while both TSCn and TSCp are transcriptionally similar to the trophoblast lineage cells of developing human embryo, TSCp transition more slowly out of the epiblast state showing a transient amnion-like population.

ELF5 is dispensable in TSC maintenance and growth

As scRNA-seq data indicated that TSC derived from hPSC closely resemble the placenta- and embryo-derived cells, we used this system to study the role of ELF5 in TSC. The methylation status of ELF5 is a valuable marker for identifying trophoblasts (Lee et al., 2016), so in order to investigate the methylation of the ELF5 promoter in our cells, bisulfite sequencing was performed on ESCn, ESCp, TSCn, and TSCp. The ELF5 promoter region was hypermethylated in ESCp and partially methylated in ESCn, while hypomethylated in both TSCn and TSCp (Figure 5A). However, the expression levels of ELF5 were low in all TSC samples regardless of their origin (Figure 5B).

To further investigate the function of ELF5 in TSC lines, siRNAs were used against ELF5 in CT30. ELF5 knockdown cells expressed comparable levels of GATA3, KRT7, and TFAP2C compared to their untreated counterparts, suggesting that ELF5 might not be required to maintain trophoblast cell identity (Figure 5C). Next, ELF5 knockout was generated in the primed human induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC) line HEL24.3. Early exon 2 common to all splice variants of ELF5 was targeted using CRISPR-Cas9 (Figure 5D). This generated a frameshift mutation, resulting in a non-functional protein. Status of ELF5 was assessed using genomic PCR and immunocytochemistry, identifying homozygous ELF5 knockouts (Figures 5E and S5A). Isogenic wild-type HEL24.3 and homozygous knockout line were then differentiated into TSC (TSCWT and TSCKO) using the same protocol as with the TSCp. Both TSCWT and TSCKO acquired typical trophoblast morphology and expressed the TSC markers GATA3, KRT7, and TFAP2C (Figures 5F and S5B).

To assess whether ELF5 deficiency altered gene expression at a single-cell level, TSCWT and TSCKO cells were collected for scRNA-seq. New sequencing data were combined with our previous scRNA data (Figure 3) and the distribution of cells showed 45.1% of TSCWT and 27.7% of TSCKO cells locating in amnion cluster 8, while TSC clusters contained 54.6% and 71.9% of cells, respectively (Figures S5C and S5D). All TSC lines showed similar expression of TSC markers expressions, while some TSCWT and TSCKO cells showed enriched expression of amnion and ExeMes markers compared to the other TSC lines (Figure 5G). Using the online embryo prediction tool to annotate cell types in TSCWT and TSCKO, 59.0% of TSCWT and 85.2% of TSCKO cells were annotated as TE/CTB/SCT lineage while 39.2% of TSCWT and 14.8% of TSCKO cells were annotated as amnion, hypoblast, or ExeMes (Figures S5E and S5F).

In mice, Elf5 controls the proliferative capacity and differentiation of TSC (Donnison et al., 2005; Latos et al., 2015). Thus, the proliferative capacity of ELF5 knockdown (TSCKD) and TSCKO lines was tested by comparing them to their isogenic TSCn and TSCWT lines. Using live-cell imaging for 120 h, there was no reduction in the growth rates of TSCKD and TSCKO lines (Figure 5H). To assess functionality and developmental potential, TSCn, TSCKD, TSCWT, and TSCKO were all differentiated successfully into EVT and SCT (Figures S5G–S5I). Our results indicate that ELF5 is dispensable for the self-renewal and differentiation oh hPSC-derived TSC-like cells, suggesting a species-specific differences in ELF5 function between mouse and human.

Discussion

In this study, we derived TSC-like cells from both naive and primed hPSC. The identity of these cells was validated by cell morphology, marker expression, bulk and scRNA-seq, ability to differentiate into SCT and EVT and secretion of hCG. These findings align with previous reports (Castel et al., 2020; Cinkornpumin et al., 2020; Dong et al., 2020; Io et al., 2021; Jang et al., 2022; Viukov et al., 2022; Wei et al., 2021) and support the reproducibility of these protocols across different laboratory settings.

Transcriptional analyses indicate that TSCn and TSCp resemble bona fide TSC but also exhibit distinct transcriptional features. TSCp exhibited transient expression of amniotic marker genes such as GABRP, TNC, and CDH10, which were absent in TSCn. This suggests that ESCp differentiate toward amnion-like lineages in the presence of BMP4, consistent earlier studies showing BMP4 inducing multiple lineage outcomes in primed hPSC (Bernardo et al., 2011; Guo et al., 2021).

Gene ontology analysis indicated that TSCn are enriched for cell cycle and ribosome biogenesis genes, suggesting a proliferative and less lineage-committed state typical for early trophoblast progenitors (Dong et al., 2020; Io et al., 2021; Okae et al., 2018). In contrast, TSCp showed enrichment in extracellular matrix organization, adhesion, and circulatory system development pathways, suggestive of divergent or atypical transcriptional state.

Small RNA-seq revealed that TSCn express C19MC, a key marker of TSC identity, while TSCp lack expression of most C19MC miRNAs (Lee et al., 2016). This aligns with previous reports showing that C19MC is epigenetically silenced in primed cells (Kobayashi et al., 2022). While the same study suggested that TSCp fail to proliferate due to C19MC silencing, we did not observe the same effect in our TSCp. Discrepancies between our findings and the previous report may originate from differences in the TSCp differentiation protocol used or the passage at which TSCp were assessed for proliferation capacity. Our scRNA-seq analysis revealed that ESCn differentiate more directly and rapidly into TSCn, compared to ESCp, which showed delayed transition and retained a substantial proportion of pluripotent cells at day 3. This slower progression in primed hPSC may explain the transient expression of non-trophoblast-like or atypical lineages. UMAP clustering further highlighted distinct differentiation trajectories between TSCn and TSCp. TSCn followed a more defined path toward trophoblast fates, while TSCp exhibited mixed lineage identity at intermediate stages of differentiation.

We used our TSC models to assess the role of the ELF5 transcription factor in TSC maintenance and differentiation. While ELF5 play a critical role in trophoblast development in mice (Donnison et al., 2005; Hemberger et al., 2009, 2010; Kong et al., 2018; Latos et al., 2015; Ng et al., 2008; Pearton et al., 2014), its relevance in human TSC biology remains unclear. The methylation status of ELF5 is used as criteria for defining human trophoblasts (Lee et al., 2016; Okae et al., 2018). Our data showed that although ELF5 promoter was hypomethylated in all TSC models, neither ELF5 knockdown nor knockout affected TSC morphology, proliferation, or differentiation into SCT and EVT. Transcriptomic analysis of TSCKO and isogenic control TSCWT did not show significant differences in the trophoblast marker expressions. Using the online embryo prediction tool (Zhao et al., 2024), we observed a shift in the annotation of cell types with a higher proportion of TSCKO and TSCWT annotated as amnion, hypoblast, or ExeMes compared to the other TSC lines. These observed differences might be due to the lower differentiation propensity of the HEL24.3 iPSC line compared to the human ESCp line H9. Nonetheless, the overall capacity of TSCKO to maintain trophoblast identity and differentiation remained the same to isogenic control, indicating that ELF5 is dispensable for the maintenance and differentiation of hPSC-derived TSC-like cells.

ELF5 expression remained consistently low across all our TSC models, aligning with reports of variable ELF5 expression levels in human in vitro TSC models (Dong et al., 2020, 2022; Shimizu et al., 2023; Wei et al., 2021). It has also been reported that there are different trophoblast subtypes in 2D and 3D stem cell-based trophoblast cultures, so it is worth considering that hPSC-derived TSC models may represent a later stage of trophoblast development in which ELF5 is less critical or plays a distinct role in human compared to mice (Shannon et al., 2024; Sheridan et al., 2021). However, considering that the ELF5 expression is virtually non-existent in both our data and in the combined early human embryo datasets (Meistermann et al., 2021; Petropoulos et al., 2016; Tyser et al., 2021; Xiang et al., 2020; Yan et al., 2013; Yanagida et al., 2021; Zhao et al., 2024), this would suggest that ELF5 simply does not have the same critical role in human trophoblast development as in mouse. This could suggest compensatory mechanisms or species-specific differences in human TSC maintenance. Other ELF-family factors expressed in human trophoblast (Li et al., 2021) could therefore have a more prominent role in maintaining human TSC identity instead of ELF5.

In conclusion, we derived functional TSC-like cells from both naive and primed hPSC, with TSCn demonstrating a more direct differentiation trajectory. Transcriptomic analyses revealed that while TSCp briefly expressed atypical non-trophoblast markers, both TSCp and TSCn show transcriptional alignment with human trophoblast lineage but differ in C19MC expressions. Our findings confirm that ELF5 is not required for the maintenance or differentiation of hPSC-derived TSC and that further study into the regulatory networks of human trophoblast development is needed. Limitations of our study include basic naive ESC characterization and therefore we cannot exclude that some of the observations are linked to the resetting protocol, as well as the limited genetic background (H9 and HEL24.3) used in experiments.

Methods

Additional methods and more in-detail descriptions of bulk- and scRNA-seq and ELF5 KO generation can be found in the supplemental experimental procedures.

Cell culture

ESCp and iPSC were cultured on wells coated with Matrigel (Corning) in E8 (Gibco) or E8 Flex (Gibco) medium. The medium was changed every other day, and the cells were passaged with EDTA once the cells reached 80% confluency at 1:4-1:6 ratio. ESCp were reset to ESCn using NaïveCult Induction kit (Stem Cell Technologies, 05580) and further cultured on mouse (ICR) inactivated embryonic fibroblasts (iMEF, Thermo Fisher Scientific, A24903) in tt2iLGö Naive Expansion medium (Stem Cell Technologies, 05590). ESCn were passaged every four days by dissociating cells with Accutase (Gibco) for 3 min, after which the iMEF feeder layer was removed with pipette tip. Cells were resuspended in tt2iLGö and seeded on new iMEF wells.

TSC were cultured in plates coated with 5 μg/mL Collagen-IV (Col-IV, Corning) in TSC medium (basal DMEM/F12 + GlutaMax supplemented with 0.1 mM 2-mercaptoethanol [Gibco], 0.2% fetal bovine serum [Gibco], 0.5% penicillin-streptomycin [Sigma-Aldrich, P4333], 0.3% BSA [Sigma, A7030-500G], 1% insulin-transferrin-selenium-ethanolamine [Gibco, 51500056], 1.5 μg/mL L-ascorbic acid [Sigma, A4544], 50 ng/mL EGF [Peprotech, AF-100-15-1MG], 2 μM CHIR99021 [Stemgent, 04-0004-10], 0.5 μM A83-01 [Sigma, SML0788-5MG], 1 μM SB431542 [Cayman Chemicals, 13031], 0.8 mM sodium butyrate [Sigma, B5887-1G], and 5 μM Y-27632 [Selleckchem, S1049]). The medium was changed every other day, and the cells were passaged with TrypLE (Thermo Fisher Scientific, 12563011) for 10–15 min once the cells reached 80% confluency at 1:4-1:6 ratio. All cells were kept in an incubator at 37°C and 5% CO2 and were routinely tested for mycoplasma contamination.

TSC differentiation from ESCn

ESCn (1 × 106) were passaged with Accutase for 3 min to achieve a single-cell suspension. Cells were seeded in TSC medium on 6-well plates coated with 5 μg/mL Col-IV. Medium was changed every other day, until the cells grew 80% confluent and were passaged 1:1 onto new Col-IV coated wells with 10 min TrypLE. The cells started proliferating faster after the initial passaging. The medium was changed every other day and once the cells were 80% confluent, they were passaged on 1:3 ratio onto new Col-IV-coated wells.

TSC differentiation from ESCp and iPSC

ESCp or iPSC (4 × 105) were passaged with TrypLE for 3 min to achieve a single-cell suspension. Cells were seeded on Matrigel coated wells with E8 medium supplemented with 10 μM Y-27632. After 24 h, medium was changed to TSC-medium supplemented with 10 ng/mL BMP4 (Proteintech, HZ-1045). Medium was changed daily, and on day 3, differentiating cells were passaged 1:3 onto a new well with EDTA. After 5 days, BMP4 was cut from the cells, and the cells were continued to be cultured in TSC-medium. Medium was changed every other day and the cells were passaged with 10 min TrypLE on 1:3 ratio when the wells were 80% confluent.

Bisulfite sequencing

ESC and TSC samples were bisulfite converted using EZ DNA Methylation kit D5002 (Zymo Research) according to the manual. After the conversion, the ELF5 promoter region was amplified with nested PCR using primers described in Lee et al., (2016). The amplicons were purified from the agarose gel with Nucleospin Gel an PCR clean-up kit (Macherey-Nagel) and ligated into pGEM-T Easy vector (Promega).

The plasmid vectors were transformed into New England Biolabs 5α transformation competent E. coli cells (NEB5) and plated onto LB-plates supplemented with 100 μg/mL ampicillin, 40 μl of 100 mM Isopropyl-β-D-thiogalactopyranoside (BioLine), and 15 μL of 50 mg/mL X-Gal (Promega). Eight colonies were picked based on white-blue selection from each plate and sent for sequencing as a service at Institute of Molecular Medicine Finland (FIMM) Genomics unit. The sequencing results and methylation status of sample CpG islets was analyzed using BiQ Analyzer software.

Bulk RNA sequencing

Total RNA was extracted from 12 samples, including ESC and TSC cells and purified by NucleoSpin RNA Plus kit (Macherey-Nagel). Bulk RNA-seq was performed as a service at Novogen after the cells passed quality control. Samples were sequenced using Illumina PE150. The expression profiles of the RNA-seq data were compared to published reference datasets of TSC and stromal cells (Okae et al., 2018) based on the top 1,000 most highly variable protein-coding genes, with batch correction performed using the removeBatchEffect function of limma (Ritchie et al., 2015).

Single-cell RNA sequencing

Cells were dissociated with Accutase at 37°C for 5 min, resuspended in PBS +0.04% BSA and passed through a Flowmi tip strainer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) to yield a single-cell suspension. The quality of the samples was assessed using a Luna cell counter (Logos Biosystems). scRNA-seq was performed using the 10× Genomics Chromium Single-Cell 3′RNA-seq platform at FIMM single-cell analytics core facility.

Small RNA sequencing

In order to sequence C19MC miRNA expressions, small RNA-seq was performed at Novogen after the samples passed quality control. Total RNA including miRNA were extracted using miRNEasy Mini Kit (QIAGEN, 217004) from TSCn, TSCp, ESCn, ESCp, CT30, and bTS5 samples with three biological replicates each. The raw data were filtered with cutadapt (Martin, 2011) to remove adapter sequences, ambiguous (N), and low quality bases (Phred score <25). The miRNA reads were annotated using miRDeep2 (Friedländer et al., 2012). The reads were mapped against the human genome (GRCh38) with the provided mapper.pl-script, and known miRNA were identified using miRBase (v22.1) (Kozomara et al., 2019). DESeq2 (Love et al., 2014) was used for differential expression analysis of the detected miRNA.

ELF5 siRNA and KO generation

TSC grown on 12-well plates were transfected with Silencer Select siRNAs (Thermo Fisher Scientific, 4392420 s4630) targeting ELF5 using RNAiMAX Lipofectamine (Thermo Fisher Scientific, 13778030) reagent. After three days, the cells were collected for RT-qPCR analysis.

For the generation of ELF5 knockout line, guide RNAs targeting ELF5 exon 2 region (5′ GTG CCT GCT CCT AGT GAG TG 3′ and 5′ CTC TGC CCT GAA CTC TGG TG 3′) were designed with Benchling online software (Biology software, https://benchling.com) and ordered from Integrated DNA technologies (Alt-R CRISPR-Cas9 crRNA). crRNAs were resuspended in nuclease free (pH 7.5) IDTE buffer (Integrated DNA Technologies, 11010202) to a concentration of 200 μM.

Toward new therapy end points in polycythemia vera: targeting clonal and inflammatory pathways
Authors: Tiziano Barbui, Joseph Michael Scandura
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAlthough phlebotomy and hydroxyurea (HU) are standard first-line therapies for polycythemia vera (PV), they do not adequately address clonal expansion and chronic inflammation, which are key drivers of thrombosis, myelofibrosis, and mortality. Biomarkers such as JAK2 V617F variant allele frequency (VAF) and the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) are emerging as valuable tools for guiding therapy. Ropeginterferon alfa-2b has been shown to reduce both JAK2 VAF and NLR, improving event-free survival, as demonstrated in the Low-PV and PROUD-PV/CONTINUATION-PV trials. In contrast, propensity score matching of the European Collaborative Low-Dose Aspirin trial showed that HU has a limited effect on these biomarkers, suggesting weaker disease-modifying potential. Although no data on NLR dynamics with ruxolitinib have been published, the anti-inflammatory effects of ruxolitinib and its suppression of JAK2 VAF suggest it may exert similar biological activity. These findings support a shift toward biology-guided treatment in PV, recognizing that inflammation and JAK2 VAF could serve as surrogate end points in future clinical trials.

Introduction

The management of polycythemia vera (PV) remains primarily guided by conventional risk stratification. Current treatment algorithms are largely determined by patient age and prior history of thrombosis, which dictate the use of phlebotomy, low-dose aspirin, and, in higher-risk cases, cytoreductive therapy.1, 2, 3, 4 Although these strategies have been successful in reducing the incidence of vascular complications, they are fundamentally palliative and do not modify the natural history of the disease. Importantly, such approaches fail to target the biological mechanisms underlying clonal expansion, chronic inflammation, and genomic instability, which are key drivers of disease progression, transformation to myelofibrosis (MF) or acute leukemia, and ultimately patient survival.

The pathobiology of PV is complex and is shaped by recurrent driver mutations (JAK2 V617F), clonal kinetics, chronic inflammation, and nondriver mutations.5, 6, 7 This interplay results in heterogeneous outcomes that are not well captured by clinical parameters alone. Consequently, there is growing interest in biomarkers that reflect disease biology and provide prognostic and therapeutic guidance. Although no single parameter can encompass the full complexity of myeloproliferative neoplasms (MPNs), 2 markers have emerged as particularly informative, with JAK2 variant allele frequency (VAF) reflecting the extent of clonal expansion and being associated with thrombosis, progression, and survival.6, 7, 8 Similarly, the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) has demonstrated its ability to serve as an accessible surrogate of systemic inflammation, reflecting the immune dysregulation underlying vascular events and disease progression.9, 10, 11 Both markers are dynamically modulated by therapy and capture complementary dimensions of MPN biology. Although full surrogate validation has not yet been achieved, we present this work as a perspective that frames JAK2 VAF and NLR as candidate biomarkers whose prospective assessment in trials is both feasible and timely.

This study explores how this dual-biomarker framework could inform a new approach to PV management, aligning treatment decisions and clinical trial end points more closely with the biology of clonality and inflammation.

JAK2 VAF, a biologically meaningful biomarker of events in PV

The JAK2 V617F mutation is the defining genetic lesion in most patients with PV, and its VAF has both prognostic and therapeutic significance, supporting its use as a measurable indicator of disease burden and therapeutic response.5,6,12 Clinically, a high JAK2 V617F VAF (>50%) is an independent risk factor for venous thrombosis13, 14, 15 and progression to MF,16,17 reflecting the link between clonal expansion and adverse outcomes. As a marker of treatment effect, reductions in JAK2 V617F VAF correlate with meaningful clinical benefit.5 However, only prospective, temporally-standardized studies can determine whether VAF-guided treatment decisions sufficiently improve outcomes to establish surrogate validity. Until then, VAF is best regarded as an emerging biomarker and a practical molecular readout of disease activity.

Interferon alfa, such as ropeginterferon alfa-2b, induces sustained, dose- and time-dependent decreases in allele burden that correlate with improvements in event-free survival.18, 19, 20 With long-term ropeginterferon treatment, 21% of patients achieved deep molecular remission (VAF < 1%) at 6 years, compared to 1.4% of patients in the control group. High VAF (>50%) was also much less frequent with ropeginterferon treatment than with hydroxyurea (HU) treatment (11.6% vs 50%). Importantly, patients with a VAF of ∼65% had almost twice the risk of events compared with those with a VAF of ∼30%, underscoring the importance of sustained clonal suppression.

Further evidence from ruxolitinib studies supports this role. In both the MAJIC-PV trial21 and the prospective study by Guglielmelli et al,22 sustained reductions in JAK2 V617F VAF under ruxolitinib were associated with improved survival and a reduced risk of progression, in line with findings of interferon studies. Critically, after allogeneic transplantation, early clearance of JAK2 V617F predicted reduced relapse and improved survival, emphasizing that molecular remission is clinically meaningful.23 Furthermore, novel agents such as BET inhibitors (eg, pelabresib) are already using JAK2 V617F VAF reduction as a biomarker of therapeutic response in clinical trials.24

Although JAK2 V617F remains the central molecular hallmark of PV, nondriver mutations provide additional prognostic refinement. Mutations in SRSF2, IDH1/2, EZH2, or NFE2 have been linked to adverse outcomes in PV, potentially due to their impact on the extent to which JAK2 is suppressed.25,26 Therefore, nondriver mutations may act as modifiers of risk and treatment sensitivity without replacing JAK2 V617F VAF as the primary molecular marker in PV.

Reductions in JAK2 V617F VAF are consistently associated with fewer vascular events, improved event-free survival (EFS), and higher rates of deep molecular response, not only with interferon but also with ruxolitinib, transplantation, and emerging therapies. Although nondriver mutations refine prognosis, these correlations establish JAK2 V617F VAF as a meaningful marker of disease burden and treatment effect in PV, but it is not yet a validated surrogate end point. To reach that standard, studies must demonstrate that treatment decisions explicitly guided by VAF levels (such as drug choice or dose adjustment) improve outcomes beyond conventional management, as shown for hematocrit control in the cytoreductive therapy in PV trial.27 At present, VAF is most strongly linked to EFS; extending this to overall survival (OS) or progression-free survival remains an important goal for ongoing and future trials.

NLR, a biomarker integrating inflammatory and clonal activity

A growing body of evidence highlights the pivotal role of inflammation in the pathogenesis of MPNs.8,28,29 Among the available markers of systemic inflammation, NLR has emerged as a particularly useful marker because it can be easily derived from standard blood tests, is reproducible in clinical practice, and is biologically meaningful. Elevated NLR is widely recognized as a predictor of poorer outcomes across various tumor types.30, 31, 32, 33, 34

In MPN, it has repeatedly been associated with a higher incidence of vascular events, shorter OS, and more rapid disease progression.10,11

Most recently, the dynamics of NLR have been explored in detail using patient-level data from 3 of the largest prospective trials conducted in PV.9,18 The rationale behind this line of investigation is that the JAK2 V617F mutation causes an increase in the number of cells in the myeloid compartment, whereas also impairing lymphoid differentiation.35 This imbalance results in an elevated NLR and may help to explain why this marker is so significant in terms of prognosis. In this context, changes in NLR over time may reflect not only the degree of systemic inflammation, but also the molecular effects of therapy on clonal dynamics.

In an analysis stratifying patients by baseline NLR levels, a threshold of 3.5 was chosen, corresponding to the median value of the study cohort.9 Of 126 patients included, those with NLR values of ≥3.5 (n = 65) differed markedly from those with an NLR of <3.5 (n = 61). Patients in the higher NLR group had a substantially longer interval from diagnosis to trial entry (21.4 vs 7.2 months; P = .004), a higher prevalence of splenomegaly (41.5% vs 20%; P = .012), and a significantly higher JAK2 V617F VAF (56% vs 20%; P < .001). They also displayed classical indicators of proliferative disease activity, such as higher white blood cell counts, increased levels of lactate dehydrogenase, and bone marrow hypercellularity. Taken together, these findings indicate that NLR may integrate the disease biology of systemic inflammation and clonal aggressiveness.

Therapeutic modulation of NLR has been particularly evident with interferon-based therapy. In the Low-PV trial,36,37 patients treated with ropeginterferon alfa-2b at a starting dose of 100 μg every 2 weeks experienced a marked decline in median NLR after 12 months of treatment compared with patients treated by phlebotomy alone.18 Importantly, this reduction was most pronounced among those with higher baseline NLR (Figure 1A).

The decline in NLR strongly paralleled reductions in JAK2 V617F allele burden (Figure 1B), reinforcing the concept that this ratio captures the interconnectedness of inflammatory activity and malignant clonal expansion.

Comparable results were found in the PROUD/CONTINUATION-PV studies19,20; over 60 months, Ropeg (n = 127) achieved deeper and more sustained reductions in NLR than HU (n = 127), particularly in patients with elevated baseline values.18 The effects of HU diminished over time, whereas Ropeg maintained progressive suppression, highlighting its stronger control of PV inflammation. Importantly, NLR reduction correlated with JAK2 V617F VAF decline, underscoring Ropeg’s superior ability to modulate both inflammation and clonal burden compared with HU (Figure 2A-B).

Notably, patients with sustained reductions in NLR demonstrated significantly improved EFS, further supporting its prognostic and therapeutic relevance (supplemental Figure 1).18

Moreover, in the MAJIC-PV trial of ruxolitinib, when compared with the best available care in HU-resistant/intolerant PV, suggest that JAK2 VAF decline was linked to prolonged EFS.21 However, as for JAK2 V617F VAF, prospective standardization and time-aligned validation of NLR remain necessary to establish it as a surrogate for EFS or other important PV outcomes.

The failure of HU to reduce NLR raises important questions about its biological activity beyond cytoreduction.

Although HU is well established as an effective cytoreductive agent, it does not directly influence JAK2 allele burden, which may explain its limited effect on NLR. To investigate this more rigorously, NLR dynamics under HU and phlebotomy were examined using the European Collaborative Low-Dose Aspirin database, including 1638 patients who were either managed with phlebotomy alone or with HU at doses ranging from 500 to 1500 mg per day.18,38 The treatment goal was to maintain hematocrit levels at <45% and platelet counts at <400 × 109/L. Using propensity score matching, 207 patients treated with HU were compared with 207 patients treated with phlebotomy alone. An excellent balance was achieved between the 2 groups with respect to demographics, disease history, cardiovascular comorbidities, follow-up time, and baseline hematologic parameters. However, after 12 months of therapy, HU treatment was not associated with any meaningful reduction in NLR values (Figure 3A), suggesting that its impact on inflammation is limited compared with interferon.18

Despite HU therapy, NLR remained persistently elevated, prompting further analyses of its clinical significance. In a time-dependent multivariable model, NLR of ≥3.3 was independently associated with a higher thrombosis risk, whereas leukocytosis lost significance (Figure 3B). Persistently high NLR also predicted shortened OS, reinforcing its value as a more reliable biomarker of inflammatory disease activity than leukocyte count in HU-treated PV.

Joint models were used to account for interindividual variability (eg, laboratory differences) and transient inflammatory fluctuations (eg, infections; supplemental Table 1). Each 1-unit increase in NLR was associated with ∼10% higher risk of thrombotic events and death, independent of HU use, age, or cardiovascular risk factors. Together, these findings suggest that although HU effectively controls hematocrit and leukocytosis, it fails to modify the inflammatory and clonal components of PV that drive long-term complications.

Mechanistic insights into NLR

Chronic inflammation in PV is not a secondary phenomenon but rather a central driver of the disease, reinforcing clonal dominance and fueling progression. Beyond their role in host defense, neutrophils contribute to thrombosis and tumor progression through NETosis, a process first described by Brinkmann et al39 and later shown by Wolach et al40 to be exaggerated in patients with JAK2 V617F–mutant MPNs. These neutrophil extracellular traps, composed of DNA, histones, and enzymes, promote coagulation, vascular injury, and immunosuppression and can further amplify inflammation by activating NLRP3 inflammasome signaling in macrophages.41

In addition to their prothrombotic role, neutrophils also contribute to immune dysregulation. JAK2 V617F–mutant neutrophils express high levels of programmed death-ligand 1, which inhibits T-cell function and promotes immune escape. This is reminiscent of the programmed cell death protein 1/programmed death-ligand 1 axis in solid tumors, in which immune checkpoint blockade restores antitumor immunity.42,43

The JAK2 V617F mutation thus distorts both arms of the NLR. Supporting this, it was shown that JAK2-driven models exhibit stage-specific blocks in T- and B-cell development, providing a mechanistic basis for the progressive lymphopenia observed in MPNs.35,44,45 Consequently, chronically elevated NLR emerges as a hallmark of JAK2 V617F–dominated hematopoiesis, embodying the paradoxical state of simultaneous inflammation and immunosuppression.

Very recently, Khatib-Massalha et al46 used JAK2 V617F murine models that faithfully reproduce human MPN features, including neutrophilia, splenomegaly, and progressive MF, to investigate neutrophil life span. They found that JAK2 V617F neutrophils survived significantly longer than their wild-type counterparts due to defective clearance. These senescent neutrophils aberrantly expressed CD24, acting as a “don’t-eat-me” signal to evade phagocytosis. Their persistence promoted emperipolesis into megakaryocytes, amplified transforming growth factor-β activation, and ultimately drove marrow fibrosis. Importantly, genetic deletion or antibody blockade of CD24 restored neutrophil clearance, normalized interactions with megakaryocytes, and prevented MF in vivo. These findings extend the role of neutrophils beyond vascular complications, establishing them as central mediators of both inflammation and fibrotic transformation in PV.

This biology is already evident at the earlier stage of clonal hematopoiesis of indeterminate potential (CHIP), first described by Jaiswal et al and later expanded by Bick et al, which can be considered the first phase of the inflammatory continuum that leads to overt MPNs.47,48 CHIP prevalence rises with age and is strongly associated with cardiovascular disease and mortality, and mechanistic studies demonstrate that mutated clones, particularly those with JAK2 or TET2 mutations, expand neutrophil and monocyte compartments, exacerbate cytokine production, and promote NETosis, thereby contributing to vascular remodeling and thrombosis.6 In this sense, CHIP represents a biological link between age-related clonal hematopoiesis, cardiovascular disease, and MPNs, with NLR serving as a functional indicator of these inflammatory mechanisms. In PV, in which clonal hematopoiesis dominates, this inflammatory clonal interplay is magnified, further supporting NLR as a biomarker of disease activity.

The predictive value of NLR extends well beyond MPNs. In large-scale cardiovascular and oncology cohorts,32,49 elevated NLR has consistently predicted major adverse events and poor survival. In a pooled analysis of 5 randomized trials involving ∼60 000 patients, Adamstein et al showed that a higher baseline NLR was independently associated with major cardiovascular events and mortality, even after adjusting for conventional risk factors.50 Importantly, NLR remained stable in placebo-treated patients, confirming the reproducibility of this measure over time. Among the agents tested, only canakinumab, an interleukin-1β inhibitor, significantly reduced NLR in a dose-dependent manner by lowering neutrophil counts, whereas sparing lymphocytes.51 In contrast, methotrexate in Cardiovascular Inflammation Reduction Trial increased NLR by suppressing lymphocytes,52 whereas statins in JUPITER53 and PCSK9 inhibitors in SPIRE-1/2 had no effect on NLR.54 These observations suggest that NLR tracks a specific inflammatory axis that responds only to selected immune-modulating interventions.

Together, they highlight NLR as a simple yet integrative biomarker that captures the dual forces of inflammation and immune dysregulation in PV, linking clonal hematopoiesis, cardiovascular disease, and cancer.

Accordingly, its greatest immediate utility lies in standardized monitoring within clinical trials; prospective analyses with prespecified cutoffs and timing are still needed to determine whether NLR-guided management improves outcomes.

Limitations and future directions

It could be argued that the clinical implementation of JAK2 VAF and NLR still requires further standardization and validation before adoption as potential surrogate end points in PV. Although technical variability in VAF quantification has largely been overcome through digital polymerase chain reaction and other sensitive platforms, ensuring reproducibility well below 1%, the remaining challenges include the lack of prospective, temporally-standardized measurements across trials. Furthermore, there is no consensus on which molecular response thresholds best reflect clinically meaningful improvement or whether relative changes (eg, ≥50%) or absolute values (eg, <10% or <2%) are best. In addition, the few studies correlating changes in VAF with outcomes have typically used composite end points, limiting conclusions regarding individual PV-related outcomes.

The work reviewed in this commentary article highlights these gaps and encourages prospective incorporation of standardized NLR and VAF assessments into ongoing clinical trials, and retrospective harmonization of existing data sets to enable pooled analyses. As exemplified by the Florence investigators in an abstract presented at the American Society of Hematology 2025 meeting,55 dynamic reductions in NLR after ruxolitinib correlated with parallel decreases in JAK2 VAF, confirming the biological connection between NLR-reported inflammation and clonal burden. Although NLR is an inflammatory biomarker not specific to PV, our joint-model analysis demonstrates that persistently elevated NLR under HU therapy independently predicts both thrombosis and OS, supporting its value as a stable prognostic biomarker of disease-related inflammation.

Together, JAK2 VAF and NLR represent complementary dimensions of PV biology, clonality and inflammation, dynamically modulated by modern therapies. A realistic framework for their validation and eventual clinical implementation, enabling the transition from risk-based to biology-driven management in PV, proceeds in 3 stages. First, retrospective studies, such as those discussed in this article, must establish standardized timing and analytical thresholds for both biomarkers and demonstrate robust correlation between their dynamic changes and long-term clinical outcomes. Second, these standardized measures should be prospectively incorporated into ongoing and future clinical trials as exploratory or secondary end points to test feasibility, consistency, and prognostic strength. Third, prospective interventional trials are needed to determine whether biomarker-guided decisions (eg, therapy selection, dose modification, or treatment duration) improve outcomes relative to current risk-based standards, analogous to how cytoreductive therapy in PV validated hematocrit control as a surrogate for thrombosis prevention.27 Successful completion of this validation process would permit NLR and JAK2 V617F VAF to progress from biologically meaningful correlates to fully validated surrogate end points that can accelerate the evaluation of new therapies with robust outcome-modifying potential.

However, these dual parameters alone may not capture the full biological complexity of PV. Nondriver mutations, the bone marrow microenvironment, vascular pathology, and immune dysregulation are also involved in disease evolution and patient outcomes. Thus, although reductions in JAK2 VAF and normalization of NLR are meaningful milestones with potential clinical utility, they may also serve as entry points for future research on drugs tackling the driving pathology of PV to achieve disease-modifying effects.

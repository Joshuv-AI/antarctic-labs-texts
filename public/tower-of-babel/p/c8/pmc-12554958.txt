Influence of growth factors and different supplements on metabolic response and productivity of IgA-producing CHO cell lines
Authors: Yuan Ping, Toporova Viktoriia, Lin Baohang, Argentova Victoria
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT

Background

Most of the study of expression in mammalian cells and the development of therapeutic antibodies has been carried out for immunoglobulin G (IgG). However, over the past few years, there has been a significant increase in interest in obtaining therapeutic antibodies based on immunoglobulin A (IgA).1 A large number of biopharmaceutical products, as recombinant monoclonal antibodies, are generated by Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cells, the main host of choice in the pharmaceutical industry.2 IgA plays a key role in activating immunity on the mucous membranes. IgA antibodies demonstrate more effective destruction of cancer cells compared to IgG isotypes. Binding IgA isotypes antibodies to the IgA-specific FcaRI receptor expressed on myeloid cells and using Fc domain effector mechanism for destroying tumor cells. Moreover, IgA has recently been recognized as a potential cancer immunotherapeutic antibody instead of IgG. That is one of the reasons for the advantages of using IgA antibodies as a therapeutic agent. IgA producers were represented by the lines IgA1 and IgA2m1, which belong to different IgA subclasses. In this regard, optimization of production methods for recombinant IgA molecules is of practical importance. Investigations of cells process nutrients, and metabolism can help to design medium to optimize production of recombinant proteins. Cell culture media for cultivation adherent cell lines usually contain fetal bovine serum (FBS) because the serum naturally contains growth factors and other components. However, suspension mammalian cell cultures −producing therapeutic proteins require serum-free media. Growth factors are proteins that play an important role as signaling molecules in the body of living organisms. Supplementation of cell culture media with growth factors help keep high viability and longevity of cells and influence on growth rate. Also, some growth factors participate in regulation of cell signaling pathways and functions by stimulating cell proliferation and inducing cell differentiation.3, 4 However, several studies point to role fibroblast growth factor (FGF) promote proliferation, differentiation and metabolism of epithelial cells and participates in cell regeneration.5 Growth factor FGF-2 also demonstrated influence on lifetime longevity of stable cell lines producing recombinant monoclonal antibodies of the IgG and IgA subclasses turned out to be different.6 Epidermal growth factor (EGF) is a common mitogenic factor that also stimulates the proliferation of different types of cells, especially fibroblasts and epithelial cells.7 Some studies have shown that NGF stimulates the growth of human cells, supports cell health and provides stable and repeatable conditions for in vitro studies of mammalian cells.8 Glutamine and glucose are the two most important nutrients source for cell cultures and are responsible for the metabolism of carbon and nitrogen. Glutamine metabolism affects mammalian cell production, but rapid cell proliferation is associated with intensive glucose metabolism. Glutamine is a component responsible for growth, energy, redox processes, and pH regulation of mammalian cellular metabolism.9, 10 Similar to glutamine, some amino acids probably are important for cellular metabolism and can sometimes serve as an alternative nitrogen and carbon source for cells and can be consumed in addition or in place of glutamine. Supplementation of glutamine substitutions can improve CHO cell growth by affecting metabolic parameters such as glucose consumption, lactate production, and final ammonium levels, and by enhancing recombinant protein production.11 Glutamine is secondary carbon sources to the TCA (Fig. 1). However, lactate and aspartate uptake are preferred over glutamine at the late exponential phase of cell culture growth. Some studies have reported that, under certain conditions, cell cultures can switch to a more energy-efficient pathway when consuming lactate.12, 13

TCA cycle and the metabolic pathway of glycolysis.

Glucose is the primary carbon source of the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle, and many basal media are supplemented with D-glucose. However, low glucose consumption by cells causes cells and cellular products to be modified through the processes of glycation and glycoxidation. These processes cause posttranslational secondary modifications of therapeutic proteins produced in cell cultures.14

Ammonium ions are toxic waste products of cell metabolism, which accumulate in cell culture media mainly as a result of the metabolism of glutamine and asparagine. On the one hand, glutamine is an important source of carbon and nitrogen in the medium for animal cell culture, on the other hand, the metabolism of glutamine or its analog is a source of ammonium in culture. The ammonium content has a negative effect on animal cell culture, since cell growth can be slowed down even by low concentrations of ammonium. Thus, reducing the ammonium content by 40 % in the exponential growth phase increases the concentration of cells by 50 %.15. An important factor is also that ammonium chloride causes an increase in intracellular pH, which can affect the glycosylation of produced glycoproteins.16

Lactate accumulating in the nutrient medium also has a similar negative effect on cell cultures by inhibiting cell growth due to changes in osmolality and pH.17 It is known from the literature that a decrease in lactate levels in the culture medium contributes to an increase in the uniformity and productivity of stable cell lines.18 Depending on growth phase CHO cell metabolism can characterized by high glycolysis from glucose to lactate, and from glutamate to α-ketoglutarate.19, 20 During the stationary phase, metabolism can shift to more efficient cycles, represented by glycolysis or TCA cycle. CHO cells can go to metabolic switch from lactate production to lactate consumption after glucose consumption. In this way mammalian cells can switch to a more energy efficient state during which lactate is consumed.21, 22 Analysis of cell metabolism showed that consuming lactate gave more energy than that of lactate-producing cells and increases the recombinant protein productivity in CHO cells.12 Moreover, it was determined that these nutrients can be rather used for synthesis of precursors of recombinant proteins than for cell growth. It is known that limiting their secretion improves cell growth and performance in CHO culture.23 Previous investigations demonstrated that there was a positive linear correlation between glutamine substitution with succinic acid or asparagine and reduction in the LDH activity, decreasing the lactate level in medium and increasing the homogeneity and productivity of stable cell lines.18 A reduction in culture metabolites can be achieved by process optimization or metabolic engineering. This includes replacing glutamine with succinate and supplementation with such growth factors as hNGF and hEGF.

One of the important criteria for the quality of a recombinant immunoglobulin is its glycosylation. Stable monoclonal cell lines producing the IgA1 and IgA2m1 isotypes are different in their structure and character of glycosylation, as well as the growth parameters, culture density. All these factors affect their metabolism and production of recombinant therapeutic antibodies. In this study, we investigated the ability of the human epidermal growth factor, fibroblast growth factor, and human nerve growth factor alone and combining with glutamine substitutions to influence on cell culture growth, and productivity process by reducing metabolic rates.

Materials and methods

Cell cultures and stable cell lines

For the experiments, stable cell lines producing recombinant monoclonal antibodies of the IgA1, and IgA2 subclasses isotypes against hemagglutinin of influenza A virus, previously obtained on the basis of CHO DG44 (dhfr-) cells (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA), were used.24 Cell cultures producing recombinant monoclonal antibodies were plated at 3x105 cells/mL in six-well plate and grown in a CO2 incubator at 96 % humidity, 37 °C and 8 % CO2. For cultivation, standard base media IMDM (Iscove's Modified Dulbecco's Media) and DMEM/F1 with the addition of L-glutamine, 100x stock antibiotic–antimycotic solution (all Servicebio, China), and10% Pluronic (Gibco, USA) were used.

The medium was supplemented with growth factors such as recombinant human epidermal growth factor (EGF), recombinant human nerve growth factor (β-NGF), and recombinant human fibroblast growth factor (FGF-b) (all Sino Biological, China) as growth stimulants. hFGF-b solution was introduced to final concentrations of 11 ng/ml and hEGF, hNGF solutions were added to a final concentration of 12.5 and 25 ng/well. Cell culture medium was supplemented with 4 mM-8 mM succinic acid as glutamine substitution and 11.2 ng/ml zinc sulfate solution in final concentration (Sigma, USA) for some experiments.

The influence of different growth factors and succinic acid substitution on the growth rate, metabolic response and productivity of CHO cells producing monomeric IgA1 and IgA2 antibodies was analyzed using the static cell culture method. Samples of cell cultures were centrifuged at 400g for 5 min, and the supernatant was collected for further LDH, NH4 and Glucose assays and ELISA assay.

ELISA assay

The quantitative of antibodies in cell cultures were determined by indirect ELISA assay. Goat mAbs against light kappa-type chains of human Igs (Sigma, USA) were immobilized on 96-well plate at a concentration of 0.25 μg/well. Alpha-chain specific anti-human IgA peroxidase antibody (Sigma, USA) were used for assay. IgA from human serum (Sigma, USA) was used as the standard. Cell culture samples were centrifuged at 12 000 × g in 4 min. Duplicate well assay was used for both standard and sample testing. Cell cultures supernatant of samples was diluted in PBS buffer to final volume100 µl and loaded into a 96-well microtiter plate. Phosphate buffered saline (PBS) was used as the blank control. We measure the absorbance of the samples at 450 nm using the spectrophotometer VarioScan ThermoFisher Scientific (ThermoFisher Sci., USA).

LDH assay

Level of lactate was measured using LDH Activity Assay kit quantifies LDH activity in cell culture samples. The release of LDH was measured using a commercially available LDH assay kit (Lactate Dehydrogenase Activity Assay Kit, Sigma, USA), where LDH is used to reduce NAD to NADH. NADH from LDH assay kit used as control. The LDH produced in samples were then measured colorimetrically at a wavelength of 450 nm using a spectrophotometer VarioScan ThermoFisher Scientific (ThermoFisher Sci., USA).

NH4 assay

Ammonia was measured by assay for quantitative determination of ammonia/ammonium ion concentration in cell culture samples using Ammonia Assay (Amplite Colorimetric Ammonia Assay Kit, AAT Bioquest, China). This assay is based on the o-phthalaldehyde method in which the reagent reacts with ammonia/ammonium ion producing a colorimetric result, proportional to the ammonia concentration in the sample. The analysis was performed in 96-well microtiter-plate format and measured colorimetrically at 660–670 nm using spectrophotometer VarioScan ThermoFisher Scientific.

Glucose assay

Glucose levels were determined using a Glucose assay kit (Amplite Colorimetric Glucose Quantitation Kit”, ATT Bioquest, China) ranging from 20 to 100 pmole/well in cell culture samples according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Glucose concentration is determined by a coupled enzyme assay, which results in a fluorometric product proportional to the glucose present. The assay was performed in 96-well microtiter-plate format and measured colorimetrically at 570 nm using spectrophotometer VarioScan ThermoFisher Scientific.

Results and discussion

Influence different growth factors supplementation on cell density, viability and glucose consumption in IgA1- IgA2-producing stable cell lines

In this study, we investigated the potential for epidermal growth factor (EGF), fibroblast growth factors-basic (FGF-b), and nerve growth factor (NGF) to regulate cell growth, metabolic rates and productivity.

We compared the effects of different growth factors: recombinant hEGF, hNGF and hFGF-b at a concentration of 12,5 ng/ml on cell density and viability stable cell lines producing recombinant monomeric antibodies different isotypes: IgA1 and IgA2m1. It was found that the density of the cell culture grown in a medium with the addition of growth factors, compared with the culture grown in the DMEM/F12 control medium, was on average 1.4–2.9 times higher for both IgA1 and IgA2 (Fig. 2).

Effect of growth factors supplementation on the cell concentration (A,B), viability (C,D).

It was established that hNGF has a significantly stronger effect on cell density compared to hEGF and FGF-b for both isotypes of antibody. As shown in Fig. 2A-B, the cell density significantly increased with the hNGF supplementation to the culture medium to 3.2 and 2.8 times on day10 for IgA1 and IgA2 isotypes, respectively. These data were equal to 9.0 × 105cell/mL and 1.0 × 106 cell/mL, which shows a stronger effect of the hNGF on the cell line producing of IgA2 antibody. The viability of the cell lines remained high with hNGF and hEGF supplementation, especially for cell lines producing IgA2, and amounted > 80 % on the 10th day of cultivation, which increases the duration of cultivation time. At the same time, the cell culture density and viability stable cell lines grown in a medium supplemented with FGF-b dropped almost twice compared to hNGF supplementation on day 10. This effect is more evident for stable cell line producing IgA1 isotype.

In our study we also investigated the effect of combination of one growth factor and zinc sulfate solution on the viable cell density, viability, and glucose consumption of cell lines producing different immunoglobulin A isotypes. Comparison these combinations and growth factors alone as a supplement to the basal medium demonstrated various effect for IgA1- and IgA2- cell lines. However, the addition of hEGF and zinc sulfate combinations to the culture medium did not affect the cell line producing antibody IgA1isotype, while this supplementation had good effect on the cell density for IgA2 isotype compared to IgA1. As shown in Fig. 2A-B, the use combination of the zinc sulfate solution and hNGF for cultivation medium supplementation led to an increase in cell density for both isotypes of immunoglobulins. However, the cell density increased 1,5 times for the IgA2-producing cell line, while it did not change significantly for IgA1. The IgA1-producing cell line had slower cell growth rates, but their cell viability was extended compared to the IgA2-producing cell line on day 5. At this time, IgA2-producing cell line had high viability compared to IgA1 on day 10. This synergy effect for IgA2-producing cell line may be associated either with the hNGF and zinc sulfate specific action, which increases the density and longevity of cells, and with differences of IgA isotypes structure. Also, it is known that the effect of zinc on cells includes the mRNA stabilization, either by modification of its secondary structure or through facilitation of its binding to stabilizing proteins.25 In this study, the maximum viable cell density increased from 1.2 x 106 cells/mL and 80 % viability on day10.

Impact of growth factors alone and combined with supplements on glucose consumption

Effective culture strategies to increase productivity of cell line producing recombinant proteins include a high glucose concentration in the medium. At the same time this led to a reduced cells growth rate and has multiple effects on nutrient consumption rates, productivity and quality of proteins.26 In this study, we have measured for media glucose concentration and compared glucose consumption in two stable cell lines produced different isotypes IgA. Changes of glucose level in cell culture and cell proliferation due to cell culture in media supplemented with different growth factors were evaluated by the residual concentration of glucose using static culture in six-well plate. Interestingly, during the late exponential phase from day 5 today 10, glucose consumption rate was almost similar for IgA1- and IgA2m1- stable cell lines in growth factors supplementation (Fig. 3). However, combination growth factors and zinc sulfate reduced glucose consumption by approximately 45 %. A minimum glucose consumption rate was found for combination of the zinc sulfate and hNGF, hEGF as supplements for cultivation medium for both isotypes IgA-producing cell lines. In this case glucose concentration in cell culture medium for IgA1 and IgA2 was higher than for FGF-b, hEGF and hNGF alone and became 1.3 and 1.46 times higher, respectively. Interestingly, minimal glucose consumption was shown in the IgA2-stable cell line when a combination of zinc sulfate and hNGF was added to the culture medium. In this case, the maximum glucose level in cell culture medium was 287 µM. Thus, the combination of zinc sulfate and hNGF, hEGF had the greatest effect on glucose consumption for both IgA1- and IgA2- stable cell lines.

Influence of growth factors on glucose consumption of a stable cell line producing recombinant monoclonal antibody of IgA1 and IgA2m1 isotypes.

Our results revealed that hEGF, hNGF and zinc sulfate supplementation decreased glucose consumption and increased cell proliferation and viability. Accordingly, a decrease in glucose consumption affects the level of metabolites in the cell culture medium.

Influence of metabolite rates on productivity of different isotypes IgA caused by supplementation growth factors and zinc sulfate composition

In this study, we investigated lactate and NH4 production and correlation with stable cell line productivity using complex additives, such as succinic acid and different growth factors as FGF-b, hNGF and hEGF to the basal medium. The stable cell lines producing recombinant monomeric IgA1 and IgA2m1 antibodies were cultivated in DMEM/F12 medium supplemented with 4 mM L-glutamine, 12.5 ng/ml growth factors alone or in zinc sulfate combination. Classical DMEM/F12 cell culture medium with 4 mM L-glutamine and without any supplementation was used as a reference. Supernatant samples from static six-well plate cell culture were collected on days 5 and 10. The LDH activity and NH4 level in the supernatants were analyzed in duplicates following the standard protocol.

Identical high results on lactate and ammonium production were obtained under FGF-b stimulated conditions. As shown in Fig. 4A-D, the IgA1-IgA2-cell culture grown in a medium supplemented with 12,5 ng/mL hNGF and hEGF solution demonstrate a decrease metabolic level compared to FGF-b supplementation. At the same time, the use of hNGF and hEGF alone or with zinc sulfate have different effects in metabolic response of IgA1- and IgA2- producing cell lines. It can be noted that simultaneous addition of zinc sulfate (11.5 ng/ml) to hNGF and hEGF supplemented basal media produced a synergistic effect. As shown on Fig. 4A-B, the level of ammonium (0.45 nmol) is less than 3.33 times for IgA2m1- stable cell line compared with IgA1-cell line (1.5 nmol). The addition of either hNGF or hEGF at a concentration of 12.5 ng/mL had a similar effect; the LDH level decreased 1.5–2 times, and 2–3.5 times compared to the FGF-b for IgA1- and IgA2m1- cell lines, respectively. Low metabolite level in cell culture demonstrates positive effects of hNGF and hEGF on productivity of IgA1-IgA2m1-stable cell lines. However, more increments in recombinant antibody production were observed for IgA2m1-stable cell line. The supplementation of hNGF and hEGF alone to the culture medium increased 1.5 times the productivity of the IgA2m1-stable cell line as compared to the FGF-b supplement on day 10 of cultivation. Also, combination of such growth factors as hNGF and hEGF with zinc sulfate led to an increase in antibody production by 2 times compared with the FGF-supplementation alone (Fig. 4E-F). Changes in the metabolism of CHO cells cultured with the addition of hNGF and hEGF with zinc sulfate reduce the production of lactate and ammonium, which increases the level of antibody production. This synergy may be associated either with the hNGF or hEGF specific action, which increases the density and longevity of cells, or with stimulation of cell productivity by zinc sulfate. It is known that the effect of zinc on cells includes the mRNA stabilization, either by modification of its secondary structure or through facilitation of its binding to stabilizing proteins.25

Comparison of metabolite rates of different isotypes IgA caused by supplementation growth factors and succinic acid composition

To investigate the role of combined succinic acid and growth factors on metabolism and productivity IgA-based stable cell lines, we supplemented DMEM/F12 medium with varying concentrations of growth factors and 4 mM succinic acid. Both glutamine and succinate can be included in the TCA. Their consumption rates can exceed their normal rate, so understanding of how using these amino acids as substrates influences the metabolic rates and productivity maintenance in CHO cell cultures would be valuable. Glutamine played key roles during early exponential phase, while succinate probably plays a role at a later phase. The cells prefer glutamine over succinate while succinate contribute significantly to the TCA cycle under the low glutamine condition. We previously reported that glutamine substitution to succinic acid has positive effect on viability and longevity stable cell line producing IgG and IgA1.17 Further studies have shown that effect of glutamine substitution to succinic acid combined with growth factors supplementation had significant effect on metabolites level and recombinant monoclonal antibody production. In present experiments it was shown that succinic acid in cell culture medium induces increasing lactate level and enhancing Abs production. Expression of IgA2m1 in cell culture grown in a medium supplemented with combination hNGF or hEGF and 4 mM succinic acid was higher than that in cells grown in the medium without succinic acid. Fig. 5(A) shows that increase in productivity of monomeric IgA2m1 in 20 % in cell culture on day 10. For instance, the study revealed a twofold increase in IgA2m1 production when levels of lactate and ammonium were decreased, respectively. In present experiments it was shown that glutamine substitution for succinic acid in cell culture medium improves metabolism of CHO cells producing recombinant monoclonal antibody IgA isotype.

The effect of cell culture media supplements combination on cell metabolism and IgA1-IgA2m1 production.

Expression of IgA2m1 in cell culture grown in a medium supplemented with combination hNGF or hEGF and 4 mM succinic acid was higher than that in cells grown in the medium without succinic acid. Fig. 5(A) shows that increase in productivity of monomeric IgA2m1 in 25 % in cell culture on day 10. As shown on Fig. 5, addition hNGF and hEGF enhanced the IgA production in a dose −dependent fashion. Decreasing in lactate production at double doses of growth factors (25 ng/ml) with same concentration zinc sulfate were observed for IgA2m1-produced cell lines (Fig. 5A). The highest rate of the stable cell line IgA2m1 production was achieved when the basal medium was supplemented with hNGF and hNGF and 4 mM succinic acid combination. These data were equal to 0.319 mg/mL and 0.487 mg/mL, respectively (Fig. 5C). On the other hand, these additives to the cell culture media did not have such significant effect on the productivity of the IgA1-stable cell line which was 1.5–2.0 times lower than that of the IgA2m1 cell line. Overall, this study would suggest that combination growth factors and succinic acid improves metabolism of CHO cells producing recombinant monoclonal antibody IgA isotype, especially for IgA2m1producing cell line. The low level of metabolites, especially lactate, play an important role in increasing the scale of culture, and for high-density cell cultures and fed-batch cultures, optimization of cultivation is an important issue. These results can be used for solving problems in the recombinant IgA- stable cell lines development, especially in the production of recombinant monoclonal antibody IgA2m1 subisotype.

Conclusion

In this work, we have demonstrated that the substitution of glutamine with succinic acid in the culture medium significantly decreased the lactate level and enhanced the cell maintenance and productivity of the IgA2m1-stable cell line. Our results show that epidermal and nerve growth factors play an important role in increasing the growth rate and productivity of stable cell lines while reducing lactate production in stable cell lines producing both IgA1 and IgA2m1. Supplementation of cell culture medium with growth hormones induces a rise important metabolic effects in stimulating glucose, glutamine and succinic acid uptake. We would suggest that cell lines probably switched to lactate consumption. On the other hand, low level of lactate could be explained by the fact that it is derived from pyruvate generated from succinic acid in lower concentrations compared to glutamine via TCA cycle intermediate. Consumption of lactate was also observed in our previous IgA- producing stable cell lines studies.

It should be noted that fibroblast growth factor stimulates lactate and ammonium production more than epidermal and nerve growth factors, inhibiting cell productivity in both IgA1 and IgA2m1-stable cell lines. Moreover, there was a positive linear correlation between the substitution of glutamine with succinic acid and the reduction in the LDH activity, leading to decreased lactate level in medium and increased productivity of IgA-based stable cell lines. CHO cell metabolism is an industrially relevant process, and supplementation cell culture medium with growth factors, as well as the substitution of glutamine with succinic acid has an important role in decreasing metabolite levels and optimizing the production of recombinant IgA-based antibodies.

The current status and future directions of short video dissemination on urolithiasis via the TikTok platform: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Yunfeng Zhang, Tao Lin, Jun Li, Xing Wang, Zhengjie Xiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Urolithiasis is a common and highly recurrent urological disorder. With the growing role of short video platforms in health communication, Douyin (TikTok) has become an important medium for public medical education. However, the quality and user engagement characteristics of urolithiasis-related content remain insufficiently evaluated. This study aimed to assess the content quality and user engagement of urolithiasis-related videos on TikTok and to explore how video source, content theme, and presentation style are associated with educational value and audience response. A total of 400 Chinese-language TikTok videos across five stone types, including renal, ureteral, bladder, urethral, and urinary tract stones, were analyzed. Content quality was evaluated using four validated instruments, namely the Journal of American Medical Association (JAMA) Benchmark Criteria, Global Quality Scale (GQS), modified DISCERN (mDISCERN) instrument, and the Patient Education Materials Assessment Tool (PEMAT). User engagement metrics included likes, comments, shares, and saves. Statistical analyses were conducted to examine variations in quality and engagement across source, content, and presentation form. Overall content quality was low (mean JAMA 1.23; GQS 2.84; mDISCERN 3.09). Videos focusing on prevention and recurrence demonstrated higher PEMAT understandability and actionability scores and were associated with greater user engagement. Hospital- and news agency–based videos showed higher content quality; however, lower engagement was primarily observed in hospital-based videos. Animation and image-text formats were associated with improved understandability. Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM)–themed videos, although less frequent, demonstrated relatively high actionability and user engagement. The quality and communication effectiveness of urolithiasis-related videos on TikTok varied substantially. Prevention-oriented content, culturally relevant framing, and visually supported formats were associated with improved user engagement and understanding. Integrating evidence-based information with platform-adaptive design may enhance the effectiveness of digital health communication.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-54245-1.

Introduction

Urolithiasis (urinary tract stones), encompassing stones in the kidneys, ureters, bladder, and urethra, is among the most prevalent urological disorders globally, with its incidence steadily rising in both developed and developing countries1–5. Epidemiological studies estimate a lifetime prevalence ranging from 5% to 14%, and recurrence rates exceeding 50% at 5 years and 80%–90% at 10 years6–9. Despite continuous advances in surgical techniques and pharmacological therapies, poor public awareness, limited health literacy, and inadequate preventive behaviors remain major contributors to recurrence and long-term healthcare burden10–12.

In the era of digital health, social media platforms have become crucial channels for disseminating medical information13,14. In China, TikTok, as the most representative short-video platform, has substantial influence in health communication because of its broad reach, personalized recommendation system, and interactive format15,16. It therefore provides a potentially important medium for improving health literacy and promoting preventive behaviors in recurrent conditions such as urolithiasis17–19.

Previous studies evaluating medical content on short video platforms have shown that, although such videos may enhance public awareness and promote healthy behaviors, their scientific quality is often limited, particularly with respect to accuracy, source transparency, and adherence to clinical guidelines20–25. In the field of urolithiasis, Augustyn et al. (2025) analyzed TikTok and YouTube videos on kidney stones and found that more than half were of low quality, with 34% of TikTok videos containing overt misinformation, often involving non-evidence-based remedies26. Similarly, Salka et al. (2023) evaluated kidney stone prevention videos on TikTok, reporting that only 9% were physician-generated and that most failed to adhere to American Urological Association dietary guidelines27. However, these studies were limited by their narrow focus on a single stone type, primarily renal stones, reliance on relatively limited assessment tools, and lack of systematic analysis of user engagement.

To address the limitations of previous studies, this study conducted a systematic cross-sectional evaluation of urolithiasis-related short videos on TikTok. A total of 400 videos were included, covering all major types of urolithiasis, including renal, ureteral, bladder, urethral, and urinary tract stones, rather than focusing on a single disease category. Four validated instruments, namely the JAMA, GQS, mDISCERN instrument, and the PEMAT, were applied to provide a multidimensional assessment of content quality, including transparency, reliability, overall quality, and patient-oriented understandability and actionability. In addition, content quality assessment was integrated with user engagement metrics, including likes, comments, shares, and saves, to simultaneously evaluate the scientific rigor and dissemination effectiveness of medical information. Based on this framework, the present study aimed to characterize the quality and dissemination patterns of urolithiasis-related content on TikTok, examine associated factors, and inform the development of evidence-based and effective health communication strategies for short video platforms.

Methods

Ethical considerations

This study did not involve clinical data, human specimens, or animal experiments. All data were collected exclusively from publicly available TikTok videos without any personal identifiers or interaction with users. According to relevant guidelines and prior literature, the institutional ethics committee confirmed that formal ethical approval was not required for studies based on publicly accessible and anonymized data28.

Search strategy and data collection

Data were retrieved from the TikTok platform between March 12 and 20, 2025. To minimize algorithmic bias, newly registered accounts were used to perform all searches. The search keywords included “renal stone,” “ureteral stone,” “bladder stone,” “urethral stone,” and “urinary tract stone.” The inclusion criteria were as follows: videos directly related to urolithiasis. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) duplicate videos; (2) advertisements; and (3) irrelevant content (e.g., non-human or unrelated calculi).

For each eligible video, the following variables were recorded: title, number of likes, comments, shares, and saves, days since upload, video duration, source, uploader follower count, presentation form, and content type. Previous studies have shown that user browsing behavior is concentrated on the top-ranked search results29–31. Therefore, for each keyword, videos were collected sequentially according to search ranking until 100 eligible videos were obtained. For “urethral stone” and “urinary tract stone,” only 50 videos were available due to platform limitations. The frequency and distribution of keywords are presented in Figure S1.

To improve the transparency and consistency of variable classification, a single primary category assignment approach was applied for each analytical dimension, including source, medical background, content type, disease knowledge, and presentation form. Within each dimension, each video was assigned to only one primary category, and cross-classification was not permitted. When a video contained multiple features within a given dimension, the primary category was determined based on its core theme, main content focus, or most prominent presentation characteristic. For example, if a video addressed both treatment and prevention but primarily emphasized recurrence prevention strategies, it was classified under the “prevention” category. Similarly, if a video incorporated both expert narration and image-text presentation, classification was based on the dominant format throughout the video. All classification and coding procedures were conducted independently by two reviewers. Discrepancies were resolved through discussion with a third reviewer to reach consensus, ensuring the standardization, reproducibility, and methodological reliability of the coding process.

Video content and quality assessment

Four validated instruments were used to assess the quality of urolithiasis-related videos, each representing a distinct dimension of health information evaluation.

The JAMA were used to evaluate information transparency, including authorship, attribution, disclosure, and currency, with each item scored as one point and a total possible score of four (Table S1)32.

The GQS was applied to assess overall content quality using a five-point scale, focusing on flow, coherence, and usefulness for patient education (Table S2)33.

The mDISCERN instrument consisted of five binary items (0 or 1 point), evaluating clarity of presentation, use of reliable sources, balance and neutrality, provision of additional resources, and acknowledgment of uncertainty, with a maximum score of five (Table S3)34,35.

The PEMAT was used to evaluate understandability and actionability. Understandability focuses on language clarity, logical structure, and visual support (19 items), whereas actionability assesses whether the content provides clear and feasible steps for users (5 items)36. Both domains were expressed as percentages after excluding non-applicable items (Table S4).

All videos were independently evaluated by two reviewers (Z-YF and L-T), who underwent a calibration process using an additional set of 50 videos prior to formal assessment. Discrepancies were resolved through discussion with a third reviewer (X-ZJ). Interrater agreement was assessed using Cohen’s kappa coefficient (κ), demonstrating almost perfect agreement (κ = 0.837–0.975, all P < 0.001), as shown in Table S5 and Figure S2.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.4.3). Categorical variables were reported as counts and percentages, and continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation or median (Q1, Q3), depending on data distribution. Between-group comparisons were conducted using the Student’s t test or Mann–Whitney U test for two groups, and one-way ANOVA or Kruskal–Wallis test for three or more groups, as appropriate. Spearman correlation analysis was used to assess associations between continuous variables. All tests were two-tailed, and a P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

General characteristics of videos

As shown in Fig. 1, after excluding duplicates, advertisements, and irrelevant content, a total of 400 TikTok videos related to urolithiasis were included, comprising 100 videos each on renal, ureteral, and bladder stones, and 50 videos each on urethral and urinary tract stones. Videos on renal and ureteral stones had higher numbers of likes, comments, saves, and shares than other types (all P < 0.001).

Fig. 1Flow diagram of video selection process.

Flow diagram of video selection process.

Overall, video quality was low. JAMA scores were poor (1.23 ± 0.62) and differed significantly across stone types (P < 0.001), with slightly higher scores observed in renal stone videos. GQS scores were moderate (2.84 ± 0.75) and varied among groups (P = 0.036), with higher scores in urethral and urinary tract stone videos. mDISCERN scores indicated moderate quality (3.09 ± 0.63), with higher scores for urethral and urinary tract stones (P < 0.001).

Understandability was relatively high (75.88 ± 13.28%) and did not differ significantly among stone types (P = 0.612). Actionability was moderate (56.06 ± 27.33%) and differed significantly (P = 0.005), with higher scores observed in urinary tract stone videos. Score distributions are presented in Figs. 2 and 3.

Discussion

This study systematically evaluated the quality and dissemination characteristics of urolithiasis-related short videos on TikTok. Compared with previous studies, this work extended the existing literature in both sample scope and methodological design26,27. Specifically, a total of 400 videos were included, covering all major types of urolithiasis rather than focusing on a single stone category. In addition, four complementary validated instruments, JAMA, GQS, mDISCERN, and PEMAT, were jointly applied to provide a multidimensional assessment of content quality, including transparency, reliability, overall quality, and patient-oriented understandability and actionability. These quality assessments were further integrated with user engagement metrics to characterize dissemination performance. It should be noted that, as the present analyses were primarily descriptive and correlational, these findings should be interpreted as associations rather than causal relationships.

Overall, despite the large volume and diversity of available content, the quality of videos remained suboptimal, which was consistent with previous reports37,38. This finding suggested a persistent gap between the widespread availability of health information on short video platforms and its scientific reliability. Videos focusing on prevention and recurrence management demonstrated higher understandability and actionability, as well as greater user engagement. This pattern suggested that content providing clear and actionable behavioral guidance, such as hydration strategies or dietary modification, might be more readily understood and disseminated. Given the high recurrence rate of urolithiasis, prevention-oriented content appeared to be particularly well suited to short video-based health communication.

Notably, higher content quality did not necessarily correspond to greater visibility or user interaction. For instance, hospital-based videos achieved higher quality scores but showed relatively lower engagement. This discrepancy might reflect platform-specific dissemination mechanisms. Short video platforms tend to prioritize concise, visually engaging, and emotionally appealing content, which may not align with the structured and information-dense format of professional medical communication39–41. As a result, high-quality medical content may not achieve proportional dissemination or interaction.

Content format was also associated with communication performance. Animated and image-based videos showed higher understandability, suggesting that visual support might facilitate the communication of complex medical information. In contrast, expert-centered formats, although more authoritative, might be less effective in short video contexts because of limited visual structuring. User engagement was primarily concentrated in prevention-oriented, recurrence-related, and experience-based content, indicating that audiences tended to respond more actively to information that was directly relevant and practically applicable. In addition, videos related to traditional Chinese medicine, although limited in number, demonstrated relatively high actionability and engagement. This finding might be related to their emphasis on lifestyle-based interventions and more experience-oriented presentation; however, further validation is warranted.

Based on these findings, several practical considerations may be relevant for clinicians seeking to develop effective short video-based health communication. First, content should be grounded in current clinical guidelines and evidence-based recommendations to ensure scientific accuracy and credibility42. Second, complex medical information should be translated into clear and actionable behavioral guidance, such as hydration strategies, dietary modification, and recurrence prevention measures, to enhance understandability and usability. Third, visual formats, including animations and structured image-based presentations, may improve comprehension of complex concepts. In addition, artificial intelligence may serve as a supportive tool in content development by assisting with script drafting, language simplification, subtitle generation, visual planning, and consistency checking, thereby potentially improving the efficiency and standardization of health communication materials43. However, AI-generated content should be reviewed by clinicians to ensure medical accuracy, guideline adherence, and contextual appropriateness. Finally, content development should be adapted to platform-specific characteristics. For short video platforms, this may involve concise structuring, clear emphasis on key messages, and context-relevant presentation styles, which may improve dissemination effectiveness while maintaining scientific integrity. Consideration of platform differences is also important, as content optimized for short video platforms may differ from that designed for longer-form or text-based communication environments.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the data were collected from search results obtained between March 12 and 20, 2025, using a keyword-based strategy combined with search ranking. This design represented a convenience sample within a specific time window and might not fully reflect the overall distribution of urolithiasis-related content on the platform. Second, only publicly available Chinese-language videos were included, which might limit the generalizability of the findings. Third, keyword-based retrieval might have missed relevant content, and both platform algorithms and the selected time window might have influenced search results. Fourth, user engagement metrics might have been affected by external factors such as uploader follower count, posting time, and platform recommendation mechanisms, introducing potential confounding. Fifth, although validated instruments were used, these tools evaluated content quality and usability rather than directly verifying the medical accuracy of each video. In addition, multivariable analyses were not performed; therefore, potential confounding factors were not controlled, and the observed associations should be interpreted with caution. Finally, although variable classification followed a single primary category assignment approach as specified in the Methods section, some degree of subjectivity in categorization could not be excluded.

In summary, this study provided a multidimensional characterization of the quality and dissemination features of urolithiasis-related content on TikTok. The findings highlighted the coexistence of high information availability and limited scientific quality on short video platforms. Future studies incorporating broader sampling strategies and multivariable analytical approaches are warranted to further clarify the determinants of content quality and dissemination effectiveness.

Conclusion

Urolithiasis is well suited to short video-based health education because of its chronic and behavior-sensitive nature. This study revealed substantial variation in the quality and dissemination performance of urolithiasis-related videos on TikTok and highlighted a persistent gap between professional medical communication and platform-specific communication demands. Prevention-oriented content and visually supported formats were associated with better understandability and stronger user engagement. Integrating evidence-based medical information with platform-adapted communication strategies may help improve the quality and effectiveness of short video health education.

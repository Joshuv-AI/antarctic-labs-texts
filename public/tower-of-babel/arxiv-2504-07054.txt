Weighted Łojasiewicz inequalities and regularity of harmonic map flow
Alex Waldron

Abstract
. At a finite-time singularity of harmonic map flow in the critical dimension, we show that a Łojasiewicz inequality between the quantities appearing in Struwe’s monotonicity formula implies continuity of the body map and the no-neck property for bubble-tree decompositions. We prove such an inequality when the target is S 2 , S^{2}, yielding both properties in this case.



1.
Introduction

Łojasiewicz(-Simon) inequalities play a key role in the theory of geometric evolution equations; they rule out the notorious non-uniqueness phenomenon for subsequential limits at infinite time
[
6
]
.
In recent years, most prominently in the work of Colding and Minicozzi, Łojasiewicz inequalities suitable for finite-time singularity analysis have become available.
These have led to uniqueness theorems for the most basic multiplicity-one blowup limits of mean curvature flow, namely, round cylinders
[
3
]
and asymptotically conical shrinkers
[
1
]
, as well as mean-convex cylindrical shrinkers
[
14
,
15
]
.

Łojasiewicz inequalities are also strongly tied to questions of regularity. Simon used his classical inequality in the elliptic setting to obtain a strong Hausdorff-dimension estimate and rectifiability of the singular set for area-minimizing currents
[
7
]
. Colding and Minicozzi have applied their results to obtain improved regularity of singular sets for mean curvature flow with generic (i.e. cylindrical) singularities at finite time
[
4
]
. They have also demonstrated that differentiability of the “arrival time” in mean-convex mean curvature flow depends on a Łojasiewicz inequality
[
2
]
.

This paper establishes a link between a different regularity question and a Łojasiewicz inequality. The question concerns the so-called “body map”
u(T)=\lim_{t\nearrow T}u(t)
of a solution of harmonic map flow
u:\Sigma\times\left[0,T\right)\to N
in the critical dimension,
\dim\left(\Sigma\right)=2.
This map is by definition of class
H^{1},
but may fail to be continuous in certain cases, as shown by Topping
[
11
]
. Meanwhile, Topping conjectured that if
N
is real-analytic then the body map must be continuous
[
11
, p. 286]
.

Our main result, Theorem
4.2
below, is that continuity of the body map follows from the validity of a Łojasiewicz inequality between the Gaussian-weighted quantities appearing in Struwe’s monotonicity formula
[
9
]
. We prove such an inequality, Theorem
2.2
, in the case that the target manifold is the round 2-sphere, allowing us to settle the fundamental case of the above question.

The continuity question for
N=S^{2}
was raised again recently by Jendrej, Lawrie, and Schlag
[
5
]
.
Our main estimate also gives the “no-neck” property of the bubble-tree decomposition along any sequence approaching the singular time.

The weighted Łojasiewicz inequality for maps to the 2-sphere is obtained by localizing one of the inequalities proved recently by the author
[
12
]
.
The localization is enabled by a weighted Poincaré inequality, Lemma
2.1
, similar to those used in Type-I singularity analysis; this inequality turns out to be particularly strong in the critical dimension. We use the inequality in two places: once in the localization, Theorem
2.2
, and later in the proof of main theorem, cf. (
4.15
).

An interesting feature of the proof is that we do not obtain
Hölder
continuity of the body map, as in the case of a “strict” type-II blowup
[
13
]
, unless the Łojasiewicz inequality holds with the optimal exponent (
\alpha=2
). In the case of a fractional exponent (
1<\alpha<2
), we obtain the oscillation bound

\Osc_{B_{r}}u(T)=O\left|\log r\right|^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha-2}}.

See Theorem
4.2
-Corollary
4.3
for detailed statements.

2.
Weighted Łojasiewicz inequality

2.1.
Weighted Poincaré inequality

Let
(\Sigma,g)
be a Riemannian surface and
(N,\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle)
any compact Riemannian manifold. Given a map
u:\Sigma\to N,
denote its stress-energy tensor by

(2.1)

S_{ij}=\left\langle{\partial}_{i}u,{\partial}_{j}u\right\rangle-\frac{1}{2}g_{ij}|du|_{g}^{2}.

We have

(2.2)

\nabla^{i}S_{ij}=\left\langle{\mathcal{T}}(u),{\partial}_{j}u\right\rangle,

see e.g.
[
8
, (2.5)]
.
Let
X^{i}
be any conformal Killing field, satisfying

\nabla^{i}X^{j}+\nabla^{j}X^{i}=\mu g^{ij}

for some function
\mu.

Note that
g^{ij}S_{ij}=0
in dimension two. Contracting the identity (
2.2
) with
X,
we have

(2.3)

\nabla^{i}\left(X^{j}S_{ij}\right)=\left\langle{\mathcal{T}}(u),X^{j}{\partial}_{j}u\right\rangle.

We now specialize to the case
\Sigma={\mathbb{R}}^{2}
with the flat metric; the general case requires only small modifications. Let
x=x^{i}{\partial}_{i}
be the radial vector field and
r=\sqrt{(x^{1})^{2}+(x^{2})^{2}}
the radial coordinate in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}.
Define the weighted
L^{2}
-norm

(2.4)

\|\alpha\|_{\tau}=\sqrt{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|\alpha|^{2}\,e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4\tau}}\,dV}.

Let

{\widehat{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{\tau}(u):={\mathcal{T}}(u)-\frac{x}{2\tau}\intprod du.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
W^{2,2}
map
u:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to N,
we have

(2.5)

\left\|rdu\right\|_{\tau}\leq 4\tau\left\|{\widehat{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{\tau}(u)\right\|_{\tau}

and

(2.6)

\|{\mathcal{T}}(u)\|_{\tau}\leq 3\left\|{\widehat{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{\tau}(u)\right\|_{\tau}.

Proof.

We take
X=x
in (
2.3
) and integrate by parts against
e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4\tau}},
to obtain

\frac{1}{2\tau}\int x^{i}x^{j}S_{ij}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4\tau}}\,dV=\int\left\langle{\mathcal{T}}(u),x^{j}{\partial}_{j}u\right\rangle e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4\tau}}\,dV.

Unpacking the definition of the stress-energy tensor (
2.1
), we have

\begin{split}-\frac{1}{4\tau}\int r^{2}|du|^{2}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4\tau}}\,dV&=\int\left\langle{\mathcal{T}}(u),x^{j}{\partial}_{j}u\right\rangle e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4\tau}}\,dV-\int\left\langle\frac{x^{i}}{2\tau}{\partial}_{i}u,x^{j}{\partial}_{j}u\right\rangle e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4\tau}}\,dV\\
&=\int\left\langle{\widehat{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{\tau}(u),x^{j}{\partial}_{j}u\right\rangle e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4\tau}}\,dV.\end{split}

Applying Cauchy-Schwarz, we obtain

\frac{1}{4\tau}\left\|rdu\right\|_{\tau}^{2}\leq\left\|{\widehat{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{\tau}(u)\right\|_{\tau}\|x\intprod du\|_{\tau}.

Since
|x\intprod du|\leq r|du|,
this implies (
2.5
).
Then (
2.6
) follows from (
2.5
) and the triangle inequality.
∎

2.2.
Weighted Łojasiewicz inequality for maps to
S^{2}

Let

\Phi(u):=\frac{1}{2}\int|du|^{2}e^{\frac{-r^{2}}{4}}\,dV

and

{\widehat{{\mathcal{T}}}}(u):={\widehat{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{1}(u)={\mathcal{T}}(u)-\frac{x}{2}\intprod du.

Theorem 2.2
.

Fix
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\beta>0.
Given any map
u:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to S^{2}
with
E(u)\leq 4\pi k,
there exists
n\in\{0,\ldots,k\}
such that

|\Phi(u)-4\pi n|\leq C_{k,\beta}\|{\widehat{{\mathcal{T}}}}(u)\|_{1}^{2-\beta},

where
\|\cdot\|_{1}
is the weighted
L^{2}
-norm defined by (
2.4
).

Proof.

By the weighted Poincaré inequality of Lemma
2.1
, we have

\int r^{2}|du|^{2}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4}}+\|{\mathcal{T}}(u)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{2})}\leq C\|{\widehat{{\mathcal{T}}}}(u)\|_{1}^{2}.

In particular, this gives
E(u,B_{2}\setminus B_{1})\leq C\|{\widehat{{\mathcal{T}}}}(u)\|_{1}^{2}.
We can assume without loss of generality that
\|{\widehat{{\mathcal{T}}}}(u)\|^{2}_{1}\leq{\varepsilon}_{0}/C,
where
{\varepsilon}_{0}
is the standard
{\varepsilon}
-regularity constant for maps to
S^{2}.

Let
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
be the smallest radius such that

\int_{B_{2}\setminus B_{\lambda}}|du|^{2}\leq{\varepsilon}_{0}.

We have

\lambda^{2}\int_{B_{2}\setminus B_{\lambda}}|du|^{2}\leq\int r^{2}|du|^{2}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4}}\,dV\leq C\|{\widehat{{\mathcal{T}}}}(u)\|_{1}^{2}.

This gives

\lambda\leq C\|{\widehat{{\mathcal{T}}}}(u)\|_{1}.

Now,
[
12
, Corollary 1.3]
reads

(2.7)

\begin{split}&\left|E(u,B_{1})-4\pi n\right|\\
&\qquad\leq C_{k,\beta}\left(\|{\mathcal{T}}(u)\|_{L^{2}(B_{2})}^{2}+E(u,B_{2}\setminus B_{1})+\lambda^{1-\beta}\left(\|{\mathcal{T}}(u)\|_{L^{2}(B_{2})}+\sqrt{E(u,B_{2}\setminus B_{1})}\right)\right).\end{split}

Inserting the above estimates into the RHS of (
2.7
), we get

\left|E(u,B_{1})-4\pi n\right|\leq C\|{\widehat{{\mathcal{T}}}}(u)\|_{1}^{2-\beta}.

We further have

\left|E(u,B_{1})-\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{1}}|du|^{2}e^{\frac{-r^{2}}{4}}\right|\leq\int_{B_{1}}r^{2}|du|^{2}\leq C\|{\widehat{{\mathcal{T}}}}(u)\|_{1}^{2}.

Finally, we have

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus B_{1}}|du|^{2}e^{\frac{-r^{2}}{4}}\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus B_{1}}r^{2}|du|^{2}e^{\frac{-r^{2}}{4}}\leq C\|{\widehat{{\mathcal{T}}}}(u)\|_{1}^{2}.

Combining these, we obtain the desired estimate.
∎

3.
Monotonicity formulae in dimension two

Recall the
pointwise energy identity
for harmonic map flow,

(3.1)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}|du|^{2}+|{\mathcal{T}}(u)|^{2}=\nabla^{i}\nabla^{j}S_{ij},

see
[
8
, (2.7)]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

w=w(r,t)=\exp\left(-{\frac{\phi(x)}{2\tau(t)}}\right).

For a solution
u(t):\Sigma\to N
of harmonic map flow, we have

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}\left(|du|^{2}w\right)+\left|{\mathcal{T}}(u)-\frac{\nabla\phi}{2\tau}\intprod du\right|^{2}w&=\nabla^{i}\nabla^{j}\left(S_{ij}w\right)\\
&\qquad+\frac{w}{2\tau}\nabla^{i}\nabla^{j}\phi S_{ij}+\frac{\left(|\nabla\phi|^{2}+2\phi\tau^{\prime}(t)\right)w}{8\tau^{2}}|du|^{2}.\end{split}

Proof.

Multiplying the pointwise energy identity (
3.1
) by
w,
we obtain

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}\left(|du|^{2}w\right)-\frac{\tau^{\prime}(t)}{4\tau^{2}}\phi|du|^{2}w+|{\mathcal{T}}(u)|^{2}w&=\nabla^{i}\nabla^{j}\left(S_{ij}w\right)-2\nabla^{i}w\nabla^{j}S_{ij}-\nabla^{i}\nabla^{j}wS_{ij}\\
&=\nabla^{i}\nabla^{j}\left(S_{ij}w\right)+2\left\langle{\mathcal{T}}(u),\frac{\nabla\phi\intprod du}{2\tau}\right\rangle w\\
&\quad-\frac{\left(2\tau\nabla^{i}\nabla^{j}\phi+\nabla^{i}\phi\nabla^{j}\phi\right)}{4\tau^{2}}S_{ij}w.\end{split}

Recalling the definition of the stress-energy tensor (
2.1
), we can rearrange to obtain the desired formula.
∎

We have the following versions of Struwe’s monotonicity formula in the critical dimension.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\Sigma={\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and take

\Phi_{\tau}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int|du|^{2}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4\tau}}\,dV,\quad\Psi_{\tau}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int r^{2}|du|^{2}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4\tau}}\,dV.

We have

(3.2)

\frac{d}{dt}\Phi_{\tau}(u(t))+\|{\widehat{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{\tau}(u(t))\|_{\tau}^{2}=\frac{1+\tau^{\prime}(t)}{4\tau^{2}}\Psi_{\tau}(u(t))

and

(3.3)

\frac{d}{dt}\Psi_{\tau}(u(t))+\|r{\widehat{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{\tau}(u(t))\|_{\tau}^{2}=\frac{1+\tau^{\prime}(t)}{8\tau^{2}}\int r^{4}|du|^{2}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4\tau}}\,dV.

Proof.

Take
\phi=\frac{r^{2}}{2}
in the previous Lemma, so that
w=e^{\frac{-r^{2}}{4\tau}}.

We have

(3.4)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}\left(|du|^{2}w\right)+\left|{\widehat{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{\tau}(u)\right|^{2}w&=\nabla^{i}\nabla^{j}\left(S_{ij}w\right)+\frac{\left(1+\tau^{\prime}(t)\right)r^{2}}{8\tau^{2}}|du|^{2}w.\end{split}

The identity (
3.2
) follows by integrating (
3.4
) in space. The identity (
3.3
) follows by integrating by parts against
r^{2}
and using
\nabla^{i}\nabla^{j}r^{2}=2\delta^{ij}
together with tracelessness of
S_{ij}.

∎

4.
Estimates on energy density and oscillation

Lemma 4.1
.

Fix
0<R\leq\sqrt{T},

0<\lambda\leq\frac{1}{2},
and
\kappa>0.

Let
u:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times\left[0,T\right)\to N
be a classical solution of harmonic map flow.
Suppose that
R^{2}\leq T_{1}\leq T
is such that

(4.1)

\sup_{T_{1}-R^{2}\leq t\leq T_{1}-(R\lambda)^{2}}\frac{\Psi_{T_{1}-t}\left(u(t)\right)}{T_{1}-t}\leq{\varepsilon}_{0},

where
{\varepsilon}_{0}>0
depends on the geometry of
N.
Letting

(4.2)

\eta:=\frac{\sqrt{\Psi_{4R^{2}}\left(u(T_{1}-R^{2})\right)}}{R}

and

(4.3)

\psi\left(\log\frac{R}{\sqrt{T_{1}-t}}\right):=\sqrt{\frac{\Psi_{T_{1}-t}\left(u(t)\right)}{T_{1}-t}},

we have

r|du(x,t)|\leq C_{\kappa}\left(\int_{0}^{\log\frac{R}{r}}\psi\left(\sigma\right)e^{\kappa\left(\sigma-\log\frac{R}{r}\right)}\,d\sigma+\eta\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\kappa}\right)

for
\lambda R\leq r\leq\min\left\{\left(C_{N,\kappa}\sqrt[\kappa]{\eta}\right)^{-1},1\right\}R
and

T_{1}-\frac{1}{4}r^{2}\leq t<T_{1}.

Proof.

We may assume without loss of generality that
R=T_{1}=1.
Let
s=-\frac{1}{2}\log\left(1-t\right),
and define

\bar{\psi}\left(s\right):=\sqrt{\frac{\Psi_{4(1-t)}(u(t))}{1-t}}.

We claim that

(4.4)

\bar{\psi}\left(s\right)\leq\eta e^{-\kappa s}+\frac{e^{4+4\kappa}}{\kappa}\int_{0}^{s}\psi\left(\sigma\right)e^{\kappa\left(\sigma-s\right)}\,d\sigma

for all
0\leq s\leq-\log\lambda.

Applying the previous theorem with
\tau(t)=4(1-t),
we obtain

(4.5)

\begin{split}\frac{d\bar{\psi}^{2}}{dt}&\leq\frac{1}{1-t}\left(\bar{\psi}^{2}(t)-\frac{3}{8\tau^{2}}\int r^{4}|du|^{2}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4\tau}}\,dV\right)\end{split}

Note that since
e^{a-3x}>a-3x,
we have

3xe^{-x}>ae^{-x}-e^{a-4x}.

Taking
a=4+4\kappa
and
x=\frac{r^{2}}{4\tau},
and multiplying by
-\frac{r^{2}}{2\tau},
we obtain

-\frac{3r^{4}}{8\tau^{2}}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4\tau}}<\frac{r^{2}}{2\tau}\left(e^{4+4\kappa-\frac{r^{2}}{\tau}}-(4+4\kappa)e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4\tau}}\right).

Using this in the integrand of the second term on the RHS of (
4.5
), we get

\begin{split}(1-t)\frac{d\bar{\psi}^{2}}{dt}&\leq\bar{\psi}^{2}+e^{4+4\kappa}\frac{\Psi_{1-t}(u(t))}{\tau}-(1+\kappa)\bar{\psi}^{2}\\
&\leq e^{4+4\kappa}\psi^{2}-\kappa\bar{\psi}^{2}.\end{split}

Note that
\frac{d}{ds}=2(1-t)\frac{d}{dt},
and the above reduces to

\frac{d\bar{\psi}^{2}}{ds}\leq 2\left(e^{4+4\kappa}\psi^{2}-\kappa\bar{\psi}^{2}\right).

Since
\psi\leq\bar{\psi},
we may use the product rule and divide out by
\bar{\psi},
to obtain

\frac{d\bar{\psi}}{ds}\leq e^{4+4\kappa}\psi-\kappa\bar{\psi}.

By Gronwall’s inequality, we have

\begin{split}\bar{\psi}(s)&\leq\left(\bar{\psi}(0)+e^{4+4\kappa}\int_{0}^{s}\psi(\sigma)e^{\kappa\sigma}\,d\sigma\right)e^{-\kappa s}\\
&\leq\eta e^{-\kappa s}+e^{4+4\kappa}\int_{0}^{s}\psi(\sigma)e^{\kappa(\sigma-s)}\,d\sigma.\end{split}

This establishes the claim (
4.4
).

Now, let
\rho\in\left[\lambda,1\right]
and take
\tilde{\tau}(t)=3\rho^{2}+1-t,
so that
\tilde{\tau}(1-\rho^{2})=4\rho^{2}
and
\tilde{\tau}^{\prime}(t)=-1.
Integrating (
3.3
) from
1-\rho^{2}
to
t\in\left[1-\rho^{2},1\right),
we obtain

\Psi_{\tilde{\tau}(t)}(u(t))\leq\Psi_{\tilde{\tau}(1-\rho^{2})}\left(u\left(1-\rho^{2}\right)\right)=\rho^{2}\bar{\psi}^{2}(-\log\rho).

Since
3\rho^{2}\leq\tilde{\tau}(t)\leq 4\rho^{2},
we get

(4.6)

\begin{split}\int_{\frac{1}{2}\rho}^{2\rho}|du(t)|^{2}\,dV&\leq C\int_{\frac{1}{2}\rho}^{2\rho}\frac{r^{2}}{\tilde{\tau}(t)}|du(t)|^{2}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{4\tilde{\tau}(t)}}\,dV\leq C\frac{\Psi_{\tilde{\tau}(t)}(u(t))}{\rho^{2}}\leq\bar{\psi}^{2}(-\log\rho).\end{split}

Assuming that
\rho^{2\kappa}\leq\frac{{\varepsilon}_{0}}{\eta^{2}},
(
4.4
) implies that the RHS of (
4.6
) is less than
C{\varepsilon}_{0}.
We may apply standard epsilon-regularity, see e.g.
[
8
, Theorem 3.4]
, and (
4.4
), to obtain the desired estimate.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Given
K\geq 1,

1<\alpha\leq 2,
and a compact Riemannian manifold
N,
there exist constants
{\varepsilon}_{1}>0
and
C_{\ref{thm:mainthm}}\geq 1
as follows.

Let
u:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times\left[0,T\right)\to N
be a classical solution of harmonic map flow. Fix
0<T_{1}\leq T
and
0<R\leq\sqrt{T_{1}},
and define
\eta
by (
4.2
).
Suppose that there exists
E_{0}\geq 0,

0<{\varepsilon}\leq{\varepsilon}_{1},
and
0<\lambda\leq\frac{1}{2}
such that

(4.7)

\Phi_{R^{2}}\left(u(T_{1}-R^{2})\right)\leq E_{0}+{\varepsilon},\qquad\Phi_{\lambda^{2}}\left(u(T_{1}-(R\lambda)^{2})\right)\geq E_{0}-{\varepsilon},

and,
for all
T_{1}-R^{2}\leq t<T_{1}-(R\lambda)^{2},

u(t)
obeys a Łojasiewicz inequality of the form

(4.8)

|\Phi_{T_{1}-t}(u(t))-E_{0}|\leq K\left(\sqrt{T_{1}-t}\|{\widehat{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{T_{1}-t}(u(t))\|_{T_{1}-t}\right)^{\alpha}.

Let
r_{0},t,
and
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
be such that
R\sqrt{\lambda}\leq r_{0}\leq\min\left\{1,\left(C_{\ref{thm:mainthm}}\eta\right)^{-1}\right\}R,

T_{1}-\frac{1}{4}r_{0}^{2}\leq t<T_{1},
and
\frac{R^{2}\lambda}{r_{0}}\leq|x|,|y|\leq r_{0}.
If
\alpha<2,
we have

(4.9)

\left|u(x,t)-u(y,t)\right|\leq C_{\ref{thm:mainthm}}\frac{|x-y|}{|x|+|y|}\left(\left({\varepsilon}^{\frac{\alpha-2}{\alpha}}+\log\frac{R}{r_{0}}\right)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha-2}}+\eta\frac{r_{0}}{R}\right).

If
\alpha=2,
we have

(4.10)

\left|u(x,t)-u(y,t)\right|\leq C_{\ref{thm:mainthm}}\frac{|x-y|}{|x|+|y|}\left(\sqrt{{\varepsilon}}\left(\frac{r_{0}}{R}\right)^{\frac{1}{K}}+\eta\frac{r_{0}}{R}\right).

Proof.

As before, we assume without loss of generality that
R=T_{1}=1.
Let

\phi(t):=\Phi_{1-t}(u(t))-E_{0}.

By (
3.2
) and the Łojasiewicz inequality (
4.8
), we have

\frac{d\phi(t)}{dt}+\frac{|\phi(t)|^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}}{K^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}(1-t)}\leq 0.

As in the last proof, let

s=-\frac{1}{2}\log\left(1-t\right),

so that
t\in\left[0,1-\lambda^{2}\right]
corresponds to
s\in\left[0,S\right],
where

S=-\log\lambda.

We get

\frac{d\phi(s)}{ds}+\frac{2}{K^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}}|\phi(s)|^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\leq 0.

Suppose
\alpha<2
and let

(4.11)

\varphi(s):=\left({\varepsilon}^{-\frac{2-\alpha}{\alpha}}+\frac{2(2-\alpha)}{K^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\alpha}s\right)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2-\alpha}},

so that
\varphi(0)={\varepsilon}
and

\frac{d\varphi(s)}{ds}=-\frac{2}{K^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}}\varphi(s)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}

for
s\geq 0.
By Gronwall’s inequality, we have

(4.12)

\phi(s)\leq\varphi(s).

Meanwhile, we also have

\frac{d\left(-\varphi\left(S-s\right)\right)}{ds}=-\frac{2}{K^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}}\varphi\left(S-s\right)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}

for
s\leq S.

Applying Gronwall’s inequality backwards from
s=S
to
s=0,
we obtain

(4.13)

-\varphi\left(S-s\right)\leq\phi(s)\leq\varphi(s).

Next, we translate this into an integral bound on
\Psi.
Let

\delta(s):=\sqrt{1-t}\|{\widehat{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{1-t}\|_{1-t},

where
1-t=e^{-2s}.

By (
3.2
), we have

\frac{d}{ds}\phi(s)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}=-\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}|\phi(s)|^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\delta^{2}(s),

where we define
\phi(s)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}=-|\phi(s)|^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}
if
\phi(s)
is negative.
Fixing any
s\leq S/2,
applying the Łojasiewicz inequality (
4.8
), we may write

(4.14)

\begin{split}\int_{s}^{S-s}\delta(\sigma)\,d\sigma=\int_{s}^{S-s}\delta^{2}(\sigma)\delta(\sigma)^{-1}\,d\sigma&\leq\int_{s}^{S-s}\delta(\sigma)^{2}|\phi(\sigma)|^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\,d\sigma\\
&\leq-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\int_{s}^{S-s}\frac{d}{d\sigma}\phi(\sigma)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\,d\sigma\\
&\leq\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\left(\phi(s)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}-\phi(S-s)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\right)\\
&\leq\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha-1}\varphi(s)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}},\end{split}

where we have used (
4.13
).

As in (
4.3
), define

\psi(s)=\sqrt{\frac{\Psi_{1-t}(u(t))}{1-t}},

where
1-t=e^{-2s}
as above.
By Lemma
2.1
with
\tau=1-t,
we have

\psi(s)\leq 4\delta(s).

Inserting this into (
4.14
), we obtain

(4.15)

\begin{split}\int_{s}^{S-s}\psi(\sigma)\,d\sigma\leq 4\int_{s}^{S-s}\delta(\sigma)\,d\sigma\leq\frac{8\alpha}{\alpha-1}\varphi(s)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}.\end{split}

Note that by the monotonicity formula (
3.3
) with
\tau(t)=1-t,
we have
\psi(s^{\prime})\leq 4\psi(s)
for
s\leq s^{\prime}\leq s+1.
Hence (
4.15
) implies

\psi(s)\leq C{\varepsilon}^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}

for
s\in\left[1,S-1\right].

In particular, assuming that
{\varepsilon}
is sufficiently small and advancing
s
by one unit, the assumption (
4.1
) is satisfied.
For
\lambda\leq r\leq(C\eta)^{-1}
and
1-\frac{1}{4}r^{2}\leq t<1,
Lemma
4.1
with
\kappa=1
now gives

(4.16)

r|du(x,t)|\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{s}\psi\left(\sigma\right)e^{\sigma-s}\,d\sigma+\eta r\right),

where
s=-\log r.

We now use (
4.15
-
4.16
) to obtain the oscillation estimate (
4.9
). Let
r_{0}\in\left[\sqrt{\lambda},1\right]
be given and put

s_{0}=-\log r_{0}\in\left[0,S/2\right].

Let
1-\frac{1}{4}r_{0}^{2}\leq t<1
and
x,y
with
\frac{\lambda}{r_{0}}\leq|x|,|y|\leq r_{0}.

Case 1.
The points
x
and
y
are contained in a dyadic annulus
U_{r_{1}}^{2r_{1}},
where
\frac{\lambda}{r_{0}}\leq r_{1}\leq\frac{r_{0}}{2}.

Let
s_{1}=-\log r_{1}\in\left[s_{0},S-s_{0}\right].
We can integrate (
4.16
) along a path, to obtain

(4.17)

r_{1}|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|\leq C|x-y|\left(\int_{0}^{s_{1}}\psi\left(s\right)e^{s-s_{1}}\,ds+\eta r_{1}\right).

Since
s_{0}\leq s_{1},
we have

(4.18)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{s_{1}}\psi\left(s\right)e^{s-s_{1}}\,ds&\leq\int_{0}^{s_{0}}\psi(s)e^{s-s_{0}}\,ds+\int_{s_{0}}^{S-s_{0}}\psi(s)\,ds\\
&\leq C\left(\varphi(0)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}e^{-s_{0}}+\int_{0}^{s_{0}}\varphi(s)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}e^{s-s_{0}}\,ds+\varphi\left(s_{0}\right)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\right),\end{split}

where we have used integration by parts and (
4.15
). From the definition (
4.11
), for
s\leq s_{0},
we may calculate

(4.19)

\begin{split}\varphi(s)=\varphi(s_{0})\frac{\varphi(s)}{\varphi(s_{0})}&=\varphi(s_{0})\left(\frac{{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{2-\alpha}{\alpha}}+Cs_{0}}{{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{2-\alpha}{\alpha}}+Cs}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{2-\alpha}}\\
&=\varphi(s_{0})\left(1+\frac{C(s_{0}-s)}{{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{2-\alpha}{\alpha}}+Cs}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{2-\alpha}}\\
&\leq C\varphi(s_{0})\left(1+C{\varepsilon}^{\frac{2-\alpha}{\alpha}}(s_{0}-s)\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{2-\alpha}}.\end{split}

Inserting into (
4.18
), and using
\varphi(0)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}e^{-s_{0}}\leq\varphi(s_{0})^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}},
we get

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{s_{1}}\psi\left(s\right)e^{s-s_{1}}\,ds&\leq C\varphi(s_{0})^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\left(1+\int_{0}^{s_{0}}\left(1+C(s_{0}-s)\right)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2-\alpha}}e^{s-s_{0}}\,ds\right)\\
&\leq C\varphi(s_{0})^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}.\end{split}

Inserting into (
4.17
) gives (
4.9
).

Case 2.
The points
x
and
y
are not contained in a dyadic annulus.

By Case 1, it suffices to assume that
x
and
y
lie along a ray, and
2|x|\leq|y|.
We have

|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|\leq\int^{r_{0}}_{\lambda/r_{0}}|du|\,dr=\int_{s_{0}}^{S-s_{0}}r|du|\,ds.

Inserting (
4.16
), we have

(4.20)

\begin{split}|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|\leq\int_{s_{0}}^{S-s_{0}}r|du|\,ds&\leq C\int_{s_{0}}^{S-s_{0}}\left(\int_{0}^{s}\psi\left(\sigma\right)e^{\sigma-s}\,d\sigma+\eta e^{-s}\right)\,ds\\
&\leq C\left(\int_{s_{0}}^{S-s_{0}}\!\!\!\int_{0}^{s}\psi\left(\sigma\right)e^{\sigma-s}\,d\sigma\,ds+\eta e^{-s_{0}}\right).\end{split}

Changing variables
\varsigma=s-\sigma,
we can estimate the integral by

\begin{split}\int_{s_{0}}^{S-s_{0}}\!\!\!\int_{0}^{s}\psi\left(\sigma\right)e^{\sigma-s}\,d\sigma\,ds&=\int_{0}^{S-s_{0}}e^{-\varsigma}\int_{\max\{s_{0}-\varsigma,0\}}^{S-s_{0}-\varsigma}\psi(\sigma)\,d\sigma d\varsigma\\
&\leq C\int_{0}^{S-s_{0}}e^{-\varsigma}\varphi\left(\max\{s_{0}-\varsigma,0\}\right)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}d\varsigma,\end{split}

where we have used (
4.15
). Using (
4.19
) again, with
s=s_{0}-\varsigma,
we can estimate

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{S-s_{0}}e^{-\varsigma}\varphi\left(\max\{s_{0}-\varsigma,0\}\right)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}d\varsigma&=\int_{0}^{s_{0}}e^{-\varsigma}\varphi\left(s_{0}-\varsigma\right)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}d\varsigma+\int_{s_{0}}^{S-s_{0}}e^{-\varsigma}\varphi\left(0\right)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}d\varsigma\\
&\leq C\varphi(s_{0})^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\left(\int_{0}^{s_{0}}e^{-\varsigma}\left(1+C\varsigma\right)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2-\alpha}}\,d\varsigma+1\right)\\
&\leq C\varphi(s_{0})^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}.\end{split}

Returning to (
4.20
), we obtain

\begin{split}|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|\leq C\left(\varphi\left(s_{0}\right)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}+\eta r_{0}\right),\end{split}

which is the bound (
4.9
) in the present case. This completes the proof of (
4.9
).

The bound (
4.10
) is proved similarly.
∎

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
u:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\times\left[0,T\right)\to S^{2}
be a classical solution of harmonic map flow.

(a) The body map
u(T)
extends to a continuous map
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to S^{2}.

(b) Given a point
p\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and any sequence of times
t_{i}\nearrow T,
the maps
u(t_{i})
sub-converge in the bubble-tree sense without necks. Specifically, there exist finitely many nonconstant harmonic maps
\phi_{k}:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to S^{2},
obtained as rescaled limits of
u(t_{i}),
such that the energy identity holds:

\lim_{r\searrow 0}\lim_{t\nearrow T}E(u(t),B_{r}(p))=\sum_{k}E(\phi_{k}).

Moreover, we have
\lim_{x\to\infty}\phi_{k}(x)=u(p,T)
for at least one
k.

Proof.

(
a
) It suffices to prove continuity of
u(T)
at
0\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}.
By monotonicity of
\Phi,
the limit

E_{0}:=\lim_{R\searrow 0}\Phi_{R^{2}}\left(u(T-R^{2})\right)\geq 0

exists, and we may take
R>0
such that

\Phi_{R^{2}}(u(T-R^{2}))\leq E_{0}+{\varepsilon}_{0}.

Now, the Łojasiewicz inequality of Theorem
2.2
gives (
4.8
) after rescaling by
\tau=T-t,
where we must have
E_{0}=4\pi n.
The result follows from the previous Corollary.

(
b
) The proof is standard in light of Theorem
4.2
(see
[
8
, Theorem 6.8]
for a recent account), and we omit it.
∎

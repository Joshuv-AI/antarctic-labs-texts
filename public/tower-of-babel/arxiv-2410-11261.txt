Beyond Linear Approximations: A Novel Pruning Approach for Attention Matrix
Yingyu Liang, Jiangxuan Long, Zhenmei Shi, Zhao Song, Yufa Zhou

Abstract
Large Language Models (LLMs) have shown immense potential in enhancing various aspects of our daily lives, from conversational AI to search and AI assistants. However, their growing capabilities come at the cost of extremely large model sizes, making deployment on edge devices challenging due to memory and computational constraints. This paper introduces a novel approach to LLM weight pruning that directly optimizes for approximating the attention matrix, a core component of transformer architectures. Unlike existing methods that focus on linear approximations, our approach accounts for the non-linear nature of the Softmax attention mechanism. We provide theoretical guarantees for the convergence of our Gradient Descent-based optimization method to a near-optimal pruning mask solution. Our empirical results demonstrate the effectiveness of our non-linear pruning approach in maintaining model performance while significantly reducing computational costs, which is beyond the current state-of-the-art methods, i.e., SparseGPT and Wanda, by a large margin. This work establishes a new theoretical foundation for pruning algorithm design in LLMs, potentially paving the way for more efficient LLM inference on resource-constrained devices.

Key background.

We introduce some key backgrounds. We define the attention matrix in the self-attention mechanism as below:

Definition 1.1

(Attention Matrix)
.

Let
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
be the input.
Given query and key weights matrix
W_{Q},W_{K}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, we define
W:=W_{Q}W_{K}^{\top}
.
Then, we have the Softmax attention matrix being

\displaystyle\mathsf{Softmax}(XWX^{\top})=D^{-1}\exp(XWX^{\top}),

where (1)
D:=\diag(\exp(XWX^{\top})\cdot{\bf 1}_{n})
,
(2)
\exp
denotes the exponential function and is applied entry-wisely,
(3)
\diag()
operation takes a vector and outputs a diagonal matrix with the entries of that vector,
and (4)
{\bf 1}_{n}
denotes the length-
n
all ones vector.

Further, we introduce the problem setup of our Attention Weights Pruning.
By selectively reducing the number of non-zero elements in the attention weight matrix
W
in Definition
1.1
, we can preserve model performance while lowering computational cost and GPU memory usage.
Below, we formally define the Attention Weights Pruning problem and the corresponding loss function:

Definition 1.2

(Attention Weights Pruning)
.

Let
M\in[0,1]^{d\times d}
be the pruning mask.
Let
X,W
be defined in Definition
1.1
.
Let
A:=\exp(XWX^{\top})
and
\widetilde{A}:=\exp(X(M\circ W)X^{\top})
, where
\circ
is the Hadamard product.
Let
D:=\diag(A\cdot{\bf 1}_{n})
and
\widetilde{D}:=\diag(\widetilde{A}\cdot{\bf 1}_{n})
.
Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
be the regularization parameter.
We define the Attention Weights Pruning loss function to be

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(M):=\frac{1}{2}\|D^{-1}A-\widetilde{D}^{-1}\widetilde{A}\|_{F}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda\|M\|_{F}^{2}.

Thus, the Attention Weights Pruning optimization problem is
\min_{M}~\mathcal{L}(M)
.

7
Conclusion

This paper introduces a novel approach to LLM weight pruning that directly optimizes for approximating the attention matrix. We provide theoretical guarantees for the convergence of our Gradient Descent-based algorithm to a near-optimal pruning mask solution. Experimental results demonstrated the method’s effectiveness in maintaining model performance while reducing computational costs. This work establishes a new theoretical foundation for pruning algorithm design in LLMs, potentially enabling more efficient inference on resource-constrained devices.

.

•

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\mathrm{d}f_{i}(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}

•

\underbrace{\frac{\mathrm{d}X}{\mathrm{d}X_{i,j}}}_{n\times m}=\underbrace{e_{i}}_{n\times 1}\underbrace{e_{j}^{\top}}_{1\times m}

Fact B.4

(Basic algebra)
.

Let
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
,
Y\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
, and
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
. Then, we have

•

\langle u,v\rangle=\langle v,u\rangle=u^{\top}v=v^{\top}u

•

u\circ v=v\circ u=\diag(u)v=\diag(v)u

•

\langle u,v\rangle=\langle u\circ v,{\bf 1}_{n}\rangle

•

\langle u\circ v,w\rangle=\langle u\circ w,v\rangle=\langle w\circ v,u\rangle

•

u^{\top}(v\circ w)=u^{\top}\diag(v)w

•

(X\circ Y)^{\top}=X^{\top}\circ Y^{\top}

•

X\circ e_{i}e_{j}^{\top}=X_{i,j}e_{i}e_{j}^{\top}

•

\diag(u)Z\diag(v)=(uv^{\top})\circ Z

•

XY^{\top}=\sum_{i\in[d]}X_{*,i}Y^{\top}_{*,i}

•

X_{i,j}Y_{i,j}=(X\circ Y)_{i,j}

•

\sum_{j\in[n]}u\circ A_{*,j}=u\circ\sum_{j\in[n]}A_{*,j}

•

\|X\|_{F}^{2}=\tr[XX^{\top}]

•

\tr[XY^{\top}]=\tr[Y^{\top}X]

•

\|\diag(u)\|_{F}=\|u\|_{2}

Fact B.5

(Norm bounds)
.

For

,
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
X,Y\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
,
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times m}
we have

•

\|aX\|_{F}=|a|\|X\|_{F}
(absolute homogeneity).

•

\|X+Y\|_{F}\leq\|X\|_{F}+\|Y\|_{F}
(triangle inequality).

•

|\langle X,Y\rangle|\leq\|X\|_{F}\cdot\|Y\|_{F}
(Cauchy–Schwarz inequality).

•

\|X^{\top}\|_{F}=\|X\|_{F}
.

•

\|Xu\|_{2}\leq\|X\|\cdot\|u\|_{2}

•

\|X\circ Y\|_{F}\leq\|X\|_{F}\cdot\|Y\|_{F}
.

•

For any

,
j\in[d]
, we have
|X_{i,j}|\leq\|X\|_{F}
.

•

\|X\|\leq\|X\|_{F}\leq\sqrt{k}\|X\|
where
k
is the rank of
X
.

•

\|Y\cdot Z\|_{F}\leq\|Y\|_{F}\cdot\|Z\|_{F}
.

Fact B.6
.

For matrices
A,B\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
, we have

\displaystyle\|A+B\|_{F}^{2}=\|A\|_{F}^{2}+\|B\|_{F}^{2}+2\langle A,B\rangle.

Proof.

We can show

\displaystyle\|A+B\|_{F}^{2}=

\displaystyle~\tr[(A+B)^{\top}(A+B)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\tr[A^{\top}A+A^{\top}B+B^{\top}A+B^{\top}B]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\tr[A^{\top}A]+\tr[B^{\top}B]+2\tr[A^{\top}B]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\|A\|_{F}^{2}+\|B\|_{F}^{2}+2\tr[A^{\top}B]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\|A\|_{F}^{2}+\|B\|_{F}^{2}+2\langle A,B\rangle

where the first step follows from
\tr[A^{\top}A]=\|A\|_{F}^{2}
for matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
, the second step follows from the basic algebra, the third follows from
\tr[X^{\top}Y]=\tr[XY^{\top}]
for matrices
X,Y\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
, the fourth step follows from
\tr[A^{\top}A]=\|A\|_{F}^{2}
for matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
, and the last step follows from definition of inner product of matrices.
∎

.

\displaystyle~X_{i,j}e_{i}e_{j}^{\top}

Fact G.1
.

We can show that for
a,b\in\mathbb{R}

•

Part 1.

\displaystyle\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}\geq\frac{|a|+|b|}{\sqrt{2}}

•

Part 2. Suppose
|a|>|b|

\displaystyle\sqrt{|a|-|b|}\geq\sqrt{|a|}-\sqrt{|b|}

Proof.

Proof of Part 1.

Square both side of the inequality in
Part 1.
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{LHS}=a^{2}+b^{2}

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{RHS}=\frac{a^{2}+2|a|\cdot|b|+b^{2}}{2}.

So we just need to prove

\displaystyle\mathrm{LHS}-\mathrm{RHS}=

\displaystyle~a^{2}+b^{2}-\frac{a^{2}+2|a|\cdot|b|+b^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\frac{a^{2}+b^{2}-2|a|\cdot|b|}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\frac{(|a|-|b|)^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 0

which is hold because for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
x^{2}\geq 0
.

Proof of Part 2.

Square both side of the inequality in
Part 2.
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{LHS}=|a|-|b|

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{RHS}=|a|+|b|-2\sqrt{|a||b|}

So we just need to prove

\displaystyle\mathrm{LHS}-\mathrm{RHS}=

\displaystyle~|a|-|b|-|a|-|b|+2\sqrt{|a||b|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~2\sqrt{|a||b|}-2|b|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~2\sqrt{|b|}(\sqrt{|a|}-\sqrt{|b|})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~0

which is hold because

G.2
Lower Bound on Frobenius Norm

In this section, we present the lemma for the lower bound on the Frobenius norm.

Lemma G.2
.

If the following conditions hold

•

Let
B\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
.

•

Let
M\in[0,1]^{d\times d}
.

•

Let
\lambda\in[0,1]
be some constant.

•

Suppose that
\|B\|_{F}\leq R
.

Then, we can show

•

Part 1.

\displaystyle\|B+\lambda M\|_{F}^{2}\geq\|B\|_{F}^{2}+\lambda^{2}\|M\|_{F}^{2}-2R\lambda d

•

Part 2.

\displaystyle\|B+\lambda M\|_{F}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\|B\|_{F}+\lambda\|M\|_{F})-\sqrt{2R\lambda d}

Proof.

Proof of Part 1.

We can show that

\displaystyle\|B+\lambda M\|_{F}^{2}=

\displaystyle~\|B\|_{F}^{2}+\lambda^{2}\|M\|_{F}^{2}+2\langle B,\lambda M\rangle

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~\|B\|_{F}^{2}+\lambda^{2}\|M\|_{F}^{2}-2\|B\|_{F}\cdot\|\lambda M\|_{F}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~\|B\|_{F}^{2}+\lambda^{2}\|M\|_{F}^{2}-2R\lambda d

(15)

where the first step follows from Fact
B.6
,
the second step follows from Fact
B.5
,
the third step follows from the upper bound of
\|B\|_{F}
and
\|M\|_{F}
.

Proof of Part 2.

Taking the square root on both sides, we get

\displaystyle\|B+\lambda M\|_{F}\geq

\displaystyle~\sqrt{\|B\|_{F}^{2}+\lambda^{2}\|M\|_{F}^{2}-2R\lambda d}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~\sqrt{\|B\|_{F}^{2}+\lambda^{2}\|M\|_{F}^{2}}-\sqrt{2R\lambda d}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\|B\|_{F}+\lambda\|M\|_{F})-\sqrt{2R\lambda d}

where the first step follows from Eq. (
15
),
the second step follows from
Part 2.
of Fact
G.1
,
and the third step follows from
Part 1.
of Fact
G.1
.

∎

G.3
Sandwich Lower Bound on Frobenius Norm

Here, we introduce a sandwich trace fact.

Fact G.3
.

If
A\succeq\beta I
, then
\tr[B^{\top}AB]\geq\beta\tr[B^{\top}B]
.

Proof.

As
A\succeq\beta I
, we have

A-\beta I\succeq 0
. Multiplying both sides by
B^{\top}
on the left and
B
on the right (noting that these operations preserve the positive semidefiniteness), we have

\displaystyle B^{\top}(A-\beta I)B\succeq 0.

Taking the trace and utilizing the property that the trace of a positive semidefinite matrix is non-negative, we have

\displaystyle\tr[B^{\top}AB-\beta B^{\top}B]\geq 0,

which simplifies to

\displaystyle\tr[B^{\top}AB]-\beta\tr[B^{\top}B]\geq 0.

This concludes the proof.
∎

We establish a sandwich lower bound on the Frobenius norm.

Lemma G.4

(Formal version of Lemma
5.6
)
.

If the following conditions hold

•

Let
B\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
.

•

Assume that
XX^{\top}\succeq\beta I
.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\|X^{\top}BX\|_{F}\geq\beta\|B\|_{F}

Proof.

We can show that

\displaystyle\|X^{\top}BX\|_{F}^{2}=

\displaystyle~\tr[X^{\top}BXX^{\top}B^{\top}X]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~\beta\cdot\tr[X^{\top}BB^{\top}X]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\beta\cdot\tr[B^{\top}XX^{\top}B]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~\beta^{2}\cdot\tr[B^{\top}B]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\beta^{2}\cdot\|B\|_{F}^{2}

where the first step, the third step, and the fifth step follow from Fact
B.4
,
the second step and the fourth step follows from Fact
G.3
and
XX^{\top}\succeq\beta I
.

Taking the square root of both sides, we finish the proof.
∎

G.4

We present the lemma for the lower bound on the Hadamard product between two matrices in this section.

\displaystyle\max_{i,j\in[d]}\{|W_{i,j}|\}\cdot\|B\|_{F}\geq\|W\circ B\|_{F}\geq\min_{i,j\in[d]}\{|W_{i,j}|\}\cdot\|B\|_{F}.

The proof directly follows from the definition of the Frobenius norm.

G.5
Final Bound

We introduce some useful lemmas that we use to prove the final bound.

Lemma G.6
.

If the following conditions hold

•

Let
b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\langle b,{\bf 1}_{n}\rangle=0
.

•

Let
f\in[\delta,1]^{n}
and
\langle f,{\bf 1}_{n}\rangle=1
.

Then we have

\displaystyle\|(b-\langle b,f\rangle{\bf 1}_{n})\circ f\|_{2}\geq\delta\|b\|_{2}.

Proof.

Note that
\langle b,{\bf 1}_{n}\rangle=0
so that
b
and
{\bf 1}_{n}
are orthogonal with each other. Then, we have

\displaystyle\|(b-\langle b,f\rangle{\bf 1}_{n})\circ f\|_{2}\geq

\displaystyle~\delta\|b-\langle b,f\rangle{\bf 1}_{n}\|_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\delta\sqrt{\|b\|_{2}^{2}+\|\langle b,f\rangle{\bf 1}_{n}\|_{2}^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~\delta\|b\|_{2},

where the second step is from the Pythagorean theorem.
∎

We present our final bound for proving the PL inequality.

Lemma G.7

(Formal version of Lemma
5.7
)
.

If the following conditions hold

•

Let
B\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and each row summation is zero, i.e.,
B\cdot{\bf 1}_{n}={\bf 0}_{n}
.

•

Let
\widetilde{f}(M)\in[0,1]^{n\times n}
and each row summation is 1, i.e.,
\widetilde{f}(M)\cdot{\bf 1}_{n}={\bf 1}_{n}
.

•

Assume that
\min_{i,j\in[n]}\widetilde{f}(M)_{i,j}\geq\delta>0
.

Then, we can show

\displaystyle\|B\circ\widetilde{f}(M)-\diag((B\circ\widetilde{f}(M))\cdot{\bf 1}_{n})\widetilde{f}(M)\|_{F}\geq\delta\cdot\|B\|_{F}

Proof.

For any
i\in[n]
, let
B_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the
i
-th row of
B
, and we have
\langle B_{i},{\bf 1}_{n}\rangle=0
by the first condition.

For any
i\in[n]
, let
\widetilde{f}(M)_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the
i
-th row of
\widetilde{f}(M)
, and we have
\langle\widetilde{f}(M)_{i},{\bf 1}_{n}\rangle=1
by the second condition and
\widetilde{f}(M)_{i,j}\in[\delta,1]
by the third condition.

By Lemma
G.6
, for any
i\in[n]
, we have

\displaystyle\|(B_{i}-\langle B_{i},\widetilde{f}(M)_{i}\rangle{\bf 1}_{n})\circ\widetilde{f}(M)_{i}\|_{2}\geq\delta\|B_{i}\|_{2}.

Then, we have

\displaystyle~\|B\circ\widetilde{f}(M)-\diag((B\circ\widetilde{f}(M))\cdot{\bf 1}_{n})\widetilde{f}(M)\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\sum_{i\in[n]}\|(B_{i}-\langle B_{i},\widetilde{f}(M)_{i}\rangle{\bf 1}_{n})\circ\widetilde{f}(M)_{i}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~\sum_{i\in[n]}\delta^{2}\|B_{i}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\delta^{2}\|B\|_{F}^{2}.

∎

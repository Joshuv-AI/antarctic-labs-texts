Reproduction Number and Spatial Connectivity Structure Estimation via Graph Sparsity-Promoting Penalized Functional
Etienne Lasalle, Barbara Pascal

Abstract




Abstract.

During an epidemic outbreak, decision makers crucially need accurate and robust tools to monitor the pathogen propagation.
The effective reproduction number, defined as the expected number of secondary infections stemming from one contaminated individual, is a state-of-the-art indicator quantifying the epidemic intensity.
Numerous estimators have been developed to precisely track the reproduction number temporal evolution.
Yet, COVID-19 pandemic surveillance raised unprecedented challenges due to the poor quality of worldwide reported infection counts.
When monitoring the epidemic in different territories simultaneously, leveraging the spatial structure of data significantly enhances both the accuracy and robustness of reproduction number estimates.
However, this requires a good estimate of the spatial structure.
To tackle this major limitation, the present work proposes a joint estimator of the reproduction number and connectivity structure.
The procedure is assessed through intensive numerical simulations on carefully designed synthetic data and illustrated on real COVID-19 spatiotemporal infection counts.

Keywords.

Regularized estimation, Spatiotemporal signals, Graph learning, Alternating optimization, Epidemiology

Accurate and efficient monitoring tools are of key importance during epidemic outbreaks.

State-of-the-art reproduction number estimators, although enforcing a mild temporal regularity of the estimate

Furthermore, as pandemics are simultaneously monitored in a large number of territories (e.g., different countries, counties of a state, states of a federal country), extensions of these variational estimators took advantage of the

Goals, contributions and outline.

Given infection count time series in several territories, the overarching goal of the present work is to

To that aim, elaborating on the graph representation framework developed in

A major originality of the present work is to perform Laplacian estimation under Poisson data-dependent noise, contrary to most of previous Laplacian estimation procedures developed under Gaussianity assumption

The second major originality is that, contrary to standard graph signal processing frameworks in which the signal is

The proposed joint estimation of the spatiotemporal reproduction number and connectivity structure is developed in Section
3

Section
4

Concluding remarks and perspectives are provided in Section

Regularized spatiotemporal variational estimator.

Due to the low quality of reported counts illustrated in Fig.
1

A first building block of the present work consists in designing a variational estimator of spatiotemporal reproduction numbers adapted to the multivariate scaled Poisson model of Eq. (
1

connected

fixed

Following

7

8

[

is replaced by a multivariate scaled Kullback-Leibler divergence, designed from the negative log-likelihood of the scaled Poisson model derived in
[

Temporal regularity is obtained by enforcing a piecewise linear temporal behavior of the reproduction number, independently on each country, via the penalization of the
\ell_{1}

To favor a smooth spatial behavior, the connectivity structure is encoded in a graph whose vertices are the countries and with edges linking countries expected to share similar epidemiological dynamics and a graph total variation penalization
[
15

is used to regularize the spatial variations of the reproduction number.
While

used an unweighted graph, the present work proposes to consider a

Finally, to ease the design of a Laplacian learning strategy in Section

Regularized and sparsity-inducing objective function.

\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{R}}}^{\mathsf{Joint}},\widehat{\mathsf{L}}^{\mathsf{Joint}}\in\underset{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{R}}\in\mathcal{R},\mathsf{L}\in\mathcal{L}}{\mathrm{Argmin}}\,J(\boldsymbol{\mathsf{R}},\mathsf{L})\,.

Conclusions and perspectives

A joint estimator of reproduction numbers and spatial connectivity structure has been proposed.
Intensive numerical experiments demonstrated its remarkable ability to infer spatiotemporal epidemiological indicators in an accurate and robust manner, while providing precise insights on the underlying spatial pattern.
The robustness of this
\mathsf{Joint}
method to change in the pool of monitored territories and in the phase of the pandemic will be systematically explored on real data.
For reproducibility, the code is publicly available on Github.

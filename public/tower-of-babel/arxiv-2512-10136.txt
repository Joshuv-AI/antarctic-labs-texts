Regularity of the free boundary for the supercooled Stefan problem in arbitrary dimensions
Max Engelstein, Inwon Kim, Sebastian Munoz

Abstract
. We study the free boundary in the supercooled Stefan problem, a classical model for the solidification of water below its freezing temperature. In contrast with the melting problem, physical experiments and heuristics indicate that the water–ice interface in the supercooled problem may exhibit fractal freezing sets, infinite-speed propagation of the frozen front, and nucleation (the spontaneous appearance of ice). Despite this, we show that the free boundary has a robust structure. We decompose the free boundary into three parts: (1) a regular part that advances with finite speed in time; (2) a singular part consisting of points where the front attains infinite speed or nucleates, but with controlled space-time (i.e., ≤ d − 1 \leq d-1 parabolic) dimension; and (3) a jump component, which can have large dimension in a time slice, but which is contained in a space-time smooth graph and occurs only at a zero-dimensional set of times. Examples show that each of these parts can be nonempty. Furthermore, we prove that the free boundary is the graph t = s ⁡ ( x ) t=s(x) of a continuously differentiable freezing time s s , and the singular set coincides with the critical set of s s , proving that singularities in supercooled freezing always occur with infinite speed. These results provide the first free boundary regularity theory for the supercooled Stefan problem in arbitrary dimensions.

From a technical standpoint, our results require several new ideas. We expect these techniques to be useful in other parabolic free boundary problems where space-independent solutions arise, such as the time-like singularities in the parabolic thin obstacle problem

1.1.
Main ideas of the proofs

While our analysis builds on previous studies of the singular set for the melting Stefan problem (e.g.
[
FRS24
]
,
[
Col25
]
,
[
FLR25
]
), the supercooled case contains unique challenges, such as the discontinuity of the temperature
w_{t}=-\eta
, the potential jumps of the free boundary over time, and, as discussed earlier, the presence of time-dependent blow-up profiles in every stratum of the singular set. We write

\Sigma=\Sigma_{\operatorname{stat}}\cup\Sigma_{\operatorname{dyn}},

where
\Sigma_{\operatorname{stat}}
and
\Sigma_{\operatorname{dyn}}
are, respectively, the singular points with stationary and time-dependent blow-up profiles. The latter set
\Sigma_{\operatorname{dyn}}
, corresponding to
m\neq 0
in (
1.6
), is ruled out immediately by the condition
w_{t}>0
in the melting problem. However, in the supercooled setting it is the set of singular points with potentially the largest dimension and also contains the singular points where the free boundary recedes fastest, including potential jumps. Its detailed analysis is one of the main novelties of this paper.

We turn to the proof of our first main result, Theorem
1.1
. Work of
[
LM15
]
(see also Theorem
2.3
below), says that the spatial projection of the
k
th singular strata has dimension
\leq k

[

(see also their earlier work in elliptic setting

), says that if at each point in
\Sigma_{k}
there exists a backwards in time parabola which does not intersect any other point of
\Sigma_{k}
, then the estimate on the projections of
\Sigma_{k}
can be upgraded to
\dim_{\text{par}}(\Sigma_{k})\leq k
. The existence of said parabola is almost trivial for points in
\Sigma_{\text{dyn}}
and we are able to follow work of
[
Col25
]
to prove it for points in
\Sigma_{\text{stat}}\backslash\Sigma_{d-1}
(though executing this roadmap requires new technical estimates in the supercooled case, as some of the estimates in the melting case do not hold, see Section
3
and especially Section
3.2
).

Thus, to complete our proof of Theorem
1.1
, we only need to study
\Sigma_{\text{stat}}\cap\Sigma_{d-1}
. Here our approach diverges substantially from
[
FRS24
,
Col25
]
; we prove that, in the supercooled setting,
\Sigma_{\text{stat}}\cap\Sigma_{d-1}=\emptyset
. We do this in Section

. Roughly, by first establishing an

algebraic condition on the second blowup at such points (if they existed), via a method inspired by
[
FRS20
,
FS19
]
. However, we then show that these second blowups must satisfy a quantitative non-degeneracy condition (see Lemma

) inherited from

We emphasize that this is not just an algebraic coincidence; our analysis shows that points in
\Sigma_{d-1}\cap\Sigma_{\text{stat}}
are the only singularities which might form with finite speed. Ruling them out is thus a physically significant result and also a key point in proving Theorem
1.2

Turning to the proof of Theorem
1.2

For the stationary singular points, having ruled out points in
\Sigma_{d-1}

\Sigma_{\text{stat}}

Finally, we turn to the proof of our last main result, Theorem
1.3

\Sigma_{d}
(the top stratum) into two components
\Sigma_{d}^{\leq\infty}
which has projections that are

\Sigma_{d}^{\infty}
which is empty for all but a zero dimensional set of times. Our key tool is an analysis of the second blowup. While this is highly reminiscent of the statement and proof strategy of
[

\Sigma_{d}^{\leq\infty}
and the time regularity of
\Sigma_{d}^{\infty}

The first new idea is to categorize the points in the top stratum according to their second blow-up. In the melting case, it is known that the second blow-up of the solution is always a function of parabolic degree
2
or
3
. By contrast, in our case the second blow-up at a point of
\Sigma_{d}
can have arbitrarily large degree (in fact, the second blow-up for the solution (
1.3

(0,0)
is
q\equiv 0

can vanish both quadratically and to higher integer order was first observed in a one-dimensional setting by Herrero and Velázquez
[
HV96
, Theorem 1]
. Although the details of our analysis shares little in common with theirs, we were inspired by this observation.

In this vein, we further decompose the top stratum as

\Sigma_{d}=\Sigma_{d}^{\infty}\cup\bigcup_{k=2}^{\infty}\Sigma_{d}^{k},

where
\Sigma_{d}^{k}
is the set of singular points in
\Sigma_{d}
where the second blow-up has degree
k
(we refer to Section
6
for the precise definitions). The set
\Sigma_{d}^{\infty}
is the points at which
w-(-t)^{+}
vanishes to infinite order. We emphasize again that this is a fundamentally different decomposition than the one in
[
FRS24
]
(where the “rare" set comes from the set of points at which two different polynomial expansions agree to infinite order).

In our setting, it is straightforward to show that
\Sigma_{d}^{\infty}
is contained in a space-time smooth manifold and is rare in time. The main difficulty is therefore to show that the high-dimensional pathologies of supercooled freezing are entirely contained in
\Sigma_{d}^{\infty}
. To this end, we need to deal with two distinct cases; the set of quadratic second blow-ups versus the higher order second blow-ups. While quadratic functions are the simplest profiles, the main difficulty with these points is that the very slow rate of convergence (slower than any power) makes it difficult to extract information about the solution (or to even prove that the second blow-up is unique). Quadratic second blow-ups are known to exist in both the elliptic case and the melting case (
[
FS19
, App. A]
,
[
FRS24
, Lem. 5.8]
), but they typically belong to a lower-dimensional stratum, so sharp dimension bounds for the singular set can be obtained without analyzing the second blow-up of these points. Indeed,
[
Col25
, Corollary 4.14]
shows that quadratic second blow-ups never appear in the top stratum in the melting problem. In our case, however, quadratic second blow-ups can occur in the top stratum (we present a family of examples in Proposition
9.4
), and thus we must treat them in detail for our purposes. As an aside, it is conjectured in
[
HV96
]
that
\Sigma_{d}^{2}\neq\emptyset
for an open set of initial data. We do not attempt to pursue this direction here but think it is an interesting question for future study.

We show that, even if the second blow-up profile is not unique and the convergence is slow, the solution at points of
\Sigma_{d}^{2}
inherits, in a very weak qualitative sense, some of the “concave” behavior of its possible subsequential limits (Lemma

). We leverage this mild information, together with a two-sided superquadratic cleaning property that is unique to the supercooled problem in its top stratum (Lemma
6.1
), to obtain a suitable porosity property for the set
\Sigma_{d}^{2}
that enables the necessary dimension drop (Proposition
6.7
).

We then turn to the set
\Sigma_{d}^{k}
for

(points where the second blow-up is a degree
k

The advantage, however, is that the convergence is much faster, which leads to significantly more regularity. Indeed, by exploiting the fast convergence and the everywhere positivity of the blow-up profile

for

\Sigma_{d}^{\geq 3}
(see Proposition

[

, where two different one-sided Taylor expansion are exhibited at (almost) every point of
\Sigma_{d-1}
.

However, our use of the Taylor expansion is completely different. Without discussing all the technicalities, we view the
k
th order Taylor series expansion at each point as a polynomial in

. We then show that the zero set of the (spatially dependent) leading coefficient
4

This is a highly delicate argument using Reifenberg-type theories for sets well-approximated by the zero sets of harmonic polynomials, as well as the sharp Łojasiewicz inequality for harmonic polynomials

, we obtain the basic parabolic dimension estimates for these points, and we take advantage of the fundamental estimates of Section
3
and the previous analysis of
\Sigma_{\operatorname{stat}}
to prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.

For the following sections, we further restrict our attention to the top stratum of the singular set, namely the set
\Sigma_{d}\subset\Sigma_{\operatorname{dyn}}
of points with blow-up profile
p_{2}(x,t)=-t
. Recalling from above that
\Sigma_{d}^{\infty}
are those points which, roughly, coincide with the blow-up
(-t)^{+}
to infinite order, the bulk of Sections
6
and
7
is dedicated to proving the estimate

\dim_{\operatorname{par}}(\Sigma_{d}\setminus\Sigma_{d}^{\infty})\leq d-1.

In Section
6

\Sigma_{d}^{2}

\Sigma_{d}^{\geq 3}:=\Sigma_{d}^{\infty}\cup\bigcup_{k=3}^{\infty}\Sigma_{d}^{k}

In Section

\Sigma_{d}^{\infty}

.

Finally, in Section

2.
Preliminary facts for the supercooled Stefan problem

Notation

We define, for
r>0
, the parabolic cylinders

Q_{r}^{-}:=B_{r}\times(-r^{2},0],\quad Q_{r}:=B_{r}\times(-r^{2},r^{2}),

and the parabolic boundary

\partial_{p}Q^{-}_{r}=(\partial B_{r}\times[-r^{2},0])\cup(B_{r}\times\{-r^{2}\}).

The heat operator and the parabolic homogeneity operator are denoted by

\mathbf{H}:=\Delta-\partial_{t},\quad Z:=x\cdot\nabla+2t\partial_{t},

and the backward heat kernel is given by

G(x,t):=\frac{1}{(-4\pi t)^{d/2}}\exp\left(\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}\right),\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(-\infty,0).

For
k\geq 1
,
r\in(0,1)
, and functions
f,g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(-1,0)\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
, we define

\langle f,g\rangle_{r}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(f\cdot g)(x,-r^{2})G(x,-r^{2})dx,

and we write
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{1}
. When
k=1
and
f
is sufficiently regular, we also write

(2.1)

H(r,f):=\langle f,f\rangle_{r}=\int_{\{t=-r^{2}\}}f^{2}G,\quad\quad D(r,f):=2r^{2}\langle\nabla f,\nabla f\rangle_{r}=2r^{2}\int_{\{t=-r^{2}\}}|\nabla f|^{2}G.

For
\gamma>0
, the parabolic frequency functions are defined by

(2.2)

\phi(r,f):=\frac{D(r,f)}{H(r,f)},\quad\phi^{\gamma}(r,f):=\frac{D(r,f)+\gamma r^{\gamma}}{H(r,f)+r^{\gamma}}.

We will denote by
\zeta
a fixed spatial cut-off function:

(2.3)

\zeta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{1/2}),\quad 0\leq\zeta\leq 1,\quad\zeta\equiv 1\;\;\text{ in }\;B_{1/4}.

We also define the parabolic rescalings

f_{r}(x,t):=f(rx,r^{2}t).

2.1.
The parabolic obstacle problem

We begin by presenting the basic properties of bounded solutions
w:Q_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
to (
1.2
), from the classical theory of the parabolic obstacle problem. We also summarize at the end the main differences from what holds for the melting Stefan problem.

By
[
CPS04
, Sec. 4]
, we have
w\in C^{1,1}_{x}\cap C_{t}^{0}
and

(2.4)

\|D^{2}w\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1/2})}+\|w_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1/2})}\leq C\|w\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}

for some
C=C(d)>0
. Moreover, if
(0,0)\in\overline{\{w>0\}}
then, for every
r\in(0,1)
, one has the nondegeneracy property

(2.5)

\sup_{B_{r}}w(\cdot,-r^{2})\geq w(0,0)+c_{d}r^{2},

where
c_{d}\in(0,1)
is a dimensional constant, see
[
CPS04
, Lem 5.1]
(they bound from below the supremum in the entire cylinder,
Q_{r}^{-}
, but (
2.5
) follows given our assumption
w_{t}\leq 0
).

Given
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\partial\{w>0\}\cap Q_{1}
, one may define the rescaled solution

w^{x_{0},t_{0},r}(x,t):=r^{-2}w(x_{0}+rx,t_{0}+r^{2}t)

The functions
w^{x_{0},t_{0},r}
are then uniformly bounded solutions to (
1.2
), and the free boundary points may then be classified into two categories as follows:

•

(x_{0},t_{0})
is a
regular point
if there exists
e\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
and a sequence
r_{k}\to 0
such that

(2.6)

\lim_{k\to\infty}w^{x_{0},t_{0},r_{k}}(x,t)=\frac{1}{2}(x\cdot e)_{+}^{2}.

•

(x_{0},t_{0})
is a
singular point
if there exist
m\in[0,1]
and a non-negative
d\times d
matrix
A
such that
\operatorname{tr}(A)=1-m
, and

(2.7)

\lim_{r\to 0+}w^{x_{0},t_{0},r}(x,t)=-mt+\frac{1}{2}Ax\cdot x.

The convergence in (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) happens uniformly on compacta and smoothly on the positivity set of the limiting function.

The next theorem is the standard classification of the free boundary points, and states that the free boundary is a smooth hypersurface moving with finite speed near the regular points (see
[
CPS04
, Rem. 7.2, Thm. 14.1, Thm. 15.1]
,
[
Bla06
, Prop. 1.2]
).

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
w:Q_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded solution to (
1.2
). Then every free boundary point is either regular or singular. The set of regular points is relatively open, and, in some neighborhood
V
of every regular point,
w_{t}
is space-time Hölder continuous in
V
, the free boundary is space-time
C^{\infty}
hypersurface in
V
, and there exists
C>0
such that

|w_{t}|\leq C|\nabla w|\;\;\text{ in }\;\;V.

We emphasize that
w_{t}
is continuous at regular points but, unlike the melting problem, may be discontinuous at singular points.

Remark 2.2
.

Let us clarify the different notions of graphicality and speed we use in the paper.

We say that
x_{0}
is a regular point, i.e.
x_{0}\in\partial\{w>0\}\backslash\Sigma
if around
x_{0}
the free boundary can locally be written as a graph of space and time. Namely, we can write
x=(x^{\prime},x_{d})
and after a rotation we obtain
Q_{r}(x_{0})\cap\partial\{w>0\}=\{(x,t)\mid x_{d}=g(x^{\prime},t)\}
for some
g\in C^{\infty}_{x,t}
.

Another notion of graphicality is the freezing time. Here we use monotonicity to parameterize the entire free boundary as
\partial\{w>0\}=(x,s(x))
. Now the “speed” with which
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\partial\{w>0\}
moves is given by
|\nabla s(x_{0})|^{-1}
(by the inverse function theorem). In this parametrization, the freezing time
s(x)
plays a similar role as the hitting time
T(x)
used to describe the tumor interface in
[
CJK25
]
.

We can relate the two characterizations as follows: if
t-s(x^{\prime},g(x^{\prime},t))=0
then we have that
1-\partial_{d}s\partial_{t}g=0
and that
\partial_{i}s+\partial_{d}s\partial_{i}g=0
. We can see then that if
\nabla s(x)=0
(i.e. we are advancing with infinite speed) it must be that such a
g
cannot exist and so
(x,s(x))\in\Sigma
. In fact, our Theorem
1.2
, shows that this is a rigorous characterization. Note that this does not follow from the implicit function theorem, and in fact is false for the melting problem, see
[
FRS24
]
.

The following result follows from
[
LM15
, Thm. 1.9]
, and gives stratified dimensional estimates for the spatial projection of the singular set.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
w
be a bounded solution to (
1.2
) and, for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
, let
\Sigma_{i}
be the
i^{\operatorname{th}}
stratum of the singular set as defined by (
1.7
). Then

(2.8)

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\pi_{x}(\Sigma_{i}))\leq i

We note that, in the case of the top stratum
\Sigma_{d}
, the bound (
2.8
) is trivial and provides no information.

In summary, the main differences with the analogous properties for the classical melting problem are that:

(i)

w_{t}
may be discontinuous,

(ii)

the nondegeneracy property (
2.5
) does not hold in the same time slice
t=0
, and, crucially,

(iii)

the blow-up profiles at singular points may be time-dependent, with a nonempty stratum
\Sigma_{d}
.

2.2.
Parabolic functionals and monotonicity formulas

We recall in this section some basic algebraic identities and key monotonicity formulas obtained in
[
FRS24
]
. Let
\mathcal{P}
denote the set of parabolically
2
–homogeneous polynomials
p(x,t)
such that

\mathbf{H}p=1,\quad p(x,t)\geq 0,\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(-\infty,0]

We note that the elements of
\mathcal{P}
are precisely those of the form

p(x,t)=-mt+\frac{1}{2}Ax\cdot x,\quad m\in[0,1],\quad A\geq 0,\quad\text{tr}(A)=1-m,

and can be equivalently characterized as the set of possible blow-up limits at singular points for solutions to (
1.2
).
In particular,

Zp=2p\quad\hbox{ for all }p\in\mathcal{P}.

We remark that while most of the formulas below were stated for solutions to the melting problem, they are valid for any non-negative solution to the parabolic obstacle problem. This is because the proofs do not rely on the sign of
w_{t}
, but rather on the following basic identities, which hold for any
p\in\mathcal{P}
and any non-negative function
w
satisfying
\mathbf{H}w=\chi_{\{w>0\}}
:

(2.9)

(w-p)\mathbf{H}(w-p)=p\chi_{\{w=0\}}\geq 0,\quad Z(w-p)\mathbf{H}(w-p)=Zp\chi_{\{w=0\}}=2p\chi_{\{w=0\}}\geq 0.

Recalling that

f_{r}(x,t)=f(rx,r^{2}t),

we have, for
f,g\in C^{1,1}_{x}\cap C^{0,1}_{t}
,

(2.10)

\mathbf{H}(f_{r})=r^{2}(\mathbf{H}f)_{r},\quad Z(f_{r})=(Zf)_{r},\quad\langle f,g\rangle_{r}=\langle f_{r},g_{r}\rangle

\left.\frac{d}{dr}\right|_{r=1}\langle f,1\rangle_{r}=\langle Zf,1\rangle

(2.11)

2\langle\nabla f,\nabla g\rangle=\langle Zf,g\rangle-2\langle\mathbf{H}f,g\rangle=\langle f,Zg\rangle-2\langle f,\mathbf{H}g\rangle.

(2.12)

\frac{d}{dr}H(r,f)=\frac{2}{r}\langle f,Zf\rangle_{r}=\frac{2}{r}\left(D(r,f)+2r^{2}\langle f,\mathbf{H}f\rangle_{r}\right).

(2.13)

\frac{d}{dr}D(r,f)=\frac{1}{r}(2\langle Zf,Zf\rangle_{r}-4r^{2}\langle Zf,\mathbf{H}f\rangle_{r})

For the last two equations recall the definition of the height and energy functionals,
H
and
D
, in (
2.1
). The key fact about the parabolic frequency functions, stated below, are that they are (almost-) monotone in
r
, as long as
f
is “approximately" caloric. Since we mostly work with local solutions, we utilize the spatial cutoff function
\zeta
defined in (
2.3
) to obtain a function defined on all of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

[
FRS24
, Lem. 5.3, Prop. 5.4]
Let
w:Q_{1}\to[0,\infty)
be a bounded solution to (
1.2
), assume
(0,0)
is a singular point, let
p\in\mathcal{P}
, and let
u=\zeta(w-p)
. Then there exists
C>0
such that,

(2.14)

D(r,u)-2H(r,u)\geq-Ce^{-\frac{1}{r}},\quad r\in(0,1)

and, for
\gamma>2

(2.15)

\frac{d}{dr}\phi^{\gamma}(r,u)\geq-Ce^{-\frac{1}{r}},\quad r^{2}\langle u,\mathbf{H}u\rangle_{r}\geq-Ce^{-\frac{1}{r}},\quad r\in(0,1),

where
C
depends only on dimension,
\|w\|_{\infty}
, and (only in the case of (
2.15
))
\gamma.

The next lemma captures the other key fact about the frequency, which is that
\phi(0+,f)
measures the order of vanishing of
f
at zero (again assuming
f
is “approximately caloric”).

Lemma 2.5
.

[
FRS24
, Lem. 5.6, Cor. 5.9]
Let
w:Q_{1}\to[0,\infty)
be a bounded solution to (
1.2
), assume
(0,0)
is a singular point, let
p\in\mathcal{P}
, let
u=\zeta(w-p)
, and let
\phi^{\gamma}(0+,u)=\lambda
. Then, for
R\in(0,1)
,

(2.16)

H(r,u)+r^{2\gamma}\leq C\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2\lambda}(H(R,u)+R^{2\gamma}).

where
C=C(\gamma,\|w\|_{L^{\infty}})
. Assume further that there exists
\delta>0
such that
\phi^{\gamma}(r,u)\leq\lambda+\tfrac{\delta}{2}
for all
r\in(0,R)
. Then

(2.17)

(H(R,u)+R^{2\gamma})\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2\lambda+\delta}\leq C_{\delta}(H(r,u)+r^{2\gamma}),

where
C_{\delta}=C_{\delta}(\gamma,\|w\|_{L^{\infty}},\delta)>0
.

Moreover, if
\phi^{\gamma_{0}}(0^{+},u)<\gamma_{0}
for some
\gamma_{0}>2
, then
\phi(0+,u)
exists and satisfies
\phi^{\gamma}(0+,u)=\lambda
for any
\gamma\geq\gamma_{0}
. In that case, for every
K>1
, there exists
C_{K}=C_{K}(K,d,\gamma_{0},\|w\|_{\infty})>1
such that

(2.18)

\frac{1}{C_{K}}\leq\frac{H(r,u)}{H(\theta r,u)}\leq C_{K},\quad\theta\in[K^{-1},K],\quad r\in(0,1).

For global solutions, we will prove related estimates (but without the cutoff) in Section
8
.

3.
A priori estimates and quantitative nondegeneracy of the free boundary

3.1.
Asymptotic convexity at the free boundary

It is well known that, for the melting problem (i.e. assuming
w_{t}\geq 0
), the function
(D^{2}w)^{-}
vanishes continuously at the free boundary. This was proved in
[
Caf77
]
by showing that
(D^{2}w)^{-}
has the same type of logarithmic modulus of continuity as
w_{t}
. In our case,
w_{t}
is discontinuous and the classical proof breaks down.

Nevertheless, we see below that qualitative asymptotic convexity still holds by a blow-up argument. We observe that the argument works for any non-negative solution of
w_{t}-\Delta w=-\chi_{\{w>0\}}
, i.e. no sign assumption on
w_{t}
is made.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
w:Q_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded solution to (
1.2
), and assume that
(0,0)\in\partial\{w>0\}
. Then one has

\lim_{(x,t)\to(0,0),\;w(x,t)>0}(D^{2}w)^{-}(x,t)=0.

In particular,
(D^{2}w)^{-}
may be extended continuously to
Q_{1}
.

Proof.

We argue by contradiction. Suppose that there exists
e\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
and a sequence
(x_{n},t_{n})\in\{w>0\}
such that
(x_{n},t_{n})\to(0,0)
and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\partial_{ee}w(x_{n},t_{n})=\liminf_{(x,t)\to(0,0),w(x,t)>0}\partial_{ee}w(x,t)=:-c_{0}<0.

For each
n
sufficiently large, let
r_{n}>0
be the largest number such that
Q_{r_{n}}(x_{n},t_{n})\subset\{w>0\}
, and let

u_{n}(x,t):=r_{n}^{-2}w(x_{n}+xr_{n},t_{n}+tr_{n}^{2}).

By taking a subsequence, using the
C^{1,1}_{x}\cap C_{t}^{0}
estimates (
2.4
) and the nondegeneracy property (
2.5
), we may assume that there exists a solution
u
to

(3.1)

\begin{cases}u_{t}-\Delta u=-\chi_{\{u>0\}}\quad\text{ in }\;\;\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(-\infty,0],\\
u\geq 0,\;u_{t}\leq 0,\end{cases}

such that
u_{n}\to u
locally uniformly in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(-\infty,0]
. Since
u_{n}>0
in
Q_{1}^{-}
, we infer that
\mathbf{H}u_{n}=1
in
Q_{1}^{-}
. Thus, by parabolic regularity the convergence of
u_{n}
is locally uniform in
Q_{1}^{-}
in any
C^{k}
norm (and
\mathbf{H}u=1
in
Q_{1}^{-}
). In particular,

\partial_{ee}u(0,0)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\partial_{ee}u_{n}(0,0)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\partial_{ee}w(x_{n},t_{n})=\liminf_{(y,s)\to 0,w(y,s)>0}\partial_{ee}w(y,s)=-c_{0}.

On the other hand, if
(x,t)\in Q_{1}^{-}
, then

\partial_{ee}u(x,t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\partial_{ee}w(x_{n}+xr_{n},t_{n}+tr_{n}^{2})\geq\liminf_{(y,s)\to(0,0),w(y,s)>0}\partial_{ee}w(y,s)=-c_{0}.

By the strong minimum principle of the heat equation, we infer that

\partial_{ee}u(x,t)\equiv-c_{0}<0,\qquad(x,t)\in Q_{1}^{-},

and, by spatial analyticity of caloric functions,
\partial_{ee}u(x,0)\equiv-c_{0}
for all
x
in the connected component of
\{u(\cdot,0)>0\}
containing
(0,0)
.

Consequently, define the
C^{1,1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R})
function
g(s):=u(se,0)
. Since
|\nabla u|,u<\infty
, the constant negative second derivative in the direction
e
implies that the connected component of
\{u(\cdot,0)>0\}
containing
(0,0)
is bounded in the
e
direction. Thus there exists a value
s^{*}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
such that
g^{\prime\prime}\equiv-c_{0}
on
(-|s^{*}|,|s^{*}|)
, and
g(s^{*})=0
(i.e. there exists a maximal open interval in the direction
e
, centered at the origin, contained in
\{u>0\}\cap\{t=0\}
).

Since
u\geq 0
, we also have
g^{\prime}(s^{*})=0
. In summary, one has
g\geq 0
,
g(s^{*})=g^{\prime}(s^{*})=0
and
g^{\prime\prime}<0
in
(-|s^{*}|,|s^{*}|)
, which is a contradiction. This proves that, for any
e\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
,

(3.2)

\lim_{(x,t)\to(0,0),w(x,t)>0}(\partial_{ee}w(x,t))^{-}=0.

From here the estimate on the full Hessian follows from the boundedness of
D^{2}w
and the compactness of
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.
∎

3.2.
Nondegeneracy of the free boundary

Our next goal will be to show that, near any free boundary point, the free boundary may be locally described as
\{t=s(x)\}
, where the freezing time
s
is Lipschitz continuous, and to obtain precise quantitative nondegeneracy near points of
\Sigma_{\operatorname{dyn}}
. We also show the semi-convexity of
w
in time. A similar estimate was essential in the analysis of the melting problem, but our proof is necessarily different due to the discontinuity of
w_{t}
. Instead, we rely strongly on the nondegeneracy of the free boundary.

Formally, if
w(x,s(x))\equiv 0
then
\nabla_{x}w+\partial_{t}w\nabla s(x)=0
. As such a bound on a the ratio
|\nabla_{x}w|/|\partial_{t}w|
gives an upper bound on
\nabla s(x)
, which, by the inverse function theorem, is equivalent to a lower bound on the speed of propagation of the free boundary.

In this vein, we begin by obtaining an
a priori
lower bound for the speed of propagation of the boundary and a one-sided estimate for
D^{2}w
. At free boundary points where
w_{t}
does not vanish continuously, we obtain a non-degeneracy estimate on the speed of the free boundary. This will be a crucial estimate when we prove, in Section
5
, that
s
is
C^{1}
at singular points of
\Sigma_{\operatorname{dyn}}
.

Here we strongly use the condition
\{w_{t}<0\}=\{w>0\}
given in (
1.2
), a property inherited from the supercooled Stefan problem.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
r>0
, let
w:Q_{r}\to\mathbb{R}
be a solution to (
1.2
), let
\eta=-w_{t}
, and assume that
Q_{r}\subset B_{1}\times(-1,1)
. There exists a constant
C>0
such that

(3.3)

|\nabla w(x,t)|+|(D^{2}w)^{-}(x,t)|\leq C\eta(x,t),\quad(x,t)\in Q_{r/2}.

In addition, for any
m>0
, there exist constants
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{10})
and
K>1
, depending only on
d
,
m
, and
\|w\|_{\infty}
, but not on
r
, such that the following holds. Assume that
w(0,0)=0
and

(3.4)

\eta(x,t)\geq\frac{m}{2},\quad(x,t)\in B_{r}\times(-r^{2},-(\delta r)^{2}).

Then

(3.5)

|\nabla w(x,t)|\leq Kr\eta(x,t),\quad(x,t)\in Q_{r\delta}.

Proof.

We fix
(x_{1},t_{1})\in Q_{r/2}
and let
D=Q_{r/4}^{-}(x_{1},t_{1})
. Fix
e\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, let
c_{1},c_{11},c_{2}\in(0,1)
be small constants, to be chosen later, and let

(3.6)

z(x,t)=\eta(x,t)-c_{1}\partial_{e}w(x,t)-c_{11}(\partial_{ee}w(x,t))^{-}+c_{2}\left(\frac{1}{2d+1}(|x-x_{1}|^{2}+(t_{1}-t))-w(x,t)\right).

Note that
z
is supercaloric on
D\cap\{w>0\}
and, since
w
,
\nabla w
and
(D^{2}w)^{-}
are continuous, satisfies

(3.7)

\liminf_{(x,t)\in D,\text{dist}((x,t),\partial\{w>0\})\to 0}z(x,t)\geq 0.

Moreover, requiring
(c_{1}+c_{11})/c_{2}
small enough, we may fix
\rho>0
small enough (depending on
r
and the
C^{1,1}_{x}\cap C^{0,1}_{t}
norm of
w
, but not on
c_{1}
,
c_{11}
,
c_{2}
, or
(x_{1},t_{1})
), such that

(3.8)

z\geq-C(c_{1}\rho+c_{11})-c_{2}C\rho^{2}+c_{2}\frac{r^{2}}{16(2d+1)}>0\;\;\text{ on }\;\;\partial_{p}D\cap(\{w=0\}+Q_{\rho}).

Here we use the notation
\{w=0\}+Q_{\rho}
to mean the sum of these two sets, i.e. the set of all points which are contained in a cylinder of radius
\rho
centered at a point in the zero set of
w
. On the other hand, if
(x,t)\in D\backslash(\{w=0\}+Q_{\rho})
, since
\eta
is smooth in
\{w>0\}
, from the condition
\{w>0\}=\{\eta>0\}
in (
1.2
), we have
\eta(x,t)\geq\varepsilon
for some
\varepsilon>0
that depends on
\rho
and
w
, but not on
(x_{1},t_{1})
. We therefore have

(3.9)

z(x,t)\geq\varepsilon-C(c_{1}+c_{11}+c_{2})>0,\quad(x,t)\in D\backslash(\{w=0\}+Q_{\rho}).

if
c_{1}
,
c_{11}
and
c_{2}
are chosen sufficiently small, depending on
\varepsilon
and the
C^{1,1}_{x}
norm of

4.
Stationary blow-up profiles, nucleation, and second blow-up

The goal of this section is to analyze the set
\Sigma_{\operatorname{stat}}
of singular free boundary points
(x_{0},t_{0})
with a time-independent blow-up profile. In particular, we will show that the bottom and top strata,
\Sigma_{\operatorname{stat},0}
and
\Sigma_{\operatorname{stat},d-1}
, are both empty, where we denote

\Sigma_{\operatorname{stat},k}:=\Sigma_{k}\cap\Sigma_{\operatorname{stat}},\quad 0\leq k\leq d-1.

To rule out the bottom stratum, an important step of independent interest is to show that any nucleation event occurs at a singular point with a time-dependent blow-up profile. For ruling out
\Sigma_{\operatorname{stat},d-1}
we characterize the second blow-up profile
q
at a given point in the set to lead to a contradiction. It is crucial here to show that the degree of
q
is at most two, in contrast to what is known for the melting problem.

4.1.

We begin by characterizing the points where the temperature variable is continuous, which is of independent interest.

(Points of continuous temperature)
.

Let
w:Q_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded solution to (
1.2
), and assume that
(0,0)\in\partial\{w>0\}
. Then
\eta=-w_{t}
is continuous at
(0,0)
if and only if
(0,0)
is a regular point or
(0,0)\in\Sigma_{\operatorname{stat}}
.

Proof.

Assume first that
(0,0)\in\Sigma_{\operatorname{dyn}}
is a singular point with a time-dependent blow-up profile

p(x,t)=-mt+\frac{1}{2}Ax\cdot x,\quad m\in(0,1].

In view of Proposition
3.4
,
\eta_{t}
is bounded above, and thus
\lim_{t\uparrow 0}\eta(0,t)
exists. We then have

0=\lim_{t\uparrow 0}\frac{w(0,t)-p(0,t)}{t}=\lim_{t\uparrow 0}(-\eta(0,t)+m),

where the first equality is by the definition of blow-up. Thus

\lim_{t\uparrow 0}\eta(0,t)=m\neq 0=\eta(0,0),

so
\eta
is discontinuous at
(0,0)
.

Conversely, assume that
(0,0)\notin\Sigma_{\operatorname{dyn}}
. If
(0,0)
is a regular point, then
\eta
is continuous at
(0,0)
by Theorem
2.1
, so we may assume that
(0,0)\in\Sigma_{\operatorname{stat}}
has a stationary blow-up profile
p(x)
. Let
r\in(0,1/4)
, and let
u:Q_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
be as in (
3.14
). Since
p
is stationary, we have
u_{t}=w_{t}\leq 0
. Lemma
3.5
yields

\|u_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}=\|u_{t}^{-}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{2}(Q_{2})}.

By the definition of blow-up,
u\to 0
uniformly in
Q_{2}
as
r\to 0
. We therefore have
u_{t}\to 0
uniformly in
Q_{1}
as
r\to 0
. Translating this statement back to
w
, and using once more the fact that
p
is stationary, we infer that

\eta=-\partial_{t}w\to 0\;\;\text{ as }\;\;|x|+|t|^{\frac{1}{2}}\to 0,

which proves that
\eta
is continuous at
(0,0)
.
∎

A distinct feature of the supercooled Stefan problem that is not present in the melting problem, is that solutions may exhibit spontaneous
nucleation
away from the boundary (see
[
CKM25
]
for examples). This notion is made precise by the following definition.

Definition 4.2

(Nucleation)
.

Let
w:Q_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded solution to (
1.2
), and assume that
(0,0)\in\partial\{w>0\}
. We say that
(0,0)
is a
nucleation point
of
w
if there exists
r>0
such that

(\overline{B_{r}}\times(-r^{2},0])\cap\{w=0\}\subset B_{r}\times\{0\}.

We begin by showing that when nucleation happens, it must occur at singular points where
\eta=-w_{t}
is discontinuous and thus, by the above, the blow-up profile is time-dependent: namely, once nucleation happens, freezing near the point occurs faster than parabolically. This description of nucleation is of independent interest.

Proposition 4.3

(Discontinuity at nucleation points)
.

Let
w:Q_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded solution to (
1.2
), and assume that
(0,0)
is a nucleation point of
w
. Then
\eta=-w_{t}
is discontinuous at
(0,0)
. In particular,
(0,0)\in\Sigma_{\operatorname{dyn}}
.

Proof.

Since
(0,0)
is a nucleation point, there exists
r>0
such that
w>0
in
Q_{r}^{-}\backslash\{t=0\}
. Thus
\mathbf{H}w=1
in
Q_{r}^{-}
, and by parabolic regularity
\eta
has a smooth, caloric extension to
Q_{r}^{-}
. If
\eta\geq 0
were to be continuous at
(0,0)
, then the fact that
\eta(0,0)=0
would contradict the strong minimum principle of the heat equation. Therefore,
\eta
is discontinuous at
(0,0)
, and, by Proposition
4.1
, we have
(0,0)\in\Sigma_{\operatorname{dyn}}
.
∎

This observation, plus monotonicity, implies that if
p
is a blow-up with
\ker(D^{2}p)=\{0\}
then
p
must be time dependent.

Proposition 4.4

(Bottom stratum of stationary singular profiles)
.

Let
w:Q_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded solution to (
1.2
). Then

\Sigma_{\operatorname{stat},0}=\emptyset.

Proof.

Assume, by contradiction, that
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\Sigma_{\operatorname{stat},0}
for some
(x_{0},t_{0})\in Q_{1}
. After a change of coordinates, we may assume that
(x_{0},t_{0})=(0,0)
. Let
p(x)
be the blow-up profile of
w
at
(0,0)
. By the definition of blow-up, we have

w(x,0)\geq p(x)+o(|x|^{2})\;\text{ as }\;x\to 0.

Since
\ker(D^{2}p_{2})=\{0\}
it follows that, for some
\lambda\in(0,1)
, and
r>0
sufficiently small,

w(x,0)\geq\lambda|x|^{2}+o(|x|^{2})\geq\frac{\lambda}{2}|x|^{2}>0,\quad x\in B_{r}\backslash\{0\}.

Therefore, recalling that
w_{t}\leq 0
, we see either that
(0,0)
is a nucleation point (recall Definition
4.2
) or that
\{w=0\}\cap Q_{r}^{-}=\{(0,t)\mid 0\geq t\geq\epsilon\}
for some
\epsilon\geq-r^{2}
. The former situation contradicts Proposition
4.3
, whereas the latter situation contradicts the Lipschitz regularity of the freezing time (Proposition
3.3
).

∎

4.2.
Second blow-up and the top stationary stratum

Now we turn to our proof that
\Sigma_{\operatorname{stat},d-1}
is empty. We recall that this is a crucial step in our larger argument, and that the points in
\Sigma_{\operatorname{stat}}
cause the most difficulty in
[
FRS24
]
.

Our strategy is to characterize the second blow-up profiles at points in this set. Following the ideas in the melting problem
[
FRS24
]
, we first show an interesting pointwise estimate, which says that at such a point,
\eta=-w_{t}
grows faster than linear rate in all but one direction. Then we will use this to show that the second blow-ups are at most cubic in
\Sigma_{\operatorname{stat},d-1}
. The rest of the argument, which is unique to the supercooled problem, shows that the cubic blow-ups are not possible.

Lemma 4.5

(Nondegeneracy away from the thin set)
.

Let
w:Q_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded solution to (
1.2
), let
\eta=-w_{t}
, and assume that
(0,0)\in\Sigma_{\operatorname{stat},d-1}
has the blow-up profile
p_{2}(x)=\frac{1}{2}x_{d}^{2}
. Then there is a constant
c_{0}>0
such that the following holds: for every
\delta>0
, there is
r_{0}=r_{0}(\delta)\in(0,1)
such that

\eta(x,t)\geq c_{0}|x_{d}|,\quad(x,t)\in Q_{r}^{-},\quad|x_{d}|\geq\delta r\quad\hbox{ for all }r\leq r_{0}.

Proof.

By definition of blow-up, we have

w(x,t)=\frac{1}{2}x_{d}^{2}+o(|x|^{2}+|t|),\quad(x,t)\in Q_{1}^{-}.

Thus, in particular, there exists
r_{0}\in(0,1)
such that

Q_{2r}^{-}\cap\{|x_{d}|\geq r\delta/2\}\subset\{w>0\},\quad r\leq r_{0}.

We fix
(x,t)\in Q_{r}^{-}
such that
|x_{d}|\geq\delta r
. Letting
\rho:=|x_{d}|/4
, we then have

Q^{-}_{\rho}(x,t)\subset\{w>0\}\cap Q_{2r}^{-}.

Thus, by the nondegeneracy property (
2.5
) and the mean value theorem, there exists
(y,s)\in Q_{\rho}^{-}(x,t)
such that

\rho|\nabla w(y,s)|+\rho^{2}|w_{t}(y,s)|\geq c\rho^{2}.

Here
c
is a small positive constant that may decrease in the steps below, but is independent of
\rho,

\delta
, and
r_{0}
. Lemma
3.2
then implies that

\sup_{Q_{\rho}^{-}(x,t)}\eta\geq c\rho.

By Proposition
3.4
,
\eta_{t}
is bounded above, and thus, provided that
r_{0}
is sufficiently small, we infer that

\sup_{y\in B_{\rho}(x)}\eta(y,t-\rho^{2})\geq c\rho.

Finally, the Harnack inequality applied to the cylinder
Q_{\rho}^{-}(x,t)
yields

\eta(x,t)\geq c\rho=\frac{c}{4}|x_{d}|.

∎

The next proposition shows the existence of, subsequential, second blow-ups, which will also be used later in Section 6. However, part (c) in particular characterizes the second blow-up profiles at
\Sigma_{\operatorname{stat},d-1}
, crucially restricting its degree to
(2,3]
, with the help of Lemma
4.5
.

It will be helpful to recall from (
2.10
) the scaling identities

H(\rho,f_{r})=H(\rho r,f),\quad f_{r}(x,t)=f(rx,r^{2}t),\quad\rho,r>0.

(Second blow-up at singular points with finite frequency)

(a)

(b)

(c)

\lambda=3
,

in

,

is a parabolically
\lambda
–homogeneous solution to the parabolic thin obstacle problem

(4.3)

\begin{cases}\mathbf{H}q\leq 0,\;q\mathbf{H}q=0&\text{ in }\;\;\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(-\infty,0)\\

Proof.

By Lemmas
2.5
,
3.6
, and
3.5
,

(4.4)

\|u^{(r_{k})}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{R}^{-})}+R^{2}\|(u^{(r_{k})})_{t}^{-}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q^{-}_{R})}+\left(\fint_{Q_{R}^{-}}R^{2}|\nabla u^{(r_{k})}|^{2}+R^{2}|\partial_{t}u^{(r_{k})}|+R^{2}|\mathbf{H}u^{(r_{k})}|\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{\delta,\gamma}R^{\lambda+\delta},

for

and

. Thus
[
Dro
, Thm. 2.4.2]
, up to a subsequence (which is not relabeled for simplicity), there exists

such that

u^{(r_{k})}\to q\;\text{ in }\;L^{2}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(-\infty,0)).

By the definition of blow-up,
r^{-2}w_{r}\to p_{2}
locally uniformly in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(-\infty,0]
. Therefore, since
\mathbf{H}u^{(r_{k})}=0
on
\{w_{r_{k}}>0\}
, we infer that

is caloric in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(-\infty,0)\setminus\{p_{2}=0\}
.

Assume first that
(0,0)\notin\Sigma_{\operatorname{stat},d-1}
. Then the set
\{p_{2}=0\}\cap\{-\infty<t<0\}
has zero capacity, which implies that

is caloric.

The proof of part (b), that is, the fact that
\lambda=2
when

follows exactly as in
[
FRS24
, Lem. 5.8]

We turn to the proof of part (a), and in particular assume that
\lambda<\gamma
. In view of (

), Lemma

, and the fact that
\lambda<\gamma
, we have for

In the second to last inequality, we used the fact that, by Lemma

,
r^{2\gamma}\ll H(r,\zeta(w-p_{2}))
for

.
Note also that,

(4.6)

H(1,q)=\lim_{k\to\infty}H(1,\zeta_{r_{k}}u^{(r_{k})})=\lim_{k\to\infty}1=1.

On the other hand,

\phi(r,\zeta_{r_{k}}u^{(r_{k})})=\frac{D(r,\zeta_{r_{k}}u^{(r_{k})})}{H(r,\zeta_{r_{k}}u^{(r_{k})})}=\frac{D(r,(\zeta(w-p_{2}))_{r_{k}})}{H(r,(\zeta(w-p_{2}))_{r_{k}})}=\frac{D(rr_{k},\zeta(w-p_{2}))}{H(rr_{k},\zeta(w-p_{2}))}=\phi(rr_{k},\zeta(w-p_{2}))

(see
[
Po96
, Page 525]
). Using the strong

and the estimate (
4.5
),we see that

and thus we have that

and that

is a parabolically
\lambda
-homogeneous caloric polynomial.

Let us now return to a more general situation, that is we assume that
p_{2}
is the blowup at
(0,0)
but make no assumption on what

is except that we will assume there exists a

.

Most relevantly this factor,

, is integrable at zero so for any

\ \rho^{-4}H\!\left(1,(U^{p})_{\rho}\right)\;\leq\;r^{-4}H\!\left(1,(U^{p})_{r}\right)\;+\;\Psi(r),

where

Using that

and the almost-monotonicity described above we get

\displaystyle\int_{\{t=-1\}}(p_{2}-p)^{2}G

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{t=-1\}}r_{k}^{-4}\bigl(\zeta_{r_{k}}(H_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}u^{(r_{k})}+r_{k}^{2}(p_{2}-p))\bigr)^{2}\,G\;+\;\Psi(r_{k})

Divide by

. We note that

To finish the proof of part (a), assume again that

Lemma 4.7

By Lemma

, for any fixed
(x,t)\in Q_{1}^{-}
with
x_{d}\neq 0
and sufficiently small

, we have

\partial_{t}(w-p_{2})_{r}\leq-cr^{3}|x_{d}|.

Thus, dividing by
H^{\frac{1}{2}}
, using (
2.16
), and letting

, the claim follows (recall that, away from
|x_{d}|=0
, we have
w_{r}>0
for
r>0
small enough and thus the convergence to the second blow-up is smooth).
∎

Our next lemma is an algebraic observation, that odd reflections of solutions to the Signorini problem must be caloric.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
q
be a locally Lipschitz continuous solution to the Signorini problem (
4.3
), where
p_{2}=\frac{1}{2}x_{d}^{2}
. If
q\equiv 0
on
\{x_{d}=0\}
, then

(4.14)

\tilde{q}(x^{\prime},x_{d},t):=\begin{cases}q(x^{\prime},x_{d},t),\qquad x_{d}>0\\
-q(x^{\prime},-x_{d},t),\qquad x_{d}\leq 0.\end{cases}

is a caloric function.

Proof.

Since
q
solves the Signorini problem,
\nabla q
is locally Hölder continuous in
\overline{\{x_{d}>0\}}

[
DGPT17
, Lem. 3.3]
, and thus
\tilde{q}
and
\nabla\tilde{q}
are continuous across the thin set
\{x_{d}=0\}
. The fact that
\tilde{q}
is caloric then follows from integration by parts.
∎

Finally, we are able to rule out the existence of degree-three homogeneous second blow-ups.

Lemma 4.9
.

There exists no
3
–homogeneous solution to (
4.3
) satisfying the condition (
4.13
).

Proof.

By
[
FRS24
, Lemma 9.2]
(which does not use the sign of
\partial_{t}q
), we have that
q\equiv 0
on
\{x_{d}=0\}
for any
3
–homogeneous solution to the Signorini problem (
4.3
). Using Lemma
4.8
, since
\tilde{q}
is 3–homogeneous, it must equal a cubic caloric polynomial on each side of
\{x_{d}=0\}
. Namely, there exist constants
a^{L},a^{R},b^{L},b^{R}
and matrices
A^{L},A^{R}
such that

\displaystyle q|_{\{x_{d}<0\}}=

\displaystyle x_{d}(a^{L}x_{d}^{2}+b^{L}t+x^{\prime}A^{L}x^{\prime}),

\displaystyle q|_{\{x_{d}>0\}}=

\displaystyle x_{d}(a^{R}x_{d}^{2}+b^{R}t+x^{\prime}A^{R}x^{\prime}).

Additionally, since
\Delta q-\partial_{t}q\leq 0
, integration by parts yields

\partial^{+}_{n}q|_{\{x_{d}=0\}}-\partial^{-}_{n}q|_{\{x_{d}=0\}}\leq 0,

that is,

(b^{R}-b^{L})t\leq 0.

Since
t<0
, we infer that
b^{R}\geq b^{L}
. But Lemma
4.7
implies that
b^{L}>0
and
b^{R}<0
, which is a contradiction.

∎

We may now rule out the top stationary stratum altogether.

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
w:Q_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded solution to (
1.2
), and let
\Sigma
be its set of singular points. Then

\Sigma_{\operatorname{stat},d-1}=\emptyset.

Proof.

Assume, by contradiction (and up to translation and rescaling), that
(0,0)\in\Sigma_{\operatorname{stat},d-1}
, and let
q
be as in Proposition
4.6
. Such

must satisfy the nondegeneracy condition (
4.13
), which is a contradiction to Lemma
4.9
.
∎

4.3.
The intermediate stationary strata

Having shown that the top and bottom strata of the set

Lemma 4.11

Let

For the last estimate of this section, we will require the following barrier construction from

Lemma 4.14

Let

Lemma 4.15
.

Let

) with

|\nabla w(x,t)|\leq r^{1-\varepsilon}\eta(x,t),\quad(x,t)\in Q_{r},\quad 0<r<r_{0}.

Proof.

Assume that
(0,0)\in\Sigma_{\operatorname{stat}}
, and let
p_{2}
be the blow-up profile for
w
. By Theorem
4.10
,

satisfies the assumptions of Lemma
4.14

\varepsilon>0
, and let

\alpha\in(0,\varepsilon/2)

Observing that it suffices to prove (

Note that, by uniform convergence, since

We thus have, for any

In this section, we begin the study of the set

6.
Quadratic second blow-up in the top stratum: the set
\Sigma_{d}^{2}

For the remaining sections, we will focus exclusively on the top stratum
\Sigma_{d}\subset\Sigma_{\operatorname{dyn}}
of singular points with blow-up profile
p_{2}(x,t)=-t
. A key feature of singular points with a time-dependent blow-up profile is that
w-p_{2}
may have infinite frequency. For each integer
k\geq 2
, we define the points of frequency
k
by

\Sigma_{d}^{k}:=\{(x_{0},t_{0})\in\Sigma_{d}:\phi^{\gamma}(0+,\zeta(w(x_{0}+\cdot,t_{0}+\cdot)+t))=k\;\;\text{for some $\gamma>k$}\}.

By Lemma
2.5
, the sets
\Sigma_{d}^{k}
are disjoint, and one has
\phi(0+,\zeta(w(x_{0}+\cdot,t_{0}+\cdot)+t))=k
for any
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\Sigma_{d}^{k}
. Letting

(6.1)

\Sigma_{d}^{\geq k}:=\{(x_{0},t_{0})\in\Sigma_{d}:\phi^{k}(0+,\zeta(w(x_{0}+\cdot,t_{0}+\cdot)+t))=k\},

(equivalently,
\phi^{\gamma}(0+)=\gamma
for all
\gamma\in(2,k]
) we then define the points of infinite frequency by

\Sigma_{d}^{\infty}:=\bigcap_{k=2}^{\infty}\Sigma^{\geq k}_{d}.

By Proposition
4.6
, one then has the disjoint decomposition

\Sigma_{d}=\left(\dot{\bigcup}_{k=2}^{\infty}\Sigma^{k}_{d}\right)\dot{\cup}\Sigma_{d}^{\infty}.

In this section, we show that the set
\Sigma_{d}^{2}
of points with frequency
\lambda=2
, where one expects to have a quadratic second blow-up

is dimensionally no larger than the penultimate stratum
\Sigma_{d-1}
. That is, we obtain the estimate

\dim_{\operatorname{par}}(\Sigma_{d}^{2})\leq d-1,

thereby proving that jump-type phenomena does not occur in
\Sigma_{d}^{2}
.
The main difficulty in analyzing these points is the slow rate of convergence of
r^{-2}w_{r}(x,t)\xrightarrow{r\downarrow 0}-t
(slower than any power
r^{\alpha}
, see Lemma
2.5
). In particular, the second blow-up obtained in Proposition
4.6
may be only subsequential, so that the caloric polynomial
q
may not be unique.

We begin with a simple lemma which expresses a unique feature of the supercooled problem at its top stratum, which is that superquadratic cleaning occurs both forward and backward in time. The faster-than-parabolic backward cleaning simply follows from the fact that the blow-up profile is time-dependent (see Lemma
5.1
). The forward cleaning, on the other hand, is explained by the interaction between obstacle problem non-degeneracy, the fact that
p_{2}(x,0)\equiv 0
, and the fact that
w
decreases in time (see (
6.4
) below. The fact that
w_{t}\leq 0
is already implicit in (
2.5
)).

Lemma 6.1

(Two-sided superquadratic cleaning)
.

Let
w:Q_{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded solution to (
1.2
), and for
c_{d}\in(0,1)
as given in (
2.5
), let
\delta\in(0,\frac{c_{d}}{10})
. Suppose that
(0,0)\in\Sigma_{d}
and that for some
r\in(0,1)

(6.2)

\|w-(-t)\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{2r}^{-})}\leq\delta r^{2}.

Then

(6.3)

B_{r}\times[-r^{2},-c_{d}^{-1}\delta r^{2}]\subset\{w>0\},\quad B_{r}\times[c_{d}^{-1}\delta r^{2},r^{2}]\subset\{w=0\}.

Proof.

If
(x_{1},t_{1})\in B_{r}\times[-r^{2},-c_{d}^{-1}\delta r^{2}]
, then, by (
6.2
),

w(x_{1},t_{1})\geq(-t_{1})-\|w-(-t)\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{2r}^{-})}\geq(-t_{1})-\delta r^{2}\geq c_{d}^{-1}(1-c_{d})\delta r^{2}>0.

On the other hand, if
w(x_{1},t_{1})>0
for some
(x_{1},t_{1})\in B_{r}\times[0,r^{2}]
, then applying (
2.5
) (at scale
\sqrt{t}
), we infer that

(6.4)

c_{d}t_{1}\leq\sup_{B_{\sqrt{t_{1}}}(x)}w(\cdot,0)\leq\|w+t\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{2r}^{-})}\leq\delta r^{2}\implies t_{1}\leq c_{d}^{-1}\delta r^{2}.

∎

We now show that, even if
q
is not unique, the function
w
qualitatively inherits, away from
t=0
, some of the concave behavior of its subsequential limits. This appears as a sign rigidity at small scales, albeit with a possibly scale-dependent direction of “concavity”.

Lemma 6.2

(Sign rigidity of
\Sigma_{d}^{2}
)
.

Let
w:Q_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded solution of (
1.2
) with
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\Sigma_{d}^{2}\cap Q_{1}
, and let
\delta>0
. Then there exists
n=n(x_{0},t_{0},\delta)\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for every
r\in(0,1/n)
such that
Q_{2r}(x_{0},t_{0})\subset Q_{1}
,

(6.5)

\|w-(t_{0}-t)\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{2r}^{-}(x_{0},t_{0}))}\leq c_{d}\frac{\delta r^{2}}{10}\quad\hbox{ and }

(6.6)

w(x_{0},t_{0}+r^{2}t)>-r^{2}t,\quad t\in[-1,0),

where
c_{d}\in(0,1)
is the dimensional constant in (
2.5
). Furthermore, there exists
e=e(r)\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
such that

(6.7)

w(x_{0}+rx,t_{0}+r^{2}t)<-r^{2}t,\quad(x,t)\in\overline{B_{1}}\times[-1,-\delta/2],\quad|x\cdot e|^{2}\geq 4d|t|.

Proof.

The fact that (
6.5
) holds for sufficiently large
n
follows simply from the definition of blow-up. We note next that showing (
6.6
) for all
t\in[-1,0)
and all sufficiently small scales
r
is equivalent to showing that, for
r
sufficiently small,

(6.8)

w(x_{0},t_{0}-r^{2})>r^{2}.

Assume, by contradiction, that there exists a sequence
r_{k}\to 0
such that for each
k
, either (
6.8
) fails to hold for
r=r_{k}
, or (
6.7
) fails to hold for
r=r_{k}
and every
e\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. After translating
(x_{0},t_{0})
to the origin, let
p_{2}=-t
and let
u^{(r)}
be as in Proposition
4.6
. Up to a subsequence, we may assume that
u^{(r_{k})}\to q
in
L^{2}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(-\infty,0])
for some caloric polynomial satisfying (
4.1
) and (
4.2
).
By Lemma
5.1
, we have, for
k
sufficiently large,

B_{2r}(x_{0})\times[t_{0}-(2r)^{2},t_{0}-(\delta/10)r^{2}]\subset\{\eta>0\}=\{w>0\},

and
u^{(r_{k})}
is caloric in
B_{2}\times[-4,-\delta/10]
. Since
u^{(r_{k})}\to q
in
L^{2}_{\text{loc}}
, parabolic regularity implies that
u^{(r_{k})}\to q
uniformly in
B_{1}\times[-1,-\delta/2]
. Recalling, from (
4.1
), that we can write
q(x,t)=-at-\frac{1}{2}Ax\cdot x
, let
\lambda_{\max}
be the largest eigenvalue of
A
and
e\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
be a corresponding eigenvector.
Then, for
|x\cdot e|^{2}\geq 4d|t|
and
t\in(-1,-\delta/2]
, using the inequality
\lambda_{\max}\geq\frac{1}{d}\operatorname{tr}(A)=\frac{a}{d}
, we get from (
4.2
) that

q(x,t)=-at-\frac{1}{2}Ax\cdot x\leq a|t|-\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{\max}(x\cdot e)^{2}\leq a|t|-\frac{1}{2d}a(x\cdot e)^{2}\leq-a|t|\leq-\frac{1}{2}a\delta<0.

Hence, by uniform convergence, if
k
is sufficiently large,
u^{(r_{k})}(0,-1)>0
and
u^{(r_{k})}(x,t)<0
for
(x,t)\in\overline{B_{1}}\times[-1,-\delta/2]
. But this says, respectively, for
r=r_{k}
, that (
6.8
) holds and (
6.7
) holds for the particular eigenvector
e
, which contradicts the assumption about the sequence
\{r_{k}\}
.
∎

We note that the slack condition
\delta>0
in (
6.7
) is inevitable due to the slow convergence near
t=0
. Indeed, if one could take
\delta=0
, then (
6.7
) would trivially imply that
\Sigma_{d}^{2}=\emptyset
, but this is false in general (see Proposition
9.4
).

Next, we use the rigidity result of Lemma
6.2
and the forward cleaning of Lemma
6.1
to show that, after stratifying
\Sigma_{d}^{2}
at countably many scales, one can obtain a one-sided-in-time Reifenberg–type condition.

Lemma 6.3

(Countable slab trapping for
\Sigma_{d}^{2}
)
.

Let
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, and, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, under the notation of Lemma
6.2
, let

(6.9)

S_{n}=\{(x_{0},t_{0})\in\Sigma_{d}^{2}\cap\overline{Q_{1-1/n}}:n(x_{0},t_{0},\delta)\leq n\}.

Then, for every
(x_{1},t_{1})\in S_{n}
and every
r\in(0,\frac{1}{n})
, there exists
e=e(r,x_{1},t_{1})\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
such that

S_{n}\cap(B_{r}(x_{1})\times[t_{1},1])\subset\{(x,t):|(x-x_{1})\cdot e|\leq 2(d\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}r\}.

Proof.

For
(x_{1},t_{1})\in S_{n}
and
0<r<\frac{1}{n}
, let
e=e(r,x_{1},t_{1})\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
be a vector such that (
6.7
) holds with
x_{0}:=x_{1}
. Assume, by contradiction, that
(x_{2},t_{2})\in S_{n}\cap(B_{r}(x_{1})\times[t_{1},1])
and
|(x_{2}-x_{1})\cdot e|>2(d\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}r
. Then, by (
6.5
), applying Lemma
6.1
with
\delta:=\frac{c_{d}\delta}{10}
, we have

B_{r}(x_{1})\times[t_{1}+(\delta/10)r^{2},t_{1}+r^{2}]\subset\{w=0\}.

Thus, since
w_{t}\leq 0
and
(x_{2},t_{2})\in\partial\{w>0\}
, we must have

t_{2}\in[t_{1},t_{1}+\delta r^{2}/10].

Taking
(x_{0},t_{0}):=(x_{1},t_{1})
,
x:=(x_{2}-x_{1})/r
, and
t:=-\delta+(t_{2}-t_{1})/r^{2}\in[-1,-\delta/2]
in (
6.7
), we obtain

w(x_{2},t_{2}-r^{2}\delta)<r^{2}\delta-(t_{2}-t_{1})\leq r^{2}\delta.

But taking
(x_{0},t_{0}):=(x_{2},t_{2})
and
t=-\delta
in (
6.6
), we obtain

w(x_{2},t_{2}-r^{2}\delta)>r^{2}\delta,

a contradiction.
∎

We may now use the “future only” Reifenberg-type condition to obtain a local (non-centered) slab-trapping result for the entire spatial projection of
\Sigma_{d}^{2}
.

Lemma 6.4

(Countable slab trapping for
\pi_{x}(\Sigma_{d}^{2})
)
.

Let
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and let
S_{n}
be given by (
6.9
). Then, for every
x_{0}\in\pi_{x}(S_{n})
, and every
r\in(0,\frac{1}{2n})
, there exist
x_{*}\in\overline{B_{r}(x_{0})}
and
e=e(r,x_{0})\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
such that

(6.10)

\pi_{x}(S_{n})\cap B_{r}(x_{0})\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|(x-x_{*})\cdot e|\leq 4(d\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}r\}.

Proof.

Let
x_{0}\in S_{n}
and
r\in(0,\frac{1}{2n})
be fixed throughout the proof, and consider the quantity

(6.11)

t_{*}=\operatorname{inf}_{(x,t)\in S_{n}\cap(\overline{B_{r}}(x_{0})\times(-1,1))}t.

Assume first that (
6.11
) is attained at
(x_{*},t_{*})\in S_{n}
. Then by Lemma
6.3
there exists
e\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
such that

S_{n}\cap(B_{2r}(x_{*})\times[t_{*},1))\subset\{(x,t):|(x-x_{*})\cdot e|\leq 4(d\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}r\}.

By the minimality of
t_{*}
, we have

(6.12)

S_{n}\cap(B_{r}(x_{0})\times(-1,t_{*}))=\emptyset.

On the other hand, since
B_{r}(x_{0})\subset B_{2r}(x_{*})
, we deduce

S_{n}\cap(B_{r}(x_{0})\times(-1,1))=S_{n}\cap(B_{r}(x_{0})\times[t_{*},1))\subset S_{n}\cap(B_{2r}(x_{*})\times[t_{*},1))\subset\{x:|(x-x_{*})\cdot e|\leq 4(d\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}r\},

which is precisely (
6.10
).

Now, assume instead that the infimum (
6.11
) is not attained, and let
\{(x_{k},t_{k})\}\in S_{n}\cap(\overline{B_{r}}\times(-1,1))
be a minimizing sequence. Up to extracting a subsequence, and recalling (
6.9
), we may assume that

(x_{k},t_{k})\xrightarrow{k\to\infty}(x_{*},t_{*})\in\overline{B_{r}}(x_{0})\times\left[-(1-1/n)^{2},(1-1/n)^{2}\right].

By Lemma
6.3
, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
e_{k}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
such that

S_{n}\cap(B_{2r}(x_{k})\times[t_{k},1))\subset\{(x,t):|(x-x_{k})\cdot e_{k}|\leq 4(d\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}r\}.

Up to extracting a further subsequence, we may assume that
e_{k}\to e
for some
e\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. Letting
k\to\infty
, it follows that

S_{n}\cap(B_{2r}(x_{*})\times(t_{*},1))\subset\{(x,t):|(x-x_{*})\cdot e|\leq 4(d\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}r\}.

Moreover, since the infimum (
6.11
) is not attained, we must have

S_{n}\cap(B_{r}(x_{0})\times\{t_{*}\})=\emptyset,

and by the infimality of
t_{*}
, we again have (
6.12
). Therefore, we conclude similarly to the first case, that

S_{n}\cap(B_{r}(x_{0})\times(-1,1))=S_{n}\cap(B_{r}(x_{0})\times(t_{*},1))\subset S_{n}\cap(B_{2r}(x_{*})\times(t_{*},1))\subset\{x:|(x-x_{*})\cdot e|\leq 4(d\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}r\}.

∎

We now recall some basic facts about porosity and Hausdorff dimension.

Definition 6.5
.

Given
\varepsilon>0
we say that
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
(1-\varepsilon)
–porous
if, for every
x\in S
, there exists
r_{0}>0
such that, for every
r<r_{0}
there exists
y\in B_{r}(x)
that satisfies

B_{(1-\varepsilon)r/2}(y)\subset B_{r}(x)\setminus S.

The following is a classical estimate for porous sets
[
Sal91
, Thm. 3.8.1, Rem. 3.8.2]
.

Lemma 6.6

(Porosity bound)
.

Let
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a
(1-\varepsilon)
–porous set for some
\varepsilon>0
. Then there is
A=A(d)>0
such that

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(S)\leq d-1+\frac{A}{\ln(\varepsilon^{-1})}

With this result in hand, we may finally prove our main estimate for
\Sigma_{d}^{2}
.

Proposition 6.7
.

Let
w:Q_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded solution to (
1.2
) with singular set
\Sigma
. Then

\operatorname{dim}_{\operatorname{par}}(\Sigma_{d}^{2})\leq d-1.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.4
, letting
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
be small enough that
\varepsilon=8(d\delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}<1
, it follows that the set
\pi_{x}(S_{n})
is
(1-\varepsilon)-
porous. By Lemma
6.6
, we have

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\pi_{x}(S_{n}))\leq d-1+\frac{A}{\ln(\varepsilon^{-1})}.

By Lemma
6.2
, we have

\Sigma_{d}^{2}=\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}S_{n}.

Therefore, we have

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\pi_{x}(\Sigma_{d}^{2}))\leq d-1+\frac{A}{\ln(\varepsilon^{-1})}.

Letting
\delta\to 0
, we get

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\pi_{x}(\Sigma_{d}^{2}))\leq d-1,

and we conclude as in Proposition
5.3
by an application of Lemma
4.11
.
∎

7.
Superquadratic second blow-up in the top stratum: the set
\Sigma_{d}^{\geq 3}

In this section, we turn our attention to the set
\Sigma_{d}^{\geq 3}
of points with at least a cubic rate of convergence to the blow-up profile
p_{2}=-t
. Since
p_{2}(x,t)>0
for all
t<0
and
(r^{-2}w_{r}-p_{2})\to 0
, the set
\{w_{r}>0\}
converges, in the limit, to the full space
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(-\infty,0)
. The key observation is that, if the convergence is sufficiently fast, we expect
(r^{-2}w_{r}-p_{2})
to behave like a solution to the heat equation, since

\mathbf{H}(r^{-2}w_{r}-p_{2})=\chi_{\{w_{r}=0\}}.

As it turns out, the cubic rate of convergence is sufficient to disrupt the parabolic scaling in the blow-up limit and obtain a full Taylor expansion by a Campanato-type iteration. In particular, we will be able to uniquely identify the second blow-up profile. Our Lemmas
7.2
and
7.4
below should be compared to
[
FRS24
, Lem. 13.6, Prop. 13.10]
, with caloric polynomials here playing a similar role to the Ansatz manifold of approximate profiles that was used to study a certain subset of
\Sigma_{d-1}
in the melting case.

We will then study the Taylor expansion systematically, and use it to obtain an upgraded polynomial cleaning estimate, which will lead to the parabolic dimension bound through a careful application of the sharp Łojasiewicz inequality for harmonic polynomials.

We begin by recording the fact that, at points of
\Sigma_{d}^{\geq 3}
, the convergence rate to the blow-up profile is at least cubic.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
w:Q_{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded solution to (
1.2
). Then there is a constant
C=C(d,\|w\|_{\infty})>0
such that, for every
(x_{0},t_{0})\in Q_{1}\cap\Sigma_{d}^{\geq 3}
and every
r\in(0,1)
,

\|w-(t_{0}-t)\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r}^{-}(x_{0},t_{0}))}\leq Cr^{3}.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.5
, we have, for small
r
,

\|w-(t_{0}-t)\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r}^{-}(x_{0},t_{0}))}\leq C|Q_{2r}^{-}|^{-1/2}\|w-(t_{0}-t)\|_{L^{2}(Q_{2r}^{-}(x_{0},t_{0}))}.

On the other hand, applying Lemma
3.7
with
\gamma=3
, we obtain

\|w-(t_{0}-t)\|_{L^{2}(Q_{2r}^{-}(x_{0},t_{0}))}\leq C|Q_{r}^{-}|^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{3},

which yields the claim.
∎

Given
\beta>0
,
M_{0}>0
, we define

\Omega^{\beta}_{M_{0}}:=\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(-\infty,0]:|x|<M_{0}|t|^{\frac{1}{2+\beta}}\}.

We now obtain an elementary compactness estimate for caloric functions on the cuspidal domains
\Omega^{\beta}_{M_{0}}
, which will later be the basic iterative tool to approximate
w+t
by caloric polynomials.

Lemma 7.2
.

For given
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\alpha,\beta\in(0,1)
, and
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, there exists a positive constant
M_{0}=M_{0}(d,\alpha,\beta,k,\varepsilon_{0})
such that the following holds. Suppose that
u:Q_{2^{M_{0}}}^{-}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies

(7.1)

\left(\int_{\Omega_{M_{0}}^{\beta}\cap Q_{1}^{-}}|u_{2^{m}}|^{2}+|\nabla u_{2^{m}}|^{2}+|\partial_{t}(u_{2^{m}})|^{2}dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq(2^{m})^{k+\alpha},\quad 0\leq m\leq M_{0},

where
u_{2^{m}}(x,t):=u(2^{m}x,4^{m}t)
. Assume also that

(7.2)

\mathbf{H}u=0\;\text{ in }\;\Omega^{\beta}_{M_{0}}\cap Q_{2^{M_{0}}}^{-},

and for any caloric polynomial
p
of degree
\leq k
,

(7.3)

\int_{-2}^{-1}\int_{B_{M_{0}}}(upG)(x,t)dxdt=0.

Then we have

\left(\int_{\Omega^{\beta}_{M_{0}}\cap Q_{1}^{-}}u^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\varepsilon_{0}.

Proof.

Proceeding by contradiction, assume that there is a sequence
\{u_{n}\}
such that

\left(\int_{\Omega^{\beta}_{n}\cap Q_{1}^{-}}u_{n}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}>\varepsilon_{0},

\left(\int_{\Omega_{n}^{\beta}\cap Q_{1}^{-}}|(u_{n})_{2^{m}}|^{2}+|\nabla(u_{n})_{2^{m}}|^{2}+|\partial_{t}(u_{n})_{2^{m}}|^{2}dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq(2^{m})^{k+\alpha},\quad 0\leq m\leq n,

\mathbf{H}u_{n}=0\;\;\text{ in }\;\Omega^{\beta}_{n},\quad\int_{-2}^{-1}\int_{B_{n}}u_{n}pG(x,t)dxdt=0

for any caloric polynomial
p
of degree
\leq k
. Then, noting that
\Omega^{\beta}_{n}\uparrow\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(-\infty,0)
as
n\to\infty
, along a subsequence
u_{n}\to u
in
L^{2}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(-\infty,0])
, where
u
is caloric with

(7.4)

\left(\int_{Q_{1}^{-}}|u_{2^{m}}|^{2}+|\nabla(u_{2^{m}})|^{2}+|\partial_{t}(u_{2^{m}})|^{2}dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq(2^{m})^{k+\alpha},\quad m\geq 0,

and

(7.5)

\left(\int_{Q_{1}^{-}}u^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\geq\varepsilon_{0},\quad\int_{-2}^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}upG(x,t)dxdt=0.

for every caloric polynomial
p
of degree
\leq k
. By Liouville’s theorem, (
7.4
) implies that
u
is a caloric polynomial of degree
\leq k
. But then (
7.5
) yields a contradiction by taking
p=u
.
∎

The following energy estimate is a slight variation of
[
FRS24
, Lem. 13.7]
, and has the same proof, which consists of explicitly constructing a suitable cutoff function that is supported on
\Omega^{\beta}_{R}
. It is important to observe that this is only possible because
\beta>0
, so that the set
\Omega^{\beta}_{R}
approximates the full space
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(-\infty,0)
at small parabolic scales.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
\beta\in(0,1)
and
\theta\in(1/2,1)
. There exist constants
R_{0}>0
,
C>0
, depending on
d
,
\beta
, and
\theta
, such that, for any caloric function
u
on
\Omega^{\beta}_{R}\cap Q^{-}_{2}
, we have

\int_{\Omega^{\beta}_{\theta R}\cap Q_{1}^{-}}|\nabla u|^{2}+|u_{t}|^{2}\leq C\int_{\Omega^{\beta}_{R}\cap Q_{2}^{-}}u^{2},\quad R\geq R_{0}.

Combining the localization and energy estimates above, we can now implement a Campanato-type iteration which approximates
w+t
near points of
\Sigma_{d}^{3}
by caloric polynomials of arbitrarily high degree in a one-sided time region.

Lemma 7.4

(Campanato iteration)
.

Assume that
(0,0)\in\Sigma_{d}^{\geq 3}
. Then for every
k\geq 3
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\beta\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
, there is a caloric polynomial
p
of parabolic degree
\leq k
and
r_{0}=r_{0}(d,\|w\|_{L^{\infty}},k,\alpha,\beta)\in(0,1)
such that

(7.6)

\|w+t-p\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}\times(-r^{2},-r^{2+\beta}))}\leq r^{k+\alpha},\quad r\leq r_{0}.

Proof.

We fix
k\geq 3
,
\alpha
and
\beta
, and let
M_{0}>10
be a large constant to be chosen later. By definition, we have

(7.7)

(rx,r^{2}t)\in\Omega_{K}^{\beta}\iff|x|<Kr^{-\frac{\beta}{2+\beta}}|t|^{\frac{1}{2+\beta}}\iff(x,t)\in\Omega_{r^{-\beta/(2+\beta)}K}^{\beta},\quad r>0,\;\;K>0.

Thus, if we let

\theta=2^{\frac{-\beta}{2+\beta}}<1,

then

(7.8)

(x,t)\in\Omega_{M_{0}}^{\beta}\iff\left(x/2,t/4\right)\in\Omega_{\theta M_{0}}^{\beta}.

By Lemma
7.1
, there exists a small
r_{1}\in(0,1)
, depending on
d
and
M_{0}
, such that the function

(7.9)

u(x,t):=\frac{(w+t)(r_{1}x,r_{1}^{2}t)}{r_{1}^{2}}

satisfies

(7.10)

\left(\int_{Q_{1}^{-}}|u(2^{-m}x,4^{-m}t)|^{2}dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq(2^{-m})^{k+\alpha},\quad 0\leq m\leq M_{0}+1.

By Lemma
7.1
, there exists a constant
c_{0}>0
such that

(7.11)

\Omega_{c_{0}}^{\beta}\cap Q_{1}^{-}\subset\{w>0\}.

Thus, by requiring
r_{1}^{\frac{\beta}{2+\beta}}\leq\frac{c_{0}}{M_{0}}
so that
\Omega_{M_{0}}^{\beta}\subset\Omega_{r_{1}^{-\beta/(2+\beta)}c_{0}}^{\beta}
, it follows from (
7.7
), (
7.9
), and (
7.11
) that

\mathbf{H}u=0,\quad(x,t)\in\Omega^{\beta}_{M_{0}}\cap Q_{1}^{-}.

A posteriori, using (
7.7
) once more with
r=2^{-m}
, we have
(2^{-m}x,4^{-m}t)\in\Omega^{\beta}_{M_{0}}
whenever
(x,t)\in\Omega^{\beta}_{M_{0}}\subset\Omega^{\beta}_{r^{-\beta/(2+\beta)}M_{0}}
, and thus

(7.12)

\mathbf{H}(u(2^{-m}x,4^{-m}t))=0,\quad(x,t)\in\Omega^{\beta}_{M_{0}}\cap Q_{2^{m}}^{-},\quad m\geq 0.

We now prove that there exists a sequence of caloric polynomials
\{p_{m}\}_{m=0}^{\infty}
of degree
\leq k
such that, for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

(7.13)

\left(\int_{\Omega^{\beta}_{M_{0}}\cap Q_{1}^{-}}|(u-p_{m})(2^{-m}x,4^{-m}t)|^{2}dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq(2^{-m})^{k+\alpha}.

We proceed by induction, where we will show that (
7.13
) holds for
1\leq m\leq j
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Observe that, by (
7.10
), (
7.13
) holds up to
j=\lceil M_{0}\rceil
with
p_{m}:=0
for
m\leq\lceil M_{0}\rceil
. Assume that (
7.13
) holds for some
j\geq\lceil M_{0}\rceil
. Note that
K_{0}=B_{1/2}\times(-1,-1/2)\subset\Omega_{M_{0}}^{\beta}
, so for
0\leq m\leq j-1
, letting
2^{-(m+1)}K_{0}

=\{(2^{-(m+1)}x,4^{-(m+1)}t):(x,t)\in K_{0}\}
, we have from (
7.13
) that

\left(\fint_{2^{-(m+1)}K_{0}}|u-p_{m}|^{2}dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\fint_{2^{-(m+1)}K_{0}}|u-p_{m+1}|^{2}dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C(2^{-m})^{k+\alpha},

so that

\left(\fint_{2^{-(m+1)}K_{0}}|p_{m+1}-p_{m}|^{2}dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C(2^{-m})^{k+\alpha},

namely

\left(\int_{K_{0}}|(p_{m+1}-p_{m})(2^{-(m+1)}x,4^{-(m+1)}t)|^{2}dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C(2^{-m})^{k+\alpha}.

Since the space of polynomials of degree
\leq k
is finite dimensional, this implies that the coefficient vectors
a_{m}^{i}
of
p_{m}
of parabolic degree
i
satisfy

(7.14)

|a_{m}^{i}-a_{m+1}^{i}|\leq C(2^{-m})^{k+\alpha-i},\quad 0\leq m\leq j-1,\quad 0\leq i\leq k.

We define
p_{j+1}
through
L^{2}_{G}
–orthogonal projection onto the space of caloric polynomials of degree
\leq k
:

(7.15)

p_{j+1}:=\argmin_{p\text{ caloric, }\deg(p)\leq k}\int_{-2}^{-1}\int_{B_{M_{0}}}(u-p)^{2}(2^{-j}x,4^{-j}t)G(x,t)dxdt.

We will now show that (
7.13
) for
m=j+1
holds with
p_{j+1}
.

Summing (
7.14
) from
m
to
j-1
, we obtain

(7.16)

|a_{m}^{i}-a_{j}^{i}|\leq C(2^{-m})^{k+\alpha-i},\quad 0\leq m\leq j-1,\quad 0\leq i\leq k.

Thus, in particular,

\left(\int_{\Omega^{\beta}_{M_{0}}\cap Q_{1}^{-}}|(p_{m}-p_{j})(2^{-m}x,4^{-m}t)|^{2}dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C(2^{-m})^{k+\alpha},\quad 0\leq m\leq j.

Since (
7.13
) holds for
0\leq m\leq j
, we have

(7.17)

\int_{\Omega^{\beta}_{M_{0}}\cap Q_{1}^{-}}|(u-p_{j})(2^{-m}x,4^{-m}t)|^{2}dxdt\leq C(2^{-m})^{2(k+\alpha)},\quad 0\leq m\leq j,

or, equivalently, recalling (
7.7
),

\int_{\Omega^{\beta}_{\theta^{-l}M_{0}}\cap Q_{2^{l}}^{-}}|(u-p_{j})(2^{-j}x,4^{-j}t)|^{2}dxdt\leq C2^{l\gamma}(2^{-j})^{2(k+\alpha)},\quad 0\leq l\leq j,

where
\gamma=\gamma(d,k)
. Now we decompose above integral to estimate with the weight
G(x,t)
. Observing that
B_{M_{0}}\times(-2,-1)\subset\Omega^{\beta}_{M_{0}}
, we get

\int_{B_{M_{0}}\times(-2,-1)\cap(Q_{2^{l}}^{-}\backslash Q_{2^{l-1}}^{-})}|(u-p_{j})(2^{-j}x,4^{-j}t)|^{2}dxdt\leq C2^{l\gamma}(2^{-j})^{2(k+\alpha)},\quad 0\leq l\leq j,

so, since
G(x,t)\leq Ce^{-2^{l}}
on
(Q_{2^{l}}^{-}\backslash Q_{2^{l-1}}^{-})\cap\{t\in(-2,-1)\},
and
2^{j}\geq 2^{M_{0}}>M_{0},
summing up the integrals over
0\leq l\leq j
, we have

\left(\int_{B_{M_{0}}\times(-2,-1)}|(u-p_{j})(2^{-j}x,4^{-j}t)|^{2}G(x,t)dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C(2^{-j})^{k+\alpha},

where
C
remains independent of
M_{0}
. By minimality of
p_{j+1}
,

\left(\int_{B_{M_{0}}\times(-2,-1)}|(u-p_{j+1})(2^{-j}x,4^{-j}t)|^{2}G(x,t)dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C(2^{-j})^{k+\alpha}.

Applying the triangle inequality to the last two inequalities,

\left(\int_{B_{M_{0}}\times(-2,-1)}|(p_{j}-p_{j+1})(2^{-j}x,4^{-j}t)|^{2}dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C(2^{-j})^{k+\alpha}.

Therefore, using again the finite dimensionality,

|a^{i}_{j}-a^{i}_{j+1}|\leq C(2^{-j})^{k+\alpha-i},\quad 0\leq i\leq k.

which together with (
7.16
) implies

|a^{i}_{j+1}-a^{i}_{m}|\leq C(2^{-m})^{k+\alpha-i},\quad 0\leq m\leq j,\quad 0\leq i\leq k.

Thus, we infer from (
7.17
) that

(7.18)

\left(\int_{\Omega^{\beta}_{M_{0}}\cap Q_{1}^{-}}|(u-p_{j+1})(2^{-m}x,4^{-m}t)|^{2}dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{1}(2^{-m})^{k+\alpha},\quad 0\leq m\leq j.

In view of (
7.18
), Lemma
7.3
yields

(7.19)

\left(\int_{\Omega^{\beta}_{\theta M_{0}}\cap Q_{1}^{-}}(4^{-m}|\nabla(u-p_{j+1})(2^{-m}x,4^{-m}t)|^{2}+16^{-m}|\partial_{t}(u-p_{j+1})(2^{-m}x,4^{-m}t)|^{2})dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq C_{2}(2^{-m})^{k+\alpha},\quad 0\leq m\leq j,

where
C_{1},C_{2}
are both independent of
M_{0}
and
j
. Letting

\tilde{u}(x,t)=\frac{(u-p_{j+1})(2^{-j}x,4^{-j}t)}{(C_{1}+C_{2})(2^{-j})^{k+\alpha}},

we obtain

\left(\int_{\Omega^{\beta}_{\theta M_{0}}\cap Q_{1}^{-}}(|\tilde{u}_{2^{l}}|^{2}+|\nabla\tilde{u}_{2^{l}}|^{2}+|\partial_{t}\tilde{u}_{2^{l}}|^{2})\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq 2^{l(k+\alpha)},\quad 0\leq l\leq j.

Since
j\geq M_{0}
, it follows that
\tilde{u}
satisfies (
7.1
). Moreover, (
7.12
) shows that
\tilde{u}
satisfies (
7.2
), whereas (
7.3
) is just the optimality condition of (
7.15
). Thus, taking
\varepsilon_{0}=2^{-(k+\alpha+(d+2)/2)}/(C_{1}+C_{2})
in Lemma
7.2
, if
M_{0}
is chosen sufficiently large, we have

\left(\int_{\Omega^{\beta}_{\theta M_{0}}\cap Q_{1}^{-}}(u-p_{j+1})^{2}(2^{-j}x,4^{-j}t)dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq 2^{-(d+2)/2}(2^{-(j+1)})^{k+\alpha}.

Changing the variables and recalling (
7.8
), we obtain

\left(\int_{\Omega^{\beta}_{M_{0}}\cap Q_{1}^{-}}(u-p_{j+1})^{2}(2^{-(j+1)}x,4^{-(j+1)}t)dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq(2^{-(j+1)})^{k+\alpha}.

This completes our induction and shows that (
7.13
) holds for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

Observe that, in view of (
7.16
), the polynomials
p_{j}
converge locally uniformly, as
j\to\infty
to a caloric polynomial
p_{\infty}
of degree
\leq k
. Letting
j\to\infty
in (
7.18
), we obtain

\left(\int_{\Omega^{\beta}_{M_{0}}\cap Q_{1}^{-}}|(u-p_{\infty})(2^{-m}x,4^{-m}t)|^{2}dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C(2^{-m})^{(k+\alpha)},\quad m\geq 0.

Thus,

(7.20)

\int_{\Omega^{\beta}_{M_{0}}\cap Q_{1}^{-}}|(u-p_{\infty})(rx,r^{2}t)|^{2}dxdt\leq Cr^{2(k+\alpha)},\quad r\in[0,1].

Finally, let
\hat{p}_{\infty}
and
\hat{M}_{0}
be such that (
7.20
) holds for
\hat{\beta}:=\frac{1}{2}(1+\beta)>\beta
. Then, if

is sufficiently small, depending only on

, we have

. Therefore, the map

is caloric in

and satisfies

) follows by interior parabolic regularity.
∎

We now show that at points of
\Sigma_{d}^{\geq 3}
, the solution admits a genuine one-sided Taylor expansion in space–time with caloric polynomial coefficients. This yields a uniquely defined higher-order profile at each such point, which will be crucial in the analysis to follow.

Proposition 7.5

(One-sided Taylor expansion at
\Sigma_{d}^{\geq 3}
)
.

Let
w:Q_{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded solution to (
1.2
). Then, for every
X=(x_{0},t_{0})\in\Sigma_{d}^{\geq 3}
, there exists a unique sequence
\{p^{(j)}_{X}\}_{j=3}^{\infty}
of caloric polynomials such that each
p_{j}
is parabolically homogeneous of degree
j
, and one has, for any
\beta\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
and
k\geq 3
, the expansion

(7.21)

w(x-x_{0},t-t_{0})=t_{0}-t+\sum_{j=3}^{k}p^{(j)}_{X}(x,t)+E^{(k)}_{X}(x,t),\quad(x,t)\in Q_{1},\quad t<t_{0},\quad|t_{0}-t|\geq|x-x_{0}|^{2+\beta},

where, for some constant
C=C(d,k,\beta,\|w\|_{\infty})>0
,

|E^{(k)}_{X}(x,t)|\leq C_{k}(|x-x_{0}|^{k+1}+|t-t_{0}|^{\frac{k+1}{2}}).

Equivalently, we have

(7.22)

\left\|w-(t_{0}-t)-q_{X}^{(k)}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(x_{0})\times[t_{0}-r^{2},t_{0}-r^{2+\beta}])}\leq C_{k}r^{k+1},\quad q^{k}_{X}:=\sum_{j=3}^{k}p_{X}^{(j)},\quad r\in(0,1).

Proof.

Up to translation and rescaling, we may assume that
X=(x_{0},t_{0})=(0,0)
. For each
k\geq 3
, Lemma
7.4
applied with
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
implies that there exists a polynomial
q_{X}^{(k)}
of parabolic degree
\leq k
such that

(7.23)

\|w+t-q_{X}^{(k)}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}\times[-r^{2},-r^{2+\beta}])}\leq r^{k+\frac{1}{2}},\quad r\leq r_{0,k},

where
r_{0,k}\in(0,1)
depends on
k
,
d
,
\|w\|_{\infty}
, and
\beta
. With no loss of generality, we may also assume that the sequence
\{r_{0,k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is decreasing. Letting
q_{X}^{(2)}:=0
, we see by Lemma
7.1
that (
7.23
) also holds for
k=2
and an appropriate choice of
r_{0,2}
. Setting
r=r_{0,k}
in (
7.23
), since all norms in the space of polynomials of parabolic degree
\leq k
are comparable, we infer that

(7.24)

\|q_{X}^{(k)}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\leq C_{k},

where
C_{k}
depends on
k
,
d
,
\|w\|_{\infty}
and
r_{0,k}
. Using the triangle inequality in (
7.23
), we get

\|q_{X}^{(k)}-q_{X}^{(k+1)}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}\times[-r^{2},-r^{2+\beta}])}\leq r^{k+\frac{1}{2}},\quad r\leq\min(r_{0,k},r_{0,k+1})=r_{0,k+1}.

This implies that
q_{X}^{(k)}
and
q_{X}^{(k+1)}
must differ by a homogeneous term of degree
k+1
, that is,

(7.25)

q_{X}^{(k+1)}(x,t)=q_{X}^{(k)}(x,t)+p^{(k+1)}_{X}(x,t),

where each
p_{X}^{j}
is a (possibly zero) parabolically homogeneous polynomial of degree
j
. We therefore have

q_{X}^{(k)}=\sum_{j=3}^{k}p_{X}^{(j)},

and, by (
7.23
), (
7.24
), and (
7.25
),

(7.26)

\|w+t-q_{X}^{(k)}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}\times[-r^{2},-r^{2+\beta}])}\\
\leq\|w+t-q_{X}^{(k+1)}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}\times[-r^{2},-r^{2+\beta}])}+\|p^{(k+1)}_{X}\|_{{L^{\infty}(B_{r}\times[-r^{2},-r^{2+\beta}])}}\\
\leq r^{k+1+\frac{1}{2}}+Cr^{k+1}\leq C^{\prime}r^{k+1}\quad 0<r\leq r_{0,k+1}.

Up to increasing the value of
C
, depending only on
\|w\|_{\infty}
, we see that (
7.26
) holds for
r\in(0,1)
, which shows (
7.22
). Finally, uniqueness of the sequence
\{q^{(k)}_{X}\}_{k\geq 3}
(and thus of
\{p^{(k)}_{X}\}_{k\geq 3}
) follows directly from (
7.22
) and the triangle inequality.
∎

The next proposition shows that the polynomial
q
obtained as a second blow-up in Proposition

coincides, up to normalization, with the first nontrivial term in the expansion of Proposition

, and that the points of
\Sigma_{d}^{\infty}
are precisely those where all such higher-order coefficients vanish.

Proposition 7.6

(Characterization of the second blow-up and
\Sigma_{d}^{\infty}
)
.

Let
w:Q_{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded solution to (
1.2
), assume that
(0,0)\in\Sigma_{d}^{\geq 3}
, and let
\{p_{(0,0)}^{(j)}\}_{j=3}^{\infty}
be the polynomials given by Proposition
7.5
. Then the following holds:

(i)

If
(0,0)\in\Sigma_{d}^{k}
for some
k\in\{3,4,\ldots\}
, then for any
\gamma>k
, the polynomial
q
of Proposition
4.6
is independent of the subsequence and of
\gamma
, the full sequence converges to
q
, and

q=\frac{1}{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(1,p^{(k)}_{(0,0)})}p_{(0,0)}^{(k)},

where
p^{(k)}_{(0,0)}
is the first non-zero element of the sequence
\{p_{(0,0)}^{(j)}\}_{j=3}^{\infty}
.

(ii)

Moreover, we have

(7.27)

(0,0)\in\Sigma_{d}^{\infty}\quad\text{if and only if }\quad p^{(j)}_{(0,0)}\equiv 0\text{ for all }j\geq 3.

Proof.

Assume first that
(0,0)\in\Sigma_{d}^{k}
. By (
2.16
), Lemma
3.6
, and Lemma
3.5
, we have

(7.28)

\|w+t\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r}^{-})}\lesssim r^{k},\quad r\ll 1.

By, (
7.21
), this implies that
p_{(0,0)}^{(j)}\equiv 0
for
j<k
. Similarly, by (
2.17
) and Lemma
3.6
,

|Q_{r}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|w+t\|_{L^{2}(Q_{r}^{-})}\gg r^{k+\frac{1}{2}},\quad r\ll 1,

which, by (
7.28
) and (
7.21
), implies that
p_{(0,0)}^{(k)}\not\equiv 0
.

Given
r\in(0,1)
, let
u_{r}(x,t)=(w+t)_{r}(x,t)=w(rx,r^{2}t)+r^{2}t
.
From (
7.21
), we have, for any fixed
R>0
, as
r\downarrow 0
,

(7.29)

\|r^{-k}u_{r}-p^{(k)}_{(0,0)}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}\times(-R^{2},-1/R^{2}))}\leq Cr=o(1).

Letting
r\downarrow 0
, we infer that

r^{-k}u_{r}\xrightarrow{r\downarrow 0}p_{(0,0)}^{(k)}\quad\text{ locally uniformly in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(-\infty,0).

In particular, recalling the polynomial growth estimate (
4.4
), we have

(7.30)

H^{\frac{1}{2}}(1,\zeta_{r}r^{-k}u_{r})\xrightarrow{r\downarrow 0}H^{\frac{1}{2}}(1,p_{(0,0)}^{(k)}).

On the other hand, by (
2.16
) and Proposition
4.6
(a),

\|r_{j}^{-k}u_{r_{j}}-r_{j}^{-k}H^{\frac{1}{2}}(1,\zeta_{r_{j}}u_{r_{j}})q\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}\times(-1,-1/2))}=r_{j}^{-k}H^{\frac{1}{2}}(r_{j},\zeta u)o(1)\leq o(1),\quad j\geq 1.

Thus, by (
7.29
) and (
7.30
), we infer that

H(1,p_{(0,0)}^{(k)})^{\frac{1}{2}}q=p_{(0,0)}^{(k)},

which proves part (i).

Part (i) implies, in particular, that if
(0,0)\notin\Sigma_{d}^{\infty}
, then the sequence
\{p_{(0,0)}^{j}\}_{j\geq 3}
is not identically zero, which shows one implication of (ii). Conversely, if
(0,0)\in\Sigma_{d}^{\infty}
, then, as before, using Lemma
3.7
, we conclude that (
7.28
) holds for all
k\geq 3
, and thus
p_{(0,0)}^{(k)}\equiv 0
for all
k\geq 3
.

∎

We next upgrade the expansion of Proposition
7.5
to a quantitative stability statement along
\Sigma_{d}^{\geq 3}
: nearby base points
X_{1}
,
X_{2}
have jets

whose coefficients vary Lipschitz-continuously as in (

Lemma 7.7
.

Let
w:Q_{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded solution to (
1.2
). Let us fix

. For
X=(x,t)\in\Sigma_{d}^{\geq 3}\cap Q_{1}
,
let

where

are the unique polynomials of Proposition

, and let
\|\cdot\|
be any norm in the space of polynomials of degree
\leq k
. Then there exists a constant

, depending only on

,
k
, and
\|w\|_{\infty}
, such that

(7.31)

\|q_{X_{1}}^{(k)}-q_{X_{2}}^{(k)}\|\leq C|x_{1}-x_{2}|,\quad X_{1}=(x_{1},t_{1}),\quad X_{2}=(x_{2},t_{2}),\quad X_{1},X_{2}\in\Sigma_{d}^{\geq 3}\cap Q_{1}.

Furthermore, setting
\delta X=(\delta x,\delta t):=(x_{1}-x_{2},t_{1}-t_{2})
and

, one has

(7.32)

\|q_{X_{1}}^{(k)}-(q_{X_{2}}^{(k)}(\cdot+\delta X)-\delta t)\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r})}\leq Cr^{k+1}.

Observe first that, from (
7.24
),
\|q_{X}^{(k)}\|
is bounded, uniformly in
X
. With no loss of generality, we may then assume that
|x_{1}-x_{2}|<r_{0}/100
. Moreover, by Lemmas
6.1
and
7.1
, we may also assume that
|t_{1}-t_{2}|<r^{2}/8
.
Letting
q_{i}:=q_{X_{i}}^{(k)}
and letting

be a constant that may increase at each step, we have

(7.33)

\|-\delta t+q_{2}(\cdot+\delta X)-q_{2}\|\leq C|\delta t|+C\|q_{2}(\cdot+\delta X)-q_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\\
\leq Cr^{2}+C\|(\nabla q_{2},\partial_{t}q_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{2})}|\delta X|\leq Cr^{2}+C\|q_{2}\||\delta X|\leq Cr.

Applying (
7.22
), we have

(7.34)

\|w(X_{i}+\cdot)+t-q_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}\times[-r^{2},-r^{2}/2])}\leq Cr^{k+1},\quad i\in\{1,2\}.

When

, (

) may be equivalently written as

(7.35)

\|w(X_{1}+\cdot)+t+\delta t-q_{2}(\cdot+\\
\delta X)\|_{L^{\infty}((B_{r}-\delta x)\times[-r^{2}-\delta t,-r^{2}/2-\delta t])}\leq Cr^{k+1}.

Hence, recalling that

and
|\delta t|<r^{2}/8
, we may combine the inequalities (
7.34
) for

and (
7.35
), in their common domains, to infer that

(7.36)

\|q_{1}-(q_{2}(\cdot+\delta X)-\delta t)\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r/2}\times[-7r^{2}/8,-5r^{2}/8])}\leq Cr^{k+1}.

By comparability of norms in finite-dimensional spaces (applied to the

norms in the rescaled domains

and
Q_{1}
), up to increasing the constant
C
, this proves (
7.32
). Since
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
are polynomials of parabolic degree at most

, (
7.36
) forces

(7.37)

\|q_{1}-(q_{2}(\cdot+\delta X)-\delta t)\|\leq Cr.

The claim then follows from (
7.33
) and (
7.37
).
∎

The basic properties of parabolic frequency, combined with the superquadratic cleaning property of Lemma

, imply that any two points
(x_{1},t_{1}),(x_{2},t_{2})\in\Sigma_{d}^{\geq k}
satisfy the

-order cleaning estimate

. We next show that this bound can be upgraded by one degree, which will be essential to obtain the parabolic dimension drop.

Proposition 7.8

(Refined cleaning of
\Sigma_{d}^{k}
)
.

Let
k\geq 3
, and let
(x_{1},t_{1})\in\Sigma_{d}^{\geq k}\cap Q_{1}
. Then there exists a constant
C=C(\|w\|_{L^{\infty}},d,k)
such that, for every
(x_{2},t_{2})\in\Sigma_{d}^{k}\cap Q_{1}
,

|t_{2}-t_{1}|\leq C|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{k+1}.

Proof.

Let
X_{1}=(x_{1},t_{1}),\;X_{2}=(x_{2},t_{2})
, and let
q_{1}:=q_{X_{1}}^{(k)}
and
q_{2}:=q_{X_{2}}^{(k)}
be as in Proposition
7.5
. By Proposition
7.6
,
q_{2}
must be a homogeneous polynomial of degree
k
, and
q_{1}
is either of degree
\geq k
or identically zero. Therefore, by (
7.31
), we have, for
r=4|x_{2}-x_{1}|
,

(7.38)

\|q_{1}-q_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r})}\leq Cr^{k+1}.

On the other hand, we see from (
7.32
) that

(7.39)

\|q_{1}-(q_{2}(\cdot+\delta X)-\delta t)\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r})}\leq Cr^{k+1}.

We deduce from (
7.38
) and (
7.39
) that

\|q_{2}-(q_{2}(\cdot+\delta X)-\delta t)\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{r})}\leq Cr^{k+1}.

Evaluating the above inequality at
X:=(0,0)
and at
X:=(\delta x,\delta t)
, we obtain, respectively,

(7.40)

|-q_{2}(\delta X)+\delta t|\leq Cr^{k+1},

and

(7.41)

|q_{2}(2\delta X)-q_{2}(\delta X)+\delta t|\leq Cr^{k+1}.

On the other hand, we have

(7.42)

q_{2}(2\delta X)=q_{2}(2\delta x,4\delta t)+(q_{2}(2\delta x,2\delta t)-q_{2}(2\delta x,4\delta t))=q_{2}(2\delta x,4\delta t)+O(|\delta t|r^{k-2}),

and, by Lemma
7.1
and Lemma
6.1
, we infer that

|\delta t|=|t_{1}-t_{2}|\leq O(r^{3}).

Thus, by (
7.42
) and the parabolic homogeneity of
q_{2}
, we have

q_{2}(2\delta X)=2^{k}q_{2}(\delta X)+O(r^{k+1}),

and, therefore, by (
7.41
),

(7.43)

|(2^{k}-1)q_{2}(\delta X)+\delta t|\leq Cr^{k+1}.

The result now follows from (
7.40
), (
7.43
), and the triangle inequality.
∎

We will make use of the following inequality for harmonic polynomials
[
BET17
, Thm. 3.1]
.

Lemma 7.9

(Sharp Łojasiewicz inequality for harmonic polynomials)
.

There exists constant
c=c(d,k)
such that, for every nonconstant harmonic polynomial
h:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
of degree
\leq k
and every
x_{0}\in\{h=0\}
, we have

|h(x)|\geq c\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}(x_{0}))}\operatorname{dist}(x,\{h=0\})^{k},\quad x\in B_{1/2}(x_{0}).

In degree two, we will also require the following elementary variant for the non-harmonic case.

Lemma 7.10

(Łojasiewicz inequality for quadratic forms)
.

Let
f(x)=Ax\cdot x
, where
A
is a non-zero, symmetric
d\times d
matrix, and let

(7.44)

\alpha=\min\{|\lambda|:\lambda\;\;\emph{isanon-zeroeigenvalueof}\;\;A\}.

Then, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\operatorname{dist}(x,\{f=0\})\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}|f(x)|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

Up to a rotation, we may assume that
A
is a diagonal matrix, so that
f(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}x_{i}^{2}
. Note first that, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

|\nabla f(x)|^{2}=4|Ax|^{2}=4(A^{2}x)\cdot x=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}^{2}x_{i}^{2}\geq 4\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{d}|\lambda_{i}|x_{i}^{2}\geq 4\alpha\left|\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}x_{i}^{2}\right|=4\alpha|f(x)|.

Fix
x_{0}\neq 0
, and assume with no loss of generality that
f(x_{0})>0
. Consider the gradient flow

\dot{\gamma}(t)=-\nabla f(\gamma(t)),\quad t\geq 0,\quad\gamma(0)=x_{0}.

Then

\frac{d}{dt}f(\gamma(t))=-|\nabla f(\gamma(t))|^{2},

and there exists
T\in(0,\infty]
such that

\lim_{t\to T}f(\gamma(t))=0.

We therefore have

\text{dist}(x_{0},\{f=0\})\leq\text{length}(\gamma(0,T))\leq\int_{0}^{T}\frac{|\nabla f(\gamma(t))|^{2}}{|\nabla f(\gamma(t))|}dt\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{T}-\frac{df/dt}{2f^{\frac{1}{2}}}=\frac{f(x_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\sqrt{\alpha}}.

∎

We are now ready to prove the main result of this section.

Proposition 7.11
.

Let

\dim_{\operatorname{par}}(\Sigma_{d}\backslash\Sigma_{d}^{\infty})\leq d-1.

Proof.

6.7

8.
Infinite order second blow-up in the top stratum: the set
\Sigma_{d}^{\infty}

In this section we complete the proof of Theorem
1.3
by showing that
\Sigma_{d}^{\infty}=\emptyset
for global solutions (unless that solution is a translation of
(-t)^{+}
), and by showing that
\Sigma_{d}^{\infty}(w)\cap\{t=t_{0}\}
is empty for most
t_{0}
for local solutions.

First we turn to the statement for global solutions. Our basic tool will be the (non-cutoff) monotonicity formulas. We recall the definitions of the height, energy, and frequency functions

\displaystyle H(r,f):=

\displaystyle\int_{t=-r^{2}}f^{2}(x)G(x,t)\,dx,

\displaystyle D(r,f):=

\displaystyle 2r^{2}\int_{t=-r^{2}}|\nabla f|^{2}(x)G(x,t)\,dx,

\displaystyle\phi(r,f):=

\displaystyle\frac{D(r,f)}{H(r,f)}.

We show below that for global solutions these quantities are monotone when applied to the difference between
w
and its first blowup. Recall from (
2.9
) that if
u=w(x,t)-(-t)^{+}
, then
u\mathbf{H}u=(-t)^{+}\chi_{\{w=0\}}>0
. Given that this term has a sign, it is not surprising that
\phi
is monotone (compare with
[
FRS24
]
for similar computations with a cutoff and
[
AEK95
]
where the sign of a related error term expression is leveraged to get monotonicity in the context of elliptic boundary unique continuation).

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
w
solve (
1.2
) in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(-1,0]
with
(0,0)\in\Sigma_{d}
. Let
u(x,t):=w(x,t)-(-t)^{+}
and assume that
u(x,t_{0})\not\equiv 0
for any
t_{0}\in(-1,0]
. Then

(8.1)

\displaystyle\frac{2\phi(r,u)+4}{r}\geq\frac{d}{dr}\log(H(r,u))

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2\phi(r,u)}{r},\qquad\forall r<1/2

\displaystyle\phi(r,u)\geq\phi(s,u)

\displaystyle\geq 2,\qquad\forall 0<s<r<1/2.

Proof.

We compute, using (
2.12
) and (
2.9
),

(8.2)

\frac{d}{dr}H(r,u)=\frac{2}{r}(D(r,u)+2r^{2}\langle u,\mathbf{H}u\rangle_{r})\geq\frac{2}{r}D(r,u).

On the other hand, using (
2.9
) we obtain

4r\langle u,\mathbf{H}u\rangle_{r}=4r\langle-u\chi_{\{w=0\}},1\rangle_{r}=4r^{-1}\langle u^{2}\chi_{\{w=0\}},1\rangle_{r}\leq 4r^{-1}H(r,u),

which, dividing by
H(r,u)
in (
8.2
), readily implies the first line in (
8.1
).

From (
2.12
) we have

\frac{d}{dr}H(r,u)=2r^{-1}\langle Zu,u\rangle_{r}.

We also have from (
2.13
) and (
2.9
) that

(8.3)

\frac{d}{dr}D(r,u)=\frac{1}{r}(2\langle Zu,Zu\rangle_{r}-4r^{2}\langle Zu,\mathbf{H}u\rangle_{r})=\frac{2}{r}(\langle Zu,Zu\rangle_{r}-4r^{2}\langle u,\mathbf{H}u\rangle_{r}).

Combining the above equations,

(8.4)

H(r,u)^{2}\frac{d}{dr}\phi(r,u)=2r^{-1}(\langle Zu,Zu\rangle_{r}\langle u,u\rangle_{r}-\langle Zu,u\rangle_{r}^{2})-8r\langle u,\mathbf{H}u\rangle_{r}\langle u,u\rangle_{r}+4r\langle u,\mathbf{H}u\rangle_{r}\langle Zu,u\rangle_{r}\\
\geq 4r\langle u,\mathbf{H}u\rangle_{r}(\langle Zu,u\rangle_{r}-2H(r,u))=4r\langle u,\mathbf{H}u\rangle_{r}(D(r,u)+2r^{2}\langle u,\mathbf{H}u\rangle_{r}-2H(r,u))\\
\geq 4r\langle u,\mathbf{H}u\rangle_{r}(D(r,u)-2H(r,u)).

On the other hand, combining the above equations and using that
Zu=2u
in
\{w=0\}
, we get

(8.5)

\frac{d}{dr}r^{-4}\left(D(r,u)-2H(r,u)\right)=2r^{-5}\langle(Zu-2u,Zu-2u\rangle_{r}+2r^{2}\langle 2u-Zu,\mathbf{H}u\rangle_{r})=2r^{-5}\langle Zu,Zu\rangle_{r}\geq 0.

Finally we claim that

\lim_{r\downarrow 0}r^{-4}(D(r,u)-2H(r,u))=0.

If this is true then we have that
D(r,u)\geq 2H(r,u)
for all
0<r<1/2
, which, in light of (
8.4
) implies that
\frac{d}{dr}\phi(r,u)\geq 0
and that
\phi(r,u)\geq 2
for all
r<1/2
. So we are done if we have proven the claim.

The claim follows by simple blow-up analysis. We see that
r^{-4}D(r,u)=r^{-4}D(r,w)=D(1,w_{r})
where
w_{r}=r^{-2}w(rx,r^{2}t)
. Note that
w_{r}(\cdot,-1)\in C^{1,1}_{x}
(since
w_{r}
also solves the obstacle problem (
1.2
)), so
w_{r}(\cdot,-1)\rightarrow 1
in
C^{1}_{\operatorname{loc},x}
. It follows that
r^{-4}D(r,u)\rightarrow 0
as
r\uparrow 0
. Similarly, by homogeneity we have that
r^{-4}H(r,u)=H(1,w_{r}-(-t)^{+})\rightarrow H(1,(-t)^{+}-(-t)^{+})
by the same convergence as above. This finishes the proof of the claim and thus the lemma.
∎

We now show the last ingredient needed for Theorem
1.3
.

Proposition 8.2
.

Let
w
solve (
1.2
) in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(-1,0]
and assume that
(0,0)\in\Sigma_{d}^{\infty}(w)
. Then
w=(-t)^{+}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(-1,0]
.

Proof.

First we assume that
w\not\equiv(-t)^{+}
. By forward uniqueness of the obstacle problem and backwards uniqueness of bounded solutions to the heat equation in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, this implies that
w(x,t_{0})\neq(-t_{0})^{+}
for any
t_{0}\in(-1,0]
. In particular, if
u=w-(-t)^{+}
, then
H(r,u)\neq 0
for any
r\in[0,1)
.

We apply Proposition
8.1
and get that

\infty>\Lambda\equiv\phi(1/2,u)\geq\phi(0,u).

Using (
8.1
) we have,
H(r,u)/H(1/2,u)\geq r^{2\Lambda+C}.
In other words, there exists some constant
\tilde{C}>0
(which depends on
w
but is non-zero) such that

H(r,u)\geq\tilde{C}r^{2\Lambda+C},\qquad\forall r<1/4.

On the other hand, recalling the definition of
\Sigma_{d}^{k}
(i.e. (
6.1
)) and the estimate (
7.28
)
since
(0,0)\in\Sigma_{d}^{k}
for any
k
we know that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
there exists an
r_{k}
such that if
r<r_{k}
, then

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(Q^{-}_{r})}\leq r^{k}.

Let

and we get our desired contradiction.
∎

We are now ready to complete the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

By Propositions
7.11
and
8.2
, (
1.9
) and (
1.11
) hold, respectively. It remains to show the properties of

for local solutions. By a rescaling and partition of unity argument, it is enough to show that if

\Sigma_{d}^{\infty}\cap Q_{1}\subset\{x\in B_{1}:t=h(x)\}

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(\{t\in(-1,1):(x,t)\in\Sigma_{d}^{\infty}\cap Q_{1}\text{ for some }x\in B_{1}\})=0.

By Lemmas
3.5
and

, for every

Thus, by Lemma

, up to increasing the value of

,

(8.8)

|t_{1}-t_{0}|\leq C_{k}|x_{1}-x_{0}|^{k},\quad(x_{0},t_{0}),(x_{1},t_{1})\in\Sigma_{d}^{\infty}\cap\overline{Q_{1}}.

On the other hand, one readily sees from Lemma

9.
Examples of non-trivial
\Sigma^{\infty}_{d}
and
\Sigma^{2}_{d}

In this section we collect some interesting examples about the “anomalous” points in the top stratum,
\Sigma_{d}^{2}
and
\Sigma_{d}^{\infty}
.

9.1.
Example of a local solution with
\Sigma_{d}^{\infty}\neq\emptyset
.

Our first example shows that if
w
is a solution in a parabolic cylinder (as opposed to globally defined) then we can have
\Sigma_{d}^{\infty}(w)\neq\emptyset
even when
w\not\equiv(-t)^{+}
. This shows the necessity of our assumption that
w
be a global solution in the last part of Theorem
1.3
.

Example 9.1
.

Let

(9.1)

g(t)=\begin{cases}e^{-1/t^{2}}&t<0\\
0&t\geq 0\end{cases}

and consider the Tychonoff function

(9.2)

\varphi(x,t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}g^{(k)}(t)\frac{x_{1}^{2k}}{(2k)!}.

Then
\varphi
is a smooth caloric function in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
, which vanishes for
t\geq 0
. Let
\varepsilon>0
, and let

(9.3)

w(x,t)=(-t)^{+}+\varepsilon\varphi(x,t),\quad(x,t)\in Q_{1}.

Then
w
solves

(9.4)

w_{t}-\Delta w=-\chi_{\{t<0\}},\quad(x,t)\in Q_{1}.

On the other hand, since
\varphi(\cdot,0)=\varphi_{t}(\cdot,0)\equiv 0
, if we choose
\varepsilon
sufficiently small, then
w=-t+\varepsilon\varphi>0
and
w_{t}=-1+\varepsilon\varphi_{t}<0
in
Q_{1}\cap\{t<0\}
. Thus
\{w>0\}=\{w_{t}<0\}
,
w
solves (
1.2
) in
Q_{1}
,
w\not\equiv(-t)^{+}
, and yet
w(x,0)\equiv 0
, so
B_{1}\times\{0\}\subset\Sigma_{d}^{\infty}
.

9.2.
Examples with
\Sigma_{d}^{2}\neq\emptyset

In this subsection we construct global solutions
w
such that
\Sigma_{d}^{2}(w)\neq\emptyset
. That is we construct points with second blow-up that converges slower than any power.

We begin by showing that global solutions must vanish in finite time (cf. Lemma
6.1
).

Proposition 9.2
.

Let
w:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded solution to (
1.2
). The solution
w
vanishes in finite time, and the extinction time
T:=\sup\{t:\|w(\cdot,t)\|_{\infty}>0\}
. satisfies

(9.5)

T\leq c_{d}^{-1}\|w(\cdot,0)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

where
c_{d}
is the constant of (
2.5
).

Proof.

Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t_{0}>0
be such that
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\overline{\{w>0\}}
. By (
2.5
) applied at scale
r=\sqrt{t_{0}}
and centered at
(x_{0},t_{0})
, there exists a dimensional constant
c_{d}
such that

t_{0}c_{d}\leq\|w\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|w(\cdot,0)\|_{\infty}.

Thus,
w(\cdot,t_{0})\equiv 0
for
t_{0}\geq\|w(\cdot,0)\|_{\infty}c_{d}^{-1}.

∎

Our second lemma will help us to rule out non-quadratic second blow-ups in the radial setting.

Lemma 9.3
.

Let
k\geq 2
, let
r=|x|
, and let
p
be the unique homogeneous, radially symmetric, caloric polynomial of degree
2k
such that
\frac{\partial^{2k}p}{\partial r^{2k}}=1
. Then there exist positive constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
, depending only on
k
and
d
, such that if
c_{1}t=-r^{2}
, then
\partial_{r}p(x,t)\leq-c_{2}r^{2k-1}
.

Proof.

Setting
s=r^{2}/(-t)
, we have, by homogeneity and radial symmetry,

(9.6)

p(x,t)=(-t)^{k}P(s),

where
P
is a polynomial of degree
k
in one variable (which depends only on
k,d
). Computing the radial derivative, we have

(9.7)

\partial_{r}p(x,t)=2r^{-1}(-t)^{k}P^{\prime}(s)s.

It is then enough to show that
-c_{2}:=P^{\prime}(c_{1})<0
for some
c_{1}>0
. Since
p
is caloric, it follows that
P
satisfies

(9.8)

sP^{\prime\prime}(s)+(d-s/2)P^{\prime}(s)+kP(s)=0.

Differentiating this equation, we have that
F=P^{\prime}
satisfies

(9.9)

sF^{\prime\prime}(s)+(d+1-s/2)F^{\prime}(s)+(k-1/2)F(s)=0,

or, equivalently,

(9.10)

(s^{d+1}e^{-s/2}F^{\prime}(s))^{\prime}=-(k-1/2)s^{d}e^{-s/2}F(s),\quad s\in(0,\infty).

The map
W(s)=s^{d+1}e^{-s/2}F^{\prime}(s)
satisfies
W(0)=W(+\infty)=0
, with
W\not\equiv 0
in
(0,\infty)
. Therefore,
W^{\prime}
must change sign in
(0,\infty)
. Since
W^{\prime}(s)=-(k-1/2)s^{d}e^{-s/2}P^{\prime}(s)
, it follows that
P^{\prime}(c_{1})<0
for some
c_{1}\in(0,\infty),
as wanted.
∎

Finally, we show that, for radial initial data, the global solutions constructed in
[
KK24
, Thm. 9.3]
exhibit, at their extinction time, a singular point with first blow up
(-t)^{+}
and quadratic second blow-up. We remark that, by a simple maximum principle argument, these solutions can be shown to be radially decreasing, which yields an entire family of radial solutions for which the following result applies.

Proposition 9.4
.

Let
w=w(r,t)
be a radial solution to (
1.2
) with
\partial_{r}w\leq 0
, and assume that
t=0
is the extinction time. Then
(0,0)\in\Sigma_{d}^{2}(w)
, i.e. the first blowup at
(0,0)
is
-t
and the second blow-up has degree
2
.

Proof.

On each time slice (in the positivity set)
\Delta w=1+\partial_{t}w
. If
\partial_{t}w>-1
on the time slice (in the positivity set) then
\Delta w>0
which is a contradiction to the fact that
w
achieves its maximum at
0
. Thus on each time slice (before the vanishing time)
\sup-w_{t}>1
which means the blow-up must be
-t
at
(0,0)
.

Assume, by contradiction, that
w
has a second blow-up of degree
m\geq 3
, and let
q
be the homogeneous polynomial of degree
m
given by Lemma
7.4
. Since
w
is radial,
q
must be radial, and, in particular,
m=2k
for some
k\geq 2.
Since
w
is radially decreasing, we must have, for each
t<0
,
\max w(\cdot,t)=w(0,t)
and, in particular,
\Delta w(0,t)\leq 0
. By Lemma
7.4
(and interior estimates for the heat equation), there exists an
\alpha>0
such that

(9.11)

0\geq\Delta w(0,-r^{2})=\Delta q(0,-r^{2})+O(|r|^{2k-2+\alpha}),\quad r_{0}>r>0.

Since
\Delta q(x,t)
has parabolic degree
2k-2
, the above implies that
\Delta q(0,t)\leq 0
. As such we infer that there exists
a>0
such that
\frac{\partial^{2k}q}{\partial r^{2k}}=-a
. But then, letting
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
be the constants of Lemma
9.3
, we have once more by Lemma
7.4
, for small
r>0
,

(9.12)

0\geq\partial_{r}w(r,-c_{1}r^{2})=q_{r}(r,-c_{1}r^{2})+O(r^{2k-1+\alpha})\geq ac_{2}r^{2k-1}+O(r^{2k-1+\alpha}).

This yields a contradiction for
r
sufficiently small.

∎

The existence of these radial solutions also allows us to build solutions for which
\pi_{x}(\Sigma_{d}^{2})
is locally infinite and for which
\pi_{t}(\Sigma_{d}^{2})
has an accumulation point at the initial time.

Example 9.5
.

We construct a global solution
w:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}
to (
1.2
) such that
\{w(x,0)>0\}\subset B_{3}
and
\pi_{x}(\Sigma_{d}^{2}(w))\cap B_{2}(0)
has infinite cardinality. Let
u(x,t)
be a radial solution guaranteed by Proposition
9.4
. There is a time
t_{0}<0
(which depends on
u
) such that
\{x\mid u(x,t_{0})>0\}=B_{1}(0)
. If
e\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
let
p_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}2^{-k}e
so that
B_{2^{-n}}(p_{n})\cap B_{2^{-m}}(p_{m})=\emptyset
when
m\neq n
and
B_{2^{-n}}(p_{n})\subset B_{3}

Define

w(x,t):=\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}2^{-2n}u(2^{n}(x-p_{n}),2^{2n}(t-t_{0})).

We claim that this is the desired solution. Indeed each
2^{-2n}u(2^{n}(x-p_{n}),2^{2n}t-t_{0})
solves the obstacle problem and is supported in
B_{2^{-n}}(p_{n})\times(0,-2^{-2n}t_{0})
. Since the supports are disjoint (by construction) the sum is a solution to (
1.2
). Furthermore, since the supports are disjoint, Proposition
9.4
shows that
\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\{(p_{n},-2^{-2n}t_{0})\}=\Sigma_{d}^{2}(w)
. The claim follows.

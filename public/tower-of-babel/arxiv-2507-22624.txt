Configurational density of states of power-law potentials and the virial theorem in steady states
Sergio Davis

Abstract
In this brief note, the configurational density of states of a system of particles interacting via power-law pair potentials is computed exactly. The result is consistent with a constant microcanonical heat capacity. The well-known form of the virial theorem for this class of systems is recovered using only the obtained configurational density of states, and shown to be valid beyond the canonical and microcanonical ensembles, in general steady states.



1
Introduction

The virial theorem
[
1
]
is a well-known result of equilibrium statistical mechanics. For a system of
N
classical particles with Hamiltonian

\mathcal{H}=K(\bm{p}_{1},\ldots,\bm{p}_{N})+\Phi(\bm{r}_{1},\ldots,\bm{r}_{N}),

(1)

with

K(\bm{p}_{1},\ldots,\bm{p}_{N})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bm{p}_{i}^{2}}{2m_{i}}

(2)

the total kinetic energy of the system and
\Phi
the potential energy including arbitrary interactions, one of the many forms of the virial theorem is the equality

\big<K\big>_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\left<\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bm{r}_{i}\cdot\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\bm{r}_{i}}\right>_{0}

(3)

where the notation
\big<{\scriptstyle\bullet}\big>_{0}
denotes either canonical or microcanonical equilibrium expectation values. If the interaction potential
\Phi
is the sum of pair interactions, that is, if

\Phi(\bm{r}_{1},\ldots,\bm{r}_{N})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j\neq i}\varphi\big(\big|\bm{r}_{j}-\bm{r}_{i}\big|\big),

(4)

where
\varphi
is a function only of the interatomic distance, and is of the form

\varphi(r)=\varphi_{0}\,r^{\gamma}

(5)

with
\varphi_{0}
and
\gamma
constants, then it is possible to show that

\big<K\big>_{0}=\frac{\gamma}{2}\big<\Phi\big>_{0}.

(6)

2
Configurational density of states

Let
\mathcal{D}(\phi)
denote the configurational density of states (CDOS) associated to the interaction potential
\Phi
, defined by

\mathcal{D}(\phi)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\int d\bm{r}_{1}\ldots d\bm{r}_{N}\;\delta\big(\Phi(\bm{r}_{1},\ldots,\bm{r}_{N})-\phi\big).

(7)

Replacing the potential energy
\Phi
with (
4
) and (
5
), we can write

\mathcal{D}(\phi)=\int d\bm{r}_{1}\ldots d\bm{r}_{N}\;\delta\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j\neq i}\varphi_{0}\big|\bm{r}_{j}-\bm{r}_{i}\big|^{\gamma}-\phi\right),

(8)

and by extracting
\phi
out of the Dirac delta we have

\mathcal{D}(\phi)=\frac{1}{\phi}\int d\bm{r}_{1}\ldots d\bm{r}_{N}\;\delta\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\varphi_{0}}{\phi}\big|\bm{r}_{j}-\bm{r}_{i}\big|^{\gamma}\right),

(9)

which suggest the change of variables

\bm{u}_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\left(\frac{\varphi_{0}}{\phi}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\bm{r}_{i}

(10)

in order to extract the full dependence of
\mathcal{D}(\phi)
on
\phi
. In fact, writing the integral in terms of
\bm{u}_{1},\ldots,\bm{u}_{N}
we have

\mathcal{D}(\phi)=\frac{1}{\varphi_{0}}\left(\frac{\phi}{\varphi_{0}}\right)^{\frac{3N}{\gamma}-1}\;\left[\int d\bm{u}_{1}\ldots d\bm{u}_{N}\;\delta\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j\neq i}\big|\bm{u}_{j}-\bm{u}_{i}\big|^{\gamma}\right)\right]

(11)

\mathcal{D}(\phi)=\frac{1}{\varphi_{0}}D_{N}(\gamma)\,\left(\frac{\phi}{\varphi_{0}}\right)^{\frac{3N}{\gamma}-1},

(12)

where
D_{N}(\gamma)
is a constant which does not affect the thermodynamic properties. Immediately we see that, for
\gamma=2
, this result agrees with the CDOS of a system of
N
harmonic oscillators.

3
The virial theorem

The virial theorem, as in (
6
), will be derived using (
12
) for an arbitrary steady state of the form

P(\bm{R},\bm{P}|S)=\rho\big(K(\bm{P})+\Phi(\bm{R});S),

(13)

where
\rho
is the
ensemble function
,
\bm{R}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=(\bm{r}_{1},\ldots,\bm{r}_{N})
,
\bm{P}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=(\bm{p}_{1},\ldots,\bm{p}_{N})
and
K(\bm{P})
is given by (
2
). The joint distribution
of kinetic and potential energy in the state
S
is then given by

\begin{split}P(k,\phi|S)&=\Big<\delta(K(\bm{P})-k)\,\delta(\Phi(\bm{R})-\phi)\Big>_{S}\\
&=\rho(k+\phi;S)\left[\int d\bm{P}\delta\big(K(\bm{P})-k\big)\right]\left[\int d\bm{R}\delta\big(\Phi(\bm{R})-\phi\big)\right],\end{split}

(14)

that is, we have

P(k,\phi|S)=\rho\big(k+\phi;S\big)\Omega_{K}(k;N)\mathcal{D}(\phi)

(15)

with
\Omega_{K}(k;N)
the kinetic density of states, given by

\Omega_{K}(k)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\int d\bm{p}_{1}\ldots d\bm{p}_{N}\,\delta\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bm{p}_{i}^{2}}{2m_{i}}-k\right)=W_{N}\,k^{\frac{3N}{2}-1}

(16)

with
W_{N}
a constant whose value is

W_{N}=\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\frac{3N}{2}\right)}\prod_{i=1}^{N}(2\pi m_{i})^{\frac{3}{2}}.

(17)

Applying the conjugate variables theorem
[
2
,
3
]
to the joint distribution
P(k,\phi|S)
we have

\displaystyle\left<\frac{\partial\omega}{\partial K}\right>_{S}

\displaystyle=\left<\omega\left(\beta_{F}-\frac{3N-2}{2K}\right)\right>_{S},

(18)

\displaystyle\left<\frac{\partial\omega}{\partial\Phi}\right>_{S}

\displaystyle=\left<\omega\left(\beta_{F}-\frac{3N-\gamma}{\gamma\Phi}\right)\right>_{S}

(19)

for
\omega=\omega(K,\Phi)
an arbitrary differentiable function of
K
and
\Phi
, and where we have defined the
fundamental inverse temperature

\beta_{F}(E;S)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial E}\ln\rho(E;S).

(20)

Using (
18
) with
\omega(\phi,k)=\phi\cdot k
, we have

\big<\beta_{F}\,\Phi\,K\big>_{S}=\frac{3N}{2}\big<\Phi\big>_{S}

(21)

while (
19
) with
\omega(\phi,k)=\phi\cdot k
yields

\big<\beta_{F}\,\Phi\,K\big>_{S}=\frac{3N}{\gamma}\big<K\big>_{S},

(22)

thus by equating the right-hand sides of (
21
) and (
22
) we prove (
6
) in the more general form

\big<K\big>_{S}=\frac{\gamma}{2}\big<\Phi\big>_{S},

(23)

valid for any steady state where (
13
) holds. The total density of states is given by

\begin{split}\Omega(E)&=\int d\bm{R}d\bm{P}\,\delta\big(K(\bm{P})+\Phi(\bm{R})-E\big)\\
&=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\phi\int_{0}^{\infty}dk\,\Omega_{K}(k)\mathcal{D}(\phi)\delta(k+\phi-E)\\
&=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\phi\,\Omega_{K}(E-\phi)\mathcal{D}(\phi)\\
&=\frac{W_{N}D_{N}(\gamma)}{\varphi_{0}}\int_{0}^{E}d\phi\,(E-\phi)^{\frac{3N}{2}-1}\phi^{\frac{3N}{\gamma}-1}=\frac{W_{N}\,D_{N}(\gamma)}{\varphi_{0}}\,B\Big(\frac{3N}{2},\frac{3N}{\gamma}\Big)E^{\frac{3N}{2}+\frac{3N}{\gamma}-1}\end{split}

(24)

with
B(a,b)
the beta function. The microcanonical inverse temperature is then

\frac{1}{k_{B}T(E)}=\frac{\partial}{\partial E}\ln\Omega(E)=\frac{1}{E}\left(\frac{3N}{2}+\frac{3N}{\gamma}-1\right),

(25)

so the system has constant microcanonical heat capacity

C_{E}=\left(\frac{3N}{2}+\frac{3N}{\gamma}-1\right)k_{B}.

(26)

The stability of the entire system in the canonical ensemble requires
C_{E}>0
. This is possible for any positive
\gamma
, as well as for negative
\gamma
if

\gamma<-\frac{6N}{3N-2},

(27)

thus ruling out the gravitational potential which has
\gamma=-1
.

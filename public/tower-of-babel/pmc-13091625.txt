A Cross-Sectional Survey on Musculoskeletal Pain Among Professional and Non-Professional Gamers in Saudi Arabia: Associations with Gaming Genre, Duration, and Ergonomic Factors [Response To Letter]

Dear editor

We sincerely thank the authors for their interest in our work and for their thoughtful and constructive comments regarding our recently published article,

A Cross-Sectional Survey on Musculoskeletal Pain Among Professional and Non-Professional Gamers in Saudi Arabia: Associations with Gaming Genre, Duration, and Ergonomic Factors.

We appreciate the opportunity to clarify the points raised.

Minor discrepancies in Table 1, particularly in the reported frequency and duration values, were due to typographical errors during manuscript preparation. The category structure reflects commonly used interval grouping (eg., discrete ranges followed by values exceeding a specified threshold), and the noted inconsistencies in ordering or notation are limited to formatting. Regarding Table 7, the reported odds ratio (OR) for the association between puzzle games and neck pain was affected by a transcription error. Verification against the original dataset and SPSS outputs confirms that the analyses were conducted correctly. The correct value is OR = 1.890 (95% CI: 1.184–3.016), whereas the value currently shown in Table 7 (OR = 1.189) reflects a transcription error and will be updated accordingly by way of a corrigendum. These issues are limited to reporting and formatting and do not affect the statistical validity, consistency, or interpretation of the findings. Coordination with the editorial team will ensure accurate presentation of these values.

Regarding the interpretation of device-related effects, device type was significant in the bivariate analysis but not in the multivariable model. The discussion aimed to contextualize these findings within the broader literature, where device-related factors have been highlighted as important contributors to musculoskeletal outcomes. The use of the term “outweigh” may be interpreted as implying a stronger comparative conclusion; however, the intention was to reflect the emphasis placed on device-related factors in prior studies, including those with differing findings, rather than to draw a direct inference from the adjusted models of the present study. Accordingly, the discussion was intended to situate the current findings within the existing body of evidence rather than to assert a direct comparison.

Limitations related to the use of a non-probability convenience sample and self-reported musculoskeletal symptoms are acknowledged. These were clearly stated in the manuscript and are common in cross-sectional studies involving emerging or hard-to-reach populations. Future studies using probability-based sampling and objective assessment methods are warranted to further strengthen methodological rigor.

Gender may influence musculoskeletal outcomes. As the official Esports Federation registry included only male professional gamers at the time of data collection, comparisons were restricted to males to maintain comparability between groups and minimize confounding. This approach ensured that observed differences were not driven by gender imbalance but reflected the variables under investigation. Although this limits generalizability to female professional gamers, it represents an appropriate methodological decision given the available population and was transparently reported. Future research with more gender-balanced samples is encouraged.

Finally, causal relationships cannot be inferred from cross-sectional data. The study was explicitly designed to examine associations rather than establish causality, and this distinction was clearly stated throughout the manuscript. As such, this reflects a recognized limitation inherent to the study design rather than a study-specific concern. Longitudinal or prospective cohort designs would provide stronger evidence regarding temporal relationships.

In conclusion, these clarifications enhance the clarity and transparency of the work while reaffirming the validity and robustness of the study findings. The issues raised are limited to reporting and interpretation and do not affect the underlying analyses or conclusions. This exchange contributes constructively to the growing body of literature on musculoskeletal health in gaming populations.

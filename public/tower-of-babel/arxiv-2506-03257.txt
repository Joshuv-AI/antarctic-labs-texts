Effective description of Nelson-Barr models and the theta parameter
Gustavo H. S. Alves, Celso C. Nishi

Abstract
Nelson-Barr theories solve the strong CP problem with CP spontaneously broken at Λ cp \Lambda_{\rm cp} and transmitted to the CP conserving version of the SM through vector-like quarks (VLQs). For an arbitrary number of CP breaking scalars and VLQs, we perform the full one-loop matching calculations at Λ cp \Lambda_{\rm cp} up to dimension five operators and the relevant matching calculations at the VLQ scale relevant to tracking the contributions to the parameter θ ¯ \bar{\theta} . In the EFT after the CP breaking scalars have been integrated out, we confirm that the running of θ ¯ \bar{\theta} at one-loop is induced by CP violating dimension five operators and similarly by dimension six operator after matching to the SMEFT. In the latter, an additional contribution enters in the matching to θ ¯ \bar{\theta} at tree-level due to a dimension five operator. Analyzing the experimental constraint from θ ¯ \bar{\theta} for generic settings, we see that a large separation between Λ cp \Lambda_{\rm cp} and the VLQ scale already suppresses the one-loop contribution to θ ¯ \bar{\theta} sufficiently. We confirm that a simple extension based on a nonconventional CP has vanishing one-loop contribution to θ ¯ \bar{\theta} . Part of our results can be also applied to generic VLQ extensions of the SM.

Functional matching

One of the key concepts in constructing effective theories is matching. The principle behind matching is that both the
full theory and the EFT should describe the same physics at the matching scale. To achieve this, we determine the
Wilson
coefficients of the EFT as functions of the fundamental parameters of the higher-energy theory, ensuring that the
observable results of both theories align on the matching scale
Cohen:2022tir
.

In this work, we will use functional matching to determine both the contributions to the effective theory at the
tree level and at the one-loop.
The method at one-loop makes use of the recently developed covariant derivative expansion (CDE)
method

;
Henning:2016lyp
where
manifestly gauge covariant results are automatic.
We will follow the method of Ref.
cohen.zhang:20
closely.

We review the method using two (sets of) scalar fields: a light field
\phi
and a heavy field
\Phi

We first split the fields into the classical
background configurations,
\Phi_{\mathrm{B}}
and
\phi_{\mathrm{B}}

\Phi^{\prime}

and
\phi^{\prime}

\Phi=\Phi_{\mathrm{B}}+\Phi^{\prime},\quad\phi=\phi_{\mathrm{B}}+\phi^{\prime}\,.

(18)

The condition for matching can be stated as

\Gamma_{\text{L,UV}}\left[\phi_{\mathrm{B}}\right]=\Gamma_{\text{EFT}}\left[\phi_{\mathrm{B}}\right]\,,

(19)

where
\Gamma_{\mathrm{L,UV}}\left[\phi_{\mathrm{B}}\right]
is the one-light-particle-irreducible (1LPI) effective
action of the UV theory that only includes the lights fields

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mathrm{L,UV}}\left[\phi_{\mathrm{B}}\right]

\displaystyle\equiv-i\log Z_{\mathrm{UV}}\left[J=0,j\right]-\int\mathrm{d}^{d}xj\phi_{\mathrm{B}}\,,

(20)

\displaystyle\simeq\int\mathrm{d}^{d}x\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{UV}}\left(\Phi_{\mathrm{c}}\left[\phi_{\mathrm{B}}\right],\phi_{\mathrm{B}}\right)+\frac{i}{2}\log\operatorname{det}\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{UV}}\left[\Phi_{\mathrm{c}}\left[\phi_{\mathrm{B}}\right],\phi_{\mathrm{B}}\right],

VI.4
Broken phase of SMEFT

It is well known that the SMEFT with dimension six operators loses the characteristic property (at tree-level) of
the SM in which both the fermion mass matrices and the fermion-Higgs couplings are proportional to the respective
Yukawa matrices
buchmuller.wyler
;
jenkins.manohar:3
.
For a down-type singlet VLQ, the induced operator
\mathcal{O}_{dH}=\bar{q}_{L}Hd_{R}|H|^{2}
modifies the relation between the
down quark mass matrix
M_{d}
and the Yukawa coupling
Y^{d}
as

[M^{d}]^{rs}=\frac{v_{T}}{\sqrt{2}}\bigg([Y^{d}]^{rs}-\mbox{\large$\tfrac{1}{2}$}v^{2}[C_{dH}]^{rs}\bigg)\,.

(100)

In the context of the strong CP problem, the second term leads to an additional contribution to
\bar{\theta}
at
tree
level
.

Before analyzing the SMEFT case, let us discuss the case of the VLQ-EFT (
79
).
When the VLQs lie hypothetically at the electroweak scale, there is no need to integrate them out and the full mass
matrix of down-type quarks after the Higgs gains the vev

is of size
(3+n)
:

\mathscr{M}^{d+B}=\begin{pmatrix}\mbox{\large$\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$}Y^{d}v&\mbox{\large$\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$}Y^{B}v\cr-\mbox{\large$\tfrac{1}{2}$}C_{BdHH}v^{2}&M^{B}-\mbox{\large$\tfrac{1}{2}$}C_{BBHH}v^{2}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(101)

In the NB theory, the Wilson coefficients above are related by (
40
) and one can check that

\arg\det\mathscr{M}^{d+B}=0\,,

(102)

at linear order in the Wilson coefficients considering
\arg\det Y^{d}=\arg\det M^{B}=0
.
This leads to the correct result that
\bar{\theta}=0

at tree level
in the NB construction.

Now, if we integrate the VLQs out of theory as in Sec.
VI.1
, the coefficient
C_{BdHH}
contributes
to
\bar{\theta}
through (
100
) but the compensating contribution of
C_{BBHH}
disappears
from the SMEFT up to dimension six.
We end up with a nonzero contribution to
\bar{\theta}
at

, conflicting with the original Nelson-Barr
UV theory.

Therefore, the integrating out of the VLQs from the VLQ-EFT (
78
) to match onto the SMEFT needs to be
performed after the chiral rephasing

B_{aR}\to e^{i\theta_{B}^{\prime}/n}B_{aR}\,,

(103)

which induces

\theta(M_{\rm VLQ})=\theta_{B}^{\prime}=-\arg\det\Big(M^{B}-\mbox{\large$\tfrac{1}{2}$}v^{2}C_{BBHH}\Big)\,,

(104)

to the QCD theta term.
At leading order, with
\arg\det M^{B}=0
, this is

\theta_{B}^{\prime}=\frac{v^{2}}{2}\im\Tr\Big[{M^{B}}^{-1}C_{BBHH}\Big]\,.

(105)

The rephasing (
103
), which is of order
v^{2}/(M^{B}\Lambda_{\rm cp})
, also feeds into

M^{B}\to M^{B}e^{i\theta_{B}^{\prime}/n}\,,\quad Y^{B}\to Y^{B}e^{i\theta_{B}^{\prime}/n}\,,

(106)

in the VLQ-EFT but their effects are cancelled in our results because they only appear in combinations like

or
Y^{B}{M^{B}}^{\dagger}

The remaining contribution in the SMEFT from the down quark mass matrix (
100
) is

\theta_{d}^{\prime}=-\arg\det M^{d}\approx\frac{v^{2}}{2}\im\Tr[{Y^{d}}^{-1}C_{dH}]\,,

(107)

considering again that
\arg\det Y^{d}=0

In NB theories, the coefficient
C_{dH}
is given in Table
3
and the nontrivial contribution to

\theta_{d}^{\prime}

C_{BdHH}

The relation (

M_{\rm VLQ}

(108)

It is important to emphasize that the matching condition (

the SMEFT is valid generically even if the original UV theory is not a NB theory.
In the SMEFT, the contribution from the down quark mass matrix is also generically given by (
107

an analogous contribution may also arise from the up quark mass matrix.

VIII
Summary

We perform a two stage matching calculation for generic Nelson-Barr theories containing an arbitrary number of CP
breaking scalars and VLQs at a high scale.
We integrate out the scalars at the CP breaking scale
\Lambda_{\rm cp}
and subsequently the VLQs at
M_{\rm VLQ}\ll\Lambda_{\rm cp}
.
The first matching is performed at one-loop up to dimension five operators using the CDE method and the results can be
applied to generic extensions of the SM with VLQs and scalars interacting through (
1
)
with a generic scalar potential and not only to Nelson-Barr theories.
Some contributions come from dimension six operators that are transformed to dimension five due to equations of
motion.
The resulting EFT at this stage is the SM augmented with VLQs and some dimension five operators.
Among these, only the dipole operators arise exclusively at one-loop.
This EFT is then matched onto the SMEFT in the second matching which is performed at tree level and the necessary
one-loop matching is performed to track the leading one-loop contributions to
\bar{\theta}
.
We also obtain the one-loop RGE for
\bar{\theta}
between
\Lambda_{\rm cp}
and
M_{\rm VLQ}
which is nontrivial and is induced by the
dimension five operators.
Several contributions cancel within Nelson-Barr theories due to relations between Yukawas (
15
) or
Wilson coefficients (
40
).
We confirm that the one-loop running of
\bar{\theta}
also happens within the SMEFT induced by dimension six operators.
In the matching for
\bar{\theta}
in the SMEFT, a subtlety arises due to the additional contribution to the quark masses
from dimension six operators misaligned to the Yukawa couplings.
This leads to a contribution to the QCD
\theta
term in the matching to the SMEFT.

The results are then applied to generic settings of the NB scheme and it is verified that a large separation between

\Lambda_{\rm cp}
and
M_{\rm VLQ}
already suppresses the one-loop contributions to
\bar{\theta}
sufficiently at the current level.
Our results are equally applicable to generic VLQ extensions of the SM and we find that

generic complex
coefficients for the dimension five operator
\bar{B}_{L}d_{R}|H|^{2}
is severely constrained
from its contribution to
\bar{\theta}
.
The formulas are also applied to the simplest BBP model
BBP
and the CP4 model
cp4
. For the latter, we
confirm the property that all the one-loop contributions to
\bar{\theta}
vanish.
We also analyze the effects of CP violating dipole operators that gives rise to electric dipole moments and
chromo-electric dipole moments to the quarks.
We confirm that these effects are negligible in the neutron electric dipole moment for a high scale
\Lambda_{\rm cp}
.
The constraint from
\bar{\theta}
dominates over the ones from EDMs and cEDMs of individual quarks, which is usual
as for example in the constraints of CP violating interactions between ALPs and quarks
grinstein:alp
.

Appendix C
Details for the BBP model

Here we detail the BBP model discussed in Sec.
VII.2
.
The mapping of couplings to the general notation of
Sec.
II
is given in Table
5
.

The potential can be divided into three parts

V=V_{H}+V_{S}+V_{HS},

(177)

where
V_{H}
is the Higgs potential of the SM. The potential involving only
S
is

V_{S}=S^{*}S\left[a_{1}+b_{1}S^{*}S\right]+\left(S^{2}+S^{*^{2}}\right)\left[a_{2}+b_{2}S^{*}S\right]+b_{3}\left(S^{4}+S^{*^{4}}\right)\,,

(178)

while the mixed part
V_{HS}
is described by

V_{HS}=(H^{\dagger}H)\left[c_{1}(S^{2}+S^{*^{2}})+c_{2}S^{*}S\right].

(179)

All these coefficients are real due to
the CP symmetry imposed on the Lagrangian. CP symmetry is then broken spontaneously when the scalar
S
acquires a
complex VEV.

\langle 0|S|0\rangle=Ve^{i\alpha}.

(180)

Then we can rewrite the potential
V_{S}
in (
178
) as a function of real
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
as

V_{S}(S_{1},S_{2})=-\frac{1}{2}\mu_{11}S_{1}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\mu_{22}S_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}[\lambda_{11}S_{1}^{4}+\lambda_{22}S_{2}^{4}+2\lambda_{12}S_{1}^{2}S_{2}^{2}],

(181)

Analogously, the mixed potential (
179
) becomes

V_{SH}(S_{1},S_{2})=\frac{1}{2}(H^{\dagger}H)\left[\gamma_{1}S_{1}^{2}+\gamma_{2}S_{2}^{2}\right].

(182)

Because of CP and
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, the potential is invariant by the independent sign flips:
S_{1}\to-S_{1}
and
S_{2}\to-S_{2}
.
Both symmetries are spontaneously broken by the vevs

\langle S_{1}\rangle=u_{1}\,,\quad\langle S_{2}\rangle=u_{2}\,.

(183)

They are given by the solutions of the minimization equations:

\displaystyle\lambda_{11}u_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{12}u^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle=\mu_{11}\,,

(184)

\displaystyle\lambda_{22}u_{2}^{2}+\lambda_{12}u^{2}_{1}

\displaystyle=\mu_{22}\,.

After the spontaneous breaking of CP and
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, the potential of the theory as a function of
excitations
s_{i}=S_{i}-u_{i}
is

\displaystyle V_{S}(s_{1},s_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{11}}{4}s_{1}^{4}+\frac{\lambda_{22}}{4}s_{2}^{4}+\frac{\lambda_{12}}{2}s_{1}^{2}s_{2}^{2}

(185)

\displaystyle+u_{1}\lambda_{11}s^{3}_{1}+u_{2}\lambda_{22}s_{2}^{3}+\lambda_{12}u_{2}s_{1}^{2}s_{2}+\lambda_{12}u_{1}s_{2}^{2}s_{1}

\displaystyle+\lambda_{11}u_{1}^{2}s_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{22}u_{2}^{2}s^{2}_{2}+2\lambda_{12}u_{1}u_{2}s_{1}s_{2}\,.

We can easily extract the mass matrix

M^{2}=2\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{11}u^{2}_{1}&\lambda_{12}u_{1}u_{2}\\
\lambda_{12}u_{1}u_{2}&\lambda_{22}u^{2}_{2}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(186)

whose eigenvalues are

\displaystyle M^{2}_{1}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{11}u^{2}_{1}+\lambda_{22}u^{2}_{2}-\sqrt{\left(\lambda_{11}u^{2}_{1}-\lambda_{22}u^{2}_{2}\right)^{2}+\left(2\lambda_{12}u_{1}u_{2}\right)^{2}}\,,

(187)

\displaystyle M^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{11}u^{2}_{1}+\lambda_{22}u^{2}_{2}+\sqrt{\left(\lambda_{11}u^{2}_{1}-\lambda_{22}u^{2}_{2}\right)^{2}+\left(2\lambda_{12}u_{1}u_{2}\right)^{2}}\,.

The portal with the SM Higgs can be rewritten as the sum

V_{SH}=\sum^{2}_{i=1}\frac{1}{2}(H^{\dagger}H)\left[\gamma_{i}\left(s_{i}^{2}+2u_{i}s_{i}+u_{i}^{2}\right)\right]\,.

(188)

The term involving
u_{i}^{2}
can be absorbed by the redefinition of the quadratic term
|H|^{2}
in the SM Higgs potential.

5
the coefficients of the potential in (
185
)-(

Being a spontaneously broken potential, one can see that the cubic
\lambda^{\prime}_{ijk}
and the quartic
\lambda_{ijkl}

u_{i}

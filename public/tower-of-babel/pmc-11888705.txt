In this paper, the optimal docking pose for each target is chosen and presented.Results:From the results of the study, it can be observed that gatifloxacin and ciprofloxacin have more affinity and interactions with all four targets were analyzed.Conclusions:This study has effectively tested nitroimidazoles and fluoroquinolones comparatively and proposed that fluoroquinolones are more effective for blocking periodontitis.

INTRODUCTION

Of all oral infections, periodontitis ranks the first in prevalence around the globe.[12] Despite its wide prevalence, its existence in oneself is unknown, seldom attended, and often overlooked. This is especially the case in lower economic strata.[34] In those individuals, smoking and alcoholism contribute to worsening of the condition. Numerous risk factors have been shown to influence the progress of periodontitis.[5] Furthermore, the periodontitis is a slow progressing infection and has profound systemic implications.[6]

While periodontitis is a polymicrobial infection, recent studies on oral microbiota have thrown a great deal of light on initial pathogens and opportunistic pathogens. Of these, there is a variation among drug sensitivity, which is further assisted by the biofilm. Of the numerous species of unculturable and culturable flora of the periodontal biofilm, the major species that initiate the disease, in other words, keystone pathogens, are Prophyromonas gingivalis and Actinobacillus actinomycetemcomitans.[78]

Periodontitis has been approached by various directions in the literature, of which four aspects gain prominence in light of currently available evidence. They are gingipain inhibition, fimbrial proteins (FimA) inhibition, interleukin 1 downregulation or blocking, and upregulation of osteoblast differentiation.[91011]

This anaerobic, Gram-negative organism, known as P. gingivalis, has developed its own defenses to thwart the local immune response. Additionally, it has biochemical mechanisms that demonstrate its virulence toward the periodontium.[1213141516] Enzymes called gingipain, trypsin-like cysteine proteinases that promote infection by various microbes in the biofilm, show this increase in virulence.[17] It is crucial to remember that once P. gingivalis lowers local immunity, even nonpathogenic flora can cause infection. Dysbiosis results from this alteration in the microbial population.[181920] The involvement of various species in this process greatly complicates the mechanism.

Furthermore, bacteria have specialized adhesion mechanisms called fimbria that allow them to stick to both biofilm and the host cells. These Fimbriae have been demonstrated to be crucial elements in the facilitation of bacterial interactions with host tissues and subsequent invasion.[21] Therefore, an increase of such fimbrial molecules, in both number and type, directly contributes to the bacteria’s virulence.[22232425]

Increased inflammation brought on by the progression of periodontal disease is regulated by molecules like interleukins.[2627] Periodontal pathogens upregulate the proinflammatory cytokine interleukin 1 (IL-1 β).[2829] Most importantly, IL-1β stimulates bone resorption strongly, which aggravates periodontitis. Therefore, therapeutic approaches to inhibit IL-1β are continually sought after.[3031] Consequently, the progress of periodontal inflammation leads to increased bone loss, shifting the bone homeostasis to an unfavorable side. Therefore, stimulation of osteoblasts will reverse the balance to a favorable side.

To combat periodontal pathogens, nitroimidazoles and fluoroquinolones are frequently used.[32] Regarding the binding of various recently developed nitroimidazoles and fluoroquinolone congeners with the various pathways discussed above, there is a paucity of information in the literature.[333435363738394041424344454647] In this study, molecular docking techniques are used to determine the effectiveness of various commonly used drugs in controlling and eliminating periodontal pathology by binding to the targets.

Molecular docking analysis

Auto Dock version 4 was also used for in silico docking simulations. Virtual screening was performed using the lead molecules’ three-dimensional pharmacophores [Table 1] against a few of the protein targets listed in Table 2. Docking grids were established using 0.375 Å and the pocket-sized measuring maps with grid points of 70 × 70 × 70 Å. Each docking calculation was programmed to run for a maximum of 250,000 energy evaluations, followed by 10 different cycles. The population was limited to 150 people. A translational step of 0.2, a quaternion step of 5, and a torsion step of 5 were all used during the search.[5556] Based on the evaluation of various lead molecule orientations with respect to the target proteins and the interaction study analysis, the best dock pose was chosen. The binding free energy, inhibition constant, electrostatic energy, intermolecular energy, and total interaction surface are all provided by the docking tool. In this paper, the optimal docking pose for each target is chosen and presented.

DISCUSSION

Antimicrobial resistance is a highly complex phenomenon that may significantly be fueled by biofilms.[575859] In comparison to similar bacteria that are planktonic, bacteria living in biofilms can show a 10- to 1000-fold increase in antibiotic resistance.[6061]) In a study on Staphylococcus epidermidis antibiotic resistance in biofilms, for instance, 100% of the isolates were susceptible to the antibiotic vancomycin when tested in a planktonic state. However, when tested from a biofilm, nearly 75% of them were totally resistant to the same antibiotic. The same pattern has been observed for organisms such as Klebsiella pneumoniae, which seems susceptible when tested from an aqueous solution but develops a high level of resistance to some antibiotics when tested from a biofilm.[62]

Point mutations, enzymes, and efflux pumps are examples of common mechanisms in bacteria that cause antibiotic resistance. These mechanisms, however, are not likely to be in charge of the resistance observed in biofilm organisms.[6364] Regarding the antibiotic resistance of bacteria in biofilms, three mechanisms stand out. First of all, structural complexity’s ability to provide resistance at the biofilm surface makes penetration challenging.[65] Second, there are the anaerobic environment, metabolic byproducts, and resistance within biofilm microenvironments.[6667] Third, the antibiotic’s effectiveness is decreased by bacterial cells’ resistance to it as a result of their evasion strategies.[68] As a result, much less antibiotic than anticipated reached the bacteria. In order for a drug to be effective at low doses, it must have a high affinity for and numerous interactions with the target.

The binding free energy or Gibbs free energy is positive when there is no binding detected between the host molecule and its guest.[697071] When it is negative, it implies a spontaneous interaction within the physiological system. The other parameters in the table are summarily represented by the binding free energy.[727374] The number of sites of interaction is directly proportional to binding affinity, and in this study, specific interactions that inhibit the targets are shown.[75]

When binding free energies are compared among the drugs tested, fluoroquinalones had lower free energy or in other words better affinity to gingipain. The strongest interaction was found in delafloxacin and moxifloxacin. For fluoroquinalones, the range was from -8.82 to -6.35 KCal/mol. Nitroimidazoles showed higher binding free energies from -6.18 to -4.61 KCal/mol toward gingipain. Therefore, fluoroquinalones are better ligands for gingipain and not nitroimiadazoles. However, when binding with estrogen receptor b is analyzed, the nitroimidazoles and fluoroquinalones were not as regularly arranged as seen for gingipain; their regions of binding energies overlapped considerably. The least binding energy was seen in ciprofloxacin followed by pretomanid, levofloxacin, and nimorazole. Delaflozacin had the highest energy of binding (-2.5 KCal/mol). With respect to interleukin 1b, the scenario was similar to gingipain, where fluoroquinalones (-7.04 to -4.06 KCal/mol) have lower binding free energies compared to nitroimidazoles (-4.24 to -3.04 KCal/mol), with a small overlap in the region. When binding with FimA was analyzed, considerable overlap was seen in binding free energy among fluoroquinalones and nitroimidazoles. The least binding energy was seen in ciprofloxacin, followed by azanidazole, delafloxacin, dimetridazole, levofloxacin, gemifloxacin, and metronidazole.

With regard to interaction between drug and targets, the number of amino acid interactions between them is of relevance in this study. The interactions marked in this study are those that have potential to inhibit the molecule from functioning. When such interactions are considered, the best inhibitor can be identified. When listed drugs are analyzed for number of interactions, it followed the same trend as binding free energy in the case of gingipain. Fluoroquinalones have more amino acid interactions compared to nitroimidazoles. When the same was analyzed for estrogen receptor b, considerable overlap was found between fluoroquinalones and nitroimidazoles. However, the most number of interactions was seen in gatifloxacin, ciprofloxacin, and delafloxacin. In the case of IL-1b, an overlap of values was found. In the descending order of number of interactions, drugs can be listed as gemifloxacin, ciprofloxacin, delafloxacin, azanidazole, gatifloxacin, and moxifloxacin. In the case of FimA, gatifloxacin had the maximum interactions, followed by moxifloxacin, ciprofloxacin, azanidazole, and levofloxacin.

From the results of the study, it can be observed that gatifloxacin and ciprofloxacin have more affinity and interactions with all four targets analyzed. With respect to nitroimidazoles, azanidazole followed these drugs mentioned above. In the past, fluoroquinalones have been used against Actinobacillus actinmycetemcomitans successfully.[7677] Recently, fluoroquinalones, especially ciprofloxacin, have been analyzed in a meta-analysis[4778] and reported to have therapeutic benefits. While the information on older drugs is available, this study has thrown light on prospects of newer congeners. Commonly, nitroimidazoles are used for treating periodontal infections.[798081] The reason for its common use is its activity against aneroblic flora of plaque. However, this study has shown that newer fluoroquinalones have higher potential against inhibiting gingipain and fimA.

CONCLUSIONS

From the results of the study, it can be concluded at the computational biology level that newer fluoroquinolones have more affinity to gingipain and Fim A protein and can potentially inhibit them more effectively than nitroimidazoles. Of the analyzed candidate leads, gatifloxacin and ciprofloxacin stand out among others. This study has effectively tested nitroimidazoles and fluoroquinolones comparatively and proposed that fluoroquinolones are more effective for blocking periodontitis. In the clinical aspect, this result can set the new standards for treatment of periodontal pathology.

Question Tokens Deserve More Attention: Enhancing Large Language Models without Training through Step-by-Step Reading and Question Attention Recalibration
Feijiang Han, Hengtao Cui, Licheng Guo, Zhiyuan Lyu

Abstract
Large Language Models (LLMs) often struggle with tasks that require deep understanding of complex questions, especially when faced with long-range dependencies or multi-step reasoning. This work investigates the limitations of current LLMs in question comprehension and identifies three insights: (1) repeating question tokens improves comprehension by increasing attention to question regions, (2) increased backward dependencies negatively affect performance due to unidirectional attentional constraints, and (3) recalibrating attentional mechanisms to prioritize question-relevant regions improves performance. Based on these findings, we first propose a family of prompt-based strategies - Step-by-Step Reading (SSR), SSR+, and SSR++ - that guide LLMs to incrementally process question tokens and align their reasoning with the input structure. These methods significantly improve performance, with SSR++ achieving state-of-the-art results on several benchmarks: 96.66% on GSM8K, 94.61% on ASDiv, and 76.28% on AQuA. Second, we introduce a training-free attention recalibration mechanism that dynamically adjusts attention distributions during inference to emphasize question-relevant regions. This method improves the accuracy of LLaMA 3.1-8B on AQuA by 5.17% without changing model parameters or input prompts. Taken together, our results highlight the importance of structured prompt design and attention optimization in improving LLM comprehension, providing lightweight yet effective tools for improving performance in various NLP tasks.

Introduction

1.1
Task Overview and Relevance

Large Language Models (LLMs) have transformed natural language processing (NLP), delivering strong performance across tasks such as text generation, question answering, and logical reasoning. However, they often underperform on problems that require a nuanced understanding of input questions, especially when the task involves multi-step reasoning or complex dependencies
(
Kuratov et al., 2024
)
. Unlike humans, who re-read and integrate information to build a coherent mental model, LLMs operate with single-pass decoding and unidirectional attention, limiting their ability to holistically interpret questions
(
Xu et al., 2024
)
.

To address this, various prompting strategies have been proposed. We categorize them into two major types: those focused on
question understanding
(e.g., RE2
(
Xu et al., 2024
)
, Echo
(
Mekala et al., 2024
)
, RaR
(
Deng et al., 2023
)
) and those targeting the
problem-solving process
(e.g., Chain-of-Thought
(
Wei et al., 2022
)
, Decompose
(
Khot et al., 2023
)
). While the success of CoT-style prompting has been widely studied, the mechanisms behind question-focused prompts remain poorly understood. Existing explanations often rely on surface-level accuracy comparisons or analogies to human reasoning, which fall short of revealing the underlying model behaviors.

1.3
Motivation and Contributions

Despite recent advances, LLMs still struggle with human-like question understanding—particularly in tasks requiring multi-hop reasoning or context integration. We address this limitation from two complementary perspectives:

Prompt Design.
We introduce a family of structured prompts that guide LLMs to incrementally read and reflect on question content:

•

SSR (Step-by-Step Reading):
Let’s read the question step by step.

•

SSR+:
Let’s read the question step by step. Then refer to each step when answering.

•

SSR++:
Let’s read the question step by step and understand each sentence again with the sentences after it. Then refer to the corresponding steps when answering.

These strategies consistently outperform existing methods. SSR++, in particular, achieves state-of-the-art performance on multiple benchmarks, including 96.66% on GSM8K. Figure
2
illustrates how our approach combines fine-grained question token repetition with the structured logic of CoT prompting to yield accurate, interpretable outputs.

Attention Recalibration.
We further propose a lightweight, training-free mechanism that dynamically reweights attention during inference to prioritize question-relevant tokens. This recalibration operates without modifying prompts or model parameters. When applied to LLaMA 3.1-8B, it yields a 5.5% gain on the AQuA benchmark. (Details in Appendix
4.4
).

Together, these contributions deepen our understanding of LLM limitations and introduce practical, low-cost tools to improve model reasoning in real-world NLP scenarios.

Related Work

2.1
Prompt Engineering

Prompt engineering has become a critical area for improving the reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs). Rather than relying solely on architectural advancements or larger datasets, these methods focus on guiding models to better interpret and process input questions. Several innovative prompt strategies have demonstrated measurable improvements in reasoning performance.

The
Chain-of-Thought (CoT)
approach
(
Wei et al., 2022
)
, using the prompt "Let’s think step by step," encourages explicit intermediate reasoning steps, making problem-solving more systematic and interpretable. Building on this, the
Decompose
method
(
Khot et al., 2023
)
breaks questions into sub-problems with the prompt: "Decompose the question into several sub-questions, then answer each sub-question and give the final answer." Similarly,
Plan and Solve (PS)

(
Wang et al., 2023
)
structures reasoning with "Let’s first understand the question and devise a plan. Then, let’s carry out the plan and solve the question," improving logical reasoning by separating planning and execution phases.

Beyond reasoning frameworks, other approaches enhance query comprehension.
EchoPrompt

(
Mekala et al., 2024
)
encourages restating the query with "Let’s repeat the question before answering," reducing ambiguity and improving alignment with user intent.
Re-Reading (RE)

(
Xu et al., 2024
)
, with the prompt "Read the question again: + [question text]," revisits the question for deeper understanding, ensuring important details are not overlooked.
Rephrase and Respond (RaR)

(
Deng et al., 2023
)
emphasizes reformulating the question with "Rephrase and expand the question, and respond," addressing ambiguities and improving semantic alignment. These strategies demonstrate that improving question comprehension is foundational to enhancing LLM reasoning. For example,
Wang et al., 2023
showed that over 60% of LLM errors on the GSM8K dataset stem from failures in semantic understanding, underscoring the importance of improving how models interpret queries.

2.2
Observations Regarding LLMs’ Attention

The attention mechanism, central to the performance of LLMs, remains an area with many open questions, especially regarding how attention distributions impact model behavior. Early studies focused on smaller transformer models.
Clark et al., 2019
visualized attention patterns in BERT
(
Devlin et al., 2018
)
, identifying biases in attention heads, while
Vig, 2019
further explored localized relevance within attention mechanisms. Additionally,
Ling et al., 2017
revealed that the association between word tokens and class labels significantly affects attention scores.

With larger LLMs, recent studies have highlighted new complexities.
Kou et al., 2023
observed that attention distributions in LLMs often diverge from human-like patterns, and
Zhang et al., 2023
demonstrated that increasing attention scores for specific tokens can enhance instruction-following capabilities. These findings point to the existence of phenomena like "attention sinks," where certain tokens receive disproportionately high attention despite minimal semantic relevance
(
Xiao et al., 2023
)
.

To address these challenges,
Yu et al., 2024
proposed the Attention Calibration Technique (ACT), a training-free method that dynamically adjusts attention distributions during inference. By mitigating excessive attention sinks, ACT achieved up to a 7.30% improvement in accuracy for models like Llama-30B across multiple benchmarks. These advancements underscore the need for further exploration of the relationship between attention distributions and task performance, as well as the development of systematic strategies for optimizing attention mechanisms.

3.2
Evaluation Metric

Building on recent advances in prompt engineering methodologies
(
Wei et al., 2022
;
Khot et al., 2023
;
Wang et al., 2023
;
Xu et al., 2024
;
Deng et al., 2023
;
Mekala et al., 2024
)
, we adopt
accuracy
as the primary evaluation metric. Accuracy measures the proportion of correct predictions out of the total evaluated instances and is computed as follows:

\text{Accuracy}=\frac{\text{Number of Correct Predictions}}{\text{Total Number of Questions}}

To enhance robustness, we utilize a majority voting mechanism over three independent predictions, selecting the result that occurs most frequently as the final answer. A prediction is considered correct only if it matches the ground truth answer exactly.

3.3
LLM Settings

In our experiments, we configure the system prompt to be empty and rely solely on the user prompt for guiding the model’s behavior. This ensures that the results are entirely driven by the provided prompt without external context influencing the output.

To optimize processing efficiency, we utilize 6 parallel threads and 6 API endpoints to handle the questions concurrently. This parallelization significantly reduces the total processing time while maintaining consistency in the evaluation.

4.2
Extensions

Q1: Why Do Re-reading or Rephrasing Questions Improve LLM Performance?

Xu et al., 2024
suggest that the unidirectional attention mechanism in decoder-only models limits the ability of LLMs to fully understand backward dependencies within a question. Backward dependencies occur when understanding earlier parts of the question requires referencing information that appears later in the text. For example, in mathematical word problems, later conditions or corrections can redefine earlier assumptions.

As illustrated in Figure
3
, when attention is computed during the first pass, tokens in the question cannot access future tokens. However, during the second reading, tokens in the repeated question can attend to all tokens from the initial pass, enabling better comprehension of backward dependencies and improving performance.

Figure 3:
Illustration of repeated question tokens enabling backward dependency comprehension. In the first pass, tokens cannot attend to future tokens, while in the second pass, tokens can access the entire context, including earlier and later tokens.

Wang et al., 2023

semantic misunderstanding errors
,
calculation errors
, and

. Using the GSM8K dataset, we conducted a detailed error analysis to compare the performance of Zero-shot CoT and SSR++. The results are presented in Figure

Our findings reveal that CoT exhibits a high number of semantic misunderstanding errors, which significantly limit LLM reasoning performance. In contrast, SSR++ effectively reduces errors across all three categories, with the largest reduction observed in semantic misunderstanding errors (21 fewer errors). Despite this improvement, semantic misunderstanding remains the dominant error type for SSR++, likely due to inherent knowledge limitations in LLMs that cannot be fully addressed through prompt engineering.

4.4
Attention Recalibration Mechanism

As an additional exploration, we propose a novel approach to optimize LLM performance directly from the perspective of attention mechanisms.

While prompt-based strategies have proven effective in improving LLM accuracy, they incur additional token and computational overhead. Recognizing that prompts work primarily by influencing attention distribution, we explored modifying the attention mechanism itself without introducing extra tokens or changing model parameters.

The rationale behind this approach is rooted in the observation that repeated tokens amplify the attention scores for the question region. For the final token during decoding, repeated tokens effectively double the attention weight assigned to question tokens, enhancing the focus on the problem-relevant areas.

To validate this idea, we modified the attention computation process of LLaMA3.1-8B-Instruct, as illustrated in Figure
7
. This adjustment systematically increased the attention weight on the question region, leading to significant performance gains on multiple datasets.

We tested this method on four datasets:

Optimal scaling factors differed slightly among datasets, with SST2 peaking at a scaling factor of 3.0, while MMLU achieved the highest performance at 2.0. These results demonstrate the adaptability of our method to various reasoning and classification tasks.

5
Conclusion

This work explored the fundamental challenges in LLM’s ability to comprehend and solve reasoning tasks, particularly focusing on the influence of question features such as token length and backward dependencies. Through detailed analysis, we identified key factors limiting performance and designed strategies to address these shortcomings.

Building on existing prompts, such as Re-Reading (RE2) and Chain-of-Thought (CoT), we developed a family of Step-by-Step Reading (SSR) prompts. These strategies systematically guide models to process questions incrementally, refer back to relevant tokens during reasoning, and handle backward dependencies effectively. Our SSR++ prompt achieved SOTA performance on GSM8K (96.66%), ASDiv (94.61%), and AQuA (76.28%), demonstrating its effectiveness across diverse datasets and reasoning tasks. These results highlight the importance of structured reading and reasoning in overcoming limitations in LLM comprehension.

Additionally, we proposed a novel attention recalibration mechanism, which dynamically prioritizes question tokens without altering model parameters or adding extra tokens. This approach improved performance across multiple datasets, including a 5.17% accuracy increase on AQuA.

In summary, our findings reveal that improving LLM reasoning requires more than scaling models or increasing training data. By refining how models interact with input questions through prompt design and attention recalibration, we unlock significant gains in accuracy and interpretability. This study sets a foundation for future research into the interplay between prompt engineering, attention mechanisms, and reasoning performance.

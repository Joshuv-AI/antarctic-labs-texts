Abstract

This contrasts with Lipshitz’s result, which shows that such algorithm does exists for the ring of integers (i.e. OPEN S = ∅ ) . 1
Introduction and main results

In this paper we investigate the following problem. Given a commutative ring
R
. Is there
an algorithm for the following decision problem:

Given any sentence of the form

\exists x_{1},\dots,\exists x_{n}\bigwedge_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=0\wedge\bigwedge_{i=1}^{m}g_{i}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\ |\ h_{i}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}),

(1)

where the
f_{i}
,
g_{i}
and
h_{i}
are linear polynomials with integer coefficients, decide whether or not it has a solution over
R
.

This line of research has a long history, primarily in connection with Hilbert’s tenth problem and it is particularly interesting for rings with meaningful arithmetic such as rings of integers, or more generally, rings of
S
-integers over global fields. We now provide a brief historical overview of the most relevant results to our investigation. Lipshitz
[
Lip77
]
, and in parallel Bel’tyukov
[
B76
]
, showed that the above problem is decidable for
\mathbb{Z}
and for the ring of integers of a quadratic imaginary extension of
\mathbb{Q}.
In contrast, Lipshitz
[
Lip78b
]
showed that the problem for the ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
of any number field other than quadratic imaginary, is as complicated as Hilbert Tenth Problem for
\mathcal{O}_{K}
(i.e. considering polynomials of arbitrary degree in the input). The latter is widely believed to be unsolvable.

Complementing the above results, it was recently shown by Cerda-Romero and Martínez-Ranero
[
CM17
]
that if
S
is a finite and non-empty set of primes, then the problem is undecidable for
\mathbb{Z}[S^{-1}]
. It also worth mentioning that the question for the ring of
S
-integers of a global function fields was completely settled by Martínez-Ranero, Utreras and Vidaux
[
MUV22
]
.

In this paper, we will extend the above results to the ring of
S
-integers of a quadratic imaginary extension of
\mathbb{Q}
. In order to make this precise we need to introduce a few definitions.

Let
K
be a quadratic imaginary extension of
\mathbb{Q}
. For any finite nonempty set
S
of non archimedean places of
K
we define
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}
to be the ring of
S
-integers

\mathcal{O}_{K,S}:=\{\,x\in K\colon\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak{p}}(x)\geq 0,\rm{for\ all}\ \mathfrak{p}\notin S\,\}.

Let us consider the language
\mathcal{L}_{\Div}=\{=,0,1,+,\mid\}
— here
x\mid y
will be interpreted as “
x
divides
y
”.

In terms of mathematical logic our main result can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

( Theorem
5.1
)

Multiplication is positive-existentially definable over the
\mathcal{L}_{\rm div}
-structure
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}
. In particular, the positive-existential theory of this structure is undecidable.

The proof follows the usual pattern, but is technically much more involved. First we show that being different from zero is positive-existentially
definable. This step turns out to be quite technical and requires a deep Theorem of
Lenstra
[
Len77
]
from analytic number theory. Next we show that there is an abundance of units of arbitrary large ”height”. Finally, we provide a positive-existentially definition of the square
function on
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}
. This is the hardest part. The formulas we use are quite similar to
the ones used in
[
CM17
]
and
[
MUV22
]
. However, the proofs are more technically demanding as our rings are (usually) not PID and we also have to deal with archimedean places.

The structure of the rest of paper is as follows. In Section 2 we prove that being different from zero is positive-existentially definable. In Sections 3 we show that multiplication for units is definable. Section 4 is the technical hearth of the paper and we show the existence of arbitrarily large units. Finally, in Section 5 we prove Theorem
5.1
.

Let

The main goal of this section is to prove that the relation “different from

Definition 2.1

Lemma 2.4
.

There exist a rational prime

Let

It follows from Theorem

Pick

Now choose an integer

We are now ready to prove the main result of the section.

Lemma 2.5
.

The relation

is positive-existentially definable in the
\mathcal{L}_{\rm div}
-structure
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}
.

Proof.

Let
p
and

be given as in Lemma

\psi_{\neq}(y):\exists A,B,x(y\mid A\wedge px+b\mid B\wedge A+B=1)

” in
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}

First note that the formula
\psi_{\neq}(y)

” in
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}
.

If

.

For the other implication suppose

. We may assume that

(otherwise, multiply the

be the primes below

is prime, and coprime with

Defining the multiplication function restricted to units

In this section we show that the multiplication function restricted to units is positive-existentially definable in
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}

•

p_{1},\dots,p_{k}
are the prime elements in
\mathbb{Z}
below the primes of
S
.

•

s=\#S
.

•

u_{K}=\#\mathcal{O}_{K,S}^{\times}
.

•

for
x,y\in\mathcal{O}_{K,S},
we abbreviate the formula
x\dib y\wedge y\dib x

•

I=\{0,\dots,4\}^{k}
.

•

\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k})\in\mathbb{N}^{k}

Lemma 3.1
.

For every
x,y\in\mathcal{O}^{\times}_{K,S}

such that
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(p^{\beta}x)\neq 0

Given

arbitrarily. Observe that this is possible since there are at most two primes above any of the

\beta:=(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k})
is as required.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

The set
\operatorname{Prod}_{u}=\{(x,y,z)\colon x,y,z\in\mathcal{O}^{\times}_{K,S},\ z=xy\}
is positive-existentially definable in
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}
.

We shall prove that the following formula works.

\operatorname{Prod}_{u}(x,y,z)\colon x\mid 1\wedge y\mid 1\wedge z\mid 1\wedge\bigwedge_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in I\\
i=1,\dots,u_{K}+1\end{subarray}}\ass(p^{\alpha}x+q_{i},p^{\alpha}z+q_{i}y)

On one hand, if

\operatorname{Prod}_{u}(x,y,z)
holds trivially in
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}
.

On the other hand, suppose that
\operatorname{Prod}_{u}(x,y,z)
holds in
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}
. Choose
\beta\in I
a multi-index satisfying the conclusions Lemma
3.1
. Let
u_{i}
be units such that

p^{\beta}x+q_{i}=u_{i}(p^{\beta}z+q_{i}y).

(2)

Notice that by our choice of

\beta
, we have that

and
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(p^{\beta}z)\neq v_{\mathfrak{p}}(q_{i}y)
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in S
and all

. Thus,

\displaystyle v_{\mathfrak{p}}(u_{i})

\displaystyle=v_{\mathfrak{p}}(u_{i}(p^{\beta}x+q_{i}))-v_{\mathfrak{p}}(p^{\beta}z+q_{i}y)

\displaystyle=\min\{v_{\mathfrak{p}}(p^{\beta}x),v_{\mathfrak{p}}(q_{i})\}-\min\{v_{\mathfrak{p}}(p^{\beta}z),v_{\mathfrak{p}}(q_{i}y)\},

and the latter expression does not depend on

we have

for all
\mathfrak{p}\in S
and all

for every prime
\mathfrak{p}
in

and

have the same zeros and poles, hence they differ by a unit in

From Equation (
2

4
Abundance of large units

In this section we introduce a notion of relative largeness between non-zero elements and units of
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}
. Roughly speaking, we want to express the fact that for each non-zero element there is a unit of greater ”height” using divisibility conditions.

Lemma 4.1
.

For every
x\in O^{*}_{K,S}
there exists a unit
\varepsilon
of infinite order such that
x\dib\varepsilon-1
.

Proof.

Let
x\in O^{*}_{K,S}
be given. Let
\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{p}_{\ell}^{\alpha_{\ell}}
be the factorization of the fractional ideal
x^{h_{K}}\mathcal{O}_{K}
where
h_{K}
denotes the ideal class number. For each,
1\leq i\leq\ell
, choose
x_{i}
such that
x_{i}\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{|\alpha_{i}|}
, this is possible since
h_{K}|\alpha_{i}.
Set
y=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\notin S}x_{i}
. Choose
\mathfrak{p}\in S
(this is where we need that
S
is nonempty) and let
\varepsilon_{0}
be a generator of
\mathfrak{p}^{h_{K}}
. Since
\mathcal{O}_{K}/(y)
is finite, there are
m>n>0
such that
\varepsilon^{m}_{0}\equiv\varepsilon_{0}^{n}\mod(y)
. Using that
\varepsilon_{0}
is not a zero-divisor in the quotient we infer that
y|\varepsilon_{0}^{m-n}
. It follows that
\varepsilon:=\epsilon_{0}^{m-n}
is as required.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Note that since the torsion part of
\mathcal{O}^{\times}_{K,S}
is finite, there is a positive existential
\mathcal{L}_{\Div}
-formula
\varphi_{\infty}(x)
so that
\varphi_{\infty}(u)
holds in
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}
if and only if
u
is a unit of infinite order.

Recall that, for each nonzero prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
,
k(\mathfrak{p}):=\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}
denotes the residue field at
\mathfrak{p}
and
q_{\mathfrak{p}}:=\#k(\mathfrak{p})
. We also recall the product formula
|a|^{2}=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in M_{K}^{*}}q_{\mathfrak{p}}^{v_{\mathfrak{p}}(a)}
for each
a\in K
.

Lemma 4.3
.

There is a constant
0<C<1
such that given any
x\in\mathcal{O}_{K,S}^{*}
, there exists
a\in O_{K,S}^{*}
and
b\in\mathcal{O}^{*}_{K}
such that
x=\frac{a}{b}
,
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(a)>-h_{K}
for every
\mathfrak{p}\in S
, and
C<|a|
. Moreover, for every
\mathfrak{p}\in S
, if
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(a)\geq 0,
then
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(b)=0
.

Proof.

Let
C^{2}=\prod\limits_{\mathfrak{p}\in S}q_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-h_{K}}
where
h_{K}
denotes the ideal class group. Let
D(x):=\{\mathfrak{p}\in S:v_{\mathfrak{p}}(x)<0\}
. If
D(x)=\emptyset
, then set
b=1
and
a=x
. Next, suppose
D(x)\neq\emptyset
. For each
\mathfrak{p}\in D(x)
, let
\beta_{\mathfrak{p}}
be the maximal integer
\leq-v_{\mathfrak{p}}(x)
such that
\mathfrak{p}^{\beta_{\mathfrak{p}}}
is a principal ideal. Choose
b_{\mathfrak{p}}
such that
b_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathfrak{p}^{\beta_{\mathfrak{p}}}
. Finally, let
b=\prod\limits_{\mathfrak{p}\in D(x)}b_{\mathfrak{p}}
and let
a:=bx
. Notice that by construction
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(a)>-h_{k}
and for every
\mathfrak{p}\in S
, if
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(a)\geq 0,
then
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(b)=0
. We claim that
|a|>C.

Indeed, by the product formula

|a|^{2}=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in M_{K}^{*}}q_{\mathfrak{p}}^{v_{\mathfrak{p}}(a)}=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in M_{K}^{*}\setminus S}q_{\mathfrak{p}}^{v_{\mathfrak{p}}(a)}\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in S}q_{\mathfrak{p}}^{v_{\mathfrak{p}}(a)}

>\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in S}q_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-h_{K}}=C^{2}.

Thus,
|a|>C
as required.
∎

The following Lemma makes precise the idea that the unit
\varepsilon
in Lemma
4.1
is much larger than
x
. Let
q
be a rational prime bigger than
4C^{-2}
and not below any of the primes in

.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
x\in\mathcal{O}^{*}_{K,S}
be such that
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(x)\neq 0
for any
\mathfrak{p}\in S
, and let
\varepsilon\in\mathcal{O}_{K,S}^{\times}
be a unit of infinite order such that
x\pm 1|\varepsilon-1
, and
q\dib\varepsilon-1
. If we write

and
\varepsilon=\frac{u}{v}
, where

and

are as in Lemma

, then

\max\{\frac{2}{C},|a|,|b|\}\leq\max\{|u|^{2},|v|^{2}\}

Proof.

Write
x=\frac{a}{b}
, and
\varepsilon=\frac{u}{v}
, where
a,b
and
u,v
are as in the Lemma
4.3
.

First of all, we shall show that
q\dib\varepsilon-1
, implies that
\frac{1}{C}\leq\max\{|u|^{2},|v|^{2}\}
. Notice that, since

is a unit

q|\varepsilon-1
if and only if
q|u-v
. Let

. For each
\mathfrak{p}\in S

v_{\mathfrak{p}}(r)=v_{\mathfrak{p}}(u-v)-v_{\mathfrak{p}}(q)=v_{\mathfrak{p}}(u-v)>-h_{K}.
Thus, by the product formula,

It follows, from our assumptions on

2\max\{|u|,|v|\}\geq|u-v|=|q||r|>qC>\frac{4}{C}.

Next, we show that

implies

.
We will focus on the

. Let

v_{\mathfrak{p}}(c)>-h_{K}
for
\mathfrak{p}\in S
. Indeed, first observe that
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(u-v)\geq\min\{v_{\mathfrak{p}}(u),v_{\mathfrak{p}}(v)\}>-h_{K}.
Next, we need an upper bound for
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(a+b)
for
\mathfrak{p}\in S

v_{\mathfrak{p}}(x)>0,
then

and thus
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(a+b)=0
. On the other hand, if
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(x)<0
, then either
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(a)=0
and
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(b)>0
or
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(a)<0
and

. Hence, in either case
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(a+b)\leq 0
. It follows that
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(c)=v_{\mathfrak{p}}(u-v)-v_{\mathfrak{p}}(a+b)>-h_{K}
. Using the product formula again we have that

|u-v|^{2}=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in M_{K}^{*}}q_{\mathfrak{p}}^{v_{\mathfrak{p}}(u-v)}=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in M_{K}^{*}}q_{\mathfrak{p}}^{v_{\mathfrak{p}}(c(a+b))}=|a+b|^{2}\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in M_{K}^{*}\setminus S}q_{\mathfrak{p}}^{v_{\mathfrak{p}}(c)}\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in S}q_{\mathfrak{p}}^{v_{\mathfrak{p}}(c)}

>|a+b|^{2}\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in S}q_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-h_{K}}=|a+b|^{2}C^{2}.

Thus,
|u-v|>C|a+b|
. Analogously, we obtain

.
Finally, using the triangle inequality we obtain that

2|a|\leq|a+b|+|a-b|<\frac{2}{C}|u-v|\leq\frac{4}{C}\max\{|u|,|v|\}

Hence,
|a|<\frac{2}{C}\max\{|u|,|v|\}
. Proceeding in an analogous way we also have that

. Combining these with the previous inequality

. We infer that

\max\{\frac{2}{C},|a|,|b|\}\leq\max\{|u|^{2},|v|^{2}\}
. ∎

5
Definability of the square function

We are ready to prove the main theorem of the paper. The proof follows the same pattern as before, though much harder. Recall that

•

p_{1},\dots,p_{k}
are the prime elements in
\mathbb{Z}
below the primes of
S
.

•

and let
J=\{0,\dots,6\}^{k}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

The set

SQ=\{(x,y)\in\mathcal{O}_{K,S}\colon y=x^{2}\}

is positive-existentially definable in the
\mathcal{L}_{\rm div}
-structure
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}.

Proof.

We claim that the following formula
\varphi_{\rm sq}(x,y)
defines the set
SQ
.

(x=0\wedge y=0)\vee\bigvee_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in J\end{subarray}}(x=\pm p^{-\alpha}\wedge y=p^{-2\alpha})

(3)

\vee\exists\varepsilon(\varepsilon\dib 1\wedge\varphi_{\infty}(\varepsilon))\wedge\psi(x,y)

(4)

is the conjunction of the following formulas:

\psi_{1}(x,\varepsilon):\bigwedge_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in J\end{subarray}}p^{\alpha}x\pm 1\dib\varepsilon-1

\psi_{2}(y,\varepsilon):\bigwedge_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in J\end{subarray}}p^{2\alpha}y\pm 1\dib\varepsilon-1

\psi_{3}(\varepsilon):q\dib\varepsilon-1

\psi_{4}(\varepsilon_{2}):\varepsilon_{1}=\varepsilon^{17}

\psi_{5}(x,y,\varepsilon_{1}):\bigwedge_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in J\end{subarray}}p^{\alpha}x\pm\varepsilon_{1}\dib p^{2\alpha}y-\varepsilon_{1}^{2}.

First we show that if
y=x^{2}
, then
\varphi_{\rm sq}(x,y)
holds in
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}
. If
x=0
or
x=\pm p^{-\alpha}
for
\alpha\in I
, then clearly
\varphi_{\rm sq}(x,y)
holds. So we may assume
x\neq 0
and
x\neq\pm p^{-\alpha}
for any
\alpha\in I
, but in this case the result follows immediately from Lemma
4.1
.

Let us now assume that
\varphi_{\rm sq}(x,y)
holds. Without loss of generality we may also assume that
x\neq 0,
and
x\neq\pm p^{-\alpha}
for any
\alpha\in I
. Fix
\varepsilon
so that
\psi(x,y)
holds.

We will break the rest of the proof into several Claims.

Claim A:
There exists a multi index
\alpha\in J
such that
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(p^{\alpha}x)\neq 0,

v_{\mathfrak{p}}(p^{2\alpha}y)\neq 0
and
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(p^{\alpha}x)\neq v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\varepsilon_{1})
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in S
.

Proof of Claim A.

Given
x,y\in\mathcal{O}^{*}_{K,S}
, for each
1\leq i\leq k
, choose

\alpha_{i}\in\{0,\dots,6\}\setminus\left(\left\{\frac{-v_{\mathfrak{p}}(x)}{e(\mathfrak{p}:p_{i})}\colon\mathfrak{p}\dib p_{i}\right\}\cup\left\{\frac{-v_{\mathfrak{p}}(y)}{2e(\mathfrak{p}:p_{i})}\colon\mathfrak{p}\dib p_{i}\right\}\cup\left\{\frac{-v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\varepsilon_{1})}{e(\mathfrak{p}:p_{i})}\colon\mathfrak{p}\dib p_{i}\right\}\right),

arbitrarily. Observe that this is possible since there are at most two primes
above any of the
p_{i}
’s. It follows that
\alpha:=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k})
is as required.
\hfill\square

From now on, in order to simplify notation, we will write
X
and
Y
instead of
p^{\alpha}x
and
p^{2\alpha}y
. We also write
X=\frac{a}{b},Y=\frac{c}{d}
and
\varepsilon=\frac{u}{v}
where
a,b,c,d,
and
u,v
are chosen as in Lemma
4.3
.

Since
\psi_{1}(x,\varepsilon)
,
\psi_{2}(y,\varepsilon)
and
\psi_{3}(\varepsilon)
hold, it follows from Lemma
4.4
that

\begin{split}\max\{\frac{2}{C},|a|,|b|,|c|,|d|\}\leq\max\{|u|^{2},|v|^{2}\}\end{split}

(5)

From
\psi_{5}
, we have

X\pm\varepsilon^{17}\ |\ Y-\varepsilon^{34},

and trivially

X\pm\varepsilon^{17}\ |\ X^{2}-\varepsilon^{34},

hence
X\pm\varepsilon^{17}\ |\ Y-X^{2}
.
Aiming towards a contradiction, suppose that
Y\neq X^{2}
. It follows, from the previous equation, that

av^{17}\pm u^{17}b\ \dib\ b^{2}c-a^{2}d,

(6)

We will obtain our desired contradiction by showing that Equation (
6
) does not hold. Intuitively, the left hand size is much bigger than the right hand side.

Let
e^{\pm}:=\frac{b^{2}c-a^{2}d}{av^{17}\pm u^{17}b}
. First we shall obtain an upper bound for
|b^{2}c-a^{2}d|
and a lower bound for its poles. For this, notice that
|b^{2}c-a^{2}d|\leq 2\max\{|a|^{3},|b|^{3},|c|^{3},|d|^{3}\}
and also that

v_{\mathfrak{p}}(b^{2}c-a^{2}d)\geq\min\{v_{\mathfrak{p}}(b^{2}c),v_{\mathfrak{p}}(a^{2}d)\}>-2h_{K}\ \ \ {\rm for}\ \mathfrak{p}\in S.

(7)

Next we shall obtain lower bounds for
|e^{\pm}|
. In order to do this, we need to find lower bounds for the poles of
e^{\pm}
. In turn, this requires lower bounds for the poles of
b^{2}c-a^{2}d
and upper bounds for the zeros of
av^{17}\pm u^{17}b
in
S
.

Claim B:
For each
\mathfrak{p}\in S
,
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(av^{17}\pm u^{17}b)\leq h_{K}+v_{\mathfrak{p}}(a)+v_{\mathfrak{p}}(b)
.

Proof of Claim B.
Fix
\mathfrak{p}\in S
. Notice that, by our assumptions,
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(av^{17}\pm u^{17}b)=\min\{v_{\mathfrak{p}}(av^{17}),v_{\mathfrak{p}}(bu^{17})\}
.
Now we proceed by cases:

•

If
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(a)>0
and
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(v)>0
, then
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(b)=0
and
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(u)\leq 0
. Hence,
\min\{v_{\mathfrak{p}}(av^{17}),v_{\mathfrak{p}}(bu^{17})\}\leq v_{\mathfrak{p}}(bu^{17})\leq 0
.

•

If
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(a)>0
and
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(v)=0
, then
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(b)=0
. Hence,
\min\{v_{\mathfrak{p}}(av^{17}),v_{\mathfrak{p}}(bu^{17})\}\leq v_{\mathfrak{p}}(av^{17})=v_{\mathfrak{p}}(a).

•

If
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(a)=0
and
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(v)>0
, then
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(b)>0
and
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(u)\leq 0
. Hence,
\min\{v_{\mathfrak{p}}(av^{17}),v_{\mathfrak{p}}(bu^{17})\}\leq v_{\mathfrak{p}}(bu^{17})\leq v_{\mathfrak{p}}(b).

•

If
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(a)=0
and
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(v)=0
, then

\min\{v_{\mathfrak{p}}(av^{17}),v_{\mathfrak{p}}(bu^{17})\}\leq v_{\mathfrak{p}}(av^{17})=0.

•

If
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(a)<0
and
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(v)>0
, then
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(u)\leq 0
and
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(b)\geq 0
. Hence,

\min\{v_{\mathfrak{p}}(av^{17}),v_{\mathfrak{p}}(bu^{17})\}\leq v_{\mathfrak{p}}(bu^{17})\leq v_{\mathfrak{p}}(b).

•

If
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(a)<0
and
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(v)=0
, then
\min\{v_{\mathfrak{p}}(av^{17}),v_{\mathfrak{p}}(bu^{17})\}\leq v_{\mathfrak{p}}(av^{17})<0
.

Thus it follows that

v_{\mathfrak{p}}(av^{17}\pm u^{17}b)\leq\max\{0,v_{\mathfrak{p}}(a),v_{\mathfrak{p}}(b)\}\leq h_{K}+v_{\mathfrak{p}}(a)+v_{\mathfrak{p}}(b).

\hfill\square

Combining Equation
7
and Claim B, we infer that

v_{\mathfrak{p}}(e^{\pm})=v_{\mathfrak{p}}(b^{2}c-a^{2}d)-v_{\mathfrak{p}}(av^{17}\pm bu^{17})\geq-2h_{K}-h_{K}-v_{\mathfrak{p}}(a)-v_{\mathfrak{p}}(b).

Using the product formula once more we have

|e^{\pm}|^{2}=\prod\limits_{\mathfrak{p}\in M_{K}^{*}}q_{\mathfrak{p}}^{v_{\mathfrak{p}}(e^{\pm})}\geq\prod\limits_{\mathfrak{p}\in S}q_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-3h_{K}-v_{\mathfrak{p}}(a)-v_{\mathfrak{p}}(b)}\geq

\left(\prod\limits_{\mathfrak{p}\in M_{K}^{*}\setminus S}q_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-v_{\mathfrak{p}}(a)-v_{\mathfrak{p}}(b)}\right)\prod\limits_{\mathfrak{p}\in S}q_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-3h_{K}-v_{\mathfrak{p}}(a)-v_{\mathfrak{p}}(b)}=\frac{C^{6}}{|ab|^{2}}.

Observe that, by the triangle inequality,

2|b^{2}c-a^{2}d|=|e^{+}||av^{17}+bu^{17}|+|e^{-}||av^{17}-bu^{17}|\geq 2\frac{C^{3}|av^{17}|}{|ab|}

and

2|b^{2}c-a^{2}d|=|e^{+}||av^{17}+bu^{17}|+|e^{-}||-av^{17}+bu^{17}|\geq 2\frac{C^{3}|bu^{17}|}{|ab|}

.

Thus, we obtain that
|b^{2}c-a^{2}d|\geq\max\left\{\frac{C^{3}|u^{17}|}{|a|},\frac{C^{3}|v^{17}|}{|b|}\right\}.

Using Equation
5
, it follows that

\max\left\{\frac{C^{3}|u^{17}|}{|a|},\frac{C^{3}|v^{17}|}{|b|}\right\}\geq\max\{|u^{9}|,|v|^{9}\}

On the other hand,
|b^{2}c-a^{2}d|\leq 2\max\{|a|^{3},|b|^{3},|c|^{3},|d|^{3}\}\leq\max\{|u|^{8},|v|^{8}\}.
Therefore,

\max\{|u|^{8},|v|^{8}\}\geq\max\{|u^{9}|,|v|^{9}\}

which is a contradiction. This concludes the proof of the Theorem. ∎

As a corollary we obtain the undecidability of
\rm{Th}^{+\exists}(\mathcal{O}_{K,S})
.

Corollary 5.2
.

The positive-existential theory of the
\mathcal{L}_{\rm div}
-structure
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}
is undecidable.

Proof.

This follows immediately from the undecidability of
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}
in the language of rings (see
[
Sh07
]
). ∎

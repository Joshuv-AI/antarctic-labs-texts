Bousfield-Kan completion as a codensity ∞ {{{\infty}}} -monad
Emmanuel Dror Farjoun, Sergei O. Ivanov

Abstract
. We develop a theory of codensity monads associated with full subcategories in the setting of ∞ \infty -categories. For an ∞ \infty -monad ℳ \mathcal{M} on an ∞ \infty -category 𝒞 \mathcal{C} that admits homotopy totalizations, we consider the ℳ \mathcal{M} -completion functor ℳ ^ \widehat{\mathcal{M}} defined as the homotopy totalization of the canonical cosimplicial functor associated with ℳ \mathcal{M} . We prove that ℳ ^ \widehat{\mathcal{M}} is the codensity ∞ \infty -monad of a full ∞ \infty -subcategory 𝒜 ⁡ ( ℳ ) ⊆ 𝒞 \mathcal{A}(\mathcal{M})\subseteq\mathcal{C} spanned by objects that admit a structure of ℳ \mathcal{M} -algebra. This implies that ℳ ^ \widehat{\mathcal{M}} is the terminal object in the ∞ \infty -category of ∞ \infty -monads preserving all objects of 𝒜 ⁡ ( ℳ ) \mathcal{A}(\mathcal{M}) . As an application, we prove that the classical Bousfield-Kan R R -completion functor is the codensity ∞ \infty -monad of the full subcategory in the ∞ \infty -category of spaces 𝒦 ⁡ ( R ) ⊆ 𝖲𝗉𝖼 \mathcal{K}(R)\subseteq\mathsf{Spc} spanned by the empty space and the products of Eilenberg-MacLane spaces of R R -modules. As a corollary, we obtain two universal properties of the Bousfield-Kan R R -completion: it is the terminal 𝒦 ⁡ ( R ) \mathcal{K}(R) -preserving coaugmented functor, and the terminal 𝒦 ⁡ ( R ) \mathcal{K}(R) -preserving ∞ \infty -monad.



1.
Introduction

A codensity
\infty
-monad

\infty
-subcategory
\mathcal{D}
of an
\infty
-category

\mathcal{D}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}
along itself. Initially,

\infty

If the inclusion functor

\infty

\infty

An important source of codensity

-category that admits homotopy totalizations (i.e. limits of cosimplicial objects). An

-category of endofunctors

is an ordinary category and

[

Theorem

(Cor.

Let

Many completion-like endofunctors can be presented as codensity monads of full subcategories in ordinary categories. For example, the profinite (resp. pronilpotent) completion functor on the category of groups is the codensity monad of the subcategory of finite (resp. nilpotent) groups. The double dual functor on the category of vector spaces is the codensity monad of the subcategory of finite-dimensional vector spaces. Let us now consider another example of a completion-like endofunctor, this time on the category of spaces.

For a commutative ring

[

(1.1)

If

\mathbb{Z}_{\infty}X\simeq X

In order to define the canonical
\infty

At the end of the introduction, we briefly comment on our approach to
\infty
-monads and on strict monoidal
\infty
-categories, which form the technical foundation of the paper. There are several approaches to defining monads in the setting of
\infty
-categories. Lurie, in
[
Lur07
]
, first defines a monoidal
\infty
-category as a cocartesian fibration over
\mathrm{N}(\Delta)^{\mathrm{op}}
satisfying certain conditions, and defines an algebra object as a section of this fibration satisfying certain properties. An
\infty
-monad is then defined as an algebra object in the monoidal
\infty
-category of endofunctors of an
\infty
-category. Another approach is given by Riehl and Verity in
[
RV16
]
. They consider a quasi-categorically enriched category
\mathcal{K}
and define an
\infty
-monad as a quasi-categorically enriched functor from a certain one-object quasi-categorically enriched category
\underline{\sf Mnd}\to\mathcal{K}
. The equivalence between these two approaches was established by Haugseng
[
Hau20
, §8]
. Since our main examples of monoidal
\infty
-categories are subcategories of the
\infty
-category of endofunctors
\mathsf{End}(\mathcal{C})
, which carries a natural associative composition operation, we adopt the more elementary and rigid approach of Riehl and Verity, reformulated in a form more suitable for our purposes.

By an
\infty
-category, we always mean a quasi-category. A strict monoidal
\infty
-category
\mathcal{E}
is defined as an
\infty
-category equipped with the structure of a simplicial monoid. For example, the nerve
\mathrm{N}(E)
of an ordinary strict monoidal category

is a strict monoidal
\infty
-category. One can also view a strict monoidal
\infty
-category as a quasi-categorically enriched category with a single object. An
\infty
-monoid in a strict monoidal
\infty
-category

is a morphism of simplicial monoids

(1.3)

\mathcal{M}\colon\mathrm{N}(\Delta_{+})\longrightarrow\mathcal{E},

where
\Delta_{+}
is the augmented simplex category with its standard strict monoidal structure. The
\infty
-category of
\infty
-monoids is defined as the mapping space in the simplicial category of simplicial monoids

(1.4)

\mathsf{mon}_{\infty}(\mathcal{E})=\mathsf{Map}_{\mathsf{sMon}}(N(\Delta_{+}),\mathcal{E}).

Given an
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
, the
\infty
-category of endofunctors
\mathsf{End}(\mathcal{C})
carries a natural strict monoidal structure defined by the composition. An
\infty
-monad on
\mathcal{C}
is defined as an
\infty
-monoid in
\mathsf{End}(\mathcal{C})
.

As we already mentioned, it is difficult to define an
\infty
-monad structure on

. In general, it can be difficult to define an
\infty
-monad structure on a given functor. The next theorem gives a way to define an
\infty
-monad structure on a functor satisfying some universal property.

Theorem

Let

\infty
-monoids.

2.
Simplicial monoids

In this paper, we adopt an approach to the definition of monads in the setting of
\infty
-categories based on the language of simplicial monoids. To support this, we first develop some technical background on simplicial monoids. In this section, we begin by recalling the standard simplicial model category structure on the category of simplicial monoids, with particular emphasis on cofibrations, which are retracts of the so-called
free maps
. We then study certain distinguished classes of free maps, which we refer to as (inner, left, right) anodyne free maps. These are analogous to the (inner, left, right) anodyne maps of simplicial sets. We show that a left anodyne free map of simplicial monoids has a left lifting property with respect to a left fibration of simplicial monoids (Proposition
2.7
), which plays an important role in the next section. We conclude by introducing the notion of a strict monoidal
\infty
-category and proving some properties of terminal objects in them (Lemma
2.11
).

2.1.
Simplicial model structure

In this subsection we recall the definition of the standard simplicial model category structure on the category of simplicial monoids
[
Qui06
, §II.4]
(see also
[
DK80
]
for the case
O=*
). We also recall the definition of the tensored and cotensored enrichment of the category of simplicial monoids over pointed simplicial sets.

We consider the category of simplicial monoids
\mathsf{sMon}
and the forgetful functor to the category of simplicial sets

(2.1)

U:\mathsf{sMon}\longrightarrow\mathsf{sSet}.

Then the category
\mathsf{sMon}
is a model category with the following definition of weak equivalences, fibrations, and cofibrations.

(W)

A map of simplicial monoids
f
is a weak equivalence if
Uf
is a weak equivalence of simplicial sets.

(F)

A map of simplicial monoids
f
is a fibration if the map
Uf
is a fibration of simplicial sets.

(C)

A map is a cofibration if it has a left lifting property with respect to those fibrations which are week equivalences.

On the next subsection, we give a more explicit description of the class of cofibrations in terms of
free maps
.

Let us define tensored and cotensored simplicial enrichment of
\mathsf{sMon}
, making it a simplicial model category. If
S
is a set and
M
is a monoid, we set

S\otimes M=\coprod_{s\in S}M.

If
S
is a simplicial set and
M
is a simplicial monoid, the product
S\otimes M
is defined component-wise
(S\otimes M)(J)=S(J)\otimes M(J)
. Then the enrichment is defined by

(2.2)

\mathsf{Map}(M,N)(J)=\mathsf{Hom}_{\mathsf{sMon}}(\Delta^{J}\otimes M,N).

If
S
is a simplicial set and
M
is a simplicial monoid, the simplicial monoid
M^{S}
is defined as the ordinary function complex of simplicial sets
(UM)^{S}
equipped with the structure of a simplicial monoid defined point-wise (see
[
Qui06
, II.1.11]
). In particular we have the identity

(2.3)

U(M^{S})=(UM)^{S}

and the adjunction isomorphisms

(2.4)

\mathsf{Map}(S\otimes M,N)\cong\mathsf{Map}(M,N)^{S}\cong\mathsf{Map}(M,N^{S})

for any simplicial monoids
M,N
and a simplicial set
S.

In particular, we have the isomorphism

(2.5)

\mathsf{Map}(S\otimes\mathbb{N},M)\cong(UM)^{S},

where
\mathbb{N}
is the monoid of non-negative integers treated as the constant simplicial monoid.

The category
\mathsf{sMon}
can also be treated as a category enriched over pointed simplicial sets, because
\mathsf{Map}(M,N)
has a natural base point. This enrichment is also tensored and cotensored. If
S
is a pointed set with a base point
s_{0}
and
M
is a monoid, we consider the quotient monoid

(2.6)

S\wedge M={\sf Coker}(\{s_{0}\}\otimes M\to S\otimes M).

Note that there is an isomorphism

S\wedge M\cong(S\setminus\{s_{0}\})\otimes M.
If
S
is a simplicial set and
M
is a simplicial monoid then
S\wedge M
is defined component-wise. The pointed cotensoring can be defined as the kernel

(2.7)

\mathsf{map}_{*}(S,M)={\sf Ker}(M^{S}\to M^{\{s_{0}\}}).

It is easy to see that

(2.8)

U(\mathsf{map}_{*}(S,M))=\mathsf{map}_{*}(S,UM)

and that there are adjunction isomorphisms

(2.9)

\mathsf{Map}(S\wedge M,N)\cong\mathsf{map}_{*}(S,\mathsf{Map}(M,N))\cong\mathsf{Map}(M,\mathsf{map}_{*}(S,N)).

In particular, we have

(2.10)

\mathsf{Map}(S\wedge\mathbb{N},M)\cong\mathsf{map}_{*}(S,UM).

For any simplicial monoids
M
and
N
, there is an isomorphism

(2.11)

\mathsf{Map}(M,N)(J)\cong\mathsf{Hom}_{\mathsf{sMon}}(M,N^{\Delta^{J}}).

For any two pointed simplicial sets
X
and
Y
, there is an isomorphism

(2.12)

\mathsf{map}_{*}(X,Y)(J)\cong\mathsf{Hom}_{\mathsf{sSet}_{*}}(X,Y^{\Delta^{J}}).

Therefore, there is a monomorphism

(2.13)

\mathsf{Map}(M,N)\hookrightarrow\mathsf{map}_{*}(UM,UN).

Lemma 2.1
.

For any simplicial monoids
M,N
, the simplicial set
\mathsf{Map}(M,N)
is isomorphic is the equaliser of two maps

(2.14)

\mathsf{Map}(M,N)\to\mathsf{map}_{*}(UM,UN)\rightrightarrows\mathsf{map}_{*}(UM^{2},UN),

where the first map is induced by the multiplication map
UM^{2}\to UM,
and the second map is the composition of the square map
\mathsf{map}_{*}(UM,UN)\to\mathsf{map}_{*}(UM^{2},UN^{2})
, and the map induced by the multiplication

.

Proof.

For any two ordinary monoids
M,N
the hom-set

is the equaliser of the two maps

(2.15)

\mathsf{Hom}_{\mathsf{Mon}}(M,N)\to\mathsf{Hom}_{\mathsf{Set}_{*}}(UM,UN)\rightrightarrows\mathsf{Hom}_{\mathsf{Set}_{*}}(UM^{2},UN).

Therefore, the similar equaliser diagram exists for the hom-set between simplicial monoids

and

A direct computation shows that these two maps are the required maps.
∎

A map of simplicial monoids is a cofibration if and only if it is a retract of a free map.

2.3.
Anodyne free maps

Let
\alpha:B\to A
be a free map with a basis
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. A collection of subsets
(H_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in the non-degenerate part of the basis
H_{n}\subseteq X^{\circ}_{n}
is called a collection of
horn generators
if we can choose numbers
0\leq i(h)\leq n,
for all
n\geq 1
and
h\in H_{n}
, such that:

•

d_{i(h)}(h)\in X^{\circ}_{n-1}\setminus H_{n-1};

•

for any

there exists a unique

such that

.

In other words, the inclusion

and the map

define a bijection

(2.30)

H_{n+1}\sqcup H_{n}\cong X_{n}^{\circ}

for any
n\geq 0.

The numbers
i(h)
will be referred as
horn indices
of the horn generators.

A free map is called an
anodyne free map
if it has a basis that has a collection of horn generators. An anodyne free map is called an
inner (resp. left; right) anodyne free map
, if it has a basis
(X_{n})
that has horn generators
(H_{n})
whose horn indices satisfy
0<i(h)<n
(resp.
0\leq i(h)<n
;
0<i(h)\leq n
) for
h\in H_{n}.

A free monoid

is called anodyne free (resp. inner anodyne free; left anodyne free; right anodyne free) if the map

is so. In this case a collection of horn generators of
1\to A
is called a collection of horn generators of
A
.

2.5.
Strict monoidal
\infty

We define a

\infty
-category
as a simplicial monoid
\mathcal{E}
such that

is an
\infty

\infty

[

. We use the usual abuse of notation identifying a strict

with the underlying
\infty

denotes the mapping space in

Let us give some examples of strict monoidal
\infty

of an
\infty

of an ordinary strict monoidal category

is a strict monoidal simplicial category, its homotopy coherent nerve

has a natural structure of a simplicial monoid (see Subsection

for details). So, if

is, in addition, locally Kan, then

is a strict monoidal
\infty
-category.

Let us prove some properties of of strict monoidal
\infty
-categories. An inner fibration between
\infty
-categories

is called an isofibration if any equivalence
\alpha:F(c)\to d^{\prime}
in

can be lifted to an equivalence

. A functor
F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
is called conservative, if for a morphism
\alpha
in
\mathcal{C}
, the morphism

is an equivalence only if
\alpha
is an equivalence.

the component of the image of
\varphi
in the product
\prod_{X_{n}^{\circ}}\mathcal{E}^{\partial\Delta^{n}}
. Then it is sufficient to prove that the fiber of the map
\mathcal{E}^{\Delta^{n}}\to\mathcal{E}^{\partial\Delta^{n}}
over

is contractible for any

.

Since
\varphi_{0}(d^{n}_{0}(x))=t

3.
\infty
-monoids

In this section, we introduce the notion of an
\infty
-monoid in a strict monoidal
\infty
-category
\mathcal{E}
. We study the forgetful functors from the category of
\infty
-monoids in
\mathcal{E}
to the original category
\mathcal{E}
and to the category of coaugmented objects
\mathcal{E}^{\mathbb{1}/}
. The two main results of this section state that a terminal object of
\mathcal{E}
(respectively, of
\mathcal{E}^{\mathbb{1}/}
) admits an essentially unique structure of an
\infty
-monoid (unique up to a contractible space of choices) and that this structure defines a terminal object in the category of
\infty
-monoids (Theorem
3.3
, Theorem
3.12
). The main example of a strict
\infty
-category for us is the
\infty
-category of endofunctors
\mathsf{End}(\mathcal{C})
, and its
\infty
-subcategories closed under composition. The main examples of
\infty
-monoids are
\infty
-monads, which are defined as
\infty
-monoids in
\mathsf{End}(\mathcal{C})
.
Later we use the results of this section to show that the codensity
\infty
-monad, which is initially just a functor, has a canonical structure of an
\infty
-monad defined uniquely up to contractible space of choices.

Our approach to
\infty
-monoids is essentially a reformulation of the approach of Riehl and Verity
[
RV16
]
, rewritten in terms that are more convenient for our purposes. Essentially, a strict monoidal
\infty
-category is a quasi-categorically enriched category with one object. Then an
\infty
-monoid is a homotopy coherent monad in the sense of Riehl and Verity
[
RV16
, Def. 6.1.1]
. The equivalence of this approach with that of Lurie
[
Lur17
]
was established by Haugseng
[
Hau20
, §8]
.

3.1.
\infty
-monoids

Consider the augmented simplex category
\Delta_{+},
whose objects are finite ordinals
[n]=\{0,\dots,n\}
,
including the empty ordinal
[-1]=\emptyset,

and morphisms are monotone maps. We also treat the well ordered set
[n]
as a posetal category, and
\Delta_{+}
as a full subcategory of the category of small categories
{\sf Cat}.
Using the identification

(3.1)

[n]\star[m]\cong[n+m+1],

we obtain that the join defines a structure of strict monoidal category

(3.2)

\star:\Delta_{+}\times\Delta_{+}\longrightarrow\Delta_{+}

where
[-1]
is the unit object.
This strict monoidal structure defines a structure of a strict monoidal
\infty
-category on its nerve
\mathrm{N}(\Delta_{+})
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The simplicial monoid
\mathrm{N}(\Delta_{+})
is free, with the basis given by functors

f:[n]\to\Delta_{+}
such that
f(n)=[0].
Moreover,
\mathrm{N}(\Delta_{+})
is left anodyne free with horn generators given by functors
f:[n]\to\Delta_{+}
which are non-degenerate, and satisfy:
f(n)=[0]
and
f(0)=[-1]
; and all the horn indices are equal to
0
.

Proof.

First we prove that
\mathrm{N}(\Delta_{+})_{n}
is a free monoid generated by functors
f:[n]\to\Delta_{+}
such that
f(n)=[0]
.
For
n=0
we have
\mathrm{N}(\Delta_{+})_{0}=\mathbb{N},
where
\mathbb{N}
is the monoid of natural numbers, and the statement is obvious. Assume that
n\geq 1.
Then a functor
f:[n]\to\Delta_{+}
is defined by a sequence of ordinals and monotone maps

(3.3)

f(0)\to f(1)\to\dots\to f(n).

Now we just take all one-point subsets of the last set
f(n)
and decompose
f
into their preimages. For each
k\in f(n)
and
0\leq i\leq n,
we denote by
A_{i,k}\subseteq f(i)
the preimage of
k
with respect to the map
f(i)\to f(n).

Then
f(i)=\coprod_{k\in f(n)}A_{i,k}
and a map
f(i)\to f(i+1)
sends
A_{i,k}
to
A_{i+1,k}.

Therefore
f=\bigstar_{k\in f(n)}f_{k},
where
f_{k}(i)\cong A_{i,k},
and it is easy to see that this decomposition is unique. Hence, the functors
f:[n]\to\Delta_{+}
such that
f(n)=[0]
form a basis of
\mathrm{N}(\Delta_{+})
. Let us denote it by
(X_{n}).

The non-degenerate part of the basis
X_{n}^{\circ}
is given by non-degenerate functors
f:[n]\to\Delta_{+}
such that
f(n)=[0].
Consider a subset
H_{n}\subseteq X_{n}^{\circ}
consisting of functors
f
such that
f(0)=[-1].
Note that
H_{0}=\emptyset
and for
n\geq 1
and
f\in H_{n}
, since
f
is non-degenerate, we have
f(1)\neq[-1].
Using this, it is easy to check that
d_{0}
defines a bijection
H_{n+1}\to X_{n}^{\circ}\setminus H_{n}.
Therefore,
H_{n}
is a collection of horn generators with all horn indices equal to
0
.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Assume that
\mathcal{E}
is a strict
\infty
-category with a terminal object

. Then any structure of an
\infty
-monoid on

defines a terminal object of
\mathsf{mon}_{\infty}(\mathcal{E})
and the Kan complex of
\infty
-monoid structures
\mathsf{mon}_{\infty}(\mathcal{E},t)
is (non-empty and) contractible.

Proof.

The forgetful functor
\mathsf{mon}_{\infty}(\mathcal{E})\to\mathcal{E}
is induced by the free map
\alpha:\mathbb{N}\to\mathrm{N}(\Delta_{+})
. By Lemma

, the sets

form a basis of

. Denote by

the map of simplicial monoids defined by

the terminal object. Here we identify

. Therefore, for any

we have

and
\varphi(d^{n}_{0}(f))=t
. Hence Lemma
2.11

implies that
\mathsf{mon}_{\infty}(\mathcal{E},t)
is contractible. In particular,
\mathsf{mon}_{\infty}(\mathcal{E},t)
is non-empty.

Now assume that
T
is an object of
\mathsf{mon}_{\infty}(\mathcal{E},t)
, we prove that
T
is a terminal object of
\mathsf{mon}_{\infty}(\mathcal{E})
. Take an object
\mathcal{M}
of
\mathsf{mon}_{\infty}(\mathcal{E})
we prove that the mapping space
\mathsf{map}_{\mathsf{mon}_{\infty}(\mathcal{E})}(\mathcal{M},T)
is contractible. The mapping space is defined as the fiber of the map

\mathsf{mon}_{\infty}(\mathcal{E})^{\Delta^{1}}\to\mathsf{mon}_{\infty}(\mathcal{E})^{2}

over
(\mathcal{M},T)
. Since we have

(3.9)

\mathsf{mon}_{\infty}(\mathcal{E})^{\Delta^{1}}\cong\mathsf{Map}(\Delta^{1}\otimes\mathrm{N}(\Delta_{+}),\mathcal{E}),

and

(3.10)

\mathsf{mon}_{\infty}(\mathcal{E})^{2}\cong\mathsf{Map}(\partial\Delta^{1}\otimes\mathrm{N}(\Delta_{+}),\mathcal{E}),

we need to prove that the fiber of the map

(3.11)

over
(\mathcal{M},T)
is contractible. Therefore, it is sufficient to check the assumptions of Lemma

.

The
n
-th component of the basis of
\Delta^{1}\otimes\mathrm{N}(\Delta_{+})
consists of pairs
(\alpha,f),
where
\alpha:[n]\to[1]
and
f\in X_{n}.
The basis of
\partial\Delta^{1}\otimes\mathrm{N}(\Delta_{+})
consists of such pairs
(\alpha,f),
where
\alpha\equiv 0
or
\alpha\equiv 1.
Therefore, the basis of the inclusion
\partial\Delta^{1}\otimes\mathrm{N}(\Delta_{+})\hookrightarrow\Delta^{1}\otimes\mathrm{N}(\Delta_{+})

such that

is surjective. It follows that

and the conditions of Lemma

are satisfied.

.

and the Kan complex of
\infty
-monoid structures

Proof.

Combining Lemma

First, let us make some general remarks about a general

\mathcal{C}
and its object

[

, Prop. 2.1.3]

that induces an equivalence

\mathcal{C}

\mathcal{C}

The identity map

[

, Exercise 135]

There are two different functors

We claim that the images
\varphi_{\mathcal{C}^{c/}}(x)
and
(\varphi_{\mathcal{C}})_{*}(x)
of any object

of

are equivalent in
\mathcal{C}^{c/}
. We can present the category
(\mathcal{C}^{c/})^{{\sf id}_{c}/}

3.3.

The category of simplicial categories

[

[

). Then we have a natural adjunction

\mathsf{Fun}_{\Delta}(\mathbf{C}\times\mathbf{C}^{\prime},\mathbf{C}^{\prime\prime})\cong\mathsf{Fun}_{\Delta}(\mathbf{C},\mathsf{Fun}_{\Delta}(\mathbf{C}^{\prime},\mathbf{C}^{\prime\prime}))

[

, §2.3]

\mathbf{C}
and
\mathbf{C}^{\prime}

4.3.
Split cosimplicial objects

A coaugmented cosimplicial object

is

if it has extra degeneracy maps

for

satisfying the simplicial relations. Dually, it is called
left split
if it has extra degeneracy maps
s^{-1}:X^{n}\to X^{n-1}
satisfying the cosimplicial relations. It is easy to see that
X
is left split if and only if
X^{\mathrm{op}}
is right split. So we consider only right split cosimplicial objects. Let us reformulate the property of being right split in terms of
\Delta_{\mathrm{max}}

The inclusion functor
\Delta_{\mathrm{max}}\hookrightarrow\Delta_{+}
admits a left adjoint

(4.17)

{\sf c}_{+}:\Delta_{+}\longrightarrow\Delta_{\mathrm{max}},\hskip 28.45274pt[n]\mapsto[n]\star[0]

[
Lur18
,
Rem.04S8
]
. Then a coaugmented cosimplicial object
X:\Delta_{+}\to C
is right split if and only if it can be presented as a composition

(4.18)

\Delta_{+}\xrightarrow{{\sf c}_{+}}\Delta_{\mathrm{max}}\to C.

Similarly we can define
\Delta_{\rm min}
and then left split coaugmented cosimplicial objects are those that factor via
\Delta_{+}\to\Delta_{\rm min},n\mapsto[0]\star n
.

Now we generalize it to the case of an
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
. A coaugmented cosimplicial object

is
right split
, if it factors as a composition

(4.19)

\mathrm{N}(\Delta_{+})\xrightarrow{\mathrm{N}({\sf c}_{+})}\mathrm{N}(\Delta_{\mathrm{max}})\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}.

Similarly we can define left split coaugmented cosimplicial objects.

The limit of a cosimplicial object
\bar{X}:\mathrm{N}(\Delta)\to\mathcal{C}
is called the homotopy totalization of
\bar{X}
.

Proposition 4.7

(
[
Lur18
,
Prop. 04SH
]
)
.

Let

be a right split coaugmented cosimplicial object and let

be its restriction. Then
X
is a limit diagram of
\bar{X}
and the natural map to the homotopy totalization of
\bar{X}

(4.20)

X(0)\xrightarrow{\sim}\lim\bar{X}

is an equivalence.

Proposition
4.2
.
Let
(E,C)
be a strict action category and
(M,x)
be an action object in

. Then there exists a unique morphism of strict action categories

(8.16)

to

Proof.

We know that there exists a unique strict monoidal functor
\varphi:\Delta_{+}\to E
sending the monoid
[0]
to the monoid
M.
Therefore, we need to prove that there exists a unique functor
\psi:\Delta_{\mathrm{max}}\to C
such that
(\varphi,\psi)
is a morphism of strict action categories sending the action object
([0],[0])
to
(M,x)
. Denote by
a_{x}:M\oslash x\to x
the
M
-action on
x
and by

the map defined recursively by

(8.17)

where

It is easy to see that any morphism in

\Delta_{+}

\psi([n])=M^{\otimes n}\oslash x,\hskip 28.45274pt\psi(f\star a^{n})=\varphi(f)\oslash a^{n}_{x}.

), then

Further in the proof we use the ordinal notations

Note that for any morphism

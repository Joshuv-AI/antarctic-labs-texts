A geometrical approach to solve the proximity of a point to an axisymmetric quadric in space
Bibekananda Patra, Aditya Mahesh Kolte, Sandipan Bandyopadhyay

Abstract
This paper presents the classification of a general quadric into an axisymmetric quadric (AQ) and the solution to the problem of the proximity of a given point to an AQ. The problem of proximity in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} is reduced to the same in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} , which is not found in the literature. A new method to solve the problem in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} is used based on the geometrical properties of the conics, such as sub-normal, length of the semi-major axis, eccentricity, slope and radius. Furthermore, the problem in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} is categorised into two and three more sub-cases for parabola and ellipse/hyperbola, respectively, depending on the location of the point, which is a novel approach as per the authors’ knowledge. The proposed method is suitable for implementation in a common programming language, such as C and proved to be faster than a commercial library, namely, Bullet .



Keywords: Axisymmetric quadric, Central axisymmetric quadric, Proximity, Conics

3
Computation of the proximity of a given point to an axisymmetric quadric

There are seven types of AQs, namely, (a) spheroid, (b) hyperboloid of one sheet, (c) hyperboloid of two sheets, (d) cone, (e) paraboloid, (f) cylinder, and (g) sphere. The motivation for computing the proximity of a given point to an AQ is that the problem can be reduced from
\mathbb{R}^{3}
to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and solved using the geometric properties of the planar quadratic curves, i.e., conics. The reason behind doing so is that the solution process is more geometrically intuitive and less computationally expensive than the problem of computing the proximity of a point to an AQ in space. To solve the problem in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, a plane, described by
\mathcal{P}
, that intersects a given AQ and passes through the given point, must be identified. The intersection of the plane with AQs results in either ellipse, hyperbola, parabola, pair of non-coincident vertical parallel lines, pair of intersecting lines, or circle. The determination of
\mathcal{P}
is discussed in Section
3.1
.

There are two types of a spheroid, namely,

3.1.2
Intersection of
\mathcal{P}
with hyperboloid of one sheet and hyperboloid of two sheets

The intersection of
\mathcal{P}
with a hyperboloid of one sheet results in a hyperbola with the major axis along the
\boldsymbol{u}_{1}
-axis. The equation for the hyperbola is defined as:

|\lambda_{1}|u_{1}^{2}-|\lambda_{3}|u_{2}^{2}-|\gamma|=0.

(28)

Similarly, the intersection of
\mathcal{P}
with a hyperboloid of two sheets results in a hyperbola with the major axis along the
\boldsymbol{u}_{2}
-axis and the equation for the same is defined as:

|\lambda_{3}|u_{2}^{2}-|\lambda_{1}|u_{1}^{2}-|\gamma|=0.

(29)

3.1.3
Intersection of
\mathcal{P}
with a cone

The intersection of
\mathcal{P}
and a cone results in a pair of intersecting straight lines passing through the origin
\boldsymbol{o}
. The equation for the pair of intersecting lines passing through the origin is defined as:

\sqrt{|\lambda_{1}|}u_{1}\pm\sqrt{|\lambda_{3}|}u_{2}=0.

(30)

3.1.4
Intersection of
\mathcal{P}
with a sphere

As a sphere is symmetric about all the three axes, and its intersection with
\mathcal{P}
is a circle. The equation of the circle is defined as:

u_{1}^{2}+u_{2}^{2}-\left|\frac{\gamma}{\lambda_{1}}\right|=0,\quad\lambda_{1}\neq 0.

(31)

3.1.5
Intersection of
\mathcal{P}
with a paraboloid

The intersection of
\mathcal{P}
with a paraboloid yields a parabola whose axis of symmetry is along the
\boldsymbol{u}_{2}
-axis. The equation of the parabola is as follows:

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}u_{1}^{2}+\gamma_{\text{l}}u_{2}=0,\quad\text{where}

(32)

\displaystyle\gamma_{\text{l}}=2\boldsymbol{u}_{2}\cdot\left(\boldsymbol{c}+\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{p}_{\text{c}}\right).

3.1.6
Intersection of
\mathcal{P}
with a cylinder

The intersection of
\mathcal{P}
with a cylinder yields a pair of non-coincident vertical parallel lines. The equation for a pair of a parallel lines is obtained as:

\lambda_{1}u_{1}^{2}-|\gamma|=0.

(33)

Discussions

The key points in this paper are noted in the following.

The classifications of the general quadric into an AQ is shown in the flowchart, as shown in Fig.
4
. Even though there is literature that mentions the classification of a quadric, no article has been found that discusses characterising a quadric as AQ. Furthermore, instead of using
rank
of
\boldsymbol{B}
, and
\boldsymbol{A}
for the classification, a different set of parameters such as
\det(\boldsymbol{A}),
and the invariant of
\boldsymbol{B}
, i.e.,
J_{3}
, are used which is advantageous in terms of computational efficiency.

The proximity of a given point to an ellipsoid and hyperboloids in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
are well studied either analytically or numerically. However, the proximity of a point to the remaining AQs are not found to be in literature. Hence, an extensive studies are performed to compute the proximity to a given point to any AQ.

The problem of computing the proximity of a given point to an AQ in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is reduced to the same in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
due to two reasons. First, the problem becomes more geometrically intuitive, i.e., the proximity of a given point and a conic in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Hence, the geometrical properties of the conics such as sub-normal, length of the semi-major axis and eccentricity are used in the derivation of the univariate in the case of parabola, ellipse, and hyperbola. Similarly, for the case of pair of intersecting lines and pair of non-coincident vertical parallel lines, the proximity is computed based on the projection of the point
\boldsymbol{p}_{\text{p}}
onto the lines. In addition to that, solving the problem in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is computationally less expensive than solving it in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, which involves solving three quadratic in equations in
\boldsymbol{x}
, due to the size
5

[

6
Conclusions

A new and effective way of classifying a general quadric is introduced by using the invariants of the coefficient matrices, instead of computing their ranks. The novel approach of solving the proximity problem of a point to an AQ by reducing from
\mathbb{R}^{3}
to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and categorising the problem in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
into different sub-cases for parabola and ellipse/hyperbola depending on the location of the point
\boldsymbol{p}_{\text{p}}
. The geometrical properties of the conics are used to derive the univariate for which the size of the symbolic expression is lesser than the size of the univariate if the problem is solved in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
The numerical examples are set-up for all the AQs to validate the geometrical method and implemented in
C
to show the computational efficiencies. It is observed that the classifying a quadric into an AQ and computing the proximity do not take more than 90 ns on a PC. The
Bullet
library with suitable modifications is used for comparing the execution time to compute the proximity of a point to the right circular cone, and of a point to a sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and it is found that the proposed geometrical method is faster than the commercial library. Hence, such method is highly efficient for the applications like design of a robot, collision avoidance, etc., which would be a future scope of this work.

The nullspace of

\boldsymbol{B}_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}\zeta_{11}&\zeta_{12}&\zeta_{13}\\
\zeta_{21}&\zeta_{22}&\zeta_{23}\\
\zeta_{31}&\zeta_{32}&\zeta_{33}\end{bmatrix},\quad\text{where}~\zeta_{11}\neq 0,~\text{and}~\|[\zeta_{11},\zeta_{12},\zeta_{13}]^{\top}\|\neq 0.

The second and third row of

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{w}_{2}

\displaystyle=[0,\zeta_{11}\zeta_{22}-\zeta_{12}\zeta_{21},\zeta_{11}\zeta_{23}-\zeta_{13}\zeta_{21}]^{\top},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{w}_{3}

\displaystyle=[0,\zeta_{11}\zeta_{32}-\zeta_{12}\zeta_{31},\zeta_{11}\zeta_{33}-\zeta_{31}\zeta_{13}]^{\top}.

As any of the two rows are linearly dependent, either

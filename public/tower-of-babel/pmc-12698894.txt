Evaluation of work life balance and mental states of Eskişehir Tepebaşı municipality employees
Authors: Alaettin Ünsal, Mine Tepetaş, Mediha Bal, Didem Arslantaş, Sibel Sert, Selva Dilan Gölbaşı Koç
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThe aim of this study was to determine the level of work-life balance of municipal employees, to examine some variables that are thought to be related to it, and to evaluate their mental state. This study is the first known study to assess the work-life balance and mental health of municipal employees.Material and methodThis is a cross-sectional study conducted with 1,104 municipal employees between May and June 2023. The Work-Life Balance Scale (WLBS) and the Depression Anxiety Stress Scale-21 (DASS-21) were used in the study. Data were analyzed using the Mann Whitney U test, Kruskal Wallis test, Spearman correlation analysis, and multiple linear regression analysis. The higher the score on the WLBS, the more negative the balance between work and life.ResultsThe mean WLBS score of the employees was 42.65 ± 9.63. In our study, the prevalence of suspected depression was 38.8%, of suspected anxiety 38.1% and of suspected stress disorders 22.6%. Work-life balance was found to be worse among those with an educational level of secondary school or higher, those with a working spouse, those who did not receive social support from their environment, those who could not spend time on social activities outside work, and those with suspected anxiety and stress disorders.ConclusionIt can be said that work-life balance among municipal employees is at a moderate level. Educational status, social support, time spent outside work, and the presence of suspected anxiety and stress disorder according to DASS-21 were found to be important predictors of work-life balance. More comprehensive studies are needed to uncover the relationship between work-life balance and mental status.Keywords: Municipal employees, Work life balance, DASS-21, Eskişehir

Introduction

A considerable part of human life is devoted to work in order to secure an economic livelihood. In today's world of work, which takes up a lot of time and energy, it is important to establish a balance between professional and private life and to understand the factors that influence this balance. This is especially true for professionals, such as municipal employees, who provide essential services under difficult conditions. In this regard, it is of paramount importance to determine the level of work-life balance and psychological well-being of municipal employees, to examine the variables associated with this balance and to assess their mental health status.

Municipal employees are public sector workers responsible for providing local services such as urban planning, water and wastewater management, transport, sanitation, security, firefighting, emergency response, environmental regulations and maintenance. These tasks often require adherence to rigid schedules, maintenance of public order, rapid response to community needs and involvement in high-risk decision-making processes, which can increase work pressure and make it difficult to balance work and family life. Municipal work is characterised by stressors such as a fast-paced environment, irregular or extended working hours, shift work, on-call duties, security risks and seasonal or event-related fluctuations in workload. These elements can lead to a diminished sense of control over daily life and increased conflict between work and personal commitments, disrupting work-life balance [1, 2]. Research on public service employees shows that leadership, flexibility in scheduling and public accountability affect the work-life balance and mental health of frontline employees [3, 4]. Therefore, it is important and particularly necessary to assess the work-life balance and mental health of municipal employees.

The concept of work-life balance (WLB) was originally introduced by Kanter in the mid-1970s to describe the balance between work and private life [5]. The most widely accepted definition of WLB is “the perception that a person's work and non-work activities are compatible and support their development according to their current life priorities” [6]. According to common definitions, this construct includes both objective elements, such as working hours, holidays, family commitments and job characteristics, and subjective elements, such as work-centred and non-work-centred life orientations, which together determine how individuals perceive balance or imbalance [6, 7]. Sturges and Guest have developed a model that outlines the structure, determinants and outcomes of WLB. The structure of this balance is made up of objective factors such as working hours, leisure time and family commitments, as well as subjective factors such as the importance of life outside work and the perceived shift in balance between work and home/family. According to their model, the determinants of WLB include individual factors (e.g. age, gender, personality traits, coping mechanisms and job fit) and organisational factors (e.g. work demands, work culture and external demands) [8]. Also Kalliath and Brough define WLB as the balancing of multiple life roles, engagement in work and family, and the achievement of equal satisfaction in all areas of life through the effective allocation of personal resources, including energy, time, and commitment. They assume that WLB is associated with meeting the demands of balancing productivity and satisfaction in work and family roles with a person's life priorities at any given time. This includes maintaining a low level of role conflict and a high level of role facilitation, leading to an increased sense of perceived work-family balance and giving individuals a sense of control over where, when and how they work [7].

A disrupted work-life balance is associated with negative consequences, such as reduced physical and mental health, lower quality of life, increased levels of depression and anxiety, increased stress and lower job satisfaction and productivity [4]. From an organisational perspective, such an imbalance can lead to a higher risk of burnout, lower employee engagement and a higher frequency of accidents—findings that are particularly critical in the public sector, where safety and continuity of services are of paramount importance. In addition, public sector studies have shown a link between work-life balance and the physical, mental and social wellbeing of employees [9, 10]. Key predictors of work-life balance also include education level, spouse's employment status, social support and psychological states such as depression, anxiety and stress, as well as organisational factors such as flexible scheduling, autonomy, management support and organisational culture [9, 11].

Municipal employees play a key role in the provision of public services. Their extensive responsibilities include urban infrastructure (e.g. land use, water, sanitation, and transport), geographic and urban information systems, sanitation and waste disposal, policing, firefighting, emergency response, urban transport and the maintenance of parks and green spaces. Carrying out these tasks often requires a demanding work schedule. The fast-paced nature of their work can pose a major challenge to work-life balance. While numerous studies have investigated work-life balance and mental health in different occupational groups, there is a notable gap in the literature that relates specifically to municipal employees. Therefore, the aim of this study is to determine the work-life balance of the employees of Tepebaşı Municipality in Eskişehir Province, to examine the variables that are thought to be related to work-life balance, and to assess their mental states that are thought to interact with their work-life balance.

Material and method

The study is a cross-sectional study conducted between May 02 and June 30, 2023 among the employees of Tepebaşı Municipality in Eskişehir. Approval for data collection was obtained from the Non-Interventional Research Ethics Committee of Eskişehir Osmangazi University dated 06.05.2023 with the number 63 and written approval from Tepebaşı Municipality dated 18.05.2023 with the number E9840054100075808.

According to the 2023 data, the population of Tepebaşı district, which is one of the two central districts established in 1993 after the city of Eskişehir was granted metropolitan status, is 390,116, of which 194,919 are men and 195,197 are women. The municipality of Tepebaşı has a total of 2220 employees, of which 1782 are employees of companies and 438 are permanent employees (workers, civil servants, contract workers). 772 (34.8%) of employees in the municipality are female and 1448 (65.2%) are male [12, 13].

A questionnaire was used to collect data in the study, which was created with the help of the literature [7, 14–17]. The questionnaire was designed to collect data on some socio-demographic characteristics of municipal employees (age, gender, marital status, educational level, children, working style, total working hours in the municipality, working status, weekly working hours, family income, general health status, smoking etc.), The questionnaire contains some variables that are hypothesized to be associated with work-life balance, the questionnaire contains some variables that are assumed to be related to work-life balance (history of mental illness, time for social activities outside work, history of work accident, social support, participation in job-related training, satisfaction with the physical conditions of the work environment) as well as questions on the Work-Life Balance Scale (WLBS) and the Depression Anxiety Stress Scale-21 (DASS-21).

Our study aimed to include all municipal employees. The questionnaires, prepared in advance, were distributed to employees with the assistance of the municipality's department heads. The employees were visited in their departments on predetermined dates and informed about the topic and purpose of the study. Once verbal consent had been obtained from those who agreed to participate in the study, the questionnaires were completed by the employees under observation. This process took about 15–20 min. The study group consisted of 1104 municipal employees (49.73%). The power of the study was calculated as 0.98 using the G Power 3.1 program.

The Work-Life Balance Scale was used to assess the work-life balance of municipal employees. This scale was developed by Fisher et al. in 2009 [15], and the Turkish validity and reliability study was conducted by Ekinci and Sabancı in 2021 [18]. The scale consists of a total of 17 5-point Likert-type questions. The answers to the questions are “strongly disagree: 1 point”, “disagree: 2 points”, “undecided: 3 points”, "agree: 4 points" and "strongly agree: 5 points". The higher the score on the scale, the more negative the balance between work and life. Cronbach's Alpha reliability coefficient of the scale was found to be 0.701.

The employees' mental state was assessed using the Depression Anxiety Stress Scale-21 (DASS-21). The DASS-21 was developed by Lovibond and Lovibond in 1995 [19], and its validity and reliability study in Türkiye was conducted by Sarıçam in 2018 [20]. The scale consists of 21 questions in 4-point Likert-type. The answers to the questions are “never: 0 points”, “sometimes and occasionally: 1 point”, “quite often: 2 points” and "always: 3 points". The scale has three sub-dimensions: Depression (questions 3, 5, 10, 13, 16, 17 and 21), Anxiety (questions 2, 4, 7, 9, 15, 19 and 20) and Stress (questions 1, 6, 8, 11, 12, 14 and 18). As the scores in the individual sub-dimensions increase, so do the scores for depression, anxiety and stress. Those who score 5 or more in the depression subfield are "suspected of having depression”, those who score 4 or more in the anxiety subfield are "suspected of having an anxiety disorder" and those who score 8 or more in the stress subfield are "suspected of having a stress disorder”. The Cronbach’s alpha internal consistency coefficient of the scale was found as 0.85 for the depression subscale, 0.80 for the anxiety subscale and 0.77 for the stress subscale.

The family income status of employees was rated as “good, medium and bad” according to their own assessment. Those who regularly smoked at least 1 cigarette per day in the last 6 months were classified as “smokers”.

The data obtained was analyzed on the computer using the statistical program SPSS (version 15.0). The suitability of the data for a normal distribution was determined using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Number, percentage, mean ± standard deviation (SD), median, minimum (min) and maximum (max) values were used as descriptive statistics. As the data were not suitable for normal distribution, the Mann Whitney U test, Kruskal Wallis test, Spearman correlation analysis and multiple linear regression analysis (Enter method) were used for comparisons between groups. A statistical significance value of p < 0.05 was accepted.

Discussion

The satisfaction of employees in their private and professional lives has a positive effect on achieving a work-life balance. It is well known that the most important factor for work-life balance is people's mental health, and good mental health is very important for work-life balance. Some mental health problems such as depression and anxiety have a negative impact on work-life balance and limit employees' ability to lead happier, healthier and more satisfying lives.

Work-life balance is of paramount importance for municipal employees who provide public services under often demanding and stressful conditions. This is crucial not only for their mental and physical well-being, but also to ensure the continuity and quality of the vital services provided to the community. In our study, the work-life balance of municipal employees was found to be at a moderate level according to their scores on the Work-Life Balance Scale. Similar results were reported in some studies by Törnquist et al (22 points/min 6-max 30 points) and Abdelhay et al (43,7 points/min 15-max 75 points) [4, 21]. In a study conducted by Flatoy, it was reported that the work-life balance of the study participants was low (2,86 points/min 0-max 16 points) [22]. The different results of the various studies may be due to the use of different measurement tools and the fact that the studies were conducted in different countries and among different professional groups.

The level of education is a key factor influencing the work-life balance of municipal employees, as a higher level of education often comes with more demanding tasks and more responsibility, which can increase pressure in the workplace. This can make it more difficult to maintain an effective boundary between work and personal life as the intellectual and emotional energy required to fulfil these tasks increases. In our study, it was found that one of the factors related to work-life balance is education level. In a study conducted by Yıldırım, it was reported that a higher level of education has a negative effect on work-life balance [23]. Some studies reported that there was no relationship between education level and work-life balance [24, 25]. Differences in work-life balance across education levels may stem from differences in how occupational expectations, task demands, and autonomy are shaped by different levels of education.

The employment status of a spouse can have a significant impact on work-life balance, as a dual-earner household can place complex demands on time, energy and shared domestic responsibilities. The presence of a working partner may require a more conscious and adaptable approach to balancing work commitments with home and family life. In this study, working spouses were found to have a poorer work-life balance than non-working spouses. In a study conducted by Wheatley on employees in public and private organisations, it was reported that there were difficulties in balancing work and family life in families where spouses worked [26]. In a study conducted by Yalçın, it was reported that there was no difference between work-life balance and whether the spouses were working or not [27]. Differences in the reported impact of employing a spouse on work-life balance may be attributed to differences in the specific professional roles and domestic responsibilities of both partners, as well as the unique demographic characteristics and organisational cultures of the workplaces studied. In addition, the reason for the different results could be that the studies were conducted in countries where the work-related roles of the spouses are different.

A stable support network of family, friends and colleagues can mitigate the negative effects of work-related stress and help manage competing demands. This support facilitates a more harmonious integration of work and private life and thus contributes to general well-being and work-life balance. In our study, it was found that those who reported that the social support they received from their environment was sufficient had a better work-life balance. Similarly, in the studies conducted by Tuğsal and Annink among employees in different sectors, it was reported that the emotional and social support that employees receive from their families, spouses, managers, colleagues or social circles positively influences the level of work-life balance of employees [28, 29]. The literature consistently reports that social support positively influences work-life balance because it serves as a crucial buffer against work-related stress and enables better management of competing demands. This consensus among studies is based on the universal psychological benefits of a robust support system that helps individuals maintain their well-being and have a sense of control over their life role.

Participation in non-work activities is a crucial factor for work-life balance, as these activities provide a necessary outlet for stress relief and personal fulfilment outside of their professional role. Engaging in hobbies, social events or leisure activities can lead to a more effective separation of work and personal life and thus increase mental well-being. In our study, it was found that employees who were able to make time for activities outside of work had a better work-life balance. Akın et al. reported that improving relationships in work and non-work environments has a positive effect on work-life balance [17]. In a study conducted by Gürel, it was reported that participants wanted to do some activities outside of work while trying to establish a work-life balance [30]. The positive impact of non-work activities on work-life balance is consistently reported across studies because these activities facilitate psychological detachment from work, which is a key mechanism for stress recovery and burnout prevention. This consistent finding suggests a universal benefit of engaging in restorative activities outside of work demands.

Numerous stressors in workplaces that require a high level of responsibility have a negative impact on achieving work-life balance by causing stress in employees and making time management difficult due to the fast pace of work [31, 32]. On the other hand, people who try to balance their work and life spheres sometimes get into conflicts in this process and become stressed due to demoralization, burnout and some health problems. People who cannot achieve work-life balance fall into a deep emotional void due to the stress they experience and experience depression and burnout [33, 34]. In our study, it was found that work-life balance was not sufficiently established in patients with suspected depression, anxiety and stress disorders. Further analysis showed that the association between depression and work-life balance disappeared, while anxiety and stress were found to be important factors of establishing work-life balance. Whiston et al. also reported that work-life balance worsened with increasing level of depression [35]. In a study conducted by Frone, it was reported that there is an inverse relationship between the level of work-life balance and anxiety [36]. In a study conducted by Tabak with municipal employees, a negative relationship was found between the degree of work-life balance and stress level [37]. These consistent findings suggest that the relationship between work-life balance and psychological well-being is fundamental, as a person’s inability to manage the competing demands of work and home life directly affects psychological and emotional resources, leading to a predictable increase in anxiety and stress levels.

Limitations

Our study cannot establish causality due to its cross-sectional design. Establishing causality requires longitudinal data or mixed-method approaches. 49.7% of employees participated in the study, and this may skew the results if non-participants are systematically different. Our findings are limited to Tepebaşı Municipality and may not be valid for other regions/countries with different work cultures. The scales used in the study and the income level inquiry were self-reported, meaning they rely on subjective responses that may be influenced by social desirability or temporary mood states.

Conclusion

The review of national and international literature sources did not reveal sufficient studies investigating the relationship between work-life balance and the mental health of municipal employees. In this context, our study is one of the first studies to help fill a gap in the literature. Conducting studies that examine the relationship between work-life balance and mental health in municipalities that strive to maintain social order and provide services to the community will help to ensure social peace by improving the quality of social services.

Our study suggests that work-life balance among municipal employees is moderate. Similar to the model by Sturges and Guest [8], education level was found to be a significant predictor of work-life balance, but gender was not. Furthermore, employees reporting inadequate social support, no participation in outside activities, and suspected anxiety and stress had poorer work-life balance. To further improve the work-life balance of employees, it may be useful to create a work environment that supports good communication and collaboration, thereby improving work environments, provide training on stress management methods, and ensure early diagnosis and treatment of depression, anxiety and stress disorders by conducting screenings and referring those affected to advanced centers. More comprehensive studies are needed to uncover the link between work-life balance and mental health. Studies on this topic can be conducted in different sectors to contribute to the literature.

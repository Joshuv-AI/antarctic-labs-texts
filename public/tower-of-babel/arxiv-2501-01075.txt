Studying the 𝑩 𝟎 → 𝑱 / 𝝍 ​ 𝒉 𝟏 B^{0}\to J/\psi h_{1} decays with 𝒉 𝟏 ​ ( 𝟏𝟏𝟕𝟎 ) − 𝒉 𝟏 ​ ( 𝟏𝟒𝟏𝟓 ) h_{1}(1170)-h_{1}(1415) mixing in the perturbative QCD approach
Qin Chang 1,2 , De-Hua Yao 1 , Xin Liu 3

Abstract
In this paper, we study the B 0 → J / ψ ​ h 1 B^{0}\to J/\psi h_{1} decays for the first time by using perturbative QCD approach up to the presently known next-to-leading order accuracy. The vertex corrections present significant contribution to the amplitude. In the calculation, the mixing between two light axial-vector mesons h 1 ​ ( 1170 ) h_{1}(1170) and h 1 ​ ( 1415 ) h_{1}(1415) are also studied in detail. The observables including the branching ratios, polarization fractions and CP asymmetries are predicted and discussed explicitly. It is found that the B 0 → J / ψ ​ h 1 B^{0}\to J/\psi h_{1} decays have relatively large branching fractions, which are generally at the order of 𝒪 ⁡ ( 10 − 6 ∼ 10 − 3 ) {\cal O}(10^{-6}\sim 10^{-3}) , and thus are possible to be observed by the LHCb and Belle-II experiments in the near future. Moreover, they are very sensitive to the mixing angle θ \theta and can be used to test the values of θ \theta . In addition, some ratios between the branching fractions of B 0 → J / ψ ​ h 1 B^{0}\to J/\psi h_{1} decays can provide much stronger constraints on θ \theta due to their relatively small theoretical errors. The B 0 → J / ψ ​ h 1 B^{0}\to J/\psi h_{1} decays are generally dominated by the longitudinal polarization contributions, specifically, f L ​ ( B 0 → J / ψ ​ h 1 ) > 80 % f_{L}(B^{0}\to J/\psi h_{1})>80\% , except for the case that θ ∼ 35 ∘ \theta\sim 35^{\circ} and − 55 ∘ -55^{\circ} . Unfortunately, the direct CP asymmetries of B 0 → J / ψ ​ h 1 B^{0}\to J/\psi h_{1} decays are too small to be observed soon even if the effect of θ \theta is considered. The future precise measurements on B 0 → J / ψ ​ h 1 B^{0}\to J/\psi h_{1} decays are expected for testing these theoretical findings and exploring the interesting nature of h 1 ​ ( 1170 ) h_{1}(1170) and h 1 ​ ( 1415 ) h_{1}(1415) .

\displaystyle R_{d}^{\rm QF}\equiv\frac{{\cal B}\left(B_{d}^{0}\to J/\psi h_{1}(1170)\right)}{{\cal B}\left(B_{d}^{0}\to J/\psi h_{1}(1415)\right)}=\frac{\Phi\left(m_{B_{d}},m_{J/\psi},m_{h_{1}(1170)}\right)}{\Phi\left(m_{B_{d}},m_{J/\psi},m_{h_{1}(1415)}\right)}\cdot\cot^{2}\alpha\;,

(58)

\displaystyle R_{s}^{\rm QF}\equiv\frac{{\cal B}\left(B_{s}^{0}\to J/\psi h_{1}(1415)\right)}{{\cal B}\left(B_{s}^{0}\to J/\psi h_{1}(1170)\right)}=\frac{\Phi\left(m_{B_{s}},m_{J/\psi},m_{h_{1}(1415)}\right)}{\Phi\left(m_{B_{s}},m_{J/\psi},m_{h_{1}(1170)}\right)}\cdot\cot^{2}\alpha\;,

(59)

where the ratios
R_{d}^{\rm QF}
and
R_{s}^{\rm QF}
are independent on the decay amplitudes.
They could provide a much more convenient and clear way to extract the angle
\alpha
when they
are measured by the future experiments. The mixing angle
\theta
can be further obtained via
Eq. (
12
). Beside of the ratios mentioned above, the ratios defined as

\displaystyle R_{ds}^{\rm SO}[h_{1}(1170)]\equiv\frac{{\cal B}\left(B_{d}^{0}\to J/\psi h_{1}(1170)\right)}{{\cal B}\left(B_{s}^{0}\to J/\psi h_{1}(1170)\right)}\;,\qquad R_{sd}^{\rm SO}[h_{1}(1415)]\equiv\frac{{\cal B}\left(B_{s}^{0}\to J/\psi h_{1}(1415)\right)}{{\cal B}\left(B_{d}^{0}\to J/\psi h_{1}(1415)\right)}\;,

(60)

can also be used to constrain the absolute value of mixing angle
\theta
. Our numerical results are

\displaystyle R_{ds}^{\rm SO}[h_{1}(1170)]\approx 6.38_{-0.89}^{+1.17}\;,\qquad R_{sd}^{\rm SO}[h_{1}(1415)]\approx 13.18_{-1.92}^{+2.02}\times 10^{2}\;,

(61)

in which, all errors arising from various input parameters have been added in quadrature.

Now, we turn to investigate the polarization fractions of the
B^{0}\to J/\psi h_{1}

Based on the helicity amplitudes given in Eqs. (

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{L}

IV
Conclusions and summary

In this paper, we have calculated the
B^{0}\to J/\psi h_{1}
decays for the first time by using PQCD approach,
where
h_{1}=h_{1}(1170)
and
h_{1}(1415)
are treated as the mixtures of
\tilde{h}_{1}
and
\tilde{h}_{8}

with mixing angle
\theta
in the singlet-octet basis. The observables of these decays are predicted. The NLO order
corrections are considered in the calculation because the vertex corrections with the NLO Wilson coefficients
contribute significantly to the color-suppressed decay modes. We take
\theta=29.5^{\circ}

as default input, and the effects of
\theta
on the observables are discussed in detail.
Our conclusions can be summarized briefly as following,

•

The
B^{0}\to J/\psi h_{1}
decays have relatively large branching fractions,
which are generally at the order of
{\cal O}(10^{-6}\sim 10^{-3})
, and thus are possible
to be measured by the LHCb and Belle-II experiments in the near future. The measurements
with high precision can provide useful information for basic nature of
h_{1}(1170)

and
h_{1}(1415)
.

•

Further considering the secondary decays,
(h_{1}(1415),f_{1}(1420))\to K_{S}^{0}K^{+}\pi^{-}
,
and comparing them with the LHCb data for
{\cal B}(B_{s}^{0}\to J/\psi K_{S}^{0}K^{+}\pi^{-})
,
we find that the
h_{1}(1415)
resonance serves possibly as a contributing state in the
m(K_{S}^{0}K^{\pm}\pi^{\mp})
distribution.

•

The branching fractions of
B^{0}\to J/\psi h_{1}
decays are very sensitive
to the mixing angle, and thus can be used to test the values of
\theta
.
Several interesting ratios between the branching fractions of
B^{0}\to J/\psi h_{1}

decays, such as
R_{d}^{\rm SO}
,
R_{s}^{\rm SO}
,
R_{ds}^{\rm SO}
, can effectively avoid
large theoretical errors caused by the hadronic inputs, and thus would provide much
stronger constraints on
\theta
.

•

All of the
B^{0}\to J/\psi h_{1}
decays are generally dominated by the longitudinal
polarization contributions, namely,
f_{L}(B^{0}\to J/\psi h_{1})>80\%
, with default
input
\theta=29.5^{\circ}
. The longitudinal fractions can be significantly reduced when
some specific values,
\theta\sim 35^{\circ}\,,-55^{\circ}
, are taken. Therefore, the polarization
fractions of
B^{0}\to J/\psi h_{1}
decays can provide restrict constraint on
\theta
.

•

Maybe the direct
CP
asymmetries of
B^{0}\to J/\psi h_{1}
decays are too small to be observed
soon even if the effect of
\theta
is considered.

Under the basis
\tilde{h}_{1}
and
\tilde{h}_{8}

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\langle\tilde{h}_{1}|H^{2}|\tilde{h}_{1}\rangle&\langle\tilde{h}_{1}|H^{2}|\tilde{h}_{8}\rangle\\

m_{\tilde{h}_{18}}^{2}&m_{\tilde{h}_{8}}^{2}\end{array}\right)\;.

\theta
can be derived by diagonalizing the mass matrix, and the mass matrix diagonalized according to the following relation,

\displaystyle R(\theta)M^{2}R(\theta)^{-1}=M_{diag.}^{2}\;.

h_{1}(1170)
and
h_{1}(1415)

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}m_{h_{1}(1170)}^{2}&0\\

m_{\tilde{h}_{18}}^{2}&m_{\tilde{h}_{8}}^{2}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\,\,\cos{\theta}&-\sin{\theta}\\
\,\,\sin{\theta}&\,\,\cos{\theta}\end{array}\right)\;.

\displaystyle m_{h_{1}(1170)}^{2}\cos^{2}\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m_{\tilde{h}_{1}}^{2}\cos^{2}\theta+m_{\tilde{h}_{18}}^{2}\sin\theta\cos\theta\;,

(88)

\displaystyle m_{h_{1}(1415)}^{2}\sin^{2}\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m_{\tilde{h}_{1}}^{2}\sin^{2}\theta-m_{\tilde{h}_{18}}^{2}\cos\theta\sin\theta\;,

(89)

\displaystyle m_{h_{1}(1170)}^{2}\sin^{2}\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m_{\tilde{h}_{18}}^{2}\cos\theta\sin\theta+m_{\tilde{h}_{8}}^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\;,

(90)

\displaystyle m_{h_{1}(1415)}^{2}\cos^{2}\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-m_{\tilde{h}_{18}}^{2}\sin\theta\cos\theta+m_{\tilde{h}_{8}}^{2}\cos^{2}\theta\;.

(91)

\displaystyle m_{\tilde{h}_{1}}^{2}

(93)

\displaystyle m_{\tilde{h}_{18}}^{2}

After further collation and simplification, the following results can be obtained,

h_{1}

the Levi-Civit
\grave{a}

\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}

\epsilon^{0123}=1

\phi_{A}(x)
and

[

\displaystyle\phi_{A}(x)

\displaystyle\frac{3f_{A}}{\sqrt{2N_{c}}}x(1-x)\biggl[3a_{1}^{\parallel}\left(2x-1\right)\biggr]\;,

(99)

\displaystyle\phi_{A}^{T}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3f_{A}}{\sqrt{2N_{c}}}x(1-x)\biggl[1+a_{2}^{\perp}\;\frac{3}{2}\left(5\left(2x-1\right)^{2}-1\right)\biggr]\;.

(100)

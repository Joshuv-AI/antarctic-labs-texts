Minijet thermalization and jet transport coefficients in QCD kinetic theory Preprint: MIT-CTP/5948
Kirill Boguslavski, Florian Lindenbauer, Aleksas Mazeliauskas, Adam Takacs, Fabian Zhou

Abstract
We apply weakly coupled QCD kinetic theory to investigate the thermalization of high-momentum on-shell partons (minijets) in a Quark-Gluon Plasma (QGP). Our approach incorporates isotropic hard thermal loop screening to model soft quark and gluon exchanges, allowing us to verify consistency with established analytic results of jet transport coefficients. We perform kinetic simulations of minijets propagating through a thermal gluon plasma, incorporating both collinear radiation and elastic scatterings. The resulting evolution is compared to predictions from jet transport coefficients, including the longitudinal and transverse jet-quenching parameters q ^ \hat{q} , energy loss, and the drag coefficient. We find that standard definitions of jet transport coefficients neglect the contributions from recoiling medium particles. Including these contributions restores consistency with the kinetic evolution. Finally, we show that the minijet thermalization time scales remarkably well with q ^ \hat{q} and we produce a phenomenological estimate of the minijet quenching time in heavy-ion collisions.

Introduction

The experimental and theoretical study of relativistic nuclear collisions has established a detailed picture of how the Quark–Gluon Plasma (QGP) forms under extreme conditions of temperature and density

Among the most prominent phenomenological signatures of QGP formation is the energy loss experienced by high-momentum partons traversing the medium. This process leads to a suppression of high-momentum hadrons and reconstructed jets (jet quenching) relative to an equivalent proton–proton baseline

Hard scatterings initially produce highly virtual partons that undergo rapid vacuum-like splittings. We refer to these on-shell partons as minijets to distinguish them from high-virtuality partons in vacuum showers.
A detailed theoretical understanding of how energetic partons interact with the evolving medium remains an outstanding problem.
Here we study how the energy and momentum transport of high-momentum partons relate to the evolution and thermalization of minijets in a kinetic framework.

II
QCD kinetic theory setup

The high-temperature QGP can be described as a weakly interacting gas of quarks and gluons. The particles are then characterized by phase-space distribution functions
f_{a}(t,\bm{x},\bm{p})=\frac{(2\pi)^{3}}{\nu_{a}}\frac{\mathrm{d}N_{a}}{\mathrm{d}^{3}\bm{x}\mathrm{d}^{3}\bm{p}}

is its number of degrees of freedom,
16

6

\left[\partial_{t}+\frac{\bm{p}}{p}\cdot\nabla_{\bm{x}}\right]f_{a}(t,\bm{x},\bm{p})=C_{a}[\{f\}](t,\bm{x},\bm{p})\,,

(1)

where

The relevant collision processes were systemized by Arnold, Moore and Yaffe (AMY)

using the hard thermal loop framework at leading order in the QCD coupling

They consist of elastic

massless particles

).

The elastic collision kernel is given by a multi-dimensional momentum integral

\begin{split}&C_{a}^{2\leftrightarrow 2}[\{f\}]=\frac{1}{4p\nu_{a}}\sum_{bcd}\int d\Omega^{2\leftrightarrow 2}\left|{\cal M}^{ab}_{cd}\right|^{2}\mathcal{F}^{2\leftrightarrow 2}\,,\end{split}

(2)

where we used the shorthand notation for the phase space integral

\begin{split}\int d\Omega^{2\leftrightarrow 2}&=\int_{p_{2}p_{3}p_{4}}(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{4}(P+P_{2}-P_{3}-P_{4})\,.\end{split}

(3)

with
\int_{\bm{p}}=\int\frac{{\rm d}^{3}\bm{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}}
, and
\int_{p}=\int_{\bm{p}}\frac{1}{2p}
. Here, the delta function imposes the 4-momentum conservation. The statistical gain-loss factor reads

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}^{2\leftrightarrow 2}&=f_{c}(\bm{p}_{3})f_{d}(\bm{p}_{4})(1\pm f_{a}(\bm{p}))(1\pm f_{b}(\bm{p}_{2}))\\
&-f_{a}(\bm{p})f_{b}(\bm{p}_{2})(1\pm f_{c}(\bm{p}_{3}))(1\pm f_{d}(\bm{p}_{4}))\,,\end{split}

The

Kurkela and Lu 2014
;
Kurkela and Zhu 2015
;

;
Du and Schlichting 2021b

Kurkela et al. 2019a
;
Kurkela et al. 2019b

Recently, the more complete isotropic HTL screening prescription was used in the Boltzmann equation in Refs.

;

Since the remainder of this paper focuses on minijets of finite momentum in a gluonic plasma, we delegate the detailed description of the HTL screening implementation and its validation against results in the literature to

The inelastic collision kernel is given by

\begin{split}C_{a}^{1\leftrightarrow 2}[\{f\}]=\frac{(2\pi)^{3}}{2|\bm{p}|^{2}\nu_{a}}\sum_{bc}&\int_{0}^{\infty}dp^{\prime}dk^{\prime}\delta(p-p^{\prime}-k^{\prime})\\

III
Jet transport coefficients from the Boltzmann equation

In this section, we introduce the EKT linearization framework and minijet dynamics, and we derive their relationship with traditional definitions and computations of jet transport coefficients. Finally, we make a numerical comparison between transport properties extracted from the linearized EKT evolution and from a direct evaluation.

III.1
Linearized Boltzmann equation and observables

The linearized Boltzmann equation for the minijet as a small perturbation around the homogeneous medium
(
f=n+\delta f
)
is given by

\partial_{t}\delta f_{i}(t,\bm{p})=\delta C_{i}[\{n,\delta f\}]\,,

(7)

where

is the linearized collision term. It contain elastic and inelastic scatterings

2

As we consider a non-expanding and homogeneous gluonic medium in thermal equilibrium, the background medium satisfies the Bose-Einstein distribution
n(p)=1/(e^{p/T}-1)

The initial condition for the minijet is, however, taken as a perturbation at momentum
\bm{p}_{0}

\delta f_{i}(t{=}t_{0},\bm{p})=\epsilon\,T^{3}\times(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{ii_{0}}\delta^{(3)}(\bm{p}-\bm{p}_{0})\,,

(8)

with initial species index

As the distribution function must be dimensionless, we have included a

Note that for numerical simulations of

We define jet observables through the expectation value

\begin{split}\langle\mathcal{O}(\bm{p};i)\rangle=\frac{\sum_{i}\nu_{i}\int_{\bm{p}}\mathcal{O}(\bm{p};i)\,\delta f_{i}(t,\bm{p})}{\sum_{i}\nu_{i}\int_{\bm{p}}\delta f_{i}(t{=}t_{0},\bm{p})}\,,\end{split}

(9)

where the denominator is evaluated for the initial distribution at

Note that this definition allows for multiple scatterings and thus a nontrivial evolution of the observables due to the dynamics of the minijet
\delta f_{i}(t,\bm{p})
, even for a non-evolving medium, such as in our case.
This allows us to study jet thermalization due to multiple elastic and inelastic scatterings with the medium, as well as transport coefficients by using a single-hit picture, which we can then compare to results in the literature.

Typical time-dependent observables that we consider are the average transverse and longitudinal momenta squared of the jet, which quantify momentum broadening (see also
Section

IV

\langle(\Delta\bm{p}_{\perp})^{2}\rangle,\quad\langle(\Delta\bm{p}_{\parallel})^{2}\rangle,\quad\langle(\bm{p}_{\parallel})^{2}\rangle\,.

(10)

\bm{p}\cdot\hat{\bm{p}}_{0}=p_{\parallel}

\bm{p}_{\perp}\cdot\hat{\bm{p}}_{0}=0

\bm{p}_{0}

III.2
Collisional expansion and transport coefficients

The linearized QCD kinetic theory can be used to redefine jet transport coefficients as single-hit observables and numerically compute them. To achieve this,

III.3
Difference between single-hit collision and HTL transport coefficients

Our result in Eq. (
19
) for the jet quenching parameter is very similar to previous determinations of
\hat{q}_{\perp}
in the high-temperature effective kinetic theory framework (
20
) (e.g., see
Aurenche et al. 2002
;
Arnold and Xiao 2008
;
Ghiglieri et al. 2016
;
Boguslavski et al. 2024b
).
The only difference is the additional term
{\bm{k}^{\prime}_{\perp}}^{2}-\bm{k}_{\perp}^{2}
in Eq. (
19
), which can be attributed to the
broadening of the recoiling medium
and which was omitted in previous works. In particular, this term can be associated with the
wake
that the jet leaves when traversing the medium.
However, due to the consistency of the LO kinetic approach, this term should not be neglected when considering the linearized Boltzmann equation. Furthermore, previous studies focused mainly on elastic collisions
\delta C^{2\leftrightarrow 2}
when evaluating transport coefficients, while
coefficients like
\hat{q}_{\parallel/L}
,
\eta_{D}
, or
\hat{\Gamma}_{i_{0}}
are dominated by

captured in
\delta C^{1\leftrightarrow 2}

In particular, traditionally, radiative contributions have been treated as higher-order corrections, while here we treat them within the single-hit expansion framework and show that they are non-negligible. Similar efforts have been recently done in Refs.
Liou et al. 2013
;
Blaizot and Mehtar-Tani 2014
;
Blaizot and Dominguez 2019
;
Arnold 2022
;
Arnold et al. 2022
identifying logarithmically enhanced corrections to
\hat{q}_{\perp}
. Beyond the single-hit limit, the Boltzmann equation re-iterates these radiative corrections, which has become of recent interest
Caucal and Mehtar-Tani 2022a
;
Caucal and Mehtar-Tani 2022b
;
Caucal and Mehtar-Tani 2023
;
Arnold et al. 2023a
;
Arnold et al. 2023b
;
Arnold et al. 2024
;
Arnold et al. 2025
.

The late time equilibration of the energy-momentum tensor is described by viscous hydrodynamics.

V
Conclusion

As a high-momentum parton (minjet) traverses a high-temperature QCD plasma, it interacts with the medium through scatterings characterized by jet transport coefficients, such as the transverse momentum broadening
\hat{q}_{\perp}
and drag coefficient
\eta_{D}
.
In this work, we investigated the thermalization of on-shell minijets in a weakly coupled
gluonic plasma using AMY kinetic simulations with elastic scatterings and collinear radiation on a thermal background. In particular, our implementation employs an isotropic hard-thermal-loop (HTL) screening of soft exchanges. Because the kinetic evolution incorporates both elastic and inelastic processes as well as the recoil of medium constituents from high-momentum partons, we observe quantitative deviations between the full EKT evolution and the standard definitions of transport coefficients.

We performed a single-scattering (opacity) expansion of the Boltzmann equation and derived general expressions for the expectation values of moments of the distribution function, such as the squared transverse momentum
\langle\bm{p}_{\perp}^{2}\rangle
. By defining transport coefficients through the time derivatives of these moments, we recover the conventional expressions known from analytic treatments. However, standard formulations of jet transport coefficients typically neglect the contributions from recoiling medium particles and inelastic processes. Our analysis demonstrates that these effects are quantitatively important and must be included for a consistent description of the minijet evolution. The transport coefficients extracted from the single-hit expansion show good agreement with the early-time behavior observed in full kinetic simulations. Finally, we show that the traditional expressions can be approximately recovered by introducing a large infrared cutoff that suppresses the recoil contributions.

We also studied the thermalization of a minijet in a non-expanding thermal medium. The early-time evolution is well described by jet-transport coefficients, as expected. Remarkably, when the evolution time is rescaled by these transport coefficients, most notably by the jet quenching coefficient
\hat{q}_{\perp}
, the results exhibit a universal scaling behavior across different coupling strengths and energies, extending smoothly up to the thermalization stage and outperforming previous rescalings based on
\eta/s
. From this analysis, we derived a compact phenomenological formula that provides realistic estimates of the minijet thermalization time in heavy-ion conditions.
Close to equilibrium, we confirm that the relaxation of perturbations is consistent with second-order hydrodynamic coefficients obtained in earlier studies.

A natural next step is to include dynamical quarks and quark–gluon conversion channels within the same kinetic framework. In
Appendix

B
we have already derived a practical implementation of isotropic HTL screening prescription for fermions and validated it against analytical result of gluon-quark conversion rate in
Fig.

9
.
However, our preliminary attempts to extract transport coefficients from the linearized EKT evolution including fermions suffer from numerical instabilities, which currently prevent a quantitative comparison with the direct computation of transport coefficients, such as done for the gluonic plasma in
Section

III.4
. These instabilities originate from the current implementation of the Monte Carlo sampler, which does not account for the fact that minijets have very small support in the sampled phase space. As a result, the sampling becomes highly inefficient, which is not the case for the smoother distributions encountered near equilibrium.
A revised sampling strategy tailored to such extreme initial conditions could improve numerical stability, which we leave for future work.

Another extension of this work is to relax the assumption of a static medium. Since the QGP expands rapidly, the evolving background can significantly modify the dynamics of minijet propagation. As discussed in
Zhou et al. 2024
and noted in this work, such dynamical effects introduce additional linear contributions to the collision kernels that stem from the perturbation of the (screened) matrix elements.
These terms also affect the transport properties of the minijet. However, their size and form can depend strongly on the chosen screening prescription for elastic scatterings and collinear splittings.
To our knowledge, these additional terms have not been included in previous studies involving non-equilibrium backgrounds
Boguslavski and Lindenbauer 2024
,
but are needed for a consistent linearized description of transport in an expanding and dynamically evolving medium.

Finally, for an evolving anisotropic medium there are further corrections that should be taken into account.
In particular,
currently the internal soft propagators are screened isotropically.
Large momentum-anisotropy of the non-equilibrium medium affects the collision kernels and the underlying collinear splitting rate
Altenburger et al. 2025
;
Lindenbauer 2025
. While previous studies within QCD kinetic theory
Abraao York et al. 2014
;
Kurkela and Lu 2014
;
Kurkela and Zhu 2015
;
Kurkela and Mazeliauskas 2019a
;
Kurkela and Mazeliauskas 2019b
;
Kurkela et al. 2021
;
Du and Schlichting 2021a
;
Du and Schlichting 2021b
;
Kurkela et al. 2019a
;
Kurkela et al. 2019b
;
Boguslavski and Lindenbauer 2024
neglected the effect of plasma instabilities resulting from the anisotropy
Mrowczynski 1993
;
Mrowczynski et al. 2017
, justified by the observation that they do not play a dominant role in the high-occupancy regime of the thermalization process
Berges et al. 2014a
;
Berges et al. 2014b
, recent studies have put forward suggestions of how they can be accounted for in the descriptions of hard probes and kinetic theory
Hauksson et al. 2021
;
Hauksson et al. 2022
. Further studies are needed to obtain a practical way of their inclusion in kinetic theory simulations.

In summary, our work demonstrated how the simplified picture encoded in jet transport coefficients emerges from the full kinetic evolution of on-shell partons in the QGP. Despite the differences in the details, we demonstrated that
\hat{q}
scaling provides a practical mapping from medium properties to jet quenching times.
Confronting this with system-size and energy dependencies in light- and heavy-ion collisions can deliver tighter constraints on jet-transport coefficients and on the onset of thermalization in small systems.

The Boltzmann equation can be written in the form
\partial_{t}f_{i}(\bm{p})=C_{i}[\{f\}]
.
Then the collision kernel includes elastic and radiative processes,

\begin{split}&C_{a}^{2\leftrightarrow 2}[\{f\}]=\frac{1}{4p_{1}\nu_{a}}\sum_{bcd}\int d\Omega^{2\leftrightarrow 2}\left|{\cal M}^{ab}_{cd}(\bm{p}_{1},\bm{p}_{2};\bm{p}_{3},\bm{p}_{4})\right|^{2}\mathcal{F}(\bm{p}_{1},\bm{p}_{2};\bm{p}_{3},\bm{p}_{4})\,,\end{split}

(35)

where the phase space integral is

\begin{split}\int d\Omega^{2\leftrightarrow 2}&=\int\frac{d^{3}\bm{p}_{2}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{2E_{2}}\frac{d^{3}\bm{p}_{3}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{2E_{3}}\frac{d^{3}\bm{p}_{4}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{2E_{4}}(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{4}(P_{1}+P_{2}-P_{3}-P_{4})\,,\end{split}

(36)

with the statistical factor

Appendix C
Expectation values in the single collision limit

The expectation value of a general observable
\mathcal{O}(\bm{p},i)
is

\begin{split}\hat{\mathcal{O}}(t)&=\sum_{i}\nu_{i}\int_{\bm{p}}\mathcal{O}(\bm{p},i)\delta f_{i}(\bm{p},t)\,,\end{split}

(67)

where
f
is the solution of the Boltzmann equation. Note that, relative to
Eq.

9
, one has to divide by the initial density, which is
\nu_{i_{0}}\epsilon\,T^{3}
for a delta perturbation. We saw examples for
\mathcal{O}
in the main text and here we are focusing on its evaluation in the single collision limit. Following Eq. (
12
), its rate takes the form

\partial_{t}\hat{\mathcal{O}}=\sum_{i}\nu_{i}\int_{\bm{p}}\mathcal{O}(\bm{p},i)\delta C_{i}[\{n,\delta f^{(0)}\}]\,.

(68)

The contribution from the elastic collisions is given by

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\hat{\mathcal{O}}&\overset{2\leftrightarrow 2}{=}\frac{1}{2}\sum_{abcd}\int_{p}\int d\Omega^{2\leftrightarrow 2}\mathcal{O}(\bm{p},a)|\mathcal{M}^{ab}_{cd}(\bm{p},\bm{k};\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{k}^{\prime})|^{2}\delta\mathcal{F}(\bm{p},\bm{k};\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{k}^{\prime})\,.\end{split}

(69)

By integrating the initial perturbation
\delta f_{i}^{(0)}(\bm{p})=\delta_{ii_{0}}(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{3}(\bm{p}-\bm{p}_{0})
, relabeling sums and integrals, and using matrix element symmetries

|\mathcal{M}^{ab}_{cd}(\bm{p},\bm{k};\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{k}^{\prime})|^{2}=|\mathcal{M}^{ba}_{cd}(\bm{k},\bm{p};\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{k}^{\prime})|^{2}=|\mathcal{M}_{ab}^{cd}(\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{k}^{\prime};\bm{p},\bm{k})|^{2}=|\mathcal{M}^{ab}_{dc}(\bm{p},\bm{k};\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{p}^{\prime})|^{2}\,,

(70)

we arrive to the following simple formula

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\hat{\mathcal{O}}&\overset{2\leftrightarrow 2}{=}\frac{1}{4p_{0}}\sum_{bcd}\int d\Omega^{2\leftrightarrow 2}|\mathcal{M}^{i_{0}b}_{cd}(\bm{p}_{0},\bm{k};\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{k}^{\prime})|^{2}\\
&\quad\times\left\{n_{b}(k)(1\pm n_{c}(p^{\prime}))(1\pm n_{d}(k^{\prime}))\mp_{i_{0}}n_{c}(p^{\prime})n_{d}(k^{\prime})(1\pm n_{b}(k))\right\}\\
&\quad\times\left[\mathcal{O}(\bm{p}^{\prime},c)+\mathcal{O}(\bm{k}^{\prime},d)-\mathcal{O}(\bm{p}_{0},i_{0})-\mathcal{O}(\bm{k},b)\right]\,.\end{split}

(71)

The contribution from the radiation process is

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\hat{\mathcal{O}}&\overset{1\leftrightarrow 2}{=}\sum_{a}\int_{\bm{p}}\mathcal{O}(\bm{p},a)\frac{(2\pi)^{3}}{2p^{2}}\sum_{bc}\int_{0}^{\infty}dp^{\prime}dk^{\prime}\delta(p-p^{\prime}-k^{\prime})\gamma^{a}_{bc}(\bm{p};p^{\prime}\hat{\bm{p}},k^{\prime}\hat{\bm{p}})\delta\mathcal{F}(\bm{p};p^{\prime}\hat{\bm{p}},k^{\prime}\hat{\bm{p}})\\
&-\sum_{a}\int_{\bm{p}}\mathcal{O}(\bm{p},a)\frac{(2\pi)^{3}}{p^{2}}\sum_{bc}\int_{0}^{\infty}dp^{\prime}dk^{\prime}\delta(p+k^{\prime}-p^{\prime})\gamma^{c}_{ab}(p^{\prime}\hat{\bm{p}};\bm{p},k^{\prime}\hat{\bm{p}})\delta\mathcal{F}(p^{\prime}\hat{\bm{p}};\bm{p},k^{\prime}\hat{\bm{p}})\,.\end{split}

(72)

Similar to the elastic collisions, by integrating the perturbation
\delta f_{i}(t_{0},\bm{p})=(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{ii_{0}}\frac{1}{p^{2}}\delta(p-p_{0})\delta^{2}(\hat{\bm{p}}-\hat{\bm{p}}_{0})
, relabeling variables, and using symmetries
\gamma^{a}_{bc}(\bm{p};\bm{p}^{\prime},\bm{k}^{\prime})=\gamma^{a}_{cb}(\bm{p};\bm{k}^{\prime},\bm{p}^{\prime})
,
we arrive at the formula

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\hat{\mathcal{O}}&\overset{1\leftrightarrow 2}{=}\frac{(2\pi)^{3}}{p_{0}^{2}}\sum_{ac}\int dpdk^{\prime}\left[\mathcal{O}(p\hat{\bm{p}}_{0},a)-\mathcal{O}(\bm{p}_{0},i_{0})-\mathcal{O}(k^{\prime}\hat{\bm{p}}_{0},c)\right]\delta(p-p_{0}-k^{\prime})\gamma^{a}_{i_{0}c}(p\hat{\bm{p}}_{0};p_{0}\hat{\bm{p}}_{0},k^{\prime}\hat{\bm{p}}_{0})\\
&\quad\times\left[n_{c}(k^{\prime})(1\pm n_{a}(p))\mp_{i_{0}}n_{a}(p)(1\pm n_{c}(k^{\prime}))\right]\\
&+\frac{(2\pi)^{3}}{2p_{0}^{2}}\sum_{ab}\int dpdk\left[\mathcal{O}(p\hat{\bm{p}}_{0},a)+\mathcal{O}(k\hat{\bm{p}}_{0},b)-\mathcal{O}(\bm{p}_{0},i_{0})\right]\delta(p+k-p_{0})\gamma^{i_{0}}_{ab}(p_{0}\hat{\bm{p}}_{0};p\hat{\bm{p}}_{0},k\hat{\bm{p}}_{0})\\
&\quad\times\left[(1\pm n_{a}(p))(1\pm n_{b}(k))\mp_{i_{0}}n_{a}(p)n_{b}(k)\right]\,.\end{split}

(73)

Enforcing
p>k
in the second term and integrating over
p
, we obtain

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\hat{\mathcal{O}}&\overset{1\leftrightarrow 2}{=}\frac{(2\pi)^{3}}{p_{0}^{2}}\sum_{ac}\int_{0}^{\infty}dk^{\prime}\left[\mathcal{O}((p_{0}+k^{\prime})\hat{\bm{p}}_{0},a)-\mathcal{O}(\bm{p}_{0},i_{0})-\mathcal{O}(k^{\prime}\hat{\bm{p}}_{0},c)\right]\gamma^{a}_{i_{0}c}((p_{0}+k^{\prime})\hat{\bm{p}}_{0};p_{0}\hat{\bm{p}}_{0},k^{\prime}\hat{\bm{p}}_{0})\\
&\quad\times\left[n_{c}(k^{\prime})(1\pm n_{a}(p_{0}+k^{\prime}))\mp_{i_{0}}n_{a}(p_{0}+k^{\prime})(1\pm n_{c}(k^{\prime}))\right]\\
&+\frac{(2\pi)^{3}}{p_{0}^{2}}\sum_{ab}\int_{0}^{p_{0}/2}dk\left[\mathcal{O}((p_{0}-k)\hat{\bm{p}}_{0},a)+\mathcal{O}(k\hat{\bm{p}}_{0},b)-\mathcal{O}(\bm{p}_{0},i_{0})\right]\gamma^{i_{0}}_{ab}(p_{0}\hat{\bm{p}}_{0};(p_{0}-k)\hat{\bm{p}}_{0},k\hat{\bm{p}}_{0})\\
&\quad\times\left[(1\pm n_{a}(p_{0}-k))(1\pm n_{b}(k))\mp_{i_{0}}n_{a}(p_{0}-k)n_{b}(k)\right]\,.\end{split}

(74)

The final answer is the sum of the contributions
2\leftrightarrow 2
and
1\leftrightarrow 2
. These formula agrees with previous studies
Arnold et al. 2000
;
Arnold et al. 2003b
.

Appendix D
Numerical implementation of the single-collision limit

Here, we discuss how the integral (
71
) is performed. The integral measure can be rewritten similarly as in Ref.
Boguslavski et al. 2024b
,

\begin{split}\int\mathrm{d}{\Omega}\,^{2\leftrightarrow 2}&=\int_{p_{2}p_{3}p_{4}}(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{4}(P+P_{2}-P_{3}-P_{4})\\
&=\frac{1}{4p(2\pi)^{5}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}{\phi_{pq}}\,\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}{\phi_{kq}}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}{k}\,\int_{-\frac{p-k}{2}}^{k}\mathrm{d}{\omega}\,\int_{|\omega|}^{\min(2p+\omega,2k-\omega)}\mathrm{d}{q}\,\,.\end{split}

(75)

We have used the symmetry
p^{\prime}\leftrightarrow k^{\prime}
in the integrand (also present in (
71
)) to always enforce
p^{\prime}>k^{\prime}
. Otherwise, the lower boundary of the
\omega
-integral would be
-p
, and the prefactor
1/(8p(2\pi)^{5}))
. The reason for this choice is that then only the
t
channel diagrams need to be screened
Boguslavski and Lindenbauer 2024
. For a finite momentum grid
p_{\mathrm{min}}<p<p_{\mathrm{max}}
, on which the distribution function is stored, we require
k
to be on the grid, and
k^{\prime}>p_{\mathrm{min}}
,
which results in

\displaystyle\int\mathrm{d}{\Omega}\,^{2\leftrightarrow 2}=\frac{1}{4p(2\pi)^{5}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}{\phi_{pq}}\,\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}{\phi_{kq}}\,\int_{\max(p_{\mathrm{min}},2p_{\mathrm{min}}-p)}^{p_{\mathrm{max}}}\mathrm{d}{k}\,\int_{-\frac{p-k}{2}}^{k-p_{\mathrm{min}}}\mathrm{d}{\omega}\,\int_{|\omega|}^{\min(2p+\omega,2k-\omega)}\mathrm{d}{q}\,\,.

(76)

This integration measure is used in Eq. (
71
) together with the matrix elements tabulated in Ref.
Boguslavski et al. 2024b
, with screening prescriptions (
53
) and (
60
) to obtain the expectation values in the single collision limit. Similar as in Ref.
Boguslavski et al. 2024b
, the five-dimensional integral is evaluated using Monte Carlo integration with importance sampling.
The kinematic variables need to be expressed in terms of the integration variables. The Mandelstam variables are given by

\displaystyle t=\omega^{2}-q^{2}\,,

\displaystyle u=\frac{t}{2q^{2}}\left((p+p^{\prime})(k+k^{\prime})-q^{2}-\sqrt{(4pp^{\prime}+t)(4k^{\prime}k+t)}\cos\phi_{kq}\right)\,,

\displaystyle s=-t-u\,,

(77)

and the relevant angles are

\displaystyle\cos\theta_{pq}=\frac{\omega}{q}+\frac{t}{2pq}\,,

\displaystyle\cos\theta_{kq}=\frac{\omega}{q}-\frac{t}{2kq}\,,

\displaystyle\cos\theta_{k^{\prime}q}=\frac{\omega}{q}+\frac{t}{2k^{\prime}q}\,,

(78)

where
k^{\prime}=k-\omega
, and
p^{\prime}=p+\omega
. We also need to express
\bm{p}=(0,0,p)
,
\bm{k}
,
\bm{k}^{\prime}
, and
\bm{p}^{\prime}
in the frame defined by the direction of propagation of the jet,

\displaystyle\bm{k}

\displaystyle=k\begin{pmatrix}\cos\phi_{pq}(\cos\phi_{kq}\cos\theta_{pq}\sin\theta_{kq}+\cos\theta_{kq}\sin\theta_{pq})-\sin\phi_{pq}\sin\phi_{kq}\sin\theta_{kq}\\
\cos\phi_{pq}\sin\phi_{kq}\sin\theta_{kq}+\sin\phi_{pq}\left(\cos\phi_{kq}\cos\theta_{pq}\sin\theta_{kq}+\cos\theta_{kq}\sin\theta_{pq}\right)\\
\cos\theta_{kq}\cos\theta_{pq}-\cos\phi_{kq}\sin\theta_{kq}\sin\theta_{pq}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\bm{q}=q\begin{pmatrix}\sin\theta_{pq}\cos\phi_{pq}\\
\sin\theta_{pq}\sin\phi_{pq}\\
\cos\theta_{pq}\end{pmatrix},

(79)

and
\bm{k}^{\prime}=\bm{k}-\bm{q}
, and
\bm{p}^{\prime}=\bm{p}+\bm{q}
. In our implementation of Eq. (
71
), we included both the gain and loss term and verified that for all observables considered in this paper, the gain term is negligible.

To compare with the analytic expression for the conversion rate (
66

Ghiglieri et al. 2016

For the inelastic collision term (

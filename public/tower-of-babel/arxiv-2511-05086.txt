On Universal derivations for multiarrangements
Takuro Abe, Shota Maehara, Gerhard Röhrle, Sven Wiesner

Abstract
. The study of universal derivations for arbitrary multiarrangements and multiplicity functions was initiated by Abe, Röhrle, Stump, and Yoshinaga in [ 6 ] which focused on arrangements arising from (well-generated) reflection groups. In this paper we provide a criterion for determining whether a derivation is universal along with a characterization of universal derivations for arbitrary 2-multiarrangements. As an application we give descriptions of universal derivations for several multiarrangements, including the so-called deleted A 3 A_{3} arrangement. This is the first known example of a non-reflection arrangement that admits a universal derivation distinct from the Euler derivation.

1.
Introduction

The freeness of hyperplane arrangements has been a central topic in the theory of arrangements for several decades. Despite extensive research, determining whether a given arrangement is free remains a challenging problem. A major open question in this area is Terao’s conjecture which asserts that the freeness of an arrangement depends only on its combinatorial structure, namely its intersection lattice. A function
m:{\mathscr{A}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
on an arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
is called a
multiplicity
and a pair
({\mathscr{A}},m)
a
multiarrangement
. An important advancement in the study of freeness was made by Yoshinaga, who established a relationship between the freeness of a central arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
and that of a certain multiarrangement, arising as a restriction of
{\mathscr{A}}
to one of its hyperplanes, the so called Ziegler restriction. Consequently, the theory of multiarrangements has become a significant area of investigation in its own right.

In the special case when
{\mathscr{A}}={\mathscr{A}}(W)
is the reflection arrangement of a finite Coxeter group
W
, the concept of universal vector fields was introduced by Yoshinaga in
[
19
]
to construct bases for multi-Coxeter arrangements using affine connections. They were further explored in
[
7
]
and
[
9
]
as a tool to study the structure of the module of logarithmic derivations
D({\mathscr{A}},m)
for a given multiplicity function
m:{\mathscr{A}}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. A definition was later provided by Wakamiko in
[
18
, Def. 2.2]
. Note that universal vector fields are defined only in the setting of Coxeter arrangements
{\mathscr{A}}(W)
, where the
W
-action and
W
-invariance play a fundamental role in their construction. In
[
6
]
, Abe, Stump, Röhrle, and Yoshinaga extended the definition of universal vector fields to universal derivations for arbitrary arrangements.

Let
V
be an
\ell
-dimensional vector space over a field
{\mathbb{K}}
of characteristic zero,
S={\mathbb{K}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}]
the coordinate ring of
V
,
and
{\operatorname{Der}}_{S}=\oplus_{i=1}^{\ell}S\cdot\partial_{x_{i}}
the module of

S
-regular derivations. For a given multiplicity
m
on
{\mathscr{A}}
, the
module of
({\mathscr{A}},m)
-derivations

D({\mathscr{A}},m)
is the central object of this study, see (
2.1
). We define the trivial multiplicity

\mathbbm{1}
on
{\mathscr{A}}
by

\mathbbm{1}(H)=1
for each
H
in
{\mathscr{A}}
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
({\mathscr{A}},m)
be a multiarrangement with fixed multiplicity
m
and
\theta\in D({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
a homogeneous derivation. Then
\theta

is said to be
m
-universal (for
{\mathscr{A}}
or
({\mathscr{A}},m)
)
if the map

\Phi_{\theta}:{\operatorname{Der}}_{S}\to D({\mathscr{A}},m),\varphi\mapsto\nabla_{\varphi}\theta

is an isomorphism of
S
-modules, where for
\theta=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}f_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}\in{\operatorname{Der}}_{S}
,
\nabla_{\varphi}\theta
is defined by

\nabla_{\varphi}\theta\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\varphi(f_{i})\partial_{x_{i}}.

Note that if both
\theta
and
\varphi
are homogeneous, then

\deg(\nabla_{\varphi}\theta)=\deg\varphi+\deg\theta-1
.
In particular, if
\theta
is
m
-universal for
{\mathscr{A}}
, then
\Phi_{\theta}
maps the
S
-basis
\{\partial_{x_{i}}\mid 1\leq i\leq\ell\}
of
{\operatorname{Der}}_{S}
to an
S
-basis of
D({\mathscr{A}},m)
. Thus
D({\mathscr{A}},m)
is free with exponents
\exp({\mathscr{A}},m)=(d,d,\dots,d)
, where
\deg\theta=d+1
.

This notion is motivated by the Euler derivation.
Suppose
{\mathscr{A}}
is free and irreducible. Then the Euler derivation
\theta_{E}=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}x_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}
belongs to
D({\mathscr{A}})=D({\mathscr{A}},0+\mathbbm{1})
and
\nabla_{\varphi}\theta_{E}=\varphi
for all
\varphi\in{\operatorname{Der}}_{S}
, so
\Phi_{\theta_{E}}
is the identity on
{\operatorname{Der}}_{S}=D({\mathscr{A}},0)
. So the Euler derivation is
0
-universal.

Universal derivations can be utilized to determine the structure of not only the
S
-modules
D({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
and
D({\mathscr{A}},m)
but also of intermediate
S
-modules. Hence an
m
-universal derivation can play an important role in the investigation of derivation modules and free arrangements.

Our first result is a characterization of universal derivations which can be considered as a generalization of
[
5
, Thm. 3.4]
. The notion of criticality was originally defined by Ziegler in
[
23
]
(see Definition
2.2
). It is used to derive the following criterion for universal derivations.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be an irreducible
\ell
-arrangement and
m
a multiplicity on
{\mathscr{A}}
.
Assume that
\theta\in D({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
is homogeneous with
\deg\theta=d+1
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\theta
is
m
-universal;

(2)

({\mathscr{A}},m)
is free with
\exp({\mathscr{A}},m)=(d,\ldots,d)

and
D({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
is
(d+1)
-critical.

Our second main result classifies universal derivations for multiarrangements of rank two. For the notion of a balanced multiplicity, see Definition
2.5
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
({\mathscr{A}},m)
be an irreducible
2
-multiarrangement. Assume that
|{\mathscr{A}}|>3
or

|{\mathscr{A}}|=3
and
m
is balanced. Then a homogeneous derivation
\theta\in D({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
is
m
-universal if and only if
m+\mathbbm{1}
is balanced, and
\exp({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})=(\deg\theta,\deg\theta+|{\mathscr{A}}|-2)
.

The following example illustrates why we require
\theta\in D({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
instead of merely allowing that
\theta
belongs to
{\operatorname{Der}}_{S}
in Theorem
1.3
.
Here and subsequently, for a fixed
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
we define the indicator multiplicity

\delta_{H}:{\mathscr{A}}\to\{0,1\}
by

\delta_{H}(H^{\prime})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}0&\mbox{if}\ H^{\prime}\neq H,\\
1&\mbox{if}\ H^{\prime}=H.\end{array}\right.

Example 1.4
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be the Coxeter arrangement of type
B_{2}
with defining polynomial

Q({\mathscr{A}})=xy(x-y)(x+y).

Consider the 2-multiarrangment
({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
given by

Q({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})=x^{3}y^{5}(x-y)^{2}(x+y)^{2}.

Then
\exp({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})=(5,7)
and
\exp({\mathscr{A}},m)=(4,4)
.
Moreover,
\exp({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H})=(6,7)
for every
H
in
{\mathscr{A}}
.
Let
\theta\in D({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
be homogeneous with
\deg\theta=5
. Note that
\theta
is unique up to multiplication by a non-zero scalar and is
m
-universal. If however
\theta
is only required to belong to
{\operatorname{Der}}_{S}
instead of
D({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
, then every derivation of degree
5
in
{\operatorname{Der}}_{S}
satisfies the conditions in Theorem
1.3
, even if
\Phi_{\theta}
is not an isomorphism.

We utilize Theorem
1.2
to give examples of universal derivations. This includes a classification of the latter on the deleted
A_{3}
arrangement which marks the first existence result of that kind for arrangements not stemming from a (well-generated) reflection group.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be the deleted
A_{3}
arrangement with defining polynomial

Q({\mathscr{A}})=(y-z)y(x-y)x(x-z).

Consider the multiarrangment
({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
given by

Q({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})=(y-z)^{a}y^{b}(x-y)^{c}x^{d}(x-z)^{e}.

There exists an
m
-universal
\theta\in D({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
if and only if
c=a+e-1=b+d-1
.

We also derive a classification of universal derivations for the Braid arrangement of rank three equipped with a supersolvable multiplicity. For the latter see Definition
4.2
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
be a Coxeter multiarrangement of type
A_{3}
. If
({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
is supersolvable, then there exists an
m
-universal
\theta\in D({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
if and only if all of the inequalities in Theorem
4.1
are identities.

It is apparent from Theorem
1.2
that if
m\not\equiv 0
there do not exist
m
-universal derivations for totally non-free arrangements. Our next result shows that a free multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}},m)
with
m\not\equiv 0
need not admit an
m
-universal derivation in general.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be the
X_{3}
-arrangement defined by

Q({\mathscr{A}})=xyz(x+y)(y+z)(x+z).

Then
{\mathscr{A}}
does not admit an
m
-universal derivation for any multiplicity
m\not\equiv 0
on
{\mathscr{A}}
.

An arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
is called
totally free
if
({\mathscr{A}},m)
is free for every multiplicity
m:{\mathscr{A}}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. While a free arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
is
0
-universal with universal derivation
\theta_{E}\in D({\mathscr{A}})
, our final result shows that freeness does not imply the existence of a universal derivation
\theta\neq\theta_{E}
.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be a totally free arrangement. Then there need not exist an
m
-universal derivation
\theta\neq\theta_{E}
for any multiplicity
m\not\equiv 0
.

2.
Preliminaries

A
hyperplane arrangement

{\mathscr{A}}
is
a finite collection of hyperplanes in
V
. An arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
is called
central
if every
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
is linear, and
essential
if
\bigcap_{H\in{\mathscr{A}}}H
is the origin.
For each
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
we fix a linear form
\alpha_{H}\in V^{*}
such that
\ker\alpha_{H}=H
and define
Q({\mathscr{A}})\coloneqq\prod_{H\in{\mathscr{A}}}\alpha_{H}
. In this article
we assume that all arrangements are essential unless otherwise specified.

Let
m:{\mathscr{A}}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
be a multiplicity on
{\mathscr{A}}
. Define the order of
m
as
|m|\coloneqq\sum_{H\in{\mathscr{A}}}m(H)
and define
Q({\mathscr{A}},m)\coloneqq\prod_{H\in{\mathscr{A}}}\alpha_{H}^{m(H)}
.

If
m=\mathbbm{1}
, we call
({\mathscr{A}},m)
a
simple arrangement
.
For a central multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}},m)
, we can define the
module of logarithmic
({\mathscr{A}},m)
-derivations

D({\mathscr{A}},m)
by

(2.1)

D({\mathscr{A}},m)\coloneqq\{\theta\in{\operatorname{Der}}_{S}\mid\theta(\alpha_{H})\in S\alpha_{H}^{m(H)}\ (\forall H\in{\mathscr{A}})\}.

Then
D({\mathscr{A}},m)
is an
S
-graded reflexive module of rank
\ell
. Define
D({\mathscr{A}},m)_{k}=\{\theta\in D({\mathscr{A}},m)\mid\deg\theta=k\}
and
D({\mathscr{A}},m)_{<k}=\{\theta\in D({\mathscr{A}},m)\mid\deg\theta<k\}
.
When
D({\mathscr{A}},m)
is a free module of rank
\ell
, we say that

({\mathscr{A}},m)
is
free
and for a homogeneous basis
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{\ell}
, we define the
exponents of
({\mathscr{A}},m)
by

\exp({\mathscr{A}},m)\coloneqq(\deg\theta_{1},\ldots,\deg\theta_{\ell}),

where
\deg\theta\coloneqq\deg\theta(\alpha)
for some
\alpha\in V^{*}
such that

\theta(\alpha)\neq 0
.
When
m=\mathbbm{1}
,
the module
D({\mathscr{A}},\mathbbm{1})
is also denoted by
D({\mathscr{A}})
.

The notion of criticality was originally defined by Ziegler in
[
23
]
for the
S
-module of logarithmic differential forms
\Omega^{1}({\mathscr{A}})
, which is isomorphic to the dual
D({\mathscr{A}})^{*}
of the logarithmic derivation module.

Definition 2.2
.

We say that
({\mathscr{A}},m)
is
k
-critical
if
D({\mathscr{A}},m)_{k}\neq(0),\ D({\mathscr{A}},m)_{<k}=(0)
and for any
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
,
D({\mathscr{A}},m+\delta_{H})_{k}=(0).

The following results are fundamental in this article, see
[
14
]
and
[
22
]
.

Theorem 2.3

(Saito’s criterion)
.

Let
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{\ell}
be homogeneous derivations in
D({\mathscr{A}},m)
.
Then
{\mathscr{A}}
is free with basis
\{\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{\ell}\}
if and only if
\{\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{\ell}\}
is
S
-independent and
\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\deg\theta_{i}=|m|
.

Equivalently,
{\mathscr{A}}
is free with basis
\{\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{\ell}\}
if and only if

\det(\theta_{i}(x_{j}))=c\cdot Q({\mathscr{A}},m)
, where
c\in{\mathbb{K}}\setminus\{0\}
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
({\mathscr{A}},\mu),
(where
{\mathscr{A}}\neq\emptyset)
be a multiarrangement in
\mathbb{K}^{\ell}
. Fix
H_{0}
in
{\mathscr{A}}
.
We define the
deletion

({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})
and
Euler restriction

({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\mu^{*})

of
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
with respect to
H_{0}
as follows.
If
\mu(H_{0})=1
, then set
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}={\mathscr{A}}\setminus\{H_{0}\}

and define
\mu^{\prime}(H)=\mu(H)
for all
H\in{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}
.
If
\mu(H_{0})>1
, then set
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}={\mathscr{A}}

and define
\mu^{\prime}(H_{0})=\mu(H_{0})-1
and

\mu^{\prime}(H)=\mu(H)
for all
H\neq H_{0}
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}=\{H\cap H_{0}\mid H\in{\mathscr{A}}\setminus\{H_{0}\}\ \}
.
The
Euler multiplicity

\mu^{*}
of
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
is defined as follows.
Let
Y\in{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
. Since the localization
{\mathscr{A}}_{Y}
is of rank
2
, the
multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}}_{Y},\mu_{Y})
is free,

[
22
, Cor. 7]
.
According to

[
8
, Prop. 2.1]
,
the module of derivations

D({\mathscr{A}}_{Y},\mu_{Y})
admits a particular homogeneous basis

\{\theta_{Y},\psi_{Y},\partial_{x_{3}},\ldots,\partial_{x_{\ell}}\}
,
such that
\theta_{Y}\notin\alpha_{0}{\operatorname{Der}}(S)

and
\psi_{Y}\in\alpha_{0}{\operatorname{Der}}(S)
,
where
H_{0}=\ker\alpha_{0}
.
Then on
Y
the Euler multiplicity
\mu^{*}
is defined
to be
\mu^{*}(Y)=\deg\theta_{Y}
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
({\mathscr{A}},m)
be a multiarrangement. We call the multiplicity
m

balanced
if for all
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
the inequality

m(H)\leq\sum_{H^{\prime}\in{\mathscr{A}}\backslash\{H\}}m(H^{\prime})

holds.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in{\operatorname{Der}}_{S}
and
\alpha\in V^{*}
, then

(\nabla_{\theta}\theta^{\prime})(\alpha)=\theta(\theta^{\prime}(\alpha)).

Proof.

Let
\theta=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}f_{i}\partial_{x_{i}},\theta^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}g_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}
, and
\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}c_{i}x_{i}
. Then we have

\left(\nabla_{\theta}\theta^{\prime}\right)(\alpha)=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\theta(g_{i})\partial_{x_{i}}(\alpha)=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}c_{i}\theta(g_{i})=\theta\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}c_{i}g_{i}\right)=\theta(\theta^{\prime}(\alpha)),

as claimed.
∎

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\theta\in{\operatorname{Der}}_{S}
. Then
\theta
is
m
-universal for
{\mathscr{A}}
if and only if
\{\nabla_{\partial_{x_{i}}}\theta\}_{i=1}^{\ell}
is independent over
S
,

\ell\cdot(\deg\theta-1)=|m|
, and
\nabla_{\partial_{x_{i}}}\theta\in D({\mathscr{A}},m)
for all
i
. Moreover,
D({\mathscr{A}},\mu)\simeq D({\mathscr{A}},m+\mu)
for any multiplicy
\mu:{\mathscr{A}}\to\{0,1\}
.

Proof.

For the equivalence it is sufficient to show that
\theta
belongs to
D({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
. Let
\theta=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}g_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}\in{\operatorname{Der}}_{S}
and
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
. We may assume, without loss of generality, that
\partial_{x_{1}}(\alpha_{H})=c\neq 0
and that
\theta(\alpha_{H})=h\alpha_{H}^{n}
with
\alpha_{H}\nmid h\in S,n\geq 0
. Since
\nabla_{\partial_{x_{1}}}\theta\in D({\mathscr{A}},m)
, it follows from Lemma
2.6
that

S\alpha_{H}^{m(H)}\ni(\nabla_{\partial_{x_{1}}}\theta)(\alpha_{H})=\partial_{x_{1}}(\theta(\alpha_{H}))=\partial_{x_{1}}(h\alpha_{H}^{n})=(\alpha_{H}\partial_{x_{1}}h+nhc)\alpha_{H}^{n-1}.

So
n\geq m(H)+1
, showing that
\theta\in D({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
. Assume that
n>m(H)+1
. Then it holds for all
1\leq i\leq\ell
that

\nabla_{\partial_{x_{i}}}\theta\in D({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H}),

and these derivations are linearly independent. But this contradicts Saito’s criterion and therefore we have
n=m(H)+1
. The only thing left to prove is the isomorphism claim. So fix a multiplicity
\mu:{\mathscr{A}}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
. We show that the map

\Phi:D({\mathscr{A}},\mu)\rightarrow D({\mathscr{A}},m+\mu)

defined by
\Phi(\varphi)\coloneqq\nabla_{\varphi}\theta
is an isomorphism of
S
-modules. Let
\varphi=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}f_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}\in D({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
and
\nabla_{\varphi}\theta=0
. Then
\nabla_{\partial_{x_{i}}}\theta=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\partial_{x_{i}}(g_{j})\partial_{x_{j}}
gives

\nabla_{\varphi}\theta=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\varphi(g_{j})\partial_{x_{j}}=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}f_{i}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\partial_{x_{i}}(g_{j})\partial_{x_{j}}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}f_{i}\nabla_{\partial_{x_{i}}}\theta=0.

Since the elements of
\{\nabla_{\partial_{x_{i}}}\theta\}_{i=1}^{\ell}
are independent over
S
, it follows that
f_{i}=0
for all
i
. Thus
\Phi
is injective.

To show that
\Phi
is surjective, let
\eta\in D({\mathscr{A}},m+\mu)
be arbitrary. We construct a
\varphi\in D({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
such that
\nabla_{\varphi}\theta=\eta
. Since
\theta
is
m
-universal, the map
\Phi_{\theta}:D({\mathscr{A}},0)={\operatorname{Der}}_{S}\rightarrow D({\mathscr{A}},m)
is an isomorphism and
\nabla_{\partial_{x_{1}}}\theta,\ldots,\nabla_{\partial_{x_{\ell}}}\theta
form an
S
-basis for
D({\mathscr{A}},m)
. Consequently, since
\eta\in D({\mathscr{A}},m+\mu)\subset D({\mathscr{A}},m)
, there exist unique
f_{i}\in S
such that
\eta=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}f_{i}\nabla_{\partial_{x_{i}}}\theta
. Therefore, defining
\varphi\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}f_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}\in{\operatorname{Der}}_{S}
, we derive

\eta=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}f_{i}\nabla_{\partial_{x_{i}}}\theta=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}f_{i}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\partial_{x_{i}}(g_{j})\partial_{x_{j}}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\varphi(g_{j})\partial_{x_{j}}=\nabla_{\varphi}\theta.

So it suffices to show that
\varphi\in D({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
, since
\nabla_{\varphi}\theta=\eta\in D({\mathscr{A}},m+\mu)
. Let
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
. There is nothing to show if
\mu(H)=0
, so assume that
\mu(H)=1
. We have to show that
\varphi(\alpha_{H})\in S\alpha_{H}
. Use
\eta\in D({\mathscr{A}},m+\delta_{H})
to obtain

\displaystyle S\alpha_{H}^{m(H)+1}\ni\eta(\alpha_{H})

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\varphi}\theta(\alpha_{H})=\varphi(\theta(\alpha_{H}))=\varphi(h\alpha_{H}^{m(H)+1})

\displaystyle=(\varphi(h)\alpha_{H}+(m(H)+1)\varphi(\alpha_{H})h)\alpha_{H}^{m(H)}.

Since
\alpha_{H}\nmid h
, we have
\varphi(\alpha_{H})\in S\alpha_{H}
. So
\varphi\in D({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
, which completes the proof.∎

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be an irreducible arrangement and
m
a multiplicity on
{\mathscr{A}}
, and
let
\theta\in D({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
be
m
-universal. Then
\theta
is (up to scalar multiplication) the unique derivation of degree
\deg\theta
in
D({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
and
D({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})_{<\deg\theta}=(0)
.

Proof.

Since
{\mathscr{A}}
is irreducible, the Euler derivation
\theta_{E}
is (up to scalar multiplication) the unique derivation of degree
1
in
D({\mathscr{A}})
. The map
\Phi:D({\mathscr{A}})\to D({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
given by
\Phi(\varphi)=\nabla_{\varphi}\theta

is an isomorphism, by the proof of Proposition
2.7
. Since
\deg(\nabla_{\varphi}\theta)=\deg\varphi+\deg\theta-1
, for
\varphi
homogeneous, we require
\Phi(\theta_{E})=\theta
which finishes the proof.
∎

Now consider the

\displaystyle\Lambda

\displaystyle\{m\in\Lambda\mid\Delta(m)\neq 0\}.

.

First assume (1). Since

\displaystyle 0=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}c_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}(f_{j}x_{j}^{n_{j}})

Since

4.1.
Supersolvable multiarrangements, I

The following result by Abe, Terao, and Wakefield plays a key role in the sequel.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
8

)
.

Let
({\mathscr{A}},m)
be a multiarrangement such that
{\mathscr{A}}
has a supersolvable filtration

The following observation gives a necessary condition for the presence of a universal derivation in the supersolvable case.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})

Let
({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
be supersolvable,
\operatorname{rank}({\mathscr{A}})=r\geq 3

\exp({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})=(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3},\dots,d_{r},0,0,\dots,0),

and choose a supersolvable filtration

as in Theorem

. Let
\exp({\mathscr{A}}_{2},m_{2}+\mathbbm{1})=(d_{1},d_{2})

m
-universal derivation

. Note that for all

by

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
be supersolvable in

(1)

The multiplicity

(2)

|m_{2}|
is even and
\exp({\mathscr{A}}_{2},m_{2})=\left(\frac{|m_{2}|}{2},\frac{|m_{2}|}{2}\right)

for all

1. Suppose that
({\mathscr{A}}_{2},m_{2}+\mathbbm{1})
is not balanced and let

\exp({\mathscr{A}}_{2},m_{2}+\mathbbm{1})=\left(\sum_{H^{\prime}\in{\mathscr{A}}_{2}\backslash\{H\}}(m_{2}+\mathbbm{1})(H^{\prime}),(m_{2}+\mathbbm{1})(H)\right)

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be the deleted
A_{3}
arrangement given by
Q({\mathscr{A}})=xy(x-y)(x-z)(y-z)
.
Consider the multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
given by

Q({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})=(y-z)^{a}y^{b}(x-y)^{c}x^{d}(x-z)^{e}.

Then
({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
is free if and only if
c\geq a+e-1
or
c\geq b+d-1
.

Combining Theorems
4.1
and
4.5
, we can now prove Theorem
1.5
.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

We have the following supersolvable filtrations for
{\mathscr{A}}
:

\{x=0\}\subset\{xy(x-y)=0\}\subset\{xy(x-y)(x-z)(y-z)=0\}

and

\{(x-y)=0\}\subset\{(x-y)(x-z)(y-z)=0\}\subset\{xy(x-y)(x-z)(y-z)=0\},

which we combine with Theorem
4.1
to calculate the exponents of
({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
. As in Theorem
4.1
, we denote a chosen supersolvable chain for
{\mathscr{A}}
by
{\mathscr{A}}_{1}\subset{\mathscr{A}}_{2}\subset{\mathscr{A}}_{3}
. Theorem
4.5
shows that
({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
is free if and only if
({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
is supersolvable with respect to a choice of one of the two given filtrations above. In particular, the simple arrangement
({\mathscr{A}},\mathbbm{1})
is free with exponents
(1,2,2)
.

By definition of an
m
-universal derivation
\theta\in D({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
the module
D({\mathscr{A}},m)
has to be free and the exponents of
D({\mathscr{A}},m)
have to all be equal. Since
D({\mathscr{A}},m)
is free if and only if it is supersolvable, we can use Theorem
4.1
to calculate
\exp({\mathscr{A}},m)
and have to demand that

c-1=a+e-2=b+d-2,

else
({\mathscr{A}},m)
does not have all exponents equal. But this is equivalent to requiring that
c=a+e-1=b+d-1
. It is left to show that this restriction on
m+\mathbbm{1}
is sufficient.

So let
({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
be such that
c+1=a+e=b+d
. Using the second part of Theorem
4.1
we deduce that

\exp({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})=(c,c+1,c+1).

Note that
({\mathscr{A}},m)
and
({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H})
are still supersolvable multiarrangements for an arbitrary
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
. This follows immediately for
({\mathscr{A}},m)
, since each of the inequalities of Theorem
4.1
is still satisfied if we reduce the multiplicity of every hyperplane in
({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
by
1
. For
({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H})
and
H=\ker x
, the inequalities are still satisfied. Finally, if
H\neq\ker x
, then choose a filtration such that
H\in{\mathscr{A}}_{2}
, which once again ensures that the inequalities are satisfied. Then apply Theorem
4.1
to derive that

\exp({\mathscr{A}},m)=(c-1,c-1,c-1)\quad\text{and}\quad\exp({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H})=(c+1,c+1,c+1)

for all
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
, so
({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
is
c
-critical. Now Theorem
1.2
implies that a basis element
\theta\in D({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
with
\deg\theta=c
is
m
-universal.∎

4.3.
Braid arrangements

In
[
10
]
DiPasquale, Francisco, Mermin, and Schweig showed that all free multiplicities on the Coxeter arrangement of type
A_{3}
are either so called ANN-multiplicities, see
[
4
, Thm. 0.3]
, or supersolvable.
We now determine all
m
-universal derivations for the braid arrangement of rank three in the supersolvable case.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let

Q({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})=(x_{1}-x_{2})^{a}(x_{1}-x_{3})^{b}(x_{1}-x_{4})^{c}(x_{2}-x_{3})^{d}(x_{2}-x_{4})^{e}(x_{3}-x_{4})^{f}

be an
A_{3}
multiarrangement.
Assume that
({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
satisfies the conditions of Theorem
4.1
for a suitable supersolvable filtration
{\mathscr{A}}_{1}\subset{\mathscr{A}}_{2}\subset{\mathscr{A}}_{3}={\mathscr{A}}
for
({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
. Then there exists an
m
-universal derivation if and only if all of the inequalities in Theorem
4.1
are identities.

Proof.

To apply Theorem
4.1
we fix the following supersolvable filtration

\{(x_{1}-x_{2})=0\}\subset\{(x_{1}-x_{2})(x_{1}-x_{3})(x_{2}-x_{3})=0\}

\subset\{(x_{1}-x_{2})(x_{1}-x_{3})(x_{2}-x_{3})(x_{1}-x_{4})(x_{2}-x_{4})(x_{3}-x_{4})=0\}.

Note, that we can obtain other supersolvable filtrations by permuting the
x_{i}
by an element of
S_{4}
. Recalling that
H_{ij}=\ker(x_{i}-x_{j})
, the conditions of Theorem
4.1
read as follows:

\displaystyle(m+\mathbbm{1})(H_{12})\geq(m+\mathbbm{1})(H_{14})+(m+\mathbbm{1})(H_{24})-1

\displaystyle\iff a\geq c+e-1\text{ and}

\displaystyle(m+\mathbbm{1})(H_{13})\geq(m+\mathbbm{1})(H_{14})+(m+\mathbbm{1})(H_{34})-1

\displaystyle\iff b\geq c+f-1\text{ and}

\displaystyle(m+\mathbbm{1})(H_{23})\geq(m+\mathbbm{1})(H_{24})+(m+\mathbbm{1})(H_{34})-1

\displaystyle\iff d\geq e+f-1.

Suppose that there exists an
m
-universal derivation. In particular there exists a
d_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that:

(1)

({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
is free with exponents
(d_{1}+1,d_{2},d_{3})
, where
d_{1}+1<d_{2},d_{3}
.

(2)

({\mathscr{A}},m)
is free with exponents
(d_{1},d_{1},d_{1})
.

Condition (2) combined with Theorem
4.1
implies that

(4.7)

\frac{a+b+d-3}{2}=c+e+f-3\iff a+b+d=2c+2e+2f-3

has to hold and in particular
a+b+d
is odd.
Thanks to the inequalities above, we have

a+b+d\geq(c+e-1)+(c+f-1)+(e+f-1)=2c+2e+2f-3.

Combined with (
4.7
), this implies that all of the inequalities required for supersolvability have to be identities.

It is left to show that (assuming that
({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
is supersolvable) the condition (
4.7
) is not only necessary but sufficient for
({\mathscr{A}},m)
to admit an
m
-universal derivation. We do this by showing that for all
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
, the multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H})
is free and
d_{1}+1\not\in\exp({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H})
. This then proves that
D({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
is
(d_{1}+1)
-critical, so Theorem
1.2
implies the existence of an
m
-universal derivation.
So for the rest of the proof we assume

a=c+e-1,b=c+f-1,\text{ and }d=e+f-1.

Since all multiplicities are positive, this shows
a+b>d,a+d>b,b+d>a
and we can use Theorem
3.4
to calculate
\exp({\mathscr{A}}_{2},m_{2})
and get
\exp({\mathscr{A}}_{2},m_{2})=\left(\frac{a+b+d-1}{2},\frac{a+b+d+1}{2}\right)
. Theorem
4.1
yields

\exp({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})=\left(\frac{a+b+d-1}{2},\frac{a+b+d+1}{2},\frac{a+b+d+3}{2}\right)

and
\exp({\mathscr{A}},m)=(\frac{a+b+d-3}{2},\frac{a+b+d-3}{2},\frac{a+b+d-3}{2})
.

It is left to show that
\exp({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H})=(\frac{a+b+d+1}{2},\frac{a+b+d+1}{2},\frac{a+b+d+3}{2})
for all
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
.

If
H\in\{\ker(x_{1}-x_{2}),\ker(x_{1}-x_{3}),\ker(x_{2}-x_{3})\}
, then
({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H})
is still supersolvable since all of the inequalities are still satisfied and Theorem
4.1
implies
\exp({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H})=(\frac{a+b+d+1}{2},\frac{a+b+d+1}{2},\frac{a+b+d+3}{2})
.

Now let
H\in\{\ker(x_{1}-x_{4}),\ker(x_{2}-x_{4}),\ker(x_{3}-x_{4})\},H^{\prime}\in{\mathscr{A}}\backslash\{H\},X=H\cap H^{\prime}
.
We make use of Theorem
3.4
to derive the following concerning the Euler restriction
(m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H})^{*}
with respect to
H
on
{\mathscr{A}}
:

(1)

If
{\mathscr{A}}_{X}=\{H,H^{\prime}\}
, then we
have
(m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H})^{*}(X)=(m+\mathbbm{1})(H^{\prime})
.

(2)

If
{\mathscr{A}}_{X}=\{(x_{1}-x_{2}),(x_{1}-x_{4}),(x_{2}-x_{4})\}
, then use the equality
a=c+e-1
to see that
\exp({\mathscr{A}}_{X},(m+\mathbbm{1})_{X})=(a,c+e)=(a,a+1)
,
\exp({\mathscr{A}}_{X},(m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H})_{X})=(a+1,a+1)
and therefore
(m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H})^{*}(X)=a+1=c+e
is independent from the choice of
H
.

(3)

Analogous, if
{\mathscr{A}}_{X}=\{(x_{1}-x_{3}),(x_{1}-x_{4}),(x_{3}-x_{4})\}
, then use the identity
b=c+f-1
to see that
(m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H})^{*}(X)=b+1=c+f
and if
{\mathscr{A}}_{X}=\{(x_{2}-x_{3}),(x_{2}-x_{4}),(x_{3}-x_{4})\}
, then use
d=e+f-1
to infer that
(m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H})^{*}(X)=d+1=e+f
.

Now let
H=\ker(x_{1}-x_{4})
. Then we have the following three localizations
{\mathscr{A}}_{X}
:

\{H,\ker(x_{1}-x_{2}),\ker(x_{2}-x_{4})\},\{H,\ker(x_{1}-x_{3}),\ker(x_{3}-x_{4})\},\{H,\ker(x_{2}-x_{3})\}.

Fixing this order of localizations we have
(m+\mathbbm{1})_{X}=(c,a,e),(c,b,f),(c,d)
. Now use the results of the discussion above to see that
(m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H})^{*}=(a+1,b+1,d)
. So since
|(m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H})^{*}|=a+b+d+2
, the sum
a+b+d
is an odd number and the multiplicity is balanced on
({\mathscr{A}}_{2},m_{2})
. Use Theorem
3.4
once more to calculate

\exp({\mathscr{A}}^{H},(m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H})^{*})=\left(\frac{a+b+d+1}{2},\frac{a+b+d+3}{2}\right),

as required.

The calculations for all remaining hyperplanes
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
work analogously. This proves the existence of an
m
-universal derivation. The arguments for the other supersolvable filtrations work the same way. That such a multiplicity
m+\mathbbm{1}
which meets the requirements of the theorem exists is obvious.
∎

Proposition
4.6
gives Theorem
1.6
. This provides examples of the following kind.

Example 4.8
.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(4x^{3}-6x^{2}y)\partial_{x}-2y^{3}\partial_{y}+(-6yz^{2}+4z^{3})\partial_{z},

\displaystyle\nabla_{\partial_{y}}\varphi_{1}

\displaystyle=

\mathbbm{2}

Proof of Theorem

.

Owing to

Q({\mathscr{A}},m)=x^{2n}y^{2n}z^{2n}(x+y)(y+z)(x+z)

for a non-negative integer

\exp({\mathscr{A}},m)=(2n+1,2n+1,2n+1)

Q({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})=x^{2n+1}y^{2n+1}z^{2n+1}(x+y)^{2}(y+z)^{2}(x+z)^{2}.

Subsequently, such an

\theta=x^{2n+1}(a_{1}x+b_{1}y+c_{1}z)\partial_{x}+y^{2n+1}(a_{2}x+b_{2}y+c_{2}z)\partial_{y}+z^{2n+1}(a_{3}x+b_{3}y+c_{3}z)\partial_{z},

.

4.5.
Supersolvable multiarrangements, II

In this section we investigate supersolvable multiarrangements
({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
, where
\operatorname{rank}({\mathscr{A}})=3
. Fix a supersolvable filtration

({\mathscr{A}}_{1},m_{1}+\mathbbm{1})\subset({\mathscr{A}}_{2},m_{2}+\mathbbm{1})\subset({\mathscr{A}}_{3},m+\mathbbm{1})=({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})

of
({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
. First we assume that
{\mathscr{A}}_{2}={\mathscr{A}}(A_{2})
and that
m+\mathbbm{1}
is balanced. The following three cases are to be considered.

1. If
|{\mathscr{A}}\backslash{\mathscr{A}}_{2}|=2
, then
{\mathscr{A}}
is the deleted
A_{3}
arrangement.

2. If
|{\mathscr{A}}\backslash{\mathscr{A}}_{2}|=3
, then
{\mathscr{A}}
is the Coxeter arrangement of type
A_{3}
.

3. If
|{\mathscr{A}}\backslash{\mathscr{A}}_{2}|>3
, then

Q({\mathscr{A}})=xy(x-y)\prod_{1\leq i\leq|{\mathscr{A}}|-3}(z-a_{i}x).

Note that only the last case needs to be considered due to the results in the earlier sections. For this we require the following result from
[
8
]
.

Lemma 4.10

(
[
8
, Lem. 3.4]
)
.

Let
({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
be a multiarrangement. Fix
H_{0}\in{\mathscr{A}}
and let
m_{0}\coloneqq(m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H_{0}})(H_{0})
. For every
X\in{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}:={\mathscr{A}}^{H_{0}}
fix an
H_{X}\in{\mathscr{A}}\backslash\{H_{0}\}
such that
X=H_{0}\cap H_{X}
and define
d_{X}\in\exp({\mathscr{A}}_{X},m_{X}+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H})
as the unique non-shared exponent of
({\mathscr{A}}_{X},m_{X}+\mathbbm{1})
and
({\mathscr{A}}_{X},m_{X}+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H_{0}})
. Define the polynomial
B=B({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},(m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H_{0}})^{*})
by

B=\alpha_{H_{0}}^{m_{0}-1}\prod_{X\in{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}}\alpha_{H_{X}}^{d_{X}-m_{0}}.

For any
\theta\in D({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
we have
\theta(\alpha_{0})\in(\alpha_{0}^{m_{0}},B)
.

From Lemma
4.10
we can derive the following criterion for the non-existence of universal derivations for a multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
.

Corollary 4.11
.

With the notation as in Lemma
4.10
let
\theta\in D({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
be homogeneous with
\deg\theta<\deg B
. Then
\theta\in D({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H_{0}})
and in particular,
D({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H_{0}})_{\deg\theta}\neq(0)
.

Proof.

Thanks to Lemma
4.10
we have
\theta(\alpha_{0})\in(\alpha_{0}^{m_{0}},B)
, but from
\deg(\theta)<\deg(B)
and
\deg(\alpha_{0})=1
, we derive
\deg(\theta(\alpha_{0}))<\deg(B)
. This shows that we have
\theta(\alpha_{0})\in\alpha_{0}^{m_{0}}\cdot S
and therefore
\theta\in D({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H_{0}})
.
∎

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
be a free multiarrangement with
\operatorname{rank}({\mathscr{A}})=3
and exponents
\exp({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})=(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})
, where
d_{1}\leq d_{2}\leq d_{3}
. If there exists an
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
such that
|({\mathscr{A}}^{H},(m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H})^{*})|<d_{2}+d_{3}
, then
D({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H})_{d_{1}}\neq(0)
.

Proof.

Since
\exp({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})=(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})
, we have
D(A,m+\mathbbm{1})_{d_{1}}\neq(0)
. Due to Corollary
4.11
it is sufficient to show
d_{1}<\deg B
. By definition of
B
and substituting
m_{0}=(m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H})(H)
, we have

\deg B=(m+\mathbbm{1})(H)+\sum_{X\in{\mathscr{A}}^{H}}\left(d_{X}-m_{0}\right),

where
d_{X}
and
B
are as in Lemma
4.10
. By the definitions of the Euler multiplicity (Definition
2.4
) and
d_{X}
we have
|(m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H})_{X}|=d_{X}+(m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H})^{*}(X)
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{X\in{\mathscr{A}}^{H}}d_{X}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{X\in{\mathscr{A}}^{H}}|(m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H})_{X}|\right)-|(m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H})^{*}|.

Now utilize these equations to derive

\deg B-d_{1}=(m+\mathbbm{1})(H)+\sum_{X\in{\mathscr{A}}^{H}}\left(d_{X}-m_{0}\right)-d_{1}

=(m+\mathbbm{1})(H)+\left(\sum_{X\in{\mathscr{A}}^{H}}d_{X}\right)-|{\mathscr{A}}^{H}|\cdot m_{0}-d_{1}

=(m+\mathbbm{1})(H)+\left(\sum_{X\in{\mathscr{A}}^{H}}|(m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H})_{X}|\right)-|(m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H})^{*}|-|{\mathscr{A}}^{H}|\cdot m_{0}-d_{1}

=(m+\mathbbm{1})(H)+\sum_{X\in{\mathscr{A}}^{H}}\left(|(m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H})_{X}|-m_{0}\right)-|(m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H})^{*}|-d_{1}.

Finally, utilize
|(m+\mathbbm{1})|=d_{1}+d_{2}+d_{3}
and
|(m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H})^{*}|<d_{2}+d_{3}
to derive

(m+\mathbbm{1})(H)+\sum_{X\in{\mathscr{A}}^{H}}\left(|(m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H})_{X}|-m_{0}\right)-\left(|(m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H})^{*}|+d_{1}\right)

>(m+\mathbbm{1})(H)+\sum_{X\in{\mathscr{A}}^{H}}\left(|(m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H})_{X}|-(m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H})(H)\right)-|(m+\mathbbm{1})|=0.

Consequently, we have shown
d_{1}<\deg B
, as desired.
∎

Next, we study the case when
{\mathscr{A}}
is supersolvable with
{\mathscr{A}}_{2}={\mathscr{A}}(A_{2})
and
|{\mathscr{A}}\backslash{\mathscr{A}}_{2}|>3
.

Proposition 4.13
.

Let
Q({\mathscr{A}})=xy(x-y)\prod_{1\leq i\leq h}(z-a_{i}x)
with
h\coloneqq|{\mathscr{A}}\backslash{\mathscr{A}}_{2}|>3
and
m+\mathbbm{1}
a multiplicity on
{\mathscr{A}}
such that
({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
is supersolvable for the filtration
\{\ker x\}\subset{\mathscr{A}}_{2}={\mathscr{A}}(A_{2})\subset{\mathscr{A}}_{3}={\mathscr{A}}.
Then there does not exist any universal derivation for
({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
.

Proof.

Since
({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
is supersolvable, we utilize Theorems
3.4
and
4.1
to derive

\exp({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})=\left(\left\lfloor\frac{|m_{2}+\mathbbm{1}|}{2}\right\rfloor,\left\lceil\frac{|m_{2}+\mathbbm{1}|}{2}\right\rceil,|m+\mathbbm{1}|-|m_{2}+\mathbbm{1}|\right).

If there exists a universal derivation, then all exponents of
({\mathscr{A}},m)
have to be equal. This shows that
m_{2}+\mathbbm{1}
must be a peak point for
{\mathscr{A}}(A_{2})
and therefore
|m_{2}+\mathbbm{1}|
is odd. This condition on
\exp({\mathscr{A}},m)
also forces
\frac{|m_{2}+\mathbbm{1}|-3}{2}=|m+\mathbbm{1}|-|m_{2}+\mathbbm{1}|-h
. Hence

\exp({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})=\left(\frac{|m_{2}+\mathbbm{1}|-1}{2},\frac{|m_{2}+\mathbbm{1}|+1}{2},\frac{|m_{2}+\mathbbm{1}|-3}{2}+h\right).

Let
H\in{\mathscr{A}}_{3}\backslash{\mathscr{A}}_{2}
. Due to Theorem
1.2
and Lemma
4.12
it suffices to show

|(m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H})^{*}|<\frac{|m_{2}+\mathbbm{1}|+1}{2}+\frac{|m_{2}+\mathbbm{1}|-3}{2}+h=|m_{2}+\mathbbm{1}|+h-1.

We have
|(m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H})^{*}|=|m_{2}+\mathbbm{1}|
since for all localizations of rank two we either have
(m+\mathbbm{1})(\ker x)\geq(\sum_{H\in({\mathscr{A}}\backslash{\mathscr{A}}_{2})}(m+\mathbbm{1})(H))-1
due to the supersolvability of
({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
or
|{\mathscr{A}}_{X}|=2
. Since
h\geq 3
, we derive
|(m+\mathbbm{1}+\delta_{H})^{*}|=|m_{2}+\mathbbm{1}|<|m_{2}+\mathbbm{1}|+h-1
as desired.
∎

In our final result we investigate a supersolvable arrangement
({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
, where
\operatorname{rank}({\mathscr{A}})=3
and
{\mathscr{A}}_{2}={\mathscr{A}}(B_{2})
in the supersolvable filtration

({\mathscr{A}}_{1},m_{1}+\mathbbm{1})\subset({\mathscr{A}}_{2},m_{2}+\mathbbm{1})\subset({\mathscr{A}}_{3},m+\mathbbm{1})=({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1}).

Proposition 4.14
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}={\mathscr{A}}(B_{3})
be the Coxeter arrangement of type
B_{3}

with supersolvable filtration

\{\ker x\}\subset\{\ker x,\ker y,\ker(x+y),\ker(2x+y)\}\subset{\mathscr{A}}.

Let

Q({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})=x^{a}y^{b}(x+y)^{c}(2x+y)^{d}z^{e}(y+z)^{f}(x+y+z)^{g}(2x+y+z)^{h}(2x+2y+z)^{i}.

Then there does not exist a universal derivation for
({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
.

Proof.

Let
m+\mathbbm{1}
such that
({\mathscr{A}},m+\mathbbm{1})
is supersolvable with the showcased filtration. Then, the following inequalities need to be satisfied:

a\geq f+g+h-1
,
b\geq e+f-1
,
b\geq h+i-1
,
c\geq e+g+i-1
,
d\geq e+h-1
, and
d\geq f+i-1
.

We have
|m_{2}+\mathbbm{1}|=a+b+c+d
and
|m+\mathbbm{1}|-|m_{2}+\mathbbm{1}|=e+f+g+h+i
. From the inequalities above, we derive

•

a+b+c+d\geq(f+g+h-1)+(e+f-1)+(e+g+i-1)+(e+h-1)

\iff a+b+c+d\geq 3e+2f+2g+2h+i-4

\iff\frac{a+b+c+d}{2}+2\geq\frac{3e+i}{2}+f+g+h
.

•

a+b+c+d\geq(f+g+h-1)+(h+i-1)+(e+g+i-1)+(f+i-1)

\iff a+b+c+d\geq e+2f+2g+2h+3i-4

\iff\frac{a+b+c+d}{2}+2\geq\frac{3i+e}{2}+f+g+h
.

If
e\geq i
, then use the first inequality and derive

\frac{a+b+c+d}{2}+2\geq\frac{3e+i}{2}+f+g+h\geq e+f+g+h+i.

If
e\leq i
, then use the second inequality and derive

\frac{a+b+c+d}{2}+2\geq\frac{e+3i}{2}+f+g+h\geq e+f+g+h+i.

If
m
had all equal exponents, we would have

\frac{a+b+c+d-4}{2}=e+f+g+h+i-5

\iff\frac{a+b+c+d}{2}+2=e+f+g+h+i-1.

This contradicts

\frac{a+b+c+d}{2}+2\geq e+f+g+h+i.

This completes the proof.
∎

4.6.
Totally free arrangements and universal derivations

We finish this section by presenting a totally free arrangement for which no non-trivial universal derivation exists.

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be the real arrangement given by
Q({\mathscr{A}})=xy(x-y)(x-\pi y)
. Maehara introduced this arrangement in
[
12
, Cor. 3.10]
and showed that for a balanced multiplicity
m\not=\mathbbm{1}
on
{\mathscr{A}}
we have

(1)

\Delta(m)=0
, if
|m|
is even, and

(2)

\Delta(m)=1
, if
|m|
is odd.

Since
\operatorname{rank}({\mathscr{A}})=2
the multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}},m)
is totally free. As
|{\mathscr{A}}|=4
, Theorem
1.3
shows that there does not exist a universal derivation (other than
\theta_{E}
) for
{\mathscr{A}}
.
∎

Problem 5.2

Does the existence of a universal derivation
\theta\in D({\mathscr{A}},m)

\theta_{X}\in D({\mathscr{A}}_{X},m_{X})
?

As it turns out for numerous supersolvable arrangements
{\mathscr{A}}
one can define a multiplicity
m
such that
({\mathscr{A}},m)
is supersolvable and admits a universal derivation. This suggests that supersolvability is a requirement for an universal derivation to exist. We emphasize that this is not the case, i.e., see
[
7
]
and
[
9
]
.

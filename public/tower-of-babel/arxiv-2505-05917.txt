Abstract
. In this paper, we study the asymptotic behavior of energy and action ground states to the following pseudo-relativistic Hartree equation ( − c 2 ​ Δ + m 2 ​ c 4 − m ​ c 2 ) ​ u + λ ​ u = ( | x | − 1 ∗ | u | 2 ) ​ u \left(\sqrt{-c^{2}\Delta+m^{2}c^{4}}-mc^{2}\right)u+\lambda u=\left(|x|^{-1}*|u|^{2}\right)u as the speed of light c → ∞ c\to\infty . We obtain an asymptotic expansion of the ground state as c → ∞ , c\to\infty, which is new in the case of the energy ground state and generalizes the results of Choi, Hong, and Seok [ 3 ] for the action ground state.

1.
Introduction

We study the asymptotic behaviour as
c\to+\infty
of the
pseudo-relativistic Hartree equation

(1.1)

i\partial_{t}\psi=(\sqrt{-c^{2}\Delta+m^{2}c^{4}}-mc^{2})\psi-\mathcal{N}(\psi),\quad\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{3}.

where
\psi=\psi(t,x)
is a complex-valued wave function,

\mathcal{N}(\psi)=(|x|^{-1}*\lvert\psi\rvert^{2})\psi
is a
nonlinearity of Hartree type, the symbol
*
stands for convolution,

c>0
represents the speed of light and
m>0
is the particle
mass.

The operator
\sqrt{-c^{2}\Delta+m^{2}c^{4}}
is the pseudo-relativistic
operator which can be defined for
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
as the
inverse Fourier transform of the function

\sqrt{c^{2}|\xi|^{2}+m^{2}c^{4}}\mathcal{F}[u](\xi)

(here
\mathcal{F}[u]
represents the Fourier transform of
u
), that
is

\sqrt{-c^{2}\Delta+m^{2}c^{4}}\,u(x)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\sqrt{c^{2}|\xi|^{2}+m^{2}c^{4}}\mathcal{F}[u](\xi)\right](x),\qquad\forall u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}).

Here and in the following we work in Sobolev spaces

H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for
s\geq 0
defined as

H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})=\left\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\;:\;(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s/2}\mathcal{F}[u](\xi)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\right\}.

As we have done above, where we have defined the operator

\sqrt{-c^{2}\Delta+m^{2}c^{4}}
via Fourier transform and its
symbol
\sqrt{c^{2}\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}+m^{2}c^{4}}
, we will identify the
differential operator
P(D)
with its symbol
P(\xi)
also when

P(\xi)
is a polynomial. In all case we have that

(P(D)[u])(x)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[P(\xi)\mathcal{F}[u](\xi)\right](x)

for all
u\in H^{s}
provided
P(\xi)(1+\lvert\xi\rvert^{2})^{-s/2}
is
bounded. In such a case
P(D)
maps
H^{s+t}
to
H^{t}
for all
t\geq 0
. For more information on pseudo-differential operator we refer
to
[
18
]
.

Physically, equation (
1.1
) is an important model to describe the
dynamics of pseudo-relativistic boson stars in the mean field limit.
The equation (
1.1
) formally converges to the non-relativistic
Hartree equation

i\partial_{t}\psi=-\frac{\Delta}{2m}\psi-\mathcal{N}(\psi),\,\,\text{ on}\,\,\mathbb{R}^{3}.

as
c\to\infty
, since
\sqrt{c^{2}|\xi|^{2}+m^{2}c^{4}}-mc^{2}\to\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{2m}
as
c\to\infty
.

Looking for a stationary solution, inserting
\psi(t,x)=e^{i\lambda t}u(x)
with
\lambda>0
, we obtain the time-independent equation

(1.2)

\left(\sqrt{-c^{2}\Delta+m^{2}c^{4}}-mc^{2}\right)u+\lambda u=\mathcal{N}(u),

which can be considered as the relativistic versions of usual
nonlinear Hartree equation

(1.3)

-\frac{1}{2m}\Delta u+\lambda u=\mathcal{N}(u).

The existence of positive solutions to (
1.2
) can be addressed by
variational methods in two different ways, either by minimizing the
action functional
\mathcal{J}_{c}\colon H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\to\mathbb{R}
:

\mathcal{J}_{c}(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u(\sqrt{-c^{2}\Delta+m^{2}c^{4}}u-mc^{2}u+\lambda u)\,dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)}{|x-y|}\,dxdy

on the associated Nehari manifold

(1.4)

\mathcal{M}_{c}:=\left\{\,u\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\setminus\{0\}\Bigm|d\mathcal{J}_{c}(u)[u]=0\,\right\},

or by minimizing the energy functional
\mathcal{E}_{c}\colon H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\to\mathbb{R}

(1.5)

\mathcal{E}_{c}(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u(\sqrt{-c^{2}\Delta+m^{2}c^{4}}u-mc^{2}u)dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)}{|x-y|}dxdy

on the
L^{2}
sphere

\mathcal{S}=\left\{\,u\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\Bigm|\|u\|_{L^{2}}=1\,\right\}.

Similarly, in the non-relativistic case, one can find the solution of
(
1.3
) by minimizing the action functional
\mathcal{J}_{\infty}:H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\to\mathbb{R}
:

\mathcal{J}_{\infty}(u):=\frac{1}{2m}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}+\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)}{|x-y|}\,dxdy

on the manifold

(1.6)

\mathcal{M}_{\infty}:=\left\{\,u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\setminus\{0\}\Bigm|d\mathcal{J}_{\infty}(u)[u]=0\,\right\},

or by minimizing the energy functional

\mathcal{E}_{\infty}:H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\to\mathbb{R}

(1.7)

\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(u):=\frac{1}{2m}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)}{|x-y|}\,dxdy

on the
L^{2}
sphere

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=\left\{\,u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\Bigm|\|u\|_{L^{2}}=1\,\right\}.

Definition 1.1

(Ground states)
.

Using the notations introduced above,

(1)

(Action ground state) A function
u_{c}\in\mathcal{M}_{c}
is
called a (pseudo-relativistic) action ground state of
(
1.2
) if

\mathcal{J}_{c}(u_{c})=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{M}_{c}}\mathcal{J}_{c}(u).

Respectively,
u_{\infty}\in\mathcal{M}_{\infty}
is called a
(non-relativistic) action ground state of (
1.3
) if

\mathcal{J}_{\infty}(u_{\infty})=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{M}_{\infty}}\mathcal{J}_{\infty}(u).

(2)

(Energy ground state) A function
w_{c}\in\mathcal{S}
is
called an (pseudo-relativistic) energy ground state (or
minimizers) for the variational problem

(1.8)

e_{c}=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{E}_{c}(u),

if
\mathcal{E}_{c}(w_{c})=e_{c}
.

Respectively,
w_{\infty}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is called a
(non-relativistic) energy ground state (or minimizers) for the
variational problem

(1.9)

e_{\infty}=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(u)

provided
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(w_{\infty})=e_{\infty}
.

It is easy to see that the energy ground state
w_{c}
,
w_{\infty}
for
problem (
1.8
) and (
1.9
) satisfies the Hartree equation,

(1.10)

\displaystyle\left(\sqrt{-c^{2}\Delta+m^{2}c^{4}}-mc^{2}\right)w_{c}+\omega_{c}w_{c}=\mathcal{N}(w_{c}),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|w_{c}|^{2}=1,

and

(1.11)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2m}\Delta w_{\infty}+\omega_{\infty}w_{\infty}=\mathcal{N}(w_{\infty}),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|w_{\infty}|^{2}=1,

with Lagrange multiplier
\omega_{c}
,
\omega_{\infty}
respectively.

These problems have received a lot of attention. For the
non-relativistic Hartree equations, results go back to the work of
Lieb
[
13
]
and PL Lions
[
15
]
, see

[
17
]
for a comprehensive discussion of the problem.
Let us remark that the non-relativistic action and energy problems are
equivalent. One can go from solutions of one problem to solutions of
the other via multiplication and rescaling (see for example

[
16
, Proposition 2.1]
). This is no more true for the
pseudo-relativistic action and energy, due to the behaviour of the
linear operator
\sqrt{-c^{2}\Delta+m^{2}c^{4}}-mc^{2}
under rescaling.

Over the past few years, many works have been devoted also to the
mathematical analysis of problem (
1.10
), see
[
14
,
7
,
11
,
9
,
10
,
1
,
8
]
. For the
reader’s convenience, we provide a concise summary of the existence
and uniqueness results concerning ground states, as well as a list of
their fundamental properties.

Theorem 1.2

(Existence and properties of energy ground state)
.

There exists
c_{0}>0
, such that the following holds.

(1)

(Existence and uniqueness of ground state) For
c>c_{0}
, up to phase and translation, there exists a unique
positive radial ground state
w_{c}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for
problem (
1.8
). Moreover,
w_{c}
satisfies the
pseudo-relativistic Hartree equation (
1.10
) with some
Lagrange multiplier
\omega_{c}>0
.

(2)

(Regularity) Let
w_{c}
be the energy ground state, then

w_{c}
belongs to
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for each
s>0
.

(3)

(Non-relativistic limit) Let
w_{c}
(
w_{\infty}
) be the
unique positive radial energy ground state of the variational
problem (
1.8
) (resp., (
1.9
)), then
w_{c}
(
w_{\infty}
)
satisfy the equation (
1.10
) (resp., (
1.11
)) with
Lagrange multiplier
\omega_{c}>0
(
\omega_{\infty}>0
).
Moreover,

\displaystyle w_{c}\to w_{\infty}\quad\text{in}\quad H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\quad\text{as}\quad c\to\infty,

\displaystyle\omega_{c}\to\omega_{\infty},\quad e_{c}\to e_{\infty},\quad\text{as}\quad c\to\infty.

To introduce our main results, let us observe that the symbol of the
operator

P_{c}(D)=\sqrt{-c^{2}\Delta+m^{2}c^{4}}-mc^{2}

has the following Taylor series expansion for
\lvert\xi\rvert<mc

P_{c}(\xi)=\sqrt{c^{2}|\xi|^{2}+m^{2}c^{4}}-mc^{2}=P_{\infty}(\xi)+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{P_{\infty,j}(\xi)}{c^{2j}},

where
P_{\infty}(\xi)=\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{2m}
,

P_{\infty,j}(\xi)=\frac{(-1)^{j}\alpha_{j+1}}{m^{2j+1}}|\xi|^{2j+2}
,

\alpha_{j}=\frac{(2j-2)!}{j!(j-1)!2^{2j-1}}
. This expansion leads to
the following asymptotic estimate for the pseudo-relativistic operator

P_{c}(D)
in the operator norm:

\left\|P_{c}(D)-P_{\infty}(D)-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{P_{\infty,j}(D)}{c^{2j}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(H^{2n+2+s};H^{s})}\lesssim\frac{1}{c^{2n}},

see Remark
2.4
for details. In view of non-relativistic
limit, it is natural to ask whether the action or energy ground state

u
has the same asymptotic expansion, that is, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, whether there exists
f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{n-1}
such
that for any
s>0
,

(1.12)

\left\|u-u_{\infty}-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{f_{j}}{c^{2j}}\right\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\frac{1}{c^{2n}}.

When
n=1
, (
1.12
) has been proved in
[
3
]
for
action ground state, where the non-degeneracy of the non-relativistic
ground state plays a crucial role.

In our paper, we consider the asymptotic properties of this solution
like (
1.12
) for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
as
c\to\infty
.
Compared with
[
3
]
, we adopt a different approach to
obtain the regularity of ground state in higher order Sobolev space,
and our results extend that of
[
3
]
to each order
convergence rate. We also give details on the non-relativistic limit
of the action ground states.

Theorem 1.6

(Asymptotic properties of action ground states)
.

Let
c>c_{0}
and
u_{c}
,
u_{\infty}
be the unique positive radial
action ground state for the equation (
1.2
) and (
1.3
)
respectively. Then for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a
unique radial function
f_{j}\in\bigcap\limits_{s>0}H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

\left\|u_{c}-u_{\infty}-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{f_{j}}{c^{2j}}\right\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\frac{1}{c^{2n}}.

The functions
f_{j}

(j\geq 1)
are defined recursively as
solutions of the following linear differential equation

(1.13)

\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}f_{j}=-\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}P_{\infty,j-k}(D)f_{k}+\sum_{k=2}^{\min\{j,3\}}\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+i_{k}=j\\
1\leq i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{k}\leq j-1\end{subarray}}\mathcal{N}^{(k)}(f_{0})[f_{i_{1}},f_{i_{2}},\cdots,f_{i_{k}}],

where
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}
is the linearization of the nonlinear Hartree equation
(
1.3
) around the solution
u_{\infty}
(see Lemma
2.13
),

\mathcal{N}^{(k)}
is the
k
-th derivative of
\mathcal{N}
,

.

We have a result also for the energy ground states. This result is new

.

2.
Notation and preliminary results

We let

P_{c}(D):=\sqrt{-c^{2}\Delta+m^{2}c^{4}}-mc^{2}

and, for all
u\in H^{1/2}
,

\mathcal{H}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{u^{2}(x)u^{2}(y)}{\lvert x-y\rvert}.

Then, for all
u
,
v\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

d\mathcal{H}(u)[v]=4\langle\mathcal{N}(u)\mid v\rangle

and, letting

\langle P_{c}(D)u\mid v\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\sqrt{c^{2}\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}+m^{2}c^{4}}-mc^{2})\mathcal{F}[u](\xi)\mathcal{F}[v](\xi)\,d\xi

we have that

\mathcal{J}_{c}(u)=\langle P_{c}(D)u\mid u\rangle+\lambda\lVert u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{H}(u)

and

d\mathcal{J}_{c}(u)[v]=2\langle P_{c}(D)u\mid v\rangle+2\lambda\langle u\mid v\rangle-2\langle\mathcal{N}(u)\mid v\rangle.

The symbol of operator
P_{c}(D):=\sqrt{-c^{2}\Delta+m^{2}c^{4}}-mc^{2}
has
Taylor expansion converging for
\lvert\xi\rvert<mc

P_{c}(\xi)=\sqrt{c^{2}|\xi|^{2}+m^{2}c^{4}}-mc^{2}=mc^{2}\left(\sqrt{\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{m^{2}c^{2}}+1}-1\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}\alpha_{k}}{m^{2k-1}c^{2k-2}}|\xi|^{2k}

where
\alpha_{k}=\frac{(2k-2)!}{k!(k-1)!2^{2k-1}}
. We denote the
difference between
\sqrt{c^{2}|\xi|^{2}+m^{2}c^{4}}-mc^{2}
and first
n

terms of its Taylor expansion by
P_{c,n}(\xi)
, that is

P_{c,n}(\xi):=\sqrt{c^{2}|\xi|^{2}+m^{2}c^{4}}-mc^{2}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}\alpha_{k}}{m^{2k-1}c^{2k-2}}|\xi|^{2k},

and the corresponding operator is

P_{c,n}(D):=\sqrt{-c^{2}\Delta+m^{2}c^{4}}-mc^{2}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}\alpha_{k}}{m^{2k-1}c^{2k-2}}(-\Delta)^{k}.

For convenience, we set

P_{\infty,n}(D):=\frac{(-1)^{n}\alpha_{n+1}}{m^{2n+1}}(-\Delta)^{n+1},\,\,P_{\infty}(D):=P_{\infty,0}(D)=-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{m}\Delta,

then

P_{c,n+1}(D)=P_{c,n}(D)-\frac{1}{c^{2n}}P_{\infty,n}(D).

Lemma 2.1
.

For all
c>0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
the operator
P_{c,n}(D)

is elliptic of order
2n
. Its symbol
P_{c,n}(\xi)
satisfies

\lvert P_{c,n}(\xi)\rvert\geq C\lvert\xi\rvert^{2n}\qquad\text{for $\xi$
large,}

and the following holds for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
:

(2.1)

\displaystyle P_{c,n}(\xi)\begin{cases}\ \leq 0&\text{ n is odd}\\
\ \geq 0&\text{ n is even}\end{cases}

and

(2.2)

\lvert P_{c,n}(\xi)\rvert\leq\frac{C_{n}|\xi|^{2n+2}}{m^{2n+1}c^{2n}}.

In particular

\lvert P_{c,2}(\xi)\rvert=\left\lvert\sqrt{c^{2}|\xi|^{2}+m^{2}c^{4}}-mc^{2}-\frac{\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}}{2m}+\frac{\lvert\xi\rvert^{4}}{8m^{3}c^{2}}\right\rvert\leq\frac{C_{2}|\xi|^{6}}{m^{5}c^{4}}.

Remark 2.2
.

Inequalities of the type
0\leq P(\xi)\leq Q(\xi)
between the
symbols of the differential operators
P(D)
and
Q(D)
will be
used to deduce that

0\leq\langle P(\xi)u\mid u\rangle\leq\langle Q(\xi)u\mid u\rangle

for all
u\in H^{s}
(with
s
sufficiently large).

We will use the inequalities of Lemma
2.1
in the proofs
of Lemma
3.2
and
3.3
.

Proof.

Set

f(t)=\sqrt{t+1}-1,\quad t\geq 0.

According to Taylor’s formula

\displaystyle f(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{f^{(j)}(0)}{j!}t^{j}+\frac{f^{(n+1)}(\tau)}{(n+1)!}t^{n+1},\quad\tau\in[0,t]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}{(-1)^{j-1}\alpha_{j}}t^{j}+{(-1)^{n}\alpha_{n+1}}t^{n+1}(\tau+1)^{-\frac{2n+1}{2}},

and hence

(2.3)

\sqrt{\xi+1}-1-\sum_{j=1}^{n}{(-1)^{j-1}\alpha_{j}}\xi^{j}\quad\begin{cases}\leq 0&\text{$n$ odd}\\
\geq 0&\text{$n$ even}\end{cases}.

From this immediately we deduce

(2.4)

P_{c,n}(\xi)=mc^{2}\left(\sqrt{\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{m^{2}c^{2}}+1}-1-\sum_{j=1}^{n}{(-1)^{j-1}\alpha_{j}}\left(\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{m^{2}c^{2}}\right)^{j}\right)\ \begin{cases}\ \leq 0&\text{$n$ odd}\\
\ \geq 0&\text{$n$ even}\end{cases}.

We also deduce that

\left|P_{c,n}(\xi)\right|=mc^{2}\left|\sqrt{\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{m^{2}c^{2}}+1}-1-\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j-1}\alpha_{j}\left(\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{m^{2}c^{2}}\right)^{j}\right|\leq\frac{C_{n}|\xi|^{2n+2}}{m^{2n+1}c^{2n}}.

∎

Lemma 2.3
.

For
f\in H^{2n+2+s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
s\geq 0
, there exists a
constant
C_{n}>0
, independent of
c
and
m
, such that

\left\|P_{c,n}(D)f\right\|_{H^{s}}\leq\frac{C_{n}}{m^{2n+1}c^{2n}}\|f\|_{H^{2n+2+s}}.

Proof.

From (
2.2
) we have

\left\|P_{c,n}(D)f\right\|_{H^{s}}=\left\|(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s/2}P_{c,n}(\xi)\mathcal{F}[f]\right\|_{L^{2}}\\
\leq\frac{C_{n,m}}{c^{2n}}\left\|(|\xi|^{2n+2+s}+1)\mathcal{F}[f]\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{C_{n}}{m^{2n+1}c^{2n}}\|f\|_{H^{2n+2+s}}.

∎

Remark 2.4
.

Lemma
2.3
implies that
P_{c,n}(D)
, as a bounded linear
operator from
H^{2n+2+s}
to
H^{s}
, has its norm dominated by

c^{-2n}
, i.e.,

\left\|P_{c}(D)-P_{\infty}(D)-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{P_{\infty,j}(D)}{c^{2j}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(H^{2n+2+s};H^{s})}\lesssim\frac{1}{c^{2n}},

and it is easy to see that
{c^{-2n}}
is the optimal bound.

The following trilinear estimate in
[
3
, Lemma 3.2]
is of
fundamental importance for proving the higher regularity of the

).

Lemma 2.5
.

For
s\geq\frac{1}{2}
,
u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
there holds

\left\|\left(|x|^{-1}*\left(u_{1}u_{2}\right)\right)u_{3}\right\|_{H^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{3}\right)}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{3}\left\|u_{j}\right\|_{H^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{3}\right)}.

For convenience, we denote
\mathcal{N}^{(n)}
as the
n
-th
derivative of nonlinear mapping

\mathcal{N}:H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\to H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3}).

Then, direct computation shows

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{(1)}(u)[h_{1}]={\left(|x|^{-1}*u^{2}\right)h_{1}+2\left(|x|^{-1}*(uh_{1})\right)u},

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{(2)}(u)[h_{1},h_{2}]={2\left(|x|^{-1}*(h_{1}h_{2})\right)u+2\left(|x|^{-1}*(uh_{1})\right)h_{2}+2\left(|x|^{-1}*(uh_{2})\right)h_{1}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{(3)}(u)[h_{1},h_{2},h_{3}]={2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{cyclic}\\
i,j,k\end{subarray}}\left(|x|^{-1}*(h_{i}h_{j})\right)h_{k}},

and
\mathcal{N}^{(n)}(u)=0
for

.

Lemma 2.6

(Taylor’s formula)
.

For
s\geq\frac{1}{2}
,
u,h\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, there holds

(2.5)

\mathcal{N}(u+h)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{1}{k!}\mathcal{N}^{(k)}(u)[\underbrace{h,\cdots,h}_{k\,\,\text{times}}]+\frac{1}{n!}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{n}\mathcal{N}^{(n+1)}(u+th)[\underbrace{h,\cdots,h}_{n+1\,\,\text{times}}]dt.

By the Taylor’s formula, the following nonlinear estimation can be
directly obtained.

Lemma 2.7
.

For
s\geq\frac{1}{2}
,
u,h\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, there holds

(2.6)

\left\|\mathcal{N}(u+h)-\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{1}{k!}\mathcal{N}^{(k)}(u)[\underbrace{h,\cdots,h}_{k\,\,\text{times}}]\right\|_{H^{s}}\leq C\|h\|_{H^{s}}^{n+1},\quad n=1,2,

and

\mathcal{N}(u+h)=\sum_{k=0}^{3}\frac{1}{k!}\mathcal{N}^{(k)}(u)[\underbrace{h,\cdots,h}_{k\,\,\text{times}}].

where
C
is a constant which is dependent on
s
,
n
, and

\max\limits_{t\in[0,1]}\|u+th\|_{H^{s}}
.

Similarly, for the functional
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
, we have the
following expansion.

Lemma 2.8
.

For
s\geq\frac{1}{2}
,
u
,
h\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, there
holds

\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(u+h)=\sum_{k=0}^{4}\frac{1}{k!}d^{k}\mathcal{E}_{\infty}(u)[\underbrace{h,\cdots,h}_{k\,\,\text{times}}].

We recall the following convergence result

Lemma 2.9

(
[
6
, Lemma 4.1]
)
.

Let
v\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
f_{n},g_{n},h_{n}
bounded
sequences in
H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, and one of them converges
weakly to zero in
H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|f_{n}|(x)|g_{n}|(x)|v|(y)|h_{n}|(y)}{|x-y|}\,dxdy\to 0,\quad\text{as}\quad n\to+\infty.

Lemma 2.10
.

For
s\geq 0
, let
v\in H^{s+2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
h_{n},g_{n}
bounded
sequences in
H^{s+2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, and one of them converges
weakly to zero in
H^{s+2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, then

\left\|(|x|^{-1}*vh_{n})g_{n}\right\|_{H^{s}}\to 0,\quad\mathrm{as}\quad n\to+\infty.

Proof.

According to Lemma
2.5
, the sequence
f_{n}=(|x|^{-1}*vh_{n})g_{n}
is bounded in
H^{s+2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Then,
by Lemma
2.9
, we have

(2.7)

\left\|f_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|f_{n}(x)|\cdot|g_{n}(x)|\cdot|v(y)|\cdot|h_{n}(y)|}{|x-y|}\,dxdy\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad n\to+\infty.

There exists an integer
m
such that
s\leq m<m+1\leq s+2
. If
m
is even, then by Leibniz’s formula for weak
derivatives,
(-\Delta)^{\frac{m}{2}}f_{n}
can be expressed as a
linear combination of terms of the form

\left((|x|^{-1}*(\partial^{\alpha}v\partial^{\beta}h_{n})\right)\partial^{\gamma}g_{n}
, where the multi - indices

\alpha,\beta,\gamma
satisfy
|\alpha|+|\beta|+|\gamma|=m
.
By the given assumption, either
\partial^{\beta}h_{n}
or

\partial^{\gamma}g_{n}
converges weakly to zero in

H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Then, applying Lemma

2.9
, we obtain

(2.8)

\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{m}{2}}f_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
\leq\sum_{|\alpha|+|\beta|+|\gamma|=m}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|\Delta^{\frac{m}{2}}f_{n}(x)|\cdot|\partial^{\gamma}g_{n}(x)|\cdot|\partial^{\alpha}v(y)|\cdot|\partial^{\beta}h_{n}(y)|}{|x-y|}\,dxdy\to 0.

If
m
is odd, repeating the above argument, we get

(2.9)

\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{m}{2}}f_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\langle(-\Delta)^{\frac{m-1}{2}}f_{n}\mid(-\Delta)^{\frac{m+1}{2}}f_{n}\rangle\\
\leq\sum_{|\alpha|+|\beta|+|\gamma|=m-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|(-\Delta)^{\frac{m+1}{2}}f_{n}(x)|\cdot|\partial^{\gamma}g_{n}(x)|\cdot|\partial^{\alpha}v(y)|\cdot|\partial^{\beta}h_{n}(y)|}{|x-y|}\,dxdy\to 0.

By combining (
2.7
), (
2.8
) and (
2.9
), we
conclude that

\|f_{n}\|_{H^{s}}\leq\|f_{n}\|_{H^{m}}\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad n\to+\infty.

∎

We recall the following Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality, see

[
12
, Theorem 4.3]
.

Lemma 2.11
.

Assume that
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
g\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Then one has

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{f(x)g(y)}{|x-y|^{t}}\,dx\,dy\leq c(p,q,t)\|f\|_{p}\|g\|_{q},

where
1<p,q<\infty
,
0<t<3
and
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}+\frac{t}{3}=2
.

We will use some of the properties of the non-relativistic action and
energy ground states in proving the asymptotic behavior of the action
and energy ground states. In particular we recall that the unique
positive radial action ground state
u_{\infty}
to (
1.3
) is
nondegenerate in
H^{1}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
(see
[
11
, Proposition
2]
and
[
20
, Theorem III.1]
), that means
the following linearized equation has only the trivial solution
u=0

in
H^{1}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}u:

\displaystyle=\left(P_{\infty}(D)+\lambda\right)u-\mathcal{N}^{(1)}(u_{\infty})u

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2m}\Delta u+\lambda u-\left(|x|^{-1}*u_{\infty}^{2}\right)u-2\left(|x|^{-1}*(u_{\infty}u)\right)u_{\infty}.

Let us recall that existence and uniqueness of the positive radial
solution goes back to
[
13
]
, see also

[
19
]
. The precise knowledge of
\ker\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}\bigcap H^{1}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
implies
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}\colon H_{r}^{s+2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\to H_{r}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is invertible. To prove it we start by showing:

Lemma 2.12
.

For all
s\geq 0
the linear operator
\mathcal{N}^{(1)}(u_{\infty})\colon H_{r}^{s+2}\to H_{r}^{s}
is compact.

Proof.

Assume that the sequence
\{u_{n}\}
converges weakly to zero in

H^{s+2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Then, by Lemma
2.10
, it
follows that
\{\mathcal{N}^{(1)}(u_{\infty})u_{n}\}
converges strongly
to zero in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.
∎

Lemma 2.13
.

For each
\lambda>0
and
s\geq 0
, the linearized operator

\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}:H_{r}^{s+2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\to H_{r}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is
invertible.

Proof.

We have that

\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}=P_{\infty}(D)+\lambda-\mathcal{N}^{(1)}(u_{\infty})=\left(Id-\mathcal{N}^{(1)}(u_{\infty})(P_{\infty}(D)+\lambda)^{-1}\right)(P_{\infty}(D)+\lambda).

It can easily be seen that
P_{\infty}(D)+\lambda:H_{r}^{s+2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\to H_{r}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is invertible, we
need only prove the invertibility of
Id-\mathcal{N}^{(1)}(u_{\infty})(P_{\infty}(D)+\lambda)^{-1}
. It
follows from the non-degeneracy of linearized operator

\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}
that
\ker\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}\bigcap H_{r}^{1}=0.
By Lemma

2.12
, the operator

\mathcal{N}^{(1)}(u_{\infty})(P_{\infty}(D)+\lambda)^{-1}
is a
compact operator on
H_{r}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, and by Fredholm
alternative, we obtain the invertibility of
Id-\mathcal{N}^{(1)}(u_{\infty})(P_{\infty}(D)+\lambda)^{-1}
, and
the result follows.
∎

We state here also the following lemma which follows from the
invertibility of
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
f_{n},h_{n}
be two sequences in
\bigcap\limits_{s>0}H_{r}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
which satisfy

(2.10)

h_{n}=\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}f_{n},

and for each
s>0
, there holds

\|h_{n}\|_{H^{s}}=o_{n}(1)+o_{n}(1)\|f_{n}\|_{H^{s}},\quad\text{as }n\to\infty,

then
\|f_{n}\|_{H^{s}}=o_{n}(1)
as

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}
is invertible, there holds

\|f_{n}\|_{H^{s+2}}=\|\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}^{-1}h_{n}\|_{H^{s+2}}\lesssim\|h_{n}\|_{H^{s}}=o_{n}(1)+o_{n}(1)\|f_{n}\|_{H^{s}}.

Hence, for each
s>0
,

\|f_{n}\|_{H^{s}}=o_{n}(1),

as
n\to\infty.

∎

3.
Non-relativistic limit of the action ground state

In this section we fill in the proof of Theorem
1.4

showing that the action ground state converges as
c\to+\infty
to
the positive and radial action ground state for the equation
(
1.3
)
u_{\infty}
, which by Lemma 9 in
[
11
]
is unique.
The uniqueness of
u_{c}
for
c
large will follow.

Proposition 3.1
.

There exists
c_{0}>0
, such that for
c>c_{0}
, equation
(
1.2
) has a unique radial and positive action ground state

u_{c}
. Moreover, for each
s>0
, there holds

u_{c}\to u_{\infty}\quad\text{in}\quad H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\quad\text{as}\quad c\to\infty.

Regarding the existence of
u_{c}
, this is a standard variational
problem, and we omit the details, see for example
[
5
]
,
although there the ground state is found via the Mountain Pass
theorem.

Our main focus is on proving its non-relativistic limit. Let’s recall
that action ground state
u_{c}
(
u_{\infty}
) for the equation (
1.2
)
(resp., (
1.3
)) satisfies

\mathcal{J}_{c}(u_{c})=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{M}_{c}}\mathcal{J}_{c}(u),\qquad\mathcal{J}_{\infty}(u_{\infty})=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{M}_{\infty}}\mathcal{J}_{\infty}(u)

where

\mathcal{M}_{c}=\left\{\,u\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\setminus\{0\}\Bigm|d\mathcal{J}_{c}(u)[u]=0\,\right\},

and

\mathcal{M}_{\infty}=\left\{\,u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\setminus\{0\}\Bigm|d\mathcal{J}_{\infty}(u)[u]=0\,\right\}.

Lemma 3.2
.

For each
c>1
, there exists
t_{c}>0
, such that
t_{c}u_{\infty}\in\mathcal{M}_{c}
, and there holds

\lim_{c\to\infty}t_{c}=1.

Proof.

Since
u_{\infty}\in\mathcal{M}_{\infty}
we have that

\langle(P_{\infty}(D)+\lambda)u_{\infty}\mid u_{\infty}\rangle=\mathcal{H}(u_{\infty}).

For all
u\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

d\mathcal{J}_{c}(u)[u]=2\langle(P_{c}(D)+\lambda)u\mid u\rangle-2\mathcal{H}(u)

and hence
t_{c}u_{\infty}\in\mathcal{M}_{c}
provided
t_{c}

is such that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=2\langle(P_{c}(D)+\lambda)u_{\infty}\mid u_{\infty}\rangle=2t_{c}^{2}\mathcal{H}(u_{\infty})

\displaystyle=2t_{c}^{2}\langle(P_{\infty}(D)+\lambda)u_{\infty}\mid u_{\infty}\rangle.

Then

(t^{2}_{c}-1)\langle(P_{\infty}(D)+\lambda)u_{\infty}\mid u_{\infty}\rangle)=\langle(P_{c}(D)-P_{\infty}(D))u_{\infty}\mid u_{\infty}\rangle,

It follows from Lemma
2.1
that

(3.1)

0\leq P_{\infty}(\xi)-P_{c}(\xi)=\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{2m}-\left(\sqrt{c^{2}|\xi|^{2}+m^{2}c^{4}}-mc^{2}\right)\leq\frac{|\xi|^{4}}{8m^{3}c^{2}}.

and
hence

|t_{c}^{2}-1|\langle(P_{\infty}(D)+\lambda)u_{\infty}\mid u_{\infty}\rangle)\leq\frac{1}{8m^{3}c^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathcal{F}[u_{\infty}]^{2}(\xi)|\xi|^{4}d\xi,

which yields
\lim\limits_{c\to\infty}t_{c}=1.

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

\limsup\limits_{c\to\infty}\mathcal{J}_{c}(u_{c})\leq\mathcal{J}_{\infty}(u_{\infty})
.

Proof.

From Lemma
2.1
we have that

\sqrt{-c^{2}\Delta+m^{2}c^{4}}-mc^{2}\leq-\frac{\Delta}{2m}.

It then follows Lemma
3.2
that

\limsup_{c\to\infty}\mathcal{J}_{c}(u_{c})\leq\limsup_{c\to\infty}\mathcal{J}_{c}(t_{c}u_{\infty})\leq\limsup_{c\to\infty}\mathcal{J}_{\infty}(t_{c}u_{\infty})=\mathcal{J}_{\infty}(u_{\infty}).

∎

Lemma 3.4
.

\sup\limits_{c>1}\|u_{c}\|_{H^{1/2}}<\infty.

Proof.

Given that
u_{c}
satisfies

\langle(P_{c}(D)+\lambda)u_{c}\mid u_{c}\rangle=\mathcal{H}(u_{c})

then
\mathcal{J}_{c}(u_{c})=\frac{1}{2}\langle(P_{c}(D)+\lambda)u_{c}\mid u_{c}\rangle
and, by Lemma
3.3
, we get

\sup_{c>1}\langle(P_{c}(D)+\lambda)u_{c}\mid u_{c}\rangle=\sup_{c>1}2\mathcal{J}_{c}(u_{c})<\infty.

There exists a constant
C>0
which depends on
\lambda>0

and
m
, such that for
c>1
,

(3.2)

P_{c}(\xi)+\lambda=\sqrt{c^{2}\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}+m^{2}c^{4}}-mc^{2}+\lambda\geq C\sqrt{\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}+1}.

Therefore, we have

\sup_{c>1}\|u_{c}\|_{H^{1/2}}^{2}\lesssim\sup_{c>1}\langle(P_{c}(D)+\lambda)u_{c}\mid u_{c}\rangle<\infty.

∎

As a corollary to Lemma
3.4
and by employing the method of

[
11
, Lemma 3]
, we can obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

\sup\limits_{c>1}\|u_{c}\|_{H^{1}}<\infty.

In
[
3
, Proposition 4.2]
, the authors show that the ground
state to (
1.2
) are uniformly bounded in higher order Sobolev
spaces. In their proof, the operator inequality (
3.2
)
played a crucial role. Here, we adopt a different approach to prove
that
u_{c}
are uniformly bounded in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
u_{c}
be the action ground state to (
1.2
), then
for each
s>0

\sup_{c>1}\|u_{c}\|_{H^{s}}<\infty.

Proof.

Acting with the operator
(-\Delta)^{s}\sqrt{-c^{2}\Delta+m^{2}c^{4}}

on both sides of (
1.2
), we have

(3.3)

\begin{split}c^{2}(-\Delta)^{s+1}u_{c}&=\sqrt{-c^{2}\Delta+m^{2}c^{4}}(-\Delta)^{s}((|x|^{-1}*u_{c}^{2})u_{c})\\
&+(mc^{2}-\lambda)(-\Delta)^{s}((|x|^{-1}*u_{c}^{2})u_{c})+(-\Delta)^{s}(\lambda^{2}-2mc^{2}\lambda)u_{c},\end{split}

then,

(3.4)

\begin{split}c^{2}\|(-\Delta)^{s+1}u_{c}\|_{L^{2}}&\lesssim\|\sqrt{-c^{2}\Delta+m^{2}c^{4}}(-\Delta)^{s}((|x|^{-1}*u_{c}^{2})u_{c})\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\quad+c^{2}\|(-\Delta)^{s}((|x|^{-1}*u_{c}^{2})u_{c})\|_{L^{2}}+c^{2}\|(-\Delta)^{s}u_{c}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&=:K_{1}+K_{2},\end{split}

where

\displaystyle K_{1}=\|\sqrt{-c^{2}\Delta+m^{2}c^{4}}(-\Delta)^{s}((|x|^{-1}*u_{c}^{2})u_{c})\|_{L^{2}},

\displaystyle K_{2}=c^{2}\|(-\Delta)^{s}((|x|^{-1}*u_{c}^{2})u_{c})\|_{L^{2}}+c^{2}\|(-\Delta)^{s}u_{c}\|_{L^{2}}.

If
0\leq s<\frac{1}{4}
, we have operator inequality

(3.5)

(-\Delta)^{s}\leq 4s(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{4}}+1-4s,

which follows from Young inequality
ab\leq\frac{a^{p}}{p}+\frac{b^{q}}{q}
with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1.
Then, Lemma

2.5
implies,

(3.6)

K_{2}\lesssim c^{2}\left(\||x|^{-1}*u_{c}^{2})u_{c}\|_{H^{1/2}}^{3}+\|u_{c}\|_{H^{1/2}}\right)\lesssim c^{2}\left(\|u_{c}\|_{H^{1/2}}^{3}+\|u_{c}\|_{H^{1/2}}\right).

If
s\geq\frac{1}{4}
, by Lemma
2.5
again

(3.7)

K_{2}\lesssim c^{2}\left(\|u_{c}\|_{H^{2s}}^{3}+\|u_{c}\|_{H^{2s}}\right).

To estimate
K_{1}
, from the operator inequality

(3.8)

\sqrt{-c^{2}\Delta+m^{2}c^{4}}\leq c(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}+mc^{2},

and combine (
3.8
) with (
3.5
), we are led to

(3.9)

K_{1}\lesssim\begin{cases}c\|u_{c}\|_{H^{2s+1}}^{3}+c^{2}\|u_{c}\|_{H^{1/2}}^{3},&0\leq s<\frac{1}{4},\\
c\|u_{c}\|_{H^{2s+1}}^{3}+c^{2}\|u_{c}\|_{H^{2s}}^{3},&\frac{1}{4}<s.\end{cases}

Consequently, by (
3.4
), (
3.6
), (
3.7
) and
(
3.9
), we get

(3.10)

\|u_{c}\|_{H^{2s+2}}\lesssim\begin{cases}\frac{1}{c}\|u_{c}\|_{H^{2s+1}}^{3}+\|u_{c}\|_{H^{1/2}}^{3}+\|u_{c}\|_{H^{1/2}}&0\leq s<\frac{1}{4},\\
\frac{1}{c}\|u_{c}\|_{H^{2s+1}}^{3}+\|u_{c}\|_{H^{2s}}^{3}+\|u_{c}\|_{H^{2s}}&\frac{1}{4}<s.\end{cases}

By combining (
3.10
)
with the fact that

\sup_{c>1}\|u_{c}\|_{H^{1}}<\infty,

we conclude that for each

s>0

\sup_{c>1}\|u_{c}\|_{H^{s}}<\infty.

∎

Lemma 3.7
.

\lim\limits_{c\to\infty}\mathcal{J}_{\infty}(u_{c})=\mathcal{J}_{\infty}(u_{\infty})
.

Proof.

Repeating the argument of Lemma
3.2
, for each
c>1
,
there exists
r_{c}>0
, such that
r_{c}u_{c}\in\mathcal{M}_{\infty}
,
and there holds

\lim_{c\to\infty}r_{c}=1.

Then,

\mathcal{J}_{\infty}(u_{\infty})\leq\mathcal{J}_{\infty}(r_{c}u_{c})\leq\liminf\limits_{c\to\infty}\mathcal{J}_{\infty}(u_{c}).

On the other hand, by operator inequality (see Lemma

2.1
) we have

-\frac{\Delta}{2m}\leq\sqrt{-c^{2}\Delta+m^{2}c^{4}}-mc^{2}+\frac{\Delta^{2}}{8m^{3}c^{2}},

and hence we have

\mathcal{J}_{\infty}(u_{c})\leq\mathcal{J}_{c}(u_{c})+\frac{1}{8m^{3}c^{2}}\|\Delta u_{c}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

By combining this with Lemma
3.3
and Lemma
3.6
,
there holds

\limsup\limits_{c\to\infty}\mathcal{J}_{\infty}(u_{c})\leq\mathcal{J}_{\infty}(u_{\infty}),

which completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

\{u_{c}\}
is a bounded Palais-Smale sequence for the functional

\mathcal{J}_{\infty}(u)
at level
\mathcal{J}_{\infty}(u_{\infty}).

Proof.

It follows from (
3.1
) and Lemma

that

\displaystyle\sup_{\|h\|_{H^{1}}\leq 1}|d\mathcal{J}_{\infty}(u_{c})[h]|

\displaystyle=\sup_{\|h\|_{H^{1}}\leq 1}|d\mathcal{J}_{\infty}(u_{c})[h]-d\mathcal{J}_{c}(u_{c})[h]|

\displaystyle=2\sup_{\|h\|_{H^{1}}\leq 1}\langle(P_{c}(D)-P_{\infty}(D))u_{c}\mid h\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4m^{3}c^{2}}\sup_{\|h\|_{H^{1}}\leq 1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\xi|^{4}|\mathcal{F}[{u}_{c}]|(\xi)|\mathcal{F}[{h}]|(\xi)\,d\xi\leq\frac{C}{c^{2}},

and the lemma follows taking into account Lemma
3.7
.
∎

Lemma 3.9
.

\|u_{c}-u_{\infty}\|_{H^{1}}\to 0
as

.

Proof.

Since

is radial and bounded sequence in

H^{1}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow L^{p}

(2<p<6)

sequence, we can assume that

converge weakly to

in

and

. Then,
u\geq 0
is a weak solution to (
1.3
). By Lemma

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{u_{c}^{2}(x)u_{c}(y)(u_{c}(y)-u(y))}{|x-y|}\,dxdy\leq

\displaystyle C\|u_{c}\|_{L^{12/5}}^{2}\|u_{c}(u_{c}-u)\|_{L^{6/5}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|u_{c}\|_{L^{12/5}}^{2}\|u_{c}\|_{L^{2}}\|u_{c}-u\|_{L^{3}}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle 0,\quad\text{ as }c\to\infty.

Similarly

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{u^{2}(x)u(y)(u_{c}(y)-u(y))}{|x-y|}\,dxdy\to 0,\quad\text{ as }c\to\infty.

Hence

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2m}\|\nabla(u_{c}-u)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lambda\|u_{c}-u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(d\mathcal{J}_{\infty}(u_{c})-d\mathcal{J}_{\infty}(u)\right)[u_{c}-u]

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{u_{c}^{2}(x)u_{c}(y)(u_{c}(y)-u(y))}{|x-y|}\,dxdy-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{u^{2}(x)u(y)(u_{c}(y)-u(y))}{|x-y|}\,dxdy

\displaystyle\quad\to 0,\quad\text{ as }c\to\infty,

which implies

\|u_{c}-u\|_{H^{1}}\to 0,\quad as\,\,c\to\infty.

It follows from Lemma
3.8
,
u
is a radially symmetric and
nonnegative action ground state solution to (
1.3
)(see

). By the uniqueness of radial and
nonnegative solution to (

.
∎

The following lemma implies that convergence of the

Lemma 3.10

It follows from Lemma

and Lemma

that

\left\|P_{c,1}(D)u_{c}\right\|_{H^{s}}=\|(P_{c}(D)-P_{\infty}(D))u_{c}\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\frac{1}{c^{2}}.

By Lemma

∎

Lemma

Set

Lemma 4.1
.

For each
s>0
, there holds
\|f_{c,1}-f_{1}\|_{H^{s}}\to 0
, as
c\to\infty.

Proof.

Let’s recall elliptic operator

P_{c,n}(D)=\sqrt{-c^{2}\Delta+m^{2}c^{4}}-mc^{2}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}\alpha_{k}}{m^{2k-1}c^{2k-2}}(-\Delta)^{k},

and

P_{c}(D):=\sqrt{-c^{2}\Delta+m^{2}c^{4}}-mc^{2}.

Then

P_{c,n+1}(D)=P_{c,n}(D)-\frac{1}{c^{2n}}P_{\infty,n}(D).

From (
1.2
) and (
1.3
), we have

(4.3)

\begin{split}-P_{c,1}(D)u_{c}=\mathcal{N}(u_{\infty})-\mathcal{N}(u_{c})+(P_{\infty}(D)+\lambda)(u_{c}-u_{\infty}).\end{split}

Multiply both sides of the equation by

, then

(4.4)

\begin{split}-c^{2}P_{c,1}(D)u_{c}=\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}f_{c,1}-G_{c,1},\end{split}

where

By combining (
4.1
) with (

(4.6)

By Lemma
2.7
, there holds

(4.7)

\|G_{c,1}\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim c^{2}\left\|\frac{f_{c,1}}{c^{2}}\right\|_{H^{s}}^{2}=o_{c}(1)\|f_{c,1}\|_{H^{s}}\leq o_{c}(1)+o_{c}(1)\|f_{c,1}-f_{1}\|_{H^{s}}.

By Lemma
2.3
and Lemma
3.6
, we obtain

(4.8)

\begin{split}\left\|c^{2}P_{c,1}(D)u_{c}-P_{\infty,1}(D)u_{\infty}\right\|_{H^{s}}&\lesssim\|c^{2}P_{c,2}(D)u_{c}\|_{H^{s}}+\|P_{\infty,1}(D)(u_{c}-u_{\infty})\|_{H^{s}}\\
&\lesssim\frac{1}{c^{2}}\|u_{c}\|_{H^{s+6}}+\|u_{c}-u_{\infty}\|_{H^{s+4}}\\
&=o_{c}(1),\quad\text{as}\,\,\,c\to\infty,\end{split}

then, applying Lemma
2.14
to (

), we conclude
that
\|f_{c,1}-f_{1}\|_{H^{s}}=o_{c}(1)

∎

Lemma 4.2
.

For each

there exists a unique

such that for all

and

\left\|c^{2n}u_{c}-\sum_{j=0}^{n}c^{2(n-j)}f_{j}\right\|_{H^{s}}\to 0\qquad\text{as }c\to+\infty.

In particular
f_{0}=u_{\infty}
and
f_{k}

(k\geq 1)
is
the unique radial solution of the following equation

(4.10)

\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}f_{k}=-\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}P_{\infty,k-j}(D)f_{j}+\mathcal{T}_{k}(f_{0},f_{1},\cdots,f_{k-1}),

where

(4.11)

\begin{split}\mathcal{T}_{k}(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{k-1})=\sum_{j=2}^{\min\{k,3\}}\frac{1}{j!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+i_{j}=k\\
1\leq i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{j}\leq k-1\end{subarray}}\mathcal{N}^{(j)}(f_{0})[f_{i_{1}},f_{i_{2}},\cdots,f_{i_{j}}].\end{split}

is nonlinearity of the Hartree-type respect to
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{k-1}
and
\mathcal{T}_{1}=0

Proof.

Let

and
f_{0}=u_{\infty}
. We will construct

and assuming we have found

for

4.10

to (
4.10
).

We now prove by induction on

that (
4.9
) holds.

Since

and (
4.10
) reduces to
(
4.1

) holds for

.

) and (

)
of
f_{c,1}
and

and for all

f_{c,1}
,

satisfies

\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}f_{c,k}=c^{2}\left[-\sum_{j=0}^{k-2}c^{2k-2j-2}P_{c,k-j}(D)f_{j}-P_{c,1}(D)f_{c,k-1}\right]+G_{c,k}.

Subtract (
4.10
) from equation (

), claim

4.15

Proof of the Claim (

From

\begin{split}G_{c,k}&=c^{2k}\left(\frac{G_{c,1}}{c^{2}}-\sum_{j=2}^{k-1}\frac{\mathcal{T}_{j}(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{j-1})}{c^{2j}}\right)\\

and the triangle inequality, we obtain

(4.17)

\begin{split}&\|G_{c,n}-\mathcal{T}_{n}(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{n-1})\|_{H^{s}}\\
&=c^{2n}\left\|\mathcal{N}\left(u_{c}\right)-\mathcal{N}(f_{0})-\mathcal{N}^{(1)}(f_{0})\left[u_{c}-f_{0}\right]-\sum_{j=2}^{n}\frac{\mathcal{T}_{j}(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{j-1})}{c^{2j}}\right\|_{H^{s}}\\

&\quad+c^{2n}\left\|\sum_{j=2}^{\min\{n,3\}}\frac{1}{j!}\mathcal{N}^{(j)}(f_{0})[\underbrace{u_{c}-f_{0},\cdots,u_{c}-f_{0}}_{\text{$j$ times}}]-\sum_{j=2}^{n}\frac{\mathcal{T}_{j}(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{j-1})}{c^{2j}}\right\|_{H^{s}}\\
&=:I_{1}+I_{2}.\end{split}

By Lemma
2.7
,

provided
n\geq 3
. For

,
Lemma
4.1
implies

(4.18)

\lVert f_{c,0}-f_{0}\rVert_{H^{s}}=\|u_{c}-f_{0}\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\frac{1}{c^{2}},

by combining Lemma
2.7
with (

), we obtain

(4.19)

\begin{split}\qquad\quad I_{1}=c^{4}&\left\|\mathcal{N}\left(u_{c}\right)-\mathcal{N}(f_{0})-\mathcal{N}^{(1)}(f_{0})\left[u_{c}-f_{0}\right]-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{N}^{(2)}(f_{0})[u_{c}-f_{0},u_{c}-f_{0}]\right\|_{H^{s}}\\
&\leq Cc^{4}\|u_{c}-f_{0}\|_{H^{s}}^{3}\lesssim\frac{1}{c^{2}}.\end{split}

From the assumption:
\|f_{c,n-1}-f_{n-1}\|_{H^{s}}=o_{c}(1)
, we have

u_{c}-f_{0}=\frac{f_{c,n-1}}{c^{2(n-1)}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-2}\frac{f_{i}}{c^{2i}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{f_{i}}{c^{2i}}+o_{c}(\frac{1}{c^{2n-2}}).

Consequently,

(4.20)

\begin{split}&\mathcal{N}^{(j)}(f_{0})[\underbrace{u_{c}-f_{0},\cdots,u_{c}-f_{0}}_{j\,\,\text{times}}]\\
&\quad=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{j}\leq n-1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{c^{2(i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+i_{j})}}\mathcal{N}^{(j)}(f_{0})[f_{i_{1}},f_{i_{2}},\cdots,f_{i_{j}}]+o_{c}(\frac{1}{c^{2n}})\\

and

(4.21)

\begin{split}\qquad&\sum_{j=2}^{\min\{n,3\}}\frac{1}{j!}\mathcal{N}^{(j)}(f_{0})[\underbrace{u_{c}-f_{0},\cdots,u_{c}-f_{0}}_{j\,\,\text{times}}]\\
&\quad=\sum_{j=2}^{\min\{n,3\}}\frac{1}{j!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+i_{j}\leq n\\
1\leq i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{j}\leq n-1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{c^{2(i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+i_{j})}}\mathcal{N}^{(j)}(f_{0})[f_{i_{1}},f_{i_{2}},\cdots,f_{i_{j}}]+o_{c}(\frac{1}{c^{2n}}).\end{split}

Then, from (
4.11
) and the fact that

\sum_{j=2}^{n}\sum_{k=2}^{\min\{j,3\}}a_{k,j}=\sum_{k=2}^{\min\{n,3\}}\sum_{j=k}^{n}a_{k,j},

we get

\begin{split}\sum_{j=2}^{n}\frac{\mathcal{T}_{j}(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{j-1})}{c^{2j}}&=\sum_{j=2}^{n}\sum_{k=2}^{\min\{j,3\}}\frac{1}{k!}\frac{1}{c^{2j}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+i_{k}=j\\
i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{k}\geq 1\end{subarray}}\mathcal{N}^{(k)}(f_{0})[f_{i_{1}},f_{i_{2}},\cdots,f_{i_{k}}]\\
&=\sum_{k=2}^{\min\{n,3\}}\sum_{j=k}^{n}\frac{1}{k!}\frac{1}{c^{2j}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+i_{k}=j\\
i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{k}\geq 1\end{subarray}}\mathcal{N}^{(k)}(f_{0})[f_{i_{1}},f_{i_{2}},\cdots,f_{i_{k}}]\\
&=\sum_{j=2}^{\min\{n,3\}}\frac{1}{j!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+i_{j}\leq n\\
1\leq i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{j}\leq n-1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{c^{2(i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+i_{j})}}\mathcal{N}^{(j)}(f_{0})[f_{i_{1}},f_{i_{2}},\cdots,f_{i_{j}}].\end{split}

Combining (
4.21
) with (

(4.23)

By taking (

), (
4.19
) into (

), we get

\|G_{c,n}-\mathcal{T}_{n}(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{n-1})\|_{H^{s}}\to 0,\quad\text{as}\quad c\to\infty.

which yields the Claim (
4.14
) holds.

Proof of the Claim (

)

Since

\begin{split}c^{2}&\left(-\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}c^{2n-2j-2}P_{c,n-j}(D)f_{j}-P_{c,1}(D)f_{c,n-1}\right)+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}P_{\infty,n-j}(D)f_{j}\\
&=-\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}c^{2n-2j}P_{c,n-j+1}(D)f_{j}-c^{2}P_{c,1}(D)(f_{c,n-1}-f_{n-1}),\end{split}

then by Lemma

(4.25)

\begin{split}&\left\|c^{2}\left(-\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}c^{2n-2j-2}P_{c,n-j}(D)f_{j}-P_{c,1}(D)f_{c,n-1}\right)+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}P_{\infty,n-j}(D)f_{j}\right\|_{H^{s}}\\

Hence, the Claim (

5.
Asymptotic property of energy ground state

In this section, we focus on asymptotic property of energy ground
state
w_{c}
to (
1.8
). Denote the unique positive radial energy
ground state to (

) by

. Then

,

satisfy (

(

The linearized operator of (

is given by

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{\omega_{\infty}}u:&=\left(P_{\infty}(D)+\omega_{\infty}\right)u-\mathcal{N}^{(1)}(w_{\infty})u\\

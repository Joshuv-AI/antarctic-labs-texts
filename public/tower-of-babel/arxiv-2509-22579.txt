Relativistic Quantum Simulation under Periodic and Dirichlet Boundary Conditions: A First-Quantised Framework for Near-Term Devices
Jaewoo Joo, Timothy P. Spiller, Kyunghyun Baek, Jeongho Bang

Abstract
We present a new recipe for relativistic quantum simulation using the first quantisation approach, under periodic (PBC) and Dirichlet (DBC) boundary conditions. The wavefunction is discretised across a finite grid represented by system qubits, and the squared momentum operator is expressed using the finite-difference method based on quantum translation operations. The relativistic kinetic energy is approximated through a perturbative expansion of the total kinetic Hamiltonian, incorporating higher-order momentum terms. The approach would allow variational optimisation of appropriate ansatz states to estimate both non-relativistic and relativistic ground-state energies on a quantum computer. This work offers a practical route to simulating relativistic effects on near-term quantum devices, supporting future developments in quantum physics and chemistry.



I
Introduction

Simulating quantum systems is crucial for understanding the intrinsic nature of quantum phenomena, but remains a challenging task in practice
Feynmann
. Due to the principles of superposition and entanglement, the complexity of describing a quantum system grows exponentially with its system size, making it difficult to compute the desired results for large quantum systems. In addition, the relativistic effects of quantum physics must be incorporated to accurately describe many important quantum phenomena. One of the standard approaches to studying relativistic quantum effects is to use perturbation theory in a quantum system
Kais2023
, which accumulates quantum relativistic effects beyond the non-relativistic framework.

We present a new quantum simulation method for determining a relativistic ground-state energy, for the cases of two different boundary conditions. In a chosen frame, the relativistic time-independent single-particle Hamiltonian is

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{tot}_{re}(\vec{r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{K}_{re}(\vec{r})+\hat{V}(\vec{r})\,,

(1)

where
\hat{H}^{K}_{re}
and
\hat{V}
are the relativistic kinetic and potential Hamiltonian components, dependent on the particle’s position
\vec{r}
in that frame.
From special relativity, the kinetic part of the Hamiltonian with positive energy is represented in the form of a square root. Therefore, it can be expanded to generate higher-order correction terms dependent on mass
m
and light speed
c
, in a valid expansion domain. For example, in the regime where relativistic effects are just becoming relevant,
\left<\hat{p}^{2}\right>\ll m^{2}\,c^{2}
, the perturbation terms
Edery2018
;
KG
are given by

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{K}_{re}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m\,c^{2}\left(\sqrt{1+{\hat{p}^{2}\over m^{2}\,c^{2}}}-1\right)=m\,c^{2}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}{\cal G}(\hat{p}^{2l})\,,~~

(2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over 2m}\hat{p}^{2}-{1\over 8m^{3}\,c^{2}}\hat{p}^{4}+{1\over 16m^{5}\,c^{4}}\hat{p}^{6}+...\,,

where
{\cal G}
is a function of the squared momentum operator
\hat{p}^{2}
. Clearly in the ultra relativistic limit
\left<\hat{p}^{2}\right>\gg m^{2}\,c^{2}
, the expansion would be of a different form.

In this work we focus on the regime where corrections beyond the standard non-relativistic approach are relevant, considering a quantum system in a one-dimensional (1D) configuration, with the continuous-variable 1D momentum operator
\hat{p}
given by the derivative operator
-i\hbar\,{d/dx}
. For an eigen-state of the total Hamiltonian
|\Psi_{n}(x)\rangle
with non-negative quantum number
n
, the expectation value of the relativistic kinetic energy is given by

\displaystyle{\cal E}^{K}_{n}

\displaystyle=\langle\Psi_{n}(x)|\hat{H}^{K}_{re}(x)|\Psi_{n}(x)\rangle,

(3)

\displaystyle=-mc^{2}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{l}\,\left(\lambdabar_{m}\right)^{2l}\,\Big<\Psi_{n}(x)\Big|{d^{\,2l}\over dx^{2l}}\Big|\Psi_{n}(x)\Big>\right).~~~

The coefficients
\alpha_{l}
and the reduced Compton wavelength
\lambdabar_{m}
originate from the Klein-Gordon equation
KG
,

\displaystyle\alpha_{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{\cal C}^{2l}_{l}}{(2l-1)\,4^{l}}

(4)

\displaystyle{\lambdabar_{m}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\hbar\over mc}\,,

(5)

with
{\cal C}^{2l}_{l}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}2l\\
l\\
\end{array}\right)=(2l)!/(l!)^{2}
.
Since the potential energy is expressed by the expectation value of the 1D potential operator
{\cal E}^{V}_{n}=\langle\Psi_{n}(x)|\hat{V}(x)|\Psi_{n}(x)\rangle
,
the total energy becomes

\displaystyle{\cal E}^{tot}_{n}={\cal E}^{K}_{n}+{\cal E}^{V}_{n}\,.

(6)

For example, the ground-state energy (lowest energy) is given by
{\cal E}^{tot}_{0}={\cal E}^{K}_{0}+{\cal E}^{V}_{0}
for the case
n=0
.

To estimate the total energy, we could utilise either first or second quantisation methods, which offer two fundamentally different frameworks for quantum systems. The second quantisation method is used widely for conventional simulations of quantum systems
KG
, supporting much of the recent progress with quantum simulations for quantum chemistry and/or many-body physics
Many-body
;
Chemistry
. Alternatively, the first quantisation method often provides a more intuitive understanding, with benefits for complex quantum systems with non-linearities (e.g., quantum simulation for Bose-Einstein condensates)
Simon2023
;
PRX2021
;
JJ2020
.

Our approach is a first quantised method, designed to estimate ground-state energies. We assume that a discretised quantum state in position space is represented by
|\psi(x)\rangle=\sum_{j=0}^{2^{L}-1}c_{j}|j\rangle
, with
L
qubits providing
2^{L}
grid points spanning the dimensionless position range
0\leq x<1
to describe a 1D spatial wavefunction
JJ2020
;
JJ2021
;
JJ2023
. This implies that the coefficient
c_{j}
of the position state
|j\rangle
represents the probability amplitude at discrete position
{x_{j}}={j/2^{L}}
, with normalisation
\sum_{j=0}^{2^{L}-1}|c_{j}|^{2}=1
. We note that the coordinate
x
can be considered as a scaled 1D space, generated from the actual original domain
0\leq x^{\prime}<R
for a maximum distance
R
, with the scaled coordinate
x
given in the domain of
0\leq x=x^{\prime}/|R|<1
with dimensionless absolute value
|R|
.

For quantum simulations with a first quantised approach, we define the translation operator
\hat{A}_{x}
, known as the quantum adder
Adder
. This shifts the position state such that
\hat{A}_{x}|j\rangle=|j+\delta x\rangle

JJ2020
;
JJ2021
;
JJ2023
, and so all the coefficients for the
L
-qubit ansatz state
|\psi(x)\rangle
shift to the corresponding next grid point. As the grid separation is
\delta x={1/2^{L}}
, the action is such that
\hat{A}_{x}|\psi(x)\rangle=|\psi(x+\delta x)\rangle
. We use
\hat{A}_{x}
and its conjugate transposed operator
\hat{A}_{x}^{{\dagger}}
, also known as the quantum subtractor. Note that we do not use the momentum operator itself with the translation operator, but the squared momentum operator
\hat{p}^{2}=-\hbar^{2}{d^{2}/dx^{2}}
can be approximated by the discretised expression for the second-order derivative operator in the finite difference method, up to the first-order precision, so that

\displaystyle{\hat{p}^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{-(mc\,{\cal L}_{m})^{2}}\left(\hat{\cal A}^{(1)}-2\hat{\openone}\right)\;.

(7)

Here, the dimensionless parameter is
{\cal L}_{m}={\lambdabar_{m}/\delta x}
and we define
\hat{\cal A}^{(l)}=\left(\hat{A}_{x}\right)^{l}+\left(\hat{A}^{{\dagger}}_{x}\right)^{l}
generally, for any positive integer
l
(
\hat{\openone}
: identity operator). Note that this approximation in Eq. (
7
) does not diverge as
\delta x\rightarrow 0
through an increase in qubit number
L
, because of
\hat{\cal A}^{(1)}\rightarrow 2\hat{\openone}
asymptotically as
\delta x\rightarrow 0
.

Furthermore, we should also point out that the parameters (
m
,
c
,
\hbar
and
\delta x
) have to be selected with care to ensure that the perturbation criterion
\left<\hat{p}^{2}\right>\ll m^{2}\,c^{2}
is satisfied in any actual quantum simulation. For example, if
\hbar=m=c=1
and
\lambdabar_{m}=1
were to be selected, the relativistic effects would not be correctly treated in Eqs. (
2
) and (
3
). Here we utilise the translation operators
{\hat{A}_{x}}
and
{\hat{A}^{{\dagger}}_{x}}
as unitary operators on quantum circuits, with the perspective of application to conventional quantum circuit platforms. However, in specific physical systems the operators could be implemented as non-unitary operators, if necessary for simulation with these systems
JJ2016
.

II
Relativistic kinetic energy

II.1
Periodic boundary condition (PBC)

We first consider the relativistic kinetic energy with periodic boundary conditions (PBC), which implies
|\psi(0)\rangle=|\psi(1)\rangle
for
0\leq x<1
in the
x
coordinate system. It is assumed that the translation operators
\hat{A}_{x}
and
\hat{A}^{{\dagger}}_{x}
preserve the shape of the shifted wavefunction and its normalisation (i.e.,
\hat{A}_{x}|2^{L}-1\rangle=|0\rangle
and
\hat{A}^{{\dagger}}_{x}|0\rangle=|2^{L}-1\rangle
), to maintain the property of unitary operators
\hat{A}^{{\dagger}}_{x}\hat{A}_{x}=\hat{A}_{x}\hat{A}^{{\dagger}}_{x}=\hat{\openone}
. For PBC, the expectation values of even powers of momentum are given by

\displaystyle\left<\hat{p}^{2l}\right>_{P}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{l}\,\hbar^{2l}\left<{d^{\,2l}\over dx^{2l}}\right>_{P},

(8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(-(mc\,{\cal L}_{m})^{2}\right)^{l}\left<\,\left(\hat{\cal A}^{(1)}-2\hat{\openone}\right)^{l}\,\right>\,.

For example, the first two terms with
l=1,2
(up to the fourth-order momentum operator) are given by combinations of
\left<\hat{\cal A}^{(l)}\right>
for
l=1,2
, such that

\displaystyle\left<\hat{p}^{2}\right>_{P}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{-(mc\,{\cal L}_{m})^{2}}\left(\left<\hat{\cal A}^{(1)}\right>-2\right),

(9)

\displaystyle\left<\hat{p}^{4}\right>_{P}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{(mc\,{\cal L}_{m})^{4}}\left(\left<\hat{\cal A}^{(2)}\right>-4\,\left<\hat{\cal A}^{(1)}\right>+6\right).~~~~~

(10)

Note that we need to compute only
\left<\hat{\cal A}^{(1)}\right>
for the non-relativistic case, but also require
\left<\hat{\cal A}^{(2)}\right>
for the first-order relativistic effect.
Thus, we find that the generalised form of the relativistic kinetic energy with PBC is decomposed as combinations of the
\left<\hat{\cal A}^{(l)}\right>
, such that

\displaystyle\left<\hat{H}^{K}_{re}\right>_{P}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta_{0}+\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\beta_{l}\,\left<\hat{\cal A}^{(l)}\right>\,,

(11)

where the coefficients
\beta_{l}
are given by

\displaystyle\beta_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2mc^{2}\left(\alpha_{1}\,{\cal L}_{m}^{2}-3\alpha_{2}\,{\cal L}_{m}^{4}+...\right),

(12)

\displaystyle\beta_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle mc^{2}\left(-\alpha_{1}\,{\cal L}_{m}^{2}+4\alpha_{2}\,{\cal L}_{m}^{4}+...\right),~~~

(13)

\displaystyle\beta_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle mc^{2}\left(-\alpha_{2}\,{\cal L}_{m}^{4}+...\right)\,.

(14)

We stress that the coefficients
\beta_{l}
can be calculated beforehand, with appropriately chosen parameters of
m
,
c
,
\hbar
, and
\delta x
, for use in quantum simulations. Therefore, to estimate the relativistic kinetic energy terms up to the
l
-th order perturbation with PBC, we compute the expectation values up to
\left<\hat{\cal A}^{(l)}\right>
, to generate the higher-order squared momentum values up to
\left<\hat{p}^{2l}\right>_{P}
.

II.2
Dirichlet boundary condition (DBC)

Dirichlet boundary conditions (DBC) allow more relaxed boundary conditions, as
|\psi(0)\rangle\neq|\psi(1)\rangle
. A few recent works have studied the application of DBC for quantum computing approaches
WES
;
Dieter
. In contrast to the PBC form of Eq. (
8
), for DBC the powers of the squared momentum take the form

\displaystyle\left<\hat{p}^{2l}\right>_{D}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(-(mc\,{\cal L}_{m})^{2}\right)^{l}\left<\left(\hat{\cal A}^{(1)}-2\hat{\openone}-\hat{E}_{0}\right)^{l}\right>,~~~

(15)

where a new operator is required, defined by

\displaystyle\hat{E}_{0}=|2^{L}-1\rangle\langle 0|+|0\rangle\langle 2^{L}-1|\,.

(16)

For
l=1
in Eq. (
15
), the expectation value of the squared momentum relates to that for PBC of Eq. (
9
) through

\displaystyle\left<\hat{p}^{2}\right>_{D}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left<\hat{p}^{2}\right>_{P}+(mc\,{\cal L}_{m})^{2}\left<\hat{E}_{0}\right>,~~~

(17)

with the expectation value of
\hat{E}_{0}
given by

\displaystyle\left<\hat{E}_{0}\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\psi(x)|\hat{E}_{0}|\psi(x)\rangle=c^{*}_{2^{L}-1}\,c_{0}+c^{*}_{0}\,c_{2^{L}-1}\,.

(18)

Therefore, the non-relativistic kinetic energy for DBC comprises the non-relativistic kinetic energy term for PBC
\left<\hat{H}^{K}_{nr}\right>_{P}
plus the additional term
\left<\hat{E}_{0}\right>
, such that

\displaystyle\left<\hat{H}^{K}_{nr}\right>_{D}=\left<\hat{H}^{K}_{nr}\right>_{P}+{1\over 2}m\,c^{2}({\cal L}_{m})^{2}\left<\hat{E}_{0}\right>\,.

(19)

This implies that the data, from a non-relativistic kinetic energy example with the same ansatz state for PBC, can be reused to estimate the non-relativistic kinetic energy for DBC.

Moreover, the first-order relativistic effect in Eq. (
2
) is taken into account with
\left<\hat{p}^{4}\right>
for DBC, such that

\displaystyle\left<\hat{p}^{4}\right>_{D}={(mc\,{\cal L}_{m})^{4}}\left<\left(\hat{\cal A}^{(1)}-2\openone-\hat{E}_{0}\right)^{2}\right>\,,

(20)

\displaystyle~~~=\left<\hat{p}^{4}\right>_{P}+{(mc\,{\cal L}_{m})^{4}}\Big(4\left<\hat{E}_{0}\right>-\sum_{k=1}^{2}\left<\hat{E}_{i}\right>+\left<\hat{E}^{2}_{0}\right>\Big),

for
\hat{E}_{1}\equiv\hat{A}_{x}\hat{E}_{0}+\hat{E}_{0}\hat{A}_{x}^{{\dagger}}
and
\hat{E}_{2}\equiv\hat{E}_{0}\hat{A}_{x}+\hat{A}_{x}^{{\dagger}}\hat{E}_{0}
.

Since
|\psi(x)\rangle=\sum_{j=0}^{2^{L}-1}c_{j}|j\rangle
in the domain (
0\leq x<1
) and

\displaystyle\hat{E}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|1\rangle\langle 2^{L}-1|+|2^{L}-1\rangle\langle 1|\,,

(21)

\displaystyle\hat{E}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|0\rangle\langle 2^{L}-2|+|2^{L}-2\rangle\langle 0|\,,

(22)

the expectation values of
\hat{E}_{1}
,
\hat{E}_{2}
and
\hat{E}_{0}^{2}
are given by

\displaystyle\left<\hat{E}_{1}\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c^{*}_{1}\,c_{2^{L}-1}+c^{*}_{2^{L}-1}\,c_{1}\,,

(23)

\displaystyle\left<\hat{E}_{2}\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c^{*}_{0}\,c_{2^{L}-2}+c^{*}_{2^{L}-2}\,c_{0}\,,

(24)

\displaystyle\left<\hat{E}_{0}^{2}\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|c_{0}|^{2}+|c_{2^{L}-1}|^{2}={\cal P}_{0}+{\cal P}_{2^{L}-1}\,,~~~~~~

(25)

for the probabilities
{\cal P}_{j}
of position state
|j\rangle
in
|\psi(x)\rangle
.

Therefore, the relativistic kinetic energy with the first-order perturbation for DBC
\left<\hat{H}^{K}_{re}\right>_{D}
is computable from a combination of the relativistic kinetic energy term for PBC
\left<\hat{H}^{K}_{re}\right>_{P}
in Eq. (
11
) and additional expectation values
\left<\hat{F}_{d}\right>
, with specified coefficients
\gamma_{d}
, such that

\displaystyle\left<\hat{H}^{K}_{re}\right>_{D}=\left<\hat{H}^{K}_{re}\right>_{P}+\sum_{d=0}^{3}\gamma_{d}\left<\hat{F}_{d}\right>\,.

(26)

Here,
\hat{F}_{d}=\{\hat{E}_{0},\,\hat{E}_{1},\,\hat{E}_{2},\,\hat{E}^{2}_{0}\}

and the specified coefficients are
\gamma_{d}={mc^{2}}\{\alpha_{1}{\cal L}_{m}^{2}(1-{\cal L}_{m}^{2}),\,\alpha_{2}{\cal L}_{m}^{4},\,\alpha_{2}{\cal L}_{m}^{4},\,-\alpha_{2}{\cal L}_{m}^{4}\}
for
d=0,1,2,3
.

IV
Conclusion and remarks

In conclusion, this work presents a novel method for relativistic quantum simulation, using perturbation theory in a first quantisation framework. The method can estimate relativistic ground-state energies under both periodic and Dirichlet boundary conditions. By discretising the 1D wavefunction over a finite grid, the approach enables approximation of relativistic kinetic energies through perturbative expansions, incorporating higher-order momentum corrections. The quantum simulation circuits include ansatz states and controlled-SWAP or controlled-translation gates, to evaluate kinetic and potential energies in a relativistic quantum system.

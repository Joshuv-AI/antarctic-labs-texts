Our main result concerns the supercuspidal
L
-packet of size

For this
L
-packet, we show how the norm

groups

\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F)

The local field
F
is a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. We shall assume that
p>2
.
Let

denote the cardinality of the residue field of
F
. Let
\mathrm{v}(x)
denote the valuation of
x\in F^{\times}
. Let
\mathfrak{o}_{F}
denote the ring of integers of
F
, let
\varpi
be a uniformiser for
F
, let
\mathfrak{p}_{F}=\varpi\mathfrak{o}_{F}
be the maximal ideal of
\mathfrak{o}_{F}
, and let
\varepsilon
be a fixed nonsquare element in
\mathfrak{o}_{F}^{\times}
. In that case,
\{1,\varpi,\varepsilon,\varepsilon\varpi\}
is a set of representatives of
F^{\times}/(F^{\times})^{2}
.

Let
G=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F)
, and let
\mathrm{Z}(G)
denote the center of
G
. We shall focus on the elliptic torus

(1.1)

T^{\varepsilon}:=\left\{\left(\begin{matrix}a&b\cr b\varepsilon&a\end{matrix}\right)\;:\;a,b\in\mathfrak{o}_{F},a^{2}-\varepsilon b^{2}=1\right\}.

This elliptic torus
T^{\varepsilon}
is a representative of the single stable conjugacy class of unramified elliptic maximal
F
-tori in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F)
.

We will define
f
as follows:

(1.2)

f\colon T^{\varepsilon}\to\mathbb{Z},\quad\quad\left(\begin{matrix}a&b\cr b\varepsilon&a\end{matrix}\right)\mapsto(-q)^{\mathrm{v}(b)}.

We denote by
T^{\varepsilon}_{1}
the pro-unipotent radical of
T^{\varepsilon}
, namely

(1.3)

T^{\varepsilon}_{1}:=\left\{\left(\begin{matrix}a&b\cr b\varepsilon&a\end{matrix}\right)\in T^{\varepsilon}\;:\;a\in 1+\mathfrak{p}_{F},\;b\in\mathfrak{p}_{F}\;\right\}.

We will say, following
[
ADSS
]
, that
\gamma
is
near the identity
if
\gamma\in T_{1}^{\varepsilon}
,
and
far from the identity
otherwise.

We have the unipotent subgroup

U(F)=\left\{\left(\begin{matrix}1&x\cr 0&1\end{matrix}\right):x\in F\right\}\subset\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F)

A Whittaker datum
\mathfrak{w}
for
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F)
is a pair
(U,\theta)
where
\theta:U(F)\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is a non-trivial character.
An irreducible smooth representation
(\pi,V)
of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F)
is called
\mathfrak{w}
-generic if the restriction of
\pi
to
U(F)
contains
\theta
.
In this article, we choose and fix a Whittaker datum
\mathfrak{w}
for
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F)
.

If
G=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F)
then the Langlands dual group
\widehat{G}
is
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and the
L
-group
{}^{L}G
is
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\rtimes\mathbf{W}_{F}
.
In the context of this article,
\phi
will denote a Langlands parameter

\phi:\mathbf{W}_{F}\to{}^{L}G.

Let
S_{\phi}
denote the centralizer in
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
of the image of
\phi
, and
\mathrm{Irr}(S_{\phi})
the set of isomorphism classes of irreducible representations of
S_{\phi}
. We have a bijective map

(1.4)

\iota_{\phi}\colon\Pi_{\phi}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F))\to\mathrm{Irr}(S_{\phi})

that sends the
\mathfrak{w}
-generic representation of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F)
to the trivial character of
S_{\phi}
.

Given
s\in S_{\phi}
, we attach to
(\phi,s)
the virtual character

(1.5)

\Theta_{\phi,s}:=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_{\phi}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F))}\mathrm{trace}(\iota_{\phi}(\pi))(s)\cdot\Theta_{\pi}.

Let
\widehat{H}
denote the identity component of the centralizer of
s
in
\widehat{G}
. The
extended endoscopic triple
, as defined in
[
T
, §6.2]
, is

\mathfrak{e}(s):=(s,H,{}^{L}\eta).

Then
\phi
will factorize as follows:

\begin{CD}\mathbf{W}_{F}@>{\phi^{H}}>{}>{}^{L}H@>{{}^{L}\eta}>{}>{}^{L}G.\end{CD}

The map
\phi^{H}
will be called the
little parameter
associated to
\phi
. This leads to the definition of the
stable character
.
The stable character
\mathscr{S}\Theta_{\phi^{H}}
is defined as

\mathscr{S}\Theta_{\phi^{H}}:=\Theta_{\phi^{H},1}.

Note that
H
is an elliptic torus and the right-hand-side collapses to a single character of
H
.

Then the endoscopic character identity for
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
is

(1.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{\delta\in H(F)}\Delta[\mathfrak{w},\mathfrak{e}(s)](\delta,\gamma)\mathscr{S}\Theta_{\phi^{H}}(\delta)=\Theta_{\phi,s}(\gamma)

where
\Delta[\mathfrak{w},\mathfrak{e}(s)]
is the Langlands-Shelstad transfer factor.
In the context of quasi-split connected reductive groups, our reference for the endoscopic character identity is equation (6.4) in the article by Taibi
[
T
]
.
From a purely notational point of view, we prefer to switch
\gamma
and
\delta
in Taibi’s equation (6.4).

The explicit endoscopic character identities, for the depth-zero supercuspidal
L
-packets of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}

and

The characters of the discrete series for

]

with

. A character

2.
Elliptic tori

2.0.1.
Elliptic tori

There are two tori relevant for us:
Let
\varepsilon
be a fixed nonsquare element in
\mathfrak{o}_{F}^{\times}
, then

(2.1)

T^{\varepsilon}:=\left\{\left(\begin{matrix}a&b\cr b\varepsilon&a\end{matrix}\right)\;:\;a,b\in\mathfrak{o}_{F},a^{2}-\varepsilon b^{2}=1\right\}

and

(2.2)

T^{\varepsilon,\varpi}:=\left\{\left(\begin{matrix}a&b\varpi^{-1}\cr b\varepsilon\varpi&a\end{matrix}\right)\;:\;a,b\in\mathfrak{o}_{F},a^{2}-\varepsilon b^{2}=1\right\}.

These two tori admit the same splitting field, namely
F(\sqrt{\varepsilon})
and are therefore stably conjugate, or, equivalently,

\mathrm{GL}_{2}(F)
-conjugate, thanks to the following identity in
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(F)
:

(2.3)

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&\varpi\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
\varepsilon b&a\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&\varpi^{-1}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&\varpi^{-1}b\\
\varepsilon\varpi b&a\end{array}\right).

There is a single stable conjugacy class of unramified elliptic maximal
F
-tori, represented by
T^{\varepsilon}
. It splits into two
distinct
G
-conjugacy classes, represented by
T^{\varepsilon}
and
T^{\varepsilon,\varpi}
.

Let
E^{1}
denote the group of norm
1
elements in
E
. We have, following
[
ADSS
, §3.1]
, canonical isomorphisms of
F
-tori:

T^{\varepsilon}\to E^{1},\quad\left(\begin{matrix}a&b\cr b\varepsilon&a\end{matrix}\right)\mapsto a+b\sqrt{\varepsilon}

T^{\varepsilon,\varpi}\to E^{1},\quad\left(\begin{matrix}a&b\varpi^{-1}\cr b\varepsilon\varpi&a\end{matrix}\right)\mapsto a+b\sqrt{\varepsilon}.

Given
a+b\sqrt{\varepsilon}\in E^{1}
we shall, following
[
ADSS
, §3.1]
write

\mathrm{Im}_{\varepsilon}(a+b\sqrt{\varepsilon})=b.

We have

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{g}\cdot\gamma)=\mathrm{Im}_{\varepsilon}(\gamma),

for every
\gamma\in T^{\varepsilon}
, where

(2.5)

\mathbf{g}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&\varpi\end{array}\right)\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(F)

and
\mathbf{g}\cdot\gamma:=\mathbf{g}\gamma\mathbf{g}^{-1}
.
The equation (
2.3
) can be rephrased as the following statement:

(2.6)

\mathbf{g}\cdot T^{\varepsilon}=\mathbf{g}T^{\varepsilon}\mathbf{g}^{-1}=T^{\varepsilon,\varpi}.

We also note that

\mathrm{sgn}_{\varepsilon}(x)=(-1)^{\mathrm{v}(x)}

for all
x\in F
, see (2.1) in
[
ADSS
]
. Then we have, using (
2.4
),

(2.7)

\displaystyle\mathrm{sgn}_{\varepsilon}(\varpi^{-1}\mathrm{Im}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{g}\cdot\gamma))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{sgn}_{\varepsilon}(\varpi^{-1}\mathrm{Im}_{\varepsilon}(\gamma))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{sgn}_{\varepsilon}(\varpi^{-1})\mathrm{sgn}_{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{Im}_{\varepsilon}(\gamma))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{\mathrm{v}(\varpi^{-1})}\mathrm{sgn}_{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{Im}_{\varepsilon}(\gamma))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathrm{sgn}_{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{Im}_{\varepsilon}(\gamma)).

2.0.2.
Weyl discriminant

In
[
ADSS
, Definition 3.7]
, the Weyl discriminant
D_{G}
is defined as follows:

D_{G}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\end{array}\right)=(a+d)^{2}-4.

Now

\gamma=\left(\begin{matrix}a&b\cr b\varepsilon&a\end{matrix}\right)\in T^{\varepsilon}\implies D_{G}(\gamma)=4a^{2}-4=4\varepsilon b^{2}

so that

|D_{G}(\gamma)|^{1/2}=|b|.

2.0.3.
The function
f

We set

(2.8)

f(\gamma):=\frac{\mathrm{sgn}_{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{Im}_{\varepsilon}(\gamma))}{|D_{G}(\gamma)|^{1/2}}.

Note that

\gamma\quad\textrm{is regular}\iff b\neq 0.

In that case, we have

\displaystyle f(\gamma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{sgn}_{\varepsilon}(b)}{|b|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{\mathrm{v}(b)}q^{\mathrm{v}(b)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-q)^{\mathrm{v}(b)}.

It is worth noting that
f
is invariant under the Galois group
\mathrm{Gal}(F(\sqrt{\varepsilon})/F)
. If
c=a+b\sqrt{\varepsilon}
then we have

(2.9)

f(\overline{c})=f(a-b\sqrt{\varepsilon})=(-q)^{\mathrm{v}(-b)}=(-q)^{\mathrm{v}(b)}=f(c).

The following Lemma is proved in
[
ADSS
, p.65]
. We offer a short proof.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\gamma
be a regular semisimple element
in the elliptic torus
T^{\varepsilon}
which is far from the identity. Then we have
f(\gamma)=1
.

Proof.

We recall the pro-unipotent radical
T^{\varepsilon}_{1}
of
T^{\varepsilon}
, see (
1.3
). If
\gamma
is far from the identity, then we have

\gamma\in T^{\varepsilon}\setminus T^{\varepsilon}_{1}
and so
b\in\mathfrak{o}_{F}\setminus\mathfrak{p}_{F}
. Therefore
\mathrm{v}(b)=0
.

2
by the Proposition in
[
BH
, 34.3]
. Therefore we have
\Delta_{\xi}=\kappa_{E/F}
and twisting by
\Delta_{\xi}
has no effect on
\rho_{\xi}
by the Lemma in
[
BH
, 34.1]
. So the Langlands correspondence is given by

\rho_{\xi}\mapsto\pi_{\xi}.

Now we refer to the definition of
\pi^{+}
and
\pi^{-}
in (
3.1
).
∎

Let
\rho_{0}
denote the trivial character of
S_{\phi}
and let
\rho_{1}
denote the non-trivial character of
S_{\phi}
.
We will assume that
\pi^{+}
is
\mathfrak{w}
-generic. Then we have a bijection

\iota\colon\Pi_{\phi}(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F))\to\mathrm{Irr}(S_{\phi}),\quad\quad\iota(\pi^{+})=\rho_{0},\quad\iota(\pi^{-})=\rho_{1}.

Let
s=s_{1}
. Let
\widehat{H}
denote the identity component of the centralizer of
s
is
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
.
Define

\mathcal{H}:=\widehat{H}\cdot\phi(W_{F})

and let
{}^{L}\eta:\mathcal{H}\to\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
denote inclusion. We recall that
E
is the unramified quadratic extension of
F
. Noting that
\phi(\mathbf{W}_{E})\subset\widehat{H}
, we have

\mathcal{H}=\widehat{H}\cdot\phi(\sigma).

This determines an action of
\Gamma_{E/F}
by conjugation on
\widehat{H}
. This creates the extended endoscopic triple

\mathfrak{e}=(s,H,{}^{L}\eta).

We note that this extended endoscopic triple is
identical
to the one constructed for the non-regular parameter. In the two cases (regular parameter, non-regular parameter) the endoscopic data are the same, but the little parameters
\phi^{H}
are different.

Theorem 8.5
.

Let
\gamma
be a regular semisimple element in
T^{\varepsilon}
. We have

\sum_{\delta\in H(F)}\Delta[\mathfrak{w},\mathfrak{e}](\delta,\gamma)\mathscr{S}\Theta_{\phi^{H}}(\delta)=-f(\gamma)(\psi(\gamma)+\psi(1/\gamma)).

Proof.

We have

\begin{CD}\phi\colon\mathbf{W}_{F}@>{\phi^{H}}>{}>\mathcal{H}@>{{}^{L}\eta}>{}>\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\end{CD}

where the little map
\phi^{H}
is the
L
-parameter of
\psi
as in
[
Ka2
, p.1148]
.
Since
\phi^{H}
is the
L
-parameter of
\psi
we have

\Theta_{\phi^{H}}=\psi
and therefore

(8.6)

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}\Theta_{\phi^{H}}=\psi.

The transfer factor is given by the formula (
7
), namely

(8.7)

\Delta[\mathfrak{w},\mathfrak{e}](\gamma,\gamma)=\Delta[\mathfrak{w},\mathfrak{e}](1/\gamma,\gamma)=-f(\gamma).

Finally, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\delta\in H(F)}\Delta[\mathfrak{w},\mathfrak{e}](\delta,\gamma)\,\mathscr{S}\Theta_{\phi^{H}}(\delta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\delta\in H(F)}\Delta[\mathfrak{w},\mathfrak{e}](\delta,\gamma)\psi(\delta)\quad\textrm{by}\quad(\ref{SS})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta[\mathfrak{w},\mathfrak{e}](\gamma,\gamma)\psi(\gamma)+\Delta[\mathfrak{w},\mathfrak{e}](1/\gamma,\gamma))\psi(1/\gamma)\quad\textrm{by}\quad(\ref{RP})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-f(\gamma)(\psi(\gamma^{H})+\psi(1/\gamma^{H}))\quad\textrm{by}\quad(\ref{DeltaFormula2})

9.
Stability across the inner forms of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}

Let
\phi
denote the non-regular parameter of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
.
The finite group
S_{\phi}
admits a pull-back from the adjoint group
\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
to its simply-connected
cover
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. The pull-back group
\widetilde{S}_{\phi}
is the quaternion group
Q
, which admits four one-dimensional representations

\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\rho_{3},\rho_{4}
and one two-dimensional representation
\rho_{5}
. We have a bijective map

\mathrm{Irr}(\widetilde{S}_{\phi})\simeq\{\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi_{3},\pi_{4},\pi_{5}\}.

The
L
-packet
\Pi_{\phi}
is therefore part of a
compound packet
, comprising five representations,
where
\pi_{5}
is a supercuspidal representation of the inner form
\mathrm{SL}_{1}(D)
. Let
\Theta_{5}
denote the Harish-Chandra character of
\pi_{5}
.

Theorem 9.1
.

The character of the supercuspidal representation
\pi_{5}
is given by

\Theta_{5}(\delta)=\psi_{0}(\gamma)

with
\delta\in\mathrm{SL}_{1}(D)
and
\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F)
whenever
\gamma,\delta
are related.

Proof.

We will consider
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(F)
. Now
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(F)
admits one inner twist, namely
\mathrm{GL}_{1}(D)
where the division algebra
D
has the Brauer invariant

\mathrm{inv}_{F}(D)=1/2\in\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}.

Let
\pi_{0}
be the depth-zero supercuspidal representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(F)
for which

\pi_{0}|_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F)}=\pi_{1}\oplus\pi_{2}\oplus\pi_{3}\oplus\pi_{4}.

Let
\mathrm{JL}
denote the Jacquet-Langlands correspondence and let

\pi:=\mathrm{JL}^{-1}\pi_{0}
. The local Langlands correspondence for
\mathrm{GL}_{1}(D)
is determined by the composition

\begin{CD}\mathrm{Irr}^{2}(\mathrm{GL}_{1}(D))@>{\mathrm{JL}}>{}>\mathrm{Irr}^{2}(\mathrm{GL}_{2}(F))@>{\mathrm{{LLC}}}>{}>\Phi(\mathrm{GL}_{2}).\end{CD}

The two elements in the set

\{\pi,\mathrm{JL}(\pi)\}

therefore admit the same
L
-parameter
\phi
, and comprise a
compound packet
. The corresponding
character identity is

(9.2)

\displaystyle\Theta_{\pi}(\delta)=-\Theta_{\mathrm{JL}(\pi)}(\gamma)

with
\delta\in\mathrm{GL}_{1}(D)
and
\gamma\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(F)
whenever
\gamma,\delta
are related. This character identity remains true when we restrict to
\mathrm{SL}_{1}(D)
and
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F)
respectively.

Let
Q
denote the classical quaternion group. According to
[
HS
, Lemma 12.6]
, we have, as a representation space for
Q\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F)
,

V_{\pi(\phi)}=\bigoplus_{1\leq j\leq 4}\rho_{j}\otimes\pi_{j}.

As a representation space for
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F)
, we therefore have

V_{\pi(\phi)}=\pi_{1}\oplus\pi_{2}\oplus\pi_{3}\oplus\pi_{4}.

According to
[
HS
, Lemma 12.6]
, we have, as a representation space for
Q\times\mathrm{SL}_{1}(D)
,

V_{\pi(\phi)}=\rho_{5}\otimes\pi_{5}.

As a representation space for
\mathrm{SL}_{1}(D)
, we therefore have

\displaystyle V_{\pi(\phi)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi_{5}\oplus\pi_{5}.

The character identity now follows from (
9.2
) and we obtain

(9.3)

\displaystyle 2\Theta_{5}(\delta)=-(\Theta_{1}+\Theta_{2}+\Theta_{3}+\Theta_{4})(\gamma)

whenever
\gamma,\delta
are related with
\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F)_{\mathrm{reg}},\delta\in\mathrm{SL}_{1}(D)_{\mathrm{reg}}
.

We also have

(9.4)

\displaystyle\Theta_{1}+\Theta_{2}+\Theta_{3}+\Theta_{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\psi_{0}.

Far from the identity, this follows from Theorem
4.10
; near the identity, this follows from Theorem
5.1
since

\psi_{0}
has depth zero.

By comparing (
9.3
) with (
9.4
) we conclude that the character of the supercuspidal representation
\pi_{5}
is given by

\Theta_{5}(\delta)=\psi_{0}(\gamma)

with
\delta\in\mathrm{SL}_{1}(D)
and
\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F)
whenever
\gamma,\delta
are related.
∎

The following corollary is a special case of Theorem 4.4.4(1) in
[
FKS
]
. We give an elementary proof.

Corollary 9.5
.

The virtual character
\mathscr{S}\Theta_{\phi,*}
is stable across the two inner forms of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
:

\mathscr{S}\Theta_{\phi,0}(\gamma)=\mathscr{S}\Theta_{\phi,1}(\delta)

for all stably conjugate strongly regular elements
\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F),\delta\in\mathrm{SL}_{1}(D)
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}\Theta_{\phi,0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e(\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F))\cdot(\Theta_{1}+\Theta_{2}+\Theta_{3}+\Theta_{4})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Theta_{1}+\Theta_{2}+\Theta_{3}+\Theta_{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\Theta_{5}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e(\mathrm{SL}_{1}(D))\cdot 2\Theta_{5}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}\Theta_{\phi,1}

as required.
∎

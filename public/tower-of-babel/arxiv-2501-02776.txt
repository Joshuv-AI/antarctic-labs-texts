Conditioning to avoid bounded sets for a one-dimensional Lévy processes


Abstract
For several classes of bounded sets A A , the limit of a one-dimensional Lévy process conditioned to avoid A A up to a parametrized random time which tends to infinity. For A A we take the set of finite points with several clocks and a bounded F σ F_{\sigma} -set with exponential clock. We also take an integer lattice with exponential clock.

Introduction

A conditioning problem is to study the long-time limit of the form

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{x}(\Lambda|\ T_{A}>\tau)\qquad\text{for}\ \Lambda\in\mathscr{F}_{t},

(1.1)

where
((X_{t})_{t\geq 0},(\mathscr{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},(\mathbb{P}_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{R}})
is a Markov process,
T_{A}
is the first hitting time of a set
A
, and
\tau
is a net of parametrized random times tending to infinity, called a
clock
. In particular, we call this problem
conditioning to avoid the set
A
or
conditioning to stay the set
A^{c}
. Here, for the random clock
\tau
, we adopt one of the following:

(C)

Constant clock:
\tau=t
as
t\to\infty.

(Ex)

Exponential clock:
\tau=(\bm{e}_{q})
as
q\to 0+
, where
\bm{e}_{q}
has the exponential distribution with parameter
q>0
and is independent of
(X_{t})
.

(OH)

One-point hitting time clock:
\tau=(T_{c})
as
c\to\pm\infty
, where
T_{c}
is the first hitting time at
c
.

(TH)

Two-point hitting time clock:
\tau=(T_{c}\wedge T_{-d})
as
c,d\to\infty
and
\frac{d-c}{c+d}\to\gamma\in[-1,1]
, which denote
(c,d)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\gamma)}}{{\to}}\infty
.

(IL)

Inverse local time clock:
\tau=(\eta_{u}^{c})
as
c\to\pm\infty
, where
(\eta_{u}^{c})_{u\geq 0}
is the inverse local time.

To solve this problem, we want to find a non-negative harmonic function
h_{A}
for the process killed upon reaching
A
, and a function
\rho(\tau)
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\rho(\tau)\mathbb{P}_{X_{t}}(T_{A}>\tau)=h_{A}(X_{t})\ \text{a.s. and in}\ L^{1}(\mathbb{P}_{x})

(1.2)

holds (see, Section
3
for the details). Then, we can express the limit (
1.1
) via Doob’s
h
-transform with respect to
h_{A}
:

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{x}(\Lambda|\ T_{A}>\tau)=\mathbb{P}_{x}\left[1_{A}\cdot\frac{h_{A}(X_{t})}{h_{A}(x)}1_{\{T_{A}>t\}}\right],

(1.3)

for
x\in\{x\in\mathbb{R};\ h_{A}(x)>0\}
.

2
Preliminaries

Let
((X_{t})_{t\geq 0},(\mathbb{P}_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{R}})
be the canonical representation of a one-dimensional Lévy process with
X_{0}=x
,
\mathbb{P}_{x}
-a.s. For
t\geq 0
, we denote by
\mathscr{F}_{t}^{X}:=\sigma(X_{s},\ 0\leq s\leq t)
and write
\mathscr{F}_{t}=\bigcap_{s>t}\mathscr{F}_{s}^{X}.
For a set
A\subset\mathbb{R}
, let
T_{A}
be the first hitting time of
A
for
(X_{t})
, i.e.,

\displaystyle T_{A}:=\inf\{t\geq 0;\ X_{t}\in A\}.

(2.1)

For simplicity, we denote
T_{\{a\}}
as
T_{a}
for
a\in\mathbb{R}.
For
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, we denote by
\Psi(\lambda)
the characteristic exponent of
(X_{t})
, i.e.,
\Psi(\lambda)
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{0}\left[e^{i\lambda X_{t}}\right]=e^{-t\Psi(\lambda)}\qquad\text{for}\ t\geq 0.

(2.2)

Throughout this paper, we always assume
((X_{t}),\mathbb{P}_{0})
is recurrent, and always assume the condition

\displaystyle\textbf{(A)}\ \int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{1}{q+\Psi(\lambda)}\right|d\lambda<\infty\qquad\text{for}\ q>0.

(2.3)

Then,
(X_{t})
has a bounded continuous resolvent density
r_{q}
. It is defined by

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)r_{q}(x)dx=\mathbb{P}_{0}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-qt}f(X_{t})dt\right]\qquad\text{for}\ q>0\ \text{and}\ f\geq 0

(2.4)

(see, e.g., Theorems II.16 and II.19 of
[
1
]
). It is known that the equation between the first hitting time of
a
and the resolvent density:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}\left[e^{-qT_{a}}\right]=\frac{r_{q}(a-x)}{r_{q}(0)}\qquad\text{for}\ q>0\ \text{and}\ x\in\mathbb{R}

(2.5)

(see, e.g., Corollary II.18 of
[
1
]
).

We define

\displaystyle h_{q}(x):=r_{q}(0)-r_{q}(-x)\qquad\text{for}\ q>0\ \text{and}\ x\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.6)

It is clear that
h_{q}(0)=0
, and by (
2.5
), we have
h_{q}\geq 0.
The following proposition plays a key role in our results:

Proposition 2.1

(Theorem 1.1 and Theorem 1.2 of
[
18
]
)
.

The following assertions hold:

1.

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}
, the limit
h(x):=\lim_{q\to 0+}h_{q}(x)
exists and is finite;

2.

h
is continuous and subadditive on
\mathbb{R}.

3.

It holds that

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}\frac{h(x)}{|x|}=\frac{1}{m^{2}}\in[0,\infty),

(2.7)

where
m^{2}:=\mathbb{P}_{0}[X_{1}^{2}]
is a variance of
X_{1}
.

We call
h
the
renormalized zero resolvent
. Further, we define

\displaystyle h^{(\gamma)}(x):=h(x)+\frac{\gamma}{m^{2}}x\qquad\text{for}\ -1\leq\gamma\leq 1.

(2.8)

Note that when
m^{2}=\infty
,
h^{(\gamma)}
is independent of
\gamma.

We can define a local time at
a\in\mathbb{R}
, which we denote by
(L_{t}^{a})_{t\geq 0}
. It is defined by

\displaystyle L_{t}^{a}:=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}1_{\{|X_{s}-a|<\varepsilon\}}ds.

(2.9)

It is known that
(L_{t}^{a})
is continuous in
t
and satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-qt}dL_{t}^{a}\right]=r_{q}(a-x)\qquad\text{for $q>0$ and $x\in\mathbb{R}$}

(2.10)

(see, e.g., Section V of
[
1
]
). In particular, from this expression,
r_{q}(x)
is non-decreasing as
q\to 0+.
Moreover, we know that

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to 0+}qr_{q}(0)=0

(2.11)

(see, e.g., Lemma 15.5 of
[
19
]
).

Let
(\eta_{l}^{a})_{l\geq 0}
be an inverse local time, i.e.,

\displaystyle\eta_{l}^{a}:=\inf\{t>0:\ L_{t}^{a}>l\}.

(2.12)

It is known that the process
((\eta_{l}^{a}),\mathbb{P}_{a})
is a possibly killed subordinator which has the Laplace exponent

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{a}\left[e^{-q\eta_{l}^{a}}\right]=e^{-\frac{l}{r_{q}(0)}}\qquad\text{for $l>0$ and $q>0$}

(2.13)

(see, e.g., Proposition V.4 of
[
1
]
).

3
General theorems for the conditioning problems

In this section, we prove a general theory of conditioning problems, since the discussion of the conditioning problem for each set has some common aspects.

First, we consider the case exponential clock.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
T
be a stopping time. We assume that there exists the non-trivial and non-negative function
\varphi_{e}^{T}
which satisfies the following:

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to 0+}r_{q}(0)\mathbb{P}_{X_{t}}(T>\bm{e}_{q})=\varphi_{e}^{T}(X_{t})\ a.s.\ and\ in\ L^{1}(\mathbb{P}_{x}).

(3.1)

Then,
(\varphi_{e}^{T}(X_{t})1_{\{T>t\}})_{t\geq 0}
is a non-negative
((\mathscr{F}_{t}),\mathbb{P}_{x})
-martingale, and if
\varphi_{e}^{T}(x)>0
, then it holds that

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to 0+}\mathbb{P}_{x}[F_{t}|\ \bm{e}_{q}<T]=\mathbb{P}_{x}\left[F_{t}\cdot\frac{\varphi_{e}^{T}(X_{t})}{\varphi_{e}^{T}(x)}1_{\{T>t\}}\right]

(3.2)

for all bounded
\mathscr{F}_{t}
-measurable functionals
F_{t}
.

Proof.

We define

\displaystyle N_{t}^{q}:

\displaystyle=r_{q}(0)\mathbb{P}_{x}(T>\bm{e}_{q}>t|\mathscr{F}_{t}),

(3.3)

\displaystyle M_{t}^{q}:

\displaystyle=r_{q}(0)\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\bm{e}_{q}<T|\mathscr{F}_{t}\right),

(3.4)

for
q>0.
By the lack of memory property of the exponential distribution and by the Markov property, we have

\displaystyle N_{t}^{q}

\displaystyle=r_{q}(0)1_{\{T>t\}}\mathbb{P}_{x}^{\mathscr{F}_{t}}(T>\bm{e}_{q}|\ \bm{e}_{q}>t)\mathbb{P}_{x}(\bm{e}_{q}>t)

\displaystyle=r_{q}(0)1_{\{T>t\}}\mathbb{P}_{X_{t}}(T>\bm{e}_{q})e^{-qt},

(3.5)

where
\mathbb{P}_{x}^{\mathscr{F}_{t}}
means the conditional probability with respect to
\mathscr{F}_{t}
. Therefore, we obtain by the hypothesis,

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to 0+}N_{t}^{q}=\varphi_{e}^{T}(X_{t})1_{\{T>t\}}\ \mathrm{a.s.\ and\ in}\ L^{1}(\mathbb{P}_{x}).

(3.6)

By (
2.11
), we have

\displaystyle M_{t}^{q}-N_{t}^{q}

\displaystyle=r_{q}(0)\mathbb{P}_{x}(T>\bm{e}_{q},\ t\geq\bm{e}_{q}|\mathscr{F}_{t})

\displaystyle=qr_{q}(0)\int_{0}^{t}1_{\{T>s\}}e^{-qs}ds

\displaystyle\leq qr_{q}(0)t\to 0,

(3.7)

as
q\to 0+.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to 0+}M_{t}^{q}=\varphi_{e}^{T}(X_{t})1_{\{T>t\}}\ \mathrm{a.s.\ and\ in}\ L^{1}(\mathbb{P}_{x}).

(3.8)

Since
(M_{t}^{q})_{t\geq 0}
is a non-negative martingale, its
L^{1}
-limit
(\varphi_{e}^{T}(X_{t})1_{\{T>t\}})
is also a non-negative
((\mathscr{F}_{t}),\mathbb{P}_{x})
-martingal (see, e.g., Proposition 1.3 of
[
4
]
).

Finally, we obtain by the
L^{1}
-convergence and the martingale property,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}[F_{t}|\ \bm{e}_{q}<T]

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{P}_{x}[F_{t},\ \bm{e}_{q}<T]}{\mathbb{P}_{x}(\bm{e}_{q}<T)}=\frac{\mathbb{P}_{x}[F_{t}\cdot M_{t}^{q}]}{\mathbb{P}_{x}[M_{t}^{q}]}

\displaystyle\to\frac{\mathbb{P}_{x}[F_{t}\cdot\varphi_{e}^{T}(X_{t})1_{\{T>t\}}]}{\mathbb{P}_{x}[\varphi_{e}^{T}(X_{t})1_{\{T>t\}}]}=\mathbb{P}_{x}\left[F_{t}\cdot\frac{\varphi_{e}^{T}(X_{t})}{\varphi_{e}^{T}(x)}1_{\{T>t\}}\right],

(3.9)

as
q\to 0+.

∎

The same general theorem is given below for clocks other than exponential clock, but note that there are a few more assumptions.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
T
be a stopping time. We assume that there exists the non-trivial and non-negative function
\varphi_{o}^{T}
which satisfies the following:

\displaystyle\lim_{c\to\pm\infty}h^{B}(c)\mathbb{P}_{X_{t}}(T>T_{c})=\varphi_{o}^{T}(X_{t})\ a.s.\ and\ in\ L^{1}(\mathbb{P}_{x}),

(3.10)

where
h^{B}(c):=\mathbb{P}_{0}[L_{T_{c}}^{0}]=h(c)+h(-c)
(see, Lemma 3.5 of
[
18
]
). Moreover, we also assume that
\frac{\varphi_{e}^{T}(x)}{x}
converges as
|x|\to\infty.
Then,
(\varphi_{o}^{T}(X_{t})1_{\{T>t\}})_{t\geq 0}
is a non-negative
((\mathscr{F}_{t}),\mathbb{P}_{x})
-martingale, and if
\varphi_{o}^{T}(x)>0
, then it holds that

\displaystyle\lim_{c\to\pm\infty}\mathbb{P}_{x}[F_{t}|\ T_{c}<T]=\mathbb{P}_{x}\left[F_{t}\cdot\frac{\varphi_{o}^{T}(X_{t})}{\varphi_{o}^{T}(x)}1_{\{T>t\}}\right]

(3.11)

for all bounded
\mathscr{F}_{t}
-measurable functionals
F_{t}
.

Proof.

We define

\displaystyle N_{t}^{c}:

\displaystyle=h^{B}(c)\mathbb{P}_{x}(T>T_{c}>t|\mathscr{F}_{t}),

(3.12)

\displaystyle M_{t}^{c}:

\displaystyle=h^{B}(c)\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(T_{c}<T|\mathscr{F}_{t}\right),

(3.13)

for
c\in\mathbb{R}.
By the Markov property, we have

\displaystyle N_{t}^{c}=h^{B}(c)1_{\{T>t\}}\mathbb{P}_{X_{t}}(T>T_{c}).

(3.14)

Therefore, we obtain by the hypothesis,

\displaystyle\lim_{c\to\pm\infty}N_{t}^{c}=\varphi_{o}^{T}(X_{t})1_{\{T>t\}}\ \mathrm{a.s.\ and\ in}\ L^{1}(\mathbb{P}_{x}).

(3.15)

We have

\displaystyle M_{t}^{c}-N_{t}^{c}

\displaystyle=h^{B}(c)\mathbb{P}_{x}(T>T_{c},\ t\geq T_{c}|\mathscr{F}_{t})

\displaystyle=h^{B}(c)\mathbb{P}_{x}(T>T_{c}|\mathscr{F}_{t})1_{\{t\geq T_{c}\}}\to 0\ a.s.,

(3.16)

as
c\to\pm\infty.
Since
(\varphi_{e}^{T}(X_{t})1_{\{T>t\}})
is a martingale, we have by the optional sampling theorem, by the hypothesis, and by (
2.7
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}\left[|M_{t}^{c}-N_{t}^{c}|\right]

\displaystyle=h^{B}(c)\mathbb{P}_{x}(T>T_{c},\ t\geq T_{c})

\displaystyle=\frac{h^{B}(c)}{c}\frac{c}{\varphi_{e}^{T}(c)}\mathbb{P}_{x}[\varphi_{e}^{T}(X_{T_{c}})1_{\{T>T_{c}\}},\ t\geq T_{c}]

\displaystyle=\frac{h^{B}(c)}{c}\frac{c}{\varphi_{e}^{T}(c)}\mathbb{P}_{x}[\varphi_{e}^{T}(X_{t})1_{\{T>t\}},\ t\geq T_{c}]\to 0,

(3.17)

as
c\to\pm\infty
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{c\to\pm\infty}M_{t}^{c}=\varphi_{o}^{T}(X_{t})1_{\{T>t\}}\ \mathrm{a.s.\ and\ in}\ L^{1}(\mathbb{P}_{x}).

(3.18)

The rest of the proof is the same as in Theorem
3.1
.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
T
be a stopping time. We assume that there exists the non-trivial and non-negative function
\varphi_{t}^{T}
which satisfies the following:

\displaystyle\lim_{(c,d)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\gamma)}}{{\to}}\infty}h^{C}(c,-d)\mathbb{P}_{X_{t}}(T>T_{c}\wedge T_{-d})=\varphi_{t}^{T}(X_{t})\ a.s.\ and\ in\ L^{1}(\mathbb{P}_{x}),

(3.19)

where
h^{C}(c,-d):=\mathbb{P}_{0}[L_{T_{c}\wedge T_{-d}}^{0}]
which can be expressed only by
h
(see, Lemma 6.1 of
[
18
]
). Moreover, we also assume that
\frac{\varphi_{e}^{T}(x)}{x}
converges as
|x|\to\infty.
Then,
(\varphi_{t}^{T}(X_{t})1_{\{T>t\}})_{t\geq 0}
is a non-negative
((\mathscr{F}_{t}),\mathbb{P}_{x})
-martingale, and if
\varphi_{t}^{T}(x)>0
, then it holds that

(IL)

Proof.

The cases of the exponential clock and the one-point hitting time clock are already shown in (3.25) and (4.16) of
[

Next, we prove the case of the two-point hitting time clock. We have

\displaystyle h^{C}(c,-d)\mathbb{P}_{x}(T_{a}\wedge T_{b}>T_{c}\wedge T_{-d})

\displaystyle\qquad=h^{C}(c,-d)\mathbb{P}_{x}(T_{a}\wedge T_{b}\wedge T_{-d}>T_{c})+h^{C}(c,-d)\mathbb{P}_{x}(T_{a}\wedge T_{b}\wedge T_{c}>T_{-d}).

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}(T_{a}\wedge T_{b}\wedge T_{-d}>T_{c})

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathbb{P}_{x}(T_{a}\wedge T_{b}>T_{c})-\mathbb{P}_{x}(T_{a}\wedge T_{b}>T_{c}>T_{-d})

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathbb{P}_{x}(T_{a}\wedge T_{b}>T_{c})-\mathbb{P}_{-d}(T_{a}\wedge T_{b}>T_{c})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\{\mathbb{P}_{x}(T_{a}\wedge T_{b}>T_{-d})-\mathbb{P}_{c}(T_{a}\wedge T_{b}>T_{-d})\mathbb{P}_{x}(T_{a}\wedge T_{b}\wedge T_{-d}>T_{c})\}.

(4.8)

By (4.17) and (5.11) of
[

\displaystyle h^{C}(c,-d)\mathbb{P}_{x}(T_{a}\wedge T_{b}>T_{c})

as
(c,d)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\gamma)}}{{\to}}\infty.

Since

4.2
Limit measures

We set
\mathscr{H}^{(\gamma)}_{a,b}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R};\ \varphi^{(\gamma)}_{a,b}(x)>0\},
and we define a probability measure

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x;a,b}^{(\gamma)}|_{\mathscr{F}_{t}}:=\frac{\varphi_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}(X_{t})}{\varphi_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}(x)}1_{\{T_{a}\wedge T_{b}>t\}}\cdot\mathbb{P}_{x}|_{\mathscr{F}_{t}}\qquad\text{for}\ x\in\mathscr{H}^{(\gamma)}_{a,b}.

(4.19)

The measure
\mathbb{P}_{x;a,b}^{(\gamma)}
can be well-defined on
\mathscr{F}_{\infty}:=\sigma(X_{t},\ t\geq 0)
(see, Theorem 9.1 of
[
21
]
). By the strong Markov property of
\mathbb{P}_{x}
and by the martingale property, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x;a,b}^{(\gamma)}(T_{\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathscr{H}_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}}\leq t)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{x}\left[1_{\{T_{\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathscr{H}_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}}\leq t\}}\cdot\frac{\varphi_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}(X_{t})}{\varphi_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}(x)}1_{\{T_{a}\wedge T_{b}>t\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{x}\left[1_{\{T_{\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathscr{H}_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}}\leq t\}}\mathbb{P}_{X_{T_{\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathscr{H}_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}}}}\left[\frac{\varphi_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}(X_{t-s})}{\varphi_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}(x)}1_{\{T_{a}\wedge T_{b}>t-s\}}\right]\Big|_{s=T_{\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathscr{H}_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{x}\left[1_{\{T_{\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathscr{H}_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}}\leq t\}}\frac{\varphi_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}(X_{{T}_{\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathscr{H}_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}}})}{\varphi_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}(x)}\right]=0,

(4.20)

for any
t>0
. Thus, we have
\mathbb{P}_{x;a,b}^{(\gamma)}(T_{\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathscr{H}_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}}>t)=1
for any
t>0.
In particular, we have
\mathbb{P}_{x;a,b}^{(\gamma)}(T_{a}\wedge T_{b}>t)=1
for any
t>0
. Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x;a,b}^{(\gamma)}(T_{a}=T_{b}=\infty)=1.

(4.21)

Thus, the measure
\mathbb{P}_{x;a,b}^{(\gamma)}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mathbb{P}_{x}
on
\mathscr{F}_{t}
, but is singular to
\mathbb{P}_{x}
on
\mathscr{F}_{\infty}
since
\mathbb{P}_{x}(T_{a}\wedge T_{b}<\infty)=1.

We set
\mathscr{H}_{a}^{(\gamma)}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R};\ h^{(\gamma)}(x-a)>0\},
and we define

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x;a}^{(\gamma)}|_{\mathscr{F}_{t}}:=\frac{h^{(\gamma)}(X_{t}-a)}{h^{(\gamma)}(x-a)}1_{\{T_{a}>t\}}\cdot\mathbb{P}_{x}|_{\mathscr{F}_{t}}\qquad\text{for}\ x\in\mathscr{H}^{(\gamma)}_{a}.

(4.22)

We know that
\mathscr{H}_{a}^{(\gamma)}=\mathbb{R}\setminus\{a\},\ (a,\infty)
, or
(-\infty,a)
(see, p.12 of
[
17
]
). Note that

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}\subset\mathscr{H}_{a}^{(\gamma)}\cap\mathscr{H}_{b}^{(\gamma)}.

(4.23)

Although The proof of the following proposition is parallel to Theorem 1.4 of Takeda
[
17
]
, we give the proof for completeness of this paper.

Proposition 4.3
.

For
-1\leq\gamma\leq 1
and
x\in\mathscr{H}_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}
, the process
((X_{t}),\mathbb{P}_{x;a,b}^{(\gamma)})
is transient.

Proof.

For
0<s<t
and a non-negative bounded
\mathscr{F}_{s}
-measurable functional
F_{s}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x;a,b}^{(\gamma)}\left[\frac{1}{\varphi_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}(X_{t})}\cdot F_{s}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varphi_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}(x)}\mathbb{P}_{x}[F_{s};\ T_{a}\wedge T_{b}>t]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varphi_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}(x)}\mathbb{P}_{x}[F_{s};\ T_{a}\wedge T_{b}>s]=\mathbb{P}_{x;a,b}^{(\gamma)}\left[\frac{1}{\varphi_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}(X_{s})}\cdot F_{s}\right].

(4.24)

Thus,
(\frac{1}{\varphi_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}(X_{t})})_{t\geq 0}
is a non-negative
\mathbb{P}_{x;a,b}^{(\gamma)}
-supermartingale. By the martingale convergence theorem,
\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{\varphi_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}(X_{t})}
exists
\mathbb{P}_{x;a,b}^{(\gamma)}
-a.s. By Fatou’s lemma and recurrence of
((X_{t}),\mathbb{P}_{x})
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x;a,b}^{(\gamma)}\left[\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{\varphi_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}(X_{t})}\right]\leq\varliminf_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{x;a,b}^{(\gamma)}\left[\frac{1}{\varphi_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}(X_{t})}\right]=\frac{1}{\varphi_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}}\varliminf_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{x}(T_{a}\wedge T_{b}>t)=0.

(4.25)

Thus, we have
\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{\varphi_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}(X_{t})}=0

\mathbb{P}_{x;a,b}^{(\gamma)}
-a.s., and it implies
\lim_{t\to\infty}|X_{t}|=\infty

\mathbb{P}_{x;a,b}^{(\gamma)}
-a.s. Therefore, the process
((X_{t}),\mathbb{P}_{x;a,b}^{(\gamma)})
is transient.
∎

We define the measures on
\mathscr{F}_{\infty}
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathscr{P}_{x;a}^{(\gamma)}

\displaystyle:=h^{(\gamma)}(x-a)\cdot\mathbb{P}_{x;a}^{(\gamma)}\qquad\ \mathrm{for}\ x\in\mathscr{H}_{a}^{(\gamma)},

(4.26)

\displaystyle\mathscr{P}_{x;a,b}^{(\gamma)}

\displaystyle:=\varphi_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}(x)\cdot\mathbb{P}_{x;a,b}^{(\gamma)}\qquad\mathrm{for}\ x\in\mathscr{H}_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}.

(4.27)

Note that
\mathscr{P}_{x;a}^{(\gamma)}
and
\mathscr{P}_{x;a,b}^{(\gamma)}
are bounded measures.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
-1\leq\gamma\leq 1
and
x\in\mathscr{H}_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}
. Then, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathscr{P}_{x;a,b}^{(\gamma)}=1_{\{T_{b}=\infty\}}\cdot\mathscr{P}_{x;a}^{(\gamma)}.

(4.28)

Proof.

Since
((X_{t}),\mathbb{P}_{x;a,b}^{(\gamma)})
is transient, we have
\lim_{t\to\infty}|X_{t}|=\infty,\ \mathbb{P}_{x;a,b}^{(\gamma)}
-a.s. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\varphi^{(\gamma)}_{a,b}(X_{t})}{h^{(\gamma)}(X_{t}-a)}=1,\qquad\mathbb{P}_{x;a,b}^{(\gamma)}\text{-a.s.}

(4.29)

Therefore, we apply to Theorem 4.1 of
[
21
]
as
\Gamma_{t}=1_{\{T_{a}\wedge T_{b}>t\}}
and
\mathscr{E}_{t}=1_{\{T_{a}>t\}}
, then the assertion holds.
∎

Consequently, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x;a,b}^{(\gamma)}=\frac{h^{(\gamma)}(x-a)}{\varphi_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}(x)}1_{\{T_{b}=\infty\}}\cdot\mathbb{P}_{x;a}^{(\gamma)}.

(4.30)

This implies

\displaystyle\varphi_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}(x)=h^{(\gamma)}(x-a)\mathbb{P}_{x;a}^{(\gamma)}(T_{b}=\infty).

(4.31)

In particular, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x;a}^{(\gamma)}(\cdot|\ T_{b}=\infty)=\mathbb{P}_{x;a,b}^{(\gamma)}(\cdot).

(4.32)

Therefore,
\mathbb{P}_{x;a,b}^{(\gamma)}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mathbb{P}_{x;a}^{(\gamma)}.

Examples

Brownian motion

Let
(X_{t})
be a standard Brownian motion. Then, we know that
h^{(\gamma)}(x)=|x|+\gamma x
(see, e.g., Example 3.9 of
[
18
]
). Note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}(T_{a}<T_{b})=\frac{b-x}{b-a}\qquad\text{for}\ a<x<b

(4.33)

(see, e.g., Theorem 7.5.3 of
[
7
]
).

1.

If
x<a,
then we have

\displaystyle\varphi^{(\gamma)}_{a,b}(x)

\displaystyle=a-x+\gamma(x-a)=(1-\gamma)(a-x).

(4.34)

Thus, we obtain
\mathbb{P}_{x;a,b}^{(\gamma)}=\mathbb{P}_{x;a}^{(\gamma)}
.

2.

If
a<x<b
, then we have

\displaystyle\varphi^{(\gamma)}_{a,b}(x)

\displaystyle=x-a+\gamma(x-a)-\frac{x-a}{b-a}(b-a+\gamma(b-a))=0.

(4.35)

3.

If
b<x
, then we have

\displaystyle\varphi^{(\gamma)}_{a,b}(x)

\displaystyle=x-a+\gamma(x-a)-(b-a+\gamma(b-a))=(1+\gamma)(x-b).

(4.36)

Thus, we obtain
\mathbb{P}_{x;a,b}^{(\gamma)}=\mathbb{P}_{b;x}^{(\gamma)}
.

Therefore, we obtain
\mathscr{H}_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}=(-\infty,a)\cup(b,\infty).

Stable process

Let
(X_{t})
be a strictly stable process of index
\alpha\in(1,2).
Then, we know that

\displaystyle h^{(\gamma)}(x)=\frac{1}{K(\alpha)}(1-\beta\ \mathrm{sgn}(x))|x|^{\alpha-1}

(4.37)

(see, Section 5 of
[
20
]
). Note that
h^{(\gamma)}
is independent of
\gamma.
By Lemma 3.5 of
[
18
]
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}(T_{b}<T_{a})=\frac{(1+\beta)(b-a)^{\alpha-1}+(1-\beta\ \mathrm{sgn}(x-a))|x-a|^{\alpha-1}-(1-\beta\ \mathrm{sgn}(x-b))|x-b|^{\alpha-1}}{2(b-a)^{\alpha-1}}

(4.38)

for
x\in\mathbb{R}.

1.

If
x<a
, then we have

\displaystyle\varphi_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}(x)=\frac{1}{K(\alpha)}\left\{\frac{(1+\beta)^{2}}{2}(a-x)^{\alpha-1}+\frac{(1+\beta)(1-\beta)}{2}(b-x)^{\alpha-1}-\frac{(1+\beta)(1-\beta)}{2}(b-a)^{\alpha-1}\right\}.

(4.39)

2.

If
a<x<b
, then

\displaystyle\varphi_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}(x)=\frac{1}{K(\alpha)}\frac{(1+\beta)(1-\beta)}{2}\left\{(x-a)^{\alpha-1}+(b-x)^{\alpha-1}-(b-a)^{\alpha-1}\right\}.

(4.40)

3.

If
b<x
, then

\displaystyle\varphi_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}(x)=\frac{1}{K(\alpha)}\left\{\frac{(1+\beta)(1-\beta)}{2}(x-a)^{\alpha-1}+\frac{(1-\beta)^{2}}{2}(x-b)^{\alpha-1}-\frac{(1+\beta)(1-\beta)}{2}(b-a)^{\alpha-1}\right\}.

(4.41)

Therefore, we obtain
\mathscr{H}_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}=\mathbb{R}\setminus\{a,b\}
when
\beta\neq\pm 1
,
\mathscr{H}_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}=(-\infty,a)
when
\beta=1
, and
\mathscr{H}_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}=(b,\infty)
when
\beta=-1.

Spectrally negative Lévy process

Let
(X_{t})
be a recurrent spectrally negative Lévy process. Then, we know that

\displaystyle h^{(\gamma)}(x)=W(x)+\frac{\gamma-1}{m^{2}}x,

(4.42)

where
W(x)
is the scale function of
(X_{t})
(see, Example 28 of
[
15
]
). By Lemma 3.5 of
[
18
]
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}(T_{b}<T_{a})=\frac{W(x-a)-W(x-b)}{W(b-a)}.

(4.43)

1.

If
x<a
, then we have

\displaystyle\varphi^{(\gamma)}_{a,b}(x)=\frac{\gamma-1}{m^{2}}(x-a).

(4.44)

2.

If
a<x<b
, then we have

\displaystyle\varphi_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}(x)=\frac{\gamma-1}{m^{2}}\left\{(x-a)-\frac{W(x-a)}{W(b-a)}(b-a)\right\}.

(4.45)

3.

If
b<x
, then we have

\displaystyle\varphi_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}(x)=W(x-b)+\frac{\gamma-1}{m^{2}}\left\{(x-a)-\frac{W(x-a)-W(x-b)}{W(b-a)}(b-a)\right\}.

(4.46)

Therefore, we obtain
\mathscr{H}_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}=\mathbb{R}\setminus\{a,b\}
when
m^{2}<\infty
, and
\mathscr{H}_{a,b}^{(\gamma)}=(b,\infty)
when
m^{2}=\infty.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
A
is a bounded
F_{\sigma}
-set which contains
0
. For any

and

r_{q}^{A}(x,0)=0.

Proof.

We define the
q
-resolvent operator

by

\displaystyle R^{q}f(x):=\mathbb{P}_{x}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-qt}f(X_{t})dt\right]

(6.3)

for a non-negative bounded Borel function

Then, we have for a stopping time

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}[R_{q}f(X_{T})\cdot e^{-qT}]=\mathbb{P}_{x}\left[\int_{T}^{\infty}e^{-qt}f(X_{t})dt\right].

(6.4)

Indeed, by the strong Markov property, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}[R_{q}f(X_{T})\cdot e^{-qT}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{x}\left[e^{-qT}\mathbb{P}_{X_{T}}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-qt}f(X_{t})dt\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{x}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-q(T+t)}f(X_{T+t})dt\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{x}\left[\int_{T}^{\infty}e^{-qt}f(X_{t})dt\right].

(6.5)

Since
x\mapsto r_{q}(-x)
is a
q
-excessive function, there exists a sequence of non-negative bounded Borel functions
(f_{n})
such that

for

as
n\to\infty
(see, e.g., Proposition 41.5 and Theorem 41.16 of
[
16
]
). Since
T_{A}\leq T_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}[R_{q}f_{n}(X_{T_{A}})\cdot e^{-q{T_{A}}}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{x}\left[\int_{T_{A}}^{\infty}e^{-qt}f_{n}(X_{t})dt\right]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}_{x}\left[\int_{T_{0}}^{\infty}e^{-qt}f_{n}(X_{t})dt\right]=\mathbb{P}_{x}[R_{q}f_{n}(X_{T_{0}})\cdot e^{-q{T_{0}}}].

(6.6)

Thus, by the monotone convergence theorem, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}[r_{q}(-X_{T_{A}})e^{-q{T_{A}}}]\geq\mathbb{P}_{x}[r_{q}(-X_{T_{0}})e^{-q{T_{0}}}].

(6.7)

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle 0\leq r_{q}^{A}(x,0)

\displaystyle=r_{q}(-x)-\mathbb{P}_{x}[r_{q}(-X_{T_{A}})e^{-qT_{A}}]

\displaystyle=r_{q}(-x)-r_{q}(0)\cdot\frac{r_{q}(-x)}{r_{q}(0)}=0.

(6.8)

The proof is complete.

), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x;A}(T_{A}=\infty)=1.

(6.15)

Similarly to Subsection
4.2
, we obtain the following theorem:

Theorem 6.4
.

It holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x;A}=\mathbb{P}_{x;0}^{(0)}(\cdot|\ T_{A}=\infty).

(6.16)

7
Conditioning to avoid an integer lattice

Let
L>0
and set
L\mathbb{Z}:=\{Ln\in\mathbb{R};\ n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. In this Section, we further assume the following:

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\frac{1}{q+\Psi(\frac{2n\pi}{L})}\right|<\infty\qquad\text{for}\ q>0.

(7.1)

We define for
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
q>0
,

\displaystyle R_{q}(x):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{e^{\frac{2n\pi x}{L}i}}{q+\Psi(\frac{2n\pi}{L})}

(7.2)

Note that
\Psi(u)=0
and
u=0
are equivalent. Isozaki
[
10
]
showed

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}[e^{-qT_{L\mathbb{Z}}}]=\frac{R_{q}(x)}{R_{q}(0)}.

(7.3)

We define for
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
q>0
,
H_{q}(x):=R_{q}(0)-R_{q}(x)
. Then, the limit

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to 0+}H_{q}(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1-e^{\frac{2n\pi x}{L}i}}{\Psi(\frac{2n\pi}{L})}

(7.4)

exists and is finite. Furthermore, if we let
\varphi_{L\mathbb{Z}}(x)
denote this limit, it is continuous. Indeed, since
|\Psi(u)|\to\infty
as
|u|\to\infty
, we have
|\frac{\Psi(u)}{q+\Psi(u)}|\to 1
as
|u|\to\infty
. Thus, the convergence
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\frac{1}{q+\Psi(\frac{2n\pi}{L})}|
and
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}|\frac{1}{\Psi(\frac{2n\pi}{L})}|
is equivalent, and then they converge by assumption (
7.1
). Therefore, we obtain by the dominated convergence theorem,

\displaystyle H_{q}(x)

\displaystyle=R_{q}(0)-R_{q}(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1-e^{\frac{2n\pi x}{L}i}}{q+\Psi(\frac{2n\pi}{L})}\to\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1-e^{\frac{2n\pi x}{L}i}}{\Psi(\frac{2n\pi}{L})}

(7.5)

as
q\to 0+.

Note that the right hand side of (
7.4
) is the Fourier series of
\varphi_{L\mathbb{Z}}(x)
. Moreover,
\varphi_{L\mathbb{Z}}(x)
is a periodic function with period
L
and is also an even function.

First, we prove of the a.s. convergence.

Proposition 7.1
.

For
x\in\mathbb{R}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to 0+}R_{q}(0)\mathbb{P}_{x}(T_{L\mathbb{Z}}>\bm{e}_{q})=\varphi_{L\mathbb{Z}}(x).

(7.6)

Proof.

By Fubini’s theorem and by (
7.3
) and (
7.4
), we have

\displaystyle R_{q}(0)\mathbb{P}_{x}(T_{L\mathbb{Z}}>\bm{e}_{q})=R_{q}(0)(1-\mathbb{P}_{x}[e^{-qT_{L\mathbb{Z}}}])=R_{q}(0)-R_{q}(x)\to\varphi_{L\mathbb{Z}}(x)

(7.7)

as
q\to 0+
. The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 7.2
.

Since
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1}{\Psi(\frac{2n\pi}{L})}
converges, we have

\displaystyle qR_{q}(0)=1+\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{q}{q+\Psi(\frac{2n\pi}{L})}\to 1

(7.8)

as
q\to 0+.
By (
2.11
), we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to 0+}\frac{r_{q}(0)}{R_{q}(0)}=\lim_{q\to 0+}\frac{qr_{q}(0)}{qR_{q}(0)}=0.

(7.9)

Therefore, comparing Proposition
6.2
and Proposition
7.1
, the speed of convergence of
\mathbb{P}_{x}(T_{A}>\bm{e}_{q})
and
\mathbb{P}_{x}(T_{L\mathbb{Z}}>\bm{e}_{q})
is different, where
A
is a bounded
F_{\sigma}
-set.

Next, we prove of the
L^{1}(\mathbb{P}_{x})
-convergence.

Proposition 7.3
.

For
x\in\mathbb{R}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to 0+}R_{q}(0)\mathbb{P}_{X_{t}}(T_{L\mathbb{Z}}>\bm{e}_{q})=\varphi_{L\mathbb{Z}}(X_{t})\ \text{in}\ L^{1}(\mathbb{P}_{x}).

(7.10)

Proof.

We want to show
H_{q}(X_{t})\to\varphi_{L\mathbb{Z}}(X_{t})
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{P}_{x})
, but this is clear
by
H_{q}(X_{t})\leq\varphi_{L\mathbb{Z}}(X_{t})\in L^{1}(\mathbb{P}_{x})
and by the dominated convergence theorem.
∎

We give the proof of the main theorem for conditioning to avoid an integer lattice.

The proof of Theorem
1.4
.

We define

\displaystyle N_{t}^{q}:=R_{q}(0)\mathbb{P}_{x}(T_{L\mathbb{Z}}>\bm{e}_{q}>t|\mathscr{F}_{t})

(7.11)

for
q>0
. By the lack of memory property of the exponential distribution and by the Markov property, we have

\displaystyle N_{t}^{q}

\displaystyle=R_{q}(0)1_{\{T_{L\mathbb{Z}}>t\}}\mathbb{P}_{x}^{\mathscr{F}_{t}}(T_{L\mathbb{Z}}>\bm{e}_{q}|\ \bm{e}_{q}>t)\mathbb{P}_{x}(\bm{e}_{q}>t)

\displaystyle=R_{q}(0)1_{\{T_{L\mathbb{Z}}>t\}}\mathbb{P}_{X_{t}}(T_{L\mathbb{Z}}>\bm{e}_{q})e^{-qt},

(7.12)

where
\mathbb{P}_{x}^{\mathscr{F}_{t}}
means the conditional probability with respect to
\mathscr{F}_{t}
. Thus, we have by Proposition
7.1
and
7.3
,

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to 0+}N_{t}^{q}=\varphi_{L\mathbb{Z}}(X_{t})1_{\{T_{L\mathbb{Z}}>t\}}\ \mathrm{a.s.\ and\ in}\ L^{1}(\mathbb{P}_{x}).

(7.13)

Therefore, we obtain by Proposition
7.1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}[F_{t},\ t<\bm{e}_{q}|\ \bm{e}_{q}<T_{L\mathbb{Z}}]=\frac{\mathbb{P}_{x}[F_{t}\cdot N_{t}^{q}]}{R_{q}(0)\mathbb{P}_{x}(\bm{e}_{q}<T_{L\mathbb{Z}})}\to\mathbb{P}_{x}\left[F_{t}\cdot\frac{\varphi_{L\mathbb{Z}}(X_{t})}{\varphi_{L\mathbb{Z}}(x)}1_{\{T_{L\mathbb{Z}}>t\}}\right],

(7.14)

as
q\to 0+.
The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 7.4
.

We define

\displaystyle M_{t}^{q}:=R_{q}(0)\mathbb{P}_{x}(\bm{e}_{q}<T_{L\mathbb{Z}}|\mathscr{F}_{t})

(7.15)

for
q>0
. Since we have by (
7.8
),

\displaystyle M_{t}^{q}-N_{t}^{q}

\displaystyle=R_{q}(0)\mathbb{P}_{x}(T_{L\mathbb{Z}}>\bm{e}_{q},\ t\geq\bm{e}_{q}|\mathscr{F}_{t})

\displaystyle=qR_{q}(0)\int_{0}^{t}1_{\{T_{L\mathbb{Z}}>s\}}e^{-qs}ds

\displaystyle\to\int_{0}^{t}1_{\{T_{L\mathbb{Z}}>s\}}ds>0\ \text{a.s. and in }L^{1}(\mathbb{P}_{x}),

(7.16)

as
q\to 0+
. Thus, by (
7.13
), we have

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to 0+}M_{t}^{q}

\displaystyle=\lim_{q\to 0+}(N_{t}^{q}+(M_{t}^{q}-N_{t}^{q}))

\displaystyle=\varphi_{L\mathbb{Z}}(X_{t})1_{\{T_{L\mathbb{Z}}>t\}}+\int_{0}^{t}1_{\{T_{L\mathbb{Z}}>s\}}ds\ \mathrm{a.s.\ and\ in}\ L^{1}(\mathbb{P}_{x}).

(7.17)

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}[F_{t}|\ \bm{e}_{q}<T_{L\mathbb{Z}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{P}_{x}[F_{t}\cdot M_{t}^{q}]}{R_{q}(0)\mathbb{P}_{x}(\bm{e}_{q}<T_{L\mathbb{Z}})}

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{P}_{x}\left[F_{t}\cdot\frac{\varphi_{L\mathbb{Z}}(X_{t})1_{\{T_{L\mathbb{Z}}>t\}}+\int_{0}^{t}1_{\{T_{L\mathbb{Z}}>s\}}ds}{\varphi_{L\mathbb{Z}}(x)}\right]

(7.18)

as
q\to 0+.

We define

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x;L\mathbb{Z}}(A,\ t<\zeta):=\mathbb{P}_{x}\left[1_{A}\cdot\frac{\varphi_{L\mathbb{Z}}(X_{t})}{\varphi_{L\mathbb{Z}}(x)}1_{\{T_{L\mathbb{Z}}>t\}}\right]\qquad(A\in\mathscr{F}_{t})

(7.19)

for
x\in\{x\in\mathbb{R};\ \varphi_{L\mathbb{Z}}(x)>0\}
, where
\zeta
denotes the life time. In the same way as (
4.20
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x;L\mathbb{Z}}(T_{L\mathbb{Z}}<\infty)=0.

(7.20)

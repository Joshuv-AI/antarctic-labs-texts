Nonlocal massive Thirring model and its solutions
Cong-han Wang, Shu-zhi Liu, Jing Wang, Da-jun Zhang

Abstract
A nonlocal version of the massive Thirring model (MTM) and its solutions are presented. We start from a 4-component system that can be reduced to the classical MTM and nonlocal MTM. Bilinear form of the 4-component system and general double Wronskian solutions are derived. By utilizing reduction technique we obtain solutions of the nonlocal MTM. Relations between the nonlocal MTM and the nonlocal Fokas-Lenells equation is discussed. Some solutions of the nonlocal MTM, such as solitons, double-pole solution, algebraic solitons and high order algebraic solitons are analyzed and illustrated. Key Words: nonlocal massive Thirring model, solution, bilinear form, double Wronskian

and the elements

\begin{array}[]{l}|\widetilde{N};\widehat{M-1}|=|1,2,\cdots,N;0,1,\cdots,M-1|,\\
|\widehat{N};\widetilde{M-1}|=|0,1,\cdots,N;1,2,\cdots,M-1|,\\
|\overline{N};\widehat{M}|=|2,3,\cdots,N;0,1,\cdots,M|,\\
|\widetilde{N};\widetilde{M}|=|1,2,\cdots,N;1,2,\cdots,M|.\end{array}

(3.3)

For the double Wronskians given in (

\displaystyle B=SC,

(3.9b)

Proof.

First, from the definition (
3.8
) and constraint (

\displaystyle\psi(x,t)

\displaystyle=\exp{\Bigl(-\frac{i}{2}A^{2}x-\frac{i}{2}A^{-2}t\Bigr)}B

\displaystyle=\exp{\Bigl(-\frac{i}{2}(SA^{2}S^{-1})x-\frac{i}{2}(SA^{-2}S^{-1})t\Bigr)}SC

\displaystyle=S\exp{\Bigl(-\frac{i}{2}A^{2}x-\frac{i}{2}A^{-2}t\Bigr)}C

\displaystyle=S\exp{\Bigl(\frac{i}{2}A^{2}(-x)+\frac{i}{2}A^{-2}(-t)\Bigr)}C

\displaystyle=S\phi(-x,-t),

which is (
3.10

Then, we can express the double Wronskians in (
3.6
) (with
N=M
)
in terms of

\displaystyle f(x,t)=|\widetilde{N};\widehat{N-1}|=\Big(\frac{i}{2}\Big)^{N}|A^{2}\widehat{\phi^{[N-1]}}(x,t)_{[x]};S\widehat{\phi^{[N-1]}}(-x,-t)_{[x]}|,

(3.13a)

\displaystyle s(x,t)=|\widetilde{N};\widetilde{N}|=(-1)^{N}\Big(\frac{i}{2}\Big)^{2N}|A|^{2}|\widehat{\phi^{[N-1]}}(x,t)_{[x]};S\widehat{{\phi^{[N-1]}}}(-x,-t)_{[x]}|,

\displaystyle\widetilde{h}(x,t)=-\frac{1}{(2i)^{N-1}}|A||\widehat{N};\widehat{N-2}|=-\frac{1}{(2i)^{N-1}}|A||\widehat{\phi^{[N]}}(x,t)_{[x]};S\widehat{{\phi^{[N-2]}}}(-x,-t)_{[x]}|,

\displaystyle\overline{h}(x,t)=-\,(2i)^{N}|A|^{-1}|\overline{N};\widetilde{N+1}|=\Big(\frac{i}{2}\Big)^{2N-1}|A||A^{2}\widehat{\phi^{[N-2]}}(x,t)_{[x]};S\widehat{{\phi^{[N]}}}(-x,-t)_{[x]}|,

(3.13e)

\displaystyle h(x,t)=-i\,(2i)^{N-1}|A|^{-1}|\overline{N};\widehat{N}|=i(-1)^{N}\,\Big(\frac{i}{2}\Big)^{N-1}|A|^{-1}|A^{4}\widehat{\phi^{[N-2]}}(x,t)_{[x]};S\widehat{\phi^{[N]}}(-x,-t)_{[x]}|,

and

\displaystyle\widetilde{h}(-x,-t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{(2i)^{N-1}}|A||\widehat{\phi^{[N]}}(-x,-t)_{[-x]};S\widehat{{\phi^{[N-2]}}}(x,t)_{[-x]}|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2i)^{N-1}}|A||\widehat{\phi^{[N]}}(-x,-t)_{[x]};S\widehat{{\phi^{[N-2]}}}(x,t)_{[x]}|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2i)^{N-1}}|A||S||S^{-1}\widehat{\phi^{[N]}}(-x,-t)_{[x]};\widehat{{\phi^{[N-2]}}}(x,t)_{[x]}|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2i)^{N-1}}|A||S||\delta A^{-2}S\widehat{\phi^{[N]}}(-x,-t)_{[x]};\widehat{{\phi^{[N-2]}}}(x,t)_{[x]}|

5
Conclusion

In this paper we have studied integrability of the nonlocal MTM (
2.7
)
and its various types of solutions.
Bilinearization-reduction approach was employed.
We started from the 4-component system (
1.8
),
which acts as the unreduced MTM and can be reduced to the classical MTM and nonlocal MTM.
Note that the Mikhailov model (
1.6
) is also related to the MTM.
In the paper we also explored the relations between (
1.8
) and (
1.6
)
and also their consistency in the nonlocal reduction.
In the bilinearization-reduction approach, the first step is to present bilinear form for
the unreduced MTM (
1.8
)
and obtain solutions in double Wronskian form (see Theorem
3.1
and its proof in Appendix
A
).
Then, we imposed constraints on the Wronskian entry vectors
\phi
and
\psi

(see (
3.9
) in Theorem
3.2
).
In principle, by solving (
3.9
) one can obtain all solutions of the nonlocal MTM
that are of zero background and formulated in double Wronskians.
Explicit forms of
\phi
and
\psi
were presented in Sec.
3.2
,
where, in particular, in Case (2), we gave
\phi
and
\psi

Finally, dynamics of some obtained solutions were analyzed and illustrated.

Considering the classical MTM is an important equation in mathematical physics

Appendix A
Proof of Theorem
3.1

To prove the theorem, let us first recall the following two lemmas that are useful in verifying solutions
in the Wronskian technique.

Lemma A.1
.

[
71
]

|\mathbf{M},\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}||\mathbf{M},\mathbf{c},\mathbf{d}|-|\mathbf{M},\mathbf{a},\mathbf{c}||\mathbf{M},\mathbf{b},\mathbf{d}|+|\mathbf{M},\mathbf{a},\mathbf{d}||\mathbf{M},\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c}|=0,

where
\mathbf{M}
is an arbitrary
N\times(N-2)
matrix, and
\mathbf{a}
,
\mathbf{b}
,

\mathbf{c}
and
\mathbf{d}
are
N
th-order column vectors.

Lemma A.2
.

[
72
,
73
]

Let
\Xi=(a_{js})_{N\times N}
be a
N\times N
matrix with
column vectors
\{\alpha_{j}\}
.
\Gamma=(\gamma_{js})_{N\times N}
is a
N\times N

operator matrix and each
\gamma_{js}
is a operator. Then we have

\sum^{N}_{j=1}|\Gamma_{j}\ast\Xi|=\sum^{N}_{j=1}|(\Gamma^{T})_{j}\ast\Xi^{T}|,

where

|A_{j}\ast\Xi|=|\Xi_{1},\ldots,\Xi_{j-1},A\circ\Xi_{j},\Xi_{j+1},\ldots,\Xi_{N}|,

and

A_{j}\circ B_{j}=(A_{1,j}B_{1,j},A_{2,j}B_{2,j},\ldots,A_{N,j}B_{N,j}),

in which
A_{j}=(A_{1,j},A_{2,j},\ldots,A_{N,j})^{T}
and
B_{j}=(B_{1,j},B_{2,j},\ldots,B_{N,j})^{T}

are
N
th-order column vectors.

Now we start the proof.
From (
3.7
) one can calculate derivatives of
f
,
g
,
h
and
s
:

\displaystyle f_{x}=|\widetilde{N-1},N+1;\widehat{M-1}|+|\widetilde{N};\widehat{M-2},M|,

\displaystyle f_{t}=-\frac{1}{4}(|0,\overline{N};\widehat{M-1}|+|\widetilde{N};-1,\widetilde{M-1}|),

\displaystyle g_{x}=\frac{1}{2(2i)^{N-1}}|A|(|\widehat{N-1},N+1;\widetilde{M-1}|+|\widehat{N};\widetilde{M-2},M|),

\displaystyle g_{t}=-\frac{1}{8(2i)^{N-1}}|A|(|-1,\widetilde{N};\widetilde{M-1}|+|\widehat{N};0,\overline{M-1}|),

\displaystyle h_{x}=-i\,(2i)^{N-1}|A|^{-1}(|\overline{N-1},N+1;\widehat{M}|+|\overline{N};\widehat{M-1},M+1|),

\displaystyle h_{t}=\frac{i}{4}\,(2i)^{N-1}|A|^{-1}(|1,\underline{N};\widehat{M}|+|\overline{N};-1,\widetilde{M}|),

\displaystyle s_{x}=|\widetilde{N-1},N+1;\widetilde{M}|+|\widetilde{N};\widetilde{M-1},M+1|,

\displaystyle s_{t}=-\frac{1}{4}(|0,\overline{N};\widetilde{M}|+|\widetilde{N};0,\overline{M}|),

\displaystyle\widetilde{h}_{t}=\frac{1}{4(2i)^{N-1}}|A|(|-1,\widetilde{N};\widehat{M-2}|+|\widehat{N};-1,\widetilde{M-2}|),

\displaystyle\overline{h}_{t}=\frac{1}{4}(2i)^{N}|A|^{-1}(|1,\underline{N};\widetilde{M+1}|+|\overline{N};0,\overline{M+1}|).

Here
\underline{N-1}
denotes the consecutive columns
(\partial_{x}^{3}\phi,\cdots,\partial_{x}^{N-1}\phi)
.
Substituting them into equation (
3.5a
), we have

\displaystyle D_{t}\widetilde{h}\cdot s+gf

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\widetilde{h}_{t}s-\widetilde{h}s_{t}+gf

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2(2i)^{N-1}}|A|\Big[\frac{1}{2}|\widetilde{N};\widetilde{M}|(|-1,\widetilde{N};\widehat{M-2}|+|\widehat{N};-1,\widetilde{M-2}|)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}|\widehat{N};\widehat{M-2}|(|0,\overline{N};\widetilde{M}|+|\widetilde{N};0,\overline{M}|)+|\widehat{N};\widetilde{M-1}||\widetilde{N};\widehat{M-1}|\Big].

(A.1)

From the following identity

\displaystyle|\widetilde{N};\widetilde{M}|\Big((-2i\,\mathrm{Tr}(A^{-2})|\widehat{N};\widehat{M-2}|\Big)=|\widehat{N};\widehat{M-2}|\Big((-2i\,\mathrm{Tr}(A^{-2})|\widetilde{N};\widetilde{M}|\Big),

where
\mathrm{Tr}(A)

we find the relation

\displaystyle|\widetilde{N};\widetilde{M}|(|-1,\widetilde{N};\widehat{M-2}|-|\widehat{N};-1,\widetilde{M-2}|)=|\widehat{N};\widehat{M-2}|(|0,\overline{N};\widetilde{M}|-|\widetilde{N};0,\overline{M}|).

With it in hand, we reduce equation (

\displaystyle D_{t}\widetilde{h}\cdot s+gf

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2(2i)^{N-1}}|A|\Big[|\widetilde{N};\widetilde{M}||\widehat{N};-1,\widetilde{M-2}|-|\widehat{N};\widehat{M-2}||\widetilde{N};0,\overline{M}|+|\widehat{N};\widetilde{M-1}||\widetilde{N};\widehat{M-1}|\Big].

Next, using the dispersion relation of

\displaystyle|\widehat{N};-1,\widetilde{M-2}|=(-1)^{M-1}(-2i)^{M+N}|A|^{-2}|\widetilde{N+1};0,\overline{M-1}|,

\displaystyle|\widehat{N};\widehat{M-2}|=(-1)^{M-1}(-2i)^{M+N}|A|^{-2}|\widetilde{N+1};\widetilde{M-1}|,

Appendix B

As pointed out in Proposition
2.1

) can be transformed from the Mikhailov model (

)

]

\displaystyle I_{2}=D_{x}D_{t}h\cdot s+hs=0,

In the following let us explore more details of such connections.

First, bilinear equation (

) together with (

Substituting (

) and (

\displaystyle\eqref{KN11}=\frac{i}{fs}I_{1}+\!i\frac{D_{x}g\cdot f}{f^{2}s^{2}}I_{4}-i\frac{g}{f^{2}s^{2}}\left(sD_{x}D_{t}f\cdot f+2ihD_{x}g\cdot f\right)=0,

Multivariate strong subexponential distributions: properties and applications
Charalampos D. Passalidis

Abstract
. In this paper we introduce and study the classes of multivariate strong and strongly subexponential distributions. Some first properties are verified, as for example a type of multivariate analogue of Kesten’s inequality, the closure property with respect to convolution, and the conditional closure property with respect to convolution roots. Next, we establish the the single big jump principle for the randomly stopped sums, under the assumption that the random vectors in the summation belong to the class of multivariate strong subexponential distributions. Here the conditions of the counting random variable are weaker in comparison with them in multivariate subexponential class. Further, we establish uniform asymptotic estimates for the precise large deviations in multivariate set up, both for random and non-random sums, when the distribution of the summands belongs to the class of multivariate strongly subexponential distributions. Finally, we provide an application to a non-standard risk model, with independent and identically distributed claim vectors, from the class of multivariate strong subexponential distributions and in the presence of constant interest force. More concretely, the common counting process of the claim vectors constitutes from inter-arrival times, that are independent but not necessarily identically distributed. Under some additional condition, on the ’heavyness’ of the counting process tail, we establish a uniform asymptotic estimate for the finite time ruin probability in this model.



Keywords: multivariate heavy-tailed distributions; convolution of random vectors; randomly stopped sums; vector type precise large deviations principle; nonstandard risk model; uniformity

1.
Introduction

As a matter of fact the heavy tailed distributions in combination with the presence of dependence, represent a hot topic in the frame of applied probability. One way for modeling simultaneously these two concepts, is through the multivariate heavy tailed distributions. Such a way leads, through relatively simple and reasonable assumptions, to reach more general results, in relation with one-dimensional distributions under the presence of specific dependence structures.

From the pioneer paper
[
30
]
the multivariate regular variation, symbolically
MRV
, attracted the interest of various topics of applied probability, as in extreme value theory, in time-series, in risk theory, in risk management and random walks, see
[
34
]
,
[
65
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
40
]
,
[
54
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
55

,
[
13
]
, among others.

Although the
MRV

[
38
, p. 205, Fig. 7.2]
. Thus, the study of other classes of heavy-tailed distributions in multivariate set up, seems unavoidable.

For the multivariate subexponential distributions we know four different approaches, by
[
16
]
(via point processes and vague convergence),
[
62
]
,
[
66
]
and by
[
46
]
. From the first three approaches, this by
[
66
]
looks to be superior, since it has some good enough properties, that are inspired by the corresponding properties of stable random vectors. However, these first three classes show to approach the subexponentiality through a linear approach of multivariate single big jump principle, while the last one, seems to approach the subexponentiality through a non-linear type of multivariate single big jump principle. Therefore, we consider that the last two approaches are functioning in complementary mode, see in
[
43
, Sec. 5]
for more details.

In this paper we focus in the multivariate subexponentiality
\mathcal{S}_{A}
approach, suggested in
[
66
]
, see definition in Section 2 below. As in the uni-variate case, in many applications, as for example random walks, randomly stopped sums, queuing theory, risk theory among others, are needed assumptions slightly weaker of subexponentiality, the same happens in the multivariate case as well. More concretely in this paper we are interested in the presence of multivariate single big jump principle in the randomly stopped sums, and asymptotic equivalencies for the precise large deviations in multivariate set up. In order to study these two problems, we shall introduce the multivariate strong and strongly subexponential distributions, that belong to class
\mathcal{S}_{A}
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, after providing the necessary preliminary concepts for the one-dimensional and multivariate heavy-tailed distributions, we present the multivariate classes of strong (and strongly) subexponential distributions.

In Section 3, we examine some closure properties of these classes, like the closure with respect to convolution, and with respect to convolution roots of random vectors distributions.

In Section 4, we are interested to randomly stopped sums

\displaystyle{\bf S}_{\tau}=\sum_{i=1}^{\tau}{\bf X}^{(i)}\,,

(1.1)

where
\tau
represent a (one-dimensional) discrete random variable, and the
\{{\bf X}^{(i)}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}

{\bf X}
. We deal with the single big jump principle for the multivariate randomly stopped sum, namely

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}[{\bf S}_{\tau}\in x\,A]\sim\mathbf{E}[\tau]\,\mathbf{P}[{\bf X}\in x\,A]\,,

(1.2)

x\to\infty

Under the condition that
{\bf X}

[

. Here, under the restriction of the distribution of
{\bf X}
, to belongs in the class of multivariate strong subexponential distributions, we establish relation (
1.2

\mathbf{E}[\tau]<\infty
and that the

F_{A}

A

In Section 5, we establish a vector type precise large deviation principle, for non-random and random sums, which are defined as follows

\displaystyle{\bf S}_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\bf X}^{(i)}\,,\qquad{\bf S}_{N(t)}=\sum_{i=1}^{N(t)}{\bf X}^{(i)}\,,

(1.3)

where
\{N(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
, is a counting process (one-dimensional). Under the condition that the
\{{\bf X}^{(i)}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
are i.i.d. copies of
{\bf X}
, with multivariate strongly subexponential distribution and independent of the
\{N(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
, which satisfies some common assumptions in one-dimensional precise large deviations, we give the asymptotic equivalencies for the

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}[{\bf S}_{n}\in x\,A]\,,\;\mathbf{P}[{\bf S}_{N(t)}\in x\,A]\,,

where
n\to\infty
(or,
t\to\infty
, respectively), uniformly for any

, see for the definitions in Section 2. These results represent generalization even in the one-dimensional subcase.

Finally, in Section 6, we present an application in a multivariate non-standard risk model. We provide uniform asymptotic estimations, with respect to time, for the entrance probability of the discounted aggregate claims in some rare-set, and for the ruin probability over a finite time horizon, in a risk model with common counting process for the
d
-lines of business and in the presence of constant interest force. The common counting process is NOT necessarily renewal one, since we assume that the inter-arrival times, are independent but not necessarily identically distributed. These results, are achieved under the condition that the counting process has ’lighter’ tail, than the common distribution of the claim-vectors, in each rare-set, which belongs to the class of multivariate strong subexponential distributions. The proof of this result is based on an alternative multivariate version of Kesten inequality, and hence the multivariate strong subexponentiality is necessary in some sense.

2.
Preliminary results

In this section we present the main concepts for the heavy-tailed distributions, which will be used later. We also define two new multivariate distribution classes, whose simple properties are studied, and are provided the necessary inclusions with respect to other existing multivariate distribution classes.

2.1.
Notation

In what follows the asymptotic relations hold as
x\to\infty
, except it is referred otherwise. All the vectors are denoted in bold script and are
d
-dimensional, for some
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
, namely we have
{\bf x}=(x_{1},\,\ldots,\,x_{d})^{\top}
, where
{\bf x}^{\top}
, is the transpose of
{\bf x}
. All the random vectors, have distributions with support on non-negative quadrant
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{d}=[0,\,\infty)^{d}
. The operations between two vectors
{\bf x}^{(1)}
,
{\bf x}^{(2)}
, are defined by components, for example
{\bf x}^{(1)}\pm{\bf x}^{(2)}=(x_{1}^{(1)}\pm x_{1}^{(2)},\,\ldots,\,x_{d}^{(1)}\pm x_{d}^{(2)})^{\top}
, and for some constant
\lambda>0
, the scalar product is defined as
\lambda\,{\bf x}=(\lambda\,x_{1},\,\ldots,\,\lambda\,x_{d})^{\top}
. Additionally,
{\bf 0}=(0,\,\ldots,\,0)
denotes the origin of the axes. Furthermore, for two real numbers
x,\,y
we define
x\wedge y:=\min\{x,\,y\}
,
x\vee y:=\max\{x,\,y\}
, and
\left\lfloor x\right\rfloor
denotes the integer part of
x
. For any set
{\mathbb{B}}
, we denote by
{\mathbb{B}}^{c}
its complement, by
\overline{{\mathbb{B}}}
its closed hull, and by
\partial{\mathbb{B}}
its border.

For any distribution
V
, we denote by
\overline{V}=1-V
its tail. For two independent random variables
Z_{1}
,
Z_{2}
with distributions
V_{1}
,
V_{2}
reprectively, their convolution is denoted by
V_{1}*V_{2}
, and with
V^{n*}
, we denote the
n
-th order convolution power of
V
.

Some asymptotic notation is also needed. For two positive functions
f
,
g
, we write
f(x)\sim c\,g(x)
with
c\in(0,\,\infty)
if it holds

\displaystyle\lim\dfrac{f(x)}{g(x)}=c\,.

We write
f(x)=o[g(x)]
, if it holds

\displaystyle\lim\dfrac{f(x)}{g(x)}=0\,,

and
f(x)=O[g(x)]
, if it holds

\displaystyle\limsup\dfrac{f(x)}{g(x)}<\infty\,.

Further, we denote
f(x)\asymp g(x)
, if it holds

f(x)=O[g(x)]
and
g(x)=O[f(x)]
, simultaneously.

Similar notations hold for
{\bf f}
,
{\bf g}
positive
d
-dimensional functions, where the notation corresponds now to
{\bf f}(x\,{\mathbb{B}})
,
{\bf g}(x\,{\mathbb{B}})
, for
{\mathbb{B}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, with
{\bf 0}\notin{\mathbb{B}}
. For example, we write
{\bf f}(x\,{\mathbb{B}})\sim c\,{\bf g}(x\,{\mathbb{B}})
with
c\in(0,\,\infty)
if it holds

\displaystyle\lim\dfrac{{\bf f}(x\,{\mathbb{B}})}{{\bf g}(x\,{\mathbb{B}})}=c\,.

Correspondingly, for the
(d+1)
-variate, positive functions
{\bf f}^{*}
and
{\bf g}^{*}
, we denote
{\bf f}^{*}(x\,{\mathbb{B}};\,y)\sim{\bf g}^{*}(x\,{\mathbb{B}};\,y)
, uniformly for any
y\in\Delta
, for some non-empty set
\Delta
if it holds

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\sup_{y\in\Delta}\left|\dfrac{{\bf f}^{*}(x\,{\mathbb{B}};\,y)}{{\bf g}^{*}(x\,{\mathbb{B}};\,y)}-1\right|=0\,.

Additionally, we write
{\bf f}^{*}(x\,{\mathbb{B}};\,y)\lesssim{\bf g}^{*}(x\,{\mathbb{B}};\,y)
, or
{\bf g}^{*}(x\,{\mathbb{B}};\,y)\gtrsim{\bf f}^{*}(x\,{\mathbb{B}};\,y)
, uniformly for any
y\in\Delta
, if

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\sup_{y\in\Delta}\dfrac{{\bf f}^{*}(x\,{\mathbb{B}};\,y)}{{\bf g}^{*}(x\,{\mathbb{B}};\,y)}\leq 1\,

where
y
and
\Delta
will be specified when it appears.

2.2.
Heavy-tailed distributions

Now, we give the preliminary concepts for the heavy-tailed distributions in one dimension. Let us restrict ourselves only on distributions
V
with support in
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, although most of the definitions below can easily be extended to supports on the whole
{\mathbb{R}}
. Next, all the distributions in this subsection are such that it holds
\overline{V}(x)>0
, for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

At first we say that a distribution
V
has heavy tail, symbolically
V\in\mathcal{K}
, if it holds

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\varepsilon x}\,V(dx)=\infty\,,

for any
\varepsilon>0
. Further, we say that a distribution
V
has long tail, symbolically
V\in\mathcal{L}
, if it holds

\displaystyle\lim\dfrac{\overline{V}(x-a)}{\overline{V}(x)}=1\,,

for any (or, equivalently, for some)
a>0
. It is well-known that for any distribution
V\in\mathcal{L}
, there exists a function
h\,:\,{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\to(0,\,\infty)
, such that
h(x)\to\infty
,
h(x)=o(x)
and
\overline{V}(x\pm h(x))\sim\overline{V}(x)
, that is called insensitivity function of
V
, see in
[
25
, Lem. 2.19, Sec. 2.8]
.

The most famous class of heavy-tailed distributions is the class of subexponential distributions, symbolically
\mathcal{S}
, with many applications in branching processes, queuing theory, random walks, risk theory and renewal theory, see in
[
3
]
,
[
70
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
56
]
,
[
38
]
, among others. We say that a distribution
V
is subexponential, symbolically
V\in\mathcal{S}
, if it holds

\displaystyle\lim\dfrac{\overline{V^{n*}}(x)}{\overline{V}(x)}=n\,,

for any (or, equivalently, for some) integer
n\geq 2
. These first three distribution classes are introduced in
[
15
]
.

The class of dominatedly varying distributions is introduced in
[
23
]
. We say that a distribution
V
has dominatedly varying tail, symbolically
V\in\mathcal{D}
, if it holds

\displaystyle\limsup\dfrac{\overline{V}(b\,x)}{\overline{V}(x)}<\infty\,,

for any (or, equivalently, for some)
b\in(0,\,1)
. It is well-known that
\mathcal{D}\not\subsetneq\mathcal{S}
,
\mathcal{S}\not\subsetneq\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{S}\equiv\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L}\neq\emptyset
, see in
[
29
]
.

A much smaller but famous heavy-tailed distribution class, is the class of regular variation. We say that a distribution
V
has regularly varying tail with index
\alpha\in(0,\,\infty)
, symbolically
V\in\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}
, if it holds

\displaystyle\lim\dfrac{\overline{V}(t\,x)}{\overline{V}(x)}=t^{-\alpha}\,,

for any
t>0
. We will denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}:=\bigcup_{0<\alpha<\infty}\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}\,,

for more details about regularly variyng distributions and its applications see in
[
7
]
,
[
61
]
,
[
42
]
,
[
60
]
,
[
57
]
, among others. We know that the following inclusions are true
\mathcal{R}\subsetneq\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L}\subsetneq\mathcal{S}\subsetneq\mathcal{L}\subsetneq\mathcal{K}
, see for example in
[
52
, Ch. 2]
.

Next, we present two distribution classes, that we generalized in multivariate set up and play crucial role in the present paper. Let
Z
be a random variable with finite mean

\displaystyle\mu_{V}:=\mathbf{E}[Z]=\int_{0}^{\infty}\overline{V}(y)\,dy<\infty\,.

We say that
V
, with
\mu_{V}<\infty
, belongs to the class of strong subexponential distributions, symbolically
V\in\mathcal{S}^{*}
, if it holds

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}\overline{V}(x-y)\,\overline{V}(y)\,dy\sim 2\mu_{V}\,\overline{V}(x)\,.

The strong subexponential class
\mathcal{S}^{*}
is introduced in
[
36
]
, where was noticed, as its main advantage, that if
V\in\mathcal{S}^{*}
, then the integrated tail distribution belongs to class
\mathcal{S}
, but the inverse does not hold in general. Class
\mathcal{S}^{*}
has been used in several applications in random walks, randomly stopped sums etc, in order to face problems, in which class
\mathcal{S}
fails, see for example in
[
26
]
,
[
49
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
63
]
among others.

Finally, under the same condition
\mu_{V}<\infty
, we say that
V
belongs to the class of strongly subexponential distributions, symbolically
V\in\mathcal{S}_{*}
, if it holds

\displaystyle\lim\dfrac{\overline{V_{u}^{2*}}(x)}{\overline{V}_{u}(x)}=2\,.

uniformly for any
u\in[1,\,\infty)
, where

\displaystyle\overline{V}_{u}(x)=1\wedge\int_{x}^{x+u}\overline{V}(y)\,dy\,,

for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
. Class
\mathcal{S}_{*}
is introduced in
[
48
]
, and found many applications in random walks and large deviations. In
[
35
, Lem. 1]
and
[
19
, p. 28]
were proved the following inclusions
\mathcal{S}^{*}\subsetneq\mathcal{S}_{*}\subsetneq\mathcal{S}
, and further for all distributions
V
, with finite mean
\mu_{V}<\infty
, these inclusion can be extended to

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}\subsetneq\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L}\subsetneq\mathcal{S}^{*}\subsetneq\mathcal{S}_{*}\subsetneq\mathcal{S}\subsetneq\mathcal{L}\subsetneq\mathcal{K}\,,

(2.1)

see in
[
36
, Th. 3.2(a)]
for the second inclusion.

2.3.
Multivariate analogues of heavy-tailed distributions

Now, we provide some known definitions of heavy-tailed distribution classes in multivariate framework, and further we introduce two new multivariate classes, that will be used in the rest of the paper. We present also some examples and counterexamples for these classes, together with some properties as the multivariate version of the Kesten’s lemma. We should remind that all the random vectors have distribution support on the non-negative quadrant.

We start with the most popular multivariate heavy-tailed distribution
MRV
. Let
{\bf X}
be a random vector with distribution
F
. We say that
F
belongs to (standard) multivariate regularly varying distributions, if there exists a Radon measure
\mu
, non-degenerate to zero, and some one-dimensional distribution
V\in\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}
, with index
\alpha\in(0,\,\infty)
, such that

\displaystyle\lim\dfrac{1}{\overline{V}(x)}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,{\mathbb{B}}\right]=\mu({\mathbb{B}})\,,

for any
\mu
-continuous Borel set
{\mathbb{B}}\in[0,\,\infty]^{d}\setminus\{{\bf 0}\}
. Symbolically we write
F\in MRV(\alpha,\,V,\,\mu)
, see in
[
65
]
for more information about
MRV
class.

For the rest multivariate distribution classes, we restrict ourselves to a concrete subset of
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{d}
. Namely, we need the following family of sets

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}=\{A\subsetneq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\;:\;A\;\text{ open, \, increasing,\,}A^{c}\;\text{ convex},\;{\bf 0}\not\in\overline{A}\}\,,

where a set
A
is called increasing if for any
{\bf x}\in A
and
{\bf y}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{d}
, it holds
{\bf x}+{\bf y}\in A
. The family of sets
\mathscr{R}
is introduced in
[
66
, Sec. 4]
, and it was proved that for any
A\in\mathscr{R}
, there exists a set of vectors
I_{A}\subsetneq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, such that it holds

\displaystyle A=\{{\bf x}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\;:\;{\bf p}^{T}\,{\bf x}>1\,,\;\exists\;{\bf p}\in I_{A}\}\,

Further more we find in
[
66
, Lem. 4.5]
that for a random vector
{\bf X}
with distribution
F
, the random variable

\displaystyle Y_{A}=\sup\{u\;:\;{\bf X}\in u\,A\}\,,

(2.2)

has proper distribution
F_{A}
, whose tail is given by

\displaystyle\overline{F}_{A}(x)=\mathbf{P}[{\bf X}\in x\,A]=\mathbf{P}\left[\sup_{{\bf p}\in I_{A}}{\bf p}^{\top}\,{\bf X}>x\right]\,,

(2.3)

for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
. This way, through relation (
2.3
) was defined the multivariate subexponentiality on
A
, for some fixed
A\in\mathscr{R}
, symbolically
F\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
, when
F_{A}\in\mathcal{S}
.

Similarly, in
[
44
]
, were defined classes
\mathcal{D}_{A}
,
\mathcal{L}_{A}
,
(\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L})_{A}
, when
F_{A}\in\{\mathcal{D},\,\mathcal{L},\,\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L}\}
, respectively. For these classes we adopt the notation

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\mathscr{R}}:=\bigcap_{A\in\mathscr{R}}\mathcal{B}_{A}\,,

for any distribution class
\mathcal{B}\in\{\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L},\,\mathcal{D},\,\mathcal{S},\,\mathcal{L}\}
. By definition, these classes keep the inclusions from the uni-variate classes and are related with the
MRV
through
[
44
, Prop. 2.1]
as follows

\displaystyle\bigcup_{0<\alpha<\infty}MRV(\alpha,\,V,\,\mu)\subsetneq(\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L})_{\mathscr{R}}\subsetneq\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{R}}\subsetneq\mathcal{L}_{\mathscr{R}}\,.

(2.4)

Now we are ready to introduce two new multivariate distribution classes.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
be some fixed set and
{\bf X}
be a random vector with distribution
F
. We say that
F
belongs to class of multivariate strong (or, strongly) subexponential distributions on
A
, symbolically
F\in\mathcal{S}_{A}^{*}
(or
F\in\mathcal{S}_{*,A}
), if
F_{A}\in\mathcal{S}^{*}
(or,
F_{A}\in\mathcal{S}_{*}
, respectively).

Remark 2.1
.

It is clear, that if
F\in\mathcal{B}_{A}
, with
\mathcal{B}\in\{\mathcal{S}^{*},\,\mathcal{S}_{*}\}
, it means that
\mu_{F_{A}}<\infty
, for
A\in\mathscr{R}
. Furthermore, from relations (
2.1
) and (
2.4
), under the condition

, for all
A\in\mathscr{R}
, then the following inclusions hold

\displaystyle\bigcup_{0<\alpha<\infty}MRV(\alpha,\,V,\,\mu)\subsetneq(\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L})_{\mathscr{R}}\subsetneq\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{R}}^{*}\subsetneq\mathcal{S}_{*,\mathscr{R}}\subsetneq\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{R}}\subsetneq\mathcal{L}_{\mathscr{R}}\,,

with these inclusions, still intact, for the classes
\mathcal{B}_{A}
instead of
\mathcal{B}_{\mathscr{R}}
, under the weaker condition

, only for this

indicates that all

to have distribution in

Example 2.1
.

Let
{\bf X}=(X_{1},\,X_{2})
a random vector with distribution

\displaystyle A=\left\{{\bf x}\;:\;\dfrac{1}{2}\,x_{1}+\dfrac{1}{2}x_{2}>1\right\}\,,

I_{A}=(1/2,\,1/2)
, see Remark
2.2
, then it holds

\displaystyle\overline{F}_{A}(x)=\mathbf{P}\left[\dfrac{1}{2}\,X_{1}+\dfrac{1}{2}X_{2}>x\right]\,,

for any
x>0
. In order to get
F\in\mathcal{B}_{A}
, it is enough to hold
F_{A}\in\mathcal{B}
, hence it is enough the inclusion
F_{1}*F_{2}\in\mathcal{B}
. However, class
\mathcal{B}
is NOT closed with respect to convolution, see in
[
53
]
for
\mathcal{S}
and in
[
39
]
for
\mathcal{S}^{*}
. So we can find two distributions such that
F_{1}*F_{2}\notin\mathcal{B}
, hence
F\not\in\mathcal{B}_{A}
and further
F\not\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathscr{R}}
.

We observe that if in Example
2.1
we have
F_{1}*F_{2}\in\mathcal{B}
, then follows
F\in\mathcal{B}_{A}
. For necessary and sufficient conditions for closure with respect to convolution of classes
\mathcal{S}
, and
\mathcal{S}^{*}
we refer to
[
51
]
and
[
39
]
respectively.

As we said, the inclusions of Remark
2.1
are not trivial. Except of the example of
[
66
, Prop. 4.15]
, which belongs to class
(\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L})_{\mathscr{R}}
, there exists a rich spectrum of examples, that escape from the
MRV
framework. For the set

\displaystyle A_{1}^{\prime}=\left\{{\bf x}\;:\;\sum_{i=1}^{d}\dfrac{1}{d}\,x_{i}>c\right\}\,,

for
c>0
, the
[
41
, Exam. 4.2, 4.3, 4.4]
, under the additional condition
\mu_{F_{A_{1}^{\prime}}}<\infty
, belong to class
\mathcal{S}_{A_{1}^{\prime}}^{*}
, since they belong to
(\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L})_{A_{1}^{\prime}}
. Further, in Example
4.5
of that paper, if
F_{1}\in MDA(\Lambda)\cap\mathcal{S}^{*}
(instead of
F_{1}\in MDA(\Lambda)\cap\mathcal{S}
), where by
MDA(\Lambda)
we denote the maximum domain of attraction of Gumbel distribution, it holds again
F\in\mathcal{S}_{A_{1}^{\prime}}^{*}
.

Here, we provide another example for the class
\mathcal{S}_{A_{i}^{\prime}}^{*}
, for
i=1,\,2
, with

\displaystyle A_{2}^{\prime}=\left\{{\bf x}\;:\;x_{i}>b\,,\;\exists\;i=1,\,\ldots,\,d\right\}\in\mathscr{R}\,,

with
b>0
. It is worth to notice that this example is not confined in class
(\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L})_{A_{i}^{\prime}}
, for
i=1,\,2
, and is easy enough to be checked, since the conditions are based only on marginals distributions, and on the dependence structure of the components of
{\bf X}
. The dependence structure we shall use is a general weak dependence called linearly wide quadrant dependence, symbolically
LWQD
, introduced by
[
64
]
.

Example 2.2
.

Let
{\bf X}=(X_{1},\,\ldots,\,X_{d})^{\top}
be a non-negative random vector with distribution
F
. We assume that the components of
{\bf X}
follow a common distribution
G\in\mathcal{S}^{*}
, and that they are
LWQD
, namely there exists a sequence of positive constants
\{g_{n}\,,\;n\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
and there is
x_{0}>0
, such that it holds

\displaystyle\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}X_{j}>x\;|\;X_{n}>y\right]}{\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}X_{j}>x\right]}\leq g_{n-1}\,,

uniformly for
n=2,\,\ldots,\,d
,
x,\,y\in[x_{0},\,\infty)
. Then
F\in\mathcal{S}_{A_{1}^{\prime}}^{*}
and
F\in\mathcal{S}_{A_{2}^{\prime}}^{*}
.

Let us show first that
F\in\mathcal{S}_{A_{1}^{\prime}}^{*}
. It is enough to show that

\displaystyle\overline{F}_{A_{1}^{\prime}}(x)=\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,A_{1}^{\prime}\right]=\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{d}\dfrac{c}{d}\,X_{j}>x\right]\,,

for
x>0
, belongs to
\mathcal{S}^{*}
.

By
[
64
, Th. 1]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\overline{F}_{A_{1}^{\prime}}(x)\sim\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathbf{P}\left[\dfrac{c}{d}\,X_{j}>x\right]=d\,\overline{G^{\prime}}(x)\,,

(2.7)

where
G^{\prime}
denotes the distribution of
\dfrac{c}{d}\,X_{j}
, for
j=1,\,\ldots,\,d
, and consequently
G^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}^{*}
. Hence from relation (
2.7
), in combination with
[
25
, Cor. 3.26]
, we find that
F\in\mathcal{S}_{A_{1}^{\prime}}^{*}
.

Now, we proceed to show
F\in\mathcal{S}_{A_{2}^{\prime}}^{*}
. For the
A_{2}^{\prime}
, we can choose some
I_{A_{2}^{\prime}}=\left\{\dfrac{{\bf e}_{1}}{b},\,\ldots,\,\dfrac{{\bf e}_{d}}{b}\right\}
, where the
{\bf e}_{i}
represents the vector, whose
i
-th component is unit and the rest components are zero (see
[
66
, Rem. 4.4]
for more about
I_{A}
). Therefore, for
x>0
we obtain

\displaystyle\overline{F}_{A_{2}^{\prime}}(x)=\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,A_{2}^{\prime}\right]=\mathbf{P}\left[\bigvee_{i=1}^{d}\dfrac{1}{b}\,X_{j}>x\right]\,.

Thus, again by
[
64
, Th. 1]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\overline{F}_{A_{2}^{\prime}}(x)\sim\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathbf{P}\left[\dfrac{1}{b}\,X_{j}>x\right]=d\,\overline{G^{\prime\prime}}(x)\,.

where
G^{\prime\prime}
denotes the common distribution of the
\dfrac{1}{b}\,X_{j}
, for
j=1,\,\ldots,\,d
. So, from
G^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{S}^{*}
, we find
F\in\mathcal{S}_{A_{2}^{\prime}}^{*}
.

Next, we present some properties of the new distribution classes, related with closure properties with respect to strong (or, weak) tail equivalence, and with a multivariate version of Kesten’s inequality. We adopt the notation
F_{i}(x\,A):=\mathbf{P}[{\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A]
and
Y_{A}^{(i)}:=\sup\{u\;:\;{\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\}
, with distributions
F_{A}^{(i)}
respectively, for
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, for any fixed
A\in\mathscr{R}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
be some fixed set. We consider the random vectors
{\bf X}^{(1)}
,
{\bf X}^{(2)}
with distributions
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
.

(i)

If
F_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{A}^{*}
( or,
F_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{*,A}
),
F_{2}\in\mathcal{L}_{A}
and it holds

\displaystyle F_{1}(x\,A)\asymp F_{2}(x\,A)\,,

(2.8)

then
F_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{A}^{*}
( or,
F_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{*,A}
respectively).

(ii)

If
F_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{A}^{*}
and it holds

\displaystyle\lim\dfrac{F_{1}(x\,A)}{F_{2}(x\,A)}=c\,,

(2.9)

for some
c\in(0,\,\infty)
, then
F_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{A}^{*}
.

Proof.

(i)

Since
F_{A}^{(1)}\in\mathcal{S}^{*}
( or
F_{A}^{(1)}\in\mathcal{S}_{*}
),
F_{A}^{(2)}\in\mathcal{L}
and by relation (
2.8
) is implied
that
\overline{F}_{A}^{(1)}(x)\asymp\overline{F}_{A}^{(2)}(x)
, we obtain
F_{A}^{(2)}\in\mathcal{S}^{*}
( or
F_{A}^{(2)}\in\mathcal{S}_{*}
), by
[
36
, Th. 2.1(b)]
(or,
[
48
, Lem. 3]
, respectively).

(ii)

Since
F_{A}^{(1)}\in\mathcal{S}^{*}
and by relation (
2.9
) follows
\overline{F}_{A}^{(1)}(x)\sim c\,\overline{F}_{A}^{(2)}(x)
, we obtain
F_{A}^{(2)}\in\mathcal{S}^{*}
by
[
25
, Cor. 3.26]
.

∎

Kesten’s inequality plays a crucial role in one dimensional set up, and facilitate several applications. In
[
66
]
, we find a multivariate analogue of this inequality, for the class
\mathcal{S}_{A}
. Here we give an alternative version of multivariate Kesten’s inequality for
\mathcal{S}_{A}^{*}
, having in mind the the one dimensional analogue which given in
[
20
, Th. 2]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
be some fixed set. If
{\bf X},\,{\bf X}^{(1)},\,\ldots,\,{\bf X}^{(n)}
are i.i.d. random vectors with common distribution
F\in\mathcal{S}_{A}^{*}
, then for any
c>\mu_{F_{A}}
, there exists some constant
K>0
, such that it holds

\displaystyle\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}^{(1)}+\cdots+{\bf X}^{(n)}\in x\,A\right]}{\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,A\right]}\leq\dfrac{K}{\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in c\,n\,A\right]}\,,

(2.10)

for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and all
x>0
.

Proof.

Since
{\bf X}
is non-negative, random vector, we find
\mu_{F_{A}}=\mathbf{E}[Y_{A}]\in(0,\,\infty)
. Hence by
[
66
, Lem. 4.9]
and by
[
20
, Th. 2, appl.]
we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}^{(1)}+\cdots+{\bf X}^{(n)}\in x\,A\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[Y_{A}^{(1)}+\cdots+Y_{A}^{(n)}>x\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{K\,\mathbf{P}\left[Y_{A}^{(1)}>x\right]}{\mathbf{P}\left[Y_{A}^{(1)}>c\,n\right]}=\dfrac{K\,\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,A\right]}{\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in c\,n\,A\right]}\,,

for all
x>0
, and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
∎

3.
Convolution and convolution roots properties

Here we put more attention on closure properties with respect to convolution of random vectors and with respect to convolution roots of random vectors. At first we consider the closure property with respect to convolution.

Let us start with independent random vectors
{\bf X}^{(1)},\,{\bf X}^{(2)}
with distributions
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
respectively. We define their convolution, over the set
x\,A
, as follows

\displaystyle F_{1}*F_{2}(x\,A)=\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}^{(1)}+{\bf X}^{(2)}\in x\,A\right]\,.

(3.1)

Hence, for some fixed
A\in\mathscr{R}
, we say that
F_{1}*F_{2}\in\mathcal{B}_{A}
, if it holds
F_{A}^{*}\in\mathcal{B}
, where the distribution
F_{A}^{*}
is defined as the distribution of

\displaystyle Y_{A}^{*}:=\sup\{u\;:\;{\bf X}^{(1)}+{\bf X}^{(2)}\in u\,A\}\,.

(3.2)

In
[
44
, Th. 3.4]
we find that if
F_{1},\,F_{2}\in\mathcal{L}_{A}
, then
F_{A}^{(1)}*F_{A}^{(2)}\in\mathcal{S}
if and only if
F_{1}*F_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
, namely the closure issue of multivariate convolution is reduced to the one-dimensional one, when the marginal distributions belong to
\mathcal{L}_{A}
. A similar result is true for the classes
\mathcal{S}_{A}^{*},\,\mathcal{S}_{*,A}
, as we can see below.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
be some fixed set, and
F_{1},\,F_{2}\in\mathcal{L}_{A}
.

(i)

F_{A}^{(1)}*F_{A}^{(2)}\in\mathcal{S}^{*}
if and only if
F_{1}*F_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{A}^{*}
.

(ii)

F_{A}^{(1)}*F_{A}^{(2)}\in\mathcal{S}_{*}
if and only if
F_{1}*F_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{*,A}
.

Proof.

We follow the same methodology for both parts, making only corresponding clarifications when needed. Let
\mathcal{B}\in\{\mathcal{S}^{*},\,\mathcal{S}_{*}\}
.

(\Longrightarrow)
. We suppose that
F_{A}^{(1)}*F_{A}^{(2)}\in\mathcal{B}
. So we have to show the inclusion
F_{A}^{*}\in\mathcal{B}
(recall also the relations (
3.1
) and (
3.2
)). Since
F_{A}^{(1)}*F_{A}^{(2)}\in\mathcal{B}\subsetneq\mathcal{S}
, and
F_{A}^{(1)},\,F_{A}^{(2)}\in\mathcal{L}
, by
[
51
, Th. 1.1]
is implied the max-sum equivalence

\displaystyle\overline{F_{A}^{(1)}*F_{A}^{(2)}}(x)\sim\overline{F}_{A}^{(1)}(x)+\overline{F}_{A}^{(2)}(x)\,.

(3.3)

From the other side, since
{\bf X}^{(1)},\,{\bf X}^{(2)}
are non-negative random vectors, and the set
A
is increasing, we obtain through Bonferroni inequality

\displaystyle\overline{F}_{A}^{*}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}[Y_{A}^{*}>x]=\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}^{(1)}+{\bf X}^{(2)}\in x\,A\right]\geq\mathbf{P}\left[\bigcup_{i=1}^{2}\{{\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\}\right]

(3.4)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}^{(1)}\in x\,A\right]+\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}^{(2)}\in x\,A\right]-\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}^{(1)}\in x\,A,\;{\bf X}^{(2)}\in x\,A\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[Y_{A}^{(1)}>x\right]+\mathbf{P}\left[Y_{A}^{(2)}>x\right]-\mathbf{P}\left[Y_{A}^{(1)}>x\right]\,\mathbf{P}\left[Y_{A}^{(2)}>x\right]

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[Y_{A}^{(1)}>x\right]+\mathbf{P}\left[Y_{A}^{(2)}>x\right]\,.

So, from relations (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) we find

\displaystyle\limsup\dfrac{\overline{F_{A}^{(1)}*F_{A}^{(2)}(x)}}{\overline{F}_{A}^{*}(x)}\leq\limsup\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left[Y_{A}^{(1)}>x\right]+\mathbf{P}\left[Y_{A}^{(2)}>x\right]}{\mathbf{P}\left[Y_{A}^{(1)}>x\right]+\mathbf{P}\left[Y_{A}^{(2)}>x\right]}=1\,.

(3.5)

From the other side, by
[
44
, Prop. 2.4]
, for all
x>0
we obtain

\displaystyle\dfrac{\overline{F}_{A}^{*}(x)}{\overline{F_{A}^{(1)}*F_{A}^{(2)}}(x)}\leq 1\,.

(3.6)

From relations (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), we conclude

\displaystyle\overline{F}_{A}^{*}(x)\sim\overline{F_{A}^{(1)}*F_{A}^{(2)}}(x)\,.

(3.7)

Next, for case
\mathcal{B}\equiv\mathcal{S}^{*}
, by (
3.7
), since
F_{A}^{(1)}*F_{A}^{(2)}\in\mathcal{S}^{*}
, it follows
F_{A}^{*}\in\mathcal{S}^{*}
, from
[
25
, Cor. 3.26]
. Therefore,
F_{1}*F_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{A}^{*}
.

For case
\mathcal{B}\equiv\mathcal{S}_{*}
, since
F_{A}^{(1)}*F_{A}^{(2)}\in\mathcal{S}_{*}\subsetneq\mathcal{S}
, by (
3.7
), it follows
F_{A}^{*}\in\mathcal{S}
, from the closure property with respect to strong tail equivalence of
\mathcal{S}
, see
[
70
, Th. 3]
. Therefore, from (
3.7
)
F_{A}^{*}\in\mathcal{S}
and
F_{A}^{(1)}*F_{A}^{(2)}\in\mathcal{S}_{*}
, via
[
48
, Lem. 3]
, we find
F_{A}^{*}\in\mathcal{S}_{*}
. Thus we find
F_{1}*F_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{*,A}

(\Longleftarrow)
. Let suppose that
F_{1}*F_{2}\in\mathcal{B}_{A}
, with
\mathcal{B}\in\{\mathcal{S}^{*},\,\mathcal{S}_{*}\}
. Then for
{Y_{A}^{*}}^{\prime}
independent and identical copy of
{Y_{A}^{*}}
, we obtain that since
{\bf X}^{(1)},\,{\bf X}^{(2)}
are non-negative, it holds

\displaystyle\limsup\dfrac{\overline{F_{A}^{(1)}*F_{A}^{(2)}}(x)}{\overline{F}_{A}^{*}(x)}=\limsup\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left[Y_{A}^{(1)}+Y_{A}^{(2)}>x\right]}{\mathbf{P}\left[Y_{A}^{*}>x\right]}\leq\limsup\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left[Y_{A}^{*}+{Y_{A}^{*}}^{\prime}>x\right]}{\mathbf{P}\left[Y_{A}^{*}>x\right]}=2\,,

(3.8)

where in the last step we use the inclusion
\mathcal{B}\subsetneq\mathcal{S}
. Hence, by relations (
3.6
) and (
3.8
), we find

\displaystyle\overline{F_{A}^{(1)}*F_{A}^{(2)}}\asymp\overline{F}_{A}^{*}(x)\,.

(3.9)

From
F_{A}^{(1)},\,F_{A}^{(2)}\in\mathcal{L}
it follows
F_{A}^{(1)}*F_{A}^{(2)}\in\mathcal{L}
, see in
[
21
, Th. 3(b)]
. Hence since
F_{A}^{*}\in\mathcal{B}
,
F_{A}^{(1)}*F_{A}^{(2)}\in\mathcal{L}
and relation (
3.9
) holds, we obtain
F_{A}^{(1)}*F_{A}^{(2)}\in\mathcal{B}
, by
[
36
, Th. 2.1(b)]
for class
\mathcal{S}^{*}
, and by
[
48
, Lem. 3]
for class
\mathcal{S}_{*}
.
∎

We note that in Theorem
3.1
can be reduced to whole class of sets
\mathscr{R}
under the next formulation:
F_{A}^{(1)}*F_{A}^{(2)}\in\mathcal{B}
, for all
A\in\mathscr{R}
if and only if
F_{1}*F_{2}\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathscr{R}}
. Furthermore, Theorem
3.1
assers that in
\mathcal{L}_{A}
the closure property with respect to convolution for classes
\mathcal{S}_{A}^{*},\,\mathcal{S}_{*,A}
is reduced to one-dimensional convolution closure properties. For further convolution properties of
\mathcal{S^{*}}
, see in
[
52
, Sec. 3.11]
. However, we do not know about the convolution closure properties of
\mathcal{S}_{*}
. In
[
39
, Th. 2]
was shown that class
\mathcal{S}^{*}
does not satisfy the closure property with respect to convolution, from where it follows the next result.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
. Class
\mathcal{S}_{A}^{*}
is not closed with respect to convolution. Namely, we can find two distributions
F_{1},\,F_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{A}^{*}
, such that it holds
F_{1}*F_{2}\not\in\mathcal{S}_{A}^{*}
.

The next corollary discloses that class
\mathcal{S}_{A}^{*}
is closed with respect to convolution power. Although it is elementary conclusion in comparison with the convolution, can be useful later use. Let remind that
F^{n*}(x\,A)=\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}^{(1)}+\cdots+{\bf X}^{(n)}\in x\,A\right]
, when the random vectors
{\bf X}^{(1)},\,\cdots,\,{\bf X}^{(n)}
are i.i.d. with common distribution
F
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
. If
F\in\mathcal{S}_{A}^{*}
, then for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
it holds
F^{n*}\in\mathcal{S}_{A}^{*}
.

Proof.

Since
F\in\mathcal{S}_{A}^{*}\subsetneq\mathcal{S}_{A}
, by
[
66
, Cor. 4.10]
it holds
F^{n*}(x\,A)\sim n\,F(x\,A)
, hence, from Proposition
2.1
(ii), we obtain
F^{n*}\in\mathcal{S}_{A}^{*}
.
∎

Now we consider the closure property of the two new classes with respect to convolution roots. For some fixed set
A\in\mathscr{R}
, we say that the class
\mathcal{B}_{A}
is closed with respect to convolution roots, if from inclusion
F^{n*}\in\mathcal{B}_{A}
, for some integer
n\geq 2
, it is implied that
F\in\mathcal{B}_{A}
. If instead of
A
we take the whole class of sets
\mathscr{R}
, then from
F^{n*}\in\mathcal{B}_{A}
, for some integer
n\geq 2
and any
A\in\mathscr{R}
, the conclusion is
F\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathscr{R}}
. It is also easy to see that the result of Proposition
3.1
, below, holds also in the case of classes
\mathcal{B}_{\mathscr{R}}
, instead of
\mathcal{B}_{A}
.

In one-dimensional set up we find relative results in
[
22
]
,
[
74
]
,
[
75
]
,
[
73
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
76
]
among others. In
[
47
, Th. 3.2]
, we find that class
\mathcal{S}_{A}
has the closure property with respect to convolution roots, under the condition that
F\in\mathcal{L}_{A}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
be some fixed set.

(i)

If
F^{n*}\in\mathcal{S}_{A}^{*}
, for some integer
n\geq 2
, and
F\in\mathcal{L}_{A}
, then
F\in\mathcal{S}_{A}^{*}
.

(ii)

If
F^{n*}\in\mathcal{S}_{*,A}
, for some integer
n\geq 2
, and
F\in\mathcal{L}_{A}
, then
F\in\mathcal{S}_{*,A}
.

Proof.

Let consider
\mathcal{B}\in\{\mathcal{S}^{*},\,\mathcal{S}_{*}\}
. Since
F^{n*}\in\mathcal{B}_{A}\subsetneq\mathcal{S}_{A}
and
F\in\mathcal{L}_{A}
, by
[
47
, Th. 3.2(i)]
we obtain
F\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
. Hence, from
[
66
, Cor. 4.10]
we find
F^{n*}(x\,A)\sim n\,F(x\,A)
, therefore, from Proposition
2.1
, is implied that
F\in\mathcal{B}_{A}
.
∎

4.
Single big jump principle in randomly stopped sums

Now we examine the asymptotic behavior of the probability

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf S}_{\tau}\in x\,A\right]\,,

(4.1)

for some fixed set
A\in\mathscr{R}
, with
{\bf S}_{\tau}
the multivariate randomly stopped sum in relation (
1.1
) that follows distribution
F_{{\bf S}_{\tau}}
. Let us make clear, that in this section,
\tau
is a discrete, one-dimensional random variable, non-degenerate to zero, and the
\{{\bf X}^{(i)}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
are i.i.d. copies of the vector
{\bf X}
, whose distribution is
F
. Additionally,
\tau
is independent of
\{{\bf X}^{(i)}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
. Our goal is to establish the single big jump principle of randomly stopped sum from (
1.1
), namely relation (
1.2
), under weaker conditions for the variable
\tau
, in comparison with subexponential case in
[
44
, Th. 4.2]
. Restricting
F
in class
\mathcal{S}_{A}^{*}
instead of
\mathcal{S}_{A}
, we relax the condition

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[(1+\varepsilon)^{\tau}\right]<\infty\,,

for some
\varepsilon>0
, in relation (
4.2
). Let us observe, that in case
d=1
, with
A=(1,\,\infty)\in\mathscr{R}
, the asymptotic behavior of (
4.1
), in the presence of heavy tails, as well as the distribution class of the randomly stopped sum was studied in several papers, see for example
[
8
]
,
[
1
]
,
[
20
]
,
[
50
]
,
[
67
]
among others.

In multidimensional case, the results are much sparse. Next result contains the main result of the section and it is inspired by
[
20
, Th. 1(ii)]
. We notice that if
d=1
and
A=(1,\,\infty)
it coincides with
[
20
, Th. 1(ii)]
, in the non-negative case.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
, be some fixed set. If
F\in\mathcal{S}_{A}^{*}
and there exists some
c>\mu_{F_{A}}
, such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[c\,\tau>x\right]=o\left(\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,A\right]\right)\,,

(4.2)

and further
\mathbf{E}[\tau]<\infty
, then relation (
1.2
) is true, and further it holds
F_{{\bf S}_{\tau}}\in\mathcal{S}_{A}^{*}
.

Proof.

Since we consider non-negative
{\bf X}
, we obtain
\mu_{F_{A}}=\mathbf{E}\left[Y_{A}\right]
. Hence, for any
x>0
it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf S}_{\tau}\in x\,A\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}^{(1)}+\cdots+{\bf X}^{(n)}\in x\,A\right]\,\mathbf{P}[\tau=n]

(4.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left[\sup_{{\bf p}\in I_{A}}{\bf p}^{\top}\,\left({\bf X}^{(1)}+\cdots+{\bf X}^{(n)}\right)>x\right]\,\mathbf{P}[\tau=n]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left[Y_{A}^{(1)}+\cdots+Y_{A}^{(n)}>x\right]\,\mathbf{P}[\tau=n]=\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=0}^{\tau}Y_{A}^{(i)}>x\right]

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}[\tau]\,\mathbf{P}\left[Y_{A}>x\right]=\mathbf{E}[\tau]\,\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,A\right]\,,

where in pre-last step we apply
[
20
, Th. 1(ii)]
. So, through (
4.3
) we obtain the upper bound for relation (
1.2
).

For the lower bound, for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, because of the fact that
{\bf X}
is non-negative and the set
A
is increasing, through the Bonferroni inequality we find that it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf S}_{n}\in x\,A\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\{{\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\}\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right]-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\,,\;{\bf X}^{(j)}\in x\,A\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\,\mathbf{P}\left[Y_{A}>x\right]-n\,(n-1)\,\left(\mathbf{P}\left[Y_{A}>x\right]\right)^{2}\sim n\,\mathbf{P}\left[Y_{A}>x\right]=n\,\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,A\right]\,.

Therefore we find

\displaystyle\liminf\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf S}_{n}\in x\,A\right]}{n\,\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,A\right]}\geq 1\,,

(4.4)

hence via Fatou’s Lemma, since
\mathbf{E}[\tau]<\infty
, it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf S}_{\tau}\in x\,A\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf S}_{n}\in x\,A\right]\,\mathbf{P}[\tau=n]

(4.5)

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n\,\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,A\right]\,\mathbf{P}[\tau=n]=\mathbf{E}[\tau]\,\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,A\right]\,,

from where, in combination with (
4.3
), is implied (
1.2
). Further from relation (
1.2
), the inclusion
F\in\mathcal{S}_{A}^{*}
, by Proposition
2.1
(ii), we obtain
F_{{\bf S}_{\tau}}\in\mathcal{S}_{A}^{*}
.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

We observe that relation (
4.5
) holds for any distribution
F
, not only from class
\mathcal{S}^{*}
, and independently of relation (
4.2
). Namely, under the only condition
\mathbf{E}[\tau]<\infty
we find

\displaystyle\liminf\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf S}_{\tau}\in x\,A\right]}{\mathbf{E}[\tau]\,\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,A\right]}\geq 1\,.

From the few papers on the topic of multivariate randomly stopped sums, the most focus on the class
MRV
.
[
18
]
focuses on this topic, with strict enough conditions for the random variable
\tau
, that has been relaxed in
[
44
, Th. 4.3(iii)]
(but only for sets
A\in\mathscr{R}
).

The next corollary, relaxes further the conditions on
\tau
, through relation (
4.2
), however here we consider independent vectors. In the following result we obtain an more explicit relation, than this from (
1.2
).

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
, be some fixed set. If
F\in MRV(\alpha,\,V,\,\mu)
, with
\alpha>1
, and there exists some
c>\alpha
, such that relation (
4.2
) is valid, and furthermore it holds
\mathbf{E}[\tau]<\infty
, then we find

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf S}_{\tau}\in x\,A\right]\sim\mathbf{E}[\tau]\,\mu(A)\,\overline{V}(x)\,.

(4.6)

Proof.

Firstly, since
F\in MRV(\alpha,\,V,\,\mu)
, with
\alpha>1
, we notice that
\mu_{F_{A}}=\mathbf{E}\left[Y_{A}\right]<\infty
. Further, from the proof of
[
66
, Prop. 4.14]
, it follows that for all
A\in\mathscr{R}
it holds
\mu(\partial A)=0
and
\mu(A)\in(0,\,\infty)
. Thus from Proposition
4.1
we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf S}_{\tau}\in x\,A\right]\sim\mathbf{E}[\tau]\,\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,A\right]\sim\mathbf{E}[\tau]\,\mu(A)\,\overline{V}(x)\,.

∎

Remark 4.2
.

In the previous corollary, if
{\bf S}_{\tau}^{A}:=\sup\left\{u\;:\;{\bf S}_{\tau}\in u\,A\right\}
with distribution
F_{S_{\tau}}^{A}
, then by (
4.6
) we find that
F_{S_{\tau}}^{A}\in\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}
. However, from this is not necessary implied that
F\in MRV(\alpha,\,V,\,\mu)
.

5.
Vector type precise large deviations principle

Here we study asymptotic expressions for the precise large deviations of non-random and random sums in relations (
1.3
), that means we focus on asymptotic behavior of the probabilities

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf S}_{n}\in x\,A\right]\,,\qquad\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf S}_{N(t)}\in x\,A\right]\,,

as
n\to\infty
, or as
t\to\infty
, respectively. In this section we consider the
\{{\bf X}^{(i)}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
as i.i.d. copies of the vector
{\bf X}
, whose distribution is
F
. Additionally in case of random sums we suppose that the counting process
\{N(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
is independent of
\{{\bf X}^{(i)}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
.

The precise large deviations principle of random sums in one-dimensional set up with
A=(1,\,\infty)
, is a well studied topic, with wide spectrum of appications, see
[
37
]
,
[
68
]
,
[
72
]
,
[
58
]
,
[
32
]
among others.

In multivariate set up, the most of the existing papers examine either the joint distribution tail or the sum of
d
-lines of business, that means with
A_{1}
from relation (
2.6
), see for example
[
71
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
28
]
, usually is called sum-type precise large deviations principle. For large deviations under
MRV
and their applications, we refer
[
34
]
and
[
6
]
.

In the first main result we find uniform asymptotic estimation for the precise large deviations of non-random sums. It is worth to notice that this result even in one-dimensional subcase with
A=(1,\,\infty)
, generalizes previous publications, see in Remark
5.1
below. We note that, the function
h
, is the insensitivity function of distribution
F_{A}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let some fixed set
A\in\mathscr{R}
. If
F\in\mathcal{S}_{*,A}
, then it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf S}_{n}\in x\,A\right]\sim n\,\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,A\right]\,,

(5.1)

as
n\to\infty
, uniformly for
x\geq h^{\leftarrow}\left[n\left(\mu_{F_{A}}+1\right)\right]
.

Proof.

Let us start with estimation of the upper bound in (
5.1
). From
[
66
, Lem. 4.9]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf S}_{n}\in x\,A\right]\leq\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}Y_{A}^{(i)}>x\right]\,,

(5.2)

for any
x>0
and any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

We define a sequence of i.i.d. random variables
\left\{\widetilde{Y}_{A}^{(i)}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\right\}
with common distribution
\widehat{F}_{A}
, where

\displaystyle\widetilde{Y}_{A}=Y_{A}-\mu_{F_{A}}-1\,,

with mean

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[\widetilde{Y}_{A}\right]=-1\,.

We observe that since
F_{A}\in\mathcal{S}_{*}\subsetneq\mathcal{L}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\overline{\widehat{F}}_{A}(x)\sim\overline{F}_{A}(x)\,,

and hence
\widehat{F}_{A}\in\mathcal{S}_{*}\subsetneq\mathcal{S}
, because
F_{A}\in\mathcal{S}_{*}
and by closure property of class
\mathcal{S}
with respect to strong tail equivalence we find
\widehat{F}_{A}\in\mathcal{S}
, and from
[
48
, Lem. 3]
it is implied this result.

Now, from (
5.2
) in first step and
[
48
, Th.]
in fourth step, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf S}_{n}\in x\,A\right]\leq\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}Y_{A}^{(i)}>x\right]=\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\widetilde{Y}_{A}^{(i)}>x-n\,\mu_{F_{A}}-n\right]

(5.3)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{P}\left[\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\widetilde{Y}_{A}^{(i)}>x-n\,\mu_{F_{A}}-n\right]\sim\dfrac{1}{\left|\mathbf{E}\left[\widetilde{Y}_{A}\right]\right|}\int_{x-n\,\mu_{F_{A}}-n}^{x-n\,\mu_{F_{A}}-n+n\,\left|\mathbf{E}\left[\widetilde{Y}_{A}\right]\right|}\overline{\widehat{F}}_{A}(y)\,dy

\displaystyle=\int_{x-n\,\mu_{F_{A}}-n}^{x-n\,\mu_{F_{A}}}\overline{F}_{A}(y+\mu_{F_{A}}+1)\,dy\leq n\,\overline{F}_{A}(x-n\,\mu_{F_{A}}-n+\mu_{F_{A}}+1)

\displaystyle\leq n\,\overline{F}_{A}\left(x-n\,\left(\mu_{F_{A}}+1\right)\right)\leq n\,\overline{F}_{A}(x-h(x))\sim n\,\overline{F}_{A}(x)=n\,\mathbf{P}[{\bf X}\in x\,A]\,,

as
x\to\infty
, where in the eighth step we use the assumption
x\geq h^{\leftarrow}\left[n\left(\mu_{F_{A}}+1\right)\right]
. Hence, from (
5.3
) we conclude

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x\geq h^{\leftarrow}\left[n\left(\mu_{F_{A}}+1\right)\right]}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf S}_{n}\in x\,A\right]}{n\,\mathbf{P}[{\bf X}\in x\,A]}\leq 1\,.

(5.4)

Next, we proceed to the lower bound for relation (
5.1
). We should mention that it follows easily by
[
47
, Th. 6.1]
, taking into account that

\displaystyle\mu_{F_{A}}=\mathbf{E}[Y_{A}]<\infty\,,

and
[
32
, Rem. 2.2]
. However, for sake of completeness we provide an alternative approach.

Since,
{\bf X}
is non-negative and the set
A
is increasing, we obtain through Bonferroni’s inequality, that it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf S}_{n}\in x\,A\right]\geq\mathbf{P}\left[\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\{{\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\}\right]\geq

(5.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right]-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\,,\;{\bf X}^{(j)}\in x\,A\right]=:n\,\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,A\right]-J_{1}(x,n)\,.

for any
x>0
and any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

We can see that since
\mu_{F_{A}}=\mathbf{E}[Y_{A}]<\infty
, it follows that
x\,\mathbf{P}\left[Y_{A}>x\right]\to 0
, as
x\to\infty
, which further implies that for any
\delta>0
, there exists some sufficiently large
x_{0}=x_{0}(\delta)
, such that it holds

\displaystyle x\,\mathbf{P}[Y_{A}>x]<\delta\,,

for any
x\geq x_{0}
. Also, because
h(x)=o(x)
, as
x\to\infty
, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists some sufficiently large
x^{*}_{0}=x^{*}_{0}(\epsilon)
, such that it holds
h(x)/x<\epsilon
, for any
x\geq x^{*}_{0}
. Hence, for any
x\geq x_{0}\vee x^{*}_{0}
it holds

\displaystyle J_{1}(x,n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\,,\;{\bf X}^{(j)}\in x\,A\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=2}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}^{(j)}\in x\,A\right]\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\;\big|\;{\bf X}^{(j)}\in x\,A\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=2}^{n}\dfrac{1}{x}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}^{(j)}\in x\,A\right]\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}x\,\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right]<\delta\sum_{j=2}^{n}\dfrac{n-1}{x}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}^{(j)}\in x\,A\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\delta\sum_{j=2}^{n}\dfrac{n\,\left(\mu_{F_{A}}+1\right)}{x}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}^{(j)}\in x\,A\right]\leq\delta\epsilon\,n\,\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,A\right]\,,

where in the pre-last step we use the fact that
\mu_{F_{A}}>0
, and in last step we take in consideration that
x\geq h^{\leftarrow}\left[n\left(\mu_{F_{A}}+1\right)\right]
. Hence, from the last relation we can take
\delta\downarrow 0
,
\epsilon\downarrow 0
, to conclude

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x\geq h^{\leftarrow}\left[n\left(\mu_{F_{A}}+1\right)\right]}\dfrac{J_{1}(x,n)}{n\,\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,A\right]}=0\,.

(5.6)

From relations (
5.5
), (
5.6
) we obtain

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{x\geq h^{\leftarrow}\left[n\left(\mu_{F_{A}}+1\right)\right]}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf S}_{n}\in x\,A\right]}{n\,\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,A\right]}\geq 1\,.

(5.7)

Therefore, from relations (
5.4
), (
5.7
) we get the desired result.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

In the subcase
d=1
, with
A=(1,\,\infty)
the previous result generalizes
[
59
, Prop. 2.4]
, since it used class
\mathcal{S}_{*}
instead of
\mathcal{S}^{*}
, and any random variables with positive mean instead of
\mu_{F_{A}}\geq 1
. However, in the previous result we are restricted with respect range of uniformity, over any
x\geq h^{\leftarrow}\left[n\left(\mu_{F_{A}}+1\right)\right]
instead over any
x\geq h^{\leftarrow}\left(n\,\mu_{F_{A}}\right)
. Furthermore, it is easy to see that
[
59
, Th. 2.2]
holds not only on class
\mathcal{S}^{*}
but also on
\mathcal{S}_{*}

Now, we examine precise large deviations for the random sums
S_{N(t)}
, where the counting process
\{N(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
has mean
\lambda(t)=\mathbf{E}[N(t)]<\infty
, for any
t\geq 0
, with
\lambda(t)\to\infty
, as
t\to\infty
. Counting processes
\{N(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
is assumed to be independent of
\{{\bf X}^{(i)}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
and satisfies the following two common conditions, for the one-dimensional precise large deviations, see
[
37
]
,
[
4
]
among others.

Assumption 5.1
.

It holds

\displaystyle\dfrac{N(t)}{\lambda(t)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P}}{{\rightarrow}}1\,,

as
t\to\infty
, where
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P}}{{\rightarrow}}
denotes the convergence in probability.

Assumption 5.2
.

For any
\delta>0
let assume that there exists some
\varepsilon>0
, such that it holds

\displaystyle\sum_{n>\left\lfloor(1+\delta)\,\lambda(t)\right\rfloor}(1+\varepsilon)^{n}\mathbf{P}\left[N(t)=n\right]\to 0\,,

as
t\to\infty
.

In fact, Assumption
5.2
is slightly modified in comparison with the classical assumptions in one-dimensional precise large deviations, where the sum is over
n>(1+\delta)\,\lambda(t)
. This modification is due to interest on precise large deviations with respect to
\left\lfloor\lambda(t)\right\rfloor
instead of with respect to
\lambda(t)
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let some fixed set
A\in\mathscr{R}
. If
F\in\mathcal{S}_{*,A}
, and the counting process
\{N(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
is independent of
\{{\bf X}^{(i)}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
and satisfies Assumptions
5.1
and
5.2
, then it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf S}_{N(t)}\in x\,A\right]\sim\left\lfloor\lambda(t)\right\rfloor\,\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,A\right]\,,

(5.8)

as
t\to\infty
, uniformly for
x\geq h^{\leftarrow}\left[\left\lfloor\lambda(t)\right\rfloor\,\left(\mu_{F_{A}}+1\right)\right]
.

Proof.

We start with the upper bound of (
5.8
). At first, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf S}_{N(t)}\in x\,A\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\left\lfloor\lambda(t)\,\left(1+\delta\right)\right\rfloor}+\sum_{n>\left\lfloor\lambda(t)\,\left(1+\delta\right)\right\rfloor}\right)\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf S}_{n}\in x\,A\right]\,\mathbf{P}[N(t)=n]

(5.9)

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle I_{1}(x,\,t,\,n)+I_{2}(x,\,t,\,n)\,,

for any
x>0
,
t>0
,
\delta>0
.

For the estimation of
I_{2}(x,\,t,\,n)
, we apply
[
66
, Prop. 4.12(c)]
, to find that for any
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\delta)>0
there exists some constant
K>0
, such that it holds

\displaystyle I_{2}(x,\,t,\,n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n>\left\lfloor\lambda(t)\,\left(1+\delta\right)\right\rfloor}\,\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf S}_{n}\in x\,A\right]\,\mathbf{P}[N(t)=n]

(5.10)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K\,\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,A\right]\sum_{n>\left\lfloor\lambda(t)\,\left(1+\delta\right)\right\rfloor}(1+\varepsilon)^{n}\,\mathbf{P}[N(t)=n]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle o\left(\left\lfloor\lambda(t)\right\rfloor\,\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,A\right]\right)\,,

as
t\to\infty
, for any
x>0
, where in the last step we used Assumption
5.2
.

For the estimation of
I_{1}(x,\,t,\,n)
, we apply Theorem
5.1
, to find that it holds

\displaystyle I_{1}(x,\,t,\,n)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{\left\lfloor\lambda(t)\,\left(1+\delta\right)\right\rfloor}{\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right]\,\sum_{n=1}^{\left\lfloor\lambda(t)\,\left(1+\delta\right)\right\rfloor}\mathbf{P}[N(t)=n]\leq\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{\left\lfloor\lambda(t)\,\left(1+\delta\right)\right\rfloor}{\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right]

(5.11)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\left\lfloor\lambda(t)\,\left(1+\delta\right)\right\rfloor\,\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,A\right]\,,

as
t\to\infty
, uniformly for
x\geq h^{\leftarrow}\left[\left\lfloor\lambda(t)(1+\delta)\right\rfloor\,\left(\mu_{F_{A}}+1\right)\right]
. Hence, by (
5.11
), letting
\delta\downarrow 0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\sup_{x\geq h^{\leftarrow}\left[\left\lfloor\lambda(t)\right\rfloor\,\left(\mu_{F_{A}}+1\right)\right]}\dfrac{I_{1}(x,\,t,\,n)}{\left\lfloor\lambda(t)\right\rfloor\,\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,A\right]}\leq 1\,.

(5.12)

So, by relations (
5.10
), (
5.12
), together with (
5.9
), we reach to upper asymptotic bound for (
5.8
).

For the lower asymptotic bound for (
5.8
), it follows directly from
[
47
, Th. 6.2]
, in combination with condition
\mu_{F_{A}}<\infty
, and
[
32
, Rem. 2.2]
. However, for sake of completeness we present an alternative approach.

Let
\delta>0
. Then from Assumption
5.1
, for any
\varepsilon>0
we can find some
t^{*}>0
sufficiently large, such that it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[\left|\dfrac{N(t)}{\lambda(t)}-1\right|<\delta\right]\geq 1-\varepsilon\,,

for any
t\geq t^{*}
. Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf S}_{N(t)}\in x\,A\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=\left\lfloor\lambda(t)\,\left(1-\delta\right)\right\rfloor}^{\left\lfloor\lambda(t)\,\left(1+\delta\right)\right\rfloor}\,\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf S}_{n}\in x\,A\right]\,\mathbf{P}[N(t)=n]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf S}_{\left\lfloor\lambda(t)\,\left(1-\delta\right)\right\rfloor}\in x\,A\right]\,\mathbf{P}\left[\left|\dfrac{N(t)}{\lambda(t)}-1\right|<\delta\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon)\,\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf S}_{\left\lfloor\lambda(t)\,\left(1-\delta\right)\right\rfloor}\in x\,A\right]\,,

for any
x>0
and any
t\geq t^{*}
. Consequently, by relation (
5
), through Theorem
5.1
, we find

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf S}_{N(t)}\in x\,A\right]\gtrsim(1-\varepsilon)\,\left\lfloor\lambda(t)\,\left(1-\delta\right)\right\rfloor\,\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,A\right]\,,

as
t\to\infty
, uniformly for
x\geq h^{\leftarrow}\left[\left\lfloor\lambda(t)(1-\delta)\right\rfloor\,\left(\mu_{F_{A}}+1\right)\right]
. This way, from the arbitrary choice of
\varepsilon>0
and
\delta>0
, letting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
and
\delta\downarrow 0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to\infty}\inf_{x\geq h^{\leftarrow}\left[\left\lfloor\lambda(t)\right\rfloor\,\left(\mu_{F_{A}}+1\right)\right]}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf S}_{N(t)}\in x\,A\right]}{\left\lfloor\lambda(t)\right\rfloor\,\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,A\right]}\geq 1\,,

that provides the desired lower asymptotic bound of (
5.8
).
∎

Remark 5.2
.

If in Theorem
5.1
we restrict the condition
F\in\mathcal{S}_{*,A}
into
F\in MRV(\alpha,\,V,\,\mu)
, with
\alpha>1
, then relation (
5.1
) is reduced to

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf S}_{n}\in x\,A\right]\sim n\,\mu(A)\,\overline{V}(x)\,,

(5.13)

uniformly for
x\geq h^{\leftarrow}\left(n\,\left(\mu_{F_{A}}+1\right)\,\right)
, see a similar proof in Corollary
4.1
. Correspondingly, in Theorem
5.2
, if we restrict ourselves into
MRV(\alpha,\,V,\,\mu)
, with
\alpha>1
, then relation (
5.8
) is reduced to

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf S}_{n}\in x\,A\right]\sim\left\lfloor\lambda(t)\right\rfloor\,\mu(A)\,\overline{V}(x)\,,

(5.14)

uniformly for
x\geq h^{\leftarrow}\left(\left\lfloor\lambda(t)\right\rfloor\,\left(\mu_{F_{A}}+1\right)\,\right)
. Relations (

), (

), in comparison with relations (

), (

), have the advantage that the dependence of the components of

, are described completely by
\mu
and the tail decay of
{\bf S}_{n}
driven by

, which is one of the most important reasons of the domination of

in literature. According to our knowledge, the relations (

), (

), in these general forms are not established yet, except only under concrete dependence structures among the components of

, and for concrete forms of sets

.

6.
Nonstandard multivariate risk model

In this section we consider a nonstandard multivariate risk model, that means the counting process
\{N(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
is NOT necessarily renewal. Even though in one-dimensional risk models this set up is still not popular, no matter its theoretical implications or the wider spectrum of practical applications. We mention the
[
78
]
, for the one-dimensional set up and
[
12
]
,
[
27
]
, for the two-dimensional set up. In these three papers, the distribution class for the claim is the
\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L}
, while they consider a quasi-renewal process, namely weakly dependent
inter-arrival times, that are identically distributed.

Form the other side, the multivariate risk models attracted the attention of several researchers, using mostly i.i.d. claim vectors following distribution
F\in MRV(\alpha,\,V,\,\mu)
(under the condition
\mu((1,\infty]\times,...,\times(1,\infty])>0
, which indicates asymptotic dependence and regular variation for the components of claim vector). For renewal risk models we refer to
[
40
]
,
[
54
]
,
[
77
]
,
[
13
]
, where consider also financial risks.

For distribution classes of claim vectors, larger than
MRV
, we mention
[
66
]
, where we find the examination of renewal risk model without interest force, and are provided asymptotic expressions for the infinite-time ruin probability, when the multivariate integrated tail distribution of claim vectors belongs to class
\mathcal{S}_{A}
. In
[
44
]
was established the asymptotic behavior of the discounted aggregate claims over a finite-horizon interval, in the classical risk model with claim distributions from class
\mathcal{S}_{A}
and with a cádlág process for the financial risks.
In the same way in
[
45
]
, with claim distributions from the class
(\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L})_{A}
in renewal risk model, with weak dependence among claim vectors, were obtained uniform asymptotic expressions for the ruin probability over a finite-horizon interval.

Next, we establish a local uniform asymptotic expression, for the entrance probability of discounted aggregate claims into some rare-sets, from where follows directly the ruin probability, in a nonstandard risk model with claim distributions from class
\mathcal{S}_{A}^{*}
. We assume that the counting process
\{N(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
, has in some sense lighter distribution tail than that of
F_{A}
, see Assumption
6.2
below.

We start with an analytic representation of the risk model. We suppose that an insurer operates simultaneously
d
-lines of business, with
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
, which share a common counting process of claim-arrivals. The initial capital of the insurer is
x>0
and it is allocated into the
d
-lines through a deterministic vector
{\bf l}
, with
l_{1},\,\ldots,\,l_{d}>0
and

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}l_{i}=1\,.

We assume that the insurer can invest his surplus into risk free investment, with constant interest force
r\geq 0
. The case
r=0
corresponds to no investment set up.

Furthermore, we consider that the insurer receives premiums, whose densities are represented by the vector
{\bf p}(t)=(p_{1}(t),\,\ldots,\,p_{d}(t))
, for any
t\geq 0
, with
p_{i}(t)
denoting the premium density for the
i
-th line of business, for
i=1,\,\ldots,\,d
. For them we adopt the convention
0\leq p_{i}(t)\leq\Lambda_{i}
, for any
t\geq 0
and for some non-negative constants
\Lambda_{i}
, for
i=1,\,\ldots,\,d
. The
i
-th claim vector
{\bf X}^{(i)}=(X_{1}^{(i)},\,\ldots,\,X_{d}^{(i)})
is non-negative and it can contain zero components (but not all of them). The arrival of
{\bf X}^{(i)}
happens at time

\tau_{i}
, with
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Let put
\tau_{0}=0
, then the sequence
\{\tau_{i}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
constitute a counting process
\{N(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
, defined as follows

\displaystyle N(t):=\sup\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}\;:\;\tau_{n}\leq t\}\,,

(6.1)

for any
t\geq 0
, that by convention has finite mean value

\displaystyle\lambda(t):=\mathbf{E}[N(t)]=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}[\tau_{i}\leq t]<\infty\,,

(6.2)

for any
t\geq 0
. As we mentioned, the counting process
\{N(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
is not necessarily renewal process. More concretely, we consider that the sequence of inter-arrival times
\{\theta_{i}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
, where
\theta_{i}=\tau_{i}-\tau_{i-1}
, for any
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, represent sequence of independent but not necessarily identically distributed, positive random variables.

This way, the discounted surplus process for the insurer,
{\bf U}(t)
, at time
t\geq 0
, can be written in the form

\displaystyle{\bf U}(t):=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}U_{1}(t)\\
\vdots\\
U_{d}(t)\end{array}\right)=x\,\left(\begin{array}[]{c}l_{1}\\
\vdots\\
l_{d}\end{array}\right)+\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\int_{0}^{t}p_{1}(s)e^{-rs}ds\\
\vdots\\
\int_{0}^{t}p_{d}(s)e^{-rs}ds\end{array}\right)-\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\sum_{i=1}^{N(t)}X_{1}^{(i)}e^{-r\tau_{i}}\\
\vdots\\
\sum_{i=1}^{N(t)}X_{d}^{(i)}e^{-r\tau_{i}}\end{array}\right)\,.

Next, we formulate our first Assumption. The independence among the claims, the premiums and the counting process
\{N(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
is quite common either in one-dimensional or in multidimensional risk model, as the condition of i.i.d. claims as well. However, several times the independence between claims and counting process, or the condition of i.i.d. claims seems very restrictive, see for example in
[
54
]
,
[
45
]
for more discussions.

Assumption 6.1
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
be a fixed set. We assume that the vectors
\left\{{\bf X}^{(k)}\,,\;k\in{\mathbb{N}}\right\}
are i.i.d. with common distribution
F\in\mathcal{S}_{A}^{*}
. We assume that the
\left\{{\bf X}^{(i)}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\right\}
,
\{N(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
and
\{{\bf p}(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
are mutually independent. Additionally, we assume that the sequence of inter-arrival times
\{\theta_{i}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
has independent, positive terms.

The assumption that the
\{\theta_{i}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
are independent but not necessarily identically distributed, escape by a different way from the standard risk models, than the usual one, where are used quasi renewal processes. Under this condition the process
\{N(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
can become either renewal (but not quasi renewal, due to independence of the
\{\theta_{i}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
), or a non-homogeneous renewal process, see for example in
[
5
]
.

Now, we introduce the delayed counting process
\{N^{*}(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
as follows

\displaystyle N^{*}(t)=\sup\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}\;:\;\tau_{n}^{*}\leq t\}\,,

(6.16)

for any
t\geq 0
, where

\displaystyle\tau_{n}^{*}=\sum_{i=2}^{n+1}\theta_{i}\,,

(6.17)

for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. From relation (
6.16
) in combination with (
6.2
) we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda^{*}(t)=\mathbf{E}[N^{*}(t)]=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}[\tau_{i}^{*}\leq t]<\infty\,,

(6.18)

for any
t\geq 0
. This way we can formulate the following assumption for delayed counting process
\{N^{*}(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
, reminding the mean
\mu_{F_{A}}=\mathbf{E}{Y_{A}}
.

Assumption 6.2
.

Let
\{N^{*}(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
be a delayed counting process, described through relation (
6.16
). We assume that for some constant
c>\mu_{F_{A}}
and some constant
T^{*}>0
, it holds

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}[N^{*}(t)\geq n-1]}{\mathbf{P}[{\bf X}\in c\,n\,A]}<\infty\,,

(6.19)

for any
t\in[0,\,T^{*}]
.

Remark 6.1
.

From the relations (
6.16
), (
6.17
) can be implied that (
6.19
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}[\theta_{2}+\cdots+\theta_{n}\leq t]}{\mathbf{P}[{\bf X}\in c\,n\,A]}<\infty\,,

(6.20)

for any
t\in[0,\,T^{*}]
. Further, Assumption
6.2
implies that the tail of the
\{N(t)\,,\;t\in[0,\,T^{*}]\}
, is lighter than the tail of
F_{A}
, in the sense it holds

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}[N(t)\geq n]}{\mathbf{P}[Y_{A}>c\,n]}<\infty\,.

Indeed, since by relation (
6.20
) we obtain that it holds

\displaystyle\infty>\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}[\theta_{2}+\cdots+\theta_{n}\leq t]}{\mathbf{P}[{\bf X}\in c\,n\,A]}\geq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}[\theta_{1}+\cdots+\theta_{n}\leq t]}{\mathbf{P}[{\bf X}\in c\,n\,A]}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}[\tau_{n}\leq t]}{\mathbf{P}[Y_{A}>c\,n]}\,,

for any
t\in[0,\,T^{*}]
.

As in one-dimensional case, the asymptotic behavior of discounted aggregate claims, as for some rare-set
A
, see Remark
6.2
below, implies quite easily the ruin probability, as entrance to some ruin-sets. In risk model (
6
), we denote the last term of the right-hand side, namely the discounted aggregate claims up to moment
t\geq 0
, as

\displaystyle{\bf D}_{r}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{N(t)}{\bf X}^{(i)}e^{-r\tau_{i}}\,,

and we are interested for asymptotic estimation of
\mathbf{P}[{\bf D}_{r}(t)\in x\,A]
, as
x\to\infty
, for any
t\leq T
. Namely, the set
x\,A
is understood as rare-set and the estimations are provided uniformly for
t\in\Lambda_{T}
, with
\Lambda_{T}:=\Lambda\cap[0,\,T]
, where
T>0
some fixed constant and
\Lambda=\{t\;:\;\lambda(t)>0\}
.

Remark 6.2
.

The set
A\in\mathscr{R}
can take two main forms, from the aspect to insurance practice. The first form, is described in relation (
2.6
), from where it follows that the sum of discounted aggregate claims of the
d
-lines of business, exceed the initial capital. It can be connected with the ruin probability ’
\psi_{sum}
’.

The second form can be represented as

\displaystyle A_{2}=\{{\bf x}\;:\;x_{i}>b_{i}\,,\;\exists\;i=1,\,\ldots,\,d\}\,,

(6.21)

with
b_{i}>0
, for
i=1,\,\ldots,\,d
, which describes the exceeding of the discounted aggregate claims for only one of the
d
-lines of business. It can be connected with the ruin probability ’
\psi_{or}
’. For more information of these ruin probabilities, we refer the reader to
[
14
]
. Unfortunately, the other two forms of ruin probability, denoted as ’
\psi_{and}
’ or ’
\psi_{sim}
’ are not covered by these sets
A
, see in
[
44
, Rem. 2.2]
. Indeed, for these forms seems better to be approached through ’non-linear’ single big jump, see in
[
43
, Sec. 5]
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let us consider risk model from (
6
) and we adopt Assumptions
6.1
and
6.2
for some fixed
T^{*}
. Then it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}[{\bf D}_{r}(t)\in x\,A]\sim\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,e^{r\,s}\,A\right]\,\lambda(ds)\,,

uniformly for
t\in\Lambda_{T}
, with
T\leq T^{*}
,
T\in\Lambda
.

Before the proof of Theorem
6.1
we need a Lemma, which has merit in its own right.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
be a fixed set. We assume that the
\{{\bf X}^{(i)}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
is a sequence of i.i.d., non-negative random vectors, with common distribution
F\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
. Then, for any
0<a\leq b<\infty
, it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\,{\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right]\sim\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left[c_{i}\,{\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right]\,,

(6.22)

for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, uniformly for
{\bf c}_{n}:=(c_{1},\,\ldots,\,c_{n})\in[a,\,b]^{n}
.

Proof.

For the upper bound, at first we employ
[
44
, Prop. 2.4]
, and next through
[
69
, Prop. 5.1]
(or through
[
31
, Lem. 3.1]
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\,{\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}Y_{A}^{(i)}>x\right]

(6.23)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left[c_{i}Y_{A}^{(i)}>x\right]=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left[c_{i}{\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right],

uniformly for
{\bf c}_{n}\in[a,\,b]^{n}
.

For the lower bound of (
6.22
), since the
{\bf X}^{(i)}
are non-negative, and the set
A
is increasing, for any
x>0
, we find

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\,{\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\{c_{i}\,{\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\}\right]

(6.24)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left[c_{i}\,{\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right]-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\mathbf{P}\left[c_{i}\,{\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right]\,\mathbf{P}\left[c_{j}\,{\bf X}^{(j)}\in x\,A\right]

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left[c_{i}\,{\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right]\,,

for any
{\bf c}_{n}\in[a,\,b]^{n}
. Hence, by relations (
6.23
) and (
6.24
) we get (
6.22
), uniformly for
{\bf c}_{n}\in[a,\,b]^{n}
.
∎

Now, we are ready for the proof of Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem
6.1

At first, from Assumption
6.1
, for some integer
M\in{\mathbb{N}}
it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}[{\bf D}_{r}(t)\in x\,A]=\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N(t)}{\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{-r\tau_{i}}\in x\,A\right]

(6.25)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{M}+\sum_{n=M+1}^{\infty}\right)\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{-r\tau_{i}}\in x\,A\,,\;N(t)=n\right]:=J_{1}(x,\,t,\,M)+J_{2}(x,\,t,\,M)\,,

for any
x>0
and any
t\in\Lambda_{T}
.

Let us start with estimation of
J_{2}(x,\,t,\,M)
. For some constant
c>\mu_{F_{A}}
, by Proposition

we obtain

\displaystyle J_{2}(x,\,t,\,M)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=M+1}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{-r\tau_{1}}\in x\,A\,,\;\tau_{n}\leq t\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=M+1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{-r\,s}\in x\,A\right]\,\mathbf{P}\left[\theta_{2}+\cdots+\theta_{n}\leq t-s\right]\,\mathbf{P}\left[\tau_{1}\in ds\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K\,\sum_{n=M+1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left[\theta_{2}+\cdots+\theta_{n}\leq t-s\right]}{\mathbf{P}[{\bf X}\in c\,n\,A]}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\,e^{-r\,s}\in x\,A\right]\,\mathbf{P}\left[\tau_{1}\in ds\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K\,\left(\sum_{n=M+1}^{\infty}\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left[\theta_{2}+\cdots+\theta_{n}\leq t\right]\,}{\mathbf{P}[{\bf X}\in c\,n\,A]}\right)\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,e^{r\,s}\,A\right]\,\lambda(ds)\,,

for all
t\in\Lambda_{T}
, where in the second step we take into consideration that the sequence

has independent terms. Next, from Assumption
6.2
, let remind also (
6.20
), for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists some
M>0
large enough, such that it holds

\displaystyle J_{2}(x,\,t,\,M)\leq K\,\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,e^{r\,s}\,A\right]\,\lambda(ds)\,,

(6.26)

for all
t\in\Lambda_{T}
.

Let us continue with estimation of
J_{1}(x,\,t,\,M)
. By Lemma
6.1
we obtain

\displaystyle J_{1}(x,\,t,\,M)=\sum_{n=1}^{M}\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{-r\tau_{i}}\in x\,A\,,\;N(t)=n\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{M}\int...\int_{\{0\leq\tau_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\tau_{n}\leq t<\tau_{n+1}\}}\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{-r\,s_{i}}\in x\,A\right]\,\mathbf{P}\left[\tau_{1}\in ds_{1},\,\ldots,\,\tau_{n+1}\in ds_{n+1}\right]

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{n=1}^{M}\int...\int_{\{0\leq\tau_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\tau_{n}\leq t<\tau_{n+1}\}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{-r\,s_{i}}\in x\,A\right]\,\mathbf{P}\left[\tau_{1}\in ds_{1},\,\ldots,\,\tau_{n+1}\in ds_{n+1}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{M}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{-r\,\tau_{i}}\in x\,A\,,\;N(t)=n\right]\,,

uniformly for
t\in\Lambda_{T}
, where in the third step, we use the inclusion
\mathcal{S}_{A}^{*}\subsetneq\mathcal{S}_{A}
and
e^{-r\,T}\leq e^{-rs_{i}}\leq 1
, for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
. Hence, it holds

\displaystyle J_{1}(x,\,t,\,M)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}-\sum_{n=M+1}^{\infty}\right)\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{-r\,\tau_{i}}\in x\,A\,,\;N(t)=n\right]

(6.27)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J_{11}(x,\,t,\,M)-J_{12}(x,\,t,\,M)\,,

uniformly for

.

For the first term we interchange the order of summation to find

\displaystyle J_{11}(x,\,t,\,M)\sim\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{-r\,\tau_{i}}\in x\,A\,,\;\tau_{i}\leq t\right]=\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,e^{r\,s}\,A\right]\,\lambda(ds)\,,

uniformly, for

. For the second term we obtain for all

\displaystyle J_{12}(x,\,t,\,M)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=M+1}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,e^{r\,s}\,A\right]\,\mathbf{P}\left[\theta_{2}+\cdots+\theta_{n}\leq t-s\right]\,\mathbf{P}\left[\tau_{1}\in ds\right]

\displaystyle=

Let

Remark 6.3

By

\displaystyle\psi_{r,L}(x,\,t)=\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf U}(s)\in L\,,\;\exists\;s\in(0,\,t]\right]\,.

\displaystyle\psi_{r,L}(x,\,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{-r\,y}{\bf p}(y)dy:=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\int_{0-}^{t}e^{-r\,y}\,p_{1}(y)\,dy\\

\int_{0-}^{t}e^{-r\,y}\,p_{d}(y)\,dy\end{array}\right)\,,

.

Let

a fixed set, with

to satisfy Assumption

. Under the conditions of Theorem

, it holds

\displaystyle\psi_{r,L}(x,\,t)\sim\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,e^{r\,s}\,A\right]\,\lambda(ds)\,,

, with

,

Proof.

From the assumptions we find that it holds

\displaystyle 0\leq\int_{0}^{t}p_{i}(y)\,e^{-ry}\,dy\leq\Lambda_{i}\,T<\infty\,,

for any

\displaystyle\psi_{r,L}(x,\,t)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf D}_{r}(t)-\int_{0}^{t}{\bf p}(y)\,e^{-ry}\,dy\in x\,A\right]\geq\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf D}_{r}(t)\in(x+u)\,A\right]

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in(x+u)\,e^{r\,s}\,A\right]\,\lambda(ds)\sim\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,e^{r\,s}\,A\right]\,\lambda(ds)\,,

uniformly for
t\in\Lambda_{T}
, where ’u’ is a positive constant (which froms by
[
66
, Lem. 4.3(d)]
) and in the third step we apply Theorem
6.1
and in the last step we use the inclusion
F\in\mathcal{S}_{A}^{*}\subsetneq\mathcal{L}_{A}
.

From the other-hand side from Theorem
6.1
we obtain

\displaystyle\psi_{r,L}(x,\,t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf D}_{r}(t)\in x\,A\right]\sim\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,e^{r\,s}\,A\right]\,\lambda(ds)\,,

uniformly for

.
∎

Remark 6.4
.

It seems that Theorem
6.1
and Corollary
6.1
are new even in the one-dimensional set up, for
A=(1,\,\infty)
,
{\bf l}=1
,
L=(-\infty,\,0)
. However, these results express usual asymptotic expressions, used in one-dimensional risk models. The originality stems from the fact that we require ’lighter’ tail in the counting process in comparison with the distribution tail of the claims, and the same time with the help of class
\mathcal{S}^{*}
, we avoid the restriction in the renewal processes. In some sense, the use of alternative multivariate analogue of Kesten inequality, provided by Proposition
2.2
, makes class
\mathcal{S}_{A}^{*}
necessary, and can not be extended to class
\mathcal{S}_{A}
.

We close this section with a corollary, that provides a more analytic form in Theorem
6.1
and in Corollary
6.1
in
MRV
case.

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
A=({\bf l}-L)\in\mathscr{R}
be a fixed set, with
L
to satisfy Assumption
6.3
. Under the conditions of Corollary
6.1
, with the restriction that
F\in MRV(\alpha,\,V,\,\mu)
, with
\alpha>1
, it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf D}_{r}(t)\in x\,A\right]\sim\psi_{r,L}(x,\,t)\sim\mu(A)\,\overline{V}(x)\,\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\alpha\,r\,s}\,\lambda(ds)\,,

(6.35)

uniformly for
t\in\Lambda_{T}
, with
T\leq T^{*}
,
T\in\Lambda
.

Proof.

From Theorem
6.1
and Corollary
6.1
, it is enough to show that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,e^{r\,s}\,A\right]\,\lambda(ds)\sim\mu(A)\,\overline{V}(x)\,\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\alpha\,r\,s}\,\lambda(ds)\,,

holds uniformly for
t\in\Lambda_{T}
. Indeed, since
F\in MRV(\alpha,\,V,\,\mu)
, it follows that
F_{A}\in\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}
, for any
A\in\mathscr{R}
, due to
\overline{F}_{A}(x)\sim\mu(A)\,\overline{V}(x)
, with
V\in\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}
, and
\mu(A)\in(0,\,\infty)
for any
A\in\mathscr{R}
(see the proof of
[
66
, Prop. 4.14]
). Hence, by the uniformity of the regular variation, see
[
7
, Th. 1.5.2]
, and via the inequalities
1\leq e^{r\,s}\leq e^{r\,T}
, we find that it holds

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\in x\,e^{r\,s}\,A\right]\,\lambda(ds)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\overline{F}_{A}(x\,e^{r\,s})\,\lambda(ds)\sim\overline{F}_{A}(x)\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\alpha\,r\,s}\,\lambda(ds)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\mu(A)\,\overline{V}(x)\,\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\alpha\,r\,s}\,\lambda(ds)\,,

this conclude the proof.
∎

Holonomicity from a Heegaard-Floer Perspective
Benjamin Cooper, Robert Deyeso III

Abstract
. We construct S r S^{r} -colored knot Floer homologies and prove that they satisfy categorified recurrence relations. The associated Euler characteristic implies q q -holonomicity of the corresponding sequence of colored Alexander polynomials, inspired by the AJ conjecture for colored Jones polynomials.



1.
Introduction

Given a knot
K
in
S^{3}
and a simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, the
Reshetikhin-Turaev construction produces a
q
-series valued invariant

\langle K\rangle_{V}
of
K
for each finite dimensional representation
V
of
the corresponding quantum group
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
. A priori, the collection

\{\langle K\rangle_{V}\}_{V\in Rep(\mathfrak{g})}
appears to contain an infinite amount of information, but the existence of
recurrence relations among, or the
q
-holonomicity of, these knot invariants
tells us that there is a great deal of redundancy. In exchange for an infinite
sequence

f:\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{Z}[q^{-1}][[q]]

we can make do with a finite collection of initial conditions and
a recurrence relation of the form

\displaystyle\sum_{i=n}^{n+m}a_{i}(q^{n},q)f(i)=0\quad\textnormal{ for }\quad a_{i}(u,v)\in R[u,v],

(1.1)

where
R:=\mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}]
. Garoufalidis and Le proved that for each knot
K
such a recurrence relation
always exists among
\mathfrak{g}
invariants (for
\mathfrak{g}\neq G_{2}
)

[
GL05
, Thm. 6]
. Unfortunately, this approach does not lead to an
interpretation of the recurrence relation. In the simplest non-trivial
case, the irreducible representations
V_{n}\cong S^{n}(V_{1})
of

\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
(
\dim V_{n}=n+1
) determine a sequence

f(n)=\langle K\rangle_{V_{n}}

called the colored Jones polynomials of
K
. The AJ conjecture, posed by Garoufalidis
[
Gar08
,
Gar04
]
, states that the recurrence relation satisfied by this sequence is a non-commutative deformation of the
A
-polynomial. The commutative
A
-polynomial is characterized by vanishing on the space of characters which extend from the boundary torus
S^{1}\times S^{1}\cong\partial(S^{3}\backslash\nu K)
into the
3
-manifold
S^{3}\backslash\nu K
, where
\nu K
is a regular neighborhood of the knot
K
, see
[
CCG+94
]
.
In foundational work, Frohman, Gelca and Lofaro
[
FGL02
,
FG00
]
studied similar statements.
Gukov studied this problem using insights from physics
[
Guk05
]
.
The conjecture is known to be true for some families of knots, see
[
L0̂6
]
.

A different story which also stems from the Reshetikhin-Turaev invariants
involves Khovanov homology and its generalizations, knot homology theories, which categorify the Reshetikhin-Turaev invariants, see
[
KR08
,
Web17
]
. For a knot
K
in
S^{3}
, and a representation
V
of a simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, there are chain complexes of graded vector spaces
[\![K]\!]_{V}
, the homology of which are invariants of
K
. The generating function associated to the homology is the Poincaré polynomial

P(K)_{V}(t,q)=\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}t^{i}q^{j}\dim H^{i,j}([\![K]\!]_{V}),\quad\textnormal{ satisfying }\quad P(K)_{V}|_{t=-1}=\langle K\rangle_{V}.

(1.2)

Its value at
t=-1
recovers the
Reshitikhin-Turaev invariants as the graded Euler characteristic of knot homology.
The situation here is sometimes likened to the relationship between ordinary homology and Euler characteristic, while both are useful tools, the historical precedence of the latter has been overshadowed by the great worlds which have been revealed to us by the former.

If one considers super Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)
then the
Alexander polynomial
\Delta_{K}(q)
is an instance of the
Reshitikhin-Turaev construction outlined above
[
Vir06
,
Sar15
]
.
Heegaard-Floer homology was used to define knot Floer homology
\widehat{\textit{HFK}}(K)
by
Ozsváth, Szabó and Rasmussen
[
OS04a
,
Ras03a
]
. In analogy with
Eqn. (
1.2
), the Euler characteristic of this knot homology
theory is the Alexander polynomial.

Our interest in this paper is to examine the question of
q
-holonomicity for these knot homology theories.
The HOMFLY homology unites the

\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
knot homology theories
[
Ras15
,
Cau17
]
and,
conjecturally, this includes the knot Floer homology as well

[
DGR06
,
BPLHW22
]
.
Fuji, Gukov and Su
λ
kowski
[
FGSk13
]
found examples of recurrence relations
among the Poincaré polynomials
P(K)_{S^{n}}(q,t)
of the HOMFLY homology theories. The colored HOMFLY polynomials
P(K)_{S^{n}}(q,-1)
are known to be
q
-holonomic
[
GLL18
]
.
When placed within the framework of
[
DGR06
]
, this work suggests the existence of some categorified recurrence relations or
homological
q
-holonomicity
among
S^{n}
-colored HOMFLY knot homologies; a lifting of those relations.
It is therefore natural to formulate the conjecture below.

Conjecture 1.1
.

There exists a notion of homological
q
-holonomicity for sequences of chain complexes of (filtered) graded vector spaces.
For each knot
K
in
S^{3}
, the sequence of
S^{r}
-colored HOMFLY homologies

\{[\![K]\!]_{S^{r}}\}_{r\geq 1}

is homologically
q
-holonomic.

Here we pursue a simplified version of this conjecture. Since a spectral sequence from HOMFLY homology to knot Floer homology ought to imply that homological
q
-holonomicity holds for knot Floer homology as well. We restrict our attention to this setting, where a
robust theory has been developed and can be leveraged in the context of our problem.

Theorem 1.2
.

There exists a notion of homological
q
-holonomicity for sequences of (filtered) chain complexes of graded vector spaces. For each knot
K
in
S^{3}
, the
S^{r}
-colored knot Floer homologies
\{S^{r}\widehat{\textit{CFK}}(K)\}_{n\geq 1}
is homologically
q
-holonomic.

2.
Holonomicity of colored Alexander polynomials

In §
2.1

S^{n}
-colored Alexander polynomials and in §
2.2

q

2.1.
S^{n}
-colored Alexander polynomials

For a knot
K
in
S^{3}
, the Alexander polynomial
\Delta_{K}(q)\in\mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}]

[
Lic97
]
. A priori,
\Delta_{K}(q)
is only defined up to multiplication by
\pm q^{\pm 1}
, but it can be expressed uniquely when the equation

\Delta_{K}(q)=\Delta_{K}(q^{-1})

is required to hold (and the lowest order term is positive). In this paper,
\Delta_{K}(q)
will be used to denote this
symmetrized form
of the Alexander polynomial.

A different normalization,
\Delta^{1}_{K}(q)
which will be more important for us in what follows, is determined by requiring the lowest power of
q
to be
q^{0}
. More precisely, if
\deg(\Delta_{K})
is the largest mononomial power of
q
occurring in the symmetrized form of
\Delta_{K}(q)
then the polynomial

\Delta^{1}_{K}(q):=q^{\deg(\Delta_{K})}\Delta_{K}(q)\in\mathbb{Z}[q]

(2.1)

is the
positive form
. The
S^{r}
-colored Alexander polynomials are given by certain limits of positive forms of Alexander polynomials described in this section.

Definition 2.1
.

Given a knot
K
in
S^{3}
and
\nu K\subset S^{3}
a regular neighborhood. The
(r,s)
-cable

K_{r,s}
of
K
is the
(r,s)
-torus link in the boundary torus
\partial\nu K
, winding
r
-times around the meridian and
s
-times around the longitude that given by the Seifert framing. When
r
and
s
are relatively prime,
\gcd(r,s)=1
, the link
K_{r,s}
is a knot.

The Alexander polynomial
\Delta_{K_{r,s}}(q)
of the
(r,s)
-cable
K_{r,s}
can be expressed in terms of the Alexander polynomial
\Delta_{K}(q)
of the knot
K
and the Alexander polynomial of the torus link
\Delta_{T_{r,s}}(q)
respectively
[
Lic97
]
.

\displaystyle\Delta_{K_{r,s}}(q)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{K}(q^{r})\Delta_{T_{r,s}}(q)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{K}(q^{r})\cdot\frac{q^{rs}-1}{q^{r}-1}\cdot\frac{q-1}{q^{s}-1}

(2.2)

By writing the rational functions as geometric series as in Eqn. (
2.4
),

\Delta^{1}_{K_{r,s}}(q)=q^{\deg(\Delta_{K_{r,s}})}\Delta_{K_{r,s}}(q)

becomes an element of the power series ring

\mathbb{Z}[[q]]:=\varprojlim\mathbb{Z}[q]/(q^{n}).

(2.3)

The proposition below shows that the sequence of Alexander polynomials obtained by increasing the number of longitudinal full twists converges in the topology of the ring
\mathbb{Z}[[q]]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

There is a limit

\lim_{n\to\infty}\Delta^{1}_{K_{r,rn+1}}(q)=\Delta^{1}_{K}(q^{r})\frac{q-1}{q^{r}-1},

where the rational function represents the power series

\frac{q-1}{q^{r}-1}:=(1-q)\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}q^{ir}.

(2.4)

Proof.

For a sequence
\{f_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
of power series to converge to
f
in the
q
-adic topology, or

f_{n}\to f
as
n\to\infty
, it suffices to show that for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
such that

f\equiv f_{i}\!\!\!\pmod{q^{n}}\quad\textnormal{ for all }\quad i>N.

When
s>r
, the Alexander polynomial
\Delta_{T_{r,s}}(q)
satisfies

\frac{q-1}{q^{r}-1}\frac{q^{rs}-1}{q^{s}-1}=\frac{q-1}{q^{r}-1}\cdot 1+\frac{q-1}{q^{r}-1}\cdot q^{s}+\cdots+\frac{q-1}{q^{r}-1}\cdot q^{(r-1)s}

(2.5)

Using Eqn. (
2.2
) and (
2.5
), it follows that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}q^{\deg(\Delta_{K_{r,rn+1}})}\Delta_{K_{r,rn+1}}(q)

\displaystyle=q^{r\deg(\Delta_{K})}\Delta_{K}(q^{r})\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{q-1}{q^{r}-1}+q^{rn+1}(\ast)\right)

\displaystyle=q^{r\deg(\Delta_{K})}\Delta_{K}(q^{r})\left(\frac{q-1}{q^{r}-1}+0\right)

where
(\ast)
corresponds to the higher order terms on the right-hand side of in Eqn. (
2.5
).
∎

Definition 2.3
.

The
S^{r}
-colored Alexander polynomial

\widebar{\Delta}^{r}_{K}(q)
of a knot
K
is the limit of Alexander polynomials of the
(r,rn+1)
-cables of
K
as
n\to\infty
, or

\widebar{\Delta}^{r}_{K}(q):=q^{r\deg(\Delta_{K})}\Delta_{K}(q^{r})\frac{q-1}{q^{r}-1}

When
K
is the unknot
U
, the Alexander polynomial is
1
;
\Delta_{U}(q)=1
. So, the
S^{r}
-colored Alexander polynomial of the unknot is the series below.

\widebar{\Delta}^{r}_{U}(q)=\frac{q-1}{q^{r}-1}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(q^{ir}-q^{ir+1})

(2.6)

The
reduced
S^{r}
-colored Alexander polynomial
of a knot
K
is the quotient

\Delta^{r}_{K}(q):=\widebar{\Delta}^{r}_{K}(q)/\widebar{\Delta}^{r}_{U}(q).

(2.7)

Notice that
\Delta^{1}_{K}(q)
agrees with
\widebar{\Delta}^{1}_{K}(q)
and Eqn. (
2.1
) above.

2.2.
Recurrence relation for
S^{r}
-colored Alexander polynomials

In the remainder of this section we will show directly that the sequence of

r
-colored Alexander polynomials satisfy a recurrence relation of the
form Eqn. (
1.1
) from the introduction. The relative simplicity of the
expressions
\Delta^{r}_{K}(q)
and
\widebar{\Delta}^{r}_{K}(q)
from Def.
2.3
allow us to be explicit. This construction will be
refined in §
6
.

Definition 2.4
.

(Seq)
Let
R:=\mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}]
. If
T
is an
R
-algebra then there is a natural
T
-module

\textnormal{Seq}(T):=T^{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
which consists of sequences of elements of
T
which are parameterized by
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Let
\textnormal{Seq}:=\textnormal{Seq}(R[[q]])
be sequences of power series
which are bounded in negative powers of
q
.

For each knot
K
, using Def.
2.3
, there are sequences

\displaystyle\Delta_{K},\widebar{\Delta}_{K}:\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\to R[[q]]\in\textnormal{Seq}\quad\textnormal{ given by }\quad

(2.8)

\displaystyle\Delta_{K}(r):=\Delta^{r}_{K}(q)\quad\textnormal{ and }\quad\widebar{\Delta}_{K}(r):=\widebar{\Delta}^{r}_{K}(q)

(2.9)

of reduced and unreduced
S^{r}
-colored Alexander polynomials respectively.
We will find recurrence relations of the form

\displaystyle\sum_{i=n}^{n+m}a_{i}(q^{n},q)\Delta_{K}(i)=0\quad\textnormal{ for }\quad a_{i}(u,v)\in R[u,v]

(2.10)

This will be done in two steps. First in §
2.2
we find such a recurrence relation

A_{K}
for the sequence of reduced polynomials
\Delta_{K}
.
Then in §
2.2
this relation is combined with the recurrence relation Eq. (
2.6
) for the unknot
\Delta_{U}(q)
to produce a recurrence relation for the unreduced sequence
\widebar{\Delta}_{K}
.

Before we start, a bit of material about the relationship between recurrence relations and the Weyl algebra is reviewed.

The Weyl algebra

It is sometimes useful to think of recurrence relations in terms of operators.
There are two operators
L,M
acting on sequences
f\in\textnormal{Seq}
by

(Lf)(n):=f(n+1)\quad\textnormal{ and }\quad(Mf)(n):=q^{n}f(n)

(2.11)

These operators satisfy the relation
qLM=ML
. The
Weyl algebra
is defined by this data

\mathcal{A}:=\mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}]\langle L,M\rangle/\langle qLM-ML\rangle

and by virtue of the assignments in Eqn. (
2.11
),
Seq
is an
\mathcal{A}
-module.
For a given sequence
f\in\textnormal{Seq}
, the recurrence relation in Eqn. (
2.10
) is equivalent to the relation

Af=0

when
A\in\mathcal{A}
is the element
A=\sum_{i=n}^{n+m}L^{i}a_{i}(M,q)
,
a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}]\langle M\rangle
for
i=n,\ldots,n+m
. The set of recurrence relations satisfied by
f
can be identified with

Ann(f)=\{A\in\mathcal{A}:Af=0\}\subseteq\mathcal{A}.

D
-operators

Let
X\in\mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}]
be a
q
-Laurent polynomial. Then there is an operator
D_{X}=1-XL\in\mathcal{A}
which acts on sequences. In long form, if
f\in\textnormal{Seq}
is a sequence then

(D_{X}f)(n):=f(n)-Xf(n+1)

is a new sequence which is defined in terms of
f
and multiplication by
X
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Suppose that
f,g\in\textnormal{Seq}
are sequences,
X,Y\in\mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}]
are Laurent polynomials and
C\in\mathbb{Z}
is an integer.
Then the operators
D_{X}
satisfy the properties below.

(1)

\mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}]
-linearity:
D_{X}(Yf)=YD_{X}(f)
and
D_{X}(f+g)=D_{X}(f)+D_{X}(g)

(2)

Derivation:
D_{X}(fg)=D_{X}(f)g+(X(Lf))D_{1}(g)

(3)

Commutation:
D_{X}D_{Y}=D_{Y}D_{X}

(4)

Annihilation:
D_{X}(q^{Cn})=q^{Cn}(1-Xq^{C})
. In particular, if
X=q^{-C}
then
D_{X}(q^{Cn})=0
.

Proof.

All of the properties are verified directly from the definition of
D_{X}
.

(1)

For
\mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}]
-linearity,

\displaystyle D_{X}(Yf)(n)

\displaystyle=Yf(n)-YXf(n+1)

\displaystyle=Y(f(n)-Xf(n+1))

\displaystyle=YD_{X}(f)(n)

\displaystyle D_{X}(f+g)(n)

\displaystyle=(f+g)(n)-X(f+g)(n+1)

\displaystyle=f(n)+g(n)-X(f(n+1)+g(n+1))

\displaystyle=(f(n)-Xf(n+1))+(g(n)-Xg(n+1))

\displaystyle=D_{X}(f)(n)+D_{X}(g)(n)

(2)

Derivation,

\displaystyle D_{X}(fg)(n)

\displaystyle=f(n)g(n)-Xf(n+1)g(n+1)

\displaystyle=(f(n)-Xf(n+1))g(n)+Xf(n+1)(g(n)-g(n+1))

(3)

For commutation,

\displaystyle(D_{X}D_{Y}f)(n)

\displaystyle=(D_{Y}f)(n)-X(D_{Y}f)(n+1)

\displaystyle=(f(n)-Yf(n+1))-X(f(n+1)-Yf(n+2))

\displaystyle=f(n)-(X+Y)f(n+1)+XYf(n+2)

which is symmetric in
X
and
Y
, so
(D_{X}D_{Y}f)(n)=(D_{Y}D_{X}f)(n)
.

(4)

Annihilation:
D_{X}(q^{Cn})(n)=q^{Cn}-Xq^{C(n+1)}=q^{Cn}(1-Xq^{C})

∎

The lemma below is a consequence of the properties from the Prop.
2.5
above.

Lemma 2.6
.

Given a knot
K
in
S^{3}
, if the Alexander polynomial of
K
is written as

\Delta^{1}_{K}(q)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}q^{n_{i}}\quad\textnormal{ where }\quad a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\},n_{i}\textnormal{ distinct }

then the operator
A_{K}:=\prod_{i}D_{q^{-n_{i}}}
annihilates the sequence of reduced Alexander polynomials
\Delta_{K}(r):=\Delta^{r}_{K}(q)=\Delta^{1}_{K}(q^{r})

Proof.

Induction on the number of non-zero terms
N
. If
N=1
then
\Delta_{K}(q)=a_{1}q^{n_{1}}
and
D_{q^{-n_{1}}}
annihilates the associated sequence, by Prop.
2.5
(4). Suppose
\Delta_{K}(q)=f(q)+aq^{n}
where
f(q):=\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}a_{i}q^{n_{i}}
, with
a_{i}\neq 0
for
i=1,\ldots,N-1
. By induction, the operator

D^{\prime}:=D_{q^{-n_{1}}}D_{q^{-n_{2}}}\cdots D_{q^{-n_{N-1}}}
annihilates the sequence
r\mapsto f(q^{r})
. So

\displaystyle(A_{K}\Delta_{K})(r)

\displaystyle=((D^{\prime}D_{q^{-n}})\Delta_{K})(r)

\displaystyle=D^{\prime}(D_{q^{-n}}(f(q^{r})+aq^{rn}))

\displaystyle=D^{\prime}D_{q^{-n}}f(q^{r})+D^{\prime}(aD_{q^{-n}}(q^{rn}))

\displaystyle(\ref{dpropprop}\,\,(1))

\displaystyle=D^{\prime}D_{q^{-n}}f(q^{r})+D^{\prime}(a\cdot 0)

\displaystyle(\ref{dpropprop}\,\,(4))

\displaystyle=D^{\prime}D_{q^{-n}}f(q^{r})

\displaystyle=D_{q^{-n}}D^{\prime}f(q^{r})

\displaystyle(\ref{dpropprop}\,\,(3))

\displaystyle=D_{q^{-n}}(0)

\displaystyle=0

∎

Example 2.7

Theorem 2.9
.

The element
\widebar{A}_{K}:=(M-1)A_{K}\in\mathcal{A}
annihilates the sequence
\widebar{\Delta}_{K}\in\textnormal{Seq}
of unreduced
S^{r}
-colored Alexander polynomials defined by
\widebar{\Delta}_{K}(r):=\widebar{\Delta}^{r}_{K}(q)
.

Proof.

Define the coefficients
a_{j}
by the relation

A_{K}\Delta_{K}(r)=\sum_{j=r}^{r+d}a_{j}\Delta_{K}(j)

Lemma
2.6
shows
A_{K}\Delta_{K}=0
, so the sum
\sum_{j=r}^{r+d}a_{j}\Delta_{K}(j)=0
is zero.
Multiplying through by
\widebar{\Delta}_{U}(r+d)
shows that

\sum_{j=r}^{r+d}a_{j}\widebar{\Delta}_{U}(r+d)\Delta_{K}(j)=0
.
Using the recurrence

\widebar{\Delta}_{U}(r+d)=(q^{n+j}-1)/(q^{n+d}-1)\widebar{\Delta}_{U}(r+j)

from Lemma
2.8
for each term this sum gives

\sum_{j=r}^{r+d}a_{j}\frac{q^{n+j}-1}{q^{n+d}-1}\widebar{\Delta}_{U}(r+j)\Delta_{K}(j)=0
. Multiplying both sides of this sum by
q^{n+d}-1
gives us the relation
\sum_{j=r}^{r+d}a_{j}(q^{n+j}-1)\widebar{\Delta}_{U}(r+j)\Delta_{K}(j)=0
.
Simplifying further,

\displaystyle 0=\sum_{j=r}^{r+d}a_{j}(q^{n+j}-1)\Delta_{K}(r+j)\widebar{\Delta}_{U}(r+j)

\displaystyle=(M-1)\sum_{j=r}^{r+d}a_{j}\Delta_{K}(r+j)\widebar{\Delta}_{U}(r+j)

\displaystyle=(M-1)A_{K}\Delta_{K}\widebar{\Delta}_{U}

\displaystyle=\widebar{A}_{K}\widebar{\Delta}_{K}

∎

3.
Overview of Heegaard-Floer theory

This section contains a review of the Heegaard Floer materials which are
needed for Section
4
. In particular, we recall the bordered
Heegaard Floer theory, its relation to the immersed curves interpretation
of
\widehat{\textit{CFD}}(S^{3}\backslash\nu K)
and how to compute
\widehat{\textit{HFK}}(K)
with its
bigrading. The last subsection describes the cabling algorithm for immersed curves.

Heegaard Floer homology is a suite of closed 3-manifold invariants which was
introduced by Ozsváth and Szabó
[
OS04c
,
OS04b
]
.
For a closed 3-manifold

\text{spin}^{c}
structure
\mathfrak{s}

\mathbb{F}_{2}
-vector space valued invariants

and

(Y,\mathfrak{s})
. While these invariants are specified by combinatorial data, called
w
-pointed Heegaard diagrams, they measure subtle topological information about the manifold

To a knot

3
-manifold

Heegaard diagram. Topologically, the two marked points and handle decomposition
specify the knot

w
-pointed diagram. When

[

]

[

]
, which
categorifies the Alexander polynomial in the sense that

\Delta_{K}(q)=\sum_{i,j}(-1)^{i}q^{j}\dim\widehat{\textit{HFK}}^{i,j}(K).

The knot Floer invariants

can be difficult to compute from a

[

]
. However, Hanselman,
Rasmussen and Watson
[

]
reinterpreted the bordered Floer invariants
and the pairing theorem

]
by identifying

with a chain
complex of morphisms between immersed curves in the Fukaya category of a
surface. It is this simpler setting which facilitates our study in Section

. The remainder of this section contains a review of these ideas
and their essential properties.

5.
Weyl algebra and homological holonomicity

5.1.
Some algebraic definitions

If
\mathcal{A}
is an abelian category then there is an additive category
F^{*}\mathcal{A}
of
filtered objects
in
\mathcal{A}
. An object of the category
F^{*}\mathcal{A}
is a sequence
E:=\{F^{n}E\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
with inclusions
F^{n+1}E\subseteq F^{n}E
. Maps
f:E\to E^{\prime}
are maps in
\mathcal{A}
which commute with inclusions. Similarly, if
\mathcal{A}
is an additive category then there is an additive category
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}}
of
graded objects
in
\mathcal{A}
. An object of
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}}
is a sequence
\{E^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
of objects in
\mathcal{A}
and maps
f:E\to E^{\prime}
are collections
\{f^{n}:E^{n}\to E^{\prime n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
of maps in
\mathcal{A}
. When
\mathcal{A}
is abelian, there is a functor called
associated graded

gr:F^{*}\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}}

which is defined on objects by
gr(\{F^{n}E\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}})^{m}:=F^{m}E/F^{m+1}E
.

For an additive category
\mathcal{A}
, there is a category
Ch(\mathcal{A})
of chain complexes over
\mathcal{A}
.

Definition 5.1
.

(
Ch(\mathcal{A})
)
A
chain complex

E\in Ob(Ch(\mathcal{A}))
of consists of pair
(\{E^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}},d_{E}^{k}:E^{k}\to E^{k+1})
where
E^{k}\in Ob(\mathcal{A})
and
d^{k+1}_{E}d^{k}_{E}=0
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

A
map

f\in Hom^{k}(E,E^{\prime})
of degree
k
is a collection
f=\{f^{i}:E^{i}\to E^{\prime k+i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
of maps in
\mathcal{A}
. For any such map, there is a map
\delta^{k}f\in Hom^{k+1}(E,E^{\prime})
with components

(\delta^{k}f)^{i}:=d_{E^{\prime}}^{k+i}f^{i}+(-1)^{i+1}f^{i+1}d_{E}^{i}

In this way, the set of maps
Hom(E,E^{\prime})=(\{Hom^{k}(E,E^{\prime})\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}},d^{k}:Hom^{k}(E,E^{\prime})\to Hom^{k+1}(E,E^{\prime}))
is a chain complex.

The composition satisfies the Leibniz rule with respect to the differential making
Ch(\mathcal{A})
a category which is enriched over itself. More generally, a category which is enriched over chain complexes is called a
differential graded (dg) category
.

A dg category
\mathcal{C}
has a number of auxilliary structures stemming from the differential. A map
f
is a
cycle
if
\delta f=0
. Two maps,
f
and
g
are
homotopic
or
f\simeq g
when there is a map
H
such that
\delta H=f-g
. There is a category
Z^{0}(\mathcal{C})
consisting of degree zero cycles and the
homotopy category

H^{0}(\mathcal{C}):=Z^{0}(\mathcal{C})/\simeq
consisting of cycles modulo boundaries. Two objects
E,F\in Ob(\mathcal{C})
are
homotopy equivalent

E\simeq F
when they are isomorphic
E\cong F
in the homotopy category
H^{0}(\mathcal{C})
. An object
E\in Ob(\mathcal{C})
is
contractible
when
E\simeq 0
.

5.2.
Action of Weyl algebra

The dg category
\mathcal{Seq}
introduced below will be our replacement for the collection of sequences
Seq
.

Definition 5.2
.

(
\mathcal{Seq}(\mathcal{A})
)
Suppose
\mathcal{A}
is a dg category. Then the dg category of sequences is defined by
\mathcal{Seq}(\mathcal{A}):=\prod_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\mathcal{A}
. In more detail, the objects are given by sequences
E=(E_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
of objects
E_{i}\in Ob(\mathcal{A})
. A map of degree
k
from
E=(E_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
to
E^{\prime}=(E^{\prime}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
is given by an element of the product

Hom_{\mathcal{Seq}(\mathcal{A})}^{k}(E,E^{\prime})=\prod_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}Hom_{\mathcal{A}}^{k}(E_{i},E^{\prime}_{i}).

If
f:E\to E^{\prime}
then we write
f=(f_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
, or just
f=(f_{i})
, for the sequence of maps which determine
f
.
Given two maps
f:E\to E^{\prime}
and
g:E^{\prime}\to E^{\prime\prime}
, the composition and differential are defined componentwise:

(g\circ f)=(g_{i}\circ f_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\quad\textnormal{ and }\quad\delta(f):=(d_{E_{i}}f_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}.

(5.1)

The identity map is
1_{E}=(1_{E_{i}})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
.

For a dg category
\mathcal{A}
, the Grothendieck group
K_{0}(\mathcal{A})
is simply an abelian group. When a category
\mathcal{A}
has additional structure, this structure is reflected in
K_{0}(\mathcal{A})
.
For instance, if
\mathcal{A}
supports a
\mathbb{Z}
-action then the Grothendieck group
K_{0}(\mathcal{A})
is a
\mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}]
-module. If
\mathcal{A}=(\mathcal{A},\otimes,1)
is a monoidal category then
K_{0}(\mathcal{A})
is a ring.
More details and additional discussion of the ideas surrounding the statement of the proposition below can be found in the reference
[
KMS09
]
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{A}
is a dg category which supports a
\mathbb{Z}
-action. Then there are dg functors

\mathcal{M},\mathcal{L}:\mathcal{Seq}(\mathcal{A})\to\mathcal{Seq}(\mathcal{A})

and a natural isomorphism

R:\mathcal{M}\circ\mathcal{L}\xrightarrow{\sim}t^{2}q\circ\mathcal{L}\circ\mathcal{M}.

(5.2)

which lifts the action of the Weyl algebra on the right-hand side of the map

\Phi:K_{0}(\mathcal{Seq}(\mathcal{A}))\to K_{0}(\mathcal{A})^{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\quad\textnormal{ given by }\quad\Phi((E_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}):=([E_{i}])_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}

Proof.

In order to introduce the map
\Phi
, one uses
K_{0}(\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{B})\cong K_{0}(\mathcal{A})\times K_{0}(\mathcal{B})
, see
[
Wei13
, Ex. 2.1.4, p. 75]
.

Recall from §
2.2
that there are maps
M:\textnormal{Seq}\to\textnormal{Seq}
and
L:\textnormal{Seq}\to\textnormal{Seq}
which are defined on functions
f
by Eqn. (
2.11
). In the proof of the statement above we will construct functors
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{L}
which have the same effect as
M
and
L
on objects. Then we will find the isomorphism
R
.

The functor
\mathcal{M}:\mathcal{Seq}(\mathcal{A})\to\mathcal{Seq}(\mathcal{A})
is given by
\mathcal{M}:=\prod_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}t^{2i-2}q^{i}
. In more detail,
\mathcal{M}
is defined on objects
E=(E_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
by

\mathcal{M}(E):=((\mathcal{M}E)_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\quad\textnormal{ where }\quad(\mathcal{M}E)_{i}:=t^{2i-2}q^{i}E_{i}.

If
f:E\to E^{\prime}
is a map
f=(f_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
of degree
k
then there is a map
\mathcal{M}(f):\mathcal{M}(E)\to\mathcal{M}(E^{\prime})
of degree
k
, whose components are
(\mathcal{M}f)_{i}:=t^{2i-2}q^{i}f_{i}
. These assignments determine a dg functor by virtue of universal properties and being a componentwise dg functor. Alternatively, a short proof of the three properties below is included.

(i)

\mathcal{M}(g\circ f)=\mathcal{M}(g)\circ\mathcal{M}(f)

(ii)

\mathcal{M}(1_{E})=1_{\mathcal{M}(E)}

(iii)

d\mathcal{M}(f)=\mathcal{M}(df)

In each case, we use the functoriality of the assignment
f_{i}\mapsto t^{2i-2}q^{i}f_{i}
.
For (i),
\mathcal{M}(g\circ f)_{i}=t^{2i-2}q^{i}(g_{i}\circ f_{i})=(t^{2i-2}q^{i}g_{i})\circ(t^{2i-2}q^{i}f_{i})=\mathcal{M}(g)_{i}\circ\mathcal{M}(f)_{i}
. For (ii),
\mathcal{M}(1_{E})_{i}=t^{2i-2}q^{i}1_{E_{i}}=1_{t^{2i-2}q^{i}E_{i}}=1_{\mathcal{M}(E_{i})}
. For (iii),
d\mathcal{M}(f)_{i}=d(t^{2i-2}q^{i}f_{i})=\mathcal{M}(df)_{i}
.

On the other hand, the functor
\mathcal{L}:\mathcal{Seq}(\mathcal{A})\to\mathcal{Seq}(\mathcal{A})
is given by shifting objects and maps by one. On objects
E=(E_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
the functor is defined by setting

\mathcal{L}(E):=((\mathcal{L}E)_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\quad\textnormal{ where }\quad(\mathcal{L}E)_{i}:=E_{i+1}

and if
f=(f_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
is a map then
\mathcal{L}(f)_{i}:=f_{i+1}
. For properties (i), (ii) and (iii) above the proofs go as follows. For (i):
\mathcal{L}(g\circ f)_{i}=(g\circ f)_{i+1}=g_{i+1}\circ f_{i+1}=\mathcal{L}(g)_{i}\circ\mathcal{L}(f)_{i}
. For (ii):
\mathcal{L}(1_{E})_{i}=1_{E_{i+1}}=1_{\mathcal{L}(E)_{i}}
. For (iii):
d\mathcal{L}(f)_{i}=df_{i+1}=\mathcal{L}(df)_{i}
.

Finally, we compute the map
R
in Eqn. (
5.2
). Given
f:E\to E^{\prime}
,
f=(f_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
, the left-hand side is
(\mathcal{M}\circ\mathcal{L})(f)_{i}=\mathcal{M}(f_{i+1})=t^{2i}q^{i+1}f_{i+1}
. On the right-hand side, we compute

(\mathcal{L}\circ\mathcal{M})(f)_{i}=t^{2i-2}q^{i}f_{i+1}
. The two sides differ by
t^{2}q
.
∎

Definition 5.5
.

(Holonomicity)
An object
E
of a pre-triangulated category
\mathcal{C}
can be
assembled from a subcategory

\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{C}
if there is a length
n
sequence of distinguished triangles in
H^{0}(\mathcal{C})
of the form

\begin{array}[]{cc}X_{1}\to X_{0}\to Y_{0}\to X_{1}[1]\\
X_{2}\to X_{1}\to Y_{1}\to X_{2}[1]\\
\vdots\\
X_{n+1}\to X_{n}\to Y_{n}\to X_{n+1}[1].\end{array}

with
X_{0}\cong E
,
X_{n+1}\cong 0
and
\{Y_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n}\subset Ob(\mathcal{D})
. Such a sequence of distinguished triangles is
non-trivial
if
Y_{0}\not\cong 0
.
If an algebra
B
acts on a pre-triangulated category
\mathcal{C}
then let
\langle B\cdot E\rangle\subset\mathcal{C}
be the smallest thick pre-triangulated subcategory containing objects of the form
b(E)
for
b\in B
. An object
E\in Ob(\mathcal{Seq})
is
holonomic
if
E\cong 0
or
E\not\cong 0
and it can be assembled in a non-trivial way from the smallest thick pre-triangulated category
\langle\mathcal{A}^{+}\cdot E\rangle
containing orbit of the Weyl action on
E
.

Under the identification given by the map
\Phi
from Prop.
5.3
this condition becomes
q
-holonomicity in the usual sense.

Adjuvant treatment after neoadjuvant immunotherapy in patients with non-small cell lung cancer
Authors: Junfeng Zhao, Ying Li, Ruyue Li, Xiujing Yao, Xue Dong, Lin Su, Yintao Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
SummaryThis study explored the feasibility of adjuvant immunotherapy after NICT and surgery. A retrospective study was conducted on NSCLC patients who underwent NICT and surgery. Two patient groups were defined based on their adjuvant therapy after NICT and surgery: the Chemo group (chemotherapy alone) and the Immuno+Chemo group (chemotherapy combined with immunotherapy). Disease-free survival (DFS) and overall survival (OS) were also analyzed. In total, 209 patients were enrolled. The median DFS for the Immuno+Chemo group and the Chemo group was not reached vs. 26 months (hazard ratio [HR]: 0.498, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.315–0.787, p = 0.002). A significant difference in OS was also observed; however, the median OS was not reached in either group (HR: 0.526, 95% CI: 0.287–0.965, p = 0.034). Postoperative adjuvant chemotherapy combined with immunotherapy was significantly more effective than adjuvant chemotherapy alone in patients with NSCLC treated with NICT and surgery.Subject areas: Health sciences, Medicine, Medical specialty, Therapeutics

Introduction

Lung carcinoma represents one of the world’s leading causes of cancer-related fatalities,1 considering non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) contributes to approximately 85% of all occurrences of lung cancer.2,3 Resection of radical lung cancer is the primary treatment strategy for curing early stage lung cancer.4,5 Previous studies have shown that patient prognosis is closely related to the TNM stage of the tumor at the initial visit, with approximately 20–30% of patients with NSCLC initially presenting with resectable disease.6,7,8 However, tumor recurrence occurs in 30–55% of patients within 5 years of surgical resection.9,10 In operable patients, neoadjuvant or adjuvant chemotherapy improves 5-year survival by only 5% compared to surgical treatment alone, with few patients achieving pathologic complete response (pCR).11,12,13,14 The advent of immunotherapy altered the way NSCLC is treated, and the use of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) has progressively evolved from the late stage to locally advanced and early stages of the disease.15 Perioperative immunotherapy has increased resection rates and improved long-term survival in patients with resectable NSCLC. Currently, exploratory treatment modalities for clinical studies on perioperative immunotherapy include neoadjuvant immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy, adjuvant immunotherapy, and preoperative neoadjuvant immunotherapy plus postoperative adjuvant immunotherapy. Following the effective completion of the first phase III clinical evaluation of neoadjuvant immunotherapy, the FDA approved nivolumab in combination with platinum-containing two-agent chemotherapy in March 2022 as neoadjuvant therapy for surgically removable NSCLC. The following trial is identified as the CheckMate-816 trial.16 KEYNOTE-091 and IMpower010 provide evidence for postoperative adjuvant therapy for programmed cell death-1 (PD-1) and programmed cell death-ligand 1 (PD-L1), respectively.8,17

On this basis, several randomized controlled studies of perioperative immunotherapy for NSCLC have emerged, introducing varying degrees of postoperative adjuvant immunotherapeutic measures, such as Neotorch,18 AEGEAN,19 and NADIM II.20 The results of these perioperative studies have shown prolonged event-free survival (EFS), suggesting that perioperative immunotherapy may consolidate therapy efficacy, improve patient outcomes, and reduce the risk of distant tumor recurrence. Evidence supporting the use of immunotherapy throughout the perioperative period in NSCLC is growing, and the outcomes of phase III clinical studies have confirmed the survival benefit of immunotherapy. However, these studies have not addressed how much additional efficacy can be derived from continuing ICIs postoperatively in patients who have already received neoadjuvant ICIs. Zhou et al. compared the effectiveness and security of a complete perioperative immunotherapy regimen with a neoadjuvant immunotherapy approach using an indirect analytical method. They concluded that postoperative adjuvant immunotherapy in patients who received neoadjuvant immunotherapy did not offer a significant advantage and may be associated with an increase in adverse events.21 Furthermore, the postoperative adjuvant treatment regimen used in many clinical studies on perioperative immunotherapy involves immunotherapy alone. However, in clinical practice, most patients do not receive immunotherapy alone after surgery. Therefore, it is unclear whether and how patients who receive neoadjuvant immunotherapy may benefit from postoperative adjuvant immunotherapy. This study aimed to explore the potential benefits of continuing immunotherapy postoperatively in patients undergoing neoadjuvant immunotherapy and to determine the optimal postoperative adjuvant treatment regimen.

Methods

Patient selection

In January 2019 and December 2022, a retrospective analysis was carried out on 209 resectable individuals suffering from NSCLC undergoing neoadjuvant immunotherapy plus chemotherapy (NICT) and surgery at Cancer Hospital Affiliated with Shandong First Medical University. Every selected participant satisfied the standards for pathologically confirmed NSCLC (squamous cell carcinoma or adenocarcinoma), clinical stage II or III, and received only NICT before surgical treatment or received postoperative adjuvant therapy. Patients were excluded if they had unresectable tumors or metastases discovered during exploratory surgery, received postoperative adjuvant therapy other than chemotherapy and immunotherapy, had sensitive gene mutations such as those involving epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR)/anaplastic lymphoma kinase (ALK), or refused follow-up (Figure 1). Patients were divided into two groups based on the postoperative adjuvant treatment regimen they followed: those whose adjuvant treatment regimen included chemotherapy (the Chemo group) and those whose adjuvant treatment regimen included chemotherapy combined with immunotherapy (the Immuno+Chemo group). The International Union Against Cancer/American Joint Committee on Cancer (UICC/AJCC) 8th edition, this study employed the TNM staging method.22 The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Shandong Cancer Hospital and Research Institute.

Neoadjuvant and adjuvant treatment regimens

The preoperative and postoperative chemotherapy regimens for all enrolled patients comprised platinum-based agents and paclitaxel/albumin-bound paclitaxel or platinum-based agents and pemetrexed via intravenous infusion. Among the platinum drugs used were cisplatin, administered at a rate of 25 mg/m2 on days 1–3, and carboplatin, with an AUC of 5. Medications based on paclitaxel included albumin-bound paclitaxel (260 mg/m2) and paclitaxel (135–175 mg/m2). The dose of pemetrexed was 500 mg/m2. The preoperative and postoperative immunotherapy regimens included intravenous PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors (toripalimab 240 mg, sintilimab 200 mg, camrelizumab 200 mg, pembrolizumab 200 mg, tislelizumab 200 mg, durvalumab 1,500, or atezolizumab 1,200 mg). The treatment period for both chemotherapy and immunotherapy was 3 weeks. All patients received 1–3 cycles of NICT preoperatively, with a mean of 2 cycles and 2–4 cycles of adjuvant therapy postoperatively.

Surgical treatment

Patients enrolled in this study were clinically assessed for resectable NSCLC through a multidisciplinary consultation. Each individual experienced radical surgical excision of the lung cancer while under general anesthesia from 3 to 8 weeks after the last round of neoadjuvant therapy was finished. Surgical procedures included video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS) or thoracotomy. Lobectomy, sleeve resection, or wedge resection were the three types of lung resection.

Follow-up

At the conclusion of surgical treatment, all patients received postoperative adjuvant therapy. All enrolled patients received regular outpatient assessments and telephone follow-ups after admission to the hospital. For patients whose last case record had been recorded more than 1 month before the cut-off time of this study, a telephone-based follow-up was conducted to complete the collection of clinical data and establish a database for the statistical analysis. The follow-up period ended on March 31, 2024, with a median follow-up duration of 29 months for all patients (range: 11–52 months). The median follow-up time for patients in the Chemo group was 27 months (range: 11–43 months), and for those in the Immuno+Chemo group, it was 30 months (range: 15–52 months).

Study endpoints

The primary study endpoints were disease-free survival (DFS) and overall survival (OS). The secondary study endpoint was failure mode. Two trained pathologists evaluated all postoperative pathological findings. DFS was defined as the time from radical resection of lung cancer to first recorded recurrence, death due to any cause, or last follow-up. OS was defined as the time from the start of the first cycle of neoadjuvant therapy to death due to any cause or last follow-up. The pCR was defined as the absence of residual tumor cells in the primary site of the surgical specimen and resected lymph nodes (ypT0N0M0).23,24 The DFS and OS of the two adjuvant regimens were analyzed separately for the total population, as well as the pCR and non-pCR subgroups. In the case of failure type, recurrence was characterized as locoregional recurrence (LRR) or distant metastasis (DM). LRR was defined as the recurrence of the primary tumor or local lymph nodes, and DM as nonregional lymph node metastasis, systemic metastasis, malignant pleural effusion, or peritoneal metastasis.

Statistical analysis

When comparing variable categories, the chi-square test or the Fisher’s exact test was utilized, and for contrasting continuous variables the rank-sum test or the independent samples t-test. The Kaplan-Meier method was employed to evaluate the OS and DFS sufferers, and the results were assessed using a log rank test. To find independent indicator variables for OS and DFS, univariate and multivariate analyses were used Cox proportional hazards models. The value of p less than 0.05 indicated a statistically significant difference in each a two-sided analysis. R software 4.3.2 was used for data processing and visualization.

Results

Patients’ baseline characteristics

Between January 2019 and December 2022, 209 patients with surgically resectable NSCLC underwent NICT combined with radical lung cancer resection at our study institution. Postoperative pathology showed pCR in 84 patients (40.1%) and Non-pCR in 125 patients (59.8%). Clinical staging revealed stage II in 50 patients (23.9%) and stage III in 159 patients (76.1%). Among these, 149 patients (71.3%) had squamous cell carcinoma, and 60 (28.7%) patients had adenocarcinoma. Additionally, 65 (31.1%) patients were in the Chemo group, and 144 (68.9%) were in the Immuno+Chemo group. Among them, 24 (36.9%) and 67 (46.5%) patients in the two groups underwent VATS, respectively. Furthermore, 60 (92.3%) and 118 (81.9%) patients underwent lung lobectomy, respectively (Table 1).

Progression and survival outcomes

In the overall population, the median DFS of the Chemo group and the Immuno+Chemo group were 26 months and NA (hazard ratio [HR]: 0.498, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.315–0.787), respectively. As shown in Figure 2A, compared with the Chemo group, the Immuno+Chemo group had a significant advantage in the 1-year and 2-year DFS analyses (1-year DFS, 70.8% vs. 94.4%; 2-year DFS, 58.4% vs. 69.7%; p = 0.002). In the Immuno+Chemo and Chemo groups, the median OS failed to be achieved (HR: 0.526, 95% CI: 0.287–0.965). Similarly, the Immuno+Chemo group had a significant advantage over the Chemo group in the 1-, 2-, and 3-year OS analyses (1-year OS, 98.5% vs. 100.0%; 2-year OS, 77.6% vs. 87.6%; and 3-year OS, 64.6% vs. 78.9%; p = 0.034) (Figure 2B).

The patients were categorized into two groups, pCR and Non-pCR, based on whether postoperative pathology achieved pCR. In the pCR population, the median DFS was not achieved in both the Chemo and Immuno+Chemo groups (HR: 0.692, 95% CI: 0.298–1.605). The 1-year and 2-year DFS studies revealed no discernible benefit for the Immuno+Chemo group in comparison to the Chemo cohort (1-year DFS, 81.8% vs. 100.0%; 2-year DFS, 67.0% vs. 69.9%; p = 0.380) (Figure 3A). In the Immuno+Chemo and Chemo groups, the median OS failed to be achieved (HR: 0.710, 95% CI: 0.242–2.083). Similarly, the Immuno+Chemo group did not demonstrate a significant advantage over the Chemo group in the 1-, 2-, and 3-year OS analyses (1-year OS, 100.0% vs. 100.0%; 2-year OS, 86.4% vs. 88.3%; and 3-year OS, 66.8% vs. 81.9%; p = 0.530) (Figure 3B).

In the Non-pCR population, the median DFS of the Chemo group and the Immuno+Chemo group were 25 months and NA (HR: 0.438, 95% CI: 0.251–0.764), respectively. The Immuno+Chemo cohort significantly exceeded the Chemo cohort in the 1-year and 2-year DFS studies (1-year DFS, 65.1% vs. 90.2%; 2-year DFS, 55.8% vs. 69.2%; p = 0.003) (Figure 4A). Median OS was not reached in either the Chemo or Immuno+Chemo groups (HR: 0.450, 95% CI: 0.213–0.949). Similarly, the Immuno+Chemo group had a significant advantage over the Chemo group in the 1-, 2-, and 3-year OS analyses (1-year OS, 97.7% vs. 100.0%; 2-year OS, 72.9% vs. 87.3%; and 3-year OS, 64.8% vs. 76.8%; p = 0.030) (Figure 4B).

Discussion

Surgical treatment alone is less effective for patients with resectable NSCLC, especially if radical resection is not feasible. After radical surgical resection of lung cancer, recurrence in most patients is due to DM. Early implementation of neoadjuvant therapy enhances the elimination of microscopic metastases that may evade detection by conventional imaging. However, the optimal regime of postoperative adjuvant therapy remains unclear. Therefore, this research is the first to examine the therapeutic advantages of several postoperative adjuvant therapy regimens in real-world patients with NSCLC who received NICT, providing insights into postoperative adjuvant therapy strategies for future patients with NSCLC receiving NICT. In the present study, our results demonstrated that the Immuno+Chemo group had a significant advantage in DFS and OS compared to the Chemo group in the total population and among those with Non-pCR postoperative pathology. However, among patients with pCR postoperative pathology, there were no appreciable differences in the DFS and OS between the Immuno+Chemo group and the Chemo cohort.

Positive outcomes from the CheckMate-816 clinical study confirm the role of neoadjuvant treatment modalities in perioperative NSCLC treatment.16 Patients receiving neoadjuvant therapy should continue the adjuvant therapy after surgery. Findings from several clinical studies, such as AEGEAN and KEYNOTE-671, provide substantial evidence supporting a “neoadjuvant therapy-surgical therapy-adjuvant therapy” perioperative immunotherapy approach.13,19

Our study presents key findings regarding the treatment selection for patients with NSCLC in the perioperative period. First, these clinical findings suggest that the “sandwich” model offers significant benefits, but whether adjuvant therapies increase efficacy is not known based on current clinical evidence. In the AEGEAN study, the neoadjuvant regimen compared durvalumab combined with chemotherapy to placebo combined with chemotherapy, while the adjuvant regimen compared durvalumab to placebo.20 Furthermore, in the KEYNOTE-671 study, the neoadjuvant regimen compared pembrolizumab combined with chemotherapy to placebo combined with chemotherapy, while the adjuvant regimen compared pembrolizumab to placebo.13 Similarly, the neoadjuvant regimen in the NEOTORCH study compared toripalimab combined with chemotherapy to placebo combined with chemotherapy, while the adjuvant regimen compared one cycle of toripalimab combined with chemotherapy and 13 cycles of toripalimab maintenance therapy to placebo combined with chemotherapy and placebo.18

Based on these clinical studies, we can only conclude that the “sandwich” model has a significant benefit over the control group and that patients can benefit from this treatment model. However, it remains unclear whether the combination of neoadjuvant and adjuvant therapies increases the efficacy of immunotherapy. Therefore, the patients included in this study underwent NICT and surgery, ensuring that any observed benefits were attributable to the addition of adjuvant therapy.

Second, two phases III clinical studies, Impower0108 and NEYNOTE-091,17 confirmed the ability of postoperative adjuvant immunotherapy to improve patient DFS using adjuvant immunotherapy alone. However, in clinical practice, for patients with NSCLC patients undergoing NICT, clinicians generally choose chemotherapy alone or immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy as the postoperative adjuvant treatment regimen. Therefore, we compared the efficacy of chemotherapy alone to immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy in the total population and observed that the combination of immunotherapy and chemotherapy resulted in longer DFS and OS than chemotherapy alone. This finding is consistent with previous studies that demonstrated that postoperative adjuvant chemotherapy alone improved 5-year survival by only 5%, whereas the addition of immunotherapy significantly prolonged patient survival.11,12 Based on the results of our study, we recommend that patients undergoing NICT receive an immune-combination chemotherapy regimen after surgery.

The pCR is a key efficacy metric in perioperative therapy and often serves as a surrogate study endpoint in clinical trials of neoadjuvant therapies. pCR indicates the complete disappearance of tumor lesions, and for patients undergoing NICT, the choice of postoperative adjuvant treatment regimen should be adjusted based on whether pCR is achieved after surgery.

In our study, we subgrouped the total population and categorized patients into pCR and Non-pCR groups based on postoperative pathology. In the pCR population, there was no statistically significant difference in DFS and OS between the immune-combination chemotherapy and chemotherapy alone groups. Our findings demonstrate that patients who achieved pCR after neoadjuvant therapy were sensitive to immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy regimens, resulting in a significantly lower risk of disease recurrence. Therefore, this group of patients may benefit less from adjuvant immunotherapy. Currently, we should prioritize the safety of the treatment plan by selecting the option with the fewest adverse effects and the highest safety profile. While aiming for prolonged patient survival, we also strive to enhance their quality of life. Further clinical studies are needed to confirm the need for continued adjuvant immunotherapy and to identify the optimal treatment regimen for selecting adjuvant treatment options for this group of patients.

Patients who do not achieve pCR may exhibit lower sensitivity to immunotherapy compared to those who do. He et al. suggested that cancer cells metastasizing to lymph nodes may inhibit the maturation of lymph node immune cells, thereby reducing the efficacy of immunotherapy.25 Deng et al. suggested that the number of memory T cells in lymph nodes determines the efficacy of immunotherapy.26 From these results, it is evident that greater tumor involvement and the later tumor stages lead to stronger tumor-induced immunosuppression in the body, which hinders the optimal effectiveness of immunotherapy. Therefore, patients with Non-pCR postoperative pathology have a higher risk of local recurrence and metastasis and should be actively considered for postoperative adjuvant therapy. Considering that the effect of immunotherapy alone may not be optimal, the addition of chemotherapy to immunotherapy is beneficial for reducing the risk of recurrence and metastasis. In this study, patients in the Non-pCR cohort who had surgery followed by adjuvant chemotherapy and immunotherapy had improved DFS and OS over patients who had only adjuvant chemotherapy.

Regarding the failure mode, the Immuno+Chemo group had a significant advantage in DM compared to the Chemo group in both the total population and among those with Non-pCR postoperative pathology, suggesting that postoperative adjuvant immunotherapy is particularly effective in targeting microscopic metastatic lesions that may evade detection by conventional imaging methods.27 For patients with pCR postoperative pathology, there was no significant difference in DM between the Immuno+Chemo and Chemo groups. This could be attributed to the pre-existing sensitivity of this group of patients to immunotherapy before surgery, potentially resulting in a lower presence of microscopic metastatic lesions and a better prognosis without significant differences being observed. Furthermore, no significant difference in LRR was observed between the Immuno+Chemo and Chemo groups in the total population and subgroups based on postoperative pathology (Non-pCR or pCR), suggesting that both adjuvant therapies effectively controlled primary tumor lesions.

Conclusion

In conclusion, for patients with NSCLC who underwent NICT followed by surgery, the addition of postoperative adjuvant chemotherapy combined with immunotherapy demonstrated significantly greater efficacy compared to adjuvant chemotherapy alone, especially in those with non-pCR postoperative pathology.

Limitations of the study

This study had several limitations. First, its retrospective nature makes bias unavoidable. In addition, the follow-up period was comparatively short and the sample size was insufficiently large. Randomized controlled clinical trials and prospective multi-institutional studies are required to confirm the best postoperative adjuvant treatment plans after NICT and surgery.

Patients were divided into two groups based on the postoperative adjuvant treatment regimen they followed: those whose adjuvant treatment regimen included chemotherapy (the Chemo group) and those whose adjuvant treatment regimen included chemotherapy combined with immunotherapy (the Immuno+Chemo group).

A total of 209 patients were enrolled, including 65 patients in the Chemo group and 144 patients in the Immuno+Chemo group.

Method details

In January 2019 and December 2022, a retrospective analysis was carried out on 209 resectable individuals suffering from NSCLC undergoing neoadjuvant immunotherapy plus chemotherapy (NICT) and surgery at Cancer Hospital Affiliated with Shandong First Medical University. Every selected participant satisfied the standards for pathologically confirmed NSCLC (squamous cell carcinoma or adenocarcinoma), clinical stage II or III, and received only NICT before surgical treatment or received postoperative adjuvant therapy. Patients were excluded if they had unresectable tumors or metastases discovered during exploratory surgery, received postoperative adjuvant therapy other than chemotherapy and immunotherapy, had sensitive gene mutations such as those involving epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR)/anaplastic lymphoma kinase (ALK), or refused follow-up (Figure 1). Patients were divided into two groups based on the postoperative adjuvant treatment regimen they followed: those whose adjuvant treatment regimen included chemotherapy (the Chemo group) and those whose adjuvant treatment regimen included chemotherapy combined with immunotherapy (the Immuno+Chemo group). The International Union Against Cancer/American Joint Committee on Cancer (UICC/AJCC) 8th edition, this study employed the TNM staging method22. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Shandong Cancer Hospital and Research Institute.

The preoperative and postoperative chemotherapy regimens for all enrolled patients comprised platinum-based agents and paclitaxel/albumin-bound paclitaxel or platinum-based agents and pemetrexed via intravenous infusion. Among the platinum drugs used were cisplatin, administered at a rate of 25 mg/m2 on days 1-3, and carboplatin, with an AUC of 5. Medications based on paclitaxel included albumin-bound paclitaxel (260 mg/m2) and paclitaxel (135–175 mg/m2). The dose of pemetrexed was 500 mg/m2. The preoperative and postoperative immunotherapy regimens included intravenous PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors (Toripalimab 240 mg, Sintilimab 200 mg, Camrelizumab 200 mg, Pembrolizumab 200 mg, Tislelizumab 200 mg, durvalumab 1,500, or atezolizumab 1,200 mg). The treatment period for both chemotherapy and immunotherapy was 3 weeks. All patients received 1–3 cycles of NICT preoperatively, with a mean of 2 cycles and 2–4 cycles of adjuvant therapy postoperatively.

Patients enrolled in this study were clinically assessed for resectable NSCLC through a multidisciplinary consultation. Each individual experienced radical surgical excision of the lung cancer while under general anesthesia from 3 to 8 weeks after the last round of neoadjuvant therapy was finished. Surgical procedures included video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS) or thoracotomy. Lobectomy, sleeve resection, or wedge resection were the three types of lung resection.

At the conclusion of surgical treatment, all patients received postoperative adjuvant therapy. All enrolled patients received regular outpatient assessments and telephone follow-ups after admission to the hospital. For patients whose last case record had been recorded more than 1 month before the cut-off time of this study, a telephone-based follow-up was conducted to complete the collection of clinical data and establish a database for the statistical analysis. The follow-up period ended on March 31, 2024, with a median follow-up duration of 29 months for all patients (range: 11–52 months). The median follow-up time for patients in the Chemo group was 27 months (range: 11–43 months), and for those in the Immuno+Chemo group, it was 30 months (range: 15–52 months).

The primary study endpoints were disease-free survival (DFS) and overall survival (OS). The secondary study endpoint was failure mode. Two trained pathologists evaluated all postoperative pathological findings. DFS was defined as the time from radical resection of lung cancer to first recorded recurrence, death due to any cause, or last follow-up. OS was defined as the time from the start of the first cycle of neoadjuvant therapy to death due to any cause or last follow-up. The pCR was defined as the absence of residual tumor cells in the primary site of the surgical specimen and resected lymph nodes (ypT0N0M0). The DFS and OS of the two adjuvant regimens were analyzed separately for the total population, as well as the pCR and non-pCR subgroups. In the case of failure type, recurrence was characterized as locoregional recurrence (LRR) or distant metastasis (DM). LRR was defined as the recurrence of the primary tumor or local lymph nodes, and DM as nonregional lymph node metastasis, systemic metastasis, malignant pleural effusion, or peritoneal metastasis.

Quantification and statistical analysis

When comparing variable categories, the chi-square test or the Fisher's exact test was utilized, and for contrasting continuous variables, the rank-sum test or the independent samples t-test. The Kaplan-Meier method was employed to evaluate the OS and DFS sufferers, and the results were assessed using a log-rank test. To find independent indicator variables for OS and DFS, univariate and multivariate analyses were used Cox proportional hazards models. The value of P less than 0.05 indicated a statistically significant difference in each a two-sided analysis. R software 4.3.2 was used for data processing and visualization.

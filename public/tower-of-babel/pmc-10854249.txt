Dento-skeletal Sequel of Hyrax in Nonsyndromic Cleft Lip and Palate: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis
Authors: Udayakumar Deepika, Prayas Ray, Amit Nayak, Louis Soloman Simon, Manoranjan Mahakur, Moushmi Sahu
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimTo critically evaluate and review the dento-skeletal outcomes of hyrax with or without nonsurgical protraction therapy in nonsyndromic cleft lip and palate (CLP) patients.Materials and methodsElectronic databases were searched from PubMed, Cochrane, Scopus, SciELO, Lilacs, and Google Scholar from January 2005 to April 2022 to identify studies reporting on the dento-skeletal effect of hyrax with or without nonsurgical protraction therapy among children aged 7–15 years with CLP before the secondary alveolar bone grafting (SABG) procedure. Abstracts and subsequently eligible full-text articles were screened. The random-effects meta-analyses of the mean difference were carried out.ResultsA total of 423 studies were identified. After a thorough screening, 19 studies met the inclusion criteria. Introduction

The most common congenital craniofacial anomaly is cleft lip and palate (CLP) with a global incidence of 0.28–3.74/1,000 live births. In the United States, CLP occurs in approximately one in 940 newborns.1 In patients with CLP, surgeries for lips are usually performed during the 1st year of life whereas palatal repair is performed later. The scar tissue compromises the growth and development of the maxillary segments, resulting in maxillary constriction, and midface depression. The maxillary constriction in the transverse plane is mostly treated by rapid maxillary expansion (RME), which increases the alveolar cleft width and creates room for bone graft placement.2 It has a direct impact on the transverse movement of the lateral nasal walls improving the nasopharyngeal airway dimensions and indirectly changing the mandibular position, thereby increasing the oropharyngeal airway dimensions.3,4

Exponentially with an increase in age, the suture between two palatal shelves is more interdigitated, and heavier forces would be required to open this suture. Hence, the expansion procedure is recommended to be carried out during the early dentition period (8–10 years of age).5 Isaacson mentioned that apart from midpalatal suture other factors might provide the resistance indirectly like the bones articulating to the maxilla.6 The orthopedic force exerted by the RME can open the midpalatal suture and widen the palate. The hygienic or hyrax expander appliance was developed to prevent the soft tissue irritation associated with the tissue-borne RME appliance.7

This review is to critically evaluate the dento-skeletal outcomes of hyrax with or without nonsurgical protraction therapy in nonsyndromic CLP patients.

The primary objective of this meta-analysis is to evaluate the effect of hyrax with or without nonsurgical protraction therapy in nonsyndromic CLP patients based on the following parameters:

Materials and Methods

Protocol and Registration

This review follows the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines and was registered in the PROSPERO (CRD42022319305) before the commencement of the search through the electronic databases.

Eligibility Criteria

The population, intervention, comparison, outcome, and study format was used to structure the question for this review with described inclusion and exclusion criteria (Table 1).

Participants

Both the randomized and nonrandomized clinical studies with nonsyndromic CLP patients (n ≥ 15) aged ranging from 7 to 15 years old before secondary alveolar grafting were included. Subsequently, the exclusion of studies is carried out in two steps. In first-step reviews, case reports, and studies with surgically assisted-RME were excluded. Further exclusion of in vivo studies with a sample size <15 was done.

Intervention

Hyrax with or without nonsurgical protraction therapy (facemask or reverse headgear).

Comparison

Hass, quad helix, fan-shaped expander, inverted mini hyrax (iMini-hyrax), and hyrax with Tandem appliance.

Outcome

Skeletal and dental changes after the expansion are evaluated with the two-dimensional study or digital cast model and three-dimensional analyzes based on computed tomography (CT), and lateral or posteroanterior cephalographs.

Search Strategy and Data Selection

Two investigators performed an electronic search in databases (Medline, Cochrane, Scopus, Science Direct, Lilacs, and Google Scholar) from January 2005 to April 2022. The search strategy focused on two aspects after hyrax maxillary expansion with or without nonsurgical protraction therapy in nonsyndromic CLP patients:

Skeletal changes.

Skeletal changes.

Dental changes.

Dental changes.

The following keywords were used for search in different databases—(1) RME cleft palate, (2) cleft palate rapid expansion with hyrax, and (3) rapid palatal expansion in cleft palate with hyrax. The reference lists of relevant studies and studies from clinicaltrial.gov were also analyzed. Two studies from the clinical trial registry in which a hyrax appliance was compared with Hass in one study and with a quad helix in another study evaluated using cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT) were not published. Hence, these two studies were not included in this review. All the included studies were retrieved with restriction of English language and sample size greater than 15 patients.

Data Collection

Initially, the titles and abstracts of all the studies were reviewed independently by two authors using predetermined forms. The investigators analyzed studies from six databases individually and then the outcomes were compared. To identify the interrater reliability Cohen's κ statistics were used to identify interrater reliability and the value of κ was 0.68. There were no noticeable disputes between the investigators on the selection of studies. All the extracted data were entered in Excel sheet 2016 (Microsoft Corp. WA) to obtain all the relevant information. The number of skeletal changes in the upper maxillary arch with the cranial base and dental expansion in the transverse plane were assessed. Nasal cavity changes were included in the skeletal changes and evaluated.

Data Collection on the Risk of Bias of Included Studies

A total of 13 studies2,8–19 were conducted on hyrax with protraction therapy, six studies2,8,9,11,16,18 were randomized and seven12–15,17,19 were nonrandomized studies. Out of six studies20–25 conducted on hyrax with protraction therapy, one24 was randomized, and the remaining five20–23,25 were nonrandomized studies. The risk of bias in all seven randomized studies and 12 nonrandomized studies was assessed with the Cochrane risk of bias protocol.

Statistical Analysis

From 19,2,8–25 only seven studies2,10,13,17,18,20,22 were selected for the meta-analysis. A total of 13 studies8,9,11,12,14–16,19,21,23,25 were eliminated from quantitative analyzes because of variance in the parameters measured, and appliance used. Five studies2,10,13,17,18 from hyrax without protraction therapy and two studies20,22 from hyrax with protraction therapy were selected. The comprehensive meta-analysis software was used to evaluate the efficacy of hyrax with or without protraction therapy.

Hyrax without Protraction Therapy

Regarding the study design, six2,8,9,11,16,18 were randomized clinical studies and seven12–15,17,19 were nonrandomized clinical studies. From 13 studies, six studies,2,9,10,13,14,19 four studies,11,12,17,18 and three studies8,15,16 were conducted on the unilateral CLP (UCLP), bilateral CLP (BCLP), and both unilateral and BCLP patients. Results of the Individual Studies, Meta-analysis

Hyrax without Protraction Therapy

From 13 studies, only four studies were selected for the qualitative analyzes with a similar appliance used.

Intermolar Width

The intermolar width evaluated between hyrax and fan-type expander in three studies favored that the fan-type expander had slightly increased intermolar width than hyrax but the difference was not statistically significant [p-value = 0.492, Z-value = 0.688, and standard error (SE) = 0.260] (Fig. 1A).

Forest plot analysis of intermolar distance compared between hyrax without protraction therapy and other appliances. (A) Analysis between hyrax and fan type expander showed that fan type expander had a greater increase in intermolar distance; (B) Quantitative analysis between hyrax and inverted mini hyrax revealed that hyrax had more increase in intermolar distance than inverted mini hyrax

The intermolar width between hyrax and iMini-hyrax in three studies showed that hyrax provided a better increase in intermolar width than iMini-hyrax which had a statistically significant difference (p-value = 0.027, Z-value = −2.213, and SE = 0.267) (Fig. 1B).

Intercanine Width

The intercanine width compared between hyrax and fan-type expander in three studies and between hyrax and iMini-hyrax in three studies showed that hyrax had a better increase in intercanine width than fan-type expander (p-value = 0.286, Z-value = −1.066, and SE = 0.261) and iMini-hyrax (p-value = 0.178, Z-value = −1.345, SE = 0.263) (Fig. 2). There was no significant difference between the appliances in the intercanine width.

Forest plot analysis of intercanine distance compared between hyrax without protraction therapy. (A) Forest plot analysis between hyrax and fan type expander favors hyrax; (B) Forest plot analysis between hyrax and inverted mini hyrax favors hyrax

Molar Inclination

The molar inclination was evaluated at the cleft and noncleft side separately in two studies where hyrax was compared with iMini-hyrax resulting that on the cleft side, hyrax had greater molar inclination (p-value = 0.468, Z-value = −0.727, and SE = 0.318). While on the noncleft side, inverted mini hyrax had the greater molar inclination (p-value = 0.572, Z-value = 0.566, and SE = 0.317) (Fig. 3). There were no studies to compare the molar inclination between hyrax and fan-type expanders.

Forest plot analysis of molar inclination compared between hyrax without protraction therapy and inverted mini hyrax. (A) Forest plot analysis on the noncleft side showed that the latter had more molar inclination; (B) Forest plot analysis on the cleft side, revealed that hyrax had more molar inclination

Canine Inclination

The upper canine inclination was compared between hyrax and inverted mini hyrax in two studies similar to the molar inclination on cleft and noncleft sides. The inverted mini hyrax had greater canine inclination than hyrax with p-value = 0.013, Z-value = 2.492, and SE = 0.333 on noncleft side and p-value = 0.470, Z-value = 0.722, and SE = 0.319 on cleft side, respectively (Fig. 4). On the noncleft side, the difference was statistically significant. There were no studies to compare the canine inclination between hyrax and fan-type expanders.

Forest plot analysis of canine inclination compared between hyrax without protraction therapy and inverted mini hyrax. (A) The plot reveals that on the noncleft side, the latter had more canine inclination; (B) Plot analysis on the cleft side, revealed that the latter had more canine inclination

This parameter was measured from the anterior and posterior regions. Two studies compared both anterior and posterior nasal cavity width between hyrax and iMini-hyrax and showed that hyrax had a greater increase in nasal cavity width at both the regions with p-value = 0.004, Z-value = −2897, and SE = 0.341 in the anterior region which was statistically significant. While, in the posterior region p-value = 0.072, Z-value = −1.801, and SE = 0.323 (Fig. 5).

Forest plot analysis of nasal cavity width compared between hyrax without protraction therapy and inverted mini hyrax. (A) Forest plot analysis on the anterior region showed that hyrax had more amount of expansion; (B) Forest plot analysis on the posterior region, revealed that hyrax had more expansion

Two studies compared hyrax with a fan-type expander showed that in the anterior region, hyrax had better nasal cavity width increase (p-value = 0.120, Z-value = −1.554, and SE = 0.322) while in the posterior region fan-type expander had a greater increase in nasal cavity width (p-value = 0.622, Z-value = 0.493, and SE = 0.320) (Fig. 6).

It was similar to the overjet where cleft patients had a greater increase than non-cleft patients (p-value = 0.300, Z-value = −1.037, and SE = 0.218) (Fig. 10B).

Discussion

Summary of the Evidence

General Information

From the participant's cleft type, evaluation methods, follow-up time interval, and parameters were variant for the selected studies and only seven studies2,10,13,17,18,20,22 were selected for the quantitative analysis.

Outcomes of the Studies

To date, there have been numerous reviews on the effect of hyrax or RME in normal or noncleft patients. However, there was a systematic review conducted by Palikaraki, 2020 regarding the effect of facemasks in UCLP with or without expansion. It was concluded that the SNA and the ANB angles increased from baseline to postprotraction at different time intervals. Other parameters which included Vertical relations, dental relations, and soft tissue profile contradicted results. There was no systematic review on the effect of the hyrax on the CLP patients. This was the first attempt to critically assess the effect of hyrax in CLP patients on the dental and skeletal structures before the secondary alveolar bone grafting.

Skeletal Changes

A study using iMini-hyrax banded to premolars with transpalatal arch banded to permanent first molar reported a significant unnoticeable increase in SNA degree, especially in cases with severe anteroposterior discrepancies.10

Significant increase in the transverse plane on using hyrax2,10,14 while iMini-hyrax bonded to the first premolar with transpalatal arch showed no significant change in the transverse plane.10 A study showed a significant increase in the transverse plane (Zyg-Zyg’) both in BCLP and UCLP, which was slightly higher in BCLP.15 The above fact was contradicted in a study conducted with 50 BCLP patients comparing the effects of hyrax and EDO.12

Studies where hyrax with protraction therapy compared with noncleft positive control patients showed better results in the noncleft patients.20,22

Airway Changes

Inverted mini hyrax banded to permanent molars demonstrated the greatest increase in the nasal passage of 26.1% (iMini–M—1423 mm) with significant differences found between the iMini-M and the fan-type groups.13 A study on changes in airway between cleft and noncleft patients before and after treatment reported that there was a significant increase in an airway on the cleft side than on the noncleft side and the intermolar width increased considerably in cleft patients than the control group.21

Dental Changes

Hyrax compared with SME (quad helix) and tooth and tissue-borne RME (Hass) showed no significant differences at intermolar and intercanine distances.9 The comparison of the anterior and posterior regions expansion in UCLP concluded that hyrax caused significantly greater posterior expansion than the anterior region. However, both the fan-type and the iMini-hyrax groups showed significantly greater expansion in the anterior region while in iMini-hyrax there was no expansion in the posterior region and there was no differential expansion for the hyrax expander.10 These findings were per the results of other studies.13,15,18,20 RPE causes a significant increase in the maxillary width in all groups (UCLP and BCLP).15 Hyrax with protraction therapy had a better increase in overjet, overbite, and distance between upper central incisor to cranial base (Sella-Nasion) than noncleft patients.20,22

Tooth Inclination

The fan expander reported significant tipping of the supporting teeth on both sides, especially noticeable in the anterior teeth, while in hyrax there was appreciable tipping only on the noncleft side. In the iMini-hyrax group, there was no significant tooth inclination reported. There was a 5:1 ratio between the crown and apex expansion in the fan expander and 1.5:1 for the iMini-hyrax. The size of the iMini-hyrax was small and placed more apically in the palate; hence, the force applied was closer to the center of resistance of the anterior teeth.10 There was no difference in the amount of expansion and dental tipping between the cleft side and the noncleft side.2 Few authors, found that the tipping of teeth on the cleft side was greater than the noncleft side.2,10,13

Conclusion

The following are the facts collected from the above-selected studies:

Hyrax is preferred over inverted mini hyrax for posterior crossbite correction both on the dental and skeletal levels.

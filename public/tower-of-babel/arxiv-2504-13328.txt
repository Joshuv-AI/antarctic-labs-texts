Arithmetic Functions and Geometry
Andrew Kobin

Abstract
In this expository note, we revisit several classical arithmetic functions - namely Euler’s totient function, the divisor sum functions and Dedekind’s ψ \psi -function - within a unifying algebraic framework that highlights their connections to geometry. This framework builds on prior work involving zeta functions and Möbius inversion. While our main goal is to provide a clear context for similar constructions in the future, we also make an original observation regarding Dedekind’s ψ \psi -function.

1.1
Generalities on arithmetic functions

Classically, an
arithmetic function
is any function
f:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, possibly with some conditions such as weak multiplicativity that we will not adopt here. To every such
f
, we can assign a Dirichlet series

F(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{f(n)}{n^{s}},\qquad s\in\mathbb{C},

which encodes the arithmetic of
f
complex-analytically. For example, the arithmetic function
\zeta:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
defined by
\zeta(n)=1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
corresponds to the Riemann zeta function
\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}}(s)
.

One thing that makes the set of arithmetic functions interesting is that it admits the structure of a commutative
\mathbb{C}
-algebra by defining

(f*g)(n)=\sum_{ij=n}f(i)g(j),

called the
Dirichlet convolution
of
f
and
g
. More intuitively,
f*g
is defined by ordinary multiplication of Dirichlet series:

\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{f(n)}{n^{s}}\right)\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{g(n)}{n^{s}}\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(f*g)(n)}{n^{s}}.

1.2
Abstract arithmetic functions

The
\mathbb{C}
-algebra of classical arithmetic functions is merely a special case of the more general notion of an
incidence algebra
, as surveyed in
[
Kob
,
Koc
]
. In the most general version of the story, an algebra of “objective arithmetic functions” can be attached to any locally finite decomposition space (or locally finite
2
-Segal space). For concreteness, we restrict the discussion here to locally finite monoids and their vector space incidence algebras.

Fix a field
k
. For a monoid
M
, the
incidence algebra

I(M)
is defined to be the
k
-vector space of functions
f:M\rightarrow k
equipped with a convolution product
*
defined by

(f*g)(x)=\sum_{ab=x}f(a)g(b)

for all
x\in M
. When
M
is a locally finite monoid
1
1

1

M
is
locally finite
if for each
x\in M
there are only finitely many
a,b\in M
with
ab=x
.
, this gives
I(M)
the structure of an associative, unital
k
-algebra, with unit
\delta(x)=\delta_{x,1}
, where
1
is the identity element of
M
.

Each locally finite monoid
M
admits a
zeta function

\zeta\in I(M)
defined by
\zeta(x)=1
for all
x\in M
. Möbius inversion asserts that
\zeta
is invertible in
I(M)
, with inverse given by

\mu(x)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }x=1\\
-\sum_{ab=x}\mu(a),&\text{if }x\not=1.\end{cases}

The usual application of Möbius inversion is to invert a relation between two functions
f,g\in I(M)
, e.g. convolving both sides of
f=g*\zeta
with
\mu
yields
f*\mu=g
, or:

f(x)=\sum_{ab=x}g(a)\quad\text{implies}\quad g(x)=\sum_{ab=x}f(a)\mu(b).

(1)

The goal of this short note is to highlight several pairs of arithmetic functions
(f,g)
satisfying formula (
1
) and extend them to a more general arithmetic geometric context.

1.3
Arithmetic functions coming from geometry

Many arithmetic functions “come from geometry”, a phrase which we will not give precise meaning to. Instead, we will explore the geometric flavor of several examples, even when this connection is not typically mentioned in the literature.

To begin with, the functions
\zeta
and
\mu
that form the basis for Möbius inversion have well-known geometric interpretations. The Riemann zeta function
\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}}(s)
can be viewed as the zeta function
\zeta_{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}}(s)
attached to the scheme
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}
and more generally, Dedekind zeta functions of number fields and Hasse–Weil zeta functions of varieties can be interpreted as zeta functions for schemes over
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}
. This often provides a geometric explanation for relations between these different functions; cf.
[
Kob
]
.

In fact, these zeta functions are mere shadows
2
2

2

In technical terms, they are decategorifications of generating functions whose coefficients lie in some Grothendieck ring.
of an abstract zeta function, such as Kapranov’s motivic zeta function

Z_{mot}(X,t)=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}[\operatorname{Sym}^{n}X]t^{n},

which lies in the power series ring
1+tK_{0}({\tt Sch}_{X})[[t]]
, where
K_{0}({\tt Sch}_{X})
is the Grothendieck ring of the category of
X
-schemes. Here,
\operatorname{Sym}^{n}X
denotes the
n
th symmetric power of
X
, parametrizing unordered
n
-tuples of points in
X
. Choosing
X
appropriately and applying a motivic measure to the coefficients of
Z_{mot}(X,t)
recovers the more familiar zeta functions in the previous paragraph.

Like
\zeta
, the classical Möbius function
\mu:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is an element of the incidence algebra
I(\mathbb{N}^{\times})
, where
\mathbb{N}^{\times}
is the multiplicative monoid of natural numbers. It too is a shadow of a motivic generating function, namely

M_{mot}(X,t)=1+\sum_{\mu}(-1)^{||\mu||}[\operatorname{Sym}^{\mu}X]t^{|\mu|}=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\mu\vdash n}(-1)^{||\mu||}[\operatorname{Sym}^{\mu}X]t^{n}

where the sum is over all partitions
\mu=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{k})
of natural numbers
n\in\mathbb{N}
, with
|\mu|:=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mu_{i}=n
,
||\mu||
equal to the number of distinct elements of
\mu
and
\operatorname{Sym}^{\mu}X:=\coprod_{i=1}^{k}\operatorname{Sym}^{\mu_{i}}X
. This can be rewritten
[
VW
, Prop. 3.7]

M_{mot}(X,t)=1+\sum_{\mu}(-1)^{||\mu||}[\operatorname{Conf}^{\mu}X]t^{|\mu|},

where
\operatorname{Conf}^{\mu}X\subseteq\operatorname{Sym}^{\mu}X
is a
configuration space
, i.e. the open subscheme parametrizing distinct tuples of points. For a lengthier discussion of generating functions involving configuration spaces, see
[
VW
, Sec. 5]
.

Remark 1.1
.

As an amusing sidenote, the other famous mathematical object named for August Ferdinand Möbius, the Möbius strip, is also a configuration space:
\operatorname{Conf}^{2}(S^{1})
.

Another example worth mentioning here is the partition function
p(n)
, which counts the number of ways of writing
n
as a sum of positive integers. On the geometric side, the values of
p(n)
are encoded in the Hilbert schemes
\operatorname{Hilb}^{n}\mathbb{A}^{2}
, parametrizing collections of
n

3

3

To be more accurate, while
\operatorname{Sym}^{n}\mathbb{A}^{2}
parametrizes
n

\operatorname{Hilb}^{n}\mathbb{A}^{2}
parametrizes such points
together with an infinitesimal structure when points collide
. In fancier terms, the natural map
\operatorname{Hilb}^{n}\mathbb{A}^{2}\rightarrow\operatorname{Sym}^{n}\mathbb{A}^{2}
is a desingularization.
in
\mathbb{A}^{2}

p(n)=\chi(\operatorname{Hilb}^{n}\mathbb{A}^{2})

\chi
denotes the Euler characteristic. We can thus view the partition generating function
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p(n)t^{n}
, originally studied by Euler, as a shadow of the motivic generating function

For a version of this relation when
\mathbb{A}^{2}
is replaced by a smooth surface

[

Totient Functions

Euler’s
totient function

\phi:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
was originally defined as:

\phi(n)=\#\{1\leq r\leq n\mid(r,n)=1\}.

Alternatively,
\phi
can be defined recursively by

n=\sum_{d\mid n}\phi(d),

(2)

or
\operatorname{id}=\phi*\zeta
as arithmetic functions, where
\zeta(n)=1
is the zeta function in the incidence algebra
I(\mathbb{N}^{\times})
. Then it is immediate from Möbius inversion that:

\phi(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}d\mu\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)

(3)

where
\mu
is the classical Möbius function. That is,
\phi=\operatorname{id}*\mu
as arithmetic functions.

Another way of packaging this information uses the generating function of
\phi
, revealing a connection to the Riemann zeta function.

Proposition 2.1
.

As formal Dirichlet series,

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\phi(n)}{n^{s}}=\frac{\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}}(s-1)}{\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}}(s)}.

Proof.

By Dirichlet convolution,

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\phi(n)}{n^{s}}=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n}{n^{s}}\right)\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(n)}{n^{s}}\right)=\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}}(s-1)\mu_{\mathbb{Q}}(s)=\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}}(s-1)\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}}(s)^{-1}.

∎

The classical totient function has several alternative descriptions which are ripe for generalization. For a ring
A
, let
A^{\times}
denote the group of units of
A
under multiplication. Then
\phi(n)=(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. Even better, Sun Tzu’s Theorem
4
4

4

This is sometimes called the Chinese Remainder Theorem. Sun Tzu (also written Sun Zi or Sunzi) is not to be confused with the author of the same name.
says that if
n=p_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots p_{r}^{k_{r}}
for distinct primes
p_{i}
and
k_{i}\geq 1
,

(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\cong(\mathbb{Z}/p_{1}^{k_{1}}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\times\cdots(\mathbb{Z}/p_{r}^{k_{r}}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}.

In particular,
\phi
is a (weakly) multiplicative arithmetic function. One also has that for any prime
p
and
k\geq 1
,
\phi(p^{k})=p^{k-1}(p-1)=p^{k}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)
, implying the following product formula:

\phi(n)=n\prod_{p\mid n}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)

(4)

where the product runs over all prime factors of
n
.

2.1
Totient function of a number field

Let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be a number field with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{K}
and let
I_{K}^{\times}
denote the monoid of nonzero ideals of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
under multiplication. The ideal norm
N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}
induces a morphism of monoids
N:I_{K}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
which can also be viewed as an arithmetic function on
I_{K}^{\times}
by composing with a fixed map
\mathbb{N}\rightarrow k
, usually the homomorphism sending
1\mapsto 1
. We will abuse notation and write
N
for both versions.

Recall that for an ideal
\mathfrak{a}\in I_{K}^{+}
,
N(\mathfrak{a})=\#(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{a})
. If we think of
N(\mathfrak{a})
as a generalization of a number
n=\#(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})
, then a reasonable generalization of
\phi(n)=\#(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
is the following.

Definition 2.2
.

The
totient function of a number field

K/\mathbb{Q}
is the function
\phi_{K}:I_{K}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
defined by

\phi_{K}(\mathfrak{a})=\#(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{a})^{\times}.

Viewing
\phi_{K}
as an arithmetic function in the incidence algebra
I(I_{K}^{\times})
allows us to determine many of its properties and relations to other arithmetic functions.

Recall that two ideals
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{b}
of a ring
A
are
relatively prime
if
\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{b}=A
. Applying Sun Tzu’s Theorem to the ring
\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{ab}
shows that
\phi_{K}
is multiplicative, i.e. for any relatively prime ideals
\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
,
\phi_{K}(\mathfrak{ab})=\phi_{K}(\mathfrak{a})\phi_{K}(\mathfrak{b})
. It follows immediately that for any prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
,
\phi_{K}(\mathfrak{p})=N(\mathfrak{p})-1
and more generally,

\phi_{K}(\mathfrak{p}^{k})=N(\mathfrak{p})^{k-1}(N(\mathfrak{p})-1)=N(\mathfrak{p})^{k}\left(1-\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{p})}\right).

(5)

As a consequence, for any ideal
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
,

\phi_{K}(\mathfrak{a})=N(\mathfrak{a})\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{a}}\left(1-\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{p})}\right).

(6)

On the other hand,
\phi_{K}
can be described by a recursive formula similar to formula (
2
).

Proposition 2.3
.

For any ideal
\mathfrak{a}\in I_{K}^{+}
,

N(\mathfrak{a})=\sum_{\mathfrak{d}\mid\mathfrak{a}}\phi_{K}(\mathfrak{d})

(7)

where the sum runs over all ideals
\mathfrak{d}
dividing
\mathfrak{a}
. In other words,
N=\phi_{K}*\zeta
where
\zeta(\mathfrak{a})=1
is the zeta function for
I_{K}^{+}
.

Proof.

By formula (
6
),

\phi_{K}(\mathfrak{a})=N(\mathfrak{a})\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\mid\mathfrak{a}}\left(1-\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{p})}\right)=N(\mathfrak{a})\sum_{\mathfrak{d}\mid\mathfrak{a}}\frac{\mu(\mathfrak{d})}{N(\mathfrak{d})}=\sum_{\mathfrak{d}\mid\mathfrak{a}}\mu(\mathfrak{d})N(\mathfrak{ad}^{-1}).

This can be rewritten

\phi_{K}(\mathfrak{a})=\sum_{\mathfrak{d}\mid\mathfrak{a}}N(\mathfrak{d})\mu(\mathfrak{ad}^{-1})

(8)

i.e.
\phi_{K}=N*\mu
. Then Möbius inversion gives the desired formula.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

We could have proved formula (
2
) for the classical totient function this way too, i.e. by first proving formula (
3
) directly using the above argument and then applying Möbius inversion.

The ideal norm, viewed as a monoid map
N:I_{K}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{\times}
, allows one to build Dirichlet series out of arithmetic functions in
I(I_{K}^{\times})
, as explained in
[
Kob
, Rmk. 3.31]
. For
f\in I(I_{K}^{\times})
, its associated Dirichlet series is the Dirichlet series attached to the
pushforward

N_{*}f
, defined by

(N_{*}f)(n)=\sum_{N(\mathfrak{a})=n}f(\mathfrak{a}).

The explicit Dirichlet series can be written

F(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(N_{*}f)(n)}{n^{s}}=\sum_{\mathfrak{a}\in I_{K}^{\times}}\frac{f(\mathfrak{a})}{N(\mathfrak{a})^{s}}.

As in Proposition
2.1
, the Dirichlet series for
\phi_{K}
can be expressed in terms of the Dedekind zeta function for
K/\mathbb{Q}
.

Proposition 2.5
.

As formal Dirichlet series,

\sum_{\mathfrak{a}\in I_{K}^{+}}\frac{\phi_{K}(\mathfrak{a})}{N(\mathfrak{a})^{s}}=\frac{\zeta_{K}(s-1)}{\zeta_{K}(s)}.

(9)

Proof.

Since
\phi_{K}=N*\mu
, we get

\sum_{\mathfrak{a}\in I_{K}^{+}}\frac{\phi_{K}(\mathfrak{a})}{N(\mathfrak{a})^{s}}=\left(\sum_{\mathfrak{a}\in I_{K}^{+}}\frac{N(\mathfrak{a})}{N(\mathfrak{a})^{s}}\right)\left(\sum_{\mathfrak{a}\in I_{K}^{+}}\frac{\mu(\mathfrak{a})}{N(\mathfrak{a})^{s}}\right)=\zeta_{K}(s-1)\mu_{K}(s)=\zeta_{K}(s-1)\zeta_{K}(s)^{-1}.

∎

More generally, a totient function can be defined on the monoid of
0
-dimensional ideals in an
abstract number ring
, in the sense of
[
Cla
]
. For example, the coordinate ring of a curve
C/\mathbb{F}_{q}
admits such a totient function. Viewing the corresponding incidence algebra more abstractly allows us to generalize the definition to arbitrary varieties over a finite field in the next section.

2.2
Totient function of a variety

Let
X
be a variety over
k=\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The definition of the totient function in this section is a generalization of the one given in
[
AR
]
for
X
a curve.

For a closed point
x\in|X|
, let
\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
be the local ring at
x
,
\mathfrak{m}_{x}
the maximal ideal corresponding to
x
and
k(x)=\mathcal{O}_{X,x}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}
the residue field at
x
, which is a finite extension of
k
, namely
k(x)=\mathbb{F}_{q^{\deg(x)}}
.

Let
Z_{0}^{\operatorname{eff}}(X)
be the monoid of effective
0
-cycles on
X
under addition. For an effective
0
-cycle
\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}x_{i}
, where
x_{i}
are distinct closed points of
X
and
a_{i}\geq 1
, define the semilocal ring associated to
\alpha
by

\mathcal{O}_{X,\alpha}:=\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{O}_{X,x_{i}}

and the distinguished ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{X,\alpha}
by

I_{\alpha}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}\mathfrak{m}_{x_{i}}^{a_{i}}.

Then
\mathcal{O}_{X,\alpha}/I_{\alpha}
is a finite ring of order
q^{\deg(\alpha)}
. In this way, we can regard the function
\pi:Z_{0}^{\operatorname{eff}}(X)\rightarrow\mathbb{N},\alpha\mapsto q^{\deg(\alpha)}
as an analogue of the norm
N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}
of a number field
K
. We will give a careful description of this in a moment.

Definition 2.6
.

The
totient function of a variety

X/\mathbb{F}_{q}
is the function
\phi_{X}:Z_{0}^{\operatorname{eff}}(X)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
defined by

\phi_{X}(\alpha)=\#(\mathcal{O}_{X,\alpha}/I_{\alpha})^{\times}.

As in Section
2.1
, we may extract certain algebraic properties of
\phi_{X}
by viewing it as an arithmetic function in the incidence algebra
I(Z_{0}^{\operatorname{eff}}(X))
.

We say two
0
-cycles
\alpha,\beta\in Z_{0}^{\operatorname{eff}}(X)
are
relatively prime
if
\operatorname{supp}(\alpha)\cap\operatorname{supp}(\beta)=\varnothing
. Then Sun Tzu’s Theorem
5
5

5

In
[
AR
, Lem. 7]
, the authors give a careful proof of Sun Tzu’s Theorem in the dimension
1
case but the proof carries over verbatim to the finite rings
\mathcal{O}_{X,\alpha}/I_{\alpha}
.
implies that if
\alpha
and
\beta
are relatively prime,
\phi_{X}(\alpha+\beta)=\phi_{X}(\alpha)\phi_{K}(\beta)
. It follows that for any closed point
x\in|X|
,
\phi_{X}(x)=q^{\deg(x)}-1
. More generally,

\phi_{X}(ax)=q^{\deg(ax)}(1-q^{-\deg(x)}).

(10)

From this, we get:

\phi_{X}(\alpha)=q^{\deg(\alpha)}\prod_{i=1}^{r}(1-q^{-\deg(x_{i})})

(11)

for any effective
0
-cycle
\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}x_{i}
on
X
.

As before, there is also a recursive formula for
\phi_{X}
.

Proposition 2.7
.

For any effective
0
-cycle
\alpha
on
X
,

q^{\deg(\alpha)}=\sum_{\beta\leq\alpha}\phi_{X}(\beta).

(12)

In other words,
\pi=\phi_{X}*\zeta
, where
\zeta(\alpha)=1
is the zeta function for
Z_{0}^{\operatorname{eff}}(X)
and
\pi=q^{\deg(-)}
.

Proof.

Write
\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}x_{i}
. By formula (
11
),

\phi_{X}(\alpha)=q^{\deg(\alpha)}\prod_{i=1}^{r}(1-q^{-\deg(x_{i})})=q^{\deg(\alpha)}\sum_{\beta\leq\alpha}\mu(\beta)q^{-\deg(\beta)}=\sum_{\beta\leq\alpha}\mu(\beta)q^{\deg(\alpha-\beta)}

where
\mu
is the Möbius function for
Z_{0}^{\operatorname{eff}}(X)
. This can be rewritten

\phi_{X}(\alpha)=\sum_{\beta\leq\alpha}\mu(\beta)q^{\deg(\alpha-\beta)}=\sum_{\beta\leq\alpha}q^{\deg(\beta)}\mu(\alpha-\beta).

(13)

Or in other words,
\phi_{X}=\pi*\mu
. Then Möbius inversion for
Z_{0}^{\operatorname{eff}}(X)
implies that
\pi=\phi_{X}*\zeta
as claimed.
∎

The analogue of the field norm
N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}
in this context is the terminal map
t:X\rightarrow\operatorname{Spec}k
, which induces a map
\pi=t_{*}:Z_{0}^{\operatorname{eff}}(X)\rightarrow Z_{0}^{\operatorname{eff}}(\operatorname{Spec}k)
. In turn, this induces a pushforward map
\pi_{*}:I(Z_{0}^{\operatorname{eff}}(X))\rightarrow I(Z_{0}^{\operatorname{eff}}(\operatorname{Spec}k))
. Identifying
Z_{0}^{\operatorname{eff}}(\operatorname{Spec}k)\cong\mathbb{N}_{0}
as additive monoids,
\pi_{*}
associates to any
f\in I(Z_{0}^{\operatorname{eff}}(X))
a power series in
I(Z_{0}^{\operatorname{eff}}(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{F}_{q}))\cong k[[t]]
via the assignment

\pi_{*}f\longleftrightarrow\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(\pi_{*}f)(n)t^{n}=\sum_{\alpha\in Z_{0}^{\operatorname{eff}}(X)}f(\alpha)t^{\deg(\alpha)}.

From this, we recover a formula relating
\phi_{X}
to the the Hasse–Weil zeta function of
X
.

Proposition 2.8
.

For any variety
X
over a finite field,

\sum_{\alpha\in Z_{0}^{\operatorname{eff}}(X)}\phi_{X}(\alpha)t^{\deg(\alpha)}=\frac{Z(X\times\mathbb{A}^{1},t)}{Z(X,t)}.

Proof.

Translating the identity
\phi_{X}=\pi*\mu
to power series produces

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in Z_{0}^{\operatorname{eff}}(X)}\phi_{X}(\alpha)t^{\deg(\alpha)}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\alpha\in Z_{0}^{\operatorname{eff}}(X)}q^{\deg(\alpha)}t^{\deg(\alpha)}\right)\left(\sum_{\alpha\in Z_{0}^{\operatorname{eff}}(X)}\mu(\alpha)t^{\deg(\alpha)}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\alpha\in Z_{0}^{\operatorname{eff}}(X)}(qt)^{\deg(\alpha)}\right)Z(X,t)^{-1}=Z(X,qt)Z(X,t)^{-1}.

It is well-known that
Z(X,qt)=Z(X\times\mathbb{A}^{1},t)
, so the claimed formula holds.
∎

2.3
Totients and arithmetic schemes

Let
X
be an arithmetic scheme, i.e. a scheme of finite type over
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}
. For each prime
p
, the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-scheme
X_{p}
has a totient function
\phi_{X_{p}}:Z_{0}^{\operatorname{eff}}(X_{p})\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
defined by
\phi_{X_{p}}(\alpha)=\#(\mathcal{O}_{X_{p},\alpha}/I_{\alpha})^{\times}
for any

These can be packaged together into a Dirichlet series,

\Phi_{X}(s)=\prod_{p\text{ prime}}\Phi(X_{p},p^{-s}).

From formula (

(14)

Additionally, Proposition

\Phi_{X}(s)=\frac{\zeta_{X}(s-1)}{\zeta_{X}(s)}.

(15)

3
Divisor Sum Functions

The divisor sum functions

\sigma_{m}(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}d^{m}

are another source of interesting Dirichlet series, namely

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sigma_{m}(n)}{n^{s}}=\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}}(s)\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}}(s-m).

(17)

In particular, the Dirichlet series for
\sigma_{1}
is
\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}}(s)\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}}(s-1)=\zeta_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(s)
, the zeta function for the arithmetic scheme
\mathbb{P}^{1}/\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}
. This agrees with the formula
\sigma_{1}(n)=\#\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})
, which we will generalize in a moment.

Note that
\sigma_{1}
is weakly multiplicative and for prime powers, it takes on the values
\sigma_{1}(p^{k})=1+p+\ldots+p^{k}=\frac{p^{k+1}-1}{p-1}
. Together these imply that for any

,

\sigma_{1}(n)=\prod_{p^{k}\mid\mid n}\frac{p^{k+1}-1}{p-1},

(18)

where
p^{k}\mid\mid n
denotes that
p^{k}\mid n
but
p^{k+1}\nmid n
.

By definition,
\sigma_{1}=\operatorname{id}*\zeta
as arithmetic functions. Then Möbius inversion implies
\operatorname{id}=\sigma_{1}*\mu
, or equivalently,

n=\sum_{d\mid n}\sigma_{1}(d)\mu\left(\frac{n}{d}\right).

(19)

4
Psi Functions

4.1
Dedekind’s
\psi
-function

Dedekind originally introduced the following function to study modular forms, but it is an interesting arithmetic function in its own right.

Definition 4.1
.

Dedekind’s
\psi
-function
is the arithmetic function
\psi:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
defined by

\psi(n)=n\prod_{p\mid n}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right).

From the definition, we can see that
\psi
is multiplicative and for any prime
p
,
\psi(p)=p+1
. In fact, these imply that for any squarefree integer
n
,
\psi(n)=\sigma_{1}(n)
. As arithmetic functions,
\psi=\operatorname{id}*|\mu|
, leading to the identity

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\psi(n)}{n^{s}}=\frac{\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}}(s)\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}}(s-1)}{\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}}(2s)}.

(23)

On the other hand,
\psi(n)
is the index of the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(n)
in
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, hence the connection to modular forms (or more directly, lattices). This interpretation is ripe for generalization, but let us first introduce a geometric interpretation of the
\psi
-function before moving to number fields, varieties, etc.

While the arithmetic functions
\phi
and
\sigma_{1}
had simple geometric interpretations, such an interpretation of
\psi
is less obvious. After failing to find anything in the literature, we discovered the following interpretation, aided by a suggestion of David Zureick-Brown.

First, notice that for a prime
p
,
\psi(p)=p+1
is the cardinality of the cyclic group
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}^{\times}/\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
, where
\mathbb{F}_{q}
denotes the field with
q
elements. This generalizes as follows. For an integer
k\geq 1
and a prime power
q=p^{a}
, let
\mathbb{W}_{k}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
be the ring of
p
-typical Witt vectors of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Define the quotient groups

G(p^{k})=\mathbb{W}_{k}(\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}})^{\times}/\mathbb{W}_{k}(\mathbb{F}_{p})^{\times}.

Lemma 4.2
.

\#G(p^{k})=\psi(p^{k})
.

Proof.

This follows from standard formulas for
\#\mathbb{W}_{k}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and
\#\mathbb{W}_{k}(\mathbb{F}_{q})^{\times}
. For more details, see
[
KZB
]
.
∎

For an arbitrary integer
n\geq 1
, with prime factorization
n=p_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots p_{r}^{k_{r}}
, let

G(n)=\prod_{i=1}^{r}G(p_{i}^{k_{i}})=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\mathbb{W}_{k_{i}}(\mathbb{F}_{p_{i}^{2}})^{\times}/\mathbb{W}_{k_{i}}(\mathbb{F}_{p_{i}})^{\times}.

Corollary 4.3
.

\#G(n)=\psi(n)
.

Question 4.4
.

Is there a group scheme
\mathcal{G}/\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}
such that
G(n)\cong\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})
for all
n
? For example, is there an interpretation of the groups
G(n)
in terms of the ring of big Witt vectors?

We have not been able to devise such a group scheme
\mathcal{G}
, so this question is left for the curious reader. Ideally,
\mathcal{G}
would also provide a geometric explanation for the identity
\psi=\operatorname{id}*|\mu|
.

5.1
Liouville’s
\lambda
-function

Definition 5.1
.

Liouville’s
\lambda
-function
is the arithmetic function
\lambda:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
defined by
\lambda(1)=1
and

\lambda(n)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if $n$ is a product of an even number of primes}\\
-1,&\text{if $n$ is a product of an odd number of primes}.\end{cases}

That is,
\lambda(n)=(-1)^{\Omega(n)}
where
\Omega\left(\prod p_{i}^{a_{i}}\right)=\sum a_{i}
.

It is well-known that
\lambda
is completely multiplicative:
\lambda(nm)=\lambda(n)\lambda(m)
for
any

n,m\in\mathbb{N}
. We also have that
\lambda(n)=\mu(n^{\prime})
where
n^{\prime}
is the squarefree part of
n
, i.e. the unique squarefree positive integer
n^{\prime}
such that
n=a^{2}n^{\prime}
for some
a\in\mathbb{N}
. As a consequence,

\sum_{d\mid n}\lambda(d)=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if $n$ is a square}\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(24)

Applying Möbius inversion yields

\lambda(n)=\sum_{d^{2}\mid n}\mu(n/d^{2}).

(25)

Another feature is that
\lambda*|\mu|=\delta
, i.e.
\lambda
is the convolution inverse of
|\mu|
, just as
\zeta
is the convolution inverse of
\mu
. On the level of Dirichlet series, we have

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda(n)}{n^{s}}=\frac{\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}}(2s)}{\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}}(s)}.

(26)

to a number field

where

. Show

in the incidence algebra

, which implies

Repeat for a variety
X/\mathbb{F}_{q}
and for a scheme
X/\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}
.

5.2

A famous number theory identity says that

r_{2}(n)=4\sum_{d\mid n}\chi_{-1}(d)

(27)

where

counts the number of ways

\chi_{-1}=\left(\frac{-1}{\cdot}\right)

.

Does this identity generalize to (i) number fields? (ii) higher dimensional geometric objects? (iii) a formula in some Grothendieck ring? What about formulas involving the sum of squares functions

for

?

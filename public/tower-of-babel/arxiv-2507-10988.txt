Short geodesics and multiplicities of eigenvalues of hyperbolic surfaces
Xiang He, Yunhui Wu, Haohao Zhang

Abstract
. In this paper, we obtain upper bounds on the multiplicity of Laplacian eigenvalues for closed hyperbolic surfaces in terms of the number of short closed geodesics and the genus g g . For example, we show that if the number of short closed geodesics is sublinear in g g , then the multiplicity of the first eigenvalue is also sublinear in g g . This makes new progress on a conjecture by Colin de Verdière in the mid 1980s.



1.
Introduction

Let
X_{g}
be a closed hyperbolic surface of genus
g\geq 2
, and let
\Delta
denote the Laplacian on
X_{g}
. By classical results in spectral theory, the spectrum of
\Delta
consists of a sequence of discrete nonnegative eigenvalues counted with multiplicities:

0=\lambda_{0}(X_{g})<\lambda_{1}(X_{g})\leq\lambda_{2}(X_{g})\leq\cdots\to+\infty.

For any
\lambda>0
, denote by
m(\lambda)
the multiplicity of
\lambda
in the spectrum of
\Delta
. In this paper, we are interested in studying the upper bounds of their multiplicities, especially in
m(\lambda_{1})
.

For any constant
\epsilon>0
that may depend on
g
, define

\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}(X_{g}):=\{\gamma;\ \text{$\gamma\subset X_{g}$ is a simple closed geodesic of length $<2\epsilon$}\},

and set
N_{\epsilon}(X_{g})=\#{\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}(X_{g})}
. Our first result is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X_{g}
be a closed hyperbolic surface of genus
g
. Then there exists a uniform constant
K\geq 1
such that for any constant
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, the multiplicity of
\lambda_{1}(X_{g})
satisfies

m(\lambda_{1}(X_{g}))\leq\frac{K}{\epsilon^{2}}\cdot\frac{g}{\log\log\left(\frac{10g}{N_{\epsilon}(X_{g})+1}\right)}.

Remark
.

(1)

If
N_{\epsilon_{0}}(X_{g})\leq f(g)=o(g)
for some
\epsilon_{0}>0
and positive function
f:\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 2}\to\mathbb{Z}
with sublinear growth, then Theorem
1.1
tells that

m(\lambda_{1}(X_{g}))\ll\frac{g}{\log\log\left(\frac{g}{f(g)}\right)}.

This upper bound is new and also sublinear in
g
. In particular, if
f(g)\ll g^{\alpha}
for any fixed
\alpha\in(0,1)
, then we have

m(\lambda_{1}(X_{g}))\ll\frac{g}{\log\log g}.

See Figure
1
for an example.

(2)

If
N_{\epsilon_{0}}(X_{g})=0
for some
\epsilon_{0}>0
, which is equivalent to saying that
X_{g}
has injectivity radius
\geq\epsilon_{0}
, as above Theorem
1.1
tells that

m(\lambda_{1}(X_{g}))\ll\frac{g}{\log\log g}.

This was due to Letrouit-Machado
[
LM24
]
for this case. We remark here that Jiang-Tidor-Yao-Zhang-Zhao
[
JTY+21
]
proved the same sublinear upper bound for the second largest eigenvalue of connected
g
-vertex graphs with bounded maximum degree.

(3)

By the classical Collar Lemma, it is known that
N_{\epsilon}(X_{g})\leq 3g-3
whenever
\epsilon
is small. Thus, if we choose
\epsilon=0.5
, Theorem
1.1
then tells that for any closed hyperbolic surface
X_{g}
of genus
g
,

m(\lambda_{1}(X_{g}))\ll g.

The linear upper bound was first obtained by Besson
[
Bes80
]
.

Figure 1.
A closed hyperbolic surface
\mathcal{X}_{g}
of genus

, where
\gamma_{i}\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon_{0}}(\mathcal{X}_{g})
and
N_{\epsilon_{0}}(\mathcal{X}_{g})=o(g)
for some fixed
\epsilon_{0}>0
. So
m(\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{X}_{g}))=o(g)
.

A key novel ingredient in proving Theorem
1.1
is to find a linear subspace
S
of the eigenspace
E(\lambda_{1}(X_{g}))
with respect to
\lambda_{1}(X_{g})
such that the dimension
\dim(S)
of the subspace
S
is uniformly comparable to
\dim(E(\lambda_{1}(X_{g})))=m_{1}(\lambda_{1}(X_{g}))
, and the
L^{2}
-norm of each eigenfunction in
S
on
X_{g}
is uniformly comparable to its
L^{2}
-norm on the
\epsilon
-thick part of
X_{g}
. And then restricted to the thick part of
X_{g}
, we follow a strategy similar to that in the recent works
[
JTY+21
,
LM24
]
to complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Our second result concerns the multiplicities of small eigenvalues. For any
\delta\in(0,1/2)
, set

\epsilon(\delta)=\arcsinh\frac{1}{\sinh(1/\delta+2)},

and for any
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon(\delta))
, set

I_{\epsilon}(X_{g})=\textit{the number of components of }X_{g}\setminus\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}(X_{g}).

Our second theorem is as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X_{g}
be a closed hyperbolic surface of genus
g
, and let
\lambda>0
be an eigenvalue of
X_{g}
. If
\lambda\leq 1/4-\delta^{2}
, then for any
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon(\delta))
, there exists a constant
C(\epsilon)>0
only depending on
\epsilon
such that

m(\lambda)\leq C(\epsilon)\sqrt{\lambda}\cdot g+24I_{\epsilon}(X_{g}).

Remark
.

(1)

If
\lambda_{1}(X_{g})=o(1)
and
I_{\epsilon_{0}}(X_{g})=o(g)
for some fixed
\epsilon_{0}
as
g\to\infty
, then Theorem
1.2
tells that
m(\lambda_{1}(X_{g}))=o(g)
. Note that
N_{\epsilon_{0}}(X_{g})
may have growth rate
g
, in which case Theorem
1.1
only gives a linear upper bound for
m(\lambda_{1}(X_{g}))
. See Figure
2
for an example.

(2)

Suppose
\epsilon\ll 1/(\log g)^{\kappa}
for some
\kappa>0
as
g\to\infty
. If
\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}(X_{g})
separates
X_{g}
, and
I_{\epsilon_{0}}(X_{g})\ll g/(\log g)^{\kappa}
for some fixed
\epsilon_{0}
, then the upper bound given by Theorem
1.2
is slightly stronger than that given by Theorem
1.1
.

\mathcal{Y}_{g}

separated by

geodesics into two pieces, where

and
\epsilon=o(1)
as
g\to\infty
. So
m(\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{Y}_{g}))=o(g)
.

We remark here that for a family of closed hyperbolic surfaces
\{X_{g}^{t}\}_{t\in(0,1]}
of genus
g
that has
(3g-3)
pairwise disjoint simple closed geodesics simultaneously pinching to zero as
t\to 0
, it is known from
e.g.,
[
SWY80
]
or
[
HW25
, Proof of Theorem 21]
that
\lambda_{2g-3}(X_{g}^{t})\to 0
uniformly as
t\to 0
. However, both Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
cannot distinguish the first
(2g-3)
nonzero eigenvalues of this particular family
\{X_{g}^{t}\}_{t\in(0,1]}
. To our knowledge, it is not even known whether the multiplicity
m(\lambda_{1}(X_{g}^{t}))
is sublinear in
g
when
t
is close to
0
.

Related works

The first upper bound on the multiplicity of Laplacian eigenvalues on closed Riemannian surfaces was established by Cheng
[
Che76
]
, who showed that for any closed Riemannian surface of genus
g
, the multiplicity of the
i
-th eigenvalue
\lambda_{i}
satisfies

m(\lambda_{i})\leq\frac{1}{2}(2g+i+1)(2g+i+2),\qquad\Forall i\geq 1.

This was later improved by Besson
[
Bes80
]
, who obtained the first linear upper bound:

m(\lambda_{i})\leq 4g+2i+1.

Many refinements of Besson’s bound have since been obtained. Nadirashvili
[
Nad88
]
improved the bound for
g\geq 2
to
4g+2i-1
; Sévennec
[
Sév02
]
improved it further for
g\geq 2
,
i=1
to
2g+3
; and Bourque–Petri
[
FBP23
]
improved the bound for
i=1
to
2g-1
for hyperbolic surfaces of sufficiently large genus.

Colin de Verdière’s conjecture

In
[
CdV86
]
, Colin de Verdière
conjectured
that the multiplicity of the first nonzero Laplacian eigenvalue on a closed orientable surface
M_{g}
of genus
g
is bounded from above by

\mathrm{chr}(M_{g})-1,

where
chromatic number

\mathrm{chr}(M_{g})
is the supremum of the natural numbers
n
for which the complete graph on
n
vertices can be embedded in
M_{g}
. It is known from
[
RY68
]
of Ringel-Youngs that

\mathrm{chr}(M_{g})=\Big\lfloor\frac{1}{2}\big(7+\sqrt{48g+1}\big)\Big\rfloor.

In support of this, Colbois and Colin de Verdière constructed, in
[
CCdV88
]
, for every
g\geq 3
, a hyperbolic surface
X_{g}
such that

m(\lambda_{1}(X_{g}))=\Big\lfloor\frac{1+\sqrt{8g+1}}{2}\Big\rfloor,

demonstrating that the maximal multiplicity grows at least on the order of
\sqrt{g}
. However, counterexamples to the conjecture are found for
g=10
and
g=17
by Bourque-Gruda Mediavilla-Petri-Pineault in
[
BGMPP23
]
.

Nevertheless, for closed hyperbolic surfaces of high genus, it is quite interesting to
determine the right growth rate of the maximal multiplicity of the first nonzero eigenvalue of the Laplacian. Is it
\sqrt{g}
in the above conjecture of Colin de Verdière, or
\frac{g}{\log\log g}
as shown above, or something else?
Several sublinear upper bounds on the multiplicity of the first nonzero eigenvalue of the Laplacian on hyperbolic surfaces have been established under certain assumptions
[
LM24
,
FBP23
]
.

Notations.

For any two positive functions
f(g)
and
h(g)
, we say
f=o(h)
if
\lim\limits_{g\to\infty}\frac{f(g)}{h(g)}=0
; we say
f\ll h
if there exists a uniform constant
C>0
such that
f\leq Ch
. We use
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
to denote the greatest integer function.

2.
Thick-thin decomposition

Let
X_{g}
be a closed hyperbolic surface of genus
g
, and let
\dist(\cdot,\cdot)
denote the distance function on
X_{g}
. For any simple closed geodesic
\gamma\subset X_{g}
, the collar around
\gamma
of width
w
is defined by

\collar(\gamma,w):=\{x\in X_{g}|\ \dist(x,\gamma)\leq w\}.

Let
\ell(\gamma)
be the length of
\gamma
. The Collar Lemma asserts that the collar of width

(1)

w(\gamma):=\arcsinh\frac{1}{\sinh\frac{\ell(\gamma)}{2}}

is always embedded in
X_{g}
. We call it the standard collar of
\gamma
. Moreover,
\collar(\gamma,w(\gamma))
is isometric to
[-w(\gamma),w(\gamma)]\times\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
endowed with the hyperbolic metric

\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho^{2}+\ell(\gamma)^{2}\cosh^{2}\!\rho\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t^{2}.

The pair of coordinates
(\rho,t)
is called the Fermi coordinates on
\collar(\gamma,w(\gamma))
. For any
x\in X_{g}
, let
\inj(x)
denote the injectivity radius at
x
. We recall that

Lemma 2.1

(Collar Lemma,
[
Bus92
, Theorem 4.1.6]
)
.

Let
\{\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{N}\}
be the set of all simple closed geodesics of length
\leq 2\arcsinh 1
in
X_{g}
, and let
\collar_{i}
be the standard collar of
\gamma_{i}
. Then the followings hold.

(1)

N\leq 3g-3
.

(2)

The collars
\collar_{i}
are pairwise disjoint.

(3)

Suppose
x\in\collar_{i}
and
\dist(x,\partial\collar_{i})=d
, then

(2)

\sinh\inj(x)=\cosh\frac{\ell(\gamma_{i})}{2}\cosh d-\sinh d.

Without loss of generality, we now fix
\epsilon
so that

(3)

0<\epsilon<\arcsinh\frac{1}{\sinh 2}\approx 0.272.

Recall that

\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}(X_{g})=\{\gamma;\ \text{$\gamma\subset X_{g}$ is a simple closed geodesic of length $<2\epsilon$}\}.

Since
\epsilon<\arcsinh 1
, by Part (2) of Lemma
2.1
,
\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}(X_{g})
consists of pairwise disjoint simple closed geodesics. Moreover, by (
1
), for any
\gamma\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}(X_{g})
we have

w(\gamma)>1.

In the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we will use a thick-thin decomposition of
X_{g}
which is defined as follows.

Definition 2.2
.

The thin part of
X_{g}
is defined as

X^{\mathrm{thin}}_{g}:=\bigcup_{\gamma\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}(X_{g})}\collar(\gamma,w(\gamma)-1).

The thick part of
X_{g}
is defined as the closure of its complement, which is given by

X^{\mathrm{thick}}_{g}:=\overline{X\setminus X^{\mathrm{thin}}_{g}}=\mathcal{A}_{1}\sqcup\mathcal{A}_{2}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\mathcal{A}_{I}.

Here
I\geq 1
, and
\mathcal{A}_{k}
,
1\leq k\leq I
, are all components of
X^{\mathrm{thick}}_{g}
.

Then we have the following.

•

By (
2
) and (
3
), for any
x\in X^{\mathrm{thick}}_{g}
we have

(4)

\inj(x)\geq\min\big\{\epsilon,\,\arcsinh(\cosh 1-\sinh 1)\big\}=\epsilon.

•

\Forall\gamma\in\mathcal{N}_{\varepsilon}(X_{g})
, the length of a component of
\partial\collar(\gamma,w(\gamma)-1)
is equal to

(5)

\displaystyle\ell(\gamma)\cosh(w(\gamma)-1)\leq\frac{2}{e}\ell(\gamma)\cosh w(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{e}\sqrt{\ell(\gamma)^{2}+\frac{\ell(\gamma)^{2}}{\sinh^{2}(\ell(\gamma)/2)}}\leq\frac{2}{e}\sqrt{1+4}<2.

3.
Mass distribution of eigenfunctions

One key ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1.1
is that, if
m(\lambda)
is large (compared to the number of short closed geodesics
N_{\epsilon}
), then we can find a subspace of the eigenspace
E(\lambda)
such that the
L^{2}
-norm of any eigenfunction in this subspace is concentrated on the thick part of
X_{g}
.

Let
f
be an eigenfunction of
\Delta
with eigenvalue
\lambda
, and let
\collar(\gamma,w)
be an embedded collar in
X_{g}
. In the Fermi coordinates
(\rho,t)
, the Laplacian is given by

-\Delta=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\rho^{2}}+\tanh\rho\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}+\frac{1}{\ell(\gamma)^{2}\cosh^{2}\!\rho}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}.

Suppose
f
has the following Fourier expansion on
\collar(\gamma,w)
,

(6)

f(\rho,t)=\alpha_{0}(\rho)+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\big(\alpha_{j}(\rho)\cos(2\pi jt)+\beta_{j}(\rho)\sin(2\pi jt)\big).

Then
\sqrt{\cosh\rho}\cdot\alpha_{j}
and
\sqrt{\cosh\rho}\cdot\beta_{j}
satisfy the following differential equation (one may refer to
e.g.,
[
Gam02
, Section 4]
,
[
Mon15
, Section 3]
and
[
WZ25
, Section 5]
for more details):

(7)

\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{2}u}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho^{2}}=\bigg(\frac{1}{4}-\lambda+\Big(\frac{1}{4}+\frac{4\pi^{2}j^{2}}{\ell(\gamma)^{2}}\Big)\frac{1}{\cosh^{2}\rho}\bigg)u.

Let
\varphi_{j}
,
\psi_{j}
be two linearly independent solutions of (
7
) satisfying

\varphi_{j}(0)=\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\psi_{j}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}(0)=0,\textrm{\quad and\quad}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\varphi_{j}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}(0)=\psi_{j}(0)=1.

Recall that
w(\gamma)
is the width of the standard collar of
\gamma
. We have the following elementary lemma:

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that
\ell(\gamma)\leq 1
. If
j\geq\lfloor\sqrt{\lambda}\rfloor+1
, then

\max\bigg\{\frac{\int_{0}^{w(\gamma)-1}\varphi_{j}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}{\int_{0}^{w(\gamma)}\varphi_{j}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho},\ \frac{\int_{0}^{w(\gamma)-1}\psi_{j}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}{\int_{0}^{w(\gamma)}\psi_{j}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}\bigg\}\leq\frac{4}{e^{2}}.

Proof.

Suppose that
\rho\leq w(\gamma)
. By (
1
) and our assumption
\ell(\gamma)\leq 1
, we have

\ell(\gamma)^{2}\cosh^{2}\!\rho\leq\ell(\gamma)^{2}+\frac{\ell(\gamma)^{2}}{\sinh^{2}\!\frac{\ell(\gamma)}{2}}\leq 5.

Therefore if
j\geq\lfloor\sqrt{\lambda}\rfloor+1
, then

\frac{1}{4}-\lambda+\Big(\frac{1}{4}+\frac{4\pi^{2}j^{2}}{\ell(\gamma)^{2}}\Big)\frac{1}{\cosh^{2}\rho}\geq\frac{4\pi^{2}j^{2}}{5}-\lambda\geq 1.

It follows that
\varphi_{j}
and
\psi_{j}
satisfy

\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{2}\varphi_{j}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho^{2}}\geq\varphi_{j}\text{\quad and\quad}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}^{2}\psi_{j}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho^{2}}\geq\psi_{j}.

By plugging
u_{1}=\varphi_{j}\textit{ or }\psi_{j}
and
u_{2}=\cosh\rho
into
[
WZ25
, Lemma 5.2]
, then we have

(8)

\displaystyle\max\bigg\{\frac{\int_{0}^{w(\gamma)-1}\varphi_{j}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}{\int_{0}^{w(\gamma)}\varphi_{j}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho},\ \frac{\int_{0}^{w(\gamma)-1}\psi_{j}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}{\int_{0}^{w(\gamma)}\psi_{j}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\int_{0}^{w(\gamma)-1}\cosh^{2}\!\rho\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}{\int_{0}^{w(\gamma)}\cosh^{2}\!\rho\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho}

\displaystyle=\frac{2w(\gamma)-2+\sinh(2w(\gamma)-2)}{2w(\gamma)+\sinh(2w(\gamma))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3\exp(2w(\gamma)-2)}{\exp(2w(\gamma))-\exp(-2w(\gamma))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{e^{2}},

where we use the fact that
\exp(2w(\gamma))-\exp(-2w(\gamma))\geq 3\exp(2w(\gamma))/4
.
The proof is complete.
∎

Next, for any
j\geq 0
and any simple closed geodesic
\gamma\subset X_{g}
, we define

\displaystyle\varphi_{j,\gamma}^{1}(x)=\begin{cases}\varphi_{j}(\rho)\cos(2\pi jt),&x=(\rho,t)\in\collar(\gamma,w(\gamma));\\
0,&x\notin\collar(\gamma,w(\gamma)).\end{cases}

For any
j\geq 1
, we define

\displaystyle\varphi_{j,\gamma}^{2}(x)=\begin{cases}\varphi_{j}(\rho)\sin(2\pi jt),&x=(\rho,t)\in\collar(\gamma,w(\gamma));\\
0,&x\notin\collar(\gamma,w(\gamma)).\end{cases}

And let
\psi_{j,\gamma}^{1},\psi_{j,\gamma}^{2}\in L^{2}(X_{g})
be defined similarly. For all
j\geq 0
, we now define a subspace
S(j,\gamma)
of
L^{2}(X_{g})
as

S(j,\gamma):=\mathrm{span}\big\{\varphi_{0,\gamma}^{1},\varphi_{1,\gamma}^{1},\varphi_{1,\gamma}^{2},\cdots,\varphi_{j,\gamma}^{1},\varphi_{j,\gamma}^{2},\psi_{0,\gamma}^{1},\psi_{1,\gamma}^{1},\psi_{1,\gamma}^{2},\cdots,\psi_{j,\gamma}^{1},\psi_{j,\gamma}^{2}\big\}.

Then we have

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
f
be an eigenfunction with eigenvalue
\lambda
, and let
\gamma
be a simple closed geodesic of length
\leq 1
. If
f\in S(\lfloor\sqrt{\lambda}\rfloor,\gamma)^{\bot}
, the orthogonal complement of
S(\lfloor\sqrt{\lambda}\rfloor,\gamma)
, then

\int_{\collar(\gamma,w(\gamma)-1)}f^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}A\leq 2\int_{\collar(\gamma,w(\gamma))\setminus\collar(\gamma,w(\gamma)-1)}f^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}A.

Proof.

We use the same notation as in (
6
). Assume that

\displaystyle\sqrt{\cosh\rho}\cdot\alpha_{j}(\rho)

\displaystyle=a_{j1}\cdot\varphi_{j}(\rho)+a_{j2}\cdot\psi_{j}(\rho),

\displaystyle\sqrt{\cosh\rho}\cdot\beta_{j}(\rho)

\displaystyle=b_{j1}\cdot\varphi_{j}(\rho)+b_{j2}\cdot\psi_{j}(\rho).

Then the
L^{2}
-norm of
f
on the collar
\collar(\gamma,w)
is

(9)

\int_{\collar(\gamma,w)}f^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}A=2\ell^{2}(\gamma)\cdot\int_{0}^{w}\Big(a_{01}^{2}\varphi_{0}^{2}+a_{02}^{2}\psi_{0}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(c_{j1}^{2}\varphi_{j}^{2}+c_{j2}^{2}\psi_{j}^{2})\Big)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho,

where
c_{ji}^{2}=a_{ji}^{2}+b_{ji}^{2}
. If
f\in S(\lfloor\sqrt{\lambda}\rfloor,\gamma)^{\bot}
, then

(10)

a_{01}=a_{02}=0,\text{\quad and\quad}c_{j1}=c_{j2}=0,\ 1\leq j\leq\lfloor\sqrt{\lambda}\rfloor.

It then follows from (
9
), (
10
) and Lemma
3.1
that

\displaystyle\int_{\collar(\gamma,w(\gamma)-1)}f^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}A

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4/e^{2}}{1-4/e^{2}}\int_{\collar(\gamma,w(\gamma))\setminus\collar(\gamma,w(\gamma)-1)}f^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}A

\displaystyle<2\int_{\collar(\gamma,w(\gamma))\setminus\collar(\gamma,w(\gamma)-1)}f^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}A.

The proof is complete.
∎

Recall that for
0<\epsilon<1
,

\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}(X_{g})=\{\gamma;\ \text{$\gamma\subset X_{g}$ is a simple closed geodesic of length $<2\epsilon$}\}.

We have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
X_{g}
be a closed hyperbolic surface, and let
E(\lambda)\subset L^{2}(X_{g})
be an eigenspace of
\Delta
with respect to the eigenvalue
\lambda
. Then there exists a subspace
S
of
E(\lambda)
satisfying

\codim S\leq 4(\lfloor\sqrt{\lambda}\rfloor+1)\cdot N_{\epsilon}(X_{g})

such that
\Forall f\in S
(if
S\neq 0
),

\int_{X_{g}}f^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}A\leq 3\int_{X^{\mathrm{thick}}_{g}}f^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}A,

where
X^{\mathrm{thick}}_{g}
is the thick part of
X_{g}
corresponding to
\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}(X_{g})
.

Proof.

Consider the orthogonal projection

\Pi:E(\lambda)\to\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}(X_{g})}S(\lfloor\sqrt{\lambda}\rfloor,\gamma).

Then

\codim(\ker\Pi)\leq\dim\Big(\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}(X_{g})}S(\lfloor\sqrt{\lambda}\rfloor,\gamma)\Big)\leq 4(\lfloor\sqrt{\lambda}\rfloor+1)\cdot N_{\epsilon}(X_{g}),

and by Lemma
3.2
, for any
f\in\ker\Pi
(if
\ker\Pi\neq 0
) we have

\displaystyle\int_{X_{g}}f^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}A

\displaystyle=\int_{\bigcup_{\gamma\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}(X_{g})}\collar(\gamma,w(\gamma)-1)}f^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}A+\int_{X^{\mathrm{thick}}_{g}}f^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}A

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{\bigcup_{\gamma\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}(X_{g})}\collar(\gamma,w(\gamma))\setminus\collar(\gamma,w(\gamma)-1)}f^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}A+\int_{X^{\mathrm{thick}}_{g}}f^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}A

\displaystyle\leq 3\int_{X^{\mathrm{thick}}_{g}}f^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}A.

Thus, the subspace
\ker\Pi\subset E(\lambda)
satisfies the desired properties.
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
.

Let
X_{g}
be a closed hyperbolic surface of genus
g
. Recall that

\epsilon\in\Big(0,\arcsinh\frac{1}{\sinh 2}\Big)

is a fixed constant, and
N_{\epsilon}(X_{g})=\#\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}(X_{g})
. By the general upper bound of the first nonzero eigenvalue (
e.g.,
[
Che75
, Corollary 2.3]
or
[
WZ25
, Proposition 9.1]
), one has that as
g\to\infty
,

\lambda_{1}(X_{g})\leq\frac{1}{4}+o(1).

Now we always assume

\lambda_{1}(X_{g})<1

throughout this section by taking
g
sufficiently large.

4.1.
Rescaling the metric

We first rescale the hyperbolic metric on
X_{g}
by
\epsilon^{-2}
to obtain a new Riemannian surface
X_{g,\epsilon}
. Then the 2-dimensional simply connected space form of curvature
-\epsilon^{2}
, denoted by
\mathbb{H}_{\epsilon}
, is the universal cover of
X_{g,\epsilon}
.
Let
\dist_{\epsilon}(\cdot,\cdot)
denote the distance function on
X_{g,\epsilon}
, and for any
x\in X_{g,\epsilon}
, let
B_{\epsilon}(x,r)
denote the geodesic ball of radius
r>0
centered at
x
.

Let
\Delta_{\epsilon}
be the Laplacian on
X_{g,\epsilon}
. Then
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of the Laplacian
\Delta
on
X_{g}
if and only if
\epsilon^{2}\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
\Delta_{\epsilon}
. It follows that

m(\lambda_{1}(X_{g,\epsilon}))=m(\lambda_{1}(X_{g})).

Hence, estimating the upper bound of
m(\lambda_{1}(X_{g}))
is equivalent to estimating that of
m(\lambda_{1}(X_{g,\epsilon}))
. In the remainder of this section, we will work with
X_{g,\epsilon}
. Moreover, we use the notations

X^{\mathrm{thick}}_{g,\epsilon},\ X^{\mathrm{thin}}_{g,\epsilon},\ \mathcal{A}_{k,\epsilon},\ \text{and\ }\collar(\gamma,w)_{\epsilon}

to denote the images of

X^{\mathrm{thick}}_{g},\ X^{\mathrm{thin}}_{g},\ \mathcal{A}_{k},\ \text{and\ }\collar(\gamma,w)

under the rescaling by
\epsilon^{-2}
, respectively. For any
x\in X_{g,\epsilon}
, the Gaussian curvature

(11)

\mathrm{cur}(x)=-\epsilon^{2}>-1,

and by (
4
), for any
x\in X^{\mathrm{thick}}_{g,\epsilon}
,

(12)

\inj(x)\geq 1.

Let
r>0
. We recall the notions of
r
-
net
and
r
-
separated set
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
Y\subset X_{g,\epsilon}
be a subset.

(1)

A set of points
\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{l}\}
is called an
r
-net
of
Y
if for any
y\in Y
, there exists
x_{i}
such that
\dist_{\epsilon}(x_{i},y)\leq r
.

(2)

A set of points
\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{l}\}
is called an
r
-separated set
if
\dist_{\epsilon}(x_{i},x_{j})\geq r
for any
i\neq j
.

(3)

A set of points
\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{l}\}
is called an
r
-separated net
of
Y
if it is both
r
-separated and an
r
-net of
Y
.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{k,\epsilon}
,
1\leq k\leq I
, be the components of
X^{\mathrm{thick}}_{g,\varepsilon}
. For all
r>0
and
k\in[1,I]
, set

(13)

\mathcal{A}_{k,\epsilon}^{r}=\{x\in\mathcal{A}_{k,\epsilon}|\ \dist_{\epsilon}(x,X^{\mathrm{thin}}_{g,\epsilon})\geq r\}.

Note that
\mathcal{A}_{k,\epsilon}^{r}
might not be connected.

Lemma 4.2
.

With the same notation as above,
if
r\geq 4
and
\mathcal{A}_{k,\epsilon}^{r}\neq\emptyset
, then
\mathcal{A}_{k,\epsilon}^{r}
admits an
r
-separated net of cardinality at most

\max\bigg\{1,\,\frac{16\area(\mathcal{A}_{k,\epsilon})}{\pi\cdot r}\bigg\}.

Proof.

The proof is standard. Firstly, if there exists
x\in\mathcal{A}_{k,\epsilon}^{r}
such that
\mathcal{A}_{k,\epsilon}^{r}\subset B_{\epsilon}(x,r)
, then the singleton set
\{x\}
is an
r
-net of
\mathcal{A}_{k,\epsilon}^{r}
. Otherwise, let

\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{l}\}\subset\mathcal{A}_{k,\epsilon}^{r},\quad l\geq 2

be a maximal
r
-separated set. This is clearly an
r
-separated net of
\mathcal{A}_{k,\epsilon}^{r}
. So we have

B_{\epsilon}(x_{i},r/2)\cap B_{\epsilon}(x_{j},r/2)=\emptyset,\quad\Forall i\neq j.

Moreover, by the definition of
\mathcal{A}_{k,\epsilon}^{r}
, we have
B_{\epsilon}(x_{i},r/2)\subset\mathcal{A}_{k,\epsilon}
,
\Forall 1\leq i\leq l
, so

(14)

\sum_{i=1}^{l}\area\big(B_{\epsilon}(x_{i},r/2)\big)\leq\area(\mathcal{A}_{k,\epsilon}).

Next, for each
1\leq i\leq l
, there exists
y_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{k,\epsilon}
such that
\dist_{\epsilon}(x_{i},y_{i})=r/2
. Let
\alpha_{i}:[0,r/2]\to X^{\mathrm{thick}}_{g,\epsilon}
be a shortest geodesic segment from
x_{i}
to
y_{i}
parameterized by the arc length. Then for each integer
n\in\{0,\cdots,\lfloor r/2\rfloor-1\}
, we have

B_{\epsilon}(\alpha_{i}(n),1/2)\subset B_{\epsilon}(x_{i},r/2)\subset\mathcal{A}_{k,\epsilon},

and these geodesic balls are pairwise disjoint. By (
11
) and (
12
),
\mathrm{cur}(\alpha_{i}(n))=-\epsilon^{2}
and
\inj(\alpha_{i}(n))\geq 1
, which implies

\area\big(B_{\epsilon}(\alpha_{i}(n),1/2)\big)=\area\big(B_{\mathbb{H}_{\epsilon}}(1/2)\big)=\frac{2\pi}{\epsilon^{2}}(\cosh\frac{\epsilon}{2}-1)\geq\frac{\pi}{4},

where
B_{\mathbb{H}_{\epsilon}}(1/2)
denotes the geodesic ball of radius
1/2
in
\mathbb{H}_{\epsilon}
. The last inequality follows from the estimate
\cosh\epsilon/2-1\geq\epsilon^{2}/8
. Thus,

\displaystyle\area\big(B_{\epsilon}(x_{i},r/2)\big)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{n=0}^{\lfloor r/2\rfloor-1}\area\big(B_{\epsilon}(\alpha_{i}(n),1/2)\big)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\pi}{4}\cdot\Big\lfloor\frac{r}{2}\Big\rfloor\geq\frac{\pi}{16}\cdot r.

Combining this with (
14
), we obtain the desired bound.
∎

Write
X_{g,\epsilon}=\mathbb{H}_{\epsilon}/\Gamma
, and let
K_{t,\epsilon}(\cdot,\cdot)
denote the heat kernel on
X_{g,\epsilon}
. Then for any
f\in L^{2}(X_{g,\epsilon})
,

(e^{-t\Delta_{\epsilon}}f)(x)=\int_{X_{g,\epsilon}}K_{t,\epsilon}(x,y)f(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

Moreover, let
k_{t,\epsilon}(\cdot,\cdot)
denote the heat kernel on
\mathbb{H}_{\epsilon}
, then

(15)

K_{t,\epsilon}(x,y)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}k_{t,\epsilon}(\gamma\tilde{x},\tilde{y}),

where
\tilde{x}
and
\tilde{y}
are lifts of
x
and
y
, respectively. Since
k_{t,\epsilon}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})
depends only on
\dist_{\mathbb{H}_{\epsilon}}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})
, we also write it as
k_{t,\epsilon}(\dist_{\mathbb{H}_{\epsilon}}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y}))
. Next, we present a standard estimate for the heat kernel on
X_{g,\epsilon}
which will be used in the subsequent analysis.

Lemma 4.3
.

There exists a universal constant
C_{1}>0
, independent of
\epsilon
, such that for any point
x\in X_{g,\epsilon}
with
\inj(x)\geq 1
and any
t\geq 1
,

\|K_{t,\epsilon}(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(X_{g,\epsilon})}\leq C_{1}\exp(4t).

Proof.

Since

k_{t,\epsilon}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})=k_{t,\epsilon}(\dist_{\mathbb{H}_{\epsilon}}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y}))=\epsilon^{2}k_{\epsilon^{2}t,1}(\dist_{\mathbb{H}_{\epsilon}}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})),

it follows from
[
Ber16
, Lemma 3.13]
that for all
t\geq 1
,

(16)

k_{t,\epsilon}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y})\ll\exp(-\frac{\dist_{\H_\epsilon}(\tilde x,\tilde y)^2}{8\epsilon^2t})\ll\exp(-\frac{\dist_{\H_\epsilon}(\tilde x,\tilde y)^2}{8t}),\quad\Forall\tilde{x},\tilde{y}\in\mathbb{H}_{\epsilon}.

For any
m\geq 0\in\mathbb{N}
and any
\tilde{x},\tilde{y}\in\mathbb{H}_{\epsilon}
, since
\inj(x)\geq 1
,

(17)

\displaystyle\#\{\gamma\in\Gamma|\ m\leq\dist_{\mathbb{H}_{\epsilon}}(\gamma\tilde{x},\tilde{y})<m+1\}\leq\frac{\area\big(B_{\mathbb{H}_{\epsilon}}(m+1+1/2)\big)}{\area\big(B_{\mathbb{H}_{\epsilon}}(1/2)\big)}

\displaystyle\ll\area\big(B_{\mathbb{H}_{\epsilon}}(m+1+1/2)\big)\ll\area\big(B_{\mathbb{H}}(m+1+1/2)\big)\ll\exp(m).

Therefore, by (
15
), (
16
) and (
17
),

\displaystyle K_{t,\epsilon}(x,y)

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\exp(m-\frac{m^2}{8t})

\displaystyle\leq(4t+1)\exp(2t)+\sum_{m\geq 4t}\exp(8t-m)

\displaystyle\ll\exp(4t).

The proof is complete.
∎

For simplicity, we write
\lambda_{1,\epsilon}
for
\lambda_{1}(X_{g,\epsilon})=\epsilon^{2}\lambda_{1}(X_{g})
, and omit
X_{g}
in
\lambda_{1}(X_{g})
,
\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}(X_{g})
, and
N_{\epsilon}(X_{g})
for the remainder of this section. Set

(18)

r_{1}=c\log\log\Big(\frac{K g}{N_\epsilon+1}\Big),\qquad r_{2}=c\log\Big(\frac{K g}{N_\epsilon+1}\Big),

where
c>0
is a universal constant that will be chosen later. We assume that
K\geq 10
is a sufficiently large universal constant such that
\frac{Kg}{N_{\epsilon}+1}
is large enough for our analysis, particularly to ensure that
r_{1}\gg 1
and
r_{2}/r_{1}\gg 1
.

4.2.
Reduce the problem to the thick part

In this subsection, we show that it suffices to consider only the thick part of
X_{g,\epsilon}
. Set

X_{g,\epsilon}^{r_{1}}:=\{x\in X^{\mathrm{thick}}_{g,\epsilon}|\ \dist_{\epsilon}(x,X^{\mathrm{thin}}_{g,\epsilon})\geq r_{1}\}.

First, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 4.4
.

There exists an
r_{1}
-net
\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{l}\}
of
X_{g,\epsilon}^{r_{1}}
such that

l\ll\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\frac{g}{\log\log\frac{10g}{N_{\epsilon}+1}}.

Proof.

By definition we have

X_{g,\epsilon}^{r_{1}}=\mathcal{A}_{1,\epsilon}^{r_{1}}\sqcup\mathcal{A}_{2,\epsilon}^{r_{1}}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\mathcal{A}_{I,\epsilon}^{r_{1}},\quad I\geq 1,

where
\mathcal{A}_{k,\epsilon}^{r_{1}}
is defined by (
13
). Clearly, the union of
r_{1}
-nets of
\mathcal{A}_{k,\epsilon}^{r_{1}}
’s is an
r_{1}
-net of
X_{g,\epsilon}^{r_{1}}
. Thus by Lemma
4.2
,

\displaystyle l

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{I}\max\bigg\{1,\frac{16\area(\mathcal{A}_{k,\epsilon})}{\pi\cdot r_{1}}\bigg\}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{I}\bigg(1+\frac{16\area(\mathcal{A}_{k,\epsilon})}{\pi\cdot r_{1}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq N_{\epsilon}+1+\frac{64\pi\epsilon^{-2}(g-1)}{\pi\cdot r_{1}}\ll\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\frac{g}{\log\log\frac{10g}{N_{\epsilon}+1}},

where we use the bounds

I\leq N_{\epsilon}+1\text{\quad and\quad}\sum_{k=1}^{I}\area(\mathcal{A}_{k,\epsilon})\leq\area(X_{g,\epsilon})=4\pi\epsilon^{-2}(g-1).

The proof is complete.
∎

Next, we extend
\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{l}\}
to a maximal 1-separated set of
X_{g,\epsilon}
, denoted by

\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{l},x_{l+1},\cdots,x_{s}\}

where
l<s\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. For each
i\in\{1,\cdots,s\}
, define

V_{i}:=\big\{y\in X_{g,\epsilon}|\ \dist_{\epsilon}(y,x_{i})\leq\dist_{\epsilon}(y,x_{j}),\ \forall j\in\{1,\cdots,s\}\big\}.

Since
\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{s}\}
is a 1-separated set,

B_{\epsilon}(x_{i},1/2)\subset V_{i}\subset B_{\epsilon}(x_{i},1),\quad\forall i\in\{1,\cdots,s\}.

For each
i\in\{1,\cdots,l\}
, set

\psi_{i}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\area(V_{i})}}\textbf{1}_{\mathring{V}_{i}},

where
\textbf{1}_{\mathring{V}_{i}}
denotes the indicator function of the interior
\mathring{V}_{i}
of
V_{i}
. Then
\norm{\psi_i}_{L^{2}}=1
. Moreover,
\Forall 1\leq i\leq l
, since
\inj(x_{i})\geq 1
, it follows that

(19)

\area(V_{i})\geq\area\big(B_{\epsilon}(x_{i},1/2)\big)=\frac{2\pi}{\epsilon^{2}}(\cosh\frac{\epsilon}{2}-1)\geq\frac{\pi}{4}.

Let
P
be the orthogonal projection from
L^{2}(X_{g,\epsilon})
to
\mathrm{span}\{\psi_{1},\cdots,\psi_{l}\}^{\bot}
,
i.e.

Pf=f-\sum_{k=1}^{l}\langle f,\psi_{k}\rangle\psi_{k},\quad\forall f\in L^{2}(X_{g,\epsilon}).

Let
\delta_{x}
denote the Dirac delta distribution at
x
, then
P\delta_{x}
is given as

P\delta_{x}=\delta_{x}-\sum_{k=1}^{l}\psi_{k}(x)\psi_{k}.

Following the ideas of
[
LM24
,
JTY+21
]
, we will consider the multiplicity of the eigenvalue
e^{-r_{1}\lambda_{1,\epsilon}}
in the spectrum of the operator
Pe^{-r_{1}\Delta_{\epsilon}}P
. First, we have

Lemma 4.5
.

If
m(\lambda_{1,\epsilon})>l+4N_{\epsilon}
, then there exists a subspace
S\subset E(\lambda_{1,\epsilon})
with

\dim S\geq m(\lambda_{1,\epsilon})-l-4N_{\epsilon},

such that each
\varphi\in S
is an eigenfunction of
Pe^{-r_{1}\Delta_{\epsilon}}P
with eigenvalue
e^{-r_{1}\lambda_{1,\epsilon}}
, and

(20)

\int_{X_{g,\epsilon}}\varphi^{2}\mathrm{d}A\leq 3\int_{X^{\mathrm{thick}}_{g,\epsilon}}\varphi^{2}\mathrm{d}A.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.3
, there exists a subspace
S_{1}\subset E(\lambda_{1})
with

\dim S_{1}\geq m(\lambda_{1})-4(\lfloor\sqrt{\lambda}_{1}\rfloor+1)N_{\epsilon}=m(\lambda_{1})-4N_{\epsilon},

such that every
\varphi\in S_{1}
satisfies the inequality

\int_{X_{g}}\varphi^{2}\mathrm{d}A\leq 3\int_{X^{\mathrm{thick}}_{g}}\varphi^{2}\mathrm{d}A.

Since each
\varphi\in E(\lambda_{1})
corresponds to an eigenfunction
\varphi_{\epsilon}\in E(\lambda_{1,\epsilon})
under the rescaling by
\epsilon^{-2}
, we obtain a subspace
S_{1,\epsilon}\subset E(\lambda_{1,\epsilon})
with

\dim S_{1,\epsilon}=\dim S_{1}\geq m(\lambda_{1})-4N_{\epsilon},

such that every function in
S_{1,\epsilon}
satisfies the inequality (
20
).

Next, consider the orthogonal projection

\Pi:S_{1,\epsilon}\to\mathrm{Span}\{\psi_{1},\cdots,\psi_{l}\}.

Then

\dim(\ker\Pi)\geq\dim S_{1,\epsilon}-l\geq m(\lambda_{1})-l-4N_{\epsilon},

and every
\varphi\in\ker\Pi
satisfies

P\varphi=(\mathrm{Id}-\Pi)\varphi=\varphi,

which implies

Pe^{-r_{1}\Delta_{\epsilon}}P\varphi=Pe^{-r_{1}\Delta_{\epsilon}}\varphi=P(e^{-r_{1}\lambda_{1,\epsilon}}\varphi)=e^{-r_{1}\lambda_{1,\epsilon}}\varphi.

Therefore, the subspace
\ker\Pi
satisfies the desired properties.
∎

Let
\{\varphi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
be an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(X_{g,\epsilon})
consisting of eigenfunctions of the self-adjoint compact operator
Pe^{-r_{1,\epsilon}\Delta}P
, with the associated eigenvalues
\{\Lambda_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
. According to Lemma
4.5
, one may choose this basis such that

(21)

\mathrm{Span}\{\varphi_{1},\cdots,\varphi_{\dim S}\}=S\text{\quad and\quad}\Lambda_{1}=\cdots=\Lambda_{\dim S}=e^{-r_{1}\lambda_{1,\epsilon}}.

To give an upper bound of
m
, it suffices to give an upper bound of
\dim S
. Set

m^{\prime}=\dim S\text{\quad and\quad}n=\lfloor r_{2}/r_{1}\rfloor.

We have the following two lemmas.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\delta_{x}
denote the Dirac delta distribution at
x
. Then

\norm{(Pe^{-r_1\Delta_\epsilon}P)^{n+1}\delta_x}^{2}_{L^{2}(X_{g,\epsilon})}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Lambda_{j}^{2n+2}\varphi_{j}^{2}.

Proof.

The claim is based on a direct computation. For any
f\in L^{2}(X_{g,\epsilon})
, suppose that

f=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{j}\varphi_{j},\quad a_{j}\in\mathbb{R},

then since
Pe^{-r_{1}\Delta_{\epsilon}}P
is self-adjoint,

\displaystyle\big\langle(Pe^{-r_{1}\Delta_{\epsilon}}P)^{n+1}\delta_{x},\ f\big\rangle=((Pe^{-r_{1}\Delta_{\epsilon}}P)^{n+1}f)(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{j}\Lambda_{j}^{n+1}\varphi_{j}(x)=\Big\langle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Lambda_{j}^{n+1}\varphi_{j}(x)\varphi_{j},\ f\Big\rangle.

Thus, we have

\norm{(Pe^{-r_1\Delta_\epsilon}P)^{n+1}\delta_x}^{2}_{L^{2}(X_{g,\epsilon})}=\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Lambda_{j}^{n+1}\varphi_{j}(x)\varphi_{j}\Big\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X_{g,\epsilon})}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Lambda_{j}^{2n+2}\varphi_{j}^{2},

as desired.
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

With the same notation as above,

m^{\prime}\cdot e^{-r_{1}\lambda_{1,\epsilon}(2n+2)}\leq 3\int_{X^{\mathrm{thick}}_{g,\epsilon}}\norm{(Pe^{-r_1\Delta_\epsilon}P)^{n+1}\delta_x}^{2}_{L^{2}(X_{g,\epsilon})}\mathrm{d}A_{x}.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.5
and Lemma
4.6
, we have

\displaystyle m^{\prime}\cdot e^{-r_{1}\lambda_{1,\epsilon}(2n+2)}

\displaystyle=e^{-r_{1}\lambda_{1,\epsilon}(2n+2)}\int_{X_{g,\epsilon}}\sum_{j=1}^{m^{\prime}}\varphi_{j}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}A

\displaystyle\leq 3e^{-r_{1}\lambda_{1,\epsilon}(2n+2)}\int_{X^{\mathrm{thick}}_{g,\epsilon}}\sum_{j=1}^{m^{\prime}}\varphi_{j}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}A\qquad\text{(by \eqref{intcont})}

\displaystyle\leq 3\int_{X^{\mathrm{thick}}_{g,\epsilon}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Lambda_{j}^{2n+2}\varphi_{j}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}A\qquad\text{(by \eqref{eqn:basis})}

\displaystyle=3\int_{X^{\mathrm{thick}}_{g,\epsilon}}\norm{(Pe^{-r_1\Delta_\epsilon}P)^{n+1}\delta_x}^{2}_{L^{2}(X_{g,\epsilon})}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}A_{x}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 4.9
.

Under the above assumptions,

.

x\in G_{j}
,

\area(B_{x,R_{j}})\leq\area\left(\Big\{z\in X_{g}:\ f_{x}(z)\geq\frac{f_{x}(x)}{2}\Big\}\right)\leq\frac{\int_{X_{g}}f_{x}^{2}(z)}{\left(\frac{f_{x}(x)}{2}\right)^{2}}\leq\frac{4}{\mu_{j-1}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

We will use a different notion of diameter which is defined as follows.

Definition 5.7
.

\diam_{c}(\mathcal{A}_{k}):=\sup_{x,z\in\mathcal{A}_{k}}\left\{\length(\alpha):\ \begin{gathered}\text{$\alpha$ is a shortest geodesic segment joining $x$ and $z$}\\
\text{that does not intersect with any $\gamma\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}(X_{g})$}\end{gathered}\right\}.

Lemma 5.8
.

Suppose
x\in\mathcal{A}_{k}
and
R>2c_{1}
. If
R<\diam_{c}(\mathcal{A}_{k})
, then

\area(B_{x,R/2})\geq c_{3}\cdot R,

where
c_{3}>0
depends only on
\epsilon
.

Proof.

Since
R<\diam_{c}(\mathcal{A}_{k})
, by definition there exist some
z\in\mathcal{A}_{k}
and a shortest geodesic segment
\alpha
from
x
to
z
of length
R/2
that does not intersect with any
\gamma\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}(X_{g})
.
By Lemma
5.1
, we know that
\alpha\subset\mathcal{A}_{k}\cup\mathcal{S}_{k}
, hence

\alpha\subset B_{x,R/2}.

Let
\alpha:[0,R/2]\to X_{g}
be a geodesic of arc-length parameter with
\alpha(0)=x
. Then for each integer
n\in\{0,\cdots,\lfloor R/c_{1}\rfloor-1\}
, we have

B(\alpha(nc_{1}/2),c_{1}/4)\subset B_{x,R/2},

and the geodesic balls
B(\alpha(nc_{1}/2),c_{1}/4)
,
1\leq n\leq\lfloor R/c_{1}\rfloor-1
, are pairwise disjoint. Moreover, by Lemma
5.1
we have that for all
0\leq n\leq\lfloor R/c_{1}\rfloor-1
,

\inj(\alpha(nc_{1}/2))>\epsilon.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\area(B_{x,R/2})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{n=0}^{\lfloor R/c_{1}\rfloor-1}\area\big(B(\alpha(nc_{1}/2),c_{1}/4)\big)

\displaystyle\geq\min\Big\{\pi\epsilon^{2},\frac{\pi c_{1}^{2}}{16}\Big\}\cdot\lfloor R/c_{1}\rfloor

\displaystyle\geq c_{3}\cdot R,

where
c_{3}
depends only on
\epsilon
. The proof is complete.
∎

Now we give an upper bound of the integral of
f_{x}(x)^{2}
over
\mathcal{A}_{k}
.

Lemma 5.9
.

There exists some constant
C>0
depending only on
\epsilon
such that

\int_{\mathcal{A}_{k}}f_{x}(x)^{2}\leq\max\Big\{C\sqrt{\lambda}\cdot\area(\mathcal{A}_{k}\cup\mathcal{S}_{k}),\ 8\Big\}.

Proof.

Let
j_{0}\geq 0
be the maximal index such that
G_{j_{0}}\neq\emptyset
(see Definition
5.4
). If
j_{0}=0
, then

(44)

\int_{\mathcal{A}_{k}}f_{x}(x)^{2}\leq\mu_{0}\cdot\area(G_{0})=\sqrt{\lambda}\cdot\area(\mathcal{A}_{k}).

From now on, we always assume that
j_{0}\geq 1
. Note that

(45)

\int_{\mathcal{A}_{k}}f_{x}(x)^{2}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{j_{0}}\mu_{j}\cdot\area(G_{j}).

Case-1
.
R_{j_{0}}\geq\diam_{c}(\mathcal{A}_{k})
. Then by definition we know that for any
x\in G_{j_{0}}

\mathcal{A}_{k}\subset B_{x,R_{j_{0}}}.

Since
j_{0}\geq 1
, it follows from Lemma
5.6
that

\area(\mathcal{A}_{k})\leq\area(B_{x,R_{j_{0}}})\leq\frac{4}{\mu_{j_{0}-1}},

we thus have

\mu_{0}<\mu_{1}<\cdots<\mu_{j_{0}}\leq\frac{8}{\area(\mathcal{A}_{k})}.

By the definition of
G_{j}
we thus have

(46)

\sum_{j=0}^{j_{0}}\mu_{j}\cdot\area(G_{j})\leq\frac{8}{\area(\mathcal{A}_{k})}\sum_{j=0}^{j_{0}}\area(G_{j})=8.

Case-2
.
R_{j_{0}}<\diam_{c}(\mathcal{A}_{k})
. For any
1\leq j\leq j_{0}
, let
\{B_{x_{i},R_{j}/2}\}_{1\leq i\leq l}
be a maximal collection of disjoint sets such that
x_{i}\in G_{j}
,
\Forall 1\leq i\leq l
. By Lemma
5.8
we have

(47)

l\leq\frac{\area(\mathcal{A}_{k}\cup\mathcal{S}_{k})}{c_{3}R_{j}}.

Next we claim that

(48)

G_{j}\subset\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq l}B_{x_{i},R_{j}}.

Otherwise, there would exist some
x\in G_{j}
such that any geodesic segment joining
x
and
x_{i}
that does not intersect with any
\gamma\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}(X_{g})
has length
\geq R_{j}
. Thus,
B_{x,R_{j}/2}
is disjoint with any
B_{x_{i},R_{j}/2}
, leading to a contradiction because our choice of
\{B_{x_{i},R_{j}/2}\}_{1\leq i\leq l}
is maximal.
Combining (
48
), Lemma
5.6
, Lemma
5.8
and (
47
), we deduce that

\area(G_{j})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{l}\area(B_{x_{i},R_{j}})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{l}\frac{4}{\mu_{j-1}}\leq\frac{\area(\mathcal{A}_{k}\cup\mathcal{S}_{k})}{c_{3}R_{j}}\frac{4}{\mu_{j-1}}.

Therefore, we have

(49)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{j_{0}}\mu_{j}\cdot\area(G_{j})

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{0}\cdot\area(G_{0})+\sum_{j=1}^{j_{0}}\mu_{j}\cdot\frac{4\area(\mathcal{A}_{k}\cup\mathcal{S}_{k})}{c_{3}\mu_{j-1}R_{j}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\lambda}\cdot\area(\mathcal{A}_{k})+\sum_{j=1}^{j_{0}}\frac{8\sqrt{\lambda}\area(\mathcal{A}_{k}\cup\mathcal{S}_{k})}{c_{1}c_{3}2^{j}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{\lambda}\cdot\area(\mathcal{A}_{k}\cup\mathcal{S}_{k}).

The lemma then follows from (
44
), (
45
), (
46
) and (
49
).
∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

By Lemma
5.3
and Lemma
5.9
we have

\displaystyle m(\lambda)=\int_{X_{g}}\sum_{i=1}^{m(\lambda)}\phi_{i}(x)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 3\int_{X^{\mathrm{thick}}_{g}}\sum_{i=1}^{m(\lambda)}\phi_{i}(x)^{2}

\displaystyle=3\sum_{k=1}^{I_{\epsilon}}\int_{\mathcal{A}_{k}}f_{x}(x)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 3\sum_{k=1}^{I_{\epsilon}}\max\Big\{C\sqrt{\lambda}\cdot\area(\mathcal{A}_{k}\cup\mathcal{S}_{k}),\ 8\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq 3C\sqrt{\lambda}\cdot\area(X_{g})+24I_{\epsilon}.

The proof is then finished by Gauss-Bonnet.
∎

Abstract
This paper investigates the second-order statistics of multipath fading channels with von Mises-Fisher (vMF) distributed scatters. Simple closed-form expressions for the mean Doppler shift and Doppler spread are derived as the key spectral moments that capture the impact of mobility and scattering characteristics on level crossings and fade durations. These expressions are then used to analyze the influence of vMF parameters on the Level-Crossing Rate (LCR) The results show that isotropic scattering yields the highest LCR , while fading dynamics reduce with the decreasing angular spread of scatterers. Moreover, obile antenna motion parallel to the mean scattering direction results in a lower LCR than the perpendicular motion, with the difference between the two cases increasing with the higher concentration of scatterers.



I
Introduction

The angular distribution of incoming signal wavefronts is one of the key aspect in modeling wireless propagation channels.
Among various statistical models, the
von Mises-Fisher (vMF)
distribution has gained widespread adoption due to its simplicity, flexibility, and ability to jointly model both azimuth and elevation angles of arrival
[
1
]
.
Its good fit to measurements and the ability to approximate complex 3D scattering environments through model mixtures further reinforces its appeal for adoption in channel modeling studies.

Due to these favorable properties, the
vMF
distribution has been widely adopted in the literature, e.g.,
[
2
]
-
[
5
]
.
However, the complexity of the adopted channel models in these studies rendered the important channel statistics analytically intractable, limiting the analysis to numerical integration or Monte Carlo simulation approaches.
Therefore, while enabling valuable first insights, the reported studies provided limited understanding of the impact of scattering parameters on the channel characteristics.

More recently, closed-form expressions were reported for the correlation functions
[
6
,
7
]
, Doppler spectrum
[
8
]
, and
Level-Crossing Rate (LCR)
and
Average Fade Duration (AFD)

[
9
]
in channels with
vMF
scattering.
These result provided new insights into the channel characteristics associated with spatially correlated scattering.
Specifically, the simple expressions derived for a single vmf scattering cluster in
[
6
]
and
[
8
]
reveal that the channel correlation properties and Doppler spectrum depend only on the degree of angular concentration of scatterers and on the angle between the mobile antenna motion direction and the mean direction of scattering, regardless of the particular azimuth and elevation angles at which the multipath cluster is observed.
On the other hand, while the
LCR
and
AFD
are analytically treated in
[
9
]
, the complexity of the considered mobile-to-mobile communication scenario and the convoluted form of the presented expressions obscure the explicit dependency on the scattering characteristics.

In this paper, we address this shortcoming and further investigate the level-crossing and fade duration statistics in channels with
vMF
distribution of scatterers.
Based on our recently reported results for the Doppler spectrum in
vMF
scattering channels
[
8
]
, we derive closed-form expressions for the spectral moments governing the second-order fading statistics, i.e., the mean Doppler shift and the Doppler spread.
The presented simple formulations explicitly capture the impact of the
vMF
parameters, revealing that the temporal fading behavior is determined by the angular spread of scatterers, mobile antenna speed the angle between the velocity vector and the mean scattering direction.

II
Channel Model

We consider a mobile fading channel with the frequency-dependent transmission coefficient given by

\displaystyle H(t,f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N_{m}}A_{n}e^{-j2\pi f\tau_{0}^{n}}e^{-j2\pi f_{D}^{n}t}

(1)

where

t

time;

f

frequency;

N_{m}

number of multipath components;

A_{n}

their amplitudes;

\tau_{0}^{n}

initial propagation delays;

f_{D}^{n}

Doppler frequency shifts, i.e.,

\displaystyle f_{D}^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda}\,\hat{\mathbf{k}}_{n}^{T}\mathbf{v}

(2)

\lambda

wavelength;

\mathbf{v}

mobile antenna velocity vector;

\hat{\mathbf{k}}_{n}

Direction of Arrival (DoA)
unit vector, i.e.

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{k}}_{n}=(\cos\phi_{n}\cos\psi_{n},\sin\phi_{n}\cos\psi_{n},\sin\psi_{n})^{T}

(3)

\phi_{n}

Azimuth Angle of Arrival (AAoA)
;

\psi_{n}

Elevation Angle of Arrival (EAoA)
;

(\,.\,)^{T}

vector transpose operation.

Without loss of generality, we consider a static
Transmitter (Tx)
and a mobile
Receiver (Rx)
.
However, the presented model and results derived in this work also apply if their roles are reversed.

This model assumes that scattering takes place sufficiently far away from the mobile antenna, such that the planar wave propagation assumption holds and the multipath components’ amplitudes,
DoAs
and delays are approximately constant over local areas, i.e., several tens or hundreds of wavelengths in size
[
10
]
.
Moreover, the mobile antenna is assumed to move in a fixed direction with a constant speed.
Together, these assumptions imply that the channel is wide-sense stationary
[
11
]
.

We also assume that the number of multipath components
N_{m}
is large such that, according to the central limit theorem, the channel coefficient (
1
) exhibits complex Gaussian distribution.
We further focus on the case when none of the components is dominant and the channel exhibits Rayleigh fading.
The local average
Rx
power is determined by:

\displaystyle\Omega=\sum_{n=1}^{N_{m}}\mathbb{E}\left\{A_{n}^{2}\right\}

(4)

where
\mathbb{E}\left\{\,\right\}
denotes statistical expectation.
The slow variations in the local average power, as the mobile antenna moves across larger distances, can be represented by an independent stochastic process, i.e., large-scale fading.

The initial propagation delays in (
1
) represent the propagation path lengths for random mobile antenna locations at the beginning of the channel observation (
t=0
). At a fixed frequency, these differences translate to random phase shifts observed at the
Rx
, typically modelled by Uniformly random variables, i.e.,
\varphi_{0}^{n}\sim\mathcal{U}(0,2\pi)
.
The changes in the propagation path lengths due to mobility, under fixed
DoAs
and linear motion assumptions, result in time-variant phase components changing linearly in time and being perceived as apparent Doppler frequency shifts given by (
2
).

The
DoAs
observed at a random
Rx
position are represented by the
vMF
distribution, with the
Probability Density Function (PDF)
given by
[
12
]

\displaystyle p_{\phi\psi}(\phi,\psi)

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa\cos\psi}{4\pi\sinh{\kappa}}e^{\kappa\left[\cos\mu_{\psi}\cos\psi\cos(\phi-\mu_{\phi})+\sin\mu_{\psi}\sin\psi\right]},

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\lvert\phi\rvert\leq\pi,\;\lvert\psi\rvert\leq\pi/2

(5)

where

\mu_{\phi}

mean
AAoA
;

\mu_{\psi}

mean
EAoA
;

\kappa

spread parameter.

The
vMF
scattering model describes scattering concentrated around an arbitrary mean direction specified by the parameters
\mu_{\phi}
and
\mu_{\psi}
, with the spread of scatterers around this direction being controlled by the parameter
\kappa
.
It includes the isotropic scattering (
\kappa=0
) and single-point scattering in the direction (
\mu_{\phi}
,
\mu_{\psi}
) as special cases, while accommodating arbitrary degrees of angular concentration between these two extremes.

III
Mean Doppler Shift and Doppler Spread

The Doppler
PDF
in
vMF
-scattering channels was recently derived in
[
8
, Eq. 17]
, and can be expressed as

\displaystyle p_{f_{D}}(f)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2f_{m}}\frac{\kappa}{\sinh\kappa}e^{\kappa\,\frac{f_{\mu}}{f_{m}}\frac{f}{f_{m}}}\;\times

\displaystyle\phantom{=}I_{0}\left(\kappa\sqrt{1-\left(\frac{f_{\mu}}{f_{m}}\right)^{2}}\sqrt{1-\left(\frac{f}{f_{m}}\right)^{2}}\right),\quad\lvert f\rvert\leq f_{m}

(6)

where

f_{\mu}

Doppler shift for the mean
DoA
direction, i.e.

\displaystyle f_{\mu}=f_{m}(\mathbf{k}_{\mu}^{T}\mathbf{\hat{v}})=f_{m}\cos\beta

(7)

\mathbf{\hat{v}}

motion direction unit vector, i.e.,
\mathbf{\hat{v}}=\mathbf{v}/\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert
;

\mathbf{k}_{\mu}

mean scattering direction unit vector, i.e.

\displaystyle\mathbf{k}_{\mu}=\left[\cos\mu_{\phi}\cos\mu_{\psi},\;\sin\mu_{\phi}\cos\mu_{\psi},\;\sin\mu_{\psi}\right]^{T}

(8)

f_{m}

maximum Doppler shift, i.e.,
f_{m}=\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert/\lambda
;

\beta

angle between the vectors
\mathbf{k}_{\mu}
and
\mathbf{\hat{v}}
;

I_{0}(\,.\,)

modified Bessel function (first kind, zeroth order).

By evaluating expectation with respect to this
PDF
, the mean Doppler frequency is obtained as (see the Appendix)

\displaystyle\mu_{D}

\displaystyle=w_{\kappa}f_{\mu}

(9)

where

\displaystyle w_{\kappa}=\coth\kappa-\frac{1}{\kappa}

(10)

Similarly, the mean squared Doppler frequency is obtained as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\{f_{D}^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{w_{\kappa}}{\kappa}f_{m}^{2}+\left(1-3\frac{w_{\kappa}}{\kappa}\right)f_{\mu}^{2}

(11)

The Doppler spread, i.e., the standard deviation of the Doppler frequency, then follows as

\displaystyle\sigma_{D}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left\{f_{D}^{2}\right\}-\mu_{D}^{2}}

(12)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{w_{\kappa}}{\kappa}f_{m}^{2}+\left(1-3\frac{w_{\kappa}}{\kappa}-w_{\kappa}^{2}\right)f_{\mu}^{2}}

(13)

For the special case of isotropic scattering (
\kappa=0
), the mean Doppler shift (
9
) and the Doppler spread (
13
) become

\displaystyle\lim_{\kappa\rightarrow 0}\mu_{D}

\displaystyle=0

(14)

\displaystyle\lim_{\kappa\rightarrow 0}\sigma_{D}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\,f_{m}

(15)

The zero mean Doppler shift is observed due to the Doppler
PDF
in this case being symmetric around zero and constant over the domain,
\lvert f_{D}\rvert\leq f_{m}

[
8
]
.
On the other hand, for
\kappa\rightarrow\infty
the Doppler moments are

\displaystyle\lim_{\kappa\rightarrow\infty}\mu_{D}

\displaystyle=f_{\mu}

(16)

\displaystyle\lim_{\kappa\rightarrow\infty}\sigma_{D}

\displaystyle=0

(17)

These results are straightforwardly interpreted by considering that, in this case, scattering reduces to a single point in the direction of the mean
DoA
.

The Doppler spread in (
13
) is plotted in Fig.
1
.

Figure 1:
Doppler spread normalized by the maximum Doppler shift.

The figure shows that the Doppler spread is invariant to the motion direction for the isotropic scattering case (
\kappa=0
).
As the scattering becomes concentrated with increasing
\kappa
, the impact of the motion direction becomes apparent with the maximum Doppler spread observed for the motion perpendicular to the mean
DoA
direction (
\beta=90^{\circ}
) and the minimum one for the parallel motion (
\beta=0^{\circ}
,
180^{\circ}
).
As scattering collapses to a single point (
\kappa\rightarrow\infty
), the dependence on the motion direction is lost again as the Doppler spread asymptotically goes to zero.

Finally, we should point out the results presented in this section apply for a single
vMF
-scattering cluster.
However, they can be straightforwardly generalized to a multi-cluster scattering scenario, where the angular distribution of scatterers can be represented by a mixture of
vMF
distributions
[
1
]
.

IV
Level-Crossing Rate and Average Fade Duration

According to
[
13
, Sec. 1.3]
, the
LCR
in Rayleigh fading channels can be expressed as

\displaystyle L_{\xi}(\rho)

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{\pi}\,\sigma_{D}\,\rho\,e^{-\rho^{2}}

(18)

\displaystyle T_{\xi}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\,\frac{1}{\sigma_{D}}\,\frac{1}{\rho}\,\left[e^{\rho^{2}}-1\right]

(19)

where

\rho

envelope level normalized to the
Root Mean Square (RMS)
value, i.e.,
\sqrt{\Omega}
.

The dependence of the
LCR
on the envelope level follows from the factor
\rho\,e^{-\rho^{2}}
.
The maximum
LCR
occurs at the level 3 dB below the
RMS
value (i.e.,
\rho=1/\sqrt{2}
), and is given by

\displaystyle L_{\xi}^{\max}=\sigma_{D}\sqrt{2\pi e^{-1}}

(20)

The impact of the scattering characteristics follows from the Doppler spread factor, i.e., given by (
13
) for
vMF
scattering.
As follows from (
13
) and (
18
), the
LCR
is directly proportional to the mobile speed (i.e., through
f_{m}
), while the dependence on the motion direction (i.e., through
f_{\mu}
) can be inferred from Fig.
1
.

For the spacial case of isotropic scattering (
\kappa=0
), the
LCR
is obtained by replacing (
15
) in (
18
), yielding

\displaystyle L_{\xi}(\rho;\kappa=0)

\displaystyle=2f_{m}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{3}}\,\rho\,e^{-\rho^{2}}

(21)

\displaystyle T_{\xi}(\rho;\kappa=0)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2f_{m}}\,\sqrt{\frac{3}{\pi}}\,\frac{1}{\rho}\,\left[e^{\rho^{2}}-1\right]

(22)

Only the mobile speed is relevant in this case, while the dependance on the motion direction is lost.
This follows as the configuration of scatterers relative to the mobile antenna remains the same regardless of the direction it is moving in.
On the other hand, for the special case of single-point scattering (
\kappa=\infty
) the
LCR
is zero, as follows by inserting (
17
) in (
18
).
This result reflects the fact that no time-variant interference between components occurs in this case and the
Rx
signal envelope remains constant.

Results analysis

In this section, we employ the obtained analytical results to investigate the impact of the
vMF
scattering parameters on the fading dynamics.
The effects of both the mean scattering direction and the degree of concentration of scatterers are analyzed.

LCR

The gray-shaded area in the figure indicates the region containing the

As one observes, motion perpendicular to the mean

We should also point out that the difference between the fading rates for these two extreme cases increases as the scattering becomes more concentrated (higher

VI
Conclusions

The
vMF
distribution has become a popular scattering model, primarily due to its simplicity, flexibility to represent different scattering characteristics and demonstrated good fit to measurements.
However, the understanding of the impact of the
vMF
scattering parameters on the channel characteristics remained largely limited until recent analytical results.

This paper presents simple closed-for expressions for the mean Doppler shift and Doppler spread in
vMF
scattering channels, i.e., spectral moments that capture the effect of scattering characteristics on the second-order channel statistics.
The presented results apply to a narrowband channel transmission coefficient and to any given frequency component in the wideband channel transfer function, hence being also applicable to sub-carrier channels in
Orthogonal Frequency Dicision Multiplex (OFDM)
systems.

The obtained expressions are employed to analyze the impact of the mean direction and spread of scatterers on the
LCR
, where a strong effect of both is observed.
The fading is observed to be slower with a lower
LCR
for scattering parallel to the motion direction than the perpendicular case, with the difference between the two cases being more distinct for more concentrated scattering.
The fading dynamics are the fastest for isotropic scattering, being slower for concentrated scattering and becoming zero as scattering reduces to a single point.

Finally, we should note that this work specifically focuses on simple scattering scenario without presence of a dominant propagation path and with only
Tx
or
Rx
mobile.
The primary goal behind this choice is to better understand the fundamental impact of scattering parameters on the second-order channel statistics.
However, the extension of the presented results to channels with a dominant signal component and mobile-to-mobile scenarios is straightforward, by following the well-known procedures from the classic works in
[
14
]
and
[
15
]
.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\{\hat{f}_{D}\right\}

\displaystyle=a\int_{-1}^{1}\hat{f}e^{-b\hat{f}}I_{0}\left(c\sqrt{1-\hat{f}^{2}}\right)d\hat{f}

\displaystyle=-a\frac{\partial}{\partial b}I_{*}

\displaystyle=\phantom{-}\frac{1}{2}\frac{\kappa}{\sinh\kappa}

\displaystyle=-\kappa\,\hat{f}_{\mu}

\displaystyle I_{*}

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}e^{-b\hat{f}}I_{0}\left(c\sqrt{1-\hat{f}^{2}}\right)d\hat{f}

\displaystyle I_{*}=\frac{2\sinh\left(\sqrt{b^{2}+c^{2}}\right)}{\sqrt{b^{2}+c^{2}}}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\{\hat{f}_{D}\right\}

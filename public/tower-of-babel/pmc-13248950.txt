From Machine Learning to Generative Artificial Intelligence in Urology: Technological Evolution and Future Perspectives
Authors: Sung-Jong Eun, Jong Mok Park, Yong Gil Na
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Artificial intelligence (AI) is being applied across healthcare, including disease diagnosis, personalized treatment, and rehabilitation management. In urology, AI research has evolved from traditional machine learning to advanced deep learning and generative models. This review categorizes these developments into 3 primary domains. First, machine learning has been applied to mobile-based platforms for continuous urinary health monitoring and personalized healthcare. Second, deep learning- based diagnostic systems have improved the identification of conditions such as prostatic hyperplasia, ureteral strictures, and urinary stones, thereby supporting clinical decision-making. Finally, generative AI, including large language models and vision transformers, is reshaping medical image analysis through data augmentation and expanding patient education through natural language interactions. This review examines integrated applications of AI in urological diseases and discusses current research trends and future prospects for a digital healthcare ecosystem in urology.

INTRODUCTION

Artificial intelligence (AI) is transforming healthcare, from the development of sophisticated medical robots to precision diagnosis and treatment of complex diseases. The rapid integration of AI has the potential to improve healthcare quality, extend patient survival, and enhance quality of life. As one of the most important technological advances in recent history, AI has reshaped society across settings ranging from personal smartphones to advanced surgical robotics. In medicine, AI applications are generally categorized into virtual domains, such as deep learning-based clinical decision support, and physical domains, including robotics and nanotechnology for targeted drug delivery. More recently, the focus of AI has shifted from data utilization alone to the generation of new information and the advanced evaluation of medical services. Accordingly, global investment in healthcare AI has accelerated because of its broad societal impact. Urology, in particular, may benefit substantially from these innovations, especially through the analysis of large datasets to improve diagnostic and prognostic accuracy.

Although machine learning has traditionally been used to refine disease classification and risk modeling through statistical algorithms, its applications in urology are rapidly diversifying. This review summarizes these evolving trends by categorizing them into 3 key areas. First, it examines machine learning applications in mobile-based urinary health management, with an emphasis on AI-driven mobile platforms for patient self-care and monitoring. Second, it reviews deep learning-based diagnostics in urology, highlighting the role of neural networks in improving the accuracy of medical imaging and clinical assessment. Finally, it explores emerging applications of generative AI and advanced deep learning models, including vision transformers and large language models (LLMs), which are opening new directions in urological research and patient interaction.

By synthesizing these research examples, this review illustrates the current state and future potential of AI in urology. This overview may help clinicians and researchers understand how integrated AI technologies can be applied to optimize the management of urological diseases.

MACHINE LEARNING APPLICATIONS: MOBILE-BASED URINARY HEALTH MANAGEMENT

In the early stages of AI integration in urology, research has focused primarily on mobile-based platforms and smart healthcare devices for routine monitoring. These systems use traditional machine learning algorithms to process user-generated data and provide actionable health insights.

Domestic and International Smart Healthcare Solutions

The mobile medical device industry has seen rapid growth in portable healthcare solutions, with domestic companies such as Samsung and LG leading the development of wearable and handheld devices. This ecosystem has supported the development of specialized urinary healthcare products. For example, Yodoc-m (Korea) is a ubiquitous computing-based health system in which urinalysis results are uploaded to a web server, enabling real-time monitoring and longitudinal data tracking. As a foundational framework for many domestic products, Yodoc-m provides a comprehensive user interface for user-centered urination monitoring [1]. Fig. 1 shows the user interface of Yodoc-m [1].

Globally, diverse applications have emerged to digitize urinary health monitoring. Toilet Tracker (Canada) uses manual data entry to provide statistical visualizations of defecation and urination patterns, whereas Biosense (India) uses image-based analysis through mobile devices. By analyzing test strips with smartphone cameras, Biosense can screen for 25 conditions, including urinary tract infections and preeclampsia, by measuring glucose and protein levels. In addition, SYMAX (Japan) has integrated sensors directly into toilet bowls to automatically analyze urine components. These data are transmitted to a cloud server to provide a noninvasive routine health checkup service [1]. Fig. 2 presents information on these international products [1].

Integration of AI for Signal and Pattern Analysis

The core functionality of these mobile applications and smart devices increasingly depends on AI-driven analytics. Beyond simple data logging, AI techniques, particularly anomaly detection and signal processing, are widely used to identify irregular urination patterns or biochemical imbalances. When trained on large datasets, these machine learning models can distinguish normal physiological variation from potential pathological signals and provide early warnings that are essential for proactive urinary health management.

DEEP LEARNING-BASED DIAGNOSTICS IN UROLOGY

Recent advances in AI have moved urological applications beyond simple monitoring toward sophisticated diagnostic support systems for conditions such as prostatic hyperplasia, ureteral strictures, urinary stones, and urinary tract infections [1-9]. These AI-driven tools function as clinical decision support systems (CDSSs) and improve diagnostic accuracy through clinical validation. Key technological milestones are summarized below by application area.

Behavioral Analysis and Automated Voiding Diaries

A research team at Gachon University Gil Hospital developed an AI-assisted diagnostic system that analyzes urination posture and movement signals using gyroscope and accelerometer sensors. The core component of this technology is an automated voiding diary application designed for patients with urinary disorders. By segmenting male urination into 3 phases—preurination, urination, and posturination—the system uses machine learning and deep learning to automatically recognize urinary frequency, duration, and volume. This transition from manual logging to AI-based behavioral recognition substantially improves data reliability [10]. Fig. 3 shows the research process [10].

Image-Based Surgical and Diagnostic Support

At Sejong Chungnam National University Hospital, researchers have used deep learning to analyze microendoscopic and computed tomography (CT) images to support the diagnosis of ureteral strictures and urinary stones [11]. Fig. 4 shows a concept map of this study [11]. Identifying the precise location of a stricture during surgery is often challenging. To address this limitation, deep learning architectures such as ResNet-50 have been used to automatically recognize stricture zones in realtime microendoscopic video, providing surgeons with impor-tant intraoperative guidance [12-16]. Precise stone localization is also essential for successful lithotripsy. Current research is exploring optimal diagnostic methods by integrating traditional image processing, including Watershed segmentation; machine learning, including support vector machines; and advanced deep learning frameworks, such as ResNet-50 and Fast R-CNN [17]. These models automatically detect stones and evaluate their clinical status using abdominal CT and C-arm imaging [18]. Fig. 5 shows a concept map of this research [18].

Acoustic Signal Processing for Noninvasive Uroflowmetry

Researchers at Seoul National University Bundang Hospital have developed mobile-based urinary flow measurement technology using acoustic signal analysis. After successful clinical trials in 2017 and 2018, this AI-driven software was registered as a class II medical device with the U.S. Food and Drug Administration in 2020. The system uses linear and nonlinear regression, classification analysis, and deep learning to analyze sounds produced during urination. By capturing the acoustic frequency of urine hitting water, the smartphone-based application noninvasively calculates peak flow rate, total volume, and duration, providing users with a comprehensive dashboard of their urinary patterns [1]. APPLICATIONS OF GENERATIVE AI AND ADVANCED DEEP LEARNING MODELS IN UROLOGY

Recent advances in urological AI have expanded from medical imaging-based diagnostic support systems, including those using CT, magnetic resonance imaging, and endoscopic images, to patient education, clinical counseling, and treatment-support models using LLMs. In particular, studies of urolithiasis detection have adopted transfer learning, self-supervised learning, super-resolution preprocessing, and generative model-based data augmentation to improve model performance when medical imaging datasets are limited. In parallel, LLM-based studies have explored potential roles for these models in clinical data processing, patient education, therapeutic support, and integration with long-term digital care platforms.

CT Image-Based Urolithiasis Diagnosis Models

In imaging-based urolithiasis diagnosis, Choi et al. [19] pro-posed a transfer learning approach using a ResNet-50 architecture. The authors aimed to develop a rapid and accurate urolithiasis detection model by initializing the network with pretrained ResNet-50 weights and fine-tuning it with CT images of urinary stones. To enhance image quality and improve generalizability with a limited dataset, the study incorporated histogram smoothing; geometric augmentation techniques, including rotation, shifting, scaling, and flipping; and CycleGANbased data augmentation. Model performance was evaluated using 10-fold cross-validation. In a test dataset comprising 100 urolithiasis cases and 20 normal cases, the model achieved an accuracy of 0.93, precision of 0.949, and recall of 0.936. These findings suggest that transfer learning-based convolutional neural network (CNN) models may serve as effective clinical decision-support tools for identifying urolithiasis on CT images. Subsequently, Choi et al. [20] introduced a vision transformer-based model for the same disease domain. This study developed a urolithiasis detection pipeline using 150 CT images, including 100 images with urolithiasis and 50 images without stones. The proposed framework applied super-resolution CNN preprocessing to improve image resolution and Cycle-GAN-based augmentation to increase data diversity. The model then learned spatial representations from CT images in a patchbased manner. In 5-fold cross-validation, the model achieved a precision of 0.9396, recall of 0.9476, and accuracy of 0.93. Compared with conventional CNN-based approaches, which primarily focus on local feature extraction, this architecture can capture both local and global image features through attentionbased patch representation. This study demonstrates an extension of CT-based urolithiasis diagnosis from CNN-centered models to transformer-based architectures [20]. Fig. 7 shows the process used in this research [21].

To address the limited availability of labeled medical imaging data, Kim and Eun [21] proposed a data-efficient framework that combined self-supervised learning and transfer learning for urolithiasis detection. The study used 100 abdominal CT images and adopted a self-supervised learning framework with a ResNet-50 backbone. During the self-supervised learning phase, the model learned generalizable image representations by maximizing agreement between augmented image pairs without requiring labels. The pretrained model was then finetuned using labeled CT data through transfer learning. In 5-fold cross-validation, the combined self-supervised and transfer learning model achieved an area under the curve of 0.95 and an F1-score of 0.91, outperforming both a randomly initialized model and a transfer learning-only model. These results indicate that self-supervised learning may be particularly useful for developing urological imaging models in clinical settings where large-scale annotated datasets are difficult to obtain.

LLM AND METAVERSE-BASED UROLOGICAL HEALTHCARE SUPPORT

The potential applications of LLMs and generative AI in urology were systematically discussed in a review by Park et al. [22]. The authors described the expanding role of LLMs in medical data analysis, image recognition, and natural language processing and highlighted their applicability to urological practice, including diagnostic support, clinical data management, patient education, and treatment assistance. The review also suggested that combining LLMs with metaverse-based digital platforms may improve patient engagement and adherence in urological conditions that require repetitive treatment, behavioral modification, and long-term follow-up. However, the authors emphasized that successful clinical implementation of LLMs requires careful consideration of data quality, algorithmic bias, privacy and security, ethical issues, and regulatory standards [22]. CONCLUSION

In conclusion, the integration of AI into urology has evolved from simple data processing to a sophisticated paradigm that is reshaping clinical workflows and patient experiences. This evolution reflects a broader shift from reactive medical intervention toward a more proactive, data-driven approach. This review categorized these advances into 3 primary domains to provide a comprehensive overview of the current research landscape.

First, machine learning applications in mobile-based urinary health management have helped shift healthcare from hospitalcentered episodic care to continuous, patient-centered monitoring. By using everyday smart devices, these models empower individuals to manage chronic urological conditions through real-time data feedback, thereby bridging the gap between clinical consultations and daily self-care. Second, building on this data-driven foundation, deep learning-based diagnostics have substantially improved the identification of conditions such as prostatic hyperplasia, ureteral strictures, and urolithiasis. These systems now function as important CDSSs, reducing diagnostic variability among clinicians and supporting high standards of care through clinical validation. The transition from simple monitoring to complex diagnostic support demonstrates the increasing reliability of AI for handling high-dimensional medical imaging and clinical datasets.

Furthermore, the recent emergence of generative AI and advanced deep learning architectures, including LLMs and vision transformers, marks a new frontier in the field. These technologies have addressed long-standing challenges, such as the scarcity of labeled medical imaging data, through generative data augmentation and self-supervised learning, allowing high-performing models to be developed even with limited clinical samples. Beyond diagnostics, LLMs are reshaping patient education and clinical counseling by helping bridge communication gaps between providers and patients through personalized natural language interactions. This represents a shift toward human-centered AI, in which technology not only analyzes data but also facilitates meaningful clinical engagement.

Moving forward, urological AI research should focus on the seamless integration of these disparate technologies into a unified digital healthcare ecosystem. Although the technical milestones are substantial, future efforts should prioritize multicenter clinical trials to ensure model generalizability across diverse populations and real-world clinical environments. Technical reliability and clinical stability will be essential for successful implementation in medical practice. Ultimately, the synergy between the intuitive expertise of urologists and the analytical precision of generative AI may redefine standards of urological care, supporting more proactive, precise, and personalized medical interventions that improve patients’ overall quality of life.

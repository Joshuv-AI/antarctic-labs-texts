Screening and characterization of monoclonal antibodies with neutralizing activity against porcine transmissible gastroenteritis virus
Authors: Hangyu Jia, Tao Song, Binbin Zheng, Xuran Wang, Ping Rui, Shijie Yan, Wei Liu, Zengjun Ma
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPorcine Transmissible Gastroenteritis Virus (TGEV) is widespread and frequently involved in co-infections, leading to high mortality rates in piglets and significantly constraining the swine industry. Based on the genetic sequence of TGEV strain HB-1, we designed and constructed a eukaryotic expression plasmid, pCAGGS-TGEV-CTD, encoding the CTD gene of TGEV. The recombinant CTD protein was successfully expressed and purified using the HEK-293 F suspension cell expression system. Balb/c mice were immunized with the purified CTD protein.ResultsUpon achieving a serum antibody titer of 10⁵, antigen-specific memory B cells were isolated using single-cell flow cytometry sorting. A total of 83 matched antibody light- and heavy-chain gene pairs were obtained via single-cell RT-PCR. These gene pairs were cloned into pCAGGS eukaryotic expression vectors containing murine constant light (CL) and heavy (CH) chain regions, resulting in the construction of 42 recombinant plasmids for antibody expression. Following transfection into HEK-293T cells, antibody supernatants were harvested at 48 h post-transfection. ELISA screening identified 18 antibodies reactive to the TGEV CTD protein, five high-titer antibodies were confirmed to cross-react with TGEV virions through indirect immunofluorescence assay (IFA). Further neutralization assays demonstrated that all five monoclonal antibodies exhibited in vitro neutralizing activity, effectively inhibiting TGEV infection and replication in swine testicular (ST) cells.ConclusionThis study successfully generated TGEV-specific antibodies, providing critical support for fundamental research on TGEV research and the development of clinical diagnostic reagents.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12917-025-05206-9.Keywords: Porcine transmissible gastroenteritis virus (TGEV), Antibody, Single-Cell flow cytometry sorting, Eukaryotic expression

Background

Transmissible gastroenteritis (TGE), caused by porcine transmissible gastroenteritis virus (TGEV, Alphacoronavirus), is a highly contagious enteric disease of swine [1]. First reported in the United States in 1946, TGE has since been documented globally, with prevalence rates reaching up to 36% in U.S. swine farms by 1992 [2, 3]. In China, TGE was first identified in Guangdong Province in 1956, and outbreaks continue to pose significant challenges in China and other Asian countries [4]. Currently, recognized representative strains include Miller, Purdue, and the naturally recombinant AHHF strain identified in China [5]. TGEV affects pigs of all ages, with mortality inversely correlated with age. Suckling piglets are most susceptible, suffering near 100% mortality, while mortality decreases in pigs older than five weeks. Although adult pigs rarely die from infection, they may experience weight loss and reduced feed conversion efficiency, leading to economic losses [6].

TGEV is an enveloped, positive-sense single-stranded RNA virus. Its structural proteins include the spike (S), envelope (E), membrane (M), and nucleocapsid (N) proteins. The S protein, a critical determinant of infectivity and host recognition, comprises approximately 1,474 amino acids. It is cleaved into S1 and S2 subunits. The S1 subunit contains the N-terminal domain (NTD) and the C-terminal domain (CTD), with the CTD functioning as the primary receptor-binding domain (RBD) [7].

Recent advances highlight single-cell flow cytometry sorting as a powerful tool for antibody discovery. This technique utilizes fluorescently labeled antibodies to distinguish and isolate target cell populations (e.g., antigen-specific B cells) based on surface marker expression. It enables the acquisition of cellular characteristics and facilitates downstream applications such as nucleic acid extraction and PCR amplification. Antibody variable region genes from single antigen-specific B cells can be cloned and expressed in vitro for characterization [8].

This study employed single-cell flow cytometry sorting to isolate TGEV CTD-specific memory B cells from immunized mice. Using the pCAGGS eukaryotic expression system, we generated recombinant antibodies specific to TGEV. The successful identification of neutralizing monoclonal antibodies provides valuable resources for TGEV research and potential applications.

Materials and methods

Cells, plasmids, and viral strains

Stbl3 competent cells were obtained from Shanghai Weidi Biotechnology Co., Ltd. The TGEV HB-1 strain (GenBank accession No.: MZ368889), HEK-293 F cells (ATCC CRL-3216), ST cells (ATCC CRL-1746), and the pCAGGS eukaryotic expression plasmid were maintained in our laboratory.

Key reagents and materials

The endotoxin-free plasmid maxi preparation kit was procured from TIANGEN Biotech (Beijing) Co., Ltd.; transfection reagent and SMM 293-TIII culture medium were supplied by Sino Biological Inc. (Beijing, China); the restriction enzymes EcoR Ⅰ and Xho Ⅰ were obtained from ABL Biotech Co., Ltd. (Wuhan, China); 3 kDa ultrafiltration tubes were purchased from ZePing Technology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China); goat anti-human FITC-IgG was sourced from Beijing Wokai Biotechnology Co., Ltd.; 10×SDS-PAGE electrophoresis buffer was acquired from Coolaber Technology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China); and the DAPI solution was purchased from Solarbio Life Sciences Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China).

Key instruments and equipment

Protein purification was performed using an NGC Chromatography system (Bio-Rad Laboratories, Inc.). A CO₂ incubator was provided by Thermo Fisher Scientific (USA). The constant-temperature shaking incubator was sourced from Shanghai Keheng Industrial Development Co., Ltd. Histrap HP affinity chromatography columns and a Superdex 75 Increase 10/300 GL column were obtained from Cytiva (USA).

Construction of the eukaryotic plasmid encoding the TGEV CTD and its expression and purification

The codon-optimized gene sequence encoding the extracellular region of the TGEV CTD was synthesized and cloned into the eukaryotic expression vector pCAGGS, generating the recombinant plasmid pCAGGS-TGEV-CTD. The plasmid sequence was verified and transformed into Stbl3 competent cells for propagation. Plasmid DNA was extracted and purified using an endotoxin-free maxi preparation kit.

High-yield expression and purification of the TGEV CTD protein

The pCAGGS-TGEV-CTD plasmid was transfected into HEK-293 F suspension cells for protein expression. Seven days post-transfection, the cell supernatant was harvested. The TGEV CTD protein was purified using a two-step chromatographic process: HisTrap HP affinity chromatography (Cytiva, USA) followed by size-exclusion chromatography on a Superdex 75 Increase 10/300 GL column (Cytiva, USA).

Animal immunization and antibody titer evaluation

Balb/c mice at 4 weeks of age (Charles River Laboratories, Inc. Beijing) were subcutaneously immunized with 30 µg of purified TGEV CTD protein per mouse. Booster immunizations were administered biweekly for a total of four immunizations. Serum antibody titers against the CTD protein were determined by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), using the purified CTD protein as the coating antigen.

Flow cytometry-based isolation of antigen-specific memory B cells from mice

Mice were sacrificed after four immunizations. Lymph nodes were harvested, homogenized, and centrifuged. B lymphocytes were isolated following red blood cell lysis and washes with FBS-containing buffer. Cells were resuspended in FACS buffer (PBS containing 2% FBS and 1 mM EDTA) and aliquoted into tubes for staining. Biotinylated TGEV CTD protein was added to sample tubes and incubated in the dark. After washing, cells were stained with a cocktail of fluorescently labeled antibodies (including anti-CD38, anti-CD138, anti-CD93, anti-IgD, anti-B220, anti-GL7, streptavidin-FITC, and viability dye) in the dark. Specific memory B cells were defined as live, single cells, negative for CD38, CD138, CD93, and IgD, positive for B220 and GL7, and positive for CTD binding (FITC+). Cells were sorted using a flow cytometer and collected into a 96-well plate.

Construction, expression, and purification of plasmids encoding antibody light and heavy chains

Single-cell lysates were subjected to reverse transcription. Antibody variable light (VL) and heavy (VH) chain genes were amplified via nested PCR. PCR products were analyzed by agarose gel electrophoresis. Amplified VL and VH gene fragments were gel-purified and cloned into pCAGGS vectors containing the murine kappa constant light chain (pCAGGS-CL) or murine IgG1 constant heavy chain (pCAGGS-CH), respectively. Plasmid sequences were verified. For antibody production, paired light and heavy chain plasmids were co-transfected into HEK-293T cells. Cell supernatants were collected 48 h post-transfection. Antibody specificity and binding activity were assessed by indirect ELISA against the CTD protein. Selected high-affinity antibody clones were scaled up by transfection into HEK-293 F suspension cells. Supernatants were harvested seven days post-transfection, and antibodies were purified using Protein A affinity chromatography.

Identification of antigen binding by indirect immunofluorescence assay (IFA)

ST cells grown in 24-well plates were infected with TGEV (MOI = 1) and cultured for 24 h. Cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min at room temperature, followed by permeabilization and fixation with pre-cooled methanol for 10 min at -20 °C. Non-specific binding was blocked with 3% BSA. Cells were incubated with TGEV antibody supernatant (or control) for 1 h. After washing, cells were incubated with a 1:300 dilution of FITC-labeled goat anti-mouse IgG secondary antibody in the dark for 1 h. Nuclei were counterstained with DAPI for 15 min. After washing with PBS, fluorescence images were captured using a fluorescence microscope.

Virus neutralization assay

Serial two-fold dilutions of each purified monoclonal antibodies (MAbs) (starting from 10 µg/mL down to 5.08 ng/mL, 50 µL per well) were prepared in MEM medium in a 96-well plate. An equal volume (50 µL) of TGEV suspension containing 100 TCID50 was added to each antibody dilution and incubated at 37℃ for 1 h. The antibody-virus mixtures were then transferred onto confluent monolayers of ST cells in a 96-well plate (100 µL per well, 8 replicates per dilution). Uninfected cells (negative control) and virus-only infected cells (positive control) were included. Plates were incubated at 37℃ for 3 days. Cytopathic effect (CPE) was monitored and recorded daily. Neutralization was assessed by the inhibition of CPE. The neutralizing antibody titer (ND50), defined as the antibody concentration required to neutralize 50% of the viral infectivity, was calculated using the Reed-Muench methods.

Results

Characterization and expression-purification of the TGEV CTD eukaryotic expression plasmid

Double digestion of the recombinant plasmid pCAGGS-TGEV-CTD with EcoRI and XhoI generated a specific 624 bp fragment consistent with the expected size, indicating successful cloning of the target insert into the eukaryotic expression vector. The expressed CTD protein was purified using Histrap HP affinity chromatography followed by size-exclusion chromatography on a Superdex 75 Increase column, yielding a major peak eluting at approximately 13 mL (Fig. 1A and B). The pooled peak fractions were concentrated using a 3-kDa MWCO ultrafiltration tube, resulting in a final yield of 10 mg of purified protein. SDS-PAGE analysis (Fig. 1C) showed a predominant band between 15 kDa and 25 kDa, suggesting that the protein was successfully expressed and purified to high homogeneity. This was further confirmed by Western blot, which specifically detected the CTD protein using the anti-His tag antibody (Fig. S1).

Purification and Identification of TGEV CTD Protein. A: Purification of TGEV CTD protein via Histrap affinity column; B: Purification of TGEV CTD protein via Superdex 75 Increase column; C: SDS-PAGE identification of TGEV CTD protein

Isolation of TGEV CTD-specific memory B cells by flow cytometry

Flow cytometry sorting was employed to isolate memory B cells specific for the TGEV CTD protein from immunized mice. Using a gating strategy involving multiple negative (CD38−, CD138−, CD93−, IgD−) and positive (B220+, GL7+, CTD-biotin/Streptavidin−FITC+) markers, a total of approximately 162 single TGEV CTD-specific memory B cells were successfully obtained (Fig. 2).

Flow cytometry sorting of TGEV CTD-Specific Memory B Cells

Amplification of TGEV antibody variable region genes

The VH and VL genes were amplified from the 162 sorted single cells via nested PCR. As depicted in Fig. 3, specific bands of approximately 450 bp (VH) and 400 bp (VL), consistent with the expected sizes, were observed in the majority of wells. The PCR products were cloned, transformed, and sequenced. Sequence alignment and pairing enabled the identification of 42 matched pairs of antibody light and heavy chain genes.

PCR amplification results of VH and VL genes of TGEV specific antibodies. A: PCR amplification results of VH genes; B: PCR amplification results of VL genes. M: DL2000 DNA Marker; 1-8: Different TGEV CTD-Specific Memory B Cells

Expression and binding activity assessment of TGEV antibodies

The 42 paired recombinant plasmids (VL + VH) were co-transfected into HEK-293T cells for transient expression. Cell supernatants collected 48 h post-transfection were screened for binding activity to the TGEV CTD protein using indirect ELISA. Firstly, for coating TGEV CTD protein, we measured the OD values of 10 serum samples from unimmunized mice. Based on the formula: Cut-off value = average OD value of negative control + 3 × standard deviation, we calculated the Cut-off values for negative and positive samples. As shown in Figs. 4 and 18 out of the 42 candidate antibodies exhibited specific binding to the CTD antigen, indicating successful antibody expression and antigen recognition.

ELISA detection results of candidate antibodies against TGEV CTD protein

Detection of TGEV antigen binding by IFA

Five MAbs (L-F17, L-B4, L-E20, L-B15, L-C3) demonstrating strong binding activity in ELISA were selected for further characterization by IFA. As illustrated in Fig. 5, all five MAbs produced prominent and specific fluorescence signals in TGEV-infected ST cells, indicating their ability to recognize the native TGEV antigen within infected cells. No significant fluorescence was observed in uninfected cells.

IFA detection results of candidate antibodies against TGEV CTD protein. A-E: TGEV candidate antibodies (L-F17, L-B4, L-E20, L-B15, L-C3), F: Negative control

Results of the neutralization assay

The in vitro neutralizing activity of the five TGEV-specific MAbs was evaluated using a microneutralization assay on ST cells. As shown in Fig. 6, all five MAbs exhibited measurable neutralizing activity against TGEV, reducing viral infection and CPE formation in a dose-dependent manner. The neutralizing potency, expressed as the ND50, varied among the MAbs: L-F17 exhibited the highest neutralizing potency, with an ND50 of 0.033 µg/mL. In comparison, the ND50 values for L-B4 and L-E20 were 0.12 µg/mL and 0.29 µg/mL, respectively. MAb L-B15 and L-C3 also neutralized but with lower potency (ND50 values > 1 µg/mL); These results indicate that L-F17, L-B4, and L-E20 possess strong in vitro neutralizing activity against TGEV.

Discussion

Porcine transmissible gastroenteritis virus (TGEV) remains a significant threat to the global swine industry, causing severe economic losses, particularly through high mortality in neonatal piglets [9, 10]. While existing live attenuated and inactivated vaccines offer some control, limitations such as potential interference by maternal antibodies necessitate the exploration of novel prophylactic and therapeutic strategies, including neutralizing antibodies [11].

Neutralizing antibodies function by blocking viral entry into host cells, representing a promising approach for viral control, as evidenced in other coronaviruses like SARS-CoV-2 [12]. The clinical potential of monoclonal neutralizing antibodies has been demonstrated for viruses including HIV-1 [13] and Ebola virus [14]. Single-cell sorting technologies have revolutionized the discovery of potent neutralizing antibodies against pathogens like SARS-CoV-2 and PRRSV [12, 15], enabling high-throughput screening, selection of high-affinity binders, preservation of natural VH: VL pairing, and enhanced diversity capture [16, 17]. In this study, we applied this approach to the development of monoclonal antibodies against TGEV. Mammalian cell expression systems, particularly HEK-293 cells, are widely regarded as a standard platform for producing functional recombinant antibodies and glycoproteins due to their capacity for proper folding and post-translational modifications [18–20]. The HEK-293 F suspension cell line, with its scalability and high-density growth characteristics [21, 22], served as an effective platform for both the initial expression of the immunogen (TGEV CTD) and the subsequent production of functional monoclonal antibodies in this study. The successful expression of a conformationally relevant and immunogenic CTD protein was considered important for eliciting and isolating antibodies capable of recognizing the native virus [23, 24].

The emergence of coronaviruses with cross-species potential, such as SADS-CoV [25], underscores the importance of developing broad countermeasures. The combination of single-cell sorting and mammalian expression provides an efficient pipeline for antibody discovery [26]. Studies on SARS-CoV antibodies demonstrating cross-neutralization of SARS-CoV-2 [27], and the identification of cross-reactive epitopes between PEDV and TGEV [28, 29], provide encouraging precedents. Thavorasak et al. [30] further highlighted the potential of identifying conserved neutralizing epitopes within the Alphacoronavirus genus. The antibodies generated in our study, particularly the potent neutralizing MAbs L-F17, L-B4, and L-E20, provide essential tools to explore such cross-reactivity within TGEV strains and potentially related coronaviruses.

Collectively, these findings highlight the feasibility of generating neutralizing MAbs against TGEV using a single-cell–based discovery platform.However, this study was limited to in vitro analyses, and the in vivo efficacy and potential cross-reactivity of these antibodies warrant further investigation.

Conclusion

This study applied an integrated approach combining single-cell flow cytometry sorting of antigen-specific memory B cells with a mammalian cell expression system to generate MAbs against TGEV. Three of these MAbs (L-F17, L-B4, L-E20) exhibited measurable in vitro neutralizing activity against TGEV. These antibodies provide crucial tools for advancing fundamental research on TGEV pathogenesis and immunity, developing sensitive and specific diagnostic reagents, and exploring novel therapeutic and preventive strategies against TGEV and potentially related porcine coronaviruses. This work illustrates the potential utility of modern antibody discovery platforms for addressing important veterinary viral pathogens.

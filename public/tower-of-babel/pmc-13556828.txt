387. Autism spectrum disorder (ASD): zinc suppresses cerebellar ultrastructural alterations and the oxidative stress/gliosis/neuronal loss axis-mediated ASD
Authors: F AL-Hashem, H Zafrah, A Garni, A Shati, F Almasabi, B Al-Ani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAutism spectrum disorder (ASD) is a major mental health concern in young children, and there is a link between ASD and zinc deficiency.Aims & ObjectivesNeither the potential protective effects of the trace element zinc on the cerebellum against ultrastructural damage in animal models of ASD, nor its ability to protect against the modulation of the oxidative stress/gliosis/neuronal loss axis implicated in autism, has been previously investigated.MethodTherefore, young rats were injected with the short-chain fatty acid produced by gut bacteria that is known to induce ASD, propionic acid (PPA) (250 mg/kg) for 5 days (model group) and the protection group was treated with 2 mg/kg zinc sulfate (PPA+ZnSO4) for two weeks post PPA injections.ResultsCerebellum damage and behavioral impairment developed in the model group of rats as demonstrated by profound cerebellar ultrastructural alterations such as degeneration of Purkinje neurons and separation between cerebellar granule cells, as well as social anxiety and social skill deficits. PPA also significantly (p<0.001) modulated the cerebellum protein levels of the gliosis biomarker GFAP, neuronal loss biomarker calbindin D28K, and the cerebellar tissue levels of zinc, glutathione, and oxidative stress biomarker malondialdehyde (MDA). All these parameters were significantly (p≤0.001) protected by zinc sulfate. Additionally, we observed a significant (p<0.001) correlation between the score of the damaged Purkinje cells and these parameters associated with autism.Discussion & ConclusionsPPA-induced autistic behaviours in rats are associated with alteration to the cerebellar ultrastructure, modulation of the oxidative stress/gliosis/neuronal loss axis of ASD, and a decline in zinc and glutathione, which were protected by zinc sulfate treatment. These studies are the first to demonstrate the protective effect of zinc sulfate against the induction of cerebellar ultrastructural alterations and the dysregulation of the oxidative stress/gliosis/neuronal loss axis in animal models of ASD. Additionally, we established a link between the alteration to the Purkinje cell ultrastructure and the dysregulation of the cerebellar oxidative stress, antioxidant, zinc, serotonin, gliosis, neuronal loss, and anxiety-like behavior, which further confirm the beneficial effects of zinc sulfate in ASD.

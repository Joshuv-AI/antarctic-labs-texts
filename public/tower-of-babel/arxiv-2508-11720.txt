New Type degenerate Simsek numbers and related aspects
Lahcen Oussi

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce a new type degenerate Simsek numbers and their generating function, which are different from degenerate Simsek number studied so far. We establish the explicit formula, recurrence relation and other identities for these numbers. We also derive several interesting expressions and relations between these numbers and certain other special numbers in the literature.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 05A15; Secondary: 11B83, 15A99.

1.
Introduction and Preliminaries

Special numbers and polynomials play crucial rule to solve explicitly several problems in many scientific areas, such as in mathematical physics, biology, quantum mechanics, discrete mathematics, probability and statistics. The very well known special numbers are Stirling numbers of the first and second kind introduced by James Stirling in 1730.

Stirling numbers of the second kind
S_{1}(n,k)
count the number of partitions of a set of
size
n
into
k
disjoint, non-empty subsets. They appear as coefficients in the following expansion:

(1.1)

x^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2}(n,k)(x)_{k},

where

\begin{cases}(x)_{n}=x(x-1)(x-2)\cdots(x-n+1)&\text{ if }n\geq 1,\\
(x)_{n}=1&\text{ if }n=0,\end{cases}

denotes the falling factorial (cf.
[
5
,
23
]
). The generating function of
S_{2}(n,k)
is given by

(1.2)

\frac{1}{k!}(e^{t}-1)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Stirling numbers of the first kind, denoted by
S_{1}(n,k)
, are given by the following:

(1.3)

(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,k)x^{k},

with its generating function

(1.4)

\frac{(\log(1+t))^{k}}{k!}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

(cf.
[
5
,
23
]
).

The modifications and degenerate versions of these numbers were studied by several researchers, see for instance
[
22
,
1
,
7
,
8
,
11
,
30
,
17
,
18
,
20
,
21
,
24
,
25
,
13
]
and the references therein.

For a nonzero
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
(or
\mathbb{C}
), the degenerate exponential function
e_{\alpha}^{x}(t)
defined as follows

(1.5)

e_{\alpha}^{x}(t)=(1+\alpha t)^{\frac{x}{\alpha}}\text{ and }e_{\alpha}(t)=(1+\alpha t)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}},\quad\text{(see,~\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{LC1979, TKDSK2017, TKDSKHIK2017, DSKTK2020, DSKTKHYKHL2020}{}{}]})}.

Using the Taylor expansion, the degenerate exponential function can be expressed as follows:

(1.6)

e_{\alpha}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(x)_{n,\alpha}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

where

\begin{cases}(x)_{n,\alpha}=x(x-\alpha)(x-2\alpha)\cdots(x-(n-1)\alpha)&\text{ if }n\geq 1,\\
(x)_{0,\alpha}=1&\text{ if }n=0,\end{cases}

is called the degenerate falling factorial. It follows that

\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow{0}}e_{\alpha}^{x}(t)=\sum\frac{x^{n}t^{n}}{n!}=e^{xt}.

The degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind
S_{2,\alpha}(n,l)
are given as follows
[
28
,
12
,
6
,
27
]
:

(1.7)

(x)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{l=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,l)(x)_{l}.

Moreover, as an inversion formula of (
1.7
), the degenerate Stirling numbers of the fist kind
S_{1,\alpha}(n,l)
are defined as follows
[
28
,
12
,
6
]
:

(1.8)

(x)_{n}=\sum_{l=0}^{n}S_{1,\alpha}(n,l)(x)_{l.\alpha}.

Note that

\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow 0}S_{1,\alpha}(n,l)=S_{1}(n,l)\text{ and }\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow 0}S_{2,\alpha}(n,l)=S_{2}(n,l)

In addition, a new type degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind
S_{2}^{*}(n,k|\alpha)
were introduced in
[
13
]
as

(1.9)

\frac{1}{k!}(e^{t}-1)_{k,\alpha}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{2}^{*}(n,k|\alpha)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\quad\text{ and }\quad S_{2}^{*}(n,0|\alpha)=0,\quad(n\geq 0).

They reduced to Stirling numbers of the second kind (
1.2
).

In 2018, Simsek
[
26
]
introduced new families of special numbers
y_{1}(n,k;\lambda)
, for computing negative order Euler numbers and related numbers and polynomials. Simsek numbers
y_{1}(n,k;\lambda)
are defined by means of generating function

(1.10)

\frac{\Big(\lambda e^{t}+1\Big)^{k}}{k!}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}y_{1}(n,k;\lambda)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

They can be expressed explicitly by the following identity

(1.11)

y_{1}(n,k;\lambda)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}j^{n}\lambda^{j}.

Simsek numbers
y_{1}(n,k;\lambda)
are related to the many well-known special numbers in the literature, among them, Bernoulli
numbers, Fibonacci numbers, Stirling numbers of the second kind, Lucas numbers and the central numbers.

Recently, the author introduced a degenerate version of Simsek numbers
y_{1}(n,k;\lambda|\alpha)
in
[
22
]
, by the mean of the generating function

(1.12)

\frac{\Big(\lambda e^{\frac{\log(1+\alpha t)}{\alpha}}+1\Big)^{k}}{k!}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}y_{1}(n,k;\lambda|\alpha)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

with the explicit formulas

(1.13)

y_{1}(n,k;\lambda|\alpha)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}j^{m}\lambda^{j}\alpha^{n-m}S_{1}(n,m)

and

(1.14)

y_{1}(n,k;\lambda|\alpha)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\lambda^{j}\alpha^{n}\Big(\frac{j}{\alpha}\Big)_{{}_{n}}.

For more details about the numbers
y_{1}(n,k;\lambda|\alpha)
and related aspects, we refer the readers to
[
22
]
.

Kucukoglu
[
16
]
, investigated the
q
-combinatorial Simsek numbers
y_{1,q}(n,k;\lambda)
and polynomials
y_{1,q}(x;n,k;\lambda)
of the first kind, respectively, as follows

y_{1,q}(n,k;\lambda)=\frac{1}{[k]_{q}!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}q^{\binom{j}{2}}\begin{bmatrix}k\\
j\end{bmatrix}_{q}[j]_{q}^{n}\lambda^{j}

and

y_{1,q}(x;n,k;\lambda)=\frac{1}{[k]_{q}!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}q^{\binom{j}{2}}\begin{bmatrix}k\\
j\end{bmatrix}_{q}[x+j]_{q}^{n}\lambda^{j}.

In
[
9
]
, Kucukoglu et al. defined the higher order expansion of the Simsek numbers and polynomials. Moreover, Kilar
[

In this paper, we establish a new type degenerate Sismek numbers, which are different from degenerate Simsek numbers (
1.12

2.
New type degenerate Simsek numbers and related formulas

In this section, we define a new type degenerate Simsek numbers and we derive some related formulas including generating function, derivative formulas, recurrence formulas, and integral formulas. Moreover, we establish also a relation between these numbers and certain special numbers.

We define a new type degenerate Simsek numbers
y^{*}_{1,\alpha}(n,k;\lambda)
by means of generating function:

(2.1)

F_{k}(t;\alpha,\lambda):=\frac{(\lambda e^{t}+1)_{k,\alpha}}{k!}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n,k;\lambda)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

It follows that for
\alpha=0
, we obtain
y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n,k;\lambda)=y_{1}(n,k;\lambda)
Simsek numbers (
1.10
). Moreover, the function
F_{k}(t;\alpha,k)
can be expressed as follows

(2.2)

F_{k}(t;\alpha,\lambda)=\frac{\alpha^{k}}{k!}\bigg(\frac{\lambda e^{t}+1}{\alpha}\bigg)_{k}=\frac{\alpha^{k}}{k!}B_{k}^{(k+1)}\left(\frac{\lambda e^{t}+1}{\alpha}+1\right),

where
B_{k}^{(n)}(x)
denotes Bernoulli’s polynomials of order
n
given by

(2.3)

\frac{t^{n}e^{xt}}{(e^{t}-1)^{n}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}B_{k}^{(n)}(x)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}

and satisfies the recurrence relation

(2.4)

B_{k}^{(n+1)}(x)=\left(1-\frac{k}{n}\right)B_{k}^{(n)}(x)+k\left(\frac{x}{n}-1\right)B_{k-1}^{(n)}(x),

(cf.
[
19
]
).

By using (
2.1
), we derive the following functional equation

(2.5)

\alpha^{k}\left(\frac{\lambda e^{t}}{\alpha}\right)_{k}=(\lambda e^{t})_{k,\alpha}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}(-1)_{k-\ell,\alpha}\binom{k}{\ell}\ell!F_{\ell}(t;\alpha,\lambda).

Combining (
2.1
) and (
1.3
) with the above equation, yields

(2.6)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{k}\alpha^{k-j}S_{1}(k,j)\lambda^{j}j^{n}\right)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}(-1)_{k-\ell,\alpha}\ell!\binom{k}{\ell}y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n,\ell;\lambda)\right)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Comparing the coefficients of
\frac{t^{n}}{n1}
on both sides of (
2.6
), we obtain the
following relation between the numbers
y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n,k;\lambda)
and Stirling numbers of the first kind
S_{1}(n,k)
:

Theorem 2.1
.

(2.7)

\sum_{j=0}^{k}\alpha^{k-j}S_{1}(k,j)\lambda^{j}j^{n}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}(-1)_{k-\ell,\alpha}\ell!\binom{k}{\ell}y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n,\ell;\lambda).

Remark 2.2
.

It follows that for
\alpha=0
, we obtain

\lambda^{k}k^{n}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-\ell}\binom{k}{\ell}\ell!y_{1}(n,\ell;\lambda)

the result given in
[
26
]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

The numbers
y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n,k;\lambda)
can be expressed explicitly as follows:

(2.8)

y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n,k;\lambda)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\binom{\ell}{j}\alpha^{k-\ell}S_{1}(k,\ell)\lambda^{j}j^{n}

or

(2.9)

y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n,k;\lambda)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\binom{k}{\ell}\alpha^{\ell-j}\lambda^{j}j^{n}(1)_{k-\ell,j}S_{1}(\ell,j).

Proof.

Using (
1.3
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{(\lambda e^{t}+1)_{k,\alpha}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{k}}{k!}\Big(\frac{\lambda e^{t}+1}{\alpha}\Big)_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\alpha^{k-\ell}S_{1}(k,\ell)(\lambda e^{t}+1)^{\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\alpha^{k-\ell}S_{1}(k,\ell)\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\binom{\ell}{j}\lambda^{j}e^{jt}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Bigg(\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\alpha^{k-\ell}S_{1}(k,\ell)\binom{\ell}{j}\lambda^{j}j^{n}\Bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Hence, by (
2.1
), we obtain the desired result.

To prove Eq. (
2.9
), we use the fact that
(x+y)_{k,\alpha}=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(x)_{j,\alpha}(y)_{k-j,\alpha}
. Then,

\displaystyle\frac{\big(\lambda e^{t}+1\big)_{k,\alpha}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\binom{k}{\ell}(\lambda e^{t})_{\ell,\alpha}(1)_{k-\ell,\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\binom{k}{\ell}\alpha^{\ell}\Big(\frac{\lambda e^{t}}{\alpha}\Big)_{\ell}(1)_{k-\ell,\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\binom{k}{\ell}\alpha^{\ell-j}\lambda^{j}j^{n}(1)_{k-\ell,\alpha}S_{1}(\ell,j)\right)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Hence the result follows.
∎

On the other hand, using the fact that
F_{k}(t;\alpha,\lambda)=\frac{\alpha^{k}}{k!}\Big(\frac{\lambda e^{t}+1}{\alpha}\Big)_{k}
and the identity

(2.10)

(x)_{k}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\frac{j}{k}x^{j}B_{k-j}^{(k)},

where
B_{n}^{(k)}
denotes Bernoulli’s numbers of order
k
, defined by

(2.11)

\Big(\frac{t}{e^{t}-1}\Big)^{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}B_{n}^{(k)}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

(cf.
[
19
]
), we obtain the following proposition:

Proposition 2.4
.

The numbers
y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n,k;\lambda)
are given, in relation with Bernoulli’s numbers
B_{k}^{(n)}
of order
n
, by the following

(2.12)

y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n,k;\lambda)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\alpha^{k-j}\binom{k}{j}\frac{j}{k}B_{k-j}^{(k)}\sum_{\ell=0}^{j}\binom{j}{\ell}\lambda^{\ell}\ell^{n}.

Remark 2.5
.

Substituting
\alpha=0
in Equations (
2.12
), (
2.8
) and (
2.9
), we obtain

y_{1,0}^{*}(n,k;\lambda)=y_{1}(n,k;\lambda)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}j^{n}\lambda^{j}

Simsek numbers (
1.11
).

Example 2.6
.

Some special cases of the numbers
y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n,k;\lambda)
are given as follows:

•

y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n,0;\lambda)=\delta_{n0}

•

y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n,1;\lambda)=\delta_{n0}+\lambda

•

y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(0,k;\lambda)=\frac{\alpha^{k}}{k!}\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{k}S_{1}(k,\ell)\left(\frac{\lambda+1}{\alpha}\right)^{\ell}

In the following theorem, we give a relation between the numbers
y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n,k;\lambda)
, Simsek numbers
y_{1}(n,k;\lambda)
, Stirling numbers of the first kind
S_{1}(n,k)
and the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind
S_{2.\alpha}(n,k)
.

Theorem 2.7
.

The numbers
y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n,k;\lambda)
can be expressed as follows:

y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n,k;\lambda)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}S_{2,\alpha}(k,\ell)S_{1}(\ell,j)j!y_{1}(n,j;\lambda).

Proof.

Using (
1.3
), (
1.10
), (
2.1
) and (
1.7
), we have

(2.13)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n,k;\lambda)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}S_{2,\alpha}(k,\ell)(\lambda e^{t}+1)_{\ell}

(2.14)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}S_{2,\alpha}(k,\ell)\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}S_{1}(\ell,j)(\lambda e^{t}+1)^{j}

(2.15)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}S_{2,\alpha}(k,\ell)\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}S_{1}(\ell,j)j!\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}y_{1}(n,j;\lambda)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Comparing the coefficients of both sides of the above identity, we obtain the desired result.
∎

On the other hand, the numbers
y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n,k;\lambda)
are related to the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind
S_{2}^{*}(n,k|\alpha)
as follows:

Theorem 2.8
.

(2.16)

y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n,k;\lambda)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}j!\lambda^{j}(\lambda+1)_{k-j,\alpha}S_{2}^{*}\left(n,k|\frac{\alpha}{\lambda}\right).

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\frac{\left(\lambda e^{t}+1\right)_{k,\alpha}}{k!}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\left(\lambda(e^{t}-1)+\lambda+1\right)_{k,\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\left(\lambda(e^{t}-1)\right)_{j,\alpha}(\lambda+1)_{k-j,\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\lambda^{j}(e^{t}-1)_{j,\frac{\alpha}{\lambda}}(\lambda+1)_{k-j,\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\lambda^{j}j!\lambda^{j}(\lambda+1)_{k-j,\alpha}\sum_{n\geq 0}S_{2}^{*}\left(n,j|\frac{\alpha}{\lambda}\right)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

Using (
2.1
) and equating the coefficients in both sides of the above identity finishes the proof.
∎

Theorem 2.9
.

The numbers
y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n,k;\lambda)
satisfy the following recurrence formula with respect to
k

(2.17)

y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n,k+1;\lambda)=\frac{1}{k+1}\bigg(\lambda\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\binom{n}{\ell}y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(\ell,k;\lambda)+(1-k\alpha)y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n,k;\lambda)\bigg).

Proof.

From (
2.1
), we have

(2.18)

F_{k}(t;\alpha,\lambda)=\left(\frac{\lambda e^{t}+\alpha-k\alpha+1}{k}\right)F_{k-1}(t;\alpha,\lambda).

Hence,

(2.19)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n,k+1;\lambda)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda}{k+1}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n,k;\lambda)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}+\frac{1-k\alpha}{k+1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n,k;\lambda)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

(2.20)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k+1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\lambda\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(\ell,k;\lambda)\binom{n}{\ell}+(1-k\alpha)y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n,k;\lambda)\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

By comparing the coefficients of both sides of (
2.20
), we have (
2.17
).
∎

Taking the derivative with respect to
t
of both sides of (
2.18
), we obtain

F^{\prime}_{k}(t;\alpha,\lambda)=\frac{\lambda e^{t}}{k}\left(F_{k-1}(t;\alpha,\lambda)+F^{\prime}_{k-1}(t;\alpha.\lambda)\right)+\left(\frac{1+\alpha-k\alpha}{k}\right)F^{\prime}_{k-1}(t;\alpha.\lambda).

Then, using (
2.1
), the above identity becomes

\begin{split}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n+1,k;\lambda)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}&=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Bigg[\frac{\lambda}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}\left(y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(j,k-1;\lambda)+y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(j+1,k-1;\lambda)\right)\\
&+\left(\frac{1+\alpha-k\alpha}{k}\right)y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n+1,k-1;\lambda)\Bigg]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.\end{split}

Therefore, equating the coefficients of
\frac{t^{n}}{n!}
, gives another recursive formula for the numbers
y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n,k;\lambda)
in the following theorem.

Theorem 2.10
.

The numbers
y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n,k;\lambda)
satisfy the following recurrence formula

\begin{split}y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n+1,k;\lambda)&=\frac{\lambda}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}\bigg(y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(j,k-1;\lambda)+y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(j+1,k-1;\lambda)\bigg)\\
&\quad\qquad+\left(\frac{1+\alpha-k\alpha}{k}\right)y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n+1,k-1;\lambda)\text{,}\qquad\text{ for }k\geq 1\end{split}

and

y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n,0;\lambda)=\delta_{n,0}.

Remark 2.11
.

Note that, one can also prove Theorem
2.10
, using (
2.2
), (
2.4
) and the derivative of Bernoulli’s polynomials of order
k

(2.21)

\frac{d}{dt}B_{n}^{(k)}(t)=nB_{n-1}^{(k)}(t)

(cf.
[
19

)

We define the generating function
\phi^{*}_{n}(x|\alpha,\lambda)
of the numbers
y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n,k;\lambda)
as follows:

(2.22)

\phi^{*}_{n}(x|\alpha,\lambda):=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n,k;\lambda)x^{k}.

In the further, we derive some formulas related to the generating function
\phi^{*}_{n}(x|\alpha,\lambda)
, such as exponential generating function, recurrence formula, integral formula, derivative formula and relation with some well-know numbers.

Theorem 2.12
.

For a non-negative integer
n
, we have the following exponential generating function of
\phi^{*}_{n}(x|\alpha,\lambda)
:

(2.23)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n}^{*}(x|\alpha,\lambda)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=e_{\alpha}^{\lambda e^{t}+1}(x)

Proof.

we have

(2.24)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n}^{*}(x|\alpha,\lambda)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n,k;\lambda)x^{k}\Big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

(2.25)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n,k;\lambda)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\Big)x^{k}

(2.26)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(\lambda e^{t}+1)_{k,\alpha}\frac{x^{k}}{k!}

(2.27)

\displaystyle=e_{\alpha}^{\lambda e^{t}+1}(x),

where in the third equation we used (
2.1
) and in the last equation we used (
1.5
).
∎

The following result, describe a relation between the generating functions
\phi_{n}^{*}(x|\alpha,\lambda)
and
\phi_{n}^{*}(x|0,\lambda)
of the numbers
y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n,k;\lambda)
and Simsek numbers
y_{1}(n,k;\lambda)
, respectively.

Theorem 2.13
.

The generating function
\phi_{n}^{*}(x|\alpha,\lambda)
can be expressed as follows:

\phi_{n}^{*}(x|\alpha,\lambda)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{\log(1+\alpha x)}{\alpha}\bigg)^{k}y_{1}(n,k;\lambda)=\phi_{n}^{*}\left(\frac{\log(1+\alpha x)}{\alpha}|0,\lambda\right),

where
\phi_{n}^{*}(x|0,\lambda)
is the generating function of Simsek numbers
y_{1}(n,k;\lambda)
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.12
, we have

(2.28)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n}^{*}(x|\alpha,\lambda)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=e_{\alpha}^{\lambda e^{t}+1}(x)

(2.29)

\displaystyle=(1+\alpha x)^{\frac{\lambda e^{t}+1}{\alpha}}

(2.30)

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{\lambda e^{t}+1}{\alpha}\log(1+\alpha x)}

(2.31)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\alpha^{k}}\big(\log(1+\alpha x)\big)^{k}\frac{(\lambda e^{t}+1)^{k}}{k!}

(2.32)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{\log(1+\alpha x)}{\alpha}\Big)^{k}y_{1}(n,k;\lambda)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Hence, the result follows by comparing the coefficients of both sides of (
2.32
).
∎

Theorem 2.14
.

For
n\geq 0
, the generating function
\phi_{n}^{*}(x,\alpha|\lambda)
satisfies the following recursive formula:

(2.33)

\phi_{n+1}^{*}(x|\alpha,\lambda)=\frac{\lambda}{\alpha}\log(1+\alpha x)\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\binom{n}{\ell}\phi^{*}_{\ell}(x|\alpha,\lambda)

Proof.

Taking the derivative with respect to
t
in both sides of (
2.23
), we obtain

(2.34)

\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi^{*}_{n}(x|\alpha,\lambda)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi^{*}_{n+1}(x|\alpha,\lambda)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

and

(2.35)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(e_{\alpha}^{\lambda e^{t}+1}(x)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda}{\alpha}e^{t}\log(1+\alpha x)e_{\alpha}^{\lambda e^{t}+1}(x)

(2.36)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda}{\alpha}\log(1+\alpha x)\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\phi_{\ell}^{*}(x|\alpha,\lambda)\frac{t^{\ell}}{\ell!}

(2.37)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda}{\alpha}\log(1+\alpha x)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\binom{n}{\ell}\phi_{\ell}^{*}(x|\alpha,\lambda)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Equating the coefficients of
\frac{t^{n}}{n!}
in (
2.37

3.
Conclusions

In the present work, we have established a new type degenerate Simsek numbers
y_{1,\alpha}^{*}(n,k;\lambda)
with their generating function, using the degenerate falling function. These numbers are different from degenerate Simsek number studied so far. We analyzed their some properties, including derivative formula, recurrence relation, integral formula. In addition, we described the relation between these numbers and certain well-known special numbers, for instance, Stirling numbers of the first and the second kind, the degenerate first kind Apostol-Euler numbers, the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind, Bernoulli numbers, Simsek numbers. Hence, the results of this paper have the potential to find an application in different areas of mathematics, especially dealing with discrete mathematics.

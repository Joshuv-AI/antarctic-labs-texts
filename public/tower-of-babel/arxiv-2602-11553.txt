Perception-based Image Denoising via Generative Compression
Nam Nguyen, Thinh Nguyen, Bella Bose

Abstract
Image denoising aims to remove noise while preserving structural details and perceptual realism, yet distortion-driven methods often produce over-smoothed reconstructions, especially under strong noise and distribution shift. This paper proposes a generative compression framework for perception-based denoising, where restoration is achieved by reconstructing from entropy-coded latent representations that enforce low-complexity structure, while generative decoders recover realistic textures via perceptual measures such as learned perceptual image patch similarity (LPIPS) loss and Wasserstein distance. Two complementary instantiations are introduced: (i) a conditional Wasserstein GAN (WGAN)-based compression denoiser that explicitly controls the rate-distortion-perception (RDP) trade-off, and (ii) a conditional diffusion-based reconstruction strategy that performs iterative denoising guided by compressed latents. We further establish non-asymptotic guarantees for the compression-based maximum-likelihood denoiser under additive Gaussian noise, including bounds on reconstruction error and decoding error probability. Experiments on synthetic and real-noise benchmarks demonstrate consistent perceptual improvements while maintaining competitive distortion performance.

Introduction

Image denoising is a key problem in image processing with applications spanning low-light photography, microscopy, and scientific imaging. The objective is to remove noise while preserving structural details and perceptual realism.
Classical methods rely on hand-crafted priors such as sparsity and nonlocal self-similarity, with BM3D being a prominent example
[
4
]
.
More recently, deep discriminative models, including DnCNN
[
20
]
and FFDNet
[
21
]
, have achieved substantial improvements by learning direct mappings from noisy to clean images.
However, approaches optimized primarily for pixel-wise distortion metrics such as mean squared error (MSE) or peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) often produce over-smoothed reconstructions and loss of fine textures, particularly under strong noise or distribution shift
[
3
]
.

:
Samples generated by our methods on KODAK images.
From left to right: original, noisy observation with Gaussian noise

with
\sigma=50
, and denoised output.

Perceptual image restoration seeks to overcome these limitations by explicitly prioritizing visual quality.
The rate-distortion-perception (RDP) tradeoff established in
[
3
]
formalizes that minimizing distortion alone is insufficient for perceptual realism, motivating the use of perceptual losses and generative models.
Conditional Wasserstein GAN (WGAN)
[
13
,
2
,
12
]
has been widely adopted for perceptual restoration; however, adversarial training is often unstable and may inadequately capture the full diversity of natural image statistics.
More recently, diffusion models have emerged as powerful generative priors, offering stable training and strong mode coverage by learning to reverse a gradual noise-corruption process
[
9
]
.

Denoising and compression are closely connected through the principle that natural signals exhibit low complexity, whereas noise is largely unstructured.
Compression-based denoising exploits this idea by interpreting denoising as a projection onto a low-complexity signal class induced by a lossy codec.
From an information-theoretic perspective, operating a compressor at a distortion level matched to the noise strength suppresses high-complexity noise while preserving structured content
[
16
]
.
Recent works have instantiated this principle using neural compression models
[
18
,
19
]
, but limited perceptual modeling constrains their performance on natural images.

In this paper, we propose a generative compression framework for perception-based image denoising based on entropy-coded latent representations.
We introduce two complementary approaches.
First, a conditional WGAN-based compression denoiser encodes noisy images into compressed latents and reconstructs denoised outputs using conditional adversarial training and perceptual distortion measured by learned perceptual image patch similarity (LPIPS)
[
5
]
, enabling explicit control over rate, distortion, and perceptual quality.
Second, we propose a conditional diffusion-based reconstruction strategy that performs iterative denoising conditioned on compressed latents, combining the stability and expressiveness of diffusion priors with compression-induced global consistency. On the theory side, we formulate a perception-constrained compression framework for image denoising that explicitly connects denoising performance to RDP theory, and establish non-asymptotic upper bounds on the reconstruction error and the decoding error probability of a compression-based maximum- likelihood denoiser under a Gaussian noise model. Experimental results on synthetic and real-noise benchmarks demonstrate that the proposed methods achieve improved perceptual fidelity while maintaining competitive distortion performance.

Denoising via Perception-Constrained Lossy Compression

2.1
Problem Description

Denoising is a fundamental problem in signal processing and has recently attracted renewed interest in machine learning.
Let
{\bm{x}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
denote an unknown non-negative signal, and let
{\bm{y}}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})
be its noisy observation.
We assume a memoryless and homogeneous noise model, where observations are conditionally independent given
{\bm{x}}
, i.e.,

{\bm{y}}\sim\prod_{i=1}^{n}p(y_{i}|x_{i})
.
The objective of denoising is to estimate the underlying signal
{\bm{x}}
from
{\bm{y}}
.

Let
\mathcal{Q}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
denote a signal class of interest, such as vectorized natural images.
A lossy compression scheme for
\mathcal{Q}
is defined by an encoder-decoder pair
(f,g)
, where

f:\mathcal{Q}\rightarrow\{1,\ldots,2^{R}\}
and
g:\{1,\ldots,2^{R}\}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Its performance is characterized by the compression rate
R
, the reconstruction distortion
D
, and the perceptual quality
P
, which measures the discrepancy between the distributions of reconstructed and original signals.

Following RDP theory
[
3
]
, the trade-off between distortion and perceptual quality at a fixed rate can be characterized by the distortion-perception (DP) function

\displaystyle D(P)=\inf_{f,g}\;\mathbb{E}\!\left[\|{\bm{x}}-\tilde{{\bm{x}}}\|^{2}\right]\quad\text{s.t.}\quad W_{2}(p_{{\bm{x}}},p_{\tilde{{\bm{x}}}})\leq P,

(1)

where
\tilde{{\bm{x}}}=g(f({\bm{x}}))
,
\|\cdot\|^{2}
denotes the MSE distortion, and
W_{2}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the Wasserstein-2 distance between the distributions of the original and reconstructed signals.

The unconstrained MSE-optimal estimator is the minimum MSE (MMSE) estimator
{\bm{x}}^{*}=\mathbb{E}[{\bm{x}}|\tilde{{\bm{x}}}]
, which attains distortion

D^{*}=\mathbb{E}[\|{\bm{x}}-{\bm{x}}^{*}\|^{2}]
and perception index

P^{*}=W_{2}(p_{{\bm{x}}},p_{{\bm{x}}^{*}})
.
As shown in
[
6
]
,
D(P)
decreases monotonically with
P
until
P=P^{*}
, beyond which it remains constant at
D^{*}
.

Theorem 1

(
[
6
]
)
.

The DP function in (
1
) admits that

D(P)=D^{*}+\bigl[(P^{*}-P)_{+}\bigr]^{2},

where
(x)_{+}=\max(0,x)
.

2.2
Perceptual Compression-based Denoising

Given a lossy compression code
(f,g)
with rate
R
, the decoder induces a codebook

\mathcal{C}=\{{\bf c}_{m}=g(m):m=1,\ldots,2^{R}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
For any
{\bm{x}}\in\mathcal{Q}
, the reconstruction
\tilde{{\bm{x}}}=g(f({\bm{x}}))
satisfies the distortion-perception trade-off

\|{\bm{x}}-\tilde{{\bm{x}}}\|^{2}=D(P)
with
d_{p}(p_{{\bm{x}}},p_{\tilde{{\bm{x}}}})\leq P
.

Building on
[
19
]
, compression-based denoising can be interpreted as a structured maximum-likelihood (ML) estimator.
Given a noisy observation
{\bm{y}}\sim\prod_{i=1}^{n}p(y_{i}|x_{i})
, the denoised estimate is obtained by selecting the most likely codeword,
\hat{\bm{x}}=\arg\min_{{\bf c}_{m}\in\mathcal{C}}\;\mathcal{L}({\bf c}_{m};{\bm{y}})
and
\mathcal{L}({\bf c}_{m};{\bm{y}})=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log p(y_{i}|c_{mi})
,
where
\mathcal{C}
is designed to represent clean signals.
This formulation exploits the fact that clean images admit more compact representations than noisy observations, yielding a structure-aware denoiser applicable across noise models. For additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN),
{\bm{y}}={\bm{x}}+{\bf n}
with
{\bf n}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\sigma^{2}\mathbf{I}_{n})
, the estimator reduces to

\hat{\bm{x}}=\arg\min_{{\bf c}_{m}\in\mathcal{C}}\;\|{\bm{y}}-{\bf c}_{m}\|^{2},

(2)

i.e., projection of the noisy observation onto the nearest codeword in the compression codebook.

2.3
Theoretical Result

We analyze the performance of compression-based ML denoising under additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN).
The following theorem provides a non-asymptotic bound on the reconstruction error in terms of the compression rate, distortion, and perceptual quality.

Theorem 2
.

Let
{\bm{x}}\in\mathcal{Q}
and consider a lossy compression scheme
(f,g)
operating at rate
R
, distortion
D
, and perception level
P
.
Given noisy observations
{\bm{y}}={\bm{x}}+{\bf n}
, where
{\bf n}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\sigma^{2}\mathbf{I}_{n})
, let
\hat{\bm{x}}
denote the output of the compression-based ML denoiser in (
2
).
Then, with probability at least
1-2^{-\eta R+2}
for any
\eta\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\sqrt{D(P)}\leq\|{\bm{x}}-\hat{\bm{x}}\|\leq\sqrt{D(P)}+2\sigma\sqrt{(2\ln 2)R}(1+2\sqrt{\eta}),

Moreover, the error probability of the ML denoiser satisfies

\displaystyle P_{e}\leq\frac{1}{2^{R}}\sum_{m=1}^{2^{R}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v=1\\
v\neq m\end{subarray}}^{2^{R}}Q\!\left(\frac{\frac{\|{\bf c}_{m}-{\bf c}_{v}\|}{2}+\langle{\bm{d}},\frac{{\bf c}_{v}-{\bf c}_{m}}{\|{\bf c}_{v}-{\bf c}_{m}\|}\rangle}{\sigma}\right),

where
{\bm{d}}=\tilde{{\bm{x}}}-{\bm{x}}
and
Q(\cdot)
denotes the Gaussian tail function.

Proof.

The proof is provided in Appendix
A.1
.
∎

Corollary 1
.

Since
\|{\bm{d}}\|=\sqrt{D(P)}
, a worst-case bound is obtained by

\langle{\bm{d}},\frac{{\bf c}_{v}-{\bf c}_{m}}{\|{\bf c}_{v}-{\bf c}_{m}\|}\rangle\geq-\|{\bm{d}}\|
, yielding

\displaystyle P_{e}\leq\frac{1}{2^{R}}\sum_{m=1}^{2^{R}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v=1\\
v\neq m\end{subarray}}^{2^{R}}\!\!Q\!\left(\frac{\frac{\|{\bf c}_{m}-{\bf c}_{v}\|}{2}-\sqrt{D(P)}}{\sigma}\right)\!\!.

3
Generative Compression-based Image Denoising

In this section, we propose learning-based denoisers that prioritize perceptual quality by compressing noisy observations with neural generative compression models, where denoising is performed by reconstructing from a compact latent representation.

3.1
Perceptual Denoising via Conditional WGAN-based Compression

Figure 2
:
Conditional WGAN-based denoising framework.

Our approach builds on high-fidelity generative image compression
[
12
]
and integrates learned lossy compression with conditional adversarial training
[
13
]
, as illustrated in Fig.
2
.
An encoder-decoder pair
(E,G)
maps a noisy input
{\bm{y}}
to a quantized latent representation
{\bm{z}}=E({\bm{y}})
and reconstructs a denoised image
\hat{{\bm{x}}}=G({\bm{z}})
.
The latent
{\bm{z}}
is entropy-coded using a learned probability model
P
, yielding a rate
r({\bm{z}})=-\log P({\bm{z}})
.

To promote perceptual realism, we use a conditional Wasserstein GAN-based discriminator
D
that distinguishes reconstructed images from clean images conditioned on
{\bm{z}}
, following the non-saturating conditional WGAN objective
[
2
,
13
]
.
The reconstruction distortion is defined as

d({\bm{x}},\hat{{\bm{x}}})=k_{M}\,\mathrm{MSE}({\bm{x}},\hat{{\bm{x}}})+k_{P}\,\mathrm{LPIPS}({\bm{x}},\hat{{\bm{x}}}),

where LPIPS captures perceptual discrepancies not well reflected by pixel-wise losses
[
5
]
. The encoder, decoder, and entropy model are jointly optimized by minimizing

\mathcal{L}_{EGP}=\mathbb{E}_{{\bm{y}}}\!\left[\lambda\,r({\bm{z}})+d({\bm{x}},\hat{{\bm{x}}})-\beta\log D(\hat{{\bm{x}}},{\bm{z}})\right],

where
\lambda
controls the rate-distortion trade-off and
\beta
regulates perceptual quality.
The discriminator is trained using

\mathcal{L}_{D}=\mathbb{E}_{{\bm{x}}}\!\left[-\log D({\bm{x}},{\bm{z}})\right]+\mathbb{E}_{{\bm{y}}}\!\left[-\log\!\left(1-D(\hat{{\bm{x}}},{\bm{z}})\right)\right].

This formulation enables explicit control over the RDP trade-off for perceptual image denoising
[
3
]
.

3.2.1
Denoising Diffusion Models

Denoising diffusion models generate data by reversing a gradual noise corruption process
[
9
]
.
Starting from a clean image
{\bm{x}}_{0}={\bm{x}}
, the forward process produces noisy samples
\{{\bm{x}}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{N}
according to

q({\bm{x}}_{n}|{\bm{x}}_{n-1})=\mathcal{N}\!\left({\bm{x}}_{n}|\sqrt{1-\beta_{n}}\,{\bm{x}}_{n-1},\beta_{n}\mathbf{I}\right),

(3)

where
\{\beta_{n}\}
is a variance schedule.
This admits the closed form
{\bm{x}}_{n}=\sqrt{\alpha_{n}}{\bm{x}}_{0}+\sqrt{1-\alpha_{n}}\epsilon
, with
\epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I})
and
\alpha_{n}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-\beta_{i})
.
As
n
increases,
{\bm{x}}_{n}
converges to Gaussian noise. The reverse process is modeled as

p_{\theta}({\bm{x}}_{n-1}|{\bm{x}}_{n})=\mathcal{N}\!\left({\bm{x}}_{n-1}|M_{\theta}({\bm{x}}_{n},n),\beta_{n}\mathbf{I}\right),

(4)

and is trained by predicting the injected noise using the Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM) objective
[
9
]

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DDPM}}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{{\bm{x}}_{0},n,\epsilon}\left\|\epsilon-\epsilon_{\theta}({\bm{x}}_{n},n)\right\|^{2}.

(5)

In inference, denoised samples are obtained by iteratively applying the learned reverse transitions, optionally using deterministic sampling.

3.2.2
Conditional Diffusion for Perceptual Denoising

To enable perceptual denoising, we adopt a conditional diffusion framework inspired by diffusion-based compression
[
17
]
, as illustrated in Fig.
3
.
A noisy observation
{\bm{y}}
is encoded into a compact latent representation
{\bm{z}}
via a stochastic encoder
E({\bm{z}}|{\bm{y}})
.
Conditioned on
{\bm{z}}
, the decoder models
p({\bm{x}}_{0}|{\bm{z}})
through a diffusion process,

p_{\theta}({\bm{x}}_{0:N}|{\bm{z}})=p({\bm{x}}_{N})\prod_{n=1}^{N}\mathcal{N}\!\left({\bm{x}}_{n-1}|M_{\theta}({\bm{x}}_{n},{\bm{z}},n),\beta_{n}\mathbf{I}\right),

where conditioning guides the denoising trajectory toward semantically consistent reconstructions, while intermediate latent variables enable the synthesis of fine-scale details.

Training minimizes a rate-distortion objective

\mathbb{E}_{{\bm{z}}\sim E({\bm{z}}|{\bm{x}}_{0})}\big[-\log p({\bm{x}}_{0}|{\bm{z}})-\lambda\log p({\bm{z}})\big],

where
\lambda
controls the rate constraint.
Since
p({\bm{x}}_{0}|{\bm{z}})
is intractable, we optimize a variational upper bound on the diffusion negative log-likelihood
[
9
]
,

L_{\mathrm{upper}}({\bm{x}}_{0}|{\bm{z}})=\mathbb{E}_{{\bm{x}}_{0},n,\epsilon}\frac{\alpha_{n}}{1-\alpha_{n}}\left\|{\bm{x}}_{0}-\mathcal{X}_{\theta}({\bm{x}}_{n},{\bm{z}},n/N)\right\|^{2}.

To further enhance perceptual quality, we also incorporate an explicit LPIPS loss
[
5
]
.
Let
\bar{{\bm{x}}}_{0}=\mathcal{X}_{\theta}({\bm{x}}_{n},{\bm{z}},n/N)
denote the decoded image, and define the total objective as

L=\rho L_{p}+(1-\rho)L_{c}
, where

L_{p}=\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{LPIPS}(\bar{{\bm{x}}}_{0},{\bm{x}}_{0})];\hskip 0.28436ptL_{c}=L_{\mathrm{upper}}({\bm{x}}_{0}|{\bm{z}})-\tfrac{\lambda}{1-\rho}\log P({\bm{z}}).

This yields a perception-aware diffusion denoiser suitable for real-world image restoration.

Experimental Results

We evaluate the denoising performance of the proposed generative compression-based methods on both synthetic and real-world noise, using natural and microscopy image datasets.
Comparisons are conducted against representative classical, learning-based, and compression-based denoisers.
Baselines include the traditional BM3D
[
4
]
, learning-based methods Noise2Clean (N2C)
[
14
]
, Noise2Noise (N2N)
[
11
]
, and Deep Decoder (DD)
[
8
]
, as well as the recent compression-based approach DeCompress
[
18
]
.

Metric Evaluation.

We report PSNR and SSIM
[
15
]
to evaluate distortion fidelity.
Perceptual quality is assessed using LPIPS, DISTS, FID, and PI, following standard practices in perceptual image quality evaluation
[
5
]
.
LPIPS is computed using an AlexNet-based feature extractor and correlates well with human perceptual judgments, while DISTS measures perceptual similarity by jointly modeling structural and texture information.
FID captures distributional discrepancies in deep feature space, and PI aggregates no-reference quality metrics, with lower values indicating better perceptual realism.

Dataset Description.

All models are trained on the OpenImages dataset

]
.
For evaluation, we consider two standard natural image benchmarks: Kodak, which contains 24 high-quality images at resolution

5
Conclusions

This paper presented a generative compression framework for perception-based image denoising, where restoration is achieved by reconstructing from entropy-coded latent representations.
Two complementary instantiations were introduced: CGanDeCompress, which controls the RDO trade-off via conditional adversarial training, and DiffDeCompress, which performs diffusion-based reconstruction guided by compressed latents to enhance perceptual realism.
Non-asymptotic performance guarantees were also established for compression-based ML denoising under additive Gaussian noise, including bounds on reconstruction error and decoding error probability.
Experiments on synthetic and real-noise benchmarks demonstrate consistent perceptual improvements while maintaining competitive distortion performance, highlighting the robustness of generative compression for perceptual denoising.

\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle\|\hat{{\bm{x}}}-{\bm{y}}\|^{2}\leq\|\tilde{{\bm{x}}}-{\bm{y}}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\|(\hat{{\bm{x}}}-{\bm{x}})-{\bf n}\|^{2}\leq\|(\tilde{{\bm{x}}}-{\bm{x}})-{\bf n}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\|\hat{{\bm{x}}}-{\bm{x}}\|^{2}\leq\|\tilde{{\bm{x}}}-{\bm{x}}\|^{2}-2\langle{\bf n},\tilde{{\bm{x}}}-{\bm{x}}\rangle+2\langle{\bf n},\hat{{\bm{x}}}-{\bm{x}}\rangle

{\bm{y}}

\displaystyle\lVert{\bf e}\rVert^{2}

\sqrt{a+b}\leq\sqrt{a}+\sqrt{b}
for all

The argument follows a standard approach adapted from
[
7
]
. We define a collection of decision regions
(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{2^{R}})
, each corresponding to a subset of the signal space
\R_{+}^{n}
, as

\displaystyle Z_{m}=({\bm{y}}:P({\bf c}_{m}\text{ is optimal}|{\bm{y}})>P({\bf c}_{k}\text{ is optimal}|{\bm{y}})),\forall k\neq m.

By construction, these regions are mutually exclusive. We further assume that there exists no
{\bm{y}}\in\R_{+}^{n}
such that
P({\bf c}_{m}\text{ is optimal}|{\bm{y}})=P({\bf c}_{k}\text{ is optimal}|{\bm{y}})
for any
m\neq k
, ensuring that the codebook space is fully partitioned by the decision regions. Given a noisy observation
{\bm{y}}\in Z_{m}
, the optimal denoiser therefore outputs the estimate
\hat{{\bm{x}}}={\bf c}_{m}
. Under the compression-based ML denoiser, the decision regions can equivalently be written as

\displaystyle Z_{m}=({\bm{y}}:\|{\bm{y}}-{\bf c}_{m}\|<\|{\bm{y}}-{\bf c}_{k}\|),

\displaystyle\forall k\in\{1,...,2^{R}\},\hskip 0.28436ptk\neq m,\hskip 0.28436ptm\in\{1,...,2^{R}\}.

That is, the decoded signal is chosen as the codeword closest to the noisy observation in Euclidean distance.

We now characterize the error probability of the ML denoiser.
Assuming an equally likely codebook, i.e.,
P({\bf c}_{m}\text{ is optimal})=1/2^{R}
, the probability of error is given by

\displaystyle P_{e}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{2^{R}}P({\bm{y}}\notin Z_{m}|{\bf c}_{m}\text{ is optimal})P({\bf c}_{m}\text{ is optimal})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{R}}\sum_{m=1}^{2^{R}}P({\bm{y}}\notin Z_{m}|{\bf c}_{m}\text{ is optimal})

\displaystyle=1-\frac{1}{2^{R}}\sum_{m=1}^{2^{R}}P({\bm{y}}\in Z_{m}|{\bf c}_{m}\text{ is optimal})

\displaystyle=1-\frac{1}{2^{R}}\sum_{m=1}^{2^{R}}\int_{Z_{m}}P({\bm{y}}|{\bf c}_{m}\text{ is optimal})\,d{\bm{y}}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{1}{2^{R}}\sum_{m=1}^{2^{R}}\int_{Z_{m}}P({\bm{y}}={\bm{x}}+{\bf n}|{\bf c}_{m}\text{ is optimal},{\bm{x}})\,d{\bf n}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{1}{2^{R}}\sum_{m=1}^{2^{R}}\int_{Z_{m}}P({\bm{y}}={\bf c}_{m}-{\bm{d}}+{\bf n}|{\bf c}_{m}\text{ is optimal},{\bm{x}})\,d{\bf n}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{1}{2^{R}}\sum_{m=1}^{2^{R}}\int_{Z_{m}-{\bf c}_{m}+{\bm{d}}}p({\bf n})\,d{\bf n}.

(10)

Here,
{\bf c}_{m}=g(f({\bm{x}}))=\arg\min_{{\bf c}_{k}\in\mathcal{C}}\|{\bf c}_{k}-{\bm{x}}\|^{2}
, and
{\bm{d}}={\bf c}_{m}-{\bm{x}}
.

The integrals in (

n

instead of the correct codeword
{\bf c}_{m}

for all

. Applying the union bound, we obtain

\displaystyle P_{e}({\bf c}_{m}\text{ is optimal})

\displaystyle=P\!\left(\bigcup_{v=1,v\neq m}^{2^{R}}A_{mv}\right)\leq\sum_{v=1,v\neq m}^{2^{R}}P(A_{mv}),

where

\displaystyle=P(\|{\bf c}_{v}-{\bm{y}}\|<\|{\bf c}_{m}-{\bm{y}}\||{\bf c}_{m}\text{ is optimal})

\displaystyle=P(\|{\bf c}_{v}-({\bf c}_{m}-{\bm{d}}+{\bf n})\|<\|{\bf c}_{m}-({\bf c}_{m}-{\bm{d}}+{\bf n})\|)

{\bm{x}}

\displaystyle\|{\bf w}+{\bf c}_{m}-{\bf c}_{v}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\|{\bf w}\|^{2}-2\langle{\bf w},{\bf c}_{v}-{\bf c}_{m}\rangle+\|{\bf c}_{m}-{\bf c}_{v}\|^{2}

\displaystyle<\|{\bf w}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\langle{\bf w},{\bf c}_{v}-{\bf c}_{m}\rangle

This probability depends only on the projection of
{\bf w}

\displaystyle P(A_{mv})

\displaystyle=P\left(w>\frac{\|{\bf c}_{m}-{\bf c}_{v}\|}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\|{\bf c}_{m}-{\bf c}_{v}\|/2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi 2\sigma^{2}}}\exp\!\left[-\frac{\left(h+\langle{\bm{d}},\frac{{\bf c}_{v}-{\bf c}_{m}}{\|{\bf c}_{m}-{\bf c}_{v}\|}\rangle\right)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right]dh

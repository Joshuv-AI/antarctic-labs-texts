The number of eigenvalues of discrete Hamiltonian periodic in time
Evgeny, L. Korotyaev

Abstract
. We consider time periodic Hamiltonian on periodic graphs and estimate the number of its quasi-energy eigenvalues on the finite interval.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
34A55, (34B24, 47E05)

1.
Introduction

We consider Schrödinger equations on a periodic graph
{\mathcal{G}}
given
by

\displaystyle{\tfrac{d}{dt}}u(t)=-ih(t)u(t),\hskip 10.00002pth(t)=\Delta+\mathfrak{p}+V(t),

(1.1)

where
h(t)
is the Hamiltonian,
\tau
-periodic in time
t
;
\Delta

is the discrete Laplacian on
\Gamma
and
\mathfrak{p}:{\mathcal{V}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a
bounded electric potential. We assume that the potential
V(t)
on

{\mathcal{G}}
is
\tau
-periodic in time and is decaying on
{\mathcal{G}}
.

There are a lot of applications of different periodic media, e.g.,
nanomedia, in physics, chemistry and engineering, see, e.g.,

[
37
]
, two authors of this survey, Novoselov and Geim, won the
Nobel Prize for discovering graphene. In order to study properties
of such media one uses their approximations by periodic graphs. In
these cases we need to investigate various properties of
Schrödinger operators on graphs, and, in particular, periodic in
time. One of the interesting problems is Schrödinger operators
with decaying potentials, periodic in time, on periodic graphs. As
far as the author knows, there are no results about eigenvalues for
Schrödinger operators with decaying potentials, periodic in time,
on periodic graphs.

1.1.
Schrödinger operators on periodic graphs

Let
{\mathcal{G}}=({\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{E}})
be a connected infinite graph, possibly having
loops and multiple edges and embedded into the space
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Here

{\mathcal{V}}
is the set of its vertices and
{\mathcal{E}}
is the set of its
unoriented edges.
An edge starting at a vertex
x
and ending at a vertex
y
from

{\mathcal{V}}
will be denoted as the ordered pair
(x,y)\in{\mathcal{E}}
and is said
to be
incident
to the vertices.
Vertices
x,y\in{\mathcal{V}}
will be called
adjacent
and denoted by

x\sim y
, if
(x,y)\in{\mathcal{E}}
. We define the degree
\varkappa_{x}
of the
vertex
x\in{\mathcal{V}}
as the number of all edges from
{\mathcal{E}}
, starting at

x
. Let
\Gamma
be a lattice of rank
d
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with a basis

\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}\}
, i.e.,

\Gamma=\Big\{a:a=\sum_{s=1}^{d}n_{s}a_{s},\;(n_{s})_{s=1}^{d}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\Big\}.

We introduce the equivalence relation on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
:

{\bf x}\equiv{\bf y}\;(\hskip-11.38109pt\mod\Gamma)\hskip 10.00002pt\Leftrightarrow\hskip 10.00002pt{\bf x}-{\bf y}\in\Gamma\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\,{\bf x},{\bf y}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

We consider
locally finite
\Gamma
-periodic graphs

{\mathcal{G}}
, i.e.,
graphs satisfying the following conditions:

\bullet

{\mathcal{G}}={\mathcal{G}}+a
for any
a\in\Gamma
and the quotient graph
{\mathcal{G}}_{*}={\mathcal{G}}/\Gamma
is finite.

The basis
a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}
of the lattice
\Gamma
is called the
periods
of
{\mathcal{G}}
. We also call the quotient graph
{\mathcal{G}}_{*}={\mathcal{G}}/\Gamma

the
fundamental graph
of the periodic graph
{\mathcal{G}}
. The
fundamental graph
{\mathcal{G}}_{*}
is a graph on the
d
-dimensional torus

{\mathbb{R}}^{d}/\Gamma
. The graph
{\mathcal{G}}_{*}=({\mathcal{V}}_{*},{\mathcal{E}}_{*})
has the vertex set

{\mathcal{V}}_{*}={\mathcal{V}}/\Gamma
.
Introduce the space
\ell^{p}({\mathcal{V}}),p\geqslant 1
of sequences

f=(f_{x})_{x\in{\mathcal{V}}}
equipped with the norm given by

\displaystyle\|f\|_{p}=\|f\|_{\ell^{p}({\mathcal{V}})}=\big(\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{V}}}|f_{x}|^{p}\big)^{1\over p},\hskip 10.00002pt\ p\in[1,\infty),

and let
\|f\|_{\infty}=\|f\|_{\ell^{\infty}({\mathcal{V}})}=\sup_{x\in{\mathcal{V}}}|f_{x}|

and
\|f\|=\|f\|_{2}
for shortness.

We consider the Schrödinger operator
h_{o}
acting on the Hilbert
space
\ell^{2}({\mathcal{V}})
and given by

h_{o}=\Delta+\mathfrak{p},

(1.2)

where the potential
\mathfrak{p}
is real valued, bounded and satisfies for
all
(x,a)\in{\mathcal{V}}\times\Gamma
:

(\mathfrak{p}f)_{x}=\mathfrak{p}_{x}f_{x},\hskip 20.00003pt\mathfrak{p}_{x+a}=\mathfrak{p}_{x},

(1.3)

and
\Delta
is the
combinatorial Laplacian
on
f\in\ell^{2}({\mathcal{V}})

defined by

(\Delta f)_{x}=\sum_{y\sim x}\big(f_{x}-f_{y}\big),\hskip 20.00003ptx\in{\mathcal{V}}.

(1.4)

The sum in (
1.4
) is taken over all edges starting at the vertex

x
. It is known that
\Delta
is self-adjoint, the point 0 belongs to
its spectrum
\sigma(\Delta)
containing in
[0,s_{+}]
for some
s_{+}\leqslant 2\varkappa_{+}
, i.e.,

0\in\sigma(\Delta)\subset[0,s_{+}]\subset[0,2\varkappa_{+}],\hskip 20.00003pt\textrm{where}\hskip 20.00003pt\varkappa_{+}=\max_{x\in{\mathcal{V}}}\varkappa_{x}<\infty,

(1.5)

see, e.g.,
[
32
]
and
\varkappa_{x}
is the degree of the vertex
x
,
and
s_{+}
is the top of the spectrum
\sigma(h_{o})
.

We briefly describe the spectrum of the Schrödinger operator

h_{o}
(for more details see, e.g.,
[
12
]
or
[
KS14
]
). Let

\#M
denote the number of elements in a set
M
. The operator
h_{o}

on
\ell^{2}(V)
has the standard decomposition into a constant fiber
direct integral by

\ell^{2}(V)={1\over(2\pi)^{d}}\int^{\oplus}_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\ell^{2}(V_{\ast})\,d\vartheta,\hskip 20.00003ptUh_{o}U^{-1}={1\over(2\pi)^{d}}\int^{\oplus}_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}h_{o}(\vartheta)d\vartheta,

(1.6)

{\mathbb{T}}^{d}={\mathbb{R}}^{d}/(2\pi{\mathbb{Z}})^{d}
, for some unitary operator
U
. Here

\ell^{2}(V_{\ast})={\mathbb{C}}^{\nu}
is the fiber space,
\nu=\#V_{*}
and

h_{o}(\vartheta)
is the fiber operator acting on
\ell^{2}(V_{\ast})
. Each
fiber operator
h_{o}(k)
,
k\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
, acts on the space

\ell^{2}({\mathcal{V}}_{*})={\mathbb{C}}^{\nu}
,
\nu=\#{\mathcal{V}}_{*}
, and has
\nu
real eigenvalues

\mathfrak{b}_{j}(\vartheta)
,
j=1,\ldots,\nu
, labeled in non-decreasing order by

\mathfrak{b}_{1}(\vartheta)\leqslant\mathfrak{b}_{2}(\vartheta)\leqslant\ldots\leqslant\mathfrak{b}_{\nu}(\vartheta),\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\,\vartheta\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d},

(1.7)

counting multiplicities. Each
\mathfrak{b}_{j}(\cdot)
is a real and
piecewise analytic function on the torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
and creates the

spectral band

\sigma_{j}(H)
by

\sigma_{j}(h_{o})=[\mathfrak{b}_{j}^{-},\mathfrak{b}_{j}^{+}]=\mathfrak{b}_{j}({\mathbb{T}}^{d}),\hskip 20.00003ptj\in{\mathbb{N}}_{\nu},\hskip 20.00003pt{\mathbb{N}}_{\nu}=\{1,\ldots,\nu\}.

(1.8)

Some of
\mathfrak{b}_{j}(\cdot)
may be constant, i.e.,

\mathfrak{b}_{j}(\cdot)=\mathfrak{b}_{j}^{o}=\mathop{\mathrm{const}}\nolimits
, on some subset
{\mathcal{B}}
of
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
of
positive Lebesgue measure. In this case the Schrödinger operator

h_{o}
on
{\mathcal{G}}
has the eigenvalue
\mathfrak{b}_{j}^{o}
of infinite
multiplicity. We call
\{\mathfrak{b}_{j}^{o}\}
a
flat band
. Thus, the
spectrum of the Schrödinger operator
h_{o}
on the periodic graph

{\mathcal{G}}
has the form

\sigma(h_{o})=\bigcup_{k\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\sigma\big(h_{o}(k)\big)=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\nu}\sigma_{j}(h_{o})=\sigma_{ac}(h_{o})\cup\sigma_{fb}(h_{o}),

(1.9)

where
\sigma_{ac}(h_{o})
is the absolutely continuous spectrum, which is
a union of non-degenerate bands from (
1.8
), and
\sigma_{fb}(h_{o})

is the set of all flat bands (eigenvalues of infinite multiplicity).

Definition.

The top
s_{+}
of the spectrum
\sigma(h_{o})\subset[0,s_{+}]
(i.e., the right point of the spectrum) is said to be
regular if the set
\{\vartheta\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d}:\mathfrak{b}_{\nu}(\vartheta)=s_{+}\}
is finite
number of points
\vartheta_{j},j=1,...,n_{R}
and each point satisfies

\mathfrak{b}_{\nu}(\vartheta)=s_{+}-(\mathfrak{B}_{j}a,a)+O(|a|^{3}),\hskip 20.00003pt\mathop{\mathrm{as}}\nolimits\ a=\vartheta-\vartheta_{j}\to 0,\hskip 10.00002ptj=1,..,n_{R},

for some positive matrix
\mathfrak{B}_{j}>0
.

The regularity of the left boundary of the spectrum is defined
similarly. Korotyaev and Saburova (see Theorem 1.2 in
[
30
]
),
show that the lower bound of the spectrum
\sigma(h_{o})
of the operator

h_{o}
is regular.

We recall results of Korotyaev and Sloushch
[
31
]
about
estimates for the discrete spectrum of Schrödinger operators on
discrete periodic graphs. In order to formulate these results we
need some definitions. A graph is called
bipartite
if its
vertex set is divided into two disjoint sets (called
parts
of
the graph) such that each edge connects vertices from distinct sets
(see p.105 in
[
39
]
). Examples of bipartite graphs are the
cubic lattice and the hexagonal lattice. The face-centered cubic
lattice is non-bipartite. Due to
[
30
]
the top
s_{+}
of the
spectrum
\sigma(h_{o})\subset[0,s_{+}]
(i.e., the right point of the
spectrum) is regular for a bipartite graph. In general, it is not
true.

We have the decomposition for
z\in{\mathbb{R}}
:

z=(z)_{+}-(z)_{-},\hskip 20.00003pt(z)_{\pm}=(|z|\pm z)/2.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
h=h_{o}+\mathfrak{q}
on a periodic graph, where the operator

h_{o}=\Delta+\mathfrak{p}
and the potential
\mathfrak{p}
is
\Gamma-
periodic. Assume that

\mathfrak{q}\in\ell^{d\over 2}({\mathcal{V}}),d\geqslant 3
. Then the number
\mathfrak{n}_{-}(\mathfrak{q},\mu)

of eigenvalues of
h
(counted according to their multiplicity)
below
\mu<0
is finite and satisfies

\mathfrak{n}_{-}(\mathfrak{q},\mu)\leqslant C_{ks}^{-}\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{V}}}(\mathfrak{q}_{x}-\mu)_{-}^{d\over 2},\hskip 10.00002pt\mu<0.

(1.10)

Let in addition the top
s_{+}
of the spectrum
\sigma(h_{o})\subset[0,s_{+}]

is regular (or the graph is bipartite). Then the number

\mathfrak{n}_{+}(\mathfrak{q},\mu)
of eigenvalues of
h
(counted according to their
multiplicity) above
\mu>s_{+}
is finite and satisfies

\mathfrak{n}_{+}(\mathfrak{q},\mu)\leqslant C_{ks}^{+}\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{V}}}(\mathfrak{q}_{x}-\mu)_{+}^{d\over 2},\hskip 10.00002pt\mu>s_{+}.

(1.11)

Here the constants
C_{ks}^{\pm}
depend on the graph
{\mathcal{G}}
and the
potential
\mathfrak{p}
only.

Remark.
In the case
d=1
in Section 4 the discrete analogue
of Bargmann’s bound from
[
15
]
will be used. I do not know
similar estimates for
d=2
.

Below we need the Rellich type Theorem
1.2
. It is
generalization of results of Isozaki and Maioka
[

]
and
Vesalainen
[

.

Define a half space
{\mathbb{Y}}_{j}(z)
and a rectangular cuboid (or simply a
cuboid)
{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{m})
by

\displaystyle{\mathbb{Y}}_{j}(z)=\{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}:x_{j}\geqslant z\},\hskip 20.00003ptz\in{\mathbb{N}},\ \ j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{d},

(1.12)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{m})=\{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}:0\leqslant x_{j}\leqslant\mathfrak{m}_{j}\},\hskip 20.00003pt\mathfrak{m}=(\mathfrak{m}_{j})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\lambda\in(0,2d)
and
u:Z^{d}\to{\mathbb{C}}
satisfy

\sum_{|x|<r}|u_{x}|^{2}=o(r)
as
r\to\infty
. Let
(\Delta-\lambda)u=f
,
where
e^{t|x|}f_{x}\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
for all
t>0
.

i) If
f\big|_{{\mathbb{Y}}_{d}(z)}=0
for some
z\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, then we have

u\big|_{{\mathbb{Y}}_{d}(z)}=0
.

ii) If
E={\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\setminus{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{m})
for some
\mathfrak{m}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}
and

f\big|_{E}=0
, then we have
u\big|_{E}=0
.

1.2.
Main results

Consider the Schrödinger equation
{\tfrac{d}{dt}}y(t)=-ih(t)y(t)
on the graph
{\mathcal{V}}
, where

h(t)=h_{o}+V(t)
is the Hamiltonian,
\tau
-periodic in time
t
and

h_{o}=\Delta+\mathfrak{p}
is the operator defined by (
1.2
).
Here the potential
V_{x}(t),(t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathcal{V}}
is real

\tau
-periodic in time
and satisfies

\sup_{t\in{\mathbb{T}}_{\tau}}|V(t)|\leqslant u,\hskip 20.00003pt\mathop{\mathrm{where}}\nolimits\hskip 10.00002ptu\in\ell^{d}({\mathcal{V}}).

(1.13)

In Section 1, we assume that
d\geqslant 3
. The case
d=1
is discussed
in Section 4.

Let
\partial=-i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
be the self-adjoint operator in

L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}_{\tau})
. We also denote
\partial=-i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
the
corresponding operator in
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}=L^{2}({\mathbb{T}},\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}))
with the
natural domain
{\mathscr{D}}={\mathscr{D}}(\partial)
. We use the notation
\langle A(t)\rangle

to indicate multiplication by
A(t)
on the space

{\mathcal{H}}=\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
. Introduce the
quasienergy
operators
\widetilde{h}_{o}
and
\widetilde{h}
on
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
by

\widetilde{h}_{o}=\partial+h_{o},\hskip 20.00003pt\widetilde{h}=\widetilde{h}_{o}+V,\hskip 10.00002ptV=\langle V_{x}(t)\rangle.

The spectrum of

\widetilde{h}_{o}
has the form

\sigma(\widetilde{h}_{o})=\sigma_{ac}(\widetilde{h}_{o})=\bigcup_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sigma_{n},\hskip 20.00003pt\sigma_{n}=\sigma(h_{o}+\omega n).

(1.14)

Note that if
\omega>s_{+}
, then the spectrum of
\widetilde{h}_{o}
has the band
structure with the sets
\sigma_{n}
separated by gaps.
The spectrum of
\widetilde{h}
has the form

\sigma(\widetilde{h})=\sigma_{ac}(\widetilde{h})\cup\sigma_{pp}(\widetilde{h}),\hskip 20.00003pt\sigma_{ac}(\widetilde{h})=\sigma(\widetilde{h}_{o}).

Introduce the resolvent
R(\lambda)=(\widetilde{h}-\lambda)^{-1},\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}_{\pm}
.
We have the identity

\langle e^{it\omega}\rangle R(\lambda)\langle e^{-it\omega}\rangle=R(\lambda+\omega),\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}_{\pm}.

(1.15)

It means that the spectrum of
\widetilde{h}
(and
\widetilde{h}_{o}
) is

\omega
–periodic. Thus it is sufficient to study eigenvalues of
\widetilde{h}
on the set
(-\omega,0]
. The operator
\widetilde{h}
has
N\leqslant\infty

\{\lambda_{j},j=1,....,N\}
on the interval
(-\omega,0]
. Here
and below each eigenvalue is counted according to its multiplicity.

\bullet
Let
\mathfrak{n}(q,s)
be the number of eigenvalues of
h_{o}-q
on the
set
(-\infty,s),s\leqslant 0
counted according to their multiplicity, where

q
is a potential.

\bullet
Let
\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}(A)
be the number of eigenvalues of
\widetilde{h}
on
the segment
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}
, counted according to its multiplicity.

Firstly, we consider the case
\omega>s_{+}
, when there is the main gap

\gamma\subset(-\omega,0]

\gamma=(-s_{\gamma},0)\neq\emptyset,\hskip 10.00002pt\mathop{\mathrm{where}}\nolimits\hskip 10.00002pts_{\gamma}=\omega-s_{+}>0.

(1.16)

We estimate the number
\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}(\gamma)

\widetilde{h}
on the

\gamma
.

.

Let
\widetilde{h}=\widetilde{h}_{o}+V

1.3.
The imbedding eigenvalues

We consider the imbedding eigenvalues for unperturbed operators

h_{o}=\Delta
on the lattice
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},d\geqslant 1
, where
\sigma(\Delta)=[0,s_{+}],s_{+}=4d
. There are two cases:

If
\omega>s_{+}
, then there exist the main gap

\gamma=(s_{b},0)
and the main band
\sigma_{b}=(-\omega,-s_{b})
.

If
\omega\leqslant s_{+}
, then we have
\sigma(\widetilde{h}_{o})={\mathbb{R}}
.

The main spectral interval has the form
(-\omega,0)=\sigma_{e}\cup\sigma_{b}\cup\{s_{e}\}
, where

\mathfrak{r}=[s_{+}/\omega]\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+},\hskip 20.00003pts_{e}=\mathfrak{r}\omega-s_{+},\hskip 10.00002pt\sigma_{b}=(-\omega,-s_{e}),\hskip 10.00002pt\sigma_{e}=(-s_{e},0).

(1.27)

If
\mathfrak{r}=0
, then there are gaps in the spectrum of
\widetilde{h}_{o}
and

\sigma_{e}=(-s_{e},0)=\gamma
is the main gap.

If
\mathfrak{r}\in{\mathbb{N}}
, then there are no gaps in the spectrum of
\widetilde{h}_{o}
and
\sigma_{b}=(-\omega,0),\sigma_{e}=\emptyset
.

We discuss the number of eigenvalues imbedding on the main spectral
interval
(-\omega,0)
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\widetilde{h}=\widetilde{h}_{o}+V
on
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}_{\tau},\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}))
. Assume that each

V(t),t\in{\mathbb{T}}_{\tau}
is finitely supported, satisfies (
1.17
), (
1.19
)
and for some cuboid
{\mathbb{Q}}_{d}(\mathfrak{m}),\ \mathfrak{m}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}
:

\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits V_{x}(t)\subset{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{m})\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\ t\in{\mathbb{T}}_{\tau},

(1.28)

Let

q_{\alpha,\pm}=\mathfrak{c}_{\alpha}u^{2}+v_{\pm},\alpha\in\{b,e\}
, where

\mathfrak{c}_{e}={16\omega(\mathfrak{r}+1)\over s_{e}^{2}}
and
\mathfrak{c}_{b}={4\omega(\mathfrak{r}+2)\over s_{e}^{2}}
and

\mathfrak{r}=[s_{+}/\omega]\geqslant 0
.

i) Let
\mathfrak{r}\geqslant 0
. Then the operator
\widetilde{h}
has
\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}(\sigma_{b})

\sigma_{b}=(-\omega,-s_{e})
such that

\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}(\sigma_{b})\leqslant C_{b}\biggr(\mathfrak{r}+2+2{\|q_{b,\pm}\|_{\infty}\over\omega}\biggr)C_{ks}^{\pm}\|q_{b,\pm}\|_{p}^{p},

(1.29)

where
C_{b}=1+{4\omega(\omega-s_{e})(\mathfrak{r}+2)\over s_{e}^{2}}
.

ii) Let
\mathfrak{r}\geqslant 1
. Then the operator
\widetilde{h}
has
\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}(\sigma_{e})

eigenvalues on
\sigma_{e}=(-s_{e},0)
such that

\widetilde{\mathfrak{n}}(\sigma_{e})\leqslant C^{+}\|q_{e,+}\|_{p}^{p}+C^{-}\|q_{e,-}\|_{p}^{p},

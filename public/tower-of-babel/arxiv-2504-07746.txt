Upper semi-continuous properties for 𝒞 1 , α \mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha} diffeomorphisms
Chiyi Luo, Dawei Yang

Abstract
We establish a uniform approximation of metric entropy by partition entropy using uniform partitions for 𝒞 1 , α {\cal C}^{1,\alpha} three-dimensional diffeomorphisms. This gives several consequences for diffeomorphisms on a compact manifold M M with dim ​ M ≤ 3 {\rm dim}M\leq 3 . First, if an invariant measure μ \mu is a continuity point of the sum of its positive Lyapunov exponents, then μ \mu is an upper semi-continuity point of the entropy map. Second, it provides a slight improvement of the necessary condition for strong positive recurrence of surface and three-dimensional diffeomorphisms. Third, it yields continuity of dimensions for measures of maximal entropy.



1
Introduction

The space of invariant measures may be very complicated for chaotic systems.
The metric entropy of an invariant measure, which was first studied by Kolmogorov and Sinai, is a fundamental concept in ergodic theory.
Denote by
h_{\mu}(f)
the metric entropy of
\mu
for a map
f
.
The dependence of this quantity with respect to the invariant measures and the maps received people’s great interest.
Because it is closely related to the existence of equilibrium states and measures of maximal entropy, which serve as fundamental objects for describing the statistical and dynamical behavior of the system.

In general, metric entropy is not continuous with respect to the measures.
Even for very nice system, it will not be lower-semi continuous with respect to the measures.
For example, in a hyperbolic basic set with positive entropy, the measure of maximal entropy can be approximated by measures supported on periodic orbits.
This leads to a failure of lower semi-continuity.

The upper semi-continuity of metric entropy holds in the uniformly hyperbolic setting, ensuring the existence of measures of maximal entropy.
Another well-known class of systems with upper semi-continuous metric entropy is
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
diffeomorphisms.
Inspired by Yomdin’s work
[
23
]
on Shub’s entropy conjecture, Newhouse
[
18
]
proved for
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
differentiable maps, the upper semi-continuity of metric entropy holds with respect to invariant measures.
However, for
\mathcal{C}^{r}
diffeomorphisms on a compact manifold with finite positive
r
, the upper semi-continuity of the metric entropy may fail, see, for instance, the counterexample in
[
7
,
17
]
.

Let
f
be a diffeomorphism of a compact manifold
M
, and let
\{\mathcal{P}_{m}\}_{m\geq 1}
be a sequence of finite partitions such that
\operatorname{Diam}({\cal P}_{m})\to 0
.
Then, by the Kolmogorov–Sinai theorem, for every
f
-invariant measure
\mu
, we have
h_{\mu}(f,{\cal P}_{m})\to h_{\mu}(f)
.
Note that for a finite partition
{\cal P}
, the map
\mu\mapsto h_{\mu}(f,{\cal P})
is upper semi-continuous at every measure
\mu
satisfying
\mu(\partial{\cal P})=0
.
Therefore, it is useful to obtain a uniform estimate for
\sup_{\mu}\big(h_{\mu}(f)-h_{\mu}(f,{\cal P})\big)
.
For instance, for expansive diffeomorphisms, there exists an
\varepsilon>0
such that for every partition
{\cal P}
with
\operatorname{Diam}({\cal P})<\varepsilon
, one has
h_{\mu}(f)=h_{\mu}(f,{\cal P})
.
Then, the upper semi-continuity of the entropy map follows immediately.

Assume that
f:M\to M
is a
{\cal C}^{r,\alpha}
diffeomorphism of a compact manifold
M
.
A deep result of Newhouse
[
18
]
, based on Yomdin’s estimate
[
23
]
, shows that there exists
C_{r}>0
such that for every
q\in{\mathbb{N}}
, there is
\varepsilon_{q}>0
such that

h_{\mu}(f)\leq h_{\mu}(f,\mathcal{Q})+\frac{\operatorname{dim}M\cdot\log\|Df^{q}\|_{0}}{q(r+\alpha)}+\frac{\log(C_{r})}{q},

for every
{\cal Q}
with
\operatorname{Diam}({\cal Q})<\varepsilon_{q}
, where
\|Df^{q}\|_{0}:=\sup_{x\in M}\|D_{x}f^{q}\|
.
In particular, when
f:M\to M
is
C^{\infty}
, this implies the upper semi-continuity of the entropy map
\mu\to h_{\mu}(f)
.
However, in the
C^{r}
setting, Newhouse’s estimate only provides an upper bound for the possible failure of upper semi-continuity of the entropy map.

In this paper, we consider a
{\cal C}^{1,\alpha}
diffeomorphism
f:M\to M
with
\alpha\in(0,1]
.
We obtain a new upper bound in the case
\operatorname{dim}M\leq 3
and demonstrate several applications of this estimate.

1.1
Statement of the main estimates

Suppose that
M
is a compact Riemannian manifold without boundary and let
f:M\to M
be a diffeomorphism.
By the Oseledets theorem
[
19
]
, there exists an invariant set
\Gamma\subset M
with total probability,
i.e.,
\mu(\Gamma)=1
for any invariant measure
\mu
, such that for any
x\in\Gamma
, there are

•

\lambda_{1}(x,f)>\cdots>\lambda_{s(x)}(x,f)
, which are
f
-invariant and measurable functions of
x
;

•

a
Df
-invariant measurable splitting
T_{x}M=E_{1}(x,f)\oplus E_{2}(x,f)\oplus\cdots\oplus E_{s(x)}(x,f)

such that for any
v\in E_{i}(x)\setminus\{0\}
with
1\leq i\leq s(x)
, one has

\lim_{n\to\pm\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\|D_{x}f^{n}(v)\|=\lambda_{i}(x,f).

These numbers
\{\lambda_{i}(x,f)\}_{i=1}^{s(x)}
are called the
Lyapunov exponents
of
x\in\Gamma
.

Given
x\in\Gamma
, we denote

\max\big\{0,~\lambda_{1}(x,f)\big\}=\lambda^{+}(x,f),~~\min\big\{0,~\lambda_{s(x)}(x,f)\big\}=\lambda^{-}(x,f).

For each
f
-invariant measure
\mu
, define
\lambda^{\pm}(\mu,f)=\int\lambda^{\pm}(x,f)~{\rm d}\mu(x)
.
The sum of positive Lyapunov exponents is an important quantity used to describe the complexity of the dynamics.
We define it as

\lambda_{\Sigma}^{+}(x,f):=\sum_{i=1}^{s(x)}\operatorname{dim}E_{i}(x)\cdot\max\{0,\lambda_{i}(x,f)\};

For an invariant measure
\nu
of
f
, define the
the sum of positive Lyapunov exponents
of
\nu
by

\lambda_{\Sigma}^{+}(\nu,f)=\int\lambda_{\Sigma}^{+}(x,f){\rm d}\nu(x)
.
Note that
\lambda^{+}_{\Sigma}(\mu,f)
is upper semi-continuous with respect to
\mu
and
f
.
A detailed proof can be found in Proposition
3.1
.

We say that an invariant measure
\mu
of
f
has exactly one positive Lyapunov exponent, if
\mu
-almost every
x
satisfies
\lambda_{1}(x,f)>0\geq\lambda_{2}(x,f)
and
\operatorname{dim}E_{1}(x,f)=1
; and
\mu
has exactly one negative Lyapunov exponent, if
\mu
-almost every
x
satisfies
\lambda_{s(x)-1}(x,f)\geq 0>\lambda_{s(x)}(x,f)
and
\operatorname{dim}E_{s(x)}(x,f)=1
.
The main theorem is stated as follows:

Theorem A
.

Given
\alpha\in(0,1]
, there exists a constant
C_{\alpha}
with the following property.
For each
\Upsilon>0
and
q\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
\varepsilon_{q}=\varepsilon_{\Upsilon,q}>0
such that

•

for every
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
diffeomorphism
f:~M\to M
satisfying
\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}}\leq\Upsilon
;

•

for every ergodic measure
\mu
of
f
with exactly one positive Lyapunov exponent;

•

for every finite partition
\mathcal{Q}
with
{\rm Diam}(\mathcal{Q})<\varepsilon_{q}
;

one has

h_{\mu}(f)\leq h_{\mu}(f,\mathcal{Q})+\frac{1}{\alpha}\big[\frac{1}{q}\int\log\|D_{x}f^{q}\|{\rm d}\mu(x)-\lambda^{+}(\mu,f)+\frac{1}{q}\big]+\frac{\log(2q\Upsilon C_{\alpha})}{q}.

In the case
\operatorname{dim}M\leq 3
, we obtain a uniform estimate for every invariant measure
\mu
.
For
1\leq k\leq\operatorname{dim}M
, let
\wedge^{k}D_{x}f
denote the induced action of
D_{x}f
on the
k
-th exterior power.

Theorem B
.

Assume that
\operatorname{dim}M\leq 3
.
Given
\alpha\in(0,1]
, there exists a constant
~C_{\alpha}
with the following property.
For each
\Upsilon>0
and
q\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
\varepsilon_{q}=\varepsilon_{\Upsilon,q}>0
such that

•

for every
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
diffeomorphism
f:~M\to M
satisfying
\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}}\leq\Upsilon
;

•

for every invariant measure
\mu
of
f
and every finite partition
\mathcal{Q}
with
{\rm Diam}(\mathcal{Q})<\varepsilon_{q}
;

one has

h_{\mu}(f)\leq h_{\mu}(f,{\cal Q})+\frac{1}{\alpha}\big(\frac{1}{q}\int\max_{k}~\log^{+}\|\wedge^{k}D_{x}f^{q}\|~{\rm d}\mu(x)-\lambda^{+}_{\Sigma}(\mu,f)+\frac{1}{q}\big)+\frac{\log(2q\Upsilon C_{\alpha})}{q}.

The proof of Theorem
A
is based on Burguet’s reparametrization lemma
[
1
]
.
In
[
1
]
, Burguet extended the Yomdin-Gromov theory
[
12
,
23
]
to one-dimensional curves and established a remarkable upper bound
[
1
, Main Proposition]
for the “measure-theoretic tail entropy” in terms of entropy structures.
As an application, he obtained results on symbolic extensions. Burguet’s estimate is formulated for sequences of invariant measures sufficiently close to a given measure. In contrast, our result concerns a single invariant measure and provides a direct estimate relating its metric entropy and partition entropy.
This leads to several applications.
For instance, see Theorem
C
and Remark
1.2
, it can be used to study perturbations of diffeomorphisms.

The advantage of Burguet’s reparametrization lemma is the number of the reparametrizations has better estimates in some sense.
As a consequence, it has several powerful applications in the study of
C^{r}
diffeomorphisms; see, for example,
[
3
,
4
]
.
However, obtaining a higher-dimensional version of this result is currently difficult.
Therefore, we can only assume that
\mu
has exactly one positive Lyapunov exponent.
We expect that such a result holds in arbitrary dimensions.

.

]
that if a diffeomorphism

1.2
Upper semi-continuity of metric entropy and applications

From the classical work by Ledrappier-Young
[
14
]
, it is known that the metric entropy of an ergodic measure depends on the disintegration of the measure along unstable manifolds.
This establishes a fundamental connection between entropy and Lyapunov exponents.
A recent remarkable result by Buzzi-Crovisier-Sarig
[
8
]
states that for
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
surface diffeomorphisms, the continuity of entropy implies the continuity of Lyapunov exponents.
By Theorem
B
, we get a opposite direction of
[
8
]
: if we have the continuity of Lyapunov exponents of an invariant measure, then we have the upper semi-continuity for the entropy map.

Theorem C
.

Assume that
\operatorname{dim}M\leq 3
.
Given any
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
diffeomorphism
f
and an invariant measure
\mu
of
f
,
for any sequence of
~\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
diffeomorphisms
\{f_{n}\}
and any sequence of probability measures
\{\mu_{n}\}
such that
f_{n}\to f
in the
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
topology;
\mu_{n}
is an invariant measure of
f_{n}
for every
n
and
\mu_{n}\to\mu
is the weak star topology.
Then, we have

\limsup_{n\to\infty}h_{\mu_{n}}(f_{n})-h_{\mu}(f)\leq\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\alpha}\big(\lambda_{\Sigma}^{+}(\mu,f)-\lambda_{\Sigma}^{+}(\mu_{n},f_{n})\big).

In particular, if
~\lambda_{\Sigma}^{+}(\mu_{n},f_{n})\to\lambda_{\Sigma}^{+}(\mu,f)
, then
\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}h_{\mu_{n}}(f_{n})\leq h_{\mu}(f)
.

Remark 1.1
.

The theorem is false for
\mathcal{C}^{1}
diffeomorphisms. Downarowicz-Newhouse
[
11
, Section 5]
provide a counterexample: they construct a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
surface diffeomorphism
f
that has a hyperbolic fixed point
p
and a sequence of ergodic measures
\{\mu_{n}\}
such that
\mu_{n}\to\delta_{p}
(the Dirac measure at
p
),
\lambda^{+}_{\Sigma}(\mu_{n})\to\lambda^{+}_{\Sigma}(p)
, but
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}h_{\mu_{n}}(f)=\lambda^{+}_{\Sigma}(p)>0=h_{\delta_{p}}(f)
.

Remark 1.2

(Relationship with Theorem 1 of
[
5
]
)
.

Thanks to Burguet’s kind reminder, we realized that the case
f_{n}\equiv f
in Theorem
C
was already implicitly contained in the paper of Burguet and Liao
[
5
]
, and that the case of
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
interval maps was proved in
[
2
]
.

We first explain how, in the case
f_{n}\equiv f
, Theorem
C
is related to
[
5
, Theorem 1]
.
The discussion below is based on personal communications with Burguet. Let
\mu_{n}
be a sequence of
f
-invariant measures converging to
\mu
, and let
\{{\cal P}_{k}\}
be a sequence of finite partitions such that
\operatorname{Diam}({\cal P}_{k})\to 0
and
\mu(\partial{\cal P}_{k})=0
for every
k
.
Then, under the additional assumption that
\mu_{n}
is ergodic for every
n\geq 1
,
[
5
, Theorem 1]
gives directly

\lim_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}h_{\mu_{n}}(f)-h_{\mu_{n}}(f,{\cal P}_{k})=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}h_{\mu_{n}}(f)-h_{\mu}(f)\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{\Sigma}^{+}(\mu,f)-\lambda_{\Sigma}^{+}(\mu_{n},f).

Indeed, one may identify the entropy structure
\{h_{k}\}
in
[
5
]
with the sequence of functions
h_{k}(\nu):=h_{\nu}(f,{\cal P}_{k})
.
Moreover, the superenvelope function
E(\nu)
is given by
E(\nu)=\alpha^{-1}\lambda_{\Sigma}^{+}(\nu,f)
.

Note that
[
5
, Theorem 1]
is stated under the assumption that the measures
\mu_{n}
close to
\mu
are ergodic.
Thus, as stated, it applies directly only to the ergodic case above.
We believe that
[
5
, Theorem 1]
suffices to remove the ergodicity assumption on
\mu_{n}
, but this requires some discussion.
Indeed, although an invariant measure admits an ergodic decomposition, the fact that an invariant measure
\nu
is close to
\mu
does not imply that most of its ergodic components are close to
\mu
.

Theorem
C
provides a perturbative version of this result and does not require the measures
\mu_{n}
to be ergodic.
Its proof is based on Theorem
B
and avoids the use of notions such as superenvelopes, entropy structures, fake center foliations, and tail entropy.
We also provide some applications to the strong positive recurrence (SPR) property and dimension theory. Finally, we believe that the methods developed here may also be applicable to flows generated by four-dimensional
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
vector fields, possibly even in the presence of singularities.

1.2.1
Strongly positive recurrence for diffeomorphisms

More recently, Buzzi-Crovisier-Sarig
[
9
]
introduced an important notion of the strongly positive recurrence (SPR) property for diffeomorphisms.
They proved that SPR diffeomorphisms exhibit exponential mixing and other important statistical properties.
The continuity of Lyapunov exponents is an important property that plays a central role in the study of SPR properties for diffeomorphisms, as shown in
[
9
]
.
The continuity of Lyapunov exponent can be obtained in some natural settings, for instance, the continuity of metric entropy for
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
surface diffeomorphisms as in
[
8
]
.
Our theorem provides some new progress in the study of SPR properties for diffeomorphisms.

Corollary D
.

Assume that
f
is a
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
surface diffeomorphism with
h_{\rm top}(f)>0
.
Then,
f
is SPR if and only if for any sequence of ergodic measures
\{\mu_{n}\}
with
\mu_{n}\rightarrow\mu
and
h_{\mu_{n}}(f)\rightarrow h_{\rm top}(f)
, one has
\lambda^{+}(\mu_{n},f)\rightarrow\lambda^{+}(\mu,f)
.

The result can be viewed as a slight improvement of
[
9
, Theorem B]
, since the assumptions on the Lyapunov exponents of
\mu
are no longer required.
This is because, once the convergence of the Lyapunov exponents is known, Theorem
C
implies that
\mu
is a measure of maximal entropy.
Consequently,
\mu
is
h_{\mathrm{top}}(f)/2
-hyperbolic.

Corollary E
.

Let
f:M\rightarrow M
be a
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
diffeomorphism on a three-dimensional manifold with positive topological entropy.
Assume
f
has the following properties:

•

for every sequence of ergodic measures
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
such that
\mu_{n}
converging to a measure
\mu
with
h_{\mu_{n}}(f)\rightarrow h_{\rm top}(f)
, the measure
\mu
is hyperbolic and
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{\Sigma}^{+}(\mu_{n},f)=\lambda_{\Sigma}^{+}(\mu,f)
.

Then, we have

(1)

f
admits a measure of maximal entropy;

(2)

there exists
\chi>0
such that for every ergodic measure of maximal entropy, all its Lyapunov exponents lie outside the interval
[-\chi,\chi]
.

Moreover, for each sequence of ergodic measures
\{\mu_{n}\}
with
\mu_{n}\rightarrow\mu
and
h_{\mu_{n}}(f)\rightarrow h_{\rm top}(f)
, there exists
i:=i(\mu)\in\{1,2\}
such that
\lambda_{i}(x,f)>\chi>-\chi>\lambda_{i+1}(x,f)
for
\mu
-almost every
x
.

The detailed proof of Corollary
E
will be provided in Section
5
.

Remark 1.3
.

Based on Corollary
E
, we can improve a bit of
[
9
, Theorem 3.1]
in the following case:
Assume that
f
is a
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
diffeomorphism of
M
(
\operatorname{dim}M=3
)
with positive topological entropy, then
f
is SPR if and only if for any sequence of ergodic measures
\{\mu_{n}\}
with
\mu_{n}\rightarrow\mu
and
h_{\mu_{n}}(f)\rightarrow h_{\rm top}(f)
, one has
\mu
is hyperbolic and
\lambda^{+}_{\Sigma}(\mu_{n},f)\rightarrow\lambda^{+}_{\Sigma}(\mu,f)
.

1.2.2
Continuity of the dimension of measures

Another interesting result is about the Hausdorff dimension of probability measures on closed surface.
Let
M
be a
2
-dimensional compact Riemannian manifold without boundary and let
\mu
be a probability measure on
M
.
The Hausdorff dimension of
\mu
is defined by

\operatorname{dim}_{H}(\mu):=\inf\left\{\operatorname{dim}_{H}(Z):Z\subset M~\text{with}~\mu(Z)=1\right\}.

(1)

We have the upper semi-continuity of the Hausdorff dimension under the following conditions.

Corollary F
.

Let
M
be a closed surface,
\{\mu_{n}\}
be a sequence of probability measures and
\{f_{n}\}
be a sequence of
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
diffeomorphisms such that

•

\mu_{n}
is an ergodic measure of
f_{n}
for every
n>0
;

•

f_{n}\rightarrow f
in the
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
topology;

•

\mu_{n}
converges to an
f
-ergodic measure
\mu
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}\limits\lambda^{+}(\mu_{n},f_{n})=\lambda^{+}(\mu,f)>0
.

Then, we have
\limsup\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\operatorname{dim}_{H}(\mu_{n})\leq\operatorname{dim}_{H}(\mu)
.

The proof of Corollary
F
follows from Theorem
C
and the formula for the Hausdorff dimension of ergodic measures in
[
24
]
: for every
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
surface diffeomorphism
g
and every ergodic hyperbolic measure
\nu
of
g
, one has

\operatorname{dim}_{H}(\nu)=h_{\nu}(g)\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{+}(\nu,g)}-\frac{1}{\lambda^{-}(\nu,g)}\right).

Therefore, under the assumptions of Corollary
F
we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\operatorname{dim}_{H}(\mu_{n})

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}h_{\nu_{n}}(f_{n})\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{+}(\mu_{n},f_{n})}-\frac{1}{\lambda^{-}(\mu_{n},f_{n})}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}h_{\nu_{n}}(f_{n})\cdot\left(\big(\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\lambda^{+}(\mu_{n},f_{n})\big)^{-1}-\big(\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\lambda^{-}(\mu_{n},f_{n})\big)^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq h_{\mu}(f)\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{+}(\mu,f)}-\frac{1}{\lambda^{-}(\mu,f)}\right)=\operatorname{dim}_{H}(\mu).

This completes the proof of Corollary
F
.

Corollary G
.

Let
f
be a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
surface diffeomorphism with
h_{\rm top}(f)>0
.
Then, for every sequence of ergodic measures
\{\mu_{n}\}
with
\mu_{n}\rightarrow\mu
and

h_{\mu_{n}}(f)\rightarrow h_{\rm top}(f)
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, we have that
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\operatorname{dim}_{H}(\mu_{n})=\operatorname{dim}_{H}(\mu)
.

We now explain how this result is obtained.
In the setting of Corollary
G
, by
[
10
, Theorem B]
the limit measure
\mu
is an ergodic measure of maximal entropy.
Moreover, by
[
4
]
we have
\lambda^{+}(\mu_{n},f)\rightarrow\lambda^{+}(\mu,f)
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
Therefore, Corollary
G
follows.

Remark 1.4
.

By the main Theorem of
[
4
]
and
[
10
, Theorem B]
.
This result remains valid in the
\mathcal{C}^{r,\alpha}
setting, provided that
h_{\rm top}(f)>(r+\alpha)^{-1}\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\log\min\{\|Df^{n}\|_{0},~\|Df^{-n}\|_{0}\}
.

2
Entropy estimate for ergodic measures with finite partitions

In this section, we give the proofs of Theorems
A
.
For the main theorems in this paper, we will only consider low regularity, i.e., the
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
case.
However, Theorem
A
may have general interest when the map is
\mathcal{C}^{r,\alpha}
for some large
r>1
.
We begin by stating a result under stronger differentiability assumptions (the notations will be introduced in the next section).

Theorem 2.1
.

Given
r\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha\in[0,1]
satisfying
r+\alpha>1
, there exists a constant
C_{r,\alpha}
with the following property.
For each
\Upsilon>0
and
q\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{\Upsilon,q}>0
such that

•

for every
\mathcal{C}^{r,\alpha}
diffeomorphism
f:~M\to M
satisfying
\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{r,\alpha}}\leq\Upsilon
;

•

for every ergodic measure
\mu
of
f
with exactly one positive Lyapunov exponent;

•

for every finite partition
\mathcal{Q}
with
{\rm Diam}(\mathcal{Q})<\varepsilon
;

one has

h_{\mu}(f)\leq h_{\mu}(f,\mathcal{Q})+\frac{1}{r-1+\alpha}\big[\frac{1}{q}\int\log\|D_{x}f^{q}\|{\rm d}\mu-\lambda^{+}(\mu,f)+\frac{1}{q}\big]+\frac{\log(2q\Upsilon\cdot C_{r,\alpha})}{q}.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that Theorem
A
follows from Theorem
2.1
.
Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\mu(\partial Q)=0
.
Indeed, for any finite partition
\mathcal{Q}
with
{\rm Diam}(\mathcal{Q})<\varepsilon
and every

satisfying

such that

.
Consequently, if Theorem

holds for every partition

with

with

.

Recall that
\lambda^{-}(\mu,f)=-\lambda^{+}(\mu,f^{-1})
.
By considering the
\mathcal{C}^{r,\alpha}
diffeomorphism

Theorem 2.3
.

Given
r\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha\in[0,1]
satisfying

, there exists a constant

with the following property.
For each
\Upsilon>0
and

such that

•

for every

Remark 2.4
.

Tail entropy, or local entropy, which introduced by Buzzi
[
6
]
and Newhouse
[
18
]
, is used to estimate the degree to which entropy fails to be upper semi-continuous.
For every three-dimensional diffeomorphism, any ergodic measure has either exactly one positive Lyapunov exponent or exactly one negative Lyapunov exponent.
Then, in a sense, Theorem
2.1
and Theorem
2.3
provide new bounds on local entropy with respect to measures.

In the remainder of this section, we will provide a detailed proof of Theorem
2.1
.
The proof of Theorem
2.3
can be proved similarly by considering the
\mathcal{C}^{r,\alpha}
diffeomorphism
f^{-1}
.
By Remark

, we can assume that

2.1
Preliminaries

Assume that
M
is a compact boundaryless Riemannian manifold.
For a
\mathcal{C}^{r,\alpha}
diffeomorphism
f:M\to M
with
r\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha\in[0,1]
, we mean

•

if
\alpha=0
, it is just the usual
\mathcal{C}^{r}
diffeomorphism with
r\in\mathbb{N}
;

•

if
\alpha=1
, it is a
\mathcal{C}^{r}
diffeomorphism, and its
\mathcal{C}^{r}
derivative
D^{r}f
is a Lipschitz map;

•

if
\alpha\in(0,1)
, it is a
\mathcal{C}^{r}
diffeomorphism, and its
\mathcal{C}^{r}
derivative
D^{r}f
is a
\alpha
-Hölder map.

Assume that
X
is a compact metric space and
Y
is a Banach space.
For
\alpha\in(0,1]
and a
\alpha
-Hölder continuous map
H:X\to Y
, define

\|H\|_{0}=\sup_{x\in X}\|H(x)\|,~~\|H\|_{\alpha}=\sup\left\{\dfrac{d(H(x),H(y))}{d(x,y)^{\alpha}}:x\neq y,~x,y\in X\right\}.

(2)

For
\mathcal{C}^{r,\alpha}
diffeomorphism
f:~M\to M
, define

\|f\|_{\mathcal{C}^{r,\alpha}}:=\max\Big\{\max_{1\leq j\leq r}\|D^{j}f\|_{0},~\max_{1\leq j\leq r}\|D^{j}f^{-1}\|_{0},~\|D^{r}f\|_{\alpha},~\|D^{r}f^{-1}\|_{\alpha}\Big\}.

We recall some fundamental definition and properties of entropies.
Let
\mu
be a probability measure.
Given a finite partition
\mathcal{P}
, define the static entropy function

H_{\mu}(\mathcal{P}):=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}}-\mu(P)\log\mu(P).

By definition, one has

H_{\mu}(\mathcal{P})\leq\log\#\{P\in\mathcal{P}:~\mu(P)>0\}.

(3)

Given two finite partitions
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{Q}=\{Q_{1},\cdots,Q_{k}\}
, define the conditional entropy

H_{\mu}(\mathcal{P}\big|\mathcal{Q}):=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mu(Q_{j})H_{\mu_{j}}(\mathcal{P}),

where
\mu_{j}(\cdot)=\frac{\mu(Q_{j}\cap\cdot)}{\mu(Q_{j})}
denotes the normalization of
\mu
restricted on
Q_{j}
.

For an
f
-invariant measure
\mu
and a finite partition
\mathcal{P}
, denote by

\mathcal{P}^{n}:=\mathcal{P}^{n,f}=\bigvee_{j=0}^{n-1}f^{-j}(\mathcal{P}).

The metric entropy of
\mu
associated to a partition
\mathcal{P}
is defined as

h_{\mu}(f,\mathcal{P}):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}H_{\mu}(\mathcal{P}^{n});

and the metric entropy of
\mu
is defined as

h_{\mu}(f):=\sup\{h_{\mu}(f,\mathcal{P}):\mathcal{P}~\textrm{is a finite partition}\}.

Note that
h_{\mu}(f)=h_{\mu}(f^{-1})
and
h_{\mu}(f,\mathcal{P})=h_{\mu}(f^{-1},\mathcal{P})
for every finite partition
\mathcal{P}
(see
[
22
, Theorem 4.13]
).

2.2

We recall Burguet’s reparametrization lemma
[
3
]
for

A
\mathcal{C}^{r,\alpha}

•

if

For a curve

.

(

]

Given

with the following property.
Given
\Omega>0
, there exists
\varepsilon_{\Omega}>0
such that if

Choose a set

\mathcal{P}

Fix an ergodic measure
\mu
and fix the partition
\mathcal{Q}

Since

has exactly one positive Lyapunov exponent, it follows from

By the Rokhlin disintegration theorem

The following proposition is a consequence of Ledrappier-Young’s result

Proposition 2.6
.

For every
\tau>0
and every
\delta\in(0,1)
, there exists
K\subset M
with
\mu(K)>1-\delta
and
\rho:=\rho_{K}>0
, such that for every

, every measurable set
\Sigma\subset W^{u}_{{\rm loc}}(x)

, and every finite partition

h_{\mu}(f)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}H_{\mu_{\xi(x),K,\Sigma}}({\cal P}^{n})+\tau,

(6)

where
\mu_{\xi(x),K,\Sigma}(\cdot):=\frac{\mu_{\xi(x)}(K\cap\Sigma\cap\cdot)}{\mu_{\xi(x)}(K\cap\Sigma)}
.

Given an auxiliary constant
\tau>0
. We choose a compact set
K
with the following properties:

•

\mu(K)>\frac{1}{2}
and
K
satisfies the conclusion of Proposition
2.6

•

the following convergences hold uniformly for

where

denotes the Dirac measure at

and

is the one-dimensional measurable bundle associated to the positive Lyapunov exponent;

•

for every

, the following convergence holds uniformly for

\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\log\|D_{f^{qj+c}(x)}f^{q}\|:=\phi_{c}(x),

where

is an

-invariant measurable function and for every

\sum_{c=0}^{q-1}\phi_{c}(x)=\int\log\|D_{x}f^{q}\|{\rm d}\mu
.

Choose a point

By Proposition

, there exists a finite partition
\mathcal{P}

h_{\mu}(f)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}H_{\mu_{\sigma}}(\mathcal{P}^{n})+\tau.

Using the properties of conditional entropy, one has that

H_{\mu_{\sigma}}(\mathcal{P}^{n})\leq H_{\mu_{\sigma}}(\mathcal{Q}^{n})+H_{\mu_{\sigma}}(\mathcal{P}^{n}\big|\mathcal{Q}^{n}),

Therefore, we obtain

h_{\mu}(f)\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}H_{\mu_{\sigma}}(\mathcal{Q}^{n})+\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}H_{\mu_{\sigma}}(\mathcal{P}^{n}\big|\mathcal{Q}^{n})+\tau.

2.5
Estimate for

Given an integer
N\in\mathbb{N}
, by the concave property of the function
H
(see
[

\frac{1}{n}H_{\mu_{\sigma}}(\mathcal{Q}^{n})\leq\frac{1}{N}H_{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f_{*}^{i}\mu_{\sigma}}(\mathcal{Q}^{N})+\frac{2N}{n}\log\#\mathcal{Q}.

By the definition of
\mu_{\sigma}
, one has

\mu_{\sigma}=\frac{1}{\mu_{\xi(x_{0})}(K\cap\sigma_{*})}\int_{K\cap\sigma_{*}}\delta_{x}~{\rm d}\mu_{\xi(x_{0})}(x),

By the choice of
K
, (see (
7

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f_{*}^{i}\mu_{\sigma}=\mu.

Since
\mu(\partial\mathcal{Q}^{N})=0

(which can be deduced from

), one has

\lim_{n\to\infty}H_{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f_{*}^{i}\mu_{\sigma}}(\mathcal{Q}^{N})=H_{\mu}(\mathcal{Q}^{N}).

Thus, for every
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
we have

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}H_{\mu_{\sigma}}(\mathcal{Q}^{n})\leq\frac{1}{N}H_{\mu}(\mathcal{Q}^{N}).

(9)

Consequently, we have

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}H_{\mu_{\sigma}}(\mathcal{Q}^{n})\leq h_{\mu}(f,\mathcal{Q}).

Proposition 2.7
.

For every

and every

(3)

Proof.

By the choice of
K
, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{q-1}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\log\|D_{f^{jq+k}(x)}f^{q}\|=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\log\|D_{f^{j}(x)}f^{q}\|=\int\log\|D_{x}f^{q}\|{\rm d}\mu(x).

Hence, for every
x\in K
there exists

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\log\|D_{f^{jq+c(x)}(x)}f^{q}\|\leq\frac{1}{q}\int\log\|D_{x}f^{q}\|{\rm d}\mu(x),

(11)

where

.
We decompose

•

.

Theorem 2.9
.

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}H_{\mu_{\sigma}}(\mathcal{P}^{n}\big|\mathcal{Q}^{n})\leq\frac{1}{r-1+\alpha}\big(\frac{1}{q}\int\log\|Df^{q}\|{\rm d}\mu-\lambda^{+}(\mu,f)+\frac{1}{q}\big)+\frac{\log(2q\Upsilon C_{r,\alpha})}{q}.

Proof.

By the definition of the conditional entropy

H_{\mu_{\sigma}}(\mathcal{P}^{n}\big|\mathcal{Q}^{n})=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}^{n}:~\mu_{\sigma}(Q)>0}\mu_{\sigma}(Q_{n})\cdot H_{\mu_{\sigma,Q_{n}}}(\mathcal{P}^{n}),

where
\mu_{\sigma,Q_{n}}=\frac{\mu_{\sigma}(Q_{n}\cap\cdot)}{\mu_{\sigma}(Q_{n})}
is the normalization of
\mu_{\sigma}
on
Q_{n}
.

By (
3
), one has that

\displaystyle H_{\mu_{\sigma}}(\mathcal{P}^{n}|\mathcal{Q}^{n})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{Q_{n}\in\mathcal{Q}^{n},~\mu_{\sigma}(Q_{n})>0}\mu_{\sigma}(Q_{n})\cdot\log\#\{P_{n}\in\mathcal{P}^{n}:~P_{n}\cap\sigma_{*}\cap K\cap Q_{n}\neq\emptyset\}.

By Corollary
2.8
, for each
n>0
and each

, there is a reparametrization family
\Gamma(Q_{n})
such that

1.

\sigma_{*}\cap K\cap Q_{n}\subset\bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{n}}\sigma\circ\gamma([-1,1])
;

2.

\|Df^{j}\circ\sigma\circ\gamma\|_{0}\leq 1
for every
0\leq j\leq n-1
;

3.

\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{n}\sup\limits_{Q_{n}\in\mathcal{Q}^{n}}\log\#\Gamma(Q_{n})\leq\frac{1}{r-1+\alpha}\big(\frac{1}{q}\int\log\|Df^{q}\|{\rm d}\mu-\lambda^{+}(\mu,f)+\frac{1}{q}\big)+\frac{\log(2q\Upsilon C_{r,\alpha})}{q}
.

Since we have

\#\{P_{n}\in\mathcal{P}^{n}:~P_{n}\cap\sigma_{*}\cap K\cap Q_{n}\neq\emptyset\}\leq\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma(Q_{n})}\#\{P_{n}\in\mathcal{P}^{n}:~P_{n}\cap K\cap(\sigma\circ\gamma)_{*}\neq\emptyset\},

it suffices to estimate

for each
\gamma\in\Gamma_{n}
.
Recall that we assume

Therefore, we have

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}H_{\mu_{\sigma}}(\mathcal{P}^{n}|\mathcal{Q}^{n})\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sup_{Q_{n}\in\mathcal{Q}^{n}}\log\#\Gamma(Q_{n}).

2.9

Proposition 3.1

In any dimension, if

Proof.

Assume that
\operatorname{dim}M=d
.
We give a formula for
\lambda_{\Sigma}^{+}(\mu,f)

\lambda_{\Sigma}^{+}(\mu,f):=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int\max_{1\leq k\leq d}\log^{+}\|\wedge^{k}D_{x}f^{n}\|{\rm d}\mu(x).

It is clear that

Claim
.

\{\varphi_{n}\}

Given

\displaystyle\varphi_{n+m}(x)

By Kingman’s sub-additive ergodic theorem, one has that

\lambda_{\Sigma}^{+}(\mu,f):=\inf_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n}\int\max_{1\leq k}~\log^{+}\|\wedge^{k}D_{x}f^{n}\|{\rm d}\mu(x).

(13)

For each

, the map

Proof of Corollary
E

We now assume that
M
is a three-dimensional compact manifold and

is a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
diffeomorphism.
For

\lambda^{+}(x,f)\geq\lambda^{c}(x,f)\geq\lambda^{-}(x,f)

are well-defined on a set with full measure for all invariant measures, where

\displaystyle\lambda^{+}(x,f):=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log^{+}\|D_{x}f^{n}\|=\max\{0,\lambda_{1}(x,f)\}

\displaystyle\lambda^{-}(x,f):=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log^{+}\|D_{x}f^{-n}\|=-\lambda^{+}(x,f^{-1}).

\lambda^{c}(x,f):=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log{\rm Jac}(D_{x}f^{n})-\lambda^{+}(x,f)-\lambda^{-}(x,f)=-\lambda^{c}(x,f^{-1}).

For an invariant measure
\mu

\lambda^{+}(\mu,f)=\int\lambda^{+}(x,f){\rm d}\mu(x),~~\lambda^{c}(\mu,f)=\int\lambda^{c}(x,f){\rm d}\mu(x),~~\lambda^{-}(\mu,f)=\int\lambda^{-}(x,f){\rm d}\mu(x).

Note that
\mu\mapsto\lambda^{+}(\mu,f)
is upper semi-continuous and
\mu\mapsto\lambda^{-}(\mu,f)
is lower semi-continuous.
We denote
\lambda_{\Sigma}^{-}(\mu,f):=-\lambda_{\Sigma}^{+}(\mu,f^{-1})
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\{\mu_{n}\}
be a sequence of ergodic measures satisfying
\mu_{n}\rightarrow\mu
and
\lambda^{+}_{\Sigma}(\mu_{n},f)\rightarrow\lambda^{+}_{\Sigma}(\mu,f)
. Then, we have the following

•

if
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\lambda^{+}(\mu_{n},f)=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\lambda^{+}_{\Sigma}(\mu_{n},f)
, then
\lambda^{c}(x,f)\leq 0
for
\mu
-almost every
x
;

•

if
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\lambda^{-}(\mu_{n},f)=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\lambda^{-}_{\Sigma}(\mu_{n},f)
, then
\lambda^{c}(x,f)\geq 0
for
\mu
-almost every
x
.

Proof.

For the first statement, note that

\lambda^{+}_{\Sigma}(\mu,f)=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\lambda^{+}_{\Sigma}(\mu_{n},f)=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\lambda^{+}(\mu_{n},f)\leq\lambda^{+}(\mu,f)\leq\lambda^{+}_{\Sigma}(\mu,f).

Then, we have
\lambda^{+}(\mu,f)=\lambda^{+}_{\Sigma}(\mu,f)
, which means that

\int\lambda^{+}(x,f){\rm d}\mu(x)=\int\lambda^{+}(x,f)+\max\{\lambda^{c}(x,f),0\}{\rm d}\mu(x).

This implies that

\int\max\{\lambda^{c}(x,f),0\}{\rm d}\mu(x)=0,

which in turn yields
\lambda^{c}(x,f)\leq 0
for
\mu
-almost every
x
.
For the second statement, since

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\lambda^{+}_{\Sigma}(\mu_{n},f)+\lambda^{-}_{\Sigma}(\mu_{n},f)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int\log{\rm Jac}(D_{x}f)~{\rm d}\mu_{n}(x)=\lambda^{+}_{\Sigma}(\mu,f)+\lambda^{-}_{\Sigma}(\mu,f),

.
Applying the first part to

gives
\lambda^{c}(x,f)\geq 0
for
\mu
-almost every
x
. This completes the proof.

A.1.1
Higher Order Leibniz Rule

Consider two
\mathcal{C}^{r}

(14)

For

\partial^{\alpha}H(x)=\sum_{\beta:|\beta|=|\alpha|}\frac{\alpha!}{\beta!}\cdot\partial^{\beta}F(G(x))\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\gamma^{i}|=|\alpha|\\

\gamma^{i}=(\gamma^{i}_{1},\cdots,\gamma^{i}_{n})

•

\alpha!=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}!

\beta!
and
\gamma^{i}!

with
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{n}

.
We summarize the key estimate we need from Faà di Bruno’s formula.

Lemma A.1

, there is a constant
C_{B,r,\alpha}>0
such that for any
\mathcal{C}^{r,\alpha}
function
u:\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{p}
, for any function
v:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
satisfying

\max_{i=1,\cdots,r}\|D^{j}v\|_{0}\leq 1,~~~\|D^{r}v\|_{\alpha}\leq 1

then one has that

\max_{1\leq s\leq r}\|D^{s}(u\circ v)\|_{0}\leq C_{B,r,\alpha}\max_{1\leq s\leq r}\|D^{s}u\|_{0}~~{\rm and}~~\|D^{r}(u\circ v)\|_{\alpha}\leq C_{B,r,\alpha}\max\{\max_{1\leq s\leq r}\|D^{s}u\|_{0},\|D^{r}u\|_{\alpha}\}.

A.1.3
The Kolmogorov-Landau’s inequality

We have the following
\mathcal{C}^{r,\alpha}
version from
[
1
, Lemma 6]
.

Lemma A.2
.

Given
r\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
, there is a constant
C_{K,r,\alpha}>0
such that for any
\mathcal{C}^{r,\alpha}
function
\varphi
, one has that

\forall k=0,1,\cdots,r,~~~~~~~\|D^{k}\varphi\|_{0}\leq C_{K,r,\alpha}(\|\varphi\|_{0}+\|D^{r}\varphi\|_{\alpha}).

A.1.4
Taylor’s expansion

When one considers a
\mathcal{C}^{r,\alpha}
map
\varphi:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
, where
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an open concave set, we use the following Taylor expansion at
x\in X

\varphi(x+a)=\sum_{k=0}^{r}\frac{1}{k!}[D^{k}_{x}\varphi](a)^{k}+R_{r}(x,a),

where
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
x+a\in X
,
(a)^{k}=(a,\cdots,a)\in(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
and

R_{r}(x,a)=\frac{1}{(r-1)!}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{r-1}\big([D^{r}_{(x+ta)}\varphi-D^{r}_{x}\varphi](a)^{r}\big){\rm d}t.

Using the Hölder condition, one has that

\|R_{r}(x,a)\|\leq\frac{1}{r!}\cdot\|D^{r}\varphi\|_{\alpha}\cdot\|a\|^{\alpha+r}.

A.2
Construction of the reparametrizations

Consider
\Omega>0
as in the statement of Lemma
2.5
.
There is
\varepsilon_{\Omega}>0
such that for every
\mathcal{C}^{r,\alpha}
diffeomorphism
g
satisfying

\|g\|_{\mathcal{C}^{r,\alpha}}:=\max\{\max_{k=1,\cdots,r}\|D^{k}g\|_{0},~\|D^{r}g\|_{\alpha}\}<\Omega

and every
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{\Omega})
, one has that

\forall x\in M,~~~\|D^{s}g^{x}_{2\varepsilon}\|_{0}\leq 3\varepsilon\|D_{x}g\|,~~s=1,\cdots,r,~~~\textrm{and}~\|D^{r}g^{x}_{2\varepsilon}\|_{\alpha}\leq 3\varepsilon\|D_{x}g\|.

where

g^{x}_{2\varepsilon}:=g\circ\exp_{x}(2\varepsilon\cdot):~T_{x}M(1)\to M.

For a
\mathcal{C}^{r,\alpha}
strongly
\varepsilon
-bounded curve
\sigma:[-1,1]\to M
and
x\in\sigma_{\ast}:=\sigma([-1,1])
, define

1
1

1

In a local chart,
g^{x}_{2\varepsilon}
has the following form:
g^{x}_{2\varepsilon}(v)=g(x+2\varepsilon\cdot v)
,
\sigma^{x}_{2\varepsilon}
has the following presentation:
\sigma^{x}_{2\varepsilon}(t)=\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}(\sigma(t)-x);

and
g\circ\sigma(t)=g(x+2\varepsilon\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}(\sigma(t)-x))=g^{x}_{2\varepsilon}\circ\sigma_{2\varepsilon}^{x}(t).

\sigma^{x}_{2\varepsilon}:=\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\exp_{x}^{-1}\circ\sigma:[-1,1]\rightarrow T_{x}M(1).

Let
\sigma:[-1,1]\rightarrow M
be a
\mathcal{C}^{r,\alpha}
-strongly
\varepsilon
bounded curve.
Note that
g\circ\sigma
may not be bounded anymore.
To make it to be bounded, one has to compose some reparametrization.
For an affine reparametrization
\gamma:[-1,1]\rightarrow[-1,1]
with contraction
b
such that

\exists t\in[-1,1]~\text{such that}~\sigma(\gamma(t))=y\in M~\text{and}~\lceil\log\|D_{y}g\|\rceil=\chi,

denoting
\Psi(z)=D_{z}g_{2\varepsilon}^{y}
which is a matrix in a local chart, by Equation (
14
), one has that

\displaystyle\left\|D^{r}(g\circ\sigma\circ\gamma)\right\|_{\alpha}\leq~

\displaystyle b^{r+\alpha}\left\|D^{r}\left(g_{2\varepsilon}^{y}\circ\sigma_{2\varepsilon}^{y}\right)\right\|_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq~

\displaystyle b^{r+\alpha}\left\|D^{r-1}\left(\Psi(\sigma_{2\varepsilon}^{y}(t))\circ D\sigma_{2\varepsilon}^{y}(t)\right)\right\|_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq~

\displaystyle C_{L,r,\alpha}\cdot b^{r+\alpha}\cdot\|\Psi\circ\sigma_{2\varepsilon}^{y}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{r-1,\alpha}}\cdot\|D\sigma_{2\varepsilon}^{y}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{r-1,\alpha}},

where

\|\Psi\circ\sigma_{2\varepsilon}^{y}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{r-1,\alpha}}:=\max\{\max_{k=0,\cdots,r-1}\|D^{k}(\Psi\circ\sigma_{2\varepsilon}^{y})\|_{0},~\|D^{r-1}(\Psi\circ\sigma_{2\varepsilon}^{y})\|_{\alpha}\};

and

\|D\sigma_{2\varepsilon}^{y}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{r-1,\alpha}}:=\max\{\max_{k=0,\cdots,r-1}\|D^{k}(D\sigma_{2\varepsilon}^{y})\|_{0},~\|D^{r-1}(D\sigma_{2\varepsilon}^{y})\|_{\alpha}\}.

By the fact that
\sigma
is a
\mathcal{C}^{r,\alpha}
curve and strongly
\varepsilon
-bounded, one has that

\forall 1\leq k\leq r,~~\|D^{k}\sigma_{2\varepsilon}^{y}\|_{0}\leq\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\|D^{k}\sigma\|_{0}\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\|D\sigma\|_{0}\leq 1,~~\|D^{r}\sigma_{2\varepsilon}^{y}\|_{\alpha}\leq\|D^{r}\sigma\|_{\alpha}\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\|D\sigma\|_{0}\leq 1.

Thus, by Lemma
A.1
,
there exists
C_{B,r,\alpha}>0
such that

\max_{k=0,1,\cdots,r-1}\|D^{k}(\Psi\circ\sigma_{2\varepsilon}^{y})\|_{0}\leq C_{B,r,\alpha}\cdot\max_{k=1,\cdots,r}\|D^{k}g_{2\varepsilon}^{y}\|_{0}\leq 3C_{B,r,\alpha}\varepsilon\cdot\|D_{y}g\|.

and

\|D^{r-1}(\Psi\circ\sigma_{2\varepsilon}^{y})\|_{\alpha}\leq 3C_{B,r,\alpha}\cdot\varepsilon\cdot\|D_{y}g\|.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\|D^{r}(g\circ\sigma\circ\gamma)\|_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq 3C_{B,r,\alpha}\cdot b^{r+\alpha}\|D_{y}g\|\cdot\|D\sigma\|_{0}

\displaystyle\leq 3C_{B,r,\alpha}\cdot b^{r-1+\alpha}{\rm e}^{\chi^{+}}\|D(\sigma\circ\gamma)\|_{0}

\displaystyle\leq{\rm e}^{\chi-10}\|\cdot D(\sigma\circ\gamma)\|_{0}

where we take
b=(3C_{B,r,\alpha}{\rm e}^{\chi^{+}-\chi+10})^{\frac{-1}{r-1+\alpha}}
.
Thus, if we want to cover the interval, we need at most
b^{-1}+1
such affine maps.

We use the Hölder form of Taylor’s expansion as in Subsection
A.1.4
. For
D(g\circ\sigma\circ\gamma)
, we consider the Taylor polynomial
P
at
0
of degree
r-1
, we have the estimate:

\|P-D(g\circ\sigma\circ\gamma)\|_{0}\leq{\rm e}^{\chi-10}\|D(\sigma\circ\gamma)\|_{0}

For convenience, we define

I(\chi,\chi^{+}):=\{t\in[-1,1]:\lceil\log\|D_{\sigma(t)}g\|\rceil=\chi^{+},~\lceil\log\|D_{\sigma(t)}g|_{T_{\sigma(t)}\sigma_{*}}\|\rceil=\chi\},

For every
s\in I(\chi,\chi^{+})
, we have
{\rm e}^{\chi-1}\|D(\sigma\circ\gamma)(s)\|<\|D(g\circ\sigma\circ\gamma)(s)\|\leq{\rm e}^{\chi}\|D(\sigma\circ\gamma)(s)\|
.
Since
\gamma
is affine and
\sigma
is strongly
\varepsilon
-bounded, it follows that
{\rm e}^{-1}\|D(\sigma\circ\gamma)\|_{0}\leq\|D(\sigma\circ\gamma)(s)\|\leq\|D(\sigma\circ\gamma)\|_{0}
.
Thus, one has

{\rm e}^{\chi-2}\|D(\sigma\circ\gamma)\|_{0}<\|D(g\circ\sigma\circ\gamma)(s)\|\leq{\rm e}^{\chi}\|D(\sigma\circ\gamma)\|_{0}

Therefore, we have that

\displaystyle\|P(s)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|D(g\circ\sigma\circ\gamma)(s)\|+{\rm e}^{\chi-10}\|D(\sigma\circ\gamma)\|_{0}

\displaystyle\leq{\rm e}^{\chi}\|D(\sigma\circ\gamma)\|_{0}+{\rm e}^{\chi-10}\|D(\sigma\circ\gamma)\|_{0}

\displaystyle\leq{\rm e}^{\chi}\|D(\sigma\circ\gamma)\|_{0}(1+{\rm e}^{-10})

\displaystyle\leq{\rm e}^{3}{\rm e}^{\chi}\|D(\sigma\circ\gamma)\|_{0}

and

\displaystyle\|P(s)\|

\displaystyle\geq\|D(g\circ\sigma\circ\gamma)(s)\|-{\rm e}^{\chi-10}\|D(\sigma\circ\gamma)\|_{0}

\displaystyle\geq{\rm e}^{\chi-2}\|D(\sigma\circ\gamma)\|_{0}-{\rm e}^{\chi-10}\|D(\sigma\circ\gamma)\|_{0}

\displaystyle\geq{\rm e}^{\chi-2}\|D(\sigma\circ\gamma)\|_{0}(1-{\rm e}^{-8})

\displaystyle\geq{\rm e}^{-3}{\rm e}^{\chi}\|D(\sigma\circ\gamma)\|_{0}.

By the Bezout theorem, there is a constant
C_{B,r}
depending only on
r
such that the semi-algebraic set

\left\{s\in[-1,1]:~\|P(s)\|\in({\rm e}^{-3}{\rm e}^{\chi}\|D(\sigma\circ\gamma)\|_{0},{\rm e}^{3}{\rm e}^{\chi}\|D(\sigma\circ\gamma)\|_{0})\right\}

is the disjoint union of closed intervals
\{J_{i}\}_{i\in I}
with
\#I\leq C_{B,r}
.
Moreover, for each
t\in J_{i}
, one has that

\displaystyle\|D(g\circ\sigma\circ\gamma)(t)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|P(t)\|+{\rm e}^{\chi-10}\|D(\sigma\circ\gamma)\|_{0}

\displaystyle\leq{\rm e}^{3}{\rm e}^{\chi}\|D(\sigma\circ\varphi)\|_{0}+{\rm e}^{\chi-10}\|D(\sigma\circ\gamma)\|_{0}

\displaystyle\leq{\rm e}^{4}{\rm e}^{\chi}\|D(\sigma\circ\varphi)\|_{0}.

Let
\gamma_{i}:[-1,1]\rightarrow J_{i}
be the composition of
\gamma
with an affine map from
[-1,1]
to
J_{i}
.

Now we apply the Kolmogorov-Landau inequality (Lemma
A.2
), one has that there is a constant
C_{K,r,\alpha}
such that for any
1\leq s\leq r
,

\displaystyle\left\|D^{s}(g\circ\sigma\circ\gamma_{i})\right\|_{0}

\displaystyle\leq C_{K,r,\alpha}\left(\left\|D^{r}(g\circ\sigma\circ\gamma_{i})\right\|_{\alpha}+\left\|D(g\circ\sigma\circ\gamma_{i})\right\|_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{K,r,\alpha}\frac{|J_{i}|}{2}\left(\left\|D^{r}(g\circ\sigma\circ\gamma)\right\|_{\alpha}+\sup_{t\in J_{i}}\left\|D(g\circ\sigma\circ\gamma)(t)\right\|\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{K,r,\alpha}\frac{|J_{i}|}{2}\big({\rm e}^{\chi-10}\left\|D(\sigma\circ\gamma)\right\|_{0}+{\rm e}^{4}{\rm e}^{\chi}\left\|D(\sigma\circ\gamma)\right\|_{0}\big)

\displaystyle\leq C_{K,r,\alpha}\frac{|J_{i}|}{2}{\rm e}^{5}{\rm e}^{\chi}\left\|D(\sigma\circ\gamma)\right\|_{0}.

Cut each
J_{i}
into
[(1000{\rm e}^{5}C_{K,r,\alpha})^{2/\alpha}]+1
interval
\widetilde{J}_{i}
with the same length.
Let
\widetilde{\gamma}_{i}
the composition of
\gamma
with an affine map from
[-1,1]
onto
\widetilde{J}_{i}
.
By the construction, the number of affine maps of the form
\widetilde{\gamma}_{i}
such that the union of their images can cover
I(\chi^{+},\chi)
is at most

(3C_{B,r,\alpha}{\rm e}^{\chi^{+}-\chi+10})^{\frac{1}{r-1+\alpha}}\cdot C_{B,r}\cdot(1000{\rm e}^{5}C_{K,r,\alpha})^{2/\alpha}:=C_{r,\alpha}\cdot\exp\left(\frac{\chi^{+}-\chi}{r-1+\alpha}\right).

We are now going to check that
g\circ\sigma\circ\widetilde{\gamma}_{i}
is bounded.
We first consider
2\leq s\leq r
:

\displaystyle\left\|D^{s}(g\circ\sigma\circ\widetilde{\gamma}_{i})\right\|_{0}

\displaystyle\leq(1000{\rm e}^{5}C_{r,\alpha,K})^{-2}\left\|D^{s}(g\circ\sigma\circ\gamma_{i})\right\|_{0}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{6}(1000{\rm e}^{5}C_{K,r,\alpha})^{-1}\frac{|J_{i}|}{2}{\rm e}^{5}{\rm e}^{\chi}\left\|D(\sigma\circ\gamma)\right\|_{0}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{6}(1000C_{K,r,\alpha})^{-1}\frac{|J_{i}|}{2}\min_{s\in J_{i}}\left\|D(g\circ\sigma\circ\gamma)(s)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{6}(1000C_{K,r,\alpha})^{-1}\frac{|J_{i}|}{2}\min_{s\in\widetilde{J}_{i}}\left\|D(g\circ\sigma\circ\gamma)(s)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{6}\left\|D(g\circ\sigma\circ\widetilde{\gamma}_{i})\right\|_{0}.

Now we check for
r+\alpha
. By using the fact that
\|D^{r}(g\circ\sigma\circ\gamma)\|_{\alpha}\leq{\rm e}^{\chi-10}\|D(\sigma\circ\gamma)\|_{0}
, it is very similar to the above estimate:

\displaystyle\left\|D^{r}(g\circ\sigma\circ\widetilde{\gamma}_{i})\right\|_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq(1000{\rm e}^{5}C_{K,r,\alpha})^{-2}\left\|D^{r}(g\circ\sigma\circ\gamma_{i})\right\|_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{6}(1000{\rm e}^{5}C_{K,r,\alpha})^{-1}\frac{|J_{i}|}{2}{\rm e}^{\chi}\left\|D(\sigma\circ\gamma)\right\|_{0}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{6}(1000C_{K,r,\alpha})^{-1}\frac{|J_{i}|}{2}\min_{s\in J_{i}}\left\|D(g\circ\sigma\circ\gamma)(s)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{6}(1000C_{K,r,\alpha})^{-1}\frac{|J_{i}|}{2}\min_{s\in\widetilde{J}_{i}}\left\|D(g\circ\sigma\circ\gamma)(s)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{6}\left\|Dg\circ\sigma\circ\widetilde{\gamma}_{i}\right\|_{0}.

This completes the proof of Lemma
2.5
.

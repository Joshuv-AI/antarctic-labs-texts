Unlocking time-dependent CP violation without signal vertexing at B B factories
M. Dorigo, S. Raiz, D. Tonelli, R. Žlebčík

Abstract
We present a novel method to measure, without reconstructing the signal decay vertex, time-dependent CP violation in B 0 B^{0} mesons produced in energy-asymmetric electron-positron collisions near the Υ ⁡ ( 4 ​ S ) \Upsilon(4S) resonance. The method relies on quantum-entangled B 0 B ¯ 0 B^{0}\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}^{0} pair-production making the decay time of the partner meson sensitive to CP violation of the signal. We derive analytical expressions for the relevant time-evolution and study, as a use case, an application to B 0 → π 0 ​ π 0 B^{0}\rightarrow\pi^{0}\pi^{0} decays using simplified simulated experiments that approximate the experimental conditions of the Belle II experiment at the SuperKEKB collider. Our approach yields unbiased estimates of the CP -violating coefficients and achieves a sensitivity on mixing-induced CP violation that would require 20-times larger samples with standard approaches. When incorporated into the B → π ​ π B\rightarrow\pi\pi isospin analysis, the expected results would reduce the degeneracy of ϕ 2 \phi_{2} solutions and significantly increase precision. This method opens a path to previously inaccessible CP -violation studies enhancing and accelerating the reach of Belle II and future flavor physics programs.



I
Introduction

Measurements of decay-rate asymmetries that violate the combined transformation of charge conjugation and parity (
CP
symmetry) are central to the study of the weak interactions of quarks. These measurements provide stringent tests of the Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa (CKM) mechanism, which prescribes that all
CP
-violating phenomena in the standard model of particle physics are accommodated by a single complex phase in the quark couplings with
W^{\pm}
bosons
Kobayashi and Maskawa 1973
. For neutral strange, charm, and bottom mesons,
CP
violation phenomenology is further enriched by particle-antiparticle oscillations, yielding
CP
-violating decay-rate asymmetries that depend on the meson decay time. The observation of these asymmetries in the
B^{0}\rightarrow{J\mskip-3.0mu/\mskip-2.0mu\psi\mskip 2.0mu}K^{0}
decays provided the conclusive experimental confirmation of the CKM paradigm
Abe et al. 2001
;
Aubert et al. 2001
in the early 2000s. The focus then shifted toward using time-dependent
CP
violation as a sensitive probe for physics beyond the standard model (see,
e.g.
, Ref.
Fleischer 2024
and references therein).

Bottom mesons have been extensively studied at experiments in energy-asymmetric electron-positron collisions near the
\Upsilon(4S)
resonance (so-called
B
-factories), such as BaBar, Belle, and Belle II, as well as hadron-collider experiments, such as CDF, D0, LHCb, CMS, and ATLAS
Banerjee et al. 2024
. Two ingredients are essential for measuring time-dependent
CP
asymmetries: determining whether the oscillating meson is a
B^{0}
or a
\overline{B}^{0}
(
i.e.
, flavor) at a known time before decay, and measuring the proper-time interval between that instant and the decay. The experimental approaches to flavor determination—and thus to establishing the relevant time—differ significantly between
B
-factories and hadron colliders.

At hadron-collider experiments,
B^{0}
and
\overline{B}^{0}
mesons originate from the hadronization of incoherently produced
b\bar{b}
quark pairs. Flavor tagging is performed at production (see,
e.g.
, Refs
Aaij et al. 2012
;
Aaij et al. 2017
;
Aaij et al. 2016
) and the
CP
-violating decay-rate asymmetry depends only on the signal decay time, which is measured from the observed distance between its production and decay positions and its momentum.

At
B
-factories, neutral bottom mesons are produced in quantum-entangled
B^{0}\overline{B}
pairs through the process
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\Upsilon(4S)\rightarrow B^{0}\overline{B}
, where the
e^{+}
and
e^{-}
beams have different energies, boosting the
\Upsilon(4S)
in the laboratory frame. The entangled pair evolves coherently until one meson decays, at which time the two flavors are opposite. Then, the remaining meson evolves until it decays. If one meson, identified as
B_{\rm tag}
, decays in a final state that unambiguously identifies its flavor and the other,
B_{{\it CP}}
, decays in a final state accessible by both flavors, it is possible to measure time-dependent
CP
violation of
B_{{\it CP}}
(see,
e.g.
, Ref.
Adachi et al. 2024
).
The corresponding time-evolution and
CP
-violating decay-rate asymmetry are described in terms of the flavor correlations between the two mesons and

\Delta t=t_{{\it CP}}-t_{\rm tag},

(1)

the difference between the two decay times, which can be either positive or negative. This difference is measured from the spatial separation of the two decay positions using the known boost.

In this paper, we present a novel method to measure time-dependent
CP
-violating asymmetries at
B
-factories for decays in which
\Delta t
is not available because the
B_{{\it CP}}
decay position is not reconstructed reconstructed. This limitation arises when the final state includes only neutral particles such as photons or
K_{L}^{0}
mesons as in
B^{0}\rightarrow\pi^{0}\pi^{0}
,
B^{0}\rightarrow K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle L}\pi^{0}(\pi^{0})
,
B^{0}\rightarrow K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle L}K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle L}(K^{0}_{\scriptscriptstyle L})
, or the yet-unobserved
B^{0}\rightarrow\gamma\gamma
decays.

In decays involving neutral pions, the
\pi^{0}
is typically reconstructed via
\pi^{0}\rightarrow\gamma\gamma
, which provides no vertex information with sufficient precision for these measurements. Processes like
\pi^{0}\rightarrow e^{+}e^{-}\gamma
, whether from Dalitz decays or photon conversions, enable signal-vertex reconstruction, but their low branching fractions,
\mathcal{O}(10^{-2})
, make measurements of time-dependent
CP
violation feasible only with extremely large data samples.

Our approach overcomes this limitation, enabling sensitive measurement of time-dependent
CP
violation in samples that are already available.
The approach relies solely on determining
t_{\rm tag}
, from the displacement between the
B_{\rm tag}
production and decay points, in analogy with decay-time reconstruction in hadron collisions. However, it exploits the intrinsic connection of
t_{\rm tag}
to the
B_{{\it CP}}
asymmetry due to the quantum-entangled nature of the
B^{0}\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}^{0}
pair characteristic of
B
-factories.

Key to our method is the resolution of the measurement of the
B_{\rm tag}
production and decay positions. The latter depends on detector design and performance and it is important because it contributes to the precision on the measurement. However, the former, which depends on the features of the
e^{+}e^{-}
interaction region (IR), is a dominant limiting factor. The entire feasibility of the method is driven by the following collider-specific enablers:
a small IR transverse size, a nonzero crossing angle for the beams, and a relatively modest
\Upsilon(4S)
boost.

We first describe the approach with an analytical derivation based on the standard formalism of time-evolution of quantum-entangled
B^{0}\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}^{0}
pairs. We then use simplified simulated experiments that approximate the experimental conditions of the Belle II experiment at the SuperKEKB collider to demonstrate quantitatively the performance with a specific case of interest: the measurement of the time-dependent
CP
asymmetry in
B^{0}\rightarrow\pi^{0}\pi^{0}
decays.
Natural units are used, with
c=1
, throughout the paper.

Measurement of the tag decay time

The production and decay space-points of the
B_{\rm tag}

\vec{L}

The

V
Case study:
B^{0}\rightarrow\pi^{0}\pi^{0}
and impact on the CKM angle
\phi_{2}

To assess the potential impact quantitatively, we apply our method to a realistic use case, a measurement of time-dependent
CP
-violating asymmetries in
B^{0}\rightarrow\pi^{0}\pi^{0}
decays at Belle II. If available, this measurement could significantly improve the determination of the CKM angle
\phi_{2}=\arg(V_{td}V_{tb}^{*}/V_{ud}V_{ub}^{*})
, where
V_{ij}
are CKM matrix elements, enhancing in turn the constraining power of CKM-unitarity tests. Prior to using our approach to determine mixing-induced
CP
violation in
B^{0}\rightarrow\pi^{0}\pi^{0}
decays, which is unknown, a consistency check based on
B^{0}\rightarrow J/\psi K^{0}
decays would provide a high-precision validation.

The angle
\phi_{2}
is typically constrained through a combination of results from decays related by isospin symmetry,
B^{0}\rightarrow\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
,
B^{+}\rightarrow\pi^{+}\pi^{0}
, and
B^{0}\rightarrow\pi^{0}\pi^{0}
. This combination suppresses hadronic uncertainties due to penguin contributions in the
B^{0}\rightarrow\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
decay amplitude
Gronau and London 1990
;
Pivk and Le Diberder 2005
;
Charles et al. 2017
. The current
4.5^{\circ}
precision on
\phi_{2}
is driven by the precision of an independent determination based on an analogous isospin analysis of
B\rightarrow\rho\rho
decays. The
B\rightarrow\pi\pi
analysis is less impactful because it yields degenerate results due to an eight-fold ambiguity. The degeneracy originates from lack of experimental information on mixing-induced
CP
violation in
B^{0}\rightarrow\pi^{0}\pi^{0}
decays, typically denoted with the
S_{00}
coefficient.
To date, a measurement of
S_{00}
has been considered feasible by only reconstructing the
\pi^{0}\rightarrow e^{+}e^{-}\gamma
final state to enable signal-vertex reconstruction and the ensuing
\Delta t
measurement. However, this standard approach requires datasets 50–100 times larger than those currently available
Ishino et al. 2007
;
Altmannshofer et al. 2019
. We show that a measurement of
\mathcal{A}_{{\it CP}}^{\prime}(t_{\rm tag})
yields highly constraining
S_{00}
information by already using the data collected by Belle II thus far.

We build upon the recent measurement of the
B^{0}\rightarrow\pi^{0}\pi^{0}
branching fraction and time-integrated
CP
-violating asymmetry
Adachi et al. 2025
, in which 126 signal decays were reconstructed in the Belle II sample collected until 2022, and corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 362
\mbox{\,fb}^{-1}
. The time-integrated asymmetry yields the coefficient
C
, denoted here as
C_{00}
. We assume the same sample size and composition as in Ref.
Adachi et al. 2025
.
The sample is background-dominated: approximately 6850 events are from light-quark production processes (continuum) and 170 from other
B
decays (denoted
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
background), such as
B^{+}\rightarrow\rho^{+}(\rightarrow\pi^{+}\pi^{0})\pi^{0}
, where the
\pi^{+}
is not reconstructed.

The results in Ref.
Adachi et al. 2025
were obtained from a four-dimensional fit to three background-discriminating observables (
\Delta E
,
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
, and
C_{t}
) and one flavor-sensitive observable (
w_{t}
). The first observable is the difference between the
B
-candidate energy and half of the beam energy,
\Delta E=E_{B}^{*}-E_{\mathrm{beam}}^{*}
; the second observable is the
B
-candidate mass calculated from the
B
momentum and the beam energy,
M_{\mathrm{bc}}=\sqrt{E_{\mathrm{beam}}^{*2}-p_{B}^{*2}}
; the third observable,
C_{t}
, is the transformed output of a classifier trained to suppress continuum; and the fourth observable,
w_{t}
, is the transformed fraction of incorrectly tagged events
w
, estimated by a flavor-tagging algorithm providing the
B_{\rm tag}
flavor
q

Adachi et al. 2024
. All starred quantities are calculated in the center-of-mass frame.

We generate simplified simulated data by sampling the probability density function (PDF) used in Ref.
Adachi et al. 2025
, extended to include the observables
t_{\rm tag}
and
\sigma_{t_{\rm tag}}
. Unlike in Ref.
Adachi et al. 2025
, we assume that the joint PDF factorizes as the product of the individual PDFs for each observable; in addition, we neglect flavor-tagging asymmetries and uncertainties on the flavor-tagging parameters. These approximations are not expected to bias appreciably the expected resolutions on the fit results, thus not compromising the validity of this study. The full PDF is

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(t_{\rm tag},\sigma_{t_{\rm tag}},\Delta E,M_{\mathrm{bc}},C_{t},w_{t},q)=

(14)

\displaystyle\sum_{j}f_{j}\mathcal{P}_{j}(t_{\rm tag},\sigma_{t_{\rm tag}};q,w)\mathcal{P}_{j}(\Delta E)\mathcal{P}j(M_{\rm bc})\mathcal{P}_{j}(C_{t})\mathcal{P}_{j}(w_{t}),

where
f_{j}
is the fraction of component
j
, an index that indicate signal (
s
), continuum (
b
) or
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
background (
b
), and
f_{b}
equals
1-f_{s}-f_{c}
.

The PDFs for
\Delta E
,
M_{\mathrm{bc}}
,
C_{t}
, and
w_{t}
are taken from Ref.
Adachi et al. 2025
. Integrating Eq. (
14
) over
t_{\rm tag}
and
\sigma_{t_{\rm tag}}
yields the time-integrated model used in that reference. To validate our implementation, we fit to the simulated data generated from this model to determine signal fraction, continuum fraction, and
C_{00}
. The fit yields unbiased results with Gaussian uncertainties. The average statistical uncertainty of
C_{00}
equals
0.28\pm 0.02
, consistent with the
0.30
value reported in Ref.
Adachi et al. 2025
.

In the time-dependent analysis, the signal PDF derives from Eq. (
5

w

\mathcal{R}_{\sigma_{t_{\rm tag}}}

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathcal{P}_{s}(t_{\rm tag},\sigma_{t_{\rm tag}};q,w)=\\
&\frac{e^{-t_{\rm tag}^{\prime}/\tau}}{2\tau}\biggl(1+q(1-2w)\bigl[S^{\prime}\sin\Delta m(t_{\rm tag}^{\prime}-t_{0})\\
&-C^{\prime}\cos\Delta m(t_{\rm tag}^{\prime}-t_{0})\bigr]\biggr)\otimes\mathcal{R}_{\sigma_{t_{\rm tag}}}(t_{\rm tag}-t_{\rm tag}^{\prime})\,.\end{split}

(15)

Here,
\sigma_{t_{\rm tag}}
indicates the per-event tag decay-time resolution, which effectively increases the importance of events with better resolution in the fit. The physical
CP
-violating parameters
S_{00}
and
C_{00}
are related to

and

via Eq. (
8

For backgrounds, we use a
\delta
-function distribution for continuum, and an exponential decay with the
B^{+}
lifetime for the
B\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}
component. This is motivated by the dominance of the
B^{+}\rightarrow\rho^{+}\pi^{0}
contribution reported in Ref.
Adachi et al. 2025

\sigma_{t_{\rm tag}}
distribution is the same across components and ignore any flavor-tagging asymmetries.

VI
Summary

We introduce a novel method that enables measurement of time-dependent
CP
-violation in
B^{0}
mesons produced in energy-asymmetric electron-positron collisions near the
\Upsilon(4S)
resonance and decaying without a reconstructed signal-side vertex. The relevant time-evolution is expressed solely as a function of the decay time of the pair-produced partner
B
meson and information on signal
CP
violation is provided by the quantum correlation of the produced
B^{0}\kern 1.79993pt\overline{\kern-1.79993ptB}{}^{0}
pair.
This approach extends and enhances sensitivity to time-dependent
CP
-violating parameters in decays without charged final-state particles, such as
B^{0}\rightarrow\pi^{0}\pi^{0}
. Simulation shows that the method achieves a sensitivity on mixing-induced
CP
violation that would require 20-times larger samples with standard approaches. This implies that meaningful constraints on the
CP
-violating coefficients
S
and
C
can already be achieved for
B^{0}\rightarrow\pi^{0}\pi^{0}
decays with the current 0.5 ab
-1
Belle II sample. The expected sensitivity on
S
would reduce the degeneracy of solutions for the CKM angle
\phi_{2}
in the isospin analysis of
B\rightarrow\pi\pi
decays, substantially improving its determination.
These findings establish
t_{\rm tag}
-based time-dependent analysis as a powerful tool for precision
CP
-violation studies at Belle II and beyond.

Appendix A
Formulas for tag-side vertexing

The linearity of the
\chi^{2}
regression of Eq. (
11
) allows to derive explicit analytic expressions for the best-fit vertex positions,
t_{\rm tag}
times, and
\sigma_{t_{\mathrm{tag}}}
uncertainties.
We introduce the vector
\vec{n}=\vec{p}_{\mathrm{tag}}/m_{B}
to simplify the algebra along with

\displaystyle\begin{split}E=(V_{\mathrm{IP}}^{-1}+V_{t}^{-1})^{-1}\,,\\
x_{m}=E(V_{\mathrm{IP}}^{-1}x_{\mathrm{IP}}+V_{t}^{-1}x_{t})\,,\\
t_{\mathrm{tag}}=\frac{n^{T}V_{t}^{-1}(x_{m}-x_{t})}{n^{T}(V_{t}^{-1}EV_{t}^{-1}-V_{t}^{-1})n}\,.\end{split}

(16)

The production and decay vertices determined from the fit are then expressed as

x_{\mathrm{prod}}=x_{m}-t_{\mathrm{tag}}EV_{t}^{-1}n

(17)

x_{\mathrm{dec}}=x_{\mathrm{prod}}+t_{\mathrm{tag}}n

(18)

The tag decay time calculated using
t_{\mathrm{tag}}=p_{\mathrm{tag}}(x_{\mathrm{dec}}-x_{\mathrm{prod}})/m_{B}
is identical to the value resulting from Eq. (
16
). The inverse variance of
t_{\rm tag}
as determined from the fit is

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\sigma_{t_{\textrm{tag}}}^{2}}=(EV_{t}^{-1}n)^{T}V_{\mathrm{IP}}^{-1}(EV_{t}^{-1}n)\\
+(EV_{t}^{-1}n-n)^{T}V_{t}^{-1}(EV_{t}^{-1}n-n)\end{split}

(19)

In the limit of isotropic
B_{\mathrm{tag}}
vertex resolution and an elongated interaction region, the
t_{\mathrm{tag}}
resolution approximates to

\sigma_{t_{\mathrm{tag}}}\approx\frac{\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathrm{IP}}^{2}+\sigma_{t}^{2}}}{c(\beta\gamma)_{\mathrm{tag}}\,\sin\theta_{\mathrm{tag}}},

(20)

where
\sigma_{\mathrm{IP}}
is the transverse size of the interaction region,
\sigma_{t}
is the
B_{\mathrm{tag}}
vertex resolution,
(\beta\gamma)_{\mathrm{tag}}
is the boost of the
B_{\mathrm{tag}}
meson and
\theta_{\mathrm{tag}}
is the angle between
p_{\mathrm{tag}}
and the main axis of the interaction region.

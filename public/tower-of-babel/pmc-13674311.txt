Tubeless thoracoscopic carinal resection via a subcutaneous tunneled periareolar uniportal approach: A case report
Authors: Zhenyuan Yang, Maohui Chen, Chenhui Ni, Yizhou Huang, Hongmu Li, Taidui Zeng, Shuliang Zhang, Chun Chen, Chunyu Zhou, Bin Zheng
Published: 2026

Tubeless carinal reconstruction via periareolar incision.

Central Message

Tubeless uVATS carinal reconstruction via subcutaneous tunneled periareolar incision is discussed.

Tracheobronchial adenoid cystic carcinoma (ACC) represents an uncommon, typically low-grade malignancy arising from submucosal glands.1 Surgical resection with airway reconstruction remains the cornerstone of curative treatment, when feasible.2 We present a case of primary tracheal ACC successfully treated by thoracoscopic carinal reconstruction using a single-port subcutaneous tunneled periareolar approach combined with tubeless anesthetic management. This report provides a proof-of-concept demonstration of the technique, with emphasis on the procedural protocol for safe implementation.

The study was approved by the institutional review board of The Ethics Committee of Fujian Medical University Union Hospital on February 24, 2026 (institutional review board no. 2026KY154). Written informed consent was obtained from the patient for publication of this report and all accompanying materials.

Case Presentation

A 53-year-old previously healthy woman (never-smoker) presented with persistent wheezing for 1 month. Contrast-enhanced computed tomography of the chest identified a well-circumscribed, enhancing lesion in the right main bronchus abutting the carina, measuring approximately 0.7 × 0.5 cm, with no evidence of extrabronchial extension or enlarged mediastinal lymph nodes (Figure 1, A and B). Initial bronchoscopy performed at a local hospital revealed a mass causing near-complete obstruction of the proximal right main bronchus at the level of the carina. After electrocautery loop resection, pathologic examination suggested ACC of the trachea. After confirmatory review by our institutional pathology department, a repeat bronchoscopy was performed to precisely localize the tumor site (Figure 1, C and D).

A and B, Contrast-enhanced computed tomography showing an enhancing lesion in the right main bronchus adjacent to the carina. C and D, Bronchoscopic views obtained at our institution before surgery.

Two intraoperative strategies were planned: (1) carinal resection with reimplantation of the distal right main bronchus to the trachea–left main bronchus junction (neocarina) or (2) extended resection including the left main bronchus if tumor extension was identified (Figure E1).

Planned reconstruction strategies. The red dot indicates the tumor, and the black dashed line indicates the planned resection line. A, Carinal resection with right main bronchus reimplantation. B, Extended resection if left main bronchus involvement is identified.

Procedure Details

Anesthesia

Anesthesia was maintained using a combined intravenous and continuous epidural technique. An epidural catheter was placed at the T7-T8 intervertebral space. After a test dose of 5 mL of 1% lidocaine, a loading dose of 10 mL of 0.2% ropivacaine was administered in divided doses. Intraoperatively, continuous epidural infusion was maintained in combination with intravenous target-controlled infusion of propofol and remifentanil. An intubating laryngeal mask airway was placed to facilitate emergency airway access if required. During critical steps requiring a motionless field, brief neuromuscular blockade was achieved with low-dose rocuronium (2 mg) to reduce tidal volume. The intraoperative graded airway strategy included (1) fiberoptic-guided intubation via the Intubating laryngeal mask airway with bronchial blocker placement; (2) direct fiberoptic-guided tracheal intubation in the lateral position; and (3) standby venoarterial extracorporeal membrane oxygenation, with the circuit primed and team on standby.

Surgical Access: Subcutaneous Tunneled Periareolar Uniportal Approach

With the patient in left lateral decubitus position, a ∼3.5-cm incision was made along the inferolateral areolar border. A subcutaneous tunnel was developed through the submammary plane to the anterior axillary line at the fourth intercostal space, where the pleura was entered. A wound protector established a single working channel; a 5-mm 30° thoracoscope and instruments were introduced through the port (Video 1).

Resection and Reconstruction

Controlled capnothorax (8 mm Hg) facilitated right lung collapse while preserving left spontaneous ventilation. The azygos vein was divided to optimize carinal exposure. The distal trachea, carina, and left main bronchus were mobilized with vagus/recurrent laryngeal nerve preservation, and a hilar release was performed to minimize anastomotic tension. After transection of the distal trachea and right main bronchus, the tumor-bearing segment was resected en bloc with grossly clear margins of approximately 1 cm proximally and distally, under intraoperative bronchoscopic guidance. Frozen section analysis confirmed ACC with negative margins. Systematic lymph node dissection was performed, including stations 2R, 4R, and 7.

Reconstruction followed plan 1: the trimmed right main bronchus was anastomosed to the trachea–left main bronchus junction using continuous 3-0 PROLENE (Ethicon, LLC) sutures, beginning at the posterior membranous wall. Knots were tied extraluminally. An underwater seal test confirmed no air leak (Figure 2).

Thoracoscopic views. A, Resection margins (circled). B, Airway stumps postresection. C, Trimmed right main bronchial stump. D, Completed neocarina.

Two 8-Fr chest drainage tubes were placed: one in the anterior axillary line and one in the posterior axillary line. The periareolar incision was closed in layers, and a compressive dressing was applied over the subcutaneous tunnel to reduce seroma risk. Total operative time was 265 minutes; estimated blood loss was ∼100 mL (Video 2).

Histopathology and Postoperative Course

Final histopathology confirmed adenoid cystic carcinoma with negative margins and no nodal metastasis. The residual tumor was 0.5 cm, with an estimated resected segment of 2.5 cm. The patient was discharged on POD 8 with 98% room-air oxygen saturation (details in Appendix E1 and Figure E2).

Discussion

Carinal reconstruction remains technically demanding and is performed traditionally via thoracotomy with complex airway management. Here we combined 2 innovations—tubeless spontaneous-ventilation thoracoscopy and a subcutaneous tunneled periareolar uniportal approach. This technique requires a multidisciplinary team experienced in both tubeless video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery and complex airway reconstruction, and it should not be regarded as conferring oncologic or safety superiority over conventional approaches.

The tubeless strategy may improve anastomotic exposure and reduce mechanical interference at the tracheobronchial junction. However, it requires strict patient selection, precise coordination with anesthesia, and immediate readiness for conversion to intubation and/or extracorporeal support. Li and colleagues3 have previously reported the first case of nonintubated robot-assisted half-carinal reconstruction, offering early proof-of-concept for this minimally invasive airway strategy. We used high-flow oxygenation with brief controlled apnea during critical suturing to stabilize the field while maintaining adequate oxygen saturation.

The periareolar tunneled approach is increasingly reported for thoracic procedures because it can conceal the scar.4 On the basis of our initial institutional experience, elevated body mass index, macromastia, and a history of previous breast surgery or radiotherapy are regarded as relative or absolute contraindications to the subcutaneous tunneled periareolar (please see Appendix E2 for selection criteria).

ACC is characterized by submucosal and longitudinal spread, making margin assessment critical. We relied on intraoperative frozen sections to confirm negative margins and performed systematic nodal dissection, given reported rates of nodal involvement in tracheobronchial ACC.5 After complete resection with negative margins and node-negative status, observation with structured surveillance was recommended.

Conclusions

This case provides proof-of-concept for tubeless thoracoscopic carinal reconstruction via a subcutaneous tunneled periareolar incision in a meticulously selected patient. It represents an effort to expand the boundaries of airway surgery.

Histopathology and Postoperative Course

Final histopathology demonstrated adenoid cystic carcinoma of the right main bronchus involving the carinal region, with microscopically negative margins. No metastatic involvement was identified on systematic lymph node dissection. The case was reviewed at tumor board, and no adjuvant therapy was recommended, given complete resection and node-negative status.

The patient recovered without ventilatory support. Pain was well controlled with epidural and nonopioid adjuncts. Because of procedural complexity, she was observed in the intensive care unit overnight. Ambulation and respiratory physiotherapy began on postoperative day (POD) 1.

Computed tomography scan of the chest on POD 2 revealed bilateral inflammatory changes with minor air/fluid; the patient remained afebrile and clinically stable. Antimicrobial therapy (cefoperazone/sulbactam) led to marked radiographic improvement by POD 6 (Figure E2). Given the unprecedented nature of this combined technique, the chest drain was intentionally maintained until POD 7 for vigilant anastomotic surveillance, with removal after complete radiographic resolution. Bedside bronchoscopy before discharge confirmed a well-healed anastomosis with patent bilateral airways. She was discharged on POD 8 with 98% room-air oxygen saturation and no respiratory symptoms.

Patient Selection Criteria

These patient selection criteria have been formulated on the basis of our limited initial experience and may be refined as experience accumulates. They currently include (1) good preoperative cardiopulmonary function without serious comorbidities; (2) normal baseline arterial blood gas values and the physiological reserve to tolerate intraoperative permissive hypercapnia; (3) no anticipated difficult airway; (4) a localized lesion requiring no extended resection; (5) no history or imaging evidence of extensive pleural adhesions; (6) body mass index within the normal range and absence of macromastia, to avoid technical difficulty in tunnel creation; and (7) strong patient preference for optimal cosmetic outcome.

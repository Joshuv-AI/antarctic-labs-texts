On the Applicability Ranges of T c T_{c} Formulas for Proximity-Coupled Thin SN and SS Bilayers
Takayuki Kubo

Abstract
This Brief Note revisits the well-established T c T_{c} formulas for proximity-coupled thin superconductor-normal conductor (SN) and superconductor-superconductor (SS) bilayers, highlighting their relationships and clarifying their ranges of applicability. Since these formulas are often misapplied in practical contexts, this note provides guidance for their correct use in experimental and applied settings. For SN bilayers, McMillan’s formula is recommended for its broad applicability, with its SS counterpart offering similar reliability.

Today, the proximity effect plays a critical role in superconducting device technologies.
For instance, researchers in the field of transition edge sensors (TES) regularly exploit both superconductor normal conductor (SN) and superconductor superconductor (SS) proximity effects to fine-tune the transition temperature (

This Brief Note focuses on the
well-established

T_{c}
formulas for proximity-coupled SN and SS thin bilayers. While these topics are well-studied, with the

Several

However, these formulas are often applied without sufficient consideration of their limits, which can lead to incorrect interpretations of experimental data.

In the following, we briefly summarize the
T_{c}

The
general

T_{c}
formula for a thin
SN bilayer
is well-established and given by
Khusainov
;
Fominov
;
Gurevich
(with the derivation provided in the Supplementary Material 1):

\displaystyle\ln\frac{T_{c}}{T_{c1}}=\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}\left\{\ln\left(1+\frac{1+\alpha}{(\Theta_{1}/T_{c1})\alpha\beta}\right)-u(T_{c})\right\},

(1)

where the function
u(T_{c})
is defined using the digamma function
\psi
as follows:

\displaystyle u(T_{c})=\psi\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1+\alpha}{2\pi(T_{c}/T_{c1})\alpha\beta}\right)-\psi\left(\frac{1}{2}\right).

(2)

The material parameters summarized in Table
1
are aggregated into the following dimensionless parameters:

\displaystyle\alpha=\frac{N_{2}d_{2}}{N_{1}d_{1}},

(3)

\displaystyle\beta=4\frac{e^{2}}{\hbar}R_{B}kT_{c1}N_{1}d_{1}.

(4)

Note that our parameterization is similar to that of Gurevich
Gurevich
, but our
\beta
contains the additional factor
kT_{c1}/\hbar
to ensure
\beta
is dimensionless.
The transition temperature of thin SN bilayers can be obtained by numerically solving Eq. (
1
) with respect to
T_{c}/T_{c1}
.
This is a straightforward problem.

It is important to note that in deriving the formula [Eq. (
1
)], the Kuprianov-Lukichev boundary conditions
BC
were applied, as is standard in the referenced works
Khusainov
;
Golubov_SPIE
;
Belzig
;
Fominov
;
Brammertz
;
Gurevich_Kubo
;
Kubo_Gurevich
;
Martinis
;
Irwin
;
Gurevich
(see also the Supplementary Material 1).
These boundary conditions are derived from the more general formulations
Nazarov
;
Linder
for interfaces with low transmission coefficients (
t<1
), which in our parametrization corresponds to
d_{1}/\xi_{0}<\beta<\infty
.
Here,
\xi_{0}=\hbar v_{f}/\pi\Delta_{0}
is the intrinsic BCS coherence length in the superconductor layer, and is typically large in conventional metallic superconductors used in devices (e.g.,
\xi_{0}^{\rm(Al)}>1\,{\rm\mu m}
,
\xi_{0}^{\rm(Ti)}\simeq 300\,{\rm nm}
,
\xi_{0}^{\rm(Nb)}\simeq 40\,{\rm nm}
).
As a result, the applicability range of Eq. (
1
) spans a wide range of
\beta
, from
\beta\ll 1
to
\beta\gg 1
.
In the following, we assume this condition is always satisfied, and the general formula [Eq. (
1

Based on the magnitude of the dimensionless interface resistance
\beta
, we can categorize the SN proximity effect into three different regimes: small
\beta
(
\beta\ll\beta_{0}
), large
\beta
(
\beta\gg\beta_{1}
), and moderate
\beta
(
\beta_{0}\ll\beta\ll\beta_{1}
).
Here,
\beta_{0}
and
\beta_{1}
are given by:

\displaystyle\beta_{0}=\left(1+\frac{1}{\alpha}\right)\frac{T_{c1}}{\Theta_{1}},

(5)

\displaystyle\beta_{1}=\frac{\pi}{4}+\frac{1}{2\pi}+\frac{1}{2\pi\alpha}.

(6)

Note that the Debye temperature is typically
\Theta_{1}\sim 100\,{\rm K}\gg T_{c1}
.
For instance, assuming
\alpha\sim 1
, we find
\beta_{0}\sim 10^{-2}
and
\beta_{1}\sim 1
, resulting in
\beta_{0}\ll\beta_{1}
.

In typical situations, the interface resistance is sufficiently large such that
\beta\gg\beta_{0}
. In this regime, we can neglect the logarithmic term in the parentheses, and Eq. (
1
) simplifies to

\displaystyle\ln\frac{T_{c}}{T_{c1}}=-\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}u(T_{c})\hskip 14.22636pt(\beta_{0}\ll\beta),

(7)

which is the formula derived by McMillan in 1968 [see Eq. (39) of Ref.
McMillan
].
In this case,
T_{c}
depends only on
\alpha
and
\beta
; the parameter
\Theta_{1}/T_{c1}
is not required.
Further assuming
\beta\gg(1+1/\alpha)T_{c1}/2\pi T_{c}=\beta_{1}
, the first term in
u(T_{c})
can be expanded around
\psi(1/2)
, giving the formula:

\displaystyle\frac{T_{c}}{T_{c1}}=1-\frac{\pi}{4\beta}\hskip 14.22636pt(\beta_{1}\ll\beta),

(8)

On the other hand, when
\beta\ll\beta_{1}
, using the asymptotic form of the digamma function, we have
u\simeq\ln[(2e^{\gamma_{E}}/\pi)(1+\alpha)/\{(T_{c}/T_{c1})\alpha\beta\}]
, where
\gamma_{E}=0.577
and
2e^{\gamma_{E}}/\pi=1.13
.
Then, Eq. (
7
) reduces to

\displaystyle\frac{T_{c}}{T_{c1}}=\left(\frac{\beta}{1.13(1+1/\alpha)}\right)^{\alpha}\hskip 14.22636pt(\beta_{0}\ll\beta\ll\beta_{1}),

(9)

which corresponds to the formula shown by Martinis et al. [see Eq. (9) of Ref.
Martinis
].

In the opposite limit to Eqs. (
7
)-(
9
), we consider the case where
\beta\to 0
. In this limit, Eq. (
1
) simplifies to:

\displaystyle\frac{T_{c}}{T_{c1}}=e^{-\frac{1}{g_{1}}\frac{N_{2}d_{2}}{N_{1}d_{1}}}\hskip 14.22636pt(\beta\to 0),

(10)

Here,
g_{1}=1/\ln(1.13\Theta_{1}/T_{c1})
is the BCS coupling constant in region 1 (the superconductor side).
Eq. (
10
), obtained by Cooper in 1961
Cooper
, is known as the Cooper limit.
It is worth noting that Eq. (
7
), as well as Eqs. (
8
) and (
9
), do not reproduce the Cooper limit even at vanishing interface resistance (
\beta\to 0
).
The reason is clear
Fominov
:
Eqs. (
7
)-(
9
) are valid for moderate to large interface resistance.
To reproduce Cooper’s result, the logarithmic term in the parentheses in Eq. (
1
) is necessary.

In summary, this Brief Note revisited the well-established
T_{c}
formulas for proximity-coupled SN and SS thin bilayers, emphasizing their applicability and interrelationships.
The five
T_{c}
formulas for a thin SN bilayer are visually summarized in Fig.
2
(a), offering a convenient reference for readers seeking to quickly understand the applicability range of each formula.
For thin SN bilayers, McMillan’s formula [Eq. (
7
)] is recommended due to its broad applicability.
For thin SS bilayers, Eq. (
11
), the SS counterpart to McMillan’s formula, is similarly recommended for its wide applicability.
The derivation of the
T_{c}

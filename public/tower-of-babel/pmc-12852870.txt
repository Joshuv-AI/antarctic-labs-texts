Research on multimodal interaction intention recognition based on EEG, eye movement, and gesture signals
Authors: Yeshan Zhu, Wei Wang, Sina Dang, Jue Qu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Accurately recognizing interaction intention is crucial for enhancing the efficiency of human-computer interaction. Most current studies rely on single modalities or are confined to the fusion of eye movement and EEG signals, while the integration of gesture—a fundamental interaction modality—remains scarce, resulting in underutilized multimodal complementarity. Furthermore, existing methods support only a limited range of intention categories, restricting their applicability. To address these issues, this paper proposes a multimodal fusion recognition model that integrates EEG, eye movement, and gesture signals for recognizing six categories of interaction intentions. The model employs modality-specific networks to extract core features, a cross-modal attention mechanism to dynamically establish inter-modal dependencies, and a dynamic ensemble strategy to adaptively weight models based on performance. Evaluations on a public dataset show that the proposed approach outperforms traditional multimodal fusion models. Ablation studies confirm that both the attention mechanism and ensemble strategy are essential to the model’s performance. The research findings mitigate the shortcoming in the diversity of tasks for interaction intention recognition, expand the breadth of research in this domain, and provide both a theoretical foundation and technical support for the efficient recognition of multiple intention categories.

Introduction

In the process of human-computer interaction technology evolving towards intelligence and naturalization, interaction intention recognition has become a core bridge connecting humans and machines. The accuracy and usability of interaction intention recognition are key to enhancing the efficiency of human-computer interaction and increasing system proactivity and intelligence1. Multimodal interaction intention recognition achieves an in-depth understanding and prediction of user interaction intentions by fusing information from different modalities, fully integrating the characteristics of various modal signals, thereby enhancing the robustness and reliability of interactive systems. Advancing the technology of multimodal interaction intention recognition is of significant importance for the development of the field of human-computer interaction.

In psychology, human intention is typically divided into explicit and implicit intention2. Explicit intention refers to needs and ideas that humans actively convey through direct, clear language, text, or operations, which others can obtain directly without additional inference or analysis. Implicit intention refers to intentions not explicitly declared through direct language or text, but which others can infer through physiological behaviors or signals, context, and historical preferences, using contextual analysis and model algorithms for in-depth analysis to uncover hidden needs and goals3. Many scholars have researched human implicit intention. Feng et al.4 used EEG signals from the 5 s before a jump action to recognize the subsequent jump intention and action type, achieving an recognition accuracy of 88.56%. Doshi and Trivedi5 used a Hidden Markov model combined with the external environment and driver operation context to generate and recognize driver intention. As a type of implicit intention, the recognition accuracy and usability of interaction intention impact the practicality and real-time performance of interactive systems.

From a temporal perspective, interaction intention recognition is dynamic, emphasizing the temporal evolution of interactive behavior, making it more suitable for application domains requiring real-time adaptability and understanding of behavioral sequences6–9. In the field of interaction intention recognition, multimodal intention recognition that fuses various physiological and behavioral signals is effective, high-precision, and produces highly credible results, increasingly becoming a research hotspot. Multimodal intention research often combines human physiological and behavioral signals such as EEG, gesture, facial expression, and speech, fully utilizing the role of different modal information in intention recognition. Through technical means like data processing and modal fusion, it achieves complementarity between different modalities for accurate and efficient prediction of interaction intentions10–14. Among these, EEG signals, as a direct reflection of neural activity and belonging to physiological imaging technology, have advantages such as high temporal resolution, good portability, and non-invasiveness, making them key to decoding brain intention, but they cannot reflect the intention target15–17. Eye movement signals can reflect the visual information of the interactor, accurately record the area of regard and visual trajectory, and reflect attention allocation in real-time, providing better insight into human intention without relying on body movement, but they cannot capture early intention or verify intention18,19. Gesture signals, such as position and speed, directly reflect the details of movement execution. By processing gesture movement signals, interaction intention can be recognized within 1 s after movement initiation, but prediction is impossible in the early stages of intention formation20–22. In the process of transforming interaction intention into definite interactive behavior, EEG signals change first, with neurons in specific brain areas firing synchronously; EEG is directly linked to the neural origin of intention. Eye movement signals are linked to the intention target after intention generation, representing early explicit behavior of intention. Gesture signals are intuitive signals through which humans convey intention via body movements, directly translating intention into recognizable movement commands. Fusing EEG, eye movement, and gesture signals for interaction intention recognition covers the entire process from intention generation to behavior execution, overcomes the recognition limitations of single-modal signals, achieves complementarity among modal signals, and can effectively improve the accuracy of intention recognition and enhance system robustness.

Based on the above analysis, this paper researches multimodal interaction intention recognition by fusing EEG, eye movement, and gesture signals. We propose a multimodal recognition model integrating cross-modal attention and dynamic ensemble learning, breaking through the limitations of fixed-weight fusion of modalities in traditional methods and achieving complementary diversity between modalities. The model maps EEG, eye movement, and gesture features into a unified space and constructs a multi-layer self-attention network to dynamically calculate modal weights: prioritizing the strengthening of EEG weight in the intention generation phase to utilize its early neural signals, enhancing the gesture weight in the execution phase to fully utilize movement details, and strengthening the eye movement weight in attention-dispersed scenarios to correct recognition results. A performance-based dynamic ensemble learning strategy is constructed, significantly enhancing model robustness through heterogeneous models and differentiated weights. Five sets of cross-modal attention models are trained, and weights are dynamically allocated based on validation set accuracy, achieving precise matching between models and interaction categories while preserving model diversity, and improving the recognition accuracy for intentions with small sample sizes. Related work

In this section, we provide a brief overview of recent research progress in multimodal interaction intention recognition, attention mechanisms, and dynamic ensemble strategies.

Multimodal interaction intention recognition

Multimodal interaction intention recognition involves multiple input channels, utilizing information such as EEG, eye movement, gesture, speech, and haptics to infer user interaction intention and improve interaction efficiency23. Chhabria et al.24 demonstrated that compared to unimodal approaches, multimodal interaction intention recognition enhances the naturalness and efficiency of interaction. Park et al.25 combined EEG signals with eye movement signals for intention recognition, achieving an accuracy approximately 5% higher than relying solely on EEG or eye movement signals. Chowdhury et al.26 summarized methods for detecting driver fatigue and found that fatigue recognition accuracy based on multiple physiological signals was significantly higher than that based on a single physiological signal. Mollaret et al.27 proposed a probabilistic discrete-state Hidden Markov Model that integrated multimodal information such as head pose, shoulder orientation, and speech, making it easier for robots to understand user intentions. Postelnicu et al.28 combined eye movement, EEG, and gesture signals to control a robotic hand; results showed that the SUS score for integrating multiple modal signals was significantly above average. In their study on driver intent prediction, Xue et al.29 proposed a driver intent prediction method based on multi-dimensional cross-modal information interaction and validated it on a public dataset. The results demonstrated that this method achieved competitive results in terms of accuracy while balancing performance and computational cost. Traditional multimodal intention recognition essentially uses unimodal information to serially analyze user intention and cannot fuse multiple channels in parallel to infer the user’s current behavior, thus offering limited improvement in intention recognition accuracy.

Currently, multimodal fusion strategies mainly include feature-level fusion and decision-level fusion30–32. Feature-level fusion strategies fuse the raw data of multiple modalities before sending it to a common recognition model for intention prediction. This preserves the original modal information, can mine complementary relationships between modalities, and helps achieve finer fusion33. Liu et al.34 proposed a deep learning-based multimodal fusion framework that fused speech, gesture, and body movement signals at the feature level. Compared to three unimodal fusion models, experiments demonstrated the superiority of the multimodal fusion model. Lei et al.35 proposed a novel multimodal emotion recognition fusion network named MF-Net, which enhances the efficacy of multimodal fusion while preserving the distinctive characteristics of each modality. It demonstrated state-of-the-art performance in comparative tests against baseline models. Compared to feature-level fusion strategies that require large amounts of data for training, decision-level fusion strategies have stronger temporal independence, can fuse the initial decision results of different modalities, and reduce training costs. Vu et al.36 used weight standards and optimal probability fusion methods to fuse speech and gesture signals at the decision level, designing a sentiment recognition method with high accuracy. Wang et al.37 proposed a spatio-temporal feature-based multimodal fusion method that improved the accuracy of sentiment recognition systems by fusing visual and auditory information at the decision level. Overall, feature-level and decision-level fusion strategies have different focuses and impacts on intention recognition. Feature-level fusion algorithms focus on the deep-level features of different modalities, striving to combine the characteristics of different modalities, which helps achieve feature complementarity between modalities. Decision-level fusion algorithms perform weighted fusion of the prediction results of each modality, incurring greater modal information loss, and are less sensitive to modal differences, making them suitable for tasks with small sample sizes and simple models. Therefore, this paper utilizes a cross-modal attention mechanism to dynamically fuse multimodal features at the feature level, achieve complementarity between multiple modalities, deeply integrate features from different modalities, and to a greater extent improve the accuracy of interaction intention recognition.

In terms of the types of modalities fused, many studies focus on fusing EEG and eye movement signals. The brain and eyes have a coupled relationship in neural connectivity, and the rich spatial information of eye movement signals is related to target location, compensating for the shortcoming that EEG signals reflect intention but cannot correspond to the intention target38. Zhao et al.39 proposed a decision-level human-computer interaction intention recognition method based on the fusion of EEG and eye movement, achieving synchronous intention recognition during user-computer interaction. Experimental results showed that in most cases, the intention recognition results performed better than algorithms relying on a single modality. Kim et al.40 used EEG and eye movement to control a drone, combining commands generated from EEG with the spatial information of eye movement to manipulate the drone’s flight in various directions. Research on fusing three different modalities for intention recognition is relatively scarce. Huang et al.41 proposed a human-computer interaction intention inference method fusing EEG, eye movement, and haptic feedback, recognizing 8 eye movement directions. However, this method is relatively simple, merely performing simple data fusion of the three modalities without fully utilizing the characteristics and advantages of different modalities in intention recognition, resulting in low intention recognition accuracy. As the most basic modality in interactive movement, the importance of gesture signals for intention recognition is self-evident. Domen compared the performance of different modalities in predicting human movement intention through a study; results showed that gesture signals had high accuracy but could only be predicted after movement initiation42. This characteristic of gesture signals effectively compensates for the shortcomings of EEG signals, which can only predict intention in the early stages, and eye movement signals, which can only predict the target object but cannot provide target movement information. Based on the above analysis, this paper deeply integrates EEG, eye movement, and gesture signals, fully utilizes the advantages of the three modalities, and improves the reliability of intention recognition results and the robustness of the model.

Attention mechanism

The attention mechanism originates from the human ability to process external information, referring to the fact that when processing information, people focus their attention on the information that needs attention and filter out irrelevant information. In deep learning, it is primarily used to enhance the model’s ability to focus on important information43. Many scholars have studied the application of attention mechanisms in multimodal data processing, achieving good results. Ni et al.44 proposed a network specifically for semantic segmentation in dynamic traffic scenes, which used a hierarchical attention fusion module to refine multi-scale multimodal features, significantly improving segmentation accuracy and robustness. Hu et al.45 proposed a multimodal sentiment analysis method with an attentional recurrent network; experimental results showed that this model could achieve good performance with low computational complexity. Wang46 proposed a multimodal network framework based on a cross-modal context attention model, combining a channel-space joint attention model and a self-attention model to obtain the final fusion features for each modality. Comparative experiments on multiple public datasets showed that this framework significantly improved the accuracy and robustness of pedestrian movement intention recognition. When studying driver unstable emotions, Luan et al.47 proposed a multimodal emotion recognition model MHLT, which performed model-level fusion via an attention mechanism to integrate video and audio modalities. The accuracy of emotion recognition was significantly improved, reflecting the effectiveness of the attention mechanism in multimodal fusion.

In multimodal fusion, the proportions of different modalities vary, modal features differ, and certain relationships exist between modalities. In actual training, it is easy to achieve poor model training results due to the inability to fully utilize the relationships between modalities. The self-attention mechanism canbetter solve this problem. Bian et al.48, while studying intra-modal and inter-modal interaction issues in multimodal data, proposed a hierarchical multimodal self-attention neural network that effectively solved the problem of multimodal feature fusion. Comparative experiments showed that this network’s performance was significantly better than other baseline methods. Wang et al.49 proposed a multimodal self-attention network, designing a graph self-attention block to capture the intrinsic topology and temporal relationships between joints, achieving the fusion of joints, joint motion, and bone flow. Comparative experiments showed that the model achieved the best performance in three skeleton-based action recognition datasets. Radhika50, while studying multimodal image fusion problems, proposed an adaptive fusion method that used a self-attention mechanism to enhance the adaptive fusion of features extracted from different modalities and trained this mechanism to minimize the loss function. Multiple experimental indicators showed that the self-attention mechanism has excellent performance in handling modal feature fusion problems.

When processing the model’s current information, the self-attention mechanism tends to over-focus on the model’s own information and has poor ability to capture effective modal information to be processed. To solve this problem, researchers proposed the multi-head self-attention mechanism. The multi-head attention mechanism can process different information based on the same attention mechanism and then integrate multiple attention mechanisms, transforming them through a learnable linear projection to produce the final output51. Mao et al.52 proposed a multimodal hate speech detection model that introduced a multi-head self-attention mechanism for feature fusion of images and text, effectively improving the model’s detection accuracy and precision. Its performance was better than similar detection models, proving that the multi-head self-attention mechanism can deeply extract features from different modalities and effectively support model prediction or detection. Yan et al.53 introduced a multimodal detection framework in the study of fake news automatic detection, using the multi-head self-attention mechanism to minimize the distance between information with the same label in the multimodal feature space and maximize the distance between information with different labels. This design fully utilized text and visual feature information, achieved effective detection of true and false information, and the detection performance was significantly better than similar methods. The multi-head self-attention mechanism can dynamically and adaptively assign weights to different modalities, resolve conflicts between modalities, fully mine complementary information between different modalities, and achieve deep fusion of multimodal information. Therefore, this paper designs a cross-modal attention mechanism based on the multi-head self-attention mechanism to dynamically model the dependencies between modalities, overcome the difficulty of direct fusion caused by heterogeneity among multimodal signals, improve the quality of feature fusion, and lay the foundation for high-quality interaction intention recognition.

Dynamic ensemble strategy

We have previously discussed the role of the multi-head self-attention mechanism in multimodal fusion. It is important to note that many studies based on the multi-head self-attention mechanism ignore the differences in head learning performance within the self-attention layer. Some researchers have proposed using ensemble learning to solve this problem54. Ensemble learning assumes that each base classifier is independent and uses specific strategies to combine multiple classifiers to obtain higher classification accuracy, often used for classification problems. Muthuswamy55 used a dynamic ensemble learning method based on autoencoders and convolutional neural networks to classify driver distraction behavior. Experimental results showed that the accuracy of models using different ensemble techniques varied but was higher than the accuracy of the baseline model. Chen56 applied ensemble learning to the prediction of the remaining useful life of aero-engines, dynamically selecting sensor data through an ensemble multimodal data fusion method and ensemble transfer learning technology, and constructed an ensemble transfer regression network. Comparative experiments showed that this method was superior to other state-of-the-art prediction methods. Yang et al.57 studied three dynamic weighted ensemble learning models and proposed a dynamic weighted model combined with graph neural networks for urban passenger flow prediction. Experiments on two real datasets demonstrated the help of ensemble learning for the model. In risk assessment research, Phillips58 developed a hybrid ensemble learning model to predict the severity of midline shift, achieving a prediction accuracy of 96.6%, reflecting the effectiveness of ensemble learning in predicting dynamic targets.

Overall, ensemble learning is an advanced learning paradigm that constructs a more robust and efficient model by dynamically assigning different weights to each base model during the inference phase based on the input characteristics of each model. The multimodal interaction intention recognition model proposed in this paper trains multiple base models. Each base model is limited by the capacity of the model architecture and the randomness of training, resulting in limited performance. Through the dynamic ensemble strategy, multiple base models are dynamically integrated, weights are dynamically allocated to each base model, and adjustments are made dynamically based on real-time input, giving the model certain feedback regulation capabilities and enhancing the model’s stability and robustness.

Multimodal recognition model

Overall framework overview

Addressing the core challenges of multimodal signal heterogeneity and insufficient robustness in complex scenes, this paper proposes a multimodal interaction intention recognition framework integrating cross-modal attention and dynamic ensemble learning. This framework aims to deeply mine the unique information of EEG, eye movement, and gesture modalities and the complementary dependency relationships between modalities, and further enhance the model’s generalization ability and stability through an ensemble learning strategy. The overall workflow of the model is shown in Fig. 1, mainly including the following four core stages:

Data Augmentation and preprocessing: The original multimodal signals are first transformed into high-quality feature vectors through preprocessing and feature engineering. After a series of augmentation operations, the multimodal data sample size is expanded. EEG signals focus on time and frequency domains; eye movement data extracts statistical features from fixation and saccade events; gesture data parses features such as reaction time, accuracy, and kinematics from its motion trajectory. This stage provides information-rich, dimensionally regularized input for subsequent model training.Modality-specific deep feature extraction: To fully utilize the unique characteristics of each modality’s data, this framework designs three parallel dedicated sub-networks for deep feature mining. Each sub-network adopts an architecture combining deep convolution and recurrent neural networks, aiming to capture the fine temporal patterns of EEG, the attention allocation patterns of eye movement, and the continuous motion patterns of gestures, thereby outputting high-level abstract representations for each modality.Cross-modal attention fusion: The high-level features extracted by each modal sub-network are mapped to a common feature space through linear transformation and constructed into a modal sequence. Subsequently, a multi-layer cross-modal attention mechanism is introduced. Through “Query-Key-Value” calculation, it adaptively learns the interaction weights between any two modalities, dynamically mines and fuses the nonlinear complementary relationships between modalities, and generates a powerful fused representation. In contrast to the limitations of traditional feature concatenation (which merely linearly stacks features) and gating mechanisms (which rely on static weight allocation), the cross-modal attention fusion method proposed in this study achieves deep interaction through a dual adaptive fusion architecture. Specifically: First, we reshape the features of each modality into sequential representations and employ a multi-layer, multi-head attention mechanism (8 heads in the first layer, 4 heads in the second) to construct cross-modal interactions. This enables the model to dynamically capture fine-grained dependencies between modalities in a deep semantic space, much like how it processes linguistic context. Secondly, we introduced an innovative adaptive path selection mechanism: On one hand, the importance of each modality is calculated using a Dense(1, sigmoid) layer and normalized in real-time, thereby upgrading traditional gating. On the other hand, a three-path parallel architecture—comprising direct weighted fusion, cross-modal attention features, and original features—was designed. Softmax-based path weighting allows the model to automatically adjust the optimal fusion strategy based on the quality of each specific input sample. This dual adaptive design, composed of both modality weights and path weights, and supplemented with residual connections and layer normalization to ensure training stability, further demonstrates the methodological innovativeness of our approach.Dynamic ensemble and classification output: To improve the model’s robustness in complex scenes, this paper trains multiple base models, each containing the aforementioned three stages. During inference, a dynamic ensemble strategy is adopted, assigning appropriate weights to each base model based on its performance on the validation set. The final decision is made through weighted voting, outputting the intention classification results for 6 interaction directions. The ensemble learning strategy in this study adopts a training paradigm with identical data splitting but different random initializations. First, all five ensemble models are trained on the same training/test set split, ensuring consistency and fairness in evaluation. Second, diversity at the model level is introduced by initializing the model parameters with different random seeds. This homogeneous-data-heterogeneous-initialization strategy effectively reduces model variance while maintaining consistent data distribution. Third, differentiated training configurations further enhance ensemble diversity, specifically by applying data augmentation to the first three models but not to the remaining two. This design balances the bias-variance trade-off: it avoids the potential evaluation bias introduced by different data splits, while ensuring the effectiveness of the ensemble through parameter randomness and diversity in training configurations.

The core distinction between the attention-based multimodal fusion method proposed in this study and existing similar attention fusion methods lies in its construction of a full-chain optimized architecture—comprising modality-specific feature extraction, hierarchical cross-modal interaction, and dynamic adaptive fusion—tailored to the highly heterogeneous nature of EEG, eye movement, and gesture data. The specific differences are manifested in three aspects: First, while existing attention-based multimodal fusion methods often employ a single-level cross-modal attention mechanism and lack modeling of intra-modal fine-grained features, our method first designs specific branches for each modality, then employs a two-layer differentiated multi-head cross-modal attention to achieve progressive modeling from intra-modal refinement to cross-modal collaboration, thereby avoiding the undifferentiated treatment of heterogeneous modal features by generic attention architectures. Second, whereas the attention weights in existing methods are mostly static single-path outputs, our method innovatively designs a mechanism combining dynamic modality weighting and multi-path fusion: on one hand, it calculates and normalizes the importance weights of each modality in real-time via a neural network; on the other hand, it constructs a three-path architecture—direct weighted fusion, cross-modal attention feature fusion, and original feature combination—allowing the model to dynamically adjust the fusion strategy based on input modality quality through softmax path weights, thereby addressing the issue of traditional attention methods struggling to adapt to complex modality quality fluctuations with a single fusion path. Finally, while existing attention-based multimodal methods often focus on generic fusion logic and do not incorporate the modality coordination requirements of specific tasks, our method, targeting the association characteristics between modalities and the task in hand gesture direction recognition, implicitly incorporates modality-task mapping relationships within the attention computation. Furthermore, it can be further optimized subsequently with direction-specific attention heads, a level of task-driven attention customization difficult to achieve with existing methods. In summary, the method proposed in this paper is not a simple application of the attention mechanism. Instead, it achieves deep coupling of the attention mechanism with modality modeling, fusion paths, and task adaptation, centered around the characteristics of the three heterogeneous modalities and the specific task requirements. This constitutes a fundamental distinction from existing multimodal fusion methods that primarily rely on a generic attention framework.

The model’s final objective function aims to minimize the Focal Loss between the true intention labels and the prediction results. The entire process from feature extraction to intention classification is optimized through end-to-end training. We will provide a detailed explanation of the model framework in the following sections.

Data augmentation and preprocessing

This paper uses the public dataset provided by Zhang et al.59, which includes synchronously collected EEG, fNIRS, eye movement data, and gesture data from 50 subjects (The download link for this dataset is 10.17605/osf.io/cfdsz). Zhang used a visuomotor paradigm to simultaneously detect attention and motor processes, recording data using multiple modalities. The tasks include three stages: visual search, motor response, and attention monitoring. In the visual search stage, participants need to fixate on a central cross; in the motor response stage, participants use their right hand to manipulate a joystick towards the target position (six target positions, corresponding to angles of 30°, 90°, 150°, 210°, 270°, and 330° respectively); in the attention monitoring stage, the system adjusts the time interval based on task performance. The data collection process is shown in Fig. 2. Among these, EEG signals were sampled at 500 Hz, eye movement data at 1000 Hz, and gesture data at 50,000 Hz. To ensure temporal consistency of the multimodal data, Zhang performed temporal alignment preprocessing on the data, using event markers in the EEG data as a reference to perform linear fitting on the time series of the same events in the multimodal data. After feature extraction, a multimodal dataset usable for the 6-class intention recognition task was finally obtained. This paper uses the EEG, eye movement, and gesture data from this dataset for multimodal interaction intention recognition research. After reading the dataset, it was found that the sample sizes for different directions were unbalanced (Direction 4 sample size was only 22), so this section performs data augmentation and preprocessing on the initial data.

Data augmentation

The dataset used in this paper has a limited sample size and class imbalance. Differential processing is performed according to the physiological and physical characteristics of different modalities, expanding the data while preserving the intrinsic relationships between modalities.

Gaussian Noise Injection

Gaussian Noise Injection

Gaussian Noise Injection

Since EEG, eye movement, and gesture signals are susceptible to various noises during acquisition, Gaussian random noise with a mean of 0 and a controllable standard deviation is added to the raw data to simulate environmental noise and device noise during signal acquisition. EEG signals are the weakest and most sensitive, so a Gaussian noise factor of 0.005 is introduced to the EEG features to introduce slight disturbances and enhance the model’s robustness to EEG artifacts. Eye movement and gesture signals introduce Gaussian noise factors of 0.01 and 0.008, respectively.

2.Amplitude Scaling

2.Amplitude Scaling

Amplitude Scaling

To simulate individual differences and signal strength variations, global amplitude scaling is applied to the original data sequences, with the scaling factor randomly selected from a given range. The scaling range for EEG signals is (0.95, 1.05), and for gesture signals, it is (0.9, 1.1). Since the characteristics of eye movement signals between human individuals are not significantly different, this augmentation strategy is not applied to eye movement signals.

3.Feature random dropping

3.Feature random dropping

Feature random dropping

Eye movement data is prone to signal loss during acquisition due to the movement of the subject. Therefore, a feature random dropping strategy is adopted for eye movement signals to simulate signal loss or sensor failure. 5% of the eye movement feature dimensions are randomly dropped, ensuring the integrity of core features such as fixation point coordinates during the process.

After applying the above data augmentation strategies, the dataset size is expanded to twice its original size, helping to enhance the model’s generalization and learning capabilities. The distribution of sample quantities for the six directions after data augmentation is shown in Fig. 3.

Multimodal data preprocessing

To remove noise, standardize features, and extract key information, providing high-quality input for cross-modal fusion, the augmented multimodal dataset is preprocessed, specifically including three stages: differential feature standardization, intelligent feature selection, and feature dimensionality reduction.

Differential feature standardization

Differential feature standardization

Differential feature standardization

Different standardization methods are adopted according to the feature distribution characteristics of different modalities. For EEG signals, RobustScaler is used for standardization. This method uses the median and interquartile range for scaling, is unaffected by extreme outliers, and can better preserve the true distribution of the vast majority of normal EEG signals. Eye movement features use MinMaxScaler to scale all features to the [0, 1] interval, eliminating the influence of dimension, making the model training process more stable and gradient descent more effective. Gesture signals use StandardScaler for standardization. Through centralization and scaling processing, a standard normal distribution with a mean of 0 and a variance of 1 is obtained, providing good input for the model.

2.Intelligent feature selection

2.Intelligent feature selection

Intelligent feature selection

After manual processing of the raw data, redundant and irrelevant features may still exist. Directly inputting them into the model increases computational burden and introduces noise. To address this issue, intelligent feature screening is performed. The relationships between EEG and gesture features and the target intention category are complex, so the mutual information method is used to calculate the mutual information value between each feature and the label, selecting the K features most relevant to the target category. The selection of eye movement features is usually a linear problem, so we use the F-value test to select features, with the number selected being twice the original number of features.

3.Feature dimensionality reductionAfter data augmentation and previous preprocessing operations, the feature dimensions for EEG, eye movement, and gesture are 64, 16, and 32, respectively. To reduce data complexity and model computational resources, and simultaneously remove linear correlations between multimodal features while preserving modal features to the greatest extent, Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is used to reduce the EEG, eye movement, and gesture feature dimensions to 32, 8, and 16, respectively, providing high-quality input for model training and learning.

Modality-specific deep feature extraction networks

A core challenge of multimodal data lies in its inherent heterogeneity—data from different modalities have vastly different statistical properties, spatiotemporal structures, and physical meanings. Directly using a unified, single network structure to process all modalities makes it difficult to fully mine the deepest discriminative information of each modality. To solve this problem, this paper designs dedicated deep feature extraction sub-networks for the three modalities of EEG, eye movement, and gesture, as shown in Table 1. These sub-networks work in parallel, and their design deeply integrates domain knowledge, enabling the maximum extraction of unique patterns in various signals, laying the foundation for subsequent cross-modal fusion.

EEG feature extraction sub-network

EEG signals are characterized by high dimensionality, low signal-to-noise ratio, non-stationarity, and weak amplitude. Their effective features are often hidden in complex temporal dynamics. The EEG feature extraction sub-network designed in this paper is a complex architecture that deeply integrates convolution, recurrent neural networks, and a self-attention mechanism, enabling hierarchical and progressive capture of its spatio-temporal multi-dimensional features. The input to this sub-network is the preprocessed EEG feature vector XEEG (which dimension N is 32). Its core processing flow is as follows: Reshaping and Multi-scale Convolution Fusion: First, the 1D feature vector Xeeg is converted into a 2D sequence structure to adapt to the temporal feature extractor. Subsequently, multi-scale 1D convolutional kernels (kernel sizes 3, 5, 7 respectively) are used in parallel to perform convolution operations on the input data. This achieves simultaneous capture of local temporal patterns under different receptive fields: smaller kernels focus on subtle instantaneous changes, while larger kernels focus on trend features over slightly longer periods. The outputs of the three convolutional paths are fused via a concatenation layer, followed by layer normalization, and a spatial Dropout layer is used to randomly drop entire feature maps to enhance generalization ability and prevent the network from overfitting to specific electrodes or features.Feature Deepening with Depthwise Separable Convolution: To effectively control the number of parameters while increasing feature depth, the network uses depthwise separable convolution for further processing of the fused features. This operation decomposes standard convolution into two steps: depthwise convolution and pointwise convolution, significantly reducing the model’s computational complexity and overfitting risk.Capturing Long-Range Dependencies with Bidirectional LSTM: To model contextual dependencies spanning longer time scales in EEG signals, the high-level features output from the convolution are fed into a Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory network (Bi-LSTM). The forward LSTM captures the influence of the “past” on the “current,” and the backward LSTM captures the influence of the “future” on the “current,” thus generating a representation for each time point that integrates complete contextual information.Focusing on Key Timestamps with Self-Attention Mechanism: Although Bi-LSTM can handle long-range dependencies, its hidden states assign equal importance to different time steps in the sequence. To further strengthen the model’s focus on discriminative time segments, a multi-head self-attention mechanism is introduced. This mechanism allows the model to dynamically calculate the correlation weights between any two time points in the sequence, thereby automatically focusing on key events most relevant to the task and attenuating the influence of noisy periods.Residual Connections and Global Feature Pooling: To avoid the gradient vanishing problem caused by deep networks, the outputs of the Bi-LSTM and the self-attention mechanism are combined via residual connections. Finally, the network uses both global average pooling and global max pooling to compress the variable-length sequence features into fixed-length vectors. The former extracts the overall trend, the latter captures the most salient features, and their concatenation forms the final high-level EEG representation (which dimension is 128).

Gesture feature extraction sub-network

Gesture features describe the geometric and kinematic attributes of the motion trajectory. The core task of its sub-network is to learn the complex nonlinear relationships between these features through a deep fully connected network. The input is the gesture feature vector Xgesture(which dimension K is16). The processing flow is as follows:

Deep Nonlinear Transformation: The gesture feature sub-network consists of a multi-layer deep fully connected network. Each layer includes linear transformation, batch normalization, a nonlinear activation function (ReLU), and dropout operation. Finally, the output of the main path is added element-wise to this projected residual. This residual learning mechanism ensures that the network can at least maintain the information of the original input, preventing network performance from decaying with increasing depth and making it easy to train and optimize.Feature Refinement and Output: The result after residual addition is further refined through a final fully connected layer, ultimately outputting the high-level gesture representation (which dimension is 64).

Cross-modal attention fusion module

After processing by the modality-specific deep feature extraction networks, we obtain three high-level feature representations: EEG feature FEEG (Belonging to the 128 dimensional real number space), eye movement feature Feye (Belonging to the 64 dimensional real number space) and gesture feature Fgesture (Belonging to the 64 dimensional real number space). Traditional multimodal fusion methods like early concatenation or weighted average are too simplistic and cannot effectively capture the complex and dynamic nonlinear interaction relationships between modalities. For this purpose, this paper designs a cross-modal attention fusion module based on the Transformer architecture, as shown in Fig. 4. Its core idea is to let the model automatically learn and reason about the association strength between different modal features, achieving adaptive and selective information fusion.

Dynamic ensemble strategy

In complex and variable application scenarios, a single model may exhibit performance fluctuations due to its inherent randomness (e.g., parameter initialization, training data shuffle order) or have systematic biases towards certain specific types of samples. To improve the final performance, stability, and robustness of the model, this paper proposes a novel dynamic ensemble strategy. The core idea of this strategy is to train multiple base models with excellent performance and diversity. Instead of simply averaging them, different weights are dynamically and discriminatively assigned to each model based on their performance on a validation set, thereby constructing a stronger and more stable meta-model.

Ensemble architecture

Dynamic weight allocation mechanism

Traditional ensemble methods usually assign equal weight to all base models, i.e., 1/M. However, this average allocation strategy ignores the performance differences among base models. The weight allocation strategy of this paper is dynamic weighting, where the weight allocation is determined based on the performance on an independent validation set V = {(X(j) , y(j)}Nj = 1. The performance on V determines the weight allocation.

Let Acci denote the classification accuracy of the i-th base model fi on the validation set V. This paper adopts a dynamic weight allocation strategy based on the Softmax function, converting accuracy into weight. Unlike traditional fixed weight allocation strategies, the core advantage of Softmax is its dynamic learnability, enabling it to automatically evaluate the contribution of multiple modalities and assign corresponding weights based on the input, enhancing the stability of multimodal fusion. Here, T is the temperature parameter, used to control the sharpness of the weight distribution. When T approaches 0, the weight is concentrated on the best-performing model, and the strategy reduces to selecting the optimal model; when T approaches infinity, all weights tend to be equal, and the strategy reduces to simple averaging. By adjusting parameter T (set T < 1 in this paper), the ensemble strategy tends to trust the better-performing models more while not completely discarding the complementary information provided by other models, achieving dynamic adaptation.

Dynamic ensemble strategy

In actual deployment, sensor failure or environmental interference may cause temporary absence or severe quality degradation of a certain modality’s data. The dynamic ensemble strategy has strong robustness in dealing with such situations. This paper enhances model robustness through specialized training that actively simulates modality absence, mainly conducted in the following stages: Training Phase: When training some base models, randomly discard the input of one complete modality, e.g., set the EEG feature vector to zero, forcing the model to rely only on the remaining modalities for prediction.Weight Allocation Phase: During evaluation on the validation set V, not only evaluate each model’s performance on complete data but also specifically evaluate the model’s performance in the absence of a certain modality.Dynamic Decision Phase: During actual inference, the system detects the quality indicators of each modality’s data in real-time, such as signal strength, signal-to-noise ratio, etc. If it is detected that a certain modality’s indicator is below the threshold, then when calculating the final weighted weight wi, the weight of models that performed well on Vmissing (the set with missing modality) is adaptively increased, while the weight of models that heavily rely on the missing modality is reduced.

Dynamic weighting vs. static weighting comparative test

To clearly demonstrate the improvement brought by the adaptive weighting mechanism, we compared three different fusion strategies on the same test set: the proposed dynamic weighting method, which uses Dense(1, sigmoid) with normalization to compute modality weights and softmax to compute path weights; A static weighting method (equal-weight average): we removed the adaptive weight calculation, fixed the weights of the three modalities to [1/3, 1/3, 1/3], and fixed the weights of the three paths to [1/3, 1/3, 1/3]; A static weighting method (manual optimal weights): we performed a grid search on the validation set to find a set of fixed optimal values for the modality weights and path weights (the found fixed weights were [0.4, 0.3, 0.3] and [0.5, 0.3, 0.2]). Finally, the proposed dynamic weighting method improved the accuracy by 5.1% and 3.7% compared to the equal-weight average and the manual optimal weights, respectively, demonstrating the necessity and superiority of dynamically adjusting the fusion strategy according to different input samples. Quantitative comparative experiments clearly demonstrate that our dynamic weighting mechanism brings a statistically significant performance improvement through the adaptive fusion strategy, fully proving the innovative value of this method in addressing the heterogeneity of multimodal data.

Experiment

Experimental design

The real dataset used in this paper is as described in Multimodal Recognition Model section, a multimodal dataset containing EEG, eye movement, and gesture signals. This dataset was collected while monitoring subjects moving towards six target directions, containing signals for six classifications. Therefore, this paper uses this dataset for six-class interaction intention recognition research. Data augmentation and preprocessing operations have been detailed in Multimodal Recognition Model section and will not be repeated here. In the dataset, 80% of the data is used for training, and 20% for testing. 20% of the training set is further divided out as a validation set for hyperparameter tuning and early stopping judgment.

To comprehensively evaluate model performance, this paper adopts various evaluation metrics widely used in classification tasks, including accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score. Accuracy refers to the proportion of correctly classified samples to the total number of samples, providing an intuitive measure of the model’s overall performance. Precision refers to the proportion of samples that are actually positive among all samples predicted as positive, measuring the model’s reliability. Recall refers to the proportion of samples correctly predicted as positive among all true positive samples, measuring the model’s ability to capture positive samples. The F1 score is the harmonic mean of precision and recall, used to comprehensively measure the performance of both, avoiding the limitations of a single metric.

The experiments in this paper were conducted in Python 3.11.5. The optimal hyperparameter combination was determined using a strategy combining grid search and five-fold cross-validation. The finally determined parameters are: decay factor 0.5, patience value 10 epochs, minimum learning rate 1e−7, training batch size 32, training epochs 150. Meanwhile, an early stopping strategy was applied in the experiments, monitoring the validation set accuracy with a patience value of 20 epochs. Baseline experiment

As mentioned earlier, there are no publicly available models that achieve interaction intention recognition by fusing EEG, eye movement, and gesture signals. Therefore, this paper compares our proposed model with traditional methods such as SVM, LSTM, and Random Forest under the same public dataset for baseline testing. The specific baselines are as follows:

SVM: Support Vector Machine, classifies by finding the optimal hyperplane that maximizes the class margin. It adapts to high-dimensional features and has stable generalization under small samples but cannot directly handle temporal dependencies of modalities, requiring manual extraction of temporal features. Baseline testing with SVM verifies whether the model breaks through the limitations of traditional methods in nonlinear modeling efficiency and temporal dependency capture.

LSTM: Long Short-Term Memory network, an improved version of Recurrent Neural Network (RNN). It solves the gradient vanishing problem through cell state and forget, input, and output gate mechanisms, can efficiently capture long-distance temporal dependencies, adapts to temporal data, can dynamically learn intra-modal temporal features, and can fit complex nonlinear relationships. However, it has weak ability to model spatial structural features and is prone to overfitting under small sample conditions. Baseline testing with LSTM verifies whether the model surpasses the limitations of a single LSTM model in cross-modal temporal fusion and dynamic feature interaction.

Random Forest: Random Forest constructs multiple decision trees through Bootstrap sampling and random feature selection, finally outputs results by voting or averaging. It has strong robustness, can handle high-dimensional, missing, and imbalanced data, and can output feature importance to assist in analyzing multimodal contribution. However, this method is essentially a static model, cannot directly capture temporal dependencies, and cannot establish dynamic interactions between modalities. Baseline testing with Random Forest verifies whether the multimodal model breaks through the static limitations of traditional ensemble learning methods in dynamic interaction modeling.

Figure 11 shows the baseline test results. On the same dataset, the multimodal model achieved an intention recognition accuracy of 96.2% and an F1 score of 0.962, outperforming all baseline models. It can be seen from the figure that SVM performance is poor, with an accuracy of only 34.6%, indicating that the kernel method processing capability of this model is insufficient to handle nonlinear, high-dimensional multimodal data and cannot effectively fit complex relationships. Random Forest and LSTM have relatively high accuracy, reflecting that these two methods have certain usability in adapting to multimodal features and processing multimodal data. Their F1 scores also reflect that the comprehensive performance of the two methods is relatively excellent, with pure prediction results and few missed detections. The advantage of the multimodal model on the data verifies the reliability of the multimodal fusion architecture combining self-attention mechanism and dynamic ensemble in processing multimodal data. This model can effectively capture the complementary relationships between modalities, breaking through the performance bottlenecks of traditional models, such as insufficient dynamic interaction ability between modalities and weak ability to process spatial structural features.

Discussion

This paper primarily researches six-class interaction intention recognition based on EEG, eye movement, and gesture signals, proposing a multimodal interaction intention recognition model that integrates a cross-modal self-attention mechanism and a dynamic ensemble learning strategy. Based on a multimodal public dataset, the paper verifies the effectiveness and superiority of the proposed multimodal interaction intention recognition model through three experiments: performance evaluation experiment, baseline experiment, and ablation study.

In the performance evaluation experiment, 80% of the dataset was used for model training, 20% for testing, and 20% of the training set was partitioned as a validation set for hyperparameter tuning and model early stopping judgment. Model performance was comprehensively evaluated using metrics such as accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score. After training and testing, the model achieved a classification accuracy of 96.2% on the public dataset. The recognition accuracy for the six directions was 99%, 93%, 96%, 98%, 91%, and 93%, respectively. The recall rate for each direction was above 90%, demonstrating the model’s good performance and proving the effectiveness of the model architecture. From the model accuracy and loss value change curves, it can be seen that the model accuracy and loss value converged quickly after 20 epochs of training and converged after 40 epochs without overfitting, proving that the model has good generalization ability and stability. Due to inconsistent sample sizes for each direction and different multimodal features for each direction, the model’s recognition rate varies for different directions. The highest recognition accuracy for Direction 0 reached 99%, and the lowest for Direction 4 (91%). From other indicators, the recall rate for Direction 4 is only 77%, indicating a high misjudgment rate for Direction 4. In terms of sample quantity, Direction 4 has the smallest sample size, causing the model not to learn sufficient features during training, easily leading to it being judged as other sample categories with similar features during classification. Since this dataset was collected over six consecutive angular directions, Direction 4 is adjacent to Directions 3 and 5, so the multimodal features corresponding to these three directions are relatively similar, making the decision boundary blurry and prone to category misjudgment. The normalized confusion matrix also proves this, as some samples of Direction 4 were misjudged as categories of Direction 3 and Direction 5. Additionally, misjudgments exist in other directions, reflecting that the model’s ability to define decision boundaries needs improvement. Next, we will address the issue of low model performance in specific directions by introducing class-weighted Focal Loss to force the model to prioritize correcting misclassifications for this category, implementing targeted data augmentation, and enhancing multi-scale feature fusion, among other measures. We projected the multimodal high-dimensional features into a 2D space for visualization using t-SNE to better analyze the quality of feature representation. From the three feature space distribution maps, it can be seen that the distinguishability of EEG, eye movement, and gesture features varies. The high-dimensional features have different clustering tendencies, reflecting the differences between modalities. This difference is one of the key factors limiting multimodal fusion recognition performance. Combined with the cross-modal attention weight heatmap, we can know that the feature distinguishability of a modality determines the strength of its complementarity with other modalities. The eye movement feature, which has the most dispersed spatial distribution, has weak complementarity with EEG and gesture features. This paper uses a multi-head self-attention mechanism to map the features of each modality to a unified public feature space. The weights of each modality can be learned and adjusted, to some extent compensating for the weak complementarity of eye movement features, achieving strong complementarity between multiple modalities, helping to improve the utilization rate of each modality, and thereby improving the accuracy and reliability of interaction intention recognition.

To prove the effectiveness and superiority of the proposed multimodal interaction intention recognition model, this model was compared with other baseline models. The used baselines include SVM, LSTM, and Random Forest, which are representative methods of traditional machine learning, ensemble learning, and neural networks, respectively. The comparison on the same dataset ensures good comparability. Experimental results show that the proposed model has the best recognition performance and stability. The best-performing baseline model is Random Forest, whose recognition accuracy, recall rate, and other parameters all exceed 90%. Compared to Random Forest, the proposed model improves accuracy by 5.2%, which is significant. Random Forest effectively avoids overfitting through Bagging and random feature subspaces, has strong generalization ability, and is good at capturing complex nonlinear relationships. Its ensemble learning and randomization give it powerful multimodal feature processing capabilities, also proving that multimodal features contain effective classification discriminant information. However, Random Forest only considers partial features, cannot process dynamic temporal dependencies, and its model performance is limited. LSTM can handle nonlinear data, but its feature fusion strategy is merely simple concatenation, lacking specialized mechanisms to model inter-modal interaction relationships. Experimental results reflect that deep neural networks have certain potential in handling multimodal problems but require designed specialized mechanisms to improve model performance. SVM has the worst performance because the RBF kernel processing capability is limited. When the feature dimension is high and the sample size is small, SVM struggles to find an effective hyperplane, causing the model to fall into severe overfitting. This result indicates that SVM is not suitable for multimodal fusion tasks with small sample sizes and high feature dimensions. The proposed model includes three parallel dedicated sub-networks for deep feature mining, can extract the temporal features of EEG, eye movement, and gesture to the greatest extent, effectively capturing temporal dependencies among multiple modalities. The cross-modal attention fusion module based on the Transformer architecture enables adaptive learning of inter-modal interaction weights, dynamically mining and fusing nonlinear complementary relationships between modalities, breaking through the limitations of Random Forest and LSTM, which use specific rules to statically assign modal weights. Meanwhile, the model applies a series of data augmentation methods, ensuring the sample size remains at a certain scale while effectively avoiding overfitting problems. The model has good generalization ability. Finally, the dynamic ensemble learning strategy adopted by the model ensures that the modality can maintain good performance in complex scenes, enhancing the model’s robustness and reliability. Based on the above design, the proposed model has the best performance in the multimodal interaction intention recognition task, overcoming the defect of traditional methods struggling to effectively solve multimodal heterogeneity problems, providing new ideas and solutions for processing multimodal data.

The essence of ablation study is to disassemble model modules using the controlled variable method, observe the impact of the disassembled modules on model performance, enhance model interpretability, and provide quantitative basis for model innovation. Through ablation study, this paper compared the intention recognition performance under single modality and the model’s performance after removing the cross-modal attention fusion module and the dynamic ensemble learning strategy, respectively. In the single modality performance comparison, EEG recognition accuracy is the highest, and eye movement modality recognition accuracy is the lowest. This result corresponds to the t-SNE feature space distribution characteristics of the three modalities: the differences of each modality determine their different impacts on intention recognition. EEG signals originate from the cerebral cortex, the source of human intention generation, containing the richest intention information. Therefore, using EEG signals for intention recognition has high feasibility. Gesture signals record the actual execution movement of interaction intention, including motion trajectory and starting point, which are the execution results of intention and contain sufficient information for intention judgment. Eye movement signals reflect attention distribution, which is related to human intention but is not the intention itself. Compared to EEG and gesture signals, the direct correspondence with intention is weaker. The three modalities have different characteristics and different impacts on intention recognition. However, from the experimental results, the accuracy of a single modality is low, indicating that intention recognition through a single modality has limitations and cannot utilize the characteristics of other modalities for complementarity. This result also reflects the necessity of multimodal fusion research. After removing the cross-modal attention fusion module, the model assumes that all modalities and all feature dimensions are equally important. The model degenerates into simple fusion, struggling to handle dynamic and complex multimodal relationships. The interaction intention recognition accuracy decreases by 7.2%, and the model performance significantly decreases. The proposed model does not use simple feature concatenation or voting weighting and other traditional methods when handling relationships between multiple modalities. It proposes a cross-modal attention fusion framework: designing sub-networks to extract the deep features of each modality separately, using a multi-head self-attention mechanism to model the complex relationships between modalities, solving the problem of unreasonable weight allocation caused by multimodal data heterogeneity, and achieving complementarity between modalities. Experimental results prove that this framework is more effective than traditional fusion strategies and more suitable for handling complex multimodal fusion problems. After removing the dynamic ensemble learning module, the model assigns equal weight to all base models, ignoring the performance differences of base models. The model becomes static fusion, struggling to meet the reliability requirements of the model in complex scenes, and the classification accuracy significantly decreases. The proposed model adopts a dynamic ensemble learning strategy, dynamically allocating weights based on the performance of base models on the validation set, and making final decisions through weighted voting, effectively coping with unstable modal signal quality and ensuring the model has strong stability. Additionally, this paper evaluated the data augmentation module. After removing the data augmentation module, the model performance decreased by 5.2%, proving that the data augmentation strategy adopted by the proposed model has good performance, enhancing the model’s generalization ability and robustness in complex scenes.

Conclusion

This paper addresses the problem of multi-class interaction intent recognition by proposing a multimodal interaction intent recognition model based on a cross-modal attention mechanism and a dynamic ensemble learning strategy. It achieves accurate recognition of six-class interaction intents through the effective fusion of EEG, eye movement, and gesture information. The model utilizes the cross-modal attention mechanism to deeply explore complementary information among the modalities and employs the dynamic ensemble learning strategy to adaptively assign modality weights. It achieved a recognition accuracy of 96.2% on a public dataset, demonstrating good usability of the recognition results, with performance significantly surpassing that of single-modality and traditional multimodal recognition methods. The findings provide an extensible theoretical framework for the synergistic processing of heterogeneous multimodal data, which can promote the development of highly robust interactive systems in fields such as brain-computer interfaces and intelligent driving. Furthermore, the dynamic weight allocation and multi-path integration strategies confirm their effectiveness in handling data fluctuations in real-world scenarios, providing methodological support for intent recognition in complex environments. Finally, the high-accuracy recognition of fine-grained gesture directions validates the potential of multimodal learning in overcoming the limitations of single-modality recognition, laying a technical foundation for developing next-generation human-computer interaction paradigms.

This paper provides a novel method for multi-class interaction intent recognition, offering a practical and reliable solution for intent prediction in complex scenarios. However, this study still has some limitations. For instance, the model’s training and validation rely on experimental data of a specific scale, and its generalization capability needs further verification through larger sample sizes and cross-subject evaluation; the current model’s handling of modality absence relies on passive weight adjustment, and future work should introduce explicit missingness detection and generative repair mechanisms to enhance proactive adaptation capability; the cross-modal attention and dynamic ensemble increase computational complexity and have not yet been optimized for the low computational power requirements of edge devices, limiting deployment in portable scenarios. In the future, we will conduct corresponding research to address these shortcomings, promoting the advancement of this research towards better alignment with practical needs.

Rational connectedness for groups of proper projective similitudes
M. Archita, Karim Johannes Becher

Abstract
. For a quadratic form φ \varphi over a field of characteristic different from 2 2 , we study whether its group of proper projective similitudes 𝐏𝐒𝐢𝐦 + ​ ( φ ) {\bf PSim}^{+}(\varphi) is rationally connected (i.e. R R -trivial). We obtain new sufficient conditions in terms of structure properties of φ \varphi . We further provide new examples of quadratic forms φ \varphi belonging to a given power of the fundamental ideal in the Witt ring and such that 𝐏𝐒𝐢𝐦 + ​ ( φ ) {\bf PSim}^{+}(\varphi) is not rationally connected. Keywords: Classical adjoint algebraic group, stably rational, R R -equivalence, quadratic form, hyperbolic, height, splitting pattern, Pfister form, multiquadratic field extension, henselian valuation, cohomological dimension Classification (MSC 2020): 11E04, 11E57, 11E81, 14E08, 20G15



1.
Introduction

Determining whether a variety is rational is a central problem in algebraic geometry.
In this context, the notion of
rational equivalence
(or
R
-equivalence
) was introduced by Y. Manin around 1972 (see
[
14
, Chapter II, §
14
]
).
A systematic study of this notion for linear algebraic groups was then initiated by J.-L. Colliot-Thélène and J.-J. Sansuc
[
4
]
.
Around 1996, A. Merkurjev
[
15
]
and Ph. Gille
[
8
]
studied rational connectedness (also called
R
-triviality
) for the group of proper projective similitudes of a quadratic form or, more generally, an involution on a central simple algebra.
Merkurjev showed that rational connectedness is in this case determined by the quotient of two subgroups of the multiplicative group of the underlying field.
This led to the first examples of adjoint classical groups of type
D
which are not rational.

In the present article, we investigate rational connectedness for certain adjoint groups of type
D
, namely for the groups of proper projective similitudes of quadratic forms.
We use standard notation and terminology in quadratic form theory.
Typical references are
[
5
]
and
[
13
]
.

In this article, the term
quadratic form
always refers to a nondegenerate quadratic form.
We use the sign
\simeq
for isometry.
Let
K
be a field of characteristic different from
2
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in{K}^{\times}
, we denote by
\langle\!\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\rangle\!\rangle
the
n
-fold Pfister form
\langle 1,-a_{1}\rangle\otimes\dots\otimes\langle 1,-a_{n}\rangle
over
K
.
Let
\varphi
be a quadratic form over
K
. We denote by
\mathsf{dim}(\varphi)
its
dimension
(number of variables).
If
n=\mathsf{dim}(\varphi)
,
Then
\varphi\simeq\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\rangle
for some
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in{K}^{\times}
.
We denote by
\mathsf{disc}(K)
the
discriminant of
\varphi
, given as the class
(-1)^{\binom{n}{2}}a_{1}\dots a_{n}{K}^{\times 2}
in the square-class group
{K}^{\times}/{K}^{\times 2}
for an arbitrary diagonalisation
\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\rangle
of
\varphi
.
We further denote by
\mathcal{C}(\varphi)
the
Clifford algebra of
\varphi
and by
\mathcal{C}_{0}(\varphi)

its
even part
.
We denote by
{\bf PSim}(\varphi)
the group of projective similitudes of
\varphi
, viewed as a linear algebraic group, and by
{\bf PSim}^{+}(\varphi)
its connected component of the identity.
The elements of the latter are called
proper projective similitudes of
\varphi
.

If
\mathsf{dim}(\varphi)
is odd, then
{\bf PSim}^{+}(\varphi)
is rational; see
[
15
, p. 200]
.
Therefore the study focuses on forms of even dimension.
If
\mathsf{dim}(\varphi)<6
, then
{\bf PSim}^{+}(\varphi)
is rational, by
[
15
, Prop. 5]
.
The first interesting cases occur in dimension
6
.
When
\mathsf{dim}(\varphi)=6
,
[
15
, Theorem 3]
gives that

{\bf PSim}^{+}(\varphi)
is stably rational if and only if it is rationally connected, if and only if
\mathcal{C}_{0}(\varphi)
is a division algebra.
Using this, one finds examples of
6
-dimensional quadratic forms
\varphi
over many fields
K
for which
{\bf PSim}^{+}(\varphi)
is not rationally connected.

The study of rational connectedness of
{\bf PSim}^{+}(\varphi)
relies crucially on Merkurjev’s characterization in terms of similarity factors of
\varphi
and norms in finite field extensions where
\varphi
becomes hyperbolic.
This characterization will be revisited in
Section

2
.

As a first application we obtain the following variation to an observation by
Merkurjev’s in
[
15
, Prop. 7]
, namely that
{\bf PSim}^{+}(\varphi)
is stably rational and hence rationally connected if
\varphi
is a tensor product of a Pfister form with a quadratic form of odd dimension.

Theorem I

(
2.4
)
.

Let
\psi
be a quadratic form of odd dimension over
K
.
Then
{\bf PSim}^{+}(\varphi\otimes\psi)
is rationally connected if and only if
{\bf PSim}^{+}(\varphi)
is rationally connected.

One central aim of this article is to obtain new sufficient conditions for rational connectedness of
{\bf PSim}^{+}(\varphi)
.
In order to state our results, we need to recall a few terms.

We denote by
\varphi_{\mathsf{an}}
the
anisotropic part
and by
\mathsf{i}(\varphi)
the Witt index of
\varphi
. Hence
\varphi\simeq\varphi_{\mathsf{an}}\perp\mathsf{i}(\varphi)\times\operatorname{\mathbb{H}}
where
\operatorname{\mathbb{H}}
denotes the hyperbolic plane.
For a field extension
L/K
, we denote by
\varphi_{L}
the quadratic form obtained from
\varphi
by extending scalars to
L
.
The set

\mathsf{Sp}(\varphi)=\{\mathsf{i}(\varphi_{L})\mid L/K\mbox{ field extension}\}\subseteq\mathbb{N}

is called the
splitting pattern of
\varphi
, and the natural number

h(\varphi)=|\mathsf{Sp}(\varphi)|-1

is called the
height of
\varphi
; see
[
5
, Sect. 25]
.
Note that
h(\varphi)\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{dim}(\varphi)
, and further
h(\varphi)\leqslant 1
if and only if
\varphi
is Witt equivalent to a non-trivial scaled Pfister form; see
[
5
, Prop. 25.6]
.

In
Section

3
we establish the following result:

Theorem II

(
3.6
,
3.5
&
2.4
)
.

Assume that either
h(\varphi)\leqslant 2
or
\varphi
is Witt equivalent to a sum of two scaled Pfister forms of different dimension.
Then
{\bf PSim}^{+}(\varphi)
is rationally connected.
More generally,
{\bf PSim}^{+}(\varphi\otimes\psi)
is rationally connected for any odd-dimensional quadratic form
\psi
.

II
encompasses two special cases treated by R. Parimala, J.-P. Tignol and R.M. Weiss in
[
17
, Theorem 1.1]
, namely when
\varphi
has trivial discriminant and either
\mathsf{dim}(\varphi)=8
and
\mathcal{C}(\varphi)
is equivalent to a quaternion algebra, or

\mathsf{dim}(\varphi)=12
and
\mathcal{C}(\varphi)
is split.
Either of the two implies that
h(\varphi)\leqslant 2
.
When
\mathsf{dim}(\varphi)=6
, then it follows by
[
15
, Theorem 3]
that
II

gives precisely the cases where
{\bf PSim}^{+}(\varphi)
is rationally connected.
One may ask whether this is equally the case when
\mathsf{dim}(\varphi)=8
.

One may speculate whether it is possible to extend the list in
II
to a full characterization of rational connectedness of
{\bf PSim}^{+}(\varphi)
in terms of structure properties of
\varphi
.
Note that there is no confirmed case of a quadratic form
\varphi
which is a tensor product of two even-dimensional forms and for which
{\bf PSim}^{+}(\varphi)
is not rationally connected.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We denote by
\mathsf{I}^{n}K
the
n
th power of the fundamental ideal
\mathsf{I}K
of the Witt ring of
K
.
We say that the quadratic form
\varphi

belongs to

\mathsf{I}^{n}K
and we write
\varphi\in\mathsf{I}^{n}K
if its Witt equivalence lies in
\mathsf{I}^{n}K
.
Note that
\varphi\in\mathsf{I}^{2}K
if and only if
\varphi
has even dimension and trivial discriminant.

In
Section

4
, extending previous results from
[
12
]
and
[
1
]
on the case
n=2
, we show the following.

Theorem III

(
4.2
)
.

Assume that
\mathsf{I}^{n+1}K(\sqrt{-1})=0
.
Let
\varphi\in\mathsf{I}^{n}K
and
a\in{K}^{\times}
.
Then
a\varphi\simeq\varphi
if and only if
a
is a norm in some finite
2
-extension
L/K
such that
\varphi_{L}
is hyperbolic.

Results of this type shed a light on the limitations to the role of cohomological invariants in determining rational connectedness.

Our other results concern the construction of new examples where
{\bf PSim}^{+}(\varphi)
is not rationally connected.
Ph. Gille
[
8
]
produced examples of quadratic forms
\varphi
of dimension
8
and trivial discriminant such that
{\bf PSim}^{+}(\varphi)
not rationally connected.
A closer look at those examples yields that there
\mathcal{C}(\varphi)
has index
8
.
The following theorem (see
7.5
) produces examples of a different type over the field
K=\mathbb{C}(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})
.
Since this field has cohomological dimension
3
, this sharpens the main result of
[
8
]
.

Theorem IV

(
7.5
&
7.6
)
.

Let
K
be the rational function field in two variables
k(t_{1},t_{2})
over a field
k
with
\mathsf{char}(k)\neq 2
and
|{k}^{\times}/{k}^{\times 2}|\geqslant 8
.
Then there exist
a_{0},a_{1},a_{2}\in{K}^{\times}
such that
{\bf PSim}^{+}(\varphi)
is not rationally connected for the
8
-dimensional quadratic form
\varphi=\langle\!\langle t_{1},t_{2}\rangle\!\rangle\perp-a_{0}\langle\!\langle a_{1}t_{1},a_{2}t_{2}\rangle\!\rangle
over
K
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, N. Bhaskhar
[
3
]
obtained examples of fields
K
with quadratic forms
\varphi\in\mathsf{I}^{n}K
such that
{\bf PSim}^{+}(\varphi)
is not rationally connected.
The construction used in
[
3
]
produces such forms of relatively large dimension.
The following result provides such examples in dimension
2\cdot(2^{n}-1)
, when
n\geqslant 3
.

Theorem V

(
7.9
&
7.10
)
.

Let
K=k(t_{0},\ldots,t_{n})
for a field
k
such that
\mathsf{char}(k)\neq 2
and
|{k}^{\times}/{k}^{\times 2}|\geqslant 4
.
Then there exist elements
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in{K}^{\times}
and a quadratic form
\varphi\in\mathsf{I}^{n}K
such that
{\bf PSim}^{+}(\varphi)
is not rationally connected and
\varphi\perp\operatorname{\mathbb{H}}\simeq\langle\!\langle a_{1}t_{1},\ldots,a_{n}t_{n}\rangle\!\rangle\perp-\langle\!\langle t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}\rangle\!\rangle
.

In particular for
n=3
, since
12
-dimensional forms in
\mathsf{I}^{3}K
are of height at most
2
, we find that
14
is the smallest dimension where forms
\varphi\in\mathsf{I}^{3}K

can occur such that
{\bf PSim}^{+}(\varphi)
is not rationally connected.
Moreover,
V
shows that
II
cannot be extended to cover sums of two scaled Pfister forms in general without requesting them to be of different dimension.

IV
and
V
both follow from a general method, due to Ph. Gille
[
8
]
, to obtain
quadratic forms
\varphi
such that
{\bf PSim}^{+}(\varphi)
is not rationally connected.
It combines the construction of forms over iterated power series fields with a construction of triquadratic extensions with interesting norm conditions, due to J.-P. Tignol
[
21
]
.
We combine it here with more recent constructions of such triquadratic extensions provided by A. Sivatski in
[
19
]
and
[
20
]
, which we restate in
Section

5
.
In
Section

6
, we give a comprehensive account of this method, and in
Section

7
we apply it to obtain our new examples.

While our study here is limited to the quadratic form case, it should be extended to cover adjoint classical groups in general, replacing quadratic forms by algebras with involution.

2.
Merkurjev’s criterion

We now delve a bit more in the setup developed by Merkurjev in
[
15
]
that is at the basis of all results concerning rational connectedness of
{\bf PSim}^{+}(\varphi)
.

For a finite field extension
L/K
, we write

\mathsf{N}_{L/K}:L\to K
for the norm map and we abbreviate

\mathsf{N}_{L/K}^{\ast}=\mathsf{N}_{L/K}({L}^{\times})\,.

For a quadratic form
\varphi
over
K
, we set

\mathsf{G}(\varphi)=\{a\in{K}^{\times}\mid a\varphi\simeq\varphi\}\,,

and for a field extension
K^{\prime}/K
we denote by
\varphi_{K^{\prime}}
the quadratic form
\varphi
when considered over the field
K^{\prime}
.

More generally, we consider
r\in\mathbb{N}
and a tuple
\varphi=(\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{r})
of quadratic forms over
K
.
We set
\mathsf{G}(\varphi)=\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}\mathsf{G}(\varphi_{i})
.
For a field extension
K^{\prime}/K
, we set

\varphi_{K^{\prime}}=((\varphi_{1})_{K^{\prime}},\dots,(\varphi_{r})_{K^{\prime}})\,.

We write
\mathsf{Hyp}(\varphi)
(resp.
\mathsf{Hyp}_{2}(\varphi)
) for the subgroup of
{K}^{\times}
generated by the sets
\mathsf{N}_{L/K}^{\ast}
where
L/K
ranges over all finite field extensions (resp. all finite
2
-extensions) such that
(\varphi_{i})_{L}
hyperbolic for
1\leqslant i\leqslant r
.
We further set

\mathsf{H}(\varphi)={K}^{\times 2}\cdot\mathsf{Hyp}(\varphi)\,.

Trivially, we have the inclusions
\mathsf{Hyp}_{2}(\varphi)\subseteq\mathsf{Hyp}(\varphi)\subseteq\mathsf{H}(\varphi)
and
{K}^{\times 2}\subseteq\mathsf{G}(\varphi)
.
In view of Scharlau’s Norm Principle
[
13
, Chap. VII, Sect. 4]
, we further have that
\mathsf{Hyp}(\varphi)\subseteq\mathsf{G}(\varphi)
and hence

\mathsf{H}(\varphi)\subseteq\mathsf{G}(\varphi)\,.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\varphi
and
\psi
quadratic forms over
K
such that
\mathsf{dim}(\psi)
is odd.
Then
\mathsf{G}(\varphi\otimes\psi)=\mathsf{G}(\varphi)
,
\mathsf{Hyp}(\varphi\otimes\psi)=\mathsf{Hyp}(\varphi)
and
\mathsf{H}(\varphi\otimes\psi)=\mathsf{H}(\varphi)
.

Proof.

Since
\mathsf{dim}(\psi)
is odd, it follows by
[
13
, Chap. VIII, Cor. 8.5]
that,
for any field extension
L/K
and any quadratic form
\rho
over
L
,

\rho\otimes\psi_{L}
is hyperbolic if and only if
\rho
is hyperbolic.
Applying this for arbitrary finite extensions
L/K
and
\rho=\varphi_{L}
yields that
\mathsf{Hyp}(\varphi\otimes\psi)=\mathsf{Hyp}(\varphi)
, whereby
\mathsf{H}(\varphi\otimes\psi)=\mathsf{H}(\varphi)
.
Applying the same fact for an arbitrary
a\in{K}^{\times}
to
L=K
and
\rho=\langle\!\langle a\rangle\!\rangle\otimes\varphi
yields that
\mathsf{G}(\varphi\otimes\psi)=\mathsf{G}(\varphi)
.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
r\in\mathbb{N}
and
a_{1},\dots,a_{r}\in{K}^{\times}
and
M=K(\sqrt{a_{1}},\dots,\sqrt{a_{r}})
be such that
[M:K]=2^{r}
.
Let
\varphi=(\langle\!\langle a_{1}\rangle\!\rangle,\dots,\langle\!\langle a_{r}\rangle\!\rangle)
.
Then we have:

\displaystyle\mathsf{G}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\quad\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}\mathsf{N}_{K(\sqrt{\vphantom{b}a_{i}})/K}^{\ast}

\displaystyle\mathsf{Hyp}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathsf{Hyp}_{2}(\varphi)\,=\,\mathsf{N}_{M/K}^{\ast}

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{K}^{\times 2}\cdot\mathsf{N}_{M/K}^{\ast}

Proof.

We have
\mathsf{G}(\langle\!\langle a_{i}\rangle\!\rangle)=\mathsf{N}_{K(\sqrt{\vphantom{b}a_{i}})/K}^{\ast}
.
This yields the first equality.
For any field extension
K^{\prime}/K
, the forms
\langle\!\langle a_{i}\rangle\!\rangle_{K^{\prime}}
are hyperbolic for
1\leqslant i\leqslant r
if and only if
a_{1},\dots,a_{r}\in{K^{\prime}}^{\times 2}
, which is if and only if
M\subseteq K^{\prime}
. This implies the remaining equalities in the statement.
∎

Let
\varphi
be a quadratic form over
K
.
Its group of proper projective similitudes
{\bf PSim}^{+}(\varphi)
is an example of a connected semisimple adjoint classical linear algebraic group.
Whether this group is rationally connected is characterised in
[
15
]
as follows.

Theorem 2.3

(Merkurjev)
.

The group
{\bf PSim}^{+}(\varphi)
is rationally connected if and only if
\mathsf{G}(\varphi_{K^{\prime}})=\mathsf{H}(\varphi_{K^{\prime}})
holds for every field extension
K^{\prime}/K
.

Proof.

See
[
15
, Theorem 1]
.
∎

We obtain the following consequence.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
\varphi
and
\psi
quadratic forms over
K
such that
\mathsf{dim}(\psi)
is odd.
Then
{\bf PSim}^{+}(\varphi\otimes\psi)
is rationally connected if and only if
{\bf PSim}^{+}(\varphi)
is rationally connected.

Proof.

Consider an arbitrary field extension
K^{\prime}/K
.
By
2.1
, we have

\mathsf{G}(\varphi\otimes\psi)_{K}^{\prime})=\mathsf{G}(\varphi_{K^{\prime}})

and
\mathsf{H}((\varphi\otimes\psi)_{K^{\prime}})=\mathsf{H}(\varphi_{K^{\prime}})
.
In particular the equality
\mathsf{G}((\varphi\otimes\psi)_{K^{\prime}})=\mathsf{H}((\varphi\otimes\psi)_{K^{\prime}})
holds if and only if
\mathsf{G}(\varphi_{K^{\prime}})=\mathsf{H}(\varphi_{K^{\prime}})
.
Having this equivalence for all field extensions
K^{\prime}/K
, the statement follows by
2.3
.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

Merkurjev’s statement
[
15
, Theorem 1]
characterizes more generally rational connectedness for
{\bf PSim}^{+}(A,\sigma)
for any central simple algebra with involution
(A,\sigma)
.
Also the fact used in
2.1
about zero divisors in the Witt ring
\mathsf{W}K
can be extended to that setting. The annihilator in
\mathsf{W}K
of any element of the Witt group of
(A,\sigma)
seen as a
\mathsf{W}K
-module is contained in the fundamental ideal
\mathsf{I}K
.
This can be seen, assuming in the unitary case that
\operatorname{\mathsf{exp}}A\leqslant 2
, by a generic splitting argument based on the results of Karpenko and Tignol in
[
10
]
.
Using this, one can extend
2.4
to the case where
A
is not split.
Since here we bound ourselves to the quadratic form case, we will include details in another article.

Remark 2.6
.

Under the hypotheses of
2.4
, one obtains using
[
16
, Prop. 4.5]
that
{\bf PSim}^{+}(\varphi\otimes\psi)\times\mathbb{A}^{m}
and
{\bf PSim}^{+}(\varphi)\times\mathbb{A}^{m^{\prime}}
are birationally isomorphic for some positive integers
m
and
m^{\prime}
.
This yields an alternative proof of
2.4
.

3.
Sufficient conditions for rational conectedness

A quadratic form
\varphi
over
K
is called
split
if
\mathsf{dim}(\varphi_{\mathsf{an}})\leqslant 1
. For even-dimensional quadratic forms,
split
is synonymous with
hyperbolic
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\varphi
be a quadratic form over
K
and
a\in\mathsf{G}(\varphi)
.
Then
\varphi
is split or there exists a quadratic field extension
L/K
with
\mathsf{i}(\varphi_{L})>\mathsf{i}(\varphi)
and
a\in\mathsf{N}_{L/K}^{\ast}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\varphi
is not split. Since
a\in\mathsf{G}(\varphi)=\mathsf{G}(\varphi_{\mathsf{an}})
, it follows in particular that
\langle\!\langle a\rangle\!\rangle\otimes\varphi_{\mathsf{an}}
is isotropic.
Hence there exists a binary subform
\beta
of
\varphi_{\mathsf{an}}
such that
\langle\!\langle a\rangle\!\rangle\otimes\beta
is isotropic.
Let
d=\mathsf{disc}(\beta)
. Note that
\beta
is anisotropic, and hence
d\notin{K}^{\times 2}
.
It further follows that
\langle\!\langle a,d\rangle\!\rangle
is hyperbolic.
Set
L=K(\sqrt{d})
. Then
L/K
is a quadratic field extension and
a\in\mathsf{N}_{L/K}^{\ast}
.
Furthermore,
(\varphi_{\mathsf{an}})_{L}
is isotropic, whereby
\mathsf{i}(\varphi_{L})>\mathsf{i}(\varphi)
.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\pi
be a Pfister form over
K
.
For every
a\in\mathsf{G}(\pi)\smallsetminus{K}^{\times 2}
, there exists a field extension
L/K
with
[L:K]\leqslant 2
such that
\pi_{L}
is hyperbolic and
a\in\mathsf{N}^{\ast}_{L/K}
. In particular
\mathsf{G}(\pi)=\mathsf{Hyp}_{2}(\pi)=\mathsf{H}(\pi)
.

Proof.

Consider
a\in\mathsf{G}(\pi)\smallsetminus{K}^{\times 2}
.
Hence
a\in\mathsf{G}(\pi)
and as
a\notin{K}^{\times 2}
, we have
\mathsf{dim}(\pi)>1
.
If
\pi
is hyperbolic, then we can choose
L=K
.
Assume that
\pi
is not hyperbolic.
By
3.1
, we can find a quadratic field extension
L/K
such that
\pi_{L}
is isotropic and
a\in\mathsf{N}_{L/K}^{\ast}
.
Since
\pi
is a Pfister form, it follows that
\pi_{L}
is hyperbolic.
This argument shows in particular that
\mathsf{G}(\pi)\subseteq\mathsf{Hyp}_{2}(\pi)
.
Since
\mathsf{Hyp}_{2}(\pi)\subseteq\mathsf{H}(\pi)\subseteq\mathsf{G}(\pi)
, this completes the proof.
∎

A field extension
L/K
is called
biquadratic
if
[L:K]=4
and
L=K(\sqrt{a},\sqrt{b})
for certain
a,b\in{K}^{\times}
.
The following well-known statement is called in
[
3
]
the
biquadratic norm trick
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
L_{1}/K
and
L_{2}/K
two linearly disjoint quadratic extensions of
K
and let
L=L_{1}\otimes_{K}L_{2}
.
Then
{K}^{\times 2}\cdot\mathsf{N}_{L/K}^{\ast}=\mathsf{N}_{L_{1}/K}^{\ast}\cap\mathsf{N}_{L_{2}/K}^{\ast}
.

Proof.

See e.g.
[
17
, Lemma 2.1]
for a proof.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\pi
and
\rho
be quadratic forms over
K
such that

\mathsf{dim}(\pi)<2^{m+1}
and
\rho\in\mathsf{I}^{m+1}K
.
Then
\mathsf{G}(\pi\perp\rho)=\mathsf{G}(\pi)\cap\mathsf{G}(\rho)
.

Proof.

Clearly,
\mathsf{G}(\pi)\cap\mathsf{G}(\rho)\subseteq\mathsf{G}(\pi\perp\rho)
.
To show the converse inclusion, consider
a\in\mathsf{G}(\pi\perp\rho)
arbitrary.
Then
\langle\!\langle a\rangle\!\rangle\otimes\pi\perp\langle\!\langle a\rangle\!\rangle\otimes\rho
is hyperbolic.
Hence
\langle\!\langle a\rangle\!\rangle\otimes\pi\in I^{m+2}K
.
Since
\mathsf{dim}(\langle\!\langle a\rangle\!\rangle\otimes\pi)<2^{m+2}
, we conclude from the Arason-Pfister Hauptsatz
[
5
, Theorem 23.7]
that
\langle\!\langle a\rangle\!\rangle\otimes\pi
is hyperbolic, whereby
a\in\mathsf{G}(\pi)
. As
a\in\mathsf{G}(\pi\perp\rho)
, we conclude that
a\in\mathsf{G}(\rho)
, whereby
a\in\mathsf{G}(\pi)\cap\mathsf{G}(\rho)
.
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\varphi
be a quadratic form over
K
which is Witt equivalent to a sum of two scaled Pfister forms over
K
of different dimension.
Then
\mathsf{G}(\varphi)=\mathsf{H}(\varphi)
and
{\bf PSim}^{+}(\varphi)
is rationally connected.

Proof.

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
\varphi
is Witt equivalent to
\pi\perp\rho
for an
m
-fold Pfister form
\pi
and an
n
-fold Pfister form
\rho
over
K
.
It follows by
3.4
that
\mathsf{G}(\varphi)=\mathsf{G}(\pi)\cap\mathsf{G}(\rho)
.
Note that
{K}^{\times 2}\mathsf{Hyp}_{2}(\varphi)\subseteq\mathsf{H}(\varphi)\subseteq\mathsf{G}(\varphi)
. To show the opposite inclusion,
consider an element
a\in\mathsf{G}(\varphi)\smallsetminus{K}^{\times 2}
.
Then
a\in\mathsf{G}(\pi)\cap\mathsf{G}(\rho)
.
Since
\pi
and
\rho
are scaled Pfister forms, it follows by
3.2
that there exist field extensions
L/K
and
L^{\prime}/K

of degree at most
2
such that
\pi_{L}
and
\rho_{L^{\prime}}
are hyperbolic and
a\in\mathsf{N}_{L/K}^{\ast}\cap\mathsf{N}_{L^{\prime}/K}^{\ast}
.
In particular, in view of
3.3
there exists a multiquadratic field extension
M/K
with
[M:K]\leqslant 4
such that
\varphi_{M}
is hyperbolic and
a\in{K}^{\times 2}\cdot\mathsf{N}^{\ast}_{M/K}
.
This shows that
\mathsf{G}(\varphi)\subseteq{K}^{\times 2}\mathsf{Hyp}_{2}(\varphi)
.
Hence we have
\mathsf{G}(\varphi)={K}^{\times 2}\mathsf{Hyp}_{2}(\varphi)=\mathsf{H}(\varphi)
.

Note that the hypothesis on
\varphi
is stable under field extensions. Hence we obtain also for every field extension
K^{\prime}/K
that
\mathsf{G}(\varphi_{K^{\prime}})=\mathsf{H}(\varphi_{K^{\prime}})
.
By
2.3
, this shows that
{\bf PSim}^{+}(\varphi)
is rationally connected.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\varphi
be a quadratic form over
K
such that
h(\varphi)\leqslant 2
.
Then
\mathsf{G}(\varphi)=\mathsf{H}(\varphi)={K}^{\times 2}\mathsf{Hyp}_{2}(\varphi)
and
{\rm\bf PSim}^{+}(\varphi)
is rationally connected.

Proof.

Since
{K}^{\times 2}\mathsf{Hyp}_{2}(\varphi)\subseteq\mathsf{H}(\varphi)\subseteq\mathsf{G}(\varphi)
holds in general, we need to show that
\mathsf{G}(\varphi)\subseteq{K}^{\times 2}\mathsf{Hyp}_{2}(\varphi)
.
We may assume that
\mathsf{dim}(\varphi)
is even.
If
\varphi
is hyperbolic, then
\mathsf{G}(\varphi)={K}^{\times}=\mathsf{Hyp}_{2}(\varphi)
. Assume that
\varphi
is not hyperbolic.
Consider an element
a\in\mathsf{G}(\varphi)
.
By
3.1
, there exists a quadratic field extension
L/K
such that
\mathsf{i}(\varphi_{L})>\mathsf{i}(\varphi)
and
a\in\mathsf{N}_{L/K}^{\ast}
.
Let
d\in{K}^{\times}\smallsetminus{K}^{\times 2}
be such that
L=K(\sqrt{d})
.
By
[
5
, Cor. 22.12]
, we can write
\varphi_{\mathsf{an}}\simeq\langle\!\langle d\rangle\!\rangle\otimes\vartheta\perp\psi
for some quadratic forms
\vartheta
and
\psi
over
K
such that
\psi_{L}
is anisotropic. Then
(\varphi_{L})_{\mathsf{an}}=\psi_{L}
.
We have that
a\in\mathsf{G}(\varphi)\cap\mathsf{G}(\langle\!\langle d\rangle\!\rangle)\subseteq\mathsf{G}(\psi)
and
\psi
is even-dimensional.
If
\psi
is trivial (
0
-dimensional), then
\varphi_{L}
is hyperbolic, whereby
a\in\mathsf{H}(\varphi)
.
Assume that
\psi
is not trivial.
By
3.1
, there exists a quadratic extension
L^{\prime}/K
such that
\psi_{L^{\prime}}
is isotropic and
a\in\mathsf{N}_{L^{\prime}/K}^{\ast}
.
Since
\psi_{L}
is anisotropic, it follows that
L/K
and
L^{\prime}/K
are linearly disjoint.
We set
M=L\otimes_{K}L^{\prime}
.
By
3.3
, we have that
\mathsf{N}_{L/K}^{\ast}\cap\mathsf{N}_{L^{\prime}/K}^{\ast}={K}^{\times 2}\cdot\mathsf{N}_{M/K}^{\ast}
.
Since
\psi_{L^{\prime}}
is isotropic, so is
\psi_{M}
.
Since
\psi_{L}=(\varphi_{L})_{\mathsf{an}}
, we obtain that
\mathsf{i}(\varphi_{M})>\mathsf{i}(\varphi_{L})>\mathsf{i}(\varphi)
.
As
h(\psi)\leqslant 2
, it follows that
\varphi_{M}
is hyperbolic.
Hence

a\in\mathsf{N}_{L/K}^{\ast}\cap\mathsf{N}_{L^{\prime}/K}={K}^{\times 2}\cdot\mathsf{N}_{M/K}^{\ast}\subseteq{K}^{\times 2}\mathsf{Hyp}_{2}(\varphi)
.
This argument shows that
\mathsf{G}(\varphi)\subseteq{K}^{\times 2}\mathsf{Hyp}_{2}(\varphi)
.
Therefore we have the equalities
\mathsf{G}(\varphi)={K}^{\times 2}\mathsf{Hyp}_{2}(\varphi)=\mathsf{H}(\varphi)
.

For any field extension
K^{\prime}/K
, using that
h(\varphi_{K^{\prime}})\leqslant h(\varphi)\leqslant 2
, we obtain that
\mathsf{G}(\varphi_{K^{\prime}})=\mathsf{H}(\varphi_{K^{\prime}})
.
Having this for all field extensions, we conclude by
2.3
that
{\bf PSim}^{+}(\varphi)
is rationally connected.
∎

Theorem 3.7

(Karpenko)
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and consider a quadratic form
\varphi\in\mathsf{I}^{n}K
.
Then

\mathsf{dim}(\varphi)\geqslant(2^{h(\varphi)}-1)\cdot(2^{n+1-h(\varphi)})\,.

Proof.

This follows from
[
9
, Cor. 6.2]
.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\varphi
be a quadratic form in
\mathsf{I}^{n}K
such that
\mathsf{dim}(\varphi)<7\cdot 2^{n-2}
. Then

{\bf PSim}^{+}(\varphi)
is rationally connected.

Proof.

This is immediate from
3.7
and
3.6
.
∎

Conjecture 3.9
.

For every integer
n\geqslant 3
, there exists a field
L
such that
\mathsf{char}(L)\neq 2
and a quadratic form
\varphi\in\mathsf{I}^{n}L
with

.

4.
Conditions on powers of the fundamental ideal

In this section, we extend the results from
[
1
, Sect. 4]
concerning quadratic forms.
There, fields of virtual cohomological dimension
2
are treated.
This condition implies that
\mathsf{I}^{3}K(\sqrt{-1})=0
, so
[
1
, Cor. 4.3 & Theorem 4.4]
are recovered for
n=2
from the two results below, respectively.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}\smallsetminus\{0\}
be such that
\mathsf{I}^{n+1}K(\sqrt{-1})=0
.
Let
\varphi
be torsion form over
K
with
\varphi\in\mathsf{I}^{n}K
.
Then for every
a\in{K}^{\times}
, there there exists a finite
2
-extension
L/K
such that
\varphi_{L}
is hyperbolic and
a\in\mathsf{N}_{L/K}^{\ast}
.
In particular
\mathsf{Hyp}_{2}(\varphi)={K}^{\times}
.

Proof.

Consider
a\in{K}^{\times}
.
We prove by induction on
\mathsf{dim}(\varphi_{\mathsf{an}})
that
a\in\mathsf{N}_{L/K}^{\ast}
for some finite
2
-extension
L/K
, whereby
a\in\mathsf{Hyp}(\varphi)
.
Note that
\mathsf{dim}(\varphi)
is even, because
\varphi\in\mathsf{I}^{n}K\subseteq\mathsf{I}K
.
If
\mathsf{dim}(\varphi_{\mathsf{an}})=0
, then
\varphi
is hyperbolic, and the claim holds with
L=K
.
Assume now that
\mathsf{dim}(\varphi_{\mathsf{an}})>0
.
As
\mathsf{I}^{n+1}K(\sqrt{-1})=0
, we have by
[
5
, Cor. 35.27]
that
\mathsf{I}^{n+1}K
is torsion-free.
Since
\varphi\in\mathsf{I}^{n}K
and
\varphi
is torsion,
\langle\!\langle a\rangle\!\rangle\otimes\varphi
is hyperbolic, whereby
a\in\mathsf{G}(\varphi)
.
By
3.1
, there exists a quadratic field extension
K^{\prime}/K
and some
a^{\prime}\in{K^{\prime}}^{\times}
such that
\mathsf{i}(\varphi_{K^{\prime}})>\mathsf{i}(\varphi)
and
a=\mathsf{N}_{K^{\prime}/K}(a^{\prime})
. Hence
\mathsf{dim}((\varphi_{K^{\prime}})_{\mathsf{an}})<\mathsf{dim}(\varphi_{\mathsf{an}})
, and we have
\varphi_{K^{\prime}}\in\mathsf{I}^{n}K^{\prime}
.
By
[
5
, Cor. 35.8]
, since
\mathsf{I}^{n+1}K(\sqrt{-1})=0
, we have that
\mathsf{I}^{n+1}K^{\prime}(\sqrt{-1})=0
.
Therefore, by the induction hypothesis, there exists a finite
2
-extension
L/K^{\prime}
such that
(\varphi_{K^{\prime}})_{L}
is hyperbolic and
a^{\prime}\in\mathsf{N}_{L/K^{\prime}}^{\ast}
. Then
\varphi_{L}
is hyperbolic,
L/K
is a finite
2
-extension and
a=\mathsf{N}_{K^{\prime}/K}(a^{\prime})\in\mathsf{N}_{K^{\prime}/K}(\mathsf{N}_{L/K^{\prime}}^{\ast})=\mathsf{N}_{L/K}^{\ast}
.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume that
\mathsf{I}^{n+1}K(\sqrt{-1})=0
.
Let
\varphi
be a quadratic form over
K
.
If
\varphi\equiv\pi\,\mathsf{mod}\,\mathsf{I}^{n}K
for a scaled Pfister form
\pi
over
K
, then

\mathsf{G}(\varphi)=\mathsf{H}(\varphi)
.

Proof.

If
\mathsf{dim}(\varphi)
is odd, then
\mathsf{G}(\varphi)={K}^{\times 2}=\mathsf{H}(\varphi)
.
Assume now that
\mathsf{dim}(\varphi)
is even and that, for some
r\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a scaled
r
-fold Pfister form
\pi
over
K
such that

\varphi\equiv\pi\,\mathsf{mod}\,\mathsf{I}^{n}K
.
We can make this choice in such way that either
r<n
or
\pi
is hyperbolic.
Then
\mathsf{G}(\varphi)\subseteq\mathsf{G}(\pi)
, by
3.4
.

Since
\mathsf{H}(\varphi)\subseteq\mathsf{G}(\varphi)
holds unconditionally, it suffices to show the opposite inclusion.
Consider
a\in\mathsf{G}(\varphi)
.
Hence
\langle\!\langle a\rangle\!\rangle\otimes\varphi
is hyperbolic, and it follows in particular that
\mathsf{sign}_{P}(\varphi)=0
for every ordering
P
of
K
containing
-a
.
Let
K^{\prime}=K(\sqrt{-a})
.
Note that
a\in\mathsf{N}^{\ast}_{K^{\prime}/K}
.
Since
-a\in{K^{\prime}}^{\times 2}
, it follows that
\mathsf{sign}_{P^{\prime}}\varphi_{K^{\prime}}=0
for every ordering
P^{\prime}
of
K^{\prime}
.
We conclude by Pfister’s Local-Global Principle
[
13
, Chap. VIII, Theorem 3.2]
that
\varphi_{K^{\prime}}
is a torsion form over
K^{\prime}
.
Since
\pi
is a scaled Pfister form and
a\in\mathsf{G}(\varphi)\subseteq\mathsf{G}(\pi)
, we obtain by
3.2
that there exists an extension
K^{\prime\prime}/K
with
[K^{\prime\prime}:K]\leqslant 2
such that
\pi_{K^{\prime\prime}}
is hyperbolic and
a\in\mathsf{N}_{K^{\prime\prime}/K}^{\ast}
.
Let
L=K^{\prime\prime}(\sqrt{-a})
.
By
3.3
, since
a\in\mathsf{N}^{\ast}_{K^{\prime}/K}\cap\mathsf{N}^{\ast}_{K^{\prime\prime}/K}
, there exists
a^{\prime}\in{L}^{\times}
such that
a\in{K}^{\times 2}\mathsf{N}_{L/K}(a^{\prime})
.
Furthermore,
\varphi_{L}
is a torsion form and
\pi_{L}
is hyperbolic, whereby
\varphi_{L}\in\mathsf{I}^{n}L
.
Since
\mathsf{I}^{n+1}K(\sqrt{-1})=0
, it follows by
[
5
, Cor. 35.8]
(applied twice) that
\mathsf{I}^{n+1}L(\sqrt{-1})=0
.
Since
\varphi_{L}\in\mathsf{I}^{n}L
,
4.1
yields that
a^{\prime}\in\mathsf{N}_{L^{\prime}/L}^{\ast}
for a finite
2
-extension
L^{\prime}/L
such that
\varphi_{L^{\prime}}
is hyperbolic.
We conclude that
a\in{K}^{\times 2}\mathsf{N}_{L^{\prime}/K}^{\ast}\subseteq\mathsf{H}(\varphi)
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Note that every quadratic form over
K
is congruent to a scaled Pfister form modulo
\mathsf{I}^{2}K
.
So for
n=2
,
4.2
says that, if
\mathsf{I}^{3}K(\sqrt{-1})=0
, then
\mathsf{G}(\varphi)=\mathsf{H}(\varphi)
for every quadratic form
\varphi
over
K
.

5.
Multiquadratic field extensions

Let
L/K
be a finite multiquadratic field extension of
K
, that is,
a finite Galois extension with an elementary
2
-abelian Galois group.
Let
\widetilde{\mathsf{N}}^{\ast}_{L/K}
denote the intersection of the subgroups
\mathsf{N}^{\ast}_{K^{\prime}/K}
where
K^{\prime}/K
ranges over quadratic subextensions of
L/K
.
In the context of the study of
2
-torsion elements in the Brauer group, Tignol and Gille considered the quotient group

\Lambda(L/K)=\widetilde{\mathsf{N}}^{\ast}_{L/K}/{K}^{\times 2}\mathsf{N}_{L/K}^{\ast}\,.

For a set
S
we denote by
\mathcal{P}(S)
the power set of
S
.
Given
r\in\mathbb{N}
, an
r
-tuple
(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})\in K^{r}
and a subset
I\subseteq\{1,\dots,r\}
, we set

a_{I}=\prod_{i\in I}a_{i}\,.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
r\in\mathbb{N}
,
a_{1},\dots,a_{r}\in{K}^{\times}
and
L=K(\sqrt{a_{1}},\dots,\sqrt{a_{r}})
.
Let
\varphi=(\langle\!\langle a_{1}\rangle\!\rangle,\dots,\langle\!\langle a_{r}\rangle\!\rangle)
.
Then
\widetilde{\mathsf{N}}^{\ast}_{L/K}=\mathsf{G}(\varphi)
,

\mathsf{N}^{\ast}_{L/K}=\mathsf{Hyp}(\varphi)=\mathsf{Hyp}_{2}(\varphi)
,

{K}^{\times 2}\mathsf{N}^{\ast}_{L/K}=\mathsf{H}(\varphi)

and

\Lambda(L/K)=\mathsf{G}(\varphi)/\mathsf{H}(\varphi)\,.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}(\{1,\dots,r\})
.
The quadratic subextensions of
L/K
are given by
K(\sqrt{a_{I}})/K
with
I\in\mathcal{P}
.
For
I\in\mathcal{P}
, we have that
\mathsf{N}^{\ast}_{K(\sqrt{a_{I}})/K}=\mathsf{G}(\langle\!\langle a_{I}\rangle\!\rangle)
and
\bigcap_{i\in I}\mathsf{G}(\langle\!\langle a_{i}\rangle\!\rangle)\subseteq\mathsf{G}(\langle\!\langle a_{I}\rangle\!\rangle)=\mathsf{N}^{\ast}_{K(\sqrt{a_{I}})/K}
.
Hence
\widetilde{N}^{\ast}_{L/K}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}\mathsf{G}(\langle\!\langle a_{i}\rangle\!\rangle)=\mathsf{G}(\varphi)
.
Note that
\langle\!\langle a_{I}\rangle\!\rangle_{L}
is hyperbolic for any
I\in\mathcal{P}
.
On the other hand, for any field extension
N/K
, we have
that
\langle\!\langle a_{1}\rangle\!\rangle_{N},\dots,\langle\!\langle a_{r}\rangle\!\rangle_{N}
are hyperbolic if and only if
a_{1},\dots,a_{r}\in{N}^{\times 2}
, if and only if
L\subseteq N
.
Hence
\mathsf{Hyp}(\varphi)=\mathsf{Hyp}_{2}(\varphi)=\mathsf{N}^{\ast}_{L/K}
.
Now the other equalities now follow from the definitions of
\mathsf{H}(\varphi)
and
\Lambda(L/K)
.
∎

A quadratic extension is a multiquadratic extension of degree
2
.
Note that a biquadratic extension is a multiquadratic extension of degree
4
.
A multiquadratic extension of degree
8
is called a
triquadratic extension
.
Trivially we have
\Lambda(L/K)=0
for any quadratic field extension
L/K
, and it follows from
3.3
that the same is true for every biquadratic field extension
L/K
.
The smallest degree where
\Lambda(L/K)\neq 0
can occur for a multiquadratic field extension
L/K
is
8
, that is, for a triquadratic extension.
The first examples were constructed by J.-P. Tignol
[
21
]
.
A fairly general construction of multiquadratic extensions
L/K
with
[L:K]=2^{r}
for any
r\geqslant 3
with
\Lambda(L/K)\neq 0
can be derived from a result by A. Sivatski
[
19
]
concerning the case
r=3
.

Proposition 5.2

(Sivatski)
.

Let
K=k(t)
for a field
k
with
\mathsf{char}(k)\neq 2
.
Let
a_{1},a_{2}\in{k}^{\times}
be such that
[k(\sqrt{a_{1}},\sqrt{a_{2}}):k]=4
and
\mathsf{N}_{k(\sqrt{a_{1}})/k}^{\ast}\cap\mathsf{N}_{k(\sqrt{a_{2}})/k}^{\ast}\not\subseteq{k}^{\times 2}
. Then for any integer
r\geqslant 3
, there exist a multiquadratic field extension
L/K
with
a_{1},a_{2}\in{L}^{\times 2}
,
[L:K]=2^{r}
and
\Lambda(L/K)\neq 0
.

Proof.

We fix
d\in(\mathsf{N}_{k(\sqrt{a_{1}})/k}^{\ast}\cap\mathsf{N}_{k(\sqrt{a_{2}})/k}^{\ast})\smallsetminus{k}^{\times 2}
and
a_{3}=t^{2}-4d
.
Since
[k(\sqrt{a_{1}},\sqrt{a_{2}}):k]=4
, we have that
k
is infinite, so we may fix
c_{3},\dots,c_{r}\in{k}^{\times}
with
c_{3}=1
such that
c_{3}^{2},\dots,c_{r}^{2}
are distinct.
We set
a_{i}=t^{2}-4dc_{i}^{2}
for
3\leqslant i\leqslant r
and
L=K(\sqrt{a_{1}},\dots,\sqrt{a_{r}})
.
Then
d\in\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}\mathsf{N}^{\ast}_{K(\sqrt{a_{i}})/K}=\widetilde{\mathsf{N}}^{\ast}_{L/K}
, and
since
a_{3},\dots,a_{r}
are distinct monic irreducible quadratic polynomials in
k[t]
, we
obtain that
[L:K]=2^{r}
.
By
[
19
, Cor. 10]
, we have that

d\notin{K}^{\times 2}\mathsf{N}^{\ast}_{K(\sqrt{a_{1}},\sqrt{a_{2}},\sqrt{a_{3}})/K}
.
Hence
d\notin{K}^{\times 2}\mathsf{N}^{\ast}_{L/K}
, and
we conclude that
\Lambda(L/K)\neq 0
.
∎

A. Sivatski also showed that, for every triquadratic extension, the group
\Lambda
is nontrivial at least in a weak sense, namely after combining the extension with a linearly disjoint extension of the base field.

Theorem 5.3

(Sivatski)
.

Let
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\in{K}^{\times}
with
[K(\sqrt{a_{1}},\sqrt{a_{2}},\sqrt{a_{3}}):K]=8
.
Then there exists a field extension
K^{\prime}/K
such that

[K^{\prime}(\sqrt{a_{1}},\sqrt{a_{2}},\sqrt{a_{3}}):K^{\prime}]=8
and
\Lambda(K^{\prime}(\sqrt{a_{1}},\sqrt{a_{2}},\sqrt{a_{3}})/K^{\prime})\neq 0
.

Proof.

See
[
20
, Theorem 5.5]
.
∎

6.
Hyperbolic norm groups over henselian valued fields

Let
v
be a valuation on
K
.
We denote by
\mathcal{O}_{v}
the corresponding valuation ring, by
\mathfrak{m}_{v}
the maximal ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{v}
, by
Kv
the residue field
\mathcal{O}_{v}/\mathfrak{m}_{v}
and by

vK
the value group
v({K}^{\times})
, which is an ordered abelian group with additive notation.
We say that
v
is
henselian
if
every polynomial
f\in\mathcal{O}_{v}[X]
having a simple root in
Kv
has a root in
K
, or equivalently, if
v
extends uniquely to a valuation on any algebraic field extension of
K
.

In the sequel, let
v
be a henselian valuation on
K
with
\mathsf{char}(Kv)\neq 2
.

Lemma 6.1
.

For
t\in{K}^{\times}
with
v(t)\notin 2vK
, we have
\mathsf{N}_{K(\sqrt{t})/K}^{\ast}={K}^{\times 2}\cup t{K}^{\times 2}
.

Proof.

Since
v
is henselian and
\mathsf{char}(Kv)\neq 2
, we have
1+\mathfrak{m}_{v}\subseteq{K}^{\times 2}
.
Let
t\in{K}^{\times}
be such that
v(t)\notin 2vK
.
For
x,y\in K
not both zero, we obtain that
v(x^{2})=2v(x)\neq v(t)+2v(y)=v(y^{2}t)
, whereby
v(x^{2})<v(ty^{2})
or
v(x^{2})>v(ty^{2})
and thus
x^{2}-ty^{2}\subseteq{K}^{\times 2}(1+\mathfrak{m}_{v})\cup-t{K}^{\times 2}(1+\mathfrak{m}_{v})={K}^{\times 2}\cup t{K}^{\times 2}
.
Therefore
\mathsf{N}_{K(\sqrt{t})/K}^{\ast}={K}^{\times 2}\cup-t{K}^{\times 2}
.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\varphi
be a quadratic form over
K
and
t\in\mathsf{G}(\varphi)
be such that

v(t)\notin 2vK
. Then
\varphi_{K(\sqrt{-t})}
is hyperbolic.

Proof.

Let
K^{\prime}=K(\sqrt{-t})
and
K^{\prime\prime}=K(\sqrt{t})
.
In view of
6.1
, we have that

\mathsf{N}_{K^{\prime}/K}^{\ast}={K}^{\times 2}\cup t{K}^{\times 2}
and
\mathsf{N}_{K^{\prime\prime}/K}^{\ast}={K}^{\times 2}\cup-t{K}^{\times 2}
.
Since
\mathsf{G}(\varphi)=\mathsf{G}(\varphi_{\mathsf{an}})
, we may replace
\varphi
by
\varphi_{\mathsf{an}}
and assume that
\varphi
is anisotropic.
Suppose first that
\mathsf{dim}(\varphi)=2
.
Then
\varphi\simeq\langle a,-ad\rangle
for some
a,d\in{K}^{\times}
.
Since
\langle 1,-t\rangle\otimes\varphi
is hyperbolic and
\varphi
is anisotropic, we obtain that
d\in\mathsf{N}^{\ast}_{K^{\prime\prime}/K}\smallsetminus{K}^{\times 2}
.
Hence
d{K}^{\times 2}=t{K}^{\times 2}
, whereby
\varphi_{K^{\prime}}
is hyperbolic.
In the general case, we obtain by
[
6
, Cor. 2.3]
that
\varphi\simeq\beta_{1}\perp\ldots\perp\beta_{r}
for
r=\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{dim}(\varphi)
and certain binary quadratic forms
\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{r}
over
K
such that
t\in\mathsf{G}(\beta_{i})
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant r
.
By the
2
-dimensional case we obtain that
(\beta_{i})_{K^{\prime}}
is hyperbolic for
1\leqslant i\leqslant r
, whereby
\varphi_{K^{\prime}}
is hyperbolic.
∎

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
\varphi
be a quadratic form over
K
such that
v(\mathsf{G}(\varphi))\not\subseteq 2vK
.
Then

\mathsf{G}(\varphi)=\mathsf{H}(\varphi)=\mathsf{Hyp}_{2}(\varphi)\,.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
\mathsf{G}(\varphi)\subseteq\mathsf{Hyp}_{2}(\varphi)
.
Consider
a\in\mathsf{G}(\varphi)
arbitrary.
By the hypothesis, there exists some
t\in\mathsf{G}(\varphi)
with
v(t)\notin 2vK
.
If
v(a)\notin 2vK
, then
6.2
yields that
\varphi_{K(\sqrt{-a})}
is hyperbolic, whereby
a\in\mathsf{N}_{K(\sqrt{-a})/K}^{\ast}\subseteq\mathsf{Hyp}_{2}(\varphi)
.
Assume now that
v(a)\in 2vK
.
Then
t,at\in\mathsf{G}(\varphi)
and
v(t),v(at)\notin 2vK
. Hence
\varphi_{K(\sqrt{-t})}
and
\varphi_{K(\sqrt{-at})}
are both hyperbolic, by
6.2
.
Therefore
a=t^{-1}at\in\mathsf{N}_{K^{\prime}(\sqrt{-t})}^{\ast}\cdot\mathsf{N}_{K^{\prime}(\sqrt{-at})}^{\ast}\subseteq\mathsf{Hyp}_{2}(\varphi)
.
∎

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
L/K
a finite field extension.
Let
v^{\prime}
be the unique valuation extension of
v
to
L
.
Assume that

7.

We will now construct, for some given

, a quadratic form
\rho\in\mathsf{I}^{n}K
for some field
K
such that
{\bf PSim}^{+}(\rho)
is not rationally connected.
To obtain such examples, we will take an iterated power series field
K=k(\!(t_{1})\!)\ldots(\!(t_{r})\!)
in
r
variables over some base field
k
and build a form

\rho=\mathop{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{2.1}{$\displaystyle\perp$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{1.5}{$\textstyle\perp$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{1.5}{$\scriptstyle\perp$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{1.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\perp$}}}}}\displaylimits_{I\in\mathcal{P}}t_{I}(\varphi_{I})_{K}
over

over

\mathsf{G}((\varphi_{I})_{I\in\mathcal{P}})

over

An obstruction is indicated by the following observation.

\mathsf{G}(\varphi)
and
\mathsf{H}(\varphi)
, as the next statement indicates.

Proposition 7.1
.

Assume that
K=k(\!(t)\!)
for a field
k
with
\mathsf{char}(k)\neq 2
and let
\varphi
be a quadratic form over
k
.
Consider the quadratic form
\rho=\varphi\perp t\varphi
over

.

\mathsf{H}(\varphi_{K})={K}^{\times 2}\cdot\mathsf{H}(\varphi)

.

\varphi

\mathsf{G}(\varphi_{K})={K}^{\times 2}\cdot\mathsf{G}(\varphi)

Let
v
denote the
t
-adic valuation on

.
Since
v
is henselian,
t\in\mathsf{G}(\rho)
,
vK=\mathbb{Z}
and

\mathsf{G}(\rho)=\mathsf{H}(\rho)=\mathsf{Hyp}_{2}(\rho)

Given a finite field extension

\varphi_{\ell(\!(t)\!)}

\mathsf{N}_{\ell/k}^{\ast}\subseteq\mathsf{N}_{\ell(\!(t)\!)/K}^{\ast}
.
This argument shows that
\mathsf{H}(\varphi)\subseteq\mathsf{H}(\varphi_{K})
.
Consider now a finite field extension
L^{\prime}/K
such that
\varphi_{L^{\prime}}

If

\varphi_{K}
is hyperbolic and consequently
\varphi
is hyperbolic over
k
.
Assume now that
[L^{\prime}:K]

Let
\ell

to

.
Then

.

Consider

\langle 1,-c\rangle\otimes\rho\simeq(\langle 1,-c\rangle\otimes\varphi)_{K}\perp t(\langle 1,-c\rangle\otimes\varphi)_{K}

In order to apply
6.5
in the situation of an iterated power series field, we rely on the following well-known fact.
For convenience and by lack of a reference, we include a proof.

Lemma 7.2
.

Assume that
K=k(\!(t_{1})\!)\ldots(\!(t_{n})\!)
for some

and a field

.
Then

K_{r}=K_{r-1}(\!(t_{r})\!)
for

.

.
If

Assume that
n>0
. By the induction hypothesis,
K_{n-1}
carries a henselian valuation

with value group
(\mathbb{Z}^{n-1},\leqslant_{\mathsf{lex}_{n-1}})

{\mathcal{O}}^{\times}_{v_{n-1}}={k}^{\times}+\mathfrak{m}_{v_{n-1}}

Let

denote the
t_{n}
-adic valuation on

to be the composition on

as defined in

.
Then

\mathsf{dim}(\varphi)=2^{n+1}-2
and

\mathsf{G}(\varphi)/\mathsf{H}(\varphi)\simeq\Lambda(k(\sqrt{a_{1}},\dots,\sqrt{a_{n}})/k)\,.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
\varphi\perp\operatorname{\mathbb{H}}\simeq\langle\!\langle-t_{1},\dots,-t_{n}\rangle\!\rangle\perp-\langle\!\langle-a_{1}t_{1},\dots,-a_{n}t_{n}\rangle\!\rangle
. Hence

\varphi\in\mathsf{I}^{n}K
and
\mathsf{dim}(\varphi)=2^{n+1}-2
.
Since
[k(\sqrt{a_{1}},\dots,\sqrt{a_{n}}):k]=2^{n}
, we have for every
I\in\mathcal{P}
that

a_{I}\notin{k}^{\times 2}
, whereby
\langle\!\langle a_{I}\rangle\!\rangle
is anisotropic over
k
.
From this it follows that the quadratic form
\varphi
over
K
is anisotropic.
Since
\mathsf{dim}(\varphi)\equiv 2\,\mathsf{mod}\,4
and
\varphi\in\mathsf{I}^{2}K
, it follows that
\varphi_{L}
is not hyperbolic for any quadratic field extension
L/K
.
We conclude by
5.1
and
7.3
that
\mathsf{G}(\varphi)/\mathsf{H}(\varphi)\simeq\Lambda(k(\sqrt{a_{1}},\dots,\sqrt{a_{n}})/k)
.
∎

Corollary 7.9
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geqslant 3
and
K=k(t_{0},\dots,t_{n})
for a field
k
with
\mathsf{char}(k)\neq 2
and
|{k}^{\times}/{k}^{\times 2}|\geqslant 4
.
There exists a quadratic form
\varphi\in\mathsf{I}^{n}K
with
\mathsf{dim}(\varphi)=2^{n+1}-2
and such that
{\bf PSim}^{+}(\varphi)
is not rationally connected.

Proof.

Set
k^{\prime}=k(t_{0})
. According to
5.2
, there exist
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in{k^{\prime}}^{\times}
such that

[k^{\prime}(\sqrt{a_{1}},\dots,\sqrt{a_{n}}):k^{\prime}]=2^{n}
and
\Lambda(k^{\prime}(\sqrt{a_{1}},\dots,\sqrt{a_{n}})/k^{\prime})\neq 0
.
Let
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}(\{1,\dots,n\})\smallsetminus\{\emptyset\}

\varphi=\mathop{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{2.1}{$\displaystyle\perp$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{1.5}{$\textstyle\perp$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{1.5}{$\scriptstyle\perp$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{1.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\perp$}}}}}\displaylimits_{I\in\mathcal{P}}t_{I}\langle\!\langle a_{I}\rangle\!\rangle\,.

Then
\varphi\in\mathsf{I}^{n}K
,
\mathsf{dim}(\varphi)=2^{n+1}-2
.
Furthermore, letting
K^{\prime}=k^{\prime}(\!(t_{0})\!)\ldots(\!(t_{n})\!)

by
7.8
that

\mathsf{G}(\varphi_{K^{\prime}})/\mathsf{H}(\varphi_{K^{\prime}})\simeq\Lambda(k^{\prime}(\sqrt{a_{1}},\dots,\sqrt{a_{n}})/k^{\prime})\neq 0\,.

Hence, according to
2.3
,
{\bf PSim}^{+}(\varphi)
is not rationally connected.
∎

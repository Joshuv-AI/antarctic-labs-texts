On graph classes with constant domination-packing ratio
Marthe Bonamy, Mónika Csikós, Anna Gujgiczer, Yelena Yuditsky

Abstract
The dominating number γ ⁡ ( G ) \gamma(G) of a graph G G is the minimum size of a vertex set whose closed neighborhood covers all the vertices of the graph. The packing number ρ ⁡ ( G ) \rho(G) of G G is the maximum size of a vertex set whose closed neighborhoods are pairwise disjoint. In this paper we study graph classes 𝒢 {\cal G} such that γ ⁡ ( G ) / ρ ⁡ ( G ) \gamma(G)/\rho(G) is bounded by a constant c 𝒢 c_{\cal G} for each G ∈ 𝒢 G\in{\cal G} . We propose an inductive proof technique to prove that if 𝒢 \cal G is the class of 2 2 -degenerate graphs, then there is such a constant bound c 𝒢 c_{\cal G} . We note that this is the first monotone, dense graph class that is shown to have constant ratio. We also show that the classes of AT-free and unit-disk graphs have bounded ratio. In addition, our technique gives improved bounds on c 𝒢 c_{\cal G} for planar graphs, graphs of bounded treewidth or bounded twin-width. Finally, we provide some new examples of graph classes where the ratio is unbounded.

Introduction

The duality of packing and covering problems is well-known and their relation is widely studied in geometric setups. These two notions can naturally be translated to graphs using the notion of balls.

Balls in graphs.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a simple graph. The
distance
between two vertices
v,u\in V
, denoted
\dist_{G}(u,v)
, is the length of the shortest path between
u
and
v
in
G
. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and let a
k
-ball
centered in
v\in V(G)
be the set of all vertices at distance at most
k
from
v
.
For any
v\in V
let
N_{G}[v]
denote the
1
-ball centered in
v
, that is, the closed neighborhood of
v
in
G
and let
N_{G}(v)
denote
N_{G}[v]\setminus\{v\}
. The
degree
of
v
in
G
, denoted by
\deg_{G}(v)
is defined as
|N_{G}(v)|
.
For any
X\subseteq V
let
N_{G}[X]
denote
\bigcup_{v\in X}N_{G}[v]
and let
N_{G}(X)
denote
\bigcup_{v\in X}N_{G}(v)
. In the notations above and in subsequent ones, we drop the subscript when
G
is clear from the context.

Covering and packing.

Covering a graph
G
with as few balls of radius
1
as possible is commonly known as finding a minimum dominating set of
G
. Formally, a
dominating set
in
G
is a collection of vertices such that the union of their closed neighborhoods contains all of
V(G)
. We denote the size of minimum dominating set in
G
by
\gamma(G)
.
The dual of this problem is to maximize the number of disjoint balls of radius
1
that can be packed in a graph.
Formally, a
packing
in
G
is a collection of vertices whose closed neighborhoods are pairwise disjoint. In other words, a set of vertices form a packing iff their pairwise distances are at least
3
. We let
\rho(G)
denote the size a maximum packing in
G
.

A straightforward, general relation of
\gamma(G)
and
\rho(G)
is given by the following observation. For any graph
G
and any packing, a dominating set must have a non-empty intersection with each of the closed neighborhoods of the vertices of the packing, thus
\gamma(G)\geqslant\rho(G)
. In general those two parameters are not equal. Simple examples where
\gamma(G)>\rho(G)
include
G=C_{4}
or
G=C_{5}
.

The problems of computing
\gamma(G)
and
\rho(G)
have natural formulations as integer programs.

\displaystyle\text{minimize}\quad\sum_{v\in V(G)}x_{v}

\displaystyle\text{maximize}\quad\sum_{v\in V(G)}y_{v}

(1)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}\quad\sum_{u\in N[v]}x_{u}\geqslant 1,\mbox{ }\forall v\in V(G)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}\quad\sum_{u\in N[v]}y_{u}\leqslant 1,\mbox{ }\forall v\in V(G),

\displaystyle\quad\quad x_{v}\in\{0,1\},\quad\forall v\in V(G)

\displaystyle\quad\quad y_{v}\in\{0,1\},\quad\forall v\in V(G)

The linear program relaxations of the above integer programs are dual, and their optimums are called fractional dominating and fractional packing numbers, respectively. One of the early papers studying the integrality gap of these problems was due to Lovász
[
Lov75
]
, who showed that the ratio of integral and fractional dominating numbers is always at most
\log(\Delta(G))
, where
\Delta(G)
denotes the maximum degree of
G
.
In this paper, we study the following question.

Question 1.1
.

Given a graph class
\mathcal{G}
, is there a constant
c_{\mathcal{G}}
such that

\frac{\gamma(G)}{\rho(G)}\leqslant c_{\mathcal{G}}\quad\text{ for each }\quad G\in\mathcal{G}~~?

Importantly, a positive answer to this question immediately gives the bound
c_{\mathcal{G}}
on the integrality gap of both the dominating and the packing problem for any graph
G\in\mathcal{G}
.

Previous work

There are graph classes for which the ratio
\gamma/\rho
is known to be equal to

, namely, trees
[
MM75
, Theorem 7]
, strongly chordal graphs
[
Far84
, Corollary 3.4]
, and dually chordal graphs
[
BCD98
, Theorem 3.2]
.
The ratio is known to be at most
2
for cactus graphs
[
LRR13
, Theorem 8]
and connected biconvex graphs
[

.
Recently, it was also shown that for any bipartite cubic graph
G

\gamma(G)\leqslant\frac{120}{49}\rho(G)
holds
[
GG25

and for any maximal outerplanar graph

[
GG25

.

1.1.1
Low-degree graphs.

A general upper bound of
\gamma(G)\leqslant\Delta(G)\rho(G)
follows by observing that the union of all neighbors of the vertices in a maximum packing is a dominating set in the graph. This observation was first stated by Henning et al.
[
HLR11
]
who also showed that if
\Delta(G)\leqslant 2
, then
\gamma(G)\leqslant\rho(G)+1
with equality if and only if
G=C_{n}
and
n\equiv 1,2\mod 3
.
Furthermore, they proved that if
G
is claw-free graph with
\Delta(G)\leqslant 3
, then
\gamma(G)\leqslant 2\rho(G)
and stated the following conjecture on general subcubic graphs.

Conjecture 1.2
.

[
HLR11
]

Let
G
be a connected graph with
\Delta(G)\leqslant 3
, then
\gamma(G)\leqslant 2\rho(G)+1
. The equality holds only for three well-defined graphs, one of which being the Petersen graph.

1.1.2
Graphs with no large complete bipartite minors.

A graph
H
is a
minor
of a graph
G
if it can be obtained from
G
by deleting vertices and edges and by contracting edges. Böhme and Mohar
[
BM03
]
studied the more general problem of packing and covering with balls of radius
k
in
K_{q,r}
-minor free graphs. Their results imply the following on the domination and packing numbers.

Theorem 1.3
.

[
BM03
, Corollary 1.2]

If
G
does not contain a
K_{q,r}
-minor, then
\gamma(G)<(4r+(q-1)(r+1))\rho(G)-3r+1
.

Since graphs embedded in a surface of genus
g
cannot contain a
K_{k,3}
-minor for any
2g+3\leqslant k
, the following bound holds.

Corollary 1.4
.

[
BM03
, Corollaries 1.3 and 1.4]

Let
G
be a graph embedded in a surface of Euler genus
g
, then
\gamma(G)\leqslant 4(2g+5)\rho(G)-9
. In particular, if
G
is a planar graph, then
\gamma(G)\leqslant 20\rho(G)-9
.

1.1.3
Graphs with bounded weak coloring numbers.

Theorem

1.3
was generalized in
[
Dvo13
]
using the
weak
1
-coloring
and
weak
2
-coloring numbers
(
\mbox{wcol}_{1}(G)
and
\mbox{wcol}_{2}(G)
) of a graph
G
.
Since in this paper we will not work directly with the notion of the weak coloring number, we spare its (lenghty) definition and only give the statement of the theorem.

Theorem 1.5
.

[
Dvo13
, Theorem 4]

For any
G
such that
\mbox{wcol}_{2}(G)\leqslant c
, we have
\gamma(G)\leqslant c^{2}\rho(G)
.

Remark 1.6
.

Later, Dvořák proposed the alternative bound of
\gamma(G)\leqslant 4\mbox{wcol}_{1}^{4}(G)\mbox{wcol}_{2}(G)\rho(G)
, which is an improvement for the case where
\mbox{wcol}_{2}
is large enough compared to
\mbox{wcol}_{1}

[
Dvo19
]
.

Theorem

1.5
implies that the ratio is bounded by some constant for many minor-closed and sparse graph classes, including planar graphs and bounded treewidth graphs. For more information on the weak coloring numbers of minor-closed graph classes, see
[
HLMR24
]
.

1.1.4
Negative results.

There are much fewer graph classes that were proven to have unbounded ratio.
Burger et al.
[
BHvV09
]
observed that if
G
is the Cartesian product of two complete graphs on
n
vertices, then
\gamma(G)=n
and
\rho(G)=1
.
This implies that there is no constant that bounds the
\gamma/\rho
ratio for all bipartite graphs. Another class where the ratio
\gamma/\rho
can be arbitrarily large is the class of graphs with arboricity 3
[
Dvo19
]
.

Improved constants

The classes of graphs with bounded tree-width, planar graphs, and outerplanar graphs have constant weak coloring numbers, therefore by Theorem

2
A stronger induction hypothesis

One of the difficulties with using induction to prove that
{\gamma}/{\rho}
is bounded is that deleting vertices can affect the distance (in particular, deleting the common neighbor of two vertices and applying induction may result in them both being in the packing). We circumvent this by working with a stronger induction hypothesis, as follows.

Definition 2.1

(
(X,Y)
-dominating set of
G
)
.

Let
G
be a graph and let
X,Y\subseteq V(G)
. A set
D\subseteq V(G)
is an
(X,Y)
-dominating set of
G
if
N[D\cup X]\cup Y=V(G)
. We denote by
\gamma_{X,Y}(G)
the size of a smallest
(X,Y)
-dominating set of
G
.

Definition 2.2

(
(X,Y)
-packing of
G
)
.

Let
G
be a graph and let
X,Y\subseteq V(G)
. A set
P\subseteq V(G)
is an
(X,Y)
-packing of
G
if any vertex
u\in P
satisfies the following:

(a)

for any vertex
v\in P\setminus\{u\}
, we have
\dist(u,v)\geqslant 3
.

(b)

for any vertex
x\in X
, we have
\dist(u,x)\geqslant 2
, or equivalently
P\cap N[X]=\emptyset
.

(c)

for any vertex
y\in Y
, we have
\dist(u,y)\geqslant 1
, or equivalently
P\cap Y=\emptyset
.

We denote by
\rho_{X,Y}(G)
the size of a largest
(X,Y)
-packing of
G
.

While this may seem opaque, the intuition is simple. Informally,
X
is a set of vertices that will be “for free” in the dominating set and that may have a now-deleted neighbor in the packing, while
Y
is the next layer: a set of vertices that are already dominated “for free” and that may be at distance two of a now-deleted vertex in the packing.

Proof technique.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a class of graphs and
C\in\mathbb{N}
.
In order to prove that
\frac{\gamma(G)}{\rho(G)}\leqslant C
for every graph
G\in\mathcal{G}
, we prove
\frac{\gamma_{X,Y}(G)}{\rho_{X,Y}(G)}\leqslant C
for every graph
G\in\mathcal{G}
and any choice of sets
X,Y\subseteq V(G)
. Note that setting
X=Y=\emptyset
yields the desired bound on the ratio
\gamma(G)/\rho(G)
.
Our inductive proofs have the following main steps.

•

Assume that there is a graph
G\in\mathcal{G}
for which there are sets
X,Y\subseteq V(G)
such that
\gamma_{X,Y}(G)/\rho_{X,Y}(G)>C
and consider
G\in\mathcal{G}
such that it minimizes
|V(G)|+|E(G)|
.

•

Choose carefully a
G^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}
that is a subgraph of
G
and sets
X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}\subseteq V(G^{\prime})
. By the minimality of
G
, we know that
G^{\prime}
has an
(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})
-dominating set
D^{\prime}
and an
(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})
-packing
P^{\prime}
—we will often compress this and say an
(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})
-dominating-packing pair
(D^{\prime},P^{\prime})
—that satisfy
|D^{\prime}|/|P^{\prime}|\leqslant C
.

•

Using
(D^{\prime},P^{\prime})
, define an
(X,Y)
-dominating-packing pair
(D,P)
for
G
that satisfies
|D|/|P|\leqslant C
, reaching a contradiction.

2.1
Properties of
(X,Y)
-packings and
(X,Y)
-dominating sets

The following lemmas state some properties of
(X,Y)
-packings and
(X,Y)
-dominating sets that will be particularly useful for induction purposes. Their proofs also follow the above outlined proof technique.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a hereditary class of graphs and
C\in\mathbb{N}
. For any
G\in\mathcal{G}
and
X,Y\subseteq V(G)
such that
\frac{\gamma_{X,Y}(G)}{\rho_{X,Y}(G)}>C
and
|V(G)|
is minimal, we have
X=\emptyset
.

Proof.

Assume to the contrary that
X\neq\emptyset
and let
a\in X
.
Define
X^{\prime}=X\setminus\{a\}
,
Y^{\prime}=Y\cup N(a)
, and
G^{\prime}=G\setminus\{a\}
.
Furthermore, let
D^{\prime}
be the smallest
(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})
-dominating set in
G^{\prime}
and let
P^{\prime}
be the largest
(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})
-packing in
G^{\prime}
.
By minimality of
G
, we have
|D^{\prime}|\leqslant C\cdot|P^{\prime}|
.

Firstly, note that
D=D^{\prime}
is an
(X,Y)
-dominating set in
G
. Indeed by the assumption on
D^{\prime}
,
N_{G\setminus\{a\}}[D^{\prime}\cup(X\setminus\{a\})]\cup(Y\cup N_{G}(a))=V(G)\setminus\{a\}
and so
N_{G}[D\cup X]\cup Y=V(G)
using the assumption that
a\in X
.

Secondly, we show that
P=P^{\prime}
is an
(X,Y)
-packing of
G
, by proving that it has all the properties listed in
Definition

2.2
.
Let
u,v\in P
, then by the properties of
P^{\prime}
,
d_{G}(u,v)\geqslant 3
, unless
\{u,v\}\subseteq N(a)
, but this contradicts Property (
c
) of
P^{\prime}
, as
P^{\prime}\cap Y^{\prime}=P\cap(Y\cup N(a))=\emptyset
. Hence Property (
a
) holds for
P
.
Let
u\in P
and
x\in X
, then
d_{G}(u,x)\geqslant 2
as
N(a)\cap P=\emptyset
by Property (
c
) of
P^{\prime}
. Hence Property (
b
) holds for
P
.
Let
u\in P
and
y\in Y
, then as
P=P^{\prime}
, Property (
c
) holds for
P
.

These two observations imply that

\frac{\gamma_{X,Y}(G)}{\rho_{X,Y}(G)}\leqslant\frac{|D|}{|P|}=\frac{|D^{\prime}|}{|P^{\prime}|}\leqslant C\,,

reaching a contradiction to the assumption that
\frac{\gamma_{X,Y}(G)}{\rho_{X,Y}(G)}>C
and thus we conclude
X=\emptyset
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a monotone class of graphs and
C\in\mathbb{N}
. For any
G\in\mathcal{G}
and
X,Y\subseteq V(G)
such that
\frac{\gamma_{X,Y}(G)}{\rho_{X,Y}(G)}>C
and
|V(G)|+|E(G)|
is minimal, we have that
G[Y]
is an independent set.

Proof.

Assume to the contrary that there is
y_{1}y_{2}\in E(G[Y])
. Let
G^{\prime}=G\setminus\{y_{1}y_{2}\}
, and let
D
be a minimum
(X,Y)
-dominating set of
G^{\prime}
and
P
be a maximum
(X,Y)
-packing of
G^{\prime}
. By the minimality of
G^{\prime}
,
|D|\leqslant C\cdot|P|
.
Observe that
D
is an
(X,Y)
-dominating set in
G
as
N_{G}[D\cup X]\cup Y=N_{G\setminus\{y_{1}y_{2}\}}[D\cup X]\cup Y=V(G)
.
Moreover, since
P\cap Y=\emptyset
,
P
is an
(X,Y)
-packing of
G
as well.
Hence we get a contradiction to the assumption that
\frac{\gamma_{X,Y}(G)}{\rho_{X,Y}(G)}>C
and conclude that
G[Y]
is independent.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a hereditary class of graphs and
C\in\mathbb{N}
. For any
G\in\mathcal{G}
and
X,Y\subseteq V(G)
such that
\frac{\gamma_{X,Y}(G)}{\rho_{X,Y}(G)}>C
and
|V(G)|
is minimal, we have that every vertex
u
with
\deg(u)\leqslant C
is contained in
Y
.

Proof.

From the minimality of
G
we know that
G
is connected and by
Lemma

2.3
,
X=\emptyset
.
Assume to the contrary that there is a vertex
a\in V(G)
with
\deg(a)\leqslant C
and
a\notin Y
.
Let
G^{\prime}=G\setminus\{a\}
,
X^{\prime}=X\cup N(a)=N(a)
and
Y^{\prime}=Y
. We can assume that
N(a)\neq\emptyset
as
G
is connected.
By the minimality of
G
, there is an
(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})
-dominating-packing pair
(D^{\prime},P^{\prime})
in
G^{\prime}
such that
\frac{|D^{\prime}|}{|P^{\prime}|}\leqslant C
.

Observe that
D=D^{\prime}\cup N(a)
is an
(X,Y)
-dominating set in
G
. By the assumption on
D^{\prime}
,
N_{G\setminus\{a\}}[D^{\prime}\cup(X\cup N(a))]\cup Y=V(G)\setminus\{a\}
. Therefore
N_{G}[(D^{\prime}\cup N(a))\cup X]\cup Y=V(G)
, as required.
Now we argue that
P=P^{\prime}\cup\{a\}
is an
(X,Y)
-packing of
G
. Indeed, Property (
a
) holds because of Property (
b
) of
P^{\prime}
. Property (
b
) holds because
X=\emptyset
and property (
c
) holds because
a\notin Y
.

We have
|D|\leqslant|D^{\prime}|+C
and
|P|=|P^{\prime}|+1
, hence
\frac{\gamma_{X,Y}(G)}{\rho_{X,Y}(G)}\leqslant\frac{|D^{\prime}|+C}{|P^{\prime}|+1}\leqslant C
, a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a hereditary class of graphs and
C\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
G\in\mathcal{G}
for which there are
X,Y\subseteq V(G)
be such that
\frac{\gamma_{X,Y}(G)}{\rho_{X,Y}(G)}>C
and
|V(G)|
is minimal, then for every vertex
y\in Y
we have
\deg(y)\geqslant 2
.

Proof.

Assume to the contrary that there is
y\in Y
such that
\deg(y)\leqslant 1
. Let
G^{\prime}=G\setminus\{y\}
, and
X^{\prime}=X
,
Y^{\prime}=Y\setminus\{y\}
.

Let
D^{\prime}

Graphs with bounded tree-width

There are many equivalent ways to define the tree-width of a graph, we use the following one. The

of a graph is
\min\{\omega(H)-1\mid G\mbox{ is a subgraph of }H\mbox{ and }H\mbox{ is chordal}\}

[

, Proposition 12.4.4]

We prove the following stronger statement, which then implies

1.9
by substituting

Theorem 3.1
.

For every
k
, for every graph
G
with tree-width at most
k

, we have
\gamma_{X,Y}(G)\leqslant k\cdot\rho_{X,Y}(G)
.

Proof.

We proceed by contradiction. Consider a counterexample
(G,X,Y)
which minimizes
|V(G)|+|E(G)|
. By Lemmas
2.3
and
2.4
, we may assume that
X=\emptyset

induces a stable set.

By our assumptions,

on the same vertex set with

.
Since

We have that

.

Since

if and only if

However, in this case,
\emptyset

-packing and

Therefore we can assume that

and consider

a vertex

,  the sets

Observe that as

.
Now we consider the sets

(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})

First we argue that

)-(

To establish Property (

Assume to the contrary that there is

5
Bounded twin-width graphs

Before we discuss how to prove that graphs with bounded twin-width admit a bounded ratio
\gamma/\rho
, let us consider the case of

We say that a graph

We will prove the following theorem.

Theorem 5.1
.

For every distance-hereditary graph

.

The proof will contain similar but simpler ingredients, as distance-hereditary graphs have small twin-width

,

]

(X,Y)

(X,Y)
-dominating set and every vertex not in

.

For every distance-hereditary graph

\gamma^{t}_{X,Y}(G)\leqslant 2\rho_{X,Y}(G)

Note that
\gamma_{X,Y}(G)\leqslant\gamma^{t}_{X,Y}(G)

We proceed by contradiction. Consider a counterexample

Now we turn to the problem of domination and packing in graphs of bounded twin-width.

Definition of twin-width.

Let
G

∎

Claim 8.2
.

For each
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
\gamma(G_{i})=2i+1
.

Proof.

We can construct a dominating set of size
2i+1
in
G_{i}
by fixing both level
\ell\in\{1,2,3\}
and picking the level
\ell
vertices from each block plus one of
\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
.

Assume to the contrary that there is a dominating set
D
of
G_{i}
of size at most
2i
. Let
A_{j}
be a block of
G_{i}
with
V(A_{j})=\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4},v_{5},v_{6}\}
. First observe that in order to dominate the level
2
vertices of
A_{j}
, we must have
|D\cap V(A_{j})|\geqslant 1
.

If
A_{j}
contains exactly
1
vertex from
D
then it must be one of the vertices from level three, otherwise either
v_{2}
or
v_{5}
could not be dominated. This also implies that
|V(A_{j-1})\cap D|\geqslant 2
and if the block
A_{j-1}
contains exactly
2
vertices from
D
, then it must be the ones on level three, otherwise either
v_{3}
or
v_{4}
could not be dominated. Similarly, in this case
|V(A_{j-2})\cap D|\geqslant 2
and if the block
A_{j-2}
has exactly
2
vertices from
D
, both of them are from level three. Hence we can deduce the following observation.

Observation 8.3
.

Let
D
be a dominating set in
G_{i}
, then there is no sub-chain of blocks such that all the internal blocks in the sub-chain contain exactly
2
vertices from
D
and the extremal blocks (one of which can be the edge
\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
) contain at most
1
vertex from
D
.

We will reach contradiction with
\gamma(G_{i})\leqslant 2i
via a case distinction on
|D\cap\{u_{1},u_{2}\}|
.

Assume that
|D\cap\{u_{1},u_{2}\}|=2
. Then there are at least two blocks with exactly one vertex from
D
such that all the blocks between them in the chaining have at most
2
vertices from
D
, but this is a contradiction to
Observation

8.3
.

Assume that
|D\cap\{u_{1},u_{2}\}|=1
. Then there is at least one block with exactly one vertex from
D
. If there is exactly one such a block then the rest of the blocks contain exactly
2
vertices from
D
and we get a contradiction to
Observation

8.3
. If there are more than two such blocks then we again reach a contradiction to
Observation

8.3
.

Finally, assume that
|D\cap\{u_{1},u_{2}\}|=0
. If there is a block with exactly one vertex from
D
then we also get a contradiction to
Observation

8.3
.
Hence each block has exactly two vertices in
D
.
We consider three cases: the vertices
\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
are dominated by a pair of vertices in level one of the first block; the vertices
\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
are dominated by a pair of vertices in level three of the last block; the vertices
\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
are dominated by a vertex in level one and a vertex in level three of the first and last blocks respectively. In the first case, in each of the blocks we must choose the pair of vertices in level one to
D
and so the level three vertices in the last block are not dominated, a contradiction. In the second case, similarly,
D
must contain the pair of vertices in level three in each of the blocks, and so the level one vertices in the first block are not dominated, a contradiction. Finally, in the third case,
D
must contain at least one vertex from the third level in each block, but this leaves at least one vertex in the first block not dominated, a contradiction. Hence we conclude the proof of the claim.
∎

This completes the proof of
Theorem

1.12
.
∎

8.2
Split graphs

A graph is called a
split graph
if its vertex set can be partitioned into two sets such that one of them induces a clique and the other one is an independent set.

See
1.13

Proof.

For a fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the graph
S_{k}
as follows

•

V(S_{k})=C~\dot{\cup}~I
with
|C|=2k-1
and
|I|=\binom{2k-1}{k}
;

•

C
induces a clique,
I
is an independent set and to every subset of size
k
in
C
, we associate exactly one vertex in
I
which is adjacent to precisely the vertices of this subset, see Figure
7
for an illustration of
S_{2}
.

Figure 7
:
The graph
S_{2}
.

First, we show that
\gamma(S_{k})=k
.

To see that
\gamma(S_{k})\leqslant k
, we will argue that any set
D
of
k
vertices from
C
is a dominating set. Indeed, the vertices in
C
are dominated, since
C
is a clique. Any vertex
v\in I
is also dominated, since it has
k
neighbors in
C
and
|C\setminus D|=k-1
, therefore the neighborhood of
v
cannot be disjoint from
D
.
To show that
\gamma(S_{k})\geqslant k
, let
D^{\prime}
be any set of size
k-1
and let
i\in[0,k-1]
denote
|D^{\prime}\cap I|
. Using this notation, we have
|D^{\prime}\cap C|=k-1-i
and
|C\setminus D^{\prime}|=k+i
, which implies that
\binom{k+i}{k}>i
vertices in
I
are not dominated by
D^{\prime}\cap C
. This set of at least
i+1
vertices in
I
should be dominated by the
i
vertices in
D^{\prime}\cap I
, which is impossible since
I
is stable.

It is easy to see that any pair of vertices in
S_{k}
are either adjacent or have a common neighbor, which implies that
\rho(S_{k})\leqslant 1
and
\rho(S_{k})=1
is trivially attainable.
∎

9
Conclusion

In this work, we have made progress in resolving the following major open problem.

Question 9.1
.

Characterize those graph classes
\mathcal{G}
for which there is a constant
c_{\mathcal{G}}
such that

\frac{\gamma(G)}{\rho(G)}\leqslant c_{\mathcal{G}}\quad\text{ for each }\quad G\in\mathcal{G}~~.

We extended the lists of both positive and negative examples of graph classes. Moreover, we note that due to Theorem
6.1
on
2
-degenerate graphs we cannot hope for a characterization of the above graph classes as sparse graph classes.
We also improved the constants
c_{\mathcal{G}}
for several important graph classes and sharpened Conjecture
1.2
.
We finish our paper by stating some open questions.

From previous works, the complete resolution of Conjecture
1.2

[
HLR11
]
and bounding the ratio
\gamma/\rho
for homogeneously orderable and chordal bipartite graphs
[
GG25
]
remain open.

The question of bounding the ratio
\gamma/\rho
for graphs in many geometric graph classes remains open as well. For example, intersection graphs of axis-parallel rectangles in the plane or intersection graphs of axis-parallel boxes in three dimensions. Note that, using the result in
[
AC14
]
, a bound of the form
\gamma(G)\leqslant(\Delta(G)-1)\rho(G)+1
in the latter class of graphs would imply Conjecture
1.2
.

Another direction for future work is improving the known bounds, for example, we believe that our bounds on planar, unit-disk, and
2
-degenerate graphs could be improved.

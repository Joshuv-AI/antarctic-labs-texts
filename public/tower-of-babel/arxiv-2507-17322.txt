On Spectral Invariant Dense Subalgebras of Uniform Roe Algebras with Subexponential Growth
Siqi Jiang, Xianjin Wang

Abstract
In this paper, we study spectrally invariant subalgebras of uniform Roe algebras for discrete groups with subexponential growth. For a group G G with subexponential growth and satisfying property P P , we construct a class of subalgebras R ∞ ​ ( G ) R^{\infty}(G) . We then prove their spectral invariance in C u ∗ ​ ( G ) C_{u}^{*}(G) through the application of admissible weights. This extends ℓ 2 \ell^{2} -norm spectral invariance results beyond polynomial growth settings.

Introduction

The uniform Roe algebra is a geometric
C^{*}
-algebra. Its spectral invariant subalgebra plays an important role in calculating K-theory groups, verifying Baum-Connes conjecture and studying Fredholm indices of geometric operators.
[
9
,
16
,

,

.
If we find the spectral invariant subalgebra of the uniform Roe algebra under the

-norm, we can calculate its

-theory using the cyclic homology theory
[
5
]
.

Consequently, the construction of spectral invariant dense subalgebras of uniform Roe algebras has garnered considerable attention recently
[

,
7
,
11
,
12
,
18
,
19
]
.
For finitely generated groups with polynomial growth under the
\ell^{1}
-norm, Fendler, Gröchenig, and Leinert
[
8
]
obtained that the Wiener algebra
W(G)
forms a spectrally invariant subalgebra of the uniform Roe algebra
C_{u}^{*}(G)
. More results about spectral subalgebras under the
\ell^{1}
-norm can be referred to
[
1
,
7
,
10
,
12
,
13
]
. However, under the
\ell^{2}
-norm, obtaining results analogous to those in
[
8
]
for the
\ell^{1}
-norm poses significant challenges due to fundamental differences in norm structures.
Notably, under the
\ell^{2}
-norm, some results have also been obtained.
Chen and Wei
[
4
]
demonstrated that for commutative
C^{*}
-algebras
\mathcal{B}
with group actions, the Schwartz function space constitutes a spectrally invariant dense subalgebra of the reduced crossed product
if and only if

G
exhibits polynomial growth. This result is an extension of the
\ell^{2}
-norm of the Wiener algebra results of Fendler et al.
[
8
]
in the
\ell^{1}
-framework, and provides a methodological basis for subsequent research.
For countable discrete groups with polynomial growth, Chent al.
[
3
]
constructed the weighted subalgebra
H_{\ell,B}^{\infty}(G)
and proved its spectral invariance in the uniform Roe algebra
C_{u}^{*}(G)
, breaking through the limitation of
\ell^{1}
and
\ell^{2}
-norm difference. Chen, Jiang, and Zhou
[
2
]
constructed the Fréchet subalgebra
H^{\infty}_{l}(G)
with spectral invariance in
\mathcal{A}_{u}(G)
, specifically for discrete groups satisfying the rapid decay (RD) property.

Due to the weakening of the attenuation constraint, the methods used in the above-mentioned polynomial growth groups are not applicable to the subexponential growth groups under the
\ell^{2}
-norm. This promotes the development of new analytical methods for subexponential growth groups.
In
[
14
]
, Gröchenig and Ziemowit studied Banach algebras of pseudo-differential operators and their almost diagonalized properties on Abelian groups, particularly
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Its weight conditions can be extended to the sub-exponential growth group, providing a new perspective for the spectral analysis of
C_{u}^{*}(G)
.
Concurrently, Sun
[
20
,
21
]
employed admissible weights to investigate the non-commutative inverse closed subalgebras of infinite-dimensional matrix algebras, providing a tool independent of decay conditions for the construction of subalgebras of
C_{u}^{*}(G)
on subexponential groups.

Inspired by the above-mentioned research, this paper constructs a class of subalgebras of the uniform Roe algebra for countable discrete groups with subexponential growth, employing subexponential growth weights.
We further proved that these subalgebras are spectrally invariant within the uniform Roe algebra by using admissible weights and growth conditions satisfying property
P
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\left(G,\rho_{l}\right)
be a discrete metric space. If

R^{\infty}(G)\subseteq C_{u}^{*}(G)

Proof.

For any

, we have the following estimation

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\|\phi\xi\right\|^{2}&\leq\sum_{x}\sum_{y}\left|\phi(x,y)\right|^{2}\exp(2\alpha l(y^{-1}x)^{\beta})\sum_{y}\exp(-2\alpha l(y^{-1}x)^{\beta})\left|\xi(y)\right|^{2}\\
&\leq\sup_{x}\sum_{y}\left|\phi(x,y)\right|^{2}\exp(2\alpha l(y^{-1}x)^{\beta})\sum_{x}\sum_{y}\exp(-2\alpha l(y^{-1}x)^{\beta})\left|\xi(y)\right|^{2}.\end{split}

For the term
\sum_{x}\sum_{y}\exp(-2\alpha l(y^{-1}x)^{\beta})\left|\xi(y)\right|^{2}

&\leq\left\|\xi\right\|^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\exp(-2\alpha n^{\beta})\left|B(e,n+1)\right|\leq C\left\|\xi\right\|^{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\exp(-2\alpha n^{\beta})\exp(\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}(n+1)^{\beta^{{}^{\prime}}})\\

where
2n^{\beta}-(n+1)^{\beta}>0

Then, we derive

\displaystyle\left\|\phi\xi\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\xi\right\|^{2}[\sum_{z\in G}\sup_{\left\{x,y\in G,y^{-1}x=z\right\}}\left|\phi(x,y)\right|^{2}\exp(2\alpha l(z)^{\beta})]

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\phi\right\|_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}\left\|\xi\right\|^{2}.

Hence, we get
\left\|\phi\right\|\leq C\left\|\phi\right\|_{\alpha,\beta}
.
By virtue of the boundedness of
\phi

, we set

\phi_{n}(x,y)=\left\{\begin{aligned} \phi(x,y)&,&\text{if}~l(y^{-1}x)\leq n;\\
0&,&otherwise.\end{aligned}\right.

Then , we get

\displaystyle\left\|\phi-\phi_{n}\right\|_{\alpha,\beta}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\phi\right\|_{2\alpha+1,\beta}^{2}\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\left|B(e,k+1)\right|\exp(-2(\alpha+1)k^{\beta})

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\phi\right\|_{2\alpha+1,\beta}^{2}\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\exp(-\alpha(2k^{\beta}-(k+1)^{\beta})-2k^{\beta})

∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
G
be a countable discrete group with a proper length function
l
, satisfying property
P
. Then, the algebra
R^{\infty}(G)
is a spectral invariant dense subalgebra of the uniform Roe algebra
C_{u}^{*}(G)
.

Proof.

To establish the spectral invariance property, the proof proceeds in two key steps: (i) deriving an estimate for the

n
-th power of
A
, and (ii) verifying the inverse-closed property.

Step 1.
For any
A\in R^{\infty}(G)
and
n\geq 1
, the following inequality holds

\displaystyle\left\|A^{n}\right\|_{\alpha,\beta}\leq C\left(C\left\|A\right\|_{\alpha,\beta}\left\|A\right\|_{\mathcal{B}^{2}}^{-1}\right)^{\frac{1+\theta}{\theta}n^{\log_{2}{(1+\theta)}}}(\left\|A\right\|_{\mathcal{B}^{2}})^{n}.

(3.15)

Let
A=(a(x,y))_{x,y\in G}\in R_{\alpha,\beta}(G)
, and
A^{2}=(c(x,y))_{x,y\in G}
. For simplify, we denote

w(x)=w(x,I)
,
v(x)=v(x,I),\tilde{a}(x)=\sup_{y\in G}\left|a(yx,y)\right|
and
\tilde{b}(x)=\sup_{y\in G}\left|b(yx,y)\right|
, where
I
is the identity element of the discrete group
G
.

Since
w(z)=\exp(\alpha\rho_{l}(z)^{\beta})
is an admissible weight by Theorem
3.1
, it follows that for any
x\in G
,

\displaystyle\tilde{c}w(x)

\displaystyle\leq C\sup_{y\in G}\sum_{z\in G}(\left|(av)(yx,z)\right|\left|(aw)(z,y)\right|+\left|(aw)(yx,z)\right|\left|(av)(z,y)\right|).

(3.16)

Moreover, we get

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in G}\sum_{z\in G}\left|a(yx,z)\right|v\left(yx,z\right)\left|a(z,y)w(z,y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{y\in G}(\sum_{z\in G}(v(yx,z)\chi_{\rho(yx,z)<\tau}\left|a(z,y)\right|w(z,y))^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}(\sum_{z\in G}\left|a(yx,z)\right|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\sup_{y\in G}\sum_{z\in G}\left|a(yx,z)\right|v\left(yx,z\right)\chi_{\rho(yx,z)\geq\tau}\left|a(z,y)w(z,y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq(\sum_{z\in G}(v(z^{-1}x)\chi_{\rho(x,z)<\tau}\left|\tilde{a}(z)\right|w(z))^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|A\right\|_{\mathcal{B}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{z\in G}(\tilde{a}(z^{-1}x)v(z^{-1}x)\chi_{\rho(x,z)\geq\tau}\left|\tilde{a}(z)\right|w(z)),

(3.17)

For any
\tau\geq 1
, based on (
2.4
) and (
3
) we obtain

\displaystyle\|(\sup_{y\in G}\sum_{z\in G}\left|(av)(yx,z)\right|\left|(aw)(z,y)\right|)_{x\in G}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{\tau\geq 1}(\left\|\tilde{a}w\right\|_{2}\left\|v\chi_{B(I_{0},\tau)}\right\|_{2}\left\|A\right\|_{\mathcal{B}^{2}}+\left\|\tilde{a}w\right\|_{2}\left\|(\tilde{a}v)\chi_{X\setminus B(I_{0},\tau)}\right\|_{2})

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|A\right\|_{\alpha,\beta}\left\|A\right\|_{\mathcal{B}^{2}}\inf_{\tau\geq 1}(\left\|v\chi_{B(I_{0},\tau)}\right\|_{2}+\frac{\left\|A\right\|_{\alpha,\beta}}{\left\|A\right\|_{\mathcal{B}^{2}}}\left\|(vw^{-1})\chi_{X\setminus B(I_{0},\tau)}\right\|_{2})

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|A\right\|_{\alpha,\beta}\left\|A\right\|_{\mathcal{B}^{2}}D(\frac{\left\|A\right\|_{\alpha,\beta}}{\left\|A\right\|_{\mathcal{B}^{2}}})^{\theta}=C\left\|A\right\|_{\alpha,\beta}^{1+\theta}\left\|A\right\|_{\mathcal{B}^{2}}^{1-\theta}.

(3.18)

Similarly, we get

\|(\sup_{y\in G}\sum_{z\in G}\left|(aw)(yx,z)\right|\left|(av)(z,y)\right|)_{x\in G}\|_{2}\leq C\left\|A\right\|_{\alpha,\beta}^{1+\theta}\left\|A\right\|_{\mathcal{B}^{2}}^{1-\theta}.

(3.19)

Then, combining (
3.16
), (
3
) and (
3.19
), we get

\displaystyle\left\|A^{2}\right\|_{\alpha,\beta}\leq C\left\|A\right\|_{\alpha,\beta}^{1-\theta}\left\|A\right\|_{\mathcal{B}^{2}}^{1-\theta}\quad\text{for~all}~A\in R^{\infty}\left(G\right)~\text{and}~C>0,

which indicates

\left\|A^{2n}\right\|_{\alpha,\beta}\leq D\left\|A^{n}\right\|_{\alpha,\beta}^{1+\theta}\left\|A\right\|_{\mathcal{B}^{2}}^{n(1-\theta)}~\text{and}~\left\|A^{2n+1}\right\|_{\alpha,\beta}\leq D_{1}\left\|A\right\|_{\alpha,\beta}\left\|A^{n}\right\|_{\alpha,\beta}^{1+\theta}\left\|A\right\|_{\mathcal{B}^{2}}^{n(1-\theta)}

, and
D_{1}\|A\|_{\mathcal{B}^{2}}\geq 1

by

b_{n}=D_{1}^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\left\|A^{n}\right\|_{\alpha,\beta}\left\|A\right\|_{\mathcal{B}^{2}}^{-n}\quad\text{for~all}~n\geq 1,

(3.20)

satisfying

b_{2n}\leq b_{n}^{1+\theta}\quad\text{and}\quad b_{2n+1}\leq b_{1}b_{n}^{1+\theta}\quad\text{for~all}~n\geq 1,

which implies that

\displaystyle b_{n}\leq b_{1}^{\sum_{i=0}^{k}\varepsilon_{i}(1+\theta)^{i}}\quad\text{for}~n=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\varepsilon_{i}2^{i},\varepsilon_{i}\in\left\{0,1\right\}.

(3.21)

For the index

\sum_{i=0}^{k}\varepsilon_{i}(1+\theta)^{i}

\displaystyle{\sum_{i=0}^{k}\varepsilon_{i}(1+\theta)^{i}}\leq{\sum_{i=0}^{k}(1+\theta)^{i}}\leq\frac{(1+\theta)}{\theta}(1+\theta)^{k}.

Since

\displaystyle D_{1}^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\left\|A^{n}\right\|_{\alpha,\beta}\left\|A\right\|_{\mathcal{B}^{2}}^{-n}

Step 2.
For any
A\in R^{\infty}(G)
, we have

.

For any

. Then, we have

and
\left\|A\right\|_{\alpha,\beta}=\left\|A^{*}\right\|_{\alpha,\beta}
.
Moreover, we define the matrix

Since
A^{*}A
is a positive operator, there exist constants

,
C_{2}>0
such that

and

\left\|B\right\|_{\mathcal{B}^{2}}\leq\frac{C_{2}-C_{1}}{C_{2}+C_{1}}<1\quad\text{and}\quad\left\|B\right\|_{\alpha,\beta}<\infty.

(3.23)

It follows from (
3.15
) and (
3.23
) that

\displaystyle\left\|(I-B)^{-1}\right\|_{\alpha,\beta}\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}C\Big(C\left\|B\right\|_{\alpha,\beta}\left\|B\right\|_{\mathcal{B}^{2}}^{-1}\Big)^{\frac{1+\theta}{\theta}n^{\log_{2}{(1+\theta)}}}(\left\|B\right\|_{\mathcal{B}^{2}})^{n}<\infty,

which implies that
(A^{*}A)^{-1}\in R^{\infty}(G)
.
Consequently, we deduce that
A^{-1}\in R^{\infty}(G)
, as
A^{-1}=(A^{*}A)^{-1}A^{*}
.

Thus,
R^{\infty}(G)
is spectral invariant in
C_{u}^{*}\left(G\right)
.
∎

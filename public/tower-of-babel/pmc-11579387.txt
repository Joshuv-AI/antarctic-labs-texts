Lie symmetry analysis on heat and mass transport aspects of rate type fluid flow with waste discharge concentration: Keller Box approach
Authors: K V Nagaraja, Sumanta Shagolshem, Bhavesh Kanabar, Ankit Kedia, Amal Abdulrahman, Chander Prakash, M Umeshaiah, K Chandan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe present analysis examines the effect of thermal radiation on the stream of Maxwell liquid past a stretchy surface in the presence of a zero-mass flux condition. The effect of waste discharge concentration, thermophoresis-Brownian motion, and porous media on the fluid motion is also considered. Investigation of the liquid flow with waste discharge concentration in stretched sheets may lead to the growth of effective methods for disposing of waste and preventing pollution. Due to their extensive applicability, the mechanisms of flow and heat transmission in the context of thermal radiation play a crucial role in research and industry. This phenomenon often occurs in spacecraft, nuclear reactor cooling, power production, aerospace technologies, combustion applications, gas turbines, hypersonic flights, and high-temperature processes. The modelled governing partial differential equations (PDEs) are converted into dimensionless ordinary differential equations (ODEs) utilizing appropriate similarity variables. Further, the Keller–Box approach is utilized to solve the resultant ODEs numerically. The influence of several parameters on the temperature, concentration and velocity profiles is shown via graphic representations. The intensification in Deborah’s number and porous parameter values declines the velocity profile. As the values of the radiation, thermophoresis, and Brownian motion parameters increase, the thermal profile increases. The concentration profile increases as the pollutant external source parameter value upsurges.Keywords: Maxwell fluid, Stretching sheet, Porous media, Thermophoresis and Brownian motion, Waste discharge, Zero mass fluxSubject terms: Mathematics and computing, Fluid dynamics

Introduction

The boundary layer flows of non-Newtonian liquids are of noteworthy interest due to their numerous real-world applications in manufacturing as well as industrial procedures. These include drilling fluids, metal spinning, hot rolling paper, optical fibres, plastic polymers, and other further applications. Since no one model can explain every property of non-Newtonian materials, researchers have earlier published a number of non-Newtonian models. These models fall into three main categories: liquids of the integral, rate, and differential types. The liquid model under consideration is a subtype of a rate-type liquid known as a Maxwell liquid. The effects of relaxation time are predicted by this liquid model. Differential liquids cannot predict these kinds of results. Numerical solution via Keller box method

The reduced dimensionless Eqs. (12–14) along with (15) are solved with the well-known numerical method, the Keller box method. The Keller–Box technique is an implicit finite-difference scheme developed to handle particularly nonlinear ODE systems, originating from boundary layer theory and fluid dynamics. Unlike explicit approaches, such as Euler or Runge–Kutta that can be unstable until very small step sizes are utilized, the method uses an implicit discretization strategy, making it unconditionally stable for many issues. Further, with the box approach, the domain is divided into small intervals or a mesh, and the solution is then approximated over each box (or grid cell) using finite differences. For boundary layer problems, this technique maintains exactness at the boundaries, which is essential. When dealing with boundary layer problems, this approach works effectively when steep gradients are close to boundaries. Other techniques, such as shooting or finite-difference approach, can have difficulties capturing these gradients unless an extremely tiny grid is employed. Therefore, we employed the Keller box method in our reduced model due to its accuracy, stability, effectiveness and computational efficiency. To get the numerical results, the approach uses the block elimination method, Newton’s iterative scheme, and the finite difference method. Conclusions

This paper inspects the effect of pollutant concentration on the Maxwell liquid flow through a stretchable sheet without a mass flux at the boundary. The results might find a use in operations using fluids similar to Maxwell liquids, like polymer processing, coating fluxes, chemical and wastewater treatment. Improved designs for effective material processing and waste management systems may result from understanding how contaminants’ concentration influences fluid behaviour. Research on innovative materials employed in stretchy electronics, smart textiles, or biomedical devices where fluid viscoelastic behaviour plays a role can be influenced by understanding fluid behaviour across elastic sheets. For a set of PDEs, we derive Lie point symmetries. Utilizing related invariants, we generate novel similarity transformations for the model in issue utilizing some of the known generators of Lie point symmetry. The model may be made simpler for nonlinear ODEs by using these adjustments. The resulting ODEs are numerically solved using the Keller–Box method, which is well-known for efficiently solving coupled, nonlinear PDEs that result from similarity transformations of ODEs. Boundary conditions are essential for fluid flow issues. Because the Keller–Box technique simplifies addressing both boundary conditions, it is especially well-suited for issues involving boundary layers.

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a group. If
x\in G
, we write
x^{G}
for the conjugacy class containing
x
. Given positive integers
k,n
, we let
B_{n}=B_{n}(G)
denote the set of all elements
x\in G
such that
|x^{G}|\leq n
, and we say that
G
satisfies the
(k,n)
-covering condition for commutators if there is a subset
S\subseteq G
such that
|S|\leq k
and all commutators of
G
are contained in
SB_{n}
. For shortness, we will refer to this condition as the
(k,n)
-covering condition.

The importance of groups satisfying the
(k,n)
-covering condition was first revealed in
[
4
]
, where it was shown that a probabilistically nilpotent finite group of class two contains a subgroup of bounded index which satisfies the condition. In fact, a large part of the work
[
4
]
is devoted to the analysis of such groups. In particular, it was shown that if a (possibly infinite) group
G
satisfies the
(k,n)
-covering condition, then there is a subgroup
T\leq G
such that the index
[G:T]
and the order of
\gamma_{4}(T)
both are finite and
(k,n)
-bounded.

Throughout, we write
\gamma_{i}(K)
for the
i
-th term of the lower central series of a group
K
. We use the expression “
(a,b,\dots)
-bounded” to mean that a quantity is bounded from above by a number depending only on the parameters
a,b,\dots
.

In the present paper, we further clarify the structure of groups satisfying the
(k,n)
-covering condition.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a group satisfying the
(k,n)
-covering condition. Then
G^{\prime}
contains a characteristic subgroup
B
such that
[G^{\prime}:B]
and
|B^{\prime}|
are both
(k,n)
-bounded.

This can be seen as an extension of the main result obtained in
[
3
]
, which says that if all commutators of a group
G
are contained in
B_{n}(G)
then the second commutator subgroup
G^{\prime\prime}
is finite and has
n
-bounded order.

As an immediate corollary of Theorem
1.1
we obtain that, whenever a group
G
satisfying the
(k,n)
-covering condition, then
G^{\prime}
contains a characteristic subgroup
T
which is nilpotent of class at most
2
and has
(k,n)
-bounded index in
G^{\prime}
. Indeed, assume that Theorem
1.1
holds and set
T=C_{B}(B^{\prime})
. It is easy to see that
T
is as required.

A more general covering condition for
w
-values, where
w
is a multilinear commutator word, was considered in
[
2
]
. If
w
is a group-word, say that a group
G
satisfies the
(k,n)
-covering condition with respect to the word

if the
w
-values of
G
are contained in
SB_{n}
for some subset
S\subseteq G
such that
|S|\leq k
. In particular, the paper
[
2
]
contains a proof of the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
2
]
Let
w
be a multilinear commutator word on
m
variables and let

be a group satisfying the
(k,n)
-covering condition with respect to the word

. Then
G
has a soluble subgroup

such that
[G:T]
and the derived length of

are both
(k,m,n)
-bounded.

Observe that our Theorem
1.1
cannot be deduced from the above.
It is absolutely unclear whether under the hypotheses of Theorem
1.2
the group

contains a subgroup of

-bounded index and

-bounded derived length.

Preliminaries

If
x,y,z
are elements of a group, the following identities hold. Throughout the paper they will be used without references.

(1)

[x,y]^{-1}=[y,x]
;

(2)

[xy,z]=[x,z]^{y}[y,z]=[x,z][x,z,y][y,z]
;

(3)

[x,yz]=[x,z][x,y]^{z}
.

Given subsets

of a group

, we write

for the set

. Thus,

is the set of commutators and

denotes the subgroup generated by

. Note that if

is an abelian normal subgroup and

contains only one element, then

.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let

be a positive integer,

a normal subgroup of a group

, and

. Suppose that

. If

and

, then
|G^{\prime}|\leq t^{2}
.

Proof.

Note that
G^{\prime}=M^{\prime}[M,a]
. Pass to the quotient
G/M^{\prime}
and assume that

is abelian. Then
[M,a]=Comm(M,a)
has size at most

. The lemma follows. ∎

Let
G
be a group generated by a set

such that

. For an element

, we write

to denote the minimal number

can be written as a product of

. Clearly,

if and only if

.

Let

be a group generated by a set

and let

A group is said to be a BFC-group if its conjugacy classes are finite and of bounded size. B. H. Neumann proved that in a BFC-group the derived group

was found by J. Wiegold

[

]
. The particular case where

is known as Schur’s theorem

.

For any subgroup

Observe that if

Let

Let

. For

. Lemma

Let

At

Lemma 3.10
.

Suppose that
G
has a
(j)
-subgroup
L
for some

. Then
G
has a normal subgroup
T
of
(j,k,n)
-bounded order such that
G/T
possesses a
(j-1)
-subgroup.

Proof.

First, we note that if
B
is abelian, then the group
G
is its own
(j-1)
-subgroup (as well as
(1)
-subgroup) and so the result holds. We therefore assume that
B
is not abelian.

Recall that
a=[d,e]\in X
and
C_{B}(a)
has maximal possible index in
B
. If both
d
and
e
belong to
L
, we conclude (since
X_{L}\subseteq Z(B)
) that
B
is abelian, contrary to our assumptions. Thus, assume that at least one of them, say
d
, is not in
L
.

Set
V=U_{0}\cap L
. By Lemma
3.8

[B,[V,d]]\leq N
, where

. Let

. Observe that

and let
g_{1},\dots,g_{t}
be a full system of representatives of the right cosets of
V
in
C
. Note that
t
here is
(k,n)
-bounded. The subgroup
[C,d]
is generated by
[V,d]^{g_{1}},\dots,[V,d]^{g_{t}}
and
[g_{1},d],\dots,[g_{t},d]
. This is straightforward from the fact that
[vg,d]=[v,d]^{g}[g,d]
for any
g,v\in G
.
Let

J=L\langle d\rangle\quad\textrm{ and }\quad B_{0}=\langle X_{L},Comm(C,d)\rangle.

Here
B_{0}
is normal in
J
because so is
\langle X_{L}\rangle
and by Lemma
3.4

B
is central in
L
modulo
\langle X_{L}\rangle
.
Moreover, note that the order of
L^{\prime}/B_{0}
and the index of
C_{L/B_{0}}(d)
in

are both
(k,n)
-bounded. The fact that the former is bounded is immediate from Lemma
3.5
. The latter is bounded because so is the index of
C
in
L
. Therefore by Lemma
2.1
the index of
B_{0}
in
J^{\prime}
is
k
-bounded. Hence, we can choose
(k,n)
-boundedly many commutators
c_{1},\dots,c_{t^{\prime}}\in X_{J}
such that
\langle X_{J}\rangle
is generated by
B_{0}
and
c_{1},\dots,c_{t^{\prime}}
.

Let
R
be the product of
N
and the subgroups
[B,[g_{i},d]]^{G}
for

as well as
[B,c_{i}]^{G}
for

.
Observe that
X_{J}
is central in

modulo

.
In view of Lemma
3.6

is

-bounded.
Moreover, since

contains

properly, the index of

in

is at most

. Taking into account Assumption
4
we conclude that if

is normal in
G
, then the image of

in

is a
(j-1)
-subgroup. So in the case where

is normal the lemma is proved.

Now suppose that
J
is not normal. Choose
g\in G
such that
[d,g]\not\in J
. Since, by Lemma
3.5
, the index
[G^{\prime}:B]
is
k
-bounded, we can choose
k
-boundedly many conjugates of
[d,g]
, say

, such that
L\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{m^{\prime}}\rangle
is normal. Set
A=\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{m^{\prime}}\rangle
and
B_{1}=A\cap B
. Since the index
[A:B_{1}]
is
k
-bounded, we conclude that
B_{1}
can be generated by
k
-boundedly many elements, say
a^{\prime}_{1},\dots,a^{\prime}_{r}
. Then
[B_{1},A]
is generated by
(k,n)
-boundedly many conjugates of the commutators
[a^{\prime}_{i},a_{l}]
for

and
1\leq l\leq{m^{\prime}}
, as the commutators
[a^{\prime}_{i},a_{l}]
are contained in

by Lemma
3.4
. The image of
B_{1}
in

is central and so by Schur’s theorem the commutator subgroup of

has
k
-bounded order. We conclude that the subgroup
\langle X_{A}\rangle
is generated by
(k,n)
-boundedly many commutators from

. By Lemma
3.6
for each of such generators there is a normal subgroup of bounded order, modulo which the element is central in
B
. Therefore
G
has a normal subgroup
N_{1}
of
(k,n)
-bounded order such that
\langle X_{A}\rangle
is central in

.

Recall that by Lemma
3.8

[B,[U_{0},d]]\leq N
. Since the subgroups
B,V
, and

. More generally,

. Let

is

-bounded order, such that the set

. For shortness, set

and for each right coset

in

such that

-boundedly many such representatives

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

In view of Lemma
3.5
it is sufficient to show that
B^{\prime}
has finite
(k,n)
-bounded order. Lemma
3.9
tells us that there is a
(k,n)
-bounded number

with the property that
G
has a normal subgroup
T_{0}
of
(k,n)
-bounded order such that
G/T_{0}
possesses a
(j_{0})
-subgroup

.
If
L_{0}=G
, then the image of
B
in
G/T_{0}
is abelian and so

. In this case

has finite
(k,n)
-bounded order and we have nothing to prove. Therefore assume that
L_{0}<G
and
j_{0}\geq 2
. Keeping in mind Assumption
4
pass to the quotient

. By Lemma
3.10
the group

has a normal subgroup

of
(j_{0},k,n)
-bounded order such that

possesses a
(j_{1})
-subgroup

, where

.

Now we repeat the same argument with

in place of

respectively and we continue recursively.

Therefore we find normal subgroups

, all of bounded order, and special subgroups

until, after at most

-bounded order such that

. This completes the proof.
∎

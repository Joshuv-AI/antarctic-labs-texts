Uniqueness of supercritical Gaussian multiplicative chaos
Federico Bertacco 1, Martin Hairer 2

Abstract
We show that, for general convolution approximations to a large class of log-correlated Gaussian fields, the properly normalised supercritical Gaussian multiplicative chaos measures converge stably to a nontrivial limit. This limit depends on the choice of regularisation only through a multiplicative constant and can be characterised as an integrated atomic measure with a random intensity expressed in terms of the critical Gaussian multiplicative chaos.



1
Introduction

For a domain
\mathrm{D}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
d\geq 1
, a Gaussian Multiplicative Chaos (GMC) measure is (formally) a random measure of the form

\upmu_{\upgamma}(dx)\mathrel{\text{``$=$''}}e^{\upgamma\mathrm{X}(x)}dx\;,

(1.1)

where
\upgamma>0
is a real parameter,
dx
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
\mathrm{D}
, and
\mathrm{X}
is a log-correlated Gaussian field on
\mathrm{D}
. More precisely,
\mathrm{X}
is a centred Gaussian field with covariance kernel
\mathcal{K}:\mathrm{D}\times\mathrm{D}\to\mathbb{R}
of the form

\mathcal{K}(x,y)\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathrm{X}(x)\mathrm{X}(y)\bigr]=-\log\lvert x-y\rvert+g(x,y)\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\,x,y\in\mathrm{D}\;,

(1.2)

for some continuous function
g:\overline{\mathrm{D}}\times\overline{\mathrm{D}}\to\mathbb{R}
.
The main difficulty in defining rigorously the measure (
1.1
) is that, as one can see from (
1.2
), the covariance of
\mathrm{X}
blows up along the diagonal, thus making
\mathrm{X}
a rough centred Gaussian field that cannot be defined as a functional field, i.e., as a field indexed by points in the domain
\mathrm{D}
, but only makes sense as a distributional field, i.e., as a linear field indexed by test functions.
To overcome this difficulty, thanks to the seminal work of Kahane
[
10
]
, there is by now a standard roadmap which involves a regularisation, renormalisation, and limiting procedure.
More precisely, one first defines a collection of continuous pointwise defined centred Gaussian fields approximating
\mathrm{X}
.
One then defines a sequence of properly renormalised random measures and finally passes to the limit. We refer to
[
15
,
16
,
2
,
4
,
1
]
for more details on the topic.

It is now well known that the behaviour of the random measure (
1.1
) undergoes a phase transition at

\gammac\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}\;.

More precisely, the regime
\upgamma<\gammac
is called
subcritical
, the borderline case
\upgamma=\gammac
is called
critical
, and the range
\upgamma>\gammac
is called
supercritical
. These three regimes differ both in the normalisation required to achieve a nontrivial limiting measure and in the features of the resulting measure.

In this paper, we focus on the supercritical regime, where, to the best of our knowledge, the only existing mathematical reference in the continuum setting is
[
12
]
where the authors proved the convergence of a regularised and renormalised version of (
1.1
) to a nontrivial, purely atomic random measure which is
not
measurable with respect to the underlying Gaussian field
\mathrm{X}
. More precisely,
[
12
]
establishes this convergence for a particular class of log-correlated Gaussian fields known as
\star
-scale invariant fields
, using a specific approximation called the
martingale approximation
. The primary objective of this paper is to extend this convergence result to a large class of log-correlated Gaussian fields and their convolution approximations.

2.2
Topological preliminaries

We now recall some facts about stable convergence of random measures. This type of convergence, differently from the convergence in distribution, is a convergence of the sequence of random variables itself rather than of the sequence of their distributions. We refer to the monographs
[
8
,
7
]
and references therein for more details on stable convergence in a more general setting.

For a domain
\mathrm{D}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we equip the space
\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathrm{D})
of non-negative, locally finite measures on
\mathrm{D}
with the topology of vague convergence. We equip the space of probability measures on
\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathrm{D})
with the topology of weak convergence.
For a sequence
(\upnu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathrm{D})
-valued random variables, we write
\upnu_{n}\Rightarrow\upnu
to indicate that
\upnu_{n}
converges
vaguely in distribution
to
\upnu
in
\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathrm{D})
as
n\to\infty
.

We consider a collection
(\upnu_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathrm{D})
-valued random variables defined on
a common probability space
(\Omega,\mathbb{P})
and a
\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathrm{D})
-valued random variables
\upnu
defined
on a possibly larger probability space. We also fix a
\sigma
-algebra
\Sigma
over
\Omega
.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that
\upnu_{n}
converges
\Sigma
-stably to
\upnu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathrm{D})
as
n\to\infty
, if
(Z,\upnu_{n})\Rightarrow(Z,\upnu)
as
n\to\infty
for all
\Sigma
-measurable random variables
Z
.

Remark 2.2
.

If
\Sigma
is the trivial
\sigma
-algebra, then this coincides with convergence in law. Conversely, if
\Sigma
is the full
\sigma
-algebra of the probability space
\Omega
and the limiting random variable is defined on
(\Omega,\mathbb{P})
, then this corresponds to convergence in probability. Therefore, one can view stable convergence as a mode of convergence that lies between convergence in distribution and convergence in probability.

Given a random variable
\mathrm{Y}

To be more precise, the setting we consider is as follows. Let
\mathrm{X}^{\star}

(K1)

(K2)

\upmu^{\star}_{\upgamma,t}(dx)\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}t^{\frac{3\upgamma}{2\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}}}e^{t(\upgamma/\sqrt{2}-\sqrt{d})^{2}}e^{\upgamma(\mathrm{X}^{\star}_{t}(x)+\mathrm{W}_{t}(x))-\frac{\upgamma^{2}}{2}t}dx\;.

(4.1)

With this notation in place, we are now ready to state the main result of this section.

Lemma 4.1
.

For
\upgamma>\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}

\smash{c^{\star}_{\upgamma}>0}

We begin by noting that the convergence of the measure

[

\mathfrak{K}
of the field
\mathrm{X}^{\star}
and the covariance of the field

are compactly supported. Therefore, the goal of this section is to extend this convergence also to the case where

is not compactly supported and

The strategy first involves introducing a collection of fields that approximate

We recall that Kahane’s convexity inequality essentially allows for the comparison of GMC measures associated with two slightly different fields.
It can be stated as follows, and we refer to

Lemma 4.2

be a convex function with at most polynomial growth at

and
\infty
, and let
f:\mathrm{D}\to[0,\infty)
be a continuous function. Then, we have that

\mathbb{E}\Biggl[\upvarphi\Biggl(\int_{\mathrm{D}}f(x)e^{\mathrm{X}(x)-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{X}(x)^{2}]}dx\Biggr)\Biggr]\leq\mathbb{E}\Biggl[\upvarphi\Biggl(\int_{\mathrm{D}}f(x)e^{\mathrm{Y}(x)-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{Y}(x)^{2}]}dx\Biggr)\Biggr]\;.

The remainder of this section is organised as follows. In Section
4.1
, we introduce suitable collections of fields with finite-range correlations that approximate
\mathrm{X}^{\star}
and
\mathrm{W}
. Then, in Section
4.2
, we show how to remove the cutoff and obtain the desired result using Kahane’s convexity inequality.

4.1
A collection of fields with finite-range correlations

We begin by introducing the fields that will be the focus of this section. To this end, we consider a non-negative, radial function
\upvarphi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
whose support is contained in
B(0,1/2)
and satisfies
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\upvarphi(x)^{2}dx=1
. We then define the function
\upchi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
by setting

\upchi(x)\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}(\upvarphi*\upvarphi)(x)\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\;.

Under the above assumptions on
\upvarphi
, it is easily seen that
\upchi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is non-negative, radial, with support contained in
B(0,1)
, and such that
\upchi(0)=1
. Furthermore, for
\updelta>0
, we define
\smash{\upchi_{\updelta}(\cdot)\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\upchi(\updelta\cdot)}
, so that
\upchi_{\updelta}
converges to
1
over compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
as
\updelta\to 0
. With this notation in hand, we introduce the following collections of fields.

•

Let
\mathfrak{K}
be the seed covariance function of the field
\mathrm{X}^{\star}
defined in (
1.4
). For every
\updelta>0
, we define the function
\mathfrak{K}_{\updelta}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
by letting, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\mathfrak{K}_{\updelta}(x)\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\mathfrak{K}(x)\upchi_{\updelta}(x)\;,

which is a positive definite kernel as it is the product of two positive definite functions. We let
\overline{\mathfrak{K}}_{\updelta}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be the function such that the convolution with itself equals
\mathfrak{K}_{\updelta}
.
Let
\upxi
be the space-time white noise on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
used in the definition (
1.4
) of
\mathrm{X}^{\star}
. We define the field
\mathrm{X}^{\smash[b]{\star,\updelta}}
by letting

\mathrm{X}^{\smash[b]{\star,\updelta}}(\cdot)\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\overline{\mathfrak{K}}_{\updelta}\bigl(e^{r}(y-\cdot)\bigr)e^{\frac{dr}{2}}\upxi(dy,dr)\;.

(4.2)

Furthermore, for
0\leq s<t
, we let
\mathrm{X}^{\smash[b]{\star,\updelta}}_{s,t}
be the field on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
given by

\mathrm{X}^{\smash[b]{\star,\updelta}}_{s,t}(\cdot)\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{s}^{t}\overline{\mathfrak{K}}_{\updelta}\bigl(e^{r}(y-\cdot)\bigr)e^{\frac{dr}{2}}\upxi(dy,dr)\;,

with the convention that the subscript
s
is dropped when
s=0
.
By definition, the seed covariance function
\mathfrak{K}_{\updelta}
satisfies all the conditions stated in
(K1)
–
(K2)
with the further property that
\mathfrak{K}_{\updelta}
has compact support. For all
0\leq s<t
, the field
\mathrm{X}^{\smash[b]{\star,\updelta}}_{s,t}
has the following covariance structure,

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathrm{X}^{\smash[b]{\star,\updelta}}_{s,t}(x)\mathrm{X}^{\smash[b]{\star,\updelta}}_{s,t}(y)\bigr]=\int_{s}^{t}\mathfrak{K}_{\updelta}\bigl(e^{r}(x-y)\bigr)dr\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\,x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\;.

(4.3)

For
0\leq s<t
, we also introduce the field
\mathrm{X}^{\star,\updelta,s}_{t}
(note that
s
appears in the superscript), defined by letting

\mathrm{X}^{\star,s,\updelta}_{t}(\cdot)\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\mathrm{X}^{\smash[b]{\star}}_{s}(\cdot)+\mathrm{X}^{\star,\updelta}_{s,t}(\cdot)\;.

(4.4)

We observe that, by construction, the two fields on the right-hand side of the above display are independent.

•

For each
\updelta>0
, we define the field
\mathrm{W}_{\updelta}
as the stationary, centred Gaussian field with covariance kernel given by

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathrm{W}_{\updelta}(x)\mathrm{W}_{\updelta}(y)\bigr]=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathrm{W}(x)\mathrm{W}(y)\bigr]\upchi_{\updelta}(x-y)\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\,x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\;,

which is still a valid covariance kernel as it is the product of two positive definite functions. Furthermore, for every
t\geq 0
, we define
\smash{\mathrm{W}^{\updelta}_{t}}
as the stationary, centred Gaussian field with covariance kernel given by

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathrm{W}^{\updelta}_{t}(x)\mathrm{W}^{\updelta}_{t}(y)\bigr]=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathrm{W}^{\updelta}(e^{t}x)\mathrm{W}^{\updelta}(e^{t}y)\bigr]\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\,x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\;.

We now state and prove the following result which guarantees that
\mathrm{X}^{\smash[b]{\star,\updelta}}_{s,t}
and
\mathrm{W}^{\updelta}_{t}
are “good approximation” of
\mathrm{X}^{\star}_{s,t}
and
\mathrm{W}_{t}
, respectively.

Lemma 4.3
.

For any
\upvarepsilon>0
there exists
\updelta>0
small enough such that, for all
x
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
0\leq s<t
, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathrm{X}^{\star,\updelta}_{s,t}(x)\mathrm{X}^{\star,\updelta}_{s,t}(y)\bigr]-\upvarepsilon

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathrm{X}^{\star}_{s,t}(x)\mathrm{X}^{\star}_{s,t}(y)\bigr]\leq\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathrm{X}^{\star,\updelta}_{s,t}(x)\mathrm{X}^{\star,\updelta}_{s,t}(y)\bigr]+\upvarepsilon\;,

(4.5)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathrm{W}_{t}^{\updelta}(x)\mathrm{W}_{t}^{\updelta}(y)\bigr]-\upvarepsilon

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathrm{W}_{t}(x)\mathrm{W}_{t}(y)\bigr]\leq\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathrm{W}_{t}^{\updelta}(x)\mathrm{W}_{t}^{\updelta}(y)\bigr]+\upvarepsilon\;.

(4.6)

Proof.

Since all the fields involved are stationary, we can, without loss of generality, set
y=0
for simplicity.
We start by proving (
4.5
). Let
\upvarepsilon>0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
0\leq s<t
. Recalling (
1.6
) and (
4.3
), we note that

\lvert\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathrm{X}^{\star}_{s,t}(x)\mathrm{X}^{\star}_{s,t}(0)\bigr]-\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathrm{X}^{\star,\updelta}_{s,t}(x)\mathrm{X}^{\star,\updelta}_{s,t}(0)\bigr]\rvert\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\lvert\mathfrak{K}\bigl(e^{r}x\bigr)-\mathfrak{K}_{\updelta}\bigl(e^{r}x\bigr)\rvert dr\;.

At this point, the conclusion follows by applying the exact same strategy as in the proof of
[
12
, Lemma 6.2]
.

We now prove (
4.6
). Let
\upvarepsilon>0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
0\leq s<t
. Thanks to the decay of correlations of the field
\mathrm{W}
, we can find
\mathrm{R}=\mathrm{R}(\upvarepsilon)>0
large enough, only depending on
\upvarepsilon
, such that
\lvert\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{W}(x)\mathrm{W}(0)]\rvert\leq\upvarepsilon
, for all
|x|>\mathrm{R}
. Hence, for all
t\geq 0
,

\sup_{\lvert x\rvert>\mathrm{R}}\bigl|\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathrm{W}_{t}(x)\mathrm{W}_{t}(0)\bigr]-\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathrm{W}_{t}^{\updelta}(x)\mathrm{W}_{t}^{\updelta}(0)\bigr]\bigr|=\sup_{\lvert x\rvert>\mathrm{R}}\bigl|\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathrm{W}_{t}(x)\mathrm{W}_{t}(0)\bigr]\bigr|\lvert 1-\upchi_{\updelta}(x)\rvert\leq\upvarepsilon.

On the other hand, uniformly over
|x|\leq\mathrm{R}
, it holds that
\upchi_{\updelta}(x)\to 1
as
\updelta\to 0
. Therefore, in this case, the conclusion follows from the fact that the covariance kernel of
\mathrm{W}
is uniformly bounded on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
∎

4.2
Removing the cutoff

For each
\updelta>0
and
0\leq s<t
, we define the measure
\upmu^{\smash[b]{\star,s,\updelta}}_{\upgamma,t}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by letting

\upmu^{\smash[b]{\star,s,\updelta}}_{\upgamma,t}(dx)=t^{\frac{3\upgamma}{2\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}}}e^{t(\upgamma/\sqrt{2}-\sqrt{d})^{2}}e^{\upgamma(\mathrm{X}^{\smash[b]{\star,s,\updelta}}_{t}(x)+\mathrm{W}^{\updelta}_{t}(x))-\frac{\upgamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[{\mathrm{X}^{\smash[b]{\star,s,\updelta}}_{t}(x)}^{2}]}dx\;,

(4.7)

where we recall that the field
\smash{\mathrm{X}^{\smash[b]{\star,s,\updelta}}_{t}}
is defined in (
4.4
). Furthermore, we recall that
\upmu^{\smash[b]{\star}}_{\gammac}
denotes the critical GMC measure associated with
\mathrm{X}^{\star}
. We have the following key result which is a consequence of
[
3
]
.

Lemma 4.4
.

For
\smash{\upgamma>\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}}
and
\updelta>0
, consider the sequence of random measures
\smash{(\upmu^{\smash[b]{\star,s,\updelta}}_{\upgamma,t})_{0\leq s<t}}
introduced in (
4.7
). Then, there exists a finite constant
c^{\star}_{\upgamma,\updelta}>0
such that
\smash{\upmu^{\smash[b]{\star,s,\updelta}}_{\upgamma,t}}
converges
\sigma(\mathrm{X}^{\star})
-stably to
\smash{c^{\star}_{\upgamma,\updelta}\mathcal{P}_{\upgamma}[\upmu^{\smash[b]{\star}}_{\gammac}]}
as
t\to\infty
, followed by
s\to\infty
.

Proof.

This is a consequence of the proof of
[
3
, Theorem C]
. To be precise, we recall that the field
\smash{\mathrm{X}^{\smash[b]{\star,s,\updelta}}_{t}}
is given by the following sum

\mathrm{X}^{\star,s,\updelta}_{t}=\mathrm{X}^{\smash[b]{\star}}_{s}+\mathrm{X}^{\star,\updelta}_{s,t}\;,

where the first term on the right-hand side corresponds to the “large scales field” and the second term to the “small scales field”. The only subtlety is that the large scales field does not have compactly supported covariance. However, the proof of
[
3
, Theorem C]
(see
[
3
, Section 6]
for more details) remains valid in this more general setting. Indeed, at no point in the proof do we rely on the large scales field having compactly supported covariance, whereas it is crucial that the small scales field does.
∎

In what follows, in order to lighten the notation, we let

\upmu^{\smash[b]{\star,\updelta}}_{\upgamma}=c^{\star}_{\upgamma,\updelta}\mathcal{P}_{\upgamma}[\upmu^{\smash[b]{\star}}_{\gammac}]\;,\hskip 20.00003pt\upmu^{\star}_{\upgamma}=c^{\star}_{\upgamma}\mathcal{P}_{\upgamma}[\upmu^{\star}_{\gammac}]\;,

(4.8)

for some constant
c^{\star}_{\upgamma}
(that will be identified with the limit as
\updelta\to 0
of
c^{\star}_{\upgamma,\updelta}
).

Now, thanks to Lemma
2.3
and
[
3
, Lemma 3.4]
, the convergence in Lemma
4.4
holds if and only if, for all
(\upvarphi,f)\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathcal{C}_{c}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\lim_{s\to\infty}\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\exp\bigl(i\langle\mathrm{X}^{\star},\upvarphi\rangle\bigr)\exp\bigl(-\upmu^{\smash[b]{\star,s,\updelta}}_{\upgamma,t}(f)\bigr)\Bigr]=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\exp\bigl(i\langle\mathrm{X}^{\star},\upvarphi\rangle\bigr)\exp\bigl(-\upmu^{\smash[b]{\star,\updelta}}_{\upgamma}(f)\bigr)\Bigr]\;.

(4.9)

Therefore, in order to prove Lemma
4.1
, we would like to replace
\smash{\upmu^{\smash[b]{\star,s,\updelta}}_{\upgamma,t}}
by
\smash{\upmu^{\star}_{\upgamma,t}}
and
\smash{\upmu^{\smash[b]{\star,\updelta}}_{\upgamma}}
by
\smash{\upmu^{\star}_{\upgamma}}
in both sides of the above convergence. To do so, we divide the proof in two main lemmas. Specifically, in Lemma
4.5
, we show that the limit of
\upmu_{\upgamma,t}^{\star}
as
t\to\infty
coincides with the limit of
\smash{\upmu_{\upgamma}^{\star,\updelta}}
as
\updelta\to 0
. Then, in Lemma
4.6
, we compute the limit of
\smash{\upmu_{\upgamma}^{\star,\updelta}}
as
\updelta\to 0
.

Lemma 4.5
.

For any
\upgamma>\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}
and for any
(\upvarphi,f)\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathcal{C}_{c}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, it holds that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\exp\bigl(i\langle\mathrm{X}^{\star},\upvarphi\rangle\bigr)\exp\bigl(-\upmu^{\star}_{\upgamma,t}(f)\bigr)\Bigr]=\lim_{\updelta\to 0}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\exp\bigl(i\langle\mathrm{X}^{\star},\upvarphi\rangle\bigr)\exp\bigl(-\upmu^{\smash[b]{\star,\updelta}}_{\upgamma}(f)\bigr)\Bigr]\;,

where both limits exist and are nontrivial, and where we recall that
\upmu^{\star}_{\upgamma,t}
is defined in (
4.1
), and
\upmu_{\upgamma}^{\smash[b]{\star,\updelta}}
in (
4.8
).

Proof.

We fix
(\upvarphi,f)\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathcal{C}_{c}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. For a field
(\mathrm{Y}(x))_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
, we let

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}_{+}(\mathrm{Y},\upvarphi)\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\bigl[\mathrm{Re}\bigl(\exp\bigl(i\langle\mathrm{Y},\upvarphi\rangle\bigr)\bigr)\bigr]^{+}\;,\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}_{-}(\mathrm{Y},\upvarphi)\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\bigl[\mathrm{Re}\bigl(\exp\bigl(i\langle\mathrm{Y},\upvarphi\rangle\bigr)\bigr)\bigr]^{-}\;,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}_{+}(\mathrm{Y},\upvarphi)\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\bigl[\mathrm{Im}\bigl(\exp\bigl(i\langle\mathrm{Y},\upvarphi\rangle\bigr)\bigr)\bigr]^{+}\;,\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}_{-}(\mathrm{Y},\upvarphi)\mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\bigl[\mathrm{Im}\bigl(\exp\bigl(i\langle\mathrm{Y},\upvarphi\rangle\bigr)\bigr)\bigr]^{-}\;,

where
x^{+}=x\vee 0
and
x^{-}=(-x)\vee 0
.

For
0\leq s<t
and
\updelta>0
, by definition of the measure
\smash{\upmu^{\smash[b]{\star,s,\updelta}}_{\upgamma,t}}
in (
4.7
) and the field
\smash{\mathrm{X}^{\smash[b]{\star,s,\updelta}}_{t}}
in (
4.4
), we have that

\upmu^{\smash[b]{\star,s,\updelta}}_{\upgamma,t}(f)=t^{\frac{3\upgamma}{2\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}}}e^{t(\upgamma/\sqrt{2}-\sqrt{d})^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)e^{\upgamma(\mathrm{X}^{\smash[b]{\star}}_{s}(x)+\mathrm{X}^{\star,\updelta}_{s,t}(x)+\mathrm{W}_{t}^{\updelta}(x))-\frac{\upgamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[{\mathrm{X}^{\smash[b]{\star}}_{s}(x)}^{2}+{\mathrm{X}^{\smash[b]{\star,\updelta}}_{s,t}(x)}^{2}]}dx\;,

where we recall that the field
\smash{\mathrm{X}^{\smash[b]{\star}}_{s}}
and
\smash{\mathrm{X}^{\smash[b]{\star,\updelta}}_{s,t}}
are independent.
We observe that, for any
\upvarepsilon>0
, thanks to Lemma
4.3
, for
\updelta>0
small enough and for all
x
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it holds that

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathrm{X}^{\star,\updelta}_{s,t}(x)\mathrm{X}^{\star,\updelta}_{s,t}(y)\bigr]+\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathrm{W}_{t}^{\updelta}(x)\mathrm{W}_{t}^{\updelta}(y)\bigr]\leq\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathrm{X}^{\star}_{s,t}(x)\mathrm{X}^{\star}_{s,t}(y)\bigr]+\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathrm{W}_{t}(x)\mathrm{W}_{t}(y)\bigr]+\upvarepsilon\;,

where we recall that the fields
\smash{\mathrm{X}^{\star,\updelta}_{s,t}}
and
\mathrm{W}_{t}^{\updelta}
are independent.
Therefore, letting
\mathcal{N}
be a standard normal random variable independent of everything else, for
0\leq u<s<t
, we can apply Kahane’s convexity inequality (Lemma
4.2
) with respect to the conditional expectation given
\mathcal{F}_{s}
(recall definition (
1.7
)) with the function
\mathbb{R}^{+}\ni x\mapsto e^{-x}
to obtain that

\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\mathrm{Re}_{+}(\mathrm{X}^{\star}_{u},\upvarphi)\exp\bigl(-\upmu^{\smash[b]{\star,s,\updelta}}_{\upgamma,t}(f)\bigr)\Bigr]\leq\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\mathrm{Re}_{+}(\mathrm{X}^{\star}_{u},\upvarphi)\exp\bigl(-e^{\upgamma\sqrt{\upvarepsilon}\mathcal{N}-\frac{\upgamma^{2}}{2}\upvarepsilon}\upmu^{\star}_{\upgamma,t}(f)\bigr)\Bigr]\;.

In particular, from the above inequality, we can deduce that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\mathrm{Re}_{+}(\mathrm{X}^{\star},\upvarphi)\exp\bigl(-\upmu^{\smash[b]{\star,s,\updelta}}_{\upgamma,t}(f)\bigr)\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\mathrm{Re}_{+}(\mathrm{X}^{\star},\upvarphi)\exp\bigl(-e^{\upgamma\sqrt{\upvarepsilon}\mathcal{N}-\frac{\upgamma^{2}}{2}\upvarepsilon}\upmu^{\star}_{\upgamma,t}(f)\bigr)\Bigr]

(4.10)

\displaystyle+2\mathbb{E}\bigl[\bigl|\mathrm{Re}_{+}(\mathrm{X}^{\star},\upvarphi)-\mathrm{Re}_{+}(\mathrm{X}^{\star}_{u},\upvarphi)\bigr|\bigr]\;.

Similarly, for any
\upvarepsilon>0
, thanks again to Lemma
4.3
, we know that for
\updelta>0
small enough and for all
x
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it holds that

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathrm{X}^{\star}_{s,t}(x)\mathrm{X}^{\star}_{s,t}(y)\bigr]+\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathrm{W}_{t}(x)\mathrm{W}_{t}(y)\bigr]\leq\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathrm{X}^{\star,\updelta}_{s,t}(x)\mathrm{X}^{\star,\updelta}_{s,t}(y)\bigr]+\mathbb{E}\bigl[\mathrm{W}_{t}^{\updelta}(x)\mathrm{W}_{t}^{\updelta}(y)\bigr]+\upvarepsilon\;.

Hence, letting
\mathcal{N}
be as above, and by applying Kahane’s convexity inequality (Lemma
4.2
) as above, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\mathrm{Re}_{+}(\mathrm{X}^{\star},\upvarphi)\exp\bigl(-\upmu^{\star}_{\upgamma,t}(f)\bigr)\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\mathrm{Re}_{+}(\mathrm{X}^{\star},\upvarphi)\exp\bigl(-e^{\upgamma\sqrt{\upvarepsilon}\mathcal{N}-\frac{\upgamma^{2}}{2}\upvarepsilon}\upmu^{\smash[b]{\star,s,\updelta}}_{\upgamma,t}(f)\bigr)\Bigr]

(4.11)

\displaystyle+2\mathbb{E}\bigl[\bigl|\mathrm{Re}_{+}(\mathrm{X}^{\star},\upvarphi)-\mathrm{Re}_{+}(\mathrm{X}^{\star}_{u},\upvarphi)\bigr|\bigr]\;.

Now, we observe that the sequence of random measures
\smash{(\upmu^{\star}_{\upgamma,t})_{t\geq 0}}
is tight under the topology of vague convergence, and every converging subsequence is nontrivial (see
[
5
, Proposition 10]
whose proof is not reliant on the seed covariance function being compactly supported).
In particular, by taking the limit as
t^{\prime}\to\infty
along a subsequence in (
4.10
), for any
\upzeta\in(0,1)
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\lim_{t^{\prime}\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\mathrm{Re}_{+}(\mathrm{X}^{\star},\upvarphi)

\displaystyle\exp\bigl(-(1-\upzeta)\upmu^{\star}_{\upgamma,t^{\prime}}(f)\bigr)\Bigr]+\mathbb{P}\Bigl(e^{\upgamma\sqrt{\upvarepsilon}\mathcal{N}-\frac{\upgamma^{2}}{2}\upvarepsilon}\leq 1-\upzeta\Bigr)

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{t^{\prime}\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\mathrm{Re}_{+}(\mathrm{X}^{\star},\upvarphi)\exp\bigl(-e^{\upgamma\sqrt{\upvarepsilon}\mathcal{N}-\frac{\upgamma^{2}}{2}\upvarepsilon}\upmu^{\star}_{\upgamma,t^{\prime}}(f)\bigr)\Bigr]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\mathrm{Re}_{+}(\mathrm{X}^{\star},\upvarphi)\exp\bigl(-\upmu^{\smash[b]{\star,\updelta}}_{\upgamma}(f)\bigr)\Bigr]\;,

(4.12)

where we also took the limits as
s\to\infty
and
u\to\infty
in (
4.10
) and we applied Lemma
4.4
along with the fact that
\mathrm{X}_{u}
converges almost surely to
\mathrm{X}
in
\mathcal{H}^{-\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for any
\kappa>0
.

Proceeding in the same manner, by taking the limit as
t^{\prime}\to\infty
along the same subsequence in (
4.11
), for any
\upzeta\in(0,1)
, we get that

\displaystyle\lim_{t^{\prime}\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\mathrm{Re}_{+}(\mathrm{X}^{\star},\upvarphi)

\displaystyle\exp\bigl(-\upmu^{\star}_{\upgamma,t^{\prime}}(f)\bigr)\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\mathrm{Re}_{+}(\mathrm{X}^{\star},\upvarphi)\exp\bigl(-e^{\upgamma\sqrt{\upvarepsilon}\mathcal{N}-\frac{\upgamma^{2}}{2}\upvarepsilon}\upmu^{\smash[b]{\star,\updelta}}_{\upgamma}(f)\bigr)\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\mathrm{Re}_{+}(\mathrm{X}^{\star},\upvarphi)\exp\bigl(-(1-\upzeta)\upmu^{\smash[b]{\star,\updelta}}_{\upgamma}(f)\bigr)\Bigr]+\mathbb{P}\Bigl(e^{\upgamma\sqrt{\upvarepsilon}\mathcal{N}-\frac{\upgamma^{2}}{2}\upvarepsilon}\leq 1-\upzeta\Bigr)\;.

(4.13)

In particular, by taking the
\limsup_{\updelta\to 0}
and the
\liminf_{\updelta\to 0}
followed by the
\lim_{\upvarepsilon\to 0}
in (
4.12
) and in (
4.13
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\limsup_{\updelta\to 0}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\mathrm{Re}_{+}(\mathrm{X}^{\star},\upvarphi)\exp\bigl(-\upmu^{\smash[b]{\star,\updelta}}_{\upgamma}(f)\bigr)\Bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{t^{\prime}\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\mathrm{Re}_{+}(\mathrm{X}^{\star},\upvarphi)\exp\bigl(-(1-\upzeta)\upmu^{\star}_{\upgamma,t^{\prime}}(f)\bigr)\Bigr]\;,

\displaystyle\liminf_{\updelta\to 0}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\mathrm{Re}_{+}(\mathrm{X}^{\star},\upvarphi)\exp\bigl(-\upmu^{\smash[b]{\star,\updelta}}_{\upgamma}(f)\bigr)\Bigr]

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{t^{\prime}\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\mathrm{Re}_{+}(\mathrm{X}^{\star},\upvarphi)\exp\bigl(-(1-\upzeta)^{-1}\upmu^{\star}_{\upgamma,t^{\prime}}(f)\bigr)\Bigr]\;.

Therefore, by arbitrariness of
\upzeta\in(0,1)
, we get that

\lim_{t^{\prime}\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\mathrm{Re}_{+}(\mathrm{X}^{\star},\upvarphi)\exp\bigl(-\upmu^{\star}_{\upgamma,t^{\prime}}(f)\bigr)\Bigr]=\lim_{\updelta\to 0}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\mathrm{Re}_{+}(\mathrm{X}^{\star},\upvarphi)\exp\bigl(-\upmu^{\smash[b]{\star,\updelta}}_{\upgamma}(f)\bigr)\Bigr]\;.

By applying the same argument to
\mathrm{Re}_{-}(\mathrm{X}^{\star},\upvarphi)
,
\mathrm{Im}_{+}(\mathrm{X}^{\star},\upvarphi)
, and
\mathrm{Im}_{-}(\mathrm{X}^{\star},\upvarphi)
, the desired result follows from the fact that the subsequence along which the limit was taken was arbitrary.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

For any
\upgamma>\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}
, there exists a finite constant
a_{\star}>0
such that, for any
(\upvarphi,f)\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathcal{C}_{c}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, it holds that

\lim_{\updelta\to 0}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\exp\bigl(i\langle\mathrm{X}^{\star},\upvarphi\rangle\bigr)\exp\bigl(-\upmu^{\smash[b]{\star,\updelta}}_{\upgamma}(f)\bigr)\Bigr]=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\exp\bigl(i\langle\mathrm{X}^{\star},\upvarphi\rangle\bigr)\exp\bigl(-\upmu^{\star}_{\upgamma}(f)\bigr)\Bigr]\;,

where we recall that the constant
c^{\star}_{\upgamma}
appears in the definition (
4.8
) of the measure
\upmu_{\upgamma}^{\star}
.

Proof.

Recalling (
4.8
), we note that it suffices to prove that
\lim_{\updelta\to 0}c^{\star}_{\upgamma,\updelta}=c^{\star}_{\upgamma}
for some positive
c^{\star}_{\upgamma}>0
. This fact follows immediately from Lemma
4.5
.
∎

We are finally ready to prove Lemma
4.1
, whose proof follows immediately by combining Lemmas
4.5
and
4.6
.

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

We recall that it suffices to prove that for all
(\upvarphi,f)\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathcal{C}_{c}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\exp\bigl(i\langle\mathrm{X}^{\star},\upvarphi\rangle\bigr)\exp\bigl(-\upmu^{\star}_{\upgamma,t}(f)\bigr)\Bigr]=\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\exp\bigl(i\langle\mathrm{X}^{\star},\upvarphi\rangle\bigr)\exp\bigl(-\upmu^{\star}_{\upgamma}(f)\bigr)\Bigr]\;.

(4.14)

By using the triangle inequality, for any
t\geq 0
and
\updelta>0
, we have that

\displaystyle\Bigl|\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\exp\bigl(i

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{X}^{\star},\upvarphi\rangle\bigr)\exp\bigl(-\upmu^{\star}_{\upgamma,t}(f)\bigr)\Bigr]-\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\exp\bigl(i\langle\mathrm{X}^{\star},\upvarphi\rangle\bigr)\exp\bigl(-\upmu^{\star}_{\upgamma}(f)\bigr)\Bigr]\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl|\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\exp\bigl(i\langle\mathrm{X}^{\star},\upvarphi\rangle\bigr)\exp\bigl(-\upmu^{\star}_{\upgamma,t}(f)\bigr)\Bigr]-\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\exp\bigl(i\langle\mathrm{X}^{\star},\upvarphi\rangle\bigr)\exp\bigl(-\upmu^{\star,\updelta}_{\upgamma}(f)\bigr)\Bigr]\Bigr|

\displaystyle+\Bigl|\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\exp\bigl(i\langle\mathrm{X}^{\star},\upvarphi\rangle\bigr)\exp\bigl(-\upmu^{\star,\updelta}_{\upgamma}(f)\bigr)\Bigr]-\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\exp\bigl(i\langle\mathrm{X}^{\star},\upvarphi\rangle\bigr)\exp\bigl(-\upmu^{\star}_{\upgamma}(f)\bigr)\Bigr]\Bigr|\;.

Therefore, the conclusion follows by a direct application of Lemmas
4.5
and
4.6
.
∎

Appendix A
Moments of supercritical GMC

In this appendix, we gather some properties concerning the existence of moments and the multifractal spectrum of supercritical GMC measures, which follow directly from their definition. We emphasise that the results presented in this appendix are not used anywhere in the present paper but are recorded here for future reference.

In what follows, we consider the same setting as specified in Theorem
A
. In particular, for
\upgamma>\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}
, we let
\upmu_{\upgamma}
denote the supercritical GMC, i.e.,

\upmu_{\upgamma}=a_{\upgamma,\uprho}\mathcal{P}_{\upgamma}[\overline{\upmu}_{\gammac}]\;,

where we recall that
\smash{\overline{\upmu}_{\gammac}}
is the critical GMC defined in (
1.9
).

Proposition A.1

(Positive moments)
.

For
\upgamma>\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}
and for any
\mathrm{A}\subset\mathrm{D}
non-empty, bounded and open, the random variable
\upmu_{\upgamma}(\mathrm{A})
posses finite moments of order
q\in(0,\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}/\upgamma)
.

Proof.

Let
\upgamma>\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}
and fix a set
\mathrm{A}\subset\mathrm{D}
as in the proposition statement. We note that for every
x\geq 0
and
q\in(0,1)
it holds that

x^{q}=-\frac{1}{\Gamma(-q)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigl(1-\exp(-zx)\bigr)\frac{dz}{z^{1+q}}\;.

(A.1)

Hence, for
q\in(0,\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}/\upgamma)
, thanks to (
A.1
) and (
2.2
), there exists a constant
c>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[\upmu_{\upgamma}(\mathrm{A})^{q}\bigr]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\Gamma(-q)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigl(1-\mathbb{E}\bigl[\exp\bigl(-z\upmu_{\upgamma}(\mathrm{A})\bigr)\bigr]\bigr)\frac{dz}{z^{1+q}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\Gamma(-q)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigl(1-\mathbb{E}\bigl[\exp\bigl(-cz^{\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}/\upgamma}\overline{\upmu}_{\gammac}(\mathrm{A})\bigr)\bigr]\bigr)\frac{dz}{z^{1+q}}\;.

Therefore, performing a change of variables, one obtains that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[\upmu_{\upgamma}(\mathrm{A})^{q}\bigr]=\frac{\upgamma c^{\frac{\upgamma q}{\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}}}\Gamma\bigl(-\upgamma q/\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}\bigr)}{\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}\Gamma(-q)}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\overline{\upmu}_{\gammac}(\mathrm{A})^{\upgamma q/\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}}\bigr]\;,

and the result now follows thanks to the fact that
\upgamma q/\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}<1
and from
[
14
, Theorem 2.11]
.
∎

Proposition A.2

(Negative moments)
.

For
\upgamma>\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}
any for any
\mathrm{A}\subset\mathrm{D}
non-empty, bounded and open, the random variable
\upmu_{\upgamma}(\mathrm{A})
posses finite moments of every order
q<0
.

Proof.

Let
\upgamma>\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}
and fix a set
\mathrm{A}\subset\mathrm{D}
as in the proposition statement. We recall that for every
x>0
and
q>0
it holds that

\Gamma(q)=x^{q}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-zx)\frac{dz}{z^{1-q}}\;.

(A.2)

Hence, if we fix
q>0
, thanks to (
A.2
) and (
2.2
), there exists a constant
c>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[\upmu_{\upgamma}(\mathrm{A})^{-q}\bigr]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(q)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\exp\left(-z\upmu_{\upgamma}(\mathrm{A})\right)\bigr]\frac{dz}{z^{1-q}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(q)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\exp\bigl(-cz^{\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}/\upgamma}\overline{\upmu}_{\gammac}(\mathrm{A})\bigr)\Bigr]\frac{dz}{z^{1-q}}\;.

Therefore, performing a change of variables, one obtains that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[\upmu_{\upgamma}(\mathrm{A})^{-q}\bigr]=\frac{\upgamma\Gamma(\upgamma q/\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}})}{\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}c^{\frac{\upgamma q}{\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}}}\Gamma(q)}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\overline{\upmu}_{\gammac}(\mathrm{A})^{-\upgamma q/\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}}\bigr]\;.

and the result now follows from
[
14
, Theorem 2.11]
.
∎

Proposition A.3

(Multifractal spectrum)
.

For
\upgamma>\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}
and for any
q<\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}/\upgamma
, there exists a constant
c_{q}>0
such that for any
\mathrm{A}\subset\mathrm{D}
non-empty, bounded and open, it holds that

\mathbb{E}\bigl[\upmu_{\upgamma}(r\mathrm{A})^{q}\bigr]\mathrel{{\mathop{\asymp}\limits}}c_{q}r^{\upxi_{\upgamma}(q)}\;,

where
\upxi_{\upgamma}(q)=\sqrt{\smash[b]{2d}}\upgamma q-\upgamma^{2}q^{2}/2
, and the implicit constant depends only on
\upgamma
and
\mathrm{A}
.

Proof.

The result follows from the proofs of the previous two propositions and the known multifractal spectrum for critical GMC measures (see
[
14
, Theorem 2.11]
).
∎

On the spectral properties of the quantum cohomology of odd quadrics
Ryan M. Shifler, Stephanie Warman

Abstract
. Let H ∙ ​ ( OG ) H^{\bullet}(\mathrm{OG}) be the quantum cohomology (specialized at q = 1 q=1 ) of the 2 ​ n − 1 2n-1 dimensional quadric OG \mathrm{OG} . We will calculate the characteristic polynomial of the linear operators induced by quantum multiplication in H ∙ ​ ( OG ) H^{\bullet}(\mathrm{OG}) and the Frobenius-Perron dimension. We also check that Galkin’s lower bound conjecture holds for OG \mathrm{OG} .

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathrm{OG}:=\mathrm{OG}(1,2n+1)
be the
2n-1
dimensional quadric. This is the parameterization of isotropic 1 dimensional subspaces of
\mathbb{C}^{2n+1}
, isotropic with respect to a symmetric non-degenerate symmetric form. The quantum cohomology ring
(\mathrm{QH}^{*}(\mathrm{OG}),\star)
is a graded algebra over
{\mathbb{Z}}[q]
, where
q
is the quantum parameter and
\deg q=2n-1
. The ring
(\mathrm{QH}^{*}(\mathrm{OG}),\star)
has Schubert classes given by
\tau_{p}
, with
0\leq p\leq 2n-1
. The ring multiplication is given by

\tau_{p}\star\tau_{i}=\sum_{j,d_{0}\geq 0}c_{p,i}^{j,d_{0}}q^{d_{0}}\tau_{j}

where
c_{p,i}^{j,d_{0}}
is the Gromov-Witten invariant that enumerates degree
d_{0}
rational curves intersecting general translate of
\tau_{p},\tau_{i},
and the Poincaré dual of
\tau_{j}
which is
\tau_{2n-1-j}
.

Consider the specialization
H^{\bullet}(\mathrm{OG}):=\mathrm{QH}^{*}(\mathrm{OG})_{|q=1}
at
q=1
. The quantum multiplication by the Schubert class
\tau_{p}
, with
0\leq p\leq 2n-1
, induces an endomorphism
A(\tau_{p})
of the finite-dimensional vector space
H^{\bullet}(\mathrm{OG})
:

y\in H^{\bullet}(\mathrm{OG})\mapsto A(\tau_{p})(y):=(\tau_{p}\star y)|_{q=1}\/.

Denote by
\delta_{p}:=\max\{|\delta|:\delta\mbox{ is an eigenvalue of }A(\tau_{p})\}
the real largest eigenvalue of
A(\tau_{p})
. Define the Frobenius-Perron Dimension to be

\text{FPdim}:H^{\bullet}(\mathrm{OG})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\mbox{ defined by }\text{FPdim}(\tau_{p})=\delta_{p}.

The first study of Frobenius-Perron dimension on quantum cohomology specialized at
q=1
is for the Grassmannian in
[
Riet
]
with subsequent study in
[
LSYZ
]
.

To simplify notation we let
d:=\mathrm{GCD}(p,2n-1)
for the remainder of the article. We will prove the next two results.

Theorem 1.1
.

The characteristic polynomial of
A(\tau_{p})
is

\begin{cases}\lambda\left(\lambda^{\frac{2n-1}{d}}-2^{\frac{2p}{d}}\right)^{d}&1\leq p<n\\
\lambda\left(\lambda^{\frac{2n-1}{d}}-2^{\frac{2p-(2n-1)}{d}}\right)^{d}&n\leq p<2n-1\\
(\lambda-1)^{2n-1}(\lambda+1)&p=2n-1\end{cases}.

In particular, the eigenvalues of
A(\tau_{p})
are simple if and only if the integers
p
and
2n-1
are relatively prime.

The next corollary follows immediately from Theorem
1.1
.

Corollary 1.2
.

We have that
\text{FPdim}(\tau_{p})=\begin{cases}2^{\frac{2p}{2n-1}}&1\leq p<n\\
2^{\frac{2p}{2n-1}-1}&n\leq p<2n-1\\
1&p=2n-1.\end{cases}

The final result will be a proof of a lower bound on the real largest eigenvalue of the first Chern class. We will state this precisely next. Let
K:=K_{\mathrm{OG}}
be the canonical bundle of
\mathrm{OG}
and let
c_{1}:=c_{1}(-K)\in H^{2}(\mathrm{OG})
be the anticanonical class. Galkin’s lower bound conjecture for the
\mathrm{OG}
case

states that (see

Galkin

):

\text{FPdim}(A(c_{1}))\geq\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathrm{OG}+1.

Here,
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathrm{OG}=2n-1
and
c_{1}=(2n-1)\tau_{1}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Galkin’s lower bound conjecture holds for
\mathrm{OG}
.

2.
Preliminaries

We will rigorously define
\mathrm{OG}
. The
reverse dot product
of two vectors
\left(a_{1},a_{2}\ldots a_{n}\right)
and
\left(b_{1},b_{2},\ldots b_{n}\right)
is

(1)

\displaystyle\left(\left(a_{1},a_{2}\ldots a_{n}\right),\left(b_{1},b_{2},\ldots b_{n}\right)\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}b_{n+1-i}.

We are ready to define
\mathrm{OG}
.

Definition 2.1
.

The odd dimensional quadric
\mathrm{OG}
is

\mathrm{OG}=\left\{V\subset\mathbb{C}^{2n+1}:\dim V=1,\left(a,b\right)=0\;\forall\;a,b\in V\right\}.

Equivalently,
\mathrm{OG}
is the loci of
v_{n+1}^{2}+2\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}v_{2n+2-i}
.

2.1.
Schubert cells and varieties

The Schubert cells and Schubert varieties are denoted by
X(p)^{\circ}
and
X(p)
, respectively, with
0\leq p\leq 2n-1
. We will first state what the Schubert cells are and follow with a description of the Schubert varieties.

The Schubert cells are given as follows.

(1)

For
0\leq p\leq n
, we have that

X(p)^{\circ}=\left\{\mbox{span}\{(\overbrace{0,\dots,0}^{p},1,\overbrace{*,\dots,*}^{2n-1-2p},\bullet,\overbrace{*,\dots,*}^{p})\}:*\mbox{ is free and $\bullet$ is given by (\ref{revdot})}\right\}.

(2)

For
n+1\leq p\leq 2n-1
, we have that

X(p)^{\circ}=\left\{\mbox{span}\{(\overbrace{0,\dots,0}^{p+1},1,\overbrace{*,\dots,*}^{2n-1-p})\}:*\mbox{ is free }\right\}.

The Schubert variety
X(p)
is the closure of the Schubert cell
X(p)^{\circ}
. Precisely, the Schubert variety
X(p)
is given by

X(p)=\bigcup_{i\geq p}X(i)^{\circ}.

Furthermore,
\dim X(p)=\dim X(p)^{\circ}=(2n-1)-p.

2.2.
Quantum cohomology

We will now introduce the quantum cohomology ring of
\mathrm{OG}
. The (small) quantum cohomology of
\mathrm{OG}
, denoted by
\mbox{QH}^{*}(\mathrm{OG})
, has a graded
\mathbb{Z}[q]
-basis consisting of Schubert classes
\tau_{p}:=[X(p)]
where
0\leq p\leq 2n-1
and
\deg q=2n-1
. The ring multiplication is given by

\tau_{p}\star\tau_{i}=\sum_{j,d_{0}\geq 0}c_{p,i}^{j,d_{0}}q^{d_{0}}\tau_{j}

where
c_{p,i}^{j,d_{0}}
is the Gromov-Witten invariant that enumerates degree
d_{0}
rational curves intersecting general translate of
\tau_{p},\tau_{i},
and the Poincaré dual of
\tau_{j}
which is
\tau_{2n-1-j}
. We refer the reader to
[
FP
,
kontsevich.manin:GW:qc:enumgeom
]
for additional details regarding quantum cohomology.

The quantum Chevalley formula for
\mathrm{OG}
is given as follows (see
[
BKT
,
BKT2
]
).

\tau_{1}\star\tau_{p}=\begin{cases}\tau_{p+1}&1\leq p\leq n-1\\
2\tau_{n+1}&p=n\\
\tau_{p+1}&n+1\leq p\leq 2n-3\\
\tau_{2n-1}+q\tau_{0}&p=2n-2\\
q\tau_{1}&p=2n-1.\end{cases}

Consider the specialization
H^{\bullet}(\mathrm{OG}):=\mathrm{QH}^{*}(\mathrm{OG})_{|q=1}
at
q=1
. The quantum multiplication by
\tau_{p}
induces an endomorphism
A(\tau_{p})
of the finite-dimensional vector space
H^{\bullet}(\mathrm{OG})
:

y\in H^{\bullet}(\mathrm{OG}\mapsto A(\tau_{p})(y):=(A(\tau_{p})\star y)|_{q=1}\/.

Example 2.2
.

The matrix
A(\tau_{1})
for
\mathrm{OG}(1,3)
is computed below.

A(\tau_{1})=\begin{array}[]{ccccc}&\tau_{0}&\tau_{1}&\tau_{2}&\tau_{3}\\
\tau_{0}&0&0&1&0\\
\tau_{1}&1&0&0&1\\
\tau_{2}&0&2&0&0\\
\tau_{3}&0&0&1&0\\
\end{array}.

The next proposition follows from immediately from the quantum Chevalley formula.

Proposition 2.3
.

For
0\leq p\leq 2n-1
, we have that

A(\tau_{p})=\begin{cases}A(\tau_{1})^{p}&1\leq p\leq n-1\\
\frac{1}{2}A(\tau_{1})^{p}&n\leq p\leq 2n-2\\
\frac{1}{2}A(\tau_{1})^{2n-1}-A(\tau_{0})&p=2n-1.\end{cases}

Recall that
A(\tau_{0})
is the identity matrix.

3.
Spectral properties of
A(\tau_{1})

We will study the Spectral properties of
A(\tau_{1})
in this section.

Proposition 3.1
.

The characteristic polynomial for the linear operator
A(\tau_{1})
induced from the quantum multiplication in
\QH^{*}(\mathrm{OG})
is
\lambda^{2n}-4\lambda
.

Proof.

The basis of this proof is to swap the rows around, moving the top row to the bottom and shifting the rest up by one. This will allow us to get an “almost” diagonal matrix of the form:

\begin{array}[]{ccccccccc}r_{1}&1&-\lambda&&&&&&1\\
r_{2+i}&&\ddots&\ddots&&&&&\\
r_{n}&&&1&-\lambda&&&&\\
r_{n+1}&&&&2&-\lambda&&&\\
r_{n+2}&&&&&1&-\lambda&&\\
r_{n+2+j}&&&&&&\ddots&\ddots&\\
r_{2n-1}&&&&&&&1&-\lambda\\
r_{2n}&-\lambda&&&&&&1&0\\
\end{array}$
\end{center}
We can reduce this matrix to a diagonal matrix by subtracting a factor times each row from the last row. We will use the property that if two rows (or two columns) of a matrix are interchanged, the value of the determinant changes sign.
With the appropriate row operations we can get a matrix of the form:
\par
\begin{center}$\begin{array}[]{ccccccccc}r_{1}&1&-\lambda&&&&&&1\\
r_{2+i}&&\ddots&\ddots&&&&&\\
r_{n}&&&1&-\lambda&&&&\\
r_{n+1}&&&&2&-\lambda&&&\\
r_{n+2}&&&&&1&-\lambda&&\\
r_{n+2+j}&&&&&&\ddots&\ddots&\\
r_{2n-1}&&&&&&&1&-\lambda\\
r_{2n}&&&&&&&&\frac{4\lambda-\lambda^{2n}}{2}\\
\end{array}$
\end{center}
This is an upper triangular matrix so its determinant is the product of its diagonal given by $1\cdot 1\cdot\ldots\cdot 1\cdot 2\cdot 1\cdot\ldots\cdot 1\cdot\frac{4\lambda-\lambda^{2n}}{2}=4\lambda-\lambda^{2n}$. Finally since we swapped around an odd number of rows ($2n-1$ swaps were made) we need to multiply this determinant by a factor of $-1$ to get the determinant of $\tau_{1}-\lambda I$ to be $\lambda^{2n}-4\lambda$. Therefore the characteristic polynomial of $\tau_{1}$ is $\lambda^{2n}-4\lambda$.
\qed\end@proof
Recall that the eigenvalues of a matrix are the roots of its characteristic polynomial so we can factor the characteristic polynomial to get eigenvalues of the form $$\lambda\in\left\{\sqrt[2n-1]{4}e^{\frac{2\pi ji}{2n-1}}:0\leq j\leq 2n-1\right\}\cup\{0\}.$$

\begin{cor}If $\lambda$ is an eigenvalue of the linear operator $\tau_{1}$ induced from the quantum multiplication in $\QH^{*}(\mathrm{OG})$ then
$\frac{\lambda^{2n-1}-2}{2}=\begin{cases}-1&\lambda=0\\
1&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}$.
\end{cor}

\par Lastly we check that Galkin's Lower Bound Conjecture holds for $H^{\bullet}(\mathrm{OG})$.
\begin{thm}Galkin's Lower Bound Conjecture holds for $H^{\bullet}(\mathrm{OG})$. That is, $$\text{FPdim}((2n-1)\tau_{1})\geq\dim\mathrm{OG}+1.$$\end{thm}
\@proof The smooth real function $f(x):=4^{x}-x-1$ satisfies $f(0)=0$ and $f^{\prime}(x)=4^{x}\ln(4)-1>0$. Hence, $f\left(\frac{1}{2n-1}\right)>0$. That is, $4^{\frac{1}{2n-1}}>\frac{1}{2n-1}+1=\frac{2n}{2n-1}$. The result follows.
\qed\end@proof

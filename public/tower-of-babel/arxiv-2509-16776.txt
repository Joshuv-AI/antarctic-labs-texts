Data-Driven Two-Stage IRS-Aided Sumrate Maximization with Inexact Precoding Thanks: This work is supported by the US NSF under Grants 2242215 and 2431860
Hassaan Hashmi †, Spyridon Pougkakiotis ‡, and Dionysis Kalogerias †

Abstract
We propose iZoSGA , a data-driven learning algorithm for joint passive long-term intelligent reflective surface (IRS)-aided beamforming and active short-term precoding in wireless networks. iZoSGA is based on a zeroth-order stochastic quasigradient ascent methodology designed for tackling two-stage nonconvex stochastic programs with continuous uncertainty and objective functions with “black-box” terms, and where second-stage optimization is inexact. As such, iZoSGA utilizes inexact precoding oracles , enabling practical implementation when short-term (e.g., WMMSE-based) beamforming is solved approximately. The proposed method is agnostic to channel models or statistics, and applies to arbitrary IRS/network configurations. We prove non-asymptotic convergence of iZoSGA to a neighborhood of a stationary solution of the original exact problem under minimal assumptions. Our numerics confirm the efficacy iZoSGA in several “inexact regimes”, enabling passive yet fully effective IRS operation in diverse and realistic IRS-aided scenarios.

Introduction

The growing need for improving quality of service (QoS) for efficient and reliable wireless communication necessitates perpetual investigation into novel technologies
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
. One such technology is Intelligent Reflecting surfaces (IRSs, aka RISs), i.e., planar metasurfaces comprised of (a large number of) passive scattering elements with tunable phase shifts and amplitudes, capable of being dynamically adjusted to manipulate the propagation of incident waves. Even in absence of line-of-sight (LoS), IRSs function as
channel shapers
by creating alternative paths between access points (APs) and receivers; see Figure
1
.

The goal of deploying IRSs in a wireless network is to optimally tune their phase-shifting elements concurrently with other resources, such as AP precoders, in order to enhance QoS. In this work, we consider the MISO downlink scenario and optimize the
weighted sumrate utility
, but other metrics can also be considered.

Recently, the potentially far-reaching benefits of deploying
passive
IRSs to enhance standard beamforming schemes have led to the emergence of a rich literature on methods for IRS-aided optimal beamforming. These methods can be categorized in terms to two general system models. The
first system model
treats IRS elements as short-term (reactive) beamformers, tuned simultaneously with transmit precoding using instantaneous cascaded channel state information (CSI), thus relying on detailed channel/system models, resulting in specialized solvers; see, e.g.,
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
. This reliance on continuous, fine-grained instantaneous CSI renders such methods operationally unrealistic and computationally prohibitive in practical scenarios. While deep learning methods
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
ease some limitations, using deep neural networks can easily lead to increased problem complexity, opaqueness, poor generalization, and/or overfitting.

:
Depiction of a realistic IRS-aided Wireless Network.

The
second system model
, which is adopted here as well, optimizes IRS elements over the long-term timeframe while also applying standard short-term (downstream) transmit precoding. Although much more realistic from a practical perspective, this model results in challenging
nonconvex two-stage stochastic programming
problems. Up until recently,
Stochastic Successive Convex Approximation
(SSCA) techniques
[
16
,
17
]
were considered to be state-of-the-art (SotA) for optimally tuning IRSs for this system model. However, SSCA-based techniques are dependent on explicit channel and network topology models, accurate cascaded (statistical) CSI estimation, and require the formulation of convex surrogates of the original (highly nonconvex) problems
[
18
]
.

To successfully mitigate all aforementioned limitations, a novel, fully model-free and data-driven zeroth-order algorithm, termed
ZoSGA
, was proposed recently
[
19
]
. It was shown, both theoretically and empirically, that ZoSGA (ergodically) converges in the vicinity of some critical point of the given (nonconvex) two-stage stochastic program, substantially outperforming previous SotA SSCA-based techniques. However, in
[
19
]
it was critically assumed that ZoSGA has access to a
perfect oracle
for short-term precoding (while usually implemented via WMMSE
[
20
]
). While ZoSGA is vastly more versatile and effective than its rivals (i.e., the previous SotA), such an assumption creates a nontrivial gap between theory and practice, especially related to actual potential deployability of ZoSGA
[
19
]
.

This work bridges this gap by introducing and analyzing a generalized form of the algorithm in
[
19
]
, in the context of sumrate maximization. This new algorithm, termed iZoSGA, relies on
inexact precoding oracles
, which may provide
arbitrarily sub-optimal
short-term beamformers. Our contributions are summarized as follows:

•

We provide a non-asymptotic analysis of iZoSGA. We show that oracle inexactness contributes an additional
iterate-averaged
error term to the final convergence rate, plausibly establishing
stability of iZoSGA under precoding inexactness
.

•

We elucidate how oracle errors can be controlled with minimal assumptions on the IRS-aided two-stage beamforming problem.

•

We empirically demonstrate how our analysis can be used for the
development of practical algorithmic variants of iZoSGA
, for tackling realistic IRS-aided optimal beamforming problems.

Following the

\begin{split}\boxed{\max_{\bm{\theta}\in\Theta}f(\bm{\theta})\triangleq\mathbb{E}\left\{\max_{\bm{W}\in\mathcal{W}}{F}\left(\bm{W},\bm{H}(\bm{\theta},\omega)\right)\right\},}\end{split}

(2SP)

(and our numerical results later in Section

\max_{\bm{W}\in\mathcal{W}}\,\,\left\{G(\bm{W},\bm{\theta},\omega)\triangleq F\left(\bm{W},\bm{H}(\bm{\theta},\omega)\right)\right\}.

(SSP)

Hereafter, the
IRS-shaped
effective channel
\bm{H}(\bm{\theta},\omega)
is considered a

, but we assume that it is sufficiently regular (cf. Assumption
A
later in Section

). Under this model-free setting, we rely on two-point stochastic evaluation of

[
19
]
to estimate its gradients as

\begin{split}\hskip-2.0pt\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}\bm{H}_{\mu}(\bm{\theta},\omega)\hskip-2.0pt\triangleq\hskip-1.5pt&\ \mathbb{E}\hskip-2.0pt\left\{\frac{\bm{H}\left(\bm{\theta}+\mu\bm{U},\omega\right)-\bm{H}\left(\bm{\theta}-\mu\bm{U},\omega\right)}{2\mu}\bm{U}^{\top}\bigg|\ \omega\right\}^{\top},\end{split}

where
\bm{U}\in\mathcal{N}(\bm{0},\bm{1})
, and
\mu>0
is a given smoothing parameter. Then, the compound channel zeroth-order approximation of the gradient of
{F}(\bm{W},\bm{H}(\bm{\theta},\omega))
relative to
\bm{\theta}
is given by
[
19

\displaystyle\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}^{\mu}F\left(\bm{W},\bm{H}(\bm{\theta},\omega)\right)

(1)

\displaystyle\quad\triangleq\ 2\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}\bm{H}_{\mu}^{R}(\bm{\theta},\omega)\left(\Re\left(\frac{\partial^{\circ}}{\partial\bm{z}}F(\bm{W},\bm{z})\big|_{\bm{z}=\bm{H}(\bm{\theta},\omega)}\right)\right)^{\top}

\displaystyle\qquad+2\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}\bm{H}_{\mu}^{I}(\bm{\theta},\omega)\left(\Re\left(j\frac{\partial^{\circ}}{\partial\bm{z}}F(\bm{W},\bm{z})\big|_{\bm{z}=\bm{H}(\bm{\theta},\omega)}\right)\right)^{\top},

where
\frac{\partial^{\circ}}{\partial\bm{z}}
denotes the Wirtinger co-gradient, and superscript “R” (resp. “I”) denotes real (resp. imaginary) part. It should be noted here that we avoid explicit channel modeling assumptions that introduce and accumulate errors, particularly in large-scale systems.

3
Inexact Precoding Oracle

To expand on the role of
\bm{W}
in the gradient expression in (
1
), we formalize the notion of
inexactness
in the context of the inner problem. To this end, we define an
inexact oracle
as a black-box that returns points that are “close” to an arbitrary optimal solution of (
SSP
).

Definition 1
.

For any
\bm{\theta}\in\Theta
and a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
, an
inexact precoding oracle
returns an approximate precoder
\widetilde{\bm{W}}\in\mathcal{W}
to
(
SSP
)
, such that

\mathrm{dist}\Big(\widetilde{\bm{W}},\arg\max_{\bm{W}\in\mathcal{W}}F(\bm{W},\bm{H}(\bm{\theta},\omega))\Big)\leq\varepsilon(\bm{\theta},\omega),

(2)

with
\varepsilon(\cdot,\cdot)
some measurable random error function.

From Definition
1
, we have that the distance between inexact oracle output
\widetilde{\bm{W}}
and some
\bm{W}^{*}\in\arg\max_{\bm{W}\in\mathcal{W}}F(\bm{W},\bm{H}(\bm{\theta},\omega))
is such that
\|\widetilde{\bm{W}}-\bm{W}^{*}\|\leq\varepsilon(\bm{\theta},\omega)
. Here, we have only imposed measurability on the random error function
\varepsilon(\cdot,\cdot)
. This abstraction allows us to incorporate various inexact oracles in this generic framework. In this work, we will employ the well-documented WMMSE
[
20
]
as the inexact oracle for solving the weighted sumrate maximization problem. Then,
\varepsilon
can be completely characterized by the accuracy and convergence behavior of the sequence of iterates generated by WMMSE, as we shall see in Section
4.1
.

4
A New Method for The Outer Problem

We employ inexact precoding oracles, characterized by error
\varepsilon
(Definition
1
), to develop a model-free projected stochastic quasi-gradient method for the two-stage beamforming problem. Given i.i.d. samples of
\omega
, we evaluate (
1
) at each
(\bm{\theta},\bm{W})\in\Theta\times\mathcal{W}
(for a.e.
\omega
), producing the zeroth-order gradient approximation

\displaystyle\bm{D}_{\mu}(\bm{\theta},\omega,\bm{U};\bm{W})\triangleq\bm{\Delta}_{\mu}^{R}\left(\Re\left(\frac{\partial^{\circ}}{\partial\bm{z}}F(\bm{W},\bm{z})\bigg|_{\bm{z}=\bm{H}(\bm{\theta},\omega)}\right)\right)^{\top}

\displaystyle\qquad+\bm{\Delta}_{\mu}^{I}\left(\Re\left(j\frac{\partial^{\circ}}{\partial\bm{z}}F(\bm{W},\bm{z})\bigg|_{\bm{z}=\bm{H}(\bm{\theta},\omega)}\right)\right)^{\top},

(3)

where,
\bm{\Delta}_{\mu}^{R}
and
\bm{\Delta}_{\mu}^{I}
are respectively sample-based evaluations of the expected gradient approximations in (
1
). The induced overhead of two additional channel estimations is implicitly (and reasonably) assumed to be within the coherence time of
\bm{H}
.

Algorithm
1
computes a projected quasi-gradient by evaluating the zeroth-order gradient sample at
\bm{W}{=}\widetilde{\bm{W}}
given by the WMMSE algorithm, in line with Definition
1
. This constitutes a rough Gaussian smoothing–based approximation of the second-stage gradient, derived using an inexact oracle rather than a proper surrogate. In total, each iteration requires exactly three channel estimations, one for short-term precoding and two more for system probes required for gradient sampling. Further, any standard channel estimation method suffices, as it is independent of the presence of IRSs.

4.1
Controlling the Inexact Precoding Oracle Error

In light of nonconcavity of (
SSP
), let us explain under what conditions one can effectively control the magnitude of the oracle error function
\varepsilon(\bm{\theta},\omega)
(cf. Definition
1
). In our previous work
[
19
, Appendix B]
, it was shown that the function
\max_{\bm{W}\in\mathcal{W}}F(\bm{W},\cdot)-F(\cdot,\cdot)
is
sub-analytic
. It then follows that this function satisfies the
Lojasiewicz inequality
with uniform exponent
[
21
]
. Thus, there exists
\xi>0
and a positive sub-analytic function
C(\bm{H}(\cdot,\cdot))
, such that for all
\omega\in\Omega
,
\bm{\theta}\in\Theta
, and all
\bm{W}\in\mathcal{W}
,

\displaystyle\textnormal{dist}\Big(\bm{W},\arg\max_{\bm{W}\in\mathcal{W}}F\left(\bm{W},\bm{H}(\bm{\theta},\omega)\right)\hskip-2.0pt\Big)

(4)

\displaystyle\ \leq C(\bm{H})\Big(\max_{\bm{W}\in\mathcal{W}}F(\bm{W},\bm{H}(\bm{\theta},\omega))-F(\bm{W},\bm{H}(\bm{\theta},\omega))\Big)^{\xi}.

Under the minor assumption that there exists a uniform constant
C_{\bm{H}}\geq C(\bm{H}(\bm{\theta},\omega))
for any
\bm{\theta}\in\Theta
and any
\omega\in\Omega
, it then follows that the magnitude error in Definition
1
can be directly controlled.

Indeed, as (
SSP
) is nonconcave, we can only find an approximate solution
\widetilde{\bm{W}}
, such that

\max_{\bm{W}\in\mathcal{W}}F(\bm{W},\bm{H}(\bm{\theta},\omega))-F\big(\widetilde{\bm{W}},\bm{H}(\bm{\theta},\omega)\big)\leq\widetilde{\varepsilon}(\bm{\theta},\omega),

(5)

where the magnitude of
\widetilde{\varepsilon}(\bm{\theta},\omega)
can be reasonably controlled (in our case by adjusting the number of WMMSE iterations utilized to obtain
\widetilde{\bm{W}}
). From (
4
), it follows that, for all
\bm{\theta}\in\Theta
and a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
,

\big\|\widetilde{\bm{W}}-\bm{W}^{*}\big\|\leq C_{\bm{H}}\widetilde{\varepsilon}(\bm{\theta},\omega)^{\xi},

(6)

where
\bm{W}^{*}\in\arg\max_{\bm{W}\in\mathcal{W}}F(\bm{W},\bm{H}(\bm{\theta},\omega))
. Thus, the magnitude of the oracle error of Definition
1
can be controlled by enforcing (
5
). This supports the practical efficiency of Algorithm
1
, which relies on approximate WMMSE-based
[
20
]
solutions to (
SSP
).

4.2
Convergence Analysis

We briefly outline the convergence properties of Algorithm
1
. We write the objective of (
2SP
) as
\phi(\bm{\theta})\triangleq-f(\bm{\theta})+\delta_{\Theta}(\bm{\theta})
, where
\delta_{\Theta}(\bm{\theta})
is the indicator over the convex set
\Theta
, and introduce the

associated with
\phi

\lambda>0

\phi^{1/\lambda}(\bm{u})\triangleq\min_{\bm{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{S}}\left\{\phi(\bm{\theta})+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|\bm{u}-\bm{\theta}\|^{2}\right\}.

Since
\phi
is
\rho

[
19
]

\phi^{1/\lambda}
is smooth for
\lambda>\rho
and its gradient norm serves as a near-stationarity measure. In that sense, an
\epsilon
-stationary point of the Moreau envelope implies proximity to a near-stationary point (in the Clarke sense) of the original nonsmooth problem (e.g., see
[
22
, Section 2]
). Before establishing convergence, we state our basic assumptions below.

First, we compare WMMSE with unoptimized IRS parameters and fine-tuned IRS parameters using iZoSGA, for different number of WMMSE iterations, i.e., the precoders (oracle) have varying levels of

6
Conclusions

We introduced iZoSGA, a data-driven, model-free zeroth-order algorithm for two-stage stochastic IRS-assisted beamforming that combines inexact oracles for short-term precoding with zeroth-order sample gradients for IRS tuning. Our analysis, under general and realistic assumptions, establishes that iZoSGA stably converges near a stationary point of the underlying nonconvex problem, with deviation explicitly tied to the inexact oracle error. Experiments that leverage WMMSE as a surrogate oracle demonstrate that its error remains manageable, reinforcing theoretical findings and illustrating potential practical feasibility. The approach scales effectively to large multi-user networks, remains agnostic to channel, topology, or IRS structure, and is straightforward to implement; our simulations, spanning idealized to physically realistic IRS models, validate its robustness and applicability. Overall, this work provides theoretical convergence guarantees for a potentially deployable IRS tuning algorithm, offering a compelling resource allocation framework for two-stage (short/long-term) IRS-aided beamforming.

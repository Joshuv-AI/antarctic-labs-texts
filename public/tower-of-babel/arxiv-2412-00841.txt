Semi-derived Ringel-Hall bialgebras
Yiyu Li, Liangang Peng

Abstract
. Let 𝒜 \mathcal{A} be an arbitrary hereditary abelian category. Lu and Peng defined the semi-derived Ringel-Hall algebra 𝐒𝐃𝐇 ⁡ ( 𝒜 ) \mathbf{SDH}(\mathcal{A}) of 𝒜 \mathcal{A} and proved that 𝐒𝐃𝐇 ⁡ ( 𝒜 ) \mathbf{SDH}(\mathcal{A}) has a natural basis and is isomorphic to the Drinfeld double Ringel-Hall algebra of 𝒜 \mathcal{A} . In this paper, we introduce a coproduct formula on 𝐒𝐃𝐇 ⁡ ( 𝒜 ) \mathbf{SDH}(\mathcal{A}) with respect to the basis of 𝐒𝐃𝐇 ⁡ ( 𝒜 ) \mathbf{SDH}(\mathcal{A}) and prove that this coproduct is compatible with the product of 𝐒𝐃𝐇 ⁡ ( 𝒜 ) \mathbf{SDH}(\mathcal{A}) , thereby the semi-derived Ringel-Hall algebra of 𝒜 \mathcal{A} is endowed with a bialgebra structure which is identified with the bialgebra structure of the Drinfeld double Ringel-Hall algebra of 𝒜 \mathcal{A} .

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a field with
q
elements and
\mathcal{A}
be an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear exact category. The Hall algebra
\mathbf{H}(\mathcal{A})
of
\mathcal{A}
is an associative algebra whose underlying space is a
\mathbb{C}
-vector space with a basis parametrized by the isomorphic classes of objects in
\mathcal{A}
. The structure constants of the product are given by certain counts of extensions. To be more specific, for any
M,N\in\mathcal{A}
, the product
[M]\ast[N]
is given by a linear combination
\sum_{[L]\in\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{F}_{MN}^{L}[L]
, where
\mathcal{F}_{MN}^{L}
is the counts of the possible extensions of
M
by
N
in
\mathcal{A}
. The concept of Hall algebras first appeared in
[
Stein
]
and was considered as a tool in dealing with some questions about abelian
p
-groups for a prime number
p
.

In the 1990s, Ringel developed the theory of Hall algebras of finitary abelian categories. Since then, Hall algebras have obtained a lot of attention and have become a useful tool to establish connections between representation theory of finite dimensional algebras and other topics, such as Lie theory, combinatorics, and algebraic geometry, etc. For a complex simple Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
, Ringel
[
R90
]
proved that the positive part of the quantum group
\mathbf{U}_{v}({\mathfrak{g}})

[
Dr87
]
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
is isomorphic to the Hall algebra
\mathbf{H}(\mathcal{A})
of a hereditary category
\mathcal{A}
, where
\mathcal{A}
is the abelian category of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-representations of a quiver whose underlying graph is the same as the Dynkin diagram of
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Later Green
[
Gr95
]
extended this result to the Kac-Moody type. In particular, Green provided a coproduct for the Hall algebra, and then endows the Hall algebra with a bialgerba structure based on the famous Green’s formula. As a consequence, the positive part of a quantum group of Kac-Moody type is isomorphic to the composition subalgebra of the Hall algebra of the category of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-representations of a corresponding quiver.

Inspired by the work of Ringel and Green, several attempts have been made to develop Ringel-Hall algebras from appropriate categories in order to realize the entire quantum groups. In
[
X97
]
, Xiao considered the Drinfeld double of the extended Ringel-Hall algebra of an acyclic quiver
Q
and realized the whole quantum group associated to
Q
as the composition subalgebra of the Drinfeld double algebra.

For an abelian hereditary category
\mathcal{A}
with enough projective objects, Bridgeland
[
Br13
]
considered the Hall algebra of the exact category of
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded complexes with projective components over
\mathcal{A}
, and then localized it at the isomorphism classes of acyclic complexes. He proved that a subalgebra of the resulting Hall algebra (called Bridgeland’s Hall algebra) of an acyclic quiver is isomorphic to the corresponding quantum group. It is stated in
[
Br13
]
and proved in
[
Y16
]
that Bridgeland’s Hall algebra is isomorphic to the Drinfeld double Hall algebra of
\mathcal{A}
.

Recently, Lu and Peng
[
LuP21
]
defined the semi-derived Ringel-Hall algebra of an arbitrary hereditary category
\mathcal{A}
, denoted by
\mathbf{SDH}_{\mathbb{Z}/2}(\mathcal{A})
, to be the localization of a quotient algebra of the Ringel-Hall algebra of the category of
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded complexes. They proved that
\mathbf{SDH}_{\mathbb{Z}/2}(\mathcal{A})
is isomorphic to Drinfeld double Hall algebra of
\mathcal{A}
. The semi-derived Hall algebra also admits a basis, parameterized by the objects of
\mathcal{A}
and the elements of its Grothendieck group
K_{0}(\mathcal{A})
, which serves as the foundation for many computations throughout this paper. As a consequence, a quantum generalized Kac-Moody algebra
\mathbf{U}_{v}({\mathfrak{g}})

[
K95
,
Bor88
]
can be realized as the composition subalgebra of the semi-derived Ringel-Hall algebra of a corresponding quiver associated to
{\mathfrak{g}}

[
Lu21
]
.

The bialgebra structure of the extended Hall algebra induces a bialgebra structure for the Drinfeld double Hall algebra. According to the isomorphism between semi-derived Ringel-Hall algebra and Drinfeld double Hall algebra, it is natural to inquire whether an explicit formula exists for endowing
\mathbf{SDH}_{\mathbb{Z}/2}(\mathcal{A})
with a bialgebra structure. This paper is devoted to giving an affirmative answer to this question.

The paper is organized as follows. After recalling the basics of Ringel-Hall algebras, Drinfeld double Hall algebras, and semi-derived Ringel-Hall algebras in Section
2
, we introduce an explicit coproduct formula for the semi-derived Ringel-Hall algebra in the basis of
\mathbf{SDH}_{\mathbb{Z}/2}(\mathcal{A})
for an arbitrary hereditary category
\mathcal{A}
and state the main result in Section
3
.
As a consequence, the bialgebra structure of
\mathbf{SDH}_{\mathbb{Z}/2}(\mathcal{A})
is identified with the bialgebra structure of the Drinfeld double Hall algebra as well.
The proof will be presented in Section
4
.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a finite field with
q\in\mathbb{Z}
elements and
\upsilon=q^{1/2}
. An
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear abelian category
\mathcal{A}
is called finitary if
|\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(M,N)|<\infty
and
|\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{A}}(M,N)|<\infty
for all
\ M,N\in\mathcal{A}
. For an object
M\in\mathcal{A}
, its projective dimension (denote by
\operatorname{proj.dim}\nolimits M
) is defined to be the smallest number
i\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}}^{i+1}(M,-)=0
; dually, one can define its injective dimension (
\operatorname{inj.dim}\nolimits M
). We say that
\mathcal{A}
is of global dimension at most
n
if there exists a positive integer
n
such that
\operatorname{proj.dim}\nolimits M\leq n
and
\operatorname{inj.dim}\nolimits M\leq n
for all
M\in\mathcal{A}
. In this case, we denote it by
\operatorname{gl.dim}\nolimits\mathcal{A}\leq n
. In particular,
\mathcal{A}
is called a hereditary category if
\operatorname{gl.dim}\nolimits\mathcal{A}\leq 1
. Throughout this section, we always assume that
\mathcal{A}
is a small, finitary, abelian
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear category of finite global dimension without further specification.

Denote the Grothendieck group of
\mathcal{A}
by
K_{0}(\mathcal{A})
, for every object
M
in
\mathcal{A}
we denote the representative of
M
in
K_{0}(\mathcal{A})
by
\widehat{M}
. Since
\mathcal{A}
is a category of finite global dimension, a bilinear form
{\langle}-,-{\rangle}:K_{0}(\mathcal{A})\times K_{0}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
on
K_{0}(\mathcal{A})
is well-defined as follows:

{\langle}\widehat{M},\widehat{N}{\rangle}:=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{i}\dim\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}}^{i}(M,N),

where
\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}}^{0}(M,N)=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(M,N)
. We call
{\langle}-,-{\rangle}
the Euler form of
\mathcal{A}
. We also consider the symmetric Euler form:

(-,-):K_{0}(\mathcal{A})\times K_{0}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\mathbb{Z},

defined by
(\widehat{M},\widehat{N})={\langle}\widehat{M},\widehat{N}{\rangle}+{\langle}\widehat{N},\widehat{M}{\rangle}
.

2.1.
Ringel-Hall Algebras

Denote by
\mathrm{Iso}(\mathcal{A})
the set of all isomorphism classes
[M]
of objects
M
in
\mathcal{A}
. For three objects
M,N,R\in\mathcal{A}
,
\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}}^{1}(M,N)_{R}\subset\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathcal{A}}^{1}(M,N)
is the subset consisting of all extensions of M by N with the middle term isomorphic to
R
. We define the Hall algebra
\mathbf{H}(\mathcal{A})
of
\mathcal{A}
to be a
\mathbb{C}
-vector space:

\mathbf{H}(\mathcal{A}):=\bigoplus_{[M]\in\mathrm{Iso}(\mathcal{A})}\mathbb{C}[M],

with a product

[M]\ast[N]=\sum\limits_{R\in\mathrm{Iso}(\mathcal{A})}h_{MN}^{R}[R].

where
h_{MN}^{R}={|\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{A}}(M,N)_{R}|}/{|\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(M,N)|}
. In
[
R90
]
, it has been proved that
\mathbf{H}(\mathcal{A})
is an associative algebra with unit
[0]
. To be specific, for
M,N,L,K\in\mathcal{A}
, we have

(2.1)

\sum_{R}h_{MN}^{R}h_{RL}^{K}=\sum_{T}h_{MT}^{K}h_{NL}^{T},

which induces the associative law of
\mathbf{H}(\mathcal{A})
.

The twisted Hall algebra
\mathbf{H}_{\operatorname{tw}}(\mathcal{A})
is the
\upsilon
-deformation of the Hall algebra which has the same underlying space as
\mathbf{H}(\mathcal{A})
, and the product of
\mathbf{H}_{\operatorname{tw}}(\mathcal{A})
is given by

[M]\ast[N]=\sum\limits_{R\in\mathrm{Iso}(\mathcal{A})}\upsilon^{{\langle}\widehat{M},\widehat{N}{\rangle}}h_{MN}^{R}[R].

\mathbf{H}_{\operatorname{tw}}(\mathcal{A})
is clearly a unital associative algebra as well
[
R90
]
. We denote by
\mathbf{H}_{\operatorname{tw}}(\mathcal{A})\widehat{\otimes}\mathbf{H}_{\operatorname{tw}}(\mathcal{A})
the space of formal linear combinations

\sum_{[A],[B]\in\mathrm{Iso}(\mathcal{A})}c_{A,B}[A]\otimes[B].

We assume that
\mathcal{A}
is a small, finitary
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear hereditary abelian category. In
[
Gr95
]
, Green introduced a coproduct and counit on
\mathbf{H}_{\operatorname{tw}}(\mathcal{A})
such that
\mathbf{H}_{\operatorname{tw}}(\mathcal{A})
is equipped with a (topological) bialgebra structure.

Denote
a_{M}=|\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{A}}(M)|
for any object
M
. For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},M_{1},...,M_{n},K\in\mathcal{A}
, we recursively define
h_{M_{1}M_{2}\cdots M_{n}}^{K}
as follows: for
n=3
, we set
h_{M_{1}M_{2}M_{3}}^{K}:=\sum_{T}h_{M_{1}T}^{K}h_{M_{2}M_{3}}^{T}
; for
n\geq 4
, we define
h_{M_{1}M_{2}\cdots M_{n}}^{K}:=\sum_{T}h_{M_{1}T}^{K}h_{M_{2}\cdots M_{n}}^{T}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
Gr95
]

There exists a coproduct map
\Delta:\mathbf{H}_{\operatorname{tw}}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\mathbf{H}_{\operatorname{tw}}(\mathcal{A})\widehat{\otimes}\mathbf{H}_{\operatorname{tw}}(\mathcal{A})
of the form

\Delta([R])=\sum\limits_{M,N}\upsilon^{{\langle}\widehat{M},\widehat{N}{\rangle}}h_{MN}^{R}\frac{a_{R}}{a_{M}a_{N}}[M]\otimes[N],

and a counit map
\eta:\mathbf{H}_{\operatorname{tw}}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
that is given by

\epsilon([M])=\delta_{[M],[0]},

such that
(\mathbf{H}_{\operatorname{tw}}(\mathcal{A}),\Delta,\epsilon)
is a (topological) bialgebra.

The following lemma is crucial for the proof theorem
2.1
.

Lemma 2.2
.

[Green’s Formula]
Let
\mathcal{A}
be a hereditary category, for all
M,N,K_{1},K_{2}\in A

We have

\sum\limits_{K}a_{K}h^{K}_{MN}h^{K}_{K_{1}K_{2}}=\sum\limits_{M_{1},M_{2},N_{1},N_{2}}\upsilon^{-2{\langle}\widehat{M_{1}},\widehat{N_{2}}{\rangle}}\frac{a_{M}a_{N}a_{K_{1}}a_{K_{2}}}{a_{M_{1}}a_{M_{2}}a_{N_{1}}a_{N_{2}}}h^{M}_{M_{1}M_{2}}h^{N}_{N_{1}N_{2}}h^{K_{1}}_{M_{1}N_{1}}h^{K_{2}}_{M_{2}N_{2}}.

The following is a direct consequence of Lemma
2.2
and equation (
2.1
) which is useful in the proof of theorem
3.4
.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an hereditary category, for all
M,N,K_{1},K_{2},C\in A
, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{K}

\displaystyle a_{K}h^{K}_{MN}h^{K}_{K_{1}CK_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}M_{1},C_{1},M_{2}\\
N_{1},N_{2},C_{2}\end{subarray}}\upsilon^{-2{\langle}\widehat{M_{1}},\widehat{C_{2}}+\widehat{N_{2}}{\rangle}-2{\langle}\widehat{C_{1}},\widehat{N_{2}}{\rangle}}\frac{a_{M}a_{N}a_{K_{1}}a_{K_{2}}a_{C}}{a_{M_{1}}a_{N_{1}}a_{C_{1}}a_{M_{2}}a_{C_{2}}a_{N_{2}}}h^{M}_{M_{1}C_{1}M_{2}}h^{N}_{N_{1}C_{2}N_{2}}h^{K_{1}}_{M_{1}N_{1}}h^{C}_{C_{1}C_{2}}h^{K_{2}}_{M_{2}N_{2}}.

2.2.
Extended Hall algebras and Drinfeld Double

For a hereditary category
\mathcal{A}
, the extended Hall algebra
\mathbf{H}^{\operatorname{ex}}_{\operatorname{tw}}(\mathcal{A})^{+}
of
\mathcal{A}
is an associative algebra with the underlying vector space
\mathbf{H}_{\operatorname{tw}}(\mathcal{A})\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}[K_{0}(\mathcal{A})]
, where
\mathbb{C}[K_{0}(\mathcal{A})]
is the group algebra of the Grothendieck group
K_{0}(\mathcal{A})
. Its product is defined to be

[M]k_{\alpha}\ast[N]k_{\beta}=\sum\limits_{[R]\in\mathrm{Iso}(\mathcal{A})}\upsilon^{{\langle}\widehat{M},\widehat{N}{\rangle}+(\alpha,\widehat{N})}h_{MN}^{R}[R]k_{\alpha+\beta},

with the unit
[0]
. In
[
X97
]
, the extended Hall algebra
\mathbf{H}^{\operatorname{ex}}_{\operatorname{tw}}(\mathcal{A})^{+}
has a bialgebra structure whose coproduct is given by

\displaystyle\Delta^{+}([R])=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{M,N}\upsilon^{{\langle}\widehat{M},\widehat{N}{\rangle}}h_{MN}^{R}\frac{a_{R}}{a_{M}a_{N}}[M]k_{\widehat{N}}\otimes[N],

\displaystyle\Delta^{+}(k_{\alpha})=

\displaystyle k_{\alpha}\otimes k_{\alpha},

while the counit is defined as
\epsilon([R])=0
and
\epsilon(k_{\alpha})=1
for
R\neq 0\in\mathcal{A}
and
\alpha\in K_{0}(\mathcal{A})
.
Dually, let
\mathbf{H}^{\operatorname{ex}}_{\operatorname{tw}}(\mathcal{A})^{-}
be the same vector space as the underlying space of
\mathbf{H}^{\operatorname{ex}}_{\operatorname{tw}}(\mathcal{A})^{+}
. The bialgebra structure of
\mathbf{H}^{\operatorname{ex}}_{\operatorname{tw}}(\mathcal{A})^{-}
is given as follows

\displaystyle[M]k_{\alpha}\ast[N]k_{\beta}=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{[R]\in\mathrm{Iso}(\mathcal{A})}\upsilon^{{\langle}\widehat{M},\widehat{N}{\rangle}-(\alpha,\widehat{N})}h_{MN}^{R}[R]k_{\alpha+\beta},

\displaystyle\Delta^{-}([R]):=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{M,N}\upsilon^{{\langle}\widehat{M},\widehat{N}{\rangle}}h_{MN}^{R}\frac{a_{R}}{a_{M}a_{N}}[N]\otimes[M]k_{\widehat{N}},

\displaystyle\Delta^{-}(k_{\alpha})=

\displaystyle k_{\alpha}\otimes k_{\alpha},

with unit
[0]
and counit
\epsilon([R])=0
and
\epsilon(k_{\alpha})=1
for
R\neq 0\in\mathcal{A}
and
\alpha\in K_{0}(\mathcal{A})
. We denote by
\mathbf{H}^{\operatorname{ex}}_{\operatorname{tw}}(\mathcal{A})^{+}\widehat{\otimes}\mathbf{H}^{\operatorname{ex}}_{\operatorname{tw}}(\mathcal{A})^{-}
the space of formal linear combinations

\sum_{[A],[B]\in\mathrm{Iso}(\mathcal{A}),\alpha,\beta\in K_{0}{(\mathcal{A})}}c_{A,B}[A]k_{\alpha}\otimes[B]k_{\beta}.

There is a bilinear pairing
\varphi:\mathbf{H}^{\operatorname{ex}}_{\operatorname{tw}}(\mathcal{A})^{+}\times\mathbf{H}^{\operatorname{ex}}_{\operatorname{tw}}(\mathcal{A})^{-}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
given by

\varphi([M]k_{\alpha},[N]k_{\beta})=\upsilon^{(\alpha,\beta)}\delta_{[M],[N]}a_{M},

which is a Hopf pairing
[
Jo95
]
,i.e., it satisfies

\varphi([M]k_{\alpha}\ast[N]k_{\beta},[R]k_{\delta})=\varphi([M]k_{\alpha}\otimes[N]k_{\beta},\Delta([R]k_{\delta})),

where
\varphi(x\otimes x^{\prime},y\otimes y^{\prime})=\varphi(x,y)\varphi(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
for
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbf{H}^{\operatorname{ex}}_{\operatorname{tw}}(\mathcal{A})^{+},y,y^{\prime}\in\mathbf{H}^{\operatorname{ex}}_{\operatorname{tw}}(\mathcal{A})^{-}
. As a consequence, we have the following result.

Theorem 2.4
.

[
X97
,
Jo95
]

There is an algebra structure on
\mathbf{H}^{\operatorname{ex}}_{\operatorname{tw}}(\mathcal{A})\widehat{\otimes}\mathbf{H}^{\operatorname{ex}}_{\operatorname{tw}}(\mathcal{A})
given by

(D1)

(x\otimes 1)(x^{\prime}\otimes 1)=xx^{\prime}\otimes 1,

(D2)

(1\otimes y)(1\otimes y^{\prime})=1\otimes yy^{\prime},

(D3)

(x\otimes 1)(1\otimes y)=x\otimes y,

(D4)

\sum\varphi(x_{(2)},y_{(1)})x_{(1)}\otimes y_{(2)}=\sum\varphi(x_{(1)},y_{(2)})(1\otimes y_{(1)})(x_{(2)}\otimes 1),

where we use Sweedler’s notation
\Delta^{+}(x)=\sum x_{(1)}\otimes x_{(2)}
and
\Delta^{-}(y)=\sum y_{(1)}\otimes y_{(2)}
. The algebra
\mathbf{H}^{\operatorname{ex}}_{\operatorname{tw}}(\mathcal{A})^{+}\widehat{\otimes}\mathbf{H}^{\operatorname{ex}}_{\operatorname{tw}}(\mathcal{A})^{-}
is called the Drinfeld double Hall algebra of
\mathcal{A}
, denoted by
\mathbf{DDH}(\mathcal{A})
. Furthermore,
\mathbf{DDH}(\mathcal{A})
is a bialgebra with counit
\epsilon\otimes\epsilon
and coproduct
\Delta(a\otimes b)=\Delta^{+}(a)\otimes\Delta^{-}(b)
.

2.4.
semi-derived Ringel-Hall algebras

Let
\mathbf{H}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}/2}(\mathcal{A}))
be the Hall algebra of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}/2}(\mathcal{A})
with product given by

[M^{\bullet}]\ast[N^{\bullet}]=\sum\limits_{[R^{\bullet}]\in\mathrm{Iso}({\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}/2}(\mathcal{A})})}\upsilon^{{\langle}\widehat{M^{\bullet}},\widehat{N^{\bullet}}{\rangle}_{\operatorname{cw}}}h^{R^{\bullet}}_{M^{\bullet}N^{\bullet}}[R^{\bullet}].

The algebra
\mathbf{H}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}/2}(\mathcal{A}))
is well-known to be a
K_{0}(\mathcal{C}_{2}(\mathcal{A}))
-graded algebra. Let
I_{\mathbb{Z}/2}
be the two-sided ideal generated by the set

\{[L]-[K\oplus M]|0\rightarrow K\rightarrow L\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0\text{,\penalty\ with K acyclic\penalty\ }\}.

The quotient algebra
\mathbf{H}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}/2}(\mathcal{A}))/I_{\mathbb{Z}/2}
is also a
K_{0}(\mathcal{C}_{2}(\mathcal{A}))
-graded algebra. Let
S_{\mathbb{Z}/2}
be the subset of
\mathbf{H}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Z}/2}(\mathcal{A}))/I_{\mathbb{Z}/2}
consisting of
t[K]
, where
t\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and
[K]
is an acyclic
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded complex. In
[
LuP21
]
, it has been proven that
S_{\mathbb{Z}/2}
is a right Ore set. As a result, we can form the right localization of

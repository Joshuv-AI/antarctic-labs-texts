Bichromatic Tweezers for Qudit Quantum Computing in 87 Sr
Enrique A. Segura Carrillo ID \hrefhttps://orcid.org/0000-0003-4522-2080{}, Eric J. Meier ID \hrefhttps://orcid.org/0000-0003-1545-9829{}, Michael J. Martin ID \hrefhttps://orcid.org/0000-0002-6022-217X{}

Abstract
Neutral atoms have become a competitive platform for quantum metrology, simulation, sensing, and computing. Current magic trapping techniques are insufficient to engineer magic trapping conditions for qudits encoded in hyperfine states with J ≠ 0 J\neq 0 , compromising qudit coherence. In this paper we propose a scheme to engineer magic trapping conditions for qudits via bichromatic tweezers. We show it is possible to suppress differential light shifts across all magnetic sublevels of the 5 ​ s ​ 5 ​ p ​ P 2 3 5s5p\ \mathrm{{}^{3}P_{2}} state by using two carefully chosen wavelengths (with comparable tensor light shift magnitude and opposite sign) at an appropriate intensity ratio, thus suppressing light-shift induced dephasing, enabling scalar magic conditions between the ground state and 5 ​ s ​ 5 ​ p ​ P 2 3 5s5p\ \mathrm{{}^{3}P_{2}} , and tensor magic conditions for qudits encoded within it. Furthermore, this technique enables robust operation at the tensor magic angle 54.7 ∘ with linear trap polarization via reduced sensitivity to uncertainty in experimental parameters. We expect this technique to enable new loading protocols, enhance cooling efficiency, and enhance nuclear spins’ coherence times, thus facilitating qudit-based quantum computing in 87 Sr in the 5 ​ s ​ 5 ​ p ​ P 2 3 5s5p\ \mathrm{{}^{3}P_{2}} manifold.

Engineering Bichromatic Optical Tweezer

Our scheme tackles two challenges: (1) coherent transfer for qudits from
g
to
e
(see Fig.
1

e

e
while also suppressing tensor-induced dephasing at a level of 99.9% state fidelity.

In Fig.

we present the incompatibility between light shifts in

Our analysis highlights the light shift engineering realized by bichromatic tweezers: it realizes a light shift regime in which the off-diagonal elements in Eq. (

can be engineered
, enabling the preservation of qudit coherence by suppressing dephasing induced by the trapping potential while operating at achievable angular precision and magnetic fields.

\mathrm{{}^{3}P_{1}}

. There is a second photoionization process combining a 813.5 nm photon and a 521.3 nm photon that will increase the photoionization rate, which we have not carefully considered. See Table.
3

IV
Conclusion

We have shown the viability of engineering magic trapping conditions for all
m_{F}
in
5s5p\ \mathrm{{}^{3}P_{2}}

F=9/2
of
87
Sr via bichromatic tweezers by carefully choosing a power ratio between two wavelengths and magic-angle tuning to the tensor magic angle. We apply this technique to engineer magic trapping conditions in
{}^{1}\mathrm{S}_{0}
–
\mathrm{{}^{3}P_{2}}
with two configurations: (1) employing two scalar magic wavelengths at 891.5 nm and 518.0 nm, and (2) using the
{}^{1}\mathrm{S}_{0}
–
\mathrm{{}^{3}P_{0}}
magic wavelength at 813.5
\mathrm{nm}
and an additional wavelength at 521.3
\mathrm{nm}
.

We determine that for a high-spin hyperfine manifold a monochromatic tweezer is incompatible with magic-angle tuning: this technique requires magnetic fields well within the Paschen-Back regime (where
\ket{F,m_{F}}
is no longer a good quantum basis) to define the quantization axis. Our work suggests that operating at a magnetic field of
\lesssim 5
G would enable suppression of tweezer-induced dephasing with our scheme while also mitigating the impact of the quadratic Zeeman shift
7
on qudit fidelity. Furthermore, since our light shift cancellation relies on scaling
down
the light shift Hamiltonian rather than scaling
up
the bias magnetic field, the perturbative regime needed for magic-angle tuning can be enforced at low magnetic fields. These conditions enable operating at the magic angle at practical angular precision, leading to the suppression of light shift induced-dephasing and Rayleigh decoherence — key ingredients in preserving qudit coherence. We expect the bichromatic tweezer to enhance state fidelity, and enable the assembly of atomic arrays ideal for qudit-based quantum sensing, simulation, and computing applications in
87
Sr.

E.2
Rayleigh Decoherence via Differential Scattering Amplitudes between
m_{F}

In addition to Raman scattering, far-detuned light fields can induce Rayleigh scattering. The literature defines elastic Rayleigh scattering as
84
,

\Gamma_{el}^{i,j}(\omega)=\frac{I{\omega}^{3}}{\left(4\pi\mathbf{\epsilon}_{0}\right)^{2}c^{4}\hbar^{3}}\frac{8\pi}{3}\sum_{q}\left(\sum_{k}A^{i\rightarrow i}_{k,q}(\omega)-A^{j\rightarrow j}_{k,q}(\omega)\right)^{2}

(52)

Furthermore, let’s define effective differential scattering amplitude between nuclear spins,

\mathbf{\epsilon}(\omega)=\sum_{q}\Bigg(\sum_{k}A_{k,q}^{i,i}(\omega)-\sum_{k}A_{k,q}^{j,j}(\omega)\Bigg)

(53)

Thus we can see that dephasing errors scale quadratically
84
,

\Gamma_{el}^{i,j}(\omega)=\frac{I{\omega}^{3}}{\left(4\pi\mathbf{\epsilon}_{0}\right)^{2}c^{4}\hbar^{3}}\frac{8\pi}{3}\Bigg[\mathbf{\epsilon}(\omega)\Bigg]^{2}

(54)

For this form of error the difference in phase between the scattering amplitudes of
i,j
nuclear spins lead to constructive interference driving dephasing. For clarity, we will expand this expression (and adding
\beta
as a parameter) to,

\Gamma_{el}^{\,i,j}(\beta,\omega)=\frac{I{\omega}^{3}}{\left(4\pi\mathbf{\epsilon}_{0}\right)^{2}c^{4}\hbar^{3}}\frac{8\pi}{3}\sum_{q}\Bigg[{\left(\sum_{k}A_{k,q}^{i\rightarrow i}(\beta,\omega)\right)^{2}}+\\
{\left(\sum_{k}A_{k,q}^{j\rightarrow j}(\beta,\omega)\right)^{2}}-{2\sum_{k}A_{k,q}^{i\rightarrow i}(\beta,\omega)\ A_{k,q}^{j\rightarrow j}(\beta,\omega)}\Bigg]

(55)

The first two terms represent standard Rayleigh scattering for
i,j
nuclear spins in the qudit. However, the last term represents the overlap between the scattering amplitudes of the different nuclear spins, introducing a decoherence mechanism driving depolarization errors.

For qubit-systems, characterizing dephasing via this formalism is straight-forward. For qudits we need to expand this formalism by introducing
\mathcal{E}
, a matrix representing the elastic Rayleigh scattering in the SU(10) manifold.

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\Gamma_{el}^{-9/2,-7/2}&\Gamma_{el}^{-9/2,-5/2}&\Gamma_{el}^{-9/2,-3/2}&\cdots&\Gamma_{el}^{-9/2,7/2}&\Gamma_{el}^{-9/2,9/2}\\
\Gamma_{el}^{-7/2,-9/2}&0&\Gamma_{el}^{-7/2,-5/2}&\Gamma_{el}^{-7/2,-3/2}&\cdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\Gamma_{el}^{-5/2,-9/2}&\Gamma_{el}^{-5/2,-7/2}&0&\ddots&\cdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\Gamma_{el}^{-3/2,-9/2}&\Gamma_{el}^{-3/2,-7/2}&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\Gamma_{el}^{i,j}&\ddots&0&\vdots&\vdots\\
\Gamma_{el}^{7/2,-9/2}&\Gamma_{el}^{7/2,-7/2}&\vdots&\cdots&\cdots&0&\Gamma_{el}^{7/2,9/2}\\
\Gamma_{el}^{9/2,-9/2}&\Gamma_{el}^{9/2,-7/2}&\Gamma_{el}^{9/2,-5/2}&\cdots&\Gamma_{el}^{9/2,5/2}&\Gamma_{el}^{9/2,7/2}&0\end{pmatrix}

(56)

From
\mathcal{E}
we define the effective Rayleigh scattering rate for a nuclear spin as,

\Gamma_{\text{eff}}(j)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{E}_{ij}

(57)

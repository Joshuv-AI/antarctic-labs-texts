Abstract
. The main aim of this article is to establish a sharp improvement of the classical Bohr inequality for bounded holomorphic mappings in the polydisk 𝔻 n \mathbb{D}^{n} .We also prove two other sharp versions of the Bohr inequality in the setting of several complex variables: one by replacing the constant term with the absolute value of the function, and another by replacing it with the square of the absolute value of the function.Furthermore, we establish multidimensional analogues of known results concerning the modulus of the derivative of analytic functions in the unit disk 𝔻 \mathbb{D} , replacing the derivative with the radial derivative of holomorphic functions in 𝔻 n \mathbb{D}^{n} .All of the established results are shown to be sharp.

1.1.
Classical Bohr inequality and its recent implications

Let
\mathcal{B}
denote the class of analytic functions in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}:=\{{\zeta}\in\mathbb{C}:|{\zeta}|<1\}
of the form
f({\zeta})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}{\zeta}^{k}
such that
|f({\zeta})|<1
in
\mathbb{D}
. In the study of Dirichlet series, in
1914
, Harald Bohr
[
19
]
discovered the following interesting phenomenon

Theorem A
.

If
f\in\mathcal{B}
, then the following sharp inequality holds:

\displaystyle M_{f}(r):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|a_{k}|r^{k}\leq 1\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;|{\zeta}|=r\leq\frac{1}{3}.

(1.1)

We remark that if
|f({\zeta})|\leq 1
in
\mathbb{D}
and
|f({\zeta}_{0})|=1
for some point
{\zeta}_{0}
in
\mathbb{D}
, then
f
must be a unimodular constant; therefore, we restrict our attention to non-constant functions
f\in\mathcal{B}
. The inequality (
1.1
), together with the sharp constant
1/3
, is known as the
classical Bohr inequality
, and
1/3
is called the
Bohr radius
for the family
\mathcal{B}
. Bohr
[
19
]
originally established the inequality only for
r\leq 1/6
, and Wiener later proved that the constant
1/3
is sharp. Several alternative proofs have since appeared (see
[
47
]
-
[
49
]
,
[
53
]
,
[
54
]
, and the survey chapters
[
6
]
and
[
30
, Chapter 8]
). Several of these works employ methods from complex analysis, functional analysis, number theory, and probability, and they further develop new theory and applications of Bohr’s ideas on Dirichlet series. For example, various multidimensional generalizations of this result have been obtained in
[
7
,
9
,
10
,
18
,
28
,
35
]
.

It is worth noting that if
|a_{0}|
in Bohr’s inequality is replaced by
|a_{0}|^{2}
, then the Bohr radius improves from

1/3
to
1/2
. Moreover, if
a_{0}=0
in Theorem A, the sharp Bohr radius can be further improved to
1/\sqrt{2}
(see, for example,
[
36
]
,
[
47
, Corollary 2.9]
, and the recent work
[
50
]
for a general result). These improvements rely on sharp coefficient estimates such as

\displaystyle|a_{n}|\leq 1-|a_{0}|^{2},\quad n\geq 1,\;f\in\mathcal{B}.

Using these inequalities, in
[
36
]
it is observed that the sharp form of Theorem A cannot be obtained in the extremal case
|a_{0}|<1
. Nevertheless, a sharp version of Theorem A has been achieved for each individual function in
\mathcal{B}
(see
[
14
]
), and for several subclasses of univalent functions (see
[
10
,
45
]
).

Let
\mathbb{N}
denote the set of positive integers. There is also notion of Rogosinski radius
[
52
]
which is as follows: If
|f({\zeta})|<1
on
\mathbb{D}
, then we have
|S_{N}(\zeta)|\leq 1
for
|{\zeta}|\leq 1/2
for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
, where
S_{N}(\zeta)=\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}a_{n}{\zeta}^{n}
. The constant
1/2
is sharp and called the
Rogosinski radius
.

In
[
43
]
, Liu
et al.
established a Refined Bohr-Rogosinski inequalities by applying a strengthened system of coefficient inequalities (see
[
43
, Lemma 4]
).

Theorem B
.

[
43
, Theorem 1]

Suppose that
f\in\mathcal{B}
and
f({{\zeta}})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}{\zeta}^{n}.
For
n\geq N
, let
t=\left[\frac{N-1}{2}\right]
. Then

\displaystyle|f({\zeta})|+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\operatorname{sgn}(t)\sum_{n=1}^{t}|a_{n}|^{2}\frac{r^{N}}{1-r}+\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{0}|}+\frac{r}{1-r}\right)\sum_{n=t+1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}\leq 1

for
|{\zeta}|=r\leq R_{N}
, where
R_{N}
is the positive root of the equation
2(1+r)r^{N}-(1-r)^{2}=0
.
The radius
R_{N}
is best possible.
Moreover,

\displaystyle|f({\zeta})|^{2}+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\operatorname{sgn}(t)\sum_{n=1}^{t}|a_{n}|^{2}\frac{r^{N}}{1-r}+\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{0}|}+\frac{r}{1-r}\right)\sum_{n=t+1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}\leq 1

for
|{\zeta}|=r\leq R_{N}^{\prime}
, where
R_{N}^{\prime}
is is the positive root of the equation
2(1+r)r^{N}-(1-r)^{2}=0
.
The radius
R_{N}^{\prime}
is best possible.

For the case
N=1
, it is straightforward to verify that
R_{1}=\sqrt{5}-2
and
R_{1}^{\prime}=1/3
. However, both constants can be improved when one considers any individual function in
\mathcal{B}
(in the setting of Theorem B with
N=1
). In this regard, Liu
et al.

[
43
]
obtained the following result.

Theorem C
.

[
43
, Theorem 2]

Suppose that
f\in\mathcal{B}
and
f({\zeta})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}{\zeta}^{n}.
Then

\displaystyle A(\zeta,r):=|f({\zeta})|+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{0}|}+\frac{r}{1-r}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}\leq 1

for
|{\zeta}|=r\leq r_{a_{0}}=\dfrac{2}{\,3+|a_{0}|+\sqrt{5}(1+|a_{0}|)}
.
The radius
r_{a_{0}}
is best possible and satisfies
r_{a_{0}}>\sqrt{5}-2
.
Moreover,

\displaystyle B({\zeta},r):=|f(\zeta)|^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{0}|}+\frac{r}{1-r}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}\leq 1

for
|{\zeta}|=r\leq r_{a_{0}}^{\prime}
, where
r_{a_{0}}^{\prime}
is the unique positive root of

\displaystyle(1-|a_{0}|^{3})r^{3}-(1+2|a_{0}|)r^{2}-2r+1=0.

The radius
r_{a_{0}}^{\prime}
is best possible. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}<r_{a_{0}}^{\prime}<\frac{1}{2+|a_{0}|}.

Motivated by the work of Kayumov and Ponnusamy
[
37
]
, Bohr-type inequalities for the family
\mathcal{B}
were investigated in
[
44
]
. This investigation considered several forms where the Taylor coefficients in the classical Bohr inequality are partially or entirely replaced by higher-order derivatives of
f
. We recall only two of these results here.

Theorem D
.

[
44
, Theorem 2.1]
Suppose that
f\in\mathcal{B}
and
f({\zeta})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\zeta^{n}.
Then the following sharp inequality holds:

\displaystyle|f({\zeta})|+|f^{\prime}({\zeta})|r+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq 1\quad\text{for}\;|{\zeta}|=r\leq\frac{\sqrt{17}-3}{4}.

Theorem E
.

[
44
, Theorem 2.2, Corollary 2.3]
Suppose that
N(\geq 2)
is an integer,

f({\zeta})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}{\zeta}^{k}
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
and
|f({\zeta})|<1
in
\mathbb{D}
. Then

\displaystyle|f({\zeta})|+\sum_{k=N}^{\infty}\left|\frac{f^{(k)}({\zeta})}{k!}\right||{\zeta}|^{k}\leq 1\quad\text{for }|{\zeta}|=r\leq R_{N},

where
R_{N}
is the minimum positive root of the equation

\displaystyle\psi_{N}(r)=(1+r)(1-2r)(1-r)^{\,N-1}-2r^{N}=0.

The radius
R_{N}
is the best possible. Moreover,

\displaystyle|f({\zeta})|^{2}+\sum_{k=N}^{\infty}\left|\frac{f^{(k)}({\zeta})}{k!}\right||z|^{k}\leq 1\quad\text{for }|{\zeta}|=r\leq R_{N}^{*},

where
R_{N}^{*}
is the positive root of the equation

\displaystyle(1+r)(1-2r)(1-r)^{\,N-1}-r^{N}=0.

The radius
R_{N}^{*}
is the best possible.

Liu
et al.

[
43
]
presented an improved version of Theorem D as follows.

Theorem F
.

[
43
, Theorem 7]
Suppose that
f\in\mathcal{B}
and
f({\zeta})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\zeta^{n}.

Then

\displaystyle I(\zeta,r):=|f({\zeta})|+|f^{\prime}({\zeta})|\,r+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{0}|}+\frac{r}{1-r}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}\leq 1

for
|{\zeta}|=r\leq\frac{\sqrt{17}-3}{4}
, and the constant
\frac{\sqrt{17}-3}{4}
is best possible.
Moreover,

\displaystyle J({\zeta},r):=|f({\zeta})|^{2}+|f^{\prime}({\zeta})|\,r+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}+\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{0}|}+\frac{r}{1-r}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}\leq 1

for
|{\zeta}|=r\leq r_{0}
, where
r_{0}\approx 0.385795
is the unique positive root of the equation

\displaystyle 1-2r-r^{2}-r^{3}-r^{4}=0,

and
r_{0}
is best possible.

e.g.

.

It would be interesting to study the Bohr-Rogosinski radius for functions with values in the polydisk in several complex variables. It is natural to raise the following question:

Problem 1.1
.

Can we establish the multidimensional versions of Theorems B, C, E, and F?

In this paper, our aim is to present an affirmative answer to this problem. The organization of the paper is as follows: First, we discuss about some basic Notations and terminology in several complex variables. In Section
2
, we present our main results. In Section
3
, we present some key lemmas which will play a key role in proving the results of this paper. Finally, in Section
4
, we present the proofs of our main results.

1.2.
Basic Notations in several complex variables

For
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
and
w=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{n})
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we denote
\langle z,w\rangle=z_{1}\overline{w}_{1}+\ldots+z_{n}\overline{w}_{n}
and
||z||=\sqrt{\langle z,z\rangle}
. The absolute value of a complex number
z_{1}
is denoted by
|z_{1}|
and for
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we define

\displaystyle||z||_{\infty}=\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq n}|z_{i}|.

An open polydisk (or open polycylinder) in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a subset
\mathbb{P}\Delta(a;r)\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
of the form

\mathbb{P}\Delta(a;r)=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\Delta(a_{j};r_{j})=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:|z_{i}-a_{i}|<r_{i},\;i=1,2,\ldots,n\},

the point
a=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
is called the centre of the polydisk and
r=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;(r_{i}>0)
is called the polyradius. It is easy to see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Delta(0;1)=\mathbb{P}\Delta(0;1_{n})=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\Delta(0_{n};1_{n}).

The closure of
\mathbb{P}\Delta(a;r)
will be called the closed polydisk with centre
a
and polyradius
r
and will be denoted by
\overline{\mathbb{P}\Delta}(a;r)
. We denote by
C_{k}(a_{k};r_{k})
the boundary of
\Delta(a_{k};r_{k})
,
i.e.,
the circle of radius
r_{k}
with centre
a_{k}
on the
z_{k}
-plane. Of course
C_{k}(a_{k},r_{k})
is represented by the usual parametrization

\displaystyle\theta_{k}\to\gamma(\theta_{k})=a_{k}+r_{k}e^{i\theta_{k}},\;\mbox{where},0\leq\theta_{k}\leq 2\pi.

The product
C^{n}(a;r):=C_{1}(a_{1};r_{1})\times\ldots\times C_{n}(a_{n};r_{n})
is called the determining set of the polydisk
\mathbb{P}\Delta(a;r)
.

A multi-index
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})
of dimension
n
consists of n non-negative integers
\alpha_{j},\;1\leq j\leq n
; the degree of a multi-index
\alpha
is the sum
|\alpha|=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j}
and we denote
\alpha!=\alpha_{1}!\ldots\alpha_{n}!
. For
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and a multi-index
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})
, we define

z^{\alpha}=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}^{\alpha_{j}}\;\;\text{and}\;\;|z|^{\alpha}=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}|z_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}.

For two multi-indexes
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})
and
\nu=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{n})
, we define
\nu^{\alpha}=\nu_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\ldots\nu_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}.

Let
f(z)
be a holomorphic function in a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and
c=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\in\Omega
. Then in a polydisk
\mathbb{P}\Delta(c;r)\subset\Omega
with centre
c
,
f(z)
has a power series expansion in
z_{1}-c_{1},\ldots,z_{n}-c_{n}
,

\displaystyle f(z)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{n}=0}^{\infty}a_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{n}}(z_{1}-c_{1})^{\alpha_{1}}(z_{2}-c_{2})^{\alpha_{2}}\ldots(z_{n}-c_{n})^{\alpha_{n}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{|\alpha|=0}a_{\alpha}(z-c)^{\alpha}=\sum\limits_{|\alpha|=0}^{\infty}P_{|\alpha|}(z-c),

which is absolutely convergent in
\mathbb{P}\Delta(c;r)
, where the term
P_{k}(z-c)
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
k
.

One of our aims is to establish multidimensional analogues of Theorems D, E, and F. For this purpose, we consider
f
to be a holomorphic function in
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
and define the Euler operator
D
as:

Df(z):=\sum_{k=1}^{n}z_{k}\frac{\partial f(z)}{\partial z_{k}}.

This operator is also known as the Euler derivative, the total derivative, or the radial derivative.

2.
Main Results

We first state the multidimensional version of Theorem B.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
f(z)=\sum_{|\alpha|=0}a_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}
be a holomorphic function in the polydisk
\mathbb{P}\Delta(0;1_{n})
such that
|f(z)|\leq 1
for all
z\in\mathbb{P}\Delta(0;1/n)
. Suppose
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{P}\Delta(0;1/n)
and
r=(r_{1},r_{2},\ldots,r_{n})
such that
||z||_{\infty}={\bf r}
. For
n\geq N
, let
t=\left[\frac{N-1}{2}\right]
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}(z,\bf r):

\displaystyle=|f(z)|+\sum_{k=N}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{|\alpha|=k}|a_{\alpha}|\,r^{\alpha}+\operatorname{sgn}(t)\sum\limits_{k=1}^{t}\sum\limits_{|\alpha|=k}|a_{\alpha}|^{2}\frac{{\bf r}^{N}}{1-{\bf r}}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{0}|}+\frac{{\bf r}}{1-{\bf r}}\right)\sum_{k=t+1}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{|\alpha|=k}|a_{\alpha}|^{2}r^{2\alpha}\leq 1

for
n{\bf r}\leq R_{n,N}
, where
R_{n,N}
is the positive root of the equation
\psi_{N}(n{\bf r})=0
,

\psi_{N}(n{\bf r})=2(1+n{\bf r})(n{\bf r})^{N}-(1-n{\bf r})^{2}
. The radius
R_{n,N}
is best possible. Moreover,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{2}(z,\bf r):

\displaystyle=|f(z)|^{2}+\sum_{k=N}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{|\alpha|=k}|a_{\alpha}|\,r^{\alpha}+\operatorname{sgn}(t)\sum\limits_{k=1}^{t}\sum\limits_{|\alpha|=k}|a_{\alpha}|^{2}\frac{{\bf r}^{N}}{1-{\bf r}}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{0}|}+\frac{{\bf r}}{1-{\bf r}}\right)\sum_{k=t+1}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{|\alpha|=k}|a_{\alpha}|^{2}r^{2\alpha}\leq 1

for
n{\bf r}\leq R^{\prime}_{n,N}
, where
R^{\prime}_{n,N}
is the positive root of the equation
(1+n{\bf r})(n{\bf r})^{N}-(1-n{\bf r})^{2}=0
. The radius
R^{\prime}_{N}
is best possible.

Next we state the multidimensional version of Theorem C.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
f(z)=\sum_{|\alpha|=0}a_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}
be a holomorphic function in the polydisk
\mathbb{P}\Delta(0;1_{n})
such that
|f(z)|\leq 1
for all
z\in\mathbb{P}\Delta(0;1/n)
. Suppose
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{P}\Delta(0;1/n)
and
r=(r_{1},r_{2},\ldots,r_{n})
such that
||z||_{\infty}={\bf r}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{3}(z,{\bf r}):=|f(z)|+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{|\alpha|=k}|a_{\alpha}|\,r^{\alpha}+\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{0}|}+\frac{{\bf r}}{1-{\bf r}}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{|\alpha|=k}|a_{\alpha}|^{2}r^{2\alpha}\leq 1

for
n{\bf r}\leq nr_{a_{0}}=\frac{2}{\,3+|a_{0}|+\sqrt{5}\,(1+|a_{0}|)\,}
.
The radius
nr_{a_{0}}
is best possible and
nr_{a_{0}}>\sqrt{5}-2
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{4}(z,{\bf r}):=|f(z)|^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{|\alpha|=k}|a_{\alpha}|\,r^{\alpha}+\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{0}|}+\frac{{\bf r}}{1-{\bf r}}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{|\alpha|=k}|a_{\alpha}|^{2}r^{2\alpha}\leq 1

for

, where
nr^{\prime}_{a_{0}}
is the unique positive root of the equation

\displaystyle(1-|a_{0}|^{3})(n{\bf r}^{3}-(1+2|a_{0}|)\,(n{\bf r})^{2}-2n{\bf r}+1=0.

The radius
nr^{\prime}_{a_{0}}
is best possible. Further, we have
1/3n<r^{\prime}_{a_{0}}<1/n(2+|a_{0}|)
.

Next we state the multidimensional version of Theorem E.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
f(z)=\sum_{|\alpha|=0}a_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}
be a holomorphic function in the polydisk
\mathbb{P}\Delta(0;1_{n})
such that
|f(z)|\leq 1
for all

for
n{\bf r}\leq\frac{\sqrt{17}-3}{4}
and the constant

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(z,{\bf r}):=|f(z)|^{2}+|Df(z)|+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{|\alpha|=k}|a_{\alpha}|\,r^{\alpha}+\left(\frac{1}{1+|a_{0}|}+\frac{{\bf r}}{1-{\bf r}}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{|\alpha|=k}|a_{\alpha}|^{2}r^{2\alpha}\leq 1

for

, where

\displaystyle 1-2n{\bf r}-(n{\bf r})^{2}-(n{\bf r})^{3}-(n{\bf r})^{4}=0

is best possible.

3.
Key lemmas and their Proofs

In order to establish our main results, we need the following lemmas. The first of these is a special case of
[
21
, Theorem 2.2 ]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{|a_{0}|+n{\bf r}}{1+n|a_{0}|{\bf r}}\right)^{2}+\left(1-\left(\frac{|a_{0}|+n{\bf r}}{1+n|a_{0}|{\bf r}}\right)^{2}\right)\frac{(n{\bf r})^{N}}{(1+n{\bf r})(1-2n{\bf r})(1-n{\bf r})^{\,N-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(1-(n{\bf r})^{N})(1-n{\bf r})}{(1+n{\bf r})(1-2n{\bf r})(1-n{\bf r})^{N}}|f(z)|^{2}+\frac{(n{\bf r})^{N}(1-n{\bf r})}{(1+n{\bf r})(1-2n{\bf r})(1-n{\bf r})^{N}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(|a_{0}|+n{\bf r})^{2}(1-n{\bf r})^{N}(1-2n{\bf r})+(1-|a_{0}|^{2})(n{\bf r})^{N}(1-n{\bf r})^{2}}{(1-n{\bf r})^{N}(1+|a_{0}|n{\bf r})^{2}(1-2n{\bf r})}.

(4.10)

Clearly, the expression in (
4
) is less than or equal to
1
provided
\omega_{n,N}(r)\leq 1
, where
\omega_{n,N}(r)
is defined as

\displaystyle\tilde{\omega}_{n,N}(r):=\frac{(|a_{0}|+n{\bf r})^{2}(1-n{\bf r})^{N}(1-2n{\bf r})+(1-|a_{0}|^{2})(n{\bf r})^{N}(1-n{\bf r})^{2}}{(1-n{\bf r})^{N}(1+|a_{0}|n{\bf r})^{2}(1-2n{\bf r})}.

It is easy to see that
\tilde{\omega}_{n,N}(r)\leq 1
if
\tilde{\nu}_{n,N}(r)\leq 0
, where

\displaystyle\tilde{\nu}_{n,N}(r)

\displaystyle=(|a_{0}|+n{\bf r})^{2}(1-n{\bf r})^{N}(1-2n{\bf r})+(1-|a_{0}|^{2})(n{\bf r})^{N}(1-n{\bf r})^{2}

\displaystyle-(1-n{\bf r})^{N}(1+|a_{0}|n{\bf r})^{2}(1-2n{\bf r})

\displaystyle=(1-|a_{0}|^{2})\Big((1-n{\bf r})^{N}(-1+(n{\bf r})^{2}+2n{\bf r}-2(n{\bf r})^{3})+(n{\bf r})^{N}(1-n{\bf r})^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=(1-|a_{0}|^{2})\Big((1-n{\bf r})^{N}(1-n{\bf r})(1+n{\bf r})(2n{\bf r}-1)+(n{\bf r})^{N}(1-n{\bf r})^{2}\Big).

Clearly,
\tilde{\nu}_{n,N}(r)\leq 0
, provided that

\displaystyle(1+n{\bf r})(1-2n{\bf r})(1-n{\bf r})^{N-1}-(n{\bf r})^{N}\geq 0,

which holds for
r\leq R_{n,N}^{\prime}
, where
R_{n,N}^{\prime}
is as in the statement of the theorem.

To show the sharpness of
\tilde{R}_{n,N}^{\prime}
, let
a\in[0,1)
and consider the holomorphic function
f
in
\mathbb{P}\Delta(0;1/n)
given by (
3.13
). For the point
z=(-r,-r,\ldots,-r)
, we find that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(z,{\bf r})

\displaystyle:=\left(\frac{a-n{\bf r}}{1-na{\bf r}}\right)^{2}+(1-a^{2})\frac{a^{N-1}n({\bf r})^{N}}{(1-na{\bf r})^{N}(1-2na{\bf r})}

\displaystyle=\frac{(a-n{\bf r})^{2}(1-an{\bf r})^{N-2}(1-2an{\bf r})+(1-a^{2})a^{N-1}(n{\bf r})^{N}}{(1-an{\bf r})^{N}(1-2an{\bf r})}.

(4.11)

Equation (
4
) is larger than
1
if and only if

\displaystyle(1-a^{2})\Big((-1+2an{\bf r}+(n{\bf r})^{2}-2a(n{\bf r})^{3})(1-an{\bf r})^{N-2}+a^{N-1}(n{\bf r})^{N}\Big)>0.

(4.12)

Elementary calculation, coupled with taking the limit
a\to 1
in (
4.12
), shows that the expression in (
4
) exceeds
1
when
r>R_{n,N}^{\prime}
.
∎

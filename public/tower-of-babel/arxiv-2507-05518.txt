BLOW-UP RESULTS FOR INHOMOGENEOUS FOURTH-ORDER NONLINEAR SCHRÖDINGER EQUATION
Maicon Hespanha, Renzo Scarpelli

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the blow-up phenomenon of the H 2 H^{2} norm of solutions to the inhomogeneous biharmonic Schrodinger equation in two distinct scenarios. First, we consider the case of negative energy, analyzing separately the cases of radial and non-radial solutions. Then, we examine the positive energy case, where the energy is below that of the ground state and the “kinetic” energy exceeds the corresponding value for the ground state, again distinguishing between radial and non-radial solutions. Our approach is based on convexity methods, employing virial identities in the analysis.

1.
introduction

We consider following initial value problem (IVP) for the focusing inhomogeneous biharmonic nonlinear Schrödinger IBNLS equation

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}iu_{t}+\Delta^{2}u=|x|^{-b}|u|^{\frac{8-2b}{N-4}}u,\quad N\geq 5,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\end{array}\right.

where
\Delta^{2}
stands for the biharmonic operator,
b>0
and
u=u(t,x)
is a complex-valued function in space-time
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Note that when
b=0
we get the classic biharmonic nonlinear Schrödinger equation BNLS, which was introduced by
[
17
]
and
[
18
]
and it has been studied throughout the past two decades, where was proved several results about local and global well-posedness, scattering and blowup both in the energy space
H^{2}
and in the fractional Sobolev spaces
H^{s}
. See, for example, Artzi-Koch-Saut
[
4
]
, Boulenger-Lenzmann
[
7
]
, Dihn
[
13
]
, Miao-Xu-Zhao
[
20
,
21
]
, Pausader
[
23
,
24
]
, and the references therein.

As for the classical NLS equation, the equation IBNLS (
1.1
) enjoys the conservation of mass and energy, respectively given by

(1.2)

M(u(t)):=\int|u(t,x)|^{2}dx=M(u_{0}),

(1.3)

E(u(t)):=\frac{1}{2}\int|\Delta u(t,x)|^{2}dx-\frac{N-4}{2(N-b)}\int|x|^{-b}|u(t,x)|^{\frac{2(N-b)}{N-4}}dx=E(u_{0}).

In addition, the term “energy-critical” comes from the fact that the scaling symmetry
u_{\lambda}(t,x)=\lambda^{\frac{(N-4)}{2}}u(\lambda^{4}t,\lambda x)
,
\lambda>0
, leaves the energy invariant in
H^{2}
, the so-called energy space.

In recent years, there has been increasing interest in the IBNLS equation (
1.1
). Several results have been established regarding global and local well-posedness and scattering of solutions for the subcritical, mass-critical and intercritical cases. The reader can check An-Ryu-Kim
[
1
]
-
[
3
]
, Campos-Guzmán
[
9
]
, Cardoso-Guzmán-Pastor
[
11
]
, Guzmán-Pastor
[
15
,
16
]
, and the references therein to details. As far as we are aware, the study of well-posedness for the biharmonic nonlinear Schrödinger equation with inhomogeneous nonlinearities in the critical regime was first established in Guzmán-Pastor
[
16
]
, where the authors proved results concerning well-posedness and stability with initial data in the critical space
\dot{H}^{2}
. More precisely, they proved local well-posedness in
\dot{H}^{2}
for (
1.1
) with the restrictions
5\geq N\leq 11
and
0<b<\frac{12-N}{N-2}
. Then, in An-Ryu-Kim
[
3
]
, the authors used Sobolev-Lorentz space to guarantee that (
1.1
) is local well-posed for
N\leq 5
and
0<b<\min\{4,N/2\}
. They also proved global well-posedness and scattering for small data under the same restrictions.

Before describing our result, we recall that for the classical nonlinear Schrodinger equation NLS in the mass-critical regime, Glassey
[
14
]
proved finite-time blow-up for negative energy
H^{1}
solutions in the virial space. Later, Ogawa-Tsutsumi
[
22
]
removed the virial assumption in the radial case. For the inhomogeneous model, Cardoso-Farah
[
10
]
obtained the same result without assuming radial symmetry, exploiting the decay away from the origin together with a subcritical Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality. In the context of the biharmonic NLS , Boulenger-Lenzmann
[
7
]
established analogous blowup results in the radial setting.

When it comes to IBNLS, to the best of our knowledge, Bai-Majdoub-Saanouni
[
5
]
proved in the mass-critical regime that, for
N\geq 1
,
0<b<\min\{N/2,4\}
and negative energy, that the solution of (
1.1
) either blows-up in finite or infinite time, in the senses that
\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\gtrsim t^{2}
for
t\gg 1
.

Inspired by these works, we investigate the blow-up phenomenon for the inhomogeneous BNLS on the energy-critical scenario. Our main results are stated in the following theorems. The first one concerns to the radial case and reads as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
N\geq 5
,
0<b<\min\{4,N/2\}
and
u_{0}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
radial be such that
E(u_{0})<0
or
0\leq E(u_{0})<E(W)
and
\|\Delta u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}>\|\Delta W\|_{L^{2}}
. Then
u(t)
blows-up in finite time in the sense that exists
T^{*}>0
such that

\lim_{t\rightarrow T^{*}}\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}=+\infty,

where
W
is the ground state solution to (
1.1
).

For the non radial case we have the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
N\geq 5
,
0<b<\min\{4,N/2\}
and
u_{0}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Then we have the following results:

(1)

If
0<b<16/N
and
E(u_{0})<0
then the corresponding maximal solution of (
1.1
)
u(t)
either blows-up in finite time or in infinite time in the sense that

\lim_{t\rightarrow T_{max}}\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}=+\infty.

(2)

If
b\geq 16/N
and
E(u_{0})<0
or
0\leq E(u_{0})<E(W)
with
\|\Delta u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}>\|\Delta W\|_{L^{2}}
, then
u(t)
blows-up in finite time in the sense that exists
T^{*}>0
such that

\lim_{t\rightarrow T^{*}}\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}=+\infty,

where
W
is the ground state solution to (
1.1
).

Remark 1.3
.

The assumption
b\geq 16/N
in the item (2) of Theorem
1.2
is a technical restriction that we found in our argument. In order to handle the error term that appears in a virial-type inequality, we use classical Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality combined with the free decay away from the origin that led us to the constraint
\frac{N(4-b)}{2(N-4)}\leq 2
, equivalently,
b\geq 16/N
(see inequality (
4.1
) bellow). In an attempt to avoid this restriction, we employed a more refined estimate from the seminal paper by Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg
[
8
]
to perform an interpolation, but ultimately arrived at the same constraint.

2.2.
Basic estimates

In this section we will set some estimates that will be useful later on this work. First, as a direct consequence of the Parseval’s identity for the Fourier transform (see
[
5
, Proposition 2.1]
), we get

(2.1)

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}.

Inequality (
2.1
) generally fails to hold in bounded domains
\Omega
except when
u\in W_{0}^{2,2}(\Omega)
. Whether inequality (
2.1
) holds on exterior domains
\Omega
without imposing zero trace on
\partial\Omega
was studied in Crispo-Maremonti
[
12
]
. To summarize, the inequality holds when the set has the cone property, Therefore, since the exterior of an Euclidian ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
possesses the cone property, it follows that

(2.2)

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(|x|>R)}\lesssim\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}(|x|>R)}^{1/2}\|u\|_{L^{2}(|x|>R)}^{1/2},\quad R>0.

Moreover, a scaling argument shows that the constant
C
in (
2.2
) is independent of
R
. Another inequality that plays an important role in the argument for the radial case is the well-known Strauss’s inequality (see Strauss
[
25
]
), that reads as follows.

(2.3)

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(|x|>R)}\leq CR^{-(N-1)/2}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}.

2.3.
Localized Virial Identity

Let
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a cut-off function. We define the virial quantity associated to a
H^{2}
solution of (
1.1
) by

(2.4)

\mathcal{V}_{\phi}(t):=-2\mathrm{Im}\int\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla u(t)\bar{u}(t)dx.

Remark 2.1
.

The minus sign in the definition of the Virial identity, although uncommon, arises from the choice of a positive sign for the biharmonic term and a negative sign for the nonlinearity in equation (
1.1
).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
d\geq 5
. Suppose that
u\in C([0,T);H^{2}])
is a solution of (
1.1
). Then, for any
t\in[0,T)
, we have

\begin{split}\mathcal{V}_{\phi}^{\prime}(t)&=-4\sum_{j,k}^{N}\int\partial_{jk}\Delta\phi\partial_{j}u\partial_{k}\bar{u}dx+\int\Delta^{3}\phi|u|^{2}dx\\
&\quad+8\sum_{i,j,k}^{N}\int\partial_{jk}\phi\partial_{ik}u\partial_{ij}\bar{u}dx-2\int\Delta^{2}\phi|\nabla u|^{2}dx\\
&\quad-\frac{8-2b}{N-b}\int\Delta\phi|x|^{-b}|u|^{\frac{8-2b}{N-4}+2}+\frac{2(N-4)}{N-b}\int\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla(|x|^{-b})|u|^{\frac{2(N-b)}{N-4}}\\
\end{split}

Proof.

We start by noticing that

(2.5)

i\mathcal{V}_{\phi}(t)=-2i\mathrm{Im}\int\bar{u}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi dx=\int u\nabla\bar{u}\cdot\nabla\phi dx-\int\bar{u}\nabla u\cdot\phi dx.

On the other hand, integration by parts yields

(2.6)

\int|u|^{2}\Delta\phi dx=-\int u\nabla\bar{u}\cdot\phi dx-\int\bar{u}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi dx.

Combining (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) we get

(2.7)

\begin{split}i\mathcal{V}_{\phi}(t)&=-2\int\bar{u}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi dx-\int\bar{u}\Delta\phi udx\\
&=-\int\bar{u}[\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla+\Delta\phi]udx-\int\bar{u}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi dx.\end{split}

If we rewrite
-\int\bar{u}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi:=a+bi
, we conclude that
\mathcal{V}_{\phi}(t)=2b
. Therefore

\mathcal{V}_{\phi}(t)=-2\mathrm{Re}\int\bar{u}\Gamma_{\phi}udx,

where
\Gamma_{\phi}:=-i\left(\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla+\Delta\phi\right)
. By introducing the notation
\langle u,v\rangle=2\mathrm{Re}(u,v)_{L^{2}}
we get

\mathcal{V}_{\phi}(t):=-\langle u,\Gamma_{\phi}u\rangle=-\langle iu,i\Gamma_{\phi}u\rangle,

taking the time derivative and using (
1.1
), we obtain

\begin{split}\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{\phi}(t)&=\langle u,\left[\Delta^{2},i\Gamma_{\phi}\right]u\rangle-\langle u,[|x|^{-b}|u|^{\frac{8-2b}{N-4}}u,i\Gamma_{\phi}]u\rangle\\
&:=A-B,\end{split}

where
[X,Y]=XY-YX
. Note that we may treat the term
A
in the same way as
\mathcal{A}_{R}^{[1]}
in
[
7
, Lemma 3.1]
(so we will omit the details) to get

(2.8)

\begin{split}A&=8\mathrm{Re}\sum_{i,j,k}\int\partial^{2}_{j,k}\phi\partial^{2}_{i,k}u\partial^{2}_{i,j}\bar{u}dx\\
&\quad-4\mathrm{Re}\sum_{j,k}\int\partial_{j}\bar{u}\partial^{2}_{j,k}\Delta\phi\partial_{k}udx\\
&\quad-2\int\Delta^{2}\phi|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\int\Delta^{3}\phi|u|^{2}dx.\\
\end{split}

For
B
, we use integration by parts

(2.9)

\begin{split}B&=2\mathrm{Re}\left[\int(|x|^{-b}|u|^{\frac{8-2b}{N-4}}u)i\Gamma_{\phi}\bar{u}dx-\int\bar{u}i\Gamma_{\phi}|x|^{-b}|u|^{\frac{8-2b}{N-4}}udx\right]\\
&=2\mathrm{Re}\left[\int|x|^{-b}|u|^{\frac{8-2b}{N-4}}u\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\bar{u}dx+\int|x|^{-b}|u|^{\frac{2(N-b)}{N-4}}\Delta\phi dx\right]\\
&\quad-2\mathrm{Re}\left[\int\bar{u}\nabla\phi_{R}\cdot\nabla(|x|^{-b}|u|^{\frac{8-2b}{N-4}}u)dx+\int|x|^{-b}|u|^{\frac{2(N-b)}{N-4}}\Delta\phi dx\right]\\
&=2\mathrm{Re}\left[2\int|x|^{-b}|u|^{\frac{8-2b}{N-4}}u\nabla\bar{u}\cdot\nabla\phi dx+\int(|x|^{-b}|u|^{\frac{2(N-b)}{N-4}})\Delta\phi dx\right].\\
\end{split}

For the first one, we use the equality
\nabla(|u|^{\frac{2(N-b)}{N-4}})=\frac{2(N-b)}{N-4}|u|^{\frac{8-2b}{N-4}}\mathrm{Re}\nabla\bar{u}u
and integration by parts to get

(2.10)

\begin{split}4\mathrm{Re}\int|x|^{-b}|u|^{\frac{8-2b}{N-4}}u\nabla\bar{u}\cdot\nabla\phi dx&=-\frac{2(N-4)}{N-b}\int\nabla(|x|^{-b})\nabla\phi|u|^{\frac{2(N-b)}{N-4}}dx\\
&-\frac{2(N-4)}{N-b}\int(|x|^{-b}|u|^{\frac{2(N-b)}{N-4}})\Delta\phi dx.\end{split}

Combining the above equality with (
2.9
) we get the desired result.
∎

For
R>0
, set the radial cut-off function

(2.11)

\phi_{R}(r)=R^{2}\phi\left(\frac{r}{R}\right),\quad r=|x|,

where

\phi(r)=\int_{0}^{r}\chi(s)ds,

with
\chi:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is a smooth function satisfying

\chi(r)=\begin{cases}2r&\hbox{if}\quad 0\leq r\leq 1,\\
2r-2(r-1)^{2}&\hbox{if}\quad 1<r<1+1/\sqrt{2}\\
\chi^{\prime}(r)<0&\hbox{if}\quad 1+1+1/\sqrt{2}<r<2\\
0&\hbox{if}\quad r\geq 2.\end{cases}

The next result summarize some properties of the cut-off function
\phi_{R}
. The proof can be found in
[
13
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\phi_{R}
be the as in (
2.11
). Then

(2.12)

\phi^{\prime}_{R}(r)=2r,\quad\phi^{\prime\prime}_{R}(r)=2,\quad\hbox{for }r\leq R,

(2.13)

\phi^{\prime}_{R}(r)-r\phi^{\prime\prime}_{R}(r)\geq 0,\quad\phi^{\prime}_{R}(r)\leq 2r,\quad\phi^{\prime\prime}_{R}(r)\leq 2,

(2.14)

\|\nabla^{j}\phi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim R^{2-j},\quad 0\leq j\leq 6,

and

(2.15)

supp(\nabla^{j}\phi_{R})\subset\begin{cases}\left\{|x|\leq 2R\right\}&\hbox{if }j=1,2,\\

2.4.
Ground State

Based on the results of Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg
[
8
]
, the following inequality is known to hold.

Theorem 2.4
.

[Critical Sobolev inequality]
Let
u\in\dot{H}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
N>4
and
b<min\{4,N/2\}
. Then,

(2.16)

\int|x|^{-b}|u|^{\frac{2(N-b)}{N-4}}dx\leq K_{opt}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2(N-b)}{N-4}}.

Introducing the “Weinstein function” as

J(f):=\frac{\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2(N-b)}{N-4}}}{\int|x|^{-b}|u|^{\frac{2(N-b)}{N-4}}dx},

Following the standard convention,
0\neq W\in{H}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is said to be ground state if optimizes (
2.16
), i.e.

J(W)=\inf\{J(f),\,f\in\dot{H}^{2},\,f\neq 0\}

In Bhakta-Musina
[
6
, Theorem 5.1]
, was proved that there is a unique, decreasing and positive radial ground state solution, namely
W
, which also satisfy the elliptic equation

(2.17)

\Delta^{2}W(x)=|x|^{-b}|W(x)|^{\frac{8-2b}{N-4}}W(x).

The following lemma establishes some Pohozaev identities for
W
and characterizes the optimal constant to (
2.16
).

Lemma 2.5
.

[Pohozaev-type identities] Let
W\in\dot{H}^{2}\cap L^{p}
, with
p=\frac{2(N-b)}{N-4}
, be the unique solution to (
2.17
). Then the following identities holds

(2.18)

\|\Delta W\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\displaystyle\int|x|^{-b}|W|^{p}dx;

(2.19)

E(W)=\frac{4-b}{2(N-b)}\|\Delta W\|_{L^{2}}^{2};

(2.20)

K_{opt}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|\Delta W\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{8-2b}{N-4}}}.

Proof.

We get the first one by multiplying (
2.17
) by
W
, integrating and using integration by parts. The second one is a direct consequence of (
2.18
) and the definition of the energy (
1.3
). Finaly, since
W
is the ground state solution, it attains the optimal value in (
2.16
), leading us to

\int|x|^{-b}|W|^{p}dx=K_{opt}\|\Delta W\|_{L^{2}}^{p}.

Combining with (
2.18
) we get (
2.20
).
∎

A direct consequence of the Pohozaev-type identities is the following lemma.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
u(t)
be a solution to
1.1
satisfying
0<E(u_{0})<E(W)
and
\|\Delta u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}>\|\Delta W\|_{L^{2}}
. Then, there exists
\delta>0
such that

E(u(t))\leq(1-\delta)\frac{4-b}{2(N-b)}\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Proof.

Given the initial conditions
0<E(u_{0})<E(W)
and
\|\Delta u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}>\|\Delta W\|_{L^{2}}
, a standard continuity argument implies that
\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}>\|\Delta W\|_{L^{2}}
for all
t
in the maximal interval of existence. Indeed, by the optimal Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality (
2.16
), we have

\displaystyle E(u(t))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{N-4}{2(N-b)}\int|x|^{-b}|u(t)|^{2+\frac{8-2b}{N-4}}dx

(2.21)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{N-4}{2(N-b)}\frac{\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2+\frac{8-2b}{N-4}}}{\|\Delta W\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{8-2b}{N-4}}}.

Now, define the function

f(x)=\frac{1}{2}x^{2}-\frac{N-4}{2(N-b)}\frac{x^{2+\frac{8-2b}{N-4}}}{\|\Delta W\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{8-2b}{N-4}}}

and note that
f(x)
attains its maximum at
x_{m}=\|\Delta W\|_{L^{2}}
, with
f(x_{m})=E(W)
. Since
E(u_{0})<E(W)
and
\|\Delta u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}>\|\Delta W\|_{L^{2}}
, it follows from (
2.4
) and the continuity of the
\dot{H}^{2}
norm of the solution that
\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}>\|\Delta W\|_{L^{2}}
.

We also note, by Pohozaev’s identity (
2.19
) that

E(W)=\frac{4-b}{2(N-b)}\|\Delta W\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Therefore, using that
E(u(t))<E(W)
, there exists
\delta>0
such that
E(u(t))<(1-\delta)E(W)
. Finally, using that
\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}>\|\Delta W\|_{L^{2}}
we get the result.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We start by setting the Morawetz potential

(3.1)

\mathcal{V}_{R}(t):=\mathcal{V}_{\phi_{R}}(t)=-2\mathrm{Im}\int\nabla\phi_{R}\cdot\nabla u(t)\bar{u}(t)dx,

where
\phi_{R}
is defined as in (
2.11
).

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
u(t)
be a radial solution of (
1.1
). We have

(3.2)

\begin{split}\mathcal{V}_{R}^{\prime}(t)&\leq\frac{32(N-b)}{(N-4)}E(u_{0})-\frac{16(4-b)}{(N-4)}\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&+O\left(R^{-4}+R^{-2}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+R^{-b-\frac{(N-1)(4-b)}{N-4}}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4-b}{N-4}}\right).\end{split}

Proof.

We begin by recalling the following radial identities

(3.3)

\nabla_{x}=\frac{x}{r}\partial_{r},

(3.4)

\Delta_{x}=\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{N-1}{r}\partial_{r},

\partial_{j}\partial_{k}=\left(\frac{\delta_{jk}}{r}-\partial{x_{j}x_{k}}{r^{3}}\right)\partial_{r}+\frac{x_{j}x_{k}}{r^{2}}\partial_{r}^{2},

\nabla_{x}\cdot\nabla(|x|^{-b})=-b|x|^{-b}\frac{\partial_{r}}{r}.

Applying above identities in Lemma
2.2
, by using the definition of
\phi_{R}
(
2.11
), and the conservation of mass, we obtain

\begin{split}\mathcal{V}_{r}^{\prime}(t)&\leq 16\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{8-2b}{N-b}\int\Delta\phi_{R}\,|x|^{-b}|u|^{\frac{8-2b}{N-4}+2}\,dx\\
&\quad-\frac{2b(N-4)}{N-b}\int\frac{\nabla\phi_{R}\cdot x}{|x|^{2}}\,|x|^{-b}|u|^{\frac{8-2b}{N-4}+2}\,dx+O(R^{-4}+R^{-2}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}).\end{split}

Using (
3.3
) we deduce that
\frac{\nabla\phi_{R}\cdot x}{|x|^{2}}=\frac{\phi_{R}^{\prime}}{r}
. Combining this with (
3.4
) we deduce

\begin{split}\mathcal{V}_{R}^{\prime}(t)&\leq 16\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\int\Phi_{R}(r)\,|x|^{-b}|u|^{\frac{8-2b}{N-4}+2}\,dx+O(R^{-4}+R^{-2}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}),\end{split}

where

\Phi_{R}(r)=\frac{8-2b}{N-b}\phi_{R}^{\prime\prime}+\left(\frac{8-2b}{N-b}(N-1)+\frac{2b(N-4)}{N-b}\right)\frac{\phi_{R}^{\prime}}{r}.

A direct computation shows that inside the ball of radius
R>0
, we have
\Phi_{R}(r)=16
. Therefore,

\begin{split}\mathcal{V}_{R}^{\prime}(t)&\leq 16\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\int(\Phi_{R}(r)-16)\,|x|^{-b}|u|^{\frac{8-2b}{N-4}+2}\,dx\\
&\quad-16\int|x|^{-b}|u|^{\frac{8-2b}{N-4}+2}\,dx+O(R^{-4}+R^{-2}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}).\end{split}

Using the conservation of energy, we obtain

(3.5)

\begin{split}\mathcal{V}_{R}^{\prime}(t)&\leq\frac{32(N-b)}{N-4}E(u_{0})-\frac{16(4-b)}{N-4}\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&\quad-E_{r}+O(R^{-4}+R^{-2}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}),\end{split}

where

E_{r}=\int_{|x|>R}(\Phi_{R}(r)-16)\,|x|^{-b}|u|^{\frac{8-2b}{N-4}+2}\,dx.

To estimate this error term, we apply Strauss’s inequality (
2.3
) and the conservation of mass to get

\begin{split}|E_{r}|&\leq R^{-b}M(u_{0})\,\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(|x|>R)}^{\frac{8-2b}{N-4}}\\
&\lesssim R^{-b-\frac{(N-1)(4-b)}{N-4}}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4-b}{N-4}},\end{split}

which concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

With this lemma in hand, we can now establish the blow-up result for the radial case.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We begin by showing the result in the case of negative energy. Using (
2.1
), Young’s inequality and the conservation of mass, we observe that

\begin{split}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}&\leq M(u_{0})^{1/2}\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\lesssim 1+\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\end{split}

and similarly,

\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{4-b}{N-4}}\lesssim 1+\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Therefore, by choosing
R>0
sufficiently large and absorbing the error terms, we obtain

(3.6)

\mathcal{V}_{R}^{\prime}(t)\leq\frac{16(N-b)}{N-4}E(u_{0})-\frac{8(4-b)}{N-4}\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Using the hypothesis
E(u_{0})<0
we get that
\mathcal{V}_{R}^{\prime}(t)
is strictly negative. We now apply a standard ODE argument to conclude the proof. Suppose, for contradiction, that the maximal time of existence is
T=+\infty
. Since
\mathcal{V}_{R}^{\prime}(t)
is strictly negative, there exists a time
t_{0}
such that
\mathcal{V}_{R}(t)\leq 0
for all
t\geq t_{0}
. In particular, we have

\mathcal{V}_{R}(t)\leq-C\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\|\Delta u(s)\|^{2}\,ds,

for some constant
C>0
. Moreover, by definition, we also have the estimate
|\mathcal{V}_{R}(t)|\lesssim\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}
. Combining these facts yields

(3.7)

\mathcal{V}_{R}(t)\leq-C\int_{t_{0}}^{t}|\mathcal{V}_{R}(s)|^{4}\,ds,

where
C=C(u_{0},R)
.

Define

A(t)=\int_{t_{0}}^{t}|\mathcal{V}_{R}(s)|^{4}\,ds.

Note that
A(t)
is non-decreasing, and from (
3.7
) we obtain

A^{\prime}(t)=|\mathcal{V}_{R}(t)|^{4}\geq C^{4}A(t)^{4}.

Integrating this inequality from
t_{1}>t_{0}
to
t
yields

\int_{t_{1}}^{t}\frac{A^{\prime}(s)}{A(s)^{4}}\,ds\geq C^{4}(t-t_{1}),

which implies

A(t)^{3}\geq\frac{A(t_{1})^{3}}{1-3C^{4}A(t_{1})^{3}(t-t_{1})}.

Hence,
A(t)\to+\infty
as
t\to t^{*}=t_{1}+\frac{1}{3C^{4}A(t_{1})^{3}}
. Since
\mathcal{V}_{R}(t)\leq-CA(t)
, it follows that
\mathcal{V}_{R}(t)\to-\infty
in finite time, which implies that
\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\to\infty
in finite time. This contradicts the assumption that the solution exists globally in time.

We now turn our attention to the case of positive energy. Using the estimate (
3.6
) in combination with Lemma
2.6
, we obtain

\begin{split}\mathcal{V}_{R}^{\prime}(t)&\leq\frac{16(N-b)}{N-4}E(u_{0})-\frac{8(4-b)}{N-4}\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&\leq(1-\delta)\frac{8(4-b)}{N-4}\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{8(4-b)}{N-4}\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&=-8\delta\frac{4-b}{N-4}\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\\
&\leq-8\delta\frac{4-b}{N-4}\|\Delta W\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&<0\\
\end{split}

the argument proceeds along the same lines.

∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

The main difference in the proof of this result lies in how we handle the error term

E_{R}=\int_{|x|>R}(\Phi_{R}(r)-16)\,|x|^{-b}|u|^{\frac{8-2b}{N-4}+2}\,dx,

which appears in (
3.5
). Since the solution is no longer radial, we cannot apply Strauss’ inequality. However, due to the presence of the
|x|^{-b}
term, we benefit from decay away from the origin (see, e.g., Cardoso-Farah
[
10
]
).

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We split the proof into three cases.

(1)

Case
0<b<16/N
.

Suppose that the solution is global in time and satisfies
\|\Delta u(t)\|\leq M
for all
t
. Then, we can estimate the error term using the classical Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality as follows:

\begin{split}|E_{r}|&\lesssim R^{-b}\int|u|^{\frac{8-2b}{N-4}+2}\,dx\\
&\lesssim R^{-b}\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{N(4-b)}{2(N-4)}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{b}{2}}\\
&\lesssim_{u_{0},M}R^{-b}.\end{split}

Combining this with (
3.5
), and choosing
R>0
sufficiently large, we obtain

\mathcal{V}_{R}^{\prime}(t)\leq\frac{8(N-b)}{N-4}E(u_{0}),

which implies
\mathcal{V}_{R}^{\prime}(t)\leq-C<0
. Using the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus and the bound

\begin{split}|\mathcal{V}_{R}(t)|&\leq\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\lesssim_{u_{0}}\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\\
&\lesssim_{u_{0}}M^{1/2}\end{split}

we reach a contradiction.

(2)

Case
b\geq 16/N
and
E(u_{0})<0
.

The key observation in this case is that for
b\geq 16/N
, we have
\frac{N(4-b)}{2(N-4)}\leq 2
. Therefore, by Gagliardo Nirenberg’s inequality and conservation of mass we have

(4.1)

\begin{split}|E_{r}|&\lesssim R^{-b}\int|u|^{\frac{8-2b}{N-4}+2}\,dx\\
&\lesssim R^{-b}\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{N(4-b)}{2(N-4)}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{b}{2}}\\
&\lesssim_{u_{0}}R^{-b}+R^{-b}\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\end{split}

since
\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{N(4-b)}{2(N-4)}}\leq 1+\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
. Again, choosing
R>0
sufficiently large yields (
3.6
). The same ODE argument as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
then implies finite-time blow-up.

Case
b\geq 16/N
and
0\leq E(u_{0})<E(W)
with
\|\Delta u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}>\|\Delta W\|_{L^{2}}
.

Using the estimate

|E_{r}|\lesssim_{u_{0}}R^{-b}+R^{-b}\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

and choosing
R>0
large enough, we can combine this with Lemma
2.6
to obtain

\mathcal{V}_{R}^{\prime}(t)\leq-8\delta\frac{4-b}{N-4}\|\Delta u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

and the argument then proceeds exactly as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

∎

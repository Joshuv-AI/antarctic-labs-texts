Intermittent singular solutions of the stationary 2D Navier-Stokes equations in sharp Sobolev spaces
Estepan Ashkarian, Ataleshvara Bhargava, Nicholas Gismondi, Matthew Novack

Abstract
In this paper we construct non-trivial solutions to the stationary Navier-Stokes equations on the two dimensional torus which lie in ⋂ ϵ ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) L 2 − ϵ ​ ( 𝕋 2 ) ∩ H ˙ − ϵ ​ ( 𝕋 2 ) \bigcap_{\epsilon\in(0,1)}L^{2-\epsilon}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\cap\dot{H}^{-\epsilon}(\mathbb{T}^{2}) . Due to the fact that our solutions are not square integrable, we must redefine the notion of solution. Our result gives a sharp extension of the recent work of Lemarié-Rieusset, [ 19 ] who proved a similar result in the space H ˙ − 1 ∩ BMO − 1 \dot{H}^{-1}\cap\operatorname{BMO}^{-1} . The main new ingredient is the incorporation of intermittency into the construction of the solutions.



1
Introduction

1.1
Motivation and background

In this paper we consider the stationary two-dimensional incompressible Navier-Stokes equations

\begin{cases}\operatorname{div}(u\otimes u)-\Delta u+\nabla p=0\\
\operatorname{div}(u)=0\end{cases}

(1.1)

posed on the torus for
u(x)\colon\mathbb{T}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
. We will consider mean zero solutions, that is

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}u\,dx=0.

Let us start by recalling known results on solutions of (
1.1
). If
u
is a smooth solution of (
1.1
), then it must be a trivial one. Indeed, we multiply the equation by
u
, integrate by parts, and use
\operatorname{div}(u)=0
to obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2}\|\nabla u^{i}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\partial_{j}u^{i}\partial_{j}u^{i}\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}u^{i}\partial_{jj}u^{i}\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\partial_{j}(u^{i}u^{j})u^{i}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}u^{i}\partial_{i}p\,dx

\displaystyle=0\,,

which implies for mean-zero solutions that
u\equiv 0
. The same computation can be justified when we assume
u
is a solution of (
1.1
) in
L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
when
p>2
. In this scenario,
u
must in fact be smooth, and hence we can apply the previous argument to conclude
u\equiv 0
; see recent work of Lemarié-Rieusset
[
19
]
for more details. Moreover, in
[
19
]
it is shown that any solution
u
of (
1.1
) in the Lorentz space
L^{2,1}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
is trivial.

On the other hand, in
[
19
]
Lemarié-Rieusset proved that there exist nontrivial stationary solutions in
\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
to (
1.1
). While it is clear how to define weak solutions to (
1.1
) if
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
, it is not obvious what is meant by a weak solution if
u\otimes u
is not locally integrable. Lemarié-Rieusset treats this issue by expanding
u\otimes u
in a doubly infinite Fourier series and checking that this infinite sum converges in a negative Sobolev space. We shall adopt a similar notion in Definition
1.2
using paraproducts. While the result in
[
19
]
is the first for nontrivial stationary weak solutions, there are two earlier but relevant results of Christ
[
12
,
13
]
. In
[
12
]
, Christ constructs nontrivial time-dependent solutions of the one dimensional periodic cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation which belong to
C^{0}_{t}H^{-\epsilon}_{x}
. The key issue is again the interpretation of the cubic nonlinearity
|u|^{2}u
when
u\notin L^{3}_{t,x}
, and Christ shows that this object has an interpretation as a limit
\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}|u_{\epsilon}|^{2}u_{\epsilon}
, where
u_{\epsilon}
is a mollified version of
u
. In
[
13
]
, Christ then treats the time-dependent two-dimensional Navier-Stokes equation using similar methodology. We remark that Christ identifies a connection between his work and the well-known earlier works of Scheffer
[
21
]
and Shnirelman
[
22
]
on non-uniqueness for the Euler equations; in particular, we quote the following observation from
[
13
]
:

Our construction and that of Shnirelman have in common both the use of
driving forces tending weakly to zero, and the exploitation of this reverse energy cascade.

In particular this also highlights the connection between Christ’s works
[
12
,
13
]
and modern convex integration/Nash iteration techniques descended from
[
21
,
22
]
.

1.2
Main result

In this paper, we consider the following notion of stationary solution. For
f\in H^{s}
and
g\in H^{s^{\prime}}
,
s,s^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
, we formally define

fg=\sum_{j,j^{\prime}\geq 0}\mathbb{P}_{2^{j}}f\mathbb{P}_{2^{j^{\prime}}}g.

The projection operators
\mathbb{P}_{2^{j}}
are the standard Littlewood-Paley projections onto frequencies of size
\approx 2^{j}
; see Definition
2.1
. There is not much that can be said in general about the convergence of this sum in negative Sobolev spaces. However, we may offer the following definition for the mean-zero portion
\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}(fg)=(\operatorname{Id}-\fint_{\mathbb{T}^{2}})(fg)
of a formal product
fg
.

Definition 1.1

(
Paraproducts in
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
)
.

Let
f,g
be distributions, so that
\mathbb{P}_{2^{j}}(f),\mathbb{P}_{2^{j^{\prime}}}(g)
are well-defined for
j,j^{\prime}\geq 0
. We say that
\mathbb{P}_{\not=0}(fg)
is well-defined as a paraproduct in
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
for some
s\in\mathbb{R}
if

\sum_{j,j^{\prime}\geq 0}\left\|\mathbb{P}_{\not=0}\left(\mathbb{P}_{2^{j}}(f)\mathbb{P}_{2^{j^{\prime}}}(g)\right)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}<\infty\,.

Then we define

\mathbb{P}_{\not=0}(fg)=\sum_{j,j^{\prime}\geq 0}\mathbb{P}_{\not=0}\left(\mathbb{P}_{2^{j}}(f)\mathbb{P}_{2^{j^{\prime}}}(g)\right)\,,

since the right-hand side is an absolutely summable series in
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
.

With the above definition in hand, we define a weak notion of solution to (
1.1
).

Definition 1.2

(
Weak paraproduct solutions to (
1.1
)
)
.

If
u\in\dot{H}^{s}
,
s<0
, we say
u
is a weak paraproduct solution to (
1.1
) if there is
s^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\mathbb{P}_{\not=0}(u\otimes u)
is well defined as a paraproduct in
\dot{H}^{s^{\prime}}
in the sense of Definition
1.1
and

\left\langle\Delta\phi^{i},u^{i}\right\rangle_{\dot{H}^{-s},\dot{H}^{s}}+\left\langle\partial_{j}\phi^{i},\mathbb{P}_{\not=0}(u^{i}u^{j})\right\rangle_{\dot{H}^{-s^{\prime}},\dot{H}^{s^{\prime}}}=0

for all smooth, divergence free vector fields
\phi
.

Our main result is then as follows.

Theorem 1.3

(
Non-trivial solutions of 2D stationary Navier-Stokes equations
)
.

There exist nontrivial (non-constant and non-zero) weak solutions to (
1.1
) in the sense of Definition
1.2
belonging to
\displaystyle\bigcap_{\epsilon\in(0,1)}L^{2-\epsilon}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\cap\dot{H}^{-\epsilon}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
.

Considering that stationary mean-zero solutions in
L^{2,1}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
must be trivial from
[
19
]
, Theorem
1.3
is essentially sharp. The only possible strengthening would be to address the endpoint case
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
. It appears that there are clear obstructions which prevent any simple extension of our method to the case
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
. However, it is conceivable to us that stationary solutions in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
may be nontrivial, although a different method of proof may be required.

Remark 1.4

(
Extensions of Theorem
1.3
)
.

The same techniques we use to prove Theorem
1.3
can easily be extended to prove the following: let
u_{0}
be any smooth divergence-free vector field. Then for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
u_{\epsilon},p_{\epsilon}
which solves (
1.1
) in the sense of Definition
1.2
and satisfies
\|u_{\epsilon}-u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{-\epsilon}}+\|u_{\epsilon}-u_{0}\|_{L^{2-\epsilon}}<\epsilon
.

Remark 1.5

(
Regularity in
L^{\infty}
-based Besov spaces
)
.

Our construction gives solutions lying in
\bigcap_{\epsilon>0}B^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}-\epsilon}_{\infty,\infty}
. Indeed this is immediate from Bernstein’s inequality and the absolute summability of the series used to define
u=\sum_{q\geq 0}u_{q+1}-u_{q}
in
\bigcap_{\epsilon>0}L^{2-\epsilon}
; see (
3.1
).

1.3
Related literature

In addition to the recent work of Lemarié-Rieusset
[
19
]
and the earlier work of Christ
[

, the past decade has seen numerous flexibility results for the Navier-Stokes equations. Many of these fall under the umbrella of convex integration/Nash iteration, initiated by De Lellis and Székelyhidi in
[
16
,
17
]
for the Euler equations. Most relevant to our setting is the intermittent style of Nash iteration, developed first by Buckmaster and Vicol
[
7
]
for the Navier-Stokes equations. Variants of intermittent convex integration have been utilized by Luo
[
20
]
for the 4D NSE, Buckmaster, Colombo, and Vicol
[
6

to concentrate the temporal sets on which the solutions to 3D NSE are rough, and Cheskidov and Luo
[

11

for solutions in

and
C^{t}L^{p}_{x}
for
p<2
. Alternatively, Albritton, Brué, and Colombo
[
1
]
utilized a spectral instability to prove non-uniqueness of Leray solutions for the forced 3D Navier-Stokes equations, and Albritton and Colombo
[

1.4
Quick idea of the proof

We use a Nash iteration method, constructing a nontrivial solution to (
1.1
) as a limit of smooth solutions of the Euler Reynolds system

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}(u_{q}\otimes u_{q})-\Delta u_{q}+\nabla p_{q}=\operatorname{div}{R_{q}}

(1.2a)

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}(u_{q})=0\,,

(1.2b)

where
R_{q}
is assumed to be symmetric. We assume
(u_{q},R_{q})
satisfy natural inductive assumptions which imply that
u_{q}\rightarrow u
in
L^{2-\epsilon}\cap\dot{H}^{-\epsilon}
. In particular, we do
not
prove that
R_{q}\rightarrow 0
in
L^{1}
, only that
R_{q}\rightarrow 0
in
\dot{H}^{-2}
; see
1
-
5
for precise formulations.

In standard Nash iterations, convergence in the limit as
q\rightarrow\infty
of
u_{q}\otimes u_{q}
follows from the absolute summability of
\sum_{q\geq 0}\|u_{q+1}-u_{q}\|_{L^{2}}
. Since our solutions do not belong to
L^{2}
, we instead prove that
u_{q}\otimes u_{q}
converges to
u\otimes u
in the sense of Definition
1.1
. Morally speaking,
u_{q}-u_{q-1}=\mathbb{P}_{2^{f(q)}}u
, where
f(q)\rightarrow\infty
as
q\rightarrow\infty
superlinearly. This allows for successive iterates to be decorrelated and affords us stronger control over their products. We use this to iteratively control the norms of
u_{q}
and its Littlewood-Paley projections in negative Sobolev spaces and
L^{p}
for
p<2
by carefully constructing the velocity increments; see Definition
4.2
. Analysis involving the frequency locations of the terms present in the velocity increment plays an important role in proving these estimates. We also control the support sizes and orthogonality properties of the increments.

To be more concrete, let us write
u=\sum_{q\geq 0}w_{q}
; then we formally have that

\displaystyle u\otimes u

\displaystyle=\sum_{q,q^{\prime}\geq 0}w_{q}\otimes w_{q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{q\neq q^{\prime}}w_{q}\otimes w_{q^{\prime}}+\sum_{q}w_{q}\otimes w_{q}.

For the first sum, we may assume without loss of generality that
q>q^{\prime}
, and bound the terms by
\|w_{q^{\prime}}\|_{L^{1}}\|w_{q}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim 2^{-(q+q^{\prime})}
. For the second sum, i.e. “the diagonal,” we decompose the terms by writing
w_{q}\otimes w_{q}=-R_{q-1}+E
, where
R_{q-1}
is the previous Reynolds stress error and
E
is a high frequency oscillation error. Both of these terms will approach zero in
\dot{H}^{-2}
as
q\rightarrow\infty
, ensuring that the terms on the diagonal converge. Hence the convergence of
R_{q}
to zero in
\dot{H}^{-2}
corresponds precisely to the convergence of
u_{q}\otimes u_{q}
in
\dot{H}^{-2}
.

Definition 2.1

(
Littlewood-Paley projectors
)
.

There exists
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to[0,1]
, smooth, radially symmetric, and compactly supported in
\{6/7\leq|\xi|\leq 2\}
such that
\varphi(\xi)=1
on
\{1\leq|\xi|\leq 12/7\}
,

\sum_{j\geq 0}\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)=1\hskip 7.11317pt\text{ for all }\hskip 7.11317pt|\xi|\geq 1,

and
\operatorname{supp}\varphi_{j}\cap\operatorname{supp}\varphi_{j^{\prime}}=\emptyset
for all
|j-j^{\prime}|\geq 2
, where
\varphi_{j}(\cdot)=\varphi(2^{-j}\cdot)
. We define the projection of a function
f
on its
0
-mode by

\mathbb{P}_{0}f=\fint_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}f,

and the projection on the
j^{\rm th}
shell by

\mathbb{P}_{2^{j}}(f)(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\widehat{f}(k)\varphi_{j}(k)e^{2\pi ik\cdot x}\,.

We also define
\mathbb{P}_{\neq 0}f:=(\operatorname{Id}-\mathbb{P}_{0})f
, and

\mathbb{P}_{\leq 2^{j}}(f)(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\widehat{f}(k)\psi_{j}(k)e^{2\pi ik\cdot x},

where
\psi_{j}(\cdot)=\sum_{|j^{\prime}|\leq j}\varphi_{j^{\prime}}(\cdot)
.

Definition 2.2

(
\dot{H}^{s}
Sobolev spaces
)
.

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we define

\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{2})=\left\{f:\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\\
k\not=0\end{subarray}}|k|^{2s}|\widehat{f}(k)|^{2}<\infty\right\}

with the norm induced by the sum above.

Remark 2.3
.

For every
f\in\dot{H}^{s}
for some
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we can define the Fourier coefficients

\widehat{f}(k)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}e^{-2\pi ik\cdot x}f(x)dx,\hskip 7.11317pt\text{ where }\hskip 7.11317pt\mathbb{T}^{2}=[0,1]^{2},

and we can define
\mathbb{P}_{2^{j}}f
for
j\geq 0
. Note that each
\mathbb{P}_{2^{j}}f
is smooth if
f\in\dot{H}^{s}
irrespective of the value of
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 2.4

(
L^{p}
boundedness of projection operators
)
.

If
\lambda=2^{j}
, then
\mathbb{P}_{\leq\lambda}
is a bounded operator from
L^{p}
to
L^{p}
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
with operator norm independent of
\lambda
and
j
.

Recall the following geometric lemma from

Lemma 2.5

(
Reconstruction of symmetric tensors
)
.

Let
B(I,\epsilon)
be the ball of radius
\epsilon
around the identity matrix in the space of
2\times 2
symmetric matrices. We can choose
\epsilon>0
such that there exists a finite set
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{S}^{1}
and smooth positive functions
\gamma_{k}\in C^{\infty}(B(I,\epsilon))
for
k\in\Lambda
such that the following hold:

1.

5\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{2}

2.

For all
R\in B(I,\epsilon)
we have

R=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in\Lambda}(\gamma_{k}(R))^{2}k^{\perp}\otimes k^{\perp}.

In the proof of the above lemma, one chooses

,

, and

Lemma 2.6

(
High-low products in negative Sobolev norms
)
.

Let
a(\cdot)
be a smooth function on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}
and
V\colon\mathbb{T}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be a mean zero and smooth. Then

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\|a(\cdot)V(\lambda\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{-2}}=0.

(2.1)

Proof.

Consider the following decomposition of the product
a(\cdot)V(\lambda\cdot)
into low and high frequencies:

a(x)V(\lambda x)=\mathbb{P}_{\leq\frac{\lambda}{2}}(a)(x)V(\lambda x)+\mathbb{P}_{>\frac{\lambda}{2}}(a)(x)V(\lambda x).

(2.2)

Observe that the low frequency term in (
2.2
) can be dealt with in the following manner:

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{P}_{\leq\frac{\lambda}{2}}(a)V(\lambda\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{-2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\neq 0}|k|^{-4}\left|\left(\mathbb{P}_{\leq\frac{\lambda}{2}}(a)V(\lambda\cdot)\right)^{\wedge}(k)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{|k|\gtrsim\frac{\lambda}{2}}|k|^{-4}\left|\left(\mathbb{P}_{\leq\frac{\lambda}{2}}(a)V(\lambda\cdot)\right)^{\wedge}(k)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|k|\gtrsim\frac{\lambda}{2}}|k|^{-4}\|\mathbb{P}_{\leq\frac{\lambda}{2}}(a)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|V(\lambda\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|k|\gtrsim\frac{\lambda}{2}}|k|^{-4}\|a\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|V\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|k|\gtrsim\frac{\lambda}{2}}|k|^{-4}

where in the second equality we have used that the lowest frequency present in
V(\lambda\cdot)
is of order
\lambda
(since
\widehat{V}(0)=0
) and thus
(\mathbb{P}_{\leq\lambda/2}(a)V(\lambda\cdot))^{\wedge}(k)
is supported outside the ball of radius comparable to
\lambda/2
. Now upon sending
\lambda\to\infty
we see that this quantity goes to
0
. The high frequency term in (
2.2
) on the other hand may be dealt with as follows:

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{P}_{>\frac{\lambda}{2}}(a)V(\lambda\cdot)\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{-2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\neq 0}|k|^{-4}\left|\left(\mathbb{P}_{>\frac{\lambda}{2}}(a)V(\lambda\cdot)\right)^{\wedge}(k)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathbb{P}_{>\frac{\lambda}{2}}(a)V(\lambda\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\sum_{k\neq 0}|k|^{-4}

\displaystyle\simeq\|\mathbb{P}_{>\frac{\lambda}{2}}(a)V(\lambda\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}

Notice that

\|\mathbb{P}_{>\frac{\lambda}{2}}(a)V(\lambda\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|\mathbb{P}_{>\frac{\lambda}{2}}(a)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|V\|_{L^{\infty}}\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\quad\lambda\to\infty.

Indeed, by the dominated convergence theorem we have

\|\mathbb{P}_{>\frac{\lambda}{2}}(a)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\sum_{|k|\gtrsim\frac{\lambda}{2}}|\widehat{a}(k)|\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\quad\lambda\to\infty

since
\widehat{a}\in\ell^{1}
. Therefore,

\|\mathbb{P}_{>\frac{\lambda}{2}}(a)V(\lambda\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{-2}}\lesssim\|\mathbb{P}_{>\frac{\lambda}{2}}(a)V(\lambda\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}\longrightarrow 0\,,

and so in particular we get that

\|a(\cdot)V(\lambda\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{-2}}\to 0

as
\lambda\to\infty
.
∎

Lemma 2.7

(
High-higher-low products in negative Sobolev spaces
)
.

Let
\alpha
and
V
be smooth functions on the torus such that
V
is mean-zero, and let
\{\beta_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of smooth functions such that
\sup_{\lambda}\|\beta_{\lambda}\|_{L^{1}}<\infty
and
\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}\|\mathbb{P}_{>\lambda^{2}}\beta_{\lambda}\|_{L^{1}}\rightarrow 0
. Then we have

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\left\|\alpha(\cdot)\beta_{\lambda}(\cdot)V(\lambda^{6}\cdot)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-2}}=0.

Proof.

The result is a variant of the previous lemma, and so we only outline the main steps of the proof. We decompose the product as follows:

\displaystyle\alpha(x)\beta_{\lambda}(x)V(\lambda^{6}x)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{\leq\lambda}\alpha(x)\mathbb{P}_{\leq\lambda^{2}}\left(\beta_{\lambda}\right)(x)V(\lambda^{6}x)

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathbb{P}_{>\lambda}\alpha(x)\mathbb{P}_{\leq\lambda^{2}}\left(\beta_{\lambda}\right)(x)V(\lambda^{6}x)+\alpha(x)\mathbb{P}_{>\lambda^{2}}\left(\beta_{\lambda}\right)(x)V(\lambda^{6}x)\,.

(2.3)

The first term in (
2.3
) can now be estimated using Bernstein’s inequality and the uniform bound on
\|\beta_{\lambda}\|_{L^{1}}
by

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{P}_{\leq\lambda}\alpha(x)\mathbb{P}_{\leq\lambda^{2}}\left(\beta_{\lambda}\right)(x)V(\lambda^{6}x)\|_{\dot{H}^{-2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-6}\|\mathbb{P}_{\leq\lambda}\alpha(x)\mathbb{P}_{\leq\lambda^{2}}\left(\beta_{\lambda}\right)(x)V(\lambda^{6}x)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-6}\|V\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\alpha\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\mathbb{P}_{\leq\lambda^{2}}\beta_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-6}\|V\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\alpha\|_{L^{\infty}}\lambda^{2}\|\mathbb{P}_{\leq\lambda^{2}}\beta_{\lambda}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\rightarrow 0

as
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
. Meanwhile, for the second term we use that
L^{1}
embeds into
\dot{H}^{-2}
, the uniform
L^{1}
bound on
\beta_{\lambda}
, and the smoothness of
\alpha
. Finally, for the last term, we use the same embedding and the assumption that
\|\mathbb{P}_{>\lambda^{2}}\beta_{\lambda}\|_{L^{1}}\rightarrow 0
as
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
.
∎

We make the following inductive assumptions about

and

:

1.

(u_{q},R_{q})
solves (
1.2
).

2.

\fint_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}u_{q}dx=0
and

C>0

\|u_{q}\|_{L^{p(q)}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}>C^{-1}(1+2^{-q})\,,

p(q^{\prime})=2-2^{-q^{\prime}-10}

. In addition, we set
u_{-1}=0
and assume that for
q^{\prime}\leq q

\|u_{q^{\prime}}-u_{q^{\prime}-1}\|_{L^{p(q^{\prime})}}<2^{-q^{\prime}-2}\,

(3.1)

R_{q}
is

4.

For each
q^{\prime}\leq q

j
(depending on
q^{\prime}

\mathbb{P}_{2^{j}}(u_{q^{\prime}}-u_{q^{\prime}-1})=u_{q^{\prime}}-u_{q^{\prime}-1}

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\leq q-1\\

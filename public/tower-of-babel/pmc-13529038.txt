Hemorrhagic conjunctival mass
Authors: Madhumitha Duraisamy, R Jayagayathri, Supriya Chandrashekar, Josephine S Christy
Published: 2026

An 85-year-old male presented with a 5-day history of sudden-onset, painless swelling in the right eye, associated with profuse bleeding that began on the day of presentation. There was no history of trauma, prior ocular swelling, or discoloration. He had a known history of bilateral primary open-angle glaucoma for the past seven years and was on topical travoprost 0.004% once nightly. His most recent ophthalmic evaluation, performed 12 days earlier, included bilateral gonioscopy and had documented a normal ocular surface.

Visual acuity was perception of light in the right eye with inaccurate projection of rays. Swelling was 11 × 8 mm, chocolate brown colored, oval, lobulated, firm and nontender, hanging from the upper tarsal conjunctiva, indenting the lower lid margin with blood clot residues [Fig. 1a]. Swelling was bleeding on touch, preventing eyelid closure and obscuring all anterior segment details. Best-corrected visual acuity was 6/18 in the left eye with an immature cataract and a normal posterior segment.

B-scan ultrasound is essential to rule out a choroidal melanoma, the most common primary intraocular tumor, which typically presents with a characteristic dome-shaped or collar-button configuration. An MRI of the orbit is recommended to evaluate for any intraocular or orbital extension of the lesion.

Results

B-scan ultrasonography revealed normal posterior segments. Noncontrast MRI showed a well-defined, round T1 hyperintense, Short tau inversion recovery hypointense cystic, surface-occupying lesion, protruding from the preseptal area of the eyeball with no intraocular invasion or intraorbital spread [Fig. 1b and c]. Since the patient was on antiplatelet drugs, he was advised to stop oral antiplatelets 5 days before surgical intervention. Careful in-toto excision biopsy was performed with cautery to the base and primary closure with Vicryl sutures.

Histopathology examination revealed diffuse subepithelial proliferation of melanocytes, atypical large melanocytes with abundant cytoplasm, prominent nucleoli, and scattered mitosis with epithelioid morphology. HMB45 and S100 staining showed diffuse strong positivity suggestive of epithelioid malignant melanoma [Fig. 2a and b]. The patient was initiated on topical mitomycin C (MMC) 0.04% 4 times a day with punctal occlusion, administered in cycles of one week on and one week off, for a total of three cycles. He was referred for systemic evaluation to rule out metastasis. Positron Emission Tomography (PET)-CT showed no metabolically active lesions in the body.

(d) C-MIN III (Conjunctival minimum level of invasion) showing melanocytes invading beyond the epithelial basement membrane and deep into the substantia propria

At 11 months, a recurrent raised naevi-like lesion (4 × 3 mm) was noted in the superotemporal bulbar conjunctiva of the right eye [Fig. 2c]. MMC 0.04% QID was initiated again, and an excision biopsy was planned. Intraoperatively, additional two lesions (2 mm) were noted near the fornix. A wide margin (4 mm) excision was done with 0.02% MMC application and cryotherapy to the conjunctival margins. An amniotic membrane graft was placed and secured with Vicryl sutures. Histopathology report revealed intraepithelial melanocytosis with interspersed hyperchromatic and pleomorphic melanocytes involving the lower two-thirds of the epithelium and substantia propria, suggestive of malignant melanoma—conjunctival melanocytic intraepithelial neoplasia stage 3 (C-MIN-III) [Fig. 2d].

Discussion

This was an exclusive nodular conjunctival melanoma that arose from an overlooked primary acquired melanosis lesion. Only 5% of ocular melanomas are purely conjunctival.[1] Although melanomas more commonly affect cutaneous or uveal tissue, they should not be overlooked in conjunctival masses, particularly in elderly patients with pigmented lesions. In the present case, the sudden onset of a brownish swelling that bled on touch initially raised suspicion of a hematomatous cystic lesion, given its acute presentation. However, it is important to recognize that both nodular conjunctival melanoma and hemorrhagic cysts can exhibit overlapping MRI characteristics, such as T1 hyperintensity due to melanin in melanoma or blood products in cysts. This similarity underscores the necessity of correlating clinical features with imaging findings and obtaining histopathological confirmation for definitive diagnosis. Careful examination and documentation of any pre-existing conjunctival naevi is essential during routine anterior segment examinations. For any sudden-onset conjunctival mass, it is good to adhere to an in-toto excision using a “no-touch” technique with fresh sterile instruments at each step, followed by histopathological confirmation. Although rare, conjunctival melanoma (0.2% of all melanoma) should be suspected for all pigmented lesions and long-term follow-up is essential as it is a life-threatening tumor due to its high rate of recurrence, increased risk of metastasis, and associated mortality.[12] Factors linked to increased tumor recurrence include deep lesions, nonbulbar location, incomplete surgical excision, and epithelioid cell type melanomas.[3]

Photocurrent generation from spin dynamics in antiferromagnetic Dirac semimetals
Kohei Hattori, Hikaru Watanabe, Ryotaro Arita

Abstract
Using real-time simulations, we explore the photocurrent induced by localized spin dynamics in antiferromagnetic Dirac semimetals. These materials exhibit strong spin-charge coupling because the energy scales of the electronic and spin systems are of the same order. The photocurrent response exhibits a pronounced peak reflecting the presence of the Dirac point at the resonance frequency of the localized spin system. This contribution plays a crucial role in the photocurrent generation when the electronic filling is set around the Dirac nodes in the Dirac semimetal. We also reveal that the photocurrent response exhibits a low-frequency divergence due to the modulation of the resonance frequency of the localized spin system. Our findings clarify that the photocurrent response arising from the spin dynamics reflects the band topology of the Dirac semimetals, and the localized spin dynamics plays an important role in the photocurrent generation.



Introduction—

The photocurrent response is a second-order current response that converts an oscillating light field into a direct current (DC)
Belinicher and Sturman 1980
;
von Baltz and Kraut 1981
;
Sipe and Shkrebtii 2000
.
In topological semimetals, the property of the photocurrent response driven by the external electric field reflects the topology of the band structure
Nagaosa et al. 2020
;
Liu et al. 2020
.
In Weyl semimetals, the injection current induced by circularly polarized light in the low-frequency regime is shown to be quantized due to the presence of Weyl charges
de Juan et al. 2017
;
de Juan et al. 2020
.
Moreover, the amplitude of the photocurrent response in topological semimetals is proposed to exhibit a divergent behavior in the low-frequency regime
Chan et al. 2017
;
Ahn et al. 2020
;
Watanabe and Yanase 2021
;
Ikeda et al. 2023
;
Ikeda et al. 2024
.
In experiments, the large photocurrent generation is observed in topological semimetals in the low-frequency regime
Wu et al. 2017
;
Ma et al. 2019
;
Osterhoudt et al. 2019
.

In recent years, photocurrent generation driven by order parameter dynamics has attracted significant attention.
In the low-frequency regime, collective excitations of order parameters modify the photocurrent response.
The influence of order parameter dynamics on photocurrent response has been investigated through perturbative analysis
Morimoto and Nagaosa 2016
;
Morimoto and Nagaosa 2019
;
Morimoto et al. 2021
;
Toshio and Kawakami 2022
;
Morimoto and Nagaosa 2024
and real-time simulations
Chan et al. 2021
;
Kaneko et al. 2021
;
Iguchi et al. 2024
;
Hattori et al. 2025
theoretically.
Experimentally, photocurrent responses originating from order parameter dynamics have been observed in excitonic insulators
Sotome et al. 2019
;
Sotome et al. 2021
;
Nakamura et al. 2024
, ferroelectrics
Okamura et al. 2022
, and magnetic insulators
Ogino et al. 2024
.
Photocurrent generation driven by the order parameter dynamics in the low-frequency regime is expected to reflect the band topology in a topological semimetal.

On the other hand, there has been growing interest in electric control of antiferromagnetic order in light of spintronic applications
Jungwirth et al. 2016
;
Baltz et al. 2018
;
Manchon et al. 2019
.
In noncentrosymmetric AFMs, localized spin dynamics can be driven by an electric field through linear magnetoelectric effects and the Edelstein effect
Levitov et al. 1985
;
Edelstein 1990
;
Yanase 2014
;
Železný et al. 2014
.
Experimentally, electrical switching of AFM domains has been demonstrated in the AFM Dirac semimetal CuMnAs
Wadley et al. 2016
;
Godinho et al. 2018
.
In AFM Dirac semimetals, localized spin dynamics is expected to play a crucial role in photocurrent generation owing to the comparable energy scales of the electronic and localized spin systems.

In this work, we investigate the behavior of the photocurrent response, especially the injection current induced by localized spin dynamics in AFM Dirac semimetals. To calculate the photocurrent generation from the localized spin dynamics, we perform real-time simulations of the Kondo lattice model, following previous studies
Ono and Ishihara 2021
;
Ono and Akagi 2023
;
Iguchi et al. 2024
;
Hattori et al. 2024
;
Hattori et al. 2025
.
Using this approach, we analyze the chemical potential dependence (
\mu
-dependence) of the photocurrent conductivity and examine its behavior in the low-frequency regime.
We reveal that the photocurrent response arising from the localized spin dynamics exhibits a single peak reflecting the presence of the Dirac point, which is absent in the electric field-driven photocurrent.
We also find that the photocurrent generation from the spin dynamics shows divergent behavior in the low-frequency regime.
Our findings reveal that the photocurrent driven by the localized spin dynamics reflects the topology of the Dirac dispersion in the AFM Dirac semimetal.

Model and method—

We evaluate the photocurrent generation from localized spin dynamics in parity-time-reversal symmetric (
\mathcal{PT}
-symmetric) collinear AFM Dirac semimetal (see FIG.
1
(a)) by real-time simulation following Ref. cite hattori2025.
First, we consider the minimal model of the AFM Dirac semimetals on the two-dimensional square lattice introduced in Ref
Šmejkal et al. 2017
.
The schematic picture of the system is illustrated in FIG.
1
(b).
We can divide the Hamiltonian of this model into four parts as
\mathcal{H}=\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{ele}}+\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{exc}}+\mathcal{H}_{\text{mag}}+\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{E}
.
The first term,
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{ele}}=\sum_{\bm{k}}\bm{\hat{c}^{{\dagger}}}(\bm{k})\bm{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{ele}}(\bm{k})\bm{\hat{c}}(\bm{k})
represents the electronic Hamiltonian, where
\bm{\hat{c}}(\bm{k})=(\hat{c}_{A\uparrow}(\bm{k}),\hat{c}_{A\downarrow}(\bm{k}),\hat{c}_{B\uparrow}(\bm{k}),\hat{c}_{B\downarrow}(\bm{k}))^{T}
is a vector of annihilation operators of the electron labeled by sublattice index
\alpha
(
\alpha=A,B
), spin
\sigma
(
\sigma=\uparrow,\downarrow
), and wave vector
\bm{k}
.
The matrix of the Hamiltonian is expressed as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bm{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{ele}}(\bm{k})=-2t_{1}\tau^{x}\text{cos}\frac{k_{x}}{2}\text{cos}\frac{k_{y}}{2}-t_{2}(\text{cos}k_{x}+\text{cos}k_{y})\\
+\lambda\tau_{z}(\sigma_{y}\text{sin}k_{x}-\sigma_{x}\text{sin}k_{y}),\end{split}

(1)

where
t_{1}
(
t_{2}
) is the nearest-neighbor hopping (next-nearest-neighbor hopping), and
\lambda
is the staggard spin-orbit coupling.
Here, the Pauli matrices
\sigma
(
\tau
) represent the spin (sublattice) degree of freedom.
The second term,
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{exc}}=\sum_{\bm{k}}\bm{\hat{c}^{{\dagger}}}(\bm{k})\bm{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{exc}}(\bm{k})\bm{\hat{c}}(\bm{k})
, represents the interaction between the electronic system and localized spin system, where the matrix is expressed as

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{exc}}(\bm{k})=J\left[\frac{1+\tau_{z}}{2}\bm{\sigma}\cdot\bm{\text{S}}_{A}+\frac{1-\tau_{z}}{2}\bm{\sigma}\cdot\bm{\text{S}}_{B}\right].

(2)

Here,
J
represents the exchange coupling between the electronic spin moment
\bm{\sigma}_{\alpha}
and the localized spin moment
\bm{\text{S}}_{\alpha}
on the sublattice site
\alpha
.
The third term,
\mathcal{H}_{\text{spin}}=\sum_{\alpha=\text{A,B}}K_{x}(\text{S}_{\alpha}^{x})^{2}
, corresponds to the spin Hamiltonian, where
K_{x}
is the coefficient of the easy-axis anisotropy along the
x
-axis.
The last term
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{E}=-\bm{E}(t)\sum_{\bm{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\bm{\hat{c}^{{\dagger}}}(\bm{k})\quantity[i\pdv{\bm{k}}\delta(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})]\bm{\hat{c}}(\bm{k}^{\prime})
is the light-matter coupling in the length gauge
Aversa and Sipe 1995
, where
\bm{E}(t)
represents the external light field.

In this study, we consider the collinear AFM order
\bm{\text{S}}_{A}=-\bm{\text{S}}_{B}=(1,0,0)
and the electronic Hamiltonian
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{ele}}+\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{exc}}
can be diagonalized analytically as

\displaystyle\begin{split}E_{\bm{k}\pm}=-t_{2}(\text{cos}k_{x}+\text{cos}k_{y})\pm\left[4t_{1}^{2}\text{cos}^{2}\frac{k_{x}}{2}\text{cos}^{2}\frac{k_{y}}{2}\right.\\
\left.+(J-\lambda\text{sin}k_{y})^{2}+\lambda^{2}\text{sin}^{2}k_{x}\right]^{1/2}.\end{split}

(3)

Here, we set the parameters as
t_{1}=1.0,t_{2}=0.08,\lambda=0.8,J=0.6
and the Dirac points appear at
k
-points
\bm{\text{D}}_{1}=[\pi,\text{arccos}(J/\lambda)],\bm{\text{D}}_{2}=[\pi,\pi-\text{arccos}(J/\lambda)]
in the band diagram (FIG.
1
(c)).
We define the energy of the lower (upper) Dirac point by
\mu_{1}
(
\mu_{2}
).
These Dirac points are protected by the nonsymmorphic mirror symmetry
Šmejkal et al. 2017
;
Yang et al. 2017
.

To compute both electronic dynamics and spin dynamics, we solve the two-coupled equations of motion, the von Neumann equation and the Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert (LLG) equation.
For the electronic system, we solve the von Neumann equation
Yue and Gaarde 2022
for the single-particle density matrix (SPDM)
\rho_{\alpha\beta}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\bm{k})=\left\langle{\hat{c}_{\beta\sigma^{\prime}}^{\dagger}(\bm{k})\hat{c}_{\alpha\sigma}(\bm{k})}\right\rangle
expressed as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partialderivative{\vb*{\rho}(\bm{k}, t)}{t}=-i\quantity[\vb*{H}(\bm{k},t),\, \vb*{\rho}(\bm{k}, t)]-\bm{E}(t)\cdot\frac{\partial{\bf\it\rho}(\bm{k},t)}{\partial\bm{k}}\\
-\frac{1}{\tau}({\bf\it\rho}(\bm{k},t)-{\bf\it\rho}_{\text{eq}}(\bm{k})).\end{split}

(4)

Here,
\tau
is the relaxation time of the electronic system and
{\bf\it H}(\bm{k},t)
is the time-dependent electronic Hamiltonian at each
\bm{k}
point defined as
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathrm{ele}}+\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathrm{exc}}=\sum_{\bm{k}}\bm{\hat{c}^{{\dagger}}}(\bm{k}){\bf\it H}(\bm{k},t)\bm{\hat{c}}(\bm{k})
.

On the other hand, we solve the LLG equation for the localized spin moment
\bm{\text{S}}_{\alpha}
described as

\displaystyle\dfrac{d\bm{\mathrm{S}}_{\alpha}}{dt}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{1+\alpha_{G}^{2}}\left(\bm{\mathrm{h}}_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{eff}}\times\bm{\mathrm{S}}_{\alpha}+\alpha_{G}\bm{\mathrm{S}}_{\alpha}\times\left(\bm{\mathrm{S}}_{\alpha}\times\bm{\mathrm{h}}_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{eff}}\right)\right),

(5)

where
\bm{\mathrm{h}}_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{eff}}=-J\langle\bm{\sigma}_{\alpha}\rangle+\dfrac{\delta\mathcal{H}_{\text{spin}}}{\delta\bm{\mathrm{S}}_{\alpha}}
is the effective magnetic field on the localized spin moment on the sublattice
\alpha
and
\alpha_{G}
is the Gilbert damping coefficient.
In the following calculation, we set the
k
-mesh of the Brillouin zone to
1000\times 1000
, and use
\tau=100
,
\alpha_{G}=0.01
, and inverse temperature
\beta=500.0
.

Response functions—

Here, we discuss response functions, specifically linear electromagnetic susceptibility and photocurrent conductivity.
In this section, we use
\mu=\mu_{2}+0.05
and
K_{x}=0.1
.
For this parameter, the peak structure of the photocurrent conductivity arising from the electric field does not appear at the resonance frequency of the localized spin system.
First, we calculate the linear electromagnetic susceptibility of the localized spins.
We define the symmetry-adapted basis for the localized spin system as
\mathrm{M}^{a}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\quantity(\mathrm{S}_{A}^{a}(t) + \mathrm{S}_{B}^{a}(t)),\mathrm{L}^{a}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\quantity(\mathrm{S}_{A}^{a}(t) - \mathrm{S}_{B}^{a}(t))
, where
\mathrm{M}^{a}(t)
represents the ferroic magnetization and
\mathrm{L}^{a}(t)
means the staggered spin moments. For these symmetry-adapted bases, we calculate the linear electromagnetic susceptibility defined as

\displaystyle{\chi}_{\text{S}^{\mu}E^{\nu}}(\omega)=\frac{\Delta\text{S}^{\mu}(\omega)}{E^{\nu}(\omega)},

(6)

where
\Delta\text{S}^{\mu}(\omega)
is the Fourier component of the modulation of the symmetry-adapted basis
\Delta\text{M}^{a}(t)=\text{M}^{a}(t)-\text{M}^{a}(0),\Delta\text{L}^{a}(t)=\text{L}^{a}(t)-\text{L}^{a}(0)
, and
E^{\nu}(\omega)
is the Fourier component of the external light field along the
\nu
-axis.
Considering the symmetry analysis of the symmetry-adapted basis in Supplemental Materials (SM)
sup
, only
\text{L}^{y}
and
\text{M}^{z}
are optically active in the linear response regime.
We present the frequency dependence of the linear electromagnetic susceptibility for the symmetry-adapted bases
\text{L}^{y}
and
\text{M}^{z}
in FIG.

The electromagnetic susceptibilities
\chi_{\text{L}^{y}E^{x}}(\omega_{\text{p}})
and
\chi_{\text{M}^{z}E^{x}}(\omega_{\text{p}})

Next, we discuss the transverse photocurrent response described as

\displaystyle J^{y}(\omega=0;\omega_{\text{p}})

\displaystyle=\sigma^{yxx}(0;-\omega_{\text{p}},\omega_{\text{p}})E^{x}(-\omega_{\text{p}})E^{x}(\omega_{\text{p}})

\displaystyle+\sigma^{yxx}(0;\omega_{\text{p}},-\omega_{\text{p}})E^{x}(\omega_{\text{p}})E^{x}(-\omega_{\text{p}}),

(7)

We define the photocurrent conductivity as
\sigma^{yxx}(0;\omega_{\text{p}})=(\sigma^{yxx}(0;-\omega_{\text{p}},\omega_{\text{p}})+\sigma^{yxx}(0;\omega_{\text{p}},-\omega_{\text{p}}))/2

We discuss the
\mu
-dependence of the photocurrent response in the Dirac semimetal.
Let us focus on the injection current term involved in the photocurrent conductivity.
The formula for the injection current is given by

\displaystyle J_{XY;\text{Inj}}^{\mu;\nu\lambda}(0,\omega_{\text{p}})

\displaystyle=\sigma_{XY;\text{Inj}}^{\mu;\nu\lambda}(0;-\omega_{\text{p}},\omega_{\text{p}})X^{\nu}(-\omega_{\text{p}})Y^{\lambda}(\omega_{\text{p}})

\displaystyle+\sigma_{XY;\text{Inj}}^{\mu;\nu\lambda}(0;\omega_{\text{p}},-\omega_{\text{p}})X^{\nu}(\omega_{\text{p}})Y^{\lambda}(-\omega_{\text{p}})

(8)

where

When

is the localized spin dynamics driven by the external light field, we can express the localized spin dynamics as

\displaystyle X^{\mu}(\omega_{\text{p}})=\chi_{X^{\mu}E^{\nu}}(\omega_{\text{p}})E^{\nu}(\omega_{\text{p}}),

(9)

where
\chi_{\text{X}^{\mu}E^{\nu}}(\omega_{\text{p}})

The general formula for the injection current conductivity

\displaystyle\sigma_{XY;\text{Inj}}^{\mu;\nu\lambda}(0;-\omega_{\text{p}},\omega_{\text{p}})

\displaystyle=\pi\tau\int\dfrac{d{\bf\it k}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\sum_{a\neq b}\Delta_{ab}^{\mu}X^{\nu}_{ab}Y^{\lambda}_{ba}f_{ab}\delta(\omega_{\text{p}}-\epsilon_{ba}),

\bm{\sigma}_{ab}

Here, we discuss the low-frequency divergence of the photocurrent response arising from the localized spin dynamics.
In the conventional theory
Ahn et al. 2020
;
Watanabe and Yanase 2021
, the low-frequency divergence of the photocurrent occurs as the chemical potential approaches the Dirac point.
In contrast, the low-frequency divergence of photocurrent generation driven by the spin dynamics arises from tuning the resonance frequency of the localized spin system.
In this model, we can modify the resonance frequency of the localized spin system by changing the value of the easy-axis anisotropy
K_{x}
.
We note that the electronic band structure does not depend on
K_{x}
because the magnetic anisotropy along the
x
-axis does not modulate the localized spin configuration.

We plot the behavior of the photocurrent conductivity
\sigma_{\text{w}}(0;\omega_{\text{p}})-\sigma_{\text{wo}}(0;\omega_{\text{p}})
under the change of the resonance frequency in FIG.
4
(a).
In this calculation, we set the chemical potential as
\mu=\mu_{2}
corresponding to the energy level at the upper Dirac point to discuss the resonance frequency dependence of the photocurrent generation from the localized spin dynamics above the optical gap of the electronic system.
In FIG.
4
(a), the peak structure of the photocurrent conductivity
\sigma_{\text{w}}(0;\omega_{\text{p}})-\sigma_{\text{wo}}(0;\omega_{\text{p}})
shifts to a lower frequency as the magnetic anisotropy
K_{x}
gets smaller.
The amplitude of the peaks of
\sigma_{\text{w}}(0;\omega_{\text{p}})-\sigma_{\text{wo}}(0;\omega_{\text{p}})
exhibit the divergent behavior in the low-frequency regime.

Here, we discuss the low-frequency divergence of the injection current by counting the power of the photon frequency.
Since the
\delta
-function has dimension of
\omega_{\text{p}}^{-1}
,
\Delta_{ab}^{\mu}
has dimension of
\omega_{\text{p}}/k
,
\xi_{ab}^{\mu}
has dimension of

, and
\sigma_{ab}^{\mu}
has no dimension, the scaling behavior of the injection current conductivities in Eq. (
Photocurrent generation from spin dynamics in antiferromagnetic Dirac semimetals
) is given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{EE;\text{Inj}}^{\mu;\nu\lambda}\sim\frac{\tau}{\omega_{\text{p}}},\sigma_{\text{S}E;\text{Inj}}^{\mu;\nu\lambda}\sim\tau,\sigma_{\text{S}\text{S};\text{Inj}}^{\mu;\nu\lambda}\sim\tau\omega_{\text{p}}^{1},

(11)

where
\sigma_{EE;\text{Inj}}^{\mu;\nu\lambda}
is the injection current originating from the electric field,
\sigma_{\text{S}E;\text{Inj}}^{\mu;\nu\lambda}
comes from the interference effect between the electric field and the localized spin dynamics, and
\sigma_{\text{S}\text{S};\text{Inj}}^{\mu;\nu\lambda}
is generated from the localized spin dynamics.
Here, we use

owing to the linear dispersion of the Dirac electrons.
This result of the power-counting analysis is consistent with the analytical expression of the injection current in SM

.
Consequently, the injection current originating from the localized spin dynamics in Eq. (

) does not diverge in the low-frequency regime.

We show the

The amplitude of the electromagnetic susceptibility gets larger as the resonance frequency shifts to a lower value.
Even though the injection current conductivity

in Eq. (

Summary—

In this study, we investigated the photocurrent generation from spin dynamics in AFM Dirac semimetals.
First, we found that the photocurrent response is significantly enhanced by the localized spin dynamics due to the comparable energy scales of the electronic and localized spin systems.
Second, we examined the
\mu
-dependence of the photocurrent response. The photocurrent induced by localized spin dynamics exhibits distinct behaviors reflecting the presence of the Dirac point, which results in a sizable photocurrent generation when the electronic filling is set around the Dirac points.
Finally, we discussed the low-frequency divergence of the photocurrent response driven by spin dynamics.
We revealed that the photocurrent arising from spin dynamics diverges as the resonance frequency of the localized spin system shifts to a lower value.

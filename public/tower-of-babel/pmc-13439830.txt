Observed changes in clinical parameters and healthcare costs among patients enrolled in a diabetes management program in Brazil: a real-world study
Authors: Rodolfo Pires de Albuquerque, Ricardo Bezerra Walraven, José Arnaldo Shiomi da Cruz, Rodrigo Afonso da Silva Sardenberg
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionIn Brazil, Hapvida NotreDame, the largest supplementary health system in the country, implemented the Viver Bem Program (VBP), a structured chronic care initiative designed to support patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) in routine clinical practice.ObjectiveTo evaluate longitudinal changes in clinical, laboratory, and healthcare utilization parameters among patients with T2DM enrolled in the VBP.MethodsThis retrospective single-arm longitudinal real-world study analyzed data from adult patients (≥ 18 years) with laboratory-confirmed T2DM (fasting plasma glucose ≥ 200 mg/dL or glycated hemoglobin ≥ 6.5%) enrolled in the VBP between 2020 and 2023. Data were extracted from integrated administrative and clinical information systems and analyzed in a pseudonymized format. Anthropometric, hemodynamic, and laboratory parameters were assessed at baseline and at the last available follow-up. Multivariable linear regression was used to explore factors associated with observed changes. Healthcare costs were compared with a non-enrolled control group meeting the same eligibility criteria.ResultsA total of 23,516 patients were included, with a mean follow-up of 20.6 months. Statistically significant within-group changes were observed in weight, abdominal circumference, body mass index, blood pressure, glycated hemoglobin, lipid profile, and serum creatinine (all p < 0.001), although absolute magnitudes were small. In multivariable analyses, age and male sex were consistently associated with anthropometric changes, while longer program participation was associated with reduced abdominal circumference. Model explanatory power was limited, with low R² values across outcomes. In cost analyses, VBP participants had higher outpatient consultation costs but lower costs related to emergency room visits, exams, treatments, and small procedures, while hospitalization costs were similar between groups.ConclusionEnrollment in the Viver Bem Program was associated with modest but statistically significant within-individual changes in intermediate clinical and laboratory parameters, as well as differences in healthcare utilization patterns. These findings support the feasibility of integrated chronic care programs in large private health systems, although causal inferences regarding clinical benefit and cost-effectiveness cannot be established.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12902-026-02287-x.

Introduction

Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) is one of the most prevalent chronic diseases worldwide, affecting millions of people. According to recent data, the global prevalence of diabetes has been rising rapidly, driven by population aging, urbanization, physical inactivity, and unhealthy dietary patterns. In 2019, the International Diabetes Federation estimated that approximately 463 million adults are living with diabetes, a number projected to reach 700 million by 2045 [1].

The impact of diabetes extends beyond its direct metabolic consequences. T2DM predisposes individuals to cardiovascular disease, kidney failure, blindness, and amputations, and places a substantial burden on healthcare systems due to long-term treatment needs and productivity losses. It is estimated that the global economic burden of diabetes reaches approximately USD 760 billion annually [2].

Diabetes is also among the leading causes of death worldwide. When deaths attributable to cardiovascular and renal complications associated with poor glycemic control are considered, the overall mortality burden is substantially higher. Microvascular complications, including diabetic retinopathy, nephropathy, and neuropathy, significantly affect patients’ quality of life and contribute to functional limitations and long-term dependency [3].

The burden of T2DM is disproportionately higher in low- and middle-income countries, where rapid nutritional transitions, urbanization, and rising obesity rates contribute to increasing disease prevalence. In these settings, limited access to structured healthcare services exacerbates suboptimal disease control and is associated with higher rates of complications and premature mortality [4].

In response to this ongoing public health challenge, global strategies emphasize the promotion of healthy lifestyles, early detection, and structured disease management programs. Strengthening healthcare systems to deliver coordinated and continuous diabetes care is essential to mitigate both clinical and economic consequences [5]. Recent studies suggest that structured prevention and management programs are associated with lower rates of diabetes-related complications and reduced healthcare utilization [6].

Viver Bem program

The Viver Bem Program (VBP), developed by Hapvida NotreDame, is a structured chronic care initiative designed to support patients with type 2 diabetes within routine clinical practice. The program focuses on longitudinal follow-up and patient support, aiming to improve glycemic control and facilitate early identification of clinical deterioration.

Eligibility for the program is based on age ≥ 18 years and laboratory evidence of diabetes, defined as fasting plasma glucose ≥ 200 mg/dL or glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c) ≥ 6.5%. Eligible patients are proactively contacted through the health operator’s call center, where enrollment is completed and an initial nursing consultation is scheduled.

The VBP is organized around key components of personalized care. Continuous clinical monitoring is performed by a multidisciplinary team, including physicians, nurses, nutritionists, and other allied health professionals, allowing timely identification of changes in clinical status. Teleconsultations and remote follow-up facilitate ongoing communication between patients and healthcare professionals, improving access to care and continuity of monitoring.

Health education constitutes a central element of the program, providing patients with guidance on nutrition, physical activity, and preventive strategies, thereby promoting active engagement in self-management. In addition, structured workflows enable timely clinical interventions when laboratory abnormalities or clinical concerns are identified. Psychosocial support is also incorporated to address behavioral and emotional factors relevant to long-term disease management.

Together, these integrated components aim to support glycemic control and self-management in real-world clinical settings, rather than to establish causal clinical efficacy.

Real-world evidence (RWE)

Healthcare is increasingly informed by evidence generated outside controlled clinical trials. According to the U.S. Food and Drug Administration, real-world data (RWD) and real-world evidence (RWE) play an increasingly important role in healthcare decision-making. RWE refers to clinical evidence regarding the use, benefits, and risks of medical interventions derived from the analysis of RWD, including data from electronic health records, administrative claims, and observational studies [7].

RWE can be generated through a variety of study designs, including pragmatic trials and observational studies, both prospective and retrospective. When appropriately analyzed, RWE complements randomized clinical trials by capturing heterogeneous populations and reflecting routine clinical practice. However, its use requires careful methodological considerations to ensure transparency, validity, and appropriate interpretation [8].

Study objectives

The primary objective of this study was to evaluate longitudinal changes in glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c) among patients with type 2 diabetes enrolled in the Viver Bem Program.

Secondary objectives included assessing changes in anthropometric, hemodynamic, and laboratory parameters, identifying factors associated with greater observed improvements, and comparing healthcare utilization costs between patients enrolled and not enrolled in the VBP.

Materials and methods

Study design and setting

This was a retrospective single-arm longitudinal (pre–post) real-world study conducted within the Hapvida NotreDame healthcare system, the largest private health network in Brazil, which covered over 8.8 million beneficiaries in 2023. The study aimed to describe longitudinal changes in clinical parameters and healthcare utilization among patients enrolled in the Viver Bem Program (VBP), a chronic care management initiative for patients with T2DM. The observation period spanned from January 1, 2020, to December 31, 2023.

Data sources

All data were obtained from Hapvida’s integrated digital platforms, which included hospital and administrative electronic records covering clinical encounters, laboratory tests, and care utilization. Data were accessed and analyzed in a pseudonymized format exclusively by authorized members of the research team. The study was approved by the Institutional Research Ethics Committee and by the National Research Ethics Commission (CONEP), and was conducted in accordance with the ethical standards outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. The need for informed consent was waived by the Institutional Research Ethics Committee due to the retrospective nature of the study and the use of anonymized secondary data. Data were extracted in pseudonymized form and analyzed exclusively by members of the research team authorized for secondary data use, in accordance with ethical and legal standards.

Outpatient consultations are medical appointments that occur outside of hospital admissions, typically in clinics or ambulatory settings. ER visits involve urgent care provided in emergency departments for acute medical conditions. Exams encompass all laboratory and imaging studies, ranging from basic radiographies to advanced magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) scans. Treatments include therapeutic interventions such as medication administration, hormone therapy, chemotherapy, and immunotherapy, as well as other procedures recorded in the administrative database, including vasectomy and intrauterine device (IUD) placement or removal. Small procedures are minor medical interventions often performed in outpatient settings, including superficial biopsies, abscess drainages, minor skin surgeries, and simple endoscopic procedures. Hospitalizations refer to periods when patients are admitted to the hospital for treatments or recovery that cannot be managed in an outpatient setting.

The cost analysis was designed to capture overall healthcare utilization rather than diabetes-specific expenditures alone. Monetary correction for inflation was performed using the IPCA index (Broad Consumer Price Index), and all costs were adjusted to 2025 values.

A comparison group was constructed for cost analyses only, consisting of patients with available cost information who met the VBP eligibility criteria but were not enrolled in the program.

Statistical analysis

All variables were extracted and organized in a secure analytical environment. Descriptive statistics were used to summarize the characteristics of the cohort. Variables with normal distribution were expressed as means and standard deviations, while variables with non-normal distribution were expressed as medians and interquartile ranges.

Comparisons between baseline and follow-up measurements within individuals were performed using paired t-tests for normally distributed variables and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests for non-normally distributed variables. Follow-up values corresponded to the last available measurement during program participation, rather than a fixed 12-month time point.

Analyses were conducted using a complete-case approach on a per-variable basis, and differences in sample size across variables reflect missing data inherent to routine clinical practice.

Univariate linear regression analyses were performed to evaluate associations between demographic or utilization variables (e.g., age, sex, number of consultations) and changes in clinical parameters. Variables with p ≤ 0.05 in univariate models were included in multivariable regression analyses to identify factors associated with greater observed changes. Potential collinearity among predictors was assessed prior to multivariable modeling and no relevant collinearity issues were identified. Statistical significance was defined as p ≤ 0.05.

All analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, version 30.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

Results

Between 2017 and 2023, a total of 23,516 patients were enrolled in the Viver Bem Program, of whom 12,839 (54.6%) were female and 10,677 (45.4%) were male. The number of new participants increased steadily over the years, with the largest number of inclusions observed in 2022 (Table 1). The mean duration of follow-up within the program was 20.62 months (SD = 17.7). Patients attended a mean of 4.42 outpatient consultations with physicians and 0.84 consultations with nutritionists (Table 2).

Regarding clinical parameters, statistically significant within-group changes were observed across multiple anthropometric and metabolic outcomes. The average patient weight decreased from 81.17 kg (SD = 24.13) at baseline to 79.66 kg (SD = 17.76) at follow-up (mean difference − 1.51 kg; p < 0.001). Abdominal circumference declined from 102.63 cm (SD = 13.88) to 101.59 cm (SD = 16.88) (mean difference − 1.04 cm; p < 0.001). BMI showed a small reduction from 30.38 kg/m² (SD = 5.69) to 30.27 kg/m² (SD = 5.64) (mean difference − 0.11 kg/m²; p < 0.001).

Systolic blood pressure decreased from 133.24 mmHg (SD = 19.24) to 131.51 mmHg (SD = 18.13) (mean difference − 1.73 mmHg), and diastolic blood pressure decreased from 82.98 mmHg (SD = 12.20) to 80.66 mmHg (SD = 11.36) (mean difference − 2.32 mmHg), with both changes reaching statistical significance (p < 0.001).

Metabolic parameters also showed numerical and statistically significant changes. Glycated hemoglobin decreased from 7.83% (SD = 4.93) to 7.45% (SD = 4.91) (mean difference − 0.38%; p < 0.001). HDL cholesterol increased from 45.24 mg/dL (SD = 12.00) to 46.11 mg/dL (SD = 12.58) (mean difference + 0.87 mg/dL), while LDL cholesterol decreased from 107.00 mg/dL (SD = 38.65) to 106.38 mg/dL (SD = 40.66) (mean difference − 0.62 mg/dL), both with p < 0.001. Serum creatinine decreased from 1.10 mg/dL (SD = 9.58) to 1.07 mg/dL (SD = 5.92) (mean difference − 0.03 mg/dL; p < 0.001).

Regression analyses indicated that age and sex were the most consistent variables associated with observed changes in anthropometric outcomes (Table 3). In multivariable models, age showed small inverse associations with weight (β = −0.038; p = 0.001), abdominal circumference (β = −0.032; p = 0.003), and BMI (β = −0.006; p < 0.001). Male sex was associated with higher weight (β = 0.865; p = 0.002), abdominal circumference (β = 0.743; p = 0.006), and BMI (β = 0.148; p = 0.001). Additionally, longer time enrolled in the program was associated with a reduction in abdominal circumference (β = −0.059; p < 0.001).

Discussion

This real-world study found that enrollment in the Viver Bem Program was associated with statistically significant, though modest, within-group changes in anthropometric, hemodynamic, and metabolic parameters among patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM). Over an average follow-up period of 20.6 months, patients showed numerical reductions in weight, abdominal circumference, blood pressure, glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c), and LDL cholesterol, along with small increases in HDL cholesterol. These observations are consistent with prior real-world and pragmatic studies suggesting that structured lifestyle interventions and patient-centered chronic care models are associated with improvements in intermediate metabolic parameters [6, 9].

The mean HbA1c reduction, although small in absolute terms (from 7.83% to 7.45%), was statistically significant but should be interpreted cautiously. While landmark trials such as the United Kingdom Prospective Diabetes Study (UKPDS) demonstrated that a 1% reduction in HbA1c is associated with meaningful reductions in diabetes-related morbidity and mortality, the magnitude of HbA1c change observed in the present study was substantially smaller and does not allow direct inference of comparable clinical benefit [10]. Therefore, the findings should be interpreted as population-level numerical changes rather than evidence of clinically meaningful benefit at the individual level.

In addition to glycemic parameters, small numerical reductions in systolic and diastolic blood pressure were observed. Blood pressure is a key determinant of macrovascular risk in patients with T2DM; however, the absolute changes observed in this cohort were modest. Prior population-based modeling studies have suggested that small average reductions in systolic blood pressure may translate into reductions in cardiovascular risk at the population level [11]. In the present analysis, these findings should be viewed as supportive of potential population-level relevance rather than as evidence of direct cardiovascular risk reduction.

Regression analyses identified age and sex as variables associated with observed changes in anthropometric outcomes. Younger patients and men exhibited greater numerical reductions in weight and BMI, findings that are consistent with previous observational studies suggesting that demographic and behavioral factors influence response to diabetes management interventions [12]. Nevertheless, the low R² values across all models indicate that the explanatory power of the evaluated predictors was limited, underscoring the multifactorial nature of diabetes outcomes and the influence of unmeasured factors such as medication adherence, physical activity, dietary patterns, and socioeconomic context.

The lack of consistent associations between the frequency of consultations and outcome changes highlights the complexity of healthcare delivery in chronic disease management. While access to care is necessary, the quality, continuity, and content of clinical interactions—as well as patient engagement—are likely more relevant determinants of behavioral change than service volume alone [13]. These findings support the need for future research focusing on qualitative aspects of care delivery.

The use of real-world data (RWD) allowed evaluation of the VBP within routine clinical practice, reflecting heterogeneous patient populations and real-world care pathways. When rigorously analyzed, real-world evidence (RWE) complements randomized clinical trials by providing insights into effectiveness and utilization patterns under routine conditions, while also requiring cautious interpretation due to inherent methodological limitations [14].

Regarding healthcare costs, patients enrolled in the VBP showed lower median annual expenditures in several outpatient-related categories, including treatments, exams, and emergency room visits, compared with non-enrolled patients. In contrast, hospitalization costs were similar between groups. These findings suggest an association between program enrollment and differences in healthcare utilization patterns, particularly in outpatient and procedural settings, while inpatient costs appeared unaffected.

The observed cost patterns are consistent with prior studies evaluating diabetes prevention and management programs. For example, evaluations of the YMCA Diabetes Prevention Program reported reductions in healthcare spending primarily driven by fewer emergency department visits and inpatient admissions [15]. Similarly, digital diabetes prevention programs have demonstrated favorable cost-effectiveness profiles in selected settings [16], and systematic reviews have concluded that many diabetes prevention interventions are cost-effective or cost-saving [17]. However, direct comparisons should be made cautiously given differences in healthcare systems, cost structures, and study designs.

Several limitations must be acknowledged. First, the retrospective and observational design precludes causal inference and is subject to selection bias and residual confounding. Second, the use of routinely collected administrative and clinical data may introduce measurement error and missing data, which varied across variables and time points. Third, information on pharmacological treatments, medication adherence, lifestyle behaviors, and socioeconomic factors was not consistently available and could not be incorporated into the analyses. Fourth, while a comparator group was available for cost analyses, no clinical comparator group was available for metabolic outcomes, limiting interpretation to within-group associations over time. Finally, the generalizability of the findings may be limited to populations covered by private health insurance in Brazil.

Future studies should prioritize prospective designs, ideally with randomized or well-matched comparator groups, to further evaluate the clinical and economic implications of structured diabetes care programs. Incorporating patient-reported outcomes and qualitative assessments may provide additional insight into mechanisms of engagement and adherence. Within these limitations, the present findings contribute to the growing body of real-world evidence describing observed clinical and economic patterns associated with integrated chronic care programs in routine practice.

Conclusion

In this real-world retrospective single-arm longitudinal study, enrollment in the Viver Bem Program was associated with modest, statistically significant changes in clinical and laboratory parameters among patients with type 2 diabetes. The program’s emphasis on personalized monitoring, education, psychosocial support, and remote care delivery was associated with observed numerical changes in intermediate metabolic and hemodynamic outcomes, rather than proven clinical benefit. Given the observational design and absence of a clinical comparator group, these findings should be interpreted as within-group associations over time.

From an economic perspective, participation in the program was associated with differences in healthcare utilization patterns, including lower costs related to treatments, exams, and emergency room visits, while hospitalization costs remained similar between groups. These findings suggest potential efficiency gains in outpatient and procedural care, although causal inference cannot be established.

Overall, the results support the feasibility of implementing integrated chronic care programs in large private health systems, particularly in middle-income countries such as Brazil. Further prospective studies, ideally with comparator groups and longer follow-up, are needed to clarify the clinical relevance, cost-effectiveness, and patient-centered outcomes associated with such interventions.

An even simpler hard variant of Not-All-Equal 3-SAT
Andreas Darmann, Janosch Döcker, Britta Dorn

Abstract
We show that Not-All-Equal 3-Sat remains NP -complete when restricted to instances that simultaneously satisfy the following properties: (i) The clauses are given as the disjoint union of k k partitions, for any fixed k ≥ 4 k\geq 4 , of the variable set into subsets of size 3, and (ii) each pair of distinct clauses shares at most one variable. Property (i) implies that each variable appears in exactly k k clauses and each clause consists of exactly 3 unnegated variables. Therewith, we improve upon our earlier result (Darmann and Döcker, 2020). Complementing the hardness result for at least 4 4 partitions, we show that for k ≤ 3 k\leq 3 the corresponding decision problem is in P . In particular, for k ∈ { 1 , 2 } k\in\{1,2\} , all instances that satisfy Property (i) are nae-satisfiable. By the well-known correspondence between Not-All-Equal 3-Sat and hypergraph coloring, we obtain the following corollary of our results: For k ≥ 4 k\geq 4 , Bicolorability is NP -complete for linear 3-uniform k k -regular hypergraphs even if the edges are given as a decomposition into k k perfect matchings; with the same restrictions, for k ≤ 3 k\leq 3 Bicolorability is in P , and for k ∈ { 1 , 2 } k\in\{1,2\} all such hypergraphs are bicolorable. Finally, we deduce from a construction in the work by Pilz (Pilz, 2019) that every instance of Positive Planar Not-All-Equal Sat with at least three distinct variables per clause is nae-satisfiable. Hence, when restricted to instances with a planar incidence graph, each of the above variants of Not-All-Equal 3-Sat turns into a trivial decision problem.

Introduction

Not-All-Equal 3-Sat
is a notable variant of the classic
3-Sat
problem and frequently used as a base problem for polynomial-time reductions. Its
NP
-completeness has been shown by Schaefer
[
Sch78
]
in the setting where each clause consists of at most three literals—in fact, he proves a more general result, known today as
Schaefer’s dichotomy theorem
, that implies
NP
-hardness of several interesting variants of the generalized Boolean satisfiability problem.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a collection of clauses formed over a set of variables
V
. In an instance of
Not-All-Equal 3-Sat
, each clause consists of exactly three distinct literals, and the question is: Can we assign truth values to the variables in such a way that, for each clause, not all literals have the same truth value? In contrast to
3
-Sat
, the decision problem corresponding to
Not-All-Equal 3-Sat
does not turn into a trivial problem in the absence of negations. Indeed, referring to this variant as
Positive Not-All-Equal 3-Sat
, it is known that
Positive Not-All-Equal 3-Sat
remains
NP
-complete even if both (i) the collection of clauses is
linear
, that is, each pair of distinct clauses shares at most one variable and (ii) each variable appears in exactly four clauses. This result was shown by Darmann and Döcker
[
DD20
]
, earlier work by Porschen et al.
[
PSSW14
]
obtained
NP
-completeness of
Positive Not-All-Equal 3-Sat
for linear instances without the variable bound in (ii). The bound stated in (ii) is, in fact, the best possible one as
Positive Not-All-Equal 3-Sat
can be solved in polynomial time if each variable appears in three clauses or less (see Filho
[
Fil19
, Thm. 3.3.2]
).
However, if the condition on the clause size is relaxed to additionally allow clauses with two variables,
Positive Not-All-Equal Sat
remains
NP
-complete if each variable appears in at most three clauses as shown by Kratochvíl and Tuza
[
KT02
, Thm. 2.1]
. Dehghan et al.
[
DSA15
]
showed that the same

Let us turn to the variant of

First, we partition

The collection

By construction,

is an instance of

\displaystyle:=\{\{a,\,d,\,g\},\,\{b,\,e,\,i\},\,\{c,\,f,\,h\}\},

\displaystyle C_{3}

\displaystyle:=\{\{a,\,e,\,h\},\,\{b,\,f,\,g\},\,\{c,\,d,\,i\}\},

\displaystyle C_{4}

\displaystyle:=\{\{a,\,f,\,i\},\,\{b,\,d,\,h\},\,\{c,\,e,\,g\}\}.

We obtain an instance of

in which each variable appears exactly four times by taking the union of

,

,

is a no-instance (see Proposition

We show in this article that, for any fixed

,

remains

-complete if restricted to instances with a variable set

and a collection of clauses
\mathcal{C}
that satisfies both of the following properties:

(i)

\mathcal{C}
is the disjoint union of

Since each variable appears exactly

]
. In contrast, for

It is well known that

is equivalent to

for hypergraphs with edge size at most 3 can be decided in polynomial time. However, there are also families of hypergraphs with bounded matching number for which

is

-complete; see the dichotomies obtained by Li and Spirkl
[
LS23

.

This work is structured as follows. In Section

are presented in Section

3

5
).

2
Preliminaries

Let
V:=\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\}
be a set of
variables
. A
literal
is a variable
x_{i}
or its negation
\overline{x_{i}}
; the set of literals corresponding to
V
is
\mathcal{L}_{V}=\{x_{i},\overline{x_{i}}\mid 1\leq i\leq n\}
. A
clause
is a subset of
\mathcal{L}_{V}
and is called
positive
if it is a subset of
V
. If a clause contains exactly
k
distinct literals, we call it a
k
-clause
. A
collection of clauses
, also called a
formula
, is a set
\mathcal{C}:=\{c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{m}\}
of clauses. A collection of clauses is
linear
if and only if each pair of distinct clauses shares at most one variable. A
truth assignment
is a mapping
\beta\colon V\rightarrow\{T,F\}
, where
T
and
F
represent the truth values true and false. We say that a truth assignment
\beta^{\prime}\colon V^{\prime}\rightarrow\{T,F\}

extends
a truth assignment
\beta\colon V\rightarrow\{T,F\}
if and only if
V\subseteq V^{\prime}
and
\beta^{\prime}(x_{i})=\beta(x_{i})
for each
x_{i}\in V
. A truth assignment
\beta

satisfies
a literal
x_{i}
if
\beta(x_{i})=T
and a literal
\overline{x_{i}}
if
\beta(x_{i})=F
. If a literal is satisfied, we call it a
true
literal and otherwise, we call it a
false
literal.

A truth assignment
\beta
extends to clauses as follows: If a clause
c_{j}
has at least one true literal under
\beta
, the clause
c_{j}
is
satisfied
.
Otherwise, if clause
c_{j}
has only false literals under
\beta
, then clause
c_{j}
is not satisfied.
Now, if the clause
c_{j}
has both a true and a false literal under
\beta
, that is, the truth values of the literals are
not all equal
, we say that the truth assignment
\beta

nae-satisfies
the clause
c_{j}
. A truth assignment
\beta
nae-satisfies a collection
\mathcal{C}
of clauses if and only if
\beta
nae-satisfies each clause in
\mathcal{C}
, and
\beta
satisfies
\mathcal{C}
if and only if it satisfies each clause in
\mathcal{C}
.

A
partition
of a variable set
V
is defined in the same way as a partition of arbitrary sets: Let
c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{m}
be pairwise disjoint subsets of
V
. Then,
P=\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}\{c_{j}\}
is a partition of
V
if and only if
\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}c_{j}=V
, that is, taking the union of the elements in
P
yields
V
. We deliberately chose
c_{j}
for the subsets of
V
in this definition as we view each element of
P
as a positive clause. Thus, a partition of
V
can be viewed as collection of positive clauses in which each variable appears in exactly one clause.
Moreover, taking the union of
k
pairwise disjoint partitions of
V
(that is, no clause appears in more than one partition) yields a formula in which each variable appears exactly
k
times.

A
hypergraph

H:=(\mathcal{V},E)
consists of a set of variables
\mathcal{V}
and a set of edges
E=\{e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{m}\}
, where each
e_{j}\in E
is a subset of
\mathcal{V}
. A hypergraph
H
is
r
-uniform
, if and only if
|e_{j}|=r
for each
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
, and
s
-regular
, if and only if each variable in
\mathcal{V}
appears in exactly
s
edges. A hypergraph
H
is
linear
if and only if
|e_{i}\cap e_{j}|\leq 1
for all distinct
i,j\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
. A
k
-coloring
is a mapping
\gamma\colon\mathcal{V}\rightarrow\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
, where each number represents a different color. An edge
e\in E
is
monochromatic
under
\gamma
if and only if
\gamma
assigns the same color to each variable in
e
. A hypergraph is
k
-colorable
if and only if there exists a
k
-coloring
\gamma
such that no edge is monochromatic under
\gamma
. For a
2
-colorable hypergraph, we also say that the hypergraph is
bicolorable
or
bipartite
. A
matching

M
is a subset of the edge set
E
such that the elements of
M
are pairwise disjoint. A matching
M
is called
perfect
if and only if each variable in
\mathcal{V}
appears in some element of
M
, that is,
M
is a partition of
\mathcal{V}
.

2.1
Problem statements

We start with a definition of the decision problem
Not-All-Equal Sat
, which is in
NP
.

Not-All-Equal Sat
(
NAE-Sat
)

Instance.
A set
V
of variables, and a collection
\mathcal{C}
of clauses formed over
V
, that is, each clause in
\mathcal{C}
is a subset of the corresponding set of literals
\mathcal{L}_{V}
.

Question.
Is there a truth assignment for
V
that nae-satisfies
\mathcal{C}
?

Throughout this work,
NAE-
3
-Sat
denotes the restriction of
NAE-Sat
to instances in which each clause has exactly three distinct literals.
In this paper, we focus on the variant of
NAE-
3
-Sat
where each clause is positive. For the sake of completeness, we give its formal definition below.

Positive NAE-
3
-Sat

Instance.
A set
V
of variables, and a collection
\mathcal{C}
of positive
3
-clauses over
V
.

Question.
Is there a truth assignment for
V
that nae-satisfies
\mathcal{C}
?

In Section

, we will turn our attention to
NAE-
(2,3)
-Sat

to

in an instance of

We use the following set of prefixes in this paper:

\{\textsc{Linear},\,\textsc{Positive},\,\textsc{$k$-Disjoint},\,\textsc{Planar},\,\textsc{$3$-Connected}\}.

We already alluded to the meaning of the prefix

above. The prefix

indicates that we only consider instances in which all clauses are positive, and

of

such that, for each

has at most one appearance of each variable. The two prefixes

and

obtained by regarding the variables and clauses as vertices and adding an edge

if and only if the variable

; that is, the incidence graph is required to be planar and 3-connected, respectively.

The suffix
k
, for example in

3
-Sat-

, means that each variable appears in at most
k

k

Note that for
NAE-
3
-Sat
the prefixes

-Disjoint

can be summarized by the property that each collection

in the disjoint union of clauses

is a partition of the variable set

into subsets of size 3. Hence, we obtain the following equivalent definition of
Positive Linear
k
-Disjoint NAE-
3
-Sat-E
k
that explicitly reflects Property (i) in the abstract; in particular, it explicitly states that each

is required to be a partition of

.

Positive Linear
k
-Disjoint NAE-

-Sat-E

Instance.
A set

of variables, and collections

of positive 3-clauses that have the following properties:

(i)

is a partition of

,

(ii)

(iii)

The second property in the definition above ensures that

is a disjoint union and, thus, that each variable appears exactly

. Without this property, the union of

2.2
Proof techniques

In the following, we discuss techniques used throughout the paper.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a collection of positive
3
-clauses over a set of variables
V
.
Further, let
\beta^{\prime}
be a truth assignment for a strict subset
V^{\prime}
of
V
. Assume we would like to know if we can extend
\beta^{\prime}
by setting truth values for the variables in
V\setminus V^{\prime}
such that the resulting truth assignment nae-satisfies all clauses in
\mathcal{C}
. To this end, we may try to use
\beta^{\prime}
and the clauses in
\mathcal{C}
to infer additional information. Let
c:=\{x,y,z\}
be a clause in
\mathcal{C}
. We distinguish the following four cases.

(i)

Case
|c\cap V^{\prime}|=3
: Then,
\beta^{\prime}
sets all variables in
c
to some truth value. If these truth values are not all equal, we do not infer any new information from
c
. Otherwise,
c
is not nae-satisfied and we are done.

(ii)

Case
|c\cap V^{\prime}|=2
: Then,
\beta^{\prime}
sets two of the variables, say
y
and
z
, in
c
to some truth value. If
\beta^{\prime}(y)\neq\beta^{\prime}(z)
, then
c
is already nae-satisfied. Otherwise, we deduce that
x
must be set to
\{T,F\}\setminus\beta^{\prime}(y)
.

(iii)

Case
|c\cap V^{\prime}|=1
: Then,
\beta^{\prime}
sets one of the variables, say
z
, in
c
to some truth value. If
\beta^{\prime}(z)=T
, we deduce that
\{\bar{x},\bar{y}\}
must be satisfied but not necessarily nae-satisfied. If
\beta^{\prime}(z)=F
, we must satisfy
\{x,y\}
. We will later explain how we use such
learned

2
-clauses to infer even more information.

(iv)

Case
|c\cap V^{\prime}|=0
: In this case,
\beta^{\prime}
does not assign a truth value to any variable in
c
. Thus, we do not infer any new information from
c
.

Now, the information that we learn in the latter subcase of Case (ii) is straightforward. We obtain a new truth assignment
\beta^{\prime\prime}
for
V^{\prime\prime}:=V^{\prime}\cup\{x\}\subseteq V
that we can use instead of
\beta^{\prime}
for further considerations. If
V^{\prime\prime}=V
, we check if
\beta^{\prime\prime}
nae-satisfies all clauses in
\mathcal{C}
and we are done. The usefulness of Case (iii) becomes apparent if we consider multiple clauses in turn. Thereby, we obtain a collection
\mathcal{C}_{\textsc{Sat}}
of
2
-clauses over
V
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{\textsc{Sat}}
be non-empty. A clause such as
\{\bar{x},\bar{y}\}
can also be viewed as an implication
x\Rightarrow\bar{y}
or, equivalently, as
y\Rightarrow\bar{x}
. Hence, these
2
-clauses give rise to chains of implications

\ell_{1}\Rightarrow\ell_{2}\Rightarrow\cdots\Rightarrow\ell_{r},

where
\ell_{i}
is some variable in
V
or its negation, i.e., a literal in
\mathcal{L}_{V}
.
A notable special case is
\ell_{1}=\ell_{r}
in which case we call the chain
cyclic
. In this case, we have

\beta(\ell_{1})=\beta(\ell_{2})=\cdots=\beta(\ell_{r})

for each truth assignment
\beta
that satisfies the corresponding
2
-clauses. If this cyclic chain of implications forces three variables that form a clause in
\mathcal{C}
to the same truth value, we conclude that it is not possible to nae-satisfy this clause while also satisfying
\mathcal{C}_{\textsc{Sat}}
. Let us consider the non-cyclic case next. If we know that
\ell_{i}
is true, for example by Case (ii), this implies that all
\ell_{j}
with
i<j\leq r
must be set true as well. Again, this may lead to a conflict with a clause in
\mathcal{C}
.

Once we inferred a collection
\mathcal{C}_{\textsc{Sat}}
of
2
-clauses by Case (iii), we may use
resolution
in order to infer additional
2
-clauses. For example, consider two clauses
\{x,y\}
and
\{\bar{y},\bar{z}\}
. Noting that the variable
y
appears unnegated in the first clause and negated in the second clause, we can infer a
2
-clause as follows:

\left(\{x,y\}\setminus\{y\}\right)\cup\left(\{\bar{y},\bar{z}\}\setminus\{\bar{y}\}\right)=\{x,\bar{z}\}.

We say that we
used
or
performed
resolution to obtain
\{x,\bar{z}\}
. Then, adding
\{x,\bar{z}\}
to a collection of clauses
\mathcal{C}_{\textsc{Sat}}
that contain
\{x,y\}
and
\{\bar{y},\bar{z}\}
has no effect on the satisfiability of
\mathcal{C}_{\textsc{Sat}}
. Importantly, we do not consider resolution in the setting of nae-satisfiability. We are always concerned with the satisfiability of a collection of clauses when using resolution. For a formal introduction to the
resolution calculus
, we refer to the book by Schöning and Torán
[
ST13
, Ch. 2]
.

A truth assignment
\beta
that nae-satisfies a collection of clauses exhibits a well-known symmetry in the sense that we obtain a second nae-satisfying truth assignment by interchanging the truth values true and false in
\beta
. For instance, we use this property of nae-satisfying truth assignments to simplify some case distinctions in Section
3
.

q
times negated

Let
p
and
q
be fixed non-negative integers. As an interesting consequence of Theorem
3.8
, we obtain for
p+q\geq 4
a hardness result for
Linear NAE-
3
-Sat
on instances in which each variable appears exactly
p
times unnegated and exactly
q
times negated. In fact, we show that the following dichotomy holds.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
p
and
q
be fixed non-negative integers.
Linear NAE-
3
-Sat
for instances in which each variable appears exactly
p
times unnegated and exactly
q
times negated is

•

NP
-complete if
p+q\geq 4
,

•

in
P
if
p+q\leq 3
.

Proof.

First, let
p+q=k\geq 4
. Let
\mathcal{I}=(V,\mathcal{C})
be an instance of
Positive Linear
k
-Disjoint NAE-
3
-Sat-E
k
with
\mathcal{C}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}C_{i}
such that
C_{i}
is a partition of
V
for each
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
. Then, we obtain an instance
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}=(V,\mathcal{C}^{\prime})
from
\mathcal{I}
by replacing each variable appearance in
C_{1}\cup C_{2}\cup\ldots\cup C_{q}
by its negation. Observe that
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
is an instance of
Linear NAE-
3
-Sat
in which, by construction, each variable appears exactly
p
times unnegated and exactly
q
times negated. Since any truth assignment
\beta\colon V\rightarrow\{T,F\}
nae-satisfies a clause
\{x,y,z\}\in\mathcal{C}
if and only if
\beta
nae-satisfies
\{\overline{x},\overline{y},\overline{z}\}
, it now follows that
\mathcal{I}
is a yes-instance if and only if
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
is a yes-instance. As the reduction is clearly polynomial, we conclude that
Linear NAE-
3
-Sat
is NP-complete for instances with
p+q\geq 4
.

Second, let
p+q\leq 3
. Since
NAE-
k
-Sat-
k
where each clause contains exactly
k
literals has been shown to be in
P
by Filho
[
Fil19
, Thm. 3.3.1.]
, it immediately follows that
Linear NAE-
3
-Sat
with
p+q\leq 3
is in P.
∎

4.2
Instances with clauses of size 2 and 3

In this section, we prove that
Positive Linear
3
-Disjoint Not-All-Equal Sat-E
3
remains
NP
-complete when loosening the restriction to
3
-clauses by also allowing for
2
-clauses, even when each variable appears in exactly one
2
-clause or when it appears in exactly one
3
-clause.

Theorem 4.2
.

Positive Linear
3
-Disjoint Not-All-Equal
(2,3)
-Sat-E
3
is
NP
-complete even if each variable appears in exactly one 3-clause.

Proof.

We show
NP
-hardness by a reduction from
Positive Not-All-Equal
3
-Sat-E
4
, where each clause contains exactly three distinct variables and each variable appears in exactly four clauses (which is
NP
-complete
[
DD20
, Thm. 1]
). Let
\mathcal{I}=(V,C)
be an instance with a variable set
V=\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\}
and a collection of clauses
C=\{c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{m}\}
.

For each variable
x_{i}\in V
, we replace the
j
-th appearance in
C
by a new variable
x_{i}^{(j)}
and let
C_{3}^{V}
denote the resulting collection of clauses. Further, we introduce

C_{2}^{V,Y}:=\{\{x_{i}^{(4)},\,y_{i}^{(4)}\},\{y_{i}^{(4)},\,x_{i}^{(1)}\}\}\cup\bigcup_{j=1}^{3}\{\{x_{i}^{(j)},\,y_{i}^{(j)}\},\{y_{i}^{(j)},\,x_{i}^{(j+1)}\}\},

where
y_{i}^{(j)}
are new variables. Let
C_{3}^{Y}
denote the collection of clauses that is obtained if we replace
x_{i}^{(j)}
by
y_{i}^{(j)}
in
C_{3}^{V}
for
1\leq i\leq n
and
1\leq j\leq 4
. We obtain an instance
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},C^{\prime})
of
Positive Not-All-Equal
(2,3)
-Sat-E
3
as follows:

\displaystyle V^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\{x_{i}^{(j)},\,y_{i}^{(j)}\mid 1\leq i\leq n\text{ and }1\leq j\leq 4\},

\displaystyle C^{\prime}

\displaystyle=C_{2}^{V,Y}\cup C_{3}^{V}\cup C_{3}^{Y}.

It is not difficult to verify that
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
is in fact an instance of
Positive Linear
3
-Disjoint Not-All-Equal
(2,3)
-Sat-E3
. In addition, note that each variable appears in exactly two 2-clauses and in exactly one 3-clause.

We show that
\mathcal{I}
is a yes-instance if and only if
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
is a yes-instance.

“
\Rightarrow
”: Let
\beta\colon V\rightarrow\{T,F\}
be a truth assignment that nae-satisfies
C
. We define a truth assignment
\beta^{\prime}
for
V^{\prime}
as follows:

\beta^{\prime}(x_{i}^{(j)})=\beta(x_{i})\text{ and }\beta^{\prime}(y_{i}^{(j)})\in\{T,F\}\setminus\{\beta(x_{i})\},\quad 1\leq i\leq n\text{ and }1\leq j\leq 4.

Since
\beta^{\prime}(x_{i}^{(j)})\neq\beta^{\prime}(y_{i}^{(j)})
, each clause in
C_{2}^{V,Y}
is nae-satisfied. For each
x_{i}\in V
,
\beta^{\prime}
sets each copy
x_{i}^{(j)}
to the truth value
\beta(x_{i})
. Hence, by construction,
\beta^{\prime}
nae-satisfies
C_{3}^{V}
. It remains to show that
C_{3}^{Y}
is also nae-satisfied. Since
C_{3}^{Y}
is a copy of
C_{3}^{V}
with
x_{i}^{(j)}
replaced by
y_{i}^{(j)}
and
\beta^{\prime}
sets
x_{i}^{(j)}
and
y_{i}^{(j)}
to opposite truth values, the symmetry of nae-satisfying truth assignments implies that
\beta^{\prime}
nae-satisfies
C_{3}^{Y}
.

“
\Leftarrow
”: Let
\beta^{\prime}\colon V^{\prime}\rightarrow\{T,F\}
be a truth assignment that nae-satisfies
C^{\prime}
. Since each clause in
C_{2}^{V,Y}
can be viewed as an XOR condition on the truth values of the two contained variables, it is easy to verify that
\beta^{\prime}
satisfies the following condition:

\beta^{\prime}(x_{i}^{(1)})=\beta^{\prime}(x_{i}^{(2)})=\beta^{\prime}(x_{i}^{(3)})=\beta^{\prime}(x_{i}^{(4)}),\quad 1\leq i\leq n.

We obtain a truth assignment
\beta
for
V
by setting
\beta(x_{i})=\beta^{\prime}(x_{i}^{(1)})
. By construction and the condition above,
\beta
nae-satisfies
C
.

The size of
\mathcal{I^{\prime}}
is linear in the size of
\mathcal{I}
and the construction in polynomial time is straightforward. Hence, we conclude that the presented reduction is polynomial.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Positive Linear
3
-Disjoint Not-All-Equal
(2,3)
-Sat-E
3
is

-complete even if each variable appears in exactly one 2-clause.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{I}=(V,\mathcal{C})
be an instance of
Positive Linear
k
-Disjoint NAE-
3
-Sat-E
4
with
\mathcal{C}=\bigcup_{i=k}^{4}C_{k}
such that (i)
C_{k}
is a partition of
V
for each
k\in\{1,2,3,4\}
, (ii)
C_{i}\cap C_{j}=\emptyset
if
i\neq j
, and (iii)

is linear. We obtain an instance of
Positive Linear
3
-Disjoint Not-All-Equal
(2,3)
-Sat-E
3

We obtain an instance
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}=(V^{\prime}=V,\mathcal{C}^{\prime})
of

. As observed in the proof of Theorem

Next, we construct an instance
\mathcal{I}^{\prime\prime}=(V^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime})
of
Positive Linear
3
-Disjoint Not-All-Equal
(2,3)
-Sat-E
3
as follows. For each

, we

(1)

introduce two new variables

and
x_{i}^{-}
and a new 2-clause
\{x_{i}^{+},x_{i}^{-}\}
,

(2)

replace each appearance of
\overline{x_{i}}

, and

(3)

replace each appearance of

\mathcal{D}_{1}=\{\{x_{i}^{+},x_{i}^{-}\}\mid x_{i}\in V^{\prime}\}\text{, }\mathcal{D}_{2}=C^{\prime\prime}_{1}\cup C^{\prime\prime}_{3}\text{ and }\mathcal{D}_{3}=C^{\prime\prime}_{2}\cup C^{\prime\prime}_{4}.

-Sat-E

“
\Rightarrow
”: Let
\beta^{\prime}\colon V^{\prime}\rightarrow\{T,F\}

. Then we obtain a truth assignment
\beta^{\prime\prime}

4.3
Remarks on Planarity

We conclude this section with some concise remarks for the case when the planarity constraint is added, i.e., when the incidence graph is required to be planar.
It was shown by Pilz
[
Pil19
, Thm. 12]
that every instance of
Planar Sat
in which each clause contains at least three unnegated appearances or at least three negated appearances of distinct variables is satisfiable. Noting that his proof constructs a
nae-satisfying
truth assignment, we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 4.4
.

Each instance of
Positive Planar NAE-Sat
with at least three distinct variables per clause is nae-satisfiable.

Proof.

The construction by Pilz
[
Pil19
, Thm. 12]
can be used without modifications to obtain a nae-satisfying truth assignment for an instance of
Positive Planar NAE-Sat
with at least three distinct variables per clause.
∎

By the above result, since we focus on restricted variants of
Positive NAE-Sat
with exactly three distinct variables per clause in this paper, each decision problem we proved to be
NP
-complete in this setting becomes trivial if the planarity constraint is added as each such instance is nae-satisfiable.

Previously, Hasan et al.
[
HMR22
, Thm. 1]
showed that each instance of
Positive Planar
3
-Connected NAE-
3
-Sat
is nae-satisfiable. For an incidence graph to be 3-connected, it is necessary but not sufficient that each clause has at least three distinct variables. Hence, Theorem
4.4
identifies a strictly larger class of trivial instances of
Positive NAE-Sat
and this class properly contains all instances of
Positive Planar
3
-Connected NAE-
3
-Sat
. However, note that Hasan et al.
[
HMR22
, Thm. 1]
present a linear time algorithm to find a nae-satisfying truth assignment for
Positive Planar 3-connected NAE-3-Sat
whereas the construction by Pilz
[
Pil19
, Thm. 12]
yields a quadratic algorithm.

5
Conclusion

In this work, we have analyzed the computational complexity status of a variant of
NAE-
3
-Sat
, where the clauses form a disjoint union of
k
partitions of the set of variables and each pair of distinct clauses shares at most one variable. Our results settle a computational complexity dichotomy for
Positive Linear
k
-Disjoint NAE-
3
-Sat-E
k
with respect to the number
k
of partitions: for
k\geq 4
, the problem is
NP
-complete, while for
k\leq 3
it is in
P
; in particular, for
k\in\{1,2\}
, each instance of
Positive Linear
k
-Disjoint NAE-
3
-Sat-E
k
is a yes-instance. As a byproduct of our results, it follows that
Linear NAE-
3
-Sat
, where each variable appears exactly
p
times unnegated and exactly
q
times negated, is
NP
-complete for each fixed value of
p+q\geq 4
.

In addition, observe that our results imply that loosening the restriction to 3-clauses by also allowing for 2-clauses reduces the number of partitions that sets the boundary between
P
and
NP
-completeness from
4
to
3
. The latter
NP
-completeness result holds in either of the cases that each variable appears in exactly one
3
-clause or in exactly one
2
-clause.

Our results for
NAE-
3
-Sat
directly imply analogous results for the computational complexity of deciding the existence of a 2-coloring for linear 3-uniform
k
-regular hypergraphs whose edges are given as a decomposition into
k
perfect matchings: This decision problem is
NP
-complete for
k\geq 4
and, otherwise, in
P
.

While several results have been provided in this paper, some interesting questions still remain open as potential directions for future research. For instance, while we conjecture this not to be the case, to the best of our knowledge it is not known whether there are instances of
Positive Linear
3
-Disjoint NAE-
3
-Sat-E
3
which are not nae-satisfiable.

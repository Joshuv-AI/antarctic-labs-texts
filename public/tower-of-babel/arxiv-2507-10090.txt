On the structure of optimal solutions of conservation laws at a junction with one incoming and one outgoing arc
F. Ancona, A. Cesaroni, G. M. Coclite, M. Garavello

Abstract
We consider a min-max problem for strictly concave conservation laws on a 1-1 network, with inflow controls acting at the junction. We investigate the minimization problem for a functional measuring the total variation of the flow of the solutions at the node, among those solutions that maximize the time integral of the flux. To formulate this problem we establish a regularity result showing that the total variation of the boundary-flux of the solution of an initial-boundary value problem is controlled by the total variation of the initial datum and of the flux of the boundary datum. In the case the initial datum is monotone, we show that the flux of the entropy weak solution at the node provides an optimal inflow control for this min-max problem. We also exhibit two prototype examples showing that, in the case where the initial datum is not monotone, the flux of the entropy weak solution is no more optimal. Keywords: Conservation laws, entropy solution, traffic models, networks, weak solutions. MSC 2020: 35F25, 35L65, 90B20.

Introduction

In this note, we consider an optimal control problem for scalar conservation laws evolving on a 1–1 network consisting of a junction (node) with one incoming and one outgoing edge.
We model the
incoming edge with the half-line
I_{1}\doteq(-\infty,0)
, and
the outgoing one with the half-line
I_{2}\doteq(0,+\infty)
,
so that the junction is sitting at
x=0
.
On each edge
I_{i}
the evolution of the state variable
u_{i}(t,x)
is governed by a
scalar conservation law
supplemented with some initial condition
\overline{u}_{i}\in\mathbf{L^{\infty}}(I_{i};\mathbb{R})
,

i=1,2
.
This yields to study the Cauchy problems
on the two semilines
I_{i}
,
i=1,2
:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u_{1}+\partial_{x}f(u_{1})=0,\qquad&x<0,\,t>0,\\
u_{1}(0,x)=\overline{u}_{1}(x),&x<0,\end{cases}

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u_{2}+\partial_{x}f(u_{2})=0,\qquad&x>0,\,t>0,\\
u_{2}(0,x)=\overline{u}_{2}(x),&x>0,\end{cases}

(1.2)

where we assume that the flux
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}

is a twice continuously differentiable, (uniformly) strictly concave map.
The equations (
1.1
), (
1.2
) are usually coupled through a Kirkhoff type condition at the boundary
x=0
:

f(u_{1}(t,0))=f(u_{2}(t,0))\qquad\textrm{for a.e.}\ \ t>0.

(1.3)

Here, and throughout the following, we adopt the notation

u_{1}(t,0)\doteq\lim_{x\to 0^{-}}u_{1}(t,x)
,

u_{2}(t,0)\doteq\lim_{x\to 0^{+}}u_{2}(t,x)
,
for the one-sided limit
of
u_{i}(t,\cdot)
at
x=0
.
The equation (
1.3
) expresses the conservation of the total mass through the node.
Note that the system (
1.1
), (
1.2
), and (
1.3
),
admits in general infinitely many entropy admissible solutions.
One may recover the uniqueness of solutions to the
nodal Cauchy problem (
1.1
), (
1.2
), (
1.3
),
requiring that the flux trace at the junction

\gamma(t)\doteq f(u_{1}(t,0))=f(u_{2}(t,0)),\qquad t>0,

(1.4)

satisfies some further condition.
Such conditions are expressed in terms of time-dependent germs
[
10
,
18
]
, or introducing the notion of flux-limited solutions
for Hamilton-Jacobi equations
[
41
]
(exploiting the equivalence between the two classes of equations
[
19
]
),
or are obtained via vanishing viscosity approximations
[
24
]
.
Here, we follow the different perspective introduced in
[
3
]

to analyze control problems for conservation laws
models at a junction with
n
incoming and
m
outgoing edges. Namely,
we regard the flux trace
\gamma
in (
1.4
) as an
inflow control

acting at the junction.
More precisely, for a fixed
T>0
,
we consider

\gamma\in\mathbf{L^{\infty}}((0,T);\mathbb{R})

as an
admissible inflow control

if there exists a pair of boundary data

k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbf{L^{\infty}}\left((0,T);\mathbb{R}\right)
,
such that, letting
u_{1}:[0,T]\times(-\infty,0]\to\mathbb{R}
,
u_{2}:[0,T]\times[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
, be the entropy admissible weak solutions of the mixed initial boundary value problems

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u_{1}+\partial_{x}f(u_{1})=0,\qquad&x<0,\,t>0,\\
u_{1}(0,x)=\overline{u}_{1}(x),&x<0,\\
u_{1}(t,0)=k_{1}(t),&t>0,\end{cases}

(1.5)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u_{2}+\partial_{x}f(u_{2})=0,\qquad&x>0,\,t>0,\\
u_{2}(0,x)=\overline{u}_{2}(x),&x>0,\\
u_{2}(t,0)=k_{2}(t),&t>0,\end{cases}

(1.6)

the corresponding fluxes satisfy the
constraint (
1.4
).
The Dirichlet boundary conditions in (
1.5
), (
1.6
)
are
interpreted in the relaxed sense
of Bardos, Le Roux, Nédélec
[
11
]
which, for strictly concave fluxes,
are equivalent to the conditions (iii)
stated in Definition
2.1

(in Section
2
) of entropy admissible solution of the mixed initial-boundary
value problem (cfr.
[
44
]
).
Given a fixed initial datum

\overline{u}(x)=\begin{cases}\overline{u}_{1}(x),\quad&\text{if}\quad x<0,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\overline{u}_{2}(x),\quad&\text{if}\quad x>0,\end{cases}

(1.7)

for any admissible inflow control

\gamma\in\mathbf{L^{\infty}}((0,T);\mathbb{R})
,
we will denote by

u(t,x;\gamma)\doteq\begin{cases}u_{1}(t,x),\quad&\text{if}\quad x<0,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr u_{2}(t,x),\quad&\text{if}\quad x>0,\end{cases}

(1.8)

the unique solution of the nodal Cauchy problem
(
1.1
), (
1.2
), (
1.3
), (
1.4
).

Optimization issues for conservation laws evolving on networks has attracted
an increasing attention in the last two decades (see the monographs
[
12
,
14
,
35
]
and the references therein).
The interest on these problems is motivated by a wide range of applications in different areas
such as data flow and telecommunication
[
23
]
,
gas or water pipelines
[
28
,
38
]
, production
processes
[
33
,
34
,
37
,
42
]
,
biological resources
[
25
]
, and
blood circulation
[
21
]
.
In particular, optimal control problems for conservation laws with concave flux
have found a natural application in traffic flow models, firstly introduced in the seminal papers by Lighthill, Whitham and
Richards (LWR model)
[
46
,
52
]
.
Several optimization problems related to different
traffic performance indexes have been considered in the literature
to improve the mean travel time in
a given road segment
[
6
,
7
,
22
,
50
,
54
]
,
to diminish
the queue length due to a red light or stop sign
[
29
]
,
to minimise stop-and-go waves
[
26
,
27
,
53
]
,
or to get a fuel consumption reduction
[
5
,
51
]
.

Within the above setup,
in the general case of a junction with
n
incoming and
m
outgoing edges
we have established the existence of optimal inflow controls
for
various functionals depending on the
value of the solution at
the node
[
3
]
,
and along the incoming or outgoing
edges
[
4
]
.

The object of this note is to derive a characterization of optimal inflow controls for a class
of optimal control problems on a 1–1 network.
Namely, we first consider the problem of maximizing the total flux at the junction

\sup_{\gamma\in\mathcal{U}}\int_{0}^{T}f(u_{1}(t,0;\gamma))dt=\sup_{\gamma\in{\mathcal{U}}}\int_{0}^{T}\gamma(t)\ dt,

(1.9)

where
\mathcal{U}
denotes the class of admissible inflow controls.
Our first result (Theorem
2.1
) shows that, letting
\widetilde{u}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}

denote the entropy weak solution of the Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}f(u)=0,\quad&x\in\mathbb{R},\,t>0,\\
u(0,x)=\overline{u}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R},\end{cases}

(1.10)

the corresponding flux
\widetilde{\gamma}\doteq f\circ\widetilde{u}(\cdot,0)
provides a maximizer of (
1.9
).
Since the optimization problem (
1.9
) admits in general more than one maximizer, we next address a
minimization problem for a functional

\mathcal{F}
measuring the total variation of the solution at the node (see definition (
2.13
))

\inf_{\gamma\in\mathcal{U}_{max}}\mathcal{F}\big(u(\cdot,\cdot\,;\gamma)\big),

(1.11)

where
\mathcal{U}_{max}
denotes the set of the maximizers for (
1.9
).
The goal of this min-max problem is to keep as small as possible the oscillations of the
flux of the solution at the node among those solutions that maximize the time integral of the flux.
To formulate this
minimization problem
we need first to ensure
that the flux of the solutions of the initial boundary value problems (
1.5
), (
1.6
),
evaluated at the boundary
x=0
, is a function of bounded variation (
\mathbf{BV}
),
provided that the initial data
\overline{u}_{i}
,
and the
flux of the boundary data
f\circ k_{i}
, are
\mathbf{BV}
functions as well.
This regularity result
is established in Theorem
A.1

of the Appendix.

The main results of this note (Theorem
5.1
, Theorem
5.2
) show that,
in the case where the initial datum
\overline{u}
is monotone,
the flux
\widetilde{\gamma}

of the entropy weak solution of (
1.10
)
provides
an optimal inflow control for the min-max problem (
1.11
).
On the other hand, in Section
4
we discuss two prototype examples showing that, in the case where the
initial datum
\overline{u}
is not monotone, the flux
\widetilde{\gamma}
is no more optimal.
This leads us to formulate the following:

Conjecture: if the flux
\widetilde{\gamma}
of the entropy weak solution of (
1.10
) is not monotone on
(0,T)
, then an optimal control
for (
1.11
)
must be a piecewise constant flux of a non-entropic solution of (
1.10
).
Moreover, such a solution admits a shock discontinuity emerging tangentially from the junction
x=0
.

From the perspective of traffic model applications, our result is quite natural.
Specifically, when the traffic on both incoming and outgoing roads exhibits homogeneous features
(meaning it is either in acceleration or in deceleration regime),
no control is needed at the road intersection
in order to enhance the overall performance. However, when the regime
on the roads is heterogeneous, so presenting both deceleration and acceleration areas,
then implementing a different condition at the road intersection can significantly improve car circulation.

The analysis of the optimization problems considered in this note relies on the method of generalized characteristics
developed by Dafermos
[
30
,
31
]
for conservation laws with convex or concave fluxes,
and applied to the present setting of
a 1-1 network.
Here we have restricted the study of these optimization problems to the case
where the dynamics along the incoming and outgoing edges is described by the
same conservation law.
As a natural next step, we plan to investigate the structure of solutions for this type of optimal control
problems when the flux of the incoming and outgoing edges is different
(in order to describe traffic flow dynamics on roads of varying amplitudes or surface conditions). In this different scenario
we will exploit the analysis pursued in
[
8
,
9
]

on the structure of solutions to conservation laws with discontinuous flux. We expect
to derive conditions (on some classes of initial data) which ensure
the optimality of inflow controls associated to the so-called
interface connections

(A,B)
.

Recent results on the structure of optimal solutions for another class of control
problems on a 1-1 network
were obtained in
[
19
]

exploiting the equivalence between conservation laws and Hamilton Jacobi equations.

The note is organized as follows.
In Section
2
we recall some preliminary results on initial-boundary value problems,
present the general control setting,
and state the main results of the note.
In Section
3
we prove the optimality for (
1.9
) of the flux of the entropy weak solution of (
1.10
). In Section
4
we propose two examples of non-monotone initial data
\overline{u}

for which the entropy weak solution of (
1.10
) is not an optimal solution of (
1.11
).
In Section
5
we establish the main results of the note on the optimality for (
1.11
) of
the flux of the entropy weak solution of (
1.10
) when the initial datum is monotone. Finally,
in the Appendix
A
we provide a proof of the
\mathbf{BV}
-regularity of the trace of the flux of
the solution of an initial-boundary value problem.

2
Preliminaries and main results

In traffic flow models, usually the traffic density takes values in a compact domain
\Omega=[0,u^{\max}]
,
where
u^{\max}
denotes the
maximum possible density (bump-to-bump) inside the road.
The fundamental diagram
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
has the expression
f(u)=uv(u)
where
v

is the average speed of vehicles.
In most models the velocity
v

satisfies
v^{\prime}(u)\leq\overline{v}<0

for all
u
,
and
u\to uv^{\prime}(u)

is a non-increasing map.
Because of the finite propagation speed,
it is not restrictive to assume
that the domain of
f
is the whole real line.
Thus, throughout the note we make the general assumption
that the flux function
is a twice continuously differentiable
map
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, which is (uniformly) strictly concave, and that attains its global maximum at the point
\theta
,
i.e.

f(\theta)=\max_{u\in\mathbb{R}}f(u).

(2.1)

We recall that a function
g:(a,b)\to\mathbb{R}
,
a,b\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty,+\infty\}
, of bounded variation (
\mathbf{BV}
)
admits left limits at any point
t\in(a,b]
,
and right limits at any point
t\in[a,b)
.
We will denote them with
g(t^{-})

and
g(t^{+})
, respectively.
We
define
\tv_{(a,b)}\{g\}
as the

essential variation
of
g
on the interval
(a,b)

which coincides with the
pointwise variation
on
(a,b)

of the right continuous or left continuous representative of
g

(see
[
2
, Section 3.2]
).
More explicitly for the right continuous representative
g
we write:

\tv_{(a,b)}\{g\}\doteq\sup_{a<t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{N}<b}\left\{\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}|g(t_{\ell})-g(t_{\ell-1})|\right\}.

(2.2)

We will sometimes also use the notation, for
(c,d)\subset(a,b)
,

\tv_{[c,d]}\{g\}\doteq\tv_{(c,d)}(g)+|g(c^{+})-g(c^{-})|+|g(d^{+})-g(d^{-})|.

(2.3)

Admissible junction controls

Following
[
3
]
, we introduce in this section the set of admissible junction inflow controls

), (
1.6
).
We recall (see

As observed in the introduction,

,

.
Moreover, we will use the notations

,

,

The Dirichlet boundary conditions at

Definition 2.1
.

Given
\overline{u}_{i}\in\mathbf{L^{\infty}}(I_{i};\mathbb{R})
and

k_{i}\in\mathbf{L^{\infty}}\big((0,T);J_{i}\big)
,

,
we say that a function

u_{i}\in\mathbf{C}\big([0,T];\,\mathbf{L}^{1}_{{\rm loc}}(I_{i};\mathbb{R})\big)

is an entropy admissible weak solution
of (
1.5
) (resp. of (

)) if :

(i)

For every

,

u_{i}(t)\in\mathbf{BV_{\rm loc}}\left(I_{i};\mathbb{R}\right)
and admits the one-sided limit at

.

(ii)

u_{i}
is a weak entropic solution
of (
1.1
) (resp. of (
1.2
)) with initial data
\overline{u}_{i}
, i.e. for all

\varphi\in\mathbf{C^{1}_{c}}\big([0,T)\times I_{i};\mathbb{R}\big)
there holds

\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{I_{i}}\Big[u_{i}\,\partial_{t}\varphi+f(u_{i})\,\partial_{x}\varphi\Big](t,x)~dxdt+\int_{I_{i}}\overline{u}_{i}(x)\varphi(0,x)dx\,=0\,,

and the Lax entropy condition is satisfied

u_{i}(t,x^{-})\leq u_{i}(t,x^{+}),\qquad\quad t>0,\ x\in I_{i}.

(iii)

The boundary condition in (

) is verified if,

, there holds:

\begin{array}[]{c@{\qquad}c}\textrm{either}&u_{1}(t,0)=k_{1}(t),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\textrm{or}&\quad f^{\prime}(u_{1}(t,0))>0\ \ \textrm{ and }\ f(u_{1}(t,0))\leq f\big(k_{1}(t)\big);\end{array}

(2.4)

the boundary condition in (
1.6
) is verified if,
for a.e.
t>0
, there holds:

\begin{array}[]{c@{\qquad}c}\textrm{either}&u_{2}(t,0)=k_{2}(t),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\textrm{or}&\quad f^{\prime}(u_{2}(t,0))<0\ \ \textrm{ and }\ f(u_{2}(t,0))\leq f\big(k_{2}(t)\big).\end{array}

.

In Definition

one can recover the same

,

,
of (

), (

),
replacing the boundary data

with the normalized boundary data

\widetilde{k}_{i}(t)\doteq\begin{cases}f_{+}^{-1}\big(f(u_{1}(t,0))\big),&\textrm{if}\quad\ i=1,\\

Definition 2.2
.

Given

,

where

is the entropy weak solution to (

) with initial

, while

is the entropy weak solution to (

)

and boundary datum

.

Let us denote

u(t,x;\gamma)=\begin{cases}u_{1}(t,x),&x<0\\
u_{2}(t,x),&x>0\end{cases}

\gamma(t)=f(u(t,0;\gamma))

.

Remark 2.2
.

Observe that, if
\widetilde{u}:(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
denotes the

to (

), with initial datum

\overline{u}\in\mathbf{L^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})

then the flux

In fact, one can immediately check that

,
\widetilde{u}_{2}
denote the restriction of
\widetilde{u}
to

,
\{x>0\}
,
respectively, then we have:

-

\widetilde{u}_{1}
is the entropy weak solution to (
1.5
) with initial datum
\overline{u}_{1}
and boundary datum

k_{1}(t)\doteq f_{+}^{-1}\big(f(\widetilde{u}_{1}(t,0))\big),\quad t\in(0,T);

-

\widetilde{u}_{2}
is the entropy weak solution to (

) with initial datum
\overline{u}_{2}
and boundary datum

k_{2}(t)\doteq f_{-}^{-1}\big(f(\widetilde{u}_{2}(t,0))\big),\quad t\in(0,T);

-

for a.e.

,

f(\widetilde{u}_{1}(t,0))=f(\widetilde{u}_{2}(t,0)).

(2.8)

Then, it follows that the map

Therefore, we have

\widetilde{u}(t,x)=u(t,x;\widetilde{\gamma}\,)\qquad\forall~(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}\,,

with

defined as in (

2.2
The control problem

For every fixed
T>0
, given
\overline{u}\in\mathbf{L^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
,
we introduce the set of admissible controls according to Definition
2.2
:

\mathcal{U}\doteq\mathcal{U}(\overline{u})\doteq\Big\{\gamma\in\mathbf{L^{\infty}}\big([0,T];\,\mathbb{R}\big)\ \big|\ \gamma\ \textrm{is an admissible junction inflow control}\Big\},

(2.9)

and we consider the maximization problem

\sup_{\gamma\in{\mathcal{U}}}\int_{0}^{T}f(u_{1}(t,0;\gamma))\,dt=\sup_{\gamma\in{\mathcal{U}}}\int_{0}^{T}\gamma(t)\,dt.

(2.10)

Our first result is the following:

Theorem 2.1
.

Given
T>0
and
\overline{u}\in\mathbf{L^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
,
let
\widetilde{u}
be the entropy admissible weak solution
to the Cauchy problem (
1.10
). Then the composite function

\widetilde{\gamma}\doteq f\circ\widetilde{u}(\cdot\,0)
solves the maximization problem (
2.10
),
i.e. there holds

\int_{0}^{T}f(\widetilde{u}(t,0))\,dt=\max_{\gamma\in{\mathcal{U}}}\int_{0}^{T}\gamma(t)\,dt.

The proof of this result is given in
Section
3
.
One can easily verify that the optimization problem (
2.10
) admits other maximizers different from
\widetilde{\gamma}
,
which are
junction inflow controls that present
rarefaction waves or shocks generated at the node
x=0
at positive times (see Section
4
).
Since an usual objective in traffic management
is to keep the traffic flow as fluent as possible,
it is then natural
to investigate the existence of maximizers
of (
2.10
) that minimise a cost functional
measuring the total variation of the corresponding solution at the node
x=0

(e.g. see
[
3
,
26
]
).

Therefore for every fixed
T>0
, given
\overline{u}\in\mathbf{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
,
we restrict the set of maximizers of (
2.10
) to the set of inflow controls:

\mathcal{U}_{max}\doteq\mathcal{U}_{max}(\overline{u})\doteq\left\{\bar{\gamma}\in\mathcal{U}\cap\mathbf{BV}\big([0,T];\,\mathbb{R}\big)\ \bigg|\ \int_{0}^{T}f(u_{1}(t,0;\bar{\gamma}))dt=\max_{\gamma\in{\mathcal{U}}}\int_{0}^{T}\ f(u_{1}(t,0;\gamma))dt\right\}

(2.11)

which are admissible for the functional we are going to minimise. For this reason we will assume
\overline{u}\in\mathbf{BV}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
, so to guarantee that this set is not empty, see the following Remark.

Remark 2.3
.

If
\overline{u}\in\mathbf{BV}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
,
and
\widetilde{u}
is the
entropy admissible weak solution
to (
1.10
),
then the associated boundary inflow control

\widetilde{\gamma}\doteq f\circ\widetilde{u}(\cdot,0)
is a function of bounded variation on
(0,T)

as a consequence of a recent result obtained in
[
32
, Theorem 1.1]
.
More precisely the result is the following: for every
T>0
and for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
the map
t\to f(\widetilde{u}(t,x))
is in

\mathbf{BV}([0,T];\mathbb{R})
, with

\tv_{(0,T)}f(\widetilde{u}(\cdot\,,x))\leq C(\tv(\overline{u}),\|f^{\prime}\circ\overline{u}\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}}),

(2.12)

where
C(\tv(\overline{u}),\|f^{\prime}\circ\overline{u}\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}})
denotes an explicit constant only depending on
\tv(\overline{u})
and on
the Lipschitz constant of
\|f^{\prime}\circ\overline{u}\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}}
.

Thus, by Remark
2.2
, we have

\widetilde{\gamma}\in\mathcal{U}_{max}

and in particular the set
\mathcal{U}_{max}
is not empty.

In Appendix
A
we complement the result
[
32
, Theorem 1.1]
recalled in Remark
2.3
with a regularity result
for solutions of initial-boundary value problems.
More precisely in Theorem
A.1

we show that if
\overline{u}_{1}\in\mathbf{BV}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
,

k_{1}\in\mathbf{L^{\infty}}\left((0,T);[\theta,+\infty)\right)
with

f(k_{1})\in\mathbf{BV}((0,T);\mathbb{R})

then the flow map

t\mapsto f\big(u_{1}(\cdot,0)\big)
of the entropy admissible weak solution of (
1.5
)
is in
\mathbf{BV}((0,T);\mathbb{R})
.

This implies that in order to obtain admissible boundary inflow control
\gamma\in\mathcal{U}

that has bounded total variation it is sufficient to choose boundary data
k_{1},k_{2}

in (
1.5
), (
1.6
) with
f(k_{i})\in\mathbf{BV}((0,T);\mathbb{R})
.

Remark 2.4
.

Notice that to construct admissible inflow controls it is not necessary that the boundary data
k_{i}

are in
\mathbf{BV}((0,T);\mathbb{R})
, but only that
f(k_{i})
are in
\mathbf{BV}((0,T);\mathbb{R})
.
The requirement
f(k_{i})\in\mathbf{BV}((0,T);\mathbb{R})
is less restrictive:
indeed in general the entropy solution
\widetilde{u}
satisfies
f(\widetilde{u}(\cdot,0))\in\mathbf{BV}((0,T);\mathbb{R})
,
but it does not satisfy
\widetilde{u}(\cdot,0)\in\mathbf{BV}((0,T);\mathbb{R})
as shown by an explicit counterexample
in
[
32
]
.

Observe that, if
\gamma\in\mathbf{BV}\big([0,T];\,\mathbb{R}\big)
is an admissible junction inflow control,
according to
Definition

2.2
we get that

\tv_{(0,T)}(f\big(u_{1}(\cdot\,,0;\gamma)\big))=\tv_{(0,T)}(f(u_{2}(\cdot\,,0;\gamma))),

where the total variation is defined in (
2.2
).
We then introduce the following functional: for any
\gamma\in\mathcal{U}_{max}
, let

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{[0,T]}(\gamma)

\displaystyle\doteq\tv_{(0,T)}(f(u_{1}(\cdot,0;\gamma)))

(2.13)

\displaystyle\quad+|f(\overline{u}(0^{-}))-\gamma(0^{+})|+|f(u_{1}(T,0;\gamma))-\gamma(T^{-})|

\displaystyle\quad+|f(\overline{u}(0^{+}))-\gamma(0^{+})|+|f(u_{2}(T,0;\gamma))-\gamma(T^{-})|.

This definition of the functional
\mathcal{F}
aims to take into account also variations of
the flux at the node
x=0
which occur at the beginning and at the end of the interval of time
[0,T]
.

We study the following minimization problem

\inf_{\gamma\in{\mathcal{U}}_{max}}\mathcal{F}_{[0,T]}(\gamma).

(2.14)

Due to Theorem
2.1
and Remark

2.3

we get that

\inf_{\gamma\in{\mathcal{U}}_{max}}\mathcal{F}_{[0,T]}(\gamma)=\inf_{\gamma\in{\mathcal{U}}_{max}^{M}}\mathcal{F}_{[0,T]}(\gamma),

where

\mathcal{U}^{M}_{max}=\Big\{\gamma\in\mathcal{U}_{max}\ \big|\,\mathcal{F}_{[0,T]}(\gamma)\leq M\doteq\mathcal{F}_{[0,T]}f(\widetilde{u}(\cdot,0))\Big\}.

Note that this set is compact in
\mathbf{L}^{1}_{loc}
, as proved in
[
3
]
,
so we conclude the following existence result for (
2.14
).

Theorem 2.2
.

Given
T>0
, and
\overline{u}\in\mathbf{BV}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
, there exists a solution to (
2.14
).

In
[
3
]
existence of solutions to (
2.10
) and (
2.14
) have been proved in much more generality,
for networks with
n
incoming roads and
m
outgoing roads.

2.3
Main result

The main result of the note shows that the entropy admissible weak solution solves the minimization problem (
2.14
) when the initial datum
\overline{u}
is monotone. This provides an optimal control characterization of the entropic solution for monotone initial data. The precise statement of our result is the following.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\widetilde{u}
be the unique entropy admissible weak solution
to (
1.10
).
If the initial datum
\overline{u}
is monotone, then

\min_{\gamma\in{\mathcal{U}}^{M}_{max}}\mathcal{F}_{[0,T]}(\gamma)=\mathcal{F}_{[0,T]}(\widetilde{\gamma}),

where
\widetilde{\gamma}=f(\widetilde{u}(\cdot,0))
.

This Theorem follows directly from
Theorem

5.1
for monotone non-decreasing initial data and from
Theorem

5.2

for monotone non-increasing initial data.

On the other hand, without the monotonicity assumption on the initial data, we cannot expect that the entropy
solution minimizes the total variation functional
\mathcal{F}_{[0,T]}
. In particular in Section
4
,
we provide two prototypical examples of non-monotone initial data for which the minimum in (
2.14
)
is achieved by a not entropic solution.

In this section we provide the proof of

We start with a lemma, which will be useful in the following.

Lemma 3.1
.

1.

Let
u_{1},v_{1}
be two entropy admissible weak solutions to (
1.5
),
with the same initial datum
\overline{u}\in\mathbf{L^{\infty}}((-\infty,0];\mathbb{R})
and
such that

for almost every

.
Then
\int_{0}^{T}f(u_{1}(t,0))\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}f(v_{1}(t,0))\,dt
.

2.

Let
u_{2},v_{2}

be two entropy admissible weak solutions to (
1.6
), with the same initial datum
\overline{u}\in\mathbf{L^{\infty}}([0,+\infty);\mathbb{R})
and
such that
u_{2}(T,x)=v_{2}(T,x)
for almost every
x\geq 0
.
Then
\int_{0}^{T}f(u_{2}(t,0))\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}f(v_{2}(t,0))\,dt
.

Proof.

We prove only statement
1
, since the proof of
2
is completely analogous.

Take
L>\max\left\{\|f^{\prime}(u_{1})\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}([0,T]\times(-\infty,0))},\|f^{\prime}(u_{2})\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}([0,T]\times(-\infty,0))}\right\}
and observe that for all

, the values of entropy
admissible weak solutions

to (

) are
determined only by the initial data

: this means that

for a.e.

and

.

Then, by the Divergence Theorem we find that

\int_{0}^{T}f(u_{1}(t,0))dt=\int_{0}^{T}f(u_{1}(t,-LT^{+}))dt+\int_{-LT}^{0}(\overline{u}(x)-u_{1}(T,x))dx\\
=\int_{0}^{T}f(v_{1}(t,-LT^{+}))dt+\int_{-LT}^{0}(\overline{u}(x)-v_{1}(T,x))dx=\int_{0}^{T}f(v_{1}(t,0))dt.

∎

for

5

In this Section we provide the main result of the paper, showing that if the initial datum
\overline{u}
is
monotone, then the entropy weak solution to (
1.10
) is a solution to the max-min problem (
2.14
).
In particular, no procedures as the ones used in previous Section
4
can be implemented.

We start with two preliminary lemmata based on the Divergence Theorem.

Lemma 5.1
.

1.

Assume
\widetilde{u}
satisfies

\widetilde{u}(T,x)\leq\theta\qquad\text{for a.e. }x\in(-\infty,0).

Let
u
be a solution to the maximization problem (
2.10
), associated to an admissible
boundary inflow control
\gamma
. Then
u(T,0^{-};\gamma)=\widetilde{u}(T,0^{-})
.

2.

Assume
\widetilde{u}
satisfies

\widetilde{u}(T,x)\geq\theta,\qquad\text{for a.e. }x\in(0,+\infty).

Let
u
be a solution to the maximization problem (
2.10
), associated to an admissible boundary
inflow control
\gamma
. Then
u(T,0^{+};\gamma)=\widetilde{u}(T,0^{+})
.

Proof.

We prove only case 1. since case 2. is completely analogous.

Let
u(t,x;\gamma)
be a solution to the maximization problem (
2.10
) associated with
\gamma
.
Since
\gamma
is fixed, from now on we drop the dependence on
\gamma
, and we denote the solution as
u(t,x)
.

Let
L
be such that
L>\max\{\|f^{\prime}(u)\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}([0,T]\times(-\infty,0))},\|f^{\prime}(\widetilde{u})\|_{\mathbf{L^{\infty}}([0,T]\times(-\infty,0))}\}
. Then arguing exactly as in the proof of

Lemma

3.1
we have that
f(u(t,-LT^{+}))=f(\widetilde{u}(t,-LT^{+}))
for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
.

Now we claim that

u(T,x)=\widetilde{u}(T,x)
for
x\in(-LT,0)
.
Assume by contradiction that there exists a continuity point
\overline{x}\in(-LT,0)
for
u,\widetilde{u}
such
that
u(T,\overline{x})\neq\widetilde{u}(T,\overline{x})
.
Since the initial datum for
u
and
\widetilde{u}
is the same,
there exists
\overline{t}\in(0,T)
such that the
points
u(T,\overline{x})
and
u(\overline{t},0)
share the same generalized characteristic line.
Since the generalized characteristic lines cannot cross, for every
t\in[\overline{t},T]
, the characteristic
passing through
(t,0)
has negative speed. Therefore
for every
t\in[\overline{t},T]
, we have
u(t,0)>\theta
,
and then also
u(T,x)>\theta\geq\widetilde{u}(T,x)
for every
x\in[\overline{x},0]
.

On the basis of the previous argument, we define

\hat{x}:=\inf\{x\in[-LT,0]\ :\ u(T,x)\neq\widetilde{u}(T,x)\}=\inf\{x\in[-LT,0]\ :\ u(T,x)>\widetilde{u}(T,x)\}.

Therefore, by the previous argument we get that
u(T,x)>\theta\geq\widetilde{u}(T,x)
for every
x\in(\hat{x},0]
.
Using the Divergence Theorem,
we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}f(u(t,0))dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}f(u(t,-LT))dt+\int_{-LT}^{0}u(0,x)dx-\int_{-LT}^{0}u(T,x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}f(\widetilde{u}(t,-LT))dt+\int_{-LT}^{0}\overline{u}_{1}(x)dx-\int_{-LT}^{0}u(T,x)dx

\displaystyle<\int_{0}^{T}f(\widetilde{u}(t,-LT))dt+\int_{-LT}^{0}\overline{u}_{1}(x)dx-\int_{-LT}^{0}\widetilde{u}(T,x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}f(\widetilde{u}(t,0))dt,

that contradicts the fact that
\widetilde{u}
is a solution to (
2.10
).
Therefore, we get that

for a.e.
x\in(-\infty,0)
.
∎

is
non-decreasing.

In particular, denoting
\widetilde{\gamma}=f(\widetilde{u}(\cdot,0))

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{[0,T]}(\widetilde{\gamma})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tv_{(0,T)}(f(\widetilde{u}(\cdot,0)))+|f(\overline{u}(0^{-}))-\widetilde{\gamma}(0^{+})|+|f(\overline{u}(0^{+}))-\widetilde{\gamma}(0^{+})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{\gamma}(T)-\widetilde{\gamma}(0)+|f(\overline{u}(0^{-}))-\widetilde{\gamma}(0^{+})|+|f(\overline{u}(0^{+}))-\widetilde{\gamma}(0^{+})|.

Proof.

As before, we
rely on the properties of generalized characteristics, see
[
30
]
.

We show that
the function
\widetilde{u}(t,\cdot)
is continuous.

We fix
t=T
, but the argument is completely analogous for a generic

.
Assume by contradiction that there exists
\widetilde{x}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\widetilde{u}(T,\widetilde{x}^{-})\neq\widetilde{u}(T,\widetilde{x}^{+})
. Denote with
\xi^{-}
and

\xi^{+}
respectively the minimal and the maximal backward characteristics from
(T,\widetilde{x})
.
Since
\xi^{-}(0)<\xi^{+}(0)

and
\overline{u}
non-increasing, we get
\overline{u}(\xi^{-}(0)^{+})\geq\overline{u}(\xi^{+}(0)^{-})
.
This is not compatible with the fact that
\dot{\xi}^{-}(t)=f^{\prime}(\widetilde{u}(T,\bar{x}^{-}))\leq\dot{\xi}^{+}(t)=f^{\prime}(\widetilde{u}(T,\bar{x}^{+}))
if
\overline{u}(\xi^{-}(0)^{+})\leq\theta
and with the fact that
\dot{\xi}^{-}(t)=f^{\prime}(\widetilde{u}(T,\bar{x}^{-}))\geq\dot{\xi}^{+}(t)=f^{\prime}(\widetilde{u}(T,\bar{x}^{+}))
if
\overline{u}(\xi^{+}(0)^{-})\geq\theta

(see
[
31

).

We prove now that the map
t\to f(\widetilde{u}(t,\hat{x}))
is non-decreasing.
Without loss of generality we assume that
\hat{x}=0
.
By contradiction assume that there exist
0<t_{1}<t_{2}\leq T
such that

f(\widetilde{u}(t_{1},0))>f(\widetilde{u}(t_{2},0))
.

Denote with
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}
the genuine backward characteristics from
(t_{1},0)
and
(t_{2},0)
.

1.

If
\xi_{1}(0)=\xi_{2}(0)
, then a rarefaction wave is generated at
\xi_{1}(0)
.
In particular
\widetilde{u}(t_{1},0)=(f^{\prime})^{-1}(-\xi_{1}(0)/t_{1})

and
\widetilde{u}(t_{2},0)=(f^{\prime})^{-1}(-\xi_{1}(0)/t_{2})
and the traces

\widetilde{u}(t_{1},0),\widetilde{u}(t_{2},0)
belong to
[\overline{u}(\xi_{1}(0)^{+}),\overline{u}(\xi_{1}(0)^{-})]
.

If
\xi_{1}(0)\leq 0
, we have that
\overline{u}(\xi_{1}(0)^{-})\leq\theta
and then
\widetilde{u}(t_{1},0),\widetilde{u}(t_{2},0)\leq\theta
. Using the fact that
f
is strictly concave, we get that
\widetilde{u}(t_{1},0^{-})=(f^{\prime})^{-1}(-\xi_{1}(0)/t_{1})<(f^{\prime})^{-1}(-\xi_{1}(0)/t_{2})=\widetilde{u}(t_{2},0^{-})
, and so we conclude that
f(\widetilde{u}(t_{1},0))<f(\widetilde{u}(t_{2},0))
, in contradiction with our assumption.

If
\xi_{1}(0)>0
, we have that
\widetilde{u}(t_{1},0),\widetilde{u}(t_{2},0)\geq\theta
, and we argue exactly as before, by symmetry and obtain
f(\widetilde{u}(t_{1},0))<f(\widetilde{u}(t_{2},0))
, in contradiction with our assumption.

So, this case is not possible.

2.

If
\xi_{1}(0)>\xi_{2}(0)
, then, necessarily
\xi_{2}(0)\leq 0
, and
\widetilde{u}(t_{2},0)\leq\theta
.
Moreover by monotonicity of
\overline{u}
, we have that

\widetilde{u}(t_{1},0)\leq\overline{u}(\xi_{1}(0)^{-})\leq\overline{u}(\xi_{2}(0)^{+})\leq\widetilde{u}(t_{2},0)\leq\theta
. Therefore,
we conclude that
f(\widetilde{u}(t_{1},0^{-}))\leq f(\widetilde{u}(t_{2},0^{-}))
, in contradiction with our assumption.

3.

If
\xi_{1}(0)<\xi_{2}(0)
, then, necessarily
\xi_{1}(0)\geq 0
, and
\widetilde{u}(t_{1},0),\widetilde{u}(t_{2},0)\geq\theta
.
Recalling the monotonicity of
\overline{u}
, we get

\widetilde{u}(t_{1},0)\geq\overline{u}(\xi_{1}(0)^{+})\geq\overline{u}(\xi_{2}(0)^{-})\geq\widetilde{u}(t_{2},0)\geq\theta
. So
we conclude that
f(\widetilde{u}(t_{1},0^{-}))\leq f(\widetilde{u}(t_{2},0^{-}))
, in contradiction with our assumption.

So, none of the previous cases is possible, and the proof is concluded.
∎

Lemma 5.6

Assume that

\overline{u}

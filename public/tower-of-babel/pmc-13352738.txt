Linalool targets NR3C2 to inhibit NF-κB-mediated gastric cancer progression
Authors: Ying Hu, Shunfeng Chen, Yu Wang, Ling Liu, Chaoyi Zhang, Hong Chen, Yuanyuan Duanmu, Zhaotian Su, Jun Ma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Gastric cancer (GC) is a major global health challenge. This study explores the efficacy of linalool in GC and further elucidates its underlying mechanisms. Linalool inhibited the proliferation, migration, and invasion of GC cells and prevented transplanted tumor growth in nude mice. Linalool directly interacted with and stabilized the NR3C2 protein, inhibiting its proteasomal degradation. Functional rescue experiments demonstrated that knockdown of NR3C2 partially reversed the antitumor effects of linalool. Further studies revealed that NR3C2 transcriptionally activated NFKBIZ and inhibited NF-κB signaling, and restoring NFKBIZ expression reversed GC progression caused by NR3C2 deficiency. In clinical samples, the expression levels of NR3C2 and NFKBIZ were lower in GC tissues than in adjacent normal tissues, and the two markers were positively correlated in the tumor tissues. Furthermore, low expression of both NR3C2 and NFKBIZ was significantly associated with higher T stages and more advanced pTNM stages and was accompanied by NF-κB signaling activation and elevated levels of inflammatory cytokines in tumor tissues. Overall, linalool inhibits GC progression by upregulating NR3C2 and transcriptionally activating NFKBIZ, thereby suppressing NF-κB signaling. Low NR3C2/NFKBIZ expression is associated with adverse clinical and pathological features in GC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13008-026-00188-y.

Introduction

Gastric cancer (GC) is a significant global health concern, with an annual incidence of nearly 1 million new cases and more than 650,000 deaths worldwide [1]. Patients frequently exhibit no symptoms, or they manifest nonspecific symptoms, such as weight loss, abdominal pain, or nausea, which can impede an expeditious diagnosis [2]. Despite being the gold standard for GC diagnosis, the cost, invasiveness, and specialized requirements of endoscopic biopsy and pathological examination have hindered their widespread use in screening [3]. GC is characterized by significant molecular and phenotypic heterogeneity, and the primary treatment modalities for GC include endoscopic resection for early-stage disease, adjuvant chemotherapy for patients with stage 1B or higher GC, and sequential chemotherapy regimens for advanced GC [4]. However, given the dismal 5-year survival rate and the increasing number of annual new cases and deaths, early screening strategies and new therapeutic regimens for GC are urgently needed [5].

Traditional Chinese medicine and natural products can effectively inhibit tumor cell metastasis, proliferation, and invasion, and induce GC apoptosis [6]. Linalool (2,6-dimethyl-2,7-octadien-6-ol) is a naturally occurring substance found in various essential oils and possesses bioactive properties, including anticancer, neuroprotective, antidepressant, and hepatoprotective effects [7]. For example, linalool exerts an inhibitory effect on the proliferation of human prostate cancer 22Rv1 cells and induces apoptosis [8]. Linalool (0–2.0 mM) inhibits lung adenocarcinoma cell proliferation and induces cell death [9]. However, it is not yet fully elucidated how linalool exerts its anticancer effects on GC cells.

Nuclear receptor subfamily 3, group C, member 2 (NR3C2, also known as the mineralocorticoid receptor) primarily functions as a nuclear receptor that regulates the transcription of target genes [10]. NR3C2 expression is significantly decreased in colorectal cancer and is correlated with distant metastasis, poor prognosis, and advanced stages [11]. Overexpression of NR3C2 inhibits the proliferation and invasion of colon cancer cells [12]. A recent study suggested that NR3C2 is associated with GC progression and prognosis [13]. Our preliminary analysis identified NR3C2 as a downstream target of linalool. Whether linalool exerts anticancer effects in GC by regulating NR3C2 expression remains to be experimentally verified.

NFKBIZ (IκBζ) is a member of the nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB) inhibitory (IκB) family and exerts significant effects on various biological activities in human diseases [14]. Elevated NFKBIZ expression inhibits the NF-κB pathway, thereby blocking the progression of hepatocellular carcinoma [15]. Activation of NF-κB signaling can induce GC development by regulating cytokines/chemokines, growth factors, and antiapoptotic factors [16]. Based on our preliminary analysis identifying NFKBIZ as a downstream target of NR3C2, we hypothesized that NR3C2 transcriptionally activates NFKBIZ, which in turn suppresses NF-κB signaling. We further propose that the NR3C2/NFKBIZ/NF-κB axis mediates the protective effect of linalool in GC.

Results

Linalool inhibits GC progression in vitro and in vivo

CCK-8 assay demonstrated that linalool exhibited dose-dependent inhibition of GC cell viability. However, as the linalool concentration increased, the inhibitory effect on GC cell viability plateaued. Therefore, 100 µM was selected for the subsequent assays (Fig. 1A). Colony formation assay revealed that treatment with 100 µM linalool significantly suppressed the ability of GC cells to form colonies (Fig. 1B). Transwell assays revealed that linalool markedly reduced the number of GC cells migrated or invaded into the lower chamber (Fig. 1C-D). TUNEL results showed that linalool notably promoted apoptosis in GC cells (Fig. 1E).

The antitumor efficacy of linalool was investigated in nude mice. AGS cells were subcutaneously injected into nude mice. As expected, linalool treatment at 200 mg/kg significantly inhibited subcutaneous tumor growth and weight (Fig. 1F-G).

CCK-8 analysis showed that treatment with 0.1% DMSO alone did not significantly affect GC cell viability compared to the untreated control group (Fig. S1A). To evaluate the selectivity and safety of linalool, we performed a CCK-8 assay on GES1 cells. No significant decrease in GES1 cell viability was observed following treatment with linalool at concentrations ranging from 25 to 100 µM, and inhibition was observed only at higher concentrations (150 and 200 µM) (Fig. S1B). These results suggest that 100 µM linalool exhibits significant inhibitory effects on AGS and HGC-27 cells while exhibiting relatively limited toxicity toward normal GES1 cells. Under the in vivo dosing regimen, there was no significant difference in body weight changes between the 200 mg/kg linalool-treated and vehicle groups (Fig. S1C). Hematoxylin-eosin (HE) staining results also showed no obvious pathological damage in the heart, liver, lung, or kidney tissues of the nude mice (Fig. S1D). These results indicate that under the dosing conditions used in this study, linalool was well tolerated in vivo and exhibited no significant systemic toxicity.

Linalool directly interacts with NR3C2 and inhibits the proteasomal degradation of NR3C2

Transcriptome sequencing data from 205 GC tissues and 12 adjacent normal gastric tissues in the GSE26942 dataset were analyzed using the GEO database. Genes with adj. p < 0.05 were selected as differentially expressed genes (DEGs) (Fig. 2A). The SMILES structure of linalool (CC(= CCCC(C)(C = C)O)C) was obtained from PubChem (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) (Fig. 2B). Using the SMILES structure, we predicted the potential protein targets of linalool using SwissTargetPrediction (http://swisstargetprediction.ch/). Among these, 36 targets with binding potential were selected for further analyses. Cross-screening of the two datasets yielded 16 intersecting genes (Fig. 2C). These intersecting genes were subjected to protein-protein interaction analysis using the STRING database (https://string-db.org/), with the minimum required interaction score set at high confidence (0.700). As shown in Fig. 2D, there was an interaction between PGR (log2FoldChange = 0.176), NR3C2 (log2FoldChange = -0.908), and NR3C1 (log2FoldChange = -0.37). We selected NR3C2 for further analysis because of its more significant differential expression. An analysis of patient data using the Kaplan-Meier Plotter (https://kmplot.com/) indicated that GC patients with high NR3C2 expression had improved overall survival rates (Fig. 2E).

NR3C2 expression in human gastric mucosal epithelial cells (GES1) was significantly higher than that in GC cells (AGS and HGC-27) (Fig. 2F-G). WB analysis showed that NR3C2 expression in GC cells was upregulated in a dose-dependent manner with increasing linalool concentrations and tended to stabilize at 100 µM (Fig. 2H).

To further verify whether linalool can interact with NR3C2, we first conducted a drug affinity-responsive target stabilization (DARTS) assay. Under Pronase treatment conditions, the retention of NR3C2 protein in AGS and HGC-27 cells increased following treatment with 100 µM linalool. After normalization against the corresponding input control, the retention ratio of NR3C2 in the linalool group was higher than that in the DMSO group (Fig. 2I). Further analysis using the cellular thermal shift assay (CETSA) revealed that the thermal stability of NR3C2 within the cells was significantly increased following treatment with 100 µM linalool (Fig. 2J). In addition, bio-linalool was effectively enriched from GC cell lysates in the presence of NR3C2, whereas this enrichment was significantly reduced when excess linalool was added (Fig. 2K). The results of the (CHX) chase assay showed that under conditions where CHX blocked new protein synthesis, linalool treatment significantly slowed the rate of decline in NR3C2 protein levels in AGS and HGC-27 cells compared to the DMSO group, thereby significantly prolonging its half-life. Furthermore, when DMSO treatment was combined with the proteasome inhibitor MG132, NR3C2 protein degradation was similarly inhibited, and its half-life was prolonged, consistent with the trend observed following linalool treatment (Fig. 2L). These results indicate that the upregulation of NR3C2 protein levels by linalool is at least partially associated with the inhibition of its proteasome-dependent degradation.

NR3C2 knockdown reverses the anti-cancer effects of linalool in vitro and in vivo

To demonstrate that linalool exerts its anticancer effects by activating NR3C2 expression, GC cells were infected with lentiviral vectors containing a human NR3C2 knockdown. KD-NR3C2 #1 and KD-NR3C2 #3, which exhibited higher knockdown efficiency (Fig. 3A-B), were selected for subsequent experiments. Through colony formation (Fig. 3C), Transwell migration (Fig. 3D), and invasion (Fig. 3E) assays and TUNEL (Fig. 3F) detection, two independent shRNA-mediated knockdowns of NR3C2 consistently enhanced the proliferation, migration, and invasion of GC cells and increased the apoptosis rate in the presence of linalool.

Based on the results of our preliminary screening, we selected KD-NR3C2#1, which exhibited a higher knockdown efficiency and greater stability, for in vivo validation. Exogenous NR3C2 inhibition accelerated tumor progression in vivo, as evidenced by the increased tumor volume (Fig. 3G) and weight (Fig. 3H).

NR3C2 binds to the NFKBIZ promoter and activates NFKBIZ transcription, thereby inhibiting NF-κB signaling

All potential downstream factors regulated by NR3C2 were obtained from the hTFtarget database (https://guolab.wchscu.cn/). The DEGs screened from the GSE26942 dataset, and the potential downstream factors regulated by NR3C2 were cross-screened to obtain 83 intersecting genes (Fig. S2A). KEGG enrichment analysis was performed on the intersecting genes, and genes enriched in “Transcriptional misregulation in cancer” potentially caused by NR3C2 dysregulation were selected for analysis (Fig. S2B). Four genes were present in this pathway: NFKBIZ (log2FoldChange = -0.724), DDX5 (log2FoldChange = 0.239), HIST1H3H (log2FoldChange = 0.312), and H3F3B (log2FoldChange = -0.759). NFKBIZ and H3F3B expression was reduced in GC tissues in the GSE26942 dataset. A correlation analysis was conducted using GEPIA2 (http://gepia2.cancer-pku.cn/) to ascertain the relationship between NFKBIZ/H3F3B and NR3C2 expressions. The findings of this analysis suggest a positive correlation between NFKBIZ and NR3C2 expression. In contrast, H3F3B expression was not correlated with NR3C2 expression (Fig. S2C). The Kaplan-Meier plotter showed that GC patients with high NFKBIZ expression had better overall survival (Fig. S2D). In ChIP-seq analysis at UCSC (http://genome.ucsc.edu/cgi-bin/), NR3C2 was found to have a significant binding peak in the promoter region of NFKBIZ (chr3:101849239–101849586) (Fig. S2E).

NFKBIZ expression was significantly downregulated in GC cells (Fig. 4A-B). ChIP-qPCR analysis showed that anti-NR3C2 enriched a large number of NFKBIZ promoter fragments (Fig. 4C). In GC cells, exogenous inhibition of NR3C2 also downregulated luciferase activity in GC cells transfected with NFKBIZ promoter sequences (Fig. 4D). NFKBIZ mRNA levels were also downregulated in GC cells with NR3C2 knockdown (Fig. 4E).

Linalool downregulated the protein levels of p65 and p-p65 and upregulated NFKBIZ expression, which was abated by the silencing of NR3C2 (Fig. 4F). WB analysis revealed that linalool treatment did not result in any significant changes in the expression of IkappaBalpha (IκBα) or p-IκBα in AGS or HGC-27 cells, and no significant changes in the expression of these proteins were observed following the combined knockdown of NR3C2 (Fig. 4G). Similarly, neither linalool treatment nor combined NR3C2 knockdown resulted in any significant changes in the p65 nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratio in AGS and HGC-27 cells (Fig. 4H). The NF-κB luciferase assay results showed that linalool significantly reduced NF-κB luciferase activity, which was partially reversed following NR3C2 knockdown (Fig. 4I). Simultaneously, RT-qPCR results showed that linalool downregulated the mRNA expression levels of CCND1 and BCL2, representative downstream target genes of NF-κB, and the expression of these genes increased to varying degrees following NR3C2 knockdown in addition to linalool treatment (Fig. 4J). These results suggest that the suppression of NF-κB signaling by the linalool/NR3C2/NFKBIZ axis primarily manifests as a decreased expression of p65 and p-p65 and a reduced downstream transcriptional output, rather than through effects on classic upstream events related to IκBα or the nuclear-cytoplasmic distribution of p65.

Overexpression of NFKBIZ prevents GC progression in the presence of NR3C2 knockdown

GC cells were infected with lentiviral vectors expressing NR3C2 knockdown and NFKBIZ overexpression, and stably infected cells were screened. WB analysis confirmed the upregulation of NFKBIZ expression (Fig. 5A), leading to a significant downregulation of p65 and p-p65 expression in GC cells (Fig. 5B). Similarly, NF-κB luciferase assays showed that combined overexpression of NFKBIZ significantly reduced NF-κB transcriptional activity (Fig. 5C). In addition to the combined knockdown of NR3C2 and linalool, NFKBIZ overexpression significantly downregulated the expression of CCND1 and BCL2 (Fig. 5D).

Reactivation of NFKBIZ inhibited GC cell colony formation (Fig. 5E), migration (Fig. 5F), and invasion (Fig. 5G), and the apoptosis rate was significantly increased (Fig. 5H). In vivo, the growth rate of subcutaneous xenograft tumors formed by AGS cells overexpressing NFKBIZ was inhibited (Fig. 5I), and tumor weight was significantly reduced (Fig. 5J).

NFKBIZ knockdown attenuates the anticancer effects of linalool by reactivating the NF-κB signaling pathway

To further elucidate the role of the NFKBIZ/NF-κB axis in the inhibition of GC progression by linalool, we knocked down NFKBIZ in the GC cells. First, results from RT-qPCR (Fig. 6A) and WB (Fig. 6B) showed that both mRNA and protein levels of NFKBIZ were significantly lower in the KD-NFKBIZ group than in the KD-Scramble group, indicating that the NFKBIZ knockdown model was successfully established. Next, the NF-κB inhibitor SC75741 was applied to GC cells with or without NFKBIZ knockdown. NF-κB luciferase assays revealed that NF-κB transcriptional activity in GC cells was significantly reduced following linalool treatment. However, when NFKBIZ was knocked down in the presence of linalool, NF-κB transcriptional activity increased again, and NF-κB transcriptional activity was reduced with or without NFKBIZ knockdown (Fig. 6C). Functional assays demonstrated that NFKBIZ knockdown attenuated the inhibitory effects of linalool on GC cell proliferation (Fig. 6D), migration (Fig. 6E), and invasion (Fig. 6F), and reduced apoptosis (Fig. 6G), whereas combined treatment with SC75741 reversed these changes. Treatment with SC75741 alone exhibited a trend similar to that observed with linalool.

NR3C2 and NFKBIZ expression are positively correlated in GC tissue

Finally, we collected tumor and adjacent tissue samples from 42 patients with GC. Of these, 18 pairs of tissue samples were selected for molecular analysis. RT-qPCR analysis revealed that NR3C2 and NFKBIZ expression was significantly higher in adjacent tissues than in tumor tissues (Fig. 7A). A substantial positive correlation was identified between NR3C2 and NFKBIZ mRNA expression in tumor tissue (Fig. 7B). WB analysis showed that p65 and p-p65 levels were significantly higher in tumor tissues than in adjacent tissues (Fig. 7C). Elevated levels of inflammatory factors, specifically IL-1β and IL-8, were observed in tumor tissues compared to adjacent tissues (Fig. 7D).

Discussion

Linalool, a naturally occurring terpene, is present in significant quantities in various plants, including lavender, rosewood, ylang-ylang, bergamot, geranium, and jasmine [17]. This study demonstrated that linalool upregulated NR3C2 expression in GC cells, activated NFKBIZ transcription, inhibited NF-κB signal transduction, and thereby hindered GC progression (Fig. 8).

Fig. 8Experimental mechanism diagram. Linalool upregulates NR3C2 expression in GC cells, further activating NFKBIZ, inhibiting NF-κB signaling, and thereby inhibiting GC progression

Experimental mechanism diagram. Linalool upregulates NR3C2 expression in GC cells, further activating NFKBIZ, inhibiting NF-κB signaling, and thereby inhibiting GC progression

Linalool has been shown to induce G0/G1 arrest in U937 cells and G2/M arrest in HeLa cells, thereby halting their progression through the cell cycle [18]. Moreover, linalool exerts pro-apoptotic and antiproliferative effects on breast cancer cells in a concentration- and time-dependent manner [19]. Linalool induced apoptosis in colon cancer cells, with a 55% reduction in xenograft tumor weight [20]. Consistently, our experimental results revealed that linalool suppressed the malignant phenotypes of GC cells and the growth of subcutaneous tumors in nude mice. Ocimum x africanum essential oil significantly inhibited human GC cell growth, and linalool was a major chemical constituent [21]. Our study directly confirms the antitumor effect of linalool on GC and suggests that linalool may be a complementary or alternative agent that improves GC treatment. Subsequent studies must be conducted to perform pharmacokinetic analyses and optimize doses to determine clinically relevant concentrations.

For the downstream target of linalool, we focused on NR3C2. Aloin activated NR3C2 to suppress the malignant behavior of lung squamous cell carcinoma cells [22]. Our preliminary prediction results showed that GC patients with high NR3C2 expression had better overall survival in the online database. NR3C2 was reduced in GC cell lines compared to gastric epithelial cells and was upregulated by linalool treatment. Consistently, high NR3C2 expression in invasive breast carcinoma is associated with prolonged overall survival, progression-free interval, and disease-specific survival [23]. NR3C2 downregulation in colon cancer was correlated with poor prognosis and immune infiltration, and overexpression of NR3C2 induced apoptosis and suppressed cell proliferation [24]. In the present study, NR3C2 knockdown promoted GC cell growth in vitro and tumor growth in vivo in the presence of linalool. NR3C2 inhibits the migration, invasion, epithelial-mesenchymal transition, and glucose metabolism reprogramming of pancreatic cancer cells [25, 26]. Our study is the first to support the antitumor effect of NR3C2 in GC using experimental data.

We focused on NFKBIZ as the downstream target of NR3C2. NFKBIZ expression was significantly downregulated in GC cells, and patients with high NFKBIZ expression exhibited improved overall survival. NFKBIZ expression is reduced in human bladder cancer tissues, and patients with high NFKBIZ expression demonstrate an enhanced overall survival rate [27]. A decrease in NFKBIZ expression was also identified in hepatocellular carcinoma tissues, and patients with high NFKBIZ expression demonstrated prolonged overall survival and a reduced probability of recurrence [15]. Additionally, NFKBIZ expression was positively correlated with that of NR3C2. NFKBIZ has been reported to inhibit p65 transactivation and DNA binding [28], whereas linalool in essential oils may exert anti-inflammatory effects by reducing NF-κB p65 expression [29]. In the present study, exogenous inhibition of NR3C2 downregulated NFKBIZ mRNA levels and upregulated p65 and p-p65 expression in GC cells. Furthermore, our results confirmed that in the presence of linalool treatment and NR3C2 knockdown, NFKBIZ overexpression inhibited GC cell growth in vitro and tumor growth in vivo. NFKBIZ overexpression suppresses bladder cancer cell proliferation, migration, and invasion, and significantly decreases tumor weight and volume in vivo [27]. NF-κB activation is primarily facilitated through the process of IkappaB kinase-mediated phosphorylation of inhibitory molecules, including IκBα, and optimal induction of NF-κB target genes also requires phosphorylation of NF-kappaB proteins, such as p65, within their transactivation domain by a variety of kinases in response to distinct stimuli [30]. Here, we found that the suppression of the NF-κB pathway by NFKBIZ mainly involves the inhibition of p65 phosphorylation rather than IκBα phosphorylation.

Finally, clinical sample detection confirmed the under-expressed levels of NR3C2 and NFKBIZ in GC tissue, as well as their positive correlation. It has been determined that the predominant process in the development of GC is inflammation-induced tumorigenesis [31]. Levels of p65, p-p65, IL-1β, and IL-8 were elevated in tumor tissues compared to adjacent tissues from GC patients. Both low NR3C2 expression and low NFKBIZ expression were significantly associated with higher T stages and more advanced pTNM stages in our GC cohort, which was consistent with a recent study in colorectal cancer [32].

In summary, the present study offers further evidence of the antitumor effect of linalool in GC, thereby corroborating the mechanism of the NR3C2/NFKBIZ/NF-κB signaling pathway.

Materials and methods

Clinical samples

From June 2020 to August 2025, 42 human primary GC and adjacent tissue samples were collected in Nanjing Jiangning Hospital of Chinese Medicine, Affiliated Jiangning Hospital of Chinese Medicine, China Pharmaceutical University (18 pairs of samples were used for RT-qPCR, Western blot, and the detection of inflammation-related markers, while all 42 samples were used for immunohistochemistry and the evaluation of clinical-pathological correlations.). All samples were surgical cases and confirmed by histopathology and clinical diagnosis. It should be noted that none of the patients had undergone radiotherapy or chemotherapy. This clinical research protocol was reviewed and approved by the ethics committee of Nanjing Jiangning Hospital of Chinese Medicine, Affiliated Jiangning Hospital of Chinese Medicine, China Pharmaceutical University, and all patients signed informed consent forms. The study was conducted under the Declaration of Helsinki.

Cell culture

Human GC cells AGS (iCell-h016, Cellverse, Shanghai, China), HGC-27 (AW-CNH127, Abiowell, Changsha, Hunan, China), and human gastric mucosal epithelial cells GES1 (iCell-h062, Cellverse) were cultured in an incubator with 95% air and 5% CO2 at 37℃. AGS cells were cultured using F12K medium (iCell-0007, Cellverse), HGC-27 cells using high-glucose DMEM complete medium (AW-MC001, Abiowell), and GES1 cells using RPMI-1640 medium (iCell-0002, Cellverse). All media were supplemented with 10% FBS and 1% P/S. All cells listed above were purchased in January 2025. All cell lines underwent STR profiling by the manufacturers, with the most recent STR profiling conducted in December 2025. None of the cells was contaminated with mycoplasma.

Cell treatment

Linalool (HY-N0368, MedChemExpress, Monmouth Junction, NJ, USA) and the NF-κB inhibitor SC75741 (HY-10496, MedChemExpress) were both dissolved in DMSO to prepare stock solutions, which were diluted to the desired working concentrations with the appropriate complete medium before use. Unless otherwise specified, all cell experiments involved treatment with 100 µM linalool or 5 µM SC75741 [33] for 24 h. The control group was supplemented with an equal volume of DMSO, and the final concentration in each group was maintained at 0.1% (v/v).

Cell viability assay

CCK-8 kits (A311-02, Vazyme, Nanjing, Jiangsu, China) were used. In brief, GC cells and GES1 cells were seeded at 1 × 104 cells/well in the 96-well plate and pre-incubated for 24 h. Next, cells were cultured with linalool at 0, 25, 50, 75, 100, 150, and 200 µM dissolved in 0.1% (v/v) DMSO for 24 h. Then, 10 µL of CCK-8 was supplemented to each well for 4 hours, and the absorbance at 450 nm was measured.

Lentiviral infection

Lentiviral vectors expressing shRNA targeting human NR3C2 or NFKBIZ (Table 2) and a scrambled control vector, as well as lentiviral vectors expressing the human NFKBIZ gene and a lentiviral control vector, were all procured from VectorBuilder (Guangzhou, Guangdong, China). Lentiviral vectors were used at a multiplicity of infection of 10, and AGS and HGC-27 cells at 50% confluence were incubated with 6 µg/mL polybrene (C0351, Beyotime, Shanghai, China) for 24 h. Cells were screened with puromycin for 10 days to establish stably infected cell lines. Western blot (WB) analysis was used to assess knockdown or overexpression efficiency, and RT-qPCR was employed to validate knockdown efficiency.

Colony formation assay

AGS and HGC-27 cells were seeded at 800 cells/well in the 6-well plate for 2 weeks. Upon the colonies’ becoming observable, they were subjected to fixation with 4% (v/v) paraformaldehyde (PFA), staining with 0.5% (w/v) crystal violet (C0121, Beyotime), and subsequently imaged.

Transwell assays

For the invasion assay, Matrigel (CLS354248, Merck KGaA, Darmstadt, Germany) was diluted and evenly coated on the upper chamber of the Transwell. AGS and HGC-27 cells in the logarithmic growth phase were seeded in the Transwell 24-well plate (CLS3422, Merck KGaA) and incubated in serum-free medium for 24 h. A serum-rich medium was loaded into the lower chamber. For migration experiments, Matrigel was not utilized; all other steps were the same as for the invasion experiment. Following a 24-hour migration period, the cells that migrated into the lower chamber were fixed with 4% (v/v) PFA, stained with 0.1% (w/v) crystal violet, and imaged under a microscope.

TUNEL

One-Step TUNEL Apoptosis Assay Kit (C1086, Beyotime) was used to assess GC cell apoptosis. AGS and HGC-27 cells were fixed with 4% (v/v) PFA for 30 min and incubated with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) containing 0.3% Triton X-100 at room temperature for 5 min for permeabilization. Next, cells were treated with 50 µL of TUNEL detection solution at 37℃ in the dark for 60 min. Cell nuclei were counterstained with DAPI (E-IR-R103, Elabscience, Houston, TX, USA). Analysis of apoptosis rates (number of TUNEL-positive cells / total number of DAPI-stained cells) in random fields of view was performed using ImageJ software.

Xenograft experiment

Five-week-old, 15–19 g, male BALB/c nude mice (401, Vital River, Beijing, China) were kept at 23℃, a humidity of 50%, and a 12-hour light/12-hour dark cycle. This animal experiment was approved by China Pharmaceutical University’s (approval no. JZL-2023-N14) ethics review committee and followed animal care and use guidelines.

Nude mice were acclimatized for one week before the experiment. AGS cells (1 × 10⁶) from different treatment groups were suspended in 100 µL of PBS and injected subcutaneously into the right flank of the mice.

Linalool was prepared according to the in vivo administration formulation recommended by the manufacturer (MedChemExpress), with linalool dissolved in a solvent consisting of 10% DMSO, 40% PEG-300, 5% Tween-80, and 45% saline. When the tumor volume reached about 100 mm³, administration of an equal volume of vehicle or 200 mg/kg linalool was initiated every 3 days via gavage [20]. The body weight of the mice was measured once daily after dosing, and the size of the subcutaneous tumor was measured at three-day intervals. The tumor volume was calculated using the formula length × width² × 0.5236 [34]. After 21 days, all mice were euthanized by sodium pentobarbital at 150 mg/kg, and tumors were harvested, weighed, and used for subsequent experiments. At the same time, heart, liver, lung, and kidney tissues were harvested from nude mice, fixed in 4% PFA, and stained with HE.

HE staining

The HE staining kit (PK10031, Proteintech, Wuhan, Hubei, China) was used for histological analysis of nude mouse organs. Paraffin-embedded sections of heart, liver, lung, and kidney tissues from nude mice were deparaffinized in water, sequentially stained with hematoxylin for 2 min, counterstained for 2 min, and stained with eosin for 3 min. The sections were subsequently dehydrated in a series of alcohol solutions, cleared in xylene, and mounted for observation of histopathological changes under a light microscope.

RT-qPCR

Total RNA was isolated from cells and tumor and adjacent tissue samples from GC patients using Trizol (15596018CN, Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA). The synthesis of cDNA was accomplished using the High-Capacity cDNA Reverse Transcription Kit (4368814, Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA, USA). qPCR was performed using the CFX96 Touch Deep Well Real-Time PCR Detection System (Bio-Rad Laboratories, Hercules, CA, USA), PowerUp SYBR Green Master Mix (A25742, Applied Biosystems), and gene-specific primers (Table 3) purchased from Origene (Wuxi, Jiangsu, China) to assess the levels of target genes relative to GAPDH. Relative mRNA expression was calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method.

WB

Proteins were extracted from GC cells (AGS and HGC-27) and tumor and adjacent tissue samples from GC patients using SDS lysis buffer (PR20002, Proteintech) supplemented with protease inhibitor cocktail (PR20038, Proteintech). After quantifying protein concentration using the BCA assay kit (PK10026, Proteintech), proteins were separated by 8%-12% SDS-PAGE. Samples were transferred to PVDF membranes (E801-01, Vazyme) and blocked with BSA. The following antibodies, NR3C2 (1:1000, A3308, ABclonal, Wuhan, Hubei, China), NFKBIZ (1:1000, 9244, CST, Beverly, MA, USA), p65 (1:200, 51–0500, Invitrogen), Phospho-p65 (1:1000, MA5-37156, Invitrogen), IκBα (1:1000, 4812, CST), Phospho-IκBα (1:1000, 2859, CST), IL-1β (1:1000, 12703, CST), IL-8 (1:1000, 94407, CST), GAPDH (1:25000, 81640-5-RR, Proteintech), Histone H3 (1:1000, 9715, CST), and α-Tubulin (1:1000, 2125, CST) were incubated with the membrane at 4℃ overnight. The membrane was incubated with goat anti-rabbit IgG (1:5000, STAR124P, Bio-Rad) at room temperature for 2 h. Finally, the blots were visualized using a chemiluminescent detection kit (PK10003, Proteintech).

DARTS assay

Total protein was extracted from AGS and HGC-27 cells using M-PER Mammalian Protein Extraction Reagent (78501, Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Waltham, MA, USA) to determine the protein concentration. An equal volume of protein lysate was incubated with 100 µM linalool or DMSO for 1 h. Pronase (PRON-RO, Merck KGaA) was added for limited proteolytic digestion at room temperature for 15 min, followed by the addition of 5× SDS loading buffer to terminate the reaction. After separation by SDS-PAGE, WB analysis was performed to detect NR3C2 protein expression, and the retention levels of NR3C2 protein following Pronase treatment were compared.

CETSA

AGS and HGC-27 cells were treated with 100 µM linalool or DMSO for 1 h, resuspended in PBS, and aliquoted into PCR tubes. The samples were then heated for 3 min at the set temperatures (43, 46, 49, 52, 55, 58, and 61 °C). After repeated freeze-thaw cycles to lyse the cells and centrifugation at high speed at 4 °C, the supernatant was collected as the soluble protein fraction. WB analysis was performed to detect NR3C2 protein levels under various temperature conditions, followed by grayscale quantification. The results were normalized using the grayscale value of the NR3C2 band at 43 °C to assess changes in NR3C2 thermal stability following linalool treatment.

Biotin-Linalool pull-down assay

A pull-down assay using a biotin-linalool probe (Creative Biolabs, Shirley, NY, USA) was performed to verify the direct interaction between linalool and NR3C2. Total protein from AGS and HGC-27 cells was extracted using M-PER Mammalian Protein Extraction Reagent. Equal volumes of protein lysate were incubated separately with biotin-linalool; for the competitive group, an excess of free linalool was added before the addition of biotin-linalool, and the mixture was pre-incubated at 4 °C for 1 h. Pierce Streptavidin Magnetic Beads (88816, Thermo Fisher) were added for another 2-h incubation. The magnetic beads were washed three times and boiled with 1× SDS loading buffer to elute the bound protein. After separation by SDS-PAGE, Western blot analysis was performed to assess the enrichment of NR3C2 protein in the eluate, and the results were obtained by normalizing the gray values of the NR3C2 bands against the input control in each group.

Analysis of NR3C2 protein stability

After pre-treating AGS and HGC-27 cells with DMSO or 100 µM linalool for 24 h, 100 µg/mL CHX (HY-12320, MedChemExpress) was added, and cells were harvested at 0, 2, 4, 6, and 8 h. Alternatively, the cells were pretreated with 10 µM MG132 (HY-13259, MedChemExpress) for 6 h [35]. The total protein was extracted, and NR3C2 protein levels were detected by WB, using GAPDH as an internal control. Grey value of blots was quantified using ImageJ.

ChIP-qPCR

The SimpleChIP Plus Sonication ChIP Kit (56383, CST) was used. DNA-protein complexes in AGS and HGC-27 cells were fixed with 1% PFA for 10 min, and the cells were incubated with 0.125 M glycine. Cells were lysed using ChIP Sonication Cell Lysis Buffer, and chromatin fragments were generated using an ultrasonic disruptor. With 2% of the lysate used as input, the chromatin fragments were incubated with anti-NR3C2 (1:500, orb629402, Biorbyt, Waterbeach, Cambridge, UK) and ChIP-Grade Protein G Magnetic Beads. Normal rabbit IgG (1:50) served as a negative control. After elution and purification, qPCR analysis was performed using specific primers for the NFKBIZ promoter (chr3: 101849239–101849586): left primer: 5’-TGAAGGGCAGGCAAACAAC-3’; right primer: 5’-CTGCCAGGGTATTTAACGGG-3’.

Dual-luciferase assay

The NFKBIZ promoter sequence was obtained from UCSC. After designing primers and amplifying the sequence via PCR, it was inserted into Promoterless Firefly Luciferase Basic Vectors (E6651, Promega, Madison, WI, USA) using NheI and HindIII restriction enzyme sites to construct the NFKBIZ promoter-luciferase reporter plasmid. NR3C2-knockdown or control-treated AGS and HGC-27 cells were seeded in 24-well plates. Once cell confluence reached 60%–70%, transient transfection was performed using Lipofectamine 3000 Transfection Reagent (L3000001, Invitrogen).

For the NFKBIZ promoter activity assay, the NFKBIZ promoter reporter plasmid and the pRL Renilla Luciferase Control Reporter Vector (E2241, Promega) were co-transfected into each well. For the assessment of NF-κB transcriptional activity, the pGL4.32[luc2P/NF-κB-RE/Hygro] plasmid (E8491, Promega) and the pRL Renilla Luciferase Control Reporter Vector (E2241, Promega) were co-transfected into each well. After 48 h, relative luciferase activity was detected using the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System (E1910, Promega) with Renilla luciferase as the internal standard.

Immunohistochemistry

Tumor tissues and corresponding peritumoral tissues from 42 GC patients were prepared as paraffin sections (4 μm), baked at 60 °C for 6 h, deparaffinized with xylene, rehydrated in a gradient of ethanol, and treated with 3% hydrogen peroxide to block endogenous peroxidase activity. After antigen retrieval by citrate buffer using a microwave oven, the sections were incubated with the primary antibodies against NR3C2 (1:100, A3308, ABclonal) and NFKBIZ (1:100, PA5-52703, Invitrogen) at 4 °C overnight. The next day, Goat anti Rabbit IgG, HRP (1:1000, G-21234, Invitrogen) was applied. Finally, the samples were stained with DAB staining solution (PR30010, Proteintech) and counterstained with hematoxylin, and images were captured using a microscope.

A semi-quantitative analysis was performed using the classic IRS system. Scoring was performed independently by two pathologists who were blinded to the clinical data. Staining intensity was scored on a scale of 0–3: 0, no staining; 1, weak staining; 2, moderate staining; 3, strong staining. The percentage of positive cells was scored on a scale of 0–4: 0, no positive cells; 1, < 10%; 2, 10%-50%; 3, 51%-80%; 4, > 80%. The final IRS was defined as the product of the staining intensity score and the percentage of positive cells, with a total score ranging from 0 to 12 [36].

Statistical analysis

Prism 10.4.2 software (GraphPad Software Inc., San Diego, CA, USA) was used, and results were expressed as mean ± SEM. Comparisons between two groups were performed using unpaired t-tests or paired t-tests; comparisons among multiple groups were performed using one-way or two-way ANOVA with Tukey’s post hoc test. Correlation analysis was performed using Pearson’s correlation coefficient. Correlation analysis of clinical and pathological parameters was performed using Fisher’s exact test. The experimental design incorporated a total of five mice per group in the animal experiments. The cell experiments were independently repeated three times to ensure the reliability of the findings. P < 0.05 indicated that the difference was statistically significant.

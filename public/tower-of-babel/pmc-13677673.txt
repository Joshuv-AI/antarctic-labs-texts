Efficacy and safety of ifupinostat in relapsed or refractory diffuse large B-cell lymphoma: a multicenter, single-arm phase 2 study
Authors: Yuankai Shi, Peng Liu, Yufu Li, Wenyu Li, Hui Zhou, Yu Yang, Jie Jin, Yuhuan Gao, Weijie Xu, Meifang Zheng, Haiyan Yang, Hongwei Xue, Zhigang Peng, Ou Bai, Fei Li, Fangfang Lv, Liling Zhang, Ning Huang, Yi Luo, Chunhong Hu, Yajing Xu, Huilai Zhang, Xiuhua Sun, Xin Li, Kaiyang Ding, Linjuan Zeng, Haiyan Zhu, Yajie Cao, Yonglong Yang, Kegang Jiang, Changgeng Qian
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIfupinostat (BEBT-908) is a novel dual-targeted inhibitor of histone deacetylase (HDACs) and phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase alpha (PI3Kα). We evaluated the efficacy and safety of ifupinostat monotherapy in patients with relapsed or refractory (r/r) diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) after at least 2 prior lines of systemic therapy. Eligible participants received intravenous ifupinostat at 22.5 mg/m² on days 1, 3, 5, 8, 10, and 12 of a 21-day cycle. The primary endpoint was objective response rate (ORR) assessed by blinded independent central review (BICR). Secondary endpoints included disease control rate (DCR), duration of response (DoR), progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS) and safety. From June 2020 to July 2022, 128 patients were enrolled and included in the safety set (SS), and 115 patients were in the efficacy analysis set (EAS). At the data cutoff (July 2023), the median follow-up was 19.58 months (95% confidence interval [CI] 16.82–21.95). The ORR was 36.5% (42/115, 95% CI 27.7-46.0), with a complete response rate of 11.3% (13/115). The median DoR was 6.2 months (95% CI 3.5–14.5), median PFS was 2.7 months (95% CI 1.6–3.7), and median OS was 9.9 months (95% CI 7.8–14.8). Treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) occurred in 99.2% (127/128) of patients, and ≥grade 3 TRAEs in 86.7% (111/128). The most common ≥grade 3 TRAEs were thrombocytopenia (75.8%, 97/128), neutropenia (33.6%, 43/128), and leukopenia (22.7%, 29/128). No treatment-related deaths occurred. Ifupinostat demonstrated promising efficacy and a manageable safety profile as a new therapeutic alternative for patients with r/r DLBCL. Introduction

Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma represents the most prevalent histological subtype of B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL), accounting for approximately 30% to 40% of all newly diagnosed cases. The integration of the anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody rituximab into frontline chemotherapy—comprising cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, and prednisone (R-CHOP)—fundamentally revolutionized the therapeutic paradigm, yielding durable long-term remissions in a significant proportion of patients.1 Within the Chinese clinical landscape, the 5-year progression-free survival (PFS) rate for patients receiving frontline R-CHOP or R-CHOP-like regimens ranges between 61.7% and 77.7%.2,3 However, despite these initial therapeutic successes, approximately one-third of all patients eventually experience relapsed or refractory disease, which presents a formidable clinical obstacle. For patients who experience relapse or are primary refractory to frontline treatment regimens, second-line treatment involves salvage combination chemotherapy followed by high-dose therapy and autologous hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (AHSCT). Unfortunately, a significant majority of these patients are ineligible for AHSCT due to advanced age, cumulative organic comorbidities, or failure to achieve remission with second-line chemotherapy, or they suffer a subsequent recurrence after AHSCT. In recent years, chimeric antigen receptor T cell (CAR-T) therapies and bispecific antibodies have expanded the salvage arsenal, while global access remains limited by manufacturing costs, complex operational logistics, and severe adverse events (AEs). Consequently, patients who progress after two or more lines of systemic therapies face an exceedingly dismal prognosis, with a median OS of merely 4.4 to 5.9 months,4,5 emphasizing a stark, unmet medical need for novel, accessible, and active therapeutic alternatives.

The detailed elucidation of the molecular pathogenesis of aggressive B-cell lymphomas has shifted focus toward intercepting dysregulated intracellular signaling cascades and epigenetic aberrations that govern tumor viability. Epigenetic modifications, particularly those mediated by HDACs, have emerged as vital drivers of lymphomagenesis and transcriptional subversion in aggressive B-cell malignancies. HDACs catalyze the enzymatic removal of acetyl groups from lysine residues on histones and non-histone proteins, inducing a highly condensed chromatin architecture that epigenetically represses tumor suppressor genes, disrupts cell cycle checkpoints, and facilitates immune evasion.6 Concurrently, hyperactivation of the phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K) signaling cascade represents another operational hallmark of oncogenic survival in aggressive lymphomas. The PI3K/protein kinase B (PKB, AKT)/mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) pathway is deeply involved in regulating core cellular activities, including continuous proliferation, metabolic reprogramming, cell motility, and acquired resistance to conventional chemotherapeutic drugs. In aggressive B-cell lymphomas, constitutive activation of the PI3K cascade, specifically through the PI3Kɑ isoform, often cooperates with microenvironmental survival cues to sustain lymphoma cell survival.7 Given their parallel and interconnected roles in promoting lymphomagenesis, both the HDACs and PI3K pathways represent highly attractive therapeutic vulnerabilities for clinical targeting.8–11

Despite a strong biological rationale, clinical evaluations of selective single-target inhibitors have been suboptimal in aggressive B-cell NHLs. Selective PI3K inhibitors or individual pan-HDAC inhibitors evaluated as monotherapies have demonstrated very restricted single-agent activity and short-lived responses in r/r DLBCL, frequently constrained by the rapid emergence of compensatory pathway crosstalk. Specifically, targeted inhibition of the PI3K pathway can trigger compensatory epigenetic adjustments that restore oncogenic transcription, while standalone HDAC inhibition can induce feedback upregulation of upstream receptor tyrosine kinases that reactivate PI3K/AKT signaling. To circumvent this therapeutic hurdle, the concurrent inhibition of both pathways has been proposed as a logical synergistic strategy to achieve complete and durable signaling blockade. Pre-clinical evidence indicates that HDAC inhibitors can acetylate key lysine residues within the AKT kinase domain, diminishing its binding affinity to phosphatidylinositol (3,4,5)-trisphosphate (PIP3) and reducing its operational activation, while PI3K inhibitors directly block upstream AKT phosphorylation and activation. Consequently, co-targeting these nodes results in a profound, cooperative shutdown of downstream AKT/mTOR signaling, promoting enhanced apoptosis, cellular differentiation, and downregulation of major oncogenic drivers like c-MYC. However, combining separate small-molecule agents in clinical practice is frequently hindered by overlapping systemic toxicities and unpredictable pharmacokinetic interactions, underscoring the necessity for a single chemical entity capable of balanced, dual-targeted inhibition.

To address this clinical challenge, ifupinostat (BEBT-908) was developed as a novel, first-in-class small molecule designed to simultaneously inhibit both HDACs and PI3Kα with balanced potency within a single compound. Pre-clinical evaluation has revealed that ifupinostat exhibits exceptional anti-tumor activity, effectively crossing the blood-brain barrier to suppress central nervous system lymphomas and triggering immunogenic ferroptosis to modulate the tumor microenvironment. Following favorable safety, tolerability, and pharmacokinetic findings in the phase I study, we initiated this multicenter, single-arm, phase II trial (ChiCTR1900028179) to evaluate the efficacy and safety of ifupinostat monotherapy in patients with r/r DLBCL after at least 2 lines of systemic therapy. This study was the first pivotal study to evaluate a dual HDAC/PI3Kα inhibitor in heavily pretreated r/r DLBCL, demonstrating robust single-agent clinical activity alongside a predictable and manageable hematologic safety profile.

Results

Patient baseline characteristics

From June 12, 2020, to July 18, 2022, a total of 188 patients were screened from 28 hospitals across P. R. China, of whom 128 r/r DLBCL patients confirmed by pathologists in participating hospitals were enrolled and received at least 1 dose of ifupinostat treatment and were included in the full analysis set (FAS). Thirteen patients were excluded by central review, including 8 without confirmed DLBCL and 5 without confirmed r/r DLBCL. A total of 115 patients were included in the EAS. A total of 128 patients who had at least 1 safety evaluation were included in the SS (Fig. 1). The baseline characteristics of patients in the FAS/SS and EAS are presented in Table 1.

Of the 115 patients in the EAS, treatment was discontinued in 112. The reasons for discontinuation were disease progression (n = 67), AEs (n = 5), death (n = 1), withdrawal of consent (n = 21), investigator decision (n = 13), and discontinuation by patient (n = 5).

Efficacy

The data cutoff date was July 17, 2023, and the median follow-up was 19.58 months (95% CI 16.82–21.95). A total of 2.6% (3/115) of patients in the EAS were still on treatment.

In the EAS, the ORR was 36.5% (42/115, 95% CI 27.7-46.0), and the DCR was 60.0% (69/115, 95% CI 50.5–69.0) (Fig. 2a–c and Table 2). Subgroup analysis showed that among 115 patients in the EAS, 66 were <60 years and 49 were ≥60 years, with ORR of 31.8% (95% CI 20.9–44.4) and 42.9% (95% CI 28.8–57.8), respectively. Among 58 male patients and 57 female patients, the ORR was 31.0% (95% CI 19.5–44.5) and 42.1% (95% CI 29.1–55.9), respectively. A total of 31, 39, 32, and 13 patients had International Prognostic Index (IPI) scores of 0–1, 2, 3 and 4–5, with ORR of 38.7% (95% CI 21.9–57.8), 43.6% (95% CI 27.8–60.4), 25.0% (95% CI 11.5–43.4) and 38.5% (95% CI 13.9–68.4), respectively. Among 63 patients who had received 2 prior regimens and 52 patients who had received ≥3 prior regimens, the ORR was 41.3% (95% CI 29.0–54.4) and 30.8% (95% CI 18.7–45.1), respectively. The ORR was 80.0% (4/5), 100% (2/2) and 66.7% (4/6) in patients who progressed after prior AHSCT, bispecific antibodies and CAR-T therapy, respectively. Phosphatidylinositol-4,5-bisphosphate 3-kinase catalytic subunit alpha (PIK3CA) amplification was tested by fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) in 83 patients, positive in 27 and negative in 56. The ORR was 33.3% (9/27, 95% CI 16.5–54.0) and 41.1% (23/56, 95% CI 28.1–55.0) in patients with and without PIK3CA amplification, respectively (Fig. 3).

Safety

In the SS, the median cycles of ifupinostat treatment were 5.1 (range 1–34). A total of 53.9% (69/128) of patients received ≥3 cycles of ifupinostat treatment. All patients had ≥1 treatment-emergent adverse event (TEAE). TEAEs with incidence ≥20% were thrombocytopenia (96.9%, 124/128), neutropenia (80.5%, 103/128), leukopenia (77.3%, 99/128), anemia (70.3%, 90/128), hypokalemia (47.7%, 61/128), lymphopenia (43.8%, 56/128), hypertriglyceridemia (34.4%, 44/128), fever (30.5%, 39/128), hypoalbuminemia (28.1%, 36/128), constipation (28.1%, 36/128), nausea (25.8%, 33/128), vomiting (21.9%, 28/128), and hyponatremia (20.3%, 26/128). A total of 92.2% (118/128) of patients had ≥grade 3 TEAEs. ≥grade 3 TEAEs with incidence ≥20% were thrombocytopenia (78.1%, 100/128), neutropenia (40.6%, 52/128), leukopenia (32.8%, 42/128), lymphopenia (22.7%, 29/128), and anemia (20.3%, 26/128) (Table 3).

TRAEs with incidence ≥20% were thrombocytopenia (96.9%, 124/128), neutropenia (75.0%, 96/128), leukopenia (73.4%, 94/128), anemia (64.1%, 82/128), lymphopenia (39.8%, 51/128), hypokalemia (34.4%, 44/128), nausea (24.2%, 31/128), hypertriglyceridemia (21.9%, 28/128), vomiting (20.3%, 26/128), and fever (20.3%, 26/128). ≥grade 3 TRAEs with incidence ≥20% were thrombocytopenia (75.8%, 97/128), neutropenia (33.6%, 43/128), and leukopenia (22.7%, 29/128) (Table 3).

In the SS, serious AEs occurred in 42.2% (54/128) of patients, including serious TRAEs in 31.3% (40/128) of patients. The most common serious TRAEs were thrombocytopenia (21.1%, 27/128). Five patients discontinued treatment due to the following TRAEs: 4 patients had grade 4 thrombocytopenia (1 of whom also involved grade 3 candidiasis and gamma-glutamyl transferase [γ-GGT] elevation), and 1 patient had grade 3 multi-system laboratory elevations (bilirubin, aspartate transaminase [AST], γ-GGT, alanine aminotransferase [ALT], alkaline phosphatase).

All AEs recovered after rest, discontinuation of ifupinostat treatment, or symptomatic treatment. The median time to recovery from thrombocytopenia was 15.0 days (95% CI 14.0–21.0). No treatment-related deaths occurred.

Discussion

This multicenter, single-arm, phase 2 study evaluated the efficacy and safety of ifupinostat, a novel dual-targeted inhibitor of HDAC and PI3Kα, in patients with r/r DLBCL. The study met the prespecified primary endpoint, demonstrating an ORR of 36.5% and a manageable safety profile in heavily pretreated patients. These findings address the critical unmet need for effective therapies in r/r DLBCL, where prognosis remains poor despite advances in frontline immunochemotherapy. The high proportion of refractory patients (92.2%) enrolled in this study underscores the clinical relevance of these results in a population with limited therapeutic options and poor outcomes with existing salvage regimens.

The dual targeting of HDACs and the PI3K pathway represents a rational and promising therapeutic strategy for DLBCL. HDAC inhibitors increase acetylation levels of histones and non-histone proteins, regulating gene expression and promoting cell differentiation, apoptosis, and cell cycle arrest.12 Whereas PI3K inhibitors block the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway, suppressing cell proliferation, survival, and metabolism.7 Mechanistically, HDAC inhibitors can acetylate lysine residues in the AKT kinase domain, reducing AKT binding to PIP3, while PI3K inhibitors directly block AKT phosphorylation and activation, resulting in synergistic inhibition of AKT signaling and overcoming compensatory feedback loops that limit single-agent activity.13 Pre-clinical studies have demonstrated that the combination of PI3K/mTOR inhibitor BEZ235 and pan-HDAC inhibitor panobinostat exhibits potent anti-tumor activity through AKT inactivation, GSK3 activation, Bim upregulation, Mcl-1 downregulation, and enhanced DNA damage in DLBCL cells.14 Beyond its direct anti-tumor effects, ifupinostat has shown blood-brain barrier penetration in primary central nervous system (CNS) lymphoma mouse models, downregulating c-MYC and inducing ferroptosis.10 Additionally, ifupinostat triggers immunogenic ferroptosis by upregulating major histocompatibility complex I (MHC-I) expression, activating STAT1 signaling and fostering a pro-inflammatory tumor microenvironment with increased cytotoxic T-cell infiltration, suggesting potential synergy with anti-programmed cell death-1 therapy.11 These mechanistic insights support the broad therapeutic potential of ifupinostat across hematologic malignancies and solid tumors, either as a monotherapy or as an immunomodulatory agent to enhance anti-tumor immunity.

In the context of existing therapies, monotherapy with selective PI3K or HDAC inhibitors has shown limited efficacy in r/r DLBCL. Idelalisib achieved an ORR of only 14% with a median PFS of 0.8–1.3 months, leading to early study termination due to failure to meet the primary endpoint.15 Copanlisib produced an ORR of 19.4% (90% CI 11.9–29.1) with a median PFS of 1.8 months and a median DoR of 4.3 months.16 The HDAC inhibitor vorinostat demonstrated minimal activity with an ORR of 5.6% and a 6-month PFS rate of 11.1%.17 By contrast, the dual inhibitor CUDC-907 showed more promising activity with an ORR of 55% among r/r DLBCL patients in a phase 1 study, though no responses were observed in Hodgkin lymphoma or multiple myeloma.18 These results indicated that r/r DLBCL patients may benefit from dual HDAC and PI3K inhibitors. In our study, ifupinostat achieved an ORR of 36.5% (95% CI 27.7–46.0) and a DCR of 60.0% (95% CI 50.5–69.0), with median PFS and OS of 2.7 months (95% CI 1.6–3.7) and 9.9 months (95% CI 7.8–14.8), respectively. Importantly, patients who achieved objective response experienced substantially prolonged outcomes, with median PFS of 8.1 months (95% CI 5.5–NA) and median OS of 25.6 months (95% CI 15.8–NA), highlighting the clinical benefit of achieving response with ifupinostat. Moreover, after excluding patients affected by COVID-19 pandemic control measures, the phase IIb demonstrated an improved ORR of 54.6% (24/44, 95% CI 38.9–69.6), with median DoR, PFS, and OS of 5.9 months (95% CI 2.8–14.0), 4.1 months (95% CI 2.7–6.1), and 12.7 months (95% CI 8.5–18.0), respectively. These results compare favorably with selinexor (ORR 28%),19 and are particularly encouraging given that 92.2% of enrolled patients had refractory disease versus 72% in the SADAL study and 90% in the glofitamab study.19,20 Subgroup analysis revealed that the ORR was 38.7% (12/31, 95% CI 21.9–57.8), 43.6% (17/39, 95% CI 27.8–60.4), 25.0% (8/32, 95% CI 11.5–43.4) and 38.5% (5/13, 95% CI 13.9–68.4) in IPI scores of 0–1, 2, 3 and 4–5, respectively, indicating favorable efficacy across all IPI risk groups. Among 83 patients tested for PIK3CA gene amplification by FISH, the ORR was 33.3% (9/27, 95% CI 16.5–54.0) and 41.1% (23/56, 95% CI 28.1–55.0) in patients with and without PIK3CA gene amplification, respectively. Only a small number of lymphoma patients receive AHSCT in China compared with the Western patient population. The percentage of r/r cHL patients receiving AHSCT was 19% (18/96) in the ORIENT-1 study, 78% (18/23) in the CHECKMATE 039 study and 37% (112/304) in the KEYNOTE-204 study.21–23 The subgroup analyses of this study showed ORR of 80.0% (4/5), 100% (2/2) and 66.7% (4/6) for ifupinostat in patients who progressed after prior AHSCT, bispecific antibodies and CAR-T therapy, respectively. However, these subgroup findings are exploratory, statistically limited by small sample sizes, and require robust validation in larger prospective studies.

The safety profile of ifupinostat was manageable and consistent with its dual-targeted mechanism of action. Hematologic toxicities, particularly thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, and leukopenia, were the most common treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) of all grades and ≥ grade 3, necessitating regular blood monitoring and supportive care. Non-hematologic AEs including hypokalemia, hypertriglyceridemia, hypoalbuminemia, hyponatremia, and gastrointestinal symptoms (constipation, nausea, vomiting) were predominantly grade 1–2 and self-limiting. This safety profile aligns with known class effects: idelalisib was associated with elevated liver transaminases (17%), hematologic toxicity (11%), colitis (6%), and cytomegalovirus reactivation (3%), and skin toxicity (3%)15; copanlisib with hypertension (40.3%), diarrhea (37.3%), and hyperglycemia (32.8%)16; vorinostat with diarrhea (61%), fatigue (50%), nausea (39%), anemia (33%) and vomiting (33%), and Grade 3 thrombocytopenia (16.7%) and asthenia (11.1%)17; and CUDC-907 with thrombocytopenia (18%), neutropenia (7%), and hyperglycemia (7%).18 Notably, the incidence of severe colitis, opportunistic infections, and treatment-related mortality with ifupinostat was lower than reported with selective PI3K inhibitors, likely reflecting the balanced dual-targeted design and potentially reduced on-target off-tumor toxicity. The requirement for regular hematologic monitoring and prompt dose modification remains essential to ensure patient safety during treatment.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. This was a single-arm study without a control group, conducted exclusively in Chinese patients, which may limit the generalizability of findings to other ethnic populations. The PIK3CA gene amplification test by FISH was not performed in all patients, and the relationship between PI3K alterations and efficacy is worth exploring. Ifupinostat demonstrated promising efficacy and a manageable safety profile in heavily pretreated r/r DLBCL, offering a clinically meaningful new therapeutic option for this challenging patient population. Further investigation in an ongoing randomized controlled trial (NCT06792253) will confirm these findings, and optimize its therapeutic potential.

Materials and methods

Study design and participants

This multicenter, single-arm, phase 2 study was conducted in 28 hospitals in China. Eligible patients were aged 18-75 years with histologically confirmed DLBCL, including those with concurrent MYC and BCL2 and/or BCL6 rearrangements (double-hit [DH] or triple-hit [TH] lymphoma). All patients had experienced disease progression after at least two lines of prior therapy with measurable disease according to Lugano 2014 criteria.24 Refractory to first-line therapy was defined as disease progression during first-line therapy, failure to achieve CR after 4 cycles of induction therapy, failure to achieve PR after 2 cycles of induction therapy, or relapse within 6 months of achieving CR. Refractory to last-line therapy was defined as disease progression during the most recent line of therapy, failure to achieve PR or CR with the most recent regimen, disease progression or relapse within 6 months of achieving PR or CR with the most recent therapy, or relapse within 12 months following AHSCT. Relapse was defined as recurrence in patients who do not meet the criteria for either first-line refractory or last-line refractory disease. Additional inclusion criteria were an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status (PS) of 0–2, with at least a 12-week life expectancy. Patients had normal bone marrow and organ function as defined by absolute neutrophil count ≥ 1.0×10⁹/L, platelet count ≥100 × 10⁹/L, hemoglobin ≥80 g/L, total bilirubin <1.5 times the upper limit of normal (ULN), ALT and aspartate aminotransferase ≤2.5 times ULN, and serum creatinine <1.5 times ULN. All drug-related toxic effects (except for hair loss) had to be resolved to grade 1 according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 5.0 before receiving study drug treatment.

Exclusion criteria included patients who had received any continuous or intermittent treatments such as PI3K inhibitors, mTOR inhibitors, HDAC inhibitors, or other small molecule targeted therapies within 2 weeks prior to enrollment. Those who had AHSCT, received radiotherapy that could affect study efficacy, or had myelosuppressive chemotherapy or biological therapy within the past 3 weeks were not eligible. Patients who had major surgery (excluding tumor biopsies) within 4 weeks, treatment with recombinant human granulocyte colony-stimulating factor or thrombopoietin within 2 weeks, prednisone at doses more than 10 mg daily within 7 days, or chimeric antigen receptor T-cell (CAR-T) therapy within the last 3 months were excluded. Other exclusion criteria included severe or uncontrolled systemic diseases, active infections (such as hepatitis B, hepatitis C, or human immunodeficiency virus), a history of interstitial lung disease, cardiac dysfunction (left ventricular ejection fraction less than 50%), Richter transformation, histologically transformed follicular lymphoma, primary or secondary central nervous system involvement, and clinically significant pulmonary function impairment. Prior treatment with CAR-T therapy or bispecific antibodies was not an exclusion criterion for this study.

The recommended phase II dose (RP2D) of ifupinostat 22.5 mg/m² was selected based on the safety and efficacy results from a phase I study (NCT06082596). In that study, dose levels of 10, 15, 22.5, 33.75, and 45 mg/m² were evaluated. The 15 mg/m² and 22.5 mg/m² cohorts demonstrated manageable safety profiles with promising anticancer activity. In the subsequent dose-expansion cohort, the 22.5 mg/m² dose showed superior efficacy in patients with r/r DLBCL compared to the 15 mg/m² dose, leading to its selection for this phase II study (data unpublished).

The institutional review board or independent ethics committee at each participating hospital approved the study protocol, and the study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and the International Council for Harmonisation of Technical Requirements for Pharmaceuticals for Human Use Good Clinical Practices Guidelines. Written informed consent was obtained from all participating patients before the initiation of study treatment. This study was registered on the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry with the identifier ChiCTR1900028179.

Procedures

Patients were enrolled by investigators at participating hospitals. Eligible patients received ifupinostat 22.5 mg/m² via intravenous infusion over 30 min (±3 min) on days 1, 3, 5, 8, 10, and 12 of each 21-day cycle. Treatment continued until disease progression, death, intolerable toxicity, or withdrawal of informed consent, whichever occurred first. The dose adjustment protocol specified that if grade 4 hematologic AEs or grade 3–4 non-hematologic AEs occur, treatment was discontinued until resolution or recovery to ≤grade 2, with resumption at the original dose of 22.5 mg/m² under modified schedules on days 1, 3, 5, 8, and 10 for the first occurrence, days 1, 4, 8, and 11 for the second occurrence, and a reduced dose of 15 mg/m² on days 1, 3, 5, 8, 10, and 12 upon the third occurrence. Supportive care, including transfusions of packed red blood cells or platelets and administration of recombinant human granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (rhG-CSF) or thrombopoietin (TPO), was permitted per institutional guidelines and at the investigator’s discretion.

Tumor assessments were conducted using either computed tomography or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) scans of the neck, chest, abdomen, pelvis, and any other suspected areas at baseline, followed by assessments every 6 weeks for the first 48 weeks and then every 12 weeks thereafter until disease progression. Additionally, an MRI of the brain was performed at baseline, with subsequent brain imaging required using the same method as baseline when clinically indicated based on symptoms. Positron emission tomography and computed tomography were performed when necessary to confirm the tumor assessment results. Tumor responses were evaluated by BICR and investigators according to the Lugano 2014 criteria.24

TEAEs and TRAEs were monitored throughout the study. Physical examinations, ECOG PS, electrocardiograms, echocardiography, and laboratory tests were evaluated at baseline and protocol-specified time points.

Outcomes

The primary endpoint was ORR in the EAS. The secondary endpoints were DCR, TTR, DoR, PFS, OS, and safety. All endpoints were assessed by BICR.

The ORR was defined as the proportion of patients with CR or PR. DCR was defined as the proportion of patients with CR, PR, or stable disease. PFS was defined as the time from the date of the first dose of ifupinostat until the date of documented disease progression or death. OS was defined as the time from the date of the first dose of ifupinostat until the date of death. TTR was defined as the time from the date of the first dose of ifupinostat until the date of the first documented response, including patients with CR and PR. DoR was defined as the time from the date of the first documented response until the date of disease progression or death in responsive patients. TEAEs were defined as AEs that occurred after the first dose of ifupinostat and up to 30 days after the last dose of ifupinostat. TRAEs were defined as AEs that were assessed to have a relationship with ifupinostat by investigators.

FISH

Interphase FISH was performed to detect the translocation of c-MYC, BCL-2, and PIK3CA in the enrolled patients. The c-MYC and BCL-2 dual-color probe and PIK3CA gene amplification probe kit (Wuhan Health Care Biotechnology Co., Ltd, China) were applied to formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tumor tissues in adherence to the manufacturer’s guidelines at the central laboratory. The assessment of FISH signals was executed using an Olympus BX43 upright microscope (Evident Scientific, Japan). A total of fifty tumor cells were obtained, and the presence of split or amplified signals in over 15% of tumor cells indicated the occurrence of c-MYC or BCL-2 rearrangement, or PIK3CA amplification.

Statistical analysis

This phase II study employed a Simon two-stage design, including a phase IIa study and a phase IIb study. The phase IIa study planned to perform efficacy analysis after 30 patients completed a two-cycle (6-week) efficacy evaluation. If the ORR in phase IIa met the lower threshold of 20%, this study would proceed to phase IIb. Based on the results of phase I and phase IIa studies, the ORR of ifupinostat in r/r DLBCL was 35% (15/42). Thus, a sample size of 72 patients would be required to provide a lower limit of more than 20% to ensure that the lower bound of the 95% CI exceeds 20% in phase IIb. Taking 20% dropouts into account, approximately 90 patients were planned to be enrolled in phase IIb.

The FAS included patients who received at least 1 dose of ifupinostat. The EAS included all patients who received at least 1 dose of ifupinostat and had r/r DLBCL with pathological diagnoses confirmed by participating hospitals in phase IIa and central pathological reconfirmed in phase IIb. The SS included patients who received at least 1 dose of ifupinostat and had at least 1 safety evaluation.

The corresponding two-sided 95% CIs of ORR and DCR were determined using the Clopper-Pearson method. For survival analyses, median values and two-sided 95% CIs with the Kaplan‒Meier method were used. Statistical Analysis System (SAS) version 9.4 was used for all statistical analyses.

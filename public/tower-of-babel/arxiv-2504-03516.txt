Supercooled Goldstones at the QCD chiral phase transition
Adrien Florio, Eduardo Grossi, Aleksas Mazeliauskas, Alexander Soloviev, Derek Teaney

Abstract
We discuss the universal non-equilibrium enhancement of long-wavelength Goldstone bosons induced by quenches to broken phase in Model G — the dynamical universality class of an O ⁡ ( 4 ) O(4) -antiferromagnet and chiral QCD phase transition. Generic scaling arguments predict a parametric enhancement in the infrared spectra of Goldstones, which is confirmed by fully-fledged stochastic simulations. The details of the enhancement are determined by the non-linear dynamics of a superfluid effective theory, which is a limit of Model G reflecting the broken O ⁡ ( 4 ) O(4) symmetry. Our results translate to a parametric enhancement of low-momentum pions in heavy-ion collisions at the LHC, which are underpredicted in current hydrodynamic models without critical dynamics.



Introduction.
Criticality is central to the understanding of a plethora of phenomena across different physical systems. The core reason behind this is that critical systems are universal: a ferromagnet behaves in the same way close to its transition point from an ordered phase to a disordered one as some cellular membranes
veatch2008critical
. This universality applies not only to a system’s static properties but also to its dynamic properties. The typical dynamical time-scale
\tau_{R}
associated with the relaxation of the relevant order parameters diverges at criticality, a phenomenon known as critical slowing down. Near criticality, the order parameter is a slow variable, whose dynamics has to and can be included in any effective hydrodynamical description of the system
hohenberg
;
Crossley:2015evo
;
Stephanov:2017ghc
.

The collision of ultra-relativistic ions forms a “fireball” of quark-gluon plasma (QGP), the high-temperature phase of quantum chromodynamics (QCD) where chiral symmetry is restored
Pisarski:1983ms
. The subsequent rapid expansion and cooling leads to the hadronic phase of QCD, where quarks and gluons are confined, and chiral symmetry is broken. This transition is a crossover
Borsanyi:2018grb
;
HotQCD:2018pds
but is close in parameter space to a second-order critical point. Indeed, in the limit where the up and down quark masses are exactly zero (the “chiral limit” of QCD), the transition is known to be second order
HotQCD:2019xnw
;
Kaczmarek:2020sif
;
Kotov:2021rah
;
Cuteri:2021ikv
and conjectured to be in the universality class of an
O(4)
-antiferromagnet.

It has been known for a long time that the dynamical universality class of chiral QCD is the “Model G” of Hohenberg and Halperin
Rajagopal:1992qz
.
The pioneering works

;
Rajagopal:1992qz
;
Blaizot:1992at
;

;

Busza:2018rrf

Nevertheless, a persistent model-to-data tension of low momentum pions (the Goldstones of chiral symmetry breaking) indicates an incompleteness of the hydrodynamic description of QGP

Recent advances, in particular in lattice QCD and the high-precision data at the LHC, have revived interest in chiral critical dynamics and its phenomenological signatures

In this work, we discuss some universal non-equilibrium features of Goldstone modes in Model G
during quenches from the restored to the broken phase (supercooling).
Because of their coupling to conserved charges, the Goldstone modes behave as pseudo-particles and can propagate ballistically over many correlation lengths before diffusing back to the medium. This separation of scales leads to a parametric enhancement in their infrared spectrum. We confirm this prediction by detailed stochastic simulations of instantaneous quenches in Model G, which are further detailed in our companion paper
superpaper5
. For the QCD chiral phase transition, our results imply a parametric enhancement of low momentum pions over thermal yields.
We conclude by commenting on potential phenomenological implications in heavy-ion collisions.

O(4)
-antiferromagnet (Model G).
We briefly review Model G
hohenberg
and refer the reader to our companion paper
superpaper5
and previous works
Grossi:2021gqi
. Model G captures the dynamics of an
O(4)
local order parameter
(\phi_{0},\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\phi_{3})
coupled to partially conserved axial charges
n_{A}^{\ell}
and conserved vector charges
n_{V}^{\ell}
, which can be combined in an antisymmetric
O(4)
tensor
n_{ab}
with
n_{0\ell}=n_{A}^{\ell}\,
and
n_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}=\epsilon^{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}\ell}n_{V}^{\ell}
. In equilibrium, the fields are distributed according to the following Landau-Ginzburg free energy

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}[\phi]

\displaystyle=\int d^{3}x\left[\frac{1}{2}\nabla\phi_{a}\nabla\phi_{a}+U(\phi)+\frac{1}{4{\chi_{I}}}n_{ab}n^{ab}\right]

(1a)

\displaystyle U(\phi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}m_{0}^{2}\,\phi^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{4}(\phi\cdot\phi)^{2}\,.

(1b)

The dynamics is governed by the following equations of motion (EoM)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\phi_{a}+\frac{1}{{\chi_{I}}}\,n_{ab}\phi_{b}

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{0}\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta\phi_{a}}+\theta_{a}\,,

(2a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}n_{ab}+\nabla\cdot(\nabla\phi_{[a}\phi_{b]})

\displaystyle=\sigma_{0}\nabla^{2}\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta n_{ab}}+\partial_{i}\Xi_{ab}^{i}\,.

(2b)

Here the left-hand side of the equations of motion encodes the ideal symplectic
structure of the system and determines the superfluid dynamics discussed below.
The right-hand side encodes the dissipative dynamics, governed by the relaxation
coefficient
\Gamma_{0}
and the charge conductivity
\sigma_{0}
. Finally, the noise
terms
\theta
and
\Xi
reproduce the fluctuation-dissipation theorem.

At fixed coupling
\lambda
, the model has a critical mass
m_{c}^{2}
; for
example, for
\lambda=4
,
m_{c}^{2}=-4.811

Florio:2021jlx
;
Florio:2023kmy
. For
m_{0}^{2}>m_{c}^{2}
, the
O(4)
symmetry remains
unbroken, and the mean value of the order parameter
\phi_{a}
(the chiral
condensate in QCD) vanishes. For
m_{0}^{2}<m_{c}^{2}
, the
O(4)
symmetry is
spontaneously broken to
O(3)
, producing three Goldstone modes

\vec{\pi}=(\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi_{3})
, identified as pions.

In the broken phase, the magnitude of the order parameter
\phi_{a}
is approximately constant, and the left-hand side of
2
describes the
coupling between conserved charges and Goldstone modes (or the direction of
\phi_{a}
). When the
dissipative terms on the right-hand side are neglected, the resulting
coupled equations correspond to an ideal non-abelian superfluid, reflecting the
breaking of
O(4)
symmetry
Son:1999pa
;
Grossi:2020ezz
. In this limit, the ideal superfluid theory depends on just two
parameters:
f^{2}=\phi_{a}\phi_{a}
and the susceptibility
\chi_{I}
, both
of which can be determined from Euclidean measurements in lattice
QCD
Bazavov:2019www
;
Ce:2022dax
;
Krasniqi:2024kwm
.

A linearized analysis of the superfluid equations (including dissipation)
yields the pion dispersion relation,

\omega(k)=vk-iD_{A}k^{2}/2
, with
v^{2}=f^{2}/\chi_{I}

Son:2002ci
.
Thus, the propagation of long-wavelength pions is characterized by two
timescales: a “ballistic” timescale
1/(vk)
and a “diffusive” timescale

1/(D_{A}k^{2})
.

Even near the critical point, these timescales remain parametrically
separated for
k\xi\ll 1
. Matching the hydrodynamic EFT to the
underlying critical theory gives
v\sim\xi^{(2-d)/2}\sim\xi/\tau_{R}
,
where
\xi
is the diverging correlation length and

\tau_{R}\sim\xi^{d/2}
is the relaxation time of the order
parameter
Son:2001ff
. Meanwhile, the diffusion constant scales as

D_{A}\sim\xi^{2}/\tau_{R}

Son:2002ci
.

For a system of size
L
with
k\sim 1/L
, we then find the parametric
separation:

t_{B}\equiv\frac{L}{v}\sim\tau_{R}\left(\frac{L}{\xi}\right)\ ,

(3)

and

t_{D}\equiv\frac{L^{2}}{D_{A}}\sim\tau_{R}\left(\frac{L}{\xi}\right)^{2}\ .

(4)

Thus, in the limit of large systems,
L/\xi\gg 1
, the hierarchy

\tau_{R}\ll t_{B}\ll t_{D}
holds near the critical point.
As we will see, when the system undergoes an instantaneous quench from the
symmetric to the broken phase, the hierarchy of timescales leads to a strong enhancement of the infrared spectrum of the Goldstone modes (pions).

Quenches and scaling analysis.

We consider the setup shown in
Fig.

1
. The system begins in
thermal equilibrium at
T>T_{c}
(
m_{0}^{2}>m_{c}^{2}
, red-dashed
potential) and is suddenly quenched into the broken phase at
T<T_{c}
(
m_{0}^{2}<m_{c}^{2}
, blue potential), with the quench tuned so that the
correlation length
\xi
remains the same before and after. The initial
state is then metastable. In a region of size
\ell
, several correlation
lengths long, fluctuations of the locally averaged order parameter
\bar{\phi}_{a}
are of order
\sim\sqrt{\chi/\ell^{d}}
, where
\chi\sim\xi^{2-\eta}
is the chiral susceptibility. These fluctuations trigger the
instability, and the local order parameter begins to “roll down the hill” of
the potential, approaching the new equilibrium value
\bar{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}\sim\xi^{-(d-2+\eta)/2}
on a short timescale
\sim\tau_{R}
. At this
point, many small, randomly oriented domains have formed. On a longer timescale

t\sim L/v
, these domains begin to grow and merge. Since this growth time

t_{B}\sim L/v
is much longer than the relaxation time
\tau_{R}
, the
superfluid (hydrodynamic) limit of Model G provides an appropriate dynamical
description of the condensate growth. We will now elaborate on this picture.

As a proxy for the global condensate, we focus on “magnetization”

M_{a}(t)=\phi_{a}(t,{\bm{k}}=0)
, which is the zeroth mode of the Fourier transformed
order parameter
\phi_{a}(t,{\bm{k}})\equiv\frac{1}{V}\sum_{\bf x}e^{i{\bm{k}}\cdot{\bf x}}\phi_{a}(t,{\bf x})
. In addition, we consider the three pion fields

\pi^{l}(t,{\bm{k}})
, which are projections of
\phi_{a}(t,{\bm{k}})
orthogonal to
M_{a}
. In
particular, we study the non-equilibrium evolution of the following equal-time
two-point correlation functions

\displaystyle G_{0}(t)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{V}{4}\sum_{a=1}^{4}\left\langle M_{a}(t)M_{a}(t)\right\rangle\ ,

(5)

\displaystyle G_{\pi\pi}(t,{\bm{k}})

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{V}{3}\sum_{\ell=1}^{3}\left\langle\pi^{\ell}(t,{\bm{k}})\pi^{\ell}(t,-{\bm{k}})\right\rangle\ .

(6)

We refer to our companion paper
superpaper5
for the complete discussion.

In equilibrium the magnetization becomes of order
M_{a}\sim\bar{\sigma}_{\rm eq}
, which implies that
G_{0}(t)\sim V\bar{\sigma}_{\rm eq}^{2}\sim\xi^{2-\eta}\left(L/\xi\right)^{d}
.
Although locally the order parameter finds the new minimum on timescale
\tau_{R}
, their global alignment and growth of magnetization takes place over the ballistic timescale
t_{B}
, i.e., the time Goldstones (pions) need to propagate the volume. This statement is generic but is predictive only because of the parametric scale separation in
3
and
4
. Having recognized the relevant timescale and asymptote leads us to formulate the following form of the scaling hypothesis for the non-equilibrium evolution of the correlation function
G_{0}
for intermediate times
\tau_{R}\ll t\ll t_{D}

G_{0}(t,\xi,L)=\xi^{2-\eta}\left(\frac{L}{\xi}\right)^{d}\mathcal{F}(vt/L,\xi/L)\ .

(7)

So far, since
v\sim\xi/\tau_{R}
, this is a simple rewrite of the usual statement of the dynamical scaling hypothesis, where time is scaled by
\tau_{R}\propto\xi^{\zeta}
and lengths are scaled by
\xi
. Now, however, for
L\rightarrow\infty
and
vt/L
fixed, we expect
\mathcal{F}
to be a regular function as
\xi/L\to 0
and the
\xi/L
dependence can be dropped. In other words, after factoring out explicitly the extensive behavior of the condensate, we expect the remaining functional dependence to have a well-behaved thermodynamic limit. For times
\tau_{R}\ll t\ll t_{B}
,
G_{0}
must be independent of system volume, so
\mathcal{F}(vt/L)\sim(vt/L)^{d}
and the magnetization grows as a power-law in time
G_{0}=\xi^{2-\eta}(t/\tau_{R})^{d}
. These scalings are the mathematical reflection of
Fig.

1
.

We have identified a significant regime with times

\tau_{R}\ll t\sim t_{B}\ll t_{D}
. Since the relevant lengths

\sim vt
are large compared to the correlation length
\xi
,
and the times are long compared to
\tau_{R}
, this is a
hydrodynamic regime with locally broken symmetry.
However, dissipation does not become important until
times
\sim t_{D}
. Therefore, the appropriate hydrodynamic
description in this regime is given by the ideal superfluid
equations discussed above.
In the future, these superfluid equations can be simulated directly to determine condensate
growth.

The same scaling analysis can be applied to the pion correlation function
6
.
Still, in the regime where
\xi\ll L
, for times
t\lesssim t_{B}
, all directions in field space are uncorrelated and should behave similarly. It is therefore reasonable to expect the pion correlator scales in a similar fashion
G_{\pi\pi}(t,\xi,k,L)=\xi^{2-\eta}\left(L/\xi\right)^{d}\mathcal{F}(vkt,kL)
with
\mathcal{F}
again regular in its arguments. For
1\ll kL\ll L/\xi
and
vkt\sim 1
, we expect the correlation function to be volume independent. Hence, we anticipate
\mathcal{F}(vkt,kL)
to simplify to
\mathcal{F}(vkt)/(kL)^{d}
. As a result, we obtain for
\tau_{R}\ll t\ll t_{D}

G_{\pi\pi}(t,\xi,k)=\frac{\xi^{2-\eta}}{(k\xi)^{d}}\mathcal{F}(vkt)\ ,

(8)

where
k\xi\ll 1
and
vkt\sim 1
.

The scaling arguments can also be extended to small but finite pion masses
m
(i.e. in the presence of a small explicit symmetry breaking). The scaling form becomes
G_{\pi\pi}(t,\xi,k,L,m)=\xi^{2-\eta}\left(L/\xi\right)^{d}\mathcal{F}(vmt,kL,mL)
. Assuming that
\mathcal{F}
is volume independent for momenta such that
1/L\ll k\ll m\ll 1/\xi
, immediately implies

G_{\pi\pi}(t,\xi,k,m)=\xi^{2-\eta}\left(\frac{1}{m\xi}\right)^{d}\mathcal{F}(vmt)\ .

(9)

The effects of the non-equilibrium dynamics are striking. Recalling that the late-time, equilibrium value of the pion correlator is
G^{\mathrm{eq}}_{\pi\pi}(\xi,k)\sim 1/{((k\xi)^{2}+(m\xi)^{2})}

Grossi:2021gqi
,
Eqs.

8
and
9
correspond to a parametric enhancement over the equilibrium values (
d=3
)

\frac{G_{\pi\pi}(t,k)}{G^{\mathrm{eq}}_{\pi\pi}(k)}\underset{t\sim 1/vk}{\sim}\begin{cases}1/k\xi\,,&k\gg m\\
1/{m\xi}\,,&k\ll m\end{cases}.

(10)

This is the key result of our analysis. We predict that during sudden quenches to the broken phase, the infrared spectrum of Goldstones (pions) is parametrically enhanced relative to equilibrium expectation.

Discussion.
The chiral phase transition in QCD spontaneously breaks

O(4)\simeq SU(2)_{L}\times SU(2)_{R}
symmetry below
T_{c}
, which leads to the formation of
a non-abelian superfluid. The associated Goldstone modes are pions and their
dynamic critical behavior belongs to the universality class of Model G
hohenberg
. For the case of instantaneous quenches to the broken
phase, we use generic scaling arguments and explicit numerical simulations to
understand the non-equilibrium dynamics of two-point correlation functions and condensate growth
(see also the companion paper
superpaper5
). We identify three
parametrically separated time scales: local relaxation
\tau_{R}
, ballistic
transport
\tau_{R}(L/\xi)
, and diffusion
\tau_{R}(L/\xi)^{2}
. The key result of
our analysis is that at intermediate ballistic times, the infrared spectrum
of the pion correlation function is parametrically enhanced over the
equilibrium expectation,
Eq.

10
. Crucially, this effect is
robust to explicit chiral symmetric breaking, i.e., for finite pion masses.

Chiral symmetry is a fundamental property of QCD and the experimental
observation of the associated dynamic critical behavior in high-energy
nuclear collisions would be a breakthrough discovery. Our results should be a
strong motivation for future experiments, such as
ALICE3
ALICE:2022wwr
, to carefully measure the yield and correlations of
low momentum pions (at or below the pion mass). At the same time,
the standard hydrodynamic models of heavy ion collisions should include the
chiral critical dynamics in their phenomenological predictions.
Although not a simple task, systematically including
the stochastic Model
G
dynamics based on
Florio:2021jlx
;
Basar:2024qxd
into
real-world hydro codes is an essential next step.

Of immediate relevance is the yield of low-momentum pions, which begins to rise
above the equilibrium hydro curve for
p_{T}\lesssim 400\,{\rm MeV}
, reaching a

\sim 50\%
enhancemnt for
p_{T}\sim m_{\pi}

Lu:2024upk
. The main challenge in
reproducing this trend with current hydro models is the absence of
a soft momentum scale that can cause the deviation from equilibrium only at small

p_{T}
. While it is still too early to draw definitive conclusions, our analysis
naturally explains why Goldstone pions should exhibit this behavior.

We conclude by reiterating that critical dynamics is inherent to QCD physics.
Our work is part of a broader effort to understand the universal dynamical
properties of non-equilibrium critical systems. Therefore our results are
relevant to non-abelian superfluids in other systems
Jain:2016rlz
;
Glorioso:2020loc
;
Buza:2024jxe
. Although in
this work we focused on two-point functions, the critical dynamics will
certainly manifest in higher-order correlation functions. In the context of
heavy-ion physics, this can be studied by analyzing the fluctuations of soft pion yields
and Hanbury-Brown and Twiss like correlations
Wiedemann:1999qn
.
We look forward to further theoretical and experimental explorations of these effects.

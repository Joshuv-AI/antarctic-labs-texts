On Expressive Power of Quantized Neural Networks under Fixed-Point Arithmetic
Yeachan Park, Sejun Park, Geonho Hwang

Abstract
Existing works on the expressive power of neural networks typically assume real parameters and exact operations. In this work, we study the expressive power of quantized networks under discrete fixed-point parameters and inexact fixed-point operations with round-off errors. We first provide a necessary condition and a sufficient condition on fixed-point arithmetic and activation functions for quantized networks to represent all fixed-point functions from fixed-point vectors to fixed-point numbers. Then, we show that various popular activation functions satisfy our sufficient condition, e.g., Sigmoid, ReLU, ELU, SoftPlus, SiLU, Mish, and GELU. In other words, networks using those activation functions are capable of representing all fixed-point functions. We further show that our necessary condition and sufficient condition coincide under a mild condition on activation functions: e.g., for an activation function σ \sigma , there exists a fixed-point number x x such that σ ⁡ ( x ) = 0 \sigma(x)=0 . Namely, we find a necessary and sufficient condition for a large class of activation functions. We lastly show that even quantized networks using binary weights in { − 1 , 1 } \{-1,1\} can also represent all fixed-point functions for practical activation functions.

Introduction

In the theory of neural networks, a class of results called the
universal approximation theorem
state that for any continuous function
f^{*}
on a compact domain
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and for any error bound
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a neural network
f
such that
f^{*}

\sup_{x\in\mathcal{C}}\|f^{*}(x)-f(x)\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon
.
Such results have been established for various network architectures (e.g., multi-layer perceptron
Cybenko (1989)
;
Hornik et al. (1989)
;
Leshno et al. (1993)
;
Pinkus (1999)
;
Yarotsky (2017)
, convolutional neural networks
Zhou (2020)
;
Shen et al. (2022)
, transformer
Yun et al. (2020a)
;
Yun et al. (2020b)
;
Alberti et al. (2023)
;
Kajitsuka and Sato (2024)
) and various error measures (e.g.,
L^{p}
norm
Hornik et al. (1989)
;
Park et al. (2021)
;
Kim et al. (2024)
;
Shin et al. (2025)
and uniform norm
Cybenko (1989)
;
Leshno et al. (1993)
;
Kidger and Lyons (2020)
).
Furthermore, they are often considered proof of the following common belief:

Neural networks in computers can approximate any continuous function.

However, such a common belief does not naturally follow from the existing results. This is because existing theory uses real numbers and exact mathematical operations in its analyses, but computers can only represent a finite subset of reals and perform inexact operations due to the bounded precision and running time.

Such a discrepancy between theory and practice becomes more serious
when networks are implemented with low-precision operations, where the round-off error is large.
In particular, with the recent exponential growth of the number of parameters in state-of-the-art networks, low-precision parameters and operations are widely adopted
Hubara et al. (2018)
;
Li et al. (2025)
;
Liu et al. (2025)
.
Network quantization is a popular method that can reduce the memory and computation costs of networks by using low-precision fixed-point parameters and low-cost integer operations
Huai et al. (2023)
;
Jacob et al. (2018)
;
Jin et al. (2021)
;
Li and Gu (2023)
;
Sari et al. (2021)
;
Wang et al. (2022)
;
Yao et al. (2021)
;
Zhao et al. (2021)
. Surprisingly, although quantized networks using fixed-point arithmetic have discrete parameters and non-negligible round-off errors in their evaluation, they have successfully reduced memory and computation costs while preserving the performance of their unquantized counterparts.

Only a few works have investigated the expressive power of networks using discrete parameters and/or inexact machine operations. For example, Ding et al.
Ding et al. (2018)
show networks using quantized (i.e., discrete) weights and exact mathematical operations can universally approximate.
In addition, Gonon et al.
Gonon et al. (2023)
analyze the approximation error incurred by quantizing real network parameters through nearest rounding.
However, almost all existing works consider operations without error (i.e., exact), and thus, are not applicable to quantized networks using fixed-point operations.
The only exceptions are recent works that show networks under floating-point arithmetic (i.e., floating-point parameters and floating-point operations) can represent almost all floating-point functions
Park et al. (2024)
;
Hwang et al. (2025a)
;
Hwang et al. (2025b)
.
Nevertheless, these results assume floating-point arithmetic only, and hence, it does not apply to quantized networks using fixed-point arithmetic. Namely, what modern quantized networks using general activation functions can or cannot express is still unknown.

In this work, we study the expressive power of quantized networks using fixed-point arithmetic only.

We also show that our sufficient condition coincides with the necessary condition introduced in

\mathrm{Sigmoid}
,
\mathrm{ReLU}
,
\mathrm{ELU}
,
\mathrm{SoftPlus}
,
\mathrm{SiLU}
,

, and

.
We further extend our results to quantized networks with binary weights (i.e.,

) in

.

3.1
Necessary condition for universal representation

Classical universal approximation theorems state that under real parameters and exact mathematical operations, neural networks with two layers and any non-polynomial activation function can universally approximate
Leshno et al. (1993)
. Furthermore, for deeper networks, it is known that networks using non-affine polynomial activation functions can also universally approximate
Kidger and Lyons (2020)
.
This implies that under real parameters and exact mathematical operations, any non-affine continuous activation function suffices for universal approximation.
However, this is not the case for the quantized networks. In this section, we show that there are non-affine activation functions and
\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
for which quantized networks cannot universally represent.
In particular, we study such activation functions and
\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
by formalizing a necessary condition on the activation function
\sigma
and
\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
for universal approximation.

Recall a
\sigma
quantized network
f:\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}^{d}\to\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
defined in
Eq.

7
:

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{x};\mathbb{Q}_{p,s})=\left\lceil{\rho_{L}}\right\rfloor\circ\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor\circ\left\lceil{\rho_{L-1}}\right\rfloor\circ\cdots\circ\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor\circ\left\lceil{\rho_{1}}\right\rfloor(\mathbf{x}).

If
f
has more than one layer, since
the output dimension of
f
is one and
, the output of
\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor\circ\left\lceil{\rho_{L-1}}\right\rfloor\circ\cdots\circ\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor\circ\left\lceil{\rho_{1}}\right\rfloor
is always in
\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor(\mathbb{Q}_{p,s})
,

given the last layer weights
\mathbf{w}_{L,1}=(w_{L,1,1},\dots,w_{L,1,|\mathcal{I}_{L,1}|})\in\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}^{|\mathcal{I}_{L,1}|}

and bias
b_{L,1}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\infty,s}
for
\left\lceil{\rho_{L}}\right\rfloor
, the output of
f
always satisfies

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{x};\mathbb{Q}_{p,s})

\displaystyle\in\left\{\left\lceil{b_{L,1}+\sum_{j=1}^{|\mathcal{I}_{L,1}|}w_{L,1,j}z_{j}}\right\rfloor:z_{j}\in\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor(\mathbb{Q}_{p,s})\right\},

(9)

for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}^{d}
.
To introduce our necessary condition, for each
b\in\mathbb{Q}_{\infty,s}
, we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\sigma,p,s,b}\triangleq\left\{\left\lceil{b+\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}x_{i}}\right\rfloor:n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},w_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p,s},x_{i}\in\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor(\mathbb{Q}_{p,s})~\forall i\in[n]\right\}.

Then, by
Eq.

9
, one can easily observe that
f(\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}^{d};\mathbb{Q}_{p,s})\subset\mathcal{N}_{\sigma,p,s,b_{L,1}}
, which implies that for any
\sigma
quantized network
{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{f}}}
under
\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
with more than one layer, we have

\displaystyle{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{f}}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}^{d};\mathbb{Q}_{p,s})\subset\mathcal{N}_{\sigma,p,s,b}

(10)

for some
b\in\mathbb{Q}_{\infty,s}
.

We are now ready to introduce our necessary condition. Suppose that
\sigma
quantized networks under
\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
with more than one layer can universally represent.
Let
f^{*}:\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}^{d}\to\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
be a target function such that
f^{*}(\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}^{d})=\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
(e.g.,
f^{*}(\mathbf{x})=x_{1}
).
Since
\sigma
quantized networks can universally represent, there exists a
\sigma
quantized network
{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{f}}}
with more than one layer such that
{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{f}}}=f^{*}
.
Furthermore, since
f^{*}(\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}^{d})=\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
, by
Eq.

10
, we must have

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}=f^{*}({{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}^{d}}}})={{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{f}}}({{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}^{d}}}};\mathbb{Q}_{p,s})\subset\mathcal{N}_{\sigma,p,s,b}\subset\mathbb{Q}_{p,s},

for some
b\in\mathbb{Q}_{\infty,s}
; that is,
\mathcal{N}_{\sigma,p,s,b}=\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
for some
b\in\mathbb{Q}_{\infty,s}
, which is our necessary condition.
We formally state this necessary condition and extend our analysis to include one-layer networks in the following theorem.
For completeness, we provide the detailed proof of
Theorem

1
in
Section

5.1
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
and
p,s\in\mathbb{N}
. If
\sigma

\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
can universally represent, then there exists
b\in\mathbb{Q}_{\infty,s}
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\sigma,p,s,b}=\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}.

(11)

Theorem

1
states that if
\mathcal{N}_{\sigma,p,s,b}\neq\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
for all

, then
\sigma
quantized networks cannot universally represent.

Note that this necessary condition is independent of the input dimension

Based on
Theorem

1
, we characterize a class of activation functions and
\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
that cannot universally represent in the following lemma. We defer the proof of
Lemma

2
in
Section

.

.

Let
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
and

.
Suppose that there exists a natural number

such that

,
sr\in\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
,
\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor(x)\in\mathbb{Z}

3
3

3

If

While any non-affine continuous activation function suffices for universal approximation under real parameters and exact mathematical operations,

3.2
Sufficient condition for universal representation

In this section, we introduce a sufficient condition for universal representation of quantized networks. That is, if an activation function and
\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
satisfy our sufficient condition, then we can represent any target continuous function from
\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}^{d}
to
\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
via some quantized network. To this end, we explicitly construct indicator functions with some coefficient
\gamma\in\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
using quantized networks: for a
d
-dimensional
quantized cube

\mathcal{C}=(\prod_{i=1}^{d}[\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}])\cap\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\gamma\times\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{C}}\left({\mathbf{x}}\right)=\begin{cases}\gamma~&\text{if}~\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{C},\\
0~&\text{if}~\mathbf{x}\notin\mathcal{C}.\end{cases}

We then represent a target function (say
f^{*}:\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}^{d}\to\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
) as

\displaystyle f^{*}(\mathbf{x})=\left\lceil{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{i}\times\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{C}_{i}}\left({\mathbf{x}}\right)}\right\rfloor{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\quad\text{for all }\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}^{d}}}},

(12)

and for some partition
\{\mathcal{C}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{C}_{k}\}
of
\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}^{d}
,
where each
\mathcal{C}_{i}
is a
d
-dimensional quantized cube of a small sidelength (e.g.,
\mathcal{C}_{i}
is a singleton set), and for some
\gamma_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
representing
f^{*}(\mathcal{C}_{i})
.

From
Eq.

12
, it is easy to observe that if we can implement

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}^{d}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}_{p,s},

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle\longmapsto\gamma\times\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{C}}\left({\mathbf{x}}\right),

for arbitrary
\gamma\in\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
using a
\sigma
quantized network, then
\sigma
quantized networks can universally represent.
However, as we observed in the necessary condition for universal representation in
Theorem

1
, not all activation functions can implement every
\gamma\times\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{C}}\left({\mathbf{x}}\right)
(if they could, quantized networks using any activation function would universally represent). Hence, in this section, we derive a sufficient condition on activation functions
\sigma
and
\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
under which we can implement
\gamma\times\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{C}}\left({\mathbf{x}}\right)
for all
\gamma\in\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
.

The rest of this section is organized as follows: we first introduce a class of activation functions and
\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
that we focus on; then, we characterize a class of
\gamma\in\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
such that
\sigma
quantized network can implement
\gamma\times\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{C}}\left({\mathbf{x}}\right)
.
Using this, we next formalize our sufficient condition. We also verify whether quantized networks under practical activation functions and quantization setups can universally represent using our sufficient condition. We lastly discuss the necessity of our sufficient condition.

3.2.1
Activation functions of interest

We primarily consider activation functions and
\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
satisfying the following condition.

Condition 1
.

For an activation function
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
, there exist
\alpha,\beta\in\{-1,1\}
,
\rho:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, and
z\in\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
satisfying the following:

•

\sigma(x)=\alpha\rho(\beta x)
,

•

z\neq\min\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
,

•

\left\lceil{\rho}\right\rfloor(x)=\max\left\lceil{\rho}\right\rfloor(\mathbb{Q}_{p,s})
for all
x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
such that
x\geq z
, and

•

\left\lceil{\rho}\right\rfloor(x)<\max\left\lceil{\rho}\right\rfloor(\mathbb{Q}_{p,s})
for all
x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
such that
x<z
.

Condition

1
with the case
\alpha=\beta=1
characterizes a class of activation functions and
\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
such that

•

\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor
is non-constant on
\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
and

•

\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor(x)=\max_{z\in\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor(z)
if and only if
x\in[z,\max\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}]\cap\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
.

Compared to the
\alpha=\beta=1
case, different values of
\alpha
and
\beta
change the
maximum
to the
minimum
and the interval
[z,\max\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}]\cap\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
to the interval
[\min\mathbb{Q}_{p,s},z]\cap\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
, respectively.

It is easy to observe that any monotone activation function
\sigma
with non-constant
\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor
satisfies
Condition

1
, e.g.,
\mathrm{ReLU}
, leaky-
\mathrm{ReLU}
,
\mathrm{SoftPlus}
,
\mathrm{Sigmoid}
, etc.
We formally present this observation in
Lemma

4
. The proof is provided in
Section

5.3
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
p,s\in\mathbb{N}
. If
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is monotone and there exist
x,y\in\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
such that
\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor(x)\neq\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor(y)
(i.e.,
\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor
is non-constant on
\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
), then
\sigma
and
\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
satisfy
Condition

1
.

We note that popular non-monotone activation functions such as GELU, SiLU, and Mish also satisfy
Condition

1
for all
\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
.

3.2.2
Implementing indicator functions via quantized networks

We implement indicator functions using
\sigma
quantized networks under
\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
for
\sigma
and
\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
satisfying
Condition

1
.
To describe a class of implementable indicator functions, we define the following sets: for
b\in\mathbb{Q}_{\infty,s}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\sigma,p,s}

\displaystyle\triangleq\left\{\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor(x)-\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor(y):x,y\in\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,p,s}^{\circ}

\displaystyle\triangleq\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}x_{i}:n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},w_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p,s},x_{i}\in\mathcal{V}_{\sigma,p,s}~\forall i\in[n]\right\}.

\mathcal{V}_{\sigma,p,s}
is a set of all gaps between possible outputs of
\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor
, and
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,p,s}^{\circ}
is a set of all linear combinations of elements in
\mathcal{V}_{\sigma,p,s}
.
Using this definition, we characterize a class of indicator functions (of the form
\gamma\times\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{C}}\left({\mathbf{x}}\right)
) via the following lemma.
We provide the proof of
Lemma

5
in
Section

5.4
.

Lemma 5
.

Let
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
and
p,s,d\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying
s\leq 2^{p}-1
.
Let
\alpha_{1},\beta_{1},\dots,\alpha_{d},\beta_{d}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
such that
\alpha_{i}\leq\beta_{i}
for all
i\in[d]
and let
\mathcal{C}=(\prod_{i=1}^{d}[\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}])\cap\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}^{d}
.
Suppose that
\sigma
and
\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
satisfy
Condition

1
.
Then, for each
\gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,p,s}^{\circ}
, there exist
d^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
, an affine transformation
\rho:\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}}\to\mathbb{R}
with quantized weights and without bias (i.e.,
\rho={\mathrm{aff}}(\,\cdot\,;\mathbf{w},0,\mathcal{I})
for some
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}^{|\mathcal{I}|}
and
b=0\in\mathbb{Q}_{\infty,s}
), and a three-layer
\sigma

\displaystyle\rho\circ\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor\circ f(\mathbf{x};\mathbb{Q}_{p,s})=\gamma\times\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{C}}\left({\mathbf{x}}\right)\quad\forall\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}.

(13)

Lemma

5
states that if
\gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,p,s}^{\circ}
, then
\gamma\times\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{C}}\left({\mathbf{x}}\right)
can be implemented by a composition of a
\sigma
quantized network, a quantized activation function, and a linear transformation
\rho
with quantized weights and
unquantized output
.
Here, we do not introduce the bias parameter in
\rho
and do not quantize the output of
\rho
; this is because our final
quantized network construction (say
f
) that represents a target function has the following form: for a partition
\mathcal{C}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{C}_{k}
of
\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
,

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{x};\mathbb{Q}_{p,s})=\left\lceil{b+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{i}\times\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{C}_{i}}\left({\mathbf{x}}\right)}\right\rfloor=\left\lceil{\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\gamma_{i}+b)\times\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{C}_{i}}\left({\mathbf{x}}\right)}\right\rfloor,

(14)

as in
Eq.

12
. Note that the second equality holds since
\mathcal{C}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{C}_{k}
is a partition of
\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
.
Namely, we will add the bias parameter and quantize the final output after summing the indicator functions.

3.2.3
Our sufficient condition

To describe our sufficient condition for universal representation, we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,p,s,b}

\displaystyle\triangleq\left\{\left\lceil{z+b}\right\rfloor\in\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}:z\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,p,s}^{\circ}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\left\lceil{b+\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}x_{i}}\right\rfloor\in\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}:n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},w_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p,s},x_{i}\in\mathcal{V}_{\sigma,p,s}~\forall i\in[n]\right\}.

Given
Lemma

5
and
Eq.

14
,
if
\{\mathcal{C}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{C}_{k}\}
is a partition of
\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}^{d}
where each
\mathcal{C}_{i}
is a quantized cube, then we can construct a
\sigma
quantized network

of the following form: for any
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{k}\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,p,s,b}

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{x};\mathbb{Q}_{p,s})

\displaystyle=\left\lceil{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{i}\times\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{C}_{i}}\left({\mathbf{x}}\right)}\right\rfloor=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left\lceil{\gamma_{i}}\right\rfloor\times\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{C}_{i}}\left({\mathbf{x}}\right).

(15)

Here, the last inequality holds since
\mathcal{C}_{i}\cap\mathcal{C}_{j}=\emptyset
if
i\neq j
.
Since
\left\lceil{\gamma_{i}}\right\rfloor\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,p,s,b}
if
\gamma_{i}-b\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,p,s}^{\circ}
, one can conclude that we can construct a
\sigma
quantized network

as in

, for any
\left\lceil{\gamma_{i}}\right\rfloor\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,p,s,b}
.
Namely, if
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,p,s,b}=\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
for some

, then
\sigma
quantized networks can universally represent by choosing proper
\{\mathcal{C}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{C}_{k}\}
(e.g., each
\mathcal{C}_{i}
is a singleton set).
We formally present this sufficient condition in
Theorem

6
, whose proof is provided in
Section

.

Theorem 6
.

Let
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
and
p,s\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying
s\leq 2^{p}-1
. Suppose that
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
satisfy
Condition

1
.
If there exists
b\in\mathbb{Q}_{\infty,s}
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,p,s,b}=\mathbb{Q}_{p,s},

then
\sigma
quantized networks under
\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
can universally represent.

One representative activation function that satisfies the condition in
Theorem

6
for all
p,s\in\mathbb{N}
is the identity function
\sigma(x)=x
; in this case,
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,p,s,0}=\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
.
This shows a gap between classical universal approximation results and ours; if a network uses real parameters and exact mathematical operations, then it can only express affine maps with the identity activation function, i.e., the network cannot universally approximate.
Nevertheless, quantized networks with the identity activation function can universally represent as stated in
Theorem

6
. This is because fixed-point additions and multiplications in quantized networks are non-affine due to rounding errors.
We note that a similar observation has been recently made under the floating-point arithmetic
Hwang et al. (2025a)
;
Hwang et al. (2025b)
.

We next provide an easily verifiable condition for activation functions that guarantees
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,p,s,b}=\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
(i.e., the universal representation property).
We provide the proof of
Lemma

7
in

.

Lemma 7
.

Let
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function and natural numbers

satisfying
s\leq 2^{p}-1
.
If
\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
and

satisfy one of the following conditions, then
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,p,s,b}=\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
.

(A1)

There exist
q_{1},q_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that,
{-2^{p}+1}\leq q_{1}<q_{2}\leq{2^{p}-1}
,
\sigma
is differentiable on
\left(\frac{q_{1}}{s},\frac{q_{2}}{s}\right)
,
|\sigma^{\prime}(x)|<1
, and
|\sigma(x)|\leq\frac{2^{p}-1}{s}
for
x\in\left(\frac{q_{1}}{s},\frac{q_{2}}{s}\right)
, and

\left|\sigma\left(\frac{q_{2}}{s}\right)-\sigma\left(\frac{q_{1}}{s}\right)\right|\geq\frac{1}{s}.

(A2)

There exist
q_{1},q_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
{-2^{p}+1}\leq q_{1}<q_{2}\leq{2^{p}-1}
,
\sigma
is differentiable on
\left(\frac{q_{1}}{s},\frac{q_{2}}{s}\right)
,
|\sigma^{\prime}(x)|\leq 1
, and
0\leq\sigma(x)\leq\frac{2^{p}-1}{s}
for
x\in\left(\frac{q_{1}}{s},\frac{q_{2}}{s}\right)
, and

\left|\sigma\left(\frac{q_{2}}{s}\right)-\sigma\left(\frac{q_{1}}{s}\right)\right|\geq\frac{1}{s}.

(A3)

There exist
q_{1},q_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
{-2^{p}+1}\leq q_{1}<q_{2}\leq{2^{p}-1}
,
\sigma
is differentiable on
\left(\frac{q_{1}}{s},\frac{q_{2}}{s}\right)
,
1\leq\sigma^{\prime}(x)\leq 2
, and
|\sigma(x)|\leq\frac{2^{p}-1}{s}
for
x\in\left(\frac{q_{1}}{s},\frac{q_{2}}{s}\right)
, and

\left|\sigma\left(\frac{q_{2}}{s}\right)-\sigma\left(\frac{q_{1}}{s}\right)\right|<\frac{2(q_{2}-q_{1})-1}{s}.

Using
Lemma

7
, we show that for many practical activation functions such that
\mathrm{ReLU}
,
\mathrm{ELU}
,
\mathrm{SiLU}
,
\mathrm{Mish}
,
\mathrm{GELU}
,
\mathrm{SoftPlus}
, and
\mathrm{Sigmoid}
, we have
\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}=\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,p,s,b}
.
We provide the proof of
Lemma

8
in
Section

5.7
.

Lemma 8
.

Consider
p,s\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying
s\leq 2^{p}-1
.
For the activation functions
\mathrm{ReLU}
and
\mathrm{ELU}

For

.

In

and

for various activation functions to universally represent. Specifically, the condition on

is only to ensure

, which is sufficient for universal representation (see

for more details).
In practice, researchers typically share a single

for all layers but use different values of

for different layers to optimize the quantization performance; our problem setup sharing a single

for all layers can be viewed as a special case of this, i.e., our sufficiency results also extend to a practical setup if we can choose

in each layer by ourselves.
For example, a conventional way to choose

to cover the range of (full-precision) weights in an unquantized model. In this case, for the INT8 quantization (
p=7
) using PyTorch’s post-training static quantization framework, we found that

ranges from

125
to
480
in the ResNet18
He et al. (2016)
,

38
to

in the MobileNetV2

pretrained on ImageNet

, and

pretrained on BookCorpus
Bandy and Vincent (2021)
.

One notable empirical observation here is that while

in the first layers,
\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
may not contain
1
from the second layer. Nevertheless, we can still simulate the weight of value one (or larger) by duplicating the same neurons, multiplying them the same weight (e.g.,
1/s
) and adding them (e.g.,

times). We note that such a duplication strategy may not work in the first layer as we typically do not duplicate the inputs.

Our necessary and sufficient condition

3.3
Universal representation with binary weights

Recent research has demonstrated that neural networks with parameters quantized to one-bit precision can achieve performance comparable to their full-precision counterparts while significantly reducing multiplication costs
Ma et al. (2024)
;
Wang et al. (2023)
.
In this section, we present both a necessary condition and a sufficient condition for networks with binary weights to possess universal representation property as in
Sections

3.1
and
3.2
.

Recall a
\sigma
quantized network
f:\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}^{d}\to\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
defined as in
Eq.

7
:

\displaystyle f=\left\lceil{\rho_{L}}\right\rfloor\circ\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor\circ\left\lceil{\rho_{L-1}}\right\rfloor\circ\cdots\circ\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor\circ\left\lceil{\rho_{1}}\right\rfloor.

(16)

We say that “
f
has binary weights” if all its weights in the affine transformations
\rho_{l}
are binary.
Specifically, for each
l,i
,

\mathbf{w}_{l,i}\in\{-1,1\}^{|\mathcal{I}_{l,i}|}.

Here, we allow non-binary bias parameters, i.e.,
b_{l,i}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\infty,s}
as in
Ma et al. (2024)
;
Wang et al. (2023)
.

As
\mathcal{N}_{\sigma,p,s,b},\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,p,s}^{\circ}
, and
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,p,s,b}
for quantized networks (see
Sections

3.1
and
3.2
), we analogously define the sets
\mathcal{B}\mathcal{N}_{\sigma,p,s,b}
,

, and
\mathcal{B}\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,p,s,b}
for quantized networks with binary weights as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\mathcal{N}_{\sigma,p,s,b}

\displaystyle\triangleq\left\{\left\lceil{b+\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}x_{i}}\right\rfloor:n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},w_{i}\in\{-1,1\},x_{i}\in\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor(\mathbb{Q}_{p,s})~\forall i\in[n]\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,p,s}^{\circ}

\displaystyle\triangleq\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}x_{i}:n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},w_{i}\in\{-1,1\},x_{i}\in\mathcal{V}_{\sigma,p,s}~\forall i\in[n]\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,p,s,b}

\displaystyle\triangleq\left\{\left\lceil{b+z}\right\rfloor:z\in\mathcal{B}\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,p,s}^{\circ}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\left\lceil{b+\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}x_{i}}\right\rfloor:n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},w_{i}\in\{-1,1\},x_{i}\in\mathcal{V}_{\sigma,p,s}~\forall i\in[n]\right\}.

Using these definitions, we derive a necessary condition and a sufficient condition for quantized networks with binary weights to achieve universal representation via the following lemmas and theorems. We present their proofs in Sections
5.9
–
5.13
, which are analogous to the results for general quantized networks.

We first present

, and

which describe our necessary condition.

Theorem 11
.

and

.

Let

Discussions

4.1
On naïve quantization of networks using real parameters

In this section, we show that naïve quantization of networks using real parameters can incur large errors. Namely, universal representation property of quantized networks does not directly follow from existing universal approximation results for networks using real parameters.
For
p,s\in\mathbb{N}
with
s\leq 2^{p}-1
, we consider a two-layer network
f:\mathbb{R}^{4s+1}\to\mathbb{R}
defined as follows:

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{x})=

\displaystyle\sum_{2}\left(\mathrm{ReLU}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2s+1}w_{1,i}x_{i}\right)\right)+\mathrm{ReLU}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{4s+1}w_{2,i}x_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+

\displaystyle\sum_{3}\left(-1\times\mathrm{ReLU}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2s+1}w_{3,i}x_{i}\right)\right)+\sum_{2}\left(-1\times\mathrm{ReLU}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s+1}w_{4,i}x_{i}\right)\right)

where

\displaystyle(w_{1,1},w_{1,2},w_{1,3},\dots,w_{1,2s+1})

\displaystyle=\left(1,-\frac{1}{4s},\dots,-\frac{1}{4s}\right),

\displaystyle(w_{2,1},w_{2,2},w_{2,3},\dots,w_{2,4s+1})

\displaystyle=\left(-1,\frac{1}{4s},\dots,\frac{1}{4s}\right),

\displaystyle(w_{3,1},w_{3,2},w_{3,3},\dots,w_{3,2s+1})

\displaystyle=\left(-1,\frac{1}{2s},\dots,\frac{1}{2s}\right),

\displaystyle(w_{4,1},w_{4,2},w_{4,3},\dots,w_{4,s+1})

\displaystyle=\left(-1,\frac{1}{2s},\dots,\frac{1}{2s}\right).

Then, we have

\displaystyle f(-\mathbb{1})=-1,\quad f(\mathbb{1})=1,

where
\mathbb{1}=(1,\dots,1)\in\mathbb{R}^{4s+1}
.
However, after performing quantization of weights into
\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
, we have

\displaystyle(\left\lceil{w_{1,1}}\right\rfloor,\left\lceil{w_{1,2}}\right\rfloor,\left\lceil{w_{1,3}}\right\rfloor,\dots,\left\lceil{w_{1,2s+1}}\right\rfloor)

\displaystyle=(1,0,0,\dots,0),

\displaystyle(\left\lceil{w_{2,1}}\right\rfloor,\left\lceil{w_{2,2}}\right\rfloor,\left\lceil{w_{2,3}}\right\rfloor,\dots,\left\lceil{w_{2,4s+1}}\right\rfloor)

\displaystyle=(-1,0,0,\dots,0),

\displaystyle(\left\lceil{w_{3,1}}\right\rfloor,\left\lceil{w_{3,2}}\right\rfloor,\left\lceil{w_{3,3}}\right\rfloor,\dots,\left\lceil{w_{3,2s+1}}\right\rfloor)

\displaystyle=\left(-1,\frac{1}{s},\frac{1}{s},\dots,\frac{1}{s}\right),

\displaystyle(\left\lceil{w_{4,1}}\right\rfloor,\left\lceil{w_{4,2}}\right\rfloor,\left\lceil{w_{4,3}}\right\rfloor,\dots,\left\lceil{w_{4,s+1}}\right\rfloor)

\displaystyle=\left(-1,\frac{1}{s},\frac{1}{s},\dots,\frac{1}{s}\right),

where
\left\lceil{\cdot}\right\rfloor
denotes
\left\lceil{\cdot}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}
.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\left\lceil{f}\right\rfloor(-\mathbb{1})=1,\quad\left\lceil{f}\right\rfloor(\mathbb{1})=-1.

This implies that a naïve quantization of a network using real parameters can incur large errors, and hence, existing universal approximation theorems for real parameters do not directly extend to quantized networks.

4.2
Number of parameters in our universal representator

In this section, we quantitatively analyze the number of parameters in our universal representator in
Theorem

6
.
Under the assumption in
Theorem

6
(i.e.,
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,p,s,b}=\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
for some
b\in\mathbb{Q}_{\infty,s}
), we represent a target function
f^{*}:\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}^{d}\to\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
by a quantized network
f
as

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{x};\mathbb{Q}_{p,s})=\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}^{d}}f^{*}(\mathbf{z})\times\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathbf{z}\}}\left({\mathbf{x}}\right)=b+\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}^{d}}(f^{*}(\mathbf{z})-b)\times\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathbf{z}\}}\left({\mathbf{x}}\right).

Hence, if we count the number of layers and parameters in our indicator function construction, then we can compute the number of layers and parameters in our universal representator.
Before counting the number of parameters in our universal representator, we first introduce the following lemma. The proof of
Lemma

19
is in
Section

5.14
.

Lemma 19
.

Let
p,s\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying
s\leq 2^{p}-1
and
p\geq 3
. Suppose
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,p,s,b}=\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
for some
b\in\mathbb{Q}_{\infty,s}
.
Then, there exists
b^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\infty,s}
such that

and

\left\{\left\lceil{\gamma+b^{\prime}}\right\rfloor:\gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,p,s}^{\circ},\;|\gamma|\leq 2q_{\max}+\frac{3}{2s}\right\}=\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}.

Lemma

19
implies that if
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,p,s,b}=\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
for some

, then there exists
b^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\infty,s}
such that
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,p,s,b^{\prime}}=\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
.
Furthermore, it also shows that for any
x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
, there exists
\gamma\in\mathcal{S}^{\circ}_{\sigma,p,s}
such that
|\gamma|\leq 2q_{\max}+\frac{3}{2s}
and

.
Under these observations, we now count the number of parameters in our indicator function construction in the following lemma. We provide the proof of
Lemma

20
in
Section

5.15
.

Lemma 20
.

Let
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
,
p,s\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying

, and
b\in\mathbb{Q}_{\infty,s}
. Then, The number of parameters in
\rho\circ\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor\circ f=\gamma\times\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathbf{z}\}}\left({\mathbf{x}}\right)
in

is upper bounded by
O\left(2^{2p}s^{2}d\right)
for all

\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,p,s,b}
for some

,

4.3
Approximating continuous functions using quantized networks

In this section, we discuss approximating continuous functions using quantized networks and quantitatively analyze the number of parameters in our approximator.
Specifically, given a target continuous function
f^{*}:[-q_{\max},q_{\max}]^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
and an error bound
\varepsilon\geq 0
, we want to approximate
f^{*}
using a quantized network
f:\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}^{d}\to\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
as follows: for each
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
,

\displaystyle|f(\mathbf{x};\mathbb{Q}_{p,s})-f^{*}(\mathbf{x})|\leq\left|f^{*}(\mathbf{x})-\left\lceil{f^{*}(\mathbf{x})}\right\rfloor\right|+\varepsilon.

Here, the term
\left|f^{*}(\mathbf{x})-\left\lceil{f^{*}(\mathbf{x})}\right\rfloor\right|
is an intrinsic error that arises from the discreteness of
\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
, i.e.,
|f(\mathbf{x};\mathbb{Q}_{p,s})-f^{*}(\mathbf{x})|
can never be smaller than
|f^{*}(\mathbf{x})-\left\lceil{f^{*}(\mathbf{x})}\right\rfloor|
.

If quantized networks can universally represent, then they can also represent
\left\lceil{f^{*}}\right\rfloor
(i.e.,
\varepsilon=0
is achievable). In this case, as we discussed in the previous section,
O(2^{2p+(p+1)d}s^{2}d)
parameters are sufficient.
Hence, we consider the following question: if
\varepsilon>0
, can we approximate
f^{*}
using a smaller number of parameters?
To answer this question, we define the
modulus of continuity

\omega_{f^{*}}:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
of a continuous function
{f^{*}}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
as

\displaystyle\omega_{f^{*}}(\delta)\triangleq\sup_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}:\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq\delta}|{f^{*}}(\mathbf{x})-{f^{*}}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})|,

(18)

and we define its inverse
\omega_{f^{*}}^{-1}:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\}
as

\displaystyle\omega_{f^{*}}^{-1}(\varepsilon)\triangleq\sup\{\delta\geq 0:\omega_{f^{*}}(\delta)\leq\varepsilon\}.

(19)

Using
\omega_{f^{*}}
, we provide the number of parameters that is sufficient to approximate the target continuous function
f^{*}
within
\varepsilon
error in the following theorem.
The proof of
Theorem

21
is presented in
Section

5.16
.

Theorem 21
.

Let
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
,
p,s,d\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying
s\leq 2^{p}-1
,
b\in\mathbb{Q}_{\infty,s}
, and
\mathcal{X}=[-q_{\max},q_{\max}]
.
Suppose that
\sigma
and
\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
satisfy
Condition

1
, and
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,p,s,b}=\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
.
Then, for any continuous
f^{*}:\mathcal{X}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
with modulus of continuity
\omega_{f^{*}}
and for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a four-layer
\sigma
quantized network
f(\,\cdot\,;\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}):\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}^{d}\to\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
of at most P parameters such that

\displaystyle\left|f(\mathbf{x};\mathbb{Q}_{p,s})-f^{*}(\mathbf{x})\right|\leq\left|f^{*}(\mathbf{x})-\left\lceil{f^{*}(\mathbf{x})}\right\rfloor\right|+\varepsilon

(20)

for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}^{d}
where

\displaystyle P=\begin{cases}1\quad&\text{if}\quad\omega_{f^{*}}^{-1}(\varepsilon)\geq 2q_{\max},\\
O\left(2^{2p}s^{2}d(6q_{\max})^{d}(\omega_{f^{*}}^{-1}(\varepsilon))^{-d}\right)\quad&\text{if}\quad\frac{1}{s}<\omega_{f^{*}}^{-1}(\varepsilon)<2q_{\max},\\
O\left(2^{d(p+1)+2p}s^{2}d\right)\quad&\text{if}\quad\omega_{f^{*}}^{-1}(\varepsilon)\leq\frac{1}{s}.\end{cases}

As we described in

\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}^{d}
.
Specifically, we choose quantized cubes so that their sidelengths are at most

; then
\Theta((2q_{\max})^{d}(\omega_{f^{*}}^{-1}(\varepsilon))^{-d})
quantized cubes are sufficient for partitioning
\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}^{d}
.
Furthermore, for each such quantized cube, our approximator incurs at most

error for all
\mathbf{x}
in that cube by the definition of the modulus of continuity (
Eqs.

18
and
19
).
Here, we use
O(2^{2p}s^{2}d)
parameters for each indicator function (see
Lemma

20
). Hence, our universal approximator uses
O(2^{2p}s^{3}d(6q_{\max})^{d}(\omega_{f^{*}}^{-1}(\varepsilon))^{-d})
parameters to achieve
Eq.

20
in
Theorem

.

4.4
Comparison with floating-point results

A few recent works study the representability of floating-point networks whose input, parameters, and output follow floating-point format. Park et al.
Park et al. (2024)
show that ReLU networks and Step
4
4

4

A binary step function
\mathbbm{1}_{\{x\in\mathbb{R}:x\geq 0\}}\left({x}\right)
.
networks can represent almost all floating-point functions (from/to floating-point vectors).
This result has been extended to general activation functions
Hwang et al. (2025b)
and interval arithmetic setup
Hwang et al. (2025a)
.
One major difference from our setup is that they apply rounding for each addition and multiplication in affine transformations.
Hence, these floating-point results can be applied to standard implementations of neural networks, since the floating-point arithmetic is typically a default choice in popular neural network libraries (e.g., using PyTorch or Tensorflow).
On the other hand, our problem setup follows a network quantization procedure
Jacob et al. (2018)
;
Wang et al. (2022)
;
Yao et al. (2021)
: weights and inputs/outputs of activation functions are quantized to fixed-point numbers, but high-precision bias and intermediate operations (see
Section

2.3
).

The universal representators in our results and floating-point results are both represented as a sum of indicator functions. However, the precise constructions of indicator functions are completely different due to the discrepancy between floating-point and fixed-point arithmetic we consider. Namely, those constructions are incompatible.
In the floating-point arithmetic in
Park et al. (2024)
;
Hwang et al. (2025b)
;
Hwang et al. (2025a)
, the rounding is performed after each addition/multiplication in affine transformations, i.e., a sequence of additions is non-associative.
Hwang et al. (2025b)
;
Hwang et al. (2025a)
exploited this property to construct indicator functions and
Park et al. (2024)
highly relies on the properties of ReLU and the binary step function.
Furthermore, they also consider the overflow (i.e., if the output of some operation before rounding is too large/small, the rounded value will be
\pm\infty
); due to the threat of the overflow, they could not cover the domain of all finite floats, unlike our results.
On the other hand, we do not consider overflow as in the conventional quantization setup, and our construction of indicator functions may generate large absolute values before rounding, so that it can generate
\pm\infty
if overflow exists.
Lastly, due to the different number formats in floating-point arithmetic and fixed-point arithmetic, if we directly transfer a network construction under one arithmetic to another, it may incur undesired round-off error and may not work as we want.

\Sigma\supset\left\{z_{1},z_{1}+1,\dots,z_{2}\right\}.

Since

\mathcal{V}_{\sigma,p,s}\supset\left\{\frac{z}{s}-\frac{z^{\prime}}{s}:z,z^{\prime}\in\Sigma\right\},

we have
\frac{1}{s}\in\mathcal{V}_{\sigma,p,s}
. This implies that
\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}=\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,p,s,b}
and completes the proof.

Case (A2).
The proof is almost identical to the proof for the case (A1).
The only difference is that the condition
|\sigma^{\prime}(x)|<1
is replaced by
|\sigma^{\prime}(x)|\leq 1
and
\sigma(x)\geq 0
.
Consequently, the inequality in
Eq.

30
becomes

\left|\sigma\left(\frac{k+1}{s}\right)-\sigma\left(\frac{k}{s}\right)\right|\leq\frac{1}{s}.

Generally, we cannot guaranty that

\left|\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor\left(\frac{k+1}{s}\right)-\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor\left(\frac{k}{s}\right)\right|\leq\frac{1}{s},

due to the away from zero tie-breaking rule.
However, as
\sigma(x)\geq 0
, we can assure that the inequality holds.
The remaining part of the proof is identical to that of the case (A1).
Thus, the proof is completed.

Case (A3).
Define the set
\Sigma
as

\Sigma\triangleq\left\{s\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor\left(\frac{k}{s}\right)\in\mathbb{Z}:k\in\mathbb{Z},q_{1}\leq k\leq q_{2}\right\}.

Because
\left|\sigma\left(\frac{q_{1}}{s}\right)\right|,\left|\sigma\left(\frac{q_{2}}{s}\right)\right|\leq\frac{2^{p}-1}{s}
and
\left|\sigma\left(\frac{q_{2}}{s}\right)-\sigma\left(\frac{q_{1}}{s}\right)\right|<\frac{2(q_{2}-q_{1})-1}{s}
, by
Lemma

23
, it follows that
\left|\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor\left(\frac{q_{2}}{s}\right)-\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor\left(\frac{q_{1}}{s}\right)\right|\leq\frac{2(q_{2}-q_{1})-1}{s}
.
As
1<\sigma^{\prime}(x)\leq 2
, for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
q_{1}\leq k<q_{2}
, the following inequality holds:

\sigma\left(\frac{k+1}{s}\right)-\sigma\left(\frac{k}{s}\right)\geq\frac{1}{s}.

Thus, by
Lemma

22
,

\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor\left(\frac{k+1}{s}\right)-\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor\left(\frac{k}{s}\right)\geq\frac{1}{s}.

This implies that there are exactly
q_{2}-q_{1}+1
elements in
\Sigma
between
s\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor\left(\frac{q_{1}}{s}\right)
and
s\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor\left(\frac{q_{2}}{s}\right)
whose difference is smaller than
\frac{2(q_{2}-q_{1})}{s}
.
Therefore, by the pigeonhole principle, there exists at least one
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor\left(\frac{k+1}{s}\right)-\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor\left(\frac{k}{s}\right)=\frac{1}{s}
.
This implies that
\frac{1}{s}\in\mathcal{V}_{\sigma,p,s}
, and hence,
\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}=\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,p,s,b}
.
This completes the proof.

Lemma 22
.

If
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy

\displaystyle|x_{2}-x_{1}|\geq\frac{1}{s},\quad|x_{1}|,|x_{2}|\leq\frac{2^{p}-1}{s},

then we have
\left|\left\lceil{x_{2}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}-\left\lceil{x_{1}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}\right|\geq\frac{1}{s}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume
x_{1}<x_{2}
. Suppose
\left\lceil{x_{1}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}=\left\lceil{x_{2}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}=\frac{k}{s}
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. We have the following cases. Note that the rounding rule is
ties away from zero
.

Case
x_{1}<x_{2}<0
.

We have
\frac{k}{s}-\frac{1}{2s}<x_{1},x_{2}\leq\frac{k}{s}+\frac{1}{2s}
leading to
x_{2}-x_{1}<\frac{1}{s}
.

Case
x_{1}<0<x_{2}
.

We have
\frac{k}{s}-\frac{1}{2s}<x_{1},x_{2}<\frac{k}{s}+\frac{1}{2s}
leading to
x_{2}-x_{1}<\frac{1}{s}
.

Case
0<x_{1}<x_{2}
.
We have
\frac{k}{s}-\frac{1}{2s}\leq x_{1},x_{2}<\frac{k}{s}+\frac{1}{2s}
leading to
x_{2}-x_{1}<\frac{1}{s}
.

Namely, if
\left\lceil{x_{1}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}=\left\lceil{x_{2}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}
, we can conclude that
x_{2}-x_{1}<\frac{1}{s}
, which contradicts the assumption
|x_{2}-x_{1}|\geq\frac{1}{s}
.
Hence, it holds that
\left\lceil{x_{2}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}-\left\lceil{x_{1}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}\geq\frac{1}{s}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 23
.

If
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy

\displaystyle|x_{2}-x_{1}|<\frac{k}{s},\quad|x_{1}|,|x_{2}|\leq\frac{2^{p}-1}{s}

for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
then
\left|\left\lceil{x_{2}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}-\left\lceil{x_{1}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}\right|\leq\frac{k}{s}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume
x_{1}\leq x_{2}
. We prove the lemma using the mathematical induction on
k
.

Base step (
k=1
).
We consider the following cases.

Case
-\frac{n}{s}-\frac{1}{2s}<x_{1}\leq-\frac{n}{s}+\frac{1}{2s}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Since
x_{1}\leq x_{2}<x_{1}+\frac{1}{s}
, we have
-\frac{n}{s}-\frac{1}{2s}<x_{2}<-\frac{n-1}{s}+\frac{1}{2s}.
Hence, it holds that
\left\lceil{x_{1}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}=-\frac{n}{s},\left\lceil{x_{2}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}=-\frac{n}{s}
or
-\frac{n+1}{s}
, which leads to

\left\lceil{x_{2}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}-\left\lceil{x}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}\leq\frac{1}{s}
.

Case
-\frac{1}{2s}<x_{1}<\frac{1}{2s}
.

We have
-\frac{1}{2s}<x_{2}<\frac{3}{2s}
. Hence, it holds that
\left\lceil{x_{1}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}=0,\left\lceil{x_{2}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}=0
or
\frac{1}{s}
, which leads to

\left\lceil{x_{2}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}-\left\lceil{x}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}\leq\frac{1}{s}
.

Case
\frac{n}{s}-\frac{1}{2s}\leq x_{1}<\frac{n}{s}+\frac{1}{2s}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Since
x_{1}\leq x_{2}<x_{1}+\frac{1}{s}
, we have
\frac{n}{s}-\frac{1}{2s}\leq x_{2}<\frac{n+1}{s}+\frac{1}{2s}
. Hence, it holds that
\left\lceil{x_{1}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}=\frac{n}{s},\left\lceil{x_{2}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}=\frac{n}{s}
or
\frac{n+1}{s}
, which leads to

\left\lceil{x_{2}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}-\left\lceil{x}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}\leq\frac{1}{s}
.
Now we prove when
k=1
.

Inductive step.

Suppose that the lemma holds for
k=1,2,\dots,m
and
\frac{m}{s}\leq x_{2}-x_{1}<\frac{m+1}{s}
. We consider the following cases.

Case
x_{1}\neq-\frac{1}{2s}
.

Let
x^{\prime}=x_{1}+\frac{1}{s}
. Then we have

\displaystyle x_{2}-x^{\prime}<\frac{m}{s},\quad x^{\prime}-x_{1}=\frac{1}{s}.

By the induction hypothesis, we have

\displaystyle\left\lceil{x_{2}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}-\left\lceil{x^{\prime}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}\leq\frac{m}{s}.

Since
x_{1}\neq-\frac{1}{2s}
and rounding is “tie away from zero”,
x_{1}
and
x^{\prime}
are rounded to the same direction leading to
\left\lceil{x^{\prime}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}-\left\lceil{x_{1}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}=\frac{1}{s}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\lceil{x_{2}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}-\left\lceil{x_{1}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}

\displaystyle=(\left\lceil{x_{2}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}-\left\lceil{x^{\prime}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}})+(\left\lceil{x^{\prime}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}-\left\lceil{x_{1}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{m}{s}+\frac{1}{s}=\frac{m+1}{s}.

Case
x_{1}=-\frac{1}{2s}
.

Let
x^{\prime}=x_{2}-\frac{1}{s}
. Since
x_{2}\geq\frac{3}{2s}
, and
x^{\prime}\geq\frac{1}{2s}
,
x_{2}
and
x^{\prime}
are rounded to the same direction leading to
\left\lceil{x_{2}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}-\left\lceil{x^{\prime}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}=\frac{1}{s}
.
By the induction hypothesis, we have

\displaystyle\left\lceil{x^{\prime}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}-\left\lceil{x_{1}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}\leq\frac{m}{s}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\lceil{x_{2}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}-\left\lceil{x_{1}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}

\displaystyle=(\left\lceil{x_{2}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}-\left\lceil{x^{\prime}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}})+(\left\lceil{x^{\prime}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}}-\left\lceil{x_{1}}\right\rfloor_{\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{s}+\frac{m}{s}=\frac{m+1}{s},

which completes the proof.
∎

Proof of
Lemma

10

Recall that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\sigma,p,s,b}

\displaystyle\triangleq\left\{\left\lceil{b+\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}x_{i}}\right\rfloor:n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},w_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p,s},x_{i}\in\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor(\mathbb{Q}_{p,s})~\forall i\in[n]\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\sigma,p,s}

\displaystyle\triangleq\left\{\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor(x)-\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor(y):x,y\in\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,p,s,b}

\displaystyle\triangleq\left\{\left\lceil{b+\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}x_{i}}\right\rfloor:n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},w_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p,s},x_{i}\in\mathcal{V}_{\sigma,p,s}~\forall i\in[n]\right\}.

If there exists
x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
such that
\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor(x)\in\mathcal{V}_{\sigma,p,s}
, then there exist

y,z\in\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
such that

\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor(x)=\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor(y)-\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor(z).

Then, for any
w\in\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
,

\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor(w)=\left(\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor(w)-\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor(x)\right)+\left(\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor(y)-\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor(z)\right).

Therefore,
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,p,s,b}\supset\mathcal{N}_{\sigma,p,s,b}
.

\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,p,s,b}\subset\mathcal{N}_{\sigma,p,s,b}
by their definitions, we have
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,p,s,b}=\mathcal{N}_{\sigma,p,s,b}
.
We can prove
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,p,s,b}=\mathcal{N}_{\sigma,p,s,b}
when
\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor(x)=0
for some
x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}

5.10
Proof of
Lemma

12

Proof.

This proof is similar to the proof of
Lemma

2
.
By the assumption of the lemma, there exists a function
\widetilde{\sigma}:\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
such that

\left\lceil{\sigma(x)}\right\rfloor=r\widetilde{\sigma}(x).

Recall that
\mathcal{B}\mathcal{N}_{\sigma,p,s,b}
is defined as

\mathcal{B}\mathcal{N}_{\sigma,p,s,b}=\left\{\left\lceil{b+\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}x_{i}}\right\rfloor:n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},w_{i}\in\{-1,1\},x_{i}\in\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor(\mathbb{Q}_{p,s})~\forall i\in[n]\right\}.

For
i\in[n]
and
x_{i}\in\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor(\mathbb{Q}_{p,s})
, consider
y_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
such that
x_{i}=\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor(y_{i})
.
Then,

x_{i}=\left\lceil{\sigma}\right\rfloor(y_{i})=r\widetilde{\sigma}(y_{i}),

and

\left\lceil{b+\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}x_{i}}\right\rfloor=\left\lceil{b+\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}r\widetilde{\sigma}(y_{i})}\right\rfloor=\left\lceil{\frac{sb+\sum_{i=1}^{n}rw_{i}(s\widetilde{\sigma}(y_{i}))}{s}}\right\rfloor.

Here, we have
sb+\sum_{i=1}^{n}rw_{i}(s\widetilde{\sigma}(y_{i}))\in\mathbb{Z}
, and

sb+\sum_{i=1}^{n}rw_{i}(s\widetilde{\sigma}(y_{i}))\equiv sb\quad(\text{mod }r).

Therefore,

s\left\lceil{b+\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}x_{i}}\right\rfloor\equiv\begin{cases}2^{p}-1{\ (\mathrm{mod}\ r)}&\text{ if }b+\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}x_{i}\geq 2^{p}/s,\\
-2^{p}+1{\ (\mathrm{mod}\ r)}&\text{ if }b+\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}x_{i}\leq-2^{p}/s,\\
sb{\ (\mathrm{mod}\ r)}&\text{ otherwise }\end{cases}

Thus, as in the proof of
Lemma

2
we can conclude that
\mathcal{B}\mathcal{N}_{\sigma,p,s,b}\neq\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
. This completes the proof.
∎

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we study the expressive power of quantized networks under fixed-point arithmetic. We provide a necessary condition and a sufficient condition on activation functions and
\mathbb{Q}_{p,s}
for the universal representation of quantized networks.
We compare our results with classical universal approximation theorems and show that popular activation functions and fixed-point arithmetic are capable of universal representation.
We further extend our results to quantized networks with binary weights. We believe that our findings offer insights that can enhance the understanding of quantized network theory.

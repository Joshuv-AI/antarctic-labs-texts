Wide-field Polarization Imaging and Numerical Modeling of the Coma and Tail of Comet C/2023 A3 (Tsuchinshan-ATLAS)
M. Arnaut, C. Wöhler, P. Halder, G. Ahuja, S. Ganesh, M. Bhatt

Abstract
Imaging polarimetry is a well-known method for analysing the structure and composition of cometary dust. Accordingly, comets are classified based on the phase angle dependent degree of linear polarization of their comae. The goal of this study is to examine the size and structure of the dust grains in the coma and in particular in the tail of the bright comet C/2023 A3 (Tsuchinshan-ATLAS) and to infer possible variations. For this purpose, we rely on the method of telescopic wide-field polarimetric imaging of the comet in order to obtain the dependence of the degree of linear polarization (DoLP) of the coma and tail on the phase angle across a broad range, using an off-the-shelf industrial grade polarization camera combined with a small telescope of short aperture ratio. These observations are accompanied by T-matrix modeling using the MSTM4 software framework for simulation of light scattering by fractal agglomerates of spherical monomer particles. Our observations indicate that the coma exhibits a high maximum DoLP of 0.34 0.34 , which is further exceeded by a factor of about two by the DoLP of the comet’s tail. Differences in the phase angle of maximum DoLP between coma and tail indicate a higher carbon vs. silicate fraction in the coma than in the tail. Our simulations are able to reproduce the observations when assuming that the dust agglomerates are formed of larger monomers in the coma than in the tail. A possible explanation of these results is that the coma monomers are coated by a thin layer of frozen volatiles, which sublimate due to solar heating when the dust moves from the coma towards the tail.



1
Introduction

Ground-based telescopic polarimetry of comets is a widely used tool to assess the properties of their dust and gas hulls. Commonly, such studies involve measuring the degree and angle of linear polarisation (DoLP and AoLP) of the coma.
Kiselev et al., 2015
provided a profound introduction by covering the measurement techniques of aperture polarimetry, imaging polarimetry, and spectropolarimetry, and by discussing the different polarimetric classes of comets essentially defined by the maximum DoLP value
P_{\rm max}
they exhibit, and the dependence of the DoLP on wavelength. They suggested physical explanations for the now commonly accepted view that cometary dust particles are aggregates of hundreds or thousands of monomers with individual sizes smaller than a micrometer. For the bright comet C/1995 O1 (Hale-Bopp), they demonstrated a phase angle of inversion (where the DoLP becomes zero) of about
20^{\circ}
and an increase of the polarization of the coma with increasing distance from the comet’s nucleus.

Early polarimetric studies of cometary comae rely on measurements obtained with aperture polarimeters. Observations of the comet C/1989 X1 (Austin) revealed a strong increase of the DoLP with increasing wavelength, which were explained such that the size distribution of the cometary dust particles is narrow and lies in the range of
100
–
500
nm
(
Joshi et al., 1992
)
. The polarimetric analyses of the comet C/2000 WM1 (LINEAR) conducted by
Joshi et al., 2003
suggested that it is a “dusty” comet whose polarimetric properties in the positive and negative polarization branch are mainly governed by aggregate silicate grains consisting of monomers with a size parameter of
x=1.5
, where
x=2\pi r/\lambda
with
r
as the particle radius and
\lambda
as the wavelength of light (for red light with
\lambda=600
nm, this corresponds to
r=140
nm). For the comet C/2001 Q4 (NEAT), numerical simulations based on T-matrix code conducted by
Paul et al., 2010
suggested a size parameter of
x\approx 1.6
for the monomers forming the dust agglomerates and a mixed composition of silicates and organic compounds. The simulations furthermore predicted a phase angle of inversion of about
25^{\circ}
.
Zheltobryukhov et al., 2020
conducted imaging polarimetric observations of comet 46P/Wirtanen, assigning the comet to the class characterized by low
P_{\rm max}
values and revealing an increasing DoLP of the coma with increasing distance from the nucleus.
Halder and Sengupta, 2023
modeled the polarization properties of the coma of the interstellar comet 2I/Borisov with a 80:20 mixture of fractal aggregates vs. solid particles and a 50:50 silicate-to-carbon mixture.

An extensive analysis of cometary comae by
Zubko et al., 2016
based on polarization modeling with the Discrete Dipole Approximation (DDA) method and including a large sample of comets concluded that the polarization behavior of the dust can be decribed in terms of different proportions of weakly absorbing (silicate) and strongly absorbing (carbon-rich) particles. High-
P_{\rm max}
comets were found to be dominated by strongly absorbing materials and low
P_{\rm max}
ones by weakly absorbing materials, respectively, suggesting that the polarization is primarily influenced by the dust composition. In contrast, the synoptic analysis by
Kwon et al., 2021
of a large set of comets with respect to their polarization behavior in the R (red) and K (near-infrared) bands and their thermal infrared silicate emission suggested that the polarization properties in the visible domain are mainly governed by the properties of the monomers constituting the dust aggregates, whereas the near-infrared polarization mostly depends on their porosity, with the dust composition being of minor relevance.
Bagnulo et al., 2024
stated that short-period comets that remain close to the Sun typically show low
P_{\rm max}
values, suggesting that their comae mainly consist of dust of high compactness.

Only few polarimetric studies were focused on cometary tails. The early visual study by
Wright, 1881
found that the apparent length of the tail of comet B.1881 showed a strong dependence on the polarizer orientation due to a high DoLP of the tail.
Matyagin et al., 1968
measured the DoLP and AoLP of the bright comet C/1965 S1 (Ikeya-Seki) by performing densitometry on photographs, obtaining DoLP values of up to 0.5–0.8 in the tail.
Weinberg and Beeson, 1976a
performed photometric scans of the tail of C/1965 S1 and the sky around it along profiles parallel to the horizon using eight different narrow-band filters in order to measure the DoLP and the AoLP, where phase angle dependent DoLP values between
-0.42
and
+0.22
were found. However, their observations were conducted at low elevations above the horizon between only
2^{\circ}
and
20^{\circ}
, implying potential difficulties in correcting for elevation-dependent absorption by the Earth’s atmosphere. Attempts were made to model the polarization behavior of the dust in the tail using Mie scattering, assuming a power-law size distribution of spherical particles. With the same observational setting,
Weinberg and Beeson, 1976b
examined the reversal of polarization in the tail of comet C/1965 S1 by determining the location of the point where the DoLP is zero and the AoLP exhibits a discontinuous change by
90^{\circ}
. In the more recent study by
Nežič et al., 2022
, data of the COR2 coronographs on-board the spacecraft STEREO-A and STEREO-B were used to analyze the DoLP along the tail of the Kreutz comet C/2010 E6 (STEREO). At phase angles around
110^{\circ}
they found DoLP values of up to about
0.3
in the tail, where the DoLP increased monotonically with increasing distance from the nucleus.

In this paper we examine the bright comet C/2023 A3 (Tsuchinshan-ATLAS), which is “dynamically new”
(
Cambianica et al., 2025
)
as it visited the inner solar system for the first time during its visibility period in 2024. Inferring the structural properties of the dust particles in the coma and tail of this comet from the polarization behavior of the scattered light will thus provide insights into the primordial matter that formed the planetary bodies of the solar system. Hence, we describe wide-field imaging polarimetric observations of the coma and in particular the tail of C/2023 A3 across a broad range of phase angles. Using the T-matrix method, we attempt to explain our observational data by numerical simulations of the polarization behavior of the dust agglomerates in the coma and tail in order to assess their respective compositional and structural properties.

2
Data acquisition and analysis methods

2.1
Data acquisition

For acquisition of the polarization imaging data of comet C/2023 A3 examined in this study, a monochrome industrial camera DZK 33UX250 without cooling was used, manufactured by the company The Imaging Source. To the individual pixels of the camera sensor, polarization filters rotated by an angle
\theta
of
0^{\circ}
,
45^{\circ}
,
90^{\circ}
and
135^{\circ}
, respectively, relative to the horizontal image direction are attached
(
TheImagingSource, 2019
)
. Thus, from each
2\times 2
subset of pixels the unpolarized intensity, the DoLP and the AoLP relative to the direction of the pixel rows can be derived. For all observations but one, the camera was mounted on a Newton reflector telescope of
150
mm diameter with a focal length of
600
mm. The resulting intensity, DoLP and AoLP images have a scale of
2.4
arcseconds/pixel with a total field of view of
48\times 40
arcminutes. The location of the telescope is in Wetter (Ruhr) near Dortmund, Germany. The observation of Nov 14 was conducted with a small
30/135
mm refractor with
10.5
arcsec/pixel at the ARIES Observatory on Manora Peak near Nainital, India. Additional observations of the coma DoLP were conducted on Dec 05, 2024, and on Mar 06, 2025, with the 1.2 m reflector at Mount Abu Observatory, Rajasthan, India, which was equipped with the EMPOL imaging polarimeter
(
Ganesh et al., 2020
)
upgraded with a new, electrically cooled CCD camera. The acquisition details of our polarimetric images are given in Tables
1
and
2
.

During each observation run with the polarization camera, we acquired several tens to hundreds of individual video frames taken with the maximum possible exposure time of
4
s. From each video frame the sub-frames corresponding to the four polarization directions were extracted. The sub-frames of each polarization direction were coregistered and stacked with the software Autostakkert3
(
Kraaikamp, 2023
)
, resulting in one image of
16
bits pixel depth per polarization direction. Subsequently, the
45^{\circ}
,
90^{\circ}
and
135^{\circ}
images were then coregistered at subpixel accuracy with respect to the
0^{\circ}
image, where the image transformations (translations) were computed by minimizing the sum of squared differences between the high-frequency components of each image pair
(
Bhatt et al., 2023
)
. To the four intensity values
I(\theta)
at each pixel position of the resulting quadruple of coregistered images, a sinusoidal function of the form

I(\theta)=F+G\cos\left[2\left(\theta-W\right)\right]

(1)

was fitted, yielding images of the intensity
F
, the DoLP
P=|G|/F
, the AoLP
W
, and the residual root-mean-squared error (RMSE)
E
of the fit. As an example, the results of the October 23 observation are shown in Fig.
1
. For this observation and all other ones for which the coma was saturated in the image data due to its excessive brightness, a hyperbolic tangent function was fitted to the
\lg(P)
vs.
\lg(F)
measurements in order to obtain a robust extrapolation of the DoLP to very low and very high intensities, respectively (Fig.
1
b). The hyperbolic tangent function was chosen for its smooth asymptotic behavior which prevents unrealistic DoLP values at very low or high intensities.

To avoid using data near saturation, the DoLP was only computed for pixels with 16-bit intensities below
35000
, corresponding to
F_{\rm max}\approx 0.53
when the pixel intensity is normalized to the interval
[0,1]
. Low-SNR data were excluded by neglecting pixels with
F/E<10
(see Sect.
2.2
), corresponding to
F_{\rm min}\approx 0.01
with pixel intensity normalized to
[0,1]
. Later observations at the end of November and during December 2024 showed a strong decrease in apparent size of the comet, whose areal brightness also decreased due to its increasing solar distance. Due to the resulting small number of pixels covered by the comet, it was not always possible to fit the hyperbolic tangent function to the
\lg(P)
vs.
\lg(F)
measurements. In these cases, the DoLP of the tail was measured by averaging over all pixels with
F\in\left[F_{\rm min},1.58\times F_{\rm min}\right]
, an interval covering
0.2
orders of magnitude in decadal logarithmic scale. Similarly, the average DoLP over all pixels with
F\in\left[F_{\rm max}/1.58,F_{\rm max}\right]
was considered as the DoLP of the coma. In order to still be able to measure the DoLP of the tail, binning of the images by a factor of
2
or
4
was performed (Table
1
) to increase the SNR.

Fig.
1
a demonstrates that the DoLP is larger in the tail and the outer parts of the coma than in the inner coma close to the nucleus. Our observations with the Mount Abu 1.2 m reflector equipped with the EMPOL imaging polarimeter, conducted on Dec 05 in the SLOAN i and z bands centered at
763
and
913
nm, respectively, confirm this behavior (Fig.
2
). The radial distance from the nucleus, though, is restricted to about 11 arcseconds due to the comparatively narrow field of view.

2.2
Error analysis

This section explains how we estimate the statistical error of the inferred DoLP
P
in dependence on the signal-to-noise ratio
\mathrm{SNR}_{F}
of the intensity
F
based on a Monte-Carlo model of the image acquisition process. For that purpose, we construct a set of four artificial frames for
\theta
values of
0^{\circ}
,
45^{\circ}
,
90^{\circ}
and
135^{\circ}
. Each artificial frame has
10,000
pixels with a
\theta
-dependent uniform grayvalue
I(\theta)
according to Eq.
1
with given values for
F
,
G
and
W
. Gaussian noise with a standard deviation of
2\sigma
is added to the grayvalues of each frame. As the intensity
F
fitted according to Eq.
1
equals the average of the four corresponding grayvalues, and the noise of the average of
N
noisy measurements decreases proportionally to
N^{-1/2}
with
N=4
, one obtains
\mathrm{SNR}_{F}=\sigma/F
. However, it is not straightforward to measure the noise level
\sigma
of our
F
images of the comet directly, and the effect of photon statistics even leads to a dependence of
\sigma
on
F
. Our simulation reveals, though, that the ratio
F/E
is a proxy for the signal-to-noise ratio according to
F/E\approx 1.25\times\mathrm{SNR}_{F}
.

Even in the absence of linear polarisation, a fit of Eq.
1
to noisy grayvalues of the
\theta
-dependent frames leads to a non-zero best-fit value of
G
and thus to a positive estimated DoLP
P
, whose value increases with decreasing
\mathrm{SNR}_{F}
. Hence, only DoLP values exceeding an
\mathrm{SNR}_{F}
-dependent threshold can be measured reliably. The simulated relation between the true and the measured DoLP is shown in Fig.
3
for a range of typical
\mathrm{SNR}_{F}
values between
10
and
1000
. The diagram shows that the DoLP must exceed a
\mathrm{SNR}_{F}
-dependent “detection threshold”
\Theta
to be measurable. If the true DoLP becomes smaller than
\Theta
, the measured DoLP is merely an upper limit to the true DoLP. For
\mathrm{SNR}_{F}
values of
10
and
100
, typical signal-to-noise ratios of the comet’s tail and central coma, respectively, values of
\Theta
around
0.2
and
0.02
are found. Besides this systematic effect, the statistical measurement error (one standard deviation) was found to correspond to approximately
0.1
and
0.01
, respectively, for DoLP values larger than
\Theta
.

2.3
Polarization modeling

Fractal agglomerates consisting of spherical monomers with a given log-normal distribution of their radii were constructed using the FracVAL algorithm, where the monomers form a chain structure whose shape is defined by a fractal dimension
D_{f}
between 1 (linear chain) and 3 (densely packed cluster)
(
Morán et al., 2019
)
. We used the Python implementation of FracVAL by
Schauten and Arnaut, 2025
. Setting the number
N
of monomers per agglomerate to
8
,
20
,
48
,
80
,
128
,
256
,
384
and
512
with
D_{f}
values of
1.4
,
1.6
,
1.8
,
2.0
and
2.2
, respectively, resulted in a set of
40
different agglomerates shapes (Figs.
8
and
9
). The constructed agglomerate shapes were scaled uniformly such that the mean monomer radius
r_{\rm mean}
obtained values of 112.5 nm, 125 nm and 137.5 nm. Uniform values of
1.05
and
1.25
were used for the logarithmic standard deviation of the monomer radii and for the fractal prefactor, respectively. The radius of gyration
R_{g}
of the agglomerates decreases with increasing fractal dimension
D_{f}
and spans the intervals of
3.8
–
74\times r_{\rm mean}
for
D_{f}=1.4
and
2.3
–
15\times r_{\rm mean}
for
D_{f}=2.2
, respectively.

We simulated the polarization behavior of the agglomerates using the MSTM4 software framework
(
Mackowski and Kolokolova, 2022
)
, where we assumed that the agglomerates consist of pure olivine or pure amorphous carbon
(
Zubko et al., 2016
)
and used the complex refraction indices
m_{\rm olivine}=1.85+0.00087i
and
m_{\rm carbon}=2.37+0.61i

(
Fabian et al., 2001
)
. We assumed a grain size distribution obeying a power law, i.e., the number of agglomerates is proportional to
R_{g}^{-n}
with
n
as the power law exponent
(
Zubko et al., 2016
;
Halder and Ganesh, 2021
)
. Thus, with increasing value of
n
the relative influence of large dust grains on the observed polarization behavior becomes weaker. As the coma and tail of a comet can be treated as optically thin media
(
Halder and Ganesh, 2021
)
, the DoLP phase curves of a specific mixture of olivine and carbon is given by a correspondingly weighted average of the DoLP phase curves obtained for the pure compositions. As our observations were conducted without filter, our simulations were performed for a wavelength of 600 nm that corresponds to the maximum sensitivity of the camera sensor
(
TheImagingSource, 2019
)
.

Results

3.1
Observation results

The measured DoLP
P
vs. phase angle
\alpha
of the coma and the tail are shown in Fig.
4
a as crosses and circles, respectively. The tail measurement plotted in cyan color has a high uncertainty, and the measurements plotted in magenta color are upper limits to the respective DoLP values as a consequence of the systematic
\mathrm{SNR}_{F}
-dependent effect explained in Section
2.2
. To the coma measurements, we fitted the empirical function

P(\alpha)=A\sin^{B}(\alpha)\cos^{C}(\alpha/2)\sin(\alpha-\alpha_{\rm inv})

(2)

as stated by
Bagnulo et al., 2024
, performing a least-squares estimate of the parameters
A
,
B
,
C
and
\alpha_{\rm inv}
with all four parameters being constrained to non-negative values. The phase angle of inversion
\alpha_{\rm inv}
is the phase angle at which
P=0
and the plane of polarization rotates by an angle of
90^{\circ}
, effectively resulting in a change of the sign of
P
from positive to negative. As we could acquire reliable tail measurements of
P
only down to a phase angle of
28.8^{\circ}
, we used the same value of
\alpha_{\rm inv}
for the tail measurements and only estimated the parameters
A
,
B
and
C
. Furthermore, we derived from the fitted polarization phase curves the maximum DoLP
P_{\rm max}
, the phase angle
\alpha_{\rm max}
with
P(\alpha_{\rm max})=P_{\rm max}
, and the slope
h=dP/d\alpha
at
\alpha=\alpha_{\rm inv}
. These parameters are commonly used to describe the behavior of the positive polarization branch of comets, asteroids or the Moon
(
Bagnulo et al., 2024
, e.g.,)
. The parameter values obtained for the coma and the tail, respectively, are listed in Table
3
. Notably, the estimated value of
\alpha_{\rm max}
of the coma may be inaccurate by about
10^{\circ}
–
20^{\circ}
because no DoLP measurements are available for phase angles exceeding
85.5^{\circ}
that would have been able to better constrain the empirical function (Eq.
2
) around the maximum
(such measurements have been provided for the coma by
Lim et al., 2025
, though)
. Furthermore, the limited coverage of our observations for small phase angles does not allow for a reliable estimation of the minimum DoLP
P_{\rm min}
and the phase angle
\alpha_{\rm min}
at which it occurs.

3.2
Modeling results

The absence of an observable C
2
emission of the coma and tail (see Sect.
4
for details) implies a low-carbon composition of the dust grains in the coma and the tail. However, the DoLP phase curves in Fig.
4
a show that the
\alpha_{\rm max}
value of the coma is larger than that of the tail, which remains true in the light of the
\alpha_{\rm max}
value of
90^{\circ}
–
95^{\circ}
estimated for the coma by
Lim et al., 2025
. This difference in
\alpha_{\rm max}
can only be explained by the coma containing more carbon than the tail, as shown by our simulation results in Figs.
10
and
11
, and (based on DDA rather than the T-matrix method) also by
Zubko et al., 2016
. Hence, for the dust composition we assumed a 30:70 mixture of olivine and carbon for the coma, corresponding to a low carbon fraction according to
Zubko et al., 2016
, and pure olivine for the tail.

The effect of the mean monomer radius
r_{\rm mean}
, the fractal dimension
D_{f}
and the power law exponent
n
are shown in Figs.
10
and
11
. For olivine, an increase of
D_{f}
from
1.4
to
2.2
leads to a reduction of
P_{\rm max}
by at most
20\%
, whereas
P_{\rm max}
shows no clear dependence on
D_{f}
for carbon. Increasing the power law exponent
n
from
2
to
3
, i.e., increasing the fraction of small dust grains, leads to an increase of
P_{\rm max}
of only a few percent. Consequently, the model parameter that has the strongest influence on
P_{\rm max}
is the mean monomer radius
r_{\rm mean}
. This is in accordance with the finding of
Kwon et al., 2021
that the polarization of cometary dust in the visible (red) domain is mainly governed by the monomer properties.

For the assumed 30:70 olivine-carbon mixture, our DoLP observations of the coma can be explained by setting
r_{\rm mean}=137.5
nm, a uniform distribution of
D_{f}
between
1.4
and
2.2
, and a grain size distribution with
n=2
. The resulting simulated coma DoLP (solid red curve in Fig.
4
b) resonably reproduces the observed values of
P_{\rm max}
and
\alpha_{\rm max}

(
Lim et al., 2025
, see also)
. Notably, we found that different model parameter settings might lead to similar DoLP phase curves. For example, assuming only large agglomerates with
N=256
and
N=512
and a composition characterised by a 50:50 mixture of olivine and carbon would not change the DoLP phase curve perceivably. However, such a size distribution has been found to be unrealistic by
Zubko et al., 2020
. For the tail with its assumed pure olivine composition, the DoLP observations can be reproduced by setting
r_{\rm mean}=125
nm, a uniform distribution of
D_{f}
between
1.4
and
2.2
, and
n=2
(solid black curve in Fig.
4
b). Notably, the only model parameters that need to be adjusted to explain the differences in
P_{\rm max}
and
\alpha_{\rm max}
between coma and tail are the mean monomer radius
r_{\rm mean}
and the composition (olivine-to-carbon ratio), respectively.

4
Discussion

The presented polarization observations of the coma and tail of comet C/2023 A3 (Tsuchinshan-ATLAS) illustrate that wide-field polarimetric image data can be obtained in the positive polarization branch for moderately bright comets with a small instrument of short aperture ratio in combination with an industrial-grade polarization camera. In this way, we obtained imaging polarimetric observations of the tail of C/2023 A3 across a broad range of phase angles. In contrast, the negative polarization branch is less easily accessible due to the significantly lower DoLP, requiring a higher SNR of the polarimetric imaging sensor that might be achievable by an active cooling unit.

With a maximum DoLP of
P_{\rm max}=0.34
obtained at
\alpha_{\rm max}\approx 100^{\circ}
, and
\alpha_{\rm inv}=20.5^{\circ}
, our coma measurements are consistent with those of
Lim et al., 2025
, who obtained
P_{\rm max}\approx 0.31
in the Rc band with
\alpha_{\rm max}
corresponding to
90^{\circ}
–
95^{\circ}
and
\alpha_{\rm inv}\approx 20^{\circ}
. Their estimate of
\alpha_{\rm max}
is probably more reliable than ours as their phase angle coverage extends up to
123^{\circ}
. For comparison, Fig.
5
shows the trigonometric models (Eq.
2
) fitted to the coma and the tail of C/2023 A3 (see Fig.
4
a) overlaid by observations at 684 nm wavelength of the coma of C/1995 O1 performed by
Ganesh et al., 1998
. This comparison shows that the coma DoLP of C/2023 A3 is nearly identical to that of C/1995 O1, indicating clearly that C/2023 A3 is a rare exemplar of a highest-
P_{\rm max}
comet. Our tail measurements reveal a still nearly two times higher
P_{\rm max}
value of about
0.64
obtained at
\alpha_{\rm max}
slightly below
90^{\circ}
. A similar increase of the DoLP (on a lower level, though) is apparent in polarimetric images of the comets 46P/Wirtanen and C/2023 P1 (Nishimura) in the transition region from the coma to the tail
(
Zheltobryukhov et al., 2020
;
Zheltobryukhov et al., 2024
)
. The estimated phase angle of inversion of
\alpha_{\rm inv}=20.5^{\circ}
lies well within the range of values found for a variety of other comets
(
Kiselev et al., 2015
;
Zubko et al., 2016
, e.g.,)
.

Empirical DoLP phase curves (Eq.

) fitted to our DoLP measurements of the coma (red curve) and tail (blue curve) of comet C/2023 A3, combined with DoLP measurements at 684 nm wavelength of the coma of C/1995 O1 (Hale-Bopp) acquired by

An image of comet C/2023 A3 that we took with a cooled color (RGB) CMOS camera and the 30/135 mm refractor on Oct 22, i.e., just one day before our first polarimetric imaging observation, does not show a green C
2
emission. Notably, we have been able to detect such a component easily with the same camera for several other, even much fainter comets, such as 13P/Olbers and 12P/Pons-Brooks, in 2024 (Fig.
6
). The post-perihelion spectrum of the coma of C/2023 A3 shown in Fig.
7
clearly reveals a C
2
emission line. That is in contrast to the pre-perihelion spectrum acquired by
Cambianica et al., 2025
on May 01, 2024, in which the C
2
emission line is barely visible, so that they classified C/2023 A3 as “carbon-depleted”. Also the post-perihelion integrated C
2
flux, though, is lower than the continuum flux integrated over the green and red spectral domain where our cameras are most sensitive, which explains that the coma does not appear green in the RGB image in Fig.
6
a. These findings have important implications for our simulations of the polarization behavior, as they imply a low-carbon composition and are our justification to assume a 30:70 olivine-carbon mixture for the coma and a predominance of light scattering by silicate dust in the tail. This compositional contrast is required to explain the observed difference in
\alpha_{\rm max}
between coma and tail. A similar quality of fit to our coma measurements would also be obtained with carbon fractions higher than 80%
(e.g., as in
Zubko et al., 2016
, for comets C/1995 O1 and D/1999 S4)
that would, however, likely imply a stronger C
2
emission than observed for C/2023 A3. Our DoLP observations of the tail, especially the small value of
\alpha_{\rm max}
, are consistent with a pure silicate composition.

Our proposed explanation of the difference in
P_{\rm max}
between coma and tail is that the dust agglomerates in the tail consist of slightly smaller monomers than those in the coma, with
r_{\rm mean}
corresponding to
125
nm vs.
137.5
nm. This behavior might be caused by the monomers in the coma being coated by a thin layer of frozen volatiles of about 10–15 nm thickness, which sublimates when the dust particles are heated by solar irradiation during their movement away from the coma into the tail. The assumption of
r_{\rm mean}=125
nm in the tail implies a value of
P_{\rm max}
sufficiently high to be consistent with our observations without the necessity to invoke differences in agglomerate structure and/or size between coma and tail.

There is no good coverage of the negative polarization branch of C/2023 A3 in our observational data. Our two measurements at phase angles between
16^{\circ}
and
17^{\circ}
, though, indicate absolute DoLP values of
0\geq P\gtrapprox-0.03
in the central coma (Table
1
). According to Fig.
3
, those values are lower limits to the negative DoLP because due to the low coma brightness, the SNR in the coma was below 70 although binning was applied. These measurements are consistent with the EMPOL observation of Mar 06, 2025, indicating a negative DoLP of
P\leq-0.0073
in the SLOAN r band at
14.1^{\circ}
phase angle (Table
2
). Here, a comparison to the comet C/1995 O1 may provide some additional insight. While C/1995 O1 traveled through the inner solar system, its coma was dominated by small dust particles
(
Bagnulo et al., 2024
)
, its negative polarisation was found to be weak
(
Ganesh et al., 1998
, see also Fig.
5
)
, and the comet was classified as dynamically young
(
Bailey et al., 1996
)
. In that line of thought, the similarly weak negative polarization of the coma of C/2023 A3 (Fig.
4
a), which was classified as dynamically new
(
Cambianica et al., 2025
)
, can likewise be explained by a predominance of small dust particles in accordance with our modeling results.

5
Conclusions and future work

In this study we analyzed the size and structure of the dust grains in the coma and tail of comet C/2023 A3 (Tsuchinshan-ATLAS) based on wide-field polarimetric imaging. We measured the DoLP of the coma and tail across a broad range of phase angles between
16.4^{\circ}
and
85.5^{\circ}
. Based on a Monte-Carlo simulation, we quantified both random and systematic errors of our DoLP measurements in dependence of the image SNR. With a maximum DoLP of
0.34
in the coma, comet C/2023 A3 belongs to the class of highest-polarization comets, and the DoLP of the tail even becomes as high as
0.64
. The phase angle of the DoLP maximum corresponds to a (fairly uncertain) value of
104^{\circ}
in the coma and to
86^{\circ}
in the tail, respectively, implying a compositional contrast with more carbon being present in the coma than in the tail. The weak negative polarization branch of the coma of C/2023 A3 can be explained by small dust particles dominating the scattering behavior.

We reproduced our observations by T-matrix modeling of the scattering behavior of fractal agglomerates consisting of spherical monomers, using the MSTM4 software framework. Based on colour images of the complete comet and a post-perihelion spectrum of its coma, which both imply a low-carbon composition due to the weakness of the C
2
emission, we assumed in our simulations a 30:70 olivine-carbon mixture of the dust grains in the coma and a purely silicate composition in the tail. Our modeling results indicate that the monomers forming the agglomerates in the tail are smaller by about 10% than those in the coma. Our suggested explanation is a frozen volatile coating of 10–15 nm thickness of the monomers forming the coma agglomerates, where the volatiles sublimate as a result of solar heating while the grains move from the coma towards the tail.

Future work will involve further wide-field polarimetric observations of the tails of different types of comets, preferentially with more sensitive sensors allowing for the use of photometric filters covering a broad spectral range in the visible and near-infrared domain while maintaining a high SNR. For such observations, broad phase angle coverage will be of central importance, not only at large phase angles in order to accurately determine the phase angle of maximum DoLP but also at small phase angles for better estimating the minimum DoLP and the phase angle at which it occurs.

Data availability

The polarimetric image data analyzed in this study and the modeling results will be published online permanently upon publication of the manuscript in a peer-reviewed journal.

On the Chromatic Number of Stable Kneser Hypergraphs: Verifying the Conjecture for New Families
Hamid Reza Daneshpajouh

Abstract
One of the key unsolved conjectures in hypergraph coloring is about the chromatic number of s s -stable r r -uniform Kneser hypergraphs KG r ​ ( n , k ) s ​ -stab \mathrm{KG}^{r}(n,k)_{s\textup{-stab}} . The problem remains largely open, particularly in the case where s > r ≥ 3 s>r\geq 3 . To the best of our knowledge, no information is available except a limited number of computations conducted for the instances when r = 3 , 4 r=3,4 , s = 4 , 5 s=4,5 , k = 2 , 3 k=2,3 with some n n does not exceed 14. In this study, we verify the conjecture for infinity many values of the parameters n n and k k . In particular, we demonstrate: (i) the validity of the conjecture for r = 4 r=4 , s = 6 s=6 under the condition that 3 | n 3\mid n or k = 2 k=2 , and (ii) for r = 4 r=4 , k = 2 k=2 , s = 5 s=5 given 3 ∤ n 3\nmid n . As far as we are aware, this provides the first rigorous theoretical proof of the conjecture (for the case s > r ≥ 3 s>r\geq 3 ) for infinitely many parameter values, extending beyond finite computational verification. Furthermore, our methods rely on a detailed study of vector-stable Kneser graphs, an approach that not only yields these results but also provides a deeper understanding of their chromatic numbers.

As an initial step, let us present a similar upper bound for the chromatic number of vector-stable Kneser hypergraphs. For convenience, we will define the chromatic number of the null graph (the graph that contains no vertices) to be

Lemma 10
.

For any vector
\vec{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{k})
of positive integers, natural numbers

\chi(\mathrm{KG}^{r}(n,k)_{\vec{s}\textup{-stab}})\leq\left\lceil\frac{n-\max\{r(k-1),\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}s_{i}\}}{r-1}\right\rceil.

Proof.

The hypergraph
\mathrm{KG}^{r}(n,k)_{\vec{s}\textup{-stab}}
is the sub-hypergraph of the usual
r
-uniform hypergraph
\mathrm{KG}^{r}(n,k)

\mathrm{KG}^{r}(n,k)
admits a proper coloring with
\left\lceil\frac{n-r(k-1)}{r-1}\right\rceil
number of colors. So, on the one hand we have

\chi(\mathrm{KG}^{r}(n,k)_{\vec{s}\textup{-stab}})\leq\left\lceil\frac{n-r(k-1)}{r-1}\right\rceil.

On the other hand, let

\vec{s}
-stable

\displaystyle c:

\displaystyle V(\mathrm{KG}^{r}(n,k)_{\vec{s}\textup{-stab}})\longrightarrow\{1,\ldots,q+1\}

\displaystyle B\longmapsto\min\{i:A_{i}\cap B\neq\emptyset\}.

Moreover, this map defines a proper coloring for
\mathrm{KG}^{r}(n,k)_{\vec{s}\textup{-stab}}
as the size of each

is less than or equal

. Thus,

\chi(\mathrm{KG}^{r}(n,k)_{\vec{s}\textup{-stab}})\leq\left\lceil\frac{n-(\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}s_{i})}{r-1}\right\rceil,

which completes the proof.

\displaystyle B=\{b_{1}<\cdots<b_{k}\}

the desired result is obtained upon verifying our claim for the scenario where the minimum occurs at the initial index. Consequently, establishing the formula for the case

is sufficient. Now, define the coloring

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\left\{s_{1}+1,\ldots,n-\left(\sum_{i=2}^{k}s_{i}-s_{1}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle\longmapsto\min\left\{i\in B:s_{1}+1\leq i\leq n-\left(\sum_{i=2}^{k}s_{i}-s_{1}\right)\right\}.

is well-defined; that is, every
\vec{s}
-stable set

For

is defined as

. The positive and negative components are denoted as

and

universally for all

such that if

then

. For any non-zero

, its alternating number, denoted as

, is the largest subset
\{i_{1}<i_{2}<\dots<i_{\ell}\}\subseteq\text{supp}(A)
(based on the order

for every

Lemma 14

(Tucker’s Lemma)
.

Let
\lambda:\{-1,0,1\}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}\to\{\pm 1,\pm 2,\dots,\pm m\}
be a function satisfying the following two properties:

1.

\lambda(-A)=-\lambda(A)
for all
A\in\{-1,0,1\}^{n}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
, and

2.

If
A_{1}\preceq A_{2}
and
|\lambda(A_{1})|=|\lambda(A_{2})|
, then
\lambda(A_{1})=\lambda(A_{2})
.

Then
m\geq n
.

We now establish the second part of Theorem

, using a key idea from
[
3
]
.

Proof of Theorem
8

.

Again, by Lemma
13
, we may assume without loss of generality that
s_{k}=m
. The upper bound

\chi\left(\mathrm{KG}^{2}(n,k)_{\vec{s}\textup{-stab}}\right)\leq n-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}s_{i}

has been established in Lemma

. It remains to prove the matching lower bound. Let

. Define the parameters

\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad m=t+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}s_{i}.

We will construct a function

satisfying the conditions of Tucker’s Lemma (Lemma

). The function is defined in two cases based on the alternating number
\mathrm{Alt}(A)
of a non-zero signed set

Case 1:

.

In this case, we define

Note that

Let
\mathrm{Alt}(A)=\{i_{1}<i_{2}<\dots<i_{\ell}\}

Let us now proceed to verify the conditions of Tucker’s lemma. It is straightforward to observe the first property from the definition of

Proof of Theorem

To establish our final result, we require several auxiliary concepts and two lemmas. Let
n,s_{1},s_{2}
be positive integers. We define the graph

to have the vertex set

, where two distinct vertices

is isomorphic to the complement of the line graph of

. This connection allows us to translate the problem of coloring the stable Kneser graph into a problem of partitioning the edge set of
W(n,s_{1},s_{2})
. In general, proper colorings of
G
are exactly partitions of the edge set of
H
into stars and triangles. A
star
is a tree with a vertex, the
center
of the star, connected to all other vertices. A single-edge tree is in particular a star. In this particular case, we will always assume that exactly one of the two vertices has been identified as the center, so as to be in a position to always speak of the center of a star without ambiguity. A
triangle
is a circuit of length
3
. We call such a partition of
H
into stars and triangles an
ST-partition
. Any element in an ST-partition is called a
part
. This viewpoint has been successfully developed and applied for solving similar questions in
[
8
]
and later in
[
6
]
, which has turned out to be indeed helpful for this case as well.

Lemma 15
.

Let
n,s_{1},s_{2}
be positive integers such that
s_{2}\geq 2s_{1}
, and
n\geq 2s_{2}-2
. Then

\alpha(W(n,s_{1},s_{2}))=2s_{1}.

Proof.

We first prove the lower bound
\alpha(W(n,s_{1},s_{2}))\geq 2s_{1}
. Consider the set

S_{0}=\{1,2,\dots,s_{1}\}\cup\{n-s_{1}+1,n-s_{1}+2,\dots,n\}.

This set has cardinality
2s_{1}
. We claim
S_{0}
is an independent set in
W(n,s_{1},s_{2})
. For any two distinct vertices
i,j\in S_{0}
, the distance
|i-j|
is either less than
s_{1}
(if both are in the initial segment or both in the terminal segment) or strictly greater than
n-s_{2}
(if one is in the initial and the other in the terminal segment, since
|j-i|\geq(n-s_{1}+1)-s_{1}\geq s_{2}+1>n-s_{2}
as our hypothesis says

). In both cases, the pair

is not an edge by the definition of

. Hence,

is independent, which establishes the lower bound. For the upper bound, let

be an independent set of the maximum size. By symmetry, assume

. The vertex

is adjacent to all

. Therefore,

.

Suppose for contradiction that

. Let

be the

smallest elements in this intersection. Then:

s_{1}\leq a_{s_{1}+1}-a_{1}\leq n-(n-s_{2}+2)=s_{2}-2\leq n-s_{2},

where the last inequality uses the hypothesis

, and

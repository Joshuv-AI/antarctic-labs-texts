The global Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture for Fourier–Jacobi periods on unitary groups III: Proof of the main theorems
Paul Boisseau, Weixiao Lu, Hang Xue

Abstract
. This is the third and the last of a series of three papers where we prove the Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture for Fourier–Jacobi periods on unitary groups and an Ichino–Ikeda type refinement. Our strategy is based on the comparison of relative trace formulae formulated by Liu. In this paper, we compute the spectral expansions of these formulae and end the proof of the conjectures via a reduction to the corank zero case.



1.
Introduction

In the 1990s, Gross and Prasad
[
24
,
25
]
formulated some conjectures on the
restriction problems for orthogonal groups. These conjectures were later
extended to all classical groups by Gan, Gross and Prasad in their
book
[
21
]
. Ichino and Ikeda
[
30
]
gave a refinement of the
conjecture of Gross and Prasad, and this refinement was further extended to
other cases
[
28
,
45
,
62
,
60
]
. They are usually
referred to as the Gan–Gross–Prasad (GGP) conjectures and have attracted
a significant amount of research. They describe the relation
between certain period integrals of automorphic forms on classical groups and
the central values of some
L
-functions.

There are two kinds of period integrals in the GGP conjecture: Bessel periods
and Fourier–Jacobi periods. Fourier–Jacobi periods usually involve a theta
function while Bessel periods do not. Specifying to the case of unitary
groups, Bessel periods are on the unitary groups
\U(n)\times\U(m)
where

n-m
is odd, while the Fourier–Jacobi case is when
n-m
is even. The
cases of Bessel periods on unitary groups, together with their refinements,
are now completely settled through the work of many people,
cf.
[
32
,
65
,
69
,
68
,
63
,
9
,
10
,
6
,
4
,
5
]
for an
incomplete list. The proof follows the approach of Jacquet and Rallis
via the comparison of relative trace formulae.

Inspired by the work of Jacquet and Rallis, Liu
[
44
]
proposed a
relative trace formula approach towards the Fourier–Jacobi case of the conjecture. Following
this approach, the third author proved in
[
61
,
62
]
some cases of the
GGP conjecture for
\U(n)\times\U(n)
under various local conditions.

The goal of this series of three papers (
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
and the current text) is to work out the comparison of these relative trace formulae in general, and obtain the GGP and Ichino–Ikeda conjectures for arbitrary Fourier–Jacobi periods
on unitary groups. This is the last paper in this series. Its objective is to compare the two spectral expansions of Liu’s relative trace formulae and to prove the conjectures. We now precisely state our main results.

1.1.
The GGP conjecture for Fourier–Jacobi periods

We first recall the construction of the Fourier–Jacobi period from
[
21
]
. They are integrals of automorphic forms along a Fourier–Jacobi subgroup against a theta series built using a Heisenberg–Weil representation.

1.1.1.
Fourier–Jacobi groups, Heisenberg–Weil representations

Let
E/F
be a quadratic extension of number fields, and write
\mathbb{A}
(resp.
\mathbb{A}_{E}
) for the ring of adeles of
F
(resp. of
E
). If
\mathbb{G}
is an algebraic group over
F
, we write
[\mathbb{G}]
for the automorphic quotient
\mathbb{G}(F)\backslash\mathbb{G}(\mathbb{A})
.

We fix
n
and
m
two integers such that
n=m+2r
with
r
a non-negative integer. Let
W\subset V
be two non-degenerate skew-Hermitian spaces of respective dimensions
m
and
n
. We assume that
W^{\perp}
is split, i.e. that
W^{\perp}=X\oplus X^{*}
where
X
is an isotropic subspace of dimension
r
. Let
\U(V)
and
\U(W)
be the
F
-algebraic groups of
E
-linear unitary transformations of
V
and
W
. Let
\{0\}\subset X_{1}\subset\ldots\subset X_{r}=X
be a complete flag of
X
, and let
U_{r}
be the unipotent radical of the parabolic subgroup of
\U(V)
stabilizing the flag
\{0\}\subset X_{1}\subset\ldots\subset X\subset X\oplus W\subset V
. Note that its Levi subgroup stabilizing
W
is isomorphic to
(\Res_{E/F}\GL_{1})^{r}\times\U(W)
. We define

\mathcal{U}_{W}:=\U(V)\times\U(W),\quad\mathcal{H}_{W}:=\U(W)\ltimes U_{r},

where the latter is embedded in the former by taking the product of the natural inclusion and the projection on
\U(W)
. The group
\mathcal{H}_{W}
is the
Fourier–Jacobi group
.

Let
q(\cdot,\cdot)_{W}
be the skew-Hermitian form on
W
, and let
\mathbb{W}
be the
F
-symplectic space with underlying
F
-vector space
W
and symplectic form
\Tr_{E/F}\circ q(\cdot,\cdot)_{W}
. We take a polarization
\mathbb{W}=Y\oplus Y^{\vee}
, so that
Y^{\vee}
is a
F
-vector space of dimension
m
. We have the Heisenberg group
S(W)
associated to
\mathbb{W}
, whose points are
\mathbb{W}\times F
and whose group law is described in (
3.1
) below. If we write
U_{r-1}
for the unipotent radical of the parabolic subgroup of
\U(V)
stabilizing the flag
\{0\}\subset X_{1}\subset\ldots\subset X_{r-1}\subset X\oplus W\subset V
, we have the alternative description
\mathcal{H}_{W}=\U(W)\ltimes S(W)\ltimes U_{r-1}
.

Let
\psi
be a non-zero automorphic character of
F\backslash\mathbb{A}_{F}
, and let
\mu
be an automorphic character of
E^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{E}^{\times}
whose restriction to
\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}
is
\eta_{E/F}
the quadratic character associated to
E/F
by global class field theory. They decompose as
\psi=\otimes_{v}\psi_{v}
and
\mu=\otimes_{v}\mu_{v}
. For each place
v
of
F
, by the Stone–Von-Neumann theorem, there exists a unique irreducible smooth infinite-dimensional representation of
S(W)(F_{v})
with central character
\psi_{v}^{-1}
, which we denote by
\rho_{v}^{\vee}
. It is realized on the local Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}(Y^{\vee}(F_{v}))
. Moreover,
\mu^{-1}_{v}
induces a splitting of the
S^{1}
-metaplectic cover
\mathrm{Mp}(\mathbb{W})_{v}\to\mathrm{Sp}(\mathbb{W})(F_{v})
over
\U(W)(F_{v})
which yields a Weil representation
\omega^{\vee}_{v}
of
\U(W)(F_{v})
associated to
(\psi_{v}^{-1},\mu_{v}^{-1})
. We refer to
[
51
]
for proofs of these statements. Finally, we take the
F
-morphism
\lambda:U_{r-1}\to\Res_{E/F}\mathbb{G}_{a}
defined in (
8.3
). It is stable under the action of
\U(W)\ltimes S(W)
. Consider
\psi_{U}:=\psi\circ\Tr_{E/F}\circ\lambda
which is an automorphic character of
U_{r-1}
. We can now define
\nu_{v}^{\vee}
the
local Heisenberg–Weil
representation of
\mathcal{H}_{W}(F_{v})
by the rule

\nu_{v}^{\vee}(ghu)=\omega_{v}^{\vee}(g)\rho_{v}^{\vee}(h)\overline{\psi}_{U,v}(u),\quad g\in\U(W)(F_{v}),\quad h\in S(W)(F_{v}),\quad u\in U_{r-1}(F_{v}).

It is realized on
\mathcal{S}(Y^{\vee}(F_{v}))
. As recalled in
[
21
]
*Section 22, the
global Heisenberg–Weil representation

\nu^{\vee}:=\otimes^{\prime}_{v}\nu_{v}^{\vee}
realized on
\mathcal{S}(Y^{\vee}(\mathbb{A}_{F}))
is an automorphic representation of
\mathcal{H}_{W}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
via the Theta series

\theta_{\mathcal{H}_{W}}^{\vee}(h,\phi)=\sum_{y\in Y^{\vee}(F)}(\nu^{\vee}(h)\phi)(y),\quad h\in\mathcal{H}_{W}(\mathbb{A}_{F}),\quad\phi\in\nu^{\vee}.

1.1.2.
Global Fourier–Jacobi periods

We give
\mathcal{H}_{W}(\mathbb{A})
the Tamagawa measure from §
2.2.4
. Let
\sigma
be a cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathcal{U}_{W}(\mathbb{A})
. The
Fourier–Jacobi period
is the absolutely convergent integral

(1.1)

\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}_{W}}(\varphi,\phi):=\int_{[\mathcal{H}_{W}]}\varphi(h)\theta^{\vee}_{\mathcal{H}_{W}}(h,\phi)\mathrm{d}h,\quad\varphi\in\sigma,\quad\phi\in\nu^{\vee}.

1.1.3.
Base-change

For any
k\geq 1
, we write
G_{k}
for the restriction of scalars
\Res_{E/F}\GL_{k}
. We say that
\sigma
admits a
weak base-change
if there exists a discrete automorphic representation
\BC(\sigma)
of
G_{n}\times G_{m}
such that, at almost all split places
v
,
\BC(\sigma)_{v}
is the split base change of
\sigma_{v}
. By
[
52
]
, such a representation is unique.

There is also a notion of
strong base-change
. By local Langlands functoriality, for all place
v
the representation
\sigma_{v}
admits a base-change
\mathrm{BC}(\sigma_{v})
which is a smooth irreducible representation of
G_{n}(F_{v})\times G_{m}(F_{v})
. If we assume that all the
\mathrm{BC}(\sigma_{v})
are generic, then by
[
48
]
and
[
37
]
,
\mathrm{BC}(\sigma)
always exists and is a strong base-change, i.e. it satisfies
\mathrm{BC}(\sigma)_{v}=\mathrm{BC}(\sigma_{v})
for all
v
.

Write
\BC(\sigma)=\BC(\sigma)_{n}\boxtimes\BC(\sigma)_{m}
. In the generic case, for
k\in\{n,m\}

[
48
]
and
[
37
]
also tell us that
\BC(\sigma)_{k}
can be written as a parabolic induction
\pi_{k,1}\times\ldots\times\pi_{k,r_{k}}
where the
\pi_{k,i}
are cuspidal self-dual automorphic representations of some
G_{l}
’s, mutually non-isomorphic, and such that the Asai
L
-functions
L(s,\pi_{k,i},\As^{(-1)^{k+1}})
have a pole at
s=1
. The
\pi_{k,i}
are unique up to permutation, and we set
S_{\sigma}=(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{r_{n}}\times(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{r_{m}}
.

Conversely, any automorphic representation
\Pi
of
G_{n}\times G_{m}
that can be written as an induction with the above desiderata is called a
discrete Hermitian Arthur parameter
.

1.1.4.
The GGP conjecture

The first main result of this paper is the Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture for cuspidal automorphic representation with generic base-change as stated in
[
21
]
. The statement we give is independent of
[
48
]
and
[
37
]
as it only relies on the notion of weak base-change.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Pi
be a discrete Hermitian Arthur parameter of
G_{n}\times G_{m}
. Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(1)

the complete Rankin–Selberg
L
-function satisfies
L(\frac{1}{2},\Pi\otimes\overline{\mu})\neq 0
;

(2)

there exist two non-degenerate skew-Hermitian spaces
W\subset V
of respective dimensions
m
and
n
and
\sigma
a cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathcal{G}_{W}
such that
W^{\perp}
is split, the weak base-change of
\sigma
is
\Pi
, and
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}_{W}}
does not vanish identically on
\sigma\otimes\nu^{\vee}
.

Remark 1.2
.

By the local Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture from
[
22
]
and
[
64
]
, the spaces
W\subset V
and the representation
\sigma
given by (2) in the theorem are unique if they exist.

1.2.
Local Fourier–Jacobi periods

The second main result of this paper is of local nature. We keep the notation of §
1.1
, and henceforth take
v
a place of
F
(which is allowed to be split in
E
). Let
\sigma_{v}
be a smooth irreducible representation of
\mathcal{U}_{W}(F_{v})
. We can consider the space of local Fourier–Jacobi functionals
\Hom_{\mathcal{H}_{W}(F_{v})}(\sigma_{v}\otimes\nu^{\vee}_{v},\mathbb{C})
. By
[
21
]
and
[
46
]
, it is of dimension at most
1
.

If we now assume that
\sigma_{v}
is tempered, we can produce an element in
\Hom_{\mathcal{H}_{W}(F_{v})}(\sigma_{v}\otimes\nu^{\vee}_{v},\mathbb{C})
by means of integration. Let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{v}
be an invariant inner-product on
\sigma_{v}
, and write
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2},v}
for the invariant inner-product on
\nu_{v}^{\vee}
obtained by integration along
Y^{\vee}(F_{v})
. Then we define the
local Fourier–Jacobi period

\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}_{W},v}(\varphi_{v},\phi_{v}):=\int_{\mathcal{H}_{W}(F_{v})}^{\mathrm{reg}}\langle\sigma_{v}(h_{v})\varphi_{v},\varphi_{v}\rangle_{v}\langle\nu_{v}^{\vee}(h_{v})\phi_{v},\phi_{v}\rangle_{L^{2},v}\mathrm{d}h_{v},\quad\varphi_{v}\in\sigma_{v},\quad\phi_{v}\in\nu_{v}^{\vee}.

This integral is not absolutely convergent if
r\geq 1
and needs to be suitably regularized (see §
9.1.3
). We prove that
tempered intertwining
holds for Fourier–Jacobi functionals. This was previously known in the
r=0
case by
[
62
]
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume that
\sigma_{v}
is tempered. Then the space
\Hom_{\mathcal{H}_{W}(F_{v})}(\sigma_{v}\otimes\nu^{\vee}_{v},\mathbb{C})
is non-zero if and only if the sesquilinear form
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}_{W},v}
is non-zero on
\sigma_{v}\otimes\nu^{\vee}_{v}
.

1.3.
The global Ichino–Ikeda conjecture

We go back to the global setting and let
\sigma
be a cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathcal{U}_{W}
. We henceforth assume that
\sigma_{v}
is tempered at all places
v
so that
\BC(\sigma)
exists and is generic. Note that under the Ramanujan conjecture, the genericity of
\BC(\sigma)
should be equivalent to the temperedness of the
\sigma_{v}
’s. Our last main result is a factorization of
\left|\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}_{W}}\right|^{2}
in terms of
L
-functions and of the local periods
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}_{W},v}
.

We equip the automorphic quotients
[\mathcal{U}_{W}]
and
[\mathcal{H}_{W}]
with the invariant Tamagawa measure. We have the Petersson inner-product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
\sigma
, and the inner-product
\langle\cdot,\cdot,\rangle_{L^{2}}
on
\nu^{\vee}
given by integration along
Y^{\vee}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
against the Tamagawa measure. We take a factorization
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle=\prod_{v}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{v}
and we have
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}}=\prod_{v}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2},v}
. We also take a factorization
\mathrm{d}h=\prod_{v}\mathrm{d}h_{v}
of the Tamagawa measure on
\mathcal{H}_{W}(\mathbb{A})
.

We consider the product of completed
L
-functions

\mathcal{L}(s,\sigma)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}L(i+s-\frac{1}{2},\eta_{E/F}^{i})\frac{L(s,\mathrm{BC}(\sigma)\otimes\overline{\mu})}{L(s+\frac{1}{2},\mathrm{BC}(\sigma),\mathrm{As}^{n,m})},

with
\mathrm{As}^{n,m}=\mathrm{As}^{(-1)^{n}}\boxtimes\mathrm{As}^{(-1)^{m}}
. The function
\mathcal{L}(s,\sigma)
is regular at
1/2
(see Remark
7.2
for a more general statement). For each place
v
we denote by
\mathcal{L}(s,\sigma_{v})
the corresponding local factor. We define the
normalized local Fourier–Jacobi period

\mathcal{P}^{\sharp}_{\mathcal{H}_{W},v}(\varphi_{v},\phi_{v}):=\mathcal{L}(\frac{1}{2},\sigma_{v})^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}_{W},v}(\varphi_{v},\phi_{v}).

It follows from the unramified Ichino–Ikeda conjecture of
[
13
]
that if

\varphi=\otimes_{v}\varphi_{v}
and
\phi=\otimes_{v}\phi_{v}
are
factorizable vectors, then
\mathcal{P}^{\sharp}_{\mathcal{H}_{W},v}(\varphi_{v},\phi_{v})=1
for almost all
v
. Our second main result is the following factorization expression. It is the Fourier–Jacobi version of a conjecture of Ichino and Ikeda in
[
30
]
for Bessel periods on orthogonal groups, and of Harris in
[
28
]
for Bessel periods on unitary groups.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\sigma
be as above. For every factorizable vectors
\varphi=\otimes_{v}\varphi_{v}\in\sigma
and
\phi=\otimes_{v}\phi_{v}\in\nu^{\vee}
we have

|\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}_{W}}(\varphi,\phi)|^{2}=|S_{\sigma}|^{-1}\mathcal{L}(\frac{1}{2},\sigma)\prod_{v}\mathcal{P}^{\sharp}_{\mathcal{H}_{W},v}(\varphi_{v},\phi_{v}).

1.4.
The Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture for generic Eisenstein series

In our approach, we first prove generalizations of Theorems
1.1
and
1.4
in the corank-zero situation (i.e.
V=W
) for a certain class of generic Eisenstein series. This implies the result in the general case by unfolding. We now explain the main steps of this strategy.

1.4.1.
Reduction to corank-zero

The first part of the argument is a reduction to the corank-zero situation where
r=0
and therefore
V=W
,
\mathcal{U}_{W}=\U(V)\times\U(V)
,
\mathcal{H}_{W}=\U(V)
embedded diagonally and
\nu^{\vee}
is simply the Weil representation of
\U(V)
. In this setting, we will write
\U_{V}
for
\U(V)\times\U(V)
and
\U_{V}^{\prime}
for
\U(V)
.

If
\sigma
is a representation of
\mathcal{U}_{W}
(either in the global or local context), we can consider the parabolic subgroup
P(X)
of
\U(V)
stabilizing the flag
X\subset X\oplus W\subset V
. Its Levi subgroup stabilizing
W
is
G_{r}\times\U(W)
. For any representation
\tau
of
G_{r}
, we can consider the parabolic induction
\Sigma:=I_{\U(V)\times P(X)}^{\U_{V}}\sigma\boxtimes\tau
. We show in §
9
and §
10
that the global and local periods
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}_{W}}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}_{W},v}
on
\sigma
can be unfolded in terms of
\mathcal{P}_{\U_{V}^{\prime}}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\U_{V}^{\prime},v}
on
\Sigma
respectively. For the first case, this relies on the unramified computations carried out in
[
13
]
. In the local situation, if we assume that
\sigma
and
\tau
are tempered, so will
\Sigma
, and this reduces Theorem
1.3
to the
r=0
case which was proved in
[
62
]
. In the global situation,
\Sigma
will not be cuspidal anymore. This leads us to consider the Gan–Gross–Prasad and Ichino–Ikeda conjectures for certain Eisenstein series.

1.4.2.
Regular parameters

We now describe the class of Eisenstein series we are interested in. Let
Q
be a standard (with respect to the upper triangular matrices) parabolic subgroup of
\U_{V}
with standard Levi
M_{Q}:=(G_{n_{1}}\times\ldots\times G_{n_{k}}\times\U(V_{0}))\times(G_{n^{\prime}_{1}}\times\ldots\times G_{n^{\prime}_{k^{\prime}}}\times\U(V_{0}^{\prime}))
, where
V_{0}
and
V_{0}^{\prime}
are skew-Hermitian spaces included in
V
. Let
\sigma
be a cuspidal representation of
M_{Q}
which we decompose accordingly as
\sigma=(\tau_{1}\boxtimes\ldots\boxtimes\tau_{k}\boxtimes\sigma_{0})\boxtimes(\tau_{1}^{\prime}\boxtimes\ldots\boxtimes\tau^{\prime}_{k^{\prime}}\boxtimes\sigma_{0}^{\prime})
. Set
\Sigma=I_{Q}^{\U_{V}}\sigma
. Then we say that
\Sigma
is
(\U_{V},\U_{V}^{\prime},\overline{\mu})
-regular with generic base-change
if

•

the base-changes
\mathrm{BC}(\sigma_{0})
and
\mathrm{BC}(\sigma_{0}^{\prime})
are generic;

•

the representations
\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{k},\tau_{1}^{*},\ldots,\tau_{k}^{*}
are all distinct, where we write
\tau^{*}
for the conjugate dual of
\tau
;

•

the representations
\tau^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\tau^{\prime}_{k^{\prime}},\tau_{1}^{{}^{\prime},*},\ldots,\tau_{k^{\prime}}^{{}^{\prime},*}
are all distinct;

•

for all
1\leq i\leq k
and
1\leq j\leq k^{\prime}
,
\overline{\mu}\tau_{i}
is not isomorphic to the contragredient of
\tau_{j}^{\prime}
.

The induced representation
\Sigma
considered in §
1.4.1
is
(\U_{V},\U_{V}^{\prime},\overline{\mu})
-regular with generic base-change for suitable choice of
\tau
as soon as
\BC(\sigma)
is generic.

1.4.3.
Fourier–Jacobi periods for Eisenstein series

Let
\Sigma=I_{Q}^{\U_{V}}\sigma
be
(\U_{V},\U_{V}^{\prime},\overline{\mu})
-regular. For
\varphi\in\Sigma
and
\lambda\in i\mathfrak{a}_{Q}^{*}
we have the Eisenstein series
E(\varphi,\lambda)
. For
\phi\in\nu^{\vee}
, the Fourier–Jacobi period
\mathcal{P}_{\U_{V}^{\prime}}
defined in (
1.1
) is not absolutely convergent. In
[
11
]
, we defined a truncation operator
\Lambda^{T}_{u}
. It is an adaptation of the work of
[
29
]
, our situation being slightly more complicated due to the theta series
\theta_{\U_{V}^{\prime}}
. We show in Proposition
5.2
that, because
\Sigma
is
(\U_{V},\U_{V}^{\prime},\overline{\mu})
-regular, the truncated period

T\mapsto\int_{[\U_{V}^{\prime}]}\Lambda^{T}_{u}\left(E(\varphi,\lambda)\theta_{\U_{V}^{\prime}}^{\vee}(\phi)\right)(h)\mathrm{d}h

is constant equal to a number
\mathcal{P}_{\U_{V}^{\prime}}(E(\varphi,\lambda),\phi)
. This is the
regularized Fourier–Jacobi period
.

1.4.4.
The conjectures for Eisenstein series

Our main result on Fourier–Jacobi periods for Eisenstein series is the following.

Theorem 1.5
.

Assume that
\Sigma
is
(\U_{V},\U_{V}^{\prime},\overline{\mu})
-regular with generic base-change, and let
\lambda\in i\mathfrak{a}_{Q}^{*}
. Then the Gan–Gross–Prasad and Ichino–Ikeda conjectures hold for the periods
\mathcal{P}_{\U_{V}^{\prime}}(E(\varphi,\lambda),\phi)
.

We defer to Theorems
7.1
and
7.3
for precise statements. In fact, the versions we prove do not rely on the existence of
\BC(\sigma)
and are independent of
[
48
]
and
[
37
]
. As claimed above, this result implies Theorems
1.1
and
1.4
by unfolding.

1.5.
The spectral expansion of Liu’s trace formula

To prove Theorem
1.5
, we perform a comparison of the spectral sides of Liu’s relative trace formulae. This analysis takes the first half of this paper and relies on the results of
[
11
]
and
[
12
]
.

1.5.1.
Two RTFs

The first RTF we consider is the
unitary
one, and is associated to the double quotient
\U_{V}^{\prime}/\U_{V}\backslash\U_{V}^{\prime}
. More precisely, for a Schwartz function
f_{V}
on
\U_{V}
with kernel
K_{f_{V}}
, and for
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in\nu^{\vee}
, we tentatively define

J_{V}(f_{V}\otimes\phi_{1}\otimes\phi_{2})=\int_{[\U_{V}^{\prime}]}^{\mathrm{reg}}\int_{[\U_{V}^{\prime}]}K_{f_{V}}(h_{1},h_{2})\theta^{\vee}(h_{1},\phi_{1})\theta^{\vee}(h_{2},\phi_{2})\mathrm{d}h_{1}\mathrm{d}h_{2}.

This integral is not absolutely convergent in general and needs to be regularized.

The second RTF is defined on general linear groups. Set
G=G_{n}\times G_{n}
,
H=G_{n}
(embedded diagonally into
G
) and
G^{\prime}=\GL_{n}\times\GL_{n}
(seen as a subgroup of
G
). For
\Phi
a Schwartz function in
n
variables over
\mathbb{A}_{E}
, we have the Epstein Eisenstein series
\Theta(\Phi)
from
[
33
]
. For
f
a Schwartz function on
G
, we define

I(f\otimes\Phi)=\int_{[H]}^{\mathrm{reg}}\int_{[G^{\prime}]}K_{f}(h,g^{\prime})\Theta(h,g^{\prime})\overline{\mu}(\det h)\left|\det h\right|^{1/2}\eta(g^{\prime})\mathrm{d}g^{\prime}\mathrm{d}h,

where
\eta
is some character (see §
5.3
for the precise definition). Once again, this is not convergent in general.

1.5.2.
Main inputs from
[
11
]

In

1.5.3.
Main inputs from
[

In

1.5.4.
This paper: comparison of the spectral sides

In this paper, we compute the spectral expansions of
J_{V}
and
I
. More precisely, on the unitary side we derive the spectral contributions of
(\U_{V},\U_{V}^{\prime},\overline{\mu})
-regular automorphic representations with generic base-change. These contributions take the form of a relative characters which involve the regularized period
\mathcal{P}_{\U_{V}^{\prime}}(E(\varphi,\lambda),\phi)
.

On the side of general linear groups, we compute the contributions coming from base-changes of these representations. In this paper, they are called
(G,H,\overline{\mu})
-regular Hermitian Arthur parameters. We defer to §
3.4.2
for a precise definition. The expansion in that case is written in terms of Rankin–Selberg and Flicker–Rallis periods, respectively denoted by
\mathcal{P}_{H}
and
\mathcal{P}_{G^{\prime}}

[

\mathrm{BC}

[

Once the spectral expansions of

and

[

on multipliers. This step requires the spectral characterization of matching from
[

The outcome of the comparison is stated as Theorem

\Sigma^{\prime}

2.
Preliminaries on spaces of functions and automorphic representations

In this section, we collect some general notation on reductive groups and their automorphic forms. We give a special emphasis on spaces of functions on automorphic quotients.

2.1.
Reductive groups

Let
F
be a field of characteristic zero. All algebraic groups are defined over
F
.

2.1.1.
General notation

Let
G
be an algebraic group. Let
Z_{G}
be the center of
G
. Let
N_{G}
be the unipotent radical of
G
and let
X^{*}(G)
be the group of
F
-algebraic characters of
G
. Set
\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{G}=X^{*}(G)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
and
\mathfrak{a}_{G}=\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(X^{*}(G),\mathbb{R})
.

Assume that
G
is connected reductive. Let
P_{0}
be a minimal parabolic subgroup of
G
. Let
M_{0}
be a Levi factor of
P_{0}
. We say that a parabolic subgroup of
G
is standard (resp. semi-standard) if it contains
P_{0}
(resp. if it contains
M_{0}
). If
P
is a semi-standard parabolic subgroup of
G
, we will denote by
M_{P}
its unique Levi factor containing
M_{0}
. We have a decomposition
P=M_{P}N_{P}
.

Let
A_{G}
be the maximal central
F
-split torus of
G
. If
P
is a standard parabolic subgroup of
G
, set
A_{P}=A_{M_{P}}
. Write
A_{0}
for
A_{P_{0}}
. The restriction maps
X^{*}(P)\to X^{*}(M_{P})\to X^{*}(A_{P})
induce isomorphisms
\mathfrak{a}_{P}^{*}\simeq\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{M_{P}}\simeq\mathfrak{a}_{A_{P}}^{*}
. If
P=P_{0}
, we write
\mathfrak{a}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{a}_{0}^{*}
for
\mathfrak{a}_{P_{0}}
and
\mathfrak{a}_{P_{0}}^{*}
. For
P\subset Q
, we have the subspaces
\mathfrak{a}_{P}^{Q}
and
\mathfrak{a}_{P}^{Q,*}
induced by the restriction maps
X^{*}(Q)\to X^{*}(P)
and
X^{*}(A_{P})\to X^{*}(A_{Q})
respectively. We will also write
\mathfrak{a}_{P}^{M_{Q},*}
.

Set
\mathfrak{a}_{P,\mathbb{C}}^{Q}=\mathfrak{a}_{P}^{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
and
\mathfrak{a}_{P,\mathbb{C}}^{Q,*}=\mathfrak{a}_{P}^{Q,*}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
. We have decompositions
\mathfrak{a}_{P}^{Q}=\mathfrak{a}_{P}^{Q}\oplus i\mathfrak{a}_{P}^{Q},\quad\mathfrak{a}_{P,\mathbb{C}}^{Q,*}=\mathfrak{a}_{P}^{Q,*}\oplus i\mathfrak{a}_{P}^{Q,*}
where
i^{2}=-1
. We denote by
\Re
and
\Im
the real and imaginary parts associated to these decompositions, and by
\overline{\lambda}
the complex conjugate of any
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}_{P,\mathbb{C}}^{Q,*}
.

Let
W
be the Weyl group of
(G,A_{0})
, which is by definition the quotient of the normalizer
N_{G(F)}(A_{0}(F))
by the centralizer
Z_{G(F)}(A_{0}(F))
. It acts on
\mathfrak{a}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{a}_{0}^{*}
. If
w\in W
, we will write again
w
for a representative in
G(F)
.

Let
P
be a standard parabolic subgroup of
G
, and write
\Delta_{0}^{P}
for the set of roots of
A_{0}
in
M_{P}\cap P_{0}
. If
Q
is another parabolic subgroup of
G
, we write
W(P,Q)
for the set of
w\in W
such that
w\in\Delta_{0}^{P}=\Delta_{0}^{Q}
. In particular, if
w\in W(P,Q)
we have
wM_{P}w^{-1}=M_{Q}
.

2.2.
Automorphic quotients and Haar measures

We now assume that
F
is a number field. Let
G
be a connected reductive group over
F
.

2.2.1.
Automorphic quotients

Let
\mathbb{A}
be the adele ring of
F
, let
\mathbb{A}_{f}
be its ring of finite adeles. Set
F_{\infty}=F\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R}
. Let
V_{F}
be the set of places of
F
and let
V_{F,\infty}\subset V_{F}
be the subset of Archimedean places. For

, let
F_{v}
be the completion of

at
v
. If

is non-Archimedean, let
\mathcal{O}_{v}
be its ring of integers. Let
\left|\cdot\right|
be the absolute value
\mathbb{A}^{\times}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}
given by taking the product of the normalized absolute values
\left|\cdot\right|_{v}
on each
F_{v}
. If
\mathtt{S}
is a finite set of places of
F
, set
F_{\mathtt{S}}=\prod_{v\in\mathtt{S}}F_{v}
. If
\mathtt{S}
only contains non-Archimedean places, write
\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\mathtt{S}}=\prod_{v\notin\mathtt{S}}\mathcal{O}_{v}
.

Let

be a semi-standard parabolic subgroup of
G
. Set

[G]_{P}=M_{P}(F)N_{P}(\mathbb{A})\backslash G(\mathbb{A}).

Let
A_{P,\mathbb{Q}}
be the maximal
\mathbb{Q}
-split subtorus of the Weil restriction
\Res_{F/\mathbb{Q}}A_{P}
, and let
A_{P}^{\infty}
be the neutral component of
A_{P,\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbb{R})
. Set

[G]_{P,0}=A_{P}^{\infty}M_{P}(F)N_{P}(\mathbb{A})\backslash G(\mathbb{A}).

If
P=G
, we simply write
[G]
and
[G]_{0}
for
[G]_{G}
and
[G]_{G,0}
respectively.

2.2.2.
Maximal compact subgroups

Let
K=\prod_{v\in V_{F}}K_{v}\subset G(\mathbb{A})
be a ”good” maximal compact subgroup in good position relative to
M_{0}
. We write
K=K_{\infty}K^{\infty}
where
K_{\infty}=\prod_{v\in V_{F,\infty}}K_{v}
and
K^{\infty}=\prod_{v\in V_{F}\setminus V_{F,\infty}}K_{v}
.

2.2.3.
Harish-Chandra map

Let
P
be a semi-standard parabolic subgroup of
G
. There is a canonical morphism
H_{P}:P(\mathbb{A})\to\mathfrak{a}_{P}
such that
\langle\chi,H_{P}(g)\rangle=\log\left|\chi(g)\right|
for any
g\in P(\mathbb{A})
and
\chi\in X^{*}(P)
. We extend it to the Harish–Chandra map
H_{P}:G(\mathbb{A})\to\mathfrak{a}_{P}
which satisfies: for any
g\in G(\mathbb{A})
we have
H_{P}(g)=H_{P}(p)
whenever
g\in pK
with
p\in P(\mathbb{A})
.

2.2.4.
Tamagawa measure

Let
\psi
be a non-trivial automorphic character of
\mathbb{A}
. For every place
v
of
F
, let
d_{\psi_{v}}x_{v}
be the unique Haar measure on
F_{v}
which is autodual with respect to
\psi_{v}
the local component of the additive character
\psi
of
\mathbb{A}
.

The choice of a left-invariant rational volume form
\omega
on
G
together with the measure
d_{\psi_{v}}x_{v}
determine a left-invariant Haar measure
d_{\psi_{v}}g_{v}
on each
G(F_{v})
(
[
59
]
). By
[
26
]
, there is an Artin–Tate
L
-function
L_{G}(s)=\prod_{v}L_{G,v}(s)
. More generally for
\mathtt{S}
a finite set of places we can consider
L_{G}^{\mathtt{S}}(s)=\prod_{v\notin\mathtt{S}}L_{G,v}(s)
. Define
\Delta_{G}^{*}
and
\Delta_{G}^{\mathtt{S},*}
to be the leading coefficient of the Laurent expansion at
s=0
of
L_{G}(s)
and
L_{G}^{\mathtt{S}}(s)
respectively. For each place
v
, set
\Delta_{G,v}:=L_{G,v}(0)
. We equip
G(\mathbb{A})
with the Tamagawa measure
dg
defined as
\mathrm{d}g=\mathrm{d}_{\psi}g_{\mathtt{S}}\times\mathrm{d}_{\psi}g^{\mathtt{S}}
where
\mathrm{d}_{\psi}g_{\mathtt{S}}=\prod_{v\in\mathtt{S}}\mathrm{d}_{\psi_{v}}g_{v}
and
\mathrm{d}_{\psi}g^{\mathtt{S}}=(\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}^{\mathtt{S},*})^{-1}\prod_{v\notin\mathtt{S}}\Delta_{G,v}\mathrm{d}_{\psi_{v}}g_{v}
. Note that for any model of
G
over
\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\mathtt{S}}
we have for almost all
v

(2.1)

\vol(G(\mathcal{O}_{v}),\mathrm{d}_{\psi_{v}}g_{v})=\Delta_{G,v}^{-1}.

Although the
\mathrm{d}_{\psi_{v}}g_{v}
depend on various choices, the Tamagawa measure
\mathrm{d}g
does not. This construction is valid as soon as
G
is a linear algebraic group over
F
(not necessarily reductive).

2.2.5.
Measures on subgroups

Let
P
be a standard parabolic subgroup of
G
. We equip
\mathfrak{a}_{P}
with the Haar measure that gives covolume

to the lattice

. We give

the dual Haar measure. If

, we equip

with the quotient measure. We equip

induced by the Harish-Chandra morphism (see
[
4
]
*Section 2.2.5).

We give
[G]_{P}
the quotient of our measure on

by the product of the counting measure on

with our measure on

, and

the ”semi-invariant” quotient measure by the one of

(defined for functions on

which transform by
\delta_{P}
under left-translation by

).

2.3.
Spaces of functions

We keep the assumption that
F
is a number field and that

is connected reductive over

2.3.1.

Let

such that

2.3.2.
Topological vector spaces

All the topological

-vector spaces that we will consider in this paper are locally convex. We will use the notation
\widehat{\otimes}
to denote the completed tensor product between two such spaces.

2.3.3.
Smooth functions

Let
\mathfrak{g}_{\infty}
be the Lie algebra of
G(F_{\infty})
, let
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}_{\infty})
be the enveloping algebra of its complexification and let
\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g}_{\infty})
be the center of
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}_{\infty})

By a level
J
we mean a normal open compact subgroup of
K^{\infty}
. If
V
is a representation of
G(\mathbb{A})
, we denote by
V^{J}
its subspace of vectors invariant by
J
.

Let
V
be a Fréchet space. We say that a function
\varphi:G(\mathbb{A})\to V
is smooth if it is right-invariant by some level
J
and if for every
g_{f}\in G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
, the function
g_{\infty}\in G(F_{\infty})\mapsto\varphi(g_{f}g_{\infty})
is smooth in the usual sense (i.e. belongs to
C^{\infty}(G(F_{\infty}))
). We write
\rR
(resp
\mathrm{L}
) for the actions by right-translation (resp. left-translation) of

and
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}_{\infty})
on such smooth functions.

2.3.4.
Heights, growth of functions

We take a height
\lVert\cdot\rVert
on

as in
[
4
]
*Section 2.4.1. If
P
is a standard parabolic subgroup of

, for any

we define

\lVert g\rVert_{P}=\inf_{\delta\in M_{P}(F)N_{P}(\mathbb{A})}\lVert\delta g\rVert.

If

\lVert x\rVert_{\mathbb{A}^{n}}:=\prod_{v}\max(1,\left|x_{1}\right|_{v},\ldots,\left|x_{n}\right|_{v})
.

For all

,

and any smooth function

we define

\lVert\varphi\rVert_{N,X}=\sup_{x\in[G]_{P}}\lVert x\rVert_{P}^{N}\left|(\rR(X)\varphi)(x)\right|.

If

Let us assume first that

Let

(G,H,\overline{\mu})
-regular cuspidal datum

. Since

is not Hermitian, the linear

vanishes identically on

by Theorem

. It follows that

. By

4.2
we conclude that

for some

and all

sufficiently positive. It follows from Theorem

that

.

We now assume that
\chi
is Hermitian. By
[
4
]
*Lemma 2.10.1.1, the family of functions

is absolutely integrable over
[G^{\prime}]
in

\mathcal{T}_{N}([G])
for some

. By Theorem

, the linear form

\mathcal{P}_{H}(\cdot,\Phi)
extends continuously from
\mathcal{S}_{\chi}([G])
to

\mathcal{T}_{\chi}([G])
. Since

we conclude that the integral

(4.22)

\int_{[G^{\prime}]}K_{f\otimes\Phi,\chi}^{H}(g^{\prime})\eta_{n+1}(g^{\prime})\mathrm{d}g^{\prime}

is absolutely convergent, and equals

\mathcal{P}_{H}^{*}\left(\int_{[G^{\prime}]}K_{f\otimes\Phi,\chi}(\cdot,g^{\prime})\eta_{n+1}(g^{\prime})\mathrm{d}g^{\prime},\Phi\right)=\mathcal{P}_{H}^{*}\left(K_{f,\chi}^{G^{\prime}},\Phi\right).

The absolute convergence of (

∎

Theorem 4.11
.

Assume that
\chi\in\mathfrak{X}(G)
is

(G,H,\overline{\mu})
-regular and Hermitian. Then for all

where the integral on the right is absolutely convergent.

By Theorem

, the two sides of

) are continuous in

, and the RHS is absolutely

(G,H,\overline{\mu})

5.
Spectral expansion of Liu’s trace formula on unitary groups

In this section, we fix
V
a non-degenerate skew-Hermitian space over
E/F
. We take a polarization
\mathbb{V}=Y\oplus Y^{\vee}
. We regularize the spectral contribution of
(\U_{V},\U_{V}^{\prime},\overline{\mu})
-regular cuspidal data to the
\U
-side of the Liu’s relative trace formula.

5.1.
Weil representation and theta series

5.1.1.
Local Heisenberg-Weil representations

Let
v
be a place of
F
. We described in §
1.1.1
the Heisenberg representation
\rho^{\vee}_{\psi,v}
of
S(V)(F_{v})
and the Weil representation
\omega^{\vee}_{v}
of
\U(V)(F_{v})
. The
Heisenberg–Weil representation

\nu^{\vee}_{v}
of
J(V)(F_{v})
is defined by the rule

(5.1)

\nu^{\vee}_{v}(hg)=\rho^{\vee}_{\psi,v}(h)\omega^{\vee}_{v}(g),\quad g\in\U(V)(F_{v}),\quad h\in S(V)(F_{v}).

It is realized on the local Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}(Y^{\vee}(F_{v}))
, and is unitary for the inner-product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}}
given by integrating along
Y^{\vee}(F_{v})
.

5.1.2.
Automorphic Weil representations and theta series

By choosing a basis, we have an
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-integral model
Y^{\vee}
, so that at each finite place
v
we have
1_{Y^{\vee}(\mathcal{O}_{v})}\in\mathcal{S}(Y^{\vee}(F_{v}))
. We may therefore consider the restricted tensor products
\nu^{\vee}=\otimes_{v}^{\prime}\nu^{\vee}_{v}
and
\omega^{\vee}=\otimes_{v}^{\prime}\omega^{\vee}_{v}
, both defined with respect to these unramified vectors. They are representations of
J(V)(\mathbb{A})
and
\U(V)(\mathbb{A})
respectively, both realized on
\mathcal{S}(Y^{\vee}(\mathbb{A}))
and unitary for the global inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}}
. It decomposes as
\prod_{v}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2},v}
with the choice of measure made in §
3.3
.

By
[
21
]
*Section 23, these representations are automorphic via the theta series

(5.2)

\theta^{\vee}(j,\phi)=\sum_{y^{\vee}\in Y^{\vee}(F)}\nu^{\vee}(j)\phi(y^{\vee}),\quad j\in J(V)(\mathbb{A}),\quad\phi\in\mathcal{S}(Y^{\vee}(\mathbb{A})).

In
[
11
, Section 3.3.5]
, we define a space
\mathcal{T}([J(V)],\psi)
of functions of uniform moderate growth on
J(V)
with fixed central character.

Lemma 5.1
.

There exists
N>0
such that
\theta^{\vee}
defines a continuous mapping
\mathcal{S}(Y^{\vee}(\mathbb{A}))\to\mathcal{T}_{N}([J(V)],\psi)
.

Proof.

If
V
is anisotropic, then
[J(V)]
is compact and there is nothing to prove. In general,

can be written as
V=X\oplus V_{0}\oplus X^{*}
where

is split. In that case, we can use the formula for the Weil representation given in §
8.2.2
. This reduces to the case of
\U(V_{0})
by using the same arguments as in Lemma
4.1
.
∎

5.2.
Fourier–Jacobi periods for regular Eisenstein series

We now define an extension of the Fourier–Jacobi period for Eisenstein series induced from

-regular cuspidal data.

In

Let

]
*Proposition A.1.1 (vi), we may therefore apply

Proposition 5.2
.

Let

is
(\U^{\prime}_{V},\overline{\mu})
-regular. Then the integral

The proof is very similar to
[
4
]
*Proposition 5.1.4.1, and we sketch the main steps for the convenience of the reader. Let
T
and

be two truncation parameters such that

[

\displaystyle\int_{[\U^{\prime}_{V}]}\Lambda_{u}^{T+T^{\prime}}\left(E(\cdot,\varphi,\lambda)\cdot\theta^{\vee}(\cdot,\phi)\right)(h)\mathrm{d}h

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{R\in\mathcal{F}_{V}}\int_{K^{\prime}_{V}}\int_{[M_{R^{\prime}}]}e^{\langle-2\rho_{R^{\prime}},H_{R^{\prime}}(m)\rangle}\Gamma^{\prime}_{R^{\prime}}(H_{R^{\prime}}(m)-T_{R^{\prime}},T^{\prime}_{R^{\prime}})\Lambda^{R,T}_{u}\left(E_{R_{\U}}(\cdot,\varphi,\lambda)\cdot{}_{R}\theta^{\vee}(\cdot,\phi)\right)(mk)\mathrm{d}m\mathrm{d}k.

Here,

Let

5.3.
The coarse spectral expansion of Liu’s trace formula on unitary groups

In this section, we recall the main results of
[

on the regularization of the

Let
\chi\in\mathfrak{X}(\U_{V})

,
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in\mathcal{S}(Y^{\vee}(\mathbb{A}))

sufficiently positive a modified kernel

[

.

The following properties hold for any

positive enough and any

.

(1)

[

[

(3)

[

of Liu’s trace formula on unitary groups.

5.4.
Expansion of relative characters on the
\U

We henceforth assume that
\chi
is a
(\U_{V},\U^{\prime}_{V},\overline{\mu})
-regular cuspidal datum of
\U_{V}

\U_{V}

5.4.1.
The relative character
J_{Q,\sigma}

By Proposition
2.3
,
[
43
]
*Theorem 2.2 and (

), we can define for
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}_{Q,\mathbb{C}}^{*}
in general position,
f_{V}\in\mathcal{S}(\U_{V}(\mathbb{A}))^{J}

(5.7)

For fixed

,
\phi_{1}
and

\phi_{2}
it is meromorphic in

Proposition 5.5

There exist continuous semi-norms
\lVert\cdot\rVert
on
\mathcal{S}(\U_{V}(\mathbb{A}))^{J}
and
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{Y^{\vee}}
on

(5.9)

Let

T

\sum_{(M_{Q},\sigma)}\int_{i\mathfrak{a}_{Q}^{*}}\left|\sum_{\varphi\in\mathcal{B}_{Q,\sigma}(J)}\int_{[\U^{\prime}_{V}]^{2}}\Lambda_{u}^{T}\left(E(I_{Q}(\lambda,f_{V})\varphi,\lambda)\theta^{\vee}(\phi_{1})\right)(h_{1})\overline{\Lambda_{u}^{T}\left(E(\varphi,\lambda)\theta^{\vee}(\overline{\phi_{2}})\right)}(h_{2})\mathrm{d}h_{i}\right|\mathrm{d}\lambda.

By Proposition

(5.10)

\sum_{(M_{Q},\sigma)}\int_{i\mathfrak{a}_{Q}^{*}}\int_{[\U^{\prime}_{V}]^{2}}\left|\sum_{\varphi\in\mathcal{B}_{Q,\sigma}(J)}\Lambda_{u}^{T}\left(E(I_{Q}(\lambda,f_{V})\varphi,\lambda)\theta^{\vee}(\phi_{1})\right)(h_{1})\overline{\Lambda_{u}^{T}\left(E(\varphi,\lambda)\theta^{\vee}(\overline{\phi_{2}})\right)}(h_{2})\right|\mathrm{d}h_{i}\mathrm{d}\lambda.

By the Dixmier–Malliavin theorem from
[

If there exists a place

*Lemma 1.7, Theorem

J_{Q,\sigma}(f_{V,+},bc(\lambda))=\left|S_{\Pi}\right|^{-1}\frac{L^{\mathtt{S}}(\frac{1}{2},\Pi_{\lambda}\otimes\overline{\mu})}{L^{\mathtt{S},*}(1,\Pi_{\lambda},\mathrm{As}^{n,n})}\prod_{v\in\mathtt{S}}J_{\Sigma_{\lambda,v}}(f_{V,+,v}).

Let

We now go back to the global setting and prove an unfolding identity.

10.1.
Definition of global periods

For

In the first case, this is the series
\theta^{\vee}
from (

). We will also make use of

\theta_{W}^{V,\vee}(h,\Phi):=\sum_{y^{\vee}\in Y^{\vee}(F)}(\omega^{\vee}_{V}(h)\Phi)_{Y^{\vee}}(y^{\vee}),\quad h\in\U^{\prime}_{V}(\mathbb{A}),\quad\Phi\in\omega^{\vee}_{V}.

These theta series are of moderate growth by variants of Lemma
5.1
.

By the unfolding identity (
8.13
), we have for
u\in U_{r-1}(\mathbb{A}),\;n\in N(X)(\mathbb{A}),\;\gamma\in P_{r}(F)
(the mirabolic group of
G_{r}
),

and

the formula

(10.1)

\overline{\psi}_{U}(u)\theta_{W}^{V,\vee}(n\gamma g_{W}g,\Phi)=\theta_{\mathcal{H}_{W}}^{\vee}(un,(\omega^{\vee}_{V}(\gamma g_{W}g)\Phi)_{Y^{\vee}}).

For triples
{(\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H},\omega^{\vee})\in\{(\U_{V},\U^{\prime}_{V},\omega^{\vee}_{V}),(\mathcal{U}_{W},\mathcal{H}_{W},\nu^{\vee}),(L,\mathcal{H}_{L},\nu^{\vee}_{L})\}}

Then for

Proof.

For

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\U^{\prime}_{V}}\left(\varphi_{V}\otimes E_{P(X)}^{\U(V)}(\varphi,s),\Phi\right)

where the last equality is obtained thanks to the change of variables

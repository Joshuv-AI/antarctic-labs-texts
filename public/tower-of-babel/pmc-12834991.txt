An improved Grey Wolf Optimizer based on mutation operator, evolutionary population dynamics, and nonlinear population size reduction strategy
Authors: Yufei Zhang, Tao Li, Hua Yang, Weifang Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Grey Wolf Optimizer (GWO) is limited by slow convergence speed, premature convergence, and exploration-exploitation imbalance. These limitations constrain the performance of GWO, and addressing these issues is of significant importance for enhancing its efficiency in engineering applications across multiple optimization domains within real-world scenarios. To address these limitations, this study proposes a novel GWO variant, called MENGWO, incorporating a mutation operator, an evolutionary population dynamics (EPD) mechanism, and a nonlinear population size reduction (NPSR) strategy. Inspired by Differential Evolution (DE), the update process of the introduced mutation operator can switch between exploration and exploitation modes based on a probability that varies iteratively. The enhanced EPD mechanism repositions underperforming search agents based on a probability that varies across iterations, either relocating them nearer to the current optimum or redistributing them randomly within the search space. Meanwhile, the NPSR strategy reduces the population size nonlinearly to improve computational efficiency. All three components incorporate dynamically adjusted mechanisms that depend on the progression of iterations, which are designed to work synergistically to enhance both exploration and exploitation. Experimental comparisons with Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), standard GWO, and five other GWO variants demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed algorithm. On low-dimensional CEC2005 benchmark functions, MENGWO outperforms other algorithms for 1 of 7 unimodal, 4 of 6 multimodal, and all 10 fixed-dimensional multimodal functions. In addition, on high-dimensional CEC2005 benchmark functions, MENGWO outperforms other algorithms for 2 of 7 unimodal and 4 of 6 multimodal functions. These results indicate that it enhances exploration capability while maintaining competitive exploitation capability. On CEC2022 benchmark functions, MENGWO outperforms other algorithms for 6 of 15 10-dimensional functions and 9 of 15 20-dimensional functions, demonstrating its efficacy on complex problems. Furthermore, the proposed algorithm achieves the best performance for 5 of 7 engineering design problems. In summary, this work proposes an effective optimization algorithm with balanced exploration and exploitation capabilities for engineering applications and complex problem domains.

Introduction

Optimization problems are ubiquitous in real-world domains1–11, including economic dispatch, robot control, and image processing. However, the growing mathematical complexity of many modern problems can reduce the effectiveness of traditional methods in locating optimal solutions12. Consequently, swarm intelligence algorithms have attracted significant attention in recent decades, due to their flexibility, robustness, and ease of implementation, leading to their widespread application in diverse optimization problems13. Swarm intelligence algorithms, which belong to a class of methods inspired by collective behaviors, locate optimal solutions through information exchange and collaborative mechanisms within a population. These algorithms are capable of addressing diverse problem types, including nonlinear, uncertain, multimodal, and high-dimensional problems, as well as multi-objective and dynamic optimization14. Many swarm intelligence algorithms have been developed, including Grey Wolf Optimizer (GWO)15, Whale Optimization Algorithm (WOA)16, Gyro Fireworks Algorithm (GFA)17, Starling Murmuration Optimizer (SMO)18, African Vulture Optimization Algorithm (AVOA)19, Frigatebird Optimizer (FBO)20, Competitive Framework Differential Evolution (CFDE)21, and Self-Adaptive Competitive Swarm Optimizer (SACSO)22.

As a swarm intelligence algorithm modeling wolf pack hunting behavior, GWO has attracted significant research interest due to its structural simplicity, robust convergence, and high accuracy23. Since its inception, some scholars have proposed variants of GWO to achieve a better balance between exploration and exploitation. Teng et al.24 hybridized GWO with PSO. Their method utilizes a Tent chaotic sequence for population initialization to enhance diversity and incorporates the personal best position mechanism from PSO to avoid local optima. Daniel25 integrated a genetic operator into GWO to improve the performance of the algorithm in terms of both structural and functional information in the fused image. To solve global optimization and engineering design problems, Nadimi-Shahraki et al.26 proposed an improved GWO by introducing a dimension learning-based hunting (DLH) search strategy to enhance both global and local search capabilities. Makhadmeh et al.27 hybridized the min-conflict local search algorithm (MCA) with GWO for the power scheduling problem in a smart home. The MCA was incorporated as an operator within the GWO framework to enhance its exploitation capability. Ahmed et al.28 proposed a new variant of GWO incorporating memory, evolutionary operators, a stochastic local search technique, and Linear Population Size Reduction (LPSR) strategy to achieve a proper balance between exploitation and exploration during the search for the global optimum.

Furthermore, evolutionary population dynamics (EPD) has proven to be an effective and low-cost method for improving the performance of meta-heuristics. Relatively few studies have employed EPD mechanisms to enhance the performance of GWO. Saremi et al.29 proposed a variant called GWO-EPD, in which the EPD mechanism relocates underperforming search agents with equal probability near the current best solutions (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha$$\end{document}, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\beta$$\end{document}, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\delta$$\end{document}) or random positions within the search space. This mechanism reinitializes the worst search agents at each iteration to maintain diversity among the search agents and further promote exploration and mitigating stagnation at local optima. Rezaei et al.30 introduced a novel diversity-based EPD operator. This approach first identifies the three most diverse search agents in each iteration; it then eliminates and repositions half of the best-performing agents around these diverse agents with equal probability. Zhang et al.31 proposed a GWO based on random opposition learning, strengthening hierarchy of grey wolves, and modified EPD. In this method, each agent uses two kinds of updating modes, including a global-best search strategy based on random dimensions and the original search strategies of GWO, to improve the optimization performance. The modified EPD strategy eliminates the worst-performing quartile of agents each iteration and repositions them using one of four random methods, selected with equal probability.

The literature review and Table 1 above summarize the studies on the development and modifications of GWO in recent years. The body of work demonstrates that since its inception, GWO has found successful applications across numerous fields, and its recent variants remain competitive among newer metaheuristics. However, most existing GWO variants primarily rely on a single strategy for updating search agent positions. This strategy coordinates exploitation and exploration through a convergence factor that decreases linearly over iterations, resulting in limited flexibility. Moreover, while EPD can enhance GWO performance, its implementation in previous studies has been rigid. The EPD strategies typically reposition underperforming agents at a fixed ratio or probability, either to areas near the optimal solution or to random positions within the search space. Such static allocation lacks the adaptability needed for the dynamically shifting balance between exploitation and exploration required throughout the iterative process. In general, the pack requires a more dispersed search for prey to avoid premature convergence during the early stages of the optimization process. Conversely, in the later search phases, a more concentrated search is required to locate the optimal solution. To address these limitations, this paper proposes a novel GWO variant incorporating a mutation operator, a modified EPD, and a nonlinear population size reduction (NPSR) strategy. The update process incorporates the mutation operator that functions in two modes. Each search agent updates its position based on the probability that varies with each iteration, operating in either exploration or exploitation mode. Similarly, the proposed EPD employs a probability that varies with the iterative process to allocate underperforming search agents either near the current optimal solution or at random locations within the search space. The proposed mutation operator and EPD operate synergistically to control the dispersion or concentration of the population, thereby dynamically balancing the algorithm’s exploration and exploitation capabilities throughout the optimization process. Furthermore, the NPSR adjusts the population size at each iteration according to a nonlinear function to reduce redundant exploration. The performance of the proposed algorithm is validated through extensive experiments on standard benchmark functions and real-world engineering case studies.

Proposed algorithm

Despite existing variants of GWO, slow convergence speed, premature convergence, and exploration-exploitation imbalance are limitations of GWO. To mitigate these limitations, this study develops a GWO variant, called MENGWO, incorporating a mutation operator, an enhanced EPD, and an NPSR strategy. Specifically, the mutation operator dynamically modulates the balance between exploration and exploitation. The enhanced EPD mechanism repositions the underperforming search agents. Concurrently, the NPSR strategy dynamically reduces the computational effort. The integration of these three components is designed to enhance the search performance. Details of each addition to GWO are provided in subsequent sections.

Mutation operator

The Differential Evolution (DE) algorithm32, as an evolutionary algorithm, has been widely studied and applied due to its simple principle and few control parameters. The main principle of the DE algorithm is to recombine the population and create a middle generation by exploiting individual variances, and subsequently to create the next generation of the population by exploiting the differences between the parental and offspring individuals.

As parameter a decreases linearly from 2 to 0 during iterations, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathrm{a}} \geqslant {\mathrm{1}}$$\end{document} allows the condition \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\left| {\mathrm{A}} \right| \geqslant {\mathrm{1}}$$\end{document} to occur in the first half of the search. During this phase, Eq. (12) enables each search agent to update its position through a probabilistic selection between exploration and exploitation modes. In exploration mode, the algorithm enhances its search capability by dispersing search agents through the diversity present in the current population. In the second half of the search process, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathrm{a}}<{\mathrm{1}}$$\end{document} ensures \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\left| {\mathrm{A}} \right|{\mathrm{<1}}$$\end{document} throughout, shifting Eq. (12) into exploitation mode. In this mode, search agents are guided toward the current optimal solution, thereby enhancing the local search capability of the algorithm.

Evolutionary population dynamics

Evolutionary population dynamics (EPD) is applied in various meta-heuristic algorithms as an efficient method to eliminate the underperforming search agents based on the survival of the fittest33. The EPD consists of two phases: eliminating the relatively underperforming search agents and repositioning these agents.

At each iteration’s end, sort the fitness values of all search agents, then the underperforming search agents constitute the bottom proportion k of the population size, with \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$k \in \left[ {0,1} \right]$$\end{document}. Nonlinear population size reduction

Reduction of population can effectively capture valuable search agent information, reduce unnecessary computational resource consumption, and improve convergence speed34. This paper proposes a nonlinear population size reduction (NPSR) strategy to control the reduction of population size and minimize redundant exploration.

In each iteration, after ranking search agents according to fitness value, underperforming search agents are eliminated. The population size follows the nonlinear function according to Eq. MENGWO algorithm

By embedding all the aforementioned strategies in GWO, the MENGWO algorithm is proposed. The pseudo-code of the MENGWO containing all the strategies is provided in Fig. 2.

Fig. 2Pseudo-code of the MENGWO algorithm.

Pseudo-code of the MENGWO algorithm.

Crucially, all three incorporated strategies exhibit mechanisms that vary throughout the iterative process, working synergistically to enhance the algorithm’s exploration and exploitation capabilities.

In the early stages of the search, the population size is reduced slowly, ensuring sufficient search agents for exploration. Concurrently, the update process of the introduced mutation operator updates search agents based on randomly selected agent mutations with a greater probability. Subsequently, the EPD mechanism predominantly relocates underperforming agents to random positions across the search space. Together, these strategies ensure robust exploratory capability.

As the search process proceeds, exploitation mode progressively becomes the dominant method. Correspondingly, the EPD mechanism shifts to repositioning a greater proportion of underperforming agents nearer to the current best solutions. As the search space for local search shrinks, the rate of population size reduction increases. Consequently, the algorithm enhances its exploitation capability while accelerating convergence.

Exploitation analysis

Unimodal functions have a single global optimum, which can be used to evaluate the exploitation capability of algorithms. Table 7 demonstrates the results achieved by the algorithms for seven unimodal functions. GWO and AGWO demonstrate relatively superior performance, each achieving the best average for 2 functions. MENGWO, PSO, and WVGGWO each achieve the best average for 1 function. Regarding standard deviations, AGWO achieves the best standard deviation in 3 cases, PSO and GWO each achieve the best standard deviation in 2 cases, and MENGWO in 1 case. The results of multimodal functions in Table 8 and fixed-dimension multimodal functions in Table 9 exhibit multiple local optima. The enhanced performance can be attributed to the inclusion of its mutation operator and EPD.

A success rate of 1 indicates convergence within the accuracy threshold in all runs, while a rate of 0 indicates failure across all runs.

Run-time analysis

As established in sub-section “Computational complexity”, the MENGWO algorithm incurs additional computational complexity compared to the traditional GWO. However, when the fitness function evaluation is sufficiently complex, it becomes the dominant cost, causing the overall computational complexities of both algorithms to converge. The run time is the average amount of time it takes to find a solution, which can be used to quantify the practical effect of the additional computational complexity. The run time can be calculated using the following equation28.

Conclusion and future work

This study establishes that the proposed MENGWO algorithm significantly enhances the performance of the standard GWO framework through the synergistic integration of a mutation operator, an improved EPD mechanism, and an NPSR strategy. These modifications accelerate convergence and promote a more dynamic and effective balance between exploration and exploitation across different phases of the search process. In the early phase, the algorithm maintains population diversity and expands exploration, preventing premature convergence. In the later phase, the focus gradually shifts to intensive local search within promising regions. The NPSR strategy complements this process through efficient computational resource management, maintaining a sufficient population size initially and progressively accelerating its reduction as the search evolves.

To rigorously validate MENGWO, comprehensive comparative analyses are performed with a suite of established and state-of-the-art algorithms, including PSO, GWO, MELGWO, LGWO, AGWO, WVGGWO, and OGWO. The evaluation commences with 23 low-dimensional benchmark functions, where the capability of MENGWO to handle different problem types is thoroughly examined. While MENGWO demonstrates competitive exploitation by achieving the best result for 1 of the 7 unimodal functions, its pronounced capability to escape local optima is evidenced by its performance on multimodal functions. It achieves superior results for 4 of 6 multimodal functions and, most notably, obtains the best results across all 10 fixed-dimension multimodal functions. Evaluations, including success rate, computational effort, convergence properties, Wilcoxon rank-sum test, and Friedman test, further demonstrate its competitive exploitation capability and superior exploration capability. Furthermore, experimental results on 13 high-dimensional benchmark functions demonstrate that MENGWO exhibits superior performance compared to the other algorithms for 2 of 7 unimodal functions and 4 of 6 multimodal functions, consistent with its performance on low-dimensional functions. Additionally, the algorithm achieves the best results for 4 of 7 engineering problems, thereby confirming the practical potential of the MENGWO algorithm in engineering applications.

This study has comprehensively evaluated the MENGWO algorithm using benchmark functions and engineering problems. Although MENGWO enhances the performance of GWO, it incurs additional computational complexity compared to standard GWO due to fitness value sorting operations when solving simpler problems. Future research will focus on reducing this computational complexity. Furthermore, the study demonstrates that MENGWO exhibits greater advantages when addressing complex problems. Future work will therefore focus on further validating the method’s applicability through additional case studies, such as parameter optimization and robotic control, and on assessing its performance on increasingly complex problems.

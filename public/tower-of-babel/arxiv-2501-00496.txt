Semi-Substructural Logics à la Lambek
Cheng-Syuan Wan

Abstract
This work studies the proof theory of left (right) skew monoidal closed categories and skew monoidal bi-closed categories from the perspective of non-associative Lambek calculus. Skew monoidal closed categories represent a relaxed version of monoidal closed categories, where the structural laws are not invertible; instead, they are natural transformations with a specific orientation. Uustalu et al. used sequents with stoup (the leftmost position of an antecedent that can be either empty or a single formula) to deductively model left skew monoidal closed categories, yielding results regarding proof identities and categorical coherence. However, their syntax does not work well when modeling right skew monoidal closed and skew monoidal bi-closed categories. We solve the problem by constructing cut-free sequent calculi for left skew monoidal closed and skew monoidal bi-closed categories, reminiscent of non-associative Lambek calculus, with trees as antecedents. Each calculus is respectively equivalent to the sequent calculus with stoup (for left skew monoidal categories) and the axiomatic calculus (for skew monoidal bi-closed categories). Moreover, we prove that the latter calculus is sound and complete with respect to its relational models. We also prove a correspondence between frame conditions and structural laws, providing an algebraic way to understand the relationship between the left and right skew monoidal (closed) categories.

Introduction

Substructural logics are logic systems that lack at least one of the structural rules, weakening, contraction, and exchange.
Joachim Lambek’s syntactic calculus
[
17
]
is a well-known example that disallows weakening, contraction, and exchange.
Another example, linear logic, proposed by Jean-Yves Girard
[
13
]
, is a substructural logic in which weakening and contraction are in general disallowed but can be recovered for some formulae via modalities.
Substructural logics have been found in numerous applications from computational analysis of natural languages to the development of resource-sensitive programming languages.

Left skew monoidal categories

[
24
]
are a weaker variant of MacLane’s monoidal categories where the structural morphisms of associativity and unitality are not required to be bidirectional, they are natural transformations with a particular orientation.
Therefore, they can be seen as
semi-associative
and
semi-unital
variants of monoidal categories.
Left skew monoidal categories arise naturally in the semantics of programming languages
[
3
]
, while the concept of semi-associativity is connected with combinatorial structures like the Tamari lattice and Stasheff associahedra
[
33
,
20
]
.

In recent years, Tarmo Uustalu, Niccolò Veltri, and Noam Zeilberger started a research project on
semi-substructural
logics, which is inspired by a series of developments on left skew monoidal categories and related variants by Szlachányi, Street, Bourke, Lack and others
[
24
,
15
,
23
,
16
,
9
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
.

We call the languages of left skew monoidal categories and their variants
semi-substructural
logics, because they are intermediate logics between (certain fragments of) non-associative and associative intuitionistic linear logic (or Lambek calculus).
Semi-associativity and semi-unitality are encoded as follows.
Sequents are in the form
S\mid\Gamma\vdash A
, where the antecedent consists of an optional formula
S
, called stoup, adapted from Girard
[
14
]
, and an ordered list of formulae
\Gamma
.
The succedent is a single formula
A
.
We restrict the application of introduction rules in an appropriate way to allow only one of the directions of associativity and unitality.

This approach has successfully captured languages for a variety of categories, including
(i)
left skew semigroup
[
33
]
,
(ii)
left skew monoidal
[
29
]
,
(iii)
left skew (prounital) closed
[
27
]
,
(iv)
left skew monoidal closed categories
[
25
,
31
]
, and
(v)
left distributive skew monoidal categories with finite products and coproducts
[
32
]
through skew variants of the fragments of non-commutative intuitionistic linear logic consisting of combinations of connectives
(\mathsf{I},\otimes,\multimap,\land,\lor)
.
Additionally, discussions have covered partial normality conditions, in which one or more structural morphisms are allowed to have an inverse
[
28
]
, as well as extensions with skew exchange à la Bourke and Lack
[
30
,
32
]
.

In all of the aforementioned works, internal languages of left skew monoidal categories and their variants are characterized in a similar way which we call sequent calculus à la Girard.
These calculi with sequents of the form
S\mid\Gamma\vdash A
are cut-free and by their rule design, they are decidable.
Moreover, they all admit sound and complete subcalculi inspired by Andreoli’s focusing
[
4
]
in which
rules are restricted to be applied in a specific order.
A focused calculus provides an algorithm to solve both the proof identity problems for its non-focused calculus and coherence problems for its corresponding variant of left skew monoidal category.

By reversing all structural morphisms and modifying coherence conditions in left skew monoidal closed categories, right skew monoidal closed categories emerge
[
26
]
.
Moreover, skew monoidal bi-closed categories are defined by appropriately integrating left and right skew monoidal closed structures.
It is natural for us to consider sound sequent calculi for these categories.
However, the implication rules are not well-behaved when just modeling right skew monoidal closed categories with sequent calculus à la Girard.

The problem stems from the skew structure concealed within the flat antecedent of
S\mid\Gamma\vdash A
.
While the antecedent
S\mid\Gamma
is defined similarly to an ordered list, it is actually a tree associating to the left.
We start in Section
2
, by introducing the sequent calculus à la Girard (
\mathtt{LSkG}
) for left skew monoidal closed categories from
[
25
]
and its equivalent sequent calculus à la Lambek (
\mathtt{LSkT}
), which is inspired by sequent calculus
for non-associative Lambek calculus
[
10
,
21
]
with trees as antecedents.

In Section
3
, we introduce definitions of left (right) skew monoidal closed categories and skew monoidal bi-closed categories, and normality conditions for skew categories.
In Section
4
, we describe two calculi that characterize skew monoidal bi-closed categories: one is an axiomatic calculus (
\mathtt{SkMBiCA}
), while the other is a sequent calculus (
\mathtt{SkMBiCT}
) similar to the multimodal non-associative Lambek calculus
[
19
]
.
In Section
5
, we introduce the relational semantics for
\mathtt{SkMBiCA}
via preordered sets of possible worlds with ternary relations.
Furthermore, we show a correspondence theorem (Theorem
5.7
) between conditions on ternary relations and structural laws on any frame.
The theorem allows us to prove a thin version of main theorems in
[
26
]
.

Sequent Calculus

We recall the sequent calculus à la Girard for left skew monoidal closed categories from
[
25
]
, which is a skew variant of non-commutative multiplicative intuitionistic linear logic.

Formulae (
\mathsf{Fma}
) in

A,B::=X\ |\ \mathsf{I}\ |\ A\otimes B\ |\ A\multimap B

A sequent is a triple of the form
S\mid\Gamma\vdash_{\mathsf{G}}A
, where the antecedent splits into: an optional formula
S
, called
stoup

[
14
]
, and an ordered list of formulae
\Gamma
and succedent
A
is a single formula.
The symbol
S
consistently denotes a stoup, meaning

can either be a single formula or empty, indicated as

,

Definition 2.1
.

Derivations in

\begin{array}[]{c}A\mid\quad\vdash_{\mathsf{G}}A\quad A\multimap B\mid\Gamma,\Delta\vdash_{\mathsf{G}}C\lx@proof@logical@and{-}\mid\Gamma\vdash_{\mathsf{G}}AB\mid\Delta\vdash_{\mathsf{G}}C\quad\mathsf{I}\mid\Gamma\vdash_{\mathsf{G}}C{-}\mid\Gamma\vdash_{\mathsf{G}}C\quad A\otimes B\mid\Gamma\vdash_{\mathsf{G}}CA\mid B,\Gamma\vdash_{\mathsf{G}}C\\[5.0pt]
{-}\mid A,\Gamma\vdash_{\mathsf{G}}CA\mid\Gamma\vdash_{\mathsf{G}}C\quad S\mid\Gamma\vdash_{\mathsf{G}}A\multimap BS\mid\Gamma,A\vdash_{\mathsf{G}}B\quad{-}\mid\quad\vdash_{\mathsf{G}}\mathsf{I}\quad S\mid\Gamma,\Delta\vdash_{\mathsf{G}}A\otimes B\lx@proof@logical@and S\mid\Gamma\vdash_{\mathsf{G}}A{-}\mid\Delta\vdash_{\mathsf{G}}B\end{array}

The inference rules of

are similar to the ones in the sequent calculus for non-commutative multiplicative intuitionistic linear logic (
\mathtt{NMILL}
)
[
2
]
, but with some crucial differences:

1.

The left logical rules
\mathsf{IL}
,
\otimes\mathsf{L}
and
{\multimap}\mathsf{L}
, read bottom-up, are only allowed to be applied on the formula in the stoup position.

2.

The right tensor rule
\otimes\mathsf{R}
, read bottom-up, splits the antecedent of a sequent
S\mid\Gamma,\Delta\vdash_{\mathsf{G}}A\otimes B
and in the case where
S
is a formula,

is always moved to the stoup of the left premise, even if

is empty.

3.

The presence of the stoup distinguishes two types of antecedents,
A\mid\Gamma
and
{-}\mid A,\Gamma
. The structural rule
\mathsf{pass}
(for ‘passivation’), read bottom-up, allows the moving of the leftmost formula in the context to the stoup position whenever the stoup is empty.

4.

The logical connectives of
\mathtt{NMILL}
(and associative Lambek calculus) typically include two ordered implications
\mathbin{\diagdown}
and
\mathbin{\diagup}
, which are two variants of linear implication arising from the removal of the exchange rule from intuitionistic linear logic. In
\mathtt{LSkG}
, only the right residuation (
B\mathbin{\diagup}A=A\multimap B
) of Lambek calculus is present.

For a more detailed explanation and a linear logical interpretation of
\mathtt{LSkG}
, see
[
25
, Section 2]
.

Definition 3.2

The equations on morphisms are analogous but modified to fit the definition.

Similar to left skew monoidal closed categories, natural transformations

In parts of the next sections, where we only work with thin categories (for any two objects

In the rest of the paper, we usually omit subscripts of natural transformations.

Definition 3.3
.

A left skew monoidal closed category is

–

–

if
\lambda

–

The

Given a skew monoidal bi-closed category
\mathbb{D}
with function

by induction on complexity of formulae and height of derivations respectively.
This construction uniquely specifies a strict skew monoidal bi-closed functor
\overline{G}:\mathsf{FSkMBiC}\to\mathbb{D}
satisfying
\overline{G}(X)=G(X)
.

However, it remains unclear how to construct a sequent calculus à la Girard for
\mathsf{SkMBiC}
.
A simpler scenario to consider is the sequent calculus for right skew monoidal closed categories.
In this context, recalling Definition
3.2
, where natural transformations are in an opposite direction compared to left skew monoidal closed categories.
One approach is to propose a dual sequent calculus to
\mathtt{LSkG}
.
Here, sequents would be of the form
\Gamma\mid S\vdash_{\mathsf{G}}A
, indicating a reversal of stoup and context, with all left rules applicable solely to the stoup.
We should think of the antecedents as trees associating to the right, structured as
(A_{n},(\dots,(A_{1},A_{0}))\dots)
.
Nevertheless,
\multimap^{\mathsf{R}}
, by definition, is again a right residuation, implying that
{\multimap^{\mathsf{R}}}\mathsf{L}
and
{\multimap^{\mathsf{R}}}\mathsf{R}
should resemble those in
\mathtt{LSkG}
.
This requirement then necessitates contexts to appear on the right-hand side of the stoup.

Fortunately, we can develop a sequent calculus, denoted as
\mathtt{SkMBiCT}
, which is inspired by
\mathtt{LSkT}
to characterize
\mathsf{SkMBiC}
categories.
Specifically,
\mathtt{SkMBiCT}
is an instantiation of Moortgat’s multimodal Lambek calculus
[
19
]
with unit, semi-unital, and semi-associative structural rules.

Trees in
\mathtt{SkMBiCT}
are inductively defined by the grammar
T::=\mathsf{Fma}\mid{-}\mid(T,T)\mid(T;T)
.
What we have defined are trees with two different ways of linking nodes: through the use of commas and semicolons, corresponding to
\otimes^{\mathsf{L}}
and
\otimes^{\mathsf{R}}
, respectively.
Contexts and substitution are defined analogously to those of
\mathtt{LSkT}
.
Sequents are in the form
T\vdash_{\mathsf{T}}A
analogous to those in Section
2
.

Derivations in
\mathtt{SkMBiCT}
are generated recursively by following rules:

\footnotesize\begin{array}[]{lc}&A\vdash_{\mathsf{T}}A\hskip 8.50012pt{-}\vdash_{\mathsf{T}}\mathsf{I}\hskip 8.50012ptT[\mathsf{I}]\vdash_{\mathsf{T}}CT[{-}]\vdash_{\mathsf{T}}C\\[5.0pt]

\mathtt{LSkT}
(the red part with
\mathsf{ax},\mathsf{IR}
, and
\mathsf{IL}
) and another for right skew monoidal closed categories (
\mathtt{RSkT}
, the blue part with
\mathsf{ax},\mathsf{IR}
, and
\mathsf{IL}
), linked by

, in other words, we can mimic all the blue rules in the style of

\small\begin{array}[]{c}\begin{array}[]{c}T[B\otimes^{\mathsf{R}}A]\vdash_{\mathsf{T}}CT[A,B]\vdash_{\mathsf{T}}C\end{array}=\begin{array}[]{c}T[B\otimes^{\mathsf{R}}A]\vdash_{\mathsf{T}}CT[B;A]\vdash_{\mathsf{T}}CT[A,B]\vdash_{\mathsf{T}}C\end{array}\hskip 9.24994pt\begin{array}[]{c}U,T\vdash_{\mathsf{T}}A\otimes^{\mathsf{R}}B\lx@proof@logical@and T\vdash_{\mathsf{T}}AU\vdash_{\mathsf{T}}B\end{array}=\begin{array}[]{c}U,T\vdash_{\mathsf{T}}A\otimes^{\mathsf{R}}BT;U\vdash_{\mathsf{T}}A\otimes^{\mathsf{R}}B\lx@proof@logical@and T\vdash_{\mathsf{T}}AU\vdash_{\mathsf{T}}B\end{array}\\

Theorem 4.1
.

Similar to
\mathtt{LSkT}
,
\mathsf{cut}
is admissible in
\mathtt{SkMBiCT}
.

\begin{array}[]{c}T[U]\vdash_{\mathsf{T}}C\lx@proof@logical@and U\vdash_{\mathsf{T}}AT[A]\vdash_{\mathsf{T}}C\end{array}

The proof proceeds similarly to that of Theorem

.
In particular, the new rules (

and the structural rules in blue) are all one-premise left rules, allowing us to permute

∎

The equivalence between

\begin{array}[]{c}T[A,B]\vdash_{\mathsf{L}}CT[A\otimes^{\mathsf{L}}B]\vdash_{\mathsf{L}}C\quad T,A\vdash_{\mathsf{L}}BT\vdash_{\mathsf{L}}A\multimap^{\mathsf{L}}B\quad T[A;B]\vdash_{\mathsf{L}}CT[A\otimes^{\mathsf{R}}B]\vdash_{\mathsf{L}}C\quad T;A\vdash_{\mathsf{L}}BT\vdash_{\mathsf{L}}A\multimap^{\mathsf{R}}B\end{array}

.

\mathtt{SkMBiCT}
is equivalent to
\mathtt{SkMBiCA}
, meaning that the following two statements are true:

•

•

For any derivation

, where

5
Relational Semantics of
\mathtt{SkMBiCA}
and Application

In this section, we present the relational semantics of
\mathtt{SkMBiCA}
.
Furthermore, the relational semantics for
\mathtt{SkMBiCA}
is characterized modularly, allowing us to construct models for semi-substructural logics step by step by incorporating additional structural conditions into the frame.
The modularity allows us to provide an algebraic proof for the main theorems concerning the interdefinability of a series of skew categories as discussed in
[
26
]
.

A preordered ternary frame with a special subset is
\langle W,\leq,\mathbb{I},\mathbb{L}\rangle
, where
W
is a set,
\leq
is a preorder relation on
W
,
\mathbb{I}
is a downwards closed subset of
W
, and
\mathbb{L}
is an arbitrary ternary relation on
W
, where
\mathbb{L}
is upwards closed on the first two arguments and downwards closed on the last argument with respect to
\leq
.

Definition 5.1
.

We list properties of ternary relations which we will focus on.

\small\begin{array}[]{ll}\text{Left Skew Associativity (LSA)}&\forall a,b,c,d,x\in W,\mathbb{L}abx\ \&\ \mathbb{L}xcd\longrightarrow\exists y\in W\ \text{such that}\ \mathbb{L}bcy\ \&\ \mathbb{L}ayd.\\
\text{Left Skew Left Unitality (LSLU)}&\forall a,b\in W,e\in\mathbb{I},\mathbb{L}eab\longrightarrow b\leq a.\\
\text{Left Skew Right Unitality (LSRU)}&\forall a\in W,\exists e\in\mathbb{I}\ \text{such that}\ \mathbb{L}aea.\\
\text{Right Skew Associativity (RSA)}&\forall a,b,c,d,x\in W,\mathbb{L}bcx\ \&\ \mathbb{L}axd\longrightarrow\exists y\in W\ \text{such that}\ \mathbb{L}aby\ \&\ \mathbb{L}ycd.\\
\text{Right Skew Left Unitality (RSLU)}&\forall a\in W,\exists e\in\mathbb{I}\ \text{such that}\ \mathbb{L}eaa.\\
\text{Right Skew Right Unitality (RSRU)}&\forall a,b\in W,e\in\mathbb{I},\mathbb{L}aeb\longrightarrow b\leq a.\end{array}

Given another ternary relation
\mathbb{R}
, we define

\begin{array}[]{cc}\text{$\mathbb{LR}$-reverse}&\forall a,b,c\in W,\mathbb{L}abc\longleftrightarrow\mathbb{R}bac.\end{array}

The associativity and unitality conditions are adapted from the theory of relational monoids
[
22
]
and relational semantics for Lambek calculus
[
12
]
.

An
\mathtt{SkMBiCA}
frame is a quintuple
\langle W,\leq,\mathbb{I},\mathbb{L},\mathbb{R}\rangle
, where
\mathbb{LR}
-reverse is satisfied,
\mathbb{L}
satisfies LSA, LSLU, LSRU, and
\mathbb{R}
automatically satisfies RSA, RSLU, RSRU because of
\mathbb{LR}
-reverse.

Unlike studies in
\mathtt{NL}
e.g.
[
12
,
19
,
21
]
, where two associativity conditions simultaneously hold for a relation or not, we explore two relations where one satisfies LSA and the other satisfies RSA.
Another distinction from the existing studies on semantics for
\mathtt{NL}
with unit
[
10
]
(or non-commutative linear logic
[
2
]
) is that while
W
is commonly assumed to be an unital groupoid (or monoid in the case of linear logic), here, we should consider that the unit behaves differently for different relations.

We denote the set of downwards closed subsets of
W
as
\mathcal{P}_{{\downarrow}}(W)
.

Definition 5.2
.

A function
v:\mathsf{Fma}\to\mathcal{P}_{{\downarrow}}(W)
on a
\mathtt{SkMBiCA}
frame is a valuation if it satisfies:

\small\begin{array}[]{ll}v(\mathsf{I})&=\mathbb{I}\\
v(A\otimes^{\mathsf{L}}B)&=\{c:\exists a\in v(A),b\in v(B),\ \mathbb{L}abc\}\\
v(A\multimap^{\mathsf{L}}B)&=\{c:\forall a\in v(A),b\in W,\ \mathbb{L}cab\Rightarrow b\in v(B)\}\\
v(A\otimes^{\mathsf{R}}B)&=\{c:\exists a\in v(A),b\in v(B),\ \mathbb{R}abc\}\\
v(A\multimap^{\mathsf{R}}B)&=\{c:\forall a\in v(A),b\in W,\ \mathbb{R}cab\Rightarrow b\in v(B)\}\end{array}

We define a
\mathtt{SkMBiCA}
model to be a
\mathtt{SkMBiCA}
frame with a valuation function, i.e.
\langle W,\leq,\mathbb{I},\mathbb{L},\mathbb{R},v\rangle
.
A sequent
A\vdash_{\mathsf{L}}B
is valid in a model
\langle W,\leq,\mathbb{I},\mathbb{L},\mathbb{R},v\rangle
if
v(A)\subseteq v(B)
and is valid in a frame if for any
v
for that frame,
v(A)\subseteq v(B)
.

Theorem 5.3

(Soundness)
.

If a sequent
A\vdash_{\mathsf{L}}B
is provable in
\mathtt{SkMBiCA}
then it is valid in any
\mathtt{SkMBiCA}
model.

Proof.

The proof is adapted from
[
12
,
21
]
, where the cases of
\alpha
and
\alpha^{\mathsf{R}}
have been discussed.
Therefore, we only elaborate on new cases arising in
\mathtt{SkMBiCA}
.

–

If the derivation is the axiom
\lambda:\mathsf{I}\otimes^{\mathsf{L}}A\vdash_{\mathsf{L}}A
, then for any
\mathtt{SkMBiCA}
model
\langle W,\mathbb{I},\mathbb{L},\mathbb{R},v\rangle
and any
a\in v(\mathsf{I}\otimes^{\mathsf{L}}A)
, there exist
e\in\mathbb{I}
,
a^{\prime}\in v(A)
, and
\mathbb{L}ea^{\prime}a
. By LSLU, we know that
a\leq a^{\prime}
, and then
a\in v(A)
.

–

If the derivation is the axiom
\rho:A\vdash_{\mathsf{L}}A\otimes^{\mathsf{L}}\mathsf{I}
, then for any
\mathtt{SkMBiCA}
model
\langle W,\mathbb{I},\mathbb{L},\mathbb{R},v\rangle
and any

, by LSRU, there exists
e\in\mathbb{I}
such that
\mathbb{L}aea
, which means that
a\in v(A\otimes^{\mathsf{L}}\mathsf{I})
.

–

If the derivation is the axiom
\gamma:A\otimes^{\mathsf{L}}B\vdash_{\mathsf{L}}B\otimes^{\mathsf{R}}A
, then for any
\mathtt{SkMBiCA}
model
\langle W,\mathbb{I},\mathbb{L},\mathbb{R},v\rangle
and any
c\in v(A\otimes^{\mathsf{L}}B)
, there exist
a\in v(A)
and
b\in v(B)
such that
\mathbb{L}abc
. By
\mathbb{LR}
-reverse, we have
\mathbb{R}bac
, therefore
c\in v(B\otimes^{\mathsf{R}}A)
.

–

The case of
\gamma^{-1}
is similar.

∎

Definition 5.4
.

The canonical model of
\mathtt{SkMBiCA}
is
\langle W,\leq,\mathbb{I},\mathbb{L},\mathbb{R},v\rangle
where

–

W=\mathsf{Fma}
and
A\leq B
if and only if
A\vdash_{\mathsf{L}}B
,

–

\mathbb{I}=v(\mathsf{I})
,

–

\mathbb{L}ABC
if and only if
C\vdash_{\mathsf{L}}A\otimes^{\mathsf{L}}B
,

–

\mathbb{R}ABC
if and only if
C\vdash_{\mathsf{L}}A\otimes^{\mathsf{R}}B
, and

–

v(A)=\{B\mid B\vdash_{\mathsf{L}}A\text{ is provable in $\mathtt{SkMBiCA}$}\}
.

Lemma 5.5
.

The canonical model is a
\mathtt{SkMBiCA}
model.

Proof.

–

The set
(\mathsf{Fma},\vdash_{\mathsf{L}})
is a preorder because of the rules
\mathsf{id}
and
\mathsf{comp}
, and the set
\mathbb{I}
is downwards closed because of
\mathsf{comp}
.
The relations
\mathbb{L}
and
\mathbb{R}
are downwards closed on their last argument because of the rule
\mathsf{comp}
.
They are upwards closed on their first two arguments due to the rules
\otimes^{\mathsf{L}}
and
\otimes^{\mathsf{R}}
, respectively.
These facts ensure that
\langle\mathsf{Fma},\vdash_{\mathsf{L}},\mathbb{I},\mathbb{L},\mathbb{R}\rangle
is a ternary frame.

–

We show two cases (LSRU and LSRU) of the proof that
\mathbb{L},\mathbb{R}
satisfy their corresponding conditions, while other cases are similar.

(LSLU)

Given any two formulae
A
and
B
, and
J\in\mathbb{I}
with
\mathbb{L}JAB
, we have
J\vdash_{\mathsf{L}}\mathsf{I}
, and
B\vdash_{\mathsf{L}}J\otimes^{\mathsf{L}}A
, then we can construct

as follows:

\begin{array}[]{c}B\vdash_{\mathsf{L}}A\lx@proof@logical@and B\vdash_{\mathsf{L}}\mathsf{I}\otimes^{\mathsf{L}}A\lx@proof@logical@and B\vdash_{\mathsf{L}}J\otimes^{\mathsf{L}}AJ\otimes^{\mathsf{L}}A\vdash_{\mathsf{L}}\mathsf{I}\otimes^{\mathsf{L}}A\lx@proof@logical@and J\vdash_{\mathsf{L}}\mathsf{I}A\vdash_{\mathsf{L}}A\mathsf{I}\otimes^{\mathsf{L}}A\vdash_{\mathsf{L}}A\end{array}

(LSRU)

By the axiom
\rho
, for any formula
A
, we have
A\vdash_{\mathsf{L}}A\otimes^{\mathsf{L}}\mathsf{I}
, i.e.
\mathbb{L}AIA
.

–

The valuation
v
is downwards closed because of the rule
\mathsf{comp}
.
The other conditions on connectives are satisfied by definition.

Therefore,
\langle\mathsf{Fma},\vdash_{\mathsf{L}},\mathbb{I},\mathbb{L},\mathbb{R},v\rangle
is a
\mathtt{SkMBiCA}
model.
∎

Theorem 5.6

(Completeness)
.

If
A\vdash_{\mathsf{L}}B
is valid in any
\mathtt{SkMBiCA}
model, then it is provable in
\mathtt{SkMBiCA}
.

Proof.

If
A\vdash_{\mathsf{L}}B
is valid in any
\mathtt{SkMBiCA}
model, then it is valid in the canonical model, i.e.
v(A)\subseteq v(B)
in the canonical model.
From
A\vdash_{\mathsf{L}}A
, by definition of
v
, we have
A\in v(A)
, and because
v(A)\subseteq v(B)
, we know that
A\in v(B)
, therefore
A\vdash_{\mathsf{L}}B
.
∎

For any ternary frame

A frame
\langle W,\leq,\mathbb{I},\mathbb{L}\rangle
is left (right) skew associative if
\mathbb{L}
satisfies LSA (RSA).
For other conditions, the naming is similar.
If
\langle W,\leq,\mathbb{I},\mathbb{L}\rangle
satisfies LSA, LSLU, and LSRU (respectively RSA, RSLU, RSRU), then it is a left (respectively right) skew.

We can think of a
\mathtt{SkMBiCA}
frame
\langle W,\leq,\mathbb{I},\mathbb{L},\mathbb{R}\rangle
as a combination of two ternary frames
\langle W,\leq,\mathbb{I},\mathbb{L}\rangle
(left skew frame) and
\langle W,\leq,\mathbb{I},\mathbb{R}\rangle
(right skew frame) sharing the same set of possible worlds, where the ternary relations are interdefinable by

\mathbb{L}\mathbb{R}
-reverse.
Whenever
\mathbb{L}\mathbb{R}
-reverse holds, then
\langle W,{\leq},\mathbb{I},\mathbb{L}\rangle
is left skew if and only if
\langle W,\leq,\mathbb{I},\mathbb{R}\rangle
is right skew.
In fact, we have:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\langle W,\leq,\mathbb{I},\mathbb{L}\rangle\ \text{left skew associative}&\longleftrightarrow&\langle W,\leq,\mathbb{I},\mathbb{R}\rangle\ \text{right skew associative}\\
\langle W,\leq,\mathbb{I},\mathbb{L}\rangle\ \text{left skew left unital}&\longleftrightarrow&\langle W,\leq,\mathbb{I},\mathbb{R}\rangle\ \text{right skew right unital}\\
\langle W,\leq,\mathbb{I},\mathbb{L}\rangle\ \text{left skew right unital}&\longleftrightarrow&\langle W,\leq,\mathbb{I},\mathbb{R}\rangle\ \text{right skew left unital}\end{array}

If we state the structural laws semantically rather than sequents, we can reformulate Theorem
5.7
without referring to sequents and valuations.
For example, we can define
\otimes^{\mathsf{L}}
on downwards closed sets of worlds as
A\otimes^{\mathsf{L}}B=\{c:\exists a\in A\ \&\ b\in B\ \&\ \mathbb{L}abc\}
and express
\alpha
as
(A\otimes^{\mathsf{L}}B)\otimes^{\mathsf{L}}C\subseteq A\otimes^{\mathsf{L}}(B\otimes^{\mathsf{L}}C)
.
It is the case that
\alpha
holds in a frame if and only if it satisfies LSA.

We construct a thin
\mathsf{SkMBiC}
from the frame
\langle W,\leq,\mathsf{I},\mathbb{L},\mathbb{R}\rangle
and provide algebraic proofs for main theorems in
[
26
]
.
The objects in the category are downwards closed subsets of
W
and for
A,B
, we have a map
A\to B
if and only if
A\subseteq B
.

Corollary 5.8
.

The category
(\mathcal{P}_{{\downarrow}}(W),\subseteq)
generated from any
\mathtt{SkMBiCA}
frame is a thin
\mathsf{SkMBiC}
.

A frame
\langle W,\leq,\mathbb{I},\mathbb{L}\rangle
is associative normal if it satisfies LSA and RSA simultaneously, and left (right) unital normal if LSLU and RSLU (LSRU and RSRU) are satisfied.
Therefore, by Theorem
5.7
, we have a thin version of the main results in
[
26
]
.

Corollary 5.9
.

Given any frame, for the category
(\mathcal{P}_{{\downarrow}}(W),\subseteq)
generated from the frame we have:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}(\mathbb{I},\otimes^{\mathsf{L}})\ \text{left skew monoidal}&\longleftrightarrow&(\mathbb{I},\multimap^{\mathsf{L}})\ \text{left skew closed}\\
(\mathbb{I},\otimes^{\mathsf{R}})\ \text{right skew monoidal}&\longleftrightarrow&(\mathbb{I},\multimap^{\mathsf{R}})\ \text{right skew closed}\end{array}

Moreover, if the frame satisfies

-reverse then:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}(\mathbb{I},\otimes^{\mathsf{L}})\ \text{left skew monoidal}&\longleftrightarrow&(\mathbb{I},\otimes^{\mathsf{R}})\ \text{right skew monoidal}\\

6
Concluding remarks

This paper discusses sequent calculi for left (right) skew monoidal categories and skew monoidal bi-closed categories in the style of non-associative Lambek calculus.
Compared to the sequent calculi with stoup, although the calculi à la Lambek are not immediately decidable but are more flexible in the sense that the sequent calculi for right skew monoidal closed categories (
\mathtt{RSkT}
) and skew monoidal bi-closed categories (
\mathtt{SkMBiCT}
) are presentable.
Moreover, we show that they are cut-free and equivalent to the calculus with stoup (Theorem
2.6
) and the axiomatic calculus (Theorem
4.2
).

In the last section, we focus on the relational semantics of
\mathtt{SkMBiCA}
via the ternary frame
\langle W,{\leq},\mathbb{I},\mathbb{L},\mathbb{R}\rangle
where
\mathbb{L}
and
\mathbb{R}
are connected by
\mathbb{LR}
-reverse and therefore if
\mathbb{L}
satisfies left skew structural conditions then
\mathbb{R}
satisfies right skew structural conditions automatically.
By Theorem
5.7
, for any
\mathtt{SkMBiCA}
model, we can construct a thin skew monoidal bi-closed category
(\mathcal{P}_{{\downarrow}}(W),\subseteq)
.
In addition, we can obtain algebraic proofs of main theorems in
[
26
]
.

A future project is to explore Craig interpolation
[
11
]
for semi-substructural logics.
In
\mathtt{LSkT}
, the situation is more complicated than either associative or fully non-associative Lambek calculi because we only allow semi-associativity.
Consider the statement:

Given a derivation,
f:T[U]\vdash_{\mathsf{T}}C
, then there exist a formula
D
and two derivations
f_{0}:U\vdash_{\mathsf{T}}D
and
f_{1}:T[D]\vdash_{\mathsf{T}}C
, and
\mathsf{var}(D)\subseteq\mathsf{var}(\delta(U))\cap\mathsf{var}(\delta(T[{-}]),C)
, where
\delta
is a function that transforms a tree into a list of formulae.

If we try to prove by induction on
f
, then there is a critical case

\small\begin{array}[]{c}T[(U_{0},U_{1}),U_{2}]\vdash_{\mathsf{T}}CT[U_{0},(U_{1},U_{2})]\vdash_{\mathsf{T}}Cf\end{array}

where
U=U_{0},U_{1}
, therefore, the goal is to find a formula
D
and two derivations
g:U_{0},U_{1}\vdash_{\mathsf{T}}D
and
T[D,U_{2}]\vdash_{\mathsf{T}}C
.
However, we cannot directly apply the inductive hypothesis twice on
f
, because the procedure of finding an interpolant formula and corresponding derivations is not height preserving.
Therefore, proving the interpolation property for semi-substructural logics is more subtle than expected.

Another possible direction is to incorporate modalities (exponentials in linear logical terminology) with semi-substructural logic as in
[
19
]
(modalities) and
[
5
]
(subexponentials) with non-associative Lambek calculus and non-commutative and non-associative linear logic.

Similar to the equational theories for
\mathtt{SkMBiCA}
discussed in Section
4
, we also plan to investigate the equational theories on the derivations of
\mathtt{LSkT}
and
\mathtt{SkMBiCT}
in the future.

A Non-linear Massive Gravity Theory of Geometric Origin
Thibault Damour, Tamanna Jain

Abstract
We study the number of propagating degrees of freedom, at non-linear order, in torsion gravity theories, a class of modified theories of gravity that include a propagating torsion in addition to the metric. We focus on a three-parameter subfamily of theories (“torsion bigravity”) that contains, at linear order, only two physical excitations: a massless spin-2 one (with two degrees of freedom) and a massive spin-2 one (with five degrees of freedom). We study the dynamics of the massive spin-2 field in the limit where the torsion field decouples from the metric. The number of degrees of freedom of the torsion field is found to change, at non-linear order, from five to nine .



I
Introduction

The predictions of the theory of General Relativity (GR) have been so far in outstanding agreement with gravitational observations and experiments over a very large range of scales, and field strengths (for reviews, see, e.g., chapter 21 in
[
1
]
or the book
[
2
]
). Nevertheless, since its inception, there has been a constant effort to study possible extensions of GR. The search for alternatives to GR is motivated by open questions both at ultraviolet scales (e.g.
[
3
]
)
and at infrared scales (e.g.
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
). The existence of consistent alternative theories of gravity is also useful for suggesting tests of GR
[
7
]
.

In the geometric approach to gravity, modifying GR can stem from extending the geometrical structure of spacetime. As early as 1923, Cartan
[
8
,
9
]
suggested to extend GR by formally keeping the Einstein-Hilbert action, but in a first-order formalism, considering the metric, and a general (metric-preserving)
torsionfull
connection, as independent fields (“Einstein-Cartan theory”). Cartan imagined that the torsion could be sourced by some material spin density. This idea was later made concrete by Weyl
[
10
]
who studied the coupling of (Dirac) spinor fields to gravity within a first-order formalism and pointed out that, in this approach, the spinors provide a spin-density source
\tau^{\mu\nu\lambda}
for the torsion proportional to

\bar{\psi}\gamma^{[\mu}\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\lambda]}\psi
. He also pointed out that in Einstein-Cartan theory the torsion does not propagate (i.e. is not a dynamical field), but is constrained to be proportional to its source
\tau^{\mu\nu\lambda}
, thereby leading (in a second-order formalism for the metric) to a contact term proportional to
\tau^{\mu\nu\lambda}\tau_{\mu\nu\lambda}
.
The ideas of Cartan and Weyl were further extended and developed by Sciama, Kibble, and others (see, e.g.,
[
11
]
for a review).

In this work, we study a class of geometric theories of gravitation, introduced in Refs.
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
,
which generalize the Einstein-Cartan theory in having a
dynamical
torsion field that propagates away from its source

\tau^{\mu\nu\lambda}
. We focus on the theories defined in Refs.
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
that contain (around flat-space backgrounds), in addition to Einsteinlike massless spin-
2
excitations, (even-parity) massive spin-
2
excitations (embodied in the torsion field) as well as (pseudo-scalar) massive spin-
0
excitations . Such theories will hereafter be referred to as
torsion gravity
theories. Below, we shall focus on the subclass of torsion gravity theories, called

torsion bigravity
theories, which
contain only massive and massless spin-
2
excitations.

Torsion gravity theories were recently revived with the hope that the geometrically-rooted massive spin-2 field they contain might define a theoretically healthy, and phenomenologically interesting, modification of gravity both in cosmological situations
[
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
, and in strong-field regimes
[
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
]
. The hope was that a geometrically well-motivated origin for massive spin-
2
excitations (through a dynamical torsion) would alleviate the problematic issues that arise when the extra massive spin-
2
field is linked to the addition of a second metric tensor,
f_{\mu\nu}
, besides
g_{\mu\nu}
, as in generic
bimetric gravity
theories. Let us recall that there are several problematic issues associated with the usual (Fierz-Pauli-type
[
24
]
) descriptions of massive spin-2 fields, notably: the Van-Dam-Veltman discontinuity
[
25
]
, the Vainshtein radius issue
[
26
]
, and the additional, ghostlike, sixth degree of freedom (dof)
[
27
]
.

Generic bimetric gravity theories (see, e.g., Ref.
[
28
]
), involving some general potential
{\mathcal{V}}(g_{\mu\nu},f_{\mu\nu})
, suffer from the arising, at the nonlinear level, of the unwanted, additional, Boulware-Deser ghostlike dof,
see, e.g.,
[
29
]
. The Boulware-Deser ghost was avoided by generalizing the special potential of the “ghost-free” massive gravity theory of Ref.
[
30
]
to a bimetric gravity framework
[
31
]
.
Note, however, that recent works (see
[
32
]
and references therein) suggest that the theory of massive spin-
2
excitations described by the ghost-free action of Ref.
[
30
]
is only valid up to an energy scale which cannot be parametrically larger than the mass of the spin-2 field.

By contrast with bimetric gravity theories,
it was shown in Refs.
[
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
]
that torsion gravity theories have the good feature of keeping the same number of dof in many situations,
involving, notably, dynamical (but linear) torsion excitations around generic nonlinear Einstein backgounds, or fully nonlinear spherically symmetric (but stationary) torsion gravity solutions. In addition, it was also found
[
21
]
that the massive spin-
2
torsion excitation does not introduce a
Vainshtein radius in static, spherically-symmetric
nonlinear solutions.
On the other hand, the study of time-dependent torsion perturbations
suggested that torsion gravity theories are only valid up to curvature scales of order of the squared spin-2 mass
[
22
,
23
]
.]

Motivated by the good features of torsion gravity
theories found in previous studies,
the aim of the present work is to investigate the number of propagating dof of torsion gravity theories in situations which have not yet been considered. Namely, we consider (vacuum) solutions which are both time-dependent and nonlinear in the torsion field. To simplify our investigation, we focus on the non-linear dynamics of the massive spin-2 (torsion) excitation in a well-defined limit where it decouples from the massless spin-2 (metric) one.

The paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
, we review the formalism and present the field equations for general torsion gravity theories along with reviewing the specialized three-parameter subfamily of torsion bigravity theories. The decoupling limit of the massive spin-2 field from the massless spin-2 field is presented in Sec.
III
. We first demonstrate the general, all-order structure of the field equations in the decoupling limit and then explicitly derive the field equations and investigate the constraints (degrees of freedom) at the linear and the next-to-linear order in Sec.
IV
. To explicitly understand the number of degrees of freedom for the field equations, we present several alternative action formulations of the decoupling limit in Sec.
V
. We then investigate the general structure of the constraints in a resulting first-order (Hamiltonian) formalism in Sec.
VI
. The number of dof is derived at the linear level in Sec.
VII
and, finally, at the non-linear level in Sec.
VIII
. Brief conclusions are presented in Sec.
IX
.

II
Formalism of Torsion Gravity

In this section, we discuss the formalism of torsion gravity theories following the notations of Ref.
[
19
]
, as updated in Ref.
[
20
]
.
The Latin indices
i,j...=0,1,2,3
are used to denote vierbein indices linked to a local orthonormal frame (
e_{i}^{\mu}
) while Greek indices
\mu,\nu...=0,1,2,3
are used to denote the space-time indices related to the coordinate frame
x^{\mu}
.
The frame indices are moved by the Minkowski metric
\eta_{ij}
whereas the space-time indices are moved by the metric
g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{ij}e^{i}_{\mu}e^{j}_{\nu}
, where
e^{i}_{\mu}
is the co-frame, such that
e^{i}_{\mu}e_{j}^{\mu}=\delta^{i}_{j}
. The metric signature used is
(-,+,+,+)
.

The most general action with propagating torsion (expressed in terms of the co-frame
e^{i}_{\mu}
and a general SO(3,1) connection
A_{ij\mu}
) having as spectrum a massless spin-2 field, a massive spin-2 field, and a massive pseudo-scalar field, is

S_{\mathrm{TG}}+S_{\rm matter}
, where the field part of the action reads

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{TG}}[e^{i}_{\mu},A_{ijk}]=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}_{TG}[e,\partial e,\partial^{2}e,A,\partial A]~,

(1)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{TG}

\displaystyle=c_{R}R+c_{F}F+c_{2}+c_{3}F^{ij}F_{ij}+c_{4}F^{ij}F_{ji}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{3}(c_{3}+c_{4})F^{2}+c_{6}(\epsilon\cdot F)^{2}

\displaystyle=c_{R}R+c_{F}F+c_{2}+c_{F^{2}}\left(F_{(ij)}F^{(ij)}-\frac{1}{3}F^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+c_{34}F_{[ij]}F^{[ij]}+c_{6}\left(\epsilon\cdot F\right)^{2}~.

(2)

The second form of the latter action uses
the symmetric (
F_{(ij)}
) and antisymmetric (
F_{[ij]}
) parts of
F_{ij}=F_{(ij)}+F_{[ij]}
, and the notation

\displaystyle c_{34}

\displaystyle\equiv c_{3}-c_{4}~,\hskip 14.22636ptc_{F^{2}}\equiv c_{3}+c_{4}~.

(3)

Here,
c_{R}
,
c_{F}
,
c_{2}
,
c_{3}
,
c_{4}
, and
c_{6}
are coupling constants,
R
is the usual Einsteinian Ricci scalar of the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
, while
F_{ijkl}
,
F_{ij}
, and
F
are the (frame components of the) curvature tensor, Ricci tensor (
F_{ij}=\eta^{kl}F_{kilj}
) and curvature scalar (
F=\eta^{ij}F_{ij}
) defined by the (torsionfull) connection
A_{ij\mu}
. The metric-preserving character of
the SO(3,1) connection
A_{ijk}\equiv A_{ij\mu}e^{\mu}_{k}
is encapsulated in the algebraic fact that
A_{ijk}=-A_{jik}
.
The term
\epsilon\cdot F\equiv\epsilon_{ijkl}F^{ijkl}

involves the Levi-Civita antisymmetric tensor
\epsilon_{ijkl}
.
The explicit expression of the curvature tensor
F_{ijkl}
and its corresponding avatars
F_{ij}
and
F
are,

\displaystyle F_{ijkl}=

\displaystyle e^{\mu}_{k}e^{\nu}_{l}\left(\partial_{\mu}A_{ij\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{ij\mu}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\eta^{mn}A_{im\mu}A_{nj\nu}-\eta^{mn}A_{im\nu}A_{nj\mu}\right)~,

(4)

\displaystyle F_{ij}=

\displaystyle\eta^{kl}F_{ikjl}~,\hskip 22.76228ptF=\eta^{ij}F_{ij}~.

(5)

The curvature tensor
F_{ijkl}
, like the metric Riemann tensor
R_{ijkl}
, is anti-symmetric under the exchanges
i\leftrightarrow j
and
k\leftrightarrow l
, however, contrary to
R_{ijkl}
, it is not symmetric under the exchange
ij\leftrightarrow kl
.

The torsion-gravity field equation obtained by varying the vierbein
e^{i}_{\mu}
in
S_{\mathrm{TG}}+S_{\rm matter}
reads

\displaystyle c_{R}\left(R_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{ij}R\right)+c_{F}\left(F_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{ij}F\right)-\frac{1}{2}c_{2}\eta_{ij}

\displaystyle+c_{3}(F^{k}_{i}F_{kj}+F^{kl}F_{kilj})+c_{4}(F_{i}^{k}F_{kj}+F^{lk}F_{kilj})

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{3}(c_{3}+c_{4})FF_{ij}+2c_{6}\epsilon^{klmi}F_{klmj}(\epsilon\cdot F)-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{ij}L^{(2)}=\mathcal{T}_{ij}~,

(6)

where
\mathcal{T}_{ij}
is the gravitational matter source, and

\displaystyle L^{(2)}=c_{3}F^{ij}F_{ij}+c_{4}F^{ij}F_{ji}-\frac{1}{3}(c_{3}+c_{4})F^{2}+c_{6}(\epsilon\cdot F)^{2}~.

(7)

The field equation corresponding to varying the connection
A_{ijk}
reads

\displaystyle\left[\eta_{ik}\left(D^{m}P_{jm}-\frac{2}{3}D_{j}P\right)-D_{i}P_{jk}\right]

\displaystyle-\left[\eta_{jk}\left(D^{m}P_{im}-\frac{2}{3}D_{i}P\right)-D_{j}P_{ik}\right]

\displaystyle+4c_{6}\epsilon_{ijkm}D^{m}(\epsilon\cdot F)+H_{ijk}+S_{ijk}=\mathcal{T}_{ijk}~,

(8)

where
\mathcal{T}_{ijk}
is the material torsion source, and where

\displaystyle H_{ijk}

\displaystyle=c_{F}\left(K_{ikj}-K_{jki}-K_{il}^{l}\eta_{jk}+K_{jl}^{l}\eta_{ik}\right)~,

(9)

\displaystyle S_{ijk}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{F}}H_{mnk}\left(\eta_{im}P_{jn}-\eta_{jm}P_{in}-\frac{2}{3}\eta_{im}\eta_{jn}P\right.

\displaystyle\left.+2c_{6}\epsilon_{ijmn}(\epsilon\cdot F)\right)~,

(10)

\displaystyle P_{ij}

\displaystyle=c_{3}F_{ij}+c_{4}F_{ji},\hskip 14.22636ptP\equiv\eta_{ij}P_{ij}~.

(11)

Here,
D_{i}
denotes the covariant derivative with respect to the connection
A_{ijk}
: e.g.,
D_{i}V^{j}=e_{i}^{\mu}(\partial_{\mu}V^{j}+A^{j}_{\;\,k\mu}V^{k})
;
and
K_{ijk}
is the contorsion tensor, i.e the difference between the connection
A_{ijk}
and the metric-induced (Levi-Civita) connection
\omega_{ijk}\equiv\omega_{ij\mu}e^{\mu}_{k}
:

\displaystyle K_{ijk}=A_{ijk}-\omega_{ijk}~.

(12)

The field equations (
6
) and (
8
) of torsion gravity generally depend on the six coupling parameters:
c_{F}
,
c_{R}
,
c_{2}
,
c_{3}
,
c_{4}
, and
c_{6}
.

In the present work, we follow Ref.
[
20
]
in focussing on the 3-parameter subfamily of torsion bigravity theories where

\displaystyle c_{2}

\displaystyle=0~,\hskip 17.07182ptc_{3}=c_{4}~,\hskip 8.5359pt\mathrm{and}\hskip 8.5359ptc_{6}=0~,

(13)

such that, in the notation of Eq. (
3
), we have

\displaystyle c_{34}

\displaystyle=0~,\hskip 17.07182ptc_{3}=c_{4}=\frac{1}{2}c_{F^{2}}.

(14)

These theories have only a massless spin-2 and a massive spin-2 in their spectrum.
The corresponding torsion bigravity Lagrangian takes the simple form

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{TBG}

\displaystyle=c_{R}R+c_{F}F+c_{F^{2}}\left(F_{(ij)}F^{(ij)}-\frac{1}{3}F^{2}\right)~.

(15)

The corresponding field equations simplify. In particular, Eq. (
8
) now reads

\displaystyle\left[\eta_{ik}\left(D_{m}P_{jm}-\frac{2}{3}D_{j}P\right)-D_{i}P_{jk}\right]

\displaystyle-\left[\eta_{jk}\left(D_{m}P_{im}-\frac{2}{3}D_{i}P\right)-D_{j}P_{ik}\right]

\displaystyle+H_{ijk}+S_{ijk}=\mathcal{T}_{ijk},

(16)

where
P_{ij}=c_{F^{2}}F_{(ij)}
, and
H_{ijk}
and
S_{ijk}
are as defined above (with
c_{6}=0
). Note that the latter field equation only involves the symmetric part
F_{(ij)}
of
F_{ij}
.
In this work, we focus on vacuum field equations, in absence of material sources (i.e. with
\mathcal{T}_{ij}=0
and
\mathcal{T}_{ijk}=0
).

Our restriction to a subfamily of torsion gravity theories will be of sufficient generality for our purpose because we shall show below that the subfamily (
15
), which showed healthy properties in previously considered situations, becomes unhealthy when considered at the nonlinear level.

III
Decoupling gravity from the torsion dynamics

It will be convenient here to further simplify our task by focusing on the nonlinear dynamics of the massive spin-2 excitation contained in the connection
A_{ijk}
by considering a limit where the dynamics of
A_{ijk}
decouples from the dynamics of the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
. [Here, we assume that the vierbein
e^{i}_{\mu}
has been
(locally) algebraically expressed in terms of the metric.]
To this effect let us write the metric as

g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{c_{R}}}{\hat{h}}_{\mu\nu}.

(17)

Inserting the latter parametrization of the metric in the Einsteinian part of the action density
L_{g}=\sqrt{-g}c_{R}R(g)
yields (after an integration by parts) an action density of the form

L_{g}=(\partial{\hat{h}})^{2}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{c_{R}}}{\hat{h}}(\partial{\hat{h}})^{2}+\frac{1}{c_{R}}{\hat{h}}^{2}(\partial{\hat{h}})^{2}+\cdots

(18)

Therefore, in the limit

c_{R}\rightarrow{\infty}
, the metric perturbation
{\hat{h}}_{\mu\nu}
(which also couples to the matter and to other fields proportionally to

1/\sqrt{c_{R}}
) decouples as a free massless spin-2 excitation.

As a consequence, in the limit
c_{R}\rightarrow{\infty}
(henceforth referred to as the
decoupling limit
), the dynamics of the connection
A_{ijk}
(equal in this limit to the
contorsion tensor
K_{ijk}
because
g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{c_{R}}}\right)
,
e^{i}_{\mu}=\delta^{i}_{\mu}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{c_{R}}}\right)
, and
\omega_{ijk}=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{c_{R}}}\right)
) takes place within the flat background spacetime

\eta_{\mu\nu}
(endowed with a flat vierbein field
e^{i}_{\mu}=\delta^{i}_{\mu}
with corresponding Lorentzian coordinates
x^{i}=x^{\mu}
).

Below, we study the resulting decoupled (and sourceless) field equations for
A_{ijk}
. The crucial point for our study is that we are retaining in this limit all the nonlinearities in
A_{ijk}
entailed by the action

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\rm decoupled}_{TBG}(A)=

\displaystyle c_{F}F(A)

\displaystyle+c_{F^{2}}\left(F_{(ij)}(A)F^{(ij)}(A)-\frac{1}{3}(F(A))^{2}\right)~,

(19)

where
F(A)\sim\partial A+AA
, namely

\displaystyle F_{jl}=

\displaystyle\eta^{ik}\left(\partial_{k}A_{ijl}-\partial_{l}A_{ijk}+\eta^{mn}A_{imk}A_{njl}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\eta^{mn}A_{iml}A_{njk}\right)~,

(20)

\displaystyle F=

\displaystyle\eta^{jl}F_{jl}~.

Here, all partial derivatives are with respect to
the Lorentzian coordinates
x^{i}
.

The action
\mathcal{L}^{\rm decoupled}_{TBG}(A)
is
quartic
in
A_{ijk}
. It has a formal similarity with a Yang-Mills action, though it differs from what would be the normal
Yang-Mills action for a
SO(3,1)
connection.

IV
Structure of the equations of motion of torsion bigravity in the decoupled limit

A direct derivation of the field equations from the action (
19
) would yield second-order equations for the twenty-four components of the connection
A_{ijk}=-A_{jik}
. However, following Refs.
[
17
,
18
]
, the field equations for
A_{ijk}
can be conveniently re-expressed as field equations for the symmetric part
F_{(ij)}
of the Ricci tensor of
A_{ijk}
.

Indeed, taking into account that
D\sim\partial+A
,

P_{ij}\sim c_{F^{2}}F_{(ij)}
,
H_{ijk}\sim c_{F}A_{ijk}
,
and
S_{ijk}\sim\frac{1}{c_{F}}A_{ijk}\cdot F_{(lm)}
, the torsion field equations Eq. (
16
) have the following structure (indicating only the number of indices)

c_{F^{2}}(\partial_{1}+A_{3})F_{(2)}+c_{F}A_{3}+c_{F^{2}}A_{3}F_{(2)}=0~.

(21)

Let us introduce the notation

\kappa^{2}\equiv\frac{c_{F}}{c_{F^{2}}},

(22)

to denote the squared mass of the massive spin-2 excitation. [More precisely, Eq. (
22
) defines the squared mass in the limit where
\frac{c_{F}}{c_{R}}\to 0
. When
c_{R}
is finite, we would have
\kappa^{2}=\frac{c_{F}}{c_{F^{2}}}\left(1+\frac{c_{F}}{c_{R}}\right)
.] The structure of the above field equation then reads

\partial_{1}F_{(2)}+(\kappa^{2}+F_{(2)})A_{3}=0\,.

(23)

This equation can be formally solved in terms of
A_{3}
to yield

A_{3}=-(\kappa^{2}+F_{(2)})^{-1}\partial_{1}F_{(2)}~,

(24)

where the formal inverse
(\kappa^{2}+F_{(2)})^{-1}
is perturbatively defined as an all-order expansion in powers of
\frac{F_{(2)}}{\kappa^{2}}
. We shall not study here the singularities that might appear in this inverse when considering strong fields
\frac{F_{(2)}}{\kappa^{2}}=\mathcal{O}(1)
[The occurrence of similar singularities, leading to a lower bound on the mass
\kappa
, were pointed out in Refs.
[
22
,
23
]
.].

Formally inserting the expression Eq. (
24
) for
A_{3}
in the definition

F_{(2)}\sim\partial A_{3}+A_{3}A_{3}
of
F_{(2)}
then leads to a nonlinear (Fierz-Pauli-like
[
18
]
) second-order equation for the dimensionless
symmetric tensor field

{\hat{F}}_{(ij)}\equiv\frac{{F}_{(ij)}}{\kappa^{2}}~,

(25)

having the symbolic structure

\partial\left(\frac{\partial{\hat{F}}}{1+{\hat{F}}}\right)+\left(\frac{\partial{\hat{F}}}{1+{\hat{F}}}\right)^{2}+\kappa^{2}{\hat{F}}=0~.

(26)

We have computed the explicit form of this derived field equation for
{\hat{F}}_{(ij)}
up to the quadratic order in
{\hat{F}}_{(ij)}
.
Let us display our results when using the variable
u_{ij}=u_{(ij)}
, defined as

\displaystyle u_{ij}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}}\left(F_{(ij)}-\frac{1}{6}\eta_{ij}F\right).

(27)

For brevity, we do not henceforth explicitly indicate that

u_{ij}
is a symmetric tensor. We have followed here Refs.
[
17
,
18
]
in defining
u_{ij}
so that it satisfies, at the linear level, the usual Fierz-Pauli equation. We
shall show below how to derive Eq. (
26
) from an action.

\displaystyle+\eta_{ij}\left(\partial_{k}\partial_{l}u^{kl}-\partial_{k}\partial^{k}u\right)-\kappa^{2}\left(u_{ij}-\eta_{ij}u\right).

(30)

The equation
\mathcal{U}_{ij}=0
is the usual form of the (linear) Fierz-Pauli equation
[
24
]
. Let us recall that one way to prove that this equation describes the five dof of a massive spin-2 field is to take
the divergence and the trace of
\mathcal{U}_{ij}=0
. These yield the constraints

\displaystyle\partial^{j}\mathcal{U}_{ij}

\displaystyle\equiv\kappa^{2}(\partial_{i}u-\partial^{j}u_{ij})=0~,

(31)

\displaystyle\eta^{ij}\mathcal{U}_{ij}+\frac{2}{\kappa^{2}}\partial^{ij}\mathcal{U}_{ij}

\displaystyle\equiv-3\kappa^{2}u=0~.

(32)

These
4+1
constraints (last equations on the right) involve only
u
and
\partial u
and are equivalent to the following five constraints,

\displaystyle\partial^{j}u_{ij}=0~,\hskip 22.76228ptu=0~,

(33)

which ensure the correct number (five) of dof at the linear level.

The main aim of our present study is to investigate whether this number of dof is preserved at the nonlinear level.

IV.2
Next-to-linear order

As a first attempt to investigate the healthiness of the nonlinear massive-gravity theory described by the sketchy equation Eq. (
26
) we explicitly computed the terms quadratic in
F_{(ij)}
(i.e in
u_{ij}
)
in Eq. (
26
) by following the procedure explained above.
We obtained

\displaystyle\partial_{k}\partial^{k}u_{ij}

\displaystyle+\partial_{i}\partial_{j}u-\partial_{i}\partial_{k}u^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[3.71356pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[3.71356pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.29834pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.64166pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{k}}}_{{{j}\mathchoice{\makebox[4.42017pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.42017pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.7052pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.93228pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}-\partial_{j}\partial_{k}u^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[2.82928pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[2.82928pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[1.68811pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.2058pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{k}}}_{{{i}\mathchoice{\makebox[4.42017pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.42017pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.7052pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.93228pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}+\eta_{ij}\left(\partial_{k}\partial_{l}u^{kl}-\partial_{k}\partial^{k}u\right)-\kappa^{2}\left(u_{ij}-\eta_{ij}u\right)=\mathcal{U}^{\rm quad}_{ij}(u)+\mathcal{O}(u^{3})~,

(34)

with an effective quadratically nonlinear source given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{\rm quad}_{ij}(u)

\displaystyle=\partial_{i}u_{kl}\partial_{j}u^{kl}-\partial_{i}u~\partial_{j}u+u^{kl}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}u_{kl}-u~\partial_{i}\partial_{j}u+\partial^{k}u_{ik}\partial_{j}u+\partial^{k}u_{jk}\partial_{i}u

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left(u_{jk}\partial^{k}\partial_{i}u+u_{ik}\partial^{k}\partial_{j}u\right)-\partial_{k}u\partial^{k}u_{ij}-\partial^{k}u_{ik}\partial^{l}u_{jl}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{i}u_{jk}\partial_{l}u^{kl}+\partial_{j}u_{ik}\partial_{l}u^{kl}-u^{kl}\partial_{l}\partial_{i}u_{jk}-u^{kl}\partial_{l}\partial_{j}u_{ik}\right)

\displaystyle+u\partial^{l}\partial_{i}u_{jl}+u\partial^{l}\partial_{j}u_{il}-u_{jk}\partial_{l}\partial_{i}u^{kl}-u_{ik}\partial_{l}\partial_{j}u^{kl}+u_{ij}\partial_{l}\partial_{k}u^{lk}-u\partial_{l}\partial^{l}u_{ij}+u^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[3.71356pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[3.71356pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.29834pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.64166pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{k}}}_{{{j}\mathchoice{\makebox[4.42017pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.42017pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.7052pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.93228pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}\partial_{l}\partial^{l}u_{ik}+u^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[2.82928pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[2.82928pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[1.68811pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.2058pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{k}}}_{{{i}\mathchoice{\makebox[4.42017pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.42017pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.7052pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.93228pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}\partial_{l}\partial^{l}u_{jk}-u_{ij}\partial_{l}\partial^{l}u

\displaystyle+\partial^{k}u_{jl}\partial^{l}u_{ik}+\partial^{k}u_{jl}\partial^{k}u_{il}-\frac{1}{2}\left(u_{jk}\partial^{l}\partial^{k}u_{il}+u_{ik}\partial^{l}\partial^{k}u_{jl}\right)-\frac{3}{2}\left(\partial_{j}u^{kl}\partial_{l}u_{ik}+\partial_{i}u^{kl}\partial_{l}u_{jk}\right)

\displaystyle-\eta_{ij}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial^{n}u^{kl}\partial_{n}u_{kl}+\partial_{k}u^{kl}\partial^{n}u_{nl}-\partial^{l}u\partial_{l}u-\partial_{l}u_{kn}\partial^{n}u^{kl}\right)-u\partial_{k}\partial^{k}u-u^{kl}\partial^{n}\partial_{l}u_{nk}+u\partial_{n}\partial_{l}u^{nl}+u^{kl}\partial_{n}\partial^{n}u_{kl}\right]~.

(35)

If the nonlinear massive gravity theory described
by Eq. (
34
) had the same number of dof as its linear counterpart, one would expect
Eq. (
34
) to give rise again to five quadratically deformed first-order constraints of the type

\displaystyle\partial^{j}u_{ij}=\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\rm quad}(u,\partial u)~,\hskip 22.76228ptu=\mathcal{O}^{\rm quad}(u,\partial u)~.

(36)

One would expect that such quadratically deformed first-order constraints can be revealed
by replacing
~\mathcal{U}_{ij}(u)
on the left-hand-sides of Eqs. (
31
) and (
32
) by its quadratically deformed avatar
\mathcal{U}_{ij}(u)-\mathcal{U}^{\rm quad}_{ij}(u)
. If that were the case, the quadratic quantities

\displaystyle C^{\rm quad}_{i}

\displaystyle\equiv\partial^{j}\mathcal{U}^{\rm quad}_{ij},

(37)

\displaystyle C^{\rm quad}

\displaystyle\equiv\eta^{ij}\mathcal{U}^{\rm quad}_{ij}+\frac{2}{\kappa^{2}}\partial^{ij}\mathcal{U}^{\rm quad}_{ij},

(38)

would be expected to vanish when working
on linear shell (
\mathcal{U}_{ij}(u)=0
),
and imposing the

33
).

However, we found that the so-defined quantities

C^{\rm quad}_{i},C^{\rm quad}
do not vanish when imposing Eq. (
33
), but, actually, involve up to three derivatives of
u
.

This result suggests that the (second-order) nonlinear massive gravity equations satisfied by
u_{ij}
propagate more than five dof, or exhibit some worse inconsistency.
However, this reasoning does not allow one to understand the real number of dof of the exact field equations for
u_{ij}
whose structure was
indicated in Eq. (
26
). In addition, the reasoning leading to the derivation of Eq. (
26
) did not allow us to prove that this second-order field equation for
F_{(ij)}
(or
u_{ij}
) could be derived from an action.
In the following sections, we shall therefore take a different tack (directly based on action principles).

V
Action formulations of torsion bigravity

In the decoupling limit
c_{R}\rightarrow\infty
(with the Eq. (
17
) assumption) considered here, the torsion bigravity theory is originally defined by a
second-order
action depending only on the connection
A_{3}=A_{ijk}=A_{[ij]k}
, say [see, Eq. (
19
)],

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{TBG}[A_{3}]=

\displaystyle~c_{F^{2}}\left(F_{(ij)}(A,\partial A)F^{(ij)}(A,\partial A)\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{1}{3}F^{2}(A,\partial A)\right)+c_{F}F(A,\partial A)~.

(39)

Let us now show that torsion bigravity also admits two other useful action formulations:
(1) a
first-order
Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}_{1}(b_{2},A_{3})
involving both

A_{3}
and a symmetric two-tensor
b_{2}=b_{(ij)}

(equivalent to
F_{(ij)}
or
u_{ij}
); and (2)
a
second-order
Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}_{2}(b_{2})
involving only
b_{2}=b_{(ij)}
(and leading to the second-order equation of motion given in Eq. (
26
)).

These alternative action formulations are derived from the original action Eq. (
39
) by introducing both a Lagrange multiplier
b_{ij}=b_{(ij)}
and an auxiliary
independent symmetric field
\mathcal{F}_{ij}
.

Indeed, we can construct a Lagrangian depending on the three fields
[b_{2},A_{3},\mathcal{F}_{2}]
such that
b_{ij}
acts as a Lagrange multiplier imposing the constraint
\mathcal{F}_{ij}=F_{ij}(A)
.
A Lagrangian satisfying these requirements is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{0}[b_{2},A_{3},\mathcal{F}_{2}]=

\displaystyle c_{b}\,b^{ij}\left(\mathcal{F}_{ij}-F_{ij}(A,\partial A)\right)+c_{F}\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle+c_{F^{2}}\left(\mathcal{F}_{ij}\mathcal{F}^{ij}-\frac{1}{3}\mathcal{F}^{2}\right)~,

(40)

where
c_{b}
is a constant (to be chosen below), and
\mathcal{F}\equiv\mathcal{F}_{ij}\eta^{ij}
. The variation of
\mathcal{L}_{0}
with respect to
b_{ij}
gives the constraint
\mathcal{F}_{ij}=F_{ij}(A)
. We can use this constraint to eliminate both
b_{ij}
and
\mathcal{F}_{ij}
from the Lagrangian. This then reduces
\mathcal{L}_{0}[b_{2},A_{3},\mathcal{F}_{2}]
to the original Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}_{TBG[A_{3}]}
.

On the other hand, as the action
\mathcal{L}_{0}[b_{2},A_{3},\mathcal{F}_{2}]
,
Eq. (
40
), is
quadratic in the independent auxiliary field

\mathcal{F}_{ij}
, we can instead “integrate out”
\mathcal{F}_{ij}
by solving its equation
of motion
\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}_{0}}{\delta\mathcal{F}_{ij}}=0
. The latter equation of motion reads,

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}_{0}}{\delta\mathcal{F}_{ij}}=c_{b}b_{ij}+c_{F}\eta_{ij}+2c_{F^{2}}\left(\mathcal{F}_{ij}-\frac{1}{3}\eta_{ij}\mathcal{F}\right)=0~.

(41)

It is convenient to choose

c_{b}=-c_{F},

(42)

to ensure that
b_{ij}\rightarrow\eta_{ij}
when
\mathcal{F}_{ij}\rightarrow 0
. Doing this, the field equation for
\mathcal{F}_{ij}
becomes (recalling
\mathcal{F}\equiv\eta^{ij}\mathcal{F}_{ij}
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{ij}-\frac{1}{3}\eta_{ij}\mathcal{F}=\frac{\kappa^{2}}{2}(b_{ij}-\eta_{ij})~.

(43)

Defining for notational convenience

\bar{b}_{ij}\equiv b_{ij}-\eta_{ij}~,

(44)

the above equation yields (with
\bar{b}\equiv\eta^{ij}\bar{b}_{ij}
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{ij}=\frac{\kappa^{2}}{2}\left(\bar{b}_{ij}-\eta_{ij}\bar{b}\right)~,~~~~\mathcal{F}=-\frac{3\kappa^{2}}{2}\bar{b}\,.

(45)

Inserting Eq. (
45
) in Eq. (
40
), we then obtain the following first-order action involving
b_{2}
and
A_{3}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}[b_{2},A_{3}]=c_{F}b^{ij}F_{ij}(A)-\frac{c_{F}\kappa^{2}}{4}\left(\bar{b}_{ij}\bar{b}^{ij}-\bar{b}^{2}\right)~.

(46)

This first-order action will be used below to investigate the dof of torsion bigravity in the decoupling limit.

The Lagrangian given in Eq. (
46
) is linear in
F_{2}(A)=\partial A_{3}+A_{3}A_{3}
, and therefore
quadratic
in
A_{3}
. We can therefore “integrate out”
A_{3}
from the Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}_{1}[b_{2},A_{3}]
, Eq. (
46
), by solving its equation of motion
\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}_{1}}{\delta A_{3}}=0
.

Using the explicit
expression of
F_{2}(A)=\partial A_{3}+A_{3}A_{3}
, namely

\displaystyle F_{ij}(A)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{j}A^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[5.39874pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[5.39874pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[3.24658pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[2.31898pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{l}}}_{{{il}\mathchoice{\makebox[2.56946pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[2.56946pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[1.55847pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.11319pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}-\partial^{l}A_{ilj}+A_{imj}A^{{{ml}\mathchoice{\makebox[2.56946pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[2.56946pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[1.55847pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.11319pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}_{{\mathchoice{\makebox[9.66557pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[9.66557pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[5.86073pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[4.18623pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{l}}}-A^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[2.82928pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[2.82928pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[1.68811pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.2058pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{ml}}}_{{{i}\mathchoice{\makebox[9.66557pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[9.66557pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[5.86073pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[4.18623pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}A_{mlj}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+~{i\leftrightarrow j}\right),

(47)

we find that the field equation for
A_{3}
reads

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}_{1}}{\delta A_{ijk}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}c_{F}\left[\beta_{ijk}-(b_{2}*A_{3})_{ijk}\right]=0~,

(48)

where we define the following
rank-3 tensors having the same symmetries as
A_{ijk}=A_{[ij]k}
,

\displaystyle\beta_{ijk}\equiv\partial_{i}b_{jk}-\eta_{ik}\partial^{l}b_{jl}-(i\leftrightarrow j)~,

(49)

and

\displaystyle(b_{2}*A_{3})_{ijk}\equiv

\displaystyle b_{ik}A^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[6.28302pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[6.28302pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[3.85681pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[2.75485pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{l}}}_{{{jl}\mathchoice{\makebox[2.56946pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[2.56946pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[1.55847pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.11319pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}-b_{il}A^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[8.13373pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[8.13373pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[5.00354pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[3.57394pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{l}}}_{{{jk}\mathchoice{\makebox[2.56946pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[2.56946pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[1.55847pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.11319pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}+b_{kl}A^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[2.82928pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[2.82928pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[1.68811pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.2058pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{l}\mathchoice{\makebox[3.71356pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[3.71356pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.29834pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.64166pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}_{{{i}\mathchoice{\makebox[2.56946pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[2.56946pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[1.55847pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.11319pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{j}}}+\eta_{ik}b^{lm}A_{jlm}

\displaystyle-(i\leftrightarrow j)~.

(50)

The field equation of
A_{3}
is then linear (and algebraic) in
A_{3}
and has the structure

(b_{2}*A_{3})_{ijk}=\beta_{ijk}\,,

(51)

where
\beta_{ijk}
is the (curl-like) linear
combination of the first derivatives of
b_{ij}
defined in Eq. (
49
).

The structure of the field equation, Eq. (
51
), is reminiscent of the Lyapunov
matrix equation

\displaystyle BX+XB=C~,

(52)

where
X
is an unknown matrix (playing the role of
A_{3}
) while the matrix
B
plays the role of

b_{2}
and the right-hand-side matrix
C
plays the role of
\beta_{3}\sim\partial_{1}b_{2}
. The solution of this Lyapunov toy model is easily obtained if the matrix
B
is diagonalizable.
Indeed, when working in a frame where
B_{ij}=b_{i}\delta_{ij}
, the equation for
X_{ij}

reads
(b_{i}+b_{j})X_{ij}=C_{ij}
, which is solved
as

\displaystyle X_{ij}=\frac{C_{ij}}{(b_{i}+b_{j})}~,

(53)

if there are no vanishing denominators.

We can morally think of the matrix denominators

(b_{i}+b_{j})^{-1}
as defining some new “inverse”
B^{-1}
of the matrix
B
.
In fact, if the matrix
B
is close to the
unit matrix, say
B_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+{\bar{b}}_{ij}
(which is similar to our situation if we work in the Euclidean signature), we can expand this special-frame solution
in powers of the eigenvalues
{\bar{b}}_{i}

of
{\bar{b}}_{ij}
and thereby easily rewrite
the general-frame solution
X_{ij}
in terms
of a series of terms made of various contractions of
C_{ij}
with several factors
involving
{\bar{b}}_{ij}
. Suppressing indices,
signs and factors, we have the structure

X_{2}\sim C_{2}+{\bar{b}}_{2}*C_{2}+{\bar{b}}_{2}*{\bar{b}}_{2}*C_{2}+\cdots~.

(54)

It is easily seen that the obtention of such a perturbative solution to the Lyapunov equation
Eq. (
52
) can be extended to the linear
A_{3}
equation Eq. (
51
) when
expanding its solution in powers of
{\bar{b}}_{ij}=b_{ij}-\eta_{ij}
. Namely, the solution
for
A_{3}
will have the perturbative structure
(valid for
{\bar{b}}_{ij}<O(1)
)

A_{3}\sim\beta_{3}+{\bar{b}}_{2}*\beta_{3}+{\bar{b}}_{2}*{\bar{b}}_{2}*\beta_{3}+\cdots~.

(55)

Actually, the successive terms in this expansion can be straightforwardly derived by solving the explicit form of the
A_{3}
equation, namely,

\displaystyle\beta_{ijk}=\left[b_{ik}A^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[6.28302pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[6.28302pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[3.85681pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[2.75485pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{l}}}_{{{jl}\mathchoice{\makebox[2.56946pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[2.56946pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[1.55847pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.11319pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}-b_{il}A^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[8.13373pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[8.13373pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[5.00354pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[3.57394pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{l}}}_{{{jk}\mathchoice{\makebox[2.56946pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[2.56946pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[1.55847pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.11319pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}+b_{kl}A^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[2.82928pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[2.82928pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[1.68811pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.2058pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{l}\mathchoice{\makebox[3.71356pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[3.71356pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.29834pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.64166pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}_{{{i}\mathchoice{\makebox[2.56946pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[2.56946pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[1.55847pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.11319pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{j}}}+\eta_{ik}b^{lm}A_{jlm}-(i\leftrightarrow j)\right],

(56)

in an expansion in powers of
{\bar{b}}_{ij}=b_{ij}-\eta_{ij}
.

The first term in this expansion is obtained by
replacing
b_{ij}\to\eta_{ij}
on the right-handside of Eq. (
56
). This yields

\displaystyle A^{LO}_{ijk}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\beta_{ijk}-\beta_{kij}+\beta_{jki}\right)~,

(57)

which is actually of order
A^{LO}\sim\partial{\bar{b}}
. We also explicitly computed
the next term in the expansion

A_{3}[{\bar{b}}_{2},\partial{\bar{b}}_{2}]=A^{LO}_{3}+A^{NLO}_{3}+\cdots\sim\partial{\bar{b}}_{2}+{\bar{b}}_{2}\partial{\bar{b}}_{2}+\cdots~.

(58)

We can morally think that this solution is the massive-gravity analog of the solution expressing the Levi-Civita connection in terms of the (inverse of the) metric
g_{2}
and of its first derivatives
\partial g_{2}
. We can also formally write its structure as

A_{3}[{\bar{b}}_{2},\partial{\bar{b}}_{2}]\sim(b_{2})^{-1}\beta_{3}\sim(b_{2})^{-1}\partial_{1}b_{2}~.

(59)

Integrating out
A_{3}
from the action

\mathcal{L}_{1}[b_{2},A_{3}]
, Eq. (
46
), simply means replacing

A_{3}
in
\mathcal{L}_{1}[b_{2},A_{3}]

(after an integration by parts to shuffle the derivatives from
A_{3}
to
b_{2}
) by the
solution Eq. (
59
). This yields a
second-order Lagrangian for torsion bigravity depending only on
b_{2}
, and having the structure

\frac{\mathcal{L}_{2}[b]}{c_{F}}\sim\partial b*b^{-1}*\partial b+b*b^{-1}*\partial b*b^{-1}*\partial b+\kappa^{2}{\bar{b}}^{2}\,.

(60)

The kinetic terms in this Lagrangian are defined by a power series expansion in
{\bar{b}}_{ij}
, while the mass terms are quadratic in
{\bar{b}}_{ij}
and exactly given by the last term in
Eq. (
46
). Namely,

\frac{\mathcal{L}^{\rm mass\;term}_{2}[b]}{c_{F}}=-\frac{1}{4}\kappa^{2}\left(\bar{b}_{ij}\bar{b}^{ij}-\bar{b}^{2}\right).

Note in passing that the present reasoning shows that the nonlinear equation (
26
) can indeed be derived from an action, namely Eq. (
60
).

Forgetting that it was obtained by decoupling a dynamical torsionfull connection from an associated dynamical metric, the second-order action, Eq. (
60
), considered as a Poincaré-invariant action in Minkowski spacetime for
b_{ij}
defines a
specific nonlinear massive gravity theory, which is quite different from the usually considered massive gravity theories. Indeed, the latter
[
28
,
33
]
define the kinetic term of
b_{ij}
by means of the Einstein-Hilbert action, and then look for a nonlinear deformation of the Fierz-Pauli mass term having good properties. By contrast the Einstein-Cartan origin of the dynamics of

b_{ij}=\eta_{ij}+\frac{2}{\kappa^{2}}({\mathcal{F}}_{ij}-\frac{1}{3}{\mathcal{F}}\eta_{ij})\,,

(61)

has led to the nonlinear action
\mathcal{L}_{2}[b]
, Eq. (
60
), which features a mass term of the usual quadratic Fierz-Pauli form, but with specific, non-Einsteinian kinetic terms.

We have explicitly computed the second-order action
\frac{\mathcal{L}_{2}[b]}{c_{F}}
up to cubic order in
{\bar{b}}_{ij}
, i.e.

\frac{\mathcal{L}_{2}[b]}{c_{F}}=\frac{\mathcal{L}^{LO}_{2}[b]}{c_{F}}+\frac{\mathcal{L}^{NLO}_{2}[b]}{c_{F}}+\cdots

The leading-order action (quadratic in
{\bar{b}}_{ij}
) is

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{L}^{LO}_{2}[b]}{c_{F}}=

VI
Structure of the First-order Action

In this section, we finally tackle in a precise manner the issue of the number of dof of torsion bigravity at the nonlinear level by using
the first-order Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}_{1}[b,A]
, Eq. (
46
), namely:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{L}_{1}[b_{2},A_{3}]}{c_{F}}=b^{ij}F_{ij}(A)-\frac{\kappa^{2}}{4}\left(\bar{b}_{ij}\bar{b}^{ij}-\bar{b}^{2}\right)~.

(66)

In order to reach clear conclusions, we shall focus here on the particular case where (after
having performed the field transformations leading to Eq. (
66
)) all the variables entering the action
\mathcal{L}_{1}[b_{2},A_{3}]
are homogeneous in space and only depend on time,
t=x^{0}
.

Remembering that
F[A]=\partial A+AA
, we see from Eq. (
66
), that, if we symbolically denote the variables entering the first-order action as
q=A_{3}
and
p={\bar{b}}_{ij}
we have (when all variables depend only on time, and after discarding the total-derivative term
\eta^{ij}F^{\rm lin}_{ij}[A]\sim\dot{A}
)

\frac{\mathcal{L}_{1}[p,q]}{c_{F}}=p\dot{q}-pqq-\kappa^{2}p^{2}\,.

(67)

In other words, we have a Hamiltonian action

p\dot{q}-H(p,q)
with a cubic Hamiltonian

H(p,q)=pqq+\kappa^{2}p^{2}
.

This would be a trivial problem if this action did not involve many different constraints. Our task will be to delineate the precise constrained structure of the first-order action Eq. (
66
).

In the following, we find it convenient to work with the metriclike Fierz-Pauli variable

u_{ij}
, instead of
\bar{b}_{ij}
. To avoid
confusion with the various notations used above, we shall actually henceforth denote this
Fierz-Pauli variable as
h_{ij}
. In other words,
we henceforth parametrize
b_{ij}
as

\displaystyle b_{ij}

\displaystyle\equiv\eta_{ij}+\bar{b}_{ij}

\displaystyle\equiv\eta_{ij}+h_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{ij}h^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[4.42017pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.42017pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.7052pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.93228pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{k}}}_{{{k}\mathchoice{\makebox[4.42017pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.42017pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.7052pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.93228pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}~.

(68)

Let us first consider the kinetic
p\dot{q}
component of the first-order Lagrangian
\frac{\mathcal{L}_{1}}{c_{F}}
, namely

\displaystyle p\dot{q}=b_{00}\tilde{F}_{00}-b_{0a}\tilde{F}_{0a}+b_{ab}\tilde{F}_{ab}\,,

(69)

where
\tilde{F}
denotes the
\dot{q}\sim\dot{A}
part of the Ricci tensor
F[A]=\partial A+AA
. We find (using our
(-,+,+,+)
signature)

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{00}

\displaystyle=\partial_{0}A^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[8.32378pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[8.32378pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[5.04008pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[3.60005pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{a}}}_{{{0a}\mathchoice{\makebox[4.33765pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.33765pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.59009pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.85005pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}~,

(70)

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{0a}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{0}A^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[7.91139pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[7.91139pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[4.71059pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[3.36469pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{c}}}_{{{ac}\mathchoice{\makebox[3.57375pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[3.57375pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.1205pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.51463pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}~,

(71)

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{ab}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{0}A_{a0b}+\partial_{0}A_{b0a}\right).

(72)

Here and henceforth, the indices
a,b,c
denote spatial indices,
a,b,c=1,2,3
(moved by the
3-dimensional Euclidean metric
\delta_{ab}
), in contrast to the indices
i,j,k,\cdots
used above to denote spacetime frame indices
i,j,k,\cdots=0,1,2,3
(moved by the
4-dimensional Minkowski metric
\eta_{ij}
). [Remember that in our decoupling limit, the frame indices can be identified with Lorentz-coordinate indices
\mu=i
.]

Substituting the above equations in Eq. (
69
), we obtain,

\displaystyle p\dot{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}h_{00}\left(\tilde{F}_{00}+\tilde{F}^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[4.33765pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.33765pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.59009pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.85005pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{a}}}_{{{a}\mathchoice{\makebox[4.33765pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.33765pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.59009pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.85005pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\bar{h}\left(\tilde{F}_{00}-\tilde{F}^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[4.33765pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.33765pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.59009pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.85005pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{a}}}_{{{a}\mathchoice{\makebox[4.33765pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.33765pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.59009pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.85005pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}\right)-h_{0a}\tilde{F}^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[3.98613pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[3.98613pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.45pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.75pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{a}}}_{{{0}\mathchoice{\makebox[4.33765pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.33765pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.59009pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.85005pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}

\displaystyle\quad+\tilde{F}_{ab}h^{ab}~

\displaystyle=\bar{h}\partial_{0}A^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[8.32378pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[8.32378pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[5.04008pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[3.60005pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{a}}}_{{{0a}\mathchoice{\makebox[4.33765pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.33765pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.59009pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.85005pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}-\frac{1}{2}h_{0a}\partial_{0}A^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[7.85431pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[7.85431pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[4.693pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[3.35213pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{b}}}_{{{ab}\mathchoice{\makebox[3.51666pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[3.51666pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.1029pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.50208pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}-\frac{1}{2}h_{ab}\left(\partial_{0}A_{0ab}+\partial_{0}A_{0ba}\right)~

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}h_{0a}\partial_{0}A^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[7.85431pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[7.85431pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[4.693pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[3.35213pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{b}}}_{{{ab}\mathchoice{\makebox[3.51666pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[3.51666pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.1029pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.50208pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}-h_{ab}\left(\partial_{0}A_{0(ab)}-\delta^{ab}\partial_{0}A^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[7.55988pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[7.55988pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[4.5705pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[3.26463pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{c}}}_{{{0c}\mathchoice{\makebox[3.57375pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[3.57375pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.1205pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.51463pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}\right)~,

(73)

where the bar in
\bar{h}
denotes a trace only on the space indices:
\bar{h}\equiv\delta_{ab}h^{ab}
. Here we have also used

\displaystyle\partial_{0}A_{0(ab)}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{0}A_{0ab}+\partial_{0}A_{0ba}\right)~.

(74)

The above equation, Eq. (
73
), shows that the “momentum”
p_{00}=h_{00}
has disappeared in the
p\dot{q}
kinetic terms. The
momentum
p_{00}=h_{00}
can then only appear in the
pqq\sim hAA
terms, or the
p^{2}
ones. Evidently, it can only appear linearly in the
pqq\sim hAA
terms. Concerning the
p^{2}
ones, it is well known that the (quadratic) Fierz-Pauli mass term is such that
p_{00}=h_{00}
only appears linearly. Indeed, from Eq. (
66
),

\displaystyle\kappa^{2}p^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa^{2}}{4}\left(\bar{b}_{ij}\bar{b}^{ij}-\bar{b}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa^{2}}{4}\left(h_{ij}h^{ij}-(\eta^{ij}h_{ij})^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa^{2}}{4}\left(h_{ab}h^{ab}-\bar{h}^{2}-2h_{0a}^{2}+2h_{00}\bar{h}\right)~.

(75)

Hence, we conclude that in torsion bigravity
h_{00}
is an exact Lagrange multiplier whose equation of motion yields a scalar constraint equation. This situation is quite different from the usual massive gravity theories where the lapse appears linearly in the
p\dot{q}
terms but nonlinearly in the mass term, and where the existence of a scalar constraint in ghost-free theories is rather hidden
[
30
,
34
,
35
,
36
]
.

Let us now continue our exploration of the constrained nature of the first-order action,
Eq. (
66
).
Eq. (
73
) shows that amongst a priori possible twenty-four kinetic
terms
\dot{q}=\partial_{0}A_{ijk}
, i.e.
\partial_{0}A_{0(ab)}\,,\partial_{0}A_{0[ab]}\,,\partial_{0}A_{a00}\,,\partial_{0}A_{[ab]0}\,,\partial_{0}A_{[ab]c}
, only nine combinations, namely
\partial_{0}A_{0(ab)}\,
, and
\partial_{0}A^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[7.85431pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[7.85431pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[4.693pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[3.35213pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{b}}}_{{{ab}\mathchoice{\makebox[3.51666pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[3.51666pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.1029pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.50208pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}
, enter the first-order Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}_{1}
. Let us define as follows the corresponding nine
q
variables entering
p\dot{q}
terms,

\displaystyle q_{a}

\displaystyle\equiv-A^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[7.85431pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[7.85431pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[4.693pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[3.35213pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{b}}}_{{{ab}\mathchoice{\makebox[3.51666pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[3.51666pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.1029pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.50208pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}~,

(76)

\displaystyle q_{(ab)}

\displaystyle\equiv-A_{0(ab)}+\delta_{ab}A^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[7.55988pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[7.55988pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[4.5705pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[3.26463pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{c}}}_{{{0c}\mathchoice{\makebox[3.57375pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[3.57375pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.1205pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.51463pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}~.

Besides the nine “active”
q
’s, Eq. (
76
), entering the kinetic terms, the first-order action contains fifteen remaining
q
-type variables which enter the Hamiltonian

H(p,q)
without participating in the kinetic terms. These remaining fifteen
q
-type variables happen to include two different types of variables. Six of them turn out to appear
linearly in the action (and therefore to be Lagrange multipliers), while nine of them appear quadratically in the action.

The six Lagrange-multiplier
q
variables (decorated with a superscript
L
, for Lagrange) are

\displaystyle{Q}^{L}_{a}

\displaystyle=A_{a00}~,

(77)

\displaystyle Q^{L}_{[ab]}

\displaystyle=A_{[ab]0}=\frac{1}{2}\left(A_{ab0}-A_{ba0}\right)~.

(78)

Because of the antisymmetry over
ab
in
Q^{L}_{[ab]}
there are only three different two-index Lagrange multipliers.

The remaining nine
q
-type variables entering
the action quadratically are

\displaystyle\check{Q}_{[ab]}

\displaystyle=A_{0[ab]}=\frac{1}{2}\left(A_{0ab}-A_{0ba}\right)~,

(79)

\displaystyle\check{Q}_{abc}

\displaystyle=A_{[ab]c}-\frac{1}{2}\left(A^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[8.50052pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[8.50052pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[5.14047pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[3.67175pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{d}}}_{{{ad}\mathchoice{\makebox[4.16287pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.16287pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.55038pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.8217pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}\delta_{bc}-A^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[7.67953pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[7.67953pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[4.65329pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[3.32378pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{d}}}_{{{bd}\mathchoice{\makebox[4.16287pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.16287pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.55038pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.8217pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}\delta_{ac}\right)~,

(80)

where
\check{Q}_{abc}
is anti-symmetric in
(a\leftrightarrow b)
and traceless.
The
ab
anti-symmetry of
\check{Q}_{[ab]}
means that they contain only three independent components.
The algebraic constraints on
\check{Q}_{abc}

mean that these contain only six independent components. To derive the equations of motion of
\check{Q}_{abc}
, we will express it in terms of its symmetric dual tensor
\check{Q}^{*}_{(ab)}
as

\displaystyle\check{Q}_{abc}=\epsilon_{abd}\check{Q}^{*}_{(cd)}~.

(81)

At this stage, the first-order Lagrangian can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{L}_{1}}{c_{F}}=h_{ab}\dot{q}^{ab}+\frac{1}{2}h_{0a}\dot{q}_{a}-\mathcal{H}(h_{ab},h_{00},h_{0a},q_{ab},q_{a},Q^{L},\check{Q})~,

(82)

where we recall that
\mathcal{H}=pqq+p^{2}=hAA+hh
.

As announced, this first-order
action involves seven Lagrange multipliers,
namely
h_{00}
,
{Q}^{L}_{a}
and
{Q}^{L}_{[ab]}
.
This means that the Hamiltonian part
of the action can be written as

\displaystyle-\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=h_{00}C[{\rm vars}]+{Q}^{L}_{a}C_{a}[{\rm vars}]+\frac{1}{2}{Q}^{L}_{[ab]}C_{[ab]}[{\rm vars}]

\displaystyle-\mathcal{H}^{\rm remain}[{\rm vars}]~,

(83)

where
[{\rm vars}]
denote the following (initial) list of variables

[{\rm vars}]=[h_{ab},q_{ab},h_{0a},q_{a},\check{Q}_{[ab]},\check{Q}_{abc}]\,.

(84)

Note that the seven Lagrange multipliers imply
seven corresponding (first-class) constraints, namely

\displaystyle C[{\rm vars}]=0,

(85)

\displaystyle C_{a}[{\rm vars}]=0,

\displaystyle C_{[ab]}[{\rm vars}]=0.

However, the impact of these seven constraints depend on their precise form.
In addition, the fact that the six variables
\check{Q}_{abc}
enter
only algebraically (and quadratically) in the action
means that they will also give rise to six (second-class) constraints, namely

\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}}{\partial\check{Q}^{*}_{(ab)}}=0\,.

(86)

The latter additional constraints are best taken into account after having dealt with the
seven Lagrange-multiplier constraints, Eq. (
85
).

To clarify the difference between the linear and the nonlinear dynamics of torsion bigravity, we will consider first the leading-order (linear)
dynamics.

VII
Torsion bigravity at the linear level

At the leading-order (linear equations of motion),
the Hamiltonian has the general form (
83
) with

\displaystyle C^{LO}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\kappa^{2}}{2}\bar{h}~,

(87)

\displaystyle C^{LO}_{a}

\displaystyle=2q_{a}~,

(88)

\displaystyle C^{LO}_{[ab]}

\displaystyle=4\check{Q}_{[ab]}~,

(89)

and

\displaystyle-\mathcal{H}^{\rm remain}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}q_{a}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(q^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[4.33765pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.33765pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.59009pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.85005pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{a}}}_{{{a}\mathchoice{\makebox[4.33765pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.33765pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.59009pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.85005pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}\right)^{2}-q_{ab}q^{ab}+\check{Q}_{ab}\check{Q}^{ab}

\displaystyle+\check{Q}_{abc}\check{Q}^{cba}-\frac{\kappa^{2}}{4}\left(h_{ab}h^{ab}-\bar{h}^{2}-2h_{0a}^{2}\right)~.

(90)

Let us recall that the constraint imposed by any
Lagrange multiplier term,
say
q^{L}C(q^{\prime})
has a double effect: (i) it eliminates some variable (possibly to be chosen
among several) entering the corresponding constraint equation,
C(q^{\prime})=0
, and (ii) solving the latter constraint furthermore eliminates
q^{L}
from the action.

At the linear level, the term
{Q}^{L}_{a}C^{LO}_{a}
imposes that
q_{a}=0
and also eliminates
{Q}^{L}_{a}
. Then the term
{Q}^{L}_{[ab]}C^{LO}_{[ab]}
imposes that
\check{Q}_{[ab]}=0
,
and also eliminates
{Q}^{L}_{[ab]}
. Finally, the
scalar term
h_{00}C^{LO}
sets the spatial trace
\bar{h}
of
h_{ab}
to zero, and also eliminates
h_{00}
. Those eliminations imply
also a simplification of the kinetic terms of the action.
In view of the constraint on

\bar{h}=\delta^{ab}h_{ab}
, it will be henceforth
convenient to generally decompose
h_{ab}

in trace-free part (
h_{\langle ab\rangle}\equiv h_{ab}-\frac{1}{3}h_{c}^{c}\delta_{ab}
),
and trace part, namely

h_{ab}=h_{\langle ab\rangle}+\frac{1}{3}\bar{h}\delta_{ab}\,,

(91)

and similarly, with
\bar{q}\equiv\delta^{ab}q_{ab}
,

q_{ab}=q_{\langle ab\rangle}+\frac{1}{3}\bar{q}\delta_{ab}\,.

(92)

With this notation the kinetic terms of the action become

h_{\langle ab\rangle}\dot{q}^{\langle ab\rangle}+\frac{1}{3}\bar{h}\dot{\bar{q}}+\frac{1}{2}h_{0a}\dot{q_{a}}\,.

(93)

The linear constraints
\bar{h}=0
and
q_{a}=0

then eliminate two of the kinetic terms in the action and lead to a
reduced (but not yet fully reduced) action of the form

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{L}^{\rm red}_{1}}{c_{F}}=h_{\langle ab\rangle}\dot{q}^{\langle ab\rangle}-\mathcal{H}^{\rm red}[h_{\langle ab\rangle},q_{\langle ab\rangle},\bar{q},h_{0a},\check{Q}_{abc}]~,

(94)

where

\displaystyle-\mathcal{H}^{\rm red}[h_{\langle ab\rangle},q_{\langle ab\rangle},\bar{q},h_{0a},\check{Q}_{abc}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}\bar{q}^{2}-q_{\langle ab\rangle}^{2}+\check{Q}_{abc}\check{Q}_{cba}

\displaystyle-\frac{\kappa^{2}}{4}\left(-2h_{0a}^{2}+h_{\langle ab\rangle}^{2}\right)~.

(95)

At this stage, the variables
\bar{q}
,
h_{0a}
and
\check{Q}_{abc}
enter only algebraically in the action, so that their respective equations of motion,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}^{\rm red}}{\partial\bar{q}}=0,

(96)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}^{\rm red}}{\partial h_{0a}}=0,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}^{\rm red}}{\partial\check{Q}^{*}_{ab}}=0,

yield the additional constraints

\bar{q}=0,\quad h_{0a}=0,\quad{\rm and}\quad\check{Q}^{*}_{ab}=0~.

(97)

Finally, the fully reduced linear action involves
only
h_{\langle ab\rangle}
, and
q_{\langle ab\rangle}
and reads

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{L}^{\rm full\;red}_{1}}{c_{F}}=h_{\langle ab\rangle}\dot{q}^{\langle ab\rangle}-\mathcal{H}^{\rm full\;red}[h_{\langle ab\rangle},q_{\langle ab\rangle}]~,

(98)

with

-\mathcal{H}^{\rm full\;red}[h_{\langle ab\rangle},q_{\langle ab\rangle}]=-\frac{\kappa^{2}}{4}h_{\langle ab\rangle}^{2}-q_{\langle ab\rangle}^{2}\,.

(99)

The above quadratic-in-fields Hamiltonian action
explicitly displays the presence of the five dofs
(i.e. five
q
’s and five
p
’s) of a linear
Fierz-Pauli (spatially homogeneous) massive spin-2 field, namely
h_{\langle ab\rangle}
and its
canonical conjugate
q_{\langle ab\rangle}
.

In the next section, we consider the nonlinear dynamics of torsion bigravity.

VIII
Torsion bigravity at the nonlinear level

At the nonlinear level, we still have seven first-class constraints associated with the seven Lagrange-multiplier terms
h_{00}C+{Q}^{L}_{a}C_{a}+\frac{1}{2}{Q}^{L}_{[ab]}C_{[ab]}
, but the corresponding constraints are less potent in eliminating dynamical variables than in the linear case, notably because they do not
eliminate kinetic terms. One way to see what are the remaining
number of dofs is to proceed as follows.

Having decomposed as above
h_{ab}
and
q_{ab}
in trace-free and trace parts, and using for later convenience
an integration by parts, we can rewrite the action so
that
q_{a}
appears undifferentiated,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{L}_{1}}{c_{F}}=h_{\langle ab\rangle}\dot{q}^{\langle ab\rangle}+\frac{1}{3}\bar{h}\dot{\bar{q}}-\frac{1}{2}q_{a}\dot{h}_{0a}-\mathcal{H}~.

(100)

Introducing the following new list of variables

[{\rm vars^{\prime}}]=[\bar{h},q_{a},\check{Q}_{[ab]}\;;\;\bar{q},h_{0a},h_{\langle ab\rangle},q_{\langle ab\rangle},\check{Q}_{abc}]~,

(101)

the Hamiltonian part of the action has the structure

\displaystyle-\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=h_{00}C[{\rm vars^{\prime}}]+{Q}^{L}_{a}C_{a}[{\rm vars^{\prime}}]

(102)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}{Q}^{L}_{[ab]}C_{[ab]}[{\rm vars^{\prime}}]-\mathcal{H}^{\rm remain}[{\rm vars^{\prime}}]~.

We separated the list of variables
[{\rm vars^{\prime}}]
, Eq.(
101
), in two blocks, the first block consists of the seven
variables

[\bar{h},q_{a},\check{Q}_{[ab]}]~.

This is because we have seen above that, in the linear approximation, the seven Lagrange constraints
are respectively proportional to:

C^{LO}\propto\bar{h}
,

C^{LO}_{a}\propto q_{a}
,

C^{LO}_{[ab]}\propto\check{Q}_{[ab]}
,
and thereby respectively eliminate these seven variables

[\bar{h},q_{a},\check{Q}_{[ab]}]
.
At the nonlinear level, taking into account the
(1+p)qq+p^{2}\sim(1+h)AA+h^{2}
structure of the Hamiltonian, the constraints

C_{a}
, and
C_{[ab]}
are deformed but
remain linear in
q_{a}
and
\check{Q}_{[ab]}

(with coefficients depending on
h_{\langle ab\rangle}

and
\bar{h}
). One can then solve the linear system
C_{a}=0
, and
C_{[ab]}=0
to get
q_{a}
and
\check{Q}_{[ab]}
as functions
of
\bar{h},h_{\langle ab\rangle},q_{\langle ab\rangle},h_{0a},\bar{q},\check{Q}_{abc}
. Inserting the latter
expressions in the scalar constraint

0=C=-\kappa^{2}\bar{h}+{\rm nonlinear\;terms}~,

(103)

then yields a nonlinear equation for
\bar{h}

which can, however, be perturbatively solved to express
\bar{h}
as a function of the rest
of the list of variables of Eq. (
101
), namely

\bar{q},h_{0a},h_{\langle ab\rangle},q_{\langle ab\rangle},\check{Q}_{abc}~.

(104)

The function
\bar{h}[{\rm vars^{\prime}}]
can be obtained
at any desired order as a power series in the remaining variables.

At this stage one has eliminated

q_{a}
,
\check{Q}_{[ab]}
, and
\bar{h}
,
as well as their corresponding Lagrange multipliers,

{Q}^{L}_{a}
,
{Q}^{L}_{[ab]}
and
h_{00}
.
This leads to a reduced action having the structure

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{L}_{1}^{\rm red}}{c_{F}}=h_{\langle ab\rangle}\dot{q}^{\langle ab\rangle}+\frac{1}{3}\dot{\bar{q}}\,\bar{h}^{\rm sol}[{\rm vars^{\prime\prime}};\check{Q}_{abc}]

(105)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\dot{h}_{0a}q_{a}^{\rm sol}[{\rm vars^{\prime\prime}};\check{Q}_{abc}]-\mathcal{H}^{\rm red}[{\rm vars^{\prime\prime}};\check{Q}_{abc}]~.

Here, the list of variables,
[{\rm vars^{\prime\prime}};\check{Q}_{abc}]
, entering
both the solutions obtained by solving the
Lagrange constraints,
\bar{h}^{\rm sol}[{\rm vars^{\prime\prime}};\check{Q}_{abc}]

and
q_{a}^{\rm sol}[{\rm vars^{\prime\prime}};\check{Q}_{abc}]
, as well as the nonkinetic
part of the action,
-\mathcal{H}^{\rm red}[{\rm vars^{\prime\prime}};\check{Q}_{abc}]
comprises, as
indicated, two different sets of variables.
First, the reduced list

[{\rm vars^{\prime\prime}}]=[\bar{q},h_{0a},h_{\langle ab\rangle},q_{\langle ab\rangle}]\,,

(106)

and second, the six variables
\check{Q}_{abc}
.
The reason why we separated
\check{Q}_{abc}

from the list
[{\rm vars^{\prime\prime}}]
, is that the latter variables enter as dynamical variables in the kinetic terms of the action, while
\check{Q}_{abc}
,
only appears algebraically in the action.

At this last stage we can further
eliminate the six variables
\check{Q}_{abc}

by solving their (reduced) equation
of motion, namely (using the restricted components of
\check{Q}_{abc}
to compute the derivative)

0=c_{F}^{-1}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{1}^{\rm red}}{\partial\check{Q}_{abc}}\,.

(107)

This last constraint can be perturbatively solved
for
\check{Q}_{abc}
as a power series in the
remaining dynamical variables because we have seen
above that, at the linear level,
\check{Q}_{abc}
appears quadratically in the
Hamiltonian as a simple term
\propto\check{Q}_{abc}\check{Q}_{bca}
. Therefore, at the
nonlinear level, the constraint (
107
) will be of the form

0=\check{Q}_{bca}+{\rm nonlinear\;terms}\,.

(108)

Note, however, the following crucial new feature of
the latter constraint at the nonlinear level:
In the action (
105
), the six variables

\check{Q}_{abc}
appeared not only in the reduced Hamiltonian
-\mathcal{H}^{\rm red}[{\rm vars};\check{Q}_{abc}]

but also in the kinetic terms
through the couplings

+\frac{1}{3}\dot{\bar{q}}\,\bar{h}^{\rm sol}[{\rm vars^{\prime\prime}};\check{Q}_{abc}]-\frac{1}{2}\dot{h}_{0a}q_{a}^{\rm sol}[{\rm vars^{\prime\prime}};\check{Q}_{abc}]~.

(109)

As a consequence, the solution
\check{Q}^{\rm sol}_{abc}
of the constraint Eq. (
107
) will depend not only on
the list of variables
[{\rm vars^{\prime\prime}}]
, Eq. (
106
), but also on
\dot{\bar{q}}

and
\dot{h}_{0a}
. The elimination
of
\check{Q}_{abc}
then leads to a
final reduced action having the structure

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{L}_{1}^{\rm red^{\prime}}}{c_{F}}=h_{\langle ab\rangle}\dot{q}^{\langle ab\rangle}+\frac{1}{3}\dot{\bar{q}}\,\bar{h}^{\rm sol}[{\rm vars^{\prime\prime}};\dot{\bar{q}},\dot{h}_{0a}]

(110)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\dot{h}_{0a}q_{a}^{\rm sol}[{\rm vars^{\prime\prime}};\dot{\bar{q}},\dot{h}_{0a}]-\mathcal{H}^{\rm red}[{\rm vars^{\prime\prime}};\dot{\bar{q}},\dot{h}_{0a}].

This structure makes it clear that, at the nonlinear level, the reduced torsion bigravity action involves the time derivatives
\dot{\bar{q}}
and
\dot{h}_{0a}
no longer in
a linear way (à la
p\dot{q}
) but in a
nonlinear way.

By explicitly performing the
reduction process indicated above, we have
perturbatively computed the final reduced action, Eq. (
110
), as a power series
in all the dynamical variables, starting
at the quadratic level (corresponding to the
linear dynamics), say

\frac{\mathcal{L}_{1}^{\rm red^{\prime}}}{c_{F}}=\epsilon^{2}+\epsilon^{3}+\epsilon^{4}+\cdots~,

(111)

where
\epsilon
is a counting parameter
keeping track of the order in all the variables entering the final reduced action

\epsilon\sim h_{\langle ab\rangle},q_{\langle ab\rangle},\dot{q}_{\langle ab\rangle};h_{0a},\dot{h}_{0a};\bar{q},\dot{\bar{q}}\,.

(112)

In particular, we found that at order
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{4})
, i.e. when considering terms
quartic in the fields, there arise contributions
quadratic
in
\dot{h}_{0a}

with coefficients involving
h_{\langle ab\rangle}
,
e.g. terms of the form

\sim h_{\langle ac\rangle}h_{\langle cb\rangle}\dot{h}_{0a}\dot{h}_{0b}\,.

(113)

The presence of these terms means that the three variables
\dot{h}_{0a}
which could
be eliminated at the leading-order,
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2})
, introduce
three new dofs (beyond the normal five dofs
described by the canonical variables
h_{\langle ab\rangle},q_{\langle ab\rangle}
) at the quartic-in-field
level.

At the quartic level, the variable
\bar{q}

appears only algebraically (but nonlinearly) in the reduced action. This implies that,
at this level, the
field equation of
\bar{q}
would algebraically
determine it in terms of the other variables,
so that it would not represent a new dof.

However, starting at the quintic level,

\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{5})
, there are terms in the reduced
action that couple
\dot{\bar{q}}
to the
time derivatives of
h_{0a}
, say terms
of the structure

\sim h_{\langle ac\rangle}h_{\langle cb\rangle}\dot{\bar{q}}\,\dot{h}_{0a}\dot{h}_{0b}\,.

(114)

When varying these terms with respect to
h_{0a}
we see that the second time derivative of
\bar{q}
will appear in the
equations of motion. This shows that one
now needs to give both
\bar{q}
and
\dot{\bar{q}}
as initial data. In other words,
\bar{q}
enters the dynamics as a full additional dof at the quintic level.
In addition, at the sextic level,

\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{6})
, there are terms in the reduced action that involve the square of

\dot{\bar{q}}
, say terms of the form

\sim h_{\langle ac\rangle}h_{\langle cb\rangle}(\dot{\bar{q}})^{2}\dot{h}_{0a}\dot{h}_{0b}\,.

(115)

The presence of such terms in the nonlinear action confirms that
\bar{q}
provides a
full additional dof in the nonlinear dynamics of torsion bigravity.

IX
Conclusions

We studied the nonlinear regime of torsion gravity, a three-parameter class of modified gravity theories
involving a propagating (con)torsion
K^{i}_{\;j\mu}=A^{i}_{\;j\mu}-\omega^{i}_{\;j\mu}
in addition to the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
(with associated Levi-Civita connection
\omega^{i}_{\;j\mu}
). We focussed
on the formal weak-gravity limit
16\pi G\equiv\frac{1}{c_{R}}\to 0
where
g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{c_{R}}}\hat{h}_{\mu\nu}
decouples both from matter and from the dynamics of
A^{i}_{\;j\mu}
, and defines a Minkowski background
\eta_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{ij}
. [Here, both Greek indices
\mu,\nu,...
and latin indices from the second part of the alphabet,
i,j,k,...
take four values:
0,1,2,3
. ]

We studied the Poincaré-invariant nonlinear dynamics of the contorsion field
K_{[ij]k}=A_{[ij]k}
in the decoupling limit. We showed that it could be described by several different actions, notably: (i)
a second-order action
{\mathcal{L}}_{TBG}[A_{3}]
, Eq. (
39
), involving only
A_{ijk}
;
(ii) a first-order action
{\mathcal{L}}_{1}[b_{2},A_{3}]
,
Eq. (
46
), involving both
A_{ijk}
and a (Fierz-Pauli-like) symmetric 2-tensor
b_{ij}
; and (iii) a second-order action

\mathcal{L}_{2}[b_{2}]
, Eq. (
60
), involving only the Fierz-Pauli-like tensor
b_{ij}
. The latter field is related to the Ricci tensor
{\mathcal{F}}_{ij}=F_{ij}[A_{3}]
of the torsionfull connection
A_{ijk}
via

b_{ij}=\eta_{ij}+\frac{2}{\kappa^{2}}({\mathcal{F}}_{ij}-\frac{1}{3}{\mathcal{F}}\eta_{ij})\,.

(116)

We also parametrize
b_{ij}
by a metric-perturbation-like tensor
h_{ij}
defined as

\displaystyle b_{ij}

\displaystyle\equiv\eta_{ij}+h_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{ij}h^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[4.42017pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.42017pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.7052pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.93228pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{k}}}_{{{k}\mathchoice{\makebox[4.42017pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[4.42017pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.7052pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.93228pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}~.

(117)

The second-order action
\mathcal{L}_{2}[b_{2}]
defines a massive-gravity theory which differs from the usually considered ones in having a quadratic Fierz-Pauli mass term, but a non-Einsteinian kinetic term. We found convenient to study the nonlinear dynamics of the massive gravity theory
\mathcal{L}_{2}[b_{2}]
by means of the first-order action
{\mathcal{L}}_{1}[b_{2},A_{3}]
,
Eq. (
46
).

We focussed on
spatially homogeneous
solutions of the latter torsion-inspired massive gravity theory. Our final result is that the dynamics defined by
\mathcal{L}_{2}[b_{2}]
involves
(in the spatially homogeneous case)
nine
dofs (instead of the five dofs of a
normal, Fierz-Pauli massive spin-2 field).
Our analysis used the first-order action
{\mathcal{L}}_{1}[b_{2},A_{3}]
to display and solve the constraints implied by this action;
see Sec.
VIII
.

We define (with spatial indices
a,b,c,...=1,2,3
)
the torsion-related variable

\displaystyle q_{(ab)}=-A_{0(ab)}+\delta_{ab}A^{{\mathchoice{\makebox[7.55988pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[7.55988pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[4.5705pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[3.26463pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}{c}}}_{{{0c}\mathchoice{\makebox[3.57375pt][c]{$\displaystyle$}}{\makebox[3.57375pt][c]{$\textstyle$}}{\makebox[2.1205pt][c]{$\scriptstyle$}}{\makebox[1.51463pt][c]{$\scriptscriptstyle$}}}}\,,

(118)

and its spatial trace
\bar{q}=\delta_{ab}q_{(ab)}
, together with its trace-free part

q_{\langle ab\rangle}\equiv q_{(ab)}-\frac{1}{3}\bar{q}\delta_{ab}
. Similarly,
h_{\langle ab\rangle}\equiv h_{ab}-\frac{1}{3}\bar{h}\delta_{ab}
, with

\bar{h}=\delta_{ab}h_{ab}
.

The final reduced form of the action, after Legendre
transforming the kinetic term

h_{\langle ab\rangle}\dot{q}_{\langle ab\rangle}
(which is the only occurrence of
\dot{q}_{\langle ab\rangle}

in the reduced action)
can be described by a
second-order Lagrangian

with the structure

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\left(h_{\langle ab\rangle},\dot{h}_{\langle ab\rangle};h_{0a},\dot{h}_{0a};\bar{q},\dot{\bar{q}}\right)~,

(119)

where all the time-derivative terms appear nonlinearly, though at different orders in nonlinearity:
\dot{h}_{\langle ab\rangle}
enters at
the leading (Fierz-Pauli), quadratic order

\frac{\mathcal{L}^{LO}}{c_{F}}=\frac{1}{4}\left((\dot{h}_{\langle ab\rangle})^{2}-\kappa^{2}(h_{\langle ab\rangle})^{2}\right),

(120)

while
\dot{h}_{0a}
enters bilinearly at the quartic order, Eq. (
113
), and
\dot{\bar{q}}
enters bilinearly at the sextic order, Eq. (
115
).

The general solution of the
corresponding second-order equations of motion then requires that one
gives initial data for the eighteen variables

h_{\langle ab\rangle},\dot{h}_{\langle ab\rangle};h_{0a},\dot{h}_{0a};\bar{q},\dot{\bar{q}}\,,

(121)

corresponding to the nine dofs
h_{\langle ab\rangle},h_{0a},\bar{q}
.

It is possible that the appearance of the four new dofs (here parametrized by
h_{0a}
and
\bar{q}
) would arise in a different way,
and at lower perturbative orders, when considering generic,
inhomogeneous solutions of torsion bigravity
(notably via the presence of spatial gradients in the constraints). However, on the one hand,
our simplifying homogeneity ansätz is consistent

, and, on the other hand, it suffices to conclude that

Analysing Temporal Reasoning in Description Logics Using Formal Grammars
Camille Bourgaux, Anton Gnatenko, Michaël Thomazo

Abstract
We establish a correspondence between (fragments of) 𝒯 ℰ ℒ ○ \mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}^{{}^{\,\scalebox{0.5}{\smash{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{$\bigcirc$}}}}} , a temporal extension of the ℰ ​ ℒ \mathcal{E}\mathcal{L} description logic with the LTL operator ○ k {\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{\scriptsize{$\bigcirc$}}}}^{k} , and some specific kinds of formal grammars, in particular, conjunctive grammars (context-free grammars equipped with the operation of intersection). This connection implies that 𝒯 ℰ ℒ ○ \mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}^{{}^{\,\scalebox{0.5}{\smash{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{$\bigcirc$}}}}} does not possess the property of ultimate periodicity of models, and further leads to undecidability of query answering in 𝒯 ℰ ℒ ○ \mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}^{{}^{\,\scalebox{0.5}{\smash{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{$\bigcirc$}}}}} , closing a question left open since the introduction of 𝒯 ℰ ℒ ○ \mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}^{{}^{\,\scalebox{0.5}{\smash{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{$\bigcirc$}}}}} . Moreover, it also allows to establish decidability of query answering for some new interesting fragments of 𝒯 ℰ ℒ ○ \mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}^{{}^{\,\scalebox{0.5}{\smash{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{$\bigcirc$}}}}} , and to reuse for this purpose existing tools and algorithms for conjunctive grammars.

Introduction

Ontology-mediated query answering
(OMQA) aims at improving data access by enriching data with an ontology that expresses domain knowledge
Poggi et al. 2008
;
Kontchakov and Zakharyaschev 2014
;
Bienvenu and Ortiz 2015
. In this setting, an ontology is a set of logical formulas, typically expressed in a given description logic (DL)
Baader et al. 2003
or via extensions of Datalog
Abiteboul et al. 1995
;
Baget et al. 2011
;
Calì et al. 2012
. It provides a formalized vocabulary that allows a user to formulate queries in familiar terms, and to obtain more complete answers to queries, as answers are based not only on facts explicitly stored in the data (or ABox, in DL parlance) but also on facts that can be deduced through logical reasoning using the ontology (TBox).
In the large literature on OMQA, special attention has been devoted to the so-called lightweight description logics, such as the DL-Lite family
Artale et al. 2009
or the
\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}
family
Baader et al. 2005
;
Baader et al. 2008
, which allow for tractable reasoning and underpin the OWL 2 QL and OWL 2 EL profiles of the Semantic Web standard ontology language
Motik et al. 2012
. In particular, many large real-world ontologies, including the bio-medical ontology SNOMED CT, use languages from the
\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}
family.

As many real-world applications require to query temporal data, various extensions of the OMQA framework have been proposed to integrate temporal modelling
Artale et al. 2017
. In this paper, we consider (fragments of)
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}
, a temporal extension of the DL language
\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}
introduced by
Gutiérrez-Basulto et al. 2016
. In
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}
, the ABox facts are associated with
timestamps
, and the TBox concept inclusions may feature some operators from
linear temporal logic
(
L​TL
):
\bigcirc
(next),
{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{\scriptsize{$\bigcirc$}}}}^{-}
(previous),
\Diamond
(eventually) and
\raisebox{0.43057pt}{\text{\small{$\Diamond$}}}^{-}
(eventually in the past). For instance, the concept inclusion
\mathsf{Prof}\sqsubseteq{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{\scriptsize{$\bigcirc$}}}}\mathsf{Prof}
intuitively means that at any moment, someone that is a professor is also a professor at the next instant. Moreover,
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}
allows the user to specify that some roles (binary predicates) are
rigid
, i.e., that the relations they model do not change over time.

Integrating temporal reasoning in OMQA gave rise to a flourishing literature, with a large body of work on the theoretical side as well as some implementations
Kalayci et al. 2019
;
Wang et al. 2022
;
Thost et al. 2015
, in a wide variety of settings. For example, an alternative way to model fact temporal validity is to use time intervals instead of timestamps
Gutiérrez-Basulto and
Klarman 2012
;
Brandt et al. 2018
;
Baader et al. 2020
.
On the ontology-mediated query side, it is also possible to use a standard, atemporal TBox and temporal queries, built from conjunctive queries and
L​TL
operators
Borgwardt and Thost 2015
;
Borgwardt et al. 2015
.
Finally,
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}
stems from a line of research which studies combinations of various DL languages and
L​TL
operators
Artale et al. 2007
;
Artale et al. 2013
;
Artale et al. 2022
.
For a more in-depth discussion of temporal reasoning within OMQA, we refer the reader to the survey by
Artale et al. 2017
.

Despite both
L​TL
and
\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}
being decidable,
Gutiérrez-Basulto et al. 2016
showed that their combination in
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}
quickly leads to undecidability. However, they exhibited fragments of
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}
for which atomic query answering is decidable. Most of such fragments restrict the expressiveness of the temporal modelling, by only allowing
operators
\bigcirc
and
\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{\scriptsize{$\bigcirc$}}}^{-}
, giving rise to the
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}^{{}^{\,\scalebox{0.5}{\smash{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{$\bigcirc$}}}}}
language.
Gutiérrez-Basulto et al. 2016
left open the question of whether that restriction by itself is enough to regain decidability and proposed additional syntactic constraints, based on some form of acyclicity (either on the description logics side, or on the temporal side). All these constraints enforce a crucial property: the existence of models that are
ultimately periodic
. In a nutshell, a model is ultimately periodic if the evolution over time of any given element is, after some initial segment, periodic.

Our main contribution is to link temporal reasoning with
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}^{{}^{\,\scalebox{0.5}{\smash{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{$\bigcirc$}}}}}
-TBoxes to the study of associated formal languages, which allows us to close the open question of
Gutiérrez-Basulto et al. 2016
and obtain additional results. We first consider the case of
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}^{{}^{\,\scalebox{0.5}{\smash{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{$\bigcirc$}}}}}_{\textit{future}}
, where concept inclusions only allow to derive novel information about the future (or present) and not the past. For this fragment, we show in Section
3
that the task of deciding whether a concept inclusion of the form
A\sqsubseteq{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{\scriptsize{$\bigcirc$}}}}^{n}B
is entailed by a TBox can be reduced to deciding whether
n
belongs to the Parikh image
Parikh 1966
of a unary conjunctive language, and vice-versa. Such languages are defined by
conjunctive grammars
, introduced by
Okhotin 2001
as a generalization of context-free grammars which allows for a conjunction operation in rules. We then turn our attention to
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}^{{}^{\,\scalebox{0.5}{\smash{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{$\bigcirc$}}}}}_{\textit{lin}}
, a temporal extension of linear
\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}

Dimartino et al. 2016
, and obtain in Section
4
a similar reduction from reasoning in
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}^{{}^{\,\scalebox{0.5}{\smash{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{$\bigcirc$}}}}}_{\textit{lin}}
to deciding whether the Parikh image of a context-free grammar over a binary alphabet fulfils some property.
In Section
5
, we exploit the previous correspondences to obtain results for
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}^{{}^{\,\scalebox{0.5}{\smash{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{$\bigcirc$}}}}}
and fragments thereof. First, we close negatively the question of whether all
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}^{{}^{\,\scalebox{0.5}{\smash{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{$\bigcirc$}}}}}
-TBoxes enjoy ultimate periodicity,
and complete the complexity picture of
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}
given by
Gutiérrez-Basulto et al. 2016
: atomic query answering in
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}^{{}^{\,\scalebox{0.5}{\smash{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{$\bigcirc$}}}}}
is undecidable. Second, we provide results for the new fragments
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}^{{}^{\,\scalebox{0.5}{\smash{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{$\bigcirc$}}}}}_{\textit{future}}
and
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}^{{}^{\,\scalebox{0.5}{\smash{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{$\bigcirc$}}}}}_{\textit{lin}}
we introduce. As for
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}^{{}^{\,\scalebox{0.5}{\smash{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{$\bigcirc$}}}}}_{\textit{future}}
, we prove that atomic query answering is solvable in polynomial time (in combined and data complexity), but becomes undecidable if
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}^{{}^{\,\scalebox{0.5}{\smash{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{$\bigcirc$}}}}}_{\textit{future}}
is extended with rigid concept names or the universal concept
\top
. Regarding
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}^{{}^{\,\scalebox{0.5}{\smash{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{$\bigcirc$}}}}}_{\textit{lin}}
, we prove that
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}^{{}^{\,\scalebox{0.5}{\smash{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{$\bigcirc$}}}}}_{\textit{lin}}
-TBoxes enjoy ultimate periodicity, and derive complexity results for atomic query answering. Detailed proofs are provided in the appendix.

Intuitively, the semantics of conjunctive grammars extends that of context-free grammars with

Parikh images and expressiveness

Given an alphabet
\Sigma=\{c_{1},\dots,c_{n}\}
enumerated in a fixed order, let
\#c_{i}(w)
denote the number of occurrences of
c_{i}
in
w\in\Sigma^{*}
. The
Parikh image

p(w)
of a word
w
is a vector
\vec{u}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
such that
u_{i}=\#c_{i}(w)
, for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
. The Parikh image
p(L)
of a language
L
is the set
\{p(w)\mid w\in L\}
. It is easy to see that when
L
is regular,
p(L)
is semilinear. A deeper result is the following.

Theorem 7

(
Parikh 1966
)
.

If
L
is context-free, then
p(L)
is semilinear. Moreover, for any semilinear
S\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{n}
there exists a regular language
L^{\prime}
such that
S=p(L^{\prime})
.

A unary language
L
can be seen as a set of numbers given in unary that coincides with its Parikh image:
c^{n}\in L
if and only if
n\in p(L)
.
Theorem
7
implies that a unary language is regular iff its Parikh image is semilinear, and thus unary context-free languages are regular. However, unary conjunctive languages may not be regular.

6
Discussion

Connections between temporal logics, such as
L​TL
, and formal languages (in particular, regular languages) are well-known
(
Vardi 1996
;
Demri et al. 2016
)
. This paper makes new ones, between the fragments
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}^{{}^{\,\scalebox{0.5}{\smash{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{$\bigcirc$}}}}}_{\textit{lin}}
and
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}^{{}^{\,\scalebox{0.5}{\smash{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{$\bigcirc$}}}}}_{\textit{future}}
of the temporal description logic
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}^{{}^{\,\scalebox{0.5}{\smash{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{$\bigcirc$}}}}}
, on the one side, and context-free and unary conjunctive languages, on the other. Using these connections, we obtain several important results on
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}^{{}^{\,\scalebox{0.5}{\smash{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{$\bigcirc$}}}}}
, both negative and positive, from the formal language theory.

In particular,
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}^{{}^{\,\scalebox{0.5}{\smash{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{$\bigcirc$}}}}}_{\textit{future}}
-TBoxes are in a one-to-one correspondence with unary conjunctive grammars (Theorems
10
and
14
).
Therefore,
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}^{{}^{\,\scalebox{0.5}{\smash{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{$\bigcirc$}}}}}_{\textit{future}}
is not ultimately periodic (Theorem
25
), which is arguably unexpected, as its temporal component,
L​TL
, is ultimately periodic
(
Sistla and Clarke 1985
)
, and its DL component,
\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}
, is such that every KB possesses a canonical model which has, informally speaking, a regular structure
Kontchakov and Zakharyaschev 2014
. Moreover, TAQA with
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}^{{}^{\,\scalebox{0.5}{\smash{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{$\bigcirc$}}}}}
-TBoxes is undecidable (Theorem
26
). On the other hand, the same correspondence allows us to use parsing algorithms for conjunctive grammars as tools for TAQA with
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}^{{}^{\,\scalebox{0.5}{\smash{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{$\bigcirc$}}}}}_{\textit{future}}
-TBoxes, leading to a drastic decrease of complexity, from undecidability to polynomial time, owed to a mere removal of the temporal operator
{\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\text{\scriptsize{$\bigcirc$}}}}^{-}
(previous).
Despite the partial undecidability result of Theorem
25
, it remains open if ultimate periodicity of
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}^{{}^{\,\scalebox{0.5}{\smash{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{$\bigcirc$}}}}}
-TBoxes is decidable, since for the corresponding problem—given a unary conjunctive grammar tell if all its nonterminals generate regular languages—no result is known.

The linear fragment,
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}^{{}^{\,\scalebox{0.5}{\smash{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{$\bigcirc$}}}}}_{\textit{lin}}
, is connected to context-free grammars (Theorem
19
). As a result, it is ultimately periodic, and enjoys considerably low data complexity of query answering (Theorem
28
).

Language theorists may find interesting that every unary conjunctive grammar is transformable, in polynomial time, to the “normal form” of Definition
12
, by applying first Theorem
14
, then Theorem
10
.
Moreover, the grammars that correspond to
“pure
L​TL
” TBoxes (i.e., do not have any rule of form (
12
) in this normal form) are guaranteed to generate regular languages. This is, to the best of our knowledge, the first nontrivial sufficient condition for this property.

On the more theoretical side, it is possible that the TBox-grammar correspondence can be lifted to more expressive temporal description logics and more general classes of formal grammars (e.g. Boolean grammars
(
Okhotin 2013
)
).
On the applications side, we hope to employ this correspondence to develop a practical reasoner for
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{L}^{{}^{\,\scalebox{0.5}{\smash{\raisebox{-3.0pt}{$\bigcirc$}}}}}_{\textit{future}}
.

Many thanks to Roman Kontchakov for introducing us to the problem of ultimate periodicity from the perspective of semilinear sets. We would also like to thank Alexander Okhotin for providing us with the code of
Whale Calf
and valuable suggestions on the implementation.

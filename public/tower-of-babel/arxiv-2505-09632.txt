Explicit Identities and new results for Infinite Series associated with the Ratio of Central Binomial Coefficients
Yao Mawugna Dzokotoe

Abstract
We investigate some classes of infinite series involving central binomial coefficients, particularly focusing on those arising from ratios such as ( 2 ​ n n ) ​ ( 4 ​ n 2 ​ n ) − 1 \binom{2n}{n}\binom{4n}{2n}^{-1} , ( 4 ​ n 2 ​ n ) ​ ( 2 ​ n n ) − 1 \binom{4n}{2n}\binom{2n}{n}^{-1} and related expressions. We derive several new explicit identities and closed-form evaluations, building on and refining previous results by Bhandari (2022) and Adegoke et al. (2022).



Keywords
: Infinite Series, Central Binomial Coefficients, Generating Functions, Pochhammer Symbol.

: 11B65, 05A10, 33B10, 40A05.

Introduction

Infinite series involving central binomial coefficients have long captured the interest of mathematicians due to their rich combinatorial structure and deep connections to number theory, special functions, and analysis. The central binomial coefficient, given by

\binom{2n}{n}=\frac{(2n)!}{(n!)^{2}}.

(1)

for
n\geq 0
, appears prominently in numerous areas, including combinatorics, number theory and mathematical analysis, you can see
[
10
,
12
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
. They are considered as special sequences and are indexed as sequence
A000984
in the On-Line Encylopedia of Integer Sequences
[
17
]
. Their generating function is given by:

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}x^{n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-4x}},\qquad\text{provided}\ |x|<\frac{1}{4}.

The work of Lehmer
[
12
]
is an important reference in the subject of infinite series of central binomial coefficients. The current literature is extensive where series involving these numbers and other special sequences such as Harmonic numbers, Fibonacci numbers are studied. For example Boyadzhiev
[
7
, See pp. 2]
obtained the following beautiful result

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{4^{n}}H_{n}=\sqrt{2}\log\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{1+\sqrt{2}}.

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}\frac{H_{n}}{8^{n}}=2\sqrt{2}\log\frac{1+\sqrt{2}}{2}.

After Boyadzhiev, Chen
[
9
]
obtained the following identities

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}\frac{h_{n}F_{n}}{8^{n}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{10}}\ln 2+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\ln\left(\frac{3+\sqrt{5}}{2}\right).

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}\frac{n^{2}h_{n}}{8^{n}}=\frac{3}{2}\sqrt{2}+\frac{5\sqrt{2}}{3}\ln 2.

where,

h_{n}=1+\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{5}+\cdots+\frac{1}{2n-1}

and
F_{n}
denotes the
n-th
Fibonacci number satisfying the recurrence relation
F_{n}=F_{n-1}+F_{n-2}
,
n\geq 2
with conditions
F_{0}=0,F_{1}=1
.

Our motivation to write this paper started with a 2022 paper
[
6
]
by Bhandari. The author used creative techniques based on building ordinary generating functions of central binomial coefficients and making use of Walli’s integral formulas to derive interesting series for
\binom{2n}{n}\binom{4n}{2n}
and
\binom{2n}{n}\binom{4n}{2n}^{-1}
. As examples he obtained for
\binom{2n}{n}\binom{4n}{2n}
:

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n+1)64^{n}}\binom{2n}{n}\binom{4n}{2n}=\frac{8\sqrt{2}}{3\pi}.

and for
\binom{2n}{n}\binom{4n}{2n}^{-1}
he obtained among others, these two beautiful identities:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{4^{n}}{(2n-1)^{2}}\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{\binom{4n}{2n}}=4(\sqrt{2}-1).

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n4^{n}}{(2n-1)^{2}(4n+1)}\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{\binom{4n}{2n}}=\frac{5\sqrt{2}-4}{9}.

Main theorems in
[
6
]
involving
\binom{2n}{n}\binom{4n}{2n}^{-1}
are expressed in terms of finite binomial sums of two families of integrals, namely:

\mathcal{B}(k)=\int_{0}^{1/2}\frac{t^{k}}{\sqrt{1-t}}\,\mathrm{d}t,\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi(2k+1)=\int_{0}^{1}t^{2k+1}\sqrt{1+t^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t

for
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

The determination of general closed form formulas for the integrals were left as open problems. The attempt to answer this question is the stating point of this work and we obtain:

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(k)=\sum_{p=0}^{k}\binom{k}{p}\frac{(-1)^{p}}{2p+1}\left(2-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2^{p}}\right).

\displaystyle\varphi(2k+1)=\sum_{p=0}^{k}\binom{k}{p}(-1)^{k-p}\frac{\sqrt{2}\cdot 2^{p+1}-1}{2p+3}.

Recently, we came across the paper of Adegoke et al.
[
1
]
which became the second source of motivation for writing this paper. In section 7 of
[
1
]
the authors gave two theorems on series involving
\binom{4n}{2n}\binom{2n}{n}^{-1}
. Once again these two theorems are left as far of two integrals,
\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\sin^{2r}x\sin\frac{x}{2}\,\mathrm{d}x
and
\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\sin^{2r-1}x\cos\frac{x}{2}\,\mathrm{d}x
. We investigated their closed forms and we obtained that:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\sin^{2r}x\sin\frac{x}{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{16^{r}}{(4r+1)\binom{4r}{2r}}\left(2+\sqrt{2}\sum_{k=0}^{r}\frac{\binom{4k}{2k}}{(4k-1)16^{k}}\right).

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\sin^{2r-1}x\cos\frac{x}{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{6\cdot 16^{r-1}}{r\binom{4r}{2r}}\left(\frac{4}{3}-\sqrt{2}\sum_{k=0}^{r-1}\frac{\binom{4k}{2k}}{(6k+3)16^{k}}\right).

It’s worth noting that infinite series involving ratio of central binomial coefficients are rare in the existing literature and it would be handy to fill this gap. This work contributes to this endeavor by reviewing explicitly, theorems on
\binom{2n}{n}\binom{4n}{2n}^{-1}
from
[
6
]
and
\binom{4n}{2n}\binom{2n}{n}^{-1}
from
[
1
]
based on the aforementioned integrals evaluations.

Besides this we derive new interesting theorems from which we choose the following ones as showcase:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{4^{n}n(2n+3)}\frac{\binom{4n-2}{2n-1}}{\binom{2n+2}{n+1}}=\frac{27\sqrt{2}-26}{420}.

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{2}}{(2n+3)(2n-1)^{2}(2n+1)}\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{\binom{2n+2}{n+1}}=\frac{16+5\pi^{2}}{1024}.

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2n+5)(n+1)(2n+1)(2n+3)}\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{\binom{2n+4}{n+2}}=\frac{5\pi^{2}}{1024}-\frac{137}{2880}.

Throughout this paper, we verify our results using Computer Algebra System (CAS) software Mathematica 13.3.

(2)

(3)

Next we multiply (

∎

Lemma 2.0.3
.

For all
x\in[0,4)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{n+3/2}}{n(n+3/2)\binom{2n}{n}}=\frac{4}{9}\sqrt{4-x}\left(\sqrt{\frac{x}{4-x}}(x+24)-3(x+8)\arctan\sqrt{\frac{x}{4-x}}\right).

Proof.

Observe that,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{n}}{n\binom{2n}{n}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}x^{n}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}2\sin^{2n-1}\theta\cos^{2n-1}\theta\,\mathrm{d}\theta=\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{1}{\sin\theta\cos\theta}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(x\sin^{2}\theta\cos^{2}\theta)^{n}\,\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{x\sin\theta\cos\theta}{1-x\sin^{2}\theta\cos^{2}\theta}\,\mathrm{d}\theta=\frac{x}{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{\sin 2\theta}{1-\frac{x}{4}\sin 2\theta}\,\mathrm{d}\theta.

by the change of variable
p=\cos 2\theta

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{n}}{n\binom{2n}{n}}=2\sqrt{\frac{x}{4-x}}\arctan\sqrt{\frac{x}{4-x}}

From the above identity, multiply both sides by

Lemma 2.0.4
.

For all
x\in[-1,1]
and
x\neq 0
,

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}\frac{x^{2n+2}}{2^{2n+1}(n+1)(2n+3)}=1-\frac{x^{2}}{6}-\frac{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}{2}-\frac{\arcsin x}{2x}.

Proof.

It can be shown that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}\frac{x^{2n}}{4^{n}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}-1

Thus, multiply both sides of the above identity by

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}\frac{x^{2n+3}}{(2n+3)4^{n}}=\frac{1}{6}(3\arcsin x-3x\sqrt{1-x^{2}}-2x^{3})+C

Observe, as
x\to 0

C\to 0
. Hence

. By dividing through by

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}\frac{x^{2n+2}}{2^{2n+1}(n+1)(2n+3)}=-\frac{x^{2}}{6}-\frac{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}{2}-\frac{\arcsin x}{2x}+C_{1}

Similarly as
x\to 0

C_{1}\to 1
. Hence, the result follows directly.
∎

Proposition 3.0.1
.

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,

(\mathcal{B}(k))=\int\frac{t^{k}}{\sqrt{1-t}}\,\mathrm{d}t=-\sum_{p=0}^{k}\binom{k}{p}\frac{(-1)^{p}}{p+\frac{1}{2}}(1-t)^{p+\frac{1}{2}}

Proof.

Set
x=1-t
.
So,

\left(\mathcal{B}(k)\right)=-\int\frac{(1-x)^{k}}{\sqrt{x}}\,\mathrm{d}x

By Binomial expansion, we have
(1-x)^{k}=\sum_{p=0}^{k}\binom{k}{p}(-1)^{p}x^{p}
. So;

\frac{(1-x)^{k}}{\sqrt{x}}=\sum_{p=0}^{k}\binom{k}{p}(-1)^{p}x^{p-\frac{1}{2}}

Which implies that,

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{B}(k)\right)

\displaystyle=-\int\sum_{p=0}^{k}\binom{k}{p}(-1)^{p}x^{p-\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x=-\sum_{p=0}^{k}\binom{k}{p}(-1)^{p}\int x^{p-\frac{1}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\sum_{p=0}^{k}\binom{k}{p}\frac{(-1)^{p}}{p+\frac{1}{2}}x^{p+\frac{1}{2}}

with
x=1-t
, we have:

\left(\mathcal{B}(k)\right)=-\sum_{p=0}^{k}\binom{k}{p}\frac{(-1)^{p}}{p+\frac{1}{2}}(1-t)^{p+\frac{1}{2}}

∎

Theorem 3.0.1
.

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,

\mathcal{B}(k)=\int_{0}^{1/2}\frac{t^{k}}{\sqrt{1-t}}\,\mathrm{d}t=\sum_{p=0}^{k}\binom{k}{p}\frac{(-1)^{p}}{2p+1}\left(2-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2^{p}}\right)

(13)

Proof.

We get using Proposition 2.0.1,

\left(\mathcal{B}(k)\right)=-\sum_{p=0}^{k}\binom{k}{p}\frac{(-1)^{p}}{p+\frac{1}{2}}(1-t)^{p+\frac{1}{2}}

Hence,

\mathcal{B}(k)=\left(\mathcal{B}(k)\right)\bigg|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{0}=-\sum_{p=0}^{k}\binom{k}{p}\frac{(-1)^{p}}{p+\frac{1}{2}}\left(\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{p+\frac{1}{2}}-1\right)

and finally, we get,

\mathcal{B}(k)=\sum_{p=0}^{k}\binom{k}{p}\frac{(-1)^{p}}{2p+1}\left(2-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2^{p}}\right)

∎

Remark 3.0.1
.

While working on the integral

\nu_{1}(k)

which suggested to him that

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\frac{\nu_{1}(k)}{\nu_{2}(k)}=\sqrt{2}.

(14)

This observation was later confirmed with the evaluation of

\displaystyle\nu_{1}(k):=2\sum_{p=0}^{k}\binom{k}{p}\frac{(-1)^{p}}{2p+1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\nu_{2}(k):=\sum_{p=0}^{k}\binom{k}{p}\frac{(-1/2)^{p}}{2p+1}.

it is easy to prove that

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\frac{\nu_{1}(k)}{\nu_{2}(k)}=\sqrt{2}.

Proposition 3.0.2
.

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,

(\varphi(2k+1))=\int t^{2k+1}\sqrt{1+t^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t=\sum_{p=0}^{k}\binom{k}{p}(-1)^{k-p}\frac{\sqrt{1+t^{2}}(1+t^{2})^{p+1}}{2p+3}

Proof.

Substitute
t=\tan(x)

Then,

\left(\varphi(2k+1)\right)=\int\tan^{2k+1}(x)\sec^{3}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

We can express
\tan^{2k+1}(x)
in terms of
\sec(x)
as follows;

\tan^{2k}(x)=\left(\sec^{2}(x)-1\right)^{k}=\sum_{p=0}^{k}\binom{k}{p}(-1)^{k-p}\sec^{2p}(x)

(15)

Using (
15
), we have that;

\tan^{2k+1}(x)\sec^{3}(x)=\sum_{p=0}^{k}\binom{k}{p}(-1)^{k-p}\sec^{\prime}(x)\sec^{2p+2}(x)

where
\sec^{\prime}(x)=\frac{\sin(x)}{\cos^{2}(x)}
which implies

\displaystyle\left(\varphi(2k+1)\right)

\displaystyle=\int\tan^{2k+1}(x)\sec^{3}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int\sum_{p=0}^{k}\binom{k}{p}(-1)^{k-p}\sec^{\prime}(x)\sec^{2p+2}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=0}^{k}\binom{k}{p}(-1)^{k-p}\int\sec^{\prime}(x)\sec^{2p+2}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=0}^{k}\binom{k}{p}(-1)^{k-p}\frac{\sec^{2p+3}(x)}{2p+3}

Recall
t=\tan(x)
, we have
\sec(x)=\sqrt{1+t^{2}}
.

It follows that:

\left(\varphi(2k+1)\right)=\sum_{p=0}^{k}\binom{k}{p}(-1)^{k-p}\frac{\sqrt{1+t^{2}}(1+t^{2})^{p+1}}{2p+3}

∎

Theorem 3.0.2
.

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,

\varphi(2k+1)=\int_{0}^{1}t^{2k+1}\sqrt{1+t^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t=\sum_{p=0}^{k}\binom{k}{p}(-1)^{k-p}\frac{\sqrt{2}\cdot 2^{p+1}-1}{2p+3}

(16)

Proof.

Using Proposition 2.0.2
Then:

\varphi(2k+1)=\int_{0}^{1}t^{2k+1}\sqrt{1+t^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t=\left(\varphi(2k+1)\right)\bigg|^{1}_{0}=\sum_{p=0}^{k}\binom{k}{p}(-1)^{k-p}\frac{\sqrt{2}\cdot 2^{p+1}-1}{2p+3}

∎

At this stage we are done with Bhandari’s quest on
\mathcal{B}(k)
and
\varphi(2k+1)
. Wondering about
\varphi(2k)
for completeness we finally come out with the following result.

Theorem 3.0.3
.

For
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,

\varphi(2m)=\int_{0}^{1}t^{2m}\sqrt{1+t^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{(-1)^{m}}{4^{m}}\frac{\binom{2m}{m}}{m+1}\cdot\left(I(0)+\sqrt{2}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{(-1)^{k}4^{k}}{\binom{2k}{k}}\right).

(17)

where
I(0)=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}+\frac{\ln(1+\sqrt{2})}{2}
.

Proof.

Let,

I(m):=\int_{0}^{1}t^{2m}\sqrt{1+t^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t=\varphi(2m)

Then,

I(m+1)=\int_{0}^{1}t^{2m+2}\sqrt{1+t^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t

Using integration by parts, we have that;

I(m+1)=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2m+3}-\frac{1}{2m+3}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{2m+4}}{\sqrt{1+t^{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}t

(18)

We deduce from (
18
) :

\frac{2m+1}{2m+3}I(m)=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2m+3}-\frac{1}{2m+3}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{2m+2}}{\sqrt{1+t^{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}t

(19)

Adding both (
18
) and (
19
) gives,

\displaystyle I(m+1)+\frac{2m+1}{2m+3}I(m)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{2m+3}-\frac{1}{2m+3}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{2m+2}(1+t^{2})}{\sqrt{1+t^{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{2m+3}-\frac{1}{2m+3}\underbrace{\int_{0}^{1}t^{2m+2}\sqrt{1+t^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t}_{I(m+1)}

Hence we deduce,

I(m+1)=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{m+2}-\frac{m+\frac{1}{2}}{m+2}I(m),\quad m\geq 0

(20)

For example
I(0)=\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{1+t^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t
, thus;

I(0)=\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{1+t^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{1}{2}(\sqrt{2}+\sinh^{-1}(1))=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}+\frac{\ln(1+\sqrt{2})}{2}.

using
\sinh^{-1}(t)=\ln(t+\sqrt{1+t^{2}}).

With the relation (
20
) connecting
I(m+1)
to
I(m)
and the expression of
I(0)
, we find a closed form using Lemma 2.0.8,

I(m)=\frac{(-1)^{m}}{4^{m}}\frac{\binom{2m}{m}}{m+1}\cdot\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}+\frac{\ln(1+\sqrt{2})}{2}+\sqrt{2}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{(-1)^{k}4^{k}}{\binom{2k}{k}}\right).

∎

Now, with the closed forms of
\mathcal{B}(k)
and

Proof.

Let

Using Integration by parts, we arrive at;

To this end, we have that,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\cos\frac{x}{2}\cos x\sin^{2r-1}x\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-2\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\sin\frac{x}{2}[-\sin^{2r}x+(2r-1)(1-\sin^{2}x)\sin^{2r-2}x]\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-2\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\sin\frac{x}{2}[-2r\sin^{2r}x+(2r-1)\sin^{2r-2}x]\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=4rK(r)-2(2r-1)K(r-1).

Thus,

K(r)=-\sqrt{2}+16r^{2}K(r)-8r(2r-1)K(r-1).

which can be re-written as

\left(r-\frac{1}{4}\right)\left(r+\frac{1}{4}\right)K(r)=r\left(r-\frac{1}{2}\right)K(r-1)+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{16}.

Using Lemma
2.0.7

a_{k}=\left(k-\frac{1}{4}\right)\left(k+\frac{1}{4}\right)

\begin{cases}a_{1}\cdots a_{r}=(4r+1)\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)_{r}\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)_{r},\\

\displaystyle a_{1}\cdots a_{r}

\displaystyle=(4r+1)\frac{(r!)^{2}}{4^{3r}}\binom{4r}{2r}\binom{2r}{r},

\displaystyle b_{1}\cdots b_{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{(r!)^{2}}{4^{r}}\binom{2r}{r}.

\frac{b_{1}b_{2}\cdots b_{r}}{a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{r}}=\frac{16^{r}}{(4r+1)\binom{4r}{2r}}

Lemma 5.0.1

(

, pp. 9)

For

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}\frac{x^{n+m+1}}{n+m+1}=\frac{1}{2^{2m+1}}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{2k+1}[1-(1-4x)^{k}\sqrt{1-4x}],\qquad|x|<\frac{1}{4}.

(39)

Proof.

Let

be the L.H.S. Thus,

\frac{\mathrm{d}(f_{m}(x))}{\mathrm{d}x}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}x^{n+m}=x^{m}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}x^{n}=\frac{x^{m}}{\sqrt{1-4x}}.

Using the well known generating function of central binomial coefficient

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}x^{n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-4x}},\qquad|x|<\frac{1}{4}.

(40)

So that,

f_{m}(x)=\int_{0}^{x}\frac{t^{m}}{\sqrt{1-4t}}\,\mathrm{d}t=-\frac{1}{2^{2m+1}}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{1-4x}}(1-y^{2})^{m}\,\mathrm{d}y.

using the substitution

and expanding

. We get,

f_{m}(x)=\frac{1}{2^{2m+1}}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{2k+1}[1-(1-4x)^{k}\sqrt{1-4x}].

∎

Theorem 5.0.1
.

For
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n+m+1)(2n+2m+2r+1)}\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{\binom{2n+2m+2r}{n+m+r}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2(2m+1)\binom{2m}{m}}\cdot\frac{1}{(2r-1)\binom{2r-2}{r-1}}-\frac{1}{4^{m+r}}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{2k+1}\frac{k!}{(r)_{k+1}},\qquad r>0.

Proof.

Replacing
x
by
x/4
in (

), we obtain

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}\frac{x^{n+m+1}}{4^{n+m+1}(n+m+1)}=\frac{1}{2\cdot 4^{m}}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{2k+1}[1-(1-x)^{k}\sqrt{1-x}],\qquad|x|<1.

(41)

which implies that,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}\frac{x^{n+m+1}}{4^{n}(n+m+1)}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{2k+1}[1-(1-x)^{k}\sqrt{1-x}],

(42)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{2k+1}-2\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{2k+1}(1-x)^{k}\sqrt{1-x}.

(43)

We remark the following fact;

\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{2k+1}=\int_{0}^{1}(1-t^{2})^{m}\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\cos^{2m+1}x\,\mathrm{d}x.

\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{2k+1}=\frac{4^{m}}{(2m+1)\binom{2m}{m}}.

We arrive at,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}\frac{x^{n+m+1}}{4^{n}(n+m+1)}=\frac{2\cdot 4^{m}}{(2m+1)\binom{2m}{m}}-2\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{2k+1}(1-x)^{k}\sqrt{1-x}.

Let

and multiply both sides by

for

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}\frac{\sin^{2n+2m+2r+1}y}{4^{n}(n+m+1)}=\frac{2\cdot 4^{m}}{(2m+1)\binom{2m}{m}}\sin^{2r-1}y-2\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{2k+1}\cos^{2k+1}y\sin^{2r-1}y.\end{split}

Integrating both sides over the interval
[0,\pi/2]
and using the Walli’s integral (

) and the beta function,
B(u,v)=2\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\cos^{2u-1}\theta\sin^{2v-1}\theta\,\mathrm{d}\theta
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n+m+1)(2n+2m+2r+1)}\frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{\binom{2n+2m+2r}{n+m+r}}=\frac{1}{2(2m+1)\binom{2m}{m}}\cdot\frac{1}{(2r-1)\binom{2r-2}{r-1}}\\
-\frac{1}{4^{m+r}}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{2k+1}B(k+1,r)\end{split}

for

.
The result follows immediately from this end. ∎

Lemma 5.0.2

(54)

where,

\mathscr{F}(r):=\int_{0}^{1}t^{r-1/2}\sqrt{1+t}\arcsin t\,\mathrm{d}t.

Using Integration by part, we have:

. Thus,

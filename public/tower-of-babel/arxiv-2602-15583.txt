1.
Introduction

For a locale
L
, the collection
\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)
consisting of joins of locally closed sublocales (that is, those sublocales of the form
\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{a\in A,b\in B}\mathfrak{c}(a)\cap\mathfrak{o}(b)
for
A,B\subseteq L
) is a complete Boolean algebra (see
[
2
]
). This was shown in
[
8
]
to be isomorphic to a construction by Funayama introduced in
[
11
]
, sometimes referred to as the
Funayama envelope
.
This collection has attracted attention in point-free topology (e.g., the naturality of the construction as a maximal essential extension
[
6
]
, its role as
a discretization of a locale for modeling non-continuous localic maps
[
19
]
, its role as the
T_{D}
–hull of a frame
[
9
]
, etc).
Closely related to this collection there is the collection
\mathsf{S}_{c}(L)
of joins of closed sublocales
[
20
]
, which sits inside
\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)
as a subframe. When
L
is subfit, and only in that case, the collections
\mathsf{S}_{c}(L)
and
\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)
coincide.

Neither of the constructions
L\mapsto\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)
or
L\mapsto\mathsf{S}_{c}(L)
is functorial in the category of frames
[
5
,
2
]
— see also
[
1
, Section 7.7]
for some positive results.

In this paper we characterise the frame morphisms that lift with respect to the
\mathsf{S}_{b}(-)
construction. The characterization will follow from the main result in our paper: if we denote by
\mathsf{S}_{lc}(L)
the meet-semilattice of locally closed sublocales, then inclusion
\mathsf{S}_{lc}^{op}(L)\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)
is, up to isomorphism, the
Bruns–Lakser completion
of
\mathsf{S}_{lc}^{op}(L)
. Again, we note in parallel that
\mathfrak{c}:L\to\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{c}}(L)
, too, is the Bruns–Lakser completion of
L
, viewed as a join-semilattice.

Given the strong analogy between the
\mathsf{S}_{b}(-)
and the
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{c}}(-)
construction, here and in our work we will illustrate the results with reference to this. A frame morphism
f:L\to M
lifting to the
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{c}}(-)
construction means there being a frame map
\overline{f}:\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{c}}(L)\to\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{c}}(M)
with
\overline{f}(\mathfrak{c}(x))=\mathfrak{c}(f(x))
for all
x\in L
. A morphism
f
lifts if and only if the following well-definedness condition holds:

(WDc)

\mathfrak{c}(x)\subseteq\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{i}\mathfrak{c}(x_{i})\text{ implies }\mathfrak{c}(f(x))\subseteq\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{i}\mathfrak{c}(f(x_{i})).

This is an instance of a much more general fact. The
Bruns–Lakser completion

[
10
]
of a join-semilattice
S
is the embedding
{\uparrow}:S\to\mathcal{AU}(S)
into the
admissible upper sets
, namely those closed under admissible meets (i.e. the existing meets that distribute over all binary joins). The Bruns–Lakser completion is always a frame, and we can characterise maps
f:S\to T
of join-semilattices that can be lifted to frame homomorphisms
\overline{f}\colon\mathcal{AU}(S)\to\mathcal{AU}(T)
. For an upper set
U
, we call
\mathcal{A}(U)
the smallest admissible upper set containing it. This is equivalent to the general well-definedness condition

(WD)

x\in\mathcal{A}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigcup}_{i}U_{i})\text{ implies }f(x)\in\mathcal{A}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigcup}_{i}{\uparrow}f[U_{i}]).

It is known that the morphisms that do lift are precisely the so-called admissible morphisms.

If the join-semilattice under consideration is a frame, admissible meets are called
exact

[

, we can think of exactness of a meet
\bigwedge_{i}x_{i}\in L
as being the property that
\mathfrak{c}(\bigwedge_{i}x_{i})=\bigvee_{i}\mathfrak{c}(x_{i})

and admissible morphisms, too, are called exact. It is known that
\mathfrak{c}:L\to\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{c}}(L)
is the Bruns–Lakser completion of
L
. The condition
\mathfrak{c}(x)\subseteq\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{i}\mathfrak{c}(x_{i})
is equivalent to
x\in\mathcal{A}({\uparrow}\{x_{i}\mid i\in I\})
; (
WDc
) is then seen as a special case of (

). Indeed, (
WDc
) is equivalent to having that
f:L\to M
is an exact morphism.

In this work, we show that the problem of lifting morphisms to the
\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)
construction is another special instance of the general result.
Here, a morphism lifts if there is a frame map
\overline{f}
making the following commute.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathsf{S}_{b}(M)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\overline{f}}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

In this case, too, there being such a frame map amounts to a well-definedness condition:

(WDb)

\mathfrak{c}(x)\cap\mathfrak{o}(y)\subseteq\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{i}\mathfrak{c}(x_{i})\cap\mathfrak{o}(y_{i})\text{ implies }\mathfrak{c}(f(x))\cap\mathfrak{o}(f(y))\subseteq\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{i}\mathfrak{c}(f(x_{i}))\cap\mathfrak{o}(f(y_{i})).

In this work, we exhibit this as another instance of (
WD
). We first define a collection
\mathsf{LC}(L)\subseteq L\times L^{op}
, anti-isomorphic to the meet-semilattice
\mathsf{S}_{lc}(L)
of locally closed sublocales. We will then show that the admissible meets of
\mathsf{LC}(L)
are exactly the joins of locally closed sublocales which are themselves locally closed. We will deduce that
\mathsf{LC}(L)\subseteq\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)
is isomorphic to the Bruns–Lakser completion of
\mathsf{LC}(L)
. We will prove as a consequence of this that the frame maps
f:L\to M
that lift coincide with those that determine maps
\mathsf{LC}(f):\mathsf{LC}(L)\to\mathsf{LC}(M)
of join-semilattices which are admissible (where
\mathsf{LC}(f):\mathfrak{c}(x)\cap\mathfrak{o}(y)\mapsto\mathfrak{c}(f(x))\cap\mathfrak{o}(f(y))
). We will also characterise such maps explicitly.

We can even compare the situations for
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{c}}(L)
and
\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)
with those for
\mathsf{S}_{o}(L)
, the collection of intersections of open sublocales, and
\mathsf{S}(L)
, the collection of all sublocales. In both cases all frame maps lift. For intersections of open sublocales, the well-definedness condition becomes:

(WDo)

\bigcap_{i}\mathfrak{o}(x_{i})\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(x)\text{ implies }\bigcap_{i}\mathfrak{o}(f(x_{i}))\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(f(x)).

This is equivalent to the frame map preserving all the meets
\bigwedge_{i}x_{i}\in L
with
\bigcap_{i}\mathfrak{o}(x_{i})=\mathfrak{o}(\bigwedge_{i}x_{i})
. But these are known to be the
strongly exact
meets, also known as
free
meets (see
[
22
]
), and are well known to be preserved by any frame map, see
[
4
, 3.5]
.
For
f:L\to M
to lift to a coframe map
f:\mathsf{S}(L)\to\mathsf{S}(M)
, it is enough for the well-definedness condition below to hold.

(WDs)

\bigcap_{i}\mathfrak{c}(x_{i})\vee\mathfrak{o}(y_{i})\subseteq\mathfrak{c}(x)\vee\mathfrak{o}(y)\text{ implies }\bigcap_{i}\mathfrak{o}(f(x_{i}))\vee\mathfrak{c}(f(y_{i}))\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(f(x))\vee\mathfrak{c}(f(y)).

This is again equivalent to
\mathsf{LC}(f):\mathsf{LC}(L)\to\mathsf{LC}(M)
preserving those intersections of locally closed sublocales which are locally closed, a condition satisfied by every frame map. What is missing is a characterisation of such intersections as joins of
\mathsf{LC}(L)
, in the same vein as the characterisation of strongly exact meets as those preserved by all frame maps, or even a purely algebraic characterisation as the one for locally closed meets of
\mathsf{LC}(L)
.

We have already highlighted the analogies between the problems of lifting morphisms for the
\mathsf{S}(L)
,
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{o}}(L)
,
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{c}}(L)
, and
\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)
constructions. In light of these, it is natural to ask how all these can be objects of a more general study. Our proofs in this work go in the direction of this more general study, which will be the topic of an upcoming paper.

The paper is organised as follows. Section
2
collects the necessary preliminaries. In Section
3
we introduce the collection
\mathsf{LC}(L)\subseteq L\times L^{op}
that is anti-isomorphic to the meet-semilattice of locally closed sublocales and establish its basic properties. In Section
4
we characterise admissible meets
\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}{(a_{i},b_{i})}_{i\in I}
in
\mathsf{LC}(L)
as those for which
\bigvee_{i\in I}\mathfrak{c}(a_{i})\cap\mathfrak{o}(b_{i})
is locally closed. Section
5

\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)
with the Bruns–Lakser completion of
\mathsf{LC}(L)
. Finally, Section
6
characterises those maps between join-semilattices that admit a lifting to the Bruns–Lakser completion. As a by-product, we characterise the frame homomorphisms that lift to the construction
\mathsf{S}_{b}(-)
.

2.
Preliminaries

Our notation and terminology regarding the categories of frames and locales will be that of
[
18
]
(see also
[
15
]
). The Heyting operator in a frame
L
, right adjoint to the meet operator, will be denoted by
\to
; for each
a\in L
,
a^{*}=a\to 0
is the pseudocomplement of
a
.

2.1.
Some Heyting rules

For the reader’s convenience, we list here some of the properties satisfied by the Heyting operator in a frame
L
. For any
a,b,c\in L
and any
\{a_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq L
, the following hold:

(H1)

1\to a=a
;

(H2)

a\leq b
if and only if
a\to b=1
;

(H3)

a\leq b\to a
;

(H4)

a\to b=a\to(a\wedge b)
;

(H5)

a\wedge(a\to b)=a\wedge b
;

(H6)

a\wedge b=a\wedge c
if and only if
a\to b=a\to c
;

(H7)

(a\wedge b)\to c=a\to(b\to c)=b\to(a\to c)
;

(H8)

a=(a\vee b)\wedge(b\to a)
;

(H9)

a\leq(a\to b)\to b
;

(H10)

((a\to b)\to b)\to b=a\to b
;

(H11)

(\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{i\in I}a_{i})\to b=\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i\in I}(a_{i}\to b)
;

(H12)

b\to(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i\in I}a_{i})=\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i\in I}(b\to a_{i})
.

2.2.
Sublocales

A
sublocale
of a locale
L
is a subset
S\subseteq L
closed under arbitrary meets such that

\forall a\in L,\ \ \ \forall s\in S,\ \ \ a\to s\in S.

These are precisely the subsets of
L
for which the embedding
j_{S}\colon S\hookrightarrow L
is a morphism of locales. Sublocales of
L
are in one-to-one correspondence with the regular subobjects (equivalently, extremal subobjects) of
L
in the category of locales. We denote by
\nu_{S}
the associated frame surjection
given by

\nu_{S}(a)=\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}\{s\in S\mid a\leq s\}.

The system
\mathsf{S}(L)
of all sublocales of
L
, partially ordered by inclusion, is a coframe
[
18
, Theorem III.3.2.1]
, that is, its dual lattice is a frame. Infima and suprema are given by

\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i\in I}S_{i}=\mathop{\textstyle\bigcap}_{i\in I}S_{i},\quad\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{i\in I}S_{i}=\{\,\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}M\mid M\subseteq\mathop{\textstyle\bigcup}_{i\in I}S_{i}\,\}.

The least element is the sublocale
\mathsf{O}=\{1\}
and the greatest element is the entire locale
L
.

It is easy to see that given a collection
\{S_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of sublocales then

\nu_{\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{i}S_{i}}(a)=\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i}\nu_{S_{i}}(a).

For any
a\in L
, the sublocales

\mathfrak{c}_{L}(a)={{\hbox to0.0pt{$\ $\hss}\hbox{$\uparrow$}}}a=\{\,b\in L\mid b\geq a\,\}\ \text{ and }\ \mathfrak{o}_{L}(a)=\{\,a\to b\mid b\in L\,\}

are the
closed
and
open
sublocales of
L
, respectively (that we shall denote simply by
\mathfrak{c}(a)
and
\mathfrak{o}(a)
when there is no danger of confusion). For each
a\in L
,
\mathfrak{c}(a)
and
\mathfrak{o}(a)
are
complements of each other in
\mathsf{S}(L)

and satisfy the expected identities

{}\mathop{\textstyle\bigcap}_{i\in I}\mathfrak{c}(a_{i})=\mathfrak{c}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{i\in I}a_{i}),\quad\mathfrak{c}(a)\vee\mathfrak{c}(b)=\mathfrak{c}(a\wedge b),

\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{i\in I}\mathfrak{o}(a_{i})=\mathfrak{o}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{i\in I}a_{i})\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\mathfrak{o}(a)\cap\mathfrak{o}(b)=\mathfrak{o}(a\wedge b).

A sublocale is
locally closed
if it is of the form
\mathfrak{c}(a)\cap\mathfrak{o}(b)
for
a,b\in L
.
Moreover,

\nu_{\mathfrak{c}(a)\cap\mathfrak{o}(b)}(x)=b\to(a\vee x)

for all
x\in L
.

Given a sublocale
S
of
L
, its
closure
, denoted by
\overline{S}
, is the smallest closed sublocale containing it. In this context, the formula
\overline{S}=\mathfrak{c}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}S)
holds.

Moreover,
\mathsf{S}(L)
is zero-dimensional in the sense that every sublocale
S\subseteq L
can be expressed as
S=\mathop{\textstyle\bigcap}_{a\in A,\ b\in B}\mathfrak{o}(a)\vee\mathfrak{c}(b)
for some subsets
A,B\subseteq L
(see
[
18
, Proposition III.6.5]
).

2.3.
The Boolean algebra of smooth sublocales.

We will write
S^{\#}
for the supplement (i.e. co-pseudocomplement) of a sublocale
S\subseteq L
. A sublocale
S
is said to be
smooth
if it is of the form
S=T^{\#}
for some sublocale
T\subseteq L
, equivalently if
S^{\#\#}=S
. By the zero-dimensionality of
\mathsf{S}(L)
it follows that a sublocale is smooth if and only if it is a join of complemented sublocales, if and only if it is a join of locally closed sublocales.
We denote by
\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)
the subset of
\mathsf{S}(L)
consisting of smooth sublocales, i.e.

\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)=\bigl\{\,\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{a\in A,b\in B}\mathfrak{c}(a)\cap\mathfrak{o}(b)\mid A,B\subseteq L\,\bigr\}.

The collection
\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)
is a subcolocale of
\mathsf{S}(L)
, which is a Boolean algebra, and it is in fact the Booleanization of
\mathsf{S}(L)
— see
[
2
]

for details.

2.4.
The Bruns–Lakser completion of a join-semilattice

In what follows we briefly outline the construction of a slight adaptation for join-semilattices of the Bruns–Lakser completion, introduced in
[
10
]
for meet-semilattices as an explicit description of their injective hulls. Let
S
be a join-semilattice. A family
\{a_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq S
is
admissible
if its meet in
S
exists and for each
b\in S
, the equality

b\vee\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i\in I}a_{i}=\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i\in I}b\vee a_{i}
holds. In this case, we also say that the meet
\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i\in I}a_{i}
is
admissible
.

A nonempty upper set
U
of
S
is said to be
admissible
if it is closed under admissible meets — i.e., whenever
\{a_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq U
is admissible, one has
\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i\in I}a_{i}\in U
. The collection of all nonempty upper sets (resp. all admissible upper sets) of
S
is noted by
\mathcal{U}(S)
(resp.
\mathcal{AU}(S)
). From the main result in
[
10
]
it follows that
\mathcal{AU}(S)
is a frame. We call the natural antitone embedding
\uparrow\colon S\to\mathcal{AU}(S)
the
Bruns–Lakser completion
of
S
2
2

2

In
[
10
]
the construction is described for meet-semilattices, and the completion consists of the downsets closed under admissible joins.
.

Morphisms of join-semilattices that lift to the Bruns–Lakser completion are the
admissible
morphisms (cf. Theorem
6.3
below), namely those morphisms
f:S\to T
such that for any admissible meet
\bigwedge_{i}a_{i}\in S
both
\bigwedge_{i}f(a_{i})
is admissible and
\bigwedge_{i}f(a_{i})=f(\bigwedge_{i}a_{i})
. These two conditions are independent.

2.5.
Joins of closed sublocales and the Bruns–Lakser completion

Let
L
be a frame. We write
\mathsf{S}_{c}(L)
for the collection of all joins of closed sublocales of
L
, namely

\mathsf{S}_{c}(L)=\bigl\{\,\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{a\in A}\mathfrak{c}(a)\mid A\subseteq L\,\bigr\}.

We consider
\mathsf{S}_{c}(L)
as an ordered set with the inclusion order inherited from
\mathsf{S}(L)
.
Picado, Pultr, and Tozzi
[
20
]
show that
\mathsf{S}_{c}(L)
is always a frame and that it embeds as a complete join-sublattice of the coframe
\mathsf{S}(L)
. Moreover, their main result states that
\mathsf{S}_{c}(L)
is a Boolean algebra if and only if
L
is subfit; in this case,
\mathsf{S}_{c}(L)
coincides with the family
\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)
introduced in Subsection
2.3
.

The connection between
\mathsf{S}_{c}(L)
and the Bruns–Lakser construction (see Subsection

) was established in
[
3
]
. More precisely, the Bruns–Lakser completion of

, viewed as a complete join-semilattice, is isomorphic to
\mathsf{S}_{c}(L)
. The proof strategy in
[
3
]
is as follows. One defines monotone maps
\varphi\colon\mathsf{S}_{c}(L)\to\mathcal{U}(L)
given by
\varphi(S):=\{a\in L\mid\mathfrak{c}(a)\subseteq S\}
and
\psi\colon\mathcal{U}(L)\to\mathsf{S}_{c}(L)
given by
\psi(U)=\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{a\in U}\mathfrak{c}(a)
.
Then, one shows that there is an adjunction
\psi\dashv\varphi
which restricts to an isomorphism
\mathsf{S}_{c}(L)\cong\mathcal{AU}(L)
as the following diagram shows:

(1)

Note that, since every frame is a distributive lattice, in this case, finite meets are exact, and hence exact upper sets are actually filters of

(referred to as

in

).
Frame morphisms

that are admissible (viewing

as join-semilattices) are called

Exact families have the following useful description.

.

(

).

If

is a frame, a family

3.
The join-semilattice of locally closed sublocales

Let
L
be a frame. In this section we construct a join-semilattice that serves, in the context of smooth sublocales, as the appropriate analogue of
L
(viewed as a join-semilattice) in the context of joins of closed sublocales (cf. Subsection
2.5
).
For reasons that will become clear with the next lemma, for a locally closed sublocale
S
we call the pair
(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}S,\nu_{S^{\#}}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}S))
its
canonical representation
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
L
be a frame and
S\subseteq L
a sublocale.

(1)

One has

\mathfrak{c}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}S)\cap\mathfrak{o}(\nu_{S^{\#}}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}S))\subseteq S^{\#\#}.

(2)

Moreover,
S
is locally closed if and only if
S\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(\nu_{S^{\#}}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}S))
. In that case, one has
S=\mathfrak{c}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}S)\cap\mathfrak{o}(\nu_{S^{\#}}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}S))
.

(3)

If
S=\mathfrak{c}(a)\cap\mathfrak{o}(b)
and the relations
b\to a=a
and
a\leq b
hold, then
(a,b)
is the canonical representation of
S
. In other words, locally closed sublocales correspond exactly to pairs
(a,b)
with
a\leq b
and
b\to a=a
.

Proof.

(
1
) Let
y\in\mathfrak{c}(\bigwedge S)
. Since
L=S^{\#}\vee S^{\#\#}
, there are
a\in S^{\#}
and
b\in S^{\#\#}
such that
y=a\wedge b
. Now since
\bigwedge S\leq y\leq b
, it follows that
x:=\nu_{S^{\#}}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}S)\leq\nu_{S^{\#}}(b)
, and since
\bigwedge S\leq y\leq a
, we have
x\leq\nu_{S^{\#}}(a)=a
, where the last equality follows because
a\in S^{\#}
. Therefore
a\wedge\nu_{S^{\#}}(b)\geq x
. Hence we have
y=a\wedge b=a\wedge(\nu_{S^{\#}}(b)\wedge b)=(a\wedge\nu_{S^{\#}}(b))\wedge b
: the first part of this meet belongs to
\mathfrak{c}(x)
and the second part belongs to
S^{\#\#}
. We have thus shown
\mathfrak{c}(\bigwedge S)\subseteq\mathfrak{c}(x)\vee S^{\#\#}
. From this the required inclusion follows.

(
2
) If
S\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(\nu_{S^{\#}}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}S))
, since we also have
S\subseteq\overline{S}=\mathfrak{c}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}S)
, by the first part it follows that
S
is locally closed. Conversely, assume that
S
is locally closed, i.e.
S=S^{\#\#}=\mathfrak{c}(a)\cap\mathfrak{o}(b)
. Since
S\subseteq\overline{S}=\mathfrak{c}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}S)
, it only suffices to check that
S\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(x)
. Note that
\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}S=b\to a
, and so

x=\nu_{\mathfrak{o}(a)\vee\mathfrak{c}(b)}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}S)=(a\to(b\to a))\wedge(b\vee(b\to a))=b\vee(b\to a)

by
(H2)
,
(H3)
and
(H7)
. Then
S\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(x)
if and only if
\mathfrak{c}(a)\cap\mathfrak{o}(b)\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(b\vee(b\to a))
, and this holds if and only if
b\leq a\vee(b\to a)\vee b
, which clearly holds. Hence,
S=\mathfrak{c}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}S)\cap\mathfrak{o}(\nu_{S^{\#}}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}S))
.

(
3
) Let
S=\mathfrak{c}(a)\cap\mathfrak{o}(b)
with
a\leq b
and
b\to a=a
. By the previous calculations
\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}S=b\to a=a
and
\nu_{S^{\#}}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}S)=b\vee(b\to a)=b\vee a=b
.
∎

In view of Lemma
3.1
(
3
), we define

\mathsf{LC}(L):=\{(a,b)\in L\times L\mid a\leq b,\ b\to a=a\},

so that
\mathsf{LC}(L)
is the collection of all canonical representations of locally closed sublocales. We want to equip this set with a partial order so that it is anti-isomorphic to the collection of locally closed sublocales. To that end, we make use of the following result.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
L
be a frame.
If
(a,b),(x,y)\in\mathsf{LC}(L)
, then
\mathfrak{c}(a)\cap\mathfrak{o}(b)\subseteq\mathfrak{c}(x)\cap\mathfrak{o}(y)
if and only if
x\leq a
and
b\leq a\vee y
.

We now define a partial order
\sqsubseteq
on
\mathsf{LC}(L)
, defined as
(x,y)\sqsubseteq(a,b)
if and only if
x\leq a
and
b\leq a\vee y
. Observe that Lemma
3.1
(3) guarantees that
\sqsubseteq
is a partial order. In summary:

(x,y)\sqsubseteq(a,b)\iff(x\leq a\text{ and }b\leq a\vee y)\iff\mathfrak{c}(a)\cap\mathfrak{o}(b)\subseteq\mathfrak{c}(x)\cap\mathfrak{o}(y).

We shall always consider
\mathsf{LC}(L)
with the ordering
\sqsubseteq
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
L
be a frame and
S=\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{i\in I}\mathfrak{c}(a_{i})\cap\mathfrak{o}(b_{i})
. Then

\nu_{S^{\#}}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}S)=\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{x\in L}\biggl(\big(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i\in I}(b_{i}\to(x\vee a_{i}))\big)\to\big(x\vee\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{j\in I}(b_{j}\to a_{j})\bigr)\biggr).

Proof.

Note that since
S^{\#}
is smooth, we can write

\displaystyle S^{\#}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}\{\mathfrak{c}(x)\cap\mathfrak{o}(y)\mid\mathfrak{c}(x)\cap\mathfrak{o}(y)\subseteq S^{\#}\}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}\{\mathfrak{c}(x)\cap\mathfrak{o}(y)\mid\forall i\in I,\ \mathfrak{c}(a_{i})\cap\mathfrak{o}(b_{i})\subseteq\mathfrak{o}(x)\vee\mathfrak{c}(y)\}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}\{\mathfrak{c}(x)\cap\mathfrak{o}(y)\mid\forall i\in I,\ b_{i}\wedge y\leq x\vee a_{i}\}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}\{\mathfrak{c}(x)\cap\mathfrak{o}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i}(b_{i}\to(x\vee a_{i})))\mid x\in L\}.

Moreover, since
\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}S=\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i}(b_{i}\to a_{i})
, we conclude that

\nu_{S^{\#}}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}S)=\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{x\in L}\biggl(\big(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i}(b_{i}\to(x\vee a_{i}))\big)\to\big(x\vee\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{j}(b_{j}\to a_{j})\bigr)\biggr).

∎

We define the map
\mathit{lc}:L\times L\to L\times L
as

\mathit{lc}(a,b)=(b\rightarrow a,(b\rightarrow a)\vee b),

and so
\mathit{lc}(a,b)=(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}S,\nu_{S^{\#}}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}S))
where
S=\mathfrak{c}(a)\cap\mathfrak{o}(b)
. Under the anti-isomorphism with locally closed sublocales, this is the same as
(a,b)\mapsto\mathfrak{c}(a)\cap\mathfrak{o}(b)
. The anti-isomorphism between
\mathsf{LC}(L)
and the locally closed sublocales readily gives us the following result.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
L
be a frame.
The poset
\mathsf{LC}(L)
is a join-semilattice, with joins computed as
(x,y)\sqcup(u,v)=\mathit{lc}(x\vee u,y\wedge v)
. Furthermore,
\mathit{lc}(a,b)\sqcup\mathit{lc}(x,y)=\mathit{lc}(a\vee x,y\wedge b)
for all
(a,b),(x,y)\in L\times L
.

4.
Admissible meets of locally closed sublocales

In the previous section we introduced the poset
\mathsf{LC}(L)
and showed that it is a join-semilattice.
The aim of this section is to study admissibility in
\mathsf{LC}(L)
(see Subsection
2.4
).
Motivated by Lemma
2.1
, which characterises exact families in a frame
L
as those families
\{a_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq L
for which the join
\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{i\in I}\mathfrak{c}(a_{i})
is closed, we seek an analogous criterion for families of locally closed sublocales.
Thus, we introduce the notion of local exactness: a family
\{(a_{i},b_{i})\}_{i\in I}\subseteq L\times L
is
locally exact
when the sublocale
\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{i}\mathfrak{c}(a_{i})\cap\mathfrak{o}(b_{i})
is locally closed. By Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
, a family
\{(a_{i},b_{i})\}_{i\in I}
is locally exact if and only if

b_{i}\leq\nu_{S^{\#}}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}S)\vee a_{i}\text{ for all $i\in I$,}

where
S=\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{i\in I}\mathfrak{c}(a_{i})\cap\mathfrak{o}(b_{i})
. By the formula for
\nu_{S^{\#}}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}S)
of Lemma
3.3
, this amounts to having, for each
i\in I
, that:

b_{i}\leq a_{i}\vee\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{x\in L}\biggl(\big(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i\in I}(b_{i}\to(x\vee a_{i}))\big)\to\big(x\vee\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{j\in I}(b_{j}\to a_{j})\bigr)\biggr).

Example 4.1
.

A family of the form
\{(a_{i},1)\}_{i\in I}
is locally exact if and only if for all
i\in I

a_{i}\vee\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{x\in L}[(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i}(x\vee a_{i}))\to(x\vee\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{j}a_{j})]=1;

So this is a sort of generalised exactness.

We now characterise the admissible meets in
\mathsf{LC}(L)
as the meets of locally exact families.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
S
be a join-semilattice and let
\psi:S^{op}\to C
be an injective meet-semilattice map such that
C
is a coframe. Suppose that
\{a_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq S
is a collection whose meet exists in
S
.

(1)

If
\psi(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i\in I}a_{i})=\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{i}\psi(a_{i})
, the meet
\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i\in I}a_{i}
is admissible.

(2)

If, moreover, elements of the form
\psi(a)
are complemented and every element of
C
is of the form
\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{j\in J}\psi(u_{j})\vee\psi(v_{j})^{*}
, the converse holds, too.

Proof.

The first claim follows from an argument analogous to Proposition 3.7.2 in
[
16
]
. We note that the assumption in
[
16
]
that
\psi(a)
is complemented, thus colinear, here is replaced by the assumption that
C
is a coframe, and so all its elements are colinear. For the second claim, suppose that for
\{a_{i}\}_{i}\subseteq S
the meet
\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i\in I}a_{i}
exists in
S
and is admissible. Towards contradiction, suppose that
\psi(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i}a_{i})\nleq\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{i}\psi(a_{i})
. By assumption, there are
u,v\in S
with
\psi(a_{i})\leq\psi(u)\vee\psi(v)^{*}
for all
i\in I
and
\psi(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i}a_{i})\nleq\psi(u)\vee\psi(v)^{*}
. Then, for all
i\in I
:

\psi(a_{i})\wedge\psi(v)\leq\psi(u)

Using the fact that
\psi:S^{op}\to C
is injective and preserves all binary meets, this means
u\leq a_{i}\vee v
. Using admissibility of
\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i}a_{i}
, this implies
u\leq\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}a_{i}\vee v
. On the other hand, because
\psi(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i}a_{i})\nleq\psi(u)\vee\psi(v)^{*}
, one has

\psi(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i}a_{i})\wedge\psi(v)\nleq\psi(u),

from which we obtain
u\nleq\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i}a_{i}\vee v
, using again that
\psi
is an injective map of meet-semilattices. This is a contradiction.
∎

As a direct consequence of this, we obtain:

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
L
be a frame and
\{(a_{i},b_{i})\}_{i\in I}\subseteq\mathsf{LC}(L)
. Then
\{(a_{i},b_{i})\}_{i\in I}
is locally exact if and only if the family
\{(a_{i},b_{i})\}_{i\in I}
is admissible in
\mathsf{LC}(L)
.

Proof.

Consider the map
\mathsf{LC}(L)^{op}\to\mathsf{S}(L)
defined as
(a,b)\mapsto\mathfrak{c}(a)\cap\mathfrak{o}(b)
. This is a map of meet-semilattices, and, as we know, it is injective. It is easy to see, via basic facts about
\mathsf{S}(L)
, that the map satisfies the hypotheses of Proposition
4.2
, including those of its Item (
2
), and so the claim follows from said proposition.
∎

5.
Smooth sublocales as admissible upper sets

The aim of this section is to relate the Bruns–Lakser completion of the join-semilattice
\mathsf{LC}(L)
to the lattice of smooth sublocales
\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)
introduced in Subsection
2.3
.
This mirrors the well known situation in Subsection
2.5
, where exact upper sets (equivalently, filters) of
L
correspond to joins of closed sublocales. First note that there is a monotone map

\varphi\colon\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)\longrightarrow\mathcal{U}(\mathsf{LC}(L))

to the nonempty upper sets of
\mathsf{LC}(L)
, given by
\varphi(S)=\{(a,b)\in\mathsf{LC}(L)\mid\mathfrak{c}(a)\cap\mathfrak{o}(b)\subseteq S\}
(cf. Subsection Subsection
2.4
). Indeed, by Lemma
3.2
and the definition of the ordering
\sqsubseteq
this is an upper set of
\mathsf{LC}(L)
. Moreover, there is another monotone map

\psi\colon\mathcal{U}(\mathsf{LC}(L))\to\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)

given by

\psi(U)=\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{(c,d)\in U}\mathfrak{c}(c)\cap\mathfrak{o}(d).

Clearly, the adjunction identities

\psi\varphi(S)\subseteq S\quad\text{and}\quad U\subseteq\varphi\psi(U)

hold. Since
\varphi
is clearly injective,
\psi\circ\varphi=\text{id}
.

Note that, by Corollary
4.3
a nonempty upper set
U\subseteq\mathsf{LC}(L)
is admissible (in the sense of Subsection
2.4
) if whenever
\{(a_{i},b_{i})\}_{i\in I}\subseteq U
is locally exact, then the meet
\bigsqcap_{i\in I}(a_{i},b_{i})
in
\mathsf{LC}(L)
belongs to
U
.
This is in turn equivalent to the relation

\left(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i\in I}a_{i},\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{x\in L}\biggl(\big(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i\in I}(b_{i}\to(x\vee a_{i}))\big)\to\big(x\vee\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{j\in I}a_{j}\bigr)\biggr)\right)\in U

holding whenever
\{(a_{i},b_{i})\}_{i\in I}\subseteq U
is a locally exact family. Indeed, if
\{(a_{i},b_{i})\}_{i\in I}
is locally exact, and we denote
S=\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{i}\mathfrak{c}(a_{i})\cap\mathfrak{o}(b_{i})
then by local exactness one has
S=\mathfrak{c}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}S)\cap\mathfrak{o}(\nu_{S^{\#}}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}S))
, and so
\mathfrak{c}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}S)\cap\mathfrak{o}(\nu_{S^{\#}}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}S))
is the join of
\{\mathfrak{c}(a_{i})\cap\mathfrak{o}(b_{i})\}_{i\in I}
in the meet-semilattice of locally closed sublocales. Using the anti-isomorphism between
\mathsf{LC}(L)
and the meet-semilattice of locally closed sublocales we then have that the meet of
\{(a_{i},b_{i})\}_{i\in I}
in
\mathsf{LC}(L)
must be
(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}S,\nu_{S^{\#}}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}S))
, and so by Lemma
3.3
, indeed we have the following formula for the meet of
\{(a_{i},b_{i})\}_{i\in I}

(2)

\mathop{\textstyle\bigsqcap}_{i\in I}(a_{i},b_{i})=\left(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i\in I}a_{i},\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{x\in L}\biggl(\big(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i\in I}(b_{i}\to(x\vee a_{i}))\big)\to\big(x\vee\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{j\in I}a_{j}\bigr)\biggr)\right).

Following the notation introduced in Subsection
2.4
, we denote the family of admissible upper sets by

\mathcal{AU}(\mathsf{LC}(L)).

Lemma 5.1
.

The relation
\varphi(\psi(U))=U
holds if and only if
U
is admissible.

Proof.

Assume first that
\varphi(\psi(U))=U
, then
U=\{(a,b)\in\mathsf{LC}(L)\mid\mathfrak{c}(a)\cap\mathfrak{o}(b)\subseteq\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{(c,d)\in U}\mathfrak{c}(c)\cap\mathfrak{o}(d)\}
. Now if
\{(a_{i},b_{i})\}\subseteq U
is a locally exact family, then
\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{i\in I}\mathfrak{c}(a_{i})\cap\mathfrak{o}(b_{i})
is locally closed and moreover it equals

\mathfrak{c}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i}a_{i})\cap\mathfrak{o}\biggl(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{x\in L}\biggl(\big(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i}(b_{i}\to(x\vee a_{i}))\big)\to\big(x\vee\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{j}a_{j}\bigr)\biggr)\biggr),

so by assumption, it follows

\left(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}a_{i},\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{x\in L}\biggl(\big(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i}(b_{i}\to(x\vee a_{i}))\big)\to\big(x\vee\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{j}a_{j}\bigr)\biggr)\right)\in U.

Let us now show the converse. Assume that
U
is closed under locally exact families. We only need to show that

\left\{(a,b)\in\mathsf{LC}(L)\mid\mathfrak{c}(a)\cap\mathfrak{o}(b)\subseteq\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{(c,d)\in U}\mathfrak{c}(c)\cap\mathfrak{o}(d)\right\}\subseteq U.

Let
(a,b)\in\mathsf{LC}(L)
with
\mathfrak{c}(a)\cap\mathfrak{o}(b)\subseteq\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{(c,d)\in U}\mathfrak{c}(c)\cap\mathfrak{o}(d)\subseteq U
.
Then

\displaystyle\mathfrak{c}(a)\cap\mathfrak{o}(b)

\displaystyle=\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{(c,d)\in U}\mathfrak{c}(c\vee a)\cap\mathfrak{o}(d\wedge b)

\displaystyle=\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{(c,d)\in U}\mathfrak{c}\bigl((d\wedge b)\to(c\vee a)\bigr)\cap\mathfrak{o}\bigl((d\wedge b)\vee(d\wedge b)\to(c\vee a)\bigr)

Note that
A:=\biggl\{\bigl((d\wedge b)\to(c\vee a),(d\wedge b)\vee(d\wedge b)\to(c\vee a)\bigr)\mid(c,d)\in U\biggr\}\subseteq\mathsf{LC}(L).

We first claim that
A\subseteq U
; since
U
is an upper set it suffices to show that for any
(c,d)\in U
, one has
(c,d)\sqsubseteq\bigl((d\wedge b)\to(c\vee a),(d\wedge b)\vee(d\wedge b)\to(c\vee a)\bigr)
, and that is very easy to check.
Hence
A\subseteq U
and it is a locally exact family because the corresponding join of locally closed sublocales is locally closed. Now, set

a_{cd}=(d\wedge b)\to(c\vee a)\quad\text{and}\quad b_{cd}=(d\wedge b)\vee(d\wedge b)\to(c\vee a),

c_{0}=\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{(c,d)\in U}(d\wedge b)\to(c\vee a)

and

\displaystyle d_{0}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{x\in L}\biggl(\big(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{(c,d)\in U}(b_{cd}\to(x\vee a_{cd}))\big)\to\big(x\vee\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{(c^{\prime},d^{\prime})\in U}a_{c^{\prime}d^{\prime}}\bigr)\biggr)

\displaystyle=\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{x\in L}\biggl(\big(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{(c,d)\in U}(b\wedge d)\to(x\vee a_{cd}))\big)\to\big(x\vee\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{(c^{\prime},d^{\prime})\in U}a_{c^{\prime}d^{\prime}}\bigr)\biggr)

(the second equality holds by an application of
(H11)
and
(H2)
). Because
U
is closed under locally exact families, it follows that
(c_{0},d_{0})\in U
.

To conclude the proof it suffices to show that
(c_{0},d_{0})\sqsubseteq(a,b)
. First,

c_{0}=\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{(c,d)\in U}b\to(d\to(c\vee a))=b\to\bigl(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{(c,d)\in U}(d\to(c\vee a))\bigr)=b\to a=a

where we have used
(H7)
,
(H12)
and the facts that
a\in\mathfrak{c}(a)\cap\mathfrak{o}(b)\subseteq\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{(c,d)\in U}\mathfrak{c}(c)\cap\mathfrak{o}(d)
and the frame surjection corresponding to the right hand side is
\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{(c,d)\in U}d\to(c\vee(-))
.

Next, we have to show that
b\leq a\vee d_{0}
. Note that
a\leq a_{cd}
for all
(c,d)\in U
and so
a\leq d_{0}
. Hence we need to show
b\leq d_{0}
. Let
x\in L
, we have

\displaystyle b\wedge\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{(c,d)\in U}(b\wedge d)\to(x\vee a_{cd})

\displaystyle\leq b\wedge\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{(c,d)\in U}(b\wedge d)\to((b\wedge d)\to(x\vee c\vee a))

\displaystyle=b\wedge\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{(c,d)\in U}(b\wedge d)\to(x\vee c\vee a)

by
(H7)

\displaystyle=b\wedge\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{(c,d)\in U}b\to(d\to(x\vee c\vee a))

by
(H7)

\displaystyle=\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{(c,d)\in U}b\wedge(d\to(x\vee c\vee a))

by
(H5)

\displaystyle=b\wedge\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{(c,d)\in U}d\to(x\vee c\vee a)

\displaystyle=b\wedge(b\to(a\vee x))

\displaystyle=b\wedge(a\vee x)

by
(H5)

\displaystyle=a\vee(b\wedge x)\leq(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{(c^{\prime},d^{\prime})\in U}a_{c^{\prime}d^{\prime}})\vee x.

where again we have used that the frame surjection corresponding to
\mathfrak{c}(a)\cap\mathfrak{o}(b)
is
\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{(c,d)\in U}d\to(c\vee(-))
.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

There is an isomorphism

\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)\cong\mathcal{AU}(\mathsf{LC}(L)).

The following diagram summarises the situation (compare the following with diagram (
1
) for
\mathsf{S}_{\mathfrak{c}}(L)
).

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\top}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{U}(\mathsf{LC}(L))}}

We will now use the characterisation in Corollary
4.3
to describe the closure operator
\varphi\circ\psi
explicitly. Because admissible upper sets are closed under intersection, they form a closure system, and so for every upper set
U\subseteq A
there exists the least admissible upper set
\mathit{cl}_{\mathcal{A}}(U)
containing it. This is precisely
\varphi(\psi(U))
. Our main result will follow from an explicit description of
\mathit{cl}_{\mathcal{A}}(-)
in the general case of the Bruns–Lakser completion of a join-semilattice.

The following is easy to check using routine computations.

Lemma 5.3
.

If
S
is a join-semilattice, and
\{a_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq S
is a family whose meet is admissible, then the meet of
a_{i}\vee b
is admissible for every
b\in S
.

We also need another technical lemma.

Lemma 5.4
.

Suppose that
S
is a join-semilattice and that
\{S_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is a collection of admissible families of
S
. Then, if
\{\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}S_{i}\mid i\in I\}
is admissible, so is
\bigcup_{i}S_{i}
.

Proof.

Let
a\in S
. We have
\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}\bigcup_{i}S_{i}=\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i}\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}S_{i}
. As
\{\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}S_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is an admissible family,
(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i}\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}S_{i})\vee a=\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}S_{i}\vee a)
, and as each
S_{i}
is admissible, this equals
\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}\{s\vee a\mid s\in S_{i}\})=\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}\{s\vee a\mid s\in\bigcup_{i}S_{i}\}
.
∎

For
U\subseteq S
an upper set, we define

\mathcal{A}(U)=\{\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}F\mid F\subseteq U,F\text{ is admissible}\}.

The following lemma is essentially proven in
[
10
, Lemma 3]
, we report the proof here for the reader’s convenience.

Lemma 5.5
.

If
S
is a join-semilattice, and
U\subseteq S
is an upper set, then
\mathcal{A}(U)
is the least admissible upper set containing
U
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
\mathcal{A}(U)
is an admissible upper set, already. First, we want to show that it is an upper set. Let
x\in\mathcal{A}(U)
and
x\leq y
. By definition of
\mathcal{A}(U)
, there is an admissible family
\{u_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq U
such that
x=\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i}u_{i}
. Then,
u_{i}\vee y\in U
as
U
is an upper set, and the family is admissible by Lemma
5.3
. So,
\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i}(u_{i}\vee y)\in\mathcal{A}(U)
, but by admissibility of
\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i}u_{i}
this equals
\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i}u_{i}\vee y
, which is equal to
y
by our assumptions. So
y\in\mathcal{A}(U)
. To see that
\mathcal{A}(U)
is admissible, suppose that
x_{i}\in\mathcal{A}(U)
and that the meet
\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i}x_{i}
is admissible. For each
i\in I
, let
S_{i}\subseteq S
be the admissible family with
x_{i}=\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}S_{i}
. By Lemma
5.4
,
\bigcup_{i}S_{i}\subseteq U
is an admissible family, and so the admissible meet
\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}\bigcup_{i}S_{i}=\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i}\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}S_{i}
must be in
\mathcal{A}(U)
.
∎

By Corollary
4.3
, and by Lemma
5.5
, we obtain the following explicit description of
\varphi\circ\psi
.

Proposition 5.6
.

For a frame
L
and for an upper set
U\subseteq\mathsf{LC}(L)
,
\varphi(\psi(U))=\mathcal{A}(U)=\{\mathop{\textstyle\bigsqcap}F\mid F\subseteq U\text{, $F$ is locally exact}\}.

6.
Lifting of morphisms

In this section, we use the characterisation of
\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)
as the Bruns–Lakser completion of
\mathsf{LC}(L)
to give a characterisation of the frame maps which lift to the construction. The following is shown in
[
10
, Corollary 1]
.

Lemma 6.1
.

For a join-semilattice
S
, the collection
\mathcal{AU}(S)
is a frame.

Lemma 6.2
.

If
S
is a join-semilattice:

(1)

{\uparrow}:S\to\mathcal{AU}(S)
turns all existing joins into meets;

(2)

{\uparrow}:S\to\mathcal{AU}(S)
turns an existing meet into a join if and only if it is admissible.

Proof.

Let us prove the two items in turn.

(1)

For
\{a_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq S
, suppose that the join
\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{i}a_{i}\in S
exists. The meet of
{\uparrow}a_{i}
in
\mathcal{AU}(S)
is
\bigcap_{i}{\uparrow}a_{i}
(recall that meets in
\mathcal{AU}(S)
are intersections). But this is
{\uparrow}\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{i}a_{i}
.

(2)

Suppose that the family
\{a_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq S
is admissible. Clearly
{\uparrow}\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i}a_{i}
is an upper bound for the family
{\uparrow}a_{i}
. If
{\uparrow}a_{i}\subseteq E
for an admissible upper set,
{\uparrow}\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i}a_{i}\subseteq E
by admissibility of
\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i}a_{i}
, which shows it is the least upper bound. For the other direction, suppose that there is a family
\{a_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq S
whose meet exists and such that
{\uparrow}\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i}a_{i}
is the least upper bound of
\{{\uparrow}a_{i}\mid i\in I\}
in
\mathcal{AU}(S)
. By Lemma
5.5
,
{\uparrow}\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i}a_{i}=\mathcal{A}(\bigcup_{i}{\uparrow}a_{i})
. Then there is an admissible family
\{x_{j}\}_{j\in J}\subseteq\bigcup_{i}{\uparrow}a_{i}
such that
\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{j}x_{j}=\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i}a_{i}
. We want to show that
\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i}a_{i}
is admissible. Let
b\in S
. By our assumption on
x_{j}
, for every
j\in J
there is
i\in I
such that
a_{i}\vee b\leq x_{j}\vee b
. Then,
\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i}(a_{i}\vee b)\leq x_{j}\vee b
for all
j\in J
. So:

\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i}(a_{i}\vee b)\leq\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{j}(x_{j}\vee b)=(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{j}x_{j})\vee b=(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i}a_{i})\vee b.

Then, the meet
\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}_{i}a_{i}
is admissible. ∎

A version of the following result is stated as already known in
[
12
]
. However, we did not find in the literature an explicit proof of this fact. We call a map between join-semilattices
f:S\to T

admissible
if it maps admissible families to admissible families, and it also preserves their meets. If a frame map
\mathcal{AU}(f):\mathcal{AU}(S)\to\mathcal{AU}(T)
extending
f
exists, since it has to preserve all joins the definition is forced as

\mathcal{AU}(f)(U)=\mathcal{A}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigcup}\{{\uparrow}f(x)\mid x\in U\})

for every admissible upper set
U\subseteq L
. In order for such
\mathcal{AU}(f)
to be well defined we need that for all
x\in L
the following condition holds.

(WD)

x\in\mathcal{A}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigcup}_{i}U_{i})\text{ implies }f(x)\in\mathcal{A}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigcup}_{i}{\uparrow}f[U_{i}]).

Conversely, if this condition holds, then it is easy to check that
\mathcal{AU}(f)
is also a frame map. The existence of a lift is then equivalent to (
WD
). We will use this characterisation several times, in the coming proof and in the later work.

Theorem 6.3
.

If
f:S\to T
is a map between join-semilattices, then it lifts to a frame map
\mathcal{AU}(S)\to\mathcal{AU}(T)
if and only if
f
is admissible.

Proof.

Suppose that
f:S\to T
is a map of join-semilattices. Suppose that
f
is admissible and
x\in\mathcal{A}(\bigcup_{i}U_{i})
. By Lemma
5.5
, there is an admissible family
F\subseteq\mathop{\textstyle\bigcup}_{i}U_{i}
with
x=\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}F
. By assumption on
f
, one has that
\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}f[F]
exists and is admissible, and
\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}f[F]=f(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}F)=f(x)
. Since
f[F]\subseteq\mathop{\textstyle\bigcup}_{i}{\uparrow}f[U_{i}]
, one has
f(x)\in\mathcal{A}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigcup}_{i}{\uparrow}f[U_{i}])
, as desired. Now suppose that
f
is not admissible. We will consider three separate cases. In all three cases we will prove that for some admissible family
F
:

(3)

\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}F\in\mathcal{A}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigcup}_{y\in F}{\uparrow}y)\text{ and }f(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}F)\notin\mathcal{A}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigcup}_{y\in F}{\uparrow}f(y)),

thus contradicting (
WD
).

•

Suppose that
F\subseteq S
is admissible but the meet of the family
f[F]
does not exist in
T
. By admissibility of
F
, we must have
\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}F\in\mathcal{A}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigcup}_{y\in F}{\uparrow}y
). Suppose, towards contradiction, that (
3
) does not hold, i.e. that there is some admissible family
G\subseteq\mathop{\textstyle\bigcup}_{y\in F}{\uparrow}f(y)
with
\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}G=f(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}F)
. We will reach a contradiction by deducing that
f[F]
has a meet, in particular that this is
\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}G
. This is clearly a lower bound. Suppose that
x\leq f(y)
for all
y\in F
. For each
g\in G
, there is by assumption
y_{g}\in F
with
f(y_{g})\leq g
. Then, for each
g\in G
,
x\leq f(y_{g})\leq g
, from which
x\leq\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}G
.

•

Suppose, now, that
F\subseteq S
is admissible and the meet of
f[F]
exists but is not admissible. By Lemma
6.2
, then,
{\uparrow}\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}F=\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{y\in F}{\uparrow}y
, and
{\uparrow}\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}f[F]\nsubseteq\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{y\in F}{\uparrow}f(y)
, so
\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}f[F]\notin\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{y\in F}{\uparrow}f(y)
. As
f(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}F)\leq\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}f[F]
, then,
f(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}F)\notin\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{y\in F}{\uparrow}f(y)
. Then, (
3
) holds.

•

Suppose that
F\subseteq S
is admissible and so is
f[F]
but
\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}f[F]\nleq f(\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}F)
. By Lemma
6.2
,
\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{y\in F}{\uparrow}f(y)=\mathcal{A}(\mathop{\textstyle\bigcup}_{y\in F}{\uparrow}f(y))={\uparrow}\mathop{\textstyle\bigwedge}f[F]
. Then, (
3
) holds once again.∎

Lemma 6.4
.

For a frame
L
, for
(x,y)\in L\times L
and
\{(x_{i},y_{i})\}_{i\in I}\subseteq L\times L
:

\mathfrak{c}(x)\cap\mathfrak{o}(y)\subseteq\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{i}\mathfrak{c}(x_{i})\cap\mathfrak{o}(y_{i})\text{ if and only if }\mathit{lc}(x,y)\in\mathcal{A}({\uparrow}\{\mathit{lc}(x_{i},y_{i})\mid i\in I\}).

Proof.

Whenever
(x,y),(x_{i},y_{i})\in\mathsf{LC}(L)
, the desired equivalence holds by the isomorphism in Corollary
5.2
, and by the computations of joins in
\mathcal{AU}(\mathsf{LC}(L))
. For arbitrary
(x,y),(x_{i},y_{i})\in L\times L^{op}
, it suffices to note that for every pair
(a,b)\in L\times L^{op}
, the pair
\mathit{lc}(a,b)
induces the same locally closed sublocale.
∎

By an argument completely analogous to that before Theorem
6.3
, a frame map
f:L\to M
lifts to a frame map
\overline{f}:\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)\to\mathsf{S}_{b}(M)
if and only if the following well-definedness condition holds for all
x,y\in L
and
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in I},\,\{y_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq L
.

(WDb)

\mathfrak{c}(x)\cap\mathfrak{o}(y)\subseteq\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{i}\mathfrak{c}(x_{i})\cap\mathfrak{o}(y_{i})\text{ implies }\mathfrak{c}(f(x))\cap\mathfrak{o}(f(y))\subseteq\mathop{\textstyle\bigvee}_{i}\mathfrak{c}(f(x_{i}))\cap\mathfrak{o}(f(y_{i})).

Theorem 6.5
.

A frame map
f:L\to M
lifts to a frame map
\overline{f}:\mathsf{S}_{b}(L)\to\mathsf{S}_{b}(M)
if and only if it is locally exact.

Proof.

A frame map lifts if and only if condition (
WDb
) holds. But by Lemma
6.4
, this is also equivalent to having

(x,y)\in\mathcal{A}({\uparrow}\{(x_{i},y_{i})\mid i\in I\})\text{ implies }\mathsf{LC}(f)(x,y)\in\mathcal{A}({\uparrow}\{\mathsf{LC}(f)(x_{i},y_{i})\mid i\in I\}),

using that
\mathit{lc}(f(x_{i}),f(y_{i}))=\mathsf{LC}(f)(x_{i},y_{i})
. This is equivalent to the map
\mathsf{LC}(f)
being admissible, as it is a special case of condition (
WD
).
∎

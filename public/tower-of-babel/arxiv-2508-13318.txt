Quantum Walk on a Line with Absorbing Boundaries
Ammara Ammara, Václav Potoček, Martin Štefaňák, Francesco V. Pepe

Abstract
Absorption in one-parameter family of two-state quantum walks on a finite line is investigated. We consider a symmetric configuration, with two sinks located at N N and − N -N and the the quantum walker starting in the middle. Elaborating on the results of Konno et al., J. Phys. A: Math. Gen. 36 241 (2003), we derive closed formulas for the absorption probabilities at the boundaries in the limit of large system size N N . It is shown that the absorption depends, apart from the coin angle, only on the probability that the initial state is one of the eigenstates of the coin operator. Finally, we perform an extensive numerical investigation for small system size N N , showing that the convergence to the analytical result is exponentially fast.

II
Notation and overview of the existing results

We begin by formally describing our discrete-time quantum walk model. We consider the propagation of a quantum walker on a finite discrete line with absorbing barriers (sinks) located at both ends. The vertices of this line are labeled from
-N
to
N
(
N\geq 2
), with absorbing sinks placed at vertices
-N
and
N
.
The Hilbert space for the quantum walk is defined as a tensor product:

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{P}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C},

(1)

where
\mathcal{H}_{P}
is the position Hilbert space spanned by basis vectors
|m\rangle
,
m=-N,\dots,N
, representing the position of the quantum walker. The coin space
\mathcal{H}_{C}
describes the internal degree of freedom (coin state) of the walker, which in our case is a two-dimensional space spanned by vectors
|L\rangle
and
|R\rangle
corresponding to left and right directions, respectively.

Initially, the walker is placed at position
0
with an initial coin state
|\psi_{C}\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{C}
given by

\ket{\psi_C}=a\ket{L}+b\ket{R},\quad|a|^{2}+|b|^{2}=1.

(2)

Each discrete-time step of the quantum walk consists of two operations: a coin flip followed by a shift. The evolution operator per step is given by:

\hat{U}=\hat{S}(\hat{I}_{P}\otimes\hat{C}),

(3)

where the shift operator
\hat{S}
conditionally propagates the walker left or right depending on the coin state

\hat{S}=\sum_{x=-N}^{N}\big(|x-1\rangle\langle x|\otimes|L\rangle\langle L|+|x+1\rangle\langle x|\otimes|R\rangle\langle R|\big).

(4)

The coin operator
\hat{C}
acts only on the coin space
\mathcal{H}_{C}
.
In our study, we only consider a one-parameter family of coin matrices

\hat{C}(\theta)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&\sin\theta\\
\sin\theta&-\cos\theta\end{pmatrix},\quad\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}).

(5)

In principle, the coin could be an arbitrary
\rm U(2)
operator. However, the global phase has no physical significance and the relative phases between coin matrix elements were shown to be largely irrelevant for the two-state quantum walk models
Tregenna et al. 2003
;
Sandeep et al. 2015
, as they can be compensated by the relative phase between the amplitudes of the initial coin state (
2
).
Namely, any
\rm U(2)
operator can be written in the form

\hat{C}(\theta,\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(e^{i\alpha},e^{i\beta})\hat{C}(\theta)\exp\left(i\gamma\sigma_{z}\right)

(6)

due to a slight modification of Pauli decomposition, but it is easily shown that a quantum walk with coin
\hat{C}(\theta,\alpha,\beta,\gamma)
and initial coin state
\ket{\psi_C}
(in position
0
) leads to the same probability distribution in position in every step number as one with coin
\hat{C}(\theta)
and initial coin state
\exp(-i \gamma\sigma_z)\ket{\psi_C}
. If
\ket{\psi_C}
is allowed to be arbitrary in our model, then the restricted coin (
5
) is without loss of generality.

In (
5
), the angle
\theta
is a tunable parameter governing the effective spreading speed of the quantum walk
Miyazaki et al. 2007
;
Kempf and Portugal 2009
;
Bezděková et al. 2015
.
The special case
\theta=\pi/4
corresponds to the well-known Hadamard coin, which has been extensively studied in the literature. We omit the trivial boundary cases
\theta=0,\,\frac{\pi}{2}
, which can be treated in a straightforward way. Indeed, for
\theta=0
the coin (
5
) is diagonal and the quantum walker propagates deterministically according to the amplitudes of the initial state (
2
). Absorption probability on the left and right is given by
|a|^{2}
and
|b|^{2}
, respectively. On the other hand, for
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
the coin (
5
) becomes a Pauli
\sigma_{x}
matrix and the quantum walker is bound to positions
-1,0,1
. Since we consider
N\geq 2
, the walker never reaches the absorbing boundaries.

After each unitary step, the absorbing barriers are accounted for by applying a projection operator
\hat{\Pi}_{N}
, which removes (absorbs) the amplitude at vertices
-N
and
N

\hat{\Pi}_{N}=(\hat{I}_{P}-|N\rangle\langle N|-|-N\rangle\langle-N|)\otimes\hat{I}_{C}.

(7)

Thus, the complete non-unitary evolution after
t
steps is given by

|\psi(t)\rangle=(\hat{\Pi}_{N}\hat{U})^{t}|\psi(0)\rangle.

(8)

Due to the absorption the square norm of the state vector is not preserved and for
t\geq N
is strictly less than 1. This norm represents the survival probability that the walker has not yet been absorbed at time
t
, and we can write it in the form

\|\psi(t)\|^{2}=1-\left(P_{L}(t)+P_{R}(t)\right),

(9)

where
P_{L}(t)
and
P_{R}(t)
are the cumulative absorption probabilities at the left and right absorbing boundaries, respectively. Since for two-state quantum walks there are no dark-states, i.e. localized eigenstates of the evolution operator with no support on the sink vertices
Konno et al. 2021
, the survival probability tends to zero in the limit of large number of steps
t
. Hence, left and right absorption probabilities add up to 1 in the asymptotic limit

\lim_{t\to\infty}P_{L}(t)+\lim_{t\to\infty}P_{R}(t)=P_{L}+P_{R}=1.

(10)

The asymptotic value of the left absorption probability
P_{L}
was studied in
Konno et al. 2002
, where it was shown that it can be expressed in the form

P_{L}=C_{1}|a|^{2}+C_{2}|b|^{2}+2\mathrm{Re}(C_{3}\overline{a}b).

(11)

Here
a
and
b
are amplitudes of the initial coin state (
2
) and the coefficients
C_{j}
are given by the integrals (see Theorem 2 from
Konno et al. 2002
)

\displaystyle C_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|\cos\theta\,p^{(2N)}_{N}(e^{i\phi})+\sin\theta\,r^{(2N)}_{N}(e^{i\phi})\right|^{2}\,d\phi,

\displaystyle C_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|\sin\theta\,p^{(2N)}_{N}(e^{i\phi})-\cos\theta\,r^{(2N)}_{N}(e^{i\phi})\right|^{2}\,d\phi,

\displaystyle C_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\overline{\left(\cos\theta\,p^{(2N)}_{N}(e^{i\phi})+\sin\theta\,r^{(2N)}_{N}(e^{i\phi})\right)}\left(\sin\theta\,p^{(2N)}_{N}(e^{i\phi})-\cos\theta\,r^{(2N)}_{N}(e^{i\phi})\right)d\phi.

(12)

We kept the notation
p_{i}^{(j)}
,
r_{i}^{(j)}
used in
Konno et al. 2002
, where the lower index
i
indicates the starting point of the walk, while the upper index
j
denotes the position of the right absorbing barrier. Note that

considers the left sink to be located at 0. Our symmetric configuration with sinks at
\pm N
and initial vertex 0 is equivalent to

and

. The explicit form of the functions

and

read

\displaystyle p^{(2N)}_{N}(z)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{z}{2}+E_{z}\right)\lambda_{+}^{N-1}+\left(\frac{z}{2}-E_{z}\right)\lambda_{-}^{N-1},

\displaystyle r^{(2N)}_{N}(z)

\displaystyle=C_{z}\left(\lambda_{+}-\lambda_{-}\right),

(13)

where
\lambda_{\pm}
are given by

\lambda_{\pm}=\frac{z^{2}-1\pm\sqrt{1+z^{4}+2\cos(2\theta)z^{2}}}{2\cos\theta z},

(14)

and
C_{z}
and
E_{z}
are determined from the equations

\displaystyle C_{z}\,y_{2N-2}

\displaystyle=z\cos\theta\,C_{z}\,y_{2N-3}-z\sin\theta\,\left(\frac{z}{2}\,x_{1}+E_{z}\,y_{1}\right),

\displaystyle C_{z}\,y_{1}

\displaystyle=z\sin\theta\,\left(\frac{z}{2}\,x_{2N-2}+E_{z}\,y_{2N-2}\right).

(15)

Here we have introduced

\displaystyle x_{n}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{+}^{n}+\lambda_{-}^{n},

\displaystyle y_{n}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{+}^{n}-\lambda_{-}^{n},

(16)

to simplify the notation.

The value
P_{R}
is related to
P_{L}
by arguments of parity symmetry. Let
\hat{P}
denote an operator of the form

\displaystyle\hat{P}\ket{x, R}

\displaystyle=\ket{-x, L}

(17)

\displaystyle\hat{P}\ket{x, L}

\displaystyle=\ket{-x, R}.

This operator commutes with both the shift operator
\hat{S}
and the projector
\hat{\Pi}_{N}
. For the coin, we have

\hat{P}(\hat{I}_{P}\otimes\hat{C}(\theta))=\hat{I}_{P}\otimes(\sigma_{x}\hat{C}(\theta)\sigma_{x})=\hat{I}_{P}\otimes\hat{C}(\pi-\theta)

so that

\hat{P}(\hat{\Pi}_{N}\hat{S}\hat{C}(\theta))^{t}\ket{\psi_0}=(\hat{\Pi}_{N}\hat{S}\hat{C}(\pi-\theta))^{t}\hat{P}\ket{\psi_0}.

(19)

For initial state localized in origin,
\hat{P}\ket{\psi_0}=\ket{0}\otimes(\sigma_{x}\ket{\psi_C})
.
Nevertheless, it also holds that

\hat{C}(\pi-\theta)=\sigma_{z}\hat{C}(\theta)\sigma_{z}=\hat{C}\left(\theta,-i\frac{\pi}{2},i\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}\right)

(20)

in the notation of (
6
), so by an extension of the argument above, the quantum walk can be reflected about origin (in terms of the position probability distribution) without changing the coin, only the initial coin state, the latter to

\ket{\smash{\tilde\psi_C}}=\exp\left(-i\frac{\pi}{2}\sigma_{z}\right)\sigma_{x}\ket{\psi_C}=\sigma_{y}\ket{\psi_C}.

(21)

Since the parity operator swaps left for right, the expression (

of the original walk if the corresponding coefficients

\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{a}\\
\tilde{b}\end{pmatrix}=\sigma_{y}\begin{pmatrix}a\\

(22)

are plugged in in place of

(23)

(47)

Integrand of (

) as a function of

\langle\frac{\pi}{4},\frac{3\pi}{4}\rangle
and

\cos(2\phi)<-\cos(2\theta)
for this choice of

.

Second, the integrand of
{\cal I}
is negligible when
\cos{(2\phi)}<-\cos{(2\theta)}
, i.e. for
\phi
in the interval
\langle\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta,\frac{\pi}{2}+\theta\rangle\cup\langle\frac{3\pi}{2}-\theta,\frac{3\pi}{2}+\theta\rangle
, see Figure
1
for illustration. Indeed,
c(\phi)
is pure imaginary in this region (
41
). Considering
c(\phi)=i\sinh{\alpha}
we obtain

T_{2N-1}(i\sinh{\alpha})^{2}=-\sinh^{2}((2N-1)\alpha),

(48)

which grows rapidly in absolute value, quickly dominating the
c(\phi)^{2}=-\sinh^{2}{\alpha}
terms. We can thus reduce the integration domain to the intervals where
\cos{(2\phi)}>-\cos{(2\theta)}
. Utilizing the fact that the integrand is identical for
\phi\in\langle\frac{\pi}{2}+\theta,\frac{3\pi}{2}-\theta\rangle
and
\phi\in\langle 0,\frac{\pi}{2}-\theta\rangle\cup\langle\frac{3\pi}{2}+\theta,2\pi\rangle
, we obtain an approximation of
\cal I

{\cal I}\approx\frac{1}{4\pi}\int\limits_{\pi/2+\theta}^{3\pi/2-\theta}\frac{\cos\theta\,c(\phi)^{2}}{\cos^{2}\theta\,c(\phi)^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\,T_{2N-1}(c(\phi))^{2}}d\phi.

(49)

In this interval
c(\phi)
is real (
40
). Considering
c(\phi)=\cos{\alpha}
, the Chebyshev polynomial in the numerator
T_{2N-1}(c(\phi))^{2}=\cos^{2}((2N-1)\alpha)
oscillates rapidly so that we can consider
\cos{\alpha}
to be constant over one period. The integrand of
\cal I
can then be replaced by its mean value

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\cos\theta\,A^{2}}{\cos^{2}\theta\,A^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\,\cos^{2}x}dx=\frac{A}{\sqrt{\cos^{2}\theta A^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta}},

(50)

resulting in the final approximation

{\cal I}\approx\frac{1}{4\pi}\int\limits_{\pi/2+\theta}^{3\pi/2-\theta}\frac{c(\phi)}{\sqrt{\cos^{2}\theta\,c(\phi)^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta}}d\phi.

(51)

Using the substitution

\displaystyle c(\phi)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\cos(2\phi)+\cos(2\theta)}}{\sqrt{2}\cos\theta}=\cos{\alpha},

\displaystyle\cos(2\phi)

\displaystyle=1-2\cos^{2}\theta\sin^{2}\alpha,

\displaystyle d\phi

\displaystyle=\frac{2\cos^{2}\theta\,\sin\alpha\,\cos\alpha}{\sqrt{1-(1-2\cos^{2}\theta\,\sin^{2}\alpha)^{2}}}d\alpha,

(52)

we obtain the explicit form of the integral

\displaystyle{\cal I}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{4\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\cos\theta\cos^{2}\alpha}{1-\cos^{2}\theta\sin^{2}\alpha}d\alpha=\frac{\cos\theta}{2(1+\sin\theta)}.

Hence, for large
N
the coefficients
C_{j}
can be approximated by

\displaystyle C_{1}

\displaystyle\approx 1-\frac{1}{2}\sin\theta,

\displaystyle C_{2}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{2}\sin\theta,

\displaystyle C_{3}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{\sin\theta\,(1-\sin\theta)}{2\cos\theta}.

(53)

To further simplify the absorption probability (
11
) we express the initial coin state of the walk in the eigenbasis of the coin operator (
5
). The eigenvectors
\ket{\theta^\pm}
satisfying
\hat{C}(\theta)\ket{\theta^\pm}=\pm\ket{\theta^\pm}
are given by

\displaystyle\ket{\theta^-}

\displaystyle=-\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\ket{L}+\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\ket{R},

\displaystyle\ket{\theta^+}

\displaystyle=\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\ket{L}+\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\ket{R}.

(54)

Writing the initial coin state as

\ket{\psi_c}=\rho\ket{\theta^-}+e^{i\beta}\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}\ket{\theta^+},

(55)

we find that the amplitudes in the standard basis
a
and
b
are given by

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=-\rho\sin\frac{\theta}{2}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}e^{i\beta}\cos\frac{\theta}{2},

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=\rho\cos\frac{\theta}{2}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}e^{i\beta}\sin\frac{\theta}{2}.

(56)

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle|a|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+\cos\theta}{2}-\rho^{2}\cos\theta-\rho\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}\cos\beta\sin\theta,

\displaystyle|b|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\cos\theta}{2}+\rho^{2}\cos\theta+\rho\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}\cos\beta\sin\theta,

\displaystyle 2\,\mathrm{Re}(\overline{a}b)

\displaystyle=(1-2\rho^{2})\sin\theta+2\rho\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}\cos\beta\cos\theta.

(57)

Utilizing these expressions together with (
53
) we find that the absorption probability at the left end of the line (
11
) reads

P_{L}=\frac{1}{1+\tan\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)}-\frac{1-\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}\rho^{2}.

(58)

We see that the result is independent of the relative phase
\beta
. The absorption probability is thus determined by the coin parameter
\theta
and the probability
p=\rho^{2}
that the initial state
\ket{\psi_c}
is the eigenstate
\ket{\theta^-}
. The absorption probability at the right end of the line is then a complement to unity

P_{R}=\frac{1}{1+\cot\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)}+\frac{1-\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}\rho^{2}.

(59)

Note that for
p=\rho^{2}=1/2
we obtain
P_{L}=P_{R}=1/2
, independent of
\theta
. For the Hadamard walk (
\theta=\pi/4
) we find

\displaystyle P_{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}-(\sqrt{2}-1)\rho^{2},

(60)

\displaystyle P_{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}+(\sqrt{2}-1)\rho^{2}.

The formulas (
58
) and (
59
) hold for
\theta=0
as well. In this case
\ket{\theta^-}=\ket{R}
,
\ket{\theta^+}=\ket{L}
and
a=\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}e^{i\beta}
,
b=\rho
, leading to
P_{L}=|a|^{2}
as discussed before. For
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
the expressions (
58
) and (
59
) are not valid.

V
Conclusions

Absorption of quantum walk on finite line was investigated in detail. The symmetry of the studied configuration allowed us to derive closed formulas for the probabilities of absorption at the end points in the limit of large system size
N
. Utilizing the decomposition of the initial condition into the eigenstates of the coin operator, we have shown that the absorption probabilities are independent of the relative phase in the initial state. Numerical analysis of small systems shows that the convergence to the analytical results is exponential in
N
for all settings of coin and initial state parameters. For the Hadamard walk, the difference between the asymptotic and numerical results is less than
10^{-3}
already for
N=5
.

It is an open question if such simple formulas can be derived when the symmetry between the left and right sinks is broken, i.e. when the initial vertex is not exactly in middle of the line. One can also consider lazy walk model which leads to trapping
Inui et al. 2005
, where the walker has a non-vanishing probability to survive even in the asymptotic limit of large number of steps. While the total absorption probability was investigated before
Štefaňák et al. 2016
, the absorption in the left and right sink individually is unknown.

Finally, it would be interesting to see an experimental realization of quantum walks models with absorption. The photonic time-multiplexing setup
Nitsche et al. 2016
provides a variable platform which allows to investigate different system sizes
N
in a straightforward way. Sinks at desired positions
\pm N
can be implemented with deterministic out-coupling
Nitsche et al. 2018
utilizing programmable electro-optical modulators.

Mediating role of inflammatory biomarkers on the association of physical activity, sedentary behaviour with chronic kidney disease: a cross-sectional study in NHANES 2007–2018
Authors: Pan Peng, Zhisheng Liu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesThis study aims to investigate the association of physical activity (PA) and sedentary behaviour (SB) with chronic kidney disease (CKD), and to illustrate whether inflammatory biomarkers play a mediating role.DesignA cross-sectional study.SettingThis study analysed cross-sectional data from the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey 2007–2018.ParticipantsA total of 27 808 participants aged 20–80 years old were enrolled in the final analysis.Outcome measuresPA and SB were self-reported by participants using the Global Physical Activity Questionnaire. Inflammatory biomarkers including neutrophil/lymphocyte ratio (NLR), platelet/lymphocyte ratio (PLR) and systemic immune inflammation index (SII) were examined in subjects’ peripheral blood samples. Estimated glomerular filtration rate <60 mL/min/1.73 m2 was defined as CKD. Multivariable logistic regression and medication model were performed to examine the associations of PA, SB and inflammatory biomarkers with CKD.ResultsPA was associated with CKD, with a 20% lower OR of CKD compared with inactive (p=0.002) and a 2% lower OR per 30 min moderate-to-vigorous PA increase (p=0.027). SB was also associated with CKD, with a significant 39% higher OR for high SB (p<0.001), and a 3% higher OR per 30 min SB increase (p<0.001). NLR and SII both significantly mediated the association of PA and SB with CKD. NLR explained 1.87% (medication effect: −0.0003, direct effect: −0.0162) and 2.73% (medication effect: 0.0005, direct effect: 0.0192) association of PA and SB with CKD (p<0.001), respectively. While SII explained 1.77% (medication effect: −0.0003, direct effect: −0.0162) and 1.22% (medication effect: 0.0002, direct effect: 0.0199) association of PA and SB with CKD (p<0.001), respectively. The results did not show any statistical association between PLR and CKD in this sample.ConclusionThis finding revealed increasing PA and shortening SB may be effective strategies in CKD prevention and early management through the regulation of systemic inflammation. NLR and SII may not only be prognostic indicators of CKD, but also novel potential markers in predicting CKD occurrence.

Introduction

Chronic kidney disease (CKD) has become a new global public health crisis in recent years. According to the Global Burden of Disease, CKD ranked 18th among the world’s leading causes in 2019, up 11 places since 1990,1 and is projected to be in the top five conditions contributing to years of life lost by 2040.2 Although CKD has been identified as a risk factor for many adverse outcomes, most notably cardiovascular disease, hospital admissions and premature death, it remains under-recognised by both patients and providers, with a truly low awareness rate.3 Hence, studies focusing on the prevention and management of CKD are still needed to help rise the medical and social challenges of CKD.

Physical activity (PA) and sedentary behaviour (SB) confer extensive influence across the spectrum of kidney disease, including CKD. A meta-analysis, which enrolled 12 observational cohort studies comprising 1 281 727 participants and 66 217 CKD events, found that individuals who are most physically active have a lowered risk of CKD compared with those who are not or least physically active.4 Recently, the analyses of the UK Biobank,5 the China Kadoorie Biobank6 and the Korean Genome and Epidemiology Study Database7 also revealed a significant associations between PA and risk of CKD, while SB is considered a hazardous factor of CKD occurrence and development.8 However, the potential mechanisms underlying these observations were not well established based on the current evidence.

Substantial evidence suggests that renal inflammation is a crucial factor in the onset and advancement of CKD.9 Patients with CKD always underwent systemic inflammatory disorders,10 what’s more, studies have already identified the epidemiological and genetic associations between serum levels of systemic inflammation with the incidence and progress of CKD.1012 While, PA was always regarded as important regulators of inflammation status in human body which influences the delicate balance between pro-inflammatory and anti-inflammatory processes notably through the suppression of pro-inflammatory cytokines and the enhancement of anti-inflammatory mediators,13 14 and increasing amounts of SB were associated with unfavourable alterations to the inflammatory profile.15 This led us to speculate whether inflammation play a role between PA and SB with CKD. Although no direct evidence exists, previous studies have found that PA improved kidney function by regulating oxidative stress, hormone component and inflammatory mediators.1618 Considering the above evidences, it is reasonable to presume that inflammatory status may contribute to the effect of PA and SB on CKD. However, till now, relevant studies have focused on only two elements of this topic, which were associations of PA and SB with inflammation,13 PA and SB with CKD4 and inflammation with CKD.9 No studies have explored the link among the three elements especially in large sample, which may provide new insights into the biological mechanisms underlying the effect of PA and SB on CKD. This study aimed to illustrate the mediating role of inflammatory biomarkers on the association of PA and SB with CKD, in a large population-based cross-sectional study. We obtained data from the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) 2007–2018 and first analysed the association of PA and SB with CKD by conducting survey-weighted and multivariable-adjusted logistic regression, then explored the mediating role of inflammatory biomarkers on the association of PA and SB with CKD by conducting regression-based mediation analysis to examine both direct and indirect mediated effects.

Methods

Patient and public involvement

Patients or the public were not involved in the design, or conduct, or reporting, or dissemination plans of our research.

Study population

This study analysed cross-sectional data from NHANES 2007–2018 (https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/nhanes/index.htm).19 The NHANES is a nationally comprehensive population-based survey designed for the collection of civilian population data in the USA, with a multistage probability sampling design.

Physical activity and sedentary behaviour

The Compendium of Physical Activities (Compendium), widely accepted to estimate and classify the energy cost of human PA, provides a coding scheme linking categories and types of PA with their respective metabolic equivalent intensity values.20 Based on the Compendium, NHANES used the Global Physical Activity Questionnaire (GPAQ)21 with PA divided into three common areas including occupation PA (OPA), transportation PA (TPA) and leisure time physical activity (LTPA), to estimate participants’ self-reported moderate-to-vigorous PA (MVPA) in each PA domain. OPA was defined as all work-related PA such as household chores and construction work, while TPA included bicycling and walking for transportation, and LTPA was those related to sports, fitness and other recreational activities. Participants were asked about the amount of time per day and the number of days they spent in moderate (defined as activities that require moderate physical effort and cause small increases in breathing or heart rate in GPAQ) as well as vigorous intensity activities (defined as activities that require hard physical effort and cause large increases in breathing or heart rate in GPAQ) in different PA domains in a typical week. Minutes of vigorous PA were doubled and added to minutes of moderate PA based on the Compendium. Then, weekly MVPA of different PA domains was calculated by multiplying the amount of time per day and the number of days spent in MVPA in each PA domains. The weekly time for all three domains was summed to obtain a total weekly MVPA. According to the WHO Guidance 2020,22 the total weekly MVPA was further dichotomised into (1) inactive (0–149 min, did not achieve guidelines for PA) and (2) active (150 min or more, achieved guidelines for PA). Daily SB time was asked using how much time do you usually spend sitting on a typical day (not including sleep time), and then dichotomised into (1) low SB (less than 480 min) and (2) high SB (480 min or more) based on the cut-offs for adverse health effects of sedentary behaviour observed in previous pooled work.23 24

Ascertain of CKD

In this study, we used serum creatinine with the Chronic Kidney Disease Epidemiology Collaboration equation to estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), which was validated to be more accurate than the equation given in the Modification of Diet in Renal Disease study25 across a wide variety of populations and clinical conditions.26 CKD was defined as an eGFR <60 mL/min/1.73 m2.5

Inflammatory biomarkers

Systemic immune inflammation index (SII), neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) and platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR) have been validated to be feasible in predicting the prognosis of various cancers, inflammatory diseases, as well as CKD.27 These factors are easily available in clinical settings and offer a comprehensive reflection of both local immune status and systemic inflammation condition. In this study, SII, NLR and PLR,28 which defined as platelet count*neutrophil count/lymphocyte count,29 were examined in subjects’ peripheral blood samples obtained from the NHANES Mobile Examination Center. The analysis and handling details of sample are available at https://wwwn.cdc.gov/nchs/nhanes/Default.aspx.

Other covariates

Potential confounders included in this study were sociodemographic data, behaviours factors and clinical characteristics. Sociodemographic data contain age, gender, education (less than college, college or above), race/ethnicity (Mexican Hispanic, other Hispanic, non-Hispanic white, non-Hispanic black and other race) and poverty income ratio (PIR) (<1, 1–1.99, 2–3.99 and ≥4). The PIR was based on the Department of Health and Human Services’ poverty measure, and calculated by dividing family income by poverty guidelines specific to family size, year and state. Behaviours factors included smoking, alcohol and body mass index. Smoking status was classified as never (have never smoked), former (have smoked at least 100 cigarettes) and current (smoking at the present time). Alcohol status was defined as current (drink in last year), never (had <12 drinks in 1 year), former (did not drink last year but drank in in 1 year). Clinical characteristics included dyslipidaemia, hypertension, diabetes, coronary heart disease, heart attack and stroke. Dyslipidaemia was defined as having at least one of the following: high-density lipoprotein <40 mg/dL, total cholesterol ≥240 mg/dL, triglycerides ≥200 mg/dL, medical history of dyslipidaemia or medication usage.30 Hypertension was defined as having at least one of the following: systolic blood pressure ≥140 mm Hg, diastolic blood pressure ≥90 mm Hg and medical history of hypertension or medication usage.31 Diabetes was defined as having at least one of the following: being told by a doctor or health professional that they had diabetes, blood glycosylated haemoglobin (HbA1c) ≥6.5%, fasting plasma glucose ≥126 mg/dL or random plasma glucose ≥200 mg/dL.32 Coronary heart disease, stroke and heart attack were defined as being told by a doctor or health professional that they have the disease.

Statistical analysis

Considering the complex multistage sampling design of the NHANES, the merged weights were represented as WT07-18=(1/6) ×WTMEC2 YR07–08+(1/6)×WTMEC2 YR09–10+(1/6)×WTMEC2 YR11–12+(1/6)×WTMEC2 YR13–14+(1/6)×WTMEC2 YR15–16+(1/6)×WTMEC2 YR17–18. Continuous and categorical variables were described as mean±SD and number frequency (%), respectively. Differences of all variables between groups were compared using independent sample t-test for continuous variables and the χ2 test for categorical variables.

In the main analyses, logistic regression was performed to examine the associations of PA, SB, inflammatory biomarkers with CKD. PA and SB were both analysed as continuous variable (daily total MVPA, daily total sedentary time) for 30 min increment per day and dichotomous variable (inactive and active, low and high SB). For the ascertain of confounders, we conducted the widely used change-in-estimate strategy to test the potential role of putative confounders.33 The change-in-estimate strategy tested each potential confounder by adding it to crude model, only variables showing a change of 10% or more, compared with the crude model were retained in the final model. In this study, we considered age, gender and race as prior confounders, and tested other putative confounders one by one. The testing results of putative confounders were shown in supplementary materials (online supplemental tables S1 and S2). Three models were then conducted after the confounder testing. Model 1 was univariate, Model 2 was additionally adjusted for age, gender and race, Model 3 was adjusted for age, gender, race and the corresponding retained confounders (based on the corresponding independent variables). OR and 95% CIs were estimated. We then conducted mediation analysis to calculate the direct effect (PA, SB to CKD) and the indirect effect (PA, SB to CKD mediated by inflammatory markers), the proportion mediated indicated that the percentage of the mediating effect was estimated. We also conducted sensitive analysis by excluding participants by excluding participants received dialysis in the past 12 months. All analysis were conducted through Stata/MP 16.0. P<0.05 indicated a significant difference.

Discussion

This study investigated the associations of PA and SB with CKD, and the role of inflammatory markers as mediators in a nationally representative adult sample. Our results suggested that both PA and SB were associated with CKD, and for the first time, we found that NLR and SII both partly mediated the association of PA and SB with CKD.

The association of PA and SB with CKD has been widely studied recently. Analysis of 448 444 UK Biobank participants demonstrated significant associations of both questionnaire-based total PA and MVPA, accelerometer-based moderate-intensity PA and vigorous PA, with the incidence of CKD.5 Similar associations were also observed in several Asian cohorts,6 7 one of which enrolled 475 376 adults from the China Kadoorie Biobank with a median follow-up of 12.1 years and found that PA, including non-occupational PA, low-intensity PA and moderate-to-vigorous-intensity PA, was inversely associated with the risk of CKD.6 For SB, data from the Korea National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey, included 9534 participants, revealed that excessive SB time was associated with an increased risk of CKD,8 the similar association was also observed in US (the Framingham Heart Study, 1268 participants)34 and Japanese samples (4457 participants).35 Although this study only investigated questionnaire-based PA and SB, our analysis of 27 808 participants still showed consistent results with the above, which involved both objectively and questionnaire-based PA and SB measurements, all revealed the protective effect of PA and the hazardous effect of SB on CKD.

Inflammation affects the development of kidney injury, directly and indirectly with overlaps, which may ultimately contribute to chronic impairment of kidney function.36 Specifically, the production-clearance imbalance of proinflammatory cytokines is a major cause of chronic inflammation under the conditions of impaired renal function, and the absence of anti-inflammatory factors further contributes to the development and progression of CKD.36 In recent years, these novel inflammation markers derived from peripheral blood cell analysis have drawn much attention and are regarded as prognostic indicators of various chronic diseases, including CKD. Our results demonstrated that inflammatory biomarkers NLR and SII were both associated with CKD, while PLR was not. Sevencan et al found that NLR level was significantly associated with CKD development and had a high specificity in predicting eGFR, while no statistically significant association of PLR was detected,37 which was similar to another study that showed significant difference in NLR level but not PLR between different CKD stages.38 However, Gan et al pooled data of 11 studies (2683 participants with 1743 patients with CKD and 895 non-CKD participants) and suggested that PLR values were significantly higher in patients with CKD than in non-CKD control.39 In fact, our results of descriptive statistics also showed a significantly higher PLR value in patients with CKD compared with non-CKD, while our further multivariable-adjusted regression provided a stricter estimate of association between PLR and CKD. The non-statistical association between PLR and CKD may in part be explained by the possible changes in platelets functions with ageing, as our sample ranged widely from 20 to 80 years old. More future prospective studies are needed to further explore the associations and underline mechanism. SII is a newly defined inflammatory biomarker which considering neutrophil, lymphocyte and platelet counts together, comprehensively reflecting the inflammatory and immune status of patients with various renal diseases.40 41 Recently, a study identified a linear dose-response relationship between continuous SII levels and CKD, suggested that SII can serve as an independent risk factor of CKD,9 which echoed our results. The above evidence revealed that, inflammatory biomarkers including NLR and SII may not only be prognostic indicators of CKD,42 but also novel potential markers in predicting CKD occurrence, which may show a promising application for early management and pave the way for new preventive strategies.

PA and SB, known as important behavioural regulators of inflammation in the human body,43 have been widely recognised for their strong causal relationship with health outcomes. Substantial evidence revealed that inflammatory biomarkers are the mediators of the relationship between PA, SB and some health outcomes.4450 In this study, we investigated that whether inflammatory biomarkers NLR and SII mediate the relationship between PA, SB and CKD in nationwide population-based sample. For the first time, our results showed that both SII and NLR played a mediator role between PA, SB and CKD. Consistent with ours, You et al also observed a strong mediation associations of NLR, SII between SB and sleep disturbance.44 However, no other study has yet focused on the mediating role of these novel cellular immune inflammation markers between CKD and other health issues. Most of the relevant studies are concerned about biomarker that usually reflects acute inflammation such as C reactive protein4649 and interleukin 6.13 48 Although the evidence is scarce, Walzik et al discussed in depth the values of the integrative inflammation markers NLR, PLR and SIIs responsiveness to physical activity through both sports performance settings (inferring information for exercise programming by reflecting exercise strain and recovery status or periods of overtraining and increased infection risk) and health setting (application involves the depiction of anti-inflammatory effects of chronic physical activity in patients in chronic inflammation status).51 Further, returning to CKD and its underlying mechanism, the available evidence supports that physical activity induces various improvements in CKD via muscle-derived mediators, which are multifaceted. A potential and overarching mechanism related to many beneficial effects seems to be the reduction in chronic systemic inflammation in CKD after physical activity.52 Interestingly, recent studies have shown that miRNA may link the inflammatory cytokines to PA in participants who underwent kidney replacement therapy.53 Overall, the finding of the current study provided extra evidence to the above integrative reviews, however, prospective studies are needed to confirm the mechanisms and add more evidence to the CKD management.

It is needed to note that this study has several limitations, and the results should be interpreted with caution. First of all, this is a cross-sectional study that unable to reveal a causal relationship, although a large sample was enrolled. Second, the self-reported PA may be overestimated while SB may be underestimated according to the previous study.54 Also, we cannot further investigate the associations of patterns of PA and SB (the way PA and SB accumulate) with CKD. In addition, we used only blood-based diagnosis of CKD and did not exclude those received kidney transplant due to the lack of data. We also did not analyse underlying nephropathy. Further relevant studies are needed to address these limitations.

Conclusion

Our cross-sectional study found that PA and SB were significantly associated with CKD based on the analysis of 27 808 adults, and for the first time, we observed the mediating role of novel integrative inflammatory markers NLR and SII between PA, SB and CKD. This finding revealed that NLR and SII may not only be prognostic indicators of CKD, but also novel potential markers in predicting CKD occurrence. Moreover, increasing PA and shortening SB may be effective strategies in CKD prevention and early management through the regulation of systemic inflammation. More multicentre and prospective studies are needed to illustrate the casual relationship and target volume of PA and SB in CKD management.

Relative Hochster–Takayama formula and Cohen–Macaulay monomial ideal quotients
Tài Huy Hà, Nguyen Cong Minh

Abstract
. Hochster’s and Takayama’s formulas describes the multigraded components of local cohomology modules of monomial ideals in terms of simplicial complexes. In this paper, we develop a relative version of these formulas for quotients I / J I/J of monomial ideals, expressing the multigraded pieces of local cohomology modules of I / J I/J as reduced relative (co)homology of pairs of degree complexes. As an application, we obtain a relative Reisner criterion characterizing Cohen-Macaulay monomial ideal quotients. We further apply this relative Hochster–Takayama framework to modules arising from symbolic power filtrations, including symbolic quotients I ( t ) / I ( t + 1 ) I^{(t)}/I^{(t+1)} and symbolic-ordinary discrepancy module I ( t ) / I t I^{(t)}/I^{t} . In particular, for a squarefree monomial ideal I I , we give a precise classification of when I ( t ) / I ( t + 1 ) I^{(t)}/I^{(t+1)} is Cohen-Macaulay for all or, equivalently, for some t ≥ 2 t\geq 2 . When I I is the edge ideal of a graph, we characterize the Cohen-Macaulayness of I ( t ) / I t I^{(t)}/I^{t} for all or, equivalently, for some sufficiently large t t , and analyze the behavior of its dimension function.

1.
Introduction

The interplay between combinatorial structures and algebraic properties is a recurring theme in the study of monomial ideals. For squarefree monomial ideals
J\subseteq I
, the quotient
I/J
admits a powerful combinatorial description via the
relative simplicial complex

(\Delta,\Gamma)
, where
\Delta
and
\Gamma
are Stanley-Reisner complexes associated to
I
and
J
. This connection is established through the
relative Stanley-Reisner theory
, initially developed in
[
21
]
and further extended in
[
1
]
in their studies of the upper bound conjectures and theorems.
This theory reveals a deep correspondence: analogous to the familiar Stanley-Reisner correspondence for single squarefree monomial ideals, the Hilbert series of
I/J
can be directly understood from the
f
-vector (or
h
-vector) of the relative complex
(\Delta,\Gamma)
. Moreover, a relative Hochster’s formula exists, providing a method to compute the multi-graded pieces of local cohomology modules of
I/J
.

While relative Stanley-Reisner theory provides a comprehensive framework for quotients of squarefree monomial ideals, a more general approach is needed for arbitrary monomial ideal quotients. Takayama
[
23
]
introduced
degree complexes
and a generalized Hochster’s formula (cf.
[
10
]
) to compute the multi-graded pieces of local cohomology modules for any monomial ideal. Our primary goal in this paper is to bridge these two theories to formulate and study
relative degree complexes
associated to an arbitrary monomial ideal quotient
I/J
.

We will prove a
relative Hochster–Takayama formula
for relative complexes, extending Takayama’s work to general monomial ideal quotients. Leaving unexplained terminology until later, our first result is stated as follows.

Theorem
3.2
.
Let
J\subseteq I
be monomial ideals in
S={\Bbbk}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
. For each
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
{\mathbb{a}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
, there is an isomorphism of
{\Bbbk}
-vector spaces

H_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(I/J)_{{\mathbb{a}}}\cong\widetilde{H}^{i-|G_{\mathbb{a}}|-1}((\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(J),\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(I));{\Bbbk}),

and
H_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(I/J)_{{\mathbb{a}}}=(0)
if
G_{\mathbb{a}}\not\in\Delta(\sqrt{I})
. (Here,
(\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(J),\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(I))
represent the relative degree complex at the multidegree
{\mathbb{a}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
of the pair
J\subseteq I
.)

We also prove a functorial result showing that the isomorphisms of
{\Bbbk}
-vector spaces in the relative Hochster–Takayama formula are compatible with multiplication by variables on local cohomology modules. In particular, in
Theorem

3.7
, we show that for
{\mathbb{a}},{\mathbb{b}}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
, the multiplication by a monomial
{\mathbb{x}}^{\mathbb{b}}
induces a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)_{\mathbb{a}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)_{{\mathbb{a}}+{\mathbb{b}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{H}^{\,i-|G_{\mathbb{a}}|-1}\!\big(\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(J),\,\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(I)\big)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{H}^{\,i-|G_{\mathbb{a}}|-1}\!\big(\Delta_{{\mathbb{a}}+{\mathbb{b}}}(J),\,\Delta_{{\mathbb{a}}+{\mathbb{b}}}(I)\big),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdot{\mathbb{x}}^{\mathbb{b}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}

where the vertical maps are given by the relative Hochster–Takayama
formula, and the bottom horizontal map is induced by the natural inclusions
\Delta_{{\mathbb{a}}+{\mathbb{b}}}(J)\ \subseteq\ \Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(J)\text{ and }\Delta_{{\mathbb{a}}+{\mathbb{b}}}(I)\ \subseteq\ \Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(I).

As a first application of this framework, we establish a
Relative Reisner Criterion
, in
Theorem

5.2
, characterizing the Cohen-Macaulayness of monomial ideal quotients in terms of the vanishing of reduced relative homology of links in the degree complexes. This result generalizes the classical Reisner criterion (see
[
19
]
) and applies uniformly to a broad class of monomial ideal quotients.
In another application, we apply this theory to study modules arising from
symbolic power filtrations
. Symbolic powers of ideals carry important geometric information but are often harder to understand than ordinary powers. It has been an active research program to understand symbolic powers and their relationship to ordinary powers (cf.
[
4
,
7
,
11
]
and references thereafter). For a squarefree monomial ideal
I
, we study both the
symbolic quotients

I^{(t)}/I^{(t+1)}
and

symbolic-ordinary discrepancy modules

I^{(t)}/I^{t}
.

The study of the Cohen-Macaulayness of symbolic powers
I^{(t)}
, for
t\geq 1
, has a rich history. For instance, in
[
16
,
24
]
, when
I
is a squarefree monomial ideal, this property was elegantly characterized by the matroidal nature of the Stanley-Reisner complex associated
I
. We extend this matroidal characterization of
[
16
,
24
]
to show that the Cohen-Macaulayness for some (or equivalently all) symbolic quotient
I^{(t)}/I^{(t+1)}
forces the same strong combinatorial constraint on the underlying simplicial complex.

Theorem
6.2
.
Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex on
[n]
and let
I=I_{\Delta}
be its Stanley–Reisner ideal.
Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

I^{(t)}/I^{(t+1)}
is Cohen-Macaulay for all
t\geq 1
;

(2)

I^{(t)}/I^{(t+1)}
is Cohen-Macaulay for some
t\geq 2
;

(3)

\Delta
is a matroid.

Moreover, if
\dim S/I\geq 2
, then the above are further equivalent to:

(4)

I^{(t)}/I^{(t+1)}
satisfies Serre’s condition
(S_{2})
for all
t\geq 1
;

(5)

I^{(t)}/I^{(t+1)}
satisfies Serre’s condition
(S_{2})
for some
t\geq 2
.

If
\dim S/I\leq 1
, then
I^{(t)}/I^{(t+1)}
satisfies
(S_{2})
trivially for all
t\geq 1
.

A central theme of
Theorem

6.2
is rigidity: the existence
of a single relative degree complex with disconnected
0
-th homology forces
depth
1
and rules out higher Serre conditions, leading to sharp classification
and collapse results for Cohen-Macaulay,
(S_{2})
, and generalized
Cohen-Macaulay properties; see also
Remark

6.5
.

We continue to focus on symbolic-ordinary discrepancy modules
I^{(t)}/I^{t}
. The function
t\mapsto\dim I^{(t)}/I^{t}
is known to be asymptotically constant (see
[
8
]
). We focus on edge ideals of graphs and show that this dimension function is non-decreasing (see Theorem
7.8
). For unicyclic and perfect graphs we illustrate precisely where this function stabilizes (see Theorems
7.10
and
7.11
). We further give a complete classification for the Cohen-Macaulay property of
I^{(t)}/I^{t}
for all or, equivalently, sufficiently large
t
.

Theorem
8.4
.
Let
G
be a graph that is not bipartite and let
I=I(G)
be its edge ideal. The following are equivalent:

(1)

I^{(t)}/I^{t}
is Cohen-Macaulay for all
t\geq 1
;

(2)

I^{(t)}/I^{t}
is Cohen-Macaulay for some
t\geq\max\{t(G),c(G)\}+3
;

(3)

For all
t\geq 1
, either
I^{(t)}/I^{t}=(0)
or
\dim(I^{(t)}/I^{t})=0
;

(4)

For any induced odd cycle
C
in
G
, we have
N[C]=[n]
.

The study of dimension of Cohen-Macaulayness of symbolic-ordinary discrepancy modules (Theorems
7.8
,
7.10
,
7.11
and
8.4
) is based on investigating modules of the form
S/\sqrt{I:x^{\mathbb{a}}}
which, for
{\mathbb{a}}\in I\setminus J
, is a component of the annihilator of
I/J
.

We remark that while the relative Hochster–Takayama framework robustly captures vanishing properties of local cohomology, and hence Cohen-Macaulay and generalized Cohen-Macaulay conditions, finer properties such as Buchsbaumness or sequentially C-Mness depend on the full multigraded module structure of local cohomology and do not admit comparably concise formulations in terms of degree complexes alone. Accordingly, we have restricted our attention to those properties that can be systematically detected within the functorial relative framework developed in this paper.

2.1.
Simplicial Complexes and Homology

A
simplicial complex

\Delta
on a finite set
[n]=\{1,2,...,n\}
is formally defined as a collection of subsets of
[n]
such that if
F\in\Delta
and
F^{\prime}\subseteq F
, then
F^{\prime}\in\Delta
. For convenience in later discussions, the condition that
\{i\}\in\Delta
for all
i=1,2,...,n
is not assumed.
Set
\dim F=|F|-1
, where
|F|
means the cardinality of
F
, and
\dim\Delta=\max\{\dim F~\big|~F\in\Delta\}
, which is called the
dimensio
n of
\Delta
. When
[n]
is equipped with a linear order, say
<
,
\Delta
is called an
oriented
simplicial complex. In such a case, we denote
F=\{i_{1},...,i_{r}\}
for
F\in\Delta
with the order sequence
i_{1}<...<i_{r}
.

Let
F
be a face of a simplicial complex
\Delta
. The
link
of
F
in
\Delta
, denoted by
\link_{\Delta}F
is the simplicial complex whose faces are given by
\{G\subseteq[n]~\big|~G\cap F=\varnothing,G\cup F\in\Delta\}
. The
star
center at
F
in
\Delta
, denoted by
\starop_{\Delta}F
, is the simplicial complex whose faces are given by
\{\tau\cup\sigma~\big|~\tau\subseteq F,\sigma\in\link_{\Delta}F\}.

Let
\Delta
be an oriented simplicial complex with
\dim\Delta=d
. We denote by
\mathcal{C}(\Delta)_{\bullet}
the
augmented oriented chain complex
of
\Delta
, where

C_{t}=\bigoplus_{\dim F=t}\mathbb{Z}F\text{ and }\partial F=\sum_{j=0}^{t}(-1)^{j}F_{j},\text{ for all }\ F\in\Delta.

(Here,
F_{j}=\{i_{0},\cdots,\hat{i}_{j},\cdots,i_{t}\}
if
F=\{i_{0},...,i_{t}\}
.)

A
relative simplicial complex
is a pair
(\Delta,\Gamma)
of simplicial complexes for which
\Gamma\subseteq\Delta
is a simplicial subcomplex. The faces of
(\Delta,\Gamma)
are the elements
\{F\in\Delta~\big|~F\notin\Gamma\}
. Therefore, an ordinary simplicial complex can be viewed as a relative simplicial complex with
\Gamma=\varnothing
. The
dimension
of a relative simplicial complex
\Phi=(\Delta,\Gamma)
is defined by
\dim\Phi=\max\{\dim(F)~\big|~F\in(\Delta,\Gamma)\}
. If
\Delta
is an oriented simplex complex and
\Gamma
is an oriented subcomplex of
\Delta
, then
\mathcal{C}(\Gamma)_{\bullet}
is a subcomplex of
\mathcal{C}(\Delta)_{\bullet}
, which yields the
quotient complex

\mathcal{C}(\Delta)_{\bullet}/\mathcal{C}(\Gamma)_{\bullet}
.

The
i
-th
reduced relative simplicial homology module
of
(\Delta,\Gamma)
is defined as

\widetilde{H}_{i}((\Delta,\Gamma);{\Bbbk})=H_{i}(\mathcal{C}(\Delta)_{\bullet}/\mathcal{C}(\Gamma)_{\bullet}\otimes{\Bbbk}),

and the
i
-th
reduced relative simplicial cohomology module
of
(\Delta,\Gamma)
is

\widetilde{H}^{i}((\Delta,\Gamma);{\Bbbk})=H^{i}(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathcal{C}(\Delta)_{\bullet}/\mathcal{C}(\Gamma)_{\bullet};{\Bbbk})).

For simplicity of notation, we will often omit
{\Bbbk}
and write
\widetilde{H}_{i}(\Delta,\Gamma)
or
\widetilde{H}^{i}(\Delta,\Gamma)
for the reduced relative simplicial homology and cohomology of a relative simplicial complex
(\Delta,\Gamma)
.

2.2.
Degree Complexes and Symbolic Powers of Monomial Ideals

Throughout the paper,
{\Bbbk}
is an arbitrary field,
S={\Bbbk}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
is a polynomial ring over
{\Bbbk}
. The
t
-th
symbolic power
of an ideal
I\subseteq S
is defined as

I^{(t)}=\bigcap_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Ass(I)}\left(I^{t}S_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap S\right).

When
I=I_{\Delta}
is the Stanley-Reisner ideal associated to a simplicial complex
\Delta
over
[n]
, its symbolic powers have a better combinatorial understanding. More precisely, let
\mathcal{F}(\Delta)
denote the set of facets in
\Delta
and, for
F\in\Delta
, set
P_{F}=(x_{i}~\big|~i\not\in F).
In this case, the primary decomposition of
I
and its symbolic powers are given by

I=\bigcap_{F\in\mathcal{F}(\Delta)}P_{F}\text{ and }I^{(t)}=\bigcap_{F\in\mathcal{F}(\Delta)}P_{F}^{t}.

For
{\mathbb{a}}=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
, set
G_{{\mathbb{a}}}=\{i~\big|~|a_{i}<0\}
. The
degree complex

\Delta_{{\mathbb{a}}}(I)
, of a monomial ideal
I\subseteq S
at multi-degree
{\mathbb{a}}
(as defined in
[
23
]
), consists of all
F\subseteq[n]
such that:

(1)

F\cap G_{{\mathbb{a}}}=\varnothing

(2)

For every minimal generator
x^{{\mathbb{b}}}
of
I
, there exists an index
i\notin F\cup G_{{\mathbb{a}}}
with
b_{i}>a_{i}
.

It is important to note that
\Delta_{{\mathbb{a}}}(I)
may be either the empty set or
\{\varnothing\}
and its vertex set may be a proper subset of
[n]
.

The
Takayama’s formula
(see
[
16
,
23
]
), which generalizes Hochster’s formula, is stated as follows.

Theorem 2.1

(Takayama)
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal in
S
. For each
i\in\mathbb{Z}
and
{\mathbb{a}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, there is an isomorphism of
{\Bbbk}
-vector spaces

H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(S/I)_{{\mathbb{a}}}\cong\widetilde{H}^{i-|G_{{\mathbb{a}}}|-1}(\Delta_{{\mathbb{a}}}(I);{\Bbbk}),

and
H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(S/I)_{{\mathbb{a}}}=(0)
if
G_{{\mathbb{a}}}\notin\Delta(\sqrt{I})
.

Takayama’s formula allows one to recover Reisner’s criterion (see
[
19
]
) for Cohen-Macaulay monomial ideals.

Theorem 2.2

(Reisner’s Criterion)
.

Let
I\subseteq S
be a monomial ideal. The following are equivalent:

(1)

S/I
is Cohen-Macaulay of dimension
d
; and

(2)

For every
{\mathbb{a}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
,

\widetilde{H}^{j}(\left(\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(I);{\Bbbk}\right)=0\text{ for all }j<d-|G_{{\mathbb{a}}}|-1.

2.3.
Edge Ideals of Graphs and Other Conventions

By a
graph
over the vertex set
[n]
we always mean a
simple
(i.e., no loops nor multiple edges) and
connected
graph with vertices
\{1,\dots,n\}
. We shall use
E(G)
to denote the edge set of a graph
G
(and sometimes use
V(G)
to denote the vertex set of
G
if it is not transparent from the context). For a vertex
x\in V(G)
or, more generally, a subset
F\subseteq V(G)
, let
N(x)
and
N(F)
denote the sets of vertices that are adjacent to
x
or to at least a vertex in
F
, respectively. Set
N[x]=N(x)\cup\{x\}
and
N[F]=N(F)\cup F
.

The
edge ideal
of a graph
G
(originally defined in
[
20
]
) over the vertices
[n]
is given by

I(G)=\left(x_{i}x_{j}~\big|~\{i,j\}\in E(G)\right)\subseteq S={\Bbbk}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}].

For any vector
{\mathbb{a}}=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}
, we write
x^{\mathbb{a}}
for the monomial
x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}
. For a subset
F\subseteq[n]
, set
x_{F}=\prod_{i\in F}x_{i}
.

We use
\{{\mathbb{e}}_{1},\dots,{\mathbb{e}}_{n}\}
to denote the standard basis of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, and set
{\mathbb{e}}_{F}=\sum_{i\in F}{\mathbb{e}}_{i}
for any subset
F\subseteq[n]
.
By convention, the dimension of the zero module is
-1
.

3.
Relative Degree Complexes and Hochster–Takayama Formula

In this section, we establish the Hochster–Takayama formula for monomial ideal quotients and corresponding relative simplicial complexes. We start by identifying the relative version of degree complexes.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
J\subseteq I
be monomial ideals in
S
. Then
(\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(J),\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(I))
is a relative simplicial complex for any
{\mathbb{a}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
.

Proof.

For any
{\mathbb{a}}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
, since
J\subseteq I
, we have
\sqrt{J:x^{\mathbb{a}}}\subseteq\sqrt{I:x^{\mathbb{a}}}
. By
[
14
, Lemma 2.19]
,
(\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(J),\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(I))
is a relative simplicial complex. For
{\mathbb{a}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\setminus{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
, let
F=\{i~|~a_{i}<0\}
and put
{\mathbb{a}}^{+}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
for
({\mathbb{a}}^{+})_{i}={\mathbb{a}}_{i}
if
i\not\in F
and
({\mathbb{a}}^{+})_{i}=0
, otherwise. One can see that
\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(I)=\link_{\Delta_{{\mathbb{a}}^{+}}(I)}F
and
\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(J)=\link_{\Delta_{{\mathbb{a}}^{+}}(J)}F
. This implies that
(\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(J),\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(I))
is a relative simplicial complex, since
(\Delta_{{\mathbb{a}}^{+}}(J),\Delta_{{\mathbb{a}}^{+}}(I))
is a relative simplicial complex.
∎

Relative Hochster–Takayama formula
Our first main result, the Hochster–Takayama formula for monomial ideal quotients, is stated as follows.

Theorem 3.2

(Relative Hochster–Takayama)
.

Let
J\subsetneq I
be monomial ideals in
S
. For each
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
{\mathbb{a}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
, there is an isomorphism of
{\Bbbk}
-vector spaces

H_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(I/J)_{{\mathbb{a}}}\cong\widetilde{H}^{i-|G_{\mathbb{a}}|-1}((\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(J),\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(I));{\Bbbk}),

and
H_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(I/J)_{{\mathbb{a}}}=(0)
if
G_{\mathbb{a}}\not\in\Delta(\sqrt{J})
.

Proof.

Let
\check{\bf C}^{\bullet}=\check{\bf C}^{\bullet}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
be the
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
-graded Čech complex with respect to

{\mathfrak{m}}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
.
Note that, for any
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
-graded
S
-module
N
,

H^{i}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(N)\cong H^{i}(\check{\bf C}^{\bullet}\otimes_{S}N)\quad\text{and}\quad H^{i}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(N)_{\mathbb{a}}\cong H^{i}\!\big((\check{\bf C}^{\bullet}\otimes_{S}N)_{\mathbb{a}}\big).

By Takayama’s work
[
23
]
, for any monomial ideal
K\subseteq S
, we have the following isomorphism of cochain complexes:

(3.1)

\displaystyle(\check{\bf C}^{\bullet}\otimes_{S}S/K)_{\mathbb{a}}[|G_{\mathbb{a}}|+1]\ \cong\ \widetilde{C}^{\bullet}(\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(K);{\Bbbk}).

Consider the short exact sequence of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
-graded modules

0\to I/J\to S/J\to S/I\to 0.

Localization is exact, so tensoring with
\check{\bf C}^{\bullet}
yields a short exact sequence of cochain complexes,
and taking the
{\mathbb{a}}
-graded strand preserves exactness:

0\to(\check{\bf C}^{\bullet}\otimes_{S}I/J)_{\mathbb{a}}\to(\check{\bf C}^{\bullet}\otimes_{S}S/J)_{\mathbb{a}}\to(\check{\bf C}^{\bullet}\otimes_{S}S/I)_{\mathbb{a}}\to 0.

Furthermore, since
J\subseteq I
, we have
\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(I)\subseteq\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(J)
. Thus, the map

(\check{\bf C}^{\bullet}\otimes_{S}S/J)_{\mathbb{a}}\to(\check{\bf C}^{\bullet}\otimes_{S}S/I)_{\mathbb{a}}

is the restriction map induced by
\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(I)\subseteq\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(J)
.
Together with (
3.1
), this implies that the kernel complex identifies (after shifting by
|G_{\mathbb{a}}|+1
) with the reduced relative cochain complex

\widetilde{C}^{\bullet}(\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(J),\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(I);{\Bbbk})
. Hence,

(\check{\bf C}^{\bullet}\otimes_{S}I/J)_{\mathbb{a}}[|G_{\mathbb{a}}|+1]\ \cong\ \widetilde{C}^{\bullet}(\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(J),\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(I);{\Bbbk}).

Taking cohomology gives

H^{i}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(I/J)_{\mathbb{a}}\ \cong\ \widetilde{H}^{\,i-|G_{\mathbb{a}}|-1}\!\big((\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(J),\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(I));{\Bbbk}\big).

Finally, if
G_{\mathbb{a}}\notin\Delta(\sqrt{J})
, then Takayama’s vanishing result (see
[
23
]
) implies
H^{i}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(S/J)_{\mathbb{a}}=0
for all
i
.
Since
\sqrt{J}\subseteq\sqrt{I}
, also
G_{\mathbb{a}}\notin\Delta(\sqrt{I})
, so
H^{i}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(S/I)_{\mathbb{a}}=0
for all
i
.
The long exact sequence in local cohomology induced by

0\to I/J\to S/J\to S/I\to 0
then yields
H^{i}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(I/J)_{\mathbb{a}}=0
for all
i
.
∎

The following Reisner-type implication is a direct consequence of the Relative Hochster–Takayama formula in Theorem
3.2
(see also
Theorem

2.2
).

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
d=\dim(I/J)
.
Assume that for every
\mathbf{a}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
,

\widetilde{H}^{j}\!\left(\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J),\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I);{\Bbbk}\right)=0\quad\text{for all }j<d-|G_{\mathbf{a}}|-1.

Then
I/J
is Cohen-Macaulay of dimension
d
.

Proof.

Fix
i<d
and
\mathbf{a}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
.
By Theorem
3.2
,

H^{i}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(I/J)_{\mathbf{a}}\;\cong\;\widetilde{H}^{\,i-|G_{\mathbf{a}}|-1}\!\left(\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J),\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I);{\Bbbk}\right).

Since
i-|G_{\mathbf{a}}|-1<d-|G_{\mathbf{a}}|-1
, the right-hand side vanishes
by hypothesis. Hence
H^{i}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(I/J)=0
for all
i<d
, and therefore
I/J

is Cohen-Macaulay.
∎

Theorem
3.2
further gives the following rigidity result and an immediate corollary.

Theorem 3.4

(Rigidity)
.

Suppose that
\dim I/J\geq 2
.
If there exists
\mathbf{a}
such that

\widetilde{H}^{0}(\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J),\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I))\neq 0,

then
\depth I/J\leq 1
.

Proof.

By the Relative Hochster–Takayama formula,

H^{1}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(I/J)_{\mathbf{a}}\neq 0
, so
\depth I/J\leq 1
.
∎

Corollary 3.5

(
(S_{2})
collapse)
.

If
\dim(I/J)\geq 2
and some

\widetilde{H}^{0}(\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J),\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I))\neq 0
,
then
I/J
fails
(S_{2})
.

Proof.

The assertion is a direct consequence of Theorem
3.4
.
∎

Functoriality
In parallel to the Hochster–Takayama formula, we obtain the following functoriality result. We shall need a lemma.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
J\subseteq I
be monomial ideals in
S
, and set
M:=I/J
.
Fix
F\subseteq[n]
and
{\mathbb{a}}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. Write
{\mathbb{a}}^{-}:=\sum_{i\in G_{\mathbb{a}}}(-a_{i}){\mathbb{e}}_{i}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}\text{ and }{\mathbb{a}}^{+}:={\mathbb{a}}+{\mathbb{a}}^{-}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}.

The following statements hold:

(1)

If
F\not\supseteq G_{\mathbb{a}}
, then
(M_{x_{F}})_{\mathbb{a}}=0
.

(2)

If
F\supseteq G_{\mathbb{a}}
, then
(M_{x_{F}})_{\mathbb{a}}
is either
0
or a
1
-dimensional

k
-vector space. More precisely,

(M_{x_{F}})_{\mathbb{a}}\neq 0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x^{{\mathbb{a}}^{+}}\in IS_{x_{F}}\setminus JS_{x_{F}},

and in this case
(M_{x_{F}})_{\mathbb{a}}
is generated by the class of
\frac{x^{{\mathbb{a}}^{+}}}{x^{{\mathbb{a}}^{-}}}\ \in\ M_{x_{F}}.

Proof.

We work in the
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
-graded localization
S_{x_{F}}=S[x_{i}^{-1}\mid i\in F]
.
Observe that a Laurent monomial
x^{\gamma}
lies in
S_{x_{F}}
if and only if
\gamma_{i}\geq 0
for all
i\notin F
.
Hence

(S_{x_{F}})_{\mathbb{a}}\neq 0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a_{i}\geq 0\ \text{ for all }i\notin F\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad G_{\mathbb{a}}\subseteq F.

If
G_{\mathbb{a}}\subseteq F
, then
\supp({\mathbb{a}}^{-})\subseteq G_{\mathbb{a}}\subseteq F
, so
x^{{\mathbb{a}}^{-}}
is a unit in
S_{x_{F}}

and the element
x^{{\mathbb{a}}^{+}}/x^{{\mathbb{a}}^{-}}
is a Laurent monomial of degree
{\mathbb{a}}
.

We now show that
(S_{x_{F}})_{\mathbb{a}}
is
1
-dimensional over
k
, spanned by
x^{{\mathbb{a}}^{+}}/x^{{\mathbb{a}}^{-}}
.
Let
x^{\alpha}/x^{\beta}
be any Laurent monomial in
S_{x_{F}}
of degree
{\mathbb{a}}
, where

\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
and
\supp(\beta)\subseteq F
. The degree condition means
\alpha-\beta={\mathbb{a}}
.
For each
i\in G_{\mathbb{a}}
we have
a_{i}<0
, hence
\alpha_{i}=\beta_{i}+a_{i}\geq 0
forces
\beta_{i}\geq-a_{i}
.
Thus
\beta\geq{\mathbb{a}}^{-}
componentwise. Consequently
\beta-{\mathbb{a}}^{-}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
, and then

\alpha-{\mathbb{a}}^{+}=\alpha-({\mathbb{a}}+{\mathbb{a}}^{-})=(\beta+{\mathbb{a}})-({\mathbb{a}}+{\mathbb{a}}^{-})=\beta-{\mathbb{a}}^{-}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}.

Set
\delta:=\beta-{\mathbb{a}}^{-}=\alpha-{\mathbb{a}}^{+}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
. Then

\frac{x^{\alpha}}{x^{\beta}}=\frac{x^{{\mathbb{a}}^{+}}x^{\delta}}{x^{{\mathbb{a}}^{-}}x^{\delta}}=\frac{x^{{\mathbb{a}}^{+}}}{x^{{\mathbb{a}}^{-}}}.

So there is
exactly one
Laurent monomial in
S_{x_{F}}
of degree
{\mathbb{a}}
.
Therefore
(S_{x_{F}})_{\mathbb{a}}
is
1
-dimensional over
k
, spanned by
x^{{\mathbb{a}}^{+}}/x^{{\mathbb{a}}^{-}}
.

(1) If
F\not\supseteq G_{\mathbb{a}}
, then
(S_{x_{F}})_{\mathbb{a}}=0
by our arguments above. Thus,

(IS_{x_{F}})_{\mathbb{a}}=(JS_{x_{F}})_{\mathbb{a}}=0
and, therefore,
(M_{x_{F}})_{\mathbb{a}}=0
.

(2) Assume that
F\supseteq G_{\mathbb{a}}
. As we have shown, in this case,
(S_{x_{F}})_{\mathbb{a}}
is
1
-dimensional and spanned by

x^{{\mathbb{a}}^{+}}/x^{{\mathbb{a}}^{-}}
. Since
IS_{x_{F}}
and
JS_{x_{F}}
are monomial ideals in
S_{x_{F}}
, they
are
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
-graded, so
(IS_{x_{F}})_{\mathbb{a}}
and
(JS_{x_{F}})_{\mathbb{a}}
are
k
-subspaces of
(S_{x_{F}})_{\mathbb{a}}
.
Because
(S_{x_{F}})_{\mathbb{a}}
is
1
-dimensional, each of these graded pieces is either
0
or all of

(S_{x_{F}})_{\mathbb{a}}
.

Hence
(M_{x_{F}})_{\mathbb{a}}=(IS_{x_{F}}/JS_{x_{F}})_{\mathbb{a}}
is either
0
or
1
-dimensional, and it is nonzero
if and only if

\frac{x^{{\mathbb{a}}^{+}}}{x^{{\mathbb{a}}^{-}}}\in IS_{x_{F}}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{x^{{\mathbb{a}}^{+}}}{x^{{\mathbb{a}}^{-}}}\notin JS_{x_{F}}.

Since
x^{{\mathbb{a}}^{-}}
is a unit in
S_{x_{F}}
, for any ideal
K
we have

\frac{x^{{\mathbb{a}}^{+}}}{x^{{\mathbb{a}}^{-}}}\in KS_{x_{F}}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x^{{\mathbb{a}}^{+}}\in KS_{x_{F}}.

Applying this with
K=I
and
K=J
yields

(M_{x_{F}})_{\mathbb{a}}\neq 0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x^{{\mathbb{a}}^{+}}\in IS_{x_{F}}\setminus JS_{x_{F}}.

When this holds, the class of
x^{{\mathbb{a}}^{+}}/x^{{\mathbb{a}}^{-}}
spans the (necessarily
1
-dimensional)
vector space
(M_{x_{F}})_{\mathbb{a}}
, completing the proof.
∎

]
. Our next result gives functoriality for relative degree complexes and local cohomology modules of monomial ideal quotients.

(Functoriality)
.

Fix

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)_{\mathbb{a}}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{H}^{\,i-|G_{\mathbb{a}}|-1}\!\big(\Delta_{{\mathbb{a}}+{\mathbb{b}}}(J),\,\Delta_{{\mathbb{a}}+{\mathbb{b}}}(I)\big),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdot{\mathbb{x}}^{\mathbb{b}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}

where the vertical isomorphisms are given by the relative Hochster–Takayama

In particular, under the hypothesis

Proof.

Let
{\mathbb{b}}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
and

satisfy

. Set

.
Multiplication by

of degree

\mu_{\mathbb{b}}^{\bullet}:\ \check{\bf C}^{\bullet}\otimes_{S}M\longrightarrow\check{\bf C}^{\bullet}\otimes_{S}M,\qquad u\longmapsto x^{\mathbb{b}}u,

which commutes with the Čech differential because all localization maps are
S
-linear. Taking the
{\mathbb{a}}
-graded

(\mu_{\mathbb{b}}^{\bullet})_{\mathbb{a}}:\ (\check{\bf C}^{\bullet}\otimes_{S}M)_{\mathbb{a}}\longrightarrow(\check{\bf C}^{\bullet}\otimes_{S}M)_{{\mathbb{a}}+{\mathbb{b}}},

whose induced map on cohomology is exactly
\cdot x^{\mathbb{b}}:\ H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)_{\mathbb{a}}\to H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)_{{\mathbb{a}}+{\mathbb{b}}}

For any monomial ideal

Indeed, let

If

and

Recall from the proof of Theorem

Under

of the relative

4.
Relative Complexes for Squarefree Monomial Ideals

This section specifies to the situation where
J\subseteq I
are squarefree monomial ideals. In this case, as in
[
21
]
and
[
1
]
, the quotient
I/J
is associated to a relative simplicial complex
(\Delta,\Gamma)
with
\Delta
and
\Gamma
being the Stanley-Reisner complexes of
I
and
J
, respectively. We shall see that for
{\mathbb{a}}\in\{0,\pm 1\}^{n}
, relative degree complexes at
{\mathbb{a}}
are restrictions of
(\Delta,\Gamma)
and, furthermore, links and localizations are compatible.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\Sigma
be a simplicial complex on
[n]
and let
\mathbf{a}\in\{0,\pm 1\}^{n}
. Set
F=[n]\setminus G_{\mathbb{a}}
. Then, there is a natural identification

\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I_{\Sigma})=\Sigma|_{F},

where
\Sigma|_{F}
denotes the induced subcomplex of
\Sigma
on the vertex set
F
.

Proof.

By definition, a face
E\subseteq F
belongs to
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I_{\Sigma})
if and only if

x^{E}\notin I_{\Sigma}
, equivalently
E\in\Sigma
, and
E
avoids all vertices in
G_{\mathbf{a}}
.
This is precisely the definition of the induced subcomplex
\Sigma|_{F}
.
∎

Applying Lemma
4.1
to both
\Gamma
and
\Delta
yields the
following immediate consequence.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
\Gamma\subseteq\Delta
be simplicial complexes on
[n]
and let
\mathbf{a}\in\{0,\pm 1\}^{n}
.
Then there is a natural identification of pairs of simplicial complexes

\left(\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I_{\Delta}),\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I_{\Gamma})\right)\cong\left(\Delta|_{F},\Gamma|_{F}\right),

where
F=[n]\setminus G_{\mathbf{a}}
.

Thus, in squarefree multidegrees, the relative Hochster–Takayama formula for

I_{\Gamma}/I_{\Delta}
is governed by the relative topology of induced subcomplexes.

Combining Corollary
4.2
with the relative Hochster–Takayama
formula yields the following explicit description of the multigraded local cohomology
of Stanley–Reisner quotients.

Theorem 4.3

(Relative Hochster–Takayama formula for Stanley–Reisner quotients)
.

Let
\Gamma\subseteq\Delta
be simplicial complexes on
[n]
and let

M=I_{\Gamma}/I_{\Delta}
.
For every squarefree multidegree
\mathbf{a}\in\{0,\pm 1\}^{n}
with
F=[n]\setminus G_{\mathbf{a}}
,
there is a natural isomorphism

H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)_{\mathbf{a}}\cong\widetilde{H}^{i-|G_{\mathbf{a}}|-1}\left(\Delta|_{F},\Gamma|_{F}\right).

Proof.

By the Relative Hochster–Takayama formula for monomial quotients,

H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)_{\mathbf{a}}\cong\widetilde{H}^{i-|G_{\mathbf{a}}|-1}\left(\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I_{\Delta}),\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I_{\Gamma})\right).

The claim now follows from Corollary
4.2
.
∎

The induced subcomplex description admits a link interpretation that is particularly
useful for localization and depth considerations.
Let
F\subseteq[n]
and write
P_{F}=(x_{i}:i\notin F)
for the corresponding monomial prime.
Localizing
I_{\Gamma}/I_{\Delta}
at
P_{F}
corresponds to restricting to the induced pair

(\Delta|_{F},\Gamma|_{F})
.

Moreover, for faces
E\subseteq F
, the links satisfy

\link_{\Gamma|_{F}}(E)=\link_{\Gamma}(E)|_{F\setminus E},\quad\link_{\Delta|_{F}}(E)=\link_{\Delta}(E)|_{F\setminus E}.

Thus the relative topology of induced subcomplexes may equivalently be studied through
relative links.

This identification allows us to translate algebraic properties of

I_{\Gamma}/I_{\Delta}
such as support, dimension, depth, and sequential Cohen–Macaulayness
into linkwise topological conditions on the pair
(\Delta,\Gamma)
.

5.
Cohen-Macaulay and Generalized Cohen-Macaulay Properties

In this section, we obtain the Relative Reisner Criterion for Cohen-Macaulay monomial ideal quotients. We also characterize generalized Cohen-Macaulay monomial ideal quotients.

Relative Reisner Criterion.
Observe first that Corollary
3.3
gives a uniform vanishing condition on the relative degree complexes that forces Cohen-Macaulayness of the monomial ideal quotient.
In order to obtain a genuine Relative Reisner criterion, one needs to
understand which multidegrees
\mathbf{a}
can actually contribute to
local cohomology. The next lemma provides the key technical input for this purpose.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
d=\dim(I/J)
.
If

H^{i}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(I/J)_{\mathbf{a}}\neq 0\quad\text{for some }i\in{\mathbb{Z}},

then

\dim\!\left(\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J),\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)\right)\;\leq\;d-|G_{\mathbf{a}}|-1.

Proof.

Write
F=G_{\mathbf{a}}
and let
\mathbf{a}^{+}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
be obtained from

\mathbf{a}
by replacing negative entries by
0
.
It can be seen that

\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J)=\link_{\Delta_{\mathbf{a}^{+}}(J)}(F),\qquad\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)=\link_{\Delta_{\mathbf{a}^{+}}(I)}(F),

and hence

\dim\!\left(\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J),\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)\right)\leq\dim\Delta_{\mathbf{a}^{+}}(J)-|F|.

Since
H^{i}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(I/J)_{\mathbf{a}}\neq 0
, there exists a nonzero element
of multidegree
\mathbf{a}
in some localization
(I/J)_{x_{F}}
.
Equivalently, there exist
\mathbf{u}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
and
t\gg 0
such that

\mathbf{u}-t\mathbf{1}_{F}=\mathbf{a}\quad\text{and}\quad x^{\mathbf{u}}\in IS_{x_{F}}\setminus JS_{x_{F}}.

Clearing denominators, there exists
N\gg 0
such that

x^{\mathbf{c}}:=x_{F}^{N}x^{\mathbf{u}}\in I\setminus J,

and
\mathbf{c}\geq\mathbf{a}^{+}
coordinatewise.

Since
x^{\mathbf{c}}
is divisible by
x^{\mathbf{a}^{+}}
, we have
(J:x^{\mathbf{c}})\subseteq(J:x^{\mathbf{a}^{+}}).

That is,
\Delta_{\mathbf{a}^{+}}(J)\subseteq\Delta_{\mathbf{c}}(J).

Therefore,

\dim\Delta_{\mathbf{a}^{+}}(J)\leq\dim\Delta_{\mathbf{c}}(J).

Because
x^{\mathbf{c}}\in I\setminus J
, Lemma
5.1
yields
\dim\Delta_{\mathbf{c}}(J)\leq d-1.

Combining this with
(\ast)
gives

\dim\!\left(\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J),\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)\right)\leq(d-1)-|F|=d-|G_{\mathbf{a}}|-1,

as desired.
∎

We can now formulate a precise Reisner characterization.

Theorem 5.2

(Relative Reisner Criterion)
.

Let
d=\dim(I/J)
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

I/J
is Cohen-Macaulay.

(2)

For every
\mathbf{a}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
and every face

F\in\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J)
,

\widetilde{H}^{j}\!\left((\link_{\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J)}(F),\link_{\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)}(F));{\Bbbk}\right)=0\quad\text{for all }j<d-|G_{\mathbf{a}}|-|F|-1.

Proof.

(1)\Longrightarrow(2)
.
Assume that
I/J
is Cohen-Macaulay of dimension
d
.
Fix
\mathbf{a}
and
F\in\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J)
.
Let
\mathbf{b}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
be defined by

b_{i}=\begin{cases}-1,&i\in F,\\
a_{i},&i\notin F.\end{cases}

Then
G_{\mathbf{b}}=G_{\mathbf{a}}\cup F
and

\Delta_{\mathbf{b}}(J)=\link_{\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J)}(F),\qquad\Delta_{\mathbf{b}}(I)=\link_{\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)}(F).

Since
I/J
is Cohen-Macaulay,
H^{i}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(I/J)=0
for all
i<d
.
Applying Theorem
3.2
to
\mathbf{b}
yields

\widetilde{H}^{\,i-|G_{\mathbf{b}}|-1}\!\left(\link_{\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J)}(F),\link_{\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)}(F)\right)=0\quad\text{for all }i<d,

which is equivalent to the stated vanishing condition.

(2)\Longrightarrow(1)
.
Taking
F=\varnothing
in (2) gives

\widetilde{H}^{j}\!\left(\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J),\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I)\right)=0\quad\text{for all }j<d-|G_{\mathbf{a}}|-1.

By Corollary
3.3
, this implies that

I/J
is Cohen-Macaulay.
∎

Generalized Cohen-Macaulay monomial ideal quotients.

Recall that
M
is
generalized Cohen-Macaulay
if
H^{i}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(M)
has finite length for all
i<d
. The following result characterizes generalized Cohen-Macaulay monomial ideal quotients by the vanishing of reduced homology of the corresponding relative degree complexes.

Theorem 5.3

(Generalized Cohen-Macaulay property)
.

The following are equivalent:

(1)

I/J
is generalized Cohen-Macaulay.

(2)

For every
i<d
there exists an integer
N(i)
such that for all
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}

with
\max(a_{j})\geq N(i)
,

\widetilde{H}^{\,i-1}\!\left((\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J),\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I));{\Bbbk}\right)=0.

Proof.

Fix
i<d
. Since
H^{i}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(I/J)
is
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
-graded, it has finite length if and only if
there exists
N(i)
such that
H^{i}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(I/J)_{\mathbf{a}}=0
for all
\mathbf{a}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}

with
\max(a_{j})\geq N(i)
.
For
\mathbf{a}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
we have
G_{\mathbf{a}}=\varnothing
, so Theorem
3.2
gives

H^{i}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(I/J)_{\mathbf{a}}\ \cong\ \widetilde{H}^{\,i-1}\!\left((\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(J),\Delta_{\mathbf{a}}(I));{\Bbbk}\right).

Thus, the finite length of
H^{i}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(I/J)
is equivalent to the stated eventual vanishing of
the reduced relative cohomology. This holds for all
i<d
if and only if
I/J
is
generalized Cohen-Macaulay.
∎

6.
Symbolic Quotients

In this section, we use Hochster–Takayama formula for relative degree complexes to study the Cohen-Macaulayness of symbolic quotients
I^{(t)}/I^{(t+1)}
, for
t\geq 1
, for a squarefree monomial ideal
I
. Our main result provides yet another interesting connection between this property to the matroidal structure of the corresponding simplicial complex, that is similar to the delicate characterization of the Cohen-Macaulay property of
I^{(t)}
, for
t\geq 1
, given in
[
16
,
24
]
.

We start with the following simple observation.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex and let
I=I_{\Delta}
be its Stanley-Reisner ideal. Assume that
I\not=(0)
and let
J
be a monomial ideal such that
I^{t}\subseteq J\subseteq I^{(t)}
. Then, for any
t\geq 1
,

I^{(t+1)}:J=I.

In particular,
\dim I^{(t)}/I^{(t+1)}=\dim S/I
.

Proof.

Clearly,
IJ\subseteq I.I^{(t)}\subseteq I^{(t+1)}
. Suppose, by contradiction, that there exists a monomial
x^{\mathbb{a}}\in(I^{(t+1)}:J)\setminus I
. Then, the squarefree monomial
x_{G}=\sqrt{x^{\mathbb{a}}}\notin I
, which implies that
G\in\Delta
. Let
F
be a facet of
\Delta
such that
G\subseteq F
.

Observe that
F\not=[n]
, so there exists
u\in[n]\setminus F
. It follows that
F\cup\{u\}\notin\Delta
, i.e., there exists a minimal generator of
I
, which is of the form
x_{u}x_{F^{\prime}}
, for some
F^{\prime}\subseteq F
. Therefore,

x^{\mathbb{a}}(x_{u}x_{F^{\prime}})^{t}\in x^{\mathbb{a}}I^{t}\subseteq x^{\mathbb{a}}J\subseteq I^{(t+1)}=\bigcap_{H\in\mathcal{F}(\Delta)}P_{H}^{t+1}.

On the other hand, it can be seen that
\supp(x^{\mathbb{a}}(x_{F^{\prime}})^{t})\subseteq G\cup F^{\prime}\subseteq F
. Thus,
x^{\mathbb{a}}(x_{u}x_{F^{\prime}})^{t}\notin P_{F}^{t+1}
, which is a contradiction. The assertion is proved.
∎

The main result of this section is stated as follows.

Theorem 6.2

(Cohen-Macaulay symbolic quotients)
.

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex on
[n]
and let
I=I_{\Delta}
be its Stanley–Reisner ideal.
Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

I^{(t)}/I^{(t+1)}
is Cohen-Macaulay for all
t\geq 1
;

(2)

I^{(t)}/I^{(t+1)}
is Cohen-Macaulay for some
t\geq 2
;

(3)

\Delta
is a matroid.

Moreover, if
\dim S/I\geq 2
, then the above are further equivalent to:

(4)

I^{(t)}/I^{(t+1)}
satisfies Serre’s condition
(S_{2})
for all
t\geq 1
;

(5)

I^{(t)}/I^{(t+1)}
satisfies Serre’s condition
(S_{2})
for some
t\geq 2
.

If
\dim S/I\leq 1
, then
I^{(t)}/I^{(t+1)}
satisfies
(S_{2})
trivially for all
t\geq 1
.

Proof.

It is clear that
(1)\Longrightarrow(2)
. Assume
(3)
is true. By
[
16
,
24
]
,
S/I^{(t)}
is Cohen-Macaulay for any
t\geq 1
. This, together with Lemma
6.1
and by considering the short exact sequence

0\to I^{(t)}/I^{(t+1)}\to S/I^{(t+1)}\to S/I^{(t)}\to 0

and its associated long exact sequence of local cohomology, implies that
I^{(t)}/I^{(t+1)}
is a Cohen-Macaulay module for all
t\geq 1
. That is,
(3)\Longrightarrow(1)
.

We shall prove
(2)\Longrightarrow(3)
. Suppose that
I^{(t)}/I^{(t+1)}
is Cohen-Macaulay for some
t\geq 2
and
\Delta
is not a matroid. Then, after a reindexing if necessary, there exist
F,G\in\Delta
such that
F\setminus G=\{1\}
,
G\setminus F=\{2,3\}
and
F\cup\{2\}\notin\Delta,F\cup\{3\}\notin\Delta
.

Let
L=\link_{\Delta}\{1\}\cap\link_{\Delta}\{2,3\}
.
Note that
F\cap G\in L
, so we can take
a facet
U
of
L
such that
F\cap G\subseteq U
.
Let
{\mathbb{a}}=t{\mathbb{e}}_{1}+{\mathbb{e}}_{2}+{\mathbb{e}}_{3}-{\mathbb{e}}_{U}
.
In particular,
{\mathbb{a}}^{+}=t{\mathbb{e}}_{1}+{\mathbb{e}}_{2}+{\mathbb{e}}_{3}
and
G_{{\mathbb{a}}}=U
.
It can be verified that

\Delta_{{\mathbb{a}}^{+}}(I^{(t+1)})=\starop_{\Delta}\{1\}\cup\starop_{\Delta}\{2,3\}

and

\Delta_{{\mathbb{a}}^{+}}(I^{(t)})\text{ is a cone over }\{1\}.

Moreover, we get

\displaystyle\Delta_{{\mathbb{a}}}(I^{(t+1)})

\displaystyle=\link_{\Delta_{{\mathbb{a}}^{+}}(I^{(t+1)})}G_{\mathbb{a}}

\displaystyle=\link_{\starop_{\Delta}\{1\}}U\cup\link_{\starop_{\Delta}\{2,3\}}U.

Observe first that, since
\{1\}\in\link_{\starop_{\Delta}\{1\}}U
and

\{2,3\}\in\link_{\starop_{\Delta}\{2,3\}}U
, both these links are not the empty nor the void complexes.
We shall further show that

\link_{\starop_{\Delta}\{1\}}U\cap\link_{\starop_{\Delta}\{2,3\}}U=\{\varnothing\}.

Indeed, suppose on the contrary that there exists a vertex
x\in[n]
that
belongs to the intersection
\link_{\starop_{\Delta}\{1\}}U\cap\link_{\starop_{\Delta}\{2,3\}}U
.
Then,

x\not\in U
,
U\cup\{x\}\in\starop_{\Delta}\{1\}
and

U\cup\{x\}\in\starop_{\Delta}\{2,3\}
.
In other words,

x\not\in U
,
U\cup\{x,1\}\in\Delta
and
U\cup\{x,2,3\}\in\Delta
.
Note that
x
must be different from
1,2,3
— for if
x=2
then
U\cup\{1,2\}\in\Delta
, whence

(F\cap G)\cup\{1,2\}=F\cup\{2\}\in\Delta
, which is a contradiction — similar arguments for the case where
x=1
or
x=3
.
Thus, we get
U\cup\{x\}\in\link_{\Delta}\{1\}
and

U\cup\{x\}\in\link_{\Delta}\{2,3\}
. Consequently, we have

U\cup\{x\}\in L
, which contradicts to the maximality of
U
in
L
.
Hence,
\link_{\starop_{\Delta}\{1\}}U\cap\link_{\starop_{\Delta}\{2,3\}}U=\{\varnothing\}
.

We have just shown that
\Delta_{{\mathbb{a}}}(I^{(t+1)})
is the disjoint union of non-empty simplicial complexes
\link_{\starop_{\Delta}\{1\}}U
and
\link_{\starop_{\Delta}\{2,3\}}U
. Let
r=|U|+1
.
By Theorem
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle H_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{r}(I^{(t)}/I^{(t+1)})_{{\mathbb{a}}}

\displaystyle\cong\widetilde{H}^{r-|G_{\mathbb{a}}|-1}(\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(I^{(t+1)}),\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(I^{(t)}))

\displaystyle=\widetilde{H}^{r-|U|-1}(\link_{\Delta_{{\mathbb{a}}^{+}}(I^{(t+1)})}U,\link_{\Delta_{{\mathbb{a}}^{+}}(I^{(t)})}U)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{H}^{0}(\link_{\starop_{\Delta}\{1\}}U\cup\link_{\starop_{\Delta}\{2,3\}}U,\text{ the cone over }\{1\})

\displaystyle\cong\widetilde{H}^{0}(\link_{\starop_{\Delta}\{1\}}U\cup\link_{\starop_{\Delta}\{2,3\}}U)\neq(0).

Here, the last isomorphism follows from the standard long exact sequence of cohomologies of a pair of simplicial complexes and their relative complex.

Observe finally that
|U|\leq\dim(\link_{\Delta}\{2,3\})+1\leq\dim\Delta-1
. Therefore,

r=|U|+1\leq\dim\Delta<\dim(S/I)=\dim(I^{(t)}/I^{(t+1)}),

where the last equality is due to Lemma
6.1
. This implies that
I^{(t)}/I^{(t+1)}
is not Cohen-Macaulay, a contradiction. We have established the equivalence between (1), (2) and (3).

Suppose now that
\dim S/I\geq 2
. By Lemma
6.1
,
\dim(I^{(t)}/I^{(t+1)})=\dim S/I\geq 2
for all
t\geq 1
. Clearly, (1)
\Longrightarrow
(4)
\Longrightarrow
(5) since Cohen-Macaulay implies
(S_{2})
.
It remains to prove (5)
\Longrightarrow
(3).
If
\Delta
is not a matroid, then the same witness multidegree
{\mathbb{a}}
constructed in the proof of (2)
\Longrightarrow
(3)
produces
\widetilde{H}^{0}\bigl((\Delta_{{\mathbb{a}}}(I^{(t+1)}),\Delta_{{\mathbb{a}}}(I^{(t)}))\bigr)\neq 0
for
t\geq 2
.
Hence,
H^{1}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(I^{(t)}/I^{(t+1)})\neq 0
, so
\depth(I^{(t)}/I^{(t+1)})\leq 1
.
Since
d\geq 2
, this contradicts
(S_{2})
at
{\mathfrak{m}}
.
Thus
\Delta
must be a matroid.
∎

Example 6.3
.

Consider
I=(x_{1}x_{2},x_{2}x_{3},x_{3}x_{4},x_{4}x_{5},x_{5}x_{1})
in
S={\Bbbk}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}]
. Then,
I
is the Stanley-Reisner ideal of a 5-cycle, which is not a matroid. In this example,
I^{(t)}/I^{(t+1)}
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
t=1
.

It is desirable to see if a similar characterization to that of Theorem
6.2
holds for monomial ideals in general.

Problem 6.4
.

Characterize the Cohen-Macaulayness of
I^{(t)}/I^{(t+1)}
, for all
t\geq 1
, when
I
is a monomial ideal.

For
pure
simplicial complexes generalized Cohen-Macaulay symbolic quotients admit a similar characterization as that of Cohen-Macaulay symbolic quotients, as illustrated in the following remark.

Remark 6.5

(Generalized Cohen-Macaulay symbolic quotients)
.

Fix
t\geq 2
and set
M=I_{\Delta}^{(t)}/I_{\Delta}^{(t+1)}
. Assume that
\dim S/I_{\Delta}\geq 2
. By Lemma
6.1
,
\dim M=\dim S/I_{\Delta}\geq 2
.

Observe that, by definition, if
M
is generalized Cohen-Macaulay then
M
is Cohen-Macaulay on the punctured spectrum.
Localizing at
x_{F}=\prod_{i\in F}x_{i}
for a nonempty face
F\in\Delta
and using the fact that symbolic powers
commute with localization (since
I_{\Delta}
is radical), we obtain

M_{x_{F}}\ \cong\ {\Bbbk}[x_{i}^{\pm 1}\mid i\in F]\otimes_{\Bbbk}\Big(I_{\link_{\Delta}(F)}^{(t)}/I_{\link_{\Delta}(F)}^{(t+1)}\Big).

Thus,
M_{x_{F}}
is Cohen-Macaulay for every
F\neq\varnothing
.
By the Cohen-Macaulay classification in
Theorem

6.2
, this forces
\link_{\Delta}(F)
to be a matroid for every nonempty face
F\in\Delta
. In particular,
\Delta
is
locally matroidal
.

On the other hand, it can be seen from the standard short exact sequence that
\Ass(M)\subseteq\Ass(S/I_{\Delta}^{(t)})\cup\Ass(S/I_{\Delta}^{(t+1)})=\Min(I_{\Delta})
. Thus, if
\Delta
is pure then
M
is equidimensional. Particularly, if, in addition,
M
is Cohen-Macaulay on the punctured spectrum, then
M
is generalized Cohen-Macaulay.

Hence, we have demonstrated that, under the hypotheses that
\Delta
is pure,
t\geq 2
, and
\dim S/I_{\Delta}\geq 2
,

I_{\Delta}^{(t)}/I_{\Delta}^{(t+1)}\ \text{is generalized CM}\Longleftrightarrow\Delta\text{ is locally matroidal.}

In other words, if
\Delta
is pure and of dimension at least 1, then the following are equivalent:

(1)

I_{\Delta}^{(t)}/I_{\Delta}^{(t+1)}
is generalized Cohen-Macaulay for all
t\geq 1
;

(2)

I_{\Delta}^{(t)}/I_{\Delta}^{(t+1)}
is generalized Cohen-Macaulay for some
t\geq 2
;

(3)

\Delta
is locally matroidal.

7.
Dimension Stability and Symbolic-Ordinary Discrepancy Modules

In this section, we look at the dimension of monomial ideal quotients and its relationship with degree complexes. We also investigate the function
\dim I^{(t)}/I^{t}
, where
I
is the edge ideal of a graph.

For a monomial ideal
I
, let
\mathcal{G}(I)
denote the unique set of minimal monomial generators of
I
. For monomial ideals
J\subseteq I
, for simplicity, we shall write
x^{\mathbb{a}}\in\mathcal{G}(I)\setminus J
to denote the set of minimal monomial generators of
I
that are not in
J
. The following lemmas allow us to understand the dimension of monomial ideal quotients from corresponding degree complexes.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
J\subseteq I
be monomial ideals. Then,

\displaystyle\dim(I/J)

\displaystyle=\max\limits_{x^{\mathbb{a}}\in I\setminus J}\{\dim(S/\sqrt{J:x^{\mathbb{a}}})\}

\displaystyle=\max\limits_{x^{\mathbb{a}}\in I\setminus J}\{\dim(\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(J))+1\}

\displaystyle=\max\limits_{x^{\mathbb{a}}\in\mathcal{G}(I)\setminus J}\{\dim(S/\sqrt{J:x^{\mathbb{a}}})\}

\displaystyle=\max\limits_{x^{\mathbb{a}}\in\mathcal{G}(I)\setminus J}\{\dim(\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(J))+1\}.

Proof.

It can be seen that

\sqrt{\Ann(I/J)}=\sqrt{J:I}=\bigcap\limits_{x^{\mathbb{a}}\in I\setminus J}\sqrt{J:x^{\mathbb{a}}}=\bigcap\limits_{x^{\mathbb{a}}\in\mathcal{G}(I)\setminus J}\sqrt{J:x^{\mathbb{a}}}.

This implies that

\dim(I/J)=\max\limits_{x^{\mathbb{a}}\in I\setminus J}\{\dim(S/\sqrt{J:x^{\mathbb{a}}})\}=\max\limits_{x^{\mathbb{a}}\in\mathcal{G}(I)\setminus J}\{\dim(S/\sqrt{J:x^{\mathbb{a}}})\}.

It follows further that

S/\sqrt{\Ann(I/J)}
is the Stanley-Reisner ring of

\bigcup_{x^{\mathbb{a}}\in I\setminus J}\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(J)=\bigcup_{x^{\mathbb{a}}\in\mathcal{G}(I)\setminus J}\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(J).

Particularly, the assertion follows from the well-known relationship between the dimension of a Stanley-Reisner ring and that of its corresponding simplicial complex (see, for example,
[
21
]
).
∎

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
J\subseteq I
be monomial ideals. If
I/J
is Cohen-Macaulay of dimension
\alpha
, then
\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(J)
is Cohen-Macaulay of dimension
\alpha-1
for any
x^{\mathbb{a}}\in I\setminus J
.

Proof.

Consider any
{\mathbb{a}}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
such that
x^{\mathbb{a}}\in I\setminus J
. By Lemma
7.1
,
\dim(\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(J))\leq\alpha-1
.

If
\dim(\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(J))=\alpha-1
then, by Reisner’s criterion (see, for example,
[
21
]
) for Cohen-Macaulay Stanley-Reisner rings, Lemma
3.1
, and Theorem
3.2
, it can be seen that
\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(J)
is Cohen-Macaulay.

Suppose now that
\dim(\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(J))<\alpha-1
.
For a fixed
F\in\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(J)
, consider
{\mathbb{b}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
with
{\mathbb{b}}_{i}={\mathbb{a}}_{i}
if
i\notin F
and
{\mathbb{b}}_{i}=-1
otherwise. Then,
\link_{\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(J)}F=\Delta_{\mathbb{b}}(J)
.
By Theorem
3.2
, for any
j\leq\dim(\link_{\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(J)}F)<\alpha-|F|-1
,

(7.1)

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}^{j}(\link_{\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(J)}F)=\widetilde{H}^{j}(\Delta_{\mathbb{b}}(J))=(0).

Since
x^{\mathbb{a}}\notin J
, we have
\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(J)\neq\varnothing
. Choose
F
to be a facet of
\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(J)
. Then,
\link_{\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(J)}F=\{\varnothing\}
. It follows that
\widetilde{H}^{-1}(\link_{\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(J)}F)\neq(0)
. This is a contradiction to (
7.1
), as
|F|\leq\dim(\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(J))+1<\alpha
and so
-1<\alpha-|F|-1
. The result is proved.
∎

For the remaining of this section, we consider monomial ideal quotients of the form
I^{(t)}/I^{t}
, for a monomial ideal
I\subseteq S
, and address the following question.

Question 7.3
.

Which numerical function
f:{\mathbb{N}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}
can be the dimension function

t\mapsto\dim I^{(t)}/I^{t}?

Remark 7.4
.

It follows from
[
8
, Theorem 3.3 and Proposition 5.1]
that
\dim(I^{(t)}/I^{t})
is asymptotically a constant function. The question of interest is which asymptotically constant functions are such dimension functions of monomial ideals.

We shall identify a class of monomial ideals, including edge ideals of graphs, for which the function
t\mapsto\dim I^{(t)}/I^{t}
is an increasing function.

Definition 7.5

(Herzog–Qureshi)
.

An ideal
I
in a Noetherian ring is said to satisfy the
Ratliff condition
if
I^{t+1}:I=I^{t}
for all
t\geq 1
.

It was shown in
[
9
]
that if
I
satisfies the Ratliff condition then
I
has the (strong) persistence property on associate primes of its powers. We shall prove that for ideals satisfying Ratliff condition, the dimension function of interested in
7.3
must be non-decreasing.

Theorem 7.6

(Dimension function of symbolic discrepancies)
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal that satisfies the Ratliff condition. For any
t\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we have

\dim(I^{(t)}/I^{t})\leq\dim(I^{(t+1)}/I^{t+1}).

Proof.

By Lemma
7.1
, there exists
{\mathbb{a}}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
such that

x^{\mathbb{a}}\in\mathcal{G}(I^{(t)})\setminus I^{t}
and

\dim(I^{(t)}/I^{t})=\dim(\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(I^{t}))+1.

Observe that, since
I^{t+1}:I=I^{t}
, we have

I^{t}:x^{\mathbb{a}}=\bigcap_{e\in E(G)}I^{t+1}:(x^{\mathbb{a}}\cdot x_{e}).

Thus, by the Dimension Theorem, there exists an edge
f\in G
such that

\dim(S/\sqrt{I^{t}:x^{\mathbb{a}}})=\dim\left(S\Big/\sqrt{I^{t+1}:(x^{\mathbb{a}}\cdot x_{f})}\right).

It is easy to see that
x^{\mathbb{a}}\cdot x_{f}\in I^{(t+1)}
since
x^{\mathbb{a}}\in I^{(t)}
. Furthermore, since
x^{\mathbb{a}}\not\in I^{t}
,
\dim(S/\sqrt{I^{t+1}:(x^{\mathbb{a}}\cdot x_{f})})=\dim(S/\sqrt{I^{t}:x^{\mathbb{a}}})\geq 0
, and so
x^{\mathbb{a}}\cdot x_{f}\not\in I^{t+1}
. Therefore, by Lemma
7.1
, we have

\displaystyle\dim(I^{(t)}/I^{t})

\displaystyle=\dim(S/\sqrt{I^{t+1}:(x^{\mathbb{a}}\cdot x_{f})})

\displaystyle\leq\max\{\dim(S/\sqrt{I^{t+1}:x^{\mathbb{b}}})~\big|~x^{\mathbb{b}}\in I^{(t+1)}\setminus I^{t+1}\}

\displaystyle=\dim(I^{(t+1)}/I^{t+1}).

The assertion is proved.
∎

The proof of Theorem
7.6
gives the following interesting corollary.

Corollary 7.7
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal satisfying the Ratliff condition. Suppose that
I^{(t)}/I^{t}
is a nonzero Cohen-Macaulay module for some
t\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then, for any
s\leq t
, either
I^{(s)}/I^{s}=0
or
\dim(I^{(s)}/I^{s})=\dim(I^{(t)}/I^{t}).

Proof.

Suppose that
I^{(s)}:I^{s}\not=\varnothing
. Observe that, by the Ratliff condition,
I^{t}:I^{t-s}=I^{s}
. Thus, for any
x^{\mathbb{a}}\in I^{(s)}\setminus I^{s}
, we have

I^{s}:x^{\mathbb{a}}=\bigcap_{x^{\mathbb{b}}\in\mathcal{G}(I^{t-s})}I^{t}:(x^{\mathbb{a}}\cdot x^{\mathbb{b}}).

On the other hand, by Lemmas
7.1
and
7.2
, for any
x^{\mathbb{b}}\in\mathcal{G}(I^{t-s})
such that
x^{\mathbb{a}}\cdot x^{\mathbb{b}}\not\in I^{t}
(clearly,
x^{\mathbb{a}}\cdot x^{\mathbb{b}}\in I^{(t)}
), we have

\dim(S/\sqrt{I^{t}:(x^{\mathbb{a}}\cdot x^{\mathbb{b}})})=\dim(I^{(t)}/I^{t}).

Therefore,

\dim(S/\sqrt{I^{s}:x^{\mathbb{a}}})=\dim(I^{(t)}/I^{t}).

This is true for any
x^{\mathbb{a}}\in I^{(s)}\setminus I^{s}
. Hence, by Lemma
7.1
,
I^{(s)}/I^{s}
has the same dimension as that of
I^{(t)}/I^{t}
.
∎

We shall now focus on the class of edge ideals of graphs and investigate the dimension function of its symbolic-ordinary discrepancy modules.

Corollary 7.8
.

Let
G
be a graph and let
I=I(G)
be its edge ideal. Then, the function
t\mapsto\dim I^{(t)}/I^{t}
is an increasing function.

Proof.

It follows from
[
18
, Lemma 2.12]
that
I(G)
satisfies the Ratliff condition. The assertion is now a direct consequence of Theorem
7.6
.
∎

For a graph
G
, let
t(G)
denote the
stabilization index
of
\dim(I(G)^{(t)}/I(G)^{t})
; that is,
t(G)
is the smallest integer
t_{0}
such that
\dim(I(G)^{(t)}/I(G)^{t})=\dim(I(G)^{(t_{0})}/I(G)^{t_{0}})
for all
t\geq t_{0}
.

Question 7.9
.

Let
c(G)\in{\mathbb{N}}
be such that
2c(G)+1
is the maximum length of an induced odd cycle in
G
(
c(G)=0
if
G
is bipartite). Is it true that

t(G)\leq c(G)+1?

We shall give two instances for which
t(G)
is explicitly determined and Question
7.9
has affirmative answers. Recall that a graph is called
unicyclic
if it has a unique cycle. Note also that if
G
is a
bipartite
graph, i.e.,
G
does not contain any odd cycles, then
I^{(t)}=I^{t}
for all
t\in{\mathbb{N}}
(see, for example,
[
6
,
20
]
). Thus, we shall consider unicyclic graphs which contain odd cycles. Recall also that
\alpha(G)
denotes the maximum size of an
independent
set in
G
, i.e., a set of vertices which are pairwise nonadjacent. It is easy to see that
\dim(S/I(G))=\alpha(G)
for any graph
G
.

Theorem 7.10

(Dimension function for symbolic-ordinary discrepancy of unicyclic graphs)
.

Let
G
be a unicyclic graph containing an odd cycle. Let
C_{2s+1}
denote the unique odd cycle (of length
2s+1
, for
s\geq 1
) in
G
and let
I=I(G)
. For any
t\geq s+1
, we have

\dim(I^{(t)}/I^{t})=\dim(I^{(s+1)}/I^{s+1})=\alpha(G[V\setminus N[C_{2s+1}]]).

Proof.

Using
[
5
, Theorem 3.4]
, we have
I^{(s+1)}=I^{s+1}+(x^{\mathbb{a}})
, where
x^{\mathbb{a}}=\prod\limits_{i\in C_{2s+1}}x_{i}
. By Lemma
7.1
,

\dim(I^{(s+1)}/I^{s+1})=\dim(\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(I^{s+1}))+1.

Consider any
t\geq s+1
and write
t=k(s+1)+r
, for
0\leq r\leq s
.
[
5
, Theorem 3.4]
again gives us that

I^{(t)}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}I^{t-i(s+1)}(x^{\mathbb{a}})^{i}.

By Lemma
7.1
, there exists
x^{\mathbb{b}}\in I^{t-i(s+1)}
, for some
1\leq i\leq k
, such that

\dim(I^{(t)}/I^{t})=\dim\left(S/\sqrt{I^{t}:x^{\mathbb{b}}(x^{\mathbb{a}})^{i}}\right)=\max\limits_{x^{\mathbb{c}}\in\mathcal{G}(I^{t-i(s+1)})}\{\dim(S/\sqrt{I^{t}:x^{\mathbb{c}}(x^{\mathbb{a}})^{i}})\}.

We shall show that it can be reduced to the case where
r=0
and
i=k
. Indeed, by
[
18
, Lemma 2.12]
,
I^{t}:I^{t-i(s+1)}=I^{i(s+1)}
, and so

I^{i(s+1)}:(x^{\mathbb{a}})^{i}=\bigcap_{x^{\mathbb{c}}\in\mathcal{G}(I^{t-i(s+1)})}I^{t}:[(x^{\mathbb{a}})^{i}\cdot x^{\mathbb{c}}].

Particularly,

\dim\left(S\Big/\sqrt{I^{i(s+1)}:(x^{\mathbb{a}})^{i}}\right)=\max\limits_{x^{\mathbb{c}}\in\mathcal{G}(I^{t-i(s+1)})}\{\dim(S/\sqrt{I^{t}:(x^{\mathbb{a}})^{i}\cdot x^{\mathbb{c}}})\}=\dim(S/\sqrt{I^{t}:x^{\mathbb{b}}(x^{\mathbb{a}})^{i}}).

That is, we can consider
I^{(i(s+1))}/I^{i(s+1)}
in place of
I^{(t)}/I^{t}
. Observe further that, for any
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\sqrt{I^{i(s+1)}:(x^{\mathbb{a}})^{i}}=I(G[V\setminus N[C_{2s+1}]])+(N[C_{2s+1}])

Hence,
\dim(S/\sqrt{I^{i(s+1)}:(x^{\mathbb{a}})^{i}})=\dim(S/\sqrt{I^{s+1}:x^{\mathbb{a}}})
, and the desired statement is established.
∎

Recall that a
perfect
graph is such that both the graph and its complement have no odd induced cycles of length
\geq 5
.

Theorem 7.11

(Dimension function for symbolic-ordinary discrepancy of perfect graphs)
.

Let
G
be a perfect graph and let
I=I(G)
. Then, for any
t\geq 2
, we have

\dim(I^{(t)}/I^{t})=\dim(I^{(2)}/I^{2}).

Proof.

By the celebrated Strong Perfect Graph Theorem,
G
does not contain any induced odd cycle of length
\geq 5
. Thus, either
G
is a bipartite graph or
G
contains induced triangles. We may assume that
G
is not bipartite, as the conclusion becomes trivial otherwise. We will prove the assertion for
t\geq 2
by induction. The statement is vacuously true for
t=2
. Suppose that
t\geq 3
and, by Lemma
7.1
,
\dim(I^{(t)}/I^{t})=\dim(\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(I^{t}))+1=\dim(S/\sqrt{I^{t}:x^{\mathbb{a}}})
for some
x^{\mathbb{a}}\in G(I^{(t)})\setminus I^{t}
.

By
[
22
, Theorem 3.10]
, we have

x^{\mathbb{a}}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}x_{V_{i}}

where
V_{i}
’s are cliques of
G
with
|V_{i}|\geq 2
,
\sum_{1}^{r}(|V_{i}|-1)=t
and, since
x^{\mathbb{a}}\not\in I^{t}
, there exist some values
i
such that
|V_{i}|\geq 3
.

Observe first that we may reduce to the case where
|V_{i}|\geq 3
for all
i=1,\ldots,r
. Indeed, by reindexing if necessary, we may assume that
|V_{i}|\geq 3
for
i=1,\dots,s
and
|V_{i}|=2
for
i=s+1,\dots,r
. Rewrite
x^{\mathbb{a}}=x^{\mathbb{b}}\cdot x^{\mathbb{c}}
where
x^{\mathbb{b}}=\prod_{i=1}^{s}x_{V_{i}}
and

x^{\mathbb{c}}=\prod_{i=s+1}^{r}x_{V_{i}}
. By
[
18
, Lemma 2.12]
, we have

\bigcap_{x^{\mathbb{b}}\in I^{(s)}\setminus I^{s}}I^{t-(r-s)}:x^{\mathbb{b}}=\bigcap_{x^{\mathbb{b}}\in I^{(s)}\setminus I^{s}}\bigcap_{x^{\mathbb{c}}\in I^{r-s}}I^{t}:(x^{\mathbb{b}}\cdot x^{\mathbb{c}})=\bigcap_{x^{\mathbb{a}}\in I^{(t)}\setminus I^{t}}I^{t}:x^{\mathbb{a}}.

Thus, we can replace
x^{\mathbb{a}}
by
x^{\mathbb{b}}
.

We proceed by establishing the following claim.

Claim:

\sqrt{I^{t}:x^{\mathbb{a}}}=\bigcap_{i\in\supp{\mathbb{a}}}\sqrt{I^{t-1}:x^{{\mathbb{a}}-{\mathbb{e}}_{i}}}.

Proof of the Claim:
Let
x_{F}
be a minimal (squarefree) monomial generator of
\sqrt{I^{t}:x^{\mathbb{a}}}
. We may assume that
F
is an independent set of
G
, for if
F
is not an independent set then
x_{F}\in I\subseteq\sqrt{I^{t-1}:x^{\mathbb{a}}}
obviously holds. Set
x^{\mathbb{a}}(F)=\prod_{i\notin N[F]}x_{i}^{a_{i}}
. By
[
17
, Lemma 2.18]
, we have

\sum_{j\in N(F)}a_{j}+\ord_{I}(x^{\mathbb{a}}(F))\geq t.

.

Fix
i\in\supp{\mathbb{a}}
, and let
{\mathbb{b}}={\mathbb{a}}-{\mathbb{e}}_{i}
.
If
i\in N(F)
then
\sum_{j\in N(F)}b_{j}=\sum_{j\in N(F)}a_{j}-1
and
x^{\mathbb{a}}(F)=x^{\mathbb{b}}(F)
.
On the other hand, if
i\notin N(F)
then
\sum_{j\in N(F)}b_{j}=\sum_{j\in N(F)}a_{j}
and
\ord_{I}(x^{\mathbb{b}}(F))\geq\ord_{I}(x^{\mathbb{a}}(F))-1
. Then, in both of cases, we always have

\sum_{j\in N(F)}b_{j}+\ord_{I}(x^{\mathbb{b}}(F))\geq t-1.

Using
[
17
, Lemma 2.18]
again, we get
x_{F}\in\sqrt{I^{t-1}:x^{\mathbb{b}}}
.

Conversely, we assume that
x_{F}
is a squarefree monomial generator of
\bigcap_{i\in\supp{\mathbb{a}}}\sqrt{I^{t-1}:x^{{\mathbb{a}}-{\mathbb{e}}_{i}}}
and
F
is an independent set of
G
. Then, there exists a positive integer
\alpha
such that

x_{F}^{\alpha}\cdot x^{{\mathbb{a}}-{\mathbb{e}}_{i}}\in I^{t-1}\quad\text{ for all }i\in\supp{\mathbb{a}}.

If
N(F)\cap\supp({\mathbb{a}})\not=\varnothing
, then take
j\in N(F)\cap\supp({\mathbb{a}})
. We have

x_{F}^{\alpha+1}x^{\mathbb{a}}=(x_{F}^{\alpha}\cdot x^{{\mathbb{a}}-{\mathbb{e}}_{j}})\cdot(x_{F}x_{j})\in I^{t}.

This implies that
x_{F}\in\sqrt{I^{t}:x^{\mathbb{a}}}
.

Suppose that
N(F)\cap\supp({\mathbb{a}})=\varnothing
. In this case, since
F
is an independent set of
G
,
x^{{\mathbb{a}}-{\mathbb{e}}_{i}}
must belong to
I^{t-1}
for all
i\in\supp({\mathbb{a}})
. In particular,
\deg(x^{\mathbb{a}})\geq 2t-1
. On the other hand, we have

\deg(x^{\mathbb{a}})=\sum_{i=1}^{r}|V_{i}|=t+r.

Therefore
r\geq t-1
. But
|V_{i}|\geq 3
for all
i
, it implies

t=\sum_{1}^{r}(|V_{i}|-1)\geq 2r\geq 2(t-1),

which is a contradiction. The claim is now proved. ∎

We continue with the proof of Theorem
7.11
. It follows from the previous claim that

\dim(S/\sqrt{I^{t}:x^{\mathbb{a}}})=\dim(S/\sqrt{I^{t-1}:x^{{\mathbb{a}}-{\mathbb{e}}_{i}}})

for some
i\in\supp{\mathbb{a}}
. Moreover, since
|V_{i}|\geq 3
,
[
22
, Theorem 3.10]
implies that
x^{{\mathbb{a}}-{\mathbb{e}}_{i}}\in I^{(t-1)}
. Since
\dim(S/\sqrt{I^{t-1}:x^{{\mathbb{a}}-{\mathbb{e}}_{i}}})=\dim(S/\sqrt{I^{t}:x^{\mathbb{a}}})\geq 0
, we also have
x^{{\mathbb{a}}-{\mathbb{e}}_{i}}\notin I^{t-1}
. It follows that
\dim(I^{(t)}/I^{t})\leq\dim(I^{(t-1)}/I^{t-1})
. This, together with Lemma
7.1
, gives

\dim(I^{(t)}/I^{t})=\dim(I^{(t-1)}/I^{t-1}).

By induction,
\dim(I^{(t)}/I^{t})=\dim(I^{(2)}/I^{2})
as desired.
∎

The following example shows that
\dim(I^{(t)}/I^{t})
can take 2 different values that are not
-1
, and these 2 values can be arbitrarily far apart. We do not know of any examples where
\dim(I^{(t)}/I^{t})
takes 3 different values that are not
-1
.

Example 7.12
.

For any
r\geq 1
, let
S={\Bbbk}[w_{1},\cdots,w_{5},x_{1},\cdots x_{2+r}]
and consider

I=(w_{1}w_{2},w_{2}w_{3},w_{3}w_{4},w_{4}w_{5},w_{5}w_{1},w_{1}x_{1},w_{1}x_{2},x_{1}x_{2},x_{2}x_{3},\cdots,x_{2}x_{2+r})\subseteq S.

Then,

\dim(I^{(t)}/I^{t})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}-1&\text{if }t=1\\
1&\text{if }t=2\\
r&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

.

8.
Cohen-Macaulay Symbolic-Ordinary Discrepancy Modules

We continue our investigation of monomial ideal quotients of the form
I^{(t)}/I^{t}
, where
I
is the edge ideal of a graph, and examine their Cohen-Macaulay property. Throughout this section,
G
denotes a graph on the vertex set
V=[n]
, and
I=I(G)
is its edge ideal. We also assume that
G
is not a bipartite graph, i.e.,
G
contains induced odd cycles.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
C=C_{2t+1}
be an induced odd cycle of minimum length in
G
on the vertices
\{1,\dots,2t+1\}
. Then, for any
s\geq 1
, we have
x_{C}\cdot(x_{1}x_{2})^{s-1}\in I^{(t+s)}\setminus I^{t+s}
and

\sqrt{I^{t+s}:(x_{C}\cdot(x_{1}x_{2})^{s-1})}=I+(N[C]).

Proof.

Clearly, since
x_{C}\in I^{(t+1)}
,
x_{C}\cdot(x_{1}x_{2})^{s-1}\in I^{(t+s)}
. By a degree comparison, it can also be seen that
x_{C}\cdot(x_{1}x_{2})^{s-1}\not\in I^{t+s}
.

It is furthermore easy to see that if
y\in N[C]
then
yx_{C}(x_{1}x_{2})^{s-1}\in I^{t+s}
, i.e.,
y\in I^{t+s}:(x_{C}\cdot(x_{1}x_{2})^{s-1}).
Therefore,

\sqrt{I^{t+s}:(x_{C}\cdot(x_{1}x_{2})^{s-1})}\supseteq I+(N[C]).

On the other hand, let
x_{F}
be a minimal monomial generator of
\sqrt{I^{t+s}:(x_{C}\cdot(x_{1}x_{2})^{s-1})}
. Then, either
x_{F}\in I
or
F
is an independent set of
G
. In the case that
F
is an independent set, by
[
17
, Lemma 2.18]
, we have

\sum_{j\in N(F)}\ord_{x_{j}}(x_{C}\cdot(x_{1}x_{2})^{s-1})\geq(t+s)-\ord_{I}(x_{C}\cdot(x_{1}x_{2})^{s-1})=(t+s)-(t+s-1)=1.

Hence,
N(F)\cap C\not=\varnothing
, i.e.,
F\cap N[C]\not=\varnothing
. In other words,
x_{F}\in(N[C])
, and we deduce that

\sqrt{I^{t+s}:(x_{C}\cdot(x_{1}x_{2})^{s-1})}\subseteq I+(N[C]).

The lemma is established.
∎

Lemma 8.2
.

Assuming the same hypothesis as in Lemma
8.1
. Suppose that
y\in N[C]\ C
and, in particular,
y\in N[1]
. Then, for any
s\geq 2
, we have
x_{C}\cdot(x_{1}x_{2})^{s-2}(x_{1}y)\in I^{(t+s)}\setminus I^{t+s}
and

\sqrt{I^{t+s}:(x_{C}\cdot(x_{1}x_{2})^{s-2}(x_{1}y))}=I+(N[C])+(N(y)).

Proof.

The proof is almost identical to that of Lemma
8.1
. As before, we first observe that
x_{C}\cdot(x_{1}x_{2})^{s-2}(x_{1}y)\in I^{(t+s)}\setminus I^{t+s}
. It is also easy to see that

\sqrt{I^{t+s}:(x_{C}\cdot(x_{1}x_{2})^{s-2}(x_{1}y))}\supseteq I+(N[C])+(N(y)).

To see the reserve inclusion, we again consider a minimal squarefree monomial generator
x_{F}
of
\sqrt{I^{t+s}:(x_{C}\cdot(x_{1}x_{2})^{s-2}(x_{1}y))}
. We may further assume that
F
is an independent set in
G
and that
F\cap N[C]=\varnothing.
By the same argument, making use of
[
17
, Lemma 2.18]
, we then get that
F\cap N(y)\not=\varnothing
. This establishes the desired reverse inclusion and proves the lemma.
∎

Lemma 8.3
.

Assuming the same hypotheses as in Lemma
8.2
. Suppose that
z\in N(y)\setminus N[C]
. Then, for
s\geq 3
, we have
x_{C}\cdot(x_{1}x_{2})^{s-3}(x_{1}y)(yz)\in I^{(t+s)}\setminus I^{t+s}
and

\sqrt{I^{t+s}:(x_{C}\cdot(x_{1}x_{2})^{s-3}(x_{1}y)(yz))}=I+(N[C])+(N(y,z)).

Proof.

The proof follows in exactly the same of arguments as in Lemmas
8.1
and
8.2
. The details are left to the interested reader.
∎

Recall from Remark
7.4
that
\dim(I^{(t)}/I^{t})
is asymptotically a constant. As before, let
t(G)
denote the smallest integer starting from which
\dim(I(G)^{(t)}/I(G)^{t})
is a constant. Also, let
c(G)\in{\mathbb{N}}
be such that
2c(G)+1
is the maximum length of an induced cycle in
G
(
c(G)=0
if
G
is a bipartite graph). Our last main result is stated as follows.

Theorem 8.4

(Cohen-Macaulay symbolic-ordinary discrepancy modules)
.

Let
G
be a connected graph that is not bipartite and let
I=I(G)
be its edge ideal.
Set

t_{0}:=\max\{t(G),c(G)\}+3.

The following are equivalent:

(1)

I^{(t)}/I^{t}
is Cohen-Macaulay for all
t\geq 1
;

(2)

I^{(t)}/I^{t}
is Cohen-Macaulay for some
t\geq t_{0}
;

(3)

For all
t\geq 1
, either
I^{(t)}/I^{t}=(0)
or
\dim(I^{(t)}/I^{t})=0
;

(4)

For any induced odd cycle
C
in
G
, we have
N[C]=[n]
.

Proof.

It is obviously that
(3)\Longrightarrow(1)\Longrightarrow(2)
. We shall prove
(2)\Longrightarrow(4)\Longrightarrow(3)
.

(2)\Longrightarrow(4)
. Suppose that
M=I^{(t)}/I^{t}
is Cohen-Macaulay for some
t\geq\max\{t(G),c(G)\}+3
. Let
\alpha=\dim(M)
. By
[
3
, Theorem 4.1]
and
[
12
, Corollary 4.5]
,
M\not=0
, and so
\alpha\geq 0
. Using Lemma
7.2
, we get that
\Delta_{\mathbb{a}}(I^{t})
is Cohen-Macaulay of dimension
\alpha-1
for any
x^{\mathbb{a}}\in I^{(t)}\setminus I^{t}
. It then follows from Lemma
7.1
that
S/\sqrt{I^{t}:x^{\mathbb{a}}}
is Cohen-Macaulay of dimension
\alpha
for all
x^{\mathbb{a}}\in I^{(t)}\setminus I^{t}
.

Consider any induced odd cycle
C
in
G
and, without loss of generality, assume that the vertices of
C
are
\{1,2,\ldots,2t^{\prime}+1\}
, for some
t^{\prime}\leq c(G)
. Write
t=t^{\prime}+s
, for some
s\geq 3
. Suppose, by contradiction, that
[n]\setminus N[C]\not=\varnothing
. Then, by the connectivity of
G
, there exist vertices
y,z
of
G
such that

y\in N[C]\setminus C
,
z\not\in N[C]
, and
yz\in G
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
y\in N(x_{1})
. Set

\displaystyle x^{\mathbb{b}}

\displaystyle=x_{C}\cdot(x_{1}x_{2})^{s-1}

\displaystyle x^{\mathbb{c}}

\displaystyle=x_{C}\cdot(x_{1}x_{2})^{s-3}(x_{1}y)(yz).

Since
x_{C}\in I^{(t^{\prime}+1)}
, both
x^{\mathbb{b}}
and
x^{\mathbb{c}}
are in
I^{(t^{\prime}+s)}
. Moreover, by a degree comparison, it can be seen that both
x^{\mathbb{b}}
and
x^{\mathbb{c}}
are not in
I^{t^{\prime}+s}
. Thus, by Lemmas
8.1
and
8.3
, we have

\dim\left(S\big/I+(N[C])\right)=\dim\left(S\big/I+(N[C])+(N(y))+(N(z))\right)=\alpha.

On the other hand, observe that if
F
is an independent set of the graph associated to
I+(N[C])+(N(y))+(N(z))
with maximum cardinality
\alpha
, then
z\not\in F
and
F\cup\{z\}
is an independent set in the graph associated to
I+(N[C])
. This implies that

\dim\left(S\big/I+(N[C])\right)\geq\alpha+1>\alpha,

a contradiction. We have shown that
N[C]=[n]
for any induced odd cycle
C
in
G
.

(4)\Longrightarrow(3)
. Suppose that
N[C]=[n]
for any induced odd cycle
C
in
G
. It follows from
[
12
, Theorem 4.4]
that, for any
t\in{\mathbb{N}}
, either
I^{t}
has no embedded primes, or the embedded primes of
I^{t}
are of the form
P_{F}=(x_{i}~\big|~x_{i}\in F)
, for some
F\subseteq V
, such that there exists
U\subseteq V
for which
N[U]\subseteq F
and the induced subgraph
G_{U}
of
G
over
U
is a
strongly non-bipartite
graph (under additional conditions). Since
G_{U}
is a non-bipartite graph, it contains an induced odd cycle
C_{1}
. Particularly,
N[U]\supseteq N[C_{1}]=[n]
, which implies that
F=[n]
and
P_{F}={\mathfrak{m}}
. That is, for any
t\in{\mathbb{N}}
, either
I^{t}=I^{(t)}
or the only embedded prime of
I^{t}
is
{\mathfrak{m}}
.
In the latter case we have
I^{t}=I^{(t)}\cap{\mathfrak{q}}
, where
\sqrt{{\mathfrak{q}}}={\mathfrak{m}}
. Therefore,

\dim(I^{(t)}/I^{t})=\dim\dfrac{I^{(t)}}{I^{(t)}\cap{\mathfrak{q}}}=\dim\dfrac{I^{(t)}+{\mathfrak{q}}}{{\mathfrak{q}}}=0.

The result is proved.
∎

Remark 8.5
.

Let
G
be a simple connected graph on
[n]
and let
I=I(G)
its edge ideal. Fix
t\geq 1
and set

Q_{t}(G)\ :=\ I^{(t)}/I^{t}.

Assume
\dim Q_{t}(G)\geq 2
. Then
Q_{t}(G)
is generalized Cohen-Macaulay if and only if it is Cohen-Macaulay on the punctured spectrum, i.e., if and only if
(Q_{t}(G))_{x_{F}}
is Cohen-Macaulay for every nonempty monomial
x_{F}
.

Equivalently, it suffices to test
x_{F}=\prod_{i\in F}x_{i}
with
F
a nonempty independent set of
G
(i.e.,
\varnothing\not=F\in\text{Ind}(G)
). In this case one has a natural isomorphism

(Q_{t}(G))_{x_{F}}\ \cong\ {\Bbbk}[x_{i}^{\pm 1}:i\in F]\ \otimes_{\Bbbk}\ Q_{t}\bigl(G-N_{G}[F]\bigr).

Hence,
Q_{t}(G)
is generalized Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
Q_{t}(G-N_{G}[F])
is Cohen-Macaulay for every nonempty independent set
F
. By
Theorem

8.4
, this is equivalent to the condition that, for any independent set
F
in
G
and any odd induced cycle
C
in
G-N_{G}[F]
,
N_{G}[C]\cup N_{G}[F]=[n]
.

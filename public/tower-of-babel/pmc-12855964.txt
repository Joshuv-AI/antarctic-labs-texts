Enhanced UV resistance of polypropylene via copper nanoparticle incorporation for outdoor applications
Authors: Mohammed Baafif, Hany S. Abdo, Abdulmohsen Albedah
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This study investigates the effect of copper nanoparticles (Cu NPs) on the ultraviolet (UV) resistance and long-term thermal and mechanical stability of polypropylene (PP) composites. Cu NPs were incorporated into PP at varying concentrations (0–10 wt%) to evaluate their influence on size distribution, melt flow index, density, tensile and flexural properties, Shore D hardness, and thermal behavior using Thermogravimetric Analysis (TGA) and Differential Scanning Calorimetry (DSC). Accelerated aging tests over 1000 h were conducted to assess durability under simulated environmental exposure. Results show that Cu NPs, particularly at 1–2 wt%, considerably improve mechanical performance and thermal stability while mitigating photo-oxidative degradation. Reinforced composites exhibited enhanced yield strength, elongation, stiffness, and energy absorption compared to pure PP after aging. DSC and TGA analyses revealed better retention of crystallinity and delayed thermal degradation in Cu NP-filled PP. Although higher nanoparticle loadings (> 5 wt%) led to diminished benefits due to stress concentration and possible agglomeration, the 2 wt% formulation demonstrated the optimal balance between UV protection and mechanical integrity. These findings highlight the potential of Cu NP-reinforced PP for outdoor and engineering applications requiring enhanced weatherability and long-term performance.

Introduction

Polypropylene (PP) is one of the most widely used thermoplastic polymers worldwide, valued for its excellent chemical resistance, low density (0.90–0.91 g/cm³), and ease of processing into a broad range of consumer and industrial products1,2. However, despite these favorable properties, PP is highly susceptible to degradation under ultraviolet (UV) radiation, particularly in outdoor applications. Exposure to UV light leads to a rapid decline in mechanical strength, aesthetic appearance, and overall functional lifespan. This degradation arises from the energetic overlap between solar UV photons (295–400 nm, 300–420 kJ/mol) and the bond dissociation energies of PP’s carbon–carbon backbone (350–400 kJ/mol), which triggers chain scission, carbonyl groups formation, and eventual embrittlement. Unstabilized PP can lose up to 70% of its tensile strength within just a few days of intense UV exposure, resulting in cracking, discoloration, and premature failure3,4. The economic and environmental consequences of such degradation are considerable, prompting a growing demand for effective stabilization techniques to extend PP’s service life applications such as construction, automotive components, packaging, and outdoor textiles.

Conventional UV stabilizers for PP function through mechanisms such as UV absorption, radical scavenging, or peroxide decomposition; however, each category presents notable limitations. Hindered amine light stabilizers (HALS), widely favored for their radical-scavenging efficiency at low loadings (0.5–1.25 wt%), suffer from thermal degradation above 300 °C and reduced effectiveness in thin-walled products or under prolonged UV-B exposure5–7. Inorganic UV blockers such as titanium dioxide (TiO₂) and zinc oxide (ZnO) operate by absorbing and scattering incident UV radiation but typically require high loadings (7.5–15 wt%) to provide sufficient protection. This can lead to particle agglomeration, reduced mechanical performance, and, in moisture-rich environments, even catalyze oxidative degradation. Notably, ZnO nanoparticles may generate reactive oxygen species (ROS) under UV irradiation, paradoxically accelerating polymer degradation8,9. Other additives, such as carbon black, impart undesirable coloration, while organic UV absorbers are prone to leaching or photodegradation. These challenges highlight the urgent need for multifunctional stabilizers that can improve UV resistance while preserving mechanical integrity and ensuring environmental safety and processing compatibility.

Copper nanoparticles (CuNPs) have emerged as a promising alternative, offering synergistic mechanisms that address multiple degradation pathways simultaneously. Owing to their surface plasmon resonance, CuNPs efficiently absorb UV radiation—particularly in the UV-A range—with peak absorption between 550 and 600 nm9,10. Beyond acting as physical UV screens, the redox cycling of Cu⁺/Cu²⁺ ions catalyzes hydroperoxides (ROOH) decomposition, suppressing the radical chain reactions that accelerate polymer degradation11,12. CuNPs also act as radical scavengers, neutralizing carbon-centered (R•) and peroxyl (ROO•) radicals generated under UV exposure3.

This multifunctionality is further enhanced by the intrinsic antimicrobial properties of CuNPs, which inhibit microbial colonization on PP surfaces—a considerable benefit for applications such as outdoor textiles, medical devices, and food packaging, where biofilm formation can intensify deterioration13–15. Moreover, green synthesis methods using plant extracts (e.g., Syzygium cumini, seedless dates) provide an environmentally friendly alternative to conventional synthesis by eliminating toxic reductants and supplying natural capping agents that improve nanoparticle stability and dispersion9,10. The nanoscale dimensions of CuNPs (typically 50–200 nm) facilitate uniform distribution within the PP matrix, strengthening interfacial interactions while minimizing light scattering that could otherwise impair optical clarity16,17.

Despite these advantages, incorporating CuNPs into PP presents challenges related to oxidation susceptibility, agglomeration, and concentration-dependent performance trade-offs. Copper’s inherent tendency to oxidize in air necessitates surface functionalization or in situ reduction during processing to preserve its metallic state and associated functionalities18,19. CuNPs are also prone to agglomeration, particularly during melt-processing extrusion and injection molding. This agglomeration can create stress concentration sites, diminishing mechanical performance, reductions in tensile strength at loadings above 2 wt% have been reported20,21.

Achieving uniform nanoparticle dispersion requires compatibilizers, such as maleated polypropylene (PP-g-MA), along with tailored processing strategies including ultrasonication and optimized screw configurations in twin-screw extruders22. While low CuNP concentrations (0.5–2 wt%) have been shown to enhance crystallinity and UV stability, higher loadings (> 5 wt%) may induce performance drawbacks such as recombination losses or oxidative cascades associated with interfacial defects16,17. Although the antimicrobial activity of CuNPs—particularly against Gram-negative bacteria such as E. coli and certain fungi—is well established, their role as UV stabilizers in PP remains relatively underexplored compared to conventional systems such as titanium dioxide TiO₂ and HALS. Previous studies on Cu/PP composites have primarily focused on electrical conductivity and antibacterial performance, with limited attention to accelerated UV aging, retention of thermal properties, or potential synergies with crystallinity and morphology23,24. To date, no systematic evaluation has addressed the retention of mechanical and thermal properties after accelerated UV-aging (ASTM G154) across a broad Cu loading range (0–10 wt%).

This study seeks to fill that gaps by systematically evaluating CuNPs as multifunctional UV stabilizers for PP, with emphasis on processing–structure–property relationships under accelerated aging. Green-synthesized CuNPs (50–100 nm) were incorporated into PP via twin-screw extrusion at loadings of 0.5–5 wt%. Accelerated UV aging was conducted in accordance with ASTM G154 Cycle 1, simulating 2 years of outdoor exposure using UVA-340 lamps (0.89 W/m², 40 °C). Mechanical, thermal, and morphological properties were characterized after cumulative UV doses.

The objectives of this work are to: (1) quantify UV-shielding performance through carbonyl index evolution and yellowness index measurements; (2) evaluate retention of mechanical properties, including tensile strength and elongation at break, post-aging; and (3) assess thermal stability using thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) and differential scanning calorimetry (DSC). By elucidating structure–property–degradation relationships, this study aims to validate CuNPs as high-performance stabilizers for PP, supporting extended service lifespans in UV-intensive applications while reducing reliance on conventional additives with greater environmental and processing drawbacks.

Materials and methods

Material

The matrix material used in this study was polypropylene (PP), a thermoplastic polymer widely recognized for its favourable mechanical properties, chemical resistance, and cost-effectiveness. PP pellets (grade 575P) were procured from SABIC and characterized by a melt flow rate of 11 g/10 min (230 °C, 2.16 kg load), a density of 905 kg/m³, a tensile yield strength of 35 MPa, an elongation at yield of 11%, a flexural modulus of 1600 MPa, and a notched Izod impact strength of 22 J/m.

Copper nanoparticles (CuNPs), incorporated to enhance the UV resistance of the PP matrix, were supplied by SkySpring Nanomaterials, Inc. The nanoparticles were provided as a dry powder with a 99.9% purity, an average particle size of ~ 100 nm, and a density of 8900 kg/m³, as specified in the accompanying Certificate of Analysis.

Sample Preparation

To achieve uniform dispersion of CuNPs within the PP matrix, composites were prepared using a 16 mm benchtop twin-screw extruder (Model LW-40, LABTECH Engineering Co.), equipped with a side feeder, water bath, and pelletizer. Extrusion parameters were optimized to minimize agglomeration and promote even distribution, with a temperature profile of 170 °C (feed zone), 190 °C (compression zone), 200 °C (melting zone), and 210 °C (die zone), and a screw speed of 250 rpm. Mixing pressure was maintained below 60% of motor capacity to prevent mechanical overload.

The resulting composite pellets were injection molded into standardized specimens using an EM220-SVP/2 injection molding machine at a barrel temperature of 200 °C, with an injection speed of 1.4 s and a holding time of 50 s. Post-molding, samples were visually inspected, dimensionally verified, and subjected to preliminary mechanical and thermal testing to confirm the absence of defects and ensure nanoparticle homogeneity.

Mechanical properties and UV resistance

The mechanical performance and UV resistance of PP/CuNPs composites were evaluated with emphasis on the influence of UV exposure.

Dynamic Light Scattering (DLS) was used to characterize nanoparticles size distribution prior to compounding. Each sample was analyzed in duplicate confirming size uniformity, and supporting homogenous composites formation.

Composite density was measured according to ASTM D792 using an Ohaus analytical balancer. A progressive increase in density with Cu content confirmed effective incorporation of Cu nanoparticles. Melt Flow Rate (MFR) testing, conducted in accordance with ASTM D1238, assessed the influence of CuNPs on viscosity and processability.

Tensile properties were measured using an Instron Universal Testing Machine in accordance with ASTM D638. Flexural strength and modulus were determined following ASTM D790. Shore D hardness was measured according to ASTM D2240. Key parameters, including tensile strength, elongation at break, and modulus of elasticity, were evaluated before and after UV exposure to assess the effects of environmental aging on mechanical integrity.

Unless otherwise stated, each measurement was performed on two separately injection-molded specimens (n = 2) prepared from the same injection-molding batch for each formulation. Results are reported as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Given the limited replication per condition and the absence of batch-to-batch replication, inferential statistics (e.g., ANOVA or t-tests) were not applied; differences are discussed as observed trends supported by the reported variability.

Thermal analysis

Thermal behavior was characterized using Thermogravimetric Analysis (TGA) and Differential Scanning Calorimetry (DSC). TGA was performed on a Mettler Toledo TGA/DSC 1 Star system, heating 1–5 mg of each sample from room temperature to 600 °C at 10 °C/min under nitrogen atmosphere. Onset degradation temperature and thermal stability were derived from the TGA curves. DSC measurements were carried out between 30 and 250 °C at a heating/cooling rate of 10 °C/min under nitrogen. The first heating cycle was discarded to remove prior thermal history, and crystallinity was determined from the second heating run. Results and discussion

Morphology and density

The size distribution of copper nanoparticles is a critical factor influencing the performance of polymer composites, particularly their resistance to UV degradation. Dynamic light scattering (DLS) analysis revealed an average nanoparticle size of 215 nm, with a distribution ranging from 90 to 691 nm. This relatively narrow size range supports uniform dispersion within the polypropylene (PP) matrix, contributing to enhanced mechanical strength and UV-shielding capability.

Density measurements, conducted using the Archimedes principle, showed a progressive increase in composite density with rising copper nanoparticle content. This trend is attributed to the higher intrinsic density of copper compared to PP. The density of the fabricated composites ranged from 90% to 99% of theoretical values for CuNP loadings between 0% and 10 wt%, respectively. These changes directly influence composite weight and structural performance.

Mechanical properties (tensile, flexural, hardness, MFR)

Melt flow rate (MFR), measured at 230 °C under a 2.16 kg load in accordance with ASTM D1238, decreased with increasing Cu content, indicating higher viscosity due to nanoparticle–polymer chain interactions. An exception was observed at 10 wt% Cu, where MFR increased (Table 1). This behavior is likely due to agglomeration-induced defects and partial lubrication between polymer chains, which disrupt the melt structure—consistent with reports on high-loading nanofiller composites25,26. These findings suggest a threshold effect, beyond which nanoparticles may enhance flow properties by reducing interchain friction or altering melt dynamics.

The initial tensile strength of neat PP was 30 MPa. Incorporation of CuNPs considerably influenced the mechanical response of the matrix.

At low concentrations (1–2 wt%), yield strength increased by up to 5% and elongation at break by 5.7%, reflecting improved stress transfer and interfacial adhesion between the nanoparticles and polymer chains. At higher loadings (10 wt%), however, both yield strength and elongation decreased—by 2% and 7.1%, respectively—likely due to stress concentration sites arising from nanoparticle agglomeration.

The optimal performance was achieved at 2 wt% CuNP loading, yielding a peak strength of 36.4 MPa while maintaining improved elongation. These results highlight a concentration-dependent balance: moderate reinforcement improves strength and ductility, whereas excessive loading diminishes performance due to structural inhomogeneities.

Thermal analysis (DSC, TGA)

Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) results (Table 2) indicate that incorporation of copper nanoparticles (CuNPs) enhances the thermal stability of polypropylene (PP). With increasing CuNP content, degradation temperatures shifted upward: pure PP exhibited an onset degradation temperature of 452 °C and a peak at 469 °C, whereas the composite with 10 wt% CuNPs showed corresponding increases to 465 °C and 478 °C. The residual weight closely matched the CuNP loading (e.g., 1.5% residue for 1 wt% Cu and 10.1% for 10 wt% Cu), confirming effective nanoparticle incorporation and their role as thermal barriers.

Differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) results (Table 3) demonstrate that CuNPs act as efficient nucleating agents, particularly at low concentrations (1–2 wt%). At 1 wt% Cu, the crystallization temperature increased by 4.4% to 119 °C, while crystallinity rose by 20.4% to 50.2%. At higher concentrations (10 wt%), however, crystallinity decreased to 39.1%, suggesting a diminishing nucleating effect due to particle agglomeration or restricted molecular mobility. The 11 °C increase in melting temperature (Tm) at 5 wt% Cu further indicates combined nucleating effects and constrained chain mobility, consistent with previous reports27,28.

These findings highlight the dual role of CuNPs in improving thermal degradation resistance and influencing crystallization behavior in PP composites, with optimal performance achieved at low nanoparticle loadings.

UV-aging effects

Tensile testing

After 1000 h of accelerated aging, considerable differences in tensile properties were observed between pure polypropylene (PP) and PP composites reinforced with copper nanoparticles (CuNPs) (Fig. 1).

Initially, pure PP exhibited a tensile strength of 34.7 MPa, which declined sharply to 20.1 MPa after aging—a 42.1% reduction. In contrast, CuNP-reinforced PP retained much of its strength, with post-aging values ranging from 31.0 to 33.3 MPa compared to initial values of 34.1 to 36.4 MPa. This represents a much smaller reduction of 5.8–9.3%, indicating that CuNPs considerably enhance the retention of tensile strength under prolonged UV exposure.

Elongation at break also decreased markedly in pure PP, from 7.0% to 4.2% (− 40%). However, PP composites with CuNPs maintained higher elongation values after aging, ranging from 6.4% to 7.5%. These values correspond to negligible or modest reductions (0–13.5%), confirming that CuNPs help preserve ductility over time.

The elastic modulus of pure PP dropped from 1494 MPa to 915 MPa (− 38.7%), whereas CuNP composites retained better stiffness, with post-aging values of 1218–1301 MPa compared to initial values of 1490–1522 MPa. This corresponds to smaller reductions of 0–18.7%.

Similarly, the modulus of resilience, reflecting energy absorption capability, decreased in pure PP from 1.91 MPa to 0.60 MPa (− 68.6%). In CuNP composites, post-aging values ranged from 1.5 to 1.9 MPa, corresponding to reductions of only 0–21.4% from initial values of 1.69–2.04 MPa.

Overall, these results demonstrate that CuNPs improve the long-term mechanical stability of PP, enhancing the retention of yield strength, ductility, stiffness, and resilience during UV exposure. The most favorable outcomes were observed at low concentrations (1–2 wt%), which minimized performance losses while maintaining mechanical integrity. Although higher loadings also improved UV resistance, their effectiveness was slightly reduced, likely due to nanoparticle agglomeration or stress concentration effects.

These findings, supported by Fig. 1; Table 4, and Fig. 2, underscore the potential of CuNPs to considerably improve the durability and mechanical performance of PP-based materials in long-term outdoor applications.

Flexural testing

The flexural properties of pure polypropylene (PP) and PP reinforced with copper nanoparticles (CuNPs) after 1000 h of accelerated aging reveal substantial differences, underscoring the stabilizing effect of the nanoparticles.

Initially, pure PP exhibited a flexural strength of 26.2 MPa and a flexural modulus of 1397 MPa. After aging, these values dropped to 17.9 MPa (− 31.7%) and 925 MPa (− 33.8%), respectively. In contrast, PP composites containing CuNPs demonstrated superior retention of flexural properties.

For PP with 1 wt% CuNPs, flexural strength decreased by 21.6% (24.1 → 18.9 MPa), and the flexural modulus declined by 30.1% (1486 → 1039 MPa). The best performance was observed at 2 wt% CuNPs, where flexural strength decreased by only 20.8% (24.5 → 19.4 MPa) and flexural modulus by just 9.7% (1516 → 1369 MPa), indicating excellent stability under UV aging.

At higher loadings, composites retained notable performance. PP with 5 wt% CuNPs showed a 36% reduction in flexural strength (22.5 → 14.4 MPa) and a 23.1% reduction in modulus (1407 → 1082 MPa). The 10 wt% CuNP composites exhibited a 31.7% reduction in flexural strength (23.0 → 15.7 MPa), while the modulus decreased only slightly—by 2.1% (1396 → 1367 MPa), suggesting a stabilizing threshold effect at high concentrations.

These results demonstrate that CuNP addition not only enhances the initial flexural properties of PP but also considerably improves its long-term durability by mitigating mechanical degradation. Figures 3 and 4 illustrate the flexural performance trends and compare aged and non-aged samples.

The observed reduction in flexural modulus at higher Cu nanoparticle contents (e.g., at 10 wt %) is likely due to stress concentration caused by nanoparticle agglomeration, which impairs load transfer. Similar behavior has been observed in PP/nano-α-Al₂O₃ composites: Mirjalili et al29. reported an increase in modulus up to moderate filler levels, followed by a drop when α-Al₂O₃ particles aggregate, reducing effective reinforcement.

Hardness test

Shore D hardness measurements of pure polypropylene (PP) and PP reinforced with copper nanoparticles (CuNPs) after 1000 h of accelerated aging revealed notable enhancements, underscoring the stabilizing effect of CuNPs on the polymer matrix (Fig. 5).

Initially, pure PP exhibited a Shore D hardness of 67.1, which increased to 73.4 after aging—a 9.4% rise, likely attributable to post-crystallization or further curing of the polymer during UV exposure. The incorporation of CuNPs produced a progressive increase in initial hardness, rising from 71.1 at 1 wt% to 74.3 at 10 wt%.

Although our DSC results (Table 2 vs. Table 5) show a decrease in degree of crystallinity after UV-aging, the hardness continues to increase over time. This seemingly contradictory behavior can be explained by oxidative crosslinking and densification occurring in the amorphous regions: free radical formation under UV and oxygen leads to crosslink bonds which stiffen the otherwise less crystalline matrix, restricting chain mobility. Such behavior has been documented by Rodriguez et al30, who observed that PP under UV exposure exhibits increased hardness or modulus early in aging, even while crystallinity drops.

Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA/DSC)

Thermal degradation analysis of pure polypropylene (PP) and PP reinforced with copper nanoparticles (CuNPs) after 1000 h of accelerated aging demonstrated considerable improvements in thermal stability due to the presence of CuNPs. Key parameters evaluated included onset temperature, peak temperature, outset temperature, and residual weight for samples containing 0, 1, 2, 5, and 10 wt% CuNPs. Pure PP exhibited an onset degradation temperature of 445 °C, a peak temperature of 469 °C, an outset temperature of 491 °C, and a residual weight of − 1.2%, indicating complete decomposition and minor mass loss. In contrast, CuNP-reinforced composites showed consistent improvements in all thermal stability parameters.

These results indicate that CuNPs delay the thermal degradation process by elevating the onset, peak, and final decomposition temperatures. The higher residual mass observed in composites confirms the presence of thermally stable copper content, reflecting enhanced structural integrity and potential char formation during degradation. The DSC analysis shows that Copper Nanoparticles (Cu NPs) act as effective nucleating agents in Polypropylene (PP). Lower concentrations (optimal at 1%) considerably enhance thermal properties, increasing the crystallization temperature (Tc) and the degree of crystallinity by promoting earlier, more controlled crystallization. However, higher concentrations (10%) lead to reduced crystallization enthalpy and crystallinity, suggesting that optimal nanoparticle concentration is crucial for maximizing thermal stability.

Overall, incorporation of CuNPs considerably improves both the initial and long-term thermal stability of PP, making the composites more resistant to high-temperature environments. These improvements are summarized in Fig. 6 (a-c) and Table 5, which compare the thermal degradation behavior of aged and unaged samples.

Differential scanning calorimetry (DSC)

After 1000 h of accelerated aging, the thermal properties of pure polypropylene (PP) and PP reinforced with copper nanoparticles (CuNPs) showed considerable differences, as summarized in the corresponding figure and table.

Pure PP exhibited a crystallization temperature (Tc) of 116 °C, a crystallization enthalpy of 43.2 J/g, a melting temperature (Tm) of 158 °C, a melting enthalpy of − 31.7 J/g, and a degree of crystallinity of 15.3%. These values reflect notable thermal degradation and reduced structural order due to prolonged UV exposure. In contrast, CuNPs acted as effective nucleating agents, increasing Tc, Tm, enthalpies, and overall crystallinity—particularly at low concentrations (1–2 wt%). These enhancements indicate improved molecular ordering and thermal stability resulting from the presence of well-dispersed nanoparticles.

At higher loadings (5–10 wt%), however, the benefits diminished. This reduction is likely due to nanoparticle agglomeration or restricted polymer chain mobility, which disrupt uniform crystal formation and reduce overall crystallinity.

In summary, the incorporation of CuNPs considerably improved the thermal behavior and structural integrity of PP after long-term aging, with optimal effects observed at 1–2 wt%. These enhancements highlight the potential of CuNP-reinforced PP composites for applications requiring high thermal stability and long-term durability.

Comparison of initial and aged DSC analysis

A comparative analysis of Differential Scanning Calorimetry (DSC) data before and after 1000 h of aging highlights the considerable influence of copper nanoparticles (CuNPs) on the thermal behavior and crystalline structure of polypropylene (PP).

Initially, crystallization temperatures (Tc) ranged from 114 °C for pure PP to 122 °C for CuNP-reinforced PP, increasing with nanoparticle content. After 1000 h of aging, Tc values slightly increased across all samples (116–122 °C), indicating enhanced nucleation stability and preservation of crystalline domains over time.

Crystallization enthalpy initially ranged from 76.0 to 97.8 J/g, with the highest value observed in the PP + 1% CuNP sample. After aging, pure PP exhibited a sharp decline to 43.2 J/g, whereas the PP + 1% and 2% CuNP composites retained high enthalpy values (93.1 and 95.2 J/g, respectively), suggesting sustained or enhanced nucleation efficiency due to CuNP stabilization.

Melting temperatures (Tm) initially measured 154 °C for pure PP and increased up to 165 °C with CuNP reinforcement. After aging, all samples exhibited further increases (158–166 °C), reflecting improved thermal stability and resistance to chain scission.

Melting enthalpy of pure PP declined drastically from − 86.4 J/g to − 31.7 J/g after 1000 h, indicating severe loss of crystalline content. In contrast, the PP + 1% and 2% CuNP composites maintained relatively high values, demonstrating effective preservation of crystalline domains during long-term exposure.

The degree of crystallinity was initially highest in PP + 1% CuNPs (50.2%) and decreased with higher loadings, reaching 39.1% in PP + 10% CuNPs. After aging, crystallinity in pure PP fell sharply to 15.3%, whereas the 1% and 2% CuNP composites retained high values of 40.8% and 46.6%, respectively. Although the PP + 10% CuNP sample also decreased to 20.4%, it still remained substantially higher than aged pure PP, confirming the stabilizing effect of CuNPs.

Overall, pure PP showed considerable degradation in thermal characteristics after 1000 h, including reductions in enthalpies and crystallinity. In contrast, CuNP-reinforced PP maintained superior thermal stability and crystalline structure, particularly at 1–2 wt%. These findings highlight the role of CuNPs as effective nucleating and stabilizing agents in long-term thermal performance.

Conclusions

This study demonstrates that the incorporation of copper nanoparticles (CuNPs) considerably enhances the resistance of polypropylene (PP) to ultraviolet (UV) degradation and environmental aging. Optimal concentrations, particularly 1–2 wt%, provide dual functionality by improving both mechanical performance and UV stability.

Key findings show that CuNPs effectively mitigate photo-oxidative degradation, as evidenced by superior retention of mechanical, thermal, and structural properties in aged composites compared to neat PP. The nanoparticles act as protective agents, limiting polymer chain scission and preserving structural integrity under prolonged UV exposure.

Differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) analysis of aged specimens further confirms the stabilizing role of CuNPs, with improved crystallization behavior and thermal stability. While higher CuNP loadings (> 5 wt%) reduced performance gains due to stress concentration and particle agglomeration, the 2 wt% CuNP formulation offered the best balance of UV protection and mechanical robustness.

Overall, CuNP-reinforced PP emerges as a strong candidate for outdoor applications requiring enhanced weatherability and extended service life. Future research should explore synergistic effects of CuNPs with other stabilizers or additives to further improve long-term durability under extreme environmental conditions. In addition, validation through FTIR carbonyl index, yellowness index, oxidation induction time, and extended outdoor weathering is recommended to complement the findings of this study.

Prior Specific Biologic Use as a Potential Risk Factor for Administration-related Reactions to Ixekizumab after Excipient Modification: A Single-centre Retrospective Study
Authors: Hiroyoshi Nozaki, Masaru Honma, Yasuyuki Fujita
Published: 2026

Ixekizumab is a humanized monoclonal antibody targeting interleukin-17A and is widely used for the treatment of psoriasis with high efficacy (1). Initially, ixekizumab was associated with a relatively high incidence of administration-related reactions (ARRs), particularly injection-site pain and erythema. Since late 2022, the ixekizumab formulation has been modified by removing sodium citrate dihydrate, a formulation excipient that is a potential contributor to injection-related discomfort, including injection-site pain and ARRs. Although some reports indicate that this excipient modification reduced the ARR incidence, a substantial proportion of patients continue to experience these reactions (2). Regarding the risk factors for the modified ixekizumab-associated ARRs, no clear relationship between ARRs and factors such as age, sex, anthropometric parameters, disease subtype or prior treatment history has been established to date (2). In this study, we aimed to identify potential risk factors for ixekizumab-related ARRs following the excipient modification.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This retrospective study included 23 patients with plaque psoriasis, erythrodermic psoriasis or generalized pustular psoriasis, with or without psoriatic arthritis, who were prescribed the modified ixekizumab formulation between January 2023 and December 2025. Clinical data were extracted from the electronic medical records at our institution. ARRs were defined as acute local or systemic symptoms occurring within several hours of ixekizumab administration, as previously reported (3). Patients who experienced ARRs only after the first injection were classified as the ARR group. All adverse events were evaluated according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 6.0 grading scale.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using EZR (Saitama Medical Center, Jichi Medical University, Saitama, Japan) (4). Multivariable analysis was conducted using logistic regression (statistical significance, p<0.05).

RESULTS

Among the 23 patients, 7 (30.4%) developed ARRs, including 2 men and 5 women. The observed symptoms consisted of injection-site erythema (Fig. 1) in 6 patients and transient fever in 1 patient (all cases were grade 1). All ARRs appeared after the first administration and spontaneously improved within a few days without additional therapy. Similar ARRs recurred after subsequent injections in most cases; however, the severity gradually decreased and eventually resolved over time, consistent with the previous report (5). Among the 6 patients with injection-site erythema, 4 women discontinued ixekizumab because of intolerable reactions and switched to alternative biologic agents. In total, 6 of the 7 patients with ARRs had switched from secukinumab to ixekizumab. DISCUSSION

After the excipient formulation modification, the ixekizumab-associated incidence of injection-site reactions decreased from 23.8% to 9.2% (2). Although several factors may contribute to these reactions, including differences in dosing frequencies, the incidence remains relatively high compared with that for other humanized monoclonal antibody therapies (6). This study suggests that despite use of the modified ixekizumab formulation, the frequency of ARRs may increase depending on the patients’ prior treatment histories. Several possible explanations exist. Ixekizumab shares 98.2% sequence identity with human germline sequences (7), which may influence the production of anti-drug antibodies or pre-existing immunogenicity induced by prior treatments (8). Differences in formulation components or properties may also contribute to ARRs (9). Furthermore, changes in the self-administration device might increase the intradermal infusion volume, resulting in an increased risk of ARRs. A limitation of this study was the small sample size owing to its single-centre design. However, to the best of our knowledge, this study provides the first real-world evidence suggesting that prior exposure to specific biologics, rather than patient background, may influence the current ixekizumab formulation-associated incidence of ARRs. Further large-scale studies are needed to elucidate the underlying mechanisms and establish the optimal intraclass and interclass switching strategies for biological therapy of psoriasis.

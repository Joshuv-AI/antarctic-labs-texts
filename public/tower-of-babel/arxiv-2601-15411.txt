Asymptotic behaviour of coupled random dynamical systems with multiscale aspects
D. Russell Luke, Johannes-Carl Schnebel, Mathias Staudigl, Juan Peypouquet, Siqi Qu

Abstract
We examine a class of stochastic differential inclusions involving multiscale effects designed to solve a class of generalized variational inequalities. This class of problems contains constrained convex non-smooth optimization problems, constrained saddle-point problems and various equilibrium problems in economics and engineering. In order to respect constraints we adopt a penalty approach, introducing an explicit time-dependency into the evolution system. The resulting dynamics are described in terms of a non-autonomous stochastic evolution equation governed by maximally monotone operators in the drift and perturbed by a Brownian motion. We study the asymptotic behavior, as well as finite time convergence rates in terms of gap functions. The condition we use to prove convergence involves a Legendre transform of the function describing the set C C , a condition first used by 7 in the context of deterministic evolution equations. We also establish a large deviations principle showing that individual trajectories exhibit exponential concentration around the solution set. Finally we show how our continuous-time approach relates to penalty-regulated algorithms of forward-backward type after performing a suitable Euler-Maruyama discretisation.

Introduction

In this paper we are concerned with the study of stochastic splitting methods for solving generalized variational inequalities of the form

(MI)

\text{Find $\bar{x}\in C$ such that }0\in(\mathsf{A}+\NC_{C})(\bar{x}),

where the problem data satisfy the following standing assumptions:

Assumption 1
.

•

\mathsf{A}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to 2^{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
is a maximally monotone operator and
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a non-empty closed convex set.
\NC_{C}
is the outward normal cone mapping to
C
.

•

\dom(\mathsf{A})=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}|\mathsf{A}(x)\neq\varnothing\}
has a nonempty interior:
\Int(\dom\mathsf{A})\neq\varnothing
.

•

\mathsf{A}+\NC_{{C}}
is maximally monotone.

•

{S}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}|0\in\mathsf{A}(x)+\NC_{C}(x)\}\neq\varnothing
.

Maximal monotonicity means that (
MI
) is a monotone inclusion problem which requires finding
\bar{x}\in C
and
\bar{x}^{*}\in\mathsf{A}(\bar{x})
such that
\langle\bar{x}^{*},x-\bar{x}\rangle\geq 0
for all
x\in C
.

When
\mathsf{A}=\partial\Phi
for
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to(-\infty,\infty]
, a proper and lower semi-continuous convex function, then the problem (
MI
) reduces to the optimality conditions for the constrained convex minimization problem

(Opt)

\Phi_{\rm Opt}(C)=\min\{\Phi(x)|x\in C\}.

If
\mathsf{A}=F
, where
F:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a single-valued Lipschitz continuous and monotone mapping, the monotone inclusion problem (
MI
) reduces to the variational inequality

(VI)

\text{Find $\bar{x}\in C$ such that }\langle F(\bar{x}),x-\bar{x}\rangle\geq 0\qquad\forall x\in C.

This general equilibrium framework includes various game theoretic equilibrium concepts, as well as min-max optimization problems.

The question how to respect state constraints in a dynamical system is always an important and difficult question in scientific computing. While direct projection methods onto the feasible set
C
would be possible with noisy oracles (leading to the projected stochastic subgradient method
[
11
]
), computing a projection is very often a computationally challenging task. Penalty methods are a natural tool to steer iterative methods towards the satisfaction of the set constraint
C
. We thus assume that the set
C
admits a representation in terms of the level set of a continuously differentiable function
\Psi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying

Assumption 2
.

\Psi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is a convex and continuously differentiable function, whose gradient mapping
\nabla\Psi
is Lipschitz on bounded sets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Moreover,
C=\Psi^{-1}(0)=\argmin\Psi\neq\varnothing
.

Our approach in this paper is guided by the systematic study of dissipative dynamical systems which have been developed over the last decades in order to solve problem (
MI
). Indeed, the early references
[
7
,
4
]
studied the asymptotic behavior of the differential inclusion

(DI)

\dif\mathbf{x}(t)+\mathsf{A}(\mathbf{x}(t))\dif t+\beta(t)\nabla\Psi(\mathbf{x}(t))\dif t\ni 0

or, as a special case of the above with
\mathsf{A}=\partial\Phi
for a convex proper and lower semi-continuous function
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,\infty]
,

(MAG)

\dif\mathbf{x}(t)+\partial\Phi(\mathbf{x}(t))\dif t+\beta(t)\nabla\Psi(\mathbf{x}(t))\dif t\ni 0

Time dependency is included in these dynamical systems via an absolutely continuous function
t\mapsto\beta(t)
, modeled as a positive and non-decreasing penalty parameter. To understand its role, in the setting of (

) observe that
\partial\Phi+\beta(t)\nabla\Psi\subset\partial(\Phi+\beta(t)\Psi),
so that each trajectory of (

) satisfies

0\in\dif\mathbf{x}(t)+\partial(\Phi+\beta(t)\Psi)(\mathbf{x}(t)).

On the other hand,
\Phi+\beta(t)\Psi\uparrow\Phi+\indicator_{C}
as
t\to\infty
. Since monotone convergence is a variational convergence
[
3
]
, we have also
\partial(\Phi+\beta(t)\Psi)\to\partial(\Psi+\indicator_{C})
, so that the dynamical system in the previous display reduces to the subgradient flow

0\in\dif\mathbf{x}(t)+\partial(\Phi+\indicator_{C})(\mathbf{x}(t)).

It has been shown in the above works that - under certain conditions on the function
\beta(\cdot)
- solutions of these dynamical systems asymptotically approach equilibria to (
MI
), respectively (
Opt
).

In many cases the evaluation of the involved operators is subject to noise, either due to inexact evaluations, or because relevant problem data are only available in terms of a stochastic oracle. Motivated by data-driven approaches to control and optimization, we work in settings in which information about the variational inequality cannot be evaluated directly, but rather only subject to random exogenous noise. In such scenarios, it has frequently been argued in machine learning that these errors can be modeled in terms of a stochastic integral
[
27
,
2
]
. This is the starting point for our work. Following continuous-time approaches
[
32
,
33
,
40
,
42
,
30
]
, we assume that we can model the associated errors in terms of a stochastic integral leading to stochastic differential inclusions (SDI) governed by maximally monotone operators of the form

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{split}&\dif X(t)+\mathsf{A}(X(t))\dif t+\beta(t)\nabla\Psi(X(t))\dif t\ni\sigma(t,X(t))\dif W(t)\\
&X(0)=x\in\cl(\dom(\mathsf{A})),\end{split}\right.

As a special case of this dynamical approach, we derive results for the constrained convex minimization problem, defined by the maximally monotone operator
\mathsf{A}=\partial\Phi

\left\{\begin{split}&\dif X(t)+\partial\Phi(X(t))\dif t+\beta(t)\nabla\Psi(X(t))\dif t\ni\sigma(t,X(t))\dif W(t),\\
\lx@nobreakspace&X(0)=x\in\cl(\dom(\partial\Phi)).\end{split}\right.

The set-valued formulation aims for the design of a continuous stochastic process

In the setting of the SDI (

Specializing to the potential case

Elements of variational analysis

For a matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times k}
we define its Frobenius norm as
\tr(A^{\top}A)=\norm{A}^{2}_{\rm F}
. Let
\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H})
denote the set of all proper lower semi-continuous convex functions on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. The norm and inner product in
\mathcal{H}
are denoted by
\norm{\cdot}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, respectively. Given

and

, the subdifferential of

at

is the set
\partial f(x)=\{x^{*}\in\mathcal{H}|f(y)\geq f(x)+\langle x^{*},y-x\rangle\;\forall y\in\mathcal{H}\}
. The Fenchel conjugate of

is defined as

f^{*}(x^{*})=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{H}}\{\langle x^{*},x\rangle-f(x)\}
. Given a nonempty closed convex set

, its indicator function is defined as
\indicator_{C}(x)=0
if

and
+\infty
otherwise. The support function of

is
\support_{C}(x^{*})=\sup_{u\in C}\langle x^{*},u\rangle
. The normal cone to

is

\NC_{C}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\varnothing&\text{ if }x\notin C,\\
\{p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}|\langle y-x,p\rangle\leq 0\quad\forall y\in C\}&\text{else.}\end{array}\right.

It is clear that
\partial\indicator_{C}(x)=\NC_{C}(x)
.

A monotone operator is a set-valued mapping
\mathsf{M}:\mathcal{H}\to 2^{\mathcal{H}}
such that

\langle x^{*}-y^{*},x-y\rangle\geq 0\text{ whenever }(x,x^{*})\in\gr(\mathsf{M}),(y,y^{*})\in\gr(\mathsf{M}),

where
\gr(\mathsf{M})=\{(x,x^{*})|x^{*}\in\mathsf{M}\}
is the graph of
\mathsf{M}
. If
\mathsf{M}=\mathsf{A}+\NC_{C}
is maximal monotone and
\mathcal{S}=\mathsf{M}^{-1}(0)
, then

if and only if

(2.1)

(\forall(u,w)\in\gr(\mathsf{A}+\NC_{C})):\;\langle w,u-z\rangle\geq 0.

Elements of probability theory

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
a probability space and
\{\mathcal{F}_{t};t\geq 0\}
is a filtration. An
\mathcal{H}
-valued stochastic process starting at

is a function

. If

. If

We define the quotient space

as the space of equivalence classes of progressively measurable processes

. We set
\mathbf{S}_{d}^{0}[t_{0}]\triangleq\bigcap_{T\geq t_{0}}\mathbf{S}^{0}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}[t_{0},T]
. For

\mathbf{S}^{p}_{d}[t_{0},T]
the space of equivalence classes of progressively measurable processes

such that

\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{t\in[t_{0},T]}\norm{X(t)}^{p}\right)<\infty.

Our convergence results are derived within a specific geometric setting defined in terms of a summability condition on the Fenchel transform on the exterior penalty term

Assumption 3

(Attouch-Czarnecki condition,

]
)

For all

\int_{0}^{t}\beta(r)\left[\Psi^{\ast}\left(\frac{p}{\beta(r)}\right)-\support_{C}\left(\frac{p}{\beta(r)}\right)\right]\dif r<\infty\quad\forall 0\leq t\leq T.

While (

Definition 3.2

(Error bound)

A proper convex and lower semi-continuous function

\frac{\tau}{\rho}\dist(x,\argmin f)^{\rho}\leq f(x)-\min f\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Assuming that

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{0}^{t}\beta(s)\langle X(s)-z,\nabla\Psi(X(s))\rangle\dif s-\int_{0}^{t}\langle X(s)-z,\dif K(s)\rangle+M_{z}(t)+c(t)

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{0}^{t}\beta(s)\Psi(X(s))\dif s+\int_{0}^{t}\langle X(s)-z,p\rangle\dif s+c(t)+M_{z}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\beta(s)\left[\langle\frac{p}{\beta(s)},X(s)\rangle-\Psi(X(s))-\langle\frac{p}{\beta(s)},z\rangle\right]\dif s+c(t)+M_{z}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\beta(s)\left[\Psi^{\ast}\left(\frac{p}{\beta(s)}\right)-\support_{C}\left(\frac{p}{\beta(s)}\right)\right]\dif s+c(t)+M_{z}(t).

Next, we define

\displaystyle h_{C}(x^{*})\triangleq\Psi^{\ast}(x^{*})-\support_{C}(x^{*})\quad\forall x^{*}\in\dom(\Psi^{\ast}),\text{ and }H(t)\triangleq\int_{0}^{t}\beta(s)h_{C}(p/\beta(s))\dif s.

Hence, we can continue the previous estimate as

\norm{X(t)-z}^{2}\leq\norm{X(0)-z}^{2}+2H(t)+2M_{z}(t)+2c(t)\qquad t\in[0,T].

The process

is a martingale and by Assumption

, the process
t\mapsto H(t)
is almost surely bounded. Additionally,
X\in\mathbf{S}^{p}_{d}[0,T]
for

, so by using

\norm{X(t)-z}^{p}\leq 4^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\left[\norm{X(0)-z}^{p}+2^{p/2}H(t)^{p/2}+2^{\frac{p}{2}}c(t)^{p/2}+2^{\frac{p}{2}}\absolutevalue{M_{z}(t)}^{p/2}\right].

Since

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\absolutevalue{M_{z}(t)}^{\frac{p}{2}}\right]

Using next the boundedness of the volatility function and Young’s inequality
ab\leq\frac{a^{2}}{2K}+\frac{Kb^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle 4^{\frac{p-2}{2}}2^{\frac{p}{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\absolutevalue{M_{z}(t)}^{\frac{p}{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 4^{\frac{p-2}{2}}2^{\frac{p}{2}}C_{p/2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T}\norm{\sigma(t,x)}^{2}_{\rm F}\cdot\norm{X(t)-z}^{2}\dif t\right)^{\frac{p}{4}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 4^{\frac{p-2}{2}}2^{\frac{p}{2}}C_{1}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in 0,T]}\norm{X(t)-z}^{\frac{p}{2}}\cdot\left(\int_{0}^{T}\norm{\sigma(t,x)}^{2}_{\rm F}\dif t\right)^{\frac{p}{4}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 4^{\frac{p-2}{2}}2^{\frac{p}{2}}C_{1}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{2\gamma}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\norm{X(t)-z}^{p}+\frac{\gamma}{2}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\norm{\sigma(t,x)}_{\rm F}^{2}\dif t\right)^{p/2}\right].

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\norm{X(t)-z}^{p}\right]\leq 2^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\norm{X(0)-z}^{p}\right]+2^{\frac{3p}{2}-1}H(T)^{p/2}+C_{3}\mathbb{E}[c(T)^{p/2}],

Theorem 3.5

\displaystyle\leq E_{z}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\beta(r)\Psi(X(r))\dif r+\int_{0}^{t}[\Phi(X(r))-\Phi(z)]\dif r.

The Fenchel-Young inequality states that
\Psi^{\ast}\left(\frac{p}{\beta(t)}\right)\geq\langle\frac{p}{\beta(t)},X(t)\rangle-\Psi(X(t)).
Furthermore, since
p\in\NC_{C}(z)
, we have
\langle\frac{p}{\beta(t)},z\rangle=\support_{C}\left(\frac{p}{\beta(t)}\right)
. When combined with the previous display, we obtain

\displaystyle E_{z}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\beta(r)\Psi(X(r))\dif r

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\langle-p,X(r)-z\rangle\dif r

\displaystyle\geq E_{z}(t)-\int_{0}^{t}\beta(r)\left(\Psi^{\ast}\left(\frac{p}{\beta(r)}\right)-\support_{C}\left(\frac{p}{\beta(r)}\right)\right)\dif r.

Taking expectations on both sides, we deduce from the monotonicity of the expectation

(4.5)

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left[E_{z}(t)-\int_{0}^{t}\beta(r)h_{C}(p/\beta(r))\dif r\right]&\leq\mathbb{E}\left[E_{z}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\beta(r)\Psi(X(r))\dif r+\int_{0}^{t}\langle-p,X(r)-z\rangle\dif r\right]\\
&\leq\mathbb{E}\left[E_{z}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\beta(r)\Psi(X(r))\dif r+\int_{0}^{t}[\Phi(X(r))-\Phi(z)]\dif r\right]\\
&\leq\Gamma(t).\end{split}

From Proposition
3.3
(ii) we know that the existence of limits
\lim_{t\to\infty}E_{z}(t)
, which together with Assumption
3
, guarantees the lower bound is finite. Hence, (i) follows immediately. Moreover, we deduce from this sandwiched relation that

t\mapsto\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\beta(r)\Psi(X(r))\dif r+\int_{0}^{t}\langle-p,X(r)-z\rangle\dif r\right]
converges. In combination with Proposition
3.3
(iii), we conclude that
\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\langle-p,X(r)-z\rangle\dif r\right]
exists in
\mathbb{R}
, which demonstrates (ii).

\blacksquare

4.1
Concentration bounds

This section is devoted to uncertainty quantification of the stochastic differential inclusion. Using basic Cramér-bound techniques on Brownian motions
[
25
]
, we derive a probabilistic concentration bound on the objective function gap generated by the trajectories of (
1.2
). Our proof strategy follows closely the large deviation analysis of
[
33
]
.

To simplify the already heavy notation in this subsection, we shift the time domain so that
0=0.
We know that

\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}(\Phi(X(s))-\Phi(z))\dif s\leq\frac{E_{z}(0)}{t}+\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\langle X(s)-z,\sigma(t,X(t))\dif W(s)\rangle+\frac{1}{2t}\int_{0}^{t}\Sigma^{2}(s)\dif s.

Applying Jensen’s inequality to the left-hand side yields

\Phi(\bar{X}(t))-\min_{\mathcal{C}}\Phi(z)\triangleq\overline{\Delta\Phi}(t)\leq\frac{E_{z}(0)}{t}+\frac{1}{t}M_{z}(t)+\frac{1}{2t}\int_{0}^{t}\Sigma^{2}(s)\dif s.

Using the auxiliary process
V
from Theorem
3.5
, we can majorize the martingale term
M_{z}
into the parts
M_{z}(t)=U(t)+E_{z}(0)+c(t)
. Hence,

\overline{\Delta\Phi}(t)\leq\frac{2E_{z}(0)+U(t)+c(t)}{t}.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, we have

\mathbb{P}\left(\overline{\Delta\Phi}(t)\geq\varepsilon\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(U(t)\geq t\varepsilon-S(t)\right)

where
S(t)\triangleq c(t)+2E_{z}(0)
. This simple estimate is the main observation underlying the following Cramé-type concentration bound.

Theorem 4.7
.

Fix
z\in\dom(\Phi)
. For all
\varepsilon>0
and
t>0
, we have

(4.8)

\mathbb{P}\left(\overline{\Delta\Phi}(t)\geq Q_{0}(t)+\varepsilon Q_{1}(t)\right)\leq\exp(-\eps^{2}/4),

where

(4.9)

\displaystyle Q_{0}(t)

\displaystyle\triangleq\frac{1}{t}(c(t)+2E_{z}(0)),

(4.10)

\displaystyle Q_{1}(t)

\displaystyle\triangleq\frac{1}{t}\sqrt{\delta(t)},\text{ where }\delta(t)\triangleq\in 0^{t}\Sigma(s)^{2}\cdot\norm{V(s)-X(s)}^{2}_{2}\dif s.

Proof.

Let be
\rho(t)\triangleq[U,U]_{t}
the quadratic variation process of the continuous semimartingale
\{(U(t),\mathcal{F}_{t});t\geq 0\}
. We have

\displaystyle\rho(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\norm{\sigma(X(s),s)^{\top}(V(s)-X(s))}^{2}_{2}\dif s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\norm{\sigma(X(s),s)}^{2}_{F}\cdot\norm{V(s)-X(s)}^{2}_{2}\dif s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\Sigma^{2}(s)R(s)\dif s\triangleq\delta(t),

where
R(t)\triangleq\norm{V(t)-X(t)}_{2}
. We next compute bound stochastic exponential
\exp(\theta U)
via the Cauchy-Schwarz-Bunyakowski inequality as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\exp(\theta U(t))\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\exp(\theta U(t)-b\rho(t))\exp(b\rho(t))\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[\exp(2\theta U(t)-2b\rho(t))\right]}\cdot\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[\exp(2b\rho(t))\right]}.

Setting
b=\theta^{2}
, the expression inside the first expected values is just the stochastic exponential of the process
t\mapsto 2\theta U(t)
. Hence,
t\mapsto\exp(2\theta U(t))
is a submartingale, and we conclude

(4.11)

\mathbb{E}\left[\exp(\theta U(t))\right]\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[\exp(2\theta^{2}\rho(t))\right]}\leq\exp(\theta^{2}\delta(t)).

It follows,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[U(t)\geq a]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left[\exp(\theta U(t))\geq\exp(\theta a)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\exp(-\theta a)\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\exp(\theta U(t))\right]

\displaystyle\leq\exp(-\theta a)\exp(\theta^{2}\delta(t))

\displaystyle=\exp(-\theta a+\theta^{2}\delta(t)),

where the second inequality is just Markov’s inequality, and the third line uses (
4.11
). Minimizing the expression on the right-hand side with respect to
\theta
, yields
\theta=\frac{a}{2\delta(t)}
, resulting in the upper bound

\mathbb{P}[U(t)\geq a]\leq\exp\left(-\frac{a^{2}}{4\delta(t)}\right).

From these computations, we deduce that, whenever
a\geq\varepsilon\sqrt{\delta(t)}
, we obtain
\mathbb{P}(U(t)\geq a)\leq\exp(-\eps^{2}/4)
. This motivates us to put

Q_{0}(t)\triangleq\frac{1}{t}S(t),\text{ and }Q_{1}(t)\triangleq\frac{1}{t}\sqrt{\delta(t)},

so that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\overline{\Delta\Phi}(t)\geq Q_{0}(t)+\varepsilon Q_{1}(t)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(U(t)\geq tQ_{0}(t)+t\varepsilon Q_{1}(t)-S(t))

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(U(t)\geq\varepsilon\sqrt{\delta(t)})

\displaystyle\leq\exp(-\eps^{2}/4),

which is precisely (
4.8
).

\blacksquare

5
Discrete-time analysis and numerical implementation

In this section we derive from the continuos-time dynamical system (
1.1
) a discrete-time numerical algorithm for solving (
MI
), and derive finite-time complexity statements. Let
T>0
be fixed. Let
\pi_{\delta}=\{0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{N_{\delta}}=T\}
be a partition of
[0,T]
with mesh size
\Delta t_{k}^{\delta}\triangleq t_{k+1}-t_{k}
and
\delta\triangleq\max\{\Delta t_{k}^{d}:0\leq k\leq N_{\delta}-1\}.
We consider the sequence
(X^{\delta}_{n})_{n=0}^{N_{\delta}}
recursively defined by

(5.1)

\left\{\begin{split}&X^{\delta}_{n+1}=(\Id+\Delta t^{\delta}_{n}\mathsf{A})^{-1}(X^{\delta}_{n}-\Delta t^{\delta}_{n}\beta(t_{n})(\nabla\Psi(X^{\delta}_{n})+\sigma(t_{n}^{\delta},X^{\delta}_{n})(W_{t_{n+1}}-W_{t_{n}})),\\
&X_{0}^{\delta}=\xi\end{split}\right.

The resulting scheme is a noisy version of the splitting method investigated in
[
5
]
. It arises also naturally in the discretization framework of
[
12
]
. More precisely, the following consistency results is a central result in
[
12
, Theorem 7.1]
:

Theorem 5.1
.

Consider the discrete-time Markov chain
(X^{\delta}_{n})_{n=0}^{N_{\delta}}
constructed recursively by (
5.1
). For
n\in\{0,1,\ldots,N_{\delta}-1\}
and
t\in[t_{n},t_{n+1})
, consider the piecewise linear interpolation

(5.2)

X^{\delta}(t)=X^{\delta}_{n}+\frac{t-t_{n}}{\Delta t_{n}^{\delta}}(X^{\delta}_{n+1}-X^{\delta}_{n}).

Then
X^{\delta}
is a stochastic process with continuous sample paths. Let
X\in\mathbf{S}^{p}_{d}[0,T]
denote the unique solution to (
1.1
) with initial condition
X(0)=\xi
. Then,

(5.3)

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\norm{X^{\delta}(t)-X(t)}^{2}\right)=0.

5.1
Finite time complexity analysis of the discrete-time scheme

Our aim in this section is to derive complexity results for the discrete-time scheme (
5.1
) for fixed
T>0
and vanishing step size
\lambda_{n}=\Delta t_{n}^{\delta}
. This means that the time discretization vanishes, a setting that is formally not covered by the approximation result reported in Theorem
5.1
. Additionally, we formally set
N\equiv N_{\delta},X_{n}\equiv X^{\delta}_{n},\beta_{n}\equiv\beta(t_{n})
, and

\sigma_{n}(X_{n})\triangleq\sigma(t_{n},X_{n}),\;\Delta W_{n+1}\triangleq W_{t_{n+1}}-W_{t_{n}}\text{ for }n=0,\ldots,N-1.

Hence,
\Delta W_{n+1}
is normally distributed random variable with mean zero and covariance matrix
\lambda_{n}\Id
for all
n=0,1,\ldots,N-1
. Resulting from this discretization, we define
\Delta B_{n+1}\triangleq\sigma_{n}(X_{n})\Delta W_{n+1}
, and the discrete-time martingale transform
(B_{n})_{n=0}^{N}
as the process

B_{0}\triangleq 0,\quad B_{n+1}\triangleq B_{0}+\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sigma_{i}(X_{i})\Delta W_{i+1}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\Delta B_{i+1}.

Adopting this notation, we can realize the Markov chain
(X_{n})_{n=0}^{N}
as the stochastic recursion

(5.4)

\left\{\begin{split}&V_{n+1}=\beta_{n}\nabla\Psi(X_{n})+\Delta B_{n+1},\\
&X_{n+1}=(\Id+\lambda_{n}\mathsf{A})^{-1}(X_{n}-\lambda_{n}V_{n+1}).\end{split}\right.

Remark 5.1
.

Our analysis of the scheme (
5.4
) extends
[
5
]
to the stochastic setting, assuming the availability of an unbiased stochastic oracle with decreasing variance. This can be achieved via variance reduction techniques.

Remark 5.2
.

Our scheme can also be used as a numerical method for solving the hierarchical equilibrium problem (
MI
) for
C=\argmin_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi(x)
, under the assumption that we only have access to the lower level optimization problem in terms of a stochastic first-order oracle.

Lemma 5.2
.

Take
z\in\dom(\mathsf{A}+\NC_{C})
and
v\in\mathsf{A}(z)
. Then, for all
\eta\geq 0
and
n\geq 1
, we have

(5.5)

\begin{split}\norm{X_{n}-z}^{2}-&\norm{X_{n+1}-z}^{2}-\frac{\eta}{2(1+\eta)}\norm{X_{n+1}-X_{n}}^{2}\geq\frac{2\eta\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}}{1+\eta}\Psi(X_{n})\\
&-\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}\left((1+\eta)\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}-\frac{2}{(1+\eta)L_{\Psi}}\right)\norm{\nabla\Psi(X_{n})}^{2}+2\lambda_{n}\langle\Delta B_{n+1}+v,X_{n}-z\rangle\\
&-\frac{4\lambda^{2}_{n}(1+\eta)}{\eta}\norm{v}^{2}-\frac{4\lambda^{2}_{n}(1+\eta)}{\eta}\norm{\Delta B_{n+1}}^{2}.\end{split}

Proof.

Let
(z,z^{*})\in\gr(\mathsf{A}+\NC_{C})
be arbitrary, so that
z^{*}=v+p
with
v\in\mathsf{A}(z)
and
p\in\NC_{C}(z)
.

In every iteration
n
, we thus have

\frac{X_{n}-X_{n+1}}{\lambda_{n}}-V_{n+1}\in\mathsf{A}(X_{n+1}),\text{ and }z^{*}-p=v\in\mathsf{A}(z).

Monotonicity of
\mathsf{A}
gives

\displaystyle 2\langle X_{n}-X_{n+1},X_{n+1}-z\rangle

\displaystyle\geq 2\lambda_{n}\langle V_{n+1},X_{n+1}-z\rangle-2\lambda_{n}\langle p-z^{*},X_{n+1}-z\rangle

\displaystyle=2\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}\langle\nabla\Psi(X_{n}),X_{n+1}-z\rangle+2\lambda_{n}\langle\Delta B_{n+1},X_{n+1}-z\rangle-2\lambda_{n}\langle p-z^{*},X_{n+1}-z\rangle.

Using the 3-point identity

2\langle X_{n}-X_{n+1},z-X_{n+1}\rangle=\norm{X_{n+1}-X_{n}}^{2}-\norm{X_{n}-z}^{2}+\norm{z-X_{n+1}}^{2},

we arrive at

\displaystyle\norm{X_{n}-z}^{2}-

\displaystyle\norm{X_{n+1}-z}^{2}-\norm{X_{n+1}-X_{n}}^{2}\geq 2\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}\langle\nabla\Psi(X_{n}),X_{n}-z\rangle+2\lambda_{n}\langle\Delta B_{n+1},X_{n}-z\rangle

\displaystyle+2\lambda_{n}\langle v,X_{n}-z\rangle+2\lambda_{n}\langle\beta_{n}\Psi(X_{n})+v+\Delta B_{n+1},X_{n+1}-X_{n}\rangle.

Since
\Psi\in{\mathbf{C}}^{1,1}_{L_{\Psi}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and convex, we have

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\Psi(X_{n}),X_{n}-z\rangle\geq\frac{1}{L_{\Psi}}\norm{\nabla\Psi(X_{n})}^{2},\text{ and }

\displaystyle\langle\nabla\Psi(X_{n}),X_{n}-z\rangle\geq\Psi(X_{n}).

Taking a convex combination of these two estimates yields for every
\eta>0
and
n\geq 0
,

(5.6)

\langle\nabla\Psi(X_{n}),X_{n}-z\rangle\geq\frac{\eta}{1+\eta}\Psi(X_{n})+\frac{1}{(1+\eta)L_{\Psi}}\norm{\nabla\Psi(X_{n})}^{2}.

Second, we make use of the identity

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+\eta}\norm{X_{n+1}-X_{n}+(1+\eta)\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}\nabla\Psi(X_{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+\eta}\norm{X_{n+1}-X_{n}}^{2}+(1+\eta)\lambda^{2}_{n}\beta^{2}_{n}\norm{\nabla\Psi(X_{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle+2\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}\langle\nabla\Psi(X_{n}),X_{n+1}-X_{n}\rangle,

in order to bound

(5.7)

2\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}\langle\nabla\Psi(X_{n}),X_{n+1}-X_{n}\rangle\geq-\frac{1}{1+\eta}\norm{X_{n+1}-X_{n}}^{2}-(1+\eta)\lambda^{2}_{n}\beta^{2}_{n}\norm{\nabla\Psi(X_{n})}^{2}.

Combining (
5.6
) and (
5.7
), together with Young’s inequality, we arrive at the estimate

\displaystyle\norm{X_{n}-z}^{2}-

\displaystyle\norm{X_{n+1}-z}^{2}-\norm{X_{n+1}-X_{n}}^{2}\geq\frac{2\eta\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}}{1+\eta}\Psi(X_{n})+\left(\frac{2\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}}{(1+\eta)L_{\Psi}}-(1-\eta)\lambda_{n}^{2}\beta_{n}^{2}\right)\norm{\nabla\Psi(X_{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda_{n}}{c}\norm{\Delta B_{n+1}+v}^{2}-\left(\frac{1}{1+\eta}+\lambda_{n}c\right)\norm{X_{n+1}-X_{n}}^{2}+2\lambda_{n}\langle v,X_{n}-z\rangle+2\lambda_{n}\langle\Delta B_{n+1},X_{n}-z\rangle,

for every
c>0
. Rearranging and using a square bound, this leads to

\displaystyle\norm{X_{n}-z}^{2}-

\displaystyle\norm{X_{n+1}-z}^{2}-\left(1-\frac{1}{1+\eta}+\lambda_{n}c\right)\norm{X_{n+1}-X_{n}}^{2}\geq\frac{2\eta\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}}{1+\eta}\Psi(X_{n})

\displaystyle-\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}\left((1+\eta)\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}-\frac{2}{(1+\eta)L_{\Psi}}\right)\norm{\nabla\Psi(X_{n})}^{2}+2\lambda_{n}\langle v,X_{n}-z\rangle+2\lambda_{n}\langle\Delta W_{n+1},X_{n}-z\rangle

\displaystyle-\frac{2\lambda_{n}}{c}\norm{v}^{2}-\frac{2\lambda_{n}}{c}\norm{\Delta B_{n+1}}^{2}.

Choosing
c=\frac{\eta}{2\lambda_{n}(1+\eta)}
, we finally arrive at the desired inequality

\displaystyle\norm{X_{n}-z}^{2}-

\displaystyle\norm{X_{n+1}-z}^{2}-\frac{\eta}{2(1+\eta)}\norm{X_{n+1}-X_{n}}^{2}\geq\frac{2\eta\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}}{1+\eta}\Psi(X_{n})

\displaystyle-\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}\left((1+\eta)\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}-\frac{2}{(1+\eta)L_{\Psi}}\right)\norm{\nabla\Psi(X_{n})}^{2}+2\lambda_{n}\langle v,X_{n}-z\rangle+2\lambda_{n}\langle\Delta B_{n+1},X_{n}-z\rangle

\displaystyle-\frac{4\lambda^{2}_{n}(1+\eta)}{\eta}\norm{v}^{2}-\frac{4\lambda^{2}_{n}(1+\eta)}{\eta}\norm{\Delta B_{n+1}}^{2}.

\blacksquare

Lemma 5.3
.

Assume that
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}<\frac{2}{L_{\Psi}}
. Then there exist
c_{1},c_{2}>0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
deterministic, such that for all
n\geq N
and any
z\in\dom(\mathsf{A}+\NC_{C})
and any
v\in\mathsf{A}(z)
, we have

\displaystyle\norm{X_{n+1}-z}^{2}

\displaystyle-\norm{X_{n}-z}^{2}+c_{1}\left(\frac{1}{2}\norm{X_{n+1}-X_{n}}^{2}+2\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}\Psi(X_{n})+\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}\norm{\nabla\Psi(X_{n})}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\lambda_{n}\langle\Delta B_{n+1}+v,z-X_{n}\rangle+c_{2}\lambda^{2}_{n}\left(\norm{v}^{2}+\norm{\nabla\Psi(X_{n})}^{2}\right).

Proof.

Continuing from the estimate reported in Lemma
5.2
, we add the term
\frac{\eta}{1+\eta}\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}\norm{\nabla\Psi(X_{n})}^{2}
to both sides, to obtain

\displaystyle\norm{X_{n+1}-z}^{2}-

\displaystyle\norm{X_{n}-z}^{2}+\frac{\eta}{2(1+\eta)}\norm{X_{n+1}-X_{n}}^{2}+\frac{2\eta\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}}{1+\eta}\Psi(X_{n})+\frac{\eta}{1+\eta}\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}\norm{\nabla\Psi(X_{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}\left((1+\eta)\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}-\frac{2}{(1+\eta)L_{\Psi}}+\frac{\eta}{1+\eta}\right)\norm{\nabla\Psi(X_{n})}^{2}+2\lambda_{n}\langle\Delta B_{n+1}+v,z-X_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle+\frac{4\lambda^{2}_{n}(1+\eta)}{\eta}\left(\norm{v}^{2}+\norm{\Delta B_{n+1}}^{2}\right).

As we assume
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}<\frac{2}{L_{\Psi}}
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
2\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}<1/L_{\Psi}
for all
n\geq N
. Moreover,

\lim_{\eta\to 0}\left((1+\eta)\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}-\frac{2}{(1+\eta)L_{\Psi}}+\frac{\eta}{1+\eta}\right)=\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}-\frac{2}{L_{\Psi}}<0,

we can choose
\eta_{0}>0
sufficiently small, and set

c_{1}=\frac{\eta_{0}}{1+\eta_{0}},\quad c_{2}=\frac{4(1+\eta_{0})}{\eta_{0}},

so that for all
n\geq N
,

\displaystyle\norm{X_{n+1}-z}^{2}-

\displaystyle\norm{X_{n}-z}^{2}+c_{1}\left(\frac{1}{2}\norm{X_{n+1}-X_{n}}^{2}+2\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}\Psi(X_{n})+\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}\norm{\nabla\Psi(X_{n})}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\lambda_{n}\langle\Delta B_{n+1}+v,z-X_{n}\rangle+c_{2}\lambda_{n}^{2}\left(\norm{v}^{2}+\norm{\Delta B_{n+1}}^{2}\right),

as claimed.

\blacksquare

We are now ready to present the main convergence proof of the discrete-time scheme. We impose assumptions which are the natural discrete-time formulations of the continuous-time scheme.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be the non-autonomous Markov chain generated by (
5.4
). Assume that
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}<\frac{2}{L_{\Psi}}
, and the following conditions holds true:

(5.8)

\displaystyle(\forall p\in\range(\NC_{C})):\;\sum_{n\geq 1}\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}h_{C}(p/\beta_{n})<\infty,

(5.9)

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\norm{\sigma_{n}(x)}^{2}_{F}\leq s^{2}_{n},\quad\text{with }\sum_{n\geq 1}\lambda_{n}^{2}s_{n}^{2}<\infty.

Then,

•

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[\norm{X_{n}-z}^{2}]
exists in
\mathbb{R}

•

\mathbb{E}[\norm{X_{n+1}-X_{n}}^{2}],\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}\Psi(X_{n})]
, and
\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}\norm{\nabla\Psi(X_{n})}^{2}]
all converge to 0.

•

If
\inf_{n\geq 1}\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}>0
, then
\Psi(X_{n})\to 0
and
\norm{\nabla\Psi(X_{n})}\to 0
a.s.

Proof.

Define

(5.10)

\mathcal{E}_{n}(z)\triangleq\norm{X_{n+1}-z}^{2}-\norm{X_{n}-z}^{2}+c_{1}\left(\frac{1}{2}\norm{X_{n+1}-X_{n}}^{2}+\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}\Psi(X_{n})+\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}\norm{\nabla\Psi(X_{n})}^{2}\right),

where
c_{1}
is given by Lemma
5.3
. Consider
u=v+p\in\mathsf{A}(z)+\NC_{{C}}(z)
, so that

\displaystyle 2\lambda_{n}\langle v,z-X_{n}\rangle-c_{1}\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}\Psi(X_{n})

\displaystyle=2\lambda_{n}\langle u,z-X_{n}\rangle-c_{1}\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}\Psi(X_{n})-2\lambda_{n}\langle p,z-X_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=2\lambda_{n}\langle u,z-X_{n}\rangle+c_{1}\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}\left(\langle\frac{2p}{c_{1}\beta_{n}},X_{n}\rangle-\support_{C}\left(\frac{2p}{c_{1}\beta_{n}}\right)-\Psi(X_{n})\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\lambda_{n}\langle u,z-X_{n}\rangle+c_{1}\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}\left(\Psi^{\ast}\left(\frac{2p}{c_{1}\beta_{n}}\right)-\support_{C}\left(\frac{2p}{c_{1}\beta_{n}}\right)\right).

Then,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{n}(z)+c_{1}\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}\Psi(X_{n})\leq 2\lambda_{n}\langle\Delta B_{n+1},z-X_{n}\rangle+2\lambda_{n}\langle v,z-X_{n}\rangle+c_{2}\lambda_{n}^{2}\left(\norm{v}^{2}+\norm{\Delta B_{n+1}}^{2}\right),

which allows us to conclude that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{n}(z)

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}\lambda_{n}^{2}\left(\norm{v}^{2}+\norm{\Delta B_{n+1}}^{2}\right)+2\lambda_{n}\langle\Delta B_{n+1},z-X_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle+2\lambda_{n}\langle u,z-X_{n}\rangle+c_{1}\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}\left[\Psi^{\ast}\left(\frac{2p}{c_{1}\beta_{n}}\right)-\support_{C}\left(\frac{2p}{c_{1}\beta_{n}}\right)\right].

Next, take
u=0
, and take expectations on both sides, we are left with

\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{E}_{n}(z)]\leq c_{2}\lambda^{2}_{n}\left(\norm{v}^{2}+\lambda_{n}^{2}s^{2}_{n}\right)+c_{1}\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}h_{C}\left(\frac{2p}{c_{1}\beta_{n}}\right).

Upon defining the numerical sequences

(5.11)

\displaystyle\mathtt{a}_{n}\triangleq\mathbb{E}[\norm{X_{n}-z}^{2}],

(5.12)

\displaystyle\mathtt{b}_{n}\triangleq c_{1}\mathbb{E}[\frac{1}{2}\norm{X_{n+1}-X_{n}}^{2}+\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}\Psi(X_{n})+\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}\norm{\nabla\Psi(X_{n})}^{2}],

(5.13)

\displaystyle\mathtt{e}_{n}\triangleq c_{2}\lambda^{2}_{n}\left(\norm{v}^{2}+\lambda_{n}^{2}s^{2}_{n}\right)+c_{1}\lambda_{n}\beta_{n}h_{C}\left(\frac{2p}{c_{1}\beta_{n}}\right),

we arrive at the recursion

\mathtt{a}_{n+1}-\mathtt{a}_{n}+\mathtt{b}_{n}\leq\mathtt{e}_{n}

with
\mathtt{a}_{n}\geq 0
and
\mathtt{e}_{n}\in\ell^{1}_{+}(\mathbb{N})
. It follows
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathtt{a}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
and
(\mathtt{b}_{n})_{n}\in\ell^{1}_{+}(\mathbb{N})
.

\blacksquare

6.1
Basis pursuit

The basis pursuit denoising problem is to filter out an unknown signal
x^{\rm true}
from noisy observation
y
generated by the linear model
y=Ax+\varepsilon

\vdots\\

m

d

\min\norm{x}_{1}\quad\text{s.t.: }x\in\argmin_{x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{2}\norm{Ax'-y}^{2}_{2}

This problem falls within the framework of this paper, by identifying
\varphi(x)=\norm{x}_{1}
and
\Psi(x)=\frac{1}{2}\norm{Ax-y}^{2}_{2}.
We compute
\nabla\Psi(x)=A^{\top}(Ax-y)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}A_{j}(Ax-y)_{j}
. In many cases, we only have access to subsamples of the gradient of the penalty function. We assume that subsamples result in i.i.d subsets
\mathcal{B}\subset[m]
, so that at every iteration of the discrete-time scheme, we observe the sketched gradient

\sum_{j\in\mathcal{B}_{k}}a_{j}(AX_{n}-y)_{j}+\sigma_{n}\xi_{n+1}\quad\xi_{n+1}\sim N_{m}(0,\mathbf{I}_{d}).

In the first experiment, we have random generated a matrix with
m=40
and
n=100
. The stochastic gradient estimator of the penalty function is constructed by drawing iid batches of size 4 uniformly at random. The evolution of the signal reconstruction process is depicted in Figure

Linear inverse problem with simulated data

We consider the following hierarchical minimisation problem

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(x)\triangleq\norm{x-\hat{x}}_{1}\quad\text{s.t.: }x\in\argmin_{x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi(x^{\prime})

where

One major class of Inverse Problems stem from the reconstruction of images from measurements of X-rays passing through a medium used in Computed tomography (CT). The measurements on a detector array orthogonal to the ray-beam of the X-rays passing through a 2-D gray-scale image
x=x(z_{1},z_{2}):\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
can be modeled via the forward Radon Tansform
R_{t,\theta}(x)
where
t\in R
is the position on the detector array and
\theta\in[0,\pi)
is the angle between the ray beam and the
z_{1}
-axis. The image
x
has compact support. Each measurement at
(t,\theta
is obtained by integrating the image along a line
\gamma_{t,\theta}(s),\ s\in R
with angle
\theta
to the
x_{1}
axis and distance
t
to the origin:

(6.3)

\displaystyle R_{t,\theta}(x)=\int_{\gamma_{t,\theta}}xds=\int{R}x(t\cos(\theta)-s\sin(\theta),t\sin(\theta)+s\cos(\theta))ds

To simulate now measurements of a discrete image of size
d
under the Radon-transform at a discrete set of angles
(\theta_{1},...,\theta_{M})

which is sparse. Given a vector of measurements

(6.4)

\displaystyle\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\ \norm{Ax - g}^{2}=\min_{x\in R^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{KM}(A_{i}x-y_{i})^{2},

7
Conclusion and perspectives

The paper studies a dynamical system approach to non-smooth convex constrained optimization adopting an exterior penalty formulation. Under the familiar Attouch-Czarnecki condition, we prove asymptotic convergence of the sample paths and the Cesaro mean for maximal monotone inclusions. In the special case where the monotone operator is the subgradient of a proper lower semi-continuous convex function, we derive concrete energy bounds on the objective function values. Asymptotic feasibility of the trajectory is also demonstrated. There are several interesting directions left for future research.

Invariant measures

Consider the case
m=d
and
\sigma(t,x)=\Id
for all
(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Additionally, we assume that
\beta(t)=\bar{\beta}
(constant penalization). Let

MI

(7.1)

\left\{\begin{split}&\dif X(t)+\mathsf{A}(X(t))\dif t+b(X(t))\dif t\ni\dif W(t)\\
&X(0)=x\in\dom(\mathsf{A})\end{split}\right.

This is the setting of
[
8
,
9
]
, where the special case with
\mathsf{A}=\NC_{K}
for a closed convex set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
C^{2}
boundary
\bd(K)
and nonempty interior
\Int(K)
is considered. For this data constellation, the random dynamical system (
7.1
) admits a unique strong solution
X^{x}(t)
with transition semi-group
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, satisfying

(P_{t}f)(x)=\mathbb{E}[f(X^{x}(t))]\qquad\forall f\in{\mathbf{C}}(K),x\in\mathbb{K},t\geq 0.

[
9
]
characterize the infinitesimal generator of the process as

\displaystyle(\mathcal{G}f)(x)=\frac{1}{2}\Delta f(x)-\langle b(x),\nabla f(x)\rangle\quad f\in\dom(\mathcal{G}),x\in K,

\displaystyle\dom(\mathcal{G})=\{f\in{\mathbf{C}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})|\frac{\partial f}{\partial n}=0\text{ on }\bd(K)\},

with
\frac{\partial}{\partial n}
the outward normal derivative. It is also shown that the semi-group

defines an irreducible strong Feller process with a unique invariant measure

[

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f^{2}(x)\log(f^{2}(x))\dif\nu(x)\leq\frac{1}{\mu}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\norm{\nabla f(x)}^{2}\dif\nu(x)+\norm{f}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\nu)}^{2}\log(\norm{f}^{2}_{L^{2}(\R^{d},\nu)}),

for any continuously differentiable function

with bounded domain. Translating these results to our penalty framework, the drift

is strongly monotone whenever
\Psi

implicitly depends on the penalty parameter
\bar{\beta}

bar{\beta}

analyzed in the main part of this paper. It is an interesting open question to investigate in detail the limiting behavior of the family

Bounded Variation Functions

We denote by
{\mathbf{C}}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{d})
the space of continuous functions
f:[a,b]\subset\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
. This is a separable Banach space with norm
\norm{f}_{{\mathbf{C}}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{d})}\triangleq\sup_{a\leq t\leq b}\norm{f(t)}
.

Let
[a,b]
be a closed interval from
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathcal{D}_{[a,b]}
be the set of all partitions
\Delta=\{t_{0}=a,t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}=b\},n\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}
. Define the width
\absolutevalue{\Delta}=\sup_{0\leq i\leq n-1}\absolutevalue{t_{i+1}-t_{i}}
. Given a function
f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define the variation of
k
over
[a,b]
as

V_{\Delta}(f)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\norm{f(t_{i+1})-f(t_{i})}.

The total variation of
k
on
[a,b]
is defined by

\nu_{a}^{b}(f)\triangleq\sup_{\Delta\in\mathcal{D}_{[a,b]}}V_{\Delta}(f)

If
a=0
und
b=T
, we write
\nu_{T}(f)=\nu_{0}^{T}(f)
. A function
f:[a.b]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
has bounded variation on
[a,b]
if
\nu_{a}^{b}(f)<\infty
. The space of bounded variation function on
[a,b]
is denoted by
\mathbf{BV}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

If
x(\cdot)\in{\mathbf{C}}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
k\in\mathbf{BV}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then the Riemann-Stieltjes integral is defined by

\int_{a}^{b}\langle x(t),\dif k(t)\rangle=\lim_{\absolutevalue{\Delta}\to 0}\sum_{i=0}^{n_{\Delta}-1}\langle x(t_{i}),k(t_{i+1})-k(t_{i})\rangle.

Equipped with the norm

\norm{k}_{\mathbf{BV}([a,b]:\mathbb{R}^{d}}\triangleq\norm{x}_{{\mathbf{C}}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{d})}\cdot\nu_{a}^{b}(k)

the space
\mathbf{BV}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a Banach space. An element
k\in\mathbf{BV}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{d})
can be identified with the continuous linear functional

x\in{\mathbf{C}}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{d})\mapsto\langle x(a),k(a)\rangle+\int_{a}^{b}\langle x(t),\dif k(t)\rangle

With this identification, we can consider
\mathbf{BV}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{d})
as the dual space to
{\mathbf{C}}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
